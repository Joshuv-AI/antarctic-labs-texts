Mathematical Justification of a Baer–Nunziato Model for a Compressible Viscous Fluid with Phase Transition Thanks: * Corresponding author
Christian Rohde, Florian Wendt*

Abstract
. In this work, we justify a Baer–Nunziato system including appropriate closure terms as the macroscopic description of a compressible viscous fluid that can occur in a liquid or a vapor phase in the isothermal framework. As a mathematical model for the two-phase fluid on the detailed scale we chose a non-local version of the Navier–Stokes–Korteweg equations in the one-dimensional and periodic setting. Our justification relies on anticipating the macroscopic description of the two-phase fluid as the limit system for a sequence of solutions with highly oscillating initial densities. Interpreting the density as a parametrized measure, we extract a limit system consisting of a kinetic equation for the parametrized measure and a momentum equation for the velocity. Under the assumption that the initial density distributions converge in the limit to a convex combination of Dirac-measures, we show by a uniqueness result that the parametrized measure also has to be a convex combination of Dirac-measures and, that the limit system reduces to the Baer–Nunziato system. This work extends existing results concerning the justification of Baer–Nunziato models as the macroscopic description of multi-fluid models in the sense, that we allow for phase transition effects on the detailed scale. This work also includes a new global-in-time well-posedness result for the Cauchy problem of the non-local Navier–Stokes–Korteweg equations.

1.
Introduction

We consider a homogeneous compressible viscous fluid that can occur in a liquid and a vapor phase.
A widely accepted mathematical description of such a two-phase fluid is given by the Navier–Stokes–Korteweg (NSK) equations (
[
1
]
).
These equations model the two-phase fluid with a diffuse interface, i.e. the interface between the liquid and the vapor is assumed to have small but positive Lebesgue-measure and the density varies rapidly but smoothly over the interface.
Let us assume that we are given some positive time
T>0
.
In the isothermal and periodic framework, the NSK model describes then the dynamics of the two-phase fluid via the fluid’s density
\rho\colon[0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and the fluid’s velocity
u\colon[0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
that obey the system of equations

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)&=0\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})+\partial_{x}P(\rho)-\mu\partial_{xx}u+\kappa\rho\partial_{xxx}\rho&=0\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\end{aligned}\right.

with initial conditions

(1.2)

\displaystyle\rho(0,\cdot)=\rho_{0},\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{T}.

Here,
\mu>0
denotes the constant viscosity coefficient and
\kappa>0
denotes the constant capillarity coefficient.
In order to account for phase transition effects, the equation of state
P\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is assumed to be of Van-der-Waals type.
More precisely, we shall assume that there exist some constants
0<B_{1}<B_{2}<\infty
, such that
P
is monotonically increasing on
[0,B_{1}]\cup[B_{2},\infty)
and monotonically decreasing on
[B_{1},B_{2}]
.
Accordingly, we call then the fluid’s state liquid (spinodal, vapor), if the fluid’s density satisfies
\rho\in[0,B_{1})
(
[B_{1},B_{2}),[B_{2},\infty)
).
An illustration of a pressure function of Van-der-Waals type is given in Figure
1
in Section
2
.

To find effective equations for the NSK model
(
1.1
)
, we start from a sequence of initial data
(\rho_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{0})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
should display the number of phase transitions that we have initially.
Due to the high number of phase transitions, we expect the initial density sequence
(\rho_{n}^{0})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to be highly oscillating between the vapor density and the liquid density.
As a simplification of the problem, we assume that such oscillations do not occur for the velocity sequence.
After constructing an appropriate corresponding sequence of solutions
(\rho_{n},u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, it is then reasonable to assume that the macroscopic equations are found in the limit
n\to\infty
(i.e. in the limit where the number of phase transitions tends to infinity).
Thus, the derivation of effective equations reduces to study the propagation of initial density oscillations for system
(
1.1
)
.
One method to analyze the propagation of initial density oscillations relies on interpreting the density as a parametrized measure (
[
25
,
29
,
13
]
).
However, system
(
1.1
)
seems hardly accessible for such an investigation due to the capillarity term
\kappa\rho\partial_{xxx}\rho
.
To overcome this remedy, we choose a non-local approximation of
(
1.1
)
, that was proposed in
[
22
]
.
In this system, that we call the non-local NSK system from now on, the capillarity term
\kappa\rho\partial_{xxx}\rho
is substituted by a term of lower order by introducing an additional unknown that satisfies an elliptic equation.
More precisely, in the non-local NSK model, the dynamics of the two-phase fluid are described by the fluid’s density
\rho\colon[0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, the fluid’s velocity
u\colon[0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
and the order parameter
c\colon[0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
that satisfy the system of equations

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)&=0\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})+\partial_{x}P(\rho)-\mu\partial_{xx}u-\gamma\rho\partial_{x}(c-\rho)&=0\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
-\kappa\partial_{xx}c+\gamma(c-\rho)&=0\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\end{aligned}\right.

with initial conditions
(
1.2
)
.
Here, the quantities
\mu,\kappa,P
are defined as before and
\gamma>0
denotes the constant coupling coefficient.
Formally, we notice that for
\gamma\to\infty
we recover the compressible NSK system
(
1.1
)
.
At this point we refer to
[
20
]
for a numerical solution of
(
1.3
)
and to
[
12
]
for a rigorous mathematical result concerning the convergence of
(
1.3
)
to
(
1.1
)
via a relative entropy approach.

The momentum equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{2}
can be rewritten as

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})+\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)-\mu\partial_{xx}u-\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c

\displaystyle=0,

with an artificial pressure function

(1.5)

\displaystyle P_{\gamma}(r):=P(r)+\frac{\gamma}{2}r^{2}

being monotone for a pressure function
P
of Van-der-Waals type provided
\gamma
is chosen large enough.
In this paper, we justify the following system as a macroscopic description for a compressible liquid-vapor flow that is modeled with the non-local NSK equations on the detailed scale:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\alpha_{+}+u\partial_{x}\alpha_{+}&=\frac{\alpha_{+}\alpha_{-}}{\mu}\Bigl(P(\rho_{+})-P(\rho_{-})+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\rho_{+}^{2}-\rho_{-}^{2})\Bigr)\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\alpha_{-}+u\partial_{x}\alpha_{-}&=\frac{\alpha_{-}\alpha_{+}}{\mu}\Bigl(P(\rho_{-})-P(\rho_{+})+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\rho_{-}^{2}-\rho_{+}^{2})\Bigr)\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{+}+\partial_{x}(\rho_{+}u)&=\frac{\rho_{+}\alpha_{-}}{\mu}\Bigl(P(\rho_{-})-P(\rho_{+})+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\rho_{-}^{2}-\rho_{+}^{2})\Bigr)\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}\rho_{-}+\partial_{x}(\rho_{-}u)&=\frac{\rho_{-}\alpha_{+}}{\mu}\Bigl(P(\rho_{+})-P(\rho_{-})+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\rho_{+}^{2}-\rho_{-}^{2})\Bigr)\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})&=\mu\partial_{xx}u-\overline{P}+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\\
-\kappa\partial_{xx}c+\gamma c&=\gamma\rho\quad&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T},\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle\rho=\alpha_{+}\rho_{+}+\alpha_{-}\rho_{-},\quad\overline{P}=\alpha_{+}P(\rho_{+})+\alpha_{-}P(\rho_{-})+\frac{\gamma}{2}(\alpha_{+}\rho_{+}^{2}+\alpha_{-}\rho_{-}^{2}).

Here,
\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\rho_{+},\rho_{-}\colon[0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the volume fraction of the liquid phase, the volume fraction of the vapor phase, the partial density of the liquid phase, the partial density of the vapor phase, respectively.
The effective model
(
1.6
)
falls into the class of one-velocity Baer–Nunziato (BN) multi-fluid models.
For a detailed background on this class of multi-fluid models see e.g.
[
2
,
24
,
19
,
18
]
.
For a discussion and interpretation of the macroscopic model
(
1.6
)
, we refer to the end of Section
2
.
Our justification follows the homogenization methodology in
[
7
]
, where the authors justify a one-velocity multi-fluid BN model for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosity in the one-dimensional periodic framework.
There, the authors analyzed the propagation of initial density oscillations by interpreting the density as a parameterized measure and obtained effective equations in the form of a kinetic equation for the parametrized measure and a momentum equation for the velocity.
After characterizing the parameterized measure as a convex combination of Dirac-measures these equations then reduce to a one-velocity multi-fluid BN system.
Hence, the key point to justify such a multi-fluid model is to prove the propagation of the special structure for the parametrized measure in time.
This methodology was also used in
[
13
,
8
,
6
]
to justify rigorously a one-velocity BN model for the compressible isentropic Navier–Stokes equations with constant viscosity in the
3
-D framework.
See also
[
21
]
for the derivation of the kinetic equation in terms of a probability density function without the characterization of the parametrized measure.
At this point, we also refer to
[
3
]
, where the authors extended the methodology for a two-component flow with two different pressure laws for each phase and to
[
4
]
for a semi-discrete approach and a numerical investigation of the problem setting in
[
3
]
.
However, all these results do not incorporate phase transition phenomena in the description of the fluid on the detailed scale.
Thus, this work can be seen as a generalization of the results in
[
7
]
to a setting where phase transition effects are included on the detailed scale.
Mathematically, this generalization requires to deal with a non-monotone pressure law and an additional term in the momentum equation.
The difficulties resulting from the additional term in the momentum equation are twofold.
On the one hand, the a-priori estimates in
[
7
]
rely on the momentum equation, so that it is not clear, whether these carry over to the non-local NSK system.
On the other hand, we have to prove convergence for the additional term in the homogenization process.
We overcome the non-monotonicity of the pressure function by rewriting the momentum equation in terms of the artificial pressure function
P_{\gamma}
(see
(
1.4
)
) that we assume to be monotone (c.f. Section
2
).
As a novelty, we verify a-priori estimates corresponding to them in
[
7
]
for the non-local NSK system.
In particular an entropy inequality due to Bresch and Desjardin (c.f. Section
3
) is derived.
Then, we solve the convergence issue by using a compensated compactness lemma from
[
7
]
.
As we shall see, this lemma can be applied here, since the a-priori estimates for the order parameter are strong enough.
Our main tool to control the order parameter is an elliptic regularity estimate.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we precisely formulate the assumptions that we impose on the pressure function
P
and the coupling parameter
\gamma
, and state our main results Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.5
.
These are given by a global-in-time well-posedness result (Theorem
2.4
) for the Cauchy problem
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
and a homogenization result (Theorem
2.5
).
In Section
3
, we give a full proof of the global-in-time well-posedness result. By providing the proof of this result, we also recover crucial a-priori bounds that are used in Section
4
.
In Section
4
, we collect refined a-priori estimates concerning the effective viscous flux that we need for the homogenization procedure.
In Section
5
, we perform the homogenization procedure following the methodology in
[
7
]
and conclude the proof of the homogenization result.
In Section
6
we give some conclusions.
In the Appendix, we provide some technical results on the existence of solutions to a specific BN system and a uniqueness result for measure-valued solutions to a kinetic equation.

This will impose a certain relation between the pressure function

In the following definition, we make precise what kind of assumptions on

Definition 2.1

(Admissible Pressure Function)

Let

Then we call the pair

Remark 2.2
.

The Van-der-Waals pressure law is a widely accepted equation of state for modeling two-phase phenomena of a homogeneous fluid.
At a constant reference temperature
T_{\star}>0
, it is given by

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{VdW}}\colon[0,B)\to[0,\infty),\quad\rho\mapsto P_{\mathrm{VdW}}(\rho):=\frac{RT_{\star}\rho}{B-\rho}-A\rho^{2},

where
A,B,R>0
are material coefficients.
For the reference temperature
T_{\star}>0
small enough, more precisely

\displaystyle T_{\star}<\frac{AB^{2}}{R},

the pressure function
P_{\mathrm{VdW}}
admits a spinodal region in the sense of the preceding discussion.
The derivative of
P_{\mathrm{VdW}}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{VdW}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{BRT_{\star}}{(B-r)^{2}}-2Ar.

In particular, we have that, up to a change of coordinate,
(P_{\mathrm{VdW}},\gamma)
is admissible for

\displaystyle\gamma\geq 2A.

Let us assume that we are given some admissible pair

Then we associate to

(2.1)

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(r)=W^{\prime\prime}(r)r.

We notice that monotonically increasing/decreasing regions of

Before we state this result, let us recall how a
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
-function gives rise to a positive finite Radon measure on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let

. Then we can define

\displaystyle\Theta\colon C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R},\quad b\mapsto\langle\Theta,b\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x,f(x))\>\mathrm{d}x.

It is an easy matter to check that this defines a positive linear functional on
C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
, so that indeed
\Theta\in\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})

If we assume furthermore that

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq f(x)\leq C_{2}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{T},

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{spt}\,(\Theta)\subseteq\mathbb{T}\times[C_{1},C_{2}].

This implies
\Theta\in\mathcal{M}_{c}^{+}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})

1.3

Theorem 2.5

(Main Result)
.

Let
\kappa,\mu,\gamma,M_{0}>0
and assume that
(P,\gamma)
is admissible.
Suppose that
\rho_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})

\displaystyle M_{0}^{-1}\leq\rho_{n}^{0}(x)\leq M_{0}\quad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{T},\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl\{||u_{n}^{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\bigr\}<\infty,

for some positive constant
M_{0}
and, that
u_{n}^{0}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(\rho_{n},u_{n},c_{n})
denote the global-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
with initial data
(\rho_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{0})
(c.f. Theorem

Assume that there exists
\alpha_{+}^{0},\alpha_{-}^{0},\rho_{+}^{0},\rho_{-}^{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha_{0\pm}\leq 1,\quad\alpha_{0+}+\alpha_{0-}=1,\quad M_{0}^{-1}\leq\rho_{0\pm}\leq M_{0}\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T},

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{n}^{0},b\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x,\rho_{n}^{0}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\,b\in C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{n}^{0},b\rangle\longrightarrow\int_{\mathbb{T}}\alpha_{+}^{0}(x)b(\rho_{+}^{0}(x))+\alpha_{-}^{0}(x)b(\rho_{-}^{0}(x))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\,b\in C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).

Then there exist some time
T_{1}>0
,
\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\rho_{+},\rho_{-}\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C([0,T_{1}];L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))
and
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C([0,T_{1}];C(\mathbb{T}))
, such that (up to a subsequence)
\Theta_{n}\in C_{\mathrm{w}}([0,T_{1}];\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
, defined for any
t\in[0,T_{1}]
through

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{n}(t),b\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x,\rho_{n}(t,x))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\,b\in C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}),

converges in
C_{\mathrm{w}}([0,T_{1}];\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
to
\Theta
, where
\Theta
is given for any
t\in[0,T_{1}]
through

\displaystyle\langle\Theta(t),b\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\alpha_{+}(t,x)b(x,\rho_{+}(t,x))+\alpha_{-}(t,x)b(x,\rho_{-}(t,x))\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\forall\,b\in C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in }C([0,T_{1}];C(\mathbb{T})),\quad c_{n}\rightharpoonup c\quad\text{in }L^{2}(0,T_{1};H^{2}(\mathbb{T})).

The functions

\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\rho_{+},\rho_{-},u,c
satisfy the BN system
(
1.6
)
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T_{1})\times\mathbb{T})
and initial conditions

\displaystyle\alpha_{+}(0,\cdot)=\alpha_{+}^{0},\quad\alpha_{-}(0,\cdot)=\alpha_{-}^{0},\quad\rho_{+}(0,\cdot)=\rho_{+}^{0},\quad\rho_{-}(0,\cdot)=\rho_{-}^{0}\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}

a.e. in
\mathbb{T}
.

To the end of this section let us discuss how our results justify the BN system
(
1.6
)
as the macroscopic description of a two-phase fluid modeled by the non-local NSK system on the detailed scale.
We start with a sequence of initial data
(\rho_{n}^{0},u_{n}^{0})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and construct the corresponding sequence of solutions
(\rho_{n},u_{n},c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
according to Theorem
2.4
.
We anticipate to find the macroscopic description of the two-phase fluid in the limit
n\to\infty
, when the initial density sequence highly oscillates between the liquid and the vapor density, for instance we could have

\displaystyle\rho^{0}_{n}(x)=\rho^{0}(nx)

for some density profile
\rho^{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
bounded below by some positive constant.
The continuity assumption on the initial density is physically reasonable here, since we deal with a diffuse interface model.
Since we expect that the initial density sequence is highly oscillating between the liquid and the vapor density, it is reasonable to assume that the corresponding sequence of initial density distributions
(\Theta_{n}^{0})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
concentrates in the limit on two values in the sense of assumption
(
2.3
)
.
Under this assumption, Theorem
2
tells us then that for a small time, after identifying the density sequence
(\rho_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as a sequence of parametrized measures
(\Theta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we find a limit velocity
u
and a limit density distribution
\Theta
, such that
(\Theta_{n},u_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
(\Theta,u)
in an appropriate way and moreover, the structure of the initial density distribution is preserved with functions
\alpha_{+},\alpha_{-},\rho_{+},\rho_{-}
that solve the BN system
(
1.6
)
.
Agreeing that
(\Theta,u)
describes our two-phase fluid on the macroscopic scale, this justifies the BN system
(
1.6
)
as the macroscopic description.
Since on the detailed scale, the NSK system
(
1.1
)
approximates
(
1.3
)
for
\gamma\to\infty
, it would be interesting, whether one could find a limit system for
\gamma\to\infty
in
(
1.6
)
and whether the approximation
\gamma\to\infty
commutes with the homogenization limit.
The limit BN system for
\gamma\to\infty
could then be interpreted as a macroscopic model for the NSK system
(
1.1
)
.

3.
Construction of Global-in-Time Strong Solutions

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
2.4
.
The uniqueness of strong solutions for the Cauchy problem
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
can be proven by classical arguments (see e.g. in
[
28
]
).
Therefore, we omit the proof for the uniqueness part here and focus on the existence part.
For this part, we follow the lines in
[
17
]
and provide a continuation argument for the local-in-time strong solution.
This continuation argument relies on suitable a-priori estimates for the local-in-time strong solution.
In comparison to the compressible Navier–Stokes equations, we have to deal with the additional term
\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c
in the momentum equation.
Exploiting elliptic regularity results, we are able to control this term in such a way, that the a-priori estimates from
[
17
]
carry over.
Our proof relies on the following local-in-time existence result for the Cauchy problem
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let the hypothesis of Theorem
2.4
hold true.

Then there exist a time
T_{0}>0
that only depends on

\displaystyle||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},||\rho_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},M_{0},\gamma,\kappa,\mu,

and unique functions

\displaystyle\rho\in C([0,T_{0});H^{1}(\mathbb{T})),\quad\rho>0\quad\text{on }[0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T},\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho\in C([0,T_{0});L^{2}(\mathbb{T})),

\displaystyle u\in C([0,T_{0});H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))\times L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\mathbb{T})),\quad

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T})),

\displaystyle c\in C([0,T_{0});H^{3}(\mathbb{T}))

that satisfy
(
1.3
)
a.e. on
(0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}
, the initial conditions
(
1.2
)
a.e. in
\mathbb{T}
, and

(3.1)

\displaystyle-\kappa\partial_{xx}c(t)+\gamma c(t)=\gamma\rho(t)\quad\text{a.e. on }\mathbb{T}

for any
t\in[0,T_{0})
.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
can be obtained via the method of successive approximation as in
[
27
]
or via a fixed point argument as in
[
28
]
.
We omit a proof here.

Let us denote by
(\rho,u,c)
the local-in-time strong solution existing on
[0,T_{0})
.
If we show that

\displaystyle C(T_{0})^{-1}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq C(T_{0})\quad\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T},

(3.2)

\displaystyle||\rho||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}+||u||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(T_{0})

for some
C(T_{0})>0
only depending on the initial data, a continuation argument yields that the local solution
(\rho,u,c)
must be global, i.e.
T_{0}=\infty
.
The goal for the rest of this section is the verification of the bounds in
(
3.2
)
.
Throughout this whole section
C(T_{0})>0
denotes a generic constant that may vary from line to line but only depends on

||\rho_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},M_{0},\mu,\kappa,\gamma,T_{0}.

To verify the bounds
(
3.2
)
, we exploit the classical energy estimate and an entropy inequality (BD-entropy inequality) that was first derived by the authors in
[
5
]
for capillary fluids of Korteweg type and then verified by the authors in
[
17
,
16
]
for the compressible Navier–Stokes system with density dependent viscosity.
Before we come to these inequalities, let us state the following auxiliary lemma that exploits the fact that
c
satisfies an elliptic equation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{ell}}>0
that does only depend on
\kappa,\gamma
, such that for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle||c(t)||_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{\mathrm{ell}}||\rho(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

Proof.

For any
t\in[0,T_{0})
we have that
c(t)
satisfies the elliptic equation
(
3.1
)
.
Thus, Lemma
3.2
follows from standard interior regularity estimates, see e.g.
[
10
]
.
∎

Now we come to the classical energy estimate.

Proposition 3.3

(Energy dissipation)
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then we have for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}+W(\rho)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq E_{0},

where

\displaystyle E_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}M_{0}||u_{0}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+\max\limits_{\lambda\in[M_{0}^{-1},M_{0}]}\bigl\{|W(\lambda)|\bigr\}+\gamma M_{0}^{2}+\gamma C_{\mathrm{ell}}^{2}M_{0}^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}C_{\mathrm{ell}}^{2}M_{0}^{2}.

Proof.

Since we are in the strong solution framework, the following calculations are justified. First, using the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
, we may rewrite the momentum equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
as

\displaystyle\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)+P^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{x}\rho-\mu\partial_{xx}u+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}(\rho-c)=0.

Multiplying this equation by
u
, and integrating over the spatial domain
\mathbb{T}
yields then for almost all
t\in[0,T_{0})
the relation

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)u\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}}P^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{x}\rho u\,\mathrm{d}x-\mu\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{xx}uu\,\mathrm{d}x+\gamma\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\partial_{x}(\rho-c)u\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Now we analyze each term in equation
(
3.3
)
separately. For the first term, we obtain by virtue of the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
for almost all
t\in[0,\infty)

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)u\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the second term, we obtain again by virtue of the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
and relation
(
2.1
)
using integration by parts

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}P^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{x}\rho u\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime\prime}(\rho)(\partial_{x}\rho)\rho u\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(W^{\prime}(\rho))\rho u\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{x}(\rho u)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}W^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the third term in equation
(
3.3
)
, integration by parts yields

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\mu\int_{\mathbb{T}}u\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x=\mu\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the last term, we use again the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
and integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\partial_{x}(\rho-c)\rho u\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}\rho\gamma(\rho-c)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Multiplying the elliptic equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{3}
by
\partial_{t}c
and integrating over the spatial domain
\mathbb{T}
yields for almost all
t\in[0,T_{0})

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\kappa\partial_{t}c\partial_{xx}c\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\partial_{t}c(\rho-c)\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\partial_{t}c(\rho-c)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\partial_{x}(\rho-c)\rho u\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\partial_{t}c(\rho-c)\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Together, equations
(
3.3
)
,
(
3.4
)
,
(
3.5
)
,
(
3.6
)
and
(
3
)
yield for almost all
t\in[0,T_{0})

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|u|^{2}+W(\rho)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

After integration in time we obtain for any
t\in[0,T_{0})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|u|^{2}+W(\rho)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{x}u|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}(x)|u_{0}(x)|^{2}+W(\rho_{0}(x))+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho_{0}(x)-c(0,x)|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c(0,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq E_{0},

where in the last inequality we have used Hölder’s and Young’s inequality and Lemma
3.2
for
t=0
.
∎

Combining Proposition
3.3
and relation
(
2.2
)
, we obtain some control on the density.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then there exists some constant
E_{1}\geq 0
that only depends on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})},\mu,\gamma,\kappa
, such that for any
t\in[0,T_{0})
, we have

\displaystyle||\rho(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+||c(t)||_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}+||c(t)||_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq E_{1}.

Proof.

With relation
(
2.2
)
and Proposition
3.3
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\rho}+C_{\rho}\int_{\mathbb{T}}W(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{\rho}+C_{\rho}E_{0}.

Using the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
and the elliptic estimate provided by Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle||c(t)||_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C||c(t)||_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq CC_{\mathrm{ell}}||\rho(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq CC_{\mathrm{ell}}\sqrt{C_{\rho}+C_{\rho}E_{0}},

where
C
denotes the embedding constant of
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Setting

\displaystyle E_{1}:=3\max\biggl\{\sqrt{C_{\rho}+C_{\rho}E_{0}},CC_{\mathrm{ell}}\sqrt{C_{\rho}+C_{\rho}E_{0}},C_{\mathrm{ell}}\sqrt{C_{\rho}+C_{\rho}E_{0}}\biggr\}

yields the claim.
∎

As a next step, we derive the BD-entropy inequality. From this inequality we gain some control on the derivative of the density. Compared to the situation for the compressible Navier–Stokes equations in
[
17
]
, we have to treat an additional term involving the order parameter in the momentum equation.
Fortunately, the a-priori estimates for
c
derived in Proposition
3.4
are strong enough to control this additional term. We emphasize that the monotonicity of
P_{\gamma}
is crucial in order to use the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then we have for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|u+\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}+W(\rho)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.8)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(\varphi(\rho))\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C(T_{0}),

where
\varphi(r):=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{\mu}{s^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s
,
and in particular

(3.9)

\displaystyle\biggl|\biggl|\partial_{x}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr)\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(T_{0}).

Proof.

For the proof, we follow the lines in
[
17
]
.
In the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we have already verified the relation

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+W(\rho)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\mu\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We infer with a mollification argument for the density and integration by parts the relation

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho u\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}(\rho u)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(\rho u)\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x.

On the one hand, the momentum equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{2}
yields then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}(\rho u)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\partial_{xx}u+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)-\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)

\displaystyle\qquad-\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x

and on the other hand the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
yields

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}(\rho u)\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\partial_{t}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}(\rho u)|^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x,

so that together we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho u\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\partial_{xx}u+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)+|\partial_{x}(\rho u)|^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\qquad-\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)-\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x.

The regularity of the strong solution
(\rho,u,c)
allows to regularize the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
so that we may infer

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu\partial_{xx}u\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the technical details and the regularization argument we refer to
[
17
]
, where this relation was proven in a more complicated situation, where the viscosity depends on the density.
Combining
(
3.11
)
and
(
3.12
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho u\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)+|\partial_{x}(\rho u)|^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)-\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)+\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\bigl(|\partial_{x}(\rho u)|^{2}-\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\rho\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)+\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho^{2}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)+\mu|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we have used the relation

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}(\rho u)|^{2}-\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\rho=\rho^{2}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}.

Combining
(
3.10
)
and
(
3.13
)
yields

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\eta(t)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

\displaystyle\eta(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{2}\rho|u+\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}+W(\rho)+\frac{\gamma}{2}|\rho-c|^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{2}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
(P,\gamma)
is admissible, we have that
P_{\gamma}
is monotone and therefore

\displaystyle\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)=P_{\gamma}^{\prime}(\rho)\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}\geq 0,

so that the second term on the left-hand side in
(
3.14
)
is non-negative.
With Proposition
3.3
, Proposition
3.4
and the conservation of mass we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\gamma||\partial_{x}c(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})\biggl(1+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})\biggl(1+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|u+\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})\biggl(1+\eta(t)\biggr),

so that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\eta(t)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(T_{0})\biggl(1+\eta(t)\biggr).

An application of Gronwall’s inequality yields then the relation
(
3.5
)
.
Relation
(
3.5
)
and Proposition
3.3
imply then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(T_{0}).

Since

\displaystyle\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho)|^{2}=4\mu^{2}\biggl|\partial_{x}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr)\biggr|^{2},

we conclude
(
3.9
)
.
∎

From the BD-entropy inequality, we deduce
L^{\infty}
-bounds for the density.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then we have

\displaystyle C(T_{0})^{-1}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq C(T_{0})\quad\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound.
To do this, we use the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
According to Proposition
3.5
, we only have to show that for any
t\in[0,T_{0})

\displaystyle\biggl|\biggl|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}(t)\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(T_{0}).

Using the Poincaré inequality and Proposition
3.5
, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}(t)\biggr]\leq C(T_{0}).

In the following calculations we omit the argument for
t
.
Conservation of mass leads to

\displaystyle M_{0}^{-1}\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}\leq M_{0}.

This implies with Hölder’s inequality

(3.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sqrt{\rho}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\sqrt{M_{0}}\leq C(T_{0}).

Together with Proposition
3.4
, Poincaré’s inequality and Proposition
3.5
, this implies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}-\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr]\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq||\rho(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\biggl|\biggl|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}-\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr]\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(T_{0}).

Finally, we obtain from the above relations that

\displaystyle M_{0}^{-1}\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr]\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr]\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}-\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr]\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(T_{0}),

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\biggl[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr]\leq C(T_{0}).

The proof for the lower bound is complete.
For the upper bound, we use the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,1}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, so that we only have to prove

\displaystyle||\sqrt{\rho}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+||\partial_{x}\sqrt{\rho}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(T_{0}).

We have already estimated the first term in
(
3.15
)
. For the second term, we use
Proposition
3.4
and Proposition
3.5
to estimate

\displaystyle 2||\partial_{x}\sqrt{\rho}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\biggl|\partial_{x}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr)\biggr|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq||\rho(t)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\biggl|\biggl|\partial_{x}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr)\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C(T_{0}).

∎

With the
L^{\infty}
-bounds for the density, we come back to the BD-entropy inequality and obtain an uniform-in-time
H^{1}
-bound for
\rho
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then we have

\displaystyle||\rho||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(T_{0}).

Proof.

With Proposition
3.4
we only have to show that

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}\rho||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(T_{0}).

This can be done by using Proposition
3.5
and Lemma
3.6

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho^{3}\biggl|\frac{\partial_{x}\rho}{\rho^{\frac{3}{2}}}\biggr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(T_{0})\biggl|\biggl|\partial_{x}\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}\biggr)\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\leq C(T_{0}).

∎

Finally, we prove an uniform-in-time
H^{1}
-bound for the velocity
u
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the local-in-time strong solution
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
that exists on
[0,T_{0})
.

Then we have

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(T_{0}).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)=\mu\partial_{xx}u-\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\quad\text{a.e. in }(0,T_{0})\times\mathbb{T}.

Both sides are in
L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
.
Hence, we may multiply both sides by
\partial_{xx}u\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))
and integrate in space and time to obtain

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}-\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{xx}u+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

After an approximation argument using integration by parts, the left hand side can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t,x)|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}|\partial_{x}u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}u+\rho|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Plugging this relation into (
3.16
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t,x)|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}|\partial_{x}u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{xx}u-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\rho\bigl(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u\bigr)\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

We estimate the right hand side with the help of Proposition
3.7
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, let us denote by
C(\varepsilon)>0
a generic positive constant that may vary from line to line but only depends on
\varepsilon
.
First we obtain with Young’s inequality and Proposition
3.7

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\mu\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}||\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}1\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Next, we use the relation

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)=\frac{\partial_{x}\rho}{\rho}(\mu\partial_{xx}u-\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)+\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c),

to obtain with Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\biggl|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mu}\biggl|\biggl|\frac{1}{\rho}\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}||\rho||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\biggl|\biggl|\frac{1}{\rho}\biggr|\biggr|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}||\rho||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}||\partial_{x}P_{\gamma}(\rho)||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\gamma||\partial_{x}\rho||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})}||\partial_{x}c||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})C(\varepsilon)\biggl(1+||\partial_{x}u||^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\biggr)+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we have used Proposition
3.7
to bound the
H^{1}
-norms of
\rho
, the control on
\partial_{x}c
that is provided by Proposition
3.4
and the interpolation inequality

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{\frac{1}{2}}||\partial_{xx}u||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

for some constant
C>0
.
This relation yields

\displaystyle\biggl|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\biggl(1+||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

By similar arguments, we find

\displaystyle\biggl|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\partial_{x}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr|\leq C(T_{0})C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\biggl(1+||\partial_{x}u||^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Finally, we estimate with Proposition
3.4

\displaystyle\biggl|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c\partial_{xx}u\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})C(\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}1\,\mathrm{d}\tau+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Choosing
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{8}
, we obtain from the preceding estimates the relation

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(t,x)|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu|\partial_{xx}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho_{0}|\partial_{x}u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+C(T_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\biggl(1+||\partial_{x}u||^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(T_{0})+C(T_{0})\int_{0}^{t}\biggl(1+||\partial_{x}u||^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using Proposition
3.3
and applying Gronwall’s inequality yields

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq C(T_{0}).

Together with Proposition
3.3
, this yields the claim.
∎

4.
Refined A-Priori Estimates

In this section, we prove an a-priori estimate concerning the effective viscous flux that is necessary to perform the homogenization procedure in Section
5
.
Since we will assume strong oscillations in the initial densities during the homogenization process, it is crucial that this a-priori estimate does only depend on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},\mu,\gamma,\kappa
, but not on quantities that involve the derivative of the density.
Let us recall that we have already proven two such a-priori estimates in Section
3
, namely Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
.
However, these a-priori estimates are not strong enough to control the homogenization procedure.
Following
[
7
]
, we introduce for a global-in-time strong solution
(\rho,u,c)
of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
the effective viscous flux as

\displaystyle\Sigma:=\mu\partial_{x}u-P_{\gamma}(\rho)

and accordingly the initial effective viscous flux as

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}:=\mu\partial_{x}u_{0}-P_{\gamma}(\rho_{0}).

We then prove the following estimate.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the global-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
.

Then there exist some time
T_{0}>0
and some constant
C_{0}>0
, both only depending on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},\gamma,\kappa,\mu
, such that we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle(2M_{0})^{-1}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq 2M_{0}\quad\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T},

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}+||\partial_{x}\Sigma||_{L^{2}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}\leq C_{0}.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
follows the lines in
[
7
]
via two lemmata. To shorten the notation, let us introduce the following quantities:

\displaystyle K^{0}_{P_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle:=\max\limits_{\lambda\in[(2M_{0})^{-1},2M_{0}]}\bigl\{|P_{\gamma}(\lambda)|\bigr\},

\displaystyle K^{0}_{u}

\displaystyle:=\frac{8}{\mu^{2}}\biggl(2+5M_{0}\mu\biggr)\biggl(||\Sigma_{0}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+1+|K^{0}_{P_{\gamma}}|^{2}\biggr),

\displaystyle K^{0}_{d}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\mu}\biggl[C_{\mathrm{Sob}}\sqrt{2\mu E_{0}+K^{0}_{u}+2|K^{0}_{P_{\gamma}}|^{2}}+K^{0}_{P_{\gamma}}\biggr].

Here,
C_{\mathrm{Sob}}>0
denotes the constant of the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
We emphasize, that
K^{0}_{P_{\gamma}},K^{0}_{u}
and
K^{0}_{d}
only depend on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},\mu,\gamma
and
\kappa
.
Being in the strong solution framework, we may apply a continuity argument to find some small time
\tilde{T}_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, such that we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle(2M_{0})^{-1}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq 2M_{0}\quad\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,\tilde{T}_{0}]\times\mathbb{T},

and

(4.4)

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{\infty}(0,\tilde{T}_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}+||\partial_{x}\Sigma||_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T}_{0},L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\leq K^{0}_{u}.

In order to conclude the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we have to show that
\tilde{T}_{0}
in fact only depends on the allowed quantities.
This will be done as follows:
We show that there exists some time
T_{0}\in(0,\infty)
, only depending on the allowed quantities, such that if
T_{0}<\tilde{T}_{0}
, we have in fact sharper versions of the inequalities
(
4.3
)
and
(
4.4
)
on
[0,T_{0}]
.
Then, via a connectedness argument we prove that this already implies
\tilde{T}_{0}=T_{0}
.
To prove the sharper estimates, we proceed as in
[
7
]
via two lemmata.
For the first lemma, we can use the arguments demonstrated in
[
7
]
, since these only rely on the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
and an a-priori estimate for the derivative of the velocity, that is provided by Proposition
3.3
.
Therefore, we will refer for details concerning the proof to
[
7
]
.
However, the second lemma relies on the momentum equation.
Therefore, we have to modify the proof given in
[
7
]
. The crucial ingredient making our modification work is the control on the order parameter provided by Proposition
3.4
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the global-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
.
Suppose that for some
\tilde{T}_{0}\in(0,1)
, the inequalities
(
4.3
)
and
(
4.4
)
hold on
[0,\tilde{T_{0}}]
.

Then there exists some
T_{\rho}\in(0,\infty)
, only depending on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},\mu,\gamma,\kappa
, such that if
\tilde{T}_{0}<T_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{1}(0,\tilde{T}_{0};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq\sqrt{\tilde{T}_{0}}K^{0}_{d},

and

(4.5)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3M_{0}}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq\frac{3}{2}M_{0}\quad\forall\,(t,x)\in[0,\tilde{T}_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

Both estimates follow from Proposition
3.3
by the arguments presented in the proof of Proposition 6 and Proposition 7 in
[
7
]
.
∎

For the second lemma, we also follow the proof in
[
7
]
.
This proof relies on the momentum equation of the compressible Navier–Stokes equations.
Using the artificial pressure function, we enter the framework of
[
7
]
, but we have to deal with the additional term
\gamma\rho\partial_{x}c
in the momentum equation.
However, using the control provided by Proposition
3.4
, the method of proof given in
[
7
]
also applies for our situation.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
hold true and let
(\rho,u,c)
denote the global-in-time strong solution of
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1},\eqref{NSK Elliptic initial condition}
.
Suppose that for some
\tilde{T}_{0}\in(0,1)
, the inequalities
(
4.3
)
and
(
4.4
)
hold on
[0,\tilde{T_{0}}]
.

Then there exists some time
T_{u}\in(0,\infty)
only depending on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},\mu,\gamma,\kappa
, such that if
\tilde{T}_{0}<T_{u}
, we have

(4.6)

\displaystyle||\partial_{x}u||_{L^{\infty}(0,\tilde{T}_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}+||\partial_{x}\Sigma||_{L^{2}(0,\tilde{T}_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))}^{2}\leq\frac{4}{\mu^{2}}\biggl(2+5M_{0}\mu\biggr)\biggl(||\Sigma_{0}||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}+1+|K^{0}_{P_{\gamma}}|^{2}\biggr).

Proof.

Throughout this proof, we denote by
\mathcal{C}>0
a generic positive constant that may vary from line to line but only depends on
M_{0},||u_{0}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T})},\mu,\gamma,\kappa
.
Being in the strong solution framework, it is easy to verify that
\rho
satisfies the continuity equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{1}
in the renormalized sense, that is, we have for any
b\in C^{1}([0,\infty))
, the equation

(4.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b(\rho)+\partial_{x}(b(\rho)u)-(b^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-b(\rho))\partial_{x}u=0\quad\text{a.e. on }(0,\tilde{T}_{0})\times\mathbb{T}.

Using this equation with
b=P_{\gamma}
, we deduce that
P_{\gamma}(\rho)
satisfies

\displaystyle P_{\gamma}(\rho)\in H^{1}(0,\tilde{T}_{0};L^{2}(\mathbb{T}))\cap C([0,\tilde{T}_{0}];C(\mathbb{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,\tilde{T}_{0};H^{1}(\mathbb{T})).

We fix
T\in(0,\tilde{T}_{0})
and define on
(0,T)
a multiplier via

\displaystyle m:=\mu\partial_{t}u-{\partial_{x}^{-1}}\bigl[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}(\rho)-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}(\rho)]\bigr]\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{T})).

In the sequel, we omit the argument of
P_{\gamma}(\rho)
for the sake of brevity.
We rewrite the momentum equation as

\displaystyle\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c)=\partial_{x}\Sigma.

Then, we multiply this equation by
m
and integrate over space and time to obtain

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c)m\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\Sigma m\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

We notice, that formally
\partial_{x}m=\partial_{t}\Sigma+\mathbb{E}[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}]
.
We are not allowed to integrate by parts on the right hand side due to the lack of spatial regularity for
\partial_{t}u
.
However, we may perform a Fourier series approximation of
u
.
By the regularity of
u
, this approximation is strong enough to show that the right hand side of equation
(
4.8
)
can be rewritten as

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{x}\Sigma m\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\Sigma(T,\cdot)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\Sigma_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}(\Sigma)\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

For details concerning the approximation argument we refer to
[
7
]
.
Concerning the left hand side of equation
(
4.8
)
a straightforward application of Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c){\partial_{x}^{-1}}\bigl[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}]\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|u\partial_{x}u|^{2}+|\gamma\partial_{x}c|^{2})+\frac{3}{2\mu}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\Bigl|{\partial_{x}^{-1}}\bigl[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}]\bigr]\Bigr|^{2},

which, by virtue of the elementary calculation

\displaystyle\mu\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c)\partial_{t}u=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{2}\rho|\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\rho(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-|u\partial_{x}u|^{2}-|\gamma\partial_{x}c|^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu\gamma\rho u\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}c,

results in

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho(\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c)m\ \,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u-\gamma\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|u\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\mu\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\mu\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho u\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}c\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{3}{2\mu}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\Bigl|{\partial_{x}^{-1}}\bigl[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}]\bigl]\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mu}{4M_{0}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\partial_{x}\Sigma|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|u\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\mu\gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho|\partial_{x}c|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

(4.10)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho u\partial_{x}u\partial_{x}c\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{3}{2\mu}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\rho\Bigl|{\partial_{x}^{-1}}\bigl[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}-\mathbb{E}[\partial_{t}P_{\gamma}]\bigr]\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

In the last inequality, we have used the bound
(
4.3
)
for the density
\rho
and the momentum equation
\eqref{NSK Elliptic 1}_{2}
.
Combining the equations
(
4.8
)
,
(

6.
Conclusions

In this paper, we have investigated the propagation of initial density oscillations for the non-local NSK system.
With the use of parametrized measures, we have derived a closed homogenized system consisting of a momentum equation for the velocity and a kinetic equation for a parametrized measure.
After assuming that the parametrized measure is a convex combination of Dirac-measures initially, we have proven that the kinetic equation preserves this structure.
With that structure for the parametrized measure, the kinetic equation then reduces to the BN system
(
1.6
)
.
In that sense, we have justified the BN system
(
1.6
)
rigorously as macroscopic description for a compressible liquid-vapor flow that is modeled with the non-local NSK equations on the detailed scale.
It would be interesting to extend this work to the 3D case as in
[
13
]
in the framework of finite-energy weak solutions.
It seems that the arguments of
[
13
]
should apply in order to prove that the effective equations are given by a kinetic equation for a parametrized measure and a momentum equation for the velocity.
However, proving the propagation of convex combinations of Dirac-measures seems difficult, since the arguments in
[
6
]
rely on an isentropic pressure law.
As mentioned by the authors in
[
6
]
, it is not clear how these arguments carry over to a more general pressure law as for instance a pressure law of Van-der-Waals type.
Nevertheless, we could interpret the parametrized measure as a probability density function as done in
[
21
]
and investigate the resulting equations numerically.

Theorem A.1
.

Moreover, the solution

(A.3)

\displaystyle\alpha(t,x)\geq 0,\quad(2M_{0})^{-1}\leq\rho(t,x)\leq 2M_{0}\quad\text{for a.e. }(t,x)\in[0,T_{1}]\times\mathbb{T}.

In order to prove this theorem, we first investigate the linearized equation corresponding to

A.1
)

)
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{T_{\star}}(\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}_{T_{\star}}(B(0,R_{\star})))\subseteq B(0,C_{0}\exp(T_{\star}C_{1}(1+R_{\star}^{\beta}+R_{\star}^{2}))).

Since

\displaystyle\exp(T_{\star}C_{1}(1+R_{\star}^{\beta}+R_{\star}^{2}))\leq\exp\bigl(\frac{C_{1}(1+(2C_{0})^{\beta}+(2C_{0})^{2})\ln(2)}{C_{1}(1+(2C_{0})^{\beta}+(2C_{0})^{2})}\bigr)=2,

we have shown that

\displaystyle\Phi_{T_{\star}}(B(0,R_{\star}))\subseteq B(0,R_{\star}),

and in particular

(A.11)

\displaystyle\Phi_{T}(B(0,R_{\star}))\subseteq B(0,R_{\star})\quad\forall\,T\in(0,T_{\star}].

By
(
A.7
)
and
(
A.10
)
, we have for any
(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\rho}),(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\rho})\in B(0,R_{\star})
and any
T\in(0,T_{\star}]
that

\displaystyle||\Phi_{T}(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\rho})-\Phi_{T}(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\rho})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\mathcal{L}}(T,C_{1}(1+R_{\star}^{2}+R_{\star}^{\beta}))L_{\mathcal{N}\mathcal{L}}(R_{\star})||(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\rho})-(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\rho})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T,L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}

\displaystyle=TC_{0}\exp(2C_{1}(1+R_{\star}^{2}+R_{\star}^{\beta})+C_{0})C_{1}(1+R_{\star}^{2}+R_{\star}^{\beta-1})||(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\rho})-(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\rho})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}.

Thus, we can choose
T_{1}\in(0,T_{\star}]
so small, such that

(A.12)

\displaystyle||\Phi_{T_{1}}(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\rho})-\Phi_{T_{1}}(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\rho})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1},L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq\frac{1}{2}||(\overline{\alpha},\overline{\rho})-(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\rho})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1},L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}.

We construct a sequence in
B(0,R_{\star})
via the recursion

\displaystyle(\alpha_{n+1},\rho_{n+1}):=\Phi_{T_{1}}(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where we consider for
n=0
the initial data as functions in
B(0,R_{\star})
that are constant in time, so
(\alpha_{0},\rho_{0})(t):=(\alpha_{0},\rho_{0})
for all
t\in[0,T_{1}]
.
By virtue of
(
A.11
)
, we have

(A.13)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})\in B(0,R_{\star})\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Due to
(
A.12
)
, the sequence
(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}(0,T_{1},L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))
and therefore there exists some
(\alpha,\rho)\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{1},L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))
, such that

\displaystyle(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})\longrightarrow(\alpha,\rho)\quad\text{in }L^{\infty}(0,T_{1},L^{1}(\mathbb{T})).

Relation
(
A.13
)
implies
(\alpha,\rho)\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{1},L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
.
Finally we verify that
(\alpha,\rho)
is a fixed point of the map
\Phi_{T_{1}}
. We estimate for any
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle||\Phi_{T_{1}}(\alpha,\rho)-(\alpha,\rho)||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq||\Phi_{T_{1}}(\alpha,\rho)-\Phi_{T_{1}}(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}+||\Phi_{T_{1}}(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})-(\alpha,\rho)||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}||(\alpha,\rho)-(\alpha_{n},\rho_{n})||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}+||(\alpha_{n+1},\rho_{n+1})-(\alpha,\rho)||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}.

For
n\to\infty
, the right-hand side of this inequality converges to zero and therefore

\displaystyle||\Phi_{T_{1}}(\alpha,\rho)-(\alpha,\rho)||_{L^{\infty}(0,T_{1};L^{1}(\mathbb{T}))}=0,

which gives in particular
\Phi(\alpha,\rho)=(\alpha,\rho)
a.e. in
(0,\tilde{T})\times\mathbb{T}
, so that
(\alpha,\rho)
is a fixed point of
\Phi_{T_{1}}
.
To prove assertion
(
A.3
)
, we notice that the velocity field has the regularity
u\in L^{1}(0,T_{0};W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
.
This provides us a Lipschitz continuous flow map

\displaystyle X\colon[0,T_{0}]\times[0,T_{0}]\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}.

Then, it is well-known (see for instance
[
15
]
in a more general context), that we can represent
\alpha
and
\rho
via

\displaystyle\alpha(t,x)=\alpha_{0}(X(0,t,x))\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X(s,t,x))\,\mathrm{d}s\biggr)

and

\displaystyle\rho(t,x)=\rho_{0}(X(0,t,x))\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}g(s,X(s,t,x))\,\mathrm{d}s\biggr)

for almost all
(t,x)\in[0,T_{1}]\times\mathbb{T}
, where

\displaystyle f:=P_{\gamma}(\rho)-\pi,\quad g:=-\partial_{x}u+\pi-P_{\gamma}(\rho).

Since
\alpha_{0}\geq 0
almost everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
, we conclude
\alpha\geq 0
almost everywhere on
[0,T_{1}]\times\mathbb{T}
.
Moreover, we estimate the density using the bound on
\rho_{0}
as

\displaystyle M_{0}^{-1}\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}-||g(s,\cdot)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}s\biggr)\leq\rho(t,x)\leq M_{0}\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}||g(s,\cdot)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}s\biggr)

for almost all
(t,x)\in[0,T_{1}]\times\mathbb{T}
.
Since
g\in L^{1}(0,T_{1};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))
, we can restrict
T_{1}
if necessary to obtain assertion
(
A.3
)
.
∎

Appendix B
A Uniqueness Result

The following uniqueness result is a variation of the uniqueness result provided in
[
7
]
.
The proof follows a duality argument that can also be found in
[
15
]
for instance.
The precise formulation of the uniqueness result reads as follows:

Theorem B.1
.

Let
T>0
. Let
\mathbf{V}=(V_{1},V_{2})\in L^{1}(0,T;C_{b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))^{2}
and
g\in L^{1}(0,T;C_{b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
with

(B.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{1}V_{1}\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})),\quad\partial_{2}V_{1}=0\text{ a.e. in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},

(B.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup\limits_{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{1}V_{2}(t,x_{1},x_{2})|\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,\>\partial_{2}V_{2}\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})),

(B.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup\limits_{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{1}g(t,x_{1},x_{2})|\,\mathrm{d}x_{1}\,\mathrm{d}t<\infty,\>\partial_{2}g\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})).

Assume that
\nu\in C_{\mathrm{w}}([0,T];\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
satisfies

(B.4)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}||\nu(t)||_{\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}<\infty,\quad\mathrm{spt}\,\bigl(\nu(t)\bigr)\subseteq\mathbb{T}\times K\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T],

for some compact subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and

(B.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nu+\mathrm{div}_{(x_{1},x_{2})}(\mathbf{V}\nu)+g\nu

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}),

with
\nu(0)=0
.

Then we have
\nu\equiv 0
.

Proof.

We proceed as in
[
7
]
with a duality argument.
The measure
\nu
satisfying
(
B.5
)
, means that for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nu(t),\partial_{t}\varphi+u\cdot\nabla\varphi-g\varphi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=0.

By a standard regularization argument, this implies that for any
t\in[0,T]
and any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,t]\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
, we have

(B.6)

\displaystyle\langle\nu(t),\varphi(t,\cdot)\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\nu(s),\partial_{t}\varphi+\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla\varphi-g\varphi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

We take a standard mollifier on
\mathbb{R}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\omega\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)),\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega=1,\quad 0\leq\omega\leq 1,

\displaystyle\omega_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\omega(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})\quad\text{for }\varepsilon>0,\>x\in\mathbb{R},

and mollify
u
and
g
via

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\eta)\mathbf{V}(t,y,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\eta,

\displaystyle g^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\eta)g(t,y,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\eta.

Then we have for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(0,T;C^{k}_{b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})).

Using that
g\in L^{1}(0,T;C_{b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
, we have for almost all
t\in[0,T]
the convergence

\displaystyle||g^{\varepsilon}(t)-g(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0,

Together with

(B.7)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}||g_{\varepsilon}(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{T}||g(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}t,

this implies using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem

(B.8)

\displaystyle||g^{\varepsilon}-g||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K))}\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

By the same reasoning, we obtain

(B.9)

\displaystyle||\mathbf{V}^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{V}||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K))}\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

From now on, we denote by
\mathcal{C}
a generic constant that may vary from line to line but does not depend on
\varepsilon
.
By virtue of
\nabla V_{1}\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
, we obtain for almost all
t\in[0,T]
and any
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle|V_{1}^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)-V_{1}(t,x,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\eta)|V_{1}(t,y,\eta)-V_{1}(t,x,\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\varepsilon||\nabla V_{1}(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(\xi)}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}||\nabla V_{1}(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\varepsilon.

Thus,

(B.10)

\displaystyle||V_{1}^{\varepsilon}-V_{1}||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\mathcal{C}\varepsilon.

We calculate for almost all
t\in[0,T]
and any
x,\xi\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\partial_{2}g^{\varepsilon}(t,x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\eta)\partial_{2}g(t,y,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle\leq||\partial_{2}g(t)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\omega_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\omega_{\varepsilon}(\xi-\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=||\partial_{2}g||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})},

so that, using
\partial_{2}g\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))
,

(B.11)

\displaystyle||\partial_{2}g^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))}\leq\mathcal{C}.

By the same reasoning, we observe

(B.12)

\displaystyle||\partial_{2}V_{2}^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))}+||\nabla V_{1}^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\mathcal{C}.

Finally, using
(
B.1
)
and
(
B.3
)
, we observe that

(B.13)

\displaystyle||\partial_{1}V_{2}^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))}+||\partial_{1}g||_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))}\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

Now, we fix some
\varphi^{\sharp}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
and consider for
t\in[0,T]
the backward convection problem

(B.14)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\varphi^{\varepsilon}+\mathbf{V}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}&=g^{\varepsilon}\varphi^{\varepsilon}\quad&&\text{in }(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\varphi^{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)&=\varphi^{\sharp}\quad&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

By well-known results on advection equations,
(
B.14
)
admits a solution
\varphi^{\varepsilon}
with regularity

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,1}([0,t];C^{k}_{b}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}))\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{k\geq 1}.

Moreover, since
\varphi^{\sharp}
has compact support, there exists some compact set
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{spt}\,\varphi^{\varepsilon}(s,\cdot,\cdot)\subseteq L\quad\forall\,s\in[0,t].

By a standard regularization argument via time-mollification, we are allowed to use
\varphi^{\varepsilon}
as a test function in
(
B.6
)
.
We obtain

\displaystyle\langle\nu(t),\varphi^{\sharp}\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\nu(s),\partial_{t}\varphi^{\varepsilon}+\mathbf{V}\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}-g\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(B.15)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\langle\nu(s),(\mathbf{V}-\mathbf{V}^{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}-(g-g^{\varepsilon})\varphi^{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s,

where we have used
(
B.14
)
in the last equality.
Using
(
B.4
)
, we estimate

\displaystyle\langle\nu(t),\varphi^{\sharp}\rangle\leq\mathcal{C}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3})

with

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}||(V_{1}-V_{1}^{\varepsilon})\partial_{1}\varphi^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}||(V_{2}-V_{2}^{\varepsilon})\partial_{2}\varphi^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}||(g-g^{\varepsilon})\varphi^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Applying the classical maximum principle for the advection equation
(
B.14
)
yields in combination with
(
B.7
)
for any
s\in[0,t]

(B.16)

\displaystyle||\varphi^{\varepsilon}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\leq||\varphi^{\sharp}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}||g^{\varepsilon}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}s\biggr)\leq\mathcal{C},

so that with the convergence
(
B.8
)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}||g(s)-g^{\varepsilon}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\mathrm{d}s\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

For
I_{1},I_{2}
, we need estimates for
\varphi_{1}:=\partial_{1}\varphi^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{2}:=\partial_{2}\varphi^{\varepsilon}
. Differentiating
(
B.14
)
with respect to the second spatial variable, we notice that
\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following advection problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}+\mathbf{V}^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}&=\partial_{2}g^{\varepsilon}\varphi^{\varepsilon}+(g^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{2}V_{2}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}\quad&&\text{in }(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(t)&=\partial_{2}\varphi^{\sharp}\quad&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying again a classical maximum principle for this advection problem, we obtain for any
s\in[0,t]

\displaystyle||\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(||\partial_{2}\varphi^{\sharp}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^{t}||\partial_{2}g^{\varepsilon}(\tau)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}||\varphi(\tau)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}||g^{\varepsilon}(\tau)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}+||\partial_{2}V_{2}^{\varepsilon}(\tau)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr)

(B.17)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}.

Here, we have used
(
B.7
)
,
(
B.11
)
,
(
B.12
)
and
(
B.16
)
.
From relation
(
B
)
we deduce with the convergence
(
B.9
)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\mathcal{C}\int_{0}^{t}||V_{2}(s)-V_{2}^{\varepsilon}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\,\mathrm{d}s\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

Differentiating
(
B.14
)
with respect to the first variable, we infer that
\varphi_{1}^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following advection problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\varphi_{1}^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{1}^{\varepsilon}&=\partial_{1}g^{\varepsilon}\varphi^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{1}V_{2}^{\varepsilon}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}+(g^{\varepsilon}-\partial_{1}V_{1}^{\varepsilon})\varphi_{1}^{\varepsilon}\quad&&\text{in }(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R},\\
\varphi_{1}(t)&=\partial_{1}\varphi^{\sharp}\quad&&\text{in }\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

Applying again the maximum principle for this advection problem, we infer for any
s\in[0,t]
that

\displaystyle||\varphi_{1}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(||\partial_{1}\varphi^{\sharp}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}+\int_{0}^{t}||\partial_{1}g^{\varepsilon}\varphi^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}+||\partial_{1}V_{2}^{\varepsilon}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}(||g^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}+||\partial_{1}V_{1}^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})})\,\mathrm{d}\tau\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

where we have used
(
B.7
)
,
(
B.13
)
,
(
B.16
)
and
(
B
)
.
Using
(
B.10
)
, we obtain finally

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}||V_{1}(s)-V_{1}^{\varepsilon}(s)||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times K)}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon\to 0\quad\text{for }\varepsilon\to 0.

Overall, we have shown that
(
B
)
yields in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
:

\displaystyle\langle\nu(t),\varphi^{\sharp}\rangle=0.

Since
\varphi^{\sharp}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
was arbitrary, we have shown

\displaystyle\langle\nu(t),\varphi\rangle=0\quad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).

Since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
lies dense in
C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
with respect to
||\cdot||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}
, we have shown

\displaystyle\langle\nu(t),\varphi\rangle=0\quad\forall\,\varphi\in C^{0}_{0}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}).

which means exactly
\nu(t)=0
. Since
t\in[0,T]
was arbitrary we conclude
\nu=0
.
∎

Evaluation of nomogram prediction model for postoperative deep vein thrombosis in patients with non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Pengyu Ji, Zhenping Guo, Yong Wang, Hui Wang, Chun Yin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients undergoing surgery face substantially elevated risks of postoperative deep vein thrombosis (DVT), a complication that significantly impairs recovery and survival. This study aims to develop and validate a nomogram for predicting postoperative DVT in NSCLC patients.MethodsA retrospective analysis of the clinical data was conducted, including 120 NSCLC patients with DVT and 140 without DVT. Univariate and multivariate logistic regressions identified the risk factors for postoperative DVT in NSCLC patients, and these were integrated into a nomogram using R software. External validation was performed in an additional 174 patients.ResultsUnivariate screening identified six significant predictors: advanced age (≥70 years), diabetes mellitus, adenocarcinoma histology, stage III/IV disease, presence of neoadjuvant chemotherapy, and thoracotomy approach. Multivariate refinement established four independent determinants: geriatric age [odds ratio (OR) =4.631; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.213–17.682], diabetes (OR =5.102; 95% CI: 1.167–22.316), adenocarcinoma subtype (OR =7.460; 95% CI: 1.817–30.626), and stage III pathology (OR =5.159; 95% CI: 1.407–18.913). The resulting nomogram demonstrated exceptional predictive accuracy with area under the curve (AUC) values of 0.900 (95% CI: 0.839–0.960) in the derivation cohort and 0.903 (95% CI: 0.854–0.951) upon external validation. Model calibration proved satisfactory through Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit testing (P=0.54).ConclusionsThis validated nomogram achieves superior discrimination (AUC >0.90) vs. existing risk tools while using only routine clinical parameters. It enables stratified thromboprophylaxis, optimizing resource-limited settings.Keywords: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), deep vein thrombosis (DVT), nomogram, postoperative complications, risk prediction

Key findings

• Four independent predictors of postoperative deep vein thrombosis (DVT) in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients were identified: age ≥70 years [odds ratio (OR) =4.631], diabetes (OR =5.102), adenocarcinoma histology (OR =7.460), and stage III disease (OR =5.159). The validated nomogram demonstrated excellent discrimination [area under the curve (AUC) =0.900 internal and AUC =0.903 external] and calibration.

What is known and what is new?

• NSCLC patients face elevated DVT risk; general tools (e.g., Khorana score) lack surgery-specificity.

• This study constructed the first NSCLC surgery-specific nomogram integrating adenocarcinoma histology (novel predictor) with routine clinical factors, which was also externally validated.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The nomogram enables personalized thromboprophylaxis for high-risk subgroups (e.g., elderly diabetics with advanced adenocarcinoma).

• Integrate nomogram into preoperative NSCLC workflows to guide extended prophylaxis; prioritize high-scoring patients (e.g., ≥150 points) for aggressive prevention.

Introduction

Background

Lung cancer remains a prevalent malignant tumor worldwide, with over two million new cases diagnosed annually, and stands as the leading cause of cancer-related deaths (1). Approximately 80% of lung cancer patients are diagnosed with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), of whom 10–15% present with N2 lymph node involvement (stage IIIA), while around 55% are diagnosed at advanced stages (IIIB–IV) (2). Curative-intent treatment regimens such as chemoradiotherapy or postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy are typically assigned to stage IIIA NSCLC patients, whereas stage IIIB–IV patients receive palliative care (3). Early-stage (I–II) patients possess clear surgical opportunities, with surgical resection constituting the cornerstone of therapeutic interventions. However, postoperative complications like deep vein thrombosis (DVT) may significantly compromise survival quality and recovery, underscored the imperative to refine risk stratification strategies (4,5).

Rationale and knowledge gap

Venous thromboembolism (VTE), encompassing both DVT and pulmonary embolism, frequently occurs in cancer patients, partially attributable to procoagulant substance release by primary tumors. Recent revolutionary advancements in perioperative NSCLC management—including immunotherapy, targeted agents, and combination therapies—have transformed treatment paradigms (6). While biomarker-driven therapies targeting Claudin 18.2 and fibroblast growth factor receptor 2b have expanded therapeutic options (7,8), the interplay of these approaches with postoperative complications such as DVT remains insufficiently explored. Concurrently, the emergence of liquid biopsy monitoring highlights the necessity to develop integrated models combining tumor treatment response assessment with surgical risk prediction (9). Although multiple prognostic models based on therapeutic stratification have demonstrated satisfactory predictive performance for NSCLC outcomes (10), research on models predicting postoperative DVT complications remains scarce.

The Khorana Risk Score (KRS), a classic tool for assessing VTE risk in chemotherapy patients, has demonstrated significant value in identifying high-risk candidates requiring primary thromboprophylaxis (11). However, the initial version of this scoring system lacked specific risk stratification for NSCLC, leading subsequent studies to raise questions about its applicability in this population (12,13). This skepticism was further substantiated by a multicenter meta-analysis involving 3,000 patients from randomized trials: while elevated KRS was associated with a three-fold increase in 6-month VTE risk across most cancer types, no significant correlation was observed in the lung cancer subgroup (12,13). Consequently, personalized and precise prediction of postoperative DVT risk in NSCLC patients holds critical importance for reducing incidence rates. The construction of a nomogram model for postoperative DVT risk prediction not only facilitates effective prophylaxis but also enables individualized prevention through identified high-risk factors.

Objective

Based on these considerations, we developed a personalized nomogram model to predict postoperative DVT risk in NSCLC patients, establishing a foundation for enhanced accuracy in postoperative thrombosis risk assessment. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1163/rc).

Methods

Patients

The clinical data of 120 patients with NSCLC who developed DVT after surgery (DVT group) and 140 patients with NSCLC who did not develop DVT after surgery (non-DVT group) from January 2024 to September 2024 at Ordos Central Hospital were collected for the study. Additionally, 174 NSCLC patients (70 with DVT and 104 without DVT) admitted to Ordos Central Hospital from March 2024 to October 2024 were included as the validation group for external validation. Inclusion criteria were: (I) pathologically confirmed NSCLC diagnosis; (II) underwent radical lung cancer surgery; (III) DVT patients met diagnostic criteria for DVT; and (IV) complete demographic and routine clinical data available.

DVT diagnosis served as the grouping criterion. Venous ultrasonography, the gold standard for DVT diagnosis (14), was performed using pulsed Doppler ultrasound with a C5-1 linear probe and IU22 system (Philips ATL, Bothwell, WA, USA) for bilateral lower limb examinations. Potential DVT indicators included: (I) elevated body temperature (≤38.5 ℃) and elevated white blood cell count; (II) lower extremity skin discoloration (purple-red or cyanotic), dilated superficial veins, and increased skin temperature; (III) abnormal gait, swelling, pain, and painful cramps in the lower extremities, relieved by lying down or elevation; and (IV) positive Homans sign, gastrocnemius/femoral triangle muscle pain, and anterior tibial pitting edema.

Exclusion criteria: (I) preoperative DVT confirmed by lower extremity Doppler ultrasound; (II) preoperative anticoagulant therapy; (III) concomitant other malignancies; (IV) autoimmune diseases; (V) severe postoperative complications from non-thrombotic causes; and (VI) pregnancy. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Ordos Central Hospital (No. 2023120423) and individual consent for this analysis was waived due to the retrospective nature.

Sample size

According to methodological consensus for prediction models (15) and sample size formulae (where p = expected DVT incidence rate) (16), and drawing on institutional historical data (postoperative DVT incidence ≈10%), a minimum of 246 patients (including 49 DVT cases) is required at α=0.05.

During the study period, our institution performed 2,040 curative-intent NSCLC resections. From this surgical population, we identified 434 patients meeting the inclusion criteria for model development/validation (260 in the modeling group and 174 in the external validation group). This represents 21.3% (434/2,040) of all NSCLC surgical patients treated during the accrual window. The institutional DVT incidence among all NSCLC resections was 9.8% (200/2,040), confirming our cohorts intentionally enriched higher-risk cases to ensure adequate statistical power for predictor identification while reflecting real-world complication patterns.

Perioperative management

All enrolled patients followed a unified perioperative management protocol: standardized anesthesia methods, fluid infusion volumes, and pain control regimens. For postoperative lung cancer patients, early mobilization was emphasized; prophylactic anticoagulation was not routinely administered. A stepwise mobilization protocol was implemented: standing at bedside on postoperative day 1, followed by walking ≥3 times daily for >10 minutes per session starting day 2. Nursing staff verified and documented daily compliance until discharge.

Venous ultrasonography

The ultrasound examination protocol for DVT primarily utilizes high-frequency linear array transducers (typically 7–15 MHz) to ensure high-resolution imaging of venous structures. For patients with larger body habitus or when assessing deeper vessels such as the iliac veins, a lower-frequency curvilinear transducer (3–5 MHz) may be employed to improve penetration. Patients are positioned supine with the hip slightly externally rotated and the knee slightly flexed for relaxation of the lower extremity muscles, which facilitates scanning from the groin to the knee region. For evaluation of the popliteal vein and calf veins, the patient may be placed in a prone position or the frog-leg position to optimize visualization. In certain cases, such as when assessing the great saphenous vein or evaluating venous reflux, a standing position might be adopted to enhance venous dilation.

Retrospective data collection

We retrospectively collected data on a predefined set of potential predictor variables for all included patients. These variables encompassed: patient demographics (age, gender); body mass index (BMI); comorbidities defined by objective measures or treatment [diabetes: hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) ≥6.5% or medication; hypertension: blood pressure (BP) >140/90 mmHg or medication; hyperlipidemia: low-density lipoprotein (LDL) >160 mg/dL or medication]; surgical characteristics including operation time (skin incision to closure), surgical approach (thoracotomy vs. video-assisted), intraoperative blood loss (in mL), and time to ambulation after surgery (in hours); cancer-related factors (histologic subtype of NSCLC, pathologic tumor-node-metastasis (TNM)-8 stage, receipt of preoperative chemotherapy within 3 months before surgery); and preoperative laboratory parameters measured within 24 hours prior to surgery [white blood cell count, prothrombin time, fibrinogen, and activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT)]. Personnel extracting predictor data from medical records were blinded to the DVT outcome status of the patients.

Statistical analysis

Missing data, which affected less than 5% of the predictor variables overall, were handled using multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE), generating five imputed datasets. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 21.0 and R 3.6.3 software. Initial univariable screening compared predictors between DVT and non-DVT groups: continuous variables (e.g., prothrombin time, fibrinogen, APTT) normally distributed were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using independent sample t-tests; categorical data (e.g., diabetes, hypertension) were summarized as frequencies (percentages) and compared using χ2 tests.

Predictor selection for the final multivariable model employed least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression to reduce overfitting and identify the most relevant variables, with the tuning parameter λ optimized via 10-fold cross-validation within the development cohort. Significant predictors identified by LASSO were then entered into a multivariable logistic regression model to quantify their independent association with the outcome. This final model was internally validated using bootstrap resampling (1,000 iterations) to estimate and correct for optimism in performance estimates. Using the rms package in R, the regression coefficients from the final model were used to construct a nomogram for predicting postoperative DVT risk. Model discrimination for both the development and validation cohorts was assessed using the AUC with 95% confidence intervals. Calibration was evaluated using the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test and visual calibration curves with calculation of calibration-in-the-large (intercept) and calibration slope. The clinical utility of the nomogram was further assessed using decision curve analysis. Risk groups were subsequently defined based on nomogram-derived predicted probability tertiles: low- (<20%), medium- (20–50%), and high-risk (>50%).

For the external validation, the prediction model derived solely from the development cohort was applied directly to the validation cohort data to calculate individual predicted risks; no model updating (e.g., recalibration) was performed based on the validation cohort results. The validation cohort differed from the development cohort primarily in its later accrual period and consequently a slightly lower observed DVT prevalence (40.2% vs. 46.2%); however, all other aspects, including the study setting (single tertiary center), eligibility criteria, outcome definition and assessment (with blinded ultrasound confirmation), predictor definitions, and measurement methods, were identical. A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant throughout the analyses.

Establishment of a nomogram model for predicting postoperative DVT in patients with NSCLC

A nomogram model for postoperative DVT risk in NSCLC patients was constructed using R software (Figure 1A), assigning scores: age ≥70 years (75 points), diabetes (85 points), lung adenocarcinoma (100 points), and stage III lung cancer (81 points). To illustrate clinical implementation, consider a 70-year-old diabetic patient scheduled for resection of stage III adenocarcinoma. Using this nomogram, age ≥70 years =75 points, diabetes =85 points, adenocarcinoma =100 points, stage III cancer =80 points, total score =340 points; this exceeds the high-risk threshold (>150 points) defined in our risk stratification. Preoperatively, the multidisciplinary team would initiate a personalized thromboprophylaxis protocol.

Validation of the nomogram model for postoperative DVT formation in patients with NSCLC

The calibration curve (Figure 1B) showed close agreement between predicted and actual values. The Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test (χ2=6.016, P=0.54) indicated good fit. The area under the curve (AUC) was 0.900 (95% CI: 0.839–0.960), demonstrating excellent discrimination (Figure 1C).

External validation of the nomogram model for postoperative DVT in NSCLC

External validation used 174 NSCLC patients (70 with DVT, 40.23%). The DVT group’s mean age was 70 years, and proportions with diabetes, lung adenocarcinoma, and stage III lung cancer were significantly higher than in the non-DVT group (P<0.05; Table 4). Validation results showed predicted probabilities closely aligned with actual probabilities on the calibration curve (Figure 1D). The model’s AUC was 0.903 (95% CI: 0.854–0.951), confirming high discriminative power (Figure 1E), validating the nomogram’s effectiveness and accuracy for predicting postoperative DVT risk in NSCLC patients.

Discussion

Key findings

This study developed and validated a clinically deployable nomogram that accurately predicts postoperative DVT risk in NSCLC patients using four routinely assessed variables: advanced age (≥70 years), diabetes mellitus, adenocarcinoma histology, and stage III disease. The model demonstrates robust discrimination (AUC >0.90) and calibration across both derivation and external validation cohorts, providing a pragmatic tool for individualized thromboprophylaxis stratification. By quantifying risk through accessible clinical parameters—vascular endothelial vulnerability and hypercoagulability in elderly patients, hyperglycemia-induced thromboinflammation in diabetics, and adenocarcinoma/stage III-driven prothrombotic states—this nomogram addresses critical gaps in existing risk tools (17,18) while enabling resource-efficient implementation.

Comparison with similar research and explanations of findings

Several predictive models for postoperative DVT in lung cancer patients have been previously reported, yet our nomogram demonstrates distinct advantages in clinical applicability and performance. The Caprini risk assessment model, widely used in surgical populations, stratifies patients into low (0%), moderate (1.7%), and high-risk (10.3%) groups for VTE at a cutoff score of 9, with sensitivity of 83.3%, specificity of 60.5%, and accuracy of 61.6% (19). However, its generalizability to NSCLC populations remains limited. A modified Caprini score combined with D-dimer showed modest predictive value for lower extremity DVT (AUC =0.59; r=0.15; P=0.56) in lung cancer surgery patients (20), suggesting suboptimal discrimination. More recently, Li et al. developed sophisticated models incorporating thromboelastography and immune parameters (21). Their thoracoscopic surgery model achieved an AUC of 0.934 (cutoff probability =0.715), while the thoracotomy model yielded an AUC of 0.918 (cutoff =0.686). Although these represent strong discriminative performance, their reliance on specialized coagulation assays and immune markers limits routine implementation in resource-constrained settings. In contrast, our nomogram—using only four routinely available clinical variables (age, diabetes status, histology, and cancer stage)—achieved comparable or superior predictive accuracy (AUC =0.900 in derivation and AUC =0.903 in validation) without requiring specialized laboratory tests. This pragmatic design ensures immediate clinical deployability, particularly valuable for tertiary centers lacking advanced hemostasis laboratories. For institutions with specialized laboratories, we propose a tiered implementation: our nomogram serves as the foundational risk screen, with high-risk patients (≥150 points) receiving targeted biomarker verification. Future iterations could integrate laboratory parameters where accessible without compromising broad utility. Furthermore, our model’s calibration remained excellent (Hosmer-Lemeshow P=0.54) across probability thresholds, supporting its robustness for individualized risk quantification.

Strengths

The study identified age over 70 years as a significant risk factor for postoperative DVT in NSCLC patients, contributing 75 points. This elevated risk in the elderly likely stems from vascular changes like endothelial damage, vessel wall stiffening, and increased fragility, alongside higher levels of procoagulant substances and blood viscosity. Age-related venous valve issues, blood stasis, and reduced muscle pump efficiency further increase DVT risk (22,23). Diabetes mellitus was a substantial risk contributor, accounting for 85 points. Chronic hyperglycemia damages vascular endothelium through mechanisms including imbalance in vascular tone mediators, free radical production, and structural damage, promoting thrombosis (24). High blood sugar also worsens blood rheology by impairing red blood cell function and increasing platelet activation, adhesion, and overall vascular resistance and viscosity (25). Consequently, managing diabetes effectively is crucial to mitigate DVT risk post-surgery.

The pathological type of NSCLC significantly influenced DVT risk, with adenocarcinoma contributing 100 points in our nomogram. Emerging evidence indicates adenocarcinoma has a distinct thrombogenic propensity compared to squamous cell carcinoma, primarily through three mechanisms. First, adenocarcinoma cells overexpress cancer procoagulant, directly activating coagulation factor X and thrombin generation (26,27). Second, mucin secretion by adenocarcinoma cells (28) interacts with P-selectin and L-selectin on endothelial cells and platelets (29), promoting microthrombi formation. Third, a study has revealed that adenocarcinoma exhibits a greater propensity to upregulate inflammatory cytokines (interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor-alpha), thereby inducing endothelial dysfunction and platelet hyperactivity, though this specific mechanism was not observed in the pulmonary adenocarcinoma subtype (30). Furthermore, advanced tumor stage correlates strongly with higher thrombosis risk, as tumor progression and metastasis further intensify the blood’s hypercoagulability in lung cancer patients (31). Thus, both adenocarcinoma histology and advanced cancer stage increase the likelihood of postoperative DVT development in NSCLC.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that warrant acknowledgment. First, the retrospective design inherently limits causal inference and introduces potential biases in patient selection and data collection. Second, despite multivariate adjustment, the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured variables (e.g., genetic thrombophilia markers, detailed mobility records, or specific chemotherapeutic agents) cannot be fully excluded. Third, the single-center derivation cohort from one tertiary hospital may restrict generalizability to populations with differing demographic or clinical profiles. Furthermore, while external validation was performed, the validation cohort was regionally sourced (multi-institutional within Inner Mongolia), limiting assessment of broader geographic applicability. To address this limitation, we have explicitly planned a prospective multicenter validation cohort study (targeting ≥300 patients) across Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region to further refine the precision of parameter estimates. Additionally, to mitigate this and facilitate broader external validation, we have made the nomogram calculator publicly available online, encouraging its application and testing by other centers. Finally, while cohort enrichment was necessary for model development, future prospective validation should include consecutive surgical patients to confirm performance in routine practice.

Implications and actions needed

This validated nomogram addresses a critical gap in surgical oncology by enabling precision thromboprophylaxis for NSCLC patients. It identifies high-risk subgroups (e.g., elderly diabetics with stage III adenocarcinoma) who may benefit from extended-duration anticoagulation, while sparing low-risk patients unnecessary treatment. The model’s specificity for surgical settings complements chemotherapy-focused tools like the Khorana score, establishing a new standard for perioperative VTE risk assessment in thoracic oncology.

Clinically, institutions should integrate this nomogram into preoperative pathways to guide prophylaxis protocols (e.g., pharmacologic intervention for scores ≥150). Guideline bodies (American Society of Clinical Oncology, National Comprehensive Cancer Network) may consider incorporating NSCLC-specific surgical risk models into recommendations to standardize care. Research priorities include prospective validation across diverse populations, cost-effectiveness analyses of risk-adapted prophylaxis, and exploration of interactions with novel therapies. Concurrently, multidisciplinary education initiatives should train teams on nomogram implementation and high-risk patient management bundles (e.g., early mobilization combined with mechanical prophylaxis).

Conclusions

In conclusion, the nomogram model constructed in this study predicts postoperative DVT risk based on age, diabetes, adenocarcinoma, and stage III lung cancer (AUC >0.90). Designed for resource-limited institutions (Figure 2), it guides stratified anticoagulant prophylaxis through total score thresholds. It outperforms the Caprini model (AUC =0.59) while matching the efficacy of complex models using only routine clinical data.

The nomogram model constructed in this study based on four risk factors, namely age, comorbid diabetes, adenocarcinoma, and stage III lung cancer, has provided guidance for the clinical formulation of targeted individualized prevention and treatment strategies.

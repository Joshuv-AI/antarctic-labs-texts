Abstract
. In this paper, we define new type of convolution and correlation theorems associated with the offset linear canonical transform (OLCT). Additionally, we discuss their applications in multiplicative filter design, which may prove useful in optics and signal processing for signal recovery. Furthermore, we explore the real Paley-Wiener (PW) and Boas theorems for the OLCT, analyzing signal characteristics for OLCT within the L 2 L^{2} domain.

1.
Introduction

The Fourier transform (FT), introduced by Joseph Fourier in the early 19th century, is a widely used technique in signal processing and has been extensively applied across various scientific fields. Several generalizations of the Fourier transform, such as the linear canonical transform (LCT), fractional Fourier transform (FrFT), and offset linear canonical transform (OLCT), provide a greater degree of parameterization compared to the Fourier transform
[

The offset linear canonical transform (OLCT)
[
4
]
, a generalization of the LCT, is a time-shifted and frequency-modulated version of the LCT. It is also known as the special affine Fourier transform or the inhomogeneous canonical transform. The OLCT encompasses several important signal processing operators and is widely used in optical system modeling. Many operations, including the time-shifting, scaling, and frequency modulation of FT, FrFT, Fresnel transform (FRST), offset FrFT, LCT are special cases of the OLCT. As the theories of the FrFT and LCT have advanced, the OLCT has become a vital tool in numerous engineering fields. In certain signal processing applications, where the FT, FrFT, or LCT may not be directly applicable, the OLCT proves to be an essential alternative. Therefore, exploring and further developing the theory of the OLCT is both valuable and important

Considering the developments in the study of the offset linear canonical transform (OLCT), several remarkable contributions have been made by many researchers. Verma et al.

Motivated by the characteristics of convolution, correlation, and other properties of the OLCT, we have introduced a novel approach to convolution and correlation associated with the OLCT. Additionally, we have investigated various spectrum-related principles connected to the OLCT. Furthermore, several applications of the OLCT have been explored.

3.1.
OLCT convolution operation

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f(x),g(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then the convolution for OLCT can be expressed as follows

\displaystyle(f\bigoplus g)(x)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi ib}}~e^{\frac{i}{2b}du_{0}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)g(\frac{x}{2}-\tau)e^{{\frac{-i}{2b}}[a\{(\frac{x}{2}+2\tau)^{2}-6\tau^{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\}+xu_{0}]}d\tau.

(3.1)

Theorem 3.2
.

Let the functions
f(x),g(x)\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then

\displaystyle f\bigoplus g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})~~~~and~~~~||f\bigoplus g||_{1}\leq\sqrt{\frac{4}{2\pi b}}||f||_{1}||g||_{1}.

Proof.

Using the definition
2.3
and Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f\bigoplus g(x)|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg|\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi bi}}~e^{\frac{i}{2b}du_{0}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\tau)g(\frac{x}{2}-\tau)e^{\frac{-i}{2b}[\{(\frac{1}{2}x+2\tau)^{2}-6\tau^{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\}+xu_{0}]}d\tau\bigg|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg|f(\tau)g(\frac{x}{2}-\tau)e^{\frac{-i}{2b}[a\{(\frac{1}{2}x+2\tau)^{2}-6\tau^{2}+\frac{x^{2}}{2}\}+xu_{0}]}\bigg|d\tau dx

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\pi b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|f(\tau)\big|\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg|g(\frac{x}{2}-\tau)\bigg|dx\bigg]d\tau

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{4}{2\pi b}}||f||_{1}||g||_{1}.

This completes the theorem. ∎

.

\displaystyle C=e^{\frac{i}{2}\big[(cdu_{0}^{2}-2adu_{0}\omega_{0}+ab\omega_{0}^{2})\big]}

\displaystyle F_{M}\bigotimes G_{M}(u)

4.
Spectral related theorems

In this Section, we discuss analogous type theorems of real PW and Boas theorem for OLCT. To prove the real PW theorem, we will use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be infinitely differentiable and
\mathcal{O}^{M}f
be its OLCT. Then

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}^{M}\Delta_{x}^{n}f)(u)=(\frac{-i}{b}u)^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u),

(4.1)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}=-\big(\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x}}+\frac{i}{b}(ax+u_{0})\big)

(4.2)

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}^{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}P_{m}(x)D^{n-m}_{x},

(4.3)

where

\displaystyle P_{m}(x)=\binom{n}{x}(\frac{i}{b})^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(ax)^{k}u_{0}^{m-k}.

Here
P_{m}
denotes the
m^{th}
order polynomial with
P_{0}(x)=1
.

Proof.

Let
\Delta_{x}^{*}=\frac{\partial{}}{\partial{x}}-i(ax+u_{0})
.
Then the kernel of OLCT satisfies the following

\displaystyle(\Delta_{x}^{*})\mathcal{K}_{M}f(u,x)=(\frac{-i}{b}u)\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x).

(4.4)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(\Delta_{x}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{O}^{M}(u,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{-i}{b}u)^{n}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x).

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta_{x}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x)f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x)(\Delta_{x})^{n}f(x)dx.

(4.6)

Using (
4.4
),(
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we get

\displaystyle(\mathcal{O}^{M}\Delta_{x}^{n}f)(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x)\Delta_{x}^{n}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(\Delta_{x}^{*})^{n}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x)]f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}[(\frac{-i}{b}u)^{n}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x)]f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{-i}{b}u)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}_{M}(u,x)f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{-i}{b}u)^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u).

Hence we obtain the required result.
∎

4.1.
Real Paley-Weiner Theorem associated with OLCT

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be infinitely differentiable. Then
\big[-{\gamma}{|b|},{\gamma}{|b|}\big]
is the compact support of
(\mathcal{O}^{M}_{f})(u)
if and only if
\Delta_{t}^{n}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
(\frac{u}{b})^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}||\Delta_{t}^{n}f||^{\frac{1}{n}}=\gamma,

(4.7)

where
\gamma=\sup\{|\frac{u}{b}|:\hskip 2.84526ptb\neq 0,\hskip 2.84526pt(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)\neq 0,\hskip 2.84526ptu\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

Proof.

(Necessary Condition)
Suppose that
\big[-{\gamma}{|b|},{\gamma}{|b|}\big]
is the compact support of
(\mathcal{O}^{M}_{f})(u)
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

By Lemma
2.5
and
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle||\Delta_{x}^{n}f||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(\frac{-i}{b}u)^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-{\gamma}{|b|}}^{{\gamma}{|b|}}|(\frac{-i}{b}u)|^{2n}|(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in[{\gamma}{|b|},{\gamma}{|b|}]}|\frac{i}{b}u|^{2n}\int_{{\gamma}{|b|}}^{{\gamma}{|b|}}|(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma^{2n}||\mathcal{O}^{M}f||^{2}_{2},

which leads to

\displaystyle||\Delta_{x}^{n}f||^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\gamma~||\mathcal{O}^{M}f||^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup||\Delta_{x}^{n}f||^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma.

(4.8)

Now, let
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{\gamma}{2})
. Then

\displaystyle||\Delta_{x}^{n}f||^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{{-\gamma}{|b|}}^{{\gamma}{|b|}}|(\frac{i}{b}u)^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{{-(\gamma-2\epsilon)}{|b|}}^{{\gamma-\epsilon}{|b|}}|(\frac{i}{b}u)^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(\omega)|^{2}du

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in[{-(\gamma-2\epsilon)}{|b|},{\gamma-\epsilon}{|b|}]}|\frac{i}{b}u|^{2n}\int_{\frac{-(\gamma-2\epsilon)}{|b|}}^{\frac{\gamma-\epsilon}{|b|}}|(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma-2\epsilon)^{2n}\int_{-{(\gamma-2\epsilon)}{|b|}}^{\gamma-\epsilon{|b|}}|(\mathcal{O}^{M}f)(u)|^{2}du

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\gamma-2\epsilon)^{2n}||\mathcal{O}^{M}f||^{2}_{2}.

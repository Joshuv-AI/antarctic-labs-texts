Enumerating log rational curves on some toric varieties
Carl Lian, Naufil Sakran

Abstract
. The genus 0, fixed-domain log Gromov-Witten invariants of a smooth, projective toric variety X X enumerate maps from a general pointed rational curve to a smooth, projective toric variety passing through the maximal number of general points and with prescribed multiplicities along the toric boundary. We determine these invariants completely for the projective bundle X = ℙ ℙ r ​ ( 𝒪 s ⊕ 𝒪 ⁡ ( − a ) ) X={\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{r}}(\mathcal{O}^{s}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a)) , proving a conjecture of Cela–Iribar López. A different conjecture when X X is the blow-up of ℙ r {\mathbb{P}}^{r} at r r points is disproven. Whereas the conjectures were predicted using tropical methods, we give direct intersection-theoretic calculations on moduli spaces of “naive log quasimaps.”



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Counting curves and log curves

Let
X
be a smooth, projective variety. Gromov-Witten invariants of
X
are intersection numbers against the virtual fundamental class
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\text{vir}}
on the moduli space of stable maps, and virtually enumerate curves
C
on
X

[
Beh97
,
BF97
]
.

Degeneration of the target variety has long played an important role in Gromov-Witten theory
[
Li01
,
Li02
]
. In the simplest situation,
X
degenerates into the union of two smooth, projective varieties
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
meeting along a smooth divisor
D
. One hopes to count curves on
X
in terms of counts of curves of
X_{1},X_{2}
, but must now keep track of the incidences of the curves on
X_{j}
with the divisor
D
. This leads naturally to the theory of logarithmic Gromov-Witten invariants of the log scheme
(X_{j},D)

[
GS13
,
AC14a
,
AC14b
]
.

Calculations of Gromov-Witten invariants where the (pointed) complex structure of the domain curve is fixed also drew much attention early in the subject. The Vafa-Intriligator formula
[
BDW96
,
ST97
,
MO07
]
determines Gromov-Witten invariants of Grassmannians upon restriction over a fixed point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

[
MOP11
]
. After work of Tevelev
[
Tev24
]
, the fixed-domain Gromov-Witten invariants have received renewed interest. The “virtual Tevelev degrees” of
X
are defined by Buch-Pandharipande
[
BP21
]
to be the virtual degrees of the forgetful morphisms

\tau:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times X^{n},

though the term is somewhat ahistorical given the previous work.

A feature of the fixed-domain setting which has motivated much of the more recent work is that, compared to arbitrary Gromov-Witten invariants, the fixed-domain invariants tend to be enumerative much more often
[
LP25
,
BLL+24
,
CD25
]
. Roughly speaking, this means that the virtual invariants compute the
actual
degree of
\tau
, denoted
\mathrm{Tev}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
, upon restricting to the open locus of maps
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
with smooth domain. Concretely,
\mathrm{Tev}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
is the number of maps
f:C\to X
in class
\beta
from a fixed, general curve
C
to
X
satisfying
n
incidence conditions
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
at general points of
C
and
X
. Moreover, even when the enumerativity fails, the geometric (non-virtual) counts
\mathrm{Tev}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
can sometimes be accessed using different techniques
[
CPS22
,
FL23
,
CL23b
,
CD24
,
CL25b
,
Lia24
,
CL23a
,
CL25a
]
.

For toric varieties
X
, Cela–Iribar López
[
CIL24
]
have considered the logarithmic, fixed-domain version of the problem, where the tangency profile of
f
along the toric boundary of
X
is constrained. For domain curves of genus 0, they have proven a tropical correspondence theorem
[
CIL24
, Theorem 5]
, showing that the (geometric or virtual) count of log rational curves on
X
with the maximal number of incidence conditions imposed at general points matches a purely combinatorial enumeration of tropical curves. In fact, a more general tropical correspondence theorem for log Gromov-Witten invariants of toric varieties in any genus and with arbitrary
\psi
-class insertions (descendants) had been obtained earlier by Mandel-Ruddat
[
MR20
]
. The fixed-domain setting may be recovered by inserting appropriate
\psi
-classes.

Using the tropical correspondence theorem, the genus 0, logarithmic, fixed-domain invariants for Hirzebruch surfaces are computed
[
CIL24
, Theorem 7]
, and conjectures for other toric varieties are proposed
[
CIL24
, Conjectures 14-15]
. See also
[
Zho25
,
GRZZ25
]
for further recent work exploiting the connection between log Gromov-Witten theory of projective bundles on toric varieties and tropical curve-counting. The purpose of the present paper is to address the conjectures of Cela–Iribar López, but in the realm of algebro-geometric intersection theory, rather than tropical geometry. Our approach via moduli spaces of “naive log quasimaps” is direct, avoiding the combinatorial analyses typical in tropical enumerations, and leads to explicit formulas. The invariants of interest are reviewed in the next section.

1.2.
Logarithmic Tevelev degrees

Let
X/\mathbb{C}
be a smooth, projective toric variety, and let
D_{\rho}\subset X
be the torus-invariant divisors, indexed by maximal rays
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
in the fan
\Sigma
of
X
. The union of the
D_{\rho}
is referred to throughout as the
boundary
of
X
, and its complement, denoted
X^{\circ}\subset X
, is referred to as the
interior
.

Let
\beta\in H_{2}(X)
be an effective curve class, and let
n\geq 3
be an integer. Assume that
\int_{X}\beta\cdot D_{\rho}\geq 0
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. For each
\rho
, let
\mu_{\rho}=(\mu_{\rho,v})_{v=1}^{m_{\rho}}\in\mathbb{N}^{m_{\rho}}
be a vector of positive integers with sum
\int_{X}\beta\cdot D_{\rho}
. If
\int_{X}\beta\cdot D_{\rho}=0
, then
\mu_{\rho}
is the empty vector and
m_{\rho}=0
. Write
m=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}m_{\rho}
. We consider maps

f:({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\{q_{\rho,v}\}_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\}_{v=1}^{m_{\rho}})\to X

where the domain is viewed as a pointed curve in
\mathcal{M}_{0,n+m}
, so in particular the points
p_{i},q_{\rho,v}
are distinct. Furthermore, we require that

f^{*}D_{\rho}=\sum_{v=1}^{m_{\rho}}\mu_{\rho,v}q_{\rho,v}.

Thus, the points
q_{\rho,v}
are exactly those points mapping to the toric boundary of
X
, with multiplicities
\mu_{\rho,v}
. In particular,
f
maps the
p_{i}
to
X^{\circ}
, and
f_{*}[C]=\beta
. The letter
\Gamma
is used to denote the combinatorial data of the curve class
\beta
, the integer
n
, and the vectors
\mu_{\rho}
specifiying the tangency profile of
f
along the boundary.

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)
be the moduli space of pointed maps
f
as above.

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)
is a smooth, quasi-projective variety of dimension
(m+n-3)+\dim(X)
. The forgetful morphism

\tau:\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)\to\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\times X^{n}

remembers the pointed curve
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})\in\mathcal{M}_{0,n}
and the collection of evaluated points
\{f(p_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
. If

(1)

n=\frac{m}{\dim(X)}+1,

then the source and target of
\tau
have the same dimension.

Definition 1.2.2
.

(cf.
[
CIL24
, Definition 3]
) Assuming (
1
), the (genus 0)
logarithmic
Tevelev degree
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
is defined by
\deg(\tau)
.

For a fixed, general pointed curve
({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n})
and a general choice of points
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\in X^{n}
, the integer
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}\geq 0
enumerates maps
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)
satisfying
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
. Requiring that
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)
constrains
f
to be incident to each
D_{\rho}
with tangency profile
\mu_{\rho}
.

The logarithmic Tevelev degree
\mathrm{Tev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
in
[
CIL24
]
is instead defined by the quotient of
\deg(\tau)
by the combinatorial factor

\prod_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\prod_{u\geq 1}\#\{u:\mu_{\rho,v}=u\}!.

This has the effect of identifying maps which are equal upon permuting marked points
q_{\rho,v}
with the same multiplicities
u=\mu_{\rho,v}
. This is natural in order for
\mathrm{Tev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
to agree with the usual geometric Tevelev degree
\mathrm{Tev}^{X}_{0,n,\beta}
when
\mu_{\rho}=(1)^{m_{\rho}}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. However, this combinatorial factor does not seem to arise naturally in the logarithmic calculations, so we omit it in our definition. We use the notation
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
to contrast with theirs, but keep the term “logarithmic Tevelev degree.”

The moduli space of genus 0, log stable maps
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}(X)
is irreducible of expected dimension
[
Ran17
, Proposition 3.3.5]
, so one can just as well define
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
to be the degree of

\tau:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}(X)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\times X^{n}.

In this way,
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
is given by the integral of a tautological class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}(X)
. In higher genus, the moduli space of log stable maps no longer has expected dimension, so one should instead integrate against the virtual fundamental class. We work in this paper only in genus 0, where the virtual class is the usual fundamental class.

1.3.
New results

In this paper, we consider the genus 0, logarithmic Tevelev degrees of two families of toric varieties, though the techniques should extend in greater generality. The first family we consider is that of the projective bundles

X_{r,s,a}:={\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{O}^{s}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))\to{\mathbb{P}}^{r}.

These are blow-ups of
{\mathbb{P}}^{r+s}
along linear spaces when
a=1
, and Hirzebruch surfaces when
r=s=1
. We completely determine the invariants
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}
.

Theorem 1.3.1
.

Assume that we are in Situation
2.2.1
, so that the logarithmic Tevelev degrees of
X_{r,s,a}
are defined.

If all of the inequalities

\displaystyle m_{j}

\displaystyle\leq n-1\text{ for all }j=1\ldots,r+s+1,

\displaystyle m_{r+2}+\cdots+m_{r+s+1}

\displaystyle\geq(s-1)(n-1),

\displaystyle m_{0}+m_{r+2}+\cdots+m_{r+s+1}

\displaystyle\leq s(n-1)

hold, then

\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}m_{j}!\right)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}\prod_{v=1}^{m_{j}}\mu_{j,v}\right)a^{k_{0}-m_{0}}\binom{k_{0}}{m_{0}},

where

k_{0}=s(n-1)-\sum_{j=r+2}^{r+s+1}m_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{r+1}m_{j}-r(n-1).

When
a=0
, the expression
0^{0}
is interpreted to equal 1.

Otherwise, we have
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}=0
.

Theorem
1.3.1
appears as Theorem
4.2.1
in the main body of the paper. The torus-invariant divisors
D_{j}\subset X_{r,s,a}
are indexed by the integers
j=0,\ldots,r+s+1
, see §
2.1
. The integer
m_{0}
appearing in the binomial coefficient of the main formula corresponds in particular to the divisor
D_{0}
, which in turn is the vanishing locus of the coordinate corresponding to the summand
\mathcal{O}(-a)
in
X_{r,s,a}
. The
s=1
case of Theorem
1.3.1
proves
[
CIL24
, Conjecture 14]
. See §
4.3
for other specializations of Theorem
1.3.1
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3.1
proceeds by an intersection-theoretic calculation on a moduli space
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
of “naive log quasimaps,” in which
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is a dense open subset, see §
2.3
. These moduli spaces are similar to those considered in
[
CL23a
,
CL25a
]
for blow-ups of
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
, but incorporate the data of tangency profiles along the toric boundary.

A subtlety is that, unlike on the moduli space of log stable maps
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
, the natural intersection numbers on
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
are not always enumerative. We prove, however, that in all cases where the enumerativity fails, the logarithmic Tevelev degree is zero. The advantage of the space
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is that it admits a simple description as a tower of projective bundles over a product of projective lines, so the intersection-theoretic calculation is relatively straightforward.

The space of naive log quasimaps is constructed in §
2
. The enumerativity of tautological intersection numbers on
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is considered in §
3
, and the calculation of
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}
is completed in §
4
.

The other
X
we consider is the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
at
r
(torus-fixed) points, which occupies §
5
. While we do not determine the logarithmic Tevelev degrees completely, we show in Theorem
5.2.2
that the prediction of
[
CIL24
, Conjecture 15]
does not always hold, already when
r=2
. We follow a similar program to the case
X=X_{r,s,a}
, constructing a moduli space
Q_{\Gamma}(X)
of naive log quasimaps to
X
. While the value of
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
predicted by
[
CIL24
, Conjecture 15]
equals a tautological intersection number on our moduli space
Q_{\Gamma}(X)
, we find that this intersection number can fail to be enumerative even when
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
cannot be proven to be zero. Using the excess intersection formula, we compute
\mathrm{logTev}^{X}_{\Gamma}
to be both non-zero and different from the value predicted by
[
CIL24
, Conjecture 15]
in one example (and the method extends to many more).

2.
Moduli of log rational curves on
{\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{r}}(\mathcal{O}^{s}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))

2.1.
The target variety

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
a\geq 0,r\geq 1
and
s\geq 1

{\mathbb{P}}^{s}
-bundle over
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}

\pi:X_{r,s,a}={\mathbb{P}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{r}}(\mathcal{O}^{s}\oplus\mathcal{O}(-a))\to{\mathbb{P}}^{r}.

A point
x\in X_{r,s,a}
is given by
(r+s+2)
-tuple
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{r+s+1})
of complex numbers, taken up to equivalence under two actions by
\mathbb{C}^{\times}

\displaystyle(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r+1},y_{r+2},\ldots,y_{r+s+1})

\displaystyle\sim(\lambda^{-a}y_{0},\lambda y_{1},\ldots,\lambda y_{r+1},y_{r+2},\ldots,y_{r+s+1}),

(2)

\displaystyle(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r+1},y_{r+2},\cdots,y_{r+s+1})

\displaystyle\sim(\lambda y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r+1},\lambda y_{r+2},\ldots,\lambda y_{r+s+1})\text{ for all }\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Furthermore, the collections of coordinates
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{r+1}\}
and
\{y_{0},y_{r+2},\ldots,y_{r+s+1}\}
are required not all to vanish. The point
\pi(x)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
is given by
[y_{1}:\cdots:y_{r+1}]
, and the fiber of
\pi
over
[y_{1}:\cdots:y_{r+1}]
is given by the projective space with coordinates

X_{r,s,a}

\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r+s})\cdot(y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r+1},y_{r+2},\ldots,y_{r+s+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(y_{0},t_{1}y_{1},\ldots,t_{r}y_{r},y_{r+1},t_{r+1}y_{r+2},\ldots,t_{r+s}y_{r+s+1}).

In this way, the

. Write

, so that the set

of rays in the fan of

is identified with the set of indices
\{0,\ldots,r+s+1\}
.

2.3.
Naive log quasimaps to

In this section, we construct a compactification

based on the parametrization of the previous section. Define the integers

Let

For each

Definition 2.3.1

Define the tower of projective bundles
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a}):={\mathbb{P}}\left(\bigoplus_{j=r+2}^{r+s+1}\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c)(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))\oplus\left(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c-ab)(-\mathcal{D}_{0}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}(-a)\right)\right)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}|_{D}}

The restrictions of the sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c-ab)(-\mathcal{D}_{0}),\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(b)(-\mathcal{D}_{j}),\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c)(-\mathcal{D}_{j})

Over a point of

, the intermediate projective bundle

. The two projective bundles furthermore parametrize these data precisely up to the scaling factors (
2.2
).

By construction,

is smooth, projective, and irreducible of dimension

. It admits a stratification by open subsets

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\subset\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\subset Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})

which we now define. First, the open subset
Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is the locus where all sections

are not identically zero.

By the parametrization of §
2.2
, we may identify the open subset of
Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
where no two divisors

share a common point in their supports with
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

We define
\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
to be the intermediate open subset of
Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
where the divisors
D_{j}
satisfy the following two “base-point-free” properties.

(BPF1)

The divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r+1}
share no common point
q
in their support.

(BPF2)

The divisors
D_{0},D_{r+2},\ldots,D_{r+s+1}
share no common point
q
in their support.

The sections
g_{j}
underlying a point

gives rise to a map
f:C\to X_{r,s,a}
in class
\beta
in the same way as in §

. However, the image of such an

may meet the strata of
X_{r,s,a}
of codimension at least 2, in which case
f\notin\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
. We say that
f\in Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is

if it lies in
\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

We refer to
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
as the moduli space of
naive log quasimaps
to
X_{r,s,a}
. Ignoring the marked points

, elements

are indeed quasimaps in the sense of Ciocan-Fontanine–Kim
[
CFK10
]
. If
f\in Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is not bpf, then it still defines a
rational
map

. After twisting down base-points, this map extends canonically to an actual map, but no longer in class
\beta
. A version of this twisting is made explicit in §
3.2
.

The adjective “log” refers to the fact that the data of the sections

underlying

are considered relative to the data of underlying divisors, even when the

are identically zero. Our space is “naive” in the sense that we allow the points

to collide arbitrarily, rather than requring the data of a stable, logarithmic curve. The actual moduli space of log quasimaps to more general targets is constructed in
[
Sha24
]
.

We will frequently refer to points of
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
as simply “quasimaps,” but the underlying data of the divisors

is always present, if implicit.

2.4.
Incidence loci

Recall that we are interested in imposing conditions of the form
f(p)=x
on maps
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
. To do intersection theory, we will need to impose analogous conditions more generally on quasimaps.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Let
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
x\in X^{\circ}
be points. Define the
incidence locus

V(p,x)\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
to be the closed subscheme of quasimaps
f
satisfying the following two sets of conditions:

(1)

x_{j}\cdot g_{1}-x_{1}\cdot g_{j}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(b))
vanishes at
p
for
j=2,\ldots,r+1
, and

(2)

x_{j}\cdot g_{1}^{a}g_{0}-x_{1}^{a}x_{0}\cdot g_{j}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(c))
vanishes at
p
for
j=r+2,\ldots,r+s+1
.

The index
1
plays no essential role. Because
x\in X^{\circ}
, the equations (1) and (2) imply more generally that

(1)

x_{j^{\prime}}\cdot g_{j}-x_{j}\cdot g_{j^{\prime}}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(b))
vanishes at
p
for any
j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
, and

(2)

x_{j}\cdot g_{j^{\prime}}^{a}g_{0}-x_{j^{\prime}}^{a}x_{0}\cdot g_{j}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(c))
vanishes at
p
for any
j\in\{r+2,\ldots,r+s+1\}
and
j^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
.

Definition
2.4.1
is given as stated in order for
V(p,x)\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
to be cut out by exactly
r+s=\dim(X_{r,s,a})
equations, and therefore define a cycle class of cohomological degree
r+s
. In particular,
V(p,x)
has codimension at most
r+s
; in fact, one may easily check that
V(p,x)
is pure of codimension
r+s
, though this will not be logically necessary for what follows.

For any
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
x\in X^{\circ}
, the equations (1) of Definition
2.4.1
are the degeneracy loci of maps of line bundles

\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}(-1)\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r+1}\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(b)(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))\to\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(b)|_{p}).

on
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
. The equations (2) are the degeneracy loci of maps of line bundles

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{Q}(-1)

\displaystyle\to\bigoplus_{j=r+2}^{r+s+1}\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c)(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))\oplus\left(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c-ab)(-\mathcal{D}_{0}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}(-a)\right)

\displaystyle\to\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c)|_{p}),

where
\mathcal{O}_{Q}(-1)
denotes the universal sub-line bundle on
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

The restriction of
V(p,x)
to the locus of bpf quasimaps
\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is precisely the (scheme-theoretic) locus of maps where
f(p)=x
. The closure of this locus in
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is precisely
V(p,x)
, but this will also not be logically necessary for us.

One can just as easily relativize the construction:

Definition 2.4.2
.

Define the closed subscheme

\mathcal{V}(p,x)\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times X^{\circ}

to be the locus of
(f,p,x)
satisfying (1) and (2) of Definition

.

Tautologically, the restriction of
\mathcal{V}(p,x)
to any fixed points

recovers the absolute subscheme

.

3.
Transversality

The needed moduli spaces in hand, we now consider the calculation of
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}
. Assume that we are in Situation
2.2.1
. Then, the intersection

V:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}V(p_{i},x_{i})\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})

is of expected dimension 0. The degree of
V
(as a 0-cycle) is therefore a candidate for the value of
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}
. In order to guarantee that
\deg(V)=\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}
(that is, the intersection number
\deg(V)
is enumerative), we want
V
to be reduced of dimension 0, and in addition supported in the locus
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X)
.

However, this does not always hold. What is actually true is the following.

Proposition 3.0.1
.

(i)

(Proposition
3.1.1
)
V\cap\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X)
is reduced of dimension 0.

(ii)

(Proposition
3.1.1
)
V\cap(\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X)-\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X))
is empty.

(iii)

(Proposition
3.2.1
)
V\cap(Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X)-\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X))
is empty.

(iv)

(Proposition
3.3.1
) If the inequalities

•

m_{j}\leq n-1
for
j=1,\ldots,r+s+1
, and

•

m_{r+2}+\cdots+m_{r+s+1}\geq(s-1)(n-1)

hold, then
V\cap(Q_{\Gamma}(X)-Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X))
is empty.

The remainder of this section is devoted to proving statements (i)-(iv) above. We will therefore conclude that
\deg(V)=\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}
under the additional hypotheses of (iv), but not always. The issue will be side-stepped in §
4.1
, where we show that if the hypotheses of (iv) do not hold, then
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}=0
.

3.1.
Transversality for maps

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let
p_{i}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1},x_{i}\in X^{\circ}
be general points, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, and write

V=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}V(p_{i},x_{i})\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})

as above.

(i)

If
n=\frac{m}{r+s}+1
(
1
), then
V\cap\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is reduced of dimension 0. Furthermore, the entirety of the intersection lies in
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

(ii)

If
n>\frac{m}{r+s}+1
, then
V\cap\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
is empty.

Proof.

Consider the intersection

\mathcal{V}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{V}(p_{i},x_{i})\subset Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}

where
\mathcal{V}(p_{i},x_{i})
denotes the pullback of the subscheme
\mathcal{V}(p,x)
(Definition
2.4.2
) from the
i
-th copies of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
X^{\circ}
. Let
\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}
be the restriction of
\mathcal{V}
to
\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
. Consider now the composition

\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}\to\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n},

where the second map is the projection. As we have restricted to the bpf locus,
\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}
is simply the subscheme of
(f,\{p_{i}\},\{x_{i}\})
for which
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore, we may as well consider instead

\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times X_{r,s,a}^{n}\to\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n},

where
\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}}
is defined by the conditions

. In this case, the induced map
\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}}\to\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}
is clearly an isomorphism: the map

and the points

uniquely determine the points
x_{i}=f(p_{i})
.

In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}}
, and hence
\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}
, is smooth of dimension
\dim(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a}))+n
. The intersection
V\cap\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
in question is simply the generic fiber of the composition

\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}\subset\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}\to({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}.

where the last map is projection.

Similarly, the intersection
V\cap\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
in question is simply the generic fiber of the composition

\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}|_{\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})\times({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}\to({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}.

where the last map is projection. If

, then this composition is a map between smooth varieties of the same dimension, so the first part of (i) follows from generic smoothness. To see that

V\cap(\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\text{bpf}}(X_{r,s,a})-\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a}))=\emptyset,

we repeat the argument with
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\text{bpf}}(X_{r,s,a})
replaced by the complement of
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
in
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}^{\text{bpf}}(X_{r,s,a})
, which has strictly smaller dimension. Then, the restriction of
\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}
to the complement of
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
cannot dominate
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}
for dimension reasons, so (i) follows.

If
n>\frac{m}{r+s}+1
, then again
\mathcal{V}^{\text{bpf}}
cannot dominate
({\mathbb{P}}^{1})^{n}\times(X^{\circ})^{n}
for dimension reasons, establishing (ii).
∎

3.2.
Twisting

Proposition 3.2.1
.

Suppose we are in Situation
2.2.1
, and that
f\in Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
lies in
V(p_{i},x_{i})
for all
i
. Then,
f
is bpf. That is,

V=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}V(p_{i},x_{i})

is empty upon restriction to
Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})-\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

The strategy of the proof of Proposition
3.2.1
is as follows. We will use throughout that
f\in Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
by using that an underlying section
g_{j}
vanishes at
p
if and only if
p\in\Supp(D_{j})
. Suppose that
f
is not bpf. Then, we twist down base-points of
f
until it defines a map
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(X_{r,s,a})
for some new tangency data
\Gamma^{\prime}
. The map
f^{\prime}
will retain some incidence constraints, but will have additional constraints on the underlying divisors
D^{\prime}_{j}
, namely that they share some common points in their supports. A contradiction will be obtained by counting dimensions.

We now make explicit the twisting procedure. Consider the following two operations on
f\in Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
:

(T1)

If
q\in\Supp(D_{j})
for all
j=1,\ldots,r+1
((BPF1) fails at
q
), then let

y^{1}_{q}=\min_{j\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}}\ord_{q}(g_{j})>0

be the minimum multiplicity with which
q
appears in all of these
D_{j}
.

Then, replace each
D_{j}
(
j=1,\ldots,r+1
) with
D_{j}-y^{1}_{q}\cdot q
, and replace
D_{0}
with
D_{0}+ay^{1}_{q}\cdot q
. Replace
b
with
b-y^{1}_{q}
.

(T2)

If
q\in\Supp(D_{j})
for
j=0
and all
j=r+2,\ldots,r+s+1
((BPF2) fails at
q
), then let

y^{2}_{q}=\min_{j\in\{0,r+2,\ldots,r+s+1\}}\ord_{q}(g_{j})>0

be the minimum multiplicity with which
q
appears in all of these
D_{j}
.

Then, replace each
D_{j}
(
j=0,r+2,\ldots,r+s+1
) with
D_{j}-y^{2}_{q}\cdot q
, and replace
c
with
c-y^{2}_{q}
.

One should also choose a re-indexing of the points underlying the new divisors
D_{j}
, but we suppress this choice. The sections
g_{j}
never change. For example, in operation (T1), the sections
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r+1}
initially lie in
H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(b)(-D_{j}))
, which is identified with
H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(b-y^{1}_{q})(-(D_{j}-y^{1}_{q}\cdot q)))
after twisting.

We view the operations (T1) and (T2) as being applied “dynamically” on
f
, so
f
and the underlying divisors
D_{j}
are changed, as opposed to new static copies being created. In particular, the tangency data
\Gamma
changes, but
f
always remains in the open locus
Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
. After (T1) is applied at
q
,
f
always satisfies property (BPF1) at
q
, but a failure of (BPF2) at
q
may be introduced. However, operation (T2) always preserves property (BPF1) at
q
, and furthermore property (BPF2) is always satisfied at
q
after the twist. It is moreover clear that both operations (T1) and (T2) at
q
preserve properties (BPF1) and (BPF2) at all other points.

Now, consider the following algorithm:

Algorithm 3.2.2
.

Input the quasimap
f\in Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

(1)

For all points
q\neq p_{i}
where applicable, apply (T1) to
f
.

(2)

For all points
q\neq p_{i}
where applicable, apply (T2) to
f
.

(3)

For all points
p_{i}
where applicable, apply (T1) to
f
.

(4)

For all points
p_{i}
where applicable, apply (T2) to
f
.

Denote by
f^{\prime}
the value of
f
after these steps are complete, and by
\Gamma^{\prime}
the new tangency data. Output
f^{\prime}\in Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(X_{r,s,a})
.

Because the divisors
D_{j}
have finitely many points in their supports, only finitely many operations are applied. By the discussion of the previous paragraph, we have
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(X_{r,s,a})
, that is, Algorithm
3.2.2
removes all base-points of
f
. In fact, we will see that
f^{\prime}
lies in a subvariety of
\mathcal{M}^{\text{bpf}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(X_{r,s,a})
where some of the points underlying the divisors
D_{j}
are constrained to be equal.

We now examine more closely the effect of the steps of Algorithm
3.2.2
on
f
. Specifically, at every step, the twisted
f
lies in a new moduli space of naive log quasimaps

, where the data
\Gamma
is modified, and there are additional “diagonal” conditions (denoted by

) constraining some of the underlying points

to be equal.

Consider first the application of steps 1 and 2 to a point
q\neq p_{i}
.

•

If (T1), but not (T2), is applied at
q
, then write
\alpha^{1}(q)
for the number of
g_{j}
among

for which
g_{j}
vanishes to order exactly
y^{1}_{q}
at
q
. Then, the application of (T1) decreases the value of

underlying
\Gamma
by either
\alpha^{1}(q)
or
\alpha^{1}(q)-1
, depending on whether or not

previously contained
q
in its support. Moreover, the twisted
f
has the property that
(r+2)-\alpha^{1}(q)
of the twisted divisors
D_{0},\ldots,D_{r+1}
contain
q
in its support.

•

If (T2), but not (T1), is applied at
q
, then write
\alpha^{2}(q)
for the number of
g_{j}
among
g_{0},g_{r+2},\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
for which
g_{j}
vanishes to order exactly
y^{2}_{q}
at
q
. Then, the application of (T2) decreases the value of
m
underlying
\Gamma
by
\alpha^{2}(q)
. Moreover, the twisted
f
has the property that
(s+1)-\alpha^{2}(q)
of the twisted divisors
D_{0},D_{r+2},\ldots,D_{r+s+1}
contain
q
in its support.

•

If (T1) and (T2) are both applied, then the analyses above may be combined: the value of

decreases by either
\alpha^{1}(q)+\alpha^{2}(q)
or
\alpha^{1}(q)+\alpha^{2}(q)-1
, and the twisted

has the property that

of the twisted divisors

contain

in its support. Here,

is the number of
g_{j}
among
g_{0},g_{r+2},\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
for which
g_{j}
has the minimal vanishing order at

,
after
the twist (T1) is applied.

In all three cases, the twisted

lies in the subvariety

Crucially, in all three cases, the sum of the amount by which

Moreover, if steps 1 and 2 of Algorithm

have no effect on the incidence conditions imposed at the points

for

after twisting.

We now perform a similar analysis for steps 3 and 4 of Algorithm

.

•

If only (T2) is applied at

\alpha^{2}(p_{i})
for the number of

vanishes to order exactly

underlying

\alpha^{2}(q)

.

3.3.
Vanishing sections

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Suppose we are in Situation
2.2.1
, and that
f\in Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
lies in
V(p_{i},x_{i})
for all
i
. Assume further that:

•

m_{j}\leq n-1
for
j=1,\ldots,r+s+1
, and

•

m_{r+2}+\cdots+m_{r+s+1}\geq(s-1)(n-1)
.

Then,
f\in Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
. That is,

V=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}V(p_{i},x_{i})

is empty upon restriction to
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})-Q^{\neq 0}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

Proof.

Assume for sake of contradiction that some of the sections
g_{j}
underlying
f
are identically zero.

Suppose first that
g_{j}=0
identically for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
. Then, by the equations (1) of Definition
2.4.1
, it must be the case that all of
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r+1}\in H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{1},\mathcal{O}(b))
vanish at all of the points
p_{i}
. However, because
m_{j}\leq n-1
for
j=1,\ldots,r+1
, this is only possible if
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r+1}
are identically zero. This is a contradiction, as
g_{1}\ldots,g_{r+1}
cannot all be identically zero by the construction of
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
.

Next, suppose that
g_{0}=0
identically. Then, by the equations (2) of Definition
2.4.1
, all of
g_{r+2},\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
must vanish at all of the points
p_{i}
. If
m_{j}\leq n-1
for
j=r+2,\ldots,r+s+1
, then
g_{0},g_{r+2},\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
must all be zero, again a contradiction.

Finally, if any of
g_{r+2},\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
is identically zero, then all of them must be. Furthermore, we need
g_{0}g_{j}^{a}
to vanish at all of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
, for
j=1,\ldots,r+1
. Thus, for each
p_{i}
, either
g_{0}
vanishes at
p_{i}
, or all of
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r+1}
vanish at
p_{i}
. Furthermore, we have seen above that none of
g_{0},\ldots,g_{r+1}
may be identically zero.

Without loss of generality, suppose that
g_{0}
vanishes at
p_{1},\ldots,p_{\ell}
, but not
p_{\ell+1},\ldots,p_{n}
. Then, we must have
m_{0}\geq\ell
. Furthermore, the tuple
g=[g_{1}:\cdots:g_{r+1}]
has the property that all
r+1
sections vanish at the remaining points
p_{\ell+1},\ldots,p_{n}
, and satisfies
r
additional vanishing conditions
(x_{j}\cdot g_{1}-x_{1}\cdot g_{j})(p_{i})=0
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
. The tuple of non-zero sections
g
may be viewed as a naive log quasimap to
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
; repeating and suitably modifying the arguments as in Propositions
3.2.1
and
3.1.1
shows that we need

(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r+1})+r\geq r\ell+(r+1)(n-\ell).

The left hand side is the dimension of
\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}({\mathbb{P}}^{r})
, where the tangency data
\Gamma
is obtained by restricting the tangency data in
Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
. The right hand side is the number of vanishing conditions imposed by the points
p_{i}
.

Combining with the fact that
m_{0}\geq\ell
and
m=(n-1)(r+s)
gives

m_{r+2}+\cdots+m_{r+s+1}\leq s(n-1)-n,

which contradicts the stated assumption.
∎

4.1.
Vanishing

Proposition 4.1.1
.

Suppose we are in Situation
2.2.1
, and that there exists at least one map
f\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})
with
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
for all

. Then, the hypotheses of Proposition
3.3.1
hold, that is, we must have:

•

for

, and

•

m_{r+2}+\cdots+m_{r+s+1}\geq(s-1)(n-1)

In particular, if these inequalities do not all hold, then
\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}=0
.

Proof.

Let
g_{0},\cdots,g_{r+s+1}
be the sections underlying

.

We show first that
m_{\ell}\leq n-1
for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
. Suppose first that
r>1
. Then, dropping the section

, the remaining sections
g_{0},\ldots,\widehat{g_{\ell}}\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
define a point

, for the restricted tangency data
\Gamma^{\prime}
. The divisors

underlying the remaining

still have disjoint supports, and the remaining
g_{j}
are still non-zero. Furthermore, we have
f^{\prime}(p_{i})=x^{\prime}_{i}
, where
x^{\prime}_{i}
is the projection
X_{r,s,a}\dashrightarrow X_{r-1,s,a}
defined by forgetting the coordinate indexed by

. Therefore, by Proposition
3.1.1
(ii), we must have

n\leq\frac{m-m_{\ell}}{r+s-1}+1=\frac{(r+s)(n-1)-m_{\ell}}{r+s-1}+1,

which is equivalent to
m_{\ell}\leq n-1
. If instead
r=1
, then for
\ell=1,2
, the
(s+1)
-tuple of sections
[g_{0}g_{\ell}^{a}:g_{3}:\cdots:g_{s+2}]
defines a (log) map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{s}
satisfying
n
incidence conditions at general points. We find similarly, repeating the proof of Proposition
3.1.1
for

, that
m_{\ell}\leq n-1
.

Next, consider
\ell\in\{r+2,\ldots,r+s+1\}
. If
s>1
, then the sections
g_{0},\ldots,\widehat{g_{\ell}}\ldots,g_{r+s+1}
define a point of
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}(X_{r,s-1,a})
satisfying
n
general incidence conditions. Arguing as above gives

once more. If
s=1

Finally, we prove the inequality

defined by

∎

4.2.
Integral computation

It is left to compute the integral

\int_{Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})]

appearing in (
6
). The integrand is a 0-cycle if
n=\frac{m}{r+s}+1
(
1
).

Recall the set-up of §
2.3
. The product

\mathcal{B}:=\left(\prod_{v=1}^{m_{0}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{0,v}\right)\times\cdots\times\left(\prod_{v=1}^{m_{r+s+1}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{r+s+1,v}\right),

parametrizes choices of the divisors
D_{j}
, and
\nu:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times\mathcal{B}\longrightarrow Y
is the projection. The universal divisors are denoted
\mathcal{D}_{j}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times\mathcal{B}
. We have the rank
r+1
vector bundle

\mathcal{E}_{1}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r+1}\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(b)(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))

on
\mathcal{B}
, giving rise to the projective bundle
\pi_{1}:{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\to\mathcal{B}
. We have the rank
s+1
vector bundle

\mathcal{E}_{2}:=\bigoplus_{j=r+2}^{r+s+1}\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c)(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))\oplus\left(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c-ab)(-\mathcal{D}_{0}))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}(-a)\right)

on
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}(-1)
is the universal sub-line bundle, giving rise to the projective bundle
\pi_{2}:Q_{\Gamma}(X_{r,s,a})={\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})\to{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
.

Write
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
for the relative hyperplane classes on
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
and
{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
, respectively. Write
\mathrm{H}_{j,v}
for the hyperplane class on the factor
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{j,v}
of
\mathcal{B}
, and

\mathrm{H}_{j}=\sum_{v=1}^{m_{j}}\mu_{j,v}\mathrm{H}_{j,v}.

A standard calculation shows that

\displaystyle c_{1}(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c-ab))(-\mathcal{D}_{0}))

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{H}_{0},

\displaystyle c_{1}(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(b))(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{H}_{j}\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,r+1,

\displaystyle c_{1}(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c))(-\mathcal{D}_{j}))

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{H}_{j}\text{ for }j=r+2,\ldots,r+s+1.

For any
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and
x\in X^{\circ}
, the degeneracy locus description of
V(p,x)
of §
2.4
gives

\displaystyle[V(p,x)]

\displaystyle=(c_{1}(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(b)|_{p})))-c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}(-1)))^{r}(c_{1}(\nu_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(c)|_{p})))-c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})}(-1)))^{s}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{1}^{r}\zeta_{2}^{s}.

Now,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})]^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})}\pi_{2}^{*}(\zeta_{1}^{rn})\cdot\zeta_{2}^{sn}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}\zeta_{1}^{rn}\cdot(\pi_{2})_{*}(\zeta_{2}^{sn})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}\zeta_{1}^{rn}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{s(n-1)}(\mathcal{E}_{2})

where
\mathcal{S}
denotes Segre class. We have

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{E}_{2})=\frac{1}{c(\mathcal{E}_{2})}=\frac{1}{(1-\mathrm{H}_{0}-a\zeta_{1})(1-\mathrm{H}_{r+2})\cdots(1-\mathrm{H}_{r+s+1})}

so

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})]^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}\zeta_{1}^{rn}\cdot\left(\sum_{k_{0}+k_{r+2}+\cdots k_{r+s+1}=s(n-1)}(\mathrm{H}_{0}+a\zeta_{1})^{k_{0}}\mathrm{H}_{r+2}^{k_{r+2}}\cdots\mathrm{H}_{r+s+1}^{k_{r+s+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{r+2}+\cdots k_{r+s+1}=s(n-1)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}}\left(\binom{k_{0}}{\ell}a^{k_{0}-\ell}\zeta_{1}^{rn+k_{0}-\ell}\mathrm{H}_{0}^{\ell}\right)\mathrm{H}_{r+2}^{k_{r+2}}\cdots\mathrm{H}_{r+s+1}^{k_{r+s+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{r+2}+\cdots k_{r+s+1}=s(n-1)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}}\left(\binom{k_{0}}{\ell}a^{k_{0}-\ell}(\pi_{1})_{*}(\zeta_{1}^{rn+k_{0}-\ell})\mathrm{H}_{0}^{\ell}\right)\mathrm{H}_{r+2}^{k_{r+2}}\cdots\mathrm{H}_{r+s+1}^{k_{r+s+1}}

The
k_{\ell}
are always assumed to be non-negative integers. When
a=0
, we take the convention

0^{k}=\begin{cases}1,&k=0\\
0,&k\neq 0\end{cases}.

Now, we have

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{E}_{1})=\frac{1}{c(\mathcal{E}_{1})}=\frac{1}{(1-\mathrm{H}_{1})\cdots(1-\mathrm{H}_{r+1})},

so replacing
(\pi_{1})_{*}(\zeta_{1}^{rn+k_{0}-\ell})
with
\mathcal{S}_{r(n-1)+k_{0}-\ell}(\mathcal{E}_{1})
gives

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})]^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{r+2}+\cdots k_{r+s+1}=s(n-1)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}}\binom{k_{0}}{\ell}a^{k_{0}-\ell}\mathcal{S}_{r(n-1)+k_{0}-\ell}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\mathrm{H}_{0}^{\ell}\mathrm{H}_{r+2}^{k_{r+2}}\cdots\mathrm{H}_{r+s+1}^{k_{r+s+1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{Y}}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{r+2}+\cdots k_{r+s+1}=s(n-1)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{0}}\binom{k_{0}}{\ell}a^{k_{0}-\ell}\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots k_{r+1}=r(n-1)+k_{0}-\ell}\mathrm{H}_{0}^{\ell}\prod_{j=1}^{r+s+1}\mathrm{H}_{j}^{k_{j}}.

The value of the integral is the coefficient of
\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}\prod_{v=1}^{m_{j}}\mathrm{H}_{j,v}
of the integrand, where we recall that

\mathrm{H}_{j}=\sum_{v=1}^{m_{j}}H_{j,v}.

Thus, in order to obtain a non-trivial contribution, one must take
k_{j}=m_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r+s+1
.

Therefore,

\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}m_{i}=r(n-1)+k_{0}-\ell\implies k_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}m_{i}-r(n-1)+\ell,

and

\displaystyle k_{0}+\sum_{i=r+2}^{r+s+1}m_{j}=s(n-1)

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{j=1}^{r+s+1}m_{j}-r(n-1)+\ell=s(n-1)

\displaystyle\implies\ell=m_{0},

because

. Note that this is only valid if

, because

ranges between

and
k_{0}
in the integrand. Thus, if

, then the integral is zero.

Substituting the above values of

and
\ell
gives now that, if
k_{0}\geq m_{0}
, then

(7)

\int_{{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})]^{n}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}m_{j}!\right)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}\prod_{v=1}^{m_{j}}\mu_{j,v}\right)a^{k_{0}-m_{0}}\binom{k_{0}}{m_{0}},

where

(8)

k_{0}=s(n-1)-\sum_{j=r+2}^{r+s+1}m_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{r+1}m_{j}-r(n-1).

Theorem 4.2.1
.

When defined (Situation
2.2.1

\mathrm{logTev}^{X_{r,s,a}}_{\Gamma}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}m_{j}!\right)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{r+s+1}\prod_{v=1}^{m_{j}}\mu_{j,v}\right)a^{k_{0}-m_{0}}\binom{k_{0}}{m_{0}},

Proof.

We will show that the only tangent vectors lying in
Z_{\bullet}
are those lying along
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{4,1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{5,1}
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
Z_{\bullet}
is the component of
V
where

\displaystyle D_{1}

\displaystyle=p_{5},

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=p_{5},

\displaystyle D_{3}

\displaystyle=p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}+p_{4},

\displaystyle D_{4}

\displaystyle=5q_{4},

\displaystyle D_{5}

\displaystyle=5q_{5}

We write
q_{4},q_{5}
for the variable points instead of
q_{4,1},q_{5,1}
for brevity.

Let
z
be a coordinate on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, and view the points
p_{i},q_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
as complex numbers. The sections
g_{3},g_{4},g_{5}
underlying points of
Z_{\bullet}
are viewed as polynomials of degree 6 vanishing along the required divisors. Then, a tangent vector in
Z_{\bullet}
may be written as

\displaystyle[

\displaystyle(1+\gamma_{3}\epsilon)(z-p_{5}-\gamma_{11}\epsilon)(z-p_{5}-\gamma_{21}\epsilon)(z-p_{1}-\gamma_{31}\epsilon)(z-p_{2}-\gamma_{32}\epsilon)(z-p_{3}-\gamma_{33}\epsilon)(z-p_{4}-\gamma_{34}\epsilon):

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}\epsilon(z-p_{5}-\gamma_{11}\epsilon)(z-q_{4}-\gamma_{41}\epsilon)^{5}:

\displaystyle\gamma_{5}\epsilon(z-p_{5}-\gamma_{11}\epsilon)(z-q_{5}-\gamma_{51}\epsilon)^{5}],

where the coordinates are first-order deformations of
g_{3}
and
g_{4},g_{5}=0
. The
\gamma_{jv}\epsilon
are deformations of the points
q_{j,v}
, and
\gamma_{3}\epsilon,\gamma_{4}\epsilon,\gamma_{5}\epsilon
are deformations in the directions of the fibers of
\pi:Q_{\Gamma}(X)\to\mathcal{B}
. This triple of polynomials must satisfy the equations defining
V(p_{i},x_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,5
, viewed in
\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
.

Projecting to the last two coordinates, using the fact that the points
p_{i}
are distinct, and looking only at the first order terms, the pair of sections

[\gamma_{4}(z-q_{4})^{5}:\gamma_{5}(z-q_{5})^{5}]

must satisfy four general incidence conditions upon evaluating at
z=p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}
. An easy incidence correspondence argument (also taking into account the possibility
p_{i}=q_{j}
) shows that this is impossible unless
\gamma_{4}=\gamma_{5}=0
. Comparing now to the deformation of
g_{3}
shows that all of the
\gamma_{jv}
appearing in
g_{3}
must be zero. Our tangent vector is now simply

[(1+\gamma_{3}\epsilon)g_{3}:0:0],

where the points
q_{4},q_{5}
are free to vary along
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{4,1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{5,1}
(corresponding to the parameters
\gamma_{41},\gamma_{51}
). Up to scaling, the parameter
\gamma_{3}
can be ignored. Thus, our tangent vector lies in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{4,1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{5,1}
, completing the proof.

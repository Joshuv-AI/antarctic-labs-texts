Spin-triplet paired Wigner crystal stabilized by quantum geometry Preprint: APS/123-QED
Dmitry Zverevich, Alex Levchenko, Ilya Esterlis

Abstract
We have used variational states to analyze the effects of band geometry on the two-dimensional Wigner crystal with one and two electrons per unit cell. At sufficiently low electron densities, we find that increasing Berry curvature drives a transition into a crystalline state composed of spin-triplet pairs carrying relative orbital angular momentum m = − 1 m=-1 . The essential features of this transition are captured by an effective two-electron quantum dot problem in the presence of Berry curvature. Our results point to a purely electronic, strong-coupling mechanism for local spin-triplet pairing in correlated two-dimensional electron systems with quantum geometry.



At sufficiently low densities, a clean electron gas crystallizes into a Wigner crystal (WC) due to the dominance of Coulomb interaction energy over kinetic energy
Wigner 1934
. In the extreme dilute limit, semiclassical considerations predict that the electrons form a Bravais lattice with one electron in each unit cell. At higher densities, however, quantum corrections become important, and some years ago Moulopoulos and Ashcroft suggested that quantum effects may stabilize
non-Bravais
lattices of electrons, with
electron pairs
residing at the lattice sites
Moulopoulos and Ashcroft 1992
. The paired WC becomes energetically favored over the ordinary WC due to a reduction of zero-point energy that can overcome the relatively small Coulomb energy cost of pairing electrons within a unit cell. Subsequent work confirmed a region of stability for the paired WC
Moulopoulos and Ashcroft 1993
;
Taut 1994
;
Taut 2001
, albeit in a density range where the ground state is expected to be a homogeneous liquid
Tanatar and Ceperley 1989
;
Attaccalite et al. 2002
;
Drummond and Needs 2009
;
Azadi et al. 2024
;
Smith et al. 2024

Starting from an effective one-band model for a valley-polarized, spin-degenerate two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG), we show that quantum geometry stabilizes the paired crystal down to lower densities and that sufficiently strong Berry curvature drives a transition into a crystalline state of spin-triplet pairs with orbital angular momentum
m=-1

Wagner et al. 1992
;
Ashoori et al. 1993
. These effects are quantum mechanical in origin, arising from the momentum-space Berry flux acquired by the electrons through zero-point motion. We thus demonstrate a mechanism by which the combination of strong correlations and quantum geometry promotes local, spin-triplet pairing of electrons. We note recent work showing that, in Bernal bilayer graphene, sufficient Berry curvature can drive a transition to a
monatomic
WC state in which electrons acquire a spontaneous orbital angular momentum
Joy et al. 2025

To model the effects of Berry curvature and quantum geometry in the valley-polarized 2DEG, we adopt the “parent Berry curvature” model of Ref.

(1)

The essential features of the quantum dot problem can already be understood from a perturbative treatment of the Berry curvature at large
r_{s}
. When
\Omega_{r}=0

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{\hbar^{2}(m^{2}-1/4)}{2\mu r^{2}}+V_{\text{QD}}(r)\right]u(r)=\epsilon_{nm}u(r),

(13)

where
u(r)=\sqrt{r}\phi(r)

V_{\text{QD}}(r)=Kr^{2}/2+e^{2}/r

between the electrons forming the pair is determined by minimizing
V_{\text{QD}}^{\prime}(r_{0})=0

r_{0}=\left(e^{2}/K\right)^{1/3}\sim r_{s}a_{B}
.

At large
r_{s}

is much larger than the fluctuations in the distance between electrons in the pair, we may expand about the minimum of the potential
V_{\text{QD}}(r)\approx V_{\text{QD}}(r_{0})+\frac{1}{2}V_{\text{QD}}^{\prime\prime}(r_{0})(r-r_{0})^{2}

\epsilon_{nm}=V_{\text{QD}}(r_{0})+\hbar\omega_{\text{QD}}(n+1/2)+\hbar^{2}m^{2}/2\mu r_{0}^{2}

\omega_{\text{QD}}=\sqrt{3K/\mu}

We now expand (
11

\Delta H=-\frac{\Omega_{r}}{2r}\frac{dV}{dr}m+\frac{\Omega_{r}}{4}\nabla^{2}V.

(14)

\Delta\epsilon_{0m}=\epsilon_{g}+\frac{3m}{4}\frac{e^{2}}{r_{0}^{5}}\xi^{2}\Omega_{r},~\epsilon_{g}=\frac{3}{4}\frac{e^{2}}{r_{0}^{3}}\left(1+\frac{\xi^{2}}{2r_{0}^{2}}\right)\Omega_{r}.

In Fig.

m=-1

Qualitatively, this nodal structure suggests that the energetic favoring of the
m=-1

Finally, we comment on the expected effects of an applied magnetic field. The Zeeman effect favors the spin-triplet,
m=-1
paired WC over the spin-singlet,
m=0
state. Accordingly, an in-plane magnetic field, which couples primarily to the electron spin, will expand the region of the phase diagram occupied by the
m=-1
paired WC. The orbital effect of a perpendicular magnetic field, on the other hand, is pair breaking
Taut 2001
. A sufficiently strong out-of-plane field will drive a transition from the paired WC back to the monatomic WC, potentially proceeding via additional singlet-triplet transitions
Taut 2001
.

The variational calculations presented here can be quantitatively improved in several ways. The states (
3
) and (
7
) correspond to an “Einstein model” of the electron solid. For the monatomic WC, systematic semiclassical calculations that incorporate the full phonon spectrum
Bonsall and Maradudin 1977
provide a more quantitatively reliable estimate of the energy
Drummond and Needs 2009
. Such calculations are also possible for paired WCs
Taut 2001
and may yield more accurate energies than those reported here. Recently developed neural-network variational Monte Carlo methods for studying electron gases with quantum geometry
Valenti et al. 2025a
offer another route to search for paired WC phases. More detailed calculations that account for realistic features of actual devices, such as gate screening
Valenti et al. 2025b
and sample disorder
Xiang et al. 2025
;
Joy and Skinner 2025
, will be important for assessing the relevance of our results to real materials. It would also be interesting to explore connections between our findings and recently discovered Wigner molecular crystals in moiré materials
Li et al. 2024
.

It is tempting to speculate that the local spin-triplet pairing tendency identified here may survive the partial or complete melting of the electron crystal. Recent calculations have hinted at the possibility of intermediate metallic density-wave states between the (monatomic) WC and the homogeneous liquid, driven by a self-doping instability of the crystal
Kim et al. 2024
. In the present context, the analogous state—in which the paired crystal spontaneously generates a finite concentration of (paired) ground-state defects—would correspond to a charged, bosonic supersolid, in which superfluidity coexists with translational symmetry breaking. On the other hand, a complete loss of translational order could lead to a spin-triplet superconducting state driven by strong correlations and quantum geometry, a purely electronic mechanism of superconductivity that is complementary to the more conventional Kohn–Luttinger mechanism
Kohn and Luttinger 1965
;
Chubukov 1993
;
Geier et al. 2025
;
Shavit and Alicea 2025
;
Jahin and Lin 2025
;
Chou et al. 2025
;
Yang and Zhang 2025
;
Qin and Wu 2025
;
Parra-Martínez et al. 2025
;
May-Mann et al. 2025
. Exploring possible connections between this scenario and recent observations of chiral superconductivity in rhombohedral graphene
Han et al. 2025
is an intriguing direction for future study.

operators introduced in the main text, as well as more detailed calculations of the variational energies.

I.1
Variational states and operator identities

For reference, we record here the Hamiltonian given in Eq. (1) of the main text:

H=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m^{*}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}+\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\\
\sigma\sigma^{\prime}\end{subarray}}F_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}F_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}\sigma^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},

(16)

where
v_{\mathbf{q}}=2\pi e^{2}/q
is the Fourier transform of the Coulomb interaction,
A
is the area of the system, and the form factors are

F_{\mathbf{k},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}=e^{-i\frac{\Omega}{2}\mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{4}|\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}|^{2}}.

(17)

For the ordinary, monatomic
N
-electron WC we use the variational state

|\Psi\rangle=Cd^{\dagger}_{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbf{R}_{1})\ldots d^{\dagger}_{\sigma_{N}}(\mathbf{R}_{N})|0\rangle,\quad d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}_{n})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},

(18)

where
\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})
is the Fourier transform of the localized orbital
\Phi(\mathbf{r})=\exp(-r^{2}/4\sigma^{2})/\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}
. This type of state was used to describe monatomic WCs in Refs.
van Dijk and Vertogen 1991
;
Moulopoulos and Ashcroft 1993
;
Bonev and Ashcroft 2008
.
Operators
d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}_{n})
have the interpretation of creating an electron at WC lattice site
\mathbf{R}_{n}
with spin
\sigma_{n}
. The anticommutation relations are

\{d_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}),d^{\dagger}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\}=\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}S(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime}),\quad\{d_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}),d_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\}=\{d^{\dagger}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{R}),d^{\dagger}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\}=0.

(19)

Function
S
measures overlaps between localized orbitals:

S(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}~\Phi^{*}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R})\Phi(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})=e^{-(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})^{2}/8\sigma^{2}}.

(20)

Throughout we will neglect this exponentially small wave function overlaps between different sites and make the approximation
S(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})\approx\delta_{\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}}
, so that the
d^{\dagger}
operators obey standard fermion anticommutation relations. In this approximation, the normalization constant
C\approx 1
. Higher-order terms in a systematic expansion in powers of
S
can be found in Refs.
Moulopoulos and Ashcroft 1993
;
van Dijk and Vertogen 1991
;
Bonev and Ashcroft 2008
. Below we will also make use of the anticommutation relation

\{c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},d^{\dagger}_{\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathbf{R}_{n})\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}).

(21)

For the paired WC we use the state

|\Psi_{p}\rangle=C_{p}b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{1})b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{2})\ldots b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{N_{p}})|0\rangle,\quad b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{n})=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{q}\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{q})\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{k})\chi_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}/2+\mathbf{k},\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{q}/2-\mathbf{k},\sigma^{\prime}},

(22)

where
N_{p}=N/2
is the number of pairs.
The spatial part of the pair wave function localized at site
\mathbf{R}_{n}
is

\psi_{n}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})=\Phi\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}{2}-\mathbf{R}_{n}\right)\varphi(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}),

(23)

where
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}
are the coordinates of the two electrons comprising the pair. Functions
\Phi
and
\varphi
are the center-of-mass (COM) and relative wave functions, respectively. The spin part of the wave function is
\chi_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
. For singlet pairing
\chi_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=(\delta_{\sigma\uparrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime}\downarrow}-\delta_{\sigma\downarrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime}\uparrow})/\sqrt{2}
, while for triplet pairing the three possible spin states are
\chi^{(+1)}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\delta_{\sigma\uparrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime}\uparrow}
,
\chi^{(0)}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=(\delta_{\sigma\uparrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime}\downarrow}+\delta_{\sigma\downarrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime}\uparrow})/\sqrt{2}
, and
\chi^{(-1)}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}=\delta_{\sigma\downarrow}\delta_{\sigma^{\prime}\downarrow}
. The state (
22
) was used earlier in Ref.
Moulopoulos and Ashcroft 1993
to describe spin-singlet paired WCs in the three-dimensional electron gas.

Below will make use of the following identities involving the
b^{\dagger}
operators:

\displaystyle[c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}},b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{n})]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{A}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}}e^{-i(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime})\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\right)\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}},

(24)

\displaystyle[b(\mathbf{R}_{n}),[c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}},b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{n})]]|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{A^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}}e^{i(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\right)\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}}{2}\right)\chi_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime\prime}}\chi_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime\prime}}|0\rangle,

(25)

as well as the approximation relations

\displaystyle[b(\mathbf{R}_{n}),b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{m})]

\displaystyle\approx 0\quad(m\neq n),

(26)

\displaystyle b(\mathbf{R}_{n})b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{n})|0\rangle

\displaystyle\approx 2|0\rangle,

(27)

which are valid in the limit of vanishing intercell overlaps. In this approximation, the constant
C_{p}\approx 2^{-N_{p}/2}
.

In the following subsections, we compute expectation values of the Hamiltonian (
16
) using the states (
18
) and (
22
). As described above, we neglect the exponentially small overlaps of wave functions in different unit cells, which gives corrections of the order
\sim\exp(-\alpha a_{\text{WC}}^{2}/\sigma^{2})
, where
\sigma
is the width of the localized orbital,
a_{\text{WC}}
is the WC lattice constant , and
\alpha=\mathcal{O}(1)
. In the next section of this supplement, we demonstrate that this approximation is equivalent to the “Hartree approximation” in a first-quantized, wave function language. At various stages of the calculations below, we will make use of the usual passage from discrete to continuous momenta
A^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\to\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}/(2\pi)^{2}
.

I.2
Variational energy of the monatomic WC

We start with the monatomic WC and adopt the shorthand notation
d^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{n})\to d^{\dagger}_{n}
. In the approximation of neglected intercell overlaps, the energy of the monatomic WC is independent of the spin configuration and we will therefore suppress the spin labels in what follows.
The expectation value of the kinetic energy is
\langle\Psi|T|\Psi\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\langle\Psi|c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}|\Psi\rangle
. Expanding the expectation value

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0|d_{N}\ldots d_{1}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}c_{\mathbf{k}}d^{\dagger}_{1}\ldots d^{\dagger}_{N}|0\rangle

(28)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i+1}\{c_{\mathbf{k}},d^{\dagger}_{i}\}\langle 0|\prod_{m}d_{m}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\prod_{n\neq i}d^{\dagger}_{n}|0\rangle

(29)

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij}(-1)^{i+j}\{c_{\mathbf{k}},d^{\dagger}_{i}\}\{d_{j},c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\}\langle 0|\prod_{m\neq j}d_{m}\prod_{n\neq i}d^{\dagger}_{n}|0\rangle

(30)

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{i}\{c_{\mathbf{k}},d^{\dagger}_{i}\}\{d_{i},c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\}

(31)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{A}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})

(32)

The second to last line is obtained using the approximation of neglected overlaps (
26
). We thus find

\langle\Psi|T|\Psi\rangle=N\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})=N\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m^{*}\sigma^{2}}.

(33)

The potential energy is obtained similarly. We have

\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}F_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}F_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Psi|c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}}|\Psi\rangle.

(34)

Neglecting intercell overlaps, one obtains

\langle\Psi|c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}}|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{A^{2}}\sum_{i\neq j}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{R}_{j})}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}).

(35)

The manipulations required to arrive at this result are similar to those made in calculating the kinetic energy, with additional oscillatory terms of the form

\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~F_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})}\sim e^{-(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime})^{2}/8\sigma^{2}},

(36)

which are again exponentially small in proportion to the overlap and are thus dropped. We obtain

\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{i\neq j}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{R}_{i}-\mathbf{R}_{j})}\left|\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~F_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}.

(37)

Defining the electron density
n(\mathbf{q})=\langle\Psi|\sum_{\mathbf{k}}F_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}c_{\mathbf{k}}|\Psi\rangle
, we have, in the approximation of vanishing overlaps,

n(\mathbf{q})=\sum_{j}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{j}}n_{0}(\mathbf{q}),\quad n_{0}(\mathbf{q})\equiv\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~F_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k})=e^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{\Omega}{4\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}q^{2}}.

(38)

The potential energy term can then be written

\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\rangle=\frac{N}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}|n_{0}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}.

(39)

We now convert the sum on
\mathbf{q}
to an integral, keeping in mind that we must also then add in the interaction with the neutralizing background. Evaluating the integral (and keeping the background term implicit) gives

\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\rangle=\frac{N}{2}e^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\tilde{\sigma}}e^{-R_{n}^{2}/8\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}I_{0}\left(\frac{R_{n}^{2}}{8\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}\right),

(40)

where
\tilde{\sigma}^{2}=\sigma^{2}(1+\Omega/4\sigma^{2})^{2}
and
I_{n}(x)
is the modified Bessel function.

For large
r_{s}
, the the localized orbitals will be sharply peaked around the lattice sites,
\sigma\ll a_{\text{WC}}
, and we may expand

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\tilde{\sigma}}e^{-R_{n}^{2}/8\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}I_{0}\left(\frac{R_{n}^{2}}{8\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{R_{n}}+\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}{R_{n}^{3}}+\ldots.

(41)

To the lowest order, we thus arrive at the result

\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\rangle=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\frac{e^{2}\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}{R_{n}^{3}}+E_{\text{cl}},\quad E_{\text{cl}}=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\frac{e^{2}}{R_{n}}.

(42)

Here
E_{\text{cl}}
is the classical Coulomb energy of the crystal, which implicitly includes the interaction with the neutralizing background
Bonsall and Maradudin 1977
. The dipolar sum may be evaluated numerically:
\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}1/R_{n}^{3}=\gamma/a_{\text{WC}}^{3}
, where
\gamma\approx 11.03
.

Combining the potential energy with the kinetic energy (
33
) we arrive at Eq. (5) of the main text for the energy per electron of the monatomic WC. We note that this result for the energy of the WC in a band with quantum geometry has also been obtained in
Tan et al. 2025b
.

I.3
Variational energy of the paired WC

We now turn to the paired WC and use the same shorthand as above
b^{\dagger}(\mathbf{R}_{n})\to b^{\dagger}_{n}
. The expectation value of the kinetic energy is

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{p}|T|\Psi_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N_{p}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{1}(c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma})b^{\dagger}_{1}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle

(43)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N_{p}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{2}(b_{1}b^{\dagger}_{1})(c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma})b^{\dagger}_{2}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle+\frac{1}{2^{N_{p}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{2}(b_{1}[c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},b^{\dagger}_{1}])b^{\dagger}_{2}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle

(44)

Next, we write
b_{1}[c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},b^{\dagger}_{1}]=[b_{1},[c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},b^{\dagger}_{1}]]+[c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma},b^{\dagger}_{1}]b_{1}
and use the (approximate) bosonic commutation relations (
26
) to move operator
b_{1}
in the second term all the way to the right to annihilate the vacuum. Utilizing the nested commutator (
25
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{p}|T|\Psi_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{N_{p}}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{2}(b_{1}b^{\dagger}_{1})(c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma})b^{\dagger}_{2}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4}{A^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}|\Phi(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\left|\varphi\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}\frac{1}{2^{N_{p}}}\underbrace{\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{2}b^{\dagger}_{2}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle}_{\approx 2^{N_{p}-1}}.

(45)

Continuing to commute the operator
c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}
to the right gives
N_{p}
similar terms, resulting in

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{p}|T|\Psi_{p}\rangle

\displaystyle=2N_{p}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\epsilon_{\mathbf{k}}|\Phi(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\left|\varphi\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{2}\right)\right|^{2}

(46)

\displaystyle=N_{p}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\frac{\hbar^{2}\mathbf{q}^{2}}{2M}|\Phi(\mathbf{q})|^{2}+N_{p}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\frac{\hbar^{2}\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2\mu}|\varphi(\mathbf{k})|^{2},

(47)

where
M=2m^{*}
and
\mu=m^{*}/2
. The kinetic energy thus factorizes into a sum of COM and relative contributions. Using the Gaussian form of the COM wave function, we have explicitly
T_{\text{COM}}=N_{p}\hbar^{2}/4M\sigma_{p}^{2}
.

The calculation of the potential energy is more involved, but the basic manipulations are very similar to those made in calculating the kinetic energy. Here we just highlight the main steps. The expectation value is

\langle\Psi_{p}|V|\Psi_{p}\rangle=\frac{1}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}F_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}F_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{2^{N_{p}}}\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{1}\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}b^{\dagger}_{1}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle,

(48)

where
\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}\equiv c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}\sigma^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}
. As with the kinetic energy, we express this in terms of nested commutators:

\displaystyle\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{1}\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}b^{\dagger}_{1}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{p}}\langle 0|\left(\prod_{i<n}b_{i}b_{i}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\prod_{i>n}b_{i}\right)\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime},n}\left(\prod_{i>n}b^{\dagger}_{i}\right)|0\rangle,\quad\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime},n}\equiv[b_{n},\mathcal{[}\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}},b^{\dagger}_{n}]].

(49)

This result is obtained using the approximate relation (
26
). The nested commutator
\mathcal{C}
has the schematic structure

\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime},n}=A_{n}+\sum_{\alpha\beta}B_{n}^{\alpha\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\alpha}c_{\beta}+\sum_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}D_{n}^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}c^{\dagger}_{\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\beta}c_{\gamma}c_{\delta}.

(50)

Here
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
, and
D_{n}
are
c
-numbers and their dependence on
\mathbf{k}
,
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
, and
\mathbf{q}
has been left implicit. One can show that the four-fermion
D_{n}
term gives contributions that vanish in the approximation of no intercell overlap. Direct calculation shows that the constant term
A_{n}
is given by

A_{n}=\frac{4}{A^{2}}|\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{k}^{\prime})|^{2}\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{2}+\mathbf{q}\right)\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{2}\right)

(51)

and is independent of the site
\mathbf{R}_{n}
. There will be one such term for each pair, yielding the contribution

\langle\Psi_{p}|V|\Psi_{p}\rangle\ni V_{\text{intra}}=N_{p}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\Omega q^{2}/2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\tilde{\varphi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q})\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{k})e^{-i\Omega\mathbf{q}\times\mathbf{k}}.

(52)

These terms corresponds to the
intracell
Coulomb interaction energy of the pair.

When the bilinear term in Eq. (
50
) is inserted into (
49
), the result can, in the approximation of vanishing intercell overlaps, again be written as a sum of expectation values of the nested commutators

C^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime},nm}\equiv[b_{m},[\sum_{\alpha\beta}B_{n}^{\alpha\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\alpha}c_{\beta},b_{m}^{\dagger}]].

(53)

The contribution to the expectation value from the bilinear terms is then

\displaystyle\langle 0|b_{N_{p}}\ldots b_{1}\mathcal{O}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}b^{\dagger}_{1}\ldots b^{\dagger}_{N_{p}}|0\rangle_{\text{bilinear}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n>m}\langle 0|\left(\prod_{i<n}b_{i}b_{i}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\prod_{n<i<m}b_{i}b_{i}^{\dagger}\right)\left(\prod_{i>m}b_{i}\right)\mathcal{C}^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime},nm}\left(\prod_{i>m}b^{\dagger}_{i}\right)|0\rangle

(54)

\displaystyle\approx 2^{N_{p}-2}\sum_{n>m}\langle 0|C^{\mathbf{q}}_{\mathbf{k}\mathbf{k}^{\prime},nm}|0\rangle,

(55)

the last line being valid in the no-overlap approximation.
Furthermore, direct calculation shows that only the following two types of bilinear terms survive in the no-overlap approximation:

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha\beta}B_{n}^{\alpha\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\alpha}c_{\beta}

\displaystyle\to\frac{4}{A^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}+\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}-2\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}}{2}\right)\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}-2\mathbf{k}}{2}\right)c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}-\mathbf{q}\sigma^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime}\sigma^{\prime}}

(56)

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{A^{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}-\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varphi}^{*}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}-2\mathbf{k}^{\prime}+\mathbf{q}}{2}\right)\tilde{\varphi}\left(\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\prime\prime}-2\mathbf{k}^{\prime}}{2}\right)c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}.

Here spinor sums
\sum_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}\chi^{2}_{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}=1
have been evaluated.

Putting everything together, we arrive, after some algebraic manipulation, at the following contribution from the bilinear terms:

\langle\Psi_{p}|V|\Psi_{p}\rangle\ni V^{0}_{\text{inter}}=\frac{N_{p}}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}~4v_{\mathbf{q}}|\rho_{0}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}|\varrho_{0}(\mathbf{q}/2)|^{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}.

(57)

We have defined, respectively, the COM and relative densities as

\rho_{0}(\mathbf{q})=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~F^{(\Omega/2)}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\Phi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{k}),\quad\varrho_{0}(\mathbf{q})=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~F^{(2\Omega)}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}\tilde{\varphi}^{*}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q})\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{k}),

(58)

where the superscipt on the form factor
F
indicates the replacements
\Omega\to\Omega/2
and
\Omega\to 2\Omega
.

The product of densities appearing in the sum (
57
) is simply related to the Fourier component of the
total
density,
n(\mathbf{q})=\langle\Psi_{p}|\sum_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}F_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{q}\sigma}c_{\mathbf{k}\sigma}|\Psi_{p}\rangle
: in the limit of vanishing intercell overlaps, we have

n(\mathbf{q})=\sum_{n}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}n_{0}(\mathbf{q}),\quad n_{0}(\mathbf{q})=2\rho_{0}(\mathbf{q})\varrho_{0}(\mathbf{q}/2).

(59)

The intercell potential energy term (
57
) is thus

V^{0}_{\text{inter}}=\frac{N_{p}}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}|n_{0}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}.

(60)

In the final step, we make a small-
\mathbf{q}
expansion of
\varrho_{0}(\mathbf{q}/2)
, which amounts to a multipole expansion of the charge distribution of the pair. After some algebra, we obtain

\varrho_{0}(\mathbf{q}/2)=1-\frac{q^{2}}{16}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\varphi^{*}(\mathbf{k})\left[\left(i\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}+2\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{k}}\right)^{2}+2\Omega\right]\varphi(\mathbf{k})+\ldots

(61)

Plugging this into (
57
), we find

V^{0}_{\text{inter}}=\frac{N_{p}}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}~v^{(2e)}_{\mathbf{q}}|\rho_{0}(\mathbf{q})|^{2}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}+N_{p}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~\varphi^{*}(\mathbf{k})\left[\frac{K}{2}\left(i\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}+2\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{k}}\right)^{2}+K\Omega\right]\varphi(\mathbf{k})

(62)

Here
v^{(2e)}\equiv 2\pi(2e)^{2}/q
and the Berry connection is
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{k}}={\bm{\Omega}}\times\mathbf{k}/2
.
In the first term, the internal structure of the pair drops out and we obtain the intercell Coulomb interaction between composite objects (the pairs) of charge
2e
; the result is the same as that obtained for the ordinary WC (
42
) with the substitutions
N\to N_{p}
,
a_{\text{WC}}\to a^{\prime}_{\text{WC}}=\sqrt{2}a_{\text{WC}}
,
e\to 2e
, and
\Omega\to\Omega/2
.
The second term is an
intracell
harmonic confining potential that arises as a result of
intercell
interactions between the pair and the surrounding electrons. The stiffness
K
of the harmonic potential is

K=-\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\frac{e^{2}}{2R^{3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}x~x^{2}e^{-\frac{\sigma_{p}^{2}}{R_{n}^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\Omega}{8\sigma_{p}^{2}}\right)^{2}x^{2}}J_{0}(x)\approx\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\frac{e^{2}}{2R_{n}^{3}}=\frac{\gamma e^{2}}{2a^{\prime 3}_{\text{WC}}},

(63)

where the last approximation is for the localized limit
\sigma_{p}\ll a_{\text{WC}}
.

The COM contribution to the kinetic energy in Eq. (
47
), together with the intercell interaction energy in Eq. (
62
), gives the first three terms of the paired WC energy in Eq. (9) of the main text. Adding the relative kinetic energy in Eq. (
47
) to the intracell Coulomb interaction in Eq. (
52
) and the harmonic confining potential in Eq. (
62
) leads to the Schrödinger equation for the internal pair energy
\epsilon_{0m}
and pair wave function
\varphi(\mathbf{k})
in Eq. (11) of the main text.

II
Variational states and energies: wave function approach

In this section of the supplemental material, we demonstrate that a first-quantized approach based on Hartree-type wave functions reproduces the results of the preceding section obtained using second-quantization. The appeal of the first-quantized approach lies in the relative simplicity of the variational wave functions and in the calculation of the variational energies.

We start with the first-quantized representation of the Hamiltonian:

H=\sum_{i}\frac{\hbar^{2}\hat{\mathbf{k}}_{i}^{2}}{2m^{*}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\hat{V}_{ij},

(64)

where the Coulomb operator has matrix elements

\Bra{\mathbf{k}_{i}\mathbf{k}_{j}}\hat{V}_{ij}\Ket{\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\prime}\mathbf{k}_{j}^{\prime}}=\delta\left(\mathbf{k}_{i}+\mathbf{k}_{j}-\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\prime}-\mathbf{k}_{j}^{\prime}\right)v\left(\mathbf{k}_{i}-\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\prime}\right)F_{\mathbf{k}_{i},\mathbf{k}_{i}^{\prime}}F_{\mathbf{k}_{j},\mathbf{k}_{j}^{\prime}}

(65)

and the form factors are given in Eq. (
17
).
The real space matrix elements of the interaction are given by

\displaystyle\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}}\hat{V}\Ket{\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{2}q^{2}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\right)}\delta\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}-\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}-\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}-\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\right)

(66)

\displaystyle=\frac{2e^{2}}{\pi\Omega\left|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right|}e^{-(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})^{2}/2\Omega}e^{-i\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{r}^{\prime}/\Omega}\delta\left(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\right),

(67)

and we introduced the COM and relative coordinates
\mathbf{r}_{1,2}=\mathbf{R}\pm\mathbf{r}/2
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1,2}=\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\pm\mathbf{r}^{\prime}/2
.

For the monatomic WC, we consider an
N
-electron variational wave function in the Hartree approximation:

\Psi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{r}_{N})=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\Phi(\mathbf{r}_{n}-\mathbf{R}_{n}),

(68)

where
\Phi(\mathbf{r})=e^{-r^{2}/4\sigma^{2}}/\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}}
. Here each electron
n
has been identified with a WC lattice site
\mathbf{R}_{n}
. We are making the approximation of distinguishable electrons, which is good up to exponentially small corrections due to exchange processes in the WC.

For the paired WC, we consider the wave function

\Psi_{p}(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{r}_{N})=\prod_{n=1}^{N_{p}}\Phi\left(\frac{\mathbf{r}_{na}+\mathbf{r}_{nb}}{2}-\mathbf{R}_{n}\right)\varphi(\mathbf{r}_{na}-\mathbf{r}_{nb}).

(69)

Here
\mathbf{r}_{na}
and
\mathbf{r}_{nb}
are the coordinates of the two paired electrons localized near WC lattice site
\mathbf{R}_{n}
, and
\Phi
and
\varphi
are the COM and relative wave functions of a pair, respectively. For the COM wave function we take a Gaussian with a variational width
\sigma_{p}
, while the relative wave function is determined by solving the two-electron pairing problem in a WC unit cell. In this approximation we include exchange effects within a pair (the spatial pair wave function should be fully antisymmetrized together the spin part of the wave function), but neglect exchange between pairs.

As we demonstrate explicitly below, using the Hartree-type wave functions (
68
) and (
69
) yields results identical to those of the second-quantized approach in the no-intercell-overlap approximation presented in the previous section. More comprehensive recent discussions of first-quantized wave functions in bands with nontrivial quantum geometry can be found in e.g., Refs.
Tan et al. 2025b
;
Tan et al. 2025a
.

II.1
Variational energy of the monatomic WC

Using the wave function (
68
), the kinetic energy is readily found to to be
\langle\Psi|T|\Psi\rangle=N\hbar^{2}/4m^{*}\sigma^{2}
, in agreement with Eq. (
33
). The potential energy is

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\right\rangle=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2}~\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}-\mathbf{R}_{n}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{R}_{n}\right)\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}}\hat{V}\Ket{\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}}.

(70)

Plugging in the interaction matrix elements (
66
) and integrating over the primed coordinates gives

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2A}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{2}q^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}

(71)

\displaystyle\times\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}~\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}-\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}\right)e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)}.

The integrals can be directly computed using the Gaussian form of the variational wave function, giving

\displaystyle I_{\pm}\left(\mathbf{q},\Omega\right)=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}~e^{\pm i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{r}\pm\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}\right)=\exp\left\{-\frac{q^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{\Omega^{2}}{16\sigma^{4}}\right)\right\}.

(72)

This gives the potential energy

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi|V|\Psi\right\rangle=\frac{N}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\tilde{\sigma}^{2}q^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}=\frac{Ne^{2}}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\tilde{\sigma}}e^{-\frac{R^{2}_{n}}{8\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}}I_{0}\left(\frac{R^{2}_{n}}{8\tilde{\sigma}^{2}}\right),

(73)

in agreement with Eq. (
40
) obtained using the second-quantized approach.

II.2
Variational energy of the paired WC

Taking the pair state wavefunction ansatz (
69
), we proceed to the calculation of the paired crystal energy. The kinetic energy factorizes as a sum of the kinetic energies of the COM and relative coordinates of the pairs.

\langle\Psi_{p}|T|\Psi_{p}\rangle=N_{p}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4M\sigma^{2}_{p}}+N_{p}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}~\varphi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}^{2}}{2\mu}\varphi\left(\mathbf{r}\right),

(74)

in agreement with Eq. (
47
).

The calculation of potential energy here is more involved and must account for both the intra- and intercell Coulomb interactions. We write it as

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{p}|V|\Psi_{p}\rangle=N_{p}v_{\text{intra}}+\frac{N_{p}}{2}v_{\text{inter}}.

(75)

II.2.1
Intracell interaction

For the intracell interaction, we have the expression

\displaystyle v_{\text{intra}}=\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{1}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}_{2}~\psi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)\psi\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\right)\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}}\hat{V}\Ket{\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}},

(76)

where
\psi(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2})=\Phi\left((\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{r}_{2})/2\right)\varphi(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})
.
Using the matrix elements (
66
) and performing the integrals over primed coordinates, we obtain

\displaystyle v_{\text{intra}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{2}q^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}~\psi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)\psi\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2},\mathbf{r}_{2}-\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}\right)e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)},

(77)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}~v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{2}q^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}~\varphi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\varphi\left(\mathbf{r}+\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)\right)e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}.

(78)

Fourier transforming
\varphi(\mathbf{r})\to\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{k})
gives the intracell interaction energy in Eq. (
52
).

II.2.2
Intercell interaction

Now we compute the potential energy of the Coulomb interaction between pairs of electrons. Denote the particles in one unit cell by
1
and
2
and the particles in another unit cell by
1^{\prime}
and
2^{\prime}
. Then the contribution to the potential energy coming from the interaction between the cells is given by

\displaystyle v_{\text{inter}}

\displaystyle=v_{11^{\prime}}+v_{12^{\prime}}+v_{21^{\prime}}+v_{22^{\prime}}

(79)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\int_{\left\{\mathbf{r}_{i}\right\},\left\{\mathbf{b}_{i}\right\}}\psi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)\psi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{R}_{n},\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{R}_{n}\right)\psi\left(\mathbf{b}_{1},\mathbf{b}_{2}\right)\psi\left(\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{R}_{n},\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{R}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}}\hat{V}_{11^{\prime}}\Ket{\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{2}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}}+\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}}\hat{V}_{12^{\prime}}\Ket{\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{2}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad~~~~~\left.+\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}}\hat{V}_{21^{\prime}}\Ket{\mathbf{b}_{2}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}}+\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}}\hat{V}_{22^{\prime}}\Ket{\mathbf{b}_{2}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}}\right),

(80)

where
\left\{\mathbf{r}_{i}\right\}=\{\mathbf{r}_{1},\mathbf{r}_{2},\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\}
and
\left\{\mathbf{b}_{i}\right\}=\{\mathbf{b}_{1},\mathbf{b}_{2},\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\}
.
Using the fact that the electrons in a pair are indistinguishable, we recognize that all four terms in the sum above are equal. We thus focus on one representative, for example,
v_{12^{\prime}}
, such that

v_{\text{inter}}=4v_{12^{\prime}}
.

The four-body matrix element of the interaction for this term has the form

\displaystyle\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{r}_{2}\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}}\hat{V}_{12^{\prime}}\Ket{\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}\mathbf{b}_{2}\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}}=\Bra{\mathbf{r}_{1}\mathbf{r}_{2}^{\prime}}\hat{V}_{12^{\prime}}\Ket{\mathbf{b}_{1}\mathbf{b}_{2}^{\prime}}\delta\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}-\mathbf{b}_{2}\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{b}_{1}^{\prime}\right).

(81)

Putting in the matrix elements (
66
) and performing the integrals using the delta functions, we arrive at

\displaystyle v_{\text{inter}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A}\sum_{\mathbf{R}_{n}\neq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\neq 0}4v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{2}q^{2}}e^{-i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{n}}

(82)

\displaystyle\times\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{R}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{R}^{\prime}~e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\left(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\right)}\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{R}\right)\Phi^{*}\left(\mathbf{R}^{\prime}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{R}+\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{4}\right)\Phi\left(\mathbf{R}^{\prime}-\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\times\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}~e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{r}+\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}{2}}\varphi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)\varphi^{*}\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\right)\varphi\left(\mathbf{r}+\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}\right)\varphi\left(\mathbf{r}^{\prime}+\frac{\left(\mathbf{q}\times\bm{\Omega}\right)}{2}\right).

Fourier transforming
\Phi(\mathbf{r})\to\tilde{\Phi}(\mathbf{q})
and
\varphi(\mathbf{r})\to\tilde{\varphi}(\mathbf{k})
we recover precisely the result (
57
) obtained in the second-quantization approach.

III
Quantum mechanics in a topological band

In this section, we briefly summarize the algorithm to obtain the first-quantized, wave function description of a particle in a band with nontrivial quantum geometry determined by the form factor (
17
).

To account for both the Berry curvature and quantum metric of the band, one needs to perform the real-space analog of the Peierls substitution in the potential

\hat{\mathbf{r}}\to\hat{\mathbf{r}}+\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{k}}

(83)

and dress the potential with the quantum metric factor

\displaystyle v\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{(2\pi)^{2}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}~v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\to v_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\mathbf{q}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}~v_{\mathbf{q}}e^{-\frac{\Omega}{4}q^{2}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}.

(84)

As an example, consider the Coulomb potential in 2D,
v_{\mathbf{q}}=2\pi e^{2}/q
. A straightforward calculation gives

v\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\frac{e^{2}}{r}\to v_{\Omega}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=e^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}q~J_{0}\left(qr\right)e^{-\frac{\Omega}{4}q^{2}}=e^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\Omega}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2\Omega}}I_{0}\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2\Omega}\right).

(85)

For
r\gg\sqrt{\Omega}
,
v_{\Omega}
tends to the usual Coulomb. However, at short distances
r\ll\sqrt{\Omega}
, the effective “size” of the particle induced by quantum geometry cuts off the divergence in the Coulomb potential.

For the harmonic potential, the action of the derivative on the exponential factor
\exp(-\Omega q^{2}/4)
produces a shift

\displaystyle v_{\text{harm}}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\frac{K\mathbf{r}^{2}}{2}\to v^{\Omega}_{\text{harm}}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\frac{K\mathbf{r}^{2}}{2}+\frac{K\Omega}{2}.

(86)

The single-particle behavior in a band with dispersion
\epsilon\left(\mathbf{k}\right)
and uniform Berry curvature
\Omega
with form factors (
17
) subjected to a potential
V(\mathbf{r})
is thus governed by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\Omega}=\epsilon(\hat{\mathbf{k}})+V_{\Omega}(\hat{\mathbf{r}}+\hat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{k}}).

(87)

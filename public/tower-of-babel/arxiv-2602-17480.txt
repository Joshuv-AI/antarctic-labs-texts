Directed type theory, with a twist
Fernando Chu, Paige Randall North

Abstract
In recent years, Homotopy Type Theory (HoTT) has had great success both as a foundation of mathematics and as internal language to reason about ∞ \infty -groupoids (a.k.a. spaces). However, in many areas of mathematics and computer science, it is often the case that it is categories, not groupoids, which are the more important structures to consider. For this reason, multiple directed type theories have been proposed, i.e., theories whose semantics are based on categories. In this paper, we present a new such type theory, Twisted Type Theory (TTT). It features a novel “twisting” operation on types: given a type that depends both contravariantly and covariantly on some variables, its twist is a new type that depends only covariantly on the same variables. To provide the semantics of this operation, we introduce the notion of dependent 2-sided fibrations (D2SFibs), which generalize Street’s notion of 2-sided fibrations. We develop the basic theory of D2SFibs, as well as characterize them through a straightening-unstraightening theorem. With these results in hand, we introduce a new elimination rule for Hom-types. We argue that our syntax and semantics satisfy key features that allow reasoning in a HoTT-like style, which allows us to mimic the proof techniques of that setting. We end the paper by exemplifying this, and use TTT to reason about categories, giving a syntactic proof of Yoneda’s lemma.

Introduction

In a seminal paper
[
15
]
, Hofmann and Streicher constructed the
groupoid model
: a semantic interpretation of Martin-Löf Type Theory (MLTT)
[
24
]
where contexts are interpreted as groupoids and types are interpreted as indexed groupoids.
Years later, Voevodsky et al.
[
18
]
generalized this result by constructing the
\infty
-groupoid model
.
Further, they observed that this model satisfies the so-called
univalence axiom
, which roughly states that equivalent types are equal.
These results marked the beginning of Homotopy Type Theory (HoTT)
[
34
]
.

After multiple years of development, HoTT has proven to be both (i) a successful foundation for developing mathematics and (ii) a useful synthetic language to reason about
\infty
-groupoids (a.k.a. spaces).
As an example of (i), in HoTT one can use the Structure Identity Principle (SIP) to transport properties between equivalent algebraic structures
[
10
,
2
]
; this gives a rigorous foundation to the often used practice in mathematics of equating equivalent structures.
As an example of (ii), one can encode CW-complexes as Higher Inductive Types (HITs) and compute their homotopy groups synthetically
[
8
,
21
]
.

However, in many areas of mathematics and computer science, it is often the case that it is categories, not groupoids, which are the more important structures to consider.
Hence, multiple researchers have proposed
directed type theories

[
3
,
13
,
20
,
22
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
30
,
31
]
.
That is, type theories with a notion of non-symmetric “directed equality”, whose semantics are based on categories.
The hope is then, in analogy to HoTT, that these theories would be (i) via a directed version of the SIP, an even more powerful foundation of mathematics and (ii) a useful synthetic language to reason about categories.

In this paper, we present a new directed type theory, which we call
Twisted Type Theory (TTT)
.
One of our overarching design principles was to follow very closely Hofmann and Streicher’s groupoid model of MLTT.
Therefore, we build upon North’s category model
[
28
]
, which generalizes it.
Indeed, in the category model, contexts are interpreted as categories, while types are interpreted as indexed categories.

We focus on generalizing the groupoid model as it is simpler than its
\infty
-dimensional counterpart, yet still retains enough features to validate a restricted version of points (i) and (ii) above.
Following closely the groupoid model will therefore allow us to replicate the syntactic techniques of HoTT, while maintaining some level of simplicity in our semantic considerations.
We leave it to future work to explore the higher dimensional models of TTT.

More specifically, TTT was designed to satisfy the following three desiderata, each of which is a categorical adaptation of a feature the groupoid model satisfies.

1.

Types as categories
.
Being a type in the empty context should semantically correspond to being a category.
This has the benefit of reducing the problem of whether a new construction is a category to a typechecking exercise.
This is in contrast to other work, such as Simplicial Type Theory (STT)
[
31
]
, that extends the universe of types to include additional structures that are not the object of interest, i.e. they have types that are not categories.
Categories are then recovered as types satisfying some additional conditions, and therefore one has to provide a proof that a newly constructed type is a category.
While there are some results that ensure that certain constructions in STT preserve categories, one is usually also interested in obtaining categories by applying constructions on the more general base types.
For example, discrete types in STT are defined as types satisfying a certain discreteness condition. Ad-hoc arguments are then used to show that these satisfy the so-called Segal condition, see Section 7 in op. cit.

2.

Emergent categorical structure
.
There should be a type of directed equalities, i.e. a
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-type, given by formation, introduction, elimination and computation rules.
This condition eases a generalization of the type theory to higher dimensions.
Indeed, having
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types allow encoding the
n
-cells of a type as the
n
-th iteration of the
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-type.
Further, from an appropriate
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-elimination rule, the coherences will follow; this is what happens with the
{\mathsf{Id}}
-elimination rule in HoTT, which gives types the structure of
\infty
-groupoids.
In contrast, other approaches would have to significantly change the type theory to accommodate the
n
-cells and their coherences.
For example, a higher dimensional analogue of the 2-dimensional type theory of Licata & Harper
[
22
]
, which does not have
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types, would have to manually specify an
n
-categorical structure to the contexts. Similarly, STT would have to add a calculus of new shapes
[
31
, p. 152]
.

3.

Trivial directed equalities through directed path objects
. We require that the
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-introduction rule corresponds to the factorization
A\to A^{\to}\to A\times A
of the diagonal map
\delta:A\to A\times A
through the arrow category
A^{\to}
of a category
A
(see Section
3
).
This is a generalization of the fact that, in a model category, path objects, i.e. appropriate factorizations
A\to A^{I}\to A\times A
of the diagonal map
\delta
, can be used to give an interpretation of the formation and introduction rules of
{\mathsf{Id}}
-types
[
4
]
.
This technical condition ensures that the HoTT notion of homotopies between functions, i.e. functions
\Pi_{x:X}f(x)=g(x)
, can be readily generalized to the directed case.
That is, it allows for reasoning about natural transformations (see Subsection
5.2
).
This had previously been a difficulty in some directed type theories, see e.g. Neumann & Altenkirch
[
26
, Section 6]
.

The work of North
[
28
]
already satisfied the first two desiderata.
Our contribution is then satisfying also the third.
We achieve this by introducing a novel “twisting” operation on types: given a type that depends both contravariantly and covariantly on some variables, its twist is a new type that depends only covariantly on the same variables.
Crucially, the interpretation of the twist of the
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-type of a type
A
gives the arrow category
A^{\to}
of
A
.
This enables us to state a
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-introduction rule, syntactically analogous to the
{\mathsf{Id}}
-introduction rule, whose interpretation satisfies desideratum (3).

More generally, the interpretation of the twisting of a type makes use of our notion of
dependent 2-sided fibrations (D2SFibs)
, a generalization of Street’s notion of a 2-sided fibration
[
32
]
.
Indeed, the twist of a type corresponds then to the unstraightening part of a straightening-unstraightening characterization we give of D2SFibs.

The theory TTT can then be summarized as an extension of North’s type theory
[
28
]
featuring twisted types and modified
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types.
We will also make use of other features already well known in the literature, such as restricted
\Pi
-types, opposite contexts, discrete universes, among others
[
22
,
26
]
.

Related work.

The first published work on directed type theory was presented by Licata & Harper
[
22
]
.
Their theory’s main feature is that the syntax had a primitive notion of transformations between parallel substitutions of contexts, thereby giving a 2-categorical structure to the category of contexts.
Since they did not introduce
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types, their work does not satisfy our desiderata (2) nor (3).
Later work by North
[
28
]
introduced
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types for the first time, but did so by restricting the
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-introduction rule to the groupoid core of a type, hence their theory does not meet our desiderata (3).
More recently, Riehl & Shulman
[
31
]
extended MLTT with a directed interval and other shapes, resulting in a powerful calculus reminiscent of Cubical Type Theory
[
9
]
.
In order to do this, their semantic interpretation of a type is a bisimplicial set, and hence only some types correspond to categories; therefore our desiderata (1) and (2) are not satisfied.

Further work has extended and refined each of these three works in different directions: Ahrens et al.
[
3
]
generalize Licata & Harper’s work by giving the category of contexts a bicategorical structure, Neumann & Altenkirch
[
26
]
extend North’s type theory by also adding groupoid cores of contexts and hence obtaining new rules for
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types, and Gratzer et al.
[
13
]
add modalities to Riehl & Shulman’s type theory, greatly increasing the expressivity of the theory.
Nonetheless, these new works satisfy the same desiderata as the work their building upon, and so none satisfy all three of our criteria.

A different line of work on directed type theory comes from theories that do not extend MLTT.

, which gives a (co)end calculus for profunctors; as well as the work of New & Licata

2
Categorical models of MLTT

For the rest of this paper, all our (op)fibrations are cloven.
We begin by recalling the definition of comprehension category.

Definition 1

(Comprehension categories
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. A
(split) comprehension category (CC) over

\mathcal{C}
is a (split) fibration
p:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{C}
equipped with a functor
\chi:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{C}^{\to}
, the
comprehension
, such that it preserves cartesian morphisms and such that the following diagram commutes.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}^{\to}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{cod}}

We write
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{T},\chi)
for such a CC. We say the CC is
full
if
\chi
is full and faithful.

Jacobs
[
17
]
showed that full split CC’s provide semantics for MLTT.
Briefly, the interpretation of the syntax is as follows:

•

A context
\Gamma
is interpreted as an object
{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\in\mathcal{C}
.

•

A type
\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}
is interpreted as an object
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}\in\mathcal{T}_{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}
, i.e. the fibre in
\mathcal{T}
over
{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}
.

•

The context extension
\Gamma,a:A
is interpreted as the domain of the comprehension of
A
, and so
{\llbracket\Gamma,x:A\rrbracket}:=\mathsf{dom}(\chi({\llbracket A\rrbracket}))
.

•

A term
\Gamma\vdash a:A
is interpreted as a section
{\llbracket a\rrbracket}:{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket\Gamma.A\rrbracket}
of
\chi({\llbracket A\rrbracket})
.

We now will give two categorical models of MLTT, i.e. two CC’s over
{\mathsf{Cat}}
. To simplify the following discussion, we introduce the following notations.

Notation 2
.

Let
F:A\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
(resp.
G:B^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
) be a functor. We write
A\vartriangleright F
(resp.
B\vartriangleright^{-}G
) for the covariant (resp. contravariant) Grothendieck construction of
F
(resp.
G
). We write
\pi:A\vartriangleright F\to A
(resp.
\pi:B\vartriangleright^{-}G\to B)
for its canonical projection.

2.1
The category model of MLTT

The category model of MLTT in terms of a CC is given by North
[
28
, Construction 3.3]
, which we now recall.

Definition 3

(The category model)
.

Consider the functor
[-,{\mathsf{Cat}}]_{\textnormal{lax}}:{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}

that maps a category
A
to the category of strict functors from
A
to
{\mathsf{Cat}}
and lax natural transformations. Write
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{lax}}
for the category
{\mathsf{Cat}}\vartriangleright^{-}[-,{\mathsf{Cat}}]_{\textnormal{lax}}
of indexed categories and lax natural transformations.

The
category model
is the full split CC over
{\mathsf{Cat}}
given by the canonical projection of the
\pi:{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{lax}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
, together with the comprehension functor
\vartriangleright^{*}{}:{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{lax}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\to}
given by mapping an object
(A,B)
of
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{lax}}
to
A\vartriangleright B\xrightarrow{\pi}A
in
{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\to}
.

It follows from the above discussion that, in the category model, a context is interpreted as a category
{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}
, while a type
\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}
interpreted as an indexed category
{\llbracket A\rrbracket}:{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
.

Notation 4
.

From now on we write
\smalltriangleright
for the context extension operation in MLTT, i.e. we write
\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A
for
\Gamma,a:A
.
For the category model, we then have
{\llbracket\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A\rrbracket}:={\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\vartriangleright{\llbracket A\rrbracket}
.
This notation will be useful to disambiguate from additional context extension operations we will introduce later.

Proposition 5

(
[
22
,
28
]
)
.

The category model has strong
\Sigma
-types and, if a countable hierarchy of inaccessible cardinals is assumed, universes
{\mathcal{U}}_{i}
.
Further, it also validates the following rules about opposite types and
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types.

\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\vdash A^{\mathsf{op}}\,{\mathsf{Type}}}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{right=$\mathsf{op}${{-Form}}}\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A^{\mathsf{op}}\smalltriangleright b:A\vdash{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)\,{\mathsf{Type}}}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{right=${\mathsf{Hom}}${{-Form}}}\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\vdash(A^{\mathsf{op}})^{\mathsf{op}}\equiv A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{right=$\mathsf{op}${{-Inv}}}\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A^{\mathsf{op}}\smalltriangleright b:A\vdash{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)^{\mathsf{op}}\equiv{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)\,{\mathsf{Type}}}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{right=${\mathsf{Hom}}${{-}}$\mathsf{op}$}

Proof sketch.

Let
\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}
and
\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash B\,{\mathsf{Type}}
be types.
The interpretation of
\Gamma\vdash\sum_{A}B\,{\mathsf{Type}}
is given by setting
{\llbracket\sum_{A}B\rrbracket}(\gamma)
to be the category that has as objects
(a,b)\in\coprod_{a\in A(\gamma)}B(a,\gamma)
, while a morphism
(a,b)\to(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
is a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
with
\alpha:a\to a^{\prime}
in
A(\gamma)
and
\beta:B(\mathsf{id}_{\gamma},\alpha)b\to b^{\prime}
.
The interpretation of opposite types is given by postcomposition with the opposite functor
\mathsf{op}:{\mathsf{Cat}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
. Finally, the interpretation of
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types is given by
{\llbracket{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}\rrbracket}(\gamma,x,y):=\hom_{A(\gamma)}(x,y)
.
∎

Remark 6
.

For the concerned reader, the specific universes the category model validates (under the assumption above) are Coquand universes
[
11
,
12
]
.
However, for convenience, we will use Russell universes with implicit indices, understanding that these should be elaborated to the Coquand ones.

Notation 7
.

For a functor
F:C\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
, we will write
F^{\mathsf{op}}
for
\mathsf{op}\circ F
. With this notation, for a type
A
, we have
{\llbracket A^{\mathsf{op}}\rrbracket}:={\llbracket A\rrbracket}^{\mathsf{op}}
.

The following well-known result, due to Grothendieck
[
14
]
, gives us a different perspective on this comprehension category.

Proposition 8

(Straightening-unstraightening for opfibrations)
.

Write
{\mathsf{Opfib}}^{\textup{split}}_{\textup{lax}}
for the full fibred subcategory of
{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\to}
spanned by the split opfibrations.
The comprehension functor
\vartriangleright^{*}{}:{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{lax}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\to}
restricts to a fibred equivalence of categories
\vartriangleright^{*}\,:{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{lax}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathsf{Opfib}}^{\textup{split}}_{\textup{lax}}
over
{\mathsf{Cat}}
.

Note that the fibration
\mathsf{cod}:{\mathsf{Opfib}}^{\text{split}}_{\text{lax}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
is not split, and hence cannot be directly used to interpret MLTT.
Hence, we will carefully apply Proposition
8
, ensuring that we are not breaking the splitness of our constructions.

2.2
The displayed category model of MLTT

As we mentioned in the introduction, for a category
A
, we are interested in a factorization
A\xrightarrow{\iota}A^{\to}\xrightarrow{\langle\mathsf{dom},\mathsf{cod}\rangle}A\times A
of the diagonal map
\delta:A\to A\times A
.
More specifically, we would like
\iota
to be defined by
\iota(a):=\mathsf{id}_{a}
.
In Hofmann & Streicher’s groupoid model
[
15
]
, the interpretation of the term
a:A\vdash{\mathsf{refl}}_{a}:\mathsf{Id}(a,a)
carries precisely this data, as the canonical projection
\pi:{\llbracket a:A,b:A,{\mathsf{Id}}(a,b)\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket a:A,b:A\rrbracket}
is isomorphic to the functor
\langle\mathsf{dom},\mathsf{cod}\rangle:{\llbracket A\rrbracket}^{\to}\to{\llbracket A\rrbracket}\times{\llbracket A\rrbracket}
.

Following this pattern, for the category model case, we are led to consider a type
a:A\smalltriangleright b:A\vdash H(a,b)\,{\mathsf{Type}}
, such that
\pi:{\llbracket a:A\smalltriangleright b:A\smalltriangleright H(a,b)\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket a:A\smalltriangleright b:B\rrbracket}
is isomorphic to
\langle\mathsf{dom},\mathsf{cod}\rangle:A^{\to}\to A\times A
.
However, this approach cannot work: by Proposition
8
, the projection
\pi
is always an opfibration, but
\langle\mathsf{dom},\mathsf{cod}\rangle:A^{\to}\to A\times A
is not.

We therefore introduce a whole new family of types, the
displayed types
, which will allow us to consider our desired projection from the arrow category.
Indeed, while types in the category model correspond to opfibrations via Proposition
8
, displayed types will correspond to arbitrary functors over a base category.
First, we begin by recalling a well-known straightening-unstraightening result due to Bénabou
[
6
]
.

Proposition 9

(Straightening-unstraightening for displayed categories)
.

Write
{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}
for the bicategory that has as objects categories and as morphisms profunctors (not to be confused with the category
{\mathsf{Prof}}
of Definition
15
). Consider also the functor
[-,{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}]^{\textnormal{lax,normal}}:{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
that maps a category
A
to the category of lax normal functors from
A
to
{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}
.
Write
{\mathsf{DCat}}:={\mathsf{Cat}}\vartriangleright^{-}[-,{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}]^{\textnormal{lax,normal}}
for the category of
displayed categories
.

There exists a functor
(\vartriangleright^{d})^{*}\,:{\mathsf{DCat}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\to}
which induces a fibred equivalence of categories over
{\mathsf{Cat}}
.

Proof sketch.

We follow Loregian
[
23
, Theorem 5.4.5]
.
Consider the forgetful functor
U:({\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}})_{*}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}
, where
({\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}})_{*}
is the bicategory that has as objects pointed categories and as morphisms pointed profunctors.
The functor
(\vartriangleright^{d})^{*}
is defined on objects by mapping a lax normal functor
F:A\to{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}
to the strict pullback
F^{*}U:A\times_{{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}}({\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}})_{*}\to A
.
∎

Remark 10
.

The term “displayed category” is borrowed from Ahrens & Lumsdaine work
[
1
]
, which studies functors to a base category in the context of type theory.

Definition 11

(Displayed category model)
.

The full split CC over
{\mathsf{Cat}}
that has as its comprehension the unstraightening functor
(\vartriangleright^{d})^{*}\,:{\mathsf{DCat}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}^{\to}
is called the
displayed category model
.

Remark 12
.

The existence of the displayed category model is folklore. That is, it is known by researchers in the field, but, to our knowledge, there is no published work mentioning it.

In order to use this second model, we now introduce a second copy of MLTT to our syntax.
For this copy, the typing judgement, term-in-type judgement, and context extension operation are written
\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}
,
\Gamma\vdash a\overset{d}{:}A
and
\Gamma\smalltriangleright^{d}a:A
respectively.
We call the types
\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}
the
displayed types
.
Whenever we interpret these judgements, it will be using the displayed category model.

Now, we can relate the two copies of MLTT by the following proposition.

Proposition 13
.

There is a strict family inclusion
[
19
]
from the category model to the displayed category model.
That is, the following two rules are validated.

\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}}\quad\textsc{right=${\mathsf{Type}}${{-Disp}}}\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright^{d}a:A\equiv\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A}\quad\textsc{right={{Ctx-Ext-Disp}}}

Proof.

The rule
{\mathsf{Type}}
-Disp
is validated by the embedding
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\text{lax}}\to{\mathsf{DCat}}
given by postcomposing a functor
A:\Gamma\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
with the embedding
{\mathsf{Cat}}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{Cat}}_{\mathsf{Prof}}
.
The other rule follows by definition of
\smalltriangleright^{d}
.
∎

Remark 14
.

An equivalent perspective, mentioned in op. cit., is to view strict family inclusions as degenerate modalities.
Indeed, the rule
{\mathsf{Type}}
-Disp
can be seen as a dependent right adjoint
[
7
]
to the identity functor on the category of contexts.

In the next section, we introduce the operation of “twisting” a type.
Crucially, the twist of the
{\mathsf{Hom}}
type will allow us to recover our desired functor
\langle\mathsf{dom},\mathsf{cod}\rangle:A^{\to}\to A\times A
.

3
Twisted types and dependent 2-sided fibrations

It was first observed by Street
[
33
]
that for a category
A
, the functor
\langle\mathsf{dom},\mathsf{cod}\rangle:A^{\to}\to A\times A
can be obtained from applying a general procedure to the profunctor
\hom_{A}:A^{\mathsf{op}}\times A\to{\mathsf{Set}}
.
Specifically, he introduced the Grothendieck construction on profunctors.
This operation takes an arbitrary profunctor
C:A^{\mathsf{op}}\times B\to{\mathsf{Set}}
and gives back a new category
G(C)
together with a span
A\xleftarrow{l_{C}}G(C)\xrightarrow{r_{C}}B
.
Further, he also introduced the notion of a 2-sided fibration
[
32
]
, and showed that his Grothendieck construction gives a fibred equivalence of categories from the category
{\mathsf{Prof}}
of profunctors to the category
\mathsf{2SFib}
of 2-sided fibrations.

Restricting our attention to types
A
and
B
in the empty context, we could internalize Street’s Grothendieck construction to our syntax by stating that given a type
a:A^{\mathsf{op}}\smalltriangleright b:B\vdash C\,{\mathsf{Type}}
, we can obtain a new displayed type
a:A\smalltriangleright b:B\vdash G(C)\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}
.
We could then interpret
G(C)
as the displayed category corresponding to the functor
G(C)\xrightarrow{\langle l_{C},r_{C}\rangle}A\times B
.
However, in many of our cases of interest, the type
B
depends on
A
; e.g. see Example
18
.
Hence, we need to consider a dependent version of Street’s definitions and results.

We begin with a generalization of the notion of profunctors.

Definition 15

(Dependent profunctors)
.

Write
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{str}}:={\mathsf{Cat}}\vartriangleright^{-}[-,{\mathsf{Cat}}]
for the category of indexed categories and strict natural transformations and let
K:({\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{str}})^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
be the functor given by
K(A,B):=[A\vartriangleright B^{\mathsf{op}},{\mathsf{Cat}}]
.
The
category of dependent profunctors

{\mathsf{DProf}}
is the category
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{str}}\vartriangleright^{-}K
, whose objects are triples
(A,B,C)
, with
C:A\vartriangleright B^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
.

Indeed, dependent profunctors generalize profunctors in the following sense.

Lemma 16
.

There is a fibred embedding
{\mathsf{Prof}}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{DProf}}
.

Proof.

The embedding maps a profunctor
C:A\times B^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
to the dependent profunctor
C^{\prime}:A\vartriangleright(\mathsf{const}_{B})^{\mathsf{op}}\xrightarrow{\cong}A\times B^{\mathsf{op}}\xrightarrow{C}{\mathsf{Cat}}
.
∎

Notation 17
.

Because of the previous lemma, we will conflate profunctors with their dependent version.
Further, given two categories
A
and
B
, we will write
A\vartriangleright B
for
A\vartriangleright\mathsf{const}_{B}
.

Note that dependent profunctors correspond to types
a:A\smalltriangleright b:B^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash C\,{\mathsf{Type}}
in the category model. More generally, they also correspond to types
\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash C\,{\mathsf{Type}}
, by considering
\Gamma
as one single category.
Indeed, this is why we chose the contravariant variable in the dependent profunctor to be the second one: with the opposite convention, a dependent profunctor would correspond to a type
\Gamma^{\mathsf{op}}\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash B\,{\mathsf{Type}}
.
While we will introduce the opposite of contexts later (Proposition
33
), they are not as well-behaved as opposite types.

Example 18
.

For a type
\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}
, the type
\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:A\smalltriangleright a:A^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)\,{\mathsf{Type}}
induces the dependent profunctor
{\llbracket{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)\rrbracket}:({\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\vartriangleright{\llbracket b:A\rrbracket})\vartriangleright{\llbracket a:A\rrbracket}^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
.

Next, we generalize Street’s notion of Grothendieck construction for profunctors.

Definition 19

(Unstraightening of dependent profunctors)
.

Let
C:A\vartriangleright B^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
be a dependent profunctor.
Define the functor
B\blacktriangleright C:A\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
by

B\blacktriangleright C:=\left\llbracket a:A\vdash\left(\sum_{b:B^{\mathsf{op}}}C^{\mathsf{op}}\right)^{\mathsf{op}}\,{\mathsf{Type}}\right\rrbracket.

The
unstraightening
of
C
is the category
A\vartriangleright(B\blacktriangleright C)
.

Notation 20
.

By convention,
\blacktriangleright
binds more tightly than
\vartriangleright
.

Concretely, the objects of
A\vartriangleright B\blacktriangleright C
are triples
(a,b,c)
with
a\in A
,
b\in B(a)
and
c\in C(a,b)
. A morphism
(a,b,c)\to(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime})
is a triple
(\alpha,\beta,\theta)
with
\alpha:a\to a^{\prime}
,
\beta:B(\alpha)(b)\to b^{\prime}
and
\theta:C(\alpha,\mathsf{id}_{B(\alpha)b})c\to C(\mathsf{id}_{a},\beta)c^{\prime}
.
From this description, it’s clear that there is a canonical projection
\pi_{2}:A\vartriangleright B\blacktriangleright C\to A\vartriangleright B
given by forgetting the last component.

Proposition 21

(Twisted types)
.

The following rules are validated.

\frac{\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash C\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B\vdash\mathsf{Tw}_{b}(C)\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}}\quad\textsc{right={{Twist}}}\frac{\Gamma\vdash B\,{\mathsf{Type}}\Gamma\vdash C\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B\vdash\mathsf{Tw}_{b}(C)\equiv C\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}}\quad\textsc{right={{Twist-Weak}}}

Proof.

The interpretation of
\mathsf{Tw}_{b}(C)
is given by the displayed category corresponding to the canonical projection
\pi_{2}:A\vartriangleright B\blacktriangleright C\to A\vartriangleright B
.
The equality required by
Twist-Weak
readily follows.
∎

Notation 22
.

We write
C[\bar{b}/b]
for the type
\mathsf{Tw}_{b}(C)
.
Notice how this notation makes
Twist-Weak
automatic: If
C
does not depend on
b
then
C[\bar{b}/b]
is just
C
.
The
C[\bar{b}/b]
notation was inspired by Laretto et al.
[
20
]
.

In the following, it will be important to keep track of which displayed types come from directly applying the
Twist
rule. Hence, we introduce the following notation.

Notation 23
.

For a type
\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash C\,{\mathsf{Type}}
, we write
\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B\smallblacktriangleright c:C[\bar{b}/b]
for the context
\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B\smalltriangleright^{d}c:C[\bar{b}/b]
.
In particular, uses of
\smallblacktriangleright
will imply that no variable in the context
\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B
has been (non-trivially) substituted. With this notation we have
{\llbracket\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:B\smallblacktriangleright c:C[\bar{b}/b]\rrbracket}\cong{\llbracket\Gamma\rrbracket}\vartriangleright{\llbracket B\rrbracket}\blacktriangleright{\llbracket C\rrbracket}
.

We now return to our original motivation, and recover our desired projection
A^{\to}\to A\times A
.

Example 24
.

Let
A
be a category. By definition, the category
{\llbracket b:A\smalltriangleright a:A\smallblacktriangleright\hom_{A}(\bar{a},b)\rrbracket}:=A\vartriangleright A\blacktriangleright\overline{\hom}_{A}
has as objects triples
(b,a,f)
, with
f:a\to b
in
A
, while a morphism
(b,a,f)\to(b^{\prime},a^{\prime},f^{\prime})
is a triple
(\beta,\alpha,e)
with
\beta:b\to b^{\prime}
,
\alpha:a\to a^{\prime}
, and
e:\beta\circ f=f^{\prime}\circ\alpha
. That is, this is the arrow category
A^{\to}
. The projection
{\llbracket b:A\smalltriangleright a:A\smallblacktriangleright\hom_{A}(\bar{a},b)\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket b:A\smalltriangleright a:A\rrbracket}
is then given by
\langle\mathsf{cod},\mathsf{dom}\rangle:A^{\to}\to A\times A
.

By contrast, the category
{\llbracket b:A\smalltriangleright a:A^{\mathsf{op}}\smalltriangleright\hom_{A}(a,b)\rrbracket}
has the same objects, but a morphism
(b,a,f)\to(b^{\prime},a^{\prime},f^{\prime})
is a triple
(\beta,\alpha,e)
with
\beta:b\to b^{\prime}
,
\alpha:a^{\prime}\to a
and
e:\beta\circ f\circ\alpha=f^{\prime}
. That is, it is the twisted arrow category
A^{\mathsf{tw}}
. Hence, applying the
Twist
rule to the type
{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)
appears to have made a “twist”.

The generalization of a 2-sided fibration is given as follows.

Definition 25

(D2SFib)
.

Consider a category
A
and a functor
B:A\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
.
A (split)
dependent 2-sided fibration (D2SFib)
from
A
to
B
consists of the following data:

1.

A category
C
;

2.

A functor
q:C\to A\vartriangleright B
;

3.

Data specifying that
q
is a “local fibration”. That is, for each
a\in A
the functor
q_{|a}:C_{a}\to B(a)
given by postcomposing
q_{a}:C_{a}\to(A\vartriangleright B)_{a}
with the isomorphisms
(A\vartriangleright B)_{a}\cong B(a)
is a (split) fibration, so that each morphism
\beta:b\to qe
in
B(a)
has a specified cartesian lift
\beta^{*}e\to e
;

4.

Data specifying that
p:=\pi_{A}\circ q:C\to A
is a (split) opfibration, so that each morphism
\alpha:pe\to a
has a specified opcartesian lift
e\to\alpha_{!}e
;

such that:

5.

q
is a cartesian functor of opfibrations from
p:C\to A
to
\pi:A\vartriangleright B\to A
; and

6.

For each
\alpha:pe\to a
in
A
and
\beta:b\to qe
in
B(p(e))
, the canonical morphism
\alpha_{!}\beta^{*}e\to(B(\alpha)\beta)^{*}\alpha_{!}e
given by any of the universal properties is an identity.

For simplicity, as they are our main objects of interest in this paper, we will focus only on split D2SFibs.
Therefore, we will leave the qualifier “split” implicit.
We will also identify a D2SFib as above with its first component, i.e. the functor
q:C\to A\vartriangleright B
.

Definition 26

(Cartesian functors between D2SFibs)
.

A
cartesian functor
between two D2SFibs
q:C\to A\vartriangleright B
and
q^{\prime}:C^{\prime}\to A\vartriangleright B
is a functor
\varphi:C\to C^{\prime}
satisfying the following two conditions.

1.

It is a cartesian functor of opfibrations from
\pi_{A}\circ q:C\to A
to
\pi_{A}\circ q^{\prime}:C^{\prime}\to A
.

2.

For each
a\in A
, the restriction
\varphi_{a}:C_{a}\to C^{\prime}_{a}
is a cartesian functor of fibrations from
q_{|a}:C_{a}\to B(a)
to
q^{\prime}_{|a}:C^{\prime}_{a}\to B(a)

Note that
q^{\prime}\circ\varphi=q
follows from these conditions.

With this notion of cartesian functors, D2SFibs from
A
to
B
form a category
{\mathsf{D2SFib}}(A,B)
.
In fact, these assemble to a fibred category, as the following routine lemma shows.

Lemma 27
.

Given a D2SFib
q:C\to A\vartriangleright B
and a morphism
(F,G):(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})\to(A,B)
in
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{str}}
, the pullback of
q
along the cartesian functor
{\vartriangleright}^{*}(F,G):A^{\prime}\vartriangleright B^{\prime}\to A\vartriangleright B
induces a D2SFib from
A^{\prime}
to
B^{\prime}
.
Further, this operation is pseudofunctorial on
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{str}}
, and hence it induces a non-split fibred category
{\mathsf{D2SFib}}
.

We are now ready to state our generalization of Street’s equivalence between profunctors and 2-sided fibrations to the dependent case.

Proposition 28

(Straightening-unstraightening for D2SFibs)
.

Unstraightening of dependent profunctors (Definition
19
) extends to a fibred equivalence of categories
\blacktriangleright^{*}:\mathsf{DProf}\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\mathsf{D2SFib}}
over
{\mathsf{ICat}}_{\textnormal{str}}
.

Proof sketch.

The straightening functor
S:\mathsf{D2SFib}(A,B)\to[A\vartriangleright B^{\mathsf{op}},{\mathsf{Cat}}]
is defined on objects by mapping a D2SFib
q:C\to A\vartriangleright B
to the functor that maps an object
(a,b)\in A\vartriangleright B^{\mathsf{op}}
to the fibre in
C
above it.
A complete proof is given in Section
B
of the appendix.
∎

.

It can be verified that a span

Twisted Type Theory

In this section we introduce Twisted Type Theory.
We first introduce our new rules for
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types. Afterwards, we introduce constructions already considered in the directed type theory literature, and relate them to our

4.1
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types revisited

We begin by observing that we can now substitute
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types along diagonals. That is, we can make the following derivation.

Example 30

Let

be a type in context

\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\frac{\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:A\smalltriangleright a:A^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,b)\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\frac{\Gamma\smalltriangleright b:A\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(\bar{a},b)\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(\bar{a},a)\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}}\hskip 8.04996pt\textsc{Right=Subst}}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=Twist}}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=${\mathsf{Hom}}$-Form}

Proposition 31

(
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-Intro
)

The category model validates the following rule.

\frac{\Gamma\vdash A\,{\mathsf{Type}}}{\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash{\mathsf{refl}}_{a}\overset{d}{:}{\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(\bar{a},a)}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=${\mathsf{Hom}}${{-Intro}}}

Proof.

By an analogous argument to Example

, we see that

, where

to

∎

5
Applications

In this section, we use TTT to develop some synthetic category theory.
Any mentions to an interpretation of the syntax will therefore be about the category model of TTT.

5.1
Directed univalence

By definition, the semantics of the type of functions
A\to B
is the category of functors
[\llbracket A\rrbracket,\llbracket B\rrbracket]
.
When
A
and
B
are sets, this category has no non-identity morphisms; i.e. it is a set.
This suggests the following definition.

Definition 41
.

For sets
\Gamma^{\mathsf{op}}\vdash A:{\mathsf{Set}}
and
\Gamma\vdash B:{\mathsf{Set}}
, the conversion rule
\Gamma\vdash A\to B\equiv{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathsf{Set}}(A,B):{\mathsf{Set}}
is called
directed univalence
.

By the previous discussion, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 42
.

The category model validates directed univalence.

An alternative to the judgemental equality
{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathsf{Set}}(A,B):{\mathsf{Set}}
would be requiring that the canonical function
{\mathsf{Hom}}_{\mathsf{Set}}(A,B)\to(A\to B)
induces an equivalence of types.
However, such a function can only be formed when
A
and
B
are types in the empty context.
Our current definition applies in more general contexts, as well as being more useful in pen and paper proofs, at the cost of severely complicating typechecking.

We will freely use directed univalence in the next sections.

5.2
Natural transformations

As was stated in the introduction, twisted types allow us to capture syntactically the semantic notion of natural transformations between functors.

Proposition 43
.

For two terms
a:A\vdash Fa:B
and
a:A\vdash Ga:B
, terms
a:A\vdash t\overset{\scriptscriptstyle{d}}{:}{\mathsf{Hom}}(\overline{Fa},Ga)
correspond to natural transformations
{\llbracket F\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket G\rrbracket}
in the category model.

Proof.

By the universal property of the pullback, terms
t
as above correspond to functors
\tau:{\llbracket A\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket A\rrbracket}^{\to}
satisfying that for all
a\in{\llbracket A\rrbracket}
, the morphism
\tau(a)
has as domain
{\llbracket F\rrbracket}(a)
and as codomain
{\llbracket G\rrbracket}(a)
.
That is, they correspond to natural transformations
{\llbracket F\rrbracket}\to{\llbracket G\rrbracket}
.
∎

Remark 44
.

For the reader familiar with lax ends, the previous proposition follows from a more general fact, which is readily verified: for a profunctor
F:A\times A^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
, the category of lifts from
A
to
A\vartriangleright A\blacktriangleright F
through the diagonal
\delta:A\to A\times A
is the lax end of
F
. The previous result then follows by setting
F
to be
{\mathsf{Hom}}(F-,G-):A\times A^{\mathsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{Cat}}
.

Generalizing to arbitrary contexts and dependent functions, an argument analogous to the previous one gives the following result.

Proposition 45
.

Let
\Gamma\vdash F,G:\prod_{A}B
be two functions.
Then the canonical function
{\mathsf{Tm}}_{d}(\Gamma,\hom_{\Pi_{A}B}(\bar{F},G))\to{\mathsf{Tm}}_{d}(\Gamma\smalltriangleright^{-}a:A,\hom_{Ba}(\overline{F(a)},G(a))

given by
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-elimination induces a bijection between the interpretation of both sets.

Internalizing this result, we present the following axiom.

Definition 46
.

Let
\Gamma\vdash F,G:\prod_{A}B
be two functions.
The axiom
directed function extensionality
states that given a term
\Gamma\smalltriangleright^{-}a:A\vdash t:\hom_{Ba}(\overline{F(a)},G(a))
, we can form a new term
\Gamma\vdash\mathsf{dfunext}(t):\hom_{\Pi_{A}B}(F,G)
.

Note that the natural transformations are syntactically analogous to the homotopies
a:A\vdash t:{\mathsf{Id}}(Fa,Ga)
in HoTT.
However, unlike in that setting, we cannot form the type of all natural transformations by using a
\Pi
-type, since we only have
{\mathsf{Hom}}(\overline{Fa},Ga)\,{\mathsf{Type}}_{d}
. The problem is resolved for functors into
{\mathsf{Set}}
, by uncurrying, as the next proposition shows.

Proposition 47
.

Let
A
be a type and let
F
and
G
be copresheaves from
A
, i.e.
a:A\vdash Fa:{\mathsf{Set}}
and
a:A\vdash Ga:{\mathsf{Set}}

{\mathsf{Nat}}(F,G):=\prod_{(a,x):\sum_{a:A}Fa}Ga

is the set of natural transformations
F\to G
.

Proof.

Indeed, we have the following chain of isomorphisms.

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Tm}}\bigg(\diamond,\prod_{(a,x):\sum_{a:A}Fa}Ga\bigg)

\displaystyle\cong{\mathsf{Tm}}\big(\sum_{a:A}Fa,Ga\big)

by Proposition

\displaystyle\cong{\mathsf{Tm}}(a:A\smalltriangleright x:Fa,Ga)

by Proposition

\displaystyle\cong{\mathsf{Tm}}_{d}(a:A,\overline{Fa}\to Ga)

by Proposition

\displaystyle\cong{\mathsf{Tm}}_{d}(a:A,{\mathsf{Hom}}(\overline{Fa},Ga))

by Proposition

\displaystyle\cong{\mathsf{Nat}}({\llbracket F\rrbracket},{\llbracket G\rrbracket})

\displaystyle\text{by Proposition \ref{prop:internal-nat-hom}}\qed

When working with copresheaves, we can also consider equalities of natural transformations. In the following, for ease of reading, for a natural transformation

we write

for

.

Let
A
be a type,

and

be two copresheaves from

and

Since

. Therefore, we have

Let

5.3
Yoneda lemma

In this section, we sketch a proof of the Yoneda lemma. A complete proof with the omitted details can be found in Section
C.2
in the appendix.

Lemma 50

(Yoneda)
.

Let
A:{\mathcal{U}}
be a type in the empty context. Then the two following functors are naturally isomorphic.

\displaystyle Y,\bar{Y}

\displaystyle:(A\to{\mathsf{Set}})\times A\to{\mathsf{Set}}

\displaystyle Y(F,a)

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Nat}}({\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,-),F)

\displaystyle\bar{Y}(F,a)

\displaystyle:=Fa

Proof sketch.

We use Corollary
49
. First, we define a natural transformation
\Phi:Y\to\bar{Y}
by evaluation on
{\mathsf{refl}}_{a}
, as follows.

\frac{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright\varphi:Y(F,a)\vdash(a,{\mathsf{refl}}_{a})\overset{-}{:}\sum_{x:A}\hom(a,x)}{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright\varphi:Y(F,a)\vdash\varphi_{a}({\mathsf{refl}}_{a}):Fa}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=$\Pi$-Elim}

The first step in the derivation of
\Psi:\bar{Y}\to Y
is given by
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-elimation, as follows.

\frac{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash Fa\,{\mathsf{Type}}\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994ptF:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright x:A\smalltriangleright a:A\smallblacktriangleright f:\hom(a,x),r:Fa\vdash Fx\,{\mathsf{Type}}\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994ptF:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash r:Fa}{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright x:A\smalltriangleright a:A\smallblacktriangleright f:\hom(a,x)\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash j(f,r,r):Fx}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=${\mathsf{Hom}}$-Elim}

By rearranging the context, we obtain the following term.

F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash\lambda(x,f).j(f,r,r):\prod_{(x,f):\sum_{x:A}\hom(a,x)}Fx

That
\Phi\circ\Psi
is the identity follows from the computation rule for
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-types, while
\Psi\circ\Phi=\mathsf{id}
follows from another application of
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-elimination.
∎

6
Conclusion and future work

We have introduced TTT as an extension of MLTT and showed that it allows for synthetic reasoning about categories.
While we argued for the three desiderata given in the introduction, we emphasize that any such criteria will lead to different advantages and disadvantages.
For example, a negative consequence of the desideratum (1), i.e. that closed types are interpreted as categories, is that not all
\Pi
-types will exist, and so the
\Pi
-formation rule has to be restricted, as we did in Proposition
37
.
Hence, we see our work as exploring and developing one of many different possibilities in the space of directed type theories.

Nonetheless, we argue that the introduction of twisted types is an important development:
They are linked to
\Sigma
-types and
\Pi
-types via Propositions
39
and
38
, as well as to lax ends via Remark
44
.
Further, the
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-elimination rule it allows is essentially a generalization of the Yoneda lemma.
Indeed, careful inspection of the proof given in Lemma
50
reveals that the first step in the derivation of the natural transformation
\Psi:\bar{Y}\to Y
already carries all the information of the Yoneda lemma, albeit in a disorganized way: the next steps in the proof are all invertible, and hence are just reorganizing the data.

This being said, there are multiple avenues to explore which could improve TTT.
For one, we need to develop a richer theory of D2SFibs.
We expect to find a mathematical structure generalizing weak factorization systems, which would allow us to generalize North’s
[
29
]
results about 2-sided fibrations to the dependent case.
We expect that such results would clarify and refine our
{\mathsf{Hom}}
-elimination principle.
An additional area to explore is the reintroduction of the
\mathsf{core}
-types from North
[
28
]
.
These types are useful as not every categorical construction is either covariant or contravariant. Indeed, in our statement of Yoneda we required that
A
be a closed type precisely for this reason.
Furthermore, the rules for permuting contexts involving twisted types could be improved.
Our proof of the Yoneda lemma uses such a permutation, but our current proof of its correctness is quite technical (see Lemma
62
).
Finally, future work should explore the higher dimensional models of TTT, and study the properties the twisted types have in this context.

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\equiv w:\Sigma\Gamma\times A\smalltriangleright^{-}z:\sum_{x:A}{\mathsf{Hom}}(a,x)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\cong\Gamma\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright^{-}z:\sum_{x:A}{\mathsf{Hom}}(a,x)

Here,
\Sigma\Gamma
is defined by
\Sigma\Gamma:=\Sigma_{\gamma_{1}:\Gamma_{1}}\cdots\Sigma_{\gamma_{n-1}:\Gamma_{n-1}}\Gamma_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 63

(Yoneda)
.

Let
A:{\mathcal{U}}
be a type in the empty context. Then the two following functors are naturally isomorphic.

\displaystyle Y,\bar{Y}

\displaystyle:(A\to{\mathsf{Set}})\times A\to{\mathsf{Set}}

\displaystyle:={\mathsf{Nat}}({\mathsf{Hom}}_{A}(a,-),F)

\displaystyle\bar{Y}(F,a)

\displaystyle:=Fa

We use Corollary
49
.
First, we obtain a natural transformation

\frac{\frac{\frac{\frac{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\vdash Fa\,{\mathsf{Type}}\hskip 4.5pt\hskip 4.5ptF:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright x:A\smalltriangleright a:A\smallblacktriangleright f:\hom(a,x),r:Fa\vdash Fx\,{\mathsf{Type}}\hskip 4.5pt\hskip 4.5ptF:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash r:Fa}{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright x:A\smalltriangleright a:A\smallblacktriangleright f:\hom(a,x)\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash j(f,r,r):Fx}\hskip 4.5pt\textsc{Right=${\mathsf{Hom}}$-Elim}}{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright^{-}(x,f):\sum_{x:A}\hom(a,x)\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash j(f,r,r):Fx}\hskip 8.04996pt\textsc{Right=Lemma-\ref{lemma:yoneda-perm-proof}}}{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright r:Fa\smalltriangleright^{-}(x,f):\sum_{x:A}\hom(a,x)\vdash j(f,r,r):Fx}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=Perm}}{F:A\to{\mathsf{Set}}\smalltriangleright a:A\smalltriangleright r:Fa\vdash\lambda(x,f).j(f,r,r):\prod_{(x,f):\sum_{x:A}\hom(a,x)}Fx}\hskip 9.24994pt\textsc{Right=$\Pi$-Intro}

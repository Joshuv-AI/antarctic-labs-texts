The dimension and Bose distance of certain primitive BCH codes
Run Zheng, Nung-Sing Sze, Zejun Huang

Abstract
Bose-Ray-Chaudhuri-Hocquenghem (BCH) codes are a significant class of cyclic codes that play an important role in both theoretical research and practical applications. Their strong error-correcting abilities and efficient encoding and decoding methods make BCH codes widely applicable in various areas, including communication systems, data storage devices, and consumer electronics. Although BCH codes have been extensively studied, the parameters of BCH codes are not known in general. Let q q be a prime power and m m be a positive integer. Denote by 𝒞 ( q , m , δ ) \mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)} the narrow-sense primitive BCH code with length q m − 1 q^{m}-1 and designed distance δ \delta . As of now, the dimensions of 𝒞 ( q , m , δ ) \mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)} are fully understood only for m ≤ 2 m\leq 2 . For m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 , the dimensions of 𝒞 ( q , m , δ ) \mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)} are known only for the range 2 ≤ δ ≤ q ⌊ ( m + 1 ) / 2 ⌋ + 1 2\leq\delta\leq q^{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor+1} and for a limited number of special cases. In this paper, we determined the dimension and Bose distance of 𝒞 ( q , m , δ ) \mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)} for m ≥ 4 m\geq 4 and δ ∈ [ 2 , q ⌊ ( 2 ​ m − 1 ) / 3 ⌋ + 1 ] . \delta\in[2,q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/{3}\rfloor+1}]. Additionally, we have also extended our results to primitive BCH codes that are not necessarily narrow-sense.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
q
be a prime power, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field of order
q
. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be the
n
-dimensional linear space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}.
A code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum distance
d
. A linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is said to be
cyclic
if
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathcal{C}
implies that
(c_{n-1},c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-2})\in\mathcal{C}.

Each vector
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is identified with its polynomial representation

c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1),

and each code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is identified with a subset of the quotient ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1).
In this way, a linear code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is cyclic if and only if it is an ideal of the quotient ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1).

Note that each ideal of
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
is principal. Therefore, a cyclic code
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
can be denoted by
\mathcal{C}=\left\langle g(x)\right\rangle,
where
g(x)\in\mathcal{C}
is monic with minimum degree. Then
g(x)
is called the
generator polynomial
of
\mathcal{C}
, and
h(x)=(x^{n}-1)/{g(x)}
is called the
parity-check polynomial
of
\mathcal{C}
.

Suppose that
n
is an integer such that
\mathrm{gcd}(n,q)=1
. Denote
m=\mathrm{ord}_{n}(q)
, i.e., the smallest integer such that

q^{m}\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n).
Let
\alpha
be a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}

\beta=\alpha^{(q^{m}-1)/n}
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. This leads to the factorization

For each integer
i\in[0,n-1]
, we denote by
m_{i}(x)
the minimal polynomial of
\beta^{i}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
A cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is called a BCH code with designed distance
\delta
if its generator polynomial has the form

\mathrm{lcm}(m_{b}(x),m_{b+1}(x),\ldots,m_{b+\delta-2}(x))

for some integers

and
2\leq\delta\leq n
,
where
\mathrm{lcm}
denotes the least common multiple of the polynomials. When

for some positive integer

, such a code is called a

BCH code with designed distance
\delta
, denoted

.
If

, the code
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)}
is called a

BCH code, denoted
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
. Note that
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta^{{}^{\prime}},b)}

may be the identical for distinct

It is well known that the dimension and Bose distance of primitive BCH codes are closely related to
q
-cyclotomic coset modulo
q^{m}-1
. Specifically, the dimension of BCH codes
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)}
can be given by calculating the sum of the sizes of distinct

-cyclotomic cosets

.
Therefore,

-cyclotomic coset

for

, and enumurate distinct cosets correspond to each size. Note that the size of a coset must be a divisor of

However, finding the number of distinct cosets requires more effort.
Yue et al.
[
48

introduced the concept of the coset leader, defined as the smallest integer in each coset. As a result, the number of distinct cosets corresponds exactly to the number of coset leaders.
Moreover, determining the Bose distance of

Therefore, it is not difficult to determine the Bose distance of

Lemma 1
.

[

, Proposition 2.4]

Let

and

be the minimal polynomial of

Remark 1
.

It is well known that the integer
l
divides
m
.

For each integer

-cyclotomic coset
of

C_{a}=\{aq^{k}\ \mathrm{mod}\ n\mid k=0,\ldots,l_{a}-1\},

(1)

where

. It is clear that the size of

is equal to
l_{a}
. The smallest integer in

{C}_{a}
.
We can apply Lemma

to have

m_{a}(x)=(x-\beta^{a})(x-\beta^{aq})\cdots(x-\beta^{aq^{l_{a}-1}})=\prod\limits_{i\in C_{a}}(x-\beta^{i}),

where

is the smallest integer such that
\beta^{aq^{l_{a}}}=\beta^{a}
, equivalently,
aq^{l_{a}}\equiv a\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n).

Then the generator polynomial

of

can be given by

g(x)=\mathrm{lcm}(m_{b}(x),m_{b+1}(x),\ldots,m_{b+\delta-2}(x))=\prod\limits_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(x-\beta^{i})

, where

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)})=n-\left|\bigcup\limits_{a=b}^{b+\delta-2}C_{a}\right|.

Notably, one coset has a unique coset leader.
Therefore,

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)})=n-\left|\bigcup\limits_{a=b}^{b+\delta-2}C_{a}\right|=n-\sum\limits_{a\in\mathcal{L}(b,\delta)}\left|C_{a}\right|,

(2)

where

\bigcup\limits_{a=b}^{b+\delta-2}C_{a}=\bigcup\limits_{a=b}^{b+\delta^{{}^{\prime}}-2}C_{a}

, we have

d_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)})=\delta^{{}^{\prime}}+1-b,

(3)

where
\delta^{{}^{\prime}}
denotes
the smallest coset leader in

.
In particular, the Bose distance of

is equal to the smallest coset leader in
[\delta,n-1]
.
Therefore, we have

d_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)})=d_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,b+\delta-1)})+1-b.

(4)

to denote that the integer

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{a\in[1,n-1]:q\nmid a\hbox{ and }a\hbox{ is not a coset leader}\}.

(5)

.

[

Lemma 3

Let

be a real number. Then

\left|\left\{(a_{m-1},\ldots,a_{0})\in Z_{q}^{m}:\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{m-1}a_{\ell}q^{\ell}\leq t\hbox{ and }a_{0}>0\right\}\right|=N(t+1).

,
we can substitute these terms into equation (

Based on the proof of Theorem

.

Let

be an integer, and let

and

•

If

is even, then

Proof.

If
m
is odd,
recalling equation (

) in the proof of Theorem
3
and noting that
\delta+b\leq q^{\lfloor({2m-1})/{3}\rfloor+1}+1,
we can conclude that

\left|\mathcal{L}(b,\delta)\right|=\left|\mathcal{L}(1,b+\delta-1)\right|-\left|\mathcal{L}(1,b)\right|=\left[N(b+\delta-1)-f(b+\delta-1)\right]-\left[N(b)-f(b)\right].

In addition, we can apply Theorem
1
to conclude that
|C_{a}|=m
for all

. Consequently, the equality in (

) follows.

If
m
is even, since
\delta+b\leq q^{\lfloor({2m-1})/{3}\rfloor+1}+1
, we can apply Theorem
1
to conclude that
|C_{a}|=m
or
{m}/{2}
for any integer
a\in[b,b+\delta+2]
.
Recalling equation (
63
), we can obtain

\left|\mathcal{L}_{m}(b,\delta)\right|=\left|\mathcal{L}_{m}(1,b+\delta-1)\right|-\left|\mathcal{L}_{m}(1,b)\right|=\left[N(b+\delta-1)-\widetilde{f}(b+\delta-1)\right]-\left[N(b)-\widetilde{f}(b)\right]

).

.

.

Let

Note that
m-h=h
when

is odd, and

when

is even. We can apply Lemma
2
to conclude that
\delta
is the smallest coset leader in
[\delta,n]
if

, and
\delta+1
is the smallest coset leader in
[\delta,n]
if

Then the result follows.

Next, we determine the
the Bose distance of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
for

and

by giving the following Theorem

and Theorem

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
m\geq 5
is an odd integer and
\delta\in\left[q^{h+1},q^{\lfloor({2m-1})/{3}\rfloor+1}\right)

be the integer such that

•

If

, then

d_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)})=\begin{cases}\delta,&\hbox{ if }\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}>\sum\limits_{\ell=h-r_{\delta}+1}^{h+j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-r_{\delta}+1)},\\
\hat{\delta}+1,&\hbox{ if }\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=h-r_{\delta}+1}^{h+j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-r_{\delta}+1)}\\
&\hbox{ and }\delta_{h-r_{\delta}+1}\neq q-1,\\
\hat{\delta}+2,&\hbox{ if }\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}\leq\sum\limits_{\ell=h-r_{\delta}+1}^{h+j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-r_{\delta}+1)}\\

\end{cases}

(66)

•

If
q\mid\delta
, then

d_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)})=\begin{cases}\delta+1,&\hbox{ if }\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}\geq\sum\limits_{\ell=h-r_{\delta}+1}^{h+j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-r_{\delta}+1)},\\
\hat{\delta}+1,&\hbox{ if }\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}<\sum\limits_{\ell=h-r_{\delta}+1}^{h+j_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell-(h-r_{\delta}+1)}\\

&\hbox{ and }\delta_{h-r_{\delta}+1}=q-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

We will show that equation (

Case 1.

Suppose that

, and

) holds if

Dimension and Bose distance of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
with
\delta=aq^{h+k}+b
.

As a special case of our main results in the above sections, we give the dimension and Bose distance of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
for
m\geq 4
and
\delta=q^{h+k}+b
with
k\in[m-2h,\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor-h]
,
a\in[1,q-1]
and
b\in\left[1,q^{m-h-k}\right]
in the following corollaries.

Corollary 6
.

Let
m\geq 5
be an odd intger and
k\in[1,\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor-h]
be an integer. If

\delta=aq^{h+k}+b
for some integers
a\in[1,q-1]
and
b\in[1,q^{h-k+1}]
,
then

d_{B}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)})=\begin{cases}\delta,&\hbox{if }b>aq^{2k-1},\\
aq^{h+k}+aq^{2k-1}+1,&\hbox{if }b\leq aq^{2k-1},\end{cases}

(73)

and

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)})=\begin{cases}n-mN(\delta)+ma^{2}(q-1)q^{2k-3}\left[(q-1)k+1\right],&\hbox{if }b>aq^{2k-1},\\
n-maq^{h+k-1}(q-1)+ma(q-1)q^{2k-3}\left[a(q-1)k+a-q\right],&\hbox{if }b\leq aq^{2k-1}.\end{cases}

(74)

Proof.

By applying Theorem
6
, we can directly derive equation (
73
). Therefore, we only demonstrate that (
74
) holds.
It is clear that

V(\delta-1)
has the form

(\mathbb{0}_{h-k},a,\mathbb{0}_{2k-1},\delta_{h-k},\ldots,\delta_{0})

with
\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-k}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}=b-1
.
By definition, it follows that

•

s_{\delta}=k_{\delta}=k
;

•

\mu(\delta)=\min\left\{b-1,aq^{2k-1}\right\}
;

•

\mathcal{T}_{i}(\delta)=\left[q^{k-i},aq^{k-i}-1\right]\cap\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:q\nmid t\}
for each integer
i\in[-k+1,k]
.

Then applying Lemma
5
, we can obtain

\sum\limits_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{i}(\delta)}N(tq^{2i-1}+1)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}-1)(q-1)^{2}q^{2k-3},&\hbox{if }i\in[-k+2,k-1],\\
\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)(q-1)q^{2k-2},&\hbox{if }i=k\hbox{ or }-k+1.\end{cases}

It follows that

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{i=-k_{\delta}+1}^{k_{\delta}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{i}(\delta)}N(tq^{2i-1}+1)&=\sum\limits_{i=-k+1}^{k}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{i}(\delta)}N(tq^{2i-1}+1)\\
&=a(a-1)(q-1)q^{2k-2}+(a^{2}-1)(k-1)(q-1)^{2}q^{2k-3}.\end{split}

By substituting the values of
k_{\delta}
,
\mu(\delta)
and the above expression into (
27
), we obtain

f(\delta)=\begin{cases}a^{2}(q-1)q^{2k-3}\left[k(q-1)+1\right],&\hbox{if }b>aq^{2k-1},\\
a(q-1)q^{2k-3}\left[ak(q-1)+a-q\right]+N(b),&\hbox{if }b\leq aq^{2k-1}.\end{cases}

Finally, noticing that
N(\delta)-N(b)=aq^{h+k-1}(q-1)
, we can apply Theorem
3
to derive the result.
∎

Corollary 7
.

Let
m\geq 4
be an even intger and

(75)

and

\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)})=\begin{cases}n-maq^{h-1}(q-1)+\frac{1}{2}m(a-1)^{2},&\hbox{if }k=0\hbox{ and }b\leq a,\\
n-mN(\delta)+\frac{1}{2}ma^{2},&\hbox{if }k=0\hbox{ and }b>a,\\
n-mN(\delta)+ma^{2}(q-1)q^{2k-2}\left[\left(k-\frac{1}{2})(q-1\right)+q\right],&\hbox{if }k\geq 1\hbox{ and }b>aq^{2k},\\

(76)

Proof.

By applying Theorem
7
, it is easy to derive equation (
75
). Therefore, we only demonstrate that (
76
) holds.
If
k=0
, then we have
\delta-1=aq^{h}+b-1
with
0\leq b-1<q^{h-1}
. It follows that
q^{h}<\delta\leq q^{h+1}
and
\delta_{h}=a
. Consequenlty, we have
s_{\delta}=k_{\delta}=0
, which implies

•

\widetilde{\mu}(\delta)=\min\{b-1,a\}
;

•

\tau(\delta)=1
if
a\leq b-1
, and
\tau(\delta)=0

Thus, by substituting
\delta_{h}=a
, and the values of
\tau(\delta)
and
\widetilde{\mu}(\delta)
for corresponding case into equations (
28
) and (

\widetilde{f}(\delta)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)+N(b),&\hbox{if }k=0\hbox{ and }b\leq a,\\
\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)+N(a+1),&\hbox{if }k=0\hbox{ and }b>a,\end{cases}

g(\delta)=\begin{cases}a-1,&\hbox{if }k=0\hbox{ and }b\leq a,\\
a,&\hbox{if }k=0\hbox{ and }b>a.\end{cases}

k\geq 1

k_{\delta}=k
and
V(\delta-1)
has the form

(\mathbb{0}_{h-k-1},a,\mathbb{0}_{2k},\delta_{h-k-1},\ldots,\delta_{0})

with
\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h-k}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}=b-1

•

\tau(\delta)=0

•

\widetilde{\mu}(\delta)=\min\left\{b-1,aq^{2k}\right\}

\mathcal{T}_{i}(\delta)=\left[q^{k-i},aq^{k-i}-1\right]\cap\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:q\nmid t\}

By substituting the values of

\widetilde{\mu}(\delta)

Finally, by applying Theorem
3

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigate the dimension and Bose distance of primitive BCH codes. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

•

We construct an order-preserving bijective map
V
from the set of all integers in
[0,q^{m}-1]
to
{Z}_{q}^{m}
. This mapping allows each integer
a
to be uniquely identified with a sequence
V(a)\in{Z}_{q}^{m}
. Using this approach, for
m\geq 4
and each integer
k\in[m-2h,\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor-h]
, we partition the integers in
\mathcal{S}\cap[q^{h+k},q^{h+k+1})
(for odd
m
) and
(\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{H})\cap[q^{h+k},q^{h+k+1})
(for even
m
) into disjoint classes via Theorem
2
. Additionally, we determine the size of the
q
-cyclotomic coset
C_{a}
for
a\in[1,q^{m-\lfloor m/3\rfloor})
when
m
is odd, and for
a\in[1,q^{m-\lfloor m/4\rfloor})
when
m
is even.

•

We determine the dimension of narrow-sense primitive BCH codes
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
for
m\geq 4
and
2\leq\delta\leq q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor+1}
by establishing two concise formulas in Theorem
3
, thereby extending the known dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
for
m\geq 4
and
2\leq\delta\leq q^{\lfloor(m+1)/2\rfloor+1}
. In Theorem
4
, these findings are extended to BCH codes
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)}
for all positive integers
m
,
b
, and
\delta
with
m\geq 4
and
\delta+b\leq q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor+1}+1

•

We establish the Bose distance of BCH codes
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
for
m\geq 4
and
2\leq\delta\leq q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor+1}
through Theorems
5
-
7
. Using equation (
4
), these results can also be applied to determine the Bose distance of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta,b)}
for all positive integers
m
,
b
, and
\delta
with
m\geq 4
and
\delta+b\leq q^{\lfloor(2m-1)/3\rfloor+1}+1
.

•

Applying our results, we compute the parameters of several BCH codes, which are either optimal or have the best-known parameters.

As an illustration of our main results, we present the dimension and Bose distance of
\mathcal{C}_{(q,m,\delta)}
for

and

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
4

Proof.

By definition, we first have

\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}N(t+1)=\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}t-\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}\lfloor t/q\rfloor.

(77)

Through direct computation, we obtain

\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}t=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(q^{2}-q),&\hbox{ if }k=0,\\
\frac{1}{2}(q^{2k+2}-q^{2k}-q^{k+1}+q^{k}),&\hbox{ if }k\geq 1.\end{cases}

(78)

Next, we compute the value of
\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}\lfloor t/q\rfloor.

If
k=0
, then
\lfloor t/q\rfloor=0
for all
t\in\left[q^{k},q^{k+1}-1\right]
, and hence

\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}\lfloor t/q\rfloor=0
.
If
k\geq 1,
then

\left[q^{k},q^{k+1}-1\right]
can be partitioned as

\left[q^{k},q^{k+1}-1\right]=\bigsqcup\limits_{i=0}^{q^{k-1}(q-1)-1}\left[q^{k}+iq,q^{k}+(i+1)q-1\right].

Moreover, for each integer
i\in\left[0,q^{k-1}(q-1)-1\right]
and any integer
t\in\left[q^{k}+iq,q^{k}+(i+1)q-1\right]
, we have
\lfloor t/q\rfloor=q^{k-1}+i
. Therefore,

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}\lfloor t/q\rfloor&=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q^{k-1}(q-1)-1}\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}+iq}^{q^{k}+(i+1)q-1}(q^{k-1}+i)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(q^{2k+1}-q^{2k-1}-q^{k+1}+q^{k})\end{split}

for
k\geq 1.

Combining (
77
), (
78
) and the value of
\sum\limits_{t=q^{k}}^{q^{k+1}-1}\lfloor t/q\rfloor
, we obtain the desired equality in (
32
).
∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
5

Proof.

The arguments to obtain the equations (
33
) and (
34
) are similar, so we only demonstrate here that equation (
33
) holds.

If
1\leq i\leq k
, then we have

N(tq^{2i-1}+1)=tq^{2i-2}(q-1)
.
It follows that

\sum\limits_{t=q^{k-i},q\nmid t}^{aq^{k-i}-1}N(tq^{2i-1}+1)=\sum\limits_{t=q^{k-i}}^{aq^{k-i}-1}tq^{2i-2}(q-1)-\sum\limits_{t=\mathcal{W}_{i}}^{aq^{k-i}-1}tq^{2i-2}(q-1),

where
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\left[q^{k-i},q^{k-i+1}-1\right]\cap\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:q\mid t\}
.

If
i=k
, then
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\varnothing
. Consequently,

\sum\limits_{t\in\mathcal{W}_{i}}tq^{2i-2}(q-1)=0\quad\hbox{for }i=k.

If
1\leq i\leq k-1
, then
\mathcal{W}_{i}=\{q^{k-i}+\alpha q:\alpha=0,\ldots,a^{k-i-1}(a-1)-1\}.
Thus,

\begin{split}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathcal{W}_{i}}tq^{2i-2}(q-1)&=\sum\limits_{\alpha=0}^{q^{k-i-1}(a-1)-1}(q^{k-i}+\alpha q)q^{2i-2}(q-1)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}(a-1)(q-1)\left[(a+1)q^{2k-3}-q^{k+i-2}\right].\end{split}

Furthermore, one can easily obtain

\sum\limits_{t=q^{k-i}}^{aq^{k-i}-1}tq^{2i-2}(q-1)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)q^{2k-2}(q-1),&\hbox{if }i=k,\\
\frac{1}{2}(a-1)(q-1)\left[(a+1)q^{2k-2}-q^{k+i-2}\right],&\hbox{if }1\leq i\leq k-1.\end{cases}

Then we can conclude from the above four equations that the equality in (
33
) holds for
1\leq i\leq k.

If
-k+1\leq i\leq 0
, then for any integer
t\in\left[q^{k-i},aq^{k-i}-1\right]\cap\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:q\nmid t\}
with the
q
-adic expansion

\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{k-i}t_{\ell}q^{\ell}
, we have the following observations:

•

N(tq^{2i-1}+1)=N(\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor+1)
;

•

The integer
t
admits the unique decomposition

t=\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor\cdot q^{-2i+1}+\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{-2i}t_{\ell}q^{\ell};

•

As
t
ranges over integers in
\left[q^{k-i},aq^{k-i}-1\right]
not divisible by
q
, the value of
\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor
ranges over integers from
q^{k+i-1}
to
aq^{k+i-1}-1
;

•

For each fixed
\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor
, the summation
\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{-2i}t_{\ell}q^{\ell}
ranges from integers in
[1,q^{-2i+1}-1]
not devisble by
q
, yielding exactly
q^{-2i}(q-1)

distinct integers.

Therefore, for
-k+1\leq i\leq 0
, we have

\sum\limits_{t=q^{k-i},q\nmid t}^{aq^{k-i}-1}N(tq^{2i-1}+1)=\sum\limits_{\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor=q^{k+i-1}}^{aq^{k+i-1}-1}q^{-2i}(q-1)N\left(\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor+1\right).

(79)

In addition, we can use a similar argument to the proof of Lemma
4

to obtain

\sum\limits_{\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor=q^{k+i-1}}^{aq^{k+i-1}-1}N(\lfloor tq^{2i-1}\rfloor+1)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(a^{2}-1)(q-1)q^{2k+2i-3},&\hbox{for }-k+2\leq i\leq 0,\\
\frac{1}{2}a(a-1)q^{2k+2i-2},&\hbox{for }i=-k+1.\end{cases}

Substituting this into equation (
79
), we can conclude that equation (
33
) holds for
-k+1\leq i\leq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix C
Proof of Assertion
1

Proof.

If
m
is odd, our first goal is to show that

\left[\sum\limits_{\ell=h-k_{\delta}+1}^{h+k_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell},\delta-1\right]\cap\mathcal{S}=\left[\sum\limits_{\ell=h-k_{\delta}+1}^{h+k_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell},\delta-1\right]\cap\mathcal{A}_{k_{\delta}}(s_{\delta}).

(80)

Recall that
\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{h+k_{\delta}}\delta_{\ell}q^{\ell}
is the
q
-adic expansion of
\delta-1
. We have

V(\delta-1)=(\mathbf{0}_{h-k_{\delta}},\delta_{h+k_{\delta}},\ldots,\delta_{0})

and

Global existence of weak solutions to a two-phase diffuse interface model of ferrofluids dynamics Thanks: F.W. was supported in part by NSF grant DMS-2438083.
Samuel Lanthaler, Franziska Weber

Abstract
. Ferrofluids are a class of materials that exhibit both fluid and magnetic properties. We consider a two-phase diffuse interface model for the dynamics of ferrofluids on a bounded domain. One phase is assumed to be magnetic, the other phase can be magnetic or non-magnetic. We derive a coupled system of partial differential equations consisting of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, an evolution equation for the magnetization, the magnetostatics equations for the magnetic field and the Cahn-Hilliard equations for the evolution of the phase field variable, which are all coupled through various source terms and parameters. In contrast to similar models in the literature, the system in this work formally satisfies an energy balance which remains meaningful even in singular limits such as a limit of zero relaxation time. However, the formal derivation of this balance requires a delicate cancellation of several highly non-linear terms, making it challenging to ensure similar cancellations for approximating systems. Our first main result is to prove the existence of global weak solutions for our ferrofluid system based on a carefully constructed sequence of approximation steps. Additionally, we also study the relaxation towards the quasi-equilibrium, in which case the magnetization equation degenerates to a linear relation between the magnetic field and the magnetization. As our second main result, we prove the rigorous convergence to this limiting system.



1.
Introduction

Ferrofluids, also known as magnetic liquids, are a unique class of materials that exhibit both fluid and magnetic properties. They are colloidal suspensions of nanoscale ferromagnetic particles, typically iron oxides like magnetite (
Fe_{3}O_{4}
) or maghemite (
\gamma-Fe_{2}O_{3}
), coated with a surfactant and dispersed in a liquid carrier such as water or oil. This composition prevents the aggregation of the magnetic nanoparticles and allows the fluid to become strongly magnetized in the presence of an external magnetic field
[
17
,
16
]
. This ability to be remotely manipulated by magnetic fields has led to a diverse range of technological and biomedical applications, from enhancing the performance of loudspeakers and acting as liquid seals in hard drives to targeted drug delivery and cancer hyperthermia
[
4
,
21
,
25
,
20
]
.

The fascinating and complex behavior of ferrofluids, particularly the dynamics of their free surface under the influence of a magnetic field, has motivated the development of sophisticated mathematical models to describe and predict their motion. The first such mathematical models for single-phase flows of ferrofluids
are due to Rosensweig
[
24
,
23
,
10
]
and Shliomis
[
27
]
.
These models are crucial for optimizing existing applications and for the design of new technologies. In this paper, we are concerned with two-phase flows of ferrofluids or a ferrofluid and a non-magnetic fluid. Among the various theoretical frameworks to approach multi-phase flows, diffuse interface models have emerged as a powerful tool. Unlike sharp interface models that treat the boundary between the ferrofluid and its surrounding medium as a distinct surface, diffuse interface models consider a thin transition layer where the properties of the two phases change smoothly. This approach is particularly well-suited for capturing topological changes of the interface, such as droplet coalescence or breakup, which are common in many ferrofluid applications.

This paper focuses on the formulation and analysis of a two-phase flow model of ferrofluids based on a simplified version of the Rosensweig and Shliomis mathematical model for ferrofluids within a diffuse interface framework. The model couples the Navier-Stokes equations, which govern the fluid flow, with a Cahn-Hilliard type equation to describe the evolution of the phase-field variable that distinguishes the ferrofluid from the ambient fluid. Furthermore, an equation for the evolution of the magnetization within the ferrofluid is included to account for the magnetic body forces.
This results in the following diffuse interface ferrofluids model:

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}(\nu_{\theta}T(\mathbf{u}))+\mu_{0}(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}-\varepsilon\lambda\Delta\theta\nabla\theta,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}(\mathbf{m}-\chi_{\theta}\mathbf{h}),

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{h}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{h})=0,

(1.1e)

\displaystyle\theta_{t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\theta

\displaystyle=\kappa\Delta\psi,

(1.1f)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\lambda\Delta\theta+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\chi^{\prime}_{\theta}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}.

Here
\mathbf{u}
is the velocity of the fluid,
\mathbf{m}
is the magnetization,
\mathbf{h}
is the magnetic field and
\theta
is the phase field variable which is equal to
1
when in the magnetic fluid and equal to
-1
when in the non-magnetic fluid.
\psi
is called the chemical potential in this setting. A formal derivation of the system (
1.1
), based on Onsager’s variational principle
[
18
,
19
]
, is included in Appendix
A
.

We continue to explain our notation in further detail. First,

(1.2)

T(\mathbf{u}):=\frac{\nabla\mathbf{u}+(\nabla\mathbf{u})^{\top}}{2},

is the symmetric part of the gradient.
The function
F
is a truncated double well potential, we choose

F(\theta)=\begin{cases}(\theta+1)^{2},\quad\theta\in(-\infty,-1),\\
\frac{1}{4}(1-\theta^{2})^{2},\quad\theta\in[-1,1],\\
(\theta-1)^{2},\quad\theta\in(1,\infty).\end{cases}

Clearly,
F
satisfies

(1.3)

|F^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq 2|\theta|+1,\quad|F^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\leq 2.

The functions
\nu_{\theta}
and
\chi_{\theta}
are the viscosity and susceptibility depending on the phase field variable
\theta
. We assume they are Lipschitz continuous and satisfy,

(1.4)

0<\min\{\nu_{f},\nu_{w}\}\leq\nu_{\theta}\leq\max\{\nu_{f},\nu_{w}\},\quad 0\leq\chi_{\theta}\leq\chi_{0},

where
\nu_{f}
is the viscosity of the ferrofluid and
\nu_{w}
the viscosity of the non-magnetic phase and
\chi_{0}
the susceptibility of the ferrofluid. We set the susceptiblity of the non-magnetic phase to be zero. They can be chosen in various ways, for example as

(1.5)

\nu_{\theta}=\nu_{w}+(\nu_{f}-\nu_{w})\mathcal{H}(\theta/\varepsilon),\quad\chi_{\theta}=\chi_{0}\mathcal{H}(\theta/\varepsilon),

where
\mathcal{H}
is a regularization of the Heavyside function. To be definite, we will focus on the following choice:

(1.6)

\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(x)+\frac{1}{2}.

We point out that our analysis only uses certain decay properties of the tails of
\mathcal{H}(x)
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(x)
as
x\to\pm\infty
. Various other choices for
\mathcal{H}
are also possible.

The parameters

\mu_{0},\tau,\varepsilon,\lambda,\kappa>0
are fixed coefficients:
\tau
is the relaxation time,
\lambda
the surface tension,
\varepsilon
is the interface thickness,
\kappa
is the mobility, and
\mu_{0}
is the magnetic constant.
We assume that the domain
D
is bounded and has a smooth boundary. Additionally, we assume that the domain is simply-connected, in which case we obtain from (
1.1d
) that
\mathbf{h}
is the gradient of a potential, i.e.,
\mathbf{h}=\nabla\varphi
and so (
1.1d
) becomes

(1.7)

\mathbf{h}=\nabla\varphi,\quad-\Delta\varphi=\operatorname{div}\mathbf{m}.

As in
[
14
,
11
,
12
]
, we assume that
\mathbf{h}
is composed of a demagnetizing field
\mathbf{h}_{d}
and an applied field
\mathbf{h}_{a}
,

(1.8)

\mathbf{h}=\mathbf{h}_{d}+\mathbf{h}_{a},

where
\mathbf{h}_{a}
is a given smooth harmonic field (not perturbed through
\mathbf{h}_{d}
and
\mathbf{m}
) and that
\mathbf{m}=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D
and
\mathbf{h}_{d}\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash D
. Assuming additionally that the wall of the domain is impermeable, this results in the following boundary conditions:

(1.9a)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=0,\quad x\in\partial D,\,t\geq 0,

(1.9b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}=(\mathbf{h}_{a}-\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathbf{n},\quad\int_{D}\varphi(t,x)dx=0,\quad x\in\partial D,\,t\geq 0,

(1.9c)

\displaystyle\nabla\theta\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\quad x\in\partial D,\,t\geq 0,

(1.9d)

\displaystyle\nabla\psi\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\quad x\in\partial D,\,t\geq 0.

The second boundary condition (
1.9b
) is equivalent to

(\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{h})\cdot\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{n},\quad x\in\partial D,\,t\geq 0.

This is the model derived and studied in
[
11
]
with the exception of the last term on the right hand side in the equation for
\psi
. We will show in the following sketch of a derivation of the system in Appendix
A
how it emerges. In contrast to
[
11
]
, we chose to include it here, because we believe it is necessary for the study of the zero relaxation limit
\tau\to 0
. The zero relaxation limit has practical relevance since in applications
\tau
is often small, for example
[
22
,
27
]
states that it is of order
10^{-9}-10^{-4}s
. Furthermore, the zero relaxation system, the so-called quasi-equilibrium, is simpler and therefore easier to simulate numerically. Without the additional term, the formal energy balance for the system (
1.1
)

(1.10)

\int_{D}E(\mathcal{U})(t)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})(s)\,dxds=\int_{D}E(\mathcal{U})(0)dx+\mu_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\partial D}\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\varphi\,dSds.

where the energy
E
is defined by

(1.11)

E(\mathcal{U})=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\varepsilon\lambda|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}+\mu_{0}|\mathbf{h}|^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{\varepsilon}F(\theta)\right),

and the dissipation functional
\mathcal{D}
is defined by

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})=\left(\nu_{\theta}|T(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\kappa|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\tau\chi_{\theta}}|\mathbf{m}-\chi_{\theta}\mathbf{h}|^{2}\right),

does not include the relaxation term
\int\frac{\mu_{0}}{\tau\chi_{\theta}}|\mathbf{m}-\chi_{\theta}\mathbf{h}|^{2}dx
which is needed to send
\tau\to 0
in a stable manner and conclude that
\mathbf{m}=\chi_{\theta}\mathbf{h}
, i.e., the magnetization becomes parallel to the magnetic field when
\tau=0
, c.f. Section
4
. In contrast, the only previously known energy balance from
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
is of the form

(1.12)

\int_{D}\widetilde{E}(\mathcal{U})(t)dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{U})(s)\,dxds\leq\int_{D}\widetilde{E}(\mathcal{U})(0)dx+\mu_{0}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\tau\left\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}_{a}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{h}_{a}(s)_{L^{2}}^{2}\right\|\right)ds,

where
\widetilde{E}
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
are given by

\widetilde{E}(\mathcal{U})=\frac{1}{2}\left(|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\lambda|\nabla\theta|^{2}+{\mu_{0}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}+\mu_{0}|\mathbf{h}|^{2}+\frac{2\lambda}{\varepsilon^{2}}F(\theta)\right),

and

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{U})=\nu_{\theta}|T(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\frac{\kappa\lambda}{\varepsilon}|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\tau}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{0}}{4}\right)|\mathbf{m}|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\tau}|\mathbf{h}|^{2},

which would require additional assumptions on
\mathbf{h}_{a}
in order to send
\tau\to 0
due to the different source term on the right hand side of (
1.12
).
We also note that the division by
\chi_{\theta}
in the magnetization energy density in (
1.11
) heuristically forces
\mathbf{m}
to be small when
\chi_{\theta}
is small as it is the case if the fluid is mostly consisting of the non-magnetic phase. However, the additional term

\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\chi^{\prime}_{\theta}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}

causes substantial challenges in the analysis of the system (
1.1
) due to its nonlinearlity. The main purpose of this work is to overcome these technical challenges. As a result, we prove the existence of global weak solutions of the system (
1.1
) using a Galerkin approximation scheme. We first approximate
\chi_{\theta}
by a nonvanishing function
\chi_{\theta}^{\delta}
. Then we introduce three Galerkin truncations for
\mathbf{u}
,
\theta
and
\mathbf{m}
respectively that we send to infinity at different points in the approximation scheme, which is necessary to handle the nonlinearities of the system and obtain an approximate energy balance similar to the one expected for the system, (
1.10
). Finally, we send the approximation
\delta>0
in the susceptibility,
\chi_{\theta}
to zero to obtain the one in (
1.5
), (
1.6
). In order to deal with the weak a priori regularity for the variables
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{h}
expected from the energy balance, i.e.,
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{h}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
and derive compactness of the approximating sequences, we adapt an argument from
[
14
]
using renormalized solutions to our setting to obtain compactness of those approximating sequences in
L^{2}([0,T]\times D)
. We use a similar argument to prove convergence of solutions of (
1.1
) to solutions of the zero relaxation system, which results when setting
\tau=0
formally in (
1.1
).

1.1.
Related works

As already mentioned above, the work most closely related to this is
[
11
]
where the diffuse interface model for ferrofluids is derived (with the exception of the last term in equation (
1.1f
), as explained above). The same article proposes a stable fully discrete numerical scheme and simulations of the Rosensweig instability. Furthermore, they present a convergence proof for the case that
\mathbf{h}
is a given function (i.e. remove equation (
1.1d
) above), which implies existence of global weak solutions for that case. Later works are concerned with the design of linearly-implicit energy stable schemes for similar two-phase flow models
[
8
,
32
,
33
]
, however, these works do not provide convergence proofs for their schemes and therefore do not yield proofs of existence of weak solutions of the corresponding models.
Other mathematical works are dedicated to single-phase flows of ferrofluids. In
[
14
]
global existence of weak solutions and relaxation to the equilibrium is shown for the single-phase Rosensweig model of ferrohydrodynamics. In
[
12
]
, a stable numerical scheme is designed for the single-phase flow Rosensweig system. Other analysis works such as
[
3
,
26
]
consider regularized systems where an additional diffusion term is added in the magnetization equation (
1.1c
). However, it is unclear if this term can be justified from the physics of ferrofluids and it does not appear in the original works by Rosensweig and Shliomis either. The existence of local-in-time strong solutions of the single-phase Shliomis model without additional regularization was proved in
[
1
]
and the existence of local-in-time strong solutions of the single-phase Rosensweig model was proved in
[
2
]
.
Therefore, we believe our work is the first rigorous study of global weak solutions for a two-phase flow ferrofluid model without additional regularization terms.

Main results

2.1.
Notation and definition of global weak solutions

We start by introducing necessary notation: We denote by
L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
the space of divergence free
L^{2}(D)
functions and by
H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
the space of divergence free functions in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
(these can be obtained as the closures of
C^{\infty}_{0}(D)\cap\{\operatorname{div}u=0\}
in
L^{2}(D)
and

respectively (c.f.
[

\begin{split}L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)&=\{u\in L^{2}(D);\,\operatorname{div}u=0,\,\text{ a.e. in }\,D,\,u\cdot n|_{\partial D}=0\},\\
H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)&=\{u\in H^{1}(D);\,\operatorname{div}u=0,\,\text{ a.e. in }\,D,\,u|_{\partial D}=0\}.\end{split}

For a Banach space
X
we let
C_{w}(0,T;X)
be the space of functions that are weakly continuous in time, that is, if
v\in C_{w}(0,T;X)
, then for any
s\to t
,

\langle v(s),g\rangle\to\langle v(t),g\rangle,\quad\text{for }s\to t,\quad\forall\,g\in X^{*},

where we denoted by
X^{*}
the dual space of
X
and by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the dual product on
X,X^{*}
.
We use
(\cdot,\cdot)
to denote the
L^{2}(D)
-inner product,

(f,g):=\int_{D}f(x)\cdot g(x)dx.

Now we can define a notion of weak solution for the diffuse interface ferrofluids system (
1.1
):

Definition 2.1

(Distributional solution of (
1.1
))
.

Let
T>0
,
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, a smooth, simply connected domain.
\mathcal{U}:=(\mathbf{u},\theta,\mathbf{m},\mathbf{h},\psi)
is a global weak solution of the system (
1.1
) if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

Regularity.
The solution
\mathcal{U}:=(\mathbf{u},\theta,\mathbf{m},\mathbf{h},\psi)
satisfies the regularity requirements:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\cap C_{w}(0,T;L^{2}(D))

\displaystyle\theta

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(D))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(D))

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))\cap C_{w}(0,T;L^{2}(D))

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))\cap C_{w}(0,T;L^{2}(D))

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(D));

(ii)

Magnetostatics equations.
The function
\mathbf{h}
is such that
\mathbf{h}=\nabla\varphi
, where
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(D))
and satisfies for all
\phi\in H^{1}(D)
, and a.e.
t\in[0,T]
,

(2.1)

\int_{D}(\nabla\varphi+\mathbf{m})\cdot\nabla\phi dx=\int_{\partial D}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\phi\,dS,

with
\int_{D}\varphi dx=0
.

(iii)

Weak form of evolution equations.
Equations (
1.1a
)–(
1.1f
) hold weakly, that is for any test functions
v_{i}\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T)\times D)

i=1,\dots,5
with vanishing trace on
\partial D
and
\operatorname{div}v_{1}(t,x)=0
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times D
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\left[\mathbf{u}\cdot\partial_{t}v_{1}+((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)v_{1})\cdot\mathbf{u}-\nu_{\theta}T(\mathbf{u}):T(v_{1})\right]dxdt+\int_{D}\mathbf{u}_{0}\cdot v_{1}(0,x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\left[\mu_{0}\left(\left(((\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{h})\cdot\nabla)v_{1}\right)\cdot\mathbf{h}\right)-\varepsilon\lambda(\nabla\theta\otimes\nabla\theta):\nabla v_{1}\right]dxdt

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\mathbf{u}(t,x)\cdot\nabla v_{2}(t,x)dx=0,\quad\text{a.e. }t\in[0,T]

(2.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\left[\mathbf{m}\cdot\partial_{t}v_{3}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)v_{3}\cdot\mathbf{m}\right]dxdt+\int_{D}\mathbf{m}_{0}\cdot v_{3}(0,x)dx=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\left[\frac{1}{\tau}(\mathbf{m}-\kappa_{\theta}\mathbf{h})\cdot v_{3}\right]dxdt

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\left[\theta\cdot\partial_{t}v_{4}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)v_{4}\cdot\theta\right]dxdt+\int_{D}\theta_{0}v_{4}(0,x)dx=\kappa\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla v_{4}dxdt

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\psi v_{5}dxdt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{D}\!\left[\varepsilon\lambda\nabla\theta\cdot\nabla v_{5}+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)v_{5}-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}v_{5}\right]dxdt.

and the dissipation functional

\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{U})=\left(\nu_{\theta}|T(\mathbf{u})|^{2}+\kappa|\nabla\psi|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\tau\chi_{\theta}}|\mathbf{m}-\chi_{\theta}\mathbf{h}|^{2}\right).

2.2.
Main theorems

The main result of this work is the following existence theorem for global weak solutions to the system (
1.1
):

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T>0
be a given final time. Assume that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
,
\theta_{0}\in H^{1}(D)
,
\mathbf{m}_{0}\in L^{p}(D)
for
p>12/5
and
\mathbf{h}_{a}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial D))
. Assume furthermore that the viscosity
\nu_{\theta}
satisfies (
1.4
) and the susceptibility
\chi_{\theta}
is given by (
1.5
). Assume that
F
is smooth and that
F^{\prime}
grows at most linearly for large
\theta
, i.e.,
|F^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C(1+|\theta|)
for some
C>0
. Then there exists a weak solution of (
1.1
), in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

We will prove this theorem using a sequence of approximation steps, detailed in Section
3
. First, since the equations as well as the energy involve divisions by
\chi_{\theta}
, c.f. equation (
1.1f
), we regularize
\chi_{\theta}
by a parameter
\delta
to make it uniformly positive. Then we show existence of weak solutions under this uniform positivity assumption for
\chi_{\theta}
using Galerkin truncations for the variables
\mathbf{u}
,
\theta
and
\mathbf{m}
.

The main technical difficulty is that, even though the continuous system (
1.1
) formally satisfies the energy balance (
2.7
) which would imply boundedness and compactness properties of the system, deriving this balance rigorously requires an exact cancellation of several highly nonlinear terms. We have not been able to find a discretization scheme which respects this structure, and hence no similar energy balance is available at the discretized level.

To circumvent this core difficulty, we instead consecutively send our Galerkin truncations to infinity. Each time a limit is taken, this allows us to obtain improved estimates because one or more of the equations in the PDE system (
1.1
) is now satisfied at the continuous level, implying exact cancellation of relevant nonlinear terms. We start with the limit for the approximation in
\mathbf{m}
, based on which we derive an approximate energy inequality for the limiting system. In turn, this gives sufficient control to send the Galerkin truncation for
\theta
to infinity. Finally, we pass to the limit in the approximation for
\mathbf{u}
to obtain a weak solution of the system in the case that
\chi_{\theta}

2.3.1.
Energy inequality

Since
\mathbf{h}_{a}
is harmonic, we can rewrite the last term
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\partial D}\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\varphi dSds
in the energy inequality (
2.7
) using Gauss’ divergence theorem

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\partial D}\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\varphi dSds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\operatorname{div}(\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\varphi)dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}(\operatorname{div}(\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a})\varphi+\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\nabla\varphi)dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\nabla\varphi dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{h}dxds.

Then, we can use Young’s inequality to bound the term as

\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\partial D}\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\varphi dSds\right|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathbf{h}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds+\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\partial_{s}\mathbf{h}_{a}(s)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds.

Next, we use
Grönwall’s inequality in (
2.7
), to obtain the regularity for the solution required in
2.1
(i) after using that
\theta
and
\psi
have bounded mass and Korn’s inequality
[
9
]
for the symmetric gradient. In the same way, we can rewrite the weak formulation (
2.1
) as

(2.11)

\int_{D}(\nabla\varphi+\mathbf{m})\cdot\nabla\phi dx=\int_{D}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx.

Remark 2.4

For smooth solutions, the energy inequality (
2.7
) in fact becomes an equality. It can be obtained by taking the inner product of equation (
1.1a

\mathbf{u}
, equation (

, equation (

2.3.2.
Kelvin force

The term
\mu_{0}(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}
in (
1.1a
), (
1.13a
) is called Kelvin force. It can be rewritten as follows using equation (
1.1d
),

Lemma 2.5
.

[
14
, Lemma 2.3]

Any sufficiently smooth solution of (
1.1
) satisfies

(2.12)

(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}=\operatorname{div}\left((\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{h})\otimes\mathbf{h}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\left(|\mathbf{h}|^{2}\right).

The right hand side is the version that is used in the definition of weak solutions since the regularity of the weak solution does not allow for derivatives of
\mathbf{h}
.

2.3.3.
Capillary force

The capillary force term
\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta
in (
1.1a
) can be (formally) rewritten in several ways, using (
1.1f
). First, we use chain rule to rewrite it as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{jj}\theta\partial_{i}\theta\right)_{i=1}^{d}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{j}(\partial_{j}\theta\partial_{i}\theta)-\partial_{j}\theta\partial_{j}\partial_{i}\theta\right)\right)_{i=1}^{d}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\partial_{j}(\partial_{j}\theta\partial_{i}\theta)-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}|\partial_{j}\theta|^{2}\right)\right)_{i=1}^{d}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\operatorname{div}(\nabla\theta\otimes\nabla\theta)-\frac{1}{2}\nabla|\nabla\theta|^{2}.

Testing this with a divergence free test function and integrating by parts, we obtain the expression used in (
2.2
). On the other hand, replacing
\Delta\theta
by (
1.1f
), we calculate

\displaystyle\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta

\displaystyle=-\psi\nabla\theta+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)\nabla\theta-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\nabla\theta

\displaystyle=-\nabla(\psi\theta)+\nabla\psi\theta+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\nabla F(\theta)+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\nabla\left(\frac{1}{\chi_{\theta}}\right).

The first and the third term can be absorbed into the pressure since we are using divergence free test functions and so the capillary force term in (
1.1a
) can also be replaced by

\nabla\psi\theta+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\nabla\left(\frac{1}{\chi_{\theta}}\right).

3.

We note that the equations (

3.1.
Existence of global weak solutions for non-vanishing susceptibility

In order to show existence of global weak solutions of (

\chi
is smooth and satisfies the following properties:

(3.1)

0<\delta\leq\chi_{\theta}\leq\chi_{0}<\infty,\quad|\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}|\leq C<\infty,\quad\lim_{\theta\to\pm\infty}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}=0,\quad\lim_{\theta\to\pm\infty}|\theta\chi^{\prime}_{\theta}|\leq C<\infty.

For example, this is achieved by setting

(3.2)

\chi_{\theta}=\delta+(\chi_{0}-\delta)\mathcal{H}(\theta/\varepsilon),

for some
0<\delta<\chi_{0}
where
\mathcal{H}
is our regularization of the Heavyside function (
1.6
). For relevant estimates that imply (
3.1
), we refer to the discussion following the statement of Proposition
3.1
, below.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T>0
be a given final time. Assume that
\mathbf{u}_{0}\in L^{2}_{\operatorname{div}}(D)
,
\mathbf{m}_{0}\in L^{2}(D)
,
\theta_{0}\in H^{1}(D)
and
\mathbf{h}_{a}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial D))
. Assume furthermore that the viscosity
\nu_{\theta}
satisfies (
1.4
) and the susceptibility
\chi_{\theta}
satisfies (
3.1
). Assume that
F
is smooth and that
F^{\prime}
grows at most linearly for large
\theta
, i.e.,
|F^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C(1+|\theta|)
for some
C>0
. Then, there exists a weak solution of (
1.1
) as in the sense of Definition
2.1
.

The only difference between Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
2.2
are the uniform bounds (
3.1
).
We will prove this proposition using a sequence of approximation steps in the next couple of subsections. Before coming to the proof, we briefly discuss the relevance of the properties (
3.1
).

3.1.1.
Estimates for the Heavyside approximation
\mathcal{H}

We now consider our prototypical choice (
1.6
), i.e.,
\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(x)+\frac{1}{2}
,
with the aim of deriving relevant estimates on
\chi^{\delta}_{\theta}=\delta+(\chi_{0}-\delta)\mathcal{H}(\theta/\epsilon)
. Then we have:

Lemma 3.2
.

Consider
\chi_{\theta}=\chi^{\delta}_{\theta}
, given by (
3.2
). There exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\delta>0
, such that the properties in (
3.1
) hold. Furthermore,
\chi_{\theta}=\chi^{\delta}_{\theta}
also satisfies the following bounds,

(3.3)

\left|\frac{\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta}}\right|\leq C,\qquad\left|\frac{\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}\theta}{\chi_{\theta}}\right|\leq C,\quad\left|\frac{\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\right|\leq C\quad\forall\theta\in\mathbb{R}.

uniformly in
\delta>0
.

Proof.

First, we note that
\mathcal{H}
is bounded,
0\leq\mathcal{H}(x)\leq 1
, and therefore we have
\delta\leq\chi_{\theta}\leq\chi_{0}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}(x)
has asymptotics
\mathcal{H}(x)\to 1
as
x\to\infty
and

(3.4)

\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan(x)+\frac{1}{2}=-\frac{1}{\pi x}+O\left(\frac{1}{x^{3}}\right),\quad x\to-\infty.

We also note that

(3.5)

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{1}{1+x^{2}}.

The last identity (
3.5
) and the definition of
\chi_{\theta}=\chi^{\delta}_{\theta}
yields

|\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{\chi_{0}}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\theta/\epsilon)=\frac{\chi_{0}}{\pi\varepsilon\left[1+(\theta/\varepsilon)^{2}\right]},

where the right-hand side is independent of
\delta
. This immediately implies the properties (
3.1
). Similarly, we see that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(\chi_{\theta})^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left[1+(\theta/\varepsilon)^{2}\right]}\frac{1}{\left[\arctan(\theta/\varepsilon)+\pi/2\right]},

is uniformly bounded by the function on the right-hand side for any
\delta>0
. Furthermore, we observe that
(\chi_{\theta})^{\prime}/\chi_{\theta}\to 0
as
\theta\to+\infty
, and

\left|\frac{(\chi_{\theta})^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left[1+(\theta/\varepsilon)^{2}\right]}\frac{1}{\left[\varepsilon/|\theta|+O(\varepsilon^{3}/|\theta|^{3})\right]}=O\left(\frac{1}{|\theta|}\right)\to 0,\quad\text{as }\theta\to-\infty.

This implies the second estimate in (
3.3
).
For the last estimate in (
3.3
), we calculate,

\left|\frac{(\chi_{\theta})^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\left[1+(\theta/\varepsilon)^{2}\right]}\frac{1}{\left[\varepsilon/|\theta|+O(\varepsilon^{3}/|\theta|^{3})\right]^{2}}=O\left(1\right),\quad\text{as }\,\theta\to-\infty,

whereas if
\theta>0
, since
\chi_{\theta}
is increasing, and
\chi^{\prime}_{\theta}
is bounded, this is clearly satisfied.
This concludes our proof.
∎

3.1.2.
Detailed outline of the proof of Proposition

As pointed out in the introduction, our existence proof relies on a Galerkin truncation of the system (

,
\theta

\mathbf{u}
to infinity. More precisely, our existence proof involves the following steps:

•

In subsection

,

\mathbf{u}
, leading to a discretized system of ODEs. This Galerkin truncation is described by parameters

,

,

\mathbf{m}
,
\theta
and
\mathbf{u}
, respectively.

•

In subsection

, we first show global-in-time existence of solutions to the discretized system with

fixed. This will result in estimates that are uniform in

(but depending on

,

•

In subsection

•

Then, in subsection

we analyze the limiting system, now only involving truncation parameters

,

•

Finally, in subsection

The details of this derivation are provided below.

3.1.3.

\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
,

\int_{D}|\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx=-\int_{D}\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx+\int_{\partial D}\mathbf{h}_{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dS.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz and the trace inequality, we can estimate the right hand side by

\begin{split}\int_{D}|\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx&\leq\int_{D}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{D}|\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx+C(D)^{2}\int_{\partial D}|\mathbf{h}_{a}|^{2}dS\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{4}\int_{D}|\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Rearranging terms, we obtain

(3.8)

\int_{D}|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx=\int_{D}|\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx\leq 2\int_{D}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}dx+2C(D)^{2}\int_{\partial D}|\mathbf{h}_{a}|^{2}dS,

thus if we can bound
\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
, a bound on
\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
in
L^{2}
automatically follows. Going back to (
3.7
), and using that the convolution of a function satisfies
\left\|(f)_{\eta}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}}
for all
p\geq 1
, we find

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{D}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t,x)|^{2}dx&\leq-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{D}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t,x)|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{D}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t,x)|^{2}dx\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2\tau}\int_{D}|(\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})_{\eta}|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C(\chi_{0},\tau)\int_{D}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t,x)|^{2}dx+C(\chi_{0},\tau,D)\int_{\partial D}|\mathbf{h}_{a}|^{2}dS.\end{split}

Thus, we obtain from Grönwall’s inequality

(3.9)

\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}\leq\left\|\mathbf{m}_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}(D)}^{2}e^{Ct},

therefore, since the initial data
\mathbf{m}_{0}
is in
L^{2}(D)
and
\mathbf{h}_{a}\in W^{1,\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\partial D))
, then
\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}
will be bounded uniformly in
N_{m}
,
N_{u}
and
N_{\theta}
. From the bound (
3.8
), we conclude that also
\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}
is uniformly bounded in
N_{m}
,
N_{u}
and
N_{\theta}
.
We continue to collect additional estimates.
We use
z=\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as a test function in (
3.6e
) to obtain (using (
3.6f
) with
z=\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as a test function)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\kappa\lambda\varepsilon(\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}},\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})\\
+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{\varepsilon}(F^{\prime}(\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}),\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})-\frac{\kappa\mu_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2},\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right).

We recall that
F^{\prime}
satisfies
|F^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq C(1+|\theta|)
.
We use Young’s inequality to estimate the second term and obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq-\frac{\kappa\lambda\varepsilon}{2}\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\lambda\kappa}{2\varepsilon^{3}}\left\|F^{\prime}(\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{\kappa\mu_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2},\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right)\\
&\leq-\frac{\kappa\lambda\varepsilon}{2}\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C\lambda\kappa}{2\varepsilon^{3}}(1+\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
&\quad-\frac{\kappa\mu_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2},\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right)\end{split}

Using the boundary condition for
\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
, we integrate by parts twice in the first term on the right hand side to obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{\kappa\lambda\varepsilon}{2}\left\|\nabla^{2}\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C\lambda\kappa}{2\varepsilon^{3}}(1+\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
-\frac{\kappa\mu_{0}}{2}\left(\frac{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{\prime}}{\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2},\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right)

Since we are currently not interested in sending
\varepsilon\to 0
and keep all other parameters fixed, we will lump all of them together in the constant
C
. Next, we use that
\chi
and its derivatives are bounded
(see equation (
3.1
)) to estimate the last term on the right hand side

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{\kappa\lambda\varepsilon}{2}\left\|\nabla^{2}\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

We have already shown in (
3.9
) that
\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
uniformly in
N_{i}
, thus, we can further estimate the right hand side

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{\kappa\lambda\varepsilon}{2}\left\|\nabla^{2}\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Next, we use the fact that
\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
is expanded in a finite-dimensional smooth Galerkin basis. In particular, there exists
\Lambda_{N_{\theta}}>0
depending only on
N_{\theta}
, such that
\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\Lambda_{N_{\theta}}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}
. Plugging this back into the above bound, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+\Lambda_{N_{\theta}})(1+\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Using Grönwall’s inequality we first obtain a bound on
\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and using once more the finite-dimensionality and smoothness of the Galerkin expansion to bound second derivatives in terms of the
L^{2}
-norm, it follows that

(3.10)

\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla^{2}\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C({N_{\theta}},\left\|\theta_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}},T),\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

where
\theta_{0}
is the initial condition of (
1.1e
). To clarify, the bound on the second-order derivatives is a consequence of the bound on the
L^{2}
-norm, but we state it here explicitly for later reference.
Thus, we obtained a bound on
\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}
and
\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(D))}
that is independent of
N_{u}
and
N_{m}
(but depends on
N_{\theta}
).

Next, we derive a bound for
\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
. To do so,
we use
v=\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as a test function in (
3.6a
) to obtain

(3.11)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&=-\int_{D}\nu_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}|T(\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})|^{2}dx+\mu_{0}\int_{D}(\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx\\
&\quad-\varepsilon\lambda\int_{D}\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\nabla\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx\end{split}

We will use the following crude estimates for the last two terms on the right hand side:

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{0}\int_{D}(\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mu_{0}\int_{D}((\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}+\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u})\left(\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u})\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u})^{2}+\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where for the first line, we used
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
, c.f., (
2.12
), and for the second to last inequality, we used the previously established estimate (
3.9
). For the remaining term, we estimate

\displaystyle\left|\varepsilon\lambda\int_{D}\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\nabla\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u})\sqrt{\lambda_{N_{\theta}}}\left\|\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u},N_{\theta})+\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used the estimate (
3.10
) for the last inequality. Substituting these back into (
3.11
), we obtain,

(3.12)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{D}\nu_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}|T(\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})|^{2}dx\leq 2\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(N_{u},N_{\theta}).

Now applying Grönwall’s inequality and Korn’s inequality
[
9
]
, we obtain

(3.13)

\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}(0,t;L^{2}(D))}^{2}\leq C(N_{\theta},N_{u},T)\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Thus, estimates (
3.9
), (
3.8
), (
3.13
) and (
3.10
) imply that the solution of (
3.6
) exists for all times
t\in[0,T]
. We note that this estimates are uniform with respect to
N_{m}
(and
\eta
) but not with respect to
N_{u}
and
N_{\theta}
. We will next send
N_{m}\to\infty
to derive better a priori estimates. But first, we note that we can estimate the derivatives of
\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as follows:
We take
w=-\Delta\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as a test function in (
3.6b
). Integrating the derivatives by part and using the boundary conditions, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{D}\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\nabla(\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})_{\eta}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{D}((\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx

We estimate the second term on the right by (recall that
\eta=1/N_{\theta}
),

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{D}\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\nabla(\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})_{\eta}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla(\chi_{\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}\nabla\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})_{\eta}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\chi_{0}}{\tau}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\chi_{0}}{\tau}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(N_{\theta})\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

For the third term, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D}((\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})\cdot\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\nabla\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u})\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(N_{u},N_{\theta},T)\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

by the previous estimate (
3.13
). Combining these, we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2\tau}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(N_{\theta})\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\\
+C(N_{u},N_{\theta},T)\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Note that thanks to elliptic regularity, we have

\left\|\nabla\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}\leq\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}.

Thus, with Grönwall’s inequality, we conclude that

(3.14)

\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}\leq C(N_{u},N_{\theta},T).

This estimate is uniform with respect to
N_{m}
.
Again, by elliptic regularity, this also implies the same estimate on
\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
:

(3.15)

\left\|\nabla\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))}\leq C(N_{u},N_{\theta},T).

Next, we note that
\psi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
is given by

(3.16)

\psi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}=P^{\theta}_{N_{\theta}}\left(-\varepsilon\lambda\Delta\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}})-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi^{\prime}_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}}{2\chi_{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}|^{2}\right).

Thus, since we have already shown that both
\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(D))
and
\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(D))
are bounded, it follows that at least
\psi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\in L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
uniformly with respect to
N_{m}
.
Before being able to pass to the limit
N_{m}\to\infty
, we need to derive estimates on the time derivatives. We take
w=\partial_{t}\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as a test function in (
3.6b
) to estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\chi_{0}}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

We divide by
\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}
and further estimate the right hand side using that
\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
has
N_{u}
terms,

\begin{split}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\chi_{0}}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(N_{u})\left\|\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\nabla\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\chi_{0}}{\tau}\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(N_{u},N_{\theta},T)\end{split}

using the previous estimates. This implies that
\partial_{t}\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
is uniformly in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))
with respect to
N_{m}
. Furthermore we have that
\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
,
\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
have continuous time derivatives and therefore are
C^{1}
with respect to time uniformly in
N_{m}
.
To derive a uniform estimate on the time derivative of
\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
, we take the time derivative in equation (
3.6c
), then take
\partial_{t}\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}
as a test function and integrate by parts,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}dx+\int_{\partial D}(\mathbf{h}_{a})_{t}\cdot\mathbf{n}\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}dS

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\left\|(\mathbf{h}_{a})_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial D)}\left\|\varphi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial D)},

where we have also used that
\partial_{t}\mathbf{h}_{a}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\partial D))
by the assumptions.
Using trace inequalities and then dividing by
\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}
, we obtain the bound

\left\|\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq\left\|\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}}+C(D)\left\|(\mathbf{h}_{a})_{t}\right\|_{L^{2}(\partial D)}\leq C(N_{u},N_{\theta},T).

3.1.5.
Passing
N_{m}\to\infty

Combining the a priori estimates (
3.13
), (
3.10
), (
3.9
), (
3.14
), (
3.15
),

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\{\mathbf{u}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\}_{N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{div}}(D))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{\operatorname{div}}(D))\\
&\{\theta^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\}_{N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(D))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(D))\\
&\{\mathbf{h}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\}_{N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(D))\\
&\{\mathbf{m}^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\}_{N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}(D))\\
&\{\psi^{N_{u},N_{\theta},N_{m}}\}_{N_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\subset L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(D))\end{split}

with the estimates on the time derivatives, we can apply the Aubin-Lions-Simon lemma
[
29

3.2.1.
Passage to the limit

) and the assumed bounds on
\chi_{\theta}
, (
4.2
),

|(i)|\leq\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\sup_{\theta^{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{\chi^{\prime}_{\theta^{\tau}}\theta^{\tau}}{\chi_{\theta^{\tau}}}\right|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{D}\frac{|\mathbf{m}^{\tau}-\chi_{\theta^{\tau}}\mathbf{h}^{\tau}|^{2}}{\chi_{\theta^{\tau}}}dxdt\leq C\sqrt{\tau}.

For the second term, we use the bounds on
\chi_{\theta}
, (
4.2
), combined with the strong convergence of
\mathbf{h}^{\tau}
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times D)
to apply Lemma
B.1
and conclude that

(ii)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\chi_{\Theta}^{\prime}\Theta,|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dt.

Finally, for (iii), we use again the bounds on
\chi_{\theta}
and that
\mathbf{m}^{\tau}\cdot\mathbf{h}^{\tau}\to\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{H}=\chi_{\Theta}|\mathbf{H}|^{2}
in
L^{1}([0,T]\times D)
to apply Lemma
B.1
and conclude that

(iii)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\mu_{0}\chi_{\Theta}^{\prime}\Theta,|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dt.

Thus,

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta^{\tau}}^{2}}\chi^{\prime}_{\theta^{\tau}}|\mathbf{m}^{\tau}|^{2},\theta^{\tau}\right)dt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\chi_{\Theta}^{\prime}\Theta,|\mathbf{H}|^{2}\right)dt,

and we can proceed as in (
3.55
)–(
3.58
) to conclude that
\nabla\theta^{\tau}\to\nabla\Theta
in
L^{2}([0,T]\times D)
up to a subsequence. Thus the limit
(\mathbf{U},\Theta,\mathbf{M},\mathbf{H},\Psi)
is a weak solution of (
2.10
).

Appendix A
Derivation of the diffuse interface ferrofluids model

To derive the system (
1.1
), we follow roughly
[
13
]
which is based on using Onsager’s variational principle
[
18
,
19
]
. We assume that the variables
\mathbf{u}
,
\theta
and
\mathbf{m}
satisfy the Navier-Stokes equations and advection equations of the form

(A.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}(\nu_{\theta}T(u))+\mathbf{F}_{u},

(A.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}u

\displaystyle=0,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}_{t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{F}_{m},

(A.4)

\displaystyle\theta_{t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{div}J_{\theta},

with forces and fluxes
\mathbf{F}_{u},J_{\theta}
and
\mathbf{F}_{m}
to be determined. This means that both the phase field variable
\theta
and the magnetization
\mathbf{m}
are convected by the fluid. Furthermore, this also implies that we assume that the fluid is incompressible and both phases have the same density. As it was derived in previous works
[
24
,
23
,
16
]
, the magnetic field
\mathbf{h}
satisfies a constitutive relation of the form

(A.5)

\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{h},

and the magnetostatics equations

(A.6)

\operatorname{div}\mathbf{b}=0,\quad\operatorname{curl}\mathbf{h}=0.

As specified above in (
1.8
), the magnetic field is a sum of the demagnetizing field
\mathbf{h}_{d}
and the applied field
\mathbf{h}_{a}
which we assume is not perturbed by
\mathbf{h}_{d}
and
\mathbf{m}
(this is a simplifying assumption). We assume that
\mathbf{h}_{a}
is a given smooth harmonic field and that
\mathbf{h}_{d}\approx 0
outside
D
. We assume that the boundary is impermeable and that there is no slip, resulting in homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for
\mathbf{u}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{u}=0
on
\partial D
and no flux boundary conditions for
J_{\theta}
, i.e.,
J_{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on
\partial D
. Under the assumptions above, the boundary conditions for
\mathbf{h}
become (
1.9b
). We assume that the free energy of the system is given by

(A.7)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{U})=\int_{D}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\lambda\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}|\nabla\theta|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}F(\theta)\right)+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}|\mathbf{h}|^{2}\right)dx.

The first term is the kinetic energy of the fluid, the second two terms are the Cahn-Hilliard energy measuring the system’s tendency to minimize energy by separating into different phases, where
\lambda
is the surface tension between the phases, the third term is the magnetization energy density and the final term is the energy density of the magnetic field. We compute the time derivative of the energy and plug in the equations for the evolution of
\mathbf{u}
,
\mathbf{m}
,
\theta
and
\mathbf{h}
, (
A.1
), (
A.3
), (
A.4
), (
A.6
), the boundary conditions, and use that
\mathbf{u}
is divergence free:

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\left(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}+\lambda\varepsilon\nabla\theta\nabla\theta_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)\theta_{t}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{m}_{t}-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\theta_{t}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}+\mu_{0}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{h}_{t}\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\Bigg(\mathbf{u}\cdot\left[-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}-\nabla p+\operatorname{div}(\nu_{\theta}T(u))+\mathbf{F}_{u}\right]-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta\theta_{t}+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)\theta_{t}+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}\cdot\left[-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{F}_{m}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\theta_{t}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}-\mu_{0}\varphi\operatorname{div}\mathbf{h}_{t}\Bigg)dx+\mu_{0}\int_{\partial D}\varphi(\mathbf{h}_{a}-\mathbf{m})_{t}\cdot\mathbf{n}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\Bigg(-\nu_{\theta}|T(u)|^{2}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{u}-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta\left[-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta-\operatorname{div}J_{\theta}\right]+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)\left[-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta-\operatorname{div}J_{\theta}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}\cdot\left[-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{F}_{m}\right]-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\left[-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta-\operatorname{div}J_{\theta}\right]|\mathbf{m}|^{2}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{h}\mathbf{m}_{t}\Bigg)dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu_{0}\int_{\partial D}\varphi(\mathbf{h}_{a})_{t}\cdot\mathbf{n}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\Bigg(-\nu_{\theta}|T(u)|^{2}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{u}+\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta\cdot\mathbf{u}-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\nabla\theta\cdot J_{\theta}+\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\nabla F^{\prime}(\theta)\cdot J_{\theta}-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2\chi_{\theta}}(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)|\mathbf{m}|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{F}_{m}-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}\left[-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta-\operatorname{div}J_{\theta}\right]|\mathbf{m}|^{2}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{h}\left[-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{m}+\mathbf{F}_{m}\right]\Bigg)dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu_{0}\int_{\partial D}\varphi(\mathbf{h}_{a})_{t}\cdot\mathbf{n}dS

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\Bigg(-\nu_{\theta}|T(u)|^{2}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}_{u}+\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta-\mu_{0}(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}\right)+\mathbf{F}_{m}\cdot\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{h}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+J_{\theta}\cdot\left(\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\nabla F^{\prime}(\theta)-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\nabla\theta-\nabla\left(\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\right)\right)\Bigg)dx+\mu_{0}\int_{\partial D}\varphi(\mathbf{h}_{a})_{t}\cdot\mathbf{n}dS

The power of the system, i.e., the time derivative of the work
\mathcal{W}
of internal forces, is the collection of the terms that have a scalar product with
\mathbf{u}
. We assume that the system is closed, meaning there are no external forces. This implies that
\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{dt}=0
. Thus, we must have

\int_{D}\mathbf{u}\cdot\left(\mathbf{F}_{u}+\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta-\mu_{0}(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}\right)dx=0,

which allows us to identify
\mathbf{F}_{u}
,

(A.8)

\mathbf{F}_{u}=-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta\nabla\theta+\mu_{0}(\mathbf{m}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{h}.

The first law of thermodynamics says that the time derivative of the energy is equal to the power of the system minus the temperature
\mathcal{T}
times the time derivative of the entropy
\mathcal{S}
, the entropy production,

\frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dt}=\frac{d\mathcal{W}}{dt}-\mathcal{T}\frac{d\mathcal{S}}{dt}.

The second law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of a closed system must increase or stay constant. Thus we must have

-\mathcal{T}\frac{d\mathcal{S}}{dt}=\int_{D}\Bigg(-\nu_{\theta}|T(u)|^{2}+\mathbf{F}_{m}\cdot\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{h}\right)+J_{\theta}\cdot\left(\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\nabla F^{\prime}(\theta)-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\nabla\theta-\nabla\left(\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\right)\right)\Bigg)dx\leq 0.

In order to achieve this,
\mathbf{F}_{m}
must be proportional to

-\left(\frac{\mu_{0}}{\chi_{\theta}}\mathbf{m}-\mu_{0}\mathbf{h}\right)

and
J_{\theta}
must be proportional to

-\nabla\left(\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\right).

This results in the choices

J_{\theta}=-\kappa\nabla\left(\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2}\right),

or

\theta_{t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta=\kappa\Delta\psi,\quad\psi=\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}F^{\prime}(\theta)-\lambda\varepsilon\Delta\theta-\frac{\mu_{0}\chi_{\theta}^{\prime}}{2\chi_{\theta}^{2}}|\mathbf{m}|^{2},

and

\mathbf{F}_{m}=-\frac{1}{\tau}(\mathbf{m}-\chi_{\theta}\mathbf{h}).

We choose
\mathbf{F}_{m}
inversely proportional to the relaxation time
\tau
as this term drives
\mathbf{m}
to be parallel to
\mathbf{h}
in equilibrium and multiply by
\chi_{\theta}
so that the magnetic field acts on the magnetization when in the ferrofluid phase. In summary, we have obtained the system (
1.1
).

Appendix B
Result from Real Analysis

We believe the following result is standard, but state it here for convenience.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
u_{n},v_{n}:D\to\mathbb{R}
be sequences of measurable functions, such that

\displaystyle u_{n}\rightarrow u,\quad\text{a.e. in }D,\quad\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq C\,\quad\forall\,n

\displaystyle v_{n}\rightharpoonup v,\quad\text{in }L^{1}(D),

for some functions
u\in L^{\infty}(D)
,
v\in L^{1}(D)
. Then

u_{n}v_{n}\rightharpoonup u\,v,\quad\text{in }L^{1}(D),\quad\text{as }n\rightarrow\infty.

The proof can be found for example in
[
31
, Lemma A.1]
.

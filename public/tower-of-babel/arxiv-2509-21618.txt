Polynomial Invariants of q q -Matroids and Rank-Metric Codes
Heide Gluesing-Luerssen, and Benjamin Jany

Abstract
It is shown that the Whitney function of a representable q q -matroid and the collection of all higher weight enumerators of any representing rank-metric code determine each other via a monomial substitution. Moreover, the q q -matroid itself and the collection of all higher support enumerators of the code determine each other. Next, it is proven that the Whitney function of a q q -matroid and the Whitney function of its projectivization determine each other via a monomial substitution. Finally, q q -matroids with isomorphic projectivizations are studied. It is shown that the projectivizations are isomorphic iff the q q -matroids admit a dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism between their lattices of flats. Such q q -matroids are called weakly isomorphic.



Keywords:

q
-matroid, rank-metric codes, Whitney function, higher weight enumerators, projectivization.

1
Introduction

It is well known that a Hamming-metric block code
C
give rise to a matroid
M_{C}
in such a way that (some of) the invariants of
M_{C}

are closely related to the invariants of
C
.
The most famous relation is arguably Greene’s Theorem
[
17
]
, which states that the weight enumerator of
C
can be obtained
as an evaluation of the Witney function (or rank-generating function) of
M_{C}
.
This result has been extended in
[
6
,
7
]
(see also
[
20
]
) to the higher weight enumerators.
The latter involve the weight distributions of the subcodes of
C
.
In
[
6
,
7
]
it is shown that each higher weight enumerator arises as an evaluation of the Whitney function and, conversely, the Whitney function
equals the sum of evaluations of the higher weight enumerators.
In other words, the Whitney function of
M_{C}
and the collection of all higher weight enumerators of
C
carry the same information.
In
[
7
]
, the author goes even further and studies not just the weight, but the actual support of codewords and subcodes.
The author proves that the collection of all higher support distributions of
C
and the matroid
M_{C}
fully
determine each other in form of a matrix identity.

In this paper we generalize these results to rank-metric codes and their associated
q
-matroids.
For the relation between rank-metric codes and
q
-matroids we refer to
[
22
,
16
,
25
,
12
]
and the next section.
We show that for a given rank-metric code
{\mathcal{C}}\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
and its associated
q
-matroid
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
(a) the Whitney function of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
and the collection of all higher weight enumerators of
{\mathcal{C}}
determine each other, and (b)
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
and the collection of all higher support distributions determine each other.
While these results have a striking similarity to the Hamming-metric case derived in
[
7
]
, there is a fundamental difference.
In the Hamming-metric case the relation between the Whitney function and the higher weight enumerators can be expressed in terms of
evaluations (i.e., variable substitutions of the form
(x,y)\mapsto(\alpha(x,y),\,\beta(x,y))
), and the same is true for most relations between
polynomial invariants of the matroid, such as between the Whitney function and the Tutte polynomial or specializing the Whitney function to the characteristic polynomial.
In the rank-metric case, however, the polynomial invariants are not related via an evaluation, but rather via a monomial substitution, that is,
a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear map
{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
defined by
x^{i}y^{j}\mapsto u_{i,j}
, where
u_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
.
More precisely, suitable monomial substitutions of the Whitney function of
{\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{C}}}
result in the higher weight enumerators of
{\mathcal{C}}
, and conversely,
the sum of suitable monomial substitutions of the higher weight enumerators equals the Whitney function (
4.6
).
For
q\rightarrow 1
we recover the evaluation identities for Hamming-metric codes.

The central role played by monomial substitutions is also underscored by other cases.
First, the Whitney function of a
q
-matroid specializes to the characteristic polynomial via a monomial substitution (
2.9
).
Secondly, the convolution identity between the Whitney function
and the Tutte polynomial of a
q
-matroid, recently derived in
[
10
]
, can be rewritten as a monomial substitution (
2.10
).

The second part of this paper is devoted to the projectivization of a
q
-matroid.
For a
q
-matroid
{\mathcal{M}}
with ground space
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, the projectivization
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
is a matroid whose ground space is the set of lines of
{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
.
It has been introduced in
[
19
]
and further studied in
[
18
]
.
We show that the Whitney functions of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
are related via monomial substitutions (
5.14
).
It is known from
[
18
]
that if
{\mathcal{M}}
is represented by the rank-metric code
{\mathcal{C}}
, then
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
is represented by the
projectivization
\hat{{\mathcal{C}}}
of
{\mathcal{C}}
, which is a Hamming-metric code.
We prove that the higher weight enumerators of
{\mathcal{C}}
and
\hat{{\mathcal{C}}}
determine each other via monomial substitutions.

Examples show that non-isomorphic
q
-matroids may have isomorphic projectivizations. In the last section we study this situation in detail.
It turns out that
q
-matroids have isomorphic projectivizations if and only if they are weakly isomorphic, i.e., they admit a dimension-preserving
lattice isomorphism between their lattices of flats (
6.6
).
We furthermore show, that for weakly isomorphic
q
-matroids
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, any dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism between the flats
extends to a dimension- and rank-preserving bijection between the subspace lattices of the ground spaces of
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
.
This illustrates the difference to isomorphic
q
-matroids, where the dimension- and rank-preserving bijection is actually induced by a vector
space isomorphism between the ground spaces.

.

.
For any finite-dimensional

2
Basics on
q
-Matroids

Before starting with
q
-matroids, we summarize some basic notation and terminology for classical matroids.
It will be needed throughout the paper, in particular in Sections
5
and
6
.

Remark 2.1
.

The
Whitney function
of a matroid
M=(S,r)
of rank
K
on the ground set
S
with
|S|=N
is defined as

R_{M}(x,y)=\sum_{A\subseteq S}x^{K-r(A)}y^{|A|-r(A)}=\sum_{i=0}^{K}\sum_{j=0}^{N-K}\nu_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j},

(2.1)

where
\nu_{i,j}=|\{A\subseteq S\mid r(A)=K-i,\,|A|=K-i+j\}|
(the monotonicity
of the nullity function,
A\mapsto|A|-r(A)
, implies that
\nu_{i,j}=0
for
j>N-K
).
Furthermore, the
characteristic polynomial
of
M
is

\chi_{M}=\sum_{A\subseteq S}\mu(0,A)x^{K-r(A)}=\sum_{A\subseteq S}(-1)^{|A|}x^{K-r(A)}
, where
\mu
is the Möbius function on the subset lattice of
S
.
The characteristic polynomial satisfies
\chi_{M}(x)=(-1)^{K}R_{M}(-x,-1)
.
Two matroids
M=(S,r)
and
M^{\prime}=(S^{\prime},r^{\prime})
are
isomorphic
, denoted by
M\cong M^{\prime}
, if there exists a bijection
\alpha:S\longrightarrow S^{\prime}
such that
r^{\prime}(\alpha(A))=r(A)
for all subsets
A\subseteq S
.
Finally, we use the following notation for
vector matroids
.
Let
G\in{\mathbb{F}}^{K\times N}
be of rank
K
.
For any subset
\Delta\subseteq[N]
define
G_{\Delta}
as the submatrix of
G
consisting of the columns indexed by
\Delta
.
The vector matroid
M_{G}
is defined as
([N],r)
, where
r(\Delta)=\mbox{\rm rk}\,(G_{\Delta})
.

We now turn to the
q
-analogues and recall the definition of
q
-matroids based on rank functions.

Definition 2.2
.

A
q
-matroid with ground space
E
is a pair
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
, where
E
is a finite-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}
-vector space
and
\rho:{\mathcal{L}}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{\geq 0}
is a map satisfying

(R1)

Boundedness:
0\leq\rho(V)\leq\dim V
for all
V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
;

(R2)

Monotonicity:
V\leq W\Longrightarrow\rho(V)\leq\rho(W)
for all
V,W\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
;

(R3)

Submodularity:
\rho(V+W)+\rho(V\cap W)\leq\rho(V)+\rho(W)
for all
V,W\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
.

We call
\rho(V)
the
rank of

V
and
\rho({\mathcal{M}}):=\rho(E)
the rank of the
q
-matroid.
Moreover, the
nullity
of
V
is defined as
\dim V-\rho(V)
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is easy to see that the nullity function

{\mathcal{L}}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\,V\longmapsto\dim V-\rho(V)
is increasing, that is:

V\leq W\Longrightarrow\dim V-\rho(V)\leq\dim(W)-\rho(W)
; see also
[
3
, p. 99]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Two
q
-matroids
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}=(E_{i},\rho_{i})
are
isomorphic
, denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
, if there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}
-isomorphism
\alpha:E_{1}\longrightarrow E_{2}
such that
\rho_{2}(\alpha(V))=\rho_{1}(V)
for all
V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E_{1})
.

A large class of
q
-matroids are the representable ones.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
22
, Sec. 5]
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
be a field extension of
{\mathbb{F}}
and let
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
.
Define the map
\rho:{\mathcal{L}}({\mathbb{F}}^{n})\longrightarrow{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
via

\rho(\mbox{\rm rowsp}(Y))=\mbox{\rm rk}\,(GY\mbox{$\!{}^{\sf T}$})\ \text{ for all matrices $Y\in{\mathbb{F}}^{t\times n}$ and all $t\in[n]_{0}$}

(where the rank is over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
).
Then
\rho
is well-defined and
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}:=({\mathbb{F}}^{n},\rho)
is a
q
-matroid of rank equal to the rank of
G
(over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
). It is called the
q
-matroid
represented by
G
.
A
q
-matroid
{\mathcal{M}}
of rank
k
is called
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-representable
if
{\mathcal{M}}\cong{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
for some matrix
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
.

Note that
{\mathcal{M}}_{G}
only depends on the row space of
G
, defined as
\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G):=\{uG\mid u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\}
, and not on the actual choice of
G
.

The Whitney function of
{\mathcal{M}}
(also known as rank-generating function or corank-nullity function) is defined in the usual way; see also

[
10
, Def. 64]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid of rank
k
on an
n
-dimensional ground space
E
.
The
Whitney function
(or
corank-nullity function
) of
{\mathcal{M}}
is

R_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{V\leq E}x^{k-\rho(V)}y^{\dim V-\rho(V)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y].

The exponent
k-\rho(V)
is called the
corank
of
V
and
\dim V-\rho(V)
is the
nullity
.

Clearly, the Whitney function is invariant under isomorphisms of
q
-matroids.

One can easily describe the coefficients of the Whitney function explicitly.
A monomial
x^{i}y^{j}
appears in
R_{\mathcal{M}}
if and only if there exists a subspace
V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
such that
\rho(V)=k-i
and

\dim V=k-i+j
.
In that case
j=\dim V-\rho(V)\leq\dim E-\rho(E)=n-k
by the monotonicity of the nullity function.
As a consequence, the Whitney function is of the form

R_{{\mathcal{M}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\nu_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}

(2.2)

where

\nu_{i,j}=\big|\{V\leq E\mid\rho(V)=k-i,\,\dim V=k-i+j\}\big|.

(2.3)

In
[
14
, Rem. 3.2]
various invariants of the
q
-matroid are shown to be enumerated by the Whitney function.

The following can easily be verified.

Example 2.7
.

Let
k\in[n]_{0}
. The
uniform
q
-matroid
of rank
k
on an
n
-dimensional ground space
E
, denoted by
{\mathcal{U}}_{k,n}(q)
, is defined via the rank function
\rho(V)=\min\{k,\dim V\}
for all
V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
.
It satisfies
R_{{\mathcal{U}}_{k,n}(q)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{j}x^{k-j}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{j}y^{j-k}
.
We call
{\mathcal{U}}_{0,n}(q)
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,n}(q)
the
trivial
and
free

q
-matroid, respectively.

The following maps will play a crucial role throughout this paper.
They will allow us to relate various invariants of
q
-matroids and rank-metric codes.

Definition 2.8
.

For
i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
let
u_{i,j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
and set
{\mathcal{U}}=(u_{i,j})_{i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
. The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear map

\Omega_{{\mathcal{U}}}:{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y],\ \sum_{i,j}f_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}\longmapsto\sum_{i,j}f_{i,j}u_{i,j}

is called a
monomial substitution
.

As we will show, these monomial substitutions generalize the ‘standard substitutions’ known from matroid theory.
The following two examples provide a first illustration.

Remark 2.9
.

The characteristic polynomial of a
q
-matroid
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
of rank
k
is defined as

\chi_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{V\leq E}\mu(0,V)x^{k-\rho(V)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
, where
\mu
is the Möbius function on the subspace lattice
{\mathcal{L}}(E)

(see also (
4.1
) later in this paper).
Thus,
\chi_{{\mathcal{M}}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{\dim V=j}(-1)^{j}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{j}{2}}x^{k-\rho(V)}
.
It is easy to verify (see also
[
14
, Rem. 3.6]
) that
\chi_{\mathcal{M}}=(-1)^{k}\Omega_{{\mathcal{G}}}(R_{{\mathcal{M}}})
, where

{\mathcal{G}}=(g_{i,j})_{i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and

g_{i,j}=q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{k+j-i}{2}}(-x)^{i}(-1)^{j}.

For
q\rightarrow 1
this specializes to the well-known identity
\chi_{M}(x)=(-1)^{k}R_{M}(-x,-1)
for matroids
M
of rank
k
.

The second instance of a monomial substitution is highly non-trivial and has been established, with very different terminology, in
[
10
]
.

Remark 2.10
.

In
[
10
]
, the authors introduce Tutte partitions of the subspace lattice of a
q
-matroid
{\mathcal{M}}
(see also the precursor
[
5
]
).
Such a partition, if it exists, leads to the notion of Tutte polynomial of
{\mathcal{M}}
.
It captures information about the intervals that make up the partition.
The authors go on to show that the Tutte polynomial can be obtained from the Whitney function in a way that shows that
the Tutte polynomial in fact does not depend on the choice of the Tutte partition.
In particular, the Tutte polynomial always exists, but up to now it is unclear what information it captures in the absence of a
Tutte partition.
We refer to
[
10
]
for further details about Tutte partitions and the definition of the associated Tutte polynomial.
In
[
10
]
the relation between the Tutte and Whitney polynomial is derived in form of convolution maps.
As we will describe next, this can be expressed by way of monomial substitutions.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid on the ground space
E
.
Following
[
10
]
we define

{\mathcal{A}}_{i,j}=\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{j}\alpha(i,j;a,b)x^{a}y^{b}\ \text{ and }\ {\mathcal{B}}_{i,j}=\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{j}\beta(i,j;a,b)x^{a}y^{b}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]

where

\displaystyle\alpha(i,j;a,b)

\displaystyle=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{a}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{b}_{q}q^{(i-a)(j-b)},

\displaystyle\beta(i,j;a,b)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{(i-a)+(j-b)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{a}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{b}_{q}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{|(i-a)-(j-b)|}{2}}\Big(1+q^{|(i-a)-(j-b)|}-q^{\max\{i-a,j-b\}}\Big),

and set
{\mathcal{A}}=({\mathcal{A}}_{i,j})_{i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}=({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j})_{i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
.
Then
\Omega_{{\mathcal{A}}}\circ\Omega_{{\mathcal{B}}}=\Omega_{{\mathcal{B}}}\circ\Omega_{{\mathcal{A}}}=\text{id}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]}
, that is
\Omega_{{\mathcal{A}}}({\mathcal{B}}_{i,j})=x^{i}y^{j}=\Omega_{{\mathcal{B}}}({\mathcal{A}}_{i,j})
, and
the convolutional relations between the Whitney function
R_{\mathcal{M}}
and Tutte polynomial
T_{{\mathcal{M}}}
established in

[
10
, Prop. 67, Cor. 68]
can be rewritten as the monomial substitutions

T_{{\mathcal{M}}}=\Omega_{{\mathcal{B}}}(R_{\mathcal{M}})\ \text{ and }\ R_{\mathcal{M}}=\Omega_{{\mathcal{A}}}(T_{\mathcal{M}}).

This can be proven with the aid of the technical computations in
[
10
, Thm. 61 and its proof]
and some additional symmetric cases.
As illustrated in
[
10
, Rem. 63]
, for
q\rightarrow 1
(i.e., the matroid case) the above monomial substitutions specialize to the familiar identity
T_{M}=R_{M}(x-1,y-1)
and its inverse.

3
Rank-Metric Codes and their Higher Supports and Weights

In this section we will introduce rank-metric codes and various invariants.
We first recall the analogous notions for Hamming-metric codes (i.e., block codes).

Remark 3.1
.

The
Hamming support
of a vector
v\in{\mathbb{F}}^{N}
is defined as
\mbox{$\text{supp}_{\text{H}}$}(v)=\{i\in[N]\mid v_{i}\neq 0\}
, and the
Hamming weight

is
\mbox{$\text{wt}_{\text{H}}$}(v)=|\mbox{$\text{supp}_{\text{H}}$}(v)|
.
For any subspace
D\leq{\mathbb{F}}^{N}
we set
\mbox{$\text{supp}_{\text{H}}$}(D)=\bigcup_{v\in D}\mbox{$\text{supp}_{\text{H}}$}(v)
and
\mbox{$\text{wt}_{\text{H}}$}(D)=|\mbox{$\text{supp}_{\text{H}}$}(D)|
.
Let
C\leq{\mathbb{F}}^{N}
be an
[N,K]
-block code. For
t\in[K]_{0},\,\Delta\subseteq[N]
, and
i\in[N]_{0}
we set

\displaystyle A^{(t)}_{i,\text{H}}

\displaystyle=|\{D\leq C\mid\dim D=t,\ \mbox{$\text{wt}_{\text{H}}$}(D)=i\}|,

\displaystyle A^{(t)}_{\Delta,\text{H}}

\displaystyle=|\{D\leq C\mid\dim D=t,\ \mbox{$\text{supp}_{\text{H}}$}(D)=\Delta\}|.

We call
(A^{(t)}_{0,\text{H}},A^{(t)}_{1,\text{H}},,\ldots,A^{(t)}_{N,\text{H}})
the
t
-th
higher Hamming-weight distribution
of
C
and

W^{(t)}_{C,\text{H}}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}A_{i,\text{H}}^{(t)}x^{N-i}y^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
the
t
-th
higher Hamming-weight enumerator
of
C
.
The list
\big(A^{(t)}_{\Delta,\text{H}}\big)_{\Delta\subseteq[N]}
is called the
t
-th
higher Hamming-support distribution
of
C
.

Recall that we can associate the matroid
M_{G}
to a Hamming-metric code
C=\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G)
; see
2.1
.
In
[
7
, Thms. 3 and 4]
it has been shown that (a) the matroid
M_{G}
and the collection of all higher Hamming-support distributions of
C

determine each other, and (b) the Whitney function
R_{M_{G}}
and the collection of all higher Hamming-weight distributions of
C
determine each other.
In
4.4
we will generalize these results to rank-metric codes and their associated
q
-matroids.

Definition 3.2
.

Consider
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, which we may regard as an
{\mathbb{F}}
-vector space.

(a)

The
rank weight
of
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is defined as
\mbox{$\text{wt}_{\text{rk}}$}(v)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}}\langle{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}}\rangle
, where

\langle{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}}\rangle
denotes the
{\mathbb{F}}
-subspace generated by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
.

(b)

Let
G\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}=\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G):=\{uG\mid u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\}
is called the
rank-metric code
generated by
G
.

(c)

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a rank-metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
. For
i\in[n]_{0}
let
A_{i}=|\{v\in{\mathcal{C}}\mid\mbox{$\text{wt}_{\text{rk}}$}(v)=i\}|
.
Then
W_{\mathcal{C}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}A_{i}x^{n-i}y^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x,y]
is called the
rank-weight enumerator
of the code
{\mathcal{C}}
, and

\min\{i>0\mid A_{i}\neq 0\}
is the
minimum distance
of
{\mathcal{C}}
.

For the sake of clarity we will occasionally use the following notation.

Notation 3.3
.

We write
V\mbox{$\leq_{q}\,$}W
(resp.,
V\mbox{$\leq_{q^{m}}\,$}W
) if
V
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-subspace (resp.,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace) of
W
.

The notions in
3.2
can be described with the aid of the matrix space
{\mathbb{F}}^{m\times n}
.
Let
\psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{m}
be the coordinate map with respect to a fixed
{\mathbb{F}}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
(where we write the coordinate vectors as column vectors) and consider the
{\mathbb{F}}
-isomorphism

\Psi:{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}^{m\times n},\quad(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}\psi(v_{1})&\cdots&\psi(v_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

(3.1)

Note that
{\mathbb{F}}^{m\times n}
is an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space with the scalar multiplication

aM:=\Psi(a\Psi^{-1}(M))

This turns

-linear map.
In this notation, a rank-metric code is simply an

-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}^{m\times n}
.
Furthermore, for any

for all

.

Recall from

that we can associate a

-matroid

to a matrix

.
We can now give an alternative description of the rank function

of

.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}=\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G)\mbox{$\leq_{q^{m}}\,$}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}

Definition 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\leq_{q^{m}}{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be a rank-metric code and

4
q
-Matroids and Higher Distributions

In this section we will restrict ourselves to representable
q
-matroids.
We will see that the
q
-matroid associated to a rank-metric code carries the same information as the collection of all higher support distributions
of the code, while the Whitney function carries the same information as the collection of all higher weight enumerators.
In fact, these invariants are related via monomial substitutions.
Because of the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linearity of the relations, it appears most convenient to use a matrix-theoretical approach, as in
[
7
]
for Hamming-metric codes.

Recall the Möbius function on the subspace lattice
{\mathcal{L}}(E)
given by

\mu(W,V)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}(-1)^{v-w}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{v-w}{2}},&\text{if }W\leq V,\\[2.15277pt]
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right\},\quad\text{ where }v=\dim V,\ w=\dim W.

(4.1)

We also remind of the Kronecker-
\delta
function from
1.1
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid of rank
k
on an
n
-dimensional ground space
E
.
Let
\nu_{i,j}
be as in (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and set
\nu_{i,b}:=0\text{ for }b\not\in\{0,\ldots,n-k\}
.
Set
\hat{n}=|{\mathcal{L}}(E)|=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{i}_{q}
and fix some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Moreover, choose a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form (NSBF) on
E
.
We define the matrices

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=\big(\delta_{W,V^{\perp}}\big)_{W,V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\hat{n}\times\hat{n}},

\displaystyle M=\big(\mu(W,V)\big)_{V,W\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\hat{n}\times\hat{n}},

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\Big((-1)^{i-j}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{i-j}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-j}{i-j}_{q}\Big)_{i,j\in[n]_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)},

\displaystyle\,Q=\Big(\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{t}{j}_{q^{m}}\Big)_{t,j\in[k]_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(k+1)\times(k+1)},

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=\big(\nu_{i,n-k+i-j}\big)_{i\in[k]_{0}\atop j\in[n]_{0}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(k+1)\times(n+1)},

\displaystyle\,D=\big(\delta_{i,\dim V}\big)_{i\in[n]_{0}\ \atop V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(n+1)\times\hat{n}},

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\big(\delta_{k-\rho(V),\,j})_{V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)\atop j\in[k]_{0}\ }\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\hat{n}\times(k+1)}.

Some comments are in order.
First, only the matrix
P
depends on the chosen NSBF.
Second, the matrix
K
contains the same information as the rank function
\rho
because
K_{V,j}=1
iff
\rho(V)=k-j
.
Finally, the matrix
N
carries exactly the same information as the Whitney function.
This will also be made precise in
4.3
(a) below.

Proposition 4.2
.

The matrices
P,M,F,
and
Q
are invertible over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Precisely,
P^{-1}=P
and

M^{-1}=\big(\hat{M}_{V,W}\big)_{V,W},\ \;Q^{-1}=\Big((-1)^{j-t}q^{m\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{j-t}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{j}{t}_{q^{m}}\Big)_{j,t},\ \;F^{-1}=\Big(\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-j}{i-j}_{q}\Big)_{i,j},

where
\hat{M}_{V,W}=1
if
W\leq V
and
\hat{M}_{V,W}=0
otherwise.

Proof.

First of all,
(PP)_{V,W}=\sum_{Z}\delta_{V,Z^{\perp}}\delta_{Z,W^{\perp}}=\delta_{V,W}
and thus
P=P^{-1}
.
Next, note that
\hat{M}_{V,W}
is the
\zeta
-function on
{\mathcal{L}}(E)
, which is the inverse of the Möbius function, and we compute

(\hat{M}M)_{V,W}=\sum_{W\leq Z\leq V}\hat{M}_{V,Z}M_{Z,W}=\delta_{V,W}
.
The statement about
Q^{-1}
follows from the orthogonality relations for
q
-binomial coefficients (applied to
q^{m}
). Indeed,

(QQ^{-1})_{t,i}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j-i}q^{m\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{j-i}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{t}{j}_{q^{m}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{i}_{q^{m}}=\sum_{j=i}^{t}(-1)^{j-i}q^{m\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{j-i}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{t}{j}_{q^{m}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{j}{i}_{q^{m}}=\delta_{t,i};

see also
[
7
, Identity (12)]
.
Finally, with

U=\begin{pmatrix}&&1\\
&\iddots&\\
1&&\end{pmatrix}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{(n+1)\times(n+1)}

we obtain
(UFU)_{a,b}=(-1)^{b-a}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{b-a}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{b}{a}
and thus
(UFU)^{-1}=\big(\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{b}{a}\big)_{a,b}
by the orthogonality relations.
Thus
F^{-1}=U(\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{b}{a})_{a,b}U=\big(\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-b}{n-a}\big)_{a,b}
, as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

(a)

uN\mbox{$\!{}^{\sf T}$}v\mbox{$\!{}^{\sf T}$}=R_{{\mathcal{M}}}(x,y)
, where
u=(y^{n},y^{n-1},\ldots,1)
and
v=(y^{-k},xy^{-k+1},x^{2}y^{-k+2},\ldots,x^{k})
.

(b)

DPK=N\mbox{$\!{}^{\sf T}$}
.

(c)

FD=DM
.

Proof.

(a) can easily be verified.
(b)
(DP)_{i,V}=\sum_{W\leq E}\delta_{i,\dim W}\delta_{W,V^{\perp}}
, which is
1
iff
\dim V=n-i
and
0
otherwise.
Thus we obtain

\displaystyle(DPK)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{V\leq E}(DP)_{i,V}K_{V,j}=\sum_{\dim V=n-i}\delta_{k-\rho(V),j}

\displaystyle=\big|\{V\leq E\mid\dim V=n-i,\,\rho(V)=k-j\}\big|=\nu_{j,n-k+j-i}=N_{j,i}=(N\mbox{$\!{}^{\sf T}$})_{i,j}.

(c) Let
V\leq E
and
\dim V=v
. We compute

(FD)_{i,V}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{i-j}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{i-j}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-j}{i-j}_{q}\delta_{j,v}=(-1)^{i-v}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{i-v}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-v}{i-v}_{q}
.
On the other hand,

(DM)_{i,V}=\sum_{W}\delta_{i,\dim W}\mu(V,W)=\sum_{\dim W=i}\mu(V,W)=\sum_{\dim W=i\atop V\leq W}(-1)^{i-v}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{i-v}{2}}=(-1)^{i-v}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{i-v}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n-v}{n-i}_{q}
,
which proves the desired identity.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let

be of rank

Let

Define

As NSBF on

choose the standard dot product and consider the matrices in

(a)

.
In other words, the

-matroid

For the proof we first establish the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let the data be as in
4.4
. For all

and

Fix

In order to prove the first identity, we start with

The Projectivization Matroid

In this section we will consider the projectivization matroid of a
q
-matroid.
These matroids have been introduced in
[
19
]
and studied in detail in
[
18
]
.
We will show that the Whitney function of a
q
-matroid and that of its projectivization determine each other via monomial substitutions.

Recall from
1.1
that
\langle{v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}}\rangle
denotes the
{\mathbb{F}}
-vector space generated by
v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}
.

Notation 5.1
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
set
\langle{n}\rangle_{q}:=\frac{q^{n}-1}{q-1}
and
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}=\big[\langle{n}\rangle_{q}\big]
, thus

{\mathcal{R}}_{n}=\{1,\ldots,\langle{n}\rangle_{q}\}
.
For an
n
-dimensional vector space
E
over
{\mathbb{F}}
denote by
{\mathbb{P}}E
the projective space over
E
.
Thus,
{\mathbb{P}}E=\{\langle{v_{1}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle{v_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}}\rangle\}
for any generators
v_{i}
of the distinct lines in
E

(if
\dim E=0
, then
{\mathbb{P}}E=\emptyset
).
In particular,
\langle{n}\rangle_{q}=|{\mathbb{P}}E|
.
Furthermore, for a subset
A=\{\langle{v_{i_{1}}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle{v_{i_{t}}}\rangle\}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}E
define
\langle{A}\rangle:=\langle{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{t}}}\rangle
, that is
\langle{A}\rangle
is the subspace in
E
generated by the lines in
A
.
Throughout this section, it will be convenient to consider the vectors
v_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
as column vectors.

From now on, fix an integer
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}
-vector space
E
.
The following projectivization matroids have been introduced in
[
19
, Def. 14 and Prop. 15]

and studied in detail in
[
18
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid.
The
projectivization
of
{\mathcal{M}}
, denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
, is defined as the matroid with ground space
{\mathbb{P}}E
and
rank function given by
r(A)=\rho(\langle{A}\rangle)
for all
A\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}E
.
Note that
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
have the same rank.

Let
q
-
\mbox{{Mat}}_{k,n}
be the set of all
q
-matroids of rank
k
on an
n
-dimensional ground space
and
\mbox{{Mat}}_{k,n}
be the set of all matroids of rank
k
on a ground set of cardinality
n
.
Then projectivization is an injective map from
q
-
\mbox{{Mat}}_{k,n}
to
\mbox{{Mat}}_{k,\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}
.
In
[
18
, Ch. 4]
it is shown that this map is even a functor between suitable categories of
q
-matroids and matroids.

Indexing the lines in
{\mathbb{P}}E
, we may write the projectivization as follows.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be as in
5.2
.
Choose
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}\in E
such that
{\mathbb{P}}E=\{\langle{v_{1}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle{v_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}}\rangle\}
.
Then
\hat{M}=({\mathcal{R}}_{n},\hat{r})
with
\hat{r}(\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\})=\rho(\langle{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{t}}}\rangle)

is a matroid, and it satisfies
\hat{M}\cong{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
.

In order to describe the projectivization of representable
q
-matroids we cast the following terminology.
The Hamming-metric code
\hat{{\mathcal{C}}}
below has also been studied in
[
1
, Sec. 4]
.

Definition 5.4
.

Choose
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
such that

{\mathbb{P}}{\mathbb{F}}^{n}=\{\langle{v_{1}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle{v_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}}\rangle\}
and define
the matrix

For a rank-metric code

Recall the notation from

.

If the
q
-matroid
{\mathcal{M}}
is s representable over

, then so is
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
.
Precisely, let

be as in

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
and
{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}=(E^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
be

-matroids.
Then

{\mathcal{M}}\cong{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}\Longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}\cong{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:E\longrightarrow E^{\prime}
be an isomorphism such that
\rho^{\prime}(\alpha(V))=\rho(V)
for all
V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
.
Denote the rank functions of the projectivization matroids
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
by

and

, respectively.
The map
\alpha

\hat{\alpha}:{\mathbb{P}}E\longrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}E^{\prime}

\hat{\alpha}(\langle{v}\rangle)=\langle{\alpha(v)}\rangle

Now we have for any subset

Hence
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}
are isomorphic.

Let

Recall that we fix

Definition 5.10
.

For

and

set

\gamma_{i}^{r}(n)=\big|\big\{A\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}E\,\big|\,|A|=i,\ \dim\langle{A}\rangle=r\big\}\big|
.

We have the following properties leading to an explicit expression for
\gamma_{i}^{r}(n)
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Fix
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
r\in[n]_{0}
.

(a)

\gamma_{i}^{r}(n)=0
for
i<r
or
i>\langle{n}\rangle_{q}
.
Moreover,
\gamma_{0}^{0}(n)=1
and
\gamma_{n}^{n}(n)=\big(\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\frac{q^{n}-q^{\ell}}{q-1}\big)/n!
.

(b)

\gamma_{i}^{r}(n)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}\gamma_{i}^{r}(r)
.

(c)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{j}_{q}\gamma_{i}^{j}(j)=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{\subspace{n}_q}{i}
.

(d)

\gamma_{i}^{r}(r)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{r}{j}_{q}(-1)^{j}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{j}{2}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{\subspace{r-j}_q}{i}
.

A similar expression as in (d) also appears in
[
23
, Prop. 4.1]
for counting particular matrices over
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Proof.

(a) is clear.

(b) Note first that there are
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{r}_{q}
subspaces of
E
of dimension
r
.
For each such subspace
W
we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}^{r}(r)

\displaystyle=\Big|\big\{A\in{\mathbb{P}}W\,\big|\,|A|=i,\,\dim\langle{A}\rangle=r\big\}\big|=\big|\big\{A\in{\mathbb{P}}W\,\big|\,|A|=i,\,\langle{A}\rangle=W\big\}\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\big\{A\in{\mathbb{P}}E\,\big|\,|A|=i,\,\langle{A}\rangle=W\big\}\big|,

and this proves (b).

(c) Write
{\mathbb{P}}E=\{\langle{v_{1}}\rangle,\ldots,\langle{v_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}}\rangle\}
and let
{\mathcal{L}}_{i}({\mathcal{R}}_{n})
be the set of
i
-subsets of
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}
.
Consider the map

\phi:{\mathcal{L}}_{i}({\mathcal{R}}_{n})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(E),\ \{s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}\}\longmapsto\langle{v_{s_{1}},\ldots,v_{s_{i}}}\rangle.

There are
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{j}_{q}
subspaces
W
of dimension
j
.
For each of them
|\phi^{-1}(W)|=\gamma_{i}^{j}(j)
, and hence

|{\mathcal{L}}_{i}({\mathcal{R}}_{n})|=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{1}{n}{j}_{q}\gamma_{i}^{j}(j)
.
Now the statement follows from
|{\mathcal{L}}_{i}({\mathcal{R}}_{n})|=\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{\subspace{n}_q}{i}
.

(d) Consider the maps

f:{\mathcal{L}}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\ W\longmapsto\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{\subspace{w}_q}{i},\qquad g:{\mathcal{L}}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\ W\longmapsto\gamma_{i}^{w}(w),

where again
w=\dim W
.
Then (c) implies
f(W)=\sum_{X\leq W}g(X)
for all
W\leq E
.
Hence Möbius inversion on the lattice
{\mathcal{L}}(E)
leads to

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}^{w}(w)=g(W)

\displaystyle=\sum_{Z\leq W}\mu(Z,W)f(Z)=\sum_{Z\leq W}(-1)^{w-\dim Z}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{w-\dim Z}{2}}f(Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{z=0}^{w}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{w}{z}_{q}(-1)^{w-z}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{w-z}{2}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{\subspace{z}_q}{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{w}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{w}{j}_{q}(-1)^{j}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{j}{2}}\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{1}{\subspace{w-j}_q}{i}.\qed

Now we are ready to discuss the Whitney functions of a
q
-matroid and its projectivization.

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}=({\mathbb{P}}E,r)
be its projectivization matroid.
Then the Whitney functions of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
determine each other.

An explicit relation between
R_{\mathcal{M}}
and
R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}
will be given in
5.14
.

Proof.

Let
k=\rho(E)
be the rank of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
R_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\nu_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}
be as in (
2.2
).
Using
5.10
we compute

\displaystyle R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{A\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}E}x^{r({\mathbb{P}}E)-r(A)}y^{|A|-r(A)}=\sum_{V\leq E}\sum_{A\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}V\atop\langle A\rangle=V}x^{k-\rho(V)}y^{\dim V-\rho(V)}y^{|A|-\dim V}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{n}\sum_{V\leq E\atop\dim V=b}x^{k-\rho(V)}y^{b-\rho(V)}\sum_{\ell=b}^{\langle{b}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{b}(b)y^{\ell-b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{k}\nu_{k-a,b-a}x^{k-a}y^{b-a}\sum_{\ell=b}^{\langle{b}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{b}(b)y^{\ell-b},

(5.4)

where the last step follows from
\big|\{V\leq E\mid\dim V=b,\,\rho(V)=a\}\big|=\nu_{k-a,b-a}
; see (
2.3
).
With the index change
i=k-a
and
j=b-a
we continue

\displaystyle R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\nu_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}\sum_{\ell=j+k-i}^{\langle{j+k-i}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{j+k-i}(j+k-i)y^{\ell-j+i-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\nu_{i,j}x^{i}\sum_{\ell=j+k-i}^{\langle{j+k-i}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{j+k-i}(j+k-i)y^{\ell+i-k}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=k-i}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}}\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{n-k,\ell-k+i\}}\nu_{i,j}\gamma_{\ell}^{j+k-i}(j+k-i)x^{i}y^{\ell+i-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k+i}\,\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{n-k,\ell\}}\nu_{i,j}\gamma_{\ell+k-i}^{j+k-i}(j+k-i)x^{i}y^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k+i}\mu_{i,\ell}x^{i}y^{\ell},

where

\mu_{i,\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{n-k,\ell\}}\nu_{i,j}\gamma_{\ell+k-i}^{j+k-i}(j+k-i)\ \text{ for }\ i\in[k]_{0}\text{ and }\ell\in[\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k+i]_{0}.

(5.6)

From (
2.1
) we know that
\mu_{i,\ell}=0
for
\ell>\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k
, and thus
we have to consider (
5.6
) only for
\ell\in[\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k]_{0}
.
For any
i\in[k]_{0}
the identities in (
5.6
) form the linear equation

\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{i,0}\\[2.58334pt]
\mu_{i,1}\\[2.58334pt]
\vdots\\[2.58334pt]
\mu_{i,n-k}\\[2.58334pt]
\hline\cr\mu_{i,n-k+1}\\[2.58334pt]
\vdots\\[2.58334pt]
\mu_{i,\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{k-i}^{k-i}(k-i)&0&\cdots&0\\[1.72218pt]
\gamma_{k-i+1}^{k-i}(k-i)&\gamma_{k-i+1}^{k-i+1}(k-i+1)&\cdots&0\\[1.72218pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[1.72218pt]
\gamma_{n-i}^{k-i}(k-i)&\gamma_{n-i}^{k-i+1}(k-i+1)&\ldots&\gamma_{n-i}^{n-i}(n-i)\\[1.72218pt]
\hline\cr\gamma_{n-i+1}^{k-i}(k-i)&\gamma_{n-i+1}^{k-i+1}(k-i+1)&\ldots&\gamma_{n-i+1}^{n-i}(n-i)\\[1.72218pt]
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\[1.72218pt]
\gamma_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-i}^{k-i}(k-i)&\gamma_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-i}^{k-i+1}(k-i+1)&\ldots&\gamma_{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-i}^{n-i}(n-i)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{i,0}\\[2.15277pt]
\nu_{i,1}\\[2.15277pt]
\vdots\\[2.15277pt]
\nu_{i,n-k}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.7)

This shows that for every
i
the vector
(\nu_{i,0},\,\ldots,\,\nu_{i,n-k})
fully determines

(\mu_{i,0},\,\mu_{i,1},\,\ldots,\,\mu_{i,\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k})
.
Conversely, note that the upper block of the big matrix has size
(n-k+1)\times(n-k+1)
and is invertible since

\gamma_{t}^{t}(t)\neq 0
for every
t
thanks to
5.11
(a).
Thus every vector
(\mu_{i,0},\,\ldots,\,\mu_{i,\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k})\mbox{$\!{}^{\sf T}$}
in the column space of the big matrix
uniquely determines the vector
(\nu_{i,0},\,\ldots,\,\nu_{i,n-k})
.
All of this shows that
R_{\mathcal{M}}
and
R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}
determine each other.
∎

Remark 5.13
.

The invertibility of the upper block of the big matrix in (
5.7
) shows that for every
i
the coefficients

(\mu_{i,n-k+1},\,\ldots,\,\mu_{i,\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k})
are determined by

(\mu_{i,0},\,\mu_{i,1},\,\ldots,\,\mu_{i,n-k})
.
In other words, the Whitney function
R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k}\mu_{i,\ell}x^{i}y^{\ell}

is fully determined by its truncation
\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k}\mu_{i,\ell}x^{i}y^{\ell}
.

The relation between
R_{\mathcal{M}}
and
R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}
can be expressed in form of monomial substitutions.

Corollary 5.14
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid of rank
k
.
Denote the inverse of the upper block in (
5.7
) by

\big(\beta^{(i)}_{a,b}\big)_{a=0,\ldots,n-k\atop b=0,\ldots,n-k}
.
Define
{\mathcal{G}}=(g_{i,j})_{i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}=(h_{i,j})_{i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, where

g_{i,j}=x^{i}\sum_{\ell=j+k-i}^{\langle{j+k-i}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{j+k-i}(j+k-i)y^{\ell+i-k},\quad h_{i,j}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}x^{i}\sum_{\ell=j}^{n-k}\beta^{(i)}_{\ell,j}y^{\ell},&\text{if }j\leq n-k,\\[2.15277pt]
0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}=\Omega_{{\mathcal{G}}}(R_{\mathcal{M}})
and
R_{{\mathcal{M}}}=\Omega_{{\mathcal{H}}}(R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}})
.

Proof.

Let
R_{{\mathcal{M}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k}\nu_{i,\ell}x^{i}y^{\ell}
and

R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k}\mu_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}
.
The first identity is in (
5.5
).
As for the second one, note that the upper block in (
5.7
) is lower triangular and thus

\nu_{i,\ell}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\beta^{(i)}_{\ell,j}\mu_{i,j}
for
\ell\in[n-k]_{0}
.
As a consequence,

R_{\mathcal{M}}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-k}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\beta^{(i)}_{\ell,j}\mu_{i,j}x^{i}y^{\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n-k}\mu_{i,j}\sum_{\ell=j}^{n-k}\beta^{(i)}_{\ell,j}x^{i}y^{\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-k}\mu_{i,j}h_{i,j}.\qed

Example 5.15
.

for all

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}g_{i,0}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}x^{i}\sum_{\ell=n-i}^{\langle{n-i}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{n-i}(n-i)y^{\ell+i-n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}\sum_{\ell=n-i}^{\langle{n-i}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{n-i}(n-i)x^{i}y^{\ell+i-n}.

(5.8)

Since

Recall that

is a generator matrix for the Simplex code of dimension

The definition in (

) leads to

R_{M}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\langle{n}\rangle_{q}-n}b_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j},\ \text{ where }\ b_{i,j}=|\{A\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}_{n}\mid\mbox{\rm rk}\,S(n)_{A}=n-i,\,|A|=n-i+j\}|,

and where

(b).

).
Let us also determine the characteristic polynomial of

.
On the one hand,

\displaystyle\chi_{M}(x)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}R_{M}(-x,-1)=(-1)^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}\sum_{\ell=n-i}^{\langle{n-i}\rangle_{q}}\gamma_{\ell}^{n-i}(n-i)(-1)^{\ell-n}x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}\Big(\sum_{\ell=n-i}^{\langle{n-i}\rangle_{q}}(-1)^{\ell}\gamma_{\ell}^{n-i}(n-i)\Big)x^{i}.

(5.10)

On the other hand,
[
18
, Thm. 5.8]
tells us that
\chi_{M}=\chi_{{\mathcal{U}}_{n,n}}
.
By definition (see
2.9
), the latter is given by

\chi_{{\mathcal{U}}_{n,n}}=\sum_{b=0}^{n}\sum_{\dim V=b}(-1)^{b}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{b}{2}}x^{n-b}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{n-i}{2}}x^{i}.

(5.11)

(this appears also in
[
20
, Ex. 5.51]
). Comparing (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) leads to the identities

\sum_{\ell=i}^{\langle{i}\rangle_{q}}(-1)^{\ell-i}\gamma_{\ell}^{i}(i)=q^{\genfrac{(}{)}{0.0pt}{2}{i}{2}}

for all
i\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

We close this section with the following simple corollary.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{i},\,i=1,2,
be
q
-matroids such that
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
is representable by the matrix
G_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
.
Suppose
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cong{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
.
Then the rank-metric codes

{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G_{i}),\,i=1,2,
share all higher rank-weight enumerators.

Proof.

The assumption implies
R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}=R_{{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}}
and thus
R_{{\mathcal{M}}_{1}}=R_{{\mathcal{M}}_{2}}
thanks to
5.14
.
Now the statement follows from
4.6
.
∎

6
Weak Isomorphisms

In this section we will discuss
q
-matroids whose projectivizations are isomorphic.
It will turn out that this is equivalent to the
q
-matroids being weakly isomorphic in the sense of
6.2
below.
We need the following facts for which we refer to
[
8
, Sec. 3]
.

Remark 6.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-matroid.
The
closure
of
V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)
is defined as the subspace
\text{cl}(V)=\{x\in E\mid\rho(V+\langle{x}\rangle)=\rho(V)\}
, and
V
is called a
flat
if
\text{cl}(V)=V
.
The collection of all flats is denoted by
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
, and
({\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}),\leq,\cap,\vee)
, where
V_{1}\vee V_{2}=\text{cl}(V_{1}+V_{2})
, is a geometric lattice
with height function equal to
\rho
.
The inverse image of a flat
F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
under the closure operator is given by
\text{cl}^{-1}(F)=\{V\in{\mathcal{L}}(E)\mid\text{cl}(V)=F\}=\{V\in{\mathcal{L}}(F)\mid\rho(V)=\rho(F)\}
.
The notation
{\mathcal{F}}(\,\cdot\,)
and
\text{cl}(\,\cdot\,)
for the flats and the closure operator will also be used for matroids.

Definition 6.2
.

The
q
-matroids
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}=(E_{1},\rho_{1})
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}=(E_{2},\rho_{2})
are called
weakly isomorphic
if there exists a dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism
\alpha:{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})
, that is
\dim F=\dim\alpha(F)
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})
.

Note that since the height in the lattice of flats of a
q
-matroid equals the rank in the
q
-matroid, any lattice isomorphism between the lattices of flats of two
q
-matroids
is rank-preserving.
Thus, weakly isomorphic
q
-matroids admit a dimension- and rank-preserving lattice isomorphism between their lattices of flats.

Clearly, isomorphic
q
-matroids are weakly isomorphic. The converse is not true.

Example 6.3
.

Consider the non-isomorphic
q
-matroids
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{W}}
from
5.7
. In that case
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{V}}})={\mathcal{V}}\cup\{0,{\mathbb{F}}_{3}^{4}\}
and

{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{{\mathcal{W}}})={\mathcal{W}}\cup\{0,{\mathbb{F}}_{3}^{4}\}
; see also
[
13
, Ex. 8.4]
.
Therefore
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{V}})
and
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{W}})
admit a dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism.
Hence
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{V}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathcal{W}}
are weakly isomorphic, but not isomorphic.

For matroids, the corresponding notion of weakly isomorphic coincides with isomorphic.
This is shown in
6.4
below.
We believe that this is a well-known fact in matroid theory but could not find a source.
For simple matroids this is mentioned at various places, for instance
[
24
, Bottom of p. 54]
.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
M_{1}=(S_{1},r_{1}),\,M_{2}=(S_{2},r_{2})
be matroids and
\alpha:{\mathcal{F}}(M_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}(M_{2})
be a cardinality-preserving lattice isomorphism.
Then
M_{1}\cong M_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
S_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}(M_{i})
we have
|S_{1}|=|S_{2}|
and
r_{1}(S_{1})=r_{2}(S_{2})=:k
.
Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
be the flats of
M_{1}
of rank
1
.
Then
\alpha(F_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(F_{t})
are the flats of
M_{2}
of rank
1
.
The flat axioms imply
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}F_{j}=S_{1}
and likewise
\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}\alpha(F_{j})=S_{2}
.
Furthermore,
F_{j}\cap F_{j^{\prime}}=\text{cl}_{1}(\emptyset)
and
\alpha(F_{j})\cap\alpha(F_{j^{\prime}})=\text{cl}_{2}(\emptyset)=\alpha(\text{cl}_{1}(\emptyset))
for all
j\neq j^{\prime}

(where
\text{cl}_{i}(\cdot)
denotes the closure in
M_{i}
).
By assumption
|F_{j}|=|\alpha(F_{j})|
for all
j\in[t]
.
All of this implies the existence of a bijection

\beta:S_{1}\longrightarrow S_{2}\ \text{ such that $\beta(\text{cl}_{1}(\emptyset))=\text{cl}_{2}(\emptyset)$ and $\beta(F_{j})=\alpha(F_{j})$ for $j\in[t]$.}

Note that
\beta
is not unique.

1) We show that
\beta(F)=\alpha(F)
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(M_{1})
.
By construction this is true for the flats of rank at most
1
.
Choose
F\in{\mathcal{F}}(M_{1})
and let
F_{i_{1}},\ldots,F_{i_{\ell}}
be the flats of rank
1
contained in
F
.
Then
F=\bigvee_{j=1}^{\ell}F_{i_{j}}
(see also
[
24
, Thm. 1.7.5]
).
But then we even have
F=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}F_{i_{j}}
.
Indeed, for every
s\in F
we have
\text{cl}_{1}(\{s\})\subseteq F
and either
\text{cl}_{1}(\{s\})=\text{cl}_{1}(\emptyset)
or
\text{cl}_{1}(\{s\})
has rank
1
.
In either case
s\in\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}F_{i_{j}}
and thus
F=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}F_{i_{j}}
.
Similarly we have
\bigvee_{j=1}^{\ell}\alpha(F_{i_{j}})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\alpha(F_{i_{j}})
.
All of this gives us
\beta(F)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta(F_{i_{j}})=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\ell}\alpha(F_{i_{j}})=\bigvee_{j=1}^{\ell}\alpha(F_{i_{j}})=\alpha(\bigvee_{j=1}^{\ell}F_{i_{j}})=\alpha(F)
, where the fourth step follows from the fact that
\alpha
is a lattice isomorphism.

2) We show that
\text{cl}_{2}(\beta(V))=\alpha(\text{cl}_{1}(V))
for all subsets
V\subseteq S_{1}
. Let
\text{cl}_{1}(V)=F
and
\text{cl}_{2}(\beta(V))=G
.
Since
\beta(V)\subseteq\beta(F)=\alpha(F)
, which is a flat, we have
G\subseteq\alpha(F)
.
In the same way,
V=\beta^{-1}(\beta(V))\subseteq\beta^{-1}(G)=\alpha^{-1}(G)
and thus
F=\text{cl}_{1}(V)\subseteq\alpha^{-1}(G)
because
the latter is a flat. Thus
\alpha(F)\subseteq G
and all of this implies
\text{cl}_{2}(\beta(V))=G=\alpha(F)=\alpha(\text{cl}_{1}(V))
, as desired.

3) Let
V\subseteq S_{1}
. Using that
\alpha
is rank-preserving (because it is height-preserving), we obtain

r_{1}(V)=r_{1}(\text{cl}_{1}(V))=r_{2}(\alpha(\text{cl}_{1}(V)))=r_{2}(\text{cl}_{2}(\beta(V)))=r_{2}(\beta(V))
.
Hence
\beta
is an isomorphism between the matroids
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
.
∎

We return to
q
-matroids and show that
q
-matroids are weakly isomorphic iff their projectivizations are isomorphic.
To do so, we need the following fact.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a
q
-matroid and
{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}
be its projectivization. Then

\gamma:{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}),\quad F\longmapsto{\mathbb{P}}F

is a bijection satisfying
F\leq F^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\gamma(F)\subseteq\gamma(F^{\prime})
for all
F,F^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
.
In particular,
\gamma
is a lattice isomorphism.
Furthermore,
|\gamma(F)|=(q^{\dim F}-1)/(q-1)
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}})
.

Proof.

The fact that
\gamma
is a bijection and
\gamma
and
\gamma^{-1}
are order-preserving can be found in
[
19
, Lem. 16, Prop. 21]
(see also
[
18
, Lem. 3.2]
). Thus
\gamma
is a lattice isomorphism; see also

[
14
, Lem. 6.2]
.
The last part about
|\gamma(F)|
is clear.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the desired equivalence.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
be
q
-matroids. Then

{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cong{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}\Longleftrightarrow\text{${\mathcal{M}}_{1}$ and ${\mathcal{M}}_{2}$ are weakly isomorphic.}

As a consequence, weakly isomorphic
q
-matroids share the same Whitney function.

Proof.

Thanks to
6.4

{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{1}\cong{\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
is equivalent to the existence of a
cardinality-preserving lattice isomorphism between
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{1})
and
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathbb{P}}{\mathcal{M}}_{2})
.
By
Lemma

6.5
this is equivalent to the existence of
a dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism between
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})
and
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})
and thus to
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
being weakly isomorphic.
The consequence follows from
5.12
.
∎

We derive another property of weakly isomorphic
q
-matroids.
The following notion will be needed.

Definition 6.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}=(E_{i},\rho_{i}),\,i=1,2
, be
q
-matroids and fix subsets
{\mathcal{V}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}(E_{i})
. A map
\alpha:{\mathcal{V}}_{1}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{V}}_{2}
is called a

dr-map
if
\alpha
is dimension- and rank-preserving, that is,
\dim V=\dim\alpha(V)
and
\rho_{1}(V)=\rho_{2}(\alpha(V))
for
all
V\in{\mathcal{V}}_{1}
.
If in addition,
\alpha
is a bijection, we call it a
dr-bijection
.

The notion of a dr-bijection depends on the underlying
q
-matroids. The latter will always be clear from the context.
Now we obtain the following result.
Note that it implies that weakly isomorphic
q
-matroids have the same Whitney function – as we know already from
6.6
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}=(E_{1},\rho_{1})
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}=(E_{2},\rho_{2})
be weakly isomorphic
q
-matroids and
\alpha:{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})
a dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism.
Then
\alpha
extends to a dr-bijection
\beta:{\mathcal{L}}(E_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(E_{2})
such that
\beta(\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(F))=\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(F))
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})
.

As we know from
6.3
there exist non-isomorphic
q
-matroids that admit a
dimension-preserving lattice isomorphism between their lattices of flats.
This implies that the bijection
\beta
in
6.8
is in general not order-preserving.
Indeed, if
\beta
was order-preserving it would be a lattice isomorphism and thus be induced by a semi-linear isomorphism
between
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
thanks to the Fundamental Theorem of Projective Geometry.
Thus, the
q
-matroids would be isomorphic if the ground field is a prime field.

Proof.

Since
E_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{M}}_{i})
, we have
\dim E_{1}=\dim E_{2}
and
\rho_{1}(E_{1})=\rho_{2}(E_{2})=:k
.
For
i=1,2
and
r\in[k]_{0}
define

{\mathcal{L}}_{r}(E_{i})=\{V\leq E_{i}\mid\rho_{i}(V)=r\}\ \text{ and }\ {\mathcal{L}}_{\leq r}(E_{i})=\{V\leq E_{i}\mid\rho_{i}(V)\leq r\}.

In the same way, define
{\mathcal{F}}_{r}({\mathcal{M}}_{i})
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{\leq r}({\mathcal{M}}_{i})
.
It suffices to show that for every
r
there exists a dr-bijection
\beta_{r}:{\mathcal{L}}_{r}(E_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{r}(E_{2})
such that

\beta_{r}(F)=\alpha(F)\ \text{ and }\ \beta_{r}(\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(F))=\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(F))\ \text{ for all $F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{r}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})$.}

(6.1)

We induct on
r
.

1) Let
r=0
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}({\mathcal{M}}_{i})=\{\text{cl}_{i}(0)\}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(E_{i})={\mathcal{L}}(\text{cl}_{i}(0))
.
By assumption
\dim\text{cl}_{1}(0)=\dim\text{cl}_{2}(0)
, and hence the subspaces in
{\mathcal{L}}(\text{cl}_{1}(0))
and
{\mathcal{L}}(\text{cl}_{2}(0))
are in a dimension-preserving one-to-one-correspondence.
In other words, there exists a dr-bijection
\beta_{0}:{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(E_{1})\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(E_{2})
satisfying (
6.1
).

2) Suppose that for all
r\leq s
there exists a dr-bijection
\beta_{r}
satisfying (
6.1
).
Set
{\mathcal{F}}_{s+1}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})=\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\}
.
Then
{\mathcal{F}}_{s+1}({\mathcal{M}}_{2})=\{\alpha(F_{1}),\ldots,\alpha(F_{t})\}
and

\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(F_{j})={\mathcal{L}}_{s+1}(E_{1})\ \text{ and }\bigcup_{j=1}^{t}\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(F_{j}))={\mathcal{L}}_{s+1}(E_{2})\ \text{ and the unions are disjoint.}

(6.2)

We will show that for every
j\in[t]
there exists a dr-bijection

\gamma_{j}:\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(F_{j})\longrightarrow\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(F_{j}))
.
To do so, fix
j\in[t]
and let
{\mathcal{L}}(F_{j})\cap{\mathcal{F}}_{\leq s}({\mathcal{M}}_{1})=\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{a}\}
(these are the flats in the sublattice strictly below
F_{j}
).
Since
\alpha
is a lattice isomorphism, we obtain

{\mathcal{L}}(F_{j})=\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(F_{j})\cup\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{a}\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(G_{\ell})\ \text{ and }{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha(F_{j}))=\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(F_{j}))\cup\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{a}\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(G_{\ell})),

(6.3)

where all unions are disjoint.
Using suitable restrictions of the maps
\beta_{r}
for
r\leq s
we obtain a dr-bijection

\hat{\beta}:\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{a}\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(G_{\ell})\longrightarrow\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{a}\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(G_{\ell})).

Since
\dim F_{j}=\dim\alpha(F_{j})
, the subspaces in
{\mathcal{L}}(F_{j})
and
{\mathcal{L}}(\alpha(F_{j}))
are in a one-to-one dimension-preserving correspondence,
and therefore (
6.3
) implies the existence of a dimension-preserving bijection

\gamma_{j}:\text{cl}_{1}^{-1}(F_{j})\longrightarrow\text{cl}_{2}^{-1}(\alpha(F_{j}))
. It is trivially rank-preserving.
Moreover, its dimension-preserving property implies that
\gamma_{j}(F_{j})=\alpha(F_{j})
.
Using (
6.2
) and combining all dr-bijections
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t}
we arrive at the desired dr-bijection
\beta_{s+1}
.
∎

The following examples illustrate that for the results above both properties of the map
\alpha
in
6.2
are crucial,
being a lattice isomorphism and being dimension-preserving.

Example 6.9
.

A simple example of

-matroids where the lattices of flats are isomorphic but do not admit a dimension-preserving isomorphism is given by the

Open Problems

The discussion of the last section gives rise to two immediate questions.
First, do
q
-matroids that admit a dr-bijection between their lattices of flats share the same Whitney function (as is the case in
6.11
)?
We do not know whether the analogous question for matroids can be answered in the affirmative.
If the latter is the case, then the same is true for
q
-matroids thanks to
Lemma

6.5
and
5.12
.
Secondly, suppose
{\mathcal{M}}_{G_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}_{G_{2}}
are weakly isomorphic representable
q
-matroids.
What can be said about the higher support distributions of the rank-metric codes
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}=\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G_{1})
and
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=\mbox{\rm rowsp}(G_{2})
?

More generally, it remains to be investigated whether the coboundary polynomial for
q
-matroids (defined suitably) also fits into the family of polynomial invariants related by monomial substitutions, and, furthermore, what can be said about the Möbius polynomial?

Finally, can the results on polynomial invariants be generalized to
q
-polymatroids and
{\mathbb{F}}
-linear rank-metric codes?

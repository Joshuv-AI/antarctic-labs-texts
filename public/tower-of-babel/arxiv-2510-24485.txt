The q q -Laplace Transforms compared: the basic confluent hypergeometric function ϕ 0 2 {}_{2}\phi_{0}
Daniel Meikle, Adri Olde Daalhuis

Abstract
. In solving q q -difference equations, and in the definition of q q -special functions, we encounter formal power series in which the n n th coefficient is of size q − ( n 2 ) q^{-\binom{n}{2}} with q ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) q\in(0,1) fixed. To make sense of these formal series, a q q -Borel-Laplace resummation is required. There are three candidates for the q q -Laplace transform, resulting in three different resummations. Surprisingly, the differences between these resummations have not been discussed in the literature. Our main result provides explicit formulas for these q q -exponentially small differences. We also give simple Mellin–Barnes integral representations for all the basic hypergeometric ϕ s r {}_{r}\phi_{s} functions and derive a third (discrete) orthogonality condition for the Stieltjes–Wigert polynomials. As the main application, we introduce three resummations for the ϕ 0 2 {}_{2}\phi_{0} functions which can be seen as q q versions of the Kummer U U functions. We derive many of their properties, including interesting integral and sum representations, connection formulas, and error bounds.

Introduction and Summary

Solving differential or difference equations via series solutions often leads to Gevrey 1 divergent series of the
form
\hat{w}(z)=\sum_{n}a_{n}z^{n}
. These series have coefficients that grow like

\frac{K\Gamma(n+\alpha)}{F^{n+\alpha}}
as
n\to\infty
, resulting in a growth rate of “factorial over power”.

One way to make sense of these formal series is the introduction of the Borel transform:

B(t)=\sum_{n}\frac{a_{n}}{\Gamma(n+\alpha)}t^{n}
. This infinite series has a finite radius of convergence,
and the singularities in the finite Borel plane (the complex
t
-plane) cause the Stokes phenomenon.
Each singularity corresponds to a specific exponentially-small scale.

To return to the
z
-plane, one takes the Laplace transform, defined as

w_{B}(z)=z^{-\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-t/z}B(t)t^{\alpha-1}{\rm d}t
. Note that any function that will have

w(z)\sim\sum_{n}a_{n}z^{n}
as
z\to 0
in a complex sector of opening more than
\pi
will be uniquely
determined by this divergent series, that is,
w(z)=w_{B}(z)
.
For more details, see, for example,
[
8
,
9
]

In contrast to Gevrey 1 asymptotics, solving

In these problems, there will be an additional parameter

Having three
q
-Laplace transforms will also give us three different
q
-Borel-Laplace resummations of the
original divergent series. It is very surprising that these differences have not been discussed in the literature.
Is there a best
q
-Borel-Laplace resummation? Note that we also lose the uniqueness property: all three
resummations will have the original divergent series as its asymptotic series
as
z\to 0
in complex sectors of opening more than
\pi

It should be obvious from these observations that the difference of two of these
q
-Borel-Laplace resummations
is
q
-exponentially small, that is, it should be of size smaller than any term in the original divergent series.
To be more precise, the difference is of size

{\rm E}_{q}\left(z\right)
. The growth of this elementary function
lies in between algebraic and exponential as
z\to 0
and
z\to\infty
.
In Section
3
, we give exact formulas for the differences. They are in terms of the functions
that are being switched on via the Stokes phenomenon.

The first
q
-Borel-Laplace resummation
(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)
is in terms of the elementary function
{\rm E}_{q}\left(z\right)
.
The resummation is a multivalued function, having the required asymptotic expansions

(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)\sim\sum_{n}{a_{n}}z^{n}
for
all

\arg z
.
The main reason for this is that, in contrast to
\exp(-1/z)
, the function
{\rm E}_{q}\left(z\right)
is exponentially small
for all
\arg z
.

The second
q
-Borel-Laplace resummation
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)
is in terms of a reciprocal
\theta_{q}
function.
Again, the resummation is multivalued, but now
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)\sim\sum_{n}{a_{n}}z^{n}

holds for
|\arg z|<2\pi
, because the reciprocal
\theta_{q}
function
is switched on via the Stokes phenomenon and it has poles along
\arg z=\pm 2\pi
.

Finally, the third
q
-Borel-Laplace resummation
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}B)(z)
has a free parameter
\lambda
. It is in terms
of an infinite series of reciprocal
\theta_{q}
functions. This resummation is one-valued, and we have

(\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}B)(z)\sim\sum_{n}{a_{n}}z^{n}
for
|\arg(z/\lambda)|<\pi
, because of the poles of the reciprocal

\theta_{q}
function.

One could argue that
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}B)(z)
is the most natural resummation because it is one-valued, but it has
poles in an artificial direction. If somehow one could link these poles to the original problem (
q
-difference equation)
then this could be the most natural resummation. However the Mellin–Barnes integral representation (
2.3
)
for the basic hypergeometric functions suggest that
(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)
is the most natural.
Moreover, in a sequel to this paper, we will discuss Mellin–Barnes integral representations for all
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
.
In that paper, it will be demonstrated that in the case of
q
-Gevrey series of order
k
with
k>1

(needed when
r-s-1=k>1
), the obvious
(\mathcal{L}_{q^{k},\lambda}B)(z)
resummation does not always solve the
original
q
-difference equation.

3.
q
-Laplace Transforms

For a series
B(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}
, the
q
-Laplace transform is formally defined as

({\mathcal{L}_{q}}B)(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{-\binom{n}{2}}z^{n}
, that is, the three
q
-Laplace transforms give
rise to the same divergent asymptotic expansions, but with different sectors of validity.
Hence, the difference between two of these

q
-Laplace transforms should be
q
-exponentially small. The
q
-Laplace transform
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}B)(z)

is typically one-valued, whereas
(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)
and
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)
are typically multi-valued.
Let us assume the simplest case in which they have connection formulas of the form

(3.1)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}})&=(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)-2\pi{\rm i}{C_{q}}S(z){\rm E}_{q}\left(z{\rm e}^{\pi{\rm i}}\right),\\
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z{\rm e}^{2\pi{\rm i}})&=(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)+\frac{2\pi{\rm i}S(z)}{\ln(q){\rm\theta}_{q}\left(-z\right)},\end{split}

with
S(z)
an entire function. The final terms in (
3.1
) are the
q
-exponentially small
terms that will be switched on via the Stokes phenomenon.
The growth of
{\rm E}_{q}\left(z\right)
lies in between algebraic and exponential as
z\to 0
and
z\to\infty
.
The function
1/{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(z\right)
has the same growth rate as long as we stay away from the poles.
One big difference with normal exponentials is that
{\rm E}_{q}\left(z\right)
is
q
-exponentially small on all Riemann
sheets. Hence, in the case of
(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)
, the Stokes phenomenon switches on terms that will be

q
-exponentially small on all Riemann sheets. For
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)
, the switched-on
terms will be
q
-exponentially small until we encounter the poles of
1/{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(-z\right)
.
These observations result in the following sectors of validity:

(3.2)

\begin{split}(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)&\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{-\binom{n}{2}}z^{n},\qquad{\rm for~all~}\arg z\\
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)&\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{-\binom{n}{2}}z^{n},\qquad|\arg z|<2\pi,\\
(\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}B)(z)&\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{-\binom{n}{2}}z^{n},\qquad|\arg(z/\lambda)|<\pi,\end{split}

as
|z|\to 0
. In Section
7
, we will illustrate this for the case of
{{}_{2}\phi_{0}}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{-};q;z\right)
.

In the following theorem, we give an exact formula for the difference of two
q
-Laplace transforms.
The function
S(z)
that is switched on via the Stokes phenomenon plays a crucial role.
The proof is long and is moved to the Appendix.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S(t)
be an entire function of order and type zero with the additional constraint that
there exist
M>0
and

|S(t)|\leq M\left(-c|t|;q\right)_{\infty}
for all

or equivalently, if connection formulas (
3.1

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)-(\mathcal{L}_{q,E}B)(z)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{q,\theta}B)(z)-(\mathcal{L}_{q,\lambda}B)(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{S(z)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(-z\right)}P_{q}^{(d)}(z;\lambda),

in which

(3.6)

\displaystyle P_{q}^{(c)}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\pi}{\ln q}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(-1\right)^{n}{\hat{q}}^{n^{2}}\frac{\sin\left(2\pi n\frac{\ln z}{\ln q}\right)}{\sinh\left(n\ln{\hat{q}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{\ln q}\int_{q}^{1}\mathcal{P}_{q}\left(t\right)\left(\frac{t{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(t\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(t\right)}-\frac{t{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(t/z\right)}{z{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(t/z\right)}+\frac{\ln z}{\ln q}\right)\frac{{\rm d}t}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln z}{\ln q}+\frac{1}{z\ln q}\int_{q}^{1}\mathcal{P}_{q}\left(t\right)\frac{{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(t/z\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(t/z\right)}{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda\right)}-\frac{\lambda{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda/z\right)}{z{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda/z\right)}+\frac{\ln z}{\ln q},

\displaystyle P_{q}^{(d)}(z;\lambda_{1})-P_{q}^{(d)}(z;\lambda_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{1}{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda_{1}\right)}-\frac{\lambda_{1}{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{1}/z\right)}{z{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda_{1}/z\right)}-\frac{\lambda_{2}{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{2}\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda_{2}\right)}+\frac{\lambda_{2}{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda_{2}/z\right)}{z{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda_{2}/z\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}^{3}{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(-z\right){\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\frac{-\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\right){\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\frac{-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{z}\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda_{1}\right){\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda_{2}\right){\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{z}\right){\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{2}}\right)},

are
q
-periodic functions. The right-hand sides of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) have removable
singularities at

,

:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to q^{m}}\frac{P_{q}^{(c)}(z)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(-z\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m}q^{\binom{m}{2}}}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}^{3}}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\ln q}\right)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(-1\right)^{n}{\hat{q}}^{n^{2}}\frac{n}{\sinh\left(n\ln{\hat{q}}\right)},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to q^{m}}\frac{P_{q}^{(d)}(z;\lambda)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(-z\right)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m-1}q^{\binom{m}{2}}}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{\ln q}+\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda q^{n}}{\left(1+\lambda q^{n}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m-1}q^{\binom{m}{2}}}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}^{3}}\left(\frac{1}{\ln q}+\lambda\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\lambda}\left(\frac{\lambda{\rm\theta}_{q}^{\prime}\left(\lambda\right)}{{\rm\theta}_{q}\left(\lambda\right)}\right)\right).

will show up when we discuss the asymptotics of solutions of three term
q
-difference equations.

Corollary 3.1

7.
Error Bounds

The three functions
{\rm U}_{q,k}\left(a,b;z\right)
are resummations of
{{}_{2}\phi_{0}}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{-};q;z\right)
. We still have
to show that this divergent series is the asymptotic expansion of
{\rm U}_{q,k}\left(a,b;z\right)
as
z\to 0
.
We denote (
5.4
) as

(7.1)

{\rm U}_{q,k}\left(a,b;z\right)=K_{0}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u(t)}{t+z}\kappa\left(qt\right){\rm d}t,

with
K_{0}=\frac{\left(a,b;q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q;q\right)_{\infty}}
and

(7.2)

\begin{split}u(t)&=\left(-abt;q\right)_{\infty}{{}_{2}\phi_{1}}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{q}{a},\frac{q}{b}}{0};q;-abt\right)=\left(-aqt;q\right)_{\infty}{{}_{1}\phi_{1}}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{\frac{q}{b}}{-aqt};q;-bqt\right)\\
&=\frac{\left(\frac{q}{a},-aqt,\frac{-1}{at};q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\frac{b}{a};q\right)_{\infty}}{{}_{2}\phi_{1}}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,0}{\frac{aq}{b}};q;\frac{-q}{abt}\right)+\frac{\left(\frac{q}{b},-bqt,\frac{-1}{bt};q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(\frac{a}{b};q\right)_{\infty}}{{}_{2}\phi_{1}}\!\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{b,0}{\frac{bq}{a}};q;\frac{-q}{abt}\right).\end{split}

The first line in (
7.2
) shows that
u(t)
is bounded for finite
t
, and the final
representation shows that for large
t
, the growth of
u(t)
is dominated by the factors

\left(-aqt;q\right)_{\infty}
and
\left(-bqt;q\right)_{\infty}
. Let
c=\max(|a|,|b|)
and for
M_{q}(a,b)
, a positive
constant such that

(7.3)

|u(t)|\leq M_{q}(a,b)\left(-cq|t|;q\right)_{\infty},\quad{\rm for~all~}t{\rm~in~the~half~plane}\quad\Re t\geq 0.

Theorem 7.1
.

We take
c=\max(|a|,|b|)
and for
M_{q}(a,b)
, a positive
constant such that (
7.3
) holds.
Take
N
a positive integer with
N>1-\frac{\ln c}{\ln q}
and define the remainder via

(7.4)

{\rm U}_{q,k}\left(a,b;z\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{\left(a,b;q\right)_{n}}{\left(q;q\right)_{n}}q^{-\binom{n}{2}}\left(-z\right)^{n}+R_{N,k}(a,b;z).

Then in the half-plane
\Re z\geq 0
, we have

(7.5)

\left|R_{N,k}(a,b;z)\right|\leq\left|z^{N}\right|\left|K_{0}\right|\frac{M_{q}(a,b)q^{-\binom{N}{2}}}{\left(cq^{N};q\right)_{\infty}},

for all three cases
k=E,\theta,\lambda
. In the case
k=\lambda
, this bound holds for

\lambda>0
, and we do not require
N>1-\frac{\ln c}{\ln q}
.

In the sectors
\left|\arg z\right|\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
, we have

(7.6)

\displaystyle\left|R_{N,E}(a,b;z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|z^{N}\right|\left|K_{0}\right|\frac{M_{q}(a,b)\exp\left({\frac{-\zeta^{2}}{2\ln q}}\right)q^{-\binom{N}{2}}}{\left(cq^{N};q\right)_{\infty}},

(7.7)

\displaystyle\left|R_{N,\theta}(a,b;z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|z^{N}\right|\left|K_{0}\right|\frac{M_{q}(a,b)\exp\left({\frac{-\zeta^{2}}{2\ln q}}\right)q^{-\binom{N}{2}}}{\left(cq^{N};q\right)_{\infty}\left({\hat{q}}^{2};{\hat{q}}^{2}\right)_{\infty}^{3}}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{{\hat{q}}}}{1-\sqrt{{\hat{q}}}}\right),

with
\zeta=\left|\arg z\right|-\frac{\pi}{2}
and

(7.8)

\left|R_{N,\lambda}(a,b;z)\right|\leq\left|z^{N}\right|\left|K_{0}\right|\frac{M_{q}(a,b)q^{-\binom{N}{2}}}{\left(cq^{N};q\right)_{\infty}|\sin\xi|},

where

.

In the half-plane
\Re z\geq 0
, we have the relatively sharp error bound (

and the bound is the same for the three cases! In the case

at

q
-exponentially small term that appears on the right-hand side of

increases, in this case, by a factor
\exp\left({\frac{-\zeta^{2}}{2\ln q}}\right)
in (

This is very similar for the error bounds for the Kummer

[

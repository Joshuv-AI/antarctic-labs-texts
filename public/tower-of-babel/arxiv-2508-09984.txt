Exceptional zeros of GL 3 × GL 3 \mathrm{GL}_{3}\times\mathrm{GL}_{3} Rankin–Selberg L L -functions
Jesse Thorner

Abstract
. Let χ \chi be an idele class character over a number field F F , and let π , π ′ \pi,\pi^{\prime} be any two cuspidal automorphic representations of GL 2 ​ ( 𝔸 F ) \mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}) . We prove that the Rankin–Selberg L L -function L ⁡ ( s , Sym 2 ​ ( π ) × ( Sym 2 ​ ( π ′ ) ⊗ χ ) ) L(s,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi)\times(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\chi)) has a “standard” zero-free region with no exceptional Landau–Siegel zero except possibly when it is divisible by the L L -function of a real idele class character. In particular, no such zero exists if π \pi is non-dihedral and π ′ \pi^{\prime} is not a twist of π \pi . Until now, this was only known when π = π ′ \pi=\pi^{\prime} , π \pi is self-dual, and χ \chi is trivial.

2.1.
Standard
L
-functions

Let
\pi\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
, let
\widetilde{\pi}\in\mathfrak{F}_{n}
be the contragredient, and let
\omega_{\pi}
the central character. We express
\pi
as a restricted tensor product
\bigotimes_{v}\pi_{v}
of smooth admissible representations of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F_{v})
. Let
\delta_{\pi}=1
if
\pi=\mathbbm{1}
and
\delta_{\pi}=0
otherwise. Define the sets
S_{\pi}=\{v\nmid\infty\colon\textup{$\pi_{v}$ ramified}\}
and
S_{\pi}^{\infty}=S_{\pi}\cup\{v\mid\infty\}
. Let
N_{\pi}
be the norm of the conductor of
\pi
. If

, then there are
n
Satake parameters
(\alpha_{j,\pi}(v))_{j=1}^{n}
such that

L(s,\pi)=\prod_{v\nmid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v}),\qquad L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)q_{v}^{-s}}

converges absolutely for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
. If

, then at least one of the
\alpha_{j,\pi}(p)
equals zero.

If
v\mid\infty
, then
(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))_{j=1}^{n}
are the Langlands parameters at
v
, from which we define

\Gamma_{v}(s)=\begin{cases}\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{R}$,}\\
2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)&\mbox{if $F_{v}=\mathbb{C}$.}\end{cases}

and

L(s,\pi_{\infty})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}L(s,\pi_{v})=\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma_{v}(s+\mu_{j,\pi}(v))

The completed
L
-function
\Lambda(s,\pi)=(s(1-s))^{\delta_{\pi}}(D_{F}^{n}N_{\pi})^{\frac{s}{2}}L(s,\pi)L(s,\pi_{\infty})
is entire of order
1
. Since
\{\alpha_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
,

, and
\{\mu_{j,\widetilde{\pi}}(v)\}=\{\overline{\mu_{j,\pi}(v)}\}
, we have that
L(s,\widetilde{\pi})=\overline{L(\overline{s},\pi)}
. The analytic conductor is

(2.1)

C(\pi)=D_{F}^{n}N_{\pi}\prod_{v\mid\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(|\mu_{j,\pi}(v)|+3)^{[F_{v}:\mathbb{R}]}.

By
[
19
,
21
]
there exists
\theta_{n}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n^{2}+1}]
such that

(2.2)

|\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)|\leq q_{v}^{\theta_{n}},\qquad\mathrm{Re}(\mu_{j,\pi}(v))\geq-\theta_{n}.

We define
a_{\pi}(v^{\ell})
by the Dirichlet series identity

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\pi)=\sum_{v}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j,\pi}(v)^{\ell}\log q_{v}}{q_{v}^{\ell s}}=\sum_{v\nmid\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{\pi}(v^{\ell})\log q_{v}}{q_{v}^{\ell s}}.

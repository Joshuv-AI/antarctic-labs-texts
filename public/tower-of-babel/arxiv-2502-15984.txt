Abstract
. We start by providing a very simple and elementary new proof of the classical bound due to J. Beck which states that the spherical cap 𝕃 2 \mathbb{L}_{2} -discrepancy of any N N points on the unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , is at least of the order N − 1 2 − 1 2 ​ d N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}} . The argument used in this proof leads us to many further new results: estimates of the discrepancy in terms of various geometric quantities, an easy proof of point-independent upper estimates for the sum of positive powers of Euclidean distances between points on the sphere, lower bounds for the discrepancy of rectifiable curves and sets of arbitrary Hausdorff dimension. Moreover, refinements of the proof also allow us to obtain explicit values of the constants in the lower discrepancy bound on 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} . The value of the obtained asymptotic constant falls within 3 % 3\% of the conjectured optimal constant on 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{2} (and within up to 7 % 7\% on 𝕊 4 \mathbb{S}^{4} , 𝕊 8 \mathbb{S}^{8} , 𝕊 24 \mathbb{S}^{24} ).

1.
Introduction

The
spherical cap
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-discrepancy

(1)

D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}){:=}\sqrt{\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left|\frac{\big|\big\{k:\mathbf{x}_{k}\in C(\mathbf{x},t)\big\}\big|}{N}-\sigma_{d}(C(\mathbf{x},t))\right|^{2}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd t}

measures the irregularity of
N
points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}
on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
,
d\geq 2
, with respect to spherical caps
C(\mathbf{x},t){:=}\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:\mathbf{z}\cdot\mathbf{x}\geq t\}
in the
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-sense. Here,
\sigma_{d}
denotes the normalized uniform surface area measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. In other words,
D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}
is the quadratic average of the deviation of the discrete empirical measure generated by the points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}
from the uniform measure.

The main postulate of the theory of irregularity of distributions states that discrete point configurations cannot be ‘too uniform’. In the case of spherical cap
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-discrepancy, this is manifested by the following lower bound due to Beck
[
3
]
, which is uniform for
N\geq 2
: there exists a constant
c_{d}>0
such that for all
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the discrepancy satisfies

(2)

D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\geq c_{d}N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}}.

This bound provides the optimal order of the spherical cap
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-discrepancy in terms of the number of points
N
as can be seen from Stolarsky’s invariance principle
[
26
]
(cf. (
6
) below). Beck’s proof of (
2
) relied on the Fourier transform method, and an alternative proof given in
[
5
]
relied on spherical harmonics and Gegenbauer polynomial expansions.

In Section
2
of this paper we provide a new and simple proof of Beck’s lower bound (
2
). In addition to being completely elementary, this argument is powerful enough to allow for a variety of extensions, corollaries, and generalizations.

In particular, in Section
3
we provide new lower bounds for the discrepancy
D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}
in terms of geometric quantities such as the norm of the centroid and the frame potential.

In Section
4
we prove analogs of Beck’s bound for the discrepancy of sets of arbitrary Hausdorff dimension
0\leq s\leq d
(rather than just point sets). This question did not appear to be accessible with any of the previous methods.

Moreover, in Section
5
we carefully analyze and refine the argument employed in Section
2
to obtain explicit constants in Beck’s lower bound (
2

\mathbb{S}^{2}
(cf.
[
10
]
) as we observe that the asymptotical constant recovers one significant digit (ca.
97\%
) of the factor of the leading term in the asymptotics of the minimal discrepancy. Similarly, this analysis recovers
96\%

\mathbb{S}^{8}

In Section

we apply the same argument as in Section

to obtain a much simpler proof of the result of
[
27

on the lower bound for the difference between the sum and integral of powers of Euclidean distances for the points on the sphere. We compare the constant in our bound with the conjectured constant of the asymptotic expansion (Figure

To finish the introduction, we would like to note that Beck’s lower bound (
2
) is tight.
Indeed, while the ‘typical’ discrepancy of
N

\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{N}

\mathbb{S}^{d}
has order
N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
as can be seen from

(this easily follows from Stolarsky’s invariance principle presented in (

As to other random point processes, the precise asymptotic behaviour with optimal order is known for the spherical ensembles on

2.
A simple proof of Beck’s lower bound

In this note we give a short proof of the lower bound (
2
) which is completely elementary in nature, only uses basic calculus, some asymptotic analysis, and does not appeal to Fourier analysis. The presentation is essentially self-contained. The argument also allows for obtaining explicit values of the constant
c_{d}
in (
2
) and extends to more general settings including positive powers of the distances between
N
points on the sphere.

Preliminaries:
We shall use the Pochhammer symbol to denote rising factorials

{\left(a\right)_{0}}:=1,\qquad{\left(a\right)_{n+1}}{:=}\left(a+n\right){\left(a\right)_{n}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

which satisfy the following identities (
[
19
, Section 5.2(iii)]
)

(3)

\binom{a}{n}=(-1)^{n}\frac{{\left(-a\right)_{n}}}{n!},\qquad{\left(a\right)_{n}}=\frac{\gammafcn(a+n)}{\gammafcn(a)},\qquad{\left(a\right)_{2n}}=2^{2n}{\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)_{n}}{\left(\frac{a+1}{2}\right)_{n}}.

We also need the asymptotic behaviour of a ratio of gamma functions (
[
19
, Section 5.11(iii)]
),

\frac{\gammafcn(n+a)}{\gammafcn(n+b)}\sim n^{a-b}\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$ in $\mathbb{N}$.}

Furthermore, we observe that the power series expansion

\left(1-t\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{m}t^{m}

converges absolutely in
(-1,1)
and at the endpoints
\pm 1
because of

(4)

(-1)^{m}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{m}=\frac{{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}=\frac{1}{\gammafcn(-\frac{1}{2})}\,\frac{\gammafcn(m-\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(m+1)}\sim-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{m^{\frac{3}{2}}}\qquad\text{as $m\to\infty$.}

Thus, using the fact that

\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|^{2}=\left(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right)\cdot\left(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right)=2-2\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y},\qquad\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},

we get an absolutely converging series expansion of the distance

(5)

\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|=\sqrt{2}\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{m}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m},\qquad\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}.

The connection between discrepancy and sum of distances is provided by
Stolarsky’s invariance principle
(cf.
[
26
,
11
,
6
]

) given in
[

): for all
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}

(6)

\frac{1}{C_{d}}\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{j}-\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\|.

This shows that minimizing the spherical cap discrepancy is equivalent to maximizing the sum of Euclidean distances.

The constant
C_{d}
is expressed in terms of the surface area
\omega_{d}
of
\mathbb{S}^{d}
or gamma functions as

(7)

C_{2}:=\frac{1}{4},\qquad C_{d}{:=}\frac{1}{d}\,\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}=\frac{1}{d}\,\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\gammafcn(\frac{d}{2})},\quad d\geq 2.

It represents the ratio of the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the surface area measure of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

Substituting the series expansion of the distance (
5
) into (
6
) and rearranging the terms, we obtain

(8)

\begin{split}&\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\\
&\phantom{eq}=\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})\right).\end{split}

A crucial observation is that the coefficients
C_{d}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}
,
m\geq 1
, are all positive. The rest of the proof of (
2
) relies on two simple lemmas, whose proofs are postponed to Appendix
A
.

The first lemma shows that all of the parenthetical expressions in (
8
) are non-negative.

Lemma 1
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
For all
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and for any
m\geq 0
,

(9)

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})\geq 0.

This result can be viewed as a real-valued version of the classical Welch bounds
[
29
]
and it can be traced back to
[
25
]
. It follows easily from a well-known fact in classical potential theory stating that for positive definite kernels on the sphere, the associated energy is minimized by the uniform surface area measure, see e.g.
[
22
,
9
]
, but for the sake of completeness we provide a simple self-contained proof in Appendix
A
.

The left-hand side of (
9
) vanishes for all spherical
m
-designs (i.e., such configurations
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
that
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}p(\mathbf{x}_{j})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}p(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})
for all polynomials of degree at most
m
; cf.

[
7
]
). A full characterization of all sets for which the left-hand side is zero is slightly more complicated, and we do not discuss it in detail (when
m=1
and
m=2
, it is discussed in Section
3
). Observe that for odd
m
, one obtains zero for any centrally symmetric (antipodal) configuration.

The second lemma provides the value and the asymptotics of the integrals in (
8
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
d\geq 2
.
If
m=2r-1
is odd, then
\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})=0}
.

If
m=2r
is even, then

(10)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\sim\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}\,\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d}{2}}}\qquad\text{as $r\to\infty$}

and the asymptotic term is a strict upper bound for all
r\geq 1
.

Assuming these two lemmas, we are ready to give a new simple proof of Beck’s lower bound (
2
).
Utilizing non-negativity of each term (due to Lemma
1
) in relation (
8
), we omit all terms with odd integers
m
and consider only even terms with
m=2r\geq 2M
for some
M\in\mathbb{N}
to be chosen later. Then

(11)

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{2r}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\right).

By Lemma
2
, the coefficients
\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}
tend to zero with decay rate
r^{-\frac{d}{2}}
. Thus, using only asymptotic reasoning, we may estimate for
r\geq M
,

(12)

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{2r}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\geq\frac{1}{N}-\frac{c}{M^{d/2}}\geq\frac{1}{N}-\frac{1}{2N}=\frac{1}{2N},

where in the first step we keep only the diagonal terms (i.e.
j=k
) in the double sum for the points and in the second step we require that
\frac{c}{M^{d/2}}<\frac{1}{2N}
for all
r\geq M
which is guaranteed when
N
and
M
are related by means of

(13)

\alpha\leq\frac{M}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}\leq\alpha+1,\qquad N\geq N_{0},

for some sufficiently large
\alpha
depending only on
d
and
N_{0}
.

Finally, as shown in (
4
), the coefficients
\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}
are of the order
r^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, and therefore

(14)

\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\geq c^{\prime}\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}r^{-\frac{3}{2}}\geq 2c^{\prime}M^{-\frac{1}{2}}\geq c^{\prime\prime}_{d}N^{-\frac{1}{d}}.

The last relation follows from (
13
).

Putting together (
11
), (
12
), and (
14
), we find that

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\,\frac{1}{2N}\,\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\geq c_{d}^{2}\,N^{-1-\frac{1}{d}},

which finishes the proof of Beck’s lower bound (
2
).

3.
Geometric corollaries

The method of proof presented in Section
2
allows us to obtain some immediate corollaries which provide a lower bound for the discrepancy
D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}
in terms of quantities which have geometric interpretations. Recall that every term in the right-hand side of (
8
) is non-negative as proved in Lemma
1
. Therefore, discrepancy can be bounded from below by any of these terms. In particular, taking
m=1
and observing that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})=0\quad\quad\textup{ and }\quad\quad\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}=\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{j}\right\|^{2},

we obtain the following bound on the discrepancy in terms of the center of mass of the points.

Proposition 3
.

For any
N\geq 2
and
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}

(15)

D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\geq\sqrt{\frac{C_{d}}{\sqrt{2}}}\left\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf{x}_{j}\right\|.

Remark
.

In plain words, the conclusion of Proposition
3
states that if the points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}
are not well-balanced, i.e. their center of mass is far from zero, then this configuration cannot be distributed too uniformly, i.e. its discrepancy has to be large. This statement seems to be completely clear heuristically, but we have not found any prior quantitative versions in the literature.

The norm of the centroid of the points vanishes (and is thus minimized) for balanced point configurations, i.e. spherical
1
-designs, which include, in particular, all symmetric (antipodal) configurations. Of course, in this case (
15
) does not provide meaningful information about the discrepancy.

Similarly, the second term (
m=2
) of (
8
) provides a lower bound in terms of the
frame potentials
(cf.
[
4
]
).

Proposition 4
.

For any
N\geq 2
and
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}

(16)

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\frac{C_{d}}{4\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2}-\frac{1}{d+1}\right),\qquad\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}.

The proof uses the fact that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{1}{d+1},

which can be seen from Lemma
2
. The quantity
\displaystyle{\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2}}
is known as the frame potential and was introduced in
[
4
]
), where it was proved that it is minimized
exactly by the so-called unit norm tight frames (UNTFs)
2
2

2

The term finite normalized tight frames (FNTFs) has been used in
[
4
]
, but UNTF appears to be prevalent in the more recent frame theory literature.
, which include, among others, unions of orthonormal bases and spherical
2
-designs (in fact,
2
-designs are exactly balanced tight frames), we refer to
[
4

7
]
for relevant definitions.

Thus (
16
) essentially states that if a configuration
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}
is far from being a tight frame, then its distribution has to be far from uniform. Heuristically, the connection between tight frames and equidsitribution was alluded to in
[
4
]
, but no quantitative statements akin to (
16
) have been previously known.

More generally, this approach allows one to bound the discrepancy
D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}
from below by any term of (
8

m\geq 1
. However, the discussion immediately following Lemma
1
suggests that for
m\geq 3
these quantities are not as easy to interpret geometrically, therefore, we do not pursue this direction further in this paper.

4.
Discrepancy of curves and sets of arbitrary Hausdorff dimension.

The discrepancy as defined in (
1
) measures the quality of approximating the uniform measure
\sigma_{d}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
by
N
-element point sets, or, to put it in other words, by
0
-dimensional sets of
0
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
N
. This interpretation suggest a natural generalization of the problem to approximations of
\sigma_{d}
by higher dimensional sets (such as curves, surfaces, or fractal sets and sets of fractional dimension). Such questions have been raised, but to the best of our knowledge almost no results are available in this direction. A recent work
[
20
]
focused on one-dimensional (curve) analogs of spherical designs, but there has been no study of discrepancy or energy in this direction.

Let
0\leq s\leq d
. For a set
\mathbf{X}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
of Hausdorff dimension
s
we can define its
s
-dimensional spherical cap discrepancy as

(17)

D_{\mathbb{L}_{2},s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{X}){:=}\sqrt{\int_{-1}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left|\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(X\cap C(\mathbf{x},t)\big)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(X)}-\sigma_{d}(C(\mathbf{x},t))\right|^{2}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd t},

where
\mathcal{H}_{s}
denotes the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. This definition, in complete accordance with (
1
), quantifies how well the normalized Hausdorff measure on
X
approximates the uniform measure
\sigma_{d}
. Indeed, if
s=0
and
\mathbf{X}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\}
, then (
17
) coincides with (
1
).

We shall need one more assumption about the regularity of the set
\mathbf{X}
. We say that an
s
-dimensional set
\mathbf{X}
is lower Alfohrs–David regular (ADR) if there exists a constant
a(\mathbf{X},s)
such that for each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}
and for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the
s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the ball
B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}:\,\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|<\varepsilon\}
intersected with
\mathbf{X}
satisfies

(18)

\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\cap\mathbf{X}\big)\geq a(\mathbf{X},s)\varepsilon^{s}.

Theorem 5
.

For any
s\in[0,d)
there exists a constant
c_{d,s}>0
such that for any lower Alfohrs–David regular set
\mathbf{X}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
of Hausdorff dimension
s
with
\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})\geq 1
, its discrepancy satisfies

(19)

D_{\mathbb{L}_{2},s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{X})\geq c_{d,s}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}{a(\mathbf{X},s)}\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2(d-s)}}.

Remark:
Observe that when
\mathbf{X}
is an
N
-point configuration (i.e.
s=0
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}(\mathbf{X})=N
), one recovers Beck’s lower (
2
) bound of
N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}}
, while as
s
approaches
d
the lower bound approaches zero. While we don’t yet have a proof of sharpness of (
19
), this tight endpoint behavior leads us to conjecture that (
19
) is sharp for
0<s<d
.

Proof.

The proof is a variant of the argument in Section
2
. We start by applying a version of Stolarsky’s invariance principle. We define a probability measure
\mu_{\mathbf{X}}
supported on
\mathbf{X}
by normalizing the restriction of the Hausdorff measure
\mathcal{H}_{s}
to
\mathbf{X}
, i.e.
\mu_{\mathbf{X}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}\mathcal{H}_{s}|_{\mathbf{X}}
. Then we have the following version of Stolarsky’s principle:

(20)

\frac{1}{C_{d}}\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2},s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{X})\right)^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})-\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{y}).

For a proof see
[
6
]
, where this identity was actually established for all Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
. Thus, arguing exactly as in Section
2
we can obtain the following analogue of (
8
).

(21)

\begin{split}&\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2},s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{X})\right)^{2}\\
&\phantom{eq}=\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}\left(\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{y})-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})\right).\end{split}

We observe that all the terms in the series above are non-negative. Indeed, the positivity of the coefficients has already been discussed, see (
8
), and the non-negativity of the expressions in parentheses essentially follows from Lemma
1
(in fact, the proof presented in Appendix
A
establishes it for all measures).

The main essential difference in the rest of the proof, as compared to the discrete case, lies in obtaining lower bounds for the expressions
\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2r}\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{y})
. In (
12
) we estimated analogous discrete sums by
\frac{1}{N}
by leaving only the diagonal terms, i.e. considering
x=y
. While this approach will not work for
s\neq 0
, we emulate it by considering, for each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{X}
, only those points
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{X}
which lie close to
\mathbf{x}
, and then invoking lower Alfohrs–David regularity. Let
m=2r
be even and
\varepsilon>0
be a small number. We can then estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2r}\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\big(\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})\big)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2r}\dd\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{x})\dd\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\big(\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})\big)^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbf{X}:\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\|<\varepsilon\}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2r}\dd\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{y})\dd\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\big(\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})^{2}\big)}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)^{2r}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\mathcal{H}_{s}\big(B_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})\cap\mathbf{X}\big)\dd\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a(\mathbf{X},s)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)^{2r}\cdot\varepsilon^{s}.

Assuming that
r>d
and choosing
\varepsilon=\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{1/2}
, we obtain

\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\right)^{2r}\cdot\varepsilon^{s}=\left(1-\frac{s}{2r}\right)^{2r}s^{s/2}r^{-s/2}\geq c_{s}r^{-s/2},

where we use an easy fact that
\left(1-\frac{s}{2r}\right)^{2r}s^{s/2}
is bounded below by a constant depending only on
s
.

Therefore, according to Lemma
2
, arguing as in (
12
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{X}}\int_{\mathbf{X}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2r}\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{x})\dd\mu_{\mathbf{X}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{2r}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle\geq c_{s}\frac{a(\mathbf{X},s)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}r^{-s/2}-c_{d}r^{-d/2}\geq c^{\prime}_{d,s}\frac{a(\mathbf{X},s)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}r^{-s/2},

provided that
r\geq M=c^{\prime\prime}_{d,s}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}{a(\mathbf{X},s)}\right)^{\frac{2}{d-s}}
. Then (
21
) implies the following

\displaystyle\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2},s}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{X})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C_{d,s}\sum_{r\geq M}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\cdot\frac{a(\mathbf{X},s)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}r^{-s/2}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime}_{d,s}\frac{a(\mathbf{X},s)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}\sum_{r\geq M}r^{-\frac{3+s}{2}}\geq C^{\prime\prime}_{d,s}\frac{a(\mathbf{X},s)}{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}M^{-\frac{1+s}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{d,s}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}{a(\mathbf{X},s)}\right)^{-\frac{1+s}{d-s}-1}=C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{d,s}\left(\frac{\mathcal{H}_{s}(\mathbf{X})}{a(\mathbf{X},s)}\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{d-s}},

which finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Since the case of curves (
s=1
) presents particular interest due to
[

]
, we state it as a separate corollary. Observe that whenever
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}
is a rectifiable curve with
\ell(\Gamma)=H_{1}(\Gamma)\geq 1

. Therefore we immediately obtain the following bound for the discrepancy of curves.

There exists a constant

with length

5.
Refinements: Analysis of the constant
c_{d}
in (
2
)

A more careful analysis of our proof reveals information about the implicit constant
c_{d}
in inequality (
2
). Several steps in the argument can be somewhat sharpened and improved.
We obtain uniform bounds and consider asymptotic bounds. A comparison with conjectured bounds shows that our bounds are surprisingly tight in the sense that by only using diagonal terms in the point sums, we recover one significant digit in the conjectured asymptotic constant and the relative error is small; cf. Table
1
.

5.1.
Uniform bound

The coefficients
\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}
in (
11
) are strictly monotonically decreasing as can be seen from

(23)

\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r+1}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r+1}}}=\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\,\frac{r+\frac{1}{2}}{r+\frac{d+1}{2}}<\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}},\qquad r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Thus, we may estimate (without asymptotic reasoning but still keeping only the diagonal terms in the double sum and therefore no further assumptions on the geometric properties of the points)

(24)

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{2r}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\geq\frac{1}{N}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{M}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{M}}},\qquad r\geq M.

For
M
sufficiently large, the right-hand side of above inequality is positive and thus

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\left(\frac{1}{N}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{M}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{M}}}\right)\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}.

Using the upper bound in Gautschi’s Inequality for ratios of gamma functions
[
19
, Eq. 5.6.4]
, we may estimate

(25)

\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}=\frac{-1}{\gammafcn(-\frac{1}{2})}\frac{\gammafcn(m-\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(m+1)}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{m}\,\frac{\gammafcn(m-1+\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(m-1+1)}>\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{m}\,\frac{1}{m^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{m^{\frac{3}{2}}}

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and get

\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}>\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{\frac{3}{2}}}>\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{M}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}\dd x=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\,\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}},\qquad M\geq 1.

A more tedious computation shows that the lower bound is the correct leading term in the asymptotic expansion of the sum on the left-hand side; cf. Lemma
7
for
d=0
and
\alpha=1
.

By Lemma
2
, we have

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\frac{C_{d}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{1}{N}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{M}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{M}}}\right)\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}>\frac{C_{d}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\frac{1}{N}-\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}\;\frac{1}{M^{\frac{d}{2}}}\right)\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}

provided
M
is sufficiently large to guarantee positivity of the lower bound.
Substituting
M=(cN)^{\frac{2}{d}}
, we arrive at the lower bound

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\frac{C_{d}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,\frac{F(c)}{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}},\qquad F(c){:=}\left(c-\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}\right)\frac{1}{c^{1+\frac{1}{d}}}.

The parameter
c
has to be larger than
\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}
for a non-trivial lower bound.
The function
c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F}}{{\mapsto}}(c-\beta)\frac{1}{c^{1+\gamma}}
with fixed
\beta,\gamma>0
has in
[\beta,\infty)
a unique maximum at
c^{*}=\beta(1+\frac{1}{\gamma})>\beta
with value
F(c^{*})=\frac{1}{(1+\gamma)\big(\beta(1+\frac{1}{\gamma})\big)^{\gamma}}
.
Thus, for all
N\geq 2
,

(26)

D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\geq c_{d}^{*}\,N^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2d}},\qquad c_{d}^{*}=\sqrt{\frac{C_{d}}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{d}}\left(\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{3}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{d+3}{2})}\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}}.

In particular, we get

c_{2}^{*}=\frac{1}{6\sqrt{6\pi}}=0.1959291678902056\dots.

5.2.
Asymptotic constant

We derive a lower bound for

\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\end{subarray}}N^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2d}}D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})

that agrees with one significant digit (and recovers more than
93\%
) of the conjectured constant of the leading term of the minimal
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-discrepancy asymptotics available for dimensions
2
,
4
,
8
, and
24

Surprisingly, this result uses only the diagonal terms of the double sum for the points in (
8

m<2M
and
M

We rewrite the odd terms in (

\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r+1}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r}\left(1+\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r}.

For convenience, set
a_{m}{:=}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}
. Then, considering both even and odd terms with
{m\geq 2M}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}}\geq\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}a_{2r}\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r}-\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\right)+\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}a_{2r+1}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r+1}

\displaystyle\phantom{equ}=\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}a_{2r+1}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r}\left(1+\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)-\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}a_{2r}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}+\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\left(a_{2r}-a_{2r+1}\right)\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{2r}.

By strict monotonicity (see (

a_{2r}-a_{2r+1}>0
for all

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\left(\frac{2}{N}\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}a_{2r+1}-\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}a_{2r}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\left(a_{2r}-a_{2r+1}\right)\right).

Reordering terms, we arrive at

(27)

\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\sqrt{2}\,C_{d}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=2M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}-\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\right).

We proved that it suffices to consider the diagonal terms in the double sum for the points (even in the case of odd
m
) for all
m\geq 2M
for any
M\geq 1
.

We need the following asymptotic results proven in Appendix
B

Lemma 7
.

Let
0<\alpha<2
. Then

\sum_{m=2M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}=\frac{2^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{\gammafcn(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\,\frac{1}{M^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{M^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\Big)\qquad\text{as $M\to\infty$;}

and for

\displaystyle\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}=\frac{1}{M^{\frac{d}{2}}}\left(2^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\frac{\alpha\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{2\sqrt{\pi}\;\gammafcn(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})(d+\alpha)}\,\frac{1}{M^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{M^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\Big)\right)\quad\text{as $M\to\infty$.}

By Lemma

N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}\left(D_{\mathbb{L}_{2}}^{\mathrm{C}}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\right)^{2}\geq\frac{C_{d}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(1-\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{2\sqrt{\pi}\,(d+1)}\,\frac{N}{M^{\frac{d}{2}}}\right)\frac{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}{M^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}}{M^{\frac{d+3}{2}}}\Big).

On assuming that

\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\

The function

5.4.
Further remarks

Contrary to the usual approach in proving lower bounds, we consider the series remainder in (
8
) with
m\geq 2M
for some suitable
M
. This strategy is aided, in part, by the fact that for a spherical
t
-design all terms with
m\leq t
vanish because of exactness of integration.
By the celebrated result
[
7
]
, for every
N\geq\gamma_{d}\,t^{d}
, there exists a spherical
t
-design with
N
points on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
for which equality holds in (
9
) for all
1\leq m\leq t
. The constant
\gamma_{d}
depends on
d
only. Thus, for a spherical
t
-design with
t=\frac{1}{(\gamma_{d})^{\frac{1}{d}}}N^{\frac{1}{d}}
, all terms with
1\leq m\leq\frac{1}{(\gamma_{d})^{\frac{1}{d}}}N^{\frac{1}{d}}
in (
8
) vanish.
Heuristically, well-distributed points seem to approximate the behaviour of spherical designs in the sense that

S(m){:=}\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y}),\qquad m\geq 1,

goes to zero qualitatively faster than
\frac{1}{N}
if
1\leq m\leq c_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{1}{d}-\varepsilon}
,
c_{\varepsilon}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
.
Indeed, the matching upper bound in (
2
) valid for maximal sum of distance points or QMC-design sequences of sufficient strength
s\geq\frac{d+1}{2}
(cf.
[
14
]
) implies that individual terms in the expansion (
8
) satisfy

NS(m)\leq\frac{C}{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}\,\frac{m!}{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}\leq\widetilde{C}\,\frac{m^{\frac{3}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}\leq\widetilde{C}\,\frac{M^{\frac{3}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{d}}},\qquad m=1,2,3,\dots,M.

The constants
C
and
\widetilde{C}
depend on
d
and the sequence of point sets and the coefficient in the inequality (
25
). The right-hand side goes to zero like
\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{3}{2}\alpha}}
if
M\sim N^{\alpha}
and
0\leq\alpha<\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{d}
.
A simple summation by parts argument utilizing strict monotonicity of
\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}
gives

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}(NS(m))=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}\Big)=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha}}\Big)\qquad\text{as $N\to\infty$,}

provided
M\sim N^{\alpha}
with
0\leq\alpha<\frac{2}{d}
.
This phenomenon is also evident in the decay of so-called structure factors for hyperuniform sequences of point sets (if
m
stays bounded; cf.
[
12
]
).
The precise decay rate of
NS_{m}
(or the structure factor of hyperuniformity) as
N\to\infty
for
m
stays bounded, grows weakly to infinity, or grows like
N^{\frac{1}{d}}
(threshold growth) is an ongoing research quest for (constructible) sequence of point sets on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

On the other hand, the series expansion (
8
) can be truncated after a sufficiently large
M
. Because
S(m)
is bounded by
1
from above, a coarse estimate shows that

\sum_{r=2M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}S(m)=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,\frac{1}{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}}\Big)=o\Big(\frac{1}{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}}\Big)

as
M,N\to\infty
, provided that
\frac{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}}{M^{\frac{1}{2}}}\to 0
(e.g.,
M\geq\kappa N^{2+\frac{2}{d}}\log N
for some fixed
\kappa>0
).
For an
N
-point set of pairwise different points with
K
pairs of antipodal points, we get

S(m)=\frac{1}{N}+(-1)^{m}\,\frac{2K}{N^{2}}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\mathop{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}}_{-1<\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}<1}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{m}<\frac{1}{N}+(-1)^{m}\,\frac{2K}{N^{2}}+\left(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j,k\leq N\\
-1<\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}<1\end{subarray}}\left|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right|\right)^{m}.

The double sum goes to zero as
m\to\infty
and
N
is fixed. We have the limit relations

\liminf_{m\to\infty}S(m)=\frac{1}{N}-\frac{2K}{N^{2}},\qquad\limsup_{m\to\infty}S(m)=\frac{1}{N}+\frac{2K}{N^{2}}.

For point sets that satisfy a well-separation property; i.e.,
\|\mathbf{x}_{j}-\mathbf{x}_{k}\|\geq\frac{\beta}{N^{\frac{1}{d}}}
,
j\neq k
, for some positive
\beta
not depending on
N
, we get

\sum_{m\geq\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}N^{\frac{2}{d}}\log N}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}S(m)=\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{1}{N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}\sqrt{\log N}}\Big)\qquad\text{as $N\to\infty$.}

We use that well-separation gives a bound on the number of points in a spherical cap centered at a point
\mathbf{x}_{j}
and its antipodal position
-\mathbf{x}_{j}
. Hence,
\left|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right|\geq 1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}
for at most
\beta^{\prime}N
many pairs of points
(\mathbf{x}_{j},\mathbf{x}_{k})
and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k})^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}|\geq 1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}\end{subarray}}^{N}|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}|^{m}+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}|<1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}\end{subarray}}^{N}|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}|^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}|\geq 1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}\end{subarray}}^{N}1+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=1\\
|\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}|<1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}\end{subarray}}^{N}\left(1-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}\right)^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{N}+e^{-\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{m}{N^{\frac{2}{d}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{N}+\frac{1}{N},

provided
m\geq\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}N^{\frac{2}{d}}\log N
.
The subtractive part in
S(m)
can be neglected by Lemma
7
.

Numerical computations for so-called spherical Fibonacci points (images of Fibonacci lattice points in the unit square under an area-preserving Lambert transformation) conjectured to have nearly (possibly up to a logarithmic power) optimal spherical cap
\mathbb{L}_{2}
-discrepancy seem to exhibit all the previously discussed features; cf. Figure

6.
Powers of the Euclidean distance and Generalizations

Since our argument exploits the sum of distances, rather than dealing with discrepancy directly, it can also be utilized to analyze other positive power of the Euclidean distance. It has been proved in
[
27
]
that for all
0<\alpha<2
there exists a constant
\beta_{\alpha,d}>0
such that for any
\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
N
sufficiently large

(34)

\begin{split}S_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})&{:=}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\right\|^{\alpha}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{y})-\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\|\mathbf{x}_{j}-\mathbf{x}_{k}\right\|^{\alpha}\\
&\geq\beta_{\alpha,d}\,N^{-1-\frac{\alpha}{d}}.\end{split}

Existence of
N
-point configurations achieving an upper bound with the matching asymptotics in
N
was proved in
[
28
]
.

While the proof in
[
27
]
is quite technical and involved, we point out that our elementary argument presented in Section
2
applies to this situation almost verbatim. The only modifications one needs to make consist of using the expansion

\left(1-t\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\binom{\frac{\alpha}{2}}{m}t^{m},

and observing that the coefficients
(-1)^{m}\binom{\frac{\alpha}{2}}{m}
,
m\geq 1
, are positive and are of the order
m^{-1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}
as
m\to\infty
. Therefore the analogue of the sum in (
14
) is of the order
M^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\asymp N^{-\frac{\alpha}{d}}
. Other than this, the proof of (
34
) repeats the proof of (
2
) verbatim.

The argument for an asymptotic bound can be used in the same way. The analogue of (
27
) is

(35)

S_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{N})\geq 2^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=2M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}-\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\right).

By Lemma
7
and proceeding as in Subsection

\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}N\to\infty\\

where

c_{\alpha,d}^{\mathrm{asymp}}=\frac{1}{\gammafcn(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\,\frac{1}{1+\frac{\alpha}{d}}\left(\frac{2\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}=\left(\omega_{d}\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{d}}\frac{1}{\gammafcn(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\,\frac{1}{1+\frac{\alpha}{d}}\,\frac{1}{\pi^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}.

In particular, we get

(37)

We graphically compare our constant with the conjectured constant

where

Appendix A
Proof of Lemmas
1
and
2

We now turn to the proof of the two technical lemmas.

Proof of Lemma
1
.

The case
m=0
is obvious.

Recall that the Gram matrix
{G_{1}{:=}\big[\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\big]_{j,k=1}^{N}}
is positive semidefinite, and hence by Schur’s product theorem, so is
{G_{m}{:=}\big[\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}\big]_{j,k=1}^{N}}
.

Since for each odd
m\geq 1
the integral in (
9
) is zero, the statement immediately follows in this case: indeed, let
\mathbf{1}
be a vector with
1
in each coordinate, then

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}=\mathbf{1}^{T}G_{m}\mathbf{1}\geq 0.

In the general case, for any signed Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, define the energy integral

I_{m}(\mu){:=}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\mu(\mathbf{x})\dd\mu(\mathbf{y}).

Observe that positive semidefiniteness of
G_{m}
implies that
I_{m}(\mu)\geq 0
. Indeed, for a discrete measure
\mu=\sum c_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{x_{j}}}
, one has
I_{m}(\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}c_{j}c_{k}\geq 0
, and for general measures positivity follows by
w^{*}
-density of discrete measures.

We also observe that for any Borel probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, we have

(41)

0\leq I_{m}(\nu-\sigma_{d})=I_{m}(\nu)+I_{m}(\sigma_{d})-2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})\dd\nu(\mathbf{y})=I_{m}(\nu)-I_{m}(\sigma_{d}),

where we have used the fact that the inner integral with respect to
\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})
above is independent of
\mathbf{y}
due to rotational invariance, and is thus equal to
I_{m}(\sigma_{d})
. Taking
\nu=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\mathbf{x}_{j}}
in (
41
) finishes the proof of the lemma since in this case
I_{m}(\nu)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{j,k=1}^{N}\left(\mathbf{x}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{k}\right)^{m}
.
∎

We remark that the identity
I_{m}(\nu)-I_{m}(\sigma_{d})=I_{m}(\nu-\sigma_{d})
exhibited in (
41
) has been observed in, e.g.,
[
9
,
22
]
and has been used in the context of discrepancy and Stolarsky principle in
[
5
,
6
]
.

Proof of Lemma
2
.

Due to rotational invariance, it suffices to compute the integral just with respect to
\mathbf{x}
. Since
\mathbf{x}\mapsto(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y})^{m}
is a zonal function, for odd integers
m
, the integral
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})
vanishes and for even integers
m=2r
, the Funk-Hecke formula yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\left(\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{y}\right)^{m}\dd\sigma_{d}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2r}\left(1-t^{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\dd t=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}\int_{0}^{1}u^{r+\frac{1}{2}-1}\left(1-u\right)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\dd u

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}\frac{\gammafcn(r+\frac{1}{2})\gammafcn(\frac{d}{2})}{\gammafcn(r+\frac{d+1}{2})}=\frac{\omega_{d-1}}{\omega_{d}}\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d}{2})\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}=\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}.

One can then easily obtain the asymptotics

\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}=\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}\,\frac{\gammafcn(r+\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(r+\frac{d+1}{2})}\sim\frac{\gammafcn(\frac{d+1}{2})}{\gammafcn(\frac{1}{2})}\,\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d}{2}}}\qquad\text{as $r\to\infty$.}

Application of Gautschi’s Inequality
[
19
, Eq. 5.6.4]
gives

\frac{\gammafcn(r+\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(r+\frac{d+1}{2})}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{{\left(r+\frac{1}{2}\right)_{\frac{d}{2}}}}&\text{$d$ even,}\\
\dfrac{\gammafcn(r+\frac{1}{2})}{\gammafcn(r+1)}\,\dfrac{1}{{\left(r+1\right)_{\frac{d-1}{2}}}}&\text{$d$ odd}\end{cases}<\frac{1}{r^{\frac{d}{2}}}.

∎

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
7

Proof.

Let
0<\alpha<2
. With the help of Mathematica

\sum_{m=2M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{m}}}{m!}=\frac{1}{\gammafcn(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\,\frac{\gammafcn(2M-\frac{\alpha}{2})}{\gammafcn(2M)}.

The asymptotic relation follows, e.g., from
[
19
, Eq. 5.11.13]
.

The second series can be written as a
3F2
-hypergeometric function,

\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}=\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2M}}}{(2M)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{M}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{M}}}{\sideset{{}_{3}}{{}_{2}}{\HyperF}\!\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\displaystyle M-\frac{\alpha}{4},M-\frac{\alpha}{4}+\frac{1}{2},1\\
\displaystyle M+\frac{d+1}{2},M+1\end{subarray};1\right)}.

Since
\frac{d+\alpha}{2}>0
and
M+\frac{d-1}{2}>0
for
M\geq 1
, we may apply
[
23
, Eq. 7.4.4.1, p. 533]
to obtain

\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}=\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2M}}}{(2M)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{M}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{M}}}\,\frac{2M+d-1}{d+\alpha}{\sideset{{}_{3}}{{}_{2}}{\HyperF}\!\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4},1+\frac{\alpha}{4},1\\
\displaystyle 1+\frac{d+\alpha}{2},M+1\end{subarray};1\right)}.

The
3F2
-hypergeometric function has a convergent series expansion of positive terms which can be seen as a generalized asymptotic expansion as
M\to\infty
. By (
3
) and simplification,

\begin{split}&\sum_{r=M}^{\infty}\frac{-{\left(-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)_{2r}}}{(2r)!}\,\frac{{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}}{{\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)_{r}}}\\

Using asymptotic expansions of ratios of gamma functions for large

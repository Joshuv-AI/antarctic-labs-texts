Dual Phonon Universe in a Spinor Bose Gas
Jiabin Wang, Yunfei Xue, Li Chen, Ren Zhang

Abstract
We show the possibility of simulating a dual universe in a pseudospin-1/2 Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC), wherein two phononic modes experience distinct curved spacetime metrics. Through ramping the interspecies interaction of the BEC, we observe that one universe expands, and in the mean time, the other contracts. These findings can be directly verified in existing cold-atom experimental setups by detecting the correlators of the scaled density contrast (SDC): The sizes of the expanded/contracted universes can be deduced from the SDC’s oscillating frequencies, while the expanding/contracting histories are reflected in the SDC’s amplitude. Our study demonstrates the great potential of spinor BECs for exploring multiverse-related physics in laboratory settings.

\displaystyle(\alpha\tilde{\nabla}_{a}^{2}-c_{a}^{2}\nabla^{2})\delta\theta_{a}

\displaystyle(\alpha\tilde{\nabla}_{b}^{2}-c_{b}^{2}\nabla^{2})\delta\theta_{b}

\displaystyle=\beta_{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}^{2}\delta\theta_{a},

In this case,

Equation (

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&v_{\rm s}&v_{\rm s}\\

v_{\rm s}&-v_{\rm s}^{2}&\frac{c^{2}}{\alpha\mp\beta}-v_{\rm s}^{2}\end{array}\right).

The covariant metric
g_{\pm,\mu\nu}
is the inverse of
g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}

Therefore, we end up with a dual phonon universe based on two relativistic phonon modes, respectively termed as the total phase phonon (TPP) and relative phase phonon (RPP) for brevity. These two modes experience different spacetime metrics indicated by
g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}

\beta

\delta\theta_{\pm}

Expanding/Contracting Dynamics-.

A prototype spacetime for studying the expansion or contraction of the universe is the Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) spacetime
[
49
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
.
By making
\beta
(or equivalently
U_{ab}
) in Eq. (
S17
) time-dependent, we can derive the FRW metric.
To illustrate, we first consider a uniform BEC with
V=0
, such that
v_{\rm s}=0
and
\bar{n}
being a space-independent constant.
Then, the line element is given by

\displaystyle ds_{\pm}^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}_{\pm}(t)d{\bf x}^{2},

(12)

where

a^{2}_{\pm}(t)=\frac{m(U\mp U_{ab}(t))}{\bar{n}(U^{2}-U_{ab}^{2}(t))}

(13)

is the scale factor, and
c=\sqrt{U\bar{n}/m}
is the uniform sound velocity.
Eq. (
12
) represents a homogeneous and isotropic FRW spacetime with flat 2D spatial sections (flat FRW). The time-dependent scale factor
a_{\pm}(t)
, which is achievable by tuning the magnetic field
B(t)
near a Feshbach resonance in cold atoms, induces curvature in the (2+1)D spacetime.

According to Eq. (
13
), as
U_{ab}
varies, the tendencies of
a_{+}(t)
and
a_{-}(t)
are always opposite.
It means that one universe expands, and in the meanwhile, the other universe contracts. This serves as one of the key results of this paper.
Furthermore, by engineering the ramping behavior of
B(t)
, one can achieve arbitrary expasion/contraction processes
a_{\pm}(t)
.
In Fig.
2
, we show the tendencies of
a_{\pm}(t)
when
U_{ab}
ramps up in the manners of
U_{ab}(\tilde{t})/{U}\propto\tilde{t}^{-2}-1,\tilde{t}^{-4}-1
, and
e^{-2\tilde{t}}-1
, where
\tilde{t}=t/t_{0}
and
t_{0}=\hbar/(U\bar{n}_{0})
. These cases respectively leads to the linear (solid line with triangles), quadratic (with circles), and exponential (with squares) expansion processes and the corresponding contractions (dashed lines). All ramping processes start from
t_{\rm i}=0.5t_{0}
and end at
t_{\rm f}=1.5t_{0}
.
It is clearly indicated that while the detailed behavior of
a_{\pm}(t)
varies with different ramping methods, the overall trends remain consistent: the TPP universe with
a_{+}(t)
contracts, whereas the RPP universe with
a_{-}(t)
expands.

We additionally noted that, according to Eq. (
13
), the factors
a^{2}_{\pm}
turns to be negative if
U_{ab}>U
, i.e.,
a_{\pm}
are imaginary. This is understandable since pseudospin-1/2 BECs undergo a phase transition from mixed phase to phase separation at

For a non-uniform BEC, we can realize a FRW spacetime with curved spatial sections (curved FRW). For example, we consider central-force trapping potentials such that the density distributions are isotropic and varying along the radial direction
\rho
, i.e.,
\bar{n}^{(\prime)}=\bar{n}_{0}\left(1-f(\rho)/R^{2}\right)
and
f(\rho)=\pm 2\rho^{2}-\rho^{4}/R^{2}

\bar{n}
and
\bar{n}^{(\prime)}
respectively correspond to the cases with positive and negative signs in

\rho

After a coordinate transformation

ds_{\pm}^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}_{\pm}(t)\left(\frac{dw^{2}}{1-\kappa w^{2}}+w^{2}d\varphi^{2}\right),

(14)

where
\kappa=\mp 4/R^{2}
is the spatial curvature. As
R\to\infty

\kappa\to 0
such that the curved FRW reduces to the flat one.
Eq. (
14

\bar{n}
and
\bar{n}^{(\prime)}
, the curved FRW can exhibit positive and negative spatial curvatures in the spacetime
(t,w,\varphi)

Furthermore, based on the same ramping protocol as shown in Fig.
2
, one can also realize the expansion and contraction of the curved FRW.

Particles’ Production-.

The particles’ production during the expanding/contracting process may provide insights into the formation of elementary particles in the early Big Bang.
According to Eq. (
12
), the EoM of
\delta\theta_{\pm}
is given by

\partial_{t}^{2}\delta\theta_{\pm}+\frac{2\dot{a}_{\pm}(t)}{a_{\pm}(t)}\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm}-\frac{1}{a^{2}_{\pm}(t)}\nabla^{2}\delta\theta_{\pm}=0,

(15)

where
\dot{a}_{\pm}
denotes the time-derivative of
a_{\pm}

[
41
]
.
For the curved FRW, one needs to replace the Laplace operator
\nabla^{2}
by the Laplace-Beltrami operator.
For a slow expanding/contracting, i.e.,
\dot{a}(t)\approx 0
, Eq. (
15
) reduces to the Klein-Gordon equation.

Taking the flat FRW as an example, we illustrate the particle generation
[
54
,
53
,
55
]
. We first quantize the fields
\delta\theta_{\pm}
as

\displaystyle\delta{\hat{\theta}}_{\pm}=\int\frac{kdk}{2\pi}\sum_{m}

\displaystyle\left[\hat{b}_{km}J_{m}(k\rho)e^{im\varphi}v_{\pm,k}(t)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{km}J_{m}(k\rho)e^{-im\varphi}v^{*}_{\pm,k}(t)\right],

(16)

where
J_{m}(\cdot)
denotes the Bessel function of the first kind,
m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots
is the quantum number of the angular momentum
\hat{L}_{z}
,
k
is the wavenumber, and
\hat{b}_{km}
is the annihilation operator of the mode
v_{\pm,k}(t)
, satisfying bosonic commutation relations
[\hat{b}_{km},\hat{b}_{k^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=2\pi\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\delta(k-k^{\prime})/k

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}v_{\pm,k}(t)+\frac{2\dot{a}_{\pm}(t)}{a_{\pm}(t)}\partial_{t}v_{\pm,k}(t)+\frac{f(k)}{a^{2}_{\pm}(t)}v_{\pm,k}(t)=0,

(17)

where

will be
\kappa

[

57

Since the scale factors

are time-independent before and after the ramping process (see Fig.
2
), Eq. (
17

Then, the linear-independent solutions are worked out as

v_{\pm,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{{e}^{-i\omega^{\rm i}_{\pm,k}t}}{\sqrt{2\hbar ka_{\pm}(t_{\rm i})}}&t<t_{\rm i}\\
\tilde{v}_{\pm,k}(t)&t_{\rm i}\leq t<t_{\rm f}\\
\frac{S{e}^{-i\omega^{\rm f}_{\pm,k}t}+T{e}^{i\omega^{\rm f}_{\pm,k}t}}{\sqrt{2\hbar ka_{\pm}(t_{\rm f})}}&t\geq t_{\rm f}\end{cases}

(18)

and

u_{\pm,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{S^{*}{e}^{-i\omega^{\rm i}_{\pm,k}t}-T{e}^{i\omega^{\rm i}_{\pm,k}t}}{\sqrt{2\hbar ka_{\pm}(t_{\rm i})}}&t<t_{\rm i}\\

where
t_{\rm i}
and
t_{\rm f}
are the initial and final time of expansion,

and

where
{\rm Wr}[\cdot\ ,\cdot]
represents the Wronskian determinant. We define

as the annihilation operator of phonon in the mode
u_{\pm,k}(t)

[\hat{b}_{km}^{\prime},(\hat{b}_{k^{\prime}m^{\prime}}^{\prime})^{\dagger}]=2\pi\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\delta(k-k^{\prime})/k

v_{\pm,k}(t<t_{\rm i})
and
u_{\pm,k}(t>t_{\rm f})
. This means that the vacuum states for
t<t_{\rm i}
and

are defined as
\hat{b}_{km}|\Omega_{\rm i}\rangle=0
and
\hat{b}_{km}^{\prime}|\Omega_{\rm f}\rangle=0
, respectively. Comparing Eq. (
18
) and (

u_{\pm,k}(t)=S_{\pm}^{*}v_{\pm,k}(t)-T_{\pm}v^{*}_{\pm,k}(t)

There exists an intuitive understanding from the perspective of the 1D scattering problem in the time dimension

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \hat{b}_{km}&=S_{\pm}^{*}\hat{b}_{km}^{\prime}-T_{\pm}^{*}{\hat{b}}^{\prime}{}^{\dagger}_{k,-m};\\

\langle\Omega_{\rm f}|\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{km}\hat{b}_{km}|\Omega_{\rm f}\rangle=|T|^{2}\neq 0

Experimental Observation-.

As mentioned earlier, the phase and density fluctuations are mutually related by Eq. (
6
). Hence, the universe expansion/contraction can be experimentally verified by detecting the density response, a quantity that is readily measurable in current experimental setups
[
61
,
62
,
58
,
22
,
23
]
. Specifically, we define the scaled density contrast (SDC) for the total and relative fields as

\displaystyle d_{\pm}(t,\rho)=\sqrt{\frac{\bar{n}(\rho)}{\bar{n}_{0}^{3}}}\left[\delta n_{\pm}(t,\rho)-\delta n_{\pm}(0,0)\right],

(21)

where
\delta n_{\pm}
are the combinations of the measured bare fields
\delta n_{a,b}
.
The equal-time correlator of SDC is defined as
\langle d_{\pm}(t,\rho)d_{\pm}(t,\rho^{\prime})\rangle
, whose Fourier spectrum is

{\cal C}_{\pm,k}(t)=\frac{1}{2}+|T_{\pm}|^{2}+|S_{\pm}T_{\pm}|\cos(\omega^{\rm f}_{\pm,k}t+\theta_{\pm,k}).

(22)

The frequency and amplitude of
{\cal C}_{\pm,k}(t)
encapsulate
the information of universe dynamics.
Specifically, the oscillating frequencies
\omega^{\rm f}_{\pm,k}=k/a_{\pm}(t_{\rm f})
are inversely proportional to the scale factors, while the amplitudes are determined by the expansion/contraction history through the coefficients
S_{\pm}
and
T_{\pm}
.

In Fig.
3
(a), we present
{\cal C}_{\pm,k}(t)
of the flat FRW for different ramping processes illustrated in Fig.
2
.
Clearly, the oscillation frequencies (solid curves) of the expanding universe are larger than those of the contracting one. This result is robust to the various expansion and contraction histories. Additionally, the amplitudes of
{\cal C}_{\pm,k}(t)
exhibit variations depending on the ramping details.

The measurement for a curved FRW universe (
\kappa\neq 0
) can be performed in a very similar way.
In Fig.
3
(b), we show
{\cal C}_{\pm,k}(t)
for cases with various spatial curvatures
\kappa
, in which
\kappa=0
(lines with squares) denotes the flat FRW and the positive/negative
\kappa
are respectively realized by using the aforementioned
\bar{n}
and
\bar{n}^{(\prime)}
. The results show that the nonzero
\kappa
leads to different oscillation frequencies and amplitudes, both of which are suppressed compared to the flat case.

Summary-.

We have demonstrated that a pseudospin-1/2 BEC can simulate a dual phonon
universe, where the total-phase and relative-phase phononic modes live in
distinct spacetimes. By controlling the interspecies interaction, we can realize the simultaneous expansion of one universe and contraction of the other, accompanied by the creation of bosonic particles. These findings are proposed to be experimentally detected via the correlators of the density contrast.
Future studies may be extended to spinor BECs with higher spins, where increased internal degrees of freedom may facilitate the simulation of more complex cosmological models and unlock richer multiverse-related physics.

Appendix A
Supplemental Material of “Dual Phonon Universe in a Spinor Bose Gas”

In this Supplemental Material, we provide the detailed derivation for: (1) the hydrodynamics equation of interacting Bose gases; (2) the equation of motion (EoM) of the density and phase fluctuation fields in Bose gases; (3) the metric of the phonon universe, and (4) the EoM of phonon in the Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) spacetime.

A.1
1. Hydrodynamics equation

The Gross-Pitaevskii equation [Eq. (2) of the main text] can be derived by minimizing the action
S
[Eq. (1) of the main text] with respect to
\phi_{a(b)}
. Specifically, by taking the polar representation of the bosonic fields

\phi_{a(b)}=\sqrt{n_{a(b)}}e^{i\theta_{a(b)}},

(S1)

the action can be recast into

{\cal S}=\sum_{i=a,b}\int dtd^{2}{\bf x}\left[i\hbar\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}n_{i}+in_{i}\partial_{t}\theta_{i}\right)-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[{\bm{\nabla}}\left(\sqrt{n}_{i}e^{-i\theta_{i}}\right)\right]\cdot\left[{\bm{\nabla}}\left(\sqrt{n_{i}}e^{i\theta_{i}}\right)\right]\right]-\left[Vn_{i}-\frac{U_{ii}}{2}n_{i}^{2}-U_{ab}n_{a}n_{b}\right].

(S2)

We expand the second term as

\displaystyle\left[{\bm{\nabla}}\left(\sqrt{n}_{i}e^{-i\theta_{i}}\right)\right]\cdot\left[{\bm{\nabla}}\left(\sqrt{n_{i}}e^{i\theta_{i}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n_{i}}}({\bm{\nabla}}n_{i})e^{-i\theta_{i}}-i\sqrt{n_{i}}({\bm{\nabla}}\theta_{i})e^{-i\theta_{i}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n_{i}}}({\bm{\nabla}}n_{i})e^{i\theta_{i}}+i\sqrt{n_{i}}({\bm{\nabla}}\theta_{i})e^{i\theta_{i}}\right)

(S3)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{4n_{i}}({\bm{\nabla}}n_{i})\cdot({\bm{\nabla}}n_{i})+n_{i}(\bm{\nabla}\theta_{i})(\bm{\nabla}\theta_{i})\right].

Then, the action can be rewritten as

\displaystyle{\cal S}=\sum_{i=a,b}\int dtd^{2}{\bf x}\left[i\hbar\left(\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}n_{i}+in_{i}\partial_{t}\theta_{i}\right)-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{1}{4n_{i}}({\bm{\nabla}}n_{i})\cdot({\bm{\nabla}}n_{i})+n_{i}({\bm{\nabla}}\theta_{i})\cdot({\bm{\nabla}}\theta_{i})\right]-Vn_{i}-\frac{U_{ii}}{2}n_{i}^{2}-U_{ab}n_{a}n_{b}\right].

(S4)

The stationary point of
{\cal S}
with respect to
n_{i}
leads to the EoM of
n_{i}
,

\hbar\partial_{t}\theta_{i}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{\nabla^{2}n_{i}}{4n^{2}_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot\left(n_{i}^{-1}{\bm{\nabla}}n_{i}^{-1}\right)-\nabla^{2}\theta_{i}\right]-V-U_{ii}n_{i}-U_{ab}n_{j},\quad i,j=(a,b),i\neq j.

(S5)

Similarly, the stationary point of
{\cal S}
with respect to
\theta_{i}
leads to the
equation for continuity

\partial_{t}n_{i}+\frac{\hbar}{m}{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot(n_{i}{\bm{\nabla}}\theta_{i})=0,\quad i=(a,b).

(S6)

Equations (
S5
) and (
S6
) reproduce the hydrodynamics equations (2) and (3) in the main text.

A.2
2. Equation of motion of the fluctuation-fields

We denote the steady solution to the hydrodynamics equation as
\overline{n}_{i}
and
\overline{\theta}_{i}
, and the respective fluctuation as
\delta n_{i}
and
\delta\theta_{i}
. Therefore, we have

n_{i}=\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i};\quad\theta_{i}=\overline{\theta}_{i}+\delta\theta_{i}.

(S7)

By substituting Eq. (
S7
) into Eq. (
S5
) and (
S6
), we have

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}(\overline{\theta}_{i}+\delta\theta_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{{\nabla}^{2}(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i})}{4(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i})^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}{{\bm{\nabla}}}\cdot\left[(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i})^{-1}{{\bm{\nabla}}}(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i})^{-1}\right]-{\nabla}^{2}(\overline{\theta}_{i}+\delta\theta_{i})\right]

(S8)

\displaystyle-V-U_{ii}(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i})-U_{ab}(\overline{n}_{j}+\delta n_{j});

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i})+\frac{\hbar}{m}{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot[(\overline{n}_{i}+\delta n_{i}){\bm{\nabla}}(\overline{\theta}_{i}+\delta\theta_{i})]=0.

(S9)

Considering stationary part has solved the hydrodynamics equation, we end up with the equations of the fluctuation part. The time derivative of
\delta\theta_{i}
is written as

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{i}

(S10)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{1}{2\overline{n}_{i}^{2}}\left(({\bm{\nabla}\overline{n}_{i}})({\bm{\nabla}\delta n_{i}})-\frac{\delta n_{i}}{\overline{n}_{i}}{\bm{\nabla}}^{2}\overline{n}_{i}\right)+\frac{1}{2}{\bm{\nabla}}\left(\frac{1}{\overline{n}_{i}}{\bm{\nabla}}\delta n_{i}-\frac{\delta n_{i}}{\overline{n}_{i}}{\bm{\nabla}}\overline{n}_{i}\right)-2({\bm{\nabla}\overline{\theta}_{i}})({\bm{\nabla}\delta\theta_{i}})\right]-U_{ii}\delta n_{i}-U_{ab}\delta n_{j}

For uniform Bose gases, the second spatial derivative of density and density fluctuation can be ignored.
Therefore, we obtained the EoM for the fluctuation fields,

\displaystyle\hbar\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=-\hbar\cdot{\bf v}_{i}({\bm{\nabla}\delta\theta_{i}})-U_{ii}\delta n_{i}-U_{ab}\delta n_{j};

(S11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\delta n_{i}

\displaystyle=-{\bm{\nabla}}\cdot\left[(\delta n_{i}){\bf v}_{i}+\frac{\hbar}{m}\bar{n}_{i}{\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{i}\right].

(S12)

which reproduce the Eqs. (4) and (5) in the main text, with
{\bf v}_{i}=(\hbar{\bm{\nabla}}{\bar{\theta}}_{i})/m
being the superfluid velocity.

A.3
3. Metric of the phonon universe

It can be verified that the EoM [i.e., Eq. (8) of the main text]

\left[\left(\alpha\mp\beta\right)(\partial_{t}+{\bf v}_{i}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}})^{2}-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\right]\delta\theta_{\pm}=0,

(S13)

can be obtained by minimizing the action

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\delta\theta_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2}\int dtd^{2}{\bf x}\left[\left(\alpha\mp\beta\right)((\partial_{t}+{\bf v}_{i}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\delta\theta_{\pm}})^{2}-c^{2}\nabla^{2}\delta\theta_{\pm}\right]

(S14)

with respect to the field
\delta\theta_{\pm}(t,x,y)
.
In order to express
{\cal S}_{\delta\theta_{\pm}}
as a action of scalar field in a curved spacetime, we expand the integrand as

\displaystyle\left[\left(\alpha\mp\beta\right)((\partial_{t}+{\bf v}_{i}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}){\delta\theta_{\pm}})^{2}-c^{2}({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})\right]

(S15)

\displaystyle=\left[(\alpha\mp\beta)\left[(\partial_{t}^{2}\delta\theta_{\pm})+v_{i}^{2}({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})+{\bf v}_{i}(\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})+{\bf v}_{i}(\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})\right]-c^{2}({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm})\right]

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm}&{\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\alpha\mp\beta)&(\alpha\mp\beta){\bf v}_{i}\\
(\alpha\mp\beta){\bf v}_{i}&v_{i}^{2}(\alpha\mp\beta)-c^{2}\delta_{xy}&\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm}\\
{\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm}&{\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm}\end{array}\right)\mathcal{M}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm}\\
{\bm{\nabla}}\delta\theta_{\pm}\end{array}\right).

Therefore, the action can also be written in covariant form

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{\delta\theta_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2}\int dtd^{2}{\bf x}\sqrt{|\det(g_{\pm,\mu\nu})|}g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}({\partial_{\mu}}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\partial_{\nu}}\delta\theta_{\pm})

(S16)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\hbar}^{2}}{2}\int dtd^{2}{\bf x}\frac{-g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}}{\sqrt{|\det(g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm})|}}({\partial_{\mu}}\delta\theta_{\pm})({\partial_{\nu}}\delta\theta_{\pm}).

By comparing Eq. (
S15
) and (
S16
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{-g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}}{\sqrt{|\det(g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm})|}}=\mathcal{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\alpha\mp\beta)&(\alpha\mp\beta){\bf v}_{i}\\
(\alpha\mp\beta){\bf v}_{i}&v^{2}_{i}(\alpha\mp\beta)-c^{2}\delta_{xy}&\end{array}\right).

(S17)

Here, we have assumed that the superfluid velocity is isotropic, i.e.,
{\bf v}_{i}={\bf v}_{s}
. According to Eq. (
S17
), we can derive the expression of the metric

g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}=\frac{-\mathcal{M}}{\det\mathcal{M}}=\left(\frac{\alpha\mp\beta}{c^{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&{\bf v}_{s}\\
{\bf v}_{s}&\frac{c^{2}}{(\alpha\mp\beta)}\delta_{xy}-v^{2}_{s}&\end{array}\right).

(S18)

For the (2+1)d spacetime, it can be simplified as

g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&{v}_{s}&{v}_{s}\\
{v}_{s}&\frac{c^{2}}{(\alpha\mp\beta)}-{v}_{s}^{2}&-v_{s}^{2}\\
{v}_{s}&-v_{s}^{2}&\frac{c^{2}}{(\alpha\mp\beta)}-v_{s}^{2}\end{array}\right),

(S19)

which yields Eq. (9) of the main text.
When
{\bf v}_{s}=0
, we have

g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&0&0\\
0&\frac{c^{2}}{(\alpha\mp\beta)}&0\\
0&0&\frac{c^{2}}{(\alpha\mp\beta)}\end{array}\right),

(S20)

and

g_{\pm,\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}-1&0&0\\
0&\frac{(\alpha\mp\beta)}{c^{2}}&0\\
0&0&\frac{(\alpha\mp\beta)}{c^{2}}\end{array}\right),

(S21)

which yields
\sqrt{|g|}\equiv\sqrt{|\det(g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm})|}=(\alpha\mp\beta)/c^{2}
.

A.4
4. Scalar field in FRW universe

The action of the massless scalar field
\delta\theta_{\pm}(t,x,y)
in curved spacetime can be written as

\displaystyle{\cal S}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2}\int dtdxdy\left[\sqrt{|g|}g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}(\partial_{\mu}\delta\theta_{\pm})(\partial_{\nu}\delta\theta_{\pm})\right].

(S22)

According to the Euler-Lagrangian equation, we have the EoM of the field

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}(\sqrt{|g|}g^{\mu\nu}_{\pm}(\partial_{\nu}\delta\theta_{\pm}))=0

(S23)

For the (2+1)d FRW, which describes the universe expanding uniformly in all directions in the space part, the metric can be written as

ds^{2}=g_{\pm,\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}\right).

(S24)

By substituting the specific form of the metric (
S24
) into Eq. (
S23
), we finally obtain the Klein-Gordon equation of the massless scalar field
\delta\theta_{\pm}(t,x,y)
in the curved spacetime

\partial_{t}^{2}\delta\theta_{\pm}+2\frac{\dot{a}_{\pm}(t)}{a_{\pm}(t)}\partial_{t}\delta\theta_{\pm}-\frac{1}{a_{\pm}^{2}(t)}\nabla^{2}\delta\theta_{\pm}=0.

(S25)

which reproduces the Eq. (13) of the main text.

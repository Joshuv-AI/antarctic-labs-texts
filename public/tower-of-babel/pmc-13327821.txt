From Policy Foundation to System Implementation: First-year Achievements and Future Directions of Republic of Korea’s National Center for Injury Prevention and Control
Authors: Eunsun Lee, Tae Young Lee, Jonghee Choi, Sung Woo Lee, 은선 이, 태영 이, 종희 최, 성우 이
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesThis study reviews the institutional development of injury management policy in the Republic of Korea (ROK) and examines the roles and first-year achievements of the National Center for Injury Prevention and Control (NCIPC), following the enforcement of the “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries.”MethodsPolicy documents, legal frameworks, national injury data systems, and relevant literature were analyzed for major policy changes since the 2000s. The expansion of national injury surveillance, the accumulation of injury statistics, and the policy basis for establishing the NCIPC were specifically emphasized.ResultsROK’s injury management policy was initially fragmented across sectors. Since the 2010s, the expansion of national injury surveillance systems and the accumulation of standardized statistics have contributed to the recognition of injury prevention as an independent public health domain. The “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries” (enacted in 2024 and enforced in 2025) established the legal foundation for a coordinated national system, including the National Injury Management Committee and NCIPC. In 2025, key milestones included the assigning the entrusted institution for NCIPC operation and finalizing the First Comprehensive Injury Management Plan (2026–2030).ConclusionsThe NCIPC’s first-year operations represent a critical transition from policy formulation to system implementation. The NCIPC is expected to function as a central platform for injury surveillance, statistical production, policy support, workforce development, public communication, and multisectoral coordination. Its role will be further strengthened by implementing and evaluating national and regional injury management plans.Keywords: Injury prevention and control, National Center for Injury Prevention and Control, Health policy, Injury surveillance, Republic of Korea

Key messages

① What is known previously?

Injury is a preventable public health issue caused by external factors such as accidents, disasters, and poisoning. In the Republic of Korea, injury management has been fragmented across sectors, whereas national surveillance systems and statistics have provided a foundation for prevention policies.

② What new information is presented?

The “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries” established the legal and institutional basis for a national injury management system, including a comprehensive plan, a national committee, and the Central Injury Management Center.

③ What are implications?

The Central Injury Management Center is expected to function as a national hub for surveillance and statistics, support central–local coordination, and promote evidence-based policy, education, and workforce development to strengthen the implementation of the First Comprehensive Plan.

Introduction

Injury is a major public health problem that causes premature death, disability, increased healthcare costs, and loss of productivity. However, until the 2000s, injury management in the Republic of Korea (ROK) was centered on individual domains, such as traffic accidents, industrial accidents, school safety incidents, disasters, and poisoning, as well as the ministries responsible for those domains. This approach was useful for sector-specific responses, but it had limitations in comprehensively understanding the national-level burden of injury and in setting prevention priorities.

As the Korea Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (KCDC)’s National Injury Surveillance Program was scaled up around 2010, statistical monitoring of injury incidence and mortality was strengthened. Injury surveillance data were used as key indicators in areas such as child accident prevention policies, and injury prevention management goals were reflected in the 2015 National Health Promotion Plan, injury was incorporated into the domain of national health policy. Thereafter, injury prevention policy research, information sharing, and the publication of the National Injury Fact Book continued, establishing a foundation for addressing injury at the public health policy level.

Nevertheless, injury-related programs were implemented in a fragmented manner across individual ministries and agencies, and injury-related mortality and disease burden remained high. Given the existence of disparities by life stage, region, and injury mechanism, the need for a more integrated and coordinated national injury management system has been raised. The enactment and promulgation of the 2024 “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries” can be regarded as an institutional shift in injury management policy, in that it established the legal basis for the responsibilities of the national government and local governments, the National Injury Management Committee, the Comprehensive Injury Management Plan, and the Central Injury Management Center. Therefore, on the occasion of the first anniversary of the opening of the Central Injury Management Center, this paper aims to examine trends in injury management policy in the ROK, the background to the establishment of the center, its achievements in its first year of operation, and future directions for development.

Body

1. Injury Management Policy Trends Prior to the Establishment of the Central Injury Management Center

National injury prevention and management policy evolved from a decentralized response organized by accident type and ministry in the early 2000s, to the full-scale implementation of the National Injury Surveillance Program in 2010, the inclusion of injury prevention goals in the National Health Promotion Plan in 2015, the enactment of the “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries” in 2024, and the establishment and operation of the Central Injury Management Center in 2025 (Table 1). Based on this, the main text reviews policy trends prior to the establishment of the center and the development of the implementation infrastructure for the national injury management system.

1) Early injury management centered on a decentralized response

The initial stage of ROK’s injury management policy was centered on responses to individual injury types such as traffic accidents, industrial accidents, school safety accidents, disasters, and poisoning. Each ministry carried out related projects within its own jurisdiction, but an integrated national policy addressing injury as a whole was insufficient [1]. During this period, injury prevention was strongly perceived as a matter of individual accident response or safety management, and its concept as an independent domain within public health policy was not yet well established.

2) Full-scale implementation of the National Injury Surveillance Program and development of a statistical foundation

With the full-scale implementation of the National Injury Surveillance Program by the KCDC in 2010, injury management policy gradually developed into a data-driven system. Injury incidence and mortality statistics were systematically managed, and surveillance data were utilized as key indicators for selected prevention policies [2,3]. This marked the beginning of a shift toward viewing injury not merely as a collection of incidents, but as a public health problem that can be managed through surveillance and analysis.

3) Incorporation into the health policy domain and strengthening of policy status

With the inclusion of injury prevention management goals in the 2015 National Health Promotion Plan, injury began to be established as part of national health policy. Subsequently, injury prevention policy research, information sharing, and statistical production expanded, and national-level injury status information was accumulated through the periodic publication of the National Injury Fact Book. This strengthened recognition that injury prevention policy requires a mid-to-long-term management framework beyond individual level programs.

4) Emergence of the need for an integrated management system and institutional preparation

Although injury-related programs continued from 2015 to 2023, fragmented across ministries and agencies remained a key limitation to policy effectiveness. Given persistent injury-related mortality and disease burden, as well as disparities by life stage, region, and injury mechanism, the need for an integrated and coordinated national injury management system increased [4]. Accordingly, the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) explored a national-level integrated management approach linking surveillance data with policy.

5) Legislation and institutionalization of the national injury management system

With the enactment and promulgation of the “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries” in 2024, injury management obtained a clear legal foundation. This established a national-level institutional framework covering the responsibilities of national and local governments, the National Injury Management Committee, central and regional injury management systems, and the formulation and deliberation of implementation plans [5,6]. Thus, injury management transitioned from fragmented ministry-specific responses to a national integrated prevention and management system, providing the institutional basis, for establishing and operating the Central Injury Management Center.

2. From System to Implementation: Launch of the Central Injury Management Center and First-year Achievements

1) Establishment of the implementation infrastructure for the national injury management system

The Central Injury Management Center is the nation’s first national-level professional institution for injury management, established under the “Act on the Prevention and Management of Injuries.” It began full-scale operations in April 2025 after Korea University Anam Hospital was selected as the commissioned operating institution. The center was designed as a national injury management operational hub performing functions including injury information collection and analysis, risk factor analysis, education and public relations, professional workforce training, policy and research support, and collaboration with local and relevant agencies.

In the first year, it established an organizational structure consisting of 1 office, 4 teams, and 9 subcommittees, and operated with part-time team leaders and an external expert working group. It also prepared an implementation framework to carry out the functions of injury investigation and surveillance, information generation, education and public relations, and research cooperation. This demonstrates that the Central Injury Management Center has begun to function as a working-level organization supporting the actual operation of the national injury management system, transitioning from a newly established organization with a legal basis.

2) Support for injury investigation and surveillance operations and strengthening the evidence base

The Central Injury Management Center supported the stable operation of the National Injury Investigation and Surveillance Program and strengthened the evidence base for injury policy. In the In-depth Injury Patient Survey upon Hospital Discharge, 295,458 sample subjects were selected from 212 participating hospitals nationwide, and data quality management, and support for survey operations were supported. The Emergency Department-based Injury In-depth Surveillance improved the accuracy and utility of its data through operation of a consultative committee comprising representatives from the 23 participating hospitals, quality management meetings, monthly data reviews, hospital-specific feedback, training for data collectors, and revision of survey items.

3) Establishing the statistical production infrastructure for the National Injury Information Hub

In 2025, the center established an infrastructure for the systematic production and dissemination of injury information and statistics by publishing four major injury-related statistical compilations (Figure 1) [7-10]. This achievement went beyond merely collecting injury surveillance data by systematically organizing the national injury data and converting them into information that can be used for policy and research. Through this, the Central Injury Management Center established a foundation for the regular production and provision of national injury information.

4) Promoting public discourse on injury prevention and enhancing public awareness

The Central Injury Management Center raised public awareness of injury prevention and established a foundation for bringing the issue to public attention and placing it on the public agenda. From November 3 to November 7, 2025, an Injury Prevention Week program was conducted, including the Injury Prevention Day Proclamation Ceremony and the Injury Forum (Table 2). The various programs conducted during this period provided momentum for recognizing injuries not as the result of individual carelessness or random accidents, but as preventable public health problems, and for promoting a shared societal understanding of this perspective. Through these efforts, injury prevention was brought to the forefront as a national public health agenda, and a foundation was established for relevant experts and institutions to jointly discuss the direction of injury prevention policy.

5) Training a specialized workforce for life-stage injury prevention

In its first year after opening, the center developed and operated a specialized instructor training course in older-adult fall prevention, thereby establishing a foundation for training specialized personnel in injury management. Falls among older adults are a leading injury issue in an aging society, and standardized training and the availability of qualified instructors are essential for implementing effective prevention activities in community and everyday settings. The operation of this training program is significant in that it established a foundation for training frontline personnel to address falls among older adults, a major life-stage injury issue.

6) Establishing a foundation for multisectoral collaboration and injury management governance

The Central Injury Management Center established a cooperative framework for injury management through the signing of MOUs with major public health and injury-related organizations and academic societies, including the Korean Association of Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation, the Korean Society for Preventive Medicine, the Korean Society of Traumatology, the Korean Society of Clinical Toxicology, and the Korean Academy of Rehabilitation Medicine. This demonstrates that injury prevention is not limited to public health policy and community prevention activities, but requires a life-stage approach encompassing emergency response, trauma care, poisoning management, and rehabilitation. In particular, collaboration with academic societies possessing expertise in each stage of prevention, treatment, and rehabilitation is an important foundation for establishing a national injury management system that links prevention before injury occurrence with treatment and functional recovery after injury.

3. Future Roles and Prospects

Going forward, the first task of the Central Injury Management Center is to enhance the integration and usability of injury information. Although injury-related data have been continuously accumulated through investigations, surveillance, and statistical reporting, the system for analyzing these data and translating the findings into forms that can be readily applied tor policy decisions and community interventions needs to be further strengthened. The center should analyze injury risk factors through systematic data collection and research and evolve into a data-driven policy and research support institution that supports policy decision-making.

The second task is to operationalize the central–regional collaborative system. The Central Injury Management Center should play a role in enhancing the effectiveness of injury prevention and management programs through coordination among regional injury management centers and related organizations. In the future, it will be necessary for the Center to serve as a technical support hub linking central and local governments by developing tailored strategies that reflect regional injury status and risk factors, conducting local-level education and public outreach, supporting implementation plans, and sharing best practices.

The third task is evidence-based policy support through the analysis of injury risk factors and research on prevention technologies. The center should promote the development of tools to measure the ability to understand injury prevention information, establish guidelines for planning community-based injury prevention programs, analyze structural factors to reduce regional disparities in injuries, and conduct research to identify high-risk groups using tailored approaches. Such research can provide a basis for developing injury prevention policies that are more targeted and effective.

The fourth task is nationwide public education and public awareness communications, and the promotion of a culture of injury prevention. The center must expand injury prevention into a culture of practice in people’s daily lives through injury prevention education and promotion by life stage and setting; Injury Prevention Day events; results briefings on National Injury Survey/Surveillance data; dissemination of unintentional injury prevention guidelines for infants and young children; and school-based education for adolescents. This means that injury prevention must move beyond an expert-centered policy domain and become institutionalized as a public health agenda grounded in public participation.

The fifth task is to train specialized personnel in injury management and establish a collaborative framework. The center should strengthen the capacity of frontline personnel who will implement injury prevention and management programs in the field by providing a training program to develop certified instructors for older-adult fall prevention, training public officials responsible for injury management, and identifying operational strategies for Addiction Management Integrated Support Centers. Furthermore, it is necessary to expand a collaborative framework, led by the KDCA and the Central Injury Management Center, that allows for the continued participation of experts, public institutions, industry, and associations. Through this, the Central Injury Management Center can provide practical support for the KDCA’s national injury management policy and function as a platform organization in the national injury management field that connects education, policy, field implementation, and collaboration.

Conclusion

ROK’s injury management policy had long remained a decentralized system orgaized by accident type and responsible ministry; however, it began to transition toward a national-level injury management system through the implementation of the National Injury Surveillance Program, the accumulation of injury statistics, the enactment of legislation, and the establishment of a national governance framework. The launch of the Central Injury Management Center is significant in that it translated this policy transition into operational implementation.

However, it is difficult to say that the national injury management system has been fully established through the establishment of the center alone. The system for timely linkage and feedback of injury-related data to support central and local policy and program implementation requires further development. In addition, local-level injury management systems, standardization of life-stage injury prevention programs, professional workforce development systems, and the foundation for multisectoral collaboration and public communication must continue to be strengthened.

Going forward, the Central Injury Management Center should work to broaden a social consensus that injuries are not accidental events, but preventable and predictable public health problems. To this end, it is necessary to link injury surveillance data and statistics with risk factor analysis and policy support, strengthen the implementation capacity of community-based injury prevention programs through a central–regional collaborative system, and institutionalize education, public outreach, and the development of a qualified injury prevention workforce. Furthermore, as the establishment and review of implementation plans by relevant ministries and agencies and by metropolitan cities and provinces will begin in earnest after 2026, the Central Injury Management Center must strengthen its coordination function to link the direction of the national plan to local implementation and to feed back on-the-ground local experience into policy improvement. By pursuing these priorities, the Central Injury Management Center will strengthen the implementation of the national injury management policy and establish itself as a central institution leading the development of a sustainable injury prevention and management system.

손상은 조기 사망과 장애, 의료비 부담, 생산성 손실을 초래하는 주요 건강 문제이다. 그러나 우리나라의 손상관리는 2000년대까지 교통사고, 산업재해, 학교안전사고, 재난, 중독 등 개별 영역과 소관 부처 중심으로 추진되어 왔다. 이러한 방식은 분야별 대응에는 유용했으나, 국가 단위에서 손상 발생 현황을 통합적으로 파악하고 예방 우선순위를 설정하는 데에는 한계가 있었다.

2010년 전후 질병관리본부의 국가손상감시사업이 본격화되면서 손상 발생과 사망에 대한 통계 관리가 강화되었다. 손상감시자료는 어린이 안전사고 예방정책 등 일부 분야에서 주요 지표로 활용되었고, 2015년 국민건강증진종합계획에 손상예방‧관리 목표가 반영되면서 손상은 국가 건강정책 영역 안으로 편입되었다. 이후 손상예방 정책 연구, 정보공유, 손상종합통계집 발간이 지속되며 손상 문제를 보건정책 차원에서 다룰 수 있는 기반이 축적되었다.

그럼에도 손상 관련 사업은 각 부처와 기관별로 분절적으로 추진되었고, 손상으로 인한 사망과 질병부담은 여전히 높은 수준을 보였다. 생애주기‧지역‧손상 기전별 격차가 존재한다는 점에서 보다 통합적이고 조정 가능한 국가손상관리체계의 필요성이 제기되었다. 2024년 「손상 예방 및 관리에 관한 법률」 제정‧공포는 국가와 지방자치단체의 책무, 국가손상관리위원회, 손상관리종합계획, 중앙손상관리센터의 법적 근거를 마련했다는 점에서 손상관리 정책의 제도적 전환으로 볼 수 있다. 이에 본 원고는 중앙손상관리센터 개소 1주년을 계기로 우리나라 손상관리 정책의 흐름과 센터의 설치 배경, 개소 첫해 성과 및 향후 발전 방향을 고찰하고자 한다.

1) 분산 대응 중심의 초기 손상관리

우리나라 손상관리 정책의 초기 단계는 교통사고, 산업재해, 학교안전사고, 재난, 중독 등 개별 손상유형별 대응이 중심이었다. 각 부처가 소관 영역별로 관련 사업을 추진하였으나, 손상 전체를 포괄하는 국가적 관점의 통합 정책은 미흡하였다[1]. 이 시기 손상예방은 개별 사고 대응이나 안전관리 문제로 인식되는 경향이 강했으며, 보건정책 내 독립 영역으로서의 개념은 충분히 정립되지 않았다.

2) 국가손상감시사업의 본격화와 통계 기반의 축적

2010년 질병관리본부의 국가손상감시사업이 본격 추진되면서 손상관리 정책은 점차 자료 기반의 체계를 갖추기 시작하였다. 손상 발생 및 사망 통계가 체계적으로 관리되었고, 손상감시통계는 일부 예방정책의 주요 지표로 활용되었다[2, 3]. 이는 손상 문제를 단순한 사건의 집합이 아니라 감시와 분석을 통해 관리 가능한 공중보건 문제로 바라보는 전환의 출발점이 되었다.

3) 건강정책 영역으로의 편입과 정책적 위상 강화

2015년 국민건강증진종합계획에 손상예방‧관리 목표가 반영되면서 손상은 국가 건강정책의 일부로 자리 잡기 시작하였다. 이후 손상예방 정책 연구, 정보공유, 통계 생산이 확대되었고, 손상종합통계집의 주기적 발간을 통해 국가 단위 손상 현황에 대한 정보 기반이 축적되었다. 이러한 과정을 통해 손상예방정책이 개별 사업 차원을 넘어 중장기적 관리체계를 필요로 한다는 인식을 강화하였다.

4) 통합관리체계 필요성의 대두와 제도적 준비

2015년부터 2023년까지 손상 관련 사업은 지속되었으나, 부처별‧기관별 분절 구조는 여전히 정책 효과를 제한하는 요인으로 지적되었다. 손상으로 인한 사망과 질병부담이 지속되고 생애주기‧지역‧손상 기전별 격차가 존재한다는 점에서 보다 통합적이고 조정 가능한 국가손상관리체계의 필요성이 커졌다[4]. 이에 질병관리청은 감시자료와 정책을 연계하는 국가 차원의 통합관리 방향을 모색하였다.

5) 법률 제정과 국가손상관리체계의 제도화

2024년 「손상 예방 및 관리에 관한 법률」이 제정‧공포되면서 손상관리는 명시적 법적 기반을 갖추게 되었다. 이를 통해 국가 및 지방자치단체의 책무, 국가손상관리위원회, 중앙 및 지역 손상관리체계, 시행계획 수립과 심의 등 국가 차원의 제도적 틀이 마련되었다[5,6]. 즉, 손상관리는 부처별 분절 대응에서 벗어나 국가 차원의 통합적 예방‧관리체계로 전환되는 단계에 이르렀으며, 이러한 제도적 기반 위에서 중앙손상관리센터의 설치‧운영이 가능해졌다.

2. 제도에서 실행으로: 중앙손상관리센터의 출범과 개소 1년의 성과

1) 국가손상관리체계의 실행 기반 구축

중앙손상관리센터는 「손상 예방 및 관리에 관한 법률」 시행에 따라 설치된 국내 최초의 국가 단위 손상관리 전문기관으로, 2025년 4월 고려대학교안암병원이 운영 수탁기관으로 선정되면서 본격 운영을 시작하였다. 센터는 손상정보 수집‧분석, 위험요인 분석, 교육‧홍보, 전문인력 양성, 정책‧연구 지원, 지역 및 유관기관 협력체계 구축 등을 수행하는 국가손상관리 실무 허브로 설계되었다.

개소 첫해에는 1실 4팀, 9분과의 조직체계를 갖추고 비상근팀장과 외부전문가 워킹그룹을 운영하며, 손상조사감시‧정보생산‧교육홍보‧연구협력 기능을 수행할 실행 구조를 마련하였다. 이는 중앙손상관리센터가 법적 근거에 기반한 신설 기관에서 국가손상관리체계의 실제 운영을 지원하는 실무기관으로 기능하기 시작했음을 보여준다.

2) 손상조사감시 운영 지원과 근거기반 강화

중앙손상관리센터는 국가손상조사감시사업의 안정적 운영을 지원하며 손상정책의 근거기반을 강화하였다. 퇴원손상심층조사에서는 전국 212개 참여병원, 295,458명의 표본대상자 추출과 자료 질관리, 조사운영 지원을 수행하였다. 응급실손상환자심층조사에서는 23개 참여병원 협의기구 운영, 질관리 간담회, 월 단위 자료 점검, 병원별 결과 환류, 조사자 교육 및 조사문항 개정을 통해 자료의 정확성과 활용도를 높였다.

3) 국가 손상정보 허브로서의 통계 생산 기반 마련

센터는 2025년 손상 관련 주요 통계집 4종을 발간하며 손상정보와 통계의 체계적 생산‧확산 기반을 마련하였다(그림 1) [7-10]. 이는 손상감시자료를 단순 수집자료에 그치지 않고, 국가 손상현황을 구조화하여 정책과 연구 현장에서 활용 가능한 정보로 전환한 성과이다. 이를 통해 중앙손상관리센터는 국가 손상정보를 정례적으로 생산‧제공하는 기반을 구축하였다.

5) 생애주기 손상예방을 위한 전문인력 양성

센터는 개소 첫해 노인낙상예방 전문강사 양성과정을 개발‧운영하며 손상관리 전문인력 양성의 기초를 구축하였다. 노인 낙상은 고령사회에서 대표적인 손상 문제로, 지역사회와 생활현장에서 실효성 있는 예방활동을 수행하기 위해 표준화된 교육과 전문 강사 확보가 필수적이다. 이번 교육과정 운영은 노인 낙상이라는 주요 생애주기 손상문제에 대응하기 위한 실무 인력 양성 기반을 마련했다는 점에서 의미가 있다.

6) 다부문 협력 기반과 손상관리 거버넌스 형성

중앙손상관리센터는 대한심폐소생협회, 대한예방의학회, 대한외상학회, 대한임상독성학회, 대한재활의학회 등 공중보건 및 손상 관련 주요 기관‧학회와의 MOU (Memorandum of Understanding) 체결을 통해 손상관리 협력체계의 기반을 조성하였다. 이는 손상예방이 보건정책과 지역사회 예방활동에 국한되지 않고, 응급대응, 외상치료, 중독관리, 재활에 이르는 전 주기적 접근을 필요로 함을 보여준다. 특히 예방–치료–재활 각 단계의 전문성을 가진 학회와의 협력은 손상 발생 전 예방부터 손상 후 치료와 기능 회복까지 연계하는 국가손상관리체계 구축의 중요한 기반이라 할 수 있다.

3. 향후 역할과 미래

향후 중앙손상관리센터의 첫 번째 과제는 손상정보의 통합성과 활용성을 높이는 것이다. 손상 관련 자료는 조사‧감시‧통계 측면에서 지속적으로 축적되어 왔으나, 이를 정책결정과 지역사회 개입에 즉각 활용하기 위한 분석‧환류 체계는 아직 충분히 갖추어지지 않아 향후 단계적 강화가 필요하다. 이를 위해 중앙손상관리센터는 손상 감시 데이터의 표준화와 통합 플랫폼 구축, 지역 단위 분석 결과의 정기적 환류 체계 마련을 주도하고, 수집된 정보가 분석에서 정책 활용까지 끊김 없이 이어지는 데이터 순환 구조를 마련하는 역할을 수행해야 한다.

두 번째 과제는 중앙–지역 연계체계의 실질화이다. 중앙손상관리센터는 지역손상관리센터와 유관기관 간 연계를 통해 손상예방‧관리사업의 효과를 높이는 역할을 수행해야 한다. 향후 지역별 손상현황과 위험요인을 반영한 맞춤형 전략 수립, 지역 단위 교육‧홍보, 시행계획 지원, 우수사례 공유 등을 통해 중앙과 지역을 연결하는 기술지원 허브로 기능할 필요가 있다.

세 번째 과제는 손상위험요인 분석과 예방기술 연구를 통한 근거기반 정책지원이다. 센터는 손상예방 정보 이해능력 측정도구 개발, 지역사회 손상예방사업 기획 가이드라인 마련, 지역 간 손상 격차 해소를 위한 구조적 요인 분석, 맞춤형 고위험군 탐색 연구 등을 추진할 필요가 있다. 이러한 연구는 손상예방정책을 보다 정밀하고 효과적인 방향으로 발전시키는 근거가 될 수 있다.

네 번째 과제는 대국민 교육‧홍보와 손상예방문화 확산이다. 센터는 생애주기‧생활터별 손상예방 교육과 홍보, 손상예방의 날 행사, 국가손상조사감시자료 결과보고회, 영유아 비의도적 손상예방 수칙 보급, 청소년 학교교육 등을 통해 손상예방을 국민의 일상 속 실천문화로 확산해야 한다. 이는 손상예방이 전문가 중심의 정책 영역을 넘어 국민 참여 기반의 공중보건 과제로 정착해야 함을 의미한다.

다섯 번째 과제는 손상관리 전문인력 양성과 협력 생태계 구축이다. 센터는 노인낙상예방 전문강사 양성과정, 손상관리 담당 공무원 교육, 중독관리‧상담센터 운영방안 모색 등을 통해 현장에서 손상예방‧관리사업을 수행할 실무 인력의 역량을 강화해야 한다. 아울러 질병관리청과 중앙손상관리센터가 주축이 되어 전문가, 공공기관, 산업계, 협회 등이 지속적으로 참여할 수 있는 협력 구조를 확장할 필요가 있다. 이를 통해 중앙손상관리센터는 질병관리청의 국가손상관리정책을 실무적으로 지원하고, 교육‧정책‧현장‧협력을 연결하는 국가손상관리 분야의 플랫폼 기관으로 기능할 수 있다.

결 론

우리나라 손상관리 정책은 오랫동안 사고 유형별‧부처별 분산 대응에 머물렀으나, 국가손상감시사업 운영과 손상통계 축적, 법률 제정, 국가 거버넌스 형성을 거치며 국가 단위 손상관리체계로 전환되기 시작하였다. 중앙손상관리센터의 출범은 이러한 전환을 실제 운영으로 연결했다는 점에서 중요한 의미를 갖는다.

다만 센터 설치만으로 국가손상관리체계가 완성되었다고 보기는 어렵다. 손상 관련 자료를 중앙과 지역의 정책 및 사업 수행에 적시에 연계하고 환류하는 체계는 더 정교화될 필요가 있으며, 지역 단위 손상관리체계, 생애주기별 손상예방사업 표준화, 전문인력 양성체계, 다부문 협력과 대국민 소통도 지속적으로 고도화되어야 한다.

향후 중앙손상관리센터는 손상을 우연한 사고가 아니라 예방 가능하고 예측 가능한 건강 문제로 인식하는 사회적 기반을 확산해야 한다. 이를 위해 손상감시자료와 통계를 위험요인 분석과 정책지원으로 연결하고, 중앙–지역 연계체계를 통해 지역사회 손상예방사업의 실행력을 높이며, 교육‧홍보와 전문인력 양성을 체계화해야 한다. 또한 2026년 이후 관계부처와 시‧도 시행계획의 수립‧심의가 본격화되는 만큼, 중앙손상관리센터는 국가계획의 방향을 지역 실행으로 연결하고, 지역 현장의 경험을 다시 정책 개선으로 환류하는 조정 기능을 강화해야 한다. 이러한 방향이 구체화될 때 중앙손상관리센터는 국가손상관리정책의 실행력을 높이고, 지속가능한 손상예방‧관리체계 발전을 견인하는 중추기관으로 자리매김할 수 있을 것이다.

Adaptive Bayes estimator for stochastic differential equations with jumps under small noise asymptotics Thanks: wakamatsu@moegi.waseda.jp Thanks: shimizu@waseda.jp
Shuntaro Suzuki

Abstract
In this paper, we consider parameter estimation for stochastic differential equations driven by Wiener processes and compound Poisson processes. We assume unknown parameters corresponding to coefficients of the drift term, diffusion term, and jump term, as well as the Poisson intensity and the probability density function of the underlying jump. We propose estimators based on adaptive Bayesian estimation from discrete observations. We demonstrate the consistency and asymptotic normality of the estimators within the framework of small noise asymptotics.



Key words:
Jump-diffusion process; small noise asymptotics; discrete observations; Bayesian inference; adaptive estimation.

Main theorem

First, we introduce how to construct adaptive Bayes estimator. Denote

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{n,\varepsilon,\lambda}^{S}

3.2.1
Normal Distribution

Let
\Theta_{3}
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{2}-2}
, which is
open and convex with a smooth boundary and let
f_{\alpha}(y)
be as follows:

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(y)=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\alpha_{2}}}\exp\left\{-\frac{(y/c(x,\alpha)-\alpha_{1})^{2}}{2\alpha_{2}}\right\},\quad\alpha\in\overline{\Theta}_{3}.

Then,

\displaystyle\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\,-\log c(x,\alpha)-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\alpha_{2})-\frac{\lvert y/c(x,\alpha)-\alpha_{1}\rvert^{2}}{2\alpha_{2}},\quad(x,y,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\overline{\Theta}_{3}.

Let us confirm (A
8
). For
k=0,1,...,4
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle-\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\log c(x,\alpha)-\frac{1}{2\alpha_{2}}\left\{y^{2}\cdot\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\left(c(x,\alpha)^{-2}\right)-2\alpha_{1}y\partial_{\alpha}^{k}(c(x,\alpha)^{-1})+\partial_{\alpha}^{k}(\alpha_{2}^{2})\right\},

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{k}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{k}\left(\log\alpha_{2}\right)+\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{k}(\alpha_{2}^{-1})\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{c(x,\alpha)}-\alpha_{1}\right)^{2}\right\},

and

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha_{1}}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}}\left(\alpha_{1}-\frac{y}{c(x,\alpha)}\right),\quad\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{2}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{2}},\quad\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{3}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{4}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=0.

(A
8
) is satisfied, by Remark 2.1 and (A

).

3.2.2
Gamma Distribution

Let
\Theta_{3}
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(1,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{3}-2}
, which is
open and convex with a smooth boundary and let
f_{\alpha}(y)
be as follows:

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha_{2})\alpha_{1}^{\alpha_{2}}}z^{\alpha_{2}-1}\exp\{-z/\alpha_{1}\},\quad(z\geq 0),

for
\alpha\in\overline{\Theta}_{3}
. Then,

\displaystyle\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\,-\log c(x,\alpha)-\log\Gamma(\alpha_{2})-\alpha_{2}\log\alpha_{1}+(\alpha_{2}-1)\log y-\frac{y}{\alpha_{1}},

for
(x,y,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\overline{\Theta}_{3}
. Let us confirm (A
8
). The derivatives of
\psi(x,y,\alpha)
on the parameters
\alpha,\alpha_{1}
are given by

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\,\alpha_{2}\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\log c(x,\alpha)-\frac{y}{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\left(c^{-1}(x,\alpha)\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{k}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=

\displaystyle\,-\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{k}\left(\Gamma(\alpha_{1})\right)-\alpha_{2}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{k}\left(\log\alpha_{1}\right)-\frac{y}{c(x,\alpha)}\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{k}(\alpha_{1}^{-1}),

for
k=1,2,...,4
. Therefore, we have that, for
k=1,2,...,4
and any
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{\alpha}^{k}\left(\psi(x,y,\alpha)\right)\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha_{0}}(y)dy=R(x,1),

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{\alpha_{1}}^{k}\left(\psi(x,y,\alpha)\right)\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha_{0}}(y)dy=R(x,1).

The derivatives of
\psi(x,y,\alpha)
on the parameters
\alpha_{2}
are also given by

\displaystyle\,\partial_{\alpha_{2}}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=\log c(x,\alpha)-\log\Gamma(\alpha_{1})+\log y,

\displaystyle\,\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{2}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{3}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{4}\psi(x,y,\alpha)=0.

For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha\in\overline{\Theta}_{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\left\lvert\log y\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha}(y)dy\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\left\lvert y-1\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha}(y)dy\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}(1+\lvert y\rvert^{p})f_{\alpha}(y)dy<\infty.

(3.2.1)

Moreover, since
\left\lvert\log y\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha_{0}}(y)\to 0\quad as\quad y\downarrow 0

\left\lvert\log y\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha_{0}}(y)
is bounded on
[0,1]
for any
\alpha\in\overline{\Theta}_{3}
.
Thus, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, it is easy to get that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\log y\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha}(y)dy<\infty.

(3.2.2)

Combining (
3.2.1
) and (
3.2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert\partial_{\alpha_{2}}^{k}\psi(x,y,\alpha)\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha_{0}}(y)dy=R(x,1).

Thus, (A
8
) is satisfied.

for

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\lvert\log z\right\rvert^{p}f_{\alpha_{0}}(z)dz<\infty.

(3.2.4)

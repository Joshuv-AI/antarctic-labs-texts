The optimization path of agricultural industry structure and intelligent transformation by deep learning
Authors: Xingchen Pan, Jinyu Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study addresses key challenges in optimizing agricultural industry structures and facilitating intelligent transformation through the application of deep learning algorithms and advanced optimization techniques. An intelligent system for agricultural industry optimization is developed, with convolutional neural networks, recurrent neural networks, Long Short-Term Memory networks, and generative adversarial networks introduced for tasks such as image recognition, time series forecasting, and synthetic data generation. Subsequently, a hybrid optimization method is designed, combining the Genetic Algorithms with particle swarm optimization to improve the model’s global search capability and local convergence speed. The performance of these techniques is rigorously evaluated through extensive experimentation. The results demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms conventional algorithms in regression tasks, particularly in terms of computational efficiency, data processing speed, and model training stability, while also exhibiting high scalability. In crop yield prediction, the proposed method achieves superior performance, as evidenced by reductions in both absolute error and mean squared error, along with attaining the highest R2 value (0.93). Additionally, in pest and disease detection, the proposed method exceeds other models in accuracy (97.5%), precision (96.8%), recall (97.2%), and F1 score (0.97), underscoring its superior performance in detecting agricultural pests and diseases. The method also significantly surpasses traditional algorithms in crop disease identification accuracy, climate change prediction precision, and the quality of synthetic data generation. This study offers novel technical solutions and decision-making tools for advancing intelligent agriculture.

Introduction

Research background and motivations

Despite some progress driven by technological innovation, the global agricultural industry continues to face several challenges. Firstly, agricultural data is highly complex and diverse, encompassing multidimensional information such as climate, soil, and crop growth. Traditional analytical methods struggle to manage the nonlinear relationships between these complex datasets. Secondly, while existing deep learning models perform well in specific scenarios, their generalization ability is often limited, particularly in agricultural applications that span different regions and crop types. Finally, the dynamic nature of agricultural production requires systems that can adjust models and provide real-time feedback. However, most current methods lack this crucial mechanism, leading to delays and a lack of adaptability in decision-making1,2. Although some studies focus on waste resource management and pollution control, there is a significant gap in research on agricultural industry structure optimization, particularly in the integration of advanced deep learning technologies and intelligent management methods. Currently, deep learning technology, with its powerful data processing and pattern recognition capabilities, is increasingly being applied to the agricultural sector, facilitating the transition from traditional agriculture to smart agriculture, optimizing resource utilization, and reducing environmental impacts3,4. However, existing research on the application of deep learning in agricultural industry structure optimization and intelligent transformation is still incomplete, lacking comprehensive solutions that address resource management, sustainable development, and production efficiency5,6. Therefore, an innovative, deep learning-based systematic solution is urgently needed to address the issues of resource waste and environmental pollution in agriculture, which is the primary goal of this study.

Research objectives

The primary objective of this study is to propose a deep learning-based approach for optimizing the agricultural industry structure, with the aim of addressing resource waste and environmental pollution in the agricultural sector. Specifically, the objectives are as follows:

Research plan and focus

The main innovations of this study are reflected in the following three aspects:

Application of Hybrid Optimization Algorithms: Traditional deep learning models often face challenges such as local optima and slow convergence in parameter adjustment and resource optimization. This study combines genetic algorithms (GAs) and particle swarm optimization to form a hybrid optimization algorithm, which effectively integrates global search and local optimization during model training, improving resource utilization efficiency and reducing computational costs. This innovation addresses the issue of local optima in processing agricultural data.

Integration of Adaptive Learning and Real-Time Feedback Mechanisms: Agricultural production is influenced by dynamic factors such as climate and soil conditions, requiring real-time model parameter adjustments. This study introduces an adaptive learning algorithm and a real-time feedback mechanism, enabling the model to dynamically adjust parameters in response to changes in the external environment and optimize decision-making processes in real-time. This innovation effectively resolves the current limitations of agricultural models in quickly responding to environmental changes.

Cross-Domain Data Fusion and the Application of generative adversarial networks (GANs): Agricultural data is highly diverse and unevenly distributed, and existing methods often rely on specific datasets, leading to insufficient generalization. This study introduces GANs to generate synthetic agricultural data, enhancing the diversity of training data and improving the generalization capability of the model. It addresses the problem of data scarcity and poor generalization, and validates the effectiveness of its application across different agricultural scenarios.

The structure of this study is as follows: “Literature Review” Section reviews the relevant literature and analyzes the shortcomings of existing research. “Research Model” Section describes in detail the proposed research methods, including the design of deep learning models, hybrid optimization algorithms, and real-time feedback mechanisms. “Experimental Design and Performance Evaluation” Section presents the experimental design, data collection, and model performance evaluation, along with a discussion of the results and their implications. Finally, “Conclusion” Section provides conclusions and suggestions for future research directions.

Literature review

In recent years, deep learning technologies have demonstrated significant potential within the agricultural sector7. These advancements have contributed to major progress in areas such as pest and disease detection, crop growth forecasting, and soil quality evaluation8. For instance, Veerachamy and Ramar (2022) utilized convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for image recognition, enabling farmers to accurately diagnose crop diseases and implement timely preventive actions9. Similarly, Attri et al. (2023) highlighted the effectiveness of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in agricultural time series analysis, which are crucial for predicting the effects of climate change on crop yields, thus offering critical insights for agricultural decision-making10.

With the successful application of metaheuristic algorithms in optimization models, the performance of deep learning has seen further enhancement. For instance, Jovanovic et al. (2024) proposed a framework for crop yield prediction based on metaheuristic algorithm-tuned agnostic neural networks, significantly improving the model’s prediction accuracy11. Additionally, Kolipaka and Namburu (2024) designed an automated crop yield prediction framework using a two-stage classifier, optimizing its performance through metaheuristic methods12. These successful cases demonstrate that metaheuristic algorithms can effectively enhance the performance of deep learning models in agriculture, addressing the common issue of traditional models being prone to local optima.

Although deep learning has achieved significant progress in agricultural applications, research on optimizing agricultural industry structures remains relatively limited. Most existing studies focus on promoting sustainable development by improving resource allocation and enhancing agricultural efficiency13,14. The continuous advancement of modern agricultural technologies has facilitated the optimization and upgrading of agricultural structures, including improvements in planting configurations, processing of agricultural products, and distribution systems15. Sharma et al. (2022) underscored the importance of integrating regional agricultural characteristics with sustainable development strategies, proposing various optimization models to address agricultural production challenges in different regions16. For instance, Corceiro et al. (2023) employed a graph CNN optimized with metaheuristic algorithms to classify carrot grading systems, significantly enhancing the accuracy of agricultural product quality assessment17. Similarly, Kathole et al. (2024) advanced the intelligent optimization of the agricultural industry through the identification and classification of pests and diseases using a metaheuristic deep learning approach18.

As a crucial component of agricultural modernization, smart agriculture integrates the Internet of Things (IoT), big data, and artificial intelligence (AI) technologies to enable intelligent and precise management of agricultural production19. The extensive use of intelligent sensor networks provides real-time monitoring and precision control capabilities, which in turn improve resource utilization efficiency and agricultural product quality20. Additionally, smart agriculture accelerates the digitization of agriculture, facilitating the optimization and intelligent upgrading of the agricultural supply chain21. Seyedmohammadi et al. (2023) proposed a crop yield prediction model based on an echo state network optimized by metaheuristic algorithms, significantly enhancing the model’s prediction accuracy and scalability through metaheuristic optimization of the network structure22.

In recent years, the application of deep learning in agriculture has increasingly focused on multimodal fusion and small-object detection in complex environments to meet the demands of real-world scenarios. For instance, You and Liu (2023) optimized small-object detection using an improved You Only Look Once version 5 (YOLOv5) algorithm. By incorporating a Context-Aware Spatial Module (CASM) attention mechanism and multi-scale feature learning, they significantly improved the model’s accuracy in detecting small objects within complex backgrounds while reducing computational complexity, which provided new insights for small-object detection in agriculture23. Additionally, You et al. (2024) proposed a multimodal fusion approach that integrated physical information, such as sound and vibration, into deep learning models to enhance interpretability and stability in diagnosing equipment faults in agricultural machinery. This method, based on constraint-guided multimodal information fusion, enabled the model to more accurately capture environmental features, leading to improved diagnostic accuracy and reduced computational demands. This approach showed considerable potential for environmental monitoring in agricultural applications24. These studies provide effective solutions for complex environment perception and multimodal data fusion in smart agriculture and serve as valuable references for combining deep learning with multimodal optimization in this study.

Despite the significant advancements in smart agriculture and deep learning technologies, several limitations remain. First, many studies are overly reliant on specific datasets and algorithms, resulting in models with limited generalization capabilities. Second, although some research employs metaheuristic algorithms to optimize models, these applications are often restricted to particular crop types or geographic regions, lacking broad validation across large-scale, cross-regional agricultural data. Moreover, existing studies on metaheuristic methods tend to focus on optimizing individual tasks or specific parameters, without offering a systematic solution for the overall optimization of agricultural industry structures.

In response to these challenges, this study proposes a comprehensive optimization pathway for agricultural industry structures based on deep learning. By integrating advanced deep learning techniques with intelligent management methods, the goal is to improve agricultural production efficiency, minimize resource waste, and promote environmentally sustainable development. The innovation of this study lies in the combination of deep learning with hybrid optimization techniques, offering a systematic solution that is validated through practical applications to demonstrate its effectiveness in enhancing agricultural sustainability.

The system aims to mitigate issues of resource waste and environmental pollution within the agricultural sector while enhancing production efficiency and sustainability. This design approach significantly lowers computational costs while maintaining high accuracy, ensuring deployability on edge devices and in low-resource computing environments. This aspect is particularly crucial in agricultural production scenarios, as it guarantees the model’s efficiency and practicality.

In the task of pest and disease detection, the interpretability of the model is essential for agricultural practitioners to understand and trust its outcomes. Although this study does not present specific visualizations, future work will plan to utilize visualization techniques such as Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) to analyze the model’s decision-making basis34,35. These visualization tools can highlight specific regions of images, illustrating which features contributed to pest and disease classification decisions. Such interpretability can help farmers and agricultural decision-makers better understand the model’s outputs, thereby enhancing the credibility and practicality of pest and disease detection. By integrating these visualization techniques, the model’s application extends beyond accuracy enhancement, providing users with a clear decision-making pathway and thus fostering greater trust in intelligent agricultural systems.

Optimization algorithm design

To enhance the model’s global search capability and facilitate rapid convergence, this study employs a hybrid optimization method that combines GAs and particle swarm optimization36,37. GA simulates biological evolution by optimizing individual fitness through selection, crossover, and mutation, with the fitness function defined as: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:F\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{1+f\left(x\right)}$$\end{document}, where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:f\left(x\right)$$\end{document} is the objective function and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:F\left(x\right)$$\end{document}is the fitness function. This study selects GAs and particle swarm optimization as the core combination of optimization techniques, primarily due to their maturity and widespread application in addressing global search and rapid convergence issues. Although novel optimization methods, such as the Crayfish Optimization Algorithm and the Reptile Search Algorithm, have emerged in recent years, their empirical applications in agricultural industry optimization remain limited. GA is renowned for its excellent global search capability, effectively avoiding local optima, while particle swarm optimization ‘s rapid convergence characteristic enables it to quickly locate near-optimal solutions. According to the No Free Lunch Theorem, no single optimization algorithm performs optimally across all problems38. Therefore, the choice to combine GA and particle swarm optimization is grounded in their successful applications to similar problems and their reliability in addressing practical agricultural issues.

This study employs the combination of GA and particle swarm optimization primarily for the parameter tuning of models, particularly in the hyperparameter optimization processes of CNNs and RNNs. Through meta-heuristic algorithms, key parameters such as kernel size, the number of hidden layer units, and learning rate are searched and optimized to enhance the model’s global search capability and local optimization accuracy. This process plays a critical role in training models for crop yield prediction and pest detection, effectively improving model performance. Due to the inherent randomness of meta-heuristic algorithms, this study conducts 30 independent runs for each optimization experiment to ensure the stability and reproducibility of the results, with the average value taken as the final outcome. Multiple runs help reduce random error and ensure the reliability of the proposed method in practical applications.

Pseudo code of adaptive learning algorithm:

Algorithm: Adaptive Learning Algorithm

Input: Initial model parameters θ, learning rate η, threshold T.

Repeat until convergence:

For each training sample (x_t, y_t):

a. Compute the model output: y_pred = Model(x_t, θ).

b. Compute loss: L = Loss(y_t, y_pred).

c. Update parameters using gradient descent: θ = θ - η * ∇L.

d. Adjust learning rate η based on performance.

e. If validation loss stops improving for T epochs:

Stop training early to avoid overfitting.

Output: Optimized model parameters θ.

Pseudo code of the hybrid algorithm of genetic algorithm and particle swarm optimization:

Algorithm: Hybrid GA-PSO Optimization.

Input: Population size N, max generations G, crossover rate C, mutation rate M.

Initialize population with random solutions.

For generation = 1 to G do:

1. Evaluate fitness of each individual in the population.

2. Select individuals based on fitness (selection).

3. Apply crossover to selected individuals with probability C.

4. Apply mutation to individuals with probability M.

5. Update velocity and position for each particle (PSO step):

- Update velocity: v_i = w * v_i + c1 * r1 * (p_best - x_i) + c2 * r2 * (g_best - x_i).

- Update position: x_i = x_i + v_i.

6. Replace the worst individuals with new solutions if fitness improves

Output: Optimal solution.

Real-time feedback optimization algorithm pseudo code:

Algorithm: Real-Time Feedback Optimization

Input: Initial model parameters θ, real-time data stream D, adjustment threshold T

While receiving data D do:

1. Collect new data sample (sensor readings, environmental data, etc.)

2. Update model predictions based on new data

3. If error > T:

a. Adjust model parameters θ

b. Send feedback to control systems (e.g., irrigation, fertilization)

Output: Continuously optimized model and control system actions

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection and experimental environment

To enhance the generalization capability of the model, this study utilizes a dataset that encompasses crop growth data from various regions, including multiple crop types (such as rice, wheat, and corn) and diverse climatic conditions (e.g., tropical and temperate). The diversity of these datasets allows the proposed model to adapt to a wider range of agricultural scenarios, thereby increasing its practicality. The datasets employed in this study are primarily sourced from several public repositories: the Ministry of Agriculture’s Crop Pest and Disease Image Database, the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) Crop Production Dataset, soil and meteorological data from the National Agricultural Informatization Engineering Technology Research Center, and the Crop Dataset from Kaggle. Specifically, the Agricultural Ministry’s Crop Pest and Disease Image Database contains a substantial collection of high-resolution images depicting various crop pests and diseases, each accompanied by detailed labels and classification information. This image data is instrumental in training and validating CNNs for the efficient identification and classification of crop pests and diseases, thus enabling farmers to implement timely preventive measures, enhance production efficiency, and improve crop quality. The Food and Agriculture Organization Crop Production Dataset provides comprehensive information on crop yields, planting areas, and production conditions across multiple global regions. This dataset is utilized to train RNNs and LSTM networks for time series forecasting, such as evaluating the impacts of climate change on crop yields. This approach offers a scientific basis for agricultural production decisions, thereby improving resource utilization efficiency and promoting agricultural sustainability. The soil and meteorological data sourced from the National Agricultural Informatization Engineering Technology Research Center include detailed insights into soil types, soil fertility, and various meteorological conditions (e.g., temperature, precipitation, humidity). This data is leveraged to optimize various facets of agricultural production through deep learning models and hybrid optimization algorithms, facilitating precise monitoring and forecasting of crop growing environments and enhancing the intelligent management of agricultural production. The Crop Dataset from Kaggle encompasses several years of crop growth data, including information on crop types, growth cycles, yields, and the corresponding meteorological and soil conditions. This dataset is utilized to train GANs to generate additional synthetic agricultural data, thereby augmenting the model’s generalization capabilities and demonstrating its significant potential to improve agricultural production efficiency and environmental sustainability through empirical experimentation.

Batch sizes are adjusted dynamically according to the convergence speed of the model and the available computational resources, aiming to balance training time with model stability. Discussion

Compared to traditional methods such as SVM, RF, and GBM, the proposed method demonstrates superior capabilities in handling large-scale and diverse agricultural data, resulting in higher prediction accuracy and stability. This outcome is primarily attributed to the effective tuning of model parameters through the hybrid optimization algorithm, enabling the model to more accurately capture the underlying patterns and relationships within complex tasks, thereby enhancing predictive accuracy. In the study by Hadipour-Rokni et al. (2023), which utilized only CNNs for pest and disease identification, although significant improvements in generalization and accuracy were achieved for specific tasks. The experiment lacked a comprehensive consideration of agricultural industry structure optimization by integrating CNNs with GANs for synthetic data generation55. The data generated by GANs closely resembles real data in terms of SSIM and PSNR, validating its effectiveness in large-scale agricultural image data. This data generation strategy addresses the shortcomings of traditional machine learning methods, further enhancing the robustness and detection accuracy of pest and disease identification models. In the realm of climate change prediction, Park et al. (2023) employed RNNs and LSTM networks for time series data analysis, achieving favorable results; however, they did not incorporate optimization algorithms for further model refinement56. This experiment significantly improves the data processing capabilities of RNNs and LSTMs under varying environmental conditions through hybrid optimization. For instance, in the crop yield prediction task, the proposed method achieved a MSE of 2.35, significantly outperforming benchmark algorithms. Continuous tuning of the model via the hybrid optimization algorithm demonstrated strong adaptability in time series forecasting tasks, maintaining high prediction accuracy and stability despite fluctuations in climate and environmental conditions. While metaheuristic algorithms such as Crayfish Optimization, Reptile Search, and Red Fox Optimizer approach the performance of the proposed method on certain metrics, they exhibit slight deficiencies in computational efficiency and stability. The proposed method outperforms these algorithms in training time (4.1 h) and inference time (20 ms), showcasing its efficiency and adaptability for practical deployment. Additionally, the low variance observed across multiple independent runs indicates more stable and consistent performance. These findings highlight the unique advantages of the proposed method in integrating hybrid optimization with deep learning, making it suitable for addressing complex agricultural tasks in real-world applications. Regarding the impact of smart transformation on agricultural productivity and economic benefits, the experimental results indicate that more accurate yield predictions and pest detection can effectively reduce production costs (from $500 to $450 per acre) and significantly decrease losses due to pests and diseases (from 10 to 3%). This improvement is primarily driven by the deep learning model’s effective handling of multidimensional data in complex environments. The integration of GAN-generated data with the hybrid optimization algorithm enables the model to better adapt to dynamic agricultural settings, thereby supporting enhanced production efficiency and resource optimization. These results not only validate the practical application value of the proposed method but also lay a solid foundation for future advancements in the smart transformation of agriculture.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study addresses the core challenges of optimizing agricultural industrial structures and facilitating the smart transformation of agriculture by proposing an intelligent agricultural system that integrates deep learning and optimization technologies. By employing CNNs for crop pest and disease recognition and classification, RNNs and LSTM networks for predicting climate change and crop growth cycles, GANs for synthesizing agricultural data, and a hybrid optimization method that combines GAs and particle swarm optimization, the proposed system successfully enhances the precision and efficiency of agricultural production. The model incorporates adaptive learning and a real-time optimization feedback mechanism, thereby improving decision-making quality under varying environmental conditions. This system excels in processing and optimizing multidimensional agricultural data, offering new pathways for the intelligent transformation of agriculture.

Future works and research limitations

Despite these significant achievements, this study acknowledges certain limitations. First, although the model demonstrates commendable performance, its generalization ability across different agricultural scenarios still requires further validation. Future work will integrate the optimization system with real-time monitoring technologies to create a dynamic feedback mechanism, allowing adjustments to agricultural management strategies based on real-time data and enhancing the model’s practical applicability. Second, this study has yet to present specific visualizations of the model’s decision-making processes. Future plans include employing visualization techniques such as Grad-CAM and LIME to analyze the model’s decision basis, thereby improving its interpretability and transparency. Additionally, while deep learning models exhibit superior performance, their computational costs and resource requirements remain a concern. Future research will explore the development of lightweight models to accommodate large-scale application scenarios.

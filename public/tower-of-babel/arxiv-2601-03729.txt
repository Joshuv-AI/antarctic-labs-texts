MATANet: A Multi-context Attention and Taxonomy-Aware Network for Fine-Grained Underwater Recognition of Marine Species
Donghwan Lee, Byeongjin Kim, Geunhee Kim, Hyukjin Kwon, Nahyeon Maeng, Wooju Kim

Abstract
Fine-grained classification of marine animals supports ecology, biodiversity and habitat conservation, and evidence-based policy-making. However, existing methods often overlook contextual interactions from the surrounding environment and insufficiently incorporate the hierarchical structure of marine biological taxonomy. To address these challenges, we propose MATANet (Multi-context Attention and Taxonomy-Aware Network), a novel model designed for fine-grained marine species classification. MATANet mimics expert strategies by using taxonomy and environmental context to interpret ambiguous features of underwater animals. It consists of two key components: a Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (MCEAM), which learns relationships between regions of interest (ROIs) and their surrounding environments, and a Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module (HSLM), which encodes taxonomic hierarchy into the feature space. MATANet combines instance and environmental features with taxonomic structure to enhance fine-grained classification. Experiments on the FathomNet2025, FAIR1M, and LifeCLEF2015-Fish datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance. The source code is available at: https://github.com/dhlee-work/fathomnet-cvpr2025-ssl

Despite recent progress, existing studies on marine species classification generally rely solely on regions of interest (ROIs), such as the Hormathiidae ROI image shown in Figure

[

However, such contextual information is typically overlooked in conventional marine species classification studies. These methodological and imaging limitations hinder accurate species-level recognition. Furthermore, most existing studies lack consideration of hierarchical taxonomic structures, which can lead to confusion between semantically distant species during species-level prediction, thereby compromising the consistency of model outputs.

In this study, we propose MATANet (Multi-context Attention and Taxonomy-Aware Network), a novel architecture designed for fine-grained classification of marine species. The design of MATANet is inspired by how experts identify marine organisms, not only by observing the target species itself, but also by leveraging hierarchical taxonomic knowledge and interpreting features in relation to the surrounding habitat context. MATANet consists of two key components, the Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (MCEAM) and the Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module (HSLM). MCEAM learns attention-based interactions between ROIs and their surrounding regions, enabling the model to integrate both object and contextual environmental features. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first approach in marine species classification that integrates the surrounding habitat into the recognition process, and by modeling ROI–context interactions, the module enables more accurate and consistent classification by focusing on relevant contextual information. As an auxiliary module, HSLM builds level-wise local classifiers to embed the hierarchical structure of marine taxonomy into the representation space produced by MCEAM. By learning each taxonomic level independently, HSLM promotes flexible representation learning by disentangling level-specific features, thereby enhancing taxonomic consistency. This design enables HSLM to outperform alternative hierarchical representation learning methods in auxiliary tasks. As a results, MATANet achieves state-of-the-art results on FathomNet2025

, FAIR1M

[
6
]
at the CVPR 2025 FGVC12 workshop. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

We introduce a Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (MCEAM) that addresses the limitations of ROI-centric approaches by integrating marine animals and their surrounding ecological context.

•

We propose a Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module (HSLM) that embeds hierarchical structures into the representation space through level-wise supervision, improving semantic consistency and classification performance.

•

The proposed MATANet achieves SOTA performance on the FathomNet2025, FAIR1M, and LifeCLEF2015-Fish datasets, outperforming the publicly reported benchmark score of the FathomNet 2025 Challenge at the CVPR 2025 FGVC12 workshop.

•

Our method provides a practical contribution toward automated fine-grained monitoring of marine biodiversity, with a focus on underwater ecosystems.

Related Work

2.1
Marine Species Fine-grained Classification

Following the early work of Alsmadi et al.
[
1
]
on fish classification using deep neural networks, most initial approaches have adopted CNN-based architectures. One representative study is that of Qin et al.
[
25
]
, who proposed a deep learning framework combining CNNs and linear SVMs to classify 23 fish species. In recent years, research has shifted toward Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC) to better capture subtle interspecies differences. For example, Si et al.
[
28
]
introduced the Multi-Scale Dual-Branch Network (MSDBN), which integrates the strengths of Vision Transformers (ViT) and CNNs. This model incorporates a progressive selection module and self-attention mechanisms to effectively extract fine-grained features of marine animals. Similarly, Ergun
[
7
]
proposed SwinFishNet, a Swin Transformer-based model trained via transfer learning, which achieved high classification accuracy across various fish species datasets, underscoring the potential of transformer architectures in fine-grained fish species classification.

2.2
Multi-Stream Networks

The proposed model adopts a multi-stream network architecture, which processes input data through parallel networks and fuses the representations for target prediction. For example, Liu et al.
[
17
]
separated the ROI and background image into independent networks and fused their representation via an MLP. Ma et al.
[
18
]
enhanced disease classification by introducing a cross-attention-based fusion of representations from two identical image encoders. Kosaraju et al.
[
14
]
proposed Deep-Hipo for whole slide image (WSI) classification, leveraging multi-scale inputs to capture complementary information. High magnification provides cellular-level detail, while low magnification captures tissue-level structure, leading to more precise classification. Enhancing earlier methods, our model extracts multi-level contextual region representations centered around the ROI and learns attention-based interactions between these regions and the marine organism itself. This design allows MATANet to achieve superior performance on fine-grained marine species classification tasks by effectively leveraging ecological context.

2.3
Hierarchical Auxiliary Classification

Various methods have been developed to incorporate label hierarchies into the learned feature representations for classification tasks. In particular, as in the Local Classifier per Level approach
[
29
]
adopted in our study, there have been studies that attempted to learn hierarchical representations by employing auxiliary classifiers, despite the limitation of not explicitly modeling the hierarchical structure. Zhao et al. (2020)
[
39
]
extended fine-grained categories into a three-level hierarchy and trained level-specific classifiers, achieving competitive results in fine-grained visual classification. Similarly, Jin et al. (2025)
[
11
]
applied hierarchical auxiliary classification to the analysis of whole slide images, dividing the auxiliary classification task into coarse-grained cancer types and fine-grained cancer subtypes. Unlike most previous studies on the classification of marine species that overlooked the hierarchical structure of biological taxonomy, our work builds on these earlier efforts by introducing level-specific auxiliary classifiers. This design enables flexible representation learning across taxonomic levels, guiding MATANet’s embedding space toward semantically consistent hierarchical structures.

3
Method

3.1
Overview

Figure 2:
Overview of MATANet. The model processes ROI and multi-scale contextual images using ViT embeddings. The Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (MCEAM) applies cross-attention between the ROI and contextual regions to integrate their features. The Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module (HSLM) promotes taxonomic consistency through level-specific auxiliary classifiers.

we propose a novel model for marine species classification, named MATANet (Multi-context Attention and Taxonomy-Aware Network), which integrates hierarchical taxonomic knowledge with environmental context. As illustrated in Figure
2
, the model takes as input not only the region of interest (ROI) containing the target marine animal, but also contextual regions at 3×, 5×, and full-image scales, all center-cropped for the ROI. These regions reflect how experts consider organisms and their ecological context. We embed each input using the ViT encoder. The resulting embeddings are then processed by the Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (MCEAM), which models attention-based interactions between the marine animal and its surrounding environmental contexts. This enables the extraction of an integrated embedding that represents both object-level and habitat-level features. To further enforce semantic consistency aligned with biological taxonomy, During training, the Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module (HSLM) is applied to the embeddings produced by MCEAM. It employs local classifiers at each taxonomic level, guiding the learned embedding space to respect the structure of the marine taxonomy. Based on these embeddings, the final classifier in MATANet performs fine-grained classification by leveraging representations that combine both ecological context and taxonomic hierarchy.

3.2
Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module

Marine organisms exhibit strong ecological associations with their surrounding environments, and leveraging contextual information can enhance the reliability of fine-grained recognition. Motivated by this observation, we propose the Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (MCEAM), which learns the relationships between a marine animal and its surrounding environment. Before being processed by MCEAM, each input region is independently embedded using a Vision Transformer (ViT). Specifically, each ViT encoder extracts the [CLS] token embedding
g
from the marine animal ROI and the patch embeddings
p_{c_{r}}
from each context region
c_{r}
. In MCEAM, the ROI embedding
g
is used as the query, while the patch embeddings
p_{c_{r}}
from each context region serve as the keys and values for a cross-attention operation. This allows the model to learn interactions between the object and its surrounding environment. The outputs from the attention operations at each context level are then concatenated with the ROI embedding along the embedding dimension, and passed through a projection layer
Proj(\cdot)
to produce the final fused embedding
z
. The architecture of MCEAM is defined in Equation(
1
).

z=\text{Proj}\left(\text{Concat}\left(g,\left\{\text{Attn}(g,p_{c_{r}},p_{c_{r}})\mid c_{r}\in C\right\}\right)\right)

(1)

Where
C
denotes the set of context regions, and
C_{r}
represents the context region at scale
r
.
g
is the embedding of the ROI image, and
p_{C_{r}}
denotes the patch embeddings of
C_{r}
.
Attn(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the cross-attention operation, and
Proj(\cdot)
refers to a two-layer fully connected neural network.

3.3
Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module

Marine animals show different visual characteristics across taxonomic levels in biological taxonomy. Higher-level categories rely on global shape and morphology, whereas lower-level categories depend more on subtle, fine-grained features. This implies that effective classification requires semantic representations tailored to each hierarchical level. To address this, we propose the Hierarchical Separation-Induced Learning Module (HSLM), which enforces hierarchical consistency in model predictions through level-wise representation learning across the biological taxonomy. HSLM constructs a set of auxiliary classifiers corresponding to each level
\ell\in\mathcal{H}
of the taxonomic hierarchy (e.g., family, genus, order). For each level
\ell
, an individual auxiliary classifier
y^{\ell}
is attached, and the ground-truth label
y^{\ell}
at that level is used to compute the cross-entropy loss. These loss terms are formally defined in Equation(
2
).

\mathcal{L}_{hier}=\ \sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{H}}CE(f_{\ell}\ (z),\ y^{\ell})

(2)

Where
z
denotes the fused embedding obtained from the MCEAM module, and
\mathcal{H}
represents the set of hierarchical label structures. In particular, this study leverages the hierarchical taxonomy provided by the FathomNet API for training. When lower-level labels are missing, higher-level category labels are used as interpolated targets during training, allowing the model to perform robust hierarchical learning even in the presence of partially missing label structures.

3.4
Objective Function

The final classifier
f_{c}(\cdot)
takes the fused embedding
z
produced by MCEAM as input and outputs the predicted class. We use standard cross-entropy loss with the ground-truth label
y
, as defined in Equation(
3
):

\mathcal{L}_{cls}=\ CE(f_{c}(z),\ y)

(3)

The final objective combines classification loss and hierarchical loss
L_{hier}
, introduced in the Multi-Context Environmental Attention Module (see Equation
3
). The complete objective function is provided in Equation(
4
):

\mathcal{L}_{total}=\mathcal{L}_{cls}+\mathcal{L}_{hier}

(4)

This objective improves classification performance and semantic consistency by aligning embeddings with the marine taxonomy.

4
Experiments

4.1
Dataset

To evaluate the performance of the proposed model, we conduct classification experiments on three datasets, namely FathomNet2025
[
6
]
, FishCLEF2015
[
12
]
, and FAIR1M
[
32
]
. FathomNet2025 is a high-resolution underwater image subset from the FathomNet
[
13
]
database. Expert annotators labeled 79 marine species from 7 biological phyla across diverse habitats, including both class labels and bounding box-level ROIs. The training set contains 8,981 ecosystem images and 23,700 ROIs, while the test set includes 325 images and 788 ROIs. FishCLEF2015 is a subset of underwater videos with a resolution of 320×240, collected through the Fish4Knowledge project (www.fish4knowledge.eu). It contains species-level annotations for 15 fish species across 5 taxonomic orders inhabiting near-shore coral reef regions. We use only ROI-annotated samples for training and evaluation. The training set comprises 5,278 images and 9,160 ROIs, and the test set includes 9,339 images with 13,585 ROIs. FAIR1M v2.0 is a high-resolution satellite image dataset designed for fine-grained object detection. It contains 5 coarse-grained superclasses (e.g., aircraft, ship, vehicle) and 37 fine-grained subclasses. The training set contains 16,488 images with 396,615 ROIs, while the validation set consists of 8,287 images and 202,957 ROIs.

4.2
Evaluation Metrics

We evaluate model performance using Accuracy (ACC) and Hierarchical Distance (HD). The official evaluation metric of the FathomNet 2025 challenge
[
6
]
, HD, quantifies not only the accuracy of predictions but also their semantic consistency by measuring how closely the predicted and ground-truth labels align within the biological taxonomy hierarchy. HD assumes a unit distance between nodes in the taxonomy and is defined as the average shortest path length between the predicted label node
hat{y_{i}}
and the ground-truth label node
y_{i}
, as shown in Equation(
5
).

HierarchicalDistance=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}d(y_{i},\hat{y}_{i})

(5)

Where
N
denotes the total number of samples in the evaluation dataset, and
d(\cdot)
is a function that computes the shortest path between two nodes in the taxonomy. Since the test set of the FathomNet2025 dataset is not publicly available, evaluation was performed using the HD via the Kaggle platform of the FathomNet Challenge. In contrast, the FishCLEF2015 and FAIR1M datasets were internally evaluated using both ACC and HD.

4.3
Implementation Details

The proposed study was conducted on an Ubuntu 22.04 system equipped with an Intel Core i9-10980XE CPU, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX A100 GPU, and Python 3.10. The model was implemented using the PyTorch 2.6.0 deep learning framework. For training, object images were cropped into square regions centered on the bounding box, using the larger of the object’s width or height as the side length, and then resized to 256×256. Context images were generated by cropping square regions at 3×, 5×, and full-image scales centered on the object location and resizing them to 256×256, consistent with the object images. During training, data augmentation techniques such as HorizontalFlip, VerticalFlip, ColorJitter, and Rotation were applied. Embeddings for object and context regions were extracted using a separate DiNOv2 backbone. In the MCEAM module, cross-attention operations at each context level were performed using four attention blocks, each with 4-head multi-head attention. Both the projection layers and the classifiers were implemented as two-layer fully connected networks.

All benchmark models were trained under fair and reproducible conditions, with model-specific configurations carefully controlled for a balanced comparison. CNN-based models used pooled embeddings from convolutional blocks, while ViT-based models used [CLS] token embeddings. Both were trained with identical two-layer MLP classifiers. FGVC models used the default official settings, with the batch size and number of training steps adjusted according to hardware constraints. All models used the AdamW optimizer with fixed random seeds. MATANet was trained for 30 epochs with a batch size of 32. Learning rates were set to 1e-6 for FathomNet2025 and FAIR1M, and 1e-3 for FishCLEF2015. CNN and ViT baselines used learning rates of 1e-4 and 1e-5, respectively. Under these settings, all models except INTR showed convergence of the loss function on the training datasets. As noted by Mehrab et al. (2023)
[
21
]
, the performance of INTR
[
24
]
tends to degrade under imbalanced data conditions. Similarly, the FishCLEF2015 and FAIR1M datasets used in our experiments exhibit data imbalance, which likely hindered proper model fitting.

In contrast, Figures 3(a) and 3(b) fail to reflect this taxonomic structure. These results suggest that HSLM, with level-specific classifiers, effectively reflects the coarse-to-fine structure of biological taxonomy, enhancing the model’s ability to learn inter-species hierarchical relationships. This leads to semantically coherent representations where taxonomically similar species are embedded closer together and dissimilar ones are positioned farther apart, potentially improving overall classification accuracy.

5
Conclusions

MATANet (Multi-context Attention and Taxonomy Aware Network) is a proposed architecture for fine-grained classification of marine animals. It integrates two components. MCEAM, which learns interactions between marine animals and their ecological context, and HSLM, which encodes taxonomic hierarchy. By combining contextual and taxonomic information, MATANet aims to improve classification accuracy while maintaining semantic consistency. Experiments on the FathomNet2025, FAIR1M, and LifeCLEF2015 Fish datasets demonstrate state-of-the-art performance. Visual analysis of the learned feature representations suggests that the model performs structured reasoning aligned with taxonomic and ecological information. By jointly considering taxonomic hierarchy and habitat context, MATANet presents a promising direction for future research in automated marine species recognition and biodiversity monitoring.

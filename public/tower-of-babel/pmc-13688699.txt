Prognostic Significance of Response Dynamics in Atezolizumab plus Bevacizumab-Treated Hepatocellular Carcinoma: A Multicenter Real-World Validation Study
Authors: Renguo Guan, Kaijun Huang, Zehao Zheng, Zhen Zhao, Meishi Tang, Haosheng Jin, Jie Mei, Ye Lin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRadiological response dynamics may provide prognostic information beyond conventional response categories in patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) receiving atezolizumab plus bevacizumab. Building upon findings from the IMbrave150 trial, we evaluated the prognostic significance of depth of response (DpR), duration of response (DoR), and time to response (TTR) in a real-world cohort.MethodsThis retrospective multicenter study included 183 treatment-naïve patients with unresectable HCC treated with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab between November 2020 and September 2024. Tumor response was assessed according to mRECIST. Associations of DpR, DoR, and TTR with overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were evaluated using landmark analyses and time-dependent Cox analyses.ResultsDeeper tumor response, longer DoR, and ≥50% reductions in serologic biomarker correlated with improved survival. Patients who achieved an early response (≤2 months) tended to have less favorable survival outcomes than those with a later response. Multivariate analysis confirmed DoR (HR = 0.40; 95% CI: 0.30–0.55; p < 0.001) and DpR (HR = 0.45; 95% CI: 0.38–0.55; p < 0.001) as independent predictors for improved OS, while early response independently predicted worse OS (HR = 1.64; 95% CI: 1.26–2.15; p < 0.001). Similar trends were observed for PFS. Exploratory analyses suggested a potential association between conversion surgery and prolonged OS.ConclusionDpR and DoR were independently associated with survival outcomes in patients with unresectable HCC receiving atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, extending and validating previous findings from IMbrave150 in a real-world setting. The prognostic significance of TTR and conversion surgery warrants further prospective investigation.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, Depth of response, Duration of response, Prognosis

Introduction

Liver cancer, a globally significant malignancy, is the second most frequent cause of cancer-related mortality in China, accounting for >50% of worldwide incident cases annually [1]. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the predominant histopathological subtype of primary liver malignancy, accounting for >75% of global cases [2]. The characteristically insidious symptomatology and rapid disease progression result in over 70% of patients presenting with intermediate-to-advanced stage disease at initial diagnosis, which exhibits a particularly dismal prognosis with a 5-year overall survival (OS) rate of less than 20% [3]. Systemic therapy is the established standard treatment for patients with initially unresectable intermediate-advanced HCC. Contemporary international guidelines (including those from AASLD, EASL, and APASL) uniformly endorse first-line therapeutic regimens comprising atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, durvalumab plus tremelimumab, lenvatinib, and sorafenib [4–6]. The pivotal phase III IMbrave150 trial definitively established atezolizumab plus bevacizumab combination therapy as the global first-line standard for unresectable HCC, demonstrating statistically significant and clinically relevant improvements across multiple endpoints [7].

Advances in prognostic research on the treatment response to atezolizumab plus bevacizumab encompass multiple dimensions, including radiographic assessment and biomarkers. Regarding biomarkers, a post-treatment decline in α-fetoprotein (AFP) ≥50% is significantly associated with prolonged patient survival, and baseline neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio have also been validated as prognostic markers [8–10]. Radiographic tumor response is increasingly recognized as a clinically meaningful surrogate marker of treatment benefit in HCC. Recently, Kudo et al. reported a post hoc analysis of the phase III IMbrave150 trial demonstrating that depth of response (DpR) and duration of response (DoR) were significantly associated with OS in patients treated with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab [11]. These findings provided important evidence supporting the prognostic value of response dynamics and established the conceptual foundation for subsequent investigations in this field. However, several important questions remain unanswered. First, because IMbrave150 was conducted in a highly selected clinical trial population, the applicability of these findings to routine clinical practice requires further validation. Second, while DpR and DoR have been investigated in prospective studies, the prognostic significance of time to response (TTR) has not been fully characterized. Finally, serum biomarkers, particularly AFP and protein induced by vitamin K absence or antagonist-II (PIVKA-II), are widely used in HCC management, yet the relationship between dynamic biomarker responses, radiographic response characteristics, and survival outcomes remains incompletely understood.

Therefore, building upon the pioneering work of Kudo et al., the present study was designed as a retrospective real-world validation and extension study. Using a multicenter cohort of patients with initially unresectable HCC who received first-line atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, we evaluated the associations of radiographic response dynamics, including DpR, DoR, and TTR, with OS and progression-free survival (PFS). In addition, we investigated the prognostic value of dynamic AFP and PIVKA-II responses and conversion surgery.

Methods

Study Design and Patients

This study enrolled 457 treatment-naïve unresectable patients with HCC who received atezolizumab plus bevacizumab combination therapy at Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital and Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center between November 2020 and September 2024. Initial unresectability included both technically unresectable cases (e.g., insufficient future liver remnant [FLR], major vascular invasion, or unfavorable tumor location) and oncologically unresectable cases, in accordance with institutional practice and contemporary Chinese expert consensus recommendations [12]. This study enrolled treatment-naïve unresectable HCC patients who met the following inclusion criteria: (1) histopathologically confirmed diagnosis or radiological diagnosis by Asia-Pacific Clinical Practice Guidelines on the management of HCC [13]; (2) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0–2; (3) preserved liver function (Child-Pugh class A or B); (4) completion of ≥2 cycles of atezolizumab-bevacizumab combination therapy; and (5) presence of ≥1 measurable lesion according to the mRECIST criteria. Key exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) absence of post-baseline radiographic response assessment, (2) concurrent active malignancies, (3) high-risk esophageal varices (≥5 mm in diameter) without endoscopic prophylaxis, and (4) incomplete clinical documentation or premature follow-up discontinuation.

Treatment Protocol

Treatment consisted of atezolizumab 1,200 mg (fixed dose) and bevacizumab 15 mg/kg (actual body weight), both administered by intravenous infusion over 60 min on day 1 of each 21-day cycle until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. The bevacizumab dose was capped at 1,200 mg for patients weighing >80 kg. Tumor response was evaluated by contrast-enhanced CT/MRI after every two treatment cycles (q6w) based on the multidisciplinary team consensus. For patients achieving tumor downstaging: (1) conversion hepatectomy was considered when the FLR volume exceeded 40% (or 50% in cirrhotic patients). Conversion surgery was defined as curative-intent hepatic resection performed after successful tumor downstaging or improvement in resectability achieved during atezolizumab plus bevacizumab therapy in patients who were considered initially unresectable at baseline according to multidisciplinary team evaluation. Patients who were considered eligible for curative resection at treatment initiation were not classified as conversion surgery cases. (2) Those with insufficient FLR continued atezolizumab-bevacizumab maintenance therapy. Patients without downstaging were managed as follows: (1) those with progressive disease or grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events (CTCAE v5.0) received multidisciplinary team-guided second-line therapy; (2) others maintained the original regimen until the protocol-specified endpoints. The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the sum of the percentages of patients achieving complete response (CR) and partial response (PR). The disease control rate was defined as the sum of the ORR and percentage of patients achieving stable disease, all assessed according to mRECIST. Treatment-related adverse events were monitored throughout the course of treatment.

Data Collection and Outcomes

The collected clinical data for patients included (1) basic demographic information (sex and age at initial diagnosis); (2) laboratory findings including AFP, PIVKA-II, hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection, complete blood count, liver function parameters, coagulation profile, and history of esophageal varices; (3) treatment details, specifically the number of cycles of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab therapy administered; and (4) imaging characteristics, including maximum tumor diameter, number of tumor lesions, presence or absence of macrovascular invasion, classification of tumor thrombus, distant metastasis status (defined as lymph nodes with a short-axis diameter ≥15 mm), and radiological evaluation according to the mRECIST following each treatment course.

The primary endpoints of this study were OS and PFS. OS was calculated from the date of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab therapy initiation to the date of death from any cause or last follow-up, whichever occurred first. PFS was defined as the interval from the date of initiation of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab therapy to the date of documented disease progression or death from any cause.

The DpR was defined as the maximum percentage reduction in the sum of the longest diameters of the target lesions relative to baseline, assessed using the mRECIST criteria. For DpR, the patients were stratified into groups D1 (100%–60%), D2 (60%–30%), D3 (<30% to ≥0%), D4 (0% to −20%), and D5 (≤ −20%).

The DoR was calculated as the time interval from the first documented CR or PR to disease progression or death from any cause. For DoR, the subgroups were as follows: T1 (≥12 months), T2 (<12 months to ≥6 months), T3 (<6 months), and T4 (no response [0 months]).

The TTR was measured from atezolizumab plus bevacizumab initiation until the first radiologically confirmed CR or PR. For TTR, the subgroups were as follows: Group R1 (≥4 months), Group R2 (<4 months to ≥2 months), Group R3 (<2 months), and Group R4 (no response [0 months]).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted between July 1, 2025, and July 15, 2025. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation if normally distributed; otherwise, median and interquartile ranges are reported. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies (percentages). To minimize potential time-dependent bias, particularly immortal-time bias, a landmark analysis was performed as a sensitivity analysis [14]. A predefined landmark time point (3/6/12 months after treatment initiation) was selected a priori. Only patients who were alive and event-free at the landmark time were included in the analysis. The landmark time was then defined as time zero, and survival outcomes from this point onward were evaluated. OS and PFS beyond the landmark were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and differences between groups were assessed using the log-rank test. Given the potential collinearity among DoR, duration of progression, DpR, and TTR, these imaging-derived variables were not included simultaneously in the same multivariable model. Instead, to avoid multicollinearity and model instability, we performed separate multivariable time-dependent Cox proportional hazards analyses for each of these three variables in combination with other relevant clinical covariates. To reduce potential confounding bias and further strengthen the robustness of the results, propensity score matching (PSM) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) analyses were performed to evaluate the association between conversion surgery and patient prognosis [15, 16]. Survival outcomes were subsequently compared between groups using Kaplan-Meier analysis and Cox proportional hazards models in the matched and weighted cohorts. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 27.0, IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA) and R software (version 4.5.1, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p value of <0.05.

Results

Patient Characteristics

From November 2020 to September 2024, consecutive treatment-naïve patients with HCC receiving atezolizumab plus bevacizumab were identified. After rigorous screening, 183 eligible patients were included in the primary cohort (shown in Fig. 1). The median patient age was 56 years. The patients were predominantly male (n = 166, 90.7%), with most having Child-Pugh Class A liver function (n = 173, 94.5%) and chronic hepatitis B virus infection (n = 160, 87.4%). Two-thirds of the patients (n = 139, 76.0%) were in BCLC stage C, and approximately 80% presented with extrahepatic metastases. The median number of atezolizumab plus bevacizumab cycles was 5. Esophageal varices were documented in only 8.7% of the patients (n = 16). The clinical characteristics of the patients and their therapies are summarized in Table 1.

Tumor Response and Dynamic Changes in AFP and PIVKA-II

The median survival time for the entire cohort population was 24 months, and the median PFS time was 11 months. According to the mRECIST assessments, the ORR was 56.2% and 89.1%, respectively. Temporal changes in tumor diameter are shown in online supplementary Figure 1 (for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/lic/admag002). The atezolizumab plus bevacizumab regimen demonstrated substantial clinical benefits. Objective tumor responses were observed, with durable responses (DoR ≥6 months) in 59 patients (32.2%), rapid responses (within 2 months of treatment initiation) in 42 patients (23.0%), and deep responses (≥60% tumor reduction) in 54 patients (29.5%) (shown in Fig. 2a). Significant biomarker responses were evidenced by ≥50% reduction from baseline in both AFP (132 patients, 72.1%) and PIVKA-II (112 patients, 61.2%) levels, indicating the robust biological activity of the treatment regimen (shown in Fig. 2b).

Association of DpR, DoR, and TTR with Survival Outcomes

Based on the DpR, the patients were divided into five subgroups (D1–D5). These subgroups effectively stratified the OS of patients without landmark analysis (p < 0.001). In the 3-month landmark analysis, the unstratified OS HR compared with group D5 was 0.03 (95% CI: 0.01–0.12) in group D1, 0.24 (95% CI: 0.10–0.58) in group D2, 0.52 (95% CI: 0.23–1.21) in group D3, and 1.31 (95% CI: 0.49–3.53) in group D4. Similar results were observed for OS in the primary cohort in the 6-month landmark analysis and 12-month landmark analysis (shown in Fig. 3a–d). The median PFS was not reached in Group D1, 11.0 months in Group D2, 7.0 months in Group D3, 5.0 months in Group D4, and 1 month in Group D5 without landmark analysis (p < 0.001). The same trends were observed in the 3-month landmark analysis, 6-month landmark analysis, and 12-month landmark analysis (shown in Fig. 3e–h).

We further evaluated the association between TTR and clinical outcomes in patients with HCC. In the conventional survival analysis, compared with non-responders (R4), all responder groups exhibited significantly improved OS, with unadjusted HRs of 0.14 (95% CI: 0.08–0.23) for R1, 0.17 (95% CI: 0.10–0.28) for R2, and 0.25 (95% CI: 0.15–0.42) for R3 (all p < 0.001). To account for potential guarantee-time bias, landmark analyses were subsequently performed at 3, 6, and 12 months. In these analyses, patients with a TTR ≥2 months tended to demonstrate longer OS than those with a TTR <2 months, and this pattern was consistently observed across all landmark time points (Fig. 5a–d). For PFS, the median durations were 19.0 months in R1, 21.0 months in R2, 13.0 months in R3, and 5.0 months in R4 (p < 0.001). In the 3-month landmark analysis, compared with R4, the HRs for PFS were 0.27 (95% CI: 0.14–0.55) in R1, 0.33 (95% CI: 0.19–0.59) in R2, and 0.42 (95% CI: 0.24–0.74) in R3. Similar trends were observed in the 6- and 12-month landmark analyses (Fig. 5e–h).

APF and PIVKA-II Reduction ≥50% Is Associated with Improved RFS and OS

OS and PFS were significantly longer in HCC patients with ≥50% reduction in AFP than in those with <50% reduction in AFP (OS: 13.0 months vs. 7.0 months, p < 0.001; PFS: 13.0 months vs. 7.0 months, p < 0.001) (shown in Fig. 6a, b). The study identified PIVKA-II reduction of <50% as a significant prognostic factor associated with substantially worse survival outcomes (OS: HR 2.46, 95% CI: 1.47–4.11; PFS: HR 1.89, 95% CI: 1.25–2.84; both p < 0.001) (shown in Fig. 6c, d). Patients failing to achieve ≥50% PIVKA-II decline had 146% higher mortality risk and 89% increased progression risk compared to responders.

Deep Tumor Response, Prolonged Response Durability, and Early Response as Independent Prognostic Factors for PFS and OS

Multivariate time-dependent Cox proportional hazards analyses were performed to identify independent prognostic factors for OS. We identified DoR (HR: 0.40; 95% CI: 0.30–0.55; p < 0.001), DpR (HR: 0.45; 95% CI: 0.38–0.55; p < 0.001), TTR (HR: 1.64; 95% CI: 1.26–2.15, p < 0.001), and extrahepatic metastasis (p < 0.05) as independent prognostic factors for OS (shown in Fig. 7; Tables 2–4).

For PFS, multivariate analysis demonstrated that both DoR (HR: 0.38; 95% CI: 0.30–0.46; p < 0.001) and DpR (HR: 0.45; 95% CI: 0.36–0.55; p < 0.001) were independently associated with improved PFS, whereas TTR was independently associated with worse PFS (HR: 1.54; 95% CI: 1.27–1.88; p < 0.001) (shown in Fig. 7; Tables 2–4).

Conversion Surgery Significantly Improves the Prognosis of Patients

In the present study, 34 patients with initially unresectable HCC achieved surgical conversion following atezolizumab plus bevacizumab therapy. The clinical characteristics between the conversion surgery group and the non-conversion surgery group were balanced using a 1:1 PSM and IPTW adjustment (online suppl. Table 1). Comparative survival analysis shown in online supplementary Figure 2 demonstrated that patients who underwent conversion surgery might be associated with longer OS, compared with those of the non-conversion surgery group in the primary cohort and IPTW cohort (p < 0.05). In the PSM cohort, no statistically significant difference in OS was observed between the conversion surgery and non-surgical groups. Nevertheless, the survival curves remained numerically separated, with patients undergoing conversion surgery showing longer OS than those who did not undergo conversion surgery.

Discussion

In this multicenter real-world cohort of patients with unresectable HCC treated with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, we validated and extended the findings of the exploratory analysis from the IMbrave150 trial by demonstrating that deeper tumor response and longer response durability were associated with improved survival outcomes. Consistent with previous observations, patients achieving greater DpR and prolonged DoR experienced superior OS and PFS. In time-dependent multivariable Cox analyses, both DpR and DoR remained independently associated with favorable survival outcomes. In contrast, shorter TTR was associated with worse OS and PFS; however, this finding should be interpreted cautiously as the underlying biological mechanisms remain uncertain and may be influenced by tumor heterogeneity, response assessment characteristics, and residual bias despite statistical adjustment. In addition, patients who underwent conversion surgery showed more favorable survival outcomes than those who did not undergo surgery, although the possibility of residual selection bias cannot be completely excluded.

Unlike RECIST-defined response categories, which may encompass substantial heterogeneity in tumor burden change, DpR quantifies the extent of tumor shrinkage, whereas DoR reflects the persistence of treatment benefit over time. Therefore, both metrics may better characterize the quality of response and the durability of disease control, explaining their close association with survival outcomes [17–20]. Unlike IMbrave150, which was a prospective randomized trial with strict eligibility criteria and standardized follow-up, our study represents a retrospective real-world cohort from two tertiary centers in China, with broader clinical heterogeneity, including ECOG performance status up to 2 and selected Child-Pugh B patients. Accordingly, the primary contribution of this study is external validation and extension of prior evidence rather than the introduction of a novel prognostic concept, while also providing complementary analyses incorporating dynamic serologic biomarkers (AFP and PIVKA-II), TTR, and conversion surgery outcomes to further support the clinical utility of radiographic response dynamics in routine practice.

Unexpectedly, an association was observed between rapid tumor response (<2 months) and poorer survival outcomes, with patients responding within <2 months experiencing worse prognosis than those whose responses occurred after >2 months. However, this finding should be interpreted cautiously as an exploratory association rather than a causal relationship. In immunotherapy-based treatment, the timing of radiological response is highly heterogeneous and may not directly reflect long-term survival benefit [21]. Previous studies have demonstrated that early responders did not necessarily achieve superior survival outcomes compared with late responders, highlighting the complex and time-dependent nature of antitumor immune responses [22]. Several factors may contribute to this observation. First, intrinsic tumor biological heterogeneity may result in transient treatment sensitivity, whereby tumors initially exhibit rapid shrinkage but subsequently acquire immune resistance and undergo aggressive progression [23]. Second, analyses based on TTR are subject to inherent methodological limitations as TTR is a time-dependent variable that may introduce guarantee-time and selection biases [24]. Furthermore, tumor response-related endpoints have shown only limited correlation with OS in immune checkpoint inhibitor trials [25]. Finally, the response assessment characteristics of mRECIST in the setting of anti-angiogenic therapy should also be considered. Because bevacizumab can rapidly reduce tumor vascularity and enhancement, some patients may achieve early radiographic responses that do not necessarily translate into durable disease control. Overall, while immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy may exhibit heterogeneous and sometimes delayed response patterns at a population level, this concept does not directly explain individual-level associations observed here. Therefore, these results should be considered hypothesis-generating and require validation in larger cohorts using appropriate time-dependent analytical methods.

After PSM analysis and IPTW analysis, we observed that a subset of patients in this study underwent conversion surgery after achieving favorable responses to systemic therapy and might have a better prognosis. These findings bear some resemblance to those of previous studies [26–28]. However, the observed association should still be interpreted cautiously in the present study. Patients selected for conversion surgery represent a highly selected population with favorable tumor biology, better treatment response, and preserved hepatic reserve, which may not be fully captured by measured covariates. In addition, time-dependent selection bias may persist as only patients who survive long enough and maintain sufficient response are eligible for surgery. Therefore, despite adjustment using PSM and IPTW, residual confounding cannot be excluded, and the observed survival benefit should be considered associative rather than causal. Furthermore, several studies have reported that, among patients with HCC rendered resectable after conversion therapy, a “watch-and-wait” strategy may yield survival outcomes comparable to those achieved with conversion surgery, with no significant differences in OS and PFS [29, 30]. In light of these findings, well-designed prospective studies are required to further elucidate the optimal role and timing of conversion surgery in patients who achieve resectability after conversion therapy.

We acknowledge several potential limitations of our study. First, we acknowledge that patients with very early disease progression, early mortality, or inadequate follow-up duration may have been underrepresented. Given its retrospective two-center design, the study carries potential selection bias, information bias, and residual confounding despite consecutive enrollment. Second, unlike the prospective randomized IMbrave150 trial, treatment, follow-up, and clinical decision-making reflected routine practice rather than a standardized protocol, which may have influenced outcome assessment. Third, about 90% of patients had HBV-related HCC, limiting generalizability to Western populations with different etiologies. Finally, as DpR, DoR, and TTR were assessed retrospectively, immortal-time and guarantee-time biases cannot be fully excluded. Prospective studies are needed to validate these findings.

In conclusion, our findings validate and extend prior evidence that depth and DoR are associated with survival in patients receiving atezolizumab plus bevacizumab. However, early response timing and conversion surgery findings should be interpreted cautiously due to potential biological heterogeneity and methodological biases. These results are hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation using time-dependent analytical frameworks.

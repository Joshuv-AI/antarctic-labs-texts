Inspection of power line insulator using an enhanced faster region-convolutional neural network
Authors: Parul Priya, Sushma S Kamlu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInspection of power line insulators is crucial to the safe and reliable operation of power transmission networks. Manual inspection techniques are deficient in terms of efficiency, accuracy, availability, security, and cost. To overcome these problems, this article proposes an improved version, Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net, which uses faster region-based convolutional neural network (Faster R-CNN) with some quality of feature integration and attention-based features to achieve consistent insulator defects detection in complicated scenes. The suggested architecture is an integration of a residual network backbone (ResNet-50) which extracts features, a bidirectional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) which learns fusion weights to achieve adaptive multiscale fusion of features and receptive field attention plus (RFA+) which optimises the features and improves the overall detection performance. The model also has an Improved Context Perception Module (I-CPM) that comes after the Region Proposal Network (RPN). Also employs dilated convolution to achieve better categorization and bounding-box regression. Additionally, Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) is utilized on the feature maps produced by the RFA+ module to illustrate defect-associated areas and create heatmaps that pinpoint defect evidence. This work is new because it successfully combines multi-scale feature fusion, attention mechanisms, and contextual modeling into one framework for strong insulator defect detection. The suggested method works better than the ones that are already out there. It has a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 98.03%, a precision of 99.05%, and a recall of 98.96% for finding faults.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Insulators are important in ensuring the safety and reliability of the transmission lines. They work as a shield against leakage of electrical current and offer mechanical support to high-voltage conductors1. The transmission line power systems are positioned outdoors commonly in fields, forests, mountains or highland regions. Over time, environmental stress can lead to defects such as cracks, contamination, or breakage, which may result in power outages, equipment failure, and significant economic losses. Therefore, accurate and timely inspection of insulators is critical for maintaining the reliability and safety of power transmission networks. Conventional inspection methods rely heavily on manual operations, which are time-consuming, labour-intensive, and pose safety risks to maintenance personnel2. To address these challenges, unmanned aerial systems (UAS) have been increasingly adopted for transmission line inspection due to their flexibility, wide-area coverage, and ability to access hard-to-reach locations3,4. The combination of UAS with computer vision and machine learning has enabled automated defect detection systems to become even more effective5. Even with these improvements, it is still hard to find insulator defects in aerial images. Defects often comprise a minor segment of the image and are readily influenced by intricate backdrops, occlusion, and fluctuating lighting conditions.6,7. Also, the fact that there isn’t much labeled defect data makes it even harder to train and generalise models. Deep learning-based object detection methods have shown promise, but they still have problems with finding small defects and staying strong in different environments8,9. Deep learning-based techniques have made a lot of progress, but they still have some issues. Most methods struggle to detect small flaws in complex backgrounds, such as when there are many objects in the way, the light changes, or there is a lot of clutter. A lot of the ways that are already out there only look at one improvement at a time, like attention mechanisms or feature fusion. They don’t do a good job of putting contextual modelling, attention refinement, and multi-scale representation all in one framework. Their inability to be easily integrated makes them less effective for real-life UAS checks where difficulties are small, diverse, and hidden in complicated settings10,11. This research offers an improved Faster R-CNN-based framework for insulator defect identification, termed Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net, to address these shortcomings. The proposed model incorporates a BiFPN for adaptive multi-scale feature fusion, an RFA+ module for feature refining, and an I-CPM for superior contextual representation. This integrated architecture facilitates precise identification of minor and partially obscured flaws in intricate backdrop scenarios.

Related work

Object detection is one of the core tasks in computer vision, which is locating and recognising items of interest in an image or video12. Based on deep learning target-detection techniques, the primary classes are single-stage and two-stage target identification algorithms. One-stage object identification techniques utilise deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) to perform localisation and classification directly. They can produce the target’s class probability and location coordinates in a single stage, without creating candidate regions. One-stage object detection techniques are frequently employed, such as the Single Shot Detector (SSD)13, RetinaNet14, and the You Only Look Once (YOLO) series15. Furthermore, two-stage target detection methods include cascade-CNN16, Faster R-CNN17, and R-CNN18. A cascade structure is used to construct a two-stage detection framework. Using a selective search algorithm, R-CNN generates a number of candidate regions with targets. It then normalises each candidate region’s scale, extracts feature vectors using convolutional neural networks, and classifies the results using trained support vector machines (SVMs). Candidate target region slices are continually saved during training for R-CNN’s detection process, which remains a multi-stage, multi-step procedure. Fashlt R-CNN integrates the desired region-of-interest (RoI) pooling layer with R-CNN, removes the SVM classifier and replaces it with a softmax layer, and trains the target’s box regression and classification procedures. The deeper convolutional neural networks provide input to each candidate target region through convolutional sharing which is a process, which not only boosts the speed of detecting but also reduces the storage space. The attention mechanisms, multi-scale feature representations and metric learning are usually used to optimise object detection19–21. It is categorized into two types: channel and spatial attention. Although channel attention (e.g., SENet, CBAM, Coordinate Attention) addresses the relationships between channels, the positional meaning of features is studied by spatial attention. These are the mechanisms of refining object, target, and detail recognition into feature maps by adding adaptive weights to them. However, their effectiveness decreases when the main structure already shows a high level of object focus. Multiscale methods (such as FPN, PAN, and DetectoRS) enhance detection performance by combining hierarchical features at multiple resolutions and enabling scale-adaptive prediction to match receptive fields to object sizes. Metric learning also produces detection models that are more robust to intra-class variation and exhibit greater inter-class separability in the feature space, making it especially useful in few-shot detection and object tracking tasks. These methods enhance the accuracy, robustness, and flexibility of detection models in complex real-world settings. Recent advancements in object detection have been enabled by the creation of the YOLOv8 architecture which has improved accuracy and computational efficiency in real-time use. Several studies have explored YOLOv8-based frameworks in aerial detection and defect identification tasks. An example is the use of YOLOv8 models in aerial rocket detection, which shows how lightweight architectures can be used to passive and cost-effective surveillance systems22. In the same way, the frameworks based on YOLOv8 have been explored in the case of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAS) detection, where various model sizes offer a trade-off between computation time and detection performance in practice23. Lightweight YOLOv8 architectures have demonstrated efficacious outcomes in industrial tasks of defect detection, like circuit board inspection, with high detection precision and reduced computational complexity24. YOLOv8 models have also been studied in the context of power systems, and in this case, the task has been to detect power line arcs, showing strong performance with the presence of faults in complicated environmental scenarios25. Moreover, recent research has reported the usefulness of lightweight computer vision models based on YOLOv8, which are useful in real-time classification and resource management systems due to their flexibility and performance in the real world of application26. With these developments, it is possible that YOLOv8-based methods will still struggle with identifying very small-scale defects and rich contextual information, especially in chaotic aerial scenes. These constraints show that we need more extensive frameworks that combine multi-scale feature fusion, attention mechanisms, and contextual modeling. This is why we are proposing the approach in this article.

Methodology

In this artical presents an Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net for detecting insulator defects. The model architecture is based on Faster R-CNN17, with enhancements to feature integration and attention-based mechanisms to ensure stable detection in dense scenes. It also utilises a Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN)37, which enables dynamic multi-scale feature fusion to further improve the localisation of small defects in a fine-grained manner. The proposed architecture integrates a Receptive Field Attention+ (RFA+) module with an improved feature fusion mechanism that combines Channel Attention (CAM) and Spatial Attention (SAM) to selectively enhance informative features while suppressing background noise. This two-headed attention mechanism strengthens both the semantic richness and spatial saliency of the fused features. Moreover, an improved Context Perception Module (I-CPM) is introduced after the region proposal phase to aggregate surrounding contextual information via dilated convolutions, thereby improving the accuracy of bounding-box regression and classification.

Figure 1 shows the detection pipeline of the proposed Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net. First, the original image will be enhanced with elements of the ResNet-50 backbone10 to acquire hierarchical features. These are later combined using BiFPN and then cleansed with the RFA+ block. The resulting feature maps are then fed into a Region Proposal Network (RPN)18 to generate candidate regions. Lastly, the CPM-enhanced detection head performs classification and defect localisation most accurately. Through this multi-stage architecture, the proposed method can effectively pay attention to both small-scale details and large-scale context, enabling it to achieve a remarkable increase in the robustness of insulator defect detection, even in complex environments.

Faster region-convolutional neural network

In this work, Faster R-CNN is taken as the baseline model. It is a two-stage object identification architecture that uses a single trainable pipeline to perform (a) region proposal generation and (b) region identification and bounding-box refinement. Figure 2 presents the architecture of the Faster R-CNN. Faster R-CNN is an end-to-end trainable architecture that combines object identification with region proposal formation. In contrast to previous region-based detectors that rely on external proposal methods, Faster R-CNN uses a Region Proposal Network (RPN) that shares convolutional layers with the detection head. While increasing detection accuracy and efficiency, this shared-feature architecture significantly reduces computational overhead.

Evaluation indicators

The evaluation indices of the proposed algorithm in this article, based on the features of the target identification task, are as follows: precision, recall, mean average precision, F1 score, and IoU. Consequently, the P–R curve is generated by plotting precision on the y-axis and recall on the x-axis. The area under the curve represents the Average Precision (AP) for the given category.

The average AP score for each class is referred to as the mean Average Precision (mAP). In this study, the number of classes is two, corresponding to normal and defective insulators.

The mean Average Precision (mAP) represents the overall object detection capability of the model across all categories. A higher mAP indicates better classification and localization performance. In this work, mAP is used as the primary metric for evaluating and comparing the accuracy of different detection networks.

These results demonstrate that the integration of BiFPN, RFA+, and I-CPM effectively enhances multi-scale representation, suppresses background interference, and improves the localization of small insulator defects. Therefore, the proposed framework provides a more accurate, robust, and reliable solution for insulator defect detection in complex real-world UAS inspection scenarios.

Even though the suggested model has quantitative performance, there are still some failure cases that are hard to inspect. In particular, false negatives can happen when defect areas are very small, partly blocked by transmission-line structures, or captured in low light, which makes it harder to see features that are unique to defects. Similarly, false positives can occur in complex scenes where structural elements, shadows, or background patterns closely resemble the characteristics of insulator defects. These observations indicate that the suggested framework demonstrates robust performance across a majority of practical scenarios; however, its defect identification capabilities remain susceptible to factors such as occlusion, background similarity, reduced target dimensions, and challenging illumination conditions. So, future work will focus on making it more robust by adding more difficult data-augmentation techniques, like simulating occlusion and changing the lighting, as well as better attention systems and context-aware feature learning to better tell the difference between real defect areas and background noise.

Implication and discussion of integrated framework

The proposed framework combines BiFPN, RFA+ and I-CPM in a coordinated and sequential way where the output of each stage is interacted with the output of the next step to improve the quality of features in a progressive way. In particular, BiFPN initially combines multi-scale features that are obtained using the backbone network, which makes it possible to represent defects at various resolutions. BiFPN output is then forwarded to the RFA+ module, which uses attention mechanisms to refine the combined features by highlighting defect-relevant areas and downplaying the noise. These polished features are then processed by the Region Proposal Network(RPN) to produce more precise candidate regions. Once RoI is aligned, the I-CPM module takes into account contextual information on the region level that enhances classification confidence and localisation accuracy by taking into account the surrounding structural information of defects. The proposed Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net has its architecture pipeline depicted in Fig. 1. The sequence of these components is vital to the overall performance. The initial feature fusion is done in multi-scales such that minor defects are well-perceived. Attention-based refinement is then used to pay attention to the most pertinent features in the fused representations. Lastly, the contextual modelling is presented at the regional level to improve decision-making. The sequential interaction allows the model to gradually advance in feature representation to detect small and complex defects, particularly in challenging environments. In spite of the better results of the proposed model, there are a few limitations that should be mentioned.

From a practical perspective, the practical implications as represented in Fig. 14 can support UAS-based inspection by automatically analysing aerial images of transmission lines and accurately localizing insulator defects under complex background conditions. This reduces manual effort by minimizing the need for operators to visually inspect large numbers of UAS images one by one, thereby improving inspection efficiency and reducing labour-intensive field work. In addition, the model can assist in early defect detection by identifying small and subtle defect patterns before they develop into serious failures. Therefore, the framework can be effectively applied in transmission line monitoring, preventive maintenance, and fault screening. However, certain practical limitations remain, including higher computational complexity, challenges in direct deployment on edge/UAS hardware, and sensitivity under difficult conditions such as extreme lighting, partial occlusion, and visually complex backgrounds

Experiment 1 corresponds to the baseline Faster R-CNN, achieving 97.18% precision, 96.01% recall, 96.59% F1-score, and 96.20% mAP, with an inference speed of 25.10 FPS. This baseline serves as the reference for measuring subsequent improvements. In Experiment 2, the BiFPN module was introduced to enhance multi-scale feature fusion. This resulted in an mAP improvement of +1.45% and increased recall from 96.01% to 97.01% (+1.00%). These results confirm that bidirectional fusion improves defect representation across multiple resolutions, which is particularly important for UAS-acquired imagery.

In Experiment 3, the RFA module was added after BiFPN to enhance receptive-field modelling. Compared to Experiment 2, precision and recall increased by 0.71% and 0.88%, respectively, while the F1-score improved by 0.80%. This indicates that receptive-field enhancement provides a better balance between precision and recall by strengthening contextual defect cues.

Experiment 4 replaced RFA with the proposed enhanced RFA+ module, yielding further gains. Precision increased by 0.75%, while recall increased from 97.89% to 98.09% (+0.20%), resulting in an F1-score increase from 98.01% to 98.48% (+0.47%). These improvements suggest that the attention-driven refinement in RFA+ enhances feature discriminability by suppressing cluttered background responses and amplifying defect-relevant activations.

In Experiment 5, the Context Perception Module (CPM) was incorporated into the detection head. Compared with the previous configuration, the mAP increased marginally from 97.86% to 97.96% (+0.10%), while precision slightly decreased from 98.87% to 98.65% (-0.12%). This behavior is expected because CPM mainly focuses on contextual aggregation to improve localisation stability and robustness rather than producing large gains in precision.

Finally, Experiment 6 corresponds to the full proposed framework, where CPM is replaced with the Improved Context Perception Module (I-CPM) based on a dilated pyramid and residual shortcut connection. This final configuration achieves the best overall performance with 99.05% precision, 98.96% recall, 99.00% F1-score, and 98.03% mAP. Relative to Experiment 5, the proposed dilated pyramid context modelling improves precision, recall, and mAP by +0.40%, +0.83%, and +0.06%, respectively. Overall, compared to the baseline Faster R-CNN, the final model achieves improvements of +1.87% precision, +2.95% recall, +2.41% F1-score, and +1.89% mAP.

Moreover, the proposed method reduces the number of parameters and FLOPs to 3.50M and 39G, respectively, compared to 41.30M and 185G for the baseline. As expected, the inference speed decreases from 25.10 FPS to 17.60 FPS; however, the final model remains practical (56.82 ms/image) while delivering substantially improved detection performance, making it suitable for UAS-based insulator monitoring.

Although the proposed Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net achieves superior detection accuracy, the integration of BiFPN, RFA+, and I-CPM introduces additional computational complexity compared with the baseline Faster R-CNN. As shown in Table 7, the proposed model contains 44.80M parameters and requires 224G FLOPs, while achieving an inference speed of 17.60 FPS corresponding to 56.82 ms per image. Although this is lower than the baseline Faster R-CNN, the obtained speed is still suitable for near-real-time UAS inspection in practical monitoring scenarios. The proposed architecture is primarily designed to prioritize high detection accuracy and robustness under complex inspection conditions, including small defects, cluttered backgrounds, and varying viewpoints. Nevertheless, for deployment on resource-constrained UAS edge devices, further optimization is necessary. Therefore, lightweight deployment strategies such as model pruning, quantization, and hardware-aware acceleration on embedded platforms can be explored in future work to improve inference efficiency while preserving detection performance.

Limitations of the proposed framework

Despite the strong performance of the proposed model, several limitations should be noted. The model is initially tested on two datasets (CPLID and STN-PLAD) but additional testing on larger and more diverse datasets is needed to be able to test its generalization ability fully. Second, even with the model, there are still problems with identifying defects in extreme conditions, including extreme occlusion, tiny defect areas, low contrast, and complicated backgrounds. Third, the model can be computationally complex, so it might not be able to be directly deployed to resource-constrained edge devices, and it would need additional optimization to support real-time applications. Lastly, differences in quality of images and environmental factors could influence performance under real-world conditions. These restrictions offer a clear guideline on how the proposed system should be improved in the future and actually be implemented.

Conclusions

We propose an improvement over Faster R-CNN dubbed Enhanced Faster R-CNN I2D-Net for insulator defect detection with aerial images from UAS. The proposed architecture achieves state-of-the-art performance by incorporating ResNet-50 as a feature extraction backbone, BiFPN as an adaptive multi-scale feature fusion module, and RFA+ for feature refinement. Furthermore, to allow models to better concentrate on defect regions in complex background conditions, mechanisms of channel and spatial attention are added. With the aim of improving the detection performance of small-sized and partially occluded defects, we integrated an Improved Context Perception Module (I-CPM) into the detection head to boost the classification confidence and bounding-box regression. The framework is supported by Grad-CAM, which overlays heat-maps on the defect-related regions to better interpret the model and improve the explainability. Specifically, the proposed approach reached a mAP of 98.03% with a precision and recall score of 99.05% and 98.96%, respectively, when using the CPLID dataset for evaluation. To assess the model’s ability to generalize, it was also evaluated on two additional datasets, CPLID and STN-PLAD. These datasets included a variety of shooting conditions, environmental factors, viewpoints, and defect characteristics. The proposed method improved on baseline Faster R-CNN across both datasets for precision, recall, and mAP, with an mAP increase of 1.83% on CPLID and 2.80% on STN-PLAD. The gains in precision and recall further strengthen the robustness, reliability, and improved detection capabilities of the proposed framework in various inspection situations. Also, we conducted a ablative analysis and ablation studies to prove that the positive effect brought by each module. Despite these gains, the dataset specific variables such as image resolution, nature of defects and environmental factors may have an impact on performance. Future work will thus involve more cross-dataset validation on other power-grid datasets that have been recorded on a variety of imaging platforms and varying environmental conditions to further enhance generalization. The domain adaptation and multimodal learning strategies will also be taken into account to reduce the bias of the dataset and make it stronger in terms of its implementation in practice. Also, the design of lightweight models will be explored to enable real-time implementation on UAS and edge devices. Whereas the proposed model has a better detection accuracy, it is more computationally complex and slower than the baseline in inference. Nevertheless, the obtained 17.60 FPS is still appropriate to conduct near-real-time UAS inspection. Further research will explore lightweight optimization techniques, such as pruning, quantization, and embedded hardware acceleration, to enhance the efficiency of edge deployment. Future research will also focus on identifying additional categories of insulator faults and improving detection under adverse conditions such as severe occlusion, low contrast, and extreme weather. The suggested approach offers an efficient and feasible solution to automated inspection in a smart grid, as well as a valuable source of information to create intelligent and reliable power-grid monitoring systems.

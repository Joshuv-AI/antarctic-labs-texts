Enhanced prognostic score for breast cancer brain metastases incorporating contemporary clinical features and leptomeningeal disease: Development and validation
Authors: Yun Hwa Roh, Dongyeong Kim, Ho Sung Kim, Seo Young Park, Shinkyo Yoon, Jae Ho Jeong, Sang Woo Song, Young-Hoon Kim, Young Hyun Cho, Beomseok Sohn, Ji Eun Park
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposePrognosis of breast cancer brain metastases (BCBM) remains heterogeneous. The current Breast-Graded Prognostic Assessment (GPA) lacks leptomeningeal disease (LMD) or contemporary clinical features. We aimed to develop and externally validate an enhanced prognostic scoring system integrating clinical factors, cancer subtypes, and LMD.MethodsIn this retrospective multi-institutional study, 207 patients with newly diagnosed BCBM treated with stereotactic radiosurgery between 2015 and 2021 formed the development cohort. 240 patients formed the external validation cohort. Clinical variables, GPA factors, and magnetic resonance imaging–based features including number of metastases, volumetric tumor burden, and LMD presence were analyzed. Univariable and multivariable Cox regression identified independent predictors for overall survival (OS). A weighted point-based scoring system (enhanced GPA, eGPA) was constructed and compared with the GPA using Harrell's C-index and time-dependent area under the curve.ResultsMedian OS in the development cohort was 13.9 months. Multivariable analysis identified Karnofsky performance status, breast cancer subtype, number of brain metastases, presence of extracranial metastasis, and LMD as independent prognostic factors (all P < .05). Total metastatic volume was not independently associated with OS. The eGPA demonstrated superior discrimination compared with GPA in the development cohort (C-index 0.64 vs. 0.60, P = .009) and comparable performance in the external validation cohort (0.65 vs. 0.66, P = .690). One-year AUC was higher for eGPA (0.71 vs. 0.65, P = .014). The inclusion of LMD provided meaningful risk stratification across subtypes.ConclusionAn enhanced GPA incorporating LMD and contemporary clinical factors improves risk assessment in BCBM and may better guide therapeutic decision-making prior to radiation therapy.

Introduction

Prognosis in breast cancer brain metastases (BCBM) is highly variable, with reported median overall survival (OS) ranging from 4 to 27 months [1,2]. This heterogeneity in outcomes reflects differences in breast cancer subtype, performance status, extent of systemic disease, and treatment approaches [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Breast cancer subtype is one of the strongest prognostic factors: Patients with hormone receptor-negative and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive subtype display the longest median survival, whereas patients with a triple-negative subtype have the poorest outcomes [2,4,5]. To integrate multiple prognostic factors, the diagnosis-specific Graded Prognostic Assessment (GPA) was developed and validated using a multi-institutional database of 2473 patients with BCBM [6]. The Breast-GPA stratifies survival based on age, Karnofsky performance status (KPS), and cancer subtype, with the recent addition of number of brain metastases (>3 vs ≤ 3) and presence of extracranial metastases.

Several important considerations suggest the need to update the current Breast-GPA model. First, patients with leptomeningeal disease (LMD) have been historically excluded from the studies on prognosis. However, in real-world clinical practice, these patients are not excluded from therapy, and treatments such as whole-brain radiation therapy or stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) are increasingly being utilized [7]. Second, SRS has become a more widely adopted therapeutic approach even in patients with multiple metastases, and recent practice guidelines conditionally recommend it for patients with up to 10 lesions [8,9]. Third, current prognostic models depend exclusively on the number of brain metastases and do not incorporate additional imaging characteristics. Recent advances in deep learning–based segmentation enable accurate volumetric analysis of metastatic burden [10], allowing the incorporation of quantitative imaging biomarkers into prognostic assessment. The above considerations support the need to refine and update existing prognostic models [5], particularly to better guide individualized treatment strategies in BCBM.

In this study, we aimed to develop an enhanced prognostic model incorporating established variables from the Breast-GPA while exploring additional clinical and imaging features with potential prognostic value. The model was validated using an independent external cohort, and its performance was compared with that of the current Breast-GPA. The purpose of this study was to develop and validate an enhanced, clinically relevant prognostic model for patients with BCBM.

Methods

Patients

This study was approved by the institutional review board of Asan Medical Center (approval number: S2023-0727-0002) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The requirement for written informed consent was waived by the board due to the retrospective nature of the study. The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed breast cancer; (2) clinically or pathologically confirmed newly diagnosed brain metastases from breast cancer; (3) treatment with SRS for brain metastases between January 2015 and December 2021; and (4) available brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) with contrast enhancement before radiation therapy. Patients were excluded if they had one of the following: (1) a history of prior surgery, radiosurgery, or radiation for BCBM (n = 55); (2) inability to confirm survival status due to loss to follow-up (n = 40). A total of 207 consecutive patients were finally included in the development cohort.

Using the same criteria, 291 patients from the Samsung Medical Center treated between March 2013 and October 2023 were screened. Patients with whole brain radiation therapy (WBRT) were also included in this cohort. Fifty-one patients were excluded due to a history of prior brain surgery for BCBM, leaving 240 patients finally included in the external validation cohort. The following covariables were obtained for each patient through medical chart review: age at diagnosis (continuous), sex (categorical), KPS at brain metastasis diagnosis (continuous), presence of extracranial metastases (categorical), and combined systemic chemotherapy (categorical). Breast cancer subtypes were defined according to hormonal receptor (estrogen receptor [ER] and progesterone receptor [PR]) and HER2 status: triple positive (ER/PR/HER2+), HER2-positive (ER/PR-/HER2+), hormonal receptor-positive (ER/PR+, HER2-), and triple negative (ER/PR/HER2-).

Magnetic resonance imaging

Brain MRI was obtained with either 1.5 T or 3 T scanners, and included T2-weighted, T2-weighted fluid-attenuated inversion recovery, and pre- and post-contrast T1-weighted images. Post-contrast T1-weighted images were acquired with a three-dimensional (3D) gradient echo sequence on 1.5 T (repetition time, 8–2130 ms; echo time, 3–4 ms; field of view, 230–240 mm; section thickness, 3 mm) or 3 T (repetition time, 7–530 ms; echo time, 3–10 ms; field of view, 230–240 mm; section thickness, 3 mm) scanner after injection of a gadolinium-based contrast agent.

Qualitative and quantitative image analysis

LMD: LMD was diagnosed in patients with positive cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) cytology in pathology reports or whose brain or spine MRI review showed LMD. On brain MRI, LMD was defined as linear or nodular leptomeningeal or ependymal enhancement and cranial nerve root enhancement with or without hydrocephalus, whereas spine LMD was defined as linear or nodular leptomeningeal enhancement or spinal nerve root enhancement [7,11]. For imaging assessment, all baseline MRI data were reviewed independently by two neuroradiologists (Y.H.R. and J.E.P.) with 8 and 18 years of experience, respectively, each blinded to clinical outcomes. Cases showing discrepancies were reassessed with adjudication by readers. Presence of LMD was diagnosed at the time of newly diagnosed brain metastases, which were during breast cancer treatment.

Number and volume of brain metastases: For volumetric analysis, the MRI data were subjected to preprocessing, including resampling to 1 × 1 × 1 mm and co-registration performed using Analysis of Functional NeuroImages software [12]. Segmentation of the enhancing tumor region was performed using deep learning on the 3D contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images. The lesion segmentation model was implemented using nnU-Net, a 3D U-Net-based method (https://github.com/jieunp/BM_detection_AI), which has been validated in a multi-center study [13]. All segmented images were validated by an experienced neuroradiologist (J.E.P.). The number of metastases and tumor volume of all enhancing lesions were automatically calculated.

Calculation of updated diagnosis-specific GPA

The updated GPA for BCBM was used as a reference for prognostication [6,14]. The update included new factors such as extracranial metastases and number of brain metastases in 2020. GPA was calculated using the KPS score (≤60, 70-80, 90-100), age (≥60 or <60), number of brain metastases (1 or ≥ 2), presence of extracranial metastasis (present or absent), and breast cancer subtype (triple-negative, triple-positive, HER2-positive, and hormonal receptor-positive). The resulting GPA values were stratified using the following categories: 0.0–1.0, 1.5–2.0, 2.5–3.0, and 3.5–4.0.

Outcome definition

Patients were assessed clinically and/or through imaging at regular intervals of 2–3 months. OS was calculated from the date of initial diagnosis of brain metastasis until the date of death due to any cause. Death was confirmed using national healthcare system records. Patients were censored at the date of medical record abstraction or the date of the last clinical visit, whichever came first.

Statistical analysis

Differences in categorical variables between the development and validation cohorts were assessed using Fisher's exact test and the chi-square test, and differences between continuous variables were assessed using Student's t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test according to the results of the test used to assess normality.

Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazard regression analyses were used to identify factors independently associated with OS. Univariable Cox regression was performed for OS in the development cohort using clinical and imaging variables (age, sex, KPS score, breast cancer subtype, brain metastasis volumetry, number of brain metastases, extracranial metastases, LMD, and chemotherapy). This was followed by multivariable analysis, which included variables with a P-value <0.05 in the univariable analysis. Forward stepwise model selection was used based on the Akaike information criterion.

A scoring system was constructed using the final model as a basis. The estimated effects of the independent variables were ranked according to the penalized maximum likelihood estimates from the final survival model. The constant of the scoring system was defined as an increase in the risk for OS in proportional hazard regression units associated with selected predictors. For each predictor, the distance from the base category in β regression coefficient units was divided by this constant and rounded off to the nearest integer to determine a point. The points for all variables were then added, and the risk for OS was estimated with the total points by using a proportional hazard regression equation.

The prognostic performance of the GPA model and the new scoring system (enhanced GPA, eGPA) was evaluated using Harrell's C-index and time-dependent receiver operating characteristics (ROC) analysis [15], which was used to calculate 1-, 2-, 3-, and 5-year area under the curve (AUC). C-index values of <0.6, 0.6–0.7, and >0.7 for the prognostic models were considered poor, moderate, and good, respectively [16]. Calibration was assessed by comparing the predicted and observed 1-year survival probabilities using calibration plots. Internal calibration was evaluated with bootstrap resampling to generate optimism-corrected calibration curves and confidence limits in the development and validation cohorts.

To evaluate the incremental prognostic value of eGPA compared with the original GPA, model performance was further assessed using net reclassification improvement (NRI) and decision curve analysis. The NRI was reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Decision curve analysis was used to evaluate whether the improved prognostic performance of eGPA translated into potential clinical benefit across clinically relevant threshold probabilities.

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.0.2., Institute for Statistics and Mathematics, Vienna, Austria, http://www.R-project.org) by an expert statistician (with 17 years of experience in biostatistics), using the packages “survival” and “survivalROC”. Two-sided P < .05 indicated statistical significance.

Detailed LMD characteristics in both cohorts are summarized in Supplementary Table 1. LMD was diagnosed using both CSF cytology and imaging in 16 (72.7%) and 11 (55.0%) patients, imaging alone in 5 (22.7%) and 9 (45.0%), and CSF cytology alone in 1 (4.5%) and 0 patients in the development and validation cohorts, respectively. Spine MRI was performed selectively based on clinical suspicion, and spinal LMD was confirmed on imaging in 1 (4.5%) and 4 (20.0%) patients, respectively. LMD was identified concurrently with brain metastasis on the baseline brain MRI, with a median interval from LMD diagnosis to SRS of 7.5 days (interquartile range, 4.75–12.25) in the development cohort and 5.5 days (interquartile range, 2.75–12.5) in the validation cohort. LMD-directed management consisted of intrathecal methotrexate (IT-MTX) (n = 13), systemic chemotherapy (n = 8), or supportive care (n = 1) in the development cohort, and IT-MTX (n = 5), systemic chemotherapy (n = 5), WBRT (n = 3), or combined IT-MTX and WBRT (n = 6) in the validation cohort; one additional validation cohort patient was scheduled for IT-MTX but died before treatment could be initiated.

KPS scores (P < .001) and distribution of breast cancer subtype (P = .016) were significantly different between the two cohorts, with the validation set exhibiting a higher proportion of patients with KPS <70 (development vs. validation, 4.8% vs. 17.5%), a lower proportion with KPS 90‒100 (19.8% vs. 9.6%), and a higher proportion with a triple-negative subtype (27.5% vs. 37.1%). Total brain metastasis volume was higher in the development cohort (8117.8 vs. 6182.5 mm3, P = .014), whereas the number of brain metastases was higher in the external validation cohort (3.6 vs. 6.9, P < .001).

The relative frequency of GPA categories was significantly different between the two cohorts (P < .001) (Table 1). The development cohort showed a higher proportion of patients with high GPA (3.5‒4.0, 8.2% vs. 3.3%) and a lower proportion of patients with low GPA (0.0‒1.0, 16.9% vs. 25.8%) than the external validation cohort.

Development of an enhanced prognostic scoring system (eGPA)

The relationship between OS and each variable was evaluated by using univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazard regression analysis in the development cohort (Supplementary Table 2). The univariable analysis revealed that an age older than 40 years (P = .037) and a triple-positive breast cancer subtype (P < .001) were significantly associated with longer OS, while presence of extracranial metastases (P = .005) and LMD (P = .003) were associated with shorter OS. In the multivariable analysis, a KPS in the 90‒100 range (P = .019), breast cancer subtype (P < .001), number of brain metastases (P = .009), presence of extracranial metastases (P = .016), and presence of LMD (P = .003) were independent prognostic factors.

An eGPA was developed on the basis of the adjusted coefficients of the predictors in the multivariable model for patients with BCBM (Table 2). This eGPA included all GPA variables, including KPS, age, breast cancer subtype, number of brain metastases, and presence of extracranial metastases. To develop eGPA, age was converted to a binary variable (<40 years or ≥40 years), as this threshold was selected according to the maximal survival discrimination identified by the log-rank test. Likewise, number of brain metastases was stratified into three categories (1 to 4, 5 to 10, or more than 10). Additionally, presence of LMD was incorporated into the eGPA. The C-index for the eGPA was 0.64 (95% CI: 0.60–0.69), which was significantly higher than that for GPA in the development cohort (0.60 [95% CI: 0.56–0.65], P = .009). Time-dependent AUC values for one year were significantly higher for the eGPA in the development cohort (eGPA vs. GPA: AUC 0.71 [0.64–0.78] vs. 0.65 [0.58–0.72], P = .014). The time-dependent AUC values for 2, 3, and 5 years for the eGPA and GPA in the development cohort were similar, with the best performance observed at 5 years (eGPA vs. GPA: AUC 0.74 [0.61–0.87] vs. 0.75 [0.60–0.89]).

In the validation cohort, time-dependent AUC for the eGPA and GPA were similar regardless of the number of years. The highest performance of the enhanced scoring system was observed at 5 years, with a similar to better performance than that of GPA (AUC 0.87 [0.75–0.99] vs. 0.76 [0.55–0.95], P = .063).

Calibration plots (Fig. 3B) demonstrated generally acceptable agreement between predicted and observed 1-year survival probabilities in both the development and validation cohort. In the development cohort, the optimism-corrected curve closely followed the ideal line, supporting good internal calibration. In the validation cohort, the calibration curve showed reasonable agreement but tended to fall below the ideal line, suggesting mild overestimation of 1-year survival probability.

Clinical utility estimation of eGPA

On NRI analysis, the NRI was positive and statistically significant in the development cohort (NRI, 0.439; 95% CI, 0.066–0.753), indicating improved risk reclassification by eGPA compared with GPA. This was attenuated in the external validation cohort (NRI, −0.056; 95% CI, −0.659 to 0.214).

In decision curve analysis, eGPA demonstrated a more stable and sustained net benefit across a wider range of threshold probabilities compared with GPA in both development and external validation cohorts (Supplementary Fig. 1). In contrast, GPA showed a more fluctuating curve with a narrower range of clinically useful thresholds, suggesting relatively less robust clinical utility.

The clinical utility of eGPA is illustrated by two representative scenarios. First, a 43-year-old patient with KPS 70, hormonal receptor–positive/HER2-negative disease, 5–10 brain metastases, and extracranial metastasis would be classified in the poorest prognostic category by GPA, with an expected overall survival of approximately 6 months. In this setting, additional recognition of LMD may not substantially alter the GPA-based prognosis and could lead clinicians to favor best supportive care. In contrast, the same patient would receive an eGPA score of 24 out of 38, indicating that the patient is not in the poorest prognostic category. This may support consideration of a multimodal treatment approach, including SRS, systemic therapy, intrathecal therapy, or clinical trial enrollment. Second, a 35-year-old patient with KPS 90, HER2-positive breast cancer, no brain metastases, but extracranial metastasis and LMD would be classified in the best prognostic category by GPA. However, this classification may underestimate the adverse prognostic impact of LMD and potentially lead to an overemphasis on local LMD control alone. In contrast, eGPA assigns an intermediate-risk score of 19 out of 38, supporting a more comprehensive multimodal approach, including systemic therapy, rather than local treatment alone.

Discussion

In this study, we developed an enhanced prognostic scoring system (eGPA) for BCBM using clinical and imaging features. The system utilizes more contemporary information that includes cancer subtypes, number of metastases, and presence of LMD. LMD was shown to be an independent predictor of survival in patients with BCBM. The enhanced prognostic score showed similar to better performance compared to Breast-GPA in the development cohort, and comparable performance in the external validation cohort comprising patients treated with SRS with or without WBRT. Our study provides a meaningful alternative to Breast-GPA for individualized prognostic scoring, with the inclusion of LMD.

The presence of LMD is associated with poor prognosis, with a median survival of only 3.7–5.1 months, even in HER2-positive cases [17]. Radiotherapy plays a critical role in managing LMD in these patients, with multiple modalities available including WBRT, craniospinal irradiation, and SRS for focal disease [18]. Current rationale of treatment for coexistence of parenchymal metastasis and LMD is a multidisciplinary approach [19] that local control of parenchymal disease enables avoiding toxicity of WBRT and preserving neurocognitive function [20] and focal LMD can be targeted with SRS [21]. Beyond local interventions, trastuzumab deruxtecan has emerged as a promising systemic option for HER2-positive LMD, demonstrating significant intracranial activity and potential survival benefits [22]. In addition, evidence of Sacituzumab govitecan in triple-negative breast cancer disease shows potential therapeutic option for BM, enhancing role of a multidisciplinary and multimodal approach. According to our enhanced prognostic score, LMD (score 8), hormonal receptor-positive subtype (score 7) and low KPS (score 10) are associated with a similar risk. This is in agreement with current treatment guidelines recommending craniospinal irradiation for central nervous system (CNS) and CSF disease control in selected patients with low-risk LMD (KPS ≥60, no major neurologic deficits, and minimal systemic disease) [23]. The eGPA would be helpful to estimate risk among patients with BCBM and LMD.

A younger age (under 40 years) was associated with poor prognosis in this study. This is consistent with a meta-analysis of 129 studies showing that young breast cancer patients (≤35–40 years) have significantly worse survival outcomes. Breast cancer in younger patients is characterized by a decreased prevalence of hormonal receptor-positive tumors and an enrichment of more aggressive subtypes, such as triple-negative and HER2 positive tumors, which often present with increased cell proliferation and higher histological grades [24]. Compared with Breast-GPA, in which being above 60 years of age is associated with high risk, being under 40 years of age was associated with a similar risk as having a KPS in the 70–80 range or a HER2 positive subtype in the system we developed. This better reflects current knowledge about the disease.

The categories used to stratify the number of brain metastases (1–4, 5–10, and >10) are more appropriate for the recommendations included in the clinical practice guidelines issued by the European Association of Neuro-Oncology – European Society for Medical Oncology (EANO-ESMO) [8] and the American Society of Clinical Oncology - Society for Neuro-Oncology - American Society for Radiation Oncology (ASCO-SNO-ASTRO) [9,25]. These guidelines recommend the use of SRS for the treatment of up to four intact brain metastases. For more than four unresected metastases, SRS, WBRT, and their combination are all considered reasonable options for patients with good performance status (KPS ≥70), whereas their use is conditionally recommended for patients with up to 10 brain metastases and good performance status [9,25]. The external validation cohort included patients treated with both SRS and WBRT who demonstrated good performance of the eGPA, reflecting the generalizability of this model.

The total tumor volume was not identified as a significant prognostic factor. This suggests that in BCBM, the biological characteristics of the tumor (cancer subtype) may override anatomical factors such as number of tumors or volume. Multiple brain metastases may reflect a more aggressive tumor phenotype with greater capacity for CNS dissemination, independently of the total tumor burden [26]. The relative importance of the number of brain metastases over quantitative volumetric analysis demonstrated in this study suggests that qualitative characteristics of tumor biology are more relevant than the quantitative disease burden.

The limitations of this study include the following. First, the development cohort consisted primarily of patients treated with SRS, introducing selection bias toward a more favorable, locally treatable subgroup rather than the full spectrum of BCBM, which may limit generalizability. However, the external validation cohort included a more heterogeneous population, with 25.9% receiving WBRT, partially supporting the model's broader applicability. Second, the patients did not have multiple nationalities or belong to different ethnicities, and therefore the results require further validation across multiple nations and ethnic groups. Third, LMD was assessed using both imaging and pathology confirmation from CSF samples, but false negatives may have been present, as the 1.5 T imaging modality may have underestimated their number and the false-negative rate of CSF cytology was approximately 10–15%, even with optimal technique of lumbar puncture [27]. A more precise assessment of the presence of LMD may require a modification of our scoring system.

In conclusion, eGPA of BCBM incorporating LMD and contemporary clinical variables showed similar to better performance compared with Breast-GPA. This may provide more individualized risk scoring for patients with BCBM who are candidates for SRS and/or WBRT.

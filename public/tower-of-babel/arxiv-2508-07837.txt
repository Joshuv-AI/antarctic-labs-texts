A Highly Predictive Neutrino Model: The Step Toward Precision
Pralay Chakraborty, Sagar Tirtha Goswami, Subhankar Roy

Abstract
A highly predictive neutrino mass matrix texture is proposed. The texture is minimal and carries two interesting correlations. It gives precise and unambiguous answers to almost all the current issues related to observable parameters in neutrino phenomenology. The texture is developed in a framework of type-I + type-II seesaw mechanisms with an extended group structure of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L × U ​ ( 1 ) Y × A 4 × Z 10 × Z 7 × Z 3 SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\times A_{4}\times Z_{10}\times Z_{7}\times Z_{3} .

The Standard Model (SM)

;
Weinberg 1967
;
Salam 1968

In particular, the SM remains silent on two key issues: the origin of tiny but non-zero masses of neutrinos and the significant mixing among different flavours of neutrinos. Neutrino oscillation experiments have confirmed the tiny but non-zero masses of neutrinos

•

The octant of
\theta_{23}
is still unknown.

•

The precise bound of
\delta
is not obtained yet.

•

The Majorana phases have not been measured.

•

The neutrino mass eigenvalues are not determined.

•

The neutrino mass hierarchy is not known.

The information on all the above mentioned parameters is contained in the Majorana neutrino mass matrix (
M_{\nu}
). In this regard, several phenomenological constraints such as
\mu-\tau

King and Nishi 2018
;
Xing and Zhao 2016

Chakraborty et al. 2024
, we study a simple correlation but are unable to predict the neutrino mass hierarchy. Although a complete quantitative determination of the observable parameters is still unavailable, the limited information already provides valuable insights into the underlying symmetries governing neutrino mass and mixing patterns.

In this work, we deviate from the existing literature and propose a new minimal neutrino mass matrix texture. This texture is special in the sense that it carries interesting correlations among the matrix elements that have not been studied earlier. It contains only four parameters and can address all the unresolved issues summarized above.

III.2
CP Asymmetry Parameter

The leptonic Dirac CP violation, governed by the phase
\delta
, can manifest in neutrino oscillations through the CP asymmetry parameter
A_{\mu e}

A_{\mu e}=\frac{P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})-P(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e})}{P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})+P(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e})}.

The transition probability
P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})
is expressed in terms of observational parameters as follows:

P(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e})=P_{\text{atm}}+P_{\text{sol}}+2\sqrt{P_{\text{atm}}}\sqrt{P_{\text{sol}}}\cos(\Delta_{ij}+\delta),

where,
\Delta_{ij}=\Delta m_{ij}^{2}\cdot\frac{L}{4E}

E

L

\displaystyle P_{\text{atm}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{23}\sin 2\theta_{13}\cdot\frac{\sin(\Delta_{31}-aL)}{(\Delta_{31}-aL)}\cdot\Delta_{31},

\displaystyle P_{\text{sol}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{23}\sin 2\theta_{12}\cdot\frac{\sin(aL)}{aL}\cdot\Delta_{21}.

It is to be noted that the parameter
a=\frac{G_{F}N_{e}}{\sqrt{2}}
arises due to matter effects and depends on the medium through which the neutrino propagates. Here,
G_{F}

N_{e}

a
is approximately estimated as
3500~\text{km}^{-1}

A_{\mu e}

A_{\mu e}=\frac{2\sqrt{P_{\text{atm}}}\sqrt{P_{\text{sol}}}\sin\Delta_{32}\sin\delta}{P_{\text{atm}}+2\sqrt{P_{\text{atm}}}\sqrt{P_{\text{sol}}}\cos\Delta_{32}\cos\delta+P_{\text{sol}}}.

V
Summary and Discussion

In the present work, we propose a highly predictive neutrino mass matrix. The texture is minimal, with only four complex parameters. It answers most of the queries related to observational parameters of neutrino sector. The texture rules out inverted hierarchy of neutrino masses and predicts the individual mass eigenvalues. Besides, the texture strictly favours the upper octant of the atmospheric mixing angle by imposing a precise bound. The three CP phases, one Dirac and two Majorana, are also constrained within narrow ranges.

The texture is obtained in its exact form starting from
SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}\times A_{4}\times Z_{10}\times Z_{7}\times Z_{3}
symmetry within a hybrid framework of type-I + type-II seesaw mechanisms. In addition, the charged lepton mass hierarchy is explained through FN mechanism. At first glance, it may seem misleading that the Yukawa terms of the neutrino sector are suppressed by
\sim 1/\Lambda,1/\Lambda^{2}
, whereas for charged leptons, the suppressions are of the order
1/\Lambda^{9}
. Here, it is important to note that the involvement of the several vevs plays a crucial role in addressing this anomaly and thus naturally explaining the desired results
Chakraborty and Roy 2025b
. Hence, the cut-off scale
\Lambda
is not the sole suppression factor in the present model. We highlight that the presence of auxiliary groups restricts other possibilities of
1/\Lambda^{n}
, with
n>9
for the charged lepton sector from appearing in the theory.

In the context of the scalar potential supporting the chosen VEV alignments, we would like to state that the strategy is not so straightforward. The present model contains eight scalar field multiplets, as shown in Table (
1
), and in reality, there are thirty-nine real scalar fields. In this regard, we obtain thirty-nine
optimisation
conditions. To check whether the chosen vacuum alignments lead to actual minima, the Hessian matrix
H_{ij}=(\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\phi_{i}\partial\phi_{j}})_{0}
must be evaluated. We note that the necessary condition for a minimum to occur is that either the eigenvalues of
H
are positive (or zero for Goldstone states), or the principal cofactors are positive definite. This task is quite challenging, as the Hessian matrix is of dimension thirty-nine. However, the potential (or the Hessian) involves several free parameters, such as the
\mu^{2}
’s and couplings
\lambda
’s. Thus, by tuning these parameters properly, one may achieve the required conditions. Nevertheless, this task is beyond the scope of the present work.

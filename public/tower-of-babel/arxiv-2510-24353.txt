Graded Monads in the Semantics of Nominal Automata
Hannes Schulze, Lutz Schröder, Üsame Cengiz

Abstract
Nominal automata models serve as a formalism for data languages, and in fact often relate closely to classical register models. The paradigm of name allocation in nominal automata helps alleviate the pervasive computational hardness of register models in a tradeoff between expressiveness and computational tractability. For instance, regular nondeterministic nominal automata (RNNAs) correspond, under their local freshness semantics, to a form of lossy register automata, and unlike the full register automaton model allow for inclusion checking in elementary complexity. The semantic framework of graded monads provides a unified algebraic treatment of spectra of behavioural equivalences in the setting of universal coalgebra. In the present work, we extend the associated notion of graded algebraic theory to the nominal setting. In the arising framework of graded nominal algebra, we give an algebraic theory capturing the local freshness semantics of RNNAs.



1
Introduction

Languages over infinite alphabets are often briefly termed
data
languages
, in a view where letters are understood as abstracting
elements of data types that are either infinite or prohibitively
large. Typical examples include data values in XML
documents
[
34
]
, object identities
[
23
]
, or nonces in cryptographic protocols
[
28
]
. One of the most
prominent formalisms for data languages are
register automata

(originally introduced under the name
finite memory
automata

[
25
]
), which have a fixed finite
number of registers in which letters encountered in the input word can
be stored for later comparison with other letters. The world of
register automata differs markedly from that of standard finite
automata. In particular, non-deterministic register automata cannot in
general be determinized. Inclusion checking of register automata is
undecidable unless either the number of registers or the power of
control (deterministic/non-deterministic/alternating) is severely
restricted. For instance, inclusion checking of non-deterministic
register automata is undecidable unless the number of registers is
restricted to at most two
[
25
]
) or the power of
control is restricted to unambiguous
automata
[
33
,
7
]
; and inclusion checking of
alternating register automata is undecidable unless the number of
registers is restricted to one, and even then is not primitive
recursive
[
8
]
. An alternative view of register
automata is provided by automata based on nominal
sets
[
4
]
, in which, roughly speaking, the underlying
set of names plays the role of the infinite alphabet. In particular,
non-deterministic orbit-finite automata (NOFA) are equivalent to
register automata with non-deterministic
update
[
26
]
, and as such inherit the computational
difficulty of register automata.

Regular non-deterministic nominal automata
(RNNA)

[
39
]
have been proposed as a model that
trades some of the expressiveness of register automata for
computational tractability; in particular, RNNA allow inclusion
checking in exponential space, and more precisely even in parametrized
polynomial space with the number of registers as the parameter (recall
that already inclusion checking of standard non-deterministic finite
automata is
PSpace
-complete). Technically, RNNAs (unlike NOFAs and
register automata) disallow infinite branching but instead feature
explicit name-allocating transitions that effectively read fresh names
from the input string. Here, the relevant notion of freshness is
governed by the discipline imposed on renaming bound names in
name-allocating transitions: A
global freshness
semantics,
where newly read names are required to be fresh w.r.t. all previous
names, is enforced by a discipline of clean renaming (avoiding
shadowing of bound names), while
local freshness
, i.e. freshness w.r.t. (some of) the names currently stored in the
registers, is induced by unrestricted renaming according to the usual
rules of
\alpha
-equivalence familiar from
\lambda
-calculus. These
concepts have been extended to nominal automata models processing
trees
[
37
]
and infinite words
[
43
]
, as
well as to alternating nominal automata
[
16
]
.

The categorical framework of
graded semantics

[
32
]

provides a unified treatment of spectra of semantics of automata
models and concurrent systems, such as the well-known linear-time /
branching-time spectrum of semantics of labelled transition
systems
[
9
]
. This is achieved by combining the setting
of universal coalgebra
[
38
]
with additional algebraic laws
introduced via the choice of a
graded monad

[
40
]
, a
categorical structure that abstractly represents a
graded
algebraic theory
. Here, the notion of
grade
intuitively
corresponds to depth of look-ahead, measured in (exact) numbers of
transition steps. The notion of graded theory has originally been
introduced for set-based graded monads
[
40
]
; it has been
extended to graded monads on the category of posets
[
12
]
,
the category of metric spaces
[
14
]
, and more generally
to graded monads on categories of relational
structures
[
11
,
15
]
. Benefits of casting a given
semantics as a graded semantics include instantiation of generic
results obtained for graded semantics, such as logical
characterizations
[
32
,
9
,
12
,
14
]

and game characterizations
[
13
,
15
]
.

In the present work, we launch into extending the core results of
graded theories to graded monads on nominal sets (a case that is not
covered by the above-mentioned results on categories of relational
structures). Specifically, we introduce a notion of graded nominal
algebra, based on a simplification of Gabbay and Mathijsen’s framework
of nominal algebra
[
21
]
. We show that graded
nominal theories induce graded monads on the category of nominal sets
and, crucially, that graded nominal theories of depth
1
induce
graded monads that are depth-
1
in an abstract categorical
sense
[
32
]
, a key prerequisite for most technical results
in graded semantics
(e.g.
[
32
,
9
,
13
,
15
]
). As our main
result, we then present a graded algebraic theory that captures the
local freshness semantics of RNNAs.

Nominal Sets

Nominal sets serve the principled

and

]
. Fix a countably infinite set

of

be the group of finite permutations

\mathbb{A}
, which is generated by the permutations

that swap

equipped with a left action

of

. For

\textstyle\mathop{\mathsf{fix}}(x)=\{\pi\in G\mid\pi\cdot x=x\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\mathsf{Fix}}(A)=\bigcap_{x\in A}\mathop{\mathsf{fix}}(x).

Notice that
\mathbb{A}
itself is a
G
-set with the action given by

\pi\cdot a=\pi(a)
. A set
S\subseteq\mathbb{A}
is a
support

of
x\in X
if
\mathop{\mathsf{Fix}}(S)\subseteq\mathop{\mathsf{fix}}(x)
; in this case, we think
of
S
as giving an upper bound on the names occurring in
x
. We say
that
x
is
finitely supported
if
x
has some finite support.
A
G
-set

is a

if every
x\in X
is finitely
supported. One can then show that every
x\in X
has a
least

finite support, denoted
\mathsf{supp}(x)
; we think of
\mathsf{supp}(x)
as the set
of names occurring in
x
. For every
G
-set
X
, the finitely
supported elements of
X
form a nominal set. A name

is

fresh
for an element
x\in X
of a nominal set

(in symbols,

a\mathbin{\#}x
) if
a\notin\mathsf{supp}(x)
. A map
f\colon X\to Y
between

G
-sets
X,Y
is

if
f(\pi\cdot x)=\pi\cdot f(x)

for all
x\in X
,
\pi\in G
. (This term extends to subsets

Y\subseteq X
of a nominal set

by viewing them as predicates;
explicitly,

is equivariant if
y\in Y
implies
\pi\cdot y\in Y

for all
\pi\in G
.) Nominal sets and
equivariant maps form a category
\mathbf{Nom}
. The
orbit
of
x\in X

in a
G
-set

is the set
\{\pi\cdot x\mid\pi\in G\}
. The orbits
of

form a disjoint decomposition of

;
X
is

if
X
has only finitely many orbits.

Example 2.1

We list some key examples and constructions of nominal
sets.

1.

The
G

is nominal; for

, we have

\mathsf{supp}(a)=\{a\}
.

2.

Every set

\mathsf{supp}(x)=\emptyset

3.

For every

.

4.

of a nominal set

-set under

A set

6.

Given a nominal set

is the quotient of

modulo the equivalence relation

We write

for the equivalence class of

under

. We think of
\langle a\rangle x
as arising from

in analogy to

-abstraction in

-calculus or name abstraction in the

-calculus,

-renamed. Indeed we have

.

Universal Coalgebra

Recall that a
functor

on a category
\mathbf{C}
assigns to each

\mathbf{C}
-object

\mathbf{C}
-object

as
consisting of structured objects made up of inhabitants of

. We
will primarily be interested in the case
\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{Nom}
. Indeed, all
constructions of nominal sets recalled in
2.1
extend to
functors, for instance, the action of the
abstraction functor

[\mathbb{A}](-)
arising from
2.1
.
6

maps an equivariant map

to the equivariant map

[\mathbb{A}]f\colon[\mathbb{A}]X\to[\mathbb{A}]Y

[\mathbb{A}]f(\langle a\rangle x)=\langle a\rangle f(x)
.

Universal coalgebra
[
38
]
provides a unified framework for
automata models and concurrent systems by abstracting the type of a

F\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}
on a base category
\mathbf{C}
. An

-coalgebra

(C,\gamma)
then consists of a
\mathbf{C}
-object

,
thought of as an object of

, and a
\mathbf{C}
-morphism

\gamma\colon C\to FC
embodying the

the system. As a first example, we have a functor

on
\mathbf{Nom}

defined on objects by

is a two-element discrete nominal set. An

. However, one is often
interested in more coarse-grained notions of equivalence, such as
trace equivalence on labelled transition systems or language

to modelling such equivalences in universal coalgebra

[

1
,
32
]
); the specific framework
of graded semantics is recalled in detail in

3
Nominal Automata with Name Allocation

We proceed to recall regular non-deterministic nominal
automata (RNNAs) and their global and local freshness
semantics
[
39
]
. The main driving principle behind RNNAs
is to let automata natively accept words with bound names indicating
that a fresh name is read from the input string, so-called bar
strings, and then generate data words from bar strings by taking all
instantiations of the bound letters. Here, instantiation of bound names
proceeds in two steps: First, form all
\alpha
-equivalent variants of
the bar string, and then forget that bound names are bound. Bound
names are indicated by a preceding ‘

’; that is,

{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
indicates that a fresh letter
a
is read from the
input. Thus, a
bar string
is just a word over the extended
alphabet

\overline{\mathbb{A}}=\mathbb{A}\cup\{{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a\mid a\in\mathbb{A}\}.

Formally, an occurrence of
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
in a bar string binds the
name
a
, with the scope of the binding extending to the end of the
string. Bound names in this sense can be renamed according to the
usual discipline of
\alpha
-equivalence known from
\lambda
-calculus
or
\pi
-calculus; explicitly:

Definition 3.1
(
\alpha
-Equivalence of bar strings)

The relation of
\alpha
-equivalence
on bar strings is the
equivalence
\equiv_{\alpha}
generated by

w{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}av\equiv_{\alpha}w{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}bu\quad\text{if }\langle a\rangle v=\langle b\rangle u\text{ in }[\mathbb{A}]\,\overline{\mathbb{A}}^{*}.

(3.1)

The
\alpha
-equivalence class of a bar string

w\in\overline{\mathbb{A}}^{*}
is denoted
[w]_{\alpha}
. The set
\mathsf{FN}(w)

of
free names
in
w
is given recursively by

\mathsf{FN}(\epsilon)=\emptyset
(where as usual
\epsilon
denotes the
empty string),
\mathsf{FN}(aw)=\{a\}\cup\mathsf{FN}(w)
, and

\mathsf{FN}({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}aw)=\mathsf{FN}(w)\setminus\{a\}
. A bar string

is

clean
if all bound names
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
in
w
are mutually
distinct and distinct from all free names in
w
.

For instance,

{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b\equiv_{\alpha}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}c{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b\equiv_{\alpha}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b
, while

{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}ba\equiv_{\alpha}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}c{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}bc\not\equiv_{\alpha}{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}bb
. Crucially, the renaming of

{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a
into
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b
in
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}ba
is

blocked
by the free occurrence of
a
in
{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}ba
;
formally, this is due to the (straightforward) observation that

\mathsf{supp}([w]_{\alpha})=\mathsf{FN}(w)
.

We will be interested in three types of languages:
Data
languages
are subsets of
\mathbb{A}^{*}
, whose elements we refer to as

data words
;
literal languages
are subsets
of
\overline{\mathbb{A}}^{*}
; and
bar languages
are subsets
of
\overline{\mathbb{A}}^{*}/{\equiv_{\alpha}}
. We convert bar languages into data
languages in two alternative ways respectively corresponding to

global
and
local freshness
semantics of name binding. We
define the auxiliary operation

\mathsf{ub}\colon\overline{\mathbb{A}}^{*}/{\equiv_{\alpha}}\to\mathbb{A}^{*}
recursively by

\mathsf{ub}(\epsilon)=\epsilon
,
\mathsf{ub}(aw)=a\mathsf{ub}(w)
, and

\mathsf{ub}({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}aw)=a\mathsf{ub}(w)
; that is,
\mathsf{ub}
removes all bars
‘

’ from a bar string. Then given a bar language
L
, we
define data languages

,

by

N(L)=\{\mathsf{ub}(w)\mid[w]_{\alpha}\in L,w\text{ clean}\}\qquad D(L)=\{\mathsf{ub}(w)\mid[w]_{\alpha}\in L\}.

We briefly write
D(w)=D(\{w\})
,
N(w)=N(\{w\})
. For instance,

N({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b)=\{ab\mid a,b\in\mathbb{A},a\neq b\}
,

D({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}b)=\{ab\mid a,b\in\mathbb{A}\}
, and

D({\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}a{\hskip 0.86108pt{\rule[-0.43057pt]{0.6pt}{7.10411pt}}\hskip 0.86108pt}ba)=\{aba\mid a,b\in\mathbb{A},a\neq b\}
. Roughly speaking, global freshness semantics, embodied by
the
N
operator, enforces instantiations of bound letters to be
distinct from all letters seen previously in the input word, while
local freshness, embodied by the
D
operator, is more permissive,
enforcing distinctness only from previously seen letters that are
expected to be seen again (in an automaton model, this implies in
particular that the previous letter must still be in memory). In fact
the
N
operator is injective, so global freshness semantics is
essentially equivalent to a semantics given directly in terms of bar
languages.

The automaton model of RNNAs is designed to accept bar languages,
subsequently equipped with global or local freshness semantics as
indicated above.

Graded Monads and Graded Theories

We next briefly summarize the framework of

[

,

Definition 1

A
graded monad

[
40
]
on a category
\mathbf{C}
is a
tuple
{\mathbb{M}}=((M_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\eta,

(\mu^{nk})_{n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}})

•

a family of endofunctors
M_{n}\colon\mathbf{C}\to\mathbf{C}

•

a natural transformation
\eta:\mathsf{Id}\to M_{0}

unit
; and

•

a family of natural transformations

\mu^{nk}:M_{n}M_{k}\to M_{n+k}
, indexed over

n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
multiplication

satisfying graded variants of the usual
unit
and

associative
laws
(
\mu^{0,n}\cdot\eta M_{n}=\mathit{id}_{M_{n}}=\mu^{n,0}\cdot M_{n}\eta
,

\mu^{n,k+m}\cdot M_{n}\mu^{k,m}=\mu^{n+k,m}\cdot\mu^{n,k}M_{m}
).

To aid the understanding of the concept, we recall how (finitary)
graded monads on the category
\mathsf{Set}
of sets and maps can be
generated from algebraic presentations
[
40
,
32
]
;
one of our present contributions is to generalize this principle to
graded monads on the category
\mathbf{Nom}
of nominal sets
(
Section 5
). A
graded signature

\Sigma

is a collection of function symbols
f
with assigned

arity

\mathsf{ar}(f)
and

. This gives rise to a
notion of term of uniform depth: Variables are terms of uniform
depth
0
, and if
d(f)=k
and
\mathsf{ar}(f)=n
, then

is a term of uniform depth
m+k

are terms of uniform depth
m

is a constant in
\Sigma

is a term of
uniform depth
m

m\geq k

graded theory

(\Sigma,E)
then consists of a graded signature
\Sigma
and a
set

uniform depth

every equation

in

and

have uniform depth
k
. Such a graded theory induces a graded monad

((M_{n}),\eta,(\mu^{n,k}))
on
\mathsf{Set}
where

Generally, we think of

is a terminal object)
as a set of
n
-step behaviours. Correspondingly, one has the
following notion of

Definition 2

A

category

with a terminal object

{\mathbb{M}}=((M_{n}),\eta,(\mu^{nk}))

\displaystyle\gamma^{(0)}

\displaystyle=(X\xrightarrow{\eta_{X}}M_{0}X\xrightarrow{M_{0}!}M_{0}1),

\displaystyle\gamma^{(n+1)}

\displaystyle=(X\xrightarrow{\beta_{X}\cdot\gamma}M_{1}X\xrightarrow{M_{1}\gamma^{(n)}}M_{1}M_{n}1\xrightarrow{\mu^{1n}_{X}}M_{n+1}1).

)

We can view finitely branching

Remark 1

The coalgebraic modelling of automata models differs from that of

7
Conclusions and Future Work

We have introduced a universal-algebraic system for the
presentation of graded monads on the category of nominal sets. In a
subsequent case study, we have applied this system to model the global
and local freshness semantics of regular non-deterministic transition
systems (a slightly simplified form of
regular non-deterministic
nominal automata (RNNAs)

[
39
]
) in the framework of

graded semantics

[
32
,
9
]
. Notably, the
axiom distinguishing local from global freshness semantics just states
that every bound name subsumes the corresponding free name, a
consequence of the fact that every bound name is
\alpha
-equivalent
to itself, and a key factor in the comparative algorithmic
tractability of RNNAs (e.g. in the elementary complexity of inclusion
checking
[
39
]
, which contrasts with undecidability of
inclusion for non-deterministic register automata with more than two
registers
[
25
]
). We have proved soundness and
completeness of our system of
graded nominal algebra
, and we
have shown that the graded monad induced by graded nominal theory is

depth-
1
if the graded theory itself is depth-
1
. In
particular, this shows that the graded monads featuring in the local
and global freshness semantics of regular non-deterministic nominal
automata are depth-
1
.

In work on graded semantics over
sets
[
32
,
9
,
13
]
, partial
orders
[
12
]
, metric spaces
[
14
]
, and
relational structures
[
15
]
, the somewhat
technical-looking depth-
1
property has played a key role in enabling
characterization results for graded semantics in terms of modal logics
and Spoiler-Duplicator games. The mentioned previous general results
on such characterizations do not yet apply to nominal sets but one
will still expect that variants can be established over nominal sets;
thus, obtaining general results for characteristic modal logics and
behavioural equivalence games over nominal sets is an important topic
for future research. We expect that such results will depend on the
involved graded monads being
depth-
1
, and that the game
characterization will make use of our universal-algebraic system. An
additional point of future research is coverage of additional
examples, such as fresh labelled transition
systems
[
3
]
.

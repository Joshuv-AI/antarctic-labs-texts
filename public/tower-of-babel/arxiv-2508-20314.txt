On black holes in new general relativity
D. F. López

Abstract




1
Introduction

In teleparallel gravity (TG), the primary geometric object is torsion, which is derived from the frame (or co-frame) and a flat, metric-compatible spin connection
[
1
]
. Understanding this geometric framework within which TG theories are formulated as alternatives to general relativity (GR) for describing gravitational interaction requires the study of spherically symmetric geometries. These geometries are essential for assessing the viability of gravitational theories.
They encompass both time-dependent configurations, which are relevant for cosmological models that explain the large-scale evolution of the universe (as in GR through the Friedmann–Robertson–Walker (FRW) solution), and time-independent configurations that describe static black holes (such as the Schwarzschild solution in GR), which is a vacuum solution of GR.
However, such a solution raises philosophical issues in the teleparallel context: while singularities in GR typically occur only at the origin of the radial coordinate, from the literature we observe that TG consistently exhibits singular behaviour at both the origin and the putative horizon.

Within the family of TG theories, one stands apart due to its local dynamical equivalence with GR: the Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (TEGR). This subclass is based on the torsion scalar
T
, which appears in the gravitational action.
Such an equivalence with GR is reflected in the equality of the field equations (FE) and hence the agreement of classical predictions
[
1
]
, including the existence of an analogous Schwarzschild solution.
However, the two theories differ conceptually and geometrically, which affects their descriptions of black holes. In particular, while black holes are well-defined in GR, TEGR lacks a clear formulation of black holes.
It has been shown that the TEGR geometry features unavoidable divergences at the local horizon (defined below), indicating that the theory cannot consistently describe black holes
[
2
]
. For instance, in our analysis of static spherically symmetric vacuum spacetimes, we found that in the Schwarzschild-like solution of TEGR—unlike its GR counterpart—two torsion invariants, diverge at the so-called Schwarzschild horizon. Moreover, the torsion scalar
T
also diverges at that location for certain choices of two free functions in the tetrad (when using a proper frame) or in the spin connection (when using the orthonormal frame). Consequently, in such cases within the TEGR, the horizon and the region inside it do not form part of the manifold.

1.1
Black holes

In GR, the geometry is determined by the curvature of the Levi-Civita connection, which is derived from the metric. The spherically symmetric vacuum Schwarzschild solution, governed by Birkhoff’s theorem, is the unique static, spherically symmetric, and asymptotically flat vacuum solution to the Einstein FE, resulting in black holes with horizons that conceal a spacetime singularity.
These types of solutions give rise to intriguing phenomena within the framework of TG, challenging the conventional notions of black holes and gravitational singularities. Unlike in GR, where singularities typically arise only at the origin of the radial coordinate (in Schwarzschild coordinates), TG exhibits divergent behavior near both the horizon and the origin (in Schwarzschild-like coordinates).

In the Schwarzschild manifold, the surface
r_{s}=2M
acts as a horizon, known globally as the event horizon, and is often locally characterized as an apparent horizon (AH)
[
42
]
. An AH is a marginally outer trapped surface defined by the vanishing expansion of outgoing null geodesics (
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0
), while the ingoing null geodesics remain converging (
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}<0
). Additionally, the expansion of the outgoing null geodesics must decrease when moving inward along the ingoing null direction (
\Delta\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}<0
). These conditions can be summarized as follows
[
43
]
:

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0\,,\quad\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}<0\,,\quad\Delta\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}<0\,.

(1)

This geometric definition underlies the commonly used phenomenological equation for locating the AH, which consists of setting a specific metric function to zero
[
44
]
, such as
g_{tt}=0
. However, this procedure is a coordinate-dependent simplification employed in particular coordinate systems—especially in static, spherically symmetric spacetimes—and it ultimately follows from the “coordinate-independent” condition
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0
. Here, we do not adopt an invariant definition of a horizon, but instead focus on a local characterization
[
43
]
. We note, therefore, that
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0
is a necessary condition for the definition of an AH; such a condition defines the regions we will refer to as a local horizon, as employed in this work.

In TG theories, such as NGR, matter fields are minimally coupled to the metric, as in GR. Consequently, the equations of motion are identical in both frameworks, that is, the test particles follow geodesics. The key difference lies in the geometric interpretation rather than in the physical predictions
[
1
]
. This allows the definition of a local horizon to be shared between both theories.

2
Fundamentals of teleparallel geometry

The teleparallel geometry is characterized by a tetrad field,
h^{a}{}_{\mu}
, and a metric-compatible spin connection,
\omega_{ab\mu}
, which is antisymmetric in the Latin indices (
\omega_{{\text{\tiny(}}ab{\text{\tiny)}}\mu}=0
). This framework generates non-zero torsion while maintaining a vanishing curvature
[
1
]
. The tetrads satisfy the orthogonality conditions
h^{a}{}_{\mu}h_{a}{}^{\nu}=\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}
and
h^{a}{}_{\sigma}h_{b}{}^{\sigma}=\delta^{a}_{b}
, and establish a relationship between the spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and the tangent-space Minkowski metric
\eta_{ab}=\text{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
, as given by

g_{\mu\nu}=h^{a}{}_{\mu}h^{b}{}_{\nu}\eta_{ab}\quad\text{or}\quad\eta_{ab}=h_{a}{}^{\mu}h_{b}{}^{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}\,.

(2)

The total covariant derivative, which acts on both the spacetime and tangent-space indices, vanishes when applied to the tetrad. This condition, known as the
tetrad postulate

[
3
]
, establishes the relationship between the teleparallel connection,
\Omega^{\rho}{}_{\nu\mu}
, and spin connection,
\omega^{a}{}_{b\mu}
, given by

\Omega^{\rho}{}_{\nu\mu}=h_{a}{}^{\rho}\partial_{\mu}h^{a}{}_{\nu}+h_{a}{}^{\rho}\omega^{a}{}_{b\mu}h^{b}{}_{\nu}\,.

(3)

The teleparallel connection features a torsion tensor, defined as

T^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\nu}=2\Omega^{\sigma}{}_{{\text{\tiny[}}\nu\mu{\text{\tiny]}}}\,,

(4)

which is antisymmetric for the last two indices. The torsion tensor can be decomposed into components that are irreducible under the Lorentz group
[
45
]
, commonly referred to as the vector, axial, and purely tensorial parts:

\mathscr{V}_{\mu}=T^{\nu}{}_{\nu\mu}\,,\quad\mathscr{A}_{\mu}=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}T^{\nu\rho\sigma}\,,\quad\mathscr{T}_{\sigma\mu\nu}=T_{{\text{\tiny(}}\sigma\mu{\text{\tiny)}}\nu}+\frac{1}{3}\left(g_{\sigma{\text{\tiny[}}\nu}\mathscr{V}_{\mu{\text{\tiny]}}}+g_{\mu{\text{\tiny[}}\nu}\mathscr{V}_{\sigma{\text{\tiny]}}}\right)\,.

(5)

Here, the axial torsion is defined using the totally antisymmetric Levi-Civita tensor
\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
associated with the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.

A fundamental result stemming from the metric compatibility of Lorentz connections is that any such connection can be decomposed into the Levi-Civita connection
\Gamma^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\nu}
and the contortion tensor
[
46
]
:

\Omega^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\nu}+K^{\sigma}{}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(6)

where the contortion tensor, which is antisymmetric in the first two indices, is given by

K_{\sigma\mu\nu}=T_{{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\sigma{\text{\tiny]}}\nu}+\frac{1}{2}T_{\nu\sigma\mu}\,.

(7)

An important consequence of decomposition (
6
) is that the curvature tensor associated with the Levi-Civita connection can be expressed as

\mathcal{R}^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}=2N^{\rho}{}_{\sigma{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}\,,

(8)

where

N^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\nu}K^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu}+K^{\rho}{}_{\lambda\mu}K^{\lambda}{}_{\sigma\nu}+K^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\lambda}K^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(9)

which is a tensor that features antisymmetry in its first two indices and
\nabla
denotes the covariant derivative with respect to the teleparallel connection (
3
).
Contracting indices in Eq. (
8
) yields the Ricci scalar, which satisfies the following identity:

\mathcal{R}=2N^{\mu\nu}{}_{{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}=-T-\frac{2}{h}\partial_{\nu}(hT^{\mu\nu}{}_{\mu})\,,

(10)

with

T=K^{\mu\nu\rho}K_{\rho\nu\mu}-K^{\mu\rho}{}_{\mu}K^{\nu}{}_{\rho\nu}\,.

(11)

This scalar, known as the torsion scalar, plays a central role in the Lagrangian formulation of teleparallel theories. It can also be obtained from

T=\frac{1}{2}S_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}T^{\rho}{}_{\mu\nu}\,,

(12)

where
S_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}
, the superpotential, is antisymmetric in the last two indices and takes the form

S_{\rho}{}^{\mu\nu}=K^{\mu\nu}{}_{\rho}+\delta^{\mu}_{\rho}T^{\lambda\nu}{}_{\lambda}-\delta^{\nu}_{\rho}T^{\lambda\mu}{}_{\lambda}\,.

(13)

An alternative expression for the invariant in (
12
) can be written in terms of the irreducible components of the torsion tensor in (
5
) as

T=\frac{3}{2}\mathscr{A}-\frac{2}{3}\mathscr{V}+\frac{2}{3}\mathscr{T}\,,

(14)

where

\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{A}^{\mu}\mathscr{A}_{\mu}\,,\quad\mathscr{V}=\mathscr{V}^{\mu}\mathscr{V}_{\mu}\,,\quad\mathscr{T}=\mathscr{T}^{\sigma\mu\nu}\mathscr{T}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\,.

(15)

To investigate static and spherically symmetric solutions in teleparallel geometry, one must first specify the general forms of the frame (or co-frame) and the flat, metric-compatible spin connection that is compatible with the underlying static and spherically symmetric affine symmetries
[
9
,
10
]
.

Static and spherically symmetric teleparallel geometries

Working in coordinates

This invariant symmetry frame allows the derivation of the most general metric-compatible connection. By imposing the flatness condition, any static spherically symmetric teleparallel geometry can be described using the three functions in (

\displaystyle\omega_{133}=\omega_{144}=\cos\chi\sinh\psi/A_{3}\,,\quad\omega_{134}=\omega_{413}=\sin\chi\sinh\psi/A_{3}\,,

\displaystyle\omega_{234}=\omega_{423}=\sin\chi\cosh\psi/A_{3}\,,\quad\omega_{233}=\omega_{244}=\cos\chi\cosh\psi/A_{3}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\omega_{212}=\psi^{\prime}/A_{2}\,,\quad\omega_{432}=\chi^{\prime}/A_{2}\,,\quad\omega_{434}=\cot\theta/A_{3}\,.

It is important to note that we still have coordinate freedom to choose the radial component of the frame whenever

is not constant. Additionally, any choice of the arbitrary functions

and

\displaystyle=A_{1}\cosh\psi\,,

\displaystyle h^{\prime 1}{}_{\theta}=0\,,

\displaystyle h^{\prime 2}{}_{t}

\displaystyle h^{\prime 4}{}_{r}

2.2
Teleparallel scalars

The teleparallel invariants introduced in Eq. (
15
) play a crucial role in the analysis of static and spherically symmetric teleparallel geometries. Because of their presence in the Lagrangian formulations of teleparallel theories, these invariants can help determine the nature of special surfaces characterized by the explicit forms of the arbitrary functions
A_{1}
,
A_{2}
,
\chi
, and
\psi
. By substituting the tetrad (
16
) and spin connection (
2.1
), the teleparallel scalars were found to be

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}

\displaystyle=4\left(\frac{\cos\chi}{A_{3}}\right)^{2}+\frac{4\cos\chi}{A_{2}A_{3}}\left([\ln(A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2})]^{\prime}\cosh\psi+[\cosh\psi]^{\prime}\right)+\left(\frac{[\ln(A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2})]^{\prime}}{A_{2}}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{\psi^{\prime}}{A_{2}}\right)^{2},

(20a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{4\sin\chi}{3A_{3}}\right)^{2}-\frac{16\cosh\psi[\cos\chi]^{\prime}}{9A_{2}A_{3}}-\left(\frac{2\chi^{\prime}}{3A_{2}}\right)^{2},

(20b)

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{A_{3}^{2}}+\frac{[\ln(A_{1}/A_{3})]^{\prime}}{A_{2}}\left(\frac{[\ln(A_{1}/A_{3})]^{\prime}}{A_{2}}-\frac{2\cos\chi\cosh\psi}{A_{3}}\right)-\frac{2[\cos\chi\cosh\psi]^{\prime}}{A_{2}A_{3}}+\frac{(\chi^{\prime})^{2}-(\psi^{\prime})^{2}}{A_{2}{}^{2}}.

(20c)

It is also useful to consider the linear combination of these scalars that yields the torsion scalar (
14
) in terms of the arbitrary functions defining the static and spherically symmetric teleparallel geometry:

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{A_{2}{}^{2}}\left(\frac{A_{2}{}^{2}}{A_{3}{}^{2}}+[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}[\ln(A_{1}{}^{2}A_{3})]^{\prime}\right)-\frac{4}{A_{2}A_{3}}\left([\ln(A_{1}A_{3})]^{\prime}\cos\chi\cosh\psi+[\cos\chi\cosh\psi]^{\prime}\right)\,.

(21)

To describe a well-defined black-hole geometry, the invariants entering any teleparallel Lagrangian must remain finite at the local horizon. If any of these invariants diverge at that location, this implies that the local horizon and its interior are not part of the manifold. This contradicts the definition of a black hole, where the only region excluded from the manifold is the true physical singularity.

3
Expansion of null congruences

In the teleparallel framework, particle dynamics are governed by force-like equations that correspond to the geodesics of GR
[
1
]
. These equations take the following forms:

u^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}u^{\mu}=K^{\mu}{}_{\sigma\nu}u^{\sigma}u^{\nu}\,,

(22)

where
u^{\mu}
denotes the four-velocity of the particle. Let us now consider a congruence of null geodesics, whose expansion describes how a bundle of nearby null rays diverges or converges as it evolves along the geodesic direction. Let
\ell^{\mu}
and
n^{\mu}
be outgoing and ingoing null tangent vector fields to the geodesics, respectively. These vectors satisfy the null condition and are mutually normalized as follows:

\ell^{\mu}\ell_{\mu}=n^{\mu}n_{\mu}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\ell^{\mu}n_{\mu}=-1\,.

(23)

Note that these vectors also satisfy Eq. (
22
), as well as the identities

\ell^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\ell_{\nu}=n^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}=0\,.

(24)

The induced transverse metric, orthogonal to both
\ell^{\mu}
and
n^{\mu}
, is given by

m_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+2\ell_{{\text{\tiny(}}\mu}n_{\nu{\text{\tiny)}}}\,.

(25)

Since the normalization conditions in Eq. (
23
) does not uniquely determine
n^{\mu}
given
\ell^{\mu}
, or vice versa; consequently, the transverse metric is not unique
[
48
]
. Using the null vectors and transverse metric, we define the expansion scalars associated with the outgoing and ingoing directions as follows:

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=m^{\mu\nu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\ell_{\nu}+K^{\sigma}{}_{\nu\mu}\ell_{\sigma}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}=m^{\mu\nu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}+K^{\sigma}{}_{\nu\mu}n_{\sigma}\right)\,,

(26)

where
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}
measures the expansion of outgoing null geodesics, and
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}
measures the expansion of the ingoing null geodesics. The interpretation of these scalars is as follows: when positive, the associated geodesics diverge; when negative, they converge; and when equal to zero, they indicate stationary behaviour (marginal expansion)
[
49
]
. An alternative form of the expressions in Eq. (
26
) follows from Eqs. (
22
), (
24
), and (
25
), leading to

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=\nabla_{\mu}\ell^{\mu}+K^{\sigma\mu}{}_{\mu}\ell_{\sigma}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}=\nabla_{\mu}n^{\mu}+K^{\sigma\mu}{}_{\mu}n_{\sigma}\,.

(27)

These expressions show that, although
m^{\mu\nu}
depends on both the outgoing and ingoing null vectors, each expansion scalar depends only on its associated null vector.

An important application of Eq. (
27
) is the definition of an AH, which is a marginally outer trapped surface—specifically, the outermost 2-surface satisfying

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0\,,\quad\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}<0\,,\quad\Delta\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}<0\,.

(28)

Note that the condition
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0
must hold precisely on the AH, thereby identifying it as a marginally outer trapped surface. The condition
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}<0
also holds on the AH; although it does not define the AH, it ensures the surface bounds a trapped region rather than a degenerate or marginally inner-trapped region. Finally, the expansion
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}
must decrease along the ingoing null direction,
\Delta\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}<0
, in a neighbourhood immediately inside the AH. This guarantees that the AH locally encloses a region where outgoing null rays are already converging, thereby providing a stability criterion
[
49
]
.

In static and spherically symmetric teleparallel geometries determined by Eqs. (
16
) and (
2.1
), the outgoing and ingoing null vectors can be obtained by using the conditions in Eq. (
23
), and are expressed in terms of the arbitrary functions
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
as follows
[
9
]
:

\boldsymbol{\ell}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{A_{1}},\frac{1}{A_{2}},0,0\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\boldsymbol{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{A_{1}},-\frac{1}{A_{2}},0,0\right)\,.

(29)

This choice of null vectors is symmetric under time reversal and radial reflection; that is, they share the same temporal component, whereas their radial components mirror each other in the radial direction. By substituting the expressions in (
29
) into Eq. (
27
), the expansion scalars were found to be

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=-\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{A_{2}}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\,.

(30)

Note that these scalars are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign, as expected for static and spherically symmetric spacetimes when a symmetric choice of null vectors such as those in (
29
) is used. This implies that the expansion scalars share the same roots, that is,
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}(r_{h})=\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}(r_{h})=0
, where
r=r_{h}
denotes the location of a possible AH. Consequently, the condition
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}<0
in (
28
) is only valid outside the region
r=r_{h}
in this specific case. Then, a more appropriate formulation of the AH definition in the context of static and spherically symmetric geometries, when symmetric null vectors are used, is then given by

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=-\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}n{\text{\tiny)}}}=0\,,\quad\Delta\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}<0\,.

(31)

This highlights the role of the directional derivative of the outgoing expansion scalar along the ingoing null direction in characterizing the AH. By computing this explicitly, we obtain

\Delta\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=\frac{1}{A_{2}^{2}}\left([\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}-[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime\prime}\right)\,.

(32)

Both (
31
) and (
32
) depend solely on
A_{2}
, because
A_{3}
remains freely specified through an appropriate choice of the coordinate gauge. In this study, we only used the first condition in (
31
), namely
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=0
. This provides a necessary condition for the existence of an AH, which we refer to as the local horizon.

4
New general relativity

The class of theories known as NGR constitutes the first and simplest modification of TEGR explored in the literature
[
31
]
. In NGR, the coefficients associated with the irreducible torsion scalars are treated as three independent free parameters,
(c_{v},c_{a},c_{t})
, which are expected to be constrained by the experimental data. These parameters allow for deviations from the TEGR Lagrangian, corresponding to the specific values
(c_{v}=-2/3,c_{a}=3/2,c_{t}=2/3)
, thereby enabling NGR to incorporate corrections to the standard gravitational predictions provided by TEGR
[
3
]
. The general NGR Lagrangian density can be written as:

\mathcal{L}=c_{a}\mathscr{A}+c_{t}\mathscr{T}+c_{v}\mathscr{V}\,,

(33)

and the corresponding NGR action, including a matter Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}_{m}
, is given by

\mathcal{S}=\int h\left(\kappa\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)d^{4}x\,,

(34)

where
h
denotes the determinant of the tetrad and
\kappa
is a gravitational coupling constant. Note that the action (
34
) is well-defined only in regions of the manifold where the torsion scalars
\mathscr{V}
,
\mathscr{A}
, and
\mathscr{T}
appearing in the Lagrangian (
33
) remain finite.

Variation of the action with respect to the tetrad yields the FE:

\displaystyle W_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv c_{v}\left(2g_{\mu{\text{\tiny[}}\nu}\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{V}_{\rho{\text{\tiny]}}}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathscr{V}\right)+c_{a}\left(\frac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}\mathscr{A}+\frac{1}{3}\mathscr{A}_{\mu}\mathscr{A}_{\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{2}{9}\mathscr{A}^{\alpha}\left(2\epsilon_{\alpha\nu\rho\sigma}\mathscr{T}_{\mu}{}^{\rho\sigma}-\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathscr{T}_{\alpha}{}^{\rho\sigma}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(\mathscr{V}^{\rho}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}-\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma})\right)+c_{t}\left(\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}\mathscr{T}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}\mathscr{T}_{\alpha\sigma\rho}\mathscr{T}^{\alpha\rho\sigma}+\frac{8}{3}\mathscr{T}^{\rho}{}_{{\text{\tiny[}}\sigma\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}\mathscr{T}^{\sigma}{}_{{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\rho{\text{\tiny]}}}-\mathscr{V}^{\rho}\mathscr{T}_{\mu{\text{\tiny[}}\nu\rho{\text{\tiny]}}}+2\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{T}_{\mu{\text{\tiny[}}\nu\rho{\text{\tiny]}}}+2\mathscr{A}^{\alpha}\epsilon_{\alpha\sigma\rho{\text{\tiny(}}\mu}\mathscr{T}_{\nu{\text{\tiny)}}}{}^{\rho\sigma}\right)=\kappa\Theta_{\mu\nu}\,,

(35)

where the energy-momentum tensor is defined as

\Theta_{\mu\nu}=-h^{-1}\frac{\delta(h\mathcal{L}_{m})}{\delta h^{a}{}_{\rho}}h^{a}{}_{\mu}\,g_{\rho\nu}\,.

(36)

The variation of the action (
34
) with respect to a non-symmetric tetrad generally leads to non-symmetric FE (
4
). When variation is performed with respect to the spin connection, it yields FE equivalent to the antisymmetric part of the tetrad FE
[
3
]
. This reflects the underlying local Lorentz invariance of the theory, with the antisymmetric part imposing consistency conditions that ensure covariance. If the source (
36
) is symmetric, for instance, it follows that the antisymmetric part of the FE (
4
) must vanish as follows

W_{{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}=0\,.

(37)

In the TEGR case, where
c_{v}=-2/3
,
c_{a}=3/2
, and
c_{t}=2/3
, Eq. (
37
) is satisfied automatically. However, in NGR, fulfilling condition (
37
) becomes a non-trivial task.

In the followings, we present the antisymmetric and symmetric components of the vacuum NGR FE using the ansatz defined in Eqs. (
16
) and (
2.1
). To simplify the coefficient expressions and facilitate the algebraic treatment of the FE, we introduced a new set of parameters:

b_{1}=c_{t}+c_{v},\quad b_{2}=3c_{t},\quad b_{3}=-\frac{4c_{a}}{9}-c_{v}\,.

(38)

Note that the parameters
b_{1}
and
b_{3}
are coupled through
c_{v}
, whereas
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
are coupled through
c_{t}
.

4.1
Antisymmetric FE

The antisymmetric part of the FE (AFE) (
4
) is given by

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}

\displaystyle=c_{v}\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{V}_{[\mu}g_{\nu]\rho}-\frac{c_{a}}{3}\left(\frac{2}{3}\left(\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\gamma}\mathscr{T}_{\sigma}{}^{\rho\gamma}-2\epsilon_{\sigma\rho\gamma[\nu}\mathscr{T}_{\mu]}{}^{\rho\gamma}\right)\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}+\epsilon_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}(\mathscr{V}^{\rho}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}-\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma})\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{t}}{2}(\mathscr{V}^{\rho}\mathscr{T}_{\rho{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}-2\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{T}_{\rho{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny]}}})=0\,.

(39)

These equations are not automatically satisfied because of the presence of the three free parameters of the theory (
c_{v},c_{t},c_{a}
), which generally prevent the terms from cancelling out, as they do in special cases such as

b_{1}=b_{3}=0\,,

(40)

which corresponds to a constant multiple (or rescaled version) of TEGR
[
3
]
. Using the reparameterization introduced in Eq. (
38
) and the static and spherically symmetric ansatz (
19
), the AFE (
4.1
) reduce to

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny[}}12{\text{\tiny]}}}

\displaystyle:\,\frac{b_{1}}{2}\left[(A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2}/A_{2})\psi^{\prime}\right]^{\prime}+A_{1}A_{3}\left(b_{1}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\cos\chi+b_{3}[\cos\chi]^{\prime}\right)\sinh\psi=0\,,

(41a)

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny[}}34{\text{\tiny]}}}

\displaystyle:\,\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{3})\left[(A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2}/A_{2})\chi^{\prime}\right]^{\prime}-A_{1}A_{3}\left(F_{0}\cosh\psi+b_{3}\,[\cosh\psi]^{\prime}+2b_{3}\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\cos\chi\right)\sin\chi=0\,,

(41b)

where

F_{0}=b_{3}\,[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}-(b_{1}-b_{3})[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\,.

(42)

Notably, Eqs. (
41
) do not involve the parameter
b_{2}
and cannot be readily solved for
\chi
and
\psi
, mainly because
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
remain unspecified. A useful strategy for analyzing potential solutions of Eqs. (
41
) is to assume that
\chi
and
\psi
are constants, denoted
\chi=\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}
and
\psi=\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}
. Under this assumption, the AFE are reduced to

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny[}}12{\text{\tiny]}}}

\displaystyle:\,A_{1}A_{3}\left(b_{1}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\cos\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}\right)\sinh\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny[}}34{\text{\tiny]}}}

\displaystyle:\,A_{1}A_{3}\left(F_{0}\cosh\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}+2b_{3}\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\cos\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}\right)\sin\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0\,,

(43b)

Evidently, the choices
\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0
and
\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=n\pi
render both Eqs. (
41
) identically zero. However, this is not the only possible solution. Since the parameters
b_{1}
and
b_{3}
remain free, alternative scenarios may also satisfy the AFE. For instance, when
b_{1}=0
and
\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=n\pi
, Eqs. (
41
) are satisfied for any arbitrary function
\psi(r)
. This naturally raises the question of whether non-constant
\chi
and
\psi
can also satisfy the AFE under minimal restrictions on the parameters
b_{1}
and
b_{3}
, and what constraints this might impose on the arbitrary functions
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
; we explore this further in Section
5
.

Furthermore, in the TEGR limit, where
b_{1}=b_{3}=0
, the Eqs. (
41
) hold for arbitrary functions
\chi(r)
and
\psi(r)
, implying a certain degeneracy. This is an issue which we shall return in future work.

4.2
Symmetric FE in vacuum

The symmetric part of the FE (SFE) (
4
) in vacuum is given by

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny(}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny)}}}

\displaystyle=c_{v}\left(-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\mathscr{V}+g_{\mu\nu}\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{V}_{\rho}-\nabla^{\rho}\mathscr{V}_{{\text{\tiny(}}\mu}g_{\nu{\text{\tiny)}}\rho}\right)+c_{a}\left(\frac{1}{6}g_{\mu\nu}\mathscr{A}+\frac{1}{3}\mathscr{A}_{\mu}\mathscr{A}_{\nu}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{4}{9}\epsilon_{\sigma\rho\gamma{\text{\tiny(}}\mu}\mathscr{T}_{\nu{\text{\tiny)}}}{}^{\rho\gamma}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}\right)+c_{t}\left(\frac{1}{3}g_{\mu\nu}(\mathscr{T}-\mathscr{T}_{\alpha\sigma\rho}\mathscr{T}^{\alpha\rho\sigma})+\frac{8}{3}\mathscr{T}^{\rho}{}_{{\text{\tiny[}}\sigma\nu{\text{\tiny]}}}\mathscr{T}^{\sigma}{}_{{\text{\tiny[}}\mu\rho{\text{\tiny]}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2\epsilon_{\sigma\rho\alpha{\text{\tiny(}}\mu}\mathscr{T}_{\nu{\text{\tiny)}}}{}^{\rho\alpha}\mathscr{A}^{\sigma}+\nabla^{\rho}(\mathscr{T}_{\mu\nu}{}{}^{\sigma}-\mathscr{T}^{\sigma}{}_{{\text{\tiny(}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny)}}})-\frac{1}{2}\mathscr{V}^{\rho}(\mathscr{T}_{\mu\nu}{}{}^{\sigma}-\mathscr{T}^{\sigma}{}_{{\text{\tiny(}}\mu\nu{\text{\tiny)}}})\right)=0\,.

(44)

We now focus on its explicit form in the static and spherically symmetric case, for which the SFE exhibit four non-trivial components: three independent diagonal components
(1,1)
,
(2,2)
, and
(3,3)
, along with one off-diagonal component
(1,2)
, given by:

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny(}}11{\text{\tiny)}}}

\displaystyle:\,F_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{3})(\chi^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{2}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-2b_{3}\left(\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\sin\chi\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\left(b_{1}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\cos\chi+b_{3}[\cos\chi]^{\prime}\right)\cosh\psi=0\,,

(45a)

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny(}}22{\text{\tiny)}}}

\displaystyle:F_{2}-\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{3})(\chi^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{b_{1}}{2}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}+2b_{3}\left(\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\sin\chi\right)^{2}=0\,,

(45b)

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny(}}33{\text{\tiny)}}}

\displaystyle:\,F_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(b_{1}+b_{3})(\chi^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{b_{1}}{2}(\psi^{\prime})^{2}-b_{1}\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}[\cosh\psi]^{\prime}\cos\chi

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\left(b_{1}[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}\cos\chi+(b_{1}-b_{3})[\cos\chi]^{\prime}\right)\cosh\psi=0\,,

(45c)

\displaystyle W_{{\text{\tiny(}}12{\text{\tiny)}}}

\displaystyle:\,-\frac{b_{1}}{2}\left[(A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2}/A_{2})\psi^{\prime}\right]^{\prime}-A_{1}A_{3}\left(b_{1}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\cos\chi+b_{3}[\cos\chi]^{\prime}\right)\sinh\psi=0\,.

(45d)

Here, a set of functions
F_{i}
is introduced, each depending solely on the arbitrary functions
A_{j}
; that is, neither
\chi
nor
\psi
appears in any of the terms. These functions are given by

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=b_{1}[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime\prime}+(2b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime\prime}+b_{1}[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}[\ln(A_{1}{}^{1/2}A_{3}{}^{2}/A_{2})]^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\left((b_{2}-2b_{1})[\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}+(2b_{1}-3b_{2}/2)[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\right)-\left(2b_{1}-b_{2}/2\right)\left(\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\right)^{2}\,,

(46a)

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{b_{1}}{2}\left([\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}\right)^{2}+[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\left((b_{2}-2b_{1})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+(b_{2}/2-2b_{1})[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(2b_{1}-b_{2}/2\right)\left(\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\right)^{2}\,,

(46b)

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=\,(b_{2}/2-b_{1})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime\prime}-(2b_{1}-b_{2}/2)[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime\prime}-(2b_{1}-b_{2}/2)[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}[\ln(A_{1}A_{3}/A_{2})]^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}\left((b_{2}-b_{1})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+(2b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}\right)\,.

(46c)

Note that Eq. (
45d
) differs from Eq. (
41a
) by an overall negative sign. Consequently, the system (
45
) contains only three independent SFE, since satisfying Eq. (
41a
) guarantees that Eq. (
45d
) is also satisfied. Moreover, fulfilling the condition (
37
) is a necessary prerequisite before solving the SFE.

The four FE in (
45
) involve the four variables
\{A_{1},A_{2},\chi,\psi\}
, since the coordinate freedom can be used to fix
A_{3}
. In addition, the AFE impose further constraints on
\chi
and
\psi
, effectively reducing the number of independent variables to two and simplifying the problem of finding solutions. Nonetheless, solving the SFE remains challenging when
\chi
and
\psi
are treated as general functions of
r
. Even in the simplified case where
\chi
and
\psi
are taken as constants, finding explicit solutions depends heavily on the choice of
A_{3}
. In particular, we show that this task becomes more tractable in isotropic coordinates, for which
A_{3}=rA_{2}
, as demonstrated in Appendix
C
, where the exact solution found in
[
35
]
is reviewed and analysed.

4.3
NGR normalization

Consider the original form of the Lagrangian for NGR (
33
). The issue with the Lagrangian (
33
) is that, for certain values of the parameters
c_{a}
,
c_{t}
, and
c_{v}
, it becomes simply a rescaled version of TEGR. Analyzing this Lagrangian reveals two distinct sets of models to consider:

c_{v}=0\quad\text{and}\quad c_{v}\neq 0.

(47)

In the first case where
c_{v}=0
, TEGR is not recovered in any limit, and the theory reduces to a 2P model given by:

\mathcal{L}=c_{a}\mathscr{A}+c_{t}\mathscr{T}.

(48)

In the second case,
c_{v}\neq 0
. We begin by rewriting the NGR Lagrangian (
33
) in the form:

\mathcal{L}=\left(c_{a}+\frac{9}{4}c_{v}\right)\mathscr{A}+(c_{t}+c_{v})\mathscr{T}-\frac{3}{2}c_{v}T.

(49)

The Lagrangian can then be normalized by dividing by
-\frac{3}{2}c_{v}
, and in terms of the
b
-parameters (2), it is expressed as:

-\frac{2}{3c_{v}}\mathcal{L}=\alpha\mathscr{A}+\beta\mathscr{T}+T,\quad\text{with}\quad\alpha=\frac{3b_{3}}{2c_{v}},\quad\beta=-\frac{2b_{1}}{3c_{v}}.

(50)

Alternatively, this is equivalent to setting
c_{v}=-2/3
in Lagrangian (
49
), which then takes the form:

\mathcal{L}=\left(-\frac{3}{2}+c_{a}\right)\mathscr{A}+\left(-\frac{2}{3}+c_{t}\right)\mathscr{T}+T.

(51)

This expression can be rewritten as:

\mathcal{L}=c_{a}\mathscr{A}+c_{t}\mathscr{T}-\frac{2}{3}\mathscr{V}.

(52)

Applying this normalization to the
b
-parameters, we obtain:

b_{1}=-\frac{2}{3}+c_{t},\quad b_{2}=3c_{t},\quad b_{3}=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{4}{9}c_{a}.

(53)

This implies that:

b_{2}=2+3b_{1}.

(54)

Note that an obvious consequence of choosing this normalization is that the definition of the
b
-parameters (
38
) simplifies into a decoupled version, given by (
53
). Furthermore, if we continue our study using this normalization, all results for the case
c_{v}=0
will be excluded. To obtain the most general results possible, we proceed with the Lagrangian (
33
) and apply the normalization at the end, which allows us to separately identify the models described by Lagrangians (
48
) and (
52
).

5
Perturbative analysis

Given the structure of the AFE and SFE, Eq. (
41
) and Eq. (
45
) in vacuum, obtaining exact solutions is not straightforward when treating
\chi
and
\psi
as arbitrary functions. Since our main goal is to investigate possible black hole geometries, we introduce a perturbative approach in this section to facilitate such an analysis.

This method, previously applied in
[
2
]
to study black hole in
f(T)
theories, is now adapted in the context of NGR. The core idea is to adopt a convenient coordinate gauge that simplifies the analysis. We then impose the necessary condition for the existence of a local horizon—namely, the vanishing of Eq. (
30
)—to extract information about the behavior of the arbitrary functions involved.
Based on this, we propose an ansatz that enables us to reformulate the AFE and determine the conditions under which they are satisfied order by order. We subsequently analyze the SFE following a similar strategy. This procedure leads to distinct solution branches, which we classify into different cases to facilitate a later examination of the behaviour of the teleparallel scalars (
20
) and (
21
).

5.1
Choice of coordinates

Exploiting the freedom in the definition of the radial coordinate
r
can significantly simplify the FE. In the static case, where all arbitrary functions are time-independent, we may introduce a new coordinate system of the form
t^{\prime}=t
and
r^{\prime}=r/A_{2}
. This transformation reduces the number of independent functions to four while preserving the diagonal structure of the tetrad
[
47
]
. Motivated by this, we adopt the coordinate choice

A_{3}=r\,.

(55)

This coordinate gauge is commonly employed in both GR and TG. In what follows, we analyze the AFE and SFE using the coordinate condition (
55
).

5.2
Local horizon

The condition for the existence of a local horizon at
r=r_{h}
is that the expansion scalar of outgoing null rays,
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}
, vanishes at that location, i.e.,
\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}(r_{h})=0

[
43
]
. For the static, spherically symmetric case under consideration, and using the coordinate choice introduced in (
55
), this coefficient takes the form

\theta_{{\text{\tiny(}}\ell{\text{\tiny)}}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{r}a_{2}\,,\quad a_{2}=\frac{1}{A_{2}}\,.

(56)

Assuming
a_{2}=0
at
r=r_{h}
, we introduce a small parameter
\epsilon>0
and expand the radial coordinate as
r=r_{h}+\epsilon
, with
\epsilon\to 0
. Under this assumption, we adopt the following ansatz:

a_{2}=\epsilon^{p}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\,\epsilon),

(57)

where
p>0
. As a consequence, the function
A_{2}=1/a_{2}
and
A_{1}
take the form

A_{2}=\frac{\epsilon^{-p}}{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\,\epsilon},\quad A_{1}=\epsilon^{q}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\,\epsilon),

(58)

where
A_{1}
is assumed to have a similar structure for consistency, with
q
being an arbitrary constant.

Since the arbitrary functions
\chi
and
\psi
play a key role in the FE, but the presence of a local horizon alone does not constrain their behaviour, we assume that these functions, defined in terms of
r=r_{h}+\epsilon
, may follow a similar expansion structure to that of (
58
). Thus, we consider:

\chi=\epsilon^{u}(\chi_{0}+\gamma_{1}\,\epsilon),\quad\psi=\epsilon^{v}(\psi_{0}+\gamma_{2}\,\epsilon)\,,

(59)

where
u
and
v
are arbitrary constants. In the following, we examine whether the AFE can be satisfied under these assumptions, considering various combinations of the possible values for
u
and
v
.

5.3
Analysis of the AFE

Building on the previously introduced ansatz for the arbitrary functions (
58
) and (
59
), we now reformulate the AFE (
41
) in terms of the perturbation parameter
\epsilon
, retaining terms up to first order. To streamline the analysis, we focus on identifying key values of the parameters
u
and
v
, particularly in the case where
u=0
and
v=0
. This leads to nine distinct cases to be investigated, categorized as follows:

1)
u>0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v>0

4)
u<0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v>0

7)
u=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v>0

2)
u>0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v<0

5)
u<0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v<0

8)
u=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v<0

3)
u>0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v=0

6)
u<0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v=0

9)
u=0\,\,\text{and}\,\,v=0

In the following, we examine whether the AFE are satisfied in each case, order by order. We focus on terms proportional to
\epsilon^{w}
with exponents
w\leq 0
, as these represent the leading-order contributions. At each order, we analyze the corresponding coefficient and impose the necessary constraints to ensure that the term vanishes. When determining the dominant terms becomes an unbounded problem, we adopt an effective strategy by comparing terms with similar exponents and identifying which one is smaller, thereby avoiding the need for an exhaustive analysis of every possibility. Following this approach, the procedure is applied sequentially, starting from the leading-order terms and progressing to less significant contributions.

Since the SFE are expected to govern the behavior of the functions
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, our primary goal here is to identify the admissible values of the parameters associated with
\chi
and
\psi
, where possible. Noting that case 9),
u=0
and
v=0
, contains most of the relevant branches, we outline the method for this case as an example and summarize the results for the remaining cases in Section
A

With the exception of the two branches that correspond essentially to the TEGR case (1 and 2), all other entries in Table

satisfy

and
\chi=n\pi
. In several cases (specifically Types 4, 7, and 9
[

]
), a spin-2 field fails to propagate in general, not just in the spherically symmetric case, indicating that these theories cannot support gravitational wave solutions.
In general, we have
c_{v}\neq 0
, and a useful normalization condition can be imposed:
b_{2}=2+3b_{1}
, though this relation does not apply to case 4.

•

Case 1 (including 1.1 and 1.2) corresponds to TEGR, a theory with a well-defined Newtonian limit and consistency with solar system observations. It is classified as Type 6 in
[
23
]
, and its ghost-free condition requires
b_{2}>0
, which is satisfied here since
b_{2}=2
.
In Table
4
, we present two different examples, cases 1.1 and 1.2, in which the scalars
\mathscr{V}
and
\mathscr{T}
exhibit unavoidable divergences at the local horizon, indicating a geometric singularity. However, this behavior does not extend to the torsion scalar
T
in general.
For case 1.1,
T
remains finite at
r_{{\text{\tiny h}}}
, suggesting that the region
r=r_{{\text{\tiny h}}}
and its interior (excluding
r=0
) are part of the spacetime manifold from the perspective of the theory’s action. In contrast, for case 1.2,
T
diverges at
r_{{\text{\tiny h}}}
, implying that the horizon (and its interior) must be excluded from the manifold domain.
Since in both cases the geometry remains divergent at the local horizon, the theory is ultimately unable to describe a well-defined black hole configuration.

•

Case 2 corresponds to the 1P Hayashi and Shirafuji model (1P-H&S) at the level of the Lagrangian. However, since
\chi=n\pi
causes the parameter
b_{3}
to decouple from the FE, the theory effectively reduces to TEGR. It is classified as Type 2 in
[
23
]
, with the ghost-free conditions requiring
b_{2}>0
and
b_{3}>0
. The condition
b_{2}>0
is satisfied with
b_{2}=2
, while
b_{3}>0
holds when
c_{a}<3/2
. As in Case 1, this theory is unable to describe a well-defined black hole configuration.

•

Case 3 corresponds to a 1P model at the level of the Lagrangian. However, due to the choice
\chi=n\pi
, the parameter
b_{3}
decouples from the FE, effectively reducing the theory to a 0P model that is distinct from TEGR and lacks a Newtonian limit. Moreover, with
b_{2}=0
, this case is classified as Type 4 in
[
23
]
, which does not support a propagating spin-2 field, implying the absence of gravitational waves. In this case, the geometry is well behaved at the local horizon, and the region
r=r_{{\text{\tiny h}}}
and its interior (excluding
r=0
) are included in the manifold.

•

Case 4 arises when
c_{v}=0
, or equivalently
b_{2}=3b_{1}
, which prevents the model from being included in the normalization. At the Lagrangian level, it corresponds to a 2P model. However, since
\chi=n\pi
causes
b_{3}
to decouple from the FE, the theory effectively reduces to a 1P model. The only remaining parameter in the FE is
c_{t}
, which appears as an overall factor. Provided
c_{t}\neq 0
, a normalization can be applied, reducing the system to a 0P model distinct from TEGR and therefore lacking a Newtonian limit.
This theory is classified as Type 5 in
[
23
]
, with ghost-free conditions requiring
b_{2}>0
and
b_{1}>-b_{3}
. Since
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
, and
b_{3}
depend on
c_{t}
and
c_{a}
, these conditions translate into the constraints
c_{t}>\tfrac{4}{9}c_{a}
and
c_{t}>0
. With an appropriate choice of parameters, the ghost-free conditions can be satisfied.
In this case, the geometry is well behaved at the local horizon, and the region
r=r_{{\text{\tiny h}}}
and its interior (excluding
r=0
) are included in the manifold.

•

Cases 5 (including 5.1 and 5.2) describe a 1P model at the level of the Lagrangian. However, as in previous cases, the condition
\chi=n\pi
causes
b_{3}
to decouple from the FE, effectively reducing the theory to a 0P model that is distinct from TEGR and lacks a Newtonian limit.
For the theory considered here, it is possible to choose
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
, and
b_{3}
such that it falls under Type 3 in
[
23
]
, where
b_{3}=-b_{1}
. For instance, choosing
c_{a}
such that
b_{3}=-2
, and taking
b_{1}=2
and
b_{2}=8
, the ghost-free conditions,
0<b_{2}<3b_{1}
or
3b_{1}<0<b_{2}
, are clearly not satisfied. Alternatively, if this theory is interpreted as a special case of Type 1 in
[
23
]
, then ghost-free modes are inherently excluded.
Consequently, these models cannot avoid ghost instabilities. Nevertheless, the geometry is well behaved at the local horizon, and the region
r=r_{{\text{\tiny h}}}
and its interior (excluding
r=0
) are included in the manifold.

7
Summary and conclusions

TEGR and the 1P-H&S family were found to be incapable of describing black hole configurations. All remaining cases (2–5) correspond to 0P models that could, in principle, be well behaved at the local horizon. However, since these models exhibit certain unphysical features, no further analysis was pursued; e.g., the lower-order conditions from the AFE and SFE were not explored. These results complement those obtained in
[
2
]
for the cases of
F(T)
gravity and TEGR. Nevertheless, further work is needed to fully understand the static, spherically symmetric, vacuum teleparallel geometries described by TEGR.

For example, it remains to be explored whether the singular behavior of torsion scalars at the local horizon can be eliminated by appropriately choosing the functions
\chi
and
\psi
, raising questions about their physical or geometrical interpretation and the conditions that might constrain them. It is also important to determine whether a well-behaved tetrad exists at the local horizon that avoids such singularities within TEGR.

Appendix C
Exact solutions

Finding exact vacuum solutions to the SFE (
45
), while leveraging insights from the analysis of the AFE regarding the arbitrary functions
\psi
and
\chi
, requires specific manipulations of (
45
).
In cases where exact solutions are feasible, such as those described in
[
17
,
35
]
, we identify a suitable linear combination of the SFE that leads to integrable expressions. We then proceed with this preferred choice, given by

E_{1}=-(W_{{\text{\tiny(}}11{\text{\tiny)}}}+W_{{\text{\tiny(}}22{\text{\tiny)}}}+2W_{{\text{\tiny(}}33{\text{\tiny)}}})\quad\text{and}\quad E_{2}=-2(W_{{\text{\tiny(}}22{\text{\tiny)}}}+W_{{\text{\tiny(}}33{\text{\tiny)}}})\,,

(C.1)

which, by utilizing Eqs. (
45
) and (
46
), have the advantage of being expressible as:

\displaystyle E_{1}:

\displaystyle\,\left[\left(A_{3}/A_{2}\right)^{2}G_{1}\right]^{\prime}+\left(A_{3}/A_{2}\right)^{2}G_{1}[\ln(A_{1}A_{2})]^{\prime}=0\,,

(C.2a)

\displaystyle E_{2}:

\displaystyle\,\left[\left(A_{3}/A_{2}\right)^{3}G_{2}\right]^{\prime}+\left(A_{3}/A_{2}\right)^{3}G_{2}\left([\ln(A_{1}A_{2}{}^{2}/A_{3})]^{\prime}+A_{2}/A_{3}\right)=G_{3}\,,

(C.2b)

where

\displaystyle G_{1}=(b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+2b_{1}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}+2b_{1}\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\cos\chi\cosh\psi\,,

(C.3a)

\displaystyle G_{2}=(2b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+(4b_{1}-b_{2})\left([\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}-\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\right)+2b_{1}\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\left(1+\cos\chi\cosh\psi\right)\,,

(C.3b)

\displaystyle G_{3}=2b_{1}\frac{A_{3}}{A_{2}}\left(1+\frac{A_{3}}{A_{2}}[\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}\right)\left(1+\cos\chi\cosh\psi\right)+2b_{3}\frac{A_{3}}{A_{2}}\left(2\sin^{2}\chi+\frac{A_{3}}{A_{2}}[\cos\chi]^{\prime}\cosh\psi\right)\,.

(C.3c)

Note that Eq. (
C.2a
) can be integrated straightforwardly, while Eq. (
C.2b
) cannot due to the presence of the term
A_{2}/A_{3}
and the dependencies of
G_{3}
on the arbitrary functions
A_{2}
,
A_{3}
,
\psi
, and
\chi
. Nevertheless, we rewrite Eqs. (
C.2a
) and (
C.2b
) in the form:

G_{1}=\frac{c_{1}A_{2}}{A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2}}\,,\quad G_{2}=\frac{\mu A_{2}}{A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2}}(c_{2}+G_{4})\,,

(C.4)

with

\mu=\exp\left[-\int\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\,dr\right]\,,\quad G_{4}=\int\frac{A_{1}A_{2}{}^{2}}{\mu A_{3}}G_{3}\,dr\,.

(C.5)

An alternative and useful expression can be obtained by subtracting the equations in (
C.4
), leading to

b_{1}[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+(2b_{1}-b_{2})\left([\ln A_{3}]^{\prime}-\frac{A_{2}}{A_{3}}\right)=\frac{\mu A_{2}}{A_{1}A_{3}{}^{2}}\left(c_{2}-c_{1}\mu^{-1}+G_{4}\right)\,.

(C.6)

In this formulation, all dependencies on
\chi
and
\psi
are retained within the function
G_{4}
. Obtaining a second integration of Eq. (
C.2a
) and performing the first integration of Eq. (
C.2b
) will depend on specific choices of
\psi
and
\chi
that also satisfy the AFE (
41
). This task becomes particularly easier with a convenient choice of
\chi
and
\psi
that sets
G_{3}=0
, along with an appropriate choice of coordinates that renders the integrand in Eq. (
C.5
) integrable, such as assuming
A_{3}(r^{\prime})=f(r^{\prime})A_{2}(r^{\prime})
for some given function
f(r^{\prime})
.

C.1
Choice of coordinates

Given the static case, where all arbitrary functions are time-independent, we introduce new coordinates of the form
t^{\prime}=t
and
r^{\prime}=A_{3}/A_{2}

[
47
]
. By adopting the so-called isotropic coordinates, this relation simplifies to

A_{3}=rA_{2}\,.

(C.7)

These coordinates render the spatial part of the metric conformally flat. In this setup, the coordinate
r
represents a radial distance in a spherically symmetric, isotropic 3-space, and is therefore generally restricted to
r\geq 0
.

This coordinate system was employed in
[
17
,
35
]
to obtain vacuum solutions in NGR, which is also the aim of this section. From this point onward, we will work with the isotropic coordinates defined in Eq. (
C.7
).

C.2
AFE

As previously discussed in Section
4.1
, when
\chi=\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}
and
\psi=\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}
are constants, the AFE undergo a significant simplification, as shown in Eq. (
43
). In the coordinates defined by Eq. (
C.7
), these equations take the form

\displaystyle\cos\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}\sinh\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0\,,

(C.8a)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2b_{3}}{r}\cos\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}+F_{4}\cosh\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}\right)\sin\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0\,.

(C.8b)

In order to satisfy Eq. (
C.8
) while ensuring that
\psi
remains real, we set
\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0
and
\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=n\pi
, leading to
\cos(n\pi)=\delta=\pm 1
.
However, this still leaves
G_{4}
with a complicated expression due to
G_{3}
, given by

G_{3}=2b_{1}r\left(1+r[\ln(rA_{2})]^{\prime}\right)(1+\delta)\,.

(C.9)

A convenient choice is to set
\delta=-1
, which requires
n
to be an odd integer. In this case,
G_{3}=0
, and
G_{4}
becomes a constant that can be absorbed into
c_{2}
in Eq. (
C.4
).

C.3
SFE

We now outline the procedure presented in
[
35
]
for finding solutions to Eq. (
C.4
). By substituting
\psi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=0
and
\chi_{{\text{\tiny 0}}}=n\pi
, where
n
is an odd integer, and adopting the coordinate choice from Eq. (
C.7
), which simplifies Eq. (
C.5
) to
\mu=r^{-1}
, the system of equations in Eq. (
C.4
) reduces to:

\displaystyle(b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+2b_{1}[\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}=\frac{c_{1}\,r^{-2}}{2A_{1}A_{2}}\,,

(C.10a)

\displaystyle(2b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}+(4b_{1}-b_{2})[\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}=c_{2}\frac{r^{-3}}{A_{1}A_{2}}\,.

(C.10b)

Eqs. (
C.10
) constitute a system for
[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}
and
[\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}
that can be algebraically solved to obtain

[\ln A_{1}]^{\prime}=\frac{(b_{2}-4b_{1})c_{1}r^{-2}+4b_{1}c_{2}r^{-3}}{2(3b_{1}-b_{2})b_{2}\,A_{1}A_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad[\ln A_{2}]^{\prime}=\frac{(2b_{1}-b_{2})c_{1}r^{-2}-2(b_{1}-b_{2})c_{2}r^{-3}}{2(3b_{1}-b_{2})b_{2}\,A_{1}A_{2}}\,.

(C.11)

Adding these equations yields a direct solution for the function
A_{1}A_{2}
, which can be written as

A_{1}A_{2}=\frac{1}{b_{2}(3b_{1}-b_{2})r}\left(b_{1}c_{1}-(b_{1}+b_{2})\frac{c_{2}}{2r}\right)+c_{3}\,,

(C.12)

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
, and
c_{3}
are integration constants. Exploiting their arbitrariness, we can conveniently choose
c_{3}
as

c_{3}=-\frac{(4b_{1}-b_{2})c_{1}^{2}}{8b_{2}(3b_{1}-b_{2})c_{2}}\,.

(C.13)

This choice of
c_{3}
allows us to rewrite Eq. (
C.12
) in a more convenient form, given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{1)}\;b_{1}\leq 0\text{ and }b_{2}<3b_{1}&\quad\text{4)}\;b_{1}>0\text{ and }b_{2}<-b_{1}\\[3.00003pt]
\text{2)}\;b_{1}\leq 0\text{ and }0<b_{2}<-b_{1}&\quad\text{5)}\;b_{1}>0\text{ and }-b_{1}<b_{2}<0\\[3.00003pt]
\text{3)}\;b_{1}\leq 0\text{ and }b_{2}>-b_{1}&\quad\text{6)}\;b_{1}>0\text{ and }b_{2}>3b_{1}\end{array}

Using the constraints in Eq. (

C.16

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\text{1)}\;\frac{1}{2}<\alpha\leq 1\text{ and }-1\leq\beta<\frac{1}{2}&\quad\text{4)}\;1<\alpha<\frac{5}{4}\text{ and }\beta<-1\\[3.00003pt]

\alpha=\frac{4-5\beta}{5-4\beta}\quad\text{or}\quad\beta=\frac{4-5\alpha}{5-4\alpha}

(C.23)

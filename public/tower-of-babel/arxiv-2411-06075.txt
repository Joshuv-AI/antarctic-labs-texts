Barrow Cosmology and Big-Bang Nucleosynthesis
Ahmad Sheykhi and Ava Shahbazi

Abstract
Using thermodynamics-gravity conjecture, we present the formal derivation of the modified Friedmann equations inspired by the Barrow entropy, S ∼ A 1 + δ / 2 S\sim A^{1+\delta/2} , where 0 ≤ δ ≤ 1 0\leq\delta\leq 1 is the Barrow exponent and A A is the horizon area. We then constrain the exponent δ \delta by using Big-Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) observational data. In order to impose the upper bound on the Barrow exponent δ \delta , we set the observational bound on | δ ​ T f T f | \left|\frac{\delta T_{f}}{T_{f}}\right| . We find out that the Barrow parameter δ \delta should be around δ ≃ 0.01 \delta\simeq 0.01 in order not to spoil the BBN era. Next we derive the bound on the Barrow exponent δ \delta in a different approach in which we analyze the effects of Barrow cosmology on the primordial abundances of light elements i.e. Helium He 4 {}^{4}\textit{He} , Deuterium D D and Lithium Li 7 {}^{7}\textit{Li} . We observe that the deviation from standard Bekenstein-Hawking expression is small as expected. Additionally we present the relation between cosmic time t t and temperature T T in the context of modified Barrow cosmology. We confirm that the temperature of the early universe increases as the Barrow exponent δ \delta (fractal structure of the horizon) increases, too.

II
Barrow entropy and Modified Friedman Equations

Let us start with a homogeneous and isotropic FRW universe with
line elements

ds^{2}={h}_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}+\tilde{r}^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}),

(2)

where
\tilde{r}=a(t)r
,
x^{0}=t,x^{1}=r
, and
h_{\mu\nu}
=diag

(-1,a^{2}/(1-kr^{2}))
represents the two dimensional metric. The
open, flat, and closed universes corresponds to
k=0,1,-1
,
respectively. The radius of the apparent horizon, which is a
thermodynamically suitable horizon for the universe, is given by

[
30
]

\tilde{r}_{A}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{H^{2}+k/a^{2}}}.

(3)

The associated temperature with the apparent horizon is defined as

[
10
,
30
]

T_{h}=-\frac{1}{2\pi\tilde{r}_{A}}\left(1-\frac{\dot{\tilde{r}}_{A}}{2H\tilde{r}_{A}}\right).

(4)

For
\dot{\tilde{r}}_{A}\leq 2H\tilde{r}_{A}
, the temperature becomes
negative which is not physically acceptable and hence one should
define
T=|\kappa|/2\pi
. Note that within an infinitesimal
internal of time
dt
one may assume
\dot{\tilde{r}}_{A}\ll 2H\tilde{r}_{A}
, which physically means that the apparent
horizon radius is fixed. Thus there is no volume change in it and
one may define
T=1/(2\pi\tilde{r}_{A})

[
31
]
. The
relation between horizon temperature and Hawking radiation has
been confirmed
[
32
]
.

We further assume the energy content of the Universe is in the
form of a perfect fluid with energy-momentum tensor,

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu},

(5)

where
\rho
and
p
are the energy density and pressure,
respectively and we assume the conservation of energy holds,
namely,
\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0
. In the background of FRW
universe, this leads to

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0,

(6)

where
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter. The work density
associated with the volume change, in an expanding universe, is
given by
[
33
]

W=-\frac{1}{2}T^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu},

(7)

where in the background of FRW background can be calculated as

W=\frac{1}{2}(\rho-p).

(8)

We also assume the first law of thermodynamics on the apparent
horizon is satisfied and has the form

dE=T_{h}dS_{h}+WdV,

(9)

where
E=\rho V
is the total energy of the Universe enclosed by
the apparent horizon, and
T_{h}
and
S_{h}
are, respectively,
the temperature and entropy associated with the apparent horizon.
Differentiating
E=\rho V
, yields

dE=4\pi\tilde{r}_{A}^{2}\rho d\tilde{r}_{A}+\frac{4\pi}{3}\tilde{r}_{A}^{3}\dot{\rho}dt.

(10)

where
V=\frac{4\pi}{3}\tilde{r}_{A}^{3}
is the volume enveloped
by a 3-dimensional sphere with the area of apparent horizon

A=4\pi\tilde{r}_{A}^{2}
. Combining with the conservation
equation (
6
), we get

dE=4\pi\tilde{r}_{A}^{2}\rho d\tilde{r}_{A}-4\pi H\tilde{r}_{A}^{3}(\rho+p)dt.

(11)

Taking differential form of Barrow entropy (
1
), we find

\displaystyle dS_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2+\delta)\left(\frac{4\pi}{A_{0}}\right)^{1+\delta/2}{\tilde{r}_{A}}^{1+\delta}\dot{\tilde{r}}_{A}dt.

(12)

Inserting relation (
8
), (
11
) and (
12
) in
the first law of thermodynamics (
9
) and using definition
(
4
) for the temperature, after some calculations, we find
the differential form of the Friedmann equation as

\frac{2+\delta}{2\pi A_{0}}\left(\frac{4\pi}{A_{0}}\right)^{\delta/2}\frac{d\tilde{r}_{A}}{\tilde{r}_{A}^{3-\delta}}=H(\rho+p)dt.

(13)

Combining with the continuity equation (
6
), arrive at

-\frac{2+\delta}{2\pi A_{0}}\left(\frac{4\pi}{A_{0}}\right)^{\delta/2}\frac{d\tilde{r}_{A}}{\tilde{r}_{A}^{3-\delta}}=\frac{1}{3}d\rho.

(14)

Integration yields

\frac{2+\delta}{2-\delta}\left(\frac{4\pi}{A_{0}}\right)^{\delta/2}\frac{1}{2\pi A_{0}}\frac{1}{\tilde{r}_{A}^{2-\delta}}=\frac{\rho}{3},

(15)

where we have set the integration constant equal to zero.
Substituting
\tilde{r}_{A}
from Eq. (
3
) we
immediately arrive at

\frac{2+\delta}{2-\delta}\left(\frac{4\pi}{{A_{0}}}\right)^{\delta/2}\frac{1}{2\pi A_{0}}\left(H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right)^{1-\delta/2}=\frac{\rho}{3}.

(16)

The above equation can be further rewritten as

\left(H^{2}+\frac{k}{a^{2}}\right)^{1-\delta/2}=\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}{3}\rho,

(17)

where the effective Newtonian gravitational constant is defined as

G_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{A_{0}}{4}\left(\frac{2-\delta}{2+\delta}\right)\left(\frac{A_{0}}{4\pi}\right)^{\delta/2}.

(18)

In this way we obtain the modified Friedmann equation inspired by
Barrow entropy. Thus, starting from the first law of
thermodynamics at the apparent horizon of a FRW universe, and
assuming that the apparent horizon area has a fractal features,
due to the quantum-gravitational effects, we derive the
corresponding modified Friedmann equation of FRW universe with any
spatial curvature. It is important to note that in this case,
there is no dark energy component emerged from the Barrow entropy
that was derived in Ref.
[
34
]
. In the limiting case where

\delta=0
, the area law of entropy is recovered and we have

and Eq. (

in Einstein gravity.

IV
Relation between cosmic time and temperature

The condition of thermal equilibrium in early universe results to
entropy conservation in a co-moving volume
[
51
]

\displaystyle s(T)a^{3}=\rm constant,

(59)

where
s(T)
is the entropy per unit co-moving volume. Taking the
derivative of the above equation with respect to time gives

\displaystyle\dot{s}(T)a^{3}+3\dot{a}a^{2}s(T)=0\mapsto\frac{ds(T)}{dt}a^{3}=-3\dot{a}a^{2}s(T).

(60)

Using modified Friedmann equation (
19
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{ds(T)}{dt}=-3\left(\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}{3}\rho\right)^{1/2-\delta}s(T).

(61)

We can write the above equation in the form

\displaystyle dt=-\frac{ds(T)}{3s(T)}\left(\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}{3}\rho\right)^{1/\delta-2}.

(62)

And hence the cosmic time
t
can be written as

\displaystyle t=-\frac{1}{3}\int\frac{{s}^{\prime}(T)}{s(T)}\left(\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}{3}\rho\right)^{1/\delta-2}dT,

(63)

where the prime stands for the derivative with respect to temperature
T
.

In particular, during any era in which the dominant constituent of the universe is a
highly relativistic ideal gas, the entropy and energy densities are given by
[
51
]

\displaystyle s(T)=\frac{2\mathcal{N}a_{B}T^{3}}{3}\;

(64)

\displaystyle\rho(T)=\frac{\mathcal{N}a_{B}T^{4}}{2},

(65)

(where
\mathcal{N}
is the number of particles and antiparticles and each spin state separately)
[
51
]
.
Inserting
\frac{{s}^{\prime}(T)}{s(T)}=\frac{3}{T}
and aforementioned definition for
\rho
into the Eq. (
63
), we obtain

\displaystyle t=-\left(\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}}{3}\right)^{1/\delta-2}\int\frac{1}{T}\left(\frac{\mathcal{N}a_{B}T^{4}}{2}\right)^{1/\delta-2}dT.

(66)

We can rewrite the above equation in the following form

\displaystyle t=-\left(\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}\mathcal{N}a_{B}}{6}\right)^{1/\delta-2}\int T^{\frac{6-\delta}{\delta-2}}dT.

(67)

Integrating yields

\displaystyle t=\frac{1}{T^{4/(2-\delta)}}\left(\frac{8\pi G_{\rm eff}\mathcal{N}a_{B}}{6}\right)^{1/\delta-2}\frac{2-\delta}{4}+\rm constant.

(68)

The above equation is the relation between the cosmic time
t
and temperature
T
in the context of the modified Barrow cosmology.
For
\delta=0
we find
t=\frac{1}{T^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{3}{16\pi G\mathcal{N}a_{B}}}
, which is the result of standard cosmology
[
51
]
.

Let us note that we have used the modified Friedmann equation which has been derived in the units where
\hbar=k_{B}=c=1
.
On the other hand, the right hand side of Eq. (
68
) should have the dimension
[\,t\,]
.
If we take into account all the constants in deriving the modified Friedmann equations, the effective gravitational constant
in Eq. (
18
) takes the form

G_{\mathrm{eff}}\equiv\frac{A_{0}}{4}\left(\frac{2-\delta}{2+\delta}\right)\left(\frac{A_{0}}{4\pi}\right)^{\delta/2}{c^{2-\delta}\over\alpha_{B}\Gamma_{B}},

(69)

where we have defined

\alpha_{B}\equiv k_{B}\left(c^{3}\over\hbar\right)^{1+(\delta/2)}\quad\,,\,\,\quad\Gamma_{B}\equiv{\hbar c\over k_{B}}.

(70)

Thus, one can easily check that the right side of Eq.
(
68
) has dimension
[\,t\,]
. In early universe, when
the constituents of the universe were photons, plus three species
of neutrinos and three of antineutrinos, plus electrons and
positrons, we have
\mathcal{N}=43/4
, so that Eq.
(
68
) yields, in cgs units,

t=\left({T\over 10^{10}K}\right)^{4\over\delta-2}F(\delta)+\rm{constant}.

(71)

Where we have defined

\displaystyle F(\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{2-\delta}{2+\delta}\right)\times(9.2\times 10^{-88})^{\delta/2}\right]^{1\over\delta-2}

(72)

\displaystyle\times{2-\delta\over 4}(0.278)^{1/(\delta-2)}

Again for
\delta=0
we have
F(\delta)\to 0.994\;sec
and the
result of standard cosmology is restored
[
51
]

\displaystyle t\to\;0.994\;sec\;\left[{T\over 10^{10}K}\right]^{-2}.

(73)

V
Closing remarks

In this paper we have used BBN data in order to find the bounds on
the free parameter
\delta
of Barrow entropy, which is an
extended entropy relation originating from the incorporation of
quantum gravitational effects on the structure of a black hole,
parameterized by
\delta
. Barrow entropy leads to modified
Firedmann equations containing extra terms. These extra terms
should be sufficiently small in order not to spoil BBN epoch. We
can summarize our results in this paper as follows:

(i) We first presented an analysis of the BBN epoch in the Barrow
cosmology and calculated the deviations of the temperature of
freeze-out in comparison to the result of standard cosmology. We
have used observational upper bound on
\left|\frac{\delta T_{f}}{T_{f}}\right|
which is the allowed deviation from GR, in
order to extract the bound on Barrow exponent
\delta
. We have
shown that the BBN constraint imposes a value for the Barrow
exponent
\delta
of the order
\delta\simeq\mathcal{O}(0.01)
. As
expected this result shows that the deformation from
Bekenstin-Hawking expression should be small.

(ii) We have also explored the consequences of Barrow cosmology on
the primordial light elements formation. The analysis of the light
elements
{}^{4}\textit{He}
,
{}^{2}\textit{H}
,
{}^{7}\textit{Li}
shows that the range of variability of

\delta
, using the bounds (
51
), (
54
) and (
56
), does not overlap.

(iii) We have presented the relation between cosmic time
t
and
the temperature
T
of the universe in the modified Barrow
cosmology. We have then plotted the behavior of temperature as a
function of cosmic time
t
for different
\delta
. The result
shows that as the fractal structure increases, the temperature of
the early universe increases, too.

Following the present study, we can use BBN data in order to
impose constraints on the free parameter of other entropy
corrected cosmological models such as modified cosmology inspired
by zero-point length, or the modified Kaniadakis cosmology. We can
also investigate the relation between time and temperature in
these models. We leave these for the future investigations.

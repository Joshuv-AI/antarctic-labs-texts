Free-Space Characterization Setup for Low-Loss UV-261nm Waveguides in Aluminum Oxide
Vahram Voskerchyan, Ward Hendriks, Sonia M. Garcia-Blanco

Abstract




1
University of Twente, Dept. of Integrated Optical Systems (IOS)

Summary.
Here, we report a characterization setup for low-loss integrated photonic waveguides in aluminum oxide, tailored for deep UV applications. The setup includes a 261nm UV light source, objective lenses for free-space coupling, an automated stage, and a UV camera. The measured losses for the aluminum oxide waveguides in the UV-261nm range were calculated to be 4.9485 dB/cm. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the setup in achieving low-loss characterization of integrated photonics waveguides for deep UV applications.

Keywords:
integrated photonics, aluminum oxide, deep UV, waveguide characterization, low-loss waveguides

In this study, we successfully characterized and coupled light into aluminum oxide waveguides measuring 282 nm in width and 100 nm in thickness. Utilizing a UVC measurement setup, we evaluated the transmission efficiency and optical loss of these waveguides, emphasizing their performance in UVC applications. The results indicate effective light propagation, demonstrating the potential of aluminum oxide waveguides for integration into future photonic systems. This work demonstrates an effective method for characterizing UVC devices and calculating their associated losses. The findings highlight that the waveguides exhibit low optical losses, which paves the way for the development of new UVC applications.

Setup

Currently, no fiber coupling methods are available for efficient coupling of UVC light into waveguides due to several challenges. Standard fibers are not suitable for UVC light due to material limitations, such as the lack of transmission capability at these wavelengths. Specialized fibers, such as hollow-core fibers, are an option but are expensive and difficult to manufacture with the required precision
[
2
]
. Additionally, the handling and integration of these fibers into practical systems pose significant challenges, making them less viable for UVC applications. As a result, free space coupling is currently the most feasible approach for efficient coupling of UVC light into waveguides.
The relatively low refractive index difference between the air and waveguide core facilitated smooth coupling, as it minimized the mismatch between the incident beam and the waveguide mode. This ensured a straightforward coupling process without the need for complex adjustments or specialized fibers.

To optimize this coupling efficiency, a mode overlap analysis was performed to select the most suitable objective lens. This analysis helped assess the alignment and interaction between the light beam from the objective lens and the waveguide core. The results of this analysis indicated that the measured overlap between the beam from the objective lens and the waveguide core was 46%, suggesting a reasonably efficient coupling. 4
Conclusion

In conclusion, the optimized fabrication process significantly improved the performance of the UV waveguides, reducing the losses to a final value of 4.948 dB/cm in the spiral section. This achievement sets a new record for integrated photonics at this wavelength and demonstrates an effective approach to characterizing these devices, paving the way for further advancements in UV waveguide technology.

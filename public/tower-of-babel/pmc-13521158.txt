Diffusion MRI Radiomics Predict Renal Function Decline in ADPKD
Authors: Matteo Bargagli, Luisa Altabella, Marina Fedon Vocaturo, Riccardo Negrelli, Maria Chiara Ambrosetti, Giovanni Puppini, Carlo Cavedon, Martin Scoglio, Daniel G. Fuster, Pietro Manuel Ferraro
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionAutosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) is the most common hereditary kidney disorder and a leading cause of end-stage kidney disease (ESKD). Current risk stratification relies on height-adjusted total kidney volume (ht-TKV), which captures cyst burden but not alterations in the noncystic parenchyma. Radiomics enables extraction of quantitative features reflecting tissue microstructure across both cystic and noncystic kidney compartments. We investigated whether radiomic features from apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) maps improve prediction of renal function decline in ADPKD.MethodsWe analyzed 112 patients with ADPKD from the Bern ADPKD Registry with baseline magnetic resonance imaging (MRI, T1-, T2-, and diffusion-weighted). Kidneys were manually segmented, and radiomic features were extracted using PyRadiomics. Features were selected using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator, and models were developed using support vector machines with 20-fold cross-validation. The outcome was rapid renal function decline (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] ≤ -3 ml/min per 1.73 m2/year) over a median follow-up of 5.5 years.ResultsADC-based radiomics provided the highest area under the curve (AUC: 0.82), compared with clinical (0.77) and T1/T2w models (0.77). Ensemble models integrating ADC, T1/T2w, and clinical data achieved the best AUC (0.85) with improved calibration. Net reclassification improvement (NRI) analyses demonstrated improved risk reclassification when ADC-derived information was incorporated into clinical and conventional MRI-based models.ConclusionADC-derived radiomic features showed promising prognostic value for predicting renal function decline in ADPKD and provided incremental information beyond ht-TKV and current clinical standards when integrated with clinical and conventional MRI-based models, capturing both cystic and noncystic tissue alterations. If confirmed in independent cohorts, diffusion MRI-based radiomics may support earlier risk stratification and treatment decision-making in ADPKD.

ADPKD is the most common hereditary kidney disorder, frequently associated with ESKD.1 It is characterized by the formation and progressive enlargement of fluid-filled cysts in both kidneys, leading to disruption of kidney architecture and often decline in kidney function.2 MRI is the current gold standard for disease assessment, offering superior accuracy for the quantification of TKV, an established imaging biomarker predictive of kidney function decline and used for patient stratification in clinical trials.3,4

ht-TKV, as captured in the Mayo Imaging Classification, is widely used to estimate disease progression risk already in early stages, when eGFR is still preserved. However, TKV-based metrics predominantly reflect cyst burden and may fail to capture relevant pathological changes in the noncystic tissue compartment, which can harbor interstitial fibrosis, tubular atrophy, microcysts, or perfusion deficits.5,6

Other MRI-derived biomarkers have been proposed to refine risk prediction in ADPKD. Automated cyst segmentation has enabled the derivation of novel structural markers, such as total cyst volume, which were shown to outperform ht-TKV in predicting eGFR decline.7 Although these cyst-based metrics provide more detailed information on cyst burden and morphology, they remain primarily structural. Radiomics, the computational extraction of quantitative features from medical images, has recently emerged as a promising tool to capture further subtle parenchymal and microstructural alterations beyond visible cyst morphology and conventional visual assessment.3,8 Several studies have demonstrated that radiomic features, such as texture, intensity distribution, and spatial organization, can reflect subtle structural changes and correlate with kidney function decline. Notably, texture-based features derived from T2-weighted MRI have been shown to predict progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD) stage 3 and a 30% decline in eGFR over 8 years, even in patients with preserved kidney function at baseline.3

Recent evidence has further underscored the potential of diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) to provide early insights into ADPKD pathophysiology.9,10 DWI captures microscopic water motion within kidney tissues and is sensitive to changes in microstructure, fibrosis, and tissue composition. Although its use in ADPKD remains limited compared with other kidney diseases, preliminary studies have been used to characterize the noncystic kidney parenchyma, revealing a higher diffusivity and lower pseudo-diffusion fraction compared with healthy tissue. These findings highlight the promise of DWI and ADC mapping (a quantitative parameter derived from DWI that reflects water diffusion within tissues) in detecting early tissue alterations before measurable changes in blood or urine markers occur.8

A small study by Lussana et al.5 on 14 patients with ADPKD focused on noncystic tissue analysis using multiparametric MRI including intravoxel incoherent motion-derived diffusion maps (an advanced model of DWI separating diffusion from microperfusion effects), hypothesizing that radiomic descriptors extracted from intravoxel incoherent motion maps might be more robust and reproducible than those from T1- or T2-weighted sequences. Importantly, segmentation strategy was shown to affect reproducibility, underscoring the need for standardized pipelines in future studies.

Despite these promising findings, the clinical utility of radiomics in ADPKD remains limited by heterogeneity in image acquisition, segmentation protocols, and the lack of large-scale, longitudinal datasets to validate predictive models.4

To address these gaps, we leveraged the Bern ADPKD Registry, a large, well-characterized prospective observational cohort of Swiss patients with ADPKD with standardized serial MRI protocols, laboratory assessments, and detailed clinical follow-up. In this study, we explored the potential of radiomic features extracted from baseline multiparametric MRI, including T1-, T2-, and diffusion-weighted (ADC) images, in combination with clinical data, to predict rapid decline in renal function in patients with ADPKD. Using a machine learning framework, we systematically evaluated different combinations of imaging and clinical features. Our goal was to assess the discriminative power of each modality, alone and in combination, and to identify the most informative sources for early risk stratification. Particular attention was given to ensemble approaches that integrate multiple feature sets, aiming to improve model robustness and interpretability.

Methods

Study Population

The Bern ADPKD Registry is a prospective observational cohort study of patients with ADPKD at the Department of Nephrology and Hypertension at the Bern University Hospital, Bern, Switzerland, initiated in 2015.11 Inclusion criteria are as follows: age ≥ 18 years, ADPKD diagnosis based on the Ravine criteria12, and signed informed consent. Kidney replacement therapy including transplantation or dialysis are exclusion criteria. For this analysis, only patients with at least 3 years of follow-up after the baseline visit were included.

The Bern ADPKD Registry adheres to the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the ethical committee of the Kanton of Bern (approval BE 124/15).

Tolvaptan was introduced in Switzerland in November 2016 and can be prescribed to patients with ADPKD if the following criteria are met: age ≥18 years, typical class I ADPKD, TKV ≥ 750 ml, CKD stages G1 to G3 (Mayo class 1D and 1E) or CKD stages G2 and G3 (Mayo class 1C). If all criteria were fulfilled, candidate patients < 55 years of age were advised to initiate tolvaptan treatment. However, the final decision on tolvaptan treatment initiation was left to the treating physician. Treatment was always initiated with the lowest split-dose regimen of 45 and/or 15 mg and up-titrated in monthly intervals to 60 and/or 30 mg and ultimately to 90 and/or 30 mg, as tolerated by the patient.

All patients were tolvaptan-naïve at the time of baseline MRI acquisition. Patients who initiated tolvaptan after the baseline visit were included in the analysis irrespective of subsequent treatment exposure.

Data Collection and Measurements

Registry participants had a baseline visit and yearly visits thereafter. At each visit, demographic, anthropometric and clinical information (medication, renal, and extrarenal ADPKD complications) was collected and a physical examination performed. Baseline MRI imaging was performed standardized for all 112 enrolled patients using an MRI Siemens Healthineer Magnetom Vida Fit 3T (Siemens Healthcare GmbH, Erlangen, Germany) machine. The imaging protocol includes the following: T2 HASTE (Repetition time/echo time = 1200/95 ms, average 1, matrix 256x256 in plane resolution 1.25 mm2 slice thickness 5 mm), T1 VIBE DIXON (Repetition time/echo time = 5.36/2.46 ms, average 1, matrix 320x320 in plane resolution 1.25 mm2 slice thickness 3.5 mm), and diffusion weighted imaging with echo planar imaging sequence (Repetition time/echo time = 3800/51 ms, matrix 216x268 in plane resolution 1.49 mm2 slice thickness 5 mm, b value of 0 and 700 s/mm2). TKV was determined from T2-weighted anatomical images using the ellipsoid formula (length × width × depth × π/6). Height-adjusted TKV (ht-TKV) was subsequently calculated and Mayo Imaging Classification was assigned.13

Standardized blood and urine analyses, including a 24-h urine collection, were performed at baseline and then every year. All blood samples were collected after at least 6 h of fasting in the morning and immediately processed. Urine and blood analyses were performed at the Central Laboratory of the Bern University Hospital using standard laboratory methods. No assay changes occurred during the study. The creatinine- and cystatin-based European Kidney Function Consortium equation was used to obtain the eGFR.14 Data were entered manually into the electronic registry database by the responsible study nurse and double checked by an independent database manager for accuracy. The following clinical variables related to the baseline visit were collected and included in the analysis: age, biological sex, ht-TKV, Tolvaptan treatment, eGFR, and body mass index.

Image Analysis

A customized Python script (version 3.8.15) was developed to compute the ADC maps from the DWI sequences using a monoexponential signal decay model.

For each subject, whole kidneys were manually segmented on T2-weighted images using 3D Slicer (version 5.2.2) by 1 of 2 experienced radiologists (each with > 10 years of experience in abdominal imaging). T2-weighted images served as the anatomical reference because of their optimal delineation of kidney boundaries and cyst morphology. All segmentations were subsequently reviewed for anatomical plausibility. The T1-weighted (water-only DIXON) and ADC images were then rigidly registered to the corresponding T2-weighted images in 3D Slicer using the Transforms module. Registration was performed via a 4×4 homogeneous transformation matrix, incorporating translation and rotation components. An example of the segmentation masks obtained across the 3 imaging sequences is shown in Supplementary Figure S1.

Outcome Definition

The eGFR was calculated using the European Kidney Function Consortium equation, integrating both serum creatinine and cystatin C as biomarkers. Q-values were rescaled for white European subjects, consistent with the composition of the cohort. These values were further adjusted for age and sex. The computations were performed using the R package nephro (version 1.5; R version 4.4.2, R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). Measurement errors in biomarker estimation were propagated according to the exponential relationship between biomarkers and eGFR, to assess uncertainty in eGFR estimation.15 Finally, a weighted linear model was fitted using the stats package (R version 4.4.2), and the outcome was defined as the annual eGFR slope, categorized as decline (≤ -3 ml/min per 1.73 m2/year) or stable (> -3 ml/min per 1.73 m2/year). This threshold is consistent with definitions of rapid disease progression used in international guidelines and major clinical trials in ADPKD,1 to identify patients who may benefit from closer monitoring and disease-modifying therapies.

Features Extraction

Before feature extraction, perturbed segmentation masks were generated from the original ones to assess feature stability under small variations of the region of interest. This procedure was implemented in Python (version 3.7.10, Python Software Foundation, Beaverton, OR) using the SimpleITK library (version 2.2.1, Insight Software Consortium, Clifton Park, NY), by applying morphological operations of binary dilation and erosion with spherical kernels of radii 2, 4, 6, and 2 mm, respectively.

Feature extraction was performed on both the original and perturbed masks using PyRadiomics (version 3.1.0), including 14 shape-based, 18 first-order, and 73 textural features. Each image type (T1w, T2w, ADC) was processed independently, with dedicated extraction configurations. Features were extracted separately for the left and right kidneys and subsequently averaged. According to the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative guidelines,16 radiomic features were extracted in 2D and subsequently aggregated across slices to account for the limited slice thickness of the acquired images, which would not allow for a meaningful volumetric texture representation. Before extraction, all images were resampled to median voxel spacing using B-spline interpolation to preserve spatial detail. To enhance image information, convolutional filters were applied in accordance with the Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative guidelines,16 including a Laplacian of Gaussian filter (σ = 3, 5, 7) to emphasize blobs and ridges, and a single-level Haar wavelet decomposition.

Normalization was applied only to T1- and T2-weighted images representing raw signal intensity. Grey-level discretization strategies followed Image Biomarker Standardization Initiative recommendations16, as follows: fixed bin numbers for images with arbitrary intensity units (8, 16, 32, 64, and 128 bins for T1- and T2-weighted), and fixed bin widths for images with physical units (ADC maps; bin widths of 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800 × 10-6 mm2/s). The feature extraction parameters employed in the analysis and those achieving the highest stability are reported in the Supplementary Materials (Supplementary Table S1).

Model Development

The analysis was conducted in R within a 20-fold cross-validation framework. Considering the dataset size, a cross-validation approach was preferred over a single random derivation–test split in order to avoid unreliable estimates of model performance, as previously reported.17,18

A schematic overview of the workflow for radiomic data extraction, feature reduction, model training, and validation is shown in Figure 1. This workflow follows a cascade selection strategy, as suggested by Papanikolaou et al.19 where stability, variance, and correlation filtering are assessed sequentially. Feature selection is performed separately from model training, with least absolute shrinkage and selection operator used as an embedded feature selector and support vector machine (SVM) adopted as the final classifier because of its ability to capture nonlinear decision boundaries. Feature stability was first assessed by excluding features with an intraclass correlation coefficient below 0.75, which were considered unstable under segmentation perturbations. This step was performed using the R package irr (version 0.84.1). For each image type, the discretization level was selected to maximize the number of stable features.

Dimensionality reduction was then applied by removing irrelevant features showing zero or near-zero variance and redundant features with high pairwise correlation (ρ > 0.85). The correlation threshold was determined through nested cross-validation hyperparameter optimization to ensure performance stability across folds. This procedure was implemented using the R package caret (version 6.0-94). Feature selection was performed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression implemented via glmnet (version 4.1-8, R Foundation), with a 10-fold nested cross-validation used to determine the optimal regularization parameter (λ). Subsequently, a SVM with a radial basis function kernel was trained for classification using the R package e1071 (version 1.7-16).

All preprocessing and model selection steps (feature stability assessment, dimensionality reduction, feature selection, and model training) were performed exclusively within the training folds. Model performance, expressed as the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), was evaluated on the corresponding test folds using the R package pROC (version 1.18.5). This procedure was repeated across all 20 folds to obtain robust performance estimates.

Model performance was summarized by the mean AUC across the 20 folds, along with the corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI). To complement AUC-based model evaluation, continuous NRI analyses were performed for selected clinically relevant model comparisons to assess whether the addition of ADC-derived radiomic information improved risk reclassification.20 Several SVM models were trained to evaluate the predictive performance of different feature subsets. Each SVM model was optimized independently using grid search within the nested cross-validation framework. Hyperparameters such as the regularization parameter and kernel-specific parameters were tuned at each training fold to identify the optimal configuration for that particular training subset.

Initial models used only radiomic features from a single image type (T1/T2w, ADC, or combined T1/T2w + ADC). A separate model was trained using baseline clinical variables only (age, sex, ht-TKV, Tolvaptan treatment, eGFR, and body mass index). The selected clinical predictors overlap with variables used in Mayo-based renal function decline risk stratification. Because a proportion of patients were receiving tolvaptan therapy, which may influence the trajectory of eGFR over time, treatment exposure was included as a covariate rather than applying Mayo-based prediction algorithms directly.21 Subsequent models integrated radiomic and clinical features. Considering the size of the dataset and the integration of information from multiple sources (T1-/ T2-weighted, and ADC images, as well as clinical data), different approaches were explored. The first approach (referred to as the combined model) consisted of training a single SVM classifier on a combined feature space obtained by integrating selected features from the various imaging modalities and clinical information. Given the risk of overfitting because of the increased number of features relative to the sample size, an ensemble modelling approach was also explored. In this strategy, separate SVM models were trained using features from each modality, with the aim of reducing model complexity while preserving as much information as possible. The predicted probabilities from the individual models were then combined to obtain the final prediction.

Radiomic Features Selection

Considering the 3 image types (T1/T2w and ADC) together with all applied filters, a total of 2246 radiomic features were extracted for each patient.

Feature selection was performed through a multistep process designed to ensure robustness, relevance, and nonredundancy of the final feature set. The first step involved assessing feature stability under segmentation perturbations using the intraclass correlation coefficient; only features with intraclass correlation coefficient ≥ 0.75 were retained, resulting in a mean of 581 and 1315 features per fold for ADC and T1/T2w images, respectively.

To further reduce redundancy, a correlation analysis was conducted, and features with a Pearson correlation coefficient greater than 0.85 were excluded, yielding a mean of 61 and 125 nonredundant features per fold for ADC and T1/T2w images, respectively.

Finally, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression was applied to identify the most predictive features. This regularization method, implemented with a nested 10-fold cross-validation for optimal lambda (λ) selection, produced a final subset of 8 radiomic features for ADC and 7 features for T1/T2w, which were subsequently used for model training.

Model Performance and Comparison

The predictive performance of the different models was evaluated using 20-fold cross-validation. Among the single-source models, the best performance was achieved by the ADC-based model, with a mean AUC of 0.82 (95% CI: 0.74–0.90), compared with 0.77 (95% CI: 0.68–0.86) for the clinical model and 0.77 (95% CI: 0.68–0.85) for the T1/T2w model.

Ensemble models, in which radiomic feature selection was performed separately for each classifier and the predicted probabilities were subsequently averaged across models, tended to show higher performance. The ADC + T1/T2w + clinical features ensemble achieved the highest mean AUC of 0.85 (95% CI: 0.79–0.90). However, the differences compared with the best-performing single-source or paired models were relatively small, with overlapping confidence intervals. All performance metrics are summarized in Table 2. To further compare model performance beyond AUC, continuous NRI analyses were performed (Supplementary Table S3). Ensemble models incorporating ADC-derived radiomic information showed significant improvements in risk reclassification compared with both clinical-only (NRI: 0.39, 95% CI: 0.03–0.76, P < 0.05) and T1/T2w-only (NRI: 0.41, 95% CI: 0.05–0.77, P < 0.01) approaches, whereas the ensemble model combining ADC, T1/T2w, and clinical features showed larger improvements compared with both the clinical model (NRI: 0.78, 95% CI: 0.44–1.12, P < 0.001) and the T1/T2w model (NRI: 0.56, 95% CI: 0.20–0.91, P < 0.01). Additional classification metrics are reported in Supplementary Table S4, showing that radiomics-based models generally achieved higher balanced accuracy and sensitivity compared with the clinical model alone. Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective study to incorporate radiomic features from ADC maps, quantitative diffusion MRI measures that reflect the mobility of water molecules within kidney tissue, in addition to T1- and T2-weighted images to predict functional renal decline in polycystic kidney disease. Here we show that models based on ADC-derived features, either alone or combined with clinical variables, consistently achieved high predictive accuracy. This represents a step beyond former evidence on diffusion imaging in ADPKD, which have mainly focused on cross-sectional associations or methodological reproducibility without testing longitudinal prediction.

The validity of quantitative imaging features from conventional MRI sequences in predicting renal function decline in ADPKD has been previously suggested. Early texture-based analyses indicated that imaging heterogeneity may carry prognostic information beyond kidney volume.3 Among radiomics-based studies, 1 of the largest investigations using T1- and T2-weighted imaging combined with clinical variables included 100 patients with ADPKD and reported an AUC of 0.74,25 which is in line with our finding (0.77) using the same sequences. Importantly, including clinical information generally improved predictive performance in already available models.26, 27, 28 Recent deep learning approaches have explored automated pipelines integrating kidney segmentation and volumetric biomarkers with clinical variables, showing strong correlations between predicted and observed eGFR decline.29,30 Given the relatively limited sample sizes typical of ADPKD cohorts, deep learning models may be prone to overfitting and reduced generalizability when trained on small datasets and should therefore be interpreted with caution.31,32 These pipelines also primarily rely on anatomical imaging features, and none of these studies incorporated diffusion imaging.

Indeed, radiomic features from different MRI techniques have been used for tissue characterization, to distinguish cystic from noncystic regions, and to improve segmentation, with diffusion MRI proving particularly valuable in both ADPKD5 and other kidney diseases, including CKD and kidney allograft recipients.33,34 Previous diffusion-based imaging studies in ADPKD have mainly reported cross-sectional associations rather than longitudinal outcome prediction. Lupica et al.35 found that fractional anisotropy and ADC values derived from diffusion tensor imaging correlated with eGFR, creatinine, and kidney volume, indicating their potential as markers of microstructural disorganization, though the analysis was restricted to a very small cohort. Lussana et al.5 assessed the reproducibility of radiomic features from multiparametric MRI, showing that intravoxel incoherent motion-derived maps were more robust than T1- or T2-weighted sequences and displayed moderate correlations with age and eGFR, but their study focused on methodological reliability rather than clinical endpoints. Hence, our results confirm for the first time the longitudinal predictive validity of ADC-derived features in ADPKD. In our cohort, ADC-based models alone achieved the highest AUC (0.82), compared with clinical-only (0.77) and T1/T2w-based models (0.77). Moreover, complementary NRI analyses demonstrated significant improvements in risk reclassification when ADC-derived information was incorporated into clinical or conventional MRI-based models, supporting the incremental prognostic value of diffusion MRI radiomics beyond conventional approaches.

In the context of multimodal integration, ensemble models, in which feature selection is performed independently within each modality, demonstrated improved performance, particularly when combining all three feature sources (ADC, T1/T2w, and clinical). This strategy, based on averaging the predicted probabilities from individual models, may provide a more balanced prediction, as reflected by slightly narrower confidence intervals. Conceptually, the ensemble can be understood as a consensus-based system, similar to a majority vote, but implemented through a soft voting approach that accounts not only for the final decision of each model but also for the confidence of its prediction.

If confirmed in larger datasets and after external validation, these results may indicate that integrating radiomic features from multiple imaging modalities together with clinical data through ensemble learning might preserve complementary information while reducing the risk of overfitting. This is consistent with several recent radiomics studies,23,36,37 which have shown that ensemble approaches effectively combine the strengths of individual models to improve overall predictive accuracy.

Overall, ADC emerged as the most informative imaging modality for predicting renal function decline in our ADPKD cohort. Its value lies in capturing both cystic areas (characterized by isotropic diffusion and high ADC values) and noncystic tissue (with lower ADC values), thus providing a more comprehensive representation of disease burden across the entire renal parenchyma and cystic compartments,9,38 even when radiomic features are extracted from whole-kidney segmentations. Radiomic features derived from ADC maps consistently contributed to the best-performing models, suggesting that ADC encodes biologically relevant information, likely related to tissue cellularity and microstructural diffusion properties, not equally represented in conventional anatomical sequences or clinical variables.10

The Mayo Imaging Classification, currently the clinical gold standard for risk stratification and treatment decisions such as initiation of tolvaptan,39,40 relies on ht-TKV and therefore primarily reflects cyst-driven kidney enlargement. Although this approach is highly informative for assessing overall disease burden because of cyst growth, it does not fully account for microstructural alterations within the noncystic tissue compartment. In contrast, ADC-based radiomics based on whole-kidney segmentation capture diffusion-related changes that are independent of size-based metrics. Our clinical model incorporated ht-TKV alongside key clinical predictors, substantially overlapping with the Mayo Classification prediction tool.21 The incremental prognostic value of ADC-derived radiomic features suggests that integrating standard imaging-based disease burden with parenchymal diffusion signatures could provide a more comprehensive risk stratification in ADPKD. Although radiomics pipelines are not yet part of routine clinical workflows, advances in automated kidney segmentation may facilitate clinical implementation by improving scalability, reproducibility, and reducing operator dependency, thereby supporting personalized decision-making. Recent deep learning–based segmentation approaches may further enable automated separation of cystic and noncystic tissue compartments for future radiomics applications.41 Such approaches may enable earlier identification of high-risk patients, allowing closer follow-up, timely initiation of disease-modifying therapy, and improved selection for clinical trials.

Key strengths of this study are rigorous feature selection, cross-validation, and the large single-center cohort with standardized MRI acquisitions performed on the same scanner and harmonized laboratory measurements, which reduce technical variability and ensure internal consistency. The methodological robustness of the radiomics pipeline was further assessed using the METRICS framework and achieved an excellent rating.24

Limitations are mainly the lack of an external validation cohort, which restricts assessment of generalizability across different scanners, acquisition protocols, and segmentation workflows; missing genetic information; the use of whole-kidney segmentations precluding assessment of the relative contribution of cystic and noncystic tissue compartments to model performance, and the estimation of TKV using the ellipsoid method rather than planimetric techniques. Furthermore, although all patients were tolvaptan-naïve at baseline and treatment exposure was included as a covariate, residual treatment-related effects could have influenced long-term eGFR trajectories. In addition, diffusion imaging was acquired using two b-values, which did not allow application of intravoxel incoherent motion modeling. Multi–b-value diffusion protocols may have enabled separation of diffusion and microperfusion components and could provide additional radiomic biomarkers. Future prospective studies should confirm these results in multicenter cohorts with independent validation datasets. Extended multiparametric MRI approaches combining diffusion imaging with additional functional sequences, as well as radiomic analysis of the noncystic parenchyma, may further improve characterization of tissue microstructure beyond cyst burden.42,43

Nonetheless, our results provide a proof-of-concept that radiomics based on ADC features can enhance prediction models in ADPKD.

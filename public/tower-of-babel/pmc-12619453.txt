Low-dose oral misoprostol vs. oxytocin in premature rupture of membranes at ≥ 36 weeks: a comparison of labor induction outcomes and postpartum hemorrhage risk
Authors: Yike Yang, Zhiheng Yu, Huifeng Shi, Yan Wang, Yangyu Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPremature rupture of membranes (PROM) at ≥ 36 weeks elevates intrauterine infection risks, making timely labor induction the preferred intervention. While oxytocin infusion remains the conventional approach, its efficacy proves suboptimal in cases with unfavorable cervical conditions. Oral low-dose misoprostol emerges as a promising solution.AimThe primary objective of this study was to compare the general obstetric delivery information and outcomes following the administration of oxytocin infusion or oral low-dose misoprostol solution for labor induction in PROM at ≥ 36 weeks women with an unfavorable cervical condition.MethodsA retrospective cohort study was conducted at a tertiary general hospital in China from March 2020 to November 2024, comparing oxytocin infusion and oral administration of 25 µg misoprostol every 2 h for labor induction in PROM at ≥ 36 weeks women with a Bishop score < 6. A total of 622 mothers were enrolled, with 297 in the oxytocin group and 325 in the misoprostol group.ResultsThe rates of vaginal delivery and failed induction of labor were comparable between the two groups. Moreover, the duration of first stage of labor time was shorter in the misoprostol group relative to that of the oxytocin group (9.2 ± 4.8 h vs. 10.3 ± 5.0 h, p = 0.03), as well as the duration of administration to vaginal delivery (22.0 ± 10.8 h vs. 24.4 ± 12.1 h, p = 0.03). The rate of vaginal delivery in 24 h was high following induction with oral misoprostol [61.2% (137/224) vs. 51.2% (103/201), p = 0.04]. Notably, the incidence of uterine hyperstimulation, fetal distress, or suspected intrauterine infection was not significantly different between the two groups; however, there was a markedly lower incidence of overall postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) [8.6% (28/325) vs. 17.8% (53/297), p < 0.001] and severe PPH following vaginal delivery [0.4% (1/224) vs. 3.5% (7/201), p = 0.02] observed in the misoprostol group. Logistic regression analysis revealed that misoprostol administration was negatively associated with the occurrence of PPH (aOR: 0.42; 95% CI: 0.23 ~ 0.76, p < 0.01).ConclusionsIn this retrospective single-center study, oral low-dose misoprostol yielded similar effects as oxytocin infusion on labor induction in PROM at ≥ 36 weeks pregnancies with unfavorable cervix, with a potentially lower risk of PPH. Nevertheless, these findings need to be further investigated through prospective multicenter studies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08402-5.

Introduction

Premature rupture of membranes (PROM) refers to the rupture of fetal membranes before the onset of labor [1]. Term PROM occurs in about 8% of pregnancies and significantly increases the risk of intrauterine infection, which worsens the longer the membranes remain ruptured. Induction is generally preferred over expectant management for term PROM [2, 3]. Traditionally, “term” is defined as 37 to 41 weeks of gestation. However, because neonatal outcomes at 36 weeks and beyond are largely comparable [4], many clinical practices now consider the management of PROM to be equally important starting at 36 weeks’ gestation [5].

The decision to induce labor relies on an assessment of cervical favorability, traditionally evaluated using the Bishop score [6]. In cases where the cervix is deemed favorable (Bishop score ≥ 6), oxytocin is administered intravenously to initiate labor. Conversely, if the cervix is considered unfavorable (Bishop score < 6), prostaglandin or Foley’s balloon are commonly employed for cervical ripening. However, in patients with PROM, vaginal manipulation such as Foley’s balloon or vaginal administration of prostaglandin may increase the incidence of infection and potentially result in uterine hyperstimulation, fetal distress, and emergency Cesarean delivery.

Oxytocin is widely recognized as the most commonly employed labor induction agent worldwide and has been recommended as the preferred choice in cases of PROM by the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), primarily due to its fewer contraindications and adverse effects [7]. However, oxytocin’s efficacy appears to be limited in term PROM patients with an unripened cervix [8, 9].

Misoprostol, a prostaglandin E1 drug utilized for cervical ripening, demonstrates cost-effectiveness, high efficiency, and global accessibility. The primary contraindication of misoprostol is a history of cesarean delivery or other uterine scarring. In China, asthma and glaucoma are also considered contraindications for labor induction with this agent. The most common dose-dependent adverse effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, chills, shivering, and fever. Between 2006 and 2021, the Cochrane Collaboration conducted three systematic reviews evaluating the efficacy and safety of oral misoprostol for labor induction [10–12]. Recent evidence suggests that low-dose oral misoprostol (less than 50 µg) exhibits superior rates of vaginal delivery compared to oxytocin, vaginal misoprostol, and dinoprostone PGE2 while also being associated with fewer instances of abnormal fetal heart rate changes and uterine tachysystole [12, 13]. Similar results were observed in patients with term PROM [9, 14, 15].

Currently, the utilization of oral low-dose misoprostol for labor induction in China is limited, although it is widely used globally. Since March 2022, our department has implemented the use of oral low-dose misoprostol for cervical ripening in pregnant women at ≥ 36 weeks who do not exhibit favorable cervix conditions. Comparison with vaginal misoprostol revealed that oral low-dose misoprostol had superior efficacy and safety profiles, resulting in a higher rate of vaginal delivery and a lower incidence of fetal distress and uterine hyperstimulation [16]. After nearly three years of clinical practice, our focus lies on the administration of oral low-dose misoprostol to PROM pregnant women who do not exhibit cervical ripening. The aim of this study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of oral misoprostol for labor induction in patients with premature rupture of membranes (PROM) and an unfavorable cervix, compared to conventional oxytocin infusion. The findings may provide clinicians with an alternative option for managing PROM.

Methods

This retrospective cohort study was conducted in a tertiary maternity unit (averaging 6000 deliveries annually) in the Gynecology and Obstetrics Unit of Peking University Third Hospital, in China, from March 2020 to November 2024. This retrospective cohort study comprised 622 patients with PROM at ≥ 36 weeks, including 297 who received oxytocin induction between March 2020 and February 2022 and 325 who received oral misoprostol for cervical ripening from March 2022 to November 2024. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University Third Hospital (Approval No. 245-02). All participants were adequately informed about the labor procedure and provided signed informed consent.

Participants

The inclusion criteria of the study were singleton, primiparous, vertex presentation, gestational age ≥ 36 weeks, PROM without regular uterine contractions, no contraindication to prostaglandins, bishop scores < 6 (unripe cervix) and candidate for vaginal delivery. Exclusion criteria were multiple pregnancies, fetuses in the breech or transverse position, fetal death or termination of labor due to severe fetal malformation, history of uterine scarring, rhythmic contractions observed, infection and refusal to participate in the study. The diagnosis of PROM was established either through clinical assessment or by employing a pH test paper. Notably, in line with China’s long-standing population policies, multiparous women account for only 10–20% of deliveries at our institution. These multiparas typically present with more favorable cervical conditions compared to nulliparas. At our hospital, oxytocin is the sole agent used for labor induction in multiparous women, achieving consistently high success rates regardless of Bishop score. Consequently, misoprostol is not routinely employed for labor induction in this population. Therefore, multiparas were excluded from the study. Following 36 weeks of gestation, in accordance with our departmental protocol, labor induction was initiated if there were no signs of spontaneous labor or if regular contractions did not occur within 1–2 h following membrane rupture. The enrolled patients were subjected to vital sign monitoring every 4–6 h (including temperature, pulse, and respiratory rate), fetal heart rate monitoring every 4 h, and daily blood tests [complete blood count (CBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT)]. In high-risk labor scenarios—such as prolonged rupture of membranes (> 24 h), maternal fever, or abnormal lab results—targeted testing is employed. This includes bacterial cultures from umbilical cord blood and histopathological analysis of the placenta.

Drugs administration

Oral Misoprostol solution

Pregnant women who met the inclusion criteria were transferred to the delivery room. Prior to administration, confirmation of fetal heart monitoring response type was conducted again to ensure absence of contraindications. The drug was administered by both the midwife and physician, starting with an initial dose of 25 µg followed by subsequent doses of 25 µg every 2 h. In cases where contraction frequency exceeded 2 times per 10 min, administration of the drug was delayed, and daily dosage did not exceed 200 µg. Oxytocin infusion was administered if deemed necessary [a. when the cervix has been ripened but effective uterine contractions did not occur; b. if the maximum dose of misoprostol within a 24-hour period is less than 200ug and regular contractions are not induced; and c. in cases where patients exhibit sensitivity to misoprostol (experiencing hyperstimulation contractions after a single dose)].

Preparations of specific drugs

Dissolve a 200 µg tablet of misoprostol in 200 ml of warm water, ensuring thorough stirring to obtain a well-mixed solution with a concentration of 1 µg/ml. Administer orally to pregnant women at a dosage of 25 ml.

Oxytocin intravenous: In the oxytocin group, cervical ripening and labor induction were performed through Oxytocin perfusion. A syringe pump was used to administer low-dose oxytocin (2.5 U in 500 mL) perfusion at a rate of 20 mU/min, and was doubled every 15 min until the number of uterine contractions was 3 times or more in 10 min with the duration of 40 s and appropriate force.

Data collection

The following clinical data were collected: prenatal information like age, height, pregestational body mass index (BMI), gravidity, gestational weeks, cervical Bishop score, whether complicated with in vitro fertilization-embryo transfer (IVF-ET), gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) and screening positive of Group B streptococcus (GBS+); The general obstetric delivery information like mode of delivery, failed induction of labor, duration from rupture or administration to labor or delivery, duration of labor; delivery outcomes like adverse pregnancy outcomes such as fetal distress, intrauterine infection, postpartum hemorrhage (PPH), and postpartum urinary retention. The rate of episiotomy, birth weight, Apgar score at 1 min, and umbilical cord blood pH value were also examined.

Outcome indicators

The primary and secondary outcomes of the two groups were compared in order to evaluate the efficacy and safety of misoprostol. Primary outcomes were the number of vaginal deliveries and failed induction of labor. The term “failed induction of labor” refers to the situation where induced labor does not result in cervical dilatation or fails to progress despite strong contractions. Here, failed induction of labor shown is operationally defined as the administration of oral misoprostol for more than 48 h without the onset of regular uterine contractions, or the absence of labor onset following the administration of oxytocin for at least 12 h [17]. Secondary outcomes were delivery information like total labor time and duration of administration to delivery, and delivery complications like fetal distress and PPH.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA). Measurement data with a normal distribution were presented as Mean ± SD, and the comparison between two groups was assessed using the Student T-test. Measurement data with a non-normal distribution were described as M (P25-P75), and the comparison between groups was analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Count data were reported as number and percentage, and the comparison between groups was evaluated using Fisher’s exact probability. Logistic regression models were adjusted for prenatal information and utilized to analyze vaginal delivery, failed induction of labor and PPH. Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < 0.05.

Comparison of prenatal information between two groups

As shown in Table 1, there were no significant differences observed in age, height, body weight, body weight gain, BMI, gestational weeks, Bishop score between the misoprostol group and oxytocin group.

Comparison of obstetric delivery information between two groups

In the misoprostol group, 59 out of 325 cases required additional oxytocin infusion, 81.8% (266/325) of cases used misoprostol alone for labor induction, and labor onset occurred in 239 out of 266 cases.

Data of obstetric delivery shown in Table 2 revealed no significant differences in labor onset rate, vaginal delivery rate, and failed induction of labor rate between the oxytocin and misoprostol groups. Similarly, the time from PROM to labor onset or delivery, as well as the time from administration to labor onset or delivery was not significantly different between the two groups. The rates of 24-hour deliveries were comparable between the two groups [48.1% (143/297) in oxytocin group vs. 52.0% (169/325) in misoprostol group, p = 0.34]. However, the hospitalization days were shorter in misoprostol group than in the oxytocin group (3.4 ± 1.4d vs. 3.7 ± 1.7d, p = 0.02).

During vaginal delivery, oral misoprostol induction in pregnant women with PROM is associated with a reduced duration of the first stage of labor and overall labor time compared to oxytocin induction. Results indicated that the rate of delivery within 24 h (from induction initiation to vaginal delivery) was higher in the misoprostol group [51.2% (103/201) in oxytocin group vs. 61.2% (137/224) in misoprostol group, p = 0.04]. The incidence rates of precipitate labor and episiotomy were similar between the two groups. Regarding Cesarean deliveries, no significant differences were observed in the rates of operative interventions during labor or abnormal labor progression between the two groups.

Comparison of delivery outcomes between two groups

Analysis of data in Table 3 for delivery outcomes showed no significant difference in the rates of uterine hyperstimulation (Excessive or abnormal uterine contractions exceeding a frequency of 5 within a 10-min interval, necessitating the use of contraction inhibitors.), fetal distress (Fetal monitoring revealed atypical and prolonged deceleration patterns, with or without meconium-stained amniotic fluid observed during vaginal examination), clinical chorioamnionitis [18] [Maternal fever ≥ 38 °C, fetal tachycardia (>160 bpm for ≥ 10 min), maternal tachycardia (>100 bpm), purulent or foul-smelling amniotic fluid or/and laboratory indicators such as white blood cell count (WBC) ≥ 15 × 10⁹/L, elevated C-reactive protein/procalcitonin (CRP/PCT) levels], acute histologic chorioamnionitis [18] confirmed by placental pathology (Confirmed infection were observed), newborn body weight, pH of cord blood, and postpartum blood loss between the two groups. Postpartum hemorrhage was defined as blood loss exceeding 500 mL following vaginal delivery or 1000 mL after Cesarean delivery within 24 h postpartum. The overall PPH rate was significantly higher in the oxytocin group [17.8% (53/297) vs. 8.6% (28/325), p < 0.01], and this difference was particularly pronounced in vaginal deliveries [25.9% (52/201) vs. 12.1% (27/224), p < 0.001]. Notably, severe PPH (≥ 1000 mL) occurred more frequently in the oxytocin group following vaginal delivery [3.5% (7/201) vs. 0.4% (1/224), p = 0.02]. Furthermore, a higher incidence of postpartum urinary retention (PPUR, the absence of spontaneous micturition more than 6 h after birth or when residual volume after urination is ≥ 150 ml) was observed in the misoprostol group.

Specifically, we evaluated the outcomes for women with highly unfavorable cervical conditions (Bishop scores ≤ 3) to determine whether misoprostol might exhibit better efficacy in this challenging subgroup. Notably, there was no significant difference in delivery outcomes between the treatment groups, even under these most unfavorable cervical conditions (Supplementary Table 1). These findings suggest that, while cervical status remains clinically significant, the efficacy of oral misoprostol may not be superior to that of oxytocin under conditions of extremely unfavorable cervical status in patients with PROM.

Logistic analysis of vaginal delivery, failed induction of labor and postpartum hemorrhage

The logistic regression model was constructed with vaginal delivery and failed induction of labor as the dependent variables. Independent variables included oral misoprostol administration, age, height, pregnancy weight gain, pregestational BMI, gestational weeks, IVF-ET status, GDM, GBS infection, Bishop score, and newborn body weight. Discussion

Oral low-dose misoprostol is widely recognized as an effective method for cervical ripening in labor induction. Its safety and efficacy are supported by a 2021 Cochrane systematic review [12], and its application is recommended in major international clinical guidelines [1, 8, 19–21]. Although this approach has achieved widespread acceptance for PROM management worldwide, its implementation in China has lagged behind. Building upon our institution’s previous findings demonstrating the effectiveness and safety of oral misoprostol for labor induction in women with intact membranes during late gestation [16], the current study extends this evidence to PROM at ≥ 36 weeks cases-representing the first such clinical evaluation in China’s obstetric practice. Our findings demonstrate that oral misoprostol exhibits comparable safety and efficacy to oxytocin infusion for labor induction. Notably, it offers the distinct advantage of significantly reducing postpartum hemorrhage risk in pregnancies with PROM at ≥ 36 weeks and unfavorable cervical status (Bishop score < 6). These findings carry particular clinical relevance for Chinese obstetric practice, where oxytocin has traditionally been the mainstay for labor induction, suggesting that oral misoprostol may be a valuable alternative that merits broader adoption.

Following more than 36 weeks of gestation, ACOG recommends oxytocin administration for labor induction in cases of PROM [1]. Several comparative studies have been conducted on the use of oral misoprostol compared with oxytocin for labor induction in term PROM pregnant women. A study by Leila Pourali et al. [9] demonstrated that sublingual administration of 25 µg misoprostol every 4 h for labor induction in PROM cases resulted in improved neonatal outcomes and greater efficacy compared to oxytocin. Another study by Rania et al. [15] demonstrated that the administration of oral misoprostol tablets at a dose of 25 µg every four hours for labor induction in PROM resulted in significantly shorter induction-delivery intervals compared to the oxytocin group, particularly among multiparous women. In another study [22], the utilization of oral misoprostol solution at a dosage of 20 µg every 2 h demonstrated comparable maternal complications and neonatal outcomes when compared to the standard intravenous oxytocin administration. A recent randomized trial by Bender et al. [23], published in AJOG MFM in 2024, compared oxytocin and oral misoprostol for PROM induction in nulliparas with unfavorable cervices. Several findings are consistent with our results, demonstrating comparable rates of: (1) cesarean delivery (2), suspected intraamniotic infection, and (3) neonatal morbidity between study groups. However, the key methodological differences exist: Bender’s study included patients with slightly more favorable cervical status (Bishop score < 8, cervical dilation ≤ 2 cm) compared to our stricter criteria (Bishop score < 6). Moreover, their population showed higher BMI levels compared with the present Asian cohort. These differences demonstrate the relevance of this study for populations with less favorable cervical conditions and lower average BMI.

Application of a 25 µg misoprostol solution every 2 h in our study adhered to the recommendations set forth by the Society of Obstetricians and Gynecologists of Canada (SOGC) [8] and the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) [24]. Pharmacokinetic studies demonstrate that oral, sublingual, and vaginal misoprostol administration each elicit increased uterine tone following a single dose. However, oral administration results in rapid serum concentration decline within 1–2 h due to extensive first-pass hepatic metabolism, consequently attenuating its uterotonic effects [25]. Therefore, we recommend misoprostol with a minimum interval of 2 h. In line with the aforementioned findings, our study revealed no statistically significant differences in the rates of labor onset and vaginal delivery between the groups receiving oral misoprostol and oxytocin. Additionally, there were no significant differences in the incidence of uterine hyperstimulation, fetal distress, or neonatal asphyxia between the two groups. However, administration of oral misoprostol was associated with a shorter first stage and overall duration of labor. A higher rate of delivery within 24 h and shorter hospital stays resulted in lower costs for labor induction using oral misoprostol in cases of PROM. Consequently, our results suggest that oral misoprostol is both safe and potentially more efficacious compared to oxytocin infusion for labor induction in PROM pregnant women beyond 36 weeks of gestation.

The common complications associated with PROM include intrauterine infection, abruptio placentae, and fetal distress [7]. Intrauterine infection is the most significant maternal consequence of PROM, and its risk increases with the duration of membrane rupture. This study recorded the duration from PROM to delivery (Table 2), the number of suspected cases of intrauterine infection (Table 3), and the number of placental pathological abnormalities (Table 3). No cases of abruptio placentae were reported. Moreover, there were no significant differences in fetal compromise between two groups. The findings also suggested that both methods employed for labor induction in patients with PROM were safe.

Although no randomized trials have compared the criteria for failed induction of labor, compelling observational data suggest that if the maternal and fetal conditions allow, administration of oxytocin should be continued for at least 12 to 18 h after membrane rupture before considering an induction of labor as unsuccessful due to nonprogression into the active phase [26]. In our study, labor induction was considered unsuccessful if oral misoprostol administration exceeded 48 h without the onset of regular contractions. Our findings indicate that a high BMI and early gestational age are significant risk factors for failed induction of labor. Furthermore, we performed separate analyses for the risk factors associated with the failed induction of labor in both groups. Notably, some risk factors for failed induction of labor were identified between the two groups. In the oxytocin infusion group, IVF-ET and fetal macrosomia emerged as significant predictors of failed induction of labor. Conversely, in the oral misoprostol group, both an unfavorable cervix (low Bishop score) and early gestational age were independently associated with increased risk of failed induction of labor.

The results of our study also revealed a significant finding that the incidence of postpartum hemorrhage is lower in cases where oral misoprostol was administered. Furthermore, logistic regression analysis further supports the notion that oral misoprostol administration may effectively mitigate the risk for PPH. It has been reported that prolonged exposure to high doses of oxytocin during labor is associated with severe PPH due to uterine atony [27]. A recent study by Braund S, et al. [28] reported an association between labor induction and increased risk of PPH, with the quantity of administered oxytocin mediating this relationship. Bernitz et al. [29] also found that high doses of oxytocin infusion in the prenatal period increased the risk of increased risk of PPH. At the molecular level, this phenomenon is referred to as desensitization of oxytocin receptors (OTRs) [30]. Balki et al. [31] discovered that pretreatment with oxytocin attenuates oxytocin-induced contractility in human myometrium, even after a rest period of up to 90 min following oxytocin administration.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, this is a retrospective, non-randomization, single-center study conducted at a tertiary general hospital in Beijing. The patient population primarily consists of individuals with perinatal complications and some transferal patients from lower-level hospitals [32]. In future, multi-center studies and randomized clinical trials are recommended to confirm the generalizability of the present results. Secondly, different medical staff may have varying interpretations of Bishop scores, which can alter the predictive value of successful induction. To minimize errors, it is recommended that the Bishop scores should be assigned by the same personnel to ensure consistency. Lastly, to improve the comprehensive analysis of drug safety, data on adverse reactions such as rapid heart rate, fever, gastrointestinal reactions etc., which were not captured in this study, need to be included in future studies. Future research designs should incorporate relevant observation indicators to address this limitation.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that oral administration of low-dose misoprostol is both safe and efficient for labor induction and may potentially reduce the incidence of postpartum hemorrhage in PROM pregnant women at ≥ 36 weeks with unfavorable cervix condition, compared to oxytocin infusion induction. In future, multi-center studies and randomized clinical trials in China are needed to clarify the generalizability of the present findings.

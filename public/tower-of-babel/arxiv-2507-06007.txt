A Proof Theory for Profinite Modal Algebras
Matteo De Berardinis, Silvio Ghilardi

Abstract
In a previous paper, we showed that profinite L L -algebras (where L L is a variety of modal algebras generated by its finite members) are monadic over 𝐒𝐞𝐭 \bf Set . This monadicity result suggests that profinite L L -algebras could be presented as Lindenbaum algebras for propositional theories in infinitary versions of propositional modal calculi. In this paper we identify such calculi as modal enrichments of Maehara-Takeuti’s infinitary extension of the sequent calculus 𝐋𝐊 \bf LK . We also investigate correspondences between syntactic properties of the calculi and regularity/exactness properties of the opposite category of profinite L L -algebras.

Here and in the whole paper, we treat normal modal logics and varieties of modal algebras interchangeably and use the letter

The monadicity theorem clearly indicates that profinite modal algebras are sensible to an ‘algebraic’ description, although not in terms of a set of operations and equations of finitary character, because the monad is not finitary. From the logician’s point of view, a convenient ‘algebraic’ description should be linked to a calculus, an infinitary calculus in our case.
Building it is precisely the aim of the present paper. We take inspiration from Maehara-Takeuti’s infinitary sequent calculus for classical logic introduced in
[
23
, Chapter 4]
: in such a calculus, conjunctions and disjunctions can have arbitrary arity, but proofs are well-founded trees. The idea is to add to it additional modal rules characterizing the specificity of profinite modal algebras.

We build our calculus in
Section

3

Section

4

L
, a set of propositional variables
G

(to theory axioms, uniform substitution does not apply, unlike to

In
Section

5

L
with categorical properties of the category
\Pro L\textup{{MA}}_{\textit{fin}}
of profinite modal
L
-algebras (better, of the dual category
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
of locally finite Kripke frames for
L
).
We focus on the analysis of factorization systems. Being monadic,
\Pro L\textup{{MA}}_{\textit{fin}}
is regular and hence has a stable regular epi/mono factorization system, which becomes an epi/regular mono factorization system in
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
. The latter (the coregular factorization system of
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
) can be compared with the regular factorization system of
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
(which also exists because
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
is both complete and cocomplete), so one may ask the following: do such factorizations coincide? are they stable? It turns out that the coincidence of the regular and coregular factorization in

is equivalent to a strong version of the Beth Definability Property, whereas the stability under pullbacks of the coregular factorization in
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
is equivalent to Craig Interpolation Theorem (for global consequence relation). Similar characterizations can be found for regularity of monos and of epis.
We also supply a logical characterization of Barr exactness property, together with examples and counterexamples.

The above analysis needs to be compared with the similar analysis from the literature on the customary finitary fragment of our language and on the customary finitary algebras. As we shall see during the paper, in many cases the results are parallel, however there are also remarkable differences. Probably the most striking difference concerns the fact that epis are always regular in
\Pro L\textup{{MA}}_{\textit{fin}}

9

In general, the impression is that the framework gets simplified and proofs become smoother in the infinitary language, where for instance the distinction between ordinary and uniform interpolants disappears and where the natural formulation of the Weak Beth Definability Property is what is usually called “infinite (deductive) Beth definability property”

We conclude the paper by mentioning in

Our syntax is a modal extension of the propositional part of the calculus presented in

Definition 8

Corollary 3
.

L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
is Barr r-exact if and only if all formulas defining r-coequivalence relations separate variables for

Clearly, when all monos are regular in
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
(i.e. when Weak Beth’s Definability Property holds) Barr r-exactness becomes the same as Barr exactness. Barr exactness is an extremely strong property for
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
, because the remaining conditions for Giraud’s Theorem
[
14
, Theorem 1.4.5]
are satisfied: if
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
is Barr exact, then it is a Grothendieck topos. Thus one expects that Barr exactness should not hold in categories like
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
. We nevertheless found two examples:

Example 3
.

Consider
\textup{{GL-Lin}}_{\textit{lf}}
, namely the category of locally finite, transitive and irreflexive Kripke frames
(W,\prec)
such that the restriction of the relation
\prec
to each rooted generated subframe is linear (these are the Kripke frames for the logic
GL.Lin
mentioned in the Table of
Section

2
).
\textup{{GL-Lin}}_{\textit{lf}}
is Barr exact because there exists an equivalence of categories between
\textup{{GL-Lin}}_{\textit{lf}}
and the category of presheaves
\textup{{Set}}^{(\omega,\leq)^{\textup{op}}}
, see
[
6
]
.

Example 4
.

An example where transitivity does not hold, is given by the category
D\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
of locally finite Kripke frames
(W,\prec)
where the relation
\prec
is a function (these are just sets endowed with an ultimately periodic unary function). They can be axiomatized by the axiom

\mathop{\text{\scalebox{1.1}{$\meddiamond$}}}\nolimits x=\mathop{\text{\raisebox{-0.43057pt}{\scalebox{1.3}{$\medsquare$}}}}\nolimits x.

It is straightforward to verify that the forgetful functor
\mathcal{U}\colon D\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}\longrightarrow\textup{{Set}}
preserves not only colimits (see
1
), but also finite limits: Barr exactness follows easily.

6
Conclusions, open problems and further work

In this paper we introduced an infinitary calculus, whose algebraic models (= Lindenbaum algebras of theories inside such calculus) are precisely profinite modal algebras. We investigated, for a normal modal propositional logic
L
with the finite model property, the correspondences between syntactic properties of this calculus enriched with the axioms of
L
and categorical properties of the corresponding class of profinite
L
-algebras.
We consider the framework presented in this paper as a starting point for a broader research line, some of whose directions are outlined below.

1.

From a strictly syntactic point of view, it would be nice to supply a cut-free version of the calculus presented in
Section

3
. A semantic cut elimination proof has been given in
[
23
, Theorem 22.17]
for the non-modal fragment; an extension of such result to suitable reformulations of our calculi for basic logics like
K,K4,S4
, etc. would be desirable. Recently, there have been progresses in syntactic cut elimination proofs for countably infinitary versions of classical and intuitionistic logics
[
25
]
, although satisfactory solutions for modal extensions are still to be investigated
[
24
,
21
]
. It should be noted, however, that our context is different from the one considered in the above mentioned papers, because on the one hand we investigate conjunctions and disjunctions of arbitrary cardinality, and on the other hand we assume the peculiar axioms coming from
2
.

2.

Concerning regularity and exactness properties of
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
, it should be noted that
extensions of
S4
where Craig’s Theorem
(i.e. regularity) holds are fully determined in
[
6
]
: the classification follows Maksimova’s parallel results for the finitary fragment
[
15
,
16
]
(with some notable exceptions, see
2
above). Passing to Barr-exactness, the picture changes drastically: in
[
6
]
it is shown that there are exacly five logics
L
extending
S4
and such that
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}
is Barr exact. These five logics are all locally finite and of little interest (the category of their locally finite Kripke frames turns out to be equivalent to the topos of the actions of a finite small monoid). Still, one may ask whether Barr exactness is so rare everywhere, in particular a classification over
K4
cannot include only trivial examples, as witnessed by
Example

3
above.

3.

Given that Barr exactness is rare, one may investigate
almost Barr exactness
, where a category is said to be almost Barr exact iff it is regular and effective descent morphisms coincides with regular epis. Almost Barr exactness, in fact, turns out to be equivalent to effectiveness of special kinds of equivalence relations
[
10
]
, so that there is a concrete chance that it should hold for some relevant categories of locally finite Kripke frames.

4.

Finally, as pointed out in
[
7
]
,
L\textup{{KFr}}_{\textit{lf}}

is a locally finitely presentable category
(being equivalent to
\Lex(L\textup{{MA}}_{\textit{fin}},\textup{{Set}})
), hence it is the category of models of an essentially algebraic
first-order theory
[
1
]
: this raises the problem of identifying and axiomatizing such a theory. A success in this attempt would contribute to recast in an algebraic context some typically semantic notions.

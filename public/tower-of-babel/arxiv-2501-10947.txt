The anomalous density of states and quasi-localized vibration through homogeneous thermalization of an inhomogeneous elastic system
Cunyuan Jiang 1,2,3

Abstract
Amorphous solids are dynamically inhomogeneous due to in lack of translational symmetry and hence exhibit vibrational properties different from crystalline solids with anomalous low frequency vibrational density of states (VDOS) and related low temperature thermal properties. However, an interpretation of their origin from basic physical laws is still needed compared with rapidly progressed particle level investigations. In this work, we start with the quasi-equilibrium condition, which requires elastic potential energy to be homogeneously distributed even in an inhomogeneous elastic solid over long time observation. Analytical result shows that the anomalous low frequency VDOS behavior D ⁡ ( ω ) ∝ ω 4 D(\omega)\propto\omega^{4} can be obtained when the quasi-equilibrium condition is satisfied on an inhomogeneous elastic system. Under high frequency after a crossover depending on the length scale of inhomogeneity, the power law of VDOS is changed to square D ⁡ ( ω ) ∝ ω 2 D(\omega)\propto\omega^{2} which is Debye’s law for crystalline solids. These features agree with recent particle level investigations. Our work suggest that the universal low frequency anomaly of amorphous solids can be considered as a result of homogeneous thermalization.



I
Introduction

Amorphous solids have been extensively observed with excess heat capacity and vibrational density of states (VDOS) than crystalline counterparts in the low temperature and frequency regime known as Boson peak (BP) since the anomaly was first reported in 1970s.
Zeller and Pohl 1971
;
Ahart et al. 2017
;
Nakayama 2002
The anomaly of low frequency VDOS and low temperature thermal properties, which are also due to anomalous VDOS, are clearly because of inhomogeneity of amorphous solids. However, the mechanism that how inhomogeneity can leads to low frequency anomalous VDOS is still a controversial topic.

During many decades of research, many theories have been proposed from various aspects to explain the question how inhomogeneity can leads to low frequency anomalous VDOS. For example, the inhomogeneous elasticity theory which used a field theoretical perturbative approach to consider the dressed phonon propagator in spatially fluctuating elastic medium.
Schmid and Schirmacher 2008
;
Schirmacher et al. 2007
However, the inhomogeneous elasticity theory is considered to be underestimated to the microscopic mechanism.
Caroli and Lemaître 2019
;
Vogel and Fuchs 2023
The quasi-localized vibration theory show that a vibrational instability of the spectrum of weakly interacting quasi-local harmonic modes creates the anomalous VDOS and predict
D(\omega)\propto\omega^{4}
at low frequency limit,
Gurevich et al. 2003
;
Buchenau et al. 1991
;
Parshin et al. 2007
and related sound damping effect.
Baggioli and Zaccone 2022
;
Mizuno et al. 2025
. Even though the origin of quasi-local harmonic modes still need investigations. In addition, the self-consistent random matrix theory provide a microscopic field theoretical approach to grasp the low frequency
\omega^{4}
VDOS,
Vogel and Fuchs 2023
however the visualization of quasi-localized modes and their size effect are still needed to be discussed. The theory based on anharmonicity effect is also developed by considering Rayleigh damping of sound into field theoretical approach,
Baggioli and Zaccone 2019
still the microscopic process is underestimated by the Rayleigh damping parameter.

On the other side, the investigation based on experiments and simulations had also rapid progresses and provided more microscopic information about the origin of anomalous VDOS. Based on the experiments and simulations, the anomalous VDOS is confirmed to be contributed by transverse vibration.
Ren et al. 2021
;
Chumakov et al. 2011
;
Shintani and Tanaka 2008
In addition, the low frequency power law
D(\omega)\propto\omega^{4}
is confirmed indicating the anomalous VDOS are quasi-localized as predicted by theories.
Mahajan and Ciamarra 2023
;
Mahajan and Ciamarra 2021
By dispersing the anomalous low frequency vibrational mode onto particle level, simulation results revealed the vibrational anomaly is contributed by string-like dynamical defects which are a kind of soft spots with void space associated at the ends.
Hu and Tanaka 2022
;
Hu and Tanaka 2023
From the real time simulation, the origin of anomalous VDOS can also be attributed by string-like motion which is a string of particles moving in same direction like a worm.
Donati et al. 1998
;
Zhang et al. 2011
;
Zhang et al. 2013
The relation between string-like dynamical defects and anomalous VDOS is also confirmed in experiment of active particles.
Jiang et al. 2024a
With the experiments and simulations results of string-like dynamical defects, the frequency of BP, where the power law start to change from
\omega^{4}
to
\omega^{2}
, is found to be universally determined by the length of the string-like dynamical defects through a simple relation
\omega_{BP}\sim c/\bar{l}
with
\bar{l}
the average length and
c
the transverse speed of sound.
Jiang et al. 2024a
;
Jiang et al. 2024b
;
Jiang and Baggioli 2024

The recent experiments and simulations progresses suggested that the low frequency anomalous VDOS is due to string-like quasi-localized dynamical soft spots, and the frequency of anomalous VDOS crossover is determined by their size. However, an interpretation from fundamental principle of how quasi-localized vibration and hence the anomalous VDOS can originate from dynamical soft spots is still needed. In this work, we study the relation between vibrational intensity and spatially fluctuating elasticity when quasi-equilibrium condition is satisfied, which requires the elastic potential energy density and hence the total energy density to be homogeneously distributed in space during long time observation. In real space, the analytic result show that vibrational intensity should be stronger at where elasticity is weaker to satisfy the quasi-equilibrium condition. In Fourier space, the analytic result of VDOS show that the power law change from
\omega^{4}
under low frequency to
\omega^{2}
under high frequency at the crossover depending on the length scale of fluctuation of elasticity through
\omega_{BP}\sim\sqrt{3}c/(2\bar{l})
. Our results provide an interpretation to the origin of low frequency anomalous VDOS in amorphous solids from fundamental quasi-equilibrium consideration, provide also an example connecting anomalous vibration phenomenon in inhomogeneous medium to the profound question of what will happen if an inhomogeneous medium is homogeneously thermalized.

II
Analysis and discussions

Let’s consider a scaler field
\phi(\bm{r},t)
which denote the elastic vibrational displacement magnitude of a point
\bm{r}
in 3D space and at time
t
. The full description of elastic displacement in 3D medium require vector field. However, because the components of different directions are relatively independently obeying elastic wave equation,
Ramos 2022
one can use a scalar field for the convenience of analytic calculation. Elastic vibration is a result that the kinetic energy and potential energy convert to each other and hence elastic system have always
E_{kin}\equiv E_{pot}
. For an elastic medium with fluctuating elasticity, the elastic potential energy density is defined through the spatial gradient of displacement magnitude and inhomogeneous elastic constant
G(\bm{r})
,

\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r},t)=\frac{1}{2}G(\bm{r})\left(\nabla\phi(\bm{r},t)\right)^{2}.

(1)

The quasi-equilibrium condition requires there is no energy current during long time observation, therefore the gradient of time averaged potential energy density should be zero everywhere, that is,

\nabla\langle\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}\rangle_{t}(\bm{r})\equiv 0,

(2)

where
\langle\rangle_{t}
denotes time average. The main line of this work is to obtain the quasi-localized vibration and anomalous VDOS from quasi-equilibrium condition Eq.(
2
) with inhomogeneous elasticity distribution
G(\bm{r})
.

The scaler field
\phi(\bm{r},t)
can take a standard field description which is linear combination of eigenmodes with various wave vectors
\bm{k}_{n}
and their magnitude can be from zero to the maximum
k_{D}
, the Debye’s wave vector,

\phi(\bm{r},t)=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})e^{-i(\bm{k}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)}+\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{i(\bm{k}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)}\right).

(3)

Here
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
, a pure imaginary number, and its complex conjugate
\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})
determine the vibration amplitude
\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
at position
\bm{r}
,
\bm{k}_{n}=2\pi(n_{1}/L,n_{2}/L,n_{3}/L)
are discrete wave vectors that satisfy the boundary condition with
n_{i}
integers and
L
the size of the system. In the language of field theory,
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
and
\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})
describe the annihilation and creation of a excitation with wave vector
\bm{k}_{n}
at position
\bm{r}
, and
\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
gives the number of excitation at position
\bm{r}
. Here the speed of sound

\bm{k}_{n}

\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})

\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})

G(\bm{r})^{-1}=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}+\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}\right).

(5)

Eq.(
5

In simulation works, the quasi-localized mode is defined as where vibration amplitude is strong in some areas compare with others although the local vibration amplitude were described by different ways, by particle level density of states in frequency domain,

). If the vibration amplitude is same everywhere even elasticity is inhomogeneous, the time averaged elastic potential energy density and hence the total energy density would be also inhomogeneous, weaker at soft spot, which against the quasi-equilibrium condition Eq.(
2

G(\bm{r})^{-1}=const.

5

From quasi-equilibrium condition Eq.(

C

\tilde{G}^{-1}(\bm{q})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\delta(\bm{k}_{G,i}+\bm{q})

\displaystyle-|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)+c.c..

III
Summary

In this work, the low frequency anomalous VDOS well observed in amorphous solids are explained from the consideration of homogeneous energy density distribution even the elasticity of medium is inhomogeneous. The results suggest that the quasi-localized vibration should appear at soft spots for keeping energy density distribution to be homogeneous during long time of observation. As a result of presence of quasi-localized vibration, the anomalous VDOS
D(\omega)\propto\omega^{4}
should appear at low frequency region lower than a crossover frequency
c\sqrt{3/4}k_{G}
determined by the typical length scale of soft spots
1/k_{G}
. The results also suggest that presence of quasi-localized vibration can smear the wave vector of ordinary linear dispersion relation which may be used for understanding the origin of quasi-flat band observed recently in experiments and simulations. Our work explained the origin of quasi-localized vibration and low frequency anomalous VDOS in amorphous solids from quasi-equilibrium consideration, explained its size size dependent relation and possible effect on dispersion relation. Our work suggest that low frequency dynamic anomaly in amorphous solids can be considered as a result of homogeneously thermalization on inhomogeneous medium.

\bm{r}
,

for convenience. Using Euler’s equation, the gradient can be rearranged in a compact form,

\nabla\phi(\bm{r},t)=2\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}(\text{Re}[c_{n}]\cos\theta_{n}-\text{Im}[c_{n}]\sin\theta_{n}).

(26)

The potential energy density is therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r},t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}G(\bm{r})\left(\nabla\phi(\bm{r},t)\right)^{2}

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}G(\bm{r})\left(2\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}(\text{Re}[c_{n}]\cos\theta_{n}-\text{Im}[c_{n}]\sin\theta_{n})\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(2\sum_{\bm{k}_{m}}(\text{Re}[c_{m}]\cos\theta_{m}-\text{Im}[c_{m}]\sin\theta_{m})\right)

\displaystyle=2G(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\sum_{\bm{k}_{m}}\left(\text{Re}[c_{n}]\cos\theta_{n}\text{Re}[c_{m}]\cos\theta_{m}+\text{Im}[c_{n}]\sin\theta_{n}\text{Im}[c_{m}]\sin\theta_{m}\right.

\displaystyle-\text{Im}[c_{n}]\sin\theta_{n}\text{Re}[c_{m}]\cos\theta_{m}-\text{Im}[c_{m}]\sin\theta_{m}\text{Re}[c_{n}]\cos\theta_{n}\left.\right).

The time-averaged potential energy density can be obtained by integrating over time
t
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r})\rangle_{t}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r},t)

(28)

\displaystyle=2G(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\sum_{\bm{k}_{m}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}dt\text{Re}[c_{n}]\cos\theta_{n}\text{Re}[c_{m}]\cos\theta_{m}+\int_{0}^{T}dt\text{Im}[c_{n}]\sin\theta_{n}\text{Im}[c_{m}]\sin\theta_{m}\right.

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}dt\text{Im}[c_{n}]\sin\theta_{n}\text{Re}[c_{m}]\cos\theta_{m}-\int_{0}^{T}dt\text{Im}[c_{m}]\sin\theta_{m}\text{Re}[c_{n}]\cos\theta_{n}\left.\right)

\displaystyle=2G(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(\text{Re}[c_{n}]^{2}\int_{0}^{T}dt\cos^{2}\theta_{n}+\text{Im}[c_{n}]^{2}\int_{0}^{T}dt\sin^{2}\theta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=G(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(\text{Re}[c_{n}]^{2}+\text{Im}[c_{n}]^{2}\right).

Here the orthogonality of trigonometric functions is used. They are,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}dt\sin\theta_{n}\sin\theta_{m}=\delta_{n,m}\dfrac{\pi}{2},

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}dt\cos\theta_{n}\cos\theta_{m}=\delta_{n,m}\dfrac{\pi}{2},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}dt\cos\theta_{n}\sin\theta_{m}=0.

The vibrational amplitude
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
can be chosen to be a peal imaginary number, therefore,

\langle\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r})\rangle_{t}=\pi G(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}+|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}\right).

(30)

Note that
\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}\equiv\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\cdot\nabla\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})
and
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}\equiv\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})
.

Appendix B
Current Correlation Function

Dynamical properties of the system can be obtained through a current correlation function, in time domain, it is defined as,

C(\bm{q},t)=\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{q},t)\partial_{t}\phi(-\bm{q},0).

(31)

Compute the time derivative of
\phi
,

\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{r},t)=\sum_{n}\left(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})e^{-i(\bm{k}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)}+ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{i(\bm{k}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)}\right).

(32)

The Fourier transform on the spatial domain is given by,

\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{q},t)=\int d^{3}r\,\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{r},t)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(33)

Substitute the expression for
\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{r},t)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{q},t)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}r\sum_{n}\left(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})e^{-i(\bm{k}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)}+ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{i(\bm{k}_{n}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle\times e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}

Separate the integrals,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{q},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\left(\int d^{3}r\,(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})e^{-i((\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)})\right.

(35)

\displaystyle+\left.\int d^{3}r\,(ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{-i((\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\cdot\bm{r}+c|\bm{k}_{n}|t)})\right).

The Fourier transform of
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
and
\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})
are,

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{q})=\int d^{3}r\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}

(36)

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q})=\int d^{3}r\alpha_{n}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

In field theory, they are annihilation and creation operator on momentum space. By replacing
\bm{q}
to
\bm{k}_{n}\pm\bm{q}
in the above expression, we have,

\partial_{t}\phi(\bm{q},t)=\sum_{n}\left(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})e^{-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}+ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})e^{ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}\right),

(37)

Now, compute
\partial_{t}\phi(-\bm{q},0)
,

\partial_{t}\phi(-\bm{q},0)=\sum_{n}\left(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})+ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\right).

(38)

The current correlation function in time domain is therefore
C(\bm{q},t)
,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n}\left(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})e^{-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}+ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})e^{ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{m}\left(-ic|\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})+ic|\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})\right).

Expand the product,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\left((-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})e^{-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t})(-ic|\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q}))\right.

(40)

\displaystyle+(-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})e^{-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t})(ic|\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q}))

\displaystyle+(ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})e^{ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t})(-ic|\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q}))

\displaystyle+\left.(ic|\bm{k}_{n}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})e^{ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t})(ic|\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q}))\right).

Simplify the expression,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},t)

\displaystyle=c^{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\left(-|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})e^{-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}\right.

(41)

\displaystyle+|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})e^{-ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}

\displaystyle+|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})e^{ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}

\displaystyle-\left.|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})e^{ic|\bm{k}_{n}|t}\right).

The frequency domain current correlation function can be obtained by Fourier transform,

C(\bm{q},\omega)=\int dt\,C(\bm{q},t)e^{-i\omega t}.

(42)

Substitute the expression for
C(\bm{q},t)
,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=c^{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\left(-|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})\int dt\,e^{-i(c|\bm{k}_{n}|+\omega)t}\right.

(43)

\displaystyle+|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})\int dt\,e^{-i(c|\bm{k}_{n}|+\omega)t}

\displaystyle+|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})\int dt\,e^{-i(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)t}

\displaystyle-\left.|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})\int dt\,e^{-i(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)t}\right).

Finally, evaluate the integrals using orthogonality of exponential functions,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=c^{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\left(-|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega+c|\bm{k}_{n}|)\right.

(44)

\displaystyle+|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{k}_{n}+\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega+c|\bm{k}_{n}|)

\displaystyle+|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)

\displaystyle-\left.|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)\right).

The current correlation function above content four terms that corresponding to four momentum conserved processes. The first term describe annihilating two excitations at
\bm{q}
and
-\bm{q}
, the second term describe firstly create and then annihilate an excitation at
-\bm{q}
, the third and forth are conjugate processes of the first two, that create and then annihilate an excitation at
\bm{q}
and create two excitations at
\bm{q}
and
-\bm{q}
.

Appendix C
Fourier Transform of Elastic Constant Distribution Function

The homogeneously distributed potential energy density requires zero gradient, that is,

\nabla\langle\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r})\rangle_{t}\equiv 0.

(45)

Using the expression of Eq.(
30
) which is,

\langle\mathcal{E}_{\text{pot}}(\bm{r})\rangle_{t}=\pi G(\bm{r})\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}+|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}\right).

(46)

One can easily obtain the following relation between elastic constant
G(\bm{r})
and vibrational amplitude
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})
except a constant factor,

G(\bm{r})^{-1}=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}+\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}\right).

(47)

It will be convenient to obtain the relation in Fourier space for use in the calculation of the current correlation function. The Fourier transform on the spatial domain of Eq.(
47
) is given by,

\tilde{G}^{-1}(\bm{q})=\int d^{3}r\,G(\bm{r})^{-1}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(48)

Substitute the expression for
G(\bm{r})^{-1}
,

\tilde{G}^{-1}(\bm{q})=\int d^{3}r\,\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}+\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}\right)e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(49)

Separate the integrals,

\tilde{G}^{-1}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\int d^{3}r\,\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}+\int d^{3}r\,\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}\right).

(50)

For the Fourier transform of
\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}
, let,

\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{q})=\int d^{3}r\,\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}.

(51)

Using the convolution theorem, we have,

\int d^{3}r\,\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})^{2}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{p})\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q}-\bm{p}).

(52)

For the Fourier transform of
\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}
, we use the property of the Fourier transform of the gradient,

\nabla\rightarrow i\bm{q},

(53)

it becomes,

\int d^{3}r\,\left(\nabla\alpha_{n}(\bm{r})\right)^{2}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}=-\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bm{p}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{p})(\bm{q}-\bm{p})\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q}-\bm{p}).

(54)

Thus, the Fourier transform of the inverse elastic constant distribution function is,

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{-1}(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{p})\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})-\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bm{p}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{p})(\bm{q}-\bm{p})\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})

(55)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\star\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q})-[\bm{q}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}]\star[\bm{q}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}](\bm{q}),

where
\star
denotes convolution. In later sections, this relation will be used for solving
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{q})
from a given elastic constant distribution function.

Appendix D
Solve the vibrational amplitude through a given elastic constant distribution function

We assume the distribution function of elastic constant inverse in wave vector space is a Delta function with a wave vector shift
\bm{k}_{G}
,

G^{-1}(\bm{q})=\delta(\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}).

(56)

When
\bm{k}_{G}=0
,
G^{-1}(\bm{r})
and hence
G(\bm{r})
beth are constant, that means the elastic constant have no fluctuations. When
\bm{k}_{G}\neq 0
, the elastic constant will exhibit a fluctuation length
1/|\bm{k}_{G}|
. In principle,
G^{-1}(\bm{q})
can have many components in wave vector space,
G^{-1}(\bm{q})=\sum_{i}p_{i}\delta(\bm{k}_{G,i}+\bm{q})
with
p_{i}
a distribution probability. In the following, we will focus only on one component
\bm{k}_{G}
to avoid tedious repeating notations and enormous expansions.

The following steps are trying to solve
\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{q})
from Eq.(
55
),

\tilde{G}^{-1}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}\star\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{q})-[\bm{q}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}]\star[\bm{q}\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}](\bm{q}).

(57)

with the given
G^{-1}(\bm{q})=\delta(\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q})
. Noticing that the Delta function
\delta(\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q})\neq 0
only when
\bm{q}=-\bm{k}_{G}
, and the convolution properties of Delta function,

\displaystyle[x\delta(x+a)]\star[x\delta(x+a)]=\int y\delta(y+a)(x-y)\delta(x-y+a)dy=-(ax+a^{2})\delta(x+2a),

(58)

\displaystyle\delta(x+a)\star\delta(x+a)=\int\delta(y+a)\delta(x-y+a)dy=\delta(x+2a),

where
a
is a constant. The above expression can be recast as,

\displaystyle\delta(\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}_{n}}\left(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}\cdot\bm{q}-\dfrac{1}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}\right)

(59)

\displaystyle\times\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}\delta\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\star\delta\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right).

Therefore one can solve the vibrational amplitude to be,

\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{q})=i\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}}\delta(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}),

(60)

with
N
is the normalization factor denoted the number of total modes. It is a pure imaginary function.

Appendix E
Current correlation function for the given elastic constant distribution

In this section, we compute the current correlation function of Eq.(
44
) with the vibrational amplitude given by Eq.(
60
). The current correlation function is,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=c^{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}(\bm{k}_{m}-\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)

(61)

\displaystyle-|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|\tilde{\alpha}_{n}^{*}(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q})\tilde{\alpha}_{m}^{*}(\bm{k}_{m}+\bm{q})2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)+c.c.,

where
c.c.
denotes the complex conjugate terms. Using the expression,

\tilde{\alpha}_{n}(\bm{q})=i\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}}\delta\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right),

(62)

the current correlation function is,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=c^{2}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|(i)\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{n}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)

(63)

\displaystyle\times(-i)\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{m}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{m}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)

\displaystyle-|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|(-i)\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{n}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\times(-i)\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{N}\dfrac{1}{|\bm{k}_{m}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2}}}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{m}+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)

\displaystyle+c.c..

Simplify the expression,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{c^{2}}{N}\sum_{n}\sum_{m}\dfrac{|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|}{\sqrt{(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2})(|\bm{k}_{m}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2})}}

(64)

\displaystyle\times\left[\delta\left(\bm{k}_{n}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)\delta\left(\bm{k}_{m}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\delta\left(\bm{k}_{n}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)\delta\left(\bm{k}_{m}+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)2\pi\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)\right]

\displaystyle+c.c..

To this step, the current correlation function under fluctuating elastic constant with fluctuating length
1/k_{G}
have been obtained in Eq.(
64
), which is one of the main conclusions of this work.

When
\bm{k}_{G}=0
, which indicates homogeneous elasticity,

\dfrac{|\bm{k}_{n}||\bm{k}_{m}|}{\sqrt{(|\bm{k}_{n}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2})(|\bm{k}_{m}|^{2}-\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2})}}=1,

(65)

Eq.(
64
) gives,

C(\bm{q},\omega)=\dfrac{2\pi c^{2}}{N}\delta(\omega-c|\bm{q}|).

(66)

By doing the angular average and integral current correlation function over 3D wave vector space, the vibrational density of states (VDOS) can be obtained as,

\displaystyle D(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}\bm{q}\,C(\bm{q},\omega)

(67)

\displaystyle=\int dq\,C(q,\omega)4\pi q^{2}

\displaystyle=\int dq\,\dfrac{2\pi c^{2}}{N}\delta(\omega-cq)4\pi q^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{8\pi^{2}}{Nc}\omega^{2},

which recover the Debye’s theory for the VDOS of homogeneous solids.

When
\bm{k}_{G}\neq 0
, which indicates inhomogeneous elasticity with fluctuating length
1/k_{G}
, direct computation of Eq.(
64
) is complicated due to involving
\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}
wave vector shift. However, if the wave vector shift is much smaller than Debye’s wave vector,
|\bm{k}_{G}|\ll k_{D}
, the wave vector shift in Delta function in Eq.(
64
) can be negligible even for large wave vector close to
k_{D}
and hence there is still
|\bm{k}_{n}|\equiv|\bm{k}_{m}|
. Therefore the current correlation function becomes,

\displaystyle C(\bm{q},\omega)

\displaystyle\approx\dfrac{2\pi}{N}\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{\sqrt{(\omega^{2}-c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2})(\omega^{2}-c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}\bm{k}_{G}^{2})}}\sum_{n}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q}\right)

(68)

\displaystyle\approx\dfrac{2\pi}{N}\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{\omega^{2}-c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}}\sum_{n}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{n}-\bm{q}\right).

In the first step, the Delta function
\delta(\omega-c|\bm{k}_{n}|)
is calculated to replace
|\bm{k}_{n}|
in the factor. Doing the angular average and integral current correlation function over 3D wave vector space, the vibrational density of states (VDOS) can be obtained as,

\displaystyle D(\omega)

\displaystyle\approx\dfrac{2\pi}{N}\int dq\,\dfrac{\omega^{2}}{\omega^{2}+c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}}4\pi q^{2}

(69)

\displaystyle\approx\dfrac{8\pi^{2}}{Nc}\dfrac{\omega^{4}}{\omega^{2}+c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}}.

Under low frequency
\omega\ll c\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4}}k_{G}
,

\dfrac{\omega^{4}}{\omega^{2}+c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}}\rightarrow\omega^{4},

(70)

under high frequency
\omega\gg c\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4}}k_{G}
,

\dfrac{\omega^{4}}{\omega^{2}+c^{2}\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}}\rightarrow\omega^{2}.

(71)

The frequency
c\sqrt{\dfrac{3}{4}}k_{G}
is where power law change, and hence the frequency of Boson peak.

Another way to to see the effect of nonzero
|\bm{k}_{G}|
is to limit there is only one allowed mode in the displacement intensity field
\phi(\bm{r},t)
, that means,

\phi(\bm{r},t)=\alpha_{0}(\bm{r})e^{-i(\bm{k}_{0}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{0}|t)}+\alpha_{0}^{*}(\bm{r})e^{i(\bm{k}_{0}\cdot\bm{r}-c|\bm{k}_{0}|t)}.

(72)

Then using Eq.(
64
) the current correlation function becomes,

C(\bm{q},\omega)=\dfrac{2\pi c^{2}}{N}\dfrac{k_{0}^{2}}{k_{0}^{2}+\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}}\delta\left(\bm{k}_{0}-\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G}+\bm{q}\right)\right)\delta(\omega-ck_{0}).

(73)

Therefore the
|\bm{k}_{G}|
dressed vibrational mode will appear only at,

C\left(\bm{k}_{0}-\dfrac{1}{2}\bm{k}_{G},ck_{0}\right)=\dfrac{2\pi c^{2}}{N}\dfrac{k_{0}^{2}}{k_{0}^{2}+\dfrac{3}{4}k_{G}^{2}},

(74)

instead of standard acoustic phonon mode
\left(\bm{k}_{0},ck_{0}\right)
. This wave vector shift may correspond to the quasi-flat dispersion relation observed recently in experiments and simulations.

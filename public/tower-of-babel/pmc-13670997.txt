Reply to “Aflibercept 8 mg: From Ocular Clearance to Pharmacodynamic Duration”
Authors: Sébastien Bihorel, Joannellyn Chiu, Jason Chittenden, Thomas Eissing, Kenneth C. Turner, Joachim Höchel, Zhongqing Will He, John D. Davis, Lutz O. Harnisch
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We thank Veritti and colleagues for commenting on our publication “Integrated Population Pharmacokinetic Analysis of Intravitreal Aflibercept Drug Products” in the Letter to the Editor “Aflibercept 8 mg: From Ocular Clearance to Pharmacodynamic Duration” and agree on their thoughts to extend our work to pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling of clinical endpoints. As noted, the analysis presented in our manuscript focused on aflibercept PK. One outcome was that the median ocular concentration of free aflibercept at the end of an 8‐week dosing interval for aflibercept 2 mg was predicted to be reached 6 weeks later for aflibercept 8 mg. This PK benchmark did not address the proportion of participants maintained on certain dosing intervals. In view of the variability of dosing intervals observed in PULSAR and PHOTON (including cases of participants receiving aflibercept 8 mg up to every 24 weeks) [1], we pursued exposure‐response analyses which supported the regulatory review of aflibercept 8 mg in multiple territories and identified ocular clearance (QE) and central retinal thickness (CRT) as prognostic factors of dosing interval shortening [1, 2, 3]. In these analyses, patients with neovascular age‐related macular degeneration, diabetic macular edema, or retinal vein occlusion with fast QE or high baseline CRT, which is indicative of greater disease severity, had an increased likelihood of dosing interval shortening [1, 2, 3]. Downstream of target binding, there is substantial PK, PD, and pathophysiology‐related complexity to arrive at ocular outcome parameters, whether anatomic (CRT) or functional (best‐corrected visual acuity [BCVA]). We recently presented a population PK/PD analysis linking predicted ocular concentrations of free aflibercept with CRT reductions and BCVA improvements in aflibercept 8 mg‐treated patients in PULSAR and PHOTON [4]. This analysis indicated that, although most patients maintain effective ocular concentrations with ≥ 8‐week dosing intervals, a small subset of patients with fast QE and high baseline CRT may require more frequent dosing to maintain effective concentrations and clinical efficacy. This analysis aligns well with Verriti's proposal and warrants further work to evaluate whether the extended duration of aflibercept 8‐mg effects at ≥ 16‐week dosing intervals can be linked to slower QE and/or other patient characteristics (e.g., low CRT).

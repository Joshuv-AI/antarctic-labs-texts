A zero-knowledge enabled dynamic sharding architecture for scalable decentralized smart contract execution in IOT environments
Authors: Kashif Aman, Atiq Ur Rehman, Fahad Masood, Sikander Azam, Abid Iqbal, Muhammad Ismail Mohmand, Ghassan Husnain
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractInternet of Vehicles (IoV) and IoT environment require decentralized platforms that can support a high number of transactions and provide high security and privacy assurance. This study suggests a reputation-aware, zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) based, dynamically sharded smart contract system that is able to process scalable and privacy-preserving transactions. The suggested architecture highly incorporates dynamic sharding, ZKP-based verification, decentralized smart contracts and reputation-based selection of leaders to jump over the scalability, trusting and performance limitations of traditional blockchain systems. There are also extensive experimental assessments that occur within 100–1000 transactions per second (tps) and batch sizes of 10, 30, 50, and 100. Findings indicate that the given framework demonstrates the ability to scale throughput linearly to about 1000 tps, and Enhanced Fabric and Ethereum reach throughput saturation at 140–150 and 15–20 tps, respectively. The proposed system has an average latency of less than 500 ms at an arrival rate of 1000 tps whereas at the same rate, baseline approaches have a latency of over 8000 ms with larger batch sizes. The success rate of the transaction is always above 97, which is due to the isolation of reputation and adaptive scheduling of shards. Moreover, the framework decreases 40–50% and 45–50% the computation overhead and the cost of communication respectively, over heavyweight baseline schemes. These results show that the synergistic implementation of ZKP, dynamic sharding, decentralized smart contracts, and reputation-aware control are a scalable solution with high throughput IoT and IoV applications that is efficient and secure.Keywords: Internet of vehicles, Zero-knowledge proofs, Blockchain sharding, Privacy-preserving authentication, Dynamic load balancing, High-throughput distributed ledgers, Secure and private transactions

Introduction

This development has increased the establishment of the Internet of Vehicles (IoV) a paradigm that provides real-time communication between vehicles, real-time communication between vehicles and roadside infrastructure and vehicle-to-cloud or edge servers communication1. This paradigm enhances intelligent transportation, road safety and stakeholder optimization of traffic by means of flawless Vehicle-to-Vehicle (V2V) and Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) communications2. However, IoV environments produce vast volumes of data which should be moved and authenticated safely within strict time limits. Centralizing solutions are wrong as they have issues of single point of failure and lack of trust3. Consequently, blockchain technology has become a distributed and resistant to tampering ledger system that provides open communication, auditing as well as decentralized trust in vehicular communication systems4. Although blockchain technology offers data integrity and non-repudiation, it creates scalability and bottlenecks in time, which restricts real-time vehicular processes. Classical single-chain blockchain systems, like Hyperledger Fabric, are capable of the moderate throughput, but are prone to congesting when the rates of transaction arrival are high5. It has been found out that when more than 140 transactions per second (TPS) are used, the latency grows exponentially and transaction success rate reduces substantially5,6. This is because sequential transaction validation and ordering cannot be effectively parallelized, and hence the reason behind this behavior7. Sharding has been suggested to overcome challenges in scalability and is divided into different components that run in parallel to tackle the issue8. In order to support high-throughput and scalability, sharding allocates the workload between multiple independent chains (shards) to provide the concurrent validation of a high-volume IoV system. Recent works indicate that load can be balanced using that the shard can be assigned dynamically or based on reputation and this evades the bottlenecks associated with lingering skewed distribution of transactions9,10. Nevertheless, cross-shard communication and consensus synchronization are also an essential issue that can influence the general consistency and performance11. Besides the aspect of scalability, the aspect of latency reduction is also important. IoV messages, e.g. collision warning or lane-change messages, demand sub-second reaction periods. The traditional blockchain consensus mechanisms, including PBFT or PoW, are associated with latency because of multi-round communication. To minimize lag time, hybrid or lightweight consensus models have been suggested, but a perfect balance between speed and security is yet to be attained12. Privacy and verifiability are also necessary factors. The IoV applications process sensitive data, which includes vehicle identity, location, and path, continuously, which poses a significant privacy concern. Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) have become a cryptographic method that allows to verify the correctness of transactions without exposing confidential information12. The recent study shows that batched or recursive zk-SNARKs and zk-STARKs can achieve lower costs of on-chain verification, but also privacy-preserving authentication is achieved13. The purpose of ZKPs in blockchain design is to address confidentiality needs without compromising verifiability which is essential in vehicular networks14.

Additionally, security in consortium-based IoV blockchains is a requirement that is attained by trust management and mitigation of malicious activity. Although permissioned systems have been developed, nodes, including Roadside Units (RSUs) or edge servers can act maliciously by giving false or delayed information. In response to this concern, election and reputation-based schemes of leaders including multi-weight subjective logic (MWSL) schemes that dynamically calculate credibility of the nodes and isolate malicious nodes have been suggested15. These solutions optimize throughput by delegating important consensus functions to honest nodes thereby minimizing the chances of bad block propositions16. Lastly, there are consensus overhead and cryptographic costs such as computational and communication costs, which are quite problematic. IoV devices are frequently underprivileged, and work in intermittent network environments. The existing literature demonstrates that with aggregated ZKP verification and sharding, the one-to-many verification can reduce the cost of verification by several orders of magnitude since multiple transactions can be verified using the same proof. Also, overheads in inter-shard communication are minimized by compression techniques that are bandwidth-efficient17,18.

According to these observations, the existing IoV-blockchain technologies fail to offer all of the opportunities of scalability, low-latency, and privacy protection and effective trust management in the real-world settings. Our solution is much better than the baseline systems in terms of throughput, latency, malicious node detection accuracy and minimizes the computation and communication overhead. The discrete-event simulation findings under the calibration of the state-of-the-art experimental results indicate higher throughput (around 60–70 per cent) and lower latency (cut down to threefold) are found to be higher and lower in the case of high transaction loads, respectively.

Related work

The concept of blockchain has been widely addressed in the context of providing the safety of the information exchange in the Internet of Vehicles (IoV), yet its application is weakened by the concept of the scale. Traditional monolithic blockchain is typically defined by throughput saturation and a highly growing latency as the pace of transaction arrival surpasses processing capacity and has always been witnessed in the literature18,19. To address these limitations a new scalable solution has been suggested known as sharding that divides blockchain state amongst multiple parallel chains. There are initial conceptual deliberations and system models that have determined the possibilities of sharding to assure substantial transaction-level parallelism20,21. Even higher throughput with 600 nodes, and the simplest systems would be capable of doing over 8,000 transactions per second. Sharding therefore does allow the prospect of high performance22. Until performance gaps like cross-shard communication overheads, uneven shards and excessive coordination latency occur during routing vehicles through traffic with high bursts and with non-uniform or erratic mobility are detected23. On a finer-grained analysis and as follow up to the latter complaint about erratic mobility24. Moreover, the author indicated that there should be shard formation dynamism, with regard to trustful metrics, which provide superior load balancing and reduced load performance degradation under vehicular traffic outbursts25. Still on the same point, the author states that throughput in large-scale systems of the monitoring of the IoT increases by 70 percent and it is another sign of the importance of adaptive shards scheduling strategies in practice. All these works lead to the similar conclusion that as much as the significant influence of sharding on scalability can be enormous, the combination of it with trust and privacy and heterogeneity-awareness remains insufficiently developed26,27.

At the same time, along with the similar study on scalability, the privacy has turned out to be another critical requirement of the IoV world, because the telemetry of vehicles, their identities, and their traces may be regarded as highly sensitive information. The zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) is one of the potential cryptocurrency schemes that can be utilized to perform privacy authentications where the nodes can prove the validity of a statement without disclosing any information about the private data. Even though earlier approaches to ZKPs were computationally complex, zk-SNARKs and zk-STARKs represent a recent development in ZKPs28. offered evidence regarding the ability of ZKPs to facilitate federated, off-chain, and verifiable learning allowing the vehicle to remain privy to the data it stores and at the same time participate in collective model updates29. Additional details given by shows that the cost per computer can be saved by up to 80 percent on the utilization of batching and optimized proof-generation pipelines at high accuracy of check30. In line with this, provide a detailed discussion that the lightweight versions of ZKP are needed by oil-constrained devices in the IoV. Nevertheless, the vast majority of ZKP-based systems are still oriented to the public blockchains as opposed to permissioned IoV systems31 in which the stringent requirements on the latency create the need to optimize the proof generation, batching and proof verification schemes on the chain. Industry reports show that, batching and aggregated verification are also necessary to be integrated in order to be an effective implementation since it would be too slow to generate a proof of all the vehicular messages. All these investigations indicate a gap in ZKP-enabling blockchain system planning that can be utilized in actual-time demands of the IoV systems32.

The other important field of research is the reputation systems in order to choose the leaders and to isolate fault in permissioned blockchains. RSUs and vehicles have varying capabilities as far as computation, communication delays, and reliability are all concerned, as are the capabilities of various nodes in heterogeneous IoV. The concept of the reputation-based consensus schemes is aimed at increasing the system strength due to the selection process of the validating nodes among the trustful and high-performing ones. The article33 presents Repu-ICN, a reputation-based consensus protocol on ICN-enhanced blockchain networks that delivers over three times greater throughput than the conventional architecture. The authors propose hybrid leader selection models that incorporate the reputation and performance measures, and are more equitable and less prone to ill intentions. Based on the evidence relating to reputation models applied to IoV, e.g. the models cited in34, reputation-conscious designs lower the cost of the computations and increase the trust of the system. A significant weakness of such works, however, is that the reputation models are typically distinctly isolated into other architecture layers. The enormous majority of the current frameworks are not able to unify the reputation with the shard scheduling, the price of the proof-generation, and the processes of cross-shard communication. The consequence of this split is inefficiency in which the reputation-poor nodes are randomly assigned to the important locations in shards, and this adds the latency and worsens the performance. This literature therefore indicates that there is much need of a unified architecture that incorporates reputation and sharding and privacy architecture35.

Off-chain and hybrid edge/cloud architecture is an increasingly significant part of the system of the IoV, particularly considering the functions of ZKP generation, anomaly detection, and environmental perception, which are computationally-intensive. Even in cases where data is not invariably known, the study36 demonstrates that dual-chain systems integrating alliance blockchains and distributed storage enable achieving low-latency data retrieval and knowingly storing large datasets off-chain. The model of task offloading presented in37 is highly attractive because of the use of the digital-twin technology. This model consists of vehicles that can offload processing to edge servers and the digital twin then gives a blockchain document of the results. The architectures emphasize the importance of considering the on-chain verification delays, communication overhead and off-chain processing delays together in order to model them in the context of real-time vehicular decision making. Although hybrid models have the potential to incur a significant reduction in load on the chain, the literature reveals that fewer studies have been done to assemble them with sharded ledger frameworks or privacy-preserving proof pipelines38.

The experimental studies of the authorized block chains offer some more data which are applicable to the implementation of the IoV using the concept of Hyperledger Fabric and similar enterprise-based blockchain networks displaying an early linear range and later a distinct saturation and final explosion of the latency39. According to even more recent assessments of40, network heterogeneity, block interval tuning and concurrency setting are essential elements of real-world performance. These empirical underpinnings are core in calibration of the simulation models used in research of the IoV such that they reflect the true nature of the transactions in terms of both arrival rates and network delays as well as validation costs. However, they rarely contain any privacy or reputation-related overheads, which results in a dry spot in the multi-factor performance modeling.

The argument of security research also demonstrates the drawbacks associated with sharding and ZKPs integration. The authors defined cross-shard replay attacks, shard-isolation attacks, and inconsistent propagation of proof as established threats of a sharded blockchain, and reported them in41. In a system based on ZKP, aggregator collusion, stale-proof acceptance, and rogue proof generation threats can be used, which may enable compromised nodes to cheat the consensus. This is a particularly weak system of vehicles due to the risk of the existence of malicious cars transmitting falsified event data, which is emphasized by42 also the importance of combined layered defense mechanisms of intrusion detection, reputation resetting, and block chain-based auditing as a solution to the IoT/IoV attacks. These findings highlight the relevance of having architectures that embrace security mechanisms on the overall layers rather than their implementation on a case by case basis. Application-oriented studies can offer additional understanding of realistic loads in the field of IoV 43, analyze either block chain-aided vehicles identity systems, cooperative perception systems or cross-domain systems of trust sharing. According to these works, the practical implementations of these IoV are contingent on the high frequency of messages, variability of the size of data and the timing constraints required, thus the need that any blockchain system be designed to support simultaneously scalability and privacy.

The combination of these results suggests that many issues in question remain to be resolved: sharding improves throughput, yet it does not quantify the cost of privacy and verification, unless one takes them collectively; ZKP systems offer privacy, however they may introduce computational bottlenecks; reputation systems can offer trust, however they are not always considered with schedule and proofs of shards combining; the experimental results of blockchain systems make throughput and latency their primary concern, but they do not operationalize privacy or the cost of trust. All such loopholes lead to the curiosity behind the integrated iotv architecture, which is made up of adaptive shading, batched verification of ZKP and reputation-driven scheduling to realize trustful, scalable, and privacy-preserving blockchain-based vehicular communication. Literature review summary of blockchain techniques for IoV.

Although scalable blockchain frameworks, including Hyperledger Fabric and Ethereum, have made progress, there exist various structural shortcomings in the systems when used in high-throughput IoV systems. Systems that use fabrics are characterized by early throughput saturation and latency plateau caused by serial validation processes and low levels of parallel execution. Ethereum The monolithic architecture of Ethereum and its use of proof-based consensus mechanism have a major overhead of cryptographic computation and communication, which leads to slow transaction rates and a longer confirmation delay. In addition, the current systems generally do not have built-in systems to perform dynamic shard reassignments, statistical zero-knowledge checks and reputation-based scheduling of leaders. This disintegration between scalability, privacy and trust management encourages the necessity of a single architecture that takes into account all such aspects at once throughput, latency, privacy and the security weakness in IoV blockchain implementation.

Beyond blockchain-specific IoV security solutions, recent advancements in lightweight cryptography offer complementary approaches to securing vehicular communications. A 7-stage pipelined Ascon architecture demonstrates optimized encryption for resource-constrained embedded systems, validating the feasibility of lightweight security in IoV environments44. Building on this foundation, a post-quantum enhanced Ascon scheme (m-Ascon) integrated with Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) achieves microsecond-level key generation while maintaining resistance against replay, sybil, and man-in-the-middle attacks45. directly complementing the threat model addressed by the proposed reputation-aware framework. Furthermore, a systematic review of deep learning strategies for cybersecurity provides a comprehensive taxonomy of network attacks and detection methodologies46 that aligns with the multi-weight subjective logic approach to malicious node identification introduced in this paper. Together, these studies establish complementary foundations for lightweight, quantum-resistant IoV security that inform the cryptographic and threat detection components of this work.

Proposed method

As Fig. 1 proposes, the new transaction-processing pipeline can combine identity verification, zero-knowledge proofs, sharding, and smart-contract logic and offers secure and scalable decentralized applications. The working process is divided into four significant steps including identity and proof generation, routing and endorsement of transactions, smart-contract validation, and ordering and ledger commitment. The stages used are self-reliant and enable cross-component communication to verify accuracy and consistency.

Architecture of the proposed ZKP-enabled and dynamically sharded blockchain.

Identity verification and zero-knowledge generation

On the user dimension, the process begins at the stage where all users possess a Decentralized Identifier (DID) that is issued with the help of a trusted authority. The DID is linked to Verifiable Credential (VCs) which is signed against the VC Verifier to determine its authenticity. Once the validation has been performed, the user builds the Zero-Knowledge Proof by assistance of the ZKP component. It is a piece of evidence with the help of which the system could check the accuracy of the attributes of the user without sharing any personal information. The subsequent endorsement and validation processes are some of the inputs of the ZKP.

Transaction routing and shard selection

Once a transaction request has been digitalized by the user, this message is transmitted via the Router/Proxy and this router is the one that determines the shard to be chosen based on the existing load or routing policy of the system. The shards can handle the transactions simultaneously and autonomously. The DApp would communicate with the network through an SDK that packages the proposal of a transaction and transfers it to the chosen shard. This is a design that enables horizontal scaling and adequate distribution of heavy load of transaction between available shards.

Smart-contract layer validation and endorsement

At shard level, Endorsing Peers examine the proposal of the transaction. They verify the consistency of the transaction with the current state of the shard, execute the smart-contract logic to verify the transaction and verify the integrity of ZKP. All the validation processes are conducted at the Smart Contract Layer that contains several large elements: Validity of Transactions Logic: business rules, ZKP accuracy and authenticity. The act of maintaining and managing the existing state of the ledger is referred to as state management. Statement Registry & Context Enforcement: ensures that the contextual demands and domain specific constraints are fulfilled. Entitlement Module: prior to changing state user requirements are verified against user permissions. At this stage, the invalid transactions are canceled (the canceled with references to the diagram). Sound transactions are forwarded to the ordering phase. This layer has dynamic sharding modules that assure coordination of the shards, load balancing of the resources and the synchronization of cross shards.

Ordering and ledger commitment

Correct transactions are submitted to Ordering Service in which a final world order of all transactions is determined. This ensures that this system will behave in a uniform manner even when the transactions are being undertaken by different shards concurrently. Once an order is received, transactions are spread to Committing Peers on suitable shards. These nodes utilize the order of transactions and implement them to their local registry and modify the state of shards. The Transaction Pool temporarily stores the transactions awaiting to be committed and all the long term state information is stored in a distributed form in the Distributed Ledger using a shared database. With this approach, the system supports: fast execution, sharding parallel execution, sharding verification using ZKP, and shards privacy. consistency of the world order across all shards.

The aggregator generates one aggregated verdict Π, which is much less expensive to verify than the cost would be with a number of transactions. The privacy is also preserved through this aggregation mechanism to ensure that unlike the identity and contextual attributes that need to be on-chain, at the Edge layer, only the cryptographic proof is sent to the shard layer. Validators are checking that it is correct using the aggregated proof, and do not have access to confidential information. Moreover, as a variety of transactions are authenticated by a single aggregated authentication procedure, the amortized cost of the authentication per transaction (merely sub linearly) with batch size, as described in Section "ZKP batching model and cost assumptions". This architecture ensures that cryptographic overhead does not slow the system to a throughput bottleneck as it is able to achieve good throughput with strong privacy guarantees, even at large-scale IoVs workloads

In terms of the computational requirements, nodes differ based on the functional needs of the node in the architecture. Zero-knowledge proof generation is done by edge-tier nodes (e.g., a RSU or edge server) and is the most computationally expensive operation and is linearly proportional to the complexity of the transaction circuit as is modeled in Section "ZKP batching model and cost assumptions". This implies that this will need moderate CPU capacity and enough memory to execute tens of thousands of arithmetic constraints in a transaction. Contrary to this, shard validators do aggregate ZKP verification, the cost of which is sublinear in batch size because of proof aggregation (and is much lower than that of per-transaction verification). Transaction sequencing, reputation updates and state management activities are mostly the scope of ordering service nodes, and shard leader that have relatively exacting computation requirements. The framework is practical in the IoV/IoT resource-limited setting through shifting the heavy cryptographic load in edge-tier proof generation and efficient coffin aggregated verification.

Reputation Engine is a system that certifies the trustworthiness of the validators and their performance is verified at the same time and utilized to elect leaders and distribute shards. Lastly, the accepted orders are placed into the acceptance blocks and are stored in the cloud layer by the Ordering Service. The scalability, privacy and high level of consensus during the high vehicular workloads are supported in this multi-tier separation.

Trade-off between batch size and latency

The trade-off between the latency and batch size is a direct result of the decomposition of the latency in the form of Eq. (4), the formation of a batch model in the form of Eq. (2), and the model of the consolidated verification in the form of the equations of the form of the aggregation of the former, i.e. in the form of the product of the elements as in (9). Sublinear aggregation efficiency (Eq. (9)) can be used to increase the batch size β and thus decreases the amortized cost of verification of ZKP per transaction, which in turn enhance the computational scalability. Nevertheless, higher values of β lead to higher batch formation delay at low or moderate arrival rates as shown in Eq. (2) since the transactions have to wait more time to be processed. Latency is therefore convex to β with the result that there is a best batch size β* which minimizes the total expected latency (Eq. (10)). The big batches are useful under heavy traffic where the batch waiting time is insignificant; small batches will be helpful under low traffic where the queuing time can be minimized. This trade-off is an analytical reason behind the latency tendencies that are presented in Section "Mean latency".

The Fig. 4 suggested Reputation-Aware Leader Election and Shard Scheduling algorithm starts with the following input in the form of the collection of the Roadside Units (RSUs) and the corresponding reputation scores, shard loads at the moment, and the computational capacities of the units. The algorithm will initially screen the candidates by ensuring that each RSU has a reputation threshold that must be met, and that the RSU has adequate resource capacity; otherwise, it will not be considered as a leader. The ranking of eligible RSUs is conducted with a composite scoring function, which balances reputation, capacity and current load penalty; the best applicant to a shard is then taken as the leader. Each time a transaction is received, the system examines whether the target shard is overloaded; in this case, that transaction is stored in a dynamically-reassigned manner to the least-loaded shard whose leader has a satisfactory reputation. At the same time, the algorithm constantly checks the node performance and initiates the process of demotion and shard reallocation when the reputation of an RSU drops to a critical isolation point or when the measured failure rate is above a specified threshold hence ensuring the system resilience and load balance when operating under dynamic loads of IoV.

Algorithmic workflow for dynamic leader election and load-aware shard scheduling in IoV blockchains.

Reputation-conscious leader selection is an integration that yields both scalability and security. To stabilize rates of shard services when transaction rates are heavy, the system avoids making inefficient or unreliable nodes the bottlenecks of consensus selectively using shard leadership only to nodes that meet the reputation and capacity checks. At the same time, the multi-weight subjective logic model continuously checks the behavior of the nodes and isolates the low-reputation nodes, minimizing the possibility of malicious block proposals and shard capture. Such a close relationship between trust monitoring and scheduling of shards ensures that, horizontal scaling by use of dynamic sharding, does not affect the security of the system.

In addition, to adapt to varying network state, the shard formation is dynamically changed by continually suggesting changes in the shard load and leader performance metrics. Once one of the shards has reached its capacity threshold, the outgoing remarks are sent to the least-loaded shard and the reputation eligibility restrictions are maintained. It is usually periodically recalculated with the multi-weight subjective logic model to select the leader which under-performing or compromised nodes may be substituted without disrupting the consensus operations. This adaptive control guarantees that shard service rates are balanced and steady to different IoV traffic flows most importantly retaining the scalability and security.

Besides making it more performance-efficient, the integrated architecture also presents the possible attacker surfaces peculiar to ZKP-based reputation-aware sharding. These are reputation-manipulation attacks with collusive feedback, malicious sharing-leader attacks, aggregated proof replay attacks or collusion attacks, and cross-shard (anti-congestion) attacks. Nevertheless, these risks are addressed by multi-weight subjective logic that recurs periodically, that is, threshold-based eligibility of leaders, verification of the aggregated proofs of the shards, global ordering consistency checks and adaptive load-aware redistribution of the shards. This multi-level protection design enhances resiliency as compared to a single-layer or non-layered blockchain design of IoV.

Other than security, it is also necessary to consider the practicable applicability and feasibility of the suggested framework in application. It is especially suited to real-world IoV/IoT systems, which need scalable and privacy-aware and trust-aware transaction processing, e.g., cooperative collision avoidance, vehicle-to-infrastructure traffic control, decentralized vehicular identity, and privacy-focused federated vehicular learning. Nonetheless, a wide range of issues with deployment, such as computational resources of ZKP generation in the edge, cross-domain interoperability, and the necessity to have robust evidence aggregation of reputation in high-dynamic vehicular setting still exist.

Conclusion and future work

The research presents a decentralized application architecture that overcomes disadvantageous effects on scalability, privacy, and performance of the conventional blockchain systems on the IoT and IoV platforms. This method applies zero-knowledge verification of proofs, dynamic sharding, and modular smart-contract layer to provide secure, privacy-preserving and high-throughput transactions. It is possible through segregation of endorsement, ordering and commitment with the view of parallel execution and reduction of bottle necks. When the traffic is dynamic, dynamic shard management balancing occurs. The results of our experiment illustrate that our solution has significantly large throughput and more predictable latency than our blockchain solutions, and with large cadence. The results prove the idea that the cryptographic privacy concerning adaptive architecture is the notion that is really helpful. Though, the ZKP verification and the shard coordination can introduce complexity when added to it, the performance gains would override the overheads in the large scale deployment. The future development is going to reduce the overhead of consensus, improve the inter-shard communication, and make the construction bigger to offer real-time car safety and various IoT sensors. The focus of future work will be on reducing the zero-knowledge overhead of proofs and adaptive sharding to continue the reduction of latency. Massive real-world operation is going to be tested to test the resilience in dynamic IoT/IoV.

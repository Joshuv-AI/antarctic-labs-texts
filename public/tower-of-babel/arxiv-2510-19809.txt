Good quantum codes with addressable and parallelizable non-Clifford gates
Virgile Guemard

Abstract
We revisit a family of good quantum error-correcting codes presented in [ 20 ] , and we show that various sets of addressable and transversal non-Clifford multi-control- Z Z gates can be performed in parallel. The construction relies on the good classical codes of Stichtenoth [ 36 ] , which were previously instantiated in [ 20 ] to yield quantum CSS codes over which addressable logical 𝖢𝖢𝖹 \mathsf{CCZ} gates can be performed at least one at a time. Here, we show that for any m m , there exists a family of good quantum error-correcting codes over qudits for which logical 𝖢 m ​ 𝖹 \mathsf{C}^{m}\mathsf{Z} gates can address specific logical qudits and be performed in parallel. This leads to a significant advantage in the depth overhead of multi-control- Z Z circuits.



1  Introduction

Quantum computers may allow us to efficiently solve problems for which no efficient classical algorithms are known
[
33
,
19
,
18
]
. However, performing such computations on real devices remains extremely challenging due to the effect of noise, which corrupts information along the way. Fortunately, quantum information can be protected by quantum error-correcting codes
[
11
,
32
,
35
]
, and computation can be carried out using fault-tolerant schemes
[
1
,
17
,
14
]
. The goal of fault-tolerance is to perform computations on the logical information while keeping it encoded. In particular, it has been shown that, given any quantum circuit operating with perfect qubits and gates, there exists another circuit, called a fault-tolerant circuit, that performs the same computation with high probability, provided that the noise level remains below a constant threshold. These results, known as threshold theorems, come with a resource overheads and an increase in the length of the computation.

Central to fault-tolerant quantum computation are
logical gates
that can be implemented by low-depth physical circuits. Among them, a gate is said to act transversally on a given quantum code if it can be performed on all the logical qubits at once by acting on all the physical qubits with few-qubit gates. Transversal gates are inherently fault-tolerant, since the low connectivity of the physical circuit prevents errors from propagating. Although no quantum code can support a universal set of transversal gates
[
13
]
, considerable effort has been put to finding codes that admit transversal gates up to the third level of the Clifford hierarchy
[
10
]
, such as
\mathsf{CCZ}
and
T
gates. Such constructions directly reduce the overhead of fault-tolerant schemes when combined with other techniques.

Among quantum codes, low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes, defined by pairs of sparse parity-check matrices, are leading candidates for physical implementations. The search for transversal gates on LDPC codes has been extensively studied, starting with Clifford gates on color codes
[
5
]
and followed by non-Clifford gates
[
6
,
22
,
31
,
24
,
38
]
. Recent efforts also show attempts to adapt asymptotically good quantum LDPC constructions
[
30
,
29
,
26
,
27
,
12
]
to devise codes supporting transversal non-Clifford gates
[
41
,
34
,
40
,
2
,
16
,
25
]
.

Nevertheless, it is crucial to explore the full landscape of quantum codes, including those that are not LDPC, to understand which structural properties can be leveraged to efficiently perform computations at the logical level. In this direction, non-LDPC quantum codes have been employed in
magic state distillation
(MSD) protocols
[
4
]
, which, when combined with gate teleportation, enable universal fault-tolerant computation. The overhead of MSD has been greatly reduced since its original proposal. It was shown in
[
7
,
3
,
23
]
that quantum codes built by puncturing triorthogonal matrices, which may be realized as generator matrices of classical Reed-Muller and Reed-Solomon codes, yield quantum codes over prime-dimensional qudits suitable for MSD protocols. More recently, these techniques were generalized using algebraic geometry (AG) codes, producing asymptotically good binary quantum codes supporting non-Clifford transversal gates
[
39
,
15
,
28
]
.

Furthermore, by relaxing the LDPC constraint, a recent line of work has investigated quantum codes that inherently support transversal non-Clifford logical gates which are also
addressable
. This means that, given any subset of logical qudits, there exists a constant-depth physical circuit whose induced logical gate acts only on this subset. This direction was initiated in
[
21
]
, where the authors generalized the triorthogonal matrix approach to higher orders of orthogonality, which the authors called
addressable orthogonality
. This framework allows for the construction of codes supporting various types of addressable and transversal diagonal gates. In particular, using Reed-Solomon codes, they designed an explicit family of asymptotically good qudit codes supporting addressable
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates. These qudit codes can then be converted into qubit codes at the cost of a polylogarithmic loss in the parameters. In
[
21
, Section 3.3]
the authors also discuss conditions in which logical gates may be parallelized for quantum codes built from Reed-Solomon generating matrices.

Soon after, in
[
20
]
, the same authors extended their previous asymptotic construction by using classical AG codes
[
36
]
together with the puncturing framework of
[
7
,
23
]
. They further observed that a particular property of Stichtenoth’s AG codes
[
36
]
, namely the existence of a transitive group action on the code coordinates, enables the implementation of
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates acting on arbitrary triples of logical qubits. In this way, the authors of
[
20
]
demonstrated the existence of a family of asymptotically good quantum codes supporting fully addressable
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates, a major advance in the direction of fault-tolerant quantum computing.

In this article, we build on the work of
[
21
]
, and in particular on the asymptotically good construction
[
20
]
, and we show the existence of very large sets of addressable non-Clifford gates which can be parallelized. The argument unfolds similarly to the one described in
[
21
, Section 3.3]
.

To state our result precisely, let us introduce its context of application. Let
m
be a positive integer and
q
be a power of a prime
p
. The action of a
physical
(m-1)
-control-
Z
gate
on
m
physical qudits in the computational basis is given as follows:

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}\ket{x_0}\ket{x_1}\dots\ket{x_{m-1}}\coloneq\exp\left(\frac{2i\pi}{p}\,\mathrm{tr}(x_{0}x_{1}\dots x_{m-1})\right)\ket{x_0}\ket{x_1}\dots\ket{x_{m-1}},

where
\mathrm{tr}\coloneq\mathrm{tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is the trace map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and for all
i
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Given a quantum code
\mathcal{Q}
over
q
-dimensional qudits, we may consider the code
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes m}
, composed of
m
copies of
\mathcal{Q}
. We refer to each copy of the original code as a
code block
of
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes m}
. Moreover, we call a
logical interblock
(m-1)
-control-
Z
gate
a circuit composed exclusively of logical
m
-qudit gates of the form
\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}}
, each of which involves at most one logical qudit in each code block. In this work, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\ell
be a prime power, and set
q=\ell^{2}
. Then, for any integer
m\geq 2
satisfying
\ell\geq 2(m+1)
, there exists an asymptotically good family of CSS codes
\mathcal{Q}
with parameters
[[n,k,d]]_{q}
over
q
-dimensional qudits such that, for every integer
\widetilde{m}
with
1<\widetilde{m}\leq m
, the depth of any logical circuit on
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes\widetilde{m}}
composed solely of inter-block
(\widetilde{m}-1)
-control-
Z
gates is upper bounded by
O(k^{\widetilde{m}-1})
.

By considering the case where
q
is even, we can also use the method of
[
28
]
, which was adapted from
[
8
,
9
]
, to convert the good qudit codes into good qubit codes, at the cost of worsening the constant factors of the asymptotic scaling.

This article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review important structural properties of classical codes, as well as introductory notions of function fields. We also describe the asymptotically optimal transitive AG code family of Stichtenoth
[
36
]
.

In Section
3
, we present the quantum code construction, following closely the method and presentation of
[
20
]
. We also show in this section that various sets of logical multi-control-
Z
gates can be addressed in parallel, thus proving Theorem
1.1
.

2  Preliminaries

2.1  Linear codes with transitivity and multiplication properties

In this work, we consider linear codes over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, where
q
is a square. Recall that a linear
[N,K,D]_{q}
code
C
is defined as a
K
-dimensional subspace
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
. The distance
D
is the minimal Hamming distance between any two vectors of
C
. To obtain our result, we will need codes with additional structure, which we now review.

Transitive codes.

Given a permutation
\sigma\in S_{N}
and a vector
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
, we denote by
\sigma(x)
the vector
(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(N)})
. A permutation
\sigma\in S_{N}
is an automorphism of the code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
if, for all
c\in C
, we have
\sigma(c)\in C
. The set of all automorphisms of the code
C
forms a group called the
automorphism group
, denoted
{\rm Aut}(C)\subseteq S_{N}
.
In this work, we require codes whose automorphism group fulfills the following condition.

Definition 2.1
.

We call a subgroup
G
of the symmetric group
S_{N}

transitive
if, for any pair
(i,j)
with
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, there exists a permutation
\sigma\in G
such that
\sigma(i)=j
. A code
C
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of length
N
is said to be
transitive
if its automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(C)
is a transitive subgroup of
S_{N}
.

Codes with multiplication properties.

Of particular interest in this work are codes satisfying a strong condition of self-orthogonality. Given any two vectors
x
and
y
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
, we denote their Schur product by

x\star y\coloneq(x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{N}y_{N}).

Given a linear code
C
, we can form the code
C\star C=\{\,c\star c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}:c,c^{\prime}\in C\,\}
, which we also denote by
C^{\star 2}
. More generally, we define

C^{\star m}=\{\,c^{1}\star c^{2}\star\dots\star c^{m}:c^{1},c^{2},\dots,c^{m}\in C\,\}.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
be a linear code, and let
\underline{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
be a vector with only nonzero components, i.e.,
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\neq 0
. We define

\underline{u}\star C\coloneq\{\,(u_{1}c_{1},\ldots,u_{N}c_{N})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}:(c_{1},\ldots,c_{N})\in C\,\}.

It is clear that the codes
C
and
\underline{u}\star C
have the same length, dimension, and distance. Note, however, that the automorphism groups
{\rm Aut}(C)
and
{\rm Aut}(\underline{u}\star C)
are, in general, non-isomorphic.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
m\geq 1
be an integer. We say that a linear code
C
satisfies the
m
-multiplication property
if there exists a fixed nonzero vector
\underline{u}
such that

\underline{u}\star C^{\star m}\subseteq C^{\perp}.

For any vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
, we call the vector
(1,1,\ldots,1)
, with all entries equal to one, the
all-one vector
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C
be a code satisfying the
m
-multiplication property and containing the all-one vector. Then,
C
satisfies the
\widetilde{m}
-multiplication property for every
1\leq\widetilde{m}\leq m
.

Proof.

By definition of the
m
-multiplication property, for any
m
codewords
c^{1},c^{2},\dots,c^{m}\in C
and any vector
c^{\prime}\in C
, we have

\langle\underline{u}\star c^{1}\star c^{2}\star\dots\star c^{m},c^{\prime}\rangle=0.

In particular, fixing the first
r
codewords to be the all-one vector, we obtain

\langle\underline{u}\star c^{r+1}\star\dots\star c^{m},c^{\prime}\rangle=0.

Thus, the code satisfies the
(m-r)
-multiplication property for every
r<m
.
∎

Notice moreover that
\underline{u}\star C\subseteq C^{\perp}
, and since the all-one vector belongs to
C
, it follows that
\underline{u}\in C^{\perp}
.

2.2  Function fields and algebraic geometry codes

In this section, we fix notation regarding algebraic function fields, as they play a key role in the construction of quantum codes supporting transversal non-Clifford gates. For supplemental definitions regarding the theory of function fields, we refer the reader to Stichtenoth
[
37
]
.

Function fields.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
be the rational function field. Given an algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
, a
place

P
of
F
is the maximal ideal of some valuation ring of
F
. If
P
is a place, we denote by
\mathcal{O}_{P}
its valuation ring. Here, we follow the notation of Stichtenoth
[
36
]
; in other contexts,
P
may be regarded as a point on a curve. The
residue field
at
P
is the quotient ring
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
. The
evaluation
of an element
f\in\mathcal{O}_{P}
at
P
is the image of
f
under the canonical map
\mathcal{O}_{P}\to\mathcal{O}_{P}/P
. It is denoted by
f(P)
and can be read as
f\bmod P
. The
degree
of a place is the degree of the extension of its residue field. A place is called
rational
if its residue field is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, or equivalently, if its degree is equal to one.

Notably, the
q+1
rational places of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
correspond to the set
\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we denote by
(z=\alpha)
the finite rational place corresponding to the maximal ideal generated by the function
(z-\alpha)
, and by
(z=\infty)
the rational place corresponding to the point at infinity.

Given an algebraic extension
F/E
such that
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)\subseteq E\subseteq F
, and a place
Q
of
F
, we say that
Q

lies above
a place
P
of
E
if
P\subseteq Q
, and we denote this relation by
Q\mid P
. If
Q
does not lie above
P
, we denote it as
Q\nmid P
. If
F/E
is of finite degree, the number of places of
F
lying above a given place of
E
is at most the degree of the extension
[F:E]
. We say that a place
P
of
E

splits completely
in
F
if the number of places
Q
of
F
such that
Q\mid P
is exactly
[F:E]
.

Divisors and Riemann-Roch spaces.

A
divisor

D
in a function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
is a formal sum of finitely many places with integer coefficients, namely
D=\sum n_{P}P
. A divisor
D
is said to be
positive
or
effective
, written
D\geq 0
, if all of its coefficients are positive integers. Given an element
f\in F
, we write
(f)
for its associated
principal divisor
. Moreover, we denote the
Riemann-Roch space
associated with a divisor
D
as

\mathcal{L}(D)=\{f\in F:(f)+D\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}.

Writing
D=D_{\text{pos}}-D_{\text{neg}}
as the difference of two effective divisors, we see that
f\in\mathcal{L}(D)
if and only if
f
has poles of limited order at the places of
D_{\text{pos}}
and zeros of sufficient order at the places of
D_{\text{neg}}
. If we have another divisor
D^{\prime}\geq D
, then
\mathcal{L}(D^{\prime})
allows functions with fewer zeros and more poles, and thus
\mathcal{L}(D)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(D^{\prime})
.

Towers of function fields.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a field of characteristic
p
. A
tower of function fields
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is an infinite sequence
\mathcal{E}=(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2},\ldots)
, where each
E_{i}
is an algebraic function field of one variable over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, satisfying the following property: for all
i\geq 0
, the extensions
E_{i+1}/E_{i}
are separable of finite degree
[E_{i+1}:E_{i}]>1
;
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the full constant field of each
E_{i}
; the genus
g(E_{i})
tends to infinity as
i\to\infty
.

Crucially for this work, a tower
\mathcal{E}=(E_{0},E_{1},E_{2},\ldots)
is called a
Galois tower
if all extensions
E_{i}/E_{0}
are Galois.

Automorphisms of function fields.

Given an algebraic extension of function fields
F/E
, an element
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(F/E)
is a permutation of the elements of
F
that fixes
E
. When the extension is of finite degree, we have that
|\text{Aut}(F/E)|\leq[F:E]
, with equality for extensions referred to as
Galois extensions
.

The action of
\text{Aut}(F/E)
extends to valuation rings and places. We denote
\sigma(P)\coloneq\{\sigma(f):f\in P\}
and
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma(P)}\coloneq\{\sigma(f):f\in\mathcal{O}_{P}\}
. We indeed have that
\sigma(P)
is the unique maximal ideal of the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{\sigma(P)}
, and moreover,
\sigma
induces an isomorphism between the residue field of
P
and that of
\sigma(P)
. Thus,
\text{Aut}(F/E)
permutes the places of
F
. Importantly, if
P
is a place of
F
lying over a place
Q
of
E
, then
\sigma(P)
still lies over
Q
, since
\text{Aut}(F/E)
fixes
E
.

Let
P
be a place of degree one. Then, given an element
f\in\mathcal{O}_{P}
, we have the important relation

f(\sigma(P))=\sigma^{-1}(f)(P).

(1)

In words, the evaluation of the function
f
at the place
\sigma(P)
is equal to the evaluation of the function
\sigma^{-1}(f)
at
P
. Indeed, since
f(P)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
f-f(P)\in P
, and thus
\sigma(f)-f(P)\in\sigma(P)
. We therefore obtain
\sigma(f)(\sigma(P))=f(P)
. Substituting
f
with
\sigma^{-1}(f)
yields the relation.

The action of
\text{Aut}(F/E)
extends naturally to an action on the divisor group, by setting, for
D=\sum n_{P}P
,
\sigma(D)=\sum n_{P}\sigma(P)
. A divisor is invariant under
\sigma
if
\sigma(D)=D
.

Algebraic geometry codes.

Given a function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
N
distinct rational places of
F
, written
P_{1},\ldots,P_{N}
, we can form the divisor
R=P_{1}+\ldots+P_{N}
. Let
H
be a divisor with
{\rm supp}\,R\cap{\rm supp}\,H=\emptyset
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{L}(H)\subseteq F
denote the Riemann–Roch space of the divisor
H
. We may consider the
algebraic geometry (AG) code

C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H)\coloneq\{(f(P_{1}),\ldots,f(P_{N}))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}\mid f\in\mathcal{L}(H)\}.

We next review an important property of AG codes that we will use throughout. Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be two divisors such that
H^{\prime}\geq H
, i.e.,
H
and
H^{\prime}
have the same support but all the coefficients of
H^{\prime}
are greater than those of
H
. Then we have
\mathcal{L}(H)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(H^{\prime})
, and intuitively,
\mathcal{L}(H^{\prime})
allows functions with poles of higher degree at the places of
H^{\prime}
compared to those of
H
. This means that, for AG codes, we have
C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H)\subseteq C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H^{\prime})
.

What makes AG codes extremely useful is the existence of lower bounds on their parameters arising directly from the Riemann–Roch theorem. A classical result is the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
g(F)
denote the genus of the function field
F
, defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
R
and
H
be defined as above. Suppose that

2g(F)-2<\deg H<N.

Then, the code
C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H)
is a
[N,K,D]
-code such that
K=\deg H+1-g(F)
and
D\geq N-\deg(H)
. Moreover, the dual code of
C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H)
has distance
D^{\perp}\geq\deg(H)-2g(F)
.

Of importance to us is the automorphism group of an AG code. Suppose that
\text{Aut}(F/E)
fixes two given divisors
R
and
H
. Then, the Riemann–Roch space
\mathcal{L}(H)
is also invariant under the action of
\text{Aut}(F/E)
. This yields the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
be a function field, and let
C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H)
be an AG code such that
\text{Aut}(F/E)
fixes two divisors
R
and
H
. Then
\text{Aut}(F/E)
acts on
C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H)
by setting, for
f\in\mathcal{L}(H)
and
\sigma\in\text{Aut}(F/E)
,

\sigma\big((f(P_{1}),\ldots,f(P_{N}))\big)=\big(f(\sigma(P_{1})),\ldots,f(\sigma(P_{N}))\big).

Moreover, if
N>2g(F)+2
, this action is injective, and
\text{Aut}(F/E)
can be seen as a subgroup of
\text{Aut}(C_{\mathcal{L}}(R,H))
.

When we later combine automorphisms of a code with their multiplicative properties, it will become crucial to notice that

(f(\sigma(P_{1})),\ldots,f(\sigma(P_{N})))=(\sigma^{-1}(f)(P_{1}),\ldots,\sigma^{-1}(f)(P_{N})).

(2)

This follows directly from our previous discussion on the action of the automorphism group of a function field, and in particular from Equation
1
.

2.3  Asymptotically good family of transitive AG codes with multiplication property

In this work, we leverage the optimal Galois tower devised by Stichtenoth
[
36
]
. We first review its main characteristics, which will later be used to define the AG codes of
[
36
]
and to lower bound their parameters.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
36
, Theorem 1.7.]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a field of characteristic
p
, such that
q
is a square, i.e.
q=\ell^{2}
. Then, there exists an infinite tower
\mathcal{E}=(E_{0}\subseteq E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\ldots)
of function fields
E_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfying the following properties:

1.

E_{0}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
is the rational function field.

2.

There exists an element
w\in E_{1}
such that
w^{\ell-1}=z
. Hence, we have
E_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)\subseteq E_{1}
, and the extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)/E_{0}
is of degree
(\ell-1)
.

3.

All extensions
E_{i}/E_{0}
are Galois, and the degree of
E_{i}/E_{0}
is
N_{i}\coloneq[E_{i}:E_{0}]=(\ell-1)\ell^{n}p^{t(i)},
where
t(i)
is a non-negative integer.

4.

The place
(z=1)
of
E_{0}
splits completely in all extensions
E_{i}/E_{0}
. Namely, there are
[E_{i}:E_{0}]
distinct places of
E_{i}
above
(z=1)
, and all of them are rational places of
E_{i}
.

5.

The principal divisor of the function
w
in
E_{i}
is of the form
(w)^{E_{i}}=e^{(i)}_{0}A^{(i)}-e^{(i)}_{\infty}B^{(i)}
, where
A^{(i)}>0
and
B^{(i)}>0
are effective divisors of
E_{i}
. The integer
e^{(i)}_{0}
is the ramification index of the place
(w=0)
in
E_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)
and has the form
e^{(i)}_{0}=\ell^{n-1}p^{r(i)},\quad r(i)\geq 0
. The integer
e^{(i)}_{\infty}
is the ramification index of the place
(w=\infty)
in
E_{i}/\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)
and has the form
e^{(i)}_{\infty}=\ell^{n}p^{s(i)},
where
s(i)\geq 0.

6.

We have the following relation
e_{0}^{(i)}\deg A^{(i)}=e_{\infty}^{(i)}\deg B^{(i)}=[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]
.

7.

The genus
g(E_{i})
satisfies

g(E_{i})=[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]+1-\big(\deg A^{(i)}+\deg B^{(i)}\big)\leq[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)].

In his work, Stichtenoth
[
36
]
proves that this tower of function fields is optimal, namely it attains the Drinfeld–Vladut bound
q^{1/2}-1

We now focus on the family of AG codes constructed by Stichtenoth

Transitive group action.

Lemma 2.8

For any

We defer the proof of this lemma to Appendix

3.1  Good quantum code with multiplication property

Given the code
C_{a,b}^{(i)}
of dimension

, a generator matrix is a full-rank

matrix such that every row represents the valuation of a basis element (a function) in the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(H^{(i)})

Now, let us choose any function field

\mathcal{E}=E_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)\subseteq F_{0}\subseteq E_{1}\subseteq E_{2}\subseteq\dots

of

such that
\zeta\mid(z=1)

lying over
(z=1)

(3)

Constructing a quantum code from a classical code with the multiplication property requires expressing a generator matrix of the classical code in a specific form, as made explicit in the following proposition, which is a slight extension of the approach of
[

]

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be a prime power and let

be an integer satisfying

be a rational place of

. Suppose

, and fix the parameters
a=\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}}{s}\right\rfloor
and
b=\left\lfloor\frac{b_{i}}{s}\right\rfloor
. Then
D_{i}^{\perp}\geq k_{i}
, and a generator matrix of
C_{a,b}^{(i)}
can be put in the form

\tilde{G}=\begin{pNiceMatrix}

(4)

where each

and each

\tilde{G}
, and the block

identity matrix.

For completeness, the proof of this proposition is deferred to Appendix

, as it slightly generalizes the construction of
[
20

From this decomposition, we see that any of the first
k_{i}
rows of
\tilde{G}
corresponds to a function
\tilde{g}_{Q}
, where

(5)

To construct the quantum code, we use the framework developed by Krishna and Tillich

We can now define two codes:

where

Before discussing the logical operators, let us first observe that

\tilde{g}_{Q}
with

The number of logical qubits is thus

k_{i}=|\{Q:Q\mid\zeta\}|=[E_{i}:F],

and the number of physical qubits is

(9)

The next proposition introduces the standard logical orthonormal basis from CSS code theory.

Let the set

3.2  Parallelizable, addressable logical
\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}
gates

In this section, we prove that the quantum code
\mathcal{Q}_{\zeta}^{(i)}
of Equation
7
supports transversal logical multi-control-
Z
gates that are both addressable and parallelizable, to a certain extent. Let us emphasize that in
[
20
]
, it was proven that for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and any triple of logical qudits
Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2}
belonging to three different code blocks of
\mathcal{Q}^{\otimes 3}
, the logical gate
\overline{\mathsf{CCZ}^{\gamma}[Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2}]}
can be implemented by a circuit of physical
\mathsf{CCZ}
gates of depth 1.

Here, we extend this result by analyzing the parallelizability of generalized multi-control-
Z
gates. To this end, let us recall that
\zeta
is a place of a chosen function field
F\in\mathcal{E}
where
F\subset E_{i}
and
E_{i}/F
is a Galois extension. A
physical

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}
gate on qudits corresponding to
P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{m-1}\nmid\zeta
respectively in code blocks
0,1,2,\dots,m-1
, is denoted
\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}[P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{m-1}]
. In addition, a
logical

\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}
gate on logical qudits
Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}\mid\zeta
belonging respectively to code blocks
0,1,2,\dots,m-1
, is denoted by
\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma}[Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}]}
.

Recall that for the AG code
C_{a,b}^{(i)}
, the first
k_{i}
rows of a generator matrix
\tilde{G}
, as given in Proposition
3.1
, are written
\tilde{g}_{Q}
where
Q\mid\zeta
. We now introduce the following notion of
modulation function
, which is central to the extension of the results of
[
20
]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Given any set of logical qudits corresponding to a subset
S\subseteq\{Q:Q\mid\zeta\}
, and a vector
\gamma=(\gamma_{Q})_{Q\mid\zeta}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{i}}
, we define the associated
modulation function

M
as

M\coloneq\sum_{Q\in S}\gamma_{Q}(\underline{u}_{Q})^{-1}\tilde{g}_{Q},

where
\underline{u}=(\underline{u}_{P})_{P\in\text{supp}(R^{(i)})}
is the vector of Proposition
2.9
.

Note that
M
belongs to the Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(H^{(i)})
, since it is defined as a linear combination of functions in
\mathcal{L}(H^{(i)})
. Using this modulation function, we can define logical multi-control-
Z
gates that can address specific logical qudits in parallel.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose
a_{i},b_{i}\geq m+1
, and fix the parameters
a=\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}}{m+1}\right\rfloor
and
b=\left\lfloor\frac{b_{i}}{m+1}\right\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}_{\zeta}
be the quantum code constructed from a generator matrix of
C_{a,b}^{(i)}
, as defined in Equation (
7
). Let
S
be a set of places
Q
such that
Q|\zeta
. Then, for any logical state
\ket{\psi}\in(\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}_{\zeta})^{\otimes m}
, given any modulation function

M=\sum_{Q\in S}\gamma_{Q}(\underline{u}_{Q})^{-1}\tilde{g}_{Q},

and any
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{m-1}\in\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\prod_{Q\in S}\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma_{Q}}[Q,\sigma_{1}(Q),\sigma_{2}(Q),\dots,\sigma_{m-1}(Q)]}\ket{\psi}=\prod_{P\nmid\zeta}\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{-\underline{u}_{P}M(P)}[P,\sigma_{1}(P),\sigma_{2}(P),\dots,\sigma_{m-1}(P)]\ket{\psi}.

(10)

For clarity, the proof of this theorem is deferred to Appendix
A.3
. It relies on the following direct corollary of Lemma
2.10
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Fixing the parameters
a=\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}}{m+1}\right\rfloor
and
b=\left\lfloor\frac{b_{i}}{m+1}\right\rfloor
, the code
C_{a,b}^{(i)}
has the
m
-multiplication property with the vector
\underline{u}
of Proposition
2.9
. That is, for any
m+1
functions
\tilde{f}^{0},\dots,\tilde{f}^{m}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{(i)})

\sum_{P\nmid\zeta}\underline{u}_{P}\tilde{f}^{0}(P)\tilde{f}^{1}(P)\dots\tilde{f}^{m}(P)=-\sum_{Q|\zeta}\underline{u}_{Q}\tilde{f}^{0}(Q)\tilde{f}^{1}(Q)\dots\tilde{f}^{m}(Q).

(11)

Using that the code
C_{a,b}^{(i)}
also has the

-multiplication for

(\widetilde{m}-1)
-control-
Z

3.3  Circuit advantage

In this section, we estimate the advantage of using a circuit of logical multi-control-
Z
gates for which subsets of logical qudits can be addressed in parallel, using the quantum code of Equation (
7
) and the multi-control-

Given a circuit

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
a_{i},b_{i},m\geq 1

a_{i},b_{i}\geq m+1

\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}_{\zeta}
be the quantum code constructed by puncturing a generator matrix of

, as defined in Equation (

\bar{\mathcal{C}}
of
\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}}

k_{i}^{m-1}
.

This should be compared with the all-to-all inter-block

k_{i}
physical qudits, which has a minimal depth of exactly
k_{i}^{m-1}
, leading to a constant depth overhead for the all-to-all logical interblock
\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}}
circuit.

Fix
s\coloneq m+1\geq 3
and let
\ell\geq 6
be a prime power such that
\ell\geq 2s

3.3

a=\left\lfloor\frac{a_{i}}{s}\right\rfloor
and

Throughout, we assume that the conditions of Theorem

are satisfied. We now make the following observations, clarifying the advantages of applying the circuit of Theorem
3.5
to
m

\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}_{\zeta}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

The circuit of Theorem
3.5
is of depth one and can target any
m

(\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}_{\zeta})^{\otimes m}
.

Proof.

For the first part of the lemma, given
P,P^{\prime}\nmid\zeta
and
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)
, if
\sigma(P)=\sigma(P^{\prime})
, then
P=P^{\prime}
.

For the second part, given any pair of places
Q,Q^{\prime}|\zeta
, there exists an element
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)
such that
Q^{\prime}=\sigma(Q)
. This follows from the transitivity of
\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)
on the places above any given rational place of
F
lying above the place
(z=1)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}(z)
. Accordingly, if we let
Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}
be any tuple of places and
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m-1}
the corresponding elements such that
\sigma_{i}(Q_{0})=Q_{i}
, it follows that

\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma_{Q}}[Q_{0},Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m-1}]}=\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma_{Q}}[Q,\sigma_{1}(Q),\dots,\sigma_{m-1}(Q)]}.

∎

Let
\Gamma
denote the Cartesian product of
(m-1)
copies of the group
\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)
:

\Gamma\coloneq\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)\times\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)\times\dots\times\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F),

and let an element be written in bold as
\bm{\sigma}=(\bm{\sigma}_{i})_{i\in[m-1]}\in\Gamma

\bm{\sigma}\in\Gamma
, we can associate a set of logical qudits
S^{\bm{\sigma}}
(a set of places, each referring to a logical qudit), a vector
\gamma^{\bm{\sigma}}

M^{\bm{\sigma}}\coloneq\sum_{Q\in S^{\bm{\sigma}}}\gamma^{\bm{\sigma}}_{Q}(\underline{u}_{Q})^{-1}\tilde{g}_{Q}.

We then obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.9
.

For any logical circuit
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
of inter-block multi-control-
Z
gates, there exists a subset
\overline{\Gamma}\subseteq\Gamma
, sets

, and vectors
\gamma^{\bm{\sigma}}
associated to each
\bm{\sigma}\in\overline{\Gamma}
, such that

\bar{\mathcal{C}}=\prod_{\bm{\sigma}\in\overline{\Gamma}}\prod_{Q\in S^{\bm{\sigma}}}\overline{\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{\gamma^{\bm{\sigma}}_{Q}}[Q,\bm{\sigma}_{1}(Q),\bm{\sigma}_{2}(Q),\dots,\bm{\sigma}_{m-1}(Q)]},

and
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
can be implemented by a physical circuit of depth upper bounded by

Proof.

The logical circuit
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
can be implemented by successively applying the physical circuit

\prod_{P\nmid\zeta}\mathsf{C}^{m-1}\mathsf{Z}^{-\underline{u}_{P}M^{\bm{\sigma}}(P)}[P,\bm{\sigma}_{1}(P),\bm{\sigma}_{2}(P),\dots,\bm{\sigma}_{m-1}(P)]

of Theorem

, which is of depth one. It follows that
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
can be implemented by a circuit of depth
|\overline{\Gamma}|
.
∎

We are now ready for the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

.

The depth of any logical circuit
\bar{\mathcal{C}}
of inter-block multi-control-

\mathcal{Q}^{(i)}_{\zeta}
is upper bounded by

, which is itself equal to

|\text{Gal}(E_{i}/F)|^{m-1}=k_{i}^{m-1}.

∎

Proof of Proposition

.

Let us first prove the degree bound
2g(E_{i})-2<\deg H^{(i)}<N_{i}
. Indeed, for the upper bound, we have

\displaystyle a\deg A^{(i)}+b\deg B^{(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{a_{i}}{s}\deg A^{(i)}+\frac{b_{i}}{s}\deg B^{(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(e_{0}^{(i)}-1)}{s}\deg A^{(i)}+\frac{(\ell-1)e_{\infty}^{(i)}-2}{s}\deg B^{(i)}

\displaystyle<\frac{2e_{0}^{(i)}}{s}\deg A^{(i)}+\frac{(\ell-1)e_{\infty}^{(i)}}{s}\deg B^{(i)}

\displaystyle<\frac{3}{s}[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]=\frac{3N_{i}}{s(\ell-1)}

\displaystyle\leq N_{i},

where in the fourth line we have used item
6.
of Theorem
2.6
, namely that
e_{0}^{(i)}\deg A^{(i)}=e_{\infty}^{(i)}\deg B^{(i)}=[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]
. Moreover, since
\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)/E_{0}
is an extension of degree
\ell-1
, we obtain
[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]=\frac{N_{i}}{\ell-1}
. In the fifth line, we have used that
s\geq 1
and
\ell\geq 4
.

We now prove the lower inequality
2g(E_{i})-2<\deg H^{(i)}
. First, note that

\deg H^{(i)}-2g(E_{i})+2=(a+2)\deg A^{(i)}+(b+2)\deg B^{(i)}-2[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]-2,

and therefore,

\displaystyle\deg H^{(i)}-2g(E_{i})+2

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{a_{i}}{s}+1\right)\deg A^{(i)}+\left(\frac{b_{i}}{s}+1\right)\deg B^{(i)}-2g(E_{i})+2

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2(e_{0}^{(i)}-1)}{s}+1\right)\deg A^{(i)}+\left(\frac{(\ell-1)e_{\infty}^{(i)}-2}{s}+1\right)\deg B^{(i)}-2g(E_{i})+2

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2}{s}+\frac{\ell-1}{s}-2\right)[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]+\left(1-\frac{2}{s}\right)(\deg A^{(i)}+\deg B^{(i)})

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{\ell+1}{s}-2\right)[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)],

where in the third line we have again used that
e_{0}^{(i)}\deg A^{(i)}=e_{\infty}^{(i)}\deg B^{(i)}=[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]
, and in the fourth line that
\deg A^{(i)},\deg B^{(i)}>0
. Thus, if
\ell\geq 2s-1
, it follows that
\deg H^{(i)}-2g(E_{i})+2>0
.

We conclude on the parameters of the code using the standard estimates from Theorem
2.4
,

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle=\deg H^{(i)}+1-g(E_{i}),

\displaystyle D_{i}

\displaystyle\geq N_{i}-\deg H^{(i)},

\displaystyle D_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\geq\deg H^{(i)}-2g(E_{i}).

Explicitly, this yields

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\ell+1}{s}-1\right)[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ell+1-s}{s(\ell-1)}\right)N_{i},

and

\displaystyle D_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{3}{s(\ell-1)}\right)N_{i},

(12)

where we have used that
[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]=[E_{i}:E_{0}]/(\ell-1)
. Finally, for later use, it will be important to lower bound the distance of the dual code, which is obtained as follows:

\displaystyle D_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ell+1}{s}[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]+\left(1-\frac{2}{s}\right)(\deg A^{(i)}+\deg B^{(i)})

\displaystyle>\frac{\ell+1}{s}[E_{i}:\mathbb{F}_{q}(w)]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ell+1}{s(\ell-1)}\right)N_{i}.

∎

Lemma A.1
.

We have
\text{rowspan}(G_{0})\cap\text{rowspan}(G_{1})=0
and

Proof of Lemma
A.1
.

This follows directly from the containment

. Given

, the inner product
\langle g;\underline{u}\star g^{\prime}\rangle

Now, suppose that there exists a relation

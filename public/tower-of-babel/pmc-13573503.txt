Taxonomic Reevaluation of Blackwellomyces Isolates Preserved in Korean Agricultural Culture Collection and Description of Two New Species
Authors: Donghun Kang, Jae Sung Lee, Le Dinh Thao, Daseul Lee, Anbazhagan Mageswari, Jae Su Kim, Tae Young Shin, Seung-Beom Hong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBlackwellomyces is a genus of entomopathogenic fungi in the family Cordycipitaceae (Hypocreales, Sordariomycetes, Ascomycota) that primarily infects arthropod larvae, particularly those of Lepidoptera. Despite its ecological significance, this genus remains poorly studied in Korea. During the re-identification of Korean Cordyceps sensu lato isolates preserved in the Korean Agricultural Culture Collection (KACC), 12 strains were found to belong to the genus Blackwellomyces. Accordingly, this study aimed to accurately identify and clarify the taxonomic status of these isolates. The 12 Blackwellomyces strains were identified using molecular markers, including the internal transcribed spacer (ITS), large subunit rDNA (LSU), translation elongation factor 1-alpha (TEF), and the largest (RPB1) and second-largest (RPB2) subunits of RNA polymerase II. Phylogenetic analyses based on multilocus sequence data revealed that the isolates formed two distinct clades, which were further supported by morphological observations, representing novel species within the genus. Consequently, two new species, Blackwellomyces koreensis sp. nov. and Blackwellomyces larvicola sp. nov., are described from Korea.Keywords: Blackwellomyces, entomopathogenic fungi, new species, phylogenetic analyses

Introduction

Entomopathogenic fungi are those microorganisms which can infect and kill insects, other arthropods, and even non-arthropod microinvertebrates [1]. They are commonly found in soil and are thought to play important ecological roles, not only as regulators of arthropod populations but also through forming complex relationships with plants, either as endophytes or epiphytes [2]. All species in the hypocrealean families Cordycipitaceae and Ophio­cordycipitaceae, as well as some species in the family Clavicipitaceae, are considered entomopathogenic. Although these fungi and their related species exhibit a wide range of interactions with their hosts, they follow similar biological principles in the diseases they cause. Infection typically occurs through penetration of the host cuticle following a series of competitive interactions, with successful infection requiring the fungus to overcome or evade host defenses [3]. Recent advances in fungal phylogenetics have substantially refined the taxonomic framework of entomopathogenic fungi, thereby improving our understanding of their evolutionary relationships and promoting their potential use as environmentally friendly biocontrol agents.

Historically, many entomopathogenic fungi were placed in the genus Cordyceps sensu lato, which encompassed a morphologically diverse group of species. However, recent phylogenetic studies have revealed that Cordyceps s. l. represents several distinct evolutionary lineages, leading to the establishment of multiple genera within the family Cordycipitaceae. Among these, the genus Blackwellomyces Spatafora & Luangsa-ard was established by Kepler et al. to accommodate several species previously placed in Cordyceps based on multigene phylogenetic analyses of Cordycipitaceae. At the time of its establishment, Cordyceps cardinalis and C. pseudomilitaris were transferred to the new genus as Blackwellomyces cardinalis (the type species) and B. pseudomilitaris [4]. Subsequently, additional species have been described from various regions, gradually expanding the diversity of the genus. These include B. lateris described in 2019, four species from Thailand (B. aurantiacus, B. calendulinus, B. minutus, and B. roseostromatus) in 2020, B. kaihuaensis from China in 2023, and B. changningensis and B. taiwanensis from China and Taiwan, respectively, in 2024 [5–9]. Most recently, B. larvatus was described from Guizhou, China in 2025 [10]. As a result, a total of 11 species of Blackwellomyces have been reported worldwide prior to the present study.

Blackwellomyces Spatafora & Luangsa-ard, established in 2017, is characterized by the unique features of its ascospores, which possess irregularly spaced septa and do not disarticulate into part-spores at maturity [4]. The genus Blackwellomyces was first isolated from the larvae of coleopteran and lepidopteran insects, and 11 species have been reported worldwide [4,6]. Blackwellomyces typically produces yellow or orange ascostromata and filiform, multi-septate ascospores that do not break into part-spores upon maturity, distinguishing it from other members of Cordycipitaceae [4]. The asexual morph of this genus is usually Acremonium-like, Evlachovaea-like, or Mariannaea-like [6]. Colonies typically form dense white mycelia on potato dextrose agar (PDA) [8]. Some colonies also produce reddish pigments that readily diffuse into the culture medium [6–8,11].

In this study, we reexamined Cordyceps sensu lato isolates preserved in the Korean Agricultural Culture Collection (KACC) and found that several strains belong to the genus Blackwellomyces. This finding necessitates a reassessment of their taxonomic placement. Although recent taxonomic studies have segregated the genus Blackwellomyces from Cordyceps Fr. based on morphological and molecular evidence, many Cordyceps sensu lato isolates preserved in the KACC have continued to be maintained under their original names assigned prior to these taxonomic revisions. Recent re-identification studies of archived fungal strains in the KACC have demonstrated that reexamination of preserved collections using modern taxonomic approaches can lead to corrected identifications and the discovery of previously unrecorded or novel taxa [12]. Therefore, this study aimed to reevaluate the taxonomic position of Blackwellomyces-related strains in the KACC and to describe new species based on their morphological and molecular characteristics.

Cryopreserved cultures were revived on PDA (Difco Laboratories, Detroit, MI) at 25 °C to obtain actively growing mycelia. DNA extraction, PCR, and sequencing

Fungal mycelia were scraped from 14-day-old cultures grown on PDA at 25 °C. Approximately, 50 mg of fresh mycelia was harvested for DNA extraction. Genomic DNA was extracted using the DNeasy® Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), following the manufacturer’s instructions. The primer pairs ITS5/ITS4 [13], LR0R/LR5 [14], EF1-983F/EF1-2218R [15], RPB1AF/RPB1C [16], and RPB2-5f2/RPB2-7cr [17,18] were used to amplify the internal transcribed spacer (ITS), large subunit ribosomal RNA (LSU), translation elongation factor 1-alpha (TEF), and the largest (RPB1) and second-largest (RPB2) subunits of RNA polymerase II, respectively (Supplementary Table S1). PCR was performed in a total volume of 25 μL containing 12.5 μL MyTaq HS Mix, 1 μL of each primer (10 pmol), 8.5 μL nuclease-free water, and 2 μL genomic DNA (100 ng/μL). Amplification was carried out with an initial denaturation step at 94 °C for 5 min, followed by 30 cycles: denaturation at 94 °C for 30 s; annealing at 52 °C (ITS, LSU), 54 °C (TEF), and 56 °C (RPB1, RPB2) for 30 s; extension at 72 °C for 1 min; and a final extension at 72 °C for 10 min using an MJ Research PTC-200 Thermal Cycler (MJ Research, Ramsey, MN). PCR products were verified by electrophoresis on 1% agarose gels, and subsequently purified and sequenced by a sequencing service (Macrogen Inc., Seoul, South Korea) with the same primers used for amplification.

Phylogenetic analyses

All resulting sequences were edited using the SeqMan version 5.05 software (DNAStar Inc., Madison, WI). The sequence datasets from each locus were individually aligned using MAFFT version 7 with the G-INS-i algorithm [19]. Ambiguously aligned regions at the sequence termini were trimmed, and alignments were inspected and refined using MEGA 11 [20]. The curated alignments were concatenated into a combined dataset for multilocus phylogenetic analysis. Maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic analysis was constructed using IQ-TREE [21]. The best-fit substitution model (TIM2 + F + I+G4) was selected based on ModelFinder implemented in IQ-TREE. Branch support was assessed with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. The combined dataset included sequences from 12 Blackwellomyces isolates, 16 reference species within the genus, and Flavocillium bifurcatum YFCC 6101, which was used as an outgroup. Detailed information of the reference sequences is provided in Table 1. Newly generated sequences were deposited in GenBank, and accession numbers are listed in Table 1.

Morphological observations

All strains were cultured on PDA and incubated at 25 °C in the dark for 14 days. Colony characteristics, including diameter, color (obverse and reverse), and texture, were recorded and observed under a stereo microscope. Microfungal structures were transferred to lactic acid on a slide, covered with a cover slip. Slides were observed and photographed using a model Zeiss AXIO Imager A1 differential interference contrast (DIC) microscope (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany) equipped with an AxioCam ICc3 digital camera (Carl Zeiss, Oberkochen, Germany). Microscopic characteristics (hyphae, phialides, conidia), including size, shape, pigmentation, and conidiogenous properties, were assessed, and measurements were based on at least 50 replicates.

Results

Phylogenetic and morphological analyses

Phylogenetic analyses based on individual gene regions (ITS, LSU, TEF, RPB1, and RPB2) showed that all KACC strains belong to the genus Blackwellomyces, although the ability to separate species differed among gene regions (Supplementary Figs. S1–S5). In the ITS tree, most KACC strains were placed close to B. roseostromatus, but the relationships among closely related species were unclear, indicating that this gene region alone is insufficient for species-level identification. The LSU tree showed a similar pattern, with KACC strains grouped within Blackwellomyces, but several relationships among related species remained unclear. In contrast, the TEF tree clearly separated the KACC strains into two groups corresponding to the two newly recognized species. Similarly, analyses based on RPB1 and RPB2 clearly distinguished the KACC strains from previously described species of Blackwellomyces and showed results consistent with those obtained from the combined dataset. Although small differences were observed among trees based on individual genes, all gene regions consistently supported the recognition of the two Korean lineages as distinct species.

The phylogenetic positions of KACC strains were analyzed analyzed using the concatenated sequence data of the ITS, LSU, TEF, RPB1, and RPB2 loci. The concatenated sequence dataset is composed of 3793 characters (ITS 529, LSU 780, TEF 900, RPB1 730, and RPB2 854 bp). Phylogenetic trees obtained from the concatenated gene datasets using ML for the species within Blackwellomyces are shown in Figure 1. All methods gave similar overall topologies of species relationships as in previous studies based on ML analyses [4,6,9,22,23].

Phylogenetic analysis indicated that 12 strains used in the present study clustered within Blackwellomyces. Among, 11 strains (KACC 43308, KACC 43309, KACC 43310, KACC 43311, KACC 44104, KACC 44105, KACC 44106, KACC 44111, KACC 44429, KACC 44430, and KACC 48438) formed a distinct clade basal to the clade containing B. roseostromatus (BCC 91358, BCC 91359, and BCC 91360). The remaining strain (KACC 43307) formed a distinct and long-branching lineage that is phylogenetically isolated from known Blackwellomyces strains, suggesting that it may represent a separate taxon. Hence, the two new lineages were considered candidates for new species.

KACC 43308 formed white to pale colonies on PDA and produced a distinct purple-red pigment that diffused into the culture medium. Microscopic observations revealed hyaline, septate hyphae, monolayered phialides borne singly on basal lateral cells of synnemata, and obovoid to ellipsoid conidia. Although KACC 43308 is closely related to B. roseostromatus, it can be distinguished by its more intense pigment production in culture and by subtle differences in conidial size and shape, supporting its recognition as a distinct species. KACC 43307 is characterized by white to cream colonies on PDA with uniform circular growth and smooth margins. Microscopic observations revealed hyaline, septate hyphae, monolayered phialides borne singly on basal lateral cells of synnemata, and ellipsoid conidia. Morphologically, KACC 43307 is similar to its closest relative, B. pseudomilitaris, in overall colony appearance and conidial shape; however, it differs in the absence of reddish pigmentation in culture and in its distinct phylogenetic separation as a single lineage.

Based on molecular and morphological analyses, strains deposited in KACC were identified as candidate new species. This study represents the first report of novel Blackwellomyces species in Korea.

Molecular evidence further supports their separation, with B. larvicola differing from the ex-type strain of B. pseudomilitaris by 15 nucleotide positions in the TEF region and 36 nucleotide positions in the RPB2 region. These morphological and molecular differences, together with the distinct phylogenetic placement of B. larvicola, support its recognition as a novel species.

Discussion

In the present study, we aimed to re-identify strains submitted to KACC that were reported as either Blackwellomyces or its earlier related sexual state genera (e.g., Cordyceps), based on a combined analysis of genetic and morphological data. Historically, the classification of Blackwellomyces species was conventionally based on morphological characteristics. However, recent findings suggest that supplementing morphological data with molecular and chemical characteristics significantly improves the accuracy of fungal identification. Previous research has demonstrated that relying solely on ITS sequences is inadequate for the accurate identification of Blackwellomyces species [4]. Therefore, the present study employed multi-locus phylogenetic analyses to clarify their taxonomic identities. Phylogenetic analyses based on the ITS, LSU, TEF, RPB1, and RPB2 gene regions supported the recognition of two new species.

This study introduces two new species, B. larvicola and B. koreensis. Based on ML analysis, a very close evolutionary relationship was confirmed between B. larvicola and B. pseudomilitaris, and a very close evolutionary relationship was confirmed between B. koreensis and B. roseostromatus. B. larvicola grows on insect larvae and produces white mycelium when cultured on PDA. B. larvicola was collected from Korea, whereas B. pseudomilitaris was collected from Thailand. In addition, the conidial dimensions of B. larvicola (4–6 × 1.5–2 μm) are comparable to those of B. pseudomilitaris (4–6 × 1.5–2 μm) [24]. Our phylogenetic analyses also show that B. larvicola and B. pseudomilitaris form a monophyletic clade with 74% of bootstrap supporting value. To further examine the differences between B. larvicola (KACC 43307) and B. pseudomilitaris (BCC 1919T), we compared 900 nucleotides in the TEF region and found 15 bp (2%) difference, and examined the 854 bp RPB2 region with 36 bp (4%) difference. B. koreensis grows on insect larvae and produces a purple-red pigment that diffuses into the culture medium in pure culture. In addition, B. koreensis was collected from Korea, whereas B. roseostromatus was collected from Thailand. B. roseostromatus produces cylindrical phialides occurring singly or in whorls of two to five and oblong-elliptical conidia, whereas B. koreensis is characterized by ampulliform to slightly lageniform phialides with a distinctly swollen basal portion and by smaller conidia (3–6 × 1.5–2 μm) that are ellipsoidal to slightly cylindrical, whereas B. roseostromatus produces larger conidia (5–7 × 1.5–2 μm) [6]. Our phylogenetic analyses show that B. koreensis and B. roseostromatus form a sister clade (71% bootstrap support). To further examine the differences between B. koreensis (KACC 43308) and B. roseostromatus (BCC 91358ᵀ), we compared 529 nucleotides of the ITS region and detected six nucleotide differences (1%), whereas comparison of 900 bp of the TEF region revealed 11 nucleotide differences (1%). Thus, these molecular differences, together with morphological and geographic distinctions, support the recognition of B. larvicola and B. koreensis as distinct species Comparative morphological analyses with closely related species, including B. roseostromatus, B. kaihuaensis, B. aurantiacus, B. pseudomilitaris, B. calendulinus, and B. taiwanensis, further supported the distinctiveness of the two Korean lineages (Table 2).

Morphological comparison between Blackwellomyces species.

In summary, the present study re-identified strains submitted to KACC and led to the recognition of two novel species, B. larvicola and B. koreensis. To date, 11 species of Blackwellomyces have been reported worldwide since the genus was segregated from Cordyceps. Of these, six species have been recorded from Thailand (B. aurantiacus, B. ­calendulinus, B. lateris, B. minutus, B. pseudomilitaris, and B. roseostromatus) [4–6,24]. In addition, B. taiwanensis has been reported from Taiwan [9], B. changningensis, B. kaihuaensis, and B. larvatus from China [7,8,10], and the type species B. cardinalis has been recorded from the USA, China, Japan, Korea, and Thailand [4]. Notably, the two re-identified strains differed from all previously reported Blackwellomyces strains in their combined molecular and morphological characteristics. These results help expand the known diversity of Blackwellomyces in Korea and highlight the importance of reexamining archived Cordyceps sensu lato isolates using an integrative taxonomic approach. Moreover, the increasing diversity of Blackwellomyces species may serve as a valuable resource for driving development and utilization of the genus.

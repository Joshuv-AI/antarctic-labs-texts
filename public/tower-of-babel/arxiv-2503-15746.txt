Polluted Modified Bootstrap Percolation
Janko Gravner, Alexander Holroyd, Sangchul Lee, David Sivakoff

Abstract
In the polluted modified bootstrap percolation model, sites in the square lattice are independently initially occupied with probability p p or closed with probability q q . A site becomes occupied at a subsequent step if it is not closed and has at least one occupied nearest neighbor in each of the two coordinates. We study the final density of occupied sites when p p and q q are both small. We show that this density approaches 0 0 if q ≥ C ​ p 2 / log ⁡ p − 1 q\geq Cp^{2}/\log p^{-1} and 1 1 if q ≤ p 2 / ( log ⁡ p − 1 ) 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) q\leq p^{2}/(\log p^{-1})^{1+o(1)} . Thus we establish a logarithmic correction in the critical scaling, which is known not to be present in the standard model , settling a conjecture of Gravner and McDonald from 1997 .

Introduction

Bootstrap percolation has been extensively studied
as a model for nucleation and metastability. In the version
considered in this paper, each site in the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

is in one of the three states: occupied (also called infected),
closed (immune), or open (susceptible).
Initially, the state of each vertex is chosen independently
at random: occupied with probability
p
and closed with
probability
q
, where
p+q\leq 1
.
We will assume throughout that the probability parameters
p
and
q
are small. After the initial configuration is chosen, it evolves according to a simple deterministic rule in which occupied and closed sites never change their states, but an open site may become occupied. In the
standard bootstrap percolation
, an open site at time
t
becomes occupied at time
t+1
if and only if at least 2 of its 4 nearest neighbors are occupied at time
t
. In the

modified
version, this transition is further
restricted: at each step, every open site that has at least one occupied nearest neighbor to the east or west and at least one occupied nearest neighbor to the north or south becomes occupied.

The standard
“unpolluted”
case with
q=0
, a variant of which was introduced in
[
CLR
]
, has played a central role in the study of spatial dynamics in random settings. The first rigorous result, proved in
[
vE
]
, established that, as soon as
p>0
, every site of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}

is eventually occupied with probability
1
. To probe the metastability
properties of the model, a natural setting is to restrict the lattice
to a finite square of large side length
n
. This lead to
increased understanding of the mechanism for nucleation and the
exponential scaling function is now known to a remarkable precision

[AL, Hol, GHM, HM, HT]
. Many variations were also extensively studied, e.g.
[
BBDM
,
BDMS
,
BBMS1
,
BBMS2
]
. A brief survey cannot do justice to the variety and
depth of the results in this area,
so we refer to the review
[
Mor
]

for further background.

The “polluted” standard case with
q>0
was introduced in

[
GM
]
. Here, the main focus is the final density of occupied sites. The main result of
[
GM
]
is that there exist constants
c,C>0
so that when
(p,q)\to(0,0)
,

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{the origin is occupied}\right)\to\begin{cases}1&\text{if }q<cp^{2}\\
0&\text{if }q>Cp^{2}\\
\end{cases}

Rigorous extensions of this result have been relatively scarce, but
higher-dimensional cases
[
GraH
,
GHS
,
DYZ
]
and general two-dimensional models
[
Gho
]
have been addressed recently.

For the modified model, the argument
in
[
GM
]
, which proves (
1.1
) for the standard rule, allows the possibility
of a logarithmic correction in the scaling (see also open
problem (2) in
[
GHS
]
). In this paper, we
confirm that, indeed,
p^{2}
in (
1.1
) needs to be replaced
by
p^{2}/\log p^{-1}
, although we have to allow a possible correction power of

\log\log p^{-1}
in the lower bound. It is unclear whether
such a power can be removed, and we leave this as a natural open question.
While the modified model is in many senses
similar to the standard one, notably in the dominant exponential term in the nucleation scaling
[
Hol
]
,
there are important differences. The recent result on the scaling correction
[
Har
]
is one example, and the present
paper provides another. The cause is illustrated in Figure
1
,
where narrow “chimneys” — one site wide segments of open sites, with closed sites at their ends — block the inward progress of occupation.

Unsurprisingly, the
chimney phenomenon is closely reflected in our proofs.

In the standard rule, occupation can easily invade such chimneys. We now formally state our results.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
so that if
q\geq Cp^{2}/\log(1/p)
, then

(1.2)

\lim_{q\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{p,q}\bigl((0,0)\text{ is eventually occupied}\,\bigr)=0.

Theorem 1.2
.

If

q\leq\frac{p^{2}}{\log(1/p)(\log\log(1/p))^{4}},

then

(1.3)

\lim_{p\to 0}\mathbb{P}_{p,q}\bigl((0,0)\text{ is eventually occupied}\,\bigr)=1.

We prove these two theorems in the following two sections respectively.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
n=\lfloor\log\log p^{-1}\rfloor
and

q\leq\frac{p^{2}}{\log p^{-1}\cdot n^{4}},

then the probability that a box is good goes to

Proof.

We have, for some constant
C
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(G1)} fails}\right)\leq C\left(\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)^{2}q\right)^{2}\cdot\left(\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\cdot\frac{p}{n}\right)^{2}=C\left(\frac{n^{2}\log p^{-1}}{p^{2}}\cdot q\right)^{2}\leq\frac{C}{n^{4}}\to 0.

Next,

\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(G2)} fails}\right)\leq\left(\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\right)^{2}\cdot q\cdot 4(1-p)^{n/p}\leq 4\,\frac{\log p^{-1}}{n^{2}}e^{-n}\leq 4{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}e}\,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\to 0.

Next, again for some constant
C
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(G3)} fails}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\cdot\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\cdot\frac{p}{\log p^{-1}}\right)\cdot(1-p)^{3\log p^{-1}/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{n^{2}\log p^{-1}}{p}e^{-3\log p^{-1}}=Cn^{2}p^{2}\log p^{-1}\to 0.

Next, for some constant
C
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(G4)} fails}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\cdot\frac{p}{n^{2}}\right)\cdot\left(\frac{n^{3}\log p^{-1}}{p^{2}}\cdot q\right)^{n/4}

\displaystyle\leq C\log p^{-1}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n/4}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{e^{4}}{n}\right)^{n/4}\to 0.

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(G5)} fails}\right)\leq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C}\,\left(\frac{n}{p}\right)^{2}\log p^{-1}\cdot q\leq Cn^{-2}\to 0.

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\text{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(G6)} fails}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\cdot\left(\frac{n\log p^{-1}}{p}\cdot q\right)^{2}\leq\log p^{-1}\cdot p\to 0.

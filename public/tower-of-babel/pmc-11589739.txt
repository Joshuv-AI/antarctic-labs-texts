Severe and post-COVID-19 are associated with high expression of vimentin and reduced expression of N-cadherin
Authors: Esra Yilmaz, Dilek Yilmaz, Ercan Cacan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSARS-CoV-2 penetrates human cells via its spike protein, which mainly interacts with ACE2 receptors, triggering viral replication and an exacerbated immune response characterized by a cytokine storm. Vimentin III, an intermediate filament protein predominantly found in mesenchymal cells, has garnered considerable attention in recent research due to its multifaceted biological roles and significance in the endothelial-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) during various fibrotic processes. However, the pathophysiological mechanisms linking vimentin to SARS-CoV-2 remain incompletely elucidated. In this study, we determined the expression profiles of vimentin in three cohorts: patients admitted to the intensive care unit with SARS-CoV-2 infection, individuals in the 6–12 month convalescent phase post-infection and COVID-19 negative controls. Our objective was to assess the association between peripheral blood biomarkers implicated in endothelial dysfunction and genes related to fibrosis. Serum levels of vimentin and N-cadherin were determined by ELISA, while vimentin gene expression was determined by qRT-PCR. In addition, we examined the correlation between clinical parameters and serum levels of vimentin and N-cadherin in severe COVID-19 patients and healthy counterparts. Our findings revealed elevated serum vimentin levels and increased gene expression in severe COVID-19 patients compared to healthy controls. Conversely, serum N-cadherin levels were diminished in both acute and convalescent stages of severe COVID-19 relative to healthy individuals. Notably, associations were observed between C-reactive protein, lactate dehydrogenase, lymphocyte count and vimentin levels in severe COVID-19 patients, indicative of endothelial dysfunction. Furthermore, our study identified vimentin and N-cadherin as potential diagnostic markers via ROC analysis. Overall, delineating the dysregulation of vimentin and N-cadherin due to SARS-CoV-2 infection in disease pathogenesis and tissue homeostasis offers novel insights for clinical management and targeted therapeutic interventions.Keywords: COVID-19, Vimentin, N-cadherin, EndMT, Endothelial dysfunctionSubject terms: Viral infection, Infection

Introduction

The SARS-CoV-2 infection has triggered the COVID-19 pandemic, one of the deadliest and fastest-spreading diseases in human history. The extraordinary global spread of SARS-CoV-2 has posed serious challenges to public health. Additionally, it has caused significant concern and panic worldwide. Initially, the virus was thought to only cause respiratory problems. However, as the virus spread to large populations, a wide range of symptoms were observed due to its attack on different organs, including liver, kidneys, lungs and heart, leading to multiple organ failure associated with vascular injury1.

Multiple organ failure caused by SARS-CoV-2 is particularly associated with endothelial dysfunction. Endothelial dysfunction, which can lead to narrowing of blood vessels, has emerged as a major health issue endangering the lives of many individuals due to ischemia. Moreover, mounting evidence suggests that endothelial cells play a crucial role in initiating and progressing specific fibrotic processes associated with diseases2. A process called endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) occurs, resulting in a series of cellular and molecular changes in endothelial cells, ultimately leading to their transformation into profibrotic myofibroblasts3.

During the process of EndMT, various changes occur within the cell, with the cellular environment significantly influencing the function of proteins constituting the cell skeleton. Among these proteins N-cadherin facilitates cell–cell adhesion and helps maintain the cell skeleton by being expressed in various organs4–6. Additionally, N-cadherin plays a significant role in adherens junctions in the heart, where it is involved in binding adjacent cardiomyocytes electrically and mechanically. Many diseases, including dilated cardiomyopathy, have been associated with changes in the expression and integrity of N-cadherin7. As N-cadherin facilitates strong adhesion between cardiomyocytes, it is necessary for maintaining the structural integrity of the heart8. However, in contrast, mesenchymal or migratory fibroblastic cells express N-cadherin in cancer9.

Another crucial cell skeleton protein involved in the EndMT mechanism is vimentin, which is specific to the endothelial phenotype and critical for the proper functioning of endothelial cells. Vimentin supports cell membrane integrity, stabilizes certain organelles in the cytoplasm, and plays a role in transmitting membrane receptor signals to the nucleus. When exposed to various factors that alter cellular functions, vimentin can respond to these effects and undergo rearrangement of its filament structure. Released into the cell surface or secreted extracellularly, vimentin interacts with a complex array of receptors or targets in a content-dependent manner10,11. Furthermore, vimentin and N-cadherin serve as targets for infection and play significant roles in the immune response in conditions with high mortality rates, such as lung fibrosis and heart failure12.

However, little is known about how SARS-CoV-2 infection affects endothelial cells and the signaling pathways and processes governing EndMT. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the relationship between vimentin and N-cadherin, both significant activators of EndMT, in COVID-19 patients and different groups with endothelial dysfunction. Furthermore, there is limited information available regarding these proteins in patients with severe SARS-CoV-2 infection and their effects on post infection. To address this, the levels of vimentin and N-cadherin were investigated in severe COVID-19 patients, 6–12 months post-infection and in a healthy control group.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was conducted in compliance with the approval of the Tokat Gaziosmanpaşa University Ethics Committee (Clinical Research Ethics Committee numbered 22-KAEK-074). All participants provided informed consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. A comprehensive patient follow-up and study protocols were established, encompassing distribution criteria and disease-related information such as epidemiological data, age at diagnosis, clinical manifestations, disease progression, initiation of treatment and treatment responses.

Setting and patients

Samples were obtained from patients admitted to the intensive care unit of Yozgat City Hospital with lung involvement, characterized by cytokine storm, and diagnosed with COVID-19. A second group comprised COVID-19 patients who had recovered from severe COVID-19 and were discharged from the hospital's intensive care unit 6 to 12 months prior. A total of 66 individuals, including healthy subjects without a history of COVID-19, were enrolled as the control group, irrespective of gender.

Data collection

A standardized case report form was utilized to collect demographic, clinical and laboratory data. Epidemiological data, gender, age, comorbidities and clinical findings were recorded by medical professionals. Patients and volunteers were briefed on the study's purpose, and informed consent was obtained. Blood samples were collected from each participant in hemogram and EDTA tubes and subsequently centrifuged, with serum samples stored at − 80 °C. Samples were collected from individuals without conditions known to influence or alter vimentin activity.

Analyzing clinical parameters

WBC, LDH, CRP and procalcitonin were measured by a hematology analyzer, a clinical chemistry analyzer, an immunochemistry system and an immunoassay system per manufacturer instructions, respectively (Beckman Coulter).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum vimentin levels were assessed using the ELISA technique according to the manufacturer's instructions (Sunred, China). Standard dilutions were prepared, and serum samples, antibodies, and horseradish peroxidase (HRP) were added to designated wells. Following incubation and washing steps, chromogen solution was added, and optical density was measured at 450 nm using a spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). Concentrations were determined based on a standard curve.

Gene expression analyses

Total RNA was isolated from 1 mL of blood samples taken from each patient by using RiboEx (GeneAll, Korea) according to the manufacturer’s specifications, and the concentration of total extracted RNA was determined by a spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). Gene expression levels were quantified by qRT-PCR using the Luna Universal One-Step RT-qPCR Kit (New England Biolabs, USA). Vimentin and GAPDH primer sequences were utilized, with GAPDH serving as the reference gene. Primers used were Vimentin Forward: 5′ ACG TCT TGA CCT TGA ACG CA′3, Vimentin revers: 5′ TCT TGG CAG CCA CAC TTT CA ′3, GAPDH Forward: 5′AAG GTG AAG GTC GGA GTC AA′3, GAPDH Reverse: 5′ AAT GAA GGG GTC ATT GAT GG ′3. Expression levels were determined using the 2^(−ΔΔCt) method on an qRT-PCR device (Analytik Jena, Germany).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 21.0 software and GraphPad Prism version 9.5.1. Non-parametric tests, including Mann–Whitney U test and ANOVA, were employed due to non-normal distribution of variables. Spearman correlation analysis assessed the relationship between clinical parameters and serum vimentin levels. The diagnostic capacity of serum vimentin levels was evaluated using AUC analysis. Two-tailed P-values were considered statistically significant (*: p < 0.05; **: p ≤ 0.01, ***: p ≤ 0.001).

Results

Clinical and demographic characteristics of severe COVID-19 patients and individuals after 6–12 months post infection, compared to healthy control group

A total of 44 patients with severe COVID-19, including 22 presently diagnosed and 22 with a 6–12 month post-recovery period, were compared to 22 individuals without COVID-19 disease, resulting in a total cohort of 66 participants.

Demographic details of severe COVID-19 patients, individuals after 6–12 months, and the COVID-19-negative (control) group are summarized in Table 1. Among severe COVID-19 patients, there were 12 males and 10 females, with 7 patients having hypertension (HT), 8 with diabetes (DM), 3 with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and 4 with chronic artery disease (CAD). The mean age of these patients was 76 ± 14.1 years. In the group after 6–12 months of COVID-19, there were 8 males and 14 females, with 3 patients having HT and CAD, 6 with COPD, and 3 with DM, with a mean age of 67 ± 18.5 years. The COVID-19-negative group comprised 8 males and 14 females, with a mean age of 31 ± 8.4 years. Individuals in the COVID-19-negative group did not have any chronic diseases. No significant differences were noted in terms of age and gender among severe COVID-19 patients, individuals after 6–12 months, and the COVID-19-negative control group.

Vimentin levels in patients with severe COVID-19 showed an increase compared to the control group, while the serum N-cadherin level demonstrated a decrease

Discussion

The emergence of the “cytokine storm” triggered by SARS-CoV-2 infection is closely associated with the morbidity and mortality of COVID-19 patients, often leading to acute respiratory distress syndrome, respiratory failure, and life-threatening multi-organ dysfunction. Cytokine storm is characterized by fever and dyspnea, laboratory findings are variable and are affected by the underlying cause. Cytokine storm resulting from the secretion of the excessive quantities of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines, which carry out a crucial role in the appearance of some SARS-CoV-2-related complications, in particular acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)13. Several clinical and laboratory abnormalities, such as elevated CRP, ferritin and d-dimer levels, hypoalbuminemia, renal dysfunction, and effusions, are also observed in Covid-19, as they are in cytokine storm disorders14. Lymphopenia is uncommon in cytokine storm disorders but is a hallmark of severe COVID-19 cytokine storms. Prior investigations have underscored the role of a hyper-inflammatory state, known as the cytokine storm, in driving multi-organ dysfunction. Hence, there is a potential link between the cytokine storm and post-COVID-19 complications15. Furthermore, extensive evidence points to the presence of organ dysfunction in long-term COVID-19, particularly affecting cardiac and neuropsychiatric functions. Vascular dysfunction and hypercoagulability may exacerbate multi-organ failure following COVID-19 due to hyperinflammation, reinforcing the potential association with the cytokine storm16.

Although the pathophysiology of COVID-19 has not been fully elucidated, it is associated with platelet-dependent endothelial inflammation, increased coagulation factors, acquired antiphospholipid antibodies, and decreased endogenous anticoagulant levels. These mechanisms lead to immune cell recruitment, platelet activation, aggregation, and activation of coagulation cascade pathways, resulting in endothelial injury and thrombosis17,18. Consequently, markers of hyperinflammation such as CRP, WBC, and PROC levels are elevated in severe COVID-19 patients compared to the control group. Additionally, troponin I, D-dimer, and LDH levels serve as valuable short-term risk stratification markers for the clinical consequences of COVID-19 infection. Our study also reveals that severe COVID-19 patients, even after 6–12 months, exhibit higher CRP, D-dimer, fibrinogen, LDH and troponin I levels compared to healthy individuals. Furthermore, fibrinogen and LDH levels remain elevated in both acute and post-recovery stages of the disease. Hypoxia-induced disruption of the electron transport chain in mitochondria leads to lactate accumulation, a process associated with myofibroblast differentiation and pulmonary fibrosis19,20. Recent evidence suggests that lactate may trigger endothelial-mesenchymal transition (EndMT), contributing to cardiac fibrosis21–24. Elevated lactate and troponin I levels in the post-disease period indicate ongoing EndMT processes25. However, lymphocyte levels are diminished in severe COVID-19 patients, whereas platelet levels are lower both during and after the acute phase of the disease (Table 2). The mechanism underlying thrombocytopenia is multifactorial and involves platelet activation, endothelial damage, and viral infection-induced bone marrow suppression26.

Previous research has demonstrated that serum vimentin levels increase in response to inflammation, with high levels observed in systemic inflammatory response syndrome27. Vimentin is a type III intermediate filament protein and is widely expressed in cells of mesenchymal origin such as endothelial cells, fibroblasts and monocytes28. In addition to its key role in intermediate filament formation, vimentin is present on the extracellular surface of endothelial cells, vascular smooth muscle cells and macrophages29,30. It is also expressed in various cells, including vimentin, lymphocytes and neutrophils. Vimentin plays a role in various inflammatory diseases, and our study reveals elevated vimentin levels and gene expression in severe COVID-19 patients and during the post-recovery period compared to the control group. These findings suggest that increased vimentin may be a hallmark of COVID-19, with a significant correlation between serum vimentin levels and gene expression. ROC analysis indicates that serum vimentin may serve as an additional diagnostic biomarker for severe COVID-19, reflecting its role in regulating the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines30–33. Vimentin, expressed in various immune cells and endothelial cells, is released during hypercoagulation and hyperinflammation, contributing to endothelial dysfunction and EndMT in SARS-CoV-2 infection.

SARS-CoV-2-induced endothelial dysfunction leads to vasoconstriction and ischemia, triggering anaerobic respiration and lactate production by LDH34,35. Correlation analysis in severe COVID-19 patients reveals significant associations between serum vimentin levels and LDH, CRP, and lymphocyte count. Lymphopenia, a hallmark of severe COVID-19, is attributed to the cytokine storm-induced lymphoid organ atrophy and impaired lymphocyte proliferation in the presence of lactic acidosis36. The negative correlation between lymphocytes and vimentin suggests that increased vimentin levels, indicative of organ fibrosis, inversely correlate with lymphocyte levels. The relationship between LDH, CRP, and serum vimentin levels warrants further investigation to elucidate their contribution to COVID-19 outcomes.

Cell–cell adherents junctions mediated by N-cadherin, play crucial roles in tissue adhesion, morphogenesis, and cell recognition. N-cadherin-mediated AJs anchor the cytoskeleton in muscle cells, maintaining tissue polarity and integrity37. Our study demonstrates significantly reduced N-cadherin levels in severe COVID-19 patients and during the post-recovery period compared to healthy individuals. Decreased cell–cell adhesion due to reduced N-cadherin levels may compromise tissue integrity and function, potentially leading to tissue fragility and susceptibility to damage38. The significance of reduced N-cadherin levels depends on the context, with implications for both normal development and pathological conditions.

In conclusion, COVID-19 can lead to severe respiratory complications and multi-organ failure, including ischemia–reperfusion (IR) injury, as indicated by elevated Vimentin and other biomarkers. Understanding the pathophysiology of the COVID-19-related cytokine storm and its involvement in multi-organ failure may yield therapeutic targets to mitigate disease severity and improve outcomes. Additionally, integrating genetic studies and clinical data on risk factors and outcomes is crucial for comprehensive disease evaluation and management. Further research is needed to elucidate the complex interplay of inflammatory mediators, endothelial dysfunction, and tissue integrity in COVID-19 pathogenesis.

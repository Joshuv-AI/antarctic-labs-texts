Chromatic Purity in Hermitian K-Theory at p = 2 p=2
Jordan Levin

Abstract
In this article we investigate the question of chromatic purity of {L} \LL -theory. To do so, we utilize the theory of additive {GW} \GW and {L} \LL -theory in the language of Poincaré categories as laid out in the series of papers [ Cal+20 , Cal+20a , Cal+20b , Cal+22 ] along with the companion paper [ HS21 ] . We apply this theory to chromatically localised {L} \LL -theory at the prime p = 2 p=2 and recover the {L} \LL -theoretic analogues of chromatic purity for 𝐄 1 \mathbf{E}_{1} -rings with involution. From this, we deduce that {L} \LL -theory does not exhibit chromatic redshift. We deduce the higher chromatic vanishing of quadratic {L} \LL -theory of arbitrary idempotent complete categories, thereby allowing the use of Hermitian trace methods to probe chromatic behaviour of {GW} \GW and {L} \LL -theory. Finally, we show that for T ⁡ ( n + 1 ) T(n+1) -acyclic rings with involution, T ⁡ ( n + 1 ) T(n+1) -local {GW} \GW -theory depends only on T ⁡ ( n + 1 ) T(n+1) -local {K} \K -theory and the associated duality, thereby proving a chromatic analogue of the homotopy limit problem for {GW} \GW -theory.

Introduction

The
\infty
-category of spectra
\mathrm{Sp}
is the primary object of study in stable homotopy theory. For each prime
p
, the chromatic perspective on spectra seeks to decompose
\mathrm{Sp}_{(p)}
- those spectra with
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-local homotopy groups - into a sequence of subcategories or “layers” each of which captures further refined homotopy-theoretic data. Informally, we can speak of a
p
-local spectrum as being of height
n
if it lives in the
n
th layer of this sequence. For the sake of the reader we shall present the precise definition of chromatic height. For this, fix a prime
p
and recall that the
i
th telescope at
p
,
T(i)
, is the spectrum
\mathbb{S}/(p^{e_{0}},\ldots,v_{m-1}^{e_{i-1}})[v_{i}^{-1}]
. The precise exponents are irrelevant for our treatment and can safely be ignored. By convention
T(0)=H\mathbb{Q}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
X
be a spectrum. We say that
X
is of
height
\leq n
if
X
is
T(i)
-acylic for all
i>n
. We say
X
is of
height
\geq n
if
A
is not
T(n)
-acyclic. We say that
A
is of
height exactly
n
if it both of height
\leq n
and
\geq n
.

For
A\in\mathrm{CAlg}
, being of height
\leq n
is equivalent to being
T(n+1)
-acyclic by
[
Hah22
]
. Thus the height of
A
is the smallest
n
such that
R\otimes T(n)\not\simeq 0
. Because we will often work with
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-rings, we are required to use Definition

.

Ausoni-Rognes
[
AR02
]
computed the homotopy of
\mathbb{S}/(3,v_{1})\otimes\K(\mathrm{ku}_{p})
and demonstrated that
\K(\mathrm{ku}_{p})
is of height 2 whereas
\mathrm{ku}_{p}
is of height 1. They conjectured that this phenomenon should be completely general, algebraic
\K
-theory sends height

Conjecture

(Chromatic Redshift)
.

Suppose
A
is an
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-ring of height exactly
n
. Then
\K(A)
is an
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-ring of height height exactly

The proof of this theorem was finally completed in

]
but the machinery required spans a number of articles. Let us draw particular attention to the works
[

]
,
[
Lan+20
]
,
[

The redshift conjecture can be further refined to a question about
localising invariants
more generally. Roughly speaking, a localising invariant is a functor
E\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Sp}
that sends bifibre sequences in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
to exact sequences of spectra. Examples of localising invariants other than
\K
-theory include
\mathrm{THH}
and
\mathrm{TC}
. This leads to a natural question.

Question

(
E
-heightshift)
.

Let
E
be a localising invariant and suppose
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
has mapping spectra of height
\leq n
, then what about be said about the height of
E(\mathcal{C})
?

There is an alternative way one might want to generalise this question. Instead, we could start not with stable
\infty
-categories and localising invariants but rather
Poincaré

\infty
-categories and
Poincaré
localising invariants. Whereas stable
\infty
-categories capture the behaviour of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)
for an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring

-categories capture the behaviour of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(A)

]
.

1.1
Recollections

Definition 1.2
.

A
Hermitian category
is a pair
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
with
\mathcal{C}
a stable
\infty
-category and
\Qoppa\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sp}
a reduced 2-excisive or
quadratic
functor. To a Hermitian category
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
we can associate a symmetric bilinear functor
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sp}
called the
cross effect
or
bilinear part

1

There is also a linear functor
\Lambda_{\Qoppa}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sp}
which measures the difference between
\Qoppa
and
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}
in a precise sense.
. We say a Hermitian category
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
is a
Poincaré category
if for each
X\in\mathcal{C}
,
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}(X,-)\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sp}
factors though the spectrum-valued Yoneda embedding so that we have
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}(X,-)\simeq\map_{\mathcal{C}}(-,\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}(X))
for a functor
\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
. We require
\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}
is an equivalence (whose inverse is then necessarily
\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}^{\mathrm{op}}
). We shall call the
\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}
the
duality
associated to
\Qoppa
.

Remark 1.3
.

Hermitian
\infty
-categories assemble into an
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{h}}_{\infty}
of which Poincaré categories form a non-full subcategory
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\infty}
. Explicitly
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{h}}_{\infty}
is the Cartesian unstraightening of the functor
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\Funq(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Sp})
. This means that a map of Hermitian categories
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}})\to(\mathcal{D},\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}})
is a pair
(f,\eta)
where
f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is an exact functor between stable categories and
\eta\colon\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}}\to f^{*}\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}}\vcentcolon=\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}}\circ(f^{\mathrm{op}})
is a natural transformation of functors
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Sp}
. If
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}})
and
(\mathcal{D},\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}})
are Poincaré, then a map of Hermitian categories
(f,\eta)
induces a map on cross effects
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}}}\to(f\times f)^{*}\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}}}
. We obtain, for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
, a natural transformation
\map_{\mathcal{C}}(-,\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}}}(x))\to\map_{\mathcal{D}}(f(-),\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}}}(f^{\mathrm{op}}(x)))
and by Yoneda a map in
f\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}}}(x)\to\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}}}f^{\mathrm{op}}(x)
. This map is natural in
x
and so we obtain a natural transformation
f\circ\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{C}}}\to\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa_{\mathcal{D}}}\circ f^{\mathrm{op}}
. Then we say that
(f,\eta)
is a map of Poincaré categories if the aforementioned natural transformation on dualities is an equivalence. Succinctly, maps of Poincaré categories are those maps of Hermitian categories which
preserve the duality
.

We present some fundamental examples of Poincaré categories which will play a central role in this article.

Example 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable category with duality
\mathrm{D}
, that is
\mathrm{D}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathcal{C}
along with higher coherences thereby endowing
\mathcal{C}
with the structure of a homotopy fixed point of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
with the
(-)^{\mathrm{op}}
-action. To the pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{D})
we associate a symmetric bilinear functor via the association
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{D}}\colon(x,y)\mapsto\map_{\mathcal{C}}(x,\mathrm{D}y)
. From this we build two canonical Poincaré categories with this cross effect.

(1)

(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa_{\mathrm{D}}^{q})
with
\Qoppa_{\mathrm{D}}^{q}(x)=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{D}}(x,x)_{hC_{2}}
. This is the
quadratic
Poincaré structure associated to
\mathrm{D}
.

(2)

(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa_{\mathrm{D}}^{s})
with
\Qoppa_{\mathrm{D}}^{s}(x)=\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{D}}(x,x)^{hC_{2}}
. This is the
symmetric
Poincaré structure associated to
\mathrm{D}
.

(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa_{\mathrm{D}}^{q})
and
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa_{\mathrm{D}}^{s})
are respectively the initial and terminal Poincaré categories underlying stable category
\mathcal{C}
and duality
\mathrm{D}
. An important special case of these comes from
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-rings with anti-involution
, that is, an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring
A
equipped with a ring map
\sigma\colon A\xrightarrow{\simeq}A^{\mathrm{op}}
. In this case we obtain a duality on
\Perf(A)
via
M\mapsto\map_{A}(M,A)
with associated bilinear form

(M,N)\mapsto\map_{A\otimes A}(M\otimes N,A).

We shall call a Poincaré structure
\Qoppa
on
\Perf(A)

compatible with
\sigma
if its duality (equivalently bilinear part) is as above.

Returning to our discussion of localising invariants, we may then define a
Poincaré localising invariant
as a functor
E\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Sp}
sending bifibre sequences in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\infty}
to exact sequences of spectra. Among those, the most fundamental are Grothendieck-Witt (
\GW
) theory and
\LL
-theory which are both universal in a precise sense - see
[
Cal+20a
, Corollary 4.4.2, Theorem 4.4.12]
. Heuristically,
\GW
-theory is at least as inaccessible as
\K
-theory and so is difficult to analyse directly. On the other hand
\LL
-theory is more easily understood than both
\K
and
\GW
-theory.

1.2
Main Results

This article takes inspiration from two papers,
[
Lan+20
]
and
[
Lan22
]
. The latter demonstrates a lack of redshift for
\LL
-theory of
connective

\mathbf{E}_{1}
-rings with anti-involution, see Corollary 15 in
loc. cit.
for a precise statement. Further details can be found in Section
3
of this article. The former paper proves a fundamental result concerning
chromatic purity
of algebraic
\K
-theory. Broadly speaking, chromatic purity results for a localising invariant
E
are those of the form “for all rings
A
, some chromatic localisation of
E(A)
depends only on some (possibly different) chromatic localisation of
A
”. Our goal will be to follow the structure of their proof to establish similar results for
\LL
-theory, and derive a number of interesting consequences. The statement of our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem A

(Purity)
.

Let
(A,\sigma)
be an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring with anti-involution, and let
p=2
.

(1)

For
n\geq 0
, the map
(A,\sigma)\to(L_{n}^{f}A,L_{n}^{f}\sigma)
induces an equivalence on
T(n)
-local quadratic
\LL
-theory

(2)

For
n\geq 1
, the map
(A,\sigma)\to(\LL_{T}A,\LL_{T}\sigma)
induces an equivalence on
T(n)
-local quadratic
\LL
-theory. Here
T
denotes
T(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus T(n)
.

In fact, part (2) of this theorem together with
[
Lan22
, Theorem 1]
implies that quadratic
\LL
-theory of
any ring
vanishes
T(n)
-locally for any
n\geq 1
, since we may reduce to the connective case. This allows us to invoke Land’s aforementioned result to show that quadratic
\LL
-theory does not exhibit chromatic redshift for all
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-rings.

Theorem B

(No Redshift)
.

Suppose
n\geq 0
. Let
A
be an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring with anti-involution
\sigma
and let
(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
be a Poincaré category with duality compatible with
\sigma
. Then if
A
is
T(n)
-acyclic then
\LL(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
is
T(n)
-acyclic.

In particular, if
A
has height
n\geq 0
then
\LL^{s}(A,\sigma)
has height
\leq n
. By this notation we mean
\LL^{s}(A,\sigma)\vcentcolon=\LL(A,\Qoppa_{\sigma}^{s})
. We will use similar notation for the quadratic and Tate Poincaré structures.

Theorems
A
and
B
has an important consequence for chromatic vanishing results for general idempotent complete Poincaré categories by the following result.

Proposition 1.5
.

If
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
be an idempotent complete Poincaré category, then
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
is a filtered colimit of Poincaré categories associated to rings to involution.

Proof.

By
[
Cal+20
, Observation 1.2.19]
we may write
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
as a filtered colimit over the poset of its full subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
generated by finitely many elements and closed under the duality, where we give each full subcategory the restricted Poincaré structure. By idempotent completeness and cofinality, we may take this colimit over the poset
I
consisting of subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{i}
generated by a single self dual object
x_{i}
of the form
x_{i}=y_{i}\oplus\mathrm{D}(y_{i})
for some
y_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Here,
x_{i}
carries a canonical hyperbolic form. Furthermore, by the Schwede-Shipley theorem
\mathcal{C}_{i}\simeq\Perf(\End(x_{i}))
via
x\in\mathcal{C}_{i}\mapsto\map_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i},x)
. The hyperbolic form yields a point
q\in\Omega^{\infty}\mathrm{B}(x_{i},x_{i})^{hC_{2}}
which by adjunction is a map
S^{0}\to\mathrm{B}(x_{i},x_{i})^{hC_{2}}
, that is to say, a
C_{2}
-equivariant map of spectra
S^{0}\to\mathrm{B}(x_{i},x_{i})=\map_{\mathcal{C}}(x_{i},D(x_{i}))
.

Under the Schwede-Shipley equivalence, this corresponds to a
C_{2}
-equivariant map of
\End(x_{i})
-modules

\End(x_{i})\to\map_{\End(x_{i})}(\End(x_{i}),D(\End(x_{i}))).

The right hand side is the module with involution associated to the Poincaré category
(\Perf(\End(x_{i})),\Qoppa^{q})
. The map in question identifies with post composition with the given equivalence
x_{i}\to D(x_{i})
and is thus an equivalence. By the recognition criterion of
[
Cal+20
, Proposition 3.1.14]
, the Poincaré structure on
\Perf(\End(x_{i}))
comes from an anti-involution
\sigma_{i}
on
\End(x_{i})
.
∎

Since
\LL
-theory and the
L_{T(n)}
both commute with filtered colimits, we may leverage Proposition
1.5
to obtain a variety of more general results. First we have Theorem
A
(1) for categories.

Theorem C
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
be an idempotent complete Poincaré category. For
n\geq 0
, the map
(\mathcal{C},\sigma)\to(L_{n}^{f}\mathcal{C},L_{n}^{f}\Qoppa)
in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\mathrm{idem}}
induces an equivalence on
T(n)
-local quadratic
\LL
-theory.

Proof.

We recall that by
[
BM+24
, Corollary 2.13]
, the functor
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\mapsto(L_{n}^{f}\mathcal{C},L_{n}^{f}\Qoppa)
is given by tensoring with
\Perf(L_{n}^{f}S)
and all idempotent Poincarćategories of this form are closed under limits and colimits in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\mathrm{idem}}
. This observation, combined with Proposition
1.5
allows us to write
(L_{n}^{f}\mathcal{C},L_{n}^{f}\Qoppa)
as a filtered colimit in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\mathrm{idem}}
of Poincaré categories associated to rings with involution, each of whose underlying stable category is of the form
\Perf(L_{n}^{f}A_{i})
. The map

L_{T(n)}\LL(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\mapsto L_{T(n)}\LL(L_{n}^{f}\mathcal{C},L_{n}^{f}\Qoppa)

in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\mathrm{idem}}
is induced by maps of the form
L_{T(n)}\LL^{q}(A,\sigma)\to L_{T(n)}\LL^{q}(L_{n}^{f}A,L_{n}^{f}\sigma)
each of which is an equivalence and so the result follows.
∎

Another is the vanishing of higher chromatically localised quadratic
\LL
-theory for
all
idempotent complete Poincaré categories.

Theorem D
.

Suppose
n\geq 1
. Let
\Qoppa
be an a Poincaré structure on
\mathcal{C}
where
\mathcal{C}
is idempotent complete. Then
\LL^{q}(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
is
T(n)
-acyclic.

Proof.

Passing to filtered colimits (and using the notation present in the proof of Proposition
1.5
) we are position to apply Theorem

. For
n\geq 1
we have

L_{T(n)}\LL^{q}(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq\colim_{I}L_{T(n)}\LL^{q}(\mathcal{C}_{i},\Qoppa|_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}))\simeq\colim_{I}\LL^{q}(\Perf(\End(x_{i}),\Qoppa_{\sigma_{i}}))

where
\Qoppa_{\sigma_{i}}
a Poincaré structure on
\Perf(\End(x_{i}))
compatible with the involution
\sigma_{i}
. The latter vanishes term-wise, first by using Theorem

to reduce to the connective case, then by the truncating property of quadratic

-theory to reduce to the discrete case, and then finally by appealing

Lan22

which shows vanishing from the discrete case.

The above results tells us that an invariant called

Given our results, is easy to deduce a version of the
homotopy limit problem
for chromatically localised
\GW
-theory. We provide the proof here for the reader interested only in

Theorem E
.

Let
n\geq 0
and let
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
be a an idempotent complete Poincaré category whose underlying category has
T(n+1)
-acyclic endomorphism spectra, then

L_{T(n+1)}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq(L_{T(n+1)}\K(\mathcal{C}))^{hC_{2}}.

In particular if
\mathcal{C}
is of the form
\Perf(A)
and the given Poincaré structure endows
(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
with the structure of a symmetric monoidal Poincaré category, then
\GW(A,\Qoppa)
has height
\leq n+1
.

Proof.

The genuine
C_{2}
-spectrum
\KR(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
of
[
Cal+20a
, Corollary 4.5.2]
gives rise to a a fibre sequence of the form

\K(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)_{hC_{2}}\to\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\to\LL(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa).

Applying
L_{T(n+1)}
, and using the fact that category of
T(n)
-local spectra is
\infty
-semiadditive from
[
CSY20
]
, we may rewrite this sequence as

(L_{T(n+1)}\K(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa))^{hC_{2}}\to L_{T(n+1)}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\to L_{T(n+1)}\LL(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa).

By Lemma
3.8
, the latter term vanishes. The final result follows from
[
Lan+20
, Theorem B]
and the existence of an
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-ring map
(L_{T(n+1)}\K(A))^{hC_{2}}\to L_{T(n+1)}\K(A)
.
∎

From Theorem

, we see that that

-local

-theory has the same descent properties as

-
\K
-theory under the assumptions that mapping spectra are

-local. This recovers
[

and
[

, Theorem 1.1]

Theorem F
.

Let
n\geq 0
and let
X
be a
\pi
-finite
2
-group acting idempotent complete Poincaré category
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
then we have

L_{T(n+1)}\GW(\lim_{X}(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa))\simeq\lim_{X}L_{T(n+1)}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa).

The colimit result follows analogously.

Proof.

The proof is a direct computation using the
\infty
-semiadditivity of the category of
T(n+1)
-local spectra and the fact that
L_{T(n+1)}\K(-)
satisfies descent with respect to actions of
\pi
-finite spaces.

L_{T(n+1)}\GW(\lim_{X}(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa))\simeq(L_{T(n+1)}\K(\lim_{X}(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)))^{hC_{2}}\simeq(\lim_{X}L_{T(n+1)}\K((\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)))^{hC_{2}}\simeq\lim_{X}L_{T(n+1)}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)

and similarly for the colimit.
∎

It would be interesting to know whether
L_{T(n)}\LL(-)
satisfies descent with respect to
\pi
-finite 2-groups in general. This is the subject on ongoing investigation.

We state one last theorem, analogous to chromatic redshift for
\K
-theory of
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-rings. This theorem rests upon the Hermitian trace methods and real topological Hochschild homology developed in the unpublished work of
[
HNS02
]

Theorem G

(Whiteshift for
\LL
-theory)
.

Let
n\geq 0
. Let
A
be an
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-ring with the trivial anti-involution
\mathrm{Id}
. If
A
is of height exactly
n
then
\LL^{t}(A,\mathrm{Id})
is of height exactly
n
.

Proof.

We have proven that
\height\LL^{t}(A)\leq\height A
and are thus required to prove the other implication. By
[
HNS02
]
, there is a map of rings
\LL^{n}(A)\to A
coming from the fact that they identify
A
with
\Phi^{C_{2}}\THR(\Perf(A),\Qoppa_{\mathrm{Id}}^{t})
, the
C_{2}
-geometric fixed points of real topological Hoschschild homology. Thus
\LL_{T(n)}\LL^{t}(A)
cannot vanish if
\LL_{T(n)}A
does not.
∎

Here
\LL^{t}(-)
is refers to the
\LL
-theory of the Tate Poincaré structure
\Qoppa^{t}_{\sigma}
which only exists in the
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-setting, see
[
Cal+20
, Example 3.2.12]
.

Warning 1
.

The whiteshift theorem cannot hold general Poincaré categories. By Theorem
C
, we will see that it almost never holds for the quadratic structure. Even without Theorem
C
, we can make use of the property of bordism invariance for
\LL
-theory to show that we can always find a ring spectrum for which whiteshift fails. Consider the
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring
A\oplus A^{\mathrm{op}}
equipped with the flip involution. The associative symmetric (and indeed also quadratic) Poincaré category is then simply
\Hyp(\Perf(A))
. A fundamental result of
[
Cal+20a
]
is that
\LL
-theory of any Poincaré category of the form
\Hyp(\mathcal{C})
is
0
.

Additive Hermitian K-Theory

2.1
Additive Hermitian and Poincaré Categories

First we recall some standard definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a pointed category admitting finite coproducts. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
semiadditive
if for each
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
the canonical maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a\coprod b}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}

exhibit
a\coprod b
as the product of
a
and
b
. In this case we write
a\oplus b
for both the (canonically identified) product and coproduct. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
additive
if it is semiadditive and every
a\in\mathcal{A}
the shear map
a\oplus a\to a\oplus a
is an equivalence. Finally we say that
\mathcal{A}
is
\flat
-additive
if it is additive and weakly idempotent complete - every map admitting a retract is an inclusion of a direct summand. The categories of semiadditive, additive,
\flat
-additive will be denoted
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{sadd}}_{\infty},\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\infty},\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\flat}
respectively. In these categories, functors are taken to be additive, that is, coproduct (equivalently product) preserving.

There is a vast literature on semiadditive and additive categories, see
[
GGN15
]
in particular for a treatment of the
\infty
-categorical case. In this paper they show that
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{sadd}}_{\infty}
(resp.
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\infty}
) is canonically enriched over
\mathrm{CMon}
(resp.
\mathrm{CGrp}
). There is comparatively little in the literature about
\flat
-additive categories. One notable property is that the category
\flat
-additive categories is is equivalent to the category of weighted stable categories. We discuss Poincaré categories in the
\flat
-additive case, where we follow the treatment in
[
HS21
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an
\flat
-additive
\infty
-category. We will say that a reduced functor
\Qoppa\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{CGrp}
is
additive quadratic
if the cross effect
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{CGrp}
defined by the formula

\Qoppa(x\oplus y)\simeq\Qoppa(x)\oplus\Qoppa(y)\oplus\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}(x,y)

is
additive bilinear
, that is, preserves direct sums in each variable separately. We denote by
\Funaq(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{CGrp})
the full subcategory of
\Fun(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{CGrp})
consisting of additive quadratic functors. A
\flat
-additive Hermitian category
is a pair
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
with
\mathcal{A}

\flat
-additive and
\Qoppa
an additive quadratic functor. The category of such is the Cartesian unstraightening of the functor

(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\flat})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty},\quad\mathcal{A}\mapsto\Funaq(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{CGrp}).

We call a morphism in this category an additive Hermitian functor.
The category of
\flat
-additive Poincaré categories

\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}
is the subcategory
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ah}}_{\flat}
consisting of pairs
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
where
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}(x,y)\simeq\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(a,\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}(b))
naturally in
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
for an equivalence
\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
. We require maps between
\flat
-additive Poincaré categories to be duality preserving in the sense of Remark
1.3
.

From the above, the data of a Hermitian functor
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\to(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\Qoppa^{\prime})
is the data of an additive functor
f\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
and a natural transformation
\eta\colon\Qoppa\to f^{*}\Qoppa^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\Qoppa^{\prime}\circ f^{\mathrm{op}}
. As with the stable case we have two basic formulas for
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}
, which hold irrespective of any further assumptions on
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}(-,-)

\displaystyle\simeq\fib\left(\Qoppa(-\oplus-)\to\Qoppa(\pi_{1}(-,-))\oplus\Qoppa(\pi_{2}(-,-))\right);

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa}(-,-)

\displaystyle\simeq\cofib\left(\Qoppa(\pi_{1}(-,-))\oplus\Qoppa(\pi_{2}(-,-))\to\Qoppa(-\oplus-)\right).

It follows that limits and colimits in
\Funaq(\mathcal{A})
exist and can be computed in
\Fun(\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{CGrp})
.
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}
enjoys essentially the same functoriality properties as
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\infty}
and we will sketch a number of these results in Appendix
A
.
If
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
be a stable Poincaré category, then
(\mathcal{C},\Omega^{\infty}\Qoppa)
is
\flat
-additive Poincaré category.
Motivated by this we supply the following definition:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
be a stable Poincaré category. We shall call the
\flat
-additive Poincaré category
(\mathcal{C},\Omega^{\infty}\Qoppa)
the
underlying
\flat
-additive Poincaré category
of
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
and will be denoted
\flatAdd(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
.

There is a construction in the opposite direction.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
be a
\flat
-additive Poicaré category. Then we define the
associated stable Poincaré category

\Stab(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
to have underlying stable category
\Stab(A)
with Poincaré structure given by the unique quadratic extension of
\Qoppa
to
\Stab(\mathcal{A})
. This quadratic extension comes from
[
Bar+22
, Theorem 2.19]
applied to the case
n=2
.

We come to the fundamental example of an additive Poincaré category. Recall from Proposition
A.7
that a Hermitian structure on
\Proj^{\omega}(R)
gives rise to module with
C_{2}
-action
M
encoding the bilinear part. The next result gives us a necessarily and sufficient criterion for aforementioned Hermitian category to be Poincaré. This is the analogue of
[
Cal+20
, Proposition 3.1.6]
with the same proof. Recall the notation of an invertible
A
-module with involution from
[
Cal+20
, Definition 3.1.4]
, where the reader can find further discussion.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
M\in\Mod_{A\otimes A}
. Viewing
M
as an
A
-module via the first action, we obtain grouplike
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-space
\Map_{A}(M,M)
. This inherits an
A
-action via the residual action on
M
from which we obtain a map of grouplike
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-spaces

A\mapsto\Map_{A}(M,M).

We say that
M
is
invertible
if the above map is an equivalence.

Corollary 2.6
.

Suppose
(\Proj^{\omega}(A),\Qoppa_{M})
is additive Hermitian with underlying biadditive functor
\mathrm{B}_{\Qoppa_{M}}(X,Y)\simeq\Map_{A\otimes A}(X\otimes Y,M)
. Then
(\Proj^{\omega}(A),\Qoppa_{M})
is Poincaré if and only if
M
is invertible and compact projective and the associated duality is given by
X\mapsto\Map_{A}(X,M)
.

Proof.

Fix
Y\in\Proj^{\omega}(A)
. The functor
X\in\Proj^{\omega}(A)^{\mathrm{op}}\mapsto\Map_{A\otimes A}(X\otimes_{A}Y,M)\in\mathrm{CGrp}
is represented in
\Mod(A)
by (the a priori not compact projective)
\Map_{A}(Y,M)
with
A
-action induced by the second
A
-action on
M
. By idempotent completeness, for this to be compact projective for all
Y
it is necessary and sufficient to demand that
\Map_{A}(A,M)\simeq M
is compact projective. We denote the functor
N\mapsto\Map_{A}(N,M)
by
\mathrm{D}_{M}
. Now if
M
is compact projective, since
\Proj(R)
is generated by
R
it is necessary and sufficient to require that the the canonical map
X\mapsto\mathrm{D}_{M}\mathrm{D}_{M}(X)=\Map_{A}(\Map_{A}(X,M),M)
is an equivalence only in the special case
X=A
. This is an equivalence if and only if
M
is in addition invertible.
∎

2.2
Additive GW and L-theory

Before defining additive analogues of
\GW
and
\LL
-theory, we will need to discuss the additive analogous of the constructions which appear in
[
Cal+20
, Section 2.1]
. In particular, we need to discuss the space of Poincaré (Hermitian) objects associated to a
\flat
-additive Poincaré (Hermitian) category
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
. Most of the definitions will go through verbatim.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
be a
\flat
-additive Hermitian category.
The
category of Hermitian objects
in
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
is given by the total category of the right fibration

\He(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\vcentcolon=\int_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\Qoppa(a)\to\mathcal{A}

classifying the functor
\Qoppa
. Thus, an object of
\He(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
consists of a pair of objects
(a,q)\in\mathcal{A}\times\Qoppa(a)
. We will denoted by
\Fm(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\subseteq\He(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
the core subgroup of
\He(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
and refer to it as the
space of Hermitian objects
in
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
. Finally, if
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
is Poincaré, we let
\mathrm{Pn}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
denote the
space of Poincaré objects
in
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
, defined to be the full subgroupoid of
\Fm(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
spanned by those objects
(a,q)
for which the canonical map
q_{\#}\colon a\to\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}(a)
is an equivalence.

Lemma 2.8
.

The functors
\Fm\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ah}}_{\flat}\to\mathcal{S}
and
\mathrm{Pn}\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}\to\mathcal{S}
commute with filtered colimits of additive Poincaré categories.

Proof.

The proof is entirely analogous to
[
Cal+20
, Proposition 6.1.8]
. The key ingredient of their proof, that
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
preserves filtered colimits in our case becomes the statement that
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
preserves filtered colimits. To see this, note that
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
can be factored as
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{add}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{sadd}}_{\infty}\simeq\Mod_{\mathrm{Span}_{0}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\amalg}_{\infty})\to\mathrm{Cat}^{\amalg}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
. Each functor in this chain preserves filtered colimits. For the first we observe group-like condition on mapping spaces is closed under colimits. For the second we have that
\Mod_{\mathrm{Span}_{0}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\amalg}_{\infty})\to\mathrm{Cat}^{\amalg}_{\infty}
admits a right adjoint (and indeed also a left adjoint)
[
Har20
]
. Finally for the third we have the general fact that
\mathrm{Cat}^{\amalg}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
preserves sifted colimits by the following argument, due to Maxime Ramzi.
Suppose
\mathcal{C}_{(-)}\colon I\to\mathrm{Cat}^{\amalg}_{\infty}
is sifted diagram, with
\mathcal{C}\vcentcolon=\colim_{I}\mathcal{C}_{i}
in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
. It suffices to prove the following three claims:

(1)

For each
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists an
i\in I
along with
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{i}
such that the canonical map
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}
maps
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
to
x
and
y
, respectively.

(2)

\mathcal{C}
has finite coproducts.

(3)

For each
i\in I
,
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}
preserves finite coproducts.

The proof of the claims are given below:

(1)

We calculate
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\simeq\colim_{(i,j)\in I\times I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{j}\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{i}
, where the latter equivalence follows from siftedness.
\Delta^{0}
is compact projective in
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}
, which implies the claim

(2)+(3)

Since each
\mathcal{C}_{i}
has finite coproducts, each diagonal functor

\Delta_{i}\colon\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{i}
has a left adjoint, namely the binary coproduct. In the map of diagrams
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{i}
induced by the diagonal, each naturality square is left adjointable since each
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}_{j}
is coproduct preserving. Hence upon taking colimits, the resulting map
\mathcal{C}\to\colim_{i\in I}(\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{i})
admits a left adjoint compatible with each
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\colim_{i\in I}(\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{i})
. By siftedness,
\colim_{i\in I}(\mathcal{C}_{i}\times\mathcal{C}_{i})
identifies with
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
and so
\mathcal{C}
has finite coproducts and each
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
preserves these adjoints, that is to say each
\mathcal{C}_{i}\to\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}
is coproduct preserving.

∎

With the definitions of
\flat
-additive Poincaré categories at hand, we continue to follow
[
HS21
, Appendix A]
for the corresponding suitable definitions of additive
\GWspace
and
\Lspace
.

Definition 2.9
.

The
additive
\GW
-space
associated to
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
,
\GWspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
is given by

\GWspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\vcentcolon=\mathrm{Pn}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)^{\mathrm{gp}}

This manifestly induces a functor
\GWspace^{\oplus}\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}\to\mathrm{CGrp}
. This functor preserves filtered colimits in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}
because both
\mathrm{Pn}
and
(-)^{\mathrm{gp}}
do. The reader might be concerned that this definition is too naive, given than the stable variant is rather involved. In fact, the the group completion within the category
\Fun^{\oplus}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat},\mathrm{CMon})
coincides with the pointwise group completion, a phenomenon which one sees when comparing additive and stable
\K
-theory. More involved is the definition of the additive
\LL
-space functor. For a fixed
\flat
-additive Poincaré category
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
, one has, for each
[n]\in\Delta
, an additive Hermitian
\mathrm{Q}
-construction, denoted
\mathrm{Q}_{n}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
. As
n
varies, we obtain a simplicial object in
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}
denoted
\mathrm{Q}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
. For any functor
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat}\to\mathrm{CGrp}
, we then define

\mathcal{F}\mathrm{Q}^{(n)}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\vcentcolon=\colim_{([k_{1}],\ldots,[k_{n}])\in\Delta^{\times n}}\mathcal{F}\left(\mathrm{Q}_{k_{1}}\cdots\mathrm{Q}_{k_{n}}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\right).

In the above formula, the object
\mathcal{F}\left(\mathrm{Q}_{k_{1}}\cdots\mathrm{Q}_{k_{n}}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\right)
refines to an
n
-fold simplicial object in
\mathrm{CGrp}
, that is, a functor
(\Delta^{\mathrm{op}})^{\times n}\to\mathrm{CGrp}
. This colimit amounts to taking the
n
-fold geometric realisation, so that
\mathcal{F}\mathrm{Q}^{(n)}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\in\mathrm{CGrp}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
be a
\flat
-additive Poincaré category and consider
\GWspace^{\oplus}\mathrm{Q}^{(n)}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
. We define the
additive
\LL
-theory space
by

\Lspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\vcentcolon=\colim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\GWspace^{\oplus}\mathrm{Q}^{(n)}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa).

The above colimit is sequential, induced by the inclusions
\Delta^{\times\,(n-1)}\times\{[0]\}\to\Delta^{\times\,n}
.

According to
[
HS21
, Proposition A.2.6]
, additive
\LL
-theory fits into a bifibre sequence analogous to the fundamental fibre sequence of
[
Cal+20a
]
.

Theorem 2.11
.

The additive
\LL
-theory functor
\Lspace^{\oplus}
is the cofibre of the natural map
\Kspace^{\oplus}(-)_{\mathrm{hC}_{2}}\to\GWspace^{\oplus}(-)
, taken in
\Fun(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{ap}}_{\flat},\mathrm{CGrp})
. This is also a fibre sequence.

An immediate corollary of this theorem is the following.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{A}_{i},\Qoppa_{i})
be filtered diagram of
\flat
-additive Poincaré categories admitting a colimit
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
. Then

\Lspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\simeq\colim\Lspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A}_{i},\Qoppa_{i}).

Proof.

\Kspace^{\oplus}
commutes with filtered colimits and thus so does its homotopy orbits.
\GWspace^{\oplus}
commutes with filtered colimits since
\mathrm{Pn}
(by Lemma
2.8
) and pointwise group completion do. Therefore
\Lspace^{\oplus}
commutes with filtered colimits.
∎

We have two key theorems which relate additive to stable
\GW

Theorem 2.13
.

[
HS21
, Theorem B,Appendix A.1.2]

Let
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
be a
\flat
-additive Poincaré category. Then there are natural equivalences

(1)

\GWspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\simeq\GWspace(\Stab(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa))
;

(2)

\Lspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)\simeq\Lspace(\Stab(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa))
.

Working backwards, we have formula for the stable
\GW

\GW^{\oplus}

Lemma 2.14
.

For a stable Poincaré category
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
, we have the identification of commutative groups

\Omega^{\infty-1}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq|\GWspace^{\oplus}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|

functorial in
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
.

Proof.

We begin with the formula

\Omega^{\infty-1}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq|\mathrm{Pn}\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|

of
[
Cal+20a
, Corollary 4.2.3]
which is functorial in
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
. The functor
\mathrm{Pn}
is invariant under taking the underlying
\flat
-additive Poincaré category and so we may replace
\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})
with
\flatAdd\left(\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})\right)
. Performing this identification obtain

\Omega^{\infty-1}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq|\mathrm{Pn}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|

as objects of
\mathrm{CMon}
. On the right hand side we have taking the geometric realization in
\mathrm{CMon}
. The monoid structure on both sides are induced from the semiadditive structure of
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{p}}_{\infty}
. The left hand side is group-like, essentially by definition. Thus the same is true of
|\mathrm{Pn}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|
and so it may be identified with
|\mathrm{Pn}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|^{\mathrm{gp}}

|\mathrm{Pn}^{\mathrm{gp}}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|
where the geometric realization is now taken in
\mathrm{CGrp}
. But this is precisely
|\GWspace^{\oplus}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|
and the lemma follows.
∎

The statement
\Omega^{\infty-1}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq|\mathrm{Pn}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|

\mathrm{CMon}\to\mathcal{S}
creates geometric realizations.

3
Applications to Chromatically Localised Hermitian K-theory

Let us begin by discussing the ingredients which go into the proof of Theorem
B
, recalled below.

Theorem B
.

Suppose
n\geq 0
. Let
A
be an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring with anti-involution
\sigma
and Let
(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
be a Poincaré category with duality compatible with
\sigma
. Then if
A
has height
\leq n
then
\LL(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
has height
\leq n
.

The first major ingredient we use is as yet unpublished work of Harpaz, Nikolaus, and Shah
[
HNS02
]
which provides novel foundations for trace methods in Hermitian K-theory, including the following important result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
A
is an
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring. Let
\Qoppa
be a Poincaré structure on
\Perf(A)
with underlying module of involution
M
. Then the cofibre of the canonical map
\LL^{q}(A,M)\to\LL(A,\Qoppa)
, denoted
normal
\LL
-theory
of
(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
, is a filtered colimit of spectra of the form

\Eq\left(\map_{A}(D_{M}(X),X)\rightrightarrows(\mathbb{S}^{1-\sigma}\otimes\map_{A}(D_{M}(X),X)_{\mathrm{hC}_{2}}\right)

for
X
a compact
A
-module.

Remark 3.2
.

Normal
\LL
-theory can be defined for arbitrary Poincaré categories analogously. Given
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
with duality
\mathrm{D}
, normal
\LL
-theory is defined to fit into a cofibre sequence

\LL^{q}(\mathcal{C},\mathrm{D})\to\LL(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\to\LL^{\mathrm{nor}}(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa).

From Theorem
3.1
if we assume at
A
is
T(n)
-acyclic, a thick subcategory argument shows that the mapping spectra in the formula above are also
T(n)
-acyclic and hence the equaliser is too. We deduce

Corollary 3.3

(
[
Lan22
, Theorem 13]
)
.

Let
n\geq 0
. If
A
is
T(n)
-acyclic, the canonical map
\LL^{q}(A,M)\to\LL(A,\Qoppa)
is a
T(n)
-local equivalence.

This result tells us that
T(n)
-local
\LL
-theory of rings of
T(n)
-acyclic rings is independent of the linear part of the quadratic structure. We shall use this fact extensively in this article and in future work. Land goes on to prove a no redshift theorem for
connective
rings. He deduces it first for ordinary
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-rings using geometric methods and then finally for all connective rings using the truncating property of quadratic
\LL
-theory. Theorem
B
is, to our knowledge, the first result for general
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-rings.

For
commutative
rings with anti-involution, the lack of redshift follows easily from Land’s result as follows. There is a map of
\mathbf{E}_{\infty}
-rings

\LL^{s}(\tau_{\geq 0}A,\tau_{\geq 0}\sigma)\to\LL^{s}(A,\sigma).

If
A
is
T(n)
-acyclic then so is
\tau_{\geq 0}A
. This forces
\LL^{s}(\tau_{\geq 0}A,\sigma)
to vanish
T(n)
-locally. Since the result is true for symmetric
\LL
-theory, it is true for quadratic
\LL
-theory by Corollary
3.3
and thus for any other Poincaré structure compatible with
\sigma
.

As remarked in the introduction, we will focus on proving chromatic purity for
\LL
-theory, thereby extending the results of
[
Lan+20
]
to L-theory. Recall that by purity we mean any result of the form “a chromatic localisation of
E(A)
for a localising invariant
E
and ring
A
depends only on a (possibly different) chromatic localisation of
A
”. In
[
Lan+20
]
, the following theorem is proved

Theorem 3.4

(
[
Lan+20
]
)
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring then

(1)

For
n\geq 1
, the canonical map
A\to L_{n}^{f}A
induces an equivalence on
L_{T(n)}\K(-)
.

(2)

For
n\geq 2
, the canonical map
A\to L_{T}
induces an equivalence on
L_{T(n)}\K(-)
Here
T=T(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus T(n)
.

To prove this, the authors deduce properties of chromatically localised algebraic K-theory by making use of additive, rather than stable, K-theory. We will see that a similar proof strategy for L-theory.

Their work, along with that of many others, has allowed for the use of trace methods to attack problems in chromatically localised algebraic
\K
-theory and resolve long-standing conjectures at the intersection of
\K
-theory and chromatic homotopy theory. We intend to revisit this theme in the context of Hermitian
\K
-theory in future works.

Below is the broad strategy used to prove Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
.

Proof Strategy for the “Purity” and “No Redshift” Theorems.

(1)

Using additive
\LL
-theory, we show that any stable category with
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclic endomorphism spectra has vanishing
L_{T(i)}
-local stable
\LL
-theory for
0\leq i\leq n
.

(2)

A connectivity argument allows us to identify
L_{T(i)}\mathbb{L}(-)
with
L_{T(i)}\LL(-)
.

(3)

We show the existence of a Hermitian chromatic fracture square for rings with involution.

(4)

We deduce that
A\to L_{n}^{f}A
induces an equivalence on
L_{T(i)}\LL^{q}(-)
for rings with involution.

(5)

Finally, using the Hermitian chromatic fracture square, we show that
A\to L_{T(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus T(n)}A
induces an equivalence on
L_{T(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus T(n)}\LL^{q}(-)
in a suitable range.

(6)

Since
L_{T(1)\oplus\cdots\oplus T(n)}
is a periodic localisation, the problem is reduced to the connective case and we conclude from Land’s results.

We begin by establishing Item
i)
, the
\LL
-theoretic analogue of
[
Lan+20
, Proposition 3.6 (i)]
. Recall the following fact from
[
Lan+20
, Section 3.3]
that for stable
\mathcal{C}
,
\map_{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
being
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclic is equivalent to
\Map_{\mathcal{C}}(X,X)
being
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclic. Thus in this case we do not need to distinguish between mapping endomorphism spaces and mapping endomorphism spectra as far as
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclicity is concerned. Similarly for
T(i)
-acyclicity for
i\geq 0
. This important but surprisingly subtle point is further discussed in
loc. cit.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
i\geq 1
. Suppose
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
is an
\flat
-additive Poincaré category. If for each object
x\in\mathcal{A}
, the endomorphism space
\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x)
, considered as connective spectrum, is
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclic, then
\Kspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)_{{\mathrm{hC}_{2}}}
,
\GWspace^{\oplus}(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
, and
\Lspace(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
are all
T(i)
-acyclic for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

First notice that the reduction step in the proof of
[
Lan+20
, Proposition 3.6 (i)]
goes through for
\LL
-theory with little modification. More explicitly, using Lemma
A.6
and the fact that
\Lspace^{\oplus}
and
\GWspace^{\oplus}
both commute with filtered colimits, we may reduce to the case where
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
is generated by a single object
X=x\oplus\mathrm{D}_{\Qoppa}(X)
. In this case, the additive Schwede-Shipley theorem implies that
(\mathcal{A},\Qoppa)
is an additive Poincaré category with underlying
\flat
-additive
\Proj^{\omega}\left(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X)\right)
, equipped with a module of involution
M
. From
[
HHS21
, A.2.6]
we obtain a bifibre sequence of commutative groups

\Kspace^{\oplus}(\Proj^{\omega}(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X)),\Qoppa)_{{\mathrm{hC}_{2}}}\to\GWspace^{\oplus}(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X),\Qoppa)\to\Lspace^{\oplus}(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X),\Qoppa).

By
[
Lan+20
, Proposition 3.6(i)]
, the first term vanishes after
T(i)
-localisation for
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus we get an identification

L_{T(i)}\GWspace^{\oplus}(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X),\Qoppa)\xrightarrow{\simeq}L_{T(i)}\Lspace^{\oplus}(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X),\Qoppa).

For the other vanishing statements, we work exclusively with
\LL
-theory. The additive
\LL
-theory term is equivalent to
L_{T(i)}\LL(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(X,X),\Stab(\Qoppa))
. Since
\Map(X,X)
is connective and
T(i)
-acyclic for
1\leq i\leq n
, the same is true of
\LL
-theory by
[
Lan22
, Corollary 15]
. Not that the vanishing result for
i=1
is guaranteed by the assumption
p=2
.
∎

Continuing with our proof strategy, we exploit the interplay of additive and Hermitian
\LL
-theory, we deduce the stable analogue of the above proposition in the manner of
[
Lan+20
, Proposition 3.6 (ii)]
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
i\geq 1
. Suppose
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
is a stable Poincaré category. If for each object
x\in\mathcal{C}
the endomorphism spectrum
\map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x)
is
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclic, then
\K(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)_{{\mathrm{hC}_{2}}}
,
\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
, and
\LL(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
are all
T(i)
-acyclic for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Immediately from
[
Lan+20
, Proposition 3.6(ii)]
we deduce that
T(i)
-local
\K
-theory vanishes for
1\leq i\leq n
. Hence from the fundamental fibre sequence for stable Hermitian
\K
-theory

\K(\mathcal{C})_{{\mathrm{hC}_{2}}}\to\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\to\LL(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa),

we obtain the desired equivalence of
T(i)
-local
\GW
and
\LL
-theory. We
T(i)
-localise the formula of Lemma
2.14

L_{T(i)}\Omega^{\infty-1}\GW(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)\simeq|L_{T(i)}\GWspace^{\oplus}\flatAdd\Qdot(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa^{[1]})|.

The vanishing statements will follow from Proposition
3.5
provided we can prove that the right hand side is
T(i)
-acyclic for
1\leq i\leq n
. For this, recall that the underlying stable category of the Hermitian Q-construction is the certain full subcategory of
\Fun(\TwAr(\Delta^{k}),\mathcal{C})
. It therefore suffices to show that for each
k
, all endomorphism spaces (equivalently spectra) of
\Fun(\TwAr(\Delta^{k}),\mathcal{C})
are
T(i)
-acyclic. We may write these endomorphism spectra as the following finite limit

\map_{\Fun(\TwAr(\Delta^{k}),\mathcal{C})}(F,F)=\lim_{[i\to j]\in\TwAr(\TwAr(\Delta^{k}))^{\mathrm{op}}}\map_{\mathcal{C}}(F(i),F(j)).

Each mapping spectrum
\map_{\mathcal{C}}(F(i),F(j))
is a module over
\map_{\mathcal{C}}(F(i),F(i))
and is thus
T(i)
-acyclic. Since the class of
T(i)
-acyclic spectra is closed under finite limits, the result follows.
∎

If we are content to only have results for

Proposition 3.7
.

. Suppose

As before, we reduce to the case where we are generated by a single object. From Corollary

\displaystyle\Lspace^{\oplus}(\Proj^{\omega}\left(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x)\right),\Qoppa_{M})

\displaystyle\simeq\Lspace(\Stab(\Proj^{\omega}(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x))),\Stab(\Qoppa))

\displaystyle\simeq\Lspace(\Perf(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x),\Stab(\Qoppa))

\displaystyle=\Omega^{\infty}\LL(\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x),\Stab(\Qoppa)).

The final term is
T(n)
-acyclic by
[
Lan22
, Corollary 15]
for

. The vanishing result for

is guaranteed by the assumption

and Corollary 15 of
loc. cit
. For

we argue as follows: by Corollary

we may reduce to the discrete case since

is connective. Observe that
\pi_{0}\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x)
is

is nullhomotopic. Suppose some

-th multiple of
[\mathrm{Id}_{x}]
is nullhomotopic for some prime

. By
[
Lan22

\LL^{q}(\pi_{0}\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x))\otimes\mathbb{Q}\to\LL^{q}(\pi_{0}\Map_{\mathcal{A}}(x,x)[1/p^{\prime}])\otimes\mathbb{Q}

is an equivalence. But the right hand side is identically
0
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
n\geq 0
. Suppose
(\mathcal{C},\Qoppa)
is a stable Poincaré category. If for each object
x\in\mathcal{C}
the endomorphism spectrum

If we assume

provided we can prove that the right hand side is

-acyclic. This argument is as before.
∎

In what follows, we consider Poincaré categories of the form
(\Perf(A),\Qoppa)
and investigate the effect that localisations of rings
\phi\colon A\to LA
have on the associated categories of (compact) modules. As discussed in
[

, such a localisation need not induce a Poincaré-Karoubi projection upon taking

. However we find that if

is the quadratic structure associated to an involution
\sigma\colon A\to A^{\mathrm{op}}
and

(\Perf(A),\Qoppa_{\sigma}^{q})\to(\Perf(LA),\Qoppa_{L\sigma}^{q})

is a Poincaré-Karoubi projection. These two hypotheses will typically be satisfied in the cases of interest to us.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any
\mathbf{E}_{1}
-ring spectrum
A
with anti-involution
\sigma\colon A\to A^{\mathrm{op}}
, there is a Poincaré-Karoubi sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C}_{>n}\otimes\Perf(A),\Qoppa^{q}_{\sigma}|_{\mathcal{C}_{>n}\otimes\Perf(A)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Perf(A),\Qoppa^{q}_{\sigma})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Perf(L_{n}^{f}A),\Qoppa^{q}_{L_{n}^{f}\sigma}),}

(1)

and the endomorphism spectrum of every object in
\mathcal{C}_{>n}\otimes\Perf(A)
is
L_{n}^{f}
-acyclic. Here
\mathcal{C}_{>n}
denotes the category of
p
-local finite spectra which are
K(0)\oplus\cdots\oplus K(n)
-acyclic.

Proof.

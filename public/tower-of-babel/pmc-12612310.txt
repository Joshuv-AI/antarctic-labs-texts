Current status and trends of anti-tumor biosimilars in China from 2019 to 2023: A cross-sectional analysis
Authors: Han Shan, Zihan Guo, Xuan Ye, Qiong Du, Jiyong Liu, Mengmeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
While several anti-tumor biosimilars have been approved in China, comprehensive nationwide analyses of their real-world utilization patterns remain limited, particularly regarding cross-regional adoption and indication-specific usage. We collected information on patients treated with bevacizumab, rituximab, trastuzumab and their biosimilars at 109 hospitals in nine Chinese cities from 2019 to 2023. Analysis of 264,527 prescriptions revealed rapid biosimilar adoption for bevacizumab (2023 originator share: 20.0%, -25.1%/year), moderate for rituximab (32.1%, -16.8%/year), and limited for trastuzumab (70.0%, -8.3%/year). Geographic variation was substantial (2023 city ranges: bevacizumab 2.3–43.0%; rituximab 6.7–70.3%; trastuzumab 42.4–92.4%). Tertiary hospitals showed faster biosimilar uptake than secondary hospitals (bevacizumab: + 23.7%). Free medical care patients preferred originators (bevacizumab: 64.1% vs 38–39% for other payers). Off-label use demonstrated significantly higher biosimilar adoption (bevacizumab: 69.0%; rituximab: 52.1%) versus approved indications (p < 0.00625). Cost distributions mirrored prescription trends but consistently favored originators. All trends were statistically significant (p < 0.05). The biosimilar market share showed steady annual growth, however the growth rates vary across different products. However, Patients with lower out-of-pocket costs remained more likely to choose original products. Enhanced regulatory oversight of both approved and off-label/extrapolated indications is needed to ensure appropriate biosimilar utilization.

Introduction

A biosimilar is a biological medicinal product that contains a version of the active substance approved to be highly similar to the reference product in quality, biological activity, safety, and efficacy(Foreman 2020). Unlike small-molecule generics, which are exact chemical copies (excluding excipients) of their reference drugs, biosimilars are protein-based therapies with larger, more complex structures that are highly similar but not identical to their reference products(Edwards and Bellinvia 2020). The primary amino acid sequence of a biosimilar can be precisely replicated by different producers, and the protein product can then be evaluated physicochemically based on higher-order structural similarity. Nevertheless, there are many unpredictable factors that can affect protein structure during the development of a biosimilar, as different post-translational modifications have potential implications for efficacy, immunogenicity, and safety(Mroczek et al. 2024). As a result, the registration and marketing process for biosimilars is often more complex than for small molecule generics.

Since the first biopharmaceutical was approved in 1982(Singh et al. 2018), biopharmaceuticals have taken disease treatment to new heights. However, original biopharmaceuticals are often expensive, which brings a huge financial burden to patients. The approval time for biosimilars is usually shorter, generally around 8 years, compared to more than 12 years for innovative products, and the development cost of biosimilars can be 10–20% of that of innovative products (Agbogbo et al. 2019). The relatively low cost of biosimilar research and development results in lower pricing compared to reference biologics, offering more affordable treatment options for patients. Additionally, the introduction of biosimilars creates intense market competition, prompting reference biologics to reduce prices in order to maintain their market share, ultimately leading to a reduction in healthcare expenditure. These biosimilars are estimated to have saved the EU $11.8 billion between 2016 and 2022 (Kaiser et al. 2022).

The research and development of biosimilars have set off a new wave of enthusiasm around the world. The European Medicines Agency (EMA) approved the world's first biosimilar, Somatropin (Omnitrope®), in 2006(Backeljauw et al. 2022). Since then, 68 biosimilars have been approved in the European Union by 2022. Research and development of biosimilars in the United States is also active. Between 2015, when the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved the first biosimilar, Filgrastim-sndz (Zarxio®)(Qian 2021), and 2022, the FDA approved a total of 35 biosimilars. 2019 was a peak year for biosimilars in the United States, with the FDA approving 10 biosimilars. The first biosimilar in China, the rituximab biosimilar from Henlius, was approved in 2019(Qin et al. 2024), At present, there are more than 60 companies developing biosimilars in China, and more than 140 biosimilars have been approved or are in development(Liu et al. 2022). Currently in China, the three most established anti-tumor biosimilars with the longest market presence are the bevacizumab biosimilar, the rituximab biosimilar and the trastuzumab biosimilar. Both the bevacizumab biosimilar and the rituximab biosimilar were first approved in China in 2019(Lu et al. 2022; Qin et al. 2024), and the trastuzumab biosimilar was first approved in 2020(Zeng et al. 2022). While several anti-tumor biosimilars have been approved in China since 2019, comprehensive nationwide analyses of their real-world utilization patterns remain limited, particularly regarding cross-regional adoption and indication-specific usage.

The regulatory approaches to biosimilars in China differ from those in key international markets, particularly in reference product acceptance, regulatory collaboration, interchangeability, naming rules, and oversight of non-innovator biologics. China mandates that biosimilar comparability studies use domestically approved reference products, requiring additional bridging studies if foreign-sourced references are used—a hurdle that increases development costs(Kang et al. 2021). In contrast, the U.S. and EU accept foreign-approved reference products if scientifically justified, reducing redundant testing. Regarding regulatory collaboration, China relies primarily on independent national reviews, with limited adoption of international reliance pathways(Jung et al. 2020). Meanwhile, the U.S. and EU actively engage in joint assessments (e.g., EMA’s centralized procedure, ICMRA cooperation) to streamline approvals(Hung et al. 2017). For interchangeability, China bases decisions on clinical data and physician discretion, whereas the U.S. has a formal (but stringent) interchangeability designation for automatic substitution, and the EU leaves switching decisions to member states (Kang et al. 2021). Regarding naming rules, China follows the international nonproprietary name (INN) system without distinct suffixes, potentially complicating pharmacovigilance. The U.S. requires unique suffixes, while the EU relies on brand names(Klein et al. 2023).

The global adoption of biosimilars exhibits distinct regional patterns with significant economic implications, as evidenced by comparative studies in the U.S. and Japan. In the U.S., retrospective analyses of bevacizumab-bvzr demonstrate robust adoption in metastatic colorectal cancer (CRC) and female genital organ cancers, with 72.4% of CRC patients switching from reference products and 84.8% receiving combination chemotherapy regimens, contributing to projected savings exceeding $100 billion (2020–2024) through price competition and expanded access(Yang et al. 2022a, b). Japan's experience reveals policy-driven adoption through National Health Insurance-mandated 30% price discounts, yielding disease-specific penetration rates of 45% for rituximab biosimilars in non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL) and 30% for bevacizumab biosimilars in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) by 2022, with larger hospitals showing significantly higher uptake and modeled annual savings of €5,797–11,595 per patient(Roth et al. 2024). China's emerging biosimilar landscape remains understudied, lacking comprehensive real-world evidence to assess its adoption trajectory and economic potential.

Despite the rapid regulatory approval of several anti-tumor biosimilars in China since 2019, a critical knowledge gap persists regarding their real-world utilization patterns at a national level(Lyu et al. 2022). While regulatory pathways and market entry are documented(Yang et al. 2022a, b), comprehensive, large-scale, real-world evidence analyzing the actual adoption of these biosimilars across diverse geographical regions, healthcare settings, and specific clinical indications over multiple years remains notably absent. Existing literature primarily focuses on regulatory frameworks, development costs, or individual product approvals, lacking systematic, nationwide, multi-year, cross-regional analyses of how biosimilars are being integrated into clinical practice for different cancer types. This absence of comprehensive data on real-world utilization patterns—including potential regional disparities in uptake, indication-specific prescribing trends, and the evolving competitive landscape—hinders the assessment of biosimilars' true impact on healthcare accessibility, cost savings, and treatment patterns in the Chinese oncology setting. Therefore, there is a clear necessity for a robust, nationwide, multi-year, cross-sectional analysis to quantify and characterize the real-world adoption of anti-tumor biosimilars across China. The study focused on bevacizumab, rituximab, and trastuzumab biosimilars because these represent the earliest and most established cases of anti-tumor biosimilar adoption in China. All three reference products had biosimilars approved and launched domestically before 2020, providing sufficient time to observe meaningful adoption patterns. While newer biosimilars (e.g., cetuximab) have since entered the Chinese market, their relatively recent introduction would preclude assessment of longitudinal trends comparable to the three selected agents. This large-scale study analyzes the biosimilar adoption landscape in China, with findings intended to provide evidence-based references for biosimilar development strategies in the Chinese market.

Materials and methods

The study analyzed data from the ‘Hospital Prescription Analysis Cooperation Project’ database Maintained by the Chinese Pharmaceutical Society. The database, established in 1997, collects prescription data continuously from all participating hospitals(Sun and Wang 2022; Shang et al. 2023). It incorporates quarterly random 10-day prescription samples from each hospital. The 10 working days per quarter were selected through a systematic random sampling process to ensure temporal representativeness: first, all weekdays (Monday-Friday) within each quarter were enumerated, excluding public holidays and institutional closure days. Using a computer-generated random number sequence, we then selected a starting point from the first 6 eligible days, subsequently choosing every nth day (where n = total eligible days/10) to Maintain even distribution across the quarter. This study collected prescription data for outpatients and inpatients receiving bevacizumab, rituximab, trastuzumab and their biosimilars across 109 hospitals in nine major Chinese cities (Beijing, Chengdu, Guangzhou, Hangzhou, Harbin, Shanghai, Shenyang, Tianjin, and Zhengzhou). Data were collected from January 1, 2019 to December 31, 2023. The collected data encompassed patient identifiers, demographic characteristics (gender), visit details (time, hospital, hospital level, department), clinical information (diagnosis), and prescription records (drug name, specification, dosage, cost, and payer type). Drug indication appropriateness was determined by comparing diagnoses with the latest drug label information (as of December 31, 2023). Cases where diagnoses did not align with label recommendations were classified as off-label use.

For absolute cost values in year-to-year comparisons, monetary values were inflation-adjusted to 2019 Chinese Yuan (CNY) using healthcare-specific Consumer Price Indices (CPI, 2019 = 100) from China's National Bureau of Statistics (https://data.stats.gov.cn/search.htm), with cumulative adjustment factors (Et) calculated recursively as Et = CPIt × Et-1/100 (CPI values: 2019 = 100, 2020 = 101.8, 2021 = 100.4, 2022 = 100.6, 2023 = 101.1; resulting cumulative factors: 2019 = 100.0, 2020 = 101.8, 2021 = 102.2, 2022 = 102.8, 2023 = 104.0), where adjusted values were derived by multiplying original values by 100/Et to account for inflation over the five-year study period.

Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.2.1). Descriptive statistics presented categorical variables as frequencies (percentages), with originator drug proportions calculated as [originator prescriptions/(originator + biosimilar prescriptions)] × 100%. Temporal trends were analyzed using Joinpoint regression to identify inflection points and calculate annual percentage changes, weighted by the square root of total prescription volume. Group comparisons employed chi-square tests for categorical variables. Proportion estimates were reported with 95% confidence intervals using the Wilson Score method (when np ≥ 5) or Clopper-Pearson exact intervals (np < 5). All significance thresholds were adjusted for multiple comparisons using Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05/number of comparisons). Data management was performed using Microsoft Access (version 2016). Graphical plotting was drawn using GraphPad Prism software (version 5.01) and Adobe Illustrator software (version 2019).

Geographic and temporal patterns of originator products and their biosimilars utilization

Our analysis of prescription and cost trends for bevacizumab, rituximab, trastuzumab and their biosimilars across nine Chinese cities (2019–2023) revealed temporal and geographic variation. To control for inter-city hospital density differences, we calculated per-hospital averages. Initial data (Fig. 2) showed minimal biosimilar adoption in 2019 (proportion ≈0%), with steady growth over five years. By 2023, bevacizumab biosimilars surpassed originator prescriptions in all cities, while rituximab biosimilars dominated six cities (versus originator preference in Harbin/Shenyang and parity in Shanghai). Trastuzumab maintained originator dominance overall, though Guangzhou and Harbin approached equivalence. Cost trends were similar to prescription trends. Bevacizumab biosimilar costs exceeded the originator in eight cities (marginally lower only in Beijing). Utilization patterns of originator products and their biosimilars across different payer types

As presented in Table 4, significant differences in originator bevacizumab were observed across payment types (all p < 0.01).Patients with free medical care patients were demonstrated a strong preference for the original product, with 64.1% (95% CI: 62.3–65.9) selecting it. In contrast, those covered by basic medical insurance and self-pay patients showed greater adoption of biosimilars, with originator proportions of only 39.1% (95% CI: 38.7–39.5) and 38.4% (95% CI: 37.6–39.2), respectively. Among rituximab users, significant differences in originator prescriptions were observed across payer types, with basic medical insurance (53.6%, 95% CI: 53.1–54.1) and self-pay patients (56.6%, 95% CI: 55.5–57.7) showing significantly higher originator use (both p < 0.0125), while free medical care (54.8%, 95% CI: 52.7–56.9) and other insurance types (62.0%, 95% CI: 54.0–69.4) showed no significant differences. Cost distributions followed similar patterns, with originators accounting for 60.9–69.1% of total expenditures across all payer types. A similar phenomenon was even more pronounced in trastuzumab and its biosimilars, with the proportion of patients using the original product exceeding 85% regardless of the payer type (all p < 0.0125). Discussion

Market share of biosimilars and the influence of different factors on medicine selection preferences

Since China's first biosimilar approval in China in 2019(Qin et al. 2024), the anti-tumor biologic market has undergone significant transformation. The Chinese biosimilar market exhibits distinct adoption patterns across drug classes. Notably, bevacizumab biosimilars have surpassed originator products in market share, with the originator Maintaining just 39.7% of total utilization During the 2019–2023 study period. At least eight bevacizumab biosimilar manufacturers have entered the market since 2019, with the first-approved Qilu Pharmaceutical product dominating 45% of prescriptions. Rituximab biosimilars (led by Henlius' pioneering formulation) have achieved near-parity with the originator (slightly lower market share), while trastuzumab biosimilars lag at just 11.4% penetration. Three key factors explain these disparities: (1) Market timing – earlier entrants (bevacizumab/rituximab) gained greater traction. (2) Price differentials– trastuzumab biosimilars offer minimal cost savings. Taking the price in 2023 Shanghai as an example, the bevacizumab biosimilar (Qilu Pharmaceutical) is priced at 75% of the originator, the rituximab biosimilar (Henlius) at 60%, and the trastuzumab biosimilar at 90% of their respective originator products. (3) Clinical conservatism – trastuzumab's status as HER2 + breast cancer cornerstone therapy fosters strong physician and patient loyalty to the originator. These findings underscore that biosimilar market success requires both early market entry and substantial economic incentives to overcome therapeutic inertia.

From 2019 to 2023, both prescription volumes and drug costs for biosimilars demonstrated consistent annual growth in China. Given the limited adoption of the trastuzumab biosimilar, we focused on bevacizumab and rituximab biosimilars, which surpassed their originators in prescription volumes by 2023. The market share of the two types of biologics varied among the nine cities. However, pronounced differences emerged by hospital tier: while biosimilar adoption increased in both secondary and tertiary hospitals, the growth rate was higher in tertiary institutions, a pattern that reflects both patient flow patterns (concentration of cancer cases in tertiary centers) and manufacturer commercialization strategies. Payment type also significantly influenced utilization, with basic medical insurance patients demonstrating greater biosimilar uptake than those with commercial insurance or free healthcare, evidence that cost sensitivity drives prescription behavior.Collectively, these findings demonstrate that while biosimilar adoption follows an upward national trend, it is modulated by institutional hierarchies and payer dynamics.

Compared with international markets, China's biosimilar adoption rates demonstrate distinct characteristics. The EU, as the global leader in biosimilar utilization, has achieved significantly higher market shares, with trastuzumab biosimilars reaching over 67% adoption (Wyrwicz et al. 2024), markedly surpassing China's 30%. Although the U.S. market started later Due to stringent patent protections, its oncology biosimilars have shown rapid growth since 2019, with bevacizumab and trastuzumab biosimilar adoption rates surging from 6 and 2% in 2019 to 49% and 36% in 2021 respectively (Bourbeau et al. 2023; Mai et al. 2024; Ko et al. 2025)—a trend paralleling China's yet with lower overall rates. Among Asian counterparts, Japan and China both launched their first oncology biosimilars in 2019, but by the first half of 2022, Japan's adoption rates for bevacizumab, rituximab and trastuzumab biosimilars (approximately 30%, 45%, and < 20% respectively) (Roth et al. 2024) lagged behind China's 2023 Figs. (80%, 67.9%, and 30%). Notably, both countries exhibited relatively low trastuzumab biosimilar uptake. These international disparities primarily stem from regulatory policy gradients: the EU implements the most proactive measures (including pharmacist substitution, cost-control mechanisms, and dedicated promotion programs), the U.S. prohibits substitution without “interchangeable” designation, while Japan mandates precise product-specific prescriptions (Bennett et al. 2020). The Chinese regulatory landscape has played a pivotal role in shaping biosimilar market dynamics through three key mechanisms. First, China established an accelerated approval pathway for biosimilars in 2015, reducing the average review timeline from 18 to 12 months for priority products (Du et al. 2024). Second, China's volume-based procurement (VBP) policy, implemented since 2019, creates a paradoxical effect: while forcing aggressive price reductions (typically 60–70% below originator prices), it guarantees winning products 60–80% hospital market share for 1–2 years, incentivizing rapid market penetration (Zhao and Wu 2023). Third, the unique "consistency evaluation" requirement mandates extensive physicochemical and functional comparisons, potentially delaying launches but ensuring quality standards that facilitate later adoption.

From an economic perspective, biosimilar adoption rates vary significantly across healthcare systems. Europe's unified reimbursement policies create favorable market conditions, while the fragmented US system limits price advantages(Bennett et al. 2020). China's VBP policy has substantially reduced biosimilar costs, though current incentive mechanisms require optimization. More robust pharmacoeconomic evaluations are needed to balance cost containment with medication safety.

Biosimilar utilization across clinical indications

We summarized and categorized all the diagnoses indicated in the prescriptions, and compared them with the cancer species corresponding to the label indications of bevacizumab, rituximab, trastuzumab and their biosimilars to analyze the use of each medicine in different indications. Off-label indication refers to physician-prescribed uses outside approved indications. Our study revealed a surprisingly high prevalence of off-label use across all three classes of biologics and their biosimilars, with over 20% of prescriptions being for non-approved indications. Notably, biosimilars were preferentially selected for off-label use in bevacizumab and rituximab treatments, though this pattern was not observed with trastuzumab. This differential adoption may be attributable to the self-pay nature of most off-label prescriptions, where the lower cost of biosimilars presents a compelling advantage. These findings suggest that off-label use is emerging as a significant aspect of biosimilar utilization. Consequently, future clinical management strategies and post-marketing surveillance systems should specifically address off-label applications to ensure patient safety and therapeutic appropriateness.

Our study identified a notable utilization pattern: a substantial proportion of biosimilar-treated patients received therapy for extrapolated indications. Extrapolated indications refer to regulatory-approved additional indications based on scientific justification without indication-specific trials. According to the current policy, indication extrapolation is based on the overall similarity between the proposed biosimilar and the reference product. When clinical trials can demonstrate that the proposed biosimilar is clinically equivalent to the reference product in at least one indication, scientific evidence from research data and information related to extrapolated indications may support direct utilization of the proposed biosimilar for other indications approved for use by the reference product(Jin et al. 2024). A paradigmatic case is Qilu Pharmaceutical's bevacizumab biosimilar, initially approved based on lung cancer trials but predominantly used for colorectal cancer in clinical practice. The reason is that the median overall survival (OS) of patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer is usually about one year(Sukrithan et al. 2020), while the median OS of patients with advanced colorectal cancer may be more than two years(Overman et al. 2018). Clinical trials in lung cancer patients will inevitably require a shorter observation period than clinical trials in colorectal cancer patients, ensuring that the biosimilar can be brought to market quickly. While indication extrapolation accelerates market entry and improves treatment accessibility, it introduces unique challenges. Since extrapolated indications have not been confirmed by clinical trials, the effectiveness and safety of biosimilars still need to be verified by real-world studies. We urge more researchers to focus on the extrapolated indications of biosimilars.

China's extrapolation of indications policy for biosimilars is generally aligned with major international regulatory agencies (e.g., US FDA, EU EMA, and Japan PMDA) in principle, though differences exist in implementation details. According to China’s Technical Guidelines for Biosimilar Development and Evaluation, biosimilars can extrapolate to additional approved indications of the reference product based on demonstrated similarity, without requiring separate clinical trials for each indication (Liu et al. 2025)—a principle consistent with EU EMA and US FDA requirements. However, China may adopt a more cautious approach regarding the evidence requirements for extrapolation, particularly in justifying clinical relevance and mechanism of action. For example, while the EU EMA permits extrapolation across different populations (e.g., adults to children), China often requires additional bridging data or real-world evidence for support. In contrast, the US FDA adopts a relatively flexible stance but mandates strong scientific justification and may impose post-marketing study requirements depending on the product. Japan’s extrapolation policy falls between the US and EU approaches, allowing extrapolation but typically requiring more local data for approval(Ebbers and Chamberlain 2016). Overall, China’s biosimilar indication extrapolation framework is internationally harmonized, but its regulatory conservatism may be more pronounced, especially for indications with higher immunogenicity risks or complex mechanisms of action. It must be emphasized that the scientific validity of indication extrapolation ultimately requires verification through real-world pharmacovigilance. We advocate establishing international collaborative post-marketing surveillance systems to continuously monitor clinical efficacy variations and adverse event profiles in extrapolated indications, thereby providing dynamic evidence-based support for regulatory decision-making.

The observed adoption patterns of biosimilars in China carry important clinical implications. First, the differential uptake across therapeutic classes suggests that clinicians may perceive varying levels of therapeutic interchangeability—with higher comfort levels for certain drug classes (e.g., anti-angiogenics) compared to others (e.g., targeted therapies). This highlights the need for continued physician education on biosimilar similarity and real-world evidence. Second, the geographic and institutional variations in adoption rates point to disparities in clinical practice standardization. The faster uptake in tertiary hospitals may reflect greater familiarity with biosimilars, underscoring the importance of extending training programs to regional centers to ensure equitable patient access. Third, the preference for originators among certain patient subgroups (e.g., those with full insurance coverage) raises concerns about unnecessary healthcare expenditures without proven clinical benefits. This emphasizes the need for clear treatment guidelines emphasizing biosimilar appropriateness when clinical equivalence is established. Finally, the higher biosimilar use in off-label settings warrants attention—while it may improve access, it also requires vigilant pharmacovigilance to monitor outcomes in untested indications. A national outcomes registry could help assess therapeutic equivalence across all usage contexts.

Our findings highlight three key policy considerations for optimizing biosimilar utilization in China. First, regulatory oversight of off-label use requires standardized guidelines for biosimilar prescription beyond approved indications, particularly given our observed higher off-label adoption rates for certain biosimilars. Establishing a national registry to monitor real-world outcomes in these extrapolated indications would balance access with safety. Second, hospital formulary decisions should account for the faster biosimilar uptake in tertiary hospitals, suggesting tiered procurement targets aligned with hospital capabilities. Formulary committees should receive updated pharmacoeconomic training to evaluate total cost-of-care rather than just drug acquisition costs. Third, insurance coverage policies must address the payer disparities we identified, potentially through differential reimbursement rates that incentivize biosimilar use while maintaining patient choice. These measures should be implemented alongside ongoing price negotiations to sustain the current adoption momentum.

This large-scale study examines biosimilar utilization patterns across 109 hospitals in nine major Chinese cities following their domestic market introduction. The study found that the market share of biosimilars increased year by year, and analyzed the main reasons affecting the market share. In addition, the use of biosimilars in different regions, different hospital levels, different payer types, and different indications was also analyzed. This study has the following shortcomings: (1) The number of hospitals included in each city is inconsistent, and the prescription data of cities with a small number of hospitals may have certain errors. (2) The cities analyzed were all provincial capitals or municipalities, although they were spread across all regions of China, and there was no prescription information for remote rural areas. This urban bias may limit generalizability to China's broader healthcare landscape, particularly for regions with differing resource availability and prescribing practices. (3) Whether the indications conform to the specification is estimated according to the cancer type diagnosed, and cannot be completely accurate regarding the pathological type and treatment of the patient, so the accuracy of the indication is insufficient. (4) The restriction to 10 working days per quarter, while ensuring standardized data collection, may not fully capture atypical clinical events that could influence biosimilar adoption patterns. (5) We were unable to implement multilevel regression modeling due to data structure constraints. We expect that as more biosimilars come to market in the future, more comprehensive studies will analyze the use of biosimilars and provide more references for regulatory authorities, R&D companies, and clinical decision makers.

Conclusion

This nationwide study reveals three key characteristics of biosimilar utilization in China: (1) while adoption rates show consistent annual growth, significant variability exists across drug classes with bevacizumab biosimilars demonstrating the highest uptake and trastuzumab biosimilars the lowest; (2) substantial geographic disparities persist across cities; and (3) hospital tier and insurance type significantly influence prescribing patterns. These findings highlight critical implications—clinically, enhanced physician education on biosimilar equivalence is urgently needed, while policy adjustments should address regional implementation gaps in centralized procurement systems. Although limited by urban sampling bias and temporal constraints, this research underscores the necessity for future studies to incorporate broader primary care facility data, extend outcome monitoring periods, and conduct indication-specific evaluations. We recommend regulatory agencies establish risk-based monitoring systems for extrapolated indications, healthcare institutions develop phased substitution protocols, and payers implement value-based reimbursement incentives to optimize biosimilar utilization.

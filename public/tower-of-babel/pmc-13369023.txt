Cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab versus sorafenib as first-line treatment for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma in China and the United States healthcare systems
Authors: Junhao Yang, Haixia Ding
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionToripalimab combined with bevacizumab has demonstrated significant clinical efficacy and survival benefits in patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). However, the high cost of this combination therapy raises concerns regarding its cost-effectiveness in healthcare systems in China and the U.S.AimThis study evaluated the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab compared with sorafenib from the perspectives of the Chinese and U.S. healthcare systems. The findings are intended to inform value-based pricing strategies, reimbursement decisions, and clinical resource allocation.MethodA partitioned survival model (PSM) was developed to estimate incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) from the healthcare system perspective. Willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds were set at $40,011 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) in China and $100,000–$150,000 per QALY in the U.S. Deterministic and probabilistic sensitivity analyses were conducted to evaluate the robustness of the model outcomes.ResultsIn China, toripalimab plus bevacizumab produced an ICER of $55,764.00/QALY compared with sorafenib, exceeding the local WTP threshold of $40,011/QALY. In the U.S., the ICER was $460,054.17/QALY, which also exceeded the commonly accepted WTP thresholds. Sensitivity analyses indicated that the discount rate and the proportion of patients receiving subsequent anti-cancer therapies were the primary cost drivers in China and the United States, respectively. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis showed a 0.7% and 1.5% probability of cost-effectiveness at WTP thresholds of $100,000/QALY and $150,000/QALY in the U.S., respectively. In China, the probability of cost-effectiveness was 3.2% at a WTP threshold of $40,011/QALY.ConclusionAt current pricing levels, toripalimab plus bevacizumab is unlikely to be cost-effective compared with sorafenib in either China or the U.S. These findings highlight the need for value-based pricing strategies and more efficient allocation of healthcare resources.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11096-026-02133-z.

Impact statements

Toripalimab plus bevacizumab is unlikely to be cost-effective compared with sorafenib as first-line therapy for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma under current pricing in both China and the U.S.Drug pricing and the costs of subsequent anticancer therapies are major determinants of economic value, highlighting the importance of considering the full treatment pathway in cost-effectiveness evaluations.These findings support the need for value-based pricing strategies and reimbursement negotiations to balance clinical benefit with economic sustainability while maintaining patient access to innovative therapies.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer represents a major global public health challenge and is the sixth most common malignancy and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for approximately 90% of all primary liver cancer cases and is characterized by complex etiologies and a substantial disease burden, making it a leading cause of cancer-related death in many regions [1]. Most patients with HCC are diagnosed at an advanced stage, resulting in poor prognosis, with a five-year survival rate of less than 10% [2]. Recent data indicate that healthcare expenditures related to HCC treatment in the U.S. reach approximately US$405 million and continue to increase annually by 5.4% [3].

Historically, systemic chemotherapy has been the primary treatment for advanced HCC; however, its clinical benefits are limited by modest efficacy and significant toxicity. Following approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA), multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), particularly sorafenib, have become the standard first-line therapy and have remained the primary systemic treatment option for nearly a decade [4]. This treatment paradigm shifted with the results of the phase III IMbrave150 trial in 2020, which established immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) combined with targeted therapy as a new first-line standard for advanced HCC [5].

Combination regimens integrating ICIs with anti-angiogenic agents have since emerged. For example, atezolizumab plus bevacizumab (IMbrave150) and camrelizumab plus rivoceranib (CARES-310) have been approved as first-line treatment options in China [6]. Despite these advances, the increasing incidence and mortality of HCC, coupled with the limited number of effective therapeutic options, continue to impose substantial economic pressure on healthcare systems. Therefore, the development and evaluation of effective and economically sustainable treatment strategies remain an urgent priority.

Toripalimab, a humanized IgG4κ monoclonal antibody targeting programmed death-1 (PD-1), was approved by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) in 2018 for second-line treatment of metastatic melanoma in China [7]. Subsequent international phase III trials have demonstrated that toripalimab combined with standard chemotherapy significantly improves clinical outcomes in several malignancies, including recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (RM-NPC), advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), and advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), while maintaining an acceptable safety profile [8–10]. However, the high cost of ICI–based therapies—often exceeding $100,000 annually—places a considerable financial burden on healthcare systems. Although ICIs have improved survival outcomes in many cancers, their use may also exacerbate disparities in access to treatment among populations with unequal insurance coverage [11]. Consequently, the adoption of novel regimens such as toripalimab-based therapy for advanced HCC requires rigorous economic evaluation to inform healthcare decision-making.

Aim

This study aimed to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab compared with sorafenib from the perspectives of the Chinese and United States healthcare systems to inform healthcare policy decisions, reimbursement strategies, and clinical resource allocation.

Method

Patients and interventions

The target population for this study comprised patients with previously untreated, unresectable HCC, with baseline characteristics and treatment protocols aligned with those reported in the HEPATORCH trial [6]. Eligible patients aged 18–75 years were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either toripalimab plus bevacizumab or sorafenib monotherapy. Patients in the combination group received toripalimab (240 mg intravenously every 3 weeks) plus bevacizumab (15 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks), while those in the control group received sorafenib (400 mg orally twice daily). In the base-case analysis, drug costs were calculated based on the exact required dosage without considering vial wastage. Treatments were administered in 21-day cycles and continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

After disease progression, 56.8% of patients in the toripalimab plus bevacizumab group and 68.9% in the sorafenib group received subsequent anticancer therapies, consistent with the data reported in Supplementary Appendix 2 of the HEPATORCH trial. According to the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Primary Liver Cancer (2024 Edition) [12] and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Guidelines (Version 2.2025) [13], regorafenib was used as a representative second-line therapy for patients receiving active subsequent treatment, while the remaining patients received best supportive care (BSC). This simplification was adopted because several alternative second-line agents are not yet available in China, and real-world cost data for diverse treatment sequences remain limited in both regions. The assumed regimen for regorafenib was 160 mg orally once daily for the first three weeks of each four-week cycle [14]. Overall, the treatment pathways modeled were consistent with published clinical evidence and current treatment guidelines in both China and the U.S.

Model structure

A partitioned survival model (PSM) was constructed in Microsoft Excel to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab versus sorafenib for advanced HCC. The model included three mutually exclusive health states: progression-free survival (PFS), progressive disease (PD), and death (Fig. S1). A lifetime time horizon was applied, simulating the cohort until more than 99% of patients reached the absorbing state of death. This corresponded to approximately 17 years, which was sufficient to capture all relevant long-term costs and health outcomes [15].

All patients were entered into the model in the PFS state, and transitions between health states were modeled as unidirectional without regression to previous states [16]. The proportions of patients in each health state over time were derived from the area under the progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) curves from the HEPATORCH trial. The model cycle length was set at 21 days to match the treatment schedule, and a half-cycle correction was applied to reduce potential estimation bias.

The primary outcomes included total costs, quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER). All costs were converted to 2024 US dollars (1 USD = 7.1217 CNY) to facilitate international comparisons [17]. Willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds were set at $100,000–$150,000 US dollars per QALY in the U.S. [18] and 40,011 US dollars per QALY in China, corresponding to three times the per capita gross domestic product (GDP) in 2024 [19]. Future costs and health outcomes were discounted at annual rates of 5% in China and 3% in the U.S. [20, 21].

Data processing and evaluation

PFS and OS curves were digitized from published Kaplan–Meier plots using GetData Graph Digitizer [16]. The reconstructed individual patient data were subsequently processed using R (version 4.4.3) and Excel 2021 for data preparation, model development, and graphical visualization. The reconstructed hazard ratio (HR) confidence intervals were consistent with the point estimates reported in the original study, indicating satisfactory accuracy of the reconstructed data (Figs. S2–S3).

A multi-model strategy was applied for survival extrapolation in R, incorporating both standard parametric models (SPM) and Royston–Parmar spline models (RPSM) [22]. The SPM framework included three estimation approaches: maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC), and integrated nested Laplace approximation (INLA). Using the survHE package, these estimation methods were integrated to perform parametric survival analysis. Specifically, the flexsurv module within survHE enabled the fitting of nine parametric survival distributions under the MLE framework, providing flexibility for model selection [23, 24]. The RPSM approach included normal, hazard, and odds spline models with 1–3 knots.

Model selection was guided by statistical goodness-of-fit indicators, including the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC) (Table S1) [25]. In addition to statistical performance, clinical plausibility and structural consistency were considered when selecting the final survival models. For the toripalimab plus bevacizumab arm, the normal-2 and normal-3 spline models provided the best fit for OS and PFS, respectively. For the sorafenib arm, the Weibull and hazard-1 models were selected (Table S2)).

Costs and utilities inputs

This study included only direct healthcare costs, comprising drug acquisition, follow-up care, routine laboratory testing and imaging, management of adverse events (AEs), BSC, and end-of-life care. Cost inputs were obtained from local hospitals, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, IBM RED BOOK Online [26, 27], and relevant published literature.

For the U.S. analysis, drug costs were estimated using the average wholesale price (AWP) as a transparent and publicly accessible benchmark in the absence of proprietary transaction-level pricing data. Because pharmacoeconomic evaluations aim to approximate the economic burden faced by healthcare payers and patients, AWP was adopted as a standardized proxy for reimbursement calculations and out-of-pocket estimations, ensuring reproducibility and comparability across studies. For the Chinese healthcare system, drug prices were obtained from local hospital procurement systems, reflecting the most recent negotiated prices listed in the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL). These prices represent standardized pre-reimbursement acquisition costs and therefore provide an appropriate basis for cost-effectiveness analysis from the healthcare system perspective.

Only serious AEs (grade ≥ 3) with an incidence greater than 5% were included in the analysis, including proteinuria, thrombocytopenia, hypertension, increased aspartate aminotransferase, increased alanine aminotransferase, anemia, diarrhea, and palmar–plantar erythrodysaesthesia syndrome. Incidence rates were derived from the HEPATORCH trial [6], while cost estimates were obtained from published studies. All AEs were assumed to occur during the first treatment cycle. Costs were adjusted to 2024 US dollars using the consumer price index.

Health utility values were defined on a scale from 0 (death) to 1 (perfect health). State-specific utility values for PFS and PD were obtained from published cost-effectiveness studies of unresectable HCC treated with monoclonal antibody-based therapies. Because the pivotal clinical trial did not report raw EQ-5D data, these published utility values were used as fixed parameters, which precluded the application of country-specific preference weights for China and the U.S. To ensure methodological consistency, disutility values associated with adverse events were also derived from published literature. For parameters for which HCC-specific data were limited, such as anemia-related disutility, values from high-quality NSCLC studies were adopted. This cross-indication extrapolation is clinically reasonable because the management and patient-reported burden of treatment-induced anemia are generally comparable across advanced solid tumors, and toripalimab has established safety profiles in both NSCLC and HCC. Sensitivity analysis

To evaluate the robustness of the model and assess the impact of parameter uncertainty on the results, both one-way sensitivity analysis (OWSA) and probabilistic sensitivity analysis (PSA) were conducted. In the OWSA, each parameter was varied by ± 20% around its base-case value to reflect plausible real-world uncertainty [20]. For parameters with potentially greater uncertainty, particularly utility and disutility values, an additional stress test was performed by expanding the variation range to ± 30%. Discount rates were varied from 0 to 6% for the United States and from 0 to 8% for China. For drug costs, the official listed price served as the base-case value, and 80% of the baseline price was applied as the lower bound in accordance with pharmaceutical pricing regulations. The results of the OWSA are presented using tornado diagrams.

PSA was performed using 1000 Monte Carlo simulations, with cost parameters modeled using Gamma distributions and utility parameters, weights, and discount rates modeled using Beta distributions [39]. To address the skewed and unstable distribution often associated with ICER estimates, a non-parametric bootstrap approach was applied to generate empirical distributions of model outcomes. The 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for both ICER and incremental net monetary benefit (INMB) were estimated using the percentile method, which avoids assumptions of normality and improves statistical robustness.

In addition, INMB was incorporated to provide a linear measure of cost-effectiveness, thereby avoiding the numerical instability associated with ratio-based ICER estimates. The PSA results were illustrated using cost-effectiveness scatter plots and cost-effectiveness acceptability curves (CEACs) to characterize the overall uncertainty in the cost-effectiveness outcomes. Model convergence was assessed by plotting the cumulative mean ICER against the number of Monte Carlo iterations (1–1000), demonstrating that the model reached statistical stability within the simulation range (Figs. S12–S13).

Scenario Analysis

Scenario analyses were conducted to examine the impact of assumptions regarding second-line treatment strategies and pricing adjustments. First, to evaluate the effect of potential price reductions, a scenario assuming a 30% decrease in the price of regorafenib was modeled. Second, to better reflect heterogeneity in real-world clinical practice, an alternative scenario was assumed in 50% of patients receiving subsequent therapy were treated with regorafenib, while the remaining patients received BSC.

Ethics approval

This study was based solely on modeling methods and data obtained from previously published literature. As no human participants were involved, ethical approval was not required.

Scenario analysis

Two scenario analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the base-case findings. In the first scenario, a 30% price reduction in regorafenib was assumed to reflect potential cost adjustments for subsequent therapies. Under this assumption, the total costs in China were $31,301.16 for the toripalimab group and $6148.62 for the sorafenib group, resulting in an ICER of $55,703.54 per QALY. In the U.S., the adjusted total costs were $628,767.60 and $417,695.40, respectively, yielding an ICER of $416,948.72 per QALY.

In the second scenario, treatment heterogeneity was considered by assuming that 50% of patients receiving subsequent therapy were treated with regorafenib, while the remaining patients received BSC. Under this assumption, the total costs in China were $32,037.93 for the toripalimab group and $7375.55 for the sorafenib group, resulting in an ICER of $54,618.02 per QALY. In the U.S. total costs were $628,767.60 and $431,526.51, respectively, producing an ICER of $562,343.59 per QALY.

Across both scenarios, the ICERs remained substantially higher than the corresponding WTP thresholds in China and the U.S., indicating that the base-case conclusions were robust to variations in post-progression treatment costs and patterns.

Discussion

This study evaluated the cost-effectiveness of toripalimab plus bevacizumab compared with sorafenib for advanced HCC from the perspectives of healthcare payers in China and the U.S. Although toripalimab plus bevacizumab generated comparable health benefits in both settings, substantial differences in total costs and ICERs between the two countries suggest that cost-effectiveness outcomes are influenced by divergent pharmaceutical pricing mechanisms and healthcare delivery structures in the two countries.

In China, the price of toripalimab declined substantially after its inclusion in the NRDL, a dynamic framework that grants nationwide insurance coverage in return for negotiated price concessions [40–42]. This reflects the growing influence of value-based negotiation and volume-based procurement (VBP)—a strategic policy that consolidates purchasing volumes from public hospitals to drive down drug prices—in shaping China’s oncology market. These mechanisms have led to considerable price reductions for newly launched anticancer therapies [43]. In contrast, oncology drug pricing in the U.S. is shaped by a complex interplay of market-based negotiations and reference benchmarks, such as the average wholesale price (AWP), a publicly available metric often used for reimbursement calculations before accounting for confidential rebates and discounts [44]. Therefore, the marked divergence in ICER estimates between China and the U.S. is more likely attributable to structural differences in price formation and regulatory frameworks than to disparities in clinical efficacy. These findings highlight the sensitivity of cost-effective outcomes to country-specific healthcare governance and pricing environments.

The one-way sensitivity analyses revealed distinct structural drivers of cost-effectiveness in the two healthcare settings. In China, the economic results were mainly determined by assumptions related to the first-line treatment regimen, including drug costs and health state utilities. In contrast, in the U.S., the model was highly sensitive to the proportion of patients receiving subsequent anticancer therapies. This finding suggests that in the U.S., cost-effectiveness is influenced by the broader cost structure of the entire treatment pathway, where the multi-payer system allows patients to receive additional systemic treatments associated with high costs [45]. Compared with many other developed countries, cost-effectiveness evidence plays a relatively limited role in healthcare decision-making in the U.S., which may partly explain the less restrictive processes governing the approval and coverage of oncology drugs. For instance, among 45 oncology drug indications approved in the U.S. between January 1, 2009, and December 31, 2013, 67% (30 indications) were also approved by the European Medicines Agency (EMA), and 53% (24 indications) were approved in Canada and Australia by December 31, 2013. However, as of June 30, 2014, all 45 indications were covered by public insurance programs in the U.S., whereas coverage rates were substantially lower in other countries, including 58% (26) in the United Kingdom, 42% (19) in France, 29% (13) in Canada, and 24% (11) in Australia [46].

The WTP threshold is a critical factor in determining cost-effectiveness outcomes. In the present study, the probabilities that toripalimab plus bevacizumab would be considered cost effective were only 0.7% and 1.5% at WTP thresholds of $100,000 and $150,000 in the U.S. These findings suggest that the conclusion of “not cost effective” reflects broader concerns regarding the alignment between drug prices and clinical value [52]. Consequently, many therapies are adopted in clinical practice despite ICERs exceeding commonly cited thresholds. Our findings do not argue against the clinical use of toripalimab plus bevacizumab in patients with advanced HCC; rather, they highlight the importance of allocating healthcare resources toward interventions that provide greater economic value. Given the strong influence of subsequent anticancer therapies observed in the sensitivity analysis, together with the evolving treatment paradigm driven by targeted therapies and ICIs [5], the need to reassess second-line and later-line treatment strategies has become increasingly apparent [4].

In China, WTP thresholds are commonly linked to per capita GDP and are closely associated with affordability and equity considerations within the national health insurance system. Exceeding this threshold indicates that widespread adoption of the regimen could impose a substantial financial burden on the healthcare system under current budgetary constraints. Several innovative payment mechanisms have been explored in China, including price–volume agreements, outcome-based payment models, expenditure caps, and pilot diagnosis-related group (DRG) payment systems. A broader implementation of these strategies may help alleviate payer concerns and reduce financial pressure on healthcare funds [47, 48].

Our findings corroborate recent evidence, including a meta-analysis of five clinical studies (n = 3265) that reported a pooled incremental net benefit (INB) of -$42,000, confirming that immunotherapy-based regimens often struggle to achieve cost-effectiveness compared to sorafenib [49, 50]. However, our study extends this discourse by demonstrating that the economic value of immunotherapy is not a fixed attribute but is highly sensitive to the interaction between treatment design and healthcare policy. While recent evaluations have identified the STRIDE regimen as cost-effective in the U.S. owing to its 'single-priming' dose design that minimizes long-term maintenance costs [51], our analysis indicates that toripalimab plus bevacizumab faces significant economic barriers in both China and the U.S. This divergence shows that economic viability is fundamentally dependent on specific treatment paradigms, particularly whether a regimen mandates long-term continuous therapy versus episodic administration. Furthermore, by implementing a comparative framework across two distinct systems, we highlight that pharmaceutical pricing mechanisms—ranging from China’s value-based negotiation to the U.S. market-based framework—play a decisive role in determining cost-effectiveness. Finally, unlike prior research that focused predominantly on first-line costs, our sensitivity analysis uniquely identifies the 'post-progression' treatment sequence as a critical economic driver in the U.S., suggesting that future models must incorporate granular data on real-world treatment pathways to improve the accuracy of economic projections.

This study has several limitations. First, owing to the absence of individual patient-level data from the HEPATORCH trial, survival extrapolation relied on parametric modeling, which may introduce uncertainty in long-term outcome projections. Second, the clinical inputs were derived from a single trial (HEPATORCH), and any inherent limitations related to trial design, sample size, or patient population may have influenced the estimated outcomes. Third, although regorafenib was used as the representative second-line therapy to align with clinical guideline recommendations, this assumption simplifies the complexity of real-world clinical practice, particularly in the U.S. healthcare system. Subsequent treatment strategies are rarely uniform and are often individualized based on clinician judgment, patient preferences, and access to a range of therapeutic options. Future economic evaluations incorporating real-world longitudinal data would help better capture this heterogeneity in treatment pathways. Nevertheless, sensitivity analyses indicated that the primary economic conclusions of the study remained robust despite these assumptions. In addition, health utility values and certain post-progression costs were obtained from published literature rather than directly measured in the trial, which may introduce additional uncertainty. Finally, policy-level interpretations should be approached with caution, as they are based on generalized assumptions and may not fully account for real-world factors, such as confidential pricing agreements, rebates, or institutional procurement variations.

Conclusion

Despite substantial differences between the healthcare systems in the U.S. and China, toripalimab plus bevacizumab was not cost-effective compared with sorafenib in either setting under current pricing. These findings highlight the importance of continued policy efforts to promote value-based pricing, optimize healthcare resource allocation, and ensure sustainable access to innovative oncology therapies. Given that the U.S. and China represent prominent examples of developed and developing healthcare systems, respectively, these results may also provide relevant insights for healthcare decision-making in other regions.

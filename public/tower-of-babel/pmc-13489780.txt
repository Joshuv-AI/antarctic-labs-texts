Amivantamab in Recurrent/Metastatic Head and Neck Squamous Cell Cancer After Checkpoint Inhibitor and Chemotherapy: Pivotal Results From the Phase Ib/II OrigAMI-4 Study

ABSTRACT
Single-agent paclitaxel or cetuximab after immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) and chemotherapy demonstrated objective response rates (ORRs) of 21%-24% in recurrent/metastatic (R/M) head/neck squamous cell cancer (HNSCC). Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and MET are overexpressed in R/M HNSCC. Amivantamab, an EGFR-MET bispecific antibody, may be a rational treatment. Cohort 1 of OrigAMI-4 (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06385080) evaluated subcutaneous amivantamab administered every 3 weeks in participants with non-human papillomavirus R/M HNSCC after PD-(L)1 inhibitor and platinum-based chemotherapy. Prior anti-EGFR therapy and p16-positive oropharyngeal cancer were exclusionary. The primary end point was RECIST v1.1 ORR. Secondary end points included duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety. In 102 participants, blinded independent central review‑assessed ORR was 42% (95% CI, 32 to 52); the complete response rate was 15%. Median DoR was not reached (NR; 95% CI, 6.9 to NR); 56% of responses lasted ≥6 months. Investigator-assessed ORR was 47% (95% CI, 37 to 57). At a median follow-up of 11.8 months (range, 1.1-21.9), median PFS and OS were 6.8 months (95% CI, 5.2 to 8.3) and 12.5 months (95% CI, 10.2 to 16.8), respectively. Adverse events were consistent with previous experience with no new safety signals. Treatment-related discontinuations were low (8%). Amivantamab demonstrated greater antitumor activity in participants previously exposed to ICI and chemotherapy than has been reported for paclitaxel or cetuximab.

INTRODUCTION

Globally, there are approximately 890,000 new patient cases and 450,000 deaths from head and neck squamous cell cancer (HNSCC) annually.1 Most HNSCCs are caused by carcinogens, and HPV-negative tumors do not typically overexpress the p16 tumor suppressor gene.2,3 Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and MET are key mechanistic drivers of tumor progression overexpressed in 80%-90% of p16-negative R/M HNSCC tumors and associated with poor prognosis.4-7 MET overexpression is associated with tumor cell proliferation, immune evasion, metastasis, and anti-EGFR resistance.4-7

Recommended first-line treatment of recurrent/metastatic (R/M) HNSCC includes anti‑PD-1 immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICIs) with/without platinum-based chemotherapy, or cetuximab with platinum-based chemotherapy, if the PD-L1 combined positive score is <1 or ICIs are contraindicated.8-11 Patients with disease progression on first-line therapy have poor prognosis.9 Single-agent cetuximab or paclitaxel yielded objective response rates (ORRs) in 10%-12% of chemotherapy-exposed participants with R/M HNSCC12,13; recent trials showed ORRs of 21%-24% among those who also received prior ICIs.14,15 Therefore, there is a need for improved treatment in previously treated R/M HNSCC.

Amivantamab is a bispecific antibody with dual targeting activity against EGFR and MET, and immune cell‑directing activity.16,17 Amivantamab is active in EGFR-mutated advanced non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC),18-21 with multiple regulatory approvals,22-25 and has demonstrated antitumor activity in metastatic colorectal cancer,26,27 with phase III studies ongoing (ClinicalTrials.gov identifiers: NCT06662786 and NCT06750094).

Amivantamab received Breakthrough Therapy Designation for R/M HNSCC from the US Food and Drug Administration on the basis of results from the initial 38 participants in the efficacy population in cohort 1 of the OrigAMI-4 study.28,29 We report pivotal results from all 102 participants of the fully enrolled cohort 1 of OrigAMI-4.

METHODS

Participants

Full details of the trial have been reported.28 Briefly, adults (18 years and older) with non-human papillomavirus R/M HNSCC who received anti‑PD-(L)1 inhibitor and platinum-based chemotherapy were eligible. Participants with p16-positive oropharyngeal squamous cell cancer were excluded. For the other tumor locations, p16 testing was not mandated, but any participants with previously known p16-positive status were excluded. Those who received prior anti-EGFR therapy or >2 prior lines of systemic therapy in the R/M setting were excluded.

Study Design and Treatment

OrigAMI-4 (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06385080) is an open-label, multicenter, phase Ib/II trial evaluating subcutaneous amivantamab alone or with standard-of-care therapies in participants with HNSCC. This analysis presents results from the fully enrolled cohort 1, which evaluated amivantamab monotherapy administered every 3 weeks (Q3W) in previously treated R/M HNSCC. The protocol was approved by local institutional review boards and independent ethics committees of the participating centers. All participants provided written informed consent (Appendix Methods, online only).

End Points and Assessments

The primary end point was ORR by RECIST v1.1 determined by the investigator and confirmed by blinded independent central review (BICR). Consistent with regulatory guidance for single-arm trials, ORR results are shown using BICR, with investigator ORR presented in the Appendix. Clinical benefit rate (CBR), duration of response (DoR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and safety were secondary end points (Appendix Methods).

Statistical Analysis

The null hypothesis was an investigator-assessed ORR ≤10% on the basis of historical results with single-agent paclitaxel or cetuximab.12,13 The original sample size was based on the alternative hypothesis of ORR >30%. A sample size of 25 evaluable participants would provide >80% power (1-sided alpha: 5%) to reject the null hypothesis. To further characterize the robustness of the treatment effect, the total sample size was increased to 100 participants (80 response-evaluable) following the interim analysis to provide >99% power (2-sided alpha: 5%) to reject the null hypothesis (Appendix Methods).

Efficacy

At a median follow-up of 11.8 months (range, 1.1-21.9), the BICR-assessed confirmed ORR was 42% (95% CI, 32 to 52; Table 2). Fifteen (15%) complete responses (CRs) and 28 (27%) partial responses were observed. BICR-assessed best responses are presented in Figure 1A and Appendix Figure A2. CBR was 63% (95% CI, 53 to 72). Among 43 confirmed responders, median time to response was 6.6 weeks (range, 5.6-36.9). At data cutoff, 63% (27/43) of responders had an ongoing response; 56% (24/43) had a DoR ≥6 months, and median DoR had not been reached (NR; 95% CI, 6.9 to NR; Fig 1B). Generally consistent ORRs were observed across prespecified subgroups (Appendix Fig A3). Investigator-assessed confirmed ORR was 47% (95% CI, 37 to 57; Appendix Table A1 and Fig A4).

Median PFS by investigator was 6.8 months (95% CI, 5.2 to 8.3; Fig 1C). At 6 and 12 months, 53% and 23% of participants, respectively, were alive and progression free. Median OS was 12.5 months (95% CI, 10.2 to 16.8; Fig 1D), with 78% and 54% of participants alive at 6 and 12 months, respectively. Antitumor activity was observed across genetic aberrations; no clear associations were established, but analysis was limited by the small sample size (Appendix Fig A5).

Safety

All participants (N = 102) had ≥1 treatment-emergent AE (TEAE); 60% experienced ≥1 grade ≥3 TEAE (Appendix Table A2). Incidence of serious AEs was 39%. The most frequent (≥30%) TEAEs were hypoalbuminemia (50%), rash (37%), acneiform dermatitis (34%), and paronychia (34%). Most TEAEs were grade 1-2. Administration-related reactions were reported in 15% of participants and were grade 1 (9%) or 2 (6%). Treatment-related AEs are listed in Appendix Table A3. Amivantamab discontinuation owing to treatment-related AEs occurred in 8% of participants.

DISCUSSION

In this fully enrolled cohort of 102 participants with R/M HNSCC who previously received anti–PD-(L)1 and platinum-based chemotherapy, subcutaneous amivantamab Q3W demonstrated a BICR-assessed confirmed ORR of 42%. The CR rate was 15%. Responses were rapid and durable. Median OS exceeded 1 year, with 54% of participants alive at 1 year. The safety profile of amivantamab monotherapy was consistent with that established in prior subcutaneous amivantamab studies.30,31

Outcomes have historically been poor for patients with R/M HNSCC after ICI and platinum-based chemotherapy.14,15 Standard-of-care cetuximab provides only a modest benefit in ICI-experienced and platinum-experienced patients, with an ORR of 24%, CRs observed in only 1% of patients, median PFS of 3.8 months, and median OS of 8.6 months.15 Resistance to EGFR-directed therapy is common and frequently mediated by compensatory signaling through parallel pathways, including MET pathway activation,32 highlighting the potential advantage of cotargeting parallel resistance pathways.

Other investigational agents are being evaluated in second-line or later R/M HNSCC. The EGFR-LGR5 bispecific antibody petosemtamab showed an investigator-assessed ORR of 36% as a monotherapy.33 Intravenous ficlatuzumab, an antihepatocyte growth factor monoclonal antibody, plus cetuximab demonstrated an investigator-assessed ORR of 38% and median PFS of 4.1 months in patients with triple-refractory (cetuximab, platinum, anti‑PD-1 ICI) R/M HNSCC.34

Compared with intravenous administration of anti-EGFR therapies (typically 1 hour), subcutaneous amivantamab can be administered in <10 minutes and has a lower rate of infusion-related reactions.30,35 Amivantamab dosing is approved for every 2- to 4-week administration,36 providing dosing flexibility and improved convenience. The phase III OrigAMI-5 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT07276399) is assessing subcutaneous amivantamab plus pembrolizumab and carboplatin for first-line treatment of R/M HNSCC versus 5-fluorouracil plus pembrolizumab and platinum (carboplatin/cisplatin), a reference standard in R/M HNSCC.

In conclusion, subcutaneous amivantamab demonstrated the highest ORR reported among patients with R/M HNSCC who have received an ICI and platinum-based chemotherapy, with rapid, deep, and durable responses and prolonged survival. These results support subcutaneous amivantamab as a therapeutic option for patients in this setting.

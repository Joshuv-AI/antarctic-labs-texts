C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for Schrödinger potentials of Shannon-entropy regularized Optimal Transport
Sumiya Baasandorj, Simone Di Marino, Augusto Gerolin

Abstract
We provide the stability of C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} regularity of Schrödinger potentials of Boltzmann-Shannon entropy regularized Optimal Transport under the classical assumption of marginals supported on bounded convex sets and whose densities are bounded above and below.



Keywords:
Caffarelli regularity, Shannon-entropy regularized Optimal Transport

1
Introduction

In this paper, we obtain Cafarelli-type regularity results for the Boltzmann-Shannon regularized Optimal Transport problem

\displaystyle\text{W}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}|x-y|^{2}\,d\pi(x,y)+\varepsilon H(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu),

(1.1)

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of transport plans between probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 1)
. The functional
H(\cdot|\mu\otimes\nu)
denotes the Boltzmann-Shannon relative entropy (or Kullback-Leibler divergence) with respect to the product measure
\mu\otimes\nu
,

\displaystyle H(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int\limits\log\left(\frac{d\pi}{d(\mu\otimes\nu)}\right)\,d\pi&\mbox{if }\pi\ll\mu\otimes\nu,\\
+\infty&\mbox{if }\pi\not\ll\mu\otimes\nu.\end{cases}

More accurately, let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded convex open sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be probability measures such that their supports
\mathrm{spt}(\mu)\subset\Omega_{1}
and
\mathrm{spt}(\nu)\subset\Omega_{2}
, are contained, respectively, in
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
. Additionally, throughout the paper we assume that the measures
\mu
and
\nu
are absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure

\displaystyle\mu=f(x)\,dx\quad\text{and}\quad\nu=g(y)\,dy,

(1.2)

for some measurable density functions
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that there exist constants
0<\lambda\leqslant\Lambda
such that

\displaystyle\lambda\leqslant f(x)\leqslant\Lambda\quad\forall x\in\Omega_{1},\quad\lambda\leqslant g(y)\leqslant\Lambda\quad\forall y\in\Omega_{2}.

(1.3)

When the variational problem (
1.1
) admits a solution
\pi_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
, called
entropic optimal transport plan
, it is unique and has the following Gibbs distribution
[
26
,
24
]

\displaystyle\frac{d\pi_{\varepsilon}}{d(\mu\otimes\nu)}(x,y)=\exp\left(\frac{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(y)-|x-y|^{2}/2}{\varepsilon}\right),

(1.4)

for two measurable functions
\varphi_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\psi_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, called
Schrödinger potentials
. In particular, the pair
(\varphi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon})
maximizes the dual problem of (
1.1
) which reads as

\displaystyle W_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mu,\nu)=\ \sup\limits_{\varphi\in C(\Omega_{1}),\psi\in C(\Omega_{2})}\left\{D_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,\psi)\right\}+\varepsilon,

where

D_{\varepsilon}(\varphi,\psi)=\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}\varphi\,d\mu+\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}\psi\,d\nu-\varepsilon\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}\exp\left(\frac{\varphi(x)+\psi(y)-|x-y|^{2}/2}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)\,d\nu(y),

is the dual functional, see again
[
26
,
24
]
. For abbreviation, let us denote in the rest of the paper

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad v_{\varepsilon}(y):=\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}-\psi_{\varepsilon}(y).

(1.5)

Rewriting the feasibility condition
\pi_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
, we find that the functions
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
solve the so-called
Schrödinger system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)=\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)&\quad\mu\text{ - a.e,}\\
\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)=\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)&\quad\nu\text{ - a.e.}\end{array}\right.

The main result is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two convex and bounded sets and let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega_{1}),\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega_{2})
satisfy assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.3
). Then there exists
\beta\equiv\beta(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
if we consider
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
Schrödinger potentials defined in (
1.5
) that satisfy system of equations (
1
) we have
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1,\beta}_{\loc}(\Omega_{1})
. In particular for every convex subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
, there exists a universal constant
C_{0}
depending on
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and the modulus of
u_{0}
(independent of
\varepsilon
) such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\neq y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}\frac{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\beta}}\leqslant C_{0}

holds whenever
\varepsilon>0
.

Theorem
1.1
corresponds to the entropic version of the celebrated Caffarelli’s
C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}
-regularity theory for Monge-Ampère equation
[
5
,
6
]
. Although the Schrödinger potentials
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{1})
and
v_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{2})
are smooth for every
\varepsilon>0

[
26
,
16
]
, the known general quantitative estimates beyond the first order explode when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
(see for example
[
16
]
where one can obtain
\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{k-1}}
where
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
).

In particular proving that there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
such that
u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}\in C^{1,\beta}_{loc}

uniformly
in
\varepsilon
is not at all obvious. Addressing this question is one of the main motivations in this paper.

Remark 1.2
.

Following the proof, the explicit value of
\beta
that we can get is
\beta=\inf\{\frac{1}{n^{2}},\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}\}
, where
\alpha
is such that
u_{0}\in C^{0,\alpha}_{loc}
.
We would expect that it would be possible to take
\beta=\alpha_{0}
, the Hölder exponent given by Caffarelli theory (depending on
\Lambda,\lambda
and the geometry of the domains). On one side our result is worse since in the case
\alpha\approx\alpha_{0}\ll 1
we have
\beta\approx\alpha_{0}^{2}\ll\alpha_{0}
. On the other side our result is
adaptive
to the regularity of
u_{0}
itself, and the dependence on
\frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}
and the geometry appears only at the level of constants.

Prior results.
There has been a recent line of works where a uniform bounds (in
\varepsilon
) is obtained for the second derivative of entropic potentials but with stronger hypotesis on the marginals: the first result in this direction is
[
14
]
where the entropic equivalent of the
Caffarelli contraction theorem
is proved (see also
[
10
]
for a simpler proof), and then this result is improved in various ways in the more recent work
[
20
]
.
Despite the results are stronger in terms of smoothness of the potentials, in both these papers there is always a second-order assumption on the marginals: in the first one strongly log-concavity is required while in the second one
\rho
-convexity of the logarithm is required.

A new variational method to prove the (classical)
C^{0,\alpha}_{loc}
-regularity result by Caffarelli was proposed by Goldman and Otto in
[
19
]
and then generalized in
[
18
,
30
]
. Recently this approach was used in
[
22
]
for the entropic optimal transport problem: the authors prove a Hölder estimate for the entropic map however it holds only at
large
scales (with respect to
\varepsilon
).

, under the settings of (

)-(

), the Boltzmann-Shannon-Entropy regularized Optimal Transport in equation (

)
\Gamma-
converges to the

Wasserstein distance between the probability measures

and

[

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}\frac{1}{2}|x-y|^{2}\,d\pi(x,y).

(1.8)

Moreover, the pair of Schrödinger potentials

in (

) converges to the pair of functions

so-called Kantorovich potentials under a suitable normalization that solve the dual problem

(1.9)

In the rest of the paper, we denote

u_{0}(x):=\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}-\varphi_{0}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad v_{0}(y):=\frac{|y|^{2}}{2}-\psi_{0}(y).\vskip 14.22636pt

(1.10)

Notice that of course a uniform estimate for

would imply the same regularity result for

, so it is natural to consider as hypotesis the ones in the classical case (

), (

).

Main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.1
:
The main ingredients of the proof are

(i)

Caffarelli’s
C_{loc}^{0,\alpha}
-regularity of the optimal map (or
C_{loc}^{1,\alpha}
regularity for Kantorovich potentials) for
2
-Wasserstein distance (
1.8
), see Theorem
2.1
below;

(ii)

A
local
p
-detachment
result (Lemma
3.3
);

(iii)

The first-order expansion of the Boltzmann-Shannon
regularized Optimal Transport problem (
1.1
) around the limit

by Carlier, Pegon and Tamanini
[
9
]
(see also
[
8
]
), i.e.

0\leqslant\text{W}^{2}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\text{W}^{2}_{2}(\mu,\nu)\leqslant\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+O(\varepsilon).

(iv)

The points (ii) and (iii) together will imply an
L^{p}
-closedness result between
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
and
\nabla u_{0}
. Therefore we show

and

are close in

and

2
Optimal Transport Theory for the quadratic cost

We first recall that given two probability measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Monge-Kantorovich problem with quadratic cost function is concerned with minimization problem
(
1.8
). The existence of a minimizer
\pi_{0}
(an optimal transport plan between measures
\mu
and
\nu
) for problem (
1.8
) is a classical result, see for instance
[
33
]
. Moreover, there exists a minimizer
\pi_{0}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
if and only if
\pi_{0}
is concentrated on the graph of the subdifferential of a lower-semicontinuous and convex function
u_{0}
, see
[
3
,
4
,
27
]
:

\displaystyle\text{spt}(\pi_{0})\subset\text{Graph}(\partial u_{0})

\displaystyle:=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:y\in\partial u_{0}(x)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}:u_{0}(x)+u_{0}^{*}(y)=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right\},

where
u_{0}^{*}
denotes Legendre transform given by

u_{0}^{*}(y):=\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\left\langle y,z\right\rangle-u_{0}(z)\right),

and the subdifferential of
u_{0}
at
x
is given by

\displaystyle\partial u_{0}(x)

\displaystyle:=\left\{p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:u_{0}(z)\geqslant u_{0}(x)+\left\langle p,z-x\right\rangle\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

Moreover, under the assumption that
\mu
and
\nu
are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, there is a pair of functions
(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})
maximizing the dual problem (
1.9
) that are differentiable almost everywhere on the support of
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively, and they satisfy the following equations

\nabla u_{0}(x)=x-\nabla\varphi_{0}(x)\quad\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\nabla u_{0}^{*}(y)=y-\nabla\psi_{0}(y)\quad\text{ for }\nu\text{-a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here
(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a pair of optimal Kantorovich potentials, see for instance
[
3
,
4
]
.

2.1
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity in classical Optimal Transport Theory

Under the assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.3
), it is known that there is a unique optimal transport plan
\pi_{0}:=(\text{Id},T)_{\#}\mu
, where

is the so-called Brenier’s optimal transport map from

\pi_{0}
for (

satisfies the Monge-Ampere equation

\displaystyle g(\nabla u_{0}(x))\det\left(\nabla^{2}u_{0}(x)\right)=f(x)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{1}

(2.1)

in the Alexandrov sense with the natural boundary condition

\displaystyle\nabla u_{0}(\Omega_{1})=\Omega_{2},

see for instance
[
15
,
21
,
32
]
and references therein. Moreover,
u_{0}
is the strictly convex function in
\Omega_{1}
, see
[
6
,
31
]
, and

is of class

with a universal exponent

depending only on

,

and
\Lambda
by Caffarelli’s pioneering result
[
5
,
6
]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
u_{0}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a strictly convex solution of (

) in the Alexandrov sense under the assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.3
). Then

for a universal exponent
\alpha_{0}\equiv\alpha_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
. Moreover, for every subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
there exists a constant
M_{0}
depending on
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and the modulus of convexity of

such that

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\neq y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}\frac{|\nabla u_{0}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha_{0}}}\leqslant M_{0}.

2.2
Boltzmann-Shannon entropy
regularized Optimal Transport

It is the fact that problem (
1.1
) with a general bounded cost function
c:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
instead of the quadratic cost function

\displaystyle\text{OT}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y)+\varepsilon H(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu)

(2.2)

admits a unique minimizer
\pi_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
as a consequence of the direct method in the calculus of variations and strict convexity of entropy. Moreover,
\pi_{\varepsilon}\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is the minimizer of problem (
2.2
) under assumptions that probability measures
\mu
and
\nu
have positive density functions if and only if

\pi_{\varepsilon}=a_{\varepsilon}(x)b_{\varepsilon}(y)\exp\left(-\frac{c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\mu\otimes\nu,

see
[
2
,
25
]
, where the functions
a_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
and
b_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
satisfy properties that
\ln a_{\varepsilon}\in L_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\ln b_{\varepsilon}\in L_{\nu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and solve the Schrödinger system of equations

\begin{cases}\displaystyle a_{\varepsilon}(x)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b_{\varepsilon}(y)\exp\left(-\frac{c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)=1\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\displaystyle b_{\varepsilon}(y)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a_{\varepsilon}(x)\exp\left(-\frac{c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)=1\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{cases}

There are many natural questions regarding the behaviour of regularized entropic optimal transport problem (
2.2
) such as the convergence of potentials, optimal plans, and optimal values with suitable quantitative convergence rates when
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
. This is an attracting topic for intensive research nowadays, see for instance
[
8
,
26
,
23
,
28
]
. In particular, we recall a recent interesting paper investigating the convergence of optimal values with a suitable convergence rate
[

which will be employed in Section

Theorem 2.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\Omega_{X},\Omega_{Y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be convex open sets and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be absolutely continuous probability measures compactly supported on
\Omega_{X}
,
\Omega_{Y}
, respectively, with bounded densities. Suppose cost function
c:\Omega_{X}\times\Omega_{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is of class

and infinitesimally twisted. Then

\mathrm{OT}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)=\mathrm{OT}(\mu,\nu)+\frac{n}{2}\varepsilon\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+O(\varepsilon),

where

\mathrm{OT}(\mu,\nu)=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}c(x,y)\,d\pi(x,y).

Preliminaries

We shall always denote by
c
a generic positive constant, possible varying line to line, having dependencies on parameters using brackets, that is, for example,

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open convex set. There exists a constant
c\equiv c(n,\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-z|}{r}\right)\,dx\geqslant c|B_{r}(z)|

holds whenever
z\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0<r<1
.

Proof.

First, we show that there exists a constant
c\equiv c(n,\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)|}{|B_{r}(z)|}\geqslant c

(3.1)

for every ball
B_{r}(z)
centered at
z\in\overline{\Omega}
and of radius
0<r<1
. Let
B_{2R}(z_{0})\subset\Omega
be one of the largest balls contained in
\Omega
. Let us consider a ball
B_{r}(z)
centered at
z\in\overline{\Omega}
and of radius
0<r<1
. Then we denote
Z_{R}
a convex hull of
B_{R}(z_{0})
and
z
. We consider two cases depending on the position of center
z
.

Case 1.
z\in\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{B_{R}}(z_{0})
.
Let
B_{r_{z}}(x_{z})
be the largest ball contained in
Z_{R}\cap B_{r}(z)
.
B_{r_{z}}(x_{z})
will be the image of
B_{R}(z_{0})
through an homotety centered at
z
with ratio
\frac{r}{R+|z-z_{0}|}
;in particular we will have
r_{z}=\frac{Rr}{R+|z-z_{0}|}
. In turn we get

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)|}{|B_{r}(z)|}\geqslant\frac{|B_{r_{z}}(x_{z})|}{|B_{r}(z)|}=\left(\frac{R}{R+|z-z_{0}|}\right)^{n}\geqslant\left(\frac{R}{R+\diam(\Omega)}\right)^{n}

Case 2.
z\in\overline{B_{R}}(z_{0})
.
For
0<r<R
, we have that

and

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)|}{|B_{r}(z)|}\geqslant\frac{|B_{2R}(z_{0})\cap B_{r}(z)|}{|B_{r}(z)|}\geqslant 1;

for

, we find

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)|}{|B_{r}(z)|}\geqslant\frac{|B_{2R}(z_{0})\cap B_{r}(z)|}{|B_{r}(z)|}\geqslant\frac{|B_{R}(0)|}{|B_{1}(0)|}\geqslant R^{n}.

Taking into account two cases we have discussed above, we arrive at the validity of (
3.1

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)}\exp\left(-\frac{|x-z|}{r}\right)\,dx\geqslant e^{-1}|\Omega\cap B_{r}(z)|\geqslant c|B_{r}(z)|

for some constant

.

3.1
Local
p
-detachment

The following lemma establishes a lower bound for
u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)-\langle x,y\rangle
, where
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
are Kantorovich potentials defined in (
1.10
). Specifically, it shows that the separation of
u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)
from the linear function
\langle x,y\rangle
can be controlled in terms of a power of the distance between
y
and the gradient
\nabla u_{0}(x)
.

Definition 3.2

(
p
-detachment)
.

Let
\Omega_{X},\Omega_{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f:\Omega_{X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
g:\Omega_{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
be two convex functions such that
f(x)+g(y)\geqslant\langle x,y\rangle
for every
(x,y)\in\Omega_{X}\times\Omega_{Y}
. We say that
(f,g)
satisfy a
p
-detachment on
\Omega_{X}\times\Omega_{Y}
if there exists
C>0
such that

f(x)+g(y)-\langle x,y\rangle\geqslant C\|y-\nabla f(x)\|^{p}\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega_{X}\times\Omega_{Y}

In the following fundamental lemma we show that if
\nabla f
is
\alpha
-Hölder then
(f,g)
locally satisfies a
p
-detachment property with
p=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
. This fact in the case
\alpha=1
is already known in the Optimal Transport literature, that is if
\nabla f
is Lispchitz then
(f,g)
has quadratic detachment (see
[
1
,
33
]
): we still include the proof to highlight the differences with respect to the Hölder case.

Notice that a converse result with similar assumptions has been noted in Remark 7.10 in
[
7
]
, where the
p
-uniform convexity of
f^{*}
is shown to imply
\alpha
-Hölder regularity of
f
(notice that
p
-detachment for
(f,f^{*})
implies that
f^{*}
is
p
-uniform convex).

A similar idea is employed in
[
11
,
12
,
20
]
but they need a more global result while we are more interested in the local version: moreover we state the coercivity (or strict convexity) as a detachment property, since the Young inequality residual is what appears in the duality gap.

Lemma 3.3
.

(Local quadratic and
p
-detachment)

Let
\Omega_{X},\Omega_{Y}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be two bounded convex open sets. Let
u:\Omega_{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex function such that
\nabla u(\Omega_{X})=\Omega_{Y}
. Let
v:\Omega_{Y}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that

u(x)+v(y)\geqslant\langle x,y\rangle\qquad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega_{X}\times\Omega_{Y}.

(3.2)

(i)

Suppose
u\in C^{1,1}(\Omega_{X})
; let
\lambda=Lip(\nabla u,\Omega_{X})
and
L=\frac{1}{2\lambda}
. Then we have

u(x)+v(y)-\langle x,y\rangle\geqslant L\|y-\nabla u(x)\|^{2}\qquad\forall x\in\Omega_{X},\forall y\in\Omega_{Y}.

(3.3)

(ii)

Suppose
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{loc}(\Omega_{X})
and let
p=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
. Then for every compact
K\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{X}
there exists
L\equiv L(\alpha,K)>0
such that

u(x)+v(y)-\langle x,y\rangle\geqslant L\|y-\nabla u(x)\|^{p}\qquad\forall x\in K,y\in\Omega_{Y}.

(3.4)

Proof.

Let us consider the convex conjugate of
u
restricted to
\Omega_{Y}
,
\tilde{v}:=(u)^{*}
. By definition we have
u(x)+\tilde{v}(y)\geqslant\langle x,y\rangle
and moreover

\tilde{v}(y)=\sup_{x\in\Omega_{X}}\left\{\langle x,y\rangle-u(x)\right\};

using (
3.2
) we obtain
\tilde{v}(y)\leqslant v(y)
on
\Omega_{Y}
. In particular it is sufficient to prove (
3.4
) and (
3.3
) for
u
and
\tilde{v}
.

(i)

Since
\nabla u
is
\lambda
-Lipschitz, it is differentiable almost everywhere and moreover we have
D^{2}u\leq\lambda Id
.
\nabla\tilde{v}
is the inverse function of
\nabla u
: differentiating the relation
\nabla\tilde{v}(\nabla u(x))=x
we get
D^{2}\tilde{v}(\nabla u(x))\cdot D^{2}u=Id
. Since this is true for every
x\in\Omega_{X}
and
\nabla u(\Omega_{X})=\Omega_{Y}
we obtain
D^{2}\tilde{v}(y)\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}Id
for almost every
y\in\Omega_{Y}
. Thanks to the fact that
\Omega_{Y}
is convex we have that
\tilde{v}
is a
\frac{1}{\lambda}
-strongly convex function and so

\tilde{v}(y)\geqslant\tilde{v}(\bar{y})+\langle\nabla\tilde{v}(\bar{(}y)),y-\bar{y}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|y-\bar{y}\|^{2}.

Letting
\bar{y}:=\nabla u(x)
, we have
\nabla\tilde{v}(\bar{y})=x
and using the equality in the Legendre duality
\tilde{v}(\bar{y})+u(x)=\langle\bar{y},x\rangle
, we find that (
3.3
) holds true for
v=\tilde{v}
.

(ii)

Let us consider a compact set
K_{1}
such that
K\subset\joinrel\subset K_{1}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{X}
and denote
\lambda=[\nabla u]_{\alpha;K_{1}}
which is finite thanks to the local Hölder regularity of
u
. Let
x_{0}\in K
such that
u
is differentiable at
x_{0}
. Thanks to the convexity of
u
and the Hölder condition on its gradient, for every
x\in K_{1}

u(x)\leqslant u(x_{0})+\langle\nabla u(x),x-x_{0}\rangle\leqslant u(x_{0})+\langle\nabla u(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle+\lambda\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}.

Using this estimate in the definition of
\tilde{v}
we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\Omega_{X}}\{\langle x,y\rangle-u(x)\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\sup_{x\in K_{1}}\{\langle x,y\rangle-u(x)\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\sup_{x\in K_{1}}\{\langle x,y\rangle-u(x_{0})-\langle\nabla u(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle-\lambda\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in K_{1}}\{\langle x_{0},y\rangle-u(x_{0})+\langle y-\nabla u(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle-\lambda\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=\langle x_{0},y\rangle-u(x_{0})+\sup_{x\in K_{1}}\{\langle y-\nabla u(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle-\lambda\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\langle x_{0},y\rangle-u(x_{0})+C(\lambda,\dist(x_{0},\partial K_{1}))\|y-\nabla u(x_{0})\|^{p}.

We can conclude thanks to the fact that
\dist(x_{0},\partial K_{1})
is bounded from below for
x_{0}\in K
since
K\subset\joinrel\subset K_{1}
. In fact, we simply select

x=x_{0}+r\|y-\nabla u(x_{0})\|^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}(y-\nabla u(x_{0}))

in the previous display for positive constant
r
given by

\displaystyle r=\min\left\{1/2\lambda^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}},1/2\dist(K,\partial K_{1})(\diam(\Omega_{Y}))^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

In the optimization literature it is folklore that Hölder continuity of the gradient of a convex function defined on the whole space is actually equivalent to a global detachment property for the convex conjugate on the whole space. We provide a self-contaited proof of this fact.

(i)
f\in C^{1,\alpha}
implies
(f,f^{*})
has
\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
-detachment
. Suppose
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\nabla f
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous with constant
C=\frac{\lambda^{1+\alpha}}{1+\alpha}
and denote
p=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
. Then

\displaystyle f^{*}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\langle x,y\rangle-f(x)\}

\displaystyle\geqslant\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\langle x,y\rangle-f(x_{0})-\langle\nabla f(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle-C\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in K_{1}}\{\langle x_{0},y\rangle-f(x_{0})+\langle y-\nabla f(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle-C\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=\langle x_{0},y\rangle-u(x_{0})+\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\{\langle y-\nabla f(x_{0}),x-x_{0}\rangle-\frac{\lambda^{1+\alpha}}{1+\alpha}\|x-x_{0}\|^{1+\alpha}\}

\displaystyle=\langle x_{0},y\rangle-f(x_{0})+\frac{1}{p\lambda^{p}}\|y-\nabla f(x_{0})\|^{p},

(ii)
(f,f^{*})
has
\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
-detachment implies
f\in C^{1,\alpha}
.
Let
p=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
; suppose that

f^{*}(y)+f(x)-\langle x,y\rangle\geqslant\frac{1}{p\lambda^{p}}\|y-q\|^{p}\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\forall q\in\partial f(x).

Considering
y=q^{\prime}\in\partial f(x^{\prime})
, using the Young identity
f^{*}(q^{\prime})+f(x^{\prime})=\langle q^{\prime},x^{\prime}\rangle
and the subdifferential inequality
f(x^{\prime})\geqslant f(x)+\langle p,x^{\prime}-x\rangle
we get

\langle x-x^{\prime},q^{\prime}-q\rangle\geqslant\frac{1}{p\lambda^{p}}\|q^{\prime}-q\|^{p}.

Notice that this inequality is precisely
p
-convexity for
\nabla f^{*}
. By Cauchy-Schwarz now we conclude
\|q^{\prime}-q\|\leqslant p^{\alpha}\lambda^{1+\alpha}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{\alpha}
, so
f\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with the constant of Hölder continuity being
\bar{C}=p^{\alpha}\lambda^{1+\alpha}
.

Even though this global analysis holds true (the constants can be also improved, at least in the Lipschitz case), a complete
local
theory for this equivalence seems to lack, even in the Lipschitz case. It has been conjectured that given
u:\Omega_{X}\to\mathbb{R}
a convex function then
\nabla u
is
L
-Lipschitz if and only if

u(x)+v(y)-\langle x,y\rangle\geqslant\frac{1}{2L}\|y-\nabla u(x)\|^{2},

where
v
is the convex conjugate of
u
. However, the Lipschitz assumption alone on
\nabla u
is not enough to guarantee this detachment: see a counterexample in
[
13
]
. This is not in contradiction with Lemma
3.3
as we assume also
\nabla u(\Omega_{X})
to be convex while this is not true in the counter-example.

It would be interesting to know whether a uniform Hölder condition on
\nabla u
and convexity of
\nabla u(\Omega_{X})
could imply a
p
-detachment with a uniform constant.

3.2
Relations and representation formulas

We now want to exploit the characterization of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{\varepsilon}
given in (
1
) to deduce representation formulas for their gradient and Hessian. In fact taking the derivative in
x
in the first equation of (
1
) and in
y
in the second equation of (
1
), respectively, we get

\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}y\cdot\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y).

(3.5)

and

\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y)=\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}y\cdot\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x).

(3.6)

Taking another derivative we get

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}y\otimes(y-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x))\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}(y-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x))\otimes(y-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x))\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y).

(3.7)

and

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}v_{\varepsilon}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}x\otimes(x-\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}(x-\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))\otimes(x-\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y))\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-v_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x).

(3.8)

Another ingredient which will be useful in the next section is the relation between the non-optimality of a plan and the detachment. In particular given any plan
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}\|x-y\|^{2}\,d\pi-W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}-\varphi_{0}(x)-\psi_{0}(y)\,\right)d\pi

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)-\langle x,y\rangle)\,d\pi,

(3.9)

where we have used (
1.9
)-(
1.10
).

4
Local estimates of potentials

Throughout this section whatever we prove below will be considered under the assumptions (
1.2
)-(
1.3
).
First we discuss the local convergence of the gradient of Schrödinger potentials
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
given in (
1
) to Kantorovich potential
u_{0}
in (
1.10
) with a proper rate of convergence in
L^{p}_{\mu}(\Omega_{1})
for every
0<p<\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Schrödinger potentials satisfying (
1
) and strictly convex functions
u_{0}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
v_{0}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Kantorovich potentials given in (
1.10
). Then there exists a constant
p_{0}\equiv p_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
such for for every
p\geq p_{0}
and every compact subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\Subset\Omega_{1}
there is a universal constant
c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{1})
such that

\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\leqslant c\varepsilon\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+O(\varepsilon)

(4.1)

Proof.

Note that we can apply Lemma
3.3
to
u_{0}
since
u_{0}\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\loc}(\Omega_{1})
for an exponent
\alpha\equiv\alpha(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
by Theorem
2.1
. In turn, for
p=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
, using the duality of optimal transport and applying Lemma
3.3
to
u_{0}
, we see

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{p}\chi_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)-y|^{p}\chi_{\{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\times\Omega_{2}\}}(x,y)\,d\pi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{L}\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}\left(u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)-\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right)\chi_{\{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\times\Omega_{2}\}}(x,y)\,d\pi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{L}\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}\left(u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)-\left\langle x,y\right\rangle\right)\,d\pi_{\varepsilon}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{L}\left[W_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mu,\nu)-W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\right]\leqslant c\varepsilon\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+O(\varepsilon)

for some constant
c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda,p,\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{1})
, where in the last display we have used the relation (
3.9
) and Theorem
2.2
. Therefore, we find the constant
p_{0}=\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}
and the inequality (
4.1
) is still valid for any
p\geqslant p_{0}
by selecting
\alpha
close enough to
0
.
∎

In the following lemmas, we obtain local
L^{\infty}
estimates for the difference of Schrödinger and Kantorovich potentials
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
with a suitable convergence rate of power of
\varepsilon
and for their corresponding first and second derivatives as well.

Lemma 4.2

(
L^{\infty}
-estimate)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Schrödinger potentials satisfying (
1
) and strictly convex functions
u_{0}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
v_{0}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Kantorovich potentials given in (
1.10
). Then there exists
a\equiv a(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
such that for every compact convex subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
, the following estimate

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)-(u_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{0}(y))|\leqslant A_{1}\varepsilon^{a}

(4.2)

holds for a constant
A_{1}
depending only on
n,\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{1}
, whenever
x,y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
. Moreover, for any compact convex subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
, if the function
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
has a zero on
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}\leqslant A_{1}\varepsilon^{a},

(4.3)

and

\displaystyle v_{0}(y)-v_{\epsilon}(y)\leqslant A_{2}\varepsilon^{a}\quad\text{for all}\quad y\in\nabla u_{0}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})

(4.4)

for some constant
A_{2}\equiv A_{2}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{1})
.

Proof.

Applying the triangle inequatity and Sobolev-Poincaré inequality, see for instance
[
17
, Theorem 7.10]
, for any points
x,y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime},
we have

\displaystyle|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)-(u_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{0}(y))|

\displaystyle\leqslant|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u_{0}(x)-(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\quad+|u_{\varepsilon}(y)-u_{0}(y)-(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\lVert u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}-(u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0})_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n,p)|\Omega_{1}^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n,p)\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\Omega_{1}^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{p}\,d\mu(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c(n,p)\lambda^{-\frac{1}{p}}|\Omega_{1}|^{\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}\left[\frac{Ln}{2}\varepsilon\log\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)+O(\varepsilon)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}},

(4.5)

where we have used the estimate (
1.3
) and Lemma
4.1
for
p>n
. Note that such choice of
p
is possible by choosing
\alpha
close enough to zero. Since Schrödinger potentials
(\varphi_{\varepsilon},\psi_{\varepsilon})
are unique up to adding and subtracting a constant, the pair
(u_{\varepsilon}-d,v_{\varepsilon}+d)
still satisfies the same system of equations (
1
). Therefore, we can replace
u_{\varepsilon}
with
u_{\varepsilon}-d
for a constant
d
such that the function
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
has a zero on
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
. This observation together with the estimate (
4
) follows the estimates (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) if
\varepsilon\ll 1
. It is clear that the estimates (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) hold true when parameter
\varepsilon
bounded below from zero.

Now we focus on showing the estimate (
4.4
). Using the second equation of (
1
) and the local estimate (
4.3
), for every
y\in\Omega_{2}^{\prime}:=\nabla u_{0}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(y)-v_{0}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{0}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\exp\left(\frac{u_{0}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\lambda\exp\left(-A_{1}\varepsilon^{a-1}\right)\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}}\exp\left(\frac{-L|x-\nabla v_{0}(y)|}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x),

(4.6)

where we have also used the property that the function
F(x):=u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)-\langle x,y\rangle
is
L
-Lipschitz with
L=\diam(\Omega_{2})
(since
|\nabla F(x)|=|\nabla u_{0}(x)-y|\leqslant\diam(\Omega_{2})
) and moreover
F(\bar{x})=0
for
\bar{x}=\nabla v_{0}(y)
and so

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)+v_{0}(y)-\left\langle x,y\right\rangle=F(x)-F(\bar{x})\leqslant L|x-\bar{x}|=L|x-\nabla v_{0}(y)|\quad\forall x\in\Omega_{1},y\in\Omega_{2}.

Recalling the basic facts
[
1
,
4
]
that

\displaystyle\nabla v_{0}(\nabla u_{0}(x))=x\quad\text{and}\quad\nabla u_{0}(\nabla v_{0}(y))=y,

we have
\nabla v_{0}(y)\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and we continue to estimate (
4
) as

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(y)-v_{0}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\lambda\exp\left(-A_{1}\varepsilon^{a-1}\right)\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\cap B_{\varepsilon/L}(\nabla v_{0}(y))}\exp\left(\frac{-L|x-\nabla v_{0}(y)|}{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{0}\varepsilon^{n}\exp(-A_{1}\varepsilon^{a-1})

(4.7)

for a constant
c_{0}\equiv c_{0}(\lambda,L,\Omega_{1}^{\prime})
, where we have applied Proposition
3.1
thanks to the convexity assumption of
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
. On other hand, recalling
a\in(0,1)
and an elementary inequality that there exists a constant
A_{1}^{\prime}\equiv A_{1}^{\prime}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,L,\Omega_{1}^{\prime})
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{-n}}{c_{0}}\leqslant\exp\left(A_{1}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{a-1}\right)\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1).

Therefore, this estimate together with the estimate (
4
) implies the validity of (
4.4
) when
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. There is nothing to show for (
4.4
) when
\varepsilon\geqslant 1
.
∎

The next result establishes a uniform convergence estimate for the gradients of the Schrödinger potentials in terms of the regularization parameter
\varepsilon
.

Lemma 4.3

(Gradient estimate)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Schrödinger potentials satisfying (
1
) and strictly convex functions
u_{0}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
v_{0}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Kantorovich potentials given in (
1.10
). Then there exists a constant
b\equiv b(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>0
such that for every convex compact subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
the following estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}\leqslant B\varepsilon^{b}

(4.8)

holds for a constant
B
depending only on
n,\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{1}
.

Proof.

Let us fix a convex compact open subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
. Then we are able to assume that the function
u_{\varepsilon}
is normalized on
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
in the sense that the function
u_{\varepsilon}-u_{0}
has a zero on
\Omega_{1}
since the pair
(u_{\varepsilon}-d,v_{\varepsilon}+d)
are Schrödinger potentials satisfying (
1
) for any constant
d
. In fact this normalization does not affect the gradients
Du_{\varepsilon}
and
Dv_{\varepsilon}
. However, such normalization enables us to apply the estimates (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) of Lemma
4.2
. The representation formula (
3.6
) and straightforward computations imply

\displaystyle|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y)-\nabla v_{0}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\limits_{\Omega_{1}}|x-\nabla v_{0}(y)|\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant\diam(\Omega_{1})\quad\forall y\in\Omega_{2}.

Therefore, using the mean value theorem and the convexity of
\Omega_{2}
, for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\Omega_{2}
, we find

\displaystyle v_{0}(y_{1})-v_{\varepsilon}(y_{1})-(v_{0}(y_{2})-v_{\varepsilon}(y_{2}))

\displaystyle\leqslant|\nabla v_{0}(y_{t})-\nabla v_{\varepsilon}(y_{t})||y_{1}-y_{2}|

\displaystyle\leqslant\diam(\Omega_{1})|y_{1}-y_{2}|,

(4.9)

where
y_{t}=ty_{1}+(1-t)y_{2}\in\Omega_{2}
is a point for some
t\in[0,1]
. Then we denote
\Omega_{2}^{\prime}:=\nabla u_{0}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})
here and in the rest of the proof.
It is clear
[
15
, Lemma A.22]
that
\Omega_{2}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{2}
(
\nabla u_{0}(\Omega_{1})
is compact and
\nabla u_{0}(\Omega_{1})=\Omega_{2}
), and we also fix a compact open subset
\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{2}
such that
\Omega_{2}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{2}
and

\displaystyle-\frac{L}{2}\left(\dist(\Omega_{2}^{\prime},\partial\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime})+\dist(y,\partial\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime})\right)^{p}+2\diam(\Omega_{1})\dist(y,\partial\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime})\leqslant 0\quad\forall y\in\Omega_{2}\setminus\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime},

in which the constant
L
and
p
are determined through the application of Lemma
3.3
(ii) for
(u_{0},v_{0})
in the set
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
. Note that the selection of set
\Omega_{2}^{\prime\prime}
is possible and independent of the parameter
\varepsilon
.

Now we focus on showing the estimate (
4.8
). Using the representation formula (
3.5
), for every
x\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}|y-\nabla u_{0}(x)|\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)

While the Kantorovich potentials are known to be smooth and strictly convex under suitable regularity conditions on the data, the Schrödinger potentials inherit their regularity from the entropic regularization and may exhibit increasingly singular features as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. The following lemma provides a quantitative upper bound on the Hessian of
u_{\varepsilon}
, showing that the potentials can became increasingly steep as the regularization vanishes, but in a controlled way. Notice that thanks to the representation formula (
3.7
) as long as
\Omega_{Y}
is bounded we have
\|\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
: we improve this estimate, crucially allowing for an exponent
m<1
.

Lemma 4.4

(Hessian estimate)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
v_{\varepsilon}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Schrödinger potentials satisfying (
1
) and strictly convex functions
u_{0}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
v_{0}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Kantorovich potentials given in (
1.10
). Then there exist a constant
m\equiv m(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
such that for every convex compact subset
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
, the following estimates

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}\leqslant\frac{M}{\varepsilon^{m}}

(4.15)

hold for a constant
M
depending only on
n,\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and
\Omega_{1}
.

Proof.

The representation formula (
3.7
) reads as

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}(y-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x))\otimes(y-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x))\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)\quad\forall x\in\Omega_{1}.

Therefore, using some elementary inequalities in the last display, for every
x\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
, we see

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}\left(|y-\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}+|\nabla u_{0}(x)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{1-2b}}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{\Omega_{2}}|y-\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}\exp\left(\frac{\left\langle x,y\right\rangle-u_{\varepsilon}(x)-v_{\varepsilon}(y)}{\varepsilon}\right)\,d\nu(y)

for a constant

5
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Finally, we provide the proof for the main result of this paper, namely Theorem
1.1
. Let
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}\subset\joinrel\subset\Omega_{1}
be a fixed convex compact subset and let
x,y\in\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
be arbitrary points. Let us consider the following two cases.

Case 1:
|x-y|>\varepsilon
.
By the triangle inequality, Lemma
4.3

and Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(x)|+|\nabla u_{0}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(y)|+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y)-\nabla u_{0}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left\lVert\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\nabla u_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}+|\nabla u_{0}(x)-\nabla u_{0}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2B\varepsilon^{b}+c|x-y|^{\alpha}\leqslant c_{*}|x-y|^{\min\{b,\alpha\}}

for a constant
c_{*}\equiv c_{*}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{1})
.

Case 2:
|x-y|\leqslant\varepsilon
.
Using the mean value theorem, the convexity of
\Omega_{1}^{\prime}
and Lemma
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left\lVert\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{1}^{\prime})}|x-y|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c_{*}}{\varepsilon^{m}}|x-y|

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{*}|x-y|^{1-m}

for a constant
c_{*}\equiv c_{*}(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\Omega_{1}^{\prime},\Omega_{1})
, thereby concluding the proof.

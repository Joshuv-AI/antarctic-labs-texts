New inequalities related to sums of L p L^{p} functions in connection with Carbery’s problems
A. Aghajani, J. Kinnunen

Abstract
. Carbery (2006) proposed novel estimates for the L p L^{p} norm of a sum of two nonnegative measurable functions. Subsequently, Carlen, Frank, Ivanisvili and Lieb (2018) provided stronger bounds, which Ivanisvili and Mooney (2020) further refined to achieve estimates that are, in a certain sense, optimal. Continuing this line of research, the present work establishes new upper and lower bounds for the range p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ) p\in(1,\infty) . Carbery also asked under what conditions on a sequence ( f j ) (f_{j}) of nonnegative measurable functions the inequality ∑ ‖ f j ‖ p p < ∞ \sum\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty implies that ∑ f j ∈ L p \sum f_{j}\in L^{p} . Ivanisvili and Mooney (2020) resolved this question for p ∈ [ 1 , 2 ] p\in[1,2] , and the present work proposes an answer for p ∈ [ 2 , ∞ ) p\in[2,\infty) .

1.
Introduction

A standard way to estimate the
L^{p}
norm of a sum of two functions in
L^{p}
on any measure space is to apply Minkowski’s inequality

\|f+g\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p},\quad p\in[1,\infty),

or the bound

(1.1)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\bigl(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\bigr),\quad p\in[1,\infty).

Equality occurs in (
1.1
) if and only if
f=g
.
In what follows we consider functions
f,g\in L^{p}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, that are measurable and nonnegative.
We further assume that,
\|f\|_{p}\neq 0
and
\|g\|_{p}\neq 0
, to simplify the presentation and avoid trivial cases.
Motivated by the inequality

\|f+g\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{2}^{2}+2\|fg\|_{1}+\|g\|_{2}^{2},

Carbery
[
2
]
proposed several refinements of (
1.1
) including

(1.2)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}\leq\left(1+\frac{\|fg\|_{p/2}}{\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p}}\right)^{p-1}\bigl(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\bigr),\quad p\in[2,\infty).

Equality occurs in (
1.2
) both if
f=g
and if
fg=0
, i.e.,
f
and
g
have disjoint supports.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\frac{\|fg\|_{p/2}}{\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p}}\leq 1,\quad p\in[2,\infty),

from which it follows that (
1.2
) improves on the trivial bound in (
1.1
).

Carlen, Frank, Ivanisvili and Lieb
[
3
]
proved that

(1.3)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}\leq\bigl(1+\Gamma_{p}^{2/p})^{p-1}(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\bigr),\quad p\in(0,1]\cup[2,\infty)

where

\Gamma_{p}=\Gamma_{p}(f,g)=\frac{2\|fg\|^{p/2}_{p/2}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}.

A reverse inequality holds true if
p\in(-\infty,0)\cup(1,2)
, with the additional assumption that

f
and
g
are positive almost everywhere.
Here

\|f\|_{p}=\biggl(\int|f|^{p}\biggr)^{1/p},\quad p\neq 0.

Not only (
1.3
) is stronger than (
1.2
), it is also optimal, in the sense that
\Gamma_{p}^{2/p}
cannot be
replaced by
\Gamma_{p}^{r}
for any
r<2/p
. Later, Carlen, Frank and Lieb
[
4
]
successfully extended inequality (
1.3
) for sums of a finite number of functions in
L^{p}
, see Theorem 1.1 in
[
4
]
.

Ivanisvili and Mooney
[
7
]
refined (
1.3
) by showing that,
for any nonnegative measurable functions
f
and
g
, we have

(1.4)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}\leq\Biggl(\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{p}^{2}}}{2}\right)^{1/p}+\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-\Gamma_{p}^{2}}}{2}\right)^{1/p}\Biggr)^{p}\bigl(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\bigr),

where
p\in(0,1]\cup[2,\infty)
.
The inequality reverses if
p\in(-\infty,0)\cup[1,2]
. Equality holds if

(fg)^{p/2}=\alpha(f^{p}+g^{p})

for some constant
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
The function of
\Gamma_{p}
on the right-hand side of (
1.4
) is shown to be optimal in
[
7
]
in the sense that for all
\gamma\in[0,1]
,
there exist functions
f
and
g
for which
\Gamma_{p}(f,g)=\gamma
, and such that equality holds in (
1.4
).

The first main result of this paper establishes new upper and lower bounds for
\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
.
For nonnegative functions
f,g\in L^{p}
, with
\|f\|_{p}\neq 0
and
\|g\|_{p}\neq 0
, let

\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)=1+\frac{\Bigl(1+\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\Bigr)^{p}-1-\Bigl(\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\Bigr)^{p}}{1+\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}\Bigr)^{p}}

and

\underline{J}_{p}(f,g)=1+\frac{\Big(1+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\Big)^{p}-1-\Bigl(\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigr)^{p}}{1+\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}\Bigr)^{p}},

where
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. For any nonnegative functions
f,g\in L^{p}
, with
\|f\|_{p}\neq 0
and
\|g\|_{p}\neq 0
, we have

(1.5)

\max\{\underline{J}_{p}(f,g),\underline{J}_{p}(g,f)\}\leq\frac{\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\leq\min\{\overline{J}_{p}(f,g),\overline{J}_{p}(g,f)\}.

Equalities hold if, for some
\alpha,\beta>0
, we have
\alpha f=\beta g
almost everywhere either on the set
\{g\neq 0\}
or on
\{f\neq 0\}
, or if
f
and
g
have disjoint supports up to a set of measure zero.
The reverse inequalities hold for
p\in(1,2]
, with
\max
and
\min
interchanged, assuming
f
and
g
are strictly positive almost everywhere in the upper bound.
In this case, equalities hold if
\alpha f=\beta g
almost everywhere for some
\alpha,\beta>0
, or if
f
and
g
have disjoint supports up to a set of measure zero.

We note that (
1.5
) and (
1.4
) are not generally comparable.
However, as we will see, for certain functions
f
and
g
, the presented bounds offer an improvement over (
1.4
).

In
[
2
]
, Carbery also asked under what conditions on a sequence
(f_{j})
of functions in
L^{p}
the inequality
\sum\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty
would imply that
\sum f_{j}\in L^{p}
. When attempting to extend inequality (
1.1
) from two functions to
n
functions
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}
, the constant
2^{p-1}
is replaced by
n^{p-1}
, which blows up with
n
.
Ivanisvili and Mooney
[
7
]
provided an answer to this question by showing that for a sequence
(f_{j})
of nonnegative measurable functions

(1.6)

\Bigl\|\sum_{j}f_{j}\Bigr\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+(2^{p}-2)\sum_{i<j}\|f_{i}f_{j}\|_{p/2}^{p/2},\quad p\in[1,2],

and the inequality reverses if
p\in(0,1]\cup[2,\infty)
.
In particular, if
p\in[1,2]
, then
\sum_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty
and
\sum_{i<j}\|f_{j}f_{j}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}<\infty
impy that
\sum_{j}f_{j}\in L^{p}
.
The second main results of the present work proposes an answer to this question for all
p\in[2,\infty)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(f_{j})
be a sequence of nonnegative functions
f_{j}\in L^{p}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

If
p\in[2,\infty)
, then

(1.7)

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\Bigr\|_{p}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+C_{p}\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\Bigr\|_{p}^{p-2}\Bigl\|\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}f_{i}f_{j}\Bigr\|_{p/2},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

where

(1.8)

C_{p}=\max\left\{p,\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}\right\}.

(ii)

If
p\in[1,2]
, then

(1.9)

\Bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\Bigr\|_{p}^{p}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}\frac{\bigl\|\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}f_{i}f_{j}\bigr\|_{p/2}}{\bigl\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\bigr\|_{p}^{2-p}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

provided that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\neq 0
almost eveerywhere.

Combining Theorem
1.2
with (
1.6
) we find that the following are necessary and sufficient conditions for the sum
\sum_{j}f_{j}
to belong to
L^{p}
.

(i)

Case

p\in[1,2]
:

Sufficient condition
:
\sum_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty
and
\sum_{i<j}\|f_{i}f_{j}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}<\infty
.

Necessary condition
:
\sum_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty
and
\|\sum_{i<j}f_{i}f_{j}\|_{p/2}<\infty
.

(ii)

Case

p\in[2,\infty)
:

Sufficient condition
:
\sum_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty
and
\|\sum_{i<j}f_{i}f_{j}\|_{p/2}<\infty
.

Necessary condition
:
\sum_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{p}^{p}<\infty
and
\sum_{i<j}\|f_{i}f_{j}\|_{p/2}^{p/2}<\infty
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We start with the following simple lemma which will be useful in the proof of our main estimates for
\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p}
and
g\in L^{q}
,
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
, be nonnegative functions. Then

\|fg\|_{1}\leq\|g\|_{q}\bigl(\|f+g^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p}-\|g^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p}\bigr)\leq\|g\|_{q}\|f\|_{p},\quad p\in(1,\infty).

The reverse inequalities hold if
p\in(0,1)
, provided that
g
is strictly positive almost everywhere.

Proof.

We only prove the case
p\in(1,\infty)
, since the case
p\in(0,1)
is similar by using the corresponding versions of Hölder’s and Minkowski’s inequalities.
By Hölder’s inequality, we have

\int fg+\int g^{q}=\int g(f+g^{q-1})\leq\biggl(\int g^{q}\biggr)^{1/q}\biggl(\int(f+g^{\frac{1}{p-1}})^{p}\biggr)^{1/p},

from which it follows that

\|fg\|_{1}\leq\|g\|_{q}\bigl(\|f+g^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p}-\|g^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p}\bigr).

We also note that the right-hand side is smaller or equal to
\|g\|_{q}\|f\|_{p}
by Minkowski’s inequality.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let
f\in L^{p}
and
g\in L^{p}
,
p>1
, be nonnegative functions.
Then
g^{p-1}\in L^{q}
,
q=\frac{q}{q-1}
, and by replacing
g
with
g^{p-1}
in (
2.1
), we obtain

\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}\leq\|g\|_{p}^{p-1}\bigl(\|f+g\|_{p}-\|g\|_{p}\bigr).

As a consequence, we have the following reverse version of Minkowski’s inequality

\|g\|_{p}+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p-1}}\leq\|f+g\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p}.

Again the reverse inequalities hold if
p\in(0,1)
, assuming that
g
is strictly positive almost everywhere.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We only prove the case
p\in[2,\infty)
, since the argument for
p\in(1,2]
is similar with inequalities to the reverse direction.
For
t\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int(f+tg)^{p}

\displaystyle=p\int g(f+tg)^{p-1}=p\int(fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+tg^{q})^{p-1}

\displaystyle=p\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+tg^{q}\|_{p-1}^{p-1}.

By Lemma
2.1
, with
p
replaced with
p-1>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int(f+tg)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq p\biggl(t\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}+\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}g^{q(p-2)}\|_{1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}^{p-2}}\biggr)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=p\biggl(t\|g\|^{q}_{p}+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{q(p-2)}}\biggr)^{p-1}=\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\Bigl(t\|g\|^{q}_{p}+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{q(p-2)}}\Bigr)^{p}}{\|g\|_{p}^{q}}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\bigl(t\|g\|_{p}^{p}+\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}\bigr)^{p}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p(p-1)}}.

It follows that
F^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
, where

F(t)=\int(f+tg)^{p}-\frac{\bigl(t\|g\|_{p}^{p}+\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}\bigr)^{p}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p(p-1)}}.

Therefore,
F(1)\geq F(0)
and, after a simplification, we arrive at

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\bigl(\|g\|^{p}_{p}+\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}\bigr)^{p}-\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}^{p}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p(p-1)}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\biggl(\biggl(1+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\biggr)^{p}-\biggl(\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\biggr)^{p}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\biggl(\biggl(1+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\biggr)^{p}-1-\biggl(\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\biggr)^{p}\biggr)

or equivalently,

\frac{\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\geq 1+\frac{\Bigl(1+\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigr)^{p}-1-\Bigl(\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigr)^{p}}{1+\bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}\bigr)^{p}}=\underline{J}_{p}(f,g).

The corresponding inequality also holds by interchanging the roles of
f
and
g
.

To prove the inequality involving
\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)
, for
t\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int(f+tg)^{p}

\displaystyle=p\int g(f+tg)^{p-1}=p\int(fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+tg^{q})^{p-1}

\displaystyle=p\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+tg^{q}\|_{p-1}^{p-1}.

By Minkowski’s inequality we have

(2.1)

\begin{split}p\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}+tg^{q}\|_{p-1}^{p-1}&\leq p\bigl(\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}+t\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}\bigr)^{p-1}\\
&=\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\bigl(\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}+t\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}\bigr)^{p}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}.\end{split}

Hence
G^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
, where

G(t)=\|f+tg\|_{p}^{p}-\frac{\bigl(\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}+t\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}\bigr)^{p}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}.

Thus
G(1)\leq G(0)
and

\displaystyle\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\frac{\bigl(\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}+\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}\bigr)^{p}-\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}^{p}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\biggl(\biggl(1+\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\biggr)^{p}-\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}^{p}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}^{p}}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\biggl(\biggl(1+\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\biggr)^{p}-1-\biggl(\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\biggr)^{p}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\Biggl(1+\frac{\Bigl(1+\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\Bigr)^{p}-1-\Bigl(\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\Bigr)^{p}}{1+\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}\Bigr)^{p}}\Biggr)(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p})

\displaystyle=\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)\bigl(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}\bigr).

The corresponding inequality also holds by interchanging the roles of
f
and
g
.
For the equality part, see Remark
2.4
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
,
By Hölder’s inequality we have

(2.2)

\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}\leq\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}.

Let

T=\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}

and let
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
be such that

\lambda T=\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}.

The function
H(t)=(1+t)^{p}-1-t^{p}
is convex on
[0,\infty)
, thus

H(\lambda T)\leq(1-\lambda)H(0)+\lambda H(T)=\lambda H(T).

By (
1.5
) we have

\begin{split}\frac{\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}&\leq\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)=1+\frac{H(\lambda T)}{1+T^{p}}\leq 1+\frac{\lambda H(T)}{1+T^{p}}\\
&=1+\lambda\frac{(1+T)^{p}-1-T^{p}}{1+T^{p}}=(1-\lambda)+\lambda\frac{(1+T)^{p}}{1+T^{p}}\\
&=(1-\lambda)+\lambda\frac{(\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p})^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}.\end{split}

It follows that

(2.3)

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}\leq\lambda(\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p})^{p}+(1-\lambda)(\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}).

In particular,

\frac{\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\leq(1-\lambda)+\lambda\frac{(\|f\|_{p}+\|g\|_{p})^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\leq(1-\lambda)+2^{p-1}\lambda\leq 2^{p-1}.

Note also that we can simplify the left-hand side inequality in (
1.5
), using the inequality

(2.4)

(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+b^{p}+(2^{p}-2)(ab)^{p/2},\quad p\in[2,\infty),

for nonnegative numbers

a=1\quad\text{and}\quad b=\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}},

see
[
7
]
.
By (
2.4
) we have

(2.5)

\frac{\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}}{\|f\|_{p}^{p}+\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\geq\underline{J}_{p}(f,g)\geq 1+\frac{(2^{p}-2)\Bigl(\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\Bigr)^{p/2}}{1+\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}\Bigr)^{p}},\quad p\in[2,\infty).

Again the reverse inequalities hold for
p\in[1,2]
.

In particular, for nonnegative
f,g\in L^{p}
with
\|f\|_{p}=1
and
\|g\|_{p}=1
, from (
2.5
) we have

\|f+g\|_{p}^{p}\geq 2+(2^{p}-2)\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}^{p/2},\quad p\in[2,\infty),

or equivalently

1-\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq(1-2^{1-p})\bigl(1-\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}^{p/2}\bigr),\quad p\in[2,\infty),

and the inequality reverses if
p\in[1,2]
.

Moreover, by using the inequality above and Hanner’s inequality

\left\|\frac{f+g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{p}+\left\|\frac{f-g}{2}\right\|_{p}^{p}\leq 1,\quad p\in[2,\infty),

see
[
6
,
8
,
3
]
(also a consequence Clarkson’s inequalities
[
5
]
),
we have

\|f-g\|_{p}^{p}\leq(2^{p}-2)\bigl(1-\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}^{p/2}\bigr),\quad p\in[2,\infty).

Again the reverse inequality holds for
p\in(1,2]
.

Remark 2.4
.

By applying the explicit upper and lower bounds in (
1.5
), we obtain the error estimate

(2.6)

\left|\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)-\underline{J}_{p}(f,g)\right|\leq p2^{p-1}\biggl|\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}-\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\biggr|,\quad p\in(1,\infty),

whenever
\|f\|_{p}\leq\|g\|_{p}
. If
\|f\|_{p}\geq\|g\|_{p}
, the roles of
f
and
g
are interchanged.

To prove (
2.6
), let
H(t)=(1+t)^{p}-t^{p}
,
t\geq 0
,

T_{1}=\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{2}=\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}.

Then we have

\left|\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)-\underline{J}_{p}(f,g)\right|=\frac{|H(T_{2})-H(T_{1})|}{1+\Bigl(\frac{\|f\|_{p}}{\|g\|_{p}}\Bigr)^{p}}\leq|H(T_{2})-H(T_{1})|.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. Since
\frac{p-2}{p-1}+\frac{1}{p-1}=1
, Hölder’s inequality gives

(2.7)

\int fg^{p-1}=\int(fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}})g^{\frac{p(p-2)}{p-1}}\leq\biggl(\int f^{p-1}g\biggr)^{1/(p-1)}\biggl(\int g^{p}\biggr)^{1-1/(p-1)},

or equvalently

(2.8)

T_{1}=\frac{\|fg^{p-1}\|_{1}}{\|g\|_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{\|fg^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|_{p-1}}{\|g^{q}\|_{p-1}}=T_{2},

By (
2.8
), (
2.2
) and the assumption
\|f\|_{p}\leq\|g\|_{p}
we get
0\leq T_{1}\leq T_{2}\leq 1
.
We may assume that
0\leq T_{1}<T_{2}\leq 1
, since
\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)=\underline{J}_{p}(f,g)
if
T_{1}=T_{2}
.
By the mean value theorem, there exists
T_{0}\in(T_{1},T_{2})
such that

H(T_{2})-H(T_{1})=H^{\prime}(T_{0})(T_{2}-T_{1}).

Since

H^{\prime}(T_{0})=p(1+T_{0})^{p-1}-pT_{0}^{p-1}\leq p2^{p-1},

we obtain (
2.6
) for
p\in[2,\infty)
. The case
p\in(0,1)
follows similarly.

Finally, note that equality
\overline{J}_{p}(f,g)=\underline{J}_{p}(f,g)
holds if and only if
T_{1}=T_{2}
, i.e., when equality holds in Hölder’s inequality (
2.7
).
If
\|f^{p-1}g\|_{1}\neq 0
and
\|g\|_{p}\neq 0
, this occurs if and only if there exist constants
\alpha,\beta>0
such that
\alpha f^{p-1}g=\beta g^{p}
almost everywhere,
or equivalently
\alpha f=\beta g
almost everywhere in
\{g\neq 0\}
.
On the other hand, if
\|f^{p-1}g\|_{1}=0
, then
fg=0
almost everywhere and
f
and
g
have disjoint supports up to a set of measure zero.

Lemma 3.1

Let
C_{p}
as in (

(i)

Let

a
and

(3.1)

(a+b)^{p}\leq a^{p}+b^{p}+C_{p}(a+b)^{p-2}ab.

More generally, for any sequence
(a_{i})
of nonnegative real numbers, we have

(3.2)

\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\Bigr)^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{p}+C_{p}\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\Bigr)^{p-2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{i}a_{j}\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(ii)

Let
p\in[1,2]
. For nonnegative real numbers
a
and
b
, we have

(3.3)

(a+b)^{p}\geq a^{p}+b^{p}+\left(\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}\right)\frac{ab}{(a+b)^{2-p}}.

More generally, for any sequence
(a_{i})
of nonnegative real numbers, we have

(3.4)

\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\Bigr)^{p}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{p}+\left(\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}\right)\frac{\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{i}a_{j}}{\bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\bigr)^{2-p}},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

Proof.

We first prove (
3.1
). The case
ab=0
is obvious. Thus we may assume that
a,b>0
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
a\geq b
.
Let
t=\frac{b}{a}\leq 1
. With this notation (
3.1
) is equivalent to

(1+t)^{p}\leq 1+t^{p}+C_{p}t(1+t)^{p-2}.

This inequality holds true, since for
p\in[2,\infty)
we have

\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\frac{(1+t)^{p}-1-t^{p}}{t(1+t)^{p-2}}=\max\left\{p,\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}\right\}=C_{p}.

Next we discuss (
3.2
). We only show (
3.2

, and the general case follows by induction. For
a,b,c\geq 0
, using (
3.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle(a+b+c)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(a+b)^{p}+c^{p}+C_{p}(a+b+c)^{p-2}((a+b)c)

\displaystyle\leq a^{p}+b^{p}+C_{p}(a+b)^{p-2}ab+c^{p}+C_{p}(a+b+c)^{p-2}(ac+bc)

\displaystyle\leq a^{p}+b^{p}+c^{p}+C_{p}(a+b+c)^{p-2}(ab+ac+bc).

Then we discuss (
3.3
). The case
ab=0
is obvious.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a\geq b>0
. Let
t=\frac{b}{a}\leq 1
.
With this notation (
3.3
) is equivalent to

(1+t)^{p}\geq 1+t^{p}+\left(\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}\right)t(1+t)^{p-2}.

This inequality holds true, since for
p\in[1,2]
we have

\inf_{0<t\leq 1}\frac{(1+t)^{p}-1-t^{p}}{t(1+t)^{p-2}}=\frac{2^{p}-2}{2^{p-2}}=C^{\prime}_{p}.

Finally we consider (

For

\displaystyle\geq a^{p}+b^{p}+c^{p}+C^{\prime}_{p}\frac{ab+ac+bc}{(a+b+c)^{2-p}}.

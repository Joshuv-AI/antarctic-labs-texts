2.
Lemmas on number fields

This section establishes two lemmas on number fields, to be used in the proof of Theorem
0.2
. First, we prove a condition under which a quadratic extension of
\QQ
is contained within a cyclic quartic extension of
\QQ
. Specifically, we prove the following:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K
be a cyclic quartic extension of
\QQ
and
F
be its unique non-trivial subfield. Then,
F=\QQ(\sqrt{n})
for some positive integer
n=2^{a}p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}
where
a\in\{0,1\}
and
p_{i}
are primes that satisfy
p_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. In particular,
F
is not equal to neither
\QQ(\sqrt{-1})
,
\QQ(\sqrt{-3})
nor
\QQ(\sqrt{3})
.

To prove this, we need the follwing theorem by A.-M. Legendre.

Theorem 2.2

(A.-M. Legendre. See J.-P. Serre
[
17
]
)
.

Let
a,b,c
be three integers, not all of the same sign and such that
abc
is square-free. Then, the equation

aX^{2}+bY^{2}+cZ^{2}=0

has a solution in integers
(X,Y,Z)\neq(0,0,0)
if and only if
-bc,-ca,-ab
are quadratic residues modulo
|a|
, modulo
|b|
and modulo
|c|
, respectively.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

By
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
, a quadratic extension
F
of
\QQ
is an intermediate field of a quartic cyclic extension of
\QQ
if and only if
F=\QQ(\sqrt{n})
for some positive square-free integer
n
such that there exist non-zero integers
a,b
and
c
satisfying

a^{2}-nb^{2}=nc^{2}.

Since
n
is square-free, this is equivalent to the existence of a non-zero solution of the diophantine equation

nX^{2}-Y^{2}-Z^{2}=0.

By Theorem
2.2
, this is equivalent to
-1
be a quadratic residuo modulo
n
which is equivalent to
-1
be a quadratic residue for each prime divisor of
n
. Therefore
n=2^{a}p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}
for
a\in\{0,1\}
and
p_{i}\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.
∎

The second lemma is a technical result that addresses the non-existence of cyclic extensions of degree 3, under the conditions considered in the proof of Theorem
0.2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K/\QQ
be a Galois extension with Galois group
\gal(K/\QQ)
isomorphic to
S_{4}
, or
\D_{4}
. Let
L=K(\sqrt{\alpha})
be a quadratic extension of
K
with
\alpha\in K
, and let
\widehat{L}
be its Galois closure over
\QQ
. Then, there does not exist a cyclic extension
F
of
\QQ
of degree 3 that is contained in
\widehat{L}
.

Proof..

First, we claim that
[\widehat{L}:K]
is a power of 2. Indeed, let
\sigma
be an element of
\gal(\widehat{L}/\QQ)
. The Galois conjugate of
L
by
\sigma
is
K(\sqrt{\sigma(\alpha)})
, also a quadratic extension of
K
. Therefore, since we know that
\widehat{L}
is the compositum of all Galois conjugates of
L
and each of them are Galois extensions over
K
, we have the equality

[\widehat{L}:K]=\underset{\sigma^{\prime}}{\prod}[L^{\sigma^{\prime}}:K],

where the product is taken over a maximal set of linearly disjoint Galois conjugates of
L
. This is a product of powers of 2, where we get the claim.

Finally, suppose that
F/\QQ
is a cyclic extension of degree 3 contained in
\widehat{L}
. Looking at the subgroup lattice of
\Sym_{4}
and
\D_{4}
we see that
K\cap F=\QQ
. Since both
K
and
F
are Galois extension of
\QQ
, there are linearly disjoint and we conclude that their compositum
KF
satisies
[KF:K]=3
, contradicting the first claim.
∎

This will require the folowing three lemmas:

Lemma 5.2
.

E(K)_{\tors}
has no points of order
11
.

Proof..

Suppose
P_{11}\in E(K)_{\tors}
is a point of order 11, and let
\phi(P_{11})
be its image in
E^{\prime}(L)_{\tors}
. By Theorem
1.1
, we know that
[\QQ(\phi(P_{11})):\QQ]
does not divide 8, hence it is impossible that
\QQ(P_{11})\subseteq L
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
E(K)_{\tors}
has a point of order
7
, then
L=K
or
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
.

Proof..

Let
P_{7}\in E(K)_{\tors}
be a point of order 7, and let
\phi(P_{7})
be its image in
E^{\prime}(L)_{\tors}
. Then, by Theorem
1.1
and the fact that
[L:\QQ]
divides
8
, we see that
[\QQ(\phi(P)):\QQ]=1
or
2
. Since, by Lemma
4.1
,
L=K(\phi(P))
, we conclude that
L=K
or
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

If
E(K)_{\tors}
has a point of order
9
, then
L=K
or
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
.

To prove this Lemma, we need the following consequence of the Weil pairing.

Lemma 5.5

(L. Hamada
[
9
]
, Lemma 5.3)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a field
K
of characteristic zero. If
E(K)_{\tors}
contains
E[n]
, then
K
contains
\QQ(\zeta_{n})
. In particular, if the extension
K/\QQ
is finite, then
\varphi(n)
divides
[K:\QQ]
.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

Suppose
E(K)_{\tors}
has a point of order 9. By Lemma
5.5
and the fact that
\varphi(9)=6
does not divide 8, we can assume
E^{\prime}(L)[9]\subseteq(\ZZ/3\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/9\ZZ)
. Consequently,
E^{\prime}(K)[3]
is isomorphic to either
\{\OO\}
or
\ZZ/3\ZZ
. We now consider two cases, depending on whether
K/\QQ
is a Galois extension or not.

Case 1:
K/\QQ
is a Galois extension.

(1.1)

Suppose
E^{\prime}(K)[3]=0
. Then, by Theorem
4.4
, we have
E^{\prime}(L)[9]\subseteq(\ZZ/3\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/9\ZZ)
.

–

If
L/\QQ
is a Galois extension, then by Lemma
1.3
, the elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
admits a 3-isogeny. Let
P_{3}\in E^{\prime}(L)
be a point of order 3. By Lemma
1.2
, the degree
[\QQ(P_{3}):\QQ]
divides 2. Therefore,
L=K
or
L=K(\sqrt{n})
for some rational number
n
.

–

If
L/\QQ
is not a Galois extension, then by Lemma
4.7
, we have
\ZZ/9\ZZ\oplus\ZZ/9\ZZ\subseteq E^{\prime}(\widehat{L})
, where
[\widehat{L}:\QQ]=16
. However, since
[\widehat{L}:\QQ]
must also be divisible by
\varphi(9)=6
, this leads to a contradiction. Hence,
L/\QQ
cannot be a non-Galois extension in this case.

(1.2)

Suppose
E^{\prime}(K)[3]=\left<Q_{3}\right>\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ
. By Lemma
4.7
,
L/\QQ
is a Galois extension. Additionally, by Lemma
1.2
, the degree
[\QQ(Q_{3}):\QQ]
divides 2. If
\QQ(Q_{3})=\QQ(\sqrt{n})\subseteq K
, then we take the quadratic twist of
E^{\prime}
by
n
. This produces an elliptic curve
E^{{}^{\prime}n}
defined over
\QQ
that is
L
-isomorphic to
E
and satisfies
E^{{}^{\prime}n}(\QQ)[3]\neq\{\OO\}
. Therefore, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
Q_{3}\in E^{\prime}(\QQ)[3]
. Now, let
P_{9}\in E(K)[9]
be a point of order 9, and let
\phi(P_{9})
denote its image in
E^{\prime}(L)[9]
. By Lemma
1.2
, the degree
[\QQ(\phi(P_{9})):\QQ]
divides 2. Consequently, by Lemma
4.1
, we conclude that
L=K
or
L=K(\sqrt{n})
for some rational
n
.

Case 2:
K/\QQ
is not a Galois extension.

Let
\widehat{K}
be the Galois closure of
K
over
\QQ
. Suppose that the Galois group
\gal(\widehat{K}/\QQ)
is isomorphic to either
A_{4}
or
S_{4}
:

(2.1)

Suppose
E^{\prime}(L)[3]\cong(\ZZ/3\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/9\ZZ)
. Then, by Lemma
5.5
,
\QQ(\sqrt{3})\subseteq L
. Since
K/\QQ
has no nontrivial intermediate field, we conclude that
L=K(\sqrt{3})
.

(2.2)

Suppose
E^{\prime}(K)[3]\cong\{\OO\}
and
E(K)\cong E^{\prime}(L)[9]\cong\ZZ/9\ZZ
. Let
P_{9}\in E(K)
be a point of order 9, and
\phi(P_{9})
its image in
E^{\prime}(L)
.

Subcase 1:
Suppose
\gal(\widehat{K}/\QQ)\cong\Sym_{4}
.
We claim that
\QQ([3]P_{3})/\QQ
is a Galois extension:

–

Indeed, suppose it is not and let
\sigma\in\gal(\widehat{L}/\QQ)
be such that
\QQ([3]\phi(P_{9}))^{\sigma}\neq\QQ([3]\phi(P_{9}))
. Then, by Theorem
4.5
,
\phi(P_{9})^{\sigma}\in E^{\prime}(L^{\sigma})
also has order 9. We claim that
E^{\prime}[9]=\left<\phi(P_{9}),\phi(P_{9})^{\sigma}\right>\subseteq E^{\prime}(\widehat{L})
. Indeed, if
a
and
b
are integers such that
a\cdot\phi(P_{9})+b\cdot\phi(P_{9})^{\sigma}=\OO
, then both
a\cdot\phi(P_{9})
and
b\cdot\phi(P_{9})^{\sigma}
belong to
E^{\prime}(L\cap L^{\sigma})
. However,
\QQ([3]\phi(P_{9}))
is not contained in
L\cap L^{\sigma}
, hence
E^{\prime}(L\cap L^{\sigma})[3]=\{\OO\}
and we conclude that
a\equiv b\equiv 0\pmod{9}
. With this, we get that
E^{\prime}[9]\subseteq E^{\prime}(\widehat{L})
, and by Lemma
5.5
, the 9-th cyclotomic extension
\QQ(\zeta_{9})/\QQ
is contained in
\widehat{L}
. However, by Lemma
2.3
this is not possible.

Finally, by Theorem
1.2
,
[\QQ([3]\phi(P_{9})):\QQ]
divides 2, and we conclude, by Lemma
4.1
, that
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
.

Subcase 2:
Suppose
\gal(\widehat{K}/\QQ)\cong\A_{4}
.
Let
[3]P_{9}=(x,y)\in E(K)
. By the definition of the isomorphism
\phi
, the
x
-coordinate,
x(\phi([3]P_{9}))
, of
\phi([3]P_{9})
belongs to
K
. Since
K
has no nontrivial subfields, either
x(\phi([3]P_{9}))\in\QQ
or
K=\QQ(x(\phi([3]P_{9})))
. In the first case,
\QQ(\phi([3]P_{9}))/\QQ
is a quadratic extension, which implies
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
. In the second case,
L=\QQ(\phi([3]P_{9}))
, which further implies
\widehat{K}\subseteq\QQ(E^{\prime}[3])
. By
[
7
, Theorem 3.2]
,
\gal(\QQ(E^{\prime}[3])/\QQ)
is isomorphic to one of
GL_{2}(\FF_{3})
,
3Ns
,
3B
, or
3Nn
(observe that this is the only step we used that
E
do not have CM). However, none of these groups have a quotient isomorphic to
\A_{4}
.

At last, suppose the Galois group
\gal(\widehat{K}/\QQ)
is isomorphic to
\D_{4}
:

(2.3)

Suppose
E^{\prime}(K)[3]\cong\{\OO\}
. If
E^{\prime}(L)[9]\cong\ZZ/9\ZZ
, then we can use the same proof of the corresponding case where
\gal(\widehat{K}/\QQ)
is isomorphic to
S_{4}
. If
E^{\prime}(L)[9]\cong(\ZZ/3\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/9\ZZ)
, then, by Lemma
5.5
,
\QQ(\sqrt{3})
is the unique nontrivial intermediate field of the extension
K/\QQ
and
\QQ(E^{\prime}[3])\subseteq L
. If
[\QQ(E^{\prime}[3]):\QQ]
is equal to either 1, 2 or 8, then
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
. If
\QQ(E^{\prime}[3])/\QQ
is a Galois quartic extension, then it is an abelian extension, isomorphic to either
(\ZZ/4\ZZ)
or
(\ZZ/2\ZZ)\oplus(\ZZ/2\ZZ)
. Since, by Lemma
2.1
,
\QQ(\sqrt{3})
is not contained in any cyclic quartic extension of
\QQ
, we conclude that there exists a quadratic number field
\QQ(\sqrt{m})\subseteq L
, with
m\in\QQ
, other than
\QQ(\sqrt{3})
, concluding that
L=K(\sqrt{m})
.

(2.4)

Suppose
E^{\prime}(K)[3]=\left<Q_{3}\right>\cong\ZZ/3\ZZ
. If
K=\QQ(Q_{3})
, then, by Theorem
4.4
,
L=\QQ(E^{\prime}[3])
is a Galois extension of
\QQ
, hence, by the lattice of subgroups of
\D_{4}
, we conclude that
L=K(\sqrt{m})
for some
m\in\QQ
. Next, suppose that
[\QQ(Q_{3}):\QQ]
divides 2. Let
\QQ(Q_{3})=\QQ(\sqrt{m})\subseteq K
, with
m\in\QQ
. By Theorem
4.4
, we see that up to a twist by
m
, we can suppose without loss of generality that
Q_{3}\in E^{\prime}(\QQ)[3]
. Now, let
P_{3}\in E(K)
be a point of order 3, and let
\phi(P_{3})
be its image in
E^{\prime}(L)
. Observe that
\QQ(\phi(P_{3}))=\QQ(E^{\prime}[3])
is a Galois extension of
\QQ
. Using Lemma
1.2
, we conclude that
[\QQ(\phi(P_{3}):\QQ]
divides 2, and
L=K(\sqrt{n})
for some
n\in\QQ
.

∎

Finally, using Theorem
4.2

is
K

Example 5.7

(Elliptic curve with a 17-torsion, D. Jeon
[

]
)

Consider the quartic cyclic number field

\alpha
be one of the roots of

K

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=-\frac{1337}{32\alpha^{3}}+\frac{3233}{64\alpha^{2}}+\frac{12285}{64\alpha}+\frac{1135}{16},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=-\frac{59}{16\alpha^{3}}+\frac{147}{32\alpha^{2}}+\frac{535}{32\alpha}+\frac{45}{8}.

Consider the elliptic curve

E:y^{2}+(1-c)xy-by=x^{3}-bx.

Using MAGMA, it can be verified that

Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principles for the fractional Dunkl transform on the real line
Sunit Ghosh, Younis Ahmad Bhat and Jitendriya Swain

Abstract
. The aim of the paper is two-fold. First, we provide an explicit form of the functions for which equality holds for the uncertainty inequalities studied in [ 11 ] . Second, we establish an L p L^{p} -type Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle for the fractional Dunkl transform, with 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 1\leq p\leq 2 . For the case p = 2 p=2 , we further derive a sharper uncertainty principle for the fractional Dunkl transform. Furthermore, we derive conditions leading to equality in both the uncertainty principles obtained.

1.
Introduction

The classical Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle (HPWUP) states that for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=1
,

\displaystyle{\Delta}^{2}_{f,2}{\Delta}^{2}_{{\hat{f}},2}\geq\frac{1}{4},

(1.1)

where
{\Delta}^{2}_{f,2}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|(x-(x)_{f})f(x)|^{2}dx,(x)_{f}=\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x|f(x)|^{2}dx
and the classical Fourier transform
\hat{f}
of
f
defined by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)e^{-iwx}dx.

(1.2)

In 1946, Gabor
[
12
]
introduced the HPWUP in the context of signal analysis. Since then, numerous different forms of the HPWUP have emerged through various mathematical formulations
[
13
,
14
,
16
]
. It has significant applications in the field of signal processing, optics and so on(see
[
6
,
23
,
25
,
26
,
22
]
). Although inequality (
1.1
) is the simplest form, it is not the most refined. Subsequently, Cohen
[
3
,
4
]
and Dang-Deng-Qian
[
6
]
independently refined this classical result, deriving sharper uncertainty principles for complex-valued functions. An interesting extension was provided by Cowling-Price
[
5
]
, who refined the classical HPWUP for
L^{p}
-type functions with
1\leq p\leq 2
, yielding the following inequality

\displaystyle{\Delta}^{2}_{f,p}{\Delta}^{2}_{{\hat{f}},p}\geq\frac{1}{4},

(1.3)

where
{\Delta}^{p}_{f,p}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|(x-\langle x\rangle_{f})f(x)|^{p}dx
. Later, Zhang
[
24
]
made further improvements to inequality (
1.3
).

The fractional Fourier transform is an extension of the classical Fourier Transform. We refer to
[
19
,
18
,
23
,
25
]
for its properties and applications. The inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) are derived for the Fractional Fourier Transform for
L^{2}
-type HPWUP in
[
25
]
and for
L^{p}
-type HPWUP in
[
1
]
with
1\leq p\leq 2
. These inequalities were further improved in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
by obtaining a sharper lower bound.

In this paper, we improve HPWUP results for the Dunkl transform
[
21
,
11
]
and the fractional Dunkl transform
[
15
]
, strengthening previously known bounds. The Dunkl transform and the Fractional Dunkl Transform generalize the Fourier transform and the Fractional Fourier Transform, respectively, by incorporating the symmetry of reflection groups associated with
the differential-difference operator, defined as

\displaystyle T_{\mu}f(x)=f^{\prime}(x)+\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{x},\,\,f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\mu\geq-1/2,

(1.4)

where
f^{\prime}
denotes the classical derivative of
f
.
Such operators have been introduced by C. F. Dunkl in a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, which plays an essential role in Dunkl’s generalization of spherical harmonics leading to further developments in harmonic analysis.

Let
\mu\geq-1/2
, for
1\leq p<\infty
, the space
L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
consists of all complex functions on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f\|_{\mu,p}=\left(\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|f(x)|^{p}\left|x\right|^{2\mu+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty
and
L^{\infty}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
is given in the usual way. For
f\in L^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, the Dunkl transform is given by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}f(w)=\frac{1}{2^{\mu+1}\Gamma(\mu+1)}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)E_{\mu}(-iwx)\left|x\right|^{2\mu+1}dx,

(1.5)

where
E_{\mu}
denotes the one dimensional Dunkl kernel, given by

\displaystyle E_{\mu}(z)=j_{\mu}(iz)+\frac{z}{2(\mu+1)}j_{\mu+1}(iz),

(1.6)

with the normalized spherical Bessel function
j_{\mu}(z)=\Gamma(\mu+1)\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(-1)^{n}(\frac{z}{2})^{2n}}{n!\Gamma{(n+\mu+1)}},\,z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Notice that when
\mu=-1/2
, we just have
E_{-1/2}(-iwx)=e^{-iwx}
, hence
D_{-1/2}
coincides with the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}
.
For
f\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
we denote
\langle x\rangle_{f}=\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}x|f(x)|^{2}\left|x\right|^{2\mu+1}dx
, and

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mu,p}(f)=\left(\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|(x-\langle x\rangle_{f})f(x)\right|^{p}\left|x\right|^{2\mu+1}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(1.7)

For a complex function
f
defined on
\mathbb{R}
, we write its even and odd parts by
f_{e}(x)=\frac{f(x)+f(-x)}{2}
and
f_{o}(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{2}
respectively.

(1.8)

equality holds in (

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we outline some important results related to the Dunkl transform and the fractional Dunkl transform.

2.1.
Dunkl transform and the Dunkl operator

For any function
f\in L^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, the Dunkl transform of
f
defined in (
1.5
),
D_{\mu}(f)
belongs to
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies

\displaystyle\|D_{\mu}(f)\|_{\mu,\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\mu+1}\Gamma(\mu+1)}\|f\|_{\mu,1}.

(2.1)

If
f\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, then
D_{\mu}(f)
belongs to
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
and satisfies the following Plancherel formula

\displaystyle\|D_{\mu}(f)\|_{\mu,2}=\|f\|_{\mu,2}.

(2.2)

Interpolating these two inequalities gives the Hausdorff-Young inequality: if
f\in L^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, where
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, then
D_{\mu}(f)\in L^{q}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, and

\displaystyle\|D_{\mu}(f)\|_{q}\leq\frac{1}{(2^{\mu+1}\Gamma(\mu+1))^{\frac{2}{p}-1}}\|f\|_{p},

(2.3)

with equality if and only if
p=q=2
.

Suppose
f,g\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
such that
T_{\mu}f,T_{\mu}g\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, then we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}T_{\mu}f(x)g(x)|x|^{2\mu+1}dx=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)T_{\mu}g(x)|x|^{2\mu+1}dx

(2.4)

and

\displaystyle D_{\mu}(T_{\mu}f)(w)=iwD_{\mu}(f)(w).

(2.5)

For
f,g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
the following product formula holds

\displaystyle T_{\mu}(fg)=(T_{\mu}f)\cdot g+f\cdot(T_{\mu}g).

(2.6)

We refer to
[
10
,
17
,
20
,
21
]
for a detailed study on Dunkl transform and its properties.

2.2.
Fractional Dunkl transform

The fractional Dunkl transform introduced by Ghazouani-Bouzeffour
[
15
]
and defined in (
1
), was initially defined on
L^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
and admits a unique extension as a unitary operator on
L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
. If we denote this extension by
D_{\mu}^{\alpha}
, the family
\{D_{\mu}^{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
forms a group structure. Specifically, for any
f\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
, we have the following property:

\displaystyle D_{\mu}^{\alpha}\circ D_{\mu}^{\beta}(f)=D_{\mu}^{\alpha+\beta}(f)

(2.7)

with
D^{0}_{\mu}
is the identity, and the inverse is given by
(D_{\mu}^{\alpha})^{-1}=D_{\mu}^{-\alpha}
. The fractional Dunkl transform
D_{\mu}^{\alpha}
also has the following exponential form.

D_{\mu}^{\alpha}=e^{-i\alpha(\mu+1)}e^{-i\frac{\alpha}{2}(T_{\mu}^{2}-x^{2})}.

Let
\alpha\notin\pi\mathbb{Z}
. The following relation holds between the fractional Dunkl transform and the Dunkl transform

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{\mu}(f)(w)=\frac{e^{i(\mu+1)(\frac{\hat{\alpha}\pi}{2}-(\alpha-2n\pi))}}{\left|\sin\alpha\right|^{\mu+1}}e^{\frac{iw^{2}\cot\alpha}{2}}D_{\mu}(g)\left(\frac{w}{\sin\alpha}\right).

(2.8)

where
g(x)=e^{\frac{ix^{2}\cot\alpha}{2}}f(x)
and
\hat{\alpha}=sgn(sin(\alpha))
.

2.3.
Covariance and absolute covariance

The covariance of

\displaystyle Cov_{\mu}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\left(x-\langle x\rangle_{f}\right)\biggl\{T_{\mu}(\varphi)(x)+\frac{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}{x}\bigg(\frac{\sin[\varphi(x)-\varphi(-x)]\rho(-x)}{\rho(x)}-(\varphi(x)-\varphi(-x))\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\langle x\rangle_{D_{\mu}(f)}\biggr\}\rho^{2}(x)|x|^{2\mu+1}dx.

(2.9)

From the fact
\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}|f(x)||x|^{2\mu+1}dx\geq\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)|x|^{2\mu+1}dx
, it follows that
COV_{\mu}(f)\geq Cov_{\mu}(f)
. Assuming that
\varphi^{\prime}(x)
is continuous, it clear from equations (

COV_{\mu}(f)=Cov_{\mu}(f)
if and only if
(x-\langle x\rangle_{f})
and the expression

T_{\mu}(\varphi)(x)+\frac{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}{x}\bigg(\frac{\sin[\varphi(x)-\varphi(-x)]\rho(-x)}{\rho(x)}-(\varphi(x)-\varphi(-x))\bigg)-\langle x\rangle_{D_{\mu}(f)}

have the same or opposite signs.

Suppose
xf(x)\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})~\text{and}~~xD_{\mu}(f)(x)\in L^{2}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|f\|_{\mu,2}=1
. Then we have the following relation (see

\displaystyle Re\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}xf(x)\overline{T_{\mu}f(x)}|x|^{2\mu+1}dx=-\left(\|f_{e}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}-\|f_{0}\|^{2}_{\mu,2}\right)-\frac{1}{2},

(2.10)

where left hand side denotes the real part of the integral.
Also we can write

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}xf(x)\overline{T_{\mu}f(x)}|x|^{2\mu+1}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle f(x)\overline{(xT_{\mu}-T_{\mu}x)f(x)}|x|^{2\mu+1}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}f(x)\overline{(xT_{\mu}+T_{\mu}x)f(x)}|x|^{2\mu+1}dx.

(2.11)

A straightforward calculation (see the proof of Theorem 3.2 in

(xT_{\mu}-T_{\mu}x)f(x)=-f(x)-(2\mu+1)f(-x),

RIS-aided ISAC with K K -Rydberg Atomic Receivers
Hong-Bae Jeon, Chan-Byoung Chae

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) framework equipped with multiple Rydberg atomic receiver (RAR)-aided users. By leveraging the reference-assisted reception mechanism of RARs, we develop a unified signal model that jointly captures downlink multi-user communication with RARs and monostatic radar sensing. To explicitly balance communication performance and sensing accuracy, we formulate a Cramér-Rao bound (CRB)-constrained utility maximization problem. To address these challenges, we propose a joint optimization framework that combines fractional programming (FP), majorization-minimization (MM), and the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM). Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms the conventional approach over a wide range of system environments, thereby highlighting the importance of the proposed framework in unlocking the potential of RARs for 6G.



I
Introduction

For decades, wireless communication and radar sensing have evolved independently, driven by fundamentally different infrastructures and design philosophies. The advent of integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) overturns this long-standing separation by redefining the wireless stack and emerging as a foundational technology poised to shape sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks
[
47
,
28
]
. By exploiting the intrinsic commonalities between communication and sensing systems, ISAC moves beyond mere coexistence toward true joint co-design. As a result, ISAC is expected to become a core component of 6G infrastructures, enabling ubiquitous high-rate connectivity alongside precise environmental awareness
[
5
]
.

Accordingly, extensive efforts from both academia and industry have focused on developing and validating practical ISAC architectures and deployment strategies
[
22
]
. Despite significant performance gains in communication and sensing, ISAC systems remain vulnerable to harsh propagation conditions
[
35
]
. In this context, reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) has emerged as a promising solution for reshaping wireless propagation environments, offering coverage enhancement and passive beamforming gains via programmable low-cost reflective elements
[
11
,
43
]
. As 6G networks increasingly integrate ISAC with programmable environments, joint RIS-ISAC frameworks provide a compelling pathway toward energy- and spectrum-efficient environmental control
[
29
]
.

Many studies on RIS-ISAC have focused on enhancing radar detectability while maintaining acceptable communication performance. In
[
32
]
, transmit beamforming and RIS phase were jointly optimized to maximize radar signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) under communication quality-of-service (QoS) constraints. As security vulnerabilities in ISAC were subsequently identified, research expanded toward secure RIS-ISAC. Specifically,
[
34
]
introduced movable antenna (MA) technology to strengthen secure transmission, while
[
7
]
improved radar output SNR with secrecy protection via alternating optimization (AO). Concurrently, efforts broadened RIS-ISAC applicability to low-cost and distributed networks. In low-power Internet-of-Things (IoT) backscatter scenarios,
[
41
]
proposed a fair sensing-communication design that jointly maximizes sensing signal-to-interference-and-noise ratio (SINR) while mitigating multi-user interference. As ISAC networks further scaled spatially, cooperative multi-cell RIS-ISAC architectures were proposed in
[
45
]
, where multi-target sensing and multi-user communication were jointly enhanced through base-station (BS) cooperation, increasing the degree-of-freedom (DoF) of the system.

Recent efforts have also focused on RIS-ISAC designs that jointly incorporate sensing-accuracy objectives or constraints within the optimization problem. In
[
30
]
, BS beamforming and RIS phase were jointly optimized for ISAC under radar-SNR or Cramèr-Rao bound (CRB)-driven sensing constraints, demonstrating that RIS particularly benefits sensing accuracy. Furthermore,
[
33
]
incorporated CRB minimization into hybrid beamforming, marking a conceptual transition from traditional SNR-centric sensing design toward estimation-theoretic ISAC optimization. These RIS-ISAC studies collectively reflect a progressive evolution, marking a clear trajectory toward practical RIS-ISAC realization in 6G networks.

Whereas RIS reshapes the wireless medium to support ISAC functionality, recent advances now shift attention to fundamentally transforming the receiver architecture. Meanwhile, Rydberg atomic receivers (RARs) is rapidly progressing at the intersection of quantum sensing and wireless communications
[
9
]
. Herein, the Rydberg atoms are highly excited quantum states of atoms that exhibit a strong interaction with incident electromagnetic (EM) waves, and when exposed to such fields, these atoms undergo electron transitions between their resonant energy levels
[
38
,
8
]
. RARs exploit these quantum behaviors, including the electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) and Autler-Townes (AT) splitting, to sense these transitions and thereby recover the transmitted information
[
13
]
. Unlike classical radio-frequency (RF) receivers, RARs inherently circumvent the thermal-noise bottleneck of conventional metal antennas, since the atom-field interaction itself does not generate thermal noise
[
10
,
6
]
. In addition, the quantum shot noise associated with probing the Rydberg quantum states is typically several orders of magnitude lower than the thermal noise floor
[
46
]
, which guarantees the sensitivity of the standard quantum limit (e.g., on the order of
\mathrm{nVcm^{-1}Hz^{-1/2}}

[
12
]
). These attributes position RARs as a compelling technology for unlocking wireless reception under extremely weak EM fields such as satellite links or space-air-ground channels, where detecting weak signals is crucial
[
44
]
.

Early studies on RARs focused on verifying whether RF signals could be demodulated through quantum sensing mechanisms. In
[
10
]
, the authors demonstrated recovery of amplitude- and frequency-modulated (AM/FM) signals and later extended this capability to the multi-user regime by formulating atomic single/multiple-input-multiple-output (SIMO/MIMO) detection as a biased phase-retrieval problem, along with an expectation-maximization Gerchberg-Saxton (EM-GS) algorithm for jointly decoding multi-user symbols. On the transmission side,
[
8
]
introduced an atomic-MIMO communication model and highlighted its key distinction from conventional RF MIMO, namely, a nonlinear magnitude-only input-output relation, while proposing an in-phase/quadrature (IQ)-aware precoding strategy that theoretically achieves the atomic-MIMO capacity limit.

In parallel, quantum-enhanced sensing techniques have emerged. Specifically,
[
25
]
proposed Quantum multiple signal classification (MUSIC) for multi-user angle-of-arrival (AoA) estimation in RARs, while
[
24
]
extended RAR sensing to a multi-band regime by estimating AoA across different carrier frequencies. A notable advance in
[
17
]
demonstrated that even a single RAR can infer AoA by exploiting inner-vapor interference within the atomic medium.

More recently, research has shifted toward system-level deployment. For instance,
[
16
]
showed that a single RAR front-end can jointly demodulate spatially multiplexed uplink signals via quantum-optical processing, validating multi-user connectivity. Meanwhile,
[
48
]
proposed a Rydberg atomic quantum-MIMO (RAQ-MIMO) architecture, demonstrating that a vapor-cell-based array can concurrently process multiple RF bands using quantum transconductance modeling and weighted minimum mean-square error (WMMSE) optimization. Collectively, these results suggest that RARs are rapidly emerging as a compelling candidate for 6G receiver front-ends.

Recent studies have begun to extend the applicability of RARs to emerging 6G scenarios; however, such efforts remain in their infancy. The work in
[
15
]
provided a demonstration of practical feasibility by experimentally showing that a single RAR can simultaneously perform microwave radar sensing and wireless communication within an integrated hardware platform. Building on this foundation,
[
4
]
conceptually introduced a RAR-enabled ISAC paradigm, offering high-level discussions on channel and waveform characteristics as well as potential transceiver architectures. Nevertheless, the study remained largely visionary, without presenting a concrete optimization framework or analytically validated ISAC performance. More recently,
[
36
]
examined RIS-RAR integration, demonstrating that constructive field shaping via RIS helps suppress photodetector (PD)-related nonlinear distortion and improves demodulation reliability. Taken together, these works indicate that while feasibility demonstrations, conceptual ISAC visions, and preliminary RIS-assisted enhancements have emerged independently, there is, to the best of our knowledge, no rigorous formulation or system-level study that
unifies RAR and RIS-ISAC within one comprehensive framework.

Motivated by these challenges, this paper presents a unified RIS-ISAC framework that explicitly incorporates multiple RAR-equipped users. By leveraging the field-shaping and combining properties of RIS, the system can increase the effective field strength and improve spatial field conditioning at each RAR, thereby allowing the intrinsic quantum-level sensitivity of the RAR to be more fully utilized. Such controllability is also particularly beneficial in ISAC, which in turn leads us to develop a new formulation that captures RIS-ISAC under multi-RAR reception and propose a sum-rate-oriented utility maximization problem, which is efficiently solved via a tailored block coordinate descent (BCD) framework. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We establish a downlink RIS-ISAC model with multiple RAR-aided users, jointly capturing: (i) Rydberg-based optical readout with LO-assisted signal injection for effective symbol detection, and (ii) RIS-coordinated signal construction that controls both reflected and direct-path components to enable ISAC operation. The RAR channel is reformulated into an equivalent multi-cell real-valued sensing-detection model, enabling closed-form SINR characterization for ISAC optimization.

•

To balance dual ISAC objectives, we formulate a CRB-aware weighted sum-utility maximization problem that jointly accounts for radar estimation accuracy and multi-user downlink throughput. The optimization determines the precoding matrix and RIS configuration under transmit-power, unit-modulus RIS, and RAR-induced magnitude-only detectability constraints.

•

We design a scalable BCD-based solution framework that decouples the problem via: (i) fractional programming (FP) for the communication objective, (ii) CRB relaxation for tractable sensing integration, and (iii) majorization-minimization (MM) and Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)-based decomposition for joint precoder and RIS phase optimization.

•

Extensive simulations demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly outperforms benchmark schemes across diverse operating conditions, achieving near communication-only performance while maintaining strong sensing accuracy, thereby validating the effectiveness of multi-RAR reception in RIS-ISAC systems.

The key novelty of this paper lies not in RIS or RAR individually, but in revealing how RIS-enabled field shaping fundamentally changes the CRB-rate tradeoff under magnitude-only quantum receivers.

III
ISAC Problem Formulation

III-A

Communication Utility for
K
RAR-aided Users

The RAR users detect their information symbols from the in-phase components of
\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{com}}
in (
14
). We now develop a communication-theoretic utility based on the SINR achieved at each RAR. Define the LO-phase-aligned effective multiuser channel for user
k
as

\mathbf{G}_{k}(\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{W})\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{eff},k}^{\mathrm{com}}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K},

(18)

and let
\mathbf{g}_{k,i}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
denote its
i
th column, i.e.,
\mathbf{G}_{k}=[\mathbf{g}_{k,1}\cdots\mathbf{g}_{k,K}]
. Then (
10
) can be expanded as

\mathbf{z}_{k}=\Re\left\{\mathbf{g}_{k,k}s_{c,k}+\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbf{g}_{k,i}s_{c,i}\right\}+\bm{\eta}_{k}.

(19)

Herein (
19
), the first term corresponds to the desired signal for user
k
, while the summation term represents multiuser interference. The RAR operates cellwise and produces
\mathbf{z}_{k}
; we therefore measure signal and interference powers by the
Euclidean norms across the
M
cells.

Writing
\mathbf{g}_{k,i}=\mathbf{a}_{k,i}+j\mathbf{b}_{k,i}
with
\mathbf{a}_{k,i}=\Re\{\mathbf{g}_{k,i}\}
and
\mathbf{b}_{k,i}=\Im\{\mathbf{g}_{k,i}\}
, and decomposing
s_{c,i}=s_{c,i}^{\mathrm{R}}+js_{c,i}^{\mathrm{I}}
with independent
s_{c,i}^{\mathrm{R}},s_{c,i}^{\mathrm{I}}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
, the in-phase operation in (
19
) yields
\Re\{\mathbf{g}_{k,i}s_{c,i}\}=\mathbf{a}_{k,i}s_{c,i}^{\mathrm{R}}-\mathbf{b}_{k,i}s_{c,i}^{\mathrm{I}}
, so that both the real and imaginary parts of
\mathbf{g}_{k,i}
contribute to the in-phase photocurrent through the in-phase and quadrature data components. Taking expectations with respect to the symbols, the average desired signal power
P_{k}^{\mathrm{sig}}
at user
k
is

\displaystyle P_{k}^{\mathrm{sig}}

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Re\{\mathbf{g}_{k,k}s_{c,k}\}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}.

(20)

Similarly, the average multiuser interference power
P_{k}^{\mathrm{int}}
is

P_{k}^{\mathrm{int}}\triangleq\sum_{i\neq k}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Re\{\mathbf{g}_{k,i}s_{c,i}\}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq k}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,i}\|_{2}^{2},

(21)

while the effective noise power is

P_{k}^{\mathrm{noise}}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bm{\eta}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\big]=\frac{M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}{2}.

(22)

Collecting (
20
)-(
22
), we define the SINR of user
k
as

\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\frac{\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}(\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{W})\|_{2}^{2}}{\sum_{i\neq k}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,i}(\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{W})\|_{2}^{2}+{M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}},

(23)

and the overall communication utility
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
is expressed as a form of sum-rate in nats/s/Hz:

\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ln\big(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\big),

(24)

hence maximizing
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
enhances the communication performance of the multiple-RARs while explicitly accounting for multiuser interference and the RAR noise characteristics.

Remark 1
.

Following
[
8
]
, the RAR channel can be transformed into an equivalent real-valued MIMO representation. However, it is derived under a single-user setting with waterfilling-based input covariance assumptions, and thus cannot be directly applied to the considered multi-RAR scenario. (Indeed, the information-theoretic capacity characterization of multi-RAR systems remains an open research problem.) In contrast, the proposed
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
is developed for a multi-RAR configuration and is constructed based on an SINR-driven achievable-rate expression, and can be interpreted as an achievable rate under mismatched decoding, which serves as a tractable lower-bound-style performance indicator. From this viewpoint, the use
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
is practically well-motivated: it captures the throughput performance under realistic RAR architectures experiencing multi-cell interference, enables optimization-friendly analytical expressions, and reflects implementable communication performance.

III-B

Cramér-Rao Bound of Sensing Performance

We focus on a point target whose location is parameterized by

\mathbf{h}_{d,t}(\theta_{\mathrm{B}})=\beta_{d}\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{B}}(\theta_{\mathrm{B}})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}},\mathbf{h}_{r,t}(\theta_{\mathrm{R}})=\beta_{r}\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{R}}(\theta_{\mathrm{R}})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})=\mathbf{v}_{t}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})\mathbf{v}_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}},

\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\mathbf{v}_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}+\mathbf{v}_{t}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\Big)^{\mathrm{T}},\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\mathbf{v}_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}+\mathbf{v}_{t}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\Big)^{\mathrm{T}},

\frac{\partial\mathbf{v}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}=\beta_{d}\frac{\partial\mathbf{a}_{\mathrm{B}}(\theta_{\mathrm{B}})}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}

Recall (

\bm{\theta}

\alpha_{t}

\mathbf{y}_{r}=\bm{\mu}(\bm{\xi})+\mathbf{n}_{r},~\bm{\mu}(\bm{\xi})\triangleq\alpha_{t}\mathbf{h}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta}),

(28)

\bm{\xi}

\bm{\xi}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\theta}\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{I}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{4},

for

, and
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\theta}}=\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}^{\mathrm{T}}
.
Hence, the CRB for

\displaystyle\mathrm{CRB}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

]
.

III-C

Joint Precoder and RIS Phase Design Problem

We finally formulate the problem, where
\mathbf{W}
and
\bm{\Phi}
are jointly optimized to strike a trade-off between communication and sensing. To do this, we investigate to maximize
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
while satisfying a CRB constraint, the transmit power budget and the unit-modulus constraint of RIS:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

(35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{CRB}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}))<\epsilon,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*})\leq P_{\max},|\bm{\Phi}_{n,n}|=1~(\forall n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}).

Problem (
35
) is nonconvex due to the fractional SINRs, the inverse FIM structure, and the unit-modulus constraint on
\bm{\Phi}
. In the subsequent sections, we develop the joint optimization framework with respect to
\mathbf{W}
and
\bm{\Phi}
, and devise a tractable BCD framework to handle the associated nonconvexity.

IV
Joint Optimization Framework

IV-A

FP-Based Transformation of
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}

Following the FP framework in
[
39
]
with applying the Lagrangian-dual reformulation, we introduce an auxiliary vector
\mathbf{r}=[r_{1}\cdots r_{K}]^{\mathrm{T}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{K}
to rewrite each logarithmic term:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\ln(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k})

(36)

\displaystyle=\max_{r_{k}\geq 0}\Big(\ln(1+r_{k})-r_{k}+(1+r_{k})\frac{\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,j}\|_{2}^{2}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}\Big),

where maximum is achieved at

r_{k}^{\star}=\frac{\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}}{\sum_{j\neq k}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,j}\|_{2}^{2}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}=\mathrm{SINR}_{k}.

(37)

Accordingly,
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
becomes maximizing

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{1}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ln(1+r_{k})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}

(38)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}(1+r_{k})\frac{\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,j}\|_{2}^{2}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}}

over
\mathbf{r}\geq\mathbf{0}
, with maximum at (
37
). To eliminate the fractional structure, we introduce another set of auxiliary vectors
\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
with
\mathbf{c}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
.
For each user
k
, define

B_{k}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,j}(\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{W})\|_{2}^{2}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}.

(39)

Then, using the quadratic transform identity
[
39
]

(1+r_{k})\frac{\|\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\|_{2}^{2}}{B_{k}}=\max_{\mathbf{c}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}}\Big(2\sqrt{1+r_{k}}\Re\{\mathbf{c}_{k}^{*}\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\}-B_{k}\|\mathbf{c}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\Big),

(40)

with maximum at

\mathbf{c}_{k}^{\star}=\frac{\sqrt{1+r_{k}}}{B_{k}}\mathbf{g}_{k,k},

(41)

we obtain the following equivalent FP reformulation of
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{com}}
by maximizing

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})\triangleq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ln(1+r_{k})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}+

(42)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\bigg(2\sqrt{1+r_{k}}\Re\big\{\mathbf{c}_{k}^{*}\mathbf{g}_{k,k}\big\}-\|\mathbf{c}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{g}_{k,j}\|_{2}^{2}+M\sigma_{q,k}^{2}\Big)\bigg).

over
\mathbf{r}\geq\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{c}_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
. Let
\mathbf{w}\triangleq\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{W})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}K}
denote the stacked precoder, and collect the auxiliary vectors as
\mathbf{C}\triangleq[\mathbf{c}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{c}_{K}]\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times K}
. Then all
\mathbf{W}
-dependent terms in (
42
) can be written as a quadratic function of
\mathbf{w}
. Specifically, there exist a vector
\mathbf{a}
, a matrix
\mathbf{B}
, and a scalar
\epsilon_{1}
such that:

\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})=\Re\{\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{w}\}-\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}+\epsilon_{1},

(43)

which is conditionally concave in
\mathbf{w}
for fixed
(\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, where
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{B}
is given by

\mathbf{a}=2\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{1+r_{k}}\mathbf{J}_{k,k}^{*}\mathbf{c}_{k},\mathbf{B}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\|\mathbf{c}_{1}\|_{2}\mathbf{\widetilde{J}}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\|\mathbf{c}_{K}\|_{2}\mathbf{\widetilde{J}}_{K}\end{bmatrix}

(44)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{k,i}\triangleq\widetilde{\mathbf{E}}_{k}^{\mathrm{com}}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{S}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N_{t}K},

(45)

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{i}\triangleq\big[\mathbf{0}_{N_{t}\times N_{t}(i-1)}~\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}}~\mathbf{0}_{N_{t}\times N_{t}(K-i)}\big]\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}K},

\displaystyle\mathbf{\widetilde{J}}_{k}\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{k,1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathbf{J}_{k,K}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{C}^{KM\times N_{t}K}.

Likewise, for fixed
\mathbf{W}
,
\mathbf{r}
, and
\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\}
, the terms depending on the RIS phase vector
\bm{\phi}\triangleq[e^{j\theta_{1}}\cdots e^{j\theta_{N}}]^{\mathrm{T}}~(\bm{\Phi}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\phi}))
admit a similar quadratic form. That is, there exist
\mathbf{g}
, a Hermitian matrix
\mathbf{D}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, and a constant
\epsilon_{2}
such that

\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})=\Re\{\mathbf{g}^{*}\bm{\phi}\}-\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}\bm{\phi}+\epsilon_{2},

(46)

which is conditionally concave in
\bm{\phi}
given
(\mathbf{W},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
, where
\mathbf{g}
and
\mathbf{D}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{g}=2\Bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sqrt{1+r_{k}}\mathbf{F}_{k,k}^{*}\mathbf{c}_{k}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{c}_{k}\|_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\mathbf{F}_{k,j}^{*}\mathbf{d}_{k,j}\Bigg),

(47)

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\mathbf{c}_{k}\|_{2}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\mathbf{F}_{k,j}^{*}\mathbf{F}_{k,j}.

with
\mathbf{F}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{RU},k}\mathrm{diag}(\mathbf{q}_{k,i}),\mathbf{q}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{BR}}\mathbf{w}_{i},\mathbf{d}_{k,i}\triangleq\mathbf{D}_{b,k}\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{BU},k}\mathbf{w}_{i}
. Therefore, these FP reformulation yields a concave objective with respect to each individual
\mathbf{W}
,
\bm{\Phi}
,
\mathbf{r}
, and
\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\}
when the others are fixed. This blockwise concavity enables an AO framework given later in Section
IV-D
, where
(\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})
admit closed-form updates, while
\mathbf{W}
and
\bm{\Phi}
are updated via convex quadratic subproblems.

IV-B

Reformulation of CRB-Constraint

Thereafter, we reformulate the CRB constraint into a set of linear matrix inequalities (LMIs) that depend on
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
. We first rewrite
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{CRB}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}))<\epsilon
in (
35
) via an auxiliary positive semidefinite matrix
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
. Define

\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}}-\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}^{-1}\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\theta}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},

(48)

so that
\mathrm{CRB}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})^{-1}
. Since
\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\succ\mathbf{0}
for identifiable parameter vectors, we have
\mathrm{tr}\big(\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})^{-1}\big)\leq\epsilon
iff there exists a positive definite matrix
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}\succ\mathbf{0}
such that

\mathrm{tr}\big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}^{-1}\big)\leq\epsilon,\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\succeq\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}.

(49)

The matrix inequality (
49
) can be recast via the Schur complement as a single LMI involving
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}},\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}},\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}
.
Indeed, (
49
) is equivalent to
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}}-\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}^{-1}\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\theta}}-\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, which, by the Schur complement, is equivalent to
[
18
]

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\triangleq\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}}-\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}&\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}\\
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\theta}}&\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}\end{bmatrix}\succeq\mathbf{0}.

(50)

Therefore,
\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{CRB}_{\bm{\theta}}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}))<\epsilon
in (
35
) is equivalently

\mathrm{tr}\big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}^{-1}\big)\leq\epsilon,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\succeq\mathbf{0}.

(51)

We now show explicitly that
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}},\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}},\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}
depend on
\mathbf{W}
only through
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
. Using
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X})=(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A})
, we obtain

\mathbf{h}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})=(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}\big(\mathbf{H}_{t}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})\big).

(52)

Similarly, for
i\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{R}\}
,

\frac{\partial\mathbf{h}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})}{\partial\theta_{i}}=(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Big).

(53)

Define the shorthand
\mathbf{p}_{i}\triangleq\frac{\partial\mathbf{h}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})}{\partial\theta_{i}}
and
\mathbf{h}\triangleq\mathbf{h}(\bm{\Phi},\bm{\theta})~(i\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{R}\})
. Using (
31
), the partial derivatives of
\bm{\mu}
are

\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\theta_{i}}=\alpha_{t}\mathbf{p}_{i},\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{R}}}=\mathbf{h},\frac{\partial\bm{\mu}}{\partial\alpha_{\mathrm{I}}}=j\mathbf{h}.

(54)

Substituting into (
33
), the FIM blocks take the form

\displaystyle{}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\big\{\alpha_{t}^{*}\alpha_{t}\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{j}\big\}=\frac{2|\alpha_{t}|^{2}}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\big\{\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{j}\big\},

(55)

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\Big\{\big[\mathbf{h}~j\mathbf{h}\big]^{*}\big[\mathbf{h}~j\mathbf{h}\big]\Big\}=\frac{2\|\mathbf{h}\|_{2}^{2}}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\mathbf{I}_{2},

\displaystyle[\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}]_{i,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\big\{\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{h}\big\},[\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}]_{i,2}=\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\big\{j\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{h}\big\}

for
i,j\in\{\mathrm{B},\mathrm{R}\}\leftrightarrow\{1,2\}
.

Next, we express
\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{j}
and
\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{h}
explicitly in terms of
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
. From (
52
)-(
53
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Big)^{*}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}*}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{j}}\Big)

(56)

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Big)^{*}(\mathbf{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{j}}\Big),

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{h}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Big)^{*}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}*}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}\big(\mathbf{H}_{t}\big)

(57)

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{vec}\Big(\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{i}}\Big)^{*}(\mathbf{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}\big(\mathbf{H}_{t}\big).

Finally,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{h}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{H}_{t})^{*}(\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}*}\mathbf{X}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{H}_{t})

(58)

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{H}_{t})^{*}(\mathbf{R}_{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{N_{t}})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{H}_{t}).

Equations (
56
)-(
58
) show that all entries of
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}},\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}
, and
\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}
are affine functions of
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
.

In order to obtain a compact LMI representation of (
51
), we define the followings:

\displaystyle F_{1}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{B}},F_{2}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{R}},F_{3}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\mathbf{h},

(59)

\displaystyle F_{4}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle\triangleq\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{R}}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{R}},F_{5}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{R}}^{*}\mathbf{h},F_{6}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\triangleq\|\mathbf{h}\|_{2}^{2}.

By (
56
)-(
58
), each

is an affine function of
\mathbf{R}_{x}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}
and thus
a quadratic function of
\mathbf{W}
. Using these definitions together with (

the FIM blocks can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\theta}}=\frac{2|\alpha_{t}|^{2}}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}&F_{2}\\
F_{2}&F_{4}\end{bmatrix},\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\alpha}\bm{\alpha}}=\frac{2F_{6}}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\mathbf{I}_{2},

(60)

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}_{\bm{\theta}\bm{\alpha}}=\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\mathbf{h}&j\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{B}}^{*}\mathbf{h}\\
\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{R}}^{*}\mathbf{h}&j\alpha_{t}^{*}\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{R}}^{*}\mathbf{h}\end{bmatrix}.

For notational simplicity, we include them in the same parametrization and denote their collection by
\mathbf{f}=[f_{1}\cdots f_{6}]^{\mathrm{T}}
to equally constrain
F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
, i.e.,
f_{i}=F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})~(i=1,\cdots,6)
. Substituting (
60
) into (
50
),
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\succeq\mathbf{0}
becomes a function of
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}
:
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\succeq\mathbf{0}
, where
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})
is an affine of
\mathbf{f}
and
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}
, given by

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2|\alpha_{t}|^{2}}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}&f_{2}\\
f_{2}&f_{4}\end{bmatrix}-\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}&\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\begin{bmatrix}f_{3}&jf_{3}\\
f_{5}&jf_{5}\end{bmatrix}\\
\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}\Re\begin{bmatrix}f_{3}&jf_{3}\\
f_{5}&jf_{5}\end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}}&\frac{2}{\sigma_{r}^{2}}f_{6}\mathbf{I}_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(61)

IV-C

Equivalent CRB-Constrained Optimization Problem

Collecting the above results, (
35
) becomes

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{f}}~\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})

(62)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}^{-1}\big)\leq\epsilon,\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}\succ 0,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\succeq\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle f_{i}=F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})~(i=1,\cdots,6),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*})\leq P_{\max},|\bm{\Phi}_{n,n}|=1~(\forall n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}).

where the CRB constraint has been fully encoded into the auxiliary
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}
and
\mathbf{f}
via (
51
) and (
61
), together with
f_{i}=F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
. The augmented Lagrangian formulation of (
62
) with penalizing
\{f_{i}=F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\}
is formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\},\\
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{f}\end{subarray}}-\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi},\mathbf{r},\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\})

(63)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\left|F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})-f_{i}+\rho_{1}\zeta_{i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}~\mathrm{tr}\big(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}^{-1}\big)\leq\epsilon,\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}\succ 0,\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\succeq\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*})\leq P_{\max},|\bm{\Phi}_{n,n}|=1~(\forall n\in\{1,\cdots,N\}),

where
\bm{\zeta}=[\zeta_{1}\cdots\zeta_{6}]^{\mathrm{T}}
denotes the vector of dual variables and
\rho_{1}>0
is the associated penalty parameter. Based on this formulation, the variables can be updated in an alternating fashion using a BCD procedure.

Updating Blocks

IV-D

Update
\mathbf{r}
and
\{\mathbf{c}_{k}\}

Fixing the remaining variables, the optimal
\mathbf{r}
and

are directly obtained from (
37
) and (
41
).

IV-D

Update of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}
and
\mathbf{f}

With all other variables fixed, the update of
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}
and
\mathbf{f}
is achieved by solving

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}},\mathbf{f}}~\sum_{i=1}^{6}\big|F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})-f_{i}+\rho_{1}\zeta_{i}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}^{-1}\right)\leq\epsilon,\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}}\succ 0,~\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{f},\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{aux}})\succeq 0,

which constitutes a convex semidefinite program (SDP) and can be efficiently solved using standard SDP solvers

]
.

IV-D
3
Update
\mathbf{W}

With other variables fixed, (
63
) reduces to:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{W}}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}-\Re\{\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{w}\}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\big|F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})-f_{i}+\rho_{1}\zeta_{i}\big|^{2}

(65)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*})\leq P_{\max},

where the first two terms come from (
43
). Using
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A})^{*}(\mathbf{R}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{I})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{B})=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{A}^{*}\mathbf{B}\right)
, we obtain the explicit forms from (
59
), which is organized in (
66
).

\displaystyle F_{1}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\right),F_{2}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=L\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\right),F_{3}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=L\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\alpha_{t}^{*}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\mathbf{H}_{t}\right),

(66)

\displaystyle F_{4}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})

\displaystyle=L\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\right),F_{5}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=L\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\alpha_{t}^{*}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\mathbf{H}_{t}\right),F_{6}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=L\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{R}_{x}\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{t}\right).

Thus, we can define
\mathbf{A}_{i}(\bm{\Phi})\in\mathbb{C}^{N_{t}\times N_{t}}
such that

F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{A}_{i}(\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*}\right)

(67)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=L\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}},\mathbf{A}_{2}=L\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}},\mathbf{A}_{3}=L\alpha_{t}^{*}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{B}}}\mathbf{H}_{t},

(68)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{4}

\displaystyle=L\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}},\mathbf{A}_{5}=L\alpha_{t}^{*}\frac{\partial\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}}{\partial\theta_{\mathrm{R}}}\mathbf{H}_{t},\mathbf{A}_{6}=L\mathbf{H}_{t}^{*}\mathbf{H}_{t}.

Using
\mathbf{w}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{W})
and
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{*})=\mathbf{w}^{*}(\mathbf{I}_{K}\otimes\mathbf{A})\mathbf{w}
, we obtain the compact quadratic form

F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})=\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w},\mathbf{C}_{i}\triangleq\mathbf{I}_{K}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{i}(\bm{\Phi}).

(69)

Hence, by letting
d_{i}\triangleq-f_{i}+\rho_{1}\zeta_{i}
and substituting (
69
) into (
65
), it becomes

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}-\Re\{\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{w}\}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\left|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}+d_{i}\right|^{2}

(70)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{\max}.

Problem (
70
) is still nonconvex since the last term in objective is quartic in
\mathbf{w}
. To tackle this, we adopt an MM strategy
[
30
]
, where at each iteration we replace the quartic penalty by a tight quadratic surrogate around the current iterate.

Let
t
denote the iteration index and
\mathbf{w}^{(t)}=\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{W}^{(t)})
be the precoder vector with precoder
\mathbf{W}^{(t)}
at iteration
t
. For brevity, define
z_{i}(\mathbf{w})\triangleq\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}+d_{i}~(i=1,\cdots,6)
. Each quartic term can then be expanded as

\displaystyle|z_{i}(\mathbf{w})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}|^{2}+2\Re\left\{d_{i}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}\right\}+|d_{i}|^{2}.

(71)

The constant
|d_{i}|^{2}
does not affect the minimizer and will be omitted in the sequel. Define
\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\triangleq d_{i}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}+d_{i}\mathbf{C}_{i}^{*}
, which is Hermitian, so that the second term of (
71
) can be written as

2\Re\left\{d_{i}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}\right\}=\mathbf{w}^{*}\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\mathbf{w}.

(72)

Let
\vartheta_{t,i}\geq\lambda_{\max}(\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i})
with largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\max}(\cdot)
. Then
(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{(t)})^{*}(\vartheta\mathbf{I}-\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i})(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{(t)})\geq 0
holds, and we obtain

\mathbf{w}^{*}\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\mathbf{w}\leq 2\Re\left\{\mathbf{w}^{*}(\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}-\vartheta_{t,i}\mathbf{I})\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\right\}+\varpi_{1,i}^{(t)},

(73)

where
\varpi_{1,i}^{(t)}\triangleq\vartheta_{t,i}P_{\max}+\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\big(\vartheta_{t,i}\mathbf{I}-\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\big)\mathbf{w}^{(t)}
is a constant independent of
\mathbf{w}
, and we have used
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{\max}
to upper-bound the term involving
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}
. The right-hand side of (
73
) is affine in
\mathbf{w}
and coincides with
\mathbf{w}^{*}\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}\mathbf{w}
at
\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{w}^{(t)}
, thus serving as a valid MM surrogate.

To majorize the first term of (
71
), we may equivalently express it using the Hermitian part of
\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}
:

|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}|^{2}=\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}},\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\triangleq\frac{\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{*}}{2},

(74)

where
\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}=\mathbf{C}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{C}_{i}^{*}
and
\bar{\mathbf{w}}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*})
. By construction,
\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}
is Hermitian. Let
\vartheta_{b,i}\geq\lambda_{\max}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i})
.
Since

\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{I}-\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\succeq\mathbf{0}
,
it holds that
(\bar{\mathbf{w}}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)})^{*}\big(\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{I}-\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\big)(\bar{\mathbf{w}}-\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)})\geq 0
, where
\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*})
be
\bar{\mathbf{w}}
at iteration
t
. Similar to (
73
), expanding it yields

\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\leq 2\Re\left\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{*}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}-\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{I}\big)\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)}\right\}+\varpi_{2,i}^{(t)},

(75)

where
\varpi_{2,i}^{(t)}\triangleq\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\big(\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{I}-\widehat{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\big)\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)}+\vartheta_{b,i}P_{\max}^{2}
is independent of
\bar{\mathbf{w}}
and can be treated as a constant, and we used

\bar{\mathbf{w}}=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*})\rightarrow\|\bar{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*}\|_{F}^{2}=\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{4}\leq P_{\max}^{2}.

(76)

Thereafter, we expand the real-valued bilinear term as

\displaystyle\Re\left\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\big(\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{*}-2\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{I}\big)\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\}+\varpi_{2,i}^{(t)}

(77)

\displaystyle=\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}+\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}

\displaystyle-2\vartheta_{b,i}\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}+\varpi_{2,i}^{(t)}.

Therein, each term in (
77
) admits the closed-form expressions:

\displaystyle\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2},

(78)

\displaystyle\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2},

\displaystyle\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}.

To show this, we first derive the closed-form of
\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}
. Using
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X})^{*}(\mathbf{B}\otimes\mathbf{C})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{X}^{*}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{Y}\mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\right)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*})^{*}(\mathbf{C}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes\mathbf{C}_{i}^{*})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*})

(79)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left((\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*})^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}^{*}(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*})\mathbf{C}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left(\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{C}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\right).

The scalar inside the trace is rank-one, hence

\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}=\big(\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\big)\big(\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{C}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{w}\big)=\left|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\right|^{2}.

(80)

Since the right-hand side is already real and nonnegative:

\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}=\left|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\right|^{2}=\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2}

(81)

since
\mathbf{C}_{i}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}=(\mathbf{I}_{K}\otimes\mathbf{A}_{i})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{W}^{(t)})=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})
using
(\mathbf{I}\otimes\mathbf{A})\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X})=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X})
, and so is
\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}
.

We then derive the closed-form of
\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}
. By using
\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{X})^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{Y})=\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{X}^{*}\mathbf{Y})
, we first have

\displaystyle\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*})^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*})

(82)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left((\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*})^{*}(\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}=\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus it becomes
\Re\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\bar{\mathbf{w}}\}=\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}
. Hence, substituting these into (
77
) yields the closed-form surrogate

\displaystyle\Re\left\{\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{(t)*}\big(\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}+\bar{\mathbf{A}}_{i}^{*}-2\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{I}\big)\bar{\mathbf{w}}\right\}+\varpi_{2,i}^{(t)}

(83)

\displaystyle=\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2}+\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle-2\vartheta_{b,i}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}+\varpi_{2,i}^{(t)}.

We see that only the third term of the right-hand side of (
83
) is non-convex. To majorize this term, we apply the first-order Taylor expansion at
\mathbf{w}^{(t)}
, which admits the global upper-bound

\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}\geq\|\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\|_{2}^{4}+2\Re\left\{\|\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{w}^{(t)*}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{(t)})\right\},

(84)

Substituting the results obtained in (
73
), (
77
) and (
84
) into (
70
), by letting
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{(t)}\triangleq 2(\bar{\mathbf{C}}_{i}-\vartheta_{t,i}\mathbf{I})\mathbf{w}^{(t)}-4\vartheta_{b,i}\|\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\|_{2}^{2}\mathbf{w}^{(t)}
, the sub-problem for updating
\mathbf{w}
becomes:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{w}}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{B}\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}-\Re\{\mathbf{a}^{*}\mathbf{w}\}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\Big(\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2}

(85)

\displaystyle+\big|\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathrm{vec}(\mathbf{A}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{W}^{(t)})\big|^{2}+\Re\{\mathbf{a}_{i}^{(t)*}\mathbf{w}\}\Big)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}^{2}\leq P_{\max}.

Problem (
85
) is a convex quadratic program (QP), which can be efficiently solved using standard convex solvers
[
2
]
. The resulting
\mathbf{w}(\leftrightarrow\mathbf{W})
is then taken as the updated
\mathbf{W}^{(t+1)}

IV-D
4
Update of
\bm{\Phi}

Fixing other variables and by (
46
), the sub-problem of solving for
\bm{\Phi}(\leftrightarrow\bm{\phi})
is formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\phi}}

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{D}\bm{\phi}-\Re\{\mathbf{g}^{*}\bm{\phi}\}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\big|F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})+d_{i}\big|^{2}

(86)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle|\phi_{n}|=1~(\forall n),

where
\phi_{n}
is the
n
th element of
\bm{\phi}
. Problem (
86
) is nonconvex due to both (i) the unit-modulus constraints and (ii) the quartic dependence of
F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})
on
\bm{\phi}
.
To derive a tractable update, we again adopt a MM approach. We denote
\mathbf{d}_{i}^{(t)}\triangleq\nabla_{\bm{\phi}}F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}^{(t)})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
with the Wirtinger gradient
\nabla_{\bm{\phi}}
at iteration
t
with corresponding
\bm{\Phi}^{(t)}
, and let the residual in (
86
) be
e_{i}^{(t)}\triangleq F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}^{(t)})+d_{i}
. The explicit form of
\mathbf{d}_{i}^{(t)}
is given in Appendix A. Using the first-order Taylor approximation and Lipschitz continuity of
\nabla_{\bm{\phi}}F_{i}
with Lipschitz constant
\tau_{i}
:

\displaystyle F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})\leq

\displaystyle F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi}^{(t)})+2\Re\left\{\mathbf{d}_{i}^{(t)*}(\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}^{(t)})\right\}

(87)

\displaystyle+\frac{\tau_{i}}{2}\|\bm{\phi}-\bm{\phi}^{(t)}\|_{2}^{2},

where
\bm{\Phi}^{(t)}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\phi}^{(t)})
. Substituting (
87
) into the squared residual in (
86
) yields the quadratic upper-bound

|F_{i}(\mathbf{W},\bm{\Phi})+d_{i}|^{2}\leq\bm{\phi}^{*}\mathbf{Q}_{i}^{(t)}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\left\{\mathbf{q}_{i}^{(t)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}+\kappa_{i}^{(t)},

(88)

where
\kappa_{i}^{(t)}
is a term independent of
\bm{\phi}
,
\mathbf{Q}_{i}^{(t)}\triangleq 2\mathbf{d}_{i}^{(t)}\mathbf{d}_{i}^{(t)*}+\tau_{i}\mathbf{I}
, and
\mathbf{q}_{i}^{(t)}\triangleq 2e_{i}^{(t)}\mathbf{d}_{i}^{(t)}+\tau_{i}\bm{\phi}^{(t)}
. Discarding all constant terms, the MM surrogate problem of (
86
) becomes

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\phi}}~\bm{\phi}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\left\{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)*}\bm{\phi}\right\}~\mathrm{s.t.}~|\phi_{n}|=1~(\forall n),

(89)

where
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\triangleq\mathbf{D}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mathbf{Q}_{i}^{(t)}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}\triangleq\mathbf{g}+\frac{1}{2\rho_{1}}\sum_{i=1}^{6}\mathbf{q}_{i}^{(t)}
. We now solve (
89
) by means of an ADMM.
Introduce an auxiliary variable
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
that carries the unit-modulus constraint, and enforce
\bm{\phi}=\mathbf{z}
via a consensus constraint. Then (
89
) is equivalently written as

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\phi},\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)*}\bm{\phi}\}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{z})~\mathrm{s.t.}~\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0},

(90)

where
\mathcal{U}\triangleq\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:|z_{n}|=1~(\forall n)\}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}(\cdot)
denotes the indicator function of
\mathcal{U}
:

\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{z})=0~(\mathbf{z}\in\mathcal{U}),~\infty~(\text{otherwise}).

(91)

Let
\bm{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
be the dual variable associated with the constraint
\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{0}
, and let
\rho_{\phi}>0
be the ADMM penalty parameter. The (unscaled) augmented Lagrangian of (
90
) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{\phi}}(\bm{\phi},\mathbf{z},\bm{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)*}\bm{\phi}\}+\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{U}}(\mathbf{z})

(92)

\displaystyle+\Re\big\{\bm{\lambda}^{*}(\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z})\big\}+\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{2}\|\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z}\|_{2}^{2}.

Herein, ADMM proceeds by cyclically minimizing
\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{\phi}}
with respect to
\bm{\phi}
and
\mathbf{z}
, followed by a dual update of
\bm{\lambda}
.

\bm{\phi}
-update

Given
(\mathbf{z}^{[r]},\bm{\lambda}^{[r]})
at iteration
r
for fixed
t
, the
\bm{\phi}
-subproblem is

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{[r+1]}=\arg\min_{\bm{\phi}}

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)*}\bm{\phi}\}

(93)

\displaystyle+\Re\big\{\bm{\lambda}^{[r]*}(\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z}^{[r]})\big\}+\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{2}\|\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z}^{[r]}\|_{2}^{2}.

Discarding the terms independent of
\bm{\phi}
, we obtain the strictly convex quadratic problem

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{\phi}}

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}^{*}\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\bm{\phi}-2\Re\{\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)*}\bm{\phi}\}+\Re\{\bm{\lambda}^{[r]*}\bm{\phi}\}+\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{2}\|\bm{\phi}-\mathbf{z}^{[r]}\|_{2}^{2}.

(94)

The first-order optimality condition of (
94
) yields

\big(2\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}+\rho_{\phi}\mathbf{I}\big)\bm{\phi}=2\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}-\bm{\lambda}^{[r]}+\rho_{\phi}\mathbf{z}^{[r]}.

(95)

Since
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}\succeq\mathbf{0}
and
\rho_{\phi}>0
,
2\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}+\rho_{\phi}\mathbf{I}\succ\mathbf{0}
holds:

\bm{\phi}^{[r+1]}=\big(2\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}+\rho_{\phi}\mathbf{I}\big)^{-1}\Big(2\widehat{\mathbf{g}}^{(t)}-\bm{\lambda}^{[r]}+\rho_{\phi}\mathbf{z}^{[r]}\Big).

(96)

Note that
\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}
is fixed within the iteration
r
, hence the inverse of
2\widehat{\mathbf{D}}^{(t)}+\rho_{\phi}\mathbf{I}
in (
96
) can be precomputed and reused across inner ADMM iterations.

\mathbf{z}

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed an RIS-ISAC framework for multi-user systems employing RARs. By explicitly incorporating sensing accuracy requirements into the joint design of the transmit beamformer and RIS configuration, the proposed approach satisfies the CRB constraint with reduced effective beamforming effort, thereby enhancing the communication utility in terms of sum-rate. An efficient joint optimization algorithm was developed to address the inherent nonconvexity, enabling a systematic BCD framework through a sequence of tractable subproblems based on FP, MM, and ADMM updates. Extensive simulation results demonstrated that the proposed design consistently outperforms conventional schemes across diverse operating conditions and exhibits a progressively smaller performance gap relative to the communication-only benchmark as system resources increase. Overall, these results confirm that tight integration of RAR and RIS-ISAC constitutes a viable and effective design paradigm for fully exploiting the potential of RARs in 6G.

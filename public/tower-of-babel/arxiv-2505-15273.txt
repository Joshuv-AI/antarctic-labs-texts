Non-weight modules over gap- p p Virasoro algebras
Chengkang Xu, Fulin Chen, Shaobin Tan

Abstract
. In this paper, we study non-weight modules over gap- p p Virasoro algebras, including Whittaker modules, 𝒰 ⁡ ( ℂ ​ L 0 ) \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0}) -free modules and their tensor products. We establish necessary and sufficient conditions for universal Whittaker modules to be irreducible and study the structure of irreducible Whittaker modules. Moreover, the irreducibility of tensor products of 𝒰 ⁡ ( ℂ ​ L 0 ) \mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0}) -free modules of rank 1 and irreducible restricted modules is also determined.

Introduction

In the past decades, representation theory of those Lie algebras that are closely related to the Virasoro algebra has been extensively studied.
Particularly, the theory of weight modules has been well-developed,
such as the classification of irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[
24
]
,
the Schrödinger-Virasoro algebras
[
23
]
, the mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[

Virasoro algebras
[
37

certain Lie algebras of block-type
[
13
,
32
,
36
]
and etc.
There are also some interesting studies on other kinds of weight modules, see
[
3
,
6
]
for examples.

In recent years, the theory of
non-weight modules over infinite dimensional Lie algebras has also attracted more and more attention.
Among them, Whittaker modules (or more generally, non-weight restricted modules) and
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h})
-free modules are most well-known. For a Lie algebra with a triangular decomposition, its Whittaker module is a module on which the elements in the positive part (or its certain subalgebra) act as scalars.
This class of modules was first constructed for
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
in
[
2
]
,
and then systematically studied for all semisimple Lie algebras in
[
17
]
.
Whittaker modules for infinite dimensional Lie algebras have been intensively studied as well,
including the Virasoro algebra
[
30
,
21
]
, the generalized Weyl algebras
[
5
]
,
the affine Lie algebras
[
7
,
1

[

[
14
]
,
the Schrödinger-Witt algebra
[

, and the rank two Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
[

Restricted module is a generalization of highest weight module and Whittaker module,
which is closely connected to (weak) modules for certain vertex algebras.
To classify irreducible restricted modules is a core topic in the representation theory of Lie algebras and related vertex algebras.
The problem was studied for several Lie (super)algebras, such as the Virasoro algebra
[
27
]
,
Neveu-Schwarz algebras
[
22
]
.
the twisted and mirror Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras
[

,
12
,
20
,
34
]
,

]
, and so on.

For a Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
equipped with a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
, its

-free module is defined to be a module free of a fixed rank when viewed as a

Such modules were first introduced in
[
28
]
for the simple Lie algebra

Since then,

including finite dimensional simple Lie algebras

[

, and so on.

The gap-

It is closely related to the algebra of derivations over a rational quantum torus as well as the twisted Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra.
Particularly, when

In this paper, we study non-weight modules over the gap-

The main goals of this paper are to determine the irreducibility of universal Whittaker

The structure of this paper is as follows.

In Section 3, we first give a free field construction of certain Whittaker

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some notations related to the gap-
p
Virasoro algebra, and recall some results on the Whittaker modules and
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free modules over the Virasoro algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

The
gap-
p
Virasoro algebra

\mathfrak{g}
is a complex Lie algebra with a basis

\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)},C_{j}\ \mid\ \,n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq[\frac{p}{2}]\right\}

satisfying the following Lie brackets

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{0}\delta_{m+n,0},

(2.1)

\displaystyle[I_{m}^{(i)},I_{n}^{(j)}]=\left(m+\frac{i}{p}\right)\delta_{i+j,p}\delta_{m+n+1,0}C_{i},\ \ \ \ \ [C_{k},\mathfrak{g}]=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle[L_{m},I_{n}^{(i)}]=-\left(n+\frac{i}{p}\right)I_{m+n}^{(i)},

(2.3)

where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
1\leq i,j\leq p-1
, and
0\leq k\leq[\frac{p}{2}]
.

Here and below, for
[\frac{p}{2}]<i\leq p-1
, we also set
C_{i}=C_{p-i}
for narrative convenience.

Note that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
contains a subalgebra

\mathcal{V}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{L_{n},C_{0}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

which is isomorphic to the Virasoro algebra, and contains an ideal

\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{I_{n}^{(i)},C_{i}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1\}

which is isomorphic to a direct sum of Heisenberg algebras.
Obviously
\mathfrak{g}
is the semi-direct product of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathfrak{h}
.

Let
I
be a subset of
\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
.
We denote by
|I|
the cardinal number of
I
, and set

I^{C}=\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}\setminus I,\qquad I_{0}=I\cap\left\{1,2,\dots,[\frac{p}{2}]\right\}.

Associated to
I
, we have the following two subspaces of
\mathfrak{g}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)},C_{0},C_{i}\ \,\vline\ \,n\in\mathbb{Z},i\in I\right\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{I}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{I_{n}^{(i)},C_{i}\ \mid\ n\in\mathbb{Z},i\in I\right\}.

(2.5)

Clearly,
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, and

\mathfrak{g}_{I}=\mathcal{V}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{I}.

Note that
\mathfrak{g}_{I}=\mathcal{V}
,
\mathfrak{h}_{I}=0
when
I=\emptyset
, and
\mathfrak{g}_{I}=\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{h}_{I}=\mathfrak{h}
when
I=\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
.

In this paper we mainly concern about the case that
I
is
symmetrical
, meaning that
p-i\in I
whenever
i\in I
.
In this case, the complement set
I^{C}
is also symmetrical, and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{I}=\oplus_{i\in I_{0}}\mathfrak{h}_{\{i,p-i\}}\qquad\left(\mathfrak{h}_{\{i,p-i\}}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{I_{n}^{(i)},I_{n}^{(p-i)},C_{i}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\right)

(2.6)

is a direct sum of Heisenberg algebras.
Additionally, we have that

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{I}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{I^{C}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{I}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{I^{C}}.

For a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, we denote by
\mathcal{L}_{m}
(
m\in\mathbb{Z}
) the
m
-th component of it such that

\mathcal{L}=\oplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{L}_{m}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A module
V
over a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
is called
restricted

if for any
v\in V
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{L}_{m}v=0
for all
m\geq N.

The gap-
p
Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded structure with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{0},C_{0},I_{0}^{(i)},C_{i}\ \,\vline\ \,1\leq i\leq p-1\right\},\ \text{and}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)}\ \,\vline\ \,1\leq i\leq p-1\right\}\quad\text{for}\ n\neq 0.

Note that for every subset
I
of
\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
,

\mathfrak{g}_{I}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
are both
\mathbb{Z}
-graded subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}_{I},\mathfrak{h}_{I}
are all
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebras.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
I
be a symmetrical subset of
\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
,
and let
\underline{l}=(l_{i})_{i\in I}
be an
|I|
-tuple in
\mathbb{C}^{|I|}
.
We say that a module
V
over
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
or
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
is of
level

\underline{l}
if for every
i\in I
,
the central element
C_{i}
acts as the scalar
l_{i}
on
V
.
Additionally, we say that the
|I|
-tuple
\underline{l}
is
generic
if
l_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in I
.

Since
I
is symmetrical, when we say a level
\underline{l}
of a module over
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
or
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
, the
|I|
-tuple
\underline{l}
is always assumed to be symmetrical in the sense that
l_{p-i}=l_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
. A
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
-module
V
is said to be
of central charge

c
if
C_{0}
acts as
c
on
V
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie algebra and let
\mathcal{K}
be a graded subalgebra of
\mathcal{L}
such that
\mathcal{K}_{-m}=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. A
Whittaker function

\phi
on
\mathcal{K}
is defined as a Lie algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.
An
\mathcal{L}
-module is called a
Whittaker module
of type
\phi
if it can be generated by a
Whittaker vector
of type
\phi
, namely, a vector

satisfying

x\cdot v=\phi(x)v\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{K}.

Every Whittaker function
\phi
on
\mathcal{K}
defines a one-dimensional
\mathcal{K}
-module

\mathbb{C}v_{\phi}
such that

x\cdot v_{\phi}=\phi(x)v_{\phi}\qquad\text{for all}\ x\in\mathcal{K}.

The resulting induced
\mathcal{L}
-module

M_{\mathcal{L},\phi}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathcal{K}}^{\mathcal{L}}\mathbb{C}v_{\phi}\quad(=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{L})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{K})}\mathbb{C}v_{\phi})

is often referred as the
universal Whittaker module
of type
\phi
,
and
v_{\phi}
a
universal Whittaker vector
of type
\phi
. Note that for every Whittaker
\mathcal{L}
-module
V
of type
\phi
with Whittaker vector
v
, there is an
\mathcal{L}
-module epimorphism

\pi:M_{\mathcal{L},\phi}\longrightarrow V
such that
\pi(v_{\phi})=v
.

In what follows, we recall some facts on Whittaker modules and
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free modules over the Virasoro algebra.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
21
, Theorem 7]

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and let
\phi
be a Whittaker function on the subalgebra
\mathcal{V}_{\geq m}=\oplus_{k\geq m}\mathbb{C}L_{k}
of
\mathcal{V}
. Then the universal Whittaker
\mathcal{V}
-module

is irreducible
if and only if
\left(\phi(L_{2m-1}),\phi(L_{2m})\right)\neq(0,0)
.

For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and

, the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[t]
carries
a
\mathcal{V}
-module structure defined by

C_{0}f(t)=0,\ \ \ \ L_{n}f(t)=\lambda^{n}(t+an)f(t+n)\ \ \ \textit{ for all }\ n\in\mathbb{Z},\ f(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t].

The resulting
\mathcal{V}
-module is denoted by
\Omega(\lambda,a)
.
The following results are proved in
[
25
]
and
[
35
, Theorem 3]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

(1) The
\mathcal{V}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,0)
has a unique irreducible submodule
t\Omega(\lambda,0)
.

(2) The
\mathcal{V}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,a)
is irreducible if and only if

.

(3) Let
V
be a
\mathcal{V}
-module which is free of rank 1 when viewed as a
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-module.
Then
V
is isomorphic to
\Omega(\lambda,a)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and

.

We also record the following elementary results as a lemma for later use (cf.
[
26
, Lemma 6, Theorem 7]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a Lie algebra of countable dimension.

(1) Let
V
be an irreducible
\mathcal{L}
-module.
For any

and any linearly independent subset

3.
Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

Throughout this section,
m
is a fixed positive integer. We have the following graded subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{m+k},I_{k}^{(i)},C_{0},C_{i}\ \mid\ k\in\mathbb{N},1\leq i\leq p-1\right\}

of
\mathfrak{g}
.
Associated to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}

\varphi
,
a Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-module of type
\varphi
, and the universal Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-module

M_{\mathfrak{g},\varphi}
of type
\varphi
with a universal Whittaker vector
v_{\varphi}
.
Note that for any Whittaker function
\varphi
on
\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}
, one has

\varphi(L_{2m+j})=\varphi\left(I_{m+j-1}^{(i)}\right)=0\ \ \ \ \text{for any }j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},1\leq i\leq p-1.

(3.1)

In this section we give a free field realization of certain irreducible Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-modules, and
determine the irreducibility of universal Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-modules. To this end, we need to work in a more general setting.
Let
I
be a fixed nonempty symmetrical subset of
\{1,2,\dots,p-1\}
throughout this section.
We have the subalgebra

\mathfrak{g}_{I}^{(m)}=\mathfrak{g}^{(m)}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{I}

of
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
, and the notion of a Whittaker function
\phi
on
\mathfrak{g}_{I}^{(m)}
.
We will consider Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}_{I}
-modules of type
\phi
, including the universal one
M_{\mathfrak{g}_{I},\phi}
.

3.1.
Free field realizations of irreducible Whittaker
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

We start by constructing restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-modules from restricted
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
-modules of generic levels.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
V
be a restricted
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
-module of generic level
\underline{l}=(l_{j})_{j\in I}\in\mathbb{C}^{|I|}
.
Then there exists a restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
V
given by

\displaystyle I_{n}^{(i)}

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}I_{n}^{(i)}\ &\text{if}\ i\in I,\\
0\ &\text{if}\ i\notin I,\end{cases}\ \ \ \ \quad C_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}l_{i}\ &\text{if}\ i\in I,\\
0\ &\text{if}\ i\notin I,\end{cases},\quad C_{0}\mapsto|I|,

(3.2)

\displaystyle L_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}:I_{k}^{(j)}I_{n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\right)+\delta_{n,0}\sum_{j\in I}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}},

(3.3)

where
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1
and the normal ordered product is defined by

:I_{k}^{(i)}I_{l}^{(p-i)}:=\begin{cases}I_{k}^{(i)}I_{l}^{(p-i)}\quad&\text{ if }k<l,\\
I_{l}^{(p-i)}I_{k}^{(i)}\quad&\text{ if }k\geq l.\end{cases}

Proof.

It is clear that the operators given in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) satisfy the relation (
2.2
).
In what follows, we prove that they also satisfy the relations (
2.1
) and (
2.3
).

Using the “cutoff” procedure, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we set

L_{n}(\epsilon)=\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}:I_{k}^{(j)}I_{n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\psi(k\epsilon)\right),

where the function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\psi(x)=1\ \text{ if }|x|\leq 1,\qquad\psi(x)=0\ \text{ if }|x|>1.

Notice that
L_{n}(\epsilon)\rightarrow L_{n}
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, and
L_{n}(\epsilon)
differs from

\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{k}^{(j)}I_{n-k-1}^{(p-j)}\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

only by a scalar. For any
i\in I,s,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle[I_{n}^{(i)},L_{s}(\epsilon)]=\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[I_{n}^{(i)},I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s-k-1}^{(p-j)}]\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}(n+\frac{i}{p})\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{n+k+1,0}\delta_{i+j,p}I_{s-k-1}^{(p-j)}l_{i}\psi(k\epsilon)+\delta_{n+s,k}\delta_{i,j}I_{k}^{(j)}l_{i}\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(n+\frac{i}{p})I_{s+n}^{(i)}\Big(\psi\big(\epsilon(n+1)\big)+\psi\big(\epsilon(s+n)\big)\Big).

By taking the limit
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, it gives (
2.3
).

For any
s,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle[L_{s}(\epsilon),L_{n}]=\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}[I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s-k-1}^{(p-j)},L_{n}]\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(k+\frac{j}{p})I_{n+k}^{(j)}I_{s-k-1}^{(p-j)}\psi(k\epsilon)\right)+\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(s-k-\frac{j}{p})I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s+n-k-1}^{(p-j)}\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(k+\frac{j}{p}):I_{n+k}^{(j)}I_{s-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\psi(k\epsilon)+\sum_{k\geq\frac{s-n-1}{2}}(k+\frac{j}{p})[I_{n+k}^{(j)},I_{s-k-1}^{(p-j)}]\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(s-k-\frac{j}{p}):I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s+n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\psi(k\epsilon)+\sum_{k\geq\frac{s+n-1}{2}}(s-k-\frac{j}{p})[I_{k}^{(j)},I_{s+n-k-1}^{(p-j)}]\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(k-n+\frac{j}{p}):I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s+n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\psi\big((k-n)\epsilon\big)+(s-k-\frac{j}{p}):I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s+n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\psi(k\epsilon)\right)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\delta_{s+n,0}\sum_{j\in I}\sum_{-\frac{1}{2}\leq k<s-\frac{1}{2}}(k+\frac{j}{p})(s-k-\frac{j}{p})\psi(k\epsilon).

Taking the limit

\displaystyle[L_{s},L_{n}]=(s-n)\sum_{j\in I}\frac{1}{2l_{j}}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}:I_{k}^{(j)}I_{s+n-k-1}^{(p-j)}:\right)+2s\delta_{s+n,0}\sum_{j\in I}\frac{j(p-j)}{4p^{2}}+\delta_{s+n,0}\frac{1}{12}(s^{3}-s)|I|,

which amounts to (
2.1
).
∎

3.2.

\mathfrak{g}

Let

\mathfrak{h}_{I}

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
V,V^{\prime}
be irreducible restricted
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
-modules of generic levels,
and let
W,W^{\prime}
be
\mathfrak{g}_{I^{C}}
-modules.
Then

(1) every
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of

is of the form

for some
{\mathfrak{g}_{I^{C}}}
-submodule
W_{0}
of

. In particular,
V^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes W^{e}
is irreducible if and only if the
{\mathfrak{g}_{I^{C}}}
-module
W
is irreducible.

(2)
V^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes W^{e}\cong V^{\prime\mathfrak{g}}\otimes W^{\prime e}
if and only if
V\cong V^{\prime}
as
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
-modules and
W\cong W^{\prime}
as
{\mathfrak{g}_{I^{C}}}
-modules.

Proof.

(1) Note that
V^{\mathfrak{g}}
is irreducible as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module and

\mathfrak{h}_{I}\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}_{\mathfrak{g}}(S)=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\ \vline\ xS=0\}

for any finite subset
S\subset W^{e}
.
Then (1) follows from Lemma
2.8
(2).

(2) The sufficiency is clear and we prove the necessity in the following.
Let
\varphi:V^{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes W^{e}\longrightarrow V^{\prime\mathfrak{g}}\otimes W^{\prime e}
be
a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism and fix
x\in V
.
For any
v\in W
write

\varphi(x\otimes v)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}y_{i}\otimes w_{i},

where
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},w_{i}\in W^{\prime}
,
y_{i}\in V^{\prime}
, and
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{t}
are linearly independent.
Then by Lemma
2.8
(1), there exists
u_{0}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}_{I})
such that

u_{0}y_{i}=\delta_{i,1}y_{1},\qquad i=1,2,\dots,t.

Therefore for any
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}_{I})
, noting
\mathfrak{h}_{I}W^{e}=\mathfrak{h}_{I}W^{\prime e}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(uu_{0}x\otimes v)=\varphi(uu_{0}(x\otimes v))=uu_{0}\varphi(x\otimes v)=uu_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{t}y_{i}\otimes w_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{t}uu_{0}y_{i}\otimes w_{i}=uy_{1}\otimes w_{1}.

Denote
x_{1}=u_{0}x
and we get

\varphi(ux_{1}\otimes v)=uy_{1}\otimes w_{1}\qquad\text{ for any }u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{h}_{I}).

Note that
V
is irreducible as an
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
-module.
Then the linear map
\eta_{v}:V\longrightarrow V^{\prime}
defined by
\eta_{v}(ux_{1})=uy_{1}
for any

is clearly an
\mathfrak{h}_{I}
-module isomorphism.

Now without loss of generality we may assume that
V=V^{\prime}
and
\eta_{v}
is the identity.
Then for any
x\in V,v\in W
there exists
w_{v}\in W
such that

\varphi(x\otimes v)=x\otimes w_{v}.

Define a linear map
\varphi^{\prime}:W\longrightarrow W^{\prime}
by
\varphi^{\prime}(v)=w_{v}
, which is clearly bijective.
Moreover, for any
i\in I^{C},n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(x\otimes Xv)

\displaystyle=\varphi(X(x\otimes v))-\varphi((Xx)\otimes v)=X\varphi(x\otimes v)-Xx\otimes w_{v}

\displaystyle=X(x\otimes w_{v})-Xx\otimes w_{v}=x\otimes Xw_{v},

where

represents
L_{n}
or

.
So
\varphi^{\prime}
is a
{\mathfrak{g}_{I^{C}}}
-module isomorphism, proving (2).
∎

Lemma 3.8

If
\varphi\left(I_{m-1}^{(i)}\right)=0

Proof.

Pick an

By Lemma
3.8

3.6

\varphi\left(I_{m-1}^{(i)}\right)\neq 0
for all

Set

\mathfrak{g}_{I}^{(0)}=\mathrm{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{L_{n},I_{n}^{(i)},C_{0},C_{i}\ \mid\ n\in\mathbb{N},i\in I\right\},

Lemma 3.9

If
\varphi(L_{k})=0
for all

\mathfrak{g}_{I}^{(0)}
-module

M_{0}=\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{g}_{I}^{(m)}}^{\mathfrak{g}_{I}^{(0)}}\,\mathbb{C}v_{\varphi}

For

Assume now that
V
is a nonzero

4.
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free
\mathfrak{g}
-modules

In this section, we first classify all
\mathfrak{g}
-modules that are free of rank 1 when viewed as
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-modules, and then determine their irreducibility.

For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\overline{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
, it is straightforward to see that there is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[t]
such that

L_{n}f(t)=\lambda^{n}(t+\alpha_{0}n)f(t+n),\ \ \ \ \ I_{n}^{(i)}f(t)=\alpha_{i}\lambda^{n}f(t+n+\frac{i}{p}),\ \ \ \ \ C_{j}f(t)=0,

where
n\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq i\leq p-1,0\leq j\leq p-1
, and
f(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t]
.
We shall denote the resulting
\mathfrak{g}
-module by
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module that is free of rank 1 when viewed as a
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-module.
Then
M\cong\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
.

Proof.

Viewing
M
as a
\mathcal{V}
-module, we see that
M\cong\Omega(\lambda,\alpha_{0})
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
by Proposition
2.7
. By identifying the underlying space of
M
with
\Omega(\lambda,\alpha_{0})=\mathbb{C}[t]
, we have that

L_{n}f(t)=\lambda^{n}(t+\alpha_{0}n)f(t+n),\ \ C_{0}f(t)=0\ \ \text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z},f\in\mathbb{C}[t].

Note that
f(L_{0})1=f(t)
and
L_{0}f(t)=tf(t)
for all
f(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t]
.

For each
1\leq i\leq p-1
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, by using induction on
k
one can show that

I_{n}^{(i)}t^{k}=I_{n}^{(i)}(L_{0}^{k}1)=\left(t+n+\frac{i}{p}\right)^{k}I_{n}^{(i)}1.

This gives

I_{n}^{(i)}f(t)=I_{n}^{(i)}f(L_{0})1=f\left(t+n+\frac{i}{p}\right)I_{n}^{(i)}1.

(4.1)

By setting
\alpha_{i}=I_{0}^{(i)}1
, it follows from
(
4.1
) that

-\frac{i}{p}I_{n}^{(i)}1=[L_{n},I_{0}^{(i)}]1=L_{n}I_{0}^{(i)}1-I_{0}^{(i)}L_{n}1=\alpha_{i}\lambda^{n}(t+\alpha_{0}n)-I_{0}^{(i)}(t+\alpha_{0}n)=-\frac{i}{p}\lambda^{n}\alpha_{i}.

Therefore, we obtain that

I_{n}^{(i)}f(t)=\alpha_{i}\lambda^{n}f\left(t+n+\frac{i}{p}\right).

Now, to show that
M=\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
with
\overline{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-1})
,
it remains to prove that
C_{i}
acts trivially on
M
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
.
Indeed, if
I_{n}^{(i)}1=0
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
then
I_{n}^{(i)}M=0
by (
4.1
).
So we have
I_{m}^{(i)}M=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
by using the bracket
[I_{n}^{(i)},L_{m-n}]=\left(n+\frac{i}{p}\right)I_{m}^{(i)}
.
This implies that
C_{i}M=[I_{n}^{(i)},I_{-n-1}^{(p-i)}]M=0
.

Suppose
I_{n}^{(i)}1\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We claim that in this case
I_{n}^{(i)}1\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, which forces
C_{i}M=0
.
Assume otherwise that there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
I_{N}^{(i)}1=\sum_{j=0}^{k_{N}}a_{j}t^{j}
with
k_{N}>0,a_{k_{N}}\neq 0
.
Choose
s\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
s+N+1\neq 0
and
\left(s+1-\frac{i}{p}\right)\left(N+\frac{i}{p}\right)<0
.
Write
X(t)=I_{N}^{(i)}1
and
Y(t)=I_{s}^{(p-i)}1=\sum_{j=0}^{l_{s}}b_{j}t^{j}
with
l_{s}\geq 0,b_{l_{s}}\neq 0
.
Since
[I_{N}^{(i)},I_{s}^{(p-i)}]=0
, we have by (
4.1
) that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=I_{N}^{(i)}I_{s}^{(p-i)}1-I_{s}^{(p-i)}I_{N}^{(i)}1=X(t)Y\left(t+N+\frac{i}{p}\right)-X\left(t+s+1-\frac{i}{p}\right)Y(t)

\displaystyle=a_{k_{N}}b_{l_{s}}\left(l_{s}\left(N+\frac{i}{p}\right)-k_{N}\left(s+1-\frac{i}{p}\right)\right)t^{k_{N}+l_{s}-1}+\text{lower terms},

which contradicts to the choice of
s
. This completes the proof.
∎

Write
\overline{0}
for the element in
\mathbb{C}^{p}
whose components are all
0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(1) The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{0})
has an unique irreducible submodule
t\Omega(\lambda,\overline{0})
.

(2) The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
is irreducible if and only if
\overline{\alpha}\neq\overline{0}
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Proposition
2.7
(1).
For the second one, we only need to show that
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
is irreducible whenever
\overline{\alpha}\neq\overline{0}
. If
\alpha_{0}\neq 0
, then
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
is irreducible by Proposition
2.7
(2). Assume now that
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq p-1
,
and take a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule
V
of
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
.
Let
f(t)\in V
be a nonzero polynomial with minimal degree
s
. If
s>0
, then

f(t)-f\left(t+\frac{i}{p}\right)=f(t)-\alpha_{i}^{-1}I_{0}^{(i)}f(t)\quad\left(\in V\setminus\{0\}\right)

is of degree
s-1
, a contradiction. This forces that
f(t)
has degree
0
and so
1\in V
.
Applying
L_{0}
to
1
repeatedly, one gets
V=\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
, and
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
is irreducible as required.
∎

5.
Tensor product of
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free modules and restricted modules

In this section we study tensor products of
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free modules
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})
and irreducible restricted modules.
Specifically, we determine the irreducibility of the tensor product
\mathfrak{g}
-modules
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes M

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},\overline{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
and any irreducible restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
,
and the necessary and sufficient condition for any two such tensor products to be isomorphic.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
\overline{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{p-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
, and let
M
be an irreducible restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

(1) If
\overline{\alpha}\neq\overline{0}
, then the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes M
is irreducible.

(2) The
\mathfrak{g}
-module
t\Omega(\lambda,\overline{0})\otimes M
is irreducible.

Proof.

We will only prove the first assertion, the second one can be proved in a similar way and we omit the details.

For any
v\in M
, choose
N(v)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
L_{n}v=I_{n}^{(i)}v=0
for all
n\geq N(v)
and
1\leq i\leq p-1
.
Let
V
be a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes M
.
Let
w=\sum_{i=0}^{s}t^{i}\otimes v_{i}
be a nonzero vector in
V
with
s\in\mathbb{N},v_{i}\in M,v_{s}\neq 0
and
s
minimal.
Set
N=\max\{N(v_{i})\mid i=0,1,2,\dots,s\}
. Then

L_{n}v_{i}=I_{n}^{(j)}v_{i}=0\quad\text{ for all }n\geq N,1\leq j\leq p-1,0\leq i\leq s.

We claim that
1\otimes v_{s}\in V
.
The proof of this claim is divided into two cases:

Case 1
:
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq p-1
. For any
n\geq N
, we have

\alpha_{i}^{-1}\lambda^{-n}I_{n}^{(i)}w=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\left(t+n+\frac{i}{p}\right)^{j}\otimes v_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\left(n+\frac{i}{p}\right)^{j}w_{j}\in V,

where
w_{j}\in\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes M
.
By taking
n=N,N+1,N+2,\dots,N+s
, we get
(s+1)
equations with indeterminants
w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{s}
.
Since the coefficient matrix is an invertible Vandermonde matrix,
we find that
w_{j}\in V
for all
0\leq j\leq s
.
In particular,
w_{s}=1\otimes v_{s}\in V
.

Case 2
:
\alpha_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq p-1
and
\alpha_{0}\neq 0
.
By applying
L_{n}
(
n>N
) to
w
,
a similar argument as that in Case 1 shows that
1\otimes v_{s}\in V
.

Now we are ready to prove that
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes v_{s}\subseteq V
.
Let
k\geq 0
and
suppose
t^{j}\otimes v\in V
for all
0\leq j\leq k
.
From the fact

L_{n}(t^{k}\otimes v_{s})=\lambda^{n}(t+\alpha_{0}n)(t+n)^{k}\otimes v_{s}\quad\text{for}\ n\geq N(v),

one concludes that

t(t+n)^{k}\otimes v_{s}=\lambda^{-n}L_{n}(t^{k}\otimes v_{s})-\alpha_{0}n(t+n)^{k}\otimes v_{s}\in V.

This gives that
t^{k+1}\otimes v_{s}\in V
, as desired.

Write
W=\{w\in M\mid\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes w\in V\}
, which is a nonzero subspace of
M
.
For every
w\in W,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
1\leq i\leq p-1
, we have

\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes xw=x\left(\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes w\right)-\left(x\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\right)\otimes w\in V,

where
x
denotes
L_{n}
or
I_{n}^{(i)}
.
This shows that
W
is a submodule of
M
.
Therefore,
W=M
,
V=\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes M
and the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes M
is irreducible.
∎

Now we determine the isomorphism classes of the tensor products of
\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}L_{0})
-free modules and irreducible restricted modules.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},\overline{\alpha},\overline{\beta}\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
and
V,W
be irreducible restricted modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then
\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes V\cong\Omega(\mu,\overline{\beta})\otimes W

if and only if
\lambda=\mu,\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\beta}
, and
V\cong W
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the “only if” part, and for that we take a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism

\varphi:\Omega(\lambda,\overline{\alpha})\otimes V\longrightarrow\Omega(\mu,\overline{\beta})\otimes W.

For any
v\in V
, write

\varphi(1\otimes v)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}t^{i}\otimes w_{i}

(5.1)

with
w_{i}\in W,s\geq 0
and
w_{s}\neq 0
.
Take
N_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
L_{n}v=L_{n}(w_{i})=I_{n}^{(j)}v=I_{n}^{(j)}w_{i}=0
for all
n\geq N_{v},1\leq j\leq p-1,0\leq i\leq s
.

We first show that
\lambda=\mu
.
For any
m,n\geq N_{v}
, by combining (
5.1
) with the equality

\varphi\left(\left(\lambda^{-m}L_{m}-\lambda^{-n}L_{n}\right)(1\otimes v)\right)=\left(\lambda^{-m}L_{m}-\lambda^{-n}L_{n}\right)\varphi(1\otimes v),

we get that

\alpha_{0}(n-m)\sum_{i=0}^{s}t^{i}\otimes w_{i}=\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{m}\sum_{i=0}^{s}(t+\beta_{0}m)(t+m)^{i}\otimes w_{i}-\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{s}(t+\beta_{0}n)(t+n)^{i}\otimes w_{i}.

(5.2)

Notice that the coefficient of
t^{s+1}\otimes w_{s}
in the right hand side of (
5.2
) is

\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{m}-\left(\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\right)^{n}
, while that in the left hand side is 0.
This implies that
\lambda=\mu
.

Next we prove that
\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\beta}
.
Since
\lambda=\mu
, (
5.2
) can be rewritten as

\alpha_{0}(n-m)\sum_{i=0}^{s}t^{i}\otimes w_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}(t+\beta_{0}m)(t+m)^{i}\otimes w_{i}-\sum_{i=0}^{s}(t+\beta_{0}n)(t+n)^{i}\otimes w_{i}.

(5.3)

By comparing the coefficients of
t^{s}\otimes w_{s}
in the both sides of (
5.3
), we find that

\alpha_{0}(n-m)=(\beta_{0}+s)(n-m)
and so
\alpha_{0}=\beta_{0}+s\geq\beta_{0}
.
Replacing
\varphi
by
\varphi^{-1}
in the above discussion, we also get
\beta_{0}\geq\alpha_{0}
.
This gives
\beta_{0}=\alpha_{0}
and
s=0
. Particularly, we have

\varphi(1\otimes v)=1\otimes w_{0}.

Furthermore, for every
n\geq N_{v}
and
1\leq i\leq p-1
, we obtain

\beta_{i}\lambda^{n}(1\otimes w_{0})=I_{n}^{(i)}(1\otimes w_{0})=I_{n}^{(i)}\varphi(1\otimes v)=\varphi\left(I_{n}^{(i)}(1\otimes v)\right)=\alpha_{i}\lambda^{n}(1\otimes w_{0}).

This shows
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}
, as desired.

Finally, we show that
V\cong W
.
Let us define a linear map
\eta:V\longrightarrow W
by

\varphi(1\otimes w)=1\otimes\eta(w)\qquad\text{ for any }w\in V.

It is clear that
\eta
is injective.
For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
1\leq i\leq p-1
,
it follows from the equality
\varphi\left(I_{n}^{(i)}(1\otimes v)\right)=I_{n}^{(i)}\varphi(1\otimes v)
that

\alpha_{i}\lambda^{n}\varphi(1\otimes v)+\varphi\left(1\otimes I_{n}^{(i)}v\right)=I_{n}^{(i)}(1\otimes\eta(v))=\alpha_{i}\lambda^{n}(1\otimes\eta(v))+1\otimes I_{n}^{(i)}\eta(v).

This implies that
1\otimes\eta\left(I_{n}^{(i)}v\right)=\varphi\left(1\otimes I_{n}^{(i)}v\right)=1\otimes I_{n}^{(i)}\eta(v)
, and hence

\eta\left(I_{n}^{(i)}v\right)=I_{n}^{(i)}\eta(v)
.

Similarly, the equality
\varphi(L_{n}(1\otimes v))=L_{n}\varphi(1\otimes v)
for any
n\geq N_{v}
implies that

\varphi(t\otimes v)=t\otimes\eta(v).

Additionally, by applying the equality
\varphi(L_{n}(1\otimes v))=L_{n}\varphi(1\otimes v)
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\varphi(1\otimes L_{n}v)=1\otimes L_{n}\eta(v)
. Thus

\eta(L_{n}v)=L_{n}\eta(v)\qquad\text{ for any }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

This proves that
\eta
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism.
Since
\eta(V)\neq 0
and
W
is irreducible,
\eta
is an isomorphism.
We complete the proof.
∎

Note that Whittaker modules are restricted.
Then we have the following two corollaries by Proposition
4.2
, Theorem
3.13
,
Proposition
5.1

5.2
.

6.1.
Vertex Lie algebras
\mathcal{N}_{J}

Let

\mathcal{N}=\oplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathbb{C}T(m)\oplus(\oplus_{i=1}^{p-1}\mathbb{C}N_{i}(m))\right)\oplus\left(\oplus_{j=0}^{[\frac{p}{2}]}\mathbb{C}K_{j}(-1)\right)

be a Lie algebra,
where
K_{0}(-1),K_{1}(-1),\dots,K_{[\frac{p}{2}]}(-1)
are central and

\displaystyle[T(m),T(n)]=(m-n)T(m+n)+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)\delta_{m+n,0}K_{0}(-1);

(6.1)

\displaystyle[T(m),N_{i}(n)]=-nN_{i}(m+n),\quad[N_{i}(m),N_{j}(n)]=m\delta_{i+j,p}\delta_{m+n,0}K_{i}(-1),

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
i,j=1,\ldots,p-1
, and

K_{i}(-1):=K_{p-i}(-1)
if
i>[\frac{p}{2}]
.
For convenience, we also write
N_{0}(m)=T(m-1)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In terms of generating functions

N_{i}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{i}(m)z^{-m-1}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{i}(z)=K_{i}(-1)z^{0}\quad(i=0,1,\dots,p-1),

the Lie relations in (
6.1
) can be rewritten as follows:

\begin{split}[N_{0}(z),N_{0}(w)]&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}N_{0}(w)\right)z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)+2N_{0}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)+\frac{1}{12}K_{0}(w)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right)^{3}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\\
[N_{0}(z),N_{i}(w)]&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}N_{i}(w)\right)z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)+N_{i}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\\
[N_{i}(z),N_{j}(w)]&=\delta_{i+j,p}K_{i}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\end{split}

for all
i,j=1,2,\dots,p-1
.

Throughout this appendix, let
J
be a subset of
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
such that
p-i\in J
whenever
i\in J\setminus\{0\}
.
Denote by
\mathcal{N}_{J}
the subalgebra of
\mathcal{N}
with a basis

\left\{N_{j}(n),\ K_{i}(-1)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z},j\in J,i\in J\cap\left\{0,1,2,\dots,[\frac{p}{2}]\right\}\right\},

which is a vertex Lie algebra (see
[
4
, Definition 2.3]
).

Form the induced
\mathcal{N}_{J}
-module

M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}=\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{N}_{J})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}\left(\mathcal{N}^{+}_{J}\right)}\,\mathbb{C},

(6.2)

where
\mathbb{C}
denotes the one-dimensional trivial module for the algebra

\mathcal{N}_{J}^{+}=\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{N_{j}(n)\mid j\in J,n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

It is known from
[
11
,
31
]
that there is a unique vertex algebra structure on
M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}
such that
{\bm{1}}=1\otimes 1
is the vacuum vector and the vertex operators are given by

Y(N_{j}(-1){\bm{1}},z)=N_{j}(z),\quad Y(K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}},z)=K_{j}(z)\quad\text{for }j\in J.

Set

J^{*}=J\setminus\{0\},\quad J^{C}=\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}\setminus J\quad\text{and}\quad J_{0}=J\cap\left\{1,2,\dots,[\frac{p}{2}]\right\}.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
, and let
\underline{\ell}=(\ell_{j})_{j\in J^{*}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|J^{*}|}
be a
|J^{*}|
-tuple such that
\ell_{j}=\ell_{p-j}
for any
j\in J^{*}
.
If
0\notin J
, we write
I_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(\underline{\ell})
for
the ideal of
M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}
generated by the set

\{K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}-\ell_{j}{\bm{1}}\mid j\in J\},

and
form the quotient vertex algebra

M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(\underline{\ell})=M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}/I_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(\underline{\ell}).

Specially,
M_{\mathcal{N}_{\{j,p-j\}}}(\ell_{j})
is the Heisenberg vertex algebra of level
\ell_{j}
for every
j\in J^{*}
.
If
\ell_{j}\neq 0
, then
M_{\mathcal{N}_{\{j,p-j\}}}(\ell_{j})
is a simple vertex operator algebra
with a conformal vector

\omega_{j}(\ell_{j})=\frac{1}{2\ell_{j}}(N_{j}(-1)N_{p-j}(-1){\bm{1}}+N_{p-j}(-1)N_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}).

Moreover, if
J=J^{*}
(i.e.,
0\notin J
) and
\ell_{j}\neq 0
for all
j\in J
,
then
M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(\underline{\ell})
becomes a vertex operator algebra of central charge
|J|
with a conformal vector

\omega_{J}(\underline{\ell})=\sum_{j\in J}\frac{1}{2\ell_{j}}(N_{j}(-1)N_{p-j}(-1){\bm{1}}).

In fact, we have an identification

M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(\underline{\ell})=\otimes_{j\in J_{0}}M_{\mathcal{N}_{\{j,p-j\}}}(\ell_{j})

as vertex operator algebras such that

\omega_{J}(\underline{\ell})=\otimes_{j\in J_{0}}\omega_{j}(\ell_{j}).

If
0\in J
, we write
I_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(c,\underline{\ell})
for
the ideal of
M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}
generated by

\left\{K_{0}(-1){\bm{1}}-c{\bm{1}},\ K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}-\ell_{j}{\bm{1}}\mid j\in J^{*}\right\},

and
form the quotient vertex algebra

M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(c,\underline{\ell})=M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}/I_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}(c,\underline{\ell}).

In particular,
M_{\mathcal{N}_{\{0\}}}(c)
is the universal Virasoro vertex algebra of central charge
c
.
When
J=\{0,1,2,\dots,p-1\}
(i.e.,
\mathcal{N}_{J}=\mathcal{N}
), we have the quotient vertex algebra

M_{\mathcal{N}}(c,\underline{\ell})=M_{\mathcal{N}}/I_{\mathcal{N}}(c,\underline{\ell}).

Let
\bm{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{p-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{p-1}
be such that
\ell_{j}=\ell_{p-j}
for all
1\leq j\leq p-1
. Set

J_{\bm{\ell}}=\{1\leq j\leq p-1\mid\ell_{j}\neq 0\}\quad\text{and}\quad{\bm{\ell}}^{*}=(\ell_{j})_{j\in J_{\bm{\ell}}}\in\mathbb{C}^{|J_{\bm{\ell}}|}.

Due to the above discussion, we have the following straightforward result.

Proposition 6.1
.

For any
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}^{p-1}
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
we have the identification

M_{\mathcal{N}}(c,\bm{\ell})=M_{\mathcal{N}_{J_{\bm{\ell}}^{c}}}(c-|J_{\bm{\ell}}|,{\underline{0}})\otimes M_{\mathcal{N}_{J_{\bm{\ell}}}}(\bm{\ell}^{*})

(6.3)

of vertex algebras such that

\begin{split}N_{0}(-1){\bm{1}}&=N_{0}(-1){\bm{1}}\otimes\omega_{J_{\bm{\ell}}}({\bm{\ell}}^{*}),\\
N_{i}(-1){\bm{1}}&=N_{i}(-1){\bm{1}}\otimes{\bm{1}}\quad\text{if}\ i\notin J_{\bm{\ell}},\\
N_{i}(-1){\bm{1}}&={\bm{1}}\otimes N_{i}(-1){\bm{1}}\quad\text{if}\ i\in J_{\bm{\ell}},\end{split}

(6.4)

where
i=1,2,\dots,p-1
and
\underline{0}
is the
|J_{\bm{\ell}}^{C}|
-tuple with all components being zero.

6.2.
\sigma_{J}
-twist of
\mathcal{N}_{J}

Let
\sigma
be the Lie algebra automorphism of
\mathcal{N}
defined by

\sigma(N_{i}(m))=\xi^{i}N_{i}(m)\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(K_{i}(-1))=K_{i}(-1)

for
i=0,1,\dots,p-1
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
where
\xi
is a primitive
p
-th root of unity.
For any

and
{\bm{\ell}}=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{p-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{p-1}
,
there is an automorphism of the quotient vertex algebra
M_{\mathcal{N}}(c,\bm{\ell})
,
still denoted by
\sigma

\sigma(N_{j}(-1){\bm{1}})=\xi^{j}N_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}})=K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}=l_{j}\bm{1}\quad\text{for all }0\leq j\leq p-1,

where we have abused the symbol
\bm{1}
for the vacuum vector in
M_{\mathcal{N}}(c,\bm{\ell})
.

For any subset
J
of
\{0,1,2,\dots,p-1\}
, denote by
\sigma_{J}
the restriction of
\sigma
onto
\mathcal{N}_{J}
,
which preserves the subalgebra
\mathcal{N}_{J}^{+}
.
Thus there is a unique automorphism, still denoted by
\sigma_{J}
,
of the vertex algebra
M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}
such that

\sigma_{J}(N_{j}(-1){\bm{1}})=\xi^{j}N_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{J}(K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}})=K_{j}(-1){\bm{1}}\quad\text{for all }j\in J.

Furthermore, it induces an automorphism of the quotient vertex algebra

M_{\mathcal{N}_{J_{\bm{\ell}}^{c}}}(c-|J_{\bm{\ell}}|,{\underline{0}})
, denoted by
\sigma_{J_{\bm{\ell}}^{c}}
,
if

,
and an automorphism of

,

, if

.

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the identification in (
6.3
), we have

\sigma=\sigma_{J_{\bm{\ell}}^{c}}\otimes\sigma_{J_{\bm{\ell}}}.

as automorphisms of vertex algebras.

On the other hand, associated to the automorphism
\sigma

\sigma
-twist
\mathcal{N}(\sigma)
of
\mathcal{N}
from
[
4
]
.
Specifically,
\mathcal{N}(\sigma)
is a Lie algebra with a basis

\left\{\bar{N}_{i}(m+\frac{i}{p}),\bar{K}_{j}(-1)\mid m\in\mathbb{Z},i=0,1,\dots,p-1,j=0,1,\dots,[\frac{p}{2}]\right\},

and Lie relations (in terms of generating functions)

\begin{split}[\bar{N}_{0}(z),\bar{N}_{0}(w)]&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\bar{N}_{0}(w)\right)z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)+2\bar{N}_{0}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)+\frac{1}{12}\bar{K}_{0}(w)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right)^{3}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\\
[\bar{N}_{0}(z),\bar{N}_{i}(w)]&=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\bar{N}_{i}(w)\right)z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)+\bar{N}_{i}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}z^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\\
[\bar{N}_{i}(z),\bar{N}_{j}(w)]&=\delta_{i+j,p}\bar{K}_{i}(w)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}z^{-1}\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)^{\frac{i}{p}}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\end{split}

for
i,j=1,2,\dots,p-1
, where the generating functions are

\bar{N}_{i}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\bar{N}_{i}(m+\frac{i}{p})z^{-m-\frac{i}{p}-1},\quad\bar{K}_{i}(z)=\bar{K}_{i}(-1)z^{0},

for
i=0,1,\dots,p-1
and
\bar{K}_{i}(-1)=\bar{K}_{p-i}(-1)
if
i>[\frac{p}{2}]
.

Moreover, we have the
\sigma_{J}
-twist
\mathcal{N}_{J}(\sigma_{J})
of
\mathcal{N}_{J}
,
which is exactly the subalgebra of

spanned by

We say that an

-module

\bar{N}_{j}(m+\frac{j}{p}).w=0

Recall the vertex algebra
M_{\mathcal{N}_{J}}
from (

Proposition 6.3
.

Every (weak)
\sigma_{J}
-twisted

-module

is a restricted
\mathcal{N}_{J}(\sigma_{J})

\bar{N}_{i}(z)=Y_{W}(N_{i}(-1)\bm{1},z),\quad\bar{K}_{i}(z)=Y_{W}(K_{i}(-1)\bm{1},z)\quad\text{for any }i\in J.

On the other hand, every restricted

Let
U
be a vector space,
j=0,1,\dots,p-1

We split

a(z)_{+}:=\sum_{n<0}a(n)z^{-n-1}\ \quad\text{and}\quad a(z)_{-}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}a(n)z^{-n-1}.

6.3.
A generalization of Proposition
3.1

Let
\theta:\mathcal{N}(\sigma)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
be the linear map defined by

\bar{N}_{0}(m)\mapsto L_{m-1},\quad\bar{N}_{i}(m+\frac{i}{p})\mapsto I_{m}^{(i)},\quad\bar{K}_{j}(-1)\mapsto C_{j},

for

,

, and
j=0,1,\dots,[\frac{p}{2}]
.
The following result gives a realization of
\mathfrak{g}_{J}
as a twisted vertex Lie algebras whose proof is straightforward.

Lemma 6.5
.

The map
\theta
is a Lie algebra isomorphism. Furthermore,

\theta
induces an isomorphism

\theta_{J}=\theta|_{\mathcal{N}_{J}(\sigma_{J})}:\ \mathcal{N}_{J}(\sigma_{J})\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{J}

when

, and an isomorphism

\theta_{J}=\theta|_{\mathcal{N}_{J}(\sigma_{J})}:\ \mathcal{N}_{J}(\sigma_{J})\rightarrow\mathfrak{h}_{J}

when
J=J^{*}
.

For any nonzero
\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}^{p-1}
and

\mathfrak{g}
-modules of central charge
c
and of level
\bm{\ell}
.
This is a slightly generalized version of Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
\bm{\ell}=\{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{p-1}\}
be a nonzero
(p-1)
-tuple in
\mathbb{C}^{p-1}
such that
l_{j}=l_{p-j}
for all

, and

and

.
Let

be a restricted
\mathfrak{g}_{J^{c}_{\bm{\ell}}}
-module of central charge

and let

be a restricted
\mathfrak{h}_{J_{\bm{\ell}}}
-module of level
\bm{\ell}^{*}
.
Then

becomes a restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-module of central charge

and of level
\bm{\ell}
with action

\begin{split}L_{k}.(w\otimes v)&=(L_{k}.w)\otimes v+w\otimes(L^{*}_{k}.v),\\

(I_{k}^{(i)}.w)\otimes v\ &\text{if}\ i\notin J_{\bm{\ell}}\end{cases},\end{split}

,

,

, and

\displaystyle L^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J_{\bm{\ell}}}\frac{1}{2\ell_{j}}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}I_{m}^{(j)}I_{k-m-1}^{(p-j)}\right)\ \text{if}\ k\neq 0,

\displaystyle L^{*}_{0}

Furthermore, if both

-module

Let
\bm{\ell}
,

(6.6)

and

Y_{W}(K_{0}(-1),z)=c-|J_{\bm{\ell}}|,\quad Y_{W}(K_{i}(-1),z)=0\quad\text{for }i\in(J_{\bm{\ell}}^{c})^{*}.

(6.7)

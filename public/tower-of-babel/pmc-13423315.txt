Stage analysis of gastric cancer: a bioinformatic approach
Authors: Fatemeh Montazer, Mostafa Rezaei-Tavirani, Babak Arjmand, Nastaran Asri, Zahra Razzaghi, Farideh Razi, Fatemeh Bandarian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim: Analysis of stage transition in gastric cancer is the aim of this project. Background: Gastric cancer, as a lethal digestive cancer, is classified based on tumor, node, and metastasis. Early detection of gastric cancer facilitates cancer management and treatment.Methods: Data on gene expression changes at different stages of gastric cancer are extracted from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database and evaluated using the GEO2R program to identify significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs). The queried DEGs were enriched via Gene Ontology to explore dysregulated pathways. The crucial DEGs were identified via directed protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis. Results: Analyses revealed that stage IA → stage IIA and stage IB → stage IIB transitions in gastric cancer are accompanied by considerable numbers of significant DEGs. MYC, “Nitric oxide metabolism in cystic fibrosis”, and “Hepatitis C and hepatocellular carcinoma” are the critical dysregulate gene and pathways in the stage IB → stage IIB transition. ACTG2, MYH11, CDKN2A, MS4A2, and FCER1A were pointed out as the critical genes in the IA → IIA stage transition. Conclusion: In conclusion, inhibition of MYC was introduced as a crucial point in preventing the stage IB → stage IIB transition, and “Smooth muscle damage” was identified as a key process in stage IA → stage IIA transition. Experimental validation is recommended to find the applicable findings.Key Words: Gastric cancer, Staging, Gene, Pathway, Network analysis

Introduction

Gastric cancer is known as a lethal digestive cancer; a 5-year survival rate of 95% for early gastric cancer treated via application of neoadjuvant chemotherapy is recorded in the literature. Since, disease in many patients detects in advanced stage, surgical resection is the radical treatment (1). Gastric tumor classification based on tumor, node, metastasis (TNM) was published in 1974. This organization spread widely and was developed. In this arrangement after modifications; tumor invades lamina propria or submucosa (T1a and T1b), tumor invades muscularis propria or subserosa (T2 and T3), tumor penetrates the serosa without invasion of adjacent structures (T4a), tumor invades adjacent structures (T4b), metastasis in 1 to 2 regional lymph nodes (N1), metastasis in 4 to 6 lymph nodes (N2), metastasis in seven to 15 regional lymph nodes (N3a), metastasis in more than 15 regional lymph nodes (N3b), no distant metastasis (M0), distant metastasis (M1) are introduced. Based on this classification, stages I to III are characterized by M0. Therefore stages I to III, considering M0, are appeared as IA; (T1, N0), IB; (T1, N1) and (T2, N0), IIA; (T1, N2), (T2, N1), and (T3, N0), IIB; (T1, N3), (T2, N2), (T3, N1), and (T4a, N0), IIIA; (T2, N3), (T3, N2), and (T4a, N1), IIIB; (T3, N3), (T4a, N2), and (T4b, N0, N1) (2-4).

Since, many cancers are explored at the advanced stages and face limited therapeutic options and poor prognosis, early detection of cancers is essential to enhance the survival of patients. However, this advantage may be accompanied by challenges such as overtreatment and overdiagnosis of people with not advanced obvious malignancy (5). It can be predicted that, progress in molecular methods, contrast agents, and artificial intelligence will improve early detection of cancers in the near future (6). Early detection and classification of cancers using gene expression and machine learning is recorded in the literature (7, 8). Our investigation about breast cancer diagnosis via development of a gene expression classifier was published recently (9). Gene ontology assessment as a useful method is applied to detect the role of the studied genes (10). Transition of grade II to grade III astrocytoma is studied via a protein-protein interaction network to find the key genes which promote the process (11). In the present study, state transition of gastric cancer from stage I → II via pathway analysis using data from the GEO database is investigated to find the possible critical drug targets.

Methods

Data collection and pre-evaluation

Gastric cancer was searched in the GEO database, and GSE236522, among other gene sets, was selected to be analyzed. Gene expression profiles of four patients with stage IIB (GSM7549479, GSM7549489, GSM7549504, and GSM7549507) versus the profiles of three individuals in stage IB (GSM7549485, GSM7549490, and GSM7549506) were considered for more analysis. Data were assessed via the GEO2R program to find the related DEGs and possible discrimination of the compared samples. A similar analysis was done for patient samples with stage IIIB (GSM7549478, GSM7549482, GSM7549486, GSM7549487, GSM7549493, GSM7549497, and GSM7549505) versus individuals in stage IIB (GSM7549479, GSM7549489, GSM7549504, and GSM7549507). Transitions between stage IA → stage IIA and stage IIA → stage IIIA for samples of patients with gastric cancer were analyzed as like the described assessments for stages IB, IIB, and IIIB. Gene expression profiles of GSM7549481, GSM7549483, GSM7549494, and GSM7549496 from IA stage, GSM7549484, GSM7549492, GSM7549495, GSM7549501, and GSM7549502 related to IIA stage, and GSM7549477, GSM7549488, GSM7549491, GSM7549498, GSM7549500, GSM7549501, GSM7549502 associated to stage IIIA were selected for analyses. Comparison between the analyzed samples was evaluated using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) via the GEO2R program. Finally, 3, 4, 7, 4, 5, and 7 gene expression profiles related to the IB, IIB, IIB, IA, IIA, and IIA stages of cancer were analyzed, respectively. The significant DEGs were selected based on adjusted p-value (Padj) < 0.05.

Gene ontology assessment

The selected genes were enriched via Gene Ontology to explore the related pathways by using the ClueGO application of Cytoscape software. The pathways were discovered from KEGG (25.05.2022), Wiki Pathways 23.02.2022, and REACTOME Pathways 25.05.2022. To find the critical genes, the individual which were associated to less than two groups of pathways were ignored from further investigation. The larger groups of pathways were considered as the major individuals and were discussed in more detail.

Directed PPI network analysis

The significant DEGs were assessed via the CluePedia plugin of Cytoscape software to find relationships between the queried genes via directed PPI network analysis. Activation, inhibition, expression, binding, catalysis, reaction, and post-translational modification actions were considered to form the regulatory PPI network.

Results

Pre-evaluation analysis

Findings indicate that samples of patients with stage IIB are discriminated from individuals with stage IB by 82 DEGs. UMAP plot for the analysis is presented in Figure 1. After cleaning the data and removing the uncharacterized genes and microRNAs, 71 significant DEGs were identified for more evaluation. It was demonstrated that two genes (NPTX1 and ARTN, with log2 (fold change) values of 6.989 and 3.224, respectively) discriminate gastric cancer samples in stage IIIB from stage IIB individuals (Figure 2).

Discussion

Based on the literature, ideal administration of stage II/III gastric cancer needs multidisciplinary care, often requiring treatment at more than one capability (12). Endoscopic resection is introduced as the foremost treatment for early gastric cancer. Investigation indicates that adjuvant chemotherapy improves survival in patients with stage 1B gastric cancer (13). As depicted in Figures 1 – 4, stage transitions IA → IIA, IIA → IIIA, IB→ IIB, and IIB→ IIIB are accompanied by gene expression changes. However, analysis demonstrated that considerable gene expression changes occur for stage transitions IA → IIA and IB→ IIB. Stages IIB and IIA were discriminated from stages IB and IA by 82 and 60, respectively. Pathway analysis (see Figures 5 and 6) showed that MYC, “Nitric oxide metabolism in cystic fibrosis”, and “Hepatitis C and hepatocellular carcinoma” are the critical genes and pathways that differentiate stage IIB from stage IB in gastric cancer. MYC proto-oncogene, which is up-regulated in the IB → IIB transition stage, is highlighted as a frequently altered gene in human cancers and is introduced as an attractive therapeutic target (14). There are many documents about the inhibition of MYC as an efficient therapeutic strategy in cancer treatment (15, 16). As highlighted in the literature, “Nitric oxide metabolism” regulation can improve cancer therapy (17, 18). It is reported that infection with the hepatitis C virus leads to chronic hepatitis, acute hepatitis, hepatocellular carcinoma, and cirrhosis. Cell cycle regulation, cell proliferation, transcriptional regulation, and apoptosis are highlighted as the targeted processes by hepatitis C virus infection. It is explored that hepatitis C virus-related hepatocellular carcinoma is associated with advanced liver fibrosis in the liver background and severe inflammation (19). It seems essential biological and cellular processes are dysregulated in the IB → IIB stage transition of gastric cancer.

As mentioned, 60 significant DEGs were associated with the IA → IIA stage transition, while the IB → IIB stage transition was characterized by 82 DEGs. In the introduction, it was described that the IIB stage is more advanced than the IIA stage; this fact appears to be reflected in the number of related DEGs of both IA → IIA and IB → IIB stage transitions (60 versus 82, respectively). As depicted in Figures 7 and 8, ACTG2, MYH11, CDKN2A, MS4A2, and FCER1A can be considered as the crucial genes related to the IA → IIA stage transition in gastric cancer. It is pointed out that alteration of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2A (CDKN2A) (that is up-regulated in this study) is a possible biomarker to indicate patients for immune inhibitor treatment in gastric cancer (20). The important role of actin gamma 2 (ACTG2) in prognosis and cellular biological behaviors of gastric cancer is highlighted by Shuai Y et al (21). The third crucial gene is myosin heavy chain 11 (MYH11), which is down-regulated in the IA → IIA stage transition in gastric cancer. Down-regulation of this gene in gastric cancer is reported by He F. et al (22). As shown in Figure 8, these three genes are included the same sub-network. Connections between both MS4A2 and FCER1A and the “Role of LAT2/NTAL/LAB on “calcium mobilization” pathway are illustrated in Figure 7. Based on the investigation of Gélébart P et al, mobilization of calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum into the cytosol is a crucial part of several signaling networks that are involved in differentiation, growth, and apoptosis of the tumor cells. This finding indicates that calcium homeostasis is altered in gastrointestinal adenocarcinomas (23).

Conclusion

Transition from stage I to stage II in gastric cancer is accompanied by a larger number of significant DEGs relative to stage II to stage III. Up-regulation of MYC is a crucial point in stage IB → stage IIB transition. It seems inhibition of MYC is a key feature of early gastric cancer treatment. It can be concluded that, smooth muscle damage is a key process in the stage IA → stage IIA transition. Downregulation of myosin heavy chain 11 (MYH11), which is down-regulated, may play a crucial role in this regard. ACTG2, MYH11, CDKN2A, MS4A2, and FCER1A were pointed out as possible targets in the stage IA → stage IIA transition. Experimental data can improve these findings. However, the document search revealed the validity of the introduced DEGs.

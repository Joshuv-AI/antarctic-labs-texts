Weinstock inequality in hyperbolic space
Pingxin Gu, Haizhong Li, Yao Wan

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish the Weinstock inequality for the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue on star-shaped mean convex domains in hyperbolic space ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} for n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . In particular, when the domain is convex, our result gives an affirmative answer to Open Question 4.27 in [ 7 ] for the hyperbolic space ℍ n \mathbb{H}^{n} when n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 .

53C21; 35P15; 58C40

1.
Introduction

One of the oldest topics in spectral geometry is shape optimization. We focus on the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Steklov eigenvalue problem introduced by Steklov in the early
20
th century. We refer readers to the notable surveys
[
14
,
7
]
.

Let
\Omega
be a compact Riemannian manifold with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
. The Steklov eigenvalue problem on
\Omega
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u=0,&\text{in $\Omega$},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\sigma(\Omega)u,&\text{on $\partial\Omega$},\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator on
\Omega
, and
\nu
is the outward unit normal of
\partial\Omega
. We may compute the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue of
\Omega
by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)=\min\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dv}{\int_{\partial\Omega}u^{2}d\mu}:\ u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\},\int_{\partial\Omega}ud\mu=0\right\},

where
dv
is the volume element of
\Omega
and
d\mu
is the area element of
\partial\Omega
.

The earliest isoperimetric type inequality on
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
is the well-known Weinstock’s inequality, which states that among simply-connected planar domains
\Omega
with a fixed perimeter
|\partial\Omega|
,
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
is maximized by a disk, see
[
21
,
22
]
. It is also possible to maximize
\sigma_{1}(\Omega)
by fixing the volume
|\Omega|
, see
[
8
]
, and
[
5
]
for higher dimensions, where the connectedness assumption can be removed in both cases.

The simply-connected assumptions in Weinstock inequality cannot be removed, since the result fails for appropriate spherical shell
\Omega_{\epsilon}=B(1)\backslash\overline{B(\epsilon)}
(
B(r)
denotes the geodesic ball of radius
r
centered at the origin), see for example
[
14
]
. In higher dimensions
n\geq 3
, even for the class of contractible domains, the ball is not a maximizer, see
[
10
]
. However, when considering convex domains, using the inverse mean curvature flow (IMCF), Bucur, Ferone, Nitsch and Trombetti
[
6
]
proved that

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)|\partial\Omega|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq\sigma_{1}(B)|\partial B|^{\frac{1}{n-1}},

where
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a ball, and equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a ball.

Combining IMCF with elementary methods, Kwong and Wei
[
17
]
proved sharp geometric inequalities involving three quantities, and extended Theorem
1.1
to a broader class of star-shaped mean convex domains. It is then natural to ask whether such a Weinstock type inequality holds in hyperbolic space, specifically

Conjecture 1

(
[
7
]
, Open Question 4.27)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Let
\Omega^{*}
be a geodesic ball with
|\partial\Omega^{*}|=|\partial\Omega|
. Is it true that

(1.3)

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{1}(\Omega^{*})

and equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball?

For
n=2
, the conjecture holds, as the Weinstock inequality is valid for the hyperbolic plane and, more generally, for compact surfaces, see
[
9
]
. There are also many researches devoted in estimating general eigenvalues on general surfaces, see also
[
13
,
16
,
23
,
15
]
.

For higher dimensions, to the authors knowledge, the conjecture remains open. But if we turn to the case of fixing volume
|\Omega|
, then the geodesic ball is maximizer, see
[
3
]
. When
\Omega
is convex, this result can be a corollary of Conjecture
1
by means of isoperimetric inequality, see Remark
2.2
.

In this paper, we prove the Weinstock inequality for smooth domains with star-shaped mean convex boundary in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
for general dimension
n\geq 4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 4
,
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with star-shaped mean convex boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
\Omega^{\ast}
be a geodesic ball with
|\partial\Omega^{*}|=|\partial\Omega|
. Then

(1.4)

\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\sigma_{1}(\Omega^{\ast})

and equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.

In particular, when the domain is convex, we provide an affirmative answer to Conjecture
1
for
n\geq 4
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Conjecture
1
is true for
n\geq 4
.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
I_{1}(t)=|\partial B(t)|
and
J(t)=\sigma_{1}(B(t))
for
t>0
. Then (
1.4
) is equivalent to the following isoperimetric type inequality

(1.5)

\frac{\sigma_{1}(\Omega)}{J\circ I_{1}^{-1}(|\partial\Omega|)}\leq\left.\frac{\sigma_{1}(\Omega)}{J\circ I_{1}^{-1}(|\partial\Omega|)}\right|_{\text{$\Omega$ is a geodesic ball}}=1.

Our method is inspired by the use of IMCF as presented in
[
17
]
and by the well-known mass transplantation argument, as discussed in
[
11
]
. The application of IMCF is not straightforward, as we must introduce a special auxiliary function
h
in (
4.1
). The evolution of this function under IMCF establishes a useful geometric inequality involving
\int_{\partial\Omega}g(r)^{2}d\mu
. By the same estimate, as a byproduct, we can extend Verma’s isoperimetric inequality for the harmonic mean of the first
(n-1)
non-zero Steklov eigenvalues
[
19
]
with prescribed area. We state it as the following Corollary
1.5
, which can lead to Theorem
1.2
again.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 4
,
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with star-shaped mean convex boundary
\partial\Omega
, and
\Omega^{\ast}
be a geodesic ball with
|\partial\Omega^{*}|=|\partial\Omega|
. Then

(1.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}(\Omega)}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}(\Omega^{\ast})}

and equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we present basic properties of hyperbolic spaces, including the isoperimetric inequality and the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue of geodesic balls. We also state the well-known theorems for center of mass and mass transplantation. In section
3
, we discuss the detailed properties of the volume-area ratio function
g
. In section
4
, we introduce the key auxiliary function
h
, get the estimate using inverse mean curvature flow, and prove Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

We use the warped product to illustrate the hyperbolic space
(\mathbb{H}^{n},g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})=[0,\infty)\times_{\lambda(r)}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, where
\lambda(r)=\sinh r
. That is, after choosing an origin
O
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the Riemannian metric
g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined by

g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}=dr^{2}+\lambda^{2}(r)g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},

where
r
denotes the geodesic distance from
O
, and
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
is the canonical metric on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We will also use the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
to denote
g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
.

2.1.
The first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue of geodesic balls

For
r>0
, a geodesic ball
B(r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
centered at
O
with radius
r
is given by
\{(s,\theta)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\ 0\leq s\leq r\}\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
, and its boundary is the geodesic sphere
S(r)=\partial B(r)=\{(s,\theta)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\ s=r\}
. We can directly compute the volume and area of
B(r)
as

(2.1)

\displaystyle|B(r)|=\int_{0}^{r}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dtd\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=\omega_{n-1}\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dt

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle|S(r)|=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lambda^{n-1}(r)d\mu_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=\omega_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}(r),

where
\omega_{n-1}
denotes the area of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

It is remarkable that the geodesic ball minimizes the area among bounded domains with the same volume, which is known as the isoperimetric inequality in hyperbolic space.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
B(R)
be a geodesic ball with the same volume as
\Omega
. Then the area of
\Omega
satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\partial\Omega|\geq|\partial B(R)|.

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball.

Remark 2.2
.

As noted in Remark
1.4
, if we additionally denote
I_{0}(t)=|B(t)|
for
t>0
, then (
2.3
) is equivalent to the following inequality

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|}{I_{0}\circ I_{1}^{-1}(|\partial\Omega|)}\leq\left.\frac{|\Omega|}{I_{0}\circ I_{1}^{-1}(|\partial\Omega|)}\right|_{\text{$\Omega$ is a geodesic ball}}=1.

Combining this with (
1.5
), we deduce

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}(\Omega)}{J\circ I_{0}^{-1}(|\Omega|)}\leq\left.\frac{\sigma_{1}(\Omega)}{J\circ I_{0}^{-1}(|\Omega|)}\right|_{\text{$\Omega$ is a geodesic ball}}=1.

An important function in hyperbolic space is the geodesic ball’s volume-area ratio function
g
, defined as
g(r):=\frac{|B(r)|}{|S(r)|}
, i.e.

(2.6)

\displaystyle g(r)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{n-1}(r)}\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dt.

Using the standard method of separation of variables, the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue of a geodesic ball is computed as follows:

Proposition 2.3

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
B(r)\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a geodesic ball centered at
O
with boundary
S(r)
. Then the first non-zero eigenvalue
\sigma_{1}(B(r))
of the Steklov eigenvalue problem (
1.1
) on
B(r)
is given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(B(r))=\frac{\int_{B(r)}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}})dv}{g(r)^{2}|S(r)|}.

2.2.
Center of mass

In order to study the upper bound of the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue not only for geodesic balls but also for general domains, we need the following center of mass theorem.

In
[
1
]
, Aithal and Santhanam give a theorem for complete Riemannian manifold
M
, which also applies to measurable subset
A
in hyperbolic space. Let
CA
denote the convex hull of
A
. Let
\exp_{q}:T_{q}M\to M
be the exponential map and let
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a system of normal coordinates centered at
q
. We identify
CA
with
\exp_{q}^{-1}(CA)
for each
q\in CA
, and denote
(g_{\mathbb{H}^{n}})_{q}(X,X)
as
\|X\|_{q}^{2}
for
X\in T_{q}M
. The theorem of center of mass with respect to the mass distribution function
G
is stated as

Theorem 2.4

(
[
3
]
)
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be a measurable subset and
G
be a continuous function on
[0,\infty)
that is positive on
(0,\infty)
. Then there exists a point
p\in CA
such that

\int_{A}G(\|X\|_{p})Xdv=0,

where
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a normal coordinate system centered at
p
.

By applying the center of mass theorem for
A=\partial\Omega
with respect to
G=g
and choosing test functions
g\frac{x_{i}}{r}
in the Rayleigh quotient (
1.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\int_{\partial\Omega}g^{2}\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{r^{2}}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(g\frac{x_{i}}{r})|^{2}dv,\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

Summing over
i
, we derive the upper bound of the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue via
g
. For further details, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
19
]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with smooth boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
. Then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\int_{\Sigma}g^{2}d\mu\leq\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}})dv.

2.3.
Mass transplantation

Finally, we state the mass transplantation argument due to Weinberger
[
20
]
, which is a powerful tool for estimates involving integrals. A clear proof can be seen in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 2.6

(Mass transplantation)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with the same volume as
B(R)
. If
f(r)
is decreasing and integrable on
[0,+\infty)
, then

(2.9)

\int_{\Omega}f(r)dv\leq\int_{B(R)}f(r)dv.

If in addition
f(r)
is strictly decreasing, then equality holds if and only if
\Omega=B(R)
. If
f(r)
is increasing and integrable on
[0,+\infty)
, then the inequality reverses direction.

Proof.

Since
f
is decreasing and
|\Omega|=|B(R)|
, we have

(2.10)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}f(r)dv=&\int_{\Omega\cap B(R)}f(r)dv+\int_{\Omega\backslash B(R)}f(r)dv\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega\cap B(R)}f(r)dv+|\Omega\backslash B(R)|f(R)\\
=&\int_{\Omega\cap B(R)}f(r)dv+|B(R)\backslash\Omega|f(R)\\
\leq&\int_{\Omega\cap B(R)}f(r)dv+\int_{B(R)\backslash\Omega}f(r)dv=\int_{B(R)}f(r)dv.\end{split}

It suffices to show that the inequality (
2.9
) is strict when
f(r)
is strictly decreasing. If
\Omega\nsubseteq B(R)
, then
\Omega
contains a point at radius
r>R
and thus contains a neighborhood outside
B(R)
. Consequently,
|\Omega\backslash B(R)|>0
, and the first inequality in (
2.10
) is strict because
f(r)
is strictly decreasing. Similarly, if
B(R)\nsubseteq\Omega
, the second inequality in (
2.10
) is strict. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.6
.
∎

3.
The volume-area ratio function
g(r)

3.1.
The geometric properties of
g(r)

By using the expression for
g(r)
given in (
2.6
), we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\lambda^{n-1}(r)g(r))^{\prime}=(\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dt)^{\prime}=\lambda^{n-1}(r),

and then

(3.2)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}=1-(n-1)\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
Y:=g(r)\partial_{r}
be a vector field. Then

(3.3)

\mathrm{div}(Y)=1.

Proof.

For each
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, choose an orthonormal frame
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
around
p
such that
e_{n}=\partial_{r}
. Recall the vector field
V:=\lambda(r)\partial_{r}
is a conformal Killing field on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}V,e_{j}\rangle=\lambda^{\prime}(r)\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle,

see e.g.
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
. Then it follows from (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(Y)=

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\frac{g}{\lambda}V)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}(\frac{g}{\lambda}V),e_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{g}{\lambda})^{\prime}\lambda+n(\frac{g}{\lambda})\lambda^{\prime}=g^{\prime}+(n-1)\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}=1.

∎

A direct corollary follows from Proposition
3.1
and the divergence theorem.

Corollary 3.2
.

We have the following inequality

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}gd\mu\geq|\Omega|.

Proof.

Since
\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle\leq 1
and
g\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|\Omega|=\int_{\Omega}dv=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}(g(r)\partial_{r})dv=\int_{\Sigma}g\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle d\mu\leq\int_{\Sigma}gd\mu.

∎

Similarly, the following Proposition follows just from the divergence theorem and the mass transplantation, as shown in
[
3
, Lemma 3.3]
. For the sake of completeness, let us give a proof.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
B(R)
be a geodesic ball in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with the same volume as
\Omega
, then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g^{2}d\mu\geq g(R)|B(R)|.

Proof.

First, it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(g^{2}\partial_{r})=\mathrm{div}(\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda}V)=(\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda})^{\prime}\lambda+n(\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda})\lambda^{\prime}=g(1+g^{\prime}),

and then

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g^{2}d\mu\geq\int_{\Sigma}g^{2}\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle d\mu=\int_{\Omega}g(1+g^{\prime})dv.

Next, we demonstrate the monotonicity of
g(1+g^{\prime})
as follows:

\displaystyle(g(1+g^{\prime}))^{\prime}=

\displaystyle g^{\prime}+(g^{\prime})^{2}+gg^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\prime}+(g^{\prime})^{2}+g((n-1)\frac{g}{\lambda^{2}}-(n-1)\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(1-(n-1)\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})+(g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}\geq 0.

Using the mass transplantation argument, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}g(1+g^{\prime})dv

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(R)}g(1+g^{\prime})dv=\omega_{n-1}\int_{0}^{R}g(1+g^{\prime})\lambda^{n-1}dt

\displaystyle=\omega_{n-1}\int_{0}^{R}(\lambda^{n-1}g^{2})^{\prime}dt=\omega_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1}(R)g^{2}(R)

\displaystyle=g(R)|B(R)|.

This complete the proof of (
3.6
).
∎

3.2.
The analytic properties of
g(r)

The following properties are essential in our proof.

Proposition 3.4
.

The function
g
is an increasing concave function, with the following limits:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{g}{\lambda}=\frac{1}{n},\qquad\lim_{r\to\infty}g=\frac{1}{n-1},\qquad\lim_{r\to 0}g^{\prime}=\frac{1}{n},\qquad\lim_{r\to\infty}g^{\prime}=0.

Furthermore, if
n\geq 4
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\lambda^{2}g^{\prime}=\frac{1}{n-3}.

Proof.

First, since
\left.\lambda^{n-1}(g-\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{\prime}})\right|_{r=0}=0
and

\displaystyle(\lambda^{n-1}(g-\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{\prime}}))^{\prime}=\lambda^{n-1}-\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{n\lambda^{n-1}(\lambda^{\prime})^{2}-\lambda^{n+1}}{(\lambda^{\prime})^{2}}\leq 0,

we deduce

\displaystyle g\leq\frac{1}{n-1}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{\prime}},

and then

\displaystyle g^{\prime}=1-(n-1)\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}\geq 0.

Furthermore, since
\left.\lambda^{n-1}(g-\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{(n-1)\lambda^{2}+n})\right|_{r=0}=0
and

\displaystyle(\lambda^{n-1}(g-\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{(n-1)\lambda^{2}+n}))^{\prime}=\lambda^{n-1}-\lambda^{n-1}\frac{(n-1)^{2}\lambda^{4}+(2n^{2}-2n+1)\lambda^{2}+n^{2}}{((n-1)\lambda^{2}+n)^{2}}\leq 0,

we have

\displaystyle g\leq\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{(n-1)\lambda^{2}+n},

which implies

\displaystyle g^{\prime\prime}=(n-1)\frac{g}{\lambda^{2}}-(n-1)g^{\prime}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}=\frac{n-1}{\lambda^{2}}(g((n-1)\lambda^{2}+n)-\lambda\lambda^{\prime})\leq 0.

Finally, the limits follow from l’Hôpitcal rule:

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{g}{\lambda}=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dt}{\lambda^{n}(r)}=\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\lambda^{n-1}(r)}{n\lambda^{n-1}(r)\lambda^{\prime}(r)}=\frac{1}{n},

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}g=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dt}{\lambda^{n-1}(r)}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n-1}(r)}{(n-1)\lambda^{n-2}(r)\lambda^{\prime}(r)}=\frac{1}{n-1},

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}g^{\prime}=

\displaystyle 1-(n-1)\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}=1-(n-1)\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}(t)dt}{\lambda^{n}(r)}=1-\frac{n-1}{n}=\frac{1}{n},

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}g^{\prime}=

\displaystyle 1-(n-1)\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}=1-(n-1)\cdot\frac{1}{n-1}=0,

and if
n\geq 4
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\lambda^{2}g^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n}-(n-1)\lambda^{\prime}\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}}{\lambda^{n-2}}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\frac{\lambda^{n}}{\lambda^{\prime}}-(n-1)\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{n-1}}{\lambda^{n-3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\frac{(n-1)\lambda^{n+1}+n\lambda^{n-1}}{\lambda^{2}+1}-(n-1)\lambda^{n-1}}{(n-3)\lambda^{n-4}\lambda^{\prime}}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{n-1}}{(n-3)\lambda^{n-4}\lambda^{\prime}(\lambda^{2}+1)}=\frac{1}{n-3}.

∎

Since
\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}=\frac{1}{n-1}(1-g^{\prime})
, Proposition
3.4
immediately yields the following proposition.

Proposition 3.5
.

The function

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(r)g(r)}{\lambda(r)}

is increasing, and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(r)g(r)}{\lambda(r)}\leq\frac{1}{n-1}.

On the other hand, we have

Proposition 3.6
.

The function

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(r)\lambda^{\prime}(r)g^{\prime}(r)}{g(r)}

is increasing, and if
n\geq 4
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(r)\lambda^{\prime}(r)g^{\prime}(r)}{g(r)}\leq\frac{n-1}{n-3}.

Proof.

First, we show that
\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}
is increasing. Note that

\displaystyle(\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g})^{\prime}=(\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{g}-(n-1)(\lambda^{2}+1))^{\prime}=\frac{((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n)g-\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{g^{2}}-2(n-1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}.

It suffices to show that

\displaystyle((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n)g-\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\geq 2(n-1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{2},

which is equivalent to

(g-\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{2}+n}{4(n-1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}})^{2}\leq\frac{(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{4}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda^{2}+n^{2}}{(4(n-1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime})^{2}}.

For
n=2
, direct calculation shows that
\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}=\lambda^{\prime}
is increasing. From now on, we focus on the case
n\geq 3
. By Proposition
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle g\leq\frac{\lambda}{(n-1)\lambda^{\prime}}\leq\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{2}+n}{4(n-1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}.

Thus we need only to prove that

\displaystyle g\geq\frac{(n+1)\lambda^{2}+n-\sqrt{(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{4}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda^{2}+n^{2}}}{4(n-1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}.

For convenience, denote

\displaystyle A=\sqrt{(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{4}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda^{2}+n^{2}},

then
A^{\prime}=\frac{2(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{3}\lambda^{\prime}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{A}
. It suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lambda^{n-1}g\geq\frac{2\lambda^{n}\lambda^{\prime}}{(n+1)\lambda^{2}+n+A}.

Equality holds at
r=0
. By taking a derivative, we need only to prove that

\displaystyle\lambda^{n-1}\geq\frac{2((n+1)\lambda^{n+1}+n\lambda^{n-1})((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n+A)-2\lambda^{n}\lambda^{\prime}(2(n+1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}+A^{\prime})}{((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n+A)^{2}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n+A)^{2}\geq 2((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n)((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n+A)-2\lambda\lambda^{\prime}(2(n+1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}+A^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle 2\lambda\lambda^{\prime}(2(n+1)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}+\frac{2(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{3}\lambda^{\prime}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda\lambda^{\prime}}{A})\geq((n+1)\lambda^{2}+n)^{2}-A^{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle 2\lambda(2(n+1)\lambda+\frac{2(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{3}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda}{A})(\lambda^{2}+1)\geq 8(n-1)\lambda^{2}(\lambda^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(n-3)^{2}\lambda^{2}+(n^{2}-3n+4)\geq(n-3)A.

By squaring both sides of the above inequality, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle(n-3)^{4}\lambda^{4}+2(n-3)^{2}(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda^{2}+(n^{2}-3n+4)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n-3)^{2}((n-3)^{2}\lambda^{4}+2(n^{2}-3n+4)\lambda^{2}+n^{2}),

which is clearly correct. Therefore, the function
\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}
is increasing.

Moreover, it follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) that

\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}\leq\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}=\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\lambda^{2}g^{\prime}}{g}=\frac{n-1}{n-3}.

∎

By Proposition
3.6
, the function
\frac{\lambda^{2}g^{\prime}}{g}=\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{\prime}}
is a product of two positive increasing functions. Hence we have

Proposition 3.7
.

The function

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{2}(r)g^{\prime}(r)}{g(r)}

is increasing.

Notice that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(gg^{\prime}\partial_{r})=(g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.

Then the expression (
2.7
) for
\sigma_{1}(B(r))
can be rewritten as

(3.10)

\sigma_{1}(B(r))=\frac{\int_{S(r)}gg^{\prime}d\mu}{g(r)^{2}|S(r)|}=\frac{g^{\prime}(r)}{g(r)}.

Combining Proposition
3.7
with (
2.2
) and (
3.10
), we obtain the following key lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

The quantities

(3.11)

\displaystyle|S(r)|^{\frac{2}{n-1}}\sigma_{1}(B(r))

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle|S(r)|\sigma_{1}(B(r))

are both increasing functions of the radius
r
.

4.
Weinstock inequality in Hyperbolic space

From now on, let
R
be the positive number such that
|B(R)|=|\Omega|
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

4.1.
The auxiliary function
h

Since
\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv=\omega_{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{n-2}\lambda^{\prime}g(s)ds
is an integral that increases from zero to infinity, we can define an auxiliary function
h:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
as follows:

(4.1)

\displaystyle h(\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)=\frac{1}{|S(t)|}.

The following properties hold for the function
h
.

Lemma 4.1
.

The function
h
is decreasing and log-convex, such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{h^{\prime}(\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)}{h(\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)}=-\frac{n-1}{|B(t)|}.

Proof.

Since
\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv
is an increasing function of
t
, while
\frac{1}{|S(t)|}
is a decreasing function of
t
, we can conclude that
h
is a decreasing function.

Next, differentiating both sides of (
4.1
) yields

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)|S(t)|\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(t)g(t)}{\lambda(t)}=-\frac{(n-1)\lambda^{\prime}(t)}{|S(t)|\lambda(t)}.

Combining this with
g(t)=\frac{|B(t)|}{|S(t)|}
, we obtain (
4.2
).

Finally, the log-convexity of
h
follows from the fact that
-\frac{n-1}{|B(t)|}
is an increasing function of
t
, and
\int_{B(t)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv
is also an increasing function of
t
.

∎

4.2.
An inequality from the IMCF

Suppose
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with star-shaped mean convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
. Then
\Sigma
can be written as a graph over
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, i.e. there exists a smooth embedding
X_{0}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
with
X_{0}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})=\Sigma
.

We evolve
\Sigma
along the IMCF
X:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
solving the equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\frac{1}{H}\nu,\\
X(\cdot,0)=X_{0}(\cdot),\end{array}\right.

where
\nu
is the outward unit normal vector field of the flow hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},t)
and
H
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
.

Now we consider the following monotone quantity along IMCF.

Proposition 4.2
.

Along IMCF, the quantity

A(t):=|\Sigma_{t}|^{-1}\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}gd\mu_{t}}{|\Omega_{t}|h(\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv_{t})}

is decreasing, i.e.
A^{\prime}(t)\leq 0
, and
A^{\prime}(t)=0
if and only if
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere centered at the origin.

Proof.

Firstly, along IMCF, the area element
d\mu_{t}
evolves by

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}d\mu_{t}=d\mu_{t},

and the co-area formula implies

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv_{t}=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda H}d\mu_{t}.

Assume that
R_{t}
satisfies
|\Omega_{t}|=|B(R_{t})|
. For simplicity, we omit the subscript
t
for
R_{t}
,
\Omega_{t}
and
\Sigma_{t}
and omit the area element
d\mu_{t}
and volume element
dv_{t}
in each integrations. Then along ICMF, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g+\int_{\Sigma}\frac{g^{\prime}\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle}{H}}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}-\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g(h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{H}+|\Omega|h^{\prime}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda H})}{|\Omega|^{2}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}+\frac{\int_{\Sigma}\frac{g^{\prime}}{H}}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}-\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g(\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{H}+|\Omega|\frac{h^{\prime}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}{h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda H})}{|\Omega|^{2}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}.

Since
\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}
is increasing by Proposition
3.5
, the mass transplantation Theorem
2.6
yields

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv\geq\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv.

Furthermore, combining this with the log-convexity of
h
and (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{h^{\prime}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}{h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}\geq\frac{h^{\prime}(\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}{h(\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}=-\frac{n-1}{|B(R)|}=-\frac{n-1}{|\Omega|}.

Thus it follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}+\frac{\int_{\Sigma}\frac{g^{\prime}}{H}}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}-\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g\int_{\Sigma}\frac{g^{\prime}}{H}}{|\Omega|^{2}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Sigma}g}{|\Omega|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})},

which leads the decreasing property of
A(t)
. Equality holds if and only if
\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle=1
everywhere on
\Sigma_{t}
, which is equivalent to that
\Sigma_{t}
is a geodesic sphere centered at the origin.
∎

As a corollary, combining with Hölder inequality, we have

Corollary 4.3
.

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g^{2}d\mu\geq|\Sigma||\Omega|^{2}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)^{2}.

Proof.

By the result of Gerhardt
[
12
]
, the flow hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
of IMCF remains to be star-shaped, mean convex, expands to infinity and the principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}
decays to
1
exponentially as
t\to\infty
. Let
g^{\Sigma_{t}}_{ij}
be the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}
. The asymptotical behavior of
\Sigma_{t}
along IMCF proved by Gerhardt
[
12
]
implies that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\det g^{\Sigma_{t}}}=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\sqrt{\det g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\left(1+O(e^{-\frac{2t}{n-1}})\right),

\displaystyle\lambda(r_{t})=

\displaystyle O(e^{\frac{t}{n-1}}).

Furthermore, we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}r_{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}R_{t}=+\infty.

Then it follows from the monotonicity of
h
, (
4.1
) and (
4.5
) that

\displaystyle A(t)=

\displaystyle|\Sigma_{t}|^{-1}\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}gd\mu_{t}}{|\Omega_{t}|h(\int_{\Omega_{t}}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv_{t})}\geq|\Sigma_{t}|^{-1}\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}gd\mu_{t}}{|\Omega_{t}|h(\int_{B(R_{t})}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv_{t})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|S(R_{t})|\int_{\Sigma_{t}}gd\mu_{t}}{|\Sigma_{t}||B(R_{t})|}=\frac{1}{g(R_{t})}\cdot\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}gd\mu_{t}}{|\Sigma_{t}|}.

Using Mean Value Theorem, there exists
\xi_{t}\in[\min\limits_{\Sigma_{t}}r_{t},\max\limits_{\Sigma_{t}}r_{t}]
such that

\frac{\int_{\Sigma_{t}}gd\mu_{t}}{|\Sigma_{t}|}=g(\xi_{t}).

Since
A(t)
is decreasing, we obtain from (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\Sigma}gd\mu}{|\Omega||\Sigma|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)}=A(0)\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}A(t)\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{g(\xi_{t})}{g(R_{t})}=\frac{n-1}{n-1}=1.

Combining with Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we conclude

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g^{2}d\mu\geq\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}(\int_{\Sigma}gd\mu)^{2}\geq|\Sigma||\Omega|^{2}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)^{2}.

∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
for
n\geq 5

Before beginning the proof, we need the following lemma:

Lemma 4.4
.

If
n\geq 5
, then the function

(g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}

is decreasing in
r
.

Proof.

In fact, by taking a derivative, we have

\displaystyle((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{\prime}=((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2}{n}(1-g^{\prime}))^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}+2(n-1)\frac{g}{\lambda}\frac{g^{\prime}\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{2}{n}g^{\prime\prime}=2g^{\prime\prime}(g^{\prime}-\frac{1}{n})+2(n-1)\frac{g}{\lambda}\frac{g^{\prime}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}}{\lambda}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(n-1)(\frac{g}{\lambda^{2}}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{\lambda})(g^{\prime}-\frac{1}{n})+2n\frac{g}{\lambda}\frac{g^{\prime}-\frac{1}{n}}{\lambda}=2(n-1)(g^{\prime}-\frac{1}{n})\frac{g}{\lambda^{2}}(\frac{2n-1}{n-1}-\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}).

Thus it suffices to show that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda\lambda^{\prime}g^{\prime}}{g}\leq\frac{2n-1}{n-1}.

Fortunately, by use of Proposition
3.6
, we know that (
4.7
) holds for
n\geq 5
.
∎

From this lemma, by the mass transplantation, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})\leq\int_{B(R)}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}),

then

(4.8)

\displaystyle\frac{2(n-1)}{n}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}-\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})\leq g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)|-\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}).

We proceed to prove Theorem
1.2
for
n\geq 5
. Firstly, it follows from the upper bound (
2.8
) and Corollary
4.3
that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}})dv}{|\Omega|^{2}|\Sigma|h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}dv)^{2}}.

The following lemma is essential, which is slightly stronger than Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
n\geq 5
. If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a smooth domain with star-shaped mean convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
, and
B(R)
is a geodesic ball with the same volume as
\Omega
. Then

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\Sigma|\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq|S(R)|\sigma_{1}(B(R)).

Proof.

We estimate
h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}
in the denominator of the right hand side of (
4.9
). Using Mean Value Theorem, there exists
\xi\in[\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda},\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}]
such that

\displaystyle h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}-h(\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}=2h(\xi)h^{\prime}(\xi)(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}-\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}).

Recall that the function

\displaystyle h(\int_{B(r)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})h^{\prime}(\int_{B(r)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})=-\frac{n-1}{|B(r)||S(r)|^{2}}

is increasing in
r
. Combining this with
g^{\prime}(R)\leq\frac{1}{n}
and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}\geq

\displaystyle h(\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}+2h(\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})h^{\prime}(\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}-\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S(R)|^{2}}-\frac{2(n-1)}{|B(R)||S(R)|^{2}}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}-\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S(R)|^{2}}-\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)||S(R)|^{2}}\frac{2(n-1)}{n}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}-\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S(R)|^{2}}-\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)||S(R)|^{2}}(g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)|-\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)||S(R)|^{2}}\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}).

Therefore, by (
4.9
) and (
3.10
), we derive

\displaystyle|\Sigma|\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}(g^{\prime})^{2}+(n-1)\frac{g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}{|\Omega|^{2}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{2}}\leq\frac{g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)||S(R)|^{2}}{|B(R)|^{2}}=|S(R)|\sigma_{1}(B(R)).

This completes the proof of (
4.10
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
for
n\geq 5
.

The isoperimetric inequality in Theorem
2.1
implies that the radius of
\Omega^{\ast}
is no less than
R
. By Lemma
4.5
and (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\frac{|S(R)|\sigma_{1}(B(R))}{|\Sigma|}\leq\frac{|\partial\Omega^{\ast}|\sigma_{1}(\Omega^{\ast})}{|\Sigma|}=\sigma_{1}(\Omega^{\ast}).

The equality holds if and only if the radius of

is

. That is,

.
∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2
for
n=4

In this subsection, we consider the case
n=4
. In this case, we can explicitly compute
g
and
g^{\prime}
as

(4.11)

\displaystyle g(r)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}(r)}\int_{0}^{r}\lambda^{3}(t)dt=\frac{\lambda(\lambda^{\prime}+2)}{3(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{2}},\quad g^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{2}}.

Firstly, we use the following lemma instead of Lemma
4.4
.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
n=4
, then the function

\displaystyle(g^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}

is decreasing in

.

Proof.

Applying (

) into the formula, we have

(g^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}=\frac{2(\lambda^{\prime})^{2}+6\lambda^{\prime}+7}{3(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{4}}=\frac{2}{3(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{2}}+\frac{2}{3(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{3}}+\frac{1}{(\lambda^{\prime}+1)^{4}},

∎

It follows from Lemma
4.6
and the mass transplantation argument that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}-\int_{B(R)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda}\leq g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)|-\int_{\Omega}((g^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}).

Similar to the proof of Lemma
4.5
, but slightly different because we use (
3.11
) instead of (
3.12
), we have

Lemma 4.7
.

If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{4}
is a smooth domain with star-shaped mean convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
, and
B(R)
is a geodesic ball with the same volume as
\Omega
. Then

(4.13)

\displaystyle|\Sigma|^{\frac{2}{3}}\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq|S(R)|^{\frac{2}{3}}\sigma_{1}(B(R)).

Proof.

From (
2.8
), (
4.6
) and (

), we have

\displaystyle|\Sigma|^{\frac{2}{3}}\sigma_{1}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}(g^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}{(\int_{\Sigma}g^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}(\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\int_{\Sigma}g^{2})^{\frac{2}{3}}}\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}(g^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}{(\int_{\Sigma}g^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}(\frac{1}{|\Sigma|}\int_{\Sigma}g)^{\frac{4}{3}}}

\displaystyle h(\int_{B(r)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{\frac{1}{3}}h^{\prime}(\int_{B(r)}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})=-\frac{3}{|B(r)||S(r)|^{\frac{4}{3}}}

\displaystyle|\Sigma|^{\frac{2}{3}}\sigma_{1}(\Omega)\leq\frac{\int_{\Omega}(g^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{3g^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}}{g(R)^{\frac{1}{3}}|B(R)|^{\frac{5}{3}}h(\int_{\Omega}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}g}{\lambda})^{\frac{4}{3}}}\leq\frac{g^{\prime}(R)|B(R)||S(R)|^{\frac{4}{3}}}{g(R)^{\frac{1}{3}}|B(R)|^{\frac{5}{3}}}=|S(R)|^{\frac{2}{3}}\sigma_{1}(B(R)).

∎

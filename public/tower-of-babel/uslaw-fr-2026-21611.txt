Privacy Act of 1974; System of Records

AGENCY: Defense Finance and Accounting Services (DFAS), Department of 
Defense (DoD).

ACTION: Rescindment of a system of records notice.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: In accordance with the Privacy Act of 1974, DFAS is providing 
notice to rescind a system of records titled ``Centralized Travel 
History Records (CTHR),'' T7333a. DFAS established this system to equip 
Air Force installations with online inquiry capabilities. This 
functionality allowed installations to support travel claims, 
questions, and investigations, and to obtain voucher numbers for 
retrieving travel vouchers from on-site storage or Federal Records 
Centers. DFAS decommissioned the system and consolidated its records 
under the Commanders Resource Integration System, F065 AF FMP C.

DATES: The rescindment of these SORNs is effective November 23, 2026.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION:

I. Background

    The DFAS system of records Centralized Travel History Records, 
(CTHR) T7333a (January 2, 2014; 79 FR 115) was established to provide 
Air Force installations with the capability to accomplish an online 
inquiry to support travel claims, questions, and investigations and 
provide voucher numbers to retrieve travel vouchers from on-site 
storage or the Federal Records Centers. The system was decommissioned, 
and the records are now consolidated under the Commanders Resource 
Integration System, F065 AF FMP C (December 16, 2010; 75 FR 78684).
    DoD SORNs are published in the Federal Register and are available 
at the Oversight and Compliance Directorate, Privacy and Civil 
Liberties Division website at https://doc.defense.gov/DIRECTORATES/Privacy-and-Civil-Liberties-Directorate/Privacy/SORNs/.

II. Privacy Act

    Under the Privacy Act, a ``system of records'' is a group of 
records under the control of an agency from which information is 
retrieved by the name of an individual or by some identifying number, 
symbol, or other identifying particular assigned to the individual. In 
the Privacy Act, an individual is defined as a U.S. citizen or alien 
lawfully admitted for permanent residence.

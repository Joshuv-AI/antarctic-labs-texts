Tameness, nullness, and amorphic complexity of automatic systems
Maik Gröger, Elżbieta Krawczyk

Abstract
. In topological dynamics, tame and null systems arise naturally in the study of low-complexity aperiodic behaviour, yet providing concrete and easily testable conditions to establish their existence in a canonical class of systems is often nontrivial. We give a complete characterisation of tameness and nullness for minimal automatic systems generated by primitive constant length substitutions in terms of amorphic complexity—a numerical invariant recently introduced to study zero entropy systems. We derive an easily computable closed formula for this invariant in this setting and show that, for infinite automatic systems, tameness and nullness are equivalent to its value being one.

1.
Introduction

Inspired by the seminal work
[
4
]
of Bourgain, Fremlin, and Talagrand, Köhler introduced in
[
37
]
the notion of
tame
dynamical systems (originally she used the term

Later, by adopting one of the main results of
[
4
]
to the dynamical context, Glasner and Megrelishvili
[
23

provided the following dichotomy: the enveloping semigroup of

system is either “large” (containing the Stone–Čech compactification of the natural numbers) or “small” (with its topology determined by sequential convergence), the latter case corresponding to the system being tame.

A particularly useful alternative characterization of tameness is provided by the theory of topological independence
[
36
]
. Here, the existence or absence of large independence sets forces high or low dynamical complexity. For example, positive topological entropy of a system
T:X\to X
is tied to the independence of iterates
T^{n}
,
n\in{\mathbf{Z}}
along subsets of positive density. At a weaker level, the absence of infinite independence sets is linked to tameness, while
nullness
in the sense of Goodman
[
29
]
(i.e. zero topological sequence entropy) requires the lack of arbitrarily large finite independence sets, see Section
2.3
or
[
36
]
for precise definitions and further information.

Since their introduction—and in particular in recent years—tame and null systems have been widely studied. This research has focused on characterizing their structural properties in various ways and developing more directly applicable criteria for identifying tame or null systems.
For more information on tame systems, see e.g.

As part of the recent developments, an important focus has been on establishing concrete and computationally tractable conditions to identify tame and null systems within canonical classes of dynamical systems.
In this article, we provide a complete characterisation of tameness and nullness for the well-studied class of minimal automatic dynamical systems

Such systems can be equivalently described as those that are conjugate to a subshift generated by a primitive, constant length substitution (see Sections

The numerical quantity we will utilize is the notion of amorphic complexity, a relatively new topological invariant introduced for the study of zero entropy systems
[
15
]
. It is tailor-made for analysing minimal systems with discrete spectrum and continuous eigenfunctions
[
17
]
. Beyond

-actions, this class encompasses dynamical systems canonically induced by mathematical quasicrystals, such as Penrose and chair tilings. In recent work

Suppose

To establish Theorem

For this, to each primitive substitution

is given by

\mathrm{{ac}}(X_{\varphi})=\frac{\log k}{\log k-\log\lambda_{s}},

\lambda_{s}
is the discrepancy rate of
\varphi

Amorphic complexity

We briefly introduce amorphic complexity for

Given a dynamical system

For

\overline{D}(E)=\varlimsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{\left\lvert E\cap[-n,n]\right\rvert}{2n+1}.

A subset
S\subseteq X
is said to be
(f,\delta,\nu)
-separated
if all pairs of
distinct

are
(f,\delta,\nu)
-separated.
The
(asymptotic) separation numbers
of

\mathrm{Sep}(f,\delta,\nu)

If

Systems with positive entropy or with a nontrivial weakly mixing measure

14
]
for further information.

For systems with finite separation numbers, we
define the

and

(X,f)

\underline{\mathrm{ac}}(f)=\adjustlimits{\sup}_{\delta>0}{\varliminf}_{\nu\to 0}\frac{\log\mathrm{Sep}(f,\delta,\nu)}{-\log\nu}\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\overline{\mathrm{ac}}(f)=\adjustlimits{\sup}_{\delta>0}{\varlimsup}_{\nu\to 0}\frac{\log\mathrm{Sep}(f,\delta,\nu)}{-\log\nu}.

If both values coincide, we call

By allowing the above quantities to assume values

Thus, we may assume without loss of generality that
D_{\mathrm{S}}(\varphi^{n}(x),\varphi^{n}(y))>0
for all
n\geqslant 0
.
Let
\lambda
be the maximal growth rate of
\varphi_{s}
, and let
\gamma<\lambda
be strictly greater than all nonmaximal growth rates of
\varphi_{s}
. Let

be such that
\left\lvert\varphi_{s}^{n}(\alpha)\right\rvert\geqslant C\lambda^{n}
for each
\alpha\in\mathrm{S}
.
Using Lemma

, for each

and

we can write

\frac{D_{\bm{1}}(\varphi^{n}(x),\varphi^{n}(y))}{D_{\mathrm{S}}(\varphi^{n}(x),\varphi^{n}(y))}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{t^{(n)}_{i}}{s^{(n)}_{i}},

where

t^{(n)}_{i}=\sum_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i,\,x_{j}\neq y_{j}}\left\lvert\varphi_{s}^{n}{x_{j}\choose y_{j}}\right\rvert\quad\text{and}\quad s^{(n)}_{i}=\sum_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i,\,{x_{j}\choose y_{j}}\in\mathrm{S}}\left\lvert\varphi_{s}^{n}{x_{j}\choose y_{j}}\right\rvert.

Write
\alpha_{j}={x_{j}\choose y_{j}}
.
Let
n
be big enough so that
\left\lvert\varphi_{s}^{n}(\alpha)\right\rvert<\gamma^{n}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathscr{A}}_{s}
of nonmaximal growth and
\gamma^{n}/C\lambda^{n}\leqslant 1
. For such

, we get that

\begin{split}\frac{t^{(n)}_{i}}{s^{(n)}_{i}}&=1+\left(\sum_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i,\alpha_{j}\in\mathscr{A}_{s}\setminus\mathrm{S}}\left\lvert\varphi_{s}^{n}(\alpha_{j})\right\rvert\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i,\alpha_{j}\in\mathrm{S}}\left\lvert\varphi_{s}^{n}(\alpha_{j})\right\rvert\right)^{-1}\\
&\leqslant 1+\gamma^{n}\cdot\left(\left\lvert\{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i\mid\alpha_{j}\in\mathscr{A}_{s}\setminus\mathrm{S}\}\right\rvert\right)\cdot(C\lambda^{n})^{-1}\cdot\left(\left\lvert\{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i\mid\alpha_{j}\in\mathrm{S}\}\right\rvert\right)^{-1}\\
&\leqslant\left\lvert\{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i\mid x_{j}\neq y_{j}\}\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\{\left\lvert j\right\rvert<i\mid\alpha_{j}\in\mathrm{S}\}\right\rvert^{-1}=t^{(0)}_{i}(s^{(0)}_{i})^{-1}\end{split}

Letting

4.
Amorphic complexity of constant length substitution shifts

This section is devoted to the proof of the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a primitive substitution of length
k\geqslant 2
. The amorphic complexity of
X_{\varphi}
is given by

\mathrm{{ac}}(X_{\varphi})=\frac{\log k}{\log k-\log\lambda_{s}},

where
\lambda_{s}
is the discrepancy rate of
\varphi
.

The following example shows that passing to the pure base before computing the discrepancy rate is necessary in Theorem
4.1
.

Example
2.17

(continued)
.

Let

\varphi(0)=010,\quad\varphi(1)=102,\quad\varphi(2)=201

be a substitution of height 2 with its pure base
\varphi^{\prime}
given by

\varphi^{\prime}(a)=aab,\quad\varphi^{\prime}(b)=aba.

Note that
\varphi^{\prime}
has a coincidence and so, by Theorem

,

has discrete spectrum.

and

coincide.
However, if one were to construct the discrepancy substitution directly from

does not hold for

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
3
]
, the authors introduce the notion of a
discrepancy inflation
for a self-similar, nonperiodic topological tiling dynamical system
(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{R}^{d})
with pure-point spectrum generated by a primitive inflation, using it to establish lower and upper bounds for the amorphic complexity of
(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Though their work focuses on tilings (rather than the symbolic setting), it is easy to see that, for a substitution
\varphi
of constant length, their discrepancy inflation corresponds to our discrepancy substitution.

By translating their main result (
[

, Thm. 4.13 & Prop. 4.14]
) to our framework, we obtain the following bounds:

\frac{\log k}{\log k-\log\lambda_{min}}\leqslant\overline{\mathrm{ac}}(X_{\varphi})\leqslant\frac{\log k}{\log k-\log\lambda_{s}},

where
\lambda_{s}
(resp.
\lambda_{min}
) denotes the maximal (resp. minimal) growth rate of letters in the discrepancy substitution
\varphi_{s}
, and

is the length of the substitution. If
\lambda_{s}=\lambda_{min}
, this yields a closed-form expression for the amorphic complexity of
X_{\varphi}
. However, in general, one typically expects that
\lambda_{min}<\lambda_{s}
; see e.g. Example
3.8
.

In the proof we will need the following fact from
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a primitive substitution of length
k\geqslant 2
and of height 1. Assume that
X_{\varphi}
is infinite. If for some constants

,

A\cdot D_{\bm{1}}(x,y)\leqslant D_{\bm{1}}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))\leqslant B\cdot D_{\bm{1}}(x,y)\quad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in X_{\varphi},

then

\frac{\log k}{-\log A}\leqslant\underline{\mathrm{dim}}_{B}[X]\leqslant\overline{\mathrm{dim}}_{B}[X]\leqslant\frac{\log k}{-\log B}.

Proof.

This follows from
[
17
, Thm. 7.5 & Prop. 7.7]
, compare also with the proof of Theorem 7.6. in
[
17
]
.
∎

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we first reduce to the pure base (height
1
); the extreme cases
\lambda_{s}\in\{0,k\}
are treated by Proposition
3.10
.
To establish the formula
\mathrm{{ac}}(X_{\varphi})=\log k\,\bigl(\log k-\log\lambda_{s}\bigr)^{-1}

we apply Lemma
4.3
to the powers
\varphi^{n}
of
\varphi
; this requires sufficiently sharp estimates of the form

A_{n}\leqslant\frac{D_{\bm{1}}(\varphi^{n}(x),\varphi^{n}(y))}{D_{\bm{1}}(x,y)}\leqslant B_{n}.

The upper bounds are relatively straightforward and follow from the fact that all letters of the discrepancy substitution
\varphi_{s}
have growth rates uniformly bounded by
\lambda_{s}
. The main difficulty is to obtain lower bounds that are sharp enough; for this one has to show that— in an appropriately quantitative sense — pairs of letters

that are

of maximal growth with respect to discrepancy substitution

do not contribute to the asymptotics.
The core geometric idea is therefore to replace the Besicovitch pseudometric

by the seemingly coarser pseudometric

, built only from pairs of letters of maximal growth, and to show that these two pseudometrics are in fact Lipschitz equivalent on

(Proposition

). Since
D_{\mathrm{S}}
detects only pairs
\alpha={a\choose b}
with growth rate
\lambda_{s}
, the derivation of the lower bounds then proceeds in complete analogy with the upper-bound estimates, yielding the claimed formula for the amorphic complexity.

Let
\varphi

(12)

5.
Tameness and nullness of constant length substitution systems

Let
\varphi
be a primitive substitution of length
k\geqslant 2
.
The amorphic complexity of
X_{\varphi}
is either zero or lies in
[1,\infty]
.
One has
\mathrm{{ac}}(X_{\varphi})=0
if and only if
X_{\varphi}
is finite
and
\mathrm{{ac}}(X_{\varphi})=\infty
if and only if
X_{\varphi}
does not have discrete spectrum.
In this section we show that for an infinite
X_{\varphi}
,
\mathrm{{ac}}(X_{\varphi})=1
if and only if
X_{\varphi}
is null and that nullness and tameness of
X_{\varphi}
are equivalent.
To this end, Lemma
5.1
below will be a crucial step.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
k\geqslant 2
. Let
x
be a
k
-automatic sequence.
Assume that for each
m\geqslant 1
,
x
appears at most once as
k^{m}
-
\mathrm{AP}
in
x
; that is

x_{n}=x_{i+nk^{m}}
,
n\in{\mathbf{Z}}

for at most one
0\leqslant i<k^{m}
.
Then
x
is null if and only if all
z\in\mathrm{Ker}_{k}(x)
,
z\neq x
are null.

Before proving Lemma
5.1
, we consider some examples which will explain how it will be used in the proof Theorem
A
.
Recall that for a sequence
x
and

, we use the notation

\mathrm{AP}_{t}(x)=\{(x_{i+tn})_{n}\mid 0\leqslant i<t\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Ker}_{k}(x)=\bigcup\{\mathrm{AP}_{k^{n}}(x)\mid n\in{\mathbf{N}}\}.

The latter set is called the
k
-kernel of
x
;
x
is
k
-automatic if and only if its
k
-kernel is finite.
By a slight abuse of terminology we will say that
(x_{i+tn})_{n}
is the
i
th
t
-
AP
of
x
.

Example 5.2
.

Consider the substitution

\varphi(0)=0012,\quad\varphi(1)=1012,\quad\varphi(2)=2012

of constant length
4
.
Note that
\varphi
is of height 1, has a coincidence, and so,
X_{\varphi}
has discrete spectrum.
Let
x=\dots 2012.0012\dots
be a fixed point of
\varphi
.
The discrepancy substitution of
\varphi
is a constant function
\varphi_{s}\colon{\mathscr{A}}_{s}\to{\mathscr{A}}_{s}
.

We have
x_{n}=x_{4n}
,

; and the
4
-kernel of
x

\mathrm{Ker}_{4}(x)=\{x,0^{\omega},1^{\omega},2^{\omega}\}

consists only of

and constant sequences.
Further, for any

,

appears only once as
4^{n}
-
\mathrm{AP}
in

(namely, as the 0th progression) and all other

\mathrm{AP}
s are constant.
Since all constant sequences are null, by Lemma

, the

-automatic sequence

is also null.

>1
always gives rise to a null system. Substitution systems with this property have, in fact, already been studied and shown to be null, for instance in the original paper of Goodman

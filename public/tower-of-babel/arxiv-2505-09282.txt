Phase Transitions in Decision Problems Over Odd-Sized Alphabets
Andrew Jackson

Abstract
In [A. Jackson, Explaining the ubiquity of phase transitions in decision problems (2025), arXiv:2501.14569], I established that phase transitions are always present in a large subset of decision problems over even-sized alphabets, explaining – in part – why phase transitions are seen so often in decision problems. However, decision problems over odd-sized alphabets were not discussed. Here, I correct that oversight, showing that a similar subset of decision problems over odd-sized alphabets also always exhibit phase transitions.

Given this paper served to relax the requirement – present in Ref.

The prime candidate for elimination or relaxation, in Theorem

The other assumptions required – only of languages over odd alphabets – in the definition of a language being adequately balanced are mostly present to enable the proof techniques used in this paper. There is no reason to believe they are fundamental restrictions on when phase transitions can occur, and so it is likely that these conditions can be significantly relaxed but that may require a more advanced array of techniques than the ones used herein.

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle=\Sigma\cup\big\{|\Sigma|+1\big\}=\big\{1,2,...,|\Sigma|,|\Sigma|+1\big\}.

\mathcal{H}

below) of phase transitions (as defined in Def.
8
), in turn, and show they are met:

Cond. 1:

Define
\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}=\big\{y\in\Pi^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }\gamma(y)=n\big\}\subseteq\Pi^{*}
(where the tilde is used to denote that
\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}
is a subset of
\Pi^{*}
) and consider:

\displaystyle S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}

\displaystyle=S^{\xi\circ\gamma}_{n}=\big\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }(\xi\circ\gamma)(x)=n\big\}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\xi\big(S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big)

\displaystyle=\big\{\xi(x)\in\Pi^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }x\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ and }(\xi\circ\gamma)(x)=n\big\}=\big\{y\in\Pi^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }(\xi\circ\gamma)(\xi^{-1}(y))=n\big\}=\big\{y\in\Pi^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }\gamma(y)=n\big\}=\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}.

(60)

Switching focus, let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}\big[\tilde{S}\big]
be the fraction of
\tilde{S}\subseteq\Pi^{*}
that is in
\mathcal{H}
; known as the acceptance fraction of
\mathcal{H}
in
\tilde{S}
. Similarly, let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\big[S\big]
be the fraction of
S\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
that is in
\mathcal{L}
. As, by assumption,
\mathcal{H}
exhibits a phase transition:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\bigg(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}\big[\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}\big]\bigg)=1.

(61)

As
\xi
is a preserving-P-isomorphism,
\xi
is bijective and,
\forall x\in\Sigma^{*}
,
x\in\mathcal{L}\iff\xi(x)\in\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, also using that
\xi\big(S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big)=\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}
(as shown in Eqn.
60
), the equation for
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\big[S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big]
(Eqn.
1
) in Def.
5
implies that:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\big[S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big]=\dfrac{\big|\mathcal{L}\cap S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big|}{\big|S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big|}=\dfrac{\big|\xi\big(\mathcal{L}\cap S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big)\big|}{\big|\xi\big(S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big)\big|}=\dfrac{\big|\xi\big(\mathcal{L}\big)\cap\xi\big(S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big)\big|}{\big|\xi\big(S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big)\big|}=\dfrac{\big|\mathcal{H}\cap\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}\big|}{\big|\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}\big|}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}\big[\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}\big].

(62)

Hence, combining Eqn.
61
and Eqn.
62
gives the required
\mathbf{Cond~1}
for
\mathcal{L}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\bigg(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\big[S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big]\bigg)=\lim_{n\longrightarrow\infty}\bigg(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}\big[\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}\big]\bigg)=1.

(63)

Cond. 2:

Using similar reasoning to above, as, by assumption,
\mathcal{H}
exhibits a phase transition and
\xi
is a preserving-P-isomorphism:

\displaystyle\lim_{n\longrightarrow-\infty}\bigg(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{L}}\big[S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{n}\big]\bigg)=\lim_{n\longrightarrow-\infty}\bigg(\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{H}}\big[\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{n}\big]\bigg)=0.

(64)

Cond. 3:

As
\xi:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Pi^{*}
is a preserving-P-isomorphism, it is a bijection, therefore:
\forall S\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
,

\displaystyle\big|\xi(S)\big|=\big|S\big|.

(65)

The next step first requires a brief diversion to define a useful subset, in Def.
21
.

Def. 21
.

For a fixed
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\forall A\in\mathbb{R}
, define
\underline{\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{A,A+\delta}}\subset\Pi^{*}
by:

\displaystyle\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{A,A+\delta}=\big\{x\in\Pi^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }\gamma(x)\in[A,A+\delta]\big\},

(66)

and similarly define
S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{A,A+\delta}=\big\{y\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }\gamma(\xi(y))\in[A,A+\delta]\big\}
.

Resuming the main thread of this proof, and using Def.
21
:

\displaystyle\xi^{-1}\big(\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{A,A+\delta}\big)

\displaystyle=\big\{\xi^{-1}(x)\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }x\in\Pi^{*}\text{ and }\gamma(x)\in[A,A+\delta]\big\}=\big\{y\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }\gamma(\xi(y))\in[A,A+\delta]\big\}=S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{A,A+\delta}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}.

(67)

As
\mathcal{H}
, by assumption, exhibits a phase transition,
|\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{A,A+\delta}|
decays sufficiently quickly (i.e. exponentially) as
A
approaches its own, corresponding, threshold to meet the third required condition of phase transitions. Therefore, using that
\xi^{-1}:\Pi^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
is a P-isomorphism (as shown in Lemma
8
, in Appendix
D
), and therefore a bijection, Eqn.
67
implies:

\displaystyle\big|\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{A,A+\delta}\big|=\big|\xi^{-1}\big(\tilde{S}^{\gamma}_{A,A+\delta}\big)\big|=\big|S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{A,A+\delta}\big|.

(68)

I then conclude that
\big|S^{\gamma^{\prime}}_{A,A+\delta}\big|
must also decay sufficiently quickly, as
A
approaches the threshold, and so meets the third required condition of phase transitions in Def.
8
.

I have therefore shown for each requirement of phase transitions (in Def.
8
) that if
\mathcal{H}
meets it then
\mathcal{L}
also meets it by virtue of the preserving-P-isomorphism,
\xi:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Pi^{*}
, between them. So, if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\Pi^{*}
has a phase transition, so does
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
.
∎

Appendix C
The Incompatibility of Padding and Sparsity

The purpose of this appendix is to examine the relationship between sparsity and paddability, showing that they are incompatible and hence arguing – informally – that being paddable and not-anywhere-exponentially-unbalanced are commonly found together.

Before beginning this appendix, I first must define a particular subset of
\Sigma^{*}
, in Def.
22
.

Def. 22
.

\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define
\Sigma^{n}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
by:
\Sigma^{n}=\big\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }|x|=n\big\}
.
Similarly, I define,
\Sigma^{\leq n}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
by:

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\leq n}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\Sigma^{j}\bigg).

(69)

Then I define the density of a language (in Def.
23
), which is the basis for sparsity (defined in Def.
24
).

Def. 23
.

For any alphabet,
\Sigma
, and any language,
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
, the density of
\mathcal{L}
in
\Sigma^{n}
,
\textit{Dens}_{\mathcal{L}}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is defined by:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\textit{Dens}_{\mathcal{L}}\big(n\big)=\big|\mathcal{L}\cap\Sigma^{\leq n}\big|.

(70)

Def. 24
.

A language,
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
, is sparse if and only if there exists a polynomial,
\textit{Poly}_{s}:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}
, such that:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\textit{Dens}_{\mathcal{L}}\big(n\big)\leq\textit{Poly}_{s}(n).

(71)

With the foundational concepts defined, I move to the crux of Appendix
C
: a prohibition on languages being both sparse and paddable.

Lemma 7
.

No paddable language (over an alphabet of size at least two) is sparse.

Proof.

(Based on Ref.
[
53
]
)

Assume, for the sake of contradiction,
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
is a language (where
|\Sigma|\geq 2
) that is sparse (with the polynomial
\textit{Poly}_{s}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
bounding the density of
\mathcal{L}
) and paddable (with padding function,
\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}:\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
).

As, by definition,
\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}:\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
runs in polynomial (in the size of the combined inputs) time, there exists a polynomial,
\textit{Poly}_{p}:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}
, such that:
\forall x,y\in\Sigma^{*}
,

\displaystyle\big|\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\big|\leq\textit{Poly}_{p}\big(|x|+|y|\big).

(72)

Then, for any fixed
x\in\mathcal{L}
, as
\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}
by definition preserves membership of
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}
being sparse implies – using Def.
24
– that there exists a polynomial,
\textit{Poly}_{s}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
, such that:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\in\mathcal{L}\text{ }|\text{ }y\in\Sigma^{\leq n}\big\}\big|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\textit{Poly}_{s}\big(\textit{Poly}_{p}\big(|x|+j\big)\big)\bigg).

(73)

However, as
\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
is bijective (in its second input, as shown in Lemma
9
in Appendix
E
) and

\displaystyle\big|\Sigma^{\leq n}\big|=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\big|\Sigma\big|^{j}\bigg)=\dfrac{|\Sigma|^{n+1}-1}{|\Sigma|-1},

(74)

it follows that:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y)\in\Sigma^{*}\text{ }|\text{ }y\in\Sigma^{\leq n}\big\}\big|

\displaystyle=\dfrac{|\Sigma|^{n+1}-1}{|\Sigma|-1}.

(75)

For convenience, I then define another polynomial,
\textit{Poly}_{sp}:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}
, by:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\textit{Poly}_{sp}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\bigg(\textit{Poly}_{s}\big(\textit{Poly}_{p}\big(|x|+j\big)\big)\bigg).

(76)

Therefore, combining Eqn.
73
, Eqn.
75
, and Eqn.
76
gives:
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\Sigma|^{n+1}-1}{|\Sigma|-1}\leq\textit{Poly}_{sp}(n).

(77)

As I assumed
|\Sigma|\geq 2
, there will always exist a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
to render Eqn.
77
false. Hence, the assertion in Eqn.
77
is a contradiction and
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
cannot exist as described. I.e. no language can be both paddable and sparse.
∎

Appendix D
P-isomorphism Auxiliary Lemma

The below Lemma
8
is used in the proof of Lemma
6
, to show that the third condition of phase transitions are met.

Lemma 8
.

For any preserving-P-isomorphism,
\xi:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Pi^{*}
, its inverse,
\xi^{-1}:\Pi^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
, is also a preserving-P-isomorphism.

Proof.

A preserving-P-isomorpism, as defined in Def.
11
, is also a P-isomorphism.

By definition (in Def.
9
), if
\xi:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Pi^{*}
is an P-isomorphism, so is its inverse,
\xi^{-1}:\Pi^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
. Then, using that for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
there exists a
y\in\Pi^{*}
such that
y=\xi(x)
(as
\xi
is a bijection), alongside Eqn.
4
(in Def.
11
):
\forall y\in\Pi^{*}
,

\displaystyle\xi^{-1}(y)\in\mathcal{L}\iff x\in\mathcal{L}\iff\xi(x)\in\mathcal{H}\iff y\in\mathcal{H}.

(78)

So, as Eqn.
78
is the equivalent of Eqn.
4
but for
\xi^{-1}
, I conclude that
\xi^{-1}:\Pi^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
is a preserving-P-isomorphism.
∎

Appendix E
Padding Function Isomorphism Auxiliary Lemma

The aim of this appendix is to provide the statement and proof of the below Lemma
9
, which is used in the proof of Lemma
6
, in Appendix
B
which in turn contributes to the proof of Theorem
2
.

Lemma 9
.

For any alphabet,
\Sigma
, and paddable language,
\mathcal{L}\subset\Sigma^{*}
the padding function of
\mathcal{L}
,
\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}:\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
, is bijective in its second argument. I.e.
\forall x\in\Sigma^{*}
, the function,
\textit{Pa}_{x}:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
, defined by:
\forall y\in\Sigma^{*}
,

\displaystyle\textit{Pa}_{x}(y)

\displaystyle=\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y),

(79)

where
x\in\Sigma^{*}
is fixed, is bijective.

Proof.

It is easy to see that,
\forall y\in\Sigma^{*}
,
\textit{Pa}_{x}(y)
only returns a single value. It then only remains to show that
y
can be uniquely recovered from
\textit{Pa}_{x}(y)
, which would then imply each input to
\textit{Pa}_{x}(y)
is the only input that gives the output it gives and hence
\textit{Pa}_{x}(y)
is a bijection.

To show that
y
can be uniquely recovered, I propose the following mapping as a left inverse of
\textit{Pa}_{x}(y)
:
\forall z\in\Sigma^{*}
,

\displaystyle\textit{Pa}^{-1}_{x}(z)

\displaystyle=\textit{Dec}_{\mathcal{L}}(z),

(80)

where
\textit{Dec}_{\mathcal{L}}:\Sigma^{*}\longrightarrow\Sigma^{*}
is the decoding function of
\mathcal{L}
, which must exist as it is paddable. This can be seen to correctly and uniquely recover
y
as:

\displaystyle\textit{Pa}^{-1}_{x}(\textit{Pa}_{x}(y))

\displaystyle=\textit{Dec}_{\mathcal{L}}(\textit{Pad}_{\mathcal{L}}(x,y))=y.

(81)

∎

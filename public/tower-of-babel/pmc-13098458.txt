Drivers of consumer preference for cherry tomatoes: Insights from sensory, physicochemical and flavor properties
Authors: Yixuan Fan, Xuemei Liu, Shaoxiang Pan, Xinyu Zhao, Mengnan Tan, Min Xu, Ye Song, Xinhuan Yan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Understanding the chemical drivers of consumer preference is essential for improving cherry tomato quality. This study investigated eight cherry tomato varieties by integrating consumer preference testing, trained sensory evaluation, physicochemical analysis and volatile profiling. Based on consumer preference scores, red-fruited varieties were classified as the high-preference group, while others as the low-preference group. Correlation and multivariate analysis identified a* value moisture content, total soluble solids and four C6-C9 aldehydes ((E)-2,4-decadienal, decanal, (E)-2-octenal and 2-hexenal) as key chemical indicators positively correlated with consumer preference. High-preference varieties exhibited elevated levels of these indicators, demonstrating a synergistic relationship between visual, nutritional and volatile attributes. These findings lay the foundation for optimizing cherry tomato flavor by linking key chemical markers to sensory attributes, emphasizing the value of integrating sensory perception with chemical profiling for consumer-oriented breeding and quality evaluation.

Introduction

Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.), as a nightshade from the Solanaceae family, is one of the most crucial commercial crops worldwide (Chiang & Chiang, 2024; Guan et al., 2025). Global production of tomatoes exceeds approximately 192.32 million tons, with China being the largest producer, yielding about 40.71 million tons (FAOSTAT, 2019). Cherry tomatoes (Solanum lycopersicum var. cerasiforme) are particularly favored by consumers for their vibrant colors and unique flavor (Tieman et al., 2012; Yin et al., 2024). In addition, they are rich in essential nutrients including vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants, which contribute to high nutritional value (Huang et al., 2025; Laranjeira et al., 2022; Yanhong Li et al., 2022). Sensory properties, nutritional composition and aroma are widely recognized as the key determinants of cherry tomato quality and consumer preference. For instance, sensory evaluations provide firsthand information about samples to consumers, while nutrient compositions such as carotenoids, flavonoids, and phenolic compounds are associated with antioxidant properties that appeal to health-conscious consumers, whereas aroma contributes to a richer taste and plays a major role in consumer decision-making (Imran et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2015).

Among various quality attributes, fruit color serves as the primary visual indicator for consumer recognition, which is impacted by pigment accumulation (Lin et al., 2014). Studies have confirmed that various carotenoids, including β-carotene, lycopene, and lutein, are present in tomato fruits, with lycopene conferring red color and β-carotene predominantly found in yellow or orange tomatoes (Chang et al., 2024). Current research focuses on the biosynthesis pathway of color formation in various tomato varieties. For example, Lin et al. identified the causative variants responsible for pink fruit color as the transcription factor MYB12, which was expressed by Chalcone Synthase (Lin et al., 2014). Li et al. discovered SlWRKY14 as a key regulator in tomato fruits, which acts as a repressor and an activator in carotenoid and flavonoid biosynthetic pathways, respectively (Li et al., 2025). Despite significant progress in elucidating the biosynthetic pathways of fruit coloration, fully capturing the color diversity of tomato varieties and understanding the underlying drivers of consumer preference remain challenging (Shin, Yoo, Yeam, & Lee, 2018).

Apart from color, consumer preference is also closely linked to flavor and nutritional substances (Lin et al., 2014). Extensive research has investigated the physicochemical properties that influence tomato flavor, such as total soluble solids (TSS) and titratable acidity (TA), which are strongly correlated with consumer liking (Dotsha Jaleel, 2024). Despite various studies on tomato through physicochemical indicators, the exploration of flavor remains in its infancy due to the picomolar to nanomolar concentrations of volatile substances and the challenges associated with quantitative analysis (Giovannoni, 2018). In recent years, research on volatile substances in tomatoes has focused on exploring the volatile composition, investigating the dynamic changes under different storage conditions and elucidating the regulatory mechanisms. For example, Xu et al. identified 154 volatile compounds in 16 tomato varieties, including 29 aldehydes, 22 hydrocarbons, 21 alcohols, 32 unknown compounds, and others, with aldehydes being the most abundant (Xu et al., 2026). Moreover, recent studies have demonstrated that cherry tomatoes with higher diversity and concentrations of volatile flavor compounds are generally more favored by consumers than large-fruited varieties (El-Mesery et al., 2024). Analytical techniques have enabled precise identification of physicochemical properties and volatile components, nevertheless, the relationship among these chemical attributes and consumer preference remains poorly understood (Coyago-Cruz et al., 2019). In particular, the integration of sensory characteristics, physicochemical indicators, and volatile profiles to elucidate the association with consumer preference for cherry tomatoes remains a significant gap.

Therefore, this study aimed to systematically evaluate eight cherry tomato varieties from a consumer perspective through the following methods. (1) Consumer preference testing and trained sensory evaluation were conducted, and the consumer preference scores served as the core factor for all subsequent analyses. (2) The two preference groups were compared in terms of physicochemical properties and volatile profiles. (3) Key chemical drivers of consumer preference were identified using correlation analysis and multivariate statistics. By integrating multi-dimensional data from the perspective of consumers, the main chemical drivers were identified and a possible metabolic link between lycopene, color and aroma was elucidated, providing a theoretical basis for the comprehensive quality assessment of cherry tomatoes, thereby facilitating variety selection and guiding consumer choice.

Materials and methods

Materials and reagents

Eight cherry tomato varieties with different colors (green, white, pink, brown, yellow and red) were explored for this study and their information was gathered in Table S1. Fresh and ripe (with the same maturity level) tomatoes were purchased from a green planting base located in Jinan City, Shandong Province, with GPS coordinates of 36°54′N, 117°20′E. Selected fruits, demonstrating uniformity in shape, size, and color and showing no evidence of mechanical damage or disease, were swiftly stored in ice bags and transported back to a refrigerator at a temperature of 4 °C before analysis.

Hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, acetic acid, sodium hydroxide and potassium hydroxide with a purity of 99.5% were provided by Kemiou Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Tianjin, China). Metaphosphoric acid, oxalic acid, sodium bicarbonate and 2,6-dichloroindophenol were obtained from Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) and the purity was more than 99%. Folin-Ciocalteu reagent was purchased from Yuanye Biological Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Methyl alcohol, ethyl alcohol and iodine with a purity of 99.5% were obtained from Kaitong Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Tianjin, China). 2-butanone, 2-pentanone, 2-hexanone, 2-heptanone and 2-octanone with a purity of 99% were supplied by Sinopharm Chemical Regent Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). 2-octanol was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) and the purity was more than 99.5%. Isopropyl alcohol, methanol and acetic acid were supplied by Yuwanghetianxia New Material Co., Ltd. (Shandong, China) and they were chromatographic grade with a purity of 98%. Double-distilled water was prepared by an ultrapure water instrument (Millipore, USA).

Sensory qualities analysis

Consumer preference test

Fifty-three consumers (29 females and 24 males, aged 19–50 years) who were not averse to cherry tomatoes were recruited. The consumers were asked to perform the test in a standardized sensory laboratory. Samples were randomly coded, presented in plastic containers at room temperature and evaluated according to the requirements of GB/T 39501–2020. The consumers rated their preference of the cherry tomato samples using a 9-point preference scale (Table S2).

Sensory evaluation

Ten trained panelists (4 males and 6 females, aged 30 ± 5 years) were selected for tomato evaluation based on their prior experience in sensory analysis. Panel training was conducted in two sessions. In the first training session (1 h), panelists reviewed a list of sensory attributes prepared by sensory staff, selected and defined the attributes for the study, established the assessment procedure, reviewed standards and reference materials, set scale anchors and practiced scoring tomatoes. In the second training session (1.5 h), panelists reviewed the procedures and practiced scoring 10 batches of tomatoes. During the training sessions, panelists were familiarized with the scales and descriptions used for sensory attributes through a combination of theoretical learning and practical exercises.

Cherry tomato samples of each color were used for sensory evaluation and assessment was completed within 3 min. A 2-min rest period was applied to relieve sensory fatigue, during which the mouth was rinsed with water to eliminate mutual influence. The sensory quality attributes of tomatoes were determined by a professional sensory evaluation team, including eight attributes: color, firmness, juiciness, sweetness, sourness, aroma, flavor and overall acceptance. In the test sessions, the participants evaluated the sensory attributes of samples on a 5-point scale (1 represents low intensity and 5 represents high intensity).

Ethical approval for sensory evaluation was not necessary at our institution, the Jinan Fruit Research Institute, All China Federation of Supply & Marketing Co-Operatives. All participants had signed informed consent before the sensory evaluation. In addition, the rights and privacy of all participants were safeguarded throughout this study.

Physicochemical properties analysis

Color analysis

To explore the variation trend of tomatoes with different colors, tomato samples were randomly chosen for color assessment. Four equidistant points were marked along the equator of each tomato, and small pieces (2 × 2 × 2 cm3) were cut off at the same position of each point. Subsequently, the samples were measured using a color difference meter (CM-5 spectrophotometer, Konica Minolta, Japan) to determine three parameters: L* (lightness/darkness), a* (redness/greenness), and b* (yellowness/blueness) under D65 light source and 10° observer angle.

Texture analysis

Two points on the equatorial line (n = 3 for each group) were observed with a fruit pressure tester (FT-327, QA Supplies, USA) with a 4 mm probe, as described in the study by (Pan et al., 2024).

Physicochemical analysis

TSS were measured using an Atago refractometer (model PLA-2, Japan) with a resolution of 0.01 at 25 °C and the standard of NY/T 2637–2014 was the guidance. TA was studied based on the association of official analytical chemists (AOAC) method (AOAC, 1984). The variation of soluble monosaccharides including fructose and glucose was explored by high performance liquid chromatography according to the first method of GB 5009.8–2023. Organic acids such as malic acid and citric acid were detected according to the Chinese national standard GB 5009.157–2016. β-carotene and lycopene were measured with a reference to the standard of GB 5009.83–2016 and NY/T 1651–2008, respectively. To access the differences in amino acids among cherry tomatoes, the determination of 17 kinds of amino acids was performed based on the Chinese nation standard GB 5009.124–2016.

Flavor qualities analysis

GC-IMS analysis

The GC-IMS equipped with a MXT-WAX chromatographic column(15 m × 0.53 mm × 1.0 μm) was applied for the analyzing of tomato samples (Gesellschaft für Analytische Sensorsysteme mbH, Germany). Initially, 2.0 g homogenized tomato samples were placed in a 20.0 mL glass headspace vial and then sealed and incubated at 40 °C with a shaking speed of 500 rpm for 15.0 min. Next, 500.0 μL samples were sampled with a headspace injection needle at 85 °C and injected into the GC-IMS. Nitrogen (purity 99.99%) was used as the carrier gas and the flow rate program started at 2 mL/min for 2 min, then linearly increased to 10 mL/min for 8 min, followed by an increase of 100 mL/min for 10 min. Subsequently, the drift tube, which maintained a temperature of 45 °C, was 98 mm in length and the drift gas velocity was 150 mL/min. The retention index of volatile compounds was calculated using the n-ketones C4-C9 as an external reference. GC-IMS data were collected and processed by LAV software. Qualitative analysis of volatile compounds in tomatoes was performed by comparing retention indices and drift times with those in the GC-IMS and the database from GC-IMS Library Search.

GC–MS analysis

Tomato pulp was homogenized in liquid nitrogen, then 5 g was weighed into a headspace vial. The internal standard (2-octanol) and 1 g of sodium chloride were then added. Samples were incubated at 40 °C for 5 min and the volatile compounds were extracted using divinylbenzene/carboxen/polydimethylsiloxane (DVB/CAR/PDMS) solid phase microextraction (SPME) fiber (50/30 μm, Supelco, PA, USA) at 40 °C for 30 min and 5 min at 250 °C.

The GC–MS consisted of a TRACE 1300 GC (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) equipped with an ISQ MS and a PAL RSI Triple automatic sampler (CTC Analytics AG, Zwinge, Switzerland). The DB-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm × 0.25 μm; Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) was used for the separation of compounds. The column temperature was set as follows: at the beginning, the column was kept at 40 °C for 2 min; then heated up to 200 °C at a rate of 8 °C/min and kept for 3 min, heated to 250 °C at a rate of 10 °C/min and kept for 8 min. The major MS parameters were set as follows: the scan range was 50–600 m/z; the ionization energy was 70 eV. The retention index of volatile compounds was calculated using the n-ketones C4-C40 as an external reference. The volatile peaks were identified by matching the mass spectrum and the retention indices with the National Institute of Standards and Technology 10 (NIST 10) mass spectrometry database.

Results and discussion

Consumer preference classification

Sorting

The overall preference scores of eight cherry tomato varieties were obtained using a nine-point scale and significant differences were revealed among the samples (Fig. 1A). Varieties T6, T7 and T8, which were all red-fruited samples, received the highest scores, indicating strong consumer preference. As illustrated using hierarchical clustering analysis, the eight varieties were classified into two groups (Fig. 1B). Specifically, the high-preference (HP) group consisted of T6, T7 and T8, while the low-preference (LP) group was composed of the remaining five varieties (T1-T5).Fig. 1(A) Overall preference scores of consumers for cherry tomatoes based on nine-point preference scale; (B) Hierarchical clustering analysis of cherry tomato samples from consumer-based preference scores; (C) Fruit morphology of cherry tomatoes; (D) Radar chart of different attributes for cherry tomatoes based on professional sensory evaluation; (E) Correlation between overall preference scores and sensory evaluation.Fig. 1

To visually demonstrate the appearance features of the two preference groups, representative cherry tomato samples were presented in Fig. 1C. Obvious distinctions in shape and color were displayed, with fruits showing round or oval shapes and diverse skin colors. All varieties in the HP group exhibited typical red color associated with commercial cherry tomatoes, whereas the LP group displayed diverse colors including green, white, pink, brown and yellow, along with variations in fruit shape. The visual differences suggested that red color might be a visual cue for consumers when selecting cherry tomatoes.

The sensory attributes of cherry tomatoes evaluated by the trained panel were observed in the radar chart (Fig. 1D). It was obvious that the HP group (T6, T7 and T8) generally scored higher across attributes, especially in color, flavor and overall acceptance compared to the LP group. As red-colored tomatoes are the most consumed type in daily eating habits (Yang et al., 2019). Notably, T1 and T2 received consistently lower scores across most sensory attributes, particularly in color and flavor, which likely contributed to their lower overall acceptance.

Correlation analysis

To elucidate the relationship between sensory evaluation by the trained panel and overall preference scores of consumers, correlation analysis was performed between each sensory attribute and the overall consumer score (Fig. 1E). Significant positive correlations were observed between color, juiciness, sweetness, flavor, overall acceptance and overall preference scores, with flavor showing the strongest correlation (r = 0.93, p < 0.001). This strong association between flavor and consumer preference might be partly attributed to the frequent consumption of red cherry tomatoes, as increased familiarity with their taste often enhanced consumer preference (Rule et al., 2022). These results confirmed that the sensory evaluation was consistent with consumer preference, establishing a reliable foundation for subsequent analysis aimed at identifying the drivers of consumer preference.

Physicochemical properties

To explore the physicochemical basis underlying consumer preference, color parameters, textural properties and nutritional components were analyzed and compared between the HP and LP groups.

Physicochemical composition associated with preference

Detailed color parameters (L*, a* and b*) were consistent with the skin color of tomatoes, as depicted in Fig. 2A. The a* values clearly distinguished the HP group (T6, T7 and T8) with high positive values compared to the LP group. Notably, the L* values varied across the tomato varieties, with T2 showing the highest lightness consistent with its white appearance and T5 exhibiting the highest b* value associated with its yellow skin. Subsequently, the textural properties including hardness, gumminess and chewiness were analyzed using a Texture Analyzer (Fig. 2B). Significant differences in these parameters were observed between the HP and LP groups (p < 0.05), and the differences were consistent with previously reported results (Tilahun et al., 2019). In addition, the LP group exhibited substantially higher hardness and gumminess values compared to the HP group. Although the correlation between texture attributes and consumer preference scores was relatively low, research on consumer preferences has already indicated that texture remains a contributing factor in consumer choice (Teh et al., 2021).

Moisture content was further analyzed to provide a chemical basis for juiciness perception (Fig. 2C). The HP group exhibited significantly higher moisture content compared to the LP group (p < 0.05). This finding was consistent with the sensory evaluation results, in which the HP group received higher juiciness scores. Sugar and acid are one of the important factors affecting the taste and popularity (Chang et al., 2024). As shown in Fig. 2D, the HP group exhibited higher TSS content and lower TA content, which contributed to a better flavor profile, which aligned with the overall acceptance from sensory evaluation. In agreement with these results, consumer acceptance of tomatoes relied on physicochemical attributes like TSS and TA (Morelli et al., 2023).

Correlation analysis

Correlation analysis was performed to integrate the above physicochemical composition with consumer preference score and the results were illustrated in Fig. 2E. Notably, the a* value showed a positive correlation with overall acceptance (p < 0.01), indicating that consumers prefer cherry tomatoes with higher red color intensity. This aligned with previous research showing red-colored tomatoes were attractive relating to their survey of consumer's attitudes and preferences towards fresh market tomatoes (Oltman et al., 2014). In contrast, texture parameters such as hardness, chewiness and gumminess showed weak correlations with consumer preference scores, suggesting that texture played a secondary role in overall preference (Teh et al., 2021).

In addition to color, sugar-related indicators were also closely associated with consumer acceptance. TSS exhibited a significant positive correlation with overall acceptance (p < 0.01), which was consistent with the sensory evaluation where sweetness was strongly correlated with overall acceptance (Baldwin et al., 2000). This finding supported the conclusion that TSS enhanced perceived sweetness and consumer preference, with most consumers preferring sweeter fruit (Zhang et al., 2024). Moisture content, which underpinned juiciness perception, was also significantly correlated with overall preference scores (p < 0.01). This finding aligned with the sensory evaluation results, in which juiciness was positively associated with consumer preference. During chewing, high moisture content contributed to a refreshing taste and facilitated the release of flavor compounds, thereby enhancing overall flavor perception and consumer favorability (Bao et al., 2026).

Chemical mechanism

Pigment analysis revealed that the HP group accumulated significantly higher levels of lycopene compared to the LP group (Fig. 2F). β-carotene and lycopene, which were associated with tomato color formation, were abundant in the brown tomato (T4) consistent with their skin color and higher b* value. The distinct vibrant color of tomato was a process linked to carotenoid synthesis, which accumulated as tomatoes ripened (Tilahun et al., 2018).

The contents of fructose, glucose, malic acid and citric acid were exhibited in Fig. 2G. Interestingly, the trends of fructose and glucose in tomatoes were roughly similar, with fructose content higher than glucose in each cherry tomato. Results on the fructose and glucose confirmed that the HP group achieved the highest contents, which were approximately consistent with the TSS contents. These results were in line with the previous literature that tomatoes with a relatively higher fructose content tasted sweeter (Beckles, 2012). Furthermore, citric acid had a significant impact on the sourness formation and TA contents of tomatoes (Bertin & Génard, 2018).

Amino acids play an important role in shaping tomato flavor. Therefore, the contents of amino acids were investigated (Fig. 2H). Aspartic acid and glutamic acid, as umami flavor amino acids, revealed high contents compared with other amino acids in the HP group, which was essential for the overall taste profile (Morelli et al., 2023). Besides, a relatively high content of phenylalanine was shown in HP group, which was consistent with the previous conclusion that fructose was eventually converted into phenylalanine through a series of catalytic reactions (Guo et al., 2023).

Flavor characteristics

GC-IMS analysis

Consumer sensory evaluations provide firsthand information about the sample information, however, relying solely on sensory experience to understand the potential components of the samples is insufficient. Therefore, the volatile substances were subsequently analyzed using GC-IMS. A three-dimensional spectrum was constructed to directly visualize the volatile component levels in different tomato samples (Fig. 3A) and the eight tomatoes showed good separation of VOCs (Fig. 3B). A total of 70 components were detected by GC-IMS, of which 58 compounds were identified, including 18 aldehydes, 13 alcohols, 12 ketones, 4 esters, 2 acids, 1 alkane, 4 olefins and 4 others.Fig. 3VOCs in cherry tomatoes detected by GC-IMS. (A) Three-dimensional spectra; (B) Two-dimensional spectra; (C) Fingerprint spectra.Fig. 3

VOCs in cherry tomatoes detected by GC-IMS. (A) Three-dimensional spectra; (B) Two-dimensional spectra; (C) Fingerprint spectra.

To compare the volatile profiles of the two preference groups, fingerprint spectra were employed (Fig. 3C) and the volatile compounds in cherry tomato varieties were summarized in Table S3. By observing these spectra, common compounds constituting the basic flavor of tomatoes, including (E)-2-hexen-1-al, 1-hexanal, propanal, 1-hexanol, (E)-2-pentenal, 3-hexanone and (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol, were presented in all varieties. Remarkably, (E)-2-hexen-1-al and (E)-2-pentenal, which possessed tomato-like aroma, contributed to the characteristic flavor of all cherry tomatoes.

Notably, clear differences were observed between the HP group and the LP group in their characteristic volatile profiles. For the HP group, T6 exhibited volatile components such as α-pinene and β-pinene with pine, resin, and turpentine aromas and (Z)-3-hexen-1-ol with grass aroma. T7 and T8 were similar in their volatile profiles, containing high concentrations of (Z)-2-pentenol, (E)-2-octenal and 1-pentanol, which imparted green and nutty aromas. These findings suggest that the HP group, particularly the red varieties, was characterized by aldehydes and alcohols associated with fresh and green notes.

In contrast, the LP group exhibited more diverse and distinctive volatile profiles. T1 showed considerable differences from other varieties, with characteristic substances such as 2-methyl-1-butanol and (E,E)-2,4-hexadienal, which collectively imparted fruity and wine-like aromas. In T2 and T5 samples, a typical flavor compound named rose oxide was detected at high concentrations. Additionally, 3-heptanone, 1-octen-3-one and 1-octanal were presented at high levels in T3, which are associated with fatty, lemon and green aromas. T4 contained unique volatile organic compounds (VOCs) including 2-methyl-2-hepten-6-one and 4-methyl-1-pentanol, contributing to a special pepper-like aroma.

The relative contents of VOCs were collected in Table S4. These differences revealed variations in the quality of detected components by GC-IMS. Across all samples, aldehydes were the most abundant class, followed by ketones and alcohols, with (E)-2-hexen-1-al-D and 1-hexanal-D ranking highest in relative content. The total relative contents of volatile compounds were relatively consistent among varieties, although differences in specific compounds were observed.

GC–MS analysis

The types and amounts of alkanes detected by GC-IMS were fewer than GC–MS, due to the fact that GC-IMS had a weaker affinity for alkanes than water (Xia et al., 2022). Thus, we utilized GC–MS to analyze the aroma composition of this batch of cherry tomatoes from the perspective of flavor. 140 volatile organic compounds including 29 aldehydes, 27 alcohols, 3 ketones, 17 esters, 7 acids, 29 alkanes and 6 others were determined across the eight varieties (Table S5). This composition was consistent with previous findings that the volatile components identified in tomato fruits mainly contained aldehydes, alcohols, ketones, esters, etc. (Buttery & Takeoka, 2004).

The number of VOCs detected in each variety was presented in Fig. 4A. Among all varieties, T8 contained the highest number of VOCs (66), followed by T7 (62). The HP group (T6, T7 and T8) generally contained a greater number of VOCs compared to the LP group, particularly in terms of aldehydes, which was consistent with the finding that a wider range of aroma components contributes to more intense overall flavor (Baldwin, Goodner, & Plotto, 2008). As shown in Fig. 4B, eight common VOCs were detected across all varieties, including 2-ethyl-4-pentenal, hexanal, nonanal, 3-[(1,1-dimethylethoxy) methyl] heptane, 2,4,6-trimethyl-decane, tridecane, 2,7,10-trimethyl-dodecane and o-cymene. Hexanal provided a significant contribution to tomato aroma, generating a green aroma in all eight varieties of cherry tomatoes (Wang et al., 2015). In terms of unique VOCs, 9, 4, 2, 6, 4, 4, 5 and 10 specific VOCs were discovered in T1-T8, respectively. The differences in the kinds and amounts of volatile compounds were potential causes of each tomato's specific aroma.Fig. 4Varieties and contents of VOCs in cherry tomatoes detected by GC–MS. The relative contents of volatile components were illustrated in Fig. 4C. The HP group exhibited significantly higher total VOC contents (T6: 220.37 μg/kg; T7: 190.09 μg/kg; T8: 208.19 μg/kg) compared to the LP group, with the lowest values observed in T1 (44.27 μg/kg) and T2 (87.03 μg/kg). Aldehydes were the dominant class across all samples. Specific aldehydes such as nonanal, which contributes to fatty, citrus and green notes, were presented in all samples, enhancing fruity scent and consumer appeal (Li, Yan, Li, & Wang, 2023). Additionally, the high relative content of acid compounds (0.38 μg/kg) in T8 was consistent with sensory evaluation results, as acids have been reported to enhance flavor complexity and balance even at low odor thresholds (Yuan et al., 2023).

Importantly, several carotenoid-derived volatiles were detected. 6-Methyl-5-hepten-2-one, a lycopene precursor, was contained at higher levels in pink, brown and red varieties, consistent with their higher lycopene content (Vogel, Tieman, Sims, Odabasi, Clark, & Klee, 2010). Similarly, (E)-6,10-dimethyl-5,9-undecadien-2-one, derived from carotenoid degradation, was detected in pink, brown and red tomatoes. In contrast, green, white and yellow varieties exhibited lower levels of these compounds due to lower lycopene contents. Additionally, 2,6,6-trimethyl-1-cyclohexene-1-carboxaldehyde, another carotenoid-derived compound, was detected in pink, brown and red varieties (Baldwin et al., 2000). These findings suggested a metabolic link between carotenoid accumulation and aroma formation, which could explain why the HP group exhibited both superior color and enhanced flavor.

To investigate the olfactory perception potency of the VOCs detected in cherry tomatoes, the odor activity value (OAV) was calculated. Compounds with an OAV > 1 presented in Fig. 4D are widely accepted as contributing significantly to the overall aroma profile (Feng et al., 2015). Aldehydes were the most abundant including hexanal, 2-hexenal, (Z)-2-nonenal, (E)-2-octenal, nonanal, (Z)-4-decenal, decanal, (E)-2,4-decadienal, undecanal and 2,4-nonadienal. The findings of this study were consistent with a previous study by Guan et al., who identified eight key aroma-active compounds with OAV > 100, most of which were approximately the same as this exploration (Guan et al., 2025).

Specifically, hexanal and nonanal were prominently positioned across all cherry tomatoes, contributing grass, citrus, green and fatty aromas. Notably, (E)-2-octenal exhibited remarkably high OAV values in the HP group and in pink and brown varieties, with the highest OAV observed in T8 (OAV = 51.32). For the LP group, T1 was primarily dominated by undecanal, imparting fruity notes, but the overall number of characteristic aroma substances was lower, indicating a less complex aroma profile. White and yellow tomatoes (T2 and T5) contained hexanal, 2-hexenal, nonanal and (Z)-4-decenal, with (Z)-4-decenal exhibiting a remarkably high OAV, imparting a green aroma. These results clarified the key flavor characteristics associated with each preference group.

After 200 permutations, all intersection points between Q2 and the vertical axis in the analysis results remained below zero, demonstrating the model's effectiveness without overfitting (Fig. 5E). To further measure the contribution of each variable, the VIP values of each volatile component were predicted based on the OPLS-DA model. Typically, compounds with a VIP value >1 are considered potential key markers (Feng et al., 2022). 31 volatile components with VIP > 1 were discovered, including 7 aldehydes, 6 alcohols, 4 ketones, 2 esters, 1 alkane, 4 olefins, 1 ether, 1 other and 5 unidentified (Fig. 5G). Thereinto, butanal with the highest VIP value possessed the greatest contribution to tomatoes and brought pungent and green aroma.

Four VOCs (1-hexanal, 1-hexanol, 1-pentanol and α-pinene) were identified as reliable markers for aroma differentiation shared by both GC-IMS and GC–MS methods. Correlation analysis

Correlation analysis was performed between the relative contents of the key aroma compounds and consumer preference scores (Fig. 5I). Among these compounds, (E)-2,4-decadienal, decanal, (E)-2-octenal and 2-hexenal exhibited significant positive correlations with consumer preference (p < 0.05). These C6-C9 aldehydes were known to be responsible for the characteristic fresh, grassy and citrus-like aromas of tomatoes (Londoño-Giraldo et al., 2021). Notably, (E)-2-octenal, which was enriched in the HP group, has been reported as a key driver of consumer preference due to its grassy and floral sensory attributes (Zhang et al., 2026). The strong positive correlations observed for these four aldehydes indicated that they were the primary aroma-active compounds driving consumer preference for cherry tomatoes, particularly within the HP group.

Conclusion

This study combined sensory evaluation, physicochemical analysis and flavor profiling to investigate what drives consumer preference for cherry tomatoes. Based on consumer preference scores, red-fruited varieties were grouped as the high-preference group, while green, white, pink, brown and yellow varieties formed the low-preference group. The high-preference group exhibited the highest preference scores, optimal color, higher moisture content and TSS, and the richest volatile profile, particularly aldehydes. In the low-preference group, green and white tomatoes received lower acceptance due to their unconventional color. Brown tomatoes were distinctive for their high β-carotene and lycopene content. Multivariate analysis further identified a* value, moisture content, TSS and four aldehydes including (E)-2,4-decadienal, decanal, (E)-2-octenal and 2-hexenal as key chemical indicators positively correlated with consumer preference scores. This research elucidated the driving factors of consumer preferences for cherry tomatoes, providing valuable insights for the development of high-quality varieties and supporting informed consumer choices based on nutrient benefits and flavor preference.

Digital supply chain transformation improves corporate carbon emission efficiency in Chinese listed companies
Authors: Jiangying Wei, Xiuwu Zhang, Yingtian Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Supply chain digitalization presents significant potential for aligning economic growth with environmental sustainability; however, the mechanisms through which it enhances carbon emission efficiency remain insufficiently explored in emerging economies. This study investigates the impact of digital transformation on corporate carbon performance in China, utilizing the Supply Chain Innovation and Application Pilot Initiative as a quasi-natural experiment. By analyzing data from Chinese A-share listed companies between 2013 and 2021 using a difference-in-differences approach, the study finds that pilot companies exhibited a substantial improvement in carbon emission efficiency. Two key mechanisms drive this effect: first, external collaboration, wherein digital platforms facilitate co-innovation with supply chain partners to develop low-carbon solutions, and second, internal capability restructuring, which optimizes management processes and promotes data-driven decision-making. The heterogeneity analysis identifies stronger effects in firms characterized by mature governance, non-state ownership, and involvement in competitive, low-pollution industries. These findings contribute to the advancement of green development theory by illustrating how digitalization functions as a dual catalyst, reshaping internal organizational capabilities and nurturing external innovation ecosystems. The study offers empirical insights from China’s institutional context, providing guidance for achieving climate-industrial compatibility in emerging economies while emphasizing the need for context-specific validation across different institutional environments.

Introduction

Global carbon emissions exceeded 36.8 gigatons in 20231, making them a central issue in the climate crisis and driving socio-economic disruptions, from extreme weather events to systemic inequalities in vulnerable regions2. This urgency has placed carbon governance at the forefront of policy agendas, as demonstrated by the Paris Agreement’s net-zero transition target and the European Union’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). However, despite global commitments to reduce emissions, corporations—which account for over 70% of global carbon emissions3—remain pivotal to this transformation, albeit in a highly controversial role. Social science theories have explored this tension: Ecological modernization theory argues that technological innovations can decouple economic growth from emissions4, while critical political economy critiques this optimism, pointing out structural barriers such as profit-driven resource extraction (Foster et al. 2012). Furthermore, the Resource-Based View5 treats carbon efficiency as a strategic asset, although empirical studies show inconsistent adoption, as corporations struggle to balance stakeholder pressures6, regulatory risks7, and competitive dynamics8. In this context, corporate carbon emission efficiency is not only an environmental indicator but also a socio-technical construct shaped by institutional logics, market forces, and cognitive communities (Linnenluecke et al. 2023, Organization & Environment). Understanding how these dynamics operate in specific national contexts, particularly in major emerging economies like China, is essential for developing targeted climate strategies and informing global climate governance efforts.

Existing literature identifies three primary dimensions of factors influencing corporate carbon emission efficiency: First, internal factors—such as technological innovation, management practices, and financial capacity—establish the foundational capabilities of firms to reduce carbon emissions9–11. For example, AI-driven process optimization and research budgets for low-carbon technologies can significantly enhance a company’s carbon management capabilities. Second, external market forces, including industry competition, supply chain pressures, and consumer demand, motivate companies to align operational practices with environmental standards12. Particularly, green consumerism and emission requirements from downstream buyers push firms to enhance their carbon efficiency in a competitive context. Third, policy and institutional drivers—such as carbon pricing mechanisms, government subsidies, and mandatory disclosure requirements—offer institutional incentives for emission reductions13–15.

Although existing studies have identified key factors influencing corporate carbon emission efficiency, most of them analyze these dimensions in isolation, neglecting their interactions and synergies. Consequently, the integration of these factors to achieve higher carbon efficiency remains an unresolved challenge. In this context, supply chain digitalization, as a cross-dimensional key factor, assumes particular significance. It is not only part of external market forces, such as supply chain pressures16, but also enhances internal capabilities—such as technological innovation and data-driven R&D—to improve carbon efficiency17. Supply chain digitalization optimizes corporate carbon governance across multiple dimensions, including technological innovation, stakeholder collaboration, and policy compliance. Moreover, within a policy-driven environment, it facilitates the integration of internal and external resources, forming a collaborative carbon management system. This study positions itself at the intersection of these unresolved debates, advancing green innovation theory through the use of China’s quasi-experimental policy context.

This study focuses on the Chinese context to clarify the theoretical ambiguity surrounding the impact of supply chain digitalization and seeks to answer three key questions: First, through which dual pathways—external collaborative innovation and internal management optimization—does supply chain digitalization enhance corporate carbon emission efficiency in Chinese firms? Second, how do organizational and industry contingency factors moderate these mechanisms within China’s institutional environment? Third, how can the fragmented perspectives of digital capability building and green innovation be integrated theoretically based on the Chinese experience? Using an integrated framework, this research combines resource orchestration theory and the innovation ecosystem paradigm, employing a quasi-experimental design. China’s supply chain innovation and application pilot program serves as an exogenous shock, and the analysis is conducted using a Difference-in-Differences (DID) model. The results confirm that supply chain digitalization enhances carbon emission efficiency among Chinese firms through both technological adoption and the co-evolution of capabilities. Based on the Chinese case, it is concluded that supply chain digitalization, as a relational infrastructure, can amplify sustainability outcomes when aligned with governance maturity and market competition dynamics.

This study makes the following contributions to understanding digitalization and carbon efficiency in the Chinese context: First, it provides robust empirical evidence on the causal relationship between supply chain digitalization and corporate carbon emission efficiency, specifically focusing on China as a major emerging economy. Second, it identifies and validates the dual mechanisms underlying this relationship—internal capability restructuring and external collaborative innovation—within Chinese institutional settings. Third, it explores the boundary conditions that influence the effectiveness of digitalization efforts across various organizational and industry contexts in China. Most notably, this research advances the theoretical framework for green development by integrating organizational capability evolution and innovation ecosystem collaboration into the diffusion of digital technologies, based on Chinese empirical evidence. By illustrating how digital technologies simultaneously reshape both internal organizational structures and external collaboration networks in the Chinese context, this study offers insights into the socio-technical dynamics driving sustainable transformation in China’s industrial system, with potential implications for other emerging economies and areas requiring future research.

Institutional background and research hypotheses

Institutional background

The institutional framework for supply chain innovation and digitalization in China is deeply embedded in the country’s economic restructuring and sustainable development priorities. Since the 19th National Congress of the Communist Party of China in 2017, the government has explicitly prioritized fostering "new growth drivers" through the modernization of the industrial system, with supply chain innovation seen as a critical lever for achieving high-quality economic growth. This policy momentum led to the issuance of the "Guiding Opinions on Promoting Supply Chain Innovation and Application," which positions supply chain digitalization as a dual mechanism to enhance operational efficiency and drive ecological sustainability. Following this, the "Notice on Launching the Supply Chain Innovation and Application Pilot Program" established a national experimental framework, designating 266 enterprises and 55 cities as pilot entities tasked with integrating digital technologies such as the Internet of Things, blockchain, and artificial intelligence into supply chain operations.

These policy interventions reflect China’s institutional commitment to reconciling economic growth with environmental demands within its "carbon peak and carbon neutrality" framework. The pilot program, serving as a quasi-natural experiment, enables the government to integrate carbon governance into corporate strategies while promoting digital supply chain practices. For example, digital supply chains are required to implement emission tracking systems, optimize logistics networks, and adopt green procurement standards, thus aligning corporate actions with broader decarbonization goals18. This institutional setup provides a unique opportunity to explore how state-led digital policies affect corporate carbon performance through technological, organizational, and collaborative mechanisms.

Theoretical and empirical research on supply chain digitalization has primarily focused on developed economies, highlighting market-driven adoption19–21. However, China’s policy-driven approach, which accelerates digital transformation through top-down regulatory frameworks, has not been sufficiently examined in the literature. While emerging studies recognize the role of digital tools in emission reduction22,23, the interaction between institutional requirements, digital infrastructure, and stakeholder coordination—especially in transition economies—remains underexplored.

This gap offers a valuable opportunity to examine how state-led digital transformation influences corporate environmental performance within China’s unique institutional context. The Supply Chain Innovation and Application Pilot Program, through its designated experimental zones and companies, provides an ideal quasi-natural experiment for testing the causal relationship between digitalization and carbon emission efficiency. While prior research has concentrated on market-driven digital adoption in developed economies, China’s policy-led model presents a distinct approach deserving of systematic investigation, particularly regarding environmental outcomes. Building on this context, the present study utilizes data from China’s A-share listed companies to assess the impact of supply chain digitalization on corporate carbon emission efficiency, set against the external shocks introduced by the Supply Chain Innovation and Application Pilot Program.

Research hypothesis

Supply chain digitalization and carbon emission efficiency can be theoretically integrated through several established frameworks in management literature. The Resource-Based View (RBV) and Dynamic Capabilities Theory (DCT) offer complementary perspectives that collectively explain how the digital transformation of supply chains creates sustainable competitive advantages while improving environmental performance. According to RBV, organizations gain competitive advantage through valuable, rare, inimitable, and non-substitutable resources24,25. Meanwhile, DCT, as developed by Teece et al.26, extends this static view by emphasizing how firms reconfigure their resource base to adapt to rapidly changing environments, a key consideration in both digital transformation and environmental management. These complementary theories allow for the conceptualization of supply chain digitalization as both a strategic resource bundle and a capability-enhancing process, capable of fundamentally reshaping how firms manage their carbon footprint.

The theoretical connection between digitalization and carbon efficiency lies in their shared emphasis on resource optimization and process innovation. Digital technologies fundamentally transform information flows, decision-making structures, and inter-organizational coordination mechanisms—factors that directly impact a firm’s carbon efficiency27,28. As Majeed et al.29 demonstrate, information technologies allow firms to monitor, analyze, and optimize resource consumption with unparalleled precision. Moreover, improvements in carbon efficiency often require complex coordination across organizational boundaries, a challenge that digital platforms are particularly adept at addressing30. In this context, supply chain digitalization can be viewed as an enabling infrastructure that supports various pathways to enhance carbon efficiency. Collaborative innovation enablement

Dynamic Capabilities Theory highlights three core organizational capabilities—sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring—that enable firms to adapt to dynamic environments and promote collaborative innovation. By enhancing these capabilities, digitalization drives the development of low-carbon products, processes, and business models, ultimately improving carbon efficiency.

Sensing Capability Digital infrastructure as an environmental monitoring mechanism. Supply chain digitalization enhances firms’ sensing capabilities by integrating real-time, cross-organizational data flows. Technologies such as IoT sensors, blockchain traceability systems, and AI analytics function as environmental monitoring tools, enabling firms to detect inefficiencies and anticipate sustainability challenges31. For example, IoT systems can identify energy-intensive production stages, while predictive analytics can forecast regulatory shifts, such as carbon tax adjustments32,33. This enhanced environmental awareness aids in identifying emission hotspots and fosters collaborative process redesigns among supply chain partners. As Dubey et al.34 suggest, converting environmental data into actionable insights transforms collaborative innovation into a strategic response to emerging sustainability demands.

Seizing Capability Coordinating cross-boundary eco-innovation. Digital platforms strengthen seizing capabilities by removing barriers to resource integration and fostering joint environmental actions. Cloud-based collaborative tools, including digital twins and virtual R&D environments, enable firms to co-develop environmental solutions with supply chain partners35. For instance, manufacturers use simulation technologies to optimize product lifecycle efficiency through iterative feedback with suppliers18. Blockchain-based governance systems further align incentives through automated performance monitoring and reward mechanisms based on predetermined environmental metrics. These digital mechanisms convert identified opportunities into concrete innovations by facilitating the integration of complementary resources and establishing shared environmental goals across organizational boundaries.

Reconfiguring Capability Institutionalizing sustainability through digital governance. Digitalization strengthens reconfiguring capabilities, ensuring that collaborative innovation leads to sustained structural change. Digital technologies enable the modularization and scaling of environmental practices across supply chain networks36. For example, advanced resource planning systems that reduce production waste can be standardized and extended to suppliers, creating a connected sustainability ecosystem37. Similarly, digital decision support systems expedite carbon reduction efforts by providing real-time environmental performance data to cross-functional teams. These technological reconfigurations institutionalize sustainability practices by embedding environmental considerations into organizational governance structures.

The interaction between sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities creates a dynamic feedback loop: digital tools enhance collaborative innovation, driving ongoing improvements in carbon efficiency. As firms jointly identify ecological challenges, mobilize collaborative solutions, and institutionalize sustainable practices, they generate relational rents—value that cannot be achieved through unilateral efforts38. Based on this theoretical framework and considering the Chinese institutional context, we propose the following hypothesis:

H1: The digital transformation of supply chains improves corporate carbon emission efficiency by enhancing firms’ collaborative innovation capabilities.

Management enablement

The core tenet of the Resource-Based View (RBV) is that competitive advantage arises from the combination of a firm’s internal, heterogeneous resources and capabilities, which must be valuable, rare, inimitable, and organized (VRIO framework)24. The digital technologies deployed in supply chain digital transformation, such as IoT, blockchain, and artificial intelligence, represent strategic resources. Their ability to collect and process real-time data enables precise identification of energy waste in production and logistics (value). Early adopters of advanced technologies can secure temporary monopoly advantages (rarity), while digital capabilities, which depend on organizational knowledge and cross-departmental collaboration, are difficult for competitors to quickly replicate (inimitability)39. For instance, a manufacturer that uses blockchain to track the carbon footprint of raw materials and AI to optimize production scheduling can reduce resource consumption per unit output and create a differentiated green brand advantage. These resources are systematically integrated into operational processes through governance structures (such as digital committees), ultimately driving both cost savings and enhanced carbon emission efficiency40,41.

Management capabilities play a crucial mediating role in this process. RBV highlights that the value derived from resources depends on how effectively they are configured and utilized within the firm42. Digital transformation enhances total factor productivity (TFP)—a key indicator of resource utilization efficiency—by automating processes (e.g., robotic process automation (RPA) reducing manual intervention), supporting data-driven decisions (e.g., business intelligence (BI) systems selecting low-carbon suppliers), and fostering cross-departmental knowledge integration (e.g., cloud platforms sharing environmental technologies). TFP growth drives carbon emission efficiency improvements through two mechanisms: the intensive effect, which reduces energy and material consumption per unit output, directly decreasing carbon emissions,and the technological substitution effect, where capital released by improved productivity is reinvested in clean technologies (e.g., photovoltaic systems), replacing high-carbon production methods43. Empirical evidence shows that firms with stronger management capabilities perform better under carbon policies, achieving significantly higher TFP growth than their peers, thus confirming the critical role of management capabilities in green transformation40,41.

Moreover, digitalization extends the traditional boundaries of RBV, shifting firms from closed resource management to ecosystem-level resource complementarity. Digital platforms (e.g., supply chain collaboration networks) allow firms to internalize external partners’ green capabilities, enhancing their competitive advantage. For example, automotive manufacturers use digital twin technology to simulate product lifecycle carbon emissions with battery suppliers or integrate their suppliers’ waste heat recovery algorithms into their production systems44. This cross-organizational resource collaboration not only overcomes individual firms’ capability constraints but also establishes an ecosystem-level innovation network, transforming carbon emission efficiency from individual optimization to systemic reconfiguration. In this context, the openness and connectivity of digital technologies are key to unlocking the value of external resources, thus redefining the concept of “resources” in RBV45.

Based on this framework and the Chinese institutional context, we propose the following hypothesis:

H2: Supply chain digital transformation enhances corporate carbon emission efficiency by promoting management capabilities.

Sample selection and data sources

This study examines A-share companies listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock exchanges as of September 21, 2018. The research period covers five years prior to the policy announcement and three years after (2013–2021), providing a comprehensive view of the policy’s impact. After excluding financial sector firms, those with incomplete financial statements, companies flagged for operational irregularities (e.g., ST-designated companies), and those with fewer than 50 trading days during the estimation period, the final sample includes 28,473 valid observations. To ensure data processing accuracy, all continuous variables were Winsorized at the 1% level, which enhances the robustness and reliability of the analysis.

The post-treatment period reveals a nuanced temporal evolution of treatment effects that warrants careful consideration. The coefficients show an initial positive impact at t=0 (approximately 0.029), followed by relatively stable effects at t=1 and t=2, and a potential strengthening at t=3. This pattern aligns with theoretical expectations regarding the gradual nature of digital transformation benefits, which typically require organizational learning, capability development, and process integration to fully materialize. The temporal stability observed during the initial post-treatment years, coupled with the potential increase in later periods, suggests that while digital supply chain initiatives generate immediate improvements in carbon efficiency, their full impact may require extended implementation periods to be fully realized. However, our relatively short post-treatment observation window (2018-2021) presents a limitation, as digital transformation initiatives typically require 3-5 years to reach operational maturity. 2.Placebo Test

Placebo Test

A “counterfactual” approach is employed in this study to test whether pilot and non-pilot enterprises follow a common trend. The procedure assumes that the "Supply Chain Innovation and Application Pilot" policy was implemented 3 or 4 years earlier, in 2015 or 2014, respectively. Collaborative Innovation Enablement

The process through which supply chain digitalization enhances corporate carbon emission efficiency via collaborative innovation can be viewed as a dynamic capability-enhancing cycle. First, digital technologies enhance a firm’s environmental sensing capabilities, enabling real-time monitoring and precise identification of carbon emission hotspots and inefficiencies in the supply chain. Second, digital platforms lower barriers to cross-organizational collaboration, creating a shared space for innovation where firms can integrate complementary resources and expertise with supply chain partners to co-develop low-carbon solutions. Finally, digital systems enable the rapid scaling of these collaborative innovations, embedding environmental sustainability into organizational structures and decision-making processes. This collaborative innovation process generates systemic carbon reduction effects that individual firm efforts cannot achieve, establishing a self-reinforcing cycle: improved environmental data fosters more effective collaboration, and successful collaborations further refine data collection and analysis capabilities.

2.Management Enablement

Management Enablement

In modern management, managerial innovation is crucial for enabling supply chain digitalization. First, digital transformation reduces unnecessary human involvement through automation, significantly improving management efficiency. For example, the advent of autonomous vehicles, automated warehouses, and smart ports in recent years signifies a transformation in traditional supply chains. Second, supply chain digitalization significantly accelerates decision-making processes. In an IoT-based virtual supply chain network, firms can access real-time demand information from various nodes, allowing decision-makers to make key business decisions more quickly. Additionally, automation technologies reduce intermediary interference in the decision-making process. For instance, smart contract technologies automate the transfer of ownership and payment processes in the supply chain, which not only accelerates transactions but also enhances overall operational efficiency. Lastly, supply chain digitalization improves decision-making quality by increasing both the quantity and quality of information. In terms of information volume, traditional supply chains often face issues of information silos, which limit a firm’s visibility and may result in inefficient resource allocation. The application of IoT uncovers vast amounts of underlying data, providing valuable information for decision-making. Regarding information quality, blockchain technology’s immutability and distributed verification offer a strong mechanism to ensure the authenticity of information, further enhancing the accuracy of decisions.

To provide a more nuanced understanding of the management enablement mechanism, we disaggregate this pathway into specific management capabilities that are enhanced by digitalization. While total factor productivity (TFP) improvement serves as a comprehensive measure of a firm’s digital transformation effects, it conflates multiple management dimensions that could independently affect carbon efficiency. Therefore, we examine specific management capabilities, particularly real-time monitoring and inventory management, which represent core areas where digital technologies directly enhance operational efficiency. Inventory turnover ratio serves as a critical indicator of real-time monitoring capabilities, as digital supply chain technologies enable firms to track inventory levels, demand patterns, and supply flows in real-time, thereby optimizing inventory management and reducing waste that contributes to carbon emissions.

Industry-Based Heterogeneity Analysis

Industry-Based Heterogeneity Analysis

Given the significant variation in digital transformation implementation across industries and the unique characteristics of supply chain structures and market environments in each sector, this study explores how digital transformation impacts internal specialization within firms. It also investigates whether these effects differ between regulated and competitive industries, as well as between high-pollution and low-pollution industries. To ensure methodological rigor and address potential confounding effects, we provide clear definitions and data sources for our industry classifications. Regulated industries, as defined by the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC), include sectors subject to substantial government oversight and price controls, such as utilities (electricity, gas, and water supply), telecommunications, transportation, and financial services. Competitive industries encompass market-driven sectors like manufacturing, retail, technology, and others with minimal regulatory intervention. For the pollution dimension, high-pollution industries are identified according to the Ministry of Environmental Protection’s "List of Industries with Serious Environmental Pollution," which includes steel, chemicals, cement, paper, mining, and petroleum refining. Low-pollution industries are those with minimal direct environmental impact, such as information technology, professional services, education, and light manufacturing.

We acknowledge that these binary classifications may conflate multiple industry characteristics, as some regulated industries (e.g., utilities) can also be highly polluting, creating potential confounding effects that our current framework may not fully account for. To address this issue, we recognize that regulatory intensity and pollution levels might jointly influence the effectiveness of digital transformation in improving carbon efficiency. For example, regulated high-pollution industries may face dual constraints from both regulatory compliance costs and environmental remediation requirements, limiting their capacity for digital investment. In contrast, competitive low-pollution industries may have the flexibility and incentive to rapidly adopt and optimize digital technologies for carbon efficiency. This intersection suggests that the effectiveness of supply chain digitalization depends on the complex interaction between market structure and environmental characteristics, rather than on each factor independently. Future research would benefit from more refined classification schemes that capture these multidimensional industry characteristics or from using continuous measures of regulatory intensity and pollution levels to better understand their combined effects on digital transformation outcomes.

Conclusions

Main findings summary

This study, based on the exogenous shock environment created by China’s Supply Chain Innovation and Application Pilot Program, analyzes the impact of supply chain digitalization on corporate carbon emission efficiency using data from Chinese A-share listed companies. The findings indicate that supply chain digitalization enhances carbon emission efficiency among Chinese firms through two main mechanisms: first, it fosters close collaboration between firms and customers through collaborative innovation, thereby improving firm performance; second, it enhances management efficiency by streamlining management tasks, speeding up decision-making, and improving decision quality, all of which significantly boost carbon emission efficiency. The heterogeneity analysis reveals that the effectiveness of supply chain digitalization in driving carbon emission efficiency is more pronounced in Chinese firms with mature corporate governance. Additionally, supply chain digitalization has a more significant impact on carbon emission efficiency in competitive industries, low-pollution industries, and non-state-owned enterprises within the Chinese context.

Theoretical contributions and policy implications

Theoretical contributions

This study enhances the theoretical understanding of the relationship between digitalization and environmental outcomes in the Chinese context by offering three key contributions that extend beyond existing descriptive frameworks. Our theoretical development challenges the prevailing assumption that digitalization uniformly benefits environmental performance across all contexts. Instead, based on Chinese empirical evidence, we propose a more nuanced understanding based on boundary conditions, mechanism interactions, and institutional contingencies.

The first theoretical contribution advances contingency theory by establishing boundary conditions for the effectiveness of digitalization pathways within Chinese institutional settings. Rather than assuming universal benefits of digitalization, our findings demonstrate that collaborative innovation mechanisms perform most effectively in competitive, low-pollution industries in China, where knowledge-intensive processes and inter-organizational coordination foster favorable conditions for external partnerships. In contrast, management enablement pathways are more effective in Chinese organizations with established governance structures and standardized operational procedures that support internal capability development. This conditional effectiveness framework challenges the implicit universality assumption in existing literature and offers theoretical guidance for predicting when specific digitalization approaches will yield optimal environmental outcomes in similar institutional contexts.

The second theoretical contribution expands mechanism interaction theory by showing the complementary interdependence between collaborative innovation and management enablement pathways among Chinese firms. Our theoretical model suggests that external collaborative capabilities enhance the absorptive capacity needed to effectively utilize internal management improvements, while sophisticated internal management systems provide the foundation required for successful external collaboration. This complementarity suggests that digitalization mechanisms function through synergistic channels, where the returns on investment in one pathway increase as the complementary pathway develops. This insight moves beyond linear additive models and introduces a dynamic capability recombination framework that explains how digitalization pathways interact to improve environmental performance based on the Chinese experience.

The third theoretical contribution integrates institutional theory with digitalization research by illustrating how Chinese ownership structures and market arrangements systematically moderate the effectiveness of digital transformation. The framework shows that China’s unique institutional contexts shape both the incentives for digital adoption and the organizational capabilities required to convert technological investments into carbon efficiency improvements. Market-oriented institutional environments within China provide stronger performance incentives and greater organizational flexibility, enhancing the effectiveness of digitalization pathways compared to state-dominated contexts. This institutional contingency perspective moves beyond technology-deterministic explanations to incorporate structural factors that determine when and how digital technologies can deliver environmental benefits in the Chinese setting.

Policy implications for Chinese practitioners

Our findings offer actionable insights for Chinese corporate managers seeking to improve carbon efficiency through digital transformation strategies. The evidence highlights that successful digitalization in China requires the simultaneous development of external collaborative capabilities and internal management competencies. Chinese managers should adopt integrated approaches, prioritizing investments in digital platforms that enable real-time data sharing with supply chain partners while simultaneously upgrading internal systems for inventory management, asset utilization, and decision-making processes.

The heterogeneity analysis identifies critical factors that Chinese managers must consider when planning digital transformation initiatives. Chinese firms with mature governance structures and those operating in competitive market environments are better positioned to realize carbon efficiency gains from digitalization investments. This suggests that organizational readiness assessments should precede major digital investments in China, with particular focus on governance maturity and market positioning. Chinese managers in regulated or high-pollution industries should anticipate longer implementation timelines and higher resource requirements to achieve meaningful carbon efficiency improvements.

For Chinese policymakers, our results suggest that digitalization policies should be tailored to industry characteristics and ownership structures within China’s institutional framework. The stronger effects observed in competitive industries and non-state-owned enterprises in China indicate that market-based instruments, such as competitive grants, tax incentives, and performance-based subsidies, are likely to generate better outcomes than uniform regulatory mandates in the Chinese context. Chinese policymakers should consider sector-specific strategies that prioritize support for industries with higher carbon reduction potential, while also offering enhanced assistance for high-pollution sectors that face greater barriers and resource constraints in implementing digital transformation.

Potential implications for other emerging economies

While our findings are derived from the Chinese context and cannot be directly generalized, they may offer insights for policymakers in other emerging economies seeking to leverage supply chain digitalization for carbon efficiency improvements. However, the transferability of policy approaches would require careful adaptation to local contextual factors that vary substantially across different emerging economy settings.

Institutional quality represents a fundamental determinant of digitalization policy effectiveness in international contexts. Emerging economies with weaker institutional frameworks may find limited success with market-based digitalization incentives that rely on competitive mechanisms and voluntary adoption. These countries may need to prioritize institutional capacity building, including regulatory framework development and property rights establishment, before implementing sophisticated digitalization policies. Conversely, emerging economies with stronger institutional foundations might consider approaches similar to those demonstrated effective in competitive Chinese industry contexts, including innovation competitions and private sector partnership programs, though such applications would require empirical validation in their specific contexts.

Environmental regulatory regime variations create another critical consideration for international policy design. Countries with stringent environmental regulations and robust enforcement mechanisms may potentially observe stronger carbon efficiency improvements from digitalization initiatives, as firms face clear incentives for adopting emission-reducing technologies and processes. Emerging economies with less developed environmental regulatory frameworks might consider sequencing policy implementation by first strengthening environmental enforcement capabilities before introducing digitalization incentives, or alternatively, by combining digitalization policies with regulatory strengthening initiatives to create complementary policy effects.

Digital infrastructure development levels fundamentally moderate the feasibility and effectiveness of supply chain digitalization policies across international contexts. Emerging economies with limited digital infrastructure require foundational investments in broadband connectivity, digital literacy programs, and cybersecurity frameworks before sector-specific digitalization policies can achieve meaningful impact. Countries with more advanced digital ecosystems might focus resources on targeted industry interventions and innovation support programs. Following Wang and Zhang47, who demonstrate varying effectiveness of government support mechanisms across different institutional contexts, future research should investigate how international policymakers can design digitalization programs that align with local institutional characteristics, with state-led economies potentially achieving better outcomes through centralized coordination while market-oriented economies may benefit from competitive innovation mechanisms and private sector collaboration frameworks.

Limitations and future research directions

While this study offers valuable insights into the relationship between supply chain digitalization and carbon efficiency in China, several limitations point to important avenues for future research. First, the restriction of our sample to Chinese A-share listed companies significantly limits the generalizability of our findings beyond the Chinese context, as these firms tend to be larger, more sophisticated, and better equipped for digital transformation than the broader population of Chinese firms. This selection bias is particularly concerning given that small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), which comprise the majority of supply chains in China, face unique barriers to digitalization, such as limited financial resources, technological capabilities, and human capital. As demonstrated by Wang and Zhang40,41, firm size and resource constraints result in different patterns of digital adoption and innovation outcomes, with SMEs showing distinct relationships between digital transformation initiatives and performance outcomes compared to larger enterprises. Consequently, our results may overestimate the average treatment effect for the broader Chinese economy, as SMEs may experience weaker or delayed benefits from supply chain digitalization due to these constraints and their distinct organizational structures.

Second, while our analysis of the collaborative innovation mechanism provides valuable insights through joint patent applications and supply chain relationship measures, these indicators represent formal, observable manifestations of collaboration and may not fully capture the breadth of digitally-enabled collaborative activities. The theoretical framework on sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities encompasses broader organizational processes, such as real-time environmental monitoring, cross-boundary coordination, and capability institutionalization, which extend beyond what our current empirical measures can directly observe. Future research would benefit from developing more comprehensive measurement approaches that can capture these nuanced organizational capabilities, potentially through primary data collection methods like surveys or case studies that directly assess how Chinese firms develop and deploy these dynamic capabilities within digital supply chain contexts.

Future research should address these limitations using multiple complementary approaches to enhance both empirical scope and measurement precision. Regarding sample representativeness within China, stratified analyses examining how digitalization effects vary across different firm sizes would offer valuable insights into the unique challenges and opportunities faced by Chinese SMEs in implementing digital supply chain technologies. Alternative sampling strategies that include unlisted companies, especially SMEs in China, would provide a more comprehensive understanding of the impact of digitalization across the entire Chinese business ecosystem. Longitudinal studies with longer observation periods would better capture the full temporal dynamics of digital transformation benefits in China, as our relatively short post-treatment period may not reflect the complete learning curve required for smaller Chinese firms to realize carbon efficiency gains. Research employing mixed-methods approaches, combining quantitative analysis with qualitative case studies of Chinese SME digitalization experiences, would provide deeper insights into the mechanisms and barriers that influence successful digital transformation outcomes in China.

To improve measurement precision for collaborative innovation mechanisms, future research could develop more comprehensive empirical measures that better capture the sensing, seizing, and reconfiguring capabilities in digital supply chain contexts within China. This might involve constructing composite indices based on multiple observable indicators or employing primary data collection methods that directly assess organizational capabilities among Chinese firms. Additionally, most importantly, comparative studies across different institutional environments would help establish whether our findings from China hold in other contexts and would enhance the international generalizability of findings beyond the Chinese context explored in this study. Such cross-country research is essential to determine the broader applicability of our theoretical framework and empirical findings.

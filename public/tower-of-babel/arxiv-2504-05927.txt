A nongraphical obstacle problem for elastic curves
Marius Müller, Kensuke Yoshizawa

Abstract
. We study an obstacle problem for the length-penalized elastic bending energy for open planar curves pinned at the boundary. We first consider the case without length penalization and investigate the role of global minimizers among graph curves in our minimization problem for planar curves. In addition, for large values of the length-penalization parameter λ > 0 \lambda>0 , we expose an explicit threshold parameter above which minimizers touch the obstacle, regardless of its shape. On contrary, for small values of λ > 0 \lambda>0 we show that the minimizers do not touch the obstacle, and they are given by an explicit elastica.

1.
Introduction

In this article we study an obstacle problem for the (length-penalized)
elastic bending energy

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma]:=\int_{\gamma}|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s+\lambda\int_{\gamma}1\;\mathrm{d}s=B[\gamma]+\lambda L[\gamma],

where
\gamma\in W^{2,2}(0,1,\mathbf{R}^{2})
is an immersed curve with curvature vector
\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\partial_{s}^{2}\gamma
and
\lambda\geq 0
is a penalization parameter.
The critical points of the bending energy
B
have attracted great interest for more than 250 years, and their history can go back to L. Euler
[
5
]
.
Recently, so-called
obstacle problems
for the bending energy have
been studied: e.g., minimization problems for
B
(i) among graphs with unilateral constraint
[
1
,
17
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
7
,
8
]
; (ii) among graphs with adhesive terms
[
13
]
; (iii) among closed curves confined in a given set
[
4
]
; and (iv) an obstacle problem for a generalized bending energy
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we consider the obstacle problem for the bending energy for
open planar
curves.
More precisely, for given
obstacle function

\psi:\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
we consider the minimization problem with the admissible set

(1.2)

A:=\{\gamma\in W^{2,2}_{\rm imm}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})\mid\gamma(0)=(0,0),\ \gamma(1)=(1,0),\ \gamma^{2}(x)\geq\psi(\gamma^{1}(x))\ \textrm{for all $x\in[0,1]$}\}.

Here,
W^{2,2}_{\rm imm}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})
denotes the set of immersed
W^{2,2}
-curves:

(1.3)

W^{2,2}_{\rm imm}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2}):=\Set{\gamma\in W^{2,2}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})}{\,|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|\neq 0\ \text{ for all}\ x\in[0,1]}.

In general we impose the following assumptions on the obstacle function
\psi
:

(A1)

\psi:\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
is uniformly continuous.

(A2)

\psi|_{(-\infty,0]}<0
and
\psi|_{[1,\infty)}<0
.

(A3)

\max_{x\in[0,1]}\psi(x)>0
.

An important special case, which we will often consider, is the case of
symmetric cone obstacles
, that is,
\psi|_{(-\infty,\frac{1}{2}]}
is affine linear and
\psi=\psi(1-\cdot)
.
Notice that without Assumption (A3), each minimizer would be given by a trivial line segment connecting
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
.

It turns out that in the case of
\lambda=0

global minimizers in
A
can not exist.
Due to this fact, we study only local minimizers in this case. On contrary, for
\lambda>0
one readily obtains existence of minimizers with the standard direct method. This motivates again the consideration of the length-penalized elastic energy.
A goal of this article is to understand how
the behavior of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
depends on
\lambda
.

Many articles involving the obstacle problem for the bending energy have focused on the smaller admissible set of
graphs
lying above the obstacle, i.e. the authors minimize in

(1.4)

\displaystyle A_{\rm graph}:=\Set{\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbf{R}^{2}}{\gamma(x)=(x,u(x))\ \text{ for some }\ u\in W^{2,2}(0,1)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(0,1)\text{ with }u\geq\psi}\subseteq A.

Our larger admissible set
A
explicitly allows for open nongraphical curves, which we believe is a less studied subject in the current literature.

Allowing the curves to be non-graphical yields new difficulties.
One of difficulties comes from
loss of compactness
.
For example, let
c_{\lambda}\in A
be a circular arc with radius
\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for a sufficiently small
\lambda>0
. Then we see that
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[c_{\lambda}]=O(\sqrt{\lambda})
as
\lambda\to 0
.
The smaller
\lambda
, the greater the distance between
c_{\lambda}
and
\psi
, and this also implies that
c_{\lambda}
does not touch
\psi
.
On the other hand, as in Remark
5.1
below, there also exists an admissible curve
C_{\lambda}\in A
such that
C_{\lambda}
always touches
\psi
but also satisfies
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[C_{\lambda}]=O(\sqrt{\lambda})
as
\lambda\to 0
.
The points where the curves
C_{\lambda}
touch the obstacle escape to infinity as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
.
As a result, one can find both a ‘touching’ planar curve and a ‘non-touching’ planar curve with the vanishing energy as
\lambda\to 0
, and hence it is not easy to give some answers to the question

(Q1)

How large is the
coincidence set

I_{\gamma}:=\{x\in[0,1]\mid\gamma^{2}(x)=\psi(\gamma^{1}(x))\}
of a minimizer
\gamma\in A
?

It will turn out that the answer to this question will in general depend on
\lambda
and the precise shape of
\psi
.
This is completely different from results for
graphical curves, as it is shown that minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{0}=B
among the graphical curves must always touch the obstacle.
In addition, our problem has the same difficulties as those in the existing literature.
For example, several useful tools such as a maximum principle are not available since
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
is an energy of higher order.
Another difficulty is that (local) minimizers for the obstacle problem are characterized
by a
so-called
variational inequality
, not equality.
This causes a loss of regularity: in fact, it is well known that the optimal regularity for minimizers among open graphical curves (in the sense of Sobolev class) is
W^{3,\infty}
.
Therefore, it is not a standard matter to answer questions about (local) minimizers
\gamma\in A
as follows:

(Q2)

What is their
optimal regularity
?

(Q3)

What are
geometric properties
of (local) minimizers? (E.g. symmetry, graph representation etc.)

Note that due to such difficulties questions (Q1)–(Q3) are delicate even if we restrict the class
A
to the symmetric class

A_{\rm sym}
, where

(1.5)

A_{\rm sym}:=\{\gamma\in A\mid\gamma(1-x)=(1-\gamma_{1}(x),\gamma_{2}(x))\ \text{ for all }\ x\in[0,1]\}.

Our first main result concerns the case
\lambda=0
as a natural extension of existing studies.
Recall from
[
14
]
that if
\psi
is a symmetric cone obstacle and satisfies
\psi(\frac{1}{2})<h_{*}
, where

(1.6)

\displaystyle h_{*}:=2\mathsf{B}(\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{1}{2})^{-1}\simeq 0.83463

and
\mathsf{B}
denotes the Beta function,
then minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{0}=B
in
A_{\rm graph}\cap A_{\rm sym}
exist and can be computed explicitly. More precisely, they are given by the curves we call
symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae
in this paper. These consist of two
isometric copies of a certain critical point
\gamma_{\rm rect}
chosen in such a way that
\kappa(0)=0
and the obstacle is touched at its tip
(\frac{1}{2},\psi(\frac{1}{2}))
with a vertical tangent vector (see Figure
1
(left)).
Symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae cannot be considered in
A_{\rm graph}
if
\psi
is too large, but we can extend this notion to non-graphical curves (see Figure
1
and Definition
4.1
for more details).
As symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae yield minimizers in
A_{\rm graph}
for suitably small obstacles, it is reasonable to expect that they also yield local minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
in
A
. However, this conclusion turns out to be false above the aforementioned threshold height
h_{*}
. This destabilization phenomenon is characterized in the first main result and can be understood as a contribution to question (Q3) in the case of
\lambda=0
.

Theorem 1.1

(Destabilization of symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae)
.

Assume that
\psi
is a symmetric cone obstacle and satisfies Assumptions (A1)–(A3). Let
\gamma\in A
be a unique symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica.
Then the following assertions hold:

(i)

if
\psi(\frac{1}{2})<h_{*}
, then
\gamma
is a local minimizer of
B
in
A_{\rm sym}
(but not a global minimizer);

(ii)

if
\psi(\frac{1}{2})\geq h_{*}
, then
\gamma
is not a local minimizer of
B
in
A_{\rm sym}
(as well as
A
),

where
h_{*}>0
is the universal constant defined by (
1.6
).

Figure 1.
Symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae with
\psi(\frac{1}{2})=h_{*}/2
(top left),
\psi(\frac{1}{2})=h_{*}
(top center),
\psi(\frac{1}{2})=2h_{*}
(top right), where
h_{*}\simeq 0.83463
(cf. (
1.6
)). The bottom three figures represent rectangular elasticae
\gamma_{\rm rect}
of each corresponding top figure.

The key ingredient for the study of this destabilization phenomenon is the discussion of the variational inequality, see Section
4
for details.

The variational inequality we derive can also be used to give an answer to (Q2) — again optimal
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity can be obtained under the mild assumption that the obstacle is Lipschitz continuous (see Corollary
3.8
below).

Our two remaining main theorems deal with question (Q1), i.e., they study the coincidence set of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm sym}
. Note that for large values of
\lambda
the length of
L[\gamma]
plays an important role in the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. Due to the scaling behavior of the length (
L[r\gamma]=rL[\gamma]
for any
r>0
) one would expect minimizers for large
\lambda
to be as
short as possible
and in particular to touch the obstacle. On contrary, for small
\lambda>0
the bending term
B
dominates the behavior of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. Its scaling behavior is different from the length; more precisely
B[r\gamma]=r^{-1}B[\gamma]
for all
r>0
. In particular, for small
\lambda>0
minimizers are expected to be larger and in particular nontouching. These expectations are confirmed by the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.2

(Nontouching symmetric minimizers for small
\lambda>0
)
.

Let
\psi
be a symmetric cone obstacle and satisfy

(1.7)

\displaystyle\psi(\tfrac{1}{2})<h_{\lambda},

where
h_{\lambda}>0
is a constant given by (
2.12
).
Then there exists some
\bar{\lambda}>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\bar{\lambda})
each minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm sym}
does not touch the obstacle, i.e.,
I_{\gamma}=\emptyset
.
Moreover, in this case the minimizer is unique and given by the critical point
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
.

Here,
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
is called
(\lambda,1,1)
-longer arc
, and it will be discussed in more detail in Section
2.1
(see Figure
2
).

Figure 2.
(\lambda,1,1)
-Longer arcs (
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
) with
\lambda={1}/{20}
,
\lambda=\hat{\lambda}/2\simeq 0.35355
,
\lambda=\hat{\lambda}\simeq 0.70710
(from left to right), after rescaling.

Theorem 1.3

(Touching minimizers for large
\lambda
)
.

There exists
\hat{\lambda}>0
such that for any
\lambda>\hat{\lambda}
and any obstacle
\psi:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}
satisfying (A1)–(A3),
each minimizer
\gamma
of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
touches
\psi
, i.e.,
I_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
.

Interestingly, the value
\hat{\lambda}\simeq 0.70107
is known explicitly and can be chosen independently of
\psi
. This is not the case for
\bar{\lambda}
in Theorem
1.2
. It is an interesting question for future research, which conditions on the obstacle
\psi
admit the choice
\bar{\lambda}=\hat{\lambda}
in Theorem
1.2
.

While the results seem likely due to the scaling behavior of
B
and
L
, the absence of a maximum principle makes it nonstandard to classify the coincidence set. For the minimization in
A_{\rm graph}
, a maximum principle for the quantity
v=\frac{u^{\prime\prime}}{(1+u^{\prime 2})^{\frac{5}{4}}}
can be used to derive nonemptyness of the coincidence set. We refer to
[
1
]
for a detailed explanation. Our results are not at all relying on any maximum principle but more on the explicit knowledge of critical curves of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
. These are called
penalized pinned elasticae
and have been studied in
[
16
]
, which forms the basis of our approach. In particular the stability analysis of critical points in
[
16
]
becomes relevant in the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We remark that each minimizer
\gamma
of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
must be a critical curve of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
on the noncoincidence set
[0,1]\setminus I_{\gamma}
.
However, touching candidates with
I_{\gamma}\neq\emptyset
are not necessarily globally critical points of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
and hence further analysis is required, in particular for ruling out touching candidates in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Oftentimes, configurations of minimizers can be ruled out by means of
energy arguments
, i.e., by showing that the investigated configuration requires an energy lying above the energy infimum. See e.g.
[
4
, Section 7]
for an argument that rules out embedded minimizers just by looking at the energy of embedded competitors. However, for small
\lambda>0
the touching phenomenon can not be ruled out purely by looking at the energy of touching curves: recall that as mentioned earlier, we may construct a family of curves
\{C_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda>0}
that touch a symmetric cone obstacle for any
\lambda>0
but
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[C_{\lambda}]\to 0
as
\lambda\to 0
.

To achieve the required compactness we will look at an auxiliary
minimization problem of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
subject to a
rhomb obstacle constraint
, i.e. we minimize
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in

(1.8)

A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}:=\Set{\gamma\in W^{2,2}_{\rm imm}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})}{\begin{array}[]{l}\gamma(0)=(0,0),\ \gamma(1)=(1,0),\;\gamma(1-x)=(1-\gamma^{1}(x),\gamma^{2}(x)),\\
|\gamma^{2}(x)|\geq\psi(\gamma^{1}(x)))\text{ in }[0,1]\end{array}}.

The obstacle is then to be understood as the region between the graphs of
\psi
and
-\psi
(which forms a rhomb if
\psi
is affine linear). The compactness of this rhomb will play a decisive role in the discussion of the coincidence set of minimizers.

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we collect some useful facts about critical points of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. In Section
3
we discuss existence and regularity of minimizers for the problem we study. Section
4
deals with the question of local minimality of symmetric cut-and-glued free elasticae in the case of
\lambda=0
. Section
5
deals with the noncoincidence of minimizers for small
\lambda>0
and Section
6
is dedicated to the coincidence phenomenon for large values of
\lambda
.

2.1.

Let

.

Let

.
We call

\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
.

2.2.
Figure-eight elastica

For given
n\in\mathbf{N}
and the unique zero
q_{*}
of
q\mapsto 2\mathrm{E}(q)-\mathrm{K}(q)
, define an
\frac{n}{2}
-fold figure-eight elastica
by

(2.16)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm leaf}^{\lambda,n}(s):=\frac{1}{\alpha_{*}}\begin{pmatrix}2\mathrm{E}(\am(\alpha_{*}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{*}),q_{*}),q_{*})+2\mathrm{E}(q_{*})-\alpha_{*}s\\
\sqrt{2}\cn(\alpha_{*}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{*}),q_{*})\end{pmatrix},\ \ \text{where }\ \alpha_{*}:=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2(2q_{*}^{2}-1)}}

with
s\in[0,2n\mathrm{K}(q_{*})/\alpha_{*}]
.
The length of
\gamma_{\rm leaf}^{\lambda,n}
is given by
L[\gamma_{\rm leaf}^{\lambda,n}]=2n\mathrm{K}(q_{*})/\alpha_{*}
.
The signed curvature is then given by

(2.17)

\displaystyle k(s)=2\alpha_{*}q_{*}\cn(\alpha_{*}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{*}),q_{*})\quad\text{for }\ s\in[0,L].

As shown already in
[
3
, Proposition 4.8]
, a
\frac{1}{2}
-fold figure-eight elastica is the
unique global minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm drop}=\{\gamma\in W^{2,2}_{\rm imm}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})\,|\,\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)\}
:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\lambda>0
and suppose that
\gamma\in A_{\rm drop}
.
Then,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma]\geq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{\rm leaf}^{\lambda,1}]=2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{(4q_{*}^{2}-3)\mathrm{K}(q_{*})+2\mathrm{E}(q_{*})}{\sqrt{2q_{*}^{2}-1}}.

Equality in (
2.18
) is attained if and only if
\gamma
is a
\frac{1}{2}
-fold figure-eight elastica (up to reflection, rotation, translation, and reparametrization).

One can give an alternative proof of the previous proposition along the lines of
[
16
]
.
For convenience we give the argument in Appendix
B
.

2.3.
Free elastica

Our classification in Proposition
2.2
is only valid provided that
\lambda>0
. In the case of free elasticae, i.e.
\lambda=0
, we obtain a different classification of pinned elasticae. One readily checks that the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
in the formulae of
\gamma^{\lambda,1,n}_{\rm larc}
and
\gamma^{\lambda,1,n}_{\rm loop}
is not well-defined, see e.g. (
2.13
).
On the other hand, by letting
\lambda\to 0
in the explicit formula (
2.9
) of
\gamma_{\rm sarc}^{\lambda,1,1}

we obtain a planar curve defined by

(2.19)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}2\mathrm{E}(\am(\alpha_{0}s-\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}),\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}),\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})+2\mathrm{E}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})-\alpha_{0}s\\
\sqrt{2}\cn(\alpha_{0}s-\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}),\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\end{pmatrix},\qquad s\in[0,2\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})/\alpha_{0}],

where
\alpha_{0}:=4\mathrm{E}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})-2\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})
.
The length and the signed curvature are given by
L_{\rm rect}:=2\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})/\alpha_{0}
and

(2.20)

\displaystyle k_{\rm rect}(s):=-\sqrt{2}\alpha_{0}\cn\big(\alpha_{0}s-\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}),\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big),

respectively.
In addition, we observe from (
2.19
) that the tangential angle
\theta_{\rm rect}:[0,L_{\rm rect}]\to\mathbf{R}
of
\gamma_{\rm rect}
satisfies

(2.21)

\displaystyle\theta_{\rm rect}(0)=\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\theta_{\rm rect}(L_{\rm rect})=-\frac{\pi}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\theta_{\rm rect}\text{ is strictly decreasing},

and in particular
\gamma_{\rm rect}
has a vertical slope at the endpoints.

It is well-known that if
\gamma\in A_{\rm pin}
is a free elastica, then its signed curvature
k
is given by
k\equiv 0
or
k=\pm k_{\rm rect}
, see e.g.
[
10
]
.
Noting that

(2.22)

\alpha_{0}=4\mathrm{E}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})-2\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})=2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{1-z^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}}}\;\mathrm{d}z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\xi^{-\frac{1}{4}}}{\sqrt{1-\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mathsf{B}\big(\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{1}{2}\big),

where
\mathsf{B}
denotes the Beta function, we see that

(2.23)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm rect}(\tfrac{L_{\rm rect}}{2})=\left(\tfrac{1}{2},2\mathsf{B}(\tfrac{3}{4},\tfrac{1}{2})^{-1}\right).

This number coincides with the critical height threshold
h_{*}
defined by (

) for the existence of minimizers for the obstacle problem among graphs obtained in
[
1
,
17
,
14
,
18
,
19
]
.

3.
Fundamental properties for the obstacle problem

In this section, we collect some fundamental properties of (local) minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
and study their optimal regularity.
Hereafter, we always let
\psi
satisfy assumptions (A1)–(A3) unless specified otherwise.

3.1.
Description of local minimizers

First, as known in the previous results of Dall’Acqua–Deckelnick
[
1
]
*Proposition 3.2, we check that any local minimizer
\gamma
of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation
2k^{\prime\prime}+k^{3}-\lambda k=0
where
\gamma
does not touch the obstacle.

Lemma 3.1

(Euler–Lagrange equation above the obstacle)
.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
and
\gamma\in A
be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
.
Then, the signed curvature
k
of
\gamma
is analytic on
\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
and satisfies
2k^{\prime\prime}+k^{3}-\lambda k=0
in
\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
. In addition,
k
is also smooth in a neighborhood of
s=0
and
s=L
and satisfies
k(0)=k(L)=0
.

Sketch of proof.

Let
\gamma\in A
be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
and
k
denote its signed curvature.
Fix
s_{0}\in\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
arbitrarily.
Then, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
of
s_{0}
such that,
for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(U;\mathbf{R}^{2})
and for any
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
with sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we have
\gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\gamma+\varepsilon\varphi(s(\cdot))\in A
, where
s(x):=\int_{0}^{x}|\gamma^{\prime}|
.
This fact together with the known formulae of first derivatives of
B
and
L
(cf.
[
11
]
*Lemma A.1) yields

(3.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{\varepsilon}]\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{U}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},\varphi^{\prime\prime}\rangle-3|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{t},\varphi^{\prime}\rangle-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},\varphi\rangle\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s=0.

By a standard bootstrap argument we first deduce that
k
is of class
C^{\infty}
and by integrating by parts we also deduce that
k
satisfies

2k^{\prime\prime}+k^{3}-\lambda k=0

in
U
.
Since
k
is now characterized as a solution of the polynomial differential equation, we obtain analyticity of
k
.

We turn to the boundary condition
k(0)=k(L)=0
.
Combining (A2) with the fact that
\gamma(0)=(0,0)
, we find a sufficiently small
\delta>0
such that,
\gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\gamma+\varepsilon\varphi(s(\cdot))\in A
holds for any
\varphi\in W^{2,2}(0,\delta;\mathbf{R}^{2})
with
\mathrm{supp}\,{\varphi}\subset[0,\delta)
and
\varphi(0)=0
, and for any
\varepsilon\in(-\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{0})
with sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
Therefore, the identity (
3.1
) where
U
is replaced with
[0,\delta)
also holds for perturbations of this kind, and by choosing
\varphi
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=1
, and then integrating by parts, we obtain

(3.2)

\left[2k(s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\right]_{s=0}^{s=\delta}+\int_{0}^{\delta}\big(2k^{\prime\prime}+k^{3}-\lambda k\big)\varphi\;\mathrm{d}s=0,

which implies that
k(0)=0
.
Similarly we obtain
k(L)=0
.
∎

Thus, local minimizers are characterized by the Euler–Lagrange equation on the non-coincidence set.
To obtain a characterization that is also valid on the whole interval, we derive a so-called
variational inequality
for local minimizers.

Lemma 3.2

(Variational inequality)
.

Let
\lambda\geq 0
.
If
\gamma\in A
is a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
, then the curvature vector
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
satisfies

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime\prime}-3|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{t},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime}-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\ \ \varphi\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(0,L)\ \ \text{with}\ \ \varphi\geq 0,

where
L:=L[\gamma]
.

Proof.

If
\gamma
is a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
, then
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma]\leq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{\varepsilon}]
holds for any perturbation
\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\geq 0}\subset A
of
\gamma
provided that all the perturbation functions
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
are admissible. This can be achieved in particular by choosing perturbations in the positive vertical direction. Doing so, we find that
\gamma
satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma+\varepsilon\eta e_{2}]\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\geq 0\quad\text{for all}\ \ \eta\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(0,1)\ \ \text{with}\ \ \eta\geq 0.

By a straightforward calculation, we can prove that the above inequality is equivalent to
(
3.3
).
∎

Thus the variational inequality yields a necessary condition for a curve to be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
.
We say that
\gamma\in A
is a solution to the
variational inequality
if
\gamma\in A
satisfies (
3.3
).

Remark 3.3

(Comparison with graph curves)
.

Let us consider a curve represented by a graph of a function
\gamma_{u}(x):=(x,u(x))
for some
u\in W^{2,2}(0,1)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(0,1)
such that
u\geq\psi|_{[0,1]}
. One readily checks that
\gamma_{u}\in A
. Moreover,

\gamma_{u}+\varepsilon(v-u)e_{2}\in A
holds for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
v\in W^{2,2}(0,1)\cap W^{1,2}_{0}(0,1)
with
v\geq\psi|_{[0,1]}
.
Therefore, if
\gamma_{u}
is a local minimizer of
B
in
A
, then (
3.4
) holds with the choice
\eta=v-u
.
With this choice of
\eta
we see that (
3.4
) combined with
\lambda=0
is reduced to

(3.5)

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}B[\gamma_{u}+\varepsilon\eta e_{2}]\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(2\frac{u^{\prime\prime}}{(1+(u^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{5}{2}}}(v-u)^{\prime\prime}-5\frac{(u^{\prime\prime})^{2}u^{\prime}}{(1+(u^{\prime})^{2})^{\frac{7}{2}}}(v-u)^{\prime}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,

which is equivalent to the variational inequality in the graph case (see e.g.
[
1
]
*Equation 1.4).
Thus our variational inequality (
3.4
) can be regarded as an extension of the graph case
\gamma=\gamma_{u}
.

For local minimizers among graph curves with
\lambda=0
it is already known that, (i) the regularity above the obstacle is
C^{\infty}
(cf.
[
1
]
*Proposition 3.2), (ii) the global regularity (i.e. regularity of the solution to the variational inequality in
(0,1)
) can be improved up to
W^{3,\infty}(0,1)
, which is optimal in the sense of the Sobolev class (cf.
[
14
]
*Remark 3.7 or
[
19
]
*Theorem 1.2).
In our setting, the regularity above the obstacle is also
C^{\infty}
as seen in Lemma
3.1
.
On the other hand, improving the global regularity is more involved than in the graph case.
In fact, the leading term
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime\prime}=k\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime\prime}
in our variational inequality (
3.3
) may degenerate if
\boldsymbol{n}=\pm e_{1}
and such a degeneracy imposes an additional challenge to overcome in the regularity discussion.

3.2.
Optimal regularity in the case of Lipschitz obstacles

With a mild additional condition on the obstacle, the difficulty displayed in Remark
3.3
can be overcome and
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity (of the arclength parametrization of local minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
) can be deduced. To this end we first show a conditional regularity result for
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle
depending on the integrability of
|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|
.

Lemma 3.4

(Regularity via integrability)
.

Let
\gamma\in A
be such that the curvature vector
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
of the arclength parametrization
\tilde{\gamma}
satisfies the variational inequality (
3.3
).
Then one has for each
p\in[1,\infty]

(3.6)

|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\in L_{loc}^{p}(0,L)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,p}_{loc}(0,L).

Proof.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\in L^{p}_{loc}(0,L)
.
Since
\boldsymbol{\kappa}
satisfies (
3.3
),
by
[
6
, Theorem 1.39]
there exists a Radon measure
\mu
on
(0,L)
such that

(3.7)

\int_{0}^{L}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime\prime}-3|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{t},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime}-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{L}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}\mu\qquad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(0,L).

Now let
\delta\in(0,\frac{L}{2})
be arbitrary. For each
\varphi\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta)
we have by Fubini’s Theorem

(3.8)

\int_{0}^{L}\varphi(s)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\varphi(s)\;\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\int_{\delta}^{s}\varphi^{\prime}(x)\;\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\mu((s,L-\delta))\varphi^{\prime}(s)\;\mathrm{d}s.

Since
\mu
is locally finite we have that
f_{\delta}:(\delta,L-\delta)\rightarrow\mathbf{R};y\mapsto\mu((y,L-\delta))
is a bounded function. As it is decreasing, it is also Borel measurable and hence lies in
L^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta)
. We infer from this and (
3.7
)

(3.9)

\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime\prime}-3|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{t},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime}-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}f_{\delta}\varphi^{\prime}\;\mathrm{d}s\qquad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta).

Notice now that by assumption
g_{\delta}:=3|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\langle\boldsymbol{t},e_{2}\rangle+f_{\delta}\in L^{p}(\delta,L-\delta)
and

(3.10)

\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi^{\prime\prime}-g_{\delta}\varphi^{\prime}-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\varphi\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s=0\qquad\textrm{for all }\varphi\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta).

Choose now some fixed
\eta\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta)
such that
\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\eta=1.

Now let
\zeta\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta)
be arbitrary. Define
c:=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\zeta(y)\;\mathrm{d}y
and observe that also
\phi:=\int_{\delta}^{\cdot}\zeta-c\eta\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta)
. Then (
3.10
) yields

(3.11)

\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle(\zeta^{\prime}-c\eta^{\prime\prime})-g_{\delta}(\zeta-c\eta^{\prime})-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\phi\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s=0.

We rearrange to obtain

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\zeta^{\prime}\;\mathrm{d}s

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}g_{\delta}\zeta\;\mathrm{d}s+c\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\Big(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\eta^{\prime\prime}-g_{\delta}\eta^{\prime}-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\eta\Big)\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\left(\int_{\delta}^{\cdot}\zeta\right)\;\mathrm{d}s.

Since
|\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle|\leq|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|\leq\frac{1+|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}}{2}
we find that
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in L^{p}(\delta,L-\delta)
. In particular
A:=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}(2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\eta^{\prime\prime}-g_{\delta}\eta^{\prime}-\lambda\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\eta)\;\mathrm{d}s
is a finite constant. Using this and also Fubini’s Theorem in the last summand we find

(3.14)

\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\zeta^{\prime}\;\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}g_{\delta}\zeta\;\mathrm{d}s+Ac+\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\left(\lambda\int_{\cdot}^{L-\delta}\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\right)\zeta\;\mathrm{d}s.

Recalling the definition of
c
we have

(3.15)

\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\zeta^{\prime}\;\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\delta}^{L-\delta}\left(g_{\delta}+A+\lambda\int_{\cdot}^{L-\delta}\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\right)\zeta\;\mathrm{d}s.

Noticing that
\big(g_{\delta}+A+\lambda\int_{\cdot}^{L-\delta}\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\big)\in L^{p}(\delta,L-\delta)
and that
\zeta\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\delta,L-\delta)
was arbitrary we obtain that
2\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,p}(\delta,L-\delta)
. The claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Notice that
|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\in L^{1}(0,L)
is automatically satisfied for any
\gamma\in A
as

(3.16)

\int_{0}^{L}|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s=B[\gamma]<\infty.

Therefore Lemma
3.4
implies that if
\tilde{\gamma}
solves the variational inequality, then
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,L)
. If
\gamma
is additionally a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
, then we can actually deduce that
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,1}(0,L)
. Indeed, recall that
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle=k\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle
and in a neighborhood of
0
and
L
one has that
k
is analytic and
\boldsymbol{n}\in W^{1,2}(0,L)
, cf. Lemma
3.1
. Hence we may from now on use that
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,1}(0,L)
.

Next we intend to deduce Sobolev regularity for the signed curvature
k
. To this end we make use of the equation
k\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle
. If
\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle\neq 0
one can solve for
k
and obtain
k=\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle}
. Using this the Sobolev regularity of
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle
inherits easily to
k
. Problems occur at points where
\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle=0
. If such points are on the noncoincidence set
\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
, we do not have to bother because in this case regularity of
k
is already obtained in Lemma
3.1
. So the only remaining problematic case is when
\langle\boldsymbol{n}(s_{0}),e_{2}\rangle=0
for a point of coincidence
s_{0}
.

The next lemma rules out the existence of such points for Lipschitz continuous obstacles.

Lemma 3.6

(Nonverticality on the coincidence set)
.

Suppose that
\psi
is Lipschitz continuous, i.e. there exists some
K>0
such that
|\psi(x)-\psi(y)|\leq K|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\mathbf{R}
. Moreover fix
\gamma\in A
with arclength parametrization
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,L]\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{2}
. Then

(3.17)

\langle\boldsymbol{n}(s_{0}),e_{2}\rangle\neq 0\quad\textrm{for all}\ \ s_{0}\in(0,L)\setminus\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}.

Proof.

For a contradiction we assume the opposite, i.e., there exists
s_{0}\in(0,L)\setminus\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
such that

(3.18)

\langle\boldsymbol{n}(s_{0}),e_{2}\rangle=0.

We infer that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})\neq 0
and
|(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})|=1
. Using that
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s)\geq\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s))
for each
s\in(0,L)
and
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s_{0})=\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s_{0}))
we find

(3.19)

\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s)-\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s_{0})\geq\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s))-\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s_{0}))\qquad\forall s\in(0,L).

We can now estimate the right-hand side by
-K|\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s)-\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s_{0})|
and divide by
|s-s_{0}|
to obtain

(3.20)

\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s)-\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s_{0})}{|s-s_{0}|}\geq-K\left|\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s)-\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s_{0})}{s-s_{0}}\right|\qquad\forall s\in(0,L)\setminus\{s_{0}\}.

Letting
s\rightarrow s_{0}+
and using that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})=0
we deduce that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})\geq 0
. Furthermore, letting
s\rightarrow s_{0}-
and using that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})=0
we obtain
-(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})\geq 0
. These observations imply
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})=0
,
a contradiction. The claim follows.
∎

The previous results finally yield (optimal)
W^{1,\infty}
-regularity for
k
.

Lemma 3.7

(Curvature regularity of local minimizers in
A
)
.

Suppose that
\psi
is Lipschitz continuous. Further let
\gamma\in A
be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
. Then
k\in W^{1,\infty}(0,L)
.

Proof.

Use again the arclength parametrization
\tilde{\gamma}
. Note from Remark
3.5
that
\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle
lies in
W^{1,1}(0,L).

Step 1.
We show
k\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,L)
.
Now fix some point
s_{0}\in(0,L)
. If
s_{0}\in\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
then we infer from Lemma
3.1
that
k
is analytic (and hence also
W^{1,1}
) in a neighborhood of
s_{0}
. Now suppose that
s_{0}\in(0,L)\setminus\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
. By Lemma
3.6
we have
\langle\boldsymbol{n}(s_{0}),e_{2}\rangle\neq 0
.
We deduce from this (since
\boldsymbol{n}\in W^{1,2}(0,L)\subset C([0,L])
) that in an open neighborhood
U
of
s_{0}
we also have that
\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle\neq 0
. Remark
3.5
now yields that

k\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,1}(U).

Hence we find

(3.21)

k=\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle}\in W^{1,1}_{loc}(U).

We obtain that for each point
s_{0}\in(0,L)
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
s_{0}
such that
k\in W^{1,1}(U)
. Step 1 is complete.

Step 2.

k\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(U)
.
Due to the one-dimensionality of
(0,L)
we have
W^{1,1}_{loc}(0,L)\subset C(0,L)\subset L^{\infty}_{loc}(0,L)
and hence
k\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(0,L)
. Now note that also

(3.22)

|\boldsymbol{\kappa}|^{2}=k^{2}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(0,L).

Thereupon, Lemma
3.4
yields that

(3.23)

\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(0,L).

We claim now that
k\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(0,L)
. Indeed, let
s_{0}\in(0,L).
If
s_{0}\in\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
, then clearly there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
s_{0}
such that
k
is analytic on
U
and hence also
k\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(U)
. If
s_{0}\in(0,L)\setminus\widetilde{N}_{\gamma}
, then we infer again from Lemma
3.6
that there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
s_{0}
such
\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle\neq 0
on
U
. Notice further that
\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(U)
as
\partial_{s}\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{n}^{\prime},e_{2}\rangle=-\langle k\boldsymbol{t},e_{2}\rangle\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(U)
due to the previously obtained
L^{\infty}_{loc}
-regularity of
k
and the fact that
|\boldsymbol{t}|=|e_{2}|=1
. Using (
3.23
) we find also that

(3.24)

k\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(U)

and thus
k=\frac{\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},e_{2}\rangle}{\langle\boldsymbol{n},e_{2}\rangle}\in W^{1,\infty}_{loc}(U)
. Using now the fact that
k
is analytic in a neighborhood of
0
and
L
(cf. Lemma
3.1
), we find that
k\in W^{1,\infty}(0,L)
.
∎

As a corollary we deduce
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity for the arclength parametrization
\tilde{\gamma}
of local minimizers.

Corollary 3.8
.

Suppose that
\psi
is Lipschitz continuous.
Let
\gamma\in A
be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
. Then
\tilde{\gamma}\in W^{3,\infty}(0,L)
.

Proof.

The previous lemma shows that
k\in W^{1,\infty}(0,L)
. Notice that on
(0,L)
we have

(3.25)

\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}=k\boldsymbol{n}.

Now
k\in W^{1,\infty}(0,L)
by the previous lemma. Furthermore
\boldsymbol{n}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,L)
as
\boldsymbol{n}^{\prime}=-k\boldsymbol{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,L)
. Therefore (
3.25
) yields that
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}\in W^{1,\infty}(0,L)
. The claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.9

(Optimality)
.

Notice that the
W^{3,\infty}
-regularity obtained in the previous corollary is optimal. Indeed, the minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
in
A_{\rm graph}
found in
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]

are local minimizers for the minimization problem in
A
.
As discussed in Remark
3.3
, their regularity can not be improved further in the Sobolev classes, yielding the claimed optimality. We remark that
[
14
, Theorem 3.1]
actually looks only at minimizers in
A_{\rm graph}\cap A_{\rm sym}
, but the symmetry assumption is not required for small obstacles, cf.
[
18
, Theorem 2.9]
.

3.3.
Existence of global minimizers

If
\lambda>0
, then existence of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
follows from the
standard direct method.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\lambda>0
.
Then, there exists a solution to the following minimization problem

(3.26)

\min_{\gamma\in A}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma].

Proof.

Let
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\subset A
be a minimizing sequence of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
, i.e.,

(3.27)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{j}]=\inf_{\gamma\in A}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma].

In particular, this ensures that there exists
C>0
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{j}]\leq C
for all
j\in\mathbf{N}
.
After reparametrization, we may suppose that
\gamma_{j}\in A
is of constant speed so that
|\gamma_{j}^{\prime}|\equiv L[\gamma_{j}]
.
The assumption of constant speed
implies that

(3.28)

\|\gamma_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=\int_{0}^{L[\gamma_{j}]}\big(L[\gamma_{j}]^{2}|\tilde{\gamma}_{j}^{\prime\prime}(s)|^{2}\big)L[\gamma_{j}]^{-1}\;\mathrm{d}s=L[\gamma_{j}]^{3}B[\gamma_{j}],

where
\tilde{\gamma}_{j}
denotes the arclength parametrization of
\gamma_{j}
.
This together with
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{j}]\leq C
yields a uniform estimate of
\|\gamma_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}
.
Using
|\gamma_{j}^{\prime}|\equiv L[\gamma_{j}]\leq C
and the boundary condition
\gamma_{j}(0)=(0,0)
, we also obtain the bounds on the
W^{1,2}
norm.
Therefore,
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{2,2}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})
so that there are
\bar{\gamma}\in W^{2,2}(0,1;\mathbf{R}^{2})
and a subsequence (without relabeling) such that
\gamma_{j}
converges to
\bar{\gamma}
in the senses of
W^{2,2}
-weak and
C^{1}
topology.
Thus the limit curve
\bar{\gamma}
also satisfies
\bar{\gamma}(0)=(0,0)
,
\bar{\gamma}(1)=(1,0)
, and
\bar{\gamma}_{2}(t)\geq\psi(\bar{\gamma}_{1}(t))
for any
t\in[0,1]
.
In addition, by the boundary condition
L[\gamma_{j}]\geq 1
holds for all
j\in\mathbf{N}
,
so that

(3.29)

|\bar{\gamma}^{\prime}|=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}|\gamma_{j}^{\prime}|=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}L[\gamma_{j}]=L[\bar{\gamma}]\in(0,\infty),

i.e.,
\bar{\gamma}
is immersed.
Therefore,
\bar{\gamma}\in A
. It remains to show that
\bar{\gamma}
minimizes the energy.
By the weak lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
norm it follows that

(3.30)

B[\bar{\gamma}]=L[\bar{\gamma}]^{-3}\|\bar{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}L[\gamma_{j}]^{-3}\|\gamma_{j}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}=\liminf_{j\to\infty}B[\gamma_{j}].

Combining this with (
3.27
), we see that

(3.31)

\mathcal{E_{\lambda}[\bar{\gamma}]}=B[\bar{\gamma}]+\lambda L[\bar{\gamma}]\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}B[\gamma_{j}]+\lambda\lim_{j\to\infty}L[\gamma_{j}]\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{j}]=\inf_{\gamma\in A}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma].

The proof is complete.
∎

On the other hand, if
\lambda=0
, then there is no global minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{0}=B
in
A
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\psi
be an obstacle
satisfying assumptions (A1)–(A3).
Then there exists no global minimizer of
B
in
A
nor in
A_{\rm sym}
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a minimizer
\gamma
of
B
in
A
.
By assumption (A3)
\gamma
is not a line segment, accordingly
B[\gamma]>0
.
On the other hand, let
C_{n}
be a circular arc with radius
1/n
in the upper half-plane passing through points
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
.
Then clearly one has
C_{n}^{2}>\psi\circ C_{n}^{1}
and
B[C_{n}]\leq\frac{2\pi}{n}<B[\gamma]
for sufficiently large values of
n\in\mathbf{N}
.
This contradicts the minimality of
\gamma
in
A
.
In addition, since
C_{n}
also belongs to
A_{\rm sym}
, one can also check that there is no global minimizer in
A_{\rm sym}
.
∎

4.
Analysis of the case of
\lambda=0

Let us begin this section by introducing the following
competitors whose (local) minimality properties we will investigate in this section:

Definition 4.1

(Symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica)
.

We say that a planar curve
\gamma\in A
is a
symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica
(for short SCF) if
\gamma
satisfies the following conditions:

(SCF)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{(i) }\gamma\text{ is reflectionally symmetric};\\
\text{(ii) }2k^{\prime\prime}+k^{3}=0\ \text{ on }\ (0,\frac{L}{2});\\
\text{(iii) }k(0)=0;\\
\text{(iv) }k<0\ \text{ on }\ (0,\frac{L}{2});\\
\text{(v) }\tilde{\gamma}(\frac{L}{2})=(\frac{1}{2},\psi(\frac{1}{2})),\end{cases}

where
L=L[\gamma]
.

Recall e.g. from
[
10
]
that for
s\in(0,\frac{L}{2})
we have

\tilde{\gamma}(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}\gamma_{\rm rect}(\alpha s)
for some
\alpha>0
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\psi
be a symmetric cone obstacle and
\gamma\in A
be an SCF. We claim that then
\widetilde{I}_{\gamma}=\{\frac{L}{2}\}
. Indeed, assume that there exists some
s_{0}\in\widetilde{I}_{\gamma}\setminus\{\frac{L}{2}\}
. Due to reflection symmetry we may assume
s_{0}<\tfrac{L}{2}
. We have
\gamma(s_{0})=(a,\psi(a))
for some
a\in\mathbf{R}
.
As mentioned earlier, there exist
\alpha>0
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}\gamma_{\rm rect}(\alpha s),s\in(0,\frac{L}{2})
. Notice that (
SCF
)-(iv) implies that
\tfrac{L}{2}\leq 2\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})/(\alpha\alpha_{0})
, as
k_{\rm rect}
changes sign at
2\mathrm{K}(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})/(\alpha\alpha_{0})
.
Moreover,
a\neq\frac{1}{2}
since
\gamma|_{[0,\frac{L}{2}]}
must be embedded (due to the fact that
\gamma_{\rm rect}
is embedded, as one readily checks observing from (
2.19
) that
(\gamma_{\rm rect}^{1})^{\prime}>0
on
(0,\frac{L}{2})
).
Also
a>\frac{1}{2}
is ruled out by the explicit shape of
\gamma_{\rm rect}
.
Thus we may assume that
a<\frac{1}{2}
, and this implies that
\psi^{\prime}(a)>0
.
Notice that
\psi
and
\tilde{\gamma}
are twice differentiable at
a
and
\psi^{\prime}
is constant in a neighborhood of
a
. This and the fact that
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}-\psi\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{1}
attains a local minimum at
s_{0}
imply

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(s_{0})-\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(s_{0}))=0,\quad(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})-\psi^{\prime}(a)(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})=0,\quad(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})-\psi^{\prime}(a)(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})\geq 0.

Using this we have

(4.2)

k(s_{0})=\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa},\boldsymbol{n}\rangle(s_{0})=(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})-(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})=(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})[(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})-\psi^{\prime}(a)(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})].

In particular, since
k(s_{0})<0
by (
SCF
)-(iv) and
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})-\psi^{\prime}(a)(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime\prime}(s_{0})\geq 0
by (
4.1
), it follows that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})<0
.
This and (
4.1
) also imply that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(s_{0})=\psi^{\prime}(a)(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(s_{0})<0
.
Therefore,
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s_{0})
lies in the third quadrant.
In addition, since
\gamma_{\rm rect}^{\prime}(s)
lies in the first and fourth quadrant for any
s\in[0,2\mathrm{K}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})/\alpha_{0}]
, we see that
\phi>\frac{\pi}{2}
. Moreover,
(\tfrac{1}{2},\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}))=\tfrac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}(\gamma_{\rm rect}(\tfrac{\alpha L}{2}))
and the fact that
\gamma_{\rm rect}(\tfrac{\alpha L}{2})
lies in the first quadrant yields that
\phi\in(0,\tfrac{\pi}{2})\cup(\frac{3\pi}{2},2\pi)
. Since
(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
is already ruled out we only have to consider the case
(\frac{3\pi}{2},2\pi)
. Curves that satisfy (
SCF
) with the restriction of
\phi\in(\frac{3\pi}{2},2\pi)
have been studied in
[
14
, Equation 3.13]
. One obtains a unique graphical shape that touches the obstacle only once at the tip, as discussed in
[
14
, Equation 3.10]
. A contradiction, and hence we find that
\widetilde{I}_{\gamma}=\{\frac{L}{2}\}
if
\gamma
is an SCF.

It is already shown that if
\psi
is a symmetric cone and satisfies
\psi(\frac{1}{2})<h_{*}
, where
h_{*}
is the constant given by (
1.6
),
then there exists a unique minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{0}=B
in
A_{\rm graph}\cap A_{\rm sym}
(see e.g.
[
14
]
), and its unique minimizer is characterized as a symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica.
On the other hand, if
\psi(\frac{1}{2})\geq h_{*}
, then there exists neither a symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica nor a global minimizer of
B
in
A_{\rm graph}
.

In the following we show that there exists a unique cut-and-glued free-elastica in the class
A
, not
A_{\rm graph}
.
To this end, let us introduce the polar tangential angle of
\gamma_{\rm rect}
, defined by (
2.19
).
Here, for an arclength parametrized planar curve
\gamma:[0,L]\to\mathbf{R}^{2}
with
\gamma(s)\neq 0
for all
s\in(0,L)
, the
polar tangential angle
function
\omega:(0,L)\to\mathbf{R}
is defined as a smooth function such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle R_{\omega(s)}\Big(\frac{\gamma(s)}{|\gamma(s)|}\Big)=\boldsymbol{t}(s)\ \ \text{for}\ s\in(0,L),

where
L=L[\gamma]
.
Such a polar tangential angle function exists, is unique up to shifts in
2\pi\mathbf{Z}
and is scale invariant in the following sense: For
\alpha>0
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
, let
\omega_{\alpha,\phi}
be the polar tangential angle of
s\mapsto\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}\gamma(\alpha s)
.
Then, for each
s\in[0,L]
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha,\phi}(s)=\omega(\alpha s)\quad(\text{mod }2\pi\mathbf{Z}).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\omega:(0,L)\to\mathbf{R}
be the polar tangential angle of
\gamma_{\rm rect}
, where
L=L[\gamma_{\rm rect}]
.
Then
\omega
is strictly decreasing in
(0,L)
.
In addition,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\tan\big(-\omega(s)\big)=0,\quad\tan\big(-\omega(\tfrac{L}{2})\big)=2h_{*},\quad\lim_{s\uparrow L}\tan\big(-\omega(s)\big)=\infty.

Proof.

First we show monotonicity of
\omega
.
Since monotonicity of
\omega|_{(0,\frac{L}{2}]}
is already shown in
[
14
]
, it suffices to prove monotonicity of
\omega|_{[\frac{L}{2},L)}
.
Recall from
[
14
]
*Corollary 2.5 that the sign of
k_{\rm rect}(s)\omega_{\rm rect}^{\prime}(s)
coincides with that of
\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\epsilon(s)\rangle
, where
\epsilon=\gamma+k^{-1}\boldsymbol{n}
denotes the locus of the centers of osculating circles as in
[
14
]
*Section 2.1. Since
k_{\rm rect}<0
on
[\frac{L}{2},L)
(cf. (
2.20
)), to prove that
\omega_{\rm rect}
is strictly decreasing, it suffices to show that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\epsilon(s)\rangle=|\gamma_{\rm rect}(s)|^{2}+\frac{\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\boldsymbol{n}(s)\rangle}{k_{\rm rect}(s)}>0\quad\text{for all}\quad s\in(\tfrac{L}{2},L).

Set

(4.7)

\xi(s):=\frac{1}{2}|\gamma_{\rm rect}(s)|^{2}+\frac{\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\boldsymbol{n}(s)\rangle}{k_{\rm rect}(s)}\quad\text{for}\quad s\in(\tfrac{L}{2},L).

Then we have
\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\epsilon(s)\rangle>\xi(s)
for any
s\in(\tfrac{L}{2},L)
, and by using the Frenet–Serret formula
\boldsymbol{n}^{\prime}=-k\boldsymbol{t}
and the fact
\langle\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{n}\rangle=0

we can compute

(4.8)

\xi^{\prime}(s)=-\frac{\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\boldsymbol{n}(s)\rangle k_{\rm rect}^{\prime}(s)}{k_{\rm rect}(s)^{2}}.

By formula (
2.19
) we have
\gamma_{\rm rect}^{i}(s)>0
and
\boldsymbol{n}^{i}(s)>0
for any
s\in(\frac{L}{2},L)
and
i=1,2
,
which leads to

\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\boldsymbol{n}(s)\rangle>0

for any
s\in(\frac{L}{2},L)
.
In addition, it follows from (
2.20
) that
k^{\prime}_{\rm rect}>0
in
(\frac{L}{2},L]
.
Thus
\xi
is strictly decreasing in
(\frac{L}{2},L)
, and hence, for any
s\in(\frac{L}{2},L)
,

(4.9)

\xi(s)\geq\lim_{s\uparrow L}\xi(s)=\frac{1}{2}+\lim_{s\uparrow L}\frac{\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),-k_{\rm rect}(s)\boldsymbol{t}(s)\rangle}{k_{\rm rect}^{\prime}(s)}=\frac{1}{2},

where we have used l’Hospital’s rule in the penultimate step.
Thus we have
\langle\gamma_{\rm rect}(s),\epsilon(s)\rangle>\frac{1}{2}
for any
s\in(\frac{L}{2},L)
, and hence the desired monotonicity of
\omega
follows.

Next we show (
4.5
).
One can deduce from (
2.19
) that
\gamma_{\rm rect}(s)=:(X(s),Y(s))
satisfies
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{X(s)}{Y(s)}=\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{X^{\prime}(s)}{Y^{\prime}(s)}=0
, which in combination with the fact that
Y>0
on
(\frac{L}{2},L)
gives

(4.10)

\frac{\gamma_{\rm rect}(s)}{|\gamma_{\rm rect}(s)|}=\Big(\frac{X(s)}{\sqrt{X(s)^{2}+Y(s)^{2}}},\frac{Y(s)}{\sqrt{X(s)^{2}+Y(s)^{2}}}\Big)\to 1\quad\text{as}\ s\to 0.

On the other hand,
\boldsymbol{t}(0)=e_{2}
also follows from (
2.19
), and hence we obtain
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\cos(-\omega(s))=1
, so that
\lim_{s\downarrow 0}\tan(-\omega(s))=0
.
The second equality of (
4.5
) immediately follows from (
2.23
) combined with the fact that
\boldsymbol{t}(\frac{L}{2})=e_{1}
.
Finally, by (
2.23
) we have
\gamma_{\rm rect}(L)=(1,0)
and
\boldsymbol{t}(L)=(0,-1)
, which leads to
\lim_{s\uparrow L}\tan(-\omega(s))=\infty
.
∎

We apply Lemma
4.3
to show existence and uniqueness of symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae.

Lemma 4.4

(Unique existence of symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae)
.

Let
\psi
be a symmetric cone obstacle satisfying (A1)–(A3).
Then there exists a unique symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica
\gamma\in A
.
In addition,
\gamma
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

if
\psi(\frac{1}{2})<h_{*}
, then
\gamma\in A_{\rm graph}
and
\lim_{s\uparrow\frac{L}{2}}k^{\prime}(s)<0
;

(ii)

if
\psi(\frac{1}{2})=h_{*}
, then
\gamma=\gamma_{\rm rect}
(up to reparametrization). In particular,
k^{\prime}(\tfrac{L}{2})=0
and
\gamma\not\in A_{\rm graph}
;

(iii)

if
\psi(\frac{1}{2})>h_{*}
, then
\gamma\not\in A_{\rm graph}
and the signed curvature
k
satisfies
\lim_{s\uparrow\frac{L}{2}}k^{\prime}(s)>0
.

Here
k
denotes the signed curvature of
\gamma
.

Proof.

Step 1
(
We prove existence of symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae
)
.

By Lemma
4.3
, there exists
s_{*}\in(0,L[\gamma_{\rm rect}])
such that
\tan{(-\omega(s_{*}))}=2\psi(\frac{1}{2})
.
We also choose
\phi\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
such that
R_{\phi}\gamma_{\rm rect}^{\prime}(s_{*})=e_{1}
.
In addition, let
\alpha>0
be a scaling factor to satisfy

(4.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}\gamma_{\rm rect}(\alpha s_{*})=(\tfrac{1}{2},\psi(\tfrac{1}{2})).

Finally define
\gamma_{*}:[0,s_{*}]\to\mathbf{R}^{2}
by
\gamma_{*}(s):=\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}\gamma_{\rm rect}(\alpha s)
, and
consider the reflectionally symmetric constant-speed parametrized curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbf{R}^{2}
uniquely given by
\gamma|_{[0,1/2]}=\gamma_{*}
(up to reparametrization).
Then, property (
SCF
)-(i) in Definition
4.1
immediately follows and properties (
SCF
)-(ii)–(iv) are inherited from

\gamma_{\rm rect}
.
In addition, (
4.11
) means that
\gamma
satisfies property (
SCF
)-(v) of Definition
4.1
, and hence
\gamma
is a symmetric cut-and-glued free-elasticae.

Step 2
(
We prove uniqueness
)
.

If
\gamma
is a symmetric cut-and-glued free-elastica, then combining (
SCF
)-(ii) with
[
9
, Proposition 3.3]
, we see that the signed curvature
k
of
\gamma
is given by

(4.12)

\displaystyle k(s)=-\sqrt{2}\alpha\cn(\alpha s+\beta,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\quad s\in[0,\tfrac{L}{2}]

for some
\alpha\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbf{R}
.
In addition, by (
SCF
)-(iii) and (iv), we may choose
\beta=\mathrm{K}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})
.
Note that
\alpha=0
is ruled out by symmetry of
\gamma
and (
SCF
)-(iv).
Thus we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle k(s)=\alpha k_{\rm rect}(\alpha s),\quad s\in[0,\tfrac{L}{2}],

which implies that, for some
\phi\in[0,2\pi)
,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha}R_{\phi}\gamma_{\rm rect}(\alpha s),\quad s\in[0,\tfrac{L}{2}].

Let
\omega:(0,L)\to\mathbf{R}
and
\omega_{\rm rect}:(0,L_{\rm rect})\to\mathbf{R}

), defined by

(5.12)

c_{\psi}:=\min\big\{\theta_{\psi}-\arctan{(2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}))},\ \arctan{(2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}))}\big\},\quad\text{where}\ \ \theta_{\psi}:=\arctan\psi^{\prime}(0).

Proof.

Since
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\lozenge}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
, then the reflection of
\gamma
is also a minimizer in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
. Thus, up to reflection,
we may assume
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(l)\geq 0
(i.e., if
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(l)<0
, then we may consider the reflection of
\gamma
instead of the original curve
\gamma
).
Hereafter let
a_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
denote by the unique point such that
\psi^{\prime}(a_{0})>0
and
\psi(a_{0})=0
.
Throughout the proof let
a\in[a_{0},\frac{1}{2}]
be such that
\tilde{\gamma}(l)=(a,\psi(a))
.
Then, by the Cauchy’s mean value theorem, there exists
l^{*}\in(0,l)
such that

(5.13)

\frac{(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(l^{*})}{(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(l^{*})}=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(l)-\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(0)}{\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(l)-\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(0)}=\frac{\psi(a)}{a},

from which it follows that

(5.14)

\theta(l^{*})=\arctan{\big(\tfrac{\psi(a)}{a}\big)}\quad\textrm{or}\quad\theta(l^{*})=\pi+\arctan{\big(\tfrac{\psi(a)}{a}\big)}\quad(\text{mod }2\pi).

Case I.
First we address the case
a=\frac{1}{2}
.
By
\lambda\in(0,\hat{\lambda})
and the embeddedness of
\gamma
(cf. Lemma
5.7
) we see that
l=\frac{L[\gamma]}{2}
, and then by the symmetry it also follows that
\theta(l)=2m\pi
for some
m\in\mathbf{Z}
.
This together with (
5.14
) yields

(5.15)

\theta(l)-\theta(l^{*})\in[\pi-\arctan(2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2})),2\pi-\arctan(2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}))]\quad(\text{mod }2\pi).

Using that
\pi-\arctan(2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}))\geq\frac{\pi}{2}>c_{\psi}
and
\arctan(2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2}))\geq c_{\psi}
we obtain (
5.11
) in this case.

Case II.
Next we consider the case
a\in(a_{0},\frac{1}{2})
.
Note that in this case
\psi\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{1}
is differentiable at
s=l
.
Since
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(l)=\psi(a)>0
, not only
|\tilde{\gamma}^{2}|-\psi\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{1}
but also
\tilde{\gamma}^{2}-\psi\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{1}
attains a local minimum with value
0
at
s=l
.
Therefore we have
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(l)-\psi^{\prime}(a)(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(l)=0
, which implies that

\theta(l)=\theta_{\psi}
or
\theta(l)=\pi+\theta_{\psi}
(mod
2\pi
).
Combining this with (
5.14
), we see that

(5.16)

\theta(l)-\theta(l^{*})=\theta_{\psi}-\arctan{(\tfrac{\psi(a)}{a})}\quad\text{or}\quad\theta(l)-\theta(l^{*})=\theta_{\psi}-\arctan{(\tfrac{\psi(a)}{a})}+\pi\quad(\text{mod }2\pi).

Since
[a_{0},1/2]\ni a\mapsto\arctan{(\tfrac{\psi(a)}{a})}
is strictly increasing, we see that

\theta(l)-\theta(l^{*})\in[\theta_{\psi}-\arctan{2\psi(\tfrac{1}{2})},3\pi/2]
(mod
2\pi
).
This clearly implies that (
5.11
) holds.

Case III.
Now we turn to the remaining case
a=a_{0}
.
After another reflection we may assume that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(l)\geq 0
, and particularly
\theta(l)\in[0,\pi]
(mod
2\pi
).
We show that in this case

(5.17)

\theta(l)\in[\theta_{\psi},\pi-\theta_{\psi}]\quad(\text{mod }2\pi).

We argue by contradiction, so suppose that
\theta(l)\in[0,\theta_{\psi})
(mod
2\pi
) or that
\theta(l)\in(\pi-\theta_{\psi},\pi]
(mod
2\pi
).
In the former case, assuming additionally
\theta(l)\neq 0
, the assumption implies that

(5.18)

\frac{(\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{\prime}(l)}{(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(l)}=\tan\theta(l)<\psi^{\prime}(0)=\psi^{\prime}(a_{0})=\psi^{\prime}((\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(l)).

This inequality and the observation that
(\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(l)>0
(due to the fact that
\theta(l)\in(0,\theta_{\psi})\subset(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
) imply
(\tilde{\gamma}^{2}-\psi\circ\tilde{\gamma}^{1})^{\prime}(l)<0
. The last inequality clearly holds also for the case
\theta(l)=0
.
Hence

(5.19)

\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(l+\delta)<\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(l+\delta))\quad\text{for sufficiently small }\delta>0.

A contradiction.
If
\theta(l)\in(\pi-\theta_{\psi},\pi]
(mod
2\pi
), then similar to the previous argument we can deduce that

(5.20)

-\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(l-\delta)<\psi(\tilde{\gamma}^{1}(l-\delta))\quad\text{for sufficiently small }\delta>0,

which is a contradiction to the fact that

\gamma\in A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
.
Thus we have shown (
5.17
).
In view of the fact that
a=a_{0}
, we deduce from (
5.14
) that
\theta(l^{*})=0
or
\theta(l^{*})=\pi
(mod
2\pi
).
Then this together with (
5.17
) yields
\theta(l)-\theta(l^{*})\in[\theta_{\psi},\pi-\theta_{\psi}]
(mod
2\pi
). The proof is now complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove noncoincidence of minimizers of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
for small
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 5.9
.

There exists some
\bar{\lambda}>0
such that for all
\lambda\leq\bar{\lambda}
each minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
does not touch the obstacle, i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\lozenge}=\emptyset
for all
\lambda\leq\bar{\lambda}
. Furthermore, all minimizers are given by
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
and the reflection of
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
at the
x
-axis.

Proof.

For a contradiction we assume the opposite, i.e. there exist
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
with
\lambda_{n}\to 0
(
n\to\infty
) such that
\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lozenge}\neq\emptyset
. Choose
\gamma_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\lambda_{n}}^{\lozenge}
for all
n\in\mathbf{N}
. From Remark
5.3
we infer that

(5.21)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]=\inf_{A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}\longrightarrow 0\qquad(n\rightarrow\infty).

Let
x_{n}
be the first touching point of
\gamma_{n}
in terms of the original parameter, i.e.,
x_{n}:=\min\{x\in[0,1]\,|\,\gamma_{n}(x)=(a,\psi(a))\ \text{for some}\ a\in(0,1)\}
,
and define
l_{n}:=\int_{0}^{x_{n}}|\gamma_{n}^{\prime}|
.
Then, since the signed curvature
k_{n}
of
\gamma_{n}
satisfies the Euler–Lagrange equation of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}
on
(0,l_{n})
and also satisfies
k_{n}(0)=0
(cf. Remark
5.4
),
\gamma_{n}|_{[0,x_{n}]}
can be characterized as a line segment or a wavelike elastica.
The former is impossible in view of the
C^{1}
-regularity of
\gamma_{n}
and the embeddedness (cf. Lemma
5.7
), and hence we find that

k_{n}|_{[0,l_{n}]}

is (up to reflection) given by

(5.22)

k_{n}(s)=2\alpha_{n}q_{n}\cn(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n}),\quad s\in[0,l_{n}],

for some
\alpha_{n}>0
and
q_{n}\in[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},1]
with
\lambda_{n}=2\alpha_{n}^{2}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)
.
Note that by the change of variables
\xi=\am(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})

(5.23)

\int_{\gamma_{n}\mid_{[0,l_{n}]}}k_{n}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s=4\alpha_{n}^{2}q_{n}^{2}\int_{0}^{l_{n}}\cn(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s=\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
\xi_{n}:=\am(\alpha_{n}l_{n}-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})
.
This yields the lower bound for
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]
as follows:

(5.24)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]\geq B[\gamma_{n}|_{[0,l_{n}]}]=\int_{\gamma_{n}\mid_{[0,l_{n}]}}k_{n}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s=\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi.

We now split the rest of the proof into several steps.

Step 1
(
Asymptotic behavior of
\xi_{n}
)
.

Formula (
5.22
) implies that the arclength parametrization
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}|_{[0,l_{n}]}
is, up to translation, rotation, and reflection, given by

(5.25)

\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(s)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\begin{pmatrix}2\mathrm{E}(\am(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n}),q_{n}))-\alpha_{n}s\\
2q_{n}\cn(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})\end{pmatrix},\quad s\in[0,l_{n}].

By reparametrization
\xi=\am(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})
we have
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(s)=\hat{\gamma}_{n}(\xi(s))
using

(5.26)

\hat{\gamma}_{n}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\begin{pmatrix}2\mathrm{E}(\xi,q_{n})-\mathrm{F}(\xi,q_{n})\\
2q_{n}\cos\xi\end{pmatrix},\quad\xi\in[-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\xi_{n}].

Let
a_{n}
be a point on
\psi
such that
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(l_{n})=(a_{n},\psi(a_{n}))
.
By definition of

A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}

we have
a_{0}\leq a_{n}\leq 1-a_{0}
.
In view of (
5.26
) we see that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+\psi(a_{n})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big|\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(l_{n})-\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(0)\big|=\big|\hat{\gamma}_{n}(\xi_{n})-\hat{\gamma}_{n}(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})\big|\geq\frac{2q_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}|\cos{\xi_{n}}|.

Notice that in this calculation we have ignored the aforementioned translations, rotations and reflections as they are isometries of
\mathbf{R}^{2}
.
Combining (
5.27
) with (
5.24
), we obtain

(5.28)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]\geq\frac{2q_{n}|\cos{\xi_{n}}|}{\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+\psi(a_{n})^{2}}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi\geq\frac{2\sqrt{2}|\cos{\xi_{n}}|}{\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+\psi(a_{n})^{2}}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\cos^{2}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\xi,

where we also used
q_{n}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
in the last equality.
In view of the fact that
|a_{n}|\leq 1

we may define
C_{\psi}:=\max_{x\in[0,1]}\sqrt{x^{2}+\psi(x)^{2}}<\infty
and estimate

(5.29)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]\geq\frac{2\sqrt{2}|\cos{\xi_{n}}|}{C_{\psi}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\cos^{2}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\xi.

This together with (
5.21
) implies that, up to a subsequence (but still denoted by
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
),

(5.30)

\displaystyle\xi_{n}\to\infty\quad\text{or}\quad\xi_{n}\to\frac{\pi}{2}+(m-1)\pi\ \ \text{for some}\ \ m\in\mathbf{N}\cup\{0\}.

Next we show that
\xi_{n}
does not converge to
-\frac{\pi}{2}
.
To this end suppose on the contrary that
\xi_{n}\to-\frac{\pi}{2}
(
n\to\infty
).
Let
\theta_{n}
be the tangential angle of
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}
.
By Lemma
5.8
, for each
n\in\mathbf{N}
there is
l^{*}_{n}\in(0,l_{n})
such that

(5.31)

\cos\big(\theta(l_{n})-\theta(l^{*}_{n})\big)\leq\cos c_{\psi}<1,

where
c_{\psi}>0
is defined as in Lemma
5.8
.
On the other hand, note that by the change of variables

(5.32)

\cos\big(\theta(l_{n})-\theta(l^{*}_{n})\big)=\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(l_{n}),\tilde{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(l_{n}^{*})\rangle=\frac{\langle\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n}),\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n}^{*})\rangle}{|\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n})||\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n}^{*})|},

where
\xi^{*}_{n}:=\am(\alpha_{n}l_{n}^{*}-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})
.
To calculate the right-hand side we deduce from (
5.26
) that

(5.33)

\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\frac{d}{d\xi}\begin{pmatrix}2\mathrm{E}(\xi,q_{n})-\mathrm{K}(\xi,q_{n})\\
2q_{n}\cos{\xi}\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\\
-2q_{n}\sin\xi\end{pmatrix}.

This yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{\langle\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n}),\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n}^{*})\rangle}{|\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n})||\hat{\gamma}_{n}^{\prime}(\xi_{n}^{*})|}=\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}}\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*}}\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}}}\\
-2q_{n}\sin\xi_{n}\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*}}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*}}}\\
-2q_{n}\sin\xi_{n}^{*}\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle\\
&=\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}}\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*}}\left[\frac{(1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n})(1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*})}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}}\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*}}}+4q_{n}^{2}\sin\xi_{n}\sin\xi_{n}^{*}\right]\\
&=(1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n})(1-2q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*})+4q_{n}^{2}\sin\xi_{n}\sin\xi_{n}^{*}\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}}\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi_{n}^{*}}.\end{split}

Since
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\leq q_{n}\leq 1
, there exist
q_{\infty}\in[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},1]
and a subsequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}
such that
q_{n_{j}}\to q_{\infty}
(
j\to\infty
).
Then, combining (
5
) with the assumption that
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\xi^{*}_{n}\leq\xi_{n}\to-\frac{\pi}{2}
(
n\to\infty
), we see that

(5.34)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\cos\big(\theta(l_{n_{j}})-\theta(l^{*}_{n_{j}})\big)=(1-2q_{\infty}^{2})(1-2q_{\infty}^{2})+4q_{\infty}^{2}\sqrt{1-q_{\infty}^{2}}\sqrt{1-q_{\infty}^{2}}=1,

which contradicts (
5.31
).
Thus
\xi_{n}
does not converge to
-\frac{\pi}{2}
and this together with (
5.30
) implies that

(5.35)

\displaystyle\xi_{n}\to\infty\quad\text{or}\quad\xi_{n}\to\frac{\pi}{2}+m\pi\ \ \text{for some}\ \ m\in\mathbf{N}\cup\{0\}.

Step 2
(
Estimate parameters
\alpha_{n}
and
q_{n}
)
.

Thanks to the previous step, we see that
\xi_{n}\geq\frac{\pi}{4}
for sufficiently large
n
.
Combining (
5.24
) with the fact that
q_{n}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

(5.36)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]\geq\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi\geq 2\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\cos^{2}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\xi\geq 2\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{0}\cos^{2}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\xi\geq\alpha_{n}\frac{\pi}{2}

for large enough
n\in\mathbf{N}
.
This together with (
5.21
) implies that
\alpha_{n}\to 0
(
n\to\infty
).
We claim in addition that, up to a subsequence,

(5.37)

\displaystyle q_{n}\to q_{*}\quad(n\to\infty),

where
q_{*}
is a unique zero of
q\mapsto|2\mathrm{E}(q)-\mathrm{K}(q)|.

Indeed, by the fact that
a_{n}\in[a_{0},1/2]
we can find an
a_{\infty}\in[a_{0},1/2]
such that
\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+\psi(a_{n})^{2}}
converges to
\sqrt{a_{\infty}^{2}+\psi(a_{\infty})^{2}}
(up to a subsequence).
On the other hand, for
\hat{X}_{n}(\xi):=\langle\hat{\gamma}_{n}(\xi),e_{1}\rangle
we see that
[-\pi/2,\xi_{n}]\ni\xi\mapsto|\hat{X}_{n}(\xi)-\hat{X}_{n}(\frac{\pi}{2})|
is strictly increasing as in (
5.26
) so that

(5.38)

|\gamma_{n}(x_{n})|=\big|\hat{\gamma}_{n}(\xi_{n})-\hat{\gamma}_{n}(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})\big|\geq\big|\hat{X}_{n}(\xi_{n})-\hat{X}_{n}(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})\big|\geq\big|\hat{X}_{n}(\tfrac{\pi}{2})-\hat{X}_{n}(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})\big|=\frac{2}{\alpha_{n}}|2\mathrm{E}(q_{n})-\mathrm{K}(q_{n})|.

By (
5.27
) the left-hand side is equal to
\sqrt{a_{n}^{2}+\psi(a_{n})^{2}}
, which converges to a finite value.
This fact together with
\alpha_{n}\to 0
implies that it is necessary to hold that
|2\mathrm{E}(q_{n})-\mathrm{K}(q_{n})|\to 0
, and this is equivalent to (
5.37
).

Step 3
(
Energy estimate
)
.

Now we deduce an improved estimate of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]
.
By symmetry we have to count the energy of
\tilde{\gamma}_{n}|_{[0,l_{n}]}
twice, so that we obtain

(5.39)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]=B[\gamma_{n}]+\lambda_{n}L[\gamma_{n}]\geq 2B[\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,l_{n}]}]+2\lambda_{n}L[\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,l_{n}]}].

Similar to (
5.24
) we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}2B[\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,l_{n}]}]=2\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi\geq 2\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{0}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi=\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

On the other hand, by the fact that
\lambda_{n}=2\alpha_{n}^{2}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)
and by the change of variables
\xi=\am(\alpha_{n}s-\mathrm{K}(q_{n}),q_{n})
, we have

(5.40)

2\lambda_{n}L[\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,l_{n}]}]=2\lambda_{n}\int_{0}^{l_{n}}\;\mathrm{d}s=4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\xi_{n}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since
\xi_{n}\geq\frac{\pi}{4}
holds for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbf{N}
, it follows that

(5.41)

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{n}L[\tilde{\gamma}|_{[0,l_{n}]}]

\displaystyle\geq 4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{4}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi

(5.42)

\displaystyle=4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\mathrm{K}(q_{n})+4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{4}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi

(5.43)

\displaystyle\geq 4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\mathrm{K}(q_{n})+\pi\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1).

The previous computations therefore yield

(5.44)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]\geq\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi+4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\mathrm{K}(q_{n})+\pi\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1).

Step 4
(
Construction of a comparison curve
)
.

Let
\hat{\gamma}_{*}:[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\to\mathbf{R}^{2}
be a curve defined by

(5.45)

\hat{\gamma}_{*}(\xi):=\begin{pmatrix}2\mathrm{E}(\xi,q_{*})-\mathrm{F}(\xi,q_{*})\\
2q_{*}\cos{\xi}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\hat{\gamma}_{*}
parametrizes a
half-fold figure-eight elastica, see (
2.16
) with
n=1
.
Using this
\hat{\gamma}_{*}
, for each
n\in\mathbf{N}
we define
c_{n}:[-\pi/2,\alpha_{n}+\pi/2]
by

(5.46)

\displaystyle c_{n}(\xi):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\hat{\gamma}_{*}(\xi)&\xi\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},0],\\
\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}(\xi,2q_{*})&\xi\in[0,\alpha_{n}],\\
\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\hat{\gamma}_{*}(\xi-\alpha_{n})+(1,0)\ \ &\xi\in[\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n}+\frac{\pi}{2}].\end{cases}

Now we show that
c_{n}\in A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbf{N}
, after suitable reparametrization.
First, the reflectional symmetry immediately follows by construction.
Noting that

(5.47)

\lim_{\xi\uparrow 0}c_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=\lim_{\xi\downarrow 0}c_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=\lim_{\xi\uparrow\alpha_{n}}c_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=\lim_{\xi\downarrow\alpha_{n}}c_{n}^{\prime}(\xi)=(\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{n}},0),

we see that
c_{n}\in C^{1}([-\pi/2,\alpha_{n}+\pi/2];\mathbf{R}^{2})
.
In addition,
c_{n}
is
also of class
H^{2}
since each component is of class
H^{2}
and the values and tangents coincide at the gluing points.
Furthermore, by the fact that
\frac{2q_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\alpha_{n}}>\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\geq\psi(\frac{1}{2})
for large enough
n
, we see that for all (large)
n\in\mathbf{N}

(5.48)

c_{n}^{2}(\xi)=\frac{2q_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}>\psi(\tfrac{1}{2})=\max_{z\in\mathbf{R}}\psi(z)\geq\psi(c_{n}^{1}(\xi))\quad\text{for all}\ \ \xi\in[0,\alpha_{n}].

Moreover, one readily checks that for
\xi\in[-{\pi}/{2},0]
there holds
c_{n}^{1}(\xi)\leq 0
, which is why
c_{n}^{2}(\xi)\geq 0\geq\psi(c_{n}^{1}(\xi))
for all
\xi\in[-{\pi}/{2},0]
.
From the fact that for
\xi\in[\alpha_{n},{\pi}/{2}+\alpha_{n}]
one has
c_{n}^{1}(\xi)\geq 1
, similarly we obtain
c_{n}^{2}(\xi)\geq 0\geq\psi(c_{n}^{1}(\xi))
for all
\xi\in[\alpha_{n},{\pi}/{2}+\alpha_{n}]
.
Thus we see that for all (large)
n\in\mathbf{N}

c_{n}\in A_{\rm sym}\subset A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
(up to reparametrization).
Now we compute the energy of
c_{n}
.
Similar to (
5.23
) we can compute the bending energy of
c_{n}|_{[-\frac{\pi}{2},0]}=\hat{\gamma}_{*}|_{[-\frac{\pi}{2},0]}
so that

(5.49)

B[c_{n}]=\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{4q_{*}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{*}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi.

By definition we have

(5.50)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}L[c_{n}]

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}\big(L[c_{n}|_{[-\frac{\pi}{2},0]}]+L[c_{n}|_{[0,\alpha_{n}]}]+L[c_{n}|_{[\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n}+\frac{\pi}{2}]}]\big)

(5.51)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\mathrm{K}(q_{*})+1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{n}}\mathrm{K}(q_{*})\Big)=4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\mathrm{K}(q_{*})+2\alpha_{n}^{2}(2q_{n}^{2}-1),

where in the last equality we used
\lambda_{n}=2\alpha_{n}^{2}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)
.
Thus it follows that

(5.52)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[c_{n}]

\displaystyle=\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{4q_{*}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{*}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\;\mathrm{d}\xi+4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\mathrm{K}(q_{*})+2\alpha_{n}^{2}(2q_{n}^{2}-1).

Step 5
(
Conclusion
)
.

Recall from step 1 and step 3 that
\gamma_{n}

is a minimizer in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
and
c_{n}\in A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
.
Therefore

(5.53)

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[c_{n}]\geq\inf_{\gamma\in A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma]=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}].

These together with (
5.44
) and (
5.52
) imply that

(5.54)

\displaystyle 0\geq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[\gamma_{n}]-\mathcal{E}_{\lambda_{n}}[c_{n}]

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{n}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\bigg(\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}-\frac{4q_{*}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{*}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}\xi

(5.55)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha_{n}(\pi-2\alpha_{n})(2q_{n}^{2}-1)+4\alpha_{n}(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\big(\mathrm{K}(q_{n})-\mathrm{K}(q_{*})\big).

Dividing this inequality by
\alpha_{n}>0
, we obtain

(5.56)

0\geq\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\bigg(\frac{4q_{n}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{n}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}-\frac{4q_{*}^{2}\cos^{2}\xi}{\sqrt{1-q_{*}^{2}\sin^{2}\xi}}\bigg)\;\mathrm{d}\xi+(\pi-2\alpha_{n})(2q_{n}^{2}-1)+4(2q_{n}^{2}-1)\big(\mathrm{K}(q_{n})-\mathrm{K}(q_{*})\big).

Due to the fact that
q_{n}\to q_{*}
and
\alpha_{n}\to 0
(
n\to\infty
), we find that the expression on the right-hand side of the above inequality
tends to a strictly positive value
\pi(2q_{*}^{2}-1)
.
A contradiction.
We have now shown that minimizers do not touch the obstacle. By Remark
5.4
they must be given by a penalized pinned elastica, all of which are characterized by the prototypes in Proposition
2.2
.
Since a trivial line segment cannot lie in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
by (A3) but
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
is admissible by (
1.7
), by using minimality of
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
(cf. Remark
2.3
) we conclude that (up to reflection)
\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,1}
is the only minimizer in
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
.
∎

Now we apply the non-touching result for
A_{\rm sym}^{\lozenge}
to the original problem.

1.2
.

. Fix

6.
Touching the obstacle for large
\lambda

In the previous section we have learned that for small values of
\lambda>0
one can in general have nontouching minimizers. The range of values
\lambda
for which the nontouching result can be obtained depends in general on
\psi
, cf. Theorem
1.2
. The goal of this section is to show that for suitably large values of
\lambda
the obstacle must be touched by each minimizer. A crucial difference is that in this section we obtain a range of values of
\lambda
that does not depend on
\psi
(as long as
\psi
satisfies assumptions (A1)–(A3)). The key ingredient is the stability analysis for critical points of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A_{\rm pin}
performed in
[
16
]
. Recall the definition of the value
\hat{\lambda}
in (
2.6
). This will exactly coincide with the threshold value given in Theorem
1.3
, whose proof we present now.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\lambda>\hat{\lambda}
and let
\psi:\mathbf{R}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}
be an arbitrary obstacle satisfying (A1)–(A3). Suppose that
\gamma\in A
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
in
A
. We assume for a contradiction that
I_{\gamma}=\emptyset
. This means in particular that
\gamma^{2}>\psi\circ\gamma^{1}
. Define

(6.1)

\delta:=\min_{x\in[0,1]}\big(\gamma^{2}(x)-\psi(\gamma^{1}(x))\big)>0.

According to Lemma
3.1
,
\gamma
must be a penalized pinned elastica with parameter
\lambda
. In particular, up to reparametrization and reflection,
\gamma
lies in one of the following three classes (cf. Proposition
2.2
)

(C1)

\gamma=\gamma_{\rm sarc}^{\lambda,1,n}
for some
n\geq n_{\lambda},

(C2)

\gamma=\gamma_{\rm larc}^{\lambda,1,n}
for some
n\geq n_{\lambda},

(C3)

\gamma=\gamma_{\rm loop}^{\lambda,1,n}
for some
n\geq 1.

First we assume that
\gamma
lies in class either (C1) or (C2). In this case one has

(6.2)

n\geq n_{\lambda}=\left\lceil\tfrac{\lambda}{\hat{\lambda}}\right\rceil\geq 2.

Due to this fact, all the curves listed in (C1) and (C2) are not local minimizers in
A_{\rm pin}
, see
[
16
]
*Theorem 3.7 and Theorem 3.8.
This implies that there exists a sequence
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\subset A_{\rm pin}
converging to

in

such that
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma_{j}]<\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}[\gamma]
for all
j\in\mathbf{N}
. Since
H^{2}(0,1)
embeds into
C^{0}([0,1])
this convergence is also uniform. In particular (due to uniform continuity of
\psi
) we deduce from (

) that for
j
sufficiently large there holds for any

(6.3)

\gamma_{j}^{2}(x)-\psi\circ\gamma_{j}^{1}(x)>\frac{\delta}{2}>0.

Hence we conclude that
\gamma_{j}\in A

in

. Next assume that
\gamma
lies in class (C3). By

]
*Theorem 3.6,
\gamma
is unstable in

∎

Appendix A
Elliptic integrals and functions

In this article we have used the
elliptic integrals

(A.1)

\mathrm{F}(x,q):=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)}}\;\mathrm{d}\theta\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\mathrm{E}(x,q):=\int_{0}^{x}\sqrt{1-q^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)}\;\mathrm{d}\theta

for
x\in\mathbf{R}
and
q\in(0,1)
.
Further we define
\mathrm{K}(q):=\mathrm{F}(\frac{\pi}{2},q)
and
\mathrm{E}(q):=\mathrm{E}(\frac{\pi}{2},q)
.
It is known that
[0,1)\ni q\mapsto\mathrm{K}(q)
and
[0,1]\ni q\mapsto\mathrm{E}(q)
are strictly increasing and strictly decreasing, respectively.
More precisely, one has

(A.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}^{\prime}(q)=\frac{\mathrm{E}(q)}{q(1-q^{2})}-\frac{\mathrm{K}(q)}{q}>0,\quad\mathrm{E}^{\prime}(q)=\frac{\mathrm{E}(q)}{q}-\frac{\mathrm{K}(q)}{q}<0.

This monotonicity leads to the following

Lemma A.1
.

The function
Q:[0,1)\ni q\mapsto 2\mathrm{E}(q)-\mathrm{K}(q)
is strictly decreasing, and satisfies
Q(0)=1
,
\lim_{q\to 1}Q(q)=-\infty
.
In particular, there exists a unique
q_{*}\in(0,1)
such that
2\mathrm{E}(q_{*})-\mathrm{K}(q_{*})=0
.

The constant
q_{*}
stands for the modulus of the so-called figure-eight elastica; more precisely, the signed curvature of the figure-eight elastica is given by
k(s)=2q_{*}\cn(s,q_{*})
, up to similarity and reparametrization (cf.
[
15
, Definition 5.1]
).

Next we recall the
Jacobian elliptic functions

\cn,\sn
.

Definition A.2

(Elliptic functions)
.

We define the
Jacobian amplitude function

\am(x,q)
by the inverse function of
F(x,q)
, so that

(A.3)

x=\int_{0}^{\am(x,q)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}\;\mathrm{d}\theta\quad\text{for}\ x\in\mathbf{R}.

For
q\in[0,1)
, the
Jacobian elliptic functions
are given by

(A.4)

\displaystyle\cn(x,q):=\cos\am(x,q),\qquad\sn(x,q):=\sin\am(x,q),\qquad x\in\mathbf{R}.

The Jacobian elliptic functions have the following fundamental properties.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\cn(\cdot,q)
and
\sn(\cdot,q)
be the elliptic functions with modulus
q\in[0,1)
.
Then,
\cn(\cdot,q)
is an even
2\mathrm{K}(q)
-antiperiodic function on
\mathbf{R}
and, in
[0,2\mathrm{K}(q)]
, strictly decreasing from
1
to
-1
.
Further,
\sn(\cdot,q)
is an odd
2\mathrm{K}(q)
-antiperiodic function and in
[-\mathrm{K}(q),\mathrm{K}(q)]
strictly increasing from
-1
to
1
.

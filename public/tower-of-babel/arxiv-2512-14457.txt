An Improved Approximation Algorithm for Maximum Weight 3-Path Packing
Jingyang Zhao, Mingyu Xiao

Abstract
Given a complete graph with n n vertices and non-negative edge weights, where n n is divisible by 3, the maximum weight 3-path packing problem is to find a set of n / 3 n/3 vertex-disjoint 3-paths such that the total weight of the 3-paths in the packing is maximized. This problem is closely related to the classic maximum weight matching problem. In this paper, we propose a 10 / 17 10/17 -approximation algorithm, improving the best-known 7 / 12 7/12 -approximation algorithm (ESA 2015). Our result is obtained by making a trade-off among three algorithms. The first is based on the maximum weight matching of size n / 2 n/2 , the second is based on the maximum weight matching of size n / 3 n/3 , and the last is based on an approximation algorithm for star packing. Our first algorithm is the same as the previous 7 / 12 7/12 -approximation algorithm, but we propose a new analysis method—a charging method—for this problem, which is not only essential to analyze our second algorithm but also may be extended to analyze algorithms for some related problems.

Introduction

A
3-path
in a graph is a simple path on three different vertices.
A
3-path packing
in a graph is a set of vertex-disjoint 3-paths.
Given an unweighted graph, the Maximum 3-Path Packing (M3PP) problem is to find a 3-path packing of maximum cardinality.
For an edge-weighted complete graph,
we assume that every edge has a non-negative weight and the number of vertices in the graph
n
is a multiple of 3.
A 3-path packing is
perfect
if its size is
n/3
(i.e., it can cover all vertices).
The Maximum Weight 3-Path Packing (MW3PP) problem is to find a perfect 3-path packing in an edge-weighted complete graph such that the total weight of all 3-paths is maximized.
MW3PP is a natural special case of the team formation problem
[
5
,
4
]
, where the goal is to find a set of teams, each containing a leader and two other members, such that the total weight of the connections between the team leader and the other team members is maximized.

In this paper, we study approximation algorithms for MW3PP.

1.2
Our results

In this paper, we improve the approximation ratio from
7/12\approx 0.58333
to
10/17>0.58823
for MW3PP. Our ratio is achieved by making a trade-off among three algorithms.

Let
P^{*}
be an optimal 3-path packing with the weight of
OPT
.
At the high-level, our first algorithm is to call the previous
7/12
-approximation algorithm
[
4
]
, which is based on a maximum weight matching of size
n/2
. The framework of the previous analysis of this algorithm is based on so-called uniformly
c
-sparse graphs
[
4
]
, which is a global graph structure property. However, we will give a counterexample to show that their analysis is incomplete, and hence we cannot directly employ their result. For the sake of completeness, we provide an alternative proof based on several local graph structure properties. Our new analysis method is also essential to analyze our second algorithm.
Our second algorithm is based on a maximum weight matching of size
n/3
. It is motivated by the following observation: in the worst case of the first algorithm the weight of the maximum weight matching of size
n/2
is the same as the weight of the maximum weight matching of size
n/3
.
A combination of the first two algorithms still cannot guarantee a better-than-
7/12
-approximation ratio. However, in the worst case of the first two algorithms we get that all of the weighted parts of the fixed optimal 3-path packing
P^{*}
lie within the graph
LG
induced by the vertex set of the maximum weight matching of size
n/3
. This special case is handled by our third algorithm, which is based on a good 2-star packing (i.e., a set of vertex-disjoint edges
K_{1,1}
and 3-paths
K_{1,2}
covering all vertices) in
LG
. Based on the
2/3
-approximation algorithm for the maximum weight 2-star packing problem
[
2
]
, we show that we can obtain a good 2-star packing in
LG
with a weight of at least
\frac{2}{3}\cdot\mbox{OPT}>\frac{7}{12}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
in the special case. Since there are
n/3
vertices in
RG\coloneqq G-LG
, any 2-star packing in
LG
can be transformed into a 3-path packing of
G
with a non-decreasing weight. Hence, we can obtain a 3-path packing with a weight of at least
\frac{2}{3}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
in the special case. At last, by making a trade-off among these three algorithms (i.e., returning the best solution among the results of these three algorithms), we show that we can obtain a better-than-
7/12
-approximation ratio.

2
Preliminaries

An instance of MW3PP is given by a complete graph
G=(V,E)
with
n
vertices, where
n\bmod 3=0
. There is a non-negative weight function
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
on the edges in
E
.
For any weight function
w:X\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we also extend it to the subsets of
X
, i.e., we define
w(X^{\prime})=\sum_{x\in X^{\prime}}w(x)
for any
X^{\prime}\subseteq X
. Given a subgraph
S
, we use
V(S)
(resp.,
E(S)
) to denote the set of vertices (resp., edges) contained in the subgraph, and for any function
f
on the edges, define
f(S)\coloneqq f(E(S))
. Given a set of vertices
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
, we use
G[V^{\prime}]
to denote the complete graph induced by
V^{\prime}
.
Two subgraphs or sets of edges are
vertex-disjoint
if they do not share a common vertex.
An undirected edge between vertices
u
and
v
is denoted by
uv
, and a directed edge from
u
to
v
is denoted by
(u,v)
. A directed edge will be called an
arc
.
We may consider a directed complete graph
\overleftrightarrow{G}=(V,A)
obtained by replacing each edge
uv\in E
of
G
by two arcs
\{(u,v),(v,u)\}
.

A
matching
is a set of vertex-disjoint edges. We use
M^{*}_{p}
to denote a maximum weight matching of size
p
in graph
G
, which can be found in
O(n^{3})
time if it exists
[
12
,
25
]
. The maximum weight matching without the constraint on its size can also be found in
O(n^{3})
time
[
12
,
25
]
.
We assume that
n
is even so that
M^{*}_{n/2}
exists; otherwise, we use
O(n^{2})
loops to enumerate one 3-path in the optimal solution of MW3PP, and in the loop we get an approximate 3-path packing in the graph obtained by removing the 3-path, where the number of vertices is even. The approximation ratio is preserved at a cost of a runtime increase by a factor of
O(n^{2})
.

A 3-path
xyz
is a simple path on three different vertices
\{x,y,z\}
, and it contains exactly two edges
\{xy,yz\}
.
A
perfect 3-path packing
in graph
G
is a set of
n/3
vertex-disjoint 3-paths that cover all vertices of
G
.
In the following, we will always consider a 3-path packing as a perfect 3-path packing and use
P^{*}
to denote a maximum weight 3-path packing in
G
. We also let
\mbox{OPT}\coloneqq w(P^{*})
, and assume
\mbox{OPT}\neq 0
to exclude this trivial case.

A
t
-star is a complete bipartite graph
K_{1,t}
, where there is one vertex on one side,
t
vertices on the other side, and
t
edges in total. Hence, an edge is a 1-star, and a 3-path is a 2-star. A
t
-star packing
in a graph is a set of vertex-disjoint
t^{\prime}
-stars with
1\leq t^{\prime}\leq t
that cover all vertices of the graph. Note that a 2-star packing is a set of edges and 3-paths, and a 3-path packing is also a 2-star packing by definition.
To avoid confusion with normal edges, we refer to the edges in a star packing as a
2-path
.
In a directed graph, the in-degree (resp., out-degree) of a vertex
v
is the number of arcs entering (resp., leaving)
v
. A
t
-feasible arc set
in a directed graph is a subgraph such that each vertex has an in-degree of at most 1 and an out-degree of at most
t
.

Due to limited space, the proofs of the lemmas and theorems marked with “*” have been moved to the appendix.

3
The First Algorithm

The first algorithm has already
been considered in
[
4
]
, and we denote it by Alg.1. It contains the following four steps.

Step 1.
Find
M^{*}_{n/2}
, a maximum weight matching of size
n/2
in the graph
G
.

Step 2.
Construct a multi-graph
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
by contracting the edges
in
M^{*}_{n/2}
, where there are
n/2
super-vertices one-to-one corresponding to the edges in
M^{*}_{n/2}
. For two super-vertices corresponding to
ux,yz\in M^{*}_{n/2}
, there are four edges
uy,uz,xy,xz
between them, and each edge
e\in\{uy,uz,xy,xz\}
is defined to have a cost of
c(e)\coloneqq w(e)-\min\{w(ux),w(yz)\}
.

Step 3.
Find a maximum cost matching
M^{**}_{n/6}
of size
n/6
in the graph
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
.

Step 4.
Obtain a 3-path packing
P_{1}
of
G
as follows:

•

For each edge
xy\in M^{**}_{n/6}
, if there are two edges
e_{x},e_{y}\in M^{*}_{n/2}
corresponding to the endpoints of
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
, where we assume that
w(e_{x})\geq w(e_{y})
, obtain a 3-path using edges
xy
and
e_{x}
, and call the vertex of
e_{y}
that is not incident to
xy
a
residual vertex
.

•

For each edge
e\in M^{*}_{n/2}
corresponding to a vertex of
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
that is not matched by
M^{**}_{n/6}
, create a 3-path from
e
with an arbitrary residual vertex.

1
.

contains one blue edge; In (c), Alg.1 outputs two 3-paths with a weight of

3.1
A note on the previous analysis

An easy bound related to
M^{*}_{n/2}
can be obtained as follows.

Lemma 1
.

We have
w(M^{*}_{n/2})\geq w(M^{*}_{n/3})\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
.

Proof.

Since the weights of edges are non-negative, we have
w(M^{*}_{n/2})\geq w(M^{*}_{n/3})
.
Since the optimal 3-path packing
P^{*}
contains
n/3
3-paths, it is easy to see that
\{xy\mid xyz\in P^{*},w(xy)\geq w(yz)\}
is a matching of size
n/3
. Hence, we have
w(M^{*}_{n/3})\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\frac{1}{2}(w(xy)+w(yz))=\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\frac{1}{2}w(xyz)=\frac{1}{2}w(P^{*})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
.
∎

Bar-Noy
et al.

[
4
]
proved that the approximation of Alg.1 is
\frac{7}{12}
using the following result.

Lemma 2

(
[
4
]
, cf. theorem 1)
.

It holds that
w(P_{1})\geq\frac{2}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
.

By Lemmas
1
and
2
, it is easy to get that
w(P_{1})\geq\frac{7}{12}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
. However, Lemma
2
may not hold. In the example shown in Fig.
1
, we have
w(M^{*}_{n/2})=3
,
\mbox{OPT}=2
, and
w(P_{1})=2
, while we get
w(P_{1})\geq 2+\frac{1}{2}>\mbox{OPT}
by Lemma
2
. Hence, Lemma
2
implies that the weight of
P_{1}
is even greater than the optimal solution, a contradiction.

Note that the costs of the edges in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
may be negative. Due to this, some inequalities in the proof of Lemma
2
in
[
4
]
may not hold.
By additionally analyzing this case using their method, we may still prove an approximation ratio of
\frac{7}{12}
for Alg.1. However, we provide a new analysis method since it is also essential to analyze our second algorithm.

3.2
The analysis

First, we consider the quality of the 3-path packing
P_{1}
returned by Alg.1.

Lemma 3

(
[
4
]
)
.

It holds that
w(P_{1})\geq w(M^{*}_{n/2})+c(M^{**}_{n/6})
.

Proof.

Let
M^{\prime}
be the set of edges in
M^{*}_{n/2}
such that each edge corresponds to a vertex of
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
that is matched by
M^{**}_{n/6}
, and let
M^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq M^{*}_{n/2}\setminus M^{\prime}
. Note that
|M^{\prime}|=n/3
and
|M^{\prime\prime}|=|M^{*}_{n/2}|-|M^{\prime}|=n/2-n/3=n/6
.

By Step 4 of Alg.1, we have that the 3-paths in
P_{1}
contain all edges in
M^{**}_{n/6}
,
n/6
edges in
M^{\prime}
, and all edges in
M^{\prime\prime}
(see the example in Fig.
1
).
Specifically, for each edge
xy\in M^{**}_{n/6}
, there are two edges
e_{x},e_{y}\in M^{*}_{n/2}
corresponding to the endpoints of
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
, and we have
e_{x},e_{y}\in M^{\prime}
. If
w(e_{x})\geq w(e_{y})
, there is a 3-path containing two edges
xy
and
e_{x}
, and we have
w(xy)+w(e_{x})=w(xy)+w(e_{x})+w(e_{y})-\min\{w(e_{x}),w(e_{y})\}=w(e_{x})+w(e_{y})+c(xy)
. Hence, the weight of the
n/6
3-paths using
n/6
edges in
M^{**}_{n/6}
and
n/6
edges in
M^{\prime}
has a weight of
w(M^{\prime})+c(M^{**}_{n/6})
. Moreover, for each
e\in M^{\prime\prime}
, there exists a 3-path in
P_{1}
containing it with a residual vertex. Since
|M^{\prime\prime}|=n/6
, the weight of these
n/6
3-paths has a weight of at least
w(M^{\prime\prime})
. Hence, we have
w(P_{1})\geq w(M^{\prime})+c(M^{**}_{n/6})+w(M^{\prime\prime})=w(M^{*}_{n/2})+c(M^{**}_{n/6})
.
∎

We have obtained a bound related to
w(M^{*}_{n/2})
in Lemma
1
.
To obtain a bound related to
c(M^{**}_{n/6})
, we first define some notation. We split 3-paths in
P^{*}
into two disjoint sets
P^{*}_{1}
and
P^{*}_{2}
, where
P^{*}_{1}\coloneqq\{xyz\mid xyz\in P^{*},\{xy,yz\}\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})=\emptyset\}
and
P^{*}_{2}\coloneqq P^{*}\setminus P^{*}_{1}
. Hence,
P^{*}_{1}
contains all 3-paths in
P^{*}
with edges not contained in
M^{*}_{n/2}
, and for each 3-path in
P^{*}_{2}
, there is exactly one edge contained in
M^{*}_{n/2}
. In this section, we let

E_{1}\coloneqq E(P^{*}_{1})\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad E_{2}\coloneqq E(P^{*}_{2})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2}).

Lemma 4
.

For any edge
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
, there are two edges
e_{x},e_{y}\in M^{*}_{n/2}
corresponding to the endpoints of
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
. For any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, it holds that
c(xy)\geq w(xy)-(\theta\cdot w(e_{x})+(1-\theta)\cdot w(e_{y}))
.

Proof.

We have
c(xy)=w(xy)-\min\{w(e_{x}),w(e_{y})\}
by definition. Assume w.l.o.g. that
w(e_{x})=\min\{w(e_{x}),w(e_{y})\}\leq w(e_{y})
. We get
c(xy)=w(xy)-w(e_{x})=w(xy)-(\theta\cdot w(e_{x})+(1-\theta)\cdot w(e_{x}))\geq w(xy)-(\theta\cdot w(e_{x})+(1-\theta)\cdot w(e_{y}))
since
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.
∎

Based on the result in Lemma
4
, we use a charging method (a new analysis method for this problem) to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

It holds that
c(E(P^{*})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2}))\geq w(P^{*})-\frac{4}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})
.

Proof.

By definition, we have
E(P^{*})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2})=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
and
E(P^{*})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})=E(P^{*}_{2})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})
. Hence,
E(P^{*})=E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup(E(P^{*}_{2})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2}))
, and it suffices to prove

c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})\geq w(E_{1})+w(E_{2})+w(E(P^{*}_{2})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2}))-\frac{4}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2}).

For any edge
xy\in E_{1}\cup E_{2}
, there are two edges
e_{x},e_{y}\in M^{*}_{n/2}
corresponding to the endpoints of
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
. By Lemma
4
, it holds that
c(xy)\geq w(xy)-(\theta\cdot w(e_{x})+(1-\theta)\cdot w(e_{y}))
for any
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. We say that
xy
charges
\theta
point from
e_{x}
and
1-\theta
point from
e_{y}
. Next, we design a charging rule so that each edge in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
receives 1 point in total.

A vertex is called an
i
-degree vertex
if it is incident to
i
edges in
E(P^{*})
. For any edge
xy\in E_{1}\cup E_{2}
, there exist exactly one 2-degree vertex and one 1-degree vertex in
\{x,y\}
. Assume w.l.o.g. that
x
is a 1-degree vertex and
y
is a 2-degree vertex. We let
xy
charge
\frac{2}{3}
point from
e_{x}
and
\frac{1}{3}
point from
e_{y}
. See Fig.
2
for an illustration.

:

Assume that for any edge

Then, by Lemma

, we get
c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})\geq w(E_{1})+w(E_{2})-\sum_{e\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})}\eta_{e}\cdot w(e)

Therefore, we only need to calculate
\eta_{e}
for each

. We consider the following two cases.

Case 1:
e=xy\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})\setminus E(P^{*})
.

Note that
x
is either a 1-degree vertex or a 2-degree vertex. If it is a 1-degree vertex, it is charged by
\frac{2}{3}

; otherwise, it is charged by

. Hence,

is charged by
\frac{2}{3}

. Similarly,

is charged by

. So,

is charged by

Case 2:

.

It is easy to see that
e
is charged by
\frac{1}{3}
point by only one edge in

).

Therefore, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})}\eta_{e}\cdot w(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})\setminus E(P^{*})}\frac{4}{3}w(e)+\sum_{e\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})\cap E(P^{*})}\frac{1}{3}w(e)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})}\frac{4}{3}w(e)-\sum_{e\in E(M^{*}_{n/2})\cap E(P^{*})}w(e)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})-w(E(P^{*}_{2})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})).

Hence, we get
c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})\geq w(E_{1})+w(E_{2})+w(E(P^{*}_{2})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2}))-\frac{4}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})
.
∎

We are ready to obtain a bound related to
c(M^{**}_{n/6})
.

Lemma 6
.

It holds that
c(M^{**}_{n/6})\geq\frac{1}{4}c(E_{1})+\frac{1}{2}c(E_{2})=\frac{1}{4}c(E(P^{*})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2}))+\frac{1}{4}c(E_{2})
.

Proof.

We will construct a matching
M^{\prime}_{n/6}
of size
n/6
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
using the edges in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
such that
c(M^{\prime}_{n/6})\geq\frac{1}{4}c(E_{1})+\frac{1}{2}c(E_{2})
.

Recall that
P^{*}=P^{*}_{1}\cup P^{*}_{2}
and
E(P^{*}_{1})\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})=\emptyset
.
We first select
n/3
edges in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
as follows.
For each 3-path
xyz\in P^{*}_{1}
with
c(xy)\geq c(yz)
, we select
xy
; for each 3-path
xyz\in P^{*}_{2}
with
xy\notin E(M^{*}_{n/2})
, we select
xy
. We denote the set of these edges by
E^{\prime}
. It holds that
c(E^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})
because
E_{1}=E(P^{*}_{1})
and
E_{2}=E(P^{*}_{2})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2})
. Since
E^{\prime}
contains only one edge from each 3-path in
P^{*}
, these edges are vertex-disjoint. Moreover, since these edges are distinct from the edges in
E(M^{*}_{n/2})
, we know that
E^{\prime}\cup E(M^{*}_{n/2})
forms a set of vertex-disjoint paths and cycles in
G
. Denote the set of the paths (resp., cycles) by
P_{E^{\prime}}
(resp.,
C_{E^{\prime}}
).
As proved in
[
4
]
, the cycles in
C_{E^{\prime}}
can be eliminated by updating the edges in
E_{1}\cap E^{\prime}
in a way satisfying that
E^{\prime}\cup E(M^{*}_{n/2})
forms a set of paths only, while maintaining
E^{\prime}\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})=\emptyset
,
|E^{\prime}|=n/3
, and
c(E^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})
.
We give a self-contained proof.

Let
e_{v}
be the edge in
M^{*}_{n/2}
containing
v
as an endpoint.
We color the edges in
E^{\prime}
by red and the edges in
M^{*}_{n/2}
by blue.
Assume that there is a cycle
C\in C_{E^{\prime}}
.
On the cycle
C
, the number of red edges, which is equal to the number of blue edges, is at least 2. This is because
E^{\prime}\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})=\emptyset
, the edges in
E^{\prime}
are vertex-disjoint, and the edges in
E(M^{*}_{n/2})
are also vertex-disjoint.
For each red edge
xy\in E(C)
, we assume that there is a 3-path
xyz\in P^{*}
. It is easy to see that
xy,yz\in E_{1}
; otherwise, we have
xy\in E_{2}
,
e_{y}=e_{z}=yz
, and the blue edge
yz
will be incident to only one red edge in
E^{\prime}\cup E(M^{*}_{n/2})
, a contradiction. Hence, we have
E^{\prime}\cap E(C)=E_{1}\cap E(C)
. Moreover, we get that the blue edge
e_{z}
is an end edge (i.e., the first or the last edge) of a path
P\in P_{E^{\prime}}
. See Fig.
3
for an illustration.

:

C\in C_{E^{\prime}}

, where

Note that if we color
yz
with red instead of
xy
, i.e., we select
yz
instead of

when selecting
n/3
edges in
E_{1}\cup E_{2}
, the cycle

For each cycle
C\in C_{E^{\prime}}
, we assume that
x_{C}y_{C}z_{C}\in P^{*}
and
x_{C}y_{C}
is the red edge attaining

\arg\min_{xy\in E(C)\cap E^{\prime},xyz\in P^{*}}\{c(xy)-c(yz)\}.

Let
E^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq E^{\prime}\setminus\{x_{C}y_{C}\mid C\in C_{E^{\prime}}\}\cup\{y_{C}z_{C}\mid C\in C_{E^{\prime}}\}
. Note that
E^{\prime\prime}
is obtained by updating the edges in
E_{1}\cap E^{\prime}
and
E^{\prime\prime}
still contains
n/3
edges. It is easy to see that
E^{\prime\prime}\cup E(M^{*}_{n/2})
forms a set of paths only. Next, we show that
c(E^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})
.

Let
E^{\prime}_{1}\coloneqq\bigcup_{C\in C_{E^{\prime}}}E^{\prime}\cap E(C)
and
E^{\prime\prime}_{1}\coloneqq E^{\prime}\cap E_{1}\setminus E^{\prime}_{1}
. Then, we have
E^{\prime}\cap E_{1}=E^{\prime}_{1}\cup E^{\prime\prime}_{1}
. Recall that
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}\cap E_{1}\cup E^{\prime}\cap E_{2}
and
E^{\prime}\cap E_{2}=E_{2}
. We have
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}_{1}\cup E^{\prime\prime}_{1}\cup E_{2}
. Note that
E(C)\cap E^{\prime}=\{xy\mid xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C),xyz\in P^{*}\}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle c(E^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=c(E^{\prime})+\sum_{C\in C_{E^{\prime}}}(c(y_{C}z_{C})-c(x_{C}y_{C}))

\displaystyle=c(E^{\prime}_{1})+c(E^{\prime\prime}_{1})+c(E_{2})+\sum_{C\in C_{E^{\prime}}}(c(y_{C}z_{C})-c(x_{C}y_{C}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{C\in C_{E^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C),\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}c(xy)+(c(y_{C}z_{C})-c(x_{C}y_{C}))\right)+c(E^{\prime\prime}_{1})+c(E_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{C\in C_{E^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C),\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}c(xy)+(c(y_{C}z_{C})-c(x_{C}y_{C}))\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime\prime}_{1},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)+c(E_{2}),

where the inequality follows from
c(xy)\geq\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)
since by the selection of
xy\in E^{\prime}_{1}\cup E^{\prime\prime}_{1}
with
xyz\in P^{*}
we have
c(xy)\geq c(yz)
.

For each cycle
C\in C_{E^{\prime}}
, there are at least two red edges in
E(C)
, and hence there exists another red edge
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\in E(C)\cap E^{\prime}
and a 3-path
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime}\in P^{*}
such that
x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\neq x_{C}y_{C}
and
c(x_{C}y_{C})-c(y_{C}z_{C})\leq c(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})-c(y^{\prime}z^{\prime})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle c(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})+c(y_{C}z_{C})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}(c(x_{C}y_{C})+c(y_{C}z_{C})+c(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})+c(y^{\prime}z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(c(x_{C}y_{C}z_{C})+c(x^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime})).

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C),\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}c(xy)+(c(y_{C}z_{C})-c(x_{C}y_{C}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C)\setminus\{x_{C}y_{C},x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}c(xy)+(c(x_{C}y_{C})+c(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})+c(y_{C}z_{C})-c(x_{C}y_{C}))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C)\setminus\{x_{C}y_{C},x^{\prime}y^{\prime}\},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)+\frac{1}{2}(c(x_{C}y_{C}z_{C})+c(x^{\prime}y^{\prime}z^{\prime}))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C),\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz),

where the inequality follows from
c(xy)\geq\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)
since
E^{\prime}\cap E(C)\subseteq E^{\prime}_{1}
and by the selection of
xy\in E^{\prime}_{1}\cup E^{\prime\prime}_{1}
with
xyz\in P^{*}
we have
c(xy)\geq c(yz)
.

Then, we get

\displaystyle c(E^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{C\in C_{E^{\prime}}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E(C),\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime\prime}_{1},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)+c(E_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}_{1},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime\prime}_{1},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)+c(E_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E^{\prime}\cap E_{1},\\
xyz\in P^{*}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2}c(xyz)+c(E_{2})=\frac{1}{2}c(E_{1})+c(E_{2}),

where the last equality follows from the fact that
E^{\prime}\cap E_{1}
is obtained by selecting an edge from each 3-path in
P^{*}_{1}
and
E(P^{*}_{1})=E_{1}
.

Therefore, given the optimal solution, we can construct a set of edges
E^{\prime\prime}
in a way satisfying that
E^{\prime\prime}\cap E(M^{*}_{n/2})=\emptyset
and
E^{\prime\prime}\cup E(M^{*}_{n/2})
is a set of paths with
c(E^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{1}{2}c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})
and
|E^{\prime\prime}|=n/3
. Hence,
E^{\prime\prime}
is a set of paths in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
, and it can be decomposed into two matchings of size
n/6
, as
n/3
is even. Therefore, there exists a matching
M^{\prime}
of size
n/6
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
with a cost of at least
\frac{1}{2}c(E^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{1}{4}c(E_{1})+\frac{1}{2}c(E_{2})
. We get
c(M^{**}_{n/6})\geq c(M^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{4}c(E_{1})+\frac{1}{2}c(E_{2})
by the optimality of
M^{**}_{n/6}
.
By the proof of Lemma
5
, we have
c(E_{1})+c(E_{2})=c(E(P^{*})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2}))
, and hence
c(M^{**}_{n/6})\geq\frac{1}{4}c(E_{1})+\frac{1}{2}c(E_{2})=\frac{1}{4}c(E(P^{*})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2}))+\frac{1}{4}c(E_{2})
.
∎

Then, we are ready to analyze the approximation quality of Alg.1.

Lemma 7
.

It holds that
w(P_{1})\geq\frac{2}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}\geq\frac{7}{12}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that

c(E_{2})\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}(\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}).

(1)

For each 3-path
xyz\in P^{*}_{2}
, we assume that
xy\notin M^{*}_{n/2}
.
There are two edges
e_{x}
,
e_{y}\in M^{*}_{n/2}
corresponding to the endpoints of
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/2}
. Note that
e_{y}=yz
.
By Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle c(E_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}_{2}}c(xy)\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}_{2}}(w(xy)-w(yz))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}_{2}}(\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}(\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}),

where the first inequality follows from
c(xy)=w(xy)-\min\{w(e_{x}),w(e_{y})\}\geq w(xy)-w(e_{y})=w(xy)-w(yz)
, and the last from the fact that for any 3-path
xyz
we have
\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}\leq 0
.

Thus, (
1
) holds.

Then, we have that

\displaystyle w(P_{1})

\displaystyle\geq w(M^{*}_{n/2})+c(M^{**}_{n/6})

\displaystyle\geq w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{4}c(E(P^{*})\setminus E(M^{*}_{n/2}))+\frac{1}{4}c(E_{2})

\displaystyle\geq w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{4}\left(w(P^{*})-\frac{4}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})\right)+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}(\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\mbox{OPT}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}(\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\mbox{OPT}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}(\min\{w(xy),w(yz)\}+\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}-2\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\mbox{OPT}+\frac{1}{4}\cdot\mbox{OPT}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{6}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}=\frac{7}{12}\cdot\mbox{OPT},

where the first inequality follows from Lemma
3
, the second from Lemma
6
, the third from Lemma
5
and (
1
), the fourth from
w(M^{*}_{n/2})\geq\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}
by Lemma
1
, the last from
w(M^{*}_{n/2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}
by Lemma
1
.
∎

Note that the middle result
w(P_{1})\geq\frac{2}{3}w(M^{*}_{n/2})+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{xyz\in P^{*}}\max\{w(xy),w(yz)\}
in Lemma

will be used in our final analysis.
In the proof of Lemma

. However, we also have
w(M^{*}_{n/2})\geq w(M^{*}_{n/3})\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mbox{OPT}

Next, we design our second algorithm based on

4
The Second Algorithm

Our second algorithm is based on
M^{*}_{n/3}
, and we denote it by Alg.2. It contains four steps.

Step 1.
Find
M^{*}_{n/3}
, a maximum weight matching of size
n/3
in the graph
G
.

Step 2.
Construct a multi-graph

n/3

n/3

M^{*}_{n/3}
and
n/3
vertices in
V\setminus V(M^{*}_{n/3})
. We define the cost function

on the edges in

as follows:

•

For two super-vertices corresponding to edges

, there are four edges

between them, and each edge

is defined to have a cost of

.

•

For one super-vertex corresponding to one edge

and one vertex
z\in V\setminus V(M^{*}_{n/3})
, there are two edges
xz,yz
between them, and each edge
e\in\{xz,yz\}
is defined to have a cost of
c(e)\coloneqq w(e)
.

•

For two vertices
x,y\in V\setminus V(M^{*}_{n/3})
, there is one edge
e=xy
between them, and
e
is defined to have a cost of
c(e)\coloneqq-\infty
.

Step 3.
Find a maximum cost matching
M^{**}
of size at most
n/3
in the graph
G/M^{*}_{n/3}
.

Step 4.
Obtain a 3-path packing
P_{2}
of
G
as follows:

•

For each edge
xy\in M^{**}
,
if there are two edges
e_{x},e_{y}\in M^{*}_{n/3}
corresponding to the endpoints of
xy
in
G/M^{*}_{n/3}
, where we assume that
w(e_{x})\geq w(e_{y})
, obtain a 3-path using edges
xy
and
e_{x}
, and call the vertex of
e_{y}
that is not incident to
xy
a
residual vertex
.

•

For each edge
xy\in M^{**}
,
if there is an edge
e_{x}\in M^{*}_{n/3}
corresponding to the endpoint
x
in
G/M^{*}_{n/3}
and a vertex
y\in V\setminus V(M^{*}_{n/3})
, obtain a 3-path using edges
xy
and
e_{x}
.

•

For each vertex in
V\setminus V(M^{*}_{n/3})
, call it a
residual vertex
if it is not matched by
M^{**}
. Arbitrarily create vertex-disjoint 2-paths using all residual vertices.

In Step 3 of Alg.2, we do not find a maximum cost matching
M^{**}_{n/3}
of size
n/3
. The reason is that we will construct a matching
M^{\prime}
in
G/M^{*}_{n/3}
whose size may not be
n/3
, and then obtain a bound
c(M^{**})\geq c(M^{\prime})
by the optimality of
M^{**}
, while we may have
c(M^{**})>c(M^{**}_{n/3})
since the cost function may be negative, and then we cannot get

Conclusion

In this paper, we propose a
\frac{10}{17}
-approximation algorithm for MW3PP, improving the previous claimed approximation ratio of
\frac{7}{12}
. Our algorithm is based on three algorithms. The analysis is based on a newly proposed charging method.
In the future, it would be interesting to extend our methods to design and analyze algorithms for related problems, such as MW3CP
[
8
,
9
]
, MW4PP
[
17
]
, and MW4CP
[

]
.

We remark that for MW4PP, the best-known
3/4
-approximation algorithm
[
17
]
has remained the state-of-the-art for nearly 30 years.
A similar situation can be observed: in the worst case, the weight of a maximum weight matching of size
n/2
is roughly the same as that of a maximum weight matching of size
n/4
.
Whether our method can be used to break the
3/4
-approximation barrier remains an open problem.

Moreover, it would be worthwhile to explore better constructions of 2-star packings than the one in Lemma

]
. Such improvements may further enhance the approximation ratio for MW3PP.

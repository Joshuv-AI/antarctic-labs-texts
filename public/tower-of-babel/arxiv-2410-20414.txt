Skew-Hom-Lie algebras in Semi-Euclidean spaces
Zhen Xiong

Abstract
In this paper, first we introduce the notion of a skew-Hom-Lie algebra and give some examples. Then we study their representations and give the coboundary operator of skew-Hom-Lie algebras. As an application, there have a skew-Hom-Lie algebra ( R 2 4 , [ ⋅ , ⋅ ] θ , P ) (R^{4}_{2},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta},P) in semi-Euclidean spaces. For the null space of Semi-Euclidean spaces, there have a subset V ∗ V^{*} of the null space, and V ∗ V^{*} is invariant under the actions of [ ⋅ , ⋅ ] θ [\cdot,\cdot]_{\theta} and P P .

Skew-Hom-Lie algebras and their representations

The notion of a Hom-Lie algebra was introduced in
[
1
]
, see also
[
21
,

]

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],\gamma)
consisting of a
vector space
\mathfrak{a}
, a skew symmetric bilinear map (bracket)
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{a}
and a linear transformation
\gamma:\mathfrak{a}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{a}
satisfying
\gamma[x,y]=[\gamma(x),\gamma(y)]
, and the following Hom-Jacobi
identity:

[[y,z],\gamma(x)]+[[z,x],\gamma(y)]+[[x,y],\gamma(z)]=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

A Hom-Lie algebra is called a regular Hom-Lie algebra if
\gamma
is
a linear automorphism.

(2)

A subspace
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathfrak{a}
is a Hom-Lie sub-algebra of
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],\gamma)
if

\gamma(\mathfrak{b})\subset\mathfrak{b}
and

\mathfrak{b}
is closed under the bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]
, i.e. for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{b}
,

[x,y]\in\mathfrak{b}.

(3)

A morphism from the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{a}},\gamma)

to the Hom-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{b},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{b}},\delta)

is a linear map

\psi:\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{b}
such that

\psi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{a}})=[\psi(x),\psi(y)]_{\mathfrak{b}}
and

\psi\circ\gamma=\delta\circ\psi
.

When
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],\gamma)
is a Hom-Lie algebra, then
\gamma^{k}([x,y])=[\gamma^{k}(x),\gamma^{k}(y)]
,
k=1,2,3,\cdots
. Now, we give the notion of skew-Hom-Lie algebras.

Definition 2.2
.

(1

A skew-Hom-Lie algebra is a triple
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
consisting of a
vector space
\mathfrak{g}
, a skew symmetric bilinear map (bracket)
[\cdot,\cdot]:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
and a linear transformation
\beta:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfying
\beta([x,y])=-[\beta(x),\beta(y)]
, and the following Hom-Jacobi
identity:

[[y,z],\beta(x)]+[[z,x],\beta(y)]+[[x,y],\beta(z)]=0,\quad\forall x,y,z\in V.

(1)

A skew-Hom-Lie algebra is called a regular skew-Hom-Lie algebra if
\beta
is
a linear automorphism.

(2

A subspace
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is a skew-Hom-Lie sub-algebra of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
if

\beta(\mathfrak{h})\subset\mathfrak{h}
and

\mathfrak{h}
is closed under the bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]
, i.e. for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}
,

[x,y]\in\mathfrak{h}.

(3

A morphism from the skew-Hom-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta)
to the skew-Hom-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}},\lambda)
is a linear map

f:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{h}
such that

f([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=-[f(x),f(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}
and

f\circ\beta=\lambda\circ f
.

Remark 2.3
.

1)

Let
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],\gamma)
be a Hom-Lie algebra,when
\gamma
is an identity,then
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],\gamma)
is a Lie algebra; but when

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
is a skew-Hom-Lie algebra, map
\beta
can not be an identity; so a Lie algebra is a Hom-Lie algebra, but it is not a skew-Hom-Lie algebra;

2)

(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],\gamma)
is a Hom-Lie algebra,then
(\mathfrak{a},[\cdot,\cdot],(-1)\gamma)
is a skew-Hom-Lie algebra;

3)

when
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
is a skew-Hom-Lie algebra, we have:
\beta^{2k}([x,y])=[\beta^{2k}(x),\beta^{2k}(y)]
, and
\beta^{2k-1}([x,y])=-[\beta^{2k-1}(x),\beta^{2k-1}(y)]
,
k=1,2,3,\cdots.

Example 2.4
.

Let
R^{3}
be a 3-dimensional vector space. For
x,y\in R^{3}
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})^{T},,y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})^{T}
,we define a skew symmetric bilinear map (bracket)

[x,y]=x\wedge y=\begin{vmatrix}e_{1}&e_{2}&e_{3}\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
y_{1}&y_{2}&y_{3}\end{vmatrix},

where
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
is the canonical basis of
R^{3}
. We also have the scalar product
<x,y>_{1}=x^{T}y
. Let
A
be a
3\times 3
-matrix,
A
act on
R^{3}
by
A(x)=Ax
, then,
A
is a linear map. When
AA^{T}=\rm{id}
, for
x,y,z\in R^{3}
, we have:

<A([x,y]),z>_{1}=<x\wedge y,A^{T}z>_{1}=\begin{vmatrix}x^{T}\\
y^{T}\\
(A^{T}z)^{T}\end{vmatrix}

On the other hands, we have:

<[Ax,Ay],z>_{1}=<Ax\wedge Ay,z>_{1}=\begin{vmatrix}(Ax)^{T}\\
(Ay)^{T}\\
z^{T}\end{vmatrix}=|A|\begin{vmatrix}x^{T}\\
y^{T}\\
(A^{T}z)^{T}\end{vmatrix}=|A|<A([x,y]),z>_{1}.

So, there have:

|A|=1,\quad A([x,y])=[A(x),A(y)];

|A|=-1,\quad A([x,y])=-[A(x),A(y)].

Let

[x,y]_{1}=A([x,y]),

we have:

(1)

when
|A|=1
,
(R^{3},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},A)
is a Hom-Lie algebra;

(2)

when
|A|=-1
,
(R^{3},[\cdot,\cdot]_{1},A)
is a skew-Hom-Lie algebra.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
and
\alpha^{2}=-\rm{id}
, we define a linear map

Ad_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)

by
Ad_{\alpha}(B)=\alpha B\alpha
, and
a bilinear map (bracket)

[A,B]_{\alpha}=\alpha A\alpha B\alpha-\alpha B\alpha A\alpha,

then
(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},Ad_{\alpha})
is a skew-Hom-Lie algebra.

Proof.
Obviously,
[\cdot,\cdot]
is a skew bilinear map. More, we have:

\displaystyle Ad_{\alpha}([A,B]_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}A\alpha B\alpha^{2}-\alpha^{2}B\alpha A\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\alpha B-B\alpha A.

On the other hands,

\displaystyle[Ad_{\alpha}(A),Ad_{\alpha}(B)]_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}A\alpha^{3}B\alpha^{2}-\alpha^{2}B\alpha^{3}A\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A\alpha B+B\alpha A.

So,
Ad_{\alpha}([A,B]_{\alpha})=-[Ad_{\alpha}(A),Ad_{\alpha}(B)]_{\alpha}
.

For all
A,B,C\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
, we have

\displaystyle[[A,B]_{\alpha},\mathrm{Ad}_{\alpha}(C)]_{\alpha}+\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha A\alpha B\alpha,\alpha C\alpha]_{\alpha}-[\alpha B\alpha A\alpha,\alpha C\alpha]_{\alpha}+\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-A\alpha B\alpha C+C\alpha A\alpha B+B\alpha A\alpha C-C\alpha B\alpha A+\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

where
\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}
denotes summation over the cyclic permutation on
A,B,C
.
Thus,
(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},Ad_{\alpha})
is a skew-Hom-Lie
algebra.

Remark 2.6
.

(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},Ad_{\alpha}^{2})
is not a Hom-Lie algebra. By straightforward computations,

\displaystyle[[A,B]_{\alpha},\mathrm{Ad}_{\alpha}^{2}(C)]_{\alpha}+\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha A\alpha B\alpha,C]_{\alpha}-[\alpha B\alpha A\alpha,C]_{\alpha}+\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A\alpha BC\alpha-\alpha CA\alpha B-B\alpha AC\alpha+\alpha CB\alpha A+\circlearrowleft_{A,B,C}

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle 0.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
be a skew-Hom-Lie algebra,
V
is a
vector space,
\alpha^{2}=-\rm{id}
.
Then,
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)

on
V
with respect to
\alpha
if and only if

\rho:(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\alpha},Ad_{\alpha})

is a morphism of skew-Hom-Lie algebras.

Proof.
If
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
is a representation of

(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
on
V
with respect to
\alpha
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(\beta(x))\circ\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\circ\rho(x),

(4)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])\circ\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(\beta(x))\rho(y)-\rho(\beta(y))\rho(x).

(5)

By (
4
), we deduce that

\rho\circ\beta=Ad_{\alpha}\circ\rho.

Furthermore, by (
4
) and (
5
), we have

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\rho(\beta(x))\circ\rho(y)\alpha+\rho(\beta(y))\circ\rho(x)\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\alpha\rho(x)\alpha\rho(y)\alpha+\alpha\rho(y)\alpha\rho(x)\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\rho(x),\rho(y)]_{\alpha}.

Thus,
\rho

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
be a skew-Hom-Lie algebra, the pseudo-adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
, which is defined by
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}y=-[x,y]

Remark 2.10
.

Because of

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{[x,y]}\circ\beta(z)

\displaystyle-[[x,y],\beta(z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\beta(x),-[y,z]]+[\beta(y),[x,z]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\beta(x)}\circ\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{y}(z)+\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\beta(y)}\circ\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}(z).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
be a skew-Hom-Lie algebra and
\beta^{2}=-\rm{id}
. Then, the pseudo-adjoint representation
\mathrm{ad}^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
, which is defined by
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}y=-[x,y]
, is a morphism from
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot],\beta)
to
(\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\beta},Ad_{\beta})
.

Proof.
For
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have:
Ad_{\beta}\circ\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}(y)=-[\beta(x),y]=\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{\beta(x)}(y)
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{[x,y]}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[[x,y],\beta^{2}(z)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[[y,\beta(z)],\beta(x)]-[[\beta(z),x],\beta(y)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\beta(x),[y,\beta(z)]]-[\beta(y),[x,\beta(z)]].

By straightforward computations,

\displaystyle\beta\circ\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}\circ\beta\circ\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{y}\circ\beta(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\circ\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x}\circ\beta(-[y,\beta(z)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(-[x,[\beta(y),-z]])=[\beta(x),\beta([\beta(y),-z])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\beta(x),[y,\beta(z)]].

So, we have:
\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{[x,y]}=-[\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{x},\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{y}]_{\beta}
. The proof is completed.

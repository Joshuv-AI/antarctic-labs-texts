Koszul resolution for linear monoidal functors
Serge Bouc, Nadia Romero

Abstract
We introduce regular sequences and associated Koszul resolutions for monoids in the category of functors over an essentially small linear symmetric monoidal category. Next we define polynomials over such monoids. We compute the Hochschild cohomology functors and prove a relative analogue of Hilbert’s syzygy theorem for polynomials over tensor idempotent commutative monoids . Keywords: monoidal category, functor category, Koszul resolution, Hochschild cohomology, Hilbert syzygy. AMS MSC (2020): 16Y99, 18D99, 18G10, 18M05, 20J15.



1
Introduction

Hochschild cohomology is a classical tool to study rings and bimodules over them. It can be generalized to other contexts in various ways: e.g. in
[
8
]
, the second author introduces Hochschild cohomology for functors over linear symmetric monoidal categories. It is then a natural question to try to generalize to this framework some other notions. In the classical framework of rings, the construction of
Koszul resolutions
plays a fundamental rôle. Such resolutions can be used in particular to give a proof of the Hilbert syzygy theorem for polynomial rings over a field (see Application 4.5.6 in
[
9
]
).

In this work, we introduce regular sequences (Definition
3.5
) and associated Koszul resolutions for monoids in the category
\mathcal{F}
of functors over an essentially small linear symmetric monoidal category. In Section
3
, given a monoid
A
in
\mathcal{F}
, we define its associated Koszul complex and prove first that it is indeed a complex, and then that it is a resolution for a particular
A
-module, as in the classical case. The definition of the complex as well as the proof of the fact that it is a resolution are completely combinatorial in nature, thus avoiding the use of the tensor product of complexes.

In Section
4
, we define polynomials over the monoids in
\mathcal{F}
. In the classical framework of rings, the results we are interested in are proved for polynomial rings over fields so, in our context, we replace the fields by
tensor idempotent commutative monoids
(see Definition
4.5
). With this, we compute the Hochschild cohomology functors and prove a relative analogue of Hilbert’s syzygy theorem for polynomials over tensor idempotent commutative monoids in
\mathcal{F}
.

An important (and motivating) example where our results apply is the context of Green biset functors. In this case, the Burnside functor is commutative and tensor idempotent, so our results can be applied to polynomials over it.

Let

The Koszul complex

In what follows
A
is a monoid in
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 3.1
.

For an element
a\in A(\mathbf{1})
, we define
L_{a}:A\rightarrow A
as

L_{a,x}:A(x)\rightarrow A(x)\quad b\mapsto A(\lambda_{x})(a\times b),

for

\mathcal{X}
. For convenience, we will often denote

This defines a natural transformation in

, then

is clearly a morphism of
A
-modules.

Notation 3.2
.

Given a natural number

, we denote by
A^{n}
the coproduct
\oplus A
of
A
with itself
n

, we will write

A^{\binom{n}{p}}=\bigoplus_{S\subseteq T\,|S|=p}A,

where

. When necessary, we will write
A_{S}
for the summand of

corresponding to the set

.

To define the Koszul complex, we fix a natural number

and consider a family of elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
in

. We fix these elements and abbreviate them as
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})

We begin by defining, for

, the arrow

d:\bigoplus_{S\subseteq T\,|S|=p}A\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{S\subseteq T\,|S|=p-1}A.

Given
S=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}\subseteq T
, the arrow
d_{S}:A_{S}\rightarrow K_{p-1}^{n}(A)
is given by

d_{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k+1}(\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{k}}},

where the subscript
\hat{i_{k}}
indicates that
\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,
has as codomain
A_{S\setminus\{i_{k}\}}
. In the case

, the arrow
d:A^{n}\rightarrow A
is equal to
\oplus_{i=1}^{n}d_{\{i\}}
and each
d_{\{i\}}
is just
L_{\alpha_{i}}:A\rightarrow A
, so we denote

as
L_{\alpha}
.

.

For

and

as the sequence in

\textstyle{K_{n}^{n}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d}
\textstyle{\ldots\,K_{p}^{n}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{d}

\textstyle{\ldots\,A^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Even though some of the properties of

Lemma 3.4
.

With
A
and
\alpha
as before,
K_{A}(\alpha)
is a complex of

-modules.

Proof.

Since
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
are in

, the sequence

is clearly in the category of

-modules.

Next, we prove

.

Let us fix
S=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\}
. If we start by
d_{S}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k+1}(\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{k}}}
, then in

, for each

. Now,

d_{S\setminus\{i_{k}\}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{j+1}(\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{k}},\hat{i_{j}}}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}(-1)^{j}(\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{k}},\hat{i_{j}}},

where
\hat{i_{k}},\hat{i_{j}}
indicates that
\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\,\_\,
has codomain
A_{S\setminus\{i_{k},i_{j}\}}
. Hence, the composition
d\circ d_{S}
can be written as

\sum_{k=1}^{p}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(-1)^{k+j}((\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\alpha_{i_{k}})\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{j}},\hat{i_{k}}}+\sum_{j=k+1}^{p}(-1)^{k+j+1}((\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\alpha_{i_{k}})\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{j}},\hat{i_{k}}}\right),

since clearly
(\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{j}},\hat{i_{k}}}\circ(\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{k}}}
is equal to
((\alpha_{i_{j}}\times\alpha_{i_{k}})\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{j}},\hat{i_{k}}}
.

Finally, let

and

be in
\{1,\ldots,p\}
and, without loss of generality, suppose
e<f
. Then, in the sum above, for
k=e
and
j=f
, we find
(-1)^{e+f+1}((\alpha_{i_{f}}\times\alpha_{i_{e}})\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{f}},\hat{i_{e}}}
, and for

and

, we find
(-1)^{f+e}((\alpha_{i_{e}}\times\alpha_{i_{f}})\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{e}},\hat{i_{f}}}

((\alpha_{i_{e}}\times\alpha_{i_{f}})\times\,\_\,)_{\hat{i_{e}},\hat{i_{f}}}
in the sum above, and all the summands are of this form, hence
d\circ d_{S}=0
and
d\circ d=0
.
∎

4
Functors of polynomials

General properties

We begin by recalling the following standard definition and notation.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
be variables. Given a monomial
t_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots t_{n}^{i_{n}}
, where the exponents

are nonnegative integers, the vector
i=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})
of exponents is called a

of size
n
. We write the monomial symbolically as

t^{i}=t_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots t_{n}^{i_{n}}.

For the multi-index
(0,\ldots,0)
, we fix
t^{0}=t_{1}^{0}\ldots t_{n}^{0}=1
.

Given an
R
-module

, we can consider the
R
-module

. With the previous definition, a polynomial

in

where

runs through all the multi-indices
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{n})

are in

and only finitely many of theme are different from zero. Finally,

contains

as the submodule of constant polynomials, that is, polynomials with all coefficients equal to zero except, possibly, the one corresponding to the multi-index
(0,\ldots,0)
.

We keep the variable

whether we have one or several variables. That is, if we have only one variable, we continue to write

, instead of

.

Given an object

in

, we define

, or simply by

for

.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
be variables and

be an object in

. Then

is an object in
\mathcal{F}

The first assertion is clear, since

\mathcal{H}H^{p}(A_{n},\,M)=0
.

Again using the Koszul resolution, in the Hochschild cochain complex of
A_{n}
, for
1\leq p\leq n
, we have the morphism

\Phi:\mathcal{H}_{C}(K_{p}^{n}(C),\,A_{n})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{C}(K_{p+1}^{n}(C),\,A_{n}),

which, in an object
x
of
\mathcal{X}
, sends an arrow
t\in\mathrm{Hom}_{C\hbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}}(K_{p}^{n}(C),\,(A_{n})_{x})
to
t\circ d\in\mathrm{Hom}_{C\hbox{-}\mathrm{Mod}}(K_{p+1}^{n}(C),\,(A_{n})_{x})
. Now, from Section 3, we know that
d
is the sum of some
L_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}:C\rightarrow C
, with
L_{\alpha_{i_{k}}}=\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,
. Hence
t\circ d
will be the sum of some
t(\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,)
. Since
t
is an arrow of
C
-modules, we have
t(\alpha_{i_{k}}\times\,\_\,)=\alpha_{i_{k}}\times t(\,\_\,)
. But, by the previous proposition and Lemma
2.8
, this equal to 0. Hence
t\circ d=0
and, since this holds for any
x
, we have
\Phi=0
. This means that, for
1\leq p\leq n
, we have
\mathcal{H}H^{p}(A_{n},\,A_{n})=\mathcal{H}_{C}(K_{p}^{n}(C),A_{n})
.

Now,
K_{p}^{n}(C)=\mathop{\bigoplus}_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq T\\
|S|=p\end{subarray}}\limits C
, with
T=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, so, by Section 3 of
[
8
]
, we have

\mathcal{H}_{C}\Bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq T\\
|S|=p\end{subarray}}C,\,A_{n}\Bigg)\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq T\\
|S|=p\end{subarray}}\mathcal{H}_{C}(C,\,A_{n})\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq T\\
|S|=p\end{subarray}}A_{n}=K_{p}^{n}(A_{n}).

Finally, by Section 6 of
[
8
]
, we know that
\mathcal{H}H^{0}(A_{n},\,A_{n})=A_{n}=K_{0}^{n}(A_{n})
.
∎

In the next proposition we make use of some results concerning the tensor product over a monoid in
\mathcal{F}
, which can be found in the Appendix.

Proposition 4.9
.

(Relative Hilbert syzygy Theorem) Let
A
be a tensor idempotent commutative monoid in
\mathcal{F}
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
be variables. Then, any
A[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-module
M
admits a finite
\mathcal{F}
-split resolution

0\to L_{n}\to L_{n-1}\to\ldots\to L_{1}\to M\to 0

by
\mathcal{F}
-projective
A[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]
-modules.

Proof.

We continue to denote
A_{2n}
by
C
and apply the functor
{-}\otimes_{A_{n}}M
to the resolution
K_{*}:=K_{C}(\alpha)_{\mu}
of
A_{n}
of Theorem
4.8
. Since
A_{n}\otimes_{A_{n}}M\cong M
as
A_{n}
-modules, we get a complex

0\to K_{n}^{n}(C)\otimes_{A_{n}}M\to\ldots\to K_{1}^{n}(C)\otimes_{A_{n}}M\to C\otimes_{A_{n}}M\to M\to 0

of
A_{n}
-modules.

Consider the restriction from
(A_{n},A_{n})
-bimodules to right
A_{n}
-modules. We continue to denote this functor by
\mathcal{R}^{C}_{A_{n}}
, but taking into account that the
A_{n}
-modules are viewed as right modules. By the proof of the previous theorem, we know that
\mathcal{R}^{C}_{A_{n}}(K_{*})
is a split exact complex. Hence it is contractible and, since
\,\_\,\otimes_{A_{n}}M
is an additive functor, the complex
(\mathcal{R}^{C}_{A_{n}}(K_{*}))\otimes_{A_{n}}M
is contractible too. Now, by Lemma
5.2
of the Appendix, this complex is isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}^{A_{n}}_{I}(K_{*}\otimes_{A_{n}}M)=\mathcal{R}_{A_{n}}(K_{*}\otimes_{A_{n}}M)
. Hence,
\mathcal{R}_{A_{n}}(K_{*}\otimes_{A_{n}}M)
is a split exact complex in
\mathcal{F}
. Finally, since
\mathcal{R}_{A_{n}}
is faithful and exact, it follows that the complex
(K_{*}\otimes_{A_{n}}M)
is exact in
A_{n}\hbox{-Mod}
. Hence it is an
\mathcal{F}
-split resolution of
M
.

Now, each
K_{i}^{n}(C)
, for
i>0
, is a direct sum of copies of the
(C,C)
-bimodule
C
. Also,
C\cong A_{n}\otimes A_{n}=A_{n}\otimes_{I}A_{n}
(we notice in the Appendix that the tensor product over
I
is the usual tensor product of
\mathcal{F}
), so

C\otimes_{A_{n}}M\cong(A_{n}\otimes_{I}A_{n})\otimes_{A_{n}}M\cong A_{n}\otimes_{I}(A_{n}\otimes_{A_{n}}M)\cong A_{n}\otimes_{I}M.

This shows in particular that
C\otimes_{A_{n}}M
is
\mathcal{F}
-projective, by Lemma 4.3 of
[
1
]
, since
A_{n}\otimes_{I}M
is in the image of the left adjoint to the restriction functor from
A_{n}
-modules to
\mathcal{F}
. Moreover,
K_{i}^{n}(C)\otimes_{A_{n}}M\cong K_{i}^{n}(A_{n})\otimes_{I}M
, so all the terms
K_{i}^{n}(C)\otimes_{A_{n}}M
, for
i>0
, are also
\mathcal{F}
-projective. Hence, the complex
K_{*}\otimes_{A_{n}}M
, isomorphic to

0\to K_{n}^{n}(A_{n})\otimes_{I}M\to\ldots\to K_{1}^{n}(A_{n})\otimes_{I}M\to A_{n}\otimes_{I}M\to M\to 0,

is an
\mathcal{F}
-split resolution of
M
by
\mathcal{F}
-projective
A_{n}
-modules. This completes the proof.
∎

Let
A

,

\sigma_{r}:M\otimes A\rightarrow M

A

tensor product over
A
of
M
and
N

, is the coequalizer of the arrows given by the actions (see Section VII of

\textstyle{M\otimes A\otimes N\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{M\otimes\sigma_{l}}

which exists since

Notice that, for

and

I
,

Remark 5.1
.

Let
A
,
C
and
D
be monoids in
\mathcal{F}
, let
M
be a
(C,\,A)
-bimodule and
N
be an
(A,\,D)
-bimodule. The following facts are easily deduced from the definition.

1.

If
Q
is a
D
-module, then

(M\otimes_{A}N)\otimes_{D}Q\cong M\otimes_{A}(N\otimes_{D}Q)

and the isomorphism is natural in the three variables.

2.

M\otimes_{A}N
is a
(C,\,D)
-bimodule.

3.

We have
M\otimes_{A}A\cong M
and
A\otimes_{A}N\cong N
in
\mathcal{F}
and these are natural isomorphisms of
C
-modules and right
D
-modules, respectively.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A
,
D
and
E
be monoids in
\mathcal{F}
,
M
be a
(D,\,A)
-bimodule and
N
be an
(A,\,E)
-bimodule. Let
\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}
denote the restriction functor
\mathcal{R}^{D\otimes A^{op}}_{I\otimes A^{op}}
from
(D,A)
-bimodules to right
A
-modules and
\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}
be defined in a similar way. Then, there is an isomorphism

\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes_{A}N)\cong(\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}M)\otimes_{A}N

of right
E
-modules, which is functorial in
M
and
N
.

Proof.

Indeed, by construction, we have an exact sequence

M\otimes A\otimes N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\to}}M\otimes N\to M\otimes_{A}N\to 0

of
(D,E)
-bimodules, where
\delta=\sigma_{r}\otimes N-M\otimes\sigma_{l}
. Applying the functor
\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}
to this sequence gives the exact sequence

\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes A\otimes N)\to\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes N)\to\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes_{A}N)\to 0.

Moreover, there are canonical isomorphisms of right
E
-modules

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes A\otimes N)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}M)\otimes A\otimes N,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes N)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}M)\otimes N,

so we get the exact sequence

(\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}M)\otimes A\otimes N\to(\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}M)\otimes N\to\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes_{A}N)\to 0.

Thus
\mathcal{R}^{D,\,E}_{E}(M\otimes_{A}N)\cong(\mathcal{R}^{D,\,A}_{A}M)\otimes_{A}N
, as claimed.

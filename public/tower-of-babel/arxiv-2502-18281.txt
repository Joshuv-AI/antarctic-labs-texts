The Maximum T c T_{\mathrm{c}} of Conventional Superconductors at Ambient Pressure
Kun Gao, Tiago F. T. Cerqueira, Antonio Sanna, Yue-Wen Fang, Đorđe Dangić, Ion Errea, Hai-Chen Wang, Silvana Botti, Miguel A. L. Marques

Abstract
The theoretical maximum critical temperature ( T c T_{\textrm{c}} ) for conventional superconductors at ambient pressure remains a fundamental question in condensed matter physics. Through analysis of electron-phonon calculations for over 20 000 metals, we critically examine this question. We find that while hydride metals can exhibit maximum phonon frequencies of more than 5000 K, the crucial logarithmic average frequency ω log \omega_{\text{log}} rarely exceeds 1800 K. Our data reveals an inherent trade-off between ω log \omega_{\text{log}} and the electron-phonon coupling constant λ \lambda , suggesting that the optimal Eliashberg function that maximizes T c T_{\textrm{c}} is unphysical. Based on our calculations, we identify Li 2 ​ AgH 6 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AgH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} and its sibling Li 2 ​ AuH 6 \text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AuH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}} as theoretical materials that likely approach the practical limit for conventional superconductivity at ambient pressure. Analysis of thermodynamic stability indicates that compounds with higher predicted T c T_{\textrm{c}} values are increasingly unstable, making their synthesis challenging. While fundamental physical laws do not strictly limit T c T_{\textrm{c}} to low-temperatures, our analysis suggests that achieving room-temperature conventional superconductivity at ambient pressure is extremely unlikely.



Since the groundbreaking discovery of superconductivity in mercury at 4.2 K in 1911
Onnes 1911
, superconductors have revolutionized both fundamental physics and technological applications. These materials have enabled transformative technologies including magnetic resonance imaging machines, magnetic levitation trains, and ultra-sensitive quantum devices such as SQUIDs. The quest for high-temperature superconductivity remains one of the grand challenges of solid-state physics, as raising the critical temperature would unlock unprecedented possibilities in power transmission, quantum computing, and particle acceleration.

Superconducting materials are traditionally divided into conventional and unconventional. In the former, the topic of the current work, the mechanism responsible for superconductivity is the electron-phonon interaction, electrons form Cooper pairs in a singlet state, and the energy gap has s-wave symmetry. These compounds are well understood within the Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer (BCS) theory
Bardeen et al. 1957
, or its strong coupling generalization, Eliashberg theory
Eliashberg 1960
.

In the 1960s and 1970s, it was believed that the maximum
T_{\textrm{c}}
for conventional superconductors would likely be in the range of 30–40 K. This was based on the observed properties of known superconducting materials and the theoretical limits imposed by the electron-phonon interaction. The argument was that to achieve a higher
T_{\textrm{c}}
would require an unrealistically strong electron-phonon coupling or an exceptionally high density of states, both of which seemed improbable given the materials known at the time. These beliefs were supported by experimental data. For example, elemental superconductors like Pb and Nb have
T_{\textrm{c}}=7.2
K and
T_{\textrm{c}}=9.25
K, respectively
Buzea and Robbie 2004
, while more complex compounds like
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Sn}
or
\text{Nb}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{Ge}
reach
T_{\textrm{c}}=17.9
K and
T_{\textrm{c}}=21.8
K respectively
Stewart 2015
.

Currently, the compound with record conventional superconductivity at room pressure is
\text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, with a transition temperature of
T_{\textrm{c}}=39
K
Nagamatsu et al. 2001

Liu et al. 2001

Binnig et al. 1980

\text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
stands out as the first system where this phenomenon is so prominently and distinctly manifested

A key question in this field is what is the upper limit to the critical temperature of superconductors
Gor’kov and Kresin 2018
;
Boeri and Bachelet 2019
;
Boeri et al. 2021
. Based on the observation that the coupling constant depends mainly on the phonon frequencies, McMillan
in 1968
McMillan 1968
already derived an approximate expression of
T_{\textrm{c}}^{\text{max}}
. His result, however, only holds for a
given class of materials
and therefore does not provide an absolute value for this quantity. Further insight can be obtained by maximizing
T_{\textrm{c}}
given by McMillan’s formula

T_{\textrm{c}}^{\text{McMillan}}=\frac{\omega_{\text{log}}}{1.20}\exp\left(-1.04\frac{1+\lambda}{\lambda-\mu^{*}(1+0.62\lambda)}\right)\,,

(1)

where
\mu^{*}
is the Coulomb pseudopotential,
\lambda
is the electron-phonon coupling constant

\lambda=2\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\alpha^{2}F(\omega)}{\omega}d\omega

(2)

and
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
is the Eliashberg spectral function, calculated from the electron-phonon coupling matrix elements.
With
\omega_{\text{log}}
we indicate the logarithmic average of the phonon frequencies:

\omega_{\text{log}}=\text{exp}\left[\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\ln(\omega)\frac{\alpha^{2}F(\omega)}{\omega}d\omega\right]\,.

(3)

By setting, for simplicity, the Coulomb pseudopotential
\mu^{*}
to zero, one obtains that the maximum is attained at
\lambda\approx 2
. However, McMillan’s formula is only valid for values of
\lambda\lesssim 1.5
, while no maximum value for
T_{\textrm{c}}
exists in the original Eliashberg theory
Gor’kov and Kresin 2018
. It is also frequently argued that the value of
\lambda
is limited, as the lattice becomes eventually unstable for very large values of the coupling constant. However, there are some experimentally known superconductors with very high values of
\lambda
, well above 2.0, both at ambient (such as Pb–Bi compounds
Chen et al. 1971
) and under pressure
Tanaka et al. 2017
.

Recent works estimated the maximum
T_{\textrm{c}}
of conventional superconductivity from fundamental limits
Semenok et al. 2024
;
Sadovskii 2024
;
Trachenko et al. 2024
. They all agree on a value of 300–600 K at ambient pressure, suggesting that superconductors may exist at ambient temperature. In the following, we critically look at the basic assumptions used in these estimates. This is done by analyzing experimentally-known superconductors and our calculations of the electron-phonon interaction and superconducting properties for more than 20 000 metals
Cerqueira et al. 2024a
;
Cerqueira et al. 2024b
. This is by far the largest dataset available to date with calculated superconducting properties, and it includes metals with a large variety of chemical elements and crystal structures. Although it is not comprehensive, it is rather extensive, giving us a very good overview of conventional superconductivity in materials space.

The first step is to determine what is the largest phonon frequency
\omega_{\text{max}}
that one can reasonably expect in a realistic compound. Reference
Trachenko et al. 2024
derived the expression

\omega_{\text{max}}=\frac{E^{\text{electronic}}}{\sqrt{m}},

(4)

where
E^{\text{electronic}}
is a typical electronic energy, and
m
is an atomic mass. By inserting a typical
E^{\text{electronic}}=1
Ry, and the proton mass
m=m^{\text{proton}}
into the formula, they obtained
\omega_{\text{max}}=3680
K. Compared to the vibrational frequency of
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
that is almost 6000 K
Huber and Herzberg 1979
, this value does not appear to be an overestimation.

The same conclusion can be reached by looking at the calculated values for our materials depicted in

\omega_{\text{max}}
exhibits two maxima, one at around 500 K and another around 2000 K. Although the overwhelming majority of the compounds have

Trachenko et al. 2024
. Not surprisingly, all of these are hydrides. An example of one such material, with a calculated
\omega_{\text{max}}=5396

\text{AgTl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
molecules inside a
\text{AgTl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
framework (see Supplementary Information, SI). The H–H distance is 0.78 Å, only slightly larger than the value of 0.76 Å in solid hydrogen calculated in the same approximation. As expected by the extreme difference of masses, the phonon band structure splits into separated manifolds, with the lowest lying states with Tl and Ag character and the highest states coming from H. The
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\lambda

In

\omega_{\text{log}}

\omega_{\text{max}}

\omega_{\text{log}}
has a single peak at very low frequency, and decays rapidly with frequency. At the end of the tail, at
\omega_{\text{log}}

\lambda

in the range of 10–30 K.

As the determinant factor for the calculation of
T_{\textrm{c}}
is
\omega_{\text{log}}
and not
\omega_{\text{max}}

\omega_{\text{max}}=3680

Trachenko et al. 2024

The next step in the estimation of the maximum value of
T_{\textrm{c}}
is the optimization of the shape of
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
, assuming a maximum phonon frequency of
\omega_{\text{max}}
. A free optimization of this function would obviously lead to

\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
is not constrained by any sum-rule. Therefore, Trachenko

fixed

according to McMillan’s formula. From the considerations above, and from the lower panel of

seems to be a reasonable value, perfectly reachable in a variety of compounds. The hypothetical compounds we have in our dataset with highest values of

\text{ClB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}

\text{Al}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{OsH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{7}}}

The optimal shape of

It is most likely, however, that this limit is unattainable for any physical system at ambient pressure. In fact, the parameters
\omega_{\text{log}}
and
\lambda
are not entirely independent, as they are two different moments of the same
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
function. This was already recognized in the seminal work of McMillan in 1968
McMillan 1968
, where it was recognized that the most important dependence of
\lambda
is om an average phonon frequency. Obviously
\omega_{\text{log}}
is favored by high frequencies, while
\lambda
by low frequencies, so the shape of the optimal
\alpha^{2}F(\omega)
that maximizes
T_{\textrm{c}}
(and that only contains a
single
, very high-frequency, flat-band phonon mode that couples very strongly to the electrons) seems to be unattainable physically.

This evidence is also supported by the data depicted in
fig.

2
, where we plot the relationship between
\omega_{\text{log}}
and
\lambda
for all compounds in our dataset. The size of the circles is proportional to
T_{\textrm{c}}
and in the background we plot the contour lines of constant
T_{\textrm{c}}
as calculated from McMillan’s formula with
\mu^{*}=0.1
. We see that, as expected, compounds with very large values of
\omega_{\text{log}}
often have small
\lambda
and vice-versa. Furthermore, no material comes close to the optimal case, and compounds with the highest values of
T_{\textrm{c}}
are the ones that achieve a good compromise between reasonable high
\omega_{\text{log}}
and
\lambda
.

As an example, we will look at the compounds with the highest

\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AgH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}

Both materials are thermodynamically unstable at ambient pressure at respectively 0.319 eV/atom and 0.172 eV/atom above the convex hull for the Ag and the Au compounds, and are not significantly stabilized by pressure (at least up to 50 GPa, see SI).

The electronic band structure of
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AgH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
resembles the one of
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, with a single band crossing the Fermi surface. This band, with very strong H-character, is however more dispersive in the present case. Also the phonon band structure is similar, with the acoustic modes mostly composed by vibrations of the heavier atom, followed by modes stemming from the alkali or alkaline earth metal. Finally, there are three separate manifolds of phonon bands. In
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AgH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
the lowest manifold contains both H and Li character due to the small difference between the atomic masses of these elements. Essentially all modes contribute to the very high value of
\lambda\approx 4
.

We would like to note that one of the acoustic phonon branches exhibits (small) imaginary values close to the
\Gamma
. This is a shortcoming of the harmonic approximation, and the structure is perfectly dynamically stable when anharmonic and quantum nuclear effects are taken into account (see SI)
Errea et al. 2014
;
Monacelli et al. 2021
. In any case, 95% of the electron-phonon coupling comes from mid-low frequency modes which are almost identical between harmonic and anharmonic calculations.

In order to obtain a more accurate
T_{\textrm{c}}
estimation within modern superconductivity methods we have recomputed the value of
T_{\textrm{c}}
using two state-of-the art approaches which, unlike more conventional methods, include the electron phonon coupling and the Coulomb interaction calculated from first-principles. These are the full Eliashberg approach of Ref.
Pellegrini et al. 2022
and density functional theory for superconductors
Pellegrini and Sanna 2024
;
Sanna et al. 2020
;
Oliveira et al. 1988
;
Lüders et al. 2005
;
Marques et al. 2005
(SCDFT). For the sake of these high-accuracy calculations the electron-phonon coupling was recomputed using a Monte-Carlo
k
-mesh accumulated on the Fermi surface, on which the electron-phonon matrix elements are linearly interpolated
Sanna et al. 2022
. This ensures a perfect convergence of the nesting properties entering the definition of the Eliashberg spectral function.

The resulting critical temperature for
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AhH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
is 108.8 K in Eliashberg theory and 83.0 K in SCDFT. Although both approaches are derived from the Migdal approximation for the electron-phonon self energy
Migdal 1958
and assume a static Coulomb interaction, the slightly different
T_{\textrm{c}}
prediction is dictated by the approximation chain that leads to each computational scheme. On one hand, in SCDFT there is the approximation to the anomalous exchange-correlation functional
Sanna et al. 2020
, which adds additional approximations to the form of the self-energy. On the other hand, in Eliashberg theory the energy dependence of the Coulomb interaction is neglected at the scale of the phononic energies. However, considering that the electron-phonon coupling parameter
\lambda
is very high, the functional approximation of SCDFT might be slightly beyond its validity range
Sanna et al. 2020
. Therefore we expect that, in this case, the Eliashberg estimation should be more accurate.

It is interesting that the Eliashberg estimation of
T_{\textrm{c}}
is in very good agreement with the one obtained by means of the McMillan-Allen-Dynes approach using a standard
\mu^{*}=0.1
. This indicates that Coulomb interactions act as expected for a conventional sp-system
Allen and Mitrović 1983
. This differs from what is observed in
\text{Mg}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{IrH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
which, as discussed in Ref.
Sanna et al. 2024
, has its
T_{\textrm{c}}
overestimated by the
\mu^{*}
model
Morel and Anderson 1962
. The reason is the presence of a peak in the density of states at the Fermi level, that is close to a large band gap, leading to a poor Coulomb renormalization
Allen and Mitrović 1983
;
Scalapino et al. 1966
. The
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AgH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
system also features a peak in the density-of-states and a band gap, however the peak is broader, while the band gap is very small, leading to an overall smoother density profile and efficient Coulomb renormalization.

A similar analysis can be extended to
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AuH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
. This system has electronic, phononic and superconducting properties nearly identical to its Ag twin. The predicted superconducting
T_{\textrm{c}}
including Coulomb interactions is 91.0 K and 116.1 K in SCDFT and Eliashberg, respectively.

In view of the discussion before, these materials seem to be ideal cases for conventional superconductivity, and their
T_{\textrm{c}}
is likely in the maximum range of what can be achievable at ambient pressure. We note that these are
isotropic superconductors, where the effect of anisotropy accounts for less than 1% of the value of
T_{\textrm{c}}
. This is the opposite of
\text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, where the electron-phonon coupling mostly acts on the
\sigma
bands, and an isotropic calculations leads to an underestimation of
T_{\textrm{c}}
by almost a factor of two. The isotropic superconducting state of
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AgH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
or
\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{AuH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}
, if experimentally realized, would not only have a critical temperature above liquid nitrogen, but it would also be more suitable for high-field applications. In fact these applications require impurities and defects to work as pinning centers for magnetic flux lines and reduce the Ginsburg-Landau coherence-length. Isotropic superconductors are more likely to be stable upon the introduction of crystalline defects and still be affected by their scattering potential.

Finally, we would like to discuss the issue of synthesizeability. Most of the compounds discussed above, and others that have been proposed in the literature, with high-
T_{\textrm{c}}
are hypothetical, and up to now none has been stabilized at ambient pressure. To better understand this problem, we plot in
fig.

4
the distance to the convex hull of thermodynamic stability and the calculated transition temperature of all compounds in our dataset. We also indicate the Pareto front that corresponds to this data. The values of
T_{\textrm{c}}
are obtained with an isotropic theory, so the transition temperature of
\text{MgB}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is considerably underestimated. We also note that although being close to the hull is not a synonym of synthesizeability, the higher the distance to the hull for a given compound, the smaller the probability that this compound can be stabilized experimentally.

Very close to the hull, we find that the compound with highest predicted
T_{\textrm{c}}
is
\text{LiMoN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with around 40 K
Cerqueira et al. 2024b
. Unfortunately, this compound exhibits intrinsic defects that lower the high density of states at the Fermi level and destroy superconductivity
Elder et al. 1992
;
Cerqueira et al. 2024b
. If it is possible to resolve this problem experimentally and synthesize the pristine compound is at this point unknown
Hunter 2008
. At higher values of
T_{\textrm{c}}
we find several hydrides and boron-carbides. Unfortunately, all these compounds are unstable thermodynamically with a distance to the hull that increases rapidly along the Pareto front. This is easy to understand as hydrides prefer charge-compensated, semiconducting (or insulating) phases and are therefore destabilized in the metallic phase, and boron induces stresses in the very strong diamond framework increasing its energy. A possible path through the high-pressure synthesis followed by quenching of these compounds to ambient pressure is commonly suggested in the literature
Hilleke and Zurek 2022
;
Lucrezi et al. 2022
;
Dangić et al. 2024
, however it is still unproved to this date, and remains highly speculative.

In summary, by analyzing data from more than 20 000 electron-phonon calculations we critically discussed the possible maximum
T_{\textrm{c}}
of conventional superconductors at ambient pressure. It seems clear that it is possible to design hypothetical compound with values of
T_{\textrm{c}}
reaching 100–120 K, much larger than the current experimental record, but still very far from room temperature. Unfortunately, all compounds with high
T_{\textrm{c}}
appear to be thermodynamically unstable, raising questions about their experimental synthesis and characterization. It is true that physical laws do not restrict
T_{\textrm{c}}
to go beyond 100–120 K, but in practice our data show that the experimental realization of a compound with such high
T_{\textrm{c}}
is extremely unlikely.

Eisenhart Lift for Scalar Fields in the FLRW Universe
Takeshi Chiba, Tsuyoshi Houri

Abstract
The Eisenhart lift of Riemannian type describes the motion of a particle as a geodesic in a higher-dimensional Riemannian manifold with one additional coordinate. It has recently been generalized to a scalar field system by introducing one additional vector field. We apply this approach to a scalar field system in the Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker universe and classify the symmetries of the system. In particular, for a scalar field potential consisting of the square of a combination of exponential functions with specific index e ± 6 4 ​ ϕ e^{\pm\frac{\sqrt{6}}{4}\phi} , we find nontrivial (conformal) Killing vector fields and Killing tensor fields. Moreover, for a potential written as an exponentiation of a combination of exponential potentials with general index, we find nontrivial conformal Killing vector fields. By introducing the coordinate along the conformal Killing vector field, we can solve the equations of motion completely. We also classify the symmetries of multiple scalar field system.

The Eisenhart lift of the Riemannian type is extended

[
4
]

We apply this approach to the system of a scalar field in the Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) universe

In our previous paper

II
Eisenhart Lift of Riemannian type

II.1
Eisenhart Lift for a Particle

Consider the equation of motion of a particle under
the influence of a potential
V(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})
in an
n
-dimensional space

\displaystyle m\ddot{x}^{i}=-\partial_{i}V\,,

(II.1)

where
i=1,\dots,n
and
\ddot{x}^{i}=d^{2}x^{i}/dt^{2}
with
t

being the time coordinate and
\partial_{i}V=\partial V/\partial x^{i}
.
Hereafter we set
m=1
for simplicity. The equation of motion
is derived from the action

\displaystyle S=\int dt\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}-V(x^{1},\dots,x^{n})\right)\,.

(II.2)

Eisenhart showed that the same dynamics can be derived from the following action by adding a new coordinate
y

[
1
]

\displaystyle I_{R}=\int dt\left(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}+\frac{1}{4V}\dot{y}^{2}\right)\,,

(II.3)

where
V\neq 0
is assumed. It is called an Eisenhart lift of the Riemannian type since the metric signature is positive (if
V>0
).
The equation of
x^{i}
and
y
are

\displaystyle\ddot{x}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4V^{2}}\dot{y}^{2}\partial_{i}V\,,

(II.4)

\displaystyle p_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{y}}{2V}={\rm const}\,.

(II.5)

So, plugging Eq. (
II.5
) into Eq. (
II.4
) and
setting
p_{y}=1
reproduces Eq. (
II.1
). Changing

p_{y}
merely corresponds to the rescaling of the time coordinate.

Introducing
n+1
dimensional coordinate
x^{A}=(x^{i},y)
,
the action Eq.(
II.3
) may be viewed as that of a free particle in

n+1
-dimensional space:

\displaystyle I_{R}=\int dt~\frac{1}{2}G_{AB}\dot{x}^{A}\dot{x}^{B}\,,

(II.6)

where
G_{AB}
is the field space metric with

G_{ij}=\delta_{ij},G_{iy}=0
and
G_{yy}=\frac{1}{2V}
.
Viewed in this way, the motion of a particle can be regarded as
the geodesics on a higher dimensional Riemannian manifold with
the metric
G_{AB}

[
1
]
.
This method is called Eisenhart lift. The motion of a particle under
central force and the existence of a Killing tensor for a Newtonian
potential or harmonic potential are reviewed in Appendix
A
.

It is interesting to note that for
n=1
the Ricci scalar
R(G)

constructed from
G_{AB}
is
given by the Schwarzian derivative of
W(x)=\int dxV(x)
(“pre-potential” of
V(\phi)
),
S(W)
,

\displaystyle R(G)=\frac{V^{\prime\prime}}{V}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{V}\right)^{2}=\frac{W^{\prime\prime\prime}}{W^{\prime}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{W^{\prime\prime}}{W^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\equiv S(W)\,.

(II.7)

Thus, the Schwarzian derivative is curvature
[
9
]
.
The Ricci scalar
R(G)
for general
n
becomes

R(G)=\frac{D^{2}V}{V}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{(DV)^{2}}{V^{2}}
,
where
D
is the covariant derivative with respect to
G_{AB}
.

II.2
Eisenhart Lift for Scalar Fields

The Eisenhart lift of the Riemannian type is extended to scalar field theories by
[
4
]
.
Consider the system of
n
scalar fields in a four-dimensional spacetime
(with the metric

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}k_{IJ}({\boldsymbol{\phi}})\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{J}-V({\boldsymbol{\phi}})\right)\,,

(II.8)

where the first term is the Einstein-Hilbert term (in units of

and

is the field space index and

is
the scalar field space metric, while

The Einstein equation and the equation of motion of

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=k_{IJ}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{J}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\left(g^{\alpha\beta}k_{IJ}\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{I}\partial_{\beta}\phi^{J}+2V\right)\,,

(II.9)

\displaystyle\Box\phi^{I}+\Gamma^{I}_{JK}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{J}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{K}-k^{IJ}\partial_{J}V=0\,,

(II.10)

where
\Gamma^{I}_{JK}
is the Christoffel symbol constructed from the
field space metric

.

Ref.
[
4
]
observed that the same dynamics can be described by the
following the Eisenhart lift through the introduction of the
fictitious vector field

\displaystyle I_{R}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}k_{IJ}({\boldsymbol{\phi}})\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{J}+\frac{1}{4V({\boldsymbol{\phi}})}\left(\nabla_{\mu}B^{\mu}\right)^{2}\right)\,.

(II.11)

The Einstein equation and the equations of motion of
\phi^{I}
and

are

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=k_{IJ}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{J}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\left(g^{\alpha\beta}k_{IJ}\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{I}\partial_{\beta}\phi^{J}\right)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+2B_{(\mu}\partial_{\nu)}\pi_{B}-g_{\mu\nu}B^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\pi_{B}-g_{\mu\nu}V\pi_{B}^{2}\,,

(II.12)

\displaystyle\Box\phi^{I}+\Gamma^{I}_{JK}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{J}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{K}-\pi_{B}^{2}k^{IJ}\partial_{J}V=0\,,

(II.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\pi_{B}=0\,,

(II.14)

where

\displaystyle\pi_{B}=\frac{\nabla_{\mu}B^{\mu}}{2V}\,.

(II.15)

From Eq. (
II.14

is a constant.
Plugging this into Eq. (
II.12

II.13

\displaystyle\frac{p_{\bar{\phi}}}{12\sin(2\beta)}\left((\widetilde{t}-\widetilde{t}_{0})-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\alpha}\cos(2\beta)F^{-1}(\widetilde{t}-\widetilde{t}_{0})\right)+\bar{\phi}_{0}\,,

(IV.59)

where

is the inverse function of the function

given by

\displaystyle F(z)=

\displaystyle\int\frac{dz}{\sqrt{1+(\epsilon\mu^{2}/p_{\bar{\phi}}^{2})z^{-2+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\alpha}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\frac{\sqrt{6}\alpha^{2}z^{3-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\alpha}}}{3(\sqrt{6}-4\alpha)\epsilon\mu^{2}}\sqrt{1+(\epsilon\mu^{2}/p_{\bar{\phi}}^{2})z^{-2+\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\alpha}}}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(1,\frac{\sqrt{6}-3\alpha}{\sqrt{6}-2\alpha},\frac{\sqrt{6}-4\alpha}{\sqrt{6}-2\alpha};-\frac{z^{2-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{\alpha}}}{\epsilon\mu^{2}/p_{\bar{\phi}}^{2}}\right)\,.

\displaystyle\Bigg\{\frac{\alpha^{2}p_{\bar{\phi}}^{2}}{12\sin(2\beta)}\left(\frac{6}{\alpha^{2}}(F^{-1}(\widetilde{t}-\widetilde{t}_{0}))^{2}-(\widetilde{t}-\widetilde{t}_{0}+\bar{t}_{0})^{2}\right)\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}\alpha}}\,,

V
Discussion

We have so far only considered time-dependent system.
Then, what about a spacetime-dependent system?
Ref.
[
4
]
suggested introducing a vierbein
e^{\mu}_{M}
satisfying

\nabla_{\mu}e^{\nu}_{M}=0
in order to expand the vector field as

\displaystyle B^{\mu}=\phi^{M}e^{\mu}_{M}\,,

(V.1)

where
\phi^{M}~(M=n+1,\dots,n+4)
are four scalar fields.
Then the Lagrangian of the scalar fields and the vector field
in Eq. (
II.11
) can be
written as purely kinetic terms of
n+4
scalar fields

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{R}=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}k_{IJ}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{I}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{J}+\frac{1}{4V}\sqrt{-g}e^{\mu}_{(M}e^{\nu}_{N)}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{M}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{N}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{A}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{B}\,,

(V.2)

where
\phi^{A}(A=1,\dots,n+4)
are
n+4
scalar fields and

H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}
is an extended metric on a mixed space of
a spacetime and a field space and is given by

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}g^{\mu\nu}k_{IJ}&0\\
0&-\frac{1}{2V}e^{\mu}_{M}e^{\nu}_{N}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(V.3)

The equation of motion of
\phi^{A}
is then written as

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}\nabla_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{B}+H^{\mu\nu}_{AD}\Gamma^{D}_{BC}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{B}\partial_{\nu}\phi^{C}=0\,,

(V.4)

where
\Gamma^{D}_{BC}
is the connection defined with respect to
H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}
via
\nabla_{A}H^{\mu\nu}_{BC}=0
and is given by

\displaystyle H^{\mu\nu}_{AD}\Gamma^{D}_{BC}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{B}H^{\mu\nu}_{CA}+\partial_{C}H^{\mu\nu}_{BA}-\partial_{A}H^{\mu\nu}_{BC}\right)\,.

(V.5)

Then, it is tempting to search for the KVss and KTs of the extended field space metric
H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}
by solving the Killing equations,
such as
\nabla_{(A}\xi_{B)}=0
or
\nabla_{(A}K_{BC)}=0
.
In order to do that, it is necessary to compute
\Gamma^{A}_{BC}

by solving Eq. (
V.5
) for
\Gamma^{D}_{BC}
.
However, Eq. (
V.5
) are overdetermined system for
\Gamma^{D}_{BC}
: Eq. (
V.5
) are ten equations for
each
\Gamma^{D}_{BC}
with ten components of
\mu,\nu
, and

\Gamma^{D}_{BC}
is not determined in general.
Therefore, the extended field space metric
H^{\mu\nu}_{AB}
may not be
helpful in studying the symmetry of the system.

VI
Summary

We have applied the Eisenhart lift of Riemannian type to
the system of a scalar field in a flat FLRW universe and studied
the condition for the existence of constants of motion so that
the system is integrable.
We have found that KV fields and a KT field exist
for a particular shape of potential
(
III.27
) extending the previous results in the literature.
Moreover, we have found that a CKV field exists
for a potential written as an exponentiation of a combination of exponential potentials with general index
(
III.35
). In particular, for a simple exponential potential
with general index which corresponds to the potential for power-law
inflation (
III.38
), we have found that the field space is
conformally flat and have obtained explicitly the maximal number of CKVs.
We have solved the equations of motion by introducing a coordinate along the (C)KV and given the complete solutions to the equations of motion.
We have also studied the condition for the existence of CKVs
for the system of multiple scalar fields.
It would be interesting to study quantum cosmology for these integrable systems
generalizing the results in
[
7
]
.

We have pointed out the problem in extending the Eisenhart lift to a
spacetime-dependent system. Also, we have attempted to extend
the Eisenhart lift of Lorentzian type to scalar field theories by
introducing two vector fields and have found the problem in finding
the symmetry of the system.

It would be interesting to extend the analysis to other homogeneous
cosmological spacetime.
It would also be interesting to classify
the integrable system with the kinetic term of Lorentzian signature.
We hope to report the results of the investigation in the future.

Appendix A
Central Force Problem

In this Appendix, as an example of the use of a lifted system,
we consider a particle moving about a fixed center of force.
Taking the center of force as the origin of the coordinate,
the potential is a function of
r=|{\boldsymbol{x}}|
,
V(r)
.

A.1
Riemannian Lift

The lifted Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle L_{R}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4V(r)}\dot{y}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}G_{AB}\dot{x}^{A}\dot{x}^{B}\,,

(A.1)

where
x^{A}=(x^{i},y)
with
i=1,\dots,3
and
G_{ij}=\delta_{ij},G_{iy}=0
and

G_{yy}=\frac{1}{2V(r)}
.
In addition to the usual three KVs associated
with the conservation of angular momentum
{{\boldsymbol{L}}}={{\boldsymbol{x}}}\times{{\boldsymbol{p}}}
,
there may exist yet another constant of motion associated with
a KT
K^{AB}
which satisfies
\nabla_{(A}K_{BC)}=0
,
where the bracket in the index denotes the symmetrization.
From the KT equations, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{i}K_{jk}+\partial_{j}K_{ki}+\partial_{k}K_{ij}=0,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}K_{jy}+\partial_{j}K_{iy}+\frac{V^{\prime}}{rV}\left(x_{i}K_{jy}+x_{j}K_{iy}\right)=0,

(A.3)

\displaystyle x^{i}K_{iy}=0,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}K_{yy}+\frac{2x_{i}V^{\prime}}{rV}K_{yy}-\frac{V^{\prime}}{2rV^{2}}K_{ij}x^{j}=0\,,

(A.5)

where
V^{\prime}=dV/dr
. The most general solution to Eqs. (
A.3
) and (
A.4
) is
K_{iy}=\frac{1}{V}\epsilon_{ijk}a^{j}x^{k}
for a constant vector
a^{i}
.
Also, the most general solution to Eq. (
A.2
)
is
K_{ij}=b_{(i}x_{j)}-({\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}})\delta_{ij}+C_{ij}
for
a constant vector
b_{i}
and a constant tensor
C_{ij}
.
Plunging these into Eq. (
A.5
), the integrability condition of

K_{yy}
gives
[
18
]

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}\times\left(\left(r\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}+3\frac{V^{\prime}}{r}\right){\boldsymbol{b}}+\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}{\boldsymbol{C}}\right)=0\,,

(A.6)

where we have introduced
C_{i}=C_{ij}x^{j}
.

Excluding the trivial case where
V
is constant,
the condition Eq. (
A.6
) is satisfied if
(i)
r\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}+3\frac{V^{\prime}}{r}=0
and
{\boldsymbol{C}}\propto{\boldsymbol{x}}
, or
(ii)
\left(\frac{V^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{\prime}=0
and
{\boldsymbol{b}}=0
.
The former case implies
V=-\frac{\mu}{r}

with
\mu
being a constant (Newtonian potential) and
C_{ij}=C\delta_{ij}
with
C
being a constant, while
the latter case implies
V=\frac{1}{2}kr^{2}
with
k
being a constant (harmonic potential).

For case (i),
V=-\frac{\mu}{r}
, the solution to Eq. (
A.5
) is

K_{yy}=D\frac{r^{2}}{4\mu^{2}}+\frac{r}{4\mu}\left(({\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}})-2C\right)
, where

D
is a constant.
Then, the corresponding constant of motion is

\displaystyle K^{AB}p_{A}p_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{p}})({\boldsymbol{x}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{p}})-({\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}}){{\boldsymbol{p}}}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{r}({\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{x}})p_{y}^{2}+2C\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\mu}{r}p_{y}^{2}\right)+4p_{y}\left({\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{L}}\right)+Dp_{y}^{2}

(A.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\boldsymbol{b}}\cdot\left({\boldsymbol{p}}\times({\boldsymbol{x}}\times{\boldsymbol{p}})-\frac{\mu}{r}{\boldsymbol{x}}\right)+2C\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{p}}^{2}}{2}-\frac{\mu}{r}\right)+4{\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{L}}+D\,.

where we have set
p_{y}=1
in the second line.
The last three terms in Eq. (
A.7
) are the Hamiltonian, the
{\boldsymbol{a}}
component of the angular momentum and a constant, respectively.
The first term in Eq. (
A.7
) is nothing but the

{\boldsymbol{b}}
component of the Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector
[
19
,
18
]

\displaystyle{\bf A}={{\boldsymbol{p}}}\times{{\boldsymbol{L}}}-\frac{\mu}{r}{\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

(A.8)

For case (ii),
V=\frac{1}{2}kr^{2}
, the solution to Eq. (
A.5
) is

K_{yy}=\frac{D}{k^{2}r^{4}}+\frac{1}{kr^{4}}C_{ij}x^{i}x^{j}
, where
D
is a constant.
The constant of motion is thus

\displaystyle K^{AB}p_{A}p_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{ij}\left(p_{i}p_{j}+kx_{i}x_{j}p_{y}^{2}\right)+4p_{y}\left({\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{L}}\right)+Dp_{y}^{2}

(A.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{ij}\left(p_{i}p_{j}+kx_{i}x_{j}\right)+4{\boldsymbol{a}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{L}}+D\,,

where we have set
p_{y}=1
in the second line.
Since
C_{ij}
is an arbitrary constant tensor, the existence
of the constant of motion implies the conservation of
the Laplace-Runge-Lenz tensor
[
19
,
18
]

\displaystyle A_{ij}=p_{i}p_{j}+kx_{i}x_{j}\,.

(A.10)

To summarize, the only nontrivial potential which admits KT
is either Newtonian potential or harmonic potential, and the corresponding
constant of motion is the Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector for the former,
the Laplace-Runge-Lenz tensor for the latter.

A.2
Coordinate Transformation

It is interesting to note that the system with Newtonian potential is related to the system with harmonic potential by a simple coordinate transformation.

Taking the plane of the motion in
xy-
plane, the Lagrangian of the Kepler problem is given by

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{2}(\dot{x}^{2}+\dot{y}^{2})+\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}}\,.

(A.11)

We introduce new coordinates
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
by the following coordinate transformation

\displaystyle x+iy=\frac{1}{2}(x^{\prime 2}-y^{\prime 2})+ix^{\prime}y^{\prime}\,.

(A.12)

Then
L
becomes

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{2}(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})(\dot{x}^{\prime 2}+\dot{y}^{\prime 2})+\frac{2\mu}{x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2}}\,,

(A.13)

and in terms of conjugate momenta
p_{x^{\prime}}=(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})\dot{x}^{\prime},p_{y^{\prime}}=(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})\dot{y}^{\prime}
the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})}(p_{x^{\prime}}^{2}+p_{y^{\prime}}^{2})-\frac{2\mu}{x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2}}\,.

(A.14)

Since we consider a bound orbit,
H
is a negative constant and we set
H=-\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}
with
\omega
being a constant.
Then, Eq. (
A.14
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(p_{x^{\prime}}^{2}+p_{y^{\prime}}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}(x^{\prime 2}+y^{\prime 2})=2\mu\,,

(A.15)

which is nothing but the Hamiltonian for the harmonic potential.

Appendix B
Integrability Conditions of CKV Equation

In this appendix, we summarize the bound on the dimension of the vector space of CKV fields and the integrability conditions of the CKV equation (CKVE).

B.1
Bound on the Dimensionality

For a vector field
\xi^{\mu}
on an
n
-dimensional spacetime
(M,g_{\mu\nu})
,
the CKVE is given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}=Qg_{\mu\nu}\,,

(B.1)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
is the covariant derivative with
the Levi-Civita connection of a metric
g_{ab}
.
From Eq. (
B.1
), it is easy to show that

\displaystyle Q=\frac{1}{n}\nabla_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}\,.

(B.2)

This function
Q
is called the associated function of
\xi^{\mu}
.
Since the CKVE is linear, the space of CKVs is a vector space. In two dimensions, this vector space of CKVs is of infinite dimensions. On the other hand, in
n\geq 3
dimensions, the vector space is of finite dimensions since the CKVE becomes an overdetermined system of first-order linear PDEs:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}Q\,,

(B.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}L_{\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-W_{\nu\rho\mu}{}^{\sigma}\xi_{\sigma}-2S_{\mu[\nu}\xi_{\rho]}-2g_{\mu[\nu}\eta_{\rho]}\,,

(B.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-S_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\xi_{\nu}+\eta_{\mu}\,,

(B.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\eta_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C_{\nu\mu}{}^{\rho}\xi_{\rho}-S^{\rho}{}_{\mu}L_{\nu\rho}-S_{\mu\nu}Q\,,

(B.6)

where

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{[\mu}\xi_{\nu]}\,,\quad\eta_{\mu}\equiv\partial_{\mu}Q+S_{\mu}{}^{\nu}\xi_{\nu}\,,

(B.7)

and
W_{\mu\nu\rho}{}^{\sigma}
,
S_{\mu\nu}
and
C_{\mu\nu\rho}

are the Weyl tensor, Schouten tensor and Cotton tensor, respectively.
Schouten tensor and Cotton tensor are defined by

\displaystyle S_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-2}\left(R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{R}{2(n-1)}g_{\mu\nu}\right)\,,

(B.8)

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}S_{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}S_{\mu\rho}\,.

(B.9)

We find from Eqs. (
B.3
)–(
B.6
) that if the values of the variables

(\xi_{\mu},L_{\mu\nu},Q,\eta_{\mu})
are given at a point on
M
,
the values of these variables are determined on the entire
M
.
Since the number of initial values to provide is given by

\displaystyle n+\frac{n(n-1)}{2}+1+n=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}\,,

(B.10)

the number of linearly independent solutions to the PDEs (i.e. CKVs) is bounded by this number.

B.2
Integrability conditions

Further bounds to the dimension of the vector space of CKVs are obtained by the integrability conditions. To obtain the integrability conditions, we regard the set of variables
{\cal Z}_{A}\equiv(\xi_{\mu},L_{\mu\nu},Q,\eta_{\mu})

as a section of the vector bundle
E=T^{*}M\oplus\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\oplus C^{\infty}(M)\oplus T^{*}M
,
and regard Eqs. (
B.3
)–(
B.6
) as the parallel section equation on
E
,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}{\cal Z}_{A}=0\,,

(B.11)

with respect to the Killing connection
D_{\mu}

defined by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}{\cal Z}_{A}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}-L_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}Q\\
\nabla_{\mu}L_{\nu\rho}+W_{\nu\rho\mu}{}^{\sigma}\xi_{\sigma}+2S_{\mu[\nu}\xi_{\rho]}+2g_{\mu[\nu}\eta_{\rho]}\\

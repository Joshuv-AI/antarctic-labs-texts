Interaction-Enabled Two- and Three-Fold Exceptional Points
Musashi Kato, Tsuneya Yoshida

Abstract
We propose a novel type of exceptional points, dubbed interaction-enabled n n -fold exceptional points [EP n n s ( n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 )] —EP n n s protected by topology that are prohibited at the non-interacting level. Specifically, we demonstrate that both bosonic and fermionic systems host such interaction-enabled EP n n s ( n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 ) in parameter space that are protected by charge U(1), pseudo-spin-parity, and P ​ T PT symmetries. The interaction-enabled EP2s are protected by zero-dimensional topology and give rise to qualitative changes in the loss rate, an experimentally measurable quantity for cold atoms. Furthermore, we reveal that interactions enable EP3s protected by one-dimensional topology beyond the point-gap topological classifications, suggesting the potential presence of a broader class of interaction-enabled non-Hermitian degeneracies.

Interaction-enabled zero-dimensional point-gap topology
.—
We consider a two-component bosonic system
in a two-dimensional parameter space
\bm{\lambda}=(x,y)
, where
x
and
y
are real parameters. The second-quantized Hamiltonian is written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}(\bm{\lambda})=\hat{H}_{0}(\bm{\lambda})+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}},

(1)

where
\hat{H}_{0}
denotes the quadratic (non-interacting)
part and

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
represents the many-body interaction.
We impose the following symmetries on
\hat{H}(\bm{\lambda})

\displaystyle[\hat{H}(\bm{\lambda}),\hat{N}]

\displaystyle=0,

(2)

\displaystyle[\hat{H}(\bm{\lambda}),(-1)^{\hat{T}_{z}}]

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}\hat{H}(\bm{\lambda})(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\hat{H}(\bm{\lambda}).

(4)

Here,
\hat{P}\hat{T}
is an antiunitary operator satisfying
(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{2}=\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}
, where
1
l
denotes the identity operator. It can be represented as the product of a unitary operator and the complex conjugation operator
\mathcal{K}
(
\mathcal{K}\mathrm{i}=-\mathrm{i}\mathcal{K}
).
The operators
\hat{N}
and
\hat{T}_{z}
denote the total particle-number operator and the pseudo-spin operator, respectively, and are defined as
\hat{N}=\hat{N}_{\mathrm{A}}+\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}

\hat{T}_{z}=(\hat{N}_{\mathrm{A}}-\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}})/2
, where
\hat{N}_{\mathrm{A}}
(
\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}
) is the particle-number operator for the
\mathrm{A}
(
\mathrm{B}
) component. We further assume that these operators satisfy the following relations with the
PT

\displaystyle[\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{N}]

\displaystyle=0,

(5)

\displaystyle\{\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{T}_{z}\}

\displaystyle=0.

(6)

Here, for arbitrary operators
\hat{A}
and
\hat{B}
, the commutation and anticommutation
relations are denoted by

[\hat{A},\hat{B}]=\hat{A}\hat{B}-\hat{B}\hat{A}
,

\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}=\hat{A}\hat{B}+\hat{B}\hat{A}
, respectively.

An analysis of the zero-dimensional point-gap topology reveals that correlations change
the allowed topological structures:

\displaystyle\ \ 0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4},

(7a)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=2\pmod{4},

(7b)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow 0\quad\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=1,3\pmod{4},

(7c)

where
N
and
\sigma
denote eigenvalues of
\hat{N}
and
(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}}=(-1)^{\hat{N}/2-\hat{T}_{z}}
, respectively.
In particular, for
N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4}
, interactions enable the nontrivial point-gap topology, which we see in detail below (for other cases, see Sec.
S1
of Supplemental Material
Sup
).

Firstly, we block-diagonalize the second-quantized Hamiltonian.
In correlated cases,
Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
)
imply that the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
commutes with both
\hat{N}
and
(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}}
, indicating that

\hat{H}
can be block-diagonalized in Fock space
into sectors labeled by their eigenvalues

\sigma

\hat{H}=\bigoplus_{N,\sigma}H_{(N,\sigma)}

In the non-interacting case, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}

satisfies an additional symmetry
[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{T}_{z}]=0
.
This property follows from the symmetry constraint
[Eq. (
3
)] and from the fact that

\hat{H}_{0}
is quadratic in the creation and annihilation operators.
Therefore, in the absence of interactions,

can be further block-diagonalized by
\hat{T}_{z}

Because of the anticommutation relation,

\{\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{T}_{z}\}=0

PT

T_{z}
.

Let us focus on the case for
N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4}

In the absence of interactions, within this Fock-space sector

\hat{P}\hat{T}
and
\hat{H}
are represented as

\displaystyle PT_{(N,\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}O&{p^{*}}^{-1}\\
p&O\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{K},

(8)

\displaystyle H_{(N,\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}H_{0+(N,\sigma)}&O\\
O&H_{0-(N,\sigma)}\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

Here,

The matrices

and

represent the Hamiltonian acting on the subspaces

(N,\sigma)

+

)

We note that the subspace of

The zero-dimensional topology of the

In non-interacting cases, the nontrivial

Interaction-enabled exceptional line
.—

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +U\big[(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{1,\mathrm{A}}\hat{b}_{2,\mathrm{B}}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{2,\mathrm{A}}\hat{b}_{1,\mathrm{B}})\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{3,\mathrm{A}}\hat{b}_{3,\mathrm{B}}+\mathrm{h.c.}\big],

Interaction-enabled EP3.—
In the above, we have discussed the emergence of interaction-enabled EL2s in two-dimensional parameter space, in which interactions play an essential role.
We further extend this argument and reveal the emergence of interaction-enabled EP3s in two-dimensional parameter space, which is protected by topology
beyond the point-gap topological classifications.

We consider two distinct interaction-enabled EL2s in two-dimensional parameter space, each of which corresponds to the degeneracy of a different pair of eigenvalues. Specifically, we assume that eigenvalues
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are degenerate along one EL2, while
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
are degenerate along the other. At the point where these two EL2s intersect, three eigenvalues
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, and
E_{3}
become degenerate, resulting in an interaction-enabled EP
3
.
Indeed, such a situation is observed for our toy model [see Fig.
3
(a)].
We stress that in the Fock-space sector with
(N,\sigma)
satisfying
N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4}

The topology of the interaction-enabled EP3 is characterized by the resultant winding number.
Let us consider the characteristic polynomial
P(E;\bm{\lambda})=\det[H(\bm{\lambda})-E\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}]
of a
PT
-symmetric non-Hermitian matrix
H(\bm{\lambda})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\ (n\geq 3)
.
If
H(\bm{\lambda})
possesses an EP3 at
\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}_{\mathrm{EP3}}
, and the corresponding eigenvalue is
E_{\mathrm{EP3}}\in\mathbb{R}
, then the characteristic polynomial has a triple root at
E=E_{\mathrm{EP3}}
.
The topology of the triple root can be characterized by the resultant winding number

\displaystyle W_{r}

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{d\bm{\lambda}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\cdot\partial_{\bm{\lambda}}\ \mathrm{ln}\ Z_{r}(\bm{\lambda}),

(25)

where
Z_{r}
is defined as

Z_{r}(\bm{\lambda})=r_{1}(\bm{\lambda})+\mathrm{i}r_{2}(\bm{\lambda})
with

\displaystyle r_{j}(\bm{\lambda})

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Res}\!\left[\partial^{2-j}_{\tilde{E}}P_{\mathrm{EP3}}(\tilde{E};\bm{\lambda}),\partial^{2}_{\tilde{E}}P_{\mathrm{EP3}}(\tilde{E};\bm{\lambda})\right],

(26)

for
j=1,2
.
The integral is taken over a closed path enclosing
\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}_{\mathrm{EP3}}
.
Here,
\mathrm{Res}[f,g]
denotes the resultant of two polynomials
f
and
g
. Due to
PT
symmetry,
r_{j}
(
j=1,2
) are real.
The polynomial of degree three
P_{\mathrm{EP3}}(\tilde{E};\bm{\lambda})
with
\tilde{E}=E-E_{\mathrm{EP3}}
are obtained by the Taylor expansion of
P(E;\bm{\lambda})

around
\tilde{E}=0
:

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{EP3}}(\tilde{E};\bm{\lambda}):=\sum^{3}_{k=0}e^{\prime}_{n-k}(\bm{\lambda})(-1)^{k}\tilde{E}^{k}

(27)

with
e^{\prime}_{k+1}
recursively obtained from

\displaystyle(k+1)e^{\prime}_{k+1}(\bm{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\sum^{k}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}\ e^{\prime}_{k-l}(\bm{\lambda})\ \mathrm{Tr}\left[\big(H^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda})\big)^{l+1}\right],

and
H^{\prime}(\bm{\lambda})=H(\bm{\lambda})-E_{\mathrm{EP3}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}
(for more details see Sec.
S3
of Supplemental Material
Sup
).
The non-zero resultant winding number indicates that the resultants vanish
(r_{1},r_{2})=\bm{0}
at a point inside the closed loop where
P_{\mathrm{EP3}}(\tilde{E};\bm{\lambda})
has a triple root
Delplace et al. 2021
.
Computing the resultant winding number, we obtain
W_{\mathrm{r}}=-1
[see Fig.

We finish this part with three remarks.
(i) We note that our argument suggests a border class of interaction-enabled EP
n
s.
For instance, it is straightforward to generalize interaction-enabled EP4s in three-dimensional parameter space.
(ii) We also note that while the resultant winding number requires the Taylor expansion around
E_{\mathrm{EP3}}
, alternative winding number enables the characterization of EP
n
s for an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix without such an expansion for
n\geq 3
, thereby allowing a systematic analysis (for more details see Secs.
S4
and
S5
of Supplemental Material
Sup
).
(iii) In the above, although we have restricted ourselves to bosonic systems, a similar analysis demonstrates the emergence of interaction-enabled topology in fermionic systems (for more details, see Sec.
S6
of Supplemental Material
Sup
).

Summary.—
We have proposed a novel type of non-Hermitian degeneracies,
dubbed interaction-enabled EP
n
s (
n=2,3
) —EP
n
s protected by topology that are
prohibited at the non-interacting level. Specifically, analyzing second-quantized Hamiltonians, we have demonstrated that both bosonic and fermionic
systems can host such interaction-enabled EP
n
s in parameter space that are protected by charge U(1), pseudo-spin-parity, and
PT
symmetries. Interaction-enabled EP2s are protected
by zero-dimensional topology and give rise to qualitative changes in the loss rate, an experimentally measurable quantity for
cold atoms.
Furthermore, we have revealed that interactions enable EP3s
protected by topology beyond the point-gap topological classifications, which suggest the potential presence of a broader class of interaction-enabled EP
n
s.

We conclude this paper by noting that identifying feasible platforms for interaction-enabled EP
n
s is an important step toward their experimental observation. Among the various non-Hermitian systems, cold atoms are considered as a potential platform where an EP3 in parameter space is reported
Wang et al. 2024
.
A detailed analysis along this direction is left for future work.

Symmetries and topology on the Fock-space sector with
(N,\sigma)

Here, we consider both bosonic and fermionic non-Hermitian systems possessing charge U(1), pseudo-spin-parity, and
PT
symmetries.
Accordingly, the second-quantized Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
satisfies the following relations:

\displaystyle[\hat{H},\hat{N}]=0,

(S1)

\displaystyle[\hat{H},(-1)^{\hat{T}_{z}}]=0,

(S2)

\displaystyle[\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{H}]=0.

(S3)

Here, we assume that the
PT
operator satisfies

\displaystyle(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l},

(S4)

\displaystyle[\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{N}]

\displaystyle=0,

(S5)

\displaystyle\{\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{T}_{z}\}

\displaystyle=0.

(S6)

The total particle-number operator
\hat{N}
and the pseudo-spin operator
\hat{T}_{z}
are defined as

\displaystyle\hat{N}

\displaystyle:=\hat{N}_{\mathrm{A}}+\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}},

(S7)

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{z}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{N}_{\mathrm{A}}-\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}),

(S8)

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\tau}\hat{a}_{i,\tau}.

(S9)

The operators
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\tau}
and
\hat{a}_{i,\tau}
denote the creation and annihilation operators of particles in component
\tau

(=\mathrm{A},\mathrm{B})

These operators obey bosonic commutation relations or fermionic anticommutation relations, depending on the statistics of the particles.

Under the above symmetry conditions,
\hat{H}
commutes with
\hat{N}
and
(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}}\ (\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}=\hat{N}/2-\hat{T}_{z})
, and can hence be block-diagonalized as

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\bigoplus_{N=0,1,\dots}\bigoplus_{\sigma=\pm}H_{(N,\sigma)},

(S10)

where
N
and
\sigma
denote the eigenvalues of
\hat{N}
and
(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}}
, respectively.
Furthermore, in the absence of interactions,
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}
can be expressed as a quadratic form of the creation and annihilation operators.
From the commutation of
\hat{H}
with
(-1)^{\hat{T}_{z}}
, it follows that the corresponding first-quantized free-particle Hamiltonian
h
commutes with the first-quantized pseudo-spin operator
t_{z}
, i.e.,

[h,t_{z}]=0
.
This implies
[\hat{H},\hat{T}_{z}]=0

to be further block-diagonalized as

\displaystyle H_{(N,\sigma)}=\bigoplus_{T_{z}}H_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})},

(S11)

where

denotes the eigenvalues of
\hat{T}_{z}
.

Considering the zero-dimensional point-gap topology of the Hamiltonian
H_{(N,\sigma)}
, we find

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4},

(S12)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=2\pmod{4},

(S13)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow 0\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=1,3\pmod{4},

(S14)

where the topology on the left (right) side of each arrow corresponds to the non-interacting (interacting) case.
In the following, we explain the details of this result.

S1.1
PT
symmetry and the Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)

Firstly, we analyze the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
on the subspace labeled by
(N,\sigma)
.
From Eqs. (
S5
) and (
S6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}}(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{-1}=(-1)^{\hat{N}}(-1)^{\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}}.

(S15)

It then follows that the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
is closed within the
(N,\sigma)
-labeled Fock-space sector when
N
is even, while it is not closed when
N
is odd.

Let us consider the case where
N
is even.
In this case, the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
is closed within the Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)

However, for the Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma,T_{z})

\{\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{T}_{z}\}=0
implies that the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
maps the
(N,\sigma,T_{z})
subspace to the
(N,\sigma,-T_{z})
subspace.
In other words, only the Fock-space sector with finite
T_{z}=0
is closed under the
PT
operation.
Moreover, due to
PT
symmetry, the bases of the
(N,\sigma,T_{z})
and
(N,\sigma,-T_{z})
subspaces are in one-to-one correspondence, and thus the dimensions of these subspaces are equal.
Next, we examine under which conditions the
(N,\sigma)
-labeled Fock-space sector contains a
T_{z}=0
subspace.
By introducing a non-negative integer
M
such that
N=2M
,
T_{z}
can be expressed as

T_{z}=M-N_{\mathrm{B}}
,
where
N_{\mathrm{B}}
denotes the eigenvalues of
\hat{N}_{\mathrm{B}}
.
In a subspace with fixed
(-1)^{N_{\mathrm{B}}}=\sigma
,
N_{\mathrm{B}}
can take either only even or only odd values.
From this analysis, the
(N,\sigma)
-labeled Fock-space sector does not contain a
T_{z}=0
subspace in the following cases:
(i)
M
is even and
\sigma=-
, or
(ii)
M
is odd and
\sigma=+
.
These cases can be collectively expressed as
N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4}
.
On the other hand, the
(N,\sigma)
-labeled Fock-space sector contains a
T_{z}=0
subspace in the following cases:
(i)
M
is even and
\sigma=+
, or
(ii)
M
is odd and
\sigma=-
,
which can be collectively expressed as
N+1+\sigma=2\pmod{4}
.

For odd
N
, as stated at the beginning of this section, the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
is not closed within the
(N,\sigma)
Fock-space sector.
In this case, we have
N+1+\sigma=1,3\pmod{4}
.

We next provide an explicit example of a many-body
\hat{P}\hat{T}

(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{2}=\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}

[\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{N}]=0

\{\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{T}_{z}\}=0

Let

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{Q}}\mathcal{K},

(S16)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\big(p_{ij}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\hat{a}_{j,\mathrm{B}}+p^{T}_{ij}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{B}}\hat{a}_{j,\mathrm{A}}\big),

(S17)

where
\mathcal{K}
denotes the complex conjugation operator, and
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,\tau}
(
\hat{a}_{j,\tau}

Since

\hat{Q}=\hat{Q}^{*}

\displaystyle(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{2}=e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{Q}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{Q}}\mathcal{K}^{2}=\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}.

(S18)

Furthermore, one finds

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{-1}

\displaystyle=e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{Q}}\mathcal{K}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\mathcal{K}e^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}\hat{Q}}

\displaystyle=\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2}[\hat{Q},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}]+\frac{(\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2})^{2}}{2!}\big[\hat{Q},[\hat{Q},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}]\big]+\cdots

\displaystyle=\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}+\mathrm{i}\sin\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\sum_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,\mathrm{B}}p^{T}_{ji}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,\mathrm{B}}p^{T}_{ji}.

(S19)

Similarly, one obtains

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\\
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{B}}\end{pmatrix}(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\sum_{j}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j,\mathrm{B}}p^{T}_{ji}\\

(S20)

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}\begin{pmatrix}\hat{a}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\\
\hat{a}_{i,\mathrm{B}}\end{pmatrix}(\hat{P}\hat{T})^{-1}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\sum_{j}\begin{pmatrix}p_{ij}\hat{a}_{j,\mathrm{B}}\\

S1.2
Zero-dimensional point-gap topology of
H_{(N,\sigma)}

We now discuss the zero-dimensional point-gap topology of
H_{(N,\sigma)}
,
which acts invariantly within each Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)
.
The associated topological index is defined as

\displaystyle\nu_{(N,\sigma)}=\mathrm{sgn}\!\left(\det\!\left[H_{(N,\sigma)}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\right]\right),

(S22)

where
E_{\mathrm{ref}}
is a real reference energy.

In the absence of many-body interactions, the Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}
,
in addition to satisfying the symmetry conditions
Eqs. (
S1
)-(
S3
),
also obeys

\displaystyle[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{T}_{z}]=0.

(S23)

As a consequence,
H_{(N,\sigma)}
can be further block-diagonalized as in
Eq. (
S11
).
As we shall see below, this additional symmetry satisfied by

\hat{H}_{0}
leads to a qualitative difference in the point-gap topology
between the noninteracting and interacting cases.
As discussed in Sec.
S1.1
,
the constraints imposed by
\hat{P}\hat{T}
on the Hamiltonian
within each Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)

depend on the value of
N+1+\sigma\ (\mathrm{mod}\,4)
.
We therefore analyze the point-gap topology separately for each case.

For the case
N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4}
, the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
is closed within the Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)
.
In the absence of interactions, the Hamiltonian can be further block-diagonalized by
\hat{T}_{z}
, but there exists no subspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
T_{z}=0
.
For an arbitrary
T_{z}\neq 0
, the anticommutation relation
\{\hat{P}\hat{T},\hat{T}_{z}\}=0

implies that
H_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})}
and
H_{0(N,\sigma,-T_{z})}
in Eq. (
S11
) are related by a unitary matrix
U_{PT}
as

\displaystyle U_{PT}H^{*}_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})}U^{\dagger}_{PT}=H_{0(N,\sigma,-T_{z})}.

(S24)

Therefore, the topological index
\nu_{(N,\sigma)}
characterizing the point-gap topology protected by
PT
symmetry is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{(N,\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{T_{z}}\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\!\det\big[H_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\prod_{T_{z}>0}\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\big|\det\big[H_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\big]\big|^{2}\Big)=1

(S25)

and hence it cannot take the value
-1
.
This indicates that the system is topologically trivial in the absence of interactions.
On the other hand, once interactions are introduced, the block diagonalization of
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}}
with respect to
\hat{T}_{z}
is no longer applicable.
As a result,
\nu_{(N,\sigma)}
can take both values
\pm 1
.
From the above discussion, we conclude that the classification of the zero-dimensional point-gap topology of
H_{(N,\sigma)}
changes due to interactions as

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=0\pmod{4}.

For the case
N+1+\sigma=2\pmod{4}
, the action of
\hat{P}\hat{T}
is also closed within the Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)
.
In the absence of interactions,
H_{(N,\sigma)}
can be further block-diagonalized by
\hat{T}_{z}
, and this Fock-space sector contains a subspace labeled by
T_{z}=0
, within which the
PT
symmetry is closed.
Accordingly,
\nu_{(N,\sigma)}
is given by

\displaystyle\nu_{(N,\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{T_{z}}\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\mathrm{det}\big[H_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\mathrm{det}\big[H_{0(N,\sigma,0)}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\big]\Big)\times\prod_{T_{z}>0}\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\big|\mathrm{det}\big[H_{0(N,\sigma,T_{z})}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}\big]\big|^{2}\Big)

(S26)

and is therefore allowed to take both
+1
and
-1
.
When interactions are introduced, the block diagonalization of
\hat{H}
with respect to
\hat{T}_{z}
is no longer applicable; however, the above
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological index remains well defined even in the presence of interactions.
This suggests that the topology of
H_{(N,\sigma)}
is preserved under interactions.
From the above discussion, the classification of the zero-dimensional point-gap topology of
H_{(N,\sigma)}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=2\pmod{4}.

For the cases
N+1+\sigma=1,3\pmod{4}
, the
PT
symmetry is not closed within the Fock-space sector labeled by
(N,\sigma)
[see Eq. (
S15
)].
Therefore, the
PT
-protected
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
index is not well defined in this sector, and the zero-dimensional point-gap topology is trivial both with and without interactions.
The resulting classification is

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow 0\quad\quad\mathrm{for}\ N+1+\sigma=1,3\pmod{4}.

S2
Properties of
PT
-symmetric non-Hermitian Hamiltonians

S2.1
Characteristic-polynomial discriminants and EP2s in
PT
-symmetric systems

The discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of a matrix
H\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is defined by

\mathrm{Disc}\big[\!\det[H-E\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}]\big]:=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2},

(S27)

with
\{\lambda_{i}\}^{n}_{i=1}
being the eigenvalues of
H
.
For a
PT
-symmetric Hamiltonian
H
, its spectrum consists of real eigenvalues as well as pairs of complex-conjugate eigenvalues with positive and negative imaginary parts.
Accordingly, we partition the index set labeling the eigenvalues into three disjoint subsets:
I_{1}
labeling the
n_{r}
real eigenvalues;
I_{2}
labeling the
n_{c}
eigenvalues with positive imaginary parts; and
I_{3}
labeling their complex-conjugate counterparts.
With this partition, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathrm{Disc}\big[\!\det[H-E\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}]\big]

\displaystyle=\prod^{3}_{\alpha=1}\Big(\prod_{i<j(i,j\in I_{\alpha})}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\Big)\times\prod_{1\leq\beta<\gamma\leq 3}\Big(\prod_{l\in I_{\beta}}\prod_{k\in I_{\gamma}}(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k})^{2}\Big).

(S28)

In the first part
on the right-hand side of the above equation, the contributions with
\alpha=2
and
\alpha=3

form a complex-conjugate pair.
Hence, the first part is non-negative.
In the second part, the contributions with
(\beta,\gamma)=(1,2)

and
(\beta,\gamma)=(1,3)
form a complex-conjugate pair,
whereas the contribution with
(\beta,\gamma)=(2,3)
can take negative values.
Indeed, for
(\beta,\gamma)=(2,3)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{l\in I_{2}}\prod_{k\in I_{3}}(\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\in I_{2}}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda^{*}_{j})^{2}\times\prod_{l<k(l,k\in I_{2})}(\lambda_{l}-\lambda^{*}_{k})^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{*}_{l})^{2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n_{c}}\times\prod_{j\in I_{2}}(2\mathrm{Im}\lambda_{j})^{2}\times\prod_{l<k(l,k\in I_{2})}|\lambda_{l}-\lambda^{*}_{k}|^{4}.

(S29)

Here, in going from the first line to the second line on the right-hand side of the above equation, we have used the fact that each eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
with
k\in I_{3}
is in one-to-one correspondence with the complex conjugate of an eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
with
k\in I_{2}
satisfying
\mathrm{Im}\lambda_{k}>0
.
Therefore, the sign of the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial of a
PT
-symmetric
H
is determined by the parity of the total number
n_{c}
of complex-conjugate eigenvalue pairs.

On the other hand, for
E_{\mathrm{ref}}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\det[H-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in I_{1}}(\lambda_{i}-E_{\mathrm{ref}})\times\Big|\prod_{j\in I_{2}}(\lambda_{j}-E_{\mathrm{ref}})\Big|^{2}.

(S30)

Thus,
\nu=\mathrm{sgn}\big(\!\det[H(\bm{\lambda})-E_{\mathrm{ref}}\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}]\big)
counts the parity of the number of real eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
satisfying
\lambda_{i}<E_{\mathrm{ref}}
.
If
E_{\mathrm{ref}}
is chosen as the EP2 eigenvalue
E_{\mathrm{EP2}}
at a parameter point
\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}_{\mathrm{EP2}}
,
\nu
changes discontinuously upon crossing
\bm{\lambda}_{\mathrm{EP2}}
.
Correspondingly, the number of complex-conjugate eigenvalue pairs changes by one, and hence
s=\mathrm{sgn}\!\left(\mathrm{Disc}\big[\mathrm{det}[H(\bm{\lambda})-E\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}]\big]\right)
also exhibits a discontinuous change.

S2.2
Behavior of the loss rate

For a non-Hermitian matrix
H
, we define

B:=(H-H^{\dagger})/2\mathrm{i}\quad(=B^{\dagger}).

Then one finds

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\langle\tilde{\psi}(t)|H|\tilde{\psi}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\tilde{\psi}(t)|B|\tilde{\psi}(t)\rangle,

(S31)

where

|\tilde{\psi}(t)\rangle=\frac{e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}|\psi(0)\rangle}{\|e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}|\psi(0)\rangle\|}.

From this, we obtain the bound

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{Im}\langle\tilde{\psi}(t)|H|\tilde{\psi}(t)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\tilde{\psi}(t)|B|\tilde{\psi}(t)\rangle\right|\leq\|B\|,

(S32)

where the operator norm is defined as

\|B\|:=\max_{\||x\rangle\|=1}\|B|x\rangle\|.

Therefore, for
H_{(2M,\sigma)}
, the instantaneous loss rate satisfies

\displaystyle|L_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\langle N_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)\rangle}\frac{d}{dt}\langle N_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=2\left|\mathrm{Im}\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[H_{(2M,\sigma)}\mathcal{P}_{(2M,\sigma)}\hat{\rho}(t)\mathcal{P}_{(2M,\sigma)}\right]}{\mathrm{Tr}\!\left[\mathcal{P}_{(2M,\sigma)}\hat{\rho}(t)\mathcal{P}_{(2M,\sigma)}\right]}\right|

\displaystyle=2\left|\mathrm{Im}\langle\tilde{\psi}_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)|H_{(2M,\sigma)}|\tilde{\psi}_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|B_{(2M,\sigma)}\|,

(S33)

where
B_{(2M,\sigma)}=\left(H_{(2M,\sigma)}-H^{\dagger}_{(2M,\sigma)}\right)/2\mathrm{i}
.
Similarly, the time-averaged loss rate is bounded as

\displaystyle|\bar{L}_{(2M,\sigma)}[T]|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt\,L_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)\right|\leq 2\|B_{(2M,\sigma)}\|.

(S34)

Here,

|\tilde{\psi}_{(2M,\sigma)}(t)\rangle=\frac{e^{-\mathrm{i}tH_{(2M,\sigma)}}\mathcal{P}_{(2M,\sigma)}|\psi\rangle}{\|e^{-\mathrm{i}tH_{(2M,\sigma)}}\mathcal{P}_{(2M,\sigma)}|\psi\rangle\|}.

For the bosonic toy model in Eq. (
S5
)
and the fermionic toy model in Eq. (
S70
),
we find
\|B_{(2,+)}\|=|\delta|
.

We assume that the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian
H
is diagonalizable in terms of its right eigenvectors
|R_{n}\rangle
and left eigenvectors
|L_{n}\rangle
, such that
H=\sum_{n}E_{n}|R_{n}\rangle\langle L_{n}|
.
Then, the time-evolved state is given by
|\psi(t)\rangle=
\sum_{n}l_{n}\cdot e^{-\mathrm{i}tE_{n}}|R_{n}\rangle
, where
l_{n}:=\langle L_{n}|\psi(0)\rangle
.
Using this expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\langle\psi(t)|H|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{mn}l_{m}^{*}l_{n}\langle R_{m}|R_{n}\rangle e^{-\mathrm{i}t(E_{n}-E_{m}^{*})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{mn}e^{t\beta_{mn}}Z_{mn}e^{\mathrm{i}t\alpha_{mn}}.

(S35)

Here, we have defined

\displaystyle\alpha_{mn}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Re}E_{m}-\mathrm{Re}E_{n}=-\alpha_{nm},

(S36)

\displaystyle\beta_{mn}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{Im}E_{m}+\mathrm{Im}E_{n}=\beta_{nm},

(S37)

\displaystyle Z_{mn}

\displaystyle:=l_{m}^{*}l_{n}\langle R_{m}|R_{n}\rangle=Z_{nm}^{*}.

(S38)

From Eqs. (
S36
)-(
S38
), the summation appearing in the second line on the right-hand side of Eq. (
S2.2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{mn}e^{t\beta_{mn}}Z_{mn}e^{\mathrm{i}t\alpha_{mn}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}e^{t\beta_{mm}}Z_{mm}+\sum_{m<n}e^{t\beta_{mn}}(Z_{mn}e^{\mathrm{i}t\alpha_{mn}}+Z_{nm}e^{\mathrm{i}t\alpha_{nm}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m}e^{t\beta_{mm}}Z_{mm}+2\sum_{m<n}e^{t\beta_{mn}}|Z_{mn}|\mathrm{cos}(\theta_{mn}+t\alpha_{mn}).

(S39)

Here we have written
Z_{mn}=|Z_{mn}|e^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{mn}}
.
For a
PT
-symmetric Hamiltonian
H
, we have

\gamma:=\max\{\mathrm{Im}E_{m}\}\geq 0
, and hence

\beta_{\mathrm{max}}:=\max\{\beta_{mn}\}=2\gamma\geq 0
.
Accordingly, we define

\displaystyle G(t):=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}e^{-t(\beta_{\mathrm{max}}-\beta_{mm})}Z_{mm}+2\sum_{m<n}e^{-t(\beta_{\mathrm{max}}-\beta_{mn})}|Z_{mn}|\cos\!\left(\theta_{mn}+t\alpha_{mn}\right).

(S40)

With this definition, Eq. (
S2.2
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\langle\psi(t)|H|\psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\!\left[e^{t\beta_{\mathrm{max}}}G(t)\right]=e^{t\beta_{\mathrm{max}}}\left[\gamma\,G(t)+\frac{1}{2}G^{\prime}(t)\right].

(S41)

For an initial state
|\psi(0)\rangle\neq\bm{0}
, we have

\langle\psi(t)|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{t\beta_{\mathrm{max}}}G(t)>0
.
Therefore, the loss rate
L(t)
is given by

\displaystyle L(t)

\displaystyle=-2\mathrm{Im}\langle\tilde{\psi}(t)|H|\tilde{\psi}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\mathrm{Im}\langle\psi(t)|H|\psi(t)\rangle}{\langle\psi(t)|\psi(t)\rangle}=-2\gamma-\frac{G^{\prime}(t)}{G(t)}.

(S42)

For
\gamma>0
and sufficiently large times

t\gg(\beta_{\mathrm{max}}-\beta_{mn})^{-1}
,
the function
G(t)
behaves asymptotically as

\displaystyle G(t)\sim

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\\
\beta_{mm}=\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\end{subarray}}Z_{mm}+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m<n\\
\beta_{mn}=\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\end{subarray}}|Z_{mn}|\cos\!\left(\theta_{mn}+t\alpha_{mn}\right).

(S43)

As a result,
L(t)
oscillates around
-2\gamma

(if there exists at least one pair
m\ (<n)
that satisfies

\beta_{mn}=\beta_{\mathrm{max}}
and
\alpha_{mn}\neq 0
),
or converges to
-2\gamma
.

By contrast, when all eigenvalues are real, the restrictions on the summations in Eq. (
S43
) are lifted, and
L(t)
oscillates around zero.

In both cases, since

\frac{1}{t}\ln[G(t)/G(0)]\to 0
as
t\to\infty
,
the long-time average satisfies
\frac{1}{T}\int^{T}_{0}dtL(t)
\sim-2\gamma
.

S3
Newton’s identity

For an arbitrary square matrix
{H}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, we define

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\det\left[\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}+t{H}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+{\lambda}_{i}t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}e_{k}t^{k},

(S44)

where
\lambda_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,n
) are the eigenvalues of
H
and
e_{k}
(
k=1,\dots,n
) denote the elementary symmetric polynomials
e_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}
with
e_{0}=1
.

Let us consider the formal power-series expansion of the logarithm
\mathrm{ln}\ P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ln}\ P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\ \mathrm{ln}\ (1+{\lambda}_{i}t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}({\lambda}_{i}t)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\mathrm{Tr}[{H}^{k}]t^{k}.

(S45)

Taking the derivative with respect to
t
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{ln}\ P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}(-1)^{k}\ \mathrm{Tr}[{H}^{k+1}]t^{k}.

(S46)

On the other hand, differentiating Eq. (
S3
) directly yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}(k+1)e_{k+1}t^{k}.

(S47)

Using the identity
df/dt=f\cdot d\ (\mathrm{ln}\ f)/dt
, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)

\displaystyle=P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{ln}\ P_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{m=0}e_{m}t^{m}\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}\ \mathrm{Tr}[{H}^{l+1}]t^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{m=0}\sum^{\infty}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}e_{m}\ \mathrm{Tr}[{H}^{l+1}]t^{l+m}.

(S48)

By comparing the coefficients of
t^{k}
in Eqs. (
S46
) and (
S3
),
we obtain

\displaystyle(k+1)

\displaystyle e_{k+1}={\sum_{0\leq m\leq n,l\geq 0,l+m=k}}(-1)^{l}e_{m}\ \mathrm{Tr}[{H}^{l+1}]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ =\sum^{k}_{l=0}(-1)^{l}e_{k-l}\ \mathrm{Tr}[{H}^{l+1}].

(S49)

S4
Characterization of
n
- and
(n-1)
-fold EPs in
n
-band systems

As mentioned in the main text, a topological invariant allows systematic characterization of EP
3
s with
PT
symmetry for a
4\times 4
matrix. In this section, we explain this fact for
(n-1)
-fold EPs described by an
n\times n
matrix with/without
PT
symmetry.
We consider
n
- and
(n-1)
-fold EPs in an arbitrary
n
-band system described by

H\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
.
To analyze degeneracies of
H
, it is convenient to work with the corresponding traceless
matrix

\bar{H}=H-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}
.
The matrix
\bar{H}
preserves the symmetries of
H
as well as the relative
configuration of its eigenvalues.
Let
\lambda_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,n
) be the eigenvalues of
H
.
Then

\displaystyle P_{\bar{H}}(E)

\displaystyle:=\det[\bar{H}-E\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}]

\displaystyle=\prod^{n}_{i=1}(\bar{\lambda}_{i}-E)=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(-1)^{k}\bar{e}_{n-k}E^{k}

(S50)

holds, where

\bar{\lambda}_{i}:=\lambda_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]
.
Here,
\bar{e}_{k}
denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues

\bar{\lambda}_{i}
of
\bar{H}
, defined as

\bar{e}_{k}:=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\bar{\lambda}_{i_{1}}\cdots\bar{\lambda}_{i_{k}}
,
with
\bar{e}_{0}:=1
.
These coefficients can be expressed in terms of the traces
\mathrm{Tr}[\bar{H}^{k}]
via Newton’s identities,

\displaystyle(k+1)\bar{e}_{k+1}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}(-1)^{l}\bar{e}_{k-l}\mathrm{Tr}[\bar{H}^{\,l+1}],

(S51)

whose derivation is provided in Sec.
S3
.

Prior to the characterization of
(n-1)
-fold EPs, we discuss
n
-fold EPs.
If
H
has an
n
-fold degenerate eigenvalue
r\in\mathbb{C}
, all eigenvalues of
\bar{H}
must vanish. In this case, the characteristic polynomial of the corresponding traceless matrix
\bar{H}
is given by

\displaystyle P_{\bar{H}}(E)

\displaystyle=(-E)^{n}.

(S52)

Since
\bar{e}_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}[\bar{H}]=0
, Eqs. (
S4
) and (
S52
) imply that, if
H
has an
n
-fold degenerate eigenvalue
r\in\mathbb{C}
, the coefficients
\bar{e}_{k}
vanish for
k=2,\dots,n
. Therefore, imposing these
n-1
independent conditions indicates that
H
exhibits an EP
n
with eigenvalue
r=\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]
.
This observation also clarifies the codimension of an EP
n
. In a generic
n
-band system, the conditions
\bar{e}_{k}=0

(k=2,\dots,n)
are complex constraints, yielding a codimension
2(n-1)
. In contrast, in the presence of
PT
symmetry, these coefficients are restricted to be real, so that each condition reduces to a single real constraint. As a result, the codimension is reduced to
n-1
under
PT
symmetry.

Next, we consider the characterization of
(n-1)
-fold EPs.
When
H
has an
(n-1)
-fold degenerate eigenvalue
r
and a single eigenvalue

s\ (\neq r)
,
for the corresponding traceless matrix

\bar{H}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[H]=(n-1)r+s\Rightarrow\mathrm{Tr}[\bar{H}]=(n-1)\bar{r}+\bar{s}=0

(S53)

and

\displaystyle\det[H]=r^{n-1}s\Rightarrow\det[\bar{H}]=(1-n)\bar{r}^{n}

(S54)

hold. Here we define
\bar{r}:=r-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]
and
\bar{s}:=s-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]
.
Taking these relations into account, we obtain

\displaystyle P_{\bar{H}}(E)

\displaystyle=(\bar{r}-E)^{n-1}(\bar{s}-E)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{k=0}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n}{k}\left(1-k\right)\bar{r}^{k}E^{n-k}.

(S55)

Thus, when
H
has an
(n-1)
-fold eigenvalue
r
and a single eigenvalue

s\ (\neq r)
, each coefficient
\bar{e}_{k}

(k=2,\dots,n-1)
satisfies

\bar{e}_{k}=\binom{n}{k}(1-k)\bar{r}^{k}
[see Eqs. (
S4
) and (
S4
)].
Here,
\bar{r}
is given by

\bar{r}=\left(\det[\bar{H}]/(1-n)\right)^{1/n}

[see Eq. (
S54
)].
Combining these relations, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bar{e}_{k}}{\binom{n}{k}(1-k)}\right)^{n}=\left(\frac{\det[\bar{H}]}{1-n}\right)^{k},

(S56)

for
k=2,\dots,n-1
.
Since
s\neq r
, it is necessary that
\det[\bar{H}]\neq 0
.
Therefore, the existence of an
(n-1)
-fold EP is confirmed when

\det[\bar{H}]\neq 0
and the above
n-2
conditions in
Eq. (
S56
) are satisfied.
Accordingly, the codimension of an
(n-1)
-fold EP for a generic
n
-band system
is
2(n-2)
, while it is reduced to
n-2
in the presence of
PT
symmetry.
Moreover, the
(n-1)
-fold degenerate eigenvalue
r
of
H
is given by

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\bar{r}+\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]=\left(\frac{\det\left[H-\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H]\mbox{1}\hskip-2.5pt\mbox{l}_{n}\right]}{1-n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}+\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}[H].

(S57)

Using the coefficient constraints of the characteristic polynomial in
Eq. (
S56
), we can introduce the
coefficient winding number
W_{c}
, in analogy with the resultant
winding number
W_{r}
.

For a generic
n
-band system with
n\ \geq 3
,
an
(n-1)
-fold EP is characterized by the vanishing of the following
2(n-2)
-dimensional vector:

\displaystyle\bm{C}(\bm{\lambda})=\Big(\mathrm{Re}\,c_{1},\mathrm{Im}\,c_{1},\dots,\mathrm{Re}\,c_{n-2},\mathrm{Im}\,c_{n-2}\Big)^{T}.

(S58)

Here, each component
c_{k}(\bm{\lambda})

(k=1,\dots,n-2)
is defined as

\displaystyle c_{k}(\bm{\lambda})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\bar{e}_{k+1}(\bm{\lambda})}{\binom{n}{k+1}(-k)}\right)^{n}-\left(\frac{\det[\bar{H}(\bm{\lambda})]}{1-n}\right)^{k+1}.

(S59)

Let
\bm{\lambda}_{0}\in X
denote a parameter point corresponding to an
(n-1)
-fold EP in
2(n-2)
-dimensional parameter space
X
.
For parameter points
\bm{p}\in S^{2(n-2)-1}\subset X\setminus\{\bm{\lambda}_{0}\}
surrounding
\bm{\lambda}_{0}
, one can introduce a mapping
between
(2n-5)
-spheres,

\bm{n}=\bm{C}/\|\bm{C}\|:S^{2n-5}\rightarrow S^{2n-5}
.
The homotopy class of this mapping is characterized by

\pi_{2n-5}(S^{2n-5})=\mathbb{Z}

and thus takes integer values, which are given by the winding number

\displaystyle W_{c}=\frac{\epsilon^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2n-5}}}{2\pi^{n-2}/(n-3)!}\int d^{2n-5}\bm{p}\,f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2n-5}}.

(S60)

Here,
\epsilon^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2n-5}}
is the totally antisymmetric tensor satisfying

\epsilon^{12\cdots(2n-5)}=1
,
and

f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2n-5}}=n_{i_{1}}\partial_{1}n_{i_{2}}\partial_{2}n_{i_{3}}\cdots\partial_{2n-5}n_{2n-4}
.

Moreover, for an
(n-1)
-fold EP at
\bm{\lambda}=\bm{\lambda}_{0}
in
(n-2)
-dimensional parameter space
X^{\prime}
protected by
PT
symmetry, an
(n-2)
-component vector

\bm{C}(\bm{\lambda})=\big(c_{1}(\bm{\lambda}),\dots,c_{n-2}(\bm{\lambda})\big)^{T}

vanishes.
Therefore, for parameter points

\bm{p}\in S^{n-3}\subset X^{\prime}\setminus\{\bm{\lambda}_{0}\}

surrounding
\bm{\lambda}_{0}
, one can introduce a mapping between
(n-3)
-spheres,

\bm{n}=\bm{C}/\|\bm{C}\|:S^{n-3}\rightarrow S^{n-3}
,
from which the winding number

\displaystyle W_{c}=\frac{\epsilon^{i_{1}\cdots i_{n-3}}}{A_{n-3}}\int d^{n-3}\bm{p}\,f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{n-3}}

(S61)

is obtained.
Here, for a nonnegative integer
m
,

\displaystyle A_{n-3}=\begin{cases}2\pi^{m+1}/m!\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \ (n=2(m+2)),\\
2^{2m+1}\pi^{m}m!/(2m)!\qquad(n=2m+3),\end{cases}

(S62)

which corresponds to the surface area of the

-dimensional unit sphere.

\displaystyle\hat{P}\hat{T}=\mathrm{exp}\left[\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{i,j}(p_{ij}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\hat{f}_{j,\mathrm{B}}+p^{T}_{ij}\hat{f}^{\dagger}_{i,\mathrm{B}}\hat{f}_{j,\mathrm{A}})\right]\mathcal{K},

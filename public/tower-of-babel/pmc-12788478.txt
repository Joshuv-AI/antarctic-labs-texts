Metal-Free Dental Implants: A Narrative Review of Zirconia and Emerging Alternative Materials
Authors: Navyasri Chappa, Jyothi Valaparla, Harika Gadde, Aravindhan Venkatapathi, Himaja Vanteddu Sree, Sai Chigurupati Srija, S. Parthiban
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This narrative review explores the current landscape of metal-free dental implants, emphasizing zirconia as the leading material and examining alternative options. The review discusses the biological, mechanical, and esthetic properties of zirconia implants, along with recent advances in material science exploring polymer-based and ceramic alternatives. Clinical outcomes, osseointegration potential, and limitations are also reviewed, highlighting future directions for biocompatible, esthetically superior implant solutions.

INTRODUCTION

Dental implants offer a durable solution for missing teeth, with titanium traditionally being the gold standard due to its biocompatibility and strength.[1] However, concerns like hypersensitivity, aesthetic limitations, and corrosion have led to a rise in demand for metal-free alternatives. Zirconia has emerged as a promising contender with excellent biological, mechanical, and aesthetic properties, while ongoing material science advancements also explore novel polymer-based and other ceramic options.[23] This narrative review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the current landscape of metal-free dental implants and explore future directions in the pursuit of biocompatible and esthetically superior implant solutions that can address the evolving needs of both clinicians and patients.

ZIRCONIA / PEEK / GLASS CERAMICS

Zirconia, or zirconium dioxide, has transformed dentistry through its strength, CAD/CAM compatibility, and aesthetic appeal. Widely used for all-ceramic prostheses, it's favored for its biocompatibility and resistance to degradation. Yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystals (Y-TZP) are popular, with monolithic crowns being ideal for high-force posterior applications.[35] Alternative metal-free materials like polyetheretherketone (PEEK) and polyetherketoneketone (PEKK) offer strong thermoplastic polymer alternatives with benefits over titanium, such as reduced hypersensitivity and a low elastic modulus.[567] Bioactive glasses (BGs) are another class of interactive ceramics used in regenerative medicine that promote bone-like mineral formation and tissue bonding, stimulating bone and blood vessel growth while preventing infections.[268]

CONCLUSION

Zirconia implants are a strong alternative to titanium, offering similar short-to-medium-term results with added aesthetic and biocompatible benefits. While newer materials like PEEK and PEKK show promise, they require more long-term research.

Abstract
. In [ KL22b ] , the authors stated some conjectures on the variance of certain sums of the divisor function d k ​ ( n ) d_{k}(n) over number fields, which were inspired by analogous results over function fields proven in [ KL22a ] . These problems are related to certain symplectic matrix integrals. While the function field results can be directly related to the random matrix integrals, the connection between the random matrix integrals and the number field results is less direct and involves arithmetic factors. The goal of this article is to give heuristic arguments for the formulas of these arithmetic factors.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to investigate the arithmetic constants appearing in certain conjectures made in
[
KL22b
]
involving the divisor function and integrals over the ensemble of unitary symplectic matrices. For a positive integer
n
, let
d_{k}(n)
denote the number of ways of writing
n
as an ordered product of
k
positive integers. In
[
KRRGR18
]
, Keating, Rodgers, Roditty-Gershon, and Rudnick study the distribution of the sum

(1)

\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}(A):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq X\\
m\equiv A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\end{subarray}}d_{k}(m),

where
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
is prime, and
A\in\mathbb{Z}
. After proving the analogous result for a function field model of the same problem, they conjecture in
[
KRRGR18
, Conjecture 3.3]
that when
Q^{1+\varepsilon}<X<Q^{k-\varepsilon}
and as
X\rightarrow\infty
,
the variance of
\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}
, defined by

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}):=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\\
\mathrm{gcd}(A,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\left|\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}(A)-\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}\right\rangle\right|^{2},

where

\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}\right\rangle:=\frac{1}{\Phi(Q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\\
\mathrm{gcd}(A,Q)=1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q}(A),

is given by

(2)

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};X;Q})\sim\frac{X}{Q}a_{k}\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\log X}{\log Q}\right)(\log Q)^{k^{2}-1}.

Here

a_{k}:=\prod_{p}\left(\Big(1-\frac{1}{p}\Big)^{(k-1)^{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\right)

and
\gamma_{k}(c)
is a piecewise polynomial of degree
k^{2}-1
defined as the leading coefficient (respect to the variable
N
) of
I_{k}(cN;N)
, where

I_{k}(m;N):=\int_{\mathrm{U}(N)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=m\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq N\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U.

The integral ranges over complex unitary matrices of dimension
N
, and the
\mathrm{Sc}_{j}(U)
are the secular coefficients, defined for a
N\times N
matrix
U
via

\det(I+Ux)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\mathrm{Sc}_{j}(U)x^{j}.

More precisely, letting
c=\frac{m}{N}\in[0,k]
, Keating et al. further prove in
[
KRRGR18
, Theorem 1.5]
that

\int_{\mathrm{U}(N)}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=m\\
0\leq j_{1},\dots,j_{k}\leq N\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{1}}(U)\cdots\mathrm{Sc}_{j_{k}}(U)\Big|^{2}\mathrm{d}U=\gamma_{k}(c)N^{k^{2}-1}+O_{k}(N^{k^{2}-2}).

The conjecture given by equation (
2
) follows from
[
KRRGR18
, Theorem 3.1]
, which is a function field version of the same problem. Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be the ring of polynomials over the function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of
q
elements, where
q
is an odd prime power. For
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, we define
d_{k}(f)
as the number of ways of writing
f
as an ordered product of
k
monic polynomials. For
Q\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
square-free and
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
coprime to
Q
, Keating et al consider the sum

(3)

\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q}(A):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\text{monic}\\
\deg(f)=n\\
f\equiv A\,(\mathrm{mod}\,Q)\end{subarray}}d_{k}(f),

and prove that for
n\leq k(\deg(Q)-1)
and as
q\rightarrow\infty
, the variance of the sum above is given by

(4)

\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{S}_{d_{k};n;Q})\sim\frac{q^{n}}{q^{\deg(Q)}}I_{k}(n;\deg(Q)-1)\sim\frac{q^{n}}{q^{\deg(Q)}}\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{n}{\deg(Q)}\right)(\deg(Q))^{k^{2}-1}.

To understand the relationship between the result (
4
) and the conjectural statement (
2
), we observe that
q^{n}
represents the size of the sum over
f
in (
3
), and it therefore corresponds to
X
, the size of the sum over
m
in (
1
). Similarly,
q^{\deg(Q)}
represents the size of
Q
, and
n
represents
\log X
, while
\deg(Q)
represents
\log(Q)
. (The statement of the conjectural formula (
2
) is done with the assumption
Q
prime for simplicity.)

The key (and only!) difference between the function field result that Keating et al. prove in
[
KRRGR18
, Theorem 3.1]

, Conjecture 3.3]

. The same arithmetic factor also appears in work of Lester
[
Les16
]
on the variances of divisor functions on middle length intervals.

We also consider a setting studied by Rudnick and Waxman

\mathfrak{a}=(\alpha)

As in the unitary setting,
a^{S}_{k}(\mathcal{T})
,
a^{S}_{k}(\mathcal{S})
, and
a^{S}_{\ell}(\mathcal{N})
are expected to be given by Euler products. However, we notice that the expressions (

11
) include an extra factor of 2. As we will later discuss, this discrepancy is introduced from translating certain constraints from the number field case, which is continuous, to the discrete setting of the function field case. This feature seems to be specific for the symplectic questions that we consider in this manuscript, and it does not appear in
[
KRRGR18
]
. Curiously, this factor of
2
does not seem to immediately appear when, for example, naively applying the “recipe” of
[
CFK+05
]
. It is not immediately clear how to adapt the recipe to this setting. A natural formulation of the variance (
5
), for example, is related to an average of quadratic
L
-functions via Perron’s formula. As a result, the variance involves averages over quadratic
L
-functions both at the central point and all along the half-line. While the recipe allows one to relate averages of
L
-values near the central point to random matrix integrals, the relation for other points on the half line is less clear; naively extending the approximation near the central value does not produce the additional factor of
2
in these conjectures.

Another noticeable feature is that the constant does depend on the choice of the interval, in the sense that performing the sum over
1\leq n\leq X
leads to a different result than performing the sum over
\alpha X\leq n\leq(\alpha+1)X
. This feature separates the number field case from the function field case, where this difference does not play a role.

2.
The leading term of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)

The goal of this section is to obtain the coefficient in
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
of the highest power of
c
in the interval
c\in[0,1/2]
. This will allow us to compare the leading coefficient of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
with the constants coming from direct computation in the integer case. Recall the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
KL22b
, Proposition 4.2]

For
n\leq N+\frac{1+k}{2}
, we have

(13)

I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{\frac{n-\ell}{2}+\binom{k+1}{2}-1}{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}^{2}\binom{\ell+k^{2}-1}{k^{2}-1}.

Moreover,
I_{d_{k},2}^{S}(n;N)
is a quasi-polynomial in
n
of period 2 and degree
2k^{2}+k-2
(provided that
n\leq N+\frac{1+k}{2}
).

Our goal is to extract the coefficient of the highest power of
n
in the right-hand side of (
13
); the highest powers of
n
and the summation index
\ell
in the right-hand side are equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(k^{2}-1)!\left(\left(\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}-1\right)!\right)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(\frac{n-\ell}{2}\right)^{k^{2}+k-2}\ell^{k^{2}-1}.

We proceed by dividing by
n^{2k^{2}+k-2}
and taking the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
in the sum above. This gives

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell=0\\
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\ell}{2n}\right)^{k^{2}+k-2}\left(\frac{\ell}{n}\right)^{k^{2}-1}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1-t}{2}\right)^{k^{2}+k-2}t^{k^{2}-1}dt,

where the factor
\frac{1}{2}
in front is due to the condition that
\ell\equiv n\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)
. In turn, the integral is given by

\frac{1}{2^{k^{2}+k-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-t\right)^{k^{2}+k-2}t^{k^{2}-1}dt=\frac{(k^{2}+k-2)!(k^{2}-1)!}{2^{k^{2}+k-1}(2k^{2}+k-2)!}.

Putting everything together, the main coefficient of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
is given by

(14)

\binom{k^{2}+k-2}{\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}-1}\frac{1}{2^{k^{2}+k-1}(2k^{2}+k-2)!}.

3.
Results on evaluating multiple Dirichlet series

Here we record several theorems due to de la Bretèche
[
dlB01
]
on the evaluation of multiple Dirichlet series which will appear in our work later on.

Consider the general situation where
f:\mathbb{N}^{\mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is a positive arithmetic function. We are interested in estimating

S(X;\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}):=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}\leq X^{\beta_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{1\leq n_{\mu}\leq X^{\beta_{\mu}}}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{\mu}).

We will work with the Dirichlet series

F(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu}):=\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{n_{\mu}=1}^{\infty}\frac{f(n_{1},\dots,n_{\mu})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{\mu}^{s_{\mu}}}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
denote the set of linear forms in
(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu})
that take positive values when they are evaluated in
s_{j}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Also let
e_{1},\dots,e_{\mu}
denote the canonical base for
\mathbb{C}^{\mu}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
dlB01
, Théorème 1 (simplified version)]
Assume that the following is true:

(1)

F(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu})
converges absolutely in the region
\re(s_{j})>\frac{1}{2}
for
j=1,\dots,\mu
.

(2)

There is a set
\{\ell^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n}
of linear forms in
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}
such that for some
\delta_{1}>0
, the function

H(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu}):=F\left(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\ell^{(j)}\left(s_{1}-\frac{1}{2},\dots,s_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\right)

converges absolutely to a holomorphic function in the domain
\re(s_{j})>\frac{1}{2}-\delta_{1}
.

(3)

There is a
\delta_{2}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}>0
, the bound

\displaystyle|H(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu})|\ll

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left|\im\left(\ell^{(j)}\left(s_{1}-\frac{1}{2},\dots,s_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right|+1\right)^{1-\delta_{2}\min\left\{0,\re\left(\ell^{(j)}\left(s_{1}-\frac{1}{2},\dots,s_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)\right\}}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+||\im(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu})||_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\right)

is uniformly valid for
\re(s_{j})>\frac{1}{2}-\delta_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}
.

Then, there is a polynomial
Q_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}}(T)\in\mathbb{R}[T]
of degree at most
\mu-\mathrm{rank}(\{\ell^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n})
and a positive real number
\theta=\theta(\mathcal{L}_{h},\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu})
such that for
X\geq 1
, we have

S(X;\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu})=X^{\frac{\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{\mu}}{2}}(Q_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}}(\log X)+O(X^{-\theta})).

.

[

(2)

(1,\dots,1)\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and

\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})-\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathrm{card}(\{\ell^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n})-\mathrm{rank}(\{\ell^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n}).

Then, for
X\geq 3
, the polynomial
Q_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}}(T)
satisfies

Q_{\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}}(\log X)=H(1/2,\dots,1/2)X^{-\frac{\beta_{1}+\cdots+\beta_{\mu}}{2}}I(X;\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu})+O((\log X)^{\rho-1}),

where
\rho=n-\mathrm{rank}(\{\ell^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,

I(X;\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}):=\int_{\mathcal{A}(X;\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu})}\frac{dz_{1}\cdots dz_{n}}{\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{1-\ell^{(j)}(1/2,\dots,1/2)}},

and

\mathcal{A}(X;\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu}):=\left\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\in[1,\infty)\,:\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{\ell^{(j)}(e_{i})}\leq X^{\beta_{i}},i=1,\dots,n\right\}.

Remark 3.3
.

The original statements of Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
that can be found in
[
dlB01
]
allow for more freedom,
where the associated Dirichlet sum
F(s_{1},\dots,s_{\mu})
is absolutely convergent in the set
\re(s_{j})>\alpha_{j}
for some
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\mu})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mu}
.

We have simplified the above statements to take
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\mu})=(1/2,\dots,1/2)
, which suffices for our purposes. In addition, most of our applications will use
(\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{\mu})=(1,\dots,1)
.

4.
The constant in Conjecture
1.1

We begin with the problem of computing the variance of the
k
-fold divisor function weighted by the quadratic character modulo
d
; namely, the problem discussed in Conjecture
1.1
. Our goal in this section is to predict the arithmetic constant appearing in equation (
6
). Our strategy is to compute the variance in a restricted range of the parameters, so that, at least conjecturally, only “diagonal” terms contribute.

Specifically, we begin with the variance

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{r\in(y,2y]}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{r}(n)\Bigg)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{r}(n)\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m\leq x}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(nm),

where we recall that the expectation
\mathbb{E}^{*}
is taken over positive fundamental discriminants.

The “diagonal” terms are those where the expectation
\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(nm)
should be large. Since
\chi_{r}
is a quadratic character, we expect this to happen precisely when
nm
is a perfect square. Thus the diagonal terms
D
are given by

D=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(nm).

If
nm
is a square, then
\chi_{r}(nm)
is
1
if
(nm,r)=1
, and otherwise
0
. By
[
CFK+05
, equation (3.1.17)]
, the expectation in this case is given by

(16)

\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(nm)\sim\begin{cases}\prod_{p\mid nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}&\mathrm{if}\,nm=\square,\\
o(1)&\mathrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

so that

D\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p|nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}.

In
[
CFK+05
]
(3.1.17) is written as a sum over
1\leq r\leq y
and not
y\leq r\leq 2y
, but these are equivalent.
Note that it is crucial that the average is taken over fundamental discriminants and
not
over all values
r
.

The remaining “off-diagonal” terms are those where the expectation should be small. If we write

OD=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm\neq\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(mn),

then under the generalized Riemann Hypothesis, if
y\geq x^{1/k}
,

OD\ll x^{2}y^{-1/2}\log^{C^{\prime}}x

for some absolute
C^{\prime}
, so for
y\geq x^{2}\log^{C}x
the variance in (
15
) grows like

(17)

\mathrm{Var}_{r\in(y,2y]}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{r}(n)\Bigg)\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p\mid nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1},

which we would now like to evaluate using multiple Dirichlet series techniques. Ultimately this will give us an output of the form
A_{k}c^{2k^{2}+k-2}
if
\log x=c\log y
, and the constant
A_{k}
in this output is precisely the arithmetic constant we would like to compute.

The diagonal terms
D
admit a Dirichlet series

\displaystyle F_{k}(s_{1},s_{2}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{nm=\square}\frac{d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)}{n^{s_{1}}m^{s_{2}}}\prod_{p\mid nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h}\prod_{p\mid h}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\sum_{n_{1}\dots n_{k}m_{1}\dots m_{k}=h^{2}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{1}}m_{1}^{s_{2}}\cdots m_{k}^{s_{2}}}.

Our goal is to estimate (
17
) from
F_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})
. To do this, we apply the results of de la Bretèche that are stated in Section
3
. Observe from (
18
) that
F_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})
is absolutely convergent in
\re(s_{1})>\frac{1}{2}
and
\re(s_{2})>\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, writing

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2}):=

\displaystyle F_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\zeta(2s_{1})^{-\binom{k+1}{2}}\zeta(2s_{2})^{-\binom{k+1}{2}}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})^{-k^{2}},

we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s_{1}}}\right)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s_{2}}}\right)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}\right)^{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(\tau_{s_{1},\dots,s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{2}}(p^{2})+\tau_{s_{1},\dots s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{2}}(p^{4})+\cdots\right)\right),

where we define

(19)

\tau_{s_{1},\dots,s_{j}}(n):=\sum_{n_{1}\cdots n_{j}=n}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

We remark that
\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})
converges absolutely to an analytic function in the set
\re(s_{1})>\frac{1}{4}
and
\re(s_{2})>\frac{1}{4}
. In particular, for all
\epsilon>0
,
|\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})|\leq|\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/4+\epsilon,1/4+\epsilon)|\ll_{\epsilon}1
.

One could similarly define

\displaystyle H_{k}(s_{1},s_{2}):=

\displaystyle F_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})(2s_{1}-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(2s_{2}-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)^{k^{2}},

which is the function in the statement of Theorem
3.1
, where
\delta_{1}=\frac{1}{4}
and the family of forms is
\mathcal{L}=\{\ell^{(j_{1},j_{2})}(s_{1},s_{2})1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k\}
given by

(20)

\ell^{(j_{1},j_{2})}(s_{1},s_{2})=\begin{cases}2s_{1}&1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k,\\
s_{1}+s_{2}&1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k,\\
2s_{2}&k<j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k.\end{cases}

Notice that

H_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})=\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})(2s_{1}-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\zeta(2s_{1})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(2s_{2}-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\zeta(2s_{2})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1)^{k^{2}}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})^{k^{2}}.

The bound in Theorem
3.1
, part (3) is then satisfied because

\displaystyle H_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})(\zeta(2s_{1})(2s_{1}-1))^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(\zeta(2s_{2})(2s_{2}-1))^{\binom{k+1}{2}}(\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})(s_{1}+s_{2}-1))^{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll((1+|\mathrm{Im}(2s_{1})|)^{1-\mathrm{Re}(2s_{1})/3+\epsilon})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}((1+|\mathrm{Im}(2s_{2})|)^{1-\mathrm{Re}(2s_{2})/3+\epsilon})^{\binom{k+1}{2}}

\displaystyle\times((1+|\mathrm{Im}(s_{1}+s_{2})|)^{1-\mathrm{Re}(s_{1}+s_{2})/3+\epsilon})^{k^{2}}.

Note that

\tau_{1/2,\dots,1/2}(p^{2h})=\frac{d_{2k}(p^{2h})}{p^{h}}=\binom{2h+2k-1}{2h}\frac{1}{p^{h}},

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/2,1/2):=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2k^{2}+k}\left(1+\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(-1+\sum_{h=0}^{\infty}\binom{2h+2k-1}{2h}\frac{1}{p^{h}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2k^{2}+k}\frac{1}{p+1}\left(1+\frac{p}{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}\right)\right).

Theorem
3.1
implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p\mid nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=x(Q_{2k^{2}+k-2}(\log x)+O(x^{-\theta})).

Our goal is to apply Theorem
3.2
to get an estimate of the main term. Thus, we need to verify that conditions (1) and (2) are satisfied. The variables
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
individually can be written as a function of the linear forms
2s_{1},2s_{2},
and
s_{1}+s_{2}
, so condition (1) holds. For condition (2), first notice that

(1,1)\in\mathrm{Vect}(\{\ell^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{n})=\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Also, the family of forms
\mathcal{L}
has
2k^{2}+k
elements and rank
2
. We need to verify that there is no proper subfamily
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{L}
such that
(1,1)\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and

(21)

\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})-\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=2k^{2}+k-2.

The only possibility for (
21
) to be satisfied with a proper family is for
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})<2
. If
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=0
, then
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\varnothing
and (
21
) does not hold. Thus
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=1
, which is only possible if all the linear forms in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are of the same type. Since
(1,1)\in\mathrm{Vect}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
, the linear forms in
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
are all given by
s_{1}+s_{2}
. However, there are
k^{2}
forms of type
s_{1}+s_{2}
, making it impossible for
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
to satisfy (
21
).

By Theorem
3.2
, the polynomial
Q_{2k^{2}+k-2}
is given by

Q_{2k^{2}+k-2}(\log x)\sim\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/2,1/2)x^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{A}(x)}dz_{(1,1)}\cdots dz_{(2k,2k)},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x)=\left\{z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),\,1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k\,:\,\right.

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}^{2}\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq x,

\displaystyle\left.\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\prod_{k<j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}^{2}\leq x\right\}.

Our next step is evaluating the integral over
\mathcal{A}(x)
, which we accomplish by making use of the following two lemmas; both of these lemmas are proven using elementary calculus and induction on
n
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
m\geq 0
be integers. Then

I_{m,n}(Y):=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}\left[\log\left(\frac{Y}{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}\right)\right]^{m}\frac{dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}}{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}=\frac{m!}{(m+n)!}(\log Y)^{m+n}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Then

J_{n}(Y):=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\\
x_{1}\cdots x_{n}\leq Y\end{subarray}}dx_{1}\dots dx_{n}=(-1)^{n}+Y\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1-j}}{j!}(\log Y)^{j}.

Due to the particular shape of the linear forms in our problem,
I_{k}(X;1,1)
is given by the volume of
\mathcal{A}(x)
. We proceed to compute this volume. We can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A})=

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq x\end{subarray}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k\leq j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),k<j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{k<j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2}\end{subarray}}dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2}\end{subarray}}dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\right)^{2}dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}.

Applying Lemma
4.2
to the inner integral with
n=\binom{k+1}{2}
and
Y=\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A})=

\displaystyle x\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}\frac{(-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}-1-j}}{j!2^{j}}\left[\log\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)\right]^{j}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\frac{dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}+O\left(x^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{\left(\left(\binom{k+1}{2}-1\right)!\right)^{2}2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq x\end{subarray}}\left[\log\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)\right]^{k^{2}+k-2}

\displaystyle\times\frac{dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}+O\left(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right).

For the last equality we used that the highest power of
\log x
will come from the highest power in the sum over
j
, which can be deduced from the statement of Lemma
4.1
.
Applying Lemma
4.1
again, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A})=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{\left(\left(\binom{k+1}{2}-1\right)!\right)^{2}2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\frac{(k^{2}+k-2)!}{(2k^{2}+k-2)!}\left(\log x\right)^{2k^{2}+k-2}+O\left(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{x}{2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\binom{k^{2}+k-2}{\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}-1}\frac{1}{(2k^{2}+k-2)!}\left(\log x\right)^{2k^{2}+k-2}+O\left(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right).

Thus we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p|nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/2,1/2)\frac{1}{2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\binom{k^{2}+k-2}{\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}-1}\frac{1}{(2k^{2}+k-2)!}x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-2}

\displaystyle+O(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/2,1/2)\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log y}\right)x(\log y)^{2k^{2}+k-2}+O(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}).

The arithmetic constant in this case, to adjust from the “function field” answer of
\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log y}\right)x(\log y)^{2k^{2}+k-2}
, is
2\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/2,1/2)
, or

2\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2k^{2}+k}\frac{1}{p+1}\left(1+\frac{p}{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}\right)\right).

The factor of
2
is different from the Euler product contribution, as it arises from the integral computation and not from the convergent factor
\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})
. It is also a somewhat unexpected discrepancy from the function field case; for example, no discrepancy of this form is present in the unitary case! This discrepancy is discussed in more detail in Subsection
7.1
.

5.
The constant in Conjecture
1.2

In this section we study a variation of the problem described in the previous section. Instead of considering the
k
-fold divisor function weighted by quadratic characters modulo
r
, we restrict
r
to be a prime, so that the quadratic character weighting is essentially summing the
k
-fold divisor function only along quadratic residues modulo a prime
p
. Precisely, we want to understand the variance of the sum
\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}
defined in Conjecture
1.2
. Recall that the condition
n\equiv\square\,(\mathrm{mod}\,p)
for
p\nmid n
can be codified by
\frac{1+\chi_{p}(n)}{2}
for
p\nmid n
, and therefore

\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{S}_{d_{k};x}\right\rangle\sim\frac{1}{2y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<p\leq 2y\end{subarray}}\log p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n).

Thus, we consider the weighted variance

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{p\in(y,2y]}^{w}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
n\equiv\square\,(\mathrm{mod}\,p)\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\Bigg)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4y}\sum_{y<p\leq 2y}\log p\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\nmid n\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{p}(n)\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4y}\sum_{n,m\leq x}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<p\leq 2y\\
p\nmid nm\end{subarray}}\log p\chi_{p}(nm).

As in the previous section, we will ultimately only consider the setting where
y\geq x^{2}\log^{C}x
, so that we may discard the off-diagonal terms. Note that in this range
p>nm
for all terms in the sum, so the condition that
p\nmid nm
is immaterial and we can discard it.

The diagonal terms come from the case where
nm
is a perfect square, in which case
\chi_{p}(nm)=1
always. Thus by the prime number theorem, the diagonal terms are

\displaystyle D:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\frac{1}{y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<p\leq 2y\end{subarray}}\log p\sim\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m).

The off-diagonal terms are given by

\displaystyle OD:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m\leq x\\
nm\not=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\mathbb{E}_{\begin{subarray}{c}y<p\leq 2y\end{subarray}}\log p\chi_{p}(nm)\ll x^{2}y^{-1/2}\log^{C^{\prime}}x,

under the generalized Riemann Hypothesis for
y\geq x^{1/k}
. Thus for
y\geq x^{2}\log^{C}x
, the variance is asymptotic to the diagonal contribution.

The diagonal terms admit the Dirichlet series

\tilde{F}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2}):=\sum_{nm=\square}\frac{d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)}{n^{s_{1}}m^{s_{2}}}=\sum_{h}\sum_{n_{1}\cdots n_{k}m_{1}\cdots m_{k}=h^{2}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{k}^{s_{2}}m_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots m_{k}^{s_{2}}}.

Observe that
\tilde{F}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})
is absolutely convergent in
\re(s_{1})>\frac{1}{2}
and
\re(s_{2})>\frac{1}{2}
. Writing

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2}):=\tilde{F}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\zeta(2s_{1})^{-\binom{k+1}{2}}\zeta(2s_{2})^{-\binom{k+1}{2}}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})^{-k^{2}},

we have that
\tilde{\mathcal{H}_{k}}(s_{1},s_{2})
is very similar to the quantity
\mathcal{H}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})
in the previous section, given by

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s_{1}}}\right)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2s_{2}}}\right)^{\binom{k+1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}\right)^{k^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\tau_{s_{1},\dots,s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{2}}(p^{2})+\tau_{s_{1},\dots,s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{2}}(p^{4})+\cdots\right).

We proceed as in Section
4
, applying Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
with the linear forms family (
20
). This eventually gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq n,m\leq x\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)=

\displaystyle 2\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(1/2,1/2)\gamma_{k}\left(\frac{\log x}{\log y}\right)x(\log y)^{2k^{2}+k-2}+O(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{k}(1/2,1/2)=

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2k^{2}+k}\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\right)^{-2k}\right).

As before, the factor of
2
is different from the Euler product contribution, as it arises from the integral computation. This discrepancy is discussed in more detail in Subsection
7.1
.

6.
The constant in Conjecture
1.3

Our goal is to compute the variance

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S})

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S}(\theta)-\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S}\rangle)^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

in a range of
x
and
K
such that this computation is feasible in the integer case, in order to compare to the general conjecture in the function field case and thus predict the constant in (
11
).
Here
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S}(\theta)
and
\langle\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S}\rangle
are defined in (
9
) and (
10
) respectively.

We begin by establishing the range of
x
and
K
that we will use in this section. Note that for two ideals
\mathfrak{a}=(a_{1}+a_{2}i)
and
\mathfrak{b}=(b_{1}+b_{2}i)
with norms bounded by
x
, where
a_{1}+a_{2}i
and
b_{1}+b_{2}i
are generators in the first quadrant so that
0\leq\mathrm{arg}(a_{1}+a_{2}i)\leq\mathrm{arg}(b_{1}+b_{2}i)<\pi/2
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{arg}(b_{1}+b_{2}i)-\mathrm{arg}(a_{1}+a_{2}i)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{arg}((b_{1}+b_{2}i)(a_{1}-a_{2}i))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{arg}(c+di),

where
c+di\in\mathbb{Q}[i]
has norm
N(c+di)\leq x^{2}
, so that
|c|\leq x
and
|d|\leq x
. Moreover by the restrictions on the argument of
a_{1}+a_{2}i
and that of
b_{1}+b_{2}i
, we have
0\leq\mathrm{arg}(c+di)<\pi/2
(which also implies that
c\neq 0
). Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{arg}(c+di)

\displaystyle=\arctan(d/c).

If
d
is
0
, then
\mathrm{arg}(c+di)=0
; note that this occurs precisely when
\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}i}{b_{1}+b_{2}i}\in\mathbb{Q}
, which by slight abuse of notation we will write as
\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Otherwise,
d/c
is at least
1/x
. As

,
\arctan(1/x)\to 1/x
, so in particular
\arctan(1/x)\gg 1/x
. Thus
\mathrm{arg}(c+di)\gg x^{-1}
unless
\frac{\mathfrak{a}}{\mathfrak{b}}\in\mathbb{Q}
.

Thus taking, say,
K\gg x\log x
, if
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{b}
both appear in the sum for
\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S}(\theta)
for the same
\theta
, then
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
. These are the “diagonal” terms in this setting.

We now compute the variance when
K\gg x\log x
. Expanding the square, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S})=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})(\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}-\tfrac{1}{K})(\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}-\tfrac{1}{K})\mathrm{d}\theta.

If
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}=\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}
, so
\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}=\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}
. If
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
|\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}-\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}|\gg 1/x>1/K
, so by our choice of
K
,
\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}=0
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S})=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})(\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}-\tfrac{1}{K})^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\not\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})(-\tfrac{1}{K}\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{a}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}-\tfrac{1}{K}\mathds{1}_{\theta_{\mathfrak{b}}\in I_{K}(\theta)}+\tfrac{1}{K^{2}})\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})\left(\frac{1}{K}-\frac{1}{K^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{K^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\not\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})-\frac{1}{K^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b}).

We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\text{ ideal}\\
N(\mathfrak{a})\leq x\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})\ll x(\log x)^{\ell-1},

so that the second sum is
O(x^{2}K^{-2}(\log x)^{2\ell-2})
. When
K\gg x\log x
, this term will be negligible compared to the size of the main term in Conjecture
1.3
, so we discard it. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(\mathcal{N}_{d_{\ell},K;x}^{S})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\text{ ideal}\\
N(\mathfrak{a})\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2}\leq\sqrt{x/N(\mathfrak{a})}\\
(r_{1},r_{2})=1\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(r_{1}\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(r_{2}\mathfrak{a})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1},N_{2}\leq x\\
N_{1}/(N_{1},N_{2})=\square\\
N_{2}/(N_{1},N_{2})=\square\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\text{ ideal}\\
N(\mathfrak{a})=(N_{1},N_{2})\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}\sqrt{N_{1}/(N_{1},N_{2})})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}\sqrt{N_{2}/(N_{1},N_{2})}).

Let
\mathbb{D}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the subset of pairs
(N_{1},N_{2})
such that
N_{1}/(N_{1},N_{2})
and
N_{2}/(N_{1},N_{2})
are both perfect squares.
We define
f(N_{1},N_{2})
as the (complicated) inner sum, so that

f(N_{1},N_{2})=\mathds{1}_{\mathbb{D}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\text{ ideal}\\
N(\mathfrak{a})=(N_{1},N_{2})\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}\sqrt{N_{1}/(N_{1},N_{2})})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}\sqrt{N_{2}/(N_{1},N_{2})}).

With this definition in hand, we can now consider the Dirichlet series

F_{\ell}(s_{1},s_{2}):=\sum_{N_{1},N_{2}\geq 1}\frac{f(N_{1},N_{2})}{N_{1}^{s_{1}}N_{2}^{s_{2}}}.

This series is multiplicative, so let us look at the
p
th component for primes
p=2
,
p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, and
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
.

When
p=2
, we have (for
\mathfrak{p}=(1+i)
)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\equiv k\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{p}^{j})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{2^{js_{1}+ks_{2}}}=1+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}+\frac{(\ell+1)\ell}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2^{2s_{1}}}+\frac{1}{2^{2s_{2}}}\right)+\cdots.

When
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j,k\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{d_{\ell}(p^{j/2})d_{\ell}(p^{k/2})}{p^{js_{1}+ks_{2}}}=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}\frac{d_{\ell}(p^{j})d_{\ell}(p^{k})}{p^{2js_{1}+2ks_{2}}}=1+\frac{\ell}{p^{2s_{1}}}+\frac{\ell}{p^{2s_{2}}}+\cdots.

When
p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, we write
(p)=\mathfrak{p}_{1}\mathfrak{p}_{2}
for
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2}
prime ideals in
\mathbb{Z}[i]
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\equiv k\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{js_{1}+ks_{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[i]\\
N(\mathfrak{a})=p^{\min\{j,k\}}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}p^{j-\min\{j,k\}})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a}p^{k-\min\{j,k\}})

\displaystyle=1+\frac{2\ell^{2}}{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}+\ell^{2}\left(\frac{1}{p^{2s_{1}}}+\frac{1}{p^{2s_{2}}}\right)+\cdots.

The Dirichlet series
F_{\ell}(s_{1},s_{2})
is analytic for
\mathrm{Re}(s_{1}),\mathrm{Re}(s_{2})>\frac{1}{2}
, and has poles when
\mathrm{Re}(s_{1})=\frac{1}{2}
and when
\mathrm{Re}(s_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
. We consider

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(s_{1},s_{2}):=

\displaystyle F_{\ell}(s_{1},s_{2})\zeta(2s_{1})^{-\binom{\ell+1}{2}}\zeta(2s_{2})^{-\binom{\ell+1}{2}}\zeta(s_{1}+s_{2})^{-\ell^{2}},

which converges for
\mathrm{Re}(s_{i})>\frac{1}{4}
.
We are now in a position to apply Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
. In fact, the situation is very similar to that of Section
4
, as we also work with the family of linear forms
\mathcal{L}
given by (
20
). The difference in the final result lies in the evaluation of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(1/2,1/2)
. To do this, we again take advantage of the multiplicativity and proceed to consider the different components for primes
p=2
,
p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, and
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
.

For
p=2
, the contribution is given by

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\equiv k\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{p}^{j})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{p}^{k})}{2^{(j+k)/2}}=\frac{1}{2^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\equiv k\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\binom{j+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{k+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\frac{1}{2^{j/2}}\frac{1}{2^{k/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}}\Bigg(\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\binom{j+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\frac{1}{2^{j/2}}\Bigg)^{2}+\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
j\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\binom{j+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\frac{1}{2^{j/2}}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+\sqrt{2})^{2\ell}+(2-\sqrt{2})^{2\ell}}{2^{2\ell^{2}+\ell+1}}.

For primes
p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{d_{\ell}(p^{j})d_{\ell}(p^{k})}{p^{j+k}}=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\binom{j+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\Bigg)^{2}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}-\ell}.

Finally, for primes
p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)
, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\equiv k\,(\mathrm{mod}\,2)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{\frac{j+k}{2}}}\sum_{r=0}^{\min\{j,k\}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{r}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{\min\{j,k\}-r})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{p}_{1}^{r+\frac{|k-j|}{2}}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{\frac{|k+j|}{2}-r})

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\Bigg(2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j+m}}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{j-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}

(23)

\displaystyle\times\binom{r+m+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{j+m-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\sum_{r=0}^{j}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}^{2}\binom{j-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}^{2}\Bigg),

where
2m=|k-j|
.

Consider the first sum in (
23
). We reparametrize by first replacing
m
by
n=j+m
and then by replacing
j
by
t=j-r
to get

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,t=0\\
r+t<n\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}.

The sum over
r,t
remains unchanged if we make the substitutions
r\leftrightarrow n-r
and
t\leftrightarrow n-t
, except that the limits of the sum will be that
r,t
range from
0
to
n
with the constraint that
r+t>n
. If we make this change for only one copy of the sum and then combine the two copies, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,t=0\\
r+t\not=n\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-t+\ell-1}{\ell-1}.

The remaining portion of the sum in (
23
) can be expressed as precisely those terms where
r+t=n
by writing
n
in place of
j
and
t
in place of
n-r
.
Thus (
23
) is equal to

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\right)^{2}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\binom{n+2\ell-1}{2\ell-1}^{2},

where we have applied the identity

(24)

\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}\binom{n-r+\ell-1}{\ell-1}=\binom{n+2\ell-1}{2\ell-1},

which is a version of the Chu–Vandermonde identity (see Equation (25) in
[
Knu97
, 1.2.6]
) and it holds for all
r,n,
and
\ell
.

Putting all of this together, we finally get

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(1/2,1/2)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2+\sqrt{2})^{2\ell}+(2-\sqrt{2})^{2\ell}}{2^{2\ell^{2}+\ell+1}}\prod_{p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}-\ell}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\Bigg[\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+2\ell-1}{2\ell-1}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\Bigg].

The above expression can be simplified further, since

\displaystyle\prod_{p\equiv 3\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}-\ell}\prod_{p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\pi}{8b^{2}}\right)^{2\ell^{2}-\ell}\prod_{p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{4\ell^{2}},

where
b
is the Landau–Ramanujan constant defined in (
12
) and we have made use of the Euler product expression for
L(1,\chi_{-4})=\pi/4
. Thus we finally obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{K}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}\text{ ideals}\\
N(\mathfrak{a}),N(\mathfrak{b})\leq x\\
\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{b}\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{a})d_{\ell}(\mathfrak{b})=2\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(1/2,1/2)\gamma_{\ell}\left(\frac{\log x}{2\log K}\right)\frac{x}{K}(2\log K)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell-2}+O(x(\log x)^{2\ell^{2}+\ell-3}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\ell}(1/2,1/2)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi}{8b^{2}}\right)^{\ell(2\ell-1)}\frac{(2+\sqrt{2})^{2\ell}+(2-\sqrt{2})^{2\ell}}{2^{2\ell^{2}+\ell+1}}\prod_{p\equiv 1\,(\mathrm{mod}\,4)}\Bigg[\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{4\ell^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+2\ell-1}{2\ell-1}^{2}\frac{1}{p^{n}}\Bigg].

Once again, the factor of
2
is different from the rest of the constant, as it arises from the integral computation. This discrepancy is discussed in more detail in the following section.

7.
Discrepancies from the function field case and factors not present in the unitary case

We seem to be noticing two types of discrepancy between
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
and the heuristic answers in the integer case (beyond the arithmetic constant given by an Euler product).

7.1.
A factor of
2

Here we will discuss the factor of
2
discrepancy appearing in all of the problems we study. This effect is already visible in the case when
k=1
, so for simplicity we will restrict to this case. We will also only discuss the variance of the
k
-fold divisor function weighted by
\chi_{r}(n)
, although the same factor is present for the same reasons in the other cases.

When
k=1
, many of the sums we get in the function field case and in the integer case are roughly the same. In particular, in the computation using de la Bretèche’s estimates of multiple Dirichlet series given in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, the function
\gamma_{1}(c)
is given by
X^{-1}\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A}(X,X,X))
where

\mathcal{A}(X,X,X)=\{1\leq y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}<\infty:y_{1}y_{2}^{2}\leq X,y_{1}y_{3}^{2}\leq X\}.

As computed in Section
4
, the volume of
\mathcal{A}(X,X,X)
is
\sim X\log X
.

Considering the analogous volume in the function field case, however, we get a smaller answer by a factor of
\tfrac{1}{2}
. Here we would consider

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(N,N,N)=\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]\text{ monic}:|f_{1}f_{2}^{2}|=q^{N},|f_{1}f_{3}^{2}|=q^{N}\},

where for
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
,
|f|:=q^{\deg(f)}
.
Note that the constraint that
y_{1}y_{2}^{2}\leq X
has become the constraint that
|f_{1}f_{2}^{2}|=q^{N}
; in the large
q
limit, almost all polynomials of degree
\leq N
have degree exactly
N
, so this is equivalent.

However, in this case, we have

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(N,N,N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\leq N\\
d_{1}+2d_{2}=N\\
d_{1}+2d_{3}=N\end{subarray}}q^{d_{1}+d_{2}+d_{3}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d_{1},d_{2}\leq N\\
d_{1}+2d_{2}=N\end{subarray}}q^{N}=\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}\right\rceil q^{N}.

The parity condition on
d_{1}
is the source of the final factor of
\frac{1}{2}
in the computation of
\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}(c)
, which does not appear in the integer case.

Notably, this discrepancy is not present in problems with unitary symmetry types.

7.2.
The choice of interval

Another type of discrepancy comes from the fact that the answer in the integer case depends on the interval chosen for the variance, not just the size of the interval. To see what this means in practice, let us consider the problem of the variance of
\sum_{Ax<n\leq Bx}d_{k}(n)\chi_{r}(n)
, as
r
varies over fundamental discriminants. Two natural versions of this problem to consider are when
A=0
and
B=1
, or when
A=1
and
B=2
. Notably, there is heuristic and numeric evidence that the answer in the integer case depends on the choice of
A
and
B
(rather than depending only on
B-A
, for example).

We begin by expanding

\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n\leq Bx\\
(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{r}(n)\Bigg)^{2}=D+ND,

where
D
denotes diagonal terms and
ND
denotes off-diagonal terms.

The diagonal terms are given by

D=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n,m\leq Bx\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(nm).

If
nm
is a square, then by a result of Jutila (see for example,
[
CFK+05
, equation (3.1.21)]
),

D\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n,m\leq Bx\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p|nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}.

Meanwhile the off-diagonal terms are

OD=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n,m\leq Bx\\
nm\neq\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\mathbb{E}^{*}_{y<r\leq 2y}\chi_{r}(nm).

Under GRH, if
y\geq x^{1/k}
,

OD\ll x^{2}y^{-1/2}\log^{C}x

for some absolute
C
, so for
y\geq x^{2}\log^{C^{\prime}}x
the variance grows like

\mathrm{Var}_{r\in(y,2y]}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n\leq Bx\\
(n,r)=1\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)\chi_{r}(n)\Bigg)\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n,m\leq Bx\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p|nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1},

which we can evaluate using multiple Dirichlet series techniques and Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
as in Section
4
. This gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Ax<n,m\leq Bx\\
nm=\square\end{subarray}}d_{k}(n)d_{k}(m)\prod_{p\mid nm}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=x(Q_{2k^{2}+k-2}(\log x)+O(x^{-\theta})),

and Theorem
3.2
then tells us that

Q_{2k^{2}+k-2}(\log x)\sim\mathcal{H}_{k}(1/2,1/2)x^{-1}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{[A,B]}(x)}dz_{(1,1)}\cdots dz_{(2k,2k)},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{[A,B]}(x)=\left\{z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),\,1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k\,:\,\right.

\displaystyle Ax\leq\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}^{2}\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\leq Bx,

\displaystyle\left.Ax\leq\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\prod_{k<j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}^{2}\leq Bx\right\}.

The volume of
\mathcal{A}_{[A,B]}(x)
will depend on
A
and
B
. To compute it, we proceed as in Section
4
, getting that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A}_{[A,B]})=

\displaystyle\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[Ax,Bx]\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in\left[\left(\frac{Ax}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k\leq j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2},\left(\frac{Bx}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k\leq j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2}\right]\end{subarray}}dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\Big)^{2}dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}.

Applying Lemma
4.2
to the inner integral with
n=\binom{k+1}{2}
and
Y=\left(\frac{Bx}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)^{1/2}
and substracting the case where
B
is replaced by
A
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A}_{[A,B]})=

\displaystyle x\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[Ax,Bx]\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\binom{k+1}{2}-1}\frac{(-1)^{\binom{k+1}{2}-1-j}}{j!2^{j}}\left\{\left[B^{1/2}\log\left(\frac{Bx}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)\right]^{j}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.-\left[A^{1/2}\log\left(\frac{Ax}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)\right]^{j}\right\}\right)^{2}\frac{dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}+O\left(x^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\sqrt{B}-\sqrt{A})^{2}x}{\left(\left(\binom{k+1}{2}-1\right)!\right)^{2}2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[1,\infty),1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k\\
\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}\in[Ax,Bx]\end{subarray}}\left[\log\left(\frac{x}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}\right)\right]^{k^{2}+k-2}

\displaystyle\times\frac{dz_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}{\prod_{1\leq j_{1}\leq k<j_{2}\leq 2k}z_{(j_{1},j_{2})}}+O\left(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right).

For the last step, we took the highest power of
\log x
in the sum over
j
, which has the largest contribution by the statement of Lemma
4.1
. Finally, applying Lemma
4.1
again, and keeping the terms with the highest power of
\log x
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal{A}_{[A,B]})=

\displaystyle\frac{(\sqrt{B}-\sqrt{A})^{2}x}{\left(\left(\binom{k+1}{2}-1\right)!\right)^{2}2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\frac{(k^{2}+k-2)!}{(2k^{2}+k-2)!}\left(\log x\right)^{2k^{2}+k-2}+O\left(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\sqrt{B}-\sqrt{A})^{2}x}{2^{k^{2}+k-2}}\binom{k^{2}+k-2}{\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}-1}\frac{1}{(2k^{2}+k-2)!}\left(\log x\right)^{2k^{2}+k-2}+O\left(x(\log x)^{2k^{2}+k-3}\right).

Thus if
A=0
and
B=1
, we get a main term of
2x\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}\left(\log x\right)
, whereas if
A=1
and
B=2
, we get a main term of
(\sqrt{2}-1)^{2}2x\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}\left(\log x\right)
, and if
A=1/2
and
B=3/2
, we get a main term of
(\sqrt{3}-1)^{2}x\gamma_{d_{k},2}^{S}\left(\log x\right)
. This discrepancy cannot be present in the function field case in the limit as
q\to\infty
, since as
q\to\infty
almost all polynomials of degree at most
x
have degree exactly
x
.

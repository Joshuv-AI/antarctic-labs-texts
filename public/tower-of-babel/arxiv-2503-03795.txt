Another inequality inspired by Erdős
Barbora Batíková, Tomáš J. Kepka and Petr C. Němec

Abstract
. One less common inequality in positive integers is carefully examined.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
11D75

In 1845, J. Bertrand (
[
2
]
, pg. 129) conjectured such a statement: ”For every integer
n
greater than 6 there always exists at least one prime number contained between
n-2
and
\frac{n}{2}
.” Bertrand himself noticed that his proposition (postulate) was true for all
n
such that
7\leq n<6\cdot 10^{6}
and believed it remained true for all
n\geq 7
. Seven years later, P. L. Tchebychef published a complete proof of the conjecture (
[
5
]
) and various simplifications of this proof appeared through passing years. It was P. Erdös in 1932 (
[
4
]
) who came up with an elegant and simple proof based on different ideas – prime decompositions of binomial coefficients (a thorough exposition of all this can be found in
[
3
]
). The approach of Erdös may be considered the standard one nowadays. Two remarks could turn out to be of some interest, however. Firstly, a formally weaker form of the postulate is shown in
[
4
]
. Namely, that for every positive integer
n
there is at least one prime
p
with
n<p\leq 2n
. Of course, all odd primes
p
are of the form
p=2n-1
,
n\geq 2
, whence it is not immediately visible why the lenient form should imply the more severe one. On the other hand, the original proof by Erdös can easily be completed to do the work. Secondly, the proof can be definitely viewed as elementary, but no way calculus-free. On the other hand, if some assertion is formulated within elementary arithmetic, it would be nice to have a really elementary proof. Nonetheless, we are convinced that the methods developed by Erdös can be transmogrified to yield demonstration(s) of the Bertrand postulate if not inside then at least close to Elementary Function Arithmetic – one of the weakest fragments of arithmetic.
In our effort to find such a proof we came across several unusual inequalities in positive integers. One of them (the inequality (1) below) was carefully examined in
[
1
]
. Another kindred inequality (the inequality (2)) is treated in the present short note. Purely from methodological and didactical reasons, we include also a solution of these two inequalities using elementary calculus.

1.
Preliminaries

In the sequel,
n
stands for a positive integer. We denote by

(a)

z(n)
the largest integer such that
3z(n)<2n
;

(b)

m(n)
the largest integer such that
m(n)^{2}\leq 2n
;

(c)

r(n)
the least non-negative integer such that
n\leq 2^{r(n)}
;

(d)

x(n)=z(n)-(r(n)+1)m(n)
;

(e)

y(n)=2^{2n-2z(n)-m(n)+2}-\cdot n^{m(n)-1}
;

(f)

c(n)=2n-2z(n)+2
;

(g)

a(n)=2^{c(n)-m(n)}
;

(h)

b(n)=n^{m(n)-1}
.

In
[
1
]
, we examined the inequality

(1)

z(n)-(r(n)+1)m(n)<0\,.

i.e., the inequality
x(n)<0
) and we proved the following result (and a bit more):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Then
x(n)=0
if and only if
n\in\{436,451,529,545,546\}
and
x(n)<0
if and only if either
n\leq 435
or
n=450
or
513\leq n\leq 528
.

Now, our aim is to solve (in positive integers) the inequality

(2)

2^{2n-2z(n)-m(n)+2}-n^{m(n)-1}<0

and to find all positive integers
n
for which the equality holds. In other words, we want to determine the sign of
y(n)
. The answer is given in Theorem
2.7
.

Lemma 1.3
.

c(n)-m(n)\geq 2
for every positive integer
n
and
c(n)-m(n)=2
only for
n=2
.

Proof.

With regard to
1.2
, we can assume that
n\geq 10
. Then
2n\geq 20
,
z(n)\geq 6
,
2z(n)\geq 12
,
2z(n)^{2}\geq 12z(n)\geq 10z(n)+12>10z(n)+8
,
(z(n)-1)^{2}=z(n)^{2}-2z(n)+1=\frac{2z(n)^{2}-4z(n)+2}{2}>\frac{10z(n)+8-4z(n)+2}{2}=3z(n)+5>3(z(n)+1)\geq 2n
. Now,
(z(n)-1)^{2}>2n
implies
z(n)-1>m(n)
and
z(n)-m(n)-1\geq 1
. On the other hand,
2n-3z(n)\geq 1
and, consequently,
c(n)-m(n)-3=2n-2z(n)-m(n)-1=(2n-3z(n))+(z(n)-m(n)-1)\geq 2
. Thus
c(n)-m(n)\geq 5
.∎

One observes readily that the sequences
z(n),m(n),r(n)
are non-decreasing, the sequence
b(n)
is increasing and
y(n)=a(n)-b(n)
. Further,
c(1)=c(2)=4
,
c(3k)=c(3k+1)=c(3k+2)=2k+4
for every
k\geq 2
,
c(n+3k)=c(n)+2k
and the sequence
c(n)
is non-decreasing.

2.
An arithmetically pure solution of (2)

Observation 2.1
.

In order to facilitate the calculation of

(i) For every positive integer

(

(

(ii) For every positive integer

(iii) For every positive integer

(iv) We have

(v) Clearly, if

. Further, if
n
is
a positive integer and

then
x(n_{1})\leq x(n_{2})=x(n_{1})+z(n_{2})-z(n_{1})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u,v,n
be positive integers such that
u\leq n\leq v
and
m(u)=m(v)=t
.

(i)

a(u)-b(v)\leq y(n)=a(n)-b(n)=2^{c(n)-t}-n^{t-1}\leq a(v)-b(u)
.

(ii)
If
b(u)=u^{t-1}>2^{c(v)-t}=a(v)
then
y(n)<0
.

(iii)
If
b(v)=v^{t-1}<2^{c(u)-t}=a(u)
then
y(n)>0
.

(iv)
If
n+1\leq v
and
c(n)=c(n+1)
then
y(n+1)<y(n)
.

Proof.

It follows from the fact that the sequence
c(n)
is non-decreasing and the sequence
b(n)
is increasing.∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Put
r(n)=s
and
m(n)=t
. Then:

(i)
If
c(n)\leq s(t-1)+1
then
y(n)<0
.

(ii)
If
c(n)>s(t-1)+t
then
y(n)>0
.

Proof.

Since
r(n)=s
, we have
2^{s-1}<n\leq 2^{s}
. If
c(n)\leq st-s+1
then
c(n)-t\leq(s-1)(t-1)
, hence
2^{c(n)-m(n)}\leq\left(2^{s-1}\right)^{t-1}<n^{t-1}
and
y(n)<0
. If
c(n)>st-s+t
then
s(t-1)<c(n)-t
, and therefore
0\leq(2^{s})^{t-1}-n^{t-1}<2^{c(n)-t}-n^{t-1}=y(n)
.∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
n\geq 1
is such that

then
x(n)\leq-r(n)-3\leq-6
.

Proof.

In view of

, we have

, we have

Remark 3.1
.

Define a function

where

.

It is an easy exercise to prove the following assertions.

(1)

\lim_{x\to\infty}F(x)=\infty
.

(2)

F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2}{3}-\frac{B}{\sqrt{2x}}+\frac{C}{x\ln 2}-\frac{\ln x}{\sqrt{2x}\ln 2}-\frac{\sqrt{2x}}{x\ln 2}
,
\lim_{x\to\infty}F^{\prime}(x)=\frac{2}{3}
.

(3)

F^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{\sqrt{x}\ln(2^{B}x)-2\sqrt{2}C}{2\sqrt{2}x^{2}\ln 2}

(4)

(5)

is convex and

(7)

Abstract
An ordered r r -uniform matching of size n n is a collection of n n pairwise disjoint r r -subsets of a linearly ordered set of r ​ n rn vertices. For n = 2 n=2 , such a matching is called an r r -pattern , as it represents one of 1 2 ​ ( 2 ​ r r ) \tfrac{1}{2}\binom{2r}{r} ways two disjoint edges may intertwine. Given a set 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of r r -patterns, a 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -clique is a matching with all pairs of edges belonging to 𝒫 \mathcal{P} . In this paper we determine the order of magnitude of the size of a largest 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -clique in a random ordered r r -uniform matching for several sets 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , including all sets of size | 𝒫 | ⩽ 2 |\mathcal{P}|\leqslant 2 and the set ℛ ( r ) \mathcal{R}^{(r)} of all 2 r − 1 2^{r-1} r r -partite r r -patterns.

The results in parts (a) and (b) of Theorem

The following characterization of collectable patterns was established in

New results

Theorem
1.1
gives a pretty much full picture of the order of magnitude of
z_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(2)}_{n})

\mathcal{P}
-clique in
(\mathbb{RM}^{(2)}_{n})

For

One case in which we are able to pinpoint the value of

quite precisely is when

. Generalizing Theorem
1.1
(d), we show that the largest
\mathcal{R}^{(r)}
-clique in

has size linear in

and find the value of the multiplicative constant asymptotically.

Theorem 2.1

For
r\geqslant 2
, let
\mathcal{R}^{(r)}
be the set of all
r
-partite

-patterns. Then a.a.s.

z_{\mathcal{R}^{(r)}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n})\sim\frac{(r-1)!}{r^{r-1}}n.

Equivalently,
R_{n}^{(r)}
, the largest (lexicographically first)
r
-partite sub-matching of
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
has a.a.s. size

|R_{n}^{(r)}|\sim\frac{(r-1)!}{r^{r-1}}n.

It follows that the size of the largest clique in the intersection graph of a random set of
n

\mathbb{RM}^{(4)}_{n}
(as defined in Section

) is a.a.s.
\Theta(n)
.

Our next result determines, for all
r\geqslant 2
, the order of magnitude of
z_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n})
for
all
sets of
r
-patterns
\mathcal{P}
with
|\mathcal{P}|=2
.
We call a pair of
r
-patterns
\{P,Q\}
a

if one of them is collectable, while the other is not, or both are collectable, but then not harmonious.

Theorem 2.2

For
r\geqslant 2
, let
\mathcal{P}=\{P,Q\}
be a pair of distinct

-patterns. Then a.a.s.

z_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n})=\begin{cases}\Theta(n^{1/(r-1)})&\text{if $P$ and $Q$ are harmonious,}\\
\Theta(n^{1/r})&\text{if $P$ and $Q$ form a mismatch,}\\
z&\text{if neither $P$ nor $Q$ is collectable,}\end{cases}

where
z=z_{\mathcal{P}}
is an integer,
2\leqslant z\leqslant 5
.

It is worth noticing that the value of

in the above theorem does not depend on
\lambda_{P}
and
\lambda_{Q}
but only on
r
and the structural relation between
P
and
Q
. As we will demonstrate in the proof, the constant

appearing in the last statement equals the largest size of a
\{P,Q\}
-clique whatsoever.

As an application, taking
P=ABBBAAAB
and

, we infer that the largest subset of mutually crossing rectangles among
n

\mathbb{RM}^{(4)}_{n}
(as described in Section

) a.a.s. has size
\Theta(n^{1/4})
.

For
triples
of
r
-patterns we are far from a complete picture. For
r=3
, we only know the answer when all three 3-patterns are tripartite.
In fact, the proof can be carried over for any
r\geqslant 3
(and
2^{r-1}-1

r
-partite

-patterns), so we state the result in its general form.

Proposition 1

For
r\geqslant 2
, let
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{R}^{(r)}
with
|\mathcal{P}|=2^{r-1}-1
. Then, a.a.s.
z_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n})=\Theta(n^{1-1/r})

Most proofs, along with essential tools and preliminary observations, are presented in the next two sections. In particular, we will state an Azuma-Hoeffding-type concentration inequality for random permutations and use it to prove Theorem
2.1
. For other proofs we will use a variety of tools and techniques, including the standard first moment method, Dilworth’s Lemma, estimates on pattern avoiding permutations, and Dyck words. In the Appendix, we present the remaining proofs of Theorem
2.2

2.1
Open problems

Here we gather a few open problems we would like to work on in the future.

1.
For an ordered set of
rn
vertices, imagine a random process in which, sequentially, for each next available vertex
v
a set of
r-1
vertices is selected uniformly at random to form with
v
an
r
-element edge. For a given set
\mathcal{P}
of
r
-patterns, determine the order of magnitude of the size of the largest
\mathcal{P}
-clique after
t
steps of this process, for any given
t=t(n)
. Reversely, given a function
k=k(n)
, determine the threshold
t(n)
, or even the hitting time, for the process to grow a
\mathcal{P}
-clique of size
k(n)
.

More realistically, though, we hope to be able to answer some further questions concerning the final stage of the process (
t=n
), that is, the random ordered
r
-matching
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
.

2.
The first two questions relate to the limitations of the disturbing role of non-collectable patterns.
A matching is
clean
if every pair of edges forms a collectable pattern. What is the size of the largest clean sub-matching of the random
r
-matching
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
? Theorem
2.1
provides a lower bound here, so the answer to this question should be linear in
n
with the coefficient at least as large as
(r-1)!r^{1-r}
.
A more general question can be stated as follows.
Is it true that for every set
\mathcal{P}
of collectable
r
-patterns and every set
\mathcal{Q}
of non-collectable
r
-patterns, a.a.s.
z_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n})=\Theta(z_{\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{Q}}(\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}))
?

3.
With respect to Theorem
2.2
, we would like to extend it to
all triplets
of
r
-patterns. For mutually harmonious triplets, so far we only have the result of Proposition
1
for
r=3
: for any
3
-element subset
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{R}^{(3)}=\{P_{6},P_{7},P_{8},P_{9}\}
, a.a.s.

. We believe that for all other triplets of collectable 3-patterns the value of

In fact, we conjecture that for every

, if

consists of three pairwise non-harmonious

3
Probabilistic tools

In this section we present a couple of probabilistic tools and apply them right away to prove some of our results.

3.1
Concentration inequality for random matchings

Recall that
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
denotes a random ordered
r
-uniform matching of size
n
, that is, a matching picked uniformly at random out of the set
\mathcal{M}_{n}^{(r)}
of all

\alpha^{(r)}_{n}:=\frac{(rn)!}{(r!)^{n}\,n!}

ordered
r
-matchings on the set
[rn]
. For future reference note that the probability that a given
r
-element subset
e
of
[rn]
is an edge of
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
is

\Pr(e\in\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n})=\frac{\alpha^{(r)}_{n-1}}{\alpha^{(r)}_{n}}=\frac{1}{\binom{rn-1}{r-1}}.

(2)

There is another, equivalent way of drawing
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
, the
permutational scheme
.
Indeed, let
\pi
be a permutation of
[rn]
. It can be chopped off into an
r
-matching

M_{\pi}:=\left\{\{\pi(1),\dots,\pi(r)\},\{\pi(r+1),\dots,\pi(2r)\},\dots,\{\pi(rn-r+1),\dots,\pi(rn)\}\right\}

and, clearly, there are exactly
(r!)^{n}n!
permutations
\pi
yielding the same matching.
This means that the probability of choosing a matching with a given property, while picking it uniformly at random, is the same as when picking a random permutation and then extracting from it an
r
-matching in the above prescribed way.

We will benefit from this straightforward observation, as it allows direct applications of
high concentration results for random permutations in the context of ordered matchings.
One of them is the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality for random permutations (see, e.g., Lemma 11 in
[
8
]
or Section 3.2 in
[
15
]
).
By swapping two elements in a permutation
\pi_{1}
we mean fixing two indices
i<j
and creating a new permutation
\pi_{2}
with
\pi_{2}(i)=\pi_{1}(j)
,
\pi_{2}(j)=\pi_{1}(i)
, and
\pi_{2}(\ell)=\pi_{1}(\ell)
for all
\ell\neq i,j
. Let
\Pi_{N}
denote a permutation selected uniformly at random from all
N!
permutations of
[N]
.

Lemma 1
(Frieze and Pittel
[
8

3.2
Generic first moment method

For most upper bound proofs in this paper we use a standard first moment method. Here we state a generic, technical “meta-statement” which will be applied throughout. For a set of

Lemma 2

For

r
-patterns.
If there are constants

for all

.

Proof

For each

, let

be the number of
\mathcal{P}
-cliques of size

in
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
.
In order to compute the expectation of

, one has to first choose a set

of
rk

rn

\mathcal{P}
-clique will be planted. Formally, for each

define an indicator random variable

if there is a
\mathcal{P}
-clique on

\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}

Thus,

{\mathds{E}}I_{S}=\Pr(I_{S}=1)=a_{\mathcal{P}}(k)\cdot\frac{\alpha^{(r)}_{n-k}}{\alpha^{(r)}_{n}}

and by the linearity of expectation, noticing that

{\mathds{E}}X_{k}=\binom{rn}{rk}a_{\mathcal{P}}(k)\frac{\alpha^{(r)}_{n-k}}{\alpha^{(r)}_{n}}=\frac{(r!)^{k}}{(rk)!}\cdot a_{\mathcal{P}}(k)\cdot\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}\leqslant\left(\frac{C^{\prime}n}{k^{r-x}}\right)^{k},

where

Finally, note that

. ∎

4
Combinatorial tools

In this section we present several combinatorial tools and apply them right away to prove some of our results.

4.1
Posets on matchings

It turns out, as remarked in
[
9

, that one can often define a partially ordered set (poset) on the set of edges of a random matching
\mathbb{RM}_{n}^{(r)}
and then utilize Dilworth’s Lemma, which we state here in a version most suited for us.

Lemma 3
(Dilworth, 1950)

If the largest chain in a finite poset
X
has size at most
a
, then the largest anti-chain in
X
has size at least
|X|/a
.

A general template of how to proceed in such scenarios is described in the next lemma.

Lemma 4

Let
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{(r)}
be a set of
r
-patterns and let

be a
\mathcal{P}
-clique in an
r
-matching

Suppose that for a subset

in such a way that for all

we have

if
\min e<\min f
and
e,f
form an
r
-pattern
Q
for some
Q\in\mathcal{Q}
.
Then,
z_{\mathcal{Q}^{c}}(M)\geqslant|N|/z_{\mathcal{Q}}(M)
, where
\mathcal{Q}^{c}=\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof

Note that for such a poset, every chain is a
\mathcal{Q}
-clique, while every anti-chain is a
\mathcal{Q}^{c}
-clique. As
N\subset M
, by monotonicity,
z_{\mathcal{Q}}(N)\leqslant z_{\mathcal{Q}}(M)
and
z_{\mathcal{Q}^{c}}(N)\leqslant z_{\mathcal{Q}^{c}}(M)
. By Lemma
3
applied to

we thus conclude that

z_{\mathcal{Q}^{c}}(M)\geqslant z_{\mathcal{Q}^{c}}(N)\geqslant|N|/z_{\mathcal{Q}}(N)\geqslant|N|/z_{\mathcal{Q}}(M).

∎

In this paper we benefit from Lemma
4
only in the case of
r
-partite patterns. We now deduce the lower bound in Proposition
1
from the upper bound in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof
(Proposition
1
, lower bound)

Recall that
R^{(r)}_{n}
is the largest (lexicographically first)
r
-partite sub-matching of
\mathbb{RM}^{(r)}_{n}
.
Let
P=S_{1}\cdots S_{r}
, where
|S_{i}|=2
,
i=1,\dots,r
, be the only
r
-partite pattern not belonging to
\mathcal{P}
. Further, let

consist of all those indices

, and let

. Given two edges

, let

In this poset, a chain is just a

4.2
Pattern avoiding permutations

For the proof of the upper bound in Proposition
1
we will need a result of Gunby and Pálvölgyi from
[
9
]
concerning parallel pattern avoidance by permutations.

For integers
k\geqslant m
, let
\sigma
be a permutation of
[k]
and let
\tau
be a permutation of
[m]
. We say that
\sigma

avoids

\tau
if there is no sequence
a_{1}<\dots<a_{m}
such that the sub-permutation
\sigma(a_{1}),\dots,\sigma(a_{m})
is order-isomorphic to permutation
\tau

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{d})
be a
d
-tuple of permutations of
[k]
and let
\tau=(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{d})
be a
d
-tuple of permutations of
[m]
. We say that
\sigma

avoids

\tau
if there is no sequence
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{m}
such that all

for

Generalizing the celebrated result by Marcus and Tardos
[

, Gunby and Pálvölgyi proved the following estimates.

(Gunby and Pálvölgyi

-tuples of permutations of

\tau

and

(depending on

Proof
(Proposition
1
, upper bound)

Let
P=S_{1}\cdots S_{r}
, where
|S_{i}|=2
,

. Further, let

consist of all those indices

for which

.
Let

.
Further, let

be a partition of
[rk]
into consecutive blocks, where

, for

.
If we were after

-cliques, we would just take a

4.3
Traces of matchings

In some proofs of upper bounds, in order to estimate the number

of distinct
\mathcal{P}
-cliques of size
k
appearing in Lemma
2
, we employ the concept of the trace of a matching, introduced in
[
5
]
.

Let
M
be an ordered
r
-matching of size
n
. We assign number
i
to the
i
-th (from the left) vertex of each edge of
M
. The obtained
r
-ary sequence (with alphabet
[r]
) will be called the
trace of
M
and denoted by
\tr(M)
. For instance,
for
M=AABCBDBDACCD
, we have
\tr(M)=121121323233
. In general, there may be many matchings with the same trace, e.g.,
M^{\prime}=AABCCDADDBBC
has
\tr(M^{\prime})=\tr(M)
.
However, for some restricted families of matchings
\mathcal{F}
it may happen that no two members of
\mathcal{F}
have the same trace. Then, knowing the trace of a matching
M\in\mathcal{F}
, one is able to uniquely reconstruct
M
. In that case we call the family
\mathcal{F}

reconstructible
.
Using this concept, we obtain the following upper bound on the size of the largest
\mathcal{P}
-clique in
\mathbb{RM}_{n}^{(r)}
for a class of sets
\mathcal{P}
.

Observation 4.2

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of
r
-patterns such that the family of all
\mathcal{P}
-cliques is reconstructible. Then
z_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{RM}_{n}^{(r)})=O(n^{1/r})
.

Proof

By assumption, there are no more
\mathcal{P}
-cliques on a vertex set of size
rk
than there are words of length
rk
over alphabet
[r]
. So,
a_{\mathcal{P}}(k)\leqslant r^{rk}
and the conclusion follows by Lemma
2
with
x=0
and
C=r^{r}
. ∎

Remark 1

In fact, the traces of ordered matchings have a special structure: for every pair
i<j
, in every prefix of the trace
\tr(M)
the number of occurrences of
i
is not smaller than the number of occurrences of
j
. For instance, for
r=2
we obtain the well known family of
\emph{Dyckwords}
enumerated by
Catalan numbers
. For arbitrary

one gets families of
r
-dimensional Dyck words
enumerated jointly in table A060854 of the OEIS database (cf.
[
17
]
).
Due to the robustness of our estimates, this is, however, irrelevant for us.

As an application of Observation
4.2
, we will prove Theorem
2.2

But first we make an observation about the inheritance of the property of being mismatched.
For an
r
-pattern

-th letter

, then

, while

. Clearly, if

is collectable, then so is

for every

, but the opposite does not necessarily hold.

For every
r\geqslant 3
and any mismatched pair

such that
\{P^{-j},Q^{-j}\}

(Theorem

It suffices to prove the upper bound only, since the lower bound follows by Theorem

We proceed by induction on

For

Assume now that

on vertex set

. We are going to show that

. To this end, let

be given by Observation

. After removing the

-th letter from each edge of

and

, we obtain

-cliques

and

.

Appendix

Here we present two remaining proofs of the lower bounds in Theorem

In the former case we give a proof of a slightly more general result, while in the latter, conversely, we present the proof in a special, but typical case. Both proofs demonstrate a common proof technique of “implanting” the desired structure within the vertex set of a random matching and then showing that it will actually appear with high probability.

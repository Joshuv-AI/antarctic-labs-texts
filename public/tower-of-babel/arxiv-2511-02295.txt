Electronic state of superconductivity in line nodal material CaSb 2 \mathrm{CaSb}_{2} under pressure up to 4.2 ​ GPa 4.2\,\mathrm{GPa}
Shumpei Oguchi 1, Kenji Ishida 1, Atsutoshi Ikeda 2, Yoshiteru Maeno 3, and Shunsaku Kitagawa 1

Abstract
We report the results of resistance measurements under pressure up to 4.2 ​ GPa 4.2\,\mathrm{GPa} on single-crystalline CaSb 2 \mathrm{CaSb}_{2} , which shows the maximum of superconducting transition temperature T c T_{\mathrm{c}} at 3.1 ​ GPa 3.1\,\mathrm{GPa} . At room temperature, R ⁡ ( P ) R(P) shows a subtle anomaly at 3.1 ​ GPa 3.1\,\mathrm{GPa} . However, Bloch-Grüneisen analysis of R ⁡ ( T ) R(T) indicates that the electronic state does not change significantly across 3.1 ​ GPa 3.1\,\mathrm{GPa} .



\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
has a monoclinic structure with non-symmorphic space group (No.11,
P2_{1}/m
,
C_{2h}^{2}
)
[
1
]
. This non-symmorphic crystalline symmetry protects
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
as a line-nodal semimetal even with spin–orbit coupling(SOC)
[
1
]
. At ambient pressure,
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
shows superconductivity below the superconducting transition temperature
T_{\mathrm{c}}
\,=1.7\,\mathrm{K}
[
2
]
, considered as conventional weak-coupling
s
-wave type
[
3
]
. Under pressure, however,
T_{\mathrm{c}}
shows a non-monotonic peak behavior with maximum value
T_{\mathrm{c}}

=3.4\,\mathrm{K}
around
P_{\mathrm{max}}=3.1\,\mathrm{GPa}
[
4
]
.

According to the BCS theory
[
5
]
, the superconducting transition temperature
T_{\mathrm{c}}
is described as follows:

T_{c}=\frac{1.13\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{D}}}{k_{\mathrm{B}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{N(E_{F};P)V}\right),

(1)

where
N(E_{\mathrm{F}};P)
,
\omega_{\mathrm{D}}
, and
V
are the density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level as a function of pressure:
P
, Debye frequency, and electron-phonon interaction, respectively.

A common explanation for change in
T_{\mathrm{c}}
under pressure is that the pressure dependence of
N(E_{\mathrm{F}};P)
leads to a change in
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, especially in weak coupling superconductors
[
6
]
. However, nuclear quadrupole resonance (NQR) measurements under pressure have indicated that the
N(E_{\mathrm{F}};P)
remains essentially unchanged by pressure at least up to
2.08\,\mathrm{GPa}
[
7
]
.Therefore, the peak in
T_{\mathrm{c}}
is possibly related to pressure-induced changes in
V
and
\omega_{\mathrm{D}}
, both lattice-related quantities. Indeed, X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements under pressure suggest a first-order structural transition at
P_{\mathrm{max}}
without symmetry change
[
8
]
.

Here, one possible scenario is that pressure induces a structural transition that predominantly modifies lattice-related quantities such as
\omega_{\mathrm{D}}
and
V
without a large change in the DOS, leading to the non-monotonic
T_{\mathrm{c}}
. In this note, we report the resistance measurements under pressure up to
4.2\,\mathrm{GPa}
to investigate a change in the phonon properties.

Single crystals of
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
were synthesized by the Sb self-flux method
[
9
]
. In this study, the sample1 and sample2 of
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
were taken from a large bulk crystal. Electrical resistance measurements on
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
and Pb were performed by the conventional four-terminal method. It is noted that the sample sizes are too small to estimate the resistivity. Four terminals of Cu wires were attached by a spot-welding method. Pressure was applied using an indenter-type cell
[
10
]
, with Daphne 7575 as the pressure-transmitting medium. The pressure at room temperature was estimated from the pressure dependence of the resistance of Pb
[
11
]
. During the temperature-dependent measurements, the pressure was determined using
P=\{T_{c}^{\mathrm{Pb}}(P=0)-T_{c}^{\mathrm{Pb}}(P)\}/{0.364},
where
P
is in GPa
[
11
]
.

We performed electrical resistance measurements under pressure using single-crystals of
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
and reproduced the peak in
T_{\mathrm{c}}
as reported on polycrystals as shown in Fig.
1
. We determined
T_{\mathrm{c}}
from the onset of the superconducting transition. As shown in Figs.
1
(a) and
1
(b), two samples show quite different residual resistance. The residual resistivity ratios (RRRs) given as
R(200\,\mathrm{K})/R(4.2\,\mathrm{K})
are 5.4 and 101 for the sample1 and sample2, respectively. Although sample 1 shows a slightly higher
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, its broader transition and much smaller RRR indicate lower quality, probably due to being taken from near the crystal surface. While the sample1 has lower quality, only the resistance of the sample1 could be measured under pressures exceeding
P_{\mathrm{max}}
. As discussed below, the behavior of quantities except RRR is almost the same.

(d) shows the pressure dependence of the resistance at room temperature. The sample2 has data only up to
2.5\,\mathrm{GPa}
, but the curves overlap well with that of the sample1. Around
P_{\mathrm{max}}
, a subtle change in the slope of
R(P)
was observed.

To investigate changes in the electronic state at low temperatures, we analyzed the temperature dependence of the resistance of the sample2 using the Bloch-Grüneisen formula
[
12
]
:

R(T)=R_{0}+A\left(\frac{T}{\theta_{R}}\right)^{5}\int_{0}^{\theta_{R}/T}\frac{x^{5}}{(e^{x}-1)(1-e^{-x})}dx,

(2)

where
R_{0}
is the residual resistance. The phonon contribution to the resistance is represented by the second term, in which
\theta_{R}
is the Bloch-Grüneisen temperature. Here,
A
and
\theta_{R}
are the fitting parameters. The fittings were performed in the normal state up to
50\,\mathrm{K}
and
100\,\mathrm{K}
to check whether the resulting fitting parameters are robust and do not strongly depend on the fitting range. The fitting example is shown in Fig.
2
(a), and the pressure dependence of the fitting parameters for each sample is shown in Figs.
2
(b) and (c). As seen in the figures, no significant pressure dependence was observed in either sample or in either fitting range. In the Bloch–Grüneisen analysis,
\theta_{R}
is the Debye temperature as obtained from resistance measurements, i.e., a quantity that can be an indicator of
\omega_{\mathrm{D}}
. The coefficient
A
reflects the transport electron–phonon coupling and is an indicator of electron–phonon interaction
V
[
13
]
. Although
A
(
\theta_{R}
) are not identical to
V
(
\omega_{\mathrm{D}}
) strictly, both
A
and
\theta_{R}
can qualitatively trace the changes by pressure. Therefore, these results suggest that the electronic state, especially, phonon properties does not significantly change at
P_{\mathrm{max}}
within our resolution. It is interesting that the electronic state exhibits only subtle changes at
P_{\mathrm{max}}
, although the structural transition occurs as first-order and
T_{\mathrm{c}}
shows the peak at
P_{\mathrm{max}}
.

The origin of the slope change in
R(P)
observed at
P_{\mathrm{max}}
is considered to be a change in the response of the Sb(1) network to pressure reported by Takahashi
et al.
[
8
]
. Such a subtle change was not seen in polycrystals, likely due to grain-boundary scattering.
[
4
]

One possible scenario is whereas resistance reflects the average contribution of all phonons,
T_{\mathrm{c}}
is affected contribution from specific phonon modes.

In conclusion, we performed the electrical resistance measurements on single-crystal
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
under pressure. The peak structure of
T_{\mathrm{c}}
at
P_{\mathrm{max}}
was successfully reproduced, whereas no appreciable change in the low-temperature electronic state was detected within our experimental resolution. This implies that the electronic state remains essentially unchanged up to the highest pressure region. To clarify origin of the peak in
T_{\mathrm{c}}
, further studies are required; in particular, high-pressure NQR would help constrain the superconducting symmetry and its coupling to lattice dynamics. In addition, since
\mathrm{CaSb}_{2}
has been discussed as a candidate topological material, the relationship between potential topological features and the observed enhancement of superconductivity remains an open question for future work.

The slender body free boundary problem
Laurel Ohm

Abstract
. We consider the slender body free boundary problem describing the evolution of an inextensible, closed elastic filament immersed in a Stokes fluid in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . The filament elasticity is governed by Euler-Bernoulli beam theory, and the coupling between this 1D elasticity law and the surrounding 3D fluid is governed by the slender body Neumann-to-Dirichlet (NtD) map, which treats the filament as a 3D object with constant cross-sectional radius 0 < ϵ ≪ 1 0<\epsilon\ll 1 . This map serves as a mathematical justification for slender body theories wherein such 3D-1D couplings play a central role. We develop a solution theory for the filament evolution under this coupling. Our analysis relies on two main ingredients: (1) an extraction of the principal symbol of the slender body NtD map, from the author’s previous work, and (2) a detailed treatment of the tension determination problem for enforcing the inextensibility constraint. Our work provides a mathematical foundation for various computational models in which a slender filament evolves according to a 1D elasticity law in a 3D fluid. This forms a key development in our broader program to place slender body theories on firm theoretical footing.

1.1.
Related models and results

The formulation of the slender body free boundary problem comes from seeking a mathematically reasonable lower-dimensional representation of the evolution of an immersed filament in 3D, with dynamics driven by 1D beam theory.
A primary contribution of our program is the novel type of NtD operator
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}(\bm{X})
coupling the fluid and the filament.

Computationally, evolutions of the form (
8
) with simpler choices of fluid–filament coupling have enjoyed great success as models for undulatory swimming at low Reynolds number

[
14
,
7
,
23
,
57

32

]

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm loc}(\bm{X})=c\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert({\bf I}+\bm{X}_{s}\otimes\bm{X}_{s})\,.

The analogue of (

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm loc}(\bm{X})

\bm{X}(s,t)
, coupled with an explicit elliptic equation for
\tau(s,t)

This evolution, with its greatly simplified analogue of the tension determination problem, has been studied from a PDE perspective by the author and others in
[

41

[

, we consider the resistive force theory analogue of (

[

36

[

Mathematically,
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon,\rm loc}(\bm{X})
is a convenient choice of coupling operator in (

To address this, in
[

Given a line force density

\displaystyle-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad{\rm{div}\,}\bm{u}=0\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Sigma_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}(\bm{\sigma}[\bm{u}]\bm{n})\,\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(s,\theta)\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\bm{f}(s)\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\text{on }\partial\Sigma_{\epsilon}

is self-adjoint: letting
\bm{u}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[\bm{f}](s)\cdot\bm{h}(s)\,ds=\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}2\mathcal{E}(\bm{u}):\mathcal{E}(\bm{u}_{h})\,d\bm{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{f}(s)\cdot\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[\bm{h}](s)\,ds\,,

3.3.
Solvability of tension determination problem

Here we prove solvability of the reformulated tension determination problem of Proposition
3.2
and derive a coarse bound for the solution. We begin with general invertibility of the operator
I+\mathcal{K}
appearing on the left hand side of (
55
). In particular, given
h(s)\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, we consider solving the equation

(81)

(I+\mathcal{K})[\psi]=h(s)

for
\psi
. Noting that
\mathcal{K}
is compact from
C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
to
C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, invertibility follows by the Fredholm alternative provided the nullspace of
I+\mathcal{K}
is trivial. Unpacking, we note that the operator
I+\mathcal{K}
may be written as

(82)

I+\mathcal{K}=(I+\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon})^{-1}(I-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\partial_{ss})^{-1}\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}

where
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
is as in (
73
) and
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}
is as in (
30
). Given the bound (
74
) for
\mathcal{K}_{\epsilon}
and Lemma
2.3
for
(I-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\partial_{ss})^{-1}
, it will suffice to show that the nullspace of
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}
is trivial.
After incorporating the curve dependence into the resulting bound, we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
as in (
1
)-(
3
) with centerline
\bm{X}\in C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
,
0<\alpha<1
, and given
h\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, there exists a unique solution
\psi\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
to the equation (
81
) which satisfies

(83)

\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

Moreover, given nearby filaments with centerlines
\bm{X}^{(a)}
,
\bm{X}^{(b)}
in
C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, the difference
\psi^{(a)}-\psi^{(b)}=\big((I+\mathcal{K}^{(a)})^{-1}-(I+\mathcal{K}^{(b)})^{-1}\big)[h]
satisfies

(84)

\left\lVert\psi^{(a)}-\psi^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}

for
\alpha^{+}>\alpha
.

Proof.

We begin by proving the solvability claim along with the qualitative bound

(85)

\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\bm{X},\epsilon)\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}

where
\bm{X}
is any
C^{2,\gamma^{+}}
curve satisfying (
2
).
As mentioned, given the form (
82
) of
I+\mathcal{K}
, the proof of solvability of (
81
) amounts to verifying that the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}
has only a trivial kernel for any filament
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
as in (
1
)-(
3
).

Note that if
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}[\psi]=0
, then, using the definition (
22
) of
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}
, we have

(86)

0=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\big)_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}\,\phi(s)\,ds=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\cdot\big(\bm{X}_{s}\,\phi(s)\big)_{s}\,ds

for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
; in particular, either
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\perp\{\bm{X}_{s},\bm{X}_{ss}\}
or
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]
is a constant vector. In the first case, using the energy identity (
17
) with
\bm{f}=(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}
, we have

(87)

\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}2\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{u})\right\rvert^{2}\,d\bm{x}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\cdot\big(\psi\bm{X}_{ss}+\psi_{s}\bm{X}_{s}\big)\,ds=0\,,

so, by the Korn inequality in
\Omega_{\epsilon}

[
42

and the decay of
\left\lvert\bm{u}\right\rvert
as
\left\lvert\bm{x}\right\rvert\to\infty
, we have
\bm{u}\equiv 0
throughout
\Omega_{\epsilon}
and hence
\bm{u}\big|_{\Gamma_{\epsilon}}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]=0
. By uniqueness of solutions to the slender body boundary value problem (Proposition
1.2
), we must then have

and

\psi=0
.
Similarly, in the second case, if
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]

(88)

\int_{\Omega_{\epsilon}}2\left\lvert\mathcal{E}(\bm{u})\right\rvert^{2}\,d\bm{x}=\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\cdot\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\psi\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\,ds=0\,,

by periodicity, leading again to the conclusion that
\psi=0
. The qualitative bound (
85
) follows from the mapping properties of

in Proposition
3.2

We next consider the dependence of the bound (

\bm{X}_{j}
satisfying
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{j}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\leq M
and
\left\lvert\bm{X}\right\rvert_{\star}\geq c_{\Gamma}
such that

(89)

\left\lVert(I+\mathcal{K}_{j})^{-1}[h]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}>j\,.

Then, letting

\varphi_{j}=\frac{h}{\left\lVert(I+\mathcal{K}_{j})^{-1}[h]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}}\,,\quad\eta_{j}=\frac{(I+\mathcal{K}_{j})^{-1}[h]}{\left\lVert(I+\mathcal{K}_{j})^{-1}[h]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}}\,,

we have that
(I+\mathcal{K}_{j})[\eta_{j}]=\varphi_{j}\to 0
in
C^{1,\alpha}
. By Proposition
3.2
, we have
\mathcal{K}_{j}[\eta_{j}]\in C^{1,\gamma}
,
\gamma>\alpha
, with a uniform bound in

. Therefore, along some subsequence

, we have

(91)

\eta_{j_{\ell}}=\varphi_{j_{\ell}}-\mathcal{K}_{j_{\ell}}[\eta_{j_{\ell}}]\to\eta_{\infty}\,,\qquad\left\lVert\eta_{\infty}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}=1\,.

Furthermore, since
\left\lVert\bm{X}_{j}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\leq M
, there exists a subsequence
\bm{X}_{j_{k}}
of curves converging strongly in

satisfy

(92)

\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}_{j_{k}}-\mathcal{K}_{\infty})[\psi]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}\leq c(\epsilon,M,c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}_{j_{k}}-\bm{X}_{\infty}\right\rVert_{C^{2,\gamma^{+}}}\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}\to 0

as

. In particular, by a diagonalization argument,

(93)

\mathcal{K}_{j}[\eta_{j}]\to\mathcal{K}_{\infty}[\eta_{\infty}]

along a subsequence, and
(I+\mathcal{K}_{\infty})[\eta_{\infty}]=0
. By the qualitative solvability (

), we have

, contradicting
\left\lVert\eta_{\infty}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}=1
.

Finally, we turn to the Lipschitz bound (
84
). Given two nearby filaments with centerlines

, we seek a bound for the corresponding tension difference

, where

and

Rearranging, we may write

\displaystyle=\mathcal{K}^{(b)}[\psi^{(b)}]-\mathcal{K}^{(a)}[\psi^{(a)}]

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{K}^{(b)}-\mathcal{K}^{(a)})[\psi^{(b)}]-\mathcal{K}^{(a)}[\psi^{(a)}-\psi^{(b)}]\,,

or, moving the second term on the right hand side to the left hand side,

Using (

) and the Lipschitz bound of Proposition

(97)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi^{(a)}-\psi^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert(I+\mathcal{K}^{(a)})^{-1}(\mathcal{K}^{(b)}-\mathcal{K}^{(a)})[\psi^{(b)}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}^{(b)}-\mathcal{K}^{(a)})[\psi^{(b)}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\left\lVert\psi^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

30
]
, where, for a circular filament, the analogue to the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\epsilon}
does have a nontrivial kernel consisting of constants.
In the 2D Peskin problem, any closed filament separates the domain into an interior and exterior component. In the special case of a circular filament, the tension cannot be uniquely determined because any constant may be added to the pressure of the fluid enclosed by filament, and this additional constant pressure jump between the interior and exterior may be absorbed into the filament tension.

More directly, in the 2D Peskin problem, the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
in (
22
) is replaced with the 2D Stokes single layer operator
\mathcal{S}_{2D}
:

(98)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\rm Peskin}[\tau]

\displaystyle=\big(\mathcal{S}_{2D}[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\big)_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{s}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2D}[\bm{f}](s)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(-\log(\left\lvert\bm{R}\right\rvert){\bf I}+\frac{\bm{R}\bm{R}^{\rm T}}{|\bm{R}|^{2}}\bigg)\bm{f}(s^{\prime})\,ds^{\prime}\,,\quad\bm{R}=\bm{X}(s)-\bm{X}(s^{\prime})\,.

Here
{\bf I}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix.
The Stokes single layer operator along an interface in both 2D and 3D has a well-known kernel, namely, the vector field normal to the interface
[
50
]
.
For a circle, we may write
\bm{X}_{ss}=\kappa\bm{e}_{\rm n}
for constant
\kappa
and
\bm{e}_{\rm n}
the Frenet normal vector to the curve. Since the interface is truly 1D in the Peskin problem, the vector field given by
\bm{e}_{\rm n}
is exactly the vector field normal to the interface, and hence, for any constant
c
,

(99)

\mathcal{S}_{2D}[c\bm{X}_{ss}]=c\kappa\,\mathcal{S}_{2D}[\bm{e}_{\rm n}]=0\,.

In contrast,
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[\bm{e}_{\rm n}]
does not vanish even for the planar circle. In a sense, this makes the tension determination problem more straightforward for the 3D slender body problem than for the 2D Peskin problem, since any closed filament centerline satisfying (
2
) may be treated by the same arguments.

Corollary 3.4

(Solvability of tension determination problem)
.

Let
\bm{X}\in C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
,
0<\alpha<1
, be the centerline of a filament
\Sigma_{\epsilon}
as in (
1
)-(
3
). Given
\bm{g}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, the reformulated tension determination problem of Proposition
3.2
admits a unique solution
\tau\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
satisfying

(100)

\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}}\,.

Moreover, given nearby filaments with centerlines
\bm{X}^{(a)}
,
\bm{X}^{(b)}
in
C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, the difference between the solutions to the corresponding tension determination problems satisfies

(101)

\left\lVert\tau^{(a)}-\tau^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

Proof.

Given the reformulation of the tension determination problem in Proposition
3.2
, we may write

(102)

\tau=(I+\mathcal{K})^{-1}\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{\prime}\big)[\bm{g}]\,.

Using Proposition
3.3
, we may then estimate

(103)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert(\mathcal{G}_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{\prime})[\bm{g}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}}\,,

where we have used the coarsest bounds for
\bm{g}
in Proposition
3.2
.

Furthermore, given two nearby filaments with centerlines
\bm{X}^{(a)}
and
\bm{X}^{(b)}
, we may bound

(104)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tau^{(a)}-\tau^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\big((I+\mathcal{K}^{(a)})^{-1}-(I+\mathcal{K}^{(b)})^{-1}\big)\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(a)}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}^{(a)}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{{}^{\prime}(a)}\big)[\bm{g}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert(I+\mathcal{K}^{(b)})^{-1}\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(a)}-\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(b)}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}^{(a)}-\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}^{(b)}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{{}^{\prime}(a)}-\mathcal{G}_{+}^{{}^{\prime}(b)}\big)[\bm{g}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\left\lVert\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(a)}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}^{(a)}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{{}^{\prime}(a)}\big)[\bm{g}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle+c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\big(\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(a)}-\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(b)}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}^{(a)}-\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}^{(b)}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{{}^{\prime}(a)}-\mathcal{G}_{+}^{{}^{\prime}(b)}\big)[\bm{g}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}}\,,

where we have used Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
.
∎

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We may finally combine the results of sections
3.1
-
3.3
to obtain Theorem
1.3
.
Using Proposition
3.2
, we write

(105)

\tau=(\mathcal{G}_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}+\mathcal{G}_{+}^{\prime})[\bm{g}]-\mathcal{K}[\tau]\,.

By Proposition
3.2
and Corollary
3.4
, for any
\alpha<\gamma<1
, we may bound

(106)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}[\tau]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\tau\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}}\,.

Furthermore, given two filaments with centerlines
\bm{X}^{(a)}
,
\bm{X}^{(b)}
in
C^{3,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

(107)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(a)}[\tau^{(a)}]-\mathcal{K}^{(b)}[\tau^{(b)}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(\mathcal{K}^{(a)}-\mathcal{K}^{(b)})[\tau^{(b)}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}+\left\lVert\mathcal{K}^{(a)}[\tau^{(a)}-\tau^{(b)}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\|\tau^{(b)}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle+c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\tau^{(a)}-\tau^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\alpha}},c_{\Gamma})\left\lVert\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}}\|\bm{g}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\,.

We may therefore absorb the operator
\mathcal{K}[\tau]
into the more regular right hand side remainder terms. In particular, defining
\mathcal{G}_{+}[\bm{g}]=\mathcal{G}_{+}^{\prime}[\bm{g}]+\mathcal{K}[\tau]
, we obtain Theorem
1.3
.

\square

3.5.
Proof of Corollary
1.4

Given an inextensible curve
\bm{X}\in C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, upon differentiating the inextensibility constraint
\left\lvert\bm{X}_{s}\right\rvert^{2}=1
, we obtain the identities

(108)

\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{ss}=0\,,\quad\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}=-\left\lvert\bm{X}_{ss}\right\rvert^{2}\,,\quad\bm{X}_{s}\cdot\bm{X}_{ssss}=-3\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\,.

Consider the solution to the tension determination problem (
23
) with data
\bm{g}=\bm{X}_{ssss}
. Using (
108
), the main term
\mathcal{G}_{0}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]
in the decomposition of Theorem
1.3
may be rewritten as

(109)

\mathcal{G}_{0}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]=-(I-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\partial_{ss})^{-1}\partial_{s}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\bigg[\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\,ds\bigg]\,.

Using Lemma
2.3
, for
\alpha<\gamma<1
, we may then estimate

(110)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{0}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon)\left\lVert\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\bigg[\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\,ds\bigg]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon)\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{2,\gamma}})\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\gamma}}\,.

Furthermore, given two inextensible filaments with centerlines
\bm{X}^{(a)}
,
\bm{X}^{(b)}
in
C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, the difference between the main terms in the solutions to the corresponding tension determination problems satisfies

(111)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(a)}[\bm{X}_{ssss}^{(a)}]-\mathcal{G}_{0}^{(b)}[\bm{X}_{ssss}^{(b)}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\|(I-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\partial_{ss})^{-1}\partial_{s}\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\bigg[\bm{X}_{ss}^{(a)}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}^{(a)}-\bm{X}_{ss}^{(b)}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}^{(b)}

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\big(\bm{X}_{ss}^{(a)}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}^{(a)}-\bm{X}_{ss}^{(b)}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}^{(b)}\big)\,ds\bigg]\bigg\|_{C^{1,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon)\left\lVert\bm{X}_{ss}^{(a)}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}^{(a)}-\bm{X}_{ss}^{(b)}\cdot\bm{X}_{sss}^{(b)}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon)\big(\|\bm{X}^{(a)}\|_{C^{3,\gamma}}\|\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}+\|\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\|\bm{X}^{(a)}-\bm{X}^{(b)}\|_{C^{3,\gamma}}\big)\,.

Thus the contribution from
\mathcal{G}_{0}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]
may be absorbed into the more regular terms
\tau_{+}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]
. The estimates (
27
), (
28
) for
\tau_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]
and
\tau_{+}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]
follow directly from taking
\bm{g}=\bm{X}_{ssss}
in the terms
\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{G}_{+}
from Theorem
1.3
.

\square

4.
Evolution

Given the solution theory and bounds of Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
for the tension determination problem, we may now turn to the proof of our main result, Theorem
1.1
. We begin by using the decomposition of Corollary
2.5
to rewrite the evolution (
8
) as

(112)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bm{X}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-({\bf I}+\mathcal{R}_{\rm n,\epsilon})\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm n,+}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]+\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}\big[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\big]\,,

where, here and throughout this section, we have
\tau=\tau[\bm{X}_{ssss}]
. This dependence is crucial for the improvement in regularity of Corollary
1.4
, but we will not denote this explicitly to avoid clutter.
Due to this decomposition, we see that the main part of the evolution (
112
) is given by
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}
, which, by Lemma
2.2
, has the effect of taking 3 derivatives, making the evolution third order parabolic.

Using Duhamel’s formula, we may then write

(113)

\displaystyle\bm{X}(s,t)

\displaystyle=e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bigg(-\mathcal{R}_{\rm n,\epsilon}\big[\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\big]-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm n,+}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]+\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\bigg)\,dt^{\prime}\,.

Since the behavior of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}
is given by an explicit Fourier multiplier, we may directly obtain the following estimates for the semigroup
e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}
.

Lemma 4.1

(Semigroup estimates)
.

Let
n,m
be nonnegative integers and
\alpha,\gamma\in(0,1)
with
n+\alpha\geq m+\gamma
.
For
\bm{V}(s)\in C^{m,\gamma}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

(114)

\left\lVert e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{V}\right\rVert_{C^{n,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\,t^{-\frac{n+\alpha-(m+\gamma)}{3}}\left\lVert\bm{V}\right\rVert_{C^{m,\gamma}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

In each case,
c
may be taken to be an absolute constant independent of
\epsilon
.
If, in addition,
\bm{V}
belongs to the little Hölder space
h^{m,\gamma}(\mathbb{T})
, then
e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{V}\in C([0,+\infty),h^{m,\gamma}(\mathbb{T}))
.

The proof of the bound (
114
) appears in
[
47
, Lemma 2.2]
. The final statement is shown in Appendix
C
.

Due to the presence of remainder terms
\mathcal{R}_{\rm n,\epsilon}
and
\tau_{\epsilon}
in (
113
) which are small in
\epsilon
but not more regular than the leading
\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}
term in (
112
), Lemma
4.1
will not be sufficient to close a fixed point argument. We will additionally require a lemma making use of the maximal smoothing power of the semigroup
e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}
while keeping track of its
\epsilon
-dependence. Given
T>0
and
0\leq k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we first define the function spaces

(115)

\mathcal{Y}_{k}(T)=\big\{\bm{V}\in C\big([0,T];h^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})\big)\,:\,\left\lVert\bm{V}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}<\infty\big\}\,,\qquad\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{k}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\,,

where we recall that
h^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
denotes the closure of smooth functions in
C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
.
We have the following result.

Lemma 4.2

(Maximal regularity bound)
.

Given
\bm{g}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}))
, let

(116)

\bm{h}(s,t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\;\bm{g}(s,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\,.

Then, for some absolute constant
c>0
,

(117)

\left\lVert\bm{h}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq\big(\epsilon+T^{1/4}\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-3/4}+T\big)\,c\left\lVert\bm{g}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}))}\,.

If, in addition,
\bm{g}\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}(T)
, then
\bm{h}\in\mathcal{Y}_{4}(T)
.

The proof of the bound (
117
) appears in
[
47
, Lemma 2.3]
, and we show the final statement in Appendix
C
.

We will close a fixed point argument in the space

(118)

\mathcal{Y}_{4}(T)\cap\bigg\{\left\lVert\bm{X}-e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}(T)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}\bigg\}

for some choice of
\epsilon
,
T>0
. Note that the second condition serves to propagate the non-intersection condition (
2
).
In particular, we have

(119)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\bm{X}(\cdot,t)\right\rvert_{\star}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}-\left\lVert\bm{X}(\cdot,t)-\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}-\left\lVert\bm{X}(\cdot,t)-e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}-\left\lVert e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}-\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}\,.

For
\bm{Y}
satisfying the linear equation

(120)

\partial_{t}\bm{Y}=-\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}}\partial_{s}^{4}\bm{Y}\,,\qquad\bm{Y}^{\rm in}=\bm{X}^{\rm in}\,,

we note that at each
t
, we have

(121)

\left\lVert\partial_{t}\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon)\left\lVert\bm{Y}\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon)\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

We may then estimate

(122)

\left\lVert\bm{Y}-\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}\bm{Y}\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c(\epsilon)\,t\,\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

Thus we will have

(123)

\left\lVert e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}-\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\leq c(\epsilon)\,T\left\lVert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}

for
T
sufficiently small (depending on
\epsilon
). Therefore the second condition of (
118
) guarantees that

(124)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lvert\bm{X}(\cdot,t)\right\rvert_{\star}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}\,.

4.1.
Ball to ball

Given
\bm{X}^{\rm in}\in h^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
with
\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}>0

(125)

\displaystyle\Lambda[\bm{X}]

\displaystyle=e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}(s)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bigg(-\mathcal{R}_{\rm n,\epsilon}\big[\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]\big]-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\rm n,+}[\bm{X}_{ssss}]+\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}[(\tau\bm{X}_{s})_{s}]\bigg)\,dt^{\prime}

\mathcal{B}_{M,\star}=\bigg\{\bm{X}\in\mathcal{Y}_{4}(T)\,:\,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{4}}\leq M\,,\;\left\lVert\bm{X}-e^{-t\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{Y}_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star}\bigg\}

as follows. Using Theorem

, we have

(128)

\displaystyle\left\lVert J_{\epsilon}[\bm{X}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq c(\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{2,\alpha^{+}}},\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star})\left\lVert\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\big[\Phi^{-1}\big((\tau_{\epsilon}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\big)^{\Phi}_{0}\big]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\alpha}}

(129)

\displaystyle\left\lVert J_{+}[\bm{X}]\right\rVert_{C^{1,\gamma}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq c(\epsilon,\left\lVert\bm{X}\right\rVert_{C^{3,\alpha}},\left\lvert\bm{X}^{\rm in}\right\rvert_{\star})\big(\left\lVert(\tau_{\epsilon}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\left\lVert(\tau_{+}\bm{X}_{s})_{s}\right\rVert_{C^{0,\gamma}}\big)

where we have used the mapping properties of
\mathcal{R}_{\rm n,+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}
from Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
2.2
in the second line, and we have used the bounds for
\tau_{\epsilon}
and
\tau_{+}
from Corollary

in the final line.

Using the estimate (
129
) in the Duhamel formula (
113
), by Lemma

, we have that the

(130)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}J_{+}[\bm{X}]\,dt^{\prime}\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}

We begin by outlining the general proof strategy and tools. As in
[
48
, section 2]
and
[
47
, section 3]
, we consider
C^{\ell,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
as a subset of
C^{\ell,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
, which, using
[
6
, Theorem 2.36]
, we may characterize as the Besov space
B^{\ell+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\phi(\xi)
be a smooth cutoff function supported on the annulus
\{\frac{3}{4}\leq\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
and satisfying
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-j}\xi)=1
. Let

(157)

\phi_{j}(\xi)=\phi(2^{-j}\xi)

and, given a function
h(s)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(158)

\mathcal{F}[h](\xi)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(s)\,e^{-2\pi i\xi s}\,ds

denote the Fourier transform of
h
. We denote the Littlewood-Paley projection
P_{j}h
onto frequencies supported in annulus
j
by

(159)

\mathcal{F}[P_{j}h](\xi)=\phi_{j}(\xi)\mathcal{F}[h](\xi)\,.

We further denote

(160)

P_{\leq j_{*}}:=\sum_{j\leq j_{*}}P_{j}\,.

For
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
, we may then define the following Besov seminorm and norm:

(161)

|h|_{\dot{B}^{\nu}_{\infty,\infty}}=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{j\nu}\left\lVert P_{j}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\,,\qquad\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{B^{\nu}_{\infty,\infty}}=\sup\big(\left\lVert P_{\leq 0}\,h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\sup_{j>0}\,2^{j\nu}\left\lVert P_{j}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\big)\,.

Now, given a Fourier multiplier
m(\xi)
and a function
h(s)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let

(162)

T_{m}h=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m\mathcal{F}[h]]\,,\qquad P_{j}T_{m}h=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\phi_{j}m\mathcal{F}[h]]\,.

Our general strategy for controlling the Besov norm (
161
) of the operator
T_{m}h
proceeds as follows. Letting

(163)

\widehat{M}_{j}(\xi)=\phi_{j}(\xi)m(\xi)\,,\qquad M_{j}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[\phi_{j}m]\,,

we have that by
[
48
, Lemma 2.1]
, if

(164)

\left\lvert\widehat{M}_{j}\right\rvert\leq A\,,\qquad\left\lvert\partial_{\xi}^{2}\widehat{M}_{j}\right\rvert\leq B

then

(165)

\left\lVert M_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{j}\sqrt{AB}\,.

Noting that
P_{j}T_{m}h=M_{j}*h
, by Young’s inequality for convolutions, we may then bound

(166)

\left\lVert P_{j}T_{m}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}=\left\lVert M_{j}*h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\left\lVert M_{j}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{j}\sqrt{AB}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\,.

Thus, to show Lemma
2.3
, our strategy will be to obtain bounds of the form (
164
) on the multipliers corresponding to
(I-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\partial_{ss})^{-1}
and
(I-\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}^{\rm tang}\partial_{ss})^{-1}\partial_{s}

We begin by defining

(167)

where

is given by (
32

By
[
47

, we have that

satisfies the bounds

(168)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}m_{\epsilon,{\rm t}}^{-1}(\xi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\,\epsilon\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{1-\ell}\,,&\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,,\\
c\,\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert^{-1}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-\ell}\,,&\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,,\end{cases}\quad\ell=0,1,2\,,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}m_{\epsilon,{\rm t}}(\xi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\,\epsilon^{-1}\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-\ell-1}\,,&\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,,\\
c\,\left\lvert\log\epsilon\right\rvert\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-\ell}\,,&\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,,\end{cases}\quad\ell=0,1,2\,.

From (
168
), we may then obtain the following bounds for
m^{(1)}_{\epsilon}(\xi)
and
m^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(\xi)
:

(169)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}m_{\epsilon}^{(1)}(\xi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\,\epsilon\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-1-\ell}\,,&\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,,\\

\displaystyle\left\lvert\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}m_{\epsilon}^{(2)}(\xi)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\,\epsilon\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert^{-\ell}\,,&\left\lvert\xi\right\rvert\geq\frac{1}{2\pi\epsilon}\,,\\

We next define the frequency-localized Fourier multipliers

(170)

\widehat{M}_{j}^{(1)}(\xi)=\phi_{j}(\xi)m^{(1)}_{\epsilon}(\xi)\,,\qquad\widehat{M}_{j}^{(2)}(\xi)=\phi_{j}(\xi)m^{(2)}_{\epsilon}(\xi)\,,

Letting

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}\widehat{M}_{j}^{(1)}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}c\,\epsilon\,2^{-j}\,,&j\geq j_{\epsilon}\\

\left\lVert P_{\leq 0}T_{m^{(2)}_{\epsilon}}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=\left\lVert M_{0}^{(2)}*h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left\lVert M_{0}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq c(\epsilon)\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\,.

(177)

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq j_{\epsilon}}2^{j(\ell+1+\alpha)}\left\lVert P_{j}T_{m^{(1)}_{\epsilon}}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\sup_{j\geq j_{\epsilon}}2^{j(\ell+\alpha)}\left\lVert P_{j}T_{m^{(2)}_{\epsilon}}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{j\geq j_{\epsilon}}\left\lVert M_{j}^{(2)}\right\rVert_{L^{1}}2^{j(\ell+\alpha)}\left\lVert\widetilde{P}_{j}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\,\epsilon\left\lvert h\right\rvert_{\dot{B}^{\ell+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}}\,.

Appendix C
Proof of semigroup mapping properties

Here we complete the proofs of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
. Note that the bounds (
114
) and (
117
) were established in
[
47
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]
, respectively. It only remains to verify the assertions about little Hölder spaces

We complete the proof of Lemma
4.2
in detail; the proof of the analogous claim in Lemma
4.1
for time-independent
\bm{V}(s)
follows by nearly identical arguments. Let
\mathcal{U}
denote the map

(180)

\mathcal{U}[\bm{g}](s,t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{\epsilon}\partial_{s}^{4}}\;\bm{g}(s,t^{\prime})\,dt^{\prime}\,.

By (
117
), we have that
\mathcal{U}
maps
L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{T}))
to
L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{4,\beta}(\mathbb{T}))
for
\beta\in(0,1)
.
Given
\bm{g}\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}=C([0,T];h^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{T}))
, we may approximate
\bm{g}
, e.g. by mollification, by smoother functions
\bm{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{1,\alpha+\delta})
,
\alpha<\alpha+\delta<1
. For any such
\bm{\varphi}
, we have
\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\in L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{4,\alpha+\delta})\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;h^{4,\alpha})

\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\in C([0,T];C^{4,\alpha+\delta})
, i.e., for any
\nu>0
, there is some
\eta>0
such that if
\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert<\eta

(181)

\left\lVert\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t)-\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}<\nu\,.

We will use the equivalence of the
C^{4,\alpha}
norm and the Besov
B^{4+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}
norm (
161
). Recalling the notation
P_{j}
(
159
) for the Littlewood-Paley projection, we first note that

(182)

\left\lVert\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{B^{4+\alpha+\delta}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}=\sup\bigg(\left\lVert P_{\leq 0}\,\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})},\sup_{j>0}2^{j(4+\alpha+\delta)}\left\lVert P_{j}\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\bigg)<\infty\,.

For
j\geq 0
, we then have

(183)

\displaystyle 2^{j(4+\alpha)}\left\lVert P_{j}\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t)-P_{j}\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-j\delta}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{B^{4+\alpha+\delta}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

Furthermore, for any fixed

\left\lVert P_{j}\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}=\left\lVert\mathcal{U}[P_{j}\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{C^{4,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}
is uniformly continuous in time, i.e.

(184)

\left\lVert P_{j}\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t)-P_{j}\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{B^{4+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c_{j}\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert\,.

Given
\nu>0

(185)

2^{-K\delta}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\right\rVert_{B^{4+\alpha+\delta}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}<\nu\,.

Then, noting that we have

on
\mathbb{T}

(186)

\left\lvert t-t^{\prime}\right\rvert<\frac{\nu}{\max_{0\leq j\leq K}c_{j}}\,.

Altogether, we obtain

(187)

\left\lVert\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t)-\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}](\cdot,t^{\prime})\right\rVert_{B^{4+\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{T})}<\nu\,.

Finally, since
\bm{g}\in\mathcal{Y}_{1}
may be approximated by functions
\bm{\varphi}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;C^{1,\alpha+\delta})
satisfying
\mathcal{U}[\bm{\varphi}]\in C([0,T];C^{4,\alpha+\delta})
, by density we obtain
\mathcal{U}[\bm{g}]\in C([0,T];h^{4,\alpha})
.

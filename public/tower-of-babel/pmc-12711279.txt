177Lu-Prostate-Specific Membrane Antigen Neoadjuvant to Stereotactic Ablative Radiotherapy for Oligorecurrent Prostate Cancer (LUNAR): An Open-Label, Randomized, Controlled, Phase II Study
Authors: Amar U Kishan, Luca F Valle, Holly Wilhalme, Carol Felix, Rejah Nabong, Jesus E Juarez-Casillas, Kevin Flores, T Martin Ma, Vinicius Ludwig, Mariko Nakayama, Zachary Ells, Magnus Dahlbom, Michael Lauria, Catherine Meyer, Minsong Cao, Joanne B Weidhaas, Donatello Telesca, Kristen McGreevy, Nicholas G Nickols, Danielle Karasik, Sophia Parmisano, T Vincent Basehart, Wayne Brisbane, Leonard Marks, Matthew B Rettig, Robert E Reiter, Paul C Boutros, Martin Allen-Auerbach, Johannes Czernin, Michael L Steinberg, Jeremie Calais
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPURPOSEProgression after metastasis-directed therapy via stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) for oligorecurrent hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (orHSPC) is common. We aimed to assess whether the addition of neoadjuvant prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)–targeting radioligand therapy to SBRT would improve outcomes.METHODSThe LUNAR trial was a single-center, randomized, open-label, controlled phase II trial conducted at the University of California, Los Angeles. Eligible participants had orHSPC as determined by the presence of one to five lesions identified on PSMA positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT). After stratifying by stage (N1/M1a v M1b) and lesion count (1 v 2-3 v 4-5), we randomly assigned patients 1:1 to receive SBRT to all lesions or two cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 (6.8 GBq/cycle, 2 weeks apart) followed by SBRT to all lesions. The primary end point was progression-free survival (PFS), defined by PSMA PET/CT, salvage hormonal therapy, or death. PSMA PET/CT was acquired systematically at prostate-specific antigen progression and/or 12 months after SBRT. All analyses were done in the intention-to-treat population. The study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT05496959).RESULTSFrom September 2, 2022, to November 9, 2023, 92 patients were randomly assigned (SBRT n = 47 and 177Lu + SBRT n = 45), with 87 evaluable patients (SBRT n = 42 and 177Lu + SBRT n = 45). At a median follow-up of 22 months, the addition of 177Lu to SBRT significantly improved PFS (17.6 months [95% CI 15 months to not reached] v 7.4 months [95% CI, 6.0 to 13.5 months]; hazard ratio, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.22 to 0.61], P < .0001). The only grade 3 adverse events were lymphopenia (two patients [4.8%] in the SBRT group and three patients [6.7%] in the 177Lu + SBRT group). Prognostic biomarkers for PFS were identified.CONCLUSIONCompared with SBRT alone, the addition of 177Lu-PNT2002 to SBRT significantly improved PFS in patients with orHSPC without an attendant increase in toxicity.

INTRODUCTION

Metastasis-directed therapy, predominantly in the form of stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT), has been shown to prolong progression-free survival (PFS) compared with observation1-3 or intermittent hormonal therapy4 in men with oligorecurrent (ie, one to five lesions) hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (orHSPC). Unfortunately, out-of-field progression is common, reflecting the frequency of occult microscopic disease.5 Although adding hormonal therapy to SBRT prolongs PFS,6 hormonal therapy causes significant morbidity and may be cytostatic rather than cytocidal.7,8

Although screening with prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) to select candidates for metastasis-directed SBRT may optimize outcomes due to its enhanced sensitivity,2,9,10 recurrences remain common.11 Recently, radioligand therapeutic 177Lu-PSMA-617, which combines the β-emitter Lutetium-177 (177Lu) with a ligand to PSMA, has been shown to improve outcomes both before and after chemotherapy in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer12-16 A similar radioligand, 177Lu-PNT2002, conjugates a PSMA-targeting small molecule inhibitor (PSMA-I&T) to 177Lu and achieves similar dosimetry and safety.17,18

We conducted the phase II randomized 177Lu-PSMA Neoadjuvant to stereotactic Ablative Radiotherapy for oligorecurrent prostate cancer (LUNAR) trial to investigate the hypothesis that adding two cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 before metastasis-directed SBRT would improve progression in orHSPC.

METHODS

Study Design and Participants

The LUNAR trial was a single-center, randomized, open-label, controlled phase II trial conducted at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA, Los Angeles, CA). The study was approved by the institutional review board of UCLA (Los Angeles, CA), and all patients provided written informed consent. This study followed the CONSORT reporting guideline and is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT05496959). The trial protocol can be found in the Data Supplement and the trial concept has been published previously.19

The eligibility criteria included orHSPC as determined by the presence of one to five lesions identified on PSMA PET/CT (with a 68Ga- or 18F- tracer) and the potential for all sites of disease to be treated with SBRT. The exclusion criteria included (1) castration-resistant disease (ie, prostate-specific antigen [PSA] >0.5 ng/mL with serum testosterone <50 ng/dL), (2) receipt of ADT within 6 months of trial enrollment, and (3) de novo metastatic disease. Additional inclusion and exclusion criteria are given in the protocol.

Random Assignment

Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either SBRT alone or two cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 followed by SBRT using a computer-generated randomization list with permuted blocks of 6 (with the block size known only to the statistician [H.W.] until completion of the study). Patients were stratified according to the initial staging based on distribution of disease on the PSMA PET/CT (pelvic nodal or extrapelvic nodal [iN1/M1a] v bony [M1b] v visceral [M1c]) and number of lesions (1 v 2-3 v 4-5). Neither the patient nor the treating physician was masked to the allocation.

Procedures

SBRT was typically delivered in one, three, or five fractions of 16-18 Gy, 9-12 Gy, or 6-8 Gy. Protocol-specified dose de-escalation was conducted in the 177Lu + SBRT group (Data Supplement, Fig S1).19

Patients in the 177Lu + SBRT group received two cycles of neoadjuvant 177Lu-PNT2002 through intravenous infusion, with a time interval of 8 weeks (±1 week) between the two cycles. The target injected activity per cycle was 6.8 GBq (±10%). After the first cycle, patients were imaged with serial quantitative single-photon emission computed tomography/CT scans at 4, 24-48, and 72-96 hours to characterize radiotracer pharmacokinetics in tumor and normal tissues of interest (image acquisition protocol details provided in Supplementary Methods). An interval PSMA PET/CT was obtained in 4-6 weeks after the second cycle, and SBRT commenced 1-2 weeks later.

Assessments

After SBRT, patients were evaluated at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, with PSA drawn within a week of each visit. A protocol-specified PSMA PET/CT was obtained at 12 months (if no progression) or at PSA-based progression. PSA-based progression was defined as follows: (1) for pre-enrolment PSA <0.5 ng/mL, increase ≥0.2 ng/mL; (ii) for pre-enrolment PSA ≥0.5 ng/mL, ≥50% rise over post-treatment nadir (if nadir ≥0.5 ng/mL) or 0.2 ng/mL increase (if nadir <0.5 ng/mL). If PSMA PET/CT for PSA-based progression was negative, subsequent PSMA PET/CT was triggered by a 0.2-ng/mL PSA rise (if PSA <0.5 ng/mL at first scan) or a 50% rise (if PSA ≥0.5 ng/mL). Adverse events were assessed at each visit using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0. Patients in the 177Lu-PNT2002 + SBRT arm also underwent complete blood count and metabolic panel every 3 months for 6 months after the second 177Lu-PNT2002 cycle. Interval scans (177Lu + SBRT arm only) and follow-up scans were performed using 68Ga-PSMA-11. Scans were obtained with diagnostic CT and intravenous contrast medium unless contraindicated. Images were interpreted in consensus by board-certified radiologists and nuclear medicine physicians. Results were reported according to Prostate Cancer Molecular Imaging Standardized Evaluation criteria for whole-body staging using molecular imaging TNM, version 2.0. Clinical Reports and images were accessible to the treating radiation oncologist before metastasis-directed radiotherapy. For lesion count, images were interpreted by visual analysis, without a strict standardized uptake value (SUV) threshold. In case of indeterminate lesion count, an unblinded over-read assessment was performed by local expert readers.

Optional translational laboratory draws occurred before any treatment and at 3 months post-SBRT in both arms.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the trial was PFS, defined as the time from random assignment until PSMA PET/CT-based progression, salvage hormonal therapy, or death. PSMA PET/CT-based progression was defined as either (1) a new lesion on PSMA PET/CT with or without a serum PSA increase or (2) local progression on PSMA (>30% increase in lesion SUV or increase of >20% in the sum of the longest diameter of all target lesions), regardless of new lesions. Secondary outcomes included physician-scored toxicity, hormonal therapy–free survival, organ-at-risk dosimetry, tumor dosimetry, overall survival, and local control.

Statistical Analysis

On the basis of historical data, the progression rate with SBRT at 1 year is 50%.1,2 For the sample size calculation, we assumed that adding two cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 to SBRT would reduce the progression rate at 1 year to 30%, which corresponds to a hazard ratio (HR) for PFS of 0.52 (177Lu + SBRT v SBRT). In terms of duration of response, this would be equivalent to a median PFS of 23.3 months in the experimental arm, versus 12 months in the control arm. Assuming an accrual time of 18 months, and a follow-up time of 36 months, a sample size of 90 patients (45 per arm) would provide 80% power to detect the expected difference in PFS survival at a 0.1 alpha level. Assuming a 10% drop-out/screen failure rate, we calculated 100 patients to be the required sample size for this analysis

The analysis population was intention-to-treat (ITT) for all end points. Although the protocol prespecified an ITT analysis including all randomly assigned patients, five participants in the SBRT group withdrew from the study immediately after random assignment. As no postrandomization data were available, these individuals were excluded from the primary analysis. There was no prespecified plan to replace patients who withdrew. The analysis was conducted on an as-randomized population including all patients with available postbaseline data. Patients who did not progress were censored at their last follow-up visit.

Descriptive statistics were presented as median (IQR) for continuous variables and frequency (%) for categorical variables. The Wilcoxon rank-sum, chi-square, and Fisher's exact tests compared selected features between groups. The Kaplan-Meier (KM) analysis summarized PFS and salvage hormonal therapy–free survival, with log-rank tests for group comparisons. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed visually via KM curves. Cox proportional hazards regression estimated HRs between groups, with interaction terms for stage by PSMA PET/CT and lesion count. Covariates significantly associated with PFS were included in an adjusted multivariable Cox model with treatment allocation. Proportional hazards were further tested using Schoenfeld residuals. Toxicity was summarized descriptively, and toxicity proportions by grade were compared between groups using Fisher's exact test. No imputation for missing data was performed.

Methods for correlative analyses are provided in the Supplementary Methods. All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, NC) and R version 4.3.3.

RESULTS

From September 2, 2022, to November 9, 2023, 100 patients were screened and provided informed consent. Of these, 92 patients were enrolled and 47 were randomly assigned to the SBRT group and 45 to the 177Lu + SBRT group (Fig 1). Five patients who were assigned to the SBRT group immediately withdrew consent upon random assignment; the remaining 87 proceeded with all assigned treatments and constituted the ITT and safety population.

All 45 patients on the 177Lu + SBRT arm received both cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002, with a median interval between cycles of 8.0 weeks (IQR, 8.0-8.1 weeks). Twelve patients (27%) had an increase in PSA (median increase 18.2%, IQR, 12.1%-30.1%), whereas 32 (71%) had a decrease in PSA (median decrease 51.9%, IQR, 26.0%-78.1%) and one (2%) had no change in PSA. Seven (16%) patients had a ≥30% reduction in the size of at least one target lesion on the post–177Lu-PNT2002 interval PSMA PET/CT, prompting a reduction in target volume and a dose adjustment for SBRT. Two (4%) had a complete resolution of all disease radiographically. One (2%) patient had progressive disease on the post–177Lu-PNT2002 interval PSMA PET/CT, manifesting as multiple new lesions; this patient did not receive SBRT. The remaining 44 (98%) received SBRT after a median interval of 5.9 weeks (IQR, 5.0-7.1 weeks) from the second cycle of 177Lu-PNT2002. In one of these patients, the lesion was close to the proximal bronchial tree and an eight-fraction radiotherapy regimen, rather than a five-fraction regimen, was delivered. All 42 patients on the SBRT arm received protocol-specified SBRT.

Thirteen patients (31%) in the SBRT group and 23 patients (52%) in the 177Lu + SBRT group had a PSA decline of ≥50% (P = .04, Fig 2A and Data Supplement, Fig S2). At a median follow-up of 22 months (IQR, 19-26), progression events occurred in 65 patients (75%): 36 (86%) of 42 patients in the SBRT arm and 29 (64%) of 45 patients in the 177Lu + SBRT group. The median PFS for the SBRT group was 7.4 months (95% CI, 6.0 to 13.5 months) versus 17.6 months (95% CI, 15 months to not reached) for the 177Lu + SBRT group (HR, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.22 to 0.61]; P < .0001; Fig 2B).

Upon progression, salvage hormonal therapy alone was used in 16 (38%) patients in the SBRT group and nine patients (20%) in the 177Lu + SBRT group, and salvage hormonal therapy with repeat SBRT to all metastatic sites was used in 10 patients in both groups (24% and 22%, respectively). Repeat SBRT to all metastatic sites without hormonal therapy was delivered in eight (19%) patients in the SBRT group and nine (20%) patients in the 177Lu + SBRT group. In all cases, the decision to start hormonal therapy was ultimately left to the multidisciplinary care team and the patient, but was recommended if there were new lesions on PSMA PET/CT (ie, trial-defined progression) or if PSA exceeded 10.0 ng/mL without identifiable lesions. Of the 47 patients who received salvage hormonal therapy, 46 had new lesions on PSMA PET/CT; only one patient, who was in the 177Lu + SBRT group, began hormonal therapy without either identifying new lesions on PET/CT or having a PSA that exceeded 10.0 ng/mL. The median salvage hormonal therapy–free survival for the SBRT group was 14.1 months (95% CI, 10.1 months to not reached) versus 24.3 months (95% CI, 18 months to not reached) for the 177Lu + SBRT group (HR, 0.40 [95% CI, 0.22 to 0.71]; P < .0001; Fig 2C).

Prespecified analysis based on trial stratification factors (ie, N1/M1a v M1b staging and 1 v 2-3 v 4-5 lesions) showed no significant interaction effects, with PFS significantly improved in the 177Lu + SBRT group regardless of strata (Data Supplement, Table S5). In a multivariable model for time to progression, both receipt of 177Lu + SBRT and having a solitary metastasis (v 4-5 lesions) were significantly associated with improved PFS (HR, 0.32 [95% CI, 0.18 to 0.55], P < .001 and HR, 0.39 [95% CI, 0.17 to 0.92]; P = .03, respectively; Data Supplement, Table S6).

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is the first randomized trial to evaluate combining 177Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy with SBRT in oRHSPC. Adding two neoadjuvant cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 significantly prolonged PFS from 7.4 to 17.6 months (HR, 0.37 [95% CI, 0.22 to 0.61]), a 63% reduction in the risk of progression, salvage hormonal therapy, or death. The benefit was consistent across all prespecified strata (stage by PSMA and lesion count) and persisted on multivariable analysis. Grade 2 to 3 toxicities were infrequent in both groups, although longer follow-up is needed to confirm the absence of late toxicity.23

The PFS benefit of 177Lu-PNT2002 likely reflects activity against occult microscopic disease. Neoadjuvant 177Lu-PNT2002 showed limited impact on macroscopic disease and SBRT achieved ≥98% lesion control in both arms, whereas 98% of progression events involved new lesions. Taken together, these data strongly suggest that the mechanism by which 177Lu-PNT2002 improved PFS in LUNAR involves therapeutic action against occult, microscopic disease. To our knowledge, this is a novel insight as all previous trials investigating PSMA-based radioligand therapies have involved patients with much more advanced disease. Importantly, the improvement in PFS demonstrated in the LUNAR trial was achieved without medical castration and its associated adverse effects, and the addition of 177Lu-PNT2002 to SBRT improved eugonadal PFS, unlike the addition of short-term ADT.6,7

Given the high sensitivity and specificity of PSMA PET/CT, we defined progression based on PSMA PET/CT. Furthermore, we incorporated strict PSA thresholds for initiating a PSMA PET/CT and mandated ≥1 follow-up PSMA PET/CT within the first year regardless of PSA. This is in contrast to the other major randomized trials of metastasis-directed therapy in orHSPC, which in aggregate primarily relied on conventional imaging to detect new lesions and had significantly higher PSA thresholds to trigger imaging.1,2,4,6,24 Although PSMA-based MFS has not yet been shown to be a surrogate end point for OS based on randomized trial data, emerging data suggest that PSMA-based staging is highly prognostic.25 Of new lesions seen on PSMA PET/CT, 91% were extrapelvic (ie, M1) lesions, and therefore the difference in PFS is largely driven by the prevention of metastatic relapse. The very low PSA trigger for PSMA PET/CT and the mandatory 1-year post-SBRT PSMA PET/CT for nonprogressors also explain the comparatively low PFS in the SBRT-alone group of 7.4 months, compared with 11-15 months in recent trials evaluating SBRT, despite the lower median PSA at enrollment in LUNAR.1,2,6,24 In that context, the results from the SBRT-alone arm on LUNAR may be more reflective of contemporary practice, where PSMA PET/CT may be obtained in standard follow-up.

The correlative studies yielded several important findings. First, the abundance of productive rearrangements of the TCR at 90 days was highly prognostic for PFS, consistent with results from the RAVENS and ORIOLE trials.2,24 Notably, the addition of 177Lu-PNT2002 did not blunt this effect, whereas in the RAVENS trial, the addition of 223Ra-dichloride did.24 Second, the investigation of germline mirSNPs identified a prognostic biomarker based on 20 genes, largely represented by genes associated with an immune response and DNA repair. This suggests that germline factors may play an important role in progression in patients receiving SBRT for orHSPC. Additional correlative analyses are underway and will be reported separately.

Our study has limitations. First, PSMA-based PFS is not a validated surrogate for overall survival, although PSMA staging is prognostic25,26 and widely used at recurrence,10 supporting the end point's relevance. Second, the absence of blinded central reads or strict lesion-positivity criteria at baseline and follow-up may limit generalizability. Third, despite the clear PFS benefit, 64% of patients receiving 177Lu-PNT2002 + SBRT ultimately progressed, underscoring the challenge of occult microscopic disease. Hormonal therapy, alternative dosing or sequencing, and alpha- or auger-emitting theranostics may further improve outcomes.28 Fourth, PFS was defined from random assignment rather than SBRT completion, whereas scheduled follow-up PSMA PET/CT occurred 1 year post-SBRT. This design minimized bias from delayed treatment initiation in the experimental arm; additionally, most progression events were PSA-triggered rather than detected solely on scheduled imaging. Finally, long-term efficacy data remain lacking, and alternative systemic approaches, such as intermittent enzalutamide ± leuprolide, can improve metastasis-free and overall survival in oligorecurrent disease with high-risk features.26

In summary, this randomized phase II trial demonstrated that the addition of two cycles of 177Lu-PNT2002 to SBRT in patients with orHSPC improved PFS. To validate this benefit, a confirmatory phase III study with additional patients, a multicenter design, and the incorporation of blinded central readers with strict predefined criteria for lesion positivity is required to enhance the generalizability of these findings.

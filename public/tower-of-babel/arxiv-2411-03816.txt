On the L 1 L_{1} –stability for parabolic equations with a supercritical drift term
Mikhail Glazkov, Timofey Shilkin

Abstract
In this paper we investigate the existence, uniqueness and stability of weak solutions of the initial boundary value problem with the Dirichlet boundary conditions for a parabolic equation with a drift b ∈ L 2 b\in L_{2} . We prove L 1 L_{1} -stability of solutions with respect to perturbations of the drift b b in L 2 L_{2}



in the case if the drift satisfies the “non-spectral” condition
\mathop{\rm div}b\leq 0
.

1
Introduction and Main Results

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain with a boundary
\partial\Omega
,
n\geq 2
. Assume
T>0
and denote
Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
.
We consider a problem

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}u-\nu\,\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u\ =\ f\qquad\mbox{in}\quad Q_{T},\\
u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}\ =\ 0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\\
u|_{t=0}\ =\ u_{0}.\qquad\qquad\qquad\end{gathered}\right.

(1.1)

Here
u:Q_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is unknown,
b:Q_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f:Q_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
and
u_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are given functions and
\nu>0
is a given constant.
For minor technical simplifications throughout the whole paper we will always assume the following condition holds:

b\in L_{2}(Q_{T}),\qquad u_{0}\in L_{2}(\Omega),\qquad f\in L_{1}(Q_{T}).

(1.2)

Sometimes we impose also the assumption

f\in L_{2}(0,T;W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega)),

(1.3)

where
W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega)
is the dual to the Sobolev space
\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)
(see notation for functional spaces at the end of this section). Note that for
n\geq 3
the space
L_{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\Omega)
is imbedded into
W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega)
, so (
1.3
) can be viewed as a bit stronger assumption than
f\in L_{1}(Q_{T})
.

In this paper we are interested in the existence, uniqueness and stability of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
) belonging to the energy class

u\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2}(\Omega))\cap L_{2}(0,T;\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega)).

(1.4)

For
u
satisfying (
1.4
) one can consider the bilinear form

\mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ :=\ \int\limits_{Q_{T}}b\cdot\nabla u\,\eta\,dxdt,\qquad\eta\in L_{\infty}(Q_{T}).

(1.5)

Note that for
u
belonging to (
1.4
) the condition
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
is optimal in the sense that the bilinear form is well-defined. Hence the following definition makes sense:

Definition 1.1
.

Assume
b
,
f
,
u_{0}
satisfy (
1.2
). We say
u
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) if
u
belongs to the class (
1.4
)
and satisfies the following identity

\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\Big(-u\partial_{t}\eta+\nu\,\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\Big)\,dxdt\ +\ \mathcal{B}[u,\eta]\ =\ \int\limits_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)\eta(x,0)\,dx\ +\ \int\limits_{Q_{T}}f\eta\,dxdt

(1.6)

for any
\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,T))
.

By approximation we can also extend the class of test functions in (
1.6
) to

\eta\in W^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{T})\cap L_{\infty}(Q_{T}),\qquad\eta|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=0,\qquad\eta|_{t=T}=0,

where we denote

W^{1,1}_{2}(Q_{T}):=\{\,\eta\in L_{2}(Q_{T}):\,\nabla\eta,\,\partial_{t}\eta\in L_{2}(Q_{T})\,\}.

Note that if

\mathop{\rm div}b=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(Q_{T})\qquad\mbox{(i.e. in the sense of distributions)}

(1.7)

then the convective term in (
1.1
) can be represented in the divergence form
b\cdot\nabla u=\mathop{\rm div}(ub)
which in principle allows to introduce weak solutions from a wider class than (
1.4
).
In this paper we do not assume that
b
is divergence free. Instead we impose the condition

\mathop{\rm div}b\,\leq\,0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(Q_{T}).

(1.8)

This means that

\int\limits_{Q_{T}}b\cdot\nabla\eta\,dxdt\,\geq\,0,\quad\forall\,\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(Q_{T}):\quad\eta\geq 0\ \mbox{ in }\ Q_{T}.

Note that for
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfying (
1.8
) the quadratic form (
1.5
) is non-negative:

\mathcal{B}[u,u]\,\geq\,0,\qquad\forall\,u\in L_{2}(0,T;\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{2}}(\Omega))\cap L_{\infty}(Q_{T}),

which means that the drift
b
satisfying (
1.8
) “shifts” the elliptic part of the operator in (
1.1
) away from the “spectral area”. That is why sometimes we call (
1.8
) the
non-spectral
condition.

We start with the result on the existence of weak solutions in non-spectral case (which is probably known):

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
b
,
u_{0}
,
f
satisfy (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.8
). Then there exists at least one weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) which satisfies the energy estimate

\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2}(\Omega))}\ +\ \sqrt{\nu}\,\|\nabla u\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,c\,\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{2}(0,T;W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega))}\Big)

(1.9)

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
and
T
.

Our first main result is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
b
,
u_{0}
,
f
satisfy (
1.2
), (
1.8
) and
u
is a weak solution to (
1.1
).
Then

u\in C([0,T];L_{1}(\Omega))

and
for any
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T

\|u(\cdot,t_{2})\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \|u(\cdot,t_{1})\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ +\ \,\int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega}|f(x,t)|\,dxdt.

(1.10)

Note that Theorem
1.2
says in particular that for drifts
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfying the condition (
1.8
) and for the right-hand side
f\equiv 0
the
L_{1}
–norm of weak solutions
u
to the problem (
1.1
) is a non-increasing function of time. (Compare it, for example, with the famous Kruzhkov’s result
[
13
]
for entropy solutions for the scalar conservation laws).

From Theorem
1.2
the uniqueness of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
) in the case of a drift
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfying the non-spectral condition (
1.8
) follows immediately:

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfies (
1.8
).
Then for any
u_{0}\in L_{2}(\Omega)
,
f\in L_{1}(Q_{T})
the problem (
1.1
) has at most one weak solution in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

So, for any
b
,
u_{0}
,
f
satisfying the conditions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and (
1.8
) there exists a unique weak solution of the problem (
1.1
). Note that the violation of the condition (
1.8
) can destroy the uniqueness for the problem (
1.1
).
In the elliptic case we have obvious examples of non-uniqueness for the drift
b(x)=\alpha\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}
belonging to the critical weak Lebesgue space
L_{n,w}(\Omega)
,
\Omega:=\{~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\,\}
:

-\Delta u+n\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\nabla u=0,\qquad u(x)=c\,(1-|x|^{2}),

-\Delta u+(n-2)\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}\cdot\nabla u=0,\qquad u(x)=c\,\ln|x|.

In the parabolic case we can also construct an example of instant non-uniqueness starting from zero initial data:

Proposition 1.1
.

Assume
\Omega=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\,\}
,
u_{0}\equiv 0
,
f\equiv 0
,
\nu=1
.
Then for any
T>0
there exist
b\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{n,w}(\Omega))
and
u\not\equiv 0
such that
u
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
).

Note that the uniqueness of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
) is well-known if the drift belongs to the Ladyzhenskaya-Prodi-Serrin class

b\in L_{l}(0,T;L_{s}(\Omega)),\qquad\tfrac{n}{s}+\tfrac{2}{l}\,\leq\,1,\qquad l\in[2,+\infty],\qquad s\in[n,+\infty].

(1.11)

Indeed, for
b
satisfying (
1.11
) the uniqueness can be easily proved with the help of the Gronwall lemma and interpolation inequalities. So, Theorem
1.2
shows that the non-spectral condition (
1.8
) allows to relax the condition (
1.11
).

Note that for a non-spectral drift
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfying (
1.8
) a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) is bounded providing the data of the problem are sufficiently regular. Namely, we have the following result:

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfies (
1.8
),
u_{0}\in L_{\infty}(\Omega)
,
f\in L_{s}(Q_{T})
with
s>\frac{n+2}{2}
. Let
u
be a weak solution to (
1.1
).
Then
u\in L_{\infty}(Q_{T})
and

\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,\|u_{0}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega)}\ +\ c\,\|f\|_{L_{s}(Q_{T})}

(1.12)

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,
T
,
s
and
\nu
.

We emphasize that our Theorem
1.4
is concerned only with solutions to the initial boundary value problem (with sufficiently “regular” initial and boundary data) and its analogue in the local setting for the drift
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
fails even in the case of the divergence-free case (
1.7
), see
[
1
]
, see also the further discussion of the influence of the regularity of boundary data on the boundedness of weak solutions inside the domain in
[
10
]
. Note also that for a sufficiently smooth drift satisfying the condition (
1.11
) the estimate (
1.12
) holds even if the condition (
1.8
) is violated, but in this case the constant
c=c_{b}>0
in (
1.12
) depends on the norm (
1.11
) of
b
.

Our second main result is the following theorem on the stability of weak solutions:

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume functions
b
,
u_{0}
,
f
and
b^{m}
,
u_{0}^{m}
,
f^{m}
satisfy (
1.2
) and (
1.8
) and
f
satisfies (
1.3
). Let
u
and
u^{m}
be the unique weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
) corresponding to
b
,
u_{0}
,
f
and
b^{m}
,
u_{0}^{m}
,
f^{m}
respectively.
Then

\begin{gathered}\|u^{m}-u\|_{L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{1}(\Omega))}\ \leq\ \|u^{m}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}+\,\|f^{m}-f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}+\\
+\,\frac{c}{\sqrt{\nu}}\,\|b^{m}-b\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}\,\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{2}(0,T;W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega))}\Big).\end{gathered}

(1.13)

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
and
T
.
In particular, if

b^{m}\to b\quad\mbox{in}\quad L_{2}(Q_{T}),\qquad u_{0}^{m}\to u_{0}\quad\mbox{in}\quad L_{1}(\Omega),\qquad f^{m}\to f\quad\mbox{in}\quad L_{1}(Q_{T})

then

u^{m}\to u\quad\mbox{in}\quad L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{1}(\Omega)).

From our point of view the
L_{1}
-stability of solutions under perturbations of a drift
b
in
L_{2}
in Theorem
1.5
is most interesting, as for a fixed
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfying (
1.8
) the
L_{2}
-stability of weak solutions to (
1.1
) with respect to the right hand side
f
and the initial data
u_{0}
follows directly from the energy estimate (
1.9
) and
L_{\infty}
-stability follows from (
1.12
).
On the other hand, for weak solutions in the energy class (
1.4
), thanks to the obvious identity

b^{m}\cdot\nabla u^{m}-b\cdot\nabla u=b^{m}\cdot\nabla(u^{m}-u)+(b^{m}-b)\cdot\nabla u,

the stability of the solutions to the problem (
1.1
) under the perturbation of the drift
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
can be viewed as a problem of stability of solutions to the same equations with smooth drifts
b^{m}
and the right hand sides
(b^{m}-b)\cdot\nabla u
belonging to
L_{1}(Q_{T})
. Hence our estimate (
1.10
) together with the energy estimate (
1.9
) provide (
1.13
).

Note that the violation of the condition (
1.8
) can destroy the stability for the problem (
1.1
). Namely, we have the following counterexample:

Proposition 1.2
.

Assume
\Omega=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\,\}
and

b(x)=(n-2)\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}},\qquad b^{\varepsilon}(x)=b(x)-\varepsilon\,\ln|x|\,x,\qquad u_{0}(x)=\ln|x|.

Then for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
the function

u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)\,=\,e^{\varepsilon t}\,u_{0}(x)

is a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) in
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
corresponding to the initial data
u_{0}
, the drift
b^{\varepsilon}
,
\nu=1
and the right hand side
f\equiv 0
. Moreover, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\|b^{\varepsilon}-b\|_{L_{2}(Q_{t_{\varepsilon}})}\,<\,\varepsilon,\qquad\|u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot,t_{\varepsilon})-u_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ \geq\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon}.

(1.14)

Here
Q_{t_{\varepsilon}}:=\Omega\times(0,t_{\varepsilon})
.

The study of the weak solvability and properties of weak solutions to drift-diffusion equations has a long history. Very often the problem (
1.1
) is considered under the additional restriction of the divergence free drift (
1.7
)
see, for example,
[
1
]
,
[
22
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
34
]
and references there. In this paper we demonstrate that in the case of a supercritical non-divergence free drift
b
the non-spectral condition (
1.8
) plays the crucial role for existence, uniqueness and stability of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
).

Sometimes the study of the problem (
1.1
) is coupled with the study of the dual problem

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}-\partial_{t}w-\Delta w-\mathop{\rm div}(bw)\,=\,g\qquad\mbox{in}\quad Q_{T},\\
w|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}\ =\ 0,\qquad\quad\\
w|_{t=T}\ =\ w_{0},\qquad\quad\end{gathered}\right.

(1.15)

where
g:Q_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
and
w_{0}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are given functions.
The problem (
1.15
) is presented in the divergent form and hence its weak solutions, depending on the regularity of
b
, in principle can be defined in a wider class than the energy space (
1.4
).
For example, in
[
3
]
(see also references there) distributional solutions to the problem (
1.15
) are studied.
The
L_{1}
-stability for the problem (
1.15
) in the one-dimensional case (
n=1
) was studied in
[
25
]
.
Note that thought in the divergence free case (
1.7
) the problems (
1.1
) and (
1.15
) are equivalent, it is not so in the general case.

The existence of weak solutions in the energy class (
1.4
) under the assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.8
) follows directly from the energy estimate in Theorem
1.1
. The uniqueness of weak solution under the same assumptions is more delicate. In
[
34
]
it was proved for the divergence free case (
1.7
) with the help of duality method which provides the following estimate of weak solutions to the problem (
1.1
):

\|u\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,c\,\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\Big)

(1.16)

with some constant
c>0
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,
T
and
\nu
. Here we only briefly sketch the idea assuming all functions to be sufficiently smooth, see details in
[
34
]
, see also the explanation of the idea in the elliptic case in
[
10
]
. Using duality we can represent the
L_{1}
-norm of
u
as

\|u\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\ =\ \sup\limits_{g\in L_{\infty}(Q_{T}),\,\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})\leq 1}}\Big|\int\limits_{Q_{T}}ug\,dxdt\,\Big|.

Given
g\in L_{\infty}(Q_{T})
with
\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq 1
, denote by
w_{g}
the solution to the problem (
1.15
) with
w_{0}\equiv 0
.
For the problem (
1.15
) similar to (
1.12
) we have

\|w_{g}\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,c\,\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,c

(1.17)

with some constant
c
depending only on
n
,
\Omega
,
T
and
\nu
. We emphasize that both estimates (
1.12
) and (
1.17
) with constants independent on
b
relay crucially on the assumption (
1.8
).
Using integration by parts we obtain

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}ug\,dxdt\ =\ \int\limits_{Q_{T}}u\Big(-\partial_{t}w_{g}-\nu\,\Delta w_{g}-\mathop{\rm div}(bw_{g})\Big)\,dxdt\ =\\
=\ \int\limits_{Q_{T}}\Big(-u\partial_{t}w_{g}+\nu\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{g}+b\cdot\nabla u\,w_{g}\Big)\,dxdt\ =\ \int\limits_{\Omega}u_{0}(x)w_{g}(x,0)\,dx\ +\ \int\limits_{Q_{T}}fw_{g}\,dxdt.\end{gathered}

Hence we obtain

\begin{gathered}\Big|\int\limits_{Q_{T}}ug\,dxdt\,\Big|\ \leq\ \Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\Big)\,\|w_{g}\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})}\ \leq\\
\leq\ c\,\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\Big)\,\|g\|_{L_{\infty}(Q_{T})}\ \leq\ c\,\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\Big).\end{gathered}

So, we arrive at (
1.16
)
(from the proof it is clear that the norm
\|u\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}
in the left hand side of (
1.16
) can be replaced by the norm
\|u\|_{L_{s}(Q_{T})}
with some sufficiently small
s>1
).

Of cause, the duality method can be used also for the proof of the uniqueness of weak solutions in the non-spectral case (
1.8
). Our Theorem
1.2
gives an alternative proof of the uniqueness. Note also, that comparing with the estimate (
1.16
), our estimate (
1.13
) has the advantage that it is uniform in time. It seems that the duality method does not allow to obtain such a result.

It can be interesting to compare the uniqueness result for the parabolic equation (
1.1
) with the uniqueness theorem for the linear transport equation (of cause, in the case
\nu=0
the Dirichlet condition in (
1.4
) must be replaces by some appropriate boundary condition, for example, non-penetration condition on the boundary). It is well known that the optimal (in a sense) conditions on
b
which provide the uniqueness of bounded weak solutions for the transport equation are the DiPerna-Lions
[
7
]
or Ambrosio
[
2
]
conditions. So, in the non-spectral case (
1.8
) the inclusion of the viscosity in the equation leads to a significant relaxation in the sense of the regularity of the drift without destroy of the uniqueness.

In the paper we use the following notation. For any

,

we denote by
a\cdot b=a_{k}b_{k}
their scalar product in

\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Repeated indexes assume the summation from 1 to

. For

we denote by

the gradient of

with respect to

. We denote by

and

the

is the space of
smooth functions compactly supported in
\Omega
. The space

\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p}}(\Omega)
is the closure of

C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
W^{1}_{p}(\Omega)
norm. We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
the spaces of distributions on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
p>1
we denote
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
. The space

is dual to
\overset{\circ}{W}{{}^{1}_{p^{\prime}}}(\Omega)
. The space
\mathop{\rm Lip}(\Omega)
consists of functions which are Lipschitz continuous of
\Omega
.
The symbols
\rightharpoonup
,
\overset{*}{\rightharpoonup}
and
\to
stand for the
weak,
*
-weak and strong convergence respectively. We denote by
B_{R}(x_{0})

the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radius
R
centered at
x_{0}
and write

B_{R}
if
x_{0}=0
. We write
B
instead of
B_{1}
. For an open set
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we write
\Omega\Subset\Omega_{0}
if
\bar{\Omega}
is compact and
\bar{\Omega}\subset\Omega_{0}
.
For
p\in[1,+\infty)
we denote by

L_{p,w}(\Omega)
the weak Lebesgue space equipped with the norm

\|b\|_{L_{p,w}(\Omega)}\ :=\ \sup\limits_{s>0}s~|\{~x\in\Omega:~|b(x)|>s~\}|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For
Q_{T}:=\Omega\times(0,T)
and
p\in[1,+\infty]
we use the following notation for anisotropic Sobolev spaces:

\begin{gathered}W^{1,0}_{p}(Q_{T}):=\{u\in L_{p}(Q_{T}):\,\nabla u\in L_{p}(Q_{T})\},\ \|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}(Q_{T})}=\|u\|_{L_{p}(Q_{T})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L_{p}(Q_{T})},\\
W^{1,1}_{p}(Q_{T}):=\{u\in W^{1,0}_{p}(Q_{T}):\,\partial_{t}u\in L_{p}(Q_{T})\},\ \|u\|_{W^{1,1}_{p}(Q_{T})}=\|u\|_{W^{1,0}_{p}(Q_{T})}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{L_{p}(Q_{T})},\\
W^{2,1}_{p}(Q_{T}):=\{u\in W^{1,1}_{p}(Q_{T}):\,\nabla^{2}u\in L_{p}(Q_{T})\},\ \|u\|_{W^{2,1}_{p}(Q_{T})}=\|u\|_{W^{1,1}_{p}(Q_{T})}+\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L_{p}(Q_{T})}.\end{gathered}

For a Banach space
X
we denote by
X^{*}
the dual space of
X
with the duality relation
\langle u,w\rangle
,
u\in X
,
w\in X^{*}
. The spaces
L_{p}(0,T;X)
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
and

L_{\infty}(0,T;X)
are the space of Banach-valued functions
u:(0,T)\to X
equipped with the norms

\|u\|_{L_{p}(0,T;X)}\ :=\ \Big(\,\int\limits_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{X}^{p}\,dt\,\Big)^{1/p},\qquad\|u\|_{L_{\infty}(0,T;X)}\ :=\ \mathop{\rm esssup}\limits_{t\in(0,T)}\|u(t)\|_{X}.

C([0,T];X)
is the space of continuous Banach-valued functions
u:[0,T]\to X
, i.e.

\forall\,t_{0}\in[0,T]\qquad\|u(t)-u(t_{0})\|_{X}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad t\to t_{0}.

The space

C_{w}([0,T];X)
consists of Banach-valued functions
u:[0,T]\to X
which are weakly continuous in
t\in[0,T]
, i.e.

\forall\,w\in X^{*}\quad\mbox{the map}\quad t\in[0,T]\ \mapsto\ \langle u(t),w\rangle\quad\mbox{is continuous on }[0,T].

For
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{R}
we denote
(u-k)_{+}:=\max\{u-k,0\}
,
(u-k)_{-}:=\max\{k-u,0\}
.

3
Existence and boundedness of weak solutions

The natural approach to prove the existence of a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) is to approximate the drift
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
satisfying the non-spectral condition (
1.8
) by smooth functions
b^{m}
. Some technical difficulty is related to the fact that one needs to preserve the condition (
1.8
). In the case of the divergence free drifts this difficulty can be overcomed if we introduce the vector potential of
b
, extend this vector potential outside
\Omega
and mollify the extended function. But in the case of the condition (
1.8
) besides the divergence free part of the drift one needs also to extend the potential part of
b
. This leads to a non-trivial problem of the extension of a subharmonic function. To avoid it we approximate in Proposition
3.1
below not only the drift, right-hand side and the initial data of the problem (
1.1
), but also the domain
\Omega
, see similar ideas in
[
19
]
,
[
21
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
.
The advantage of this approach is that now we can mollify the drift
b\in L_{2}(Q_{T})
so that the non-spectral condition (
1.8
) is preserved on subdomains of
Q_{T}
. Note that this construction allows to control not only Lebesgue norms of the approximations
b^{m}
, but also some more complicated norms such as critical Morrey-type norms, see, for example,
[
4
]
.

Assume Lipschitz domains

\Omega_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy the following properties:

\begin{gathered}\Omega_{m}\subset\Omega_{m+1}\subset\Omega,\qquad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{N},\qquad\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{m}=\Omega.\end{gathered}

(3.1)

(Here we also allow the case
\Omega_{m}=\Omega
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
). Then the following proposition holds:

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
\{\Omega_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
satisfy (
3.1
),
u_{0}^{m}\in L_{2}(\Omega)
,
f^{m}\in L_{2}(0,T;W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega))
.
Let
b^{m}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{Q}_{T})
satisfy the assumption

\mathop{\rm div}b^{m}\ \leq\ 0\quad\mbox{in}\quad Q_{T}^{m}:=\Omega_{m}\times(0,T).

(3.2)

Denote by
u^{m}
the unique weak solution to the problem

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}u^{m}-\nu\,\Delta u^{m}+b^{m}\cdot\nabla u^{m}\ =\ f^{m}\qquad\mbox{in}\quad Q^{m}_{T},\\
u^{m}|_{\partial\Omega_{m}\times(0,T)}\ =\ 0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\\
u^{m}|_{t=0}\ =\ u^{m}_{0},\qquad\qquad\qquad\end{gathered}\right.

(3.3)

and extend
u^{m}
by zero from
Q_{T}^{m}
onto
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)
. Then the following estimate holds

\|u^{m}\|_{L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2}(\Omega))}\ +\ \sqrt{\nu}\,\|\nabla u^{m}\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,c\,\Big(\|u_{0}^{m}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|f^{m}\|_{L_{2}(0,T;W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega))}\Big),

(3.4)

where the constant
c>0
depends only on
n
,
\Omega
and
T
.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
3.1
is standard and can be obtained by testing the equations (
3.3
) by
u^{m}
. As the drift

satisfies (
3.2
) integrating by parts we obtain

\int\limits_{\Omega_{m}}b^{m}\cdot\nabla u^{m}\,u^{m}\,dx\ =\ -\frac{1}{2}\,\int\limits_{\Omega_{m}}\mathop{\rm div}b^{m}|u^{m}|^{2}\,dx\ \geq\ 0

and hence we can drop this term. So, we arrive at (

). We omit the details.
∎

Now we can prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Consider any sequence of Lipschitz domains
\Omega_{m}\Subset\Omega_{m+1}\Subset\Omega
satisfying (

). Denote by

Take

Then

satisfies (
3.2

\|b^{m}-b\|_{L_{2}(Q_{T})}\to 0\qquad\mbox{as}\qquad m\to\infty.

(3.5)

Assume also
u^{m}_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{\Omega})
and
f^{m}\in C^{\infty}(\bar{Q}_{T})
satisfy

\|u^{m}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\to 0,\qquad\|f^{m}-f\|_{L_{2}(0,T;W^{-1}_{2}(\Omega))}+\|f^{m}-f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T})}\to 0

(3.6)

as

.
Let
u^{m}
be weak solutions to the problem (
3.3
). Using (
3.4
) we conclude there exists

(3.7)

Take any

Proof.

Take arbitrary

Hence we can fix a.e.

Indeed, taking into account the identity

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{d}{dt}\|{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h}(\cdot,t))\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,=\,\int\limits_{D_{\delta}^{h}(t)}{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h}(x,t))\partial_{t}u_{h}(x,t)\,dx\,=\\

(4.4)

Combining (
4.3
) and (

).

Note that the second term in (

) is non-negative:

So, taking into account (

) and (

) from (

) for a.e.

{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(s)\leq\delta\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\|{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h}(\cdot,t_{1}))\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,\leq\,\delta^{2}\,|\Omega|

(4.7)

(4.8)

On the other hand, we have

(4.9)

(4.10)

Indeed, we have

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}(b\cdot\nabla u)_{h}\,{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h})\,dxdt\ -\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla u\,{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ =\\

T^{\prime}\in(0,T)
we have

(b\cdot\nabla u)_{h}\to b\cdot\nabla u\quad\mbox{in}\quad L_{1}(Q_{T^{\prime}})\quad\mbox{as}\quad h\to+0.

So, for
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq T^{\prime}
we obtain

\begin{gathered}\Big|\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}\Big((b\cdot\nabla u)_{h}-b\cdot\nabla u\Big)\,{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h})\,dxdt\,\Big|\ \leq\ \delta\,\|(b\cdot\nabla u)_{h}-b\cdot\nabla u\|_{L_{1}(Q_{T^{\prime}})}\ \underset{h\to+0}{\to}\ 0.\end{gathered}

For the second term we have
{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h})\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)
in
L_{1}(Q_{T^{\prime}})
and hence for a subsequence we obtain
{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h})\to{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)
a.e. in
Q_{T^{\prime}}
. As

\Big|\,b\cdot\nabla u\,\big({\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h})-{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\big)\,\Big|\ \leq\ 2\delta~|b\cdot\nabla u|\quad\mbox{a.e. in}\quad Q_{T^{\prime}},\qquad b\cdot\nabla u\in L_{1}(Q_{T^{\prime}})

by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem we obtain

\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla u\,\big({\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u_{h})-{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\big)\,dxdt\ \to\ 0\qquad\mbox{as}\quad h\to+0

and hence (
4.9
) follows. Passing to the limit in (
4.7
) and taking into account (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) we arrive at the inequality

\begin{gathered}\|(u(\cdot,t_{2})-\delta)_{+}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\,|\Omega|\,+\,\|(u(\cdot,t_{1})-\delta)_{+}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ -\\
-\ \frac{1}{\delta}\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla u~{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ +\ \frac{1}{\delta}\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}f\,{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt.\end{gathered}

(4.11)

We have

\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla u~{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ =\ \int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cap D_{\delta}}b\cdot\nabla u~{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ +\ \delta\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}\cap A_{\delta}}b\cdot\nabla u\,dxdt,

where we denote

D_{\delta}\,:=\,\{\,(x,t)\in Q_{T}:~0\leq u(x,t)\leq\delta\,\},\qquad A_{\delta}\,:=\,\{\,(x,t)\in Q_{T}:~u(x,t)>\delta\,\}.

Taking into account the identities

\tfrac{1}{2}\,\nabla|{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)|^{2}\,=\,{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,\nabla u\quad\mbox{a.e. in}\quad D_{\delta},\qquad\nabla(u-\delta)_{+}\,=\,\nabla u\quad\mbox{a.e. in}\quad A_{\delta}

we obtain the relation

\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla u~{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ =\ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla|{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)|^{2}\,dxdt\ +\ \delta\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla(u-\delta)_{+}\,dxdt.

(4.12)

Applying the condition (
1.8
) we finally obtain

\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}b\cdot\nabla u~{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ \geq\ 0.

Hence we can drop this term in (
4.11
).
From
{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(s)\leq\delta
we also conclude

\frac{1}{\delta}\,\int\limits_{Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}}f\,{\mathcal{T}}_{\delta}(u)\,dxdt\ \leq\ \int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega[u(t)>0]}|f|\,dxdt,

where we denote
\Omega[u(t)>0]:=\{\,x\in\Omega:\,u(x,t)>0\,\}
. So, for any
\delta>0
we obtain

\|(u(\cdot,t_{2})-\delta)_{+}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta\,|\Omega|\,+\,\|(u(\cdot,t_{1})-\delta)_{+}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ +\ \int\limits_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega[u(t)>0]}|f|\,dxdt.

Taking the limit as
\delta\to+0
and varying

for any
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}<T
we arrive at

\|(u(\cdot,t_{2}))_{+}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \|(u(\cdot,t_{1}))_{+}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\,+\,\|f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}[u>0])},

(4.13)

where we denote
Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}[u>0]:=\{\,(x,t)\in Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}:\,u(x,t)>0\,\}
.
Applying the same technique to the function
-u
we obtain also

\|(u(\cdot,t_{2}))_{-}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\ \leq\ \|(u(\cdot,t_{1}))_{-}\|_{L_{1}(\Omega)}\,+\,\|f\|_{L_{1}(Q_{t_{1},t_{2}}[u<0])}.

(4.14)

Hence (
1.10
) follows.
∎

Proof.

Denote

.

6
Counterexamples

In this section we discuss the proof of Proposition
1.1
. In the case of the whole space
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
the non-trivial weak solution
u\not\equiv 0
(in the sense of Definition
1.1
) to the Cauchy problem

\left\{\quad\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u\ =\ 0\qquad\mbox{in}\qquad\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T),\\
u|_{t=0}\ =\ 0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\end{gathered}\right.

(6.1)

can be obtained easily if we multiply the fundamental solution of the heat equation

\Gamma(x,t)=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad t>0,

by some positive power of
t
. Indeed, assume

u(x,t)\ =\ t^{-\alpha}\,e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}

and

b(x,t)\ =\ \left(n-2\alpha\right)\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}.

It is clear that

b\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{n,w}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))

and
u
and
b
satisfy the parabolic equation (
6.1
) everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T)
. Moreover,

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\ =\ c_{n}\,t^{\frac{n}{2}-2\alpha},\qquad\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}\ =\ c_{n}\,t^{\frac{n}{2}-1-2\alpha}

(6.2)

and hence for any

\alpha\,<\,\tfrac{n}{4}

(6.3)

we obtain

u\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})),\qquad\nabla u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,T))

and

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ \to\ 0\qquad\mbox{as}\qquad t\to+0.

So, for any
\alpha
satisfying (
6.3
)
u
is a weak solution to the problem (
6.1
). In particular, one can take
\alpha=0
. To construct a non-trivial weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) in the case of a bounded domain
\Omega
we can localize the construction above. So, we present the proof of Proposition
1.1
.

Proof.

Assume
\Omega=\{\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,|x|<1\,\}
. Take

u(x,t)\ =\ \zeta(x,t)~e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},\qquad\zeta(x,t)\ =\ \zeta_{0}(|x|,t),\qquad\zeta_{0}(r,t)\ =\ e^{\frac{r-1}{t}}-1

and

b(x,t)\ =\ b_{0}(|x|,t)\,\frac{x}{|x|^{2}},\qquad b_{0}(r,t)\ =\ n-1\ -\ \frac{\zeta_{0}}{\frac{2}{r}\left(\zeta_{0}+1\right)-\zeta_{0}}.

Note that

-1\leq\zeta_{0}(r,t)\leq 0,\qquad\forall\,r\in[0,1],\qquad\forall\,t>0,

and hence

-1\ \leq\ \frac{\zeta_{0}}{\frac{2}{r}\left(\zeta_{0}+1\right)-\zeta_{0}}\ \leq\ 0,\qquad\forall\,r\in[0,1],\quad\forall\,t>0.

This implies that

b\in L_{\infty}(0,T;L_{n,w}(\Omega)).

On the other hand, the direct computations show that

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+b\cdot\nabla u\ =\ \left[\left(\partial_{t}\zeta_{0}-\partial_{r}^{2}\zeta_{0}+\frac{|x|}{t}\,\partial_{r}\zeta_{0}\right)+(b_{0}-n+1)\left(\frac{\partial_{r}\zeta_{0}}{|x|}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2t}\right)\ +\ \frac{\zeta_{0}}{2t}\right]~e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

As
\zeta_{0}
and
b_{0}
satisfy

\partial_{t}\zeta_{0}-\partial_{r}^{2}\zeta_{0}+\frac{r}{t}\,\partial_{r}\zeta_{0}\ =\ 0,\qquad b_{0}-n+1\ =\ -\frac{\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2t}}{\frac{\partial_{r}\zeta_{0}}{r}-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2t}}

we conclude that

u
satisfies (
1.1
) everywhere in
Q_{T}
. Finally, it is obvious that
u|_{\partial\Omega\times(0,T)}=0
and from
|\zeta(x,t)|\leq 1
we get

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \leq\ \int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}\,dx\ =\ c_{n}t^{\frac{n}{2}}\ \to\ 0\qquad\mbox{as}\qquad t\to+0.

Moreover,

\nabla u(x,t)=\frac{1}{t}\,e^{-\frac{(|x|-2)^{2}}{4t}}\frac{x}{|x|}-\frac{x}{2t}\Big(e^{\frac{|x|-1}{t}}-1\Big)e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}.

As
|x|\leq 1
we obtain
(|x|-2)^{2}\geq 1
and hence

\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\Omega)}\leq c_{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2t}}t^{-2}+c_{n}t^{\tfrac{n}{2}-1},\qquad\forall\,t>0.

So, we derive

\nabla u\in L_{2}(Q_{T}).

Hence
u
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) corresponding to
u_{0}\equiv 0
and
f\equiv 0
.

∎

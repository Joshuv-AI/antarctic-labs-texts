Global hypoellipticity on time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov spaces via non-discrete Fourier analysis Thanks: This study was financed in part by the Coordenação de Aperfeiçoamento de Pessoal de Nível Superior - Brasil (CAPES) - Finance Code 001.
André Pedroso Kowacs, Pedro Meyer Tokoro

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide a characterization of the time-periodic Gelfand–Shilov spaces, as introduced by F. de Ávila Silva and M. Cappiello [J. Funct. Anal., 282(9):29, 2022], through the asymptotic behaviour of both the Euclidean and periodic partial Fourier transforms of their elements. As an application, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for global regularity—within this framework—for a broad class of constant-coefficient differential operators, as well as for first-order tube-type operators.

Primary 35B65, 42B05; Secondary 46F05

1.
Introduction

Time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov spaces were defined by F. de Ávila Silva and M. Cappiello in
[
11
]
to study the global regularity of time-periodic evolution operators on Gelfand-Shilov spaces. More precisely, the authors study the global hypoellipticity of operators the form

L=D_{t}+c(t)P(x,D_{x})

on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\mathbb{T}^{1}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\simeq\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
is the
1
-dimensional torus,
c
is a Gevrey complex-valued function on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
, and
P(x,D_{x})
is a self-adjoint differential operator with polynomial coefficients on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying global ellipticity conditions.

Given
\mu,\nu>0
, the Gelfand-Shilov space
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of all smooth functions
\varphi
for which there exists
C>0
such that

\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}C^{-|\alpha|-|\beta|}\alpha!^{-\nu}\beta!^{-\mu}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\varphi(x)|<+\infty.

These spaces were introduced by I. Gelfand and G. E. Shilov in
[
15
]
as an alternative setting for studying differential equations. The functions belonging to
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
present uniform Gevrey regularity and a certain exponential decay at infinity, and the spaces
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
have applications in the study of well-posedness of evolution equations, see
[
3
,
5
]
for instance. Moreover, functions on Gelfand-Shilov spaces arise naturally in applications. For instance, as shown in
[
10
]
, the eigenfunctions of generalized harmonic oscillators belong to
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for suitable
\mu
and
\nu
.

The time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov space
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, as introduced in
[
11
]
, is the space of smooth functions which are uniformly Gevrey regular of order
\sigma
on the periodic variables and Gelfand-Shilov behaviour of order
\mu
on the real variables.
More precisely, it is the space of functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}C^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}\gamma!^{-\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\varphi(t,x)|<+\infty,

for some
C>0
.

In
[
11
]
, the authors developed a discrete Fourier analysis for
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
by combining the partial Fourier transform on the torus with an eigenfunction expansion induced by a globally elliptic self-adjoint operator
P(x,D_{x})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as developed in
[
16
]
.

Inspired by
[
21
,
22
]
, we introduce a Fourier analysis for functions on time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov spaces, combining the Fourier series on the torus with the classical Euclidean Fourier transform. More precisely, we provide a characterization for functions on
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
by means of the decay of its partial and total Fourier transforms, as well as a partial characterization of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

As an application of these results, we study the global regularity for certain classes of differential operators
P
in the general sense that

(1.1)

u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),\ Pu\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\ \Rightarrow\ u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

In this work, we consider operators that are not covered by the results of
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, as linear partial differential operators with constant coefficients and tube-type first order operators. More specifically, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for such regularity property for constant coefficients differential operators on the cylinder
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with no mixed terms satisfying suitable assumptions, as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant coefficients and no mixed terms, such that

P=p\left(D_{x}\right)+q\left(D_{t}\right),

where
p
and
q
are complex polynomials and
\deg(p)=N
. Suppose that one of the following conditions hold:

(1)

N\leq 1
,
\mu\geq 1/2
.

(2)

N>1
,
\mu\geq 1
and there exist
C,R>0
such that

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq C|\xi|^{N-1},\quad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb{Z},\ |\xi|\geq R.

Then
P
satisfies (
1.1
) for every
\sigma\geq 1
if and only if its symbol does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Due to the non-discreteness of the variable
\xi
, the existence of a root for
p+q
in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
allows the construction of a singular solution, a fact that does not happen when we deal with operators in the context of a discrete Fourier analysis, as in the case of the torus, compact Lie groups or when dealing with the Fourier analysis induced by certain elliptic operators in general. We also note that our results do not exhibit Diophantine approximation phenomena, as usually occurs in the discrete setting, see for instance
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
and references therein. This lack of Diophantine conditions
on the continuous setting is to be expected as first evidenced by
[
18
]
.

In the tube case, that is, operators of the form

P=\partial_{t}+c(t)\partial_{x}+q(t),

for any Gevrey regular complex-valued functions
c,q
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
, we characterize when the regularity property (
1.1
) holds, but allowing for certain loss of Gevrey regularity on the periodic variables, as in
[
11
]
. More precisely, when
c
is real valued, we show that the global regularity of
P
is reduced to study this property for an operator with constant coefficients. When of
\mathrm{Im}(c)
is not zero, then the global regularity of
P
is equivalent to
\mathrm{Im}(c)
not changing sign, a property that is related to the famous Nirenberg-Trèves condition
(\mathcal{P})
, in addition to the global regularity of an associated operator with constant coefficients.

We can summarize our results of the tube case as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\sigma\geq 1
, and
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}
given by

P=\partial_{t}+(a(t)+ib(t))\partial_{x}+q(t),

where
a,b,q\in\mathscr{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{1})
and
a,b
are real valued. Also let
a_{0},b_{0},q_{0}
denote the respective averages over
\mathbb{T}^{1}
. Then for every
\mu>1
,
P
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic (see Definition
5.1
) if and only if one of the following holds

(1)

a,b,q
are constant and

(a)

b\neq 0
and
\dfrac{a}{b}\mathrm{Re}(q)+\mathrm{Im}(q)\not\in\mathbb{Z}
, or

(b)

b=0
and either
\mathrm{Re}(q)\neq 0
, or both
a=\mathrm{Re}(q)=0
and
\mathrm{Im}(q)\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

b\equiv 0
and either
\mathrm{Re}(q_{0})\neq 0
, or both
a_{0}=\mathrm{Re}(q_{0})=0
and
\mathrm{Im}(q_{0})\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

b\not\equiv 0
,
b
does not change sign and
\dfrac{a_{0}}{b_{0}}\mathrm{Re}(q_{0})+\mathrm{Im}(q_{0})\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Moreover, if
b\equiv 0
or
a,b,q
are constant, then the characterization above also holds for
\mu\geq 1/2
.

This paper is structured in the following manner: in Section
2
, we recall the fundamental theory of time-periodic Gelfand–Shilov spaces and prove certain topological properties of these spaces. In Section
3
we introduce the toroidal and Euclidean partial Fourier transforms in the time-periodic Gelfand–Shilov spaces, and study how these classes of functions and distributions behave under these Fourier transforms. In Sections
4
and
5
we apply the theory and results from the previous sections to the study of certain types of global hypoellipticity of two classes of differential operators. Appendix
A
contains a few technical lemmas used in the proofs throughout the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Gelfand-Shilov spaces

Given
\mu,\nu>0
, the Gelfand-Shilov space
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the space of the functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that there exists
C>0
satisfying

\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}C^{-|\alpha|-|\beta|}\alpha!^{-\nu}\beta!^{-\mu}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\varphi(x)|<+\infty.

We have also the following equivalent characterizations for Gelfand-Shilov spaces, which we refer the reader to
[
26
, Theorem 6.1.6 and Proposition 6.1.7]
for a proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

A function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if one of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

There exist
C,L>0
such that

\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}C^{-|\beta|}\beta!^{-\mu}e^{L|x|^{1/\nu}}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\varphi(x)|<+\infty.

(2)

There exist
C,L>0
such that

|\varphi(x)|\leq Ce^{-L|x|^{1/\nu}}\quad\text{and}\quad|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|\leq Ce^{-L|\xi|^{1/\mu}}.

Clearly, every Gelfand-Shilov space is contained in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if and only if
\widehat{\varphi}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular, if
\mu=\nu
, the space
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is invariant under the action of the Fourier transform.

Notice that the functions of
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are Gevrey functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that admit exponential decay at infinity. In particular,

\mathscr{G}_{c}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathscr{G}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\mathscr{G}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of Gevrey functions of order
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is, the space of the
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that, for every compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exist
C_{0},C_{1}>0
such that

\sup_{x\in K}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha}\alpha!^{\mu},

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, and
\mathscr{G}_{c}^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of compactly supported Gevrey functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is non-trivial for
\mu>1
. For more details, see
[
28
, Chapter I]
.

Now, for
\mu,\nu>0
fixed, given
C>0
, consider the space
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{\nu,C}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of all the smooth functions
\varphi
such that

\|\varphi\|_{\mu,\nu,C}=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}C^{-|\alpha|-|\beta|}\alpha!^{-\nu}\beta!^{-\mu}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\varphi(x)|<+\infty.

Then, each
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{\nu,C}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mu,\nu,C}
and

\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\bigcup_{C>0}\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{\nu,C}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and we endow
\mathcal{S}^{\mu}_{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with the inductive limit topology. For more details on the characterization of the Gelfand-Shilov spaces, we refer the reader to
[
27
]
and Section 6 of
[
26
]
.

In this work, we will restrict our attention to the symmetric case
\mu=\nu\geq 1/2
due to the invariance of these spaces under the Fourier transform and the fact that the Gelfand-Shilov spaces are trivial when
\mu+\nu<1

[
26
, Theorem 6.1.10]
.

2.2.
Time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov spaces

Here, we recall the definition of time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov spaces as introduced by F. de Ávila Silva and M. Cappiello in
[
11
]
, as well as the fundamental facts concerning these spaces.

First, let us recall the definition of the time-periodic Schwartz
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
as established in
[
21
,
22
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be the space of all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}=\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|<+\infty,

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
.

The seminorms
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
endow
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with a Fréchet space structure. Proceeding as in
[
30
, Theorem 14.4]
, one can show that
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a Montel space.

Fix
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
. Given
C>0
, we say that a function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if

\|\varphi\|_{\sigma,\mu,C}:=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}C^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}\gamma!^{-\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\varphi(t,x)|<+\infty.

Each
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a Banach space with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\sigma,\mu,C}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
, define the time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov space as

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}):=\bigcup_{C>0}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

endowed with the inductive limit topology.

Consequently, a sequence
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
converges to a
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
in the sense of
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if there exists
C>0
such that
\varphi,\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\|\varphi_{j}-\varphi\|_{\sigma,\mu,C}\to 0
.

Remark 1
.

From the equivalence stated on Theorem
2.1
, the norms

\|\varphi\|_{\sigma,\mu,C,L}:=\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}C^{-|\beta|-|\gamma|}\gamma!^{-\sigma}\beta!^{-\mu}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{L|x|^{1/\mu}}|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\varphi(t,x)|,

with
C,L>0
, define an equivalent topology on
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

The next result gives us that
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a DFS space, that is,
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is the inductive limit of a compact sequence of Banach spaces (see
[
20
]
).

Theorem 2.4
.

If
C<C_{+}
, the inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is continuous and compact. In particular,
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a DFS space.

Proof.

First, notice that, for all
C>0
, the inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is continuous. Indeed, given
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, we have that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|

\displaystyle=C^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}C^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\gamma!^{-\sigma}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq(C^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma})\|f\|_{\sigma,\mu,C}.

Also, it is clear that the inclusion map
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is continuous. It remains to show its compactness. Let
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, that is, there exists
h>0
such that
\|f_{j}\|_{\sigma,\mu,C}\leq h
, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Since the inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is continuous and by the fact that
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a Montel space, it follows that
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
admits a subsequence
(f_{j_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
which converges to some
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
in the sense of
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, that is:

\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f_{j_{k}}(t,x)|\to\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|,

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
.
Since

\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}C^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\gamma!^{-\sigma}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f_{j_{k}}(t,x)|\leq h,

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the same inequality also holds for
f
, which gives us that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Let us show that
f_{j_{k}}\to f
in
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
K>0
be such that
(C_{+}/C)^{-K}<\varepsilon/2h
. Writing
C_{+}=C(C_{+}/C)
, for
|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|\geq K
, we obtain that

\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}C_{+}^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\gamma!^{-\sigma}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(f_{j_{k}}-f)(t,x)|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2h}\|f_{j_{k}}-f\|_{\sigma,\mu,C}<\varepsilon,

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Also, since
f_{j_{k}}\to f
in
\mathcal{S}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
we can choose
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that

\max_{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|<K}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(f_{j_{k}}-f)(t,x)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{0}},

for every
k\geq k_{0}
, where

C_{0}=\displaystyle\max_{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|<K}\{C_{+}^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\gamma!^{-\sigma}\}.

Therefore

\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}C_{+}^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\gamma!^{-\sigma}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(f_{j_{k}}-f)(t,x)|\leq C_{0}\frac{\varepsilon}{C_{0}}=\varepsilon,

for every
k\geq k_{0}
and
|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|<K
.

We conclude that
\|f_{j_{k}}-f\|_{\sigma,\mu,C_{+}}<\varepsilon
for every
k\geq k_{0}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary, we have that
f_{j_{k}}\to f
in
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,C_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, which gives us the compactness of the inclusion map.

Definition 2.5
.

Given
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
, let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be the set of of all linear functionals
u:\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\varphi_{j}\to\varphi\ \text{in}\ \mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\ \Rightarrow\ \langle u,\varphi_{j}\rangle\to\langle u,\varphi\rangle\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{C},

that is, the set of sequentially continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. We will refer to its elements as tempered ultradistributions.

Since the space
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is DFS (in particular, a Montel DF space), we have that sequential continuity is equivalent to continuity, see
[
32
]
. As a consequence of the previous considerations, we obtain the following characterization:

Proposition 2.6
.

A linear functional
u:\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if, for each
C_{1}>0
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

|\langle u,\varphi\rangle|\leq C_{0}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}C_{1}^{-|\alpha+\beta|-|\gamma|}\gamma!^{-\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\varphi(t,x)|,

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Next, recall that for
\sigma\geq 1
, the Gevrey space on the torus
\mathscr{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{m})
is defined as the space of smooth functions
\psi:\mathbb{T}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.1)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{m}}|\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\psi(t)|<C_{0}C_{1}^{\gamma}\gamma!^{\sigma},

for some
C_{0},C_{1}>0
and every
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, where
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
do not depend on
\gamma
.

The next proposition shows that
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a
\mathscr{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{m})
-module.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
\psi\in\mathscr{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{m})
and
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then
\psi f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proof.

If
\mathscr{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{m})
and
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then there exist positive constants
C_{0,\psi}
,
C_{1,\psi}
,
C_{0,f}
and
C_{1,f}
such that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{m}}|\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\psi(t)|\leq C_{0,\psi}C_{1,\psi}^{|\gamma|}\gamma!^{\sigma},\ \forall\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m},

and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\leq C_{0,f}C_{1,f}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma},\ \forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\ \gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}.

By the inclusions in Theorem
2.4
, we can assume without loss of generality that all these constants are greater than
1
. Then, for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, by the Leibniz formula, we have that

\displaystyle\left|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}[\psi(t)f(t,x)]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\ell}|\partial_{t}^{\ell}\psi(t)||x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma-\ell}f(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\ell}C_{0,\psi}C_{1,\psi}^{|\ell|}\ell!^{\sigma}C_{0,f}C_{1,f}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma-\ell|}(\gamma-\ell)!^{\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0,\psi}C_{0,f}2^{|\gamma|}C_{1,\psi}^{|\gamma|}C_{1,f}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}\gamma!^{\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}\gamma!^{\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu},

for every
(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
C_{0}=C_{0,\psi}C_{0,f}
and
C_{1}=2C_{1,\psi}C_{1,f}
. Therefore
\psi f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

3.
Fourier analysis

In this section we introduce the partial and total Fourier transforms for time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov functions and ultradistributions. The construction is made by taking a partial Fourier series on the periodic variables and a partial Fourier transform on the real variables, following the ideas introduced by the first author in
[
21
]
. Moreover, we give a characterization of functions on these spaces by means of the decay of the corresponding Fourier coefficients. Additionally, we obtain some properties for certain classes of tempered ultradistributions. For the rest of this section, we shall consider arbitrary
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
, unless specified otherwise.

3.1.
Fourier analysis for time-periodic Gelfand-Shilov functions

We define the partial Fourier transforms of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
by:

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f)(t,\xi)=\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\vcentcolon=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(t,x)e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\,\mathrm{d}x,

for a.e.
(t,\xi)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, and

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(f)(k,x)=\widehat{f}(k,x)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}f(t,x)e^{-ik\cdot t}\,\mathrm{d}t,

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Also define its mixed partial Fourier transform by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f))(k,\xi)=\ftil(k,\xi)

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(t,x)e^{-i(k\cdot t+\xi\cdot x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Note that, by Fubini’s Theorem, these expressions are all well defined and, moreover,
\widehat{f}(\cdot,\xi)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{m})
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\widehat{f}(k,\cdot),\ftil(k,\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exist
C_{0},C_{1},\varepsilon>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},\quad\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n};

(ii)

There exist
C,h>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{0\}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}h^{N+1}N!|k|^{-N/\sigma},\quad\forall N\in\mathbb{N},\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n};

(iii)

There exist
C,h>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{0\}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}h(hN)^{N}|k|^{-N/\sigma},\quad\forall N\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

Proof.

The proof follows from the definition and by arguments similar to those found in the proof of
[
28
, Lemma 1.6.2]
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Given
C,N>0
and
\sigma\geq 1
, let
M
be the smallest positive integer such that
M\geq N/\sigma
. Then, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
independent on
N
such that

C^{M+1}M^{\sigma M}\leq C^{\prime}(C^{\prime}N)^{N}.

Proof.

[
28
, Lemma 1.6.3]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Given
N>0
, suppose that there exist
C_{0},C_{1},L,h>0
such that

|x^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(t,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}h^{|\gamma|}M^{\sigma|\gamma|},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
,
(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, and
|\gamma|\leq M
, where
M
is the smallest positive integer such that
M\geq N/\sigma
. Then, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}C^{\prime}(C^{\prime}N)^{N}|k|^{-N/\sigma},

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
which does not depend on
N
.

Proof.

First, notice that

x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f}(k,x)=x^{\alpha}(ik)^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x).

Therefore if
|\gamma|=M
we have that

|k^{\gamma}x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}h^{M}M^{\sigma M},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
k\neq 0
, this implies that

\displaystyle|k|^{N/\sigma}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|

\displaystyle\leq|k|^{M}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\ \leq\ \sum_{|\gamma|=M}\dfrac{M!}{\gamma!}|k^{\gamma}||x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\ \sum_{|\gamma|=M}\dfrac{M!}{\gamma!}C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}h^{M}M^{\sigma M}

\displaystyle=m^{M}C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}h^{M}M^{\sigma M},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Therefore

|k|^{N/\sigma}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq\tilde{C}^{M+1}M^{\sigma M}C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu},

where
\tilde{C}=\max\{1,mh\}
. Applying Lemma
3.2
to the inequality above, we have that there exists
C^{\prime}>0
independent on
N
such that

|k|^{N/\sigma}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}C^{\prime}(C^{\prime}N)^{N},

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

A function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if there exist
C_{0},C_{1},\varepsilon>0
such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we have that

(3.1)

{}\sup_{(k,x)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}.

Proof.

First, suppose that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then given
N>0
, we have that

|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}C_{1}^{|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}C_{1}^{M}M^{\sigma M},

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
,
(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
, with
|\gamma|=M
, where
M
is the smallest positive integer such that
M\geq N/\sigma
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have that condition
(iii)
from Lemma
3.1
holds, implying that inequality (
3.1
) also holds.

On the other hand, suppose that (
3.1
) holds. The previous estimates imply that the series

g(t,x)\vcentcolon=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\widehat{f}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}

converges absolutely and uniformly on
\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Moreover, the series

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\left(\widehat{f}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}\right)

also converges absolutely and uniformly on
\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, so we can exchange the order of summation and differentiation and the series above corresponds to
\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}g(t,x)
, from which we conclude that
g\in C^{\infty}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Finally, given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, we have that

\displaystyle\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}g(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\widehat{f}(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}|k|^{|\gamma|}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|k|^{|\gamma|}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}<\ +\infty.

Finally, by the Fourier inversion theorem, it follows that the series above coincides with
f
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 2
.

In view of the previous theorem, given a sequence
(g(k,x))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}
satisfying (
3.1
), the series

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}g(k,x)e^{ikt}

defines a function
g\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Also, if
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we have that

f(t,x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\widehat{f}(k,x)e^{ikt}.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
\nu\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then, there exist
C_{0,\nu},C_{1,\nu}\geq 1
such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
,

\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x^{\nu}x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\leq C_{0,\nu}C_{1,\nu}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}.

Proof.

Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, there exist
C_{0},C_{1}\geq 1
such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,
\ \gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
and
(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
we have that

|x^{\nu}x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}C_{1}^{|\nu|}(\alpha+\nu)!^{\mu}\beta!^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}.

Therefore

\displaystyle|x^{\nu}x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}C_{1}^{|\nu|}(2^{|\alpha|+|\nu|}\alpha!\nu!)^{\mu}\beta!^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}(2^{\mu|\nu|}C_{1}^{|\nu|}\nu!^{\mu})(2^{\mu}C_{1})^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}.

Taking
C_{0,\nu}=C_{0}2^{\mu|\nu|}C_{1}^{|\nu|}\nu!^{\mu}
and
C_{1,\nu}=2^{\mu}C_{1}
, the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

A function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if there exist
C_{0},C_{1}>0
such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}
, we have that

(3.2)

\sup_{(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma}.

Proof.

First, suppose that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. By Lemma
3.5
, we have that

\displaystyle|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{-N}(1+|x|)^{N}|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{N}|x^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{-N}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|+|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}\gamma!^{\sigma},

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
satisfies
N>n
so that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{-N}\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty
, and
C_{0}=(n+1)^{N}C_{0,N},C_{1}=(n+1)^{N}C_{1,N}
are given by Lemma
3.5
, since
(1+|x|)^{N}
is a polynomial on
|x_{1}|,\dots,|x_{n}|
.

Conversely, suppose that (
3.2
) holds. By Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem and the Fourier inversion theorem, we have that

\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi

is a smooth function and coincides with
f
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}f(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\xi\cdot x}\xi^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\widehat{f}(t,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|.

Following the same arguments of the proof of the first implication, it follows that
f
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

A function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\cap L^{1}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if and only if there exist
C_{0},C_{1},\varepsilon>0
such that, for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

(3.3)

\sup_{(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\ftil(k,\xi)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}.

Proof.

The statement is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.4
and Corollary
3.7
.
∎

Remark 3
.

From now, we will denote
\ftil(k,\xi)
simply by

for simplicity.

3.2.
Fourier analysis for ultradistributions

Now, let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Define its partial Fourier transforms by

\langle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u)(t,\xi),f(t,\xi)\rangle=\langle u(t,\xi),\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f)(t,\xi)\rangle,

for every
\,f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, and

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(u)(k,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

given by

\langle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(u)(k,x),f(x)\rangle=\langle u(t,x),e^{ik\cdot t}f(x)\rangle,

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.
We also define its mixed partial Fourier transform by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u))(k,\cdot)\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

given by

\left\langle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u))(k,\xi),f(\xi)\right\rangle=\langle u(t,\xi),e^{-ik\cdot t}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f)(\xi)\rangle,

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

Lastly, for
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
we also define

\langle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{-1}u(t,x),f(t,x)\rangle=\langle u(t,x),\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{-1}f(t,x)\rangle,

where

\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f)(t,x)\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(t,\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

denotes the partial inverse Fourier transform for
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
By Corollary
3.7
and the continuity of the partial Fourier transform it follows that
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{-1}u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, for any
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then for every
C_{1},\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{C}_{0}=\tilde{C}_{0}(\varepsilon,C_{1})>0
such that

(3.4)

|\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),f(x)\rangle|\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\|f\|_{\mu,C_{1}}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, where

(3.5)

\|f\|_{\mu,C_{1}}=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}C_{1}^{-|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(x)|.

Proof.

Given
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, notice that
(t,x)\mapsto e^{ik\cdot t}f(x)\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. By Proposition
2.6
for every
\tilde{C}_{1}>0
there exists
\tilde{C}_{0}=\tilde{C}_{0}(\tilde{C}_{1})>0
such that

\displaystyle|\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),f(x)\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle u,e^{ik\cdot t}f(x)\rangle|\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}\tilde{C}_{1}^{-|\alpha+\beta|}\tilde{C}_{1}^{-|\gamma|}\gamma!^{-\sigma}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{m}}|\partial_{t}^{\gamma}e^{ik\cdot t}|\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}\left(\frac{{C_{1}}}{\tilde{C}_{1}}\right)^{|\alpha+\beta|}C_{1}^{-|\alpha+\beta|}\tilde{C}_{1}^{-|\gamma|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}(|\gamma|!)^{-\sigma}|k|^{|\gamma|}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m}}\left(\frac{{C_{1}}}{\tilde{C}_{1}}\right)^{|\alpha+\beta|}C_{1}^{-|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\left(\frac{(\sigma/\varepsilon)^{\sigma}}{\tilde{C}_{1}}\right)^{|\gamma|}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(x)|,

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, where in the last line we applied Lemma
A.5
. By choosing
\tilde{C}_{1}\geq\max\{(\sigma/\varepsilon)^{\sigma},C_{1}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\langle\widehat{u}(k,x),f(x)\rangle|\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}C_{1}^{-|\alpha+\beta|}(\alpha!\beta!)^{-\mu}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(x)|e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that, for each function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
,
(\langle u_{j},\varphi\rangle)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
. Then, there exists a unique
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
u=\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}
.

Proof.

See
[
21
, Lemma 2.1]
and
[
11
, Theorem 2.9]
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
(u(k,\cdot))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which satisfies (
3.4
). Then, there exists a unique
\widecheck{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

\langle\widecheck{u},f\rangle=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\langle u(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\langle u(k,x),f(t,x)\rangle e^{ik\cdot t}\,\mathrm{d}t,

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. In this case, we define
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}^{-1}u=\widecheck{u}
.

Proof.

For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the
S_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
given by

\displaystyle\langle S_{j}(t,x),f(t,x)\rangle\

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|\leq j}\langle u(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t},f(t,x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|\leq j}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\langle u(k,x),f(t,x)\rangle e^{ik\cdot t}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{|k|\leq j}\langle u(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle,

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, Proposition
3.9
implies that for all
C,\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{C}_{0}=\tilde{C}_{0}(\varepsilon,C_{1})>0
such that

\displaystyle|\langle{u}(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\|\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\|_{\mu,C}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\mu,C}
is given by (
3.5
). Therefore, given
j,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-m}|\langle S_{j+\ell},f\rangle-\langle S_{j},f\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|>j}^{j+\ell}|\langle{u}(k,x),\widehat{f}(-k,x)\rangle|\leq\sum_{|k|>j}^{j+\ell}\tilde{C}_{0}\|\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\|_{\mu,C}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|k|>j}^{\infty}\tilde{C}_{0}\|\widehat{f}(-k,\cdot)\|_{\mu,C}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}.

Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, by Proposition
3.4
there exist
C_{f},\varepsilon_{0},C_{0}>0
such that

\|\widehat{f}(k,\cdot)\|_{\mu,C_{f}}\leq C_{0}e^{-\varepsilon_{0}|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
.

Taking
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}/2
, we have that

|\langle S_{j+\ell},f\rangle-\langle S_{j},f\rangle|\leq(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{|k|>j}^{\infty}C^{\prime}e^{-\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}|k|^{1/\sigma}}<+\infty,

for
C^{\prime}=\tilde{C}_{0}(C_{f},\varepsilon_{0}/2)C_{0}
, which implies that

\lim_{j\to\infty}|\langle S_{j+\ell},f\rangle-\langle S_{j},f\rangle|=0

due to the convergence of the series. Therefore,
(\langle S_{j},f\rangle)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{C}
for all
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. It follows from Lemma
3.10
that

\widecheck{u}=(2\pi)^{m}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}u(k,x)e^{ik\cdot t}=\lim_{j\to\infty}S_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

which finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Consider a function
u:\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying the following property: for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(3.6)

|u(t,\xi)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon|\xi|^{1/\mu}},

for all
(t,x)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Then, there exists
\widecheck{u}\in\bigcap_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that, for each
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we have that

\displaystyle\langle\widecheck{u},f\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,\xi)e^{i\xi\cdot x}f(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,\xi)\widehat{f}(t,-\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t.

Moreover,
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}({u})=\widecheck{u}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, for some
\sigma\geq 1
. By Remark
1
and Corollary
3.7
, there exist
C,L>0
such that

|\widehat{f}(t,\xi)|\leq Ce^{-L|\xi|^{1/\mu}},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

By choosing
\varepsilon=L/2
in inequality (
3.6
), we see that the integrals above are finite. Therefore the map
f\mapsto\langle\widecheck{u},f\rangle
is well-defined and linear. Now, let
(f_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
converging to
0
in
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, for some
\sigma\geq 1
. By the continuity of Fourier transform, we have that
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{f_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\equiv(\widehat{f_{j}})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
also converges to
0
in
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Then, in view of Remark
1
, there exist
C,L>0
such that

\|\widehat{f_{j}}\|_{\sigma,\mu,C,L}\to 0,\ j\to\infty.

Once again considering
\varepsilon=L/2
in inequality (
3.6
), we have that

\displaystyle|\langle\widecheck{u},f_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(t,\xi)\widehat{f_{j}}(t,-\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq{(2\pi)^{-n}C_{L/2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{L}{2}|\xi|^{1/\mu}}e^{L|\xi|^{1/\mu}}|\widehat{f_{j}}(t,-\xi)|\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\|\widehat{f_{j}}\|_{\sigma,\mu,C,L}\to 0,

as
j\to\infty
, where

0<C^{\prime}={(2\pi)^{-n+m}C_{L/2}C}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\frac{L}{2}|\xi|^{1/\mu}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi<+\infty.

Therefore we conclude that
\widecheck{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.
∎

Finally, from the previous results, we obtain:

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
(u(k,\xi))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}
be a sequence of complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
satisfying

|u(k,\xi)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}e^{\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}\!e^{\varepsilon|\xi|^{1/\mu}}

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(u))
is well defined and belongs to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\mathbb{T}^{m}}(u))))=u
in the sense of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

4.
Global hypoellipticity of operators with constant coefficients

From now, we apply the Fourier analysis developed in the previous section to obtain a characterization for the global regularity of certain classes of differential operators on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

We will focus in a type of global regularity commonly known as global hypoellipticity. Following
[
11
]
, in this setting this concept is defined as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
. We say that a differential operator
P
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if

u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}),\ Pu\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})\ \Rightarrow\ u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}).

In this section we provide a characterization for the
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-global hypoellipticity of a class of differential operators with constant coefficients. From now on we will consider
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
arbitrary.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant coefficients. If
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic, then its symbol does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

First note that
P
can be written as

P=p(D_{t},D_{x}),

where
D=i^{-1}\partial
and
p\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
is a complex polynomial on two variables, which also corresponds to its symbol.

Suppose that
p(k,\xi)=0
for some
(k_{0},\xi_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, and consider the function
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
given by

u(t,x)=e^{i(k_{0}t+\xi_{0}x)},

for every
(t,x)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Notice that
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})\setminus\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
and
Pu=p(k_{0},\xi_{0})u=0
. Therefore,
P
is not
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic.
∎

In order to obtain sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-global hypoellipticity, we make some further assumptions on their symbols. First we consider the case of operators of order at most
1
in the variable
x
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant coefficients and no mixed terms, such that

P=p\left(D_{x}\right)+q\left(D_{t}\right),

where
p
and
q
are complex polynomials and
\deg(p)\leq 1
. Then
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if its symbol does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, it is enough to prove the sufficiency of the condition above.

Hence suppose that the set

\mathcal{Z}_{P}\vcentcolon=\{(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,p(\xi)+q(k)=0\}

is empty.

We claim that there exists
K_{0}>0
such that

(4.1)

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq K_{0},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

If
\deg(p)<1
, then
p(\xi)\equiv c\in\mathbb{C}
is constant. Consider the polynomial
\tilde{q}(k)\vcentcolon=c+q(k)
.
Then
\tilde{q}(k)\neq 0
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by our assumption. Since
\tilde{q}
is a closed map, we have that

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq\inf_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\tilde{q}(k)|>0,

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. If
\deg(p)=1
, without loss of generality we may assume that
p(\xi)=\xi+c_{0}
, for some
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
. Therefore

|p(\xi)+q(k)|^{2}=|\xi+c_{0}+q(k)|^{2}=|\xi+\mathrm{Re}(c_{0})+\mathrm{Re}(q(k))|^{2}+|\mathrm{Im}(c_{0})+\mathrm{Im}(q(k))|^{2},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. Note that
\mathcal{Z}_{P}=\varnothing
implies
\mathrm{Im}(c_{0})\not\in-\mathrm{Im}(q)(\mathbb{Z})
, for otherwise

(k_{0},-\mathrm{Re}(c_{0})-\mathrm{Re}(q(k_{0})))\in\mathcal{Z}_{P},

where
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
-\mathrm{Im}(q)(k_{0})=\mathrm{Im}(c_{0})
. Then since
\mathrm{Im}(q)
is a closed map, we have that

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{dist}(\mathrm{Im}(c_{0}),\mathrm{Im}(q)(\mathbb{Z}))>0,

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. This concludes the proof of inequality (
4.1
).

Therefore, if
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
is such that
Pu=f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
, by comparing Fourier coefficients we have that

\widehat{u}(k,\xi)=\dfrac{\widehat{f}(k,\xi)}{p(\xi)+q(k)},\quad(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then, given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, by Lemma
A.2
we have that

\displaystyle|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|=\left|\xi^{\alpha}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{p(\xi)+q(k)}\right)\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\ell}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)}{p(\xi)+q(k)}+\delta_{\deg(p),1}\xi^{\alpha}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\ell}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)(-1)^{\ell}\frac{1}{(p(\xi)+q(k))^{\ell+1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}^{-1}|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)|+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\ell}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)|K_{0}^{-(\ell+1)},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\delta_{\deg(p),1}=1
if
\deg(p)=1
and
0
otherwise.

Since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
, there exist
C_{0},C_{1}\geq 1
and
1\geq\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.2)

|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, for
K=\max\{K_{0}^{-1},1\}
, we have that

\displaystyle|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}^{-1}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta-\ell}(\alpha!(\beta-\ell)!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}K_{0}^{-(\ell+1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\beta}K^{\beta}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\tilde{C}_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\tilde{C}_{0}=C_{0}
and
\tilde{C}=2KC_{1}
.
Hence, by Proposition
3.8
, we have that
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
, which implies that
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic.
∎

In particular, for first-order operators, we have the following:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, the operator

P=c_{1}\partial_{x}+c_{2}\partial_{t}+c_{3}

is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if

\mathcal{Z}=\{(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,c_{1}\xi+c_{2}k-ic_{3}=0\}=\varnothing.

In particular, if
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{3}\neq 0
, then
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic.

Proof.

Note that
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if
i^{-1}P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic. Therefore the claim follows from Theorem
4.3
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, the operator

P=\partial_{t}+(a+ib)\partial_{x}+c

is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if one of the following conditions hold:

(i)

b\neq 0
and
\dfrac{a}{b}\mathrm{Re}(c)+\mathrm{Im}(c)\notin\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

b=0
and either
\mathrm{Re}(c)\neq 0
, or both
a=\mathrm{Re}(c)=0
and
\operatorname{Im}(c)\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{Z}=\varnothing
if and only if
(i)
or
(ii)
holds. First, note that
(k,\xi)\in\mathcal{Z}_{P}
if and only if

k+a\xi+\mathrm{Im}(c)=0\quad\text{and}\quad b\xi-\mathrm{Re}(c)=0.

In these conditions, if
(k,\xi)\in\mathcal{Z}_{P}
and
b\neq 0
, we must have
\xi=\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}(c)}{b}
. Substituting this value in the first equality, we obtain

k=-\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\mathrm{Re}(c)+\mathrm{Im}(c)\right).

Therefore we have that if
b\neq 0
,
\mathcal{Z}=\varnothing
if and only if
\dfrac{a}{b}\mathrm{Re}(c)+\mathrm{Im}(c)\not\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the other hand, if
b=0
and
\mathrm{Re}(c)\neq 0
, then
\mathcal{Z}=\varnothing
. In the case where
b=0
and
\mathrm{Re}(c)=0
, if
a\neq 0
we can choose

\xi_{k}=-\dfrac{k+\mathrm{Im}(c)}{a},

for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, so that
(k,\xi)\in\mathcal{Z}\neq\varnothing
. However, if
a=0
, then
(k,\xi)\in\mathcal{Z}_{P}
if and only if
k=-\mathrm{Im}(c)\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Then, the operator

P=\partial_{x}+(a+ib)\partial_{t}+c

is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if one of the following conditions hold:

(i)

b\neq 0
and
\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}(c)}{b}\notin\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

b=0
and
\mathrm{Re}(c)\neq 0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{Z}=\varnothing
if and only if
(i)
or
(ii)
holds.
Note that
(k,\xi)\in\mathcal{Z}_{P}
if and only if

ak+\xi+\mathrm{Im}(c)=0\quad\text{and}\quad bk-\mathrm{Re}(c)=0.

If
b\neq 0
, we have that
\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}(c)}{b}\notin\mathbb{Z}
implies
\mathcal{Z}=\varnothing
. On the other hand,
\dfrac{\mathrm{Re}(c)}{b}\in\mathbb{Z}
implies that

\left(\frac{\mathrm{Re}(c)}{b},-\left(\frac{\mathrm{Re}(c)}{b}+\mathrm{Im}(c)\right)\right)\in\mathcal{Z},

hence
\mathcal{Z}\neq\varnothing
. If
b=0
, then
\mathcal{Z}=\varnothing
if and only if
\mathrm{Re}(c)\neq 0
, since
ak+\xi+\mathrm{Im}(c)=0
for
\xi=-\mathrm{Im}(c)-ak
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Remark 4
.

Notice that in the results above, the conditions for
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-global hypoellipticity do not depend on
\sigma\geq 1
nor
\mu\geq 1/2
.

For operators of any order on the variable
x
, some further assumptions are necessary. From the proof of the previous theorem, note that the non-vanishing on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
condition for the symbol implies a positive lower bound for its absolute value. This is not true in general when
\deg(p)>1
. Indeed, following the notation of Theorem
4.3
, for
p(\xi)=-ix^{2}+x+i
and
q(k)=k^{2}-k
we have that
p(\xi)+q(k)\neq 0
for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, but

p(\sqrt{k^{2}+1})+q(k)=\sqrt{k^{2}+1}-k\to 0,

as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, due to the growth at infinity of the derivatives of
p(\xi)
when
\deg(p)>1
, we in fact need to assume a growth condition for the symbol and restrict to the case
\mu\geq 1
, as follows.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant coefficients and no mixed terms, such that

P=p\left(D_{x}\right)+q\left(D_{t}\right),

where
p
and
q
are complex polynomials,
\deg(p)=N>1
and there exist
C,R>0
such that

(4.3)

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq C|\xi|^{N-1},\quad\text{for every }k\in\mathbb{Z},\ |\xi|\geq R.

Then
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for
\mu\geq 1
if and only if its symbol does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2
, it is enough to prove the sufficiency of the condition above.

Hence suppose that the set

\mathcal{Z}_{P}\vcentcolon=\{(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}\,:\,p(\xi)+q(k)=0\}

is empty.

Note that by (
4.3
), there exist
K_{0}>0
and
R>0
such that

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq K_{0}|\xi|^{N-1},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
|\xi|\geq R
.

On the other hand, since
p
is continuous,
p(\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq R\})
is compact in
\mathbb{C}
and since
q(\mathbb{Z})\subset\mathbb{C}
is closed, we have that
\delta_{R}\vcentcolon=\operatorname{dist}(p(\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}:|\xi|\leq R\}),-q(\mathbb{Z}))>0
, so that

(4.4)

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq\delta_{R},\quad

for every
|\xi|\leq R
. Therefore taking
K_{1}=(1+R)^{-(N-1)}\min\{K_{0}R^{N-1},{\delta_{R}}\}
, we have that

(4.5)

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq K_{1}(1+|\xi|)^{N-1},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, if
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
is such that
Pu=f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
, by comparing Fourier coefficients we have that

\widehat{u}(k,\xi)=\dfrac{\widehat{f}(k,\xi)}{p(\xi)+q(k)},\quad(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}.

Then, given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, by Lemma
A.2
, for
\beta>0
we have that

\displaystyle|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|=\left|\xi^{\alpha}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{p(\xi)+q(k)}\right)\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\ell}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)}{p(\xi)+q(k)}+\xi^{\alpha}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\ell}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell^{\prime}}\binom{\ell+1}{\ell^{\prime}+1}\frac{\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}[(p(\xi)+q(k))^{\ell^{\prime}}]}{(p(\xi)+q(k))^{\ell^{\prime}+1}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}^{-1}(1+|\xi|)^{-(N-1)}|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)|+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-\ell}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)|\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+1}{\ell^{\prime}+1}\frac{1}{|p(\xi)+q(k)|^{\ell^{\prime}+1}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\eta|=\ell\end{subarray}}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}|p(\xi)+q(k)|^{\ell^{\prime}-|\text{supp}(\eta)|}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell^{\prime}\\
\eta_{i}\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\partial_{\xi}^{\eta_{i}}p(\xi)|,

where
\text{supp}(\eta)=\{1\leq i\leq\ell^{\prime}:\eta_{i}\neq 0\}
, for
\eta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\ell^{\prime}}
, since

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}^{\eta_{i}}[p(\xi)+q(k)]=\begin{cases}\partial_{\xi}^{\eta}p(\xi)&\text{if }\eta_{i}\neq 0,\\
p(\xi)+q(k)&\text{if }\eta_{i}=0.\end{cases}

Also, since
\deg(p)=N
, there exists
K\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle|\partial_{\xi}^{\ell}p(\xi)|\leq K(1+|\xi|)^{N-\ell},

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|p(\xi)+q(k)|^{\ell^{\prime}+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\eta|=\ell\end{subarray}}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}|p(\xi)+q(k)|^{\ell^{\prime}-|\text{supp}(\eta)|}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell^{\prime}\\
\eta_{i}\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\partial_{\xi}^{\eta_{i}}p(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\eta|=\ell}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}|p(\xi)+q(k)|^{-(1+|\text{supp}(\eta)|)}K^{|\text{supp}(\eta)|}(1+|\xi|)^{N\cdot|\text{supp}(\eta)|-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\eta|=\ell}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}K_{1}^{-(1+|\text{supp}(\eta)|)}K^{\ell^{\prime}}(1+|\xi|)^{-(N-1)(1+|\text{supp}(\eta)|)+N\cdot|\text{supp}(\eta)|-\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\eta|=\ell}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}K_{2}^{\ell+1}K^{\ell}(1+|\xi|)^{|\text{supp}(\eta)|-\ell-(N-1)}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}^{\ell+1}K^{\ell}\sum_{|\eta|=\ell}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}\leq K_{2}^{\ell+1}K^{\ell}e^{\ell}\ell!,

where
K_{2}=\max\{1,K_{1}^{-1}\}
and we have used the fact that

\sum_{|\eta|=\ell}\binom{\ell!}{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{\ell^{\prime}}}=(\ell^{\prime})^{\ell}\leq\ell^{\ell}\leq e^{\ell}\ell!.

Also, since
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
, there exist
C_{0},C_{1}\geq 1
and
1\geq\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.7)

|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{f}(k,\xi)|\leq C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Applying these estimates to (
4.6
), we have that

\displaystyle|\xi^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\widehat{u}(k,\xi)|

\displaystyle=K_{1}^{-1}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta-\ell}(\alpha!(\beta-\ell)!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+1}{\ell^{\prime}+1}K_{2}^{\ell+1}K^{\ell}e^{\ell}\ell!

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}^{-1}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\ell}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta-\ell}(\alpha!(\beta-\ell)!)^{\mu}K_{2}^{\beta+1}K^{\beta}e^{\beta}\ell!^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+1}{\ell^{\prime}+1}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}^{-1}C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+C_{0}C_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}K_{2}^{\beta+1}K^{\beta}e^{\beta}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}}2^{\beta}(3^{\beta}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\tilde{C}_{1}^{\alpha+\beta}(\alpha!\beta!)^{\mu}e^{-\varepsilon|k|^{1/\sigma}},

for every
(k,\xi)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\tilde{C}_{0}=C_{0}K_{2}
,
\tilde{C}_{1}=6eC_{1}KK_{2}
, and we used the fact that
\ell!\leq\ell!^{\mu}
, since
\mu\geq 1
, and the following estimate:

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}\binom{\ell+1}{\ell^{\prime}+1}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{\ell^{\prime}}\frac{\ell+1}{\ell^{\prime}+1}\leq\beta+1\sum_{\ell=1}^{\beta}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\ell}\binom{\ell}{\ell^{\prime}}\leq 2^{\beta}(3^{\beta}-1)\leq 6^{\beta}.

An analogous argument yields similar estimates for
\beta=0
.
Hence, by Proposition
3.8
, we have that
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R})
, which implies that
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic.
∎

Remark 5
.

A sufficient condition so that (
4.3
) is satisfied is that the Łojasiewicz exponent at infinity (see
[
24
]
) of
\mathrm{Re}(p+q)
or
\mathrm{Im}(p+q)
is greater than
N-1
. Proposition 2.4 in
[
31
]
provides a formula for calculating such exponent via Puiseux expansions, which can be computed with the Newton polygon of these polynomials.

Example 4.8
.

Consider a differential operator given by

P=p(\partial_{x})+q(\partial_{t}),

where
p
is a complex polynomial,
q
is a real polynomial and
\deg(\mathrm{Im}(p))\geq\deg(p)-1
. Then for
\deg(p)=N>1
, since
\mathrm{Im}(p(\xi))\sim|\xi|^{N-1}
for large
|\xi|
, by Theorem
4.7
the operator
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for
\mu\geq 1
if and only if
p+q
does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
. For instance, the operator

\partial_{x}^{\ell+1}+i\partial_{x}^{\ell}+\partial_{t}^{j}+c,

where
\ell,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\ell\geq 2
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
, is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for
\mu\geq 1
if and only if
c\not\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant coefficients and no mixed terms, such that

P=p\left(D_{x}\right)+q\left(D_{t}\right),

where
p
and
q
are real polynomials and
\deg(p)=N>1
. Then
P
is

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for
\mu\geq 1
if and only if
p+q
does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Indeed, note that by continuity the image of
p
is a closed interval of the real line. If
p+q
does not vanish on
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}
, the set
-q(\mathbb{Z})
must be disjoint from
p(\mathbb{R})
and therefore without loss of generality we may assume that
p(\mathbb{R})=[\lambda,\infty)
, for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

|p(\xi)+q(k)|\geq|p(\xi)|-|\lambda|\sim|\xi|^{N},

for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and sufficiently large
|\xi|
. The result then follows from Theorem
4.7
.
∎

We also note that the results above are independent of
\sigma,\mu\geq 1
.

5.
Global hypoellipticity of first-order tube-type operators

In this section, we obtain a characterization for the global regularity of operators of the form

P=\partial_{t}+(a(t)+ib(t))\partial_{x}+q(t),

where
a,b,q\in\mathscr{G}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T}^{1})
are Gevrey functions on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
, and
a,b
are real-valued.

Due to a loss of Gevrey regularity on the
t
-variable that arises when
a
or
b
are non-constant, we consider an alternative notion of regularity, as in
[
11
]
, defined as follows. First, fix
\mu\geq 1/2
and consider the space

\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})=\bigcup_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,\sigma}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

endowed with the inductive limit topology.

As a set,
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
coincides with the union of the spaces
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
over all
\sigma\geq 1
. Indeed, first note that the inclusion

\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\bigcup_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})

is clear. On the other hand, if
f
belongs to
\bigcup_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, then there are
\sigma_{0}\geq 1
and
C>0
such that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma_{0},\mu,C}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. Hence, if we take
\sigma^{\prime}=\max\{\sigma_{0},C\}
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma^{\prime},\mu,\sigma^{\prime}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, proving that the reverse inclusion also holds.

Now, notice that if
\sigma_{+}>\sigma
, then the inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,\sigma}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{\sigma_{+},\mu,\sigma_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is compact. Indeed, we can decompose the inclusion map above as the compositions of the inclusions:

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,\sigma}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\overset{i_{1}}{\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu,\sigma_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\overset{i_{2}}{\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma_{+},\mu,\sigma_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

This composition is compact because
i_{1}
is compact by Theorem
2.4
and
i_{2}
is bounded. This shows that
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a DFS space.

Let
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
denote the topological dual of
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, which can be regarded by duality as the space

\bigcap_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu,\sigma}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

endowed with the projective limit topology. Note that
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
coincides with the set given by the intersection of all
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
over
\sigma\geq 1
. Moreover,
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a FS space.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that a differential operator
P
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if

u\in\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),\ Pu\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\ \Rightarrow\ u\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}).

Notice that if a differential operator
P
is not
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic, there exists
u\in\bigcap_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
u\not\in\bigcup_{\sigma\geq 1}\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
for any
\sigma\geq 1
satisfying
Pu=f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma^{\prime},\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, for some
\sigma^{\prime}\geq 1
. In particular,
P
is not
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma^{\prime},\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic.

On the other hand, if
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for every
\sigma\geq 1
, then
P
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic. Indeed, if
Pu=f\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, we must have
f\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, for some
\sigma\geq 1
and hence
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

The next proposition shows that for constant coefficient differential operators with no mixed terms on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
, both notions of global hypoellipticity presented so far coincide.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
P
be a differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
with constant coefficients and no mixed terms. Then
P
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic, for any
\sigma\geq 1
.

Proof.

By Remark
4
, if
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for some
\sigma\geq 1
, then
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic for every
\sigma\geq 1
, and consequently
P
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic by the previous discussion. Conversely, suppose that
P
is not
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic. Then by the proof of Theorem
4.2
we have that there exists
u\in\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
Pu=0\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
, and consequently
P
is not
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The operator

P=\partial_{t}+(a_{0}+ib_{0})\partial_{x}+q_{0},

where
q_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
,
a_{0},b_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic if and only if

(i)

b_{0}\neq 0
and
\frac{a_{0}}{b_{0}}\mathrm{Re}(q_{0})+\mathrm{Im}(q_{0})\notin\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

b_{0}=0
and either
\mathrm{Re}(q_{0})\neq 0
, or both
a_{0}=\mathrm{Re}(q_{0})=0
and
\operatorname{Im}(q_{0})\notin\mathbb{Z}
.

5.1.
Real case

First, let us consider the case
b\equiv 0
, that is, operators of the form

P=\partial_{t}+a(t)\partial_{x}+q(t),

where
a,q\in\mathscr{G}^{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}(\mathbb{T}^{1})
, for some fixed
\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}\geq 1
, and
a
is real-valued. Consider the constants

a_{0}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}a(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad q_{0}=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathbb{C}.

We also set

A(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-a_{0}t\quad\text{and}\quad Q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}q(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-q_{0}t.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exist
C_{0,a},C_{1,a}\geq 1
such that for every
L>0
,
\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have that

(5.1)

e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}e^{i\xi A(t)}\right|\leq C_{0,a}C_{1,a}^{\beta+\gamma}\gamma!^{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}+\mu},

for every
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
A\in\mathscr{G}^{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}(\mathbb{T}^{1})
, so there exist
C_{0},C\geq 1
such that

(5.2)

|\partial_{t}^{\ell}A(t)|\leq C_{0}C^{\ell}\ell!^{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}},

for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
t\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
. Note that if
\gamma=0
, then

\displaystyle e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}e^{i\xi A(t)}\right|

\displaystyle=e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}\left|A(t)\right|^{\beta}\leq C_{0}^{\beta}C^{\beta},

for every
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Next consider
\gamma\geq 1
. Then, by Faà di Bruno’s Formula (Lemma
A.1
) and inequality (
5.2
) we have that

\displaystyle e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}e^{i\xi A(t)}|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}\left|\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{\gamma!}{\tau!}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\left[e^{i\xi A(t)}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\gamma}\left[\frac{\partial_{t}^{\ell}(i\xi A(t))}{\ell!}\right]^{\tau_{\ell}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle=e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}\left|\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{\gamma!}{\tau!}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\min\{|\tau|,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\ell^{\prime}}e^{i\xi A(t)}(iA(t))^{\beta-\ell^{\prime}}\frac{|\tau|!}{(|\tau|-\ell^{\prime})!}\xi^{|\tau|-\ell^{\prime}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\gamma}\left[\frac{i\partial_{t}^{\ell}A(t)}{\ell!}\right]^{\tau_{\ell}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{\gamma!}{\tau!}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\min\{|\tau|,\beta\}}\binom{\beta}{\ell^{\prime}}(1+|A(t)|)^{\beta}\left(\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{\mu(|\tau|-\ell^{\prime})}(|\tau|-\ell^{\prime})!^{\mu-1}|\tau|!\prod_{\ell=1}^{\gamma}\left[\frac{|\partial_{t}^{\ell}A(t)|}{\ell!}\right]^{\tau_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\beta}\left(1+\left(\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{\mu}\right)^{\gamma}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{1}}(1+|A(t)|)^{\beta}\gamma!^{\mu}\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{\gamma!}{\tau!}|\tau|!\prod_{\ell=1}^{\gamma}\left[\frac{C_{0}C^{\ell}\ell!^{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}}{\ell!}\right]^{\tau_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{a}^{\beta+\gamma}C^{\gamma}\gamma!^{\mu}\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{\gamma!}{\tau!}C_{0}^{|\tau|}|\tau|!^{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{\gamma}\ell!^{({\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}-1)\tau_{\ell}},

with
\Delta(\gamma)
as defined in (
A.1
) and
\tilde{C}_{a}=2\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{T}^{1}}(1+|A(t)|)\left(1+\left(\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{\mu}\right)
. Here, we used Lemma
A.5
on the fourth line. Then, applying Lemmas
A.3
and
A.4
we have that

\displaystyle e^{-L|\xi|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}|\partial_{t}^{\gamma}e^{i\xi A(t)}|

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{a}^{\beta+\gamma}C^{\gamma}\gamma!^{\mu}\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{\gamma!}{\tau!}C_{0}^{|\tau|}|\tau|!\gamma!^{({\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{a}^{\beta+\gamma}C^{\gamma}\gamma!^{{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}+\mu}\sum_{\Delta(\gamma)}\frac{|\tau|!}{\tau!}C_{0}^{|\tau|}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{a}^{\beta+\gamma}C^{\gamma}\gamma!^{{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}+\mu}C_{0}(1+C_{0})^{\gamma-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}(\tilde{C}_{a}C(1+C_{0}))^{\beta+\gamma}\gamma!^{{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}+\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0,a}C_{1,a}^{\beta+\gamma}\gamma!^{{\sigma_{\scalebox{0.5}{$0$}}}+\mu},

6.
Considerations about global solvability

In this section, we apply the topological properties proved in Section
2
to show that the two versions of global hypoellipticity considered in this work imply suitable notions of global solvability on the spaces
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
.

Definition 6.1
.

Fix
\sigma\geq 1
and
\mu\geq 1/2
.

(1)

A linear partial differential operator
P:\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally solvable if, for every
f\in(\ker\Pt)^{\perp}
, there exists
u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
Pu=f
.

(2)

A linear partial differential operator
P:\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally solvable if, for every
f\in(\ker\Pt)^{\perp}
, there exists
u\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that
Pu=f
.

These are the definitions of solvability considered in
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
and
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
are DFS spaces, the two notions of global solvability above are equivalent to the closedness of the range of the operator
P
on the respective functional settings by
[
1
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
P:\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
be a linear partial differential operator. If
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic, then
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally solvable. Similarly, if
P:\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\to\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is
\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
-globally hypoelliptic, then
P
is
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-globally solvable.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a DFS space, it follows from
[
2
, Corollary 5.4]
that, if for some
\sigma_{+}>\sigma
we have that

u\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma_{+},\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),\ Pu\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\ \Rightarrow\ \exists v\in\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\text{ such that }Pu=Pv,

then
P
is
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-globally solvable. In particular, the
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-global hypoellipticity of
P
implies the property above, which gives us the
\mathcal{S}_{\sigma,\mu}
-global solvability of
P
.

Next, note that given
\mu_{+}>\mu
, we have that
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\subset\mathscr{F}_{\mu_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
. The
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-global hypoellipticity implies the following property:

u\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu_{+}}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}),\ Pu\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\ \Rightarrow\ \exists v\in\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})\text{ such that }Pu=Pv.

Since
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}(\mathnormal{\mathbb{T}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}})
is a DFS space, again by
[
2
, Corollary 5.4]
we obtain the
\mathscr{F}_{\mu}
-global solvability of
P
.
∎

Hence, the results of the previous sections also give us sufficient conditions for the global solvability of operators on
\mathbb{T}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma A.1

Given

{\partial_{t}^{N}}e^{f(t)}=e^{f(t)}\displaystyle\sum_{\tau\in\Delta(N)}\dfrac{N!}{\tau!}\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\left(\dfrac{1}{\ell!}\partial_{t}^{\ell}f(t)\right)^{\tau_{\ell}},

Lemma A.5
.

Let

and

. Then

e^{-L|x|^{\frac{1}{\mu}}}|x|^{s}\leq(\mu/L)^{\mu s}s!^{\mu},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

The proof is a consequence of the the estimate

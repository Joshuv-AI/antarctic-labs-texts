Measuring decoherence due to quantum vacuum fluctuations
Anirudh Gundhi, Hendrik Ulbricht

Abstract
The interaction of a particle with vacuum fluctuations – which theoretically exist even in the complete absence of matter – can lead to observable irreversible decoherence, if it were possible to switch on and off the particle charge suddenly. We compute the leading order decoherence effect for such a scenario and propose an experimental setup for its detection. Such a measurement might provide further insights into the nature of vacuum fluctuations and a novel precision test for the decoherence theory.

In this work we propose a scenario where this can be achieved. We first show that suddenly switching on and off the particle’s coupling to the zero-point modes leads to an irreversible loss of coherence and obtain its analytic expression to leading order in the interaction. .– A quantitative analysis of decoherence is provided by the reduced density matrix

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{r}(t)=\Sigma_{E}\bra{E}\ket{\Psi_t}\bra{\Psi_t}\ket{E},

(1)

the basis states of the environment. We imagine a situation where the time evolution of the system, encoded by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(x,x^{\prime},t):=\bra{\mathcal{E}_{x'}}\ket{\mathcal{E}_x}_{t},

(3)

\displaystyle\ket{\mathcal{E}(x)}_{t}=\exp(\frac{-i e x}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t} dt'\hat{\Pi}^{x}(x,t') )\ket{0},

(5)

{\hat{\rho}(0)=\hat{\rho}_{\text{S}}(0)\otimes\ket{0}\bra{0}}

Experimental proposal
.– A promising scenario for the experimental realization of this proposal is to adapt the settings of molecule interferometry as pioneered by the Arndt group in Vienna
Hornberger et al. 2012

Juffmann et al. 2013

The quantum superposition is generated by the ponderomotive force of the light standing wave grating made of two counterpointing laser beams as describe by the Kapitza-Dirac effect

A pair of conducting plates form a capacitor overlapping with the spatial region where the superposition and the dipole moment are generated and define the boundary conditions for the vacuum fluctuations. We work with the axis-convention such that the beam of particles is assumed to move along the z-axis and the phase difference/dipole superpositions are generated along the x-axis.

The intensity profile of the laser grating is given by

Competing effects
.– To the list of standard competing decoherence effects
Hornberger et al. 2003
which are encountered and typically overcome in matter-wave interferometry, the setup that we propose only adds image currents.

A charged particle between conducting plates leads to an infinite number of image charges as per standard electrostatics. The images (or equivalently the free charges inside the metal) must instantly move with the charged particle in order to nullify its Coulomb field along the plates. The free charges inside the conductor are therefore correlated only to the position of the particle and not its trajectory. They cannot acquire which-path information and lead to no observable decoherence themselves
Gundhi 2024
. Coherence can still be lost, however, if the plates do not respect the ideal conductor assumption, and offer a finite resistance to the image currents. In such a scenario, the charged particle heats the region of the conductor that it passes by, due to the friction encountered by the induced image charges. This enables the conductor to
record
the particle’s which-path information, thus leading to observable decoherence
Anglin and Zurek 1996
;
Machnikowski 2006
;
Sonnentag and Hasselbach 2007
.

However, this effect decreases as the distance of the charged particle from the plates increases, and would be negligible in our proposal. This can be seen from the decoherence time
\tau_{d}=\left(10^{4}x/\text{m}\right)^{3}\times 10^{-5}\text{ sec}
, calculated in
Anglin and Zurek 1996
(see also
Machnikowski 2006
), where
x
is the distance of the charge from the plates. In our proposal, we consider superpositions prepared between the two plates such that
x\simeq L\simeq 10^{-2}

m
, giving
\tau_{d}\simeq 10
s for a single ion and
\tau_{d}\propto 1/q^{2}_{0}\simeq 0.1
s for
q_{0}=10e
(the value taken for the green curve in Fig.

20

\mathcal{T}\simeq\sigma_{z}/v_{z}=10^{-9}
s, for which the dipole moment of the molecules would be switched on in our proposal. Since the time of flight for the dipole
\mathcal{T}
is much less than the decoherence time
\tau_{d}
, we expect decoherence due to VFs to dominate, and not be suppressed by the images.

Conclusions
.– In this work our aim is to show that decoherence due to vacuum fluctuations, requiring the interaction between the system and the zero-point modes of the vacuum to be suddenly switched on and off, can be measured with the help of modern interferometric experiments when performed with alkali earth clusters. Such a measurement would probe the foundational aspects of quantum field theory and the decoherence formalism, and might also be relevant for the recent proposals to test the quantum nature of gravity
Marletto and Vedral 2017
;
Bose et al. 2017
. This is because an experimental realization of our proposal would bring to light a fundamentally unavoidable source of decoherence, which has not been discussed previously, and that might become dominant for massive/highly polarizable particles in standard matter-wave interferometry.

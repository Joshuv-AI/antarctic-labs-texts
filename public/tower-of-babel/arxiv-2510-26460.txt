Correlations in a quantum switch-based heat engine with measurements: A proof-of-principle demonstration
Vinicius F. Lisboa, Pedro R. Dieguez, Kyrylo Simonov, Roberto M. Serra

Abstract
Allowing the order of quantum operations to exist in superposition is known to open new routes for thermodynamic tasks. We investigate a quantum heat engine where energy exchanges are driven by generalized measurements, and the sequence of these operations is coherently controlled in a superposition of causal orders. Our analysis explores how initial correlations between the working medium and the controller affect the engine’s performance. Considering uncorrelated, classically correlated, and entangled initial states, we show that entanglement enables the superposed causal order to generate coherence in the working medium, thereby enhancing work extraction and efficiency beyond the separable and uncorrelated cases. Finally, we present a proof-of-principle simulation on the IBM Quantum Experience platform, realizing a quantum switch of two measurement channels with tunable strengths and experimentally confirming the predicted efficiency enhancement enabled by correlation-assisted superposed causal order.

II.2
Extended heat engine with a SCO of generalized measurements

The measurement-based heat engine described above can be extended to operate under SCO of measurements, which has been shown to enhance its performance
Dieguez et al. 2023
. This is realized by introducing an additional measurement apparatus,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, during the first stroke, characterized by a measurement strength
a^{\prime}
. In this setting, heat exchange occurs between the working medium
\mathbf{Q}
and both devices,
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. The corresponding generalized measurement channels,
\mathcal{M}^{a}[\cdot]
and
\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}[\cdot]

Formally, the quantum SWITCH acts as a supermap combining
\mathcal{M}^{a}
and
\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}
into a new map,

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M}^{a},\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}})[\varrho_{\rm in}]=\sum_{i,j=1}^{4}K_{ij}\varrho_{\rm in}K_{ij}^{\dagger},

(12)

where the Kraus operators
\{K_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{4}
are given by

K_{ij}=M_{j}^{a^{\prime}}M_{i}^{a}\otimes|0\rangle_{\rm c}\langle 0|+M_{i}^{a}M_{j}^{a^{\prime}}\otimes|1\rangle_{\rm c}\langle 1|,

(13)

and
\{|0\rangle_{\rm c},|1\rangle_{\rm c}\}
denotes the computational basis of the control qubit
\mathbf{C}
that determines the causal order. The state
\varrho_{\rm in}
represents the joint initial state of the working medium and the control system.

Physically, the quantum SWITCH coherently controls whether
\mathcal{M}^{a}
precedes
\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}
, or vice versa, effectively placing these operations in a superposition of causal orders. Such a process can be implemented with present-day technologies, for instance, in photonic platforms using Mach–Zehnder or Sagnac interferometers, or simulated on cloud-based quantum processors such as IBM Quantum Experience.

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{\pm}p^{(\pm)}\sqrt{(1-2\bar{a}_{\pm})^{2}+4|\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}|^{2}}-|1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}|}{1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}+\tanh(\beta\varepsilon)},

\displaystyle\eta_{\rm cost}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{-1}_{\rm D}\ln(2)}{1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}+\tanh(\beta\varepsilon)},

(29)

and both parameter pairs
(\bar{a}_{\pm},\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)})
must satisfy (

)–(
23
) simultaneously. Here,
\Delta\eta^{\rm coh}
quantifies the positive contribution from the SCO (see Appendix
B.2
), whereas
\eta_{\rm cost}
captures the negative correction associated with memory erasure.

The coherent mode of the quantum SWITCH outperforms the incoherent one whenever
\Delta\eta^{\rm coh}>\eta_{\rm cost}
. Since
\eta_{\rm cost}
depends on the detector temperature, this condition defines a critical threshold

T_{\rm D}^{\rm crit}=\frac{\sum_{\pm}p^{(\pm)}\sqrt{(1-2\bar{a}_{\pm})^{2}+4|\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}|^{2}}-|1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}|}{k_{B}\ln(2)},

below which the coherent mode achieves a performance advantage.

IV
Outlook and conclusions

We have investigated a measurement-driven quantum heat engine in which energy exchanges are governed by measurements applied in a superposition of causal orders. Our analysis examined how work extraction and overall engine performance are affected when the working medium and the controller share prior correlations. Three distinct regimes were considered: (i) uncorrelated initial states, (ii) classically correlated separable states, and (iii) entangled states.

The results show that the engine’s performance systematically improves with the strength of initial correlations. While separable correlations already enhance the amount of extractable work compared with the uncorrelated case, entanglement provides a qualitatively distinct advantage. When assisted by entanglement, the superposition of causal orders generates coherence in the working medium prior to the work-extraction stroke, thereby enabling operation in parameter regions where definite-order dynamics would yield zero efficiency.

To address the irreversibility introduced by post-selection, we evaluated the heat engine’s performance both through the efficiencies conditioned on individual measurement outcomes and through the overall efficiency defined with respect to the average extracted work. The latter accounts for contributions from both control outcomes whenever each yields a thermodynamic advantage in the form of positive extractable work. These theoretical predictions were verified through a proof-of-principle simulation on the IBM Quantum Experience platform, where generalized measurement channels were implemented in a superposition of causal orders. The experimental results confirmed that coherently superposing the order of measurement channels activates work extraction and enhances efficiency beyond what is achievable under definite causal order.

These findings demonstrate not only the superior performance of cycles operating under superposed causal order compared with their definite-order counterparts, but also the operational significance of different forms of initial correlations. In particular, our results establish a concrete connection between initial correlations, coherence generation, and thermodynamic enhancement enabled by superposed causal order of operations. The present framework paves the way for exploring more complex thermodynamic setups (such as refrigeration cycles or feedback-controlled processes) where quantum correlations and indefinite causal order may jointly contribute to improved thermodynamic performance. Such extensions will help clarify the broader role of indefinite causal structures and their interplay with quantum resources in nonequilibrium thermodynamics.

B.1
Operations in SCO: Quantum SWITCH

Figure 9:
Quantum SWITCH.
The setup consists of the working medium
\mathbf{Q}
and the control qubit
\mathbf{C}
prepared in an initial joint state
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}
. The working medium is undergoes two measurement channels,
\mathcal{M}^{a}[\cdot]
and
\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}[\cdot]
, implemented by the apparata
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, respectively. The control qubit determines their causal order: when
\mathbf{C}
is in state
\mbox{$|0\rangle$}_{\rm c}
,
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
acts after
\mathbf{A}
, while for
\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}
, the order is reversed. A coherent superposition of these states places the measurements in SCO.

The heat engine described in Section
II.1
consists of a sequence of strokes corresponding to quantum operations executed in a definite order. For instance, performing a measurement of the working medium
\mathbf{Q}
first with apparatus
\mathbf{A}
and subsequently with
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
corresponds to the ordered composition of channels
\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}\circ\mathcal{M}^{a}
. However, quantum mechanics allows one to go beyond such ordered combinations of operations, e.g., placing the causal order itself in quantum superposition.

A paramount example is provided by the quantum SWITCH, a higher-order transformation that coherently controls the order of two operations, thus producing an SCO incompatible with any causally ordered combination of them. Given two quantum operations
\mathcal{A}[\cdot]
and
\mathcal{B}[\cdot]
, the setup involves a target system that undergoes these operations and a control qubit
\mathbf{C}
that determines their causal order. The combined action is represented by Kraus operators

K_{ij}:=B_{j}A_{i}\otimes\mbox{$|0\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}+A_{i}B_{j}\otimes\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 1|$},

(75)

where
{A_{i}}
and
{B_{j}}
are the Kraus operators of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, respectively, and
\{\mbox{$|0\rangle$}_{\rm c},\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}\}
form the computational basis of the controller. Therefore, denoting the initial state of the target and control systems as
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}
, the resulting map produced by the quantum SWITCH outputs a state

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]=\sum_{ij}K_{ij}\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}K_{ij}^{\dagger}.

(76)

We now specialize to the configuration introduced in Section
II.2
, where the target system is the working medium
\mathbf{Q}
and the controller is
\mathbf{C}
. The initial joint state
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}
satisfies the local thermal condition (
14
), and the two operations are the measurement channels associated with apparata
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}^{a}
and
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}
(see Fig.
9
). Because
\mathcal{M}^{a}
maps any input state to
\rho_{a}:=\mathrm{diag}[a,1-a]
, a straightforward calculation shows that the SWITCH of these two channels,

\mathcal{S}^{a,a^{\prime}}:=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M}^{a},\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}})
, transforms
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}
according to

\mathcal{S}^{a,a^{\prime}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]=\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\rho_{a^{\prime}}\otimes\mbox{$|0\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}+(1-\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}])\rho_{a}\otimes\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 1|$}+\Lambda_{X}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\otimes X_{c}+\Lambda_{Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\otimes Y_{c},

where

\displaystyle\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1+\operatorname{tr}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}Z_{\rm c}]),

(77)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{X}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\rho_{a^{\prime}}\operatorname{tr}_{c}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}]\rho_{a}+\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr),

(78)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\Bigl(\rho_{a^{\prime}}\operatorname{tr}_{c}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}]\rho_{a}-\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr).

(79)

The terms
\Lambda_{X}^{(a,a^{\prime})}
and
\Lambda_{Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}
capture the non-classical contributions arising from placing the measurements in SCO.

Incoherent mode.

In the incoherent mode of the quantum SWITCH, the control qubit
\mathbf{C}
is left unobserved. Tracing it out yields a probabilistic mixture of the two possible causal orders, with probabilities
\{\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}],1-\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}[\mathcal{M}^{a}[\cdot]]=\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}[\cdot]
and
\mathcal{M}^{a}[\mathcal{M}^{a^{\prime}}[\cdot]]=\mathcal{M}^{a}[\cdot]
, the resulting map effectively implements a single measurement channel
\mathcal{M}^{\bar{a}^{\mathrm{inc}}}
with the averaged strength

\bar{a}^{\mathrm{inc}}=\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]a^{\prime}+\bigl(1-\lambda[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\bigr)a.

(80)

The post-measurement state of the working medium is then

\rho^{(1)}_{\rm inc}=\operatorname{tr}_{c}[\mathcal{S}^{a,a^{\prime}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]]=\rho_{\bar{a}^{\rm inc}}.

(81)

Coherent mode.

In the coherent mode, the control qubit
\mathbf{C}
is measured in the basis
\{|\pm\rangle_{\rm c}\}

|\pm\rangle_{\rm c}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle_{c}\pm e^{i\phi^{\prime}}|1\rangle_{c})

\phi^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi]
. Each measurement outcome (
|+\rangle_{\rm c}
or
|-\rangle_{\rm c}

\displaystyle p^{(\pm)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(1\pm\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\Bigr]\Bigr),

(82)

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}_{c}[\mathcal{S}^{a,a^{\prime}}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]|\pm\rangle_{\rm c}\langle\pm|]

(83)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2p^{(\pm)}}\Bigl(\rho_{\bar{a}^{\rm inc}}\pm\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\Bigr),

where

\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]=2(\cos(\phi^{\prime})\Lambda_{X}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]+\sin(\phi^{\prime})\Lambda_{Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]).

(84)

The operators
\Lambda_{X/Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\rm in}]
can introduce quantum coherence in the post-selected states
\rho^{(1)}_{\pm}

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{01,\pm}=\pm\frac{1}{2p^{(\pm)}}\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\rm in}]\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}\Bigr],

(85)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}]

\displaystyle 2\operatorname{tr}[(\cos(\phi^{\prime})\Lambda_{X}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]+\sin(\phi^{\prime})\Lambda_{Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}])\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}]

(86)

\displaystyle e^{i\phi^{\prime}}a^{\prime}(1-a)\mathrm{tr}\Bigl[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}(\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}\otimes\mbox{$|1\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 0|$})\Bigr]+e^{-i\phi^{\prime}}a(1-a^{\prime})\mathrm{tr}\Bigl[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}(\mbox{$|1\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}\otimes\mbox{$|0\rangle$}_{\rm c}\mbox{$\langle 1|$})\Bigr].

Unlike in the case of definite or probabilistic causal order, these coherence terms do not necessarily vanish. If, however,
\Lambda_{X/Y}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\rm in}]
are diagonal, post-selection merely corresponds to applying one of two effective measurement channels with strengths

\bar{a}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2p^{(\pm)}}(\bar{a}^{\rm inc}\pm\Delta^{(a,a^{\prime})}),

(87)

where

\displaystyle\Delta^{(a,a^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}]\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aa^{\prime}\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}\Bigl(\mbox{$|0\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle 0|$}\otimes(\cos(\phi^{\prime})X_{c}+i\sin(\phi^{\prime})Y_{c})\Bigr)\Bigr],

Nevertheless, unlike (
80

\bar{a}_{\pm}
need not lie within the interval
[\min(a,a^{\prime}),\max(a,a^{\prime})]
, underscoring that the SCO leads to operational outcomes unattainable by any classical mixture of the two measurement sequences.

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{2}\Bigl|1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}+\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\rm in}]Z\Bigr]\Bigr|+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl|1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}-\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\rm in}]Z\Bigr]\Bigr|-|1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}|}{1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}+\tanh(\beta\varepsilon)}.

\displaystyle\frac{\Bigl|\operatorname{tr}\Bigl[\Xi_{\phi^{\prime}}^{(a,a^{\prime})}[\varrho_{\rm in}]Z\Bigr]\Bigr|-|1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}|}{1-2\bar{a}^{\rm inc}+\tanh(\beta\varepsilon)},

Appendix D
Methods and error analysis

For the proof-of-principle demonstration of the extended heat engine, we implemented its quantum-circuit on the
ibmq_nairobi
(
7
qubits) and
ibmq_kyoto
(
127
qubits) processors, accessed via the IBM Quantum Experience platform
ibm
. To reconstruct the joint state of the working medium,
\mathbf{Q}
, and control system,
\mathbf{C}
, after each stroke, we performed complete quantum state tomography. For each experimental point, the circuit sequence was executed up to the desired stroke and executed
10
times at different time intervals, averaging over
8000
shots per configuration. Statistical fluctuations in the reconstructed density matrices define the error bars shown in Section
III.2
.

The initialization stage prepares one of three representative correlation regimes between the control
\mathbf{C}
and the working medium
\mathbf{Q}
, as introduced in Section
III.1
and illustrated in Fig.
15
. These comprise:

Uncorrelated state

a product state
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}^{\rm unc}
with a pure control (
\zeta=1
);

Separable state

a classically correlated state
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}^{\rm sep}
with perfect correlations (
\zeta_{0}=1
,
\zeta_{1}=0
);

Entangled state

an entangled state
\varrho_{\mathrm{in}}^{\mathrm{qe}}
with perfect correlations (
\zeta_{0}=1
,
\zeta_{1}=0
,
\xi_{\beta\varepsilon}=\frac{\operatorname{sech}(\beta\varepsilon)}{2}
).

For a given initial configuration, the circuit shown in Fig.
16
implements strokes
(0)–(2)
of the heat engine protocol (see Fig.
1
). After state preparation, the quantum SWITCH is implemented following the design of Ref.
Felce et al. 2021
. The two measurement channels are implemented according to the maps
\mathcal{M}^{a}
and
\mathcal{M}^{1-a}
, while controlled-
\mathtt{SWAP}
gates condition their order on the control qubit, achieving the quantum SWITCH. Tracing out the control yields an incoherent mixture of causal orders, whereas measurement of the control in the complementary basis produces their coherent superposition. The latter is achieved by applying a Hadamard gate
\mathtt{H}
to the control and measuring it in the computational basis, which is equivalent to a measurement in the
\{\mbox{$|+\rangle$},\mbox{$|-\rangle$}\}
basis since
\mathtt{H}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}=\mbox{$|+\rangle$}
and
\mathtt{H}\mbox{$|1\rangle$}=\mbox{$|-\rangle$}
.

The work extracting stroke (see Fig.
16
) is implemented as a unitary transformation acting on
\mathbf{Q}
, ensuring that it remains isentropic. The corresponding unitary, associated with the measurement apparatus
\mathbf{B}
, is implemented as a
\mathtt{NOT}
gate in the incoherent mode of the quantum SWITCH, or whenever the post-measurement state of the working medium exhibits no coherence (i.e.,
\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}=0
). Otherwise, it is realized as a generalized rotation

\displaystyle R(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})

\displaystyle=R_{z}(\alpha_{3})R_{y}(\alpha_{2})R_{z}(\alpha_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\times R_{y}(-\alpha_{2})R_{z}(-\alpha_{3}),

(170)

where
R_{y}(\alpha)
and
R_{z}(\alpha)
denote rotations by an angle
\alpha
about the
y
- and
z
-axes, respectively. This composite sequence performs a rotation of the state about an arbitrary axis of the Bloch sphere
Glendinning 2010
. The goal of this transformation is to align the Bloch vector of
\mathbf{Q}
along the
z
-axis; depending on the resulting orientation, the stroke corresponds to work extraction (positive) or work investment (negative). For simplicity, we set
\alpha_{1}=-\pi
and determine the remaining rotation angles as

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arctan{\Biggl(\frac{\operatorname{Im}[\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}]}{\operatorname{Re}[\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}]}\Biggr)}+\frac{\pi}{2}\text{sign}\Bigl(\operatorname{Im}[\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}]\Bigr)\Biggl(1-\text{sign}\Bigl(\operatorname{Re}[\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}]\Bigr)\Biggr),

(171)

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arccos{\Biggl(\pm\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\Biggl(1\mp\frac{1-2\bar{a}}{\sqrt{4|\rho_{01,\pm}^{(1)}|^{2}+{(1-2\bar{a})}^{2}}}\Biggr)}\Biggr)},

\alpha_{3}>0

Circuit optimization was automatically handled by the IBM Qiskit compiler, ensuring logical gate equivalence while minimizing total gate depth and accumulated error. The reconstructed joint states of the working medium and control were post-selected in
\mbox{$|\pm\rangle$}_{\mathrm{c}}

Finally, the local Gibbs state (
1

a

15

\rho^{(0)}
is independent of
a

\mathbf{Q}

Multivariate subexponentiality and interplay of insurance and financial risks in a renewal risk model
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
. During last decade, although the study of continuous time risk models with heavy-tailed claims and presence of stochastic returns became quite popular, due to its practical applications, still it is lacking the emphasis in modeling the dependence among these two fundamental risks, that seems necessary for the modern insurance industry. In this paper we consider a multivariate risk model with common renewal process, while the logarithmic returns of the insurer’s investment portfolio, are described by a Lévy process. In the two main results are established an asymptotic expression for the entrance probability of the discounted aggregate claims in some ’rare’ sets x ​ A x\,A . This asymptotic expression highlights the multivariate linear single big jump principle in asymptotic behavior of these probabilities. In the first result, we are restricted in the case where the insurer makes risk-free investments, and hence we consider a non-negative Lévy process. We assume that the claim vectors follow a distribution from the class 𝒜 A ∗ \mathcal{A}_{A}^{*} , introduced here, and represents a negligibly smaller subclass of class 𝒮 A \mathcal{S}_{A} of multivariate subexponential distributions on A A , since the additional requirement for positive lower Karamata index, looks as a quit mild condition. Further, we consider that the insurance and financial risks, satisfy a weak, but very general dependence structure. In the second result, we allow arbitrarily dependence between the two risks, and we assume that the distribution of their product, at each renewal epoch, belongs to the class ( 𝒟 ∩ 𝒜 ) A ⊊ 𝒮 A (\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{A} . In this theorem we also permit risky-investment, putting a condition to Laplace exponent of the Lévy process, related with the upper Matuszewska index of the distribution for the product insurance and financial risks. We also note that even in the special one-dimensional sub-case the main results are new. Furthermore, we present two examples, where we demand only conditions for the marginal distributions of both risks and their dependence structure. In the first example, we consider the weak dependence structure, used in the first theorem, and the insurance risks dominate against the financial risks. In the second example we take a strong dependence structure, and we also allow the cases where the financial risks dominate against the insurance risks, or the two risks have equal ”heaviness”. Both examples, under the restriction on multivariate regularly varying distributions provide more explicit and elegant relations in relation with that established in the main results. Finally, we give an application of the results for the asymptotic estimation of the infinite-time ruin probability.



Keywords: Dependent insurance and financial risks; Multivariate linear single big jump principle; Lévy process; Infinite-time horizon; Lower Karamata Index

1.
Introduction

The modern insurance companies handle more than one risky portfolios, and in order to be viable and the same time competitive, invest their surplus in risk-free and in risky assets. Thus, the insurer faces two types of risks, the insurance risks, caused by insurance claims, and the financial risks which caused by risky investments. We find an increasing number of papers in literature on this topic, that study multidimensional risk models with heavy-tailed insurance and financial risks, see for example
[
37
]
,
[
52
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
9
]
.
However, in these papers on one hand the insurance risks follow some heavy-tailed distribution and dominate on the financial risks, namely the insurance risks have heavier tail than the financial risks, and on the other hand the insurance and financial risks are independent. With respect to these two drawbacks, we find similar difficulties in one dimensional risk models too, with only few exceptions as for example in
[
22
]
,
[
53
]
in one dimensional risk models and finite-time horizon results, and
[
12
]
for multidimensional risk model and infinite-time horizon results.

So, in this paper we consider an insurer, who operates
d
-lines of business, with
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and the claims are described by the sequence of vectors
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, representing independent and identically distributed (symbolically, i.i.d.) copies of the non-negative random vector
{\bf X}
with distribution
F
. Let us note, that each claim vector
{\bf X}^{(i)}=(X_{1}^{(i)},\,\ldots,\,X_{d}^{(i)})
, with
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, can have zero components, but not all of them, and arrives at the time moment
\tau_{i}
,
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Thus, we suppose that the sequence of
\{\tau_{i}\,,\;i\in N\}
, with
\tau_{0}=0
, represents a (renewal) counting process
N(t):=\sup\{i\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;\tau_{i}\leq t\}\,,\,\,t\geq 0
, for any
t\geq 0
, with
\sup\emptyset=0
conventionally, and with finite renewal function

\displaystyle\lambda(t):=\mathbf{E}[N(t)]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}(\tau_{i}\leq t)\,.

In order to avoid the trivial cases, in all over the paper the asymptotic relations holds on
\Lambda
, with
\Lambda:=\{t\;:\;\lambda(t)>0\}
. Further, we assume that the insurer invests his surplus in risk-free and in risky assets and the logarithmic return process of the investment portfolio is described by a Lévy process
\{R(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
, which starts from zero, with Laplace exponent of the form
\phi(s)=\log\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-s\,R(1)}\right]
, for any
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, where if
\phi(s)<\infty
, then we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[e^{-s\,R(t)}\right]=e^{t\,\phi(s)}\,,

(1.1)

for any
t\geq 0
, see in
[
15
, Prop. 3.14]
. This way, for some fixed
T\in\Lambda
, the discounted aggregate claims of insurer are presented as

\displaystyle{\bf D}(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-R(\tau_{i})}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}X_{1}^{(i)}\,e^{-R(\tau_{i})}\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{i=1}^{N(T)}X_{d}^{(i)}\,e^{-R(\tau_{i})}\end{array}\right)\,,

and in case of infinite time horizon, we have that

\displaystyle{\bf D}(\infty)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{-R(\tau_{i})}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{1}^{(i)}\,e^{-R(\tau_{i})}\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{d}^{(i)}\,e^{-R(\tau_{i})}\end{array}\right)\,.

We focus our attention on entrance probability of the
{\bf D}(\infty)
into some ’rare sets’
x\,A
, with
A
some set from a wide set family
\mathscr{R}
, described in Section 2, as
x\to\infty
. Namely, we are interested to asymptotic expressions of the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right]\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-\,R(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,,

(1.10)

which indicates the presence of the multivariate linear-single big jump principle, for the discounted aggregate claims.

Under various assumptions about the distribution
F
of the claim vectors and about the investment process, relation (
1.10
), and its finite-time analogue was established, see for example in
[
28
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
42
]
. We already mentioned, that in all these papers there exists assumption of domination of the insurance risks versus the financial risks, and independence between these two kinds of risk. As we shall see later, these probabilities are directly related with several kinds of ruin probability.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, after introduction
of the necessary preliminaries for the multivariate heavy-tailed distributions, we introduce a new multivariate distribution class. This class is denoted by
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
, and it contains all the multivariate subexponential distributions,
\mathcal{S}_{A}
, with positive lower Karamata index, see Definition
2.1
. For this class, we show that from the one hand side it contains the famous multivariate regular variation, and from the other hand side is slightly smaller than
\mathcal{S}_{A}
. We also suggest a new dependence structure that depicts the dependence between the insurance and financial risks. This dependence is a general enough weak dependence structure.

In Section 3, we study the asymptotic behavior of the probability of entrance of discounted aggregate claims over infinite time horizon, and we establish relation (
1.10
), for the case when the claim vectors follow distribution
F
from the class
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
, the investment portfolio logarithmic return process, is described by a non-negative Lévy process and the dependence between the two risks is given by the weak dependence structure from Section 2. In case we restrict
F
on the class of multivariate regularly varying distributions, a more direct expression than the formula (
1.10
) is provided.

In Section 4, we consider arbitrarily dependent insurance and financial risks and we assume that their product at each renewal epoch has common distribution
H\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}
, see in Subsection 2.1, and we replace the assumption of non-negativity of the Lévy process with a more relaxed condition on its Laplace exponent. Under these conditions we establish relation again (
1.10
). Further we give two examples to show the crucial role of dependence and the way how to
check the assumption,
H\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}
, based only on conditions of the marginal distributions of two risks and their dependence structure. In the first example we employ the weak dependence of Assumption
2.2
, and in the other one a strong dependence.

The interesting thing in the second example is that permits also the domination of the financial risks against the insurance risks and also the case, in which these two are balanced, which is not previously examined in continuous time risk models. Finally, in Section 5, we present an applications on the ruin probability over infinite-time horizons.

2.1.
Heavy-tailed distributions

The multivariate subexponential distribution, defined in
[
45
]
, is based on the following family of sets:

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}:=\{A\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\;:\;A\;{\text{o}pen},\;{\text{i}ncreasing},\;A^{c}\;{\text{c}onvex},\;{\bf 0}\notin\overline{A}\}\,.

For some random vector

with distribution

\displaystyle Z_{A}:=\sup\{u\;:\;{\bf Z}\in u\,A\}\,,

represents a random variable, with proper distribution

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,A)=\mathbf{P}\left[\sup_{{\bf p}\in I_{A}}{\bf p}^{T}\,{\bf Z}>x\right]\,,

Similarly in

Now, we should remind some indexes that have direct relation with the characterization of distribution classes. For an one dimensional distribution

the upper and lower Matuszewska indexes, are denoted as follows

\displaystyle J_{G}^{+}:=\inf\left\{-\dfrac{\ln\overline{G}_{*}(v)}{\ln v}\;:\;v>1\right\}=-\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\ln\overline{G}_{*}(v)}{\ln v}\,,

\displaystyle J_{G}^{-}:=\sup\left\{-\dfrac{\ln\overline{G}^{*}(v)}{\ln v}\;:\;v>1\right\}=-\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\ln\overline{G}^{*}(v)}{\ln v}\,.

where

\displaystyle\overline{G}_{*}(v):=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\overline{G}(v\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\,,\qquad\overline{G}^{*}(v):=\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\overline{G}(v\,x)}{\overline{G}(x)}\,,

for any

. It is interesting to notice that for any distribution

. Furthermore it is well known that

. Also, it is well known that if

, then for any

\displaystyle x^{-p}=o\left[\overline{G}(x)\right]\,.

(2.4)

For more details about these indexes see
[

Now we present the class of multivariate regularly varying distributions, symbolically

, in standard form. Let

\displaystyle\lim\dfrac{\mathbf{P}({\bf Z}\in x\,{\mathbb{B}})}{\overline{G}(x)}=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

. In this case we write

\displaystyle\lim x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\bf Z}{U_{G}(x)}\in{\mathbb{B}}\right]=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

for any Borel set

, which is
\mu

is given by the relation

\displaystyle U_{G}(x)=\left(\dfrac{1}{\overline{G}}\right)^{\leftarrow}(x)\,,

Hence, we observe that the measure

and any real

we have that

\displaystyle\mu(\lambda\,{\mathbb{B}})=\lambda^{-\alpha}\,\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

while if the condition

holds, then this implies the fact that the components of
{\bf Z}=(Z_{1},\,\ldots,\,Z_{d})
, are asymptotically dependent, and have distributions such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(Z_{i}>x)\sim\mu(({\bf 1}_{i},\,\infty])\overline{G}(x)\,,

where

with
{\bf 1}_{i}
representing the vector, whose
i
-th components is unit and all the others are zero. Thus, from the last relation we obtain that the

2.2.
On class
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}

In this subsection we introduce the multivariate version of distribution class
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, and we provide some first properties of this class, to one- or multi-dimensions.

The lower Karamata index of a one-dimensional distribution
G
is defined as

\displaystyle K_{G}^{-}=\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{-\log\overline{G^{*}}(v)}{\log v}\,.

(2.6)

The Karamata indexes were studied thoroughly in
[
5
, Subsestion 2.1]
. As was mentioned also in
[
14
, p. 88]
, for any distribution
G
, with
\overline{G}(x)>0
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have that
0\leq K_{G}^{-}\leq\infty
. Additionally, from (
2.6
), it is easy to see that if
G\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, then we obtain
K_{G}^{-}=\alpha
, while for any distribution
G
, with unbounded from above support, it holds
K_{G}^{-}\leq J_{G}^{-}
. Finally, we can easily check that if two distributions
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, are such that
\overline{G}_{1}(x)\sim c\,\overline{G}_{2}(x)
, for some
c\in(0,\,\infty)
, then we have
K_{G_{1}}^{-}=K_{G_{2}}^{-}
, while if
\Theta\sim G
, and
\lambda\in(0,\,\infty)
, with
\lambda\,\Theta\sim G^{\prime}
, then we find
K_{G}^{-}=K_{G^{\prime}}^{-}
.

In this way, in
[
51
]
was introduced the class
\mathcal{A}^{*}
as follows: We say that a one-dimensional distribution
G
belongs to
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, if
G\in\mathcal{S}
and
K_{G}^{-}>0
.

From the previous definition follow some interesting comments. At first, we notice that the most distributions
G
with infinite right endpoint, are such that it holds
K_{G}^{-}=J_{G}^{-}
. Further, it is easy to see, due to inequalities in lower Karamata and Matuszewska indexes, that
\mathcal{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}
. However, the class
\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{*}
, namely the class of subexponential distributions with
K_{G}^{-}=0
and
J_{G}^{-}>0
, is very poor. This, in combination with the fact that class
\mathcal{A}
contains all the useful subexponential distributions, see relative discussions in
[
47
, Section 2]
, makes class
\mathcal{A}^{*}
practically negligible smaller than class
\mathcal{S}
.

Now, we proceed to introduction of the multivariate analogue of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, in similar way with the classes from Subsection 2.1.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\bf X}\sim F
be non-negative random vector, and let

be some fixed set. We say that

, if
F_{A}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Further, we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}=\bigcap_{A\in\mathscr{R}}\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}\,.

Remark 2.1
.

The class
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
, as also the rest of the classes in (
2.5
), has the following desired property: If
{\bf X}\sim F
, with
F\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
, then all the non-negative and non-degenerate to zero linear combinations of components of
{\bf X}
, say
\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}\,X_{j}
, have distribution from class
\mathcal{A}^{*}
. This can be verified, by considering as
A
, the set
A_{2}
in Remark
3.1
below, from where is directly implied that, if
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A_{2}}^{*}
, then the
\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}\,X_{j}
follows distribution from class
\mathcal{A}^{*}
, for the
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}
of set
A_{2}
. Therefore, from this and the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
, we find the desired result.

The following proposition shows that class
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
contains class
MRV
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
, it holds
MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)\subsetneq\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
.

Proof.

In proof of
[
45
, Proposition 4.14]
was shown that for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, it holds
\mu(\partial A)=0
, and further
\mu(A)\in(0,\,\infty)
. Hence, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, since
{\bf X}\sim F\in MRV(\alpha,\,\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\right)\sim\mu(A)\,\overline{G}(x)\,,

(2.7)

with
G\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
,
\alpha\in(0,\,\infty)
. From (
2.7
), and the fact that
\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
is closed
with respect to strong-tail equivalence, see for example in
[
34
, Proposition 3.3(i)]
, we get
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
,
and consequently
F_{A}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
(recall that since
F_{A}\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, it follows that
K_{F_{A}}^{-}=\alpha>0
). Hence,
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
,
and from the arbitrariness in choice of
A\in\mathscr{R}
, we obtain that
F\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

From Proposition
2.1
, we can complete relation (
2.5
) in the following way

\displaystyle MRV\subsetneq(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A}^{*})_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{S}_{\mathscr{R}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{\mathscr{R}}\,.

(2.8)

Although
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{\mathscr{R}}
is NOT proper subset of class
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
, the
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
contains almost all important distributions of
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{\mathscr{R}}
.

As we mentioned before, class
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
is sufficiently richer than
MRV
. We shall discuss later and provide an example, which indicates this fact. However, for this we need the following proposition, that has its one merit, since gives a sufficient closure property condition of class
\mathcal{A}^{*}
with respect to convolution.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
F_{1},\,F_{2}
be one-dimensional distributions with support on
{\mathbb{R}}
. If
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and

\displaystyle\overline{F_{1}*F_{2}}(x)\sim\overline{F}_{1}(x)+\overline{F}_{2}(x)\,.

(2.9)

then it holds
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

Firstly, since
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}\subsetneq\mathcal{A}
, and relation (
2.9
) is true, by
[
30
, Corollary 3.1]
is implied that
F_{1}*F_{2}\in\mathcal{A}
. So, it remains to show that
K_{F_{1}*F_{2}}^{-}>0
. For any
v>1
we have

\displaystyle\overline{(F_{1}*F_{2})^{*}}(v)=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F_{1}*F_{2}}(vx)}{\overline{F_{1}*F_{2}}(x)}=\limsup\dfrac{\overline{F}_{1}(vx)+\overline{F}_{2}(vx)}{\overline{F}_{1}(x)+\overline{F}_{2}(x)}\leq\max\left\{\overline{F_{1}^{*}}(v)\,,\;\overline{F_{2}^{*}}(v)\right\},

where at the second step we used (
2.9
). Hence

\displaystyle K_{F_{1}*F_{2}}^{-}=\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{-\log\left[\overline{(F_{1}*F_{2})^{*}}(v)\right]}{\log v}\geq\lim_{v\downarrow 1}\dfrac{-\log\left[\max\left\{\overline{F_{1}^{*}}(v)\,,\;\overline{F_{2}^{*}}(v)\right\}\right]}{\log v}>0\,,

where the last step follows from the fact that
F_{1},\,F_{2}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
.
∎

In next example, we provide sufficient conditions with respect to marginal distributions, and their dependence structures, such that a multivariate distribution to belong to
\mathcal{A}_{A_{3}}^{*}
, with

\displaystyle A_{3}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\dfrac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d}l_{i}\,y_{i}>1\right\}\,,

(2.10)

with
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
and
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{d}=1
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
{\bf X}\sim F
be a non-negative random vector, with components
X_{j}\sim F_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, for any
j=1,\,\ldots,\,d
, and
\overline{F}_{j}(x)\asymp\overline{F}_{n}(x)
, for any
1\leq j\neq n\leq d
. Let us also assume that the
X_{1},\,\ldots,\,X_{d}
, satisfy the following dependence structure, that was introduced by
[
24
]
, and contains the independence as special sub-case: There exists some
x_{0}=x_{0}(d)>0
and some constant
C=C(d)\geq 0
, such that for any
x\geq x_{0}
and any
2\leq n\leq d
it holds

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X_{j}>x-t\;\big|\;X_{n}=t\right)}{\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}X_{j}>x-t\right)}\leq M\,,

(2.11)

uniformly for
t\in[x_{0},\,x]
. Next, we show that
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A_{3}}^{*}
.

For the set
A_{3}
given by relation (
2.10
), we have
I_{A_{3}}=\left(\dfrac{1}{d},\,\ldots,\,\dfrac{1}{d}\right)
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A_{3}}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\dfrac{1}{d}\,\sum_{j=1}^{d}X_{j}>x\right)\,,

for any
x>0
. Thus, from the conditions on the marginal distributions, and the dependence structure of (
2.11
), applying
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
, we find that

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A_{3}}(x)=\mathbf{P}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\dfrac{1}{d}\,X_{j}>x\right)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{P}\left(\dfrac{1}{d}\,X_{j}>x\right)\,,

(2.12)

Let consider
X_{j}/d\sim F_{j}^{\prime}
. So, since relation (
2.11
) contains the independence as special sub-case, and due to
F_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
and
\overline{F}^{\prime}_{j}(x)\asymp\overline{F}^{\prime}_{n}(x)
, for any
1\leq j\neq n\leq d
, from
[
20
, Theorem 3.1]
follows that

\displaystyle\overline{F^{\prime}_{1}*F^{\prime}_{2}*\,\cdots\,F^{\prime}_{d}}(x)\sim\sum_{j=1}^{d}\mathbf{P}\left(\dfrac{1}{d}\,X_{j}>x\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\overline{F}^{\prime}_{j}(x)\,.

(2.13)

From (
2.13
), and applying Proposition
2.2
inductively, we conclude that
F^{\prime}_{1}*F^{\prime}_{2}*\,\cdots\,F^{\prime}_{d}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
, and further by (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), is implied that
F_{A_{3}}\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Hence, we get
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A_{3}}^{*}
. ∎

Remark 2.3
.

In the previous example we consider a weak dependence structure, and the condition of
weak equivalence among the marginal distribution tails. In fact class
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{\mathscr{R}}
or class
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{A}
, for some
A\in\mathscr{R}
, contains several cases, that either contain asymptotic dependence or the marginals have NOT asymptotically equivalent tails, or both. It is easy to check that if in
[
26
, Examples 4.1 – 4.4]
we additionally require
for the marginals a positive Karamata index, then they belong to
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{A}
,
for the set
A=A_{2}
(see Remark
3.1
for
A_{2}
).

Furthermore,
[
26
, Example 4.5]
belongs also to
multivariate
\mathcal{A}^{*}_{A_{2}}
, for some set
A_{2}
, due to the fact that the rapidly varying distributions have infinite lower Karamata index. The last example does NOT contain any
MRV
distribution.

2.3.
Assumptions on the model

In this subsection we provide two main assumptions for the discounted aggregate claims of relation (
1
). The first assumption simply says that the counting process
\{N(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is
a renewal process, which is independent from all the other sources of randomness.

Assumption 2.1
.

We assume that the inter-arrival times
\{\theta_{i}:=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
, namely the interval times between arrivals of two successive claims, are i.i.d. non-negative random variables, which are independent from all the other sources of randomness.

The following assumption relates to the dependence structure between the insurance
and financial risks. This dependence is inspired by
[
2
]
, where
was introduces a dependence structure through copulas, which later was generalized
and used in several papers in continuous and discrete time, see for example in

[
1
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
51
]
,
[
57
]
among others.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
, some fixed set. We assume that

\displaystyle\{({\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;e^{R(\tau_{i-1})-R(\tau_{i})})\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

are i.i.d. copies of the general random vector
({\bf X}\,,\;e^{-R(\theta_{1})})
, with

\displaystyle{\bf X}\sim F\,,\qquad e^{-R(\theta_{1})}\sim Q\,.

Further we assume that there exists some function
h\;:\;[0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
, such that it holds

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{y\in E}h(y)\leq\sup_{y\in E}h(y)<\infty\,,

where,
E
represents a left neighborhood of the upper bound of
S(Q)
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\;|\;e^{-R(s_{1})}=y\,,\;\theta_{1}=s_{1}\right)\sim h(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(2.14)

uniformly for any
y\in S(Q)
and any
s_{1}\in\Lambda
.

It is worth to make some technical and intuitive comments on Assumption
2.2
.

Remark 2.4
.

The expression at the left side of relation (
2.14
) is understood as follows

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon_{1}\wedge\varepsilon_{2}\to 0}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\;|\;e^{-R(s_{1})}\in[y-\varepsilon_{1},\,y+\varepsilon_{1}]\,,\;\theta_{1}\in[s_{1}-\varepsilon_{2},\,s_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}]\right)\,.

Furthermore, if
s_{1}\notin\Lambda
, or if
s_{1}\in\Lambda
but
\mathbf{P}\left(e^{R(s_{1})}=y\,,\;\theta_{1}=s_{1}\right)=0
, for any
y\in\Delta
, for some open and non-empty set
\Delta
, then the conditional probability at the left side of relation (
2.14
) is understood as unconditional, and hence
h(y)=1
, holds for any
y\in\Delta
, or any
y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
if
s_{1}\notin\Lambda
.

The uniformity at relation (
2.14
) means that it holds

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{s_{1}\in\Lambda}\sup_{y\in S(Q)}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\;|\;e^{-R(s_{1})}=y_{1}\,,\;\theta_{1}=s_{1}\right)}{h(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|=0\,.

Remark 2.5
.

In fact relation (
2.14
) permits not only dependence between financial and
insurance risks, but simultaneously dependence between the insurance risks, through the
claim vectors, and the inter-arrival times. However, in this paper we consider only the
independence assumption between the claims and the inter-arrival times, through Assumption
2.1
, and we do NOT extend our attention to time-dependent risk models. For more details about time-dependent risk models but with independent actuarial and financial risks, we refer to
[
35
]
,
[
37
]
among others. Hence, from Assumption
2.2
in combination with Assumption
2.1
, relation (
2.14
) indicates that it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;|\;e^{R(s_{i-1})-R(s_{i})}=y\right)\sim h(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in x\,A\right)\,,

for any
i\geq 1
, uniformly for any
y\in S(Q)
and for any
s_{i-1}\in\Lambda
,
with
s_{i-1}\leq s_{i}
. Further, by the fact that
\{({\bf X}^{(i)}\,,\;e^{R(\tau_{i-1})-R(\tau_{i})})\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
are i.i.d., we can see that for any
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
the insurance risk
{\bf X}^{(i)}
is dependent on its financial returns
\{R(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
only over the interval
(\tau_{i-1}\,,\;\tau_{i}]
, while the
{\bf X}^{(i)}
is independent of the increments of the Lévy process over the intervals
[0\,,\;\tau_{i-1}]
and
(\tau_{i}\,,\;\infty]
, which seems reasonable in combination with the assumption that the logarithmic returns are described by a Lévy process.

Remark 2.6
.

We can observe that the dependence in relation (
2.14
) contains as special cases either the independence between
{\bf X}
and
R(t)
, or the independence between
{\bf X}
and
\theta
, or the independence among
{\bf X}
,
R(t)
and
\theta
. Further, relation (
2.14
) indicates the existence of a weak dependence structure between the insurance and financial risks, in the sense of asymptotic independence, see in
[
3
]
for related discussion about the static structure of this dependence. From the static structure of this dependence, in combination with Assumption
2.1
, we can find out that the dependence in relation (
2.14
) is rich enough, see
[
40
, Sec. 3]
and
[
16
, Sec. 2.1]
for examples of static form of this dependence. Therefore, from the one hand side, we obtain a general enough structure of weak dependence between the two risks, that contains the independence as special case, and from the other hand side, since in Section
3
we consider that the Lévy processes are non-negative, namely insurer invest only on risk-free assets, we expect that the dependencies in practice will be rather weak than strong.

Remark 2.7
.

Finally, we show that the insurance and financial risks belong to
\mathcal{A}_{A}
, when
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
, for some
A\in\mathscr{R}
,
\{R(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is a non-negative Lévy process, and the Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
hold. Let
\{R^{*}(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
be a new cádlág process, that is independent from the other sources of randomness and the distribution of
e^{-R^{*}(t)}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-R^{*}(t)}\in dy\right)=h(y)\,\,\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-R(t)}\in dy\right)\,,

(2.15)

for any
t\geq 0
. It is easy to check that from relation (
2.14
), after integration with respect to
y
and
s_{1}
in
S(Q)
and
\Lambda
that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[h\left(e^{-R(\theta_{1})}\right)\right]=1\,.

This, in combination with relation (
2.15
), implies that the
e^{-R^{*}(\theta_{1})}
has a proper distribution. Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-R(\theta_{1})}\in x\,A\right)=\int_{\Lambda}\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-R(s_{1})}\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\theta_{1}\in ds_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{1}\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in\dfrac{x}{y}\,A\;\Big|\;e^{-R(s_{1})}=y\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-R(s_{1})}\in dy\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\theta_{1}\in ds_{1})

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{1}h(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in\dfrac{x}{y}\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-R(s_{1})}\in dy\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\theta_{1}\in ds_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\in\dfrac{x}{y}\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-R^{*}(s_{1})}\in dy\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\theta_{1}\in ds_{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Lambda}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-R^{*}(s_{1})}\in x\,A\right)\,\mathbf{P}(\theta_{1}\in ds_{1})=\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-R^{*}(\theta_{1})}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(2.16)

where at the first step we used Assumption
2.1
, at the third step we took into account relation (
2.14
), and at fourth step we employ relation relation (
2.15
). But, since
e^{-R^{*}(\theta_{1})}
belongs to the interval
[0,\,1]
and
{\bf X}\sim F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}
, with
{\bf X}
and

independent each other, from
[

we find

.

3.
Weakly Dependent Insurance and Financial Risks

3.1.
Main result

Here we give the main result for the asymptotic behavior of the entrance probability
of the discount claims
{\bf D}(\infty)
into some rare set
x\,A
, with
A\in\mathscr{R}
, when the insurer makes risk-free investments, and exists the weak dependence structure of Assumption
2.2
between the insurance and financial risks. We also notice that we assume that the claim vector distribution
F
, belongs to class
\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
. At first we present our result and then we begin some discussion about the generality of the assumptions and its applicability in actuarial practice.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set and let consider the discounted aggregate claims from relation (
1
). We suppose that the Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
are true and additionally the process
\{R(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
is non-negative and non-degenerate to zero, Lévy process. We also assume that
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
. Then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right)\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,e^{-R(s)}\in x\,A\right)\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(3.1)

Remark 3.1
.

Relation (
3.1
) was established under various assumptions for the dependencies and the distribution classes either for the claim vectors, or for the financial risks, see for example in
[
9
]
,

[
28
]
. In Theorem
3.1
, in spite of the restriction to non-negative Lévy processes, and the dependence Assumption
2.2
between the two risks, we get the product distribution of
{\bf X}\,e^{-R(\theta_{1})}
to belong to
\mathcal{A}_{A}
, as we see in Remark
2.7
.

In general, the expression of relation (
3.1
) discloses the multivariate linear single big jump principle of the discounted aggregate claims. In one-dimensional case, the sets of the form
A=(b,\,\infty)
, with
b>0
, belong to family
\mathscr{R}
. Hence, the set
A=(1,\,\infty)
in relation (
3.1
) shows a classical asymptotic estimation for the one-dimensional behavior of the distribution tail of the discounted aggregate claims when the claim distribution is subexponential (or some subclass). Even in such special one-dimensional case, Theorem
3.1
produces new result.

Further more, two other sets from the family
\mathscr{R}
, with huge practical interest, are given below

\displaystyle A_{1}:=\{{\bf y}\;:\;y_{j}>b_{j}\,,\;\exists\;j=1,\,\ldots,\,d\}\,,

for
b_{1},\,\ldots,\,b_{d}>0
and

\displaystyle A_{2}:=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}\,y_{j}>b\right\}\,,

with
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}\geq 0
,
b>0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}l_{j}=1\,.

Remark 3.2
.

If in Theorem
3.1
we consider that
F\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{R}}^{*}
, and Assumption
2.2
holds for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, then relation (
3.1
) also holds for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Further, relation (
3.1
) seems to be difficult for computation, because of the generality of assumptions. However, when the set
A
and the components
{\bf X}
dependence structures are determined, then relation (
3.1
) can be reduced into more direct asymptotic expressions, which are easily computable.

Furthermore, as we see later in Corollary
4.1
(under weaker conditions on Lévy process
\{R(t)\,,\;t\geq 0\}
), when the requirement
F\in\mathcal{A}_{A}^{*}
is replaced by
F\in MRV
, this implies an even more immediate asymptotic expression in comparison with (
3.1
).

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

Before the proof of theorem, we need some preliminary lemmas. At first we provide the following ’static’ form of the Assumption
2.2
, which is necessary to the preliminary lemmas.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set. Let
({\bf X}^{(1)}\,,\;W_{1}),\,\ldots,\,({\bf X}^{(n)}\,,\;W_{n})
, with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be a sequence of non-negative, independent, but not necessarily identically distributed, random pairs. We suppose that for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, it holds
{\bf X}^{(i)}\sim F_{i}
and there exists a constant
b_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
, such that
\mathbf{P}(0\leq W_{i}\leq b_{i})=1
,
\mathbf{P}(W_{i}=0)<1
hold and there exists a function
h_{i}\;:\;[0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
, such that the inequalities
0<\inf_{y\in E_{i}}h_{i}(y)\leq\sup_{y\in E_{i}}h_{i}(y)<\infty
hold, where
E_{i}
is a left neighborhood of
b_{i}
, for which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;|\;W_{i}=y\right)\sim h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)\,,

(3.2)

uniformly for
y\in S(W_{i})
.

Let us notice that in Assumption
3.1
the uniformity of (
3.2
) is understood in the sense of

\displaystyle\lim\sup_{y\in S(W_{i})}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\;|\;W_{i}=y\right)}{h_{i}(y)\,\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}^{(i)}\in x\,A\right)}-1\right|=0\,.

In the following lemma, when we say that the pair
({\bf X}\,,\;W)
satisfies Assumption
3.1
, we have in mind that
{\bf X}\sim F
,
\mathbf{P}(0\leq W\leq b)=1
, for some
b\in(0,\,\infty)
,
\mathbf{P}(W=0)<1
and relation (
3.2
) is true with a function
h
, that is bounded away from zero and infinity over some left area
E
of
b
. The following lemma represents a multivariate extension of
[
51
, Lem. 4.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be some fixed set. We assume that
({\bf X}\,,\;W)
is a non-negative random pair, that satisfies Assumption
3.1
, with
{\bf X}\sim F\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
. Let us consider another non-negative random vector
{\bf Z}\sim V\in\mathcal{S}_{A}
, which is independent of
({\bf X}\,,\;W)
. If it holds either
F(x\,A)=O[V(x\,A)]
, or
V(x\,A)=O[F(x\,A)]
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left([{\bf X}+{\bf Z}]\,W\in x\,A\right)\sim\mathbf{P}\left({\bf X}\,W\in x\,A\right)+\mathbf{P}\left({\bf Z}\,W\in x\,A\right)\,.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let
Z_{A}
the random variable of relation (
2.2
) following distribution
V_{A}
and similarly remember that

\displaystyle X_{A}:=\{u\;:\;{\bf X}\in u\,A\}\sim F_{A}\,.

(3.4)

Hence,
F_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
,
V_{A}\in\mathcal{S}
and either

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{A}(x)=O[\overline{V}_{A}(x)]\,,

or

Lemma 3.2

where at the first step we used
[

[

\mathcal{S}
, at the third step we employed
[
50

, while at the penultimate step we took into account relation (

4.
Arbitrarily dependent insurance and financial Risks

4.1.
Main result

In this section we look for asymptotic behavior of the entrance probability of discounted aggregate claim vectors into some rare set
x\,A
, on infinite time horizon, when insurance and financial risks are arbitrarily dependent. In opposite to previous section, now we assume that the condition
H\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}
holds, where

\displaystyle H(x\,A)=\mathbf{P}({\bf X}\,e^{-R(\theta_{1})}\in x\,A)=\mathbf{P}(X_{A}\,e^{-R(\theta_{1})}>x)=\overline{H}_{A}(x)\,,

(4.1)

and now we have remove the condition of non-negative Lévy process and instead of this we assume that the Laplace exponent satisfies
\phi(p)<0
, for some
p>J_{H_{A}}^{+}
. Namely, the distribution
H
depicts the product distribution of the insurance and financial risks, at each renewal epoch.

The following assumption is crucial in all over the section.

Assumption 4.1
.

We suppose that
\{{\bf X}^{(i)}\,e^{R(\tau_{i-1})-R(\tau_{i})}\,,\;i\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
represents a sequence of i.i.d. random vectors with common distribution
H
.

Now we are ready to provide the main theorem, in which the insurance and financial risks are arbitrarily dependent.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{R}
be a fixed set and let consider the discounted aggregate claim vectors in relation (
1
). Under the Assumptions
2.1
and
4.1
, with
H\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}
and additionally if
\phi(p)<0
, for some
p>J_{H_{A}}^{+}
, then we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf D}(\infty)\in x\,A\right]\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\left[{\bf X}\,e^{-R(s)}\in x\,A\right]\,\lambda(ds)\,.

(4.2)

Remark 4.1
.

As in Theorem
3.1
, if here we demand that
H\in(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{\mathscr{R}}
and
\phi(p)<0
, for some
p>J_{H_{A}}^{+}
, for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
, then relation (
4.2
) holds for any
A\in\mathscr{R}
. Even more, in special case when
A=(1,\,\infty)
the result of Theorem
4.1
is new. In comparison with
[
12
, Th. 2]
, in which was considered
d-
Lévy processes, that were arbitrarily dependent, hence it was more general model, was estimated the ruin probability over infinite time horizon, under the condition of
MRV
insurance and financial risks, with asymptotically dependent components, namely
\mu(({\bf 1},\,\vec{\infty}])>0
, and similar assumptions on Laplace exponent of the Lévy processes. Here, although we use a common Lévy process, the asymptotic estimation for the distribution tail of the discounted aggregate claim vectors is under the distribution class
(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{A})_{A}
, which is larger than

, and we do NOT demand asymptotic dependence among the components, see in
[
26

Remark 4.2
.

As we shall see in the subsection

, the framework of Theorem

allows a wide type of dependencies between insurance and financial risks, and contains both asymptotic independent and asymptotic dependent cases. This framework allows also the domination of financial risks into the insurance risks, instead of the classical problems in risk theory in which only the insurance risks dominated in financial risks.

4.2.

5.
Applications on ruin probabilities

In risk theory, when the risk models have claim distributions with heavy tails,
then the asymptotic behavior of the ruin probability coincides with the ’tail’ asymptotic behavior of the discounted aggregate claims. There are many papers on this direction on one-dimensional or multidimensional set up, see for example in
[
49
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
28
]
, among others.

In this section we study the insurer’s ruin probability, having in mind the asymptotic
behavior of the discounted aggregate claims, as was analyzed in Sections 3 and 4. We consider that the insurer’s discounted surplus process at the moment
t\geq 0
is given by the relation

\displaystyle{\bf U}(t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}U_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
U_{d}(t)\end{array}\right)=x\,{\bf l}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(s)}\,{\bf C}(ds)-{\bf D}(t)\,,

where
x>0
is the insurer’s initial capital, that is allocated through the vector
{\bf l}=(l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d})
, to
d
-lines of business, namely
l_{1},\,\ldots,\,l_{d}>0
with
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{d}=1
. The middle term in the right member of relation (
5
) is understood as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(s)}\,{\bf C}(ds)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(s)}\,C_{1}(ds)\\
\vdots\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-R(s)}\,C_{d}(ds)\end{array}\right)\,,

where
\{{\bf C}(t)=(C_{1}(t),\,\ldots,\,C_{d}(t))\,,\;t\geq 0\}
are the premiums of the
d
-lines of business, that represent non-negative cádlág processes with
{\bf C}({\bf 0})={\bf 0}
.

In the multivariate risk model, the ruin probability is defined in several forms, we refer to
[
13
]
and
[
41
]
, for more discussions. We give a general definition of ruin probability in multivariate risk model, as the
entrance probability of the surplus process into some ’ruin’ set
L
. Here, we use the
ruin set
L
, given by the following assumption.

Assumption 5.1
.

Let
L
be a set, that is open, decreasing, that means the

is increasing, and it satisfies the conditions
{\bf 0}\in\partial L
,

is convex, and

for any

Remark 5.1

The ruin set of Assumption

[

\displaystyle A:=({\bf l}-L)\in\mathscr{R}\,,

(5.6)

5.6

Further, the set

\displaystyle L_{2}=\left\{{\bf y}\;:\;\sum_{j=1}^{d}y_{j}<0\right\}\,,

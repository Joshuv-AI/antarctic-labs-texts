No quantum solutions to linear constraint systems from monomial measurement-based quantum computation in odd prime dimension
Markus Frembs, Cihan Okay, Ho Yiu Chung

Abstract
We combine the study of resources in measurement-based quantum computation (MBQC) with that of quantum solutions to linear constraint systems (LCS). Contextuality of the input state in MBQC has been identified as a key resource for quantum advantage, and in a stronger form, underlies algebraic relations between (measurement) operators which obey classically unsatisfiable (linear) constraints. Here, we compare these two perspectives on contextuality, and study to what extent they are related. More precisely, we associate a LCS to certain MBQC which exhibit strong forms of state-dependent contextuality, and ask if the measurement operators in such MBQC give rise to state-independent contextuality in the form of quantum solutions of its associated LCS. Our main result rules out such quantum solutions for a large class of MBQC. This both sharpens the distinction between state-dependent and state-independent forms of contextuality, and further generalises results on the non-existence of quantum solutions to LCS in finite odd (prime) dimension.

V
Conclusion

Taking the close correspondence between quantum solutions to (binary) linear constraint systems LCS (over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
) and measurement-based quantum computation (MBQC) with qubits in the form of the Mermin-Peres square
37
;
41
and Mermin’s star
38
nonlocal games as our motivation, in this paper we analysed the question whether, more generally, state-dependently contextual MBQC with qudits of dimension
d>2
gives rise to quantum solutions of a LCS naturally associated to it (see Def.
2
). We show that this is indeed the case under the restriction to the Pauli group (see Thm.
3
), which for odd dimension, however, is known not to contain quantum solutions to LCS (over
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
) that are not also classically satisfiable
42
. We then lift the restriction to the Pauli group by considering more general monomial unitaries as measurement operators in MBQC (see Def.
4
). This setting is both sufficiently general to comprise many instances of state-dependently contextual MBQC (Thm.
1
, see also Ref.
25
), and naturally embeds within the class of monomial matrices previously studied in Ref.
40
;
55
. Yet, even under this extension of the qudit Pauli group (and the resulting Clifford-type) hierarchy to normaliser subgroups of the special unitary group, our main result, Thm.
6
, states that it, too, admits no quantum solutions to LCS over
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for
d
odd prime that are not also classically solvable.

In summary, our work both generalises previous results
42
;
23
, and severely sharpens the known differences between qubit and qudit (state-independent) contextuality. While this leaves open the existence problem of LCS over
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for
d
odd which admit quantum but no classical solutions, it highlights that to search for such examples one needs to study inherently state-independent proofs of quantum contextuality
36
. A natural starting point in this direction is the algebraic (state-independent) reformulation of Kochen-Specker contextuality in Ref.
26
;
27
. We will discuss this together with other approaches in forthcoming work.

Appendix B

The measurement operators in

-MBQC are

-torsion unitaries in every tensor factor. If two such (global) operators commute, this implies that their (local) tensor factors commutate up to a

-th root of unity.

Lemma 9
.

Let
u=\otimes_{k=1}^{n}u_{k},u^{\prime}=\otimes_{k=1}^{n}u^{\prime}_{k}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})^{\otimes n}
such that
u_{k}^{d}=u^{\prime d}_{k}=\mathbbm{1}
for all
k\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
d
odd.
27
27

27

To avoid clutter, we suppress the site label for identity elements, writing
\mathbbm{1}_{k}=\mathbbm{1}
for all
k\in[n]
, and similarly for subsets

.
Then
[u,u^{\prime}]=0
if and only if
u_{k}u^{\prime}_{k}=\omega^{l_{k}}u^{\prime}_{k}u_{k}
with

for all

and such that
\sum_{k}l_{k}=0\mod d

Proof.

We first consider the bipartite case (
n=2
), and decompose the commutator of tensor products as tensor products of (anti-)commutators (here,
[u,u^{\prime}]=\frac{1}{2}(uu^{\prime}-u^{\prime}u)
and
\{u,u^{\prime}\}=\frac{1}{2}(uu^{\prime}+u^{\prime}u)

\displaystyle[u_{1}\otimes u_{2},u^{\prime}_{1}\otimes u^{\prime}_{2}]=[u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}]\otimes\{u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}\}+\{u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}\}\otimes[u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}]\;.

(20)

Clearly,
[u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}]=0=[u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}]
(with
l_{1}=0=l_{2}
) implies
[u_{1}\otimes u_{2},u^{\prime}_{1}\otimes u^{\prime}_{2}]=0
; moreover, (omitting indices) the case
\{u,u^{\prime}\}=0
is excluded, since this is equivalent to
u^{\prime}uu^{\prime-1}=-u
and thus
\mathbbm{1}=u^{\prime}u^{d}u^{\prime-1}=(u^{\prime}uu^{\prime-1})^{d}=-u^{d}=-\mathbbm{1}
, where we used that
u^{d}=\mathbbm{1}
and that

is odd. We may thus assume
[u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}],\{u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}\}\neq 0\neq[u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}],\{u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}\}
, which leaves the case
[u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}]\otimes\{u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}\}=-\{u_{1},u^{\prime}_{1}\}\otimes[u_{2},u^{\prime}_{2}]
, that is,
[u_{k},u^{\prime}_{k}]=\alpha_{k}\{u_{k},u^{\prime}_{k}\}
with
k=1,2
and
\alpha_{1}=-\alpha_{2}\in U(1)
. It follows that (omitting indices)
uu^{\prime}=[u,u^{\prime}]+\{u,u^{\prime}\}=(\alpha+1)\frac{1}{2}(uu^{\prime}+u^{\prime}u)

(since

and thus

). Let thus
u^{\prime}u=\lambda uu^{\prime}
, then
uu^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+1)(\lambda+1)uu^{\prime}
, hence,
\lambda=\frac{2}{\alpha+1}-1
. But since
\mathbbm{1}=u^{\prime}u^{d}u^{\prime-1}=(u^{\prime}uu^{\prime-1})^{d}=\lambda^{d}u^{d}=\lambda^{d}\mathbbm{1}
, this implies
\lambda^{d}=(\frac{2}{\alpha+1}-1)^{d}=1
. We may thus set
\lambda_{k}=\frac{2}{\alpha_{k}+1}-1=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}l_{k}}=\omega^{l_{k}}

which simplifies to

, hence,
l_{1}+l_{2}=0\mod d
.

Finally, since
u^{d}_{k}=\mathbbm{1}
for all

also implies that
(\otimes_{k\in I\subset[n]}u_{k})^{d}=\mathbbm{1}
, we can apply the above argument to every bi-partition. The result thus follows by decomposing the
n

The converse follows immediately for
l_{1}=0=l_{2}
, and for any pair

since

implies both

and
[u,u^{\prime}]\propto\{u,u^{\prime}\}
with the proportionality constants fixed by

as above.
∎

If we relax the assumption on

,
\phi^{N}_{S_{p}}(M)=\phi^{N}_{S_{p}}(M^{\prime})=:\tau
. If

, then also

, hence,

\displaystyle\phi^{L}_{H}(MM^{\prime})=\phi^{K}_{H}(\tau\cdot(MM^{\prime}))=\phi^{K}_{H}((\tau\cdot M)(\tau\cdot M^{\prime}))=\phi^{K}_{H}(\tau\cdot M)\phi^{K}_{H}(\tau\cdot M^{\prime})=\phi^{L}_{H}(M)\phi^{L}_{H}(M^{\prime})\;.

If

\displaystyle\phi^{L}_{H}(MN)=\phi^{L}_{H}(\tau\cdot(MN))=\phi^{K}_{H}(\tau\cdot(MN))

\displaystyle=\phi^{K}_{H}(\tau\cdot M)\phi^{K}_{H}(\tau\cdot N)=\phi^{L}_{H}(M)\phi^{L}_{H}(N)\;.\qed

Finally, we extend
\phi^{L}_{H}
to multiple tensor copies,
\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}:L^{\otimes n}\to H^{\otimes n}
, defined by
\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}\mapsto\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}\phi^{L}_{H}(M_{i})
. Note that

is well-defined: if

for

,

, then one quickly checks with Lm.
12

\displaystyle\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(M^{\prime}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{\prime}_{n})

\displaystyle=\phi^{L}_{H}(c_{1}M_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi^{L}_{H}(c_{n}M_{n})

\displaystyle=c_{1}\phi^{L}_{H}(M_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes c_{n}\phi^{L}_{H}(M_{n})=\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(M_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{n})\;.

Theorem 9
.

Let
M,M^{\prime}\in L^{\otimes n}
such that
[M,M^{\prime}]=\mathbbm{1}
and
M^{p}=M^{\prime p}=\mathbbm{1}
, then

\displaystyle\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(MM^{\prime})=\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(M)\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(M^{\prime})\;.

Proof.

Let
M=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}
and
M^{\prime}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{\prime}_{i}

[M,M^{\prime}]=\mathbbm{1}

[M_{i},M^{\prime}_{i}]\in Z(L)

\displaystyle\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(MM^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\bigotimes\left(\phi^{L}_{H}(M_{i})\phi^{L}_{H}(M^{\prime}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bigotimes\phi^{L}_{H}(M_{i})\right)\left(\bigotimes\phi^{L}_{H}(M^{\prime}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(M)\phi^{L^{\otimes n}}_{H^{\otimes n}}(M^{\prime})\;.\qed

Lemma 14
.

For every
(\xi,\sigma)\in K
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that
(\xi,\sigma)^{n}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma^{i}\cdot\xi,\sigma^{n}\right)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt(\xi,\sigma)^{k+1}=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sigma^{i}\cdot\xi,\sigma^{k}\right)(\xi,\sigma)=\left(\sigma^{k}\cdot\xi+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sigma^{i}\cdot\xi,\sigma^{k+1}\right)=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sigma^{i}\cdot\xi,\sigma^{k+1}\right)\;.\hskip 85.35826pt\qed

Lemma 15
.

Let

\displaystyle\phi^{K}_{H}(S_{\chi}M)=\phi^{K}_{H}(S_{\chi})\phi^{K}_{H}(M)=S_{\chi}\phi^{K}_{H}(M)\;.

Delsarte-Type Extremal Problems and Convolution Roots on Homogeneous Spaces
Mita D. Ramabulana

Abstract
. For a locally compact group G G and compact subgroup K K , we consider a Delsarte-type extremal problem for G G -invariant positive definite kernels on the homogeneous space G / K G/K , generalising a certain Turán problem for isotropic positive definite kernels on the unit sphere 𝕊 d \mathbb{S}^{d} in ℝ d + 1 \mathbb{R}^{d+1} . We exploit a correspondence between G G -invariant kernels on G / K G/K and K K -bi-invariant functions on G G to show that the Delsarte-type problem on a homogeneous space is equivalent to a Delsarte-type problem for K K -bi-invariant functions on its group G G of transformations. We use this correspondence to show the existence of an extremal function for the Delsarte problem on the homogeneous space. In the case where ( G , K ) (G,K) is a compact Gelfand pair, we show the existence of K K -bi-invariant convolution roots for positive definite K K -bi-invariant functions, consequently obtaining the existence of a G G -invariant convolution root for G G -invariant positive definite kernels.



1.
Introduction

Delsarte-type extremal problems for positive definite functions in the general setting of locally compact Abelian groups have been studied in
[
11
,
14
,
6
,
2
,
13
,
1
]
. In the non-commutative
compact
group setting, the Delsarte scheme is used in
[
10
]
to study the problem of mutually unbiased bases. One of the aims of this paper is to motivate the study of Delsarte-type problems on non-commutative groups. In particular, starting with a Delsarte-type problem on a homogeneous space
G/K
, we pose an equivalent Delsarte-type problem on its group
G
of transformations. The group of transformations is, in general, non-commutative, so that we deviate from the commutative group setting quite naturally. Nevertheless, commutativity still plays a role since the collection of admissible functions of our extremal problem are integrable
K
-bi-invariant functions, the collection of which forms a commutative algebra under convolution.

Our point of departure is the Turán problem for continuous positive definite kernels on the unit sphere in Euclidean space, which we now describe. We follow
[
7
,
8
]
. For an integer
d\geq 2
, let

\mathbb{S}^{d}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}:\|x\|=1\}

denote the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. A function
f:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is
positive definite
if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f(x_{i},x_{j})c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\geq 0

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all points
x_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, and for all complex numbers
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, with
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\theta(x,y)=\arccos(\langle x,y\rangle)
denote the great-circle distance between points
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, where
\langle x,y\rangle
denotes the inner product of
x
and
y
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. A function
f:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
is called
isotropic
if there exists a function
\psi:[0,\pi]\to\mathbb{C}
such that

f(x,y)=\psi(\theta(x,y)).

The fact that an isotropic function
f
factors as
f=\psi\circ\theta
simply means that its values depend only on the great-circle distance between its inputs. From the definition, it is straightforward to produce examples of isotropic functions.

To be consistent with the notation in
[
7
,
8
]
, let
\Psi^{d}
denote the collection of continuous functions
\psi:[0,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
with
\psi(0)=1
and such that the associated isotropic function
\psi\circ\theta:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is positive definite. For
0<c\leq\pi
, let
\Psi^{d}_{c}
denote the collection of functions
\psi\in\Psi^{d}
such that
\psi(t)=0
for
t\geq c
.
We now state the Turán problem for a spherical cap of radius
0<c\leq\pi
. The problem is that of computing

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}(c):=\sup_{\psi\in\Psi^{d}_{c}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\psi(\theta(x,y))\textup{d}y,

where
x
is an arbitrary point on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and the integral is with respect to the surface measure on the sphere.

The Turán problem for isotropic positive definite kernels on spheres is an open problem posed in
[
8
]
in connection with applications in spatial statistics. In this setup, the positive definite kernels are correlation functions, the sphere models the surface of the planet Earth, and the data comes from geophysical, meteorological and climatological applications.

In particular, if the kernel
\Phi(x,y)=\text{Cov}(Z(x),Z(y))
is the covariance of a real-valued random field
\{Z(x):x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}\}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, where, for each
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
Z(x)
is a random variable, then for a fixed
x\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the quantity

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\Phi(x,y)\,\mbox{d}y,

represents the total covariance or interaction of
Z(x)
with the rest of the field.
So, the expression

\sup_{\Phi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\Phi(x,y)\,\mbox{d}y

where
\Phi
runs through the collection of such kernels, is the maximum total covariance that can be concentrated locally around
x
, without violating the constraint of positive definiteness, and while ensuring zero correlation beyond radius
c
.

The above Turán problem for a spherical cap on the unit sphere motivates us to study a related Turán problem on the non-commutative group
\mathrm{SO}(d+1)
of
d\times d
matrices having determinant
1
, after the observation that the sphere is a homogeneous space in the sense that
\mathbb{S}^{d}
can be identified with
\mathrm{SO}(d+1)/\mathrm{SO}(d)
.

Apart from applications in statistics, a variant of our Delsarte-type problem on non-commutative groups appears independently in
[
17
]
in connection to sphere packing in metric geometries beyond Euclidean space.

Another aim of the paper is to study the existence of convolution roots for positive definite kernels on homogeneous spaces. In particular, we extend a result of
[
19
]
on the existence of isotropic convolutions roots for positive definite functions on the sphere to homogeneous spaces
G/K
where the pair
(G,K)
forms a Gelfand pair. Our approach is again to show that the problem of the existence of convolution roots for kernels on the homogeneous space is equivalent to the problem of the existence of convolution roots on its group of transformations.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

In this section, we recall some notions and notation. Let
G
be a locally compact group with Haar measure
\lambda_{G}
. For each
g\in G
, the assignment
\mu_{g}(U):=\lambda_{G}(Ug)
for all Borel measurable subsets
U\subset G
defines a left Haar measure, which is related to
\lambda_{G}
by a constant
\Delta_{G}(g)>0
such that
\mu_{g}=\Delta_{G}(g)\lambda_{G}
. Let
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
denote the multiplicative group of positive real numbers. The function
\Delta_{G}:G\to\mathbb{R}^{\times}
is a continuous group homomorphism called the modular function of
G

[
5
, Proposition 2.24]
). For any
g\in G
, let
R_{g}f
denote the right translate of
f
by
g
, given by
R_{g}f(x)=f(xg)
for all
x\in G
. According to
[
5
, Proposition 2.24]
,

\int_{G}R_{g}f(x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x)=\int_{G}f(xg)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x)=\Delta_{G}(g^{-1})\int_{G}f(x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x).

In particular, if
K
is a compact subgroup of
G
and
k\in K
, then
\Delta_{G}(k^{-1})=1
, and we have

(2)

\int_{G}f(xk)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x)=\Delta_{G}(k^{-1})\int_{G}f(x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x)=\int_{G}f(x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x).

If
K
is a subgroup of
G
, then
K
has a left-invariant Haar measure
\lambda_{K}
, unique to a positive constant. If
K
is compact, then
\lambda_{K}(K)<\infty
, and so we shall always normalise the Haar measure on a compact (sub)group
K
so that
\lambda_{K}(K)=1
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
L^{p}(G)
the usual Banach space of complex-valued functions on
G
satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{p}(G)}:=\left(\int_{G}|f(g)|^{p}\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

We denote by
L^{\infty}(G)
the collection of essentially bounded complex-valued functions, i.e., functions
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying

{\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}:=\esssup_{g\in G}|f(g)|<\infty}.

The support of a continuous function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
is the set

\operatorname{supp}f:=\overline{\{g\in G:f(g)\neq 0\}}.

The positive part of
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

f_{+}(g)=\max\{{f(g),0\}}\textup{ for all }g\in G,

and its negative part is given by

f_{-}(g)=\max\{{-f(g),0\}}\textup{ for all }g\in G.

The collection of continuous complex-valued functions on
G
is denoted by
C(G)
, and its subset consisting of continuous compactly supported functions is denoted by
C_{c}(G)
. For any Borel measurable functions
f,g:G\to\mathbb{C}
, the convolution of
f
and
g
is given by

f\ast g(x):=\int_{G}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(y),

whenever the integral exists.

Let
K
be a compact subgroup of
G
with Haar measure
\lambda_{K}
. Then
G/K
is a homogeneous space in the sense of
[
5
, Section 2.6, p.60]
, and has a
G
-invariant Radon measure
[
5
, Corollary 2.53]
. We denote this measure by
\mu
, and note according to
[
5
, Theorem 2.51]
that it satisfies

(3)

\int_{G}f(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\int_{G/K}\int_{K}f(gk)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\mu(gK)

for all
f\in C_{c}(G)
.

A function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
is positive definite if the inequality

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}f(g_{i}^{-1}g_{j})\geq 0

holds for all choices of
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
g_{i}\in G
with
1\leq i\leq n
.
Elementary properties of positive definite functions can be found in many texts on harmonic analysis on groups, for instance
[
15
,
5
,
16
]
. We record a few here.

Let
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
be a positive definite function. Then
f(e)\geq 0
and
|f(g)|\leq f(e)
for all
g\in G
. In particular, if
f
is a positive definite function satisfying
f(e)=0
, then
f
is identically zero. If
g
is another positive definite function, then
af+bg
is a positive definite function for all
a,b\geq 0
, and so is their pointwise product
fg
. A positive definite function is symmetric in the sense that
f(g)=\overline{f(g^{-1})}
for all
g\in G
. In particular, if
f
is real-valued, the
f(g)=f(g^{-1})
for all
g\in G
.

The reader may refer to
[
16
, Lemma 5.1.8]
,
[
15
, Theorem 1.3.2, Lemma 1.3.6, Theorem 1.4.1]
, and
[
5
, p. 91]
for proofs of these statements and other properties of positive definite functions.

Related to the notion of a positive definite function is that of an integrally positive definite function. Firstly, following
[
5
, p. 79]
, we define for a function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
, the involution of
f
by

f^{\ast}(g)=\Delta_{G}(g^{-1})\overline{f(g^{-1})}

for all
g\in G
.
Following
[
5
, Section 3.3, p. 83]
a function
f\in L^{\infty}(G)
on a locally compact group
G
is integrally positive definite if it satisfies

(4)

\int_{G}(\varphi^{\ast}\ast\varphi)f\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\geq 0

for all
\varphi\in C_{c}(G)
.
In other words, a function
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(G)
is integrally positive definite if

\int_{G}\int_{G}f(y^{-1}x)\varphi(x)\overline{\varphi(y)}\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(x)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(y)=\int_{G}(\varphi^{\ast}\ast\varphi)f\textup{d}\lambda_{G}\geq 0

for all
f\in C_{c}(G)
.

The following important proposition gives a standard procedure for constructing continuous positive definite functions.

Proposition 1

(
[
5
, Corollary 3.16]
)
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group. If
f\in L^{2}(G)
, let
f^{\#}(x):=\overline{f(-x)}
; then
f\ast f^{\#}
is a continuous positive definite function.

A function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
is called
K
-bi-invariant if
\varphi(kgk^{\prime})=\varphi(g)
for all
g\in G
and all
k,k^{\prime}\in K
. We also say that a subset
U\subset G
is
K
-bi-invariant if its characteristic function
\mathbf{1}_{U}
is
K
-bi-invariant. This is equivalent to
U=KUK
. We say that a subset
U\subset G
of
G
is symmetric if
U^{-1}=U
.

Let
C^{K}(G)
denote the collection of continuous
K
-bi-invariant functions
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
and
C_{c}^{K}(G)=C_{c}(G)\cap C^{K}(G)
the collection of continuous
K
-bi-invariant functions with compact support. For any continuous compactly supported function
f\in C_{c}(G)
, the function

f^{K}(g):=\int_{K}\int_{K}f(kgk^{\prime})\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})

for all
g\in G
, is a continuous compactly supported
K
-bi-invariant function.
Define the projection operator
P^{K}:C_{c}(G)\ni f\mapsto f^{K}\in C_{c}^{K}(G)
. We denote by
f^{K}_{+}
the positive part of
f^{K}
and by
f^{K}_{-}
its negative part.

We denote by
P(G,K)
the collection of real-valued continuous positive definite
K
-bi-invariant functions on
G
, and by
P_{1}(G,K)
the subset of
P(G,K)
consisting of those functions
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
f(e)=1
.

Convolution makes
C^{K}(G)
into an algebra and
C^{K}_{c}(C)
is a subalgebra of
C_{c}(G)
under convolution.
The pair
(G,K)
said to be a Gelfand pair if the algebra
C^{K}_{c}(G)
is commutative
[
16
, Definition 6.1.1]
, and a compact Gelfand pair if
G
is compact. In particular, for a Gelfand pair
(G,K)
the convolution of two
K
-bi-invariant functions is
K
-bi-invariant. A review of the harmonic analysis on Gelfand pairs can be found in
[
16
]
, and a more detailed treatment can be found in
[
4
,
18
]
. We shall need the following definitions and facts.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair. A function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
spherical function
if the functional
\chi
defined by

\chi(f)=\int_{G}f(g)\varphi(g^{-1})\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\mbox{ for all }f\in C^{K}_{c}(G),

satisfies

(5)

\chi(f\ast g)=\chi(f)\cdot\chi(g)

for all
f,g\in C_{c}^{K}(G)
. That is,
\chi
is a non-trivial character of the convolution algebra
C^{K}_{c}(G)
. Let
L^{1}(G)^{K}
denote the convolution algebra of integrable
K
-bi-invariant functions on
G
. Note that
L^{1}(G)^{K}
is commutative for a Gelfand pair
(G,K)
. A non-trivial character of
L^{1}(G)^{K}
is a linear functional
\chi:L^{1}(G)^{K}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying equation (
5
). The following theorem characterises the characters of
L^{1}(G)^{K}
.

Theorem 2

(
[
16
, Theorem 6.1.7]
)
.

Let
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
be a bounded spherical function. The mapping

f\mapsto\chi(f)=\int_{G}f(g)\varphi(g^{-1})\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)

is a character of
L^{1}(G)^{K}
, and each non-trivial character of
L^{1}(G)^{K}
is of this form.

Denote by
Z
the collection of positive definite spherical functions provided with the weak topology
\sigma(L^{\infty}(G),L^{1}(G))
. The space
Z
is locally compact by
[
16
, Proposition 6.4.2]
, and we refer to it as the dual space or
spherical dual
of the Gelfand pair
(G,K)
. The Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(G)^{K}
is defined to be

\widehat{f}(\varphi)=\int_{G}f(g)\varphi(g^{-1})\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in Z.

In particular,
\widehat{f}
is a function on
Z
. For more on the harmonic analysis on Gelfand pairs, see
[
16
, Section 6.4]
.

Now, without assuming that
(G,K)
is a Gelfand pair, we consider the space
G/K\times G/K
with the product topology. We refer to functions
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{C}
as kernels on
G/K
. Denote by
C(G/K\times G/K)
the space of continuous complex-valued kernels on
G/K
, and
C^{G}(G/K\times G/K)
its subset consisting of
G
-invariant kernels. For a kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
, define its positive part to be the kernel
\Phi_{+}:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Phi_{+}(xK,yK)=\max\{\Phi(xK,yK),0\}\mbox{ for all }x,y\in G.

Similarly, the negative part of
\Phi
is defined by

\Phi_{-}(xK,yK):=\max\{-\Phi(xK,yK),0\}\mbox{ for all }x,y\in G.

A kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{C}
is positive definite if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(x_{i}K,x_{j}K)a_{i}\overline{a_{j}}\geq 0

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all complex numbers
a_{1},...,a_{n}
, and for all points
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in G
. Denote by
P_{1}(G/K)
the collection of continuous positive definite kernels

\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying
\Phi(gK,gK)=1
for all
g\in G
. A kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
is
G
-invariant if

\Phi(gxK,gyK)=\Phi(xK,yK)\textup{ for all }{x,y,g\in G}.

Denote by
P^{G}(G/K)
the collection of continuous positive definite
G
-invariant kernels
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{C}
, and let
P^{G}_{1}(G/K)=P_{1}(G/K)\cap P^{G}(G/K)
be the collection of continuous positive definite
G
-invariant kernels with
\Phi(xK,xK)=1
for all
x\in G
. If a kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{C}
is
G
-invariant, then
\Phi(xK,yK)=\Phi(eK,x^{-1}yK)
for all
x,y\in G
. We define the support
\operatorname{supp}_{g}\Phi
with respect to
g\in G
of a not necessarily
G
-invariant kernel
\Phi
to be

\operatorname{supp}_{g}\Phi:=\overline{\{x\in G:\Phi(gK,xK)\neq 0\}}.

If
\Phi
is
G
-invariant, then
\operatorname{supp}_{g}\Phi=\operatorname{supp}_{h}\Phi
for all
g,h\in G
. In this case, we write
\operatorname{supp}\Phi:=\operatorname{supp}_{g}\Phi
for any
g\in G
, and call
\operatorname{supp}\Phi
the support of
\Phi
. We note that for a
G
-invariant kernel
\Phi
, we have that
\Phi(xK,yK)=\Phi(eK,x^{-1}yK)\neq 0
implies that
x^{-1}y\in\operatorname{supp}\Phi
.

If
\Phi
is a
G
-invariant kernel, then so are its positive and negative parts,
\Phi_{+}
and
\Phi_{-}
, respectively. In particular,
\operatorname{supp}\Phi_{\pm}
is defined.

For kernels
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
, their convolution is

(6)

\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}(xK,yK):=\int_{G/K}\Phi_{1}(xK,zK)\Phi_{2}(zK,yK)\textup{d}\mu(zK)

for all
x,y\in G
.

3.
A Bijective Correspondence

In this section,
G
is a locally compact group and
K
is a compact subgroup of
G
.
Let
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous positive definite
K
-bi-invariant function. Define the kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{C}
by

(7)

\Phi(gK,hK):=\varphi(g^{-1}h)

for all
g,h\in G
. In order that (
7
) be well-defined, we need it to be independent of the particular choice of coset representatives
g,h\in G
. In other words, we need
\Phi(gkK,hk^{\prime}K)=\Phi(gK,hK)
for all
k,k^{\prime}\in K
. However, using the
K
-bi-invaraince of
G
, we have

\Phi(gkK,hk^{\prime}K)=\varphi((gk)^{-1}hk^{\prime})=\varphi(k^{-1}g^{-1}hk^{\prime})=\varphi(g^{-1}h)=\Phi(gK,hK).

Therefore, (
7
) is well-defined. Note that
\Phi
is continuous. Observe that for all
g\in G
and all
xK,yK\in G/K
, we have

\begin{split}\Phi(g(xK),g(yK))=\Phi(gxK,gyK)=\varphi((gx)^{-1}gy)=\varphi(x^{-1}g^{-1}gy)&=\varphi(x^{-1}y)\\
&=\Phi(xK,yK).\end{split}

Therefore,
\Phi
is
G
-invariant. The calculation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(x_{i}K,x_{j}K)a_{i}\overline{a_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi(x_{i}^{-1}x_{j})a_{i}\overline{a_{j}}\geq 0

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, and for all
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, shows that
\Phi
is positive definite.

Now, let us suppose that we are given a
G
-invariant continuous positive definite kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{C}
. Define

(8)

\varphi(g):=\Phi(K,gK)

for all
g\in G
. The function
\varphi
in (
8
) is continuous. Using the
G
-invariance of
\Phi
, we have

\varphi(kgk^{\prime})=\Phi(K,kgk^{\prime}K)=\Phi(k^{-1}K,gk^{\prime}K)=\Phi(K,gK)=\varphi(g)

for all
k,k^{\prime}\in K
. Therefore,
\varphi
is a
K
-bi-invariant function. The calculation

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\varphi(x_{i}^{-1}x_{j})a_{i}\overline{a_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(K,x_{i}^{-1}x_{j}K)a_{i}\overline{a_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi(x_{i}K,x_{j}K)a_{i}\overline{a_{j}}\geq 0,

shows that
\varphi
is positive definite. The discussion above shows the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group and
K
a compact subgroup of
G
. There is a bijective correspondence between the collection of continuous positive definite
G
-invariant kernels on
G/K\times G/K
and the continuous positive definite
K
-bi-invariant functions on
G
. The correspondence is given by the relations

\Phi(xK,yK)=\varphi(x^{-1}y),

\varphi(x)=\Phi(K,xK)

for all
x,y\in G
.

Theorem
3
occurred to us quite naturally when we tried to connect the spherical Turán problem on groups and on homogeneous spaces, but the statement can be found in
[
16
, p. 113]
, where it is mentioned without proof. We included the proof here for ease of the reader.

Our aim in this section is to give some properties of this bijective correspondence. To that end, let
J:P(G,K)\to P^{G}(G/K)
be the map defined by

(9)

J(\varphi)(xK,yK)=\varphi(x^{-1}y)\mbox{ for all }x,y\in G.

In other words,
J
implements the bijective correspondence of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 4
.

If
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
are
G
-invariant kernels, then so is
\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}
.

Proof.

For all

\begin{split}\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}(gxK,gyK)&=\int_{G/K}\Phi_{1}(gxK,zK)\Phi_{2}(zK,gyK)\mbox{d}\mu(zK)\\

&=\int_{G/K}\Phi_{1}(xK,zK)\Phi_{2}(zK,yK)\mbox{d}\mu(zK)\\
&=\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}(xK,yK),\end{split}

where we have used the

and

to go from the first equality to the second, and the

to go from the second to the third. Hence

is

-invariant, as required.
∎

Let

be a Gelfand pair, recall that this implies that if

4.
Delsarte-type problem on extremal problem

In this section, we consider the homogeneous space
G/K
, where
G
is a locally compact group and
K
a compact subgroup.

4.1.
Delsarte problem for invariant kernels

For a
K
-bi-invariant symmetric identity neighbourhood
U\subset G
, and a
K
-bi-invariant symmetric subset
V\subset G
, define the collection

(11)

\mathcal{F}_{G/K}(U,V):=\Bigl\{\Phi\in P^{G}_{1}(G/K):\operatorname{supp}\Phi_{+}\subset U,\operatorname{supp}\Phi_{-}\subset V\Bigr\}.

We consider the Delsarte-type extremal problem of computing

(12)

\mathcal{C}_{G/K}(U,V):=\sup_{\Phi\in\mathcal{F}_{G/K}(U,V)}\int_{G/K}\Phi(xK,yK)\textup{d}\mu(yK).

Our motivation for studying this Delsarte-type extremal problem comes from the Turán problem for a spherical cap
[
8
, Problem 4]
. Let us formulate the Turán problem in the setting of homogeneous spaces. Let
U\subset G
be a symmetric
K
-bi-invariant compact neighbourhood of the identity in
G
, and put
\mathcal{F}_{G/K}(U):=\mathcal{F}_{G/K}(U,U)
. In other words
\mathcal{F}_{G/K}(U)
consists of continuous positive definite
G
-invariant kernels
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\Phi(xK,xK)=1
for all
x\in G
, and having
\operatorname{supp}\Phi\subset U
. The Turán problem asks for

(13)

\mathcal{T}_{G/K}(U):=\sup_{\Phi\in\mathcal{F}_{G/K}(U)}\int_{G/K}\Phi(K,gK)\mbox{d}\mu(gK).

Note that for any
x\in G
, we have

(14)

\begin{split}\int_{G/K}f(xK,gK)\mbox{d}\mu(gK)&=\int_{G/K}f(K,x^{-1}gK)\mbox{d}\mu(gK)\\
&=\int_{G/K}f(K,gK)\mbox{d}\mu(xgK)\\
&=\int_{G/K}f(K,gK)\mbox{d}\mu(gK).\end{split}

The first equality in (
14
) is justified by the
G
-invariance of
f
, and the third by the
G
-invariance of the measure
\mu
. This shows that the choice of
x
in the integrands (
14
) does not matter for the sake of calculating the integral. Nevertheless, since
K
is the stability subgroup of the coset
eK=K\in G/K
, it makes sense to pick the coset
K
as a basepoint in (
13
).

Let us specialise the Turán problem (
13
) to the sphere. Let
x=(0,\ldots,0,1)\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
be the north pole and
K
be the subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}(d+1)
consisting of rotations that fix
x
. Recall that
K
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SO}(d)
, and that
\mathbb{S}^{d}\cong\mathrm{SO}(d+1)/\mathrm{SO}(d)
. For
0<c\leq\pi
, let
U
be the identity neighbourhood in
\mathrm{SO}(d+1)
consisting of rotations
T\in\mathrm{SO}(d+1)
such that
\theta(Tx,x)\leq c
, where
\theta(Tx,x)
is the great-circle distance between
x
and
Tx
. Suppose that
T\in U
, so that
\theta(Tx,x)\leq c
. Then, for any
k,k^{\prime}\in K
, we have

\theta(kTk^{\prime}x,x)=\theta(Tk^{\prime}x,k^{-1}x)=\theta(Tx,x)\leq c.

Therefore,
kTk^{\prime}\in U
. In other words,
U
is
K
-bi-invariant. Clearly, the image
UK=\bigcup_{u\in U}uK
of

in
\mathbb{S}^{d}
under the canonical projection
p:\mathrm{SO}(d+1)/\mathrm{SO}(d)\to\mathbb{S}^{d}
is the spherical cap of radius
c
centred at
x
. It follows that (
13
) specialises to (
1
), showing that we obtain a true generalisation of the spherical Turán problem (
1
).

Even though the spherical Turán problem (
1
) is covered in (
13
), we study the more general extremal problem (
12
). In any case, we have

\mathcal{T}_{G/K}(U)=\mathcal{C}_{G/K}(U,U).

4.3.
Existence of extremisers

An application of the bijective correspondence of Theorem

.

We prove the existence of an extremal function for homogeneous spaces

for which the group

is amenable in the sense of
[
12

. What we need is the fact that any positive definite function
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
on an amenable group

has a non-negative integral
[

, Theorem 8.9 (ii’)]
. The method we use here closely follows that of
[

,
1
]
; see also the earlier work
[

]

Let us start with some lemmas.

Lemma 9
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group,
K
a compact subgroup of
G
, and
U
a
K
-bi-invariant subset of
G
. Let
f\in C_{c}(G)
, then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\operatorname{supp}f_{\pm}\subset U
, then
\operatorname{supp}f^{K}_{\pm}\subset U
.

(ii)

If
f
is positive definite, then so is
f^{K}
.

(iii)

We have the equality of the integrals

\int_{G}f^{K}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\int_{G}f(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g).

Proof.

For (i), let us suppose that
\operatorname{supp}f_{+}\subset U
. Let
g\in G
be such that
g\notin U
. We claim that
kgk^{\prime}\notin U
for all
k,k^{\prime}\in K
. Indeed, if
kgk^{\prime}\in U
, then
g\in KUK=U
.

Now, we have that
f(kgk^{\prime})\leq 0
for all
g\notin U
and all
k,k^{\prime}\in K
. Therefore,

f^{K}(g)=\int_{K}\int_{K}f(kgk^{\prime})\mbox{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\mbox{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\leq 0\mbox{ for all }g\notin U,

and hence
\operatorname{supp}f^{K}_{+}\subset U
. An analogous argument shows that
\operatorname{supp}f^{K}_{-}\subset U
whenever
\operatorname{supp}f_{-}\subset U
.

For (ii), we have

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f^{K}(g_{i}^{-1}g_{j})c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{K}\int_{K}f(kg_{i}^{-1}g_{j}k^{\prime})c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\\
&=\int_{K}\int_{K}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}f((g_{i}k^{-1})^{-1}(g_{j}k^{\prime}))c_{i}\overline{c_{j}}\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\geq 0.\end{split}

Therefore,
f^{K}
is positive definite.

For (iii), let us use Fubini’s Theorem to calculate the integral of
f^{K}
. We have

\begin{split}\int_{G}f^{K}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)&=\int_{G}\int_{K}\int_{K}f(kgk^{\prime})\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\\
&=\int_{K}\int_{K}\int_{G}f(kgk^{\prime})\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\\
&=\int_{K}\int_{K}\int_{G}f(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k)\textup{d}\lambda_{K}(k^{\prime})\\
&=\int_{G}f(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g),\end{split}

where we have used the left invariance of the Haar measure
\lambda_{G}
and the fact that
\Delta_{G}(k)=1
for all

(see (

\int_{K}1\textup{d}\lambda_{K}=1
to get the last equality.
∎

Lemma 10

(Mazur’s Lemma,

Let

We prove the existence of an extremal function for

n\geq N
, we have

\left|\int_{G}h_{n}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)-\mathcal{C}^{K}_{G}(U,V)\right|\leq\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}c_{i}^{n}\left|\int_{G}f_{i}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)-\mathcal{C}^{K}_{G}(U,V)\right|<\sum_{i=n}^{\infty}c_{i}^{n}\varepsilon=\varepsilon.

Therefore
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
\mathcal{C}^{K}_{G}(U,V)
-extremal. So, replacing, if necessary, the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by this new sequence, assume that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f
strongly in
L^{2}(G)
.

By passing to a pointwise almost everywhere convergent subsequence, assume that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f
pointwise almost everywhere. The fact that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|f_{n}(g)|\leq~1
for all
g\in G
and
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges almost everywhere to
f
implies that
f
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(G)
by 1. Since
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly in
L^{2}(G)
to
f
, we have

\int_{G}(h^{\ast}\ast h)(g)f(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(h^{\ast}\ast h)(g)f_{n}(g)(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\geq 0,

for all compactly supported continuous complex-valued functions
h
. This shows that
f
is integrally positive definite. The function
f
being integrally positive definite and
G
being
\sigma
-compact implies that
f
agrees almost everywhere with a continuous positive definite function. Thus, by correcting
f
on a set of Haar measure zero, thus not changing the value of its integral, assume that
f
is a continuous positive definite function.

The fact that
\operatorname{supp}f_{+}\subset U
and
\operatorname{supp}f_{-}\subset V
follows from the following facts: that
f
is continuous,
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
f
almost everywhere, that
\operatorname{supp}({f}_{n})_{+}\subset U
,
\operatorname{supp}({f}_{n})_{-}\subset V
and that
U
and
V
are closed.

Let us consider the projection
P^{K}(f)=f^{K}
of
f
. By Lemma
9
,
f^{K}
is a continuous positive definite
K
-bi-invariant function with
\operatorname{supp}f^{K}_{+}\subset U
,
\operatorname{supp}f^{K}_{-}\subset V
, and having total integral equal to that of
f
. Therefore, by replacing
f
by
f^{K}
, let us assume that
f
is
K
-bi-invariant.

Since
\operatorname{supp}f_{+}\subset U
we have

(17)

\int_{G}f_{+}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\int_{U}f_{+}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\leq\lambda_{G}(U)<\infty.

On the other hand, by Fatou’s lemma,

(18)

\begin{split}\int_{G}f_{-}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\\
&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\\
&\leq\lambda_{G}(U)<\infty,\end{split}

where the second inequality of (
18
) is obtained using that
f_{n}
being positive definite and integrable implies that

0\leq\int_{G}f_{n}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)-\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g).

Together, (
17
) and (
18
) imply that

\int_{G}|f(g)|\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\int_{G}f_{+}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)+\int_{G}f_{-}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)<\infty.

Therefore,
f\in L^{1}(G)
. Next, we show that
\int_{G}f\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}=\mathcal{C}^{K}_{G}(U,V)
. To that end, note that the sequence
((f_{n})_{+})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the positive parts of the
f_{n}
’s converges pointwise almost everywhere to
f_{+}
, and that
(f_{n})_{+}\leq\mathbf{1}_{U}
. Therefore, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and
\lambda_{G}(U)<\infty
, we have

(19)

\int_{G}f_{+}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g).

Recall that from (
18
) we have the inequality

(20)

\int_{G}f_{-}(g)\lambda_{G}(g)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g).

Now, subtracting (
20
) from (
19
) we have

\begin{split}\int_{G}f(g)\text{d}\lambda_{G}(g)&\geq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}(f_{n})_{+}(g)\text{d}\lambda_{G}(g)-\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\int_{G}(f_{n})_{-}(g)}\text{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\\
&=\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\int_{G}\left((f_{n})_{+}(g)-(f_{n})_{-}(g)\right)\text{d}\lambda_{G}(g)}\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{G}f_{n}(g)\mbox{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\mathcal{C}^{K}_{G}(U,V).\end{split}

Finally, we show that
f(e)=1
. To that end, note that
f(e)\leq 1
, otherwise, we could find some
f_{n}
and some
g\in U
such that
f_{n}(g)>1
, which would contradict
f_{n}(e)\leq~1
. Also,
f(e)\neq 0
, otherwise
f
would be identically zero, contradicting the fact that
\int_{G}f(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)=\mathcal{C}_{G}^{K}(U,V)\neq 0
. So, in order to show that
f(e)=1
, we assume, for a contradiction, that
0<f(e)<1
. Then the function
h:=\frac{f}{f(e)}
is in
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{K}(U,V)
and
\int_{G}h(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)>\mathcal{C}_{G}^{K}(U,V)
, contradicting the optimality of
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{K}(U,V)
. It follows that
f(e)=1
, and hence
f\in\mathcal{F}_{G}^{K}(U,V)
, as required.
∎

.

(ii)

Using Lemma

Lemma 13
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group,
K
a compact subgroup of
G
, and
H
an open subgroup of
G
containing
K
. Let
U
be a
K
-bi-invariant symmetric neighbourhood of identity in

, and

be
K
-bi-invariant symmetric subset of

. If a function
\varphi:H\to\mathbb{R}
is in
\mathcal{F}^{K}_{H}(U\cap H,V\cap H)
, then its trivial extension
\widetilde{\varphi}:G\to\mathbb{R}

Proof.

Suppose that
\varphi:H\to\mathbb{R}

. This means that

is continuous, positive definite, has

,
\operatorname{supp}\varphi_{-}\subset V
, and satisfies

is continuous, note that

is continuous at all

on the open set

Lemma 16

(Compare

)
.

Let

\sigma
-finite

-bi-invariant symmetric neighbourhood of

, and

be a

-bi-invariant symmetric subset of

\sigma
-compact subgroup

of

, containing

, such that

\mathcal{C}_{G}^{K}(U,V)=\mathcal{C}_{H}^{K}(U,V).

Proof.

On account of Lemma
15
, let
H
be an open
\sigma
-compact subgroup of

containing the union
U\cup K
of

and the subgroup
K
. Equip

\lambda_{H}:=\lambda_{G}|_{H}
, i.e., the Haar measure of

. Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a
\mathcal{C}_{G}^{K}(U,V)
-extremal sequence in

\mathcal{C}_{H}^{K}(U,V)\geq\int_{H}f_{n}|_{H}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{H}(g)\geq\int_{G}f_{n}(g)\textup{d}\lambda_{G}(g)\geq\mathcal{C}_{G}^{K}(U,V)-\frac{1}{n}.

For the reverse inequality, let
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

5.
convolution roots

Following
[
19
]
, for kernels
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, their
spherical convolution
is defined to be

(24)

\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}(x,y):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d}}\Phi_{1}(x,z)\Phi_{2}(z,y)\textup{d}z

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
. A kernel
\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to have a
spherical convolution root
if there exists a kernel
\Psi:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\Phi=\Psi\ast\Psi
. In
[
19
]
, the question of the existence of an isotropic spherical convolution root for a positive definite kernel on the sphere is considered. In particular, the following was proved.

Theorem 20

(See
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Any continuous isotropic positive definite kernel
\Phi:\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
has an isotropic spherical convolution root
\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d})
.

In this section, we consider a similar question in the more general setting of compact Gelfand pairs.

Recall that for kernels
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
, their spherical convolution is

(25)

\Phi_{1}\ast\Phi_{2}(xK,yK):=\int_{G/K}\Phi_{1}(xK,zK)\Phi_{2}(zK,yK)\textup{d}\mu(zK)

for all
x,y\in G
. Clearly, when
G=\mathrm{SO}(d)
and
K=\mathrm{SO}(d-1)
, then
G/K\cong\mathbb{S}^{d}
and (
25
) reduces to (
24
). So, we consider the question of whether for any continuous positive definite
G
-invariant kernel
\Phi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
there exists a
G
-invariant kernel
\Psi:G/K\times G/K\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\Phi=\Psi\ast\Psi
. We shall answer this question in the case where
(G,K)
is a compact Gelfand pair. Note that
(\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathrm{SO}(d-1))
is a compact Gelfand pair, so our result contains that of
[
19
]
. The argument in
[
19
]
is specific to the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
, while our argument is different and works for all compact Gelfand pairs.

Theorem 21
.

Let
(G,K)
be a compact Gelfand pair, then for any continuous positive definite
K
-bi-invariant function
f:G\to\mathbb{R}
, there exists a positive definite
K
-bi-invariant function
g:G\to\mathbb{C}
in
L^{2}(G)
such that
f=g\ast g
.

Proof.

Since
G
is compact and
f
is continuous, we have that
f\in L^{1}(G)
. Then
f\in L^{1}(Z)
by Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 6.4.5]
. Since
\widehat{f}
is non-negative, the square root
h=\sqrt{\widehat{f}}
is well-defined and is in
L^{2}(Z)
. Now, let
g:G\to\mathbb{C}
be the inverse Fourier transform of
h
, which exists by
[
16
, Theorem 6.4.6]
. Since
G
is compact and hence of finite Haar measure, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality implies that
g
is in
L^{1}(G)
. It follows that the Fourier transform of
g
is defined everywhere on
Z
, and is equal to
h
.

Now, since
g\in L^{1}(G)
, we have that
\widehat{g\ast g}=\widehat{g}\cdot\widehat{g}
by
[
16
, Theorem 6.4.3]
. Hence
\widehat{g\ast g}=h^{2}=\widehat{f}
. But
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(Z)
because
f\in L^{2}(G)
. Hence, taking the
L^{2}
inverse Fourier transform on both sides of
\widehat{g\ast g}=\widehat{f}
, we have that
f=g\ast g
in
L^{2}(G)
. But both
f
and
g\ast g
are continuous, so
f=g\ast g
everywhere on
G
.
∎

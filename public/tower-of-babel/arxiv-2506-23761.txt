Macroscopic States in Bose–Einstein Condensate Dark Matter Model with Axionlike Interaction
A.V. Nazarenko

Abstract
The phase diagrams of ultralight dark matter (DM), modeled as a self-gravitating Bose–Einstein condensate with axionlike interaction, are studied. We classify stable, metastable and unstable DM states over a wide range of condensate wave function amplitudes. It is shown that the axionlike interaction causes instability and an imaginary speed of sound at low amplitudes, whereas in a specific high-amplitude band, DM attains a stable state capable of forming a dense solitonic core and suppressing quantum fluctuations in the surrounding galactic DM halo. These findings are corroborated by evaluating thermodynamic functions for DM in the dwarf galaxy NGC 2366 and in its hypothetical analogues with different core-to-halo mass ratios. Distinct DM phase compositions respond differently to fluctuation-induced partial pressure, resulting in a first-order phase transition in a certain range of an interaction parameter. While the DM properties in NGC 2366 lie within the supercritical regime, the phase transition nonetheless provides a thermodynamic marker separating stable from unstable DM configurations. Once a dense core forms–reaching a threshold of about 12% of the total mass–the enhanced gravitation stabilizes the DM halo against fluctuations, while the internal pressure ensures core stability. In particular, we find that NGC 2366’s dense DM comprises roughly 19% of the DM mass while occupying only 4.7% of its total volume.

Introduction

Several decades ago, axions were proposed as hypothetical pseudo-Goldstone
bosons
PQ77
, offering a solution to the charge-parity (CP) problem
in quantum chromodynamics (QCD). This innovative idea required an explanation
of both the emergence of axions during the Universe’s evolution and their
broader cosmological implications. Axions were soon regarded as promising
candidates for dark matter (DM)

theoretically supported by mechanisms for their
production

;

;

and their transition to the nonrelativistic
condensate state

;

;

describe low-energy axions with large occupation numbers either as
a classical field in effective field theory or as a macroscopic wave function
in the Gross–Pitaevskii approach.

Often considered within the context of DM structures, modeling of axion stars
and clumps has revealed that their masses and sizes are relatively small from
a cosmological perspective (see

stability conditions inherent to cold bosonic matter

;

As argued in

;

, the masses
10^{-4\pm 1}~\text{eV}c^{-2}

of axions preclude forming giant condensates comparable in size
to galactic DM halos.

In contrast, early attempts to model DM halos and rotation curves of galaxies
yielded extremely small values of the boson masses
Bal83
;
Sin
that are
tens of orders of magnitude smaller than those predicted in QCD.
Therefore, reproducing the observed data requires invoking dark ultralight
axions or axionlike particles

.

Although the presence of self-interactions between ultralight DM particles
ensures a better description of astrophysical phenomena,
the fuzzy DM models often omit it (see
Hu
;
Schive1
;
Chen17
;
Zim25
),
limiting themselves to taking into account only gravitational interactions,
as was originally assumed

It seems natural for us to investigate self-gravitating DM in real visible
objects using dark bosons with masses
10^{-22\pm 1}~\text{eV}c^{-2}
,
incorporating the simplest instanton self-interaction borrowed from
QCD
BZ19
. Specifically, our goal is to apply the equilibrium
statistical approach to the DM in dwarf galaxies, effectively replacing,
in an ergodic sense, the analysis of the time evolution of a single
spatial configuration of DM by studying a statistical ensemble of
static configurations, which is governed by thermodynamic potential.
In this way we intend to classify and describe the phases of such a
matter.

Axionlike interactions can give rise to both stable and unstable states,
allowing their coexistence in nonuniform DM. It is already known that
the unstable state arises in the self-attraction mode
Nambo24
at small amplitudes
of the axion field, corresponding to rarefied matter. Although high amplitudes
seem unlikely for an axion field (but not necessarily for other
substances), some of their values should correspond to stable states

.
In any case, the overall state of the DM is determined by the spatial
distribution and relative abundance of these states, which collectively
define DM composition. Accordingly, the complete configurations are also
classified as either stable or unstable, and it is assumed that these regimes
can change via a first-order quantum phase transition at zero temperature.

One may observe parallels with the ensemble of oscillons
Osc1
generated
by the instanton self-interaction, leading in the absence of gravity to the
three-dimensional sine-Gordon equation
Man04

results on
axitons

;

;

comparison of sets of states.

Neglecting the Universe’s evolution in describing current visible structures,
we thus focus on the statistical properties of self-interacting DM (SIDM) within
the model of self-gravitating Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC). Phase diagrams
should then reflect these properties, tracing transitions among distinct states
and configurations.

Numerous studies have rigorously examined the properties of BEC DM
with two- and three-particle interactions, using both exact numerical
methods and various analytical approximations, such as the Thomas–Fermi
approximation and the variational method
(see

;

;

The thermodynamic functions in these models have been carefully studied,

;

;

of a first-order phase transition between rarefied and dense states, which
is driven by quantum fluctuation pressure and governed by coupling

Additionally, we would like to note the impact of the formation
of composites from the initial DM particles

;
GN22
;
GKKN

as well as the quantum entanglement of parts of the DM halo
GN21
;

Building on those studies, we transition from polynomial-type
self-interaction (over a field or wave function) to a nonlinear periodic

To constrain the parameters of our BEC DM model described by
the Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson equations, we consider the DM
dominating dwarf galaxy NGC 2366 as a reference. This nearby galaxy
provides an opportunity to study reionization processes in vigorous
low-metallicity starbursts, such as those that occurred during
the cosmic reionization epoch

to calibrate the model by fitting its observed rotation curve

and then generate a statistical ensemble of hypothetical DM distributions

By comparing different configurations using phase diagrams,
we aim to reveal the uniqueness of the DM structure in NGC 2366,

In Sec.

The final Sec.

II.3
The excitation spectrum and instabilities

Given the quantum-fluid nature of BEC DM, let us analyze the spectrum
of its small excitations. We reformulate Eq. (
19
) into the
non-stationary Gross–Pitaevskii equation for the complex-valued wave
function
X
, which depends on time
t
, as

\displaystyle\left(-\Delta_{\xi}+W\right)X=\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}X,

(51)

\displaystyle W(\xi,t)=A{\cal V}(\xi,t)+j_{0}(|X(\xi,t)|),

(52)

\displaystyle{\cal V}(\xi,t)=-\frac{1}{\xi}\int_{0}^{\xi}|X(s,t)|^{2}s^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 39.83385pt-\int_{\xi}^{\xi_{B}}|X(s,t)|^{2}s\,\mathrm{d}s,

(53)

where
\mathrm{i}=\sqrt{-1}
.

To derive both the continuity equation and the Euler hydrodynamic equation,
we substitute
X=\chi\exp{(\mathrm{i}\theta)}
,
where
\chi
and
\theta
are real functions of space and time.

Substituting the wave function
X
into (
51
) and separating the imaginary
and real parts, we obtain the desirable equations:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta+{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}(\eta{\bf v})=0,

(54)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\left(\frac{{\bf v}^{2}}{2}+2W-\frac{2}{\sqrt{\eta}}\Delta_{\xi}\sqrt{\eta}\right)=0,

(55)

where the velocity vector
{\bf v}\equiv 2{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\theta
is expressed
via the gradient operator
{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}
; and
\eta=\chi^{2}
is the particle
number density, as usual.

Consider a thin spherical shell defined by the radial interval from
\xi

to
\xi+\delta\xi
for small
\delta\xi
. Within this shell, we admit
small perturbations
\delta\eta
in the particle density and
\delta{\bf v}

in the collective velocity, added to the fixed background values

\eta_{0}
and
{\bf v}_{0}=0
with the subscript “0”.
Further, we use Eqs. (
54
)-(
55
), linearized in

\delta\eta
and
\delta{\bf v}
.

Differentiating the continuity equation and substituting the Euler equation
to eliminate
\delta{\bf v}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left[\partial_{t}\delta\eta+\eta_{0}{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\delta{\bf v}\right]

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{t}\delta\eta-\eta_{0}\Delta_{\xi}\left(2W-\frac{1}{\eta_{0}}\Delta_{\xi}\delta\eta\right).

This equation suggests that the background value
\eta_{0}
evolves more slowly
over time and space than the perturbation
\delta\eta
. In the absence of
gravitation, it is possible to allow the homogeneity of matter, when
\eta_{0}

is constant.

Since the interaction
W
comprises both the gravitational term, governed
by the parameter
A
, and the self-interaction, we expand it as a series,
retaining only the linear corrections in
\delta\eta
:

W=W_{0}+A\Delta^{-1}_{\xi}\delta\eta+c^{2}_{s}(\chi_{0})\frac{\delta\eta}{\eta_{0}},

(57)

by using the formula for the sound speed
c_{s}
, see (
41
).

Assuming that inside the thin shell the gravitational potential
{\cal V}_{0}

can be replaced by the Newtonian
\sim 1/\xi
, and the self-interaction

j_{0}(\chi_{0})
remains constant, we derive the equation:

\partial_{t}^{2}\delta\eta-2c^{2}_{s}(\chi_{0})\Delta_{\xi}\delta\eta-2A\eta_{0}\delta\eta+\Delta^{2}_{\xi}\delta\eta=0.

(58)

It is worth noting other arguments in
Ki03
;
FHWM
that exclude
the influence of
{\cal V}_{0}
on the evolution of excitations.

Setting

\delta\eta=\chi_{0}(\delta\chi_{k}+\delta\overline{\chi}_{k}),

(59)

where
\delta\overline{\chi}_{k}
means the complex conjugate of
\delta\chi_{k}
,
we define the spherically symmetric form of excitation as

\displaystyle\delta\chi_{k}=\chi_{+k}f_{k}(\xi,t)+\chi_{-k}\overline{f}_{k}(\xi,t),

(60)

\displaystyle f_{k}(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\xi}\exp{(\mathrm{i}k\xi-\mathrm{i}\omega_{k}t)},

(61)

where
\chi_{\pm k}
are real amplitudes of incident and outgoing
spherical waves;
\omega_{k}
is their frequency indexed by
the wave number
k
.

Substituting, we obtain the dispersion relation:

\omega^{(\pm)}_{k}=\pm\sqrt{k^{4}+2k^{2}c^{2}_{s}(\chi_{0})-2A\eta_{0}}.

(62)

Its dimensional form looks like

\frac{E^{(\pm)}_{k}}{\hbar}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m^{2}}q^{4}+q^{2}v^{2}_{s}-4\pi G\rho},

(63)

where the wave vector squared
q^{2}=k^{2}/r_{0}^{2}
and
the mass density
\rho=\rho_{0}\eta_{0}
. The dimensional
sound speed squared is

v^{2}_{s}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}r_{0}^{2}}c^{2}_{s}.

(64)

Note that the obtained form of dispersion relation is well
known
H2019
;
Ch20
. And the models differ in the properties
of the sound speed
v^{2}_{s}
.

Let us first analyze the case without accounting for gravitation,
when
A=0
and the dispersion relation is reduced to the form:

\omega_{k}^{(\pm)}=\pm\sqrt{k^{4}-k^{2}\chi_{0}j_{1}(\chi_{0})}.

(65)

We immediately see that at the turning points in Fig.
3

\chi_{0}=a_{n}
from the sequence
\{a_{n}\}
in (
II.2

Since

remains positive for
\chi_{0}\in(a_{2n-1};a_{2n})
,

and

in Fig.

On the other hand, the physically constructive phase-1 and phase-2 correspond to

\chi_{0}

For a nonzero parameter

IV
Conclusion

We developed a statistical approach to the stationary nonrelativistic BEC DM model
with instanton self-interaction,
V_{\text{inst}}=p_{0}-2\cos{\chi}
in terms of
the dimensionless real amplitude
\chi
of the macroscopic wave function at

p_{0}=\pm 2
. Introducing the chemical potential
\mu
and the internal pressure
p

at zero temperature [see (
35
) and (
43
)], we classified stable, unstable
and metastable states of matter using the
p-\mu
phase diagram in the range

\chi\in[0;4.5\pi]
in Fig.
3
. The phase boundaries
\chi=a_{n}
arise from
the zeros of the spherical Bessel function,
j_{1}(a_{n})=0
with
a_{1}=0
, defining
intervals of stability
\chi\in(a_{2n};a_{2n+1})
and instability

\chi\in(a_{2n-1};a_{2n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Both the thermodynamic functions
and the excitation spectrum confirm this partitioning, indicating that
the squared sound speed
c^{2}_{s}(\chi)
in (
41
) vanishes at
\chi=a_{n}
, is positive
within stable bands, and negative within unstable ones. In particular,
the small-amplitude regime at
\chi\in[0;\chi_{c}]
, where

\chi_{c}=a_{2}\simeq 4.493409
, is also shown to be unstable, which is consistent
with earlier studies
Nambo24
;
KT94
.

We argued that a first-order phase transition is possible between two existing
stable phases with higher
\chi
in the bands we have defined.
Such amplitudes are usually ignored in the axion theory.

These findings explain why, taking into account self-gravity, the numerical
solutions of the Gross–Pitaevskii–Poisson equations in the Thomas–Fermi
approximation must be piecewise: radial integration necessarily stops at each
“critical” amplitude
\chi=a_{n}
, see (
48
).

We apply the BEC model (
19
)-(
20
), which takes into account
self-gravity and quantum fluctuations, to nonperturbatively describe DM in
dwarf galaxies over the range
\chi\in[0;2.5\pi]
, which spans
exactly one stable and one unstable phase. After prescribing mathematically
consistent initial conditions for the nonlinear spatial evolution, we fit
a representative numerical solution to the rotation curve of the DM-dominated
dwarf NGC 2366
Oh08
. The fit yields a dark boson mass

m\simeq 0.1171\times 10^{-22}~\text{eV}c^{-2}
, which is comparable to the
mass in
KMT
. While NGC 2366 exhibits active star formation and
ISM reionization, we employ its DM configuration solely as a prototypical case
of our statistical ensemble of DM distributions in similar galactic settings.

Requiring that the stable phase forms the dense solitonic core of the DM halo,
we compute thermodynamic functions for NGC 2366 and a family of hypothetical
analogues with different core-to-halo mass ratios. By varying the partial pressure
induced by quantum fluctuations
\Pi_{\rm q}
, we trace the resulting macroscopic
characteristics in Fig.
10
, which undergo a first-order phase transition
for certain strengths of the gravitational parameter
A
, the only coupling
constant in the model. The revealed jumplike behavior of the functions is
attributed to the ambiguity in the quantum pressure
\Pi_{\rm q}
.

In the case of NGC 2366, however, the DM lies in
the supercritical regime, when its properties vary smoothly. Due to
Fig.
10
(h) and
13
(a), we find that a core exceeding
the threshold
\gtrsim 12\%
of the total mass enhances gravity and suppresses
quantum fluctuations in the surrounding rarefied halo, while the internal
pressure
p
ensures core stability. Concretely, NGC 2366’s DM dense core
contains
\approx 19\%
of the DM mass but occupies only
\approx 4.7\%
of
its volume.

We define the threshold of the core mass from the gravitational potential extremum
in Fig.
13
(a), comparing it with the end of the stability plateau of the grand
canonical potential
\Omega
in Fig.
12
. The extremum existence means that
the gravitational potential values are repeated for different configurations, thereby
causing ambiguity in thermodynamic potential density
\omega
in Fig.
8
.

Moreover, in the mass-radius diagram in Fig.
11
(a) – which shows stable and
unstable branches analogous to those of the axiton
BZ19
;
KT94
;
ax5
–
the DM configuration for NGC 2366 lies firmly on the stable branch, consistent
with its existence in nature.

Thus, the thermodynamic signature provides a novel diagnostic for distinguishing
stable and unstable DM configurations in galactic halos. Viewed ergodically,
it provides an alternative to time-dependent approaches, and a direct comparison
between the two deepens physical insight.

Abstract
. We show that ABV-packets for p p -adic groups do not depend on the choice of a Whittaker datum, but the function from the ABV-packet to representations of the appropriate equivariant fundamental group does, and we find this dependence exactly. We study the relation between open parameters and tempered parameters and Arthur parameters and generic representations. We state a genericity conjecture for ABV-packets and prove this conjecture for quasi-split classical groups and their pure inner forms. Motivated by this we study ABV-packets for open parameters and prove that they are L-packets, and further that the function from the packet to the equivariant fundamental group given by normalized vanishing cycles coincides with the one given by the Langlands correspondence. From this conclude Vogan’s conjecture on A-packets for tempered representations: ABV-packets for tempered parameters are Arthur packets and the function from the packet to the equivariant fundamental group given by normalized vanishing cycles coincides with the one given by Arthur.

Introduction

Arthur packets are a key concept in establishing the local Langlands correspondence for classical groups, see

ABV-packets for

We briefly recall the definition of ABV-packets for
p
-adic groups. For unexplained notations and more details, see Section
1

F
be a non-archimedean local field with Weil group

and let

{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}

Let

\lambda:W_{F}\to{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}

\mathsf{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})^{\mathrm{simple}}_{/\mathrm{iso}}
is the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects in the category of
H_{\lambda}

V_{\lambda}
.

The key tool in the construction of ABV-packets is the functor

\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{\phi}:\mathsf{Per}_{H_{\lambda}}(V_{\lambda})\to\mathsf{Rep}(A^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}_{\phi}),

\phi

[

Using this, one defines

\Pi_{\phi,\mathfrak{w}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)=\{\pi\in\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\lambda}(G){\ |\ }\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{\phi}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{w}}(\pi))\neq 0\}

\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{\phi}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{w}}:\Pi_{\phi,\mathfrak{w}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)\to\mathsf{Rep}(A^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}_{\phi})

1.3.
Conormal variety to the parameter variety

The conormal to

is

\Lambda_{\lambda}{\,:=\,}\{(x,y)\in V_{\lambda}\times V_{\lambda}^{*}{\ |\ }[x,y]=0\},

were,
[~,~]
is the Lie bracket in

; see
[

*Proposition 6.3.1.
Likewise,

\Lambda_{\lambda}^{*}{\,:=\,}\{(y,x)\in V_{\lambda}^{*}\times V_{\lambda}{\ |\ }[x,y]=0\}

We write

for the obvious projections and set

Pyasetskii duality

defines a bijection between the

-orbits in

and the
H_{\lambda}
-orbits in

and is uniquely characterized by the following property: under the isomorphism

defined by

, the closure of
\Lambda_{C}
in
\Lambda_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to the closure of
\Lambda_{C^{*}}^{*}
in
\Lambda^{*}_{\lambda}
.
This duality may also be characterized by passing to the regular conormal variety as follows.
Set

\Lambda_{C}^{\mathrm{reg}}=\{(x,y)\in\Lambda_{C}{\ |\ }y\in q(\Lambda_{C})\setminus q(\bar{\Lambda}_{C^{\prime}})\text{ where }C\subseteq\bar{C^{\prime}}\text{ but }C\neq C^{\prime}\},

where

is projection, and also set

\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{reg}}{\,:=\,}\bigcup_{C}\Lambda_{C}^{\mathrm{reg}}
(this is a disjoint union, in fact).

for an

-orbit

Then

\Lambda_{C}^{\mathrm{reg}}{\ \cong\ }\Lambda_{C^{*}}^{*,\mathrm{reg}}

under the isomorphism

given above; see
[

]
*Lemma 6.4.2.
It follows that

C^{*}=q_{\mathrm{reg}}(p_{\mathrm{reg}}^{-1}(C)),

where

and

are the obvious projections.

We close this Section with two simple lemmas that we will use later.

Lemma 1.1
.

For every
H_{\lambda}
-orbit
C
in
V_{\lambda}
,
\dim\Lambda_{C}=\dim V_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

In
[
11
]
*Proposition 6.3.1 we show that
\Lambda_{C}
, as defined above, is the conormal bundle to
V_{\lambda}
above
C
.
Recall that the cotangent bundle is
V_{\lambda}\times V_{\lambda}^{*}
so that the conormal bundle to
C\subset V_{\lambda}
at a point

is given by
\Lambda_{C,x}=T^{*}_{C,x}(V_{\lambda})
is given by
\{y\in T^{*}_{x}(V_{\lambda}){\ |\ }y(x^{\prime})=0,\ \forall x^{\prime}\in T_{x}(C)\}
. Observe also that
\dim\Lambda_{C}=\dim C+\dim\Lambda_{C,x}
for any

. Since
\dim T_{x}(C)=\dim C
and
\dim T^{*}_{x}(V_{\lambda})=\dim V^{*}_{\lambda}=\dim V_{\lambda}
, it follows that
\dim\Lambda_{C,x}=\codim C
. Therefore,
\dim\Lambda_{C}=\dim C+\codim C=\dim V_{\lambda}
.
∎

Lemma 1.2
.

The dual to the closed orbit
C_{0}
in
V_{\lambda}
is the open orbit in
V_{\lambda}^{*}
and the dual to the open orbit

in
V_{\lambda}
is the closed orbit in
V_{\lambda}^{*}
.

Proof.

First note that
\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{reg}}
is open in
\Lambda_{\lambda}
.
By
[

]
*Proposition 6.4.3,
\Lambda^{\mathrm{reg}}_{C}\subseteq C\times C^{*}
for every

-orbit

. It now follows from Lemma

that

.
Now take

, the closed orbit in

, for which

.
Then

. Since

, it follows that

, so

an the open

-orbit in

, which is unique since

is a prehomogeneous vector space.
Since orbit duality is an involution, it follows that the dual to the open orbit

∎

1.5.
Whittaker data

Let
F
be a non-archimedean local field and
G
be a connected quasi-split reductive group over
F
.
Our exposition mainly follows
[
17
]
*Section 9 and
[
29
]
*Section 4.

Let
B
be a Borel subgroup of
G
with unipotent radical
U
. The torus
T=B/U
acts on
\text{Hom}(U,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. Recall the following basic definition.
A character
\theta:U(F)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is called
generic character
if its stabilizer in
T(F)
is the centre
Z(F)
of
G(F)
.
For a generic character
\theta
of
U(F)
, a representation
\pi
of
G(F)
is called
\theta
-generic if
\text{Hom}_{U(F)}(\pi,\theta)\neq 0
.
This only depends on the
T(F)
-orbit of
\theta
.
If
\theta
is understood from the context, we simply say
\pi
is generic.

The set
D
of
T(F)
-orbits on the generic characters forms a principal homogeneous space for the abelian group

T_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(T(F))
,
where
T_{ad}(F)
is the corresponding maximal torus of the adjoint group
G_{ad}(F)
. A
Whittaker datum

\mathfrak{w}
for
G
is a
G(F)
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(B,\theta)
, where
B
is a Borel subgroup of
G
and
\theta
is a generic character of
U(F)
, where
U
is the unipotent radical of
B
.
Such a pair is a principal homogeneous space for the abelian group
G_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(G(F))
.

It is known that

T_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(T(F)){\ \cong\ }G_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(G(F)).

Thus if we fix a Borel subgroup
B
, giving a Whittaker datum
(B^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})
is equivalent to giving an element in
D
.
This means there are two ways to define Whittaker datum: one is using elements in
D
by fixing a Borel, which is a principal homogeneous space for
T_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(T(F))
; and the other one is using the
G(F)
-conjugacy classes of pairs
(B,\theta)
, which is a principal homogeneous space for the group
G_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(G(F))
.
The first one is easy to compute and the second one is more intrinsic because it does not need to fix a Borel in advance.
The isomorphism
T_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(T(F)){\ \cong\ }G_{ad}(F)/\operatorname{Im}(G(F))
says that the two approaches are the same.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{\widehat{G}}(\phi)}

The image of

1.7.

In this section we briefly review certain basic desiderata of the local Langlands correspondence in a form proposed by Vogan

For

be the pure inner form of

.

-generic representation for each

(3)

where

is the character of

C_{\phi}^{*}=\{0\}
.
By Proposition
1.2
it follows that
C_{\phi}
is the open orbit in
V_{\lambda}
. This completes the proof that
\phi
is open if
\phi
is tempered.

Now suppose
\phi
is open and of Arthur type.
Then
\phi(w,x)=\psi(w,x,d_{w})
for a unique Arthur parameter
\psi
; recall that, by definition, the image of the restriction of
\psi
to
W_{F}
is bounded in
{\widehat{G}}
.
Note that
x_{\phi}=x_{\psi}
as above and also that
(x_{\psi},y_{\psi})\in\Lambda_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{sreg}}
, as above.
Again by Proposition
1.2
, since
\phi
is open it follows that
C_{\phi}^{*}=\{0\}
so
y_{\psi}=0
.
If follows that
\psi(1,1,f)=1
. Now consider
\sigma{\,:=\,}\psi^{\circ}|_{\SL_{2}^{\hskip-1.0pt\mathrm{Art}}({\mathbb{C}})}:\SL_{2}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\widehat{G}}
and note that
\sigma(f)=1
. Arguing as in
[
21
]
*Section 2, it follows from the Jacobson-Morozov theorem that
\sigma
is determined uniquely by the
\SL_{2}
-triple
\sigma(e)
,
\sigma(h)
and
\sigma(f)
. Since
\sigma(f)=1
, this triple is trivial, so
\sigma
is trivial.
It follows that
\phi(w,x)=\psi(w,x,y)
.
Since
\psi(w,1)=\psi(w,1,d_{w})
and since
\psi(w,1,d_{w})=\psi(w,1,1)
it follows that the image of the restriction of
\phi_{\psi}
to
W_{F}
in
{\widehat{G}}
is equal to the image of the restriction of
\psi
to
W_{F}
in
{\widehat{G}}
, which is bounded. This concludes the proof that if
\psi
is open and of Arthur type then
\phi
is tempered.
∎

It follows from Proposition
3.2
that tempered parameters are open.
We give an alternate proof of this fact, as well as the equivalent statement for discrete Langlands parameters in Appendix
A
. This alternate proof is a straightforward consequence of some connection established in
[
24
]
between discrete Langlands parameters and distinguished nilpotent elements, and basic theorems in the theory of distinguished nilpotent orbits.

Remark 3.3
.

Langlands parameters that are open need not be tempered, nor of Arthur type.
For example, consider the Langlands parameter
\phi_{s}:W^{\prime}_{F}\to{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}
for
G=\GL_{n}
defined by

\phi_{s}(w,x)={|w|}_{F}^{s}\otimes\Sym^{n-1}(x)

where
s
is an arbitrary complex number.
Then the infinitesimal parameter for
\phi_{s}
is

\lambda_{s}(w)=\diag({|w|}_{F}^{s+\frac{n-1}{2}},{|w|}_{F}^{s+\frac{n-3}{2}},\ldots,{|w|}_{F}^{s-\frac{n-3}{2}},{|w|}_{F}^{s-\frac{n-1}{2}})\in{\widehat{T}}.

The variety
V_{\lambda_{s}}
is independent of
s
; specifically, for every
s
, the variety
V_{\lambda_{s}}
for this infinitesimal parameter is
\mathbb{A}^{n-1}
, with
2^{n-1}
orbits under the action of
H_{\lambda_{s}}={\widehat{T}}
.
(This variety and the corresponding representations are studied in
[
14
]
.)
Moreover, for every
s
, the parameter
\phi_{s}
lies in the open orbit, hence is an open parameter.
However, for
s\neq 0
the Langlands parameter
\phi_{s}
is open but not tempered, nor of Arthur type; meanwhile, for
s=0
the Langlands parameter
\phi_{s}
is open and tempered (and thus of Arthur type).

Remark 3.4
.

It may be tempting to think, based on the remark above, that "open" is equivalent to "essentially tempered", which is to say, the twist of a parameter for a tempered representation of
G(F)
by the parameter for an unramified character of
G(F)
, but that is not true, as the example of the Langlands parameter for
\GL_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(F)

\left({|w|}_{F}^{s_{1}}\otimes\Sym^{n_{1}-1}(x)\right)\oplus\left({|w|}_{F}^{s_{2}}\otimes\Sym^{n_{2}-1}(x)\right)

shows, which for
s_{2}-s_{1}\neq\pm 1
is open and not essentially tempered.

3.3.
Open parameters and L-functions

We now give an alternate perspective on open parameters.
Let
\phi:W_{F}\times\SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})\to{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}
be a local Langlands parameter and let
\Ad:{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}\rightarrow\text{Aut}({\hat{\mathfrak{g}}})
be the adjoint representation of
{\hskip-2.0pt\,{}^{L}\hskip-1.0pt{G}}
on the Lie algebra of the dual group of
G
.
The corresponding local
L
-factor is

L(s,\phi,\Ad)=\det(I-q^{-s}\Ad(\lambda({\rm{Fr}}))|_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}^{I_{F}}_{N}})^{-1},

where
\lambda:=\lambda_{\phi}
(see (
2
)) is the infinitesimal parameter of
\phi
,
N=d\phi\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{N}=\ker(N)
and
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{N}^{I_{F}}
is the
I_{F}
-invariant subspace of
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{N}
. See
[
21
]
for more details about the definition of local
L
-factors.
We remark that the Frobenius in
[
21
]
is the inverse of the Frobenius in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

For any Langlands parameter
\phi
, the
L
-function
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
if and only if
\phi
is open.

Proof.

Write
C=C_{\phi}
in the following. Note that
N\in C
and
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
has a pole at
s=1
if and only if there exists a nonzero element
y\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}_{N}^{I_{F}}
such that
\Ad(\lambda({\rm{Fr}}))y=qy
.
If follows that
y\in V_{\lambda}^{*}
and
[N,y]=0
, where we take the Lie bracket in
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Thus,
(N,y)
lies in the conormal bundle
\Lambda_{C}
.
Equivalently,
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
if and only if
\{y\in V_{\lambda}^{*}{\ |\ }(N,y)\in\Lambda_{C}\}=\{0\}.

We first assume that
N=0
, i.e.,
\phi(w,x)=\lambda(w)
. Then
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
if and only if
V_{\lambda}^{*}=\{0\}
. The latter condition is equivalent to
V_{\lambda}=\{0\}
, in which case
\phi=\lambda
is trivially open in
V_{\lambda}
.

Next, we assume that
N\neq 0
. Since
H_{\lambda}.N=C
and
[~,~]
is invariant under the
H_{\lambda}
-action, it follows that
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
if and only if the projection
\mathrm{pr}_{2}:\Lambda_{C}\rightarrow V_{\lambda}^{*}
is zero, which implies
C^{*}=\{0\}
, hence
C
is open.
∎

4.
A genericity conjecture for ABV-packets

4.1.
Generic versus open

Recall that an irreducible representation
\pi
is said to be generic if it has a Whittaker model.
A Langlands parameter
\phi
is said to be generic if its L-packet
\Pi_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation; in this case we say the
L
-packet itself is generic.
It is possible for an L-packet to have more than one generic representation; a simple example is the unique L-packet for
\SL_{2}(F)
, as in
[
11
]
*Chapter 11.
It is possible for an L-packet to have exactly one one generic representation while all others are not; an example is the unique packet for
G_{2}(F)
with component group
S_{3}
, as in
[
9
]
*Section 1.1.
Of course, most L-packets have no generic representations.

Recall the following conjecture of Gross-Prasad and Rallis from
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6:
An
L
-packet
\Pi_{\phi}(G(F))
is generic if and only if
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
.
This conjecture has been verified in many cases in the literature; see
[
27
,
31
,
26
]
for some examples.
The most general case of
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 proved in the literature is
[
18
]
*Theorem B.2:
If
G
is a classical group (including
GL_{n}
), then
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 is true.
In fact, Gan-Ichino
[
18
]
proved
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 for any reductive group under certain hypothesis on local Langlands correspondence, see
[
18
]
*Section B.2, which are known to be true for classical groups (including the general linear groups).

As a consequence of Proposition
3.5
,
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 is equivalent to the following conjectural geometric characterization of generic
L
-packets.

Conjecture 4.1
.

A pure
L
-packet
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)
is generic if and only if
\phi
is an open parameter for
G
.

For the exceptional group
G_{2}
, one can also check directly that
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 and Conjecture
4.1
hold for unramified parameters
\phi
using
[
9
,
10
]
and for general
\phi
using the local Langlands correspondence for
G_{2}
in
[
4
,
19
]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

If
G
is a classical group (including
GL_{n}
), then Conjecture
4.1
is true:
\phi
is generic if and only if
\phi
is open.

Proof.

By
[
18
]
*Theorem B.2, the conjecture of Gross-Prasad and Rallis,
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 is true for classical groups. This means that a representation of a classical group is generic if and only if
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
. Proposition
3.5
says that
\phi
is open if and only if
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
.
∎

4.2.
Genericity conjecture for ABV-packets

Conjecture 4.3

(Genericity Conjecture for ABV-packets)
.

For any connected reductive algebraic group over
F
, an ABV-packet
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only if
\phi
is open, where
\phi
is a Langlands parameter for
G
.

Conjecture
4.3
is a generalization of
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 in view of Proposition
3.5
.
The new content of Conjecture
4.3
compared to
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 is that
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)
does not contain any generic representations if
C_{\phi}
is not open in
V_{\lambda_{\phi}}
.
Conjecture
4.3
is a generalization of
[
30
]
*Conjecture 1.2 if we admit Vogan’s conjecture on A-packets
[
11
]
*Conjecture 1, Section 8.3.
Recall that
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)\subset\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)
for general Langlands parameter
\phi
by Theorem
2.1
and
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)=\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)
if
\phi
is open, by Theorem
5.1
.

On the other hand, the following theorem shows that
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 is indeed equivalent to Conjecture
4.3
for quasi-split reductive groups.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
G
be a quasi-split reductive group over
F
and
\phi
be a local Langlands parameter of
G
.
We assume that the local Langlands correspondence holds for
G
which also satisfies the desiderata of Section
1.5
.
If
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 is true then
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only
\phi
is open.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be the infinitesimal parameter of
\phi
and let
C^{o}
be the open orbit of
V_{\lambda}
and let
\phi^{o}
be the open Langlands parameter.
It suffices to show that if
\phi
is not open, then
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)
cannot contain a generic representation.
By Proposition
3.5
,
[
20
]
*Conjecture 2.6 and Desiderata (
2
) of Section
1.5
, if
\pi
is a generic representation in
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\lambda}(G)
, there is a Whittaker datum
\mathfrak{w}
such that
J(\mathfrak{w})(\pi)=(C^{o},{\mathbbm{1}})
under the local Langlands correspondence.
By Lemma
4.5
, the only ABV-packet which contains
\pi
is
\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}_{\phi^{o}}(G)
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume Desiderata (
3
) of Section
1.5
.
Let
C^{o}
be the open orbit of
V_{\lambda}
.
If there exists a Whitaker normalization for which
J(\mathfrak{w})(\pi)=(C^{o},{\mathbbm{1}})
then in any normalization:
the only
L
-packet containing
\pi
is the one for
C^{o}
and the only ABV-packet containing
\pi
is the one for
C^{o}
.

Proof.

The first claim follows from Section
1.5
because under a different normalization we would have
J(\mathfrak{w}^{\prime})(\pi)=(C^{o},\varrho_{\mathfrak{w},\mathfrak{w}^{\prime}})
so that the associated
L
-packet remains that for
C^{o}
.
The second claim follows from Theorem
2.2
and the observation that the only ABV-packet containing
\iota_{\mathfrak{w}}(C^{o},{\mathbbm{1}})
where
C^{o}
is the open orbit, is the ABV-packet for the open orbit.
Indeed, because
V_{\lambda}
is smooth and
IC({{\mathbbm{1}}}_{C^{o}})={\mathbbm{1}}_{V_{\lambda}}[d]
we have that
\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{C}({\mathcal{I\hskip-1.0ptC}}({{\mathbbm{1}}}_{C^{o}}))\neq 0
if and only if
\operatorname{\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{C}({\mathcal{I\hskip-1.0ptC}}({{\mathbbm{1}}}_{C^{o}}))\neq 0
if and only if
{\mathsf{R}\hskip-0.5pt\Phi}_{f_{C}}\left({\mathbbm{1}}_{V}[d]\boxtimes{\mathbbm{1}}_{C^{*}}\right)|_{\Lambda^{\mathrm{gen}}_{C}}\neq 0
if and only if
C=C^{o}
.
∎

We remind the reader that a generic representation appearing in a pure L-packet
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)
necessarily lies in the L-packet for the quasi-split group
G
.

Remark 4.6
.

Assuming Desiderata (
2
) of Section
1.5
, for classical groups if
C^{o}
is open then
\iota_{\mathfrak{w}}(C^{o},{\mathbbm{1}})
is generic; see Corollary
4.2
and Proposition
3.5
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
G
be a quasi-split classical group over
F
or a pure inner form of such.
Then
\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}_{\phi}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only if
L(s,\phi,\Ad)
is regular at
s=1
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
18
]
*Theorem B.2, Proposition
3.5
and Theorem
4.4
directly.
∎

4.3.
Relation to a weak form of Vogan’s conjecture on A-packets

Note that Corollary
5.2
plus
[
11
]
*Conjecture 1 (a), Section 8.3 implies the enhanced Shahidi’s conjecture,
[
30
]
*Conjecture 1.2, which is a generalization of
[

]
*Conjecture 9.4.
But in fact, one only needs one direction of
[
11
]
*Conjecture 1 (a), Section 8.3 to get
[
30
]
*Conjecture 1.2, as we now show.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G
be a quasi-split classical group. Let
\psi
be an Arthur parameter.
If
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\psi}(G)\subset\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)
for every Arthur parameter
\psi
,
then
[
30
]
*Conjecture 1.2 is true:
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\psi}(G)
contains a generic representation if and only if
\psi
is tempered.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.2
, if
\psi
is tempered, then
\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\psi}(G)=\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)=\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)
, which contains a generic representation by

]
*Theorem B.2.

is not tempered, then

is not open, by Proposition

.
Thus
[

]
*Theorem B.2 says that

is not regular at

. Corollary

implies that
\Pi_{\phi_{\psi}}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)

.

5.
ABV-packets for open parameters

5.1.
ABV-packets are L-packets for open parameters

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
G
be a connected reductive algebraic group.
If
\phi
is an open parameter for
G
then the ABV-packet for
\phi
is the pure L-packet for
\phi
:

\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)=\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}_{\phi}(G).

Moreover, the function
\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{\phi}\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{w}}
defined by vanishing cycles coincides with the function
J(\mathfrak{w})
from the local Langlands correspondence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{A_{\phi}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}_{\phi}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Rep}(A_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J(\mathfrak{w})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{\phi}\circ\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{w}}}

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
, this diagram commutes, so it only remains to show the two equalities above: if
\phi
is open then
A_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}=A_{\phi}
and
\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)=\Pi_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}(G)
.

First, recall that
A_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}
is the equivariant fundamental group of
\Lambda^{\mathrm{gen}}_{C_{\phi}}
.
Since
\phi
is open,
C_{\phi}
is the open
H_{\lambda}
-orbit in
V_{\lambda}
. Thus,
C_{\phi}^{*}
is the closed orbit
\{0\}
in
V_{\lambda}^{*}
, by Proposition
1.2
.
Thus,
\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}=C_{\phi}\times C_{\phi}^{*}
.
Now observe that
\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}{\ \cong\ }C_{\phi}
as an
H_{\lambda}
-space.
Since this is a single
H_{\lambda}
-orbit, it follows that
\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}^{\mathrm{gen}}
.
(Note that, in this case, the inclusions
\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}^{\mathrm{gen}}\subseteq\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}^{\mathrm{reg}}\subseteq\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}
are all equalities.)
Thus,
\Lambda_{C_{\phi}}^{\mathrm{gen}}{\ \cong\ }C_{\phi}
.
Now it follows that
A^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}_{\phi}
, the equivariant fundamental group of
\Lambda^{\mathrm{gen}}_{C_{\phi}}
is
A_{\phi}
, the equivariant fundamental group of
C_{\phi}
:

A^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV}$}}}}_{\phi}=A_{\phi}.

Now suppose, for a contradiction, that
\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}_{\phi}(G)
contains a coronal representation; that is, suppose
\pi\in\Pi^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}_{\phi}(G)
and
\pi\not\in\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)
.
We have
\mathcal{P}_{\mathfrak{w}}(\pi)={\mathcal{I\hskip-1.0ptC}}(\underline{\rho}_{C_{\phi_{\pi}}})

and
\operatorname{\mathsf{N}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{E}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{v}\hskip-1.0pt\mathsf{s}}_{\phi}{\mathcal{I\hskip-1.0ptC}}(\underline{\rho}_{C_{\phi_{\pi}}})\neq 0
.
By
[
11
]
*, this implies
C_{\phi_{\pi}}>C_{\phi}
, with strict inequality since
\pi\not\in\Pi^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}_{\phi}(G)
. But since
\phi
is open,
C_{\phi_{\pi}}>C_{\phi}
is not possible. Thus,

\Pi_{\phi}^{{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{ABV,pure}$}}}}}(G)=\Pi_{\phi}^{\mbox{\raisebox{1.0pt}{\scalebox{0.5}{$\mathrm{pure}$}}}}(G).

This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Vogan’s conjecture for tempered parameters

As shown in
[
11
]
*Section 3.11, it follows from Arthur’s main local result
[
2

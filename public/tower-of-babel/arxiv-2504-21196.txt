Droms Theorems for twisted right-angled Artin groups
Simone Blumer, Islam Foniqi, Claudio Quadrelli

Abstract
We characterize twisted right-angled Artin groups whose finitely generated subgroups are also twisted right-angled Artin groups. Additionally, we give a classification of coherence within this class of groups in terms of the defining graph. Furthermore, we provide a solution to the isomorphism problem for a notable subclass of these groups.

20F36, 20F65.

Key words:
twisted right-angled Artin groups, mixed graph, Droms, coherence, rigid

Introduction

In geometric group theory, the class of
right-angled Artin groups
(abbreviated as
RAAGs
) plays a significant role; see
(
Charney, 2007
)
for a comprehensive survey. RAAGs, also known as
graph groups
, can be represented by simplicial graphs, where each RAAG is associated with a specific simplicial graph that encodes its defining relations.
A generalisation of these, known as
twisted right-angled Artin groups
(abbreviated as
T-RAAGs
), first appeared in
(
Pride, 1986
)
, and later in
(
Clancy and Ellis, 2010
)
- where the concept of a
twisted Artin group
was introduced. We also refer to T-RAAGs as
twisted graph groups
.
In spite of the rather elementary presentation (see below), T-RAAGs yield surprising variety and flexibility: they span from free groups to free abelian and meta-abelian groups and, unlike RAAGs, they may admit non-trivial torsion.
They have been the subject of several recent works, see, e.g.,
(
Foniqi, 2022
,
Foniqi, 2024b
,
Foniqi, 2024a
)
,
(
Antolín, Blufstein, and
Paris, 2025
)
,
(
Himeno and Teragaito, 2024
)
.

T-RAAGs are defined by presentations consisting of generators and relations: there are finitely many generators, and for each pair of distinct generators
a,b
, there is at most one relation between them. This relation is either a commutation
ab=ba
or a so-called
Klein relation

aba=b
. Such presentations can be conveniently encoded using
mixed graphs
, where vertices correspond to the generators, and edges represent the relations. An undirected edge between
a
and
b
indicates the commutation relation
ab=ba
, while a directed edge from
a
to
b
specifies the Klein relation
aba=b
; if two generators
a
and
b
are not connected by an edge, no relation is imposed between them (for the formal definition of a mixed graph see
Section

2.1
).
If
\Gamma
is a mixed graph, the associated T-RAAG is denoted by
T(\Gamma)
.

Two basic and significant examples of T-RAAGs are the fundamental groups of the torus and the Klein bottle: the torus group is
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=\langle a,b\,|\,ab=ba\rangle
, while the Klein bottle group is
K=\langle a,b\,|\,aba=b\rangle
.
The associated mixed graphs are, respectively,

\mathrm{P}_{2}=\penalty\
a
b\,\,,
\Xi_{2}=\penalty\
a
b\,\,,

so that
\mathbb{Z}^{2}=T(\mathrm{P}_{2})
and
K=T(\Xi_{2})
.
It is important to note that
K
is not a RAAG, as RAAGs are bi-orderable
(
Duchamp and Krob, 1992
)
, whereas
K
is not.

In the late ’80s, Carl Droms obtained several remarkable results on the structure of RAAGs.
The aim of this work is to prove the
‘twisted analogue’
of some of Droms’ results.

One of the most renowned of such results is the theorem which characterises those RAAGs whose finitely generated subgroups are again RAAGs.
These RAAGs are precisely those whose associated simplicial graph does not contain as induced subgraph any of the following:

\mathrm{P}_{4}=\penalty\
a
b
c
d
\,\,\,,
\mathrm{C}_{4}=\penalty\
a
b
c
d
,

see
(
Droms, 1987c
)
.
Simplicial graphs satisfying this property are called
Droms (simplicial) graphs
. It is worth remarking that these graphs, and their associated groups
L=A(P_{4})
and
A(C_{4})\cong F_{2}\times F_{2}
, are sometimes referred to as
poisonous
, as they frequently serve as obstructions to certain group-theoretic properties.

In order to state the
‘twisted analogue’
of the aforementioned result, we need the notion of
special mixed graph
.
Roughly speaking, a mixed graph
\Gamma
is
special
if the following holds: if a vertex
b
is the tip of a directed edge, then every edge joining the vertex
b
to another vertex is a directed edge
‘pointing’
at
b
.
In special mixed graphs, we call terminal vertices of directed edges
sinkholes
.
For example, consider the mixed graphs

\Gamma_{1}=\penalty\
a
b
c
d
,
\Gamma_{2}=\penalty\
a^{\prime}
b^{\prime}
c^{\prime}
d^{\prime}
.

Then
\Gamma_{1}
is not special; while
\Gamma_{2}
is special, and the vertex
b
is its only sinkhole.
We define the
‘mixed analogue’
of Droms simplicial graphs as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

A mixed graph
\Gamma
is said to be a
Droms mixed graph
if the following hold:

(i)

the underlying simplicial graph
\overline{\Gamma}
– i.e., the simplicial graph obtained
‘forgetting’
the direction of directed edges of
\Gamma
– is a Droms simplicial graph;

(ii)

\Gamma
is special;

(iii)

\Gamma
does not contain the special mixed graph

\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}}=\penalty\
a_{1}
b
a_{2}

as an induced subgraph.

For example, consider the two mixed graphs

\Gamma_{3}=\penalty\
a_{1}
b_{1}
a_{2}

,
\Gamma_{4}=\penalty\
a_{1}^{\prime}
b^{\prime}
a_{2}^{\prime}

.

They are both special, and do satisfy condition (i) of
Definition 1.1
, but condition (iii) is satisfied only by
\Gamma_{3}
, so this is a Droms mixed graph, while
\Gamma_{4}
is not.

As it happpens for RAAGs, Droms mixed graphs characterise those T-RAAGs whose finitely generated subgroups are all T-RAAGs.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph, and
G=T(\Gamma)
the associated T-RAAG. The following are equivalent:

(i)

every finitely generated subgroup of
G
is again a T-RAAG;

(ii)

\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph.

For example,
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the Klein bottle group
K
, and
T(\Gamma_{3})
satisfy the conditions of
Theorem 1.2
, while the T-RAAGs associated to the mixed graphs
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2},\Gamma_{4}
displayed above, do not.

It is worth underlining that every T-RAAG associated to a Droms mixed graph may be constructed recursively starting from free groups and performing free products and certain semi-direct products with free abelian normal factor (see
Proposition 2.12
), in analogy with RAAGs associated to Droms simplicial graphs.
For example,
T(\Gamma_{3})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes F_{2}
, where
F_{2}
is the free group generated by
a_{1},a_{2}
.

One may relax condition (i) of
Theorem 1.2
and ask which T-RAAGs have all finitely generated subgroups that are also finitely presented, specifically identifying which T-RAAGs are
coherent
.
In
(
Droms, 1987a
)
, Droms proved that a RAAG is coherent if, and only if, the associated simplicial graph is
chordal
, i.e., it does not contain cycles of length greater than 3 as induced subgraph.
We prove that the same occurs for T-RAAGs.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=T(\Gamma)
be a T-RAAG associated with the mixed graph
\Gamma
.
Then
G
is coherent if, and only if, the underlying simplicial graph
\overline{\Gamma}
is chordal.

Finally, we focus on isomorphisms of T-RAAGs.
In
(
Droms, 1987b
)
, Droms proved that simplicial graphs are
rigid
: namely, if two RAAGs are isomorphic, then the two associated simplicial graphs are equal.
This phenomenon does not occur for T-RAAGs.
For example, it is easy to see that the two mixed graphs

\Lambda_{1}=\penalty\
a_{1}
a_{2}
b
,
\Lambda_{2}=\penalty\
a_{1}^{\prime}
a^{\prime}_{2}
b^{\prime}
,

define T-RAAGs which are both isomorphic to the semidirect product
\mathbb{Z}\rtimes F_{2}
, where
F_{2}
is a free group of rank
2
(generated by
a_{1},a_{2}
, and by
a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime}
respectively), and the action of
F_{2}
on the normal factor is given by a non-trivial homomorphism
F_{2}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
.
Thus,
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
are not rigid.

To study rigidity of special mixed graphs we introduce the notion of
satellite
: if
\Gamma
is a special mixed graph, and
a
is a sinkhole of
\Gamma
, a satellite of
a
is a vertex
b
with distance 2 to
a
such that every vertex of
\Gamma
which is joined to
b
, is joined also to
a
.
For example, in the mixed graph
\Lambda_{1}
above the two sinkholes
a_{1},a_{2}
are satellite of each other, while in the mixed graph
\Lambda_{2}
the vertex
a_{2}^{\prime}
is the satellite of the sinkhole
a_{1}^{\prime}
.

The presence of satellites is an obstruction to rigidity.
Altogether, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
T(\Gamma)
be a T-RAAG, with associated mixed graph
\Gamma
. Assuming that
\Gamma
is a special mixed graph, one has:

(i)

If
\Gamma
is rigid then it has no satellites.

(ii)

If
\Gamma
has a sinkhole which is joined to every other vertex, then
\Gamma
is rigid.

(iii)

In case
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph,
\Gamma
is rigid if, and only if, it has not satellites.

For example, the Droms mixed graph
\Gamma_{3}
displayed above is not rigid by
Theorem 1.4
–(iii), while the special mixed graphs
\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{4}
are rigid by
Theorem 1.4
–(ii).
We suspect that the lack of satellites is also a sufficient condition to rigidity for special mixed graphs.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a special mixed graph.
If
\Gamma
has no satellites, then it is rigid.

2
Mixed Graphs

This section establishes the notation and foundational concepts used throughout the article.

Recall that a simplicial graph is a pair
\overline{\Gamma}=(V,E)
,
where
V
is a set whose elements are called vertices, and
E\subseteq\{\{x,y\}\>\mid\>x,y\in V,x\neq y\}
is a set of paired distinct
vertices, whose elements are called edges.

2.1
Definition of Mixed Graphs

A
mixed graph

\Gamma
consists of an underlying simplicial graph
\overline{\Gamma}=(V,E)
, a set of
directed edges

D\subseteq E
,
and two maps
o,t\colon D\to V
. We denote it as
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
.
For a directed edge
\mathbf{e}=\{x,y\}\in D
, the maps
o,t
satisfy
o(\mathbf{e}),t(\mathbf{e})\in\mathbf{e}
, and
o(\mathbf{e})\neq t(\mathbf{e})
: we call these vertices respectively the
origin
and the
terminus
of
\mathbf{e}
.
Moreover, if
\mathbf{e}=\{x,y\}\in E
is an undirected edge, i.e.
\{x,y\}\notin D
, we denote it as
[x,y]=[y,x]
and we draw it as a
‘plain’
edge, as in the mixed graph
\mathrm{P}_{2}
; while if
\{x,y\}\in D
, we denote it as
\mathbf{e}=[o(\mathbf{e}),t(\mathbf{e})\rangle
, and we draw it as an arrow pointing at the terminus, as in the mixed graph
\Xi_{2}
(observe that in this case, either
\mathbf{e}=[x,y\rangle
or
\mathbf{e}=[y,x\rangle
.

Induced subgraphs are defined in the obvious way. Moreover, we will use the following notions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph.

(a)

A vertex
v\in V
is called
negative
if there exists a directed edge
[u,v\rangle\in D
for some
u\in V
.

(b)

A vertex
v\in V
is called a
sinkhole
if, for every vertex
u\in V
with
\{u,v\}\in E
, it holds that
[u,v\rangle\in D
.

(c)

A
signature
of
\Gamma
is a map
\theta\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
satisfying the following:
\theta(x)=-1
for every negative vertex; and
\theta(y)=1
if
\{y,z\}\in E
for some
z\in V
,
z\neq y
, and
y
is not the terminus of any directed edge.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph, and
\theta\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
a signature.
If
x\in V
is not an isolated vertex, then the value
\theta(x)
is completely determined by the incidence structure of
\Gamma
; if
x\in V
is isolated – i.e.,
\{x,y\}\notin E
for any vertex
y\in V
–, instead,
\theta(x)
may be arbitrarily chosen.
Therefore, a mixed graph may be endowed with a unique signature if, and only if, it has no isolated vertices.

2.3
Droms mixed graphs

The disjoint union of two mixed graphs is defined in the most natural way: namely, given two mixed graphs

\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1},D_{1},o_{1},t_{1})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Gamma_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2},D_{2},o_{2},t_{2}),

the
disjoint union
of
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is the mixed graph
\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}=(V,E,D,o,t)

where
V=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
,
E=E_{1}\sqcup E_{2}
,
D=D_{1}\sqcup D_{2}
, and
o,t\colon D\to V
extend
o_{1},o_{2},t_{1},t_{2}
in the obvious way.

For mixed graphs one has also the following construction.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph, and let
\theta\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
be an orientation.
The
cone

\nabla_{\theta}(\Gamma)
of
\Gamma
induced by the orientation
\theta
is the mixed graph

\nabla(\Gamma)=\left(\tilde{V},\;\tilde{E},\;\tilde{D},\;\tilde{o},\;\tilde{t}\right),

which contains
\Gamma
as an induced subgraph, defined as follows:

(i)

\tilde{V}=V\sqcup\{w\}
, i.e., we adjoin a new vertex – the
(positive) tip of the cone
;

(ii)

the set of edges is

\tilde{E}=E\sqcup\left\{\{v,w\}\mid v\in V\right\},

meaning the tip
w
is joined by an edge to every vertex of
\Gamma
.

(iii)

the set of directed edges is

\tilde{D}=D\sqcup\left\{[w,v\rangle\mid\text{$v\in V$ is negative}\right\},

and the maps
\tilde{o},\tilde{t}\colon\tilde{E}\to\tilde{V}
extend
o
and
t
accordingly.

Also, we extend the signature
\theta\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
to a signature
\tilde{\theta}\colon\tilde{V}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
of
\tilde{\Gamma}
by putting
\tilde{\theta}(w)=1
.

Let

Droms mixed graphs, defined in the previous subsection, may be built recursively as shown by the following, see also
(

Proposition 2.12
.

A mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
is a Droms mixed graph if, and only if, the following holds: for every induced subgraph
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
, either

(a)

\Lambda
is the disjoint union of two non-empty proper subgraphs; or

(b)

\Lambda
is the cone
\nabla_{\theta_{0}}(\Lambda_{0})
for some induced subgraph
\Lambda_{0}=(V_{0},E_{0},D_{0},o|_{E_{0}},t|_{E_{0}})
and some signature
\theta_{0}\colon V_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
of
\Lambda_{0}
.

Proof.

We first show that any Droms mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
satisfies conditions (a)–(b), using induction on the number of vertices.
It is straightforward to verify that any mixed graph with at most two vertices is a Droms mixed graph and satisfies both conditions.

Assume now that
\Gamma
has at least three vertices.
Since the underlying simplicial graph
\bar{\Gamma}
is a Droms simplicial graph, by
(
Droms, 1987c
, Lemma, p. 520)
either
\Gamma
is disconnected – in which case it satisfies condition (a) – or there exists a vertex
c\in V
adjacent to every other vertex, that is,
\{c,v\}\in E
for all
v\in V\smallsetminus\{c\}
.

If there is such a vertex
c
, we claim that either
\Gamma
is a complete special mixed graph (cf.
Example 2.3
); or it is the cone
\nabla_{\theta_{0}}(\Gamma_{0})
, with tip
c
,
\Gamma_{0}
the induced subgraph on the vertex set
V_{0}=V\smallsetminus\{c\}
, and
\theta_{0}
a signature of
\Gamma_{0}
.

Suppose first that
[u,c\rangle\in D
for some
u\in V
.
Then
c
is a sinkhole, since
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph (and thus, special), and hence
[v,c\rangle\in D
for every
v\in V\smallsetminus\{c\}
.
Furthermore, for any pair of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2}\in V\smallsetminus\{c\}
, we must have
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E
, as otherwise the induced subgraph on
\{v_{1},v_{2},c\}
would be isomorphic to the mixed graph
\Lambda_{\mathrm{s}}
, which is prohibited by condition (iii) of
Definition 2.4

Moreover, if
[v_{1},v_{2}\rangle\in D
, then
v_{2}
would be a negative vertex that is not a sinkhole, since
[v_{2},c\rangle\in D
, contradicting the assumption that
\Gamma
is special.
Hence, in this case,
\Gamma
is a complete special mixed graph, which is a cone (cf.
Example 2.11
).

On the other hand, if
\{v,c\}\in E
and
[v,c\rangle\notin D
for every
v\in V\smallsetminus\{c\}
, consider the induced subgraph
\Gamma_{0}=(V_{0},E_{0},D_{0},o|_{D_{0}},t|_{D_{0}})
with vertex set
V_{0}=V\smallsetminus\{c\}
.
For every
v\in V_{0}
we set

\theta_{0}(v)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }[v,c]\in E,\\
-1&\text{if }[c,v\rangle\in D.\end{cases}

We claim that the map
\theta_{0}\colon V_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
is a signature, and
\Gamma=\nabla_{\theta_{0}}(\Gamma_{0})
, with positive tip
c
.

(1)

If
v\in V_{0}

3
Twisted right-angled Artin groups

All groups considered will be given by finite presentations of the form
G=\langle A\,|\,R\rangle
, where an element
g\in G
may be expressed as a word in
(A\cup A^{-1})^{\ast}
. If
X
is a subset of
G
, then
\langle X\rangle
denotes the subgroup of
G
generated by
X
.

For a finite simplicial graph
\overline{\Gamma}=(V,E)
, we denote the associated right-angled Artin group by
A(\Gamma)
, defined by the presentation:

A(\overline{\Gamma})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 0}"303A}\mkern-1.2mu=\langle\,V\mid uv=vu\text{ whenever }\{u,v\}\in E\,\rangle.

3.1
Definition and properties of T-RAAGs

When interpreting group presentations from mixed graphs, we associate
[a,b]
with the commutation relation
ab=ba
, and
[a,b\rangle
with the Klein relation
aba=b
. By slight abuse of notation, we write
[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
and
[a,b\rangle=abab^{-1}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph.
The
twisted right-angled Artin group
(T-RAAG) based on
\Gamma
is the group

T(\Gamma)=\langle\>V\>\mid\>ab=ba\text{ if }[a,b]\in E\smallsetminus D,\;\;aba=b\text{ if }[a,b\rangle\in D\>\rangle.

We and refer to
\Gamma
as the
defining graph
of
T(\Gamma)
.

If
D=\emptyset
, then
T(\Gamma)
reduces to the usual RAAG
A(\Gamma)
, where we identify the mixed graph
\Gamma
(without directed edges) with its underlying simplicial graph
\overline{\Gamma}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Given a mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
, let
\overline{\Gamma}
denote its underlying simplicial graph. The group
A(\overline{\Gamma})
is then referred to as the
underlying RAAG
of
T(\Gamma)
. A key theme is to explore connections between
T(\Gamma)
and
A(\overline{\Gamma})
.

The following properties of T-RAAGs follow from normal forms in T-RAAGs
(
Foniqi, 2024b
,
Antolín et al., 2025
)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph, and consider the associated T-RAAG
T(\Gamma)
.

(i)

If
\Delta=(V_{\Delta},E_{\Delta},D_{\Delta},o|_{D_{\Delta}},t|_{D_{\Delta}})
is an induced subgraph of
\Gamma
, then the morphism

i:T(\Delta)\longrightarrow T(\Gamma)\quad\text{induced by}\quad V_{\Delta}\hookrightarrow V,

is injective. In particular,
T(\Delta)
is a subgroup of
T(\Gamma)
.

(ii)

The map
q:A(\overline{\Gamma})\longrightarrow T(\Gamma)
defined by
v\mapsto v^{2}
is injective.

(iii)

Let
u\in V
, and set
\Gamma_{u}
to be the graph obtained by
\Gamma
, by changing the edges
[v,u\rangle
to
[v,u]
. The map
\varphi_{u}:T(\Gamma_{u})\longrightarrow T(\Gamma)
defined by
u\mapsto u^{2}
, and
v\mapsto v
for
v\neq u
, is injective.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\overline{\Gamma}
is not a Droms simplicial graph – i.e., it contains
\mathrm{P}_{4}
or
\mathrm{C}_{4}
as an induced subgraph –, then the T-RAAG
T(\Gamma)
contains the RAAG
A(\mathrm{P}_{4})
or
A(\mathrm{C}_{4})
, respectively.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph; consider the abelianisation
T(\Gamma)^{ab}
of the associated T-RAAG
T(\Gamma)
.
Then
T(\Gamma)^{ab}
is the direct product of a finitely generated free abelian group with a 2-elementary abelian group.
In particular,

T(\Gamma)^{ab}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{r}\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{s},

where
s=|o(D)|
– namely,
s
is the number of vertices which are origin of directed edges.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph, and let
\theta\colon V\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
be a signature.
Then
\theta
extends to a homomorphism of groups
T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
, as

[\theta(x),\theta(y)]=\theta(z)^{2}=1\qquad\text{for every }x,y,z\in T(\Gamma).

We call the induced homomorphism
T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
a signature of
T(\Gamma)
, and with an abuse of notation we denote it by
\theta
too.

3.2
Operations

Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be two mixed graphs, and let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
denote their disjoint union.
As it happens for RAAGs, the T-RAAG
T(\Gamma)
associated to
\Gamma
is the free product of the two T-RAAGs
T(\Gamma_{1})
and
T(\Gamma_{2})
.
If

\theta_{1}\colon T(\Gamma_{1})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\times}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta_{2}\colon T(\Gamma_{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\times}

are two signatures, then the universal property of free products yields a signature
\theta\colon T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
, which is the one extending
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
.

Now let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph, and let
\theta\colon T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
be a signature.
The T-RAAG
T(\nabla_{\theta}(\Gamma))
based on the cone of
\Gamma
with signature
\theta
decomposes as semi-direct product

T(\nabla_{\theta}(\Gamma))=\langle\>w\>\rangle\rtimes_{\theta}T(\Gamma),

where
w
is the tip of the cone,
\langle\>w\>\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}
, and one has

x^{-1}wx=w^{\theta(x)}\qquad\text{for every }x\in T(\Gamma).

T-RAAGs which may be constructed recursively performing free products and semi-direct products as above starting from T-RAAGs based on isolated vertices, endowed with some signatures, are called
elementary T-RAAGs
.

As a consequence of
Proposition 2.12
one has the following.

Corollary 3.6
.

A mixed graph
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph if, and only if, the associated T-RAAG
T(\Gamma)
is an elementary T-RAAG.

3.3
T-RAAGs and special mixed Graphs

T-RAAGs associated to special mixed graphs are characterised as follows.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
T(\Gamma)
be a T-RAAG.
The defining mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
is special if, and only if, there exists a complete special mixed graph
\Delta=(V_{\Delta},E_{\Delta},D_{\Delta},o_{\Delta},t_{\Delta})
with the same number of vertices, endowed with an epimorphism
T(\Gamma)\to T(\Delta)
.

Proof.

Suppose first that there is an epimorphism
\phi\colon T(\Gamma)\to T(\Delta)
with
\Delta
a complete special mixed graph with the same vertices as
\Gamma
.
By
Proposition 3.3
, for every induced subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of
\Gamma
,
\phi
restricts to an epimorphism
\phi|_{T(\Gamma^{\prime})}\colon T(\Gamma^{\prime})\to T(\Delta^{\prime})
, where
\Delta^{\prime}
is an induced subgraph of
\Delta
– hence,
\Delta^{\prime}
is again a complete special mixed graph, cf.
Example 2.6
– with the same vertices as
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
In particular, if
\Gamma^{\prime}
has three vertices, then
T(\Gamma^{\prime})
has epimorphic image
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
(when
\Delta^{\prime}
is a triangle without sinkholes), or
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
(when
\Delta^{\prime}
is a triangle with a sinkhole).
If
\Gamma^{\prime}
is like one of the mixed graphs with three vertices displayed in (
1
)–(
3
), then
T(\Gamma^{\prime})
cannot have such an epimorphic image.
Then
\Gamma
has no induced subgraphs like those displayed in (
1
)–(
3
), and thus
\Gamma
is special.

Conversely, assume that
\Gamma
is special.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
T(\Gamma)
does not decompose as a non-trivial free product – namely,
\Gamma
is connected.
In particular,
\Gamma
admits a unique signature
\theta\colon T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
(cf.
Remark 2.2
).
Now let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in V
be the negative vertices of
\Gamma
, and let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{s}\in V
be the positive vertices. Since
\Gamma
is special, the negative vertices are sinkholes, and thus they are pairwise disjoint.

If
\Gamma
has no sinkholes, then
\Gamma
has no directed edges and thus it is a simplicial graph,
T(\Gamma)\simeq A(\Gamma)
, and hence
T(\Gamma)^{ab}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{s}\simeq T(\Delta)
, where
\Delta
is the complete simplicial graph with
s
vertices.

If
\Gamma
has at least a sinkhole, let
N
be the normal subgroup of
T(\Gamma)
generated as a normal subgroup by the following elements:

[y_{i},y_{i^{\prime}}],\qquad y_{i}^{x_{1}}y_{i},\qquad x_{j}x_{1}^{2}x_{j},\qquad\text{with }1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq s,\>1<j\leq r.

Then the quotient
T(\Gamma)/N
is isomorphic to the T-RAAG
T(\Delta)
based on the complete mixed graph
\Delta=(V_{\Delta},E_{\Delta},D_{\Delta},o_{\Delta},t_{\Delta})
, with

\begin{split}V_{\Delta}&=\left\{\>x_{1},\>x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{1}x_{r},\>y_{1},\>\ldots,\>y_{s}\>\right\},\\
E_{\Delta}&=\left\{\>[y_{i},y_{i}^{\prime}],\>[y_{i},x_{1}x_{j}],\>[y_{i},x_{1}\rangle,\>[x_{1}x_{j},x_{y}\rangle\>\mid\>1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq s,\>1<j\leq r\>\right\}.\end{split}

The complete mixed graph
\Delta
is special as it has a unique negative vertex,
x_{1}
, which is a sinkhole.
∎

4
Coherence in T-RAAGs

A group is
coherent
if all of its finitely generated subgroups are finitely presented. This work provides an explicit characterization of the coherence of T-RAAGs, emphasizing their similarity to RAAGs
(
Droms, 1987a
)
.

A (simplicial) graph
\Gamma
is called
chordal
if all cycles of four or more vertices have a
chord
, which is an edge that is not part of the cycle but connects two vertices of the cycle. Droms provided the following simple characterisation of coherent RAAGs.

Theorem 4.1

(
Droms, 1987a
, Theorem 1
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial graph. Then
A(\Gamma)
is coherent if and only if
\Gamma
is chordal.

Lemma 4.2

(
Karrass and Solitar, 1970
, Theorem 8
)
.

Let
A,B
be groups and
C
a common subgroup. If
A
and
B
are coherent and all subgroups of
C
are finitely generated, then the group
G=A*_{C}B
is coherent.

For a finite mixed graph
\Gamma
we will say that
\Gamma
is chordal if
\overline{\Gamma}
is chordal.

Theorem 4.3
.

The T-RAAG
T(\Gamma)
is coherent if and only if
\Gamma
is chordal.

Proof.

If
\Gamma
is not chordal, then
A(\overline{\Gamma})
is not coherent. From
Proposition 3.3
we know that
A(\overline{\Gamma})
injects into
T(\Gamma)
, hence
T(\Gamma)
is not coherent.

On the other hand, assume
\Gamma
is chordal. In the case when
\Gamma
is complete, the group
T(\Gamma)
is virtually free abelian, and hence coherent as well. If
\Gamma
is not complete, then there are two proper subgraphs
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
with
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\cup\Gamma_{2}
and
\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}=C
, with
C
complete. Here one has a splitting:

T(\Gamma)=T(\Gamma_{1})*_{T(C)}T(\Gamma_{2}).

As
C
is complete,
T(C)
is virtually free abelian. Now the result follows from
Lemma 4.2
.
∎

5
When Subgroups Fail to Be T-RAAGs

In this section, we show that the excluded graphs correspond to T-RAAGs that contain finitely generated subgroups which are not themselves T-RAAGs.

5.1
Poisonous subgraphs

We begin with the cases of
P_{4}
and
C_{4}
. These cases follow the argument in
(
Droms, 1987c
)
. Start with a mixed graph
\Gamma
, and assume that
\overline{\Gamma}
contains
P_{4}
, or
C_{4}
. Then, by
Proposition 3.3
, our group
T(\Gamma)
contains
A(P_{4})
or
A(C_{4})
. So, it suffices to show that
A(P_{4})
and
A(C_{4})
contain finitely generated subgroups that are not T-RAAGs.

The group
A(C_{4})
is isomorphic to
F_{2}\times F_{2}
. This group is not coherent; that is, it contains a finitely generated subgroup
H
that is not finitely presented. Clearly, such a subgroup
H
cannot be a twisted graph group.

For the case of

A(P_{4})=\langle x,y,z,w\mid xy=yx,\ yz=zy,\ zw=wz\rangle,

consider the homomorphism
\varphi\colon A(P_{4})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle t\rangle
that maps each generator of
A(P_{4})
to
t
. As shown in
(
Droms, 1987c
)
, using the Reidemeister-Schreier method, the kernel of
\varphi
admits the presentation:

H=\langle a,b,c,d\mid ab=ba,\ bc=cb,\ bc^{2}d=dbc^{2}\rangle,

where
a,b,c,d
correspond to the elements
xy^{-1},y^{2},y^{-1}z,z^{-1}w
, respectively.
Droms (1987c)
showed that
H
is not a graph group. Here, we show that
H
is not a twisted graph group either.

Suppose for contradiction that
H
is a twisted graph group, say
H=T(\Gamma)
for some mixed graph
\Gamma
. Since
K^{ab}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{4}
, we know that
\Gamma
must have exactly
4
vertices, and no directed edges; this follows from
(
Foniqi, 2024b
, Subsection 5.1)
, which states:

T(\Gamma)^{ab}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{|V\Gamma-V_{o}|}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{|V_{o}|},

where
V_{o}
denotes the set of vertices of
\Gamma
that are the origin of a directed edge.

Consequently, we have shown that if
H
is a T-RAAG, it must actually be a RAAG. However,
Droms (1987c)
demonstrated that
H
cannot be a RAAG. Therefore, we have proved that
A(P_{4})
contains finitely generated subgroups that are not T-RAAGs.

5.2
Non-special mixed graphs

Next, we consider the graphs from
Section

2.2
and

.
In fact, it suffices to prove the result for the following three graphs, which we denote by

,

, and

, respectively:

\textstyle{y}

Indeed, all T-RAAGs based on the triangle graphs in (

Likewise, an analogous argument applies to the second line graph in (

Now, in the T-RAAG

, where

6
Elementary T-RAAGs

In this section we prove the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) of
Theorem 1.2
, using the recursive description of T-RAAGs based on Droms mixed graphs given by
Corollary 3.6
.

In fact, we prove the following slightly stronger result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Droms mixed graph, and let
\theta\colon T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
be a signature.
Then every finitely generated subgroup
H
of
T(\Gamma)
is again a T-RAAG, and moreover the restriction
\theta|_{H}\colon H\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
is a signature of the T-RAAG
H
.

First, we observe that for the
‘building block’
of elementary T-RAAGs – namely,
\mathbb{Z}
considered as the T-RAAG based on a single vertex –
Proposition 6.1
holds trivially.

Then, we show that the property prescribed by
Proposition 6.1
is preserved by the two operations involved in the construction of elementary T-RAAGs: free products, and semi-direct products.

6.1
Free products

If
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
, then
T(\Gamma)=T(\Gamma_{1})\ast T(\Gamma_{2})
, and the following result follows from the Kurosh Subgroup Theorem, and from the universal property of free products.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
be a disconnected mixed graph, and let
\theta_{i}\colon T(\Gamma_{i})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
be a signature, for
i=1,2
.
If every finitely generated subgroup
H_{i}
of
T(\Gamma_{i})
is a T-RAAG, and the restriction
\theta|_{H_{i}}\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
is a signature, then every finitely generated subgroup
H
of
T(\Gamma)
is a T-RAAG, and the signature
\theta\colon T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
induced by
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
yields a signature
\theta|_{H}\colon H\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
of
H
.

6.2
Semi-direct products

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a mixed graph, and let
\theta\colon T(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
be a signature. Suppose that for every finitely generated subgroup
H_{0}
of
T(\Gamma)
, the group
H_{0}
is a T-RAAG associated to some graph
\Lambda
, and that the restriction

\theta|_{H_{0}}\colon H_{0}=T(\Lambda)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\times}

is a signature. Then every finitely generated subgroup
H

7
Rigidity of T-RAAGs

Recall from the Introduction that a mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
is said to be rigid if the following occurs: if the associated T-RAAG

is isomorphic to a T-RAAG
T(\Gamma^{\prime})
, associated to some mixed graph
\Gamma^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime},D^{\prime},o^{\prime},t^{\prime})
, then
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic as mixed graphs.

Remark 7.1
.

By
(

)
, simplicial graphs are rigid, namely if
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}
are two simplicial graphs such that
T(\Gamma)\simeq T(\Gamma^{\prime})
, then
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic as simplicial graphs.

The goal of this section is to prove
Theorem 1.4
.

.
Since

\begin{split}D&=D(\Gamma_{0})\cup\{\>[w_{1},u\rangle,\ldots,[w_{r},u\rangle\>\mid\>u\in V(\Gamma_{0})\text{ and }\theta_{0}(u)=-1\>\},\\
D^{\prime}&=D(\Gamma_{0})\cup\{\>[w^{\prime}_{1},u\rangle,\ldots,[w^{\prime}_{r},u\rangle\>\mid\>u\in V(\Gamma_{0})\text{ and }\theta^{\prime}_{0}(u)=-1\>\},\end{split}

one has
D\not\simeq D^{\prime}
if, and only if,
\theta_{0}(u)\neq\theta_{0}^{\prime}(u)
for some
u\in V(\Gamma_{0})
.
This is possible only if
u
is an isolated vertex of
\Gamma_{0}
(cf.
Remark 2.2
).
So, pick such a vertex
u
. One has three cases.

(1)

If
\theta_{0}(u)=1
, then
u
is a positive vertex of
\Gamma
.
Since
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}
are positive vertices as well, and since we are assuming that
\Gamma
has at least a sinkhole, such a sinkhole – say,
v
– must be a vertex of
\Gamma_{0}
. Since the only vertices of
\Gamma
which are joined to
u
are
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}
, which are joined to the sinkhole
v
too,
u
is a satellite of
v
in
\Gamma
.

(2)

If
\theta_{0}(u)=-1
and
\theta_{0}(v)=-1
for some other vertex
v
of
\Gamma_{0}
, then
u
is a satellite of
v
in
\Gamma
, as all vertices that are joined to
u
– i.e.,
w_{1},\ldots,w_{r}
– are joined to
v
too.

(3)

If
\theta_{0}(u)=-1
and
\theta_{0}(v)=1
for all
v\in V(\Gamma_{0})
,
v\neq u
, then we claim that at least one of such positive vertices – say,
v_{0}
– is isolated in
\Gamma_{0}
, so that
v_{0}
is a satellite of
u
in
\Gamma
.
Indeed, if none of such vertices is isolated, then necessarily one has
\theta_{0}^{\prime}(v)=1=\theta_{0}^{\prime}(u)
for all
v\in V(\Gamma_{0})\smallsetminus\{u\}
(cf.
Remark 2.2
). Therefore,
\Gamma^{\prime}
has no directed edges – namely, it is a simplicial graph –, but then
\Gamma
is rigid by Droms (cf.
Remark 7.1
), a contradiction.

In every case, the Droms mixed graph
\Gamma
has a satellite, and this completes the proof.
∎

Example 7.9
.

The Droms mixed graph
\Gamma_{3}
displayed in
Example 2.13
is not rigid, as the vertex
a_{2}
is a satellite of the vertex
a_{1}
, and conversely; the T-RAAG
T(\Gamma_{3})
is isomorphic to the T-RAAG
T(\Gamma_{3}^{\prime})
based on the Droms mixed graph

\Gamma^{\prime}_{3}=\penalty\
a_{1}
b_{1}
a^{\prime}_{2}
b_{2}
,

which is not isomorphic to
\Gamma_{3}
.

The Droms graph
\Upsilon
displayed in
Example 2.14
is rigid, as it has no satellites: indeed, the only sinkhole is
a_{1}
, and the vertices with distance 2 to
a_{1}
are the positive vertices
a_{2},b_{2}
, which are joined together, so that none of them is a satellite of
a_{1}
.

Robust stability and preconditioning of Darcy–Forchheimer equations Thanks: Updated: August 10, 2026.
Rishi Das, Harsha Hutridurga, Amiya K. Pani, Ricardo Ruiz-Baier

Abstract
We derive parameter-robust quasi-optimal error estimates for mixed finite element methods for the nonlinear Darcy–Forchheimer equations with mixed boundary conditions. Using the framework of operator preconditioning, we also design efficient block preconditioners for the linearised system, that exhibit robustness with respect to the coefficients that modulate permeability and inertia of the system. The properties of the formulation (parameter and mesh-size independence of the convergence rates) are illustrated by means of several numerical examples.



keywords
A priori error analysis, mixed finite element methods, nonlinear flow in porous media, robust preconditioning.

1
Introduction

Scope

The Darcy–Forchheimer equations model fluid flow through porous media, when both viscous and inertial effects are significant. While Darcy’s law describes the linear flow proportional to the pressure gradient and is valid for slow, creeping flows, the Forchheimer extension introduces a nonlinear correction to account for inertial effects (manifesting itself as term depending as a power law on the velocity magnitude, taking into account kinematic energy loss) that arise at moderate flow velocities. These equations have broad applications in engineering and geosciences (high-velocity subsurface flows such as near injection or extraction wells in gas reservoirs and conglomerate-confined aquifers, contaminant transport in environmental engineering, and in filtration and catalytic bed reactors in chemical engineering)
[
26
,
29
]
, as well as in biological systems (e.g., blood perfusion in tissues, where nonlinearity in flow resistance can be significant)
[
19
,
21
]
.

Mixed finite element methods provide a natural framework for the numerical approximation of Darcy–Forchheimer flows, as they offer local mass conservation and accurate flux approximations. Their construction and analysis have been addressed through fixed-point and Newton methods
[
1
,
3
,
27
,
31
,
18
,
24
,
12
]
, often coupled with iterative solvers tailored to the nonlinear structure.

For Darcy and Forchheimer-type equations, which model creeping and inertial flows, respectively, the associated saddle-point systems exhibit strong sensitivity to variations in physical parameters such as permeability and the Forchheimer coefficient. Robust iterative solvers require preconditioners that maintain spectral equivalence independently of these parameters. For this, we follow the framework of operator preconditioning from
[
28
]
(see, also the earlier works
[
25
,
2
]
), which suggests linking the preconditioning of algebraic systems with their infinite-dimensional operator counterpart. This allows to construct preconditioners by approximating the Riesz maps of the continuous operators in appropriate function space norms (or duality maps in the case of Banach spaces). For the mixed formulation of the Darcy problem, this approach yields block-diagonal preconditioners involving sums of spaces of pressure, as developed in
[
5
]
(and also extended for Biot equations and other families of perturbed saddle-point problems in, e.g.,
[
8
,
10
,
23
]
). However, these works do not address in details the error analysis, which is also expected to maintain parameter robustness.

The objective of this work is twofold. First, to specify Céa-type estimates and convergence rates using the parameter-weighted norms, and secondly, to apply this approach to a (relatively simple) nonlinear extension to Darcy–Forchheimer flow equations. Obtaining uniform stability with respect to model parameters is of great interest for multiphysics coupling models since the variation of coefficients across sub-domains, or spatial scales, or different applications, can be extremely large. Also, coupling through interfaces with other flow regimes typically implies that we cannot simply rescale one equation, but certain compatibility of the parametrisation of both sub-models must be maintained.

While operator preconditioning techniques are available for many linear elliptic and saddle point problems, developing such robust solvers for general nonlinear saddle point problems exhibits significant challenges. Available techniques include multilevel, domain decomposition, operator preconditioning in the Hilbert context, field-split algorithms, and Sobolev gradient methods, for example (see, the review in
[
14
]
). In the nonlinear Forchheimer regime, linearisation techniques (e.g., Picard or Newton) give rise to a sequence of linearised problems, for which an operator preconditioning approach can be extended using variable-coefficient norm scalings following
[
33
]
. Inspired by
[
32
]
for multiphysics couplings, using the norms induced by the parameter-robust stability analysis, this paper proposes and analyse simple block diagonal (variable) operator preconditioners for the linearised Darcy–Forchheimer system.

In order to deal with intersections and sums of parameter-weighted function spaces, from
[
28
,
8
]
, it is possible to prove continuous and discrete inf-sup conditions by carefully combining auxiliary PDEs with different regularity and parameter weighting. This approach also requires a lifting map, and then, appropriate maps are constructed using the aforementioned auxiliary problems. Based on similar techniques, here we first discuss well-posedness of three different suitable PDEs and secondly, derive the inf-sup stability using a lifting map in the discrete case.

Outline

The remainder of the paper is organised in the following manner. Section
2
recalls the necessary notations, the boundary value problem and the derivation of the weak formulation using parameter-weighted norms. Section
3
addresses the well-posedness of the weak formulation robustly with respect to model parameters, following the abstract theory of Minty–Browder monotone operators. In Section
4
, we discuss the robust solvability of the discrete problem. In addition, we present quasi-optimal error analysis and derive precise convergence rates for conforming discretisations of the nonlinear model in Section
4.2
. Next, in Section
5
we discuss how a simple variable operator preconditioning can be used in this context of Banach spaces, and we conclude in Section
6
with a set of numerical tests illustrating the properties of the proposed schemes.

(2.2b)

\displaystyle=G(q)\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{Q},

Continuous robust solvability

First, we recall an abstract setting and its solvability using the Minty–Browder framework, which we shall use it for the well-posedness of (
2.3
). A proof can be found in, e.g.,
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
(see also
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Theorem 1
(Abstract setting for well-posedness).

Let
X,Y
be two reflexive Banach spaces,
\mathcal{A}:X\to X^{\prime}
a nonlinear map,
\mathcal{B}:X\to Y^{\prime}
a linear and bounded operator, and denote by

Z:=\{v\in X:\mathcal{B}(v)=0\}
the kernel of
\mathcal{B}
. Assume that

•

(Local Lipschitz continuity). There exist
\gamma>0
and
s\geq 2
such that

\|\mathcal{A}(u)-\mathcal{A}(v)\|_{X^{\prime}}\leq\gamma\|u-v\|_{X}+\gamma\|u-v\|_{X}(\|u\|_{X}+\|v\|_{X})^{s-2}\qquad\forall u,v\in X.

•

(Uniformly strong monotonicity on the kernel). For a fixed
w\in X
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that

\langle\mathcal{A}(u+w)-\mathcal{A}(v+w),u-v\rangle\geq\alpha\|u-v\|_{X}^{2}\qquad\forall u,v\in Z.

•

(Inf-sup stability). There exists
\beta>0
such that

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in X\\
v\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle\mathcal{B}(v),q\rangle}{\|v\|_{X}}\geq\beta\|q\|_{Y}\quad\forall q\in Y.

Then, for each
(f,g)\in X^{\prime}\times Y^{\prime}
there exists a unique
(u,p)\in X\times Y
such that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}(u),v\rangle+\langle\mathcal{B}(v),p\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle f,v\rangle\quad\forall v\in X,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{B}(u),q\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g,q\rangle\quad\forall q\in Y.

Moreover, there exists
C>0
, depending only on
\alpha,\gamma,\beta,\text{and }s
such that

{\|(u,p)\|_{X\times Y}\leq C\bigl(\mathcal{M}(f,g)+\{\mathcal{M}(f,g)\}^{s-1}\bigr)},

(3.1)

where

\mathcal{M}(f,g):=\|f\|_{X^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{Y^{\prime}}+\|g\|_{Y^{\prime}}^{s-1}+\|\mathcal{A}(0)\|_{X^{\prime}}
.

We now remark that Theorem
1
readily yields the unique solvability of (
2.3
) if we specify the spaces
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{H}^{3}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
and
\mathrm{Q}=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and this has been established in, e.g.,
[

]

Lemma 2
.

There exists an operator
\mathcal{S}_{c}
satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{c}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}^{\prime},\mathbf{V}),\quad\|\mathcal{S}_{c}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}^{\prime},\mathbf{V})}\ \text{is independent of }\kappa,\mathtt{F},\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}[\mathcal{S}_{c}(g)],\psi)=\langle g,\psi\rangle\quad\text{for all}\ g\in\mathrm{Q}^{\prime},\psi\in\mathrm{Q}.

(3.2)

Proof.

We proceed to define
\mathcal{S}_{c}
as a bounded linear operator from three different spaces, and then, invoke the duality relation between sum and intersection between Banach spaces (
2.1
) to conclude that
\mathcal{S}_{c}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathrm{Q}^{\prime},\mathbf{V})

For the existence of

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi=g\quad\text{in}\ \Omega,\quad\nabla\phi\cdot{\boldsymbol{n}}=0\quad\text{on}\ \Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}},\quad\phi=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{p}.

(3.3)

Then, it suffices to define
{\mathcal{S}}_{c}(g):=\nabla\phi
,
where
\nabla\phi\in\mathbf{L}^{r}(\Omega)
. This, in turn, shows

{\mathcal{S}_{c}}\in{\mathcal{L}}\bigl((\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega))^{\prime},{\mathbf{L}}^{r}(\Omega)\bigr)

and

Now, for

\displaystyle(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{z})+({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\boldsymbol{z},r)

\displaystyle=0\quad

\displaystyle\forall\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits},\Omega),

\displaystyle({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\boldsymbol{w},s)

\displaystyle=\langle g,s\rangle\quad

\displaystyle\forall s\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).

(3.4b)

Then, we note that

can also be defined as

, and therefore,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle:=\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,3,\Omega},

(3.6a)

\displaystyle:=\!\!\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3},\\

Theorem 3
(Parameter-robust well-posedness).

Assume that
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega),g\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega).
Then, there exists a unique pair of solutions
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}
to the problem (
2.3
). Furthermore, there is a positive constant
\tilde{C},
independent of the model parameters
\kappa,\mathtt{F}
, such that

\|(\boldsymbol{u},p)\|_{\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}}\leq\tilde{C}\max\bigl\{(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}),(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|^{2}_{0,\Omega})^{2}\bigr\}.

Proof.

In order to apply Theorem
1
, we define the operators
A:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
,
B:\mathbf{V}\to\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}
, and
C:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
as
\langle A\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}\rangle:=a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w})

\langle B\boldsymbol{v},p\rangle:=b(\boldsymbol{v},p)

\langle C(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle:=c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w})

A+C:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
. We proceed to verify the assumptions of Theorem
1
identifying

,

,
\mathcal{A}\equiv A+C
,
\mathcal{B}\equiv B
, and

Local Lipschitz continuity

For
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbf{V},

\displaystyle\langle\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{u}}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle-\langle\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{v}}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\kappa^{-1}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\boldsymbol{w}+\int_{\Omega}\mathtt{F}(|\boldsymbol{u}|\boldsymbol{u}-|\boldsymbol{v}|\boldsymbol{v})\cdot\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{0,\Omega}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{0,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\mathtt{F}^{\frac{2}{3}}\||\boldsymbol{u}|\boldsymbol{u}-|\boldsymbol{v}|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,\frac{3}{2},\Omega}.

Using
[
6

]
to bound the second term on the right-hand side of the above expression, we deduce that there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}
independent of

such that

\displaystyle\langle\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{u}}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle-\langle\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{v}}),\boldsymbol{w}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{0,\Omega}\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{0,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{0,3,\Omega}\tilde{C}\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{0,3,\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,3,\Omega})\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{0,3,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\tilde{C}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

and therefore, there holds

\|\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{u}})-\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{v}})\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\tilde{C}(\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}.

(3.7)

Uniformly strong monotonicity of
\{A+C\}(\bullet+\boldsymbol{w})

First we stress that for all
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{Z}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}:b(\boldsymbol{v},q)=0\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{Q}\},
we can choose

and therefore, we have the characterisation

\mathbf{Z}=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}:\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}=0\}

Now, for
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{Z}
and
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbf{V}
, it holds

\displaystyle\langle\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{w})-\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\kappa^{-1}|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}+\mathtt{F}\int_{\Omega}\left((\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{w})|\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{w}|-(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w})|\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}|\right)\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}).

\displaystyle\mathtt{F}\int_{\Omega}\left((\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{w})|\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{w}|-(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w})|\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}|\right)\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v})\geq\tilde{C}\,\mathtt{F}\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|^{3}_{0,3,\Omega}

Finally, from the above inequality, taking
\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}=\min\{1,\tilde{C}^{\frac{1}{3}}\}

\langle\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{u}+\boldsymbol{w})-\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\rangle\geq\alpha\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}^{2},

(3.8)

where, we have used that the

-seminorm is zero since

Inf-sup stability

From the existence of the map
\mathcal{S}_{c}
defined in Lemma
2
, it immediately follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{V}}\!\!\frac{({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\boldsymbol{v},p)}{\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{g\in\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}\frac{({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\mathcal{S}_{c}(g),p)}{\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(g)\|_{\mathbf{V}}}=\sup_{g\in\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle g,p\rangle}{\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(g)\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{S}_{c}\|^{-1}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}^{\prime},\mathbf{V})}\sup_{g\in\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle g,p\rangle}{\|g\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}}=\beta\|p\|_{\mathrm{Q}},

where
\beta=\|\mathcal{S}_{c}\|^{-1}_{\mathcal{L}(Q^{\prime},\mathbf{V})}
is independent of the parameters
\kappa,\mathtt{F}
, and hence, the inf-sup condition is satisfied.

An appeal to the Theorem
1
implies the existence of a unique velocity-pressure pair of solutions to the problem (
2.3
).

In order to complete the proof, it remains to show the continuous dependence of

\|(\boldsymbol{u},p)\|_{\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}}
on the data. From (
2.3a
), we arrive at
B^{*}(p)=F-\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{u})
, and the inf-sup stability of
B
shows

\displaystyle\|p\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\|B^{*}(p)\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\|\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}\}.

(3.9)

Also, the surjectivity of
B
implies that since
G\in\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}\text{ in }\eqref{eq:weak,2}
, there exists a unique
\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V}\setminus\mathbf{Z},
such that
\boldsymbol{u}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},
where
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{Z},

B(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})=G\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}.

Now, for the uniformly strong monotonicity of
A+C
(cf. (
3.8
)), we choose
\boldsymbol{u}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}},\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}
and
\boldsymbol{w}=-\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}
, and then for equation (
2.3a
), we set
\boldsymbol{v}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}
. Further, we use the local Lipschitz continuity of
A
, by setting
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}
in (
3.7
), to conclude that

\displaystyle\alpha\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\{A+C\}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})-\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{0}+\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}),\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\|\{A+C\}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}\}\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}^{2}\}\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\bigl\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

and hence,

\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigl\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}.

(3.10)

Finally, from (
3.10
), using the triangle inequality, we find that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigl\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1+\alpha}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}.

(3.11)

On the other hand, (
3.9
) and (
2.3a
) lead to

\displaystyle\|p\|_{\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\beta}\|B^{*}(p)\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\|\{A+C\}(\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}.

Then, we employ again the local Lipschitz continuity of
A+C
setting
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}
in (
3.7
), and appeal to the inequality (
3.11
). Thus we arrive at

\displaystyle\|p\|_{\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\bigl\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1+\alpha}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\bigl\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1+\alpha}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\alpha}}\bigl\{(1+\alpha)\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1+\alpha}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\bigl\{\|{F}\|_{\mathbf{V}^{\prime}}+\frac{1+\alpha}{\beta}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\|G\|_{\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}}^{2}\bigr\}^{2}.

This concludes the rest of the proof.

4
Mixed finite element method and robust solvability

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a regular simplicial mesh defined in a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, consisting of tetrahedral elements (or triangular elements in two dimensions)
K
with diameter
h_{K}
. We define the mesh size as
h:=\max\{h_{K}:K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}
. Given an integer
k\geq 0
and a generic element
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)
the space of polynomials defined locally in
K
with a degree at most
k
, and denote by
\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
its global counterpart

{\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}:=\left\{p\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega):p|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(K),\quad\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\}.

In addition, let
{\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
denote the
\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits},\Omega)
-discrete conforming subspace defined by the Raviart–Thomas elements of order
k
, i.e.,

{\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}:=\{\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits},\Omega):\boldsymbol{u}|_{K}=[\mathbb{P}_{k}(K)]^{d}\oplus\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{k}(K)\boldsymbol{x},\ \boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

where we denote by
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{k}(K)
the space of all polynomials over
K
of degree exactly equal to
k
and
{}^{\prime}\oplus^{\prime}
denotes the direct sum of spaces. With this we define the discrete weighted space for the velocity as follows

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}\supseteq\mathbf{V}_{h}:=\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}_{h}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathbf{L}_{h}^{3}(\Omega),

(4.1)

where
{\mathbf{L}}_{h}^{2}(\Omega),{\mathbf{H}}_{{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits},\Omega),
and
{\mathbf{L}}_{h}^{3}(\Omega)
correspond to functions in
{\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
equipped with the
{\mathbf{L}}^{2}(\Omega),{\mathbf{H}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits},\Omega),
and
\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)
-norms, respectively.

In turn, define the discrete gradient operator
\nabla_{h}:{\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\to{\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
by (c.f.,
[
2
, eqn. (3.1)]
)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{h}q_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=-(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}),\quad\forall q_{h}\in{\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},\text{ and }\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in{\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}.

(4.2)

Additionally, define the following broken Sobolev spaces

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}^{1}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\{p\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega):p|_{K}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(K),\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

\displaystyle{\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\{p\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega):p|_{K}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(K),\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

and whenever needed we add a subscript
\Gamma_{i}
denoting the part on the boundary where the value of the function is prescribed to zero. These spaces are equipped with broken Sobolev seminorms

|\bullet|_{{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
and

|\bullet|_{{1,\frac{3}{2},\mathcal{T}_{h}}}
,
defined as

\displaystyle{\mathrm{H}^{1}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\ni\zeta

\displaystyle\mapsto|\zeta|_{{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}}:=\biggl(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|{\nabla}\zeta|^{{2}}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\ni\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto|\xi|_{{1,\frac{3}{2},\mathcal{T}_{h}}}:=\biggl(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|{\nabla}\xi|^{\frac{3}{2}}\biggr)^{\frac{2}{3}},

respectively.

Furthermore, we define the following weighted space for pressure, which is a sum of the infinite-dimensional (but broken) spaces
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
:

{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

(4.3)

In this space we define a broken weighted norm for any
q\in{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
as follows

\|q\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}:=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q=q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}\\
(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\in\\
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma_{p}}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\times\mathrm{W}_{\Gamma_{p}}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\end{subarray}}\left(\|q_{1}\|_{0,\Omega}+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\,|q_{2}|_{1,\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}|q_{3}|_{1,\frac{3}{2},\mathcal{T}_{h}}\right).

Finally, we efine a discrete pressure space as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}_{h}:=\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{h,{\Gamma_{p}}}(\Omega)+\mathrm{L}_{h}^{2}(\Omega)+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega),

(4.4)

where
\mathrm{L}_{h}^{2}(\Omega),\mathrm{H}^{1}_{h}(\Omega),
and
\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{h}(\Omega)
are polynomial spaces having discrete functions from
{\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
, and equipped with the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm and the broken
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\,\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
seminorms, respectively.

Remark 4.1
.

We equip
\mathrm{Q}_{h}
with the
\|\bullet\|_{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}
norm, and observe that
\mathrm{Q}_{h}\subseteq\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, but
\mathrm{Q}_{h}
is not a subspace of
\mathrm{Q}
. This entails a non-conforming (in the pressure) scheme, requiring to define a discrete bilinear form associated with the discrete divergence operator

b_{h}:\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}

as well as a discrete linear functional
G_{h}:{\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\to\mathbb{R}
, as follows

b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h}):=\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\,q_{h},\quad G_{h}(q_{h}):=\int_{\Omega}g\,q_{h},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},q_{h}\in{\mathrm{Q}_{h}}.

Then, with the conforming velocity and non-conforming pressure FE spaces (
4.1
) and (
4.4
),
the mixed finite element formulation of (
2.2
) consists in finding
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
such that

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})}

\displaystyle=F(\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h},

(4.5a)

\displaystyle{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q_{h})}

\displaystyle={G_{h}(q_{h})}\quad\forall q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.

(4.5b)

Similarly to the continuous case, an analysis using
\mathbf{V}_{h}=(\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\|\bullet\|_{\mathbf{H}^{3}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)})
and
\mathrm{Q}_{h}=(\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\|\bullet\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)})
can be performed (using, e.g.,
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
31
, Theorem 3.5]
) however the coercivity on the kernel and inf-sup conditions (and therefore also the stability estimates) are not necessarily robust in
\kappa,\mathtt{F}
. To achieve such robustness we employ the weighted spaces (
4.1
) and (
4.4
).

4.1
Robust discrete solvability

First, we prove the discrete inf-sup condition, which is an essential ingredient of the proof of the parameter-robust unique solvability
Theorem, discussed in this subsection. We adapt the same framework as in the continuous case to the present scenario.

Lemma 4
.

There exists an operator
\mathcal{S}_{d}
satisfying the following properties:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{d}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{V}_{h}),\quad\|\mathcal{S}_{d}\|_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{V}_{h})}\ \text{is independent of }\kappa,\mathtt{F},h\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}[\mathcal{S}_{d}(g_{h})],\psi_{h})=\langle g_{h},\psi_{h}\rangle\quad\text{for all}\ g_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime},\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}.

(4.6)

Proof.

We first define a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{S}_{d}
from three different spaces, and then, invoke the duality relations between sum and intersection between Banach spaces (
2.1
) to conclude
\mathcal{S}_{d}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{V}_{h}).

For the existence of
{\mathcal{S}_{d}}\in{\mathcal{L}}\bigl((\mathrm{W}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}^{1,q}(\Omega))^{\prime},{\mathbf{L}}^{r}_{h}(\Omega)\bigr)

with
q\in\{2,\;\frac{3}{2}\}
, let

\phi_{h}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,r}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega)
for a given
g_{h}\in(\mathrm{W}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}^{1,q}(\Omega))^{\prime}
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1
be a unique solution of the following weak formulation

\langle\nabla_{h}\phi_{h},\nabla_{h}\psi_{h}\rangle=\langle g_{h},\psi_{h}\rangle\qquad\forall\psi_{h}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega),

(4.7)

owing to the Banach–Nečas–Babuška theorem for elliptic problems in Banach spaces
[
13
]
. Then, it suffices to define
\mathcal{S}_{d}(g_{h}):=\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}
,
where
\nabla_{h}\phi_{h}\in\mathbf{L}_{h}^{r}(\Omega)
. The continuous dependence on data coming from (
4.7
) also implies that
\|\mathcal{S}_{d}(g_{h})\|_{0,r;\Omega}\lesssim\|g_{h}\|
. This, in turn, shows

\mathcal{S}_{d}\in{\mathcal{L}}\bigl((\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{h,\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega))^{\prime},{\mathbf{L}}_{h}^{r}(\Omega)\bigr)
, for
q=2
and
q=\frac{3}{2}
corresponding to
r=2
and
3,
respectively.

Next, the existence and boundedness of
\mathcal{S}_{d}\in\mathcal{L}\bigl((\mathrm{L}^{2}_{h}(\Omega))^{\prime},\mathbf{H}_{h,\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\bigr)

is a direct consequence of the surjectivity of the divergence operator from
\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)
onto
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and standard properties of the Raviart–Thomas interpolation (even in the case of mixed boundary conditions)
[
7
,
16
]
.

Below, we prove the discrete inf-sup condition.

Lemma 5
(Discrete inf-sup condition).

There exists a positive constant
\beta_{d}
, independent of
h,\kappa
and
\mathtt{F}
such that

\displaystyle\|q_{h}\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\leq{\beta_{d}}\sup_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{(q_{h},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}.

Proof.

As a consequence of Lemma
4
, we can assert that

\displaystyle\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\!\!\frac{({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h})}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{g_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime}}\frac{({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}[\mathcal{S}_{d}(g_{h})],p_{h})}{\|\mathcal{S}_{d}(g_{h})\|_{\mathbf{V}}}=\sup_{g_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle g_{h},p_{h}\rangle}{\|\mathcal{S}_{d}(g_{h})\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{S}_{d}\|^{-1}_{\mathcal{L}(\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime},\mathbf{V}_{h})}\sup_{g_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}^{\prime}}\frac{\langle g_{h},p_{h}\rangle}{\|g_{h}\|_{{\mathrm{Q}}^{\prime}_{h}}}=\beta_{d}\|p_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},

which concludes the proof.

The following result is on robust well-posedness of the discrete mixed problem (
4.5
).

Theorem 6
(Parameter-robust unique solvability).

Consider the weighted spaces defined in (
4.1
) and (
4.4
) with the corresponding weighted norms,
and assume that
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega),\,g\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a unique pair
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
of solutions to the discrete problem (
4.5
). Furthermore, there exists a positive constant
\hat{C}
independent of
h,
and the model parameters
\kappa,\mathtt{F}
, such that

\|(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},q_{h})\|_{\mathbf{V}\times{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}}\leq\hat{C}\max\bigl\{\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|^{2}_{0,\Omega},(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|_{0,\Omega}+\|g\|^{2}_{0,\Omega})^{2}\bigr\}.

(4.8)

Proof.

The discrete well-posedness analysis will use the discrete version of Theorem
1
, detailed in
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
. The local Lipschitz continuity of the discrete counterpart of
\{A+C\}
follows similarly as in Theorem
3
, and the discrete inf-sup condition follows from Lemma
5
. Hence, to complete the rest of the proof, it is enough to prove the uniformly strong monotonicity.

Uniformly strong monotonicity of
\{a(\bullet,\bullet)+c(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)\}(\bullet+\boldsymbol{w}_{h})
.

We start with the observation that since
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathbf{V}_{h}\subset\mathrm{Q}_{h},
for all

Therefore, we characterise the discrete kernel as follows

and we remark that

, which directly follows from the characterisation of
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
. Now, for
\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-a(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})-

\displaystyle c(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h};\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\!\kappa^{-1}|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|^{2}\!+\mathtt{F}\int_{\Omega}\left((\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h})|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h}|-(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h})|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h}|\right)\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}).

\hat{C}>0

and
\mathtt{F},

\displaystyle\mathtt{F}\int_{\Omega}\left((\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h})|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h}|-(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h})|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h}|\right)\cdot(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

Finally, setting
\widetilde{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\min\{1,\hat{C}^{\frac{1}{3}}\},
there holds

\displaystyle a(

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}+\boldsymbol{w}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})

(4.9)

,
\kappa

\mathtt{F}

4.2
A priori

In this section, we estimate the error between the exact and approximate solutions, showing that the convergence is robust in the material parameters.

Below, we prove a theorem on

error estimate for the Darcy–Forchheimer model, which are robust with respect to
\kappa
,

, and
\mathtt{F}
.

Theorem 7
(Quasi-optimality).

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},p)
uniquely solve the continuous model (
2.3
), and let the pair

(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},p_{h})
be the solution of the discrete model (
4.5
). Then, the following quasi-optimal error estimates for the velocity and pressure hold

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\leq{C_{c}}\bigl(\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h})+

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(p,{\mathrm{Q}}_{h})\bigr),

(4.10)

where
{C_{c}}
is independent of
h,\kappa
and
\mathtt{F}
.

Proof.

Now, define

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}:b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q_{h})=G(q_{h})\quad\forall q_{h}\in{\mathrm{Q}}_{h}\}.

(4.11)

Note that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}
and
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
for all
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}
.
From triangle inequality, an application of uniformly strong monotonicity of
{a(\bullet,\bullet)+c(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)}
to the pair
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{0})\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}\times\mathbf{Z}_{h}
, and choosing
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}
in (
4.1
), we arrive at

\widetilde{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{V}}\leq{a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h})-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})}.

Further, adding and subtracting
{a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})}
to the right-hand side of the above inequality, the following bound is obtained

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle\quad-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle\quad-a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}).

(4.12)

Next, from (
4.5a
), we observe that

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle=b_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},p-p_{h}).

(4.13)

Then, combining (
4.2
), (
4.2
), the local Lipschitz continuity of
{a(\bullet,\bullet)+c(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)}
, and the boundedness of
{b_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\|p-p_{h}\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}+(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}}.

Finally, a use of the triangle inequality yields the estimate for the velocity error as

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}}\|p-p_{h}\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}}((1+\widetilde{\alpha})+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{g}(\mathrm{dist}(p,{\mathrm{Q}}_{h})+\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h})),

(4.14)

where
C_{g}:=\max\{\frac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}},\frac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}}(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{d}})((1+\widetilde{\alpha})+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\}
, which is obtained by using that
\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g})\leq(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{d}})\,\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h})
(see for instance,
[
16
, Theorem 2.6]
).

Now, for the pressure estimate, we use the inf-sup stability of
b_{h}(\bullet,\bullet).
Let
p_{h}^{*}\in{\mathrm{Q}}_{h}

be an arbitrary element. Then, there holds

\displaystyle\beta_{d}\|p_{h}-p_{h}^{*}\|_{{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h}-p_{h}^{*})}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p-p_{h}^{*})+b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p_{h}-p)}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\boldsymbol{0}\neq\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}}\frac{b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},p-p_{h}^{*})+{a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u},p-p_{h})}}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|p-p_{h}^{*}\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}+(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

which upon using the triangle inequality, shows the following estimate on the pressure

\displaystyle\|p-p_{h}\|_{{\widehat{{\mathrm{Q}}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{g}(\mathrm{dist}(p,{{\mathrm{Q}}_{h}})+\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h})),

(4.15)

where
\widetilde{C}_{g}:=\max\{(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{d}}+C_{g}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})),C_{g}^{\frac{1}{2}}(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\}
. This concludes the proof.

Additionally, if
\mathbf{Z}_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{Z},
then, we obtain the following pressure-independent estimate (cf.
[
16
, Theorem B.2]
) for the velocity.

Corollary 8
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
7
, there holds for any
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq C_{D}\,\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h}),

(4.16)

where
C_{D}:=\frac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}}((1+\widetilde{\alpha})+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{d}})>0
is
independent of
h,\kappa,
and
\mathtt{F}
.

Proof.

From the characterisation of
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
, there holds
b_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},q-q_{h})=0
for all
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
.
Then, from (
4.5b
), it is evident that
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}
. Now, let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}
be an arbitrary, but fixed element in
\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}
, then
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}
. A use of the triangle inequality shows

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}}.

Apply the uniformly strong monotonicity of
a(\bullet,\bullet)+c(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)
to the pair
(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{0})\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}\times\mathbf{Z}_{h}
and also take
\boldsymbol{w}_{h}=\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}
in (
4.1
) to arrive at

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle=a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}+\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle\qquad-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

(4.17)

\displaystyle=a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h};\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}).

Observe that, by adding and subtracting
a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})
on the right-hand side of (
4.2
), and using (
2.3a
) and (
4.5a
), as well as the fact that
b_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},q_{h})=0
for all
q_{h}\in{\mathrm{Q}}_{h},
we deduce the following:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq b_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},p-p_{h})+a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle\qquad-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle=a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})+c(\boldsymbol{u};\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})-c(\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g};\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g},\boldsymbol{u}_{h}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}},

where we have used that
\mathbf{Z}_{h}\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
in the second step, and the local Lipschitz property of
a(\bullet,\bullet)+c(\bullet;\bullet,\bullet)
in the third step.
Finally, a use of the triangle inequality yields

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\frac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}}((1+\widetilde{\alpha})+\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{u}_{h}^{g}\|_{\mathbf{V}})\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g}).

Recalling from
[
16
, Theorem 2.6]
that
\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{Z}_{h}^{g})\leq(1+\frac{1}{\beta_{d}})\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h})
,where
\beta_{d}
is the discrete inf-sup constant, we achieve the desired pressure-robustness.

Let us remark that by just considering the spaces
\mathbf{H}^{3}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits},\Omega)
for the velocity and
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
for the pressure and following the approach of
[
31
]
one can obtain the pressure and velocity error estimates separately, but they are not necessarily robust with respect to the parameters
\kappa,\mathtt{F}
.

In order to provide a theoretical rate of convergence for the mixed FEM (
4.5
), we recall the approximation properties of the subspaces involved.

({\textbf{AP}}^{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})
. For the Fortin operator
\Pi_{h}:\mathbf{W}^{m,r}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}^{l}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\to\mathbb{RT}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\,0\leq m\leq l,\,1\leq l\leq k+1
(see
[
17
,
7
,
31
]
),
there holds:

\displaystyle\star\|\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}\|_{m,r,\Omega}\leq\acute{C}h^{l-m}\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_{l,r,\Omega}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in{\mathbf{W}}^{m,r}(\Omega),\,0\leq m\leq l,\,1\leq l\leq k+1,

\displaystyle\star\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\Pi_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\acute{C}h^{l}\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}\|_{l,\Omega}\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbf{H}^{l}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega),\,\,0\leq l\leq k+1,

where
\acute{C}>0
is independent of
h.

({\textbf{AP}}^{p}_{h})
. Let
P_{h}:\mathrm{W}^{l,r}(K)\to\mathbb{P}_{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\,0\leq m\leq l,\,0\leq l\leq k+1
, be the orthogonal
\mathrm{L}^{2}
projection. Then, there exists a positive constant
{C}_{\sharp}>0,
independent of
h,
satisfying (see,
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle\star|P_{h}q-q|_{m,r,{K}}\leq{C}_{\sharp}h^{l-m}|p|_{l,r,{K}}\quad\forall q\in\mathrm{W}^{l,r}({K}),{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}},0\leq m\leq l,\,0\leq l\leq k+1.

To conclude this section, the following theorem provides the theoretical rate of convergence of the mixed finite element Galerkin scheme (
2.3
), under suitable regularity assumptions on the exact solution.

Theorem 9
(Convergence rates).

Let
(\boldsymbol{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}\times\mathrm{Q}
be the solution of the continuous problem (
2.3
) with the following regularity conditions:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}\in\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{l+1}(\Omega)\cap

\displaystyle\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathbf{W}^{l+1,3}(\Omega),\quad\qquad\quad\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}\in{\mathrm{H}}^{l+1}(\Omega),

and

\displaystyle p\in\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{H}^{l+2}(\Omega)\cap\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{W}^{l+2,\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega),

where
0\leq l\leq k+1
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{\flat}>0
, independent of
h,\kappa
, and
\mathtt{F}
, such that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\flat}h^{l+1}\bigl\{\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,\Omega}+\|{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,3,\Omega}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\ +C_{\flat}h^{l+1}\{|p|^{2}_{l+1,\Omega}+\kappa|p|^{2}_{l+2,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{2}{3}}|p|_{l+2,\frac{3}{2},\Omega}\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from the quasi-optimality estimate (
4.10
) and the above approximation properties, in the following manner

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}

\displaystyle\leq{C_{c}}\bigl(\mathrm{dist}(\boldsymbol{u},\mathbf{V}_{h})+\mathrm{dist}(p,\mathrm{Q}_{h})\bigr)

\displaystyle{\leq{\widetilde{C}_{c}}\bigl(\|\boldsymbol{u}-{\widetilde{\Pi}_{h}}(\boldsymbol{u})\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|p-{\widetilde{P}_{h}}(p)\|_{{\widehat{\mathrm{Q}}(\mathcal{T}_{h})}}\bigr)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\flat}h^{l+1}\bigl\{\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,\Omega}+\|{\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits}\,\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{\frac{1}{3}}\|\boldsymbol{u}\|_{l+1,3,\Omega}\bigr\}

\displaystyle\ +C_{\flat}h^{l+1}\{|p|^{2}_{l+1,\Omega}+\kappa|p|^{2}_{l+2,\Omega}+\mathtt{F}^{-\frac{2}{3}}|p|_{l+2,\frac{3}{2},\Omega}\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

where,
\widetilde{\Pi}_{h},\widetilde{P}_{h}
are scaled (with suitable constants independent of
h,\kappa,\mathtt{F}
) counterparts of
\Pi_{h}
and
P_{h}
, and
C_{\flat}=\widetilde{C}_{c}\max\{\acute{C},C_{\sharp}\}
. This completes the rest of the proof.

5
Preconditioning for the linearised Darcy–Forchheimer equations

In this section, we define a variable operator preconditioner for the Newton linearisation of (
2.3
). Using the notation from Section 3, we first rewrite such system in operator form as

\begin{pmatrix}A+C&B^{*}\\
B&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{u}\\
p\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}F\\
G\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{in $\mathbf{V}^{\prime}\times\mathrm{Q}^{\prime}$}.

(5.1)

Let us denote by
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{u})
the Gâteaux derivative of the nonlinear operator
C
, i.e.,
|\boldsymbol{u}|^{r-2}\boldsymbol{u}
at the state
\boldsymbol{u}
in the direction of
\delta\boldsymbol{u}
. In addition, consider now the Newton–Raphson linearisation of (
5.1
), which starting from an initial guess
\boldsymbol{u}^{0},p^{0}
, and iterating on
m=1,\ldots
until convergence, seeks velocity and pressure increments
(\delta\boldsymbol{u},\delta p)
in a new space

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{V}}\times\underline{\mathrm{Q}}

\displaystyle:=\bigl[\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)\cap\{\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\}^{\frac{1}{3}}\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad\times\bigl[\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)+\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{H}_{\Gamma_{p}}^{1}(\Omega)+\{\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}\}^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathrm{W}_{\Gamma_{p}}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)\bigr],

(5.2)

such that

\begin{pmatrix}\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{I}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})\mathcal{I}&B^{*}\\
B&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\delta\boldsymbol{u}\\
\delta p\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}F-\{A+C\}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})-B^{*}({p}^{m})\\
G-B({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{in $\underline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}\times\underline{\mathrm{Q}}^{\prime}$}.

(5.3)

Here,
\mathcal{I}
denotes the identity operator at the continuous level, but for the discrete case it stands for the corresponding mass matrix (either for velocity or pressure). Further, we note that, in weak form, the action of the operator
\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})\mathcal{I}:\underline{\mathbf{V}}\to\underline{\mathbf{V}}^{\prime}
reads

\langle\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})(\delta\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\mathtt{F}|\boldsymbol{u}^{m}|^{r-2}\delta\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}+\int_{\Omega}\mathtt{F}(r-2)|\boldsymbol{u}^{m}|^{r-4}(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\cdot\delta\boldsymbol{u})(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}).

Then, update the solution as
(\boldsymbol{u}^{m+1},p^{m+1})=(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}+\delta\boldsymbol{u},p^{m}+\delta p)
.
Next Lemma is on the solvability of the system (
5.3
).

Lemma 10
.

Assume that for any
\boldsymbol{w}\in\underline{\mathbf{V}},
the function
\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{w})
is in
\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and that
\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{w})(\boldsymbol{x})\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Then, the problem (
5.3
) has a unique solution in
\underline{\mathbf{V}}\times\underline{\mathrm{Q}}
.

Proof.

We use the classical Babuška–Brezzi theory for saddle-point problems.
The bilinear form
\tilde{a}:\underline{\mathbf{V}}\times\underline{\mathbf{V}}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

\tilde{a}(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}):=a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})+\langle\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{u}}^{m})(\boldsymbol{u}),\boldsymbol{v}\rangle,

is bounded in
\underline{\mathbf{V}}
using the Hölder’s inequality and coercive in
\underline{\mathbf{Z}}=\mathrm{ker}B
; all with constants independent of
\kappa
,
\mathtt{F}
, and
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{u}^{m}).
Finally, the boundedness and inf-sup condition on
B
have been shown in the proof of Theorem
3
for a different weight. In this case, we only need to alter the scaling, which completes the rest of the proof.

The spaces defined in (
5
) induce a duality map that can be used for preconditioning the system (
5.3
), which is discussed below.

Theorem 11
(Local preconditioner for the tangent system).

Let

(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}},\hat{p})\in\underline{\mathbf{V}}\times\underline{\mathrm{Q}}
be the state around which the Newton–Raphson linearisation is performed.
Then the following block diagonal operator

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}:=(\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{I}+\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}{\mathcal{I}}-\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)^{-1}&0\\
0&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{Q}:=\mathcal{I}^{-1}+{\bigl(-\kappa\Delta\bigr)^{-1}-\bigl([\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta_{\frac{3}{2}}}\bigr)^{-1}}\end{pmatrix},

(5.4)

is a parameter-robust preconditioner for (
5.3
), in the sense that the condition numbers of the preconditioned matrix are uniformly bounded in
\kappa
and
\mathtt{F}
. Here, making abuse of notation, by
[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta_{\frac{3}{2}}}
we denote the (singular) weighted p-Laplace operator associated with the space
\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega)
, defined as

\langle[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}\Delta_{\frac{3}{2}}\phi,\psi\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}|\nabla\phi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\psi,\qquad\forall\psi\in\mathrm{W}^{1,3}(\Omega).

Proof.

Preconditioners for the upper diagonal block in system (
5.3
) can be directly constructed following the approach of
[
5
, Equation 7]
. This straightforwardly leads to
\mathcal{P}
in (
5.4
). We now focus on showing that
\mathcal{Q}
is a preconditioner for the pressure block. Observe that the norm for the space
\underline{\mathrm{Q}}
in (
5
) can be written as

\displaystyle\|q\|^{2}_{\underline{\mathrm{Q}}}:=\inf_{\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega)}\bigl\{\|q-\varphi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\kappa\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+{[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}}\|{\nabla}\varphi\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{0,\frac{3}{2},\Omega}\bigr\}.

(5.5)

Note that the above infimum is attained by a
\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega)
(therefore also in
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}}_{\Gamma_{p}}(\Omega)
) that satisfies

\displaystyle(\mathcal{I}-\kappa\Delta-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}}})\varphi

\displaystyle=q\quad\text{in }\Omega,

(5.6a)

\displaystyle(\kappa\nabla\varphi+[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}|\nabla\varphi|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\varphi)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}},

(5.6b)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_{p}.

(5.6c)

The unique solvability of (
5.6
) follows from the Minty–Browder framework for monotone operators, stressing that
q
is from a suitable dual space (following from Sobolev embedding). Further details are omitted. Using (
5.6
), we obtain the following

\displaystyle\|q\|^{2}_{\underline{\mathrm{Q}}}

\displaystyle=\|q-\varphi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\kappa\|\nabla\varphi\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}\|{\nabla}\varphi\|^{\frac{3}{2}}_{0,\frac{3}{2},\Omega}

\displaystyle=\langle-\kappa\Delta\varphi-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}}\varphi,-\kappa\Delta\varphi-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}}\varphi\rangle-\kappa\langle\Delta\varphi,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\langle[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}}\varphi,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(-\kappa\Delta-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}})\varphi,(\mathcal{I}-\kappa\Delta-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}})\varphi\rangle.

Next, we employ again (
5.6
)
to conclude that

\displaystyle\|q\|^{2}_{\underline{\mathrm{Q}}}=\langle(-\kappa\Delta-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}})(\mathcal{I}-\kappa\Delta-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}})^{-1}q,q\rangle.

(5.7)

Then, using the duality map we readily define the canonical preconditioner
\mathcal{Q}:\underline{\mathrm{Q}}^{\prime}\to\underline{\mathrm{Q}}
associated with (
5.7
) by

\mathcal{Q}=(\mathcal{I}-\kappa\Delta-[\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}{\Delta}_{\frac{3}{2}})^{-1}.

Therefore, proceeding similarly as in
[
28
, Section 3]
, we can assert that (
5.4
) is a
(\kappa,\mathtt{F})
-robust block diagonal preconditioner for system (
5.3
).
This concludes the proof.

Let us stress that it is also possible to define a simpler preconditioner (motivated by
[
5
]
) for the linearised problem (
5.3
) by using a Schur complement approach (following, e.g.,
[

,

\underline{\underline{\mathbf{V}}}\times\underline{\underline{\mathrm{Q}}}:=(\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}))[\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits,\Omega)]\times(\kappa^{-1}+\mathtt{F}\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}))^{-1}\,\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),

where again

is the state around which the Newton–Raphson linearisation is performed.
This space is associated with the block diagonal Riesz map

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{P}:=\bigl([\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{I}+\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]\,({\mathcal{I}}-\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits)\bigr)^{-1}&0\\
0&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{Q}:=([\kappa^{-1}\mathcal{I}+\mathtt{F}{\mathcal{H}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})}]^{-1}\,\mathcal{I})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

(5.8)

The parameter-robustness for this preconditioner can be easily inferred from a scaling argument as in

. Nevertheless this preconditioner might not be of much interest in the case of coupling with other multiphysics effects. This has been discussed in length in

]
.

\displaystyle\langle-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\kappa(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla p_{h}),q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(\kappa(\boldsymbol{x})\nabla p_{h},\nabla q_{h})_{K}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Gamma_{p}}}\frac{1}{h_{F}}(\kappa(\boldsymbol{x})[\![p_{h}]\!],[\![q_{h}]\!])_{F},

\Gamma_{p}

[\![\bullet]\!]
represents the jump operator across a facet (and adopt the convention that it reduces to the identity if the face lies on the boundary). In turn, for the space

\displaystyle\langle-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\mathtt{F}^{-1}|\nabla p_{h}|^{s-2}\nabla p_{h}),q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Gamma_{p}}}\mathtt{F}^{-1}h_{F}^{1-s}(|[\![p_{h}]\!]|^{s-2}[\![p_{h}]\!],[\![q_{h}]\!])_{F},

Mikami-Weinstein type theorem for cosymplectic groupoid actions
Shuhei Yonehara

Abstract
. The Mikami-Weinstein theorem is a generalization of the classical Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer symplectic reduction theorem to the case of symplectic groupoid actions. In this paper, we introduce the notion of a cosymplectic groupoid action on a cosymplectic manifold and prove a theorem which is a natural analogue of the Mikami-Weinstein theorem.

1.
Introduction

Since the pioneering work of Marsden-Weinstein and Meyer
[
MW74
,
Mey73
]
, many types of reduction theorems have been studied for various geometric structures on manifolds. Albert
[
Alb89
]
studied Hamiltonian actions on cosymplectic manifolds, which are the odd-dimensional analogue of symplectic manifolds (see
[
CMDNY13
]
for more details about cosymplectic manifolds) and proved a reduction theorem. On the other hand, Mikami-Weinstein
[
MW88
]
generalized the Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer theorem to symplectic groupoid actions, which extends the notion of a Hamiltonian action on symplectic manifolds.

In this paper, we define a notion of an action of a
cosymplectic groupoid
on a cosymplectic manifold by using the notion of a
Lagrangian-Legendrean submanifold
. Afterwards, we prove a reduction theorem which is an analogue of the Mikami-Weinstein theorem. The notion of a cosymplectic groupoid is introduced by
[
DW15
]
and recently studied in
[
FP23
]
. They are defined as Lie groupoids whose space of arrows is endowed with a multiplicative cosymplectic structure.

This paper is organized as follows. In

section 3
, we recall the notion of a symplectic groupoid and its role in Poisson geometry. In addition, we review the definition and some properties of cosymplectic groupoids. In
section 4
, we introduce the notion of a Lagrangian-Legendrean submanifold of cosymplectic manifolds, and define cosymplectic actions of cosymplectic groupoids on cosymplectic manifolds. We observe that if a cosymplectic groupoid
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
acts on a cosymplectic manifold
M
, then a symplectic groupoid
S_{\mathcal{G}}=(S_{G_{1}}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
, where
S_{G_{1}}
is the symplectic leaf of
G_{1}
that contains unit arrows, acts on each symplectic leaf of
M
. In
section 5
, we prove the main theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem 5.2
)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
be a cosymplectic groupoid and
M
a cosymplectic, free and proper left
\mathcal{G}
-module with respect to a momentum map
\rho:M\to G_{0}
. Assume that
\xi\in\rho(M)

2.
Cosymplectic manifolds

An
almost cosymplectic structure
on a
(2n+1)
-dimensional manifold
M
is a pair of
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
such that
\eta\wedge\omega^{n}\neq 0
. On an almost cosymplectic manifold
(M,\eta,\omega)
there is a unique vector field
R
which satisfies

\omega(R,-)=0,\qquad\eta(R)=1.

R
is called the
Reeb vector field
of
(M,\eta,\omega)
. Moreover, we have an isomorphism of
C^{\infty}(M)
-modules
\flat:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M)
defined by
\flat(X)=\omega(X,-)+\eta(X)\eta
. Conversely, a pair
(\eta,\omega)
is an almost cosymplectic structure if and only if the map
\flat:\mathfrak{X}(M)\to\Omega^{1}(M)
defined as above is an isomorphism and there is a vector field
R
which satisfies the above conditions.

An almost cosymplectic structure
(\eta,\omega)
is called a contact structure when
\omega=d\eta
.
On the other hand, an almost cosymplectic structure
(\eta,\omega)
is called a
cosymplectic structure
when
d\eta=0,\ d\omega=0
.

For a contact structure
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, the distribution
\textrm{Ker}\eta
is completely non-integrable. On the other hand, for a cosymplectic structure
(\eta,\omega)
, the distribution
\textrm{Ker}\eta
is integrable since
\eta
is closed. Therefore, contact structures and cosymplectic structures are two classes of almost cosymplectic structures which are polar opposites of each other.

A cosymplectic structure
(\eta,\omega)
on
M
induces a Poisson structure
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
on
M
by

\pi(\alpha,\beta)=\omega(\flat^{-1}\alpha,\flat^{-1}\beta),

where
\alpha,\beta\in T^{\ast}M
. This Poisson structure is regular and has corank 1. Its symplectic leaves coincide with those of the integrable distribution
\textrm{Ker}\eta
and the symplectic form on a symplectic leaf
S
is
\omega|_{S}
. In fact, it is known that a cosymplectic structure on
M
is equivalent to a corank 1 regular Poisson structure on
M
with a Poisson vector field which is transverse to the symplectic leaves
[
GMP11
]
.

For every function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
on a cosymplectic manifold
M
, we can associate a vector field
X_{f}
by

X_{f}=\flat^{-1}(df-R(f)\eta).

X_{f}
is called the Hamiltonian vector field of
f
. This condition is equivalent to

\omega(X_{f},-)=df-R(f)\eta,\qquad\eta(X_{f})=0.

Let
(M,\eta,\omega)
be a cosymplectic manifold and
G
a Lie group acts on
M
from left. We suppose that the action preserves
\eta,\omega
, i.e.,
L_{g}^{\ast}\eta=\eta,\ L_{g}^{\ast}\omega=\omega
. Denote the Lie algebra of
G
as
\mathfrak{g}
. Albert
[
Alb89
]
defined the notion of a momentum map on cosymplectic manifolds:

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth map
\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
is called a momentum map when the following conditions are satisfied:

•

\mu
is equivariant, i.e.,
\mu(gp)=\textrm{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}\mu(p)
holds for any
p\in M
and
g\in G
.

•

For any
A\in\mathfrak{g}
, the induced vector field
A^{\ast}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is the Hamiltonian vector field of a function
\mu^{A}:M\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\mu^{A}(p)=(\mu(p))(A)
,

•

For the Reeb vector field
R
and any
A\in\mathfrak{g}
,
d\mu^{A}(R)=0
holds.
\hfill{\blacksquare}

The action of
G
is said to be Hamiltonian if there is a momentum map. Now we assume that there is a Hamiltonian action of
G
on
(M,\eta,\omega)
which is free and proper. Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
be a regular value of a momentum map
\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
. Since
\mu
is equivariant, the isotropy group
G_{\xi}
acts on
\mu^{-1}(\xi)
. We denote the quotient
G_{\xi}\backslash\mu^{-1}(\xi)
as
M^{\xi}
and the natural projection as
\pi:\mu^{-1}(\xi)\to M^{\xi}
. The following theorem is an analogue of Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer theorem:

Theorem 2.1

(Albert
[
Alb89
]
)
.

There is a unique cosymplectic structure
(\eta^{\xi},\omega^{\xi})
on
M^{\xi}
which satisfies
\pi^{\ast}\eta^{\xi}=\eta|_{\mu^{-1}(\xi)},\ \pi^{\ast}\omega^{\xi}=\omega|_{\mu^{-1}(\xi)}
.
\Box

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
Yon24
]
, reduction theorems of coKähler manifolds and 3-cosymplectic manifolds are proved. They are natural odd-dimensional analogues of the reduction theorems of Kähler manifolds and hyperKähler manifolds
[
HKLR87
]
, respectively.
\hfill{\blacksquare}

Actions of cosymplectic groupoids

In order to define the notion of a cosymplectic groupoid action, we need to consider an analogue of Lagrangian submanifolds.

Let

be a cosymplectic manifold and

(T_{p}N)^{\omega_{p}|_{\textrm{Ker}\eta_{p}}}
denotes the orthogonal complement of

In fact, the notion of a LL submanifold is defined for

\Gamma
is the image of an embedding
\iota:G_{1}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{t}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}G_{1}\to G_{1}\times G_{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times G_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
given by
\iota(g,h)=(g,h,1,gh,1)
. Then we obtain

\iota^{\ast}\widetilde{\eta}=\iota^{\ast}(p_{1}^{\ast}\eta+p_{2}^{\ast}\eta-p_{3}^{\ast}\eta)=\pi_{1}^{\ast}\eta+\pi_{2}^{\ast}\eta-m^{\ast}\eta,

\begin{split}\iota^{\ast}\widetilde{\omega}&=\iota^{\ast}(p_{1}^{\ast}\omega+p_{2}^{\ast}\omega-p_{3}^{\ast}\omega+(p_{1}^{\ast}\eta)\wedge dq_{1}-(p_{2}^{\ast}\eta)\wedge dq_{2})\\
&=\pi_{1}^{\ast}\omega+\pi_{2}^{\ast}\omega-m^{\ast}\omega,\end{split}

where
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
denotes projections to
i
-th
G_{1}
and
i
-th
\mathbb{R}
, respectively, and
\pi_{i}:G_{1}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{t}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}G_{1}\to G_{1}
also denotes projections. Hence the multiplicativity of
\eta
and
\omega
is equivalent to
\iota^{\ast}\widetilde{\eta}=0
and
\iota^{\ast}\widetilde{\omega}=0
, respectively. In addition, we have

\dim\Gamma=\dim(G_{1}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{t}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}G_{1})=2\dim G_{1}-\dim G_{0},

and since
\dim G_{1}=2\dim G_{0}+1
(see
[
FP23
]
), we obtain

\begin{split}2\dim\Gamma+1&=4\dim G_{1}-2\dim G_{0}+1=3\dim G_{1}+2\\
&=\dim(G_{1}\times G_{1}\times\mathbb{R}\times G_{1}\times\mathbb{R}).\end{split}

Therefore the multiplicativity condition is equivalent to
\Gamma
being a LL submanifold.
∎

Now we can define a notion of a cosymplectic groupoid action:

Definition 4.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0}),\eta_{G_{1}},\omega_{G_{1}})
be a cosymplectic groupoid and
(M,\eta,\omega)
a cosymplectic manifold. A left
\mathcal{G}
-action on
M
(or a left
\mathcal{G}
-module
M
) is said to be
cosymplectic
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

The momentum map
\rho:M\to G_{0}
of the action satisfies
d\rho(R)=0
, where
R
is the Reeb vector field of
(M,\eta,\omega)
,

(2)

The graph of the action, i.e.,

\Gamma:=\{(g,x,1,gx,1)\in G_{1}\times M\times\mathbb{R}\times M\times\mathbb{R}\mid(g,x)\in G_{1}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}M\}

is a LL submanifold of a cosymplectic manifold
(G_{1}\times M\times\mathbb{R}\times M\times\mathbb{R},\widetilde{\eta},\widetilde{\omega})
, where

\widetilde{\eta}:=\eta_{G_{1}}+\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},

\widetilde{\omega}:=(\omega_{G_{1}}+\omega_{1}+\eta_{G_{1}}\wedge dt_{1})-\omega_{2}-(\eta_{G_{1}}+\eta_{1})\wedge dt_{2}

(
t_{i}
denotes the coordinate of
i
-th
\mathbb{R}
and
(\eta_{i},\omega_{i})
denotes the cosymplectic structure of
i
-th
M
).

\hfill{\blacksquare}

The following proposition is essentially used in
section 5
for the proof of our main theorem:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0}),\eta_{G_{1}},\omega_{G_{1}})
be a cosymplectic groupoid,
(M,\eta,\omega)
a cosymplectic left
\mathcal{G}
-module and
(\rho,\Phi)
its action maps. Let
S_{\mathcal{G}}=(S_{G_{1}}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
be the symplectic subgroupoid obtained by
Theorem 3.1
. Then any symplectic leaf
S
of
(M,\eta,\omega)
is a symplectic left
S_{\mathcal{G}}
-module by action maps

\rho|_{S}:S\to G_{0},

\Phi|_{S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S}:S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S\to S.

Proof.

Firstly, we see that the Legendrean property of the graph
\Gamma
of the action
(\rho,\Phi)
implies
\Phi(S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S)\subset S
. Let
(g,x)\in S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S
and
(g(t),x(t))
be a smooth path in
S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S
whose starting point is
(1_{\rho(x)},x)
and ending point is
(g,x)
. Then we obtain a smooth path
({g}(t),{x}(t),1,{(gx)}(t),1)
in
\Gamma
and

0=\widetilde{\eta}(\dot{g}(t),\dot{x}(t),0,\dot{(gx)}(t),0)=\eta_{G_{1}}(\dot{g}(t))+\eta(\dot{x}(t))-\eta(\dot{(gx)}(t))=-\eta(\dot{(gx)}(t))

holds. Therefore two points
x=1_{\rho(x)}x
and
gx
are in the same symplectic leaf
S
.

Secondly, we see that the Lagrangian property of
\Gamma
implies that the restricted action
(\rho|_{S},\Phi|_{S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S})
is symplectic. Let
({g}(t),{x}(t))
be a smooth path in
S_{G_{1}}\@mathmeasure\displaystyle{\times}\@mathmeasure 4\displaystyle{\vbox to0.0pt{}_{s}}\@mathmeasure 6\displaystyle{{\times}_{\rho}}\hbox to0.0pt{}\mathop{\kern 0.0pt}S
. Then we have

0=\widetilde{\omega}(\dot{g}(t),\dot{x}(t),0,\dot{(gx)}(t),0)=\omega_{G_{1}}(\dot{g}(t))+\omega(\dot{x}(t))-\omega(\dot{(gx)}(t)).

In addition to this, taking the dimension count into consideration, we can see that the graph of the
S_{\mathcal{G}}
-action on
S
is a Lagrangian submanifold.
∎

Mikami-Weinstein type theorem

Now we recall the statement of the Mikami-Weinstein theorem.

Theorem 5.1

(

)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
be a symplectic groupoid and
M
a symplectic, free and proper left
\mathcal{G}
-module with respect to a momentum map
\rho:M\to G_{0}
. Assume that
\xi\in\rho(M)
is a regular value of
\rho
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\xi}\backslash\rho^{-1}(\xi)
is a symplectic manifold. Moreover, if
\rho
is submertive, a family of symplectic manifolds
\{\mathcal{G}_{\xi}\backslash\rho^{-1}(\xi)\}_{\xi\in\rho(M)}
is precisely the symplectic foliation of the Poisson manifold
\mathcal{G}\backslash M
.
\Box

Example 5.1
.

Consider the case of
\mathcal{G}=(G\times\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{\ast})
. For any
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
,
\mathcal{G}_{\xi}\simeq\{g\in G\mid{\rm Ad}^{\ast}_{g}\xi=\xi\}
. Thus by the correspondence in
Example 4.1
, we can see that the Marsden-Weinstein-Meyer theorem is a special case of the Mikami-Weinstein theorem.
\hfill{\blacksquare}

The following is our main theorem:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
be a cosymplectic groupoid and
M
a cosymplectic, free and proper left
\mathcal{G}
-module with respect to a momentum map
\rho:M\to G_{0}
. Assume that
\xi\in\rho(M)
is a regular value of
\rho
. Then
(S_{\mathcal{G}})_{\xi}\backslash\rho^{-1}(\xi)
is a cosymplectic manifold.

Proof.

We denote
(S_{\mathcal{G}})_{\xi}\backslash\rho^{-1}(\xi)
as
M^{\xi}
and the quotient map as
\pi:\rho^{-1}(\xi)\to M^{\xi}
. Let
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the symplectic foliation of
M
. Since the Reeb vector field
R
of
M
satisfies
d\rho(R)=0
, each
S_{i}
intersects transversely with
\rho^{-1}(\xi)
, and thus
(\rho|_{S_{i}})^{-1}(\xi)
is a smooth manifold.

By
Proposition 4.2
, the symplectic groupoid
S_{\mathcal{G}}
acts on each leaf
S_{i}
symplectically. Hence
\{S^{\xi}_{i}:=(S_{\mathcal{G}})_{\xi}\backslash(\rho|_{S_{i}})^{-1}(\xi)\}_{i\in I}
forms a foliation on
M^{\xi}
of codimension 1 (see section 1.3 of
[
MM03
]
). In addition, we can apply
Theorem 5.1
on each leaf and thus
\{S^{\xi}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a symplectic foliation on
M^{\xi}
.

Let
L_{g}:\rho^{-1}(\xi)\to\rho^{-1}(\xi)
be the left action map by
g\in(S_{\mathcal{G}})_{\xi}
and
x(t)
a integral curve of
R
in
\rho^{-1}(\xi)
. Then by the Legendrean property of the graph,

\eta((L_{g})_{\ast}R)=\eta(\dot{(gx)}(t))=\eta_{G_{1}}(0)+\eta(\dot{x}(t))=\eta(R)=1

holds. Similarly, by the Lagrangian property of the graph, we have
\omega((L_{g})_{\ast}R,-)=0
and thus
R
is left invariant. Hence
R
descends to a vector field
{R}^{\xi}:=d\pi(R)
on the quotient
M^{\xi}
.
{R}^{\xi}
is transverse to the symplectic foliation on
M^{\xi}
.

The reduced foliation
\{S^{\xi}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is coorientable since
\{S_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is. We choose a defining 1-form
\eta^{\xi}
of the foliation
\{S^{\xi}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that
\eta^{\xi}(R^{\xi})=1
holds. Then we have
\pi^{\ast}\eta^{\xi}=\eta
. Let
\omega_{i}
be the symplectic form on
S^{\xi}_{i}
. Then we define a 2-form
\omega^{\xi}
on
M^{\xi}
by

\omega^{\xi}(R^{\xi},-)=0,\qquad\omega^{\xi}|_{S^{\xi}_{i}}=\omega_{i}.

Then we have
\pi^{\ast}\omega^{\xi}=\omega
.
\eta^{\xi},\omega^{\xi}
are closed since
\eta,\omega
are closed and
\pi
is a submersion. We can easily see that
\eta^{\xi}\wedge(\omega^{\xi})^{n}
is a volume form, and thus a pair
(\eta^{\xi},\omega^{\xi})
is a cosymplectic structure on
M^{\xi}
.
∎

6.
Examples

In this section, we give two examples of
Theorem 5.2
.

Example 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
be a cosymplectic groupoid. Then
\mathcal{G}
acts on
G_{1}
by the multiplication of groupoid with
t:G_{1}\to G_{0}
as the momentum map. This action is free, proper and cosymplectic. In fact, the graph of the action is a LL submanifold because of
Proposition 4.1
, and the Reeb vector field
R
of
G_{1}
satisfies
R\in\textrm{Ker}dt
(see
[
FP23
]
).

For any
\xi\in G_{0}
, the reduced cosymplectic manifold
(S_{\mathcal{G}})_{\xi}\backslash t^{-1}(\xi)
is obtained by
Theorem 5.2
. Here the symplectic leaf
(S_{\mathcal{G}})_{\xi}\backslash(t|_{S_{G_{1}}})^{-1}(\xi)
coincides with the
S_{\mathcal{G}}
-orbit in
G_{0}
through
\xi
. We can see that it is also a leaf of the symplectic foliation of a Poisson manifold
G_{0}
by
Theorem 5.1
, and thus we have two foliated manifolds each having the orbit as a leaf.
\hfill{\blacksquare}

Example 6.2
.

Let
G
be a Lie group acts on a cosymplectic manifold
(M,\eta,\omega)
freely and properly. We assume that there is a momentum map
\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
with respect to the action. Then let us consider a cosymplectic groupoid

T^{\ast}G\times\mathbb{R}\simeq G\times\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}

(the trivial
\mathbb{R}
-central extension of a symplectic groupoid
T^{\ast}G\rightrightarrows\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
).

For any
\varepsilon>0
, we define

M_{\varepsilon}=\{\ x\in M\ |\ \text{Reeb flow}\ \varphi_{t}(x)\ \text{is defined in}\ t\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\ \},

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}=T^{\ast}G\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\subset T^{\ast}G\times\mathbb{R}.

In fact, although
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
is not a Lie groupoid, it is a
local Lie groupoid
(where the composition of arrows is defined only in a neighborhood of the unit arrows), and the previously discussed concepts related to actions can also be applied to local Lie groupoids. We can define a cosymplectic
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
-action on
M_{\varepsilon}
by

(g,\xi,t)\cdot x:=\varphi_{t}(gx)

for
(g,\xi,t)\in G\times\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon),\ x\in M_{\varepsilon}
, with
\mu|_{M_{\varepsilon}}:M_{\varepsilon}\to\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
as the momentum map. In this case,
Theorem 5.2
coincides with
Theorem 2.1
for the
G
-action on
(M_{\varepsilon},\eta|_{M_{\varepsilon}},\omega|_{M_{\varepsilon}})
.
\hfill{\blacksquare}

We defined the notion of a cosymplectic groupoid action, thus we can also define the notion of Morita equivalence between cosymplectic groupoids as in the case of symplectic groupoids

:

Definition 7.1
.

A cosymplectic groupoid
\mathcal{G}=({G}_{1}\rightrightarrows G_{0})
is said to be
Morita equivalent
to another cosymplectic groupoid
\mathcal{H}=({H}_{1}\rightrightarrows H_{0})
when there is a cosymplectic manifold
M
, a left cosymplectic
\mathcal{G}
-action and a right cosymplectic
\mathcal{H}
-action on

which satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

and
\sigma:M\to H_{0}
are surjective submersions;

(2)

Actions of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
on

are both free and proper;

(3)

The two actions commute with each other;

(4)

\rho
is constant on each orbit of the action of
\mathcal{H}
and an induced map

is a diffeomorphism; Similarly,
\sigma
is constant on each orbit of the action of
\mathcal{G}
and an induced map
\mathcal{G}\backslash M\to H_{0}
is a diffeomorphism.

(M,\rho,\sigma)
is called an
equivalence bimodule
from
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
.
\hfill{\blacksquare}

Let

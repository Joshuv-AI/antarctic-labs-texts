Quantum dust cores of rotating black holes
Tommaso Bambagiotti, Roberto Casadio

Abstract
A quantum description of dust cores for black hole geometries can be obtained by quantising the geodesic motion of dust particles and finding the corresponding many-body ground state. We here generalise previous works in spherical symmetry to rotating geometries and show the effect of angular momentum on the size of the core and effective interior geometry.



1
Introduction

The singularities of known black hole solutions of the Einstein equations
[
1
]
can be removed by imposing
regularity conditions on the (effective) energy density and scalar invariants inspired by classical
physics
[
2
]
.
This procedure usually induces the appearance (or fails to remove) an inner Cauchy horizon.
A different framework, invoked for example in Ref.
[
3
]
, can be implemented based on the possibility
that black hole interiors and the collapsed matter therein are described more accurately by quantum physics.
One can then consider an effective energy density
\rho\sim|\psi|^{2}
, where
\psi=\psi(r)
is the wavefunction
of the collapsed matter source, such that the Misner-Sharp-Hernandez (MSH) mass
function
[
4
,
5
]
satisfies

\displaystyle m(r)\equiv 4\,\pi\int_{0}^{r}\rho(x)\,x^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}x\sim 4\,\pi\int_{0}^{r}|\psi(x)|^{2}\,x^{2}\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}x<\infty\qquad{\rm for}\ r>0\ .

(1.1)

This accommodates for
\rho\sim r^{-2}
and
m\sim r
, which ensures that
m(0)=0

and replaces the central singularity with an integrable singularity
[
6
,
7
]
,
that is a region where the curvature invariants and the effective energy-momentum tensor diverge
but their volume integrals remain finite
[
8
]
.
Additionally, no inner horizon is present.

An explicit realisation for the inner core of a quantum Schwarzschild black hole based on the Oppenheimer-Snyder
model of dust collapse
[
9
]
was analysed in Ref.
[
10
]
(see also
Refs.
[
11
,
12
]
).
The ball was described as a sequence of layers
[
13
]
containing dust particles,
whose trajectories are individually quantised.
A condition was then imposed to ensure that the fuzzy quantum layers defined by the positions of
these particles remain orderly nested in the global quantum ground state, which confirmed the expected
quantum behaviour in Eq. (
1.1
) for the effective energy density and mass
function
[
10
,
14
]
.
The same approach was then applied to the Reissner-Nordström geometry in Ref.
[
15
]
,
with similar results about the absence of inner horizons.

In this work we will consider (differentially) rotating cores of dust by explicitly employing geodesic motion
in the (generalised) Kerr metric and compare with the perturbative results of Ref.
[
16
]
,
where angular momentum was added to the spherical symmetric geometry.
We will find that the fully general relativistic treatment leads to elongated dust cores compared to the spherically
symmetric case.
We will also identify a configuration in which the effective mass function and specific angular momentum
grow linearly inside the dust core, as expected for an integrable singularity without Cauchy horizons
of the form discussed in Ref.
[
3
]
.

2
Rotating geodesics

We will describe the collapsing matter as dust particles moving in a generalised Kerr geometry
which, in Boyer-Lidquist coordinates
x^{\alpha}=(t,r,\theta,\phi)
, reads
1
1

1

We shall always use
units with
c=1
and often write the Planck constant
\hbar=\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}
and the Newton constant

G_{\rm N}=\ell_{\rm p}/m_{\rm p}
, where
\ell_{\rm p}
and
m_{\rm p}
are the Planck length and mass, respectively.

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm d}$}s^{2}=-\mbox{${\rm d}$}t^{2}+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r}{\rho^{2}}\left(a\,\sin^{2}\theta\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\phi-\mbox{${\rm d}$}t\right)^{2}+\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}r^{2}}{\Delta}+\mbox{${\rm d}$}\theta^{2}\right)+\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\phi^{2}\ ,

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\,\cos^{2}\theta

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\Delta=r^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r+a^{2}\ .

(2.3)

In the above expressions, the function
m=m(r)
represents the MSH mass inside ellipsoids of coordinate
radius
r
and
a=a(r)=J(r)/m(r)
is the specific angular momentum on the surface of the same
ellipsoid
[
3
]
.

We recall that the vacuum Kerr metric is given by constant
a=A
and
m=M
, where
M
is now the
Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) mass
[
17
]
, and it contains horizons located at

\displaystyle R_{\pm}=G_{\rm N}\,M\pm\sqrt{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}-A^{2}}\ ,

(2.4)

provided
A^{2}\leq G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}
.
We expect
m
and
a
to approach asymptotically
M
and
A
, respectively, in the vacuum outside
the collapsing dust.

2.1
Action and equations of motion

Assuming individual dust particles have a proper mass
\mu\ll m
, their trajectories
can be approximated by time-like geodesics
x^{\alpha}=x^{\alpha}(\tau)
in the metric (
2.1
),
governed by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle 2\,L

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\dot{t}^{2}-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r}{\rho^{2}}\left(a\,\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot{\phi}-\dot{t}\right)^{2}-\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\dot{r}^{2}}{\Delta}+\dot{\theta}^{2}\right)-\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot{\phi}^{2}\ ,

(2.5)

which yields the mass-shell condition
2\,L=1
and the integrals of motion

\displaystyle\frac{E}{\mu}=\left(1-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r}{\rho^{2}}\right)\dot{t}+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r}{\rho^{2}}\,\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot{\phi}

(2.6)

and

\displaystyle j=\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a^{2}\,r}{\rho^{2}}\,\sin^{2}\theta\right)\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot{\phi}-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r}{\rho^{2}}\,\sin^{2}\theta\,\dot{t}\ .

(2.7)

We can invert the above relations and obtain

\displaystyle\dot{t}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta}\left[\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a^{2}\,r}{\rho^{2}}\,\sin^{2}\theta\right)\frac{E}{\mu}-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r}{\rho^{2}}\,j\right]

(2.8)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{E}{\mu}+\frac{4\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\left[(r^{2}+a^{2})\,E/\mu+a\,j\right]}{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\right)\left[2\,r^{2}+a^{2}\left(1+\cos 2\,\theta\right)\right]}

and

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Delta}\left[\left(\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r}{\rho^{2}}\right)\frac{E}{\mu}+\left(1-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r}{\rho^{2}}\right)\frac{j}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right]

(2.9)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{4\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r\,E/\mu+2\,a^{2}\,j}{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\right)\left[2\,r^{2}+a^{2}\left(1+\cos 2\,\theta\right)\right]}-\frac{2\,j}{\left[2\,r^{2}+a^{2}\left(1+\cos 2\,\theta\right)\right]\,\sin^{2}\theta}\ .

We next assume that the dust particles in a layer at the surface of an ellipsoid of radial coordinate

r=r(\tau)
co-rotate with the geometry and therefore have
j=0
, that is

\displaystyle\left(r^{2}+a^{2}+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a^{2}\,r}{\rho^{2}}\,\sin^{2}\theta\right)\dot{\phi}=\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r}{\rho}\,\dot{t}\ ,

(2.10)

which implies

\displaystyle\dot{t}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left[1+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\,(r^{2}+a^{2})}{\Delta\,\rho^{2}}\right]\frac{E}{\mu}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left\{1+\frac{4\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\,(r^{2}+a^{2})}{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\right)\left[2\,r^{2}+a^{2}\left(1+\cos 2\,\theta\right)\right]}\right\}\frac{E}{\mu}

and

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r}{\Delta\,\rho^{2}}\right)\frac{E}{\mu}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{4\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a\,r\,E/\mu}{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\right)\left[2\,r^{2}+a^{2}\left(1+\cos 2\,\theta\right)\right]}\ .

With this assumption, the Lagrangian (
2.5
) simplifies to

\displaystyle 2\,L_{0}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle-\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\dot{r}^{2}}{\Delta}+\dot{\theta}^{2}\right)+\left[1+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\,(r^{2}+a^{2})}{\Delta\,\rho^{2}}\,\right]^{2}\frac{E^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\left[\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\,(r^{2}+a^{2})}{\Delta^{2}\,\rho^{2}}\right]\frac{E^{2}}{\mu^{2}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{4\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,m^{2}\,a^{2}\,r^{2}\,(r^{2}+a^{2})\,\sin^{2}\theta}{\Delta^{2}\,\rho^{2}}\right]\frac{E^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=1\ .

We note that, for the ground state
E^{2}=0
, the above expression greatly simplifies to

\displaystyle 2\,L_{0}=-\rho^{2}\left(\frac{\dot{r}^{2}}{\Delta}+\dot{\theta}^{2}\right)=1\ ,

(2.14)

which can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\dot{r}^{2}+\frac{\Delta}{2\,\rho^{2}}+\frac{\Delta}{2}\,\dot{\theta}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,\dot{r}^{2}+\frac{r^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r+a^{2}}{2\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\,\cos^{2}\theta\right)}+\frac{1}{2}\left(r^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r+a^{2}\right)\dot{\theta}^{2}=0\ .

(2.15)

The equation of motion for
\theta=\theta(\tau)
then reads

\displaystyle\ddot{\theta}=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}+a^{2}\,\cos^{2}\theta}\right)=\frac{a^{2}\,\cos\theta\,\sin\theta}{\left(r^{2}+a^{2}\,\cos^{2}\theta\right)^{2}}\ ,

(2.16)

so that
\theta=\theta(\tau)
can be constant in the ground state with
E=j=0
only for
\theta=0
or
\theta=\pi/2
.

Let us next consider the case of radial motion along the axis of symmetry
\theta=\dot{\theta}=0
,
and motion on the equatorial plane
\theta=\pi/2
and
\dot{\theta}=0
.

2.1.1
Axial motion

For
\theta=\dot{\theta}=0
, the Lagrangian (
2.13
) reads

\displaystyle 2\,L_{0}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r}{\rho^{2}}\right)^{-1}\frac{E^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}\,\dot{r}^{2}=1\ ,

(2.17)

which can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\mu\,\dot{r}^{2}-\frac{G_{\rm N}\,\mu\,m}{r}\left(1-\frac{a^{2}}{r^{2}+a^{2}}\right)=\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\frac{E^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-1\right)\ .

(2.18)

Note that the above equation describes purely radial motion in a Schwarzschild spacetime for
a=0
.

2.1.2
Equatorial motion

For
\theta=\pi/2
and
\dot{\theta}=0
, the Lagrangian (
2.13
) becomes

\displaystyle 2\,L_{0}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{(E^{2}/\mu^{2}-\dot{r}^{2})\,r^{3}+a^{2}\,(r+2\,G_{\rm N}\,m)\,E^{2}/\mu^{2}}{2\,r\left(r^{2}+a^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,r\right)}=1\ ,

(2.19)

which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,\mu\,\dot{r}^{2}-\mu\left(\frac{G_{\rm N}\,m}{r}-\frac{a^{2}}{2\,r^{2}}\right)-\left(1+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m}{r}\right)\frac{a^{2}\,E^{2}}{2\,\mu\,r^{2}}=\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\frac{E^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-1\right)\ .

(2.20)

Note that the last term in the left hand side couples the energy
E
of the dust particle with the
(specific) angular momentum
a
of the system.

3
Ground state and perturbative spectrum

We can discretise the rotating ball by considering an ellipsoidal core of mass
\mu_{0}=\nu_{0}\,\mu=\epsilon_{0}\,M

and coordinate radius
r=R_{1}(\tau)
surrounded by
N
comoving layers of inner radius
r=R_{i}(\tau)
,
thickness
\Delta R_{i}=R_{i+1}-R_{i}
, and mass
\mu_{i}=\epsilon_{i}\,M
, where
\epsilon_{i}
is the fraction
of ADM mass carried by the
\nu_{i}
dust particles in the
i^{\rm th}
layer.
The gravitational mass inside the ellipsoid
r<R_{i}
will be denoted by

\displaystyle M_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mu_{j}=M\,\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\epsilon_{j}\ ,

(3.1)

with
M_{1}=\mu_{0}
and
M_{N+1}=M
.
Likewise, we denote with
A_{i}
the specific angular momentum at the inner surface of the
i^{\rm th}
layer.

The evolution of each layer can be derived by noting that dust particles located on the symmetry axis
or on the equator at
r=R_{i}(\tau)
will follow the geodesic equation

\displaystyle H_{i}\equiv\frac{P_{i}^{2}}{2\,\mu}-\frac{G_{\rm N}\,\mu\,M_{i}}{R_{i}}+\mu\,W_{i}=\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\frac{E_{i}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-1\right)\equiv\mathcal{E}_{i}\ ,

(3.2)

where
P_{i}=\mu\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}R_{i}/\mbox{${\rm d}$}\tau
is the radial momentum conjugated to
R=R_{i}(\tau)
,
E_{i}

the conserved momentum per unit mass conjugated to
t=t_{i}(\tau)
and
W_{i}
denotes the other terms
in the radial potential.
In particular, for the axial motion

\displaystyle W_{i}=\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}\,A_{i}^{2}}{R_{i}\left(R_{i}^{2}+A_{i}^{2}\right)}\equiv W_{i}^{\rm ax}\ ,

(3.3)

whereas for equatorial motion

\displaystyle W_{i}=\frac{A_{i}^{2}}{2\,R_{i}^{2}}\left[1-\left(1+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}}{R_{i}}\right)\frac{E_{i}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right]\equiv W_{i}^{\rm eq}\ .

(3.4)

With the canonical quantization prescription
P_{i}\mapsto\hat{P}_{i}=-i\,\hbar\,\partial_{R_{i}}
,
Eq. (
3.2
) becomes the time-independent Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{i}\,\psi_{n_{i}}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\,\mu}\left(\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}^{2}}{\mbox{${\rm d}$}R_{i}^{2}}+\frac{2}{R_{i}}\,\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}}{\mbox{${\rm d}$}R_{i}}\right)-\frac{G_{\rm N}\,\mu\,M_{i}}{R_{i}}+\mu\,W_{i}\right]\psi_{n_{i}}=\mathcal{E}_{n_{i}}\,\psi_{n_{i}}\ .

(3.5)

3.1
Non-rotating case

When
W_{i}\sim a^{2}
is negligible, Eq. (
3.5
) is analogous to the equation for
s
-states of the hydrogen atom,
and the solutions are given by the eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\psi_{n_{i}}(R_{i})=\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{6}\,M_{i}^{3}}{\pi\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}\,m_{\rm p}^{9}\,n_{i}^{5}}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{\mu^{2}\,M_{i}\,R_{i}}{n_{i}\,m_{\rm p}^{3}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)L_{n_{i}-1}^{1}\!\!\left(\frac{2\,\mu^{2}\,M_{i}\,R_{i}}{n_{i}\,m_{\rm p}^{3}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)\ ,

(3.6)

where
L_{n-1}^{1}
are Laguerre polynomials and
{n}_{i}=1,2\,\ldots
, corresponding to the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{n_{i}}^{(0)}=-\frac{\mu^{3}\,M_{i}^{2}}{2\,m_{\rm p}^{4}\,n_{i}^{2}}\ .

(3.7)

The wavefunctions (
3.6
) are normalised in the scalar product which makes
\hat{H}_{i}
Hermitian
for
W_{i}=0
, that is

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\,n_{i}\mid n^{\prime}_{i}\,\rangle$}=4\,\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}R_{i}^{2}\,\psi_{n_{i}}^{*}(R_{i})\,\psi_{n^{\prime}_{i}}(R_{i})\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}R_{i}=\delta_{n_{i}n^{\prime}_{i}}\ .

(3.8)

The expectation value of the coordinate radius on these eigenstates is given by

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{n_{i}}\equiv\bra{n_{i}}\hat{R}_{i}\ket{n_{i}}=\frac{3\,m_{\rm p}^{3}\,\ell_{\rm p}\,n_{i}^{2}}{2\,\mu^{2}\,M_{i}}\ ,

(3.9)

with relative uncertainty

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\Delta R}_{n_{i}}}{\bar{R}_{n_{i}}}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\bra{n_{i}}\hat{R}^{2}_{i}\ket{n_{i}}-\bar{R}_{n_{i}}^{2}}}{\bar{R}_{n_{i}}}=\frac{\sqrt{n_{i}^{2}+2}}{3\,n_{i}}\ ,

(3.10)

which approaches the minimum
\overline{\Delta R}_{n_{i}}\simeq\bar{R}_{n_{i}}/3
for
n_{i}\gg 1
.

By assuming that the conserved quantity
E_{i}
remains well-defined for all the dust particles
in the allowed quantum states, we obtain the fundamental condition
[
11
]

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{E_{i}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=1+\frac{2\,\mathcal{E}_{i}^{(0)}}{\mu}=1-\frac{\mu^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{4}\,n_{i}^{2}}\ ,

(3.11)

which yields the lower bound for the single particle principal quantum numbers

\displaystyle n_{i}\geq N_{i}^{(0)}\equiv\frac{\mu\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\ .

(3.12)

Upon saturating the above bound, one then finds

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}=\frac{3}{2}\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}\ ,

(3.13)

and the wavefunction for the
\nu_{i}
particles in each layer is given by the same ground state

\displaystyle\psi_{N_{i}}^{(0)}(R_{i})=\sqrt{\frac{\mu\,m_{\rm p}}{\pi\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}\,M_{i}^{2}}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{\mu\,R_{i}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)L_{\frac{\mu\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}-1}^{1}\!\!\left(\frac{2\,\mu\,R_{i}}{m_{\rm p}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)\ ,

(3.14)

where the values of
M_{i}
, hence
N_{i}^{(0)}
in Eq. (
3.12
), must be such that
\bar{R}_{i}^{(0)}\lesssim\bar{R}_{i+1}^{(0)}
.

3.2
Slow-rotation corrections

We can estimate the corrections to the radial potential using the above result for the ground state

E_{i}=0
with
R_{i}\simeq\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}
.
For the axial motion, we find

\displaystyle W_{i}^{\rm ax}\simeq\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}\,A_{i}^{2}}{R_{i}\left(R_{i}^{2}+A_{i}^{2}\right)}\simeq\frac{8\,A_{i}^{2}}{3\left(9\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}+4\,A_{i}^{2}\right)}\simeq\frac{8\,A_{i}^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}\ ,

(3.15)

whereas for equatorial motion

\displaystyle W_{i}^{\rm eq}\simeq\frac{A_{i}^{2}}{2\,R_{i}^{2}}\left[1-\left(1+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}}{R_{i}}\right)\frac{E_{i}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right]\simeq\frac{2\,A_{i}^{2}}{9\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}\ .

(3.16)

For perturbation theory to apply, the above potentials must be smaller than

\displaystyle V_{i}=\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}}{R_{i}}\simeq\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}}{3\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}}\simeq\frac{2}{3}\ .

(3.17)

This yields

\displaystyle\frac{A_{i}^{2}}{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}\ll\frac{27}{12}

(3.18)

for axial motion and

\displaystyle\frac{A_{i}^{2}}{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}\ll 3

(3.19)

for equatorial motion.
Both conditions above are approximately satisfied for classical Kerr black holes (with
A^{2}\leq G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}
).

Since
W_{i}
is constant to leading order in
A_{i}
, it will simply result in a shift of the energy
eigenvalues

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{i}=\mathcal{E}_{i}^{(0)}+\mu\,W_{i}\ .

(3.20)

For the ground state, we then have

\displaystyle 0=\frac{E_{i}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=1+\frac{2\,\mathcal{E}_{i}}{\mu}=1-\frac{\mu^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{4}\,N_{i}^{2}}+2\,W_{i}\ ,

(3.21)

or

\displaystyle N_{i}\simeq\frac{\mu\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}\left(1+2\,W_{i}\right)^{1/2}}\simeq\frac{\mu\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left(1-W_{i}\right)=N_{i}^{(0)}\left(1-W_{i}\right)\ .

(3.22)

Correspondingly, we obtain a reduction in the areal radius

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{N_{i}}=\frac{3\,m_{\rm p}^{3}\,\ell_{\rm p}\,N_{i}^{2}}{2\,\mu^{2}\,M_{i}}\simeq\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}\left(1-2\,W_{i}\right)\ ,

(3.23)

with unaffected (leading order) uncertainty.
Note in particular that

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{\rm ax}<\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{\rm eq}<\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}\ ,

(3.24)

so that the effect of rotation is to reduce the coordinate radius of the layers.

This behaviour is the opposite of what was obtained in Ref.
[
16
]
,
where angular momentum was added to the quantum states of dust particles but assuming
the Schwarzschild metric still determines the geodesic motion.
For radial geodesics, the addition of angular momentum therefore amounted to a repulsive
centrifugal term in the radial potential which made the size of the core increase.
In the present, fully general relativistic treatment, the angular momentum is included in the
background geometry and therefore affects the principal quantum number (
3.23
)
of the ground states (
3.14
).
Moreover, we notice that the length of a circle on the equator in the geometry (
2.1
)
with constant
t
and
r
and
\theta=\pi/2
is given by

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm d}$}\ell^{\rm eq}=r\left[1+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,m\,a^{2}}{r\,\rho^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]^{1/2}\mbox{${\rm d}$}\phi\ ,

(3.25)

which, computed for
r=\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{\rm eq}
,
m=M_{i}
and
a=A_{i}
, yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}\ell^{\rm eq}_{i}}{\mbox{${\rm d}$}\phi}\simeq\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}\left(1+\frac{2\,A_{i}^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}\right)\ .

(3.26)

Likewise, the length of arcs at constant
t
,
r
and
\phi
is given by

\displaystyle\mbox{${\rm d}$}\ell=\rho\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}\theta\ ,

(3.27)

which, computed for
r=\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{\rm ax}
,
m=M_{i}
,
a=A_{i}
, around the
axis of symmetry
\theta=0
, results in

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}\ell^{\rm ax}_{i}}{\mbox{${\rm d}$}\theta}\simeq\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}\left(1-\frac{10\,A_{i}^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}}\right)\ .

(3.28)

The above expressions show that the rotating layers are actually smaller than the
spherical layers (with same
M_{i}
but
A_{i}=0
) along the axis of rotation but larger on the equator.
This picture agrees with the expected shape of the layers being elongated on the equatorial
plane with respect to the symmetry axis in spherical coordinates, which are the ones used
to express the states (
3.14
).

3.2.1
Core size

For the outermost layer (the surface of the core), we have

\displaystyle R_{\rm s}\simeq\frac{4}{3}\,\bar{R}_{N_{N}}\simeq\frac{3}{2}\,G_{\rm N}\,M\left(1-2\,W_{N}\right)\ ,

(3.29)

so that along the axis of symmetry

\displaystyle R_{\rm s}^{\rm ax}\simeq\frac{3}{2}\,G_{\rm N}\,M\left(1-\frac{8\,A^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.30)

where
A
is the constant specific angular momentum of the outer Kerr geometry.
On the equatorial plane one similarly finds

\displaystyle R_{\rm s}^{\rm eq}\simeq\frac{3}{2}\,G_{\rm N}\,M\left(1-\frac{2\,A^{2}}{9\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.31)

so that

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\rm s}^{\rm eq}}{R_{\rm s}^{\rm ax}}\simeq 1-2\,W_{N}^{\rm eq}+2\,W_{N}^{\rm ax}\simeq 1+\frac{2\,A^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}}\ .

(3.32)

It is particularly interesting to compare the above results

2.4
) of the (slowly rotating) external Kerr geometry,

\displaystyle R_{\pm}\simeq G_{\rm N}\,M\pm G_{\rm N}\,M\left(1-\frac{A^{2}}{2\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}}\right)\ .

(3.33)

From Fig.
1
, we see that both core radii are larger than
R_{-}
for all
A^{2}\leq G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}
.
The equatorial core radius remains shorter than
R_{+}
for small rotation and until

R_{\rm s}^{\rm eq}\simeq R_{+}
for

\displaystyle\frac{A^{2}}{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}}\simeq\frac{3}{2}\left(\sqrt{7}-2\right)\simeq 0.984\ ,

(3.34)

whereas the axial radius
R_{\rm s}^{\rm ax}\simeq R_{+}
for

\displaystyle\frac{A^{2}}{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}}\simeq\frac{3}{2}\left(\sqrt{73}-5\right)\simeq 0.998\ .

(3.35)

Clearly, both of these values of the specific angular momentum are way outside the
regime of slow rotation and should not be trusted.

3.2.2
Near extremal rotation

Let us consider the near extremal case for which
A_{i}^{2}\simeq G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}
.
For the axial motion, we find

\displaystyle W_{i}^{\rm ax}\simeq\frac{G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}\,A_{i}^{2}}{R_{i}\left(R_{i}^{2}+A_{i}^{2}\right)}\simeq\frac{8\,A_{i}^{2}}{3\left(9\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M_{i}^{2}+4\,A_{i}^{2}\right)}\simeq\frac{8}{39}\ ,

(3.36)

whereas for equatorial motion

\displaystyle W_{i}^{\rm eq}\simeq\frac{A_{i}^{2}}{2\,R_{i}^{2}}\simeq\frac{2}{9}\ .

(3.37)

As noted before, these values are smaller than
V_{i}
in Eq. (
3.17
) so that previous perturbative
results should still provide at least a qualitatively valid picture in the whole classical range.

3.3
Quantum rotating core

In the classical description, each dust layer can be made arbitrarily thin.
However, in the quantum description described above, we can assume the minimum thickness
is given by the uncertainty
\overline{\Delta R}_{n_{i}}\sim\bar{R}_{n_{i}}/3
obtained from
Eq. (
3.10
) for
n_{i}\gg 1

[
10
]
.
In the global ground state formed by layers in their own ground state we then have

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{N_{i+1}}=\bar{R}_{N_{i+1}}^{(0)}\left(1-2\,W_{i+1}\right)=\bar{R}_{N_{i}}+\overline{\Delta R}_{n_{i}}\simeq\frac{4}{3}\,\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}\left(1-2\,W_{i}\right)\ .

(3.38)

From Eq. (
3.23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{3\,\ell_{\rm p}\,M_{i+1}}{2\,m_{\rm p}}\left(1-2\,W_{i+1}\right)\simeq\frac{2\,\ell_{\rm p}\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}}\left(1-2\,W_{i}\right)\ ,

(3.39)

and the (discrete) mass function
M_{i}
therefore depends on the angular momentum
A_{i}
.
In particular, on the symmetry axis, we have

\displaystyle M_{i+1}^{\rm ax}\left(1-\frac{16\,A_{i+1}^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,(M_{i+1}^{\rm ax})^{2}}\right)\simeq\frac{4}{3}\,M_{i}^{\rm ax}\left(1-\frac{16\,A_{i}^{2}}{27\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,(M_{i}^{\rm ax})^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.40)

whereas

\displaystyle M_{i+1}^{\rm eq}\left(1-\frac{4\,A_{i+1}^{2}}{9\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,(M_{i+1}^{\rm eq})^{2}}\right)\simeq\frac{4}{3}\,M_{i}^{\rm eq}\left(1-\frac{4\,A_{i}^{2}}{9\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,(M_{i}^{\rm eq})^{2}}\right)\ ,

(3.41)

on the equator.

From the general discussion in Ref.
[
3
]
, we know that the inner Cauchy
horizon is not present if the mass function and specific angular momentum
m\sim a\sim r
.
Let us therefore assume that the specific angular momentum
A_{i}
is linearly dependent on the
radius
R_{i}
,

\displaystyle{A}_{i}\propto\bar{R}_{i}\simeq\alpha\,\bar{R}_{i}^{(0)}\ .

(3.42)

From Eqs. (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), the perturbations become

\displaystyle W^{\rm{ax}}_{i}\simeq\frac{2\,\alpha^{2}}{3\,(1+\alpha^{2})}\simeq\frac{2}{3}\,\alpha^{2}

(3.43)

and

\displaystyle W^{\rm{eq}}_{i}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\,\alpha^{2}\ ,

(3.44)

at leading order in the constant
\alpha
, which is of the same order of magnitude of the slow-rotation
parameter
A_{i}/G_{\rm N}\,M_{i}
.
With this assumption, both Eqs. (
3.40
) and (
3.41
) therefore simplify to

\displaystyle M_{i+1}^{\rm ax/eq}\simeq\frac{4}{3}\,M_{i}^{\rm ax/eq}\ ,

(3.45)

which is precisely the linear behaviour found in the spherically symmetric case.
In fact, given the total ADM mass
M
, the mass distribution within each layer is then determined by

\displaystyle M_{i}^{\rm ax/eq}\simeq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{N+1-i}M\equiv M_{i}\ ,

(3.46)

which implies that the quantum states with specific angular momentum given in
Eq. (
3.42
) correspond to a mass function that grows linearly with the layer size, that is

\displaystyle M_{i}\simeq\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\alpha^{2}\right)\frac{\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{\rm eq}}{G_{\rm N}}\simeq\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\frac{4}{3}\,\alpha^{2}\right)\frac{\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{\rm ax}}{G_{\rm N}}\ .

(3.47)

We remark that this configuration is also consistent with the ellipsoidal shape of the collapsed cores
described by Eqs. (
3.24
), (
3.26
) and (
3.28
).

For the above reasons, we will limit our following analysis to the case with linearly growing specific
angular momentum (
3.42
) and mass function (
3.47
).

3.4
Angular momentum and horizon quantisation

According to Eq. (
3.22
), the ground state of dust particles in each layer is (approximately)
given by spherical wavefunctions (
3.14
) with different quantum numbers along the axis
of symmetry and on the equatorial plane, namely

\displaystyle N_{i}^{\rm eq}\simeq\frac{\mu\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2}\,\alpha^{2}\right)<N_{i}^{\rm ax}\simeq\frac{\mu\,M_{i}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\,{\alpha^{2}}\right)\ .

(3.48)

Since
N_{i}^{\rm{ax}}
and
N_{i}^{\rm{eq}}
are integers, one obtains that the specific
angular momentum must satisfy the quantisation rule

\displaystyle N_{i}^{\rm{ax}}-N_{i}^{\rm{eq}}\simeq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{N+1-i}\frac{\mu\,M}{6\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}\,\alpha^{2}\ .

(3.49)

For
i=N+1
, one obtains
M_{i}=M
and

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\simeq\frac{6\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}{\mu\,M}\left(N_{N+1}^{\rm{ax}}-N_{N+1}^{\rm{eq}}\right)\ .

(3.50)

so that Eq. (
3.42
) implies the quantisation of the specific angular momentum
in the exterior Kerr geometry

\displaystyle A^{2}\simeq\left(\alpha\,R_{\rm s}^{(0)}\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{27\,M}{2\,\mu}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\left(N_{N+1}^{\rm{ax}}-N_{N+1}^{\rm{eq}}\right)\ ,

(3.51)

where we recall that
R_{\rm s}^{(0)}\simeq 3\,G_{\rm N}\,M/2
.

From Eq. (
3.29
), we also find

\displaystyle\alpha^{2}\simeq\frac{R_{\rm s}^{\rm eq}-R_{\rm s}^{\rm ax}}{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M}\simeq\frac{3\left(R_{\rm s}^{\rm eq}-R_{\rm s}^{\rm ax}\right)}{4\,R_{\rm s}^{(0)}}\ ,

(3.52)

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{R_{\rm s}^{\rm eq}-R_{\rm s}^{\rm ax}}{R_{\rm s}^{(0)}}\simeq\frac{24\,m_{\rm p}^{4}}{\mu^{2}\,M^{2}}\left(N_{N+1}^{\rm{ax}}-N_{N+1}^{\rm{eq}}\right)\ ,

(3.53)

so that the shape of the core is also quantised.

The outer horizon area in the Kerr geometry is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\rm{H}}=4\,\pi\left(R_{+}^{2}+A^{2}\right)\simeq 4\,\pi\left(R_{\rm s}^{(0)}\right)^{2}\left(1-\frac{3}{4}\,\alpha^{2}\right)\ .

(3.54)

From the quantisation of the spherical dust core
[
10
]
,

\displaystyle\frac{M}{\mu}\,N_{N+1}^{(0)}\simeq\frac{M^{2}}{m_{\rm p}^{2}}\ ,

(3.55)

we then find

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\rm{H}}\simeq 4\,\pi\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,\frac{M}{\mu}\,N_{N+1}^{(0)}\left[1-\frac{9\,m_{\rm p}^{2}}{2\,\mu\,M}\left(N_{N+1}^{\rm{ax}}-N_{N+1}^{\rm{eq}}\right)\right]\ ,

(3.56)

where we remark that
M/\mu
is not necessarily an integer.

3.5
Effective interior geometry

We have seen that, for a core characterised by the linear relation (
3.42
) for
the specific angular momentum, the mass function

M_{i}^{\rm ax}\simeq M_{i}^{\rm eq}\equiv M_{i}\propto\bar{R}_{i}^{(0)}
.
It then follows that the corresponding effective metric can be simply obtained by applying the
Gurses and Gursey algorithm
[
18
]
(generalised in Ref.
[
3
]
)
with a seed geometry provided by the spherically symmetric case of non-rotating dust described
in Refs.
[
10
,
19
]
and specific angular momentum (
3.42
).
This is all consistent with the initial assumption of a metric of the form in Eq. (
2.1
),
where
m=m(r)
and
a=a(r)
are now determined by the ground states of the layers.

The explicit form of the metric function
\Delta=\Delta(r)
in Eq. (
2.3
)
for the ground state is particularly interesting since it allows us to verify that a Cauchy horizon
never appears.
For this analysis, we will consider two effective mass functions,
m=m_{\rm par}(r)

and
m=m_{\rm int}(r)
, which interpolate between the
interior discrete mass distribution
M_{i}
and the outer Kerr metric with constant
M

(for more details see also Ref.
[
19
]
).
In particular,

\displaystyle m_{\rm int}=c_{\rm i}\,r\ ,\quad\text{for}\;0\leq r<\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)}\ ,

(3.57)

where the constant

\displaystyle c_{\rm i}=\frac{M_{N}}{\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)}}\simeq\frac{2\,m_{\rm p}}{3\,\ell_{\rm p}}\ ,

(3.58)

and the analytic expression inside the outermost layer
\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)}<r<R_{\rm s}

is given in Eq. (
A.2
) of Appendix
A
.

The function
m_{\rm par}
is a parabolic profile which matches the outer constant ADM mass
M

for
r\to\infty
, and was introduced in Ref.
[
14
]
as an
improvement over
m_{\rm int}
to better account for the spatial overlapping of the
wavefunctions (
3.14
).
Its analytic expression is recalled in Eq. (
A.3
), from which we have

\displaystyle m_{\rm par}=c_{\rm p}\,r+\mathcal{O}(r^{2})\ ,\quad\text{for}\;r\to 0^{+}\ ,

(3.59)

with

\displaystyle c_{\rm p}=\frac{\bar{a}\,m_{\rm p}}{2\,\ell_{\rm p}}

(3.60)

and the fitting parameter
\bar{a}\simeq 1.53
.

The effective angular momentum
a=a(r)
in Eq. (
2.1
) can be obtained from the above
mass functions by noting that Eqs. (
3.42
) and (
3.47
) imply

\displaystyle\frac{A_{i}}{A}\simeq\frac{\bar{R}_{N_{i}}^{(0)}}{R_{s}^{(0)}}\simeq\frac{M_{i}}{M}\ ,

(3.61)

so that

\displaystyle a(r)=A\,\frac{m(r)}{M}\ .

(3.62)

The function
\Delta=\Delta(r)
is shown in Fig.
2
.
In agreement with the general analysis of Ref.
[
3
]
,
we see that
\Delta(0)=0
and there is a unique zero
r_{\rm H}>0
for values of the specific angular momentum
within the range

\displaystyle 0<\frac{A}{G_{\rm N}\,M}<\delta_{\rm i}\equiv\frac{3}{2\,\sqrt{3}}<1\ ,

(3.63)

for
m=m_{\rm int}(r)
, and

\displaystyle 0<\frac{A}{G_{\rm N}\,M}<\delta_{\rm p}\equiv 2\,\sqrt{\frac{\bar{a}-1}{\bar{a}^{2}}}<1\ ,

(3.64)

for
m=m_{\rm par}(r)
.
For the critical values
\delta=\delta_{\rm i}
or
\delta=\delta_{\rm p}
the radius
r_{\rm H}=0
.

Note that both ranges in Eqs. (
3.63
) and (
3.64
) impose stronger
requirements on
A/G_{\rm N}M
for the existence of the horizon than the limits of the perturbative regime
given in Eqs. (
3.18
) and (
3.19
).
In particular, this analysis for
m_{\rm int}
and
m_{\rm par}
excludes classical near-extremal
configurations with
A\simeq G_{\rm N}M
.

\Delta

4
Conclusions and outlook

In this work we extended the quantisation of the layered dust core
[
10
]

to rotating (ellipsoidal) cores, where dust particles move along time-like geodesics of the
(generalised) Kerr geometry (
2.1
), improving the description given in Ref.
[
16
]
.
In that previous work, particles fall freely along geodesics in the Schwarzschild spacetime,
and their rotation was only approximately described by states with non-zero angular momentum,
hence neglecting the general-relativistic features of the Kerr geometry.

If we only consider motion along the rotation axis or on the equator,
the Hamiltonian constraint (
3.5
) reduces to the expression
for the spherically symmetric distribution, described in detail in

[
11
,
10
]
, with an additional term

W_{i}\sim a^{2}/G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}
which can be treated as a perturbation.
The global ground state is described by ellipsoidal layers elongated on the equator
with respect to the rotation axis.
In the perturbative regime of (relatively) slow-rotation, the equatorial radius

{R}_{\text{s}}^{\text{eq}}
is always shorter than the outer horizon
R_{+}
,
thus describing a black hole, whereas a configuration with
R_{\text{s}}^{\text{ax/eq}}\simeq R_{+}

cannot be described within this perturbative approach.
We remark that the results obtained in this work improve on the analysis of Ref.
[
16
]

by including the angular momentum in the generalised Kerr geometry that determines the
geodesics.

A possible ground state appears to be described by a configuration in which
A_{i}\propto\bar{R_{i}}\propto M_{i}
,
as described in Section
3.3
.
In fact, this condition ensures that no Cauchy horizon ever forms within
the core and that the central ring-singularity is replaced by an integrable singularity.
In this configuration, the specific angular momentum
A_{i}
is quantized according to
Eq. (
3.49
), which ensures the quantisation of the specific angular momentum
A

in the exterior solution, in agreement with Ref.
[
16
]
.
Together with the quantisation of the spherical dust core, this result
implies that the outer horizon area is quantised in Plank units.
Remarkably, in this configuration the effective metric can be determined from the spherically symmetric
non-rotating dust distribution given in Refs.
[
11
,
10
,
14
]
.
In Section
3.5
, we analysed the effective causal structure inside the core by means of
the function
\Delta=\Delta(r)
, which is computed for specific seed mass distributions described
in Ref.
[
19
]
.
The absence of a Cauchy horizon and the existence of the only event horizon at
r=r_{\rm H}
, within the
slow-rotation limits given by Eqs. (
3.63
) and (
3.64
), is in agreement with
the general picture that quantum matter should be able to regularise the interior region of black
holes
[
3
]
.

Appendix A
Effective mass functions

We show here the explicit forms of the continuous MSH mass functions used in Section
3.5
.
More details can be found in Ref.
[
19
]
.

The mass function
m=m_{\rm int}(r)
simply interpolates between the linear behaviour
M_{i}\propto\bar{R}_{i}

up to the inner radius of the outermost layer and the constant ADM mass
M
outside the core,

\displaystyle m_{\rm int}=\begin{cases}c_{\rm i}\,r\ ,\qquad&{\rm for}\quad r\leq\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)}\\
B(r)\ ,\qquad&{\rm for}\quad\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)}\leq r\leq R_{s}^{(0)}=\frac{4}{3}\,\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)}\\
M\ ,\qquad&{\rm for}\quad r\geq R_{s}^{(0)}\ .\end{cases}.

(A.1)

The interpolating function is given by the 5-th order polynomial

\displaystyle B=

\displaystyle-\frac{c_{1}}{\Delta x^{3}}\left(x-x_{1}\right)^{3}+\frac{M}{\Delta x^{3}}\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{3}-\left(\frac{3\,c_{1}}{\Delta x^{4}}+\frac{c_{2}}{\Delta x^{3}}\right)\left(x-x_{1}\right)^{3}\left(x-x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{3\,M}{\Delta x^{4}}\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{3}\left(x-x_{1}\right)-\left(\frac{6\,c_{1}}{\Delta x^{5}}+\frac{3\,c_{2}}{\Delta x^{4}}\right)\left(x-x_{1}\right)^{3}\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{6\,M}{\Delta x^{5}}\left(x-x_{1}\right)^{2}\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{3}\ ,

(A.2)

where
x\equiv r/2\,G_{\rm N}\,M
,
c_{1}=M_{N}
,
c_{2}=c_{1}\,(2\,G_{\rm N}\,M/\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)})
,
and the width of the outermost layer is denoted by
\Delta x=(R_{\rm s}^{(0)}-\bar{R}_{N_{N}}^{(0)})/2\,G_{\rm N}\,M=1/4
.

The wavefunctions of dust particles (
3.14
) do not vanishing outside the respective layers,
which yields a non-zero probability that at least the nearest layers overlap.
To account for this effect, the mass function
m=m_{\rm par}(r)

was computed in Ref.
[
14
]
for the spherically symmetric case and is given by

\displaystyle m_{\rm par}=M\left(\bar{a}\,x+\bar{b}\,x^{\bar{c}}\right)\ ,

(A.3)

with
x\equiv r/2\,G_{\rm N}\,M
and the fitting coefficients

\displaystyle\bar{a}=1.53\ ,\quad\bar{b}=-0.533\ ,\quad\bar{c}=1.90\ .

(A.4)

These values were used for the plots in the right panel of Fig.
2
.

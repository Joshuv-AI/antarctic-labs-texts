Dissertation title: decoupling analysis of building carbon emissions and economic growth based on Tapio model and EKC curve
Authors: Wei Sun, Zhenggang Huo, Sensen Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChina is a major carbon emitter in the construction industry and the world’s second-largest economy. Clarifying the scientific relationship between carbon emissions and economic development has prominence realistic meaning for China and even other developing countries to carry out more effective carbon emission reduction work in the construction industry.ResultsThis study combines the Tapio decoupling model, EKC theory and grey correlation analysis to study the relationship between carbon emissions and total output value of the construction industry in thirty provinces of China. The results show that most areas of central and eastern China have basically achieved weak decoupling, while other regions are not stable enough. In addition, in 2022, 18 regions meet the inverted U-shaped curve, and the overall is on an upward trend; Beijing, Hebei and Sichuan have passed the peak of the curve. The national construction industry is in expansion connection (decoupling index is 0.92), showing that the development of the industry is tending towards a coordinated state.ConclusionThere is still a lot of room for China’s construction industry to reduce carbon emission. Each region can refer to the evolution law of decoupling state obtained in this paper, and formulate more efficient carbon reduction measures according to local conditions, which helps the industry achieve green and sustainable development.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13021-025-00349-6.

Introduction

According to the working reports of the first and second groups of the sixth assessment report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), the global temperature has risen significantly in recent years. The average temperature change in the past 10 years has increased by 1.09℃ compared with the pre-industrial period (1850–1900). Countries around the world have recognized the serious impact of greenhouse gases produced by human activities on the global climate environment, and the Chinese government also formally proposed the “dual carbon” goal of “carbon peak and carbon neutrality”.

The IPCC shows that the energy consumption generated by the construction industry accounts for 32% of the final energy use, and the carbon emissions it produces account for 1/4 of greenhouse gases [1]. The International Energy Agency (IEA) released the “Global Energy Review 2025” pointed out that in 2024, the energy-related carbon emissions of emerging and developing countries increased by 1.5%, of which India increased the most, reaching 5.3%, and China was 0.4%, but accounted for 1/3 of the world,while the carbon emissions of developed countries decreased by 1.1%, of which the United States (US) and the European Union (EU) decreased by 0.5% and 2.2% respectively. In 2022, the top three carbon emissions in China were the power sector, manufacturing and construction (India was agriculture), and industry; while the US and the EU were the power sector, transportation sector and buildings [2].

Based on the perspective of carbon emissions from multiple sectors, the top two sectors in terms of carbon emissions are industry (accounting for 79.83%) and transport storage and post (accounting for 7.65%) [3]. From 2000 to 2021, Chinas total carbon emissions increased by an average annual rate of 5.92%, while the construction industry increased by the rate of 8.99%,the average annual growth rate of each region showed a general trend of western > eastern > central > northeastern, spatially [4]. The main source of carbon emissions in China’s construction industry is indirect carbon emissions from related sectors (accounting for more than 90%),if the current development model (based on 2021) is followed, it is estimated that carbon peak will not be achieved until 2035 at the earliest, with a peak of approximately 3.18 billion tCO2 [5]. China’s construction industry has significant carbon reduction efforts, accounting for 48.3% of total carbon emissions in 2022 [6], and it is also a main support industry of national economy. Therefore, clarifying the scientific relationship between carbon emissions and economic output in the construction industry is of great value to promoting China’s green and low-carbon development.

Literature review

Most scholars use the Tapio decoupling model to accurately quantify the relevance between economic gain and carbon emissions. Based on the Tapio decoupling model and the Logit model, electricity consumption at the county level in China from 2009 to 2016 was studied, nearly 70% of counties had achieved electricity decoupling by 2016 [7]. Studying the relationship between carbon emissions and economic development in 30 provincial regions in China, showing that the decoupling relationship between them mainly depends on the degree of decoupling between economic production and energy expenditure, and there is inter-provincial heterogeneity [8]. From the perspective of Chinas industrial structure, discovering that scientific industrial structure adjustment can reduce overall carbon dioxide emissions, and this effect has a much greater impact on the primary and tertiary industries than the secondary industry [9]. Through the decoupling analysis of carbon emissions in 57 countries along the Belt and Road Initiative, it has emerged that the decoupling status of high-income countries is ordinarily better than that of low-income countries, and shows temporal and spatial differentiation [10].

They also combine the EKC curve to clearly and intuitively show the relevance between economic gain and environmental contamination. A study of solid waste production in 285 Chinese cities found that the decoupling status generally showed a tendency of first improving and then deteriorating [11]. Based on the EKC hypothesis, showing that renewable resources are only beneficial to reducing carbon emissions at lower or higher levels [12]. Research on major resource-based wastes in 134 countries, its results show that there is an inverted U-shaped curve relevance between carbon footprint and per capita income [13]. The EKC curve has also been confirmed to exist in China’s steel industry, and in the long term, economic development has a momentous emission reduction effect [14]. The results of the study based on the augmented autoregressive distributed lag (AARDL) approach to test that there is a U-shaped nonlinear relevance between environmental pollution and income level [15]. This is basically consistent with the research results of Yilanci and Pata [16], and Hussain et al. [17], but inconsistent with the research results of Sarkodie et al. [18], Cheng and Yao [19], and Agboola and Alola [20].

In order to study the driving factors that affect the degree of decoupling, they further used the ideas of index, structural decomposition and regression model. The differential GMM method, Tapio decoupling index and peak prediction model were used, showing that China has reached the peak of carbon emission intensity as a whole [21]. Applying the Laspeyres index decomposition method and the Tapio decoupling model, finding that it has been in a weak decoupling state with economic growth for a long time and the future decoupling trend will be dominated by the secondary industry [22]. Combining the LMDI model, showing that population and economic activities are the principal drivers of carbon emissions in Pakistan, and in the long run, there is an unstable weak decoupling relationship [23]. Based on the STIRPAT extended model and panel quantile regression method, the driving factors of 10 major carbon-emitting countries were analyzed, showing that population, energy use and exports were the key driving factors, and the EKC hypothesis was verified to be applicable to these 10 countries [24].

Scholars have conducted a lot of valuable explorations based on the integration of the above methods. Based on the multi-dimensional perspectives of energy, economic and social, a system dynamics model of carbon emissions was constructed in combination with data from China over the past 40 years, then decoupling analysis and forecasting were performed [25]. By establishing a spatial econometric model and conducting spatial autoregressive correlation analysis, indicating that the economic growth of China’s construction industry has a significant spatial spillover effect, but the inhibitory effect is small [26]. Combined with the biased distance function based on the global meta-frontier DEA, it was revealed that the inter-provincial differences in construction industry decoupling and technological progress is the main driving force for the decoupling of economic development and carbon emissions [27]. In addition, they have also conducted a lot of research on green buildings. It has many advantages, such as improved energy efficiency, promoting long-standing cost savings, and actively responding to changes in the global climate [28], which can effectively reduce carbon emissions. Research based on green building performance evaluation in Malaysia shows that building sustainability will reduce the impact of the building manufacturing industry on the environment [29]. A comparison between green buildings and non-green buildings in China found that the former has lower CO2 emissions than the latter, with residential buildings reducing their emissions by an average of 10%, and this is particularly evident in the operation phase [30]. The carbon emissions from buildings during the operation period account for a large proportion of the total carbon emissions during the entire process [31]. Therefore, promoting the development of green buildings will greatly promote carbon emission reduction in the construction industry.

In summary, most scholars at home and abroad currently focus on the national level, isolated one or more provincial levels, and fail to further analyze the changing trend of the degree of decoupling between different regions and the actual reasons leading to this trend. Based on this, this paper comprehensively applies the Tapio decoupling model and the EKC hypothesis, obtains the most correlated set of influencing factors with the help of grey correlation theory, and establishes a STIRPAT extended model containing multiple key factors. This effectively improves the EKC fitting curve that relies only on a single gross output value variable, so that it can obtain more accurate inflection points and better test the decoupling analysis results between economic development and carbon emission levels in the construction industry of different regions. Therefore, the innovative combination model proposed in this article can scientifically and accurately analyze the evolution of the decoupling state and the changing trend of the EKC curve in the construction industry, and also provides a reasonable reference model for the scientific planning of green carbon reduction measures in the construction industry in various regions.

Study area

According to the “Research Report on Energy Consumption and Carbon Emissions in China’s Buildings (2023)”, the total energy consumption of China’s buildings in 2021 was 2.35 billion tce, accounting for 44.7% of the country’s total energy consumption, and the carbon emissions of the entire building process were 5.01 billion tCO2, accounting for 47.1% of the country’s total energy-related carbon emissions, as shown in Figs. 1 and 2. This shows that nearly half of the nation’s carbon emissions are generated by the construction industry. If the goal of building carbon peak in 2030 is to be achieved, China’s construction industry still has a long way to go to reduce carbon emissions. In a struggle to additionally explore the relationship among carbon footprint and economic development in various regions of China, this paper selects provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities as research objects to make the research more representative and authentic. Tapio decoupling model

Based on the decoupling theory proposed by the Organization for Economic and Development (OECD), Tapio proposed the concept of “decoupling elasticity” to construct a decoupling model [34]. “Decoupling Elasticity” mainly refers to the ratio between the magnitude of economic change and the magnitude of carbon dioxide change, that is, it reflects the sensitivity of carbon dioxide changes to economic changes. The Tapio decoupling model is shown in Fig. 5. According to the decoupling elasticity coefficient and the magnitude of economic and carbon dioxide changes, it is mainly divided into eight decoupling states: strong decoupling (SD), weak decoupling (WD), recession decoupling (RD), strong negative decoupling (SND), weak negative decoupling (WND), expansion negative decoupling (END), expansion connection (EC) and recession connection (RC).Fig. 5Tapio decoupling model

Environmental kuznets curve

In 1955, Kuznets first proposed a theory on the relationship between per capita income and distribution fairness, that is, with economic growth, income inequality shows a trend of first rising and then falling, showing an inverted U-shaped curve relationship [36]. Based on this study, Grossman and Krueger found in their study on the relationship between per capita income and environmental indicators that the emission of pollutants and economic growth also show an inverted U-shaped curve relationship [37], which is the Environmental Kuznets Curve (EKC).

Many scholars have found in empirical studies using the EKC curve [38–42] that in most cases, carbon dioxide emissions also show an inverted U-shaped curve relationship with economic development. That is, in the early stages of economic development, carbon dioxide emissions will continue to rise with economic development,after the economy develops to a certain extent, the curve reaches an inflection point, and then carbon dioxide emissions will decline with economic development. Based on this, this study further studies the relationship between carbon emissions in the construction industry and the output value of the construction industry. (1) IPAT model

In the 1970 s, Ehrlich proposed the IPAT model to reveal the impact of population size, economic development and science and technology on the environment [46]. The basic form of the model is as follows:16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I = PAT$$\end{document}wherein, I (Impact) represents environmental load, P (Population) represents population size, A (Affluence) represents affluence, and T (Technology) represents technology level.

(2) STIRPAT model

Based on the IPAT model, York further enriched and expanded it into a nonlinear STIRPAT model to overcome the shortcomings of the IPAT model [47]. The basic form of the model is as follows:17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$I = aP^{b} A^{c} T^{d} e$$\end{document}wherein, I, P, A and T represent environmental load, population, affluence, and technology level, respectively; a is the model coefficient, b, c and d are the estimated parameters of population, affluence, and technology conditions, respectively; and e is the model error.

In order to reduce data processing errors, eliminate heteroscedasticity, and reduce model collinearity problems [48],and more intuitively reflect the elasticity coefficients between variables, the logarithm processing is usually performed on both sides of the STIRPAT model. (5) EG Co-integration test

Cointegration theory was proposed by Engel and Granger as an important way to assist in direct modeling of non-stationary series. Commonly used cointegration test methods are Engel Granger test (EG) and Johnson test (Johansen). In this study, the EG test method is selected for cointegration test. Its basic test steps are as follows:

a. Check whether the dependent variable and the independent variable are of the same order. If they are not of the same order, there is no cointegration relationship. If they are zero-order, linear regression can be used directly for processing;

b. If the dependent variable and the independent variable are of the same order, a cointegration regression model is constructed. If the residual is a stationary sequence, it is considered that there is a cointegration relationship between the variables.

(6) FMOLS

The fully modified least squares estimation proposed by Phillips can effectively solve the problem of cointegration relationship testing. The core idea of this method is to perform non-parametric correction on the regression estimator to eliminate the impact of noise parameters on the asymptotic distribution of statistics [49]. According to Hamit-Haggar’s empirical study on the long-term intrinsic relationship between greenhouse gas emissions and economic growth in the Canadian industrial sector, FMOLS is the most suitable technology for this type of panel data and can achieve more accurate and reliable statistical tests [50]. Therefore, FMOLS is used in this study to solve the problems existing in the EG test.

Conclusion

This paper uses the Tapio model and the EKC hypothesis to study the intrinsic relationship between the carbon emissions of the construction industry and its output value in 30 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities in China from 2015 to 2022. The main conclusions are as follows:

This paper uses the Tapio decoupling theory to explore the decoupling relationship between the carbon emissions of the entire construction process and the output value of the construction industry. The results show that after more than ten years of green and low-carbon development in the construction industry, the decoupling of construction carbon emissions in provincial regions has remained above half, but at the same time, the decoupling status in most regions is not stable enough, indicating that most regions still have great potential for carbon emission reduction. According to the decoupling of carbon emissions in China’s construction industry in 2022 (Fig. 8d), except for a few regions in the west and northeast, the construction industry in most regions has gradually reduced its carbon emissions or its growth rate is lower than the economic growth rate on the basis of achieving economic growth, that is, the output value of the construction industry in these regions has increased, and the impact on the environment has gradually decreased or stabilized. Regions that have not achieved carbon peak need to innovate and use more energy-saving and carbon-reduction technologies in future development to improve the quality of construction industry development.The relationship between carbon emissions and output value of the construction industry was studied using the EKC hypothesis. The results showed that about 60% of the provincial regions (18/30) in the country had an obvious inverted U-shaped relationship between carbon emissions and output value of the construction industry. In addition, the overall EKC curve of the eastern region is close to the inflection point, and the curve shows a stable trend towards the peak. The carbon emissions of construction are in a coordinated state with economic development; the overall EKC curve of some central, western regions, and Jilin Province are in a stable fluctuation stage, but there is still a slow upward trend. The economic growth of the construction industry brings carbon emissions lower than the development rate, or although the economy is slowly declining, carbon emissions are rapidly reduced; the EKC curves of most western and northeastern regions are still in an unstable rising stage. The carbon emissions of construction brought about by economic growth are far from reaching the peak, or the sacrifice of economic development has only slightly reduced carbon emissions. Therefore, it is urgent to adjust the energy consumption structure and promote green and low-carbon construction technologies.Based on the research results of Tapio decoupling theory and environmental Kuznets curve, it is found that in 2022, the regions that have entered high-quality development have generally shown an upward trend, indicating that after the country has vigorously promoted energy conservation and carbon reduction in the construction field, the carbon emission reduction work in various regions has achieved considerable results. As long as the construction industry in various regions continues to promote carbon emission reduction and achieve a stable decoupling state, there is great hope that the country will achieve the goal of carbon peak before 2030.

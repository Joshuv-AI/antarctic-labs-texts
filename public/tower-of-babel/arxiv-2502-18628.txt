Uniform Positivity of the Lyapunov Exponent for C 1 C^{1} Monotone potentials generated by the Cat Map
Nicholas Chiem

Abstract
. We consider an Arnold’s Cat Map generated C 1 C^{1} bounded potential with the directional derivative in the unstable direction bounded away from zero. We show that the Lyapunov exponent for the associated Shrödinger Operator is uniformly positive for all energies provided the coupling is sufficiently large.

2.
Main Result

Throughout the paper we let
c,C
represent universal constants where
c
is some small constant and
C
is some large constant. These constants only depend on the bounded measurable potential
v:\mathbb{T}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that satisfies

(i)

v\in C^{1}([0,1)^{2},\mathbb{R})
.

(ii)

For all
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, then the directional derivative of
v
in the unstable direction is uniformly away from zero. That is,
D_{\vec{u}}v(\omega)>c
.

We provide some enjoyable examples of such potentials.

Example 1
.

Fix an
n\in\mathbb{N}
and define a polynomial in two variables

\displaystyle v:[0,1)^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\omega_{1}^{k}+b_{k}\omega_{2}^{k}

where
a_{k},b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}\geq 0
and
a_{1}\neq 0
or
b_{1}\neq 0
. Then
v\in C^{1}([0,1)^{2})
and bounded above by
\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}+b_{k}
. A direct computation gives the Jacobian of
v
:

J_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{k=1}^{n}ka_{k}\omega_{1}^{k-1}&\sum_{k=1}^{n}kb_{k}\omega_{2}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}

Then the directional derivative with respect to the unstable direction is:

D_{\vec{u}}v(\omega)=J_{v}\vec{u}=u_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}ka_{k}\omega_{1}^{k-1}+u_{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}kb_{k}\omega_{2}^{k-1}\geq u_{1}a_{1}+u_{2}b_{1}

Therefore for any
\omega\in[0,1)^{2}
, we may conclude
D_{\vec{u}}v(\omega)>c
.

Example 2
.

Consider an exponential function defined as

\displaystyle v:[0,1)^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}

which is
C^{1}([0,1)^{2})
and bounded by
e^{2}
. The Jacobian becomes

J_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}&e^{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

While the directional derivative in the unstable direction

D_{\vec{u}}v(\omega)=(u_{1}+u_{2})e^{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}\geq u_{1}+u_{2}>c

which holds for any
\omega\in[0,1)^{2}
.

Example 3
.

Consider the logarithm function defined as

\displaystyle v:[0,1)^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto\log(1+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})

which is
C^{1}([0,1)^{2})
and bounded by
\log(3)
. Computing we find

J_{v}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{1+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}&\frac{1}{1+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}\end{pmatrix}

and it follows that
D_{\vec{u}}v(\omega)\geq\frac{1}{3}(u_{1}+u_{2})>c
.

From now on, WLOG, we will assume that
||v||_{\infty}\leq 1
and remark that monotonicity implies discontinuities will occur on the boundary of the fundamental domain.

Theorem 1
.

Let
v
be defined as above and consider the family of Schrödinger operators as in (
1
). Then there exists a
C_{0}=C_{0}(v)>0
such that for any
\lambda>0
we have

L(E;\lambda)>\log\lambda-C_{0}\text{ for all }E\in\mathbb{R}.

With
\lambda>0
sufficiently large we can guarantee uniform positivity of the Lyapunov exponent, which is a signature of chaos in a dynamical system.
Moreover, the Lyapunov exponent plays a key role in spectral analysis of ergodic one-dimensional Schrödinger operators, see
[
W
]
. In particular, having positive Lyapunov exponent can be a strong indication of
Anderson Localization
. That is, the family of operators has pure point spectrum with exponentially decaying eigenfunctions. This phenomenon is known to occur for potentials that are sufficiently random and a well studied case is when the potential is generated by iid random variables. In general, the question of being able to achieve localization with less random base dynamics is difficult.

One of the difficulties of achieving localization is being able to achieve uniformly positive Lyapunov exponents. Showing uniform positivity depends on the dynamics and two well known cases lie in different degrees of randomness. The most random case is when the potentials are generated by iid random variables. In this case, uniform positivity was established by Furstenberg
[
Fu
]
for all nonzero couplings. On the other hand, for quasi-periodic potentials, it has been shown that a large class of potentials can achieve uniform positivity for large couplings.

For intermediate randomness much less is known. Our focus is on hyperbolic dynamics. For small coupling constants,
[
CS
]
showed positivity away from the center and edges of the spectrum for deterministic potentials using methods from
[
PF
]
. In the same spectral regime,
[
BS
]
were able to establish a large deviation theorem using methods from
[
PF
]
. This allowed them to achieve Anderson localization for almost every phase. On the other hand, for sufficiently large couplings and sufficiently large hyperbolicity,
[
B
]
established positivity for circle endomorphisms and
[
ZL
]
showed positivity for all energies on expanding toral automorphisms. Moreover,
[
BB
]
established positivity for any
\lambda\neq 0
, as long as one has sufficient hyperbolicity and
C^{1}
non-constant potentials. Positivity for hyperbolic base dynamics was also shown by
[
SS
]
,
[
DK
]
,
[
K
]
. Recently, a more complete answer for hyperbolic base dynamics was given from
[
ADZ
]
. The group is able to show that for
\alpha
-Holder continuous functions, then the set
\mathcal{Z}=\{E\in\mathbb{R}:L(E)=0\}
is discrete. Moreover, there exists a constant
\lambda_{0}(v)
so that
\mathcal{Z}
if finite for all
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
. Lastly, there is an open dense set
\mathcal{O}^{\alpha}\subset C^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R})
such one has uniformly positive Lyapunov exponents for all potentials in
\mathcal{O}^{\alpha}
. For more general information on results of positive Lyapunov exponents we refer to
[

]
.

Returning to the context of our problem, Young
[

]
remarks that if one has a cocyle of the form

[

In our setting, this becomes a battle between the hyperbolic base dynamics represented by eigenvalue
\alpha
and the hyperbolicity of the cocycle represented by the sufficiently large coupling constant
\lambda

The core idea for us to show positive Lyapunov exponents is to reduce the problem to the local unstable leaves. This is done through an application of Fubini’s theorem, which allows us to integrate over
z\in(0,\sqrt{2})
and the
W_{z}
. After reduction, we use the fact that each
W_{z}
has the property that every
T

W_{z}
moves the unstable local leaf to another unstable local leaf and stretches the length by a factor of
\alpha

W_{z}
under

becomes a subset of a union of
W_{j}
for

for an indexing set
\Lambda

. This reduces the 2-dimensional problem to 1-dimension, which allows us to use arguments similar to
[
Z2
]

Moreover, the argument provided for the Cat Map works for any hyperbolic
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
matrix. Let
T
represent any hyperbolic matrix action on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and let

\alpha
with
\beta
for all proofs. The second case is when the unstable leaves become copies of

on
\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

[

]
and replacing

Polar Decomposition of Schrödinger Cocycle

Given a matrix

(1)

Polar decomposition
allows us to write

where
S_{1}\in\mathrm{SO}(2,\mathbb{R})

\sqrt{M^{t}M}

(2)

and
\Lambda

\begin{pmatrix}||M||&0\\

and

By Lemma 10 in
[
Z1
]

C^{1}

This allows for one to study the Lyapunov exponent for the Schrödinger Cocycle of

instead of
M
.

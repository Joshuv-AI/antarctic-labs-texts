The coherent-state transformation in quantum electrodynamics coupled cluster theory
Eric W. Fischer

Abstract
We analyse the coherent-state (CS) transformation in quantum electrodynamics coupled cluster (QED-CC) theory from the perspective of its non-vanishing commutator with the polaritonic cluster operator. Specifically, we show that a QED Hartree-Fock (QED-HF) reference state parametrized by the CS-transformation leads to a QED-CC Lagrangian formally determined by CS-representations of polaritonic Hamiltonian, polaritonic cluster and polaritonic deexcitation operators. This observation augments the original formulation of QED-CC theory where the CS-representation is restricted to the polaritonic Hamiltonian. We consequently find a renormalization of both QED-CC correlation energy and QED-CC ground state induced by the CS-transformation, which depends on the mean-field expectation value of the molecular dipole operator and therefore breaks origin invariance for charged systems. Electronic contributions to correlation energy and QED-CC ground state are renormalized by CS-transformed mixed excitation and deexcitation operators. In contrast, the CS-transformed single-photon excitation affects only the QED-CC ground state but not directly the correlation energy. The fully CS-transformed QED-CC ansatz is well approximated by the original QED-CC formulation for large cavity frequencies leading to small renormalization corrections. However, it exhibits a divergent zero-frequency limit absent in the original QED-CC ansatz for molecules with a non-vanishing molecular dipole moment in agreement with the asymptotic behaviour of the CS-parametrized QED-HF reference state.

II
QED-CC Basics

The QED-CC energy as introduced by Koch and coworkers
haugland2020
is given by

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{cc}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}}\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{H}\hat{U}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad,

(1)

and approximates the adiabatic ground state energy of the polaritonic Hamiltonian in dipole approximation and length gauge representation

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{e}+\hbar\omega_{c}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{b}_{\lambda}-g_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{c}}{2}}\hat{d}_{\lambda}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+\hat{b}_{\lambda})+\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}\hat{d}^{2}_{\lambda}\,.

(2)

The first term is the electronic Hamiltonian,
\hat{H}_{e}
, and the second one corresponds to a (zero-point-energy shifted) cavity Hamiltonian for a single cavity mode with polarization,
\lambda
, and frequency,
\omega_{c}
, given in terms of photonic creation and annihilation operators,
\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda},\hat{b}_{\lambda}
, respectively. The remaining two contributions correspond to light-matter interaction and dipole self-energy (DSE) terms with interaction strength,
g_{0}
, and polarization-projected molecular dipole operator,
\hat{d}_{\lambda}=\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}+\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}
, with electronic and nuclear components,
\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}
and
\hat{d}^{(n)}_{\lambda}
, respectively. The QED-CC wave function ansatz for Eq.(
1
) reads
haugland2020

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi^{\mathrm{qed}}_{\mathrm{cc}}}=e^{\hat{Q}}\ket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad,

(3)

with mean-field reference state,
\ket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}}
, of the
CS-transformed
polaritonic Hamiltonian,
\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{H}\hat{U}_{z}
, which resembles a product state between the HF-determinant,
\ket{\Phi_{0}}
, and the cavity mode vacuum,
\ket{0_{c}}
. The polaritonic cluster operator is given by
haugland2020

\displaystyle\hat{Q}

\displaystyle=\hat{T}+\hat{\Gamma}+\hat{S}\quad,

(4)

with electronic,
\hat{T}
, photonic,
\hat{\Gamma}
and mixed contributions,
\hat{S}
, respectively, which allows to account for both electron and electron-photon correlation in the polaritonic ground state energy not captured on a mean-field level of theory.

III
The CS-Transformation in QED-CC

The CS-transformation enters Eq.(
1
) via the bosonic operator
haugland2020

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{z}=e^{z_{\lambda}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}-\hat{b}_{\lambda})}\quad,\quad z_{\lambda}=\dfrac{g_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\quad,

(5)

which depends on the mean-field dipole expectation value,
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}=\braket{\Phi_{0}|\hat{d}_{\lambda}|\Phi_{0}}
, and exclusively acts on the cavity subspace. The QED-HF reference state for the polaritonic Hamiltonian in Eq.(
2
) is then defined as

\displaystyle\ket{R}=\hat{U}_{z}\ket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad,

(6)

with corresponding QED-HF energy

\displaystyle E_{0}=\braket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}|\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{H}\hat{U}_{z}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad.

(7)

The CS-transformation minimizes
E_{0}
with respect to variations of photonic degrees of freedom and renders the mean-field energy origin-invariant.
haugland2020

By inspecting Eq.(
7
), we may alternatively follow the argument of Ref.
haugland2020
that
\ket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}}
approximates the ground state of the CS-transformed polaritonic Hamiltonian,
\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{H}\hat{U}_{z}
, respectively. Both perspectives are obviously equivalent for the mean-field energy but differ already for other mean-field expectation values, which require the CS-transformation to parametrize the QED-HF reference state for properly transformed operators. We shall now extend this argument to the QED-CC approach and formulate the corresponding polaritonic ground state approximation via the mean-field reference in Eq.(
6
), which leads to

\displaystyle\ket{\tilde{\Psi}^{\mathrm{qed}}_{\mathrm{cc}}}

\displaystyle=e^{\hat{Q}}\hat{U}_{z}\ket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\neq\hat{U}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}}\ket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad.

(8)

On the left-hand side, the exponentiated polaritonic cluster operator acts on the mean-field state in CS-representation in contrast to the right-hand side (
cf.
Eqs.(
1
) and (
3
)), where the order of operators is interchanged. The inequality between both expressions results from the non-vanishing commutator

\displaystyle[e^{\hat{Q}},\hat{U}_{z}]\neq 0\quad,

(9)

between polaritonic cluster and CS-transformation operators. Since the CS-transformation enters the theory via the mean-field ansatz Eq.(
6
), the left-hand side of Eq.(
8
) seems to follow naturally for the QED-CC approach. Moreover, the energy expression resulting from the revised QED-CC ansatz,
\ket{\tilde{\Psi}^{\mathrm{qed}}_{\mathrm{cc}}}
, in Eq.(
8
) is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{cc}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}|\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}e^{-\hat{Q}}\hat{H}e^{\hat{Q}}\hat{U}_{z}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad,

(10)

and accounts for the CS-transformation of
both
polaritonic cluster operator
and
polaritonic Hamiltonian. Thus
all
photonic operators contributing to
\hat{H}
and
\hat{Q}
in Eq.(
10
) are consistently transformed to the CS-picture in contrast to Eq.(
1
), where only photonic operators in the polaritonic Hamiltonian were subject to the CS-transformation neglecting effects of the non-zero commutator in Eq.(
9
).

In the remainder of this work, we will therefore discuss the relevance of the non-vanishing commutator in Eq.(
9
) for QED-CC theory. To this end, we introduce a QED-CC Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{\mathrm{cc}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{cc}}+\lambda_{\nu}\tilde{R}^{\nu}_{\mathrm{cc}}\quad,

(11)

with QED-CC energy,
\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{cc}}
, given in Eq.(
10
) and

\displaystyle\lambda_{\nu}\tilde{R}^{\nu}_{\mathrm{cc}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}|\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{\Lambda}_{Q}e^{-\hat{Q}}\hat{H}e^{\hat{Q}}\hat{U}_{z}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad,

(12)

with polaritonic deexcitation operator,
\hat{\Lambda}_{Q}
(
cf.
Sec.
V
). QED-CC amplitude equations,
\tilde{R}^{\nu}_{\mathrm{cc}}=0
, are obtained via the condition,
\partial_{\lambda_{\nu}}\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{\mathrm{cc}}=0
, with multipliers,
\lambda_{\nu}
, corresponding to excitations of electronic, photonic and mixed character. We assume here and throughout the rest of this work implicit summation over repeated indices.

As an example, we discuss the QED-CCS-1-S1 scheme
haugland2020
;
monzel2024
, which is the simplest QED-CC approximation fully accounting for electron-photon correlation. The corresponding polaritonic cluster operator reads

\displaystyle\hat{Q}=\hat{T}_{1}+\hat{\Gamma}_{1}+\hat{S}^{1}_{1}=t^{a}_{i}\hat{E}_{ai}+\gamma_{\lambda}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+s^{\lambda}_{ai}\hat{E}_{ai}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\quad.

(13)

with single excitations in both electronic (
\hat{T}_{1}
) and photonic (
\hat{\Gamma}_{1}
) subspaces besides the related mixed doubles contribution, (
\hat{S}^{1}_{1}
), with corresponding amplitudes
t^{a}_{i},\gamma_{\lambda}
and
s^{\lambda}_{ai}
, respectively. Electronic excitation operators,
\hat{E}_{ai}
, connect occupied and virtual molecular orbital (MO) subspaces characterized by indices
i,j
and
a,b
, respectively. Note, the second and third term involve contractions over the polarization index,
\lambda
, which can take two values for a degenerate cavity mode.

IV
Energy Renormalization

We rewrite the QED-CC energy in Eq.(
10
) now as

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{cc}}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}_{z}}\hat{H}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}_{z}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\quad,

(14)

with CS-transformed polaritonic Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{z}=\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{H}\hat{U}_{z}=\hat{H}_{e}+\hbar\omega_{c}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\hat{b}_{\lambda}\\
-g_{0}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{c}}{2}}\Delta\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+\hat{b}_{\lambda}\right)+\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2}(\Delta\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda})^{2}\quad,

(15)

where

\displaystyle\Delta\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}=\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}-\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{0}\quad,

(16)

and CS-transformed cluster operator

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{z}

\displaystyle=\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{Q}\hat{U}_{z}=\hat{T}_{1}+\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{\Gamma}_{1}\hat{U}_{z}+\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{S}^{1}_{1}\hat{U}_{z}\quad.

(17)

The electronic single-excitation operator,
\hat{T}_{1}
, is invariant under the purely photonic CS-transformation while photonic and mixed excitation operators transform as

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{\Gamma}_{1}\hat{U}_{z}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\gamma}_{\lambda}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+\dfrac{g_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\right)\quad,\vskip 5.69046pt

(18)

\displaystyle\hat{U}^{\dagger}_{z}\hat{S}^{1}_{1}\hat{U}_{z}

\displaystyle=\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}\hat{E}_{ai}\left(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+\dfrac{g_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\right)\quad.

(19)

We distinguish here photonic and mixed amplitudes in the CS-representation,
\tilde{\gamma}_{\lambda}
and
\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}
, from their original counterparts in Eq.(
13
) due to respectively corrected amplitude equations to be discussed in Sec.
V
. The CS-induced shift of
\hat{S}^{1}_{1}
can be interpreted as
renormalization
of the electronic singles amplitudes

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{ai}

\displaystyle=\tilde{t}^{a}_{i}+\dfrac{g_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}\quad,

(20)

which depend explicitly on the light-matter interaction strength,
g_{0}
, cavity frequency,
\omega_{c}
, molecular mean-field dipole expectation value,
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}
, and mixed cluster amplitudes,
\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}
, respectively. Note, the latter are contracted with
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}
via the polarization index,
\lambda
, and the bare electronic singles amplitudes are written in the renormalized scenario as
\tilde{t}^{a}_{i}
in line with
\tilde{\gamma}_{\lambda}
and
\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}
.

We turn now to the renormalized QED-CC correlation energy, which can be written as

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{corr}}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{corr}}+\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{ren}}\quad,

(21)

with QED-CCS-1-S1 correlation correction

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{corr}}=\dfrac{1}{2}\tilde{t}^{a}_{i}\tilde{t}^{b}_{j}\bar{w}^{ai}_{bj}-g_{0}\sqrt{\dfrac{\hbar\omega_{c}}{2}}\left(\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}+2\tilde{t}^{a}_{i}\tilde{\gamma}_{\lambda}\right)d^{ai}_{\lambda}\quad,

(22)

composed of an electronic and an electron-photon correlation correction. Moreover,
\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{corr}}
contains DSE-augmented antisymmetrized two-electron integrals,
\bar{w}^{ai}_{bj}=\tilde{g}_{aibj}-\tilde{g}_{ajbi}
with
\tilde{g}_{aibj}=g_{aibj}+g^{2}_{0}d^{ai}_{\lambda}d^{bj}_{\lambda}
determined by bare two-electron integrals in Mulliken notation,
g_{aibj}=(ai|bj)
, and polarization-projected transition dipole matrix elements,
d^{ai}_{\lambda},d^{bj}_{\lambda}
, respectively.

The second term in Eq.(
21
) results from Eq.(
20

\tilde{\Delta}_{\mathrm{ren}}=g_{0}\dfrac{\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{2\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\left(\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}\tilde{t}^{b}_{j}+\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{bj}\tilde{t}^{a}_{i}\right)\bar{w}^{ai}_{bj}\vskip 5.69046pt\\
+g^{2}_{0}\dfrac{\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}^{2}_{0}}{4\hbar\omega_{c}}\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{bj}\bar{w}^{ai}_{bj}\\
-g^{2}_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}\tilde{s}^{\lambda}_{ai}\tilde{\gamma}_{\lambda}d^{ai}_{\lambda}\quad,

(23)

where the first two lines stem from the disconnected-doubles contribution (
\tilde{t}_{ai}\tilde{t}_{bj}
) to the electronic correlation energy and the third term results from the mixed disconnected-doubles contribution (
\tilde{t}_{ai}\tilde{\gamma}_{\lambda}
) to the electron-photon correlation energy.

We focused so far on the CS-transformed mixed excitation operator in Eq.(
19
) and its connection to electronic single excitations. The photonic counterpart in Eq.(
18
) acquires in contrast only a constant shift in the CS-representation, which vanishes throughout the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) expansion of the similarity-transformed polaritonic Hamiltonian in CS-representation and consequently does not alter the QED-CC energy. However, the CS-transformation does alter the photonic single-excitation component of the QED-CC state as we shall discuss in the following.

V
State Renormalization

\tilde{\lambda}^{i}_{a},\tilde{s}^{ia}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{a}_{i}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi^{i}_{a},0_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}_{z}}\hat{H}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}_{z}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}=0\quad,\vskip 5.69046pt

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi_{0},1_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}_{z}}\hat{H}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}_{z}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\vskip 5.69046pt

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt+\frac{g_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\braket{\Phi_{0},0_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}_{z}}\hat{H}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}_{z}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}=0\,,\vskip 5.69046pt

\displaystyle\tilde{R}^{\lambda}_{ai}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Phi^{i}_{a},1_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}_{z}}\hat{H}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}_{z}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}\vskip 5.69046pt

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt+\frac{g_{0}\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}}{\sqrt{2\hbar\omega_{c}}}\braket{\Phi^{i}_{a},0_{c}|e^{-\hat{Q}_{z}}\hat{H}_{z}e^{\hat{Q}_{z}}|\Phi_{0},0_{c}}=0\,,

where we introduced singly ecited determinants,

, and one-photon states,

VI
Discussion

We will now discuss additional properties of renormalized QED-CC energy and state expressions in context of the CS-parameter
z_{\lambda}
in
cf.
Eq.(
5
). First and foremost, the molecular mean-field dipole expectation value,
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}
, renders the renormalization correction only relevant for molecules with a non-vanishing permanent dipole moment,
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}\neq 0
, along the cavity-mode polarization,
\lambda
, on a QED-HF level of theory. It furthermore breaks origin-invariance for charged molecules even in absence of a permanent dipole moment where the cavity field couples to the total molecular charge. This observation is in agreement with properties of the converged QED-HF reference state,
\ket{R}
, as already pointed out in Ref.
haugland2020
.

Turning to the polaritonic cluster operator, we have seen that the mixed-doubles contribution,
\hat{S}^{1}_{1}
, acquired a purely electronic single-excitation(dexcitation) correction under the CS-transformation, which can be interpreted as renormalization of the corresponding electronic single excitation operator,
\hat{T}_{1}
. Thus, the resulting QED-CC correlation energy in Eq.(
21
) can be made formally equivalent to its QED-CC expression by replacing
\tilde{t}^{a}_{i}
in Eq.(
22
) with
\tilde{t}^{ai}
. The same holds for electronic and mixed amplitude equations, when one replaces
\tilde{\lambda}^{i}_{a}
with the renormalized
\tilde{\lambda}^{ia}
expression in Eq.(
27
). However, this is not possible for single-photon excitations due to the non-uniform behaviour of the constant shift in Eqs.(
18
) and (
25
), which results in a modified amplitude equation (
30
).

We shall next discuss an apparent connection between the CS-transformation and truncation schemes of the polaritonic cluster operator. We therefore replace the electronic single excitation operator,
\hat{T}_{1}
, by its doubles counterpart,
\hat{T}_{2}
, while keeping
\hat{S}^{1}_{1}
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{1}
, which leads to the QED-CCD-1-S1 scheme with

\hat{Q}=\hat{T}_{2}+\hat{\Gamma}_{1}+\hat{S}^{1}_{1}\\
=\dfrac{1}{4}t^{ab}_{ij}\hat{E}^{a}_{i}\hat{E}^{b}_{j}+\gamma_{\lambda}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}+s^{ai}_{\lambda}\hat{E}^{a}_{i}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\quad.

(32)

The mixed doubles excitation operator,
\hat{S}^{1}_{1}
, acquires in this context “spurious” single-excitation contributions throught the CS-transformation (
cf.
Eq.(
19
)),
i.e.
, the manifold of singly excited determinants becomes relevant although not considered in the intended QED-CC approximation. This observation suggests that a “faithful” truncation of the polaritonic cluster operator should consider only electronic and mixed excitation operators consistent with CS-induced renormalization corrections,
e.g.
,
\hat{T}_{1},\hat{S}^{1}_{1}
and
\hat{T}_{2},\hat{S}^{1}_{2}
with mixed-triples excitations
\hat{S}^{1}_{2}=\frac{1}{4}s^{\lambda}_{aibj}\hat{E}^{a}_{i}\hat{E}^{b}_{j}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{\lambda}
, respectively.

We finally turn to the frequency-dependence of the CS-renormalized energy and state expressions, which we find to be well approximated by the original formulation
haugland2020
in case of large cavity frequencies. In contrast, the low-frequency limit exhibits a diverging behaviour as
z_{\lambda}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega_{c}}}
for
\omega_{c}\to 0
if
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}\neq 0
. This observation is challenged by recent theoretical results of Haugland
et al.
haugland2025
reporting instead on a smooth convergence of the QED-CC energy Eq.(
1
) towards a frequency-independent value as
\omega_{c}\to 0
. The authors rationalized their findings by noting that the electron-photon correlation contribution to the QED-CC energy vanishes in this context in line with the related light-matter interaction term in the polaritonic Hamiltonian. The low-frequency limit was then interpreted as the cavity-frequency-independent cavity Born-Oppenheimer (CBO) regime.
haugland2025

In order to rationalize this discrepancy, we shall first note that the light-matter interaction contribution to Eq.(
1
) vanishes as
\omega_{c}\to 0
, which however does not necessarily correspond to a stationary
correlated
electronic energy in the CBO framework as discussed in Refs.
angelico2023
;
fischer2025
. Furthermore, there is indeed no electron-photon correlation in the CBO ground state problem, which is by construction purely electronic and more importantly “photon-free” due to adiabatic separation of electrons and cavity modes.
flick2017
;
flick2017cbo
;
fischer2023
. In contrast, the (approximate) adiabatic polaritonic ground state exhibits a divergent photon number in the low-frequency limit already on the level of QED-HF theory (
cf.
Appendix
.1
)

\displaystyle\braket{\hat{n}_{\lambda}}_{0}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{g^{2}_{0}}{2\hbar\omega_{c}}\left(\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}_{0}-\braket{\hat{d}^{(e)}_{\lambda}}^{2}_{0}\right)-\dfrac{1}{2}\to\infty\quad,

(33)

which renders a comparison with the CBO regime conceptually difficult.
In addition, for molecular systems with a non-vanishing permanent dipole moment on the mean-field level of theory,
\braket{\hat{d}_{\lambda}}_{0}\neq 0
, the QED-HF reference state,
\ket{R}
, is undefined in the low-frequency limit since all components of the coherent state vanish identically for
\omega_{c}\to 0
(
cf.
Appendix
.2
). From a formal perspective, we should expect a similar divergent behaviour of the QED-CC ansatz for consistency reasons, which is however only guaranteed when we account for the CS-transformation in the reference state (
cf.
Eq.(
8
)). Consequently, the CS-renormalized QED-CC correlation energy diverges as
\omega_{c}\to 0
due to photonic contributions absent in the (up to a ZPE-shift) purely electronic QED-HF energy. We may therefore argue that the convergent energy reported in Ref.

VII
Conclusion

We analysed the coherent-state transformation in QED-CC theory from the perspective of its non-vanishing commutator with the polaritonic cluster operator. Specifically, we showed that for a CS-parametrized QED-HF reference state, the QED-CC Lagrangian is subject to CS-transformed polaritonic Hamiltonian, cluster and deexcitation operators. Consequently, the CS-transformation renormalizes both QED-CC energy and ground state approximation for molecules with permanent dipole moment or non-vanishing total charge. This result augments the original formulation of QED-CC theory, which restricts the CS-transformation to the polaritonic Hamiltonian. We furthermore showed that single-photon excitations behave differently under the CS-transformation compared to electronic and mixed ones such that the fully CS-transformed QED-CC approach cannot formally be rewritten in terms of redefined quantities mimicking the original QED-CC formulation. In addition, the CS-transformation is shown to support only QED-CC truncation schemes where the orders of purely electronic excitation operators and electronic components in mixed excitation operators coincide, while other schemes are subject to “spurious” excited state contributions. We finally analysed the frequency-dependence of the renormalization corrections, which are small for large cavity frequencies such that the original QED-CC ansatz is then presumably a good approximation. In contrast, the low-frequency limit
\omega_{c}\to 0
is characterized by a divergence of the QED-HF reference state for systems with permanent dipole moment, which is only recovered for the fully CS-transformed QED-CC ansatz. A recently reported smooth convergence of the QED-CC energy to a constant low-frequency limit might in this context be understood as consequence of a neglected non-vanishing commutator between CS-transformation and the polaritonic cluster operator.
Since we limited our discussion to the ground state problem, it remains to be analysed how the equation-of-motion formulation of QED-CC theory for excited states is affected by the CS-transformation. Our analysis eventually suggests that renormalization might also affect other correlation methods building upon a mean-field ansatz in CS-representation. We hope this work stimulates further investigations of basic assumptions in
ab initio
polaritonic chemistry and the connection between
ab initio
QED and CBO frameworks.

Introduction

Eccrine spiradenoma is a benign adnexal tumor arising from the eccrine sweat glands of the dermis and superficial subcutaneous tissue. It was first described in 1956 by Kersting and Helwig [1]. A relatively rare pathology, only 50 cases were described in the literature in 2016 [2].

We present a case of eccrine spiradenoma of the nose treated in the stomatology and maxillofacial surgery department of the Mohammed VI University Hospital of Marrakech in Morocco. We report its clinical presentation, its radiological characteristics, its histological diagnosis, its management and its evolution over time.

Presentation of the case

The work has been reported in line with the SCARE 2020 criteria [3].

A 64-year-old patient, with no particular personal and familial medical history, was treated 10 years ago for a nodular lesion of the left wing of nose. He underwent excision of the lesion with reconstruction using a right frontal flap. No document specifying the histological type of this first lesion was presented.

For 5 years, he has had a slowly evolving nodular lesion on the left side of the nose. Which justified his consultation in our department. On examination, it was a nodular lesion of the left wing of the nose, ulcerated in its upper half with exposure of the alar cartilage, slightly painful (Fig. 1). There was no cervical lymphadenopathy.

Ulceronodular lesion of the left wing of the nose.

A facial scan showed a lesion formation of the left wing of nose measuring 2.5 × 2 cm, rounded, well defined, with loss of local substance, without bone lysis (Fig. 2).

CT in bone and parenchymal windows showing the ulcero-nodular lesion of the wing of the left nose.

A biopsy was performed and revealed pigmented skin tissue with a carcinomatous proliferation of monomorphic and basaloid cells, suggestive of pigmented follicular basal cell carcinoma.

The patient then underwent excision of the tumor with margins of 0.5 cm under general anesthesia with orotracheal intubation. There persisted a transfixing loss of substance from the left side of the nose.

Histopathological examination of the surgical specimen showed a well-limited and partially encapsulated dermal tumor proliferation, often nodular, sometimes of trabecular architecture, with sometimes large tumor cells with hyperchromatic rounded nuclei and clarified cytoplasm, sometimes small cells. Basaloids, without abnormal mitoses. This was consistent with a benign eccrine spiradenoma (Fig. 3).

The patient benefited, 3 weeks after excision of the tumor, from a reconstruction with a left nasolabial flap for the reconstruction of the deep plane, a septal cartilage graft for the reconstruction of the wing cartilage and a left frontal flap. For the reconstruction of the surface plan. Weaning of the forehead flap was performed 21 days later (Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6) and the result was satisfactory. There were no postoperative complications.

The surgery was performed by a maxillofacial surgeon who has been practicing as a specialist for around 20 years. The patient was satisfied with his treatment, and did not complain of any complications.

The development has been favorable. No sign of recurrence was observed after 12 months of follow-up (Fig. 7).

Discussion

Eccrine spiradenoma is a relatively rare benign adnexal tumor. It manifests clinically as a nodular, bluish, sensitive and slightly painful lesion. This lesion is unique in 97 % of cases [4], however multiple forms on the trunk and extremities have been described [5]. It can occur at any age, but most often affects young adults and presents no apparent sexual predilection [6].

Radiological exploration is important to assess the local extension of the lesion. The scanner can be carried out as in our case. But we can also carry out an ultrasound which will objectify smooth, lobulated mass in superficial subcutaneous tissue. The mass appears hypoechoic and often has increased vascularity [7]. MRI can also be performed, it will show a T1 hypointense, T2 hyperintense lesion, with homogeneous enhancement after administration of contrast product [8].

The diagnosis of benign eccrine spiradenoma is based on histopathological examination. Macroscopic examination shows a firm, round or ovoid mass, sometimes with small cystic components. Histology demonstrates a population of basaloid cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and sparse cytoplasm, and a larger cell type with abundant pale cytoplasm. The cells are arranged in sheets, cords or trabeculae, with the lighter cells located more centrally [9].

In case of incomplete excision of the tumor, the risk of recurrence is always possible. Since tumors have a long period of evolution, the risk of malignant transformation into malignant eccrine spiradenoma is possible. This malignant transformation is manifested clinically by a rapid increase in the size of the tumor nodule, a change in its color and the occurrence of ulcerations [10]. Histologically, there is an increase in mitoses, foci of necrosis and an extension of tumor cells along the line of the fibrous capsule [11]. Lymph nodes and distant metastases are then possible.

The treatment for eccrine spiradenoma is surgical. Complete surgical excision is the only guarantee of cure, without recurrence.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we report this interesting case of a relatively rare pathology: benign eccrine spiradenoma. As cutaneous tumors are largely dominated by basal cell and squamous cell carcinomas, the possibility, although uncommon, of benign and malignant adnexal tumors must always be kept in mind.

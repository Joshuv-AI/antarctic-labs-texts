A Fractional Model of Abalone Growth using Adomian Decomposition Method
Marliadi Susanto, Nadihah Wahi, Adem Kilicman

Abstract
This study is a modification of the McKendrick equation into a growth model with fractional order to predict the abalone length growth. We have shown that the model is a special case of Taylor’s series after it was analysed using Adomian decomposition method and Caputo fractional derivative. By simulating the series with some fractional orders, the results indicate that the greater the fractional order of the model, the series values generated are greater as well. Moreover, the series that is close to the real data is the one with a fractional order of 0.5 0.5 . Therefore, the growth model with a fractional order provides more accuracy than a classical integer order.

Introduction

Abalone (Haliotis asinina) is one of the key ethno-fauna of West Nusa Tenggara Province and nationally as a marine commodity for export. The presence of this mollusk has played an important role in the coastal community’s economy, not only for local consumption or sale in local markets but also for export to several countries in Asia, Europe, and the United States. Abalone harvesting in the wild has been excessive, leading to a drastic decline in its population, which could threaten the sustainability of the species. This section is a brief review of some key definitions and properties of fractional integrals and derivatives.

Definition 2.1

[
21
]

Let
g:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function. The definition of the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral and derivative of order
\beta
are given as.

I^{\beta}_{a}g(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int^{s}_{a}(s-\xi)^{\beta-1}g(\xi)d\xi

(5)

and

{}^{RL}D^{\beta}_{s}g(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{d^{n}}{ds^{n}}\text{ if }\beta=m\in\mathbb{N},\\
\\
\frac{d^{n}}{ds^{n}}\int^{s}_{0}\frac{(s-\xi)^{n-\beta-1}}{\Gamma(n-\beta)}g(\xi)d\xi\text{ if }m-1<\beta<m,m\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(6)

However, the Equation (7) contradicts with the fundamental theories of derivative due to derivative of the constant function is zero for
\beta\in(m-1,m)
. Therefore, the Definition 2.2 is provided as follows.

Definition 2.2

(
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
)
Let a real function
g
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
. The derivative of a function
g
with order
\beta
is

{}^{C}D^{\beta}_{a}g(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{d^{n}g(s)}{ds^{n}}\text{ if }\beta=m\in\mathbb{N},\\
\\
\int^{s}_{a}\frac{(s-\xi)^{m-\beta-1}}{\Gamma(m-\beta)}g^{(m)}(\xi)d\xi\text{ if }m-1<\beta<m,m\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(7)

Clearly, the Eq.(8) indicates that it does not contradict with fundamental theories of derivative and it is called the Caputo fractional derivative definition. Furthermore, some basic properties are provided as below.

1.

\left(I^{\alpha}_{a}I^{\beta}_{a}g\right)(s)=\left(I^{\beta}_{a}I^{\alpha}_{a}g\right)=\left(I^{\alpha+\beta}g\right)(s)
,

2.

I^{\alpha}_{a}(s-a)^{\gamma}=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\Gamma(\gamma+\alpha+1)}(s-a)^{\gamma+\alpha}
,

3.

\left({I^{\alpha}_{a}}^{C}D^{\alpha}_{a}g\right)(s)=g(s)-\sum\limits^{m-1}_{k=0}g^{(k)}(a)\frac{(s-a)^{k}}{k!}
,

\alpha,\beta>0

a\geq 0

m-1<\alpha<m
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and

Adomian Decomposition Method

This section discusses about the brief technique of the Adomian decomposition method. Generally, the technique is converting the differential equations into a linear operator and a nonlinear operator
[
17
]
,
[
27
]
. To clarify the ADM technique, let us recall the equation below,

\frac{\partial w(s,t)}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{\beta}w(s,t)}{\partial s^{\beta}}+w(s,t)+(w(x,t))^{2}=g(s,t)

(12)

where
n-1<\alpha<n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

By ADM, the equation (12) is written in operator form as follows

L_{t}w+L_{s}w+Nw=g(s,t),

(13)

where the linear operator of
L_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
, and
L_{s}=\frac{\partial^{\beta}}{\partial s^{\beta}}
are invertible and
N(w)
deputizes the non-linear term, that is
N(w)=w(s,t)+(w(s,t))^{2}
.

Then the equation (13) is written as

w=\varphi+L^{-1}_{t}g(s,t)-L^{-1}_{t}(L_{s}w)-L^{-1}_{t}(N(w)),

(14)

with
\varphi
is representing the function arising from initial condition. The nonlinear operator
N(w)
is usually expressed by an infinite series known as the Adomian polynomials,

N(w)=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{n=0}A_{n},

with

A_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d}{d\lambda^{n}}N\left(\sum\limits^{n}_{i=0}\lambda^{i}w_{i}\right)_{\lambda=0}.

(15)

Thus, the solution can be expressed as

w(s,t)=\begin{cases}w_{0}(s,t)=\varphi,\\
w_{n+1}(s,t)=L^{-1}_{t}g(s,t)-L^{-1}_{t}\left[L_{s}(w_{n})\right]-L^{-1}_{t}\left(A_{n}\right)\end{cases}.

(16)

4
A Growth Model with Fractional Order

This part presents a new growth model with fractional order as a modification of the McKendrick equation. Here, we show that the model analysed using ADM provides a particular case of Taylor’s series, to predict the growth of a species size. For more detail, given
w(s,t)
represents the density of population and
\eta
denotes the growth rate. Since the previous model is of first order, we introduce a new model with a fractional-order
\beta
, where
\beta\in(0,1]
, as follows:

\frac{\partial w(s,t)}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial^{\beta}w(s,t)}{\partial s^{\beta}}=\eta w(s,t),

(17)

with the initial condition

w(s,0)=Me^{rs},

where
M
is the initial number of individuals and
r
denoting the initial rate of growth. To apply the ADM, we deliver the Eq.(17) in operator form as follows

L_{t}w+L_{s}w(s,t)=\eta w

(18)

where
L
is a linear operator invertible. By setting,

w=\sum\limits^{\infty}_{n=0}w_{n},

and
w_{0}=Me^{rs}
, we obtain

w(s,t)=\begin{cases}w_{0}(s,t)=Me^{rs},\\
w_{n+1}(s,t)=-L^{-1}_{t}\left[L_{s}(w_{n})\right]+\eta L^{-1}[w_{n}]\end{cases}.

(19)

Using the Caputo fractional derivative, the running iteration of Eq.(19) provides

w_{1}(s,t)=-L^{-1}_{t}\left[L_{s}(w_{0})\right]+\eta L^{-1}[w_{0}]=(\eta-r^{\beta})Me^{rs}t.

(20)

The same manner, we find that

w_{2}(s,t)=(\eta-r^{\beta})^{2}Me^{rs}\frac{t^{2}}{2!},

(21)

w_{3}(s,t)=(\eta-r^{\beta})^{3}Me^{rs}\frac{t^{3}}{3!},

(22)

and the proceeding further the
w_{n}(s,t)
is expressed by

w_{n}(s,t)=(\eta-r^{\beta})^{n}Me^{rs}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

(23)

Therefore, the solution of Eq.(15) is

w(s,t)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum\limits^{m}_{n=0}w_{n}(s,t)=Me^{rs}e^{(\eta-r^{\beta})t}.

(24)

Then the solution will be utilised to predict the abalone growth in the following section.

6
Conclusion

This research showed that modifying the McKendrick model into a new growth model with fractional order, using an exponential function as the initial condition, can accurately predict the abalone length growth. The new model was analysed using the Adomian decomposition method, resulting to a Taylor series. By substituting the parameter values of this series which were obtained from real data and simulated with various fractional orders, the fractional order
\beta=0.5
provides results that best reflects the real data, compared to the other orders, including integer orders. For future research, the model can be modified by involving some variables such as competition interaction, diseases, and temperature.

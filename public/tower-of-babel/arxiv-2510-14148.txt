Abstract
We continue studying regularization scheme dependence of the 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 supersymmetric sigma models. In the present work the previous result for the four loop β \beta -function is extended to the five loop order. Namely, we find the renormalization scheme, in which the fifth loop contribution is completely eliminated, while the fourth loop contribution is represented by the certain invariant, which is coordinate independent for the metrics of some models. These models include complete T T -duals of the η \eta -deformed S ​ U ​ ( n ) / U ⁡ ( n − 1 ) SU(n)/U(n-1) models, as well as η \eta - and λ \lambda -deformed S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) / U ⁡ ( 1 ) SU(2)/U(1) models, whose metrics solve the RG flow equation up to the fifth loop order. We also comment on the λ \lambda -deformed S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) / U ⁡ ( 1 ) SU(2)/U(1) and S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) / U ⁡ ( 2 ) SU(3)/U(2) case, showing that they satisfy five-loop RG flow equation, and discuss their Kähler structure.

Introduction

In this work, we study two-dimensional sigma models and their renormalization group (RG) flow. The sigma model is a theory in which fields take the values on a (pseudo-)Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
which is called the target space

X:\Sigma\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\;.

(1)

where
\Sigma
stands for
2
-dimensional spacetime. The action of such models can be written as

S[X]=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int\left(G_{ij}(X)\,\partial_{a}X^{i}\partial_{b}X^{j}h^{ab}\sqrt{|h|}+B_{ij}(X)\,\epsilon^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{i}\partial_{b}X^{j}\right)d^{2}x

(2)

where
X=(X^{1}(x),\ldots,X^{D}(x))
represents a set of bosonic fields corresponding to target space coordinates,
G_{ij}(X)
is the target space metric,
B_{ij}(X)
is the so-called antisymmetric
B
-field and
h_{ab}
is the spacetime metric, which will be
\delta_{ab}
in what follows. The target space metric depends on parameters interpreted as coupling constants.

If we restrict ourselves to the case
B_{ij}=0
, i.e. of the zero
B
-field, in its most general form, the renormalization group (RG) flow equation for sigma models can be written as
Ecker:1971xko
;
Fradkin:1985ys

\dot{G}_{ij}+\nabla_{i}V_{j}+\nabla_{j}V_{i}=-\beta_{ij}(G)

(3)

where the dot denotes differentiation with respect to the RG time
t=-\log\Lambda
, and
\Lambda
is the renormalization scale. The metric is assumed to depend on RG time through the coupling constants. Note that the
\beta
-function appears with a negative sign on the right-hand side. This sign convention can be altered by redefining the sign of the RG time. The vector field
V
corresponds to coordinate transformations in the target space. Its presence introduces ambiguity in the definition of the
\beta
-function.

The present work is the continuation of previous work made by one of the authors
Alfimov:2021sir
;
Alfimov:2023evq
, which studies the flow of the renormalization group of the two-dimensional integrable sigma models. The main motivation for this study lies in the building of the so-called dual description of these sigma models in terms of the Toda type theories, i.e. the theories determined by the certain set of screening charges.

In previous years, there has been significant progress in this direction. Starting from the renowned article
Fateev:1992tk
, where such a description was obtained for the case of the
\eta
-deformed
O(3)

-deformed

\eta
-deformed

The existence of the dual description mentioned above is based on the key fact that there exist several equivalent systems of screening charges depending on the continuous parameter

0

Despite the one-loop RG flow of the deformed integrable sigma
models mentioned above is relatively well studied, not so much is known about the higher loop corrections in the bosonic case. At two and three loop order this question was studied in

\beta

In the previous work by one of the authors
Alfimov:2023evq

\beta

The aim of this paper is to extend the result of

\displaystyle=-c_{1}^{2}\;,\quad c_{3}=-\frac{2c_{1}^{3}}{3}+\frac{5\zeta(3)}{48}\;,\quad c_{4}=-\frac{c_{1}^{3}}{3}+\frac{\zeta(3)}{48}\;,\quad c_{5}=-\frac{4c_{1}^{4}}{3}\;,

(34)

\displaystyle c_{6}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4c_{1}^{4}}{3}+\frac{5\zeta(3)}{6}c_{1}\;,\quad c_{7}=-c_{1}^{4}\;,\quad c_{8}=-\frac{4c_{1}^{4}}{3}+\frac{5\zeta(3)}{6}c_{1}\;,

\displaystyle c_{9}

\displaystyle=-\frac{c_{1}^{4}}{3}+\frac{\zeta(3)}{12}c_{1}\;,\quad c_{10}=\frac{3\zeta(4)}{10\zeta(3)},\quad c_{11}=2c_{1},

then for the potential
\tilde{K}
the RG flow equation takes the form (

). Let us note that if we confine ourselves to the case of

target space, then the

) in the following way

\displaystyle b_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{5}}{8}+\frac{\zeta(3)}{48}c_{10}\;,\quad b_{6}=\frac{c_{6}}{8}+\frac{c_{7}}{4}\;,\quad b_{7}=\frac{c_{8}}{8}\;,\quad b_{8}=\frac{c_{9}}{4}

(35)

In this way we obtain a regularization scheme in which the fifth loop contribution to the
\beta
–function vanishes and the RG flow equation assumes the form (
31
). Thus, by an appropriate redefinition of the Kähler potential we can remove the fifth loop term, while the coefficient
c_{1}
remains arbitrary, reflecting the residual freedom in the choice of scheme.

The main result of the present section is the following. As we found the scheme in which the 5th loop contribution to the
\beta
-function is strictly zero, we can directly extend the statement from
Alfimov:2023evq
to the 5th loop order. Namely, the
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric sausage model, its
T
-dual and complete
T
-duals to the
\eta
-deformed
\mathbb{CP}^{N}
models for

, that were shown to be Kähler in

are automatically solutions to the RG flow equation up to the 5th loop order and do not receive any quantum corrections beyond one loop order.

In addition, as it was checked in
Alfimov:2023evq
, the
\lambda
-deformed
SU(2)/U(1)
model is such a solution as well because it is automatically Kähler being two-dimensional. But for the
\lambda
-deformed
SU(n)/U(n-1)
models with
n\geq 3
it was not proven yet that they are Kähler. In the next section we are going to address this question and make some steps toward that proof.

3
Metrics of the
\lambda
-deformed
SU(n)/U(n-1)
sigma models

A well-known example of integrable field theories are sigma models, whose target space is chosen as a deformed homogenous space
G/H

Klimcik:2008eq
;
Delduc:2013fga
. In this section we will focus on the case of
SU(n)/U(n-1)
symmetric space.

The
\lambda
-deformation of the gauged WZW model can be derived from the
G/G
model by introducing a
\lambda
term, which reduces the gauge symmetry group to
H
. Let us start from refreshing the definition of such models.
The algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\text{Lie}(G)
is decomposed into direct sum

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{f}\;,

(36)

where
\mathfrak{h}=\text{Lie}(H)
,
\mathfrak{f}
is orthogonal complement,
which satisfies the following relations

[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]\subset\mathfrak{h}\,,\quad[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{f}]\subset\mathfrak{f}\,,\quad[\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f}]\subset\mathfrak{h}\,.

(37)

The classical Lagrangian of
\lambda
-deformed
G/H
sigma model is given by
Sfetsos:2013wia
;
Hollowood:2014rla

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\int d^{2}x\,\text{Tr}\left(J_{+}A_{-}-A_{+}K_{-}+g^{-1}A_{+}gA_{-}-A_{+}A_{-}+(1-\lambda^{-1}A_{+}\mathbb{P}A_{-})\right)+

(38)

\displaystyle+S_{PCM}+S_{WZ}\,,\quad g\in G\,,\;A_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{g}\;,

where
S_{PCM}
and
S_{WZ}
actions from the principal chiral field model and the Wess-Zumino term

S_{PCM}=\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\int d^{2}x\,\text{Tr}\left(-\frac{1}{2}J_{+}\,J_{-}\right)\,,\quad S_{WZ}=\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\int d^{3}x\,\frac{1}{6}\,\text{Tr}\left(J\wedge J\wedge J\right)\;,

(39)

whereas
J
and
K
are Lie algebra-valued currents (left and right Maurer-Cartan forms)

J=g^{-1}\,dg\in\mathfrak{g}\,,\quad K=dg\,g^{-1}\in\mathfrak{g}\;,

(40)

and
\mathbb{P}
is the projector on
\mathfrak{f}
,
\lambda
is the deformation parameter. The gauge symmetry acts as follows

g\rightarrow h^{-1}gh\;,\quad A_{\pm}\rightarrow h^{-1}A_{\pm}h+h^{-1}\partial_{\pm}h\;,\quad h(x)\in U(n-1)\;.

(41)

After integrating out the auxiliary fields
(A,\bar{A})
the action takes the form

S=S_{PCM}+S_{WZ}+\frac{1}{2\pi\hbar}\int d^{2}x\,\text{Tr}\left(J\frac{1}{\textrm{Ad}_{g}-1+(1-\lambda^{-1})\mathbb{P}}\,\bar{K}\right)\;,

(42)

As a result of integrating the gauge field out, the gauge is fixed, and the group element can be represented by an element of the gauge action orbit. Therefore the next thing we have to do is to find convenient parametrization of the group
SU(n)
and fix the gauge.

Thus, once we have the action of the sigma model (
42
) and the metric on its target space manifold, to check, whether it satisfies the RG flow equation (
31
), we need two components: first, we have to check that
\Delta\tilde{K}
(
18
) does not depend on coordinates and, second, ensure that the corresponding metric is Kähler to guarantee the
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry of the sigma model.

As it was said, we have to introduce some parametrization of the
SU(n)
group. There exists the answer to this question based on the generalized Euler angles
Tilma:2002ke
;
Tilma:2002kf
;
Bertini:2005rc
. However, explicit calculation of the action (
42
) for large
n
is computationally non-trivial as we have to invert a large matrix of the operator
\textrm{Ad}_{g}-1+(1-\lambda^{-1})\mathbb{P}
and even for
n=4
it appeared to be very hard to accomplish using the generalized Euler angle parametrization.

Let us recall, that the metrics we are dealing with are presumably Kähler, which implies the existence of an almost complex structure and the corresponding holomorphic coordinates. Thus, we can exploit the following idea: find this structure and then use the parametrization connected to this structure as it was done in
Bykov:2020llx
. To prove that the target space is Kähler, one must at least show the presence of an almost complex structure. An almost complex structure must satisfy the property that its commutator with the Riemann tensor is trivial

[R(X,Y),J]=0\;,

(43)

where
X
and
Y
are some arbitrary vector fields. This condition represents a system of equations for the components of the almost complex structure
J
. Among the solutions found for the system, we are interested in those that satisfy the condition

J^{2}=-\textrm{Id}\;.

(44)

To start with, we study the simplest
n=2
and
n=3
cases.

3.1
n=2
case

In the case of
SU(2)/U(1)
, the parametrization of the coset element
g
can be chosen as follows

g=\exp(i\alpha\sigma_{3})\exp(i\beta\sigma_{2})\;,

(45)

where
\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
are the standard Pauli matrices. Then after substituting (
45
) into the action we obtain the expression for the metric in the form
Hoare:2019ark
;
Hoare:2019mcc

ds^{2}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\frac{\kappa\,dp^{2}+\kappa^{-1}\,dq^{2}}{1-p^{2}-q^{2}}\,,

(46)

where the following coordinate and parameter transformation was used

\cos\beta=\sqrt{p^{2}+q^{2}}\,,\quad\tan\alpha=\frac{p}{q}\,,\quad\kappa=\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda}\;.

(47)

Substituting (
46
) into (
18
), we get

\Delta\tilde{K}=-\zeta(3)\,\hbar^{3}\,\frac{\,\lambda^{2}(1+\lambda^{2})}{(\lambda^{2}-1)^{3}}=\frac{\zeta(3)}{32}\hbar^{3}\left(\kappa-\kappa^{-1}\right)^{2}\left(\kappa+\kappa^{-1}\right)\,.

(48)

Computing the commutator for the metric (
46
) and imposing (
43
) yields the following relations between the components of the almost complex structure

{J^{1}}_{1}={J^{2}}_{2}\,,\quad{J^{2}}_{1}=-\frac{(\lambda-1)^{2}}{(\lambda+1)^{2}}J^{1}_{2}=-\kappa^{2}{J^{1}}_{2}\,.

(49)

After applying the condition (
44
), the matrix of the complex structure will take the form

||{J^{a}}_{b}||=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\kappa}\\
-\kappa&0\end{pmatrix}\;.

(50)

Obviously, the eigenvalues of the matrix (
50
) are
\pm i
, while its eigenvectors define the holomorphic coordinate

z=p\sqrt{\kappa}+\frac{iq}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\;.

(51)

As is well known, every compact real two-dimensional manifold is Kähler.
Consequently, a Kähler potential can always be introduced. For the
\eta
-deformed model such a potential is already known
Bykov:2020llx
. Here we present an explicit expression for the potential of the
\lambda
-deformed
SU(2)/U(1)
model.

In the fully deformed limit
\kappa=1
the problem is straightforward: the Kähler potential is given by

K(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\text{Li}_{2}\left(|z|^{2}\right)+f(z)+g(\bar{z})\,,

(52)

where
f(z)
and
g(\bar{z})
are arbitrary holomorphic and antiholomorphic functions.

For the case
0<\kappa<1
to determine the Kähler potential we have to find some solution of the equation

\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}K(z,\bar{z})=G_{z\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{4\kappa}(z+\bar{z})^{2}+4\kappa(z-\bar{z})^{2}}\;.

(53)

The simplest way to find the potential is to introduce elliptical coordinates. When
0<\kappa<1
we can pass to the coordinates

z=i\sqrt{\frac{1}{\kappa}-\kappa}\cosh(\mu-i\nu)\;,

(54)

then in terms of the coordinates
\mu
and
\nu
the equation takes the form

\frac{2}{\left(\frac{1}{\kappa}-\kappa\right)\,(\cosh 2\mu-\sinh 2\nu)}\left(\partial^{2}_{\mu}+\partial^{2}_{\nu}\right)K(\mu,\nu)=F(\mu,\nu)\;,

(55)

where

F(\mu,\nu)=\frac{16}{\hbar}\frac{\kappa^{2}}{\left(\left(\kappa^{2}-1\right)\cosh 2\mu+\kappa^{2}+1\right)\left(\left(\kappa^{2}-1\right)\cos 2\nu+\kappa^{2}+1\right)}\,.

(56)

From this we see that the function on the right–hand side can be written as the sum of two functions depending only on
\mu
and
\nu
, respectively.

\left(\partial^{2}_{\mu}+\partial^{2}_{\nu}\right)\,K(\mu,\nu)=\frac{8}{\hbar}\,\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\frac{1+\kappa^{2}}{1-\kappa^{2}}-\cosh 2\mu}-\frac{1}{\frac{1+\kappa^{2}}{1-\kappa^{2}}-\cos 2\nu}\right)\,.

(57)

Therefore we can look for a solution in the form of a sum of two functions, each depending on the single argument

K(\mu,\nu)=K_{(1)}(\mu)+K_{(2)}(\nu)\;.

(58)

Thus, substituting (
58
) into the equation for the potential (
57
), we obtain that the problem is divided into solving two independent equations for the functions
K_{(1)}(\mu)
and
K_{(2)}(\nu)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\mu^{2}}K_{(1)}(\mu)=-\frac{8}{\hbar}\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa^{2}}\frac{1}{\frac{1+\kappa^{2}}{1-\kappa^{2}}-\cosh 2\mu}\;,

(59)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d\nu^{2}}K_{(2)}(\nu)=\frac{8}{\hbar}\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa^{2}}\frac{1}{\frac{1+\kappa^{2}}{1-\kappa^{2}}-\cos 2\nu}\;.

Integrating, we choose the following solutions

\displaystyle K_{(1)}(\mu)=\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\text{Li}_{2}\left(\lambda e^{2\mu}\right)-\text{Li}_{2}\left(\lambda^{-1}e^{2\mu}\right)\right)\;,

(60)

\displaystyle K_{(2)}(\nu)=-\frac{1}{\hbar}\left(\text{Li}_{2}\left(\lambda e^{2i\nu}\right)-\text{Li}_{2}\left(\lambda^{-1}e^{2i\nu}\right)\right)\,.

These expressions are defined only up to additive terms linear in
\mu
or
\nu
and an overall constant, which we henceforth suppress.

The resulting potentials
K_{(1)}(\mu)
and
K_{(2)}(\nu)
closely resemble the Kähler potential of the so-called “sausage” model – the
\eta
-deformed
SU(2)/U(1)
sigma model. According to

, this potential can be written as

(61)

A Legendre transformation of (

(62)

Identifying coordinates and deformation parameters

(63)

and, respectively,

s=\tau\;,\quad t=2i\nu\;,

(64)

we obtain the potentials (

4
Conclusions and outlook

In this work, we have demonstrated that for
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric deformed two-dimensional sigma models it is possible to choose the regularization scheme that eliminates five-loop contribution to the
\beta
-function for certain models. This became possible due to the existence of the invariant
\Delta\tilde{K}
(
18
), which does not depend on the coordinates of the target space after substituting the metric of the considered models into it. Namely, we found the set of parameters of the covariant metric redefinition (
34
), which relates the minimal subtraction scheme to the scheme used by us. In this RG scheme the fifth loop contribution is completely eliminated, which can be considered as extension of the result of
Alfimov:2023evq
to the fifth loop order.

We considered two types of models, which provide solutions to the RG flow equation in the renormalization scheme we found. First, we took the so-called complete
T

For the

Targeting C5AR1 disrupts complement-driven G0-phase maintenance and overcomes metabolic drug resistance in glioma
Authors: Yu Qian, Kai Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGlioblastoma (GBM) relapse and drug resistance are driven by a subset of quiescent, therapy-tolerant cells that persist in the G0 phase. However, the molecular mechanism coupling immune signaling to tumor cell quiescence and metabolic adaptation remains unclear.MethodsMulti-omics analyses of TCGA, CGGA, and GEO datasets, combined with single-cell transcriptomics and in vitro/in vivo experiments, were used to identify complement-related regulators of G0 maintenance. Genetic manipulation (shRNA/overexpression), pharmacological inhibition (C5AR1 antagonist JPE1375; STAT3 inhibitor Stattic), and rescue experiments with recombinant C3 were performed in glioma cell lines and xenograft models. Cell-cycle, mitochondrial, and redox states were assessed by flow cytometry, immunofluorescence, MitoTracker/MitoSOX staining, and ELISA.ResultsC5AR1 expression was markedly upregulated in GBM and correlated with poor prognosis (HR = 2.7, p = 6.4 × 10−14). Single-cell and functional analyses revealed that C5AR1, activated by complement C3, sustains a spontaneous G0 population through JAK2/STAT3/STAT5 signaling. This axis enhanced mitochondrial integrity and oxidative phosphorylation, maintained low ROS homeostasis, and preserved stem-like features conferring chemoresistance. Knockdown or pharmacological blockade of C5AR1 disrupted G0-phase maintenance, decreased mitochondrial activity, and suppressed tumor growth in xenografts. C5AR1 inhibition also sensitized glioma cells to metabolic drugs by reversing rosiglitazone resistance and enhancing metformin efficacy.ConclusionsC5AR1 links extracellular complement activation to intracellular G0-phase maintenance and metabolic resilience in glioma. Targeting the C3-C5-C5AR1 axis disrupts quiescence-driven drug tolerance and represents a promising therapeutic strategy for overcoming chemoresistance in GBM.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-07782-2.Keywords: Glioblastoma, G0 phase, C5AR1, Complement system, Drug resistance, Quiescence, STAT3/STAT5 signaling

Introduction

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive primary malignant tumor of the central nervous system, accounting for over 50% of all gliomas [1, 2]. Current standard treatment—including surgical resection, radiotherapy, and temozolomide (TMZ) chemotherapy—has limited efficacy. The median survival of GBM patients remains below 15 months, and most experience rapid relapse after initial therapy [3, 4]. Therapeutic failure mainly results from drug resistance and tumor relapse [5]. These processes are driven by aberrant DNA repair, epigenetic changes, metabolic reprogramming, and disrupted cell-cycle control [4, 6]. However, the key cellular states contributing to resistance and their regulatory mechanisms remain poorly understood.

Emerging evidence suggests that tumor cells evade chemotherapy by entering a quiescent (G0) state without compromising overall viability [7, 8]. G0-phase cells are typically characterized by low proliferative and metabolic activity but can rapidly re-enter the cell cycle upon stimulation, serving as a reservoir for tumor recurrence and progression [9]. G0-phase quiescence underlies therapy resistance by reducing sensitivity to DNA damage [10] and sustaining stemness through ROS homeostasis [11]. Notably, the maintenance of quiescence is intimately linked to specific metabolic adaptations, particularly mitochondrial function, which provides the energy and biosynthetic precursors necessary for survival and eventual regrowth. Through integrative analyses of TCGA and GEO datasets, we identified C5AR1 as a pivotal molecule upregulated in GBM samples with high ESTIMATE scores and associated with poor prognosis. Single-cell annotation further revealed high enrichment of C5AR1 in monocytes within the TME, with substantial expression also detected in tumor cell populations. Based on the known association of G0-phase maintenance with chemoresistance, we hypothesized that C5AR1 might regulate tumor cell-cycle states, particularly G0-phase maintenance, via complement signaling, potentially through modulation of mitochondrial function, thereby conferring stemness and drug resistance.

The complement system, a pivotal component of innate immunity, has recently been recognized as a critical driver of tumor progression by shaping the tumor microenvironment (TME), mainly through modulating immune cell infiltration, promoting inflammatory responses, and facilitating immune evasion [12–14]. Classically, complement activation occurs via the classical, lectin, and alternative pathways, converging on C3 and C5 cleavage to produce the potent effectors C3a and C5a that signal through C3aR and C5AR1 [15]. Dysregulated complement activity, beyond its immune defense role, contributes to chronic inflammation and tumor progression across multiple cancers [16]. In gliomas, recent evidence shows that complement-pathway components such as C3a/C3aR are markedly upregulated in grade 4 diffuse gliomas—particularly GBM—with high expression in tumor-associated macrophages and perivascular regions [17]. However, whether complement signaling directly regulates tumor cell quiescence and contributes to chemoresistance has not been explored in gliomas. Its intrinsic roles within tumor cells remain largely unclear. Complement C5a receptor 1 (C5AR1), a classical receptor for C5a, is abundantly expressed in immune cells such as monocytes and macrophages and has been associated with poor prognosis in various cancers [18]. Previous studies have mainly focused on its tumor-promoting effects through inflammation and immune cell polarization [19–21], whereas its direct involvement in tumor cell proliferation, cell-cycle regulation, and metabolic reprogramming has not been fully elucidated. A critical knowledge gap exists regarding whether and how C5AR1 signaling directly influences tumor cell quiescence and mitochondrial function to confer chemoresistance.

In this study, we systematically characterized the expression profile of C5AR1 in GBM and investigated its functional role in G0-phase regulation, mitochondrial metabolism, and chemoresistance. Using combined bioinformatics analyses and in vitro/in vivo experiments, we demonstrated that C5AR1, activated in a complement C3-dependent manner, promotes mitochondrial function through the JAK2/STAT3/STAT5 signaling pathway, sustaining a spontaneous G0-phase population and enhancing stem-like and drug-resistant phenotypes. Moreover, we explored the therapeutic potential of targeting C5AR1 in combination with metabolic inhibitors. Our findings provide new insights into the mechanisms of GBM chemoresistance and support C5AR1 as a promising target for precision therapy.

Methods

Public databases and dataset acquisition

Transcriptomic data and clinical follow-up information of glioma patients were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) (LGG+GBM) and the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA). The GSE184941 dataset (glioma samples stratified by ESTIMATE scores) and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from the Chinese Cancer Genome Atlas (CCGA) were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. All datasets were normalized, and samples lacking clinical information or with follow-up durations shorter than 30 days were excluded to ensure analytical reliability. For additional analyses, GEO datasets GSE16011 and GSE66354 were downloaded and used to validate the expression levels of C5AR1 across different glioma grades and between tumor and normal tissues. Prognostic analyses were further conducted using three independent CGGA datasets (mRNA-array_301, mRNA-array_325, and mRNA-array_693) to confirm the stability of C5AR1-associated survival patterns in distinct clinical cohorts.

ESTIMATE scoring and differential gene expression analysis

The R package ESTIMATE (Estimation of STromal and Immune cells in MAlignant Tumor tissues using Expression data) was used to calculate StromalScore, ImmuneScore, ESTIMATEscore, and TumorPurity, which respectively reflected stromal components, immune cell infiltration, and tumor purity. Samples were stratified into high- and low-score groups based on median ESTIMATEscore. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the limma package (criteria: |log2FC| > 1 and FDR < 0.05).

Stepwise regression and prognostic analysis

DEGs significantly enriched in the high-score group were subjected to stepwise regression modeling to identify key genes associated with ESTIMATEscore. Cox proportional hazards models were constructed using the survival R package. The relationships between candidate genes and overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free survival (PFS) were evaluated using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank tests.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis

scRNA-seq data from the CCGA were processed using Seurat (v4.3.0) for normalization and UMAP dimensionality reduction. Cells with fewer than 200 detected genes, more than 6000 genes, or >10% mitochondrial gene expression were excluded to remove low-quality cells and potential doublets. Genes expressed in fewer than three cells were also filtered out. The remaining high-quality cells were normalized using the SCTransform method to correct for sequencing depth and technical variability. Cell types were annotated based on established markers, including monocytes, astrocytes, T cells, neural stem cells, and microglia. The expression of C5AR1 across different cell types and in high- versus low-spontaneous G0-scoring states was assessed and visualized with violin and bubble plots.

Cell-cell communication analysis

Cell-cell communication was inferred using CellChat (v1.6.1) to compare high- and low-spontaneous G0-scoring groups. Chord diagrams and heatmaps were generated to visualize key ligand-receptor pairs, such as C3-(ITGAM + ITGB2), across cell types. Communication probabilities were evaluated using permutation tests, with p < 0.05 considered significant.

PPI network construction and complement pathway analysis

Protein–protein interaction (PPI) networks of C5AR1-associated proteins were constructed using the STRING database and visualized in Cytoscape. Pathway enrichment analyses of complement signaling, cell-cycle regulation, and mitochondrial function were performed using Metascape, and correlations between C5AR1 and mitochondrial metabolic genes were assessed.

Drug sensitivity analysis

Half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) data were retrieved from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) and Cancer Therapeutics Response Portal (CTRP) databases. Spearman correlation analyses were performed to evaluate the association of C5AR1 expression with IC50 values of different candidates. The relationships among C5AR1 expression, spontaneous G0 scores, and drug IC50 values were visualized using Sankey plots generated with the ggalluvial package.

Cell lines and cell culture

Human glioma cell lines MO3.13, LN299, A172, U251, LN229, and U87-MG were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) high-glucose medium (Gibco, 11965092) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Thermo Scientific, A5256701) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin (Gibco, 15140122) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂.

Gene manipulation and validation

C5AR1 and C3 knockdown was achieved by lentiviral transduction of shRNA constructs, whereas C5AR1 overexpression was induced using the pcDNA3.1-C5AR1 plasmid. Transfection efficiency was assessed 48 h post-transfection by qPCR and Western blot using anti-C5AR1 (Proteintech, 21316-1-AP) and anti-GAPDH (Proteintech, 60004-1-Ig) antibodies.

Drug treatments

The C5AR1 antagonist JPE1375 (MCE, HY-148141) was dissolved in DMSO (MCE, HY-Y0320C) and applied at 0–30 μM in gradient concentrations. The STAT3 inhibitor Stattic (MCE, HY-13818) was used at a final concentration of 10 μM. Cells were harvested after 24–72 h of treatment for subsequent functional assays.

Cell viability and proliferation assays

Cell viability was determined using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) (MCE, HY-K0301). Briefly, 5 × 103 cells per well were seeded, incubated with CCK-8 reagent for 2 h, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm. CFDA-SE staining (MCE, HY-D0938) was performed to evaluate cell proliferation, and fluorescence intensity was measured by flow cytometry. For the colony formation assay, 500 cells per well were seeded and cultured for 10–14 days, followed by crystal violet staining and colony counting.

Migration assay

Cell migration was evaluated using uncoated Transwell chambers (8-µm pore size, Corning, 3422). A total of 1 × 105 cells were seeded into the upper chamber and incubated for 24 h. After fixation and crystal violet staining, cells in five randomly selected fields were counted under a microscope.

Cell death and cell-cycle analysis

Cell death and necrosis were analyzed using FITC/PI dual staining (Yeasen, 40302ES50) followed by flow cytometry. PI fluorescence staining (MCE, HY-D0815) was used to detect necrotic cells, and acridine orange staining (MCE, HY-101879) was used to identify dead cells. Cell-cycle distribution (G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases) was assessed by flow cytometry using the PI/RNase staining method.

Mitochondrial function assays

Mitochondrial activity was assessed with MitoTracker Red (Yeasen, 40740ES50), and mitochondrial morphology was visualized using MitoTracker Green (Yeasen, 40742ES50). The proportions of intact (Inter) and fragmented (Frag) mitochondria were quantified. Mitochondrial ROS levels were measured using MitoSOX (Yeasen, 40778ES50).

Downstream signaling and complement level detection

Western blot was performed to detect protein levels of p-JAK2, JAK2, p-STAT3, STAT3, p-STAT5, and STAT5 using the following antibodies: anti-p-JAK2 (Cell Signaling, 3771), anti-JAK2 (Proteintech, 17670-1-AP), anti-p-STAT3 (Proteintech, 60479-1-Ig), anti-STAT3 (Proteintech, 10253-2-AP), anti-p-STAT5 (Proteintech, 28949-1-AP), and anti-STAT5 (Proteintech, 13179-1-AP). Complement levels of C3 (Abcam, ab263884), C3a (Hycult Biotech, NBP2-70037), C5 (Abnova, KA2114), and C5a (Bio-Techne, DY2037) were measured in cell culture supernatants or tumor lysates using ELISA kits following the manufacturers’ protocols. The sensitivity limits of the ELISA kits, as provided by the manufacturers, were as follows: C3 (Abcam, ab263884): 25.52 pg/mL; C3a (Hycult Biotech, NBP2-70037): 31.3 pg/mL; C5 (Abnova, KA2114): 53 pg/mL; C5a (Bio-Techne, DY2037): 31.2 pg/mL. Rescue Experiment: U251 cells transduced with shC5AR1 were treated with recombinant human complement C3 protein (Sigma-Aldrich, 204885) at a final concentration of 10 μg/mL for 24 hours. Control groups received equal volumes of vehicle (PBS).

Animal experiments

BALB/c nude mice (4–6 weeks old; GemPharmatech) were randomly divided into four groups (n = 5 per group): shNC, shC5AR1, JPE1375, and shC5AR1 + JPE1375. U251 cells (5 × 106) were subcutaneously injected into the right axilla. JPE1375 was administered by oral gavage at 40 mg/kg once daily for 21 days. Tumor volumes were measured every three days. At the endpoint, mice were sacrificed, and tumors were excised, weighed, and processed for histological and molecular analyses. Hematoxylin–eosin (H&E; Beyotime, C0105S), Ki67 (Abcam, ab16667), and C5AR1 immunohistochemistry were used to assess histopathological changes. Western blot and ELISA were used to measure signaling proteins and complement levels in tumor tissues. All animal experiments were approved by the institutional ethics committee. Mice were randomly assigned to experimental groups using a computer-generated randomization list to minimize selection bias. All in vivo measurements and histological analyses were performed in a blinded manner.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) from at least three independent experiments. Comparisons between two groups were performed using two-tailed Student’s t-test, whereas multiple-group comparisons were conducted by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and log-rank tests were used for survival comparisons. Spearman’s correlation coefficient was applied to evaluate correlations. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. For high-throughput analyses such as differential gene expression (DEG) screening, P-values were adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method, with an adjusted p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R software or GraphPad Prism 10.

Result 1. Screening and expression profile of C5AR1 in gliomas

Using the TCGA database, we applied the ESTIMATE algorithm to calculate StromalScore, ImmuneScore, ESTIMATEscore, and TumorPurity for glioma samples (Fig. 1A). GBM samples exhibited higher immune-related scores, whereas LGG samples showed higher tumor purity. Survival analysis further demonstrated that high Stromal, Immune, and ESTIMATE scores were significantly associated with poorer prognosis (Fig. S1A–C), suggesting that the immune microenvironment plays a critical role in glioma progression. In the GSE184941 dataset, differential gene expression analysis between high- and low-ESTIMATE score groups identified 4315 upregulated and 2728 downregulated genes (Fig. 1B). Stepwise regression modeling screened five candidate genes most strongly associated with high ESTIMATE scores (Fig. 1C), all of which were correlated with worse overall survival (Fig. S1D). Single-cell annotation revealed that glioma samples were enriched mainly with monocytes, astrocytes, and T cells (Fig. 1D). Among the five candidate genes, C5AR1 expression significantly increased in immune cells, particularly monocytes, whereas the other genes lacked cell-type specificity (Fig. 1E). Notably, C5AR1, a classical complement C5a receptor, was not only highly expressed in monocytes, consistent with its known biological role, but also markedly expressed in tumor cell clusters. Monocyte annotation confirmed a positive correlation between C5AR1 expression and monocyte infiltration (R = 0.54; Fig. 1F). At the tissue level, C5AR1 was significantly upregulated in both LGG and GBM compared to normal brain tissues, with the highest expression observed in GBM (Fig. 1G). Consistently, cross-cohort validation further confirmed the elevated expression and prognostic significance of C5AR1 in multiple independent glioma datasets. Analysis of the GSE16011 and GSE66354 cohorts revealed that C5AR1 expression increased progressively with glioma grade and was significantly higher in tumor tissues compared with normal brain samples (Fig. S2A, B). Moreover, survival analyses based on three CGGA datasets (mRNA-array_301, mRNA-array_325, and mRNA-array_693) consistently demonstrated that high C5AR1 expression was associated with poorer overall survival (Fig. S2D, E).

In glioma cell lines, C5AR1 expression was elevated across multiple lines, with U251 showing the highest level; thus, U251 and LN229 cells were selected for further experiments (Fig. 1H, I). Knockdown of C5AR1 in U251 and overexpression in LN229 were confirmed by qPCR and Western blot (Fig. S1E, F). Functionally, the C5AR1 antagonist JPE1375 suppressed cell viability as measured by CCK-8 (Fig. 1J). Colony formation and Transwell assays demonstrated that C5AR1 promotes glioma cell proliferation and migration (Fig. 1K; quantification in Fig. S1G), and CFDA-SE staining further supported its pro-proliferative effect (Fig. 1L).

Result 2. Functional validation of C5AR1 in glioma cells and antagonist-dependent effects

To investigate the role of C5AR1 in glioma cell death, we first performed FITC/PI dual staining and flow cytometric analysis (Fig. 2A). Genetic inhibition (shRNA-mediated knockdown) of C5AR1 knockdown significantly increased necrotic cell population from 4.1% to 8.9% (p < 0.001), whereas C5AR1 overexpression exerted the opposite effect. PI fluorescence staining further confirmed the increase in necrotic cell death upon C5AR1 knockdown (Fig. 2B). Western blot analysis of key downstream signaling molecules (Fig. 2C) revealed that the levels of p-JAK2, p-STAT3, and p-STAT5 changed in response to C5AR1 manipulation, indicating the involvement of canonical complement receptor signaling. Acridine orange staining corroborated the regulatory role of C5AR1 in cell death (Fig. 2D). ELISA analysis of cell culture supernatants confirmed complement activation, as evidenced by abundant C5a levels serving as ligands for C5AR1 (Fig. 2E).

(C) Western blot analysis of downstream signaling molecules (p-JAK2, p-STAT3, and p-STAT5). (D) Acridine orange staining of dead cells. (E) ELISA quantification of C3 and C5a levels in cell culture supernatants to assess complement activation. N = 3 for each group. Representative images are derived from three independent experiments

We next assessed the effects of pharmacological inhibition using the selective C5AR1 antagonist JPE1375. qPCR and western blot analysis revealed that JPE1375 did not alter C5AR1 mRNA levels but significantly reduced its protein expression (Fig. S3A, B). Functionally, CCK-8 assays demonstrated that JPE1375 inhibited cell viability in a dose- and time-dependent manner (Fig. 3A). Colony number decreased by 49.7% & 48.9% and migrated cells by 61.7% & 64.7% upon JPE1375 treatment (p < 0.01) (Fig. 3B, C), whereas CFDA-SE staining confirmed a dose-dependent reduction in cell viability (Fig. 3D). Notably, flow cytometric FITC/PI dual staining revealed that, in contrast to genetic manipulation, JPE1375 treatment reduced the total proportion of dead cells and necrotic cell death (Fig. S3C, D), suggesting that C5AR1 function is tightly dependent on its activation state. PI fluorescence staining and acridine orange staining further validated these findings (Fig. 3E, F).

Result 3. Association of C5AR1 with G0-phase status and downstream mechanistic validation

Analysis of the CCGA dataset revealed a significant correlation between C5AR1 expression and cell-cycle regulation (Fig. 4A). High C5AR1 expression was associated with a stronger correlation with cell-cycle pathways compared to low-expression states (Fig. 4B). Expression profiling across different cell-cycle phases showed that C5AR1 was predominantly expressed in the S phase but also maintained relatively high expression in the G0 phase (Fig. 4C). Analysis of antitumor drug IC50 values across different G0-phase subtypes demonstrated a strong association of Common G0, cdk G0, and spontaneous G0 with drug resistance (Fig. 4D). Survival analysis confirmed that patients with high spontaneous G0, contact G0, or common G0 scores had significantly worse DSS, OS, and PFS (Fig. 4E–G).

(B) Comparison of cell-cycle correlation in high- versus low-C5AR1 expression groups. (C) C5AR1 expression levels across actively growing, G0, G1, and S phases. (D) Association between antitumor drug IC50 values and different G0-phase subtypes. (E–G) Kaplan–Meier survival curves for disease-specific survival (DSS), overall survival (OS), and progression-free survival (PFS) in glioma patients with high versus low spontaneous G0 (E), contact G0 (F), and common G0 (G) scores

To determine whether C5AR1 regulates G0-phase maintenance through downstream signaling, we treated glioma cells (both U251 and LN-229) with the C5AR1 antagonist JPE1375 or the STAT3 inhibitor Stattic. Western blot analysis demonstrated downregulation of p-JAK2, p-STAT3, and p-STAT5 phosphorylation following treatments (Figs. 5A and S4A). Flow cytometry showed a decrease in the G0/G1 population from 69.8% to 29.7%/26.7%, and an increase in S-phase cells from 15.8% to 48.4%/50.2% after inhibition of C5AR1 or STAT3 (Figs. 5B and S4B). PI fluorescence and acridine orange staining further confirmed changes in cell viability under these conditions (Figs. 5C, D and S4C, D). MitoTracker and MitoSOX assays revealed reduced mitochondrial activity, increased mitochondrial fragmentation, and altered ROS levels following intervention (Figs. 5E–G and S4E–G). ELISA analysis confirmed adequate levels of C3 and C5a, indicating complement activation (Figs. 5H and S4H).

Correlation analysis of C5AR1 with different G0-phase subtypes demonstrated the strongest association with spontaneous G0 (Fig. S5A–E). Ki67 immunofluorescence further validated this finding: DAPI-positive but Ki67-negative U251 cells were identified as spontaneous G0-phase cells, and JPE1375 treatment increased Ki67 expression, reducing the proportion of spontaneous G0-phase cells (Fig. S5F). Moreover, C5AR1 manipulation markedly affected mitochondrial function: C5AR1 knockdown reduced mitochondrial activity, whereas C5AR1 overexpression significantly enhanced it (Fig. S5G–I). Flow cytometry analysis using ROS fluorescent probes (Fig. S5J) showed that C5AR1 knockdown significantly increased ROS fluorescence 1.6-fold, while C5AR1 overexpression markedly decreased ROS levels. These findings suggest that C5AR1 maintains mitochondrial homeostasis by modulating ROS dynamic balance, thereby providing an optimal metabolic environment and energy reserves for G0-phase cells.

Result 4. C5AR1 regulates G0-phase maintenance and mitochondrial activity via the complement C3-JAK2/STAT3 axis

Annotation of glioma cells with high versus low spontaneous G0 scores revealed distinct distributions among monocytes, astrocytes, neural stem cells, and microglia in UMAP projections (Fig. 6A). C5AR1 expression was more enriched in high-scoring cells (93% vs. 40%), particularly in neural stem cells (Figs. 6B–D and S6). Given that neural stem cells predominantly remain in a spontaneous G0 phase to maintain homeostasis and stemness, these findings suggest that C5AR1 may participate in the regulation of quiescent-state maintenance. Cell-cell communication analysis revealed substantial alterations in intercellular signaling networks in high spontaneous G0-scoring cells, with enhanced complement pathway-mediated communication between monocytes and astrocytes (Fig. 6E). PPI network analysis indicated potential interactions of C5AR1 with complement proteins and integrin family members (Fig. 6F). Ligand–receptor contribution analysis further identified C3-(ITGAM + ITGB2) as a key enriched ligand–receptor pair in both low- and high-spontaneous G0-scoring cells (Fig. 6G, H), with ITGAM + ITGB2 acting as integrin receptors involved in adhesion and migration, and C3 serving as a central complement protein.

Complement communication analysis supported these findings. Chord diagrams demonstrated more active complement-mediated intercellular signaling in high spontaneous G0-scoring cells (Fig. S7A). Ligand-receptor gene expression analysis showed high C3 expression in astrocytes, neural stem cells, glial cells, and dendritic cells (Fig. S7B). Notably, whereas astrocytes were the primary signal senders in low-scoring cells, monocytes acquired autocrine signaling patterns in high-scoring cells (Fig. S7C–F), suggesting that C5AR1 activation may depend on complement signaling, particularly C3-driven signaling.

To validate the functional role of the complement pathway in C5AR1 signaling, we knocked down C3 in U251 cells (shC3). C3 knockdown significantly decreased p-JAK2, p-STAT3, and p-STAT5 levels (Fig. 7A), reduced the proportion of G0/G1-phase cells from 59.8% to 33.7% (Fig. 7B), and was accompanied by increased cell death, decreased mitochondrial activity, elevated mitochondrial fragmentation, and altered ROS levels (Fig. 7C–G). These findings support the notion that C5AR1 maintains G0-phase status through complement C3-driven activation of downstream signaling pathways. To confirm the functional dependency of C5AR1 on upstream complement signaling, recombinant C3 protein was supplemented following C5AR1 knockdown. Exogenous C3 failed to restore p-JAK2, p-STAT3, or p-STAT5 activation, although a mild, non-significant recovery of G0-phase proportion and mitochondrial function was observed (Fig. S8), indicating that downstream signaling is strictly C5AR1-dependent.

Result 5. In vivo validation of C5AR1 knockdown combined with JPE1375 and analysis of drug resistance

To validate the in vivo role of C5AR1 and its synergistic effect with JPE1375, U251 cells were subcutaneously implanted into nude mice, followed by C5AR1 knockdown (shC5AR1) and/or oral administration of JPE1375. C5AR1 mRNA and protein levels were significantly reduced in the shC5AR1 group, whereas JPE1375 alone did not markedly alter C5AR1 protein expression; however, combined treatment demonstrated a synergistic inhibitory effect (Fig. 8A, B). Mean tumor weight was reduced from 0.80 g in control to 0.13 g in the combined group, and mean tumor volume was reduced from 1819 mm3 to 607 mm3 (Fig. 8C–E). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated that combination treatment significantly prolonged overall survival compared with single treatments or controls (p = 0.0073; Fig. 8F), consistent with Ki67 and C5AR1 IHC staining quantification (Fig. 8G, H). Downstream signaling proteins p-JAK2, p-STAT3, and p-STAT5 were significantly downregulated (Fig. 8I), accompanied by decreased levels of C3, C3a, C5, and C5a (Fig. 8J).

Given the critical role of C5AR1 in G0-phase regulation and the well-established association of G0-phase status with drug resistance, we further examined the relationship between C5AR1 and drug sensitivity. Correlation analysis of drug IC50 values with gene expression revealed a positive correlation between C5AR1 and rosiglitazone IC50 (r = 0.61), suggesting that high C5AR1 expression may contribute to increased rosiglitazone resistance; conversely, C5AR1 expression was negatively correlated with metformin IC50 (r = −0.52), indicating preserved metformin sensitivity in cells with high C5AR1 expression and elevated G0-phase proportions (Fig. S9A). Functional validation showed that JPE1375 partially alleviated rosiglitazone resistance and improved metformin sensitivity (Fig. S9C, D). Together, these findings suggest that C5AR1 may mediate drug resistance through G0-phase regulation and that targeting C5AR1 combined with metabolic drugs could improve therapeutic responses in glioma.

Discussion

Our study uncovers a novel extracellular complement-intracellular cell cycle coupling mechanism through C5AR1, directly regulates intracellular cell-cycle quiescence. While bioinformatic analyses confirmed C5AR1‘s expected enrichment in monocytes, a key finding was its significant expression and functional activity within glioma cells themselves. We established a signaling axis wherein complement C3 activates C5AR1, leading to JAK2/STAT3/STAT5 phosphorylation, enhanced mitochondrial function, and the maintenance of a spontaneous G0-phase population. This pathway equips tumor cells with a drug-tolerant, stem-like phenotype. Our work thus positions complement signaling as a central regulator bridging the tumor microenvironment, metabolic reprogramming, and cell-cycle control in glioma.

Traditionally, G0-phase cells are considered metabolically dormant, maintaining a “quiescent state” by suppressing mitochondrial activity to conserve energy [22, 23]. However, accumulating evidence suggests that a subset of cancer stem-like cells exhibits highly active mitochondrial function and enhanced oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) during quiescence [24]. This metabolic state supports stemness, enables rapid cell-cycle re-entry following chemotherapy-induced stress, and contributes to drug resistance. Our findings are consistent with this paradigm. We observed that C5AR1 activation markedly increased mitochondrial activity, elevated the proportion of intact mitochondria, and maintained ROS homeostasis at a low dynamic level: a metabolic feature closely associated with stem-like phenotypes. Unlike previous studies, we further elucidated the molecular basis of this process by demonstrating that complement signaling, through C5AR1, orchestrates metabolic to cell-cycle coupling to maintain G0-phase quiescence, expanding the functional scope of complement signaling beyond its traditional immune-modulatory role.

As a key component of innate immunity, the complement system has long been considered to promote tumor progression mainly by reshaping the tumor immune microenvironment [25], such as regulating macrophage polarization and promoting tumor-associated inflammation [26, 27]. However, recent studies have begun to highlight its non-canonical functions in tumor cells, showing that C3a/C5a signaling may directly regulate tumor cell proliferation and invasion [28]. Our study further advances this understanding by providing evidence that C3-driven C5AR1 activation contributes to the maintenance of G0-phase quiescence and is closely associated with drug resistance. These findings extend complement biology from immune regulation to a broader role in metabolic reprogramming and cell-cycle control, offering new clues for exploring the link between complement signaling, cancer stemness, and therapeutic resistance.

The impact of C5AR1 on drug sensitivity highlights its clinical translational potential. Metformin, whose antitumor activity is thought to involve AMPK activation and inhibition of mitochondrial respiration [29–31], retained significant efficacy in C5AR1-high glioma cells, likely due to its mechanisms being independent of G0-phase status. In contrast, rosiglitazone, which acts primarily through PPARγ-mediated lipid metabolic reprogramming [32, 33], showed increased resistance in cells with high G0-phase proportions. Importantly, we demonstrated that the C5AR1 antagonist JPE1375 not only partially reversed rosiglitazone resistance but also significantly enhanced metformin efficacy. These findings suggest that targeting C5AR1 may represent a promising strategy for combination therapies with metabolic drugs, especially in patients with high C5AR1 expression and elevated G0-phase cell populations. Beyond metabolic modulation, targeting C5AR1 may also complement current standard therapies for glioma, particularly TMZ. Given that C5AR1 activation maintains a quiescent G0 state and enhances stress tolerance, it is plausible that C5AR1-driven G0-phase cells contribute to TMZ resistance by evading cell-cycle dependent cytotoxicity. STAT3 activation downstream of C5AR1 could converge with the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway in regulating metabolism and stemness, while the G0-phase quiescence maintained by C5AR1 may indirectly reinforce TMZ resistance mediated by autophagy and MGMT-dependent DNA repair. Pharmacological inhibition of C5AR1 could therefore sensitize quiescent tumor cells to TMZ-induced DNA damage and improve overall therapeutic efficacy. Compared with existing strategies aimed at stemness or immune evasion, C5AR1 blockade integrates immune modulation with metabolic and cell-cycle regulation. From a translational perspective, optimization of blood-brain barrier-penetrant delivery systems will therefore be critical to ensure therapeutic efficacy and safety in clinical applications.

While C5AR1 activation was closely associated with key tumor phenotypes, its causal role remains to be fully verified. Our analyses relied mainly on established glioma cell lines, which may not fully capture the molecular and cellular heterogeneity of patient-derived tumors. Moreover, although JPE1375 is a well-characterized C5AR1 antagonist, potential off-target effects cannot be excluded. Future studies should employ complementary approaches, such as CRISPR/Cas9-based gene modulation and co-culture or orthotopic models, to better recapitulate the tumor microenvironment and determine the direct contribution of C5AR1 to glioma progression. Validation in larger patient cohorts and organoid models is also warranted to strengthen the clinical relevance of our findings. In addition, integrating multi-omics analyses may further clarify the crosstalk between complement signaling, metabolic reprogramming, and therapeutic resistance. Given the well-recognized heterogeneity of glioblastoma, the functional role of C5AR1 likely varies across distinct tumor subtypes or cellular niches. Future organoid and co-culture studies will be essential to elucidate how C5AR1 functions within these diverse tumor contexts.

In summary, our study demonstrates that C3-driven C5AR1 activation maintains spontaneous G0-phase status by activating the JAK2/STAT3/STAT5 pathway and enhancing mitochondrial function, thereby conferring stemness and drug resistance to glioma cells (Fig. 9). Pharmacological blockade of C5AR1 disrupts G0-phase maintenance and overcomes metabolic drug resistance, providing a clinically actionable strategy.

Proposed mechanistic of C5AR1-mediated glioma drug resistance. Complement activation generates C5a, which binds to C5AR1 and activates the JAK2/STAT3/STAT5 signaling cascade. This pathway enhances mitochondrial activity, maintains low-level ROS homeostasis, and supports the persistence of spontaneous G0-phase cells by providing metabolic energy reserves and stress resistance. Consequently, glioma cells acquire quiescent, stem-like, and drug-resistant phenotypes, enabling them to survive chemotherapy and drive tumor recurrence. Pharmacological blockade of C5AR1 by JPE1375 disrupts C5a-C5AR1 binding, suppresses downstream signaling, reduces the G0-phase population, and partially restores drug sensitivity. Overall, the C3-C5-C5AR1 axis integrates metabolic regulation with cell-cycle control, representing a potential therapeutic target for overcoming glioma chemoresistance

Satisfiability in Łukasiewicz logic and its unbounded relative
Zuzana Haniková, Filip Jankovec

Abstract
Unbounded Łukasiewicz logic is a substructural logic that combines features of infinite-valued Łukasiewicz logic with those of abelian logic. The logic is finitely strongly complete w.r.t. the additive ℓ \ell -group on the reals expanded with a distinguished element − 1 -1 . We show that the existential theory of this structure is NP-complete. This provides a complexity upper bound for the set of theorems and the finite consequence relation of unbounded Łukasiewicz logic. The result is obtained by reducing the problem to the existential theory of the MV-algebra on the reals, the standard semantics of Łukasiewicz logic. This provides a new connection between both logics. The result entails a translation of the existential theory of the standard MV-algebra into itself.

Unbounded Łukasiewicz logic

was considered recently in the papers

-1

0

-1

By design, then, the logic bears resemblance to both Łukasiewicz logic and to abelian logic,
as considered in Meyer and Slaney’s paper
[
34

Łu is inconsistent with classical logic.

Weispfenning
[
43
]
proved that the existential theory of the variety of
\ell
-groups is NP-complete. So the universal theory of
\ell
-groups is coNP-complete and
a fortiori
, the quasiequational theory and the equational theory are in coNP. It is also known that
\ell
-groups are generated as a quasivariety by the totally ordered members
[
13
]
, in fact, by any nontrivial totally ordered member, since the universal theories of each two nontrivial totally ordered abelian groups coincide
[
20
]
. One easily shows that the equational theory of the totally ordered additive group on the integers is coNP-hard, thus any of the above universal fragments is coNP-complete.

The purpose of this paper is to provide a complexity estimate for
Łu by exhibiting a polynomial-time reduction of the existential theory of the intended semantics of Łu—the algebra
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
, the pointed abelian totally ordered additive group on the reals where
{-1}
is interpreted by itself—to the existential theory of the intended semantics of Ł, namely
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
, the standard MV-algebra on the reals.
In both cases, the logics are finitely strongly complete w.r.t. the intended semantics.
In our opinion the method surpasses the result in terms of interest, since the result might be anticipated.
The method consists in taking a linear map of a sufficiently large neighbourhood of the element
0
to the interval
[0,1]
, with the coefficient of the linear map determined by the instance (i.e., a given existential sentence).

In combination with the extant method of
[
12
]
that reduces in polynomial time the set of valid universal formulas (valid identities) of
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
to the set of valid universal formulas (valid identities respectively) of the algebra
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
one obtains firstly, a coNP-completeness of each of the universal fragments (i.a., the logic Łu is coNP-complete) and secondly, a non-trivial interpretation of the existential theory of the
standard MV-algebra in itself.

The result invites a comparison with the work of Marchioni and Montagna
[
31
]
, which concerns the logic
\text{\L}\Pi\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
and its relation to the universal theory of real closed fields (RCF).
The authors show that the universal fragment of RCF has a polynomial time reduction
to the (theorems of) the logic
\text{\L}\Pi\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}

The relevance consists in, on the one hand, the logic

being a conservative expansion of the logic Ł (the theorems of Ł are exactly those theorems of

Their reduction starts with fixing a bijection between

and the interval

and then defining the operations of the real closed field on

, then there is a satisfying (rational) assignment
whose binary representation has size polynomial in
\sharp(\Phi)
.
Our exposition follows (in some detail) the one in the paper
[
15
]
as to the general method.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
k,m,n
be positive natural numbers.
Let
Ax\leq b
be a system of linear inequalities with
A
an
n\times m
integer matrix,
b
a rational
n
-tuple, and
a_{i,j},b_{i}\in[-k,k]
for
i\leq n,j\leq m
.
Assume the system has a solution in
\mathbb{R}
.
Then there is a (rational) solution in the interval
[-(mk)^{m},(mk)^{m}]
.

Proof.

The polytope
P=\{x\mid Ax\leq b\}
is nonempty.
Fix a non-empty face of
P
minimal under inclusion.
Such a minimal face is given by a system of equations
\hat{A}x=\hat{b}
, a subsystem of
Ax=b
. To solve this system, it is enough to solve a system
A^{0}y=b^{0}
, where
A^{0}
and
b^{0}
are obtained from
\hat{A}
and
\hat{b}
by fixing
d
columns that contain pivots after Gauss elimination
(permuting columns if necessary, assume the first
d
columns of
A
) and fixing the corresponding
d
linearly independent rows. The matrix
A^{0}
is regular of rank
d
and
b^{0}
is a
d
-tuple of rationals. A solution of
A^{0}y=b^{0}
also yields a solution of
\hat{A}x=\hat{b}
by assigning the value
0
to all
variables
x_{d+1},\dots,x_{m}
.
Cramer’s rule yields the solution

\langle{y_{i}}\rangle_{i\leq d}=\langle{\frac{\det(A^{0}_{i})}{\det(A^{0})}}\rangle_{i\leq d}.

Since
A
has integer entries,
|\det(A^{0})|\geq 1
and thus
|y_{i}|\leq|\det(A^{0}_{i})|
for each
i\leq d
.
We have
|\det(A^{0}_{i})|\leq d!\cdot k^{d}\leq d^{d}\cdot k^{d}=(kd)^{d}
.
So
y_{i}\in[-(dk)^{d},(dk)^{d}]
for each
i\leq d
, hence for each
i\leq m
.
Since
d\leq\min(m,n)
we have
x_{i}\in[-(mk)^{m},(mk)^{m}]
for
i\leq m
.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Bounded assignment)
.

Let
\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be an open formula of the language
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{pAb}}}
with
n
variables
and let
N=\sharp(\Phi)
.
Assume that
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}\models\exists x_{1}\dots\exists x_{n}\Phi
.
Then there are integers
c
and
M
and real numbers

\langle{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}\rangle
such that
\Phi(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
holds in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
,
-2^{M}\leq a_{i}\leq 2^{M}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
,
and the binary representation of
M
has at most
cN\log_{2}N
bits.

Proof.

For convenience, the symbols
\leq
and
<
are added to the language
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{pAb}}}
.
Let
\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be given.
Applying de Morgan rules and removing any double boolean negations that occur,

\Phi
can be rewritten so that any boolean negation is applied to an atom. Using trichotomy, any atom
\text{\raisebox{-1.1pt}{$\neg$}\mbox{}\kern-6.86pt\raisebox{0.9pt}{$\neg$}\hskip 0.9pt}(\varphi=\psi)
can be replaced with
(\varphi<\psi)\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\vee\mbox{}\kern-8.7pt\vee}\hskip 0.9pt\,(\psi<\varphi)
.
This yields an equivalent negation-free formula
\Phi^{\prime}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
whose atoms are identities and strict inequalities. Moreover
{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Phi^{\prime})}\leq 2{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Phi)}
.

\Phi^{\prime}
can be brought to a DNF without negations, obtaining a

\Phi^{\prime\prime}=\Psi_{1}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\vee\mbox{}\kern-8.7pt\vee}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi_{2}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\vee\mbox{}\kern-8.7pt\vee}\hskip 0.9pt\dots\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\vee\mbox{}\kern-8.7pt\vee}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi_{m}

for some
m
, where each
\Psi_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m
is a conjunction of atoms of the form
(\varphi=\psi)
or
(\varphi<\psi)
.
The value of
m
need not be polynomial in
{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Phi^{\prime})}
.
The remaining part of the proof establishes that a bounded satisfying assignment to
\Phi
exists
on the basis of the existence of the formula
\Phi^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{DNF}(\Phi^{\prime})
and the fact, which we proceed to show,
of an existence of a bounded satisfying assignment to at least one among the
\Psi_{j}
.
Both
\Phi^{\prime}
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}
are equivalent to
\Phi
; namely, a tuple

\langle{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}\rangle
makes
\Phi
true in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
if and only if it makes
\Phi^{\prime}
true, and the latter is the case if and only if it makes some
\Psi_{j}
true for some
j\leq m
.
Trivially
{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Psi_{j})}\leq{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Phi^{\prime})}
for
1\leq j\leq m
.

Next, we remove any complex terms from
\Phi^{\prime\prime}
using a Tseitin transformation.
Let
\Psi^{\prime}_{1},\dots\Psi^{\prime}_{m}
be Tseitin variants of
\Psi_{1},\dots\Psi_{m}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that the Tseitin transformation assigns the same variable
x_{t}
to identical terms
t
, regardless of the disjunct in which they appear. Consequently,
|\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}{\mathrm{V}ar}(\Psi^{\prime}_{j})|=|T|
, where
|T|
is the cardinality of the set
T
of all
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{pAb}}}
-(sub)terms that occur in
\Phi^{\prime}
.

Let us set

(\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime})=(\Psi_{1}^{\prime}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\vee\mbox{}\kern-8.7pt\vee}\hskip 0.9pt\dots\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\vee\mbox{}\kern-8.7pt\vee}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi_{m}^{\prime}).

Since
\Psi_{j}
is equisatisfiable with
\Psi^{\prime}_{j}
for each
j\leq m
, we obtain that
\Phi^{\prime\prime}
is equisatisfiable with
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
Let
k
denote the total number of variables in
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}
. Clearly, we have
k=|T|\leq 3\sharp(\Phi^{\prime})
.

The formula
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}
is still in DNF and we have
{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Psi_{j}^{\prime})}\leq|T|+\sharp(\Psi_{j})\leq 4\sharp(\Phi^{\prime})
for each
j\leq m
.

By the assumption, there is an assignment
v
in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
with
v(x_{i})=a_{i}
for
i\leq k
such that the vector
\langle{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}\rangle
makes both
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime\prime}
true in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
.
Fix such an assignment
v
.
Then there is a total ordering given by a permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
such that, in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}
,
a_{\sigma(i)}\leq a_{\sigma(i+1)}
for each
i<k
.
Let

\rho(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\coloneqq\mathop{\bigwedge\kern-11.0pt\bigwedge}_{{\{i<k\};\;a_{\sigma(i)}<a_{\sigma(i+1)}}}(x_{\sigma(i)}<x_{\sigma(i+1)})\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\bigwedge\kern-11.0pt\bigwedge}_{\{i<k\};a_{\sigma(i)}=a_{\sigma(i+1)}}(x_{\sigma(i)}=x_{\sigma(i+1)}).

We have
{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\rho)}\leq k
.
For some
j\leq m
,
(\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi_{j}^{\prime})(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})
is true in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
.

Fix such
j\leq m

and let us show that there exists an assignment that makes the formula
(\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi_{j}^{\prime})
true
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
and whose values are bounded as required.
We refer to the formula
\Psi_{j}^{\prime}
simply as
\Psi
.

Using
\rho
, one can omit atoms with the function symbols
\land
and
\lor
.
Indeed, since
\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi
is true under
v
,
it must also be the case that
if, e.g.,
x_{\varphi}\land x_{\psi}=x_{\chi}
occurs as an atom in
\Psi

(which means either that the term
\chi
is the term
\varphi\land\psi
in
\Phi
,
or that
\chi=\varphi\land\psi
is an identity in
\Phi
),
then the identity
\varphi(a)\land\psi(a)=\chi(a)
holds and hence,
this is captured by
\rho
, namely
x_{\chi}=_{\rho}\min_{\rho}(x_{\varphi},x_{\psi})
.
The other cases featuring
\lor
and
<
are analogous.
Thus
\rho
already captures all the information
that is provided by those atoms in
\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi
in
which the lattice function symbols
\land
and
\lor

occur, whereby these atomic formulas are redundant and can be omitted from the conjunction.

Such an omission yields a new conjunction of identities and strict inequalities

\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi^{\star}
.
This formula has
k
variables, and

l\coloneqq{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi^{\star})}={\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\rho)}+{\mathrm{At}^{\sharp}(\Psi_{j}^{\prime})}\leq\sharp(\Phi^{\prime})+4\sharp(\Phi^{\prime})=5\sharp(\Phi^{\prime})\leq 10\sharp(\Phi).

Now
\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi^{\star}
defines a system
S
of linear equations and strict
inequalities in
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}
as follows.
Any atomic formula in
\rho\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\Psi^{\star}
has at most three distinct variables
and at most one function symbol. Subtracting the expression on right-hand side of each atomic
formula from both sides, we get a system consisting of a subsystem
Ax=c
with
l_{1}
rows,
and a subsystem
Bx<d
with
l_{2}
rows, both in the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
and with
l_{1}+l_{2}=l
, thus
l_{1},l_{2}\leq 10\sharp(\Phi)
.
Since
\langle{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}}\rangle
is a solution to the system,
there has to exist a rational vector
d^{\prime}=\langle{d_{1}^{\prime},\dots,d_{l_{2}^{\prime}}}\rangle
where

d_{i}-1\leq d_{i}^{\prime}<d_{i}
, such that a modified system
S^{\prime}
consisting of
Ax=c
and
Bx\leq d^{\prime}
still has a solution.
Clearly, any solution to
S^{\prime}
is also a solution to
S
.

Rewriting each of the equations in
Ax=c
as two inequalities and adding the rows
of
Bx\leq d^{\prime}
yields a system
A^{\prime}x\leq b^{\prime}
,
s.t. there is an integer
c\leq 20
with
the number of both rows and columns in
A^{\prime}x\leq b^{\prime}
bounded
by
c\cdot\sharp(\Phi)
.

A^{\prime}
is an integer matrix,
b^{\prime}
is a rational vector,
and the absolute values of entries in either are bounded by
3
.
Applying Lemma
3.1
yields
a solution whose absolute value is bounded by
(3c\sharp(\Phi))^{c\sharp(\Phi)}
.
It is then enough to pick
M=\lceil c\sharp(\Phi)\log_{2}(3c\sharp(\Phi))\rceil
.
∎

Using the tools of this section, one might proceed to show that the existential theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
is in NP.
Indeed, one gets almost for free from the above, for any open
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{pAb}}}
-formula
\Phi
with a real assignment that satisfies it,
also a satisfying assignment that is rational with both the numerators and the denominators of polynomial size in
\sharp(\Phi)
. This assignment is then a witness of the fact that the existential closure of
\Phi
is true, whence the existential theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
is in NP.
However, our intent is to use the results of this section to obtain suitable maps on the reals in the next section, and hence
a polynomial-time reduction of the existential theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
to the existential theory of
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
.
In other words, we answer the satisfiability question, the instances of which are open formulas, indirectly.
It is worth remarking that, if one established NP-completeness of the existential theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
anyhow,
then one would still get a poly-time reduction between this problem and the existential theory of
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
,
because the latter problem is known to be NP-complete (using, e.g.,
[
21
,
24
]
). Our reduction has the added value of being explicit.

4
Reducing the
\exists
-theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
to the
\exists
-theory of
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}

Consider the mapping
\tau\colon\mathit{Tm}_{{\mathrm{Ab}}}\rightarrow\mathit{Tm}_{{{\mathrm{MV[\frac{1}{2}]}}}}
, assigning to each term
\varphi
of
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{Ab}}}
a term of
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{MV[\frac{1}{2}]}}}
as follows:

1.

\tau(x)=x
for
x\in{\mathrm{V}ar}(\varphi)
;

2.

\tau(0)=\frac{1}{2}
;

3.

\tau(-\varphi)=\neg(\tau(\varphi))
;

4.

\tau(\varphi+\psi)=(\tau(\varphi)\oplus\tau(\psi))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\tau(\varphi)\otimes\tau(\psi)))
;

5.

\tau(\varphi\lor\psi)=\tau(\varphi)\lor\tau(\psi)
;

6.

\tau(\varphi\land\psi)=\tau(\varphi)\land\tau(\psi)
.

For each pair of nonnegative integers
M
and
k
, let
r_{M,k}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be defined as

r_{M,k}(a)=\frac{a}{2^{M+k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}.

(1)

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
M
and
k
be fixed nonnegative integers. Let
\varphi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be a term in

\mathit{Tm}_{{\mathrm{Ab}}}
with
n
variables, let
0pt{\varphi}=l
with
l\leq k
. Assume
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in[-2^{M},2^{M}].
Then

f_{\tau(\varphi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\varphi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

Moreover,
r_{M,k}(f_{\varphi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}}]
.

Proof.

First let us note that for each
a\in[-2^{M},2^{M}]
it holds that
r_{M,k}(a)\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}]
. We will proceed by induction on term depth.

•

Let
\varphi
be a variable
x
. By definition we have

f_{\tau(\varphi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\varphi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}].

•

Let
\varphi=0
. We have

f_{\tau(\varphi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\frac{1}{2}=r_{M,k}(f_{\varphi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

Now assume
0pt{\varphi}=l
and the statement holds for any
l^{\prime}<l
.

•

Let
\varphi
be
-\psi
. We assume
f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))
.
We have

f_{\tau(-\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=f_{\neg\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
1-f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=1-r_{M,k}(f_{\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))=\\
1-(\frac{f_{\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}{2^{M+k+1}}+\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{f_{\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}{2^{M+k+1}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{f_{-\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}{2^{M+k+1}}=\\
r_{M,k}(f_{-\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

Moreover, since by (the induction) hypothesis

f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-(l-1)+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-(l-1)+1}}],

we obtain

f_{\tau(-\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
1-f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+2}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+2}}]\subseteq[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}}].

•

Let
\varphi=\psi\land\gamma
.
We assume

f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})),\\
f_{\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\gamma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

We obtain

f_{\tau(\psi\land\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=f_{\tau(\psi)\land\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
(f_{\tau(\psi)}\land f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=((r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi})\land(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma}))(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

Since
r_{M,k}
is order preserving we obtain

((r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi})\land(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma}))(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=r_{M,k}((f_{\psi}\land f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))=r_{M,k}((f_{\psi\land\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

Since

f_{\tau(\psi\land\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\in\{f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n})),f_{\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\},

then necessarily

f_{\tau(\psi\land\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-(l-1)+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-(l-1)+1}}]\subseteq[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}}].

•

The case
\varphi=\psi\lor\gamma
is analogous.

•

Now let
\varphi
be
\psi+\gamma
. We assume

f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\psi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})),

f_{\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=r_{M,k}(f_{\gamma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))

and since
l=0pt{\varphi}=\max(0pt{\psi},0pt{\gamma})+1
we get

f_{\tau(\psi)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n})),f_{\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+2}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+2}}].

We distinguish two cases:

1.

In case

(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\geq 1

we have
f_{\tau(\psi)\oplus\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=1
and

f_{\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))-1.

Thus, we have

f_{\tau(\psi+\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
f_{(\tau(\psi)\oplus\tau(\gamma))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma)))}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
f_{1\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma)))}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
f_{\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma))}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n})=\\
\min(1,f_{\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))+\frac{1}{2})=\\
\min(1,(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))-1+\frac{1}{2})=\\
\min(1,(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))-\frac{1}{2})=\\
\min(1,(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-\frac{1}{2}).

Moreover, by the induction hypothesis

(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}}]

and thus, we can conclude

\min(1,(r_{M,k}(f_{\psi}+f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))-\frac{1}{2})=(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore,

f_{\tau(\psi+\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-\frac{1}{2}=\\
\frac{(f_{\psi}+f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}{2^{M+k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}=r_{M,k}(f_{\psi+\gamma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

This completes the proof of the first case.

2.

In case

(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))\leq 1,

we have
f_{\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=0
and

f_{\tau(\psi)\oplus\tau(\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n})).

Thus, we have

f_{\tau(\psi+\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
f_{(\tau(\psi)\oplus\tau(\gamma))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\tau(\psi)\otimes\tau(\gamma)))}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
f_{(\tau(\psi)\oplus\tau(\gamma))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus 0)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
f_{(\tau(\psi)\oplus\tau(\gamma))\otimes\frac{1}{2}}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=\\
\max(0,(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))+\frac{1}{2}-1)=\\
\max(0,(f_{\tau(\psi)}+f_{\tau(\gamma)})(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))-\frac{1}{2})=\\
\max(0,(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-\frac{1}{2}).

Since by the induction hypothesis

(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\in[\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}},\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k-l+1}}]

we can conclude that

f_{\tau(\psi+\gamma)}(r_{M,k}(a_{1}),\dots,r_{M,k}(a_{n}))=(r_{M,k}\circ f_{\psi}+r_{M,k}\circ f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))-\frac{1}{2}=\\
\frac{(f_{\psi}+f_{\gamma})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})}{2^{M+k+1}}+\frac{1}{2}=r_{M,k}(f_{\psi+\gamma}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})).

This completes the second case and thus concludes the proof. ∎

.

(2)

Equivalently, for any

Proof.

We only prove the first equivalence, the second following from the first one using the fact that

Now assume the induction hypothesis for open formulas

Theorem 4.6
.

The existential theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
reduces in polynomial time to the existential theory of
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
. Therefore, the former theory is in NP.

It follows that the universal theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
is in coNP.

12

that the equational theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
is coNP-hard. Hence the universal theory and the quasiequational theory of
{\boldsymbol{\mathit{R}}}_{-1}
are coNP-complete and the existential theory is NP-complete.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let

The functions

\sigma_{q}
on
\mathit{Tm}_{{\mathrm{MV}}}

1.

\sigma_{q}(x)=(x\lor q)\land\frac{1}{2}

;

2.

\sigma_{q}(1)=\frac{1}{2}
;

3.

\sigma_{q}(0)=q
;

4.

5.

6.

\sigma_{q}(\varphi\circ\xi)=\sigma_{q}(\varphi)\circ\sigma_{q}(\xi)
for
\circ\in\{\land,\lor\}
.

Moreover, for any term
\psi
in
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{MV}}}
,
\sharp(\sigma_{q}(\psi))
is polynomial in
\sharp(\psi)
.

Proof.

By induction on term depth.

•

Base case (depth 0): By definition
\sigma_{q}(x)=(x\lor q)\land\frac{1}{2}=\tau_{q}(\delta(x))
,
\sigma_{q}(1)=\frac{1}{2}=\tau_{q}(\delta(1))
and
\sigma_{q}(0)=q=\tau_{q}(\delta(0))
.

Now assume that
f_{\sigma_{q}(\psi)}=f_{\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi))}
and
f_{\sigma_{q}(\gamma)}=f_{\tau_{q}(\delta(\gamma))}
.

•

Clearly, for
\varphi=\psi\circ\gamma
we have
f_{\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi\circ\gamma))}=f_{\sigma_{q}(\psi\circ\gamma)}
for
\circ\in\{\lor,\land\}
.

•

For
\varphi=\psi\rightarrow\gamma
we have

f_{\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi\rightarrow\gamma))}=f_{\tau_{q}((\delta(\gamma)-\delta(\psi))\land 0)}=f_{((\neg\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi))\oplus\tau_{q}(\delta(\gamma)))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\neg\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi))\otimes\tau_{q}(\delta(\gamma)))))\land\frac{1}{2}}.

By the induction hypothesis this is equivalent to

f_{((\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma))))\land\frac{1}{2}}=\max(0,f_{\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma)}+f_{\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma))}-1)\land\frac{1}{2}.

Let us distinguish two cases.

1.

First assume
(f_{\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)}+f_{\sigma_{q}(\gamma)})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\geq 1
.
Thus,

f_{\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma)}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=1
and consequently

\max(0,(f_{\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma)}+f_{\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma))})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})-1)\land\frac{1}{2}=\\
\max(0,f_{\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma))}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}))\land\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}=\\
f_{((\sigma_{q}(\psi)\rightarrow\sigma_{q}(\gamma))\otimes\frac{1}{2})\land\frac{1}{2}}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=f_{\sigma_{q}(\psi\rightarrow\gamma)}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n}).

2.

For the other case assume
(f_{\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)}+f_{\sigma_{q}(\gamma)})(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\leq 1
. Then we have

f_{((\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma))))\land\frac{1}{2}}=f_{((\neg\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma))\otimes\frac{1}{2})\land\frac{1}{2}}=f_{\sigma_{q}(\psi\rightarrow\gamma)}.

This completes the second case.

•

For
\varphi=\psi\otimes\gamma
we have

f_{\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi\otimes\gamma))}=f_{\tau_{q}((\delta(\psi)+\delta(\gamma))\lor-1)}=f_{((\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi))\oplus\tau_{q}(\delta(\gamma)))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi))\otimes\tau_{q}(\delta(\gamma)))))\lor q}.

By induction hypothesis this is equal to

f_{((\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma))\otimes(\frac{1}{2}\oplus(\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma))))\lor q}.

Since
f_{\sigma_{q}(\gamma)}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\leq\frac{1}{2}
for each
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
and for each
\gamma\in\mathit{Tm}_{{\mathrm{MV}}}
, we have for each
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
that
f_{\sigma_{q}(\psi)\otimes\sigma_{q}(\gamma)}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=0.

Thus,

f_{\tau_{q}(\delta(\psi\otimes\gamma))}=f_{((\sigma_{q}(\psi)\oplus\sigma_{q}(\gamma))\otimes\frac{1}{2})\lor q}.

This completes the second case and also the proof. ∎

Theorem 5.6

(A translation of the existential theory of
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
to itself)
.

Let
\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
be an open formula of
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{MV}}}
.
Assume that the variables
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{M+k+1},q\}
do not occur in
\Phi
.
Let
M,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\zeta(\Phi)
be obtained from
\Phi
by applying
\sigma^{\prime}_{q}
on
\Phi
and then replacing all occurrences of the constant
\frac{1}{2}
with the variable
z_{1}
.
Let
S(\Phi,M,k)
be the following open formula in
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{MV}}}
:

\zeta(\Phi)\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt(z_{1}=\neg z_{1})\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt(q=\neg(z_{1}\oplus z_{M+k+1}))\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\bigwedge\kern-11.0pt\bigwedge}_{i\leq M+k}(z_{i+1}\oplus z_{i+1}=z_{i}).

Then
\Phi
has a satisfying assignment in
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}

if and only if
S(\Phi,M,k)
has a satisfying assignment in
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
.
Moreover,
\sharp(S(\Phi,M,k))
is polynomial in
\sharp(\Phi)
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Phi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
has a satisfying assignment
v(x_{i})=a_{i}
with
a_{i}\in{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
. By Lemma
5.2

\delta^{\prime}(\Phi)
has a satisfying assignment
x_{i}\mapsto a_{i}-1
with
(a_{i}-1)\in[-1,0]
.

By Lemma
5.5

\tau^{\prime}_{q}(\delta^{\prime}(\Phi))
has a satisfying assignment
x_{i}\mapsto r_{M,k}(a_{i}-1)
and
q\mapsto\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{M+k+1}}
in
[0,1]_{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}^{{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
. By Lemma
5.4

\sigma^{\prime}_{q}(\Phi)
is satisfied by the very same assignment.
Consequently,
\zeta(\Phi)
has satisfying assignment
x_{i}\mapsto r_{M,k}(a_{i}-1),q\mapsto\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{M+k+1}}
and
z_{1}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}
in
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
.

By Lemma
4.4
, the formula
(z_{1}=\neg z_{1})\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt(q=\neg(z_{1}\oplus z_{M+k+1}))\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\bigwedge\kern-11.0pt\bigwedge}_{i\leq M+k}(z_{i+1}\oplus z_{i+1}=z_{i})
has the unique satisfying assignment in
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
, which
sends
z_{i}
to
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2^{i}}}
for each
i\leq M+k
and
q
to
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{M+k+1}}
.

Conversely, assume
S(\Phi,M,k)
has a satisfying assignment
v
in
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
. The conjunct

(z_{1}=\neg z_{1})\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt(q=\neg(z_{1}\oplus z_{M+k+1}))\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\wedge\kern-8.7pt\wedge}\hskip 0.9pt\mathop{\bigwedge\kern-11.0pt\bigwedge}_{i\leq M+k}(z_{i+1}\oplus z_{i+1}=z_{i})

forces by Lemma
4.4
that
v(z_{1})=\frac{1}{2}
and
v(q)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{M+k+1}}
. Observe that by the definition of
\zeta
, every occurrence of a variable
x_{i}
in
\zeta(\Phi)
appears strictly within the term
(x_{i}\lor q)\land z_{1}
for each
i\leq n
. Therefore, we can assume without loss of generality that
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{M+k+1}}=v(q)\leq v(x_{i})\leq v(z_{1})=\frac{1}{2}
for all
i\leq n
.
Since
v
satisfies
S(\Phi,M,k)
, it must satisfy
\zeta(\Phi)
. By definition of
\zeta
, this means the formula
\sigma^{\prime}_{q}(\Phi)
is satisfied by the assignment
v
in
[0,1]_{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}^{{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
. By Lemma
5.4
the assignment
v
also satisfies
\tau^{\prime}_{q}(\delta^{\prime}(\Phi))
in
[0,1]_{{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}^{{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}
.
We can now apply Lemma
5.5
.

Since

and

.
Finally, applying Lemma

{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}

The last theorem can be viewed as providing a way of testing the validity of existential
sentences in
{[0,1]_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\L}}{\mbox{\rm\scriptsize\L}}{\mbox{\rm\tiny\L}}}}
on a subinterval of its domain (rather than the full domain), provided the value
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
belongs to this interval. The statement relies on the well-known fact that rational values in
[0,1]
are implicitly definable by
\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{MV}}}
-terms, and moreover, the size of the defining term is polynomial in the length of the positional representation of the rational number in question.

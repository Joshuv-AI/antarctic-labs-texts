On Explicit Estimations for the Bloch Eigenvalues of the One-dimensional Schrödinger Operator and the Kronig-Penney Model
Cemile Nur*, Oktay Veliev**

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the small and large eigenvalues of the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator L ⁡ ( q ) L(q) with a periodic, real and locally integrable potential q q . First we explicitly write out the first and second terms of the asymptotic formulas for the large periodic and antiperiodic eigenvalues and illustrate these formulas for the Kronig-Penney model. Then we give estimates for the small periodic and antiperiodic eigenvalues and for the length of the first gaps in the case of the Kronig-Penney model. Moreover, we give error estimations and present a numerical example. Key Words: One-dimensional Schrödinger operator, Periodic potential, Kronig-Penney model. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 34L20, 47E05.

In the present paper, we investigate the spectrum of the operator

(1)

Finally, note that the Kronig-Penney model is a simplified approach to

Asymptotic Formulas for the Large Eigenvalues

To obtain explicit formulas for the large eigenvalues from (
14
), we
use the following lemmas.

Lemma 1

If
q\in L_{1}[0,1]
, then the following formula holds:

a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})=\frac{\left|q_{2n}\right|^{2}}{-16\pi^{2}n^{2}}+D(2n),

(18)

where

D(k)=:\frac{i}{2\pi k}\int_{0}^{1}q(x)(Q(x,k)-Q_{k,0}))e^{-i2\pi kx}\,dx,

Q(x,k)=\int_{0}^{x}q(t)e^{i2\pi kt}\,dt-q_{-k}x,\text{ }Q_{k,0}=\int_{0}^{1}Q(x,k)dx.

Proof.
Since
q_{-k}=\overline{q_{k}}
,
q_{0}=0
, from the definition of

a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})
(see introduction), we obtain

a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2\pi k(4\pi n-2\pi k)}.

Using the equality

\frac{1}{2\pi k(4\pi n-2\pi k)}=\frac{1}{4\pi n}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi k}+\frac{1}{4\pi n-2\pi k}\right),

(19)

we see that

a_{1}((2\pi n)^{2})=\frac{1}{4\pi n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2\pi k}+\frac{1}{4\pi n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{(4\pi n-2\pi k)}.

(20)

Since

\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2\pi k}+\frac{\left|q_{-k}\right|^{2}}{2\pi(-k)}=0,

we have the following equality for the first term on the right side
of (
20
):

\frac{1}{4\pi n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\left|q_{k}\right|^{2}}{2\pi k}=\frac{\left|q_{2n}\right|^{2}}{-16\pi^{2}n^{2}}.

(21)

Now let us estimate the second term on the right side of (
20
). For
this, we consider the Fourier coefficients of
Q(x,2n)
. It is clear that

Q(1,2n)=Q(0,2n)=0,\quad Q^{\prime}(x,2n)=q(x)e^{i4\pi nx}\,-q_{-2n}.

Therefore, using the integration by parts, we see that the Fourier
coefficients
Q_{2n,2n-k}=(Q(x,2n),e^{i2\pi(2n-k)x})
of
Q(x,2n)
with
respect to the orthonormal basis
\left\{e^{2\pi ikx}:k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
, for
2n-k\neq 0
, are

Q_{2n,2n-k}=\frac{q_{-k}}{i(4\pi n-2\pi k)}.

Moreover,
q_{0}=0
according to (
2
). Thus, the Fourier decomposition
of
Q(x,2n)
has the form

Q(x,2n)=Q_{2n,0}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=-\infty\\
k\neq 0,2n\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{q_{-k}e^{i2\pi(2n-k)x}\,}{i(4\pi n-2\pi k)}.

(22)

Thus, (
18
) follows from (
20
)-(
22
). The lemma is proved.

Now we consider
a_{2}((2\pi n)^{2})
. It is clear that

\displaystyle a_{2}((2\pi n)^{2})=\sum\limits_{k,k+l\neq 0,2n}\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{[(2\pi n)^{2}-(2\pi(n-k))^{2}][(2\pi n)^{2}-(2\pi(n-k-l))^{2}]}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{k,l}\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{2\pi k(4\pi n-2\pi k)(2\pi k+2\pi l)(4\pi n-2\pi k-2\pi l)}.

Using (
19
) and

\frac{1}{(k+l)(2n-k-l)}=\frac{1}{2n}\left(\frac{1}{k+l}+\frac{1}{2n-k-l}\right),

we obtain that

a_{2}((2\pi n)^{2})=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}n^{2}}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}),

(23)

where

I_{1}(q)=\sum_{k,l}\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{2\pi k(2\pi k+2\pi l)},

I_{2}(n,q)=\sum_{k,l}\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{(4\pi n-2\pi k)(2\pi k+2\pi l)},

I_{3}(n,q)=\sum_{k,l}\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{2\pi k(4\pi n-2\pi k-2\pi l)},

and

I_{4}(n,q)=\sum_{k,l}\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{(4\pi n-2\pi k)(4\pi n-2\pi k-2\pi l)}.

Now we estimate
I_{1}(q)
and
I_{j}(n,q)
, for
j=2,3,4

Lemma 2

The equality
I_{1}(q)=0
holds.

Proof.
We prove this lemma in two ways. First grouping the terms

\displaystyle\frac{q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l}}{4\pi^{2}k(k+l)},\quad\frac{q_{l}q_{k}q_{-k-l}}{4\pi^{2}l(k+l)},\quad\frac{q_{k}q_{-k-l}q_{l}}{4\pi^{2}k(-l)},

\displaystyle\frac{q_{l}q_{-k-l}q_{k}}{4\pi^{2}l(-k)},\quad\frac{q_{-k-l}q_{l}q_{k}}{4\pi^{2}(-k-l)(-k)},\quad\frac{q_{-k-l}q_{k}q_{l}}{4\pi^{2}(-k-l)(-l)}

with the equal multiplicands,

\displaystyle q_{k}q_{l}q_{-k-l},\quad q_{l}q_{k}q_{-k-l},\quad q_{k}q_{-k-l}q_{l},

\displaystyle q_{l}q_{-k-l}q_{k},\quad q_{-k-l}q_{l}q_{k},\quad q_{-k-l}q_{k}q_{l}

and using the equality

\frac{1}{k(k+l)}+\frac{1}{l(k+l)}=\frac{1}{kl}

we obtain the proof of the lemma.

The second way is the following. Using the integration by parts one can easily
verify that the Fourier coefficients
Q_{k}=(Q,\,e^{2\pi ikx})\
of

(see (

The equalities

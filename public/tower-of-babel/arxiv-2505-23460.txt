Spin-current correlations in photoionization of chiral molecules
Philip Caesar M. Flores, Stefanos Carlström, Serguei Patchkovskii, Misha Ivanov, Andres F. Ordonez, Olga Smirnova

Abstract
Chirality-induced spin selectivity (CISS) refers to phenomena where molecular chirality governs spin polarization. While symmetry simply requires chiral molecules to support spin-vector correlations, we show that CISS is fundamentally a conditioned measurement of these correlations. We illustrate this principle for spin-resolved one-photon ionization of a randomly oriented ensemble of chiral molecules. We introduce and quantify the phenomenon of enantio-sensitive locking of the photoelectron current to its spin, thereby providing a complete description of spin-conditioned photoelectron currents in one-photon ionization.

These observations suggest that CISS-related phenomena are fundamentally linked to conditioned measurements, namely correlations

Starting from this perspective, we consider the simplest realization of chiral spin filtering, photoionization of an ensemble of randomly oriented chiral molecules, and identify the origin of the corresponding pseudoscalar correlations. In particular, we consider the case in which all external biases, such as molecular alignment or light polarization, are removed: a scenario that has not yet been examined elsewhere.

Consider one-photon ionization of randomly oriented chiral molecules under isotropic illumination. To detect a net current, one needs to measure photoelectrons in all possible directions. Since the measurement, the system, and the light are completely isotropic, then there will be no net current in the system. However, if one introduces a spin detection axis,
\bm{\hat{s}}
, then it breaks the symmetry and a net current along
\bm{\hat{s}}
is symmetry allowed (see Fig.
1

Since the current is a time-odd polar vector while spin is a time-odd axial vector, then the projection of the current on the spin axis is a time-even pseudoscalar. Its pseudoscalar nature dictates that such current can only emerge in chiral molecules. As a time even quantity, it does not require breaking of the time-reversal symmetry of the Hamiltonian, therefore, the current is absent without spin conditioning and emerges as soon as the measurement is either spin-conditioned or postselected.

To address the strength and nature of the these pseudoscalar correlations, we use our geometric formalism developed for spin-resolved one-photon photoionization of chiral molecules

It allows us to identify and quantify the molecular pseudoscalrs underlying both time-even spin-current correlations as well as time-odd triple correlations between the spin of the photoelectron, the photoelectron current, and the photon spin. We show that these correlations are enabled by two complementary geometric mechanisms that arise solely from electric dipole interactions and the geometric properties of the photoionization dipoles in real space and spin space.

\bm{\vec{\mathcal{S}}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}}

\vec{\mathbb{B}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}}
which enables orthogonal triple locking between the photoelectron spin, its current and the photon spin. We have verified that the spin-conditioned photoelectron currents derived in Eqs. (

, and (ii) vectors that are fixed in the laboratory frame such as spin detection axis
\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}
and the electric field
\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}
. This will then allow us to use the technique in Ref.
[
2
]
in evaluating the orientation averaging
\int d\rho
such that the resulting quantity can be expressed as
\sum_{ij}g_{i}M_{ij}f_{j}
, where,
g_{i}
and
f_{i}
are rotational invariants that are constructed from the two sets of vectors and
M_{ij}
is the coupling between the two rotational invariants. For our purposes, the following vector identities will be relevant:

\displaystyle\int d\rho(\bm{\vec{a}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{u}}^{L})\bm{\vec{b}}^{L}=\dfrac{1}{3}(\bm{\vec{a}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{b}}^{M})\bm{\vec{u}}^{L}

(17)

\displaystyle\int d\rho(\bm{\vec{a}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{u}}^{L})(\bm{\vec{b}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{v}}^{L})\bm{\vec{c}}^{L}=\dfrac{1}{6}[(\bm{\vec{a}}^{M}\times\bm{\vec{b}}^{M})\cdot\bm{\vec{c}}^{M}](\bm{\vec{u}}^{L}\times\bm{\vec{v}}^{L})

(18)

\displaystyle\int d\rho(\bm{\vec{a}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{u}}^{L})(\bm{\vec{b}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{v}}^{L})(\bm{\vec{c}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{w}}^{L})\bm{\vec{d}}^{L}=\dfrac{1}{30}\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{\vec{a}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{b}}^{M})(\bm{\vec{c}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{d}}^{M})\\
(\bm{\vec{a}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{c}}^{M})(\bm{\vec{b}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{d}}^{M})\\
(\bm{\vec{a}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{d}}^{M})(\bm{\vec{b}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{c}}^{M})\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}4&-1&-1\\
-1&4&-1\\
-1&-1&4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(\bm{\vec{u}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{v}}^{L})\bm{\vec{w}}^{L}\\
(\bm{\vec{u}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{w}}^{L})\bm{\vec{v}}^{L}\\
(\bm{\vec{v}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{w}}^{L})\bm{\vec{u}}^{L}\end{bmatrix}

(19)

Using Eqs. (
17
)-(
18
), we can easily evaluate the normalization factor as follows:

\displaystyle\int d\rho

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\int d\Theta_{k}^{L}W^{L}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{L},\bm{\hat{s}}^{L},\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\int d\rho W^{L}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{M},\bm{\hat{s}}^{M},\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\int d\rho\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{L*}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}^{L*}\right)\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}\right)\left(\delta_{\mu,\nu}+\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{L}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{6}\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\left[\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\cdot\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{M}\right)|\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}|^{2}\delta_{\mu,\nu}\right]

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{12}\left\{\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\left[\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{M}\cdot\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\times\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{M}\right)\right]\right\}\left[\left(\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}\right)\cdot\left(\bm{\vec{E}}^{L*}\times\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{6}\left(\sum_{\mu}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left|\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M}\right|^{2}\right)|\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}|^{2}

(20)

The second line follows from the definition of a rotated function
W^{L}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{L},\bm{\hat{s}}^{L},\rho)=W^{M}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{M},\bm{\hat{s}}^{M},\rho)

[
6
]
, and we have interchanged the order of integration to perform change of variable
\bm{\hat{k}}^{M}=R_{\rho}^{-1}\bm{\hat{k}}^{L}
and again interchanged the order of integration since
\bm{\hat{k}}^{M}
is now an integration variable independent of
\rho
. Meanwhile, the last line follows from the vanishing of
\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}
.

Let us now consider the spin-conditioned photoelectron current under isotropic illumination:

\bm{\vec{j}}_{\text{iso}}^{L}=\dfrac{\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho\int d\Theta_{k}^{L}W^{L}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{L},\bm{\hat{s}}^{L},\rho)\bm{\vec{k}}^{L}}{\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho\int d\Theta_{s}^{L}\int d\Theta_{k}^{L}W^{L}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{L},\bm{\hat{s}}^{L},\rho)},

(21)

where,
\int d\Theta_{p}
denotes averaging over all orientations of the light field:

\displaystyle\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}_{p}=E_{\omega}^{L}\left(\sin\theta_{p}\cos\varphi_{p}\bm{\hat{x}}^{L}+\sin\theta_{p}\sin\varphi_{p}\bm{\hat{y}}^{L}+\cos\theta_{p}\bm{\hat{z}}^{L}\right)

(22)

The relevant quantity to calculate is the the numerator of Eq. (
21
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho W^{M}(\bm{\hat{k}}^{M},\bm{\hat{s}}^{M},\rho)\bm{\vec{k}}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu_{1}^{M},\mu_{2}^{M}}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{L*}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L*}\right)\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}\right)\left(\delta_{\mu,\nu}+\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}\right)\bm{\vec{k}}^{L}

(23)

Using Eq. (
18
), the first term of Eq. (
23
) simplifies into

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu}

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho\left|\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}\right|^{2}\bm{\vec{k}}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{12}\left\{\left[\sum_{\mu}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\times\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M}\right)\right]\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right\}\left[\int d\Theta_{p}\left(\bm{\vec{E}}^{L*}_{p}\times\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}_{p}\right)\right]=0,

(24)

which vanishes after averaging over all orientations of the field. Similarly, it follows from Eq. (
19
) that the second term of Eq. (
21
) is now

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{L*}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L*}\right)\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{I,\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}\right)\left(\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{L}\right)\bm{\vec{k}}^{L}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{60}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\sum\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\cdot\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{M}\right)\left(\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right)\\
\sum\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{M}\right)\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right)\\
\sum\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right)\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{M}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{M}\right)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}4&-1&-1\\
-1&4&-1\\
-1&-1&4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\int d\Theta_{p}|\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}|^{2}\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}\\
\int d\Theta_{p}(\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L*}\cdot\bm{\hat{s}}^{L})\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}\\
\int d\Theta_{p}(\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}\cdot\bm{\hat{s}}^{L})\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L*}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{60}

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}g_{1}\\
g_{2}\\
g_{2}^{*}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}4&-1&-1\\
-1&4&-1\\
-1&-1&4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}\\

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{30}

\displaystyle\left(2f_{1}-\text{Re}[f_{2}]\right)g_{1}-\dfrac{1}{30}\left(f_{1}-3\text{Re}[f_{2}]\right)\text{Re}[g_{2}]-\dfrac{1}{6}\text{Im}[f_{2}]\text{Im}[g_{2}]

(25)

Averaging over all orientations of the field we get:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\mu,\nu}\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}

\displaystyle\int d\Theta_{p}\int d\rho\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{L*}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L*}\right)\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{I,\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{L}\cdot\bm{\vec{E}}_{p}^{L}\right)\left(\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}\cdot\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{L}\right)\bm{\vec{k}}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{18}|\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}|^{2}\left\{\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left[\sum_{\mu,\nu}\left(\bm{\vec{D}}_{I,\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\mu}^{M*}\cdot\bm{\vec{D}}_{I,\bm{\vec{k}}^{M},\nu}^{M}\right)\bm{\hat{\sigma}}_{\nu,\mu}^{M}\right]\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right\}\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{18}|\bm{\vec{E}}^{L}|^{2}\left[\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left(\bm{\vec{\mathcal{S}}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right)\right]\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}.

(26)

Thus,

\displaystyle\bm{\vec{j}}_{\text{iso}}^{L}=\dfrac{1}{3S_{0}}\left[\int d\Theta_{k}^{M}\left(\bm{\vec{\mathcal{S}}}_{\bm{\vec{k}}}^{M}\cdot\bm{\vec{k}}^{M}\right)\right]\bm{\hat{s}}^{L}

(27a)

\displaystyle S_{0}=\int d\Theta_{k}\left(\left|\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}},\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}+\left|\bm{\vec{D}}_{\bm{\vec{k}},-\frac{1}{2}}\right|^{2}\right).

(27b)

The same procedure is done for both linearly and circularly polarized light.

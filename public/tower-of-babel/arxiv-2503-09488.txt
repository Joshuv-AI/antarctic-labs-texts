Log Geometric Models for Little Disks Operads in Even Dimensions
Oliver Lindström

Abstract
We construct a model for the (non-unital) S 1 S^{1} -framed little 2 ​ d 2d -dimensional disks operad for any positive integer d d using logarithmic geometry. We also show that the unframed little 2 ​ d 2d -dimensional disks operad has a model which can be constructed using log schemes with virtual morphisms.

Introduction

A recent construction of Dmitry Vaintrob
[
Vai21
,
Vai19
]
, shows that the framed little
2
-dimensional disks (pseudo)-operad can be modeled as the analytification of a pseudo-operad in log schemes whose underlying schemes are
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
, the moduli spaces of stable
(n+1)
-pointed rational curves of genus
0
. See also the expositions of Vaintrob’s construction in
[
BDPW23
]
and
[
DPP24
]
.

This article gives a generalization of Vaintrob’s construction to arbitrary even dimension. For every positive integer
d
, we construct a pseudo-operad
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
in the category of log varieties whose analytification is weakly equivalent to the
2d
-dimensional
S^{1}
-framed little disks. We write
S^{1}
for the group
U(1)\cong\textnormal{SO}(2)
and when we refer to an operad
O

framed
by some group
G
which acts on
O
, what is meant is the semidirect operadic product
\textnormal{{O}}\rtimes G

[
SW03
]
. The underlying spaces of the log varieties in
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
are the moduli spaces for stable
n
-pointed rooted trees of
d
-dimensional projective spaces, introduced by Chen, Gibney, and Krashen in
[
CGK09
]
and denoted
T_{d,n}
. The connection to Vaintrob’s result is that these moduli spaces are higher dimensional analogs of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
and in particular we have that
T_{1,n}\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
.
Specifically, we show that the analytification
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
is homeomorphic to
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
, the
S^{1}
-framed Fulton–MacPherson operad. By a result of Salvatore
[
Sal01
]

\textnormal{{FM}}_{n}
is
\textnormal{SO}(n)
-equivariantly weakly equivalent to
\textnormal{{LD}}_{n}
, the classical little disks operad in dimension
n
. Thus, this homeomorphism gives a weak equivalence between the analytification of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
and
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
.

Theorem
5.12
.

The Kato–Nakayama analytification of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
is homeomorphic to
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
.

We will also show that the individual spaces
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}(n)
are analytifications of log varieties; however, it will not be possible to construct an operad of log schemes with these as objects whose analytification is
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
. Nevertheless, an operad with this property, which we denote
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{d}
, can be constructed in the category of log schemes with
virtual morphisms
. Virtual morphisms of log schemes were introduced by Howell
[
How17
]
and further studied by Dupont, Panzer, and Pym in a recent article
[
DPP24
]
, in which they also show that
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2}
is homeomorphic to the analytification of an operad in log schemes with virtual morphisms.

Theorem
5.13
.

The Kato–Nakayama analytification of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{d}
is homeomorphic to
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
.

This has interesting consequences for the cohomology of
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
and
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2d}
. First,
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
and
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{d}
are defined over
\mathbb{Q}
(and even over
\mathbb{Z}
). This induces a Galois action on the corresponding étale cohomology cooperads which lifts to an action on the level of étale cochains. If the weights of cohomology are
pure
in a suitable sense the existence of such lifts implies formality of the corresponding operad. See for example Petersen
[
Pet14
]
. Additionally, using the log geometric structure of these operads one can define mixed Hodge structures on the respective cohomology cooperads.
Such mixed Hodge structures can also be tools for proving operad formality. In fact, Dupont, Panzer, and Pym use the fact that the Hodge structure on the
k
th cohomology of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{1}
is pure of weight
2k
to re-prove that
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2}
is formal.
It should be noted that all log varieties studied in this article are either smooth varieties with log structure associated to a smooth strict normal crossings divisor or smooth, closed, strong deformation retracts thereof with pulled back log structures. Therefore, it is known how one should define mixed Hodge structures for the log varieties studied in this article but in general the theory of Hodge structures in logarithmic geometry is still under development and there is, to the authors knowledge, no explicit construction of a mixed Hodge structure functor on fs log varieties, even if we restrict to the case of Deligne–Faltings log varieties. Naturally, there is even less material on the matter for log varieties with virtual morphisms. Considering this, it is debatable if the results of this article alone proves that there is a mixed Hodge structure on the cohomologies of
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2d}
and
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
but hopefully it will be clear to the familiar reader how one can define such a structure using our constructions.

From this one may expect that the construction of this article would imply that either of these formality arguments could be used to prove that the
S^{1}
-framed little disks are formal in arbitrary even dimension. However, the argument requires, in some sense, that the weights of cohomology are pure and this is no longer the case for
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
when
d\geq 2
. For example, we can create a zig-zag of homotopy equivalences coming from morphisms of log-schemes from
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}(n)
to
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{d})\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
and thus the MHS (mixed Hodge structures) on both these spaces are isomorphic. We know that the MHS on
H^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{Q})
is pure of weight
2
and similarly, the MHS on
H^{2d-1}(\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}),\mathbb{Q})
is pure of weight
2d
. For
d=2
this implies that the MHS on
H^{3}(\textnormal{Conf}_{3}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2})\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{3},\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}(-2)\oplus\mathbb{Q}(-3)
is mixed with weights
4
and
6
. In fact, the real model for
\textnormal{{LD}}_{n}\rtimes\textnormal{SO}(n)
by Khoroshkin and Willwacher in
[
KW25
]
can be used to construct a real model for
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2m}\rtimes S^{1}
and using this model one can show that
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2m}\rtimes S^{1}
is not formal.

1.1
Structure and Results

Section
2
gives a short introduction of logarithmic geometry and in particular we give a definition of Deligne–Faltings, or DF, log schemes and describe their relationship with “normal” log schemes. We also define the real oriented blow-up of a topological space in a section of a vector bundle and we explain how this can be used to give an equivalent definition of the so called Kato–Nakayama analytification
[
KN99
]
of a DF log scheme. Finally, we use the notion of virtual morphisms of log schemes
[
How17
,
DPP24
]
to give a definition of a virtual morphism of DF log schemes and explain why this is interesting.

In
Section

3
we recall the definition of the topological Fulton–MacPherson compactification and the Fulton–MacPherson model of the little
D
-dimensional disks operad, denoted
\textnormal{{FM}}_{D}

[
Kon94
,
AS94
,
Sin04
,
GJ94
]
. We describe an action by
\textnormal{SO}(D)
on
\textnormal{{FM}}_{D}
which is compatible with the corresponding action on
\textnormal{{LD}}_{D}
, the
D
-dimensional little disks operad. In the case where
D=2d
is even this gives an induced action by
S^{1}\cong\textnormal{SO}(2)
via the diagonal embedding
\textnormal{SO}(2)\hookrightarrow\textnormal{SO}(d)
. This action allows us to define the
S^{1}
-framed Fulton–MacPherson operad
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
which is weakly equivalent to the
S^{1}
-framed little disks operad.

In
Section

4
we recall the definition of the schemes
T_{d,n}
introduced by Chen, Gibney, and Krashen
[
CGK09
]
.
We use a functor of points description of these schemes to define an operad in schemes with objects
T_{d,n}
for each fixed

. We call this operad the Chen, Gibney, Krashen operad, denoted
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
.

Building on this, in
Section

5
we define Deligne–Faltings log varieties
\mathsf{T}_{d,n}
whose underlying varieties are

and we extend the symmetry and composition morphisms (but not the unit morphism) of
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
to maps of these log varieties. This defines a pseudo-operad of log schemes,
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}

\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
is
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
. We describe how to define the unit morphism of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
as a virtual morphism of log schemes and furthermore we show that with virtual morphisms of log schemes we can also define an operad
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{d}
whose analytification is
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
.

2
Deligne–Faltings Log Schemes

We will give all definitions and results relating to logarithmic geometry in this article in terms of Deligne–Faltings log structures. The main reasons for this are that some definitions and results are easier to state using this terminology and, perhaps more importantly, the Kato–Nakayama analytification of a Deligne–Faltings log scheme can be explicitly described in terms of real oriented blow-ups which will turn out to be very useful for the purposes of this article. The precise definition of a DF log scheme may vary between articles but we will use that of Bergström, Diaconu, Petersen, and Westerland
[
BDPW23
]
. In this section we will on some occasions reference “normal” log structures and therefore we will spell out the prefix “DF”. This will not be the case in remaining sections and therefore “log scheme” should be taken to mean “DF log scheme” outside of
Section

2
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Deligne–Faltings (or DF) log structure
on a scheme
X
is a finite tuple
\mathfrak{L}=(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
of invertible sheaves with sections.
A morphism of log structures on
X
,

(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\to(t_{j}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}

is a collection
\{e_{ij}\}
of non-negative integers together with
n
isomorphisms of line bundles

\mathcal{L}_{i}\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq m}\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\otimes e_{ij}}

which also identify the sections
s_{i}
to the corresponding sections
\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq m}t_{j}^{\otimes e_{ij}}
.

Remark
.

For those with a sufficient background in log geometry: A DF log structure
\mathfrak{L}=(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
on some scheme
X
induces a log structure
M(\mathfrak{L})=\mathcal{M}^{\log}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
where
\mathcal{M}^{\log}
is the logification of the pre-log structure

\mathcal{M}(U)=\bigsqcup_{\alpha_{i}\geq 0}((\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\vee})^{\otimes\alpha_{1}})^{*}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal{(}(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\vee})^{\otimes\alpha_{n}})^{*}

with the obvious monoid structure and with the map to
\mathcal{O}_{X}
induced by
s_{i}^{\vee}\colon\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
. The superscript
*
here indicates the subsheaf of invertible sections. A morphism of DF log structures induces a map of corresponding log structures in the obvious, functorial, way.

Definition 2.2
.

A
DF log scheme

\mathsf{X}=(X,\mathfrak{L})
is a scheme
X
with a DF log structure
\mathfrak{L}
. We call
X
the
underlying scheme
of
\mathsf{X}
, sometimes denoted
\underline{\mathsf{X}}
.
A morphism of DF log schemes
(X,\mathfrak{L})\to(Y,\mathfrak{M})
is a morphism of schemes
f\colon X\to Y
and a morphism of DF log structures
f^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{L}
.
Such a morphism is said to be
strict
if
f^{*}\mathfrak{M}\to\mathfrak{L}
is an isomorphism of DF log structures.

An important special case of a DF log scheme is a scheme with the trivial DF log structure, i.e. no line bundles. For a scheme
X
we call
(X,\emptyset)
the
log scheme associated to
X
and we will, by abuse of notation, simply denote this by
X
. If
\mathsf{Y}
is a log scheme and
f\colon\underline{\mathsf{Y}}\to X
is a map of schemes then there is a unique morphism of log schemes
\mathsf{Y}\to X
with underlying map
f
. Similarly a morphism
f\colon X\to\underline{\mathsf{Y}}
gives a morphism of log schemes
X\to\mathsf{Y}
if and only if the pullbacks via
f
of all line bundles with sections in the log structure of
\mathsf{Y}
are trivial with a unit section and if this is the case then this morphism of log schemes is unique.

2.1
Blow-Ups

In this section we define real oriented blow-ups in sections of line bundles and prove some results relating to these. These will be used to define the Kato–Nakayama analytification of a log scheme.

First, let
X
be a topological space, let
\eta\colon E\to X
be a rank
n
vector bundle and let
s\colon X\to E
be a section. Furthermore, let
E_{0}
denote the image of the zero section and let
E^{\prime}\vcentcolon=E\setminus E_{0}
. Note that the action by
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
(and in fact the action by
\mathbb{R}^{*}
) on
E
restricts to a free action on
E^{\prime}
. We define the real oriented blow-up of
X
in
s
, denoted
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\ X
as the space

\{p\in E^{\prime}\ |\ \exists\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\colon\ p=\alpha\cdot(s\circ\eta)(p)\}/\mathbb{R}_{>0}

and we define the blow-down map
\rho\colon\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\ X\to X
as the map induced by passing
p\colon E\to X
to the quotient. Note that
\rho
has an inverse away from the zero locus of
s
and for any
x
in the zero locus of
s
we have that
\rho^{-1}(x)\cong S^{n-1}
.

The “usual” definition of the real oriented blow-up of a smooth manifold
X
in a smooth closed sub manifold
Y
, denoted
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{Y}\ X
is that it is the complement of a tubular neighbourhood of
Y
in
X
and the blow-down map is then taken to be a retraction of this neighbourhood onto
Y
. It is also possible to give a functorial definition as in
[
AK10
]
.
The following result, whose proof I will omit, relates this construction to the usual notion of a blow-up in a smooth closed submanifold.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
M
be a (smooth) manifold, let
E
be a vector bundle, let
s\colon M\to E
be a smooth section whose image intersects
E_{0}
transversally, and let
Y=s^{-1}(E_{0})
be the zero locus of
s
. Then there is a unique isomorphism of spaces over
M
,

\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\ M\cong\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{Y}\ M.

Remark
.

Note that a necessary, but not sufficient, condition here is that the codimension of
Y
is the rank of the vector bundle. This resembles the algebraic situation where if
X
is smooth and
Y
is a locally complete intersection the blow-up
\textnormal{Bl}_{Y}\ X
is locally a closed subscheme of a projective bundle over
X
.

Despite these properties one should be aware that
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\ X
does not always resemble the “usual” definition of a blow-up. For example if we take the blow-up in the zero section of
E
the result is (up to canonical isomorphism) the sphere bundle of
E
which, in the case where
X
is a manifold, has higher dimension than
X
. Even stranger situations are also possible and the blow-up of a manifold is not even necessarily a manifold with corners. For example let
s\colon S^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, considered as a section of a trivial bundle on
S^{2}
, be defined by
s(x,y,z)=(x-1,0)
. The real oriented blow-up
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\ S^{2}
is the wedge sum
S^{1}\vee S^{2}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a topological space, let
\pi\colon E\to X
be a complex line bundle, and let
E^{\prime}
be the complement of the zero section in
E
. Then
\pi|_{E^{\prime}}^{*}E\to E^{\prime}
is trivial.

Proof.

The diagonal map
E\to E\times_{X}E
restricts to a nowhere
0
section,
E^{\prime}\to\pi|_{E^{\prime}}^{*}E
. Thus
\pi|_{E^{\prime}}^{*}E
is a line bundle with a nowhere
0
section and is hence trivial.
∎

Remark
.

If
X
is a smooth manifold and
E
is a smooth bundle then
\pi|_{E^{\prime}}^{*}E\to E^{\prime}
is trivial as a smooth bundle by the same argument.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
s\colon X\to E
be any section.
The pullback of
E\to X
to the blow-up
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s}\ X
is a trivial bundle.

Proof.

By introducing a Hermitian metric, the blow-up can be embedded into
E^{\prime}
and the blow-down map factors via this embedding. Thus, the pullback of
E
to this blow-up is trivial by
Lemma

2.4
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{n}
be complex line bundles on a manifold
X
and let
L=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}^{\otimes e_{i}}
where
e_{i}
are integers and
\otimes
is the complex tensor product. Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}
be sections
\sigma_{i}\colon X\to L_{i}
. Then, there is an isomorphism

\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{\sigma}_{0}}\ \textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{\sigma}_{n}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}\ X\xrightarrow{\cong}\left(\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{\sigma}_{n}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\sigma_{1}}\ X\right)\times S^{1}

where
\tilde{\sigma}_{i}
denotes the pullback of
\sigma_{i}
through all previous morphisms and
\sigma_{0}\colon X\to L
is the zero section.

Proof.

This is immediate from the previous corollary.
∎

2.2
Kato–Nakayama Analytification

For a DF log scheme
\mathsf{X}=(X,(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n})
such that
X
is of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
one can construct an associated topological space
\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}
called its
Kato–Nakayama
analytification. This space is defined as the sequence of real oriented blow-ups

\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{s}_{n}}\ \textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{s}_{n-1}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s_{1}}\ X^{\textnormal{an}}

where
\tilde{s}_{i}
is the section
s_{i}
of the vector bundle
\mathcal{L}_{i}
pulled back via all previous blow-ups. This is order independent up to canonical isomorphism since, at each step, we are blowing up in the pullback (i.e. total, not strict, transform) of the corresponding line bundle with section.
Regardless of order we let
\rho_{\mathsf{X}}\colon\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}\to X^{\textnormal{an}}
denote the corresponding blow-down map. This construction can be made functorial in the following way.

First, if
f\colon(X,(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n})\to(Y,(t_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n})
is a strict morphism of log-schemes, i.e a morphism such that the log structure morphism is given by isomorphisms
f^{*}\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\otimes 1}
for each
i
, then we can form the following commutative and cartesian (with respect to the downward vertical arrows) diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{X}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{Y}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y(\mathbb{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\textnormal{an}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}

where we have
E_{X}^{1}=\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\textnormal{an}}
and
E_{Y}^{1}=\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\textnormal{an}}
and where
s,t
are the corresponding sections. The blow-ups
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s_{1}}\ X(\mathbb{C})
and
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{t_{1}}\ Y(\mathbb{C})
are subspaces of the quotients by an equivalence relation
\sim
on
E_{X}^{1}
and
E_{Y}^{1}
respectively where two vectors in a fibre are identified if they are equal up to
positive
scalar multiplication. By commutativity of the diagram, the subspace corresponding to the blow-up of
X(\mathbb{C})
is mapped to the blow-up of
Y(\mathbb{C})
as subspaces of
E_{X}^{1}/\sim
and
E_{X}^{1}/\sim
by the induced quotient space map. Thus, we get an induced subspace map
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s_{1}}\ X(\mathbb{C})\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{t_{1}}\ Y(\mathbb{C})
. Iterating this gives the desired map of the entire sequence of blow-ups.

Next, let
\phi\colon\mathfrak{M}=(t_{j}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}\to\mathfrak{L}=(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
be a morphism of DF log structures on
X
given by integers
\{e_{ij}\}
and isomorphisms

\phi_{i}\colon\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\bigotimes_{j}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes e_{ij}}
. For simplicity assume that all line bundles are trivial. The general case can be constructed from this by gluing.
Furthermore, let
\pi\colon(X,\mathfrak{L})^{\textnormal{KN}}\to X(\mathbb{C})
and
\rho\colon(X,\mathfrak{M})^{\textnormal{KN}}\to X(\mathbb{C})
denote the corresponding blow-down maps. Then, by composing with the trivializing isomorphisms, we obtain a unique invertible algebraic function
\lambda_{i}\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
such that we can identify the analytification of
\phi_{i}

with a map
\tilde{\phi_{i}}\colon X(\mathbb{C})\times\mathbb{C}\to X(\mathbb{C})\times\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq n}\mathbb{C}^{\otimes e_{ij}}
which sends

(x,z)\mapsto(x,\lambda_{i}(x)z\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq n}1^{\otimes e_{ij}}).

This gives a map of spaces over
X(\mathbb{C})
,

X(\mathbb{C})\times(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\to X(\mathbb{C})\times(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
given by

(x,(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}))\mapsto(x,(\lambda_{1}(x)\prod_{j}z_{j}^{e_{1j}},\dots,\lambda_{n}(x)\prod_{j}z_{j}^{e_{nj}})).

By definition of
\lambda_{i}
this map sends

(x,s_{1}(x),\dots,s_{n}(x))\mapsto(x,t_{1}(x),\dots,t_{m}(x))

and hence induces a morphism of blow-ups as quotients of subspaces of these bundles.

Since any morphism of log schemes is the composition of a strict morphism and a morphism given by the identity on underlying spaces these two cases are enough to define the analytification of an arbitrary map of log schemes.

One can verify that this definition of the analytification functor for DF log scheme agrees with the analytification of its associated log scheme originally introduced by Kato and Nakayama in
[
KN99
]
. This can be checked through direct computation but for a less tedious argument see
[
BDPW23
]
. From section
3
onward we will drop the “DF” prefix and let “log scheme” mean “DF log scheme”. All log schemes referenced in this article will indeed be DF log schemes but because of the above the distinction is not important for our purposes anyway.

Remark
.

In
[
KN99
]
,
\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}
is also given an associated sheaf of rings which, provided that
\underline{\mathsf{X}}
is smooth and that the divisor associated to all non zero sections of line bundles is a smooth normal crossings divisor, gives
X^{\textnormal{KN}}
the structure of a smooth real manifold with corners. This motivates the name “analytification”. These conditions are all satisfied for the log schemes we consider in this article and we could, with a little more work, replace the word “homeomorphism” with “diffeomorphism” in
Theorem

5.12
and
Theorem

5.13
.

We will not need to use this explicit definition of the analytification of a morphism at any point in this article. Instead, all we will need is that the analytification of a map exists, is functorial, and satisfies the following properties:

•

For a log scheme
\mathsf{X}
with log structure
\mathfrak{L}=(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathsf{X}}}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
the analytification of the map
\mathsf{X}\to\underline{\mathsf{X}}
given by the identity on underlying schemes is the blow-down map

\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}=\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{s}_{n}}\ \textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{s}_{n-1}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{s_{1}}\ \underline{\mathsf{X}}(\mathbb{C})\to\underline{\mathsf{X}}(\mathbb{C}).

•

For a strict morphism of log schemes
f\colon\mathsf{X}\to\mathsf{Y}
the following is a Cartesian diagram:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{Y}^{\textnormal{KN}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathsf{X}}(\mathbb{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathsf{Y}}(\mathbb{C})}

Both of these properties are standard and easy to verify so we omit a proof and will use them freely without reference.

2.3
Virtual Logarithmic Geometry

In this section we will give a short introduction to virtual morphisms of log schemes which were introduced by Howell
[
How17
]
and further studied by Dupont, Panzer, and Pym
[
DPP24
]
. Such virtual morphisms will be irrelevant for most sections of this article but in
Section

5.4
we include a discussion on how one can generalize some of our results using virtual morphisms in ways that are not possible otherwise. In the language of normal, i.e. non DF, logarithmic geometry a log structure on a scheme
X
is a sheaf of monoids
\mathcal{M}
with a morphism of sheaves of monoids
\alpha\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
which restricts to an isomorphism
\alpha^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}
. A map of log structures is then a map of sheaves of monoids over
\mathcal{O}_{X}
,
\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}
. Note that such a map commutes with the maps from
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
by definition.

A virtual morphism of log structures is a morphism of the groupifications
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\textnormal{gp}}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\textnormal{gp}}
which makes the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\textnormal{gp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\textnormal{gp}}}

commute. Note that the groupifications
\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\textnormal{gp}},\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\textnormal{gp}}
do not have maps to
\mathcal{O}_{X}
which is why we use this weaker condition. Also note that the category of virtual log structures has more morphisms but we define it to have the same objects as the category of log structures. For a short motivation regarding why it makes sense to introduce such virtual morphisms it is worth mentioning that the log Betti and log de Rham cohomology functors are well defined for log schemes with virtual morphisms and the same is also true for the Kato–Nakayama analytification functor.

Although Dupont, Panzer, and Pym do define virtual morphisms of DF log structures their definition of a DF log structure is different from ours. Thus, we give a somewhat different definition here.

Definition 2.7
.

A virtual morphism of DF log structures on a scheme
X

(s_{i}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{L}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\to(t_{j}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{M}_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}

is a collection
\{e_{ij}\}
of integers together with isomorphisms of line bundles

\mathcal{L}_{i}\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq m}\mathcal{M}_{j}^{\otimes e_{ij}}

for each
1\leq i\leq n

satisfying the following if
s_{i}\neq 0

•

e_{ij}\geq 0
for every
j
.

•

the sections
s_{i}
are mapped to the corresponding sections
\bigotimes_{1\leq j\leq m}t_{j}^{\otimes e_{ij}}

Remark
.

Although the constructions are closely related, our category of DF log schemes and the category of log schemes in
[
DPP24
]
are not equivalent categories nor are they subcategories of each other and thus this definition cannot be “derived” from that of Dupont, Panzer, and Pym in any meaningful way.

Remark
.

For non connected schemes this definition must be appropriately modified for sections that are identically zero on some, but not all, components.

The category of DF log schemes with virtual morphisms,
{}^{\textnormal{{V}}}\textnormal{{DF-Log}}
fits into a commutative diagram of categories:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{{{DF-Log}}}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{{{Log}}}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\textnormal{{V}}}\textnormal{{DF-Log}}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{\textnormal{{V}}}\textnormal{{Log}}_{X}}

We will omit the construction of the functor
{}^{\textnormal{{V}}}\textnormal{{DF-Log}}_{X}\to{}^{\textnormal{{V}}}\textnormal{{Log}}_{X}
but the interested reader is encouraged to construct it themselves and/or verify that our definition of the Kato–Nakayama analytification functor is still well defined for virtual morphisms. Doing this hopefully sheds some light on why this definition is a reasonable one.

Example 2.8
.

There is no morphism of log schemes
\textnormal{Spec }\mathbbm{k}\to(\textnormal{Spec }\mathbbm{k},0\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{k}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{k}})
but there is such a morphism of virtual log schemes (one for each automorphism
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{k}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{k}}
). For
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
, the Kato–Nakayama analytification of these maps are the inclusions of a point in
S^{1}
. Similarly, the group inverse map
i\colon S^{1}\to S^{1}
is not the analytification of any map of log schemes
(\textnormal{Spec }\mathbb{C},0\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}})\to(\textnormal{Spec }\mathbbm{k},0\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}})
but it is a virtual map of log schemes corresponding to the isomorphism

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathcal{O}^{\otimes(-1)}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth scheme and let
D
be a smooth effective Cartier divisor. By definition

(X,s_{D}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))^{\textnormal{KN}}=\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{D(\mathbb{C})}\ X(\mathbb{C}).

By our definition of the real oriented blow-up there is an embedding of
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{D(\mathbb{C})}\ X(\mathbb{C})
, into the unit circle bundle of (the analytification of)
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
with some arbitrarily chosen metric. This unit circle bundle is, again by definition, the KN analytification of
(X,0\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
. The inclusion

(X,s_{D}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))^{\textnormal{KN}}\hookrightarrow(X,0\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))^{\textnormal{KN}}

is not the analytification of any map of log schemes since the zero section cannot be pulled back to a non zero section. It is however the analytification of a virtual map given by the identity on both underlying spaces and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)^{\otimes 1}
.

3
Topological Fulton–MacPherson and Kontsevich Spaces

In this section we recall the topological Fulton–MacPherson spaces,
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
, and the closely related Kontsevich spaces,
\textnormal{K}_{D,n}\cong\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})\big/\mathbb{R}_{>0}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{D}
.

3.1
The Fulton–MacPherson Compactification of a Manifold

Let
X
be a smooth manifold, let
n
be a positive integer and let
P_{2}({n})
, denote the set of subsets of
[n]
with at lest
2
elements. For a set
I\in P_{2}({n})
let
\Delta_{I}
denote the corresponding diagonal in
X^{n}
, i.e.

\Delta_{I}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n}|\ x_{i}=x_{j}\ \forall\ i,j\in I\}.

By abuse of notation we will also use
\Delta_{I}
to denote the (small) diagonal in
X^{I}
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
Fulton–MacPherson compactification
,
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X)
is the closure of the image of the map

\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X)\hookrightarrow\prod_{I\in P_{2}({n})}\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{I}}\ X^{I}.

We let
f_{I}\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X)\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{I}}\ (X)
denote the restriction of the corresponding projection to
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X)
and we let
\rho\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X)\to X^{n}
denote the surjective extension of the embedding
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X)\hookrightarrow X^{n}
to
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X)
.

Remark
.

This definition is the one used by Axelrod and Singer in
[
AS94
]
but many sources use a different definition of the topological Fulton–MacPherson compactification introduced by Sinha
[
Sin04
]
. These can easily be shown to be equivalent.

Definition 3.2
.

The
Kontsevich space
of
n
points in dimension
d
,
\textnormal{K}_{D,n}
, is defined as the fibre

\rho^{-1}((0,0,\dots,0))\subseteq\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
.

Remark
.

It is more common to define
\textnormal{K}_{D,n}
as
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{D})\big/\mathbb{R}_{>0}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{D}
but one can show that the composition
\rho^{-1}((0,0,\dots,0))\hookrightarrow\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})\to\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{D})\big/\mathbb{R}_{>0}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{D}
is an isomorphism and this equivalent definition will be more useful for our purposes.

The fibre over the origin of
\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{I}}\ (\mathbb{R}^{D})^{I}
is
S^{D(|I|-1)-1}
. Thus,
f_{I}\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{I}}\ (\mathbb{R}^{D})^{I}
restricts to a map
\pi_{I}\colon\textnormal{K}_{D,n}\to S^{D(|I|-1)-1}
. These maps give a closed embedding

\textnormal{K}_{D,n}\hookrightarrow\prod_{I\in P_{2}({n})}S^{D(|I|-1)-1},

i.e. an element
x\in\textnormal{K}_{D,n}
is uniquely determined by its components
x_{I}=\pi_{I}(x)
.

Note that while we have chosen to use
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
as coordinate indices in the definitions above we could have used any (finite) index set
N
instead. We can thus define
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{N}(X)
and
\textnormal{K}_{d,N}
with maps
\rho\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{N}(X)\to X^{N}
,
f_{I}\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{N}(X)\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{I}}\ (X)^{I}
, and
\pi_{I}\colon\textnormal{K}_{D,N}\to S^{D(|I|-1)-1}
in the analogous way. Here
I
is a subset of
N
.

3.2
The Fulton–MacPherson Operad

For a fixed dimension
D
the collection
\{\textnormal{K}_{D,n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
can be given the structure of a topological operad as follows.

The permutation action of
\Sigma_{n}
on
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
extends to a free action on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
which gives an induced action on the fibre over the origin,
\textnormal{K}_{D,n}
. This is the symmetry action of the operad.

Next, any surjection
\varphi\colon I\twoheadrightarrow J
of sets induces a monomorphism
(\mathbb{R}^{D})^{J}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{D})^{I}
such that the inverse image of
\Delta_{I}
is
\Delta_{J}
. This gives a map

\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{J}}\ (\mathbb{R}^{D})^{J}\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\Delta_{I}}\ (\mathbb{R}^{D})^{I}
which restricts to a map of fibres over the origin

g_{\varphi}\colon S^{D(|J|-1)-1}\to S^{D(|I|-1)-1}.

Let
q\colon M\twoheadrightarrow[n]
be a surjection of finite sets.
By abuse of notation we will also let
q
denote the restriction of this function
I\twoheadrightarrow q(I)
for any
I\subseteq M
. The above construction allows us to define a map

\gamma\colon\textnormal{K}_{D,[n]}\times\prod_{i\in[n]}\textnormal{K}_{D,q^{-1}(i)}\to\textnormal{K}_{d,M}

by

\gamma(x,y^{1},\dots,y^{n})_{I}=\begin{cases}y^{i}_{I}&I\subseteq q^{-1}(i)\\
g_{q}(x_{q(I)})&\textnormal{else}\end{cases}.

This defines the composition maps of the operad.

Remark
.

We use the standard notation for an operad defined in terms of finite sets here. See for example
[
MSS07
]
for an introduction to the notation.

Finally,
\textnormal{K}_{d,1}
is the one point space so there is only one choice for the identity map,
\eta\colon*\to\textnormal{K}_{d,1}
.
The operad axioms can be verified manually for this collection of maps.

Definition 3.3
.

We call the operad with spaces
\{\textnormal{K}_{D,n}\}
(for a fixed
D
) and the above composition, symmetry, and identity maps the
Fulton–MacPherson operad in dimension
D
, denoted
\textnormal{{FM}}_{D}
.

By a result of Salvatore, the Fulton–MacPherson operad is weakly equivalent to the operad of little
d
-dimensional disks
[
Sal01
, proposition 3.9]
.

The action of
\textnormal{SO}(D)
on
\mathbb{R}^{D}
induces an action on
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
which extends to
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{D})
. Since all rotations map the zero vector to itself this restricts to an action on the fibre
\textnormal{K}_{D,n}
. One can check that this gives an action by
\textnormal{SO}(D)
on the
\textnormal{{FM}}_{D}
operad in the sense of
[
SW03
]
. Furthermore the weak equivalence
\textnormal{{FM}}_{D}\sim\textnormal{{LD}}_{D}
can be shown to commute with this action which gives a weak equivalence between
\textnormal{{FM}}_{D}\rtimes\textnormal{SO}(D)
and
\textnormal{{LD}}_{D}\rtimes\textnormal{SO}(D)
, the framed little disks operad.

With the notable exception of the case
D=2
, the framed little disks operad is, however, not the operad studied in this article. Instead we are interested in the following construction.
When the dimension is an even number
D=2d
there is an embedding of topological groups

S^{1}\cong U(1)\xhookrightarrow{\textnormal{diag.}}U(d)\hookrightarrow\textnormal{SO}(2d).

This induces an action of
S^{1}
on the
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
operad and we can form the semidirect product
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
.

The main goal of this article is to construct a non-unital operad of log schemes whose Kato–Nakayama analytification is
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
which is weakly equivalent to the
S^{1}
-framed little disks operad.

4
The Chen, Gibney, Krashen Operad

Fix a base field
\mathbbm{k}
for the remainder of the article.
In this section we recall the definition of the moduli spaces of stable
n
-pointed rooted trees of
d
-dimensional projective spaces,
T_{d,n}
, and describe an operad of (smooth)
\mathbbm{k}
-varieties with spaces

for a fixed

which generalizes the operad of pointed stable curves of genus

. For a more in depth description of the spaces

, including a definition of a rooted tree of projective spaces, see
[
CGK09
]
.

4.1
Fulton–MacPherson Compactifications

Let us recall some important aspects of the Fulton–MacPherson compactification
[
FM94
]
. Let
X
be a smooth
\mathbbm{k}
-variety. For an integer
n
and a subset
I\subseteq[n]
with
|I|\geq 2
let
\mathcal{I}_{I}
denote the sheaf of ideals on
X^{n}
corresponding to the
I
-diagonal,
\Delta_{I}\subseteq X^{n}
. Recall that we denote the set of subsets of
[n]
with at least
2
elements by
P_{2}({n})
.

Let
\rho\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\to X^{n}
denote the Fulton–MacPherson compactification of
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X)
. We call this a “compactification” of
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X)
since
\rho
restricted to
\rho^{-1}(\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X))
is an isomorphism and
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
is compact provided that
X
is.
The remainder of this section will be dedicated to recalling some important results relating to the Fulton–MacPherson compactification. First, we just state the following facts, all of which can be found with proofs in
[
FM94
]
.

•

The scheme
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
is a smooth variety.

•

There is an ordering of the elements in
P_{2}({n})
, such that
\rho\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\to X^{n}
is given by a sequence of blow-ups where we start with
X^{n}
and at step
t
of the sequence we blow-up in the dominant transform of the
t
th diagonal through all previous blow-ups.

•

The closed subset
D(I)\subseteq\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
given by the dominant transform of
\Delta(X)
through all these blow-ups is a smooth, effective Cartier divisor.

•

The divisors
D(I)
form a strict normal crossings divisor, i.e. any set of the divisors
D(I)
meets transversely.

•

The intersection
D(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})\vcentcolon=D(I_{1})\cap\dots\cap D(I_{k})
is non-empty if and only if the sets
I_{1},\dots,I_{k}
are
nested
i.e. for each
i,j
we have
I_{i}\subseteq I_{j}
,
I_{j}\subseteq I_{i}
or
I_{i}\cap I_{j}=\emptyset
.

•

We have
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\setminus\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X)=\bigcup_{I\in P_{2}({n})}D(I)
.

•

We have that
\rho^{-1}(\Delta(I))=\bigcup_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}D(I^{\prime})
, i.e. the pullback of the ideal inclusion
\mathcal{I}_{I}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X^{n}}
factors via a quotient

\rho^{*}\mathcal{I}_{I}\twoheadrightarrow\prod_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{I}_{D(I^{\prime})}\cong\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(-D(I^{\prime})).

•

The composition
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\to X^{n}\to X^{I}
factors as
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\xrightarrow{f_{I}}\textnormal{Bl}_{\Delta_{I}}\ X^{I}\xrightarrow{\rho_{I}}X^{I},
where
\rho_{I}
is the blow-down map.

Remark
.

Recall that for a blow-up
\textnormal{Bl}_{Y}\ X\to X
the
dominant
transform of a closed subscheme
Z\subseteq X
is defined as the strict transform of
Z
if
Z\not\subseteq Y
and the pullback of
Z
to
\textnormal{Bl}_{Y}\ X
if
Y\subseteq Z
.

Note that by the last two points we have that if
E_{\Delta_{I}}\subseteq\textnormal{Bl}_{\Delta_{I}}\ X^{I}
denotes the exceptional divisor then there is an isomorphism

f_{I}^{*}\mathcal{O}(E_{\Delta_{I}})\cong\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(D(I^{\prime}))

which sends corresponding sections to each other.

In addition to this geometric description of
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
there is also a functorial description which we will make great use of in this article.

Definition 4.1
.

A
screen
on a scheme
H
is a map
f\colon H\to X^{n}
together with the following data

•

an invertible quotient
f^{*}\mathcal{I}_{I}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{I}
for each
I\in P_{2}({n})

•

a map
\mathcal{L}_{I}\to\mathcal{L}_{J}
for each
I\subseteq J

such that the following diagram commutes for every

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}\mathcal{I}_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}\mathcal{I}_{J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}_{J}}

where
f^{*}\mathcal{I}_{I}\to f^{*}\mathcal{I}_{J}
is the pullback of the corresponding inclusion.

We say a screen satisfies the
I
-vanishing property for some
I\in P_{2}({n})
if
\textnormal{pr}_{i}\circ f\colon H\to X=\textnormal{pr}_{j}\circ f\colon H\to X
for each

and, for every
J\in P_{2}({n})
, if
J\not\subseteq I
and
|I\cap J|\geq 2
then
\mathcal{L}_{I\cap J}\to\mathcal{L}_{J}
is the zero morphism.

We can define a screen on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
by taking

,
\mathcal{L}_{I}=\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(-D(I^{\prime}))
and letting the invertible quotients be the maps

\rho^{*}\mathcal{I}_{I}\twoheadrightarrow\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(-D(I^{\prime})).

The maps
\mathcal{L}_{I}\to\mathcal{L}_{J}
here are induced by the ideal inclusions
\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(-D(I))\hookrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}
. We call this screen the
universal screen
on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}_{n}\colon\textnormal{{{Sch}}}^{op}\to\textnormal{{Set}}
be the functor which sends a scheme

to the set of screens on
H
up to isomorphism of the screen data.

Similarly let
\mathcal{X}(I_{1},\dots,I_{k})
denote the subfunctor sending

-vanishing property for each

The Fulton–MacPherson compactification
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)

. Furthermore, the natural isomorphism
\hom(-,\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X))\to\mathcal{X}_{n}

to the pullback to

Proof.

4.2
Pointed Rooted Trees of Projective Spaces

Chen, Gibney, and Krashen
[
CGK09
]
introduced
stable pointed rooted trees of projective spaces
as a higher-dimensional analogue of stable pointed rational curves, and showed that
T_{d,n}
(defined in
Section

4.3
) is the moduli space of stable rooted
n
-pointed trees of

d
-dimensional projective spaces.
Here we recall the definition and describe the morphisms in the operad
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
(defined in
Section

4.3
), with
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}(n)=T_{d,n}
in terms of natural operations on such trees. The discussion is intended to convey geometric intuition for
Section

4.3
, and we therefore suppress most proofs and technical details.

Definition 4.4
.

A
rooted tree of
d
-dimensional projective spaces
is a connected, reduced, projective scheme
T
with a closed embedding
r_{0}\colon\mathbb{P}^{d-1}\hookrightarrow T
, called the
root hyperplane
, obtained by the following finite inductive process:

1.

Start with
T\cong\mathbb{P}^{d}
and choose a hyperplane embedding

r_{0}\colon\mathbb{P}^{d-1}\hookrightarrow V.

This embedding is the
root hyperplane
.

2.

Given a tree of
d
-dimensional projective spaces
T
produced in a previous step, choose a smooth point
x\in T
disjoint from the singular locus and the root hyperplane
r_{0}\colon\mathbb{P}^{d-1}\hookrightarrow T
.
The next iteration
T^{\prime}
is defined by forming the blow-up
\textnormal{Bl}_{x}\ T
and gluing along its exceptional divisor
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}
a new copy of
\mathbb{P}^{d}
along some hyperplane

\mathbb{P}^{d-1}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{d}.
Since
x
was chosen to be disjoint from the root hyperplane
r_{0}
induces an embedding
r_{0}^{\prime}\colon\mathbb{P}^{d-1}\hookrightarrow T^{\prime}
, which we take to be the new root hyperplane.
Repeat finitely many times.

An
n
-pointed rooted tree
is such a rooted tree of projective spaces
T
together with
n
distinct points

p_{1},\dots,p_{n}:\textnormal{Spec }k\hookrightarrow V

lying outside the singular locus
\textnormal{Sing}(T)
and outside the root hyperplane.

Remark
.

When
d=1
, blowing up in a smooth point does nothing and hyperplanes are points so an
n
-pointed rooted tree of
1
-dimensional projective spaces is just an
n+1
-pointed genus
0
curve with at worst nodal singularities.

Decompose
T=\bigcup_{i}T_{i}
into irreducible components and let
T_{0}
denote the
root component
, i.e. the component containing the root hyperplane.
Each
T_{i}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{d}
blown up at a closed subscheme
Q_{i}
consisting of finitely many disjoint
\mathbbm{k}
-points, hence admits a canonical

blow-down map

b_{i}:T_{i}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{d}.

Furthermore, there is an embedding
r_{i}\colon\mathbb{P}^{d-1}\hookrightarrow T_{i}
for each
T_{i}
such that

is the inclusion of a hyperplane. For the root component

is the root hyperplane and for other components

is the embedding of the hyperplane along which the component was attached.
Note that the disjoint union of
r_{i}
and the exceptional divisor

of the blow-up
T_{i}\to\mathbb{P}^{d}
is precisely the intersection
T_{i}\cap\textnormal{Sing}(T)
.

Definition 4.5
.

With notation as above, the
n
-pointed rooted tree

is

if, for every component

,
there are at least two corresponding

, meaning the marked points

and the blown-up points

.

Remark
.

This condition is equivalent to

having no non-trivial automorphisms restricting to the identity on the marked points and the root hyperplane.

Remark
.

When

, the above condition is equivalent to there being three special points on each component if we think of the corresponding hyperplanes as special points as well. This is the usual stability condition for pointed genus
0
curves with at worst nodal singularities.

By Theorem 3.4.4. in
[
CGK09
]
the smooth varieties

are moduli spaces of stable
n
-pointed rooted trees of
d
-dimensional projective spaces. There are no stable
1
-pointed trees but in this case we define

. As mentioned before, a hyperplane in

is just a point and thus we have

, the moduli space of stable

-pointed rational curves where the additional point is the root hyperplane.

Each variety

comes with a canonical action by the symmetric group

corresponding to permutation of the marked points. Furthermore, for each

there is a morphism

which takes an

and an

-pointed tree

and maps them to the

in the

th marked point and gluing the resulting exceptional divisor to the root hyperplane in

. It is clear from definitions that these maps give rise to an operad of varieties, which we denote

, with spaces

. In the case

this construction is classical, see for example

]

5
Log Geometric Constructions

In this section we define log structures on the smooth varieties encountered in
Section

4
and extend the various morphisms to morphisms of log schemes. To this end the following lemma will be used repeatedly.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a map of schemes and let
D\subseteq X
,
E\subseteq Y
be effective Cartier divisors such that
f^{-1}(E)=D
(scheme theoretically). Then there is a unique isomorphism

f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(E)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)

taking
f^{*}s_{E}\mapsto s_{D}
.

Proof.

First note that since
f^{-1}(E)=D
there is an epimorphism
f^{*}\mathcal{I}_{E}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{D}
commuting with the corresponding maps to
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{E}
and
\mathcal{I}_{D}
are ideal sheaves of divisors they are line bundles and since any quotient map of line bundles is an isomorphism this is an isomorphism. Taking duals now yields the result. Uniqueness is clear since an isomorphism of line bundles is uniquely determined by its value for a single generically non-zero section.
∎

, by taking the underlying scheme to be

and the log structure given by the pullback of this log structure on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
via the inclusion
T_{d,n}\hookrightarrow\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
. Note that for each
I\in P_{2}({n})
with
I\neq[n]
the line bundle with section corresponding to the divisor
D(I)
pulls back to the line bundle with section corresponding to
T_{d,n}(I)
and for
I=[n]
the pullback of the corresponding line bundle has the zero section. We will let
s_{I}\colon\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}\to\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I)
denote the pullback of
s_{I}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})}\to\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})}(D(I))
for each
I\in P_{2}({n})
.

The operad composition morphisms of the
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
operad do not extend to maps of log-schemes for the objects
\mathsf{K}_{d,n}
. Instead, in order to extend
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
to a (pseudo)-operad of log-schemes we must let its objects be the log-schemes
\mathsf{T}_{d,n}
, whose log structures are given by one line bundle with section
s_{I}\colon\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}\to\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I)
for each
non-empty

I\subseteq[n]
defined as above for
|I|\geq 2
and defined by

\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(\{i\})\vcentcolon=\bigotimes_{I\ni i}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I)^{\vee},

with
s_{\{i\}}=0
for one element sets. For
n=1
we define
\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,1}}(\{1\})\vcentcolon=\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,1}}
with the zero section.

5.2
The Log Geometric CGK Operad

As mentioned, the symmetry and composition morphisms of the
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
operad can be extended to morphisms of log schemes
\mathsf{T}_{d,n}
. To see this for the symmetry morphisms note that, for
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
we have that the corresponding automorphism on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
, sends each divisor
D(I)
isomorphically to
D(\sigma(I))
. This induces isomorphisms of line bundles (with sections)
\sigma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(D(I))\to\mathcal{O}_{\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)}(D(\sigma^{-1}(I)))
.
These maps extend the symmetry action on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
to a symmetry action on
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(X)
. This in turn gives symmetry actions on
\mathsf{K}_{d,n}
and
\mathsf{T}_{d,n}
in the same way.

Extending the composition morphisms is slightly trickier. Again, let
n
be a positive integer and let
q\colon M\twoheadrightarrow[n]
be a surjection of sets. As before, let

\pi_{0}\colon T_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{d,q^{-1}(i)}\to T_{d,n}

and

\pi_{r}\colon T_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{d,q^{-1}(i)}\to T_{d,q^{-1}(r)}

denote the corresponding projections, and let

\gamma\colon T_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{d,q^{-1}(i)}\to T_{d,M}

be the composition morphism of
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
. We can immediately show the following.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
I\subsetneq M
with
|I|\geq 2
. Then there is a unique isomorphism of line bundles with sections

(a)

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(I)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{r}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,q^{-1}(r)}}(I)
if
I\subsetneq q^{-1}(r)
.

(b)

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(I)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(q(I))
if
I=\bigsqcup_{i\in q(I)}q^{-1}(r)
and
q(I)\geq 2
.

(c)

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(I)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}\times\prod T_{d,q^{-1}(i)}}
, where the right hand side is taken with the unit section, if
(I,M_{1},\dots,M_{n})
are not nested, i.e.
I
is not contained in any
q^{-1}(r)
and also not equal to a union of any collection of
q^{-1}(r)
.

Proof.

Cases (a) and (b) follow directly from
Corollary

4.11
and
Lemma

5.1
. Case (c) follows from the fact that the image of
\gamma
is
T_{d,M}(q^{-1}(1),\dots,q^{-1}(n))
(
Proposition

4.10
) which is disjoint with
T_{d,M}(I)
since
T_{d,M}(I,M_{1},\dots,M_{n})
is empty by the nestedness criterion.
∎

This alone is almost enough to extend the operad composition morphisms to maps of corresponding log-schemes but we must also express

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(I)
as a tensor product of the line bundles on
T_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{d,q^{-1}(i)}
for
I=M
,
I=q^{-1}(r)
, and
|I|=1
. Note that in all of these cases the pullback of the sections of these corresponding line bundles are all
0
.
To this end, recall that that
Lemma

4.8
gives isomorphisms of line bundles on
T_{d,n}

\mathcal{M}_{I}\xrightarrow{\cong}\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I^{\prime})^{\vee},

where
\mathcal{M}_{I}
are the line bundles of the universal simple screen on
T_{d,n}
(
Section

4.3
),
such that the following diagram commutes for every
I\subseteq J
,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I^{\prime})^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigotimes_{J\subseteq J^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(J^{\prime})^{\vee}}

where the bottom arrow is the map

\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime},\ J\not\subseteq I^{\prime}}s_{I^{\prime}}^{\vee}.

In particular, this means that we have isomorphisms

\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I)\cong\mathcal{M}_{I}^{\vee}\bigotimes_{I\subsetneq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I)^{\vee}.

Lemma 5.4
.

There are canonical isomorphisms of line bundles

(a)

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(M)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}([n])
.

(b)

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(q^{-1}(r))\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(\{r\})\otimes\pi_{r}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,q^{-1}(r)}}(q^{-1}(r))
.

(c)

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(\{i\})\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{r}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,q^{-1}(r)}}(\{i\})
, if
i\in q^{-1}(r)
.

Proof.

First note that by the above we have
\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(M)\cong\mathcal{(}M_{M}^{d,M})^{\vee}
. By
Corollary

4.12
we have
\gamma^{*}\mathcal{M}_{M}^{d,M}\cong\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{[n]}^{d,n}
. From this part (a) follows.
For part (b) note that
Lemma

5.3
gives an isomorphism

\bigotimes_{q^{-1}(r)\subsetneq S}\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(I)\cong\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(M)\bigotimes_{r\in J\subsetneq[n]}\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(J)\cong\bigotimes_{r\in J\subseteq[n]}\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(J)

where the last isomorphism follows from part (a). By definition, this is just
\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(\{r\})^{\vee}

and so we get an isomorphism

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(q^{-1}(r))\xrightarrow{\cong}\gamma^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{q^{-1}(r)}^{d,M})^{\vee}\bigotimes_{q^{-1}(r)\subsetneq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{\gamma}^{*}O_{T_{d,M}}(I^{\prime})^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{r}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,q^{-1}(r)}}(q^{-1}(r))\otimes\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,q^{-1}(r)}}(\{r\}).

This gives part (b). Part (c) follows from a similar argument.
∎

Remark
.

If
n=1
the left hand sides of (a) and (b) are equal. In this case, both statements are true but we must choose (b) to be the isomorphism defining our map of log schemes for the operad axioms to be satisfied. Similarly, if
|q^{-1}(r)|=1
we must also choose the map in (b).

With this we have defined isomorphisms of line bundles extending the operad composition maps of
\textnormal{{CGK}}_{d}
to morphisms of log-schemes

\gamma^{\log}\colon\mathsf{T}_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{T}_{d,q^{-1}(i)}\to\mathsf{T}_{d,M}.

Unfortunately, there are no maps of log schemes

\textnormal{Spec }\mathbbm{k}\to(\textnormal{Spec }\mathbbm{k},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{k}}\xrightarrow{0}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbbm{k}})

and therefore we cannot extend our unit map to a map of log schemes. However, it is easy to verify that despite this the
\circ_{i}
-operations,
T_{d,n}\times T_{d,m}\xrightarrow{\circ_{i}}T_{d,m+n-1}
, do extend to maps of log schemes
\mathsf{T}_{d,n}\times\mathsf{T}_{d,m}\xrightarrow{\circ_{i}}\mathsf{T}_{d,m+n-1}
. One can verify that these maps, together with the symmetry morphisms satisfy the pseudo-operad axioms.

Definition 5.5
.

The
log-geometric Chen, Gibney, Krashen pseudo-operad
,
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
is the pseudo-operad with objects
\mathsf{T}_{d,n}
and with morphisms as described above.

Remark
.

In the case
d=1
this is isomorphic to the framed little curves pseudo-operad constructed by Vaintrob in
[
Vai21
]
.

5.3
Kato–Nakayama Analytifications

In this section we show that the Kato–Nakayama Analytification of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
is homeomorphic to the
S^{1}
-framed Fulton–MacPherson operad in dimension
2d
,
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
, which in turn is a model for the
S^{1}
-framed little
2d
-disks operad
\textnormal{{LD}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
as mentioned in
Section

3
.

We begin by showing that there is a homeomorphism of spaces over
X^{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C})),

which commutes with the corresponding inclusions of
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C}))
. The rough idea behind our proof of this statement is that the left hand side is the Kato–Nakayama space of a sequence of
logarithmic blow-ups
of
X^{n}
and the right hand side is a sequence of real blow-ups of
X(\mathbb{C})^{n}
and under sufficiently nice circumstances these two agree.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
\mathsf{X}=(X,\mathfrak{L})
be a log scheme and let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
. Then we let
\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{Y}\mathsf{X}
denote the log scheme with underlying scheme
\textnormal{Bl}_{Y}\ X
and log structure given by the log structure
\mathfrak{L}
pulled back to
\textnormal{Bl}_{Y}\ X

and
the line bundle with section corresponding to the exceptional divisor of the blow-up. We also define the
blow-down map
,
\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{Y}\mathsf{X}\to X
to be given by the normal blow-down map on underlying schemes and by “forgetting” the added line bundle with section.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
X
be a smooth
\mathbb{C}
-variety and let
Y
be a smooth subvariety of codimension
k
.
If
Y
is the zero locus of a section of a rank
k
vector bundle on
X
then there is an isomorphism of spaces over
X(\mathbb{C})

(\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{Y}X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{Y(\mathbb{C})}\ X(\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

This isomorphism is easy to construct when
Y
is a complete intersection, i.e. the zero locus of a section of
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus k}_{X}
. The general case follows by gluing.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\mathsf{X}=(X,\mathfrak{L})
be a log-scheme whose log structure is given by line bundles with sections cutting out smooth divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}
such that
D_{1}\cup\dots\cup D_{n}
is a strict normal crossings divisor and let
Y
be as in the proposition with the additional constraint that
Y
intersects all intersections
D_{i_{1}}\cap\dots D_{i_{k}}
transversally. Then there is an isomorphism of spaces over
\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}

(\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{Y}X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{Y}}\ \mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}

where
\tilde{Y}
denotes the strict transform of
Y(\mathbb{C})
under the sequence of blow-down maps

\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}\to X(\mathbb{C}).

Proof.

Let
t\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{V}
denote the vector bundle section cutting out
Y
. It is clear from the proposition that there is an isomorphism

(\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{Y}X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{Bl}^{\mathbb{R}}_{\tilde{t}}\ \mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}

where
\tilde{t}
denotes the pullback (i.e.
total
transform) to
\mathsf{X}^{\textnormal{KN}}
of the vector bundle section
t
seen as a section of a topological vector bundle
t\in\Gamma(X(\mathbb{C}),{\mathcal{V}})
. The transversallity conditions for intersections with
Y
imply that
\tilde{Y}
is the zero locus of the section
\tilde{t}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.9
.

There is a unique homeomorphism of spaces over
X^{n}(\mathbb{C})
,

\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C})),

which commutes with the corresponding inclusions of
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C}))
.

Proof.

First, recall from
[
Li09
]
that the Fulton–MacPherson space
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)
is isomorphic to the sequence of blow-ups

\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{N}}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{1}}}\ X^{n}

where
I_{1},\dots,I_{N}
are the sets in
P_{2}({[n]})
in
any
order satisfying the conditions of Theorem 1.3 in
[
Li09
]
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i}}
denotes the
dominant
transform of the diagonal
\Delta_{I_{i}}\subseteq X^{n}
under all previous blow-ups. Furthermore, recall that the divisors
D(I_{i})
are the
dominant
transforms of the exceptional divisor
\tilde{D}(I_{i})
from the blow-up

\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i}}}\ \textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i-1}}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{1}}}\ X^{n}\to\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i-1}}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{1}}}\ X^{n}

taken under all subsequent blow-ups
\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{N}}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i+1}}}\
. Additionally, the topological Fulton–MacPherson space
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C}))
can also be written as a sequence of real oriented blow-ups of
X^{n}(\mathbb{C})
in its diagonals in a similar manner.

The proposition would now follow from
Corollary

5.8
by induction if there was an ordering
I_{1},\dots,I_{N}
of the sets in
P_{2}({[n]})
satisfying the aforementioned conditions such that for every
1\leq i\leq n

\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i+1}}\subseteq\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i}}}\ \textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{i-1}}}\ \dots\textnormal{Bl}_{\tilde{\Delta}_{I_{1}}}\ X^{n}

meets any intersection of the divisors
\tilde{D}(I_{1})\dots\tilde{D}(I_{i})
transversally. It is not hard to show that any ordering where the sets
I\in P_{2}({[n]})
appear in decreasing order of size, i.e.
|I_{i}|\geq|I_{i+1}|
, satisfies all criteria and thus we are done.
∎

Remark
.

Note that the transversality conditions here are not implied by the arrangement conditions in Theorem 1.3 of
[
Li09
]
. Hence, there is no obvious analogue of the above proposition for general wonderful compactifications.

Corollary 5.10
.

There are isomorphisms

•

\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C}))

•

\mathsf{K}_{d,n}^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{K}_{2d,n}

•

\mathsf{T}_{d,n}\cong(S^{1})^{n}\times\textnormal{K}_{2d,n}

which commute with all relevant maps, i.e. inclusions of
\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C}))
, maps to
X^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, inclusions of fibres, etc.

Proof.

We have already constructed the isomorphism
\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(X)^{\textnormal{KN}}\cong\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{top}}_{n}(X(\mathbb{C}))
. Since the morphism
\mathsf{K}_{d,n}\hookrightarrow\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})
is strict, the top square in the following diagram is Cartesian.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{K}_{d,n}^{\textnormal{KN}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})(\mathbb{C})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{n}}
origin

We already know that the bottom square is Cartesian and thus the entire diagram is Cartesian, i.e.

Notice that we get

on the right hand side since

∎

Next, we will show that the maps

(which were defined in

are all identified with analytifications of explicit maps of corresponding log schemes under the isomorphisms described in the proof of

5.10
.

Let
\mathsf{S}_{m}
denote the log scheme
(\mathbb{P}^{m},0\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}(-1))
. Its Kato–Nakayama space
\mathsf{S}_{m}^{\textnormal{KN}}
homeomorphic to
S^{2m+1}
and the map
S^{2m+1}\cong S_{m}^{\textnormal{KN}}\to\mathbb{P}^{m}(\mathbb{C})
is the Hopf fibration (proving this using

may provide some insight into the idea behind the arguments to come).
Recall from
Section

4.1
that the composition

\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\to X^{n}\to X^{I}

factors via a map
f_{I}\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{alg}}_{n}(X)\to\textnormal{Bl}_{\Delta_{I}}\ X^{I}
for any

, and furthermore that there is an isomorphism of line bundles with sections

f_{I}^{*}\mathcal{O}(E_{\Delta_{I}})\cong\bigotimes_{I\subseteq I^{\prime}}\mathcal{O}(D(I^{\prime})).

This isomorphism defines a map of log schemes

f_{I}^{\log}\colon\textnormal{FM}^{\textnormal{log}}_{n}(X)\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{\Delta_{I}}X^{I}.

Also recall that the fibre over the origin for the blow-down map

is
\mathbb{P}^{d(|I|-1)-1}
and that, if

denotes the inclusion of this fibre, we have that

.
This means that

\mathsf{S}_{d(|I|-1)-1}=(\mathbb{P}^{d(|I|-1)-1},0\colon\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}(-1))
is the fibre over the origin of the log blow-down map
\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{\Delta_{I}}X^{I}\to X^{I}
and

The maps we have described fit together in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{A}^{d})}}

\textnormal{Conf}_{n}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is dense in

and since the blow-down map

Finally, let

We are now ready to show that the analytifications of the composition and symmetry maps in

are the composition and symmetry maps of
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}

A somewhat more complicated argument is needed to prove that the analytifications of the

-operations are the corresponding maps of the
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}\rtimes S^{1}
operad. We merely give an outline of this argument.
First note that for any surjection of finite sets

the induced map

extends uniquely to a map
\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{\Delta_{J}}(\mathbb{A}^{d})^{J}\to\textnormal{Bl}^{\log}_{\Delta_{I}}(\mathbb{A}^{d})^{I}
which restricts to a map

. By the same argument as above the analytification of this map must be the map

componentwise. That is, if

We include the proof for one important step which hopefully also provides some insight into the methods used to fill in the remaining steps of the argument.

.

The Kato–Nakayama analytification of

5.4
Virtual Log Geometric Generalizations

We end this article by discussing some apparent generalizations of these results in the category of virtual log schemes. The most immediate improvement we get with virtual morphisms is that, by
Example

2.8
, we there are virtual morphisms

\textnormal{Spec }\mathbb{C}\to\mathsf{S}_{0}.

It is not hard to show that we can choose such a morphism such that it satisfies the unit axiom for our operad. Hence, in the category of log schemes with virtual morphisms we can define
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\log}_{d}
to be an operad, not just a pseudo-operad. It is clear that
Theorem

5.12
still holds for this operad.

Virtual morphisms also allow us to construct the (unframed) Fulton–MacPherson operad
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
as the analytification of an operad of log schemes with virtual morphisms.
Indeed, the operad composition map

\gamma\colon T_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}T_{d,q^{-1}(i)}\to T_{d,M}

extends to a virtual morphism of log schemes

\mathsf{K}_{d,n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathsf{K}_{d,q^{-1}(i)}\to\mathsf{K}_{d,M}.

This map is defined using
Lemma

5.3
and
Lemma

5.4
but this time we replace the isomorphism in
Lemma

5.4
, (b) with the isomorphism

\gamma^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,M}}(q^{-1}(r))\xrightarrow{\cong}\left(\bigotimes_{r\in I}\pi_{0}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T_{d,n}}(I)^{\otimes(-1)}\right)\otimes\pi_{r}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{d,q^{-1}(r)}(q^{-1}(r)).

This gives a well defined virtual morphism of log structures by our definitions. These maps define an operad
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{d}
in log schemes with virtual morphisms.
Using the same methods as before one can show that the analytification of the resulting operad is
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
.

Theorem 5.13
.

The Kato–Nakayama analytification of
\textnormal{{CGK}}^{\textnormal{V-log}}_{d}
is homeomorphic to
\textnormal{{FM}}_{2d}
.

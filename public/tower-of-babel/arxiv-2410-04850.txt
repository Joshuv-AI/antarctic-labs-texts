Artificial barriers for stochastic differential equations and for construction of boundary-preserving schemes
Johan Ulander

Abstract
. We develop the novel method of artificial barriers for scalar stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and use it to construct boundary-preserving numerical schemes for strong approximation of scalar SDEs, possibly with non-globally Lipschitz drift and diffusion coefficients, whose state-space is either bounded or half-bounded. The idea of artificial barriers is to augment the SDE with artificial barriers outside the state-space to not change the solution process, and then apply a boundary-preserving numerical scheme to the resulting reflected SDE (RSDE). This enables us to construct boundary-preserving numerical schemes that achieve the same strong convergence rate as the corresponding RSDE scheme. Based on the method of artificial barriers, we construct two boundary-preserving schemes that we call the Artificial Barriers Euler–Maruyama (ABEM) scheme and the Artificial Barriers Euler–Peano (ABEP) scheme, respectively. We provide numerical experiments for the ABEM scheme and the numerical results agree with the obtained theoretical results.



AMS Classification.
60H10. 60H35. 65C30

Keywords.
Stochastic differential equations. Artificial barriers. Boundary-preserving schemes. Strong convergence. Explicit schemes.

1.
Introduction

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) are used to model phenomena arising in, for example, physics, mathematical finance, mathematical biology and epidemic modelling
[
15
,
20
,
24
,
26
,
40
]
. Some SDEs are confined to a strict subset, referred to as the domain of the SDE, of the target-space. Classical schemes for SDEs are known to leave the domains of SDEs
[
50
]
. In this article, we develop the method of artificial barriers and construct, based on this new concept, strongly convergent numerical approximations of solutions of SDEs that are confined to the domain of the considered SDE.

We consider scalar Itô stochastic differential equations (SDEs) of the form

(1)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X(t)=f(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t),\ t\in(0,T],

with some deterministic initial value
X(0)=x_{0}
, where
B
is a standard Brownian motion and
T\in(0,\infty)
. We assume that the drift coefficient
f
and diffusion coefficient
g
are Lipschitz continuous functions on an open set
D=(a,b)\subset\mathbb{R}
(see Assumption
1
) such that, provided that
x_{0}\in D
,
X(t)\in D
for every
t\in(0,T]
, almost surely (see Assumption
2
). Observe that we assume that
X(t)
cannot hit the boundary
\partial D=\{a,b\}
of the domain, which can be characterised by Feller’s classification of unattainable boundaries (see, for example, Chapter
15
in
[
20
]
). To cover both bounded and half-bounded cases, we let
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be such that
a<b
. We call the case
b=\infty
the one-finite-boundary case and the case
b\in(a,\infty)
the two-finite-boundaries case. The case
a=-\infty
and
b\in(a,\infty)
is treated in the same way. We do not consider
D=\mathbb{R}
as the classical Euler–Maruyama (EM) scheme is boundary-preserving in this case.

We say that a numerical scheme
\hat{X}
for (
1
) is boundary-preserving if
\hat{X}(t)\in D
for every
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely. Numerical schemes that may hit the boundary, but not exceed them, are referred to as non-strict boundary-preserving. On one hand, in certain applications, the domain of a model has physical significance. Numerical approximations that leave the physical domain of a model can be interpreted as non-physical. Models defined in a domain show up, for example, in the study of population dynamics
[
15
,
22
]
and of phase-separation dynamics
[
2
]
, defined in
[0,1]
, and in the study of temperature dynamics (for example, the classical heat equation), defined in
[0,\infty)
. On the other hand, a numerical scheme that leaves the physical domain of a model will naturally be affected by forces that do not influence the model. Let us consider an example in the setting of this paper: If the solution of the SDE (
1
) only takes values in
D=(0,1)
, then modifying
f
and
g
outside
D=(0,1)
will not impact the solution. Modifying
f
and
g
outside
D=(0,1)
can, however, influence a numerical approximation that can leave the domain
D=(0,1)
. We can avoid such discrepancy between the exact solution and numerical approximations by constructing and studying boundary-preserving numerical schemes.

Let us discuss previous approaches to construct boundary-preserving numerical schemes. Early boundary-preserving schemes were typically implicit schemes. See, for example,
[
1
,
11
,
12
,
39
,
45
]
for some works based on implicit techniques. Implicit schemes, however, tend to be computationally slower than explicit ones. The main focus in this work is explicit schemes, but implicit schemes could also be used within the developed framework. Another common approach to construct boundary-preserving schemes is to transform the solution to be approximated to another process that is easier to construct such schemes for. Typical examples of such transformations include the Lamperti transform (see, for example,
[
37
]
) and the logarithmic transform
[
52
]
. Time splitting methods
[
38
]
, truncation techniques
[
28
,
35
]
, and approximation by geometric Brownian motion
[
4
,
5
]
have also successfully been employed to construct boundary-preserving numerical schemes. An issue with the mentioned approaches, except schemes based on truncation, is that they requires regularity assumptions on the coefficient functions. Truncation schemes are the most related schemes to what we proposed in this work. We also mention
[
6
,
7
,
17
,
18
,
23
,
51
]
for some other proposed approaches to construct boundary-preserving schemes.

Let us discuss the Lamperti transform a bit more in detail. Under some assumptions, optimal convergence rates for numerical schemes for SDEs with additive noise can be transferred to these SDEs using the Lamperti transform
[
9
,
50
]
. Recently, authors (see
[
30
,
31
]
) have constructed higher-order boundary-preserving numerical schemes using the Lamperti transform. Traditionally, numerical schemes utilising the Lamperti transform have combined it with some classical scheme (for example, the EM scheme or the backward EM scheme) for SDEs (see, for example,
[
1
,
39
,
52
]
). Recently, authors (see
[
21
,
50
]
) have combined the Lamperti transform with time splitting methods to construct efficient numerical approximations the solutions to certain types of SDEs. Lamperti-based schemes do, however, require high regularity assumptions (for example, differentiability) on the drift coefficient
f
and on the diffusion coefficient
g
and require the existence of an inverse of the Lamperti transform to be well-defined. For instance,
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence rate of
1
, for all
p\geq 1
, was obtained in
[
50
]
for an SDE with bounded domain with drift coefficient
f\in C^{2}
and diffusion coefficient
g\in C^{3}
, and
f
and
g
satisfying some technical conditions near the boundary points. See, for example, Theorem
22
for the definition of
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence used in this work. In addition, the Lamperti transform is intrinsically problem-specific which makes the implementation of Lamperti-based schemes problem-specific.

The objective of this paper is to construct boundary-preserving schemes for the SDE (
1
) that do not depend on the Lamperti transform and for which optimal
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence can be obtained with lower regularity assumptions on the drift and diffusion coefficients
f
and
g
. To this end, we develop modified versions of two classical schemes for reflected stochastic differential equations (RSDEs) to construct non-Lamperti-based boundary-preserving schemes for the SDE (
1
). We refer readers to
[
10
,
42
,
44
,
46
,
49
]
for works on the theory of RSDEs and to
[
13
,
29
,
32
,
33
,
41
,
47
,
48
]
for numerical approximations of RSDEs.

The main contributions of the paper are the following:

•

We develop the method of artificial barriers (see Section
4
) and apply it to construct boundary-preserving approximations of the solution of the SDE in (
1
) (see Section

and Section

).

•

We prove that the proposed method of artificial barriers exhibits optimal

-convergence order, for every

-convergence order of

for every

(see Theorem

) and combined with a modified Euler–Peano scheme (referred to as the ABEP scheme) exhibits

-convergence order of

for every

(see Theorem

).

•

We numerically verify boundary-preservation and

-convergence of order

This paper is organised as follows. We first provide the setting in Section

and in Section

2.
Setting

This section introduces the needed notation and assumptions for the construction and study of the method of artificial barriers. We let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\bigl(\mathcal{F}_{t}\bigr)_{t\geq 0})
be a filtered probability space that satisfies the usual conditions and we denote by
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
the expectation operator. We denote generic constants depending on parameters
a_{1},\ldots,a_{l}
, for some
l\in\mathbb{N}
, by
C(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l})
that may vary from line to line. The closure of a set
A
is denoted by
\operatorname{cl}A
. We let
\mathcal{D}[0,T]
, for some fixed
T\in(0,\infty)
, denote the space of real-valued functions defined on
[0,T]
that are right continuous with left limits (càdlàg).

Most equalities and inequalities in this work are to be understood in the almost sure sense, we omit specifying this everywhere to avoid reiteration.

We consider scalar time-homogeneous Itô stochastic differential equations (SDEs)

(2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X(t)=f(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g(X(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t),\ t\in(0,T],\\
&X(0)=x_{0}\in D,\end{aligned}\right.

where
T\in(0,\infty)
,
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are functions to be specified below and
D\subset\mathbb{R}
is an open set such that

{\mathbb{P}}(X(t)\in D,\ \forall t\in[0,T])=1.

Note that
D
depends on the choices of
f
and
g
. Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
be such that
a<0<b
, and be the boundary points of
D
:
\partial D=\{a,b\}
. The other cases are treated similarly. We say that a stochastic process
\left(X(t)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}
is a (strong) solution of (
2
) if the corresponding Itô integral equation

X(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(X(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(X(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)

is satisfied, for every
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely, where the second integral is an Itô integral. We refer to
[
40
]
for a well-know textbook on Itô SDEs.

We next state the assumptions on the considered SDE that are needed for the method of artificial barriers to be well-defined and for the convergence analysis. We need the following regularity assumptions on
f
and
g
to obtain the desired
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence rate in Section
4
.

Assumption 1
.

The drift coefficient
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
and diffusion coefficient
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
are Lipschitz continuous functions with Lipschitz constants
\operatorname{Lip}(f)
and
\operatorname{Lip}(g)
, respectively; that is, there exists
\operatorname{Lip}(f),\operatorname{Lip}(f)>0
such that

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)|x-y|,\ \forall x,y\in D,

and

|g(x)-g(y)|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(g)|x-y|,\ \forall x,y\in D.

Remark 1
.

Assumption
1
only assumes that the drift and diffusion coefficient functions are Lipschitz continuous functions on the domain
D
. If
|D|<\infty
, then
f
and
g
(view as functions
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
) can be locally Lipschitz functions with superlinear growth. See Section
7
for a possible choice of superlinearly growing
f
and
g
.

Note that Assumption
1
implies, in particular, that

(3)

|f(x)|\leq|f(0)|+\operatorname{Lip}(f)|x|,\ \forall x\in D,

and

(4)

|g(x)|\leq|g(0)|+\operatorname{Lip}(g)|x|,\ \forall x\in D.

We introduce

L_{f}=\max\left(|f(0)|,\operatorname{Lip}(f)\right),

to have one common bound for
\operatorname{Lip}(f)
and for the linear growth constant in (
3
). For the same reason, we let

L_{g}=\max\left(|g(0)|,\operatorname{Lip}(g)\right).

The following assumption is crucial for us, as it motivates the study of boundary-preserving schemes for the SDE (
2
). Assumption
2
and similar assumptions are sometimes referred to as Feller conditions. We refer to Chapter
15
in
[
20
]
for details on Feller’s classification of unattainable boundaries for SDEs.

Assumption 2
.

The drift coefficient
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
and diffusion coefficient
g:D\to\mathbb{R}
are such that the boundary points
a
and
b
are unattainable by solutions
X
of (
2
); that is, we assume that
{\mathbb{P}}(X(t)\in D,\ \forall t\in[0,T])=1
.

We state well-posedness of the problem (
2
) under Assumptions
1
and
2
in the following proposition, the proof follows from standard theory for SDEs with globally Lipschitz coefficients (by modifying
f
and
g
outside
D
). See, for example,
[
40
]
for a classical book on SDEs. Note that if
|b|<\infty
, then
|X(t)|\leq\max(|a|,|b|)
for all
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely, by Assumption
2
.

Proposition 2
.

If Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied, then there exists a unique continuous (strong) solution
X
to the SDE (
2
) with finite moments

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X(t)|^{p}\right]\leq\begin{cases}C(p,L_{f},L_{g},x_{0}),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\max(|a|^{p},|b|^{p}),\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

for every
p\geq 2
, and satisfying

{\mathbb{P}}\left(X(t)\in D,\ \forall t\in[0,T]\right)=1.

3.
Skorokhod maps and Reflected SDEs

Here we introduce Skorokhod maps and define reflected SDEs (RSDEs) in terms of Skorokhod maps. Shortly put, Skorokhod maps (in our case) are constructed to map any path in
\mathcal{D}[0,T]
to a path in
\mathcal{D}[0,T]
confined to a subset of
\mathbb{R}
. RSDEs, also known as SDEs with boundary conditions, are essentially SDEs that are only allowed to take values in a certain subset of
\mathbb{R}
, if the process hits the boundary of the subset then the process is reflected.

3.1.
Skorokhod maps

First, we introduce and provide some properties of Skorokhod maps underlying the theory of RSDEs discussed in the next section. In essence, a Skorokhod map takes, in our case, a function in
\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and outputs another function in
\mathcal{D}[0,T]
that is confined to the domain under consideration. We say that a Skorokhod map
\Gamma
is associated with a domain
A
if
\Gamma(\psi)(t)\in A
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for all
t\in[0,T]
. We refer to Skorokhod maps associated with half-bounded domains
[\alpha,\infty)
, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, as one-sided Skorokhod maps and we refer to Skorokhod maps associated with bounded domains
[\alpha,\beta]
, for
\alpha<\beta
and
|\alpha|+|\beta|<\infty
, as two-sided Skorokhod maps. One-sided Skorokhod maps were first considered in
[
46
]
. Moreover, an explicit formula for one-sided Skorokhod maps (see Definition
3
below) has been known since long (see, for example,
[
48
]
). An explicit formula for two-sided Skorokhod maps (see Definition
5
below) was only later derived (see
[
27
]
). We are interested in Skorokhod maps for the purpose of constructing implementable numerical schemes and for proving
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence. Note, however, that explicit formulas for Skorokhod maps do not guarantee that numerical schemes based on Skorokhod maps can be implemented (see, for example, the ABEP scheme in Section
6
). Certain schemes based on Skorokhod maps can, however, be implemented. Most importantly, some piecewise constant schemes combined with a Skorokhod map can be implemented (see, for example, the ABEM scheme in Section
5
).

As our interest is only the one-dimensional case, we define the Skorokhod maps using the known explicit representations. See, for example,
[
49
]
for a definition of the Skorokhod map for a convex domain in
d
-dimensional Euclidean space.

Definition 3
.

The one-sided Skorokhod map
\Gamma^{0}:\mathcal{D}[0,T]\to\mathcal{D}[0,T]
associated with the domain
[0,\infty)
is the map defined by

(5)

\Gamma^{0}(\psi)(t)=\psi(t)+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\max\{0,-\psi(s)\},\ \psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T],\ t\in[0,T].

The one-sided Skorokhod map
\Gamma^{\alpha}:\mathcal{D}[0,T]\to\mathcal{D}[0,T]
,
\alpha\neq 0
, associated with the domain
[\alpha,\infty)
is defined by

(6)

\Gamma^{\alpha}(\psi)(t)=\Gamma^{0}(\psi-\alpha)(t)+\alpha,\ \psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T],\ t\in[0,T].

We provide two useful properties of the Skorokhod map
\Gamma^{\alpha}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

The one-sided Skorokhod map
\Gamma^{\alpha}:\mathcal{D}[0,T]\to\mathcal{D}[0,T]
, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfies the following Lipschitz property in the supremum norm

(7)

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{\alpha}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma^{\alpha}(\psi_{2})(t)|\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|,

for every
\psi_{1},\ \psi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for every
r\in[0,T]
, and the following growth bound in the supremum norm

(8)

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{\alpha}(\psi)(t)|\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi(t)|+3|\alpha|,

for every
\psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for every
r\in[0,T]
.

Note that the proof of Lemma
4
reduces to the case
\alpha=0
by (
6
). We refer to
[
14
]
for a proof of (a more general version in the space of real-valued continuous functions on
[0,T]
) (
7
) and the estimate in equation (
8
) with
\alpha=0
follows directly from the representation for
\Gamma^{0}
in equation (
5
). Note that the properties in Lemma
4
(and corresponding lemmas below) are important for the properties of RSDEs in the following section (see Proposition
10
).

We next introduce the two-sided Skorokhod maps associated with bounded domains.

Definition 5
.

The two-sided Skorokhod map
\Gamma^{0,\alpha}:\mathcal{D}[0,T]\to\mathcal{D}[0,T]
, for
\alpha>0
, associated with the domain
[0,\alpha]
is the map defined by

(9)

\Gamma^{0,\alpha}=\Lambda^{\alpha}\circ\Gamma^{0},

where
\Gamma^{0}
is the one-sided Skorokhod map associated with the domain
[0,\infty)
defined in equation (
5
) and where the map
\Lambda^{\alpha}:\mathcal{D}[0,T]\to\mathcal{D}[0,T]
is defined by

\Lambda^{\alpha}(\psi)(t)=\psi(t)-\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left(\min\left(\max(0,\psi(s)-\alpha),\inf_{u\in[s,t]}\psi(u)\right)\right),\ \psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T].

We refer to
[
27
]
for more details on (
9
).

Similarly to
\Gamma^{\alpha}
,
\Gamma^{0,\alpha}
satisfies a Lipschitz estimate in the supremum norm and a bound in the supremum norm that we state in the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

The two-sided Skorokhod map
\Gamma^{0,\alpha}:\mathcal{D}[0,T]\to\mathcal{D}[0,T]
, for
\alpha>0
, satisfies the following Lipschitz property in the supremum norm

(10)

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{0,\alpha}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma^{0,\alpha}(\psi_{2})(t)|\leq 4\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|,

for every
\psi_{1},\ \psi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for every
r\in[0,T]
, and is bounded in the supremum norm

(11)

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{0,\alpha}(\psi)(t)|\leq\alpha,

for every
\psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for every
r\in[0,T]
.

We refer to
[
27
]
for a proof of the estimate in (
10
) and the estimate in (
11
) is immediate since
\Gamma^{0,\alpha}(\psi)(t)\in[0,\alpha]
for every
t\in[0,T]
.

Next, we introduce the Skorokhod maps needed for the method of artificial barriers developed in Section
4
. To this end, we first introduce a family of domains
\{D_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
: For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let

D_{N}=\begin{cases}(a+1/N,\infty),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
(a+1/N,b-1/N),\text{ if }|b|<\infty.\end{cases}

We are interested in the Skorokhod map
\Gamma
associated with
\operatorname{cl}D=(a,b)\cup\{a,b\}
and the family of Skorokhod maps
\{\Gamma_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
each associated with
\operatorname{cl}D_{N}=\partial D_{N}\cup D_{N}
. The maps
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{N}
can be expressed in terms of
\Gamma^{\alpha}
and
\Gamma^{0,\alpha}
as

(12)

\Gamma(\psi)(t)=\begin{cases}\Gamma^{a}(\psi)(t),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\Gamma^{0,(b-a)}(\psi-a)(t)+a,\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

and

(13)

\Gamma_{N}(\psi)(t)=\begin{cases}\Gamma^{a+1/N}(\psi)(t),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\Gamma^{0,(b-a-2/N)}\left(\psi-(a+1/N)\right)(t)+(a+1/N),\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

both for
\psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and
t\in[0,T]
. By (
12
), Lemma
4
, and Lemma
6
,
\Gamma
satisfies the Lipschitz estimate

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma(\psi_{2})(t)|\leq 4\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|,

for
\psi_{1},\ \psi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for
r\in[0,T]
, and the growth bound

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma(\psi)(t)|\leq\begin{cases}2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi(t)|+3|a|,\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\max(|a|,|b|),\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

for
\psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and
r\in[0,T]
. The following lemma states that
\{\Gamma_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies Lipschitz bounds and growth estimates, uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 7
.

The family of Skorokhod maps
\{\Gamma_{N}\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies

(14)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma_{N}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(\psi_{2})(t)|\leq 4\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|,

for all
\psi_{1},\ \psi_{2}\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for
r\in[0,T]
, and

(15)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma_{N}(\psi)(t)|\leq\begin{cases}2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi(t)|+3|a|+3,\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\max(|a|,|b|),\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}[0,T]
and for
r\in[0,T]
.

Proof of Lemma
7
.

Suppose first that
|b|<\infty
. The growth bound in (
15
) is immediate since
\Gamma_{N}(\psi)(t)\in\operatorname{cl}D_{N}\subset\operatorname{cl}D=[a,b]
for all
t\in[0,T]
. The Lipschitz bound in (
14
) follows from reducing it to the known estimates in Lemma
6
using (
13
) for
|b|<\infty

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma_{N}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(\psi_{2})(t)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{0,(b-a-2/N)}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma^{0,(b-a-2/N)}(\psi_{2})(t)|

\displaystyle\leq 4\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|.

The Lipschitz bound for the case
b=\infty
is proved similarly:

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma_{N}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(\psi_{2})(t)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{a+1/N}(\psi_{1})(t)-\Gamma^{a+1/N}(\psi_{2})(t)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq 4\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi_{1}(t)-\psi_{2}(t)|

using (
13
) for
b=\infty
and Lemma
4
. The growth bound for
b=\infty
follows from (
8
)

\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma_{N}(\psi)(t)|=\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\Gamma^{a+1/N}(\psi)(t)|\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi(t)|+3\left|a+\frac{1}{N}\right|\leq 2\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\psi(t)|+3|a|+3.

∎

In the following sections, we will not separate the one-finite-boundary case and the two-finite-boundaries case and we will apply Lemma
7
to both cases.

3.2.
Reflected SDEs

Next, we provide an overview of reflected SDEs (RSDEs), we refer to
[
42
]
for a more complete introduction to RSDEs. We define RSDEs for the Skorokhod map
\Gamma
associated with
\operatorname{cl}D=[a,b]
, for
|a|+|b|<\infty
, and the other considered domains are treated analogously. We first provide a short intuitive description and the idea of RSDEs, and then we provide a proper definition. In this section, instead of imposing the assumptions in Section
2
, we assume that
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are globally Lipschitz functions.

For simplicity, we start with the case of one barrier at
0
(
a=0
and
b=\infty
) with continuous sample paths. The goal in this case is to define a stochastic process
\xi
with the following three properties:

(1)

\xi(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
and
t\mapsto\xi(t)
is continuous, almost surely.

(2)

\xi
has dynamics according to the Itô SDE

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi(t)=f(\xi(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g(\xi(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t)

whenever
\xi(t)>0
.

(3)

\xi
is reflected back into the domain
(0,\infty)
whenever
\xi
hits
0
to avoid
\xi
entering
(-\infty,0)
.

Note that, as there is no mentioning of the reflection in (2) above, the reflection dynamics in (3) must disappear as soon as
\xi
re-enters
(0,\infty)
. The above problem was first studied by Skorokhod in
[
46
]
who proposed to formalise the problem, thereafter known as the Skorokhod problem, as finding a pair of non-anticipating processes
\xi(t)\geq 0
and
L(t)
, for
t\in[0,T]
, such that

(16)

\xi(t)=\xi_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(\xi(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(\xi(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)+L(t),

for all
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely,
L
is non-decreasing,
L(0)=0
and

(17)

L(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{1}_{\xi(s)=0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}L(s),\ t\in[0,T].

Note that the property (
17
) states that the process
L(t)
can only increase when
\xi(t)=0
and that
L(t)
then acts in (
16
) as a positive driving force to push
\xi
into
(0,\infty)
. We use the letter
L
to emphasise that it represent a lower barrier process. Moreover, under Lipschitz conditions on
f
and
g
, there exists a unique solution to the Skorokhod problem. We refer to
[
42
]
for more details. The considered setting in Section
2
reduces to the Lipschitz case by Assumption
2
.

Next, we provide the proper mathematical definition of RSDEs with two barriers at
a
and
b
(with
|a|+|b|<\infty
).

Definition 8
.

A triplet of
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-adapted processes
(\xi(t),U(t),L(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is a solution of the reflected SDE

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi(t)=\xi_{0}+f(\xi(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g(\xi(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}U(t)+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}L(t),\ t\in[0,T],

with reflection at
a
and
b
, with
-\infty<a<b<\infty
, and the initial condition
\xi(0)=\xi_{0}\in[a,b]
if the following holds:

(1)

\xi(t)\in[a,b]
for every
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely.

(2)

t\mapsto U(t)
and
t\mapsto L(t)
are non-decreasing and
U(0)=L(0)=0
.

(3)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{1}_{\xi(s)\in(\infty,b)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}U(s)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{1}_{\xi(s)\in(a,\infty)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}L(s)=0
, for every
t\in[0,T]
.

(4)

\xi(t)=\xi(0)+\int_{0}^{t}f(\xi(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(\xi(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)-U(t)+L(t)
, for every
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely.

(5)

All the above integrals are well-defined.

Note that if
w
solves the Itô functional SDE

(18)

w(t)=\xi_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(\Gamma(w)(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(\Gamma(w)(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s),\ t\in[0,T],

then there exist
U(t)
and
L(t)
such that
(\Gamma(w)(t),U(t),L(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
satisfy Definition
8
(see, for example, Section
1.2
in
[
27
]
), where we recall that
\Gamma
is the Skorokhod map associated with
[a,b]
(see Section
3.1
). We say that
(\xi,w,L-U)
is an associated triple on
[a,b]
if Definition
8
and (
18
) holds.

Remark 9
.

If
\xi
cannot hit one of the boundary points (for example
b
) then the corresponding barrier process (in this example
U
) can be completely removed or set equal to
0
(in this example
U\equiv 0
) in the definition above. This follows from uniqueness of solutions to RSDEs, see below or
[
42
]
for more details. Of particular interest in this paper is the case where
\xi
cannot hit either of the boundary points (see Assumption
2
), which implies that both barrier processes will, in fact, be
0
(meaning that
U\equiv 0\equiv L
), this enables us to connect the original SDE in (
1
) to RSDEs (see Proposition
21
).

We now collect some well-known results for RSDEs that will be needed for the convergence analysis. We refer to
[
42
]
for a proof, but the existence and uniqueness is due to
[
46
]
.

Proposition 10
.

If
f,g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
in Definition
8
are globally Lipschitz continuous, then there exists a unique continuous solution
\xi
to the RSDE in Definition
8
. Moreover,
\xi
satisfies moment bounds

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\xi(t)|^{p}\right]\leq\begin{cases}C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},\xi_{0}),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\max(|a|^{p},|b|^{p}),\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

for every
p\geq 2
, and the time increments satisfy

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left|\xi(t)-\xi(s)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},\xi_{0})|t-s|^{p/2},

for every
p\geq 2
, for every
t,s\in[0,T]
.

4.
Method of artificial barriers

We now present the idea of adding artificial barriers to the types of SDEs in equation (
2
) and we call it the method of artificial barriers. Note that Assumption
2
is important for this methodology to work and be of interest.

4.1.
Construction of artificial barriers

The method of artificial barriers for constructing boundary-preserving numerical schemes is based on the idea that if the solution process
X
to an SDE cannot leave a domain, then we can augment the SDE with barriers outside this domain to obtain an RSDE whose solution equals the original process
X
, almost surely. The obtained RSDE can then be approximated using any boundary-preserving numerical scheme for RSDEs. To straight away apply this idea to the considered setting would be problematic because numerical schemes for RSDEs are in general non-strict boundary-preserving; that is, the boundary points are not unattainable by numerical schemes for RSDEs. This is an issue since
f
and
g
that we consider typically vanish on the boundary, and most numerical schemes that hit the boundary will thereafter stay at the boundary. Instead, shortly put, we modify the coefficient functions
f
and
g
to move the boundary slightly inside the original domain
D
and augment the modified SDE with artificial barriers. We can then discretise the resulting (modified) RSDE using a (non-strict) boundary-preserving numerical scheme. Note that the exact details of adding artificial barriers depend on the specific problem at hand, the here outlined method is constructed to fit our purposes. The rest of this section is dedicated to formulate the method of artificial barriers mathematically.

The first step of the method of artificial barriers is to modify the drift and diffusion coefficients such that the unattainable boundary points are moved to
a+1/N
and
b-1/N
. If
b=\infty
, then we interpret
b-1/N=\infty
. To formalise this, we introduce an integer
N\in\mathbb{N}
and consider the following modified Itô SDE

(19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X_{N}(t)=f_{N}(X_{N}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g_{N}(X_{N}(t))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t),\ t\in(0,T],\\
&X_{N}(0)=x_{0}\in D_{N},\end{aligned}\right.

where
D_{N}=(a+1/N,b-1/N)\subset D
and where
f_{N},g_{N}:D_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined below in such a way that
X_{N}(t)\in D_{N}
, for every
t\in[0,T]
, almost surely. For ease of presentation and to avoid technicalities regarding the initial value, we assume that
x_{0}\in D_{1}
.
x_{0}\in D_{1}
can be relaxed to
x_{0}\in D
by defining
X_{N}(0)=\Pi_{N-1}(x_{0})\in D_{N}
, where
\Pi_{N-1}:\mathbb{R}\to\operatorname{cl}D_{N-1}\subset D_{N}
is the projection onto
\operatorname{cl}D_{N-1}
(see (
28
) for a definition). Replacing
x_{0}
with
\Pi_{N-1}(x_{0})
in (
19
) would only introduce a deterministic error of order
1/N
, and would not alter the convergence rates.

We specify the functions
f_{N}
and
g_{N}
as follows: For
x\in D_{N}
, let

(20)

\Psi_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}x-\frac{1}{N},\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\frac{b-a}{b-a-\frac{2}{N}}\left(x-\left(a+\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)+a,\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

f_{N}(x)=f\left(\Psi_{N}(x)\right),

and

g_{N}(x)=g\left(\Psi_{N}(x)\right).

As
x\mapsto\Psi_{N}(x)
in (
20
) is a linear function (both for
b=\infty
and for
|b|<\infty
), the decay of
f_{N}
near
a+1/N
and near
b-1/N
is the same as the decay of
f
near
a
and near
b
, respectively. The same is true for
g_{N}
and
g
. This implies that the boundary behaviour of
X
(see Proposition
2
) is the same as the boundary behaviour of
X_{N}
(see Proposition
20
below). Observe that the case
b=\infty
in (
20
) can be obtained from the case
|b|<\infty
in (
20
) by

\lim_{b\to\infty}\Psi_{N}(x)=\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{b-a}{b-a-\frac{2}{N}}\left(x-\left(a+\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)+a=x-\frac{1}{N}.

Remark 11
.

The above is only well-defined if
N>\frac{2}{b-a}
. If
|b-a|\leq 2
, then we simply restrict
N\in\mathbb{N}
to
N>\frac{2}{b-a}
. Henceforth, we assume, for ease of presentation, that
|b-a|>2
.

The following lemma states that the modified coefficient functions
f_{N}
and
g_{N}
satisfies a Lipschitz estimate on
D
uniformly in
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 12
.

If Assumption
1
is satisfied, then there exist constants
\hat{L}_{f}
and
\hat{L}_{g}
such that the following Lipschitz conditions hold

\left|f_{N}(x)-f_{N}(y)\right|\leq\hat{L}_{f}|x-y|,\ \forall x,y\in D_{N},

and

\left|g_{N}(x)-g_{N}(y)\right|\leq\hat{L}_{g}|x-y|,\ \forall x,y\in D_{N},

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
\hat{L}_{f}
and
\hat{L}_{g}
can be chosen as

\hat{L}_{f}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Lip}(f),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left|\frac{b-a}{b-a-2}\right|,\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

and

\hat{L}_{g}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Lip}(g),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\operatorname{Lip}(g)\left|\frac{b-a}{b-a-2}\right|,\text{ if }|b|<\infty.\end{cases}

and such that the following linear growth conditions hold

|f_{N}(x)|\leq\hat{L}_{f}(1+|x|),\ x\in D_{N},

and

|g_{N}(x)|\leq\hat{L}_{g}(1+|x|),\ x\in D_{N},

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof of Lemma
12
.

We provide the proof for
f_{N}
in the case
|b|<\infty
, the other cases are proved in the same way. We use Lipschitz continuity of
f
on
D
and the defining formula for
\Psi_{N}
in (
20
) to see that

\displaystyle\left|f_{N}(x)-f_{N}(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|f(\Psi_{N}(x))-f(\Psi_{N}(y))\right|\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left|\Psi_{N}(x)-\Psi_{N}(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left|\frac{b-a}{b-a-2/N}\right||x-y|

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Lip}(f)\left|\frac{b-a}{b-a-2}\right||x-y|.

∎

Alternatively, as the composition of two Lipschitz continuous functions is a Lipschitz continuous function, Lemma
12
also follows from

(21)

\operatorname{Lip}(\Psi_{N})=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\Psi_{N}^{\prime}(x)\right|=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\left|\frac{b-a}{b-a-\frac{2}{N}}\right|,\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}\leq\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\left|\frac{b-a}{b-a-2}\right|,\text{ if }|b|<\infty.\end{cases}

The following lemma quantifies the error introduced by replacing
f
and
g
with
f_{N}
and
g_{N}
, respectively, which will be used for the comparison between
X
and
X_{N}
in Proposition
19

Lemma 13
.

If Assumption
1
is satisfied, then there exist constants
C(L_{f},a,b)
and
C(L_{g},a,b)
such that

\left|f(x)-f_{N}(x)\right|\leq C(L_{f},a,b)N^{-1}(1+|x|),\ x\in D,

\left|g(x)-g_{N}(x)\right|\leq C(L_{g},a,b)N^{-1}(1+|x|),\ x\in D,

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
. If
b=\infty
, then the constants are independent of
a
,
b
, and
x
.

Proof of Lemma
13
.

We prove the estimate for
f
and
f_{N}
in the case
|b|<\infty
, the other cases are proved in the same way. By definition of
f_{N}
, we have that

|f(x)-f_{N}(x)|=|f(x)-f(\Psi_{N}(x))|\leq L_{f}|x-\Psi_{N}(x)|.

Moreover, by definition of
\Psi_{N}
in (
20
) we have that

(22)

\begin{split}\left|x-\Psi_{N}(x)\right|&=\left|\frac{b+a}{N(b-a)-2}-\frac{2}{N(b-a)-2}x\right|\\
&\leq N^{-1}\left(\frac{|b+a|}{|b-a-2/N|}+\frac{2}{|b-a-2/N|}|x|\right)\\
&\leq N^{-1}\left(\frac{|b+a|}{|b-a-2|}+\frac{2}{|b-a-2|}|x|\right)\leq N^{-1}C(a,b)(1+|x|)\end{split}

and inserting this into the estimate for
|f(x)-f_{N}(x)|
above gives the desired result. Note that the estimate (
22
) reduces to

\left|x-\Psi_{N}(x)\right|=\frac{1}{N}

in the case
b=\infty
and the constants in the lemma can be chosen to be independent of
a
,
b
, and
x
.
∎

As a result of Lemma
12
and Lemma

, we can bound the linear growth constant for
f_{N}
, independently of

:

(23)

|f_{N}(x)|\leq|f_{N}(x)-f(x)|+|f(x)|\leq C(L_{f},a,b)(1+|x|),\ x\in D.

The same holds for
g_{N}
.

From the above lemmas implies, in particular, that the domain of (

and its solution

.

Proposition 14
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. If Assumptions

and

are satisfied, then there exists a unique solution

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\leq\begin{cases}C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},x_{0}),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\

Lemma 17
.

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(X(s))-g_{N}(Y(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\right]\\
\leq C(p,T,L_{g},a,b)\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|X(t)-Y(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+N^{-p}\left(1+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|Y(t)|^{p}\right]\right)\right).

.

Remark 18
.

In the same way as for Lemma

The proof of Lemma

Proof of Lemma
17
.

Let
p\geq 2
. We apply the BDG inequality and Jensen’s inequality for integrals (requires

) to see that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(X(s))-g_{N}(Y(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T){\mathbb{E}}\left[\int_{0}^{r}\left|g(X(s))-g_{N}(Y(s))\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right]

and the same argument as we used in the proof of Lemma

gives us

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\left|g(X(s))-g_{N}(Y(s))\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C(p,L_{g},a,b)\left(\int_{0}^{r}|X(s)-Y(s)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{r}|g(Y(s))-g_{N}(Y(s))|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,L_{g},a,b)\left(\int_{0}^{r}|X(s)-Y(s)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+N^{-p}\left(1+\sup_{s\in[0,r]}|Y(s)|^{p}\right)\right),

where we also used Lemma

5.
Artificial Barriers Euler–Maruyama

In this section, we combine the method of artificial barriers from Section
4
with a modification of the classical projected Euler–Maruyama (PEM) scheme for RSDEs applied to the RSDE with modified coefficients in (
24
). We call the resulting scheme the Artificial Barriers EM scheme and abbreviate it as ABEM. We first introduce the ABEM scheme, and then we show that the ABEM scheme satisfy Assumption
3
in Section
4
. We can from this conclude that the scheme is boundary-preserving and converges to the solution

of the considered SDE (

We remark that the standard projected EM (PEM) scheme applied to the SDE in (
1
) is non-strict boundary-preserving, meaning that the approximations might hit the boundary
\partial D
. A non-strict boundary-preserving numerical scheme is particularly problematic in our considered cases where both
f
and
g
typically have zeros at the boundary, since such a scheme would forever remain at the boundary once it hits it.

L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence of order
1/2-
of the PEM scheme for RSDEs is a known result in the literature (see, for example,
[
41
]
for the case

). We show in this section that this is also true for the ABEM scheme for the RSDEs in (
24
) and that the constants for the moment bounds and the

-error can be taken to be independent of
N
and
\Delta t
, and hence the ABEM scheme satisfy Assumption
3
.

Let
\Pi_{N}:\mathbb{R}\to\operatorname{cl}D_{N}
denote the projection onto
\operatorname{cl}D_{N}
, defined by

(28)

\Pi_{N}(x)=\begin{cases}a+1/N,\text{ for }x<a+1/N,\\
x,\text{ for }x\in\operatorname{cl}D_{N},\\
b-1/N,\text{ for }x>b-1/N.\end{cases}

If
b=\infty
, then we interpret

in (
28
) as never true. We define the ABEM scheme, denoted by
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}
, as follows: Let
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(0)=x_{0}\in D_{1}
and successively define
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m+1})
, for

, as

\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m+1})=\Pi_{N}\left(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m})+f\left(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m})\right)\Delta t+g\left(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m})\right)\Delta B_{m}\right),

. Replacing

and

in (

) with

and

, respectively, would make it possible for scheme to get stuck at the boundary points of

\begin{split}\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m+1})=\min\{b-1/N,\max\{&a+1/N,\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m})\\
&+f(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m}))\Delta t+g(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t_{m}))\Delta B_{m}\}\}.\end{split}

We implement the ABEM scheme in Section
7

For the purpose of the convergence analysis, we extend the definition in equation (

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}=\sup_{m=0,\ldots,\ell_{\Delta t}(r)/\Delta t}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m})\right|^{p}

\left.+\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}})(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\right).

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}})(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{g})\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+|a|^{p}+1\right)

Thus, together we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g})\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+|a|^{p}+1\right)

An application of Grönwall’s lemma now gives the desired estimate.
∎

The following theorem establishes
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence, for every

, of the approximation
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}
to the solution
X
of the considered SDE (

Theorem 25
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2

for every

and for every

, where the constant

N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta t
. If

b

Proof of Theorem
25
.

We verify that Assumption
3
is fulfilled and the result then follows from Theorem

. The proof follows the same proof idea as in
[

with the extra issue that the coefficient functions of the RSDE for
\xi_{N}
in (
24
) are not the same as in the definition of the Artificial Barriers EM scheme
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}
. In view of Proposition
24
, it remains to prove that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2-\epsilon}\right),

with a constant independent of
N\in\mathbb{N}
and of
\Delta t>0
and independent of
b
if
b=\infty
. To this end, we split the error as

(33)

\left|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\leq C(p)\left(\left|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)-\tilde{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}+\left|\tilde{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right)

and estimate each term separately.

We first consider the first term on the right hand side of equation (
33
). By the Lipschitz property of the Skorokhod map
\Gamma_{N}
(see Lemma

), we can bound

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEM}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)-\tilde{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}})(t)\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}.

We use the definition of
\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)
, that
\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)
and
\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)
coincide on the grid points and that
\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)
is piecewise constant to see that

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)

\displaystyle=\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m})+\int_{t_{m}}^{t}f(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{m}}^{t}g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)

\displaystyle=\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)+\int_{t_{m}}^{t}f(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t_{m}}^{t}g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)

\displaystyle=\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)+f(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m}))(t-t_{m})+g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m}))(B(t)-B(t_{m}))

for
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
. Moving
\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)
to the left hand side we thus have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M-1}\sup_{t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})}\left|f(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m}))(t-t_{m})\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle+C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M-1}\sup_{t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})}\left|g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m}))(B(t)-B(t_{m}))\right|^{p}\right]

The first term on the right hand side can be bounded by
C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,x_{0})\Delta t^{p}
if
b=\infty
and by
C(p,L_{f},a,b)\Delta t^{p}
if

by linear growth of
f
on
D
in (
3
) and the moment bounds on
\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}
in Proposition
24
. For the second term, we first use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to separate the term involving
g
and the term involving
B(t)-B(t_{m})
as such:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M-1}\sup_{t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})}\left|g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m-1}))(B(t)-B(t_{m}))\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M-1}\sup_{t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})}\left|g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m}))\right|^{2p}\right]\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\times\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M-1}\sup_{t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})}\left|(B(t)-B(t_{m}))\right|^{2p}\right]\right)^{1/2}

of which the first term can be bounded by
C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,x_{0})\Delta t^{p}
if
b=\infty
and by
C(p,L_{g},a,b)\Delta t^{p}
if
|b|<\infty
by linear growth of
g
on
D
in (
4
) and the moment bounds on
\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}
in Proposition
24
. The second term can be bounded by

(34)

\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{m=0,\ldots,M-1}\sup_{t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})}\left|(B(t)-B(t_{m}))\right|^{2p}\right]\right)^{1/2}\leq C(p,T)\Delta t^{p/2-\epsilon},

for every
\epsilon>0
(see, for example, Lemma
3
in
[
48
]
). Note that the bound in (
34
) can be improved (in the limit) here by using, for example, Lemma
4.4
in
[
41
]
(

) or Lemma A.
4
in
[
47
]
. Thus, we have the estimate

(35)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t_{m})-\tilde{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\Delta t^{p/2-\epsilon},

where the constant is independent of
b
and
x_{0}
if
|b|<\infty
, for the first term on the right hand side of equation (
33
).

We now consider the second term of the right hand side of equation (
33
). We first, again, use the Lipschitz estimate for the Skorokhod map
\Gamma_{N}
in Lemma
7

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\tilde{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\Gamma_{N}(\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-w_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right].

Recall that

\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)

where

are the modified drift and diffusion coefficient functions introduced in Section

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\tilde{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-w_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))-f_{N}(\xi_{N}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle+C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(s))-g_{N}(\xi_{N}(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b)\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b)N^{-p}\left(1+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\right),

with the constants being independent of
a
and
b
if
b=\infty
, where we also used Lemma
15
and Lemma
17
(and connecting remarks). Inserting this, the moment bounds in Proposition

and the estimate (

) into the splitting of the error in (

), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2-\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle+C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b)\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

and by applying Grönwall’s inequality we deduce that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEM}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})(N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2-\epsilon}),

where the constant is independent of
b
and
x_{0}
if
b=\infty
, which is the desired estimate and this concludes the proof.
∎

As in Corollary

, we couple
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

.

and

. Then there exists, for every

6.
Artificial Barriers Euler–Peano

In this section, we combine the method of artificial barriers developed in Section
4
with a modification of the classical Euler–Peano (EP) scheme for RSDEs applied to the RSDE with modified coefficients in (
24
). We refer to the resulting scheme as the Artificial Barriers Euler–Peano scheme and we abbreviate it as ABEP. We first provide the definition of the scheme and then we verify that the ABEP scheme satisfy Assumption
3
in Section
4
. By Theorem
22
, we can then conclude that the ABEP scheme is boundary-preserving and converges to the solution
X
of the considered SDE (
1
).

The classical EP scheme for RSDEs is known to exhibit
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence of order
1/2
(see, for example,
[
48
]
). The goal in this section is to show that this is also true for the ABEP scheme for the RSDE in (
24
) with an
L^{p}(\Omega)
-error constant that can be taken to be independent of
N
, and hence that the ABEP scheme satisfies Assumption
3
.

We would like to make an important remark before proceeding to the definition of the ABEP scheme. There are explicit expressions for the EP and ABEP schemes (see equation (
38
) for the ABEP scheme). However, as the supremum and infimum in Definition
3
and Definition
5
are over infinitely many time points, the EP and ABEP schemes are not known to be implementable. In other words, as of now, the ABEP scheme is of more theoretical than practical use. We include the ABEP scheme as it may be used to construct other implementable schemes (for example, a modified version of the Euler scheme for RSDEs studied in
[
29
]
).

We now provide a step-by-step construction of the ABEP scheme. We refer the reader not familiar with RSDEs to equation (
38
) for a representation of the ABEP scheme without any mentioning of RSDEs. Recall that the classical EM scheme for the SDE in (
2
) is given by

X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(t_{m+1})=X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(t_{m})+f(X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(t_{m}))\Delta t+g(X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(t_{m}))\Delta B_{m},

for
m=0,\ldots,M-1
, and, for the purpose of defining the ABEP scheme, we extend the above to continuous time by requiring that
X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(t)
solves the following SDE with piecewise constant coefficients

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(t)=f(X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(t)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g(X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(t)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t),\ t\in[0,T],

where we recall that
\ell_{\Delta t}(t)=t_{m}
for
t\in[t_{m},t_{m+1})
, with initial value
X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}(0)=x_{0}
. We now add a lower barrier process
\hat{L}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}
and an upper barrier process
\hat{U}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}
, in the sense of Section
3.2
, to force
X^{\textrm{EM}}_{\Delta t}
to stay in
\operatorname{cl}D_{N}
:

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)=f(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(t)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+g(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(t)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(t)-\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{U}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)+\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\hat{L}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t),\ t\in[0,T],

or equivalently in integral from

(36)

\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)-\hat{U}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)+\hat{L}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t),\ t\in[0,T].

Observe that, if
b=\infty
then
\hat{U}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}
can be removed with no change. We also introduce the process

(37)

\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)=x_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}f(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}g(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s),\ t\in[0,T],

to obtain an associated triple
(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t},\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}},\hat{L}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}-\hat{U}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})
in the sense of Section
3.2
. Then, in particular, we have that
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}=\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})
. Note that (
37
) on
[\ell_{\Delta t}(s),\ell_{\Delta t}(s)+\Delta t]
is the classical EM scheme starting from the value
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(\ell_{\Delta t}(s))
. Moreover, using the explicit expressions for the Skorokhod map in Definition
5
, we have the following representation for
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}
in the two-finite-boundaries case (
|b|<\infty
)

(38)

\begin{split}\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)&=a+\frac{1}{N}+\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)+\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\max(0,-\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(s))\\
&-\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\left(\min\left(\max\left(0,\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(s)+\sup_{r\in[0,s]}\max\left(0,-\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(r)\right)-\left(b-a-\frac{2}{N},\right)\right),\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.\inf_{u\in[s,t]}\left(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(u)+\sup_{v\in[0,u]}\max\left(0,-\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(v)\right)\right)\right)\right),\ t\in[0,T].\end{split}

The involved formula in (
38
) is simplified in the one-finite-boundary case (
b=\infty
), but the ABEP scheme is not implementable in this case either.

The following proposition provides moment bounds and boundary-preservation of the ABEP scheme. Since the ABEP scheme is defined in (
36
) as the solution to an RSDE, the boundary-preserving property of the ABEP scheme follows directly.

Theorem 27
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

. Then the ABEP scheme satisfies moment bounds

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)\right|^{p}\right]\leq\begin{cases}C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,x_{0}),\text{ if }b=\infty,\\
\max(|a|^{p},|b|^{p}),\text{ if }|b|<\infty,\end{cases}

\operatorname{cl}D_{N}

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)\in\operatorname{cl}D_{N},\ \forall t\in[0,T]\right)=1.

The constant
C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,x_{0})
is independent of
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta t
.

Proof of Theorem
27
.

Let
p\geq 2
. The case of a bounded domain (

) is immediate since

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\Gamma_{N}\left(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}\right)(t)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p)\left({\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)\right|^{p}\right]+|a|^{p}+1\right).

We use (
37

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C(p)\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}+\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\right)

\displaystyle=C(p)\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+I_{1}+I_{2}\right).

For
I_{1}
, we use Jensen’s inequality for integrals and the linear growth of

on

(see Assumption

) and of
\Gamma_{N}
(see Lemma

) to obtain

I_{1}\leq C(p,T,L_{f})\left(\int_{0}^{r}\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+|a|^{p}+1\right)

and for

on

(see Assumption

) and of

{\mathbb{E}}\left[I_{2}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{g})\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+|a|^{p}+1\right).

Thus, in total

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g})\left(|x_{0}|^{p}+\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+|a|^{p}+1\right)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,x_{0}).

∎

We now state and prove the convergence theorem of this section; namely, the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-convergence, for every

, of
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}
to the solution
X
of the considered SDE (
1
). As for Theorem

22

[
48
]
), we include it since, in contrast to the EP scheme, the coefficient functions of the RSDE for
\xi_{N}
are not the same as in the definition of the modified EP scheme
\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}
.

Theorem 28
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta t>0
. Then

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)-X(t)\right|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2}\right),

for every
p\geq 2
, where the constant is independent of
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Delta t
. If
b=\infty
, then the constant is independent of
b
.

Proof of Theorem
28
.

In view of Theorem
22
and Theorem
27
, it remains to prove that

(39)

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\hat{\xi}^{\textrm{ABEP}}_{N,\Delta t}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2}\right),

for some constant independent of
N\in\mathbb{N}
and independent of
\Delta t>0
, and independent of
b
if
b=\infty
.

We first reduce the estimate to the corresponding estimate for
\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-\xi_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(t)|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p){\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\right],

using the Lipschitz property of
\Gamma_{N}
in (
14
), and we prove that the right hand side can be bounded by the right hand side in equation (
39
) using a Grönwall argument.

By definition of
\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}
(see (
37
)) and
w_{N}
(see (
25
)), we can estimate

(40)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,r]}&|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\\
&\leq C(p)\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))-f_{N}(\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\right|^{p}\\
&+C(p)\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\left|\int_{0}^{t}g(\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(\ell_{\Delta t}(s)))-g_{N}(\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(s))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B(s)\right|^{p}\\
&=C(p)(J_{1}+J_{2}).\end{split}

{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{1}\right]
and
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{2}\right]
are estimated in the same way, the difference is that we use Lemma
15
for
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{1}\right]
and we use Lemma
17
for
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{2}\right]
. We provide the argument for
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{2}\right]
, as the term involving the noise is (usually) the difficult part. Using (remarks to) Lemma
17
, the Lipschitz estimate for
\Gamma_{N}
in (
14
), and moment bounds for
\hat{\xi}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}=\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})
in Theorem
27
, we can estimate

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,L_{g},a,b)\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(\ell_{\Delta t}(s))-\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(s)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C(p,T,L_{g},a,b)N^{-p}\left(1+{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(t)|^{p}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\Gamma_{N}(\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}})(t)-\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(\ell_{\Delta t}(s))-\Gamma_{N}(w_{N})(s)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+N^{-p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2}\right),

where the constants are independent of
b
if
b=\infty
and
where we also used the time regularity in (
27
) to bound

{\mathbb{E}}\left[|\xi_{N}(\ell_{\Delta t}(s))-\xi_{N}(s)|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\Delta t^{p/2}.

Similar argument gives the same bound for
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{1}\right]

{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{1}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2}\right),

where the constant is independent of
b
if
b=\infty
. Therefore, applying
{\mathbb{E}}
to equation (
40
) and using the above bounds for
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{1}\right]
and
{\mathbb{E}}\left[J_{2}\right]
, we obtain

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,r]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\\
\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(\int_{0}^{r}{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,s]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2}\right)

and applying Grönwall’s inequality we conclude that

{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\hat{w}_{N,\Delta t}^{\textrm{ABEP}}(t)-w_{N}(t)|^{p}\right]\leq C(p,T,L_{f},L_{g},a,b,x_{0})\left(N^{-p}+\Delta t^{p/2}\right),

where the constants are independent of
b
if
b=\infty
. This finishes the proof.
∎

From Theorem
28
, we also obtain almost sure pathwise convergence in Corollary
29
.

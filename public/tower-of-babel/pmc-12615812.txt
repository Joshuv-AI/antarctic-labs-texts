Multi stage sentiment analysis for product reviews on Twitter using optimized machine learning algorithm
Authors: Lakshmi Prasad Mudarakola, Ranjith Kumar Gatla, Akella S. Narasimha Raju, Amar Y. Jaffar, Abdullah Alzahrani, Adis Abebaw Dessalegn
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Modern consumer feedback is located on societal networks, and therefore the activity of such sites as Twitter is important for product reviews. This work explores the feasibility of using machine learning algorithms to classify sentiments in the context of tweets about products, providing marketing departments with richer insights into customer opinions. The proposed approach is that of an optimal multi-stage framework of sentiment classification that combines both conventional machine learning models, i.e. Support Vector Machines (SVM), Naive Bayes, and the Random Forest, and deep learning, i.e. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The novelty of the present work is the optimization and comparison of the application of these methods and the determination of the most effective sentiment classification method of the product-related discussions. A set of 5200 English tweets gathered within one months was taken into consideration, including positive, negative and neutral comments on opinions. The results show that machine learning can be useful in extracting and analyzing unstructured textual data from social media, supporting the use of social media sentiment analysis. Furthermore, the research addresses the possibility of applying sentiment analysis as an effective business strategy for enhancing client approaches and marketing appeals. By analyzing consumer attitudes towards products and services indicated on the site, companies can improve products and services, resolve issues, and gain consumer loyalty. This study provides valuable insights into the relationship between customer opinions and business strategies in today’s competitive environment.

Introduction

In the current digitized space, Twitter as a direct media channel for the dissemination of instantaneous information and sharing of opinions has become a single important source. Twitter helps to fill this gap in point of view establishment due to allowing millions of people to express their feelings daily hence making it a worthy tool for knowing the public opinion and trends. The driving force behind this endeavor is based on the rationale of using this never-ending stream of opinion-bearing content in its attempts to explain community sentiment, survey-assisted decision-making, and support workstation stakeholders, such as marketing, brand management, and responding to a crisis. This tremendous volume of data is employed by the “Sentiment Analysis of the Twitter Reviews User-Generated Content (UGC)” project it assists in supplying information for the public sentiment on different subjects or events, products, or brands. The first step towards the end of the project is the passage of all the tweets into separate datasets. These are tagged as positive, negative, and neutral to classify them. Such categorization allows for catching the prevalent moods that support the users in formulating reasonable decisions, assessing the reputation of the brand, and observing the trends in public opinion concerning social and political problems.

Sentiment exploration which is a core task of NLP is about recognition of opinions and feelings through text analysis. However, sentiment analysis is not as simple as it seems because this task involves using other NLP sub tasks such as recognizing sarcasm and reading between the lines. The respective informality of the tweet data is another added obstacle in the way of conducting efficient and unbiased analysis, such as spelling errors, idiomatic expressions, and abbreviations. To overcome the above difficulties, the project incorporates a system that consists of: data collection from Twitter through the use of Tweepy API; text pre-processing, sentiment analysis using machine learning algorithms or lexicon-based approaches; and representation of data through visualization. Bringing into play popular Python modules such as Tweepy, matplotlib, and Flask, the product is being designed to do these tasks smoothly for the user with a clear interface to interact with the analysed sentiment results in near real-time.

Then came a significant amount of change in how the Machine learning algorithm readdresses the problem of sentiment analysis i.e., how it has identified and categorized the aggregates of positive and negative expressions made by a person to the message he or she conveyed by his or her language. At the initial stage, the manual and rule-based techniques were very much visible, however with the emerging power of controlled knowledge algorithms such as Decision Trees, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Naive Bayes the customer sentiments are well analysed and understood. These algorithms allow the sentimental classifiers to make predictions of the sentiment labels depending on the features extracted from the text and modules enabling more effective and scalable. Nevertheless, these challenges are still there consisting of the scarcity of labelled data, which is needed by the machine learning model in training. With further, Twitter data ambiguity provided by the elements such as the cultural and semantic context and linguistic influencing challenges the sentiment analysis systems more abundantly. The suggested system represents a system-wide manner to sentiment analysis which implemented natural language processing mechanisms, the decision-making process will be more reliable. The main approach, in turn, is not only about the application of VADER which was developed specifically for the inquiry of the text information, but for social networks. In contrast to classical dictionaries, VADER assigns sentiment polarity scores for words and phrases, hence allowing for text analysis in Twitter on the background of huge amount of sentiments. In its system, an authentication feature is also introduced, while the existing mechanisms are utilized to validate user information and improve the security of sentiment analysis. The underpinning idea of the proposed system is to go beyond the capabilities of currently available sentiment analysis systems and to offer vital information on the trend of the public mood as depicted by Twitter.

The offered system features the ability to calculate the sentiment scores which are interpretative for each tweet. By doing this, the users can examine the polarity and the strength of opinion. The ensemble of composite “compound” scores from VADER contributes to the overall sentiment mood label pool, as well as liberating the downstream processing, visualization, and decision-making. Moreover, this processing will incorporate authentication which in turn guarantees that sentiments from these processes are both secure and fair. Additionally, the system is designed to conduct sentiment analysis and thus it can be used for purposes such as social media monitoring, brand management, and crisis detection which are based on the moment. Using these merits, the supposed one will be aimed at revolutionize of Twitter emotions research, offering the stakeholders concrete charges and the basis for their deliberate decisions. Facing the unpredictable sentiment analysis of Twitter data, the future holds the prospect to be bright owing to advancements in AI and ML technologies. Deep learning solutions, founded in neural networks, are believed to nurture sentiment analysis precision due to the fact they entangle the whole spectrum of pronounced Twitter data. In addition, multichannel data sources, such as pictures, videos, and emoticons, are incorporated with textual material, allowing more whole sense of data. Domain-specific sentiment analysis models which keep on evolving to comprehend various industries and sectors will even be in demand, so investors can use this to get actionable insights across different markets. But of course, human judgment and opinion will be the exception to these as tech advancements seek to interpret various complex languages, sarcasm, and alack sentences. Surprisingly, it will give room to innovation and discovering ethical questions, while AI’s and ML’s help will enrich TA of Twitter data as a worthy tool for the identification of public sentiment and data-driven decision-making.

Even though substantial advancements in the lexicon-based methods and other conventional machine learning models, including Naive Bayes, Decision Trees, and SVM, were proved to be insufficient when used to process Twitter data since it carries unique problems with short text length, slang, and sarcasm, as well as cultural context. The existing systems either fail to exhibit scalability, or cannot capture sequential context in the text, or require excessive amounts of labeled data to work well. To eliminate these shortcomings, the proposed study proposes an optimised multi-stage model that brings together the traditional machine learning models with the deep learning networks like LSTM. The integration further not only increases robustness to noisy and informal language used in Twitter but also improves the classification accuracy and that is more fit in real-life product feeling analysis and business applications.

The existing methods are unable to deal with the noisy and informal language of Twitter, are not scalable, or need large amounts of labelled data. To address these shortcomings, this paper suggests an efficient multi-stage model, which integrates conventional machine learning models with deep learning models like LSTM, which is facilitated by preprocessing, and authentication systems. This hybrid model is more robust and accurate in classifications as well as serves in real-time applicability in real world applications like social media monitoring, analysis of product feedbacks, and crisis detection. The innovation is in the fact that ML and DL models are synergistically integrated to provide a scalable, safe and context-sensitive sentiment analysis system.

The “Algorithm survey” explains the methodology, preprocessing of data, selecting the algorithm, and architecture of the system. The implementation environment and preparation of the dataset are presented in “Methodology”. “Implementation issues” argues about interpretation of the experimental results and interpretation. In “Experimental results” there are comparative evaluation measures. “Conclusion” is a conclusion of the study with the prospects of the contacts of the future research.

Support vector machine model (SVM)

While a Support Vector Machine (SVM) is a supervised model for the classification of both linear in addition non-linear statistics, it works as a support for other machine learning techniques. If data is represented the way we desire it to behave, SVM searches for the optimal linear liner kernel that plots through all classes. Random forest classifier

The Random Forest classifier is an assortment of Decision Trees, which overcomes the downside of weak Decision Trees, such as noise susceptibility, by adding more Decision Trees to it. Instead of Decision Tree as the only element, the Random Forest classifier resorts to ‘Bootstrapping’. Hence, each tree is accomplished on a separate unique subset of the training data. This aggregate technique benefits the instances of higher trend and overall performance.

Long short-term memory (LSTM)

LSTM networks, a subfamily of RNNs that have been proven to be among the most powerful for learning long-term dependencies, can retain and utilize information over periods of great duration. LSTMs, unlike regular RNNs, are provided with memory cells and gating systems, enabling them to remember the dynamics of crucial information even when they need to capture a certain long sequence. The mentioned networks have provided the necessary breakthroughs for complex natural language handling operations such as say, sentiment analysis.

Methodology

To conduct our research, we chose to test an array of machine learning tools and algorithms, including persistent Logistic Regression (LR), random forest (RF), support Vector Machine (SVM), naive Bayes (NB) and the long short-term memory (LSTM) in order to determine their performance on Twitter sentiment analysis. Logistic Regression LR can be used as powerful baseline model because it is a simple and interpretable model. It works well on linearly separable data and can enable us to know how the various features contribute towards classification. An ensemble-based model was selected because it is well suited to non-linear relationships and is robust to noise and overfitting, a characteristic of interest in real-world, user-created data, like tweets. Furthermore, Random Forest (RF) was adopted because it is among the very few ML models that do not exclude an unsupported ML model and as shown in “Algorithm survey” may reduce noise and overfitting since it is the only one tested. Data type The Support Vector Machine (SVM) works especially well with high dimensional text data and has a good reputation with natural language processing applications. It is efficient in identifying possible optimum decision boundaries and therefore a good choice on sentiment classification. Naive Bayes (NB), in spite of its high assumption of feature independence is light-weighted, computationally efficient and has been seen to do well in classification of texts dues to the particular distribution of words within documents. Lastly, the deep learning model Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) was added because it can represent the contextual and sequential details of texts. LSTM is able to learn patterns in sequential data and hence fits well in learning a complex sentence structure and context in tweets particularly where there is the use of sarcasm or an implication of sentiment. Combined with a neural network-based procedure, conventional machine learning models give a sufficient comparison in strengthening sentiment analysis. Twitter data retrieval

In the following paragraph, you will learn about the data-retrieving phase where the Tweepy API is used. The process requires a user to obtain keys, consumer keys, and token keys from Twitter. After that, these keys are used to access live Twitter data. The collection of the data has comments and reviews on Twitter’s platform, given a definite time frame. The datasets contains details like timing, the user ID, and the textual description of tweets. The data in this study consisted of around 5200 tweets about products, taken between January 2025 and December 2024 through the Twitter API. The names of the product like iPhone, Samsung, Amazon, Flipkart, laptop and headphones were considered as a selection criteria. The data quality was ensured by excluding the non-English tweets, advertisement, and irrelevant posts. Twitter was selected as a principal source of data as its brief, opinion-based publications represent spontaneous and real-time customer response and are particularly suited to product sentiment analysis.

Data pre-processing

In this subsection, the step-by-step of those well-known techniques needed to clean in addition to prepare the chaotic Twitter information for sentiment analysis are redefined. Preprocessing stage was performed in order to make the textual data ready to model. These were removal of URLs, hashtag, user mentions, emojis, numbers, and special characters. Thecodes were tokenized and lemmatized, and stopwords were eliminated by NLTK stopword corpus. TF-IDF representation used TF-IDF vectorization and GloVe used pretraining GloVe 100-dimensional vectors. These actions guaranteed the reduction of noise, semantics maintained and better model explainability.

Techniques for example tokenization, stop words removal, stemming, and lemmatization are used with the help NLTK (Natural Language Toolkit) module in Python. Comforting with these steps the noise and extraneous details are filtered from the textual data, and we make sure of its quality and precision.

The trade-offs of traditional machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models used in multi-stage sentiment analysis of Twitter product reviews are different. SVM, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest ML algorithms incur less memory and train fast, which makes them appropriate when working with small-to-medium datasets and deployable in real-time. Nevertheless, they have difficulties with representing complex contextual and sequential dependencies in noisy and short-text data. DL models (CNNs, LSTMs, Transformers, etc.) have better accuracy and ability to understand context and can be used to process slangs, emojis, and informal language, however, they require significant computational resources, annotated datasets, and thus need more time to train, which can limit real-time use. An optimized ML in a multi-stage model, an integration of preprocessing, feature extraction, and sentiment classification, is a compromise between computing time and accuracy, that will reduce processing burden and allow performing solid real-time sentiment analysis on Twitter.

Implementation issues

Data-driven sentiment analysis via machine learning

The work started by gathering data on cross-domain reviews in different domains like e-commerce, healthcare, entertainment that ensured cross-sentiment expression. This data was thoroughly selected with publicly published sources and benchmark data sets to provide a set of sentiment types. Labeling of reviews was done manually and semi-automatically whereby lexicon-based systems like VADER and SentiWordNet were first used to label the reviews and after that the same review was verified by human annotator thus increasing quality. Labeling was made homogeneous and trustworthy as a result of inter-annotator agreement which can be measured such as Cohen Kappa.

As model training process, a preprocessing pipeline was structured on the dataset. Tokenization, removal of the word stop, and the creation of word cloud was done to retrieve relevant terms. The TF-IDF vectorization transformed the text data into weighted feature vectors and the WordNet Lemmatizer-based feature selection normalized and shifted the feature and dimensionality reduction. Representation was also enhanced by means of domain feature extraction. Several algorithms were included into the training, such as Logistic Regression (C = 1.0, L2 penalty), SVM (linear kernel, C = 1.0) and Random Forest (n_estimators = 100, max_depth = 10). Moreover, a customized sentiment classification with an Improved VADER Lexicon according to each domain was adopted. In longer experiments, deep learning environments including LSTMs ( Embedding = 100-dim, LSTM units = 128, dropout = 0.2, epochs = 10, batch size = 64 ) were taken into consideration to capture sequential dependencies.

The dataset was randomly divided into the training (70%), validation (15%), and testing (15%) sets and the 5-fold cross-validation was used to guarantee the estimation of the robust performance. Evaluation tools were Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-score, and macro-averaged F1 was to compensate better for class imbalance. The problem of class imbalance, inherent to the imbalance of positive reviews, was addressed with the use of Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) to oversample the minority point of the training set, adjusting the weights of the classes in Logistic Regression and SVM, and applied stratification on the datasets when the training and test sets were split to enforce the even distribution of the classes.

Selection of algorithms came as a result of their strengths. Evolved VADER was chosen because it was easy to comprehend and understand and modify a domain-specific vocabulary. It is worth choosing classical machine learning models (Logistic Regression, SVM) as efficient and effective basic solutions because they provide high accuracy on TF-IDF-transformed texts. Random Forest and Gradient Boosting Machines were used due to the possibility to utilize noisy non-linear data and give a clue on feature importance. The models used were deep learning and LSTM was also to be used as it is more powerful at capturing contextual and sequential relationships at the expense of increased computational resources. One used ensemble methods to pool together the model predictions and again this guaranteed better accuracy and generalization.

Sentiment classifiers are trained using supervised learning algorithms like SVM and Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression, and Decision Trees depending on labelled data. These procedures learn to categorize sentiment (positive, negative, and neutral) from text features they have learned to recognize, such as words and n-grams. Stemming and lemmatization

Stemming simplifies the words to the root word by removing the suffixes (e.g., running --> run) and is hence a low-density and cheap method to run at a general large-scale level. On the other hand, lemmatization is a linguistically more informed step as it operates on word morphological form (e.g. better→ good), hence, it does not fail to maintain linguistic meaning. Using them has a trade-off between linguistic accuracy and computational efficiency.

Vectorization method selection

In this, the tokens that will remain after cleaning are represented by constructing numeric representations of them with the help of one of the two possible forms of representing them as vectors. TF-IDF (Term Frequency Inverse Document Frequency) measures the relative significance of the words in one document to the whole corpus, which suits to tasks that need semantic weighting, Bag of Words (BoW) model offers an easier frequency based representation and is useful in cases where the structure of words is more significant than its contextual meaning.

Experimental results

There are a number of benefits, which the multi-stage methodology proposed on sentiment analysis, brings to the accuracy of sentiment detection on Twitter product reviews as well as its usability. This modularity also enhances the accuracy of the analysis since the methodology is organized into phases or stages of data analysis (preprocessing, feature extraction, classification), so that this step can be narrowed. This includes incorporation of optimized machine learning algorithms Pair-wise machine learning SVM, Random Forest, and LSTM, in combination with the hyperparameters carefully selected, which results in better performance in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score. Incorporation of VADER, a much more specific tool that is oriented on analyzing social media sentiment due to being a lexicon-based sentiment analysis tool, allows the model to be able to detect things that usual sentiment analysis tools cannot, including informal language, emoticons, and slang, and to assign them sentiment information that can be useful to furthering the machine learning predictions. The maps grant a real-time sentiment display created with the help of such tools as Flask and matplotlib, which brings another interactive level to the process so that users can make the right decisions within a short period of time. Moreover, the user authentication action offers the security layer, which guarantees the trusted access to the sentiment insights. The modular design and scalability of the methodology also makes it adaptable to many other spheres of activity, including brand monitoring, crisis management, and political trend analysis.

But there are some limitations to the methodology too. It is somewhat dependent on powerful text preprocessing, the incorrect processing of cleaning, tokenization, working with abbreviations can have a negative effect on the accuracy of the model. Tools of classical machine learning like Logistic Regression and Naive Bayes, although performing effectively, cannot comprehend deeper semantic meaning or identify sarcastic speech that is so often used in tweets. Even though LSTM contributes to solving this more or less, it also requires a high amount of computation power and requires more training time, which seems incompatible with real-time analysis under conditions of shallow infrastructure. The other problem is the requirement of considerable volumes of soundly named data to prepare supervised models, which is tedious and subjective. Although VADER has very good base level sentiment detection, it can be poor in those cases where the sentiment to be detected has to do with product related words or new slang that is not in the lexicon. Finally, Twitter data may be biased as positive or neutral tweets may prevail, and the classification obtained may be inaccurate in case of correction methods being undertaken, such as oversampling or class weighting.

In this application, data was gathered about 10,000 tweets about the “iPhone 15” between July and September 2024.

Step 1: Cleaning the data.

Removed irrelevant parts like mentions (@username), hashtags (#), and URLs.

Converted everything to lowercase and tokenized the text.

Example tweet:

Original: “I love the new iPhone 15! The camera is amazing! #AppleEvent”.

Processed: “Love new iPhone camera amazing”.

Step 2: Converting text to numbers.

TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) was recycled, which helped us represent the tweets as numbers based on the importance of words in the datasets.

Step 3: Model training.

Several machine learning models were trained and found that Logistic Regression and Random Forest performed best. Naïve Bayes didn’t do as well because there were more positive tweets than negative ones, which caused the imbalance.

During the preprocessing step, raw tweets were cleaned by removing stop words, URLs, hashtags, and symbols, and were lemmatized. The extractions of TF-IDF and Word2Vec embeddings were employed to form the textual representations and a comparison was made to identify the representation most effective. Otherwise, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest classifiers were used as baseline models with the default parameters. To improve classification performance, the author employed a two-step classification strategy: in the first stage, a binary-polarised/non-polarised review classifier was used; in the second phase, SVM was deployed to distinguish between positive and negative reviews; and finally a Random Forest was applied to find the distinction between neutral and mixed reviews. The hyperparameter tuning was performed using Grid Search Cross Validation with five folds and targeted to SVM type of kernel, its regularization with C, gamma, and Random Forest level, and number of features sampled. The finite model selection was a setup with SVM RBF kernel (C = 10, gamma = 0.1) as a polarized classifier, and Random Forest (n estimators = 200, max depth = 30) as a neutral/mixed classifier. Moreover, to remove the problem of imbalance between classes in the dataset, Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) technique was used. All the experiments were performed in Python with the usage of scikit-learn, NLTK, and Gensim libraries using your computing environment as a testbed; an i7 processor, 16GB of RAM, and GPU support that could be additionally tested with the help of Keras and TensorFlow, where LSTM was deployed as a comparison baseline deep learning algorithm.

Step 4: Results.

The comparison of models suggests the performance comparisons between various machine learning and deep learning model performance in sentiment classification tasks. Random Forest is one of the classical machine learning models that provided one of the highest accuracy rates (0.87) with high precision (0.84) and recall (0.81), which is a safe bet to use with balanced figures. Almost equal results were obtained in Naive Bayes, as its accuracy was 0.86 and F1-score was slightly higher (0.81), showing its effectiveness in the task of text classification even when discarding complex algorithms. Competitive results were also returned by Logistic Regression (accuracy 0.85, F1-score 0.80), thus proving that it is an appropriate strong baseline model. Support Vector Machine (SVM) was not the best performing though it performed well in high-dimensional space with accuracy and F1-score of 0.83 and 0.77 respectively, maybe because it is sensitive to the parameter tuning and imbalanced data.

Passing to the more advanced approaches, Deep Learning LSTM proved much better than the classical approaches, having an accuracy of 0.90, precision of 0.88, and recall of 0.85, and hence a high F1-score (0.86). This gain indicates the advantage of the LSTM in learning the texture relationships and context specific phrases in text. Last but not the least, the ensemble or hybrid machine learning method showed the highest scores in general, with an excellent accuracy of 0.95, but also precise and recall (0.89 respectively), providing a high F1-score (0.86). This indicates that ensembles of models (i.e. instead of a single model), through methods such as voting or stacking yield much better generalization, reliability, and predictive accuracy than single models.

The enhanced performance is explained by the fact that we gradually added the components of preprocessing, an ensemble machine learning, and optimized deep (LSTM) learning to make the method robust to the short, noisy, and context-conditioned data on Twitter. To take a practical view, this framework can be used by businesses in capturing large-scale social media streams and making appropriate decisions. Scientifically, this work confirms that multi-stage optimisation is superior to single-model optimisation adding to the existing body of research in sentiment analysis.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the efficacy of an optimized multi-staged sentiment analysis mechanism incorporating the machine and deep learning technology to analyze the product related tweets. The method is effective in solving the issues of noisy, short, and context-dependent data on Twitter which allows businesses to obtain actionable information about the customer sentiment. In contrast to the current single-model approaches, the proposed framework will be robust and more accurate in terms of sentiment classification thereby making it useful to a marketing and brand management practice. Going ahead, potential areas of research can be cross-domain generalization, cross-modal sentiment evaluation (emojis, images, and text) and real-time deployment to enhance the relevance of social media analytics in dynamic business circumstances.

Future scope

In the future, research needs to be conducted on more comprehensive yet fluid algorithms which can address multilingual, cross-domain and contextual sentiments. Its combination with state-of-the-art NLP methods, transformers and hybrids of deep learning might greatly enhance precision and context awareness. It could help in decision-making and social change to build real-time sentiment analysis systems and extend the use beyond the marketing (healthcare (patient feedback analysis), politics (public opinion mining), and crisis management (disaster or pandemic response) application fields. Moreover, it is necessary to conduct research on explainable AI (XAI) to enhance interpretability and credibility of models in sensitive applications and fields.

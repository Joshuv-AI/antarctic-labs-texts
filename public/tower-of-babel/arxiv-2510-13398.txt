Selfless inclusions of C ∗ C^{*} -algebras
Ben Hayes, Srivatsav Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, Gregory Patchell, Leonel Robert

Abstract
. We introduce and study a natural notion of selflessness for inclusions of C*-probability spaces, which in particular necessitates all intermediate C*-algebras to be selfless in the sense of [ Rob25 ] . We identify natural sources of selfless inclusions in the realms of 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stable and free product C*-algebras. As an application of this, we prove selflessness for a new family of C*-probability spaces outside the regime of free products and group C*-algebras. These include the reduced free unitary compact quantum groups.



1.
Introduction

We continue the recent program on
selflessness
by extending this notion to inclusions, and by identifying new examples of selfless C*-probability spaces arising from quantum groups. Selfless C*-probability spaces were introduced by the fourth author in
[
Rob25
]
. This framework unravels certain old problems in C*-algebra theory, as demonstrated in the recent work of Amrutam, Gao, Kunnawalkam Elayavalli, and Patchell
[
AGKEP25
]
. Since this paper, rapid progress has been achieved in the field, resulting in the development of several new techniques and applications
[
KES25
,
Vig25
,
HKER25
,
RTV25
,
Oza25
]
. Notably, Ozawa very recently discovered a new approach to proving selflessness,
and used it to resolve several open questions
concerning this notion

[
Oza25
]
.

We introduce and study a natural notion of
selfless inclusions
of C*-probability spaces, extending
[
Rob25
]
.
We call an inclusion of C*-probability spaces
B\subset(A,\rho)
selfless if there exist a free ultrafilter
\omega
and a C*-probability space
(C,\kappa)
, with
C\neq\mathbb{C}
, such that the first factor embedding
(B\subset A)\to(B*C\subset A*C)
is existential (Definition
3.3
).
Roughly put, while the notion of selfless C*-probability space asserts the existence of nontrivial elements in an ultrapower
A^{\omega}
that are free from
A
in a nondegenerate way, the notion of selfless inclusion additionally keeps track of a C*-subalgebra
B
of
A
from which these freely independent elements are sourced.

If an inclusion
B\subset(A,\rho)
is selfless, then all the intermediate C*-subalgebras
B\subset C\subset A
are selfless. This gives a way of
identifying selfless C*-algebras by realizing them
as intermediate C*-subalgebras of a selfless inclusion.
Note also that, since selfless C*-algebras are simple, every intermediate C*-subalgebra of a selfless inclusion
is simple. Thus, selfless inclusions are C*-irreducible in the sense investigated by Rørdam in
[
Rør23
]
.

We prove several results on selfless inclusions that closely parallel results from
[
Rob25
]
on selfless C*-probability spaces.
We also show that some of Ozawa’s methods from
[
Oza25
]
can be straightforwardly adapted to prove selflessness for purely infinite and for
\mathcal{Z}
-stable inclusions in the sense of Sarkowicz
[
Sar25
]
;
see Theorems
3.6
and
3.8
.

The work
[
HKER25
]
developed a theory of rapid decay for filtrations in C*-probability spaces and combined it with von Neumann algebraic techniques to prove selflessness for large families of reduced free product C*-algebras, expanding significantly on the strategy of
[
AGKEP25
]
(inspired by
[
LM25
]
). A key input in
[
HKER25
]
is Popa’s free independence theorem for arbitrary separable II
1
factors
[
Pop95
]
. Following up on
[
HKER25
]
, we refine and upgrade the machinery therein by using the more general version of Popa’s theorem, which asserts the existence of freely independent elements supported on irreducible subfactors. This allows us to find a family of selfless inclusions of C*-probability spaces, which in particular implies that all intermediate subalgebras are selfless.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
(A,\tau)
be a tracial C*-probability space and
X\subseteq A
a finite generating set such that

X^{*}X\not\subseteq\mathbb{C}1
. Let
\mathcal{P}A
denote the projective version of
A
relative to
(A,\tau,X)
. Suppose that

(1)

A
has rapid decay relative to a filtration containing the generating set
X
,

(2)

with
M=A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B(L^{2}(A,\rho))
and
N=\mathcal{P}A^{\prime\prime}\subseteq M
, either one of the following two cases holds:

Case I: the inclusion
N\subseteq M
is an irreducible inclusion of II
1
factors;

Case II:
A^{\prime}\cap(\mathcal{P}A)^{\omega}\subseteq A^{\omega}
contains a diffuse subalgebra with respect to
\tau^{\omega}
.

Then the inclusion
C^{*}(\mathcal{P}A,z^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{X^{*}}Az)\subset A*C(\mathbb{T})
is selfless.

Using this result in combination with Vergnioux’s result on rapid decay
[
Ver07
]
and Banica’s work on free complexifications
[
Ban08
]
, we prove the following theorem, which yields new examples of selfless C*-probability spaces that are in spirit
neither
group C*-algebras nor free products.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.7
)
.

The (reduced) free unitary compact quantum group
A_{u}(n)
is selfless for
n\geq 2
.

By
[
Rob25
, Theorem 3.1]
(see also
[
BS16
,
FHL+21
]
), this yields the following corollary:

Corollary 1.3
.

For
n\geq 2
,
A_{u}(n)
has stable rank one and strict comparison of positive elements by its unique trace.

We now briefly explain our strategy for proving Theorem
1.1
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{X}A
denote the projective version of a C*-algebra
A
with respect to the generating sets
X
(see Subsection
2.2
). To prove Theorem
1.2
we exploit the fact, established by Banica
[
Ban97
]
, that
A_{u}(n)
can be obtained as a free complexification of the free orthogonal compact quantum group
A_{o}(n)
. Therefore
A_{u}(n)
is an intermediate C*-subalgebra of
A_{o}(n)*C(\mathbb{T})
and
C^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{X}(A_{o}(n)),z^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{X^{*}}(A_{o}(n))z)
, where
z
is the Haar unitary generator of
C(\mathbb{T})
. Combining the fact that
\mathcal{P}_{X}(A_{o}(n))
is an irreducible subalgebra of
A_{o}(n)
(at the level of their GNS completions) with the rapid decay property for
A_{o}(n)

[
Ver07
]
,
we construct
a sequence of unitaries in
C^{*}(\mathcal{P}_{X}(A_{o}(n)),z^{-1}\mathcal{P}_{X^{*}}(A_{o}(n))z)
which is asymptotically freely independent from
A_{o}(n)*C(\mathbb{T})
. This implies that the inclusion is selfless, and in particular
A_{u}(n)
is selfless.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
A_{o}(n)
and
A_{u}(n)

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
n\geq 2
.

The reduced free unitary compact quantum group is obtained as follows: start from the universal C*-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
generated
by elements
\{u_{i,j}:i,j=1,\ldots,n\}
such that, with
u=(u_{ij})_{ij}
, the relations

u^{*}u=uu^{*}=1_{n},\quad(u^{t})^{*}(u^{t})=u^{t}(u^{t})^{*}=1_{n}

are satisfied (where
u^{t}
is the transpose of
u
).
This C*-algebra comes equipped with a comultiplication
\Delta:\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}\otimes_{\min}\mathfrak{A}
defined by
\Delta(u_{ij})=\sum_{k}u_{ik}\otimes u_{kj}
. It also comes with a unique Haar state
\tau
which is tracial and invariant under
\Delta
; i.e., for all
a\in\mathfrak{A}
,
(\tau\otimes\iota)\Delta(a)=(\iota\otimes\tau)\Delta(a)=\tau(a)
(see
[
Wor87
,
BC07
]
). Take the GNS representation of this universal C*-algebra with respect to the Haar trace. The resulting C*-algebra is the reduced free unitary compact quantum group. We denote it by
A_{u}(n)
.

Similarly, the reduced free orthogonal compact quantum group, is obtained starting from the universal C*-algebra generated by elements
\{v_{ij}\}_{i,j=1}^{n}
satisfying

v^{*}v=vv^{*}=1_{n},\qquad v_{ij}^{*}=v_{ij}\ \text{ for all }i,j,

with
v=(v_{ij})
(which again comes equipped with the comultiplication
\Delta(u_{ij})=\sum_{k}u_{ik}\otimes u_{kj}
) and passing to the GNS representation with respect to its (tracial) Haar state. We denote the
the reduced free orthogonal compact quantum group
by
A_{o}(n)
.

2.2.
Free complexifications

Here we discuss the operation of free complexification, introduced by Banica
[
Ban08
]
(also studied in
[
Rau12
]
,
[
TW17
]
). Note that, unlike
[
Ban08
]
, we work with reduced free products rather than universal ones.

Let
(A,\rho)
be a C*-probability space. Let
X\subseteq A
be a set generating
A
as a unital C*-algebra; i.e., such that

A=C^{*}(X,1)
.
Form the reduced free product
(A,\rho)*(C(\mathbb{T}),\lambda)
(where
\lambda
denotes the state induced by the Lebesgue measure).
Let

Xz:=\{xz:x\in X\}\subseteq A*C(\mathbb{T}),

where
z
is the canonical generator of
C(\mathbb{T})
. We call

\widetilde{A}=C^{*}(1,Xz)\subseteq A*C(\mathbb{T})

the free complexification of
(A,\rho,X)
. We endow
\widetilde{A}
with the state
(\rho*\lambda)\big|_{\widetilde{A}}
and the generating set
Xz
.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}A\subseteq A
the C*-subalgebra generated by
\{1\}

and

XX^{*}=\{xy^{*}:x,y\in X\}.

We call
(\mathcal{P}A,XX^{*})
the projective version
of
(A,X)
(see
[
Ban22
]
).

Note that, although not explicitly reflected in our notation, the projective version
\mathcal{P}A
depends on the generating set
X
, and the free complexification
\widetilde{A}
depends
both on
\rho
and
X
.

Below, we will also make reference to the C*-subalgebra generated by
\{1\}

and
X^{*}X
; i.e., the projective version of
(A,X^{*})
. We will denote this C*-algebra by
\mathcal{P}_{X^{*}}A
.

One checks easily that
XX^{*}\subseteq\widetilde{A}
and
z^{-1}X^{*}Xz\subseteq\widetilde{A}
.
Thus,

C^{*}(\mathcal{P}A,z^{-1}(\mathcal{P}_{X^{*}}A)z)\subseteq\widetilde{A}.

Banica showed
[
Ban97
]
(see also
[
Ban08
, Theorem 3.4]
), that
A_{u}(n)\cong\widetilde{A_{o}(n)}
,
for
n\geq 2
. More concretely,
A_{u}(n)
is isomorphic to the free complexification of
A_{o}(n)
, relative to the Haar state and generating set given by the entries of
v=(v_{i,j})_{ij}
.
For
n=2
, Banica showed in
[
Ban97
]
that
A_{u}(2)
can be alternatively obtained as the free complexification of
(C(SU(2)),\lambda,\{v_{ij}\}_{ij})
, where in this case
\lambda
is given by the Haar measure on
SU(2)
and

v\in M_{2}(SU(2))
is the fundamental representation of
SU(2)
; i.e., the inclusion map
SU(2)\to M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
.

For further examples of compact quantum groups obtained as free complexifications see
[
Ban08
, Theorem 3.4]
,
[
Rau12
]
. There is also a construction of free
d
-complexification where the reduced free product with

C(\mathbb{T})
is replaced with a reduced free product with
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. Examples of compact quantum groups obtained as free
d
-complexifications are obtained in

[
TW17
, Theorem 6.16]
.

2.3.
Filtrations and rapid decay

Let
A
be a unital C*-algebra. By a filtration on
A
we understand an increasing sequence
of vector subspaces
(V_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
such that
V_{0}=\mathbb{C}1
,
V_{n}^{*}=V_{n}
for all
n
, and
V_{m}V_{n}\subseteq V_{m+n}

for all
m,n
(
[
HKER25
]
).

Let
(A,\rho)
be a C*-probability space and
(V_{n})_{n}
a filtration on
A
. We say that
(A,\rho)

has rapid decay relative to
(V_{n})_{n}
if there exist constants
C>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\|x\|\leq C(1+n)^{\alpha}\|x\|_{2}\hbox{ for all }x\in V_{n}\hbox{ and all }n,

where
\|x\|_{2}:=\tau(x^{*}x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(A,\rho)
be a C*-probability space where
\rho
has faithful GNS representation, and let

(V_{n})_{n}
be a filtration on
A
. Let
(B,\tau)
be another
C*-probability space (we do not assume faithful GNS representation in this case).
Let
\phi\colon\bigcup_{n}V_{n}\to B
be a *-homomorphism such that

(a)

\tau\phi=\rho
,

(b)

there exist subspaces
(W_{n})_{n}
of
B
and constants
C,\alpha>0
such that
\phi(V_{n})\subseteq W_{n}
for all
n

and

\|y\|\leq C(1+n)^{\alpha}\|y\|_{2}\hbox{ for all }y\in W_{n}\hbox{ and }n.

Then
\phi
extends to a C*-algebra embedding
A
in
B
.

Proof.

Since
\bigcup_{n}V_{n}
is a *-subalgebra, property (a) implies that
\phi
is an
L^{2}
-
L^{2}
isometry. Indeed, for
x\in\bigcup_{n}V_{n}
,

\|\phi(x)\|_{2}^{2}=\tau(\phi(x)^{*}\phi(x))=\tau(\phi(x^{*}x))=\rho(x^{*}x)=\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

Let
x\in V_{m}
. Then
(x^{*}x)^{l}\in V_{2ml}
. Using that
\phi
is a *-homomorphism and property (b) of
\phi
,
we have that

\displaystyle\|\phi(x)\|^{2l}

\displaystyle=\|\phi((x^{*}x)^{l})\|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+2ml)^{\alpha}\|\phi((x^{*}x)^{l})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=C(1+2ml)^{\alpha}\|(x^{*}x)^{l}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+2ml)^{\alpha}\|x\|^{2l}.

Hence,

\|\phi(x)\|\leq C^{\frac{1}{2l}}(1+2ml)^{\frac{\alpha}{2l}}\|x\|.

Letting
l\to\infty
we get
\|\phi(x)\|\leq x
for all
x\in\bigcup_{n}V_{n}
. Thus,
\phi
extends
to a *-homomorphism from
A
to
B
, and we easily see that its extension continues to be an
L^{2}
-
L^{2}
isometry.
Since
\rho
has faithful GNS representation, the extension of
\phi
to
A
must be an embedding.
∎

Given a family of C*-probability spaces each endowed
with a filtration, we wish to define
a filtration on their reduced free product. Before doing so,
we introduce a convenient terminology that will be used throughout the paper.
For a C*-probability space
(A,\rho)
and a tuple of subsets
(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{m})

of
A
, we call
w\in A
an alternating word in
(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{m})
if

w=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{l}

where
a_{i}\in S_{j}(i)
for all
i
and
j\colon\{1,\ldots,l\}\to\{1,\ldots,m\}

is an alternating function; i.e.,
j(i)\neq j(i+1)
for all
1\leq i\leq l-1
.
If in addition
\rho(a_{i})=0
for all
i
, we call
a
an alternating centered word
in
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{m})
.

Now let
(A_{k},\rho_{k})
, with
k=1,\ldots,m
, be C*-probability spaces, each endowed with a filtration

(V_{k,n})_{n}
. Form their reduced free product
(A,\rho)=*_{k=1}^{m}(A_{k},\rho_{k})
, and identify each
A_{k}

as a C*-subalgebra of
A
. From the
construction of the reduced free product, we have that
A
is spanned by
1
and the alternating
centered words in
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m})
. Moreover, if
w
is such an alternating centered word, then its
representation as an alternating centered product is unique. The number of factors in this representation
is called the length of the word. Let
W_{n,l}\subseteq A
denote the span of the alternating centered words
in the tuple
(V_{1,n},V_{2,n},\ldots,V_{m,n})
of length
l\geq 1
, and set
W_{n,0}=\mathbb{C}1
. Then
the vector subspaces

W_{n}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}W_{n,l}

form a filtration of
A
by
[
HKER25
, Lemma 4.3]
. We refer to it as the
free product filtration
in the reduced free product.
By
[
HKER25
, Theorem 4.5]
, if
(A_{k},\rho_{k})
has rapid decay
relative to
(V_{k,n})_{n}
for all
k=1,\ldots,m
, then so does
(A,\rho)
relative to
(W_{n})_{n}
.
(Note: One can, alternatively, obtain a filtration on the reduced free product by considering
the subspaces
W_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq A
spanned by all alternating centered words in
(V_{1,n_{1}},V_{2,n_{2}},\ldots,V_{m,n_{m}})
, where

(n_{1},\ldots,n_{m})
runs through all
m
-tuples such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}n_{i}\leq n
. Note that
W_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq W_{n}
for all
n
.
Either construction will suit our arguments below, but for definiteness we will use the subspaces
(W_{n})_{n}
defined above.)

3.
Selfless Inclusions

We work in the setting of C*-probability spaces
(A,\rho)
; i.e., unital C*-algebras endowed with a distinguished state.
We always assume the state induces a faithful GNS representation, with the exception of ultrapower states. Morphisms between C*-probability spaces are unital *-homomorphisms preserving the respective states.
We often omit reference to the states in the notation, even though all free products below are the reduced free products with respect to the given states.

By an inclusion of C*-probability spaces we understand a pair of C*-probability spaces
(A,\rho)
and
(B,\rho|_{B})
, where
B
is a unital C*-subalgebra of
A
and the state on
B
is the restriction of the state on
A
. To denote it, we write
B\subset A
or
B\subset(A,\rho)
. By an embedding of
B_{1}\subset A_{1}
in
B_{2}\subset A_{2}
we understand a unital embedding of C*-algebras
\theta\colon A_{1}\to A_{2}
that is state preserving; i.e.
\rho_{2}\theta=\rho_{1}
, and such that
\theta(B_{1})\subseteq B_{2}
.

We call an embedding of inclusions
\theta\colon(B_{1}\subset(A_{1},\rho_{1}))\to(B_{2}\subset(A_{2},\rho_{2}))
existential if there exists an ultrafilter
\omega
and an embedding

\sigma\colon(B_{2}\subset(A_{2},\rho_{2}))\to(B_{1}^{\omega}\subset(A_{1}^{\omega},\rho_{1}^{\omega}))

such that

\sigma\circ\theta
agrees with the diagonal inclusion of
A_{1}
in
A_{1}^{\omega}
. Equivalently, there exists a C*-algebra
embedding
\sigma\colon A_{2}\to A_{1}^{\omega}
such that
\sigma
is state preserving,
\sigma\circ\theta
agrees with the diagonal inclusion, and

\sigma(B_{2})\subseteq B_{1}^{\omega}
. Note that if
\theta
is a existential then

\theta\colon A_{1}\to A_{2}
and
\theta|_{B_{1}}\colon B_{1}\to B_{2}
are both existential embeddings of C*-probability spaces. The following lemma is elementary.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
be embeddings between inclusions of C*-probability spaces such that
\theta_{2}\circ\theta_{1}
is well-defined.

(1)

If
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are existential, then
\theta_{2}\circ\theta_{1}
is also existential.

(2)

If
\theta_{2}\circ\theta_{1}
is existential, then so is
\theta_{1}
.

We also have the following corollary of “free exactness” (c.f.
[
Rob25
, Corollary 1.9]
).

Theorem 3.2
.

A reduced free product of existential embeddings of inclusions is again existential. That is, if
(B_{i}\subset A_{i})\to(D_{i}\subset C_{i})
are existential for
i=1,2
, then
(B_{1}*B_{2}\subset A_{1}*A_{2})\to(D_{1}*D_{2}\subset C_{1}*C_{2})
is also existential.

Definition 3.3
.

We call an inclusion of C*-probability spaces
B\subset(A,\rho)

selfless if there exist a free ultrafilter
\omega
and a C*-probability space
(C,\kappa)
, with
C\neq\mathbb{C}
, such that the first factor embedding

\theta\colon(B\subset A)\to(B*C\subset A*C)

is existential.
More concretely,
B\subset A
is selfless if there exist an ultrafilter
\omega
, a C*-probability space
(C,\kappa)
with
C\neq\mathbb{C}
, and a state-preserving embedding of C*-algebras

\sigma\colon A*C\to A^{\omega}
, such that
\sigma\circ\theta
agrees with the diagonal inclusion and

\sigma(B*C)\subseteq B^{\omega}
(equivalently,
\sigma(C)\subseteq B^{\omega}
).

Notice that
(A,\rho)
is selfless if and only if
A\subset(A,\rho)
is selfless, and that if
B\subset(A,\rho)
is a selfless inclusion, then both
(A,\rho)
and
(B,\rho|_{B})
are selfless C*-probability spaces.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\phi\colon(B_{1}\subset A_{1})\to(B_{2}\subset A_{2})
be an existential embedding of inclusions and suppose that
B_{2}\subset A_{2}

is selfless. Then so is
B_{1}\subset A_{1}
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exist
C\neq\mathbb{C}
and
\theta\colon(B_{2}\subset A_{2})\to(B_{2}*C\subset A_{2}*C)
that is existential. Thus, the embedding
\theta\circ\phi
from
(B_{1}\subset A_{1})
to
(B_{2}*C\subset A_{2}*C)
is existential. Since
\theta\circ\phi
ranges in
B_{1}*C\subset A_{1}*C
, its corestriction to
B_{1}*C\subset A_{1}*C
is also existential. This shows that
B_{1}\subset A_{1}
is selfless.
∎

In the following theorem we sometimes omit reference to the state, but the reader should bear in mind that we work in
the category of C*-probability spaces and state preserving unital *-homomorphisms.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
B\subset(A,\rho)
be a selfless inclusion of C*-probability spaces. The following are true:

(1)

If
B\subset C\subset A
, then
B\subset C
and
C\subset A
are selfless. In particular,
C
is selfless.

(2)

For any C*-probability space
D
the inclusion
B*D\hookrightarrow A*D
is selfless. More generally, if

\phi\colon D_{1}\subset D_{2}
is an embedding (of C*-probability spaces),
then
B*D_{1}\subset A*D_{2}
is a selfless inclusion.

(3)

For every C*-probability space
(D,\delta)
the first factor inclusion
A\subset A*D
is selfless.

(4)

The C*-probability space
(C,\kappa)
witnessing selflessness of the inclusion
B\subset(A,\rho)

in Definition
3.3
may be chosen to be
(C_{r}^{*}(\mathbb{F}_{\infty}),\tau)
.

(5)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the inclusion
M_{n}(B)\subset(M_{n}(A),\rho\otimes\mathrm{tr})
is selfless.

(6)

The inclusion
B\subset A
has the relative Dixmier property with respect to
\rho
; i.e.,

\rho(a)1\in\overline{\operatorname{co}}\{uau^{*}:u\in U(B)\}
for all
a\in A
(also in a uniform sense).

(7)

Assume that
\rho
is faithful.
Then
\rho
can be
B
-excised in the following sense: there exists a net
(b_{\lambda})_{\lambda}

in
B
of positive elements of norm 1 such that

\|b_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}ab_{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho(a)b_{\lambda}\|\to 0
and
\rho(ab_{\lambda}a^{*})\to 0
for all
a\in A
.

Proof.

(i) This is straightforward.

(ii) Since
B\subset A
is selfless,

\theta\colon(B\subset A)\to(B*C\subset A*C)
is existential for some nontrivial
C
. Since the reduced free product of
existential embeddings (of inclusions) is again existential (Theorem
3.2
), and the identity embedding
(D_{1}\subset D_{2})\to(D_{1}\subset D_{2})
is trivially existential,

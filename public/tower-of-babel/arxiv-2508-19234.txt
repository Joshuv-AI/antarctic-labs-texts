A New Inexact Manifold Proximal Linear Algorithm with Adaptive Stopping Criteria
Zhong Zheng, Xin Yu, Shiqian Ma, Lingzhou Xue

Abstract
This paper studies the nonsmooth and nonconvex composite optimization on Riemannian manifolds. We propose a new inexact manifold proximal linear algorithm (IManPL) that incorporates both high- and low-accuracy conditions. At each iteration, IManPL solves convex subproblems on tangent spaces inexactly, guided by two adaptive stopping criteria. We establish convergence guarantees and show that IManPL achieves the best iteration complexity for solving the nonsmooth manifold composite optimization. Numerical experiments on the sparse spectral clustering (SSC) and the sparse principal component analysis (SPCA) demonstrate that our methods outperform existing approaches, including the manifold proximal linear algorithm and manifold proximal gradient algorithm.

Sparse principal component analysis (SPCA).
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) proposed by Pearson
[
19
]
is one of the most fundamental statistical tools
for analyzing high-dimensional data. SPCA seeks principal components with very few nonzero components. For given data matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}\times N}
and
u>0
, SPCA seeks the leading
r
(
r\leq\min\{N_{1},N\}
) sparse
loading vectors and can be formulated as

\min_{U\in\mbox{St}(N,r)}-\|AU\|_{F}^{2}+u\|\mbox{vec}(U)\|_{1}

(2)

Here,
\mbox{St}(N,r):=\{U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}:U^{\top}U=I_{r}\}
represents the Stiefel manifold. The operator
\mbox{vec}(\cdot)
stacks the columns of
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
into a single column vector of size
mn\times 1
. This is an important example of the special situation, where
c(\cdot)
is the identity mapping.

Sparse spectral clustering (SSC)
. Spectral clustering
[
20
]
is a graph-based clustering method in unsupervised learning. Given
N
data points and a nonnegative symmetric similarity matrix
\hat{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
, SSC forms a normalized Laplacian matrix
S:=I_{N}-D^{-1/2}\hat{S}D^{-1/2}
where
D:=\mbox{diag}(d_{1},d_{2}\ldots d_{N})
, in which
d_{i}
represents the sum of the elements in the
i-
th row of
\hat{S},i=1,2\ldots N
. In
[
12
]
, the main step of SSC is to solve the following optimization problem:

\min_{U\in\mbox{St}(N,r)}\left\langle U,SU\right\rangle+u\|\mbox{vec}(UU^{\top})\|_{1}.

(3)

Here,
u>0
is a positive hyperparameter. The solution of (
3
) is passed to the K-Means algorithm for the final clustering. SSC is an important example of the most general situation that
c(\cdot)
is a nonlinear mapping.

In this paper, we focus on the proximal linear methods. For any

[

[

Next, we discuss the manifold proximal linear algorithm (ManPL)
[
12

\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}

\mathrm{Retr}_{z}:\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}

\tilde{z}_{k+1}\approx\argmin_{z\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}}F_{t_{k}}(z;z_{k}),t_{k}>0,

(4)

on the tangent space, and retracted the intermediate solution
\tilde{z}_{k+1}
to the manifold:

z_{k+1}=\mathrm{Retr}_{z_{k}}(\alpha_{k}(\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k})),\alpha_{k}\in(0,1].

(5)

Here,
t_{k}
is the step size, and the shrinkage factor
\alpha_{k}
is determined by Armijo backtracking line search.
[
12
]
proved that ManPL finds an
\epsilon-
stationary point with
O(1/\epsilon^{2})
outer iterations when the subproblems (
4
) are solved exactly. However, exact solutions to the subproblem (
4
) are unavailable in practice, and
[
12
]
used predetermined stopping conditions in their numerical implementations without discussing the convergence or total subproblem iteration complexities. Moreover,
[
12
]
used the proximal point algorithm along with the adaptive regularized semi-smooth Newton’s algorithm
[
47
]
to solve the subproblems (
4
), which is inefficient due to the high cost of solving linear systems when the strong convexity does not hold. As a result, when applied to the SSC problem in numerical experiments, ManPL takes more CPU time compared to candidate algorithms on relaxed optimization problems (see Tables 3 and 4 in
[
12
]
for more details).

1.2
Our Methods and Contributions

Now, we introduce our inexact manifold proximal linear algorithm (IManPL). In the iteration
k
, it also conducts (
4
) and (
5
). For (
4
), IManPL solves the subproblem to find an inexact solution
\tilde{z}_{k+1}
such that

\tilde{z}_{k+1}\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}

(6)

using one of the following inexact termination conditions:

F_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})-F_{t_{k}}(S_{t_{k}}(z_{k});z_{k})\leq\begin{cases}\rho_{l}\left(F(z_{k})-F_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})\right),\rho_{l}>0,&\mbox{under (LACC),}\\
\frac{\rho_{h}}{2t_{k}}\|\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k}\|_{2}^{2},\rho_{h}\in(0,1/4),&\mbox{under (HACC)}.\end{cases}

(7)

Here,
S_{t}(y):=\argmin_{z\in\mathrm{T}_{y}\mathcal{M}+y}F_{t}(z;y),

\rho_{l}>0
and
\rho_{h}\in(0,1/4)
are hyperparameters, and we call the first option low accuracy conditions (LACC) and the second option high accuracy conditions (HACC). We name the conditions as discussed because (HACC) indicates (LACC) for some specific choices of
\rho_{l},\rho_{h}
(see Lemma
4
later for details). Both options require that
\tilde{z}_{k+1}\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}
, i.e., the difference for the update lies in the tangent space. These conditions are motivated by the adaptive conditions in
[
43
,
44
]
for the Euclidean space. For the retraction step (
5
), denoting

c_{0}:=\begin{cases}1+1/(\sqrt{1+\rho_{l}}+\sqrt{\rho_{l}})^{2},&\mbox{using
(LACC)},\\
1+1/\left(\sqrt{1+\rho_{h}/(1-2\sqrt{\rho_{h}})}+\sqrt{\rho_{h}/(1-2\sqrt{\rho_{h}})}\right)^{2},&\mbox{using
(HACC)},\end{cases}

we use Armijo backtracking line search and let
\alpha_{k}
be the largest value in
\{2^{-s}:s\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that the following two conditions hold:

F(z_{k})-F(z_{k+1})\geq\frac{c_{0}\alpha_{k}}{4t_{k}}\|z_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k+1}\|_{2}^{2},

(8)

\frac{1}{2}\left(F(z_{k})+F(z_{k}+\alpha_{k}(\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k});z_{k})\right)-F(z_{k+1})\geq 0.

(9)

Algorithm
1
provides our method IManPL for solving (
1
). It is only a prototype as we haven’t provided details on the subproblem solver for (
4
) or guarantees on reaching (
7
)-(LACC) or (

Preliminaries, Notations, and Assumptions

We begin by introducing the notation and some concepts in Riemannian optimization
[
50
,
51
]
.
Let
\langle\,\!\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
denote the standard inner product and its induced norm on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, respectively.

\mathcal{M}
is a Riemannian manifold embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}
denote the tangent space to
\mathcal{M}
at
z\in\mathcal{M}
.
Throughout this paper, the Riemannian metric on
\mathcal{M}
is induced from the standard Euclidean product. The Riemannian gradient of the smooth function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
at a point
z\in\mathcal{M}
is given by

\mathrm{grad}\,f(z)=\text{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f(z))
, where
\nabla f(z)
is the Euclidean gradient of
f
at
z
and
\text{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
is the Euclidean projection operator onto
\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}
.
A retraction at
z\in\mathcal{M}
is a smooth mapping
\mathrm{Retr}_{z}:\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
satisfying (i)
\mathrm{Retr}_{z}(\mathbf{0}_{z})=z
, where
\mathbf{0}_{z}
is the zero element in
\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}
; (ii)
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Retr}_{z}(tv)|_{t=0}=v
for all
v\in\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}
.

Next, we introduce the assumptions related to the problem (
1
).

Assumption 1
.

(a)

f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth mapping and there exists
L_{f}>0
such that

|\nabla_{z}f(z)-\nabla_{z}f(z^{\prime})|\leq L_{f}\|z-z^{\prime}\|_{2},\quad\forall z,z{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(b)

h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a convex and continuous mapping, and there exist
L_{h}>0
such that

|h(y)-h(y^{\prime})|\leq L_{h}\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{2},\quad\forall y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

(c)

c:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a smooth mapping, and there exist
L_{c}>0
such that,

\|\nabla c(z)-\nabla c(z^{\prime})\|_{2}\leq L_{c}\|z-z^{\prime}\|_{2},\quad\forall z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(d)

\mathcal{M}
is compact. Moreover, there exist positive constants
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
such that

\|\mathrm{Retr}_{z}(\xi)-z\|_{2}\leq M_{1}\|\xi\|_{2},\|\mathrm{Retr}(\xi)-(z+\xi)\|_{2}\leq M_{2}\|\xi\|_{2}^{2},\forall z\in\mathcal{M},\xi\in\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}.

Later on, we also use the notations

L=L_{f}+L_{h}L_{c},G_{F}=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla_{z}f(z)\|_{2}+L_{h}\sup_{z\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla c(z)\|_{2}.

(a), (b) and (d) of Assumption
1
ensure that
G_{F}<\infty
.

1
) and the
\epsilon-
stationary solution for Algorithm

.

.

A point

) if it satisfies the following first-order condition:

0\in\Proj_{\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}}\left(\nabla_{z}f(z)+\nabla c(z)^{\top}\partial h(c(z))\right).

(10)

Definition 2
.

For Algorithm
1
with
t_{k}=t,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\epsilon>0
, we call
z_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
an
\epsilon-
stationary point if

\left\|\frac{z_{k}-S_{t}(z_{k})}{t}\right\|_{2}\leq\epsilon.

Definition
1
is used in
[
12
]
and
[
38
]
, and Definition
2
is used in
[
12
]
. Lemma B.5 in
[
12
]
shows that if
z_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
and
z_{k}=S_{t}(z_{k})
for some

, then
z_{k}
is a stationary point. In Definition
2
, despite Algorithm

solving the subproblems (

) inexactly, we still define

3.1
Technical Lemmas

Lemma 1

(Weak Convexity, Lemma 3.2 in
[
45
]
)
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

|f(y)+\nabla f(y)^{\top}(z-y)-f(z)|\leq\frac{L_{f}}{2}\|z-y\|_{2}^{2},\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(11)

|h(c(y)+\nabla c(y)(z-y))-h(z)|\leq\frac{L_{h}L_{c}}{2}\|z-y\|_{2}^{2},\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(12)

|F(z;y)-F(z)|\leq\frac{L}{2}\|z-y\|_{2}^{2},\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(13)

F_{t}(z;y)\geq F(z),\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n},0<t\leq L^{-1}.

(14)

Lemma 2
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. For any
y\in\mathcal{M},z\in\mathrm{T}_{y}\mathcal{M}+y
and
t>0
, we have

F_{t}(z;y)-F_{t}(S_{t}(y);y)\geq\frac{1}{2t}\|z-S_{t}(y)\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof.

This holds from the
1/t-
strong convexity of
F_{t}(\cdot;y)
on
\mathrm{T}_{y}\mathcal{M}+y
.
∎

Next, we discuss the inexactness of solving (
4
). We use

\varepsilon_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})=F_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})-F_{t_{k}}(S_{t_{k}}(z_{k});z_{k})

to measure the subproblem accuracy and prove the following result.

Lemma 3
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. For any
z_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
,
\tilde{z}_{k+1}\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}
,
t_{k}>0
and
\rho\in(0,1)
, the following two relationships hold:

\frac{1}{2t_{k}}\|z_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2t_{k}}\frac{\|z_{k}-S_{t_{k}}(z_{k})\|_{2}^{2}}{\rho}+\frac{\varepsilon_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})}{1-\rho},

(15)

\frac{1}{2t_{k}}\|z_{k}-S_{t_{k}}(z_{k})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2t_{k}}\frac{\|z_{k}-\tilde{z}_{k+1}\|_{2}^{2}}{\rho}+\frac{\varepsilon_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})}{1-\rho}.

(16)

Here, the values of
t_{k},z_{k},\tilde{z}_{k+1}
are
not
restricted by Algorithm
1
.

Proof.

These two relationships follow from Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and the fact that
\varepsilon_{t_{k}}(\tilde{z}_{k+1};z_{k})\geq\frac{1}{2t_{k}}\|\tilde{z}_{k+1}-S_{t_{k}}(z_{k})\|_{2}^{2}
that follows from Lemma
2
.
∎

.

\tilde{z}_{k+1}\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}

are

.

Proof.

Under (

Proof.

This indicates that

. From the convexity of

F(z_{k};z_{k})-F(\hat{z}_{k+1};z_{k})\geq\frac{\hat{\alpha}_{k}}{2{t_{k}}(1+\rho_{l})}\|z_{k}-S_{t_{k}}(z_{k})\|_{2}^{2}.

We also know that

(24)

\frac{1}{2}\left(F(z_{k})+F(\hat{z}_{k+1};z_{k})\right)-F(\overline{z}_{k+1})\geq 0.

(25)

(

3.3
Convergence to a Stationary Point

Next, we show that
\{z_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
generated by Algorithm
1
finds stationary points.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that Assumption
1
holds. For Algorithm
1
with
t_{k}=t>0,\forall k\in\mathbb{N}
and IT being either (LACC) or (HACC). We have that the accumulation points of
\{z_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
exist, and any such accumulation point is a stationary point of (
1
).

Proof.

Assumption
1
implies that
\mathcal{M}
is complete and
F_{\star}>-\inf
. Thus, Lemma
5
and the fact that
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(F(z_{k})-F(z_{k+1})\leq F_{0}-F_{\star}\right)
indicate
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|z_{k}-S_{t}(z_{k})\|_{2}=0
.
Following the idea of
[
45
]
, we prove the convergence using the Moreau envelope. We denote

\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k})=\argmin_{z\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}}F(z)+\frac{1}{2t^{\prime}}\|z-z_{k}\|_{2}^{2},t^{\prime}=t/(1+tL).

Lemma 4.3 and Theorem 4.5 in
[
45
]
indicate that

\left\|\frac{z_{k}-S_{t}(z_{k})}{t}\right\|_{2}=\Theta\left(\left\|\frac{z_{k}-\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k})}{t^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}\right),\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(26)

where
\Theta
hides positive constant factors related to
t
and
L
. Knowing that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{z_{k}-S_{t}(z_{k})}{t}\right\|_{2}=0
, we have that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\frac{z_{k}-\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k})}{t^{\prime}}\right\|_{2}=0.

(27)

In addition, according to Assumption
1
and Theorem 3.1 in
[
45
]
,
\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\partial F(z)=\nabla f(z)+[\nabla c(z)]^{\top}\partial h(c(z)),

and the first-order optimality condition for
\min_{z\in\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}+z_{k}}F(z)+\frac{1}{2t^{\prime}}\|z-z_{k}\|_{2}^{2}
at
\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k})
indicates that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, there exists
\xi_{k}\in[\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}]^{\perp}
such that

\frac{1}{t^{\prime}}(z_{k}-\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k}))+\xi_{k}\in\partial F(\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k})).

(28)

(
27
) together with Assumption
1
implies
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{v\in\partial F(\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k}))}\|v\|_{2}<\infty.
Thus,
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|\xi_{k}\|_{2}<\infty
.

Next, we prove the conclusion. The compactness of
\mathcal{M}
implies that there exists a subsequence of
\{(z_{k},\xi_{k})\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
that converges to a fixed point. This proves the first argument. For any accumulation point
(z_{\star},\xi_{\star})
, we take one such subsequence
\{(z_{k(r)},\xi_{k(r)})\}_{r=0}^{\infty}
that converges to
(z_{\star},\xi_{\star})
. The compactness of
\mathcal{M}
and (
27
) implies
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}z_{k(r)}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{S}_{t^{\prime}}(z_{k(r)})=z_{\star},z_{\star}\in\mathcal{M}
. (
28
) together with Remark 1(ii) in
[
52
]
implies that

\xi_{\star}\in\partial F(z_{\star}).

(29)

Since the projection
\mbox{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{z}\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
is smooth with regard to
z
and
\xi_{k}=\mbox{Proj}_{[\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}]^{\perp}}(\xi_{k})
, we have that

\xi_{\star}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\xi_{k(r)}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mbox{Proj}_{[\mathrm{T}_{z_{k(r)}}\mathcal{M}]^{\perp}}(\xi_{k(r)})=\mbox{Proj}_{[\mathrm{T}_{z_{\star}}\mathcal{M}]^{\perp}}(\xi_{\star})
. Thus, (
29
) implies that
0\in\mbox{Proj}_{\mathrm{T}_{z_{\star}}\mathcal{M}}(\partial F(z_{\star}))
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

6
Conclusion

We proposed the Inexact Manifold Proximal Linear Algorithm (IManPL) for nonsmooth Riemannian composite optimization, which introduces adaptive subproblem stopping conditions. Our analysis established that IManPL achieves the
O(1/\epsilon^{2})
main iteration complexity and an overall
O(1/\epsilon^{3})
subproblem iteration complexity, matching or improving upon ManPL and existing Euclidean counterparts. We also proved that the accumulation points reached by IManPL are stationary solutions. Numerical experiments on sparse spectral clustering and sparse PCA confirm that IManPL outperforms ManPL or ManPG in computational efficiency. These results highlight the value of adaptive inexactness and broaden the applicability of proximal methods to large-scale manifold optimization problems.

Appendix B
Alternative Subproblem Solver When
c(\cdot)
Is the Identity Mapping

In this section, we focus on the situation that
m=n,c(z)=z,\forall z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case, the first-order Algorithm
2
might not be the best choice. Thus, we introduce the adaptive regularized semi-smooth Newton’s method (ASSN) for solving (
4
). It is proposed by
[
49
]
and used by
[
26
]
in their manifold proximal gradient method (ManPG).

Following the notations in Section
4.1
, we can also rewrite the optimization problem in (
4
) as

\min_{\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{H}_{k}(\tilde{x}):=f(z_{k})+c_{k}^{\top}\tilde{x}+h(\tilde{x}+d_{k})+\frac{1}{2t_{k}}\|\tilde{x}\|_{2}^{2},\ s.t.\ C_{k}\tilde{x}=0.

(48)

The Lagrangian function is as follows.

\tilde{L}_{k}(\tilde{x};\mu):=\tilde{H}_{k}(\tilde{x})-\mu^{\top}C_{k}\tilde{x}.

(49)

Thus, the dual problem is

\max_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}}\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu):=\min_{\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{L}_{k}(\tilde{x};\mu).

(50)

Section 4.2 in
[
26
]
shows that
\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu)
is smooth and concave,
\nabla\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu)
is Lipschitz continuous, but there is no guarantee on the strong concavity of
\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu)
. Thus, they use ASSN to solve the equation
\nabla\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu)=0
. Denote the sequence of inexact solutions that ASSN iteratively generates as
\{\mu_{k}^{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
. The corresponding primal inexact solutions are
\tilde{x}_{k}^{j}=\argmin_{\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{L}_{k}(\tilde{x};\mu_{k}^{j}),j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\argmin_{\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\tilde{L}_{k}(\tilde{x};\mu)
is Lipschitz continuous with regard to
\mu.
Theorem 3.10 in
[
49
]
shows that
\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k}^{j}
exists, and

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{k}^{j}=:\mu_{k}^{\star}\in\argmax_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{\hat{n}}}\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu).

(51)

Thus,
z_{k}+\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{x}_{k}^{j}=S_{t_{k}}(z_{k})
and
C_{k}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{x}_{k}^{j}=0
.

Next, we introduce our new subproblem termination conditions designed for ASSN. Knowing that ASSN does not guarantee
C_{k}\tilde{x}_{k}^{j}=0
, we denote
\hat{x}_{k}^{j}=\Proj_{\mathrm{T}_{z_{k}}\mathcal{M}}(\tilde{x}_{k}^{j})
and design sufficient subproblem termination conditions as follows:

F_{t_{k}}(\hat{x}_{k}^{j}+z_{k};z_{k})-\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{j})\leq\rho_{l}\left(F(z_{k})-F_{t_{k}}(\hat{x}_{k}^{j}+z_{k};z_{k})\right),\rho_{l}>0.

(52)

F_{t_{k}}(\hat{x}_{k}^{j}+z_{k};z_{k})-\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{j})\leq\frac{\rho_{h}}{2t_{k}}\|\hat{x}_{k}^{j}\|_{2}^{2},\rho_{h}\in(0,1/4).

(53)

When IT = (LACC), we terminate ASSN when it finds
\mu_{k}^{j}
such that (
52
) holds. When IT = (HACC), we terminate ASSN when it finds
\mu_{k}^{j}
such that (
53
) holds. Then, we set
\tilde{z}_{k+1}=\hat{x}_{k}^{j}+z_{k}
. By weak duality, (
52
) implies (
7
)-(LACC) and (
53
) implies (
7
)-(HACC).

[
28
,
30
]
also focuses on the situation that
c(\cdot)
is an identity mapping and solving the subproblem (
4
) via Newton’s method. Different from our methods that require the primal-dual gap to be small, their methods require that
\|\nabla\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{j})\|_{2}
to be sufficiently small. In addition, they can only guarantee the main iteration complexity
O(1/\epsilon^{2})
for finding
z_{k}
such that
\|\tilde{z}_{k+1}-z_{k}\|_{2}\leq O(\epsilon)
, but this definition is not based on the exact solution
S_{t}(z_{k})
.
[
28
]
’s condition only works for sufficiently small
t_{k}
.
[
30
]
’s condition takes the form

\|\nabla\tilde{D}_{k}(\mu_{k}^{j})\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{4t_{k}^{2}L_{h}^{2}+\|\hat{x}_{k}^{j}\|_{2}^{2}/2}-2t_{k}L_{h}.

It explicitly depends on
L_{h}
.

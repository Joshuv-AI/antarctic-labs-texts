Development of a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system for the effect evaluation of sprint training - based on AHP and entropy weight method
Authors: Peiyang Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In the field of competitive sports, the scientific evaluation of the effect of sprinting training has long faced the core challenges of insufficient integration of multi-dimensional indicators and static weight distribution. Traditional methods rely on a single physiological indicator or linear model, making it difficult to quantify the nonlinear synergy of physical fitness, skills and mental ability, especially with significant limitations on the threshold effect of psychological factors and adaptability to dynamic training cycles. To this end, this study developed a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system based on dynamic coupling of AHP-entropy weight method. By integrating the subjective experience weighting of Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) with the objective data drive of entropy weight method, a three-dimensional evaluation system covering nine secondary indicators including vertical jump height, step frequency coefficient of variation and anxiety control score was constructed. The system innovatively introduces the dynamic adjustment coefficient α to achieve adaptive optimization of subjective and objective weights at different stages of the training cycle, and uses the structural equation model to verify the path contribution rate of physical fitness, skill and psychological dimensions to the comprehensive training effect, where the marginal effect of skill parameters increases non linearly with the training intensity. The independent contribution rate of psychological factors in the pre competition period increased significantly to 31%. Empirical research shows that by using membership function quantification indicators to blur boundaries and combining multi-source data fusion techniques, the model reduced the overall prediction error by 47.7%, increased the model fit index (CFI) to 0.93, and improved the sensitivity of the psychological assessment module by 38.2% compared with traditional methods. The dynamic weighting mechanism successfully captured the cross-system association between the blood lactate threshold and psychological resilience. During the weighting transition from the enhancement period to the pre competition period, the skill weighting was dynamically adjusted from 0.46 to 0.53, and the psychological weighting jumped from 0.44 to 0.58, effectively matching the demand for neuromuscular coordination and stress control in the competition environment. The study confirmed that the millisecond-level optimized response of the fuzzy rule base to the coefficient of variation of step size and the time to touch the ground solved the problem of nonlinear modeling of technical parameters. The application of this system breaks through the empirical evaluation paradigm and provides a full-chain solution for sprinting training from data collection, dynamic decision-making to personalized intervention. Its core innovation lies in achieving three breakthroughs: dynamic weight distribution, psychophysiological collaborative quantification, and cross-cycle adaptive optimization.

Introduction

The scientific assessment of the training effects of sprinting, as a core sport of competitive sports, has always been a key topic in sports science. Conventional assessment methods often rely on a single indicator (such as maximum speed or blood lactate threshold), making it difficult to fully reflect the synergy of physical, technical and mental abilities of athletes1,2, the limitations of this one-dimensional analysis are particularly prominent in sprinting training. With the increasing complexity of sports training, the need for multi-dimensional metric fusion has become more prominent3, especially in high-intensity cycle training, the dynamic interaction of physical reserve, neuromuscular coordination and psychological stress response shows significant nonlinearity. Studies have shown that the differences in sprint performance are not only due to physiological functions (such as the peak activity of creatine phosphate kinase), but also highly correlated with technical parameters such as cadence and stride length4,5. For example, when the cadence exceeds 4.6 steps per second, the contact time needs to be synchronized and optimized to within 85 milliseconds, otherwise it will result in a 12%−15% decrease in mechanical efficiency. However, existing studies still have subjective and static problems in the allocation of metric weights6, especially when the relative importance of physical fitness, skills and mental ability shifts systematically at different stages of the training cycle, but traditional models lack a dynamic adjustment mechanism, resulting in the correlation coefficient between evaluation results and actual performance generally being less than 0.7. There is an urgent need for a dynamic and adaptive comprehensive evaluation system.

At present, fuzzy mathematics and multi-attribute decision-making methods offer new ideas for training effect evaluation7. For example, fuzzy logic can quantify the nonlinear effect of psychological factors on performance8, and when the anxiety score exceeds the 8.5 threshold, the probability of delay in the athlete’s reaction time increases by 3.2 times. This mutation effect requires gradient mapping through membership functions. The AHP-entropy weight method (Fig. 1) can effectively integrate expert experience with data-driven objective weights9,10, by constructing the dynamic coupling of the judgment matrix and the calculation of information entropy, reduced the fusion error of subjective and objective weights from 18% to within 9%. However, the existing research is mostly limited to the application of a single method and lacks systematic modeling of physical, skill and mental abilities11, particularly ignoring the cross-dimensional interaction effect among the three: Empirical data showed that when blood lactate levels were below 4.0mmol/L, an increase of 1 unit in anxiety control could improve 30-meter sprint time by 0.08 s, but this synergy was underestimated by 37% in static models. In addition, the dynamic association between physiological indicators and athletic performance has not been fully explored12, and existing studies mostly use cross-sectional data, failing to capture the lag effect of periodic fluctuations in hormone levels on explosive power indicators, which limits the predictive accuracy of assessment models13.

This study presents a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system based on AHP-entropy weight method, aiming to solve the problem of dynamic weight balance in multi-dimensional index fusion. By introducing the structural equation model (Fig. 2), the path effects of physical fitness, skills and mental ability on the comprehensive training effect were verified14,15, in which the marginal contribution rate of the skill dimension to performance increased non linearly with training intensity, reaching a peak weight of 53% in the pre competition period. For example, the AMOS analysis showed that mental ability contributed up to 31% to performance, significantly higher than the conventional linear regression model16, and this difference stemmed from the segmented processing ability of the fuzzy rule base for anxiety thresholds, which increased the sensitivity of mental assessment by 23%. Meanwhile, the dynamic weight adjustment mechanism improved the model fit by 12%, outperformed the static model, which automatically adjusted the entropy weight coefficient through the training cycle recognition algorithm, and during the transition from the base period to the pre competition period, the psychological weight was dynamically adjusted from 0.44 to 0.58 to precisely match the requirements of the competition environment. In addition, multi-source data fusion improves the reliability and validity of the system to Cronbach’s α > 0.8517, and the 2000 Hz sampling rate of the IMU can capture subtle fluctuations of the coefficient of variation of the step size at the level of 0.3%. The psychological resilience scale quantifies individual differences in stress adaptation through latent variable modeling, providing a reliable basis for training personalization.

The structure of this paper is as follows (Fig. 2): Chapter Two constructs the theoretical framework and research methods, with emphasis on explaining the dynamic coupling equation of AHP- entropy weight method and the parameter calibration rules of fuzzy membership function; Chap. 3 validates the model through empirical data from 200 athletes18, and uses cross-validation to demonstrate that the system’s MAPE drops to 7.9% when predicting 30-meter sprint results; Chap. 4 compares the prediction error of traditional methods (such as linear regression) (MAPE reduced by 22%)19 to reveal the corrective effect of dynamic weighting mechanisms on the lag in psychological metric evaluation; Chap. 5 summarizes the innovations and proposes future directions, exploring the possibility of collaborative optimization of multi-base data under the federated learning framework. By systematically integrating physiological, technical and psychological metrics, this study provides methodological support for the scientification of sprint training20, and promotes a paradigm shift in sports assessment from experience-oriented to data-driven21. The core breakthrough lies in the establishment of the first quantifiable three-dimensional evaluation system of training cycle - indicator weight - psychological threshold, which increased the correlation coefficient between comprehensive evaluation value and actual performance to 0.91.

Theoretical framework and research methods

The multi-dimensional theory of the effect of sprinting training

The evaluation of the effect of sprinting should cover the multi-dimensional synergy of physical fitness, skills and mental ability. The physical dimension includes speed, strength and endurance. Research shows that the correlation coefficient between vertical jump height and 30 m sprint performance is 0.78, confirming the core position of explosive power. The skill dimension involves a non-linear balance between cadence and stride length. Elite athletes need to keep their stride length within 1.2 times their height when cadence exceeds 4.6 steps per second to avoid energy loss22. The psychological dimension is represented by anxiety threshold control, where athletes can increase their reaction amplitude by 15% under high pressure. The multi-dimensional theory emphasizes that relying on a single indicator alone cannot explain performance differences, and it is necessary to systematically integrate multi-dimensional data.

Dynamic coupling mechanism of AHP- entropy weight method

Ahp-entropy method enhances the scientific nature of the evaluation model through the dynamic coupling of subjective and objective weights. Subjective weights are based on expert experience, for example, the initial weights for physical fitness, skills and psychology are assigned as 0.4, 0.35 and 0.25, and logical rationality is ensured through consistency tests (CR < 0.1). The entropy weight rule corrects weights based on data dispersion, for example, when the information entropy of a certain metric is 0.85, its objective weight is reduced to 0.12. The dynamic coupling mechanism integrates subjective and objective weights through weighted averaging, and experiments have shown that this method reduces the model’s prediction error from 18% to 12%23. This mechanism effectively addresses the problem of weight staticization and subjective bias in traditional methods.

Design of fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system

The fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system quantifies uncertainty through membership functions, such as defining the “excellent” grade of step frequency as 4.5–4.8 steps per second and describes its membership degree through trapezoidal distribution functions. Psychological indicators such as anxiety levels are processed using triangular fuzzy numbers to convert qualitative evaluations into quantitative scores. The system calculates the comprehensive evaluation value through a weighted average algorithm. For example, when an athlete’s physical fitness, skill, and psychological affiliation are 0.8, 0.7, and 0.6 respectively, the comprehensive score is 0.8 × 0.4 + 0.7 × 0.35 + 0.6 × 0.25 = 0.72. The study showed that the system’s sensitivity to psychological indicators increased by 23%24, significantly outperforming traditional scoring methods.

Research hypotheses (H1-H7)

The proposal of the research hypothesis is based on the gap between theory and empirical evidence. H5 (Dynamic Weighting for improved Fit) originated from study of periodically trained models, which dynamically adjusted the model’s fit by 12%. H7 (multi-source data for enhanced reliability and validity) was supported with multi-modal data fusion increasing Cronbach’s α from 0.75 to 0.8825. H6 hypothesizes a nonlinear association between mental ability and performance, for example, anxiety levels below the threshold have no significant effect on performance, while those above the threshold cause a 0.2-second delay in response time. Hypothesis validation will combine longitudinal data with a structural equation model to ensure theoretical consistency.

Structural equation model based on AMOS

This study chose AMOS instead of SmartPLS/LISREL based on three considerations: (1) Data type adaptation: AMOS’s covariance matrix algorithm is more suitable for continuous physiological indicators (such as lactate threshold) and fuzzy membership (0–1 continuous values), while SmartPLS’s PLS algorithm for discrete scale data may lose subtle differences in membership; (2) Non normal data processing: Through Bollen Stone Bootstrap (2000 samples) correction, AMOS can effectively handle non normal distribution data generated by fuzzy comprehensive evaluation (see Table 1, χ ²/df = 2.37), while LISREL requires strict multivariate normal assumptions; (3) Model interpretability: The path visualization capability of AMOS directly supports the presentation of three-dimensional interactive effects as shown in Fig. 2, and is compatible with the hierarchical weight structure of AHP entropy weight method. Comparative testing shows that AMOS has a 38% lower estimation error in the effect size of the psychological skill interaction pathway compared to SmartPLS (0.53 ± 0.04 vs. 0.41 ± 0.07), and the model convergence speed is improved by 2.3 times. The structural equation model verifies variable relationships through path coefficients. For example, the standardized path coefficient of physical ability to the overall training effect was 0.42, and the contribution rate of mental ability was 0.31, both reaching the significance level26. The model fit metric CFI = 0.93 and RMSEA = 0.06 met the strict criteria. AMOS analysis showed that the interaction between skills and psychology accounted for 19% of the variance, highlighting the necessity of multi-dimensional coupling27. The model provides a statistical validation framework for multi-dimensional evaluation of sprinting training effects.

System construction and empirical analysis

Metrics selection

Based on the core objective of “development of a fuzzy comprehensive evaluation system for the effectiveness of sprinting training”, this study constructed the index system through four stages: theoretical analysis - expert screening - data validation - method fusion. First, based on the theory of sprinting training and domestic and foreign literature, and relying on the empirical data of outstanding Chinese athletes, the framework of the three-level indicator system covering three first-level dimensions of physical fitness, skills and psychology and nine second-level indicators was initially selected.

The indicators were optimized through three rounds of Delphi expert screening, and a panel of 10 national-level coaches and sports science professors eliminated the indicators with low measurement repeatability or weak practicality in the first round (Table 2); In the second round, the Likert 5-level scale was used for scoring, and the core indicators with mean > 4.0 and coefficient of variation < 15% were retained. In the third round, the expert consensus was confirmed through the Kendall’s coefficient of coordination test, and finally nine secondary indicators were formed, covering key dimensions such as vertical jump height, 30 m sprint time, and anxiety control score, to build a scientific and practical framework for evaluating the effectiveness of sprint training. All experimental protocols were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Nantou Middle School, Shenzhen, and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all 200 athletes prior to data collection, with strict anonymization of personal identifiers. To ensure internal consistency and temporal stability of data from 200 athletes: (1) Measurement consistency: Physiological indicators (blood lactate, vertical jump) are calibrated using a unified device (Biosen C-Line, daytime error < 1.5%), and technical parameters are independently scored by 3 certified analysts (intra group correlation coefficient ICC = 0.92); (2) Testing test-retest reliability: Randomly select 30 athletes to repeat measurements at 48 h intervals, with a correlation coefficient r ≥ 0.85 for key indicators (such as stride frequency r = 0.91, p < 0.001); Anxiety score r = 0.87), mean absolute error ≤ 2.3% (e.g. ground contact time difference of 3.1 ± 1.2ms); (3) Outlier handling: Exclude data outside ± 3 σ (accounting for 0.7%), and verify through ANOVA that there is no significant difference in baseline between groups (F = 0.38, p = 0.686). This study used internationally recognized scales for psychological indicators and validated their local reliability and validity: (1) Anxiety control: Based on the cognitive anxiety dimension of the Competition State Anxiety Inventory (CSAI-2), six core items (factor loadings > 0.7) were extracted through principal component analysis, Cronbach’s α = 0.81; (2) Psychological resilience: adapted from the Connor Davidson Resilience Scale CD-RISC-10, deleting 2 items with low cultural sensitivity and retaining 8 items (α = 0.79); (3) Selective response: The Nideffer attention testing system standardized paradigm was used, with visual stimulus interval randomization (800-1200ms) to prevent expected effects, and test-retest reliability ICC = 0.89. The scale adjustments were reviewed by an expert committee (Kendall coordination coefficient W = 0.86, p < 0.001) and were significantly correlated with serum cortisol concentration (r=−0.62, p = 0.003), verifying ecological validity.

AHP- entropy weight method weight synthesis

This study adopts a dynamic coupling framework of AHP (Analytic Hierarchy Process) and entropy weight method, aiming to solve the problem of balancing subjective experience and objective data in traditional weight models Fig. 3. The process is as follows: First, the expert experience is quantified through AHP. Then, 10 national coaches and sports science experts construct the judgment matrix based on the Saaty nine-level scale method, calculate the feature vectors and pass the consistency test to generate the subjective weights of physical fitness, skills and psychology dimensions; Then, based on the measured data of 200 athletes, the information entropy and objective weights were calculated through the range method for standardization, and high discrimination indicators were screened. Finally, the dynamic adjustment coefficient α was introduced to achieve adaptive fusion of subjective and objective weights through the weighting formula. The dynamic adjustment coefficient α is determined through a three-stage process: (1) Stage identification: based on the proportion of days with blood lactate > 4.0 mmol/L, the basic stage, strengthening stage, and pre competition stage are divided through k-means clustering; (2) Optimization calibration: With the goal of minimizing the prediction error of the 30 m run, 10 fold cross validation is used to solve α, so that the combination weight (α · w_AHP+(1- α) · w_Entropy) best fits the longitudinal data pattern of 50 elite athletes; (3) Momentum smoothing: Introducing a momentum factor β = 0.9 to exponentially smooth the updated values of the alpha cycle, suppressing sudden weight changes during phase transitions. Verify that alpha is set at 0.62 ± 0.07 during the baseline period (with a focus on expert experience) and reduced to 0.39 ± 0.04 during the pre competition period (with a focus on objective data), resulting in a 38% reduction in model error (MAPE: 12.5% → 7.9%), and a coach decision matching rate of 92%. As shown in Fig. 5, The psychological dimension achieved weight reconstruction through dynamic adjustment coefficients, and the weight of anxiety control score jumped from objective 0.08 to 0.44, demonstrating the corrective effect of expert experience on the shortcomings of psychological assessment. The system achieves dynamic weight adjustment through three steps: (1) Stage recognition: When the proportion of training days with athletes’ blood lactate concentration exceeding 4.0 mmol/L exceeds 30% for two consecutive weeks, it automatically switches to the pre competition stage weight mode. (2) Dynamic mixing: Calculate the entropy weight based on the latest data every Monday and mix it proportionally with the AHP weight preset for the current stage. The mixing ratio is based on 62% expert experience and 38% objective data during the basic period, and adjusted to 39% experience and 61% data during the pre competition period. (3) Smooth transition: Using historical weight momentum smoothing technology to ensure that the weight changes of the same indicator in adjacent weeks do not exceed 5% points, avoiding drastic fluctuations. Conclusions and prospects

Research conclusions

This study, through theoretical construction and empirical analysis, established the significant advantages of the sprinting training effect evaluation system based on dynamic weight coupling in terms of scientificity, adaptability and practicability. The research confirmed that the AHP-entropy weight method fusion mechanism successfully balanced the contradiction between subjective and objective weights, and the dynamic adjustment coefficient made the weight distribution of the model at different stages of the training cycle exhibit adaptive optimization characteristics, verifying the core assumption that dynamic weights improve the model’s adaptability. The synergy of physical, skill and psychological dimensions is quantified by the path coefficients of the structural equation model, where the marginal contribution rate of skill parameters to the overall training effect varies non linearly with the training intensity, supporting the nonlinear coupling hypothesis of multi-dimensional indicators. The fuzzy membership function design of the psychological assessment module breaks through the limitations of traditional scales, and the enhanced synergistic sensitivity of anxiety control and reaction time indicators validates the threshold effect hypothesis of psychological factors, providing a precise target for psychological training intervention. The dynamic redistribution of weights reflects the stage specific adaptation of sprint training: (1) Physical fitness dominates during the baseline period: the threshold weight of blood lactate is 0.41 (Table 3), corresponding to the need for glycolysis system modification. At this time, the mitochondrial density of skeletal muscle increases by 17% (literature value 15–20%), supporting the formation of the maximum oxygen uptake plateau period in 4–6 weeks; (2) Pre competition psychological weight transition: Psychological weight increased from 0.44 to 0.58 (Table 12) in sync with the rise in cortisol/testosterone ratio (r = 0.61, p < 0.01), which conforms to the neuroendocrine pattern of HPA axis activation induced cognitive sharpening in competitive environments; (3) Skill psychological synergy: The covariation of skill weight 0.53 and psychological weight 0.58 (path coefficient 0.53 in Table 13) corresponds to the theory of neuromuscular efficiency - when the cognitive resources saved by action automation (ground contact time < 85ms) can be reinvested in stress response, reducing reaction time delay by 22ms (literature value 18-25ms). This dynamic pattern replicates the three-stage law of “metabolic foundation → neural optimization → psychological sharpening” in the classic training staging theory, but shifts the transition nodes from empirical judgment to data-driven (threshold for the proportion of days with blood lactate > 4.0 mmol/L).

The AHP-Entropy Weighting Method Dynamic Coupled Fuzzy Comprehensive Evaluation System developed in this study is designed to provide scientific, dynamic, and personalized evaluation and decision support for sprint training practices. In practice, the system has been successfully integrated into the daily training monitoring system of the national track and field team’s sprint group. For instance, during the pre-competition training of a key athlete, the system used a dynamic weighting mechanism to accurately identify that his anxiety control score was near the critical point, measured at 8.7 points, below the excellent threshold of 9 points. Additionally, skill data analysis showed that his stride frequency was 4.65 steps per second, and his ground contact time was 86 milliseconds, indicating a risk of delayed reaction time in competition, with a prediction error MAPE of 9.8%. Based on these findings, the coaching team implemented three targeted measures: psychological intervention, increased simulation of high-pressure scenarios, and real-time adjustment of anxiety levels using biofeedback technology; technical action fine-tuning, focusing on optimizing the ground contact technique at the start phase, aiming to reduce the ground contact time to within 85 milliseconds; and dynamic adjustment of training load, moderately reducing the high-intensity interval training volume based on the system’s output mean comprehensive score of 0.7 and the sub-item membership degree, prioritizing neuromuscular recovery. After two weeks of intervention, the athlete’s anxiety score increased to 9.3 points, and the prediction error MAPE for the 30-meter sprint performance decreased from an initial 8.2% to 6.5%, demonstrating the system’s effectiveness in guiding practical training optimization.

Future outlook

Future research needs to break through existing limitations in three aspects: technology deepening, application expansion and system integration. At the algorithmic level, distributed weight optimization under the federated learning framework can be explored to address the problem of data silos in multiple training bases; Introduce a time series prediction model to predict the phased decay pattern of athlete skill parameters for the design of preventive training programs. To address the reliability and validity bottleneck of psychological assessment, it is proposed to integrate near-infrared brain functional imaging and micro-expression recognition technology to build a multimodal psychological state monitoring network, breaking through the subjective limitations of traditional scales. In addition, a lightweight assessment terminal based on edge computing needs to be developed to achieve real-time biomechanical analysis on the field and dynamic tuning of the cloud model through the 5G-MEC architecture to meet the low latency requirements of high-intensity training scenarios.

In terms of application ecosystem construction, a cross-project evaluation standard migration mechanism should be established to verify the generalization ability of the model in sprint derivative events such as hurdles and relay. Explore the cross-system coupling model of muscle oxygen saturation and psychological load in combination with the latest achievements in sports medicine, and improve the injury risk early warning function. It is suggested to build a global athlete database in collaboration with IAAF and other institutions, and use blockchain technology to achieve secure sharing of training data and co-evolution of models. For adolescent athletes, it is necessary to design weight-adjusting algorithms specifically for developmental sensitive periods, incorporate biomarkers such as bone age and hormone levels, and form a life-cycle training assessment system. The research team will further promote the deep integration of the assessment system with the virtual reality training platform, simulate the competitive stress environment through digital twin technology, and accelerate the scientific development process of stress adaptability.

Experimental study on the dust control performance of rotating fog curtain under the perturbation of long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation
Authors: Jingxu Chen, Yongkai Zhi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The dust source point in the coal-breaking operation prolonged activities generate substantial dust, diminished the air quality at the tunneling face. This study focuses on the pneumatic vortex fog curtain dust control system in the underground coal mine tunneling face. It explores the dust-trapping performance of this system under the disturbance of a long-pressure and short-suction auxiliary ventilation system. The results indicate that the respiratory dust captured by the fog curtain wall on the driver’s working side can be reduced from an average concentration of 135.5 mg/m3 to 2.5 mg/m3, and on the 15 m downwind side of the coal-cutting machine, it decreases from 70.2 mg/m3 to 2.5 mg/m3. Under the disturbance of the long-pressure and short-suction ventilation system, as the wind speed increases, the rotating fog curtain gap expands, reducing the sealing performance. Without compromising the fog curtain conditions, this combined system achieves efficient dust suppression, significantly improving the working environment at the tunneling face.

Introduction

When modern mining roadheaders perform high-efficiency coal cutting operations, the large amount of dust generated by the cutter teeth cutting and crushing coal accounts for more than 80% of the entire working face, causing severe dust pollution at the working face1–3. After dust is generated at the dust source point, it is entrained by the airflow and transported along the direction of the wind flow due to the smooth airflow within the site. Additionally, the dusty airflow is slowly discharged along the return air side of the roadway, spreading throughout the entire roadway4–6. The use of traditional ventilation dust removal and atomized dust removal methods individually both have certain drawbacks, and their effectiveness in capturing suspended respirable dust is not significant7–9. Therefore, based on the dust generation characteristics of the coal mining system at the tunneling face, the use of a pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system to form a rotating fog curtain in conjunction with an auxiliary ventilation system can control the dust concentration at the tunneling face. This method effectively intercepts dust pollution at the coal cutting dust source point, forming an effective occupational hazard prevention and control method.

There has been extensive research on the use of traditional air curtains and spray dust suppression to control particulate matter concentration. Li et al. developed air curtain dust collection technology, and their research results indicated that this method can effectively control unorganized dust during the real-time operation of material dropping10. Cai et al. developed an air curtain dust prevention technology for fully mechanized mining working faces, forming a triangular air curtain between the coal cutter and the walkway. After applying this technology for dust suppression at the fully mechanized working face, it effectively prevented dust from entering the operator’s work area11. Mo et al. proposed on-board dust removal technology for coal cutters and verified the performance of the on-board air curtain dust remover through practical field tests12. Yang et al. proposed a modular diversion airflow system and dust control technology for roadheaders, studying and analyzing the influence of different side outlet airflow diversions, tilt angles, and radial-axial air volume ratios, and obtained the influence rules of air and dust coupling diffusion13. Jahanzaib et al. conducted monitoring research on air curtains from the perspective of slipstream motion, maximizing the reduction of particulate matter concentration14. Additionally, many studies have considered the impact of forced ventilation conditions within roadways on the performance of air curtain dust control15,16.

Another important spray dust suppression technology has been developed by Jing et al., who based on the air-water spray principle and vortex airflow laws, developed a new type of ultra-fine spiral atomizing nozzle. They determined the optimal spiral effect parameters and verified through field experiments that this device improved dust suppression effectiveness17,18. Peng et al. developed a new type of shearer sprayer specifically for the drum cutting process, demonstrating that this device better achieved dust control rates19. Han et al. studied the impact of forced ventilation on the diffusion patterns of droplets sprayed by external sprayers on roadheaders. They proved that using a Venturi Duct with Coanda Effect (VDCE) effectively alleviated airflow interference on droplet distribution, and VDCE improved the suppression efficiency of respirable dust20. Fang et al. researched respirable coal dust based on the atomization of gas-liquid two-phase flow nozzles, obtaining insights into the effect of humidity and demonstrating good dust suppression performance21.Xie et al. put forward the “plugging” method of fully mechanized mining face to study the effect of negative pressure dust removal after the formation of ventilation resistance in roadway. Yang Xianhang et al. put forward a modular dust suppression method by diverting ventilation and dust control, and studied the law of air-dust coupling diffusion movement under different conditions. Many other studies have also combined the atomization performance in the existing forced ventilation environment of roadways22,23,and a CFD-DEM coupling simulation method based on k-ε two-path turbulence model and Hertz-Mindlin model. These studies provide valuable references for the early research of rotating fog curtains, with most researchers considering the impact of forced ventilation disturbances on dust control equipment. Therefore, in this study, we further investigate the dust control capability of this device under forced ventilation conditions.

This study primarily focuses on the dust control performance of the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system under long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation disturbances. Therefore, a numerical simulation is employed to analyze the breakup of droplets under fluid flow conditions and the trajectory of droplet movement under long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation conditions. This analysis aims to determine the system’s resistance to disturbances. Additionally, we validate and refine the numerical simulation results by combining the simulated fluid dynamics with the constructed experimental platform, simulating the comprehensive dust control system for the tunneling face under long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation disturbances. This process confirms the effectiveness of the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system’s dust control performance under long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation disturbances.

Pneumatic spiral spray dedusting device working principle

In order to address the issue of dust pollution in the tunneling face, a pneumatic spiral spray dedusting technology is proposed. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the device mainly comprises an air inlet pipe, water pipe, annular air duct, annular water pipe, high-pressure air jet injector, and nozzles. The technology installs the air inlet pipe, the water pipe is connected with the replacement water pipe, and the high-pressure air flow ejector is uniformly arranged along the inner ring of the annular air duct to improve the droplets and air flow speed at the nozzle, and the longitudinal ring wind field is formed through the annular air duct. The external water spray is impacted by the rotating jet flow, forcibly altering the motion direction of water droplets in the spray field. The droplets moving forward gain tangential kinetic energy, forming a dense, rotating, and directed mist wall towards the working face. A conceptual 3D representation of the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting device is illustrated in Fig. 2, where R represents the atomization radius of the equipment.

Compared to traditional spray dust suppression methods, the formed dense mesh-like mist curtain wall can better seal the tunnel’s cross-section, effectively isolating dust’s diffusion and movement. Based on the preliminary experimental results analysis, first, the dust-laden airflow in the tunnel is forced to collide with the mist curtain wall; second, dust particles collide with mist droplets, condense, and rapidly settle. However, most tunnels have long-pressure and short-pumping forced ventilation systems in production processes. Therefore, we believe this system may have a certain impact on implementing this technology.

Mathematical modeling

The system operated under standard room temperature and atmospheric pressure conditions without energy conversion. Within the computational domain, air was the medium, and the fluid was treated as an incompressible gas. Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) software was employed for analysis and calculations.

Moreover, it is universal to turbulence simulation in most engineering, and can be applied to a variety of complex environments from fluid to particles, and can better simulate the diffusion and migration behavior of particles in high turbulent region. Especially in high turbulence intensity environments such as mine airflow disturbance and dust control, it shows high accuracy. Therefore, k-ε turbulence model is chosen for mathematical model construction.

In the COMSOL simulation software, the simplified models of tunnels, tunnel machines, and air pipes are discretized into grids for numerical simulation analysis, This is achieved by constructing a network simulation diagram that represents the physical model of the tunnel. Grid independence is validated through a comparison of calculation results across varying mesh densities; if results remain stable with mesh refinement, the simulation can be deemed grid-independent.

By analyzing wind velocity and dust movement in the driving section of the lane using three different grid division schemes coarse, very coarse, and fine it was found that there were no significant structural differences among these divisions. Thus, it can be concluded that the grid division satisfies numerical simulation requirements, Notably, when examining values beyond 2.6 m on the X-axis, some discrepancies in wind speed emerge between the three grid divisions. The overall trend indicates uniform changes in wind speed during simulations; therefore, refined lines were selected for further numerical analysis. A total of 1966,518 mesh units were used in the simulation. Compared with coarse mesh division and very coarse mesh division, refined mesh division has smoother result curve, and the simulation results are more close to the actual situation. The component mass distribution illustrated in the quality diagram falls within a range of 0.5 to 1, thereby confirming its reliability. Furthermore, both experimental validation and correctness assessments reinforce confidence in the adequacy of grid division.

Simulation results of the airflow field under the disturbance conditions of long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation

Using the k-ε turbulence model, calculations were performed on the rotating jet velocity field. The velocity distribution and integrity of the rotating wind curtain were obtained by analyzing the meteorological flow field and jet movement paths under different long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation conditions. This strategy aimed to analyze the formation effectiveness of the wind curtain using the long-pressure and short-pumping integrated dust control system. Note that the pressure airflow duct exit was set as the axial inlet, while the suction airflow duct served as the outlet. A pressure-to-suction ratio of 1.5 was also chosen, with a tunneling face cross-sectional area S of 15.75 m2. The pressure airflow rates of Q press were set to 150, 300, 400, and 500 m3/min, while the suction airflow rates of Q pump were set to 100, 200, 285, and 333 m3/min. The distance from the pressure airflow exit to the tunneling face LE was set to 15 m, and the distance from the suction airflow exit LF to 5 m. Following the principles of dynamic similarity theory, similar pressure airflow rates Qpress were derived as 150, 300, 400, and 500 m3/min, while similar suction airflow rates Q pump were derived as 100, 200, 285, and 333 m3/min. The distance from the pressure airflow exit to the tunneling face L press was set at 15 m, and from the suction airflow exit L pump at 5 m. The corresponding results are illustrated in Fig. 7.

From the above four simulation result graphs, we conclude that upon activating the external pressurized air duct of the shearer, air flows out from the pressurized air outlet. When the Pneumatic Spiral Spray Dedusting Device operates steadily, with increased pressurized air and extraction wind speeds, the impact intensity on the rotating wind curtain field gradually increases. During the process of expanding jet impact intensity, the pressurized air jet impacts the rotating droplets, causing them to deviate from their rotation trajectory, moving towards the negative x-axis direction. As the airflow increased, the diffusion of droplets drawn into the extraction air duct intensified. When the pressurized air volume Qpress was 3 m3/min and the extraction air volume Qpump was 285 m3/min, some droplets deviated from the rotation trajectory, and the spiral mist curtain gradually became unstable. Significant disruption occurred to the mist curtain formed by rotating droplets at the extraction duct, as depicted in Fig. 9b. The Pneumatic Spiral Spray Dedusting Device entered the extraction air duct, originally forming a closed presentation against the tunnel wall, revealing a gap. The gap in the mist curtain intensified with the increase in the Qpress and Qpump. As depicted in Fig. 9c and d, the Pneumatic Spiral Spray Dedusting Device has been completely disrupted, leading to a gradual failure of the Pneumatic Spiral Spray Dedusting System.

Dust reduction experiment design

During the experiment, the atmospheric conditions in the laboratory were set at Pamb = 0.1 MPa and Tamb = 298 K. Besides, a homemade dust generator was placed on the walls of a semi-enclosed experimental chamber as the dust source. Finely crushed dust from raw coal was used, and particles larger than 250 μm were removed using a 60-mesh sieve. The dust emission rate was set at 30 g/min. Stable dusting operations were simulated, and with the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system turned off, dust concentrations at designated points 1 and 2 were measured using the CCZ-1000 direct-reading dust meter without any dust control measures (dry operation). When the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system (Pneumatic spiral spray dedusting) was activated, the experiment space became humid, and small droplets carried by the pressurized airflow filled the entire tunnel. The high humidity in the confined space significantly affected the dust concentration meter’s dust control accuracy. For comprehensive dust control (wet operation), the AKFC-92 A dust sampler was used to sample dust in the air. The dust mass concentration in the air was determined using the formula by analyzing the increase in unit mass of the filter membrane before and after dust sampling, along with the sampling flow rate of the equipment.

Based on the comprehensive simulation of atomization disturbance, dust reduction experiments were conducted under conditions that could form a relatively complete mist curtain. The experimental conditions included:

Through the comparison of comprehensive dust suppression efficiency under long-pressure short-pumping ventilation in Fig. 14, it can be seen that as the air volume increases, the dust concentration at measurement point 1 (driver’s position) decreases, and the dust suppression efficiency steadily rises, reaching a maximum of 97.7%. However, at measurement point 2, the dust suppression efficiency first increases and then decreases. This is because the increase in pressure air volume intensifies the impact on the pneumatic vortex fog curtain, and the negative pressure generated by the exhaust air causes some droplets to deviate from their original rotational trajectory, creating gaps in the enclosure of the roadway, resulting in an increase in dust concentration at measurement point 2 and a decrease in dust suppression efficiency.

Due to the combined effect of the pressure air inlet and the exhaust air outlet, the destruction of the pneumatic vortex fog curtain is more severe. The experiment only considered the atomization dust suppression efficiency, so only two ventilation conditions were selected: Q pressure at 1.5 and 3 m3/min, and Q exhaust at 1 and 2 m3/min. Consequently, the total dust suppression efficiency at measurement point 1 was 97.6% and 97.1%, and the respirable dust suppression efficiency was 92.6% and 96.3%, respectively. At measurement point 2, the total dust suppression efficiency was 97.6% and 97.1%, and the respirable dust suppression efficiency was 96.4% and 96.4%. At measurement point 2, due to the disruption of the fog curtain by the ventilation conditions, dust escape occurred, causing an increase in dust concentration in the rear.

Conclusions

Both similar systems and numerical simulations confirm that under the disturbance of long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation conditions, when Q press is 400 m3/min and Q pump is 285 m3/min, the Velocity Planing Surface diagram indicates that the air curtain begins to experience ventilation-induced disruptions, resulting in openings. Simultaneously, gaps in the mist curtain also appear on the return airflow side. This proves that the pneumatic mist curtain can maintain its integrity at specific airflow rates, exhibiting a certain level of disturbance resistance. Combined with the pressure air duct for the tunneling face, it achieves comprehensive control, enveloping the dust source on the tunneling face.In specific conditions of long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation disturbance during the tunneling face pneumatic spiral spray dedusting experiment, it proves the disturbance resistance of the mist curtain wall formed by the aforementioned pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system. The average respirable dust concentration drops from 135.5 mg/m3 to 2.5 mg/m3 at the driver’s working location. Similarly, at 15 m on the downwind side of the coal-cutting machine, the average concentration of total dust decreases from 70.2 mg/m3 to 2.5 mg/m3. This indicates that the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system maintains high dust suppression performance even under disturbance.Furthermore, the system continues to provide effective dust reduction performance under the existing disturbance of long-pressure and short-pumping ventilation, significantly improving the working environment of the tunneling face. By employing the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system described above, the water consumption of this dust control system is equivalent to that of traditional spray systems. However, the formed mist curtain wall efficiently captures dust. Under undisturbed ventilation conditions, the dust reduction efficiency for both total and respirable dust exceeds 92%, achieving the highly efficient dust suppression goal with the pneumatic spiral spray dedusting system. Integrating the rotating mist curtain control system with the long-pressure and short-pumping system has distinct advantages in controlling dust pollution in underground mines.

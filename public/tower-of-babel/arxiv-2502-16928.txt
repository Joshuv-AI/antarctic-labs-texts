On modular representations of C-recursive integer sequences
Mihai Prunescu, Joseph M. Shunia

Abstract
Prunescu and Sauras-Altuzarra showed that all C-recursive sequences of natural numbers have an arithmetic div-mod representation that can be derived from their generating function. This representation consists of computing the quotient of two exponential polynomials and taking the remainder of the result modulo a third exponential polynomial, and works for all integers n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . Using a different approach, Prunescu proved the existence of two other representations, one of which is the mod-mod representation, consisting of two successive remainder computations. This result has two weaknesses: (i) the representation works only ultimately, and (ii) a correction term must be added to the first exponential polynomial. We show that a mod-mod representation without inner correction term holds for all integers n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . This follows directly from the div-mod representation by an arithmetic short-cut from outside. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 11B37 (primary), 39A06 (secondary). Keywords: C-recursive sequence, modular arithmetic, term representation.



1
Introduction

The
C-recursive sequences of order
d
are sequences
t:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying a relation of recurrence with constant coefficients:

t(n+d)+a_{1}t(n+d-1)+\dots+a_{d-1}t(n+1)+a_{d}t(n)=0

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. With the recurrence rule, we associate the polynomial:

B(x):=1+a_{1}X+\dots+a_{d}X^{d}.

According with Theorem 4.1.1 in
[
6
]
, and with Theorem 1 in
[
3
]
, the C-recursive sequences are exactly the sequences
(s(n))
such that the generating function:

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}t(n)z^{n}

is a rational function
A(z)/B(z)
with
\deg(A)<\deg(B)=d
.

Prunescu and Sauras-Altuzarra proved in
[
5
]
that if
f
is the generating function of a sequence consisting of natural numbers only, then

\displaystyle\exists c\in\mathbb{N}:\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},\quad t(n)=\left\lfloor c^{n^{2}}f(c^{-n})\right\rfloor\bmod c^{n}.

The exact statement will be given below, see Theorem
2.1
. When the sequence is C-recurrent, as
f
is a rational function
A(x)/B(x)
with
A(x),B(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, free terms of
A(x)
and
B(x)
positive, and
with
\deg(A)<\deg(B)=d
, one has the following
div-mod representation
:

\displaystyle t(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{c^{n^{2}+dn}A(c^{-n})}{c^{dn}B(c^{-n})}\right\rfloor\bmod c^{n}=\left\lfloor\frac{c^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(c^{n})}{\tilde{B}(c^{n})}\right\rfloor\bmod c^{n}.

Prunescu proved in
[
4
]
the following
mod-mod representation
. Let
\alpha_{d}\not=0
be the free term of the recurrence rule for a C-recursive sequence. Then there are
c,n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for
n\geq n_{0}
one has

\displaystyle t(n)=\frac{\left(\left(c^{n(d-1)+\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}-\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha_{d})\cdot c^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(c^{n})\right)\bmod\tilde{B}(c^{n})\right)\bmod c^{n}}{|\alpha_{d}|}

This representation has the advantage that
(c^{n(d-1)+\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}-\operatorname{sgn}(\alpha_{d})\cdot c^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(c^{n}))\bmod\tilde{B}(c^{n})
can be faster computed by modular arithmetic, but it has two disadvantages:

(i)

It needs in general a correction term
c^{n(d-1)+\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}
in the inner-most exponential polynomial.

(ii)

It holds only ultimately, for
n\geq
some an a priori undetermined
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. In fact such an
n_{0}
can be determined by conditions used in the proof, but it is not clear that one can always take
n_{0}=1
.

In the following we show that this result can be improved to:

If the free term of
\tilde{B}(c^{n})
is
\alpha_{d}\neq 0
, there exists
e\geq c
such that the following holds for all
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle t(n)=\frac{-1-\sign(\alpha_{d})}{2}+\frac{1}{|\alpha_{d}|}\left(\left((-\sign(\alpha_{d})\cdot e^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(e^{n}))\bmod\tilde{B}(e^{n})\right)\bmod e^{n}\right).

Moreover, this improvement is done by a short-cut of modular arithmetic. The result becomes only a corollary to the div-mod representation.

Other possibilities and methods to represent C-recursive sequences can be found in the monograph of Kauers and Paule
[
2
]
.

2
Technical preparations

We define
\mathbb{N}
as the set of natural numbers with
0
included.

The first three useful results refer to C-recursive sequences. The next Lemma can be also found in Everest and al
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[Prunescu & Sauras-Altuzarra, Lemma 4,
[
5
]
]
If
t:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is C-recursive, then there is an integer
g\geq 1
such that
|t(n)|<g^{n+1}
for every integer
n\geq 0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[Prunescu & Sauras-Altuzarra, Theorem 1 and 2,
[
5
]
] If
t:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
,
f(z)
is its generating function,
R
is the radius of convergence of
f(z)
at zero and
c
,
m
and
n
are three integers such that
c\geq 2
,
n\geq m\geq 2
,
c^{-m}<R
and
t(n)<c^{n-2}
for every integer
n\geq m
, then

t(n)=\left\lfloor c^{n^{2}}f(c^{-n})\right\rfloor\bmod c^{n}.

Also, if
c\geq 8
,
c^{-1}<R
and
t(n)<c^{n/3}
for every
n\geq 1
, then the representation works for every
n\geq 1
.

The following Corollary follows easily from
theorem

2.1
.

Corollary 2.1
.

If the representation stated in
theorem

2.1
holds for some
c\in\mathbb{N}
for all
n\geq m
, then it holds also if we replace
c
with any
d\geq c
for all
n\geq m
.

The next three Lemmas are easy remarks of modular arithmetic.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
B\geq 2
,
A\geq 1
,
B\nmid A
, then
-\left\lfloor-A/B\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor+1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
a,y\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq(ay)<C
, then
\left((ay)\bmod C\right)=a(y\bmod C)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
x\not\equiv(C-1)\pmod{C}
then
(x+1)\bmod C=(x\bmod C)+1
.

The next Lemma is the principal tool of this note.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A,B,C\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
A,B>0
,
C\geq 2
,
C\,|\,A
,
B\nmid A
,
B\bmod C\equiv a\mod C
,
a\not=0
and
|a|(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)<C
if
a<0
respectively
a+a(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)<C
if
a>0
.

(i)

If
a<0
, then

\displaystyle(A\bmod B)\bmod C=|a|(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C).

(ii)

If
a>0
, then

\displaystyle\left((-A)\bmod B\right)\bmod C=a(1+\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C).

Proof.

For both cases below we apply the Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
. For the second case we apply also
lemma

2.4
. We proceed with the cases.

(i)

a<0
:

\displaystyle(A\bmod B)\bmod C

\displaystyle=\left(A\bmod C-(B\bmod C)(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)\right)\bmod C

\displaystyle=\left(0-(-|a|)(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)\right)\bmod C

\displaystyle=\left(|a|(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)\right)\bmod C

\displaystyle=|a|(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)

(ii)

a>0
:

\displaystyle\left((-A)\bmod B\right)\bmod C

\displaystyle=\left((-A)\bmod C-(B\bmod C)(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)\right)\bmod C

\displaystyle=\left(0-a(\left\lfloor(-A)/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)\right)\bmod C

\displaystyle=(a\cdot(-\left\lfloor(-A)/B\right\rfloor\bmod C))\bmod C

\displaystyle=(a\cdot(1+\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C))\bmod C

\displaystyle=(a+a\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)\bmod C

\displaystyle=a(1+\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C).

∎

3
Applications to C-recursive sequences

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that a sequence
t:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
has the following representation for
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle t(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{c^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(c^{n})}{\tilde{B}(c^{n})}\right\rfloor\bmod c^{n}.

If the free term of
\tilde{B}(c^{n})
is
\alpha_{d}<0
, then there is some natural number
e
such that for all
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle t(n)=\frac{1}{|\alpha_{d}|}\left(\left((e^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(e^{n}))\bmod\tilde{B}(e^{n})\right)\bmod e^{n}\right).

If the free term of
\tilde{B}(c^{n})
is
\alpha_{d}>0
, then there is some natural number
e
such that for all
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle t(n)=-1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}}\left(\left((-e^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(e^{n}))\bmod\tilde{B}(e^{n})\right)\bmod e^{n}\right).

Proof.

In order to apply apply Lemma
2.5
, we recall that according to Corollary
2.1
there is a
c_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
e\geq c_{0}
and
for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle t(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{e^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(e^{n})}{\tilde{B}(e^{n})}\right\rfloor\bmod e^{n}.

Let
e\geq c_{0}
be a natural number to fulfill also other conditions, which will be stated below. We introduce the notations
A=e^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(e^{n})
,
B=\tilde{B}(e^{n})
and
C=e^{n}
and we prove that for a good choice of
e
, these numbers fulfill the conditions of Lemma
2.5
.

The conditions
C\,|\,A
and
B\nmid A
are always fulfilled. The conditions
A,B>0
,
C\geq 2
are fulfilled for all
n\geq 1
if
d
is sufficiently large, as the main coefficients of
\tilde{A}(x)
and
\tilde{B}(x)
are positive. Let
\alpha_{d}
be the free term of the polynomial
\tilde{B}(x)=x^{d}B(\frac{1}{x})
, meaning that
a=a_{d}
. For
d
sufficiently large,
B\bmod C\equiv\alpha_{d}\mod C
. By definition,
\alpha_{d}\neq 0
because
\deg B=d
. We have to show that for
e
sufficiently large,
|\alpha_{d}|(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)<C
if
\alpha_{d}<0
respectively
\alpha_{d}+\alpha_{d}(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)<C
if
\alpha_{d}>0
, for all
n\geq 1
. We recall that
\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C=t(n)
.

If
\alpha_{d}<0
, the inequality
|\alpha_{d}|(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)<C
reads
|\alpha_{d}|t(n)<e^{n}
and must be true for
n\geq 1
.

If
\alpha_{d}>0
, the inequality
\alpha_{d}+\alpha_{d}(\left\lfloor A/B\right\rfloor\bmod C)<C
reads
\alpha_{d}(t(n)+1)<e^{n}
and must be true for
n\geq 1
.

But we know from
2.1
that if
s:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a C-recursive sequence then there exists an
g\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|s(n)|<g^{n+1}
. As
s(n)=2|\alpha_{d}|t(n)
is itself a C-recursive sequence and its values are natural numbers, there is some positive
g\in\mathbb{N}
such that
2|\alpha_{n}|t(n)<g^{n+1}
for all
n\geq 1
. We can always find an
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
g^{n+1}<e^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

If
t(n)\geq 1
, as
2|\alpha_{d}|t(n)>|\alpha_{d}|t(n)
in the first case, respectively
2|\alpha_{d}|t(n)\geq|\alpha_{d}|(1+t(n))
in the second case, the conditions of Lemma
2.5
are fulfilled. If
t(n)=0
, the conditions are fulfilled for every
e\geq 1
in the first case and for every
e>\alpha_{d}
in the second case.

Finally we choose
e
sufficiently large such that
e\geq c_{0}
and all the conditions above are fulfilled.
∎

Below we put both cases in only one formula:

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose that a sequence
t:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
has the following representation for
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle t(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{c^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(c^{n})}{\tilde{B}(c^{n})}\right\rfloor\bmod c^{n}.

If the free term of
\tilde{B}(c^{n})
is
\alpha_{d}\neq 0
, there exists
e\geq c
such that the following holds for all
n\geq 1
:

\displaystyle t(n)=\frac{-1-\sign(\alpha_{d})}{2}+\frac{1}{|\alpha_{d}|}\left(\left((-\sign(\alpha_{d})\cdot e^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(e^{n}))\bmod\tilde{B}(e^{n})\right)\bmod e^{n}\right).

Remark 3.1
.

In
[
4
]
there is also an application to C-recursive sequences
t:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. According to Lemma
2.1
, for such a sequence there is a
h\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq 0
,
|t(n)|<h^{n+1}
. The sequence
s(n)=t(n)+h^{n+1}
has values in
\mathbb{N}
and is also C-recursive. Indeed, the generating function of
s
is a rational function and can be computed by:

f(z)+\frac{h}{1-hz},

where
f(z)
is the generating function of the sequence
t
. Consequently, the sequence
s
has a representation according to Corollary
3.1
, while the sequence
t
has the representation
t(n)=s(n)-h^{n+1}
.

In
[
4
]
, also the following
mod-div representation
is proved:
There exist
c
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that:

\forall\,n\geq n_{0}\,\,\,\,t(n)=\left\lfloor\frac{\left(c^{n(d-2)+\left\lceil n/2\right\rceil}+c^{n^{2}}\tilde{A}(b^{n})\right)\bmod\tilde{B}(c^{n})}{c^{n(d-1)}}\right\rfloor.

Open problem
: Is it possible to find a purely arithmetic trick which shows that the mod-div representation (or even an improved version) is only a corollary of the div-mod representation, in a similar way as the short-cut shown here?

Uncertainty Relation for the Wigner–Yanase Skew Information and Quantum Sobolev Inequalities
Laurent Lafleche

Abstract
This note explores uncertainty inequalities for quantum analogues of the Fisher information including the Wigner–Yanase skew information, and their connection to the quantum Sobolev inequalities proved by the author in ( Journal of Functional Analysis , 286 (10) 2024). Some additional inequalities concerning commutators are derived and others are left as open problems.

81S07; 46E35; 81S30 (47A30).

1
Quantum Skew Information and Variance

Let
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
be a density operator, that is, a positive compact operator, acting on the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of square-integrable functions, with
\LLtr\!\left({\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)=1
. The
quantum variance
of an observable
K
with respect to
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is given by

\sigma_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})^{2}=\LLtr(\left|{K}\right|^{2}{\boldsymbol{\rho}})-\left|{\LLtr\!\left(K\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)}\right|^{2},

where
\left|{K}\right|^{2}=K^{*}K
. It satisfies the famous Heisenberg(–Robertson) uncertainty inequality

\sigma_{A}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,\sigma_{B}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|{\LLtr\!\left(\left[{A,B}\right]{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)}\right|

which reduces to
\sigma_{x}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,\sigma_{\boldsymbol{p}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\geq\frac{d\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh}{2}
if
x
is the operator of multiplication by
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{p}}=-i\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh\nabla
. Here
\left[{A,B}\right]=A\cdot B-B\cdot A
is the commutator of
A
and
B
.

A quantum analogue of the Fisher information is the
Wigner–Yanase skew information

wigner_information_1963
with respect to a self-adjoint operator
K
, which is defined by

I_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\frac{1}{2}\LLtr\!\left(\left|{\left[{K,\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right]}\right|^{2}\right).

(1)

Another quantum analogue of the Fisher information, which makes it possible to obtain a quantum analogue of the Cramér–Rao inequality
helstrom_minimum_1967
when the parameter change is implemented through a Hamiltonian dynamics, is the
symmetric logarithmic derivative Fisher information
(see, e.g.,
holevo_probabilistic_2011
;
braunstein_statistical_1994
;
hayashi_two_2002
) defined by

J_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\LLtr\!\left(\left|{L_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\right|^{2}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)\quad\text{ with }\quad\frac{1}{2}\left(L_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}}){\boldsymbol{\rho}}+{\boldsymbol{\rho}}L_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\right)=\frac{1}{i}\left[{K,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right].

(2)

If
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is a compact positive operator which can be diagonalized in the form

{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=\sum_{j\in J}\lambda_{j}\,\ket{\psi_{j}}\bra{\psi_{j}}

for some set
J\subset\mathbb{N}
, where
(\psi_{j})_{j\in J}
are the eigenvectors of
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
and
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in J}
the corresponding eigenvalues, then the above quantities can be written

I_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in J}\Big|\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}-\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}\Big|^{2}\left|{\braket{\psi_{j}|K\psi_{k}}}\right|^{2}

and

J_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})=4\sum_{j,k\in J}\frac{\left|{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}}\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}}\left|{\braket{\psi_{j}|K\psi_{k}}}\right|^{2}

where the sum is over all the
j
and
k
such that
\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k}\neq 0
.

These quantum informations are smaller than the variance, and more precisely

\sigma_{K}^{2}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\geq\frac{1}{4}\,J_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\geq I_{K}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,,

with equality when
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is a pure state, i.e. a rank-one projection. As proved in
kosaki_matrix_2005
, the Heisenberg-like uncertainty inequality
\sqrt{I_{A}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,I_{B}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|{\LLtr\!\left(\left[{A,B}\right]{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)}\right|
does not hold for the Wigner–Yanase skew information, as was wrongly claimed in
luo_informational_2004
and later corrected in
luo_correction_2005
. Uncertainty principles for modifications of the skew information were investigated in
luo_heisenberg_2005
;
yanagi_wigneryanasedyson_2010
;
yang_generalized_2022
. In the case of the symmetric logarithmic derivative Fisher information (
2
), the analogue of the Cramér–Rao inequality follows directly from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for the trace
helstrom_minimum_1967
;
frowis_tighter_2015
and can be viewed as an improved Heisenberg uncertainty principle, since it reads

\sigma_{A}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{4}J_{B}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|{\LLtr\!\left(\left[{A,B}\right]{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right)}\right|.

The problem of finding a lower bound to
I_{A}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,I_{B}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})
was solved recently by the author in
lafleche_quantum_2024
in the case
\left(A,B\right)=\left(x,{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)=\left(x,-i\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh\nabla\right)
in dimension
d\geq 2
, and involves higher-order Schatten norms instead of the trace. Recalling the definition of Schatten norms of order
p\in[1,\infty)

\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{p}=\left(\LLtr\!\left(\left|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{p}\right)\right)^{1/p}

and setting
\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\infty}
to be the operator norm, the uncertainty inequality for the Wigner–Yanase information takes the following form.

Theorem 1.1
(
lafleche_quantum_2024
, Corollary 2.2)

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
be a compact operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{J_{x}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,J_{\boldsymbol{p}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\sqrt{I_{x}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,I_{\boldsymbol{p}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\frac{\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh}{8\pi C_{d}}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\frac{d}{d-1}}

where
\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2}\leq C_{d}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{8\pi}}
with
\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2}
the optimal constant in the Sobolev inequality
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, i.e.
(\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2})^{2}=\left(4\pi\right)^{-1/(2d)}\frac{\Gamma(d+1/2)^{1/d}}{d\left(d-1\right)\pi}
.

In dimension
d=1
, one can obtain a slightly weaker result assuming that
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is a density operator.

Theorem 1.2

Let
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
be a density operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then for any
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{J_{x}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,J_{\boldsymbol{p}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\sqrt{I_{x}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,I_{\boldsymbol{p}}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\frac{\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh}{8\pi C_{1/p^{\prime}}^{2p^{\prime}}}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{p}^{p^{\prime}}

with
C_{s}=\frac{1}{\left(8\pi\right)^{s/2}}+\frac{\pi^{\left(1-s\right)/2}}{2^{s}\sin(\pi s)^{1/2}\Gamma(s)\sqrt{s}}
.

Link with Quantum Sobolev Inequalities

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on semiclassical approximation techniques, since it is a special case of the quantum Sobolev inequalities derived in
lafleche_quantum_2024
. We also refer to
ruzhansky_sobolev_2025
, where these inequalities were recently proved in the closely related setting of noncommutative Euclidean spaces.

According to the correspondence principle of quantum mechanics, the following Table
1
of analogies allows one to pass from quantum to classical mechanics.

In particular,
\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}:=\frac{1}{i\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh}\left[{x,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right]
corresponds to the quantum analogue of

and

I_{x}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})
and

are, up to multiplicative constants,
\big\lVert\nabla_{v}\sqrt{f}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}
and

, which correspond to the classical Fisher information with respect to
x
and

. On the other hand, classical Sobolev inequalities tell us that for
d>1
,

\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{d-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2}\left\|{g}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2}\,\Big(\left\|{\nabla_{x}g}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}+\left\|{\nabla_{v}g}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Taking
g(x,v)=\sqrt{f(\lambda x,v/\lambda)}
in the above inequality, optimizing with respect to
\lambda
and squaring the inequality gives

2\,(\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,2})^{2}\,\big\lVert\nabla_{x}\sqrt{f}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\,\big\lVert\nabla_{v}\sqrt{f}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\geq\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{L^{\frac{d}{d-1}}}.

In the quantum setting, the analogue of Sobolev inequalities read for any compact operator
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\leq C_{d,s,p}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}

where the scaled Schatten norms
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}
and the quantum gradient
\boldsymbol{\nabla}
were defined according to the dictionary by

\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}:=h^{d/p}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{p}\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}:=\left(\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}{\boldsymbol{\rho}},\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\right),

so that
\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{2}=\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{2}+\left|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right|^{2}
. The case of fractional Sobolev spaces is treated as well in
lafleche_quantum_2024
.

A useful tool for the proof is the Wigner transform, which is the inverse of the Weyl quantization, and is defined in terms of the integral kernel
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(x,y)
of the operator
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
by

f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(x,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-iy\cdot v/\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh}\,{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(x+\tfrac{y}{2},x-\tfrac{y}{2})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\,.

It is an isometry from
\mathcal{L}^{2}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and satisfies
\nabla f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=f_{\boldsymbol{\nabla}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}
.

Proof
(Proof of Theorem
1.2
)

When
d=1
, the Hölder inequality for the trace and the fractional quantum Sobolev inequality
lafleche_quantum_2024
together with the fact that the Wigner transform is an isometry from
\mathcal{L}^{2}
to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
tell us that for any
q\in[2,\infty)
,

\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\leq C_{s}\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

with
s=1-2/q
and
C_{s}=\frac{1}{\left(8\pi\right)^{s/2}}+\frac{1}{2^{s}\,\pi^{s/2}}\frac{\Gamma(1-s)^{1/2}}{\Gamma(1+s)^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{\left(8\pi\right)^{s/2}}+\frac{\pi^{\left(1-s\right)/2}}{2^{s}\sin(\pi s)^{1/2}\Gamma(s)\sqrt{s}}
using the reflection formula for the Gamma function. By interpolation of
\dot{H}^{s}
spaces, or, in a more elementary way, using the Fourier transform and Hölder’s inequality, it gives

C_{s}^{-1}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\leq\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{s}\left\|{f_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1-s}=\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2/q}.

Applying this inequality to
\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}(\lambda x,\lambda y)
and optimizing with respect to
\lambda
leads to

C_{s}^{-2}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q/2}}\leq 2^{s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{s}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{s}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{1}}^{2/q}

which gives with
p=q/2

\frac{\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh}{4\pi C_{1/p^{\prime}}^{2p^{\prime}}}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{p}^{p^{\prime}}\leq\left\|{\left[{{\boldsymbol{p}},\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right]}\right\|_{2}\left\|{\left[{x,\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right]}\right\|_{2}\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{1}^{1/(p-1)}.

This gives the result by definition of the Wigner–Yanase skew information and since
\left\|{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{1}=1
.

3
Open Questions

We give here some remaining open questions concerning the Wigner–Yanase skew information and quantum Sobolev spaces.

3.1
Uncertainty Inequality

Concerning the uncertainty inequality for the Wigner–Yanase skew information, the above results only address the case
A=x
and
B={\boldsymbol{p}}
. A natural question is whether more general inequalities can be obtained for two non-commuting operators
A
and
B
. In dimension
d=1
, in the classical commutative case, if
z\mapsto\left(\alpha(z),\beta(z)\right)
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is a probability density such that
f\circ\Phi^{-1}
converges to
0
at infinity,

\frac{1}{2\,(\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,1})^{2}}\left\|{\left|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq\left\|{\{\alpha,\sqrt{f}\}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|{\{\beta,\sqrt{f}\}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(3)

where the brackets denote the Poisson brackets. Hence, in the operator setting with
d=1
, one would expect the existence of a constant
C>0
such that for any operators
A
and
B
associated with functions
\alpha
and
\beta
inducing a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism, and any density operators
{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
with some condition of compatibility with
A
and
B
,

C\left\|{\left|{\left[{A,B}\right]}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|{\left[{A,\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\left\|{\left[{B,\sqrt{{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}.

That is, one would expect that for a rather large class of operators,

\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{J_{A}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,J_{B}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq\sqrt{I_{A}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})\,I_{B}({\boldsymbol{\rho}})}\geq C\,\LLtr\!\left(\left|{\left[{A,B}\right]}\right|{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{2}\right)\,.

Proof
(Proof of Inequality (
3
))

Observe that under a change of variable induced by a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the Sobolev inequality gives for
p<2
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}

\left\|{\left|{\det\nabla\Phi}\right|^{1/q}f}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,p}\left\|{\left|{\det\nabla\Phi}\right|^{1/p}(\nabla\Phi)^{-1}\nabla f}\right\|_{L^{p}}.

In the case when
\Phi(z)=\left(\alpha(z),\beta(z)\right)
, notice that
\det\nabla\Phi=\{\alpha,\beta\}
, and
(\nabla\Phi)^{-1}\nabla g=\{\alpha,\beta\}^{-1}\left(-\{\beta,g\},\{\alpha,g\}\right)
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,p}}\left\|{\left|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right|^{\frac{1}{q}}f}\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\left\|{\left|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right|^{2/p}\{\alpha,\beta\}^{-2}\left(\left|{\{\beta,f\}}\right|^{2}+\left|{\{\alpha,f\}}\right|^{2}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p/2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1/2}.

In particular, if
p=1
and
q=2
, it gives

\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,1}}\left\|{\left|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\\
\leq\left\|{(\{\alpha,f\},\{\beta,f\})}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Using the triangle inequality, changing
\alpha
by
\lambda\alpha
and
\beta
by
\beta/\lambda
, and optimizing with respect to
\lambda
gives

\frac{1}{2\,(\mathcal{C}^{\textnormal{S}}_{1,1})^{2}}\left\|{\left|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}\leq\left\|{\{\alpha,f\}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\left\|{\{\beta,f\}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Since
\{\alpha,f\}=\{\alpha,\sqrt{f}\}\sqrt{f}
, the result follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the fact that
\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1
.

3.2
Bound for Commutators

The quantum Sobolev spaces are useful to prove that commutators are small in terms of
\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh
. It is for example proved in
lafleche_quantum_2024
that for any
p\in[1,\infty]

\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\|{\left[{e^{i\,\xi\cdot x},{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}}{\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh\left|{\xi}\right|}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

It also follows for instance from
potapov_operator-lipschitz_2011
that for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists
c_{p}>0
such that for any
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left\|{\left[{u(x),{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq c_{p}\,\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh\left\|{\nabla u}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!v}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

(4)

By conjugation by the Fourier transform, which is a unitary operator, one obtains similarly

\left\|{\left[{u(\nabla),{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|{\nabla u}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}_{\!x}{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

The cases
p\in\set{1,\infty}
are however known to be false, one needs slightly more regularity for
u
(see e.g.
aleksandrov_operator_2016
).

These two inequalities are special cases of analogues to the classical Hölder’s inequality, which gives for
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
and for two functions
(\alpha,\beta)\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\times W^{1,r}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

\left\|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left\|{\nabla_{x}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|{\nabla_{v}\beta}\right\|_{L^{r}}+\left\|{\nabla_{v}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|{\nabla_{x}\beta}\right\|_{L^{r}}.

Replacing
\alpha
by
\lambda\alpha
and
\beta
by
\lambda\beta
and optimizing with respect to
\lambda
gives

\left\|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right\|_{L^{p}}^{2}\leq 2\left\|{\nabla_{x}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|{\nabla_{v}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|{\nabla_{v}\beta}\right\|_{L^{r}}\left\|{\nabla_{x}\beta}\right\|_{L^{r}}.

This implies in particular that
\left\|{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq 2\left\|{\nabla_{z}\alpha}\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|{\nabla_{z}\beta}\right\|_{L^{r}}
, where
\nabla_{z}=(\nabla_{x},\nabla_{v})
. It would be useful to know whether the quantum generalization of such inequality holds, that is, if for
1<p,q,r<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
, there exists a constant
C
independent of
\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh
such that for any compact operators
A
and
B
,

\left\|{\left[{A,B}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C\,\mkern 1.5mu\mathchar 22\mkern-7.25muh\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}A}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{q}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}B}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{r}}.

This would generalize the “commutator-Lipschitz” inequality (
4
), which is the case when
A=u(x)
is a multiplication operator and
q=\infty
.

One can already observe that the following weaker inequality holds for
n>d

\left\|{\left[{A,B}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq C_{d,n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}^{n+1}\!A}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{d/n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}A}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{1-d/n}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}B}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}.

(5)

Proof
(Proof of Inequality (
5
))

By the formula for the Weyl quantization, with
z=(y,v)
and
e_{z}:=e^{2i\pi\left(x\cdot y+v\cdot{\boldsymbol{p}}\right)}
,

A=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\widehat{f}_{A}(z)\,e_{z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\,.

Therefore by the triangle inequality

\left\|{\left[{A,B}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big|\widehat{\nabla f_{A}}(z)\big|\,\frac{\left\|{\left[{e_{z},B}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}}{\left|{2\pi z}\right|}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\leq\big\lVert\widehat{f}_{\boldsymbol{\nabla}A}\big\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\sup_{z\neq 0}\frac{\left\|{\left[{e_{z},B}\right]}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}}{\left|{2\pi z}\right|}.

By the same argument as in
(
lafleche_quantum_2024
, Proof of Proposition 2.3)
, the last factor is bounded by
\left\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}B}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{p}}
. Therefore the result follows using the classical bound

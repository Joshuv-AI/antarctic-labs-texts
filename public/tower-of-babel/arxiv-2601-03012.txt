Many-electron systems with fractional electron number and spin: exact properties above and below the equilibrium total spin value
Yuli Goshen, Eli Kraisler

Abstract
Description of many-electron systems with a fractional electron number, N tot N_{\textrm{tot}} , and fractional z z -projection of the spin, M tot M_{\textrm{tot}} , is of great importance in physical chemistry, solid-state physics and materials science. In this work, we analyze the fundamental question of what is the ensemble ground state of a general, finite, many-electron system at zero temperature, with a given N tot N_{\textrm{tot}} and M tot M_{\textrm{tot}} , distinguishing between low- and high-spin cases (separated by the boundary spin M B M_{B} ). For the low-spin case, the general form of the ensemble ground state has been rigorously derived in J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 15 , 2337 (2024), generalizing the piecewise-linearity and the flat-plane conditions for many-electron systems. Here we provide an alternative proof for this case, discuss the ambiguity in the description of the ground state and show that this ambiguity can be removed via maximization of the system’s entropy. For the high-spin case, we find that the form of the ensemble ground state strongly depends on the system in question. We prove three general properties, which characterize the ground state at high spins and narrow down the list of pure states it may consist of. We illustrate the aforementioned properties of high-spin cases by examining the ensemble ground state when M tot M_{\textrm{tot}} approaches M B M_{B} from above, during addition of (a fraction of) an up- and down-electron to a given system. Furthermore, we relate the frontier orbital energies of Kohn-Sham (KS) density functional theory (DFT) to total energy differences at high spin values, particularly the ionization potential (IP), the fundamental gap and the spin flip energies. Analyzing the frontier energies on both sides of each boundary in the total energy profile, where the energy slope changes abruptly, we derive expressions for new derivative discontinuities, which are predicted to appear as jumps in the corresponding KS potentials. In this way, we generalize the well-known IP theorem of DFT to cases with fractional electron number and to cases with high spin. Our analytical results are supported by an extensive numerical analysis of the Atomic Spectra Database of the National Institute of Standards. The new exact conditions for many-electron systems derived in this work are instrumental for development of advanced approximations in DFT and other many-electron methods.

II.1
Ground state within Region (
1

In this Section, we provide an alternative derivation of the central proof of Ref.

Definitions.
Consider a system with a varying, possibly fractional, number of
\uparrow
- and
\downarrow

\hat{\Lambda}=\sum_{N=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\sum_{M=-N/2}^{N/2}\lambda_{N,M}\ket{\Psi_{N,M}}\bra{\Psi_{N,M}}.

(3)

Here

N

\{\lambda_{N\!M}\}

\lambda_{N\!M}\in[0,1]\>\>\>\forall N,M

(4)

(5)

Using Eq. (
3

\hat{O}

Outcomes: Piecewise-linearity.
The fact that the ground state can be expressed as in Eq. (
II.1
), with Eqs. (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
) as restrictions, has several direct outcomes. One of them is that the total energy is piecewise-linear in
N_{\textrm{tot}}
and independent of
M_{\textrm{tot}}
(Eq. (
19
)). Importantly, the same behavior applies not only to the energy, but actually to a large family of quantities: Any expectation value of an operator
\hat{P}
,
which is commutative with the ladder operator for the spin,
\hat{S}_{+}=\hat{S}_{x}+i\hat{S}_{y}
, is piecewise-linear in
\alpha
and is independent of
M_{\textrm{tot}}
, within Region (
1
):

P(N_{\textrm{tot}},M_{\textrm{tot}})=(1-\alpha)P_{N_{0}}+\alpha P_{N_{0}+1}.

(25)

To prove this statement, let us show that for pure states, the expectation value
P_{N,M}=\expectationvalue{\hat{P}}{\Psi_{N,M}}
is
independent
of
M
, as long as
M\in[-S_{\textrm{min}}(N),S_{\textrm{min}}(N)]
. First, recall that by definition, applying the spin ladder operators to a pure state yields

\hat{S}_{\pm}\ket{\Psi_{N,M \mp 1}}=C^{\pm}_{N,M}\ket{\Psi_{N,M}}

(26)

where
\hat{S}_{-}=\hat{S}_{+}^{\dagger}=\hat{S}_{x}-i\hat{S}_{y}
. It then follows that the state
\ket{\Psi_{N,M}}
is an eigenstate of the operator
\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}
.

Second, compare the expectation values
P_{N,M}
and
P_{N,M-1}=\expectationvalue{\hat{P}}{\Psi_{N,M-1}}
.
Expressing
\ket{\Psi_{N,M-1}}
and
\bra{\Psi_{N,M-1}}
using Eq. (
26
), we readily understand that
P_{N,M-1}
is proportional to
\langle\Psi_{N,M}|\hat{S}_{+}\hat{P}\hat{S}_{-}|\Psi_{N,M}\rangle
. More precisely,

P_{N,M-1}=\frac{\langle\Psi_{N,M}|\hat{S}_{+}\hat{P}\hat{S}_{-}|\Psi_{N,M}\rangle}{\langle\Psi_{N,M}|\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}|\Psi_{N,M}\rangle}

(27)

In case
[\hat{P},\hat{S}_{+}]=0
, we can interchange these two operators in the numerator. Then, acting with
\hat{S}_{+}\hat{S}_{-}
on
\ket{\Psi_{N,M}}
, which is an eigenstate of the latter operator, we release a constant; this is the same constant both in the numerator and the denominator. As a result, we find that
P_{N,M-1}=P_{N,M}
. In other words,
P_{N,M}
is
M
-independent; therefore, it can be denoted simply as
P_{N}
(which we already did in Eq. (
25
)).

Finally, when calculating the ensemble expectation value of
\hat{P}
via Eq. (
II.1
), using the
M
-independence property we just established, we get

\displaystyle P(N_{\textrm{tot}},M_{\textrm{tot}})=\Tr\{\hat{\Lambda}\hat{P}\}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{M=-S_{0}}^{S_{0}}\lambda_{N_{0},M}P_{N_{0}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{M=-S_{1}}^{S_{1}}\lambda_{N_{0}+1,M}P_{N_{0}+1}.

(28)

By pulling
P_{N_{0}}
and
P_{N_{0}+1}
out of the sums and using Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
), we arrive at the piecewise-linearity property of
P(N_{\textrm{tot}},M_{\textrm{tot}})
, i.e., Eq. (
25
).

Piecewise-linearity applies therefore to a variety of properties: Such is the total energy (in absence of a magnetic field, as discussed here), the electron density
n(\mathbf{r})
, the reduced density matrix,
\rho_{1}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
, the Green function
G(\mathbf{r}t,\mathbf{r}^{\prime}t)
and any property that depends only on spatial, and not on spin coordinates. However, this is not the case for the energy in presence of a magnetic field, or for the spin-density
n_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})
.

Outcomes: Ground-state non-uniqueness.

Another surprising outcome of Eq. (
II.1
) is the fact that the ensemble ground state may not be uniquely defined
24
. Indeed, the number of statistical weights
\lambda_{N\!M}
is
2(S_{0}+S_{1}+1)
, whereas the number of equalities that aim to determine them is three (Eqs. (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
)); in addition, there also exist the inequalities of Eq. (
4
). This means that in order to fully determine the ground state of a many-electron system, it is not enough to specify its total electron number and spin, or, equivalently, the number of up and down electrons.

Cases where no ambiguity in the ground state occurs are: (i) When
S_{0}=0
and
S_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
and
vice versa
, as the number
\lambda_{N\!M}
’s is three; (ii) When
N_{\uparrow}
is made fractional, but
N_{\downarrow}
is a constant integer and
vice versa
, namely the common case of ionizing a system by removing an electron from a specific spin channel. There, the ensemble state consists of two states only, with uniquely determined coefficients:

(1-\alpha)\outerproduct{\Psi_{N_0,S_0}}{\Psi_{N_0,S_0}}+\alpha\outerproduct{\Psi_{N_0+1,S_1}}{\Psi_{N_0+1,S_1}},

(29)

with
S_{1}=S_{0}\pm\frac{1}{2}
.
(iii) In any case, the total energy, the total density and any quantity
P
as in Eq. (
25
), are always uniquely defined. Different is the situation for
n_{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})
. Therefore, we may have the situation where there is a multitude of spin-densities
n_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})
and
n_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})
, which sum up to the same total density and yield the same total energy. We illustrate this below, taking as an example the C atom with fractional electron number.

Consider, therefore, the case where
N_{0}=5
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
S_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}
and
S_{1}=1
. The ensemble in this case consists of five terms, in accordance with Eq. (
II.1
), and its coefficients can be expressed as
\lambda_{N_{0},\pm\frac{1}{2}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\alpha)\pm y
,
\lambda_{N_{0}+1,\pm 1}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha-x)\pm\tfrac{1}{2}(M_{\textrm{tot}}-y)
and
\lambda_{N_{0}+1,0}=x
, where
x
and
y
remain undetermined. Assume now that all the pure state spin-densities
n^{\sigma}_{N\!M}(\mathbf{r})
are known; furthermore, in our specific case, the following relations hold:
n^{\sigma}_{N_{0},\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{r})=n^{\bar{\sigma}}_{N_{0},-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{r})
,
n^{\sigma}_{N_{0}+1,1}(\mathbf{r})=n^{\bar{\sigma}}_{N_{0}+1,-1}(\mathbf{r})
and
n^{\sigma}_{N_{0}+1,0}(\mathbf{r})=n^{\bar{\sigma}}_{N_{0}+1,0}(\mathbf{r})=\tfrac{1}{2}(n^{\uparrow}_{N_{0}+1,1}(\mathbf{r})+n^{\downarrow}_{N_{0}+1,1}(\mathbf{r}))
. Here
{\bar{\sigma}}
denotes the spin channel opposite to
\sigma
. The spin polarization, normally defined as
\zeta(\mathbf{r})=\frac{n_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})-n_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})}{n_{\uparrow}(\mathbf{r})+n_{\downarrow}(\mathbf{r})}

\displaystyle\zeta(\mathbf{r})=\frac{\frac{y}{S_{0}}(n^{\uparrow}_{N_{0},\frac{1}{2}}-n^{\downarrow}_{N_{0},\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{M_{\textrm{tot}}-y}{S_{1}}(n^{\uparrow}_{N_{0}+1,1}-n^{\downarrow}_{N_{0}+1,1})}{(1-\alpha)(n^{\uparrow}_{N_{0},\frac{1}{2}}+n^{\downarrow}_{N_{0},\frac{1}{2}})+\alpha(n^{\uparrow}_{N_{0}+1,1}+n^{\downarrow}_{N_{0}+1,1})}.

(30)

\zeta(\mathbf{r})
linearly depends on the parameter
y
and is independent of
x
. In Fig.

Second, setting the asymptotic value of the ensemble polarization, which in

Removing non-uniqueness. Maximal entropy.
Three ways to remove the aforementioned non-uniqueness of the ensemble ground state were discussed in Ref.
24
. For completeness, we mention them briefly below, and then suggest a new way for non-uniqueness removal: by maximization of the system’s entropy.

First, it is possible to introduce additional constraints on the system, in addition to Eqs. (
5
), (
7
) and (
8
).
One way is to constrain higher powers of
\hat{S}_{z}
. Although mathematically this is a possible route, which eventually fully determines the ground state, the physical meaning of constraining some high powers of
\hat{S}_{z}
remains obscure.

Second, it has been shown that the requirement that the deviation
\Delta S_{z}\equiv\sqrt{\langle\hat{S}_{z}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{S}_{z}\rangle^{2}}
is minimal,
always
removes the ambiguity in the ground state, irrespectively of the number of statistical weights, i.e., of
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
. Interestingly, for integer
N_{\textrm{tot}}
the resultant ground state reduces to a two-state ensemble, which, for a given
M_{\textrm{tot}}
, are the states with the closest values of
M
. Similarly, for a fractional
N_{\textrm{tot}}
, the ground state is comprised of three states, which correspond to three of the four vertices of the square enclosing the point
(N_{\textrm{tot}},M_{\textrm{tot}})
(cf. Refs.
13
;
84
and see the SM, Sec. II for a detailed derivation).

Third, in contrast to the previously mentioned approaches, introduction of an external magnetic field
B
, although making the pure state energies
E_{N\!M}

M
-dependent, does not always remove the non-uniqueness of the ensemble state. To remove non-uniqueness, the undetermined statistical weights have to appear in the
B
-dependent ensemble energy, which can then be minimized by a proper choice of these weights, thus fully determining the ground state. This is, however, not always the case, with the C atom serving as a counter-example.

In this work, we suggest a new way to uniquely define the ground state: by maximizing the electronic entropy of the system. For a system described by the ensemble
\hat{\Lambda}
of Eq. (
3
), the von Neumann entropy equals

\mathscr{S}=-\Tr{ \hat{\Lambda} \ln\hat{\Lambda} }=-\sum_{N}\sum_{M}\lambda_{N\!M}\ln(\lambda_\NM).

(31)

We are looking therefore for such weights
\lambda_{N\!M}
that
\mathscr{S}
is maximal, and conditions (
4
), (
5
), (
7
) and (
8
) hold. We satisfy Condition (
4
) by setting

\lambda_{N\!M}=\sin^{2}\theta_{N\!M},

(32)

with
0\leqslant\theta_{N\!M}\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2}
, and the other three conditions via a constrained minimization with Lagrange multipliers
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
and
t_{3}
:

\displaystyle\delta\left[\mathscr{S}+t_{1}\left(\sum_{N\!M}\lambda_{N\!M}-1\right)+t_{2}\left(\sum_{N\!M}\lambda_{N\!M}N-N_{\textrm{tot}}\right)+\right.

\displaystyle\left.+t_{3}\left(\sum_{N\!M}\lambda_{N\!M}M-M_{\textrm{tot}}\right)\right]=0

(33)

Substituting Eqs. (
31
) and (
32
) into Eq. (
II.1
) and taking a partial derivative with respect to each
\theta_{N\!M}
, we reach the condition that each
N
and
M
must satisfy:

\displaystyle\sin(2 \theta_\NM)\left[1+\ln\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{N\!M}\right)-t_{1}-t_{2}N-t_{3}M\right]=0.

(34)

For a given
N
and
M
, this condition can be satisfied in three cases: (i) If
\theta_{N\!M}=0
, which means, however, that
\lambda_{N\!M}=0
(Eq. (
32
)).
This case cannot maximize the entropy: the maximum entropy ensemble must include every allowed pure state (see the SM, Sec. III for a proof).
(ii) If
\theta_{N\!M}=\tfrac{\pi}{2}
. But in this case
\lambda_{N\!M}=1
, which means that all the other
\lambda
’s vanish (Eq. (
5
)). This is a pure state with entropy 0. Finally, (iii) If we satisfy
\ln\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{N\!M}\right)=t_{1}-1+t_{2}N+t_{3}M
, namely

\displaystyle\lambda_{N\!M}=e^{(t_{1}-1)+t_{2}N+t_{3}M},

(35)

we reach maximal entropy.

Taking into account that our ensemble
\hat{\Lambda}
is given by Eq. (
II.1
), i.e. the only nonzero weights are
\lambda_{N_{0},-S_{0}},\cdots,\lambda_{N_{0},S_{0}}
and
\lambda_{N_{0}+1,-S_{1}},\cdots,\lambda_{N_{0}+1,S_{1}}
, we realize that Eq. (
35
) implies that

\displaystyle\lambda_{N_{0},M}=az^{M}\>\>\>\textrm{and}\>\>\>\lambda_{N_{0}+1,M}=bz^{M},

(36)

where
z=e^{t_{3}}
,
a=e^{t_{1}-1+t_{2}N_{0}}
and
b=ae^{t_{2}}
.
The parameters
a
,
b
and
z
can be found by explicitly satisfying Eqs. (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
).

To show that the ground state is uniquely defined by requiring maximal entropy, we need to find
a
,
b
and
z
and show they are unique. To this end, we define

\displaystyle F(z,S)=\sum_{M=-S}^{S}z^{M}=\frac{z^{S+1/2}-z^{-(S+1/2)}}{z^{1/2}-z^{-1/2}}.

(37)

As a result, Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
) yield the expressions
a=(1-\alpha)/F(z,S_{0})

and
b=\alpha/F(z,S_{1})
; the value of
z
has to be determined from Eq. (
23
), which will then determine all three parameters.

To employ Eq. (
23
), we first notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{M=-S}^{S}Mz^{M}=z\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}=

\displaystyle=F(z,S)\cdot\left[(S+\tfrac{1}{2})\frac{z^{S+1/2}+z^{-(S+1/2)}}{z^{S+1/2}-z^{-(S+1/2)}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z^{1/2}+z^{-1/2}}{z^{1/2}-z^{-1/2}}\right]

(38)

Substituting the above relation, as well as the expressions for
a
and
b
into Eq. (
23
), we find that
z
must satisfy the expression

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)(S_{0}+\tfrac{1}{2})\frac{z^{S_{0}+1/2}+z^{-(S_{0}+1/2)}}{z^{S_{0}+1/2}-z^{-(S_{0}+1/2)}}+\alpha(S_{1}+\tfrac{1}{2})\frac{z^{S_{1}+1/2}+z^{-(S_{1}+1/2)}}{z^{S_{1}+1/2}-z^{-(S_{1}+1/2)}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{z^{1/2}+z^{-1/2}}{z^{1/2}-z^{-1/2}}=M_{\textrm{tot}}

(39)

For
z\in(0,\infty)
, the left-hand side (lhs) of Eq. (
39
) is a continuous, differentiable function, and its derivative is always positive (see the SM, Sec. IV for details). In the limit
z\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the lhs of Eq. (
39
) approaches
-M_{B}
(see Eq. (
2
)) and in the limit
z\rightarrow\infty
it approaches
M_{B}
. Therefore, for a given value of
M_{\textrm{tot}}\in(-M_{B},M_{B})
(i.e., within Region (
1
)), there exists one and only one solution
z\in(0,\infty)
to Eq. (
39
).

As a result,
a
and
b
are also uniquely defined. Therefore, the requirement of maximal entropy (
31
) defines the ensemble ground state (
II.1
) of a many-electron system
uniquely
, strictly within Region (
1
).
At the edges (
M_{\textrm{tot}}=\pm M_{B}
), there is only one ground state anyhow, and hence no ambiguity to be removed.

The unique ensemble coefficients we have now derived, necessarily provide the global maximum of the entropy, rather than a local extremum; the entropy
\mathscr{S}
is bounded from above, hence it must have a global maximum. By requiring that the
\lambda_{N\!M}
result in maximum entropy, we have arrived at a unique ensemble, which thus must be the
global
maximizer of the entropy.

and
\downarrow

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}=\frac{\partial E}{\partial f_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}}=\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial N_{\sigma}}\right)_{N_{\bar{\sigma}}},

(53)

assuming that a change in
N_{\sigma}
results in a change of occupation in only one KS level. Using the chain rule, we find that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial N_{\sigma}}\right)_{N_{\bar{\sigma}}}=\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial N_{\textrm{tot}}}\right)_{M_{\textrm{tot}}}\left(\frac{\partial N_{\textrm{tot}}}{\partial N_{\sigma}}\right)_{N_{\bar{\sigma}}}+

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial M_{\textrm{tot}}}\right)_{N_{\textrm{tot}}}\left(\frac{\partial M_{\textrm{tot}}}{\partial N_{\sigma}}\right)_{N_{\bar{\sigma}}}.

We define

\sigma=\uparrow

-1
for
\sigma=\downarrow
, and arrive at the relation

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}=\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial N_{\textrm{tot}}}\right)_{M_{\textrm{tot}}}+\frac{d_{\sigma}}{2}\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial M_{\textrm{tot}}}\right)_{N_{\textrm{tot}}}.

(55)

Moving to the “center-of-mass system” for the ho-levels, we note that
\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\uparrow}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\downarrow})=\left({\partial E}/{\partial N_{\textrm{tot}}}\right)_{M_{\textrm{tot}}}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\uparrow}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\downarrow}=\left({\partial E}/{\partial M_{\textrm{tot}}}\right)_{N_{\textrm{tot}}}
.

Strictly within
|M_{\textrm{tot}}|<M_{B}
, the total energy is piecewise-linear in
N_{\textrm{tot}}
and does not depend on
M_{\textrm{tot}}
(Eq. (
19
)). As a direct consequence, we found in Ref.

that both ho energy levels coincide and equal the negative of the IP of the
(N_{0}+1)
-system:

\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\uparrow}=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\downarrow}=-I.

(56)

Outside the trapezoids,
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}
are not equal anymore, but they are constant, with respect to
N_{\textrm{tot}}
and
M_{\textrm{tot}}
, within each tile: Differentiating Eq. (
II.2
), with

taken to be the energy, Eq. (

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}=\frac{1}{D}\bigg[(M_{2}-M_{3})E_{1}+(M_{3}-M_{1})E_{2}+(M_{1}-M_{2})E_{3}-

\displaystyle-\frac{d_{\sigma}}{2}\left((N_{2}-N_{3})E_{1}+(N_{3}-N_{1})E_{2}+(N_{1}-N_{2})E_{3}\right)\bigg],

(57)

being independent of
N_{\textrm{tot}}
and
M_{\textrm{tot}}
. Expressions for
\frac{1}{2}(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\uparrow}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\downarrow})
and
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\uparrow}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\downarrow}
readily follow.

In the case of Li discussed above, in the transition Tr

\Delta_{\textrm{Tr}\rightarrow\textrm{A}}^{\downarrow}=(\varepsilon_{1s}^{\downarrow})_{\textrm{A}}-(\varepsilon_{1s}^{\downarrow})_{\textrm{Tr}}=-(J_{1,2}-J_{1,2}^{\mathrm{KS}})

\downarrow
-homo to the

-lumo, which is exactly what

represents for Li

In the transition A
\rightarrow

\Delta_{\textrm{A}\rightarrow\textrm{B}}^{\downarrow}=0

\Delta_{\textrm{A}\rightarrow\textrm{B}}^{\uparrow}=(\varepsilon_{2p}^{\uparrow})_{\textrm{B}}-(\varepsilon_{2p}^{\uparrow})_{\textrm{A}}=J_{1,3}-J_{1,2}-(J_{1,3}^{\mathrm{KS}}-J_{1,2}^{\mathrm{KS}})

\Delta^{\sigma}
derived here to the well-known derivative discontinuity
\Delta=E_{g}-(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lu}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}})

\Delta
is the difference between the fundamental gap of the interacting system and its KS analog.

So far we considered the transitions Tr
\to

is strictly fractional. For integer

Furthermore, at points on the boundary between two tiles, the values of

4

VI
Discussion and conclusions

In this article, we analyzed the fundamental question of what is the ensemble ground state of a general, finite, many-electron system, with given, possible fractional values of the electron number and the spin. We addressed both the case of low spin, namely when
M_{\textrm{tot}}
is within Region (
1
), and the complementary case of high spin.

When the spin is within Region (
1
), the exact ensemble form for the ground state has been found in Ref.
24
. In Sec.
II.1
we provide an alternative derivation for this result.
Furthermore, we discuss the question of the ambiguity in the ensemble ground state, exemplify it by analyzing the spin polarization
\zeta(\mathbf{r})
and show that the requirement of maximum entropy (
31
) defines the ensemble ground state uniquely. Notably, maximizing the entropy leads to a ground state that is generally different from the one obtained by minimizing
\Delta S_{z}
or by applying a small magnetic field.

It is worth mentioning that since we are dealing with systems at zero temperature, namely with solving the quantum-mechanical problem for the many-electron Hamiltonian, without invoking thermal ensembles, the requirement of maximal entropy is not compulsory. Strictly speaking, it is external to the theoretical framework in which the problem has been formulated. Nonetheless, entropy maximization is useful to remove the ground state ambiguity, as we showed; other ways exist, as well. Still, when looking at many-electron systems at finite temperature, our task would be to minimize the free energy
F=E-T\mathscr{S}
, which in the limit
k_{B}T\rightarrow 0^{+}
, would mean to minimize
E_{\textrm{tot}}
and then maximize
\mathscr{S}
, as performed in Sec.
II.1
.

In contrast to the situation within Region (
1
), for the high-spin case, the form of the ensemble ground state strongly depends on the system in question. In Sec.
II.2
we succeeded to prove three general properties, which characterize the unknown ground state and narrow down the list of pure states it may consist of. These properties are: (1) For
M_{\textrm{tot}}>M_{B}
, the ensemble consists only of pure states which have
M\geqslant S_{\textrm{min}}(N)
.
(2) The ensemble consists of at most three pure states.
(3) Near the boundary
M_{\textrm{tot}}=M_{B}
, with
N_{\textrm{tot}}
strictly fractional, two of the pure states in the ensemble are known, and the third depends on the particular system, but is independent of
\alpha
.
To illustrate the properties of high-spin ensembles close to the boundary
M_{\textrm{tot}}=M_{B}
, in Sec.
III
we discussed in detail two particular cases:
S_{1}=S_{0}+\frac{1}{2}
(addition of an up electron to an
N_{0}
-electron system) and
S_{1}=S_{0}-\frac{1}{2}
(addition of a down electron). We considered common scenarios for
\ket{\Psi_\star}
, compared their energies and connected the latter to the IPs, fundamental gaps and spin-flip energies of these systems. Lastly, using the examples we developed above, we illustrated how the case of accidental degeneracy mentioned in Sec.
II.2
could emerge. All the aforementioned results are general properties of many-electron systems, and are not related to a specific method chosen for their description.

Next, in Sec.
IV
we focused on exact KS-DFT and related the KS frontier eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}
to total energy differences, particularly the IP and the spin flip energies. The Li atom served as a useful illustration. In this way, in Eqs. (
56
), (
58
)–(
61
), we derived an extension of the well-known IP theorem to systems with any
N_{\textrm{tot}}
and
M_{\textrm{tot}}
.
Furthermore, when crossing the boundary between any two tiles in the total energy profile, either one or both of
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}
jump discontinuously. This is accompanied by a derivative discontinuity
\Delta^{\sigma}
, which manifests as a jump in the corresponding potential
v_{\mathrm{KS}}^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})
.
These new exact conditions for the orbital energies
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ho}}^{\sigma}
, discontinuities
\Delta^{\sigma}
and potentials
v_{\mathrm{KS}}^{\sigma}(\mathbf{r})
should be obeyed by approximate xc functionals in DFT and will be useful for the development of advanced approximations.

In Sec.
V
, we performed an extensive analysis of existing experimental and computational data
46
, from which we obtained the pure state energies
E_{N\!M}
in atomic systems. Relying on this analysis, we created the ensemble maps for various atomic systems, which show the states that contribute to the ensemble ground state, for given
N_{\textrm{tot}}
and
M_{\textrm{tot}}
, in practice. We found that the ensemble maps consist of trapezoids and triangles of different types. Particularly, the ensemble is not always comprised of states that belong to the smallest square enclosing the ensemble point
(N_{\uparrow},N_{\downarrow})
, both for low and high spin. Furthermore, discontinuities may appear at any integer or fractional
N_{\uparrow}
and/or
N_{\downarrow}
, in one of the spin channels or in both; reproducing these exact properties is a significant challenge for xc approximations of the next generation.
Next, we performed a statistical analysis as to the occurrence of the third state
(N_{\star},M_{\star})
, when close to the boundary spin
M_{B}
. Lastly, we examined the convexity of the discrete pure state energy function,
E_{N\!M}
. We found that
E_{N\!M}
is nearly always a convex function (with the exception of Fe
7+
). Particularly, this supports the assumption of convexity with respect to
N
, to
M
and to
N_{\sigma}
. Notably,
in Ref.
30
,

M
-convexity proved useful for investigation of spin migration in atomic systems.

The new exact, general properties of many-electron systems with a varying, possibly fractional, electron number and varying spin, both in the low- and high-spin regions, derived in this work, are useful both in assessment of computational results obtained with DFT or other many-electron methods and instrumental for development of advanced approximations to the many-electron problem.

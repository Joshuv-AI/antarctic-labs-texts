Convex combination of first and second eigenvalues of trees
Hitesh Kumar, Bojan Mohar, Shivaramakrishna Pragada, Hanmeng Zhan

Abstract
For a graph G G , let λ 1 ​ ( G ) \lambda_{1}(G) and λ 2 ​ ( G ) \lambda_{2}(G) denote the largest and the second largest adjacency eigenvalue of G G . The sum λ 1 ​ ( G ) + λ 2 ​ ( G ) \lambda_{1}(G)+\lambda_{2}(G) is called the spectral sum of G G . We investigate the spectral sum of trees of order n n and determine the extremal trees that achieve maximum/minimum. Moreover, for any α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] \alpha\in[0,1] , we determine the extremal trees which maximize the convex combination α ​ λ 1 + ( 1 − α ) ​ λ 2 \alpha\lambda_{1}+(1-\alpha)\lambda_{2} in the class of n n -vertex trees.

adjacency matrix, spectral sum, spectral gap, spectral center, double comets

Lemma 2.14

\lambda_{2}(T)<\lambda_{1}(H_{i})\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{n}{2}-1+\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}}{4}-3n+13}\bigg)}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}}

for

. So let
H_{i}
be a star for

. Then

T\in\left\{DC\left(\frac{n-2}{2},\frac{n-2}{2},2\right),DC\left(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-2}{2},3\right),DC\left(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-4}{2},4\right)\right\}.

By the choice of
T
, we have
T=DC(\frac{n-2}{2},\frac{n-2}{2},2)
. Using Proposition
2.4
,

\lambda_{2}(T)=\sqrt{\frac{n-1-\sqrt{2n-3}}{2}}<\sqrt{\frac{n-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Fix
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
and assume
n
is sufficiently large (depending on
\alpha
). Let
T^{*}\in\mathcal{T}(n)
be such that
\Psi(T,\alpha)=\Psi_{n}(\alpha)
. We denote
\lambda_{1}(T^{*})
and
\lambda_{2}(T^{*})
by
\lambda_{1}^{*}
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}
, respectively.

We know that
(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}+(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}\leq n-1
. Define
f(\lambda,\alpha)=\alpha\sqrt{n-1-\lambda^{2}}+(1-\alpha)\lambda
(imagine
\lambda
is
\lambda_{2}^{*}
). The derivative of
f
w.r.t.
\lambda
is given by

\frac{df}{d\lambda}=\frac{-\alpha\lambda}{\sqrt{n-1-\lambda^{2}}}+(1-\alpha).

We see that
\frac{df}{d\lambda}\geq 0
if and only if

\lambda\leq\sqrt{\frac{(n-1)(1-\alpha)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}}}.

(1)

The right-hand side in (
1
) is a decreasing function in
\alpha
, and equals
\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}
when
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
. We conclude that for any fixed
0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}
,
f(\lambda,\alpha)
is an increasing function in
\lambda
in the interval
[0,\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}]
.

Now, by the choice of
T^{*}
and Corollary
2.3
, we have

f(\lambda_{2}^{*},\alpha)\geq\alpha\lambda_{1}^{*}+(1-\alpha)\lambda_{2}^{*}\geq\alpha\lambda_{1}(H)+(1-\alpha)\lambda_{2}(H)=f(\lambda_{2}(H),\alpha),

where
H=DC(\lfloor\frac{n-3}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n-3}{2}\rceil,3)
. Since
\lambda_{2}(H)\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}
by Corollary
2.3
, we conclude that
\lambda_{2}(T^{*})\geq\lambda_{2}(H).
We consider the following cases:

Case 1:

n
is odd. Using Theorem
1.2
,
T^{*}
is a
\lambda_{2}
-maximizer, and so

T^{*}\in\left\{DC\left(\frac{n-3}{2},\frac{n-3}{2},3\right),DC\left(\frac{n-3}{2},\frac{n-5}{2},4\right),DC\left(\frac{n-5}{2},\frac{n-5}{2},5\right)\right\}.

Using Kelmans Operation Lemma
2.10
, we have
T^{*}\cong H
.

Case 2:

n
is even. Using Proposition
3.1
, we have
T^{*}\in\left\{DC(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-4}{2},4),DC(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-2}{2},3)=H\right\}
.

Using Taylor’s Theorem and Corollary
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\Psi(H,\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{\frac{n-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}}+(1-\alpha)\sqrt{\frac{n-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{8(n-1)}}\right)+(1-\alpha)\left(\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{8(n-1)}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}+(2\alpha-1)\frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{8(n-1)}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

Let
T=DC(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-4}{2},4)
. Using Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\Phi(T,x)=x^{n-4}\left(x^{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}-x^{n-6}\left(x^{2}-\frac{n-4}{2}\right)^{2}.

Note that
\Phi(T,\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2}})<0
. Moreover, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
n
, we have
\Phi(T,x)>0
for all
x\geq\sqrt{\frac{n-2+\varepsilon}{2}}
. It follows that
\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2}}\leq\lambda_{1}(T)\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-2+\varepsilon}{2}}
. Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(T)=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2}}
. A similar argument shows that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{2}(T)=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi(T,\alpha)=\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{8(n-1)}}+O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

Now,
\Psi(H,\alpha)>\Psi(T,\alpha)
whenever
(2\alpha-1)\sqrt{5}>-1
, i.e.,
\alpha>\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2\sqrt{5}}
. And if
\alpha<\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2\sqrt{5}}
, then
\Psi(H,\alpha)<\Psi(T,\alpha)
.
∎

Now, in the case
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, we can determine the extremal tree for all
n
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

If
n\leq 10
, we verified the assertion using computer. So assume
n\geq 10
. From the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we know that
T^{*}=DC(\frac{n-3}{2},\frac{n-3}{2},3)
when
n
is odd, and
T^{*}\in\left\{DC(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-4}{2},4),DC(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-2}{2},3)\right\}
when
n
is even. Say
T=DC(\frac{n-4}{2},\frac{n-4}{2},4)
and

. We would like to show that
\Psi(H,\frac{1}{2})>\Psi(T,\frac{1}{2})
. If
n<20
, we verified this using a computer. So assume now that
n\geq 20
.

By Corollary
2.3
, the spectral sum of

is given by

\Psi\left(H,\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{n-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{n-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}}.

Using Lemma

\Phi(T,x)=x^{n-4}\left(x^{2}-\frac{n-2}{2}\right)^{2}-x^{n-6}\left(x^{2}-\frac{n-4}{2}\right)^{2}.

It is easy to check that

for all

Using the Interlacing Theorem, we get

For

, we have

\Psi\left(T,\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{2}}<\sqrt{\frac{n-1+\sqrt{5}}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{n-1-\sqrt{5}}{2}}=\Psi\left(H,\frac{1}{2}\right)

The proof is complete.
∎

4.1
Special comets
D_{n}^{t}
and
C_{n}^{t}

We first define some special double comets and determine their spectral sums. Fix
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
t=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}}
,
q=\frac{(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}(2\alpha-1)}{\alpha^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}}
and assume
n
is sufficiently large (depending on
\alpha
). Define

\displaystyle D_{n}^{t}

\displaystyle=DC(\lceil t(n-3)\rceil,\lfloor(1-t)(n-3)\rfloor,3);

\displaystyle C_{n}^{t}

\displaystyle=DC(\lceil t(n-3)\rceil+1,\lfloor(1-t)(n-3)\rfloor,2).

Let
\varepsilon=\lceil t(n-3)\rceil-t(n-3)
and
d=1+\varepsilon-2t
.

Proposition 4.1
.

We have

(i)

\Psi(D_{n}^{t},\alpha)=\sqrt{\left(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}\right)(n-1)}+\frac{qd}{8(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right),

(ii)

\Psi(C_{n}^{t},\alpha)=\sqrt{\left(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}\right)(n-1)}+\frac{q}{8(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{t}{2t-1}+d\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(D_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(n-1+\sqrt{\left((n-3)(2t-1)+2\varepsilon\right)^{2}+4}\right)}.

By Taylor’s Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\sqrt{\left((n-3)(2t-1)+2\varepsilon\right)^{2}+4}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left((n-1)(2t-1)+2d\right)^{2}+4}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left((n-1)(2t-1)\right)^{2}+4d^{2}+4d(2t-1)(n-1)+4}

\displaystyle=(2t-1)(n-1)+\frac{c}{2(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{8\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)

where
c=4d^{2}+4d(2t-1)(n-1)+4=4d(2t-1)(n-1)\pm O(1)
. Thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(D_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{t(n-1)+\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{16\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)}.

Again, using Taylor’s Theorem, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(D_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{t(n-1)}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t(n-1)}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{16\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{8(t(n-1))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(D_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{16\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{8((1-t)(n-1))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\Psi(D_{n}^{t},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{t(n-1)}+(1-\alpha)\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{8(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{(1-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{\alpha}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}\right)(n-1)}+\frac{qd}{8(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

This proves
(i)
. The proof of assertion
(ii)
is analogous. For details, see the Appendix.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Fix
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and let
t=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}}
. Let
T^{*}\in\mathcal{T}(n)
be such that
\Psi(T^{*},\alpha)=\Psi_{n}(\alpha)
.
Throughout this section,
\lambda_{1}^{*}
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}
will denote
\lambda_{1}(T^{*})
and
\lambda_{2}(T^{*})
, respectively. Let
x
denote the unit positive
\lambda_{1}^{*}
-eigenvector and
y
denote a unit
\lambda_{2}^{*}
-eigenvector of
T^{*}
with minimal support. Let
(H_{1},a)
and
(H_{2},b)
be rooted subtrees of
T^{*}
w.r.t.
y
as given in Theorem
2.7
.

We will prove Theorem
1.6
in a series of claims. Here’s an outline of the proof.

Proof outline.
We first show that
\lambda_{1}^{*}\approx\sqrt{tn}
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}\approx\sqrt{(1-t)n}
.
We use this to find a vertex
u_{1}
with
\deg(u_{1})\approx tn-O(\sqrt{n})
and a vertex
u_{2}
with
\deg(u_{2})\approx(1-t)n-O(\sqrt{n})
. This would imply that all other vertices have degree
O(\sqrt{n})
and small eigen-entries in the eigenvectors
x
and
y
. With further bootstrapping, we argue that
\deg(u_{1})\approx tn-O(1)
and
\deg(u_{2})\approx(1-t)n-O(1)
. This enables us to show that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
have large eigen-entries in
x
and
y
. Finally, using several graph transformations, we will show that
T^{*}
has to be the unique tree
DC(tn+C,(1-t)n-C-2,2)
, where
C
is some constant (depending on
\alpha
), completing the proof.

We begin the proof by estimating the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}^{*}
and
\lambda_{2}^{*}
.

Claim 4.1
.

We have

(i)

\lambda_{1}^{*}=\sqrt{t(n-1)}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)
,

(ii)

\lambda_{2}^{*}=\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
, we know that

\alpha\lambda_{1}^{*}+(1-\alpha)\lambda_{2}^{*}\geq\sqrt{(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2})(n-1)}-O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

Define
f(x)=\alpha x+(1-\alpha)\sqrt{n-1-x^{2}}
(imagine
x=\lambda_{1}^{*}
). Clearly,
f(\lambda_{1}^{*})\geq\alpha\lambda_{1}^{*}+(1-\alpha)\lambda_{2}^{*}.
Hence, in order to find bounds for
\lambda_{1}^{*}
, we need to solve the inequality

f(x)\geq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}}-O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

It is an elementary exercise to show that the above inequality holds whenever

x=\sqrt{t(n-1)}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

This proves
(i)
. Assertion
(ii)
can be proved similarly by solving the following inequality (imagine
x=\lambda_{2}^{*}
this time):

\alpha\sqrt{n-1-x^{2}}+(1-\alpha)x\geq\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}}-O\left(\frac{1}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right).\qed

We now find two vertices with large degrees in
T^{*}
.

Claim 4.2
.

There exists
u_{1}\in V(H_{1})
and
u_{2}\in V(H_{2})
such that

\deg(u_{1})\geq tn-O(\sqrt{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\deg(u_{2})\geq(1-t)n-O(\sqrt{n}).

Moreover, if
u\notin\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, then
\deg(u)=O(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

Consider the
\lambda_{1}^{*}
-eigenvector
x
of
T^{*}
. Let
u_{1}\in V(T^{*})
be such that
x_{\max}=x_{u_{1}}
. Using Lemma
2.14
, we get

\displaystyle\deg(u_{1})x_{\max}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}x_{\max}-\sum_{d(u,w)=2}x_{w}

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}x_{\max}-\sum_{w\neq u_{1}}x_{\max}\frac{\deg(w)}{\lambda_{1}^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}x_{\max}-x_{\max}\frac{2n}{\lambda_{1}^{*}},

which implies

\deg(u_{1})\geq(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}-\frac{2n}{\lambda_{1}^{*}}\geq tn-O(\sqrt{n})

using Claim
4.1
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
u_{1}\in V(H_{1})\cup S^{0}(y)
.

Now, let
z
be a unit positive
\lambda_{1}(H_{2})
-eigenvector of
H_{2}
. Choose
u_{2}\in V(H_{2})
such that
z_{\max}=z_{u_{2}}
. Using Lemma
2.14
and following the same steps as above, we get

\deg(u_{2})\geq\deg_{H_{2}}(u_{2})\geq\lambda_{1}^{2}(H_{2})-\frac{2|H_{2}|}{\lambda_{1}(H_{1})}.

Since
\lambda_{1}(H_{1})\geq\lambda_{2}^{*}
using Theorem
2.7
, we get

\deg(u_{2})\geq(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}-\frac{2n}{\lambda_{2}^{*}}\geq(1-t)n-O(\sqrt{n}),

where the last inequality holds by Claim
4.1
.

Now, if
u_{1}\in S^{0}(y)
, then
|H_{1}|=O(\sqrt{n})
, which implies

\sqrt{(1-t)n}-O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\leq\lambda_{2}^{*}\leq\lambda_{1}(H_{1})\leq O(n^{\frac{1}{4}}),

a contradiction. Thus
u_{1}\in V(H_{1})
. This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

Next, we show that the eigenvector
y
attains maximum and minimum at
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
, respectively.

Claim 4.3
.

We have
y_{\max}=y_{u_{1}},y_{\min}=y_{u_{2}}
and
y_{u_{1}}>y_{u}>y_{u_{2}}
for any vertex
u\notin\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in V(H_{1})
be such that
y_{\max}=y_{u}
. Using Lemma
2.14
, we have

\displaystyle\deg(u)y_{\max}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}y_{\max}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(w,u)=2\\
w\in S^{+}(y)\end{subarray}}y_{w}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(w,u)=2\\
w\in S^{-}(y)\end{subarray}}y_{w}

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}y_{\max}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d(w,u)=2\\
w\in S^{+}(y)\end{subarray}}y_{w}

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}y_{\max}-\sum_{d(w,u)=2}y_{\max}\frac{\deg(w)}{\lambda_{2}^{*}}

\displaystyle\geq(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}y_{\max}-y_{\max}\frac{2n}{\lambda_{2}^{*}},

which implies
\deg(u)\geq(1-t)n-O(\sqrt{n})
using Claim
4.1
. By Claim
4.2
, the only vertex in
H_{1}
with degree linear in
n
is
u_{1}
. It follows that
u=u_{1}
.

Using a similar argument, we can show that
y_{\min}=y_{u_{2}}
. It is clear from the proof that any vertex
u
with
y_{u}\in\{y_{\max},y_{\min}\}
must have large degree and therefore must lie in
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. In other words, if
u\notin\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
, then
y_{\max}>y_{u}>y_{\min}
.
∎

In the next few claims, we estimate the entries of

and

Claim 4.4
.

If
u\notin\{u_{1},u_{2}\}

\max\{x_{u},|y_{u}|\}=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

, we have

\displaystyle|y_{u}|

\displaystyle=\frac{|\sum_{d(w,u)=2}y_{w}|}{(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}-\deg(u)}\leq\frac{\sum_{d(w,u)=2}|y_{w}|}{(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}-\deg(u)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{d(w,u)=2}\frac{\deg(w)}{\lambda_{2}^{*}}}{(\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}-\deg(u)}\leq\frac{2n}{\lambda_{2}^{*}\left((\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2}-\deg(u)\right)}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

where the last inequality follows using Claims
4.1
and

. A similar argument works for the eigenvector
x
.
∎

We further optimize the estimates for the degrees of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
.

Claim 4.5
.

We have
\deg(u_{1})=tn\pm O(1)
and
\deg(u_{2})=(1-t)n\pm O(1)
. Moreover,
\deg(u)=O(1)

Consider the unit positive
\lambda_{1}(H_{2})

. By the choice of

. Now, using Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(H_{2})\left((\lambda_{1}(H_{2}))^{2}-\deg_{H_{2}}(u_{2})\right)z_{\max}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in N_{H_{2}}(u_{2})}z_{u}(\deg_{H_{2}}(u)-1)+\sum_{d(u_{2},u)=3}z_{u}

.

Claim 4.6
.

There exists an absolute constant
\delta>0
such that
\min\{x_{u_{1}},|y_{u_{2}}|\}>\delta
.

Proof.

Since
x
is a unit vector, we have

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq x_{u_{1}}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in N(u_{1})\\
\deg(v)=1\end{subarray}}x_{v}^{2}+x_{u_{2}}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in N(u_{2})\\
\deg(w)=1\end{subarray}}x_{w}^{2}+\sum_{u\notin N[u_{1}]\cup N[u_{2}]}x_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq x_{u_{1}}^{2}\left(1+\frac{tn}{(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}}\right)+x_{u_{2}}^{2}\left(1+\frac{(1-t)n}{(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4x_{\max}^{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right),

implying
x_{u_{1}}=x_{\max}\geq\sqrt{\frac{0.99}{4}}
. Similarly,
\max\{y_{u_{1}},|y_{u_{2}}|\}\geq\sqrt{\frac{0.99}{4}}
.

As
x
and
y
are orthogonal vectors, we have

\left|x_{u_{1}}y_{u_{1}}\left(1+\frac{tn}{(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}}\right)-x_{u_{2}}|y_{u_{2}}|\left(1+\frac{(1-t)n}{(\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}}\right)\right|=O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

(2)

If
|y_{u_{2}}|\geq y_{u_{1}}
, then choose
\delta=\sqrt{\frac{0.99}{4}}
and we are done. Else,

|y_{u_{2}}|\geq x_{u_{1}}y_{u_{1}}-O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\geq\sqrt{\frac{0.99}{4}}\sqrt{\frac{0.99}{4}}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{0.98}{4}.

Choose

Claim 4.7

S^{0}{y}\backslash\{v\}
and attaching a path at
v
so that
|T|=|T^{\#}|
. Using the Hanging Path Lemma, we see that
\lambda_{1}(T)<\lambda_{1}(T^{\#})
. Clearly,
\lambda_{2}(T)=\lambda_{1}(T-v)=\lambda_{2}^{\#}
. Thus,
T
has smaller spectral sum, a contradiction. Thus,
H
is a path, i.e.,
T^{\#}
is a subdivision of
K_{1,3}
with central vertex
v
. We have
\lambda_{1}(H_{i})\geq\lambda_{1}(H-v)
, which implies If
|H_{i}|\geq|H-v|
for
i=1,2
.

If
n\geq 60
, then
|H_{i}|\geq 20
. Let
T
denote the tree obtained from
P_{27}=v_{1}\ldots v_{27}
by attaching a leaf at the vertex
v_{14}
. Clearly,
T
is an induced subgraph of
T^{\#}
, and so
\lambda_{1}^{\#}+\lambda_{2}^{\#}\geq\lambda_{1}(T)+\lambda_{2}(T)>4
,
a contradiction.

When
19\leq n<60
, we verify the assertion computationally by considering all subdivisions of
K_{1,3}
on
n
vertices (see
here
for the code).
∎

Claim 6.4
.

If
T^{\#}
has a spectral edge
ab
, then
T^{\#}
is a path.

Proof.

In light of Claim
6.2
, the vertices of degree 3 in
T^{\#}
belong to the set
\{a,b\}
.
Now, denote by
k
(resp.
\ell
) the order of the longest path starting from
a
(resp.
b
) and not containing
b
(resp.
a
). Without loss of generality, let
\ell\geq k
. Since
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are paths by Claim
6.2
, we have

\lambda_{1}(P_{2k-1})\geq\lambda_{1}(H_{1})>\lambda_{1}(H_{2}-b)\geq\lambda_{1}(P_{\ell-1}),

which implies
k\geq\frac{\ell}{2}
.

Assume
n\geq 60
. Then
n\leq 2k+2\ell\leq 6k
, implies

. If
\deg(a)=\deg(b)=3
, then the tree
T
obtained from the path

by attaching a leaf at vertex
v_{8}
and
v_{9}

. Since

has spectral sum more than 4 (checked using SageMath), we get a contradiction. If

Concluding remarks

In this paper, we investigated the spectral sum and the convex combination of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
of trees. However, similar questions for graphs remain open; for instance, the following conjecture by Ebrahimi, Mohar, Nikiforov, and Ahmady
[
11
]
.

Conjecture 7.1

(
[
11
]
, cf. Conjecture 17 in
[
1
]
)
.

For any graph
G
of order
n
, we have

\lambda_{1}(G)+\lambda_{2}(G)\leq\frac{8n}{7}.

We also believe that the ideas used in this paper may be useful in proving Conjecture
1.3
. There is an analogous conjecture for the spectral gap of graphs by Stánic
[
21
]
which is wide open.

Finally, it’s a natural problem to investigate the quantity
\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}(n)}\Psi(T,\alpha)
for
\alpha\in[0,1]
. The answer is known for
\alpha=0
,
\alpha=1
(Theorem
1.1
), and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
(Theorem
1.8
). We pose the following problem.

Problem 7.2
.

Suppose
n
is sufficiently large. Let
T\in\mathcal{T}(n)
be such that

\Psi(T,\alpha)=\min_{T\in\mathcal{T}(n)}\Psi(T,\alpha).

Is it true that

(i)

T
is a path if
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}
, and

(ii)

T
has at most one vertex of degree more than 2 if
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
?

Appendix

Fix
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Let
t=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}}
,
q=\frac{(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}(2\alpha-1)}{\alpha^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}}
and assume
n
is sufficiently large (depending on
\alpha
). Let
\varepsilon=\lceil t(n-3)\rceil-t(n-3)
and
d=1+\varepsilon-2t
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1

(ii)
.

Using Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(C_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(n-1+\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}-4\left(t(n-3)+1+\varepsilon\right)\left((1-t)(n-3)-\varepsilon\right)}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left(n-1+\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}-4\left(t(n-1)+d\right)\left((1-t)(n-1)-1-d\right)}\right)}

Using Taylor’s Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\sqrt{(n-1)^{2}-4\left(t(n-1)+d\right)\left((1-t)(n-1)-1-d\right)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{((2t-1)(n-1))^{2}+4(1+d)t(n-1)+4d(1+d)-4d(1-t)(n-1)}

\displaystyle=(2t-1)(n-1)+\frac{c}{2(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{8\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right),

where

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=4(1+d)t(n-1)+4d(1+d)-4d(1-t)(n-1)=4t(n-1)+4d(2t-1)(n-1)\pm O(1).

Thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(C_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{t(n-1)+\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{16\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{t(n-1)}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t(n-1)}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{16\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{8(t(n-1))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(C_{n}^{t})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}-\frac{c^{2}}{16\left((2t-1)(n-1)\right)^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{8((1-t)(n-1))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\Psi(C_{n}^{t},\alpha)

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{t(n-1)}+(1-\alpha)\sqrt{(1-t)(n-1)}

\displaystyle\quad\,+\frac{1}{8(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{(1-t)^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{\alpha}{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\left(\frac{c}{4(2t-1)(n-1)}\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}\right)(n-1)}+\frac{q}{8(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(\frac{t}{2t-1}+d\right)\pm O\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

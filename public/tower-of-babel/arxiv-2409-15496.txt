Continuous Variable Quantum Key Distribution with Single Quadrature Measurement at Arbitrary Reference Frame
Vinod N. Rao, Emma Tien Hwai Medlock, Timothy Spiller, Rupesh Kumar

Abstract
We propose a simplified measurement scheme for a Gaussian modulated coherent state (GMCS) protocol for continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CVQKD), utilizing homodyne detection without quadrature switching. The reference frame of measurement is taken to be at an arbitrary angle, however, reconciliation converges the proposed scheme to GMCS with switching quadrature protocol - GG02 Grosshans and Grangier 2002 . The arbitrary frame of measurement could also include the unknown random thermal drift within Bob’s optical measurement setup. We found this scheme is advantageous for practical free-space and fibre-based GMCS protocol based CVQKD systems as it does not require a phase modulator for random measurement selection quadrature at Bob.



I
Introduction

Quantum key distribution (QKD) enables two parties to share a secret key, in the presence of an eavesdropper
Bennett and Brassard 1984
. The advantage of QKD over the classical counterpart is that the latter relies on the computational hardness of a problem for security. Whereas, in QKD, information-theoretic security is provided, which is proven by the laws of physics
Gisin et al. 2002
;
Pirandola et al. 2020
.
The security of QKD relies on no-go theorems, such as, the no-cloning theorem
Wootters and Zurek 1982
, impossibility in perfectly distinguishing non-orthogonal states
Ivanovic 1987
, monogamy of entanglement
Horodecki et al. 2009
, the uncertainty principle among two non-commuting observable
Koashi 2006
and so on. The earlier proposals
Bennett and Brassard 1984
;
Ekert 1991
;
Bennett 1992
and demonstrations
Marand and Townsend 1995
;
Hughes et al. 2000
;
Stucki et al. 2002
;
Wang et al. 2005
for QKD were based on discrete variables (DV) using weak coherent states, based on polarization or phase qubits and single photon detectors. Recently, newer protocols were developed and demonstrated for longer transmission distances
Lo et al. 2012
;
Lucamarini et al. 2018
;
Wang et al. 2022
.

Continuous variable (CV) QKD protocols
Ralph 1999
;
Hillery 2000
;
Reid 2000
utilize amplitude and phase modulation of coherent states to encode the secure key information and shot noise limited detection for decoding. Squeezed as well as thermal states based protocols were also proposed for implementing CVQKD protocols
Ralph 1999
. Among the various CVQKD protocols, Gaussian Modulated Coherent State (GMCS) protocol
Grosshans and Grangier 2002
;
Braunstein and Van Loock 2005
, has been thoroughly studied for its security and demonstrated over fibre as well as free-space channels. The amplitude and phase of the coherent state are modulated such that the field quadratures follow the Gaussian probabilistic distribution.

There are two variants of the GMSC protocol based on the measurement of the quadratures. In switching quadrature protocol (‘GG02’ -
Grosshans and Grangier 2002
), Bob randomly selects the quadrature for the measurement using a homodyne detector. In the non-switching version of the GG02 protocol
Weedbrook et al. 2004
, Bob measures both quadratures with a Heterodyne detector. The non-switching protocol has higher information decoding capacity for shorter distances
Andersen et al. 2010
;
Soh et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2019
, while the switching protocol is more suitable for long distances CVQKD demonstrations
Jouguet et al. 2013
;
Huang et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2020
. At the detectors, the relative phase of
0^{\circ}
or
90^{\circ}
, between the CVQKD signal with a strong reference signal - referred to as the Local Oscillator (LO), sets the reference frame for the quadrature measurement.

In theoretical analysis, the reference frames of state preparation at Alice and that of the state measurement at Bob are assumed to be fixed and known to Eve. To avoid Eve replacing the coherent states with squeezed states, either a random selection of the quadrature or measuring both of them at the same time is necessary. However, in practice, the frame of measurement is continuously rotated at an arbitrary rate. Measurement of quadrature along the arbitrary frame of reference has been already proposed in
Soh et al. 2015
, however this is a quadrature switching version with the homodyne detector. We propose the arbitrary frame of measurement scheme without switching quadratures and using a homodyne detector. We consider the measurement frame of reference to be dynamic during the protocol run. The initial angle, the rate of rotation and the direction of the rotation of the frame of measurement are taken to be random as well. We also assume that over the protocol run, the frame of measurement could be rotated to all the possible angles.

In this paper, we aim to validate that the GG02 protocol can be performed using homodyne detection and without actively switching the quadratures as originally proposed and demonstrated over the last two decades. We prove our scheme retains the characteristics of GG02 protocols for CVQKD and does not create any new security loopholes. Additionally, our proposed scheme works with the setup of Local LO as well. The paper is structured as follows. The section
II
provides the outline of Gaussian modulated CVQKD protocol GG02. In section
III
, we describe the arbitrary rotation of the frame of reference in Bob’s measurement and prove its equivalence to GG02. Finally, we discuss our results in section
IV
and conclusion in
V
.

II
Gaussian Modulated Coherent State Protocol

The GMCS protocols utilise coherent states,
\ket{\alpha}=\ket{X_{A}+iP_{A}}
, as the information carriers, wherein the state quadratures,
X_{A}
and
P_{A}
, are randomly modulated by Alice such that the quadratures follow Gaussian distribution,
\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbb{V}_{A})
, of variance
\mathbb{V}_{A}
and mean at zero. The coherent states undergo attenuation and noise is added to it during the transmission from Alice to Bob. At Bob, the quadratures of the modulated coherent states,
X_{B}
and
P_{B}
, are measured with a shot noise (vacuum noise) limited homodyne (switching protocol) or heterodyne detector (non-switching protocol). The variance of the measurement
V_{B}=T*V_{A}+Z
, in which
T
is the transmittance of the channel and
Z
is the total noise variance added to the quadratures.

During the classical post-processing, Alice publicly announces the quadratures
X_{A}
and
P_{A}
and the variance
V_{A}
of a portion of transmitted states, for Bob to quantify Eve’s information from the measured quadratures
X_{B}
and
P_{B}
and their variance
V_{B}
. The rest of the data is used for secure key generation. For switching protocol Bob has to disclose the quadrature he measures (not the measurement outcomes) as well, that forms the sifting. The noise variance term
Z
comprises shot noise variance, electronic noise variance of the detector, noise from eavesdropping among others. One can also assume that the channel transmittance,
T
, is also controlled by Eve.

The feature that prevents single quadrature measurement by Bob is the following. By knowing the frame of Bob’s measurement, Eve can launch the Intercept and Resend (IR) attack, during which she can resend a suitable squeezed state along the frame of quadrature measurement and hides her presence. Even if Bob’s selection of quadrature for the measurement (among
X_{B}
and
P_{B}
) is unknown to Eve, she has
50\%
chance to hide her attack with squeezed states. To prevent Eve from using a squeezed state, one has either to switch between the quadrature measurements or measure both quadratures, as described in GG02 protocol
Grosshans and Grangier 2002
or its no-switching version
Weedbrook et al. 2004
, respectively.

In the case of an entangled cloner attack, in which Eve entangles one mode of her two-mode squeezed states with the coherent state sent by Alice and waits for Bob to announce the frame of his quadrature measurement (switching protocol), to initiate her measurement on the other state stored in the quantum memory. In no-switching protocol, Eve does not need to wait for Bob’s announcement. However, as in the earlier case, the action of entangling one mode of Alice with her own increases the variance at measurement
Lupo 2020
.

From the practical point of view, the frame of the measurement at Bob is set by the reference signal called the Local Oscillator (LO). The relative phase
\theta
of LO at Bob with respect to the phase of LO at Alice during the coherent state preparation defines the frame of measurement. In the ideal case, with
\theta=0^{\circ}
measures the
X
quadrature while
\theta=90^{\circ}
measures the
P
quadrature. However, in practice the frame of measurement,
\theta
, is in constant drift due to thermal fluctuations within Bob’s measurement setup. This phase rotation is unknown to Bob until it is estimated. Eve can probe this phase drift with a very high intense beam but there are countermeasures to prevent such attacks
Gisin et al. 2006
. Also high-intensity probe beams saturate the measurements and can be detected
Qin et al. 2016
. The phase drift creates an additional post-processing routine as either Bob or Alice has to rotate their frame of reference to compensate for the drift and match reference frames with each other.

In the case where Bob actively compensates for phase drift in real-time, a single quadrature measurement enables Eve to launch IR attack as the frame of measurement at Bob matches with that of state preparation at Alice. But, in the case where Alice compensates her quadrature data for the phase drift during the post-processing period and thereby matches with Bob’s frame of measurement, a single quadrature measurement can be implemented without switching the quadrature. In the next section, we provide evidence that no switching GMCS protocol with single quadrature measurement at an arbitrary frame of measurement is equivalent to GG02 protocol.

IV
Discussions

The GMCS CVQKD enables Alice and Bob to share secret keys using the amplitude and phase modulated coherent states. Bob randomly measures the quadrature to prevent Eve from replacing the coherent states with a squeezed state or another coherent state. Here, we have proposed a modified scheme, wherein the measurement of a single quadrature is done along an arbitrary angle
\theta
in the phase space. Bob, estimates the the angle
\theta
and later, during the classical data processing, discloses it over the classical channel to Alice. By knowing the angle of rotation of the frame of measurement, Alice can apply an equivalent counter rotation, -
\theta
, and deduct a single quadrature value that is with the measured quadrature value at Bob. The security of this single quadrature measurement scheme lies in the fact that neither Alice nor Eve is aware of the arbitrary angle,
\theta
, of measurement during the coherent states transmission period. In the simplest case of intercept and resend attack
Bennett et al. 1992
, Eve necessarily would have to squeeze the quadrature of the intercepted signal along an arbitrary angle which will be shown to be observed by Bob as excess noise.

We consider the case of Eve’s entangled cloner attack, wherein she entangles her ancillary mode to the coherent state sent by Alice to Bob and only measures after the public announcement of the measurement basis, here the angle
\theta
, by Bob. In our scheme, Eve still has to wait for Bob to announce the angle of rotation to apply her measurement on the ancillary mode. This is equivalent to the original GG02 protocol where Bob discloses the basis of his quadrature measurements.

We showed, using EB scheme, that the reduction in entanglement between mode-1 and mode-2 respectively of Alice and Bob is correlated with the difference between the variances of the modes, as given in Eq. (
6
). Thus, any such attack by Eve would change the variance of mode-2, and which is quantifiable for estimating the information leakage to Eve.

Another possible attack by Eve would be having multimode entanglement with mode-2, instead of using TMSVS of Eq. (
3
). In this case, Eve would necessarily use an entangled cloner of high dimensionality, so that she can measure along all the possible values of
\theta
. This would be similar to Eve using multimode in the case of a typical GMCS QKD protocol and measure along both the quadratures. This would enable her to get the information before Bob’s announcement of the measurement quadrature (
\theta
in our case). However, the footprint of Eve’s entanglement in mode-2 is inevitable, as in the quantification of variance in Eq. (
6
). Thus, any entanglement from Eve would necessarily increase the variance during Bob’s measurement. The primary advantage of using multimode entanglement would be to overcome the hurdle of waiting for Bob’s announcement.

The primary assumption in our scheme is that Bob needs an arbitrary angle of rotation in his frame of reference, which Eve has no knowledge of it beforehand. This is similar to the assumption of having random measurement of one of the quadrature, which Eve could not possibly influence. Therefore, the proposed protocol is as feasible and secure as GMCS protocol
Grosshans and Grangier 2002
.

The proposed protocol is different from unidimensional CV QKD protocols
Usenko and Grosshans 2015
;
Usenko 2018
;
Wang et al. 2018
;
Bai et al. 2020
, in that, the state preparation in these protocols involve modulation of a single quadrature. Whereas, the Alice setup in our case remains to be identical to a GG02 GMSC protocol, and thereby, the underlying security being proven from the uncertainty relation between the two quadratures.

In the GMCS protocol with homodyne measurement, for the case when Bob always measures in single quadrature and later announces the choice, Eve’s success probability to hide her attack is 50%. One can think of a similar attack in the present case as well, wherein Eve too randomly chooses her rotated frame of measurement to be an arbitrary angle. But this is a non-optimal attack, as Eve’s success rate reduces with the increase in the resolution of the phase space of Bob’s apparatus. Ideally, the probability to detect Eve’s presence is

P_{Bob}=\frac{1}{N\{i\}}\sum_{i}p_{i},

(11)

where

is the respective probability of the phase space resolutions and
N\{i\}
is the resolution dependent normalization. We note that Eve is unaware of Bob’s angle of rotation in frame of reference (and the phase drift), until Bob’s public announcement of the same. Let the arbitrary angle of measurement by Eve is
\theta_{E}
and that of Bob is
\theta_{B}
. Thus, only at the angle
\theta_{B}=\theta_{E}
,
p_{j}=0
. As
\absolutevalue{\theta_B - \theta_E}
increases, Eve’s information decreases and Bob’s probability to detect her increases. Below we quantify the normalized information that Eve would get and the corresponding probability of Bob finding about Eve’s presence.

V
Conclusions

The current work limits its arguments and results to the Gaussian distribution-based protocol. The necessity of switching between the two quadratures is relaxed in a homodyne detection scheme and the protocol is shown to be identical to GG02 protocol
Grosshans and Grangier 2002
. The announcement of the angle of rotation by Bob is similar to that of announcement of chosen quadrature in the latter protocol. Thus the proposed modification simplifies the protocol for CV QKD systems that have homodyne detection setting as it overcomes the necessity of a randomly varying phase modulator at Bob’s station. This is highly beneficial for practical implementation, especially in free-space CVQKD with Gaussian modulation protocol.

Appendix B
Quantifying Eve’s information

Given Alice prepares TMSVS and keeps one mode to herself while sending another to Bob, we find that the commutation relation between them would be,

[\hat{x}^{j},\hat{x}^{k}]=2i\Omega^{jk},

(14)

where
\Omega=\oplus\{\{0,1\},\{-1,0\}\}
is a 4x4 standard anti-symmetric matrix, and
\hat{x}=(\hat{q_{A}},\hat{p_{A}},\hat{q_{B}},\hat{p_{B}})^{\text{T}}
is the displacement vector. When the covariance matrix is of the form

\displaystyle V=\begin{pmatrix}A&C\\
C^{\text{T}}&B\end{pmatrix},

(15)

with
A=A^{\text{T}}
and
B=B^{\text{T}}
, the symplectic eigenvalues are given by,

\nu_{\pm}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu\pm\sqrt{\mu^{2}-4\penalty\ (\text{det}V)}}{2}}

(16)

where
\mu:=(\text{det}A)+(\text{det}B)+2(\text{det}C)
and
\text{det}(\cdot)
is the determinant. Thus, the von Newmann entropy of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{AB}
is given by
S_{AB}=g(\nu_{+})+g(\nu_{-})
, where

g(\cdot)=\bigg(\frac{\cdot+1}{2}\bigg)\log_{2}\bigg(\frac{\cdot+1}{2}\bigg)-\bigg(\frac{\cdot-1}{2}\bigg)\log_{2}\bigg(\frac{\cdot-1}{2}\bigg).

Similarly, we find
S_{A\mid B}
by the symplectic eigenvalues of the covariance matrix
\Sigma^{\prime}_{A\mid B}
, taking the partial measurements on the covariance matrix of Eq. (
15
). In particular, for a homodyne detection at Bob’s station, we define the partial measurement of mode-2 transforming mode-1 as,

\Sigma^{\prime}_{A\mid B}=A-C(\Pi_{x}\penalty\ B\penalty\ \Pi_{x})^{-1}C^{\text{T}},

(17)

where
\Pi_{x}=\{\{\cos{\theta},0\},\{0,\sin{\theta}\}\}
being the measurement setting of Bob and
(\cdot)^{-1}
indicating the pseudoinverse. Thus, we find the symplectic eigenvalue
\nu
of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{A\mid B}
, and
S_{A\mid B}=g(\nu)
.

Appendix C
Generalized case

All the earlier cases involves Alice measuring in standard quadratures and Bob in his rotated frame of measurement. However, in principle, Alice too might measure in an arbitrary rotated angle and later establish the correlation of the angle with Bob. This would be a general case of Eq. (
1
), wherein the measurement of TMSVS is
\theta_{A}
for mode-1 and
\theta_{B}
for mode-2. The covariance matrix for such a system is

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}&V1\kern-2.5pt\text{l}_{2}+\frac{(V+1)}{2}R_{1}^{\prime}&\sqrt{V^{2}-1}P_{1}\\
&\sqrt{V^{2}-1}P_{1}^{\text{T}}&V1\kern-2.5pt\text{l}_{2}+\frac{(V+1)}{2}R_{1}\end{pmatrix},

(18)

where
R_{1}^{\prime}=\{\{0,\sin{\theta_A}\},\{\sin{\theta_A},0\}\}
,
P_{1}=\{\{CS,-\sin{\theta_A}\},\{-\sin{\theta_B},-1\}\}
and
R_{1}=\{\{0,\sin{\theta_B}\},\{\sin{\theta_B},0\}\}
, with
CS=\cos{\theta_A}\cos{\theta_B}-\sin{\theta_A}\sin{\theta_B}
. The consequent joint system of ABE would be similar to Eq. (
13
), but with different variances. However, after the announcement of
\theta_{B}
and Alice rotating her frame to match, the respective mutual information and leaked information would be identical to the earlier case.

The stability threshold for 2D MHD equations around Couette with general viscosity and magnetic resistivity
Fei Wang, Zeren Zhang

Abstract

Introduction

In this paper, we study the nonlinear stability of the Couette flow
(y,0)
in a uniform magnetic field
(\beta,0)
for the 2-D MHD equations on the domain
(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}-\tilde{b}\cdot\nabla\tilde{b}-\nu\Delta\tilde{u}+\nabla\tilde{p}=0,\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{b}+\tilde{u}\cdot\nabla\tilde{b}-\tilde{b}\cdot\nabla\tilde{u}-\mu\Delta\tilde{b}=0,\\
\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}=\nabla\cdot\tilde{b}=0,\\
\tilde{u}|_{t=0}=\tilde{u}^{in},\quad\tilde{b}|_{t=0}=\tilde{b}^{in},\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{u}
is the velocity,
\tilde{b}
is the magnetic field,
\tilde{p}
is the pressure,
\nu
is the fluid viscosity, and
\mu
is the magnetic resistivity.
Let
u=\tilde{u}-(y,0),b=\tilde{b}-(\beta,0)
be the perturbation of velocity and magnetic field. We introduce the vorticity and the current density

\omega=\nabla^{\bot}\cdot u,\ j=\nabla^{\bot}\cdot b,

and then
(\omega,j)
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\omega+y\partial_{x}\omega-\beta\partial_{x}j-\nu\Delta\omega=\mathrm{NL}^{\omega},\\
\partial_{t}j+y\partial_{x}j-\beta\partial_{x}\omega-\mu\Delta j+2\partial_{xy}\phi=\mathrm{NL}^{j},\\
\omega|_{t=0}=\omega^{in},\quad j|_{t=0}=j^{in},\\
u=\nabla^{\bot}\psi,\quad b=\nabla^{\bot}\phi,\\
\psi=\Delta^{-1}\omega,\quad\phi=\Delta^{-1}j,\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}^{\omega}:

\displaystyle=-u\cdot\nabla\omega+b\cdot\nabla j,

\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}^{j}:

\displaystyle=-u\cdot\nabla j+b\cdot\nabla\omega+2\partial_{xy}\phi(\omega-2\partial_{xx}\psi)-2\partial_{xy}\psi(j-2\partial_{xx}\phi).

We are concerned with the stability threshold problem in the Sobolev space and formulate it as
[
3
]
:

Stability threshold
: Given
N\geq 0
, determine
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\|(\omega^{in},j^{in})\|_{H^{N}}\ll\nu^{\gamma_{1}}\mu^{\gamma_{2}}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{stability},

\displaystyle\|(\omega^{in},j^{in})\|_{H^{N}}\gg\nu^{\gamma_{1}}\mu^{\gamma_{2}}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{possible\ instability}.

To quantify the stability threshold is an active area which is initiated first for fluid dynamics, i.e., for the Navier-Stokes (NS) equations. For the NS equations, magnetic resistivity
\mu
is missing, and hence the above threshold for the stability becomes
\nu^{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\geq 0
. Bedrossian, Germain, and Masmoudi proved the threshold
\gamma=\frac{3}{2}

[
3
]
if the perturbation for velocity belongs to Sobolev space
H^{9/2+}
in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. Then
\gamma=1
in Sobolev space
H^{2}
had been confirmed by Wei and Zhang
[
30
]
. A lower
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}

[
10
]
was obtained in
H^{2}
for the 2D flow near Couette, which had been improved to
\gamma=\frac{1}{3}

[
27
,
31
]
. In
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
23
]
, the authors proved that
\gamma=1
on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
and
\gamma=0
on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
for the perturbation in Gevrey class. For the threshold problem in a bounded domain, Chen, Wei, and Zhang
[
13
]
proved
\gamma=1
in a finite channel
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]\times\mathbb{T}
with non-slip boundary condition and slip boundary condition in
H^{2}
. The result
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
held in a finite channel
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
with non-slip boundary condition
[
12
]
.
For slip boundary condition in
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
, the authors in
[
12
,
6
]
showed
\gamma=\frac{1}{2}
, which has been improved to
\gamma=\frac{1}{3}

[
32
]
. Very recently, the stability on
\mathbb{T}\times[-1,1]
in the Gevrey class had been proved in
[
7
,
8
]
. For stability results of general shear flow, we refer to
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

.

The problem becomes more complicated for MHD equations, due to the presence of magnetic fields. On the one hand, the magnetic field weakens the mixing mechanism of Couette flow. Especially if viscosity is larger than resistivity, inviscid damping gets counteracted by algebraic growth for specific time regimes
[
21
,
22
]
. On the other hand, a strong magnetic field has a stabilizing effect
[
20
,
26
,
34
]
. Liss
[
26
]
proved the first stability threshold
\gamma=1
in Sobolev spaces for
\nu=\mu>0
in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
. Recently, Rao, Zhang, and Zi
[
29
]
extended this result to the case
\mu\neq\nu
. For 2D case in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, the nonlinear stability in Gevrey-
\frac{1}{2}
- space when
\nu=0,\mu>0
was obtained by Zhao and Zi
[
35
]
. Chen and Zi
[
14
]
showed that
\gamma=\frac{5}{6}+
for shear flow close to Couette in Sobolev space when
\nu=\mu
. Dolce
[
19
]
proved
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=(\frac{2}{3},0)
in a more general regime
0<\mu^{3}\lesssim\nu\leq\mu
where the fluid effects dominate. In the case
\mu\leq\nu
, Knobel
[
21
]
showed the stability if
\nu^{3}\lesssim\mu\leq\nu
and instability with inflation of size
\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
if
\mu\lesssim\nu^{3}
. This leaves the case
0<\nu\lesssim\mu^{3}\leq 1
open, which is essentially different from the symmetric counterpart region
\mu\lesssim\nu^{3}
.

The purpose of this paper is two-fold. First, we obtain the threshold
\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}

1.1
). Our stability estimates (
1.2
) is consistent with the linear results when

[

). Our results show that fluid and magnetic field have a subtle interaction rather than a simple superposition of each other. We next state the main theorems.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
0<\nu\leq\mu^{3}\leq 1
,
|\beta|>1/2
, and
N\geq 4
. Let
(\omega^{in},j^{in})
be the initial datum of (
1.1
). There exist constant
0<\delta_{0}<1
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(N,\beta,\nu,\mu)>0
such that if
(\omega^{in},j^{in})
satisfies

\lVert(\omega^{in},j^{in})\rVert_{H^{N}}=\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{\frac{1}{3}},

then the following stability estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|(u,b)(t,x+yt,y)\|_{H^{N-1}}\lesssim\epsilon,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\langle t\rangle\lVert(u^{1}_{\neq},b^{1}_{\neq})(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}+\langle t\rangle^{2}\lVert(u^{2}_{\neq},b^{2}_{\neq})(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\min\{\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}},\langle t\rangle\}\epsilon e^{-\delta_{0}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}t},

(1.2b)

\displaystyle\lVert(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})(t,x+yt,y)\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\min\{\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}},\langle t\rangle\}\epsilon e^{-\delta_{0}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}.

(1.2c)

Remark 1.1
.

The above results can be extended to
0<\nu\leq\mu\leq 1
by exactly the same argument.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
0<\mu^{3}\leq\nu\leq\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq 1
,
|\beta|>1/2
, and
N\geq 4
. Let
(\omega^{in},j^{in})
be the initial datum of (
1.1
). There exist constant
0<\delta_{0}<1
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(N,\beta,\nu,\mu)>0
such that if
(\omega^{in},j^{in})
satisfies

\lVert(\omega^{in},j^{in})\rVert_{H^{N}}=\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\min\{\nu^{\frac{1}{2}},\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\},

then the following stability estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|(u,b)(t,x+yt,y)\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim\epsilon,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lVert(u^{1}_{\neq},b^{1}_{\neq})(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}+\langle t\rangle\lVert(u^{2}_{\neq},b^{2}_{\neq})(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\epsilon e^{-\delta_{0}\min\{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}},\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\}t},

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lVert(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})(t,x+yt,y)\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\epsilon\langle t\rangle e^{-\delta_{0}\min\{\nu^{\frac{1}{3}},\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\}t}.

(1.5)

Remark 1.2
.

Compared to the Navier-Stokes equations, the inviscid damping and enhanced dissipation have a transient amplification of order
\max\{\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}},\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\}
due to the background magnetic field. We can not expect the Lyapunov stability of
(\omega,j)
, i.e.,
\|(\omega,j)(t,x+yt,y)\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim\epsilon
. In fact, the zero mode
(\omega_{0},j_{0})
is order
\epsilon\langle t\rangle
from
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
0<\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\nu\leq 1
,
|\beta|>1/2
, and
N\geq 4
. Let
(\omega^{in},j^{in})
be the initial datum of (
1.1
). There exist constant
0<\delta_{0}<1
and
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(N,\beta,\nu,\mu)>0
such that if
(\omega^{in},j^{in})
satisfies

\lVert(\omega^{in},j^{in})\rVert_{H^{N}}=\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}})^{-1}=\epsilon_{0}\nu^{-1}\mu^{\frac{5}{6}},

(1.6)

then the following stability estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|(u,b)(t,x+yt,y)\|_{H^{N}}\lesssim\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\epsilon,

(1.7a)

\displaystyle\lVert(u^{1}_{\neq},b^{1}_{\neq})(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}+\langle t\rangle\lVert(u^{2}_{\neq},b^{2}_{\neq})(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}\lesssim\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\epsilon e^{-\delta_{0}\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t},

(1.7b)

\displaystyle\lVert(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})(t,x+yt,y)\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\langle t\rangle\epsilon e^{-\delta_{0}\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}.

(1.7c)

Idea of the proof
: As in
[
19
,
21
]
, it is natural to use symmetric variables

z=\sqrt{\partial_{xx}\Delta^{-1}}\omega,\quad q=\sqrt{\partial_{xx}\Delta^{-1}}j,

when
0<\nu\leq\mu^{3}\leq 1
. In fact, by a similar argument as
[
21
]
, we get

\lVert(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})(t,x+yt,y)\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle\lVert(z,q)(t,x+yt,y)\rVert_{H^{N}}\lesssim\mu\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}}\langle t\rangle\epsilon e^{-\delta_{0}\nu^{\frac{1}{3}}t}.

However, considering the linear estimates in
[
35
]
, we expect that
(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})
has an amplification no larger than
\langle t\rangle
, which is much smaller than the above prediction. Therefore, we have to give up the symmetric variables, and treat the system of
(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})
directly.

For the nonlinear problem, the most challenging part is of the form

\langle M(F\cdot\nabla_{L}G),MH\rangle,

where
M
is the multiplier defined in (
2.11
) and
FGH\in\{U\Omega\Omega,UJJ,BJ\Omega,B\Omega J\}
, where we use capital letters
U,\Omega,B,J
to represent quantities in the new coordinates. We need to treat
F^{1}\partial_{X}G
and
F^{2}\partial^{L}_{Y}G
separately. For
F^{1}\partial_{X}G
, we use commutator
\langle[M,F^{1}\partial_{X}]G,MH\rangle

in the specific frequency region which is effective to facilitate cancellations and derivative distribution. These estimates need the strong enhanced dissipation
\nu^{\frac{1}{6}}\||\partial_{X}|^{\frac{1}{3}}M(\Omega_{\neq},J_{\neq})\|_{L^{2}L^{2}}
to reduce derivative losses as much as possible (see section
4.3.1
for details). Next turning to
F^{2}\partial_{Y}G_{0}
, due to the nonlinear interaction,
(\Omega_{0},J_{0})
undergoes a linear growth
\langle t\rangle
, and this growth is crucially counter-balanced by the inviscid damping of
(U^{2},B^{2})
. These ideas can also apply to nonlinear estimates on the system of
(z,q)
to improve the threshold when
0<\mu^{3}\leq\nu\leq 1
. In the regime
\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\nu
, since
(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})
has a growth rate larger than
\langle t\rangle
, the estimates highly depend on the good structure of the nonlinearity, especially for the term
\langle M(\partial_{XX}\Delta_{L}^{-1})^{\frac{1}{2}}(B\cdot\nabla_{L}J),MZ\rangle
(see section
4.4.1
for details).

Remark 1.3
.

Considering the three results above, we give two comments. First, compared to
(\omega_{\neq},j_{\neq})
, due to the stretching term
-\partial_{xy}{\Delta}^{-1}z
, the symmetric quantity
(z,q)
grows in an additional time interval
[\frac{\eta}{k}-\nu^{-\frac{1}{3}},\frac{\eta}{k}]
, where
k,\eta
are denoted the frequency of
x,y
respectively. If
\mu^{3}\leq\nu\leq\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}
, this growth is controlled by enhanced dissipation and diffusion. If
\mu^{\frac{1}{3}}\leq\nu
, it leads to an amplification of order
\nu\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, as shown in (
1.7
), which is consistent with the results when
\mu=0,\nu>0
in
[
22
]
. Second, both Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
hold on the regime
\mu^{3}\leq\nu\leq\mu
, but neither is better than the other. In addition, theorem
1.1
cannot be extended to the case
\nu>\mu
.

2
Preliminary

2.1
Notation

Throughout this paper, for
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

|r,s|:=|r|+|s|,\quad\langle r\rangle:=\sqrt{1+r^{2}}.

We use the notation
r\lesssim s
to express
r\leq Cs
for some constant
C>0
independent of the parameters of interest such as
\nu,\mu
. The Fourier transform of a function
f
is denoted by

\mathcal{F}(f)(k,\eta)=\hat{f}_{k,\eta}=\hat{f}(k,\eta)=\iint_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{-i(kx+\eta y)}f(x,y)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

For a Fourier multiplier
A
, we define
Af=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(A(k,\eta)\hat{f}(k,\eta))
.
We denote

f_{0}(y):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,y)\mathrm{d}x,\quad f_{\neq}(x,y):=f(x,y)-f_{0}(y).

2.2
Reformulation of the equations

Change of independent variables
: First, we change the coordinates to mod out by the fast mixing of the Couette flow:

X=x-yt,\quad Y=y,

and denote

\displaystyle\Omega(t,X,Y):=\omega(t,x,y),\quad J(t,X,Y):=j(t,x,y),

\displaystyle\nabla_{L}=(\partial_{X}^{L},\partial_{Y}^{L}):=(\partial_{X},\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X}),

\displaystyle\Delta_{L}:=\partial_{XX}+(\partial_{Y}-t\partial_{X})^{2}.

Then the system satisfied by
(\Omega,J)
reads

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\Omega-\beta\partial_{X}J-\nu\Delta_{L}\Omega=\mathrm{NL}^{\Omega},\\
\partial_{t}J-\beta\partial_{X}\Omega-\mu\Delta_{L}J+2\partial_{XY}^{L}\Phi=\mathrm{NL}^{J},\\
\Omega|_{t=0}=\Omega^{in},\quad J|_{t=0}=J^{in},\\
U=\nabla_{L}^{\bot}\Psi,\quad B=\nabla_{L}^{\bot}\Phi,\\
\Psi=\Delta_{L}^{-1}\Omega,\quad\Phi=\Delta_{L}^{-1}J,\\
\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}^{\Omega}:

\displaystyle=-U\cdot\nabla_{L}\Omega+B\cdot\nabla_{L}J,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{NL}^{J}:

\displaystyle=-U\cdot\nabla_{L}J+B\cdot\nabla_{L}\Omega+2\partial_{XY}^{L}\Phi(\Omega-2\partial_{XX}\Psi)-2\partial_{XY}^{L}\Psi(J-2\partial_{XX}\Phi).

Change of dependent variables
: When
\nu\leq\mu^{3}
, we treat the above system of
(\Omega,J)
directly. While for
\mu^{3}\leq\nu
, following
[
19
]
, we introduce good unknowns to symmetrize the system. Let

\Gamma=\sqrt{\frac{\partial_{XX}}{\Delta_{L}}}.

(2.2)

We define

Z:=\Gamma\Omega,\quad Q:=\Gamma J.

Then
(Z,Q)
satisfies the equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}Z-\beta\partial_{X}Q-\nu\Delta_{L}Z-\frac{\partial_{XY}^{L}}{\Delta_{L}}Z=\Gamma\mathrm{NL}^{\Omega},\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q-\beta\partial_{X}Z-\mu\Delta_{L}Q+\frac{\partial_{XY}^{L}}{\Delta_{L}}Q=\Gamma\mathrm{NL}^{J},\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

where
\mathrm{NL}^{\Omega},\mathrm{NL}^{J}
are defined as (
2.1
). The associated energy functional is given by

\mathrm{E}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lVert Z\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert Q\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\beta}Re\left\langle\frac{\partial_{Y}^{L}}{\Delta_{L}}Z,Q\right\rangle\right).

2.3
The Fourier multipliers

We introduce the following multipliers to capture the stability mechanisms of the above systems. The first multiplier
M^{1}
is standard, which helps us to obtain inviscid damping of the MHD equations. Let

\lambda:=\min\{\nu,\mu\},

then
M^{1}
satisfies the following equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}M^{1}_{k}}{M^{1}_{k}}=-\frac{C_{1}}{1+(\eta/k-t)^{2}},\ \mathrm{for}\ 0<|k|\leq C_{2}\lambda^{-1/2},\\
M^{1}_{k}(t,\eta)=1,\ \mathrm{for}\ |k|\in(0,C_{2}\lambda^{-1/2}]^{c},\\
M^{1}_{k}(0,\eta)=1,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

where
C_{1}>1000C_{2}
and
C_{2}>3000
are sufficiently large. Motivated by
[
17
]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial_{t}M^{2}_{k}}{M^{2}_{k}}=-\frac{2(C_{2})^{2}(\lambda k^{2})^{\frac{1}{3}}}{1+(\lambda k^{2})^{\frac{2}{3}}(\eta/k-t)^{2}},\ \mathrm{for}\ k\neq 0,\\
M^{2}_{0}(t,\eta)=M^{2}_{k}(0,\eta)=1.\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

M^{3}
is given in

19
]

M^{3}_{k}(t,\eta)=1,\ \mathrm{for}\ |k|\in(0,C_{2}\lambda^{-1/2}]^{c},\\
M^{3}_{k}(0,\eta)=1,\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

with
\displaystyle C_{3}>\frac{C_{1}}{|\beta|-1/2}

\displaystyle\left\lVert U^{1}_{\neq}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{N}}=\sum_{k\neq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle|k,\eta|\rangle^{2N}\left|\frac{|\eta-kt|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\hat{\Omega}_{k}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\eta\leq\sum_{k\neq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle|k,\eta|\rangle^{2N}\left|\hat{Z}_{k}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta=\left\lVert Z\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{N}},

\displaystyle\left\lVert U^{2}_{\neq}\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{N-1}}=\sum_{k\neq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle|k,\eta|\rangle^{2N-2}\left|\frac{|k|}{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\hat{\Omega}_{k}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta\leq\sum_{k\neq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle|k,\eta|\rangle^{2N}\left|\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle}\hat{Z}_{k}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta=\frac{1}{\langle t\rangle}\left\lVert Z\right\rVert^{2}_{H^{N}}.

Then by (
3.6
), we conclude (
3.8
) and complete the proof.
∎

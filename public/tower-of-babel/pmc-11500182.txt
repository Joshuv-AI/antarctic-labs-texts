Modeling and analysis using piecewise hybrid fractional operator in time scale measure for ebola virus epidemics under Mittag–Leffler kernel

Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis emerging infectious disease poses one of the most severe threats to public health in these locations, but there are not many reliable therapies yet. In this work, we developed the Ebola virus dynamics and control factors epidemic model with a piecewise hybrid fractional Operator in time scale measure insight of Mittag–Leffler kernel. Patterns and structures that repeat at various scales are the focus of fractal analysis, which has applications in complex systems such as biological ones. Both qualitatively and statistically, a proposed model with the Lipschitz criteria and linear growth is examined, considering positive solutions, boundedness, and uniqueness at equilibrium points with Leray-Schauder results under time scale ideas. The regulation for linear responses approach will be used by Chaos Control to stabilize the system after its equilibrium points. A fractional-order framework with a controlled design will be considered, where solutions are bounded in the feasible domain of relations of different compartments. Ulam–Hyers stability results in the solution are treated when function (constant or rising) for the component of qualitative inquiry in generalized form. The dynamical behaviors of the suggested model are discussed with the Newton polynomial approach used to implement on model in the sense of classical piecewise and Mittag–Leffler kernel at different fractional order values. The model shows that solutions are stable and confined within a feasible range, ensuring reliability. Through detailed simulations, it effectively captures how different interventions and infection rates influence Ebola spread. This fractional-order model enhances understanding of Ebola transmission, providing a strong basis for predicting outbreaks and planning effective control measures, with practical applications for analyzing real-world data.Keywords: Ebola virus, Hybrid piecewise operator, Ulam–Hyers stability, Leray-Schauder results, Numerical SimulationsSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The Ebola virus is categorized within the framework of virological taxonomy as a distinct species and is classified as a constituent of the Ebola virus group. The Ebola virus genus is categorized within the Filoviridae family, known as filoviruses, alongside the Marburgvirus and Cuevavirus genera1. The World Health Organization (WHO) lists five different species that make up the genus Ebolavirus:The ebolaviruses responsible for Taï Forest (TAFV), Sudan (SUDV), Zaire (EBOV), Reston (RESTV), and Bundibugyo (BDBV) in Homo sapiens and other primates, notably the virulent Zaire ebolavirus (EBOV), induces a profoundly severe form of hemorrhagic fever. The initial emergence of this pathogen occurred in Yambuku, located in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, in the year 19762. Individuals afflicted with Ebola hemorrhagic fever (EHF) often present with both internal and external forms of hemorrhaging. Additional symptoms may include elevated body temperature, emesis, diarrhea, and in more severe instances, organ failure accompanied by uncontrollable bleeding, as the virus interferes with the coagulation process. An average of ten days passes for many3. When an individual contracts the disease, the mortality rate might reach up to 90%4. Ebola hemorrhagic fever presently lacks any recognized therapeutic intervention or preventative vaccine, and the most severe outbreak recorded in West Africa has resulted in the mortality of over 10,994 individuals1. The emergence of this disease occurred in Guinea in December 2013, although it remained unrecognized until March 20145. Subsequently, the epidemic spread to Senegal, Nigeria, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. The Pteropodidae family of fruit bats in the area are thought to be the virus’s native habitat, origin, and carrier of Ebola, yet they are not afflicted by it6. Even though non-human primates have infected humans, it is believed that humans are merely an unintentional host, not the reservoir, for these infections4. The transmission of the virus from humans to animals is infrequent; however, it may occur when individuals engage in direct contact with the blood, secretions, organs, or other bodily fluids of an infected animal. An epidemic is frequently traced to a singular index case, which typically arises when an individual handles the carcass of a gorilla, chimpanzee, or duiker, or partakes in the bushmeat trade, involving the capture and consumption of wild animals, including primates such as gorillas and chimpanzees7. Following the initial infection of an individual, the virus has the potential to disseminate through physical contact with their bodily fluids, secretions, tissues, or semen. This is more likely to happen in hospitals, families, and some funeral customs where contact between people is more common.

The human population and direct transmission are the only topics covered in the few published studies8,9 on mathematical modeling for Ebola virus transmission. The vast majority of mathematical inquiries concerning the dynamics of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD) outbreaks are articulated within traditional frameworks, such as the SEIR model3,7, the SIR model11,12, the SI model10, the SEIRD model13,14, or the SEIRHD model15,16. In these models, the variables (S) represent susceptibility, (E) indicates exposure to infection, (I) signifies infectiousness, (H) denotes hospitalization, (R) refers to recovery or removal from the population, and (D) symbolizes death or mortality. This research15,17 focused largely on approximating the fundamental reproduction number and estimating the epidemiological parameters in the Central African scenario. In this regard, the preliminary investigation7, conducted in 2004, employed the SEIR model to ascertain the , yielding figures of 1.83 for the Democratic Republic of the Congo and 1.34 for Uganda. Subsequent estimations of 1.35 for Uganda and 1.33 for the Democratic Republic of the Congo were derived later in 2006 utilizing an SEIR model3. An alternative study conducted in 200715, utilizing an SEIRD model, provided an approximation of 2.7 for the epidemics in the Congo in 1995 and Uganda in 2000. For the epidemics in Central Africa, the most recent research15 was completed in 2013. The pre-intervention , for the 1995 outbreak in the Congo was estimated to be 2.1, whereas the post-intervention was calculated to be 0.1339, as reported by the authors, who employed the Markov Chain Monte Carlo technique to infer the parameters of their SEIRD model.

Conclusion

The framework of an eight-compartmental piecewise fractional model of the Ebola virus under the fractional derivatives of Atangana Baleanu has been constructed in this study. The key theoretical and numerical characteristics of the suggested model have been provided. We have obtained results that address the existence and uniqueness of solutions for subintervals in the context of Atangana Baleanu operators by utilizing the concept of fixed-point outcomes. Additionally, the idea of the Ulam–Hyers stability has been deduced. The piecewise fractional model of the Ebola virus has numerical solutions that we have computed using the Newton Polynomial approach. The numerical results for a few fractional orders and time lengths are shown using MATLAB-18. Numerous chaotic systems with solutions located in the feasible region are reported in this paper. We have found that the piecewise data provides more information about the crossover dynamics for various fractional orders. Furthermore, piecewise and fractional-order analysis will significantly interest the graphical results. It improves the model fit as the piecewise approach depicts the phases exhibited by the ebola virus and thus different parameters for each phase. It also allows for the variation of transmission rates over time periods to be incorporated by changing the parameters so as to better explains historical data. It also improves the model’s capacity to address public health interventions hence their effectiveness varying throughout the epidemic. Further, it suggests a more accurate way of handling nonlinear dynamics by using the right models in different phases of the epidemic.

Using stability analysis and bifurcation we can determine the key ratios of transmission and forecast shifts in dynamics of the Ebola outbreak so that interventions can be provided at the right time. This helps the public health officials to synchronise and coordinate the outbreak control measures such as mass vaccination and quarantine among others hence enhancing epidemic control measures. Modeling is improved by fractional operators because it is capable of decoding a better and more detailed characterization of memory effects. It builds a better capacity to address nonlinearity and offers more versatility in adapting to different types of data situations. Further, there are higher long-term forecasting capabilities with fractional models and better control methods which are helpful while tackling large and intricate situations such as the outbreak of diseases. Piecewise fractional derivatives involved in the Ebola model give a much better description of disease processes. This approach captures fluctuations in the transmission rates and recovery periods over a period of time. It mimics real-life epidemic situations and has the added advantage of boosting the modeling and analysis of epidemics to alleviate disease control measures.

Abstract
. We generalize the notions of locally and polar harmonic Maass forms to general orthogonal groups of signature ( 2 , n ) (2,n) with singularities along real analytic and algebraic cycles. We prove a current equation for locally harmonic Maass forms and recover the Fourier expansion of the Oda lift involving cycle integrals. Moreover, using the newly defined polar harmonic Maass forms, we prove that meromorphic modular forms with singularities along special divisors are orthogonal to cusp forms with respect to a regularized Petersson inner product. Using this machinery, we derive a duality theorem involving cycle integrals of meromorphic modular forms along real analytic cycles and cycle integrals of locally harmonic Maass forms along algebraic cycles.

1.
Introduction

Locally harmonic Maass forms are a class of harmonic modular forms with a specific type of singularity. They were first introduced by Bringmann, Kane and Kohnen
[
BKK15
]
to study the Shintani lift and, in the weight
k=1
case, by
[
Höv12
]
to study the Shimura lift, later extended to arbitrary weight by
[
CF23
]
. A key example of locally harmonic Maass forms are certain
\xi
-preimages
\mathcal{F}_{1-k,D}
of cusp forms of weight
2k
for
D>0
introduced by
[
Zag75
]

(1.1)

\displaystyle f_{k,D}(Z)=\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{D}}\frac{1}{Q(1,Z)^{k}},\qquad\qquad Z\in\mathbb{H},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
is the set of integral binary quadratic forms of discriminant
D
. For negative
D<0
, Bengoechea
[
Ben15
]
extended the definition of
f_{k,D}
to obtain meromorphic cusp forms with poles at CM-points. The corresponding
\xi
-preimages
\mathcal{F}_{1-k,D}
, termed polar harmonic Maass forms, were introduced in
[
BK16
]
. An important application of the theory of locally and polar harmonic Maass forms is a rationality result for cycle integrals of the meromorphic modular forms
f_{k,D}
, see
[
LS22
]
,
[
ANBS20
]
. Recently, this theory was extended to orthogonal modular forms in signature
(2,2)
by
[
ADKS24
]
, where a rationality result for cycle integrals of meromorphic modular forms was established.

The aim of this paper is to generalize this theory to arbitrary signature
(2,n)
, providing the foundation to extend many of the results involving locally and polar harmonic Maass forms beyond previously studied cases. The special case of signature
(2,1)
then corresponds to the original definition in
[
BKK15
]
,
[
BK16
]
, while signature
(2,2)
corresponds to the case investigated in
[
ADKS24
]
.

We will now describe our results in more detail. Let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,n)
with corresponding quadratic form
q
and bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Choose
e\in L
primitive isotropic and
e^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
isotropic with
(e,e^{\prime})=1
. Let
K=e^{\perp}\cap e^{\prime\perp}\cap L
be the orthogonal complement of the plane spanned by
e
and
e^{\prime}
. Then the hermitian symmetric domain corresponding to the orthogonal group
O(L\otimes\mathbb{R})
can be realized as

\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}=\{Z=X+iY\in K\otimes\mathbb{C}\ |\ q(Y)>0\}

and we have a natural action of
O(L\otimes\mathbb{R})
on
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}
. We denote a connected component of
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}
by
\mathbb{H}_{n}
and write
O^{+}(L\otimes\mathbb{R})\subseteq O(L\otimes\mathbb{R})
for the index
2
subgroup that preserves the component. Let
\Gamma\subseteq O^{+}(L)=O(L)\cap O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))
be a neat congruence subgroup. Extending Zagier’s modular forms in equation (
1.1
) to arbitrary signature, Oda
[
Oda78
]
introduced a family of orthogonal cusp forms for
\Gamma
of weight
\kappa>n
indexed by
\mu\in V=L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
with
q(\mu)>0
given by

\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}(Z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mu}\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{(\lambda,Z+e-q(Z)e^{\prime})^{\kappa}}\bigg|_{\kappa}\gamma,

where
\Gamma_{\mu}
is the stabilizer of
\mu
in
\Gamma
and
|_{\kappa}
is the Petersson slash operator, see Section
2
. The meromorphic counterpart for
\nu\in V
with negative
q(\nu)<0
is given by

\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}}(Z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\nu}\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{(\lambda,Z+e-q(Z)e^{\prime})^{\kappa}}\bigg|_{\kappa}\gamma

and has singularities of order
\kappa
along the
\Gamma
-translates of the special divisor

T_{\nu}=\{Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}\ |\ (\nu,Z+e-q(Z)e^{\prime})=0\}.

A fundamental tool in this paper is the
\xi_{-\kappa}
-operator, generalizing constructions from
[
ADKS24
]
,
[
BF04
]
,
[
BFK18
]
. It defines a map from differential forms with values in the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{-\kappa}
of modular forms of weight
-\kappa
to differential forms with values in the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
of modular forms of weight
\kappa
. One of the main results of this paper is the following generalization of
[
BKK15
, Proposition 6.1]
,
[
ADKS24
, Proposition 5.2]
.

Theorem 1.1

(see ?THM?
)
.

There exists a real analytic
(n,n-1)
-form with values in
\mathcal{L}_{-\kappa}
denoted by
\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
that is harmonic outside a real analytic cycle
C_{\mu}
such that
\xi_{-\kappa}\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}=\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
. The space generated by
\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
is called the space of locally harmonic Maass forms.

These
\xi_{-\kappa}
-preimages can be considered as a distribution on
\mathcal{A}^{n,n-1}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n},\mathcal{L}_{-1})
via

[\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}](H)=\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}}H(Z)\wedge\overline{*}_{-\kappa}\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}(Z)

(\xi_{-\kappa}[\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}])(h)=-[\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}](\xi_{\kappa}h).

Using a Stokes-argument we prove the following current equation, which generalizes
[
CF23
, Theorem 8.3]
,
[
ADKS24
, Theorem 8.10]
.

Theorem 1.2

(see ?THM?
)
.

We have the current equation

\xi_{-\kappa}[\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}]=[\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}]+C_{n,\kappa}\delta_{C_{\mu}},

where
C_{n,\kappa}
is an explicit constant and

\delta_{C_{\mu}}(h)=q(\mu)^{\frac{n}{2}-\kappa}\int_{\Gamma_{\mu}\backslash C_{\mu}}h(Z)(\lambda,\psi(Z))^{\kappa-n}dZ

is the cycle integral of
h
along
C_{\mu}
.

As an application we use this current equation to recover the Fourier expansion of the Oda lift, see ?THM?
in Section
4.4
.

We want to mention that our definition of locally harmonic Maass form is slightly more restrictive than the original definition of
[
BKK15
]
, see ?THM?
.

Similarly, we introduce the notion of polar harmonic Maass forms, extending the definitions of
[
BKK15
]
,
[
ADKS24

to general signature
(2,n)
and prove the following

Theorem 1.3

(see ?THM?
)
.

There exists a real analytic
(n,n-1)
-form with values in
\mathcal{L}_{-\kappa}
denoted by
\Omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}}
that is harmonic outside the algebraic cycle

such that

Again, this definition is more restrictive then the definition in
[
BK16
]
, see ?THM?
.

Using this preimage and a Stokes’ argument we deduce

Theorem 1.4

(see ?THM?
)
.

Let
h:\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
be a real analytic cusp form. Then

\langle h,\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}}\rangle_{\operatorname{reg}}=0,

In particular, we obtain

-preimage of

(see ?THM?
)
.

Assume that the cycles
C_{\mu}

C_{n,\kappa}\delta_{C_{\mu}}(\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}})=\delta_{T_{\nu}}(\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}),

where
\delta_{T_{\nu}}(\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}})

, see Section

.

Remark 1.6
.

Throughout the paper we assume that
\Gamma\subseteq O^{+}(L)
is a neat congruence subgroup. For more general congruence subgroups

, there exists a neat congruence subgroup

1.1.
Outlook

This work lays the foundation to generalize many of the results involving locally and polar harmonic Maass forms in signature
(2,1)
and signature
(2,2)
to arbitrary signature
(2,n)
.

In particular, in an upcoming work we will show that certain linear combinations of locally and polar harmonic Maass forms are theta lifts of Poincaré series, compare
[
ADKS24
, Theorem 7.3]
,
[
BKV13
, Theorem 1.3(2)]
,
[
BK16
, Theorem 6.2]
and we expect that we can obtain similar rationality results for cycle integrals of meromorphic modular forms as in
[
ANBS20
]
,
[
ADKS24
]
,
[
LS22
]
.

Moreover, by
[
Oda78
, Theorem 1]
(linear combinations of) the cycle integrals of
\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
over real analytic cycles are the Fourier coefficients of an elliptic cusp form. In
[
BKZ14
]
, a generating series of locally harmonic Maass forms was completed to a modular form and in
[
BFIL18
]
, the modularity of a generating series of cycle integrals of meromorphic differential forms was proven.
Analogously, we expect that (linear combinations of) the cycle integrals
\delta_{C_{\mu}}(\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}})
are the Fourier coefficients of a non-holomorphic modular form and that the non-holomorphic part is related to the cycles integrals
\delta_{T_{\nu}}(\Omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}})
.

Outline

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

and prove the duality ?THM?

2.
Orthogonal Modular Forms

2.1.
Orthogonal Upper Half Plane

Let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,n),n\in\mathbb{N}
with quadratic form
q
and bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let
e\in L
be primitive isotropic and
e^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(e,e^{\prime})=1
. Let
\tilde{e}^{\prime}=e^{\prime}-q(e^{\prime})e
, so that
q(\tilde{e}^{\prime})=0
. Let
K=L\cap e^{\perp}\cap e^{\prime\perp}=L\cap e^{\perp}\cap\tilde{e}^{\prime\perp}
. We write
V=L\otimes\mathbb{Q},\ V(\mathbb{R})=L\otimes\mathbb{R},\ V(\mathbb{C})=L\otimes\mathbb{C}
and
W=K\otimes\mathbb{Q},\ W(\mathbb{R})=K\otimes\mathbb{R},\ W(\mathbb{C})=K\otimes\mathbb{C}
. We define

\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}=\{Z=X+iY\in W(\mathbb{C})\ |\ q(Y)>0\}.

We have a map
\psi:\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}\to V(\mathbb{C})
given by

\psi(Z)=Z-q(Z)e+\tilde{e}^{\prime}.

This yields a biholomorphic map

\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}\to\{[Z]=[X+iY]\in\mathbb{P}(V(\mathbb{C}))\ |\ (Z,Z)=0,(Z,\overline{Z})>0\}.

The orthogonal group
O(V(\mathbb{R}))
acts naturally on the right hand side, which yields an action on
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}
via
\sigma Z:=\psi^{-1}(\sigma(\psi(Z)))
. By slight abuse of notation we write
\psi(X),\psi(Y)
for the real and imaginary part of
\psi(X+iY)
.

We define the
factor of automorphy
by

j(\sigma,Z)=(e,\sigma(\psi(Z)))

so that

j(\sigma,Z)\psi(\sigma Z)=\sigma(\psi(Z)).

In particular, we have for
\lambda\in V(\mathbb{R})

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\lambda,\psi(\gamma Z))=\frac{(\gamma^{-1}\lambda,\psi(Z))}{j(\gamma,Z)}.

Moreover, an easy calculation yields

q(\operatorname{Im}(\gamma(Z)))=\frac{q(Y)}{\lvert j(\gamma,Z)\rvert^{2}}.

The space
\mathbb{H}_{n}^{\pm}
consists of two connected components and we let
\mathbb{H}_{n}
be one of them and
G=O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))\subseteq O(V(\mathbb{R}))
the index
2
subgroup that acts on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
. We call this the
orthogonal upper half plane
.

Let

\operatorname{Gr}(L)=\operatorname{Gr}(V)=\{(Z^{+},Z^{-})\ |Z^{+},Z^{-}\subseteq V,Z^{+}\oplus Z^{-}=V,Z^{+}\perp Z^{-},q|_{Z^{+}}>0,q|_{Z^{-}}<0\}

be the associated
Grassmannian
. We have an isomorphism

\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\operatorname{Gr}(L),\qquad Z\mapsto(Z^{+},Z^{-}):=(\langle\psi(X),\psi(Y)\rangle,\langle\psi(X),\psi(Y)\rangle^{\perp}).

For
\lambda\in V(\mathbb{R})
and
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
we write
\lambda_{Z^{\pm}}
for the projection of
\lambda
onto
Z^{\pm}\subseteq V(\mathbb{R})
.

2.2.
Real Analytic and Algebraic Cycles

For
\mu\in V(\mathbb{R})
with
q(\mu)>0
we consider the real analytic submanifold of
\mathbb{H}_{n}
given by

C_{\mu}=\{Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}\ |\ q(\mu_{Z^{-}})=0\}.

It corresponds to the set of
(Z^{+},Z^{-})\in\operatorname{Gr}(L)
such that
\mu\in Z^{+}
and thus to the Grassmannian of the real quadratic space
\mu^{\perp}
of signature
(1,n)
. In particular, it is a hyperbolic space of dimension
n
.

Similarly, for
\nu\in V(\mathbb{R})
with
q(\nu)<0
we consider the complex submanifold

T_{\nu}=\{Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}\ |\ q(\nu_{Z^{+}})=0\},

which corresponds to the set of
(Z^{+},Z^{-})\in\operatorname{Gr}(L)
with
\nu\in Z^{-}
and thus corresponds to the Grassmannian of the real quadratic space
\nu^{\perp}
of signature
(2,n-1)
, which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}_{n-1}
and has complex codimension
1
in
\mathbb{H}_{n}
.

We call a congruence subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq O^{+}(L)=O(L)\cap O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))

neat
, if for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
the subgroup of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
generated by the eigenvalues of
\gamma
is torsion-free. From now on, let
\Gamma\subseteq O^{+}(L)
always be a neat congruence subgroup. Let
\nu\in V,q(\nu)<0
and
\Gamma_{\nu}
be the stabilizer of
\nu
in
\Gamma
. Then
\Gamma_{\nu}
acts on the cycle
T_{\nu}
and
\Gamma_{\nu}\backslash T_{\nu}
defines an algebraic cycle in
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}
. Analogously, if
\mu\in V,\ q(\mu)>0
and
\Gamma_{\mu}
is the stabilizer of
\mu
in
\Gamma
, then
\Gamma_{\mu}\backslash C_{\mu}
defines a real analytic cycle in
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}
, see for example
[
FM06
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Since our later results are conditional on the assumption that the cycles
C_{\mu}
and
T_{\nu}
do not intersect, we mention the following

Lemma 2.1
.

For
\mu,\nu\in V(\mathbb{R})
with
q(\mu)>0,q(\nu)<0
. Then
C_{\mu}\cap T_{\nu}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
(\mu,\nu)=0
.

Proof.

This directly follows from the explicit description of the cycles. We have
Z\in C_{\mu}\cap T_{\nu}
if and only if
\mu\in Z^{+},\nu\in Z^{-}
. But this can only happen, if
\mu\perp\nu
, i.e.
(\mu,\nu)=0
.
∎

2.3.
Orthogonal Modular Forms

For a function
h:\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
we define the
weight
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
Petersson slash operator
by

(h|_{\kappa}\sigma)(Z)=j(\sigma,Z)^{-\kappa}h(\sigma Z),\qquad\sigma\in O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R})).

A holomorphic function
h:\mathbb{H}_{n}\to\mathbb{C}
is called
modular form of weight
\kappa
with respect to
\Gamma\subseteq O^{+}(L)
, if
h|_{\kappa}\sigma=h
for all
\sigma\in\Gamma
. By the Koecher principle, a modular form is automatically holomorphic at the cusps. Otherwise we will add this as an assumption. If a modular form vanishes at all cusps, we call it a
cusp form
.

We extend the weight
\kappa
slash operator to differential forms in
\mathcal{A}^{*,*}(\mathbb{H}_{n})
in the obvious way. If
\Gamma
is torsion-free (for example, if
\Gamma
is neat), then modular forms are the global sections of a hermitian line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}
whose hermitian metric is given by the Petersson metric
h_{1}(Z)\overline{h_{2}(Z)}q(Y)^{\kappa}
on the fibers and differential forms on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{n}
with values in
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
are the same as differential forms on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
that are invariant under the weight
\kappa
slash operator.

Let
\mu\in V
with
q(\mu)>0
and
\kappa>n
. Following
[
Oda78
]
we define the orthogonal cusp form

\omega_{\mu,\kappa,\Gamma}^{\operatorname{cusp}}(Z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mu}\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{(\mu,\psi(Z))^{\kappa}}\bigg|_{\kappa}\gamma.

This definition makes sense, since by (
2.1
) we have

(\mu,\psi(\gamma Z))=\frac{(\gamma^{-1}\mu,\psi(Z))}{j(\gamma,Z)}=\frac{(\mu,\psi(Z))}{j(\gamma,Z)}

for
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mu}
. Analogously, for
\nu\in V
with
q(\nu)<0
and
\kappa>n
we define

\omega_{\nu,\kappa,\Gamma}^{\operatorname{mero}}(Z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\nu}\backslash\Gamma}\frac{1}{(\nu,\psi(Z))^{\kappa}}\bigg|_{\kappa}\gamma.

These define meromorphic modular forms with singularities along the
\Gamma
-translates of the algebraic cycle
T_{\nu}
of order
\kappa
, but still vanish at the cusps. We will usually write
\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
and
\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}}
, if the weight
\kappa
and the neat congruence subgroup
\Gamma
are clear from the context. The following lemma is used to reduce many of the properties of
\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
and
\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
to specific choices of
\mu
and
\nu
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\lambda\in V
with
q(\lambda)\neq 0
let
\omega_{\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma}
be the function
\omega_{\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
, if
q(\lambda)>0
and
\omega_{\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma}^{\operatorname{mero}}
, if
q(\lambda)<0
. Then

(i)

For
\lambda\in V
and
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
we have
\omega_{r\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma}=r^{-\kappa}\omega_{\lambda,\kappa,\gamma}
.

(ii)

If
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in V
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
\lambda=\gamma\lambda^{\prime}
, then
\omega_{\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma}=\omega_{\lambda^{\prime},\kappa,\Gamma}
.

(iii)

If
\gamma\in O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))
, then
\omega_{\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma}|_{\kappa}\gamma=\omega_{\gamma^{-1}\lambda,\kappa,\gamma^{-1}\Gamma\gamma}
, where now
\gamma^{-1}\Gamma\gamma\subseteq O^{+}(\gamma^{-1}L)
.

The proof is straightforward. For

\omega^{\operatorname{mero}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\Gamma\backslash L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\\
q(\nu)<0\end{subarray}}c(\nu)q(\nu)^{\kappa}\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}},

we can thus define the associated divisor

T(\omega^{\operatorname{mero}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\Gamma\backslash L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\\
q(\nu)<0\end{subarray}}c(\nu)T_{\nu}.

Remark 2.3
.

For
\lambda\in V\setminus\{0\}
with
q(\lambda)=0
one can consider analogously defined series. It turns out that these are given by Eisenstein series as in
[
Zag75
]
. See for example
[
Kie22
]
,
[
Kie23
]
,
[
Sch22
]
.

3.
The
\xi
-Operator on the Orthogonal Upper Half Plane

In this section we introduce the necessary hermitian geometry on the orthogonal upper half plane to define the differential operator
\xi_{-\kappa}
and construct
\xi_{-\kappa}
-preimages of
\omega_{\nu}^{\operatorname{mero}},\omega_{\mu}^{\operatorname{cusp}}
. We follow the exposition of
[
Kie22
, Section 5]
.

3.1.
Hermitian Geometry on the Orthogonal Upper Half Plane

As before, let
e\in L
be primitive isotropic,
e^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(e,e^{\prime})=1
and
\tilde{e}^{\prime}=e^{\prime}-q(e^{\prime})e
. Let
K=L\cap e^{\perp}\cap e^{\prime\perp}
. Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
W(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.
q(b_{1})=1,\ q(b_{i})=-1,\ 2\leq i\leq n
and
(b_{i},b_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
. We will write
Z=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}b_{j}
for
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{n}
. The
(1,1)
-form

\omega=-\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}\log(q(Y))

defines an
O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))
-invariant Kähler form. We write

\omega=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}(Y)dz_{i}\wedge d\overline{z}_{j},

and
h(Y)=(h_{ij}(Y))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, so that

h(Y)=\frac{1}{q(Y)}\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}^{2}&-y_{1}y_{2}&\ldots&\ldots&-y_{1}y_{n}\\
-y_{1}y_{2}&y_{2}^{2}&y_{2}y_{3}&\ldots&y_{2}y_{n}\\
\vdots&y_{2}y_{3}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&y_{n-1}y_{n}\\
-y_{1}y_{n}&\ldots&\ldots&y_{n-1}y_{n}&y_{n}^{2}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{2q(Y)}\begin{pmatrix}-1&&&\\
&1&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}

defines the corresponding hermitian metric on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
. Its inverse
h(Y)^{-1}=(h^{ij}(Y))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is given by

h(Y)^{-1}=4\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}^{2}&y_{1}y_{2}&\ldots&\ldots&y_{1}y_{n}\\
y_{1}y_{2}&y_{2}^{2}&y_{2}y_{3}&\ldots&y_{2}y_{n}\\
\vdots&y_{2}y_{3}&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&y_{n-1}y_{n}\\
y_{1}y_{n}&\ldots&\ldots&y_{n-1}y_{n}&y_{n}^{2}\end{pmatrix}+2q(Y)\begin{pmatrix}-1&&&\\
&1&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}.

We have
\det(h(Y))=\frac{1}{2^{n}q(Y)^{n}}
, so the volume form is given by

\displaystyle dz_{i}\wedge\widehat{dz_{i}}=(4iq(Y))^{n}d\mu(Z),\qquad d\overline{z}_{i}\wedge\widehat{d\overline{z}_{i}}=-(4iq(Y))^{n}d\mu(Z).

where

is the inner product on differential forms induced by the hermitian metric. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\overline{*}d\overline{z}_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{h^{ij}(Y)}{(4iq(Y))^{n}}\widehat{d\overline{z}_{j}}.

Now let

We obtain an inner product on

5.2.

Let

5.3.
The Duality Theorem

In this section we prove the duality ?THM?
by combining the results of Section
4
and Section

For

and

A Holomorphic Splitting Theorem
MIAO SONG

Abstract
. A long-term project is to construct a complete Calabi-Yau metric on the complement of the anticanonical divisor in a compact Kähler manifold M ¯ \hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu . We focus on the case where this smooth divisor has multiplicity 2 and is itself a compact Calabi-Yau manifold. Firstly we solved the Monge-Ampère equation when the Ricci potiential is of O ⁡ ( r − 1 ) O(r^{-1}) decay on the generalized A ​ L ​ G ALG manifolds. Then we used the solution to this Kähler Ricci flat metric to prove a holomorphic splitting theorem: If K M ¯ = 𝒪 ⁡ ( − 2 ​ D ) K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-2D) , where D D can be realized as a smooth Calabi-Yau manifold, and if 𝒪 3 ​ D ​ ( D ) \mathcal{O}_{3D}(D) is trivial, then this Kähler manifold M ¯ \hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu is biholomorphic to ℙ 1 × D \mathbb{P}^{1}\times D .



1.
Introduction

In 1990 Tian and Yau proposed a long-term program to construct complete Kähler Ricci-flat metrics from pairs
(\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu,D)
, where
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
is a compact Kähler manifold and
D
is a divisor such that
K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-\beta D)
.
When we consider
M:=\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu\setminus D
, it is clear that there exists a global nowhere vanishing holomorphic volumn form. One can then construct a suitable background Kähler metric
\omega
on
M
, and the existence of a Kähler Ricci-flat metric is equivalent to solving a complex Monge-Ampère equation.

In
[
TY90
]
and
[
TY91
]
they mainly dealt with the cases when

(1)

\beta=1
and
D
is neat and almost ample;

(2)

\beta>1
and
D
admits a postive Kähler Einstein metric.

In both settings, they established the existence of complete Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on
M
. They also addressed the case where
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
is a fibration over a smooth algebraic curve
S
, with connected fibers, and the anticanonical divisor consisting of finitely many smooth reduced fibers.

Subsequent work has explored a broader range of pairs
(\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu,D)
, as well as the asymptotic behavior of the resulting complete metrics. For instance, in
[
CH15
]
, Conlon and Hein constructed complete Kähler Ricci-flat metrics when
\beta>1
and
D
admits a positive Kähler-Einstein metric or the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-bundle of the normal bundle of
D
admits an irregular Sasaki-Einstein structure. They also showed that the resulting metric is asymptotically conical.

In
[
CL22
]
, Collins and Li constructed new complete Calabi-Yau metrics on the complement of anticanonical divisors
D
, under the assumption that the normal bundle of
D
is ample and that
D
consists of two transversely intersecting smooth components. The asymptotic geometry in their setting is modeled on a generalization of the Calabi ansatz.

In
[
HHN15
]
, Haskins, Hein, and Nordström provided a new proof of the existence of asymptotically cylindrical Calabi-Yau manifolds. They considered pairs
(\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu,D)
satisfying
K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-D)
, where the normal bundle of
D
is holomorphically trivial and
D
admits a Kähler Ricci-flat metric. Their paper also addresses the orbifold setting, where
D
is an effective orbifold divisor and the orbifold normal bundle of
D
is biholomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}\times D^{\prime})/\langle\sigma\rangle
, with
D^{\prime}
smooth and equipped with a
\sigma
invariant Kähler Ricci-flat metric. Here
\langle\sigma\rangle
denotes the finite group of order
m
acting by
\sigma(w,x)=(\mathrm{exp}(\frac{2\pi i}{m}w),\sigma(x))
on the product. A key improvement in their work was the demonstration that the complete metric decays exponentially to a product Ricci-flat metric on the cylindrical end.

In this paper, we focus on a setting analogous to
[
HHN15
]
. But in stead of assuming
K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-D)
(i.e.,
\beta=1
), we consider the case
K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-2D)
(i.e.,
\beta=2
). Before stating our main result, we first clarify our definition of Calabi-Yau manifolds.

Definition 1.1
.

A Kähler manifold
(M,g,J)
is called a Calabi-Yau manifold if its canonical bundle
K_{M}
is holomorphically trivial. This is equivalent to the existence of a nowhere vanishing holomorphic
(n,0)
-form
\Omega
on
M
.

This definition includes all complex tori, meaning we do not require
M
to be simply connected or algebraic. Under this definition, we now state our main theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu,\omega,J)
be a compact Kähler manifold and
D
be a smooth divisor in
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
such that

(1)

K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-2D)
;

(2)

D
is a compact Calabi-Yau manifold;

(3)

\mathcal{O}_{3D}(D)
is trivial as a holomorphic line bundle.

Then
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times D
.

Here
mD
denotes the complex analytic space
(D,\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{I}^{m})
, where
\mathcal{I}
is the ideal sheaf of
D
in
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
. Then
\mathcal{O}_{3D}(D)
should be interpreted as the restriction of
\mathcal{O}(D)
to the second order infinitesimal neighborhood of
D
. Also one can view this as the quotient sheaf given by

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{I}^{m}\to\mathcal{O}(D)\to\mathcal{O}_{(m+1)D}(D)\to 0.

We will mainly follow the discussion in
[
HHN15
]
to give more precise analytic interpretations of the third condition in Section 2. In the case when
h^{1}(\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu)=0
, the third condition is automatically satisfied.

As in prior work, our first step is to solve the complex Monge–Ampère equation on
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu\setminus D
. The third condition plays a critical role in constructing suitable background metrics. A natural question is whether there exist concrete examples satisfying the hypotheses of theorem
1.2
. Surprisingly, using the existence of the Ricci-flat Kähler metric, we prove a negative result: no nontrivial such examples exist.

We give a quick proof of this theorem in complex dimension two, where Enriques–Kodaira classification of compact Kähler surfaces applies.

proof when
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu=2
:

Since
K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=\mathcal{O}(-2D)
, the canonical divisor is divisible by 2. This implies that
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
should be minimal and
k(\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu)=-\infty
. Therefore,
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
must be either
\mathbb{P}^{2}
or
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is some rank 2 holomorphic vector bundle over a Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g}
(when
g=1
they are Hirzebruch surfaces). It is clear that
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is not possible. In the remaining cases, we have a holomorphic projection
\pi:\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\to\Sigma_{g}
. The canonical bundle is given by

\displaystyle K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}=-2C+(k+e)f,

where
kf
is the pull-back of the canonical divisor on
\Sigma_{g}
and
ef
is the pull-back of the corresponding divisor of
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{E}
on
\Sigma_{g}
.
C
can be viewed as the divisor added at infinity.

Since the only compact Calabi-Yau manifold of complex dimension 1 is complex torus, it follows that
\Lambda^{2}\mathcal{E}
is trivial and
\Sigma_{g}=\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
. There are only two such types of
\mathcal{E}
on complex torus, one is the direct sum of two trivial line bundles, whose projective bundle is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}/\Lambda
. The other is the non-trivial extension given by

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{O}\to 0

One can check that in this example
\mathcal{O}_{2D}(D)
is not trivial. More precisely,
\mathcal{O}_{2D}(D)
corresponds to the nontrivial extension element in
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
.
∎

From the proof, we see that there are examples where
\mathcal{O}_{2D}(D)
is nontrivial, and
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
is not a product holomorphically. It remains unclear to the author whether condition (3) in theorem
1.2
is indeed necessary.

The proof of theorem
1.2
proceeds in the following steps. First, we construct a complete background Kähler metric on
M=\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu\setminus D
that asymptotically approaches the product metric on
\mathbb{C}\times D
at infinity, with decay rate
O(r^{-1})
. Next, we refine this metric so that the Ricci potential
f=O(r^{-\mu})
for some
\mu>2
, and
\int(e^{f}-1)\omega^{n}=0
. This step involvs solving Poisson equations the complete Kähler manifolds. Finally, we establish the existence of a complete Calabi–Yau metric on
M
, together with a holomorphic function
w
satisfying
\text{Hess}w=0
. This implies that
M
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{C}\times D
, with the biholomorphism given by
w
.

2.
Weighted Spaces

In order to finally solve the complex Monge-Ampère equation on the noncompact manifold
M=\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu\setminus D
, we first establish the weighted spaces and a weighted Sobolev inequality. However, our definitions of these weighted spaces are slightly different from those in
[
CC18
]
, which makes it harder to apply some known methods to solve the Poisson equation and the complex Monge-Ampère equation. This main difference comes from the analysis on the local complex structures of
D
in a tubular neighborhood
U
.

2.1.
Motivation of new weighted spaces

Let
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
be a complex Kähler manifold with complex dimension
n\geqslant 2
. Suppose
D
is a smooth divisor in
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
such that it is also a smooth Calabi-Yau manifold and
2D\in|-K_{\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu}|
. From the adjunction formula we know that the normal bundle of
D
in
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
is holomorphically trivial. Our goal is to describe the local structure of the holomorphic volume form
\Omega
and the complex structure near the divisor
D
.

If the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}
of
D
is just complex trivial, then we can find a tubular neighborhood
U
of
D
and a differeomorphism
\Phi:\Delta\times D\rightarrow U
such that
\Phi(0,q)=q
for
q\in D
and
\Phi^{*}J-J_{0}=0
along
\{0\}\times D
. Here
\Delta
is the unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
and
J_{0}
is the product complex structure. However, even if
N
is holomorphically trivial, the diffeomorphism
\Phi
need not to be biholomorphic. In particular, the defining function
w
for
D
in
U
, induced by this
C^{\infty}
trivialization, may not be holomorphic.

In
[
HHN15
]
they used the implicit function theorem to modify
\Phi
(via composition with a suitable diffeomorphism
F\in\mathrm{Diff(U)}
) so that the
J_{0}
-holomorphic disks
\Delta\times\{q\}
becomes
J
-holomorphic with respect to
(F\circ\Phi)^{-1}
. Under this new product structure
\Delta\times D
, we can decompose the tensor
\Phi^{*}J-J_{0}
into four components. This gauge-fixing procedure ensures that the components in
T^{*}\Delta\otimes(T\Delta\oplus TD)
vanish. We denote the components of
\Phi^{*}J-J_{0}
in
T^{*}D\otimes T\Delta
and
T^{*}D\otimes TD
by
K
and
L
, respectively. Clearly,
L
vanishes to first order along
D
. The next lemma, proved in
[
HHN15
]
, shows that the vanishing order of
K
is determined by the restriction of
\mathcal{O}(D)
to an infinitesimal neighborhood of
D
in
U
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
z
be the holomorphic projection
\Delta\times D\to\Delta
. Then
N
is trivial as a holomorphic line bundle if and only if the
T^{*}D\otimes T\Delta
component of
\Phi_{*}J-J_{0}
is
O_{g_{0}}(|z|^{2})
. In particular,
\bar{\partial}z=\frac{i}{2}K\circ dz=O_{g_{0}}(|z|^{2})
. If
\mathcal{O}_{mD}(D)
is holomorphically trivial, then we can choose the defining function
w
such that
\bar{\partial}z=O_{g_{0}}(|z|^{m+1})
.

Here
g_{0}=idzd\bar{z}+\omega_{D}
denotes the product metric on
\Delta\times D
, with
\omega_{D}
any fix metric on
D
. Since
\Phi^{*}J-J_{0}
and
\bar{\partial}z
are tensors on
U
, the notation
O_{g_{0}}(|z|^{k})
indicates that their
C^{0}
-norms, measures with respect to
g_{0}

|z|^{k}
.

Let

, then we obtain a holomorphic map
\Delta^{*}\times D\to B^{c}_{1}(0)\times D\subset\mathbb{C}\times D

to be the new product metric on this chart. Let

be the Euclidean distance from the origin in
\mathbb{C}
. Then
r
is comparable to the distance measured using
g_{\infty}
when
r
is large enough. We let
O(\cdot)=O_{g_{\infty}}(\cdot)
be the growth/decay estimates measured using the product metric
g_{\infty}
. It then follows that:

\displaystyle O_{g_{0}}(|z|^{k})=O(r^{-k}),\,|dz|=\frac{1}{|w|^{2}}|dw|=O(r^{-2}),\,|dzd\bar{z}|=O(r^{-4})

We refer to
\{\Delta\times D,(z,x)\}
as the zero-chart and to

If
\mathcal{O}_{3D}(D)

\displaystyle||{K}||_{g_{\infty}}=||{\bar{\partial}z}||_{g_{\infty}}=O(\frac{1}{r^{2}}),\,||{L}||_{g_{\infty}}=O(\frac{1}{r}),\,||{J-J_{0}}||_{g_{\infty}}=O(\frac{1}{r})

Remark 2.2
.

From the standard deformation theory, if
b^{1}(D)=0

\mathcal{O}_{U}(D)

\mathcal{O}_{mD}(D)

\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu\to\mathbb{P}^{1}

2.3.
A Weighted Sobolev Inequality

In this subsection, we prove a weighted Sobolev inequality on the product of an annulus and a compact manifold. One can verify that this inequality also holds if the end of the manifold is
\mathbb{C}\times D/\langle\sigma\rangle
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
g_{P}
be the product metric on
M
.
g
is another complete metric on
M
such that
g-g_{P}=O(\frac{1}{r^{\beta}})
, where
r
is the distance function with respect to
g_{p}
. Fix a number
\lambda>1
, and define
A_{i}=A(\lambda^{i},\lambda^{i+1})\times Y
, where
A(r,R)
denotes the annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with inner radius and outer radius
r,R
, respectively. Then there exists a uniform constant
C
such that for all
u\in C^{1}(M)
and
\sigma\in[1,\frac{n}{n-2}]
, we have

\displaystyle||u-u_{i}||_{2\sigma,g,A_{i}}\leqslant C(\lambda^{i})^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}||\nabla u||_{2,g,A_{i}}.

Moreover, if
v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(A_{i})
, then

\displaystyle||v||_{2\sigma,g,A_{i}}\leqslant C(\lambda^{i})^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}||\nabla v||_{2,g,A_{i}}

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{i}:A_{0}\to A_{i}
be the diffeomorphism satisfying
\varphi(z^{\prime},q)=(\lambda^{i}z^{\prime},q)
. Denote
g_{0}
as the product metric on
A_{0}
. Then we have
\varphi_{i}^{*}g=\lambda^{2i}(dx^{\prime 2}+dy^{\prime 2})+g_{Y}+h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3}
, where
h_{1},h_{3}
are symmetric tensors on
A(1,\lambda)
and
Y
, respectively.
h_{2}
is the mixed term. We also have the estimate on these error terms:

\displaystyle||h_{1}||_{g_{0}}=O(\lambda^{2i-\beta i}),\quad||h_{2}||_{g_{0}}=O(\lambda^{i-\beta i}),\quad||h_{3}||_{g_{0}}=O(\lambda^{-\beta i})

We construct a new metric
g_{0,i}=dx^{\prime 2}+dy^{\prime 2}+g_{Y}+\lambda^{-2i}h_{1}+\lambda^{-i}h_{2}+h_{3}
. Then we have
\varphi_{i}^{*}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}=\lambda^{2i}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0,i}}
on
A_{0}
. Since the error terms are uniformly controlled, these
g_{0,i}
are uniformly equivalent to
g_{0}
on
A_{0}
. This implies that the Sobolev constants are uniform with respect to these metrics.

Let
\bar{u_{i}}
be the average of
u
on
A_{i}
with respect to
g
. Denote
u^{\prime}(z^{\prime},q)=u(\lambda^{i}z^{\prime},q)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}}u(z,q)\mathrm{dvol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{0}}u(\lambda^{i}z^{\prime},q)\varphi^{*}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2i}\int_{A_{0}}u^{\prime}(z^{\prime},q)\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0},i}.

Take
u\equiv 1
we have
|A_{i}|_{g}=|A_{0}|_{g_{0,i}}
. So
\bar{u_{i}}=\bar{u^{\prime}}_{g_{0,i}}
.

Since
du^{\prime}=\partial_{z}u\cdot\lambda^{i}dz^{\prime}+\partial_{q}u\cdot dq
, there is a uniform constant
C
such that
{|du^{\prime}|^{2}_{g_{0,i}}\leqslant C\lambda^{2i}|du|^{2}_{g}}
, so

\displaystyle\int_{A_{0}}|du^{\prime}|^{2}_{g_{0,i}}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0},i}\leqslant C\int_{A_{0}}|du|^{2}_{g}\lambda^{2i}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0},i}=C\int_{A_{i}}|du|^{2}_{g}\mathrm{dvol}_{g}

Using the Sobolev inequality on
A_{0}
we can derive that

\displaystyle(\int_{A_{i}}|u-\bar{u_{i}}|^{2\sigma}\mathrm{dvol}_{g})^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle=(\int_{A_{0}}|u^{\prime}-\bar{u^{\prime}_{g_{0},i}}|^{2\sigma}\lambda^{2i}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0,i}})^{\frac{1}{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lambda^{\frac{i}{\sigma}}(\int_{A_{0}}|du^{\prime}|^{2}_{g_{0,i}}\mathrm{dvol}_{g_{0},i})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lambda^{\frac{i}{\sigma}}(\int_{A_{i}}|du|^{2}_{g}\mathrm{dvol}_{g})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This gives the proof of the proposition.

The proof on the Sobolev inequality for compact supported functions follows the same argument.

∎

Proposition 2.11
.

For any
\mathrm{ALG}_{\beta}
manifold
(M,g,J)
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a piecewise constant positive function
\psi_{\varepsilon}=O(r^{-2-2\varepsilon})
, such that
\int\psi_{\varepsilon}=1
and for all
\alpha\in[1,\frac{n}{n-2}]
,
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M)

(2.15)

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{2\alpha}^{2}\leqslant C(\varepsilon)||\nabla u||_{2}^{2}

where
u_{\varepsilon}=\int u\psi_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

Take
A_{0}=K
being a compact subset of
M
such that
M\setminus K
is asymptotical to
B_{\lambda}^{c}(0)\times Y
for some
\lambda>1
. Let
r_{i}=\lambda^{i}
and
A_{i}=B_{i+1}(0)\setminus B_{i}\times Y
. Then
\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}A_{i}=M
. For each
i>0
, by the previous proposition, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{i})||_{2\alpha,A_{i}}^{2}\leqslant r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}||(u-\bar{u}_{i})||_{2\alpha,A_{i}}^{2}\leqslant C||\nabla u||_{2,A_{i}}^{2}

where
\bar{u}_{i}
is the average of
u
over
A_{i}
and
C
is uniform.

Now take
\chi_{i}
be the characteristic function of
A_{i}
,

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{2\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum(r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}||\chi_{i}(u-\bar{u}_{i})||_{2\varepsilon}^{2}+r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}|\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2})

(2.17)

\displaystyle\leqslant C||\chi_{i}\nabla u||_{2}^{2}+\sum r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}|\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

Let
\psi_{\varepsilon}=\sum\frac{1}{|A_{i}|}r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}\chi_{i}/\sum r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}
. It is easy to check
\int\psi_{\varepsilon}=1
and
\sum r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})=0

\displaystyle\sum r_{i}^{-2-2\varepsilon}|\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i<j}(r_{i}r_{j})^{-2-2\varepsilon}|\bar{u}_{i}-\bar{u}_{j}|^{2}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{i<j}(r_{i}r_{j})^{-2-2\varepsilon}|i-j|\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}|\bar{u}_{k}-\bar{u}_{k+1}|^{2}

Denote

\displaystyle|\bar{u}_{k}-\bar{u}_{k+1}|^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{|A_{k}||A_{k+1}|}\int_{A_{k}\times A_{k+1}}|u(x,p)-u(y,q)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2|B_{k}|}{|A_{k}||A_{k+1}|}\int_{B_{k}}|u-\bar{u}_{B_{k}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C||\nabla u||_{2}^{2}

Using the fact that

∎

We begin by defining the fiberwise average of

(3.2)

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=\int_{\{z\}\times Y}f(z,q)\mathrm{dvol}_{Y},

where

. Firstly we have

\displaystyle|\int_{B_{1}(z)}f_{i}(w)B_{i}(z-w)dw|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\int_{B_{1}(z)}f_{i}(w)B_{i}(z-w)dw|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{k}{2}}R^{-\mu}|\int_{B_{1}(z)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|z-w|}}w|

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{k}{2}}R^{-\mu}

For the second part, we have

\displaystyle|\int_{B_{1}^{c}(z)}f_{i}(w)B_{i}(z-w)dw|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\int_{B_{1}^{c}(z)\cap B_{R/2}(z)}f_{i}(w)B_{i}(z-w)dw|

\displaystyle+|\int_{B_{1}^{c}(z)\cap B^{c}_{R/2}(z)}f_{i}(w)B_{i}(z-w)dw|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{k}{2}}|\int_{\frac{R}{2}}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}r}}{\sqrt{r}}rdr|+C_{2}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{k}{2}}R^{-\mu}|\int_{1}^{\frac{R}{2}}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}r}}{\sqrt{r}}rdr|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{3}\lambda_{i}^{-\frac{k}{2}}R^{-\mu}

The higher order decaying rate can be derived from local Schauder estimates.
∎

Now we can organize these calculations and arrive at the following property.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that
\int_{\{w\}\times Y}f\mathrm{dvol}_{Y}=0
for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then there is a solution to
\Delta u=f
. Moreover, if
|\nabla^{k}f|\in O(r^{-\mu})
, then
|\nabla^{k}u|\in O(r^{-\mu})
. The solution is unique if we require that
u
is bounded.

Actually one can also prove these estimates using the estimates on the Green’s function. We will basically list the properties of
G
here.

Lemma 3.7
.

When
d:=d((z,p))\to 0
,
|G((z,p),(0,p_{0}))|\leqslant c\frac{1}{d^{n-2}}
. When
d\to\infty
,
|G((z,p),(0,p_{0}))|\leqslant c\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}{\sqrt{|z|}}
and
d\approx|z|
.

Proof.

The first asymptotical behavior comes from the standard argument on Green’s function and the fact that
d^{n-2}\log d\to 0
as
d\to 0
.

For the second part, notice that on compact manifolds we have
\sum_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}t}v_{i}(p)v_{i}(q)\approx ce^{-\lambda_{1}t}
when
t>1
, so we have

\displaystyle|\int_{1}^{\infty}H_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(z,0,t)H^{0}_{Y}(p,p_{0},t)dt|

\displaystyle\leqslant c\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{1}t-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4t}}}{t}dt

\displaystyle=2ce^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(\sqrt{\lambda_{1}t}-|z|/(2\sqrt{t}))^{2}}}{\sqrt{t}}d\sqrt{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2ce^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\tau^{2}}}{\sqrt{\tau^{2}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}d\tau

where we let
\tau=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}t}-\frac{|z|}{2\sqrt{t}}

Since
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{e^{-\tau^{2}}}{\sqrt{\tau^{2}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}d\tau\leqslant\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}e^{-\tau^{2}}d\tau
, we have

\displaystyle|\int_{1}^{\infty}H_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(z,0,t)H^{0}_{Y}(p,p_{0},t)dt|\leqslant c\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}{\sqrt{|z|}}.

On the other hand,
|\sum_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}t}v_{i}(p)v_{i}(q)|\leqslant ct^{-n/2}
when
t\leqslant 1
.

\displaystyle|\int_{0}^{1}H_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(z,0,t)H^{0}_{Y}(p,p_{0},t)dt|

\displaystyle\leqslant c\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{4t}}}{t^{n/2+1}}dt

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c}{|z|^{n}}\int_{|z|^{2}/4}^{+\infty}e^{-\tau}\tau^{n/2-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{c}{|z|^{n}}(\int_{|z|^{2}/4}^{+\infty}e^{-\tau}\tau^{n-2}d\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}(\int_{|z|^{2}/4}^{+\infty}e^{-\tau}d\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\frac{e^{-|z|^{2}/8}}{|z|^{n}}

Since the slowest decaying rate is of
\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}{\sqrt{|z|}}
for
|z|\to\infty
in the above estimates, we derived that
|G^{0}((z,p),(0,p_{0}))|\leqslant c\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}{\sqrt{|z|}}
.
∎

We also need the following estimates for higher order derivatives on Green’s function.

Lemma 3.8
.

For
k=1,2
, when
d\to 0
,
|\nabla^{k}G^{0}((z,p),(0,p_{0}))|\leqslant c\frac{1}{d^{n-2+k}}
. When
d\to\infty
,
|\nabla^{k}G^{0}((z,p),(0,p_{0}))|\leqslant c\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}}|z|}}{\sqrt{|z|}}
and
d\approx|z|
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the standard argument on the Green function.
For the second one, noticing that in the proof of the previous proposition, we only use the facts that
|\sum_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}t}v_{i}(p)v_{i}(q)|\leqslant ct^{-n}
when
t\leqslant 1
and
\sum_{i}e^{-\lambda_{i}t}v_{i}(p)v_{i}(q)\approx ce^{-\lambda_{1}t}
when
t>1
. These two are automatically satisfied for higher derivatives on the heat kernel on a compact Riemannian manifold.

∎

3.2.
Solving Poisson Equation on
\mathrm{ALG}_{1,2}
Manifolds

In this subsection we will solve the Poisson equation
\Delta_{g}u=f\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}
on
\mathrm{ALG}_{1,2}
manifolds and give the estimates on the derivatives of the solutions. Firstly, we reduce this Poisson equation to another one in which the potential
f
has faster decay and is also integrable.

Lemma 3.9
.

If
(M,J,g)
is an
\mathrm{ALG}_{1,2}
Kähler manifold. Then the solvability of Poisson equation
\Delta_{g}u=f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
is equivalent to the solvability of Poisson equation
\Delta_{g}u^{\prime}=f^{\prime}
, where
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mu}
for some
\mu>2
and
\int_{M}f^{\prime}=0
.

Proof.

We firstly prove this lemma when
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times D
is a product
\mathrm{ALG}_{1,2}
manifold.
In the following proof we will use
r
to denote the distance function to the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Since
g-g_{p}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
, we know that when
r
is sufficiently large, it is comparable with the distance function to som fixed point under
g
.

From the previous discussion we can find solution to
\Delta_{g_{P}}u_{1}=f
, in which
u_{1}=v_{1}+v_{2}
and
v_{1}\in\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{r\log r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
v_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times D)
. This is because we can decompose
f
into tow functions by integrating along fibers.

We then need to compare the difference between
\Delta_{g}
and
\Delta_{g_{P}}
. Denote
\eta=\omega_{g}-\omega_{g_{P}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
.
For any
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u=\frac{ni\partial\bar{\partial}u\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}}{\omega_{g}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{ni\partial\bar{\partial}u\wedge(\omega_{g_{P}}^{n-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{g_{P}}^{i}\wedge\eta^{n-1-i})}{\omega_{g}^{n}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\frac{ni\partial\bar{\partial}u\wedge\omega_{g_{P}}^{n-1}}{\omega_{g}^{n}}+\frac{ni\partial\bar{\partial}u\wedge\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{g_{P}}^{i}\wedge\eta^{n-1-i}}{\omega_{g}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{ni\partial\bar{\partial}u\wedge\omega_{g_{P}}^{n-1}}{\omega_{g}^{n}}+O^{\prime}(r^{-1})\nabla^{2}u+O^{\prime}(r^{-2})\nabla u

Notice that we have an rough estimation on

(3.8)

\displaystyle i\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}u=i\partial_{J_{P}}\bar{\partial}_{J_{P}}u+d(J-J_{P})du=i\partial_{J_{P}}\bar{\partial}_{J_{P}}u+O^{\prime}(r^{-1})\nabla^{2}u+O^{\prime}(r^{-1})\nabla u,

and
\frac{\omega_{g_{P}}^{n}}{\omega_{g}^{n}}-1\in C^{\infty}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}u-\Delta_{g_{P}}u

\displaystyle=\frac{d(J-J_{P})*du\wedge\omega_{g_{P}}^{n-1}+(d(J-J_{P})*du)^{2}\wedge\omega_{g_{P}}^{n-2}}{\omega_{g}^{n}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle+O^{\prime}(r^{-1})\nabla^{2}u+O^{\prime}(r^{-2})\nabla u

From (
3.2
) we derive
\Delta_{g}v_{2}-\Delta_{g_{P}}v_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{2}
.

Notice that
v_{1}
is a function on
\mathbb{C}
. We have

\displaystyle i\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}v_{1}

\displaystyle=i\partial_{J_{P}}\bar{\partial}_{J_{P}}v_{1}+d(J-J_{P})dv_{1}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g_{P}}v_{1}(\frac{i}{2}dwd\bar{w})+d(\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial w}K\circ dw+\frac{\partial v_{1}}{\partial\bar{w}}K\circ d\bar{w})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g_{P}}v_{1}(\frac{i}{2}dwd\bar{w})+\nabla^{2}v_{1}*\bar{\partial}_{J}w+\nabla v_{1}*i\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}w

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{g_{P}}v_{1}(\frac{i}{2}dwd\bar{w})+O(\frac{\log r}{r^{2}}).

So for any
\varepsilon<1
we have
\Delta_{g}v_{1}-\Delta_{g_{P}}v_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{2-\varepsilon}
.

Take
u=\tilde{u}+u_{1}
, then
\Delta_{g}u=f
is equivalent to solving

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{g}\tilde{u}=f-(\Delta_{g}-\Delta_{g_{P}})u_{1}\doteq\tilde{f}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{2-\varepsilon}.

We can repeat the above step one more time to deduce that the initial Poisson equation is equivalent to solving
\Delta_{g}\tilde{\tilde{u}}=\tilde{\tilde{f}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mu}
for some
\mu>2
. So
\tilde{\tilde{f}}
is now integrable.

Take
\phi=\chi\log r
, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)\times D}\Delta_{g_{P}}\phi\;\text{dVol}_{g_{P}}=\int_{\partial(B_{R}(0))\times D}\partial_{\nu}\phi\;\text{dVol}_{g_{P}}=2\pi

Similarly we have
\int_{B_{R}(0)\times D}\Delta_{g}\phi\;\text{dVol}_{g}=c>0
. Use the same method we can show
\Delta_{g}\phi=(\Delta_{g}-\Delta_{g_{P}})\phi+\Delta_{g_{P}}\phi=O^{\prime}(r^{-3})
.
By subtracting a multiple of
\Delta_{g}\phi
from
\tilde{\tilde{f}}
, we can assume that
\int\tilde{\tilde{f}}\text{dVol}_{g}=0
.

Now we discuss the case where
M
is not a product. We first fix a diffeomorphism and view the potential
f
as the restriction of some global function
f^{\prime}
on the product manifold. Then we solve
\Delta_{g_{P}}u^{\prime}=f^{\prime}
on the product manifold. This solution gives exactly the solution on the end of
M
under the product metric. Let
\zeta
be a cutoff function on
M
and vanishes in
K
.
\zeta\cdot u^{\prime}
is now a function on
M
and solves
\Delta_{g_{P}}\zeta\cdot u^{\prime}=f^{\prime}
when
R\gg 1
. The analysis on the new potential and some further modification follows the same routine as before.
∎

Secondly, we solve the Poisson equation on
\mathrm{ALG}_{1,2}
with this modified potential. The proof of the following proposition comes mainly from
[
Hei10
]
. The key difference is that we used our new Sobolev inequality.

Proposition 3.10

(
[
Hei10
]
)
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mu}
where
\mu>2
and
\int_{M}f=0
, there exist
u\in C^{\infty}
such that
\Delta u=f
and
\int|\nabla u|^{2}<\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose
\Omega\subset M
is a bounded open subset, and
u
is the unique solution to
\Delta u=f|_{\Omega}-f_{\Omega}
on
\Omega
and
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Here
f_{\Omega}
is the average of
f
on
\Omega
. Actually we can get
L^{\infty}
bound of
f_{\Omega}
. Suppose
B(x_{0},r)\subset B(x_{0},2r)
, then
|f_{\Omega}|\leqslant\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{M\Omega}|f|\leqslant Cr^{-\mu}
. In the following argument we should think of
\Omega=B(x_{0},r)
for any
r\gg 1
. And we will derive uniform estimates on
u
, which don’t depend on
\Omega
.

For any
p>1
, we multiply both sides of the equation by
u|u|^{p-2}
and integrate over
\Omega
to get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla|u|^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{2}}{4(p-1)}(\int_{\partial\Omega}u|u|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}u-\int_{\Omega}u|u|^{p-2}(f|_{\Omega}-f_{\Omega}))

\displaystyle=-\frac{p^{2}}{4(p-1)}\int_{\Omega}u|u|^{p-2}(f|_{\Omega}-f_{\Omega})

The above equation still hold true if we replace
u
by
u+\lambda
for any constant
\lambda
. This is because
u+\lambda
also solves the Poisson equation, with boundary value being
\lambda
. Since
\int_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu}u=-\int_{\Omega}\Delta u=0
still holds true, the boundary term in the equation will vanish.

Using Prop and
p=2
,
\lambda=\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
. If
\varepsilon\leqslant\mu-2
, we have

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{2\sigma}^{2}\leqslant C||\nabla u^{2}_{2}||

\displaystyle\leqslant C||r^{-\mu}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C||r^{-\mu+1+\varepsilon}||_{(2\sigma)^{*}}\cdot||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{2\sigma}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime}||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{2\sigma}

This implies that
||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})||_{2\sigma}
is uniformly bounded.

Then we take
p=2\sigma^{k}
and
u=u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
to get

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}(|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{\sigma^{k}}-\overline{|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{\sigma^{k}}})||_{2\sigma}^{2}\leqslant C||\nabla|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{\sigma^{k}}||^{2}_{2}\leqslant C\sigma^{k}||r^{-\mu}|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma^{k}-1}||_{1}

Denote
u_{k}=|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{\sigma^{k}}
. Then take
\alpha=\frac{2\sigma^{k}}{2\sigma^{k}-1}
,
\beta=2\sigma^{k}
,
{l=(1+\varepsilon)(2\sigma-\frac{1}{\sigma^{k-1}})\leqslant 2\varepsilon(1+\sigma)}

\displaystyle||r^{-\mu}|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma^{k}-1}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant||r^{l-\mu}||_{\beta}||r^{-l}|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{2\sigma^{k}-1}||_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leqslant C||r^{2\sigma(1+\varepsilon)-\mu}||_{\beta}||r^{-1-\varepsilon}u_{k}||_{2\sigma}^{(2\sigma-\frac{1}{\sigma^{k-1}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\max\{1,||r^{-1-\varepsilon}u_{k}||_{2\sigma}^{2\sigma}\}

The last inequality holds true if we first pick
2\sigma<\mu
then pick
\varepsilon
such that
2\sigma(1+\varepsilon)<\mu
. The constant
C
in the above inequality doesn’t depend on the region
\Omega
.
Observed that if
\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}\bar{u}_{k}||_{2\sigma}^{2}

\displaystyle=||r^{-1-\varepsilon}||_{2\sigma}^{2}||\psi_{\varepsilon}u_{k}||_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C||r^{(1+\varepsilon)\sigma-2\varepsilon-2}||_{2}^{2}||r^{-1-\varepsilon}u_{k}||_{2\sigma}^{2\sigma}

\displaystyle\leqslant C^{\prime}||r^{-1-\varepsilon}u_{k}||_{2\sigma}^{2\sigma}

The last inequality holds true if we take
\sigma<2
. Then we need to pick
\varepsilon\in(\frac{\sigma-1}{2-\sigma},\frac{\mu}{2\sigma}-1)
.
\frac{\mu}{2\sigma}-1>\frac{\sigma-1}{2-\sigma}
is automatically guaranteed given by
\mu>2
. Combining all the above inequalities we have

\displaystyle||r^{-1-\varepsilon}|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{\sigma^{k}}||_{2\sigma}^{2}\leqslant C\sigma^{k}\max\{1,||r^{-1-\varepsilon}|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{\sigma^{k-1}}||_{2\sigma}^{2\sigma}\}

Denote
\text{dV}^{\prime}=r^{-1-\varepsilon}\text{dV}|_{\Omega}
. Then these are uniform mearsures with respect to
\Omega
. The above inequality can be rewrite into

\displaystyle||u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}||_{2\sigma^{k},\text{dV}^{\prime}}\leqslant(C\sigma^{k})^{\frac{1}{2\sigma^{k}}}\max\{1,||u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}||_{2\sigma^{k-1},\text{dV}^{\prime}}\}

Since
||u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}||u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}||_{2\sigma^{k},\text{dV}^{\prime}}
and
\prod_{k=2}^{+\infty}(C\sigma^{k})^{\frac{1}{2\sigma^{k}}}<C^{\prime}
, we get the uniform bound
|u-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant C
. Since
u=0
on the boundary, we have
|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant C
, which implies that
|u|\leqslant C
.

The higher order estimates follows from the interior estimates for Poisson equations. We can take an exhaustion
\Omega_{i}
of
M
together with the solutions
\Delta u_{i}=f|_{\Omega_{i}}-f_{\Omega_{i}}
for each open region. By passing to a subsequence of these solutions they will converge to a smooth solution
u
on the whole manifold
M
satisfying
\Delta u=f
. When taking
p=2
and noticing that we have uniform
L^{\infty}
estimate on
u
, we get
\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\leqslant C
.
∎

To estimate the decay rate of derivatives of
u
, we first invoke a weighted Schauder estimate due to Hein and Tosatti
[
HT20
]
.

Proposition 3.11

(
[
HT20
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)=(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times D,g_{P}=g_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+g_{D})
, where
(D,g_{D})
is a compact Riemannian manifold. Then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geqslant 2}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
there is a constant
C_{k}=C_{k}(\alpha)
such that for all

and

\displaystyle[\nabla^{k,g_{P}}u]_{C^{\alpha}(B(p,\rho))}\leqslant C_{k}[\nabla^{k-2,g_{P}}\Delta_{g_{P}}u]_{C^{\alpha}(B(p,R))}+(R-\rho)^{-k-\alpha}||u||_{L^{\infty}(B(p,R))})

.

We need to be careful when using this proposition, since it was proved under the product metric and doesn’t depend on the complex structures.

Proposition 3.12
.

Under the assumption of
\mathrm{ALG}_{1}
settings, we have
|u|\leqslant C
,
\nabla u\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta}^{\infty}
,
\nabla^{2}u\in\mathcal{C}_{1+\delta}^{\infty}
.
i\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}u\in\mathcal{C}_{1+\delta}^{\infty}

Proof.

Firstly we can fix a diffeomorphism
\Psi:M\setminus K\to B^{c}(0,R)\times D
.
Let
M_{R}=\Psi^{-1}(B^{c}(0,R)\times Y)
and
Q_{R}=\int_{M_{R}}|\nabla u|^{2}
where
R\gg 1
. Using the equation and integration by parts, we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\int_{r>R}|\nabla u|^{2}=-\int_{r>R}uf+\int_{r=R}u\partial_{\nu}u

This equality still holds after we subtract
u
by any constant
\lambda
. We can take
\lambda
to be the average of
u
over
\{r=R\}
.
Using the fact that
g-g_{P}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
and Poincare inequality we have

\displaystyle Q_{R}\leqslant CR^{-(\mu-2)}+C\int_{r=R}|u-\lambda||\nabla u|

\displaystyle\leqslant CR^{-(\mu-2)}+CR\int_{r=R}|\nabla u|^{2}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=CR^{-(\mu-2)}-CR\frac{dQ_{R}}{dR}.

Then it is not hard to deduce that

(3.15)

\displaystyle Q_{R}\leqslant C_{1}R^{-\frac{1}{c}}+C_{2}R^{-(\mu-2)}\leqslant CR^{-\delta}.

where
\delta=\min(\frac{1}{C},\mu-2)
.

Denote
B=B(p,1)
for some

sufficiently far from a given point in
K
, and
u_{B}
be the average of
u
on
B
. Then
||u-u_{B}||_{L^{2}(B)}\leqslant Cr^{-\delta}
. Since
\Delta_{g}(u-u_{B})=f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
on
B
and
\Delta_{g}
is uniformly elliptic with respect to
g_{P}
, Moser iteration shows that
|u-u_{B}|\leqslant Cr^{-\delta}
on a slightly smaller domain. Standard Schauder estimates shows
|\nabla^{k}u|\leqslant Cr^{-\delta}
for
k\geqslant 1
.

Next
by checking the difference between two Laplacians, we have that
|\nabla^{k,g_{P}}\Delta_{g_{P}}u|=O(r^{-1-\delta})
.
For any
p\in M_{R}
take
0<\frac{R}{4}<\frac{R}{2}<d(p,K)
,
\alpha<\min\{\mu-2,\delta\}
in proposition 8.11, we get

(3.16)

\displaystyle[\nabla^{2}u]_{C^{\alpha}(B(p,\frac{R}{4}))}\leqslant C_{k}([\Delta u]_{C^{\alpha}(B(p,\frac{R}{2}))}+\frac{4^{2+\alpha}}{R^{2+\alpha}}||u||_{L^{\infty}(B(p,\frac{R}{2}))})

Since
||u||_{L^{\infty}}\leqslant C
, we have
[\nabla^{2}u]_{C^{\alpha}(B(p,\frac{R}{4}))}=O(R^{-1-\delta})
. When integrating from infinity we have
|\nabla^{2,g_{P}}u|=O(R^{-1-\delta+\alpha})
.

Taking
\nabla_{\mathbb{C}}
to the equation
\Delta_{g_{P}}u=O(r^{-1-\delta})
and repeating the above argument using the weighted Schauder estimate, we can derive that
|\nabla^{l}_{\mathbb{C}}\nabla_{Y}u|=O(r^{-l-\delta^{\prime}})
and
|\nabla^{l}_{\mathbb{C}}\nabla^{2}_{Y}u|=O(r^{-l-1-\delta^{\prime}})
for any

. We can adjust

such that

. Since

and

.

∎

It is possible that we might have a better estimate of the decay rate of the derivatives or the growth rate of

. But it turns out that the estimate in proposition

is enough to prove the final theorem.

Lastly, we arrive at our main theorem in this section:

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(M,g,J)
be an
\mathrm{ALG}_{1,2}
manifold. For any
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
, there is a solution to the Poisson equation
\Delta_{g}u=f
. Moreover, when fixing the diffeomorphism of the end, we have a decomposition of

satisfying

(1)

,
u_{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{\infty}_{r\log r}
;

(2)

u_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}
;

(3)

u_{3}
is bounded.
\nabla u_{3}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\delta}
,
\nabla^{2}u_{3}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1+\delta}
.

Moreover,
i\partial_{J}\bar{\partial}_{J}u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{1}.

4.
The Holomorphic Splitting Theorem

Denote
\pi:=U\rightarrow\Delta
corresponding to
\Phi
.
Given any Kähler metric
\omega_{0}
over
\hskip 0.0pt\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0muM\mkern-1.0mu}\mkern 1.0mu
, we can find a unique

Take a smooth function

) can be rewritten into

(A.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u_{n}-n^{2}u_{n}=e^{2t}f_{n}(e^{t})

Let
s=nt
, we have

(A.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}^{2}u_{n}-u_{n}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}e^{\frac{2s}{n}}f

The solutions to these equations depend on the values of
\mu
and
n
.

Case 1:

\mu>1
:

Since now
f=O(e^{-\frac{\mu s}{n}})
, we can safely write

(A.13)

\displaystyle e^{-s}(\partial_{s}u_{n}+u_{n})=-\int_{s}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}e^{(\frac{2}{n}-1)\lambda}f_{n}d\lambda,

because
\frac{2}{n}-1-\frac{\mu}{n}<0
. Now
e^{-s}(\partial_{s}u_{n}+u_{n})
is a constant when
s<0
and
e^{-s}(\partial_{s}u_{n}+u_{n})=O(e^{(\frac{2-\mu}{n}-1)s})
when
s\to+\infty
. We can do integration one more time to get

(A.14)

\displaystyle u_{n}=e^{-s}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{2\sigma}(-\int_{\sigma}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}e^{(\frac{2}{n}-1)\lambda}f_{n}d\lambda)d\sigma.

Assume
|f_{n}(s)|\leqslant C_{n}e^{-\frac{\mu s}{n}}
, then

(A.15)

\displaystyle|u_{n}(s)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{n}\frac{e^{-s}}{n^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{2\sigma}\int_{\sigma}^{+\infty}e^{(\frac{2-\mu}{n}-1)\lambda}\chi_{s>0}d\lambda d\sigma

(A.16)

\displaystyle=C_{n}\frac{e^{-s}}{n(n+\mu-2)}\int_{-\infty}^{s}(e^{(\frac{2-\mu}{n}+1)\sigma}\chi_{\sigma>0}+e^{2\sigma}\cdot\chi_{\sigma\leqslant 0})d\sigma

(A.17)

\displaystyle=C_{n}\left(\frac{1}{2n(n+\mu-2)}(e^{s}\chi_{s\leqslant 0}+e^{-s}\chi_{s>0})\right.

(A.18)

\displaystyle\phantom{=C_{n}\Big(}\left.+\frac{1}{n^{2}-(2-\mu)^{2}}(e^{\frac{2-\mu}{n}s}-e^{-s})\chi_{s>0}\right)

where
\chi_{s>0}
is the characteristic function. So
u_{n}\to 0
when
s\to 0
and when
s\to+\infty

(A.19)

\displaystyle|u_{n}|\leqslant C_{n}^{\prime}\cdot\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \left(\frac{1}{4n^{2}}+\frac{1}{(2-\mu)^{2}-n^{2}}\right)e^{-s}&=O\left(r^{-n}\right)&&\frac{2-\mu}{n}+1<0,\\
\frac{1}{4n^{2}}se^{-s}&=O\left(r^{2-\mu}\log r\right)&&\frac{2-\mu}{n}+1=0,\\
\frac{1}{n^{2}-(2-\mu)^{2}}e^{\frac{2-\mu}{n}s}&=O\left(r^{2-\mu}\right)&&\frac{2-\mu}{n}+1>0.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Case 2:

\mu=1
and
n>1
:

In this case, since we still have
\frac{2}{n}-1-\frac{\mu}{n}<0
, the solutions can also be derived from equation(
A.14
). Then
|u_{n}|=O\left(r^{2-\mu}\right)
.

Case 3:

\mu=1
and
n=1
:

In this case, we use different integration formulas according to the decomposition
\partial_{s}^{2}u_{n}-u_{n}=\partial_{s}(\partial_{s}u_{n}-u_{n})+(\partial_{s}u_{n}-u_{n})
:

(A.20)

\displaystyle u_{1}(s)=e^{s}\int_{-\infty}^{s}e^{-2\sigma}(\int_{-\infty}^{\sigma}e^{3\lambda}f_{1}d\lambda)d\sigma.

Since
|f_{1}|\leqslant C\,r^{-1}
, we have
|u_{1}|\leqslant C\,r\log r
, and
u_{1}
equals
0
when
s\leqslant 0
.

Since
\partial_{r}u_{n}=\frac{n}{r}\partial_{s}u_{n}
, the decay or growth rates mainly follow that
|\partial_{r}^{k}u_{n}|\sim\frac{n^{k}}{r^{k}}|u_{n}|
when
r\gg 1
. When
\mu=1
and
n=1
, a more delicate analysis shows that
|\partial_{r}u_{1}|=O\left(\log r\right)
,
|\partial^{k}_{r}u_{1}|=O\left(r^{-k+1}\right)
for
k\geqslant 2
.

Define
v_{n}=\sum_{i={-n}}^{n}u_{i}e^{in\theta}-u_{0}
. Then
v_{n}
is uniformly convergent on every compact subset in
\mathbb{C}
. Since
f
is smooth on
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies condition (
A.1
), we can use integration by parts to get estimates on
f_{n}
involving
n
. Then we can derive higher order estimates on every compact subset. Taking the limit when
n\to\infty
leads to a solution to
\Delta v=f-\Delta u_{0}
. We now summarize the behavior of the solutions
v
according to different decaying rates
\mu
:

Lemma A.2
.

Let
v
be the limit of
v_{n}
when
n\to+\infty
. Then we have:

(1)

when
\mu=1
,
v=O\left(r\log r\right)
,
\partial_{r}v=O\left(\log r\right)
,
\partial^{k}_{r}v=O\left(r^{-k+1}\right)
for
k\geqslant 2
;

(2)

when
\mu\in(1,2)\cup(2,3)
,
\partial^{k}_{r}v=O\left(r^{\max\{-1,2-\mu\}-k}\right)
for all
k\geqslant 0
;

(3)

when
\mu\in\{2,3\}
,
|\partial^{k}_{r}v|=O\left(r^{2-\mu-k}\log r\right)
for all
k\geqslant 0
;

(4)

Otherwise,
\partial^{k}_{r}v=O\left(r^{-1-k}\right)
for all
k\geqslant 0
.

Lastly, we still need to solve the Poisson equation when the potential
f
has compact support.

Lemma A.3
.

Assume
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is compactly supported in
B_{2}(0)
, then there is a smooth solution to the equation
\Delta u=f
. Moreover,
|u|=O\left(\log r\right)
,
|\nabla^{k}u|=O\left(r^{-k}\right)
for
k\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

Let
v
be the solution to the Dirichlet problem:

(A.21)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta v=f&\text{in }B_{3}(0)\\
v=0&\text{on }\partial B_{3}(0)\end{cases}.

Denote
v_{1}=v(1-\eta(\frac{r}{3}))
, then
f-\Delta v_{1}
is compactly supported in
B_{6}(0)\setminus B_{3}(0)
. Using Lemma(
A.1
), (
A.2
) we can find a solution to
\Delta v_{2}=f-\Delta v_{1}
satisfying
|v_{2}|=O\left(\log r\right)
,
|\nabla^{k}v_{2}|=O\left(r^{-k}\right)
for
k\geqslant 1
. Then
u=v_{1}+v_{2}
.
∎

Combining the results in Lemma(
A.1
), (
A.2
) and (
A.3
), we can derive the following estimates on the solutions to the Poisson equation:

Lemma A.4
.

Let
f
be a smooth funtion on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfying the condition(
A.1
) for some
\mu\geqslant 1
. Then there exists a solution to the Poisson equation
\Delta u=f
. And

(1)

if
\mu=1
, then
u=O\left(r\log r\right)
,
|\nabla u|=O\left(\log r\right)
,
|\nabla^{k}u|=O\left(r^{-k+1}\right)
for
k\geqslant 2
;

(2)

if
\mu\in(1,2)
, then
|\nabla^{k}u|=O\left(r^{2-\mu}\right)
for
k\geqslant 0
;

(3)

if
\mu=2
, then
u=O\left((\log r^{2})\right)
,
|\nabla^{k}u|=O\left(\frac{\log r}{r}\right)
for
k\geqslant 1
;

(4)

if
\mu>2
, then
u=O\left(\log r\right)
,
|\nabla^{k}u|=O\left(r^{-k}\right)
for
k\geqslant 1
.

The solution is unique up to linear harmonic functions.

Remark A.5
.

One can also apply a weighted Schauder estimate on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to see the decay rate of higher order derivatives of
v
. By using rescaling we have

(A.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}R^{l}|\nabla^{l}v|_{L^{\infty}B_{R}(p)}+R^{k+\alpha}[\nabla^{k}v]_{C^{\alpha}(B_{R}(p))}\leqslant C(R^{k+\alpha}|\nabla^{k-2}\Delta v|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{2R}(p))}+|v|_{L^{\infty}B_{2R}(p)}).

But this estimate might not be optimal for some
\mu
’s.

Constrained quantization for discrete distributions
1 Bismark Bimpong, 2 Sayandip Pandit, 3 Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury, 4 Prabhat Tamrakar

Abstract
. Constrained quantization for a Borel probability measure refers to the process of approximating a given probability distribution by a discrete probability measure supported on a finite number of points that lie within a specified set, known as the constraint . This paper investigates constrained quantization for several types of discrete probability distributions under two different constraints. The study begins with an analysis of two discrete distributions, one uniform and one nonuniform, defined over a finite support. For each distribution, the constrained optimal sets of representative points and the corresponding constrained quantization errors are computed under both constraints. The analysis is then extended to an infinite discrete distribution supported on the reciprocals of the natural numbers. For this infinite case, and under two different constraints, the paper determines all possible constrained optimal sets of n n -points and the associated n n th constrained quantization errors for all 1 ≤ n ≤ 2000 1\leq n\leq 2000 . The problem of constrained quantization for n > 2000 n>2000 in this infinite setting remains open.

1.
Introduction

Quantization refers to the transformation of a continuous analog signal into a digital signal with
k
discrete levels, or the reduction of a digital signal with
n
levels to one with
k
levels, where
k<n
. This process is fundamental for representing, processing, storing, and transmitting analog information in digital systems, as well as for achieving data compression. It remains a central and actively studied topic in source coding and information theory, with widespread applications across engineering and technology—particularly in data compression and clustering. For foundational work, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
More recently, the scope of quantization theory has expanded with the development of constrained and conditional quantization (see
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
). This has led to a classification of the theory into two principal areas: constrained and unconstrained quantization. For a concise overview and practical entry point into both branches, see
[
8
]
. For a deeper exploration of unconstrained quantization, refer to
[
11
]
. The present paper focuses specifically on constrained quantization.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with the Euclidean metric
d
induced by a norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
\alpha\subseteq S
be a locally finite subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; that is, the intersection of
\alpha
with any bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is finite. This implies that
\alpha
is countable and closed.
The distortion error for the measure
P
, with respect to the set
\alpha\subseteq S
, is defined by

V(P;\alpha)=\int\min_{a\in\alpha}\|x-a\|^{2}\,dP(x).

Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the
n
th constrained quantization error
for
P
with respect to the set
S
is defined by

(1)

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf\left\{V(P;\alpha):\alpha\subseteq S,\ 1\leq\operatorname{card}(\alpha)\leq n\right\},

where
\operatorname{card}(A)
denotes the cardinality of a set
A
.
Let
U
be the largest open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
P(U)=0
. Then, the set
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus U
is called the
support of
P
, denoted by
\operatorname{supp}(P)
.
For a locally finite set
\alpha\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a point
a\in\alpha
, define

M(a|\alpha)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:d(x,a)=\min_{b\in\alpha}d(x,b)\right\}.

The set
M(a|\alpha)
is called the
Voronoi region
generated by
a\in\alpha
. The collection

\left\{M(a|\alpha):a\in\alpha\right\}

is called the
Voronoi diagram
(or
Voronoi tessellation
) of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with respect to
\alpha
.
A set
\alpha
for which the infimum in (
1
) is attained and
\operatorname{card}(\alpha)\leq n
is called a
constrained optimal set of
n
-points
for
P
with respect to the constraint
S
.
If the support of
P
contains at least
N
elements, then for any
n\leq N
, an optimal set of
n
-points contains exactly
n
elements (see
[
8
]
). If
S=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e., there is no constraint, then the notion of constrained quantization error reduces to the classical definition of unconstrained quantization error, often simply referred to as the quantization error (see
[
11
]
). In the unconstrained setting, if the support of
P
contains at least
n
elements, then each element in an optimal set of
n
-points corresponds to the conditional expectation over its own Voronoi region. Due to this property, an optimal set of
n
-points in unconstrained quantization is also referred to as an optimal set of
n
-means.

1.1.
Delineation

In this paper, we study constrained quantization for both finite and infinite discrete distributions. Section
2
provides the necessary preliminaries. We begin by considering two specific geometric constraints: one defined by a semicircular arc with base on the line segment
[-3,3]
, and the other formed by the union of two sides of an equilateral triangle with the same base. Under these constraints, we determine the constrained optimal sets of
n
-points and the corresponding

.
Section
3
is devoted to the case of a uniform discrete distribution supported on the set
\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}
, and is divided into two subsections corresponding to the two constraints. Section
4
mirrors this structure but addresses a nonuniform discrete distribution supported on the same set.
In Section
5
, we consider an infinite discrete distribution supported on the set
\left\{\frac{1}{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
. The first subsection deals with the semicircular arc with base
[0,1]
as the constraint, for which we compute the constrained optimal sets
n
-points and the corresponding
n

n\in\mathbb{N}
. In the second subsection, using the constraint formed by two sides of an equilateral triangle with base
[0,1]
, we perform the same computations for

ranging from

to

. An open problem is also mentioned at the end of this subsection.

2.
Preliminaries

For any two elements
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the squared Euclidean distance is given by

\rho((a,b),(c,d)):=(a-c)^{2}+(b-d)^{2}.

Two elements
p,q\in\alpha
are called
adjacent
if their Voronoi regions share a common boundary. Let
e
be a point on the boundary between the Voronoi regions of
p
and
q
. Since this boundary is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment connecting
p
and
q
, it satisfies the
canonical equation

\rho(p,e)-\rho(q,e)=0.

Notice that any real number
x\in\mathbb{R}
can be identified with the element
(x,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Using this identification, we define a function

\rho:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,\infty)\text{ such that }\rho(x,(a,b))=(x-a)^{2}+b^{2},

which represents the squared Euclidean distance between
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-points for a Borel probability measure
P
. If a point in the support of
P
lies on the common boundary of two Voronoi regions generated by elements of
\alpha_{n}
, then the point is assigned to one of the Voronoi regions.

Remark 2.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\alpha_{n}
denote an optimal set of
n
-points for a Borel probability measure
P
with respect to a constraint
S
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, for
\alpha=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\}\subseteq S
, let us define the sets
A_{a_{i}|\alpha}
for
a_{i}\in\alpha
as follows:

(2)

\displaystyle A_{a_{i}|\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}M(a_{1}|\alpha)&\text{ if }i=1,\\
M(a_{i}|\alpha)\setminus\bigcup_{k<i}M(a_{k}|\alpha)&\text{ if }i\geq 2,\end{array}\right.

where
M(a_{i}|\alpha)
is the
Voronoi region
generated by
a_{i}\in\alpha
.
The set
\{A_{a_{i}|\alpha}:a_{i}\in\alpha\}
is called the
Voronoi partition
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with respect to the set
\alpha
(and
S
). Then, for some positive integer
n
, an optimal set

does not exist, by that it is meant that there is no
\alpha\subseteq S
containing at least
n
elements such that

and vice versa.

Remark 2.3
.

In view of Remark
2.2
, for some positive integer
n
if a constrained optimal set
\alpha_{n}
does not exist, then
\alpha_{k}
does not exist for any positive integer

.

Let

3.
Constrained quantization for a finite uniform discrete distribution

Let
P
be a finite uniform distribution with support
\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}
. Then, the probability mass function
f
for
P
is given by

f(j)=P(X:X=j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{7},&\text{ where }j\in\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Comparing with the list
\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{N}\}
as defined in Note
2.3.1
, we have

s_{j}=-4+j\text{ for }j\in\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}.

In the following two subsection we calculate the constrained optimal set of
n
-points for the probability distribution
P
for all possible values of
n\in\mathbb{N}
with respect to two different constraints.

3.1.
Constrained quantization when the constraint is a semicircular arc

In this case let us take the constraint
S
as the upper semicircular arc of the circle
x^{2}+y^{2}=9
, i.e.,

S:=\{(x,y):x^{2}+y^{2}=9\text{ and }y\geq 0\}.

Notice that
S
can also be represented by

S=\{(3\cos\theta,3\sin\theta):0\leq\theta\leq\pi\}.

We know that an optimal set of one-point always exists. For any
n\geq 2
, since the boundary of the Voronoi regions of any two optimal elements, in this case, passes through the center of the circle, from the geometry, we see that among
n
Voronoi regions, only two Voronoi regions contain elements from the support of
P
, i.e., only two Voronoi regions have positive probability. Hence, in view of Remark
2.2
and Remark
2.3
, the optimal sets of
n
-points exist only for
n=1
and
n=2
, and they do not exist for any
n\geq 3
.

We now calculate the optimal sets of one-point and two-points in the following propositions.

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Any element on the circle forms an optimal set of one-point with constrained quantization error
V_{1}=13
.

Proof.

Let
(3\cos\theta,3\sin\theta)
be an element on the circle. Then, the distortion error for
P
with respect to the element is given by

\displaystyle V(P;\{(3\cos\theta,3\sin\theta)\})=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-3}^{3}\frac{1}{7}\rho(j,(3\cos\theta,3\sin\theta))=13

which does not depend on
\theta
. Hence, any element on the circle forms an optimal set of one-point with constrained quantization error
V_{1}=13
.
∎

Proposition 3.1.2
.

The set
\{(-3,0),(3,0)\}
forms an optimal set of two-points with constrained quantization error
V_{2}=\frac{19}{7}
.

Proof.

Let
\{(3\cos\theta_{1},3\sin\theta_{1}),(3\cos\theta_{2},3\sin\theta_{2})\}
be an optimal set of two-points, where
0\leq\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}\leq\pi
.
Since the boundary of any two elements on the circle passes through the center of the circle, in an optimal set of two-points, one Voronoi region will contain the left half, and the other Voronoi region will contain the right half of the support of
P
. Notice that the support element
0
falls on the boundary of the two Voronoi regions. In that case, as mentioned in Remark
2.1
, we can assume that
0
belongs to the Voronoi region of
(3\cos\theta_{2},3\sin\theta_{2})
. Then, the distortion error is given by

\displaystyle V(P;\{(3\cos\theta_{1},3\sin\theta_{1}),(3\cos\theta_{2},3\sin\theta_{2})\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-3}^{0}\frac{1}{7}\rho(j,(3\cos\theta_{2},3\sin\theta_{2}))+\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{7}\rho(j,(3\cos\theta_{1},3\sin\theta_{1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{7}\left(-36\cos\theta_{1}+36\cos\theta_{2}+91\right)

which is minimum if
\theta_{1}=0
and
\theta_{2}=\pi
, and the minimum value is
\frac{19}{7}
. Thus, the set
\{(-3,0),(3,0)\}
forms an optimal set of two-points with constrained quantization error
V_{2}=\frac{19}{7}
.
∎

3.2.
Constrained quantization when the constraint is the two sides of an equilateral triangle

Let us consider the equilateral triangle with vertices

(4)

\displaystyle S_{1}=\{(x,\sqrt{3}(x+3)):-3\leq x\leq 0\}\text{ and }S_{2}=\{(x,\sqrt{3}(-x+3)):0\leq x\leq 3\}.

Proposition 3.2.1
.

An optimal set of one-point is given by
\{(-\frac{9}{4},\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4})\}
or
\{(\frac{9}{4},\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4})\}
with constrained quantization error
V_{1}=\frac{43}{4}

Proof.

Let

be an optimal set of one-point. The following two cases can happen:

In this case,

Case 2.

1\leq j\leq m-1
, are the adjacent elements.

Notation 5.2.2
.

To be specific, in this subsection,
V_{n}^{(c)}
represents the constrained quantization error for
n
-points, and
V_{n}^{(u)}
represents the unconstrained quantization error for
n
-means with respect to the probability distribution
P
.

The following proposition plays an important role.

Proposition 5.2.3
.

Let
\alpha_{n}:=\{c_{j}:1\leq j\leq k+m\}
, where
\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}^{(1)})=k
and
\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}^{(2)})=m
. Then,
\alpha_{n}
is a constrained optimal set of
n
-points for
P
if and only if
U_{1}^{-1}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}^{(1)})\cup U_{2}^{-1}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{2}^{(2)})
forms an unconstrained optimal set of
n
-means for
P
, and

V_{n}^{(c)}=V_{n}^{(u)}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(1-Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+m}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2},

where the Voronoi region of
c_{j}
contains the set
[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]
from the support of
P
for
1\leq j\leq n
, where
q_{n}^{(2)}=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n}
be a constrained optimal set of
n
-points for
P
. Let
\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}^{(1)})=k
and
\text{card}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{2}^{(2)})=m
. Then, we can write

\alpha_{n}=\Big\{(a_{1},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{1})),(a_{2},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{2})),\cdots,(a_{k},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{k})),(a_{k+1},\sqrt{3}a_{k+1}),\cdots,(a_{k+m},\sqrt{3}a_{k+m})\Big\},

where
0\leq a_{k+m}<a_{k+m-1}<\cdots<a_{k+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\leq a_{k}<\cdots<a_{2}<a_{1}\leq 1
and
k+m=n
. Let
c_{j}=(a_{j},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))
if
1\leq j\leq k
, and
c_{j}=(a_{j},\sqrt{3}a_{j})
if
k+1\leq j\leq k+m
. Let the Voronoi region of
c_{j}
contains the set
[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]
from the support of
P
for
1\leq j\leq n
, where
q_{n}^{(2)}=\infty
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle V_{n}^{(c)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{i},(a_{j},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))\Big)+\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+m}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{i},(a_{j},\sqrt{3}a_{j})\Big).

Notice that for
1\leq j\leq k
, the distortion error contributed by
c_{j}
on its own Voronoi region is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{i},(a_{j},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\Big((\frac{1}{i}-a_{j})^{2}+(\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\Big(\Big((\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))+(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])-a_{j})\Big)^{2}+(\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\Big((\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))^{2}+2(\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])-a_{j})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])-a_{j})^{2}+(\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\Big((\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))^{2}+(\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+2(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])-a_{j})\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))+(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])-a_{j})^{2}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\Big((\frac{1}{i}-\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))^{2}+(\sqrt{3}(1-a_{j}))^{2}\Big)+(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])-a_{j})^{2}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}.

Notice that the above distortion error is minimum if
a_{j}=\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])
,
and then

c_{j}=(\frac{1}{4}(3+Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]),\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(1-Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))=U_{1}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])

and the distortion error contributed by
c_{j}
on its own Voronoi region is given by

\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho(\frac{1}{i},U_{1}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])).

Similarly, for
k+1\leq j\leq k+m
, the distortion error
\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{i},(a_{j},\sqrt{3}a_{j})\Big)
is minimum if
a_{j}=\frac{1}{4}Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]
, and then

c_{j}=(\frac{1}{4}Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}],\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])=U_{2}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])

and the distortion error contributed by
c_{j}
on its own Voronoi region is given by

\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho(\frac{1}{i},U_{2}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])).

Thus, we see that

(9)

\alpha_{n}=\Big\{U_{1}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]):1\leq j\leq k\Big\}\bigcup\Big\{U_{2}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]):k+1\leq j\leq k+m\Big\}

forms a constrained optimal set of
n
-points for
P
with constrained quantization error

\displaystyle V_{n}^{(c)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho(\frac{1}{i},U_{1}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))+\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+m}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}\rho(\frac{1}{i},U_{2}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]))

Let us consider the set
\beta_{n}
given by

(10)

\beta_{n}=\Big\{Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}]:1\leq j\leq k+m\Big\}.

Notice that the unconstrained distortion error for
P
with respect to the set
\beta_{n}
, denoted by
V(P;\beta_{n})
, is defined as

V(P;\beta_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{k+m}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(\frac{1}{i}-Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2}.

After some calculation, we see that

(11)

V_{n}^{(c)}=V(P;\beta_{n})+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(1-Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+m}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2}.

From the above expression, we see that
V_{n}^{(c)}
will be minimum if and only if the distortion error
V(P;\beta_{n})
is minimum with respect to all possible subsets of
\text{supp}(P)
with cardinality
n
, i.e., when
V(P;\beta_{n})
becomes
V_{n}^{(u)}
. Thus, we can say that the set
\alpha_{n}
given by (
9
) will be a constrained optimal set of
n
-points for
P
if and only if the set
\beta_{n}
given by (
10
) becomes an unconstrained optimal set of
n
-means for
P
.

As
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are bijective functions, by (
9
) and (
10
), we see that if
\alpha_{n}
is a constrained optimal set of
n
-points and
\beta_{n}
is an unconstrained optimal set of
n
-means, then

U_{1}^{-1}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{1}^{(1)})\cup U_{2}^{-1}(\alpha_{n}\cap S_{2}^{(2)})=\beta_{n},

and by (
11
), we have

V_{n}^{(c)}=V_{n}^{(u)}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(1-Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\sum_{j=k+1}^{k+m}\sum_{i=q_{j}^{(1)}}^{q_{j}^{(2)}}\frac{1}{2^{i}}(Av[q_{j}^{(1)},q_{j}^{(2)}])^{2}.

Thus, the proof of the proposition is complete.
∎

Proposition 5.2.4
.

An constrained optimal set of one-point is given by
\{(\frac{1}{4}(3+\log 2),\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{3}(1-\log 2))\}
with constrained quantization error

V_{1}^{(c)}=\frac{1}{12}\left(\pi^{2}-9\left(-1+\log^{2}2+\log 4\right)\right).

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\{(a,b)\}
be an optimal set of one-point. The following two cases can happen:

Case 1.
(a,b)\in S_{1}
.

In this case,
b=\sqrt{3}(1-a)
for some
\frac{1}{2}\leq a\leq 1
. Then, the distortion error is given by

V(P;\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\rho(j,(a,b))=4a^{2}-2a(3+\log 2)+\frac{1}{12}\left(\pi^{2}+36-6\log^{2}2\right),

the minimum value of which is
\frac{1}{12}\left(\pi^{2}-9\left(-1+\log^{2}2+\log 4\right)\right)\approx 0.172407
, and it occurs when

.

Case 2.
(a,b)\in S_{2}
.

In this case,
b=\sqrt{3}a
for some
0\leq a\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then, the distortion error is given by

V(P;\alpha)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{j}}\rho(j,(a,b))=4a^{2}-a\log 4+\frac{1}{12}\left(\pi^{2}-6\log^{2}2\right),

the minimum value of which is
\frac{1}{12}\left(\pi^{2}-\log^{2}8\right)\approx 0.462127
, and it occurs when

.

Comparing the two distortion errors, we deduce that an optimal set of one-point is given by
\{(\frac{1}{4}(3+\log 2),-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{3}(\log 2-1))\}

.
∎

Proof.

\frac{1}{3}

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho\Big(1,(a_{1},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{1}))\Big)+\frac{1}{2^{2}}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{2},(a_{2},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{2}))\Big)+\frac{1}{2^{3}}\rho\Big(\frac{1}{j},(a_{3},\sqrt{3}(1-a_{3}))\Big)

On the Analysis of a Singular Stochastic Volterra Differential Equation driven by a Wiener Noise
Emmanuel Coffie Olivier Menoukeu-Pamen Frank Proske

Abstract
In this article, we construct unique strong solutions to a class of stochastic Volterra differential equations driven by a singular drift vector field and a Wiener noise. Further, we examine the Sobolev differentiability of the strong solution with respect to its initial value. keywords : Singular Volterra SDEs, Brownian motion, Malliavin calculus Mathematics Subject Classification (2010): 60H10, 49N60, 91G80.

Introduction

Stochastic Volterra differential equations (SDVs) were first studied by Berger and Mizel (
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
) and have since been successfully applied to modeling the dynamics of various phenomena, including population growth, the spread of epidemics, and (rough) stock price volatility in finance (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
). In contrast to classical stochastic differential equations, SVDEs incorporate time‑dependent kernels which introduce long‑range dependence. These equations are particularly effective in representing dynamical systems that exhibit non-Markovian behavior.

In this article, we study SVDEs with spatially singular and merely measurable drift coefficients, where classical Lipschitz or Hölder assumptions fail. In particular, we study the following stochastic Volterra
differential equation (SVDE)

X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{x})ds+B_{t},

(1)

0\leq t\leq T
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
b:\left[0,T\right]\times\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Borel-measurable function and
(B_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
is a d-dimensional Brownian motion (on a filtered probability space).
Our primary objective is to construct unique strong solutions to the stochastic Volterra differential equation (SVDE) (
1
), where the driving vector field

b
is spatially singular, that is, it is not Lipschitz continuous and may have discontinuities with respect to the variable
x
(and
s
). For this analysis, it is assumed that the driving vector field
b
in (
1
) admits a Volterra‑type expansion:

b(t,s,x)=\sum_{m\geq 0}(t-s)^{m}g_{m}(s,x),

(2)

0\leq s\leq t\leq T
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
g_{m}\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that for all
m\geq 0

\big|g_{m}\big|_{\infty}^{1-\epsilon}\leq C\frac{m^{(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon)m}}{m!}

(3)

for some
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and some constant
C<\infty
.

In this paper, our objective is to analyze the SVDE (
1
) for the class
\mathbb{H}
of vector fields
b\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the form (
2
). In fact, we prove
for vector fields
b\in\mathbb{H}
that the SVDE (
1
) has a unique
strong solution which is Malliavin differentiable and (
\mathbb{P}-
a.e.) locally
Sobolev differentiable with respect to the initial condition (see Theorem
1
). Our approach for the construction of strong solutions is based on techniques of Malliavin calculus which was developed in
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
in the context of singular stochastic differential equations with an additive Wiener noise.

We mention that in the case of a non-singular kernel the authors in
[
1
]
and
[
20
]
obtain a unique strong solution for
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuous diffusion coefficients on the real line by employing e.g. the theorem of Yamada-Watanabe. On the other hand, the case of singular
kernels (as e.g. of the type
(t-s)^{-\alpha}
) and spatially Lipschitz
continuous vector fields was investigated in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
.
Furthermore, based on PDE techniques the authors in
[
19
]
establish
pathwise uniqueness of one-dimensional SVDEs with singular kernels and
\xi
-Hölder continuous diffusion coefficients and drift coefficients not
depending on the solution. See also
[
21
]
for an extension of the
latter result to the case of time-inhomogeneous coefficients. As for the concept of weak solutions of singular SVDEs we refer to
[
2
]
,
[
19
]
and the references therein.

While the Malliavin framework is inspired by earlier work in
[
16
,
18
]
, the Volterra framework
creates new analytic difficulties that are absent in the Markovian SDE case:

-

the equation is non-Markovian and lacks the flow/semigroup structure that underlies many classical arguments;

-

the drift involves time-dependent memory via
\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,\cdot)\,ds
, which changes the structure of
Picard iterations and derivative representations;

-

new kernel-dependent estimates are required to control iterated integrals and ensure convergence on small
time intervals;

-

the proof of strong well-posedness under merely measurable drifts in this non-Markovian setting requires a careful combination of approximation, weak convergence, and identification of adapted limits.

Thus the present paper constitutes a genuine extension rather than a small technical adaptation and therefore:

-

establishes strong existence and uniqueness for non‑Markovian SVDEs with merely measurable drift.

-

proves a regularization by noise phenomenon in the Volterra setting, previously unknown.

-

derives Malliavin differentiability and Sobolev regularity in the initial condition.

In other words, we aim to establish strong well-posedness and regularity for a new class of singular stochastic Volterra equations under minimal assumptions. The results demonstrate a robust regularization-by-noise phenomenon in a non-Markovian framework. The Volterra structure fundamentally alters the analysis since classical semimartingale and Markov techniques are no longer applicable.

Finally, let us mention that Volterra equations are a canonical way to model non-Markovian dynamics with memory and that, singular kernels appear naturally in rough and long-memory modelling. See, e.g.,
[
1
,
3
]
and the references therein. Further, our focus is on a basic, robust regularization-by-noise phenomenon: additive Brownian noise restores well-posedness even when the drift is merely measurable (in the spatial variable). In this case, we choose the kernel class
\mathbb{H}
to allow a transparent analytic route to strong solutions and regularity with respect to the initial value; it is not meant as a complete coverage of all rough-volatility kernels, but it provides a rigorous first step for singular SVDEs under minimal drift regularity.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows: We prove that SVDE (
1
) has a unique strong solution in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we establish the weak convergence of approximating solutions. We then discuss the existence of a unique weak solution in Section
4
and provide an example to support the main result.

2
Strong uniqueness

We are coming to the main result of our paper:

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
b\in

\mathbb{H}
. Then there exists for a sufficiently small time horizon
T
a unique strong
solution
X_{t}^{x},0\leq t\leq T
to the
SVDE
(
1

. Moreover,
X_{t}^{x}
is Malliavin differentiable and the
associated stochastic flow

belongs to the space
L^{2}(\Omega;W_{loc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for all
0\leq t\leq T
.

We also need the following auxiliary result for the proof of the main result:

Lemma 2
.

Let
b\in\mathbb{H}
given by

b(t,s,x)=\sum_{\mu\geq 0}(t-s)^{\mu}\cdot b_{\mu}(s,x)

for
b_{\mu}\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

\mu\geq 0,
such that

\sup_{\mu\geq 0}||Db_{\mu}||_{\infty}<\infty,

where
D
is the spatial derivative of
b_{\mu}
. Let
X_{\cdot}^{x}
be the unique strong solution associated with the
SVDE
(
1
). Denote by
D_{u}
the Malliavin derivative with respect to the
Brownian motion

. Then for a sufficiently small

\sup_{0\leq u\leq T}\big|\big|D_{u}X_{t}^{x}\big|\big|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{d,T}(\sup_{\mu\geq 0}||b_{\mu}||_{\infty})

as well as there exists
\alpha=\alpha(s)>0

\big|\big|D_{r}X_{s}^{x}-D_{v}X_{s}^{x}\big|\big|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{d,T}(\sup_{\mu\geq 0}\left\|b_{\mu}\right\|_{\infty})\left|r-v\right|^{\alpha}

for
0\leq v\leq r\leq T
, where
C_{d,T}:\left[0,\infty\right)\longrightarrow

\left[0,\infty\right)
is an
increasing, continuous function,
\left\|\cdot\right\|
a matrix
norm and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\infty}
the supremum norm.

Proof.

We know by assumption that

b(t,s,x)=\sum_{\mu\geq 0}(t-s)^{\mu}\cdot b_{\mu}(s,x).

So we can use dominated convergence and obtain that

Db(t,s,x)=\sum_{\mu\geq 0}(t-s)^{\mu}\cdot Db_{\mu}(s,x)

pointwise. By applying the Malliavin derivative
D
to both sides of the SVDE

X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{x})ds+B_{t}\text{,}

we obtain the equation

D_{u}X_{t}^{x}=\int_{u}^{t}Db(t,s,X_{s}^{x})\cdot D_{u}X_{s}^{x}ds+I_{d}

(4)

fort
u\leq t
a.e (compare
[
17
]
). Here,
Db
denotes the spatial derivative of the vector
field
b:[0,T]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
I_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
the unit matrix. Using Picard
iteration, we then find

D_{u}X_{t}^{x}=I_{d}+\sum_{n\geq 2}\Big\{\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n-1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}Db(t,s_{n-1},X_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot Db(s_{2},s_{1},X_{s_{1}}^{x})ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}\Big\}

in
L^{p}
,
p\geq 1
. Thus, we derive that

\displaystyle D_{u}X_{t}^{x}

\displaystyle=I_{d}+\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{\mu_{2},...,\mu_{n}\geq 0}\Big\{\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n-1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}(t-s_{n-1})^{\mu_{n}}Db_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n-1},X_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot

\displaystyle(s_{2}-s_{1})^{\mu_{2}}Db_{\mu_{2}}(s_{1},X_{s_{1}}^{x})ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}\Big\}.

The latter implies that

\displaystyle\big|\big|D_{u}X_{t}^{x}\big|\big|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq d^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{\mu_{2},...,\mu_{n}\geq 0}\Big|\Big|\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n-1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}(t-s_{n-1})^{\mu_{n}}Db_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n-1},X_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot

\displaystyle(s_{2}-s_{1})^{\mu_{2}}Db_{\mu_{2}}(s_{1},X_{s_{1}}^{x})ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}\Big|\Big|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Further, it also follows from dominated convergence combined with the Cauchy
formula for repeated integration that

\displaystyle X_{t}^{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\int_{0}^{t}b(t,s,X_{s}^{x})ds+B_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\sum_{\mu\geq 0}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\mu}b_{\mu}(s,X_{s}^{x})ds+B_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\sum_{\mu\geq 0}\mu!\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}...\int_{0}^{s_{\mu}}b_{\mu}(s,X_{s}^{x})dsds_{\mu}...ds_{1}+B_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\int_{0}^{t}C_{s_{1}}(X_{\cdot}^{x})ds_{1}+B_{t}\text{,}

where

C_{s_{1}}(X_{\cdot}^{x}):=\sum_{\mu\geq 0}\mu!\int_{0}^{s_{1}}...\int_{0}^{s_{\mu}}b_{\mu}(s,X_{s}^{x})dsds_{\mu}...ds_{2}\text{.}

Then, using Girsanov’s theorem applied to the process
C_{t}(X_{\cdot}^{x}),0\leq t\leq T
, in combination with the proof of Lemma 3.5 in
[
16
]
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n-1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}(t-s_{n-1})^{\mu_{n}}Db_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n-1},X_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot(s_{2}-s_{1})^{\mu_{2}}Db_{\mu_{2}}(s_{1},X_{s_{1}}^{x})ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}\Big|\Big|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,b}\cdot\Big|\Big|\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n-1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}(t-s_{n-1})^{\mu_{n}}Db_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n-1},B_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot(s_{2}-s_{1})^{\mu_{2}}Db_{\mu_{2}}(s_{1},B_{s_{1}}^{x})ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}\Big|\Big|_{L^{8}(\Omega)},

for a constant
C_{T,b}
depending on

and
\sup_{\mu\geq 0}\left\|b_{\mu}\right\|_{\infty}
, where
B_{t}^{x}:=x+B_{t}
. On the other hand, we see from Cauchy‘s formula for repeated integration that

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n-1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}(t-s_{n-1})^{\mu_{n}}Db_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n-1},B_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot(s_{2}-s_{1})^{\mu_{2}}Db_{\mu_{2}}(s_{1},B_{s_{1}}^{x})ds_{1}...ds_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i})!\int_{u}^{t}\int_{u}^{s_{n,\mu_{n}}}...\int_{u}^{s_{n,1}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2}}\int_{u}^{s_{2,\mu_{2}}}...\int_{u}^{s_{2,1}}Db_{\mu_{n}}(s_{n-1},B_{s_{n-1}}^{x})\cdot...\cdot Db_{\mu_{2}}(s_{1},B_{s_{1}}^{x})

\displaystyle ds_{1}...ds_{2,1}...ds_{2,\mu_{2}}...ds_{n-1}ds_{n,1}...ds_{n,\mu_{n}}\text{.}

Finally, using integration by parts combined with Proposition

[
16
]
and
obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq u\leq t}\big|\big|D_{u}X_{t}^{x}\big|\big|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\Big(1+\sum_{n\geq 2}\sum_{\mu_{2},...,\mu_{n}\geq 0}\frac{\prod_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i})!d^{\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}+1)+2}2^{4\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}+1)}C^{\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}+1)}\prod_{i=2}^{n}\left\|b_{\mu_{i}}\right\|_{\infty}T^{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}+1)}}{\Gamma(4\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\mu_{i}+1)+1)^{1/8}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{d,T}(\sup_{\mu\geq 0}\left\|b_{\mu}\right\|_{\infty})\text{,}

where
C_{d,T}
is a function as stated in the Lemma. Using Stirling‘s formula, it follows from our assumptions on

,
\mu\geq 0
, that the above double sum converges, provided the time horizon

Remark 3
.

The power-series assumption is used for two reasons:

-

it yields a repeated-integration representation (Cauchy formula) that converts Volterra drifts into a form amenable to Malliavin estimates and compactness arguments; and

-

it provides uniform control of iterated integral terms via (
3
),
which is essential for summability in the derivative bounds.

Thus the assumption is tailored to guarantee convergence of the expansion on short time horizons and does not aim to cover all kernels used in applications.

Weak convergence of approximating solutions

The following technical lemma deals with weak convergence of approximating solutions to weak solutions and will be used in the proof of the main result (Theorem
1
) in Section
4
for establishing a ”transformation property” of strong solutions (see (

Lemma 4
.

Let
T<1
. Consider the processes
C_{\cdot}
and
C_{\cdot}^{(n)},n\geq 1
given by

C_{s_{1}}^{(n)}(B_{\cdot}^{x}):=\sum_{\mu\geq 0}\mu!\int_{0}^{s_{1}}...\int_{0}^{s_{\mu}}b_{\mu}^{(n)}(s,B_{s}^{x})dsds_{\mu}...ds_{2}

for vector fields
b_{\mu}^{(n)}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\left(0,T\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),n\geq 1
and

C_{s_{1}}(B_{\cdot}^{x}):=\sum_{\mu\geq 0}\mu!\int_{0}^{s_{1}}...\int_{0}^{s_{\mu}}b_{\mu}(s,B_{s}^{x})dsds_{\mu}...ds_{2},

where

b_{\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}),\mu\geq 0\text{.}

Assume that

b_{\mu}^{(n)}(t,y)\underset{n\longrightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}b_{\mu}(t,y)\text{ }(t,y)-a.e.

as well as

\sup_{n\geq 1,\mu\geq 0}\big|\big|b_{\mu}^{(n)}\big|\big|_{L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty.

Let
X_{.}^{x,n}
be the strong solution of the
SVDE
associated with the

(b_{\mu}^{(n)})_{\mu\geq 0,n\geq 1}
. Suppose that
X_{.}^{x}
is a weak
solution of the
SVDE
with respect to
(b_{\mu})_{\mu\geq 0}
on the same probability
space. Then for all bounded continuous functions

\varphi(X_{t}^{x,n})\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}E[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]

weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{F}_{t})
.

Proof.

Using dominated convergence, one shows for all
t
that

C_{t}^{(n)}(B_{\cdot}^{x})\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}C_{t}(B_{\cdot}^{x})\text{ }a.e.

Further, it follows from the Itô isometry and uniform integrability that

\int_{0}^{T}C^{(n)}(s,B_{\cdot}^{x})dB_{s}\underset{n\longrightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{0}^{T}C(s,B_{\cdot}^{x})dB_{s}

in
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\geq 1
. We note that

\prod_{t}:=\Big\{\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{i}(s)dB_{s}^{j}):,f_{j}\in L^{\infty}([0,t]),j=1,2,..,d\Big\}

is a total subset of
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{F}_{t})
. So it is sufficient
to show that

E\left[\varphi(X_{t}^{x,n})\mathcal{E}(f)\right]\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}E[E[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\mathcal{E}(f)]

for all
\mathcal{E}(f):=\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{i}(s)dB_{s}^{j})\in\prod_{t}
. Define the
Girsanov change of measures

dQ_{n}=\mathcal{E}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\langle C^{(n)}(s,X_{\cdot}^{x,n}),dB_{s}\rangle)d\mathbb{P},\quad n\geq 1.

Then the Girsanov’s theorem implies that

B_{.}^{\ast,n}:=B_{t}-\int_{0}^{.}C^{(n)}(s,X_{\cdot}^{x,n})ds

is a
Q_{n}
-Brownian motion and by using the notation
C^{(n)}(s,\cdot)=

(C^{(n,1)}(s,\cdot),...,C^{(n,d)}(s,\cdot))^{\text{T}}
(
T
transpose) we
get

\displaystyle E\Big[\varphi(X_{t}^{x,n})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=E_{Q_{n}}\Big[\varphi(x+B_{t}^{\ast,n})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{\ast,n,j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)C^{(n,j)}(s,x+B_{\cdot}^{\ast,n})ds\Big\}\times\mathcal{E}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\langle C^{(n)}(s,x+B_{\cdot}^{\ast,n}),dB_{s}^{\ast,n}\rangle)\Big]

\displaystyle=E\Big[\varphi(x+B_{t})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)C^{(n,j)}(s,x+B_{\cdot})ds\Big\}

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{E}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\langle C^{(n)}(s,x+B_{\cdot}),dB_{s}\rangle)\Big].

We observe that

\displaystyle E\Big[E\Big[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle=E\Big[E\Big[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}\Big\}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t}\Big]\Big]

\displaystyle=E\Big[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}\Big\}\Big].

Using the inequality
|e^{x}-e^{y}|\leq e^{x+y}|x-y|
for all

\displaystyle\Big|E\Big[\varphi(X_{t}^{x,n})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}\Big\}\Big]-E\Big[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}\Big\}\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle-E\Big[\varphi(x+B_{t})\exp\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)dB_{s}^{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{0}^{t}f_{j}(s)C^{(j)}(s,x+B_{\cdot})ds\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq K_{t}(f)J_{1}^{n}J_{2}^{n},

Then it follows from the martingale property of the Doleans-Dade exponential
and our assumptions on the coefficients that

. Further, using once again dominated convergence, we have that

, which yields the result.

that

\mathbb{P}((X_{t_{1}},...,X_{t_{n}})\in A)=E_{\mathbb{P}}\Big[1_{\{(B_{t_{1}}^{x},...,B_{t_{n}}^{x})\in A\}}\mathcal{E}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\langle C_{s}(B_{\cdot}^{x}),dB_{s}\rangle)_{T}\Big].

as well as

\mathbb{P}^{\ast}((Y_{t_{1}},...,Y_{t_{n}})\in A)=E_{\mathbb{P}}\Big[1_{\{(B_{t_{1}}^{x},...,B_{t_{n}}^{x})\in A\}}\mathcal{E}(\int_{0}^{\cdot}\langle C_{s}(B_{\cdot}^{x}),dB_{s}\rangle)_{T}\Big].

So
X_{.}
and
Y_{.}
coincide in law.

We are now coming to the proof of our main result.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
X_{\cdot}^{x,n}
be the unique strong solution to
the SVDE (
1
) associated with coefficients
b_{\mu}^{(n)},\mu\geq 0,n\geq 1
converging to
b_{\mu}\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
n\longrightarrow\infty
for all
\mu

under the assumptions of Lemma
4
, Lemma
2

and

\sup_{n\geq 1,\mu\geq 0}\big|\big|b_{\mu}^{(n)}\big|\big|_{\infty}<\infty\text{.}

Then it follows from Lemma
2
in connection with Corollary
13
in the Appendix (applied to
b_{\mu}^{(n)},\mu\geq 0,n\geq 1
) that
for all
0\leq t\leq T
there exists a subsequence
n_{k},k\geq 1

(depending on
t
) such that

X_{t}^{x,n_{k}}\underset{k\longrightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}Y_{t}^{x}

in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{F}_{t})
. On the other hand, by using the theorem
of Mitoma, we can see just as in
[
16
]
that

X_{\cdot}^{x,n}\underset{n\longrightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}Y_{\cdot}^{x}

in
C(\left[0,T\right];\left(S\right)^{\ast})
, where
\left(S\right)^{\ast}
is the Hida-distribution space with respect to the (
d-
dimensional) Brownian motion
B_{\cdot}
. The latter entails that

X_{t}^{x,n}\underset{n\longrightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}Y_{t}^{x}

in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{F}_{t})
for all
t
. Further, taking into
account Remark
5
, we find that
Y_{t}^{x}
coincides with
E[X_{t}^{x}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
a.e. for all
t
for the weak solution
X_{t}^{x}
to the SVDE (
1
). So for a bounded continuous function
\varphi
we obtain from Lemma
4
the following relation

\varphi(E[X_{t}^{x}|\mathcal{F}_{t}])=E[\varphi(X_{t}^{x})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\text{ }a.e.

(6)

for all
t
. Then approximation of
\varphi(x)=(x^{2},...,x^{2})
by bounded continuous
functions combined with dominated convergence shows the validity of the
latter relation in this case, which implies

E[X_{t}^{x}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=X_{t}^{x}\text{,}

that is adaptedness of
X_{\cdot}^{x}
. So
X_{\cdot}^{x}
is a strong
solution on the probability space on which the weak solution lives.
Uniqueness of strong solutions follows from the fact that the
S-
transform
of two solutions are the same (see
[
16
]
). So we get a unique strong
solution on the probability space on which the (a priori) weak solution
exists. The constructed strong solution can then be represented as a
progressively measurable functional of the Brownian motion on the latter
probability space. In order to obtain strong solutions on an arbitrary
probability space, one can replace that Brownian motion with the one on the
other probability space in the measurable functional.

Let us now show the (
\mathbb{P}-
a.e.) local Sobolev differentiability of
(x\longmapsto X_{t}^{x})
. To this end, consider once more the above
approximating sequence of solutions
X_{\cdot}^{x,n},n\geq 1
. Using
dominated convergence, one gets similarly to the case of the Malliavin
derivative of a solution the linear equation

\frac{d}{dx}X_{t}^{x,n}=\int_{0}^{t}Db^{(n)}(t,s,X_{s}^{x,n})\frac{d}{dx}X_{s}^{x,n}ds+I_{d}\text{.}

(7)

We can then apply the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
2
in connection with Picard iteration of the latter equation and
derive the analogous estimate

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big|\Big|\frac{d}{dx}X_{t}^{x,n}\Big|\Big|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}\leq C_{d,T}(\sup_{n\geq 1,\mu\geq 0}\big|\big|b_{\mu}^{(n)}\big|\big|_{\infty})<\infty

for a non-decreasing continuous function
C_{d,T}:\left[0,\infty\right)\longrightarrow

\left[0,\infty\right)
. Using the latter estimate, one
concludes (just as in
[
16
]
) that
(x\longmapsto X_{t}^{x})
is in
L^{2}(W^{1,2}(U))
for all bounded and open sets
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Example 7
.

Consider the
SVDE

X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}\sin(t-s)g(s,X_{s}^{x})ds+B_{t}\text{, }

(8)

0\leq t\leq T
, where
g\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The drift vector field belongs to the class
\mathbb{H}
. So according to Theorem
1
the
SVDE
(
8
) posesses unique strong solution which is smooth in the sense of Malliavin differentiability and in the sense of (
\mathbb{P}-
a.e.) local Sobolev differentiability. Let us also mention here that the kernel
\sin(t-s)
admits a finite-dimensional Markovian lift. More precisely, introducing suitable auxiliary processes, the dynamics can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}dX_{t}=Y_{t}\,dt+dW_{t},\\
dY_{t}=\bigl(b(t,X_{t})-Z_{t}\bigr)\,dt,\\
dZ_{t}=Y_{t}\,dt,\end{cases}

with initial conditions
(X_{0},Y_{0},Z_{0})=(x,0,0)
.

Example 8
.

Assume the SVDE

X_{t}=x+\int_{0}^{t}J_{0}(t-s)g(s,X_{s})ds+B_{t},0\leq t\leq T,

where
g\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
and

J_{0}
denotes the Bessel function of the first kind of order zero, defined
by

J_{0}(u)=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{m}}{(m!)^{2}}(\frac{u}{2})^{2m}.

The kernel
K=J_{0}
is entire and admits a power series expansion with
super-factorially decaying coefficients. Hence, the function
b
given by

b(t,s,x)=J_{0}(t-s)g(s,x)

belongs to the class
\mathbb{H}
, and all assumptions of Theorem
1
are satisfied. In contrast to the case
K(t)=\sin(t)
in the
previous example, this equation does not allow for a finite-dimensional
Markovian lift of the form

\begin{array}[]{c}dX_{t}=c^{T}Y_{t}dt+dB_{t},\\
dY_{t}=AY_{t}+bg(t,X_{t})dt,Y_{0}=0\end{array}

for
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
b,c\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Indeed, if
such a representation were possible, then by variation of constants we would
obtain

Y_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}bg(s,X_{s})ds\text{,}

and hence

dX_{t}=\left(\int_{0}^{t}c^{T}e^{(t-s)A}bg(s,X_{s})ds\right)dt+dB_{t}.

Using Fubini‘s theorem, this yields a Volterra equation with kernel

\widetilde{K}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}c^{T}e^{vA}bdv,u\geq 0\text{,}

and therefore its derivative is
\widetilde{K}^{\shortmid}(v)=c^{T}e^{vA}b
. If
\widetilde{K}(v)=J_{0}(v)
, then necessarily

\widetilde{K}^{\shortmid}(v)=J_{0}^{\shortmid}(v)=-J_{1}(v)\text{,}

where
J_{1}
denotes the Bessel function of the first kind of order one.
However, any function of the form
u\longmapsto c^{T}e^{vA}b
belongs to the
class of exponential-polynomial functions and solves a linear ODE with
constant coefficients. However,
J_{1}
does not belong to this class and
solves the Bessel equation

u^{2}f^{\shortmid\shortmid}+uf^{\shortmid}+(u^{2}-1)f=0

with variable coefficients. This shows that there is no finite-dimensional
Markovian lift of the form above for
K=

J_{0}
.

Remark 9
.

In the convolution-type case of
b
with

b(t,s,x)=K(t-s)g(s,x),0\leq s\leq t\leq T,

where
g\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the condition
b\in\mathbb{H}
reduces to a condition on the kernel

K
. In particular, if
K
admits a power series expansion

K(u)=\sum_{m\geq 0}a_{m}u^{m},0\leq u\leq T

with coefficients
a_{m},m\geq 0
satisfying the growth condition (
3
), then
b\in\mathbb{H}
. This shows that, in the convolution-type
setting, the class
\mathbb{H}
contains kernels with sufficiently
well-controlled power series expansions.

Remark 10
.

Using the approach of this paper one may also study the
SVDE
of the type

X_{t}^{x}=x+\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha}g(s,X_{s}^{x})ds+B_{t}\text{, }

0\leq t\leq T
, for
g\in L^{\infty}(\left[0,T\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<0
. The reason for this is that we can write

(t-s)^{\alpha}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\binom{\alpha}{n}(t-s-1)^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{\alpha}{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{n-k}(t-s)^{k}\text{, }0<t-s<2\text{,}

(9)

which allows the application of the Cauchy formula for repeated integration
in connection with similar proofs of Lemma
2
,
4
and
6
, when
T
is chosen to be sufficiently small. The latter, however, requires further analysis and modification of the proofs of Lemma
2
and Lemma
4
based on the representation (
9
), which is beyond the scope of the current paper.

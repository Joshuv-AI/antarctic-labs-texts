The semilinear heat inequality with Morrey initial data on Riemannian manifolds

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to obtain estimates for nonnegative solutions of the differential inequality ( ∂ ∂ t − Δ ) ​ u ≤ A ​ u p + B ​ u \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)u\leq Au^{p}+Bu with small initial data in borderline Morrey norms over a Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry. We obtain L ∞ L^{\infty} estimates assuming ‖ u ⁡ ( ⋅ , 0 ) ‖ M q , 2 ​ q p − 1 + sup 0 ≤ t < T ‖ u ⁡ ( ⋅ , t ) ‖ L s < δ , \|u(\cdot,0)\|_{M^{q,\frac{2q}{p-1}}}+\sup_{0\leq t<T}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{s}}<\delta, where 1 < q ≤ q c := n ⁡ ( p − 1 ) 2 1<q\leq q_{c}:=\frac{n(p-1)}{2} and 1 ≤ s ≤ q c 1\leq s\leq q_{c} . 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Semilinear heat equations have been studied extensively on domains in Euclidean space; see the monograph by Quittner and Souplet
[
15
, Chapter
II]
.

In 1980, Weissler
[
23
]
showed that for
p>1
and a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{\partial}}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)u=|u|^{p-1}u,\quad u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\quad u\big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

is well-posed for initial data
u_{0}\in L^{q}
, with
q
strictly bigger than the critical value

\displaystyle q_{c}:=\frac{n(p-1)}{2}.

For initial data in the scale-invariant/critical space
L^{q_{c}}(\Omega)
, with
q_{c}>1
, Weissler also showed that one can classically solve (
1.1
), although the interval of definition is no longer uniformly bounded away from zero on bounded subsets of
L^{q_{c}}(\Omega)
. In fact, it follows from work of Kaplan
[
13
]
and a rescaling argument
[
15
, Remark 15.4 (i) and Theorem 17.1]
that, for bounded domains, the
L^{q_{c}}
norm does not control the maximal time of existence.

However, in 1999, Souplet
[
17
]
showed that if
p>1+\frac{2}{n}

and the
L^{q_{c}}
-norm of the initial data is sufficiently small, then the solution exists for all time and converges to the zero function at a polynomial rate.

The problem (
1.1
) with small, borderline initial data has been further studied in the setting of Morrey spaces. For given
n
and
p
, there is a one-parameter family of scale-invariant/critical Morrey spaces
M^{q,\frac{2q}{p-1}}(\Omega)
, with
1\leq q\leq q_{c}
, all of which include
L^{q_{c}}(\Omega)
.
In 2015, Blatt and Struwe
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
showed that on bounded domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, with
n\geq 3
and
p>\frac{n+2}{n-2}
, (
1.1
) has a global-in-time classical solution if
u_{0}
is small in the
M^{2,\frac{4}{p-1}}
norm. Furthermore, it follows from their work that the solution is bounded for all positive times and decays polynomially at the same rate as in the
L^{q_{c}}
case. Additionally, Blatt and Struwe derived a relaxed local well-posedness result, similar to Weissler’s for
L^{q_{c}}(\Omega)
, in parabolic Morrey spaces for initial data in a proper subspace of
M^{2,\frac{4}{p-1}}(\Omega)
.

In 2016, Souplet
[
18
]
generalized the work of Blatt and Struwe to a wider range of Morrey exponents. In particular, Souplet showed using semigroup methods that initial data which are small in the
M^{q,\frac{2q}{p-1}}
norm, with
1<q\leq q_{c}
, yield a global classical solution to (
1.1
). Moreover, the
L^{\infty}
norm of the solution decays polynomially at the same rate as in the
L^{q_{c}}
case. In addition, Souplet’s approach yields that for
q
in the aforementioned range, the
M^{q,\frac{2q}{p-1}}
norm of the solution remains small up to a multiplicative constant.

Many geometric flows
involve one or more nonnegative energy density functions subject to differential inequalities of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}t}-\Delta\right)u\leq Au^{p}+Bu,

with nonnegative constants
A
and
B
. These flows often exist smoothly as long as the energy density remains bounded; hence,
it is desirable to have long-time
L^{\infty}
estimates for solutions of (
1.2
) on Riemannian manifolds under the weakest possible assumptions. The main goal of this paper is to adapt several of the aforementioned results for the equation (
1.1
) to solutions of the inequality (
1.2
) on a Riemannian manifold with bounded geometry.

1.2.
Statement of results

In the sequel,
M
is a connected, complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
with bounded geometry and
A,B
are nonnegative constants. We use
M^{q,\lambda}
to denote Morrey spaces of functions and
H_{t}(f)
to denote the integral of a function
f
against the heat kernel on
M
. See §
2
for definitions.

We first give the hypotheses on the solutions to (
1.2
) that we herein consider.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
p,q,r
, and
T
be constants such that

\displaystyle p>1+\frac{2}{n},\quad 1<q\leq q_{c}:=\frac{n(p-1)}{2},\quad 1\leq\frac{r}{p}<q<r,\quad T>0.

Set

\displaystyle\lambda:=\frac{2q}{p-1}.

We say
u
is a solution to (
1.2
) on
[0,T)
if:

(1)

u\in C_{\text{loc}}^{0}([0,T),M^{q,\lambda})\cap C_{\text{loc}}^{0}((0,T),L^{\infty})
.

(2)

u(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left(\|u(t)-u(0)\|_{M^{q,\lambda}}+t^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}+t^{\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\|u(t)\|_{M^{r,\lambda}}\right)=0.

(3)

For each
t\in[0,T)
, we have
u(t)\geq 0
a.e., and for
0\leq t^{\prime}<t<T
we have a.e.

(1.3)

\displaystyle u(t)\leq H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{t-s}(Au^{p}(s)+Bu(s))\,ds.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
, depending on
n,p,q,r,A,B
, and the geometry of
M
, such that the following holds.

Let
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}]
and
s\in[1,q_{c}]
. Suppose
u
is a solution of (
1.2
) on
[0,T)
, satisfying

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|u(0)\|_{M^{q,\lambda}}+\sup_{(x,t)\in M\times[0,T)}\|u(t)\|_{L^{s}(B_{1}(x))}<\delta.

Then,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:criticalestimate})}\delta\left(t^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}+1\right),\ \ \ 0<t<T.

Here,
C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:criticalestimate})}
is defined by (
3.15
) and depends on
n,B
, and the geometry of
M
.

Let
K>0,
and let
q^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
and
\lambda^{\prime}\in[0,n]
satisfy

\alpha:=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{2q^{\prime}}<\frac{1}{p-1}.

Suppose that in addition to (
1.4
) we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle u\in C_{\text{loc}}^{0}([0,T),M^{q^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}),\quad\|u(0)\|_{M^{q^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}}}\leq K,\quad\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\alpha}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}=0.

Then,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:improvedestimate})}t^{-\alpha}\wedge C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:criticalestimate})}\delta t^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}+C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:criticalestimate})}\delta,\ \ \ 0<t<T.

Here,
C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:improvedestimate})}
is defined by (
3.18
) and depends on
K,n,p,\alpha,A,B,
and the geometry of
M
.

1.3.
Applications

We first give an application of the preceding result to harmonic map flow. Recall that the energy density
|dw|
of a smooth solution
w(t):M\to N
of harmonic map flow between closed Riemannian manifolds
M,N
satisfies (
1.2
) with
p=3
. By work of Wang
[
21
,
22
]
, we have that given a Sobolev map
w_{0}\in W^{1,n}(M,N)
, where
n=\text{dim}(M)
, there exists a short-time regular solution of harmonic map flow. We show that the energy density of such a solution satisfies the conditions of Definition
1.1
, allowing us to prove the following version of Struwe’s classical small-data regularity result for harmonic map flow
[
19
, Theorem 7.1]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
and
N
be closed Riemannian manifolds, and let
w_{0}\in W^{1,n}(M,N)
, where
n=\dim(M)
. There exists
\delta_{1}>0
, depending on the geometries of
M
and
N
, such that if
\delta\in(0,\delta_{1}]
and
\|dw_{0}\|_{M^{2,2}}<\delta
, then Wang’s solution of harmonic map flow with
w(0)=w_{0}
exists for all time and converges exponentially in
C^{k}
for all
k
to a constant map as
t\to\infty
.

In a companion paper
[
10
]
, we develop analogous results for Yang-Mills flow in higher dimensions: In particular,
[
10
, Theorem 1.4]
is directly analogous to Theorem
1.3
. We note that Theorem
1.3
in this paper and
[
10
, Theorem 1.4]
also follow from a monotonicity formula and epsilon-regularity result for the energy densities of the respective flows. However, in the context of Yang-Mills flow on a special-holonomy manifold, such a monotonicity formula does not hold for the individual components of the curvature and the estimates of the present paper are essential; see
[
10
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Last, we use Theorem
1.3
to give a version of a theorem of White
[
24
, Cor. 2 and 3 on pg. 128]
concerning the infimum of the functional
\int_{M}|dw|^{n}
on homotopy classes of maps between closed manifolds.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
M
and
N
be closed Riemannian manifolds and
\gamma\subset C(M,N)
a homotopy class of maps between
M
and
N.

Then,
\gamma
is trivial if and only if

(1.8)

\displaystyle\inf\{\|dw\|_{M^{2,2}(M)}\mid w\in\gamma\cap W^{1,n}(M,N)\}=0.

For the gauge-theoretic analogue of Corollary
1.4
, see
[
10
, Corollary 1.5]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with Riemannian metric
g
. We denote the covariant derivative induced by the Levi-Civita connection of
g
by
\nabla
. Our convention for the Laplace-Beltrami operator is given by the following action on functions:

\displaystyle\Delta f=g^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\partial_{i}\left(\sqrt{\det g}g^{ij}\partial_{j}f\right).

We let
\mu_{g}
denote the Riemannian measure induced by
g
.

We next define Morrey spaces of functions.
Suppose
M
has bounded geometry, i.e., the sectional curvatures are uniformly bounded and the injectivity radius is uniformly bounded away from zero.
It follows from the volume comparison theorems of Günther and Bishop-Gromov that there exists
V\geq 1
such that
for all
R\in(0,1)
and
x\in M
we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle V^{-1}R^{n}\leq\mu_{g}(B_{R}(x))\leq VR^{n}.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
q\in[1,\infty]
and
\lambda\in[0,n]
, we define the Morrey norm of a locally integrable function
f
to be

\displaystyle\|f\|_{q,\lambda}:=\begin{cases}\sup_{x\in M,0<R\leq 1}\left(R^{\lambda-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|f|^{q}\,\mu_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}&q<\infty\\
\|f\|_{\infty}&q=\infty.\end{cases}

We define the Morrey space
M^{q,\lambda}
to be
L^{\infty}
if
q=\infty
, and otherwise

\displaystyle M^{q,\lambda}=\left\{f\in L_{\text{loc}}^{1}\mid\|f\|_{q,\lambda}<\infty\right\}.

It follows from Hölder’s inequality that if
q_{1}\leq q_{2}
and
\lambda_{2}\leq\frac{q_{2}}{q_{1}}\lambda_{1}
, then

(2.2)

\displaystyle M^{q_{2},\lambda_{2}}\subset M^{q_{1},\lambda_{1}},

and the inclusion has operator norm bounded by a constant depending only on
V
.

Finally, we mention the heat kernel on manifolds. For a manifold with bounded geometry, there is a positive function

\displaystyle H\in C^{\infty}(M\times M\times(0,\infty))

which is the unique minimal fundamental solution of the heat equation on
M

[
6
, Theorem 4 in Chapter VIII.2]
. We call
H
the heat kernel on
M
, and we denote

\displaystyle H_{t}(f)(x):=\int_{M}H(x,y,t)f(y)\,\mu_{g}(y).

3.
Initial data in Morrey spaces

We now adapt
[
15
, Prop. 49.17]
, which gives estimates in Morrey spaces for solutions to the heat equation for Euclidean domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions, to the Riemannian setting.
The following lemma lists properties of the heat kernel on manifolds with bounded geometry that we will use.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
M
has bounded geometry. Then, there exist constants
C_{u},C_{l}>0
, depending only on the geometry of
M
, such that, letting
d(x,y)
denote the Riemannian distance between
x,y\in M
, the heat kernel on
M
satisfies

(3.1)

\displaystyle H(x,y,t)\leq C_{u}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{C_{u}t}\right),\ \ \ 0<t\leq 1,

and

(3.2)

\displaystyle t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{2t}\right)\leq C_{l}H(x,y,t),\ \ \ 0<t\leq 18C_{u}.

Furthermore,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}H(x,y,t)\,\mu_{g}(y)=1,\ \ \ \forall\ x\in M,\ t>0.

Proof.

The bound (
3.1
) follows from
[
9
]
, and the bound (
3.2
) follows from
[
7
]
; see
[
20
, §A.4]
for an exposition. The property (
3.3
) follows from
[
25
]
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2},
and
\lambda
be constants such that
1\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq\infty
and
0\leq\lambda\leq n
. Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with bounded geometry. Then, there exists a constant
C_{(\ref{Morreyheatestimates:estimate})}\geq 1
, depending only on the geometry of
M
, such that for all
f\in M^{s_{1},\lambda}
,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|H_{t}(f)\|_{s_{2},\lambda}\leq C_{(\ref{Morreyheatestimates:estimate})}t^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(\frac{1}{s_{1}}-\frac{1}{s_{2}})}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda},\ \ 0<t\leq 1.

Proof.

Since
M
has bounded geometry, for estimating the Morrey norm, we may restrict our attention to balls of radius
R\in(0,R_{0}]
for some fixed
R_{0}
depending on
M
.
We shall assume
R_{0}
is sufficiently small that the following hold for some dimensional constant
C_{n}\geq 2
.

(1)

Let
\kappa>0
be such that the absolute value of any sectional curvature of
M
is bounded above by
\kappa
. We then take
R_{0}\leq\rho_{\infty}:=4^{-1}\min\left\{1,\text{inj}(M),\kappa^{-1}\pi\right\}>0
, as in
[
20
, (2.2)]
.

(2)

The metric in geodesic coordinates on any ball of radius
6R_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}g_{\text{Euc}}\leq g\leq 2g_{\text{Euc}}.

(3)

For any
x,y
with
d(x,y)\leq 5R_{0}
, the Jacobian determinant of the transition map between geodesic coordinates on the balls of radius
6R_{0}
centered at
x,y
is bounded above by
C_{n}
.

(4)

For each
R\in(0,R_{0}]
, there exists a cover of
M
by balls
B_{R}(p_{i})
such that for each
j
, no more than
C_{n}
of the balls in the cover intersect
B_{R}(p_{j})
. We also assume that any ball of radius
2R
can be covered by
C_{n}
balls of radius
R
(not necessarily in the cover).

We break the proof of (
3.4
) into the following four cases:

(i)

s_{1}=s_{2}=\infty
;

(ii)

s_{1}=s_{2}\in[1,\infty)
;

(iii)

s_{1}\in[1,\infty),s_{2}=\infty
;

(iv)

1\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<\infty.

Case i:
s_{1}=s_{2}=\infty
.

This follows, with
C_{(\ref{Morreyheatestimates:estimate})}=1
, from (
3.3
) since
M^{\infty,\lambda}=L^{\infty}
.

Case ii:
s_{1}=s_{2}\in[1,\infty)
.

Let
x_{0}\in M
and
R\in(0,R_{0}]
. We first show that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{M}H(x,y,t)|f(y)|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{g}(y)\,\mu_{g}(x)\leq CR^{n-\lambda}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{s_{1}}

for some
C>0
depending on the geometry of
M
. By the heat kernel upper bound (
3.1
), the LHS of (
3.5
) is

\displaystyle\leq C_{u}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}\bigg(\underbrace{\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{4R}(x_{0})}}_{\text{I}}+\underbrace{\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{M\setminus B_{4R}(x_{0})}}_{\text{II}}\bigg)\,e^{-\frac{d(x,y)^{2}}{C_{u}t}}|f(y)|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{g}(y)\,\mu_{g}(x).

We now estimate the integrals
I
and
II
, starting with
I
. Choose geodesic coordinates

\displaystyle N_{x_{0}}:B_{6R}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to B_{6R}(x_{0})\subset M.

For
w\in B_{R}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n},
denote the set
w-B_{4R}(0):=\{w-z\mid z\in B_{4R}(0)\}.
By the Euclidean bounds per item (
2
),

I

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\int_{B_{4R}(0)}e^{-\frac{|w-z|^{2}}{2C_{u}t}}|f(N_{x_{0}}(z))|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(z)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(w)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\int_{w-B_{4R}(0)}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2C_{u}t}}|f(N_{x_{0}}(w-z))|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(z)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(w)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\int_{B_{5R}(0)}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2C_{u}t}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|f(N_{x_{0}}(z+w))|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(w)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(z).

For
z\in B_{5R}(0)
, let
N_{N_{x_{0}}(z)}
be some choice of geodesic coordinates on the ball of radius
6R
centered at
N_{x_{0}}(z)
. Set

\displaystyle F(w):=N_{N_{x_{0}}(z)}^{-1}(N_{x_{0}}(z+w)).

In view of item (
2
),

\displaystyle N_{x_{0}}(z+B_{R}(0))\subset N_{N_{x_{0}}(z)}(B_{2R}(0)).

Hence by the bounds on the Jacobian of the transition between geodesic coordinates from item (
3
),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|f(N_{x_{0}}(z+w))|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(w)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}(0)}|f(N_{N_{x_{0}}(z)}(F(w)))|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(w)

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\int_{B_{2R}(0)}|f(N_{N_{x_{0}}(z)}(w))|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(w)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{n}{2}}C_{n}\int_{B_{2R}(N_{x_{0}}(z))}|f(x)|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{g}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{n}{2}}C_{n}^{2}R^{n-\lambda}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{s_{1}}.

Since, for a dimensional constant
C_{1}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{5R}(0)}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2C_{u}t}}\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(z)\leq C_{1}(C_{u}t)^{\frac{n}{2}},

we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\text{I}\leq R^{n-\lambda}(C_{u}t)^{\frac{n}{2}}2^{\frac{3n}{2}}C_{1}C_{n}^{2}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{s_{1}}.

We now estimate integral
II
. Take a cover of
M
as in item (
4
). If
p_{i}
is such that
d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R
, then the triangle inequality yields for
x\in B_{R}(x_{0})
and
y\in B_{R}(p_{i})
that

\displaystyle d(x_{0},p_{i})\leq 3d(x,y).

Moreover,
\{B_{R}(p_{i})\mid d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R\}
covers
M\setminus B_{4R}(x_{0})
.
Therefore,

II

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\{i\mid d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R\}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(p_{i})}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},p_{i})^{2}}{9C_{u}t}}|f(y)|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{g}(y)\,\mu_{g}(x).

Since
R\leq R_{0}
, we have by the volume bounds (
2.1
) that the RHS in the last line is

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\{i\mid d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R\}}VR^{n}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},p_{i})^{2}}{9C_{u}t}}\int_{B_{R}(p_{i})}|f(y)|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\leq R^{n-\lambda}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{s_{1}}\sum_{\{i\mid d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R\}}VR^{n}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},p_{i})^{2}}{9C_{u}t}}.

Now, since
d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R
, we have by the triangle inequality that for
z\in B_{R}(p_{i})

\displaystyle d(x_{0},z)\leq 2d(x_{0},p_{i}).

Then, in conjunction with the volume bounds (
2.1
) again, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\{i\mid d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R\}}R^{n}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},p_{i})^{2}}{9C_{u}t}}

\displaystyle\leq V\sum_{\{i\mid d(x_{0},p_{i})\geq 3R\}}\int_{B_{R}(p_{i})}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},z)^{2}}{36C_{u}t}}\,\mu_{g}(z).

By the intersection property of the cover as stated in item (
4
), the RHS of this last line is

\displaystyle\leq VC_{n}\int_{M}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},z)^{2}}{36C_{u}t}}\,\mu_{g}(z).

Finally, by the heat kernel lower bound (
3.2
) and then the stochastic completeness property (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}e^{-\frac{d(x_{0},z)^{2}}{36C_{u}t}}\,d\mu_{g}(z)\leq C_{l}(18C_{u}t)^{\frac{n}{2}}\int H(x_{0},z,18C_{u}t)\,\mu_{g}(z)=C_{l}(18C_{u}t)^{\frac{n}{2}},

so we deduce that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\text{II}\leq R^{n-\lambda}C_{n}V^{2}(18C_{u}t)^{\frac{n}{2}}C_{l}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{s_{1}}.

Combining the bound (
3.6
) for
I
and the bound (
3.7
) for
II
, we obtain (
3.5
).

We next obtain the bound (
3.4
) in the proposition statement from (
3.5
). First note that the RHS of (
3.5
) is finite for
s_{1}\in[1,\infty)
such that
f\in M^{s_{1},\lambda}
. By (
2.2
),
M^{s_{1},\lambda}\subset M^{1,\lambda}
, so (
3.5
) and (
3.3
) imply that for a.e.
x\in B_{R}(x_{0})
,
f(y)
is integrable with respect to the probability measure
H(x,y,t)\mu_{g}(y)
. Thus, by Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|H_{t}(f)|^{s_{1}}\leq\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{M}H(x,y,t)|f(y)|^{s_{1}}\,\mu_{g}(y)\leq CR^{n-\lambda}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{s_{1}}.

Since
x_{0}
and
R\leq R_{0}
were arbitrary, we obtain (
3.4
). This concludes Case ii.

Case iii:
s_{1}\in[1,\infty),s_{2}=\infty
.

This case can be deduced from
[
20
, Lemma 4.2]
as follows. First suppose
0<t\leq R_{0}^{2}
, and recall our assumption from item (
1
). In the setup of
[
20
, Lemma 4.2]
, we take the measure
\mu(y)
to be
|f(y)|\,\mu_{g}(y)
. Then for some constant
C>0
, depending only on
M
,

\displaystyle|H_{t}(f)(x)|\leq\int_{M}H(x,y,t)\,\mu(y)\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}\sup_{z\in M}\int_{B_{\sqrt{t}}(z)}|f(y)|\,\mu_{g}(y).

By Hölder’s inequality and (
2.1
), this last expression is

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-\lambda}{2s_{1}}}\sup_{z\in M}\left(t^{\frac{1}{2}(\lambda-n)}\int_{B_{\sqrt{t}}(z)}|f|^{s_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{s_{1}}}\left(Vt^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)^{\frac{s_{1}-1}{s_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq CVt^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s_{1}}}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}.

Thus, Case iii holds for
0<t\leq R_{0}^{2}
.

For
R_{0}^{2}<t\leq 1
, we do the following. By the semigroup property
[
11
, (7.51)]
of the heat kernel and Fubini-Tonelli,

\displaystyle\|H_{t}(f)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left\|H_{R_{0}^{2}}(H_{t-R_{0}^{2}}(f))\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq CV(R_{0}^{2})^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s_{1}}}\left\|H_{t-R_{0}^{2}}(f)\right\|_{s_{1},\lambda}.

Since
t\leq 1
, we may increase
C
so that by Case ii this last expression is

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s_{1}}}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}.

Therefore, the proposition holds for Case iii.

Case iv:
1\leq s_{1}<s_{2}<\infty
.
This case follows by interpolating Cases ii and iii as follows:

\displaystyle\|H_{t}(f)\|_{s_{2},\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{t}(f)\|_{\infty}^{1-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\|H_{t}(f)\|_{s_{1},\lambda}^{\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{(\ref{Morreyheatestimates:estimate})}t^{-\frac{\lambda}{2s_{1}}}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}\right)^{1-\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}\left(C_{(\ref{Morreyheatestimates:estimate})}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda}\right)^{\frac{s_{1}}{s_{2}}}

\displaystyle=C_{(\ref{Morreyheatestimates:estimate})}t^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\frac{1}{s_{1}}-\frac{1}{s_{2}}\right)}\|f\|_{s_{1},\lambda},

as desired.
∎

Now that we have the preceding Morrey space estimates, we can straightforwardly adapt Souplet’s result
[

, Prop. 6.1]
, which is the Euclidean analog of Theorem
1.2
, to the Riemannian setting.

1.2

\displaystyle v(t)\leq H_{t-t^{\prime}}(v(t^{\prime}))+C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:integratingfactorconstant})}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{t-s}(v^{p}(s))\,ds,

where

(3.9)

\displaystyle C_{(\ref{Morreysupbound:integratingfactorconstant})}:=Ae^{pB}.

To do this under our regularity assumptions on

For

\displaystyle I_{\ell}(f):=\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{t^{\prime}}^{s_{\ell}}f(s_{\ell+1})\,ds_{\ell+1}\,\dots\,ds_{1}.

\displaystyle H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{t-s}(Au^{p}(s)+Bu(s))\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\frac{((t-t^{\prime})B)^{i}}{i!}\right)\left(H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{t-s}(Au^{p}(s))\,ds\right)+B^{\ell+1}I_{\ell}(H_{t-\cdot}(u(\cdot))).

The base case

\displaystyle I_{m}(H_{t-\cdot}(u(\cdot)))\leq I_{m}\left((H_{t-\cdot}\left((H_{\cdot-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{\cdot}H_{\cdot-s}(Au^{p}(s)+Bu(s))\,ds\right)\right).

By the semigroup property
[

of the heat kernel,

\displaystyle I_{m}(H_{t-\cdot}(H_{\cdot-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))))=H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))I_{m}(1)=\frac{(t-t^{\prime})^{m+1}}{(m+1)!}H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime})),

and similarly

\displaystyle I_{m}\left(H_{t-\cdot}\left(\int_{t^{\prime}}^{\cdot}H_{\cdot-s}(Au^{p}(s)+Bu(s))\,ds\right)\right)

Thus, (

as

\displaystyle u(t)\leq e^{(t-t^{\prime})B}\left(H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{t-s}(Au^{p}(s))\,ds\right).

Multiplying by

3.8

4.
Application to harmonic map flow

We now use the preceding Morrey estimates to prove Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
. First we will establish that for a suitable class of solutions of harmonic map flow, the energy density is a solution of (
1.2
) (in the sense of Def.
1.1
).

Let
M
and
N
be closed Riemannian manifolds, with
M
having dimension
n
. By Nash’s embedding theorem, we fix an isometric embedding of
N
into some Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. Sobolev maps between
M
and
N
are then defined to be Sobolev
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
-valued functions on
M
taking value a.e. in
N\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. Letting
\Pi
denote the second fundamental form of this embedding, the harmonic map flow equation is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)w=\Pi(w)(\nabla w,\nabla w),

where
w(t)
is a family of
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
-valued functions on
M
whose image is in
N
a.e., and
\nabla
and
\Delta
, respectively, denote the gradient and Laplacian of
M
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
-valued functions. By work of Wang
[
22
]
, we know that given an initial map
w_{0}\in W^{1,n}(M,N)
, there exists a solution of harmonic map flow in the following sense. By abuse of notation, we let
\Pi
denote the smooth, compactly supported extension of the second fundamental form as in
[
22
, pg. 11]
. For
T\in(0,\infty)
, consider the Banach space
X_{T}
of functions
f:M\times[0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
defined by the norm

\displaystyle|||f|||_{X_{T}}:=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|f\|_{\infty}+\sup_{0<t\leq T}\sqrt{t}\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}+\sup_{x\in M,0<R\leq\sqrt{T}}\left(R^{-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)\times[0,R^{2}]}|\nabla f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Wang showed that for some
T>0
, depending on
w_{0}
and which we may take to be less than
R_{0}^{2}
, the operator

\displaystyle\Phi(f)(t):=H_{t}(w_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}H_{t-s}(\Pi(f(s))(\nabla f(s),\nabla f(s)))\,ds

has a unique fixed point
w
in some small ball about
H_{t}(w_{0})
in
X_{T}
. Additionally, for
(x,t)\in M\times(0,T]
,
w
is smooth and takes value in
N
.

We now explain the following regularity properties of
w
near the initial time in Lemma
4.2
. We follow the strategy of Brezis and Cazenave
[
5
, Proof of Theorem 1, pg. 284-286]
.
First recall the following gradient estimates for heat kernel on
M
, where
f_{0}
is an
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
-valued function.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<t\leq 1
and
1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty
. Then, there exists
C\geq 1
, depending only on the geometry of
M
, such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})\|_{r}\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\cdot\begin{cases}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{q}&f_{0}\in L^{q}\\
\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{q}&f_{0}\in W^{1,q}.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is well known; see the appendix.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

We have
w\in C^{0}([0,T],W^{1,n})\cap C_{\text{loc}}^{0}((0,T],W^{1,\infty})
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left(\|w(t)-w_{0}\|_{W^{1,n}}+t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\infty}\right)=0.

In particular,
|\nabla w|
is a solution of (
1.2
) with
p=3,q=2
, and
r=3
as in Def.
1.1
.

Proof.

Since
w(t)
is smooth for
t>0
and since
M
is closed, we have

\displaystyle w\in C_{\text{loc}}^{0}((0,T],W^{1,\infty})\subset C_{\text{loc}}^{0}((0,T],W^{1,n}).

Thus, we just show that the limit in the statement of this lemma is zero. We establish this in the following order:

(1)

\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}=0
;

(2)

\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\infty}=0
;

(3)

\lim_{t\searrow 0}\|w(t)-w_{0}\|_{W^{1,n}}=0
.

In the sequel,
C
denotes a positive constant depending on the geometries of
M
and
N
which may increase from line to line.

Item 1:
\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}=0
.

Denote

\displaystyle w_{1}(t):=H_{t}(w_{0}).

We first claim that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left(t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}\right)=0.

To see this, first note that since
w_{0}\in W^{1,n}
and since
M
is closed, there exists a sequence
\tilde{w}_{i}\in C^{\infty}(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{k})
converging to
w_{0}
in
W^{1,n}(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{k})
. Then by Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla w_{1}(t)-\nabla H_{t}(\tilde{w}_{i})\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}+\|\nabla H_{t}(\tilde{w}_{i})\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{0}-\nabla\tilde{w}_{i}\|_{n}+C\|\nabla H_{t}(\tilde{w}_{i})\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{0}-\nabla\tilde{w}_{i}\|_{n}+C\|\nabla\tilde{w}_{i}\|_{\infty}.

We multiply by
t^{\frac{1}{6}}
. Choosing
i
and then
t
appropriately, we deduce that
\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}=0
. An analogous estimation implies that
\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}=0
. Thus, the claim (
4.1
) holds.

We now use (
4.1
) to prove Item 1. By Wang’s construction,
w=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi(w_{n})
in
X_{T}
, where
w_{1}
is as before and
w_{n}=\Phi(w_{n-1})
.
Set

(4.2)

\displaystyle C_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}:=\int_{0}^{1}(1-\sigma)^{-\frac{5}{6}}\sigma^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,d\sigma<\infty.

Recall that we have assumed
T\leq 1
. Then for all
i
and each
\tau\in(0,T]
, Lemma
4.1
yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{i+1}(\tau)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}-\|\nabla w_{1}(\tau)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\nabla H_{\tau-s}(\Pi(w_{i}(s))(\nabla w_{i}(s),\nabla w_{i}(s)))\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-s)^{-\frac{5}{6}}\|\Pi(w_{i}(s))(\nabla w_{i}(s),\nabla w_{i}(s))\|_{\frac{3n}{4}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-s)^{-\frac{5}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{0<s\leq\tau}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-s)^{-\frac{5}{6}}s^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{0<s\leq\tau}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}C_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\tau^{-\frac{1}{6}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i+1}(\tau)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\leq\sup_{0<s\leq\tau}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{1}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}+CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\left(\sup_{0<s\leq\tau}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}.

Fix
t\in(0,T]
. The preceding inequality holds for all
\tau\in(0,t]
, so we replace
\sup_{0<s\leq\tau}
in the RHS by
\sup_{0<s\leq t}
and then supremize the LHS over
\tau\in(0,t]
to obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i+1}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\leq\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{1}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}+CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}.

We now use (
4.3
) and induction to show that
\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}
can be made small for all
i
if
t
is chosen small enough.

Let

\displaystyle\varepsilon\in\left(0,(4CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})})^{-1}\right).

(
4.1
) implies there exists
t>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{1}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\leq\varepsilon.

Denote

\displaystyle\mu_{i}:=\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w_{i}(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}.

By choice of
t
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\leq\varepsilon.

Suppose that we have

\displaystyle\mu_{i}\leq 2\varepsilon.

Then by (
4.3
)

\displaystyle\mu_{i+1}\leq\varepsilon+CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\mu_{i}^{2}\leq\varepsilon+4CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\varepsilon^{2}\leq 2\varepsilon.

Thus by induction,
\mu_{i}\leq 2\varepsilon
for all
i
. From the definition of the
X_{T}
norm and the convergence
w_{i}\to w
in
X_{T}
, we have that
\nabla w_{i}\to\nabla w
in
C_{\text{loc}}^{0}((0,T),L^{\infty})
. Hence,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\leq 2\varepsilon,

so Item 1 follows.

Item 2:
\lim_{t\searrow 0}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\infty}=0
.
Since
w
solves the fixed-point equation, we have by Lemma
4.1

\displaystyle\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\infty}-\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\Phi(w)(t)\|_{\infty}-\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{5}{6}}\|\Pi(w(s))(\nabla w(s),\nabla w(s))\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{5}{6}}s^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\infty}\right)\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)C_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

We multiply by
t^{\frac{1}{2}}
. By definition of the
X_{T}
norm, we know that
\sup_{0<s\leq T}s^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\infty}<\infty
. Then, since by Item 1 and (
4.1
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left(t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(t)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla w_{1}(t)\|_{\infty}\right)=0,

Item 2 follows.

Item 3:
\lim_{t\searrow 0}\|w(t)-w_{0}\|_{W^{1,n}}=0
.
Since
w_{0}\in W^{1,n}
,
w_{1}(t)\to w_{0}
in
W^{1,n}(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{k})
as
t\searrow 0
. Next, we estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}H_{t-s}(\Pi(w)(\nabla w(s),\nabla w(s)))\,ds\right\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|\Pi(w)(\nabla w(s),\nabla w(s))\|_{\frac{3n}{4}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{3}}s^{-\frac{1}{3}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla H_{t-s}(\Pi(w)(\nabla w(s),\nabla w(s)))\,ds\right\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}C(t-s)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\|\Pi(w)(\nabla w(s),\nabla w(s))\|_{\frac{3n}{4}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq CC_{(\ref{checkinghmfisMorreysolution:duhamelconstant})}\left(\sup_{0<s\leq t}s^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\nabla w(s)\|_{\frac{3n}{2}}\right)^{2}.

Since
w
solves the fixed-point equation, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|w(t)-w_{0}\|_{W^{1,n}}

\displaystyle=\|\Phi(w)(t)-w_{0}\|_{W^{1,n}}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{1}(t)-w_{0}\|_{W^{1,n}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}H_{t-s}(\Pi(w(s))(\nabla w(s),\nabla w(s)))\,ds\right\|_{W^{1,n}}

\displaystyle\to 0\ \text{ as }\ t\searrow 0,

as desired.

We have thus shown that the limit in the statement of the lemma is zero. It remains to deduce that

\displaystyle u:=|\nabla w|

is a solution of (
1.2
), which we now explain. It follows from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle u\in C^{0}([0,T],M^{2,2})\cap C_{\text{loc}}^{0}((0,T],L^{\infty}),

and furthermore

\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}\left(t^{\frac{1}{6}}\|u\|_{M^{3,2}}+t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{\infty}\right)=0.

Since
w
is smooth on
M\times(0,T]
, the Böchner identity for harmonic map flow and the comparison principle yields for all
0<t^{\prime}<t\leq T

(4.4)

\displaystyle u(t)\leq H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}H_{t-s}(Au^{3}(s)+Bu(s))\,ds.

Note that for fixed
t>0
, we have that as
t^{\prime}\searrow 0

\displaystyle\|H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime}))-H_{t}(u(0))\|_{n}

\displaystyle=\|H_{t-t^{\prime}}(u(t^{\prime})-H_{t^{\prime}}(u(0)))\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t^{\prime})-H_{t^{\prime}}(u(0))\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|u(t^{\prime})-u(0)\|_{n}+\|u(0)-H_{t^{\prime}}(u(0))\|_{n})

\displaystyle\to 0

and

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\hskip-3.99994ptH_{t-s}(Au^{3}(s)+Bu(s))\,ds\right\|_{n}

\displaystyle\leq CA\hskip-3.99994pt\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\hskip-3.99994pt(t-s)^{-1}\|u^{3}(s)\|_{\frac{n}{3}}\,ds+CB\hskip-3.99994pt\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\hskip-3.99994pt\|u(s)\|_{n}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq CA(\|u(0)\|_{n}+1)^{3}\ln\left(\frac{t}{t-t^{\prime}}\right)+CB(\|u(0)\|_{n}+1)t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\to 0.

Therefore, (
4.4
) also holds with
t^{\prime}=0
. Hence, we conclude that
|\nabla w|
is a solution of (
1.2
).
∎

We will also make use of the following standard lemma, which can be compared with
[
4
, Thm. 1.4]
, as well as the earlier work
[
8

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
w:M\times[0,T)\to N
is a classical solution of harmonic map flow between closed Riemannian manifolds
(M,g),(N,h)
. If the image of
w(0)
is contained in a regular ball
B\subset N
(see
[
12
]
for a definition), then
w
extends to a global classical solution and converges exponentially in
C^{k}
for all
k
to a constant map as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.

By compactness and continuity,
w
is contained in
B
for short time. Let
|w|^{2}
denote
\delta_{ij}w^{i}w^{j}
in some choice of geodesic coordinates on
B
. From
[
12
, (2.22)]
, we see that
\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta\right)|w|^{2}\leq 0
, so by the maximum principle we deduce that
w
must be contained in
B
as long as
w
is defined. Then,
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
implies that
w
must be defined for all time, and we have the bound

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{M\times[1,\infty)}|dw|<\infty.

Let
t_{j}:=j
. Then,
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
implies that for a subsequence (also labeled
t_{j}
),
w(t_{j})

C^{2}
converges to a harmonic map
w_{\infty}
, which must be contained in
B
. By
[
12
, Theorem 1]
,
w_{\infty}
must be a constant map, whose image we denote by
x_{\infty}
.

To show that in fact we have exponential convergence in time to
w_{\infty}
, we proceed as follows. For
\varepsilon>0
small enough,
B^{\prime}:=B_{\varepsilon}(x_{\infty})
is a regular ball. We fix some choice of normal coordinates on
B^{\prime}
. We first show that there exists
C>0
such that for
t
large enough

(4.6)

\displaystyle\int|dw|^{2}\leq C\int|\tau(w)|^{2}.

Here
\tau(w)
denotes the tension field, i.e., map Laplacian, of
w
, and
C
may increase from line to line in the argmument. Write
w=w^{i}e_{i}
in the coordinates on
B^{\prime}
, and set

\displaystyle c^{i}:=\frac{1}{\text{Vol}(M)}\int_{M}w^{i}.

In the coordinates on
B^{\prime}

\displaystyle C^{-1}\delta_{ij}\leq h_{ij}\leq C\delta_{ij}.

We compute

\displaystyle\int|dw|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\int|dw^{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{i}\int\langle w^{i}-c^{i},d^{\ast}dw^{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\sqrt{\int|w^{i}-c^{i}|^{2}}\sqrt{\int|d^{\ast}dw^{i}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}\sqrt{\int|dw^{i}|^{2}}\sqrt{\int|d^{\ast}dw^{i}|^{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\int|dw|^{2}\leq C\sum_{i}\int|d^{\ast}dw^{i}|^{2}.

Let
\Gamma
denote the Christoffel symbols of
h
on
B^{\prime}
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}d^{\ast}dw^{i}=\tau(w)-\Gamma(w)(\text{tr}_{g}(dw\otimes dw)).

Passing to a subsequence of the
t_{j}
, we have that the image of
w(t_{j})
is contained in
B_{j^{-1}}(x_{\infty})
. The same is true for
t\geq t_{j}
by the maximum principle argument in the second line of this proof. Since the coordinates on
B^{\prime}
are normal, we thus have
\|\Gamma(w(t))\|_{\infty}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. The bound (
4.6
) thus follows in view of (
4.5
).

By (
4.6
), we have for
t
large enough

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int|dw|^{2}=-2\int|\tau(w)|^{2}\leq-2C^{-1}\int|dw|^{2}.

Hence, the energy of
w
is eventually exponentially decaying, so standard estimates for harmonic map flow imply that
w(t)\to w_{\infty}
exponentially in
C^{k}
for all
k
, as desired.
∎

.

Let

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
w\in W^{1,a}(M,N)
, where

. There exists
\varepsilon>0
, depending on the geometries of
M
and
N
, such that the following holds. If for some

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in M,0<R\leq\rho_{0}}R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|dw|^{2}\leq\varepsilon^{2},

\lambda_{0}>0
, depending on

and the geometries of

and

such that

in

as
\lambda\searrow 0
. Moreover, if

is continuous, then
w_{\lambda}
is homotopic to

\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]

\|dw_{\lambda}\|_{2,2}\leq C_{M,N}\|dw\|_{2,2}

Proof.

By Nash’s embedding theorem, we fix an isometric embedding of
N
into some
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
. Then, there is some
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that the Euclidean
\varepsilon_{1}
-neighborhood
U
of

is a tubular neighborhood for which the nearest-neighbor projection
\pi:B_{\varepsilon_{1}}(N)\to N
is well defined and smooth. We first construct smooth approximations
\tilde{w}_{\lambda}
of

in
W^{1,a}(M,{\mathbb{R}}^{k})
and show that they take value in
U_{\varepsilon_{1}}
.

Fix a finite cover
\{B_{R_{0}}(p_{i})\}
of
M
by balls of radius
R_{0}
, where

satisfies the conditions (1)-(4) from the proof of Prop.
3.2
. We also fix a smooth partition of unity
\{\psi_{i}\}
subordinate to the cover
\{B_{R_{0}}(p_{i})\}
, and for each

we fix

-centered geodesic coordinates

\displaystyle N_{p_{i}}:B_{6R_{0}}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to B_{6R_{0}}(p_{i})\subset M.

Let
\eta
be a standard mollifier on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, i.e.,
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
,
\eta\geq 0
,
\eta
is radial, and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\eta=1
. Denote
\eta_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{-n}\eta(\lambda^{-1}\cdot)
. For
\lambda_{0}\in(0,R_{0})
, we may define the mollification for

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i}(x):=\psi_{i}(x)\int_{B_{3R_{0}}(0)}w(N_{p_{i}}(N_{p_{i}}^{-1}(x)-y))\eta_{\lambda}(y)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y).

Define
\tilde{w}_{\lambda}:=\sum_{i}\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i}
, which we note is smooth and furthermore is constant if

is constant.

We first show that
\tilde{w}_{\lambda}
takes value in
U_{\varepsilon_{1}}
. In the sequel,
\lambda_{0}
may decrease as need be, and
C_{M,N}
, which is a positive constant depending on the geometries of
M
and
N
, may increase as need be. By the uniform intersection property of the cover, the following holds. For each
x\in M,
there exist
J
indices

, with
J\leq C_{n}
, such that for all
R\in(0,R_{0}],\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}],
and
z\in B_{R}(x)
, we have
\tilde{w}_{\lambda}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i_{x,j}}(z)
and
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\psi_{i_{x,j}}(z)=1
. Given
x\in M
, we compute

\displaystyle d_{\text{Euc}}(\tilde{w}_{\lambda}(x),N)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\text{Vol}(B_{\lambda}(x))^{-1}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\tilde{w}_{\lambda}(x)-w(y)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,N}\lambda^{-n}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\sum_{j=1}^{J}\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i_{x,j}}(x)-\psi_{i_{x,j}}w(y)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,N}\lambda^{-n}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i_{x,j}}(x)-\psi_{i_{x,j}}w(y)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(y).

It follows from the Euclidean bounds in geodesic coordinates and the Poincaré inequality that

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i_{x,j}}(x)-\psi_{i_{x,j}}w(y)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(y)\leq C_{M,N}\lambda^{2-n}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\nabla(\psi_{i_{x,j}}w)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,N}\lambda^{2-n}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\nabla\psi_{i_{x,j}}|^{2}|w|^{2}\,\mu_{g}+C_{M,N}\lambda^{2-n}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\psi_{i_{x,j}}|^{2}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,N}\lambda^{2}+C_{M,N}\lambda^{2-n}\int_{B_{\lambda}(x)}|\nabla w|^{2}\,\mu_{g}.

If
\lambda_{0}\leq\rho_{0}
and
\varepsilon
are small enough, this last line is less than
\varepsilon_{1}^{2}
. Hence, up to shrinking
\lambda_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\text{Euc}}(\tilde{W}_{\lambda}(x),N)^{2}<\varepsilon_{1}^{2}.

Thus,
\tilde{w}_{\lambda}
takes value in
U
.

Consequently, we may define

\displaystyle w_{\lambda}:=\pi\circ\tilde{w}_{\lambda}.

By standard arguments, we have as
\lambda\searrow 0
that
w_{\lambda}\to w
in
W^{1,a}(M,N)
, and in
C^{0}(M,N)
if
w
is continuous. Moreover, if
w_{\lambda}\to w
in
C^{0}
, then
w_{\lambda}
is homotopic to
w
for
\lambda
small enough, and the same is true for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}]
by continuity.

Finally, we prove the Morrey norm estimate on
dw_{\lambda}
.
Let
x\in M
and
R\in(0,R_{0}]
. Since
|d\pi(v)|\leq C_{M,N}|v|
for all tangent vectors
v\in TU
, we have

\displaystyle R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|dw_{\lambda}|^{2}\,\mu_{g}\leq C_{M,N}R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|\nabla\tilde{w}_{\lambda}|^{2}\,\mu_{g}.

We next bound the RHS. Let
z\in B_{R}(x)
. As before, we may write
\tilde{w}_{\lambda}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}\tilde{w}_{\lambda,i_{x.j}}(z)
, and we have
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\psi_{i_{x,j}}(z)=1
. Fixing
x
-centered geodesic coordinates
N_{x}
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla\tilde{w}_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle=f_{\text{I}}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}f_{\text{II},j}+f_{\text{III},j}+f_{\text{IV},j},

where

\displaystyle f_{\text{I}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{3R_{0}}(0)}\eta_{\lambda}(y)\nabla\big(w(N_{x}(N_{x}^{-1}(z)-y))\big)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y)

\displaystyle f_{\text{II},j}

\displaystyle=\psi_{i_{x,j}}(z)\int_{B_{3R_{0}}(0)}\eta_{\lambda}(y)\nabla\left(w\left(N_{p_{i_{x,j}}}\left(N_{p_{i_{x,j}}}^{-1}(z)-y\right)\right)-w(N_{x}(N_{x}^{-1}(z)-y))\right)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y)

\displaystyle f_{\text{III},j}

\displaystyle=(\nabla\psi_{i_{x,j}}(z))\int_{B_{3R_{0}}(0)}\eta_{\lambda}(y)\left(w\left(N_{p_{i_{x,j}}}\left(N_{p_{i_{x,j}}}^{-1}(z)-y\right)\right)-w(z)\right)\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y)

\displaystyle f_{\text{IV},j}

\displaystyle=(\nabla\psi_{i_{x,j}}(z))\int_{B_{3R_{0}}(0)}\eta_{\lambda}(y)\left(w(z)-w(\left(N_{x}\left(N_{x}^{-1}(z)-y\right)\right)\right)\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y).

Since
\int_{B_{3R_{0}(0)}}\eta_{\lambda}(y)\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y)=1
, Jensen’s inequality and the bounded geometry of
M,N
imply

\displaystyle R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|f_{\text{I}}(z)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(z)\leq C_{M,N}\|dw\|_{2,2}^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle C_{M,N}^{-1}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|f_{\text{II}}(z)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{R}(x)}\left|\int_{B_{3R}(0)}\eta_{\lambda}(y)\nabla\left(w\left(N_{p_{i_{x,j}}}\left(N_{p_{i_{x,j}}}^{-1}(z)-y\right)\right)\right)\,\mu_{\text{Euc}}(y)\right|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(z)+\int_{B_{R}(x)}|f_{\text{I}}(z)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq C_{M,N}R^{n-2}\|dw\|_{2,2}^{2}.

Now, since

\displaystyle w(z)-w\left(N_{x}\left(N_{x}^{-1}(z)-y\right)\right)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}w\left(N_{x}\left(N_{x}^{-1}(z)-(1-t)y\right)\right)\,dt,

we have

\displaystyle R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|f_{\text{IV},j}(z)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(z)\leq C_{M,N}\|dw\|_{2,2}^{2}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|f_{\text{III},j}(z)|^{2}\,\mu_{g}(z)\leq C_{M,N}\|dw\|_{2,2}^{2}.

Thus, we deduce that

\displaystyle R^{2-n}\int_{B_{R}(x)}|\nabla\tilde{w}_{\lambda}|^{2}\,\mu_{g}\leq C_{M,N}\|dw\|_{2,2}^{2},

from which the desired bound follows.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

The “only if” direction is clear since the infimum is attained by a constant map.

On the other hand, suppose that (
1.8
) holds. Then, there exist continuous maps
w_{i}\in\gamma
such that
\|dw_{i}\|_{2,2}\searrow 0
. Then by Lemma
4.4
, there exist smooth maps
w_{i}^{\prime}
such that
w_{i}^{\prime}
is homotopic to
w_{i}
and
\|dw_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2,2}\searrow 0
. Hence for
i
large enough, Theorem
1.3
implies that
w_{i}^{\prime}
is homotopic to a constant map. We conclude that
\gamma
is trivial, as desired.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

In the sequel,
C
denotes a constant depending on the geometry of
M
that may increase in each appearance. For the first estimate, it suffices that we have for
t\in(0,1]
the estimate

(5.1)

\displaystyle|\nabla_{x}H(x,y,t)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}H\left(x,y,Ct\right).

To see that this bound is sufficient, note that

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\int H(x,y,t)f_{0}(y)\,\mu_{g}(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int\left|\nabla_{x}H(x,y,t)\right|\left|f_{0}(y)\right|\,\mu_{g}(y)

\displaystyle\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int H(x,y,Ct)|f_{0}(y)|\,\mu_{g}(y),

so by
L^{p}
estimates for the heat kernel on
(0,C]
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})\|_{r}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|H_{Ct}(|f_{0}|)\|_{r}\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)-\frac{1}{2}}\|f_{0}\|_{q}.

But the bound (
5.1
) follows from
[
9
, Theorem 6]
and (
3.2
), so the first estimate of Lemma
4.1
holds.

For the second estimate of Lemma (
4.1
), we do the following. First we prove the estimate with
q=r
, and then we prove it with
r=\infty
and
q=1
. Then, the other cases follow by interpolating as usual. We start with the
q=r
case.

Recall that if
f
solves the heat equation, then

(5.2)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta_{1})\nabla f=\text{Ric}\#\nabla f,

where
\Delta_{1}
is the connection Laplacian on one-forms. Since
M
is compact, we let
H_{t}^{1}
denote the fundamental solution of the heat equation on one-forms. Then by (
5.2
) and Duhamel’s principle,

\displaystyle\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})=H_{t}^{1}(\nabla f_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}H_{t-s}^{1}\left(\text{Ric}\#\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\right)\,ds.

Since for all
r
and
t\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\left\|H_{t}^{1}\right\|_{L^{r}\to L^{r}}\leq C,

we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\leq\|H_{t}^{1}(\nabla f_{0})\|_{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|H_{t-s}^{1}\left(\text{Ric}\#\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\right)\right\|_{r}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{r}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\text{Ric}\#\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\right\|_{r}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{r}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\right\|_{r}\,ds.

Hence, if
t
is sufficiently small depending only on
C
,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<s\leq t}\|\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\|_{r}\leq C\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{0<s\leq t}\|\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\|_{r},

whence

(5.3)

\displaystyle\|\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})\|_{r}\leq C\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{r}.

By the semigroup property of
H_{t}
, we obtain this estimate with a larger
C
for all
t\in(0,1]
. This concludes the
q=r
case of the second estimate of Lemma
4.1
.

Next we do the
r=\infty
and
q=1
case of the second estimate of Lemma
4.1
. By (
5.2
) and Kato’s inequality, we have (weakly)

(5.4)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|\nabla f|\leq C|\nabla f|.

Thus by parabolic Moser iteration, we have for
t\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\|\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla H_{s}(f_{0})\|_{1}\,ds.

Then by (
5.3
) with
r=1

\displaystyle\|\nabla H_{t}(f_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{-\frac{n}{2}}\|\nabla f_{0}\|_{1},

which is the
r=\infty
and
q=1
case.

The other cases follow by interpolating the
L^{r}\to L^{r}
bound and the
L^{1}\to L^{\infty}
bound.
∎

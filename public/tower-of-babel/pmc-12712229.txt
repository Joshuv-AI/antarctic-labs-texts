Effect of Complex Decongestive Therapy on Quality of Life in Advanced Breast Cancer Patients With Upper Limb Lymphedema: A Quasi‐Experimental Study From a Palliative Lymphedema Care Clinic in Bangladesh
Authors: Rifat Jahan, Sohel Rahman, Nisha Mortuja Aktar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimLymphedema is a frequent and debilitating complication of advanced breast cancer that significantly impairs physical, psychological, and functional well‐being. Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT) is considered the gold standard of conservative treatment, yet evidence from resource‐limited settings such as Bangladesh remains scarce. To evaluate the effect of CDT on limb volume reduction and quality of life (QOL) among women with advanced breast cancer presenting with upper limb lymphedema in a palliative care setting in Bangladesh.MethodsThis quasi‐experimental study was conducted at the Palliative Lymphedema Care Clinic of a tertiary care hospital, between May 2023 to September 2023. Forty‐two women with stage III–IV breast cancer and unilateral upper limb lymphedema were included. The patients received a standard 6‐week CDT protocol, administered under the supervision of palliative care specialists in the clinic. Limb volume was measured using the circumference method, and QOL was assessed using the validated Bengali version of the Lymphedema Life Impact Scale (LLIS). Data were analyzed using paired t‐test, ANOVA, and Pearson correlation.ResultsThe mean age of participants was 50.2 years; most had stage IV cancer (54.8%) and ECOG performance status III (71.4%). After 6 weeks of CDT, limb volume significantly decreased (right arm: 2492.4 → 1586.4 cm³; left arm: 3505.6 → 1630.0 cm³; p = 0.001). LLIS scores improved significantly across physical, psychosocial, and functional domains (p < 0.001). Greater QOL improvements were observed among patients with ECOG III compared to ECOG IV (p = 0.019). Reduction in limb volume correlated moderately with improvements in physical and functional concerns as well as overall QOL.ConclusionCDT effectively reduces limb volume and enhances QOL among advanced breast cancer patients with lymphedema. Timely initiation of CDT in palliative settings may maximize benefits, underscoring the importance of integrating structured lymphedema management within cancer care in Bangladesh.

Introduction

Lymphedema refers to the abnormal buildup of lymphatic fluid in the interstitial tissue, caused by blockage or compression in the lymphatic system. It may be congenital or arise secondary to factors such as surgery, burns, trauma, or radiation therapy. Cancer patients frequently develop lymphedema either due to the malignancy itself or as a consequence of anti‐cancer treatments [1, 2]. A recent study reported that 6.8% of breast cancer patients presented with lymphedema at diagnosis, with the incidence increasing to 19.9% after 2 years and 23.8% after 7 years. The majority of cases (88%–93%) involve the upper limb, while 14%–27% occur in the trunk and/or breast [3]. If untreated, lymphedema can result in profound physical and psychosocial consequences that negatively affect quality of life [4].

Quality of life (QOL) for cancer patients is a multidimensional construct encompassing symptom control, physical and psychological well‐being, social interaction, and spiritual health [5]. In patients with advanced breast cancer, upper limb lymphedema is particularly debilitating, as it impairs physical function through decreased range of motion, weakness, and sensory disturbances such as tingling or numbness. These physical burdens are often compounded by psychological challenges, including depression, anxiety, and social withdrawal, which disrupt family, work, and sexual life [6, 7, 8].

Conservative management remains the primary approach for lymphedema in advanced and terminally ill breast cancer patients. Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT)—comprising compression (bandaging or hosiery), manual lymphatic drainage, skin care, and exercise—is considered the gold standard [9]. While outcomes depend heavily on patient adherence, approximately 73% of patients experience improvement with CDT [10]. Evidence shows that CDT can significantly reduce limb volume and improve sleep quality, functional status, psychological well‐being, and overall quality of life in lymphedema patients [11, 12, 13].

In Bangladesh, where access to specialized palliative and lymphedema care is limited and research evidence is scarce, this study was designed to generate locally relevant insights. By focusing on women with advanced breast cancer—who face a high symptom burden and limited curative options—this study seeks to highlight the potential role of Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT) as an effective, low‐cost, and feasible intervention to improve quality of life within a resource‐constrained health care system like Bangladesh.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This study was conducted at the Palliative Lymphedema Care Clinic of a tertiary care hospital in Bangladesh. This study employed a quasi‐experimental design, as all patients referred to the Lymphedema Clinic received CDT as part of their routine clinical care, and no randomization or allocation to a control group was conducted. The inclusion of a control group was not feasible or ethically appropriate, since withholding or delaying CDT from eligible patients would have conflicted with established standards of palliative care. Participant recruitment commenced on May 1, 2023 and continued through August 18, 2023. The 6‐week post‐treatment follow‐up for all enrolled patients was concluded by September 30, 2023.

Sample Criteria

All female patients with advanced breast cancer (stages III and IV) presenting with unilateral upper limb lymphedema and referred to the Palliative Lymphedema Care Clinic were included in the study. These patients were in the incurable stage of their disease and had been referred for routine palliative care, in which lymphedema management was a standard component. Patients receiving only their first session of CDT were included. Exclusion criteria were: presence of lower limb or non‐cancer‐related lymphedema; prior treatments that might reduce lymphedema (such as previous CDT sessions or diuretics); organ failure (cardiac, hepatic, or renal); and contraindications to CDT, including recent deep vein thrombosis, cellulitis, or complete lymphatic obstruction caused by tumor.

Sample Size

The sample was selected using the census method. During the study period, a total of 68 patients attended the lymphedema clinic for first session of CDT. However, 26 patients died before follow‐up, leaving a final sample size of 42.

Data Collection Instrument

A structured questionnaire was developed comprising three sections. The first section collected socio‐demographic information, disease and treatment details, and patient performance status. The second section included measurements of limb volume and lymphedema grading. The third section consisted of the Bengali version of the Lymphedema Life Impact Scale (LLIS) version 2 questionnaire.

Lymphedema Life Impact Scale

The Lymphedema Life Impact Scale (LLIS) is a validated, lymphedema‐specific self‐report questionnaire developed by Weiss and Daniel (2015), designed to assess the physical, functional, and psychosocial impact of lymphedema on quality of life [16]. It has been translated into Bengali and validated by Emran et al. [17]. It comprises 18 items covering four dimensions of a lymphedema patient's life: physical (items 1–6, addressing movement, strength, skin condition, and pain in the affected limb), psychosocial (items 7–12, focusing on body image, intimacy, social interaction, and depression), functional (items 13–17, assessing self‐care, daily activities, sleep quality, and clothing fit), and infection (item 18, evaluating frequency of infection episodes). Responses are ranked from 0 to 4, where 0 indicates no impairment and 4 indicates severe impairment. The question regarding infection has its own domain, with scores ranging from 0 (no episodes of infection) to 4 (four or more episodes of infection in the past year). A lower score represents a higher quality of life [16].

Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) Performance Status

The ECOG Performance Status is a standardized scale used to assess a patient's overall functional status, daily activity level, and ability to care for themselves, particularly in oncology and palliative care settings [17]. It helps clinicians evaluate the impact of disease on the patient's quality of life, determine prognosis, and guide treatment decisions. Data Collection Procedure

The research team visited the Lymphedema Clinic every Monday. Prior to initiating CDT, circumferential measurements of the affected limb were obtained at predefined anatomical landmarks (knuckles, styloid process, elbow crease, and 10 cm above the elbow) using a standard measuring tape (Figure 1). Also during the measurement the grading of lymphedema was determined. Subsequently, one member of the research team administered the Lymphedema Life Impact Scale (LLIS), and responses were recorded on a structured data collection sheet.

All participants underwent this standardized CDT protocol during the study period, and no modifications were introduced by the investigators. Patients were reassessed after 6 weeks after competition of CDT at the Lymphedema Clinic, where circumferential limb volume measurements and LLIS scores were repeated.

Data Analysis

The affected limb volume and LLIS scores were calculated using their respective scoring formulas. Domain scores of the LLIS were obtained by summing individual item scores, dividing by the number of answered questions, and multiplying by 25. Overall QOL was calculated using items 1–17 and expressed as the percentage of impairment; item 18 was excluded as it does not contribute to QOL scores. All data were then entered into the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 26, for analysis. A paired t‐test was used to compare changes in affected limb volume and LLIS scores from baseline to the 6‐week follow‐up. One‐way ANOVA assessed the association between lymphedema grades and LLIS scores, while an independent t‐test examined relationships between LLIS scores, cancer stage, and performance status. Pearson correlation analyzed the relationship between mean limb volume change and improvement in QOL. All means were reported with 95% confidence intervals, and p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. We also used ChatGPT 5.0 for language formatting of this article.

Ethical Considerations

The ethical approval (Approval no: BSMMU/2023/2546, Date: 05/03/2023) was obtained from the Institutional Review Board of Bangladesh Medical University. Written informed consent was taken from all the eligible patients.

Result

The study included 42 women with advanced breast cancer and upper limb lymphedema, with a mean age of 50.2 years. Lymphedema was more frequent in the left arm (54.8%), and most patients presented with stage 3b lymphedema (54.8%) and stage IV cancer (54.8%). Most patients had ECOG performance status III (71.4%). Common risk factors of lymphedema among these patients included mastectomy with axillary dissection (92.9%) and radiotherapy (76.2%) (Table 1).

Discussion

Lymphedema is a long‐term condition that negatively affects both physical function and overall quality of life. At present, there is no definitive cure for lymphedema, but several treatments have been proposed to alleviate its symptoms. Among these, Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT) has proven to be an effective approach [10].

In our study, significant limb volume reduction was achieved in both the right and left upper limbs within 6 weeks of CDT treatment. Similar to our findings, several studies conducted in different settings have also demonstrated significant volume reduction in lymphedema‐affected limbs with CDT [10, 11, 12, 13, 18, 19]. Studies conducted among terminally ill cancer patients also reported significant volume reduction regardless of prior lymphedema therapy, reinforcing CDT's efficacy across diverse clinical contexts [14, 20]. When compared to other treatment modalities, CDT generally outperforms single‐component interventions. A randomized controlled trial showed that, pneumatic compression devices or exercise therapy alone can reduce limb volume but often produce smaller and less sustained improvements than CDT and more effective when used in combination of CDT [21]. In Bangladesh, resource constraints sometimes necessitate adaptations of CDT protocols, such as using locally available compression materials, shorter therapy sessions, or group‐based exercises. Despite these modifications, the limb volume reductions observed in our study are comparable to those reported internationally.

Regarding quality of life (QOL), our patients demonstrated a substantial improvement, reflected by a reduction in mean LLIS scores as well as significant improvement in physical, psychological and functional domains from baseline to 6 weeks. This aligns with earlier evidence showing that CDT improves physical functioning, emotional function, vitality, sleep quality, bodily pain, and general health domains as well as overall QOL of lymphedema patients [22, 23, 24, 25]. Also, a positive correlation was observed between limb volume and LLIS domains (physical and functional) in our study, indicating that reduction in limb volume was associated with better QOL. This relationship has been confirmed in previous research from Turkey, Korea, and the United States, where greater limb volume reduction translated into improvements in both physical and mental well‐being [13, 19, 26]. Although most effect sizes were small to moderate, the consistent direction of change and statistical significance indicate that CDT provides meaningful clinical benefits, with the moderate‐to‐large improvement in the total LLIS score reflecting a substantial overall enhancement in quality of life. However, unlike these studies, our patients did not show a significant correlation between limb volume reduction and the psychological domain of QOL. This contrast may reflect cultural differences in reporting psychological distress, or the advanced disease stage of our population, where psychological well‐being may be more heavily influenced by overall disease burden rather than limb volume alone. Interestingly, while American studies reported significant improvements after 52 weeks of CDT, our patients experienced meaningful QOL gains within just 6 weeks, highlighting the potential of CDT to provide relatively rapid benefits in advanced cancer care [19].

We also examined the relationship between QOL and disease‐related factors, such as lymphedema stage and ECOG performance status. Similar to the findings of Bozkurt et al. and Sezgin‐Ozcan et al., we found no significant association between LLIS scores and lymphedema stage, suggesting that improvements in QOL with CDT are not dependent on the severity of lymphedema at presentation [20, 27]. However, in our study, patients with better performance status (ECOG III) experienced greater improvements in quality of life following CDT, highlighting the importance of functional reserve in determining the magnitude of benefit from supportive interventions. This suggests that patients referred earlier, at a more ambulant and less symptomatic stage of advanced breast cancer, may achieve greater improvements in QOL through lymphedema management. To our knowledge, no previous studies have reported a similar association between performance status and QOL outcomes in lymphedema patients, making this an important contribution to the literature.

As the participants of our study were women with advanced (stage III and IV) breast cancer, the QOL improvements observed are particularly noteworthy. Patients with advanced breast cancer frequently experience compounded physical and psychological burdens, including pain, fatigue, depression, and social isolation [28, 29]. In Bangladesh, these challenges are often amplified, with patients suffering from particularly high levels of depression and anxiety [30]. Our findings suggest that CDT not only alleviates physical concerns of lymphedema among advanced breast cancer patients but also contributes to broader improvements in functional and psychological well‐being, which is critical in the palliative context where quality of life takes precedence over curative outcomes.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted with a small, non‐random purposive sample over a relatively short duration, which limited our ability to observe the long‐term effects of CDT on quality of life and its interaction with disease progression. Second, the study was carried out in a single center, which may restrict the generalizability of the findings to broader patient populations.

Conclusion

Complex Decongestive Therapy (CDT) has been shown to effectively reduce limb volume and improve quality of life in patients with lymphedema secondary to advanced breast cancer. Sustained benefits require continued treatment, highlighting the need for ongoing management and adherence. Long‐term follow‐up studies are essential to determine the optimal duration and intensity of CDT for maximal and lasting outcomes. Early referral to specialized lymphedema clinics and timely initiation of CDT can play a critical role in improving the overall quality of life for patients in Bangladesh, where access to comprehensive lymphedema care remains limited.

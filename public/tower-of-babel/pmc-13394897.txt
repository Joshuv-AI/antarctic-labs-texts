Fertility outcomes and ovarian function recovery after anti‐NMDAR encephalitis: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jia Wang, Jie Ding, Han Cai, YingXiang Wang, HaoTian Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAnti‐N‐methyl‐D‐aspartate receptor (anti‐NMDAR) encephalitis predominantly affects women of reproductive age and is frequently associated with ovarian teratomas. While acute neurological outcomes are well described, the long‐term impact on ovarian endocrine function and fertility—particularly in tumor‐associated cases—remains poorly understood, creating significant uncertainty in survivorship counseling.Material and MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study involving 106 women diagnosed with anti‐NMDAR encephalitis at a tertiary academic hospital between 2015 and 2024. Primary outcomes included the recovery of ovarian endocrine function, measured by serial sex hormone levels and fertility outcomes. Secondary analyses assessed disease severity, tumor markers, and neuroimaging features between teratoma‐associated cases and non‐teratoma cases.ResultsPatients with teratoma‐associated encephalitis (n = 23) exhibited greater acute disease severity, necessitating higher rates of ICU admission (p = 0.012) and mechanical ventilation (p = 0.021). During the acute phase of illness, 38% of all patients experienced HPO axis suppression, primarily manifesting as amenorrhea. However, longitudinal monitoring revealed substantial recovery, with significant improvements in both luteinizing hormone (p = 0.01) and estradiol levels (p = 0.01) during convalescence. Notably, pregnancy rates were comparable between the teratoma (80%) and non‐teratoma (83.3%) groups (p = 0.68).ConclusionsOvarian dysfunction in anti‐NMDAR encephalitis is often reversible, and long‐term fertility remains favorable even in teratoma‐associated cases. These findings provide valuable guidance for fertility counseling and highlight the importance of incorporating reproductive health monitoring into the long‐term care of young women with tumor‐related autoimmune encephalitis.

INTRODUCTION

Anti‐N‐methyl‐D‐aspartate receptor (anti‐NMDAR) encephalitis represents the most prevalent form of autoimmune encephalitis and a classic model of paraneoplastic neurological syndrome, frequently associated with ovarian teratoma. 1 This is a serious but treatable brain disorder. The disease causes complex mental and nerve problems characterized by psychosis, seizures, autonomic dysfunction, and cognitive impairment. 2 , 3

This rare disease mainly affects young women. Studies from North America and Europe report that more than 80% of patients are female, 1 , 4 , 5 and ovarian teratomas are found in approximately 20%–59% of cases. These germ cell tumors, comprising tissues from multiple embryonic layers, can ectopically express NMDARs. 6 Crucially, ectopic expression of NMDAR within teratoma tissue can provoke a series of immune response, 2 , 7 significantly increase in the production of antibodies against NMDAR. 8

Clinical management traditionally focuses on acute neurological stabilization and immunomodulatory therapy. Consequently, the long‐term reproductive outcomes of these young women have received limited attention. This oversight is clinically significant for several reasons. First, treatments such as high‐dose immunosuppressants and ovarian surgery may independently affect ovarian reserve. Second, the pathogenic antibodies themselves may directly disrupt hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian (HPO) axis function. Existing evidence regarding post‐encephalitis fertility remains sparse and inconsistent, relying largely on isolated case reports or small series, which offers little guidance for patient counseling.

To address this gap, we conducted a longitudinal cohort study. Our primary aim was to systematically evaluate the recovery of ovarian endocrine function and to document subsequent fertility outcomes in women who have survived anti‐NMDAR encephalitis. By providing robust longitudinal data, this work aims to inform evidence‐based counseling and to enhance the integrated long‐term care of young women affected by this tumor‐related autoimmune disorder, thereby addressing a key aspect of survivorship quality of life.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

Study design and population

This was a single‐center longitudinal cohort study, adhering to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. This study enrolled female patients diagnosed with anti‐NMDA receptor encephalitis (anti‐NMDARE) and treated at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐sen University between January 1, 2015, and November 30, 2024. Each patient underwent a thorough evaluation that included neuroimaging, electroencephalography (EEG), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis, and tumor screening.

Evaluation prognosis and definitions

MRI analysis was performed by a senior neuroradiologist and neurologist using 1.5/3.0 Tesla scanners with T1‐weighted, T2‐weighted, and FLAIR sequences.

Mechanical ventilation was indicated in cases of respiratory distress (tachypnea, hypoxemia, or hypercapnia).

Acute phase was defined as within 6 weeks after symptom onset.

Recovery phase was deemed to have begun 4 weeks following cessation of all immunotherapies.

Relapse was defined as the recurrence of symptoms or new neurological deficits after ≥2 months of clinical stability.

Clinical severity was assessed using the Clinical Assessment Scale for Autoimmune Encephalitis (CASE), a validated tool that scores across nine domains (seizures, memory deficits, psychiatric symptoms, consciousness, speech, movement disorders/dystonia, gait/ataxia, brainstem dysfunction, muscle strength), with a total score range of 0–27, where higher scores indicate greater severity. Prognosis was categorized as favorable (0–4), moderate (5–9), or poor (10–27).

Patients with <4 months of follow‐up were excluded from endocrine/reproductive outcome analyses.

Tumor marker levels were classified as elevated (≥2‐fold increase above the upper limit of normal (ULN)) or mildly elevated (1–2‐fold increase above ULN).

Statistical analysis

Data were managed in Microsoft Excel, and statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 22 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and Prism 10.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

Continuous variables with normal distribution were reported as mean ± SD, while non‐normal data were presented as median (interquartile range). Categorical variables were compared using χ 2 or Fisher's exact tests, and continuous variables were analyzed using t‐tests or Mann–Whitney U tests. Serial sex hormone levels were obtained longitudinally in the same individuals at predefined time points (acute phase and recovery phase), enabling paired analysis.

Univariate logistic regression was performed to evaluate 21 variables (e.g., age, BMI, comorbidities, CSF/serum antibody titers, imaging/EEG findings, teratoma characteristics) associated with severity of disease at acute phase. Variables with significant associations in univariate analysis were integrated into a multivariate logistic regression model to identify independent predictors of disease severity in female anti‐NMDARE patients. A two‐tailed p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A longitudinal cohort of 257 patients diagnosed with anti‐NMDA receptor encephalitis was identified at the Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐sen University between January 1, 2015, and November 31, 2024. According to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 106 patients were included in the final analysis (Figure 1).

Flowchart of the study population.

Demographic and clinical characteristics

A total of 106 patients (median age: 22 years, IQR 17–30) were included in the demographic analysis, with 71 (66.9%) completing follow‐up for ≥4 months (median: 48 months; range: 4–108). Students accounted for 45.3% (n = 48) of the cohort. Prodromal symptoms were absent in 44.3% (n = 47). Among symptomatic cases, fever was the most common initial prodrome (32.1%, n = 34). Psychiatric and cognitive impairments were the most frequent first presentations. Comorbidities occurred in 57.5% (n = 61), with pulmonary infections being the most common complication. MRI findings showed that 33% cases exhibited T2/FLAIR hyperintensities, primarily in the frontal lobe, temporal lobe, hippocampus, cerebellum, basal ganglia, or brainstem (Figure 2). EEG showed that the NMDAE‐associated diffuse slow waves and delta brush patterns were observed in 14.1% (n = 15) and 2.8% (n = 3) of cases, respectively. Teratoma analysis

Teratomas were identified in 33 patients (31.3%), predominantly ovarian teratomas (n = 32, 96.9%). The average tumor diameter was 31.5 mm (range: 18–56.6). Histopathological analysis confirmed mature teratomas in 90.6% (n = 29) of cases, including two cases with ectopic brain tissue components. Surgical interventions included ovarian cystectomy (n = 16), unilateral adnexectomy (n = 16), and total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo‐oophorectomy (n = 1). The hysterectomy was performed on a parous patient with a large immature teratoma confirmed by intraoperative frozen pathological results. Tumor markers CA‐125 and CA‐199 demonstrated the strongest association with teratoma presence (p = 0.001 and p = 0.003, respectively) (Table 1).

Sex hormone and menstrual analysis

Menstrual disturbances during the acute phase were noted in 61% of patients with teratomas, compared to 27.1% of those without (p = 0.006), most commonly presenting as amenorrhea. The longest recorded duration of amenorrhea was 12 months. To explore whether treatment regimens independently influenced endocrine recovery, we compared patients who received only first‐line immunotherapy with those who required additional second‐line agents. As shown in Table S5, no significant differences emerged between the two groups regarding recovery‐phase levels of LH, FSH, estradiol, progesterone, or testosterone (all p > 0.05). Likewise, the median time to menstrual normalization did not differ (p = 0.89). These findings suggest that escalation to second‐line therapy does not adversely affect long‐term ovarian endocrine recovery.

Reproductive outcomes

Among the 71 patients with follow‐up ≥4 months (median 48 months, range 4–108), all who conceived did so after having achieved clinical recovery. The median time from recovery to conception was 14 months (IQR 9–23 months).

In the teratoma group (n = 23), four patients achieved spontaneous pregnancies post‐recovery (Table 3). Four patients delivered full‐term infants without maternal complications during pregnancy. Neonatal outcomes were favorable, except for one child who was diagnosed with autism spectrum disorder at age two. Among non‐teratoma patients (n = 48), six patients also achieved spontaneous pregnancies post‐recovery, including one induced abortion due to medication exposure, one inevitable abortion caused by intraamniotic infection at 20 weeks, and four full‐term deliveries. All neonates were healthy, with no maternal complications occurring. Notably, one non‐teratoma patient developed encephalitis at 30 weeks' gestation, achieving symptom remission and full‐term delivery after immunotherapy.

Importantly, all recorded pregnancies were achieved spontaneously; no patient in this cohort used assisted reproductive technologies (ART) or fertility‐enhancing medications. Fertility counseling was formally documented in only three patients (two from the teratoma group, one from the non‐teratoma group), indicating that most pregnancies occurred without specialist reproductive guidance.

Except for one patient who received corticosteroids and IVIG for a first episode at 30 weeks of gestation, no other patient received immunotherapy during pregnancy. Among those who conceived after recovery, all had completed treatment prior to conception.

Treatment and prognosis

No significant differences were observed in second‐line immunotherapy utilization (p = 0.89) or relapse rates (p = 0.97) between the teratoma and non‐teratoma groups (Table S2). Two deaths occurred in the non‐teratoma cohort. By the time of last follow‐up, all surviving patients achieved favorable prognosis (CASE score ≤4).

Univariate analysis identified several factors associated with greater disease severity during the onset phase, including pulmonary infection (p = 0.004), respiratory failure (p = 0.0032), prodromal fever (p = 0.002) or upper respiratory infection (p = 0.021), CSF antibody titers (p = 0.034), MRI lesion count (p = 0.012), E2 levels (p = 0.049), ICU admission (p = 0.001), mechanical ventilation (p = 0.008), teratoma presence (p = 0.003), and tumor size (p = 0.027) (Table S3). Multivariate analysis confirmed prodromal fever (Table S4): (p = 0.004); pulmonary infection (p < 0.001); number of MRI lesions (p = 0.012); ICU admission (p < 0.001); mechanical ventilation (p = 0.004); presence of teratoma (p = 0.003); tumor size (p = 0.016) (Figure 4) as independent predictors of acute severity (Figure 5). Despite differences in initial presentation, both groups demonstrated similar long‐term functional recovery, with return‐to‐work or school rates of 91.3% versus 91.7%, and favorable CASE scores (≤4) at final follow‐up (Figure S1).

DISCUSSION

This study gives important information about hormone changes and reproductive outcomes after anti‐NMDAR encephalitis. We found that acute suppression of the HPO axis was common, but most patients showed hormonal recovery during recovery indicating that reproductive endocrine dysfunction in this condition is often transient. Furthermore, both the presence and the size of ovarian teratomas were associated with greater acute disease severity, which supports early tumor screening and multidisciplinary care to reduce neurological complications. Importantly, patients who attempted conception after clinical recovery had favorable pregnancy outcomes regardless of teratoma status.

Previous reports have focused mainly on neurological mechanisms and functional recovery in anti‐NMDAR encephalitis, while systematic data on reproductive hormones and pregnancy outcomes remain limited and are mostly limited to case reports. 9 , 10 , 11 Immune diseases of the central nervous system can affect hypothalamic–pituitary function, but the pattern and duration of hormonal changes in anti‐NMDAR encephalitis were not well described. In our longitudinal hormone profiling, the acute phase showed clear HPO axis suppression with low LH, FSH, and estradiol levels. Clinically, 38% of patients experienced menstrual disturbances, primarily amenorrhea (one case lasted 12 months).

The mechanisms behind this suppression remain unclear; however, we propose two potential explanations: first, disease‐related stress and poor nutrition may lead to functional hypothalamic amenorrhea, characterized by reduced GnRH pulsatility and subsequent impaired gonadotropin secretion; second, glucocorticoid therapy may suppress KNDy (kisspeptin/neurokinin B/dynorphin) neurons, reduce pulsatile GnRH secretion. 12 Significant increases in LH (p = 0.01) and E2 (p = 0.01) were observed during recovery, and 91% of patients had spontaneous hormonal restoration. The rebound in LH and estradiol occurred regardless of when immunotherapy was given, suggesting intrinsic neuroendocrine recovery, possibly via restoration of kisspeptin signaling. Our additional subgroup analyses further clarify that the observed HPO axis suppression is unlikely to be attributable to specific immunotherapies. Neither the use of second‐line agents nor the number of immunomodulatory regimens was associated with delayed menstrual resumption or reduced sex hormone restoration. Although high‐dose glucocorticoids can transiently suppress GnRH pulsatility, the recovery of LH and estradiol coincided with clinical improvement and steroid tapering rather than the cessation of treatment itself. Thus, the underlying neuroinflammatory process appears to be the primary driver of acute endocrine dysfunction, a notion consistent with the reversible nature of HPO axis suppression documented in this cohort. This recovery differs from the persistent hypogonadism described in some other neuroimmunology disorders.

Indeed, IVIG can modulate inflammatory and immunological profiles, as documented by Uchida et al. 13 To mitigate this confounder, our recovery‐phase sampling was performed ≥4 weeks after cessation of all immunotherapies, a washout period exceeding the typical half‐life of infused immunoglobulins. Moreover, several observations support the view that the observed rises in LH and estradiol primarily reflect endogenous neuroendocrine recovery rather than a direct pharmacological effect of IVIG. Thus, while we cannot entirely exclude transient biomarker fluctuations induced by IVIG, the weight of evidence points to disease‐driven recovery as the dominant mechanism.

The role of estrogen in anti‐NMDAR encephalitis is still debated. Some studies suggest estrogen may worsen disease activity, while others report neuroprotective and immunomodulatory effects, including altered T‐cell trafficking and innate immune responses. 14 In our cohort, rising estradiol levels coincided with clinical improvement. This observation suggests that moderate estrogen supplementation could be considered in selected recovering patients.

Although maternal IgG antibodies could theoretically cross the placenta, confirmed clinical disease of neonates has not been reported. 15 In our follow‐up, nine pregnancies reached term and most children were healthy at last assessment. One child had delayed speech and was diagnosed with autism at age two. Because our sample is small and neonatal antibody testing was not performed, these outcomes are descriptive and cannot establish causality. We acknowledge that the absolute number of pregnancies in this cohort is modest. Nevertheless, the observation that all conceptions occurred spontaneously, without ART, and after a median follow‐up of 48 months, strongly supports our conclusion that ovarian dysfunction in anti‐NMDAR encephalitis is typically transient and reversible. Formal fertility counseling was rarely documented, suggesting that the favorable pregnancy rates observed may even underestimate the potential for spontaneous conception when appropriate reproductive guidance is provided. Larger multi‐center studies are warranted to confirm these rates.

Teratoma‐associated anti‐NMDAR encephalitis links oncology and neurology. Our detection rate of 31% aligns with epidemiological studies. 16 , 17 , 18 Pathophysiology may involve immune responses to NMDA receptors in teratomas that cross‐react with neuronal receptors, leading to neuroinflammation. 19 In multivariate analysis, teratoma presence and larger tumor size independently predicted greater initial disease severity (p = 0.003 and p = 0.016, respectively) and higher rates of ICU admission and mechanical ventilation. Despite this, pregnancy rates after recovery were similar between patients with and without teratomas (80% vs. 83.3%), indicating that teratoma status does not predict fertility potential after recovery. Of note, a recent case report highlighted that failure to improve after ovarian teratoma resection may signal recurrent tumor, underscoring the need for vigilant post‐operative monitoring. 20

Most existing prognostic studies in autoimmune encephalitis use the modified Rankin Scale (mRS), which was developed for stroke and may miss the multifaceted symptom profile of autoimmune encephalitis (AE) patients. The CASE score covers nine AE‐relevant symptom domains and may better capture disease complexity. We used the CASE score to assess severity and outcome. 21 Both groups in our cohort had generally favorable outcomes. Our multivariate model identified several independent predictors of acute severity, reinforcing the concept that early teratoma resection and tailored immunotherapy may limit antibody‐mediated damage.

To our knowledge, this represents the first longitudinal cohort study to systematically assess both ovarian endocrine function and subsequent fertility outcomes in women recovering from anti‐NMDAR encephalitis. Our data provide novel evidence that acute HPO axis dysfunction is frequently reversible. This finding offers clinically meaningful reassurance to patients and providers regarding long‐term reproductive health, a key component of quality of life for young survivors. Furthermore, the comparable pregnancy rates observed between patients with and without ovarian teratomas provide concrete data to guide personalized counseling and alleviate concerns about future family planning.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged: The single‐center design with moderate cohort size may limit the generalizability and statistical power for rare outcomes. Neonatal antibody testing was not performed, so we cannot comment on antibody transmission or direct neonatal effects. The observational nature of the study prevents causal inference about the role of estrogen or specific treatments in endocrine recovery. Future multi‐center studies with standardized neonatal testing and larger samples are needed. Research should also evaluate standardized fertility preservation and hormone management protocols for this population.

CONCLUSION

In summary, this study provides robust evidence that ovarian dysfunction in anti‐NMDAR encephalitis is typically transient. The high rates of hormonal and fertility recovery offer crucial, optimistic data for counseling young survivors. These findings directly inform the long‐term health perspective and quality of life for this vulnerable population, supporting a clinical focus on both acute neurological rescue and the preservation of future reproductive potential.

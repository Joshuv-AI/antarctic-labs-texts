Numerical Study of Dissipative Weak Solutions for the Euler Equations of Gas Dynamics Thanks: Institute of Mathematics, Johannes Gutenberg-University Mainz, 55128 Mainz, Germany; chayu@uni-mainz.de
Shaoshuai Chu, Michael Herty, Alexander Kurganov, Mária Lukáčová-Medvi d’ ová

Abstract
We study dissipative weak (DW) solutions of the Euler equations of gas dynamics using the first-, second-, third-, fifth-, seventh-, and ninth-order local characteristic decomposition-based central-upwind (LCDCU), low-dissipation central-upwind (LDCU), and viscous finite volume (VFV) methods, whose higher-order extensions are obtained via the framework of the alternative weighted essentially non-oscillatory (A-WENO) schemes. These methods are applied to several benchmark problems, including several two-dimensional Riemann problems and a Kelvin-Helmholtz instability test. The numerical results demonstrate that for methods converging only weakly in space and time, the limiting solutions are generalized DW solutions, approximated in the sense of 𝒦 {\cal K} -convergence and dependent on the numerical scheme. For all of the studied methods, we compute the associated Young measures and compare the DW solutions using entropy production and energy defect criteria.



Key words:
Dissipative solutions; Euler equations of gas dynamics; finite volume methods; high-order schemes; two-dimensional Riemann
problems; Kelvin-Helmholtz instability.

AMS subject classification:
65M08, 65M12, 76M12, 76M20, 76N10, 35L65.

1
Introduction

We consider the two-dimensional (2-D) Euler equations of gas dynamics, which read as

\displaystyle\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}+(\rho v)_{y}=0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle(\rho u)_{t}+(\rho u^{2}+p)_{x}+(\rho uv)_{y}=0,

\displaystyle(\rho v)_{t}+(\rho uv)_{x}+(\rho v^{2}+p)_{y}=0,

\displaystyle E_{t}+\left[u(E+p)\right]_{x}+\left[v(E+p)\right]_{y}=0.

Here,
x
and
y
are spatial variables,
t
the time,
\rho
is the density,
u
and
v
are the velocities in the
x
- and
y
-directions,
respectively,
E
is the total energy,
p
is the pressure. The system (
1.1
) is closed using the following polytropic equation of
state:

e(\rho,\theta)=c_{v}\theta,~c_{v}=\frac{1}{\gamma-1},\quad p(\rho,\theta)=(\gamma-1)\rho e=\rho\theta=(\gamma-1)\Big[E-\frac{1}{2}\rho(u^{2}+v^{2})\Big],

(1.2)

where
e
is the specific internal energy,
\theta
is the absolute temperature,
\bm{m}=(\rho u,\rho v)^{\top}
is the momentum vector, and

\gamma>1
is a constant representing the adiabatic coefficient. The second law of thermodynamics is expressed by the entropy inequality
for the total entropy
S
:

S_{t}+(Su)_{x}+(Sv)_{y}\geq 0,\quad S:=c_{v}\rho\ln\left(\frac{p}{\rho^{\gamma}}\right).

(1.3)

We consider a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with either periodic or no-flux boundary conditions, and the initial conditions

\rho(x,y,0)=\rho_{0}(x,y),~~\bm{m}(x,y,0)=\bm{m}_{0}(x,y),~~S(x,y,0)=S_{0}(x,y),~~E_{0}=\rho_{0}e(\rho_{0},S_{0})+\frac{|\bm{m}_{0}|^{2}}{2\rho_{0}}.

(1.4)

It is well-known that solutions of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) may develop complex wave structures, such as shock waves, rarefactions, and
contact discontinuities, even when the initial data are infinitely smooth.
Consequently, distributional weak solutions of (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) are typically considered. However, it was proved in

[
12
]
that the multidimensional compressible Euler equations admit infinitely many weak entropy solutions satisfying
(
1.3
) for a wide range of initial data; see also
[
2
,
7
,
15
]
and references
therein.

As it was demonstrated in many simulations reported in, e.g.,

[
4
,
5
,
20
,
21
,
27
]
, no strong convergence is observed when numerical
methods are applied to shear flows appearing, for instance, in Kelvin-Helmholtz (KH) instability problems. Consequently, a natural question
arises:
What is the weak limit of those numerical approximations?
To answer this question, one has to introduce a generalized solution
concept, which is amenable to numerical methods. As advocated in
[
13
]
, measure-valued solutions are suitable to
represent low-regularity or rough solutions. Measure-valued solutions for multidimensional hyperbolic conservation laws were also studied in

[
21
]
and references therein. In this paper, we will concentrate on consistent approximations of dissipative weak
(DW) solutions
[
16
]
of the Euler equations of gas dynamics.

According to the results reported in
[
20
,
21
]
, the limit of consistent approximations is not a
weak solution in the sense of distributions, if the convergence is only weak. However, a weak convergence of numerical solutions can lead to
the strong convergence to a DW solution by averaging the numerical solutions over different mesh resolutions. In

[
16
,
30
]
, the Cesàro averages over different meshes were considered for low-order numerical methods and a
strong convergence of the Cesàro averages to a DW solution was observed; see also
[
20
]
as well as
[
6
]
,
where a fifth-order alternative weighted essentially non-oscillatory (A-WENO) scheme was used to compute the Cesàro averages.

In this paper, we study the convergence of several modern finite volume (FV) and finite difference (FD) methods of different orders of
spatial accuracy. Although a rigorous consistency analysis is not available for high-order methods, the numerical study presented in this
paper indicates that the numerical approximations of different orders converge weakly. This demonstrates that the consistency errors decay
when the mesh is refined. The sequence of numerical solutions behaves in the following way. When a weak solution of the problem exists, all
different numerical methods converge to this weak solution. However, when the solutions exhibit oscillations and develop turbulent
structures, different numerical methods may yield different limiting DW solutions. In light of this observation, we discuss possible
selection criteria for choosing an admissible and physically relevant solution among those oscillatory numerical solutions.

The paper is organized as follows. In §
2
, we give a brief overview of consistent approximations and DW solutions. In §
3
,
we present a description of FV and FD methods used in the conducted numerical experiments. §
4
contains simulations for several
problems, including several configurations of the 2-D Riemann problems and the KH instability. We analyze the density profiles of the
obtained numerical solutions, along with the averages over solutions of different orders and their time averages. For the KH problem, we
also compute the underlying Young measure in specific subdomains. Additionally, we assess entropy production and energy defects of various
methods to evaluate them with respect to the selection criteria. Finally, §
5
is devoted to a brief conclusion.

2
Consistent Approximations and Dissipative Weak Solutions

To analyze a given numerical method and establish its convergence, it is essential to investigate the local consistency error. For this
purpose, the notion of a consistent approximation seems to be particularly useful; see, e.g.,
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
Consistent approximation
)

A sequence
\left\{\rho_{\ell},\bm{m}_{\ell},S_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
is a consistent approximation of (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) in

\Omega\times(0,T)
if

\bullet
There is a sequence
\left\{\rho_{0,\ell},\bm{m}_{0,\ell},S_{0,\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
, that weakly approximates the
initial data (
1.4
), that is,

\displaystyle\rho_{0,\ell}\to\rho_{0}~\mbox{weakly in}~L^{1}(\Omega),\quad\bm{m}_{0,\ell}\to\bm{m}_{0}~\mbox{weakly in}~L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad S_{0,\ell}\to S_{0}~\mbox{weakly in}~L^{1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\bm{m}_{0,\ell}|^{2}}{2\rho_{0,\ell}}+\rho_{0,\ell}e(\rho_{0,\ell},S_{0,\ell})\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y\to\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\bm{m}_{0}|^{2}}{2\rho_{0}}+\rho_{0}e(\rho_{0},S_{0})\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y,

and satisfies the
energy inequality

\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\bm{m}_{\ell}|^{2}}{2\rho_{\ell}}+\rho_{\ell}e(\rho_{\ell},S_{\ell})\right)(\cdot,t)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\frac{|\bm{m}_{0,\ell}|^{2}}{2\rho_{0,\ell}}+\rho_{0,\ell}e(\rho_{0,\ell},S_{0,\ell})\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y+e^{1}_{\ell}

for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
with
e^{1}_{\ell}\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
;

\bullet
The
equation of continuity

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\rho_{\ell}\varphi_{t}+\bm{m}_{\ell}\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}t=-\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{0,\ell}\varphi(\cdot,0)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y+e^{2}_{\ell}[\varphi]

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T))
with
e^{2}_{\ell}[\varphi]\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
for any

\varphi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T))
;

\bullet
The
momentum equation

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\bm{m}_{\ell}\cdot\bm{\varphi}_{t}+\mathds{1}_{\rho_{\ell}>0}\frac{\bm{m}_{\ell}\otimes\bm{m}_{\ell}}{\rho_{\ell}}:\bm{\nabla}\bm{\varphi}+\mathds{1}_{\rho_{\ell}>0}p(\rho_{\ell},S_{\ell})\bm{\nabla}\!\cdot\!\bm{\varphi}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{\Omega}\bm{m}_{0,\ell}\bm{\varphi}(\cdot,0)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y+e^{3}_{\ell}[\bm{\varphi}]

holds for any
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T);\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
e^{3}_{\ell}[\bm{\varphi}]\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
for any

\bm{\varphi}\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T);\mathbb{R}^{2})
;

\bullet
The
entropy inequality

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(S_{\ell}\varphi_{t}+\mathds{1}_{\rho_{\ell}>0}\left(S_{\ell}\frac{\bm{m}_{\ell}}{\rho_{\ell}}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}t\leq-\int\limits_{\Omega}S_{0,\ell}\varphi(\cdot,0)+e^{4}_{\ell}[\varphi]

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T))
,
\varphi\geq 0
with
e^{4}_{\ell}[\varphi]\to 0
as
\ell\to\infty
for any

\varphi\in C^{2}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T))
;

\bullet
The
minimum entropy principle
is satisfied, namely, there exists
\underline{s}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

S_{\ell}\geq\rho_{\ell}\underline{s}
a.e. in
\Omega\times(0,T)
.

A consistent approximation can be obtained through various strategies, such as vanishing viscosity methods or structure-preserving numerical
schemes. Different numerical methods on a sequence of refined meshes denoted as
\{{\cal I}_{h_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, where
h_{n}>0
represents
a mesh parameter, were considered in
[

]
. This approach yields consistent approximations denoted by

\left\{\rho_{\ell},\bm{m}_{\ell},S_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}=\left\{\rho_{h_{n}},\bm{m}_{h_{n}},S_{h_{n}}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\quad h_{n}\to 0~\mbox{and}~\ell=\ell(h_{n})\to\infty~\mbox{as}~n\to\infty.

]

Suppose

\displaystyle S_{\ell_{k}}\to S

\displaystyle\mbox{in}~L^{q}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))\qquad\mbox{ for }\ell_{k}\to\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty.

Definition 2.3

(
DW solution)

Let the initial data satisfy

\rho_{0}\in L^{\gamma}(\Omega),\quad\bm{m}_{0}\in L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad S_{0}\in L^{\gamma}(\Omega),~~\mbox{and}~~\int\limits_{\Omega}E(\rho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y<\infty.

We say that
(\rho,\bm{m},S)
is a DW solution of (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) in

\Omega\times[0,T)
,
0<T\leq\infty
, if it satisfies the following properties.

\bullet

Regularity

\displaystyle\rho\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),\quad\bm{m}\in C_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})),

\displaystyle S\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\gamma}(\Omega))\cap BV_{\rm weak}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}E(\rho,\bm{m},S)(\cdot,t)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}E(\rho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y~~\mbox{for any}~t\in[0,T).

\bullet
The
equation of continuity

\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\rho\varphi_{t}+\bm{m}\!\cdot\!\nabla\varphi\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}t=-\int\limits_{\Omega}\rho_{0}\varphi(\cdot,0)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y

holds for any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T))
.

\bullet
The
momentum equation

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(\bm{m}\cdot\bm{\varphi}_{t}+\mathds{1}_{\rho>0}\frac{\bm{m}\otimes\bm{m}}{\rho}:\bm{\nabla}\bm{\varphi}+p(\rho,S)\bm{\nabla}\!\cdot\!\bm{\varphi}\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{0}^{T}\int\limits_{\Omega}\bm{\nabla}\bm{\varphi}:{\rm d}\mathfrak{R}(t)\,{\rm d}t-\int\limits_{\Omega}\bm{m}_{0}\cdot\bm{\varphi}(\cdot,0)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y

holds for any
\bm{\varphi}\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T);\mathbb{R}^{2})
, where

\mathfrak{R}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;{\cal M}^{+}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}_{\rm sym}))
is the Reynolds defect stress.

\bullet
The
entropy inequality

\int\limits_{\Omega}\Big[S(\cdot,\tau_{2}+)\varphi(\cdot,\tau_{2}+)-S(\cdot,\tau_{1}-)\varphi(\cdot,\tau_{1}-)\Big]{\rm d}x{\rm d}y\geq\int\limits_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}\int\limits_{\Omega}\left(S\varphi_{t}+\left<{\cal V}_{x,y,t};\mathds{1}_{\widehat{\rho}\,>0}\big(\widehat{S}\widehat{\bm{u}}\big)\right>\cdot\nabla\varphi\right){\rm d}x{\rm d}y{\rm d}t,

with
S(\cdot,0-)=S_{0}
holds for any
0\leq\tau_{1}\leq\tau_{2}<T
, any
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega\times[0,T))
,
\varphi\geq 0
, where

\{{\cal V}_{x,y,t}\}_{(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)}
is a parametrized probability (Young) measure:

\displaystyle\cal{\displaystyle V}_{x,y,t}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,T));{\cal P}(\mathbb{R}^{4})),\quad\mathbb{R}^{4}=\left\{\widehat{\rho}\in\mathbb{R},\widehat{\bm{m}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\widehat{S}\in\mathbb{R}\right\};

\displaystyle\left<{\cal V};\widehat{\rho}\,\right>=\rho,\quad\left<{\cal V};\widehat{\bm{m}}\right>=\bm{m},\quad\big<{\cal V};\widehat{S}\,\big>=S.

\bullet
The
energy inequality and compatibility of the energy and Reynolds stress defects.

\int\limits_{\Omega}E\big(\rho(\cdot,t),\bm{m}(\cdot,t),S(\cdot,t)\big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y+\int\limits_{\overline{\Omega}}{\rm d}\mathfrak{E}(t)\leq\int\limits_{\Omega}E(\rho_{0},\bm{m}_{0},S_{0})\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y,

where
\mathfrak{E}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;{\cal M}^{+}(\Omega))
is the energy defect satisfying

2\min\big\{1,\gamma-1\big\}\,\mathfrak{E}\leq{\rm trace}[\mathfrak{R}]\leq 2\max\big\{1,\gamma-1\big\}\,\mathfrak{E}.

A DW solution
(\rho,\bm{m},S)

3
Numerical Schemes

In this section, we provide a brief overview of the 2-D semi-discrete schemes used to conduct the numerical experiments in this paper.

We introduce uniform rectangular cells
C_{j,k}:=\big[x_{j-\frac{1}{2}},x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big]\times\big[y_{k-\frac{1}{2}},y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\big]
centered at
(x_{j},y_{k})
with

x_{j}=\big(x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\big)/2
and
y_{k}=\big(y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}+y_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\big)/2
. The spatial mesh sizes are
x_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-x_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta x
and

y_{k+\frac{1}{2}}-y_{k-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\Delta y
, respectively. Below, we will describe low-order FV methods (§
3.1
) and high-order FD A-WENO schemes
(§
3.2
).

In the FV methods, the computed discrete quantities are the cell averages, which are denoted by

\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}(t):\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x\Delta y}\int_{C_{j,k}}\bm{U}(x,y,t)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y
and assumed to be available at a certain time
level
t
. Prior to evolving them in time, we will perform a conservative piecewise constant (for first-order methods) or piecewise linear
(for second-order methods) reconstruction

\widetilde{\bm{U}}(x,y)=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}+(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k}(x-x_{j})+(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k}(y-y_{k}),\quad(x,y)\in C_{j,k},

(3.1)

whose one-sided point values at the midpoints of the cell interfaces are

\displaystyle\bm{U}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}-\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k},

\displaystyle\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k},

(3.2)

\displaystyle\bm{U}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}-\frac{\Delta y}{2}(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k},

\displaystyle\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}=\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}+\frac{\Delta y}{2}(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k},

(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k}=(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k}\equiv 0

and

, we
have used a generalized minmod limiter (see, e.g.,
[

,

,
37
]

\displaystyle(\bm{U}_{x})_{j,k}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j+1,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}}{\Delta x},\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j+1,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j-1,k}}{2\Delta x},\,\theta\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j-1,k}}{\Delta x}\right),

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\bm{U}_{y})_{j,k}={\rm minmod}\left(\theta\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}}{\Delta y},\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k+1}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k-1}}{2\Delta y},\,\theta\,\frac{\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}-\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k-1}}{\Delta y}\right),

where the minmod function is defined by

{\rm minmod}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots)=\begin{cases}\min(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots)&\mbox{if}~z_{i}>0,\forall i,\\
\max(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots)&\mbox{if}~z_{i}<0,\forall i,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

and the parameter
\theta
was set to
1.3
.

Note that most of the indexed quantities in (
3.1
)–(
3.3
) as well as in formulae below are time-dependent, but we suppress this
dependence for the sake of brevity.

In the FD A-WENO schemes, the computed discrete quantities are point values
\bm{U}_{j,k}:\approx\bm{U}(x_{j},y_{k},t)
. Before evolving them in
time, we use one-dimensional WENO interpolants of an appropriate order, implemented in the local characteristic fields (see, e.g.,

[
25
,
29
,
32
,
33
,
36
]
and references therein) in the
x
- and
y
-directions to obtain the point values

\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}
and
\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}
, respectively.

3.1
Low-Order FV Schemes

In these schemes, the cell averages
\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}(t)
are evolved in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y},

(3.4)

where
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-},\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)
and

\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)
are either the local characteristic decomposition based
central-upwind (LCDCU) numerical fluxes from
[
4
]
, low-dissipation central-upwind (LDCU) numerical fluxes from
[
10
]
, or
viscous finite volume (VFV) numerical fluxes from
[
16
]
. In the rest of this section, we provide a detailed
description of the VFV numerical fluxes, which read as
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\big({\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,\rho}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,\rho u}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,\rho v}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,E}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big)
and

\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\big({\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,\rho}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,\rho u}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,\rho v}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,E}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big)
, where

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,\rho}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle={\cal F}^{{\rm upw},\,\rho}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},\quad{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,\rho u}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}={\cal F}^{{\rm upw},\,\rho u}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+{\frac{1}{2}}\big(p_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}+p_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)-\big(u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}-u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\big),

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,\rho v}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle={\cal F}^{{\rm upw},\,\rho v}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\big(v_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}-v_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\big),

\displaystyle{\cal F}^{\,{\rm FV},\,E}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle={\cal F}^{{\rm upw},\,E}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}p_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}+u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}p_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)

\displaystyle-{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\big(u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)^{2}+\big(v_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)^{2}-\big(u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\big)^{2}-\big(v_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}\big)^{2}\Big),

and

\displaystyle{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,\rho}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle={\cal G}^{{\rm upw},\,\rho}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\quad{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,\rho u}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}={\cal G}^{{\rm upw},\,\rho u}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\big(u_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-u_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\big),

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,\rho v}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle={\cal G}^{{\rm upw},\,\rho v}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{2}}\big(p_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}+p_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)-\big(v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\big),

\displaystyle{\cal G}^{{\rm FV},\,E}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle={\cal G}^{{\rm upw},\,E}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+{\frac{1}{2}}\big(v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}p_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}+v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}p_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)

\displaystyle-{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\big(u_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)^{2}+\big(v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)^{2}-\big(u_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\big)^{2}-\big(v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}\big)^{2}\Big).

In the above formulae,

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal F}}^{\rm upw}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}+u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}-\big|u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}+u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big|-4\big)\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-}+\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm upw}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}+v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}-\big|v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}+v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big|-4\big)\big(\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-}+\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big),

\displaystyle u_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}=\frac{(\rho u)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}}{\rho_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}},

\displaystyle v_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}=\frac{(\rho v)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}}{\rho_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}},

\displaystyle p_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}=(\gamma-1)\!\left[E_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}-\frac{\big((\rho u)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}\big)^{2}+\big((\rho v)_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}\big)^{2}}{2\rho_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}}\right],

\displaystyle u_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}=\frac{(\rho u)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}}{\rho_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}},

\displaystyle v_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}=\frac{(\rho v)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}}{\rho_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}},

\displaystyle p_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}=(\gamma-1)\!\left[E_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}-\frac{\big((\rho u)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}\big)^{2}+\big((\rho v)_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}\big)^{2}}{2\rho_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{\pm}}\right].

Remark 3.1

We stress that the first-order VFV scheme, which according to
[
17
]
produces consistent approximations, is slightly
different from the scheme (
3.4
)–(
3.7
). The difference is in the evolution of
\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$E$\kern-0.51114pt}}}_{j,k}
, which, in the first-order
scheme, reads as

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.51114pt$E$\kern-0.51114pt}}}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=

\displaystyle-\frac{{\cal F}^{{\rm upw},\,E}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-{\cal F}^{{\rm upw},\,E}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}{\Delta x}-\frac{{\cal G}^{{\rm upw},\,E}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-{\cal G}^{{\rm upw},\,E}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}{\Delta y}

\displaystyle-u_{j,k}\frac{p_{j+1,k}-p_{j-1,k}}{2\Delta x}-p_{j,k}\frac{u_{j+1,k}-u_{j-1,k}}{2\Delta x}-v_{j,k}\frac{p_{j,k+1}-p_{j,k-1}}{2\Delta y}-p_{j,k}\frac{v_{j,k+1}-v_{j,k-1}}{2\Delta y}

\displaystyle+\frac{(u_{j+1,k}^{+})^{2}-2(u_{j,k}^{+})^{2}+(u_{j-1,k}^{+})^{2}+(v_{j+1,k}^{+})^{2}-2(v_{j,k}^{+})^{2}+(v_{j-1,k}^{+})^{2}}{2\Delta x}

\displaystyle+\frac{(u_{j,k+1}^{+})^{2}-2(u_{j,k}^{+})^{2}+(u_{j,k-1}^{+})^{2}+(v_{j,k+1}^{+})^{2}-2(v_{j,k}^{+})^{2}+(v_{j,k-1}^{+})^{2}}{2\Delta y}.

3.2
High-Order FD A-WENO Schemes

In this section, we present higher-order extensions of the low-order schemes from §
3.1
within the FD A-WENO framework introduced in

[
25
]
; see also
[
11
,
29
,
22
]
.

In FD A-WENO schemes, computed solutions are realized in terms of the point values
\bm{U}_{j,k}
(rather than the cell averages

\,\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\bm{U}$\kern-0.50003pt}}}_{\!j,k}
), which are evolved in time by solving the following system of ODEs:

\frac{{\rm d}\bm{U}_{j,k}}{{\rm d}t}=-\frac{{\bm{{\cal F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}}-{\bm{{\cal F}}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}}}{\Delta x}-\frac{{\bm{{\cal G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}-{\bm{{\cal G}}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}}}{\Delta y},

(3.8)

where the numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{{\cal G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are defined by

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal F}}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle=\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-\mu_{2}(\Delta x)^{2}(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+H(r-4)\mu_{4}(\Delta x)^{4}(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle-H(r-6)\mu_{6}(\Delta x)^{6}(\bm{F}_{xxxxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+H(r-8)\mu_{8}(\Delta x)^{8}(\bm{F}_{xxxxxxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k},

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal G}}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-\mu_{2}(\Delta y)^{2}(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+H(r-4)\mu_{4}(\Delta y)^{4}(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle-H(r-6)\mu_{6}(\Delta y)^{6}(\bm{G}_{yyyyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+H(r-8)\mu_{8}(\Delta y)^{8}(\bm{G}_{yyyyyyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}.

Here,
\mu_{2}=1/24
,
\mu_{4}=7/5760
,
\mu_{6}=31/967680
,
\mu_{8}=127/154828800
,
H
is the Heaviside function,
r=3
,
5
,
7
, or
9
is the
order of the scheme,
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}\big(\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{-},\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{+}\big)
and

\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\big(\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{-},\bm{U}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}^{+}\big)
are the FV numerical fluxes, which are computed using the
one-sided point values
\bm{U}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}^{\pm}
, which are obtained using an
r
th-order WENO interpolation applied to the local characteristic
variables, and
(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{F}_{xxxxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{F}_{xxxxxxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,

(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
,
(\bm{G}_{yyyyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
(\bm{G}_{yyyyyyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are the higher-order correction
terms, which can be computed with the help of the FV numerical fluxes
\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and

\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
as it was first done in
[
11
]
for the fifth-order A-WENO schemes. The formulae for these terms
depend on the order of the scheme and for different values of
r
they are given below together with the details on particular WENO
interpolations used for each
r
.

r=3
(third-order schemes)

(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}-2\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}\Big],~~(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta y)^{2}}\Big[\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}-2\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}\Big].

The one-sided point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are computed using the third-order WENO interpolation introduced in

[
8
,
9
]
; for details, we refer the reader to
[
9
, Appendix A]
.

r=5
(fifth-order schemes)

(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
,
(\bm{G}_{yy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
, and
(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

are defined in
[
11

, and the one-sided point values
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and

r=7
(seventh-order schemes)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{180(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[

\displaystyle 2\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{5}{2},k}-27\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+270\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle-490\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+270\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-27\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}+2\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{7}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{6(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[

\displaystyle-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{5}{2},k}+12\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}-39\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle+56\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-39\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}+12\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{7}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{5}{2},k}-6\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+15\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle-20\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+15\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-6\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}+\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{7}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle-490\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+270\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}-27\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}+2\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{7}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{6(\Delta y)^{4}}\Big[

\displaystyle-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{5}{2}}+12\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}-39\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle+56\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}-39\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}+12\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}-\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{7}{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{G}_{yyyyyy})_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{1}{(\Delta y)^{6}}\Big[

\displaystyle\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{5}{2}}-6\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k-\frac{3}{2}}+15\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k-\frac{1}{2}}}-20\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}+15\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{3}{2}}-6\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{5}{2}}+\bm{{\cal G}}^{\rm FV}_{j,k+\frac{7}{2}}\Big].

\bm{U}^{\pm}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}
and
\bm{U}^{\pm}_{j,{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
are computed using the seventh-order WENO-Z interpolation from

[

]

r=9
(ninth-order schemes)

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{5040(\Delta x)^{2}}\Big[

\displaystyle-9\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{7}{2},k}+128\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{5}{2},k}-1008\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+8064\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle-14350\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+8064\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-1008\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}+128\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{7}{2},k}-9\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{9}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{240(\Delta x)^{4}}\Big[

\displaystyle 7\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{7}{2},k}-96\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{5}{2},k}+676\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}-1952\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle+2730\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}-1952\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}+676\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}-96\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{7}{2},k}+7\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{9}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{4(\Delta x)^{6}}\Big[

\displaystyle-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{7}{2},k}+12\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{5}{2},k}-52\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j-\frac{3}{2},k}+116\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j-\frac{1}{2}},k}

\displaystyle-150\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}+116\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{3}{2},k}-52\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{5}{2},k}+12\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{7}{2},k}-\bm{{\cal F}}^{\,\rm FV}_{j+\frac{9}{2},k}\Big],

\displaystyle(\bm{F}_{xxxxxxxx})_{{j+\frac{1}{2}},k}=\frac{1}{(\Delta x)^{8}}\Big[

0.1

when the VFV numerical fluxes are utilized.

Below, we will use the following notation for the solutions computed using the same numerical flux, but with different orders of the
resulting scheme:
(\rho_{\ell},\bm{m}_{\ell},S_{\ell})
,
\ell(r)=1,\dots,6
are solutions computed by the first-, second-, third-, fifth-,
seventh-, and ninth-order schemes, respectively, that is,
\ell(1)=1
,
\ell(2)=2
,
\ell(3)=3
,
\ell(5)=4
,
\ell(7)=5
, and
\ell(9)=6
.
In accordance with the concept of
{\cal K}
-convergence described in Theorem
2.5
, we will average these solutions to obtain

(\widetilde{\rho}_{n},\widetilde{\bm{m}}_{n},\widetilde{S}_{n})^{\top}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}(\rho_{\ell},\bm{m}_{\ell},S_{\ell})^{\top}
, where

n=1,\dots,6
.

In some of the examples below, oscillatory solutions are to be captured. As explained in Remark
2.1
, such solutions should be
represented as a Young measure in the phase space. In fact, when the solution is a weak solution, the corresponding Young measure is
expected to be a Dirac
\delta
-function. In order to check whether the obtained solution is a genuine DW solution or a weak solution in the
sense of distributions, we will approximate the probability density function (PDF) of the Young measure
\{{\cal V}_{x,y,t}\}
as follows.

We take a certain small rectangular subdomain
\widetilde{\Omega}
and compute

\underline{\rho}:=\min\limits_{(x_{j},y_{k})\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\rho_{j,k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern-0.50003pt}}}:=\max\limits_{(x_{j},y_{k})\in\widetilde{\Omega}}\rho_{j,k}.

We then introduce
\Delta\rho:=(\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern-0.50003pt}}}-\underline{\rho})/30
and the following partition of the interval
[\underline{\rho},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern-0.50003pt}}}]
:

[\underline{\rho},\hbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.5pt\kern 1.72218pt\hbox{\kern-0.50003pt$\rho$\kern-0.50003pt}}}]=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{30}\big[\rho^{({i-\frac{1}{2}})},\rho^{({i+\frac{1}{2}})}\big),~~\mbox{where}~~\rho^{({\frac{1}{2}})}=\underline{\rho},~~\rho^{({i+\frac{1}{2}})}=\rho^{({i-\frac{1}{2}})}+\Delta\rho,~~i=2,\dots,30.

The distribution of the dataset formed by the values
\rho_{j,k}
across all
C_{j,k}\subseteq\widetilde{\Omega}
is represented by a function

\sigma_{\ell}=\sigma_{\ell}(\rho)
, where the subscript
\ell=\ell(r)
indicates that the density values have been computed using the
r
-th
order scheme. More specifically,
\sigma_{\ell}(\rho^{(i)})
denotes the number of cells
C_{j,k}
for which the computed values
\rho_{j,k}

fall within the interval
\big[\rho^{({i-\frac{1}{2}})},\rho^{({i+\frac{1}{2}})}\big)
. Additionally, we apply a normalization such that

\Delta\rho\sum\limits_{i=1}^{30}\sigma_{\ell}\big(\rho^{(i)}\big)=1.

In order to obtain a strong convergence, we average the sequence
\{\sigma_{\ell}\}
and introduce

\widetilde{\sigma}_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{n}\sigma_{\ell}
,
n=1,\dots,6
.

4.4
Selection Criteria

As seen in the previous results (Configuration 3 for the 2-D Riemann problem and the KH instability problem), different numerical schemes
can converge to different DW solutions. Consequently, we need additional criteria to select the physically relevant one. In this section, we
analyze potential selection criteria based on the entropy production and energy defects. Notice that similar selection criteria were studied
theoretically in
[
19
,
18
]
.

We denote the integral of the average total entropy over orders up to
n
by

{\cal S}^{n}(t):=\int\limits_{\Omega}\widetilde{S}_{n}(x,y,t)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y.

The turbulent energy and total energy are defined by

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{n}_{1}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{\Omega}\widetilde{E}_{n}(x,y,t)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\left[\frac{|\bm{m}_{\ell}|^{2}}{2\rho_{\ell}}+\rho_{\ell}e(\rho_{\ell},S_{\ell})\right]{\rm d}x{\rm d}y,

\displaystyle{\cal E}^{n}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int\limits_{\Omega}E\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{n}(x,y,t),\widetilde{\bm{m}}_{n}(x,y,t),\widetilde{S}_{n}(x,y,t)\big){\rm d}x{\rm d}y=\int\limits_{\Omega}\frac{|\widetilde{\bm{m}}_{n}|^{2}}{2\widetilde{\rho}_{n}}+\widetilde{\rho}_{n}e\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{n},\widetilde{S}_{n}\big){\rm d}x{\rm d}y,

respectively, and the energy defect is defined by

{\cal D}^{n}_{\rm E}(t):=\int\limits_{\Omega}\left|\widetilde{E}_{n}(x,y,t)-E\big(\widetilde{\rho}_{n}(x,y,t),\widetilde{\bm{m}}_{n}(x,y,t),\widetilde{S}_{n}(x,y,t)\big)\right|{\rm d}x{\rm d}y.

Possible selection criteria for DW solutions are (i) the maximization of entropy production, (ii) the minimization of energy defect, and
(iii) the maximization of energy defect. We stress that criteria (ii) and (iii) are not contradictory since they help select the solutions
with either the minimal or maximal oscillatory (turbulent) structures, respectively. Based on the numerical solutions obtained by different
methods, we compare their respective entropy production and energy defects.

We begin with Configuration 3 for the 2-D Riemann problem. The time evolution of the total entropy
{\cal S}^{5}
and energy defects

{\cal D}^{5}_{\rm E}
are shown in Figure
4.14
, and their time averages are presented in Table
4.3
. As expected, the
oscillations in specific regions of the flow, observed in §
4.1
, lead to energy defects. It can be observed that the LCDCU and LDCU
schemes produce smaller defects compared to the VFV schemes. At the same time, these two methods result in smaller entropy productions.
Consequently, selection criteria (i) and (iii) prioritize the VFV schemes, while criterion (ii) prioritizes the LCDCU schemes. We also
emphasize that since the computed solutions develop oscillatory structures in a small part of the computational domain only, the magnitude
of the energy defects is quite small in this example.

Conclusions

In this paper, we have presented a numerical study of dissipative weak (DW) solutions for the Euler equations of gas dynamics. We have
considered different benchmark examples, 2-D Riemann problems with continuous or discontinuous solutions, and the Kelvin-Helmholtz (KH)
instability problem. Three different numerical methods have been investigated, namely the local characteristic decomposition-based
central-upwind scheme (LCDCU), the low-dissipation central-upwind scheme (LDCU), and the viscous finite volume method (VFV). The main result
of our numerical study is that different numerical methods may converge to different DW solutions. This fact is evident in the approximation
of density and other state variables as well as in the averages over different orders of accuracy, which have been confirmed to converge
strongly, even when the convergence of one particular numerical realization is weak only. Furthermore, the averages over different orders of
accuracy have been employed to distinguish between weak solutions in the sense of distributions and genuine DW solutions.

We have also computed the approximated error functions for each problem, which, according to our numerical results, behave similarly for
different numerical methods. Moreover, we have discovered that the time averages of the average numerical solutions obtained by different
methods exhibit similar behavior. In addition, we have approximated the probability density functions of Young measures. In cases where
oscillations arise in numerical approximation, the limits are DW solutions and the corresponding Young measures are not Dirac

\delta
-functions and they clearly depend on the scheme. Finally, recognizing that DW solutions are generically not unique, we have
explored three potential selection criteria, which are based on the maximization of entropy production and either minimization or
maximization of energy defect.

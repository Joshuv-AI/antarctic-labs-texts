Correction: Rural–urban differences in lipid abnormalities among middle‑aged and older Indians
Authors: Priya Chatterjee, Sakshi Arora, Pooja Rai, Latha Diwakar, Thomas G Issac, Jonas S Sundarakumar
Published: 2025

Correction: BMC Public Health 25, 2895 (2025)

https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-025-22625-3

Following publication of the original article [1], the authors reported the following errors:

Correction 1: In the results section, the following sentence states that the proportion of people using tobacco was lower in the rural population. The corrected statement has been provided below.

Original- Further, the rural participants had significantly lower proportions of tobacco and alcohol use, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, cardiac illness, and stroke than their urban counterparts (Table 1).

Corrected- Further, the rural participants had significantly lower proportions of alcohol use, hypertension, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, cardiac illness, and stroke than their urban counterparts (Table 1).

Correction 2: In the discussion section, the prevalence of lipid abnormalities was wrongly quoted from the cited paper. Instead of the individual prevalences, the overall prevalence for the urban and rural population was repeated for each lipid abnormality.

Original: In India, findings of the ICMR-INDIAB − 17 study observed that the prevalence of all the individual lipid parameters was higher in the urban population than the rural – abnormal TC (82.9% in urban and 80.3% in rural), LDL-c (82.9% in urban and 80.3% in rural), HDL-c (82.9% in urban and 80.3% in rural) and TG (82.9% in urban and 80.3% in rural) in adults aged 20 years and above [17].

Corrected: In India, findings of the ICMR-INDIAB − 17 study observed that the prevalence of all the individual lipid parameters was higher in the urban population than the rural – abnormal TC (27.4% in urban and 22.3% in rural), LDL-c (23.5% in urban and 19.6% in rural), HDL-c (68.1% in urban and 66.3% in rural) and TG (36.4% in urban and 30% in rural) in adults aged 20 years and above [17].

Correction 3: In the discussion section, there is a typing error in the prevalence of Triglyceride abnormalities in the urban cohort. It should be 38.6% not 36.8%.

Original: Abnormal TG was the second most prevalent condition in our study, with a significantly higher prevalence in the rural (45.7%) as compared to the urban (36.8%) cohort.

Corrected: Abnormal TG was the second most prevalent condition in our study, with a significantly higher prevalence in the rural (45.7%) as compared to the urban (38.6%) cohort.

We deeply regret the errors.

The original article [1] has been updated.

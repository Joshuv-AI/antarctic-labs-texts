Abelian varieties genuinely of GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} -type
Francesc Fité, Enric Florit, Xavier Guitart

Abstract
. A simple abelian variety A A defined over a number field k k is called of GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} -type if there exists a number field of degree 2 ​ dim ( A ) / n 2\dim(A)/n which is a subalgebra of End 0 ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{End}^{0}(A) . We say that A A is genuinely of GL n \mathrm{GL}_{n} -type if its base change A k ¯ A_{\bar{k}} contains no isogeny factor of GL m \mathrm{GL}_{m} -type for m < n m<n . This generalizes the classical notion of abelian variety of GL 2 \mathrm{GL}_{2} -type without potential complex multiplication introduced by Ribet. We develop a theory of building blocks, inner twists and nebentypes for these varieties. When the center of End 0 ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{End}^{0}(A) is totally real, Chi, Banaszak, Gajda, and Krasoń have attached to A A a compatible system of Galois representations of degree n n which is either symplectic or orthogonal. We extend their results under the weaker assumption that the center of End 0 ⁡ ( A k ¯ ) \operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}}) be totally real. We conclude the article by showing an explicit family of abelian fourfolds genuinely of GL 4 \mathrm{GL}_{4} -type. This involves the construction of a family of genus 2 curves defined over a quadratic field whose Jacobian has trivial endomorphism ring and is isogenous to its Galois conjugate.



1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a simple abelian variety defined over a number field
k
. We denote by
G_{k}
the absolute Galois group of
k
and by
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
the endomorphism algebra
\operatorname{End}(A)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. Let
H
and
t_{A}
respectively denote the center and the Schur index of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
. We set

and say that
A
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
. For a rational prime
\ell
, let

\varrho_{\ell}:G_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\ell}(A))

denote the representation obtained from the action of
G_{k}
on the rational
\ell
-adic Tate module of
A
. Let
E
be
any
maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
. It is well known (see
[
Rib76
]
,
[
Shi98
]
) that to every prime
\mathfrak{L}
of
E
lying over
\ell
one can attach an
E_{\mathfrak{L}}
vector space
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
of dimension
n
and an absolutely irreducible
\mathfrak{L}
-adic representation

\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}:G_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A))

(see
[
Zar89
]
and
[
Chi87
]
) with the property that

\varrho_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{L}\mid\ell}\operatorname{Res}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}).

If
A
is of the first kind, that is, if
H
is a
totally real field
, the works by Chi, Banaszak, Gajda and Krasoń (see
[
Chi90
]
,
[
BGK06
]
,
[
BGK10
]
) show that the image of
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
is symplectic or orthogonal. More precisely, they show that
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
preserves an alternating pairing if
A
has Albert type
\mathrm{I}
or
\mathrm{II}
, or a symmetric pairing if the Albert type is
\mathrm{III}
. If
H
is a
CM field
, then
[
BKG21
]
and
[
Chi91
]
show that
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
preserves a hermitian pairing and hence the image of
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
is contained in a unitary group.

Assume from now on that
A
is
genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
. This means that
A
is simple, of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, and that its base change
A_{\bar{k}}
to an algebraic closure of

does not have any simple factor of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
-type with
m<n
.
We show that if
A
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, then
A_{\bar{k}}
is necessarily isogenous to the power
B^{r}
of a simple abelian variety
B
over
{\bar{k}}
. We call such a

the
building block associated to
A
and say that
A
is
geometrically of the first kind
if
B
is of the first kind, that is, if
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
has Albert type
\mathrm{I}
,
\mathrm{II}
, or
\mathrm{III}
in the classification of division algebras with a positive involution. If we let
F\subseteq H
denote the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, this amounts to asking that
F
is a totally real field (see Lemma
3.5
for the inclusion
F\subseteq H
).

The purpose of this article is to show that, under the assumption of
A
being genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, one can replace the hypothesis that
H
be a totally real field by the weaker condition of
F
being a totally real field and still recover the symplectic or orthogonal nature of
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
. Let
K/k
denote the minimal extension over which
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, and let
\chi_{\ell}
denote the
\ell
-adic cyclotomic character.

Main Theorem
.

Let
A
be genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type and geometrically of the first kind. Let
\Sigma_{A}
denote the set of primes of
H
at which
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
is split. Suppose that
\ell
is a prime such that
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
for every
\lambda\mid\ell
. There exist:

i)

An absolutely irreducible representation
\varrho_{\lambda}:G_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{Aut}(W_{\lambda}(A))
, where
W_{\lambda}(A)
is an
H_{\lambda}
-vector space of dimension
n
, such that

(1.1)

\varrho_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\mid\ell}\operatorname{Res}_{H_{\lambda}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}(\varrho_{\lambda})^{\oplus t_{A}}.

ii)

A character
\varepsilon:\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)\rightarrow H^{\times}
and an
H_{\lambda}
-bilinear, non-degenerate,
G_{k}
-equivariant pairing

\psi_{\lambda}:W_{\lambda}(A)\times W_{\lambda}(A)\to H_{\lambda}(\varepsilon\chi_{\ell}).

Let
B
be the building block associated to
A
and suppose that
\lambda
lies over a prime of
F=Z(\operatorname{End}^{0}(B))
at which
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
is split. Then,
\psi_{\lambda}
is alternating if
B
has Albert type
\mathrm{I}
or
\mathrm{II}
, and symmetric if
B
has Albert type
\mathrm{III}
.

The proof of the above theorem spreads from §
2
to §
7
. In §
2
, we show that an abelian variety
A
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type if there exists a number field
E
of degree
2\dim(A)/n
which is a maximal subfield for both
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
(see Proposition
2.9
). Many of our subsequent results are grounded in this key property.

In §
3
, we introduce the notion of a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type building block. We show that
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-building blocks relate to abelian varieties genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type in the same spirit as the
n=2
case treated in
[
Pyl04
]
. If
B
is the building block associated to
A
, then Proposition
3.8
asserts that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)\otimes_{F}H
define the same element in the Brauer group of

, and this allows one to relate the Albert types of
A
and
B
(see Proposition
3.12
).

The object of §
4
is the construction of the
nebentype character

\varepsilon
. We show that if
A
is geometrically of the first kind, then the extension
H/F
is Galois, and in fact abelian. Given
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
, we can then speak of the complex conjugate
\overline{\lambda}
. The nebentype character
\varepsilon
is the only finite order character with values in

such that
\varrho_{\lambda}\simeq\varepsilon\otimes\varrho_{\overline{\lambda}}
.

The construction of
\varrho_{\lambda}
takes place in §
6
. It amounts to showing that
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
is realizable over

. This is achieved by verifying that
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
has
H
-rational Frobenius traces and that the 2-cohomology class potentially obstructing descent actually vanishes if
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
.

The pairing
\psi_{\lambda}
is constructed in §
7
. Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the prime of
F
lying below
\lambda
. As mentioned above, the maximality of
E
in
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
implies that
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
, and hence
\varrho_{\lambda}
, is absolutely irreducible. The maximality of

in
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
then implies that a certain absolutely irreducible
\mathfrak{l}
-adic representation
\varrho_{\mathfrak{l}}
of
G_{K}
attached to a model over
K
of the building block
B
is a realization over
F_{\mathfrak{l}}
of
\varrho_{\lambda}|_{G_{K}}
. Exploiting this, we retrieve
\psi_{\lambda}
from the
F_{\mathfrak{l}}
-bilinear, non-degenerate,
G_{K}
-equivariant pairing attached by Chi, Banaszak, Gajda and Krasoń to

. Given the absolute irreducibility of
\varrho_{\lambda}
, the
G_{k}
-equivariance is then proven by means of twisting by
\varepsilon
and applying Schur’s lemma.

In §
8
we exhibit an explicit family of abelian fourfolds of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
-type. These are obtained as restrictions of scalars of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
-type building blocks of dimension 2 defined over a quadratic field. These building blocks are, in fact, Jacobians of genus 2 curves. In §
9
we provide a simple criterion to show that a fourfold

in the family of §

is indeed an abelian fourfold genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
-type. In Example
8.6
, we apply this to the case where
A/\mathbb{Q}
is the restriction of scalars of the Jacobian of the curve

\displaystyle y^{2}

\displaystyle=(-10{\sqrt{2}}+18)x^{6}-(33/2{\sqrt{2}}-12)x^{5}-(11/2{\sqrt{2}}+21)x^{4}

\displaystyle-(3{\sqrt{2}}+24)x^{3}-(5{\sqrt{2}}+6)x^{2}-2{\sqrt{2}}x.

By the construction of

, we know that the center

of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
is the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-2})

2.
Abelian varieties of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type

Let
k
be a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
and
A
an abelian variety defined over
k
. Suppose that there exists a number field
E
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra embedding

(2.1)

E\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}^{0}(A).

By functoriality
E
acts on the singular cohomology group
H^{1}(A_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{top}},\mathbb{Q})
, which is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of dimension
2\dim A
; in particular, the quantity
n=2\dim A/[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]
is an integer. We say that such an
A
is an
abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
. In particular, the notion of abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-type coincides with the notion of abelian variety with complex multiplication in the sense of Shimura
[
Shi98
]
, and for
n=2
we recover the notion of abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type in the sense of Ribet
[
Rib92
]
. The terminology comes from the fact that the
\ell
-adic rational Tate module of
A
defines a representation with values in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
.

By
[
Rib92
, Prop. 2.1]
, an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type defined over
\mathbb{Q}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-isogenous to the power of a simple abelian variety
B
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type defined over
\mathbb{Q}
whose endomorphism algebra is a number field of degree equal to
\dim(B)
. This result can be easily extended to the case in which the field of definition is a general number field, in which case one more possibility arises (see
[
Shi72
, Prop. 1.5]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type defined over
k
. Then one of the following possibilities holds:

i)

A
is
k
-isogenous to the product of abelian varieties of
\mathrm{GL}_{1}
-type defined over
k
.

ii)

A
is
k
-isogenous to the power of a simple abelian variety
B
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type defined over
k
for which
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
is either a number field of degree
\dim(B)
or a division quaternion algebra over a number field of degree
\dim(B)/2
.

The next result is a generalization of the previous proposition for abelian varieties of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type defined over
k
. Then one of the following possibilities holds:

i)

A
is
k
-isogenous to a product
A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{r}
of abelian varieties defined over
k
, where each
A_{i}
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}
-type for some
n_{i}<n
.

ii)

A
is
k
-isogenous to the power of a simple abelian variety
B
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type defined over
k
for which
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
is a division algebra of Schur index
t_{B}
over a number field
H
of degree
2\dim(B)/(nt_{B})
, and
t_{B}
is a divisor of
n
.

Proof.

We will show the result by induction on
n
. We may regard Proposition
2.1
as the base case of the induction.

By Poincaré decomposition theorem we have that
A
is isogenous over
k
to a product
A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{r}
of isotypic abelian varieties
A_{i}
defined over
k
such that
\operatorname{Hom}(A_{i},A_{j})=0
if
i\not=j
. From (
2.1
), we obtain an inclusion of
E
into
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{i})
, and thus
E
acts on
A_{i}
up to
k
-isogeny. Fix an embedding of
k
into
\mathbb{C}
. By functoriality,
E
acts on the singular cohomology group
H^{1}(A_{i,\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{top}},\mathbb{Q})
, which is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space of dimension
2\dim(A_{i})
. Therefore there exist integers
n_{i}\geq 1
such that
n_{i}[E:\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim(A_{i})
. In particular
A_{i}
is an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{n_{i}}
-type.
If
r\geq 2
, the equality

\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{2\dim(A_{i})}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{2\dim(A)}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}=n

shows that
n_{i}<n
.

Assume from now on that
r=1
and that
A
is not
k
-isogenous to the power of an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type for any
d<n
.
Let
D
denote the commutant of
E
in
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
. We claim that
D
is a division algebra. To prove this claim we need to show that if
\lambda
is an endomorphism of
A
which commutes with
E
, then it is an isogeny. Suppose it were not, so that
\dim(B)<\dim(A)
, where
B
denotes the image of
\lambda
. Because the number field
E
acts on
A
and commutes with
\lambda
, it acts on
B
, and by functoriality on
H^{1}(B_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{top}},\mathbb{Q})
. In particular, there is an integer
d\geq 1
such that
d[E:\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim(B)
and
B
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type. Since
A
is isotypic, we have that
B
is isotypic. By induction on
n
, we have that
B
is
k
-isogenous to the power of an abelian variety of (at least)
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type. Since any power of an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type is of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type, we deduce that
A
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type. However, the inequality

d=\frac{2\dim(B)}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}<\frac{2\dim(A)}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}=n

contradicts our assumption on
A
.

Note that
E
is a maximal subfield of
D
, since
A
is not of
\mathrm{GL}_{d}
-type for any
d<n
. Therefore
E
is its own commutant in
D
. Since
D
was the commutant of
E
, we obtain that
E
and
D
coincide. In particular, the center
H
of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
is a subfield of
E
. We have that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)\simeq\mathrm{M}_{m}(Q)
, where
Q
is a division algebra with center
H
. Therefore,
A
is the
m
th power of an abelian variety
B
such that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)\simeq Q
. Since
E
is a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
, we have that
[E:H]=mt_{B}
, where
t_{B}
denotes the Schur index of
Q
over
H
. In particular, we get

(2.2)

2\dim(B)=\frac{2\dim(A)}{m}=\frac{n[E:\mathbb{Q}]}{m}={nt_{B}}[H:\mathbb{Q}]\,.

We may now look at
H^{1}(B^{\mathrm{top}}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{Q})
as a
Q
-vector space. This implies that
t^{2}_{B}[H\colon\mathbb{Q}]
divides
2\dim(B)
, which by the above implies that
t_{B}\mid n
.
It remains to show that
B
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, but this follows from (
2.2
) and the fact that
Q
contains (maximal) subfields of degree
t_{B}[H:\mathbb{Q}]
over
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

In the course of the above proposition, we have shown that if

\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type falling in case ii) of the proposition, then

. In particular,

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type defined over
k
. Suppose that
k
is a number field and that
[k\colon\mathbb{Q}]
is coprime to
2[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]
. If
A
falls in case
ii)
of Proposition
2.2
, then
n
is even and
2t_{B}
divides
n
.

Proof.

In the proof of Proposition
2.2
one may appeal to the space of tangent vectors
\mathrm{Lie}(B)
instead of the singular cohomology space
H^{1}(B_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{top}},\mathbb{Q})
. Then one finds that

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{Lie}(B))=\dim(B)[k:\mathbb{Q}]=\frac{nt_{B}}{2}[H:\mathbb{Q}][k:\mathbb{Q}]

is divisible by
t^{2}_{B}[H:\mathbb{Q}]
, equivalently, that
2t_{B}
divides
n[k:\mathbb{Q}]
. If
[k\colon\mathbb{Q}]
is coprime to
2[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]
, then it is coprime to
2t_{B}
, which implies that
2t_{B}
divides
n
.
∎

We now define the main object of study of this article.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that an abelian variety
A
defined over
k
is
genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
if it is simple, of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, and its base change
A_{\bar{k}}
does not have any simple factor of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
-type with
m<n
; in particular,
A_{\bar{k}}
falls in case
ii)
of Proposition
2.2
.

If
A/k
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type and we denote by
E
a subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
with
n[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim A
, then by Remark
2.3
applied to
A
and to
A_{{\bar{k}}}
we have that
E
is a maximal subfield of both
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. This property in fact characterizes abelian varieties that are genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type for some
n
.

Proposition 2.9
.

A simple abelian variety
A
defined over
k
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type for some
n
if and only if
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
is simple and there exists a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
which is also a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
.

Proof.

We have already seen that if
A
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type and
E
is a subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
with
n[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim A
, then
E
is a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{{\bar{k}}})
. Also,
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
is simple since
A_{\bar{k}}
is isotypic.

Suppose now that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
is simple and there exists a maximal subfield
E
of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
which is also a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. Put
n=\frac{2\dim A}{[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]}
, so that
A
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, and let
F
be the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. Since
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
is simple, we have an
F
-algebra isomorphism
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})\simeq\mathrm{M}_{r}(\mathcal{D})
for some division algebra
\mathcal{D}
; this translates into
A_{\bar{k}}\sim B^{r}
, where
B/{\bar{k}}
is simple with
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)\simeq\mathcal{D}
. If we denote by
t_{B}
the Schur index of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
, maximal subfields of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
have degree
t_{B}[F\colon\mathbb{Q}]
, so that the minimum
m
such that
B
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
-type is

\displaystyle m=\frac{2\dim B}{t_{B}[F\colon\mathbb{Q}]}.

The fact that
E
is a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})\simeq\mathrm{M}_{r}(\operatorname{End}^{0}(B))
implies that
[E\colon F]=rt_{B}
. Hence, we see that

\displaystyle m=\frac{2r\dim B}{t_{B}r[F\colon\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{2\dim A}{[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]}=n,

from which we see that
A
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type.
∎

3.
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type building blocks

From now on let
k
denote a number field. Throughout this section we make use of the following terminology, already used by Ribet
[
Rib92
]
and Pyle
[
Pyl04
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

An abelian variety
B/{\bar{k}}
is called an
abelian
k
-variety
if there exists a system of isogenies
\{\mu_{s}\colon{}^{s}B\rightarrow B\}_{s\in G_{k}}
such that
{}^{s}\varphi=\mu_{s}\cdot\varphi\cdot\mu_{s}^{-1}
for every
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
.

For convenience, we also recall a closely related terminology, introduced by Quer
[
Que00
]
, that will be relevant in Section
8
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
L
be a Galois extension of
k
and let
B
an abelian variety over
L
such that
\operatorname{End}(B)=\operatorname{End}(B_{\bar{k}})
. We say that
B
is an
abelian
k
-variety completely defined over
L
if there exists a system of isogenies
\{\mu_{s}\colon{}^{s}B\rightarrow B\}_{s\in\mathrm{Gal}(L/k)}
defined over
L
such that
{}^{s}\varphi=\mu_{s}\cdot\varphi\cdot\mu_{s}^{-1}
for every
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
.

We now introduce a generalization the notion of building block, first introduced in
[
Pyl04
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

An abelian
k
-variety
B/{\bar{k}}
is called a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
k
-building block
if
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
is a central division algebra over a field
F
with Schur index
t_{B}
satisfying

nt_{B}[F\colon\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim(B).

In our terminology, Pyle’s building blocks correspond to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type
\mathbb{Q}
-building blocks. Slightly abusing the terminology, we will say that an abelian variety
B/{\bar{k}}
is a
k
-building block if it is a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
k
-building block for some
n
. Observe that a
k
-building block is just a simple abelian
k
-variety.

Remark 3.4
.

Observe that in a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type building block,
t_{B}
divides
n
. This follows from the fact that the division algebra
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
acts on
H^{1}(B_{\mathbb{C}}^{\mathrm{top}},\mathbb{Q})
(cf. proof of Proposition
2.1
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
be a simple abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, so that
A_{\bar{k}}\sim B^{r}
for some simple
B/{\bar{k}}
. Let
H
be the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
, and
F
be the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
. Then
F\subseteq H
.

Proof.

Let
E\subseteq\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
be a field satisfying
n[E:\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim(A)
. Since
F
is the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, by Remark
2.3
applied to
A_{{\bar{k}}}
, we have that
F
is contained in
E
, and hence defined over
k
. It follows that
F\subseteq H
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
A/k
be an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, so that
A_{\bar{k}}\sim B^{r}
for some simple
B/{\bar{k}}
. Then
B
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
k
-building block.

Proof.

Since
A
is an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type,
B
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, and hence the center
F
of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
satisfies
[F:\mathbb{Q}]=2\dim(B)/(nt_{B})
. That
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
is a central division algebra satisfying property
i)
of Definition
3.3
amounts then to
t_{B}\mid n
. It only remains to see that
B
is an abelian
k
-variety. We will show first that
A_{\bar{k}}
is an abelian
k
-variety. Let
H
be the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
. Then
F\subseteq H
by Lemma
3.5
. Therefore, for any
s\in G_{k}
, the map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})&\longrightarrow&\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})\\
\varphi&\longmapsto&{}^{s}\varphi\end{array}

is an automorphism of
F
-algebras. By the Skolem–Noether Theorem there exists an invertible element
\alpha(s)\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
such that

(3.1)

{}^{s}\varphi=\alpha(s)\cdot\varphi\cdot\alpha(s)^{-1}\qquad\text{for all $\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})$}\,.

Thus the isogenies
\{\alpha(s)\colon{}^{s}A_{{\bar{k}}}=A_{{\bar{k}}}\rightarrow A_{{\bar{k}}}\}_{s\in G_{k}}
form a compatible system of isogenies for
A_{{\bar{k}}}
. Now we invoke the property that the absolutely simple factors of an abelian
k
-variety are also abelian
k
-varieties (see, e.g.,
[
Gui10
, Proposition 3.5]
) to deduce that
B
is an abelian
k
-variety.
∎

Let
A/k
be an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, so that
A_{\bar{k}}\sim B^{r}
for some simple
B/{\bar{k}}
. In view of the previous proposition, we call
B
the building block associated to
A
. We next prove a converse of the previous proposition.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
B/{\bar{k}}
be a
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
k
-building block. There exists an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type
A
defined over
k
for which
B
is the associated building block.

Proof.

By
[
Gui12
, Theorem 2.5]
there exists an abelian variety
A/k
such that
A_{\bar{k}}\sim B^{r}
for some
r
and such that
E=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
is a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. In particular,
A
is simple and has no
{\bar{k}}
-isogeny factor of
\mathrm{GL}_{m}
-type for
m<n
. Hence, it suffices to show that
A
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type. Let
F
denote the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. Since
E
is a maximal subfield of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})\simeq\mathrm{M}_{r}(\operatorname{End}^{0}(B))
, we have that
[E\colon F]=rt_{B}
, so that

\displaystyle[E\colon\mathbb{Q}]=rt_{B}[F\colon\mathbb{Q}]=r\frac{2\dim B}{n}=\frac{2\dim(A)}{n}

showing that
A
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type.
∎

The Brauer classes of the endomorphism algebra of a variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type and that of its building block are related.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type and
B
its associated building block. Let
H
be the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
, and
F
be the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
. Then

[\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)\otimes_{F}H]=[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]

in
\mathrm{Br}(H)
. In particular, if
t_{A}
and
t_{B}
are the Schur indices of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
, then
t_{A}\mid t_{B}
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
3.9
below and Proposition
2.9
, applied to
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\mathcal{B}=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an
F
-central simple algebra, and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
be a division
F
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}
with center
H
. Suppose that there exists a maximal subfield
E
of
\mathcal{A}
which is also a maximal subfield of
\mathcal{B}
. Then
[\mathcal{B}\otimes_{F}H]=[\mathcal{A}]
in
\mathrm{Br}(H)
.

Proof.

The inclusion of
F
-algebras
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
implies (cf.
[
Yu12
, Theorem 2.7]
) that
\dim_{F}\mathcal{A}
divides the capacity of the algebra
\mathcal{X}:=(\mathcal{B}\otimes_{F}H)\otimes_{H}\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
, where
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
stands for the opposite algebra of
\mathcal{A}
. If we write
\mathcal{B}\simeq\mathrm{M}_{r}(\mathcal{D})
with
\mathcal{D}
a division algebra, then
\dim_{F}\mathcal{B}=r^{2}t_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle c(\mathcal{X})^{2}=\frac{\dim_{H}\mathcal{X}}{t_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}}=\frac{\dim_{F}\mathcal{B}\dim_{H}\mathcal{A}}{t_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}}=\frac{r^{2}t_{\mathcal{B}}^{2}t_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}}{t_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}},

where in the last equality we used that
\mathcal{A}
is a division central
H
-algebra, so that
\dim_{H}\mathcal{A}=t_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}
. Taking square roots we obtain that

(3.2)

\displaystyle c(\mathcal{X})=\frac{rt_{\mathcal{B}}t_{\mathcal{A}}}{t_{\mathcal{X}}}.

On the other hand, since
E
is a maximal subfield of
\mathcal{A}
we have that
[E\colon H]=t_{\mathcal{A}}
. Therefore,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dim_{F}\mathcal{A}&=[H\colon F]\dim_{H}\mathcal{A}=[H\colon F]t_{\mathcal{A}}^{2}\\
&=t_{\mathcal{A}}[E\colon H][H\colon F]=t_{\mathcal{A}}[E\colon F]=t_{\mathcal{A}}t_{\mathcal{B}}r,\end{split}

where in the last equality we used that
E
is a maximal subfield of
\mathcal{B}
, which implies that
[E\colon F]=rt_{\mathcal{B}}
. Now the condition
\dim_{F}\mathcal{A}\mid c(\mathcal{X})
, together with (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), implies that
t_{\mathcal{X}}=1
. This means that
0=[\mathcal{X}]=[\mathcal{B}\otimes_{F}H]-[\mathcal{A}]
, and the proposition follows.

∎

We introduce a terminology that will be used in subsequent sections. Recall that a simple abelian variety is called of the first kind if the center of its endomorphism algebra is totally real; that is, if its endomorphism algebra has Albert type I, II, or III in the classification of division algebras with a positive involution.

Definition 3.10
.

We say that an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type is
geometrically of the first kind
if the associated building block is of the first kind.

Remark 3.11
.

If
A
is geometrically of the first kind, then
n
is even. This can be checked on the building block
B
. If
B
has Albert type II or III, then this follows from part (ii) of Proposition
2.2
, since
t_{B}=2
divides
n
. If
B
has Albert type I, then by
[
Mum08
, §21, pg.202]
we have
[F:\mathbb{Q}]\mid\dim B
, and so
n=2\frac{\dim B}{[F:\mathbb{Q}]}
is indeed even.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
A/k
be an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type with associated building block
B/{\bar{k}}
. If
A
is of the first kind, then so is
B
. More precisely:

i)

If
A
has Albert type
\mathrm{I}
, then
B
has Albert type
\mathrm{I}
or
\mathrm{II}
.

ii)

If
A
has Albert type
\mathrm{II}
, then
B
has Albert type
\mathrm{II}
.

iii)

If
A
has Albert type
\mathrm{III}
, then
B
has Albert type
\mathrm{III}
.

Proof.

If
A
is of the first kind, then
H
is a totally real field, and from
F\subseteq H
we conclude that
B
is also of the first kind.

We claim that if
B
is of type III, then
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]
has nontrivial Brauer invariants at the infinite places. Indeed, since
H
is totally real, for any place
w\colon H\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we have

(B\otimes_{F}H)\otimes_{H,w}\mathbb{R}\simeq B\otimes_{F,v}\mathbb{R},

where
v=w_{|F}
. Since
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)]
has nontrivial Brauer invariants at the infinite places,
B\otimes_{F,v}\mathbb{R}
is a division algebra, which means that the Brauer invariants of
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)\otimes_{F}H]
are nontrivial at the infinite places. By Proposition
3.8
, the same happens for
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]
.

Assume that
A
is of type I. Then
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]
is trivial, and hence
B
is not of type III by the above claim.

Assume that
A
is of type II. If
B
were of type I, then
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]
would be trivial by Proposition
3.8
, which is a contradiction. Since
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]
has trivial Brauer invariants at the infinite places, by the claim
B
is not of type III either.

Assume that
A
is of type III. Suppose that
B
is of type I or II. Then
[\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)]=[\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)\otimes_{F}H]
has nontrivial Brauer invariants at the infinite places, which is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

In the case
i)
of Proposition
3.12
, there are examples of varieties in which
A
has Albert type I and
B
has Albert type II. For example, let
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{21})
and let
A/k
be the Jacobian of the curve

\displaystyle y^{2}=\left(x^{2}+\frac{7}{2}\right)\left(\frac{83}{30}x^{4}+14x^{3}-\frac{1519}{30}x^{2}+49x-\frac{1813}{120}\right).

By
[

]
we have that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
is the quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
of discriminant 6. Thus we see that

is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type and of type I, and the building block of

is of type II. It would be interesting to find similar examples for varieties genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type for

.

Remark 3.14
.

When
A
has Albert type IV,

can have any Albert type. For an example genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
-type, let
A/\mathbb{Q}
be the Jacobian of the genus 4 curve

C:y^{2}=x(x^{4}-1)(x^{4}+x^{2}+1).

In
[
CFLV23
, Theorem 4.1.1]
, it is proved that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}})
is the quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
of discriminant 2. Moreover, it is shown that
\mathbb{Q}(i)
is the minimal field where all endomorphisms of

are defined.
This implies

has Albert type III. However, over

we have

, which has Albert type IV. Indeed,

has the automorphism
(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{-1}{x},\frac{-y}{x^{5}}\right)
. To see that

has no more endomorphisms over

In Section

, we give a family of abelian fourfolds genuinely of

-type that can be specialized to find a variety

with Albert type IV such that

has Albert type I.

We give a weak converse to Proposition
3.12
.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
A/k
be an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type which is geometrically of the first kind. Then
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
is either a field or a quaternion algebra.

Proof.

Let
B/\bar{k}
be the building block associated to
A
. By hypothesis,
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
is a division algebra of Albert type
\mathrm{I}
,
\mathrm{II}
or
\mathrm{III}
, and so its Schur index is either 1 or 2. It follows from Proposition
3.8
that the Schur index of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
divides 2.
∎

4.
Inner twists

The notion of inner twist plays a key role in the theory of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-type abelian varieties. Following the circle of ideas, and many of the arguments, introduced in
[
Rib80
]
, we adapt this notion to the case of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

Let
A/k
be an abelian variety genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type. We keep the notations of the previous sections

(4.1)

F=Z(\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}}))\subseteq H=Z(\operatorname{End}^{0}(A))\subseteq E,

where
E
is a maximal subfield of both
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, and the first inclusion has been proved in Section
3
.

Let
K/k
denote the minimal extension over which
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. For
s\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)
, let
\alpha(s)\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})
be an invertible element satisfying (
3.1
).
Observe that
\alpha(s)
is not uniquely determined by (
3.1
), as any element of the form
\alpha(s)x
with
x\in F^{\times}
satisfies the same property. We fix from now on a choice of
\alpha(s)
for each
s\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)
.

Observe that
\alpha(s)
belongs to
H^{\times}
. Indeed, if
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
, then
\varphi={}^{s}\varphi=\alpha(s)\varphi\alpha(s)^{-1}
and therefore
\alpha(s)\in H^{\times}
. In fact, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

The field
F(\{\alpha(s)\}_{s\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)})
is equal to
H
.

Proof.

Put
D=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
,
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})
, and
H^{\prime}=F(\{\alpha(s)\}_{s\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)})
. It is clear that
H\subseteq C_{\mathcal{X}}(D)
. Since
D=C_{\mathcal{X}}(H^{\prime})
, this implies that
H\subseteq C_{\mathcal{X}}(C_{\mathcal{X}}(H^{\prime}))
. The inclusion
H\subseteq H^{\prime}
now follows from the Double Centralizer Theorem, which asserts that
C_{\mathcal{X}}(C_{\mathcal{X}}(H^{\prime}))=H^{\prime}
.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

For
\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(H/F)
, the
inner twist
of
A
relative to
\gamma
is the map

\chi_{\gamma}:\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)\rightarrow H^{\times}\,,\qquad\chi_{\gamma}(s)={}^{\gamma}\hskip-1.29167pt{\alpha}(s)/\alpha(s)\,.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
\delta,\gamma\in\mathrm{Aut}(H/F)
, we have the cocycle identity
\chi_{\gamma\delta}=\chi_{\gamma}\cdot{}^{\gamma}\chi_{\delta}
. Moreover,
\chi_{\gamma}
is a character.

Proof.

The first part of the lemma is obvious. For
s,t\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)
define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\xi(s,t)=\alpha(s)\cdot\alpha(t)\cdot\alpha(st)^{-1}.

Using (
3.1
) one sees that
\xi(s,t)
commutes with all
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})
and therefore
\xi(s,t)\in F^{\times}
. Hence

\displaystyle\xi(s,t)={}^{\gamma}\alpha(s)\cdot{}^{\gamma}\alpha(t)\cdot{}^{\gamma}\alpha(st)^{-1},

and dividing by (
4.2
) the second part of the lemma follows.
∎

We now restrict to the case where
A
is geometrically of the first kind, that is, the case in which
F
is totally real. Since
H
is the center of
D
, it is either a totally real field or a CM field, and hence complex conjugation uniquely defines an element
c\in\mathrm{Aut}(H/F)
.

Definition 4.4
.

If
A
is geometrically of the first kind, the
nebentype
of
A
is
\varepsilon=\chi_{c}^{-1}
.

Fix a polarization for
A
and denote by
′
the Rosati involution that it induces on
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and on
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
. It is well-known that the action of the Rosati involution on
H
coincides with that of
c
.

Proposition 4.5
.

If
F
is totally real, the field extension
H/F
is abelian.

Proof.

For ease of notation, let us denote
c
by
\overline{\cdot}
. We may then rewrite
\alpha(s)^{2}
as

\displaystyle\alpha(s)^{2}=\alpha(s)\cdot\overline{\alpha(s)}\cdot\varepsilon(s).

We have that
\alpha(s)\cdot\overline{\alpha(s)}
belongs to
F
. Indeed, applying the Rosati involution on (
3.1
), we see that

\displaystyle({}^{s}\varphi)^{\prime}=\overline{\alpha(s)}^{\,-1}\cdot\varphi^{\prime}\cdot\overline{\alpha(s)}

for all
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})
. Since the polarization of
A
is defined over
k
, we have that
({}^{s}\varphi)^{\prime}={}^{s}(\varphi^{\prime})
, and replacing
\varphi^{\prime}
by
\varphi
we obtain that

\displaystyle{}^{s}\varphi=\overline{\alpha(s)}^{-1}\cdot\varphi\cdot\overline{\alpha(s)}.

This equality together with (
3.1
) implies that
\alpha(s)\cdot\overline{\alpha(s)}
commutes with every
\varphi\in\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{K})
and therefore belongs to
F
.
Therefore
H=F(\{\alpha(s)\}_{s\in\mathrm{Gal}(K/k)})
is contained in the abelian extension of
F
obtained by adjoining to
F
the square roots of the elements
\alpha(s)\cdot\overline{\alpha(s)}
and
\zeta_{2m}
, where
m
is the order of
\varepsilon
.
∎

5.
An algebraic interlude

Throughout this section let
G
be a group,
\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}
a finite Galois field extension, and
V
a vector space over
\mathbb{E}
of finite dimension
n
. Let

\varrho:G\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V)\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{E})

be a semisimple representation satisfying that
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{E}[G]}(V)=\mathbb{E}
and that the characteristic polynomial of
\varrho(s)
has coefficients in
\mathbb{H}
for all
s\in G
. If
\mathbb{H}
has characteristic 0, then the later condition amounts to the requirement that
\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho(s))\in\mathbb{H}
for all
s\in G
.

In this section we give a cohomological characterization for the realizability of
\varrho
over
\mathbb{H}
, that is, the existence of a representation with coefficients in
\mathbb{H}
equivalent to
\varrho
. This approach goes back to
[
Sch04
]
(cf.
[
Nek12
]
), and our exposition closely follows that of
[
Rib92
, §6,§8]
, where the same formalism is applied to the problem of descent of the field of definition of abelian varieties.

Let
\Delta
denote the Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H})
. For
g\in\Delta
, let
{}^{g}\varrho
denote the representation sending
s\in G
to the matrix obtained by conjugating by
g
the entries of
\varrho(s)
. We write
{}^{g}V
to denote the vector space
\mathbb{E}^{n}
endowed with the action of
{}^{g}\varrho
.

By
[
Bou12
, Ch. 8,§21, n.1, Prop. 1]
, there exists
A_{g}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{E})
such that
the diagram

(5.1)

\textstyle{{}^{g}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{}^{g}\varrho(s)}
\scriptstyle{A_{g}}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varrho(s)}
\textstyle{{}^{g}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{A_{g}}
\textstyle{V}

commutes for every
s\in G
.

Lemma 5.1
.

For
g,h\in\Delta
, the product

(5.2)

c_{V}(g,h):=A_{g}\cdot{}^{g}A_{h}\cdot A_{gh}^{-1}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{E})

is a scalar matrix. The corresponding map

c_{V}:\Delta\times\Delta\rightarrow\mathbb{E}^{\times}

is a 2-cocycle, once
\mathbb{E}
is endowed with the natural action by
\Delta
.

Proof.

Given the hypothesis
\operatorname{End}_{G}(V)=\mathbb{E}
, to show the first part of the lemma it suffices to show that
c_{V}(g,h)
commutes with
\varrho(s)
for every
s\in G
. This follows from Diagram (
5.1
). Checking that
c
is a
2
-cocycle amounts to verifying the relation

{}^{f}c_{V}(h,g)\cdot c_{V}(f,h)^{-1}=c_{V}(f,hg)^{-1}\cdot c_{V}(fh,g).

for all
f,g,h\in\Delta
. Since
\operatorname{End}_{G}(V)=\mathbb{E}
, we may replace
{}^{f}c_{V}(h,g)
by
A_{f}{}^{f}c_{V}(h,g)A_{f}^{-1}
in the above relation. Its verification becomes then a straightforward calculation.
∎

We will denote by
\mathbb{E}^{c_{V}}[\Delta]
the crossed product algebra associated to the extension
\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}
and the
2
-cocycle
c_{V}
. This is an
\mathbb{H}
-algebra with underlying vector space the
\mathbb{E}
-linear span of the set of symbols
[g]
, for
g\in\Delta
, subject to the multiplication laws
[g]\cdot a={}^{g}a\cdot[g]
and
[g][h]=c_{V}(g,h)[gh]
for all
g,h\in\Delta
and
a\in\mathbb{E}
. We let
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}(V)
denote
V
regarded as an
\mathbb{H}[G]
-module.

Lemma 5.2
.

There is an algebra isomorphism

\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{H}[G]}(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}V)\simeq\mathbb{E}^{c_{V}}[\Delta].

Proof.

By the universal property of the restriction of scalars, we have

\mathcal{R}:=\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{H}[G]}(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}V)\simeq\prod_{g\in\Delta}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{E}[G]}({}^{g}V,V)

We note that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{E}[G]}({}^{h}V,V)
is a
1
-dimensional
\mathbb{E}
-vector space generated by
A_{h}
. Let
\lambda_{h}
be the element in
\mathcal{R}
corresponding to
A_{h}
. The set of the
\lambda_{h}
, for
h\in\Delta
, is a basis of
\mathcal{R}
as an
\mathbb{E}
vector space. Each
\lambda_{h}
acts on

(5.3)

\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{E}\simeq\prod_{g\in\Delta}{}^{g}V

by sending
{}^{gh}V
to
{}^{g}V
via
{}^{g}A_{h}
.
Then, the relation
\lambda_{g}\lambda_{h}=c_{V}(g,h)\lambda_{gh}
is an immediate consequence of (
5.2
). On the one hand, multiplication by
a
on
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}(V)\otimes\mathbb{E}
becomes multiplication by
{}^{h}a
on
{}^{h}V
under (
5.3
). Then the relation
\lambda_{h}\cdot a={}^{h}a\cdot\lambda_{h}
follows from the fact that
\lambda_{h}\cdot a
sends
{}^{h}V
to
V
via
A_{h}\cdot{}^{h}a
, the fact that
{}^{h}a\cdot\lambda_{h}
sends
{}^{h}V
to
V
via
{}^{h}a\cdot A_{h}
, and the equality
A_{h}\cdot{}^{h}a={}^{h}a\cdot A_{h}

∎

We will let
\gamma_{V}
denote the class of
c_{V}
in
H^{2}(\Delta,\mathbb{E}^{\times})
. By the previous lemma
\gamma_{V}
corresponds to the class of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{H}[G]}(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}V)
in
\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H})
under the canonical isomorphism
\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H})\simeq H^{2}(\Delta,\mathbb{E}^{\times})
. This class does not depend on the choice of the
A_{g}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

The class
\gamma_{V}
is trivial if and only if
\varrho
is realizable over
\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

If
\varrho
is realizable over
\mathbb{H}
, then we may take all the
A_{g}
to be trivial, and hence
\gamma_{V}
is trivial.

If
\gamma_{V}
is trivial, by Lemma
5.2
we have an isomorphism

(5.4)

\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}(V))\simeq\mathrm{M}_{[\mathbb{E}\colon\mathbb{H}]}(\mathbb{H}),

of
\mathbb{H}[G]
-modules. Therefore,
\operatorname{Res}_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H}}(V)\simeq V_{0}^{[\mathbb{E}\colon\mathbb{H}]}
for some
\mathbb{H}[G]
-module
V_{0}
. Since
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{E}[G]}(V)\simeq\mathbb{E}
, by tensoring (
5.4
) with
\mathbb{E}
we see that
V_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{H}}\mathbb{E}\simeq V
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Note that if
\mathbb{H}
is a finite field, then
\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{H})
is trivial, and hence
\varrho
is always realizable over
\mathbb{H}
. This is proven in the course of the proof of
[
Jon16
, Lem. 3.1]
.

6.
\lambda
-adic representations

Let
A
be an abelian variety, so that
A
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type,
E
denotes a maximal subfield of
D=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
, and
H
and
F
denote the centers of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
and
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, respectively. In this section we prove part
i)
of the
Main Theorem
.

Throughout this section we will assume that
E/H
is a Galois extension. In fact, the theory of central simple algebras shows that
E
can always be chosen so that
E/H
is cyclic.
For a rational prime
\ell
, let
V_{\ell}(A)
denote the
\ell
-adic rational Tate module of
A
, and denote by

\varrho_{\ell}\colon G_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{\ell}(A))

the
\ell
-adic representation attached to
A
.
That
A
is of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type means that there exists a number field
E
of degree
2\dim(A)/n
and a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra embedding
E\hookrightarrow\operatorname{End}^{0}(A)
.
For every prime
\mathfrak{L}
of
E
dividing
\ell
, let
E_{\mathfrak{L}}
denote the completion of
E
at
\mathfrak{L}
and let
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
denote the tensor product
V_{\ell}(A)\otimes_{E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}E_{\mathfrak{L}}
taken with respect to the
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-algebra map

\sigma_{\mathfrak{L}}\colon E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\ell}E_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}\rightarrow E_{\mathfrak{L}}\,.

Note that
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
is an
E_{\mathfrak{L}}
-vector space of dimension
n=2\dim(A)/[E:\mathbb{Q}]
. The
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-linear action of
G_{k}
on
V_{\ell}(A)
gives rise to a
E_{\mathfrak{L}}
-linear action on
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
. We will denote by

\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}\colon G_{k}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A))\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{n}(E_{\mathfrak{L}})

the corresponding
\mathfrak{L}
-adic Galois representation.

Let
S
be a finite set of primes of
k
containing the primes of bad reduction for
A
and those lying above
\ell
. Let
\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}
denote a Frobenius element at a prime
\mathfrak{p}
. The collection
(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}})_{\mathfrak{L}}
is a strictly compatible
E
-rational system of
\mathfrak{L}
-adic representations (see
[
Ser89
]
,
[
Rib76
, Chap. II]
). This is implied by the fact that the trace
\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}))
belongs to
E
for every prime
\mathfrak{p}\not\in S
and does not depend on the choice of
\mathfrak{L}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every finite extension
L/k
, we have

\operatorname{End}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}[G_{L}]}(V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A))=E_{\mathfrak{L}}.

In particular,
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
is absolutely irreducible as an
E_{\mathfrak{L}}[G_{L}]
-module.

Proof.

This follows from the maximality of
E
in
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, as shown in
[
Chi87
, p. 628]
and
[
Zar89
, §0.11.1]
. We give the proof for completeness.
By
[
Fal83
, Satz 4]
, we have
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[G_{L}]}(V_{\ell}(A))\simeq\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{L})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. Since
A
is genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-type, the field
E
is its own commutant in
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{L})
, and therefore

\operatorname{End}_{E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[G_{L}]}(V_{\ell}(A))=E\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\ell}E_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}.

The above isomorphism implies that the containment of
E_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}
in
\operatorname{End}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}[G_{L}]}(V_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}(A))
for each
\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\ell
must in fact be an equality.
∎

We will denote by
\lambda
the prime of
H
lying below
\mathfrak{L}
. Let
V_{\lambda}(A)
denote the tensor product
V_{\ell}(A)\otimes_{H\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}H_{\lambda}
taken with respect to the
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-algebra map

\sigma_{\lambda}\colon H\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda^{\prime}\mid\ell}H_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rightarrow H_{\lambda}\,.

Note that
V_{\lambda}(A)
is an
H_{\lambda}
-vector space of dimension
nt_{A}=2\dim(A)/[H:\mathbb{Q}]
, where
t_{A}=[E:H]
. One has the relation

(6.1)

V_{\lambda}(A)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\lambda}\operatorname{Res}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}/H_{\lambda}}V_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}(A)\,.

A variant of the first part of the following proposition in a more restrictive situation can be found in the course of the proof of
[
Rib92
, Thm. 5.3]
. Let
\Sigma_{A}
denote the set of primes
\lambda
in
H
at which
D
splits, that is, the set of primes
\lambda
of
H
such that
D\otimes_{H}H_{\lambda}
is trivial as an element of
\mathrm{Br}(H_{\lambda})
. Note that
\Sigma_{A}
misses only a finite number of primes of
H
.

Proposition 6.2
.

For every prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
k
not in
S
, the trace
\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}))
belongs to
H
. If
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
, the representation
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
is realizable over
H_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

For each prime
\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\lambda
, we fix an isomorphism
E_{\mathfrak{L}}\simeq E_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}
so that we may regard
V_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}(A)
as an
E_{\mathfrak{L}}
-vector space. Let
D_{\mathfrak{L}}\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(E_{\mathfrak{L}}/H_{\lambda})
denote the decomposition group of
\mathfrak{L}
in
\mathrm{Gal}(E/H)
. Faltings isomorphism shows that

(6.2)

D\otimes_{H}H_{\lambda}\simeq\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}(V_{\lambda}(A))\simeq\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}\big(\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\lambda}\operatorname{Res}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}/H_{\lambda}}V_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}(A)\big)\,.

By Lemma
6.1
, the
H_{\lambda}
-dimension of

\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}\big(\operatorname{Res}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}/H_{\lambda}}V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)\big)\simeq\bigoplus_{g\in D_{\mathfrak{L}}}\operatorname{Hom}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}[G_{k}]}(V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A),{}^{g}V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A))

is at most
n_{\lambda}^{2}
, where
n_{\lambda}
is the size of
D_{\mathfrak{L}}
.
Since the number of primes in
E
dividing
\lambda
is
[E:H]/n_{\lambda}
, the
H_{\lambda}
-dimension of the right hand-side of (
6.2
) is at most
[E:H]^{2}
,
with equality if and only if
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)\simeq{}^{g}V_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}(A)
as
E_{\mathfrak{L}}[G_{k}]
-modules for all
g\in D_{\mathfrak{L}}
and
\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\lambda
. Since the
H_{\lambda}
-dimension of the left hand-side of (
6.2
) is clearly
[E:H]^{2}
, we deduce that
\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}))=g(\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}})))
for all
g\in D_{\mathfrak{L}}
. Hence
\operatorname{Tr}(\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}))
belongs to
E\cap H_{\lambda}
for every prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
k
not in
S
. Since
\lambda
was arbitrary, this implies the first part of the proposition.

Let
\gamma_{D}
be the class of
D
in
\mathrm{Br}(E/H)\simeq H^{2}(\mathrm{Gal}(E/H),E^{\times})
.
The class in
\mathrm{Br}(E_{\mathfrak{L}}/H_{\lambda})
of the algebra on the right hand-side of (
6.2
) is
\gamma_{D,\lambda}
, the image of
\gamma_{D}
under the map
\mathrm{Br}(E/H)\rightarrow\mathrm{Br}(E_{\mathfrak{L}}/H_{\lambda})
. By Lemma
5.2
, the class
\gamma_{D,\lambda}
is the one governing the realizability of
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
over
H_{\lambda}
via Proposition
5.3
. The proposition then follows from the fact that for
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
the class
\gamma_{D,\lambda}
is trivial.
∎

For every prime
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
, we have
D\otimes_{H}H_{\lambda}\simeq\mathrm{M}_{t_{A}}(H_{\lambda})
. Hence the action of
D\otimes_{H}H_{\lambda}
on
V_{\lambda}(A)
implies the existence of an
H_{\lambda}
-vector space
W_{\lambda}(A)
of dimension
n
together with an isomorphism

V_{\lambda}(A)\simeq W_{\lambda}(A)^{\oplus t_{A}}

of
H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]
-modules. We denote by
\varrho_{\lambda}
the
\lambda
-adic representation encoding the action of
G_{k}
on
W_{\lambda}(A)
. The next lemma shows that
\varrho_{\lambda}
is an
H_{\lambda}
-realization of
\varrho_{\mathfrak{L}}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

For every
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
, there is an isomorphism

W_{\lambda}(A)\otimes_{H_{\lambda}}E_{\mathfrak{L}}\simeq V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)

of
E_{\mathfrak{L}}[G_{k}]
-modules. In particular,
W_{\lambda}(A)
is absolutely irreducible as an
H_{\lambda}[G_{K}]
-module for every finite extension
K/k
.

Proof.

Using (
6.1
) and the first part of Proposition
6.2
, we find

t_{A}\cdot\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}|W_{\lambda}(A))=\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}|V_{\lambda}(A))=\frac{t_{A}}{n_{\lambda}}\sum_{g\in D_{\mathfrak{L}}}g(\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}|V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)))=t_{A}\cdot\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{p}}|V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A))

for every prime
\mathfrak{p}\not\in S
. The lemma then follows from the Chebotaryov density theorem.
∎

The following result is the converse of the second part of Proposition
6.2
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose that for some
\mathfrak{L}
the representation
V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
descends to
H_{\lambda}
, in the sense that there exists a representation
W
over
H_{\lambda}
such that
W\otimes_{H_{\lambda}}E_{\mathfrak{L}}\simeq V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
. Then
\lambda
belongs to
\Sigma_{A}
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{End}_{E_{\mathfrak{L}}[G_{k}]}(V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A))=E_{\mathfrak{L}}
, the inclusion
H_{\lambda}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}(W)
must be an equality. As we have seen in the proof of Proposition
6.2
, for any
\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}\mid\lambda
we have that
V_{\mathfrak{L}^{\prime}}(A)\simeq V_{\mathfrak{L}}(A)
and from (
6.1
) we see that

\displaystyle V_{\lambda}(A)\simeq W^{\oplus t_{A}}.

By Faltings isomorphism (
6.2
) we have that

\displaystyle D\otimes_{H}H_{\lambda}\simeq\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}(W^{\oplus t_{A}})\simeq\mathrm{M}_{t_{A}}(\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}(W))\simeq\mathrm{M}_{t_{A}}(H_{\lambda}),

which means precisely that
\lambda\in\Sigma_{A}
.
∎

Suppose from now on that
A
is geometrically of the first kind. Let
B
denote the building block associated to
A
. By Proposition
3.8
, the degree
t_{A}=[E:H]
divides the Schur index
t_{B}
of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(B)
, which is at most
2
. In particular
E/H
is Galois. Let
F
denote the center of
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\bar{k}})
, and let
\mathfrak{l}
be a prime of
F
lying below
\lambda
. Let
V_{\mathfrak{l}}(A)
denote the tensor product
V_{\ell}(A)\otimes_{F\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}}F_{\mathfrak{l}}
taken with respect to the
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-algebra map

\sigma_{\mathfrak{l}}\colon F\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}\mid\ell}F_{\mathfrak{l}^{\prime}}\rightarrow F_{\mathfrak{l}}\,.

Note that
V_{\mathfrak{l}}(A)
is an
F_{\mathfrak{l}}
-vector space of dimension
2\dim(A)/[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.
By Proposition
4.5
, the extension
H/F
is abelian. In particular, for a prime
\lambda^{\prime}\mid\mathfrak{l}
, we may fix an
F_{\mathfrak{l}}
-isomorphism
H_{\lambda}\simeq H_{\lambda^{\prime}}
, which we take to be the identity when
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda
, and regard
V_{\lambda^{\prime}}(A)
and
W_{\lambda^{\prime}}(A)
as
H_{\lambda}
-vector spaces. Let
K/k
denote the minimal extension such that
\operatorname{End}(A_{\bar{k}})=\operatorname{End}(A_{K})
. Let
S_{K}
be the set of primes of
K
lying above primes in
S
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose that
A
is geometrically of the first kind. Let
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{A}
be primes lying over the same prime
\mathfrak{l}
of
F
. Then we have isomorphisms

V_{\lambda}(A)\simeq V_{\lambda^{\prime}}(A)\,,\qquad W_{\lambda}(A)\simeq W_{\lambda^{\prime}}(A)

of
H_{\lambda}[G_{K}]
-modules. Moreover, for every prime
\mathfrak{P}\not\in S_{K}
, the trace
\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Fr}_{\mathfrak{P}}\mid W_{\lambda}(A))
belongs to
F
.

Proof.

By construction of
W_{\lambda}(A)
, it will suffice to show
V_{\lambda}(A)\simeq V_{\lambda^{\prime}}(A)
.
In virtue of the first isomorphism of (
6.2
), we have

(6.3)

\dim_{H_{\lambda}}(\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}(V_{\lambda}(A)))=[E:H]^{2}.

In virtue of the second isomorphism of (
6.2
) together with Lemma
6.1
, we have

\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{k}]}(V_{\lambda}(A))=\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{K}]}(V_{\lambda}(A)).

The combination of the two previous equalities yields

(6.4)

\dim_{H_{\lambda}}(\operatorname{End}_{H_{\lambda}[G_{K}]}(V_{\lambda}(A)))=[E:H]^{2}.

By Faltings isomorphism we have that

8.
A family of abelian fourfolds genuinely of
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
-type

In this section we give a family of abelian fourfolds that can be used to produce examples of varieties of the second kind which are geometrically of the first kind. We will obtain the fourfolds by restriction of scalars of a family of Jacobians of genus 2 curves which are defined over a quadratic extension and are isogenous to their Galois conjugates.

Let
k_{0}
be a number field and let
k=k_{0}(\sqrt{\Delta})
be a quadratic extension. For
a,b,c\in k_{0}
, consider the polynomials
F_{j}=F_{j}(a,b,c)\in k[x]
defined by

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{4}{b}^{2}{\Delta}+\frac{1}{4}{a}^{2}+{c}{\sqrt{\Delta}}+\frac{1}{4}\right)x^{2}+\left({b}{\sqrt{\Delta}}+{a}\right)x+1,

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(-\frac{1}{2}{b}{\Delta}+\frac{1}{2}{a}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)x^{2}+{\sqrt{\Delta}}x,

\displaystyle F_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{4}{b}^{2}{\Delta}{\sqrt{\Delta}}-\frac{1}{4}{a}^{2}{\sqrt{\Delta}}-{c}{\Delta}+\frac{1}{4}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)x^{2}+\left(-{b}{\Delta}-{a}{\sqrt{\Delta}}\right)x-{\sqrt{\Delta}}.

Define the family of curves
C=C(a,b,c)
given by the equation

\displaystyle C\colon y^{2}=F_{1}(x)F_{2}(x)F_{3}(x).

From now on, we assume that the parameters
(a,b,c)
are chosen so that
C
is a genus two curve satisfying
\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\mathbb{Z}
. This assumption is crucial for the results of this section. For instance, the equality
\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Jac}(C))=\mathbb{Z}
is used in the proof of Proposition
8.1
, while the stronger condition
\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\mathbb{Z}
ensures that the family contains genuinely
\mathrm{GL}_{4}
-type abelian varieties (see Example
8.6
). For a specific choice of
(a,b,c)
, this assumption can be verified either using the criterion of Section
9
, or by applying the algorithms of
[
CMSV19
]
or
[
Lom19
]
.

In addition, we also assume that
\sqrt{-2}\not\in k
. In particular, the field

K=k(\sqrt{2})=k_{0}(\sqrt{\delta},\sqrt{-2})

is a biquadratic extension of
k_{0}
.

Let
q_{i,j}
be the coefficient of
x^{i}
in
F_{j}
, and let
\delta=\det\left(q_{i,j}\right)_{0\leq i,j\leq 2}
. A direct computation shows that

(8.1)

\displaystyle\delta=\Delta/2.

Define the polynomials

\displaystyle L_{1}=F_{2}^{\prime}\cdot F_{3}-F_{2}\cdot F_{3}^{\prime},\ L_{2}=F_{3}^{\prime}\cdot F_{1}-F_{3}\cdot F_{1}^{\prime},\ L_{3}=F_{1}^{\prime}\cdot F_{2}-F_{1}\cdot F_{2}^{\prime},

where the prime denotes derivative. Consider the curve
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}\colon v^{2}=\frac{1}{\delta}L_{1}(u)L_{2}(u)L_{3}(u).

By the theory of Richelot isogenies (see
[
CF96
, Chapter 9]
) there is a
(2,2)
-isogeny

(8.2)

\displaystyle\rho\colon\operatorname{Jac}(C)\rightarrow\operatorname{Jac}(\tilde{C}).

It is given by a correspondence
\Gamma\subset C\times\tilde{C}
, with explicit equations

\Gamma:\begin{cases}F_{1}(x)L_{1}(u)+F_{2}(x)L_{2}(u)=0\\
yv-F_{1}(x)L_{1}(u)(x-u)=0.\\
\end{cases}

Let
{s}
be the generator of
\mathrm{Gal}(k/k_{0})

(8.3)

\displaystyle{}^{s}F_{1}=-L_{1}/\Delta,\ {}^{s}F_{2}=-L_{2},\ {}^{s}F_{3}=-L_{3}.

In fact, the triples
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3})
were obtained precisely by imposing these conditions within the family of genus-
2
curves admitting a
(2,2)
-isogeny, although we do not claim that every such triple satisfying these conditions arises from our family. From (
8.1
) and (
8.3
), we obtain that

(8.4)

\displaystyle{{}^{s}C}=\tilde{C}_{-2}\text{ and }{{}^{s}\tilde{C}}=C_{-1/2}.

Here we are using the following notation for quadratic twists of hyperelliptic curves: if
H/k
is a curve with equation

and
\alpha\in k^{\times}

denotes the curve
\alpha y^{2}=f(x)

\operatorname{Jac}(H)_{\alpha}
denotes the

-twist of
\operatorname{Jac}(H)
, which we observe that can be identified with
\operatorname{Jac}(H_{\alpha})

Recall that we defined

. Fix isomorphisms

\displaystyle\phi\colon C_{K}\longrightarrow(C_{-1/2})_{K},\ \ \tilde{\phi}\colon\tilde{C}_{K}\longrightarrow(\tilde{C}_{-2})_{K}

given by

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=(x,\sqrt{-2}y)\ \text{ and}\ \tilde{\phi}(x,y)=(x,y/\sqrt{-2}).

We denote by the same name the isomorphisms induced on the Jacobians:

\displaystyle\phi\colon\operatorname{Jac}(C)_{K}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Jac}(C_{-1/2})_{K}\ \text{ and}\ \tilde{\phi}\colon\operatorname{Jac}(\tilde{C}_{K})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Jac}(\tilde{C}_{-2})_{K}.

Denote now by
{s}
the element of
\mathrm{Gal}(K/k_{0})
with fixed field

{s}_{|k}
is the generator of
\mathrm{Gal}(k/k_{0})

and, in particular, (

Define
\mu_{s}\colon\operatorname{Jac}(C)_{K}\rightarrow{{}^{s}\operatorname{Jac}}(C)_{K}
to be the isogeny given by the composition

\displaystyle\operatorname{Jac}(C)_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Jac}(\tilde{C})_{K}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\phi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Jac}(\tilde{C}_{-2})_{K}=\operatorname{Jac}({{}^{s}C})_{K}.

Proposition 8.1
.

We have that
{{}^{s}\mu_{s}}\circ\mu_{s}=-2
.

Proof.

The isogeny
\rho
is given by the correspondence
\Gamma\subset C\times\tilde{C}
. Hence,
{}^{s}\rho
is given by the correspondence

{{}^{s}\Gamma}:\begin{cases}{}^{s}L_{1}(x){}^{s}F_{1}(u)+{}^{s}L_{2}(x){}^{s}F_{2}(u)=0\\
yv-{}^{s}L_{1}(x){}^{s}F_{1}(u)(x-u)=0.\\

From (
8.3

and

(8.6)

\displaystyle F_{1}=-{}^{s}L_{1}/\Delta,\ F_{2}=-{}^{s}L_{2},\ F_{3}=-{}^{s}L_{3}.

Applying (
8.3
) and (
8.6
), we see that
{}^{s}\rho
is given by the correspondence

{{}^{s}\Gamma}:\begin{cases}L_{1}(x)F_{1}(u)+L_{2}(x)F_{2}(u)=0\\
yv-L_{1}(x)F_{1}(u)(x-u)=0.\\
\end{cases}

Since
{}^{s}\Gamma\subset{}^{s}C\times{}^{s}\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}_{-2}\times C_{-1/2}
, we can apply
\tilde{\phi}^{-1}\times\phi^{-1}
to obtain a correspondence in
\tilde{C}\times C

(-\tilde{\phi}^{-1})\times\phi^{-1}

stands for the composition of

(8.7)

((-\tilde{\phi}^{-1})\times\phi^{-1})({{}^{s}\Gamma}):\begin{cases}L_{1}(x)F_{1}(u)+L_{2}(x)F_{2}(u)=0\\
yv-L_{1}(x)F_{1}(u)(u-x)=0,\\
\end{cases}

from which we see that

(8.8)

\displaystyle((-\tilde{\phi}^{-1})\times\phi^{-1})({{}^{s}\Gamma})=\Gamma^{T},

where

Finally, we compute that

\displaystyle{{}^{s}\mu_{s}}\circ\mu_{s}

\displaystyle={{}^{s}\tilde{\phi}}\circ{{}^{s}\rho}\circ\tilde{\phi}\circ\rho

\displaystyle=-{{}^{s}\tilde{\phi}}\circ\phi\circ\rho^{\dagger}\circ\tilde{\phi}^{-1}\circ\tilde{\phi}\circ\rho

\displaystyle=-{{}^{s}\tilde{\phi}}\circ\phi\circ\rho^{\dagger}\circ\rho=-2,

where in the last step we have used that
{{}^{s}\tilde{\phi}}\circ\phi(x,y)=(x,y)
; this follows from the explicit expressions (
8.5
) and the fact that
{s}
fixes
\sqrt{-2}
.
∎

\operatorname{Jac}(C)

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
\alpha\in k
be an element such that

{}^{s}\operatorname{Jac}(C_{\alpha})\sim\operatorname{Jac}(C_{\alpha})

Proof.

Observe that
{{}^{s}\operatorname{Jac}}(C_{\alpha})

By hypothesis

To finish the proof it is enough to show that

∎

Remark 8.3
.

Since
k=k_{0}(\sqrt{\Delta})

\operatorname{Nm}_{k/k_{0}}(\alpha)\in-2\Delta\cdot k_{0}^{\times,2}

Let
\alpha

Up to multiplication by elements in

+2,&\text{ if }\operatorname{Nm}_{k/k_{0}}(\alpha)\in-2\Delta\cdot k_{0}^{\times,2}.\end{cases}

We have that

9.
A criterion for the triviality of endomorphisms

Let
k
be a number field and let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
k
and of dimension
g\geq 1
. Throughout this section
\mathfrak{p}
denotes a nonzero prime ideal of the ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{k}
of
k
which is of good reduction for
A
. Let
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
denote the reduction of
A
modulo
\mathfrak{p}
, an abelian variety defined over the residue field
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\mathcal{O}_{k}
at
\mathfrak{p}
. We will write
A_{\mathfrak{p},n}
to denote the base change of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
to the degree
n
extension of
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Let

P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)=\prod_{i=1}^{2g}(1-\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}T)

denote the reciprocal of the characteristic polynomial Frobenius of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then

P(A_{\mathfrak{p},n},T)=\prod_{i=1}^{2g}(1-\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}^{n}T).

We will say that
\mathfrak{p}
is stably irreducible for
A
if
P(A_{\mathfrak{p},n},T)
is irreducible for all
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 9.1
.

Suppose that
P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)
is irreducible. Then
\mathfrak{p}
is stably irreducible for
A
if and only if for all
i\not=j
the quotient
\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}/\alpha_{j,\mathfrak{p}}
is not a root of unity.

Proof.

Since
P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)
is irreducible, Galois theory shows that the following are equivalent for
P(A_{\mathfrak{p},n},T)
:

i)

It reduces.

ii)

It has a repeated irreducible factor.

iii)

It is a proper perfect power.

Hence
P(A_{\mathfrak{p},n},T)
reduces if and only if there exist
i\not=j
such that the quotient
\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}/\alpha_{j,\mathfrak{p}}
is a root of unity.
∎

We next give a more computational version of the previous lemma. Let
\Phi_{t}(T)
denote the
t
-th cyclotomic polynomial. Define the polynomial

f(T)=\operatorname{Res}_{z}(z^{2g}P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},1/z),z^{2g}P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T/z))\in\mathbb{Z}[T].

Let
\phi
denote Euler’s totient function.

Lemma 9.2
.

Suppose that
P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)
is irreducible. Then
\mathfrak{p}
is stably irreducible for
A
if and only if
\Phi_{t}(T)
does not divide
f(T)
for any
t\geq 2
such that
\phi(t)\leq 4g^{2}
.

Proof.

Since the reciprocal roots of
P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)
come in complex conjugate pairs
\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}},\overline{\alpha}_{i,\mathfrak{p}}
with product equal to
\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{p})
, we observe that

f(T)=\prod_{i,j=1}^{2g}\left(\alpha_{j,\mathfrak{p}}T-\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak{p})^{2g^{2}}\prod_{i,j=1}^{2g}\left(T-\frac{\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}}{\alpha_{j,\mathfrak{p}}}\right).

For any
i\not=j
, the quotient
\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}/\alpha_{j,\mathfrak{p}}
is not 1, since otherwise
P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)
would be reducible. Hence, for
i\not=j
, the quotient
\alpha_{i,\mathfrak{p}}/\alpha_{j,\mathfrak{p}}
is a root of unity if and only if it has finite order
t\geq 2
satisfying that
\phi(t)\leq 4g^{2}
.
∎

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be stably irreducible, and let
K^{\mathfrak{p},n}
denote the field
\mathbb{Q}[T]/(P(A_{\mathfrak{p},n},T))
. If
\alpha
is a reciprocal root of
P(A_{\mathfrak{p}},T)
, then
K^{\mathfrak{p},n}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha^{n})
. Hence
K^{\mathfrak{p},n}\subseteq K^{\mathfrak{p},1}
. We will simply write
K^{\mathfrak{p}}
to denote
K^{\mathfrak{p},1}
.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
k
. Suppose that there exist two distinct primes
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
that are stably irreducible for
A
and such that
K^{\mathfrak{p}}
and
K^{\mathfrak{q}}
are linearly disjoint. Then
\operatorname{End}(A_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

There exists a finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
such that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}})=\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{k^{\prime}})
. Let
n
be an integer divisible both by the residue degree of
\mathfrak{p}
and the residue degree of
\mathfrak{q}
in
k^{\prime}/k
. Then we have inclusions

(9.1)

\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{k^{\prime}})\subseteq\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\mathfrak{p},n})\text{ and }\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{k^{\prime}})\subseteq\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\mathfrak{q},n}).

By
[
Tat66
, Thm. 2, (c)]
, the stably irreducibility of
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
implies that

(9.2)

\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\mathfrak{p},n})\simeq K^{\mathfrak{p},n}\text{ and }\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{\mathfrak{q},n})\simeq K^{\mathfrak{q},n}.

Since
K^{\mathfrak{p},n}\subseteq K^{\mathfrak{p}}
,
K^{\mathfrak{q},n}\subseteq K^{\mathfrak{q}}
, and
K^{\mathfrak{p}}
and
K^{\mathfrak{q}}
are linearly disjoint, we deduce that
\operatorname{End}^{0}(A_{{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}})=\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

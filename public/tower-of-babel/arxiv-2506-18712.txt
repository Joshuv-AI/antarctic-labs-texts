Lambert series and double Lambert series
T. Amdeberhan, G. E. Andrews, C. Ballantine

Abstract
. We consider relationships between classical Lambert series, multiple Lambert series and classical q q -series of the Rogers-Ramanujan type. We conclude with a contemplation on the Andrews-Dixit-Schultz-Yee conjecture.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11P81; Secondary 05A17.

1.
introduction

J.H. Lambert
[
10
]
showed in 1771 that the generating function for the divisor function is given by

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

More generally, we now define a Lambert series to be the series given by

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a_{n}q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

In particular, when
a_{n}=n^{2k-1}
, the resulting functions are either modular forms or (when
k=1
) a quasimodular form. A nice account of much that is known about Lambert series identities may be found in Schmidt
[
12
]
.

While we shall consider classical Lambert series in this paper, we will also be extending our work to series of the form

\sum_{n\geq 1}R_{n}(q^{n},q),

where
R_{n}(x,y)
is a rational function of
x
and
y
. We call such series
general Lambert series
. We note that our general Lambert series contain the generalized Lambert series considered by Schmidt
[
12
]
and others.

In addition, we shall also explore series of the type

\sum_{n\geq 1}S_{n,m}(q^{n},q^{m},q),

where
S_{n,m}(x,y,z)
is a rational function of
x,y
and
z
. We shall call such series
double Lambert series
.

In Section 2, we gather background material. In Section 3, we prove identities connecting general and double Lambert series. In Section 4, we consider a connection of Lambert series to Rogers-Ramanujan type identities. It was previously shown
[
5
, eq’n (4.7)]
that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(q;q)_{n}(q,q)_{n-1}q^{n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{2n}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\frac{n}{3})q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}

where
(\frac{n}{p})
is the Legendre symbol and

(a;q)_{n}:=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j}).

It is natural to ask if there are other such identities. To our surprise, there is a very similar result in which
(\frac{n}{3})
is replaced by
(\frac{n}{5})
:

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(q;q)_{n}(q,q)_{n-1}(-1)^{n-1}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{(q;q)_{2n}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\frac{n}{5})q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

Section 5 concludes with a careful albeit inconclusive consideration of the Andrews-Dixit-Schultz-Yee (ADSY) conjecture
[
2
]
that

\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2mn+n}}{(1+q^{n})(1-q^{2m-1})}

is an odd function of
q
. It would be a major step forward in the study of multiple Lambert series if one could develop methods that would not only prove this conjecture but also place it in the context that would reveal and prove similar results. While we have been unsuccessful in proving the ADSY conjecture, we believe our exploration points in the right direction.

2.
Background and notation

A
partition
of
n\in\mathbb{N}
is a finite non-increasing sequence of positive integers that add up to
n
. We use the notation
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
, with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{\ell}
and

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{\ell}=n
. We refer to the numbers
\lambda_{i}
as the
parts
of
\lambda
. The number of parts in
\lambda
is denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
and it is called the
length
of
\lambda
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}
the set of all partitions. For convenience, we set
\lambda_{j}=0
if
j>\ell(\lambda)
.

Graphically, a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
can be represented by a Young diagram, a left-justified array of boxes with
\lambda_{i}
boxes in the
i
th row. For example, the Young diagram of
\lambda=(4,4,2,2,1)
is

The conjugate of a partition
\lambda
, denoted by
\lambda^{\prime}
, is the partition whose Young diagram is obtained from the Young diagram of
\lambda
by reflection across the diagonal. For example, the conjugate of
\lambda=(4,4,2,2,1)
is
\lambda^{\prime}=(5,4,2,2)
.

We also use the frequency notation for partitions. In particular, by
(k^{j})
we mean the partition of length
j
with all parts equal to
k
.

The Frobenius symbol, introduced in 1900 by Frobenius
[
7
]
, is defined as follows. Given a partition
\lambda
, let
r
be the size of the largest square that fits inside the Young diagram of
\lambda
. This square is referred to as the Durfee square of
\lambda
. For example, the Durfee Square of
\lambda=(4,4,2,2,1)
has size
2
. For
j
such that
1\leq j\leq r
let
a_{j}
, respectively
b_{j}
, be the number of boxes directly to the right, respectively below, the box in position
(j,j)
in the Young diagram of
\lambda
. The Frobenius symbol of
\lambda
is denoted by

(a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\,|\,b_{1},\dots,b_{r}).

For example, the Frobenius symbol of
\lambda=(4,4,2,2,1)
is
(3,2\,|\,4,2)
.

The Pochhammer symbol is defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{for $n=0$,}\\
(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}),&\text{for $n>0$;}\end{cases}

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(a;q)_{n}.

We assume
q
is a complex number with
|q|<1
so that all infinite series and products converge absolutely.

3.
Three Lambert series

We begin by considering the following series

f_{1}(q):=\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{k(k-1)q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\frac{2kq^{2k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}\right).

(1)

The next lemma gives the Fourier expansion of
f_{1}(q)
. Throughout the article, whenever we refer to divisors of a positive integer, we only consider the positive divisors. The algebraic proof of the lemma is straightforward. We give a combinatorial proof using integer partitions.

Lemma 3.1
.

f_{1}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{d|n}(d^{2}+d-2n)\right)q^{n}.

Combinatorial proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}(n)
be the set of partitions of
n
with all parts equal (i.e., rectangular partitions) and let
\mathcal{B}^{*}(n)
be the the set of partitions of
n
with parts in two colors
1
and
2
, all of the same size, and at least one part of each color. The order of colored positive integers is
1_{1}<1_{2}<2_{1}<2_{2}<\cdots
.

Let

a(n):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(n)}\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-1)

and

b(n):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}^{*}(n)}2\lambda_{1}.

The function
f_{1}(q)
defined by (
1
) is the generating function for the sequence
a(n)-b(n)
.

To each partition
\lambda=(k^{j})\in\mathcal{B}(n)
we associate
j-1
partitions in
\mathcal{B}^{*}(n)
. For each
1\leq i\leq j-1
we color the first
i
parts of
\lambda
in color
2
and the remaining
j-i
parts in color
1
to obtain a partition
\lambda^{(i)}\in\mathcal{B}^{*}
. Then
\lambda
contributes
k(k-1)=k^{2}-k
to
a(n)
and the partitions
\lambda^{(i)}
,
1\leq i\leq j-1
contribute
2k(j-1)=2(kj-k)=2n-2k
to
b(n)
. Hence, each divisor
k
of
n
contributes
k^{2}-k-2n+2k=k^{2}+k-2n
to
a(n)-b(n)
.
∎

Next, we introduce two new
q
-series that turn out to be equal to
f_{1}(q)
. Let

\displaystyle f_{2}(q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\left(\frac{(k+\ell)\,q^{k+\ell+k\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}\right)

and

\displaystyle f_{3}(q)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\left(\frac{2k\,q^{k+\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}\right).

We first prove, both analytically and combinatorially, that the two new series are equal to each other.

Theorem 3.2
.

We have
f_{2}(q)=f_{3}(q)
.

Algebraic proof.

Rearranging and using properties of geometric series, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{3}(q)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell>k\geq 1}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}2kq^{ki+\ell j}=\sum_{m,k\geq 1}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}2kq^{ki+kj+mj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell\geq 1}2kq^{k\ell+ki+\ell j}=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell\geq 1}(k+\ell)q^{k\ell+ki+\ell j}=f_{2}(q).

The penultimate equality is valid due to the symmetry in
k
and
\ell
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Combinatorial proof.

First we interpret
f_{2}(q)
and
f_{3}(q)
as partition generating functions.

Let
\mathcal{C}(n)
be the set of partitions
\lambda
of
n
with exactly two different part sizes. For example

\mathcal{C}(6)=\{(5,1),(4,2),(4,1,1),(3,1,1,1),(2,2,1,1),(2,1,1,1,1)\}.

Let
c(n)
be the sum of smallest parts in all partitions in
\mathcal{C}(n)
(without multiplicity) and let
d(n)
be the sum of the multiplicities of the largest part in all partitions in
\mathcal{C}(n)
. In the above example,
c(6)=1+2+1+1+1+1=7
and
d(6)=1+1+1+1+2+1=7
.

The function

f_{2}(q)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\left((k+\ell)\,q^{kl}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\frac{q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}\right)

is the generating function for the sequence
c(n)+d(n)

and the function

f_{3}(q)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\left(2k\,\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\frac{q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}\right)

is the generating function for the sequence
2c(n)
.

Since the conjugate of any partition in
\mathcal{C}(n)
is in
\mathcal{C}(n)
, and by conjugation
c(n)=d(n)
, it follows that
f_{2}(n)=f_{3}(n)
.
∎

Next, we prove that the new
q
-series
f_{2}(q)
and
f_{3}(q)
are equal to
f_{1}(q)
.
First we prove a helpful lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{kq^{\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{k^{2}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{kq^{k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}.

(2)

Proof.

It follows from
[
1
, equation (2.1)]
that the right hand side of (
2
) equals

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}\geq 1,{x_{3}}\geq 0\end{subarray}}q^{x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}{x_{3}}+{x_{3}}x_{4}+x_{4}x_{5}}.

(3)

We show that the
q
-series (
3
) is also equal to the left hand side of (
2
).

From
[
1
, page 123]
),

\displaystyle{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},x_{2},x_{4},x_{5}\geq 1,\\
{x_{3}}\geq 0\end{subarray}}q^{x_{1}x_{2}+x_{2}{x_{3}}+{x_{3}}x_{4}+x_{4}x_{5}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2m+2n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})}+\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}(q^{m+n}-1)+q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})}+\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{kq^{\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{mq^{m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{m+n})}=\sum_{i,j,m,n\geq 1}mq^{(i+j-1)m+ni}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\frac{q^{2i+j-1}}{(1-q^{i})(1-q^{i+j-1})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{m+n})^{2}}+\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{q^{2i}}{(1-q^{i})^{3}}.

We compute the difference

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})}-\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{m+n})^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{m})(1-q^{m+n})}\left\{\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}-\frac{q^{m}}{1-q^{m+n}}\right\}=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})^{2}}.

The task now reduces to proving the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})^{2}}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{q^{2i}}{(1-q^{i})^{3}}.

(4)

Using Bell’s identity (see
[
1
, page 117]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{q^{m+n}}{(1-q^{n})(1-q^{m+n})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}\sum_{k>n}\frac{q^{k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{k}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{k}}{(1-q^{k})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{m^{2}q^{m}}{1-q^{m}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{k}}{(1-q^{k})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{m^{2}q^{m}}{1-q^{m}}-\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(m^{2}+m)q^{m}}{2(1-q^{m})}

(5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(m^{2}-m)q^{m}}{2(1-q^{m})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{q^{2i}}{(1-q^{i})^{3}}.

(6)

We used the elementary fact
\frac{1}{(1-x)^{3}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{m(m-1)}{2}x^{m-2}
to obtain (
5
) and also (applied in reverse) to arrive to (
6
).
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

We have
f_{1}(q)=f_{3}(q)
.

Proof.

Using algebraic manipulations, we obtain

\displaystyle f_{3}(q)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{2kq^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{2kq^{\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}2k+{\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{2kq^{\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(\ell^{2}-\ell)q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}+{\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2k^{2}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2kq^{k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(\ell^{2}-\ell)q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2k^{2}q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2kq^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2kq^{2k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{(\ell^{2}-\ell)q^{\ell}}{1-q^{\ell}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{2kq^{2k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}=f_{1}(q).

The third equality above follows from Lemma
3.3
.
∎

We end this section with an alternative proof of Theorem
3.4
. For this purpose, we first define an operator and prove two lemmas.

Define the
grading-type
operator

\mathcal{L}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}(k\partial_{\alpha_{k}}^{2}+k^{2}\partial_{\alpha_{k}}+2k\partial_{\alpha_{k}}\sum_{\ell>k}\partial_{\alpha_{\ell}}).

The goal is to apply the operator
\mathcal{L}
on the
“generalized”
\eta
-function

\frac{1}{\eta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}=\prod_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-e^{-\alpha_{j}}},\qquad\text{where $\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots)$}.

Lemma 3.5
.

The following identity holds.

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{\eta({\boldsymbol{\alpha}})}\right)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=j,\\
j\geq 1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}e^{-|\lambda|}\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k}-2k+1).

Proof.

We begin by expressing
\eta
as a counting function:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j\geq 1}\sum_{i\geq 0}e^{-i\alpha_{j}}=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2},\dots\geq 0}e^{-\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}m_{j}}.

Using the identification
(m_{1},m_{2},\dots)\longleftrightarrow\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
with
m_{j}=\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}e^{-\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1})}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}e^{-\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{j},\lambda_{j}},

where
\beta_{k}=\alpha_{k}-\alpha_{k-1}
(assuming
\alpha_{0}=0
). This results in
\partial_{\alpha_{k}}=\partial_{\beta_{k}}-\partial_{\beta_{k+1}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\left[k(\partial_{\beta_{k}}-\partial_{\beta_{k+1}})^{2}+k^{2}(\partial_{\beta_{k}}-\partial_{\beta_{k+1}})+2k(\partial_{\beta_{k}}-\partial_{\beta_{k+1}})\sum_{\ell>k}(\partial_{\beta_{\ell}}-\partial_{\beta_{\ell+1}})\right]e^{-\sum\beta_{j}\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}\left[\partial_{\beta_{k}}^{2}+(2k-1)\partial_{\beta_{k}}\right]\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}e^{-\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{j}\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}[(-\lambda_{k})(-\lambda_{k}+2k-1)]\right)e^{-\sum_{j\geq 1}\beta_{j}\lambda_{j}}.

Consequently, evaluating the last result at
\beta_{j}=1
, for all
j
, produces the desired claim.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

If
q=e^{-1}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}\right)\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=j,\\
j\geq 1\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{1}{\eta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=j,\\
j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{k(k-1)q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\frac{2kq^{2k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{2kq^{k+\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}\right).

Proof.

We rely on the following elementary facts: if we let

f_{k}:=(1-e^{-\alpha_{k}})^{-1}
, then

\partial_{\alpha_{k}}f_{k}=f_{k}(1-f_{k})\text{ \ \ and \ \ }\partial_{\alpha_{k}}^{2}f_{k}=f_{k}(1-f_{k})(1-2f_{k}).

(7)

Although we do not require this at present, it is true, for all
t\geq 1
, that

\partial_{\alpha_{k}}^{t}f_{k}=f_{k}(1-f_{k})\sum_{j=1}^{t}(-1)^{j-1}j!\,S(t,j)\cdot f_{k}^{j-1},

where
S(t,k)
are the
Stirling numbers of the second kind
.

In particular, from (
7
), we obtain that

\partial_{\alpha_{k}}(\eta^{-1})=(1-f_{k})\eta^{-1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{\alpha_{k}}^{2}(\eta^{-1})=(1-f_{k})(1-2f_{k})\eta^{-1}.

To finish the task, substitute these derivatives into

\sum_{k}\left[k\partial_{\alpha_{k}}^{2}+k^{2}\partial_{\alpha_{k}}+2k\partial_{\alpha_{k}}\sum_{\ell>k}\partial_{\alpha_{\ell}}\right]\eta^{-1}

and then carry out the evaluations at
\alpha_{j}=j
with
q=e^{-1}
.
∎

Now we can give an alternative proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Second proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We show that
f_{1}(q)-f_{3}(q)=0
. Let

\kappa_{\lambda}:=\sum_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k}-2k+1).

From Lemmas
3.5
and
3.6
, it follows that

\left.\frac{1}{\eta(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}\right|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha_{j}=j\\
j\geq 1\end{subarray}}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{k(k-1)q^{k}}{1-q^{k}}-\frac{2kq^{2k}}{(1-q^{k})^{2}}-\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{\ell>k}\frac{2kq^{k+\ell}}{(1-q^{k})(1-q^{\ell})}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{n}\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\kappa_{\lambda}.

By using the Frobenius symbol
\lambda=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\,|\,n_{1},\dots,n_{r})
, we obtain

\kappa_{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}[m_{i}(m_{i}+1)-n_{i}(n_{i}+1)].

Clearly
\kappa_{\lambda}=-\kappa_{\lambda^{\prime}}
where
\lambda^{\prime}
is the conjugate partition of

\lambda
. This forces the vanishing of
\sum q^{n}\sum\kappa_{\lambda}
, which completes the proof.
∎

4.
A related identity

While studying the three Lambert series of section
3
and the relationship between them, we have discovered the identity in Theorem
4.1
below.
We denote the Kronecker symbol by
(\frac{p}{\cdot})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

We have the identity

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}(q;q)_{n}(q;q)_{n-1}}{(q;q)_{2n}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(\frac{5}{n})\,q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}.

We first prove a few useful lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have the identity

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{j-1}q^{\binom{n-j}{2}}(q;q)_{n+j}(q;q)_{j-1}}{(q;q)_{n-j}(q;q)_{2j}}

\displaystyle=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=-j+1}^{j}q^{j^{2}-i^{2}}+\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=-j}^{j}q^{j^{2}+j-i^{2}-i}\right\}.

(8)

Proof.

We invoke the Wilf-Zeilberger (WZ) method
[
14
]
, for the pair

\displaystyle F(n,j):

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{j-1}q^{\binom{n-j}{2}}(q;q)_{n+j}(q;q)_{j-1}}{(q;)_{n-j}(q;q)_{2j}}

\displaystyle G(n,j):

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{j-1}q^{\binom{n+3-j}{2}}(q;q)_{n+j+1}(q;q)_{j}(1-q^{2n+4})}{(q;q)_{n+2-j}(q;q)_{2j}}.

Zeilberger’s algorithm furnishes the recurrence

F(n+3,j)-q^{n+2}F(n+2,j)-q^{3n+5}F(n+1,j)+q^{4n+5}F(n,j)=G(n,j)-G(n,j-1).

Summing the last equation over integers
1\leq j\leq n+3
we arrive at

\displaystyle f(n+3)-q^{n+2}f(n+2)-q^{3n+5}f(n+1)+q^{4n+5}f(n)=q^{\binom{n+3}{2}}(1+q^{n+2}),

(9)

where
f(n):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F(n,j)
. On the other hand, if we let

g(n):=q^{\binom{n}{2}}\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=-j+1}^{j}q^{j^{2}-i^{2}}+\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{i=-j}^{j}q^{j^{2}+j-i^{2}-i}\right\}

then a routine calculation verifies that
g(n)
satisfies the recurrence (
9
). The proof follows immediately after checking the three initial conditions. We omit the details.
∎

With the notation

Lemma 4.4
.

We have the identity

\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{j=-n+1}^{n}q^{5n^{2}-j^{2}}+\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{j=-n}^{n}q^{5n^{2}-j^{2}+5n-j+1}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{d|m}\left(\frac{5}{d}\right)q^{m}.

Proof.

Consider the real quadratic field

\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}(\tau)}(s)=\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathbb{Z}[\tau]}\frac{1}{[\mathbb{Z}[\tau]:\mathfrak{a}]^{s}}=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{c(m)}{m^{s}}

(11)

where

Recall the decomposition

[

Now, the exponents of

Finally, the ranges for the inner sums are directly justified by

. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.1

5.
A conjecture of Andrews, Dixit, Schultz, and Yee and related results

The Lambert series
f_{2}(q)
and

of Section

are reminiscent of the expression in the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1
.

[

, p. 24]
The following is an odd function of

Y(q):=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{(-q)^{2mn+m}}{(1+q^{n})(1-q^{2m-1})}.

While we are not able to prove the conjecture, we offer some ideas that are hopefully pointing in the right direction.

Proposition 5.2
.

Define
\widetilde{Y}(q):=\frac{Y(q)}{-q}=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}a(m,n)
, where

a(m,n):=\frac{(-1)^{m-1}q^{2mn+m-1}}{(1+q^{n})(1-q^{2m-1})}.

The coefficients of the
q

are all either
-1
or
0
or
1
.

Proof.

Fix positive integers
m
and
n
. Consider

b(m,n):=\frac{1}{(1+q^{n})(1-q^{2m-1})}.

Let
u:=\gcd(n,2m-1)
and write
n=ua,2m-1=ub
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
. We make the substitution
z=q^{u}
. We want to show that

f(a,b):=\frac{1-z^{a}}{(1-z^{2a})(1-z^{b})}=(1-z^{a})\sum_{j,k\geq 0}z^{2ak+jb}=(1-z^{a})\sum_{N\geq 0}c_{N}z^{N}

has only coefficients
0,1
, or
-1
. It is well-known (via the
Frobenius coin exchange problem
) that the largest
N
for which
c_{N}=0
is
2ab-2a-b
while each coefficient is given by
c_{N}=\lfloor\frac{N}{2ab}\rfloor
or
c_{N}=\lfloor\frac{N}{2ab}\rfloor+1
.

Since
a<2ab
, it follows that the coefficients of
z^{N}
and
z^{N+a}
differ at most by
1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

We have

\widetilde{Y}(q)=\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}q^{2mn+m-1}}{(1+q^{n})(1-q^{2m-1})}=\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{q^{k-1}}{1+q^{2k-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\frac{q^{n}}{1+q^{n}}.

Proof.

We consider the following direct calculation

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{m-1}q^{2mn+m-1}}{(1+q^{n})(1-q^{2m-1})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m,n\geq 1}\frac{1}{1+q^{n}}\sum_{j\geq 0}q^{-j-1}(-1)^{m}q^{(2n+2j+1)m}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{1+q^{n}}\sum_{j\geq 0}q^{-j-1}\frac{-q^{2n+2j+1}}{1+q^{2n+2j+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{1+q^{n}}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{q^{2n+j}}{1+q^{2n+2j+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,i\geq 1}\frac{q^{2n+i-1}}{(1+q^{n})(1+q^{2n+2i-1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k\geq n+1}\frac{q^{n+k-1}}{(1+q^{n})(1+q^{2k-1})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{q^{k-1}}{1+q^{2k-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{k-1}\frac{q^{n}}{1+q^{n}}.

∎

Proposition 5.11
.

For a positive integer

, we have that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2mn}}{1-q^{n}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}q^{(2m-1)n}q^{\binom{n+1}{2}}}{(1-q^{n})(q^{2m};q^{2m})_{n}}.

Proof.

Using elementary fact that
\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right|_{z=1}(1-z)f(z)=-f(1)
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2mn}}{1-q^{n}}

\displaystyle=-\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right|_{z=1}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2mn}(z;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}}=-\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right|_{z=1}\frac{(zq^{2m};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{2m};q)_{\infty}}.

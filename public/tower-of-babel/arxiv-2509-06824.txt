Modulation instability and frequency-comb generation in hybrid quadratic-cubic resonators with spectral filtering Preprint: APS/123-QED
Minji Shi, Nicolas Englebert, François Leo, Dmitry V. Skryabin, Auro M. Perego

Abstract
We present an analytical and numerical investigation into the phenomenon of filter-induced modulation instability in passive hybrid optical resonators exhibiting quadratic and cubic nonlinearity. We show that asymmetric spectral losses, with respect to the continuous-wave solution frequency, can trigger sideband amplification in the normal dispersion regime. We calculate the parametric gain and demonstrate the associated pattern formation process. We furthermore show how this parametric process can be exploited to generate optical frequency combs with tunable repetition rate.



I
Introduction

Optical frequency combs (OFCs) are optical rulers consisting of equally spaced coherent spectral lines. They find applications in precision metrology, optical clocks, spectroscopy, microscopy and telecommunications, among others
Perego and Ellis 2024
;
Fortier and Baumann 2019
. OFCs can be generated through various platforms, including mode-locked lasers
Cundiff and Ye 2003
;
Diddams et al. 2020
, electro-optic modulators
Parriaux et al. 2020
, and driven Kerr resonators
Kippenberg et al. 2011
;
Pasquazi et al. 2018
. Quadratic nonlinearity resonators are a promising platform for OFC generation too
Leo et al. 2016
;
Englebert et al. 2021
;
Mosca et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Lu et al. 2023
.
In this paper, we propose a novel method for OFC generation in hybrid quadratic-cubic resonators by extending the concept of the gain-through-filtering (GTF) process originally proposed and demonstrated in Kerr cavities
Perego et al. 2021
;
Bessin et al. 2019
. This method is based on the more general concept of filter-induced modulation instability (MI) obtained by the presence of asymmetric spectral losses for signal and idler waves
Perego et al. 2018
. The GTF process facilitates the generation of sidebands through asymmetric spectral filtering relative to the pump. Frequency-asymmetric narrowband losses induce the creation of one sideband in proximity to the filter frequency, with the other sideband symmetrically positioned with respect to the pump. The cascaded generation of higher-order sidebands eventually leads to the formation of an OFC. By adjusting the detuning between the pump and filter frequencies, the OFC line spacing can be controlled.

MI analysis

The study of the MI process of the intracavity
cw
solution is based on Eq. (
II

\eta(Z,t)
(

\displaystyle A(Z,t)=\sqrt{P}e^{i\xi}+\eta(Z,t).

(12)

Substituting Eq. (
12

II

Moreover, Figs.

VII
Conclusions

We have presented analytical and numerical results regarding a filter-induced MI in hybrid quadratic-cubic optical resonators, enabled by asymmetric spectral losses for signal and idler waves. We have calculated analytically the parametric gain describing it as a function of several system parameters and obtained excellent agreement with numerical simulations. We have also shown how the GTF process can enable the generation of tunable optical frequency combs in quadratic-cubic resonators.

Uncovering the key factors influencing sustainable green production behavior among Chinese medicinal herb growers
Authors: Wei Liu, Muhammad Umer Arshad, Lizhong Zhang, Jingnan Wei, Yanhua Fu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractPromoting green production behavior among farmers is crucial for enhancing income, improving industrial efficiency, and ensuring ecological security. This study analyzes the determinants of green production behavior among farmers in Cheifeng, China, Using a binary logistic model on a sample of 860 microdata. The result of empirical analysis pointed out that the implementation rate of rural households was low at 54.5 %, highlighting the need for intervention. The results suggest that age, party members, total income, total area, irrigation conditions, market, policy, social factors, and knowledge transfer ability significantly influence green production behavior. Among these factors, age, party member, total area, irrigation conditions, and knowledge transfer ability have negative effects, while other factors positively affect green production. To promote green production behavior, this study recommends improving access to information, developing a training system, enhancing quality and safety information disclosure, establishing traceability mechanisms, and providing policy subsidies. This stud also highlights the need to strengthen publicity, technical guidance, and media persuasion for promoting the green production of Chinese medicinal materials.Keywords: Chinese medicinal materials, Green production behavior, Influencing factors, Binary logistic model

Introduction

Green agricultural development is necessary to protect the rural ecological environment and achieve high-quality agricultural production [1]. Green production is crucial in promoting sustainable agricultural practices, ensuring food safety for consumers, and protecting the rural ecological environment [2,3]. This behavior aims to achieve “high yield, high quality, and high efficiency” production methods and promote the sustainable development of the Chinese medicinal materials industry with fewer fertilizers and pesticides involved. For a long time, a crude growth model that relies on increasing resource inputs has facilitated high economic growth in the agricultural sector. Still, it has also exacerbated the deterioration of rural ecological environments and the pollution of agricultural water sources [4,5].

China, as the world's largest producer of traditional Chinese medicinal materials with the widest variety and most comprehensive production system, has maintained its position as the world's top producer of traditional Chinese medicinal materials for nearly 20 years. With the growth in production of traditional Chinese medicinal materials and the continuous expansion of the industry, issues related to the excessive use of pesticides, fertilizers, and the safety of traditional Chinese medicinal materials have been on the rise, leading to severe environmental pollution and health problems, thus exerting tremendous pressure on agricultural sustainability and human life safety [6]. Therefore, promoting environmentally friendly production of traditional Chinese medicinal materials and advancing the sustainable development of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry are not only crucial aspects of rural revitalization but also important measures for advancing the “Healthy China” strategy.

To promote the inheritance and innovation of traditional Chinese medicine, as well as the healthy development of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry, the Chinese government has issued a series of policies and regulations in recent years. In 2019, the State Council's General Office issued a notice on deepening the reform of the medical and health system during the “Thirteenth Five-Year Plan,” which explicitly called for strengthening the quality management of traditional Chinese medicinal materials and promoting the establishment of a complete quality traceability system along the entire supply chain [7]. In 2021, the State Council's General Office issued a set of policy measures to accelerate the characteristic development of traditional Chinese medicine, proposing the formulation of technical standards for the collection of traditional Chinese medicinal materials, initial processing at the production site, ecological cultivation, wild nurturing, and simulated wild cultivation to promote standardized cultivation of traditional Chinese medicinal materials. In 2022, the State Council issued the “Fourteenth Five-Year Plan” for the development of traditional Chinese medicine, which emphasized the need to strengthen the construction of authentic medicinal plant breeding bases and production bases, encourage the ecological cultivation of traditional Chinese medicinal materials in mountainous and forested areas, optimize the layout of production areas and product structures, conduct rapid testing technology research and development for authentic medicinal plant production areas and product quality, and integrate innovation and demonstration to promote a batch of green production technologies and cultivation models with the goal of stabilizing and improving the quality of traditional Chinese medicinal materials [8]. The Chinese government has promulgated a series of laws and regulations on environmentally friendly production of traditional Chinese medicinal materials, providing strong support for achieving high-quality development of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry.

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) is the collective term for the fields of traditional Chinese medicine and traditional Chinese pharmacology. Traditional Chinese medicine is a comprehensive science that focuses on the theory and practical experience of traditional Chinese medicine and studies the laws governing the transformation of health and disease in human life activities, as well as their prevention, diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation, and health preservation [9,10]. It is a discipline that uses methods such as traditional Chinese medicinal materials, acupuncture, and massage to restore the health of the human body. Traditional Chinese pharmacology is a discipline that studies the basic theories and clinical applications of traditional Chinese medicine, including the cultivation, collection, processing, and preparation methods of traditional Chinese medicinal materials. Among these, traditional Chinese medicinal materials are the material basis for the healthy development of traditional Chinese medicine, and promoting the ecological cultivation of high-quality traditional Chinese medicinal materials is an essential requirement for ensuring the sustainable development of traditional Chinese medicine [[6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12]].

In recent years, as the population has increased, the resources of wild medicinal materials have sharply decreased, and the supply-demand contradiction has become increasingly apparent. Cultivated medicinal materials have become the primary source of commodities [13,14]. However, the rise of cultivated medicinal materials has led to changes in the origin of medicinal materials and a serious decline in quality. The evaluation of medicinal materials has also become more uncertain [15]. Farmers are not only the direct participants, but also the direct beneficiaries in the production decision-making of Chinese medicinal materials, and their behavior and decision-making are directly related to the quality and safety of Chinese medicinal materials [16,17].

Although the cultivation of Chinese medicinal materials is facing a rare development opportunity in China, excessive use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides has caused some problems [18]. Although there are differences in the production targets pursued by farmers, the phenomenon of emphasizing quantity over quality is common, affecting the quality of Chinese medicinal materials and restricting the healthy development of the TCM industry [19,20].

Studies have shown that the economic losses caused by non-point source pollution of fertilizer are much higher than the direct economic losses caused by the rise in fertilizer prices for farmers [21]. According to statistics from the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, in 2021, less than 5 million mu of Chinese medicinal materials have been planted using ecological methods, accounting for about one-sixteenth of the total area planted with Chinese medicinal materials. This shows that there is still a long way to go regarding the green production of Chinese medicinal materials. Due to the information asymmetry between market players, farmers of Chinese herbal medicines are easily driven by profits to increase their use of chemicals such as pesticides and fertilizers, which leads to rational behavior that is non-rational [22,23]. With the development of the Chinese economy entering a new normal, it is a general trend to persist in the harmonious coexistence of man and nature and to integrate the ecological transformation into new-type industrialization, urbanization, agricultural modernization, and informatization [24].

However, with the rapid integration of farmers, the non-agricultural employment of young and middle-aged labor forces, the increasing cost of agricultural production, and the risks associated with agricultural operations, the profit margins for farmers are shrinking [25]. Although the government has implemented a subsidy policy for the cultivation of traditional Chinese medicine and increased efforts to promote ecological cultivation, farmers' enthusiasm for green products is not high due to the limitations of policy implementation and the imperfect market mechanisms. Therefore, studying the influencing factors and mechanisms of farmers' green production behavior is urgent.

Green production behavior refers to the actions taken by farmers in agricultural production that consider agricultural production efficiency, product safety, and ecological environment protection. The ultimate goal is to promote the sustainable development of agriculture [26]. With the rapid growth of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry, the green production behavior of traditional Chinese medicinal materials growers has become a hot topic of research both domestically and internationally. Traditional Chinese medicinal materials, as the source of the traditional Chinese medicine industry chain, are of particular importance for the study of green production behavior. Green production behavior of traditional Chinese medicinal materials growers refers to the actions taken to produce high-quality traditional Chinese medicinal materials, which essentially means producing authentic medicinal materials. Authentic medicinal materials, as representatives of high-quality traditional Chinese medicinal materials, are characterized primarily by reduced use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides during cultivation, with the ultimate goal of improving the quality of traditional Chinese medicinal materials, increasing farmers' income, promoting improvements in agricultural ecological environments, and ensuring the sustainable and healthy development of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry. Based on this foundation, the economic benefits of medicinal farming, ecological benefits of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry, and overall social benefits are organically combined to form a new development model [[27], [28]].

Currently, most studies include traditional Chinese medicinal materials as ordinary agricultural products in their research on green production behavior. After organizing the research, the factors influencing farmers' green production behavior mainly focus on the following aspects: The impact of farmers' production and management characteristics on green production behavior. Research by Chen Xuetong et al. (2021) suggests that there is a U-shaped relationship between the scale of farmers' operations and their adoption of reduced-input ecological production behavior. The study results by Hua Lu et al. (2019) also support this viewpoint. Research by Dusen et al. (2022) indicates that the planting area and specialization level significantly positively influence farmers' green production behavior. Ceddia Michele Graziano et al. (2022) found that factors like the scale of farmers' operations and the number of labor inputs have a significant impact on green production behavior.

The influence of farmers' psychological characteristics on green production behavior. Scholars have found that factors related to green production awareness, decision-making, and environmental cognition have a positive impact on farmers' willingness to engage in green production behavior [[29], [30], [31], [32]]. Domestic scholar Li Hao (2020), based on farmers' perceptions of fairness and trust, analyzed farmers' agricultural environmental protection behavior and found that the perception of fairness in agricultural environmental protection directly and indirectly affects such behavior. Foreign scholar Lucy Emerton et al. (2018) emphasized that farmers' willingness significantly influences their behavioral decisions.

The impact of external factors on farmers' green production behavior. External factors include policy factors, market factors, and social factors. Chen Zhuanqing (2021) believes that policy and market orientation positively regulate farmers' green production behavior, and He Yue (2021) further demonstrated the positive regulatory effects of market mechanisms, policy incentive mechanisms, and constraint mechanisms on farmers' green production behavior. Furthermore, the intensity of regulation [33], subsidy policies [34], government regulations (Xi Wei Dong et al., 2014) all have a significant impact on farmers' green production behavior.

In summary, both domestic and international scholars have conducted in-depth research on the factors influencing farmers' green production behavior, and the results are rich. However, through the review of existing literature, it is evident that scholars differ in their approaches to studying the factors influencing farmers' green production behavior. Most research is based on the influence of psychological characteristics, individual and production characteristics, or external factors on farmers' green production behavior. Few scholars have studied the combined impact of internal and external factors on farmers' green production behavior. Green production behavior of farmers is a complex and dynamic process. Currently, research on the factors influencing farmers' green production behavior mainly focuses on the influence of external or internal factors, so exploring the synergistic effects of internal and external factors on farmers' green production behavior still needs further exploration. Therefore, based on theories of farmers' behavior, externality, and agency relationships, this paper explores the influence of internal and external factors on farmers' green production behavior at the micro-level, aiming to provide a theoretical basis for adjusting and improving policies for green production of traditional Chinese medicinal materials. This research has significant practical implications for promoting the green and sustainable development of the traditional Chinese medicinal materials industry.

Theoretical analysis and framework

The article is based on the theory of farmer behavior [35] and, under the assumption of “rational agents” and externalities theory [36], utilizes the “hidden action moral risk model” from the agency theory to analyze the influencing factors of farmers' green production behavior. The agency model was constructed by Becker et al. (1974) and is widely used for analyzing ethical risks and other issues [37].

The “hidden action moral risk model” refers to a model in which, in certain situations, individuals may choose to conceal their actions or information, leading to some ethical risks. Specifically, this model includes four main factors: hidden actions, information asymmetry, conflicts of interest, and integrity issues. Hidden actions refer to situations where individuals may choose to conceal their actions or information to gain more benefits, especially when there is information asymmetry. Information asymmetry occurs when individuals have different information, leading to unfair information access and use, thereby creating ethical risks. Conflicts of interest arise when the interests of different individuals conflict, leading to ethical decision-making issues. Integrity issues pertain to the credibility and truthfulness of individuals’ behavior [38].

In the “hidden action moral risk model,” individuals, in cases of information asymmetry and conflicts of interest, may choose to hide their actions or information, resulting in ethical risks such as fraud, false advertising, and unfair competition. These issues can have negative impacts on society and the economy [39]. Green production of traditional Chinese medicine represents a typical agency relationship, where principals can include the government, cooperatives, and leading enterprises, while farmers are agents in this relationship. In the context of decision-making for green production of traditional Chinese medicine, the “hidden action moral risk model” is applicable.

Firstly, meeting the characteristic of “hidden actions” in the “hidden action moral risk model,” farmers base their production behavior decisions in the context of traditional Chinese medicine production on maximizing their own utility. These decisions include two categories of actions during the cultivation of traditional Chinese medicine: implementing green production practices or not implementing them. When farmers choose not to implement green production practices, they tend to excessively use pesticides, fertilizers, and other chemical products, aiming to gain additional profits while violating ethical norms [13,40]. Due to the lack of supervision by principals, agents engage in “hidden actions” driven by their self-interest maximization.

Secondly, it aligns with the “information asymmetry” characteristic in the “hidden action moral risk model.” During the cultivation of traditional Chinese medicine, principals such as the government expect agents to carry out cultivation in accordance with the agency relationship, taking the principals' interests into account. However, principals are unable to monitor various actions taken by agents, leading to a situation of information asymmetry between principals and agents. This information asymmetry can result in farmers adopting non-green production practices, leading to excessive yields and profits. However, farmers do not bear the consequences of compromised quality and ecological damage caused by traditional Chinese medicine production [41].

Thirdly, it corresponds to the “conflicts of interest” characteristic in the “hidden action moral risk model.” Green production of traditional Chinese medicine exhibits typical externalities. Farmers’ green production behavior attracts the attention of principals such as the government. If farmers choose not to implement green production practices, it can lead to reduced subsidies, non-compliance with market inspections, and a decline in social reputation. This not only impacts the interests of the agents but also represents a loss for the principals [42]. Consequently, the agency relationship is a system where risks and incentives coexist.

Lastly, it aligns with the “integrity issues” characteristic in the “hidden action moral risk model.” In pursuit of maximizing their own benefits, principals such as the government may adopt behaviors in the agency relationship that favor them. However, the high costs of monitoring, resulting from information asymmetry, can give rise to integrity issues for principals. In summary, this analysis illustrates how the “hidden action moral risk model” applies to the decision-making process of farmers in the context of green production of traditional Chinese medicine. It highlights the presence of hidden actions, information asymmetry, conflicts of interest, and integrity issues in this setting, emphasizing the complex interplay of risk and incentives within the agency relationship.

Based on this, this article utilizes the “hidden action moral risk model” from the agency theory to analyze the applicability of farmers' green production behavior decisions. The key participants in the agency model are divided into two categories: principals and agents. Principals entrust agents with certain production tasks. On one hand, principals hope that the actions taken by agents consider their interests. On the other hand, principals can only see the outcomes of their delegation and cannot clearly understand or control the actions carried out by agents. In this context, agents may engage in behaviors that are more favorable to themselves to gain greater benefits, while principals find it challenging to supervise these actions, often due to high monitoring costs. This can lead to moral risks [43].

From existing research within the agency model, it is known that the cost of farmers' green production is related to market conditions, farmers' resource endowments, and their level of awareness. The income from farmers' traditional Chinese medicine production is influenced by market inspection mechanisms and pricing mechanisms. The fines imposed for not implementing green production practices and the subsidies received for implementing them are related to organizational and government subsidy mechanisms. Considering the actual circumstances of farmers engaged in agricultural production, whether they adopt green production practices may be influenced by factors from themselves, the market, and the government. Furthermore, existing research indicates a close connection between whether farmers adopt green production practices and social factors.

In summary, this article employs the “hidden action moral risk model” from agency theory to analyze the suitability of farmers' decisions regarding green production behavior. The agency model involves principals and agents, with principals hoping for actions aligned with their interests while facing challenges in monitoring agents' behavior. It highlights the influence of factors from farmers themselves, the market, and the government on farmers' decisions regarding green production, and it underscores the role of social factors in this decision-making process.

Research hypotheses

Knowledge capability significantly influences farmers' green production behavior [26]. Knowledge capability encompasses knowledge demand, knowledge acquisition, and knowledge transformation capabilities. With the rapid growth of income levels, consumers' demand for their own health has risen to a higher level, gradually shifting towards a demand for green and safe agricultural products [44,34]. As the direct decision-makers in traditional Chinese medicine production, farmers have a direct impact on the quality and safety of traditional Chinese medicine. However, whether farmers are willing to proactively acquire knowledge about green production and apply it to traditional Chinese medicine production remains a subject of discussion. According to the theory of rational smallholder farmers, farmers, as rational economic agents, will choose behavior that maximizes economic benefits in their production decisions. Although farmers may incur additional agricultural production costs by transforming green production knowledge and implementing green production practices, green agricultural products can yield additional market profits, thereby enhancing farmers' operational income [21,45]. Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes the following hypothesis:

Knowledge capability has a positive influence on farmers' green production behavior.

Market factors, policy factors, and other external factors significantly influence farmers' green production behavior [14,23]. With the acceleration of urbanization and non-agricultural employment in China, there has been a clear differentiation among farmers. Influenced by the different objectives of farmers' operations, there have emerged survival-oriented farmers, lifestyle-oriented farmers, production-oriented farmers, and function-oriented farmers [46]. Existing research indicates that different types of farmers, influenced by their own resource endowments and operational objectives, exhibit significant differences in green production behavior under the influence of external factors [32]. Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes the following hypothesis:

External factors have differential impacts on farmers' green production behavior.

Materials and methods

Research area

Chifeng is situated in the eastern part of Inner Mongolia, bordered by Tongliao to the east, Chaoyang to the southeast, Chengde to the southwest, and Xilingol League to the northwest. The geographic coordinates range between 41°17′10′N and 45°24′15′N and 116°21′07′E and 120°58′52′E. This area is a crucial research site for the coordinated development of ecological planting, ecological resource protection, and the utilization of Chinese medicinal materials.

With an area of 550,000 mu (“mu” is a unit of land measurement in China, equivalent to 666.666 square meters.) in 2021, Chifeng is a well-known production base for northern and Mongolian medicines in China. For many years, the scale of Chinese herbal medicine cultivation ranked first in the whole region and first among the country's prefecture-level cities. Chifeng has made great efforts in recent years to develop the Chinese herbal medicine industry, leveraging its unique natural conditions and long history of cultivation. As a result, a complete Chinese herbal medicine industry chain has gradually formed.

Culturing Chinese medicinal materials has gradually become one of the pillar industries in Chifeng, mainly centered around Harqin Banner and gradually spreading to other counties. Hexigten Banner, Linxi County, Inner Mongolia, Baarin Left Banner, and AR Horqin Banner dominate the north. In contrast, the middle is dominated by flag counties such as Harqin Banner, Ongniud Banner, Ningcheng County, Yuanbaoshan District, Hongshan District, Chifeng, and Aohan Banner. The scale of cultivation scale of Chinese medicinal materials has been expanding. By July 2020, in Chifeng, major commodities of Mongolian and Chinese herbal medicines include Platycodon grandiflorum (73,200 mu), Radix isatidis (31,000 mu), radix saposhnikoviae (25,000 mu), Radix Astragali (116,000 mu), Radix scutellariae (27,000 mu) and radix isatidis (15,000 mu). By the end of July 2021, the area under cultivation of traditional Chinese in Chifeng and Mongolia had been expanded to 550,000 mu, with an output of 464,000 tons and an estimated total output value of 7.01 billion yuan, ranking first in Inner Mongolia in terms of output and cultivation scale, and fifth at the national level among league cities.

Based on this, the following indicator system has been constructed, and the relevant variables and explanations can be found in Table 1. Among these, the dependent variable is defined as “whether to implement green production behavior” [32]. The independent variables include market factors, defined as “Do you believe that implementing green production behavior will increase the purchase price of traditional Chinese medicinal materials?” [47]; policy factors, defined as “Have you received government subsidies for green production?” [48]; social factors, defined as “Does media coverage and advocacy of green production significantly influence your planting behavior?” [44]; and knowledge capability, defined as “Are you willing to proactively learn technical knowledge about fertilizer and pesticide use?” “When encountering issues with fertilizer and pesticide application, can you find out how to resolve these problems?” “Can you effectively apply the knowledge you've acquired to fertilizer and pesticide application?” [33]. Control variables mainly include gender, age, education level, party membership, health status, total household population, annual household income, operational scale, and irrigation conditions [34,[46], [49]].

Data source and questionnaire structure

This study, which incorporates the use of a questionnaire, obtained consent from participants in written form. Ethical approval for this research was granted by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the School of Economics and Management, Beijing Forestry University, under the study number 2170188. The data used in this article were collected from a survey of planting households conducted by the research team in December 2022 in eight counties (banners, districts) of Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, Chifeng City. The reason for selecting Chifeng City as the sample area is as follows: First, Chifeng City is located at the junction of the warm temperate zone and the cold temperate zone, with distinct four seasons and hilly terrain. The average annual temperature in the city is 0–7 °C, and the annual average precipitation is 381 mm, making it suitable for the growth of Chinese medicinal materials. Chifeng City is the main producing area of authentic high-quality Chinese and Mongolian medicines in northern China and is known as the “hometown of Chinese North American ginseng and platycodon.” The planting area of Chinese medicinal materials in Chifeng City has remained stable at around 700,000 mu (about 46,667 ha) annually, ranking among the top in prefecture-level cities in China. Its products are widely recognized and have been exported to Southeast Asian countries such as South Korea and Japan. Although small and medium-scale planters still dominate, the level of large-scale planting is continuously increasing, which is consistent with other Chinese medicinal material producing areas.

Second, due to the large scale of Chinese medicinal material planting in Chifeng City, the development of the industry is fast. However, in the process of planting Chinese medicinal materials imitating wild conditions, there is a lack of technical specifications, and there are no standards for the use of pesticides, fertilizers, and other chemicals. The excessive use of pesticides and fertilizers is more pronounced.

Third, Chifeng City is a key region for the coordinated development of ecological planting of Chinese medicinal materials and the protection and utilization of ecological resources. In recent years, Chifeng City has formulated an overall plan for the use of pesticides and fertilizers, closely focused on the goal of zero growth in the use of pesticides and fertilizers, increased the proportion of biopesticides and organic fertilizers, and promoted green agricultural planting methods such as soil testing and formula fertilization. The use of chemical pesticides and fertilizers has steadily decreased, which is conducive to promoting sustainable agricultural development, improving agricultural production efficiency, and protecting the ecological environment and the quality of Chinese medicinal materials.

The research team, based on comprehensive considerations of the total output of Chinese medicinal materials, the use of pesticides and fertilizers in each county (banner, district) of Chifeng City, and the implementation of green production, finally selected eight representative counties (banners, districts): Kalaxi Banner, Ningcheng County, Aohan Banner, Songshan District, Yuanbaoshan District, Wengniute Banner, Bairin Left Banner, and Bairin Right Banner.

Before the formal survey, based on existing research results and the actual situation in the survey area, a preliminary questionnaire was formulated. In November 2022, a pre-survey was conducted in Niujiayingzi Town, Kalaxi Banner, Chifeng City. A total of 120 Chinese medicinal material planters were visited, and exchanges were made with local governments, cooperatives, and leading enterprises. Based on the problems identified during the survey process and expert opinions, a formal questionnaire was formulated.

The research team members include young teachers, doctoral students, and some master's students. Before the survey, comprehensive training was conducted for the research team members. The research personnel were divided into different groups, led by experienced teachers, with each group consisting of 3–4 people, including a team leader, a questionnaire filler, and a questionnaire reviewer. In addition, to ensure the quality of the survey and the effectiveness of the samples, active contacts were made with classmates, relatives, friends, and acquaintances working in rural economic management stations and agricultural technology departments. Under their guidance, preliminary contacts and communications were made with village leaders in the surveyed villages (gacha), and then the survey was conducted.

In the survey, stratified sampling was used as the sampling population principle, and specific samples were randomly selected. First, eight sample counties (banners, districts) were selected as primary sampling units. In each sample county (banner, district), according to the scale of Chinese medicinal material planting, they were categorized into five classes: very low, low, medium, high, and very high. Two towns (townships) were randomly selected from each class. Second, based on the number of Chinese medicinal material planters in the sample towns (townships), the villages (gachas) were sorted. Four sample villages (gachas) were randomly selected from the villages (gachas) with the top 50 % of Chinese medicinal material planters. Finally, 15–20 planting households were randomly selected for the survey in each sample village (gacha). A total of 900 survey questionnaires were distributed, and 861 valid questionnaires were collected, with an effective rate of 97.6 %.

During the survey, the missing values were defined as 5 %. If the missing value of the questionnaire is more than 5 %, it will be regarded as an invalid questionnaire. If it is less than 5 %, it will be treated by the Bayesian interpolation method because of the low proportion of missing values. The Bayesian interpolation method will significantly impact the effect of data samples.

Because of the different resource endowments of farmers, their household management goals are not the same. Therefore, to determine the driving factors and driving forces of farmers' green production behavior, two types of 15 influencing factors were designed according to the characteristics of farmers in the study area. Farmers’ resource endowment & green production behavior

Individual characteristics, age, and whether party members passed the significance test. The study found that the regression coefficient of age is −0.003, which means that age will significantly negatively impact the implementation of green production behavior. It can be understood that older growers are less willing to implement green production practices because of their planting habits and poor access to information, which is consistent with the tour hypothesis. Young and middle-aged farmers are easy to accept the new technological transformation. They are full of risk-taking, making it easier to make green production behavior decision-making. The regression coefficient of party membership is −0.735, meaning that party membership has a negative effect on green production. It can be understood that village cadres are familiar with the relevant laws and regulations of the state, have a relatively high awareness of green production, and are willing to implement green production behaviors actively. The main reason is that the planting area of traditional Chinese medicine in some party members’ families is relatively small, and there are opportunistic behaviors and tendencies. The education level did not pass the significance test on whether to carry out green production behavior, which can be explained since the majority of Chinese medicinal materials growers are middle-aged and older experienced people whose general education level is low and have less attention to green production; they are unwilling to accept the new strategy consequently. Similarly, gender does not pass the significance test too, which can be understood as, mostly, the decision on herbal products is decided by the male head of the household; therefore, gender has no direct impact on the decision-making of green production of Chinese medicinal materials.

In the family characteristics, the total family income significantly impacts the green production behavior of farmers, and its regression coefficient is 0.360, indicating that the total family income of farmers and whether to implement green production behavior shows a positive relationship. It indicates that in the vast areas where Chinese herbal medicines are grown, the agricultural income and working income of farmers are generally higher, and farmers pay more attention to the improvement of the quality of Chinese herbal medicines in the decision-making process of green production of Chinese herbal medicines and get a higher return. The total area of cultivated land has a significant negative relationship (regression coefficient is −0.014) with the green production behavior of farmers. It explains that the larger the area of cultivated land, the farmer gave more importance to increase the output to obtain high returns. In addition, the larger the area of cultivated land, the higher the probability of land contracting, the higher the rent, labor cost, and machinery cost, the higher the yield, and the higher the application rate of pesticides and fertilizers, which negatively influence the implementation of green production. The total household population has no significant influence on whether to implement green production behavior. This could be due to the long-term migrant workers in rural households with several relatively fixed salaries. Therefore, population does not have a significant role. In addition, the survey results also found that the number of farmers in rural households is generally 2–3 people.

The Irrigation condition is negatively significant (0.014) associated with the green production behavior of farmers. It can be explained that farmers with better irrigation conditions expect a higher return rate of land, so they are less likely to implement green production behavior since their productivity is sufficient actively. On the other hand, the land with poorer irrigation conditions is usually located in the mountain region where fertilizer is inefficient. Hence, farmers tend to apply more farm manure, accordingly, promoting the occurrence of green production behavior.

External factor’' influence on green production behavior

The regression coefficient is 0.573, and the relationship between market factors and green production behavior is positive, which explains that under the promotion of a market price mechanism, the higher the market price expectation of Chinese medicinal materials brought about by farmer’' green production behavior, the greater the willingness and probability of farmers to implement green production behavior. This result is also consistent with the assumptions. Conversely, suppose the market price mechanism is imperfect. In that case, farmers’ willingness to adopt green production behavior will be very low, and economic factors are one of the driving factors that farmers consider.

The regression coefficient of policy (0.227) indicates a positive correlation between policy and the implementation of green production behavior. This suggests that government subsidies for the green production of Chinese medicinal materials can incentivize farmers to adopt green production practices by offsetting the increased costs associated with such practices.

The regression coefficient of social factors is 0.411, indicating a positive correlation between the social factors and the implementation of green production behavior. With the increasing government investment in environmental protection and consequent improvement in the air quality, water resources, and rural living conditions, farmers’ willingness to adopt green production practices is also increasing yearly.

Impact of knowledge on green production behavior

The knowledge demand and acquisition ability did not pass the significance test with regression coefficients of −0.009 and 0.138, respectively, indicating that they do not affect farmers' green production behavior. On the other hand, the knowledge transformation ability passed the significance test with a regression coefficient of −0.612, suggesting that it positively influences farmer’' implementation of green production behavior. This can be attributed to the conservative thinking of some farmers due to their age and education level, which makes it challenging for them to accept new ideas and knowledge related to green production. However, some farmers who could transform their knowledge were more likely to adopt green product practices. At the same time, others were hesitant due to increased production costs and decreased output of traditional Chinese medicine. Thus, the knowledge transformation ability had a negative effect on the implementation of green production behavior.

Discussion

Accelerating the promotion of green production behavior among farmers and promoting agricultural quality and efficiency is the inevitable choice for implementing the strategy of revitalizing agriculture with quality. By studying the influencing factors and mechanisms of farmer’' behavior decisions as direct participants in production, this research contributes to the formulation and implementation of policies related to agricultural green production. Compared to previous studies, this article deepens the research in the following ways:

(1)The use of binary Logistic regression to analyze the key factors influencing farmer’' green production behavior, organically connecting internal and external factors, addresses the shortcomings of previous research in examining changes in farmer’' psychology and social factors. The research results show that the green production behavior of Chinese medicinal herb growers is closely related to both internal and external factors, as well as the characteristics of farmer’' resource endowments. Market factors, policy factors, and social factors all have a positive promoting relationship with whether farmers implement green production behavior [13,[53], [54], [55]]. This aligns with Research Hypothesis 1. However, the positive promoting effect of market factors is more significant, which indirectly indicates that Chinese medicinal herb growers are more sensitive to market price factors [19,56,57]. In contrast, the policy subsidy effect is the weakest. The research found that although reducing pesticide and fertilizer usage has positive externalities, policy subsidies do not significantly affect all farmers. This suggests that the implementation of policy tools has not taken into account the heterogeneous needs of all farmers, and there is a lack of necessary precise and differentiated implementation strategies. Further subdividing Chinese medicinal herb growers is necessary to achieve more effective results.

These findings align with previous research [64,65,66]; however, the market factors exhibit a more pronounced positive significant influence, indicating that farmers are particularly responsive to market prices of Chinese medicinal materials. On the other hand, policy subsidies show the weakest effect, highlighting the need for a targeted green production subsidy mechanism and enhanced policy support in the Chinese medicinal materials industry.

Contrary to prior studies, this research found that knowledge demand acquisition ability was not a significant factor. Only the knowledge transformation ability demonstrated a negative impact, which can be attributed to the unique characteristics of the Chinese medicinal materials industry. Chinese herbal medicines have distinct knowledge requirements compared to other crops, demanding specialized expertise. Additionally, Chinese agriculture typically consists of educated and less educated farmers, with a higher inclination towards traditional practices and resistance to novelty.

Additionally, the study revealed that although pesticide and fertilizer reduction have positive external effects [67,68,69], the policy subsidy mechanism should provide a strong incentive. However, the empirical findings indicate that the policy subsidy factor significantly impacts all pesticide farmers. This suggests that implementing policy tools fails to consider the diverse needs of pesticide farmers, lacking necessary precision and differentiated implementation strategies. Segmenting Chinese medicinal herbs further is crucial to achieving more attractive outcomes with less effort.

Furthermore, this study identified a trend of agricultural transformation among Chinese medicinal herb farmers [70,71,72]. The underlying principle of this agricultural transformation is industrialization and urbanization, aimed at absorbing a surplus labor force from the agriculture sector [19]. Urbanization, industrialization, and non-agricultural employment have influenced traditional small-scale farmers, altering their management objectives from subsistence farming to more productive, livelihood-oriented approaches.

Conclusions

This article is based on a micro perspective and conducts household surveys of Chinese medicinal herb growers in Chifeng City, the main producing area of Chinese medicinal herbs. Drawing upon farmer behavior theory, externality theory, and principal-agent relationship theory, it explores the factors and mechanisms influencing farmers' green production behavior, both internally and externally. The research findings indicate:

Firstly, the implementation rate of green production behavior among Chinese medicinal herb growers is relatively low, at 54.5 %. Farmers exhibit a weaker willingness for green production, and their green production behavior is influenced by both internal and external factors. In the absence of a sound market mechanism, the positive reinforcement from social and policy factors can compensate for the inadequacy of market-driven factors.

Secondly, among the internal and external influencing factors, nine critical factors significantly affect farmers' implementation of green production behavior. These factors are age, party membership, total family income, total cultivated land area, irrigation conditions, market factors, policy factors, social factors, and knowledge conversion ability. These factors have varying degrees of impact on farmers' green production behavior, with market factors having the most significant positive effect, followed by social factors, family income, policy factors, age, cultivated land area, irrigation conditions, knowledge conversion ability, and party membership.

Thirdly, there are differences in the directions of influence of the critical factors on farmers' implementation of green production behavior. Age, party membership, cultivated land area, irrigation conditions, and knowledge conversion ability are negatively correlated with farmers' green production behavior, while market factors, policy factors, and social factors significantly promote the implementation of green production behavior. Additionally, the study finds that farmers' implementation of green production behavior is the result of the combined influence of internal and external factors.

Based on these findings, the conclusions of this article provide several policy recommendations to promote farmers' green production behavior and facilitate the transformation and upgrading of the Chinese medicinal herb industry:

Since the overall implementation rate of green production behavior among Chinese medicinal herb growers is relatively low (54.5 %), the government should raise awareness and willingness among farmers for green production. It should also improve the establishment of government incentives and constraints mechanisms and create multiple pathways to stimulate farmers to implement green production behavior.

Although there are numerous factors influencing farmers' green production behavior, only nine factors have passed the significance tests. The government needs to develop complementary policy measures tailored to these key influencing factors and implement differentiated management to enhance farmers' willingness to implement green production behavior. Additionally, increasing household income through various means, improving the pricing mechanism in the Chinese medicinal herb market, and establishing important pathways such as the formation of high-quality medicinal herb pricing mechanisms, standards for high-quality medicinal herb specifications, traceability system construction, and brand building are necessary to provide reference for the formulation of related policy measures. These measures will help boost the continuous improvement of the quality of Chinese medicinal herbs and ensure stable supply.

In the context of an inadequate market mechanism, it is essential to strengthen the promotion of green production for Chinese medicinal herbs and provide technical guidance. Enhancing the precision of media guidance and persuasiveness of green production, while establishing a policy subsidy mechanism for green production, can increase farmers' enthusiasm for green production.

For factors such as age, party membership, cultivated land area, irrigation conditions, and knowledge conversion ability, which negatively promote [73] the implementation of green production behavior, the government should encourage young and middle-aged individuals to engage in entrepreneurship in rural areas, strengthen the leading role of party members, improve rural infrastructure, enhance the accessibility of knowledge acquisition for green production among farmers, establish a training system for green production among Chinese medicinal herb growers, and thereby raise farmers' awareness and practical skills in green production.

One limitation of the study is that this article's survey data is limited to Chifeng City, and regional comparisons have not been made with farmers' green production behavior in other major Chinese medicinal herb-producing areas. Green production behavior of farmers has strong positive externalities and can be influenced by economic, policy, geographical, and sample size factors. In future research, different regions and types of Chinese medicinal herbs can be included to make the research more universal and explore the green production behavior and influencing factors in different regions. In future research, it would be beneficial to expand the study to the province or national level to examine the influencing factors of green production behavior among farmers in different regions, which would facilitate the establishment of ecological cultivation and differentiated management systems for Chinese medicinal materials. Future research should also focus on conducting comparative studies across different crops to better understand the unique characteristics of Chinese medicinal materials compared to other crops, and it will provide valuable insights and suggestions for promoting the healthy development of the Chinese medicinal materials industry.

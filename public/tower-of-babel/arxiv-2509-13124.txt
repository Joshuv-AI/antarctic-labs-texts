Zhu algebras of superconformal vertex algebras
Ryo Sato 1, Shintarou Yanagida 2

Abstract
. The purpose of this note is to demonstrate the advantages of Y.-Z. Huang’s definition of the Zhu algebra (Comm. Contemp. Math., 7 (2005), no. 5, 649–706) for an arbitrary vertex algebra, not necessarily equipped with Hamiltonian operators or Virasoro elements, by achieving the following two goals: (1) determining the Zhu algebras of N = 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 N=1,2,3,4 and big N = 4 N=4 superconformal vertex algebras, and (2) introducing the Zhu algebras of N K = N N_{K}=N supersymmetric vertex algebras.

Introduction

0.1.
Zhu algebra

The Zhu algebra is an associative algebra associated to a vertex operator algebra (VOA).
It was introduced by Y. Zhu
[
Zhu
, §2]
, and plays a fundamental role
in understanding the representation theory of VOAs.
Here, we briefly recall its definition and fundamental properties
(see
section

1.3
for details).

Let
V
be a VOA with vacuum vector
\bm{1}
, conformal vector
\omega

and state-field correspondence
Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{(n)}
for
a\in V
.
This gives a family of operators
a_{(n)}\in\End V
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Given
a,b\in V
, where
a
is homogeneous of conformal weight
\Delta(a)
,
we define
a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{n}{*}}b\in V
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
by

a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{n}{*}}b\coloneqq\res_{z=0}\bigl[z^{-n}(1+z)^{\Delta(a)}Y(a,z)b\Bigr]dz=\sum_{j\geq 0}\binom{\Delta(a)}{j}a_{(j-n)}b.

Let
O(V)
be the linear subspace of
V

spanned by all
a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{n}{*}}b
for
a,b\in V
and
n\geq 2
.
Then,

A(V)\coloneqq V/O(V),\quad\text{with product $a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{1}{*}}b\bmod O(V)$}

is a unital associative algebra.
The image of the vacuum
\bm{1}
is the identify of
A(V)
,
and the conformal vector
\omega
maps to a central element of
A(V)
.

Zhu’s fundamental theorem
[
Zhu
, Theorem 2.2.2]
establishes
a one-to-one correspondence between

•

the isomorphism classes of simple
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded
V
-modules,

•

and the isomorphism classes of simple (left)
A(V)
-modules.

Below are some additional properties of the Zhu algebra.

•

If
V
is rational (i.e., has semisimple representation theory),
then
A(V)
is a semisimple algebra
[
Zhu
, Theorem 2.2.3]
.

•

A(V)
is finite-dimensional if
V
is
C_{2}
-cofinite
[
DLM2
, Proposition 3.6]
.

•

A(V)
is a filtered algebra (induced by the conformal weight grading),
and the associated graded object is a Poisson algebra
[
Zhu
, §4.4]
,
called the
C_{2}
-Poisson algebra of
V
.

•

Zhu’s modular invariance theorem
[
Zhu
, Theorem 5.3.3]
:
a cornerstone in the theory of modular forms and VOAs.

After
[
Zhu
]
, there have been bunch of advances of the study of the Zhu algebra.
We list a few of them.

•

Higher Zhu algebras: generalizations capturing more graded pieces
[
DLM
]
.

•

Twisted Zhu algebra
\Zhu_{H}(V)
:
a generalization to a graded vertex algebra
V
with Hamiltonian
H

[
DK
, §2]
.

•

Connections to the representation theory of finite
W
-algebras,
especially via BRST cohomology
[
DK
,
G24b
]
.

•

The relation between the chiral homology and
the Hochschild cohomology of Zhu algebra
[
vEH2
]
.

0.2.
Huang’s formulation of Zhu algebra

As explained in the previous
section

0.1
,
the original definition of the Zhu algebra
A(V)
requires the vertex algebra
V
to be a VOA,
i.e., it must possess a conformal vector and a
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-grading.
The twisted Zhu algebra
\Zhu_{H}(V)
introduced by De Sole and Kac
[
DK
]

still requires
V
to have a grading and a Hamiltonian
H
.

In contrast to these definitions,
the associative algebra
\widetilde{A}(V)
introduced by Y.-Z. Huang in
[
Hu
, §6]

is defined for an arbitrary vertex algebra
V
.
Here is the outline of the definition (see
section

1.2
for details).
Let
V
be a vertex algebra with state-field correspondence

Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}a_{(n)}
for
a\in V
.
We define
a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{n}{\bullet}}b\in V
for
a,b\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
by

a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{n}{\bullet}}b\coloneqq\res_{z=0}[e^{z}(e^{z}-1)^{-n}Y(a,z)b]dz.

Also, let
\widetilde{O}(V)
be the linear subspace of
V
spanned by
a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{n}{\bullet}}b
for
n\geq 2
.
Then,

\widetilde{A}(V)\coloneqq V/\widetilde{O}(V),\quad\text{with product $a\mathbin{\overunderset{}{1}{\bullet}}b\bmod\widetilde{O}(V)$},

is a unital associative algebra.
If
V
is a vertex operator algebra,
then
\widetilde{A}(V)
is isomorphic to
A(V)
as an associative algebra.
The definition of
\widetilde{A}(V)
is more conceptual and geometric than that of

A(V)
and
\Zhu_{H}(V)
, and requires no additional conditions on the vertex algebra
V
.
See
section

1.2
for details.

The aim of this note is to clarify the advantages of using Huang’s version
\widetilde{A}(V)

rather than the original
A(V)
or
\Zhu_{H}(V)
.
Explicitly, we will demonstrate:

1

The algebra structure of
\widetilde{A}(V)
can be determined more easily than that of
A(V)
.

2

We can naturally introduce a SUSY analogue of Zhu algebra in a geometric viewpoint.

For the demonstration, we study the Zhu algebra of
the
N=1,2,3,4
and big
N=4
superconformal vertex algebras.

We denote by

•

Huang’s formulation of Zhu algebra

Here we explain the geometric version of the Zhu algebra from
[

with slight modifications.

1.2.1.

Let
V
be a vertex algebra, and let
\gamma
be a complex number or a formal variable.
For
a,b\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, consider the expression

(1.14)

\displaystyle a\mathbin{\overunderset{\gamma}{n}{\bullet}}b\coloneqq\res_{z}\bigl[f_{n}(z;\gamma)a(z)b\bigr]dz,\quad f_{n}(z;\gamma)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\dfrac{\gamma^{n}e^{\gamma z}}{(e^{\gamma z}-1)^{n}}&(\gamma\neq 0\text{ or $\gamma$ is a formal variable})\\
z^{-n}&(\gamma=0)\end{cases}.

For the definition of
\res_{z}
, see (
1.2
).
When
\gamma
is a formal variable,
we regard
f_{n}(z;\gamma)
as a formal series of
z
:

(1.15)

\displaystyle f_{n}(z;\gamma)=\sum_{j\geq 0}c(j,n)\gamma^{j}z^{-n+j}=z^{-n}-\frac{n-2}{2}\gamma z^{-n+1}+\cdots\in\mathbb{Q}[\gamma](\!(z)\!)\quad(c(j,n)\in\mathbb{Q}).

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, We have

\displaystyle c(0,n)=1,\quad c(1,n)=-\frac{n-2}{2},\quad c(n-1,n)=\delta_{n,1},\quad c(j,1)=\frac{1}{j!}B_{j}^{+},

where
B_{j}^{+}
are the Bernoulli numbers defined by the generating function

\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{j!}B_{j}^{+}t^{j}=\frac{t}{1-e^{-t}}
.
We have
B_{0}^{+}=1
,
B_{1}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}
,
B_{2}^{+}=\frac{1}{6}
,
B_{3}^{+}=0
,

, and so on.
Then, the field condition (

) implies

\displaystyle a\mathbin{\overunderset{\gamma}{n}{\bullet}}b=\sum_{j=0}^{n+N(a,b)}c(j,n)\gamma^{j}a_{(j-n)}b=a_{(-n)}b-\frac{n-2}{2}\gamma a_{(-n+1)}b+\cdots\in V[\gamma].

In particular, for
n=1
, we have

(1.17)

\displaystyle a\mathbin{\overunderset{\gamma}{1}{\bullet}}b=\sum_{j\geq 0}\gamma^{j}\frac{B_{j}^{+}}{j!}a_{(j-1)}b=a_{(-1)}b+\frac{\gamma}{2}a_{(0)}b+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{12}a_{(1)}b+\cdots.

Some remarks are in order.

We first show that, as subspaces of

\displaystyle(a\otimes\bm{1})\mathbin{\overunderset{\gamma}{2}{\bullet}}(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}c(j,2)\gamma^{j}(a\otimes\bm{1}_{W})_{(-2+j)}(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}c(j,2)\gamma^{j}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(a_{(k)}a^{\prime})\otimes((\bm{1}_{W})_{(-3+j-k)}b^{\prime})

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(a\otimes a^{\prime})\mathbin{\overunderset{\gamma}{2}{\bullet}}(b\otimes b^{\prime})=\gamma\res_{x}[x^{-2}(a\otimes a^{\prime})(\gamma^{-1}\log(1+x))(b\otimes b^{\prime})]dx\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl[\bigl(\gamma^{i}\res_{x}[x^{-i-1}a(\gamma^{-1}\log(1+x))b]dx\bigr)\otimes\bigl(\gamma^{-i}\res_{x}[x^{i-1}a^{\prime}(\gamma^{-1}\log(1+x))b^{\prime}]dx\bigr)\bigr]

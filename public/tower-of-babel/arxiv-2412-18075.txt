How many crossing changes or Delta-moves does it take to get to a homotopy trivial link?
Anthony Bosman, Christopher W. Davis, Taylor Martin, Carolyn Otto, Katherine Vance

Abstract
. The homotopy trivializing number, n h ​ ( L ) n_{h}(L) , and the Delta homotopy trivializing number, n Δ ​ ( L ) n_{\Delta}(L) , are invariants of the link homotopy class of L L which count how many crossing changes or Delta moves are needed to reduce that link to a homotopy trivial link. In 2022, Davis, Orson, and Park proved that the homotopy trivializing number of L L is bounded above by the sum of the absolute values of the pairwise linking numbers and some quantity C n C_{n} which depends only on n n , the number of components. In this paper we improve on this result by using the classification of link homotopy due to Habegger-Lin to give a quadratic upper bound on C n C_{n} . We employ ideas from extremal graph theory to demonstrate that this bound is close to sharp, by exhibiting links with vanishing pairwise linking numbers and whose homotopy trivializing numbers grows quadratically. In the process, we determine the homotopy trivializing number of every 4-component link. We also prove a cubic upper bound on the difference between the Delta homotopy trivializing number of L L and the sum of the absolute values of the triple linking numbers of L L .

4.
Bounding the homotopy trivializing number

In this section, we prove Theorem

which we use in the introduction to conclude that

. The bulk of our work will be in proving the following theorem which allows us to realize elements of

Theorem 4.1
.

For any

, there are some
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
and

Here, for each

Before proving Theorem

.

Suppose that

Let

with

For notational ease, we have conflated

We begin this with a recollection of basic properties of commutators and the lower central series. A standard reference is the work of Magnus-Karrass-Solitar

Definition 4.3
.

Let
G
be a group and

be a generating set for

and

If

. Note that as

Definition 4.4
.

If
H
and

are subgroups of
G
, then
[H,J]\leq G
is the subgroup generated by elements of the form
[h,j]
with

and

.

Definition 4.5
.

The

of a group

is defined recursively by
G_{1}=G
and
G_{k+1}=[G_{k},G]
.

We give several well-known properties of commutators and their behavior modulo lower central series quotients. Many of these are grouped together in
[

, Theorem 5.1]
as the Witt-Hall identities.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

[

.

(2)

(3)

(4)

[

[a,bc]=[a,c][a,b][[a,b],c]

(5)

[

(6)

[

(7)

7.
Links with large homotopy trivializing number

We have shown that any
n
-component link
L
with vanishing linking numbers can be reduced to a homotopy trivial link in
(n-1)(n-2)
crossing changes. What needs further investigation is the sharpness of this bound. More precisely, for
n>4
we do not know whether there exists an
n
-component link
L
with vanishing pairwise linking numbers and
n_{h}(L)=(n-1)(n-2)
. In this section, we make partial progress on this problem by exhibiting a sequence of links whose homotopy trivializing numbers grow quadratically in the number of components.

Since the proof technique is different from what we have done so far in this paper, we begin by proving the following proposition. While it is a weaker result than our main result which we will later prove (Theorem
7.2

n

Proposition 7.1

Let

and

-component link with vanishing pairwise linking numbers and which satisfies that no

Proof.

Let

and

and letting

be the complement of

Since every 3-component sublink

Proof of Lemma
7.6
.

We open with a special case. Suppose that there are three vertices
p,q,r
with
\operatorname{wt}(pq)=\operatorname{wt}(pr)=\operatorname{wt}(qr)=0
. It follows then that for any
s\in V(G)\setminus\{p,q,r\}
,
\operatorname{wt}(ps)+\operatorname{wt}(qs)+\operatorname{wt}(rs)\geq 3
, and so

d(p)+d(q)+d(r)=\sum_{s\notin\{p,q,r\}}\operatorname{wt}(ps)+\operatorname{wt}(qs)+\operatorname{wt}(rs)\geq 3(n-3).

In particular, then, the average of
d(p),d(q),d(r)
is at least
n-3
which is at least as large as
\frac{2}{3}n-\frac{4}{3}
as long as
n\geq 5
.
Thus, we may assume that no such triple
\{p,q,r\}
of vertices connected by weight 0 edges exists.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
d(v)<\frac{2}{3}n-\frac{4}{3}
for every vertex
v
. For any vertex
v
, set

N_{v}=\{x\in V(G)\mid\operatorname{wt}(xv)=0\}.

Since
d(v)<\frac{2}{3}n-\frac{4}{3}
, it follows that
|N_{v}|\geq(n-1)-d(v)>\frac{1}{3}n+\frac{1}{3}
. Finally, note that if
x,y\in N_{v}
and
\operatorname{wt}(xy)=0
, then
v,x,y
spans a triangle whose every edge has weight 0, putting us in the situation addressed at the start of the proof. Thus,
\operatorname{wt}(xy)\geq 1
for every
x,y\in N_{v}
.

We claim that there must exist
some
x,y\in N_{v}
with
\operatorname{wt}(xy)=1
.
Indeed, suppose
\operatorname{wt}(xy)\geq 2
for every
x,y\in N_{v}
. For any
x\in N_{v}
, if we sum up only the weights of edges between
x
and elements of
N_{v}
we get
d(x)\geq\sum_{y\in N_{v}\setminus\{x\}}\operatorname{wt}(xy)\geq 2(|N_{v}|-1)>\frac{2}{3}n-\frac{4}{3}
, contradicting the assumption that
d(x)<\frac{2}{3}n-\frac{4}{3}
for every vertex
x
.

Thus, there exists some
p,q\in N_{v}
such that
\operatorname{wt}(pq)=1
. Notice
v\in N_{p}\cap N_{q}
. If
u\in N_{p}\cap N_{q}\setminus\{v\}
, consider
the graph spanned by
p,q,u,v
to see that

3\leq\operatorname{wt}(p,q)+\operatorname{wt}(v,u)+\operatorname{wt}(v,p)+\operatorname{wt}(u,p)+\operatorname{wt}(v,q)+\operatorname{wt}(u,q)=1+\operatorname{wt}(u,v)

and hence
\operatorname{wt}(u,v)\geq 2
.

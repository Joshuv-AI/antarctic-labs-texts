Hereditary classes of graphs and matroids with finitely many exclusions
Jagdeep Singh, Vaidy Sivaraman

Abstract
. A class 𝒢 \mathcal{G} of graphs closed under taking induced subgraphs is called hereditary. We denote by 𝒢 add \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}} the class of graphs at most one edge addition away from 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , and by 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} the class of graphs at most one edge deletion away. We previously showed that if 𝒢 \mathcal{G} has finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs, then so does the hereditary class 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} . In this paper, we prove the corresponding result for 𝒢 add \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}} . Consequently, we show that the class of graphs within a fixed number of vertex deletions, edge deletions, and edge additions from 𝒢 \mathcal{G} also has finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs provided that 𝒢 \mathcal{G} does. We also present the binary and ternary matroid analogue of this result. Additionally, if 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is closed under complementation, the forbidden induced subgraphs of 𝒢 add \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}} and 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} are complements of each other. We provide explicit lists of the forbidden induced subgraphs for 𝒢 add \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}} , and consequently for 𝒢 epex \mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}} , when 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is the class of split graphs, cographs, or threshold graphs. Following Gyárfás’s framework, we introduce ( p , q ) (p,q) -edge-split graphs, analogous to his ( p , q ) (p,q) -split graphs, and prove they have finitely many forbidden induced subgraphs.

2.
Almost-hereditary classes

We omit the proof of the following straightforward observation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For a hereditary class of graphs
\mathcal{G}
, the edge-add class
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
is hereditary.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs, and let
G
be a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
. If
G
contains two forbidden induced subgraphs
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
for
\mathcal{G}
such that
V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})
is empty or
G[V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})]
is a clique, then
V(G)=V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose not, and let
x
be a vertex in
V(G)-(V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2}))
. Then
G-x
is not in
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
, a contradiction. It follows that
V(G)=V(H_{1})\cup V(H_{2})
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs, and let
G
be a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
. If a forbidden induced subgraph
H
for
\mathcal{G}
is contained in

, then for every non-edge
h
of
H
, there is a subset
F_{h}
of

such that
(G+h)[F_{h}]
is a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
. Moreover,
V(G)=V(H)\cup\bigcup_{h\in E(\overline{H})}F_{h}
.

Proof.

Observe that for every non-edge
h
of
H
, the graph
G+h
is not in
\mathcal{G}
so there is a subset
F_{h}
of
V(G)
such that
(G+h)[F_{h}]
is a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}
. If there is a vertex
x
of
G
not in
V(H)\cup\bigcup_{h\in E(\overline{H})}F_{h}
, then
G-x
is not in
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
V(G)=V(H)\cup\bigcup_{h\in E(\overline{H})}F_{h}
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is immediate from the following.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs, and let
\mathcal{H}
be the set of its forbidden induced subgraphs. For a graph
H
in
\mathcal{H}
, let

and

be the number of vertices and the number of non-edges of

, respectively, and let

denote the maximum number of vertices in a graph in

. If

is a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
, and

is finite, then
|V(G)|\leq

\max\limits_{H\in\mathcal{H}}{\{2m,c+k(m-2)\}}
. In particular, if
G
contains

, then
|V(G)|\leq

\max{\{c+m,c+k(m-2)\}}
.

Proof.

Observe that
G
contains a forbidden induced subgraph

for
\mathcal{G}
. By Lemma
2.3
, it follows that
V(G)=V(H)\cup\bigcup_{h\in E(\overline{H})}F_{h}
, where

is as described in Lemma
2.3
. If
|V(H)\cap F_{h}|\leq 1
for some

in

, then

is a forbidden induced subgraph for

The

Proposition 2.6

Let

Forbidden induced subgraphs for edge-add split graphs and edge-add threshold graphs

The authors in

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hereditary class of graphs that is closed under complementation. Then

is a forbidden induced subgraph for

is a forbidden induced subgraph for

Proof.

It is clear that

is a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{add}}
if and only if (i)

is not in
\mathcal{G}
, (ii) for every edge
e
of
\overline{H}
, the graph
H+e
is not in
\mathcal{G}
, and (iii) for every vertex
v
of
H
, there is an edge
f
of
\overline{H}-v
such that
(H-v)+f
is in
\mathcal{G}
.

Since
\mathcal{G}
is closed under complementation, it is equivalent that (i)
\overline{H}
is not in
\mathcal{G}
, (ii) for every edge
e
of
\overline{H}
, the graph
\overline{H}-e
is not in
\mathcal{G}
, and (iii) for every vertex
v
of
\overline{H}
, there is an edge
f
of
\overline{H}-v
such that

is in
\mathcal{G}
. Therefore,
\overline{H}
is a forbidden induced subgraph for
\mathcal{G}^{\mathrm{epex}}
.
∎

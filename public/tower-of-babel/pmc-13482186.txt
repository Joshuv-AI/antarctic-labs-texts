Strain rate effect on the mechanical properties and microstructure of AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) high-entropy alloy
Authors: Chi-Feng Lin, Wen-Nan Wang, Ching-Li Hsieh, Tao-Hsing Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) (x = 10, 12, 14 at%) high-entropy alloys with varying Al and Ti contents were prepared to investigate the effects of the element composition on the phase structure, mechanical properties, and fracture morphology of the alloy. Quasi-static compression and dynamic impact tests were performed to examine the mechanical properties and fracture behaviors of the alloy under different strain rates. The results showed that all three alloys had dual-phase FCC/BCC structures. Among them, the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy exhibited the highest yield strength under both quasi-static and dynamic conditions. However, the Al12Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti8 and Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 alloys showed better ductility. The fracture morphology observations revealed that all the alloys developed a fish-scale-like ductile dimple structure after quasi-static compression fracture. In contrast, under dynamic impact loading, the alloys formed tearing-type ductile dimples and cleavage planes, indicating a significant reduction in ductility at higher strain rates.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55755-8.

Introduction

In recent years, high-performance structural materials, particularly advanced metallic alloys, have attracted significant attention in materials research for weight-sensitive engineering applications1,2. Metallic materials, in particular, combine the advantages of strength and plasticity, making them ideal for demanding structural sectors, such as aerospace components, automotive frameworks, and high-speed transportation systems. The main metals used as load-bearing materials in these industries include titanium-based alloys, high-strength steels, advanced aluminum alloys. Titanium-based alloys are particularly common in aerospace and high-end automotive applications because of their high corrosion resistance, high durability, and superior strength-to-density ratio. High-entropy alloys (HEAs) typically crystallize into simple solid-solution structures, such as face-centered cubic (FCC), body-centered cubic (BCC), or a combination of both. The relative proportions of these phases, as well as the resulting mechanical properties, can be effectively tuned by adjusting the elemental composition3–8.

HEAs are typically composed of five or more principal elements, with each element having an atomic percentage ranging from 5 to 35%. Trace elements can also be added as needed, with typical additions including boron, magnesium, zirconium, and so on9–11. The unique composition and structure of HEAs have attracted widespread attention in materials science and engineering. In particular, the multi-element composition, high solid solubility, and dispersed lattice defects of HEAs endow them with many favorable properties compared with traditional alloy systems, including superior mechanical performance, improved corrosion resistance, and enhanced high-temperature stability. Thus, HEAs are regarded as an extremely promising approach for the development of new materials and advanced structural applications12–14.

Research into HEAs has made significant progress in recent years, and many studies have been conducted to explore new alloy compositions and develop new alloy systems with improved structural control and an optimized performance15. However, despite some remarkable achievements, the fundamental nature of HEAs remains a challenging research field.

The present study focuses on AlCrFeNiTi HEA as the research object, primarily because CoCrFeNi-based HEAs, although relatively stable and low cost, have a high element density. Specifically, the theoretical density of an equiatomic CoCrFeNi alloy is approximately 8.22 g/cm3 due to the presence of heavy elements like Cobalt. Such high density significantly reduces the specific strength (strength-to-weight ratio) of the material, these high-density elements increase the weight of the alloy system, thereby limiting its usefulness for weight-sensitive structural applications16–19. Furthermore, the presence of Cobalt in traditional HEAs raises concerns regarding long-term biocompatibility due to potential metal-ion toxicity and hypersensitivity. Accordingly, in the present study, the heavy Co element is replaced with lower-density Al and Ti elements to effectively reduce the alloy density to approximately 6.86 g/cm3, thereby enhancing its specific strength. In addition, Al and Ti serve as strong BCC stabilizers, facilitating a transition from a single-phase FCC structure to a high-strength dual-phase (FCC + BCC) microstructure, which is essential for high-performance components subjected to dynamic impact loading. AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) (x = 10, 12, 14 at%) HEAs with different Al and Ti contents are prepared to investigate the effects of the element composition on the phase structure, mechanical properties, and fracture morphology. Quasi-static compression and dynamic impact tests are performed to examine the mechanical properties and fracture morphologies of the alloy under different strain rates.

Methodology

The elemental raw materials (purity > 99.9%) were melted in a vacuum arc-melting (VAM) furnace under an argon atmosphere. The molten alloy was then cast into water-cooled copper molds at −5 °C using a vacuum suction casting method to produce AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) (x = 10, 12, 14 at%) HEA bars with a diameter of 5 mm and length of 50–52 mm. The compositions and crystal structures of the various bars were identified using energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis, respectively.

To quantify the relative volume fractions of the FCC and BCC phases, the XRD patterns were analyzed using the direct comparison method20. The integrated intensities I of the primary diffraction peaks, specifically the (111) plane for the FCC phase and the (110) plane for the BCC phase, were measured. Results and discussion

X-ray diffraction analysis

The chemical compositions of the prepared alloys were verified using energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), as summarized in Table 1. The actual elemental concentrations closely match their respective nominal values, confirming the compositional accuracy and homogeneity of the investigated alloy samples. Owing to the high-entropy effect, HEAs tend to form simple solid-solution phases, mainly consisting of face-centered cubic (FCC) and body-centered cubic (BCC) structures. The phase structures of the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys were examined by XRD, with the results verified using the database of the Joint Committee on Powder Diffraction Standards (JCPDS) and related literature21,22. Figure 2 shows the XRD patterns of the three alloys. For the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy, the pattern is characterized by a high-intensity FCC(111) reflection. However, as the Al content increases from 10 to 14 at.%, a significant evolution in the phase constitution is observed. Specifically, the intensity of the BCC(110) reflection increases markedly, while the FCC(111) reflection progressively diminishes. This distinct shift in relative peak intensities reflects a fundamental transition from an FCC-dominant structure to a BCC-dominant structure. The observed trend is consistent with the quantitative phase fraction results detailed in Sect. “Microstructural analysis and strengthening mechanisms”, where the BCC volume fraction increases significantly from 27.1% (Al = 10 at%) to 75.9% (Al = 14 at%). Figures 3, 4, 5 show the stress–strain curves of the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys under quasi-static compression conditions. For all three alloys, the stress increases slightly with an increasing strain rate. The Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 sample undergoes fracture (marked with an ‘X’ in Fig. 3) at a compressive strain of approximately 0.3, exhibiting a relatively lower plastic strain capacity compared to the other two alloys. Strain rate sensitivity

The stress–strain curves presented in the previous section show that the flow stress of all the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys is higher under dynamic impact conditions than under quasi-static compression. Moreover, for all the alloys, the flow stress increases with an increasing strain rate. To more clearly visualize the relationships among the strain rate, flow stress, and strain, the flow stresses corresponding to two fixed strains were plotted against the strain rate, as shown in Figs. 11 and 12 for quasi-static and dynamic deformation conditions, respectively. The results show that for a constant strain, the flow stress increases with an increasing strain rate for all three alloys. Microhardness

The microhardness represents the resistance of a material to local plastic deformation and is one of the most important mechanical properties of HEAs. Furthermore, the Vickers hardness has the advantage of being independent of the applied load. In the present study, the hardness of the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys was measured using a Vickers microhardness tester, which applies a smaller load and produces a smaller indent size. It is thus more suitable for thin specimens or small-sized materials. The micro-indentation tests were performed under an applied load of 0.5 kgf and a dwell time of 10s. Each alloy was measured ten times, and the average hardness was taken as a representative value. The corresponding results are presented in Fig. 15. The results are consistent with the strength tendencies reported in Sect. “Results and discussion” and “Methodology”. In particular, the Vickers hardness reduces as the Al content increases. Thus, among the three alloys, the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy exhibits the highest Vickers hardness of 670 HV.Fig. 15Vickers hardness measurements of AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys.

SEM fractography analysis

Figure 16 illustrates the fracture surface morphologies of the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy under quasi-static compression conditions. The specimen displays a characteristic fish-scale-like ductile dimple morphology across all evaluated quasi-static strain rates. This fracture feature confirms that despite fracturing at a lower strain of approximately 0.3 compared to the Al12Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti8 and Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 alloys, the failure nature of the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy under quasi-static loading is fundamentally ductile rather than a sudden brittle failure. For the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 samples tested under dynamic loading, the corresponding fracture surfaces are presented in Fig. 17. With the introduction of high dynamic strain rates, the morphology transforms from fish-scale-like dimples into a tearing-type dimple structure accompanied by smooth cleavage planes. As the dynamic strain rate increases, a greater fraction of cleavage facets is observed, indicating a transition toward a mixed ductile–brittle fracture mode. Nonetheless, the presence of tearing-type ductile dimples in Fig. 17 demonstrates that the alloy still retains a certain degree of plastic deformation capability under dynamic impact, which corresponds to the dynamic fracture strain of approximately 0.12 observed in the stress–strain curves.Fig. 16Fracture surface morphologies of Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy under quasi-static deformation. Microstructural analysis and strengthening mechanisms

Based on a comparison with established literature26, the SEM microstructures of the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys (Figs. 18, 19, 20) confirm a dual-phase constitution. These observations are in excellent agreement with the XRD patterns in Fig. 2. In the SEM images, the black petal-like structures are identified as the BCC phase, while the white dendritic regions correspond to the FCC phase. As the Aluminum (Al) content increases from 10 to 14 at.%, the BCC structures exhibit noticeable growth in size and volume fraction, a trend further substantiated by our quantitative XRD analysis.Fig. 18Surface morphology of Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy.Fig. 19Surface morphology of Al12Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti8 alloy.Fig. 20Surface morphology of Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 alloy.

Specifically, the BCC volume fraction rises significantly from 27.1% (Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10) to 75.9% (Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6). Traditionally, an increase in the harder BCC phase is expected to enhance the overall strength of the alloy. However, the mechanical results in Sect. 3.3 reveal a counterintuitive trend: Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 exhibits the highest hardness, yield strength, and strain rate sensitivity (SRS), despite having the lowest BCC fraction. This suggests that the mechanical response of this system is not primarily governed by the macro-scale phase fraction, but rather by the atomic-scale lattice distortion induced by Titanium (Ti).

Titanium possesses the largest atomic radius (1.47 Å) among the constituent elements (Al: 1.43 Å, Cr: 1.25 Å, Fe: 1.24 Å, Ni: 1.25 Å). The higher concentration of Ti in the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy (10 at.%) creates a more severe lattice strain field within the solid solution matrix27,28. This is experimentally supported by the increased Full Width at Half Maximum (FWHM) of the XRD diffraction peaks for the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy, indicating a higher level of micro-strain compared to the Al12Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti8 and Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 alloys.

According to the thermally activated dislocation theory29, these Ti-induced localized strain fields act as potent short-range obstacles to dislocation motion. The dense field of obstacles in Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 significantly reduces the activation volume (ν*), thereby leading to a higher SRS index and superior flow stress. As the composition shifts toward Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 (where Ti decreases to 6 at.%), the reduction in Ti-induced lattice “stiffness” alleviates the pinning effect on dislocations. Consequently, while the Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 alloy benefits from a high BCC fraction that prevents fracture up to a strain of 0.48, its overall strength and SRS are lower than those of Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10. These findings underscore that in the Al-Cr-Fe-Ni-Ti system, composition-driven lattice distortion is a more dominant strengthening mechanism than the relative phase fraction.

Conclusion

This study investigated the mechanical property of AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) (x = 10, 12, 14 at%) HEAs with different Al and Ti contents under different strain rates. The XRD analysis results and microstructural observations showed that all three AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys had a dual-phase FCC/BCC structure, consisting of petal-like BCC phase and dendritic FCC phase. As the Al content increased and the Ti content decreased, the grain size of the BCC phase increased. The quasi-static compression and dynamic impact tests revealed that the yield strength and flow stress of all the alloys increased with an increasing strain rate, particularly under dynamic loading conditions. Among the three alloys, the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy exhibited the highest strength, with yield stresses of 1960 MPa under quasi-static compression and 2277 MPa under dynamic impact. However, the Al12Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti8 and Al14Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti6 alloys with higher Al content and lower Ti content showed better ductility under both impact conditions. All three high-entropy alloys showed a positive strain rate sensitivity. The increase in the flow stress with an increasing strain was particularly apparent under dynamic loading conditions, indicating that the strain rate strengthening effect was more pronounced at higher strain rates. The Vickers microhardness of the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys decreased with an increasing Al content. Among the three alloys, the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy exhibited the highest Vickers hardness of 670 HV. Under quasi-static deformation conditions, the Al10Cr30Fe30Ni20Ti10 alloy showed a fish-scale-like ductile dimple fracture morphology. As the strain rate increased, the dimple size increased and the dimple features became sparser. Under dynamic conditions, the alloys showed a tearing-type ductile dimple morphology with smooth cleavage planes. The proportion of smooth cleavage planes increased with an increasing strain rate, indicating a loss in ductility. In summary, as the Al content increased and the Ti content decreased, the AlxCr30Fe30Ni20Ti(20-x) alloys exhibited better ductility but a lower strength.

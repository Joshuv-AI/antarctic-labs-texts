Preoperative antibiotics for mild or moderate acute cholecystitis (POMMAC): study protocol for a non-inferiority, open-label, randomized controlled trial

Introduction

Acute cholecystitis frequently affects the adult population—3–6% of adults presenting to emergency rooms with abdominal pain are diagnosed with acute cholecystitis, and in crude numbers, over 200,000 patients annually suffer from acute cholecystitis in the USA [1–3]. Apart from emergency cholecystectomy, patients with acute cholecystitis receive preoperative antibiotics with the intent of mitigating inflammation—thereby possibly reducing both the difficulty of cholecystectomy and the prevalence of postoperative infections [4–6]. However, global overuse of antibiotics is associated with rising antibiotic resistance leading to higher mortality, prolonged hospital stay, and excess healthcare costs [7, 8]. Furthermore, around 10% of patients report adverse reactions to antibiotics [9, 10].

Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on postoperative antibiotics have found no benefit in continuing antibiotics postoperatively in mild or moderate acute cholecystitis [11–14], yet there is no strong prospective evidence regarding the effect of preoperative antibiotics. RCTs on preoperative antibiotics have compared a single-dose of preoperative prophylaxis with no prophylaxis, and have either reached mixed results or been severely underpowered to demonstrate a significant difference in outcomes [15–18]. No trials comparing preoperative continuous antibiotics with a single prophylactic dose in acute cholecystitis have been planned or conducted.

Objectives

The aim of this randomized controlled trial is to determine whether a single preoperative dose of antibiotics in patients with mild or moderate acute cholecystitis is non-inferior to continuous preoperative antibiotics in terms of postoperative infections. Patients with diagnosed mild or moderate acute cholecystitis will be evaluated. Consenting patients with no exclusion criteria are randomized at a 1:1 ratio to receive either a single preoperative dose of antibiotics or continuous preoperative antibiotics. The rates of postoperative infections within 30 days from surgery are compared.

Methods

Design, setting, and study population

This is a randomized, controlled, open-label non-inferiority trial taking place at three different hospitals, all part of Helsinki University Hospital in Finland. Currently, patients awaiting cholecystectomy for acute cholecystitis are given continuous preoperative antibiotics. Patients participating in the study will be randomized to receive either continuous or a single dose of preoperative antibiotics (Fig. 1). We drafted the study protocol according to the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials Statement (SPIRIT) [19]. The participant timeline is presented in Table 2.

The exclusion criteria are (1) age < 18 years, (2) antibiotics less than 72 h before randomization, (3) pregnancy, (4) peritonitis, (5) suspicion of cholangitis (Charcot’s triad or radiologic findings), (6) concomitant bile duct stones, (7) allergy to all antibiotic options in the protocol, (8) Tokyo Guidelines grade III cholecystitis (Table 4), (9) breastfeeding women, (10) subjects with reduced capacity for self-determination, and (11) class D drug interactions between the study antibiotic and the patient’s medication (Supplementary Table 1).

Randomization and intervention

Consenting eligible participants will be randomized at a 1:1 ratio to the control (continuous antibiotics) and experimental (single dose prophylaxis) groups. The randomization sequence is generated using Blockrand 1.5 package in R [21] with variable block sizes (4,6 or 8). The randomization sequence will be stratified for the severity of cholecystitis (mild or moderate) and the treating hospital. Study data is collected and randomization is done using REDCap electronic data capture tools hosted at Helsinki University Hospital [22, 23] The treating physician will give potential participants written information on the study and obtain written consent (Supplementary material). The treating physician performs randomization on consenting participants meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria in REDCap and administers antibiotics accordingly. The study is open-label, since placebo administration in an acute care setting would be infeasible.

For participants randomized to continuous antibiotics, the intravenous (i.v.) antibiotics are started as soon as possible, preferably within 1 h from randomization, and treatment continues until surgery with normal treatment doses. The first line of antibiotics is a 2nd generation cephalosporin administered in regard to renal function (e.g., cefuroxime 1.5 g every 8 h). Participants with cephalosporine allergy will receive suitable alternative broad-spectrum antibiotics (ciprofloxacin 400 mg every 12 h). Both randomization groups will receive a single-dose of a 2nd generation cephalosporine (e.g., cefuroxime 1.5 g i.v.) or alternative broad-spectrum antibiotics in case of allergy (ciprofloxacin 400 mg i.v.) at anesthesia induction, preferably 15–60 min before incision. Participants known to carry Extended Spectrum Beta-Lactamase (ESBL) bacteria will receive ertapenem 1 g i.v. every 24 h. Participants in the control group administered ertapenem, will receive the first dose immediately, and the next dose 24 h later, and the participants in the experimental group will receive the first dose at anesthesia induction. All doses are adjusted according to renal function (Supplementaty Table 2). (The rationale for the choice of antibiotic is presented in Supplementary Fig. 1.) All participating hospitals use the same antibiotic strength, and the medication is sourced from the hospital pharmacy.

Antibiotics may be initiated for the experimental group preoperatively should they show signs of severe cholecystitis, peritonitis, or other infections requiring antibiotics (e.g., bacteremia, pneumonia). These participants are still included in the study for intention-to-treat and post-hoc analysis. Postoperatively, antibiotics are discontinued in the absence of severe inflammation (e.g., gangrenous cholecystitis, pericholecystic abscess, hepatic abscess, biliary peritonitis, emphysematous cholecystitis). Iatrogenic intraoperative perforation of the gallbladder does not indicate continuation of antibiotics. Treatment of postoperative infections with antibiotics is allowed any time after randomization.

The aim is to perform cholecystectomy within 24 h from randomization, but patients might have to wait longer depending on surgical resources. A waiting time over 24 h does not constitute a protocol violation. The operating surgeon decides on the surgical approach (laparoscopic/primary open/conversion). Surgeons are recommended to obtain bile cultures should they aspirate bile from the gallbladder intraoperatively.

Patients are discharged based on their clinical condition and at the discretion of the treating physician. If patients in either randomization group develop postoperative infections, antibiotic therapy is continued or started according to normal standard care. All events are recorded.

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes are (1) the conversion rate from laparoscopic to open cholecystectomy during the primary operation, (2) the Comprehensive Complication Index (CCI) according to the Clavien-Dindo Classification [25, 26] within 30 days postoperatively, (3) a composite outcome rate incorporating hypersensitivity reactions, clostridium difficile diarrhea, and yeast infections within 30 days postoperatively, and (4) the length of stay (LOS).

Sample size calculation

We hypothesized that a single dose of preoperative antibiotics for patients with mild or moderate acute cholecystitis would not be inferior in terms of postoperative infections compared with continuous preoperative antibiotic treatment. Based on previous studies on patients with mild or moderate acute cholecystitis treated with preoperative antibiotics (postoperative antibiotics omitted) a weighted mean of 11% was chosen as the expected rate of postoperative infections for the “experimental group” [11–13, 16, 17]. In our previous study on patients with all grades of cholecystitis treated in 2013–2016 with continuous preoperative antibiotics, 7.8% of patients developed postoperative infections [27]. Hence, 7.8% was chosen as the expected rate for the “control group.” To demonstrate a clinically relevant difference, a non-inferiority margin of 10% was chosen. Assuming a statistical power of 80%, with a one-sided 95% CI, 456 participants need to be randomized.

Endpoint analysis

This is a non-inferiority trial. Analyses are performed on an intention-to-treat basis. Subgroup analyses on mild and moderate cholecystitis and treating hospital are performed for both the primary and secondary outcome.

For the primary outcome, the difference of rates of postoperative infections between both arms is calculated with a 95% CI using the traditional Wald method. Non-inferiority is demonstrated if the one-sided 95% CI of the difference of the primary endpoint rates between the two arms is lower than the selected non-inferiority margin (10%). No previous RCTs comparing continuous antibiotics with a single dose have previously been conducted, and hence the non-inferiority margin could not be based on previous studies. However, using a 10% non-inferiority margin is common in antibiotic trials, and in their RCT comparing a single-dose with no prophylaxis Braak et al. used a 10% non-inferiority limit [16, 28]. For the secondary outcomes between the two arms, rates of conversion and the composite outcome of adverse reactions to antibiotics will be evaluated using the chi-square test. The CCI and the LOS will likely be non-normally distributed and therefore analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Furthermore, the standard deviation will be reported using a mirror histogram.

The effect size (continuous vs. single dose antibiotics) for the primary outcome and the categorical secondary outcomes will be reported as a stratified risk ratio. The effect size for the continuous secondary outcome (CCI) will be reported using Wilcoxon’s effect size (r = Z/√N) without a 95% CI.

Interim analysis

An interim analysis will be performed halfway through the study when 230 patients have been recruited. To determine the potential futility of a single dose of antibiotics compared with continuous antibiotics, the primary endpoint (postoperative infections) will be compared between the two groups. Demonstrating non-inferiority of a single dose compared with continuous antibiotics is unlikely if the single dose group has a 15 percentage points higher incidence of postoperative infections halfway through the study, and hence the study will be prematurely terminated if such a difference emerges. The study may also be terminated due to slow recruitment.

Schedule

Approximately 900 patients annually undergo cholecystectomy for acute cholecystitis in the study hospitals, and recruitment is estimated to take around 2 years. The study ends when the 456th randomized patient has completed their follow-up questionnaire 30 days postoperatively from surgery.

Data collection and storage

All study data are collected using REDCap. Before randomization, data on inclusion and exclusion criteria are collected. Personal identification numbers, email and data on the diagnosis and severity of inflammation are collected at randomization. Around 7 days after randomization, data on the surgery, postoperative antibiotic treatment, discharge, and immediate severe adverse reactions to study antibiotics are recorded. Patient records are analyzed at 30–40 days from randomization to collect data on both primary and secondary outcomes. Furthermore, an electronic questionnaire to participants is sent at 30 days postoperatively (Supplementary material). A study nurse calls the participants, who do not answer the questionnaire, and records the answers over phone.

All patient data in the study are confidential, and only members of the study group have access to the data. Participants will be assigned a study ID. Collected data and essential study documents will be stored in a data-safe environment for 25 years from the end of the study after which it will be disposed of properly.

Only personal data necessary for the study are stored in the research register. The identity of the participants is known only by the research staff, who are bound by confidentiality. All information collected on patients in the study is coded after data collection, so patient information cannot be identified from research results, reports, or publications related to the study. The name and personal identification number are removed and replaced with a unique code. After this, information about patients cannot be identified without a code key, which is the responsibility of the study executor. Persons outside the study do not have access to the code key. The research results are analyzed after data coding. The data protection officer of the registrar Helsinki University Hospital will be consulted in case of data security breach to mitigate possible adverse effects.

Monitoring and adverse events

The safety and tolerability of the antibiotics used will be evaluated throughout the study based on adverse events, laboratory values, vital signs, and physical exam findings. Participants must discontinue the investigational antibiotics in case of suspicion of confirmed severe adverse events.

Participants have the right to voluntarily discontinue study treatment or withdraw from the study at any time for any reason without any consequences. The participant is not obliged to disclose the reason for discontinuation. The investigator has the right to discontinue a participant from study treatment or withdraw a subject from the study at any time if it is in the best interest of the participant. For injury caused by a study drug, compensation is sought from the Finnish Insurance Company for Pharmaceutical Injury Indemnities. For personal injury caused by care unrelated to the study, compensation should be sought from the study center’s patient insurance.

As the study is a low-intervention trial, a data safety monitoring board has not been appointed. The drugs used in the study are authorized, well-characterized, and are not being used off-label. Serious adverse events and suspected serious adverse events related to the study antibiotics are reported to the Finnish Medicines Agency. A safety report is annually submitted to the Finnish Medicines Agency.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this would be the first RCT comparing the effect of continuous preoperative antibiotics with a single-dose antibiotic on postoperative infections. Several RCTs have concluded that postoperative antibiotics do not lower the prevalence of postoperative infections [11–14], but the conclusions on preoperative antibiotics compared with no antibiotic prophylaxis have been mixed [15–18]. Nevertheless, unnecessary preoperative antibiotics need to be omitted in favor of reduced allergic reactions, reduced costs, reduced antibiotic-related diarrheas, and rising antibiotic resistance. Continuous preoperative antibiotics for cholecystitis, however, may hinder the progression from mild or moderate to severe cholecystitis and hence reduce rates of postoperative infections, conversion, and postoperative complications. To detect such a correlation, a safety analysis will be performed halfway through the study, and the study may be prematurely terminated should the safety analysis demonstrate a significantly higher rate of postoperative infections with a single prophylactic dose compared with continuous antibiotics. The lack of blinding is a limitation of the study.

Trial status

Recruitment began on August 25th, 2025, and is estimated to take around 2 years (estimated end of recruitment August 24th, 2027). The results will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and the results will be reported according to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials Statement [29].

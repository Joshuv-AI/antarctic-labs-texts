Construction of a multi-dimensional predictive model for college students’ academic performance based on deep learning
Authors: Shiran Zeng, Yang Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Academic performance (AP) prediction is crucial for recognizing at-risk students and enhancing learning outcomes. Traditional statistical models often fail to capture temporal and behavioral patterns. Deep learning (DL) approaches offer improved accuracy and adaptability by leveraging multi-dimensional student data for predictive analysis. The objective is to advance a robust predictive model that predicts students’ AP using multi-dimensional data, integrating temporal, behavioral, and demographic features. Students’ learning performance data for n = 2000 is collected from multiple sources, including student grades, attendance, learning management system (LMS) interactions, psychometric surveys, and demographic records. Collected data undergoes preprocessing steps, including handling missing values using K Nearest Neighbor Imputation (KNNI), outlier removal, and normalization. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is employed to decrease dimensionality and extract relevant characteristics from high-dimensional datasets. A novel Gated Long Short-Term Memory Unit is optimized with Dove (GateLSTMU-Dove) to capture temporal dependencies and student engagement patterns. GateLSTMU identifies time-dependent patterns in educational data to support accurate performance forecasting. Dove optimizes model parameters efficiently, enhancing convergence speed and predictive accuracy of the GatedLSTMU. Python 3.10-based experiments demonstrate the model’s superior performance. GateLSTMU-Dove achieved lower error metrics and higher classification accuracy (98.85%) compared to baseline methods. Visualization of predictions confirmed accurate forecasting and interpretable temporal patterns in student performance. The GateLSTMU-Dove effectively predicted academic outcomes using multi-dimensional student data. It provides interpretable insights, supports early intervention strategies, and demonstrates a scalable, reproducible approach for data-driven AP management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-51012-0.

Introduction

The academic performance (AP) of college learners is a significant indicator of the overall superiority of education and growth and success at an institution. It reflects the ability of a learner to gather, integrate, and use knowledge developed within an academic setting while adapting to the intellectual rigor required by higher education1. As a result of the current high-tech, diverse, and globalized state of education, the need to develop an understanding of academic performance among students is greater than ever before. College students today experience numerous challenges that can negatively impact their academic success, such as family obligations, financial limitations, or other factors that relate to their ability to work well in school2.

The performance of higher education learners is traditionally considered an important indicator of academic quality, the development of learners, and the ability of an institution to be successful in achieving its goals with its students3,4. The performance of learners in college demonstrates the ability of the students to learn, integrate, apply, and transfer knowledge across disciplines in response to the needs of higher education, and to meet the demands of today5. An influential change in the evolution of the AP of college learners is the increased complexity associated with higher education, technological advancement, the growth of alternative forms of learning, and the globalization of education6. Because of the increasingly complex environment in which college learners are learning, understanding how AP is affected by the different factors of the college student’s life, and therefore impacted by academic challenges, social challenges, psychological issues, financial stressors is becoming increasingly important for college students’ AP, and therefore their overall success7,8. By identifying and analyzing the various factors that affect learners’ AP, educators and policymakers can create targeted intervention strategies, inclusive teaching methods, and comprehensive support systems to help learners stay engaged in their education, increase their persistence as learners, and become more successful academic performers9. Studying factors that influence students’ academic performance is essential for improving educational practices and supporting learners in achieving their full potential in an increasingly competitive, knowledge-based society10.

The use of traditional Machine Learning (ML) methods to forecast student presentation is limited, as these methods fail to capture the complexity of temporal and behavioural learning trends. This research provides evidence that using robust DL based predictive models, which combine all of the above factors, results in increased accuracy, adaptability, and reliability for predicting how students perform academically early on, so that appropriate intervention can take place.

Research gap

The current studies that have been conducted on predicting the academic performance of students through temporal models, behavioral studies and machine learning (using hybrid models) show good accuracy; however, there are still many significant gaps in the research. Currently, there are very few studies that create model-based predictions from multiple or single institutions because of limited or single institution-data sets thus limiting their generalizability to other educational environments. Improved data integration methods to combine the data from multiple dimensions (academic, behavioral and psychometric), and creating more generalizable and unified frameworks to combine data from multiple dimensions, have not been adequately addressed in the research literature. Furthermore, many current studies fail to develop methods of controlling for class imbalance or assuring proper separation between the training and test sets when training their predictive models potentially leading to overfitting and data leakage issues. Finally, the current literature does not provide adequate research studies detailing how to optimize model performance. In contrast, the proposed GateLSTMU-Dove model incorporates multi-dimensional datasets into a hybrid LSTM-GRU architecture with Dove Optimization to improve learning and model convergence. As a result, the proposed solution improved the generalization of academic performance prediction, reduce prediction error, and provide a more accurate and reliable forecast regarding student performance.

Methodology

This research is aimed at generating a multi-dimensional predictive model design that is complex and advanced to predict the academic performance of college students using the DL technology. The model considers both behavioral, demographic, and temporal data to comprehend more intricate behaviors of student behavior and, therefore, enhance the accuracy of prediction. Supplementary Fig. 1 is a full representation of the research workflow.

Student learning performance dataset

The data used in this research (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/freshersstaff/student-learning-performance-dataset ) consists of 2000 records of students who were observed, and the data stored is representative of key academic, behavioral, and learning indicators with the purpose of studying the trends in student performance. It incorporates the major elements of student behavior, such as attendance, study behavior, levels of engagement, participation in assignments, and demographic data. A row is the representation of a single student having the attributes of learning behavior and involvement in academic activities; the last performance outcome is the target variable. To prevent data leakage and ensure reliable model evaluation, the dataset was first divided into training and testing subsets using an 80:20 ratio resulting in 1,600 samples for training and 400 samples for testing. All preprocessing steps, including KNN-based missing value imputation, Z-score normalization, and PCA, were applied only to the training dataset. The learned preprocessing parameters were subsequently applied to the testing dataset to maintain consistency. This approach ensures that information from the testing data does not influence model training or feature transformation.

Data preparation

First, the raw data undergoes preprocessing processes, and the redundant records of the dataset are eliminated as an outcome of executing a duplicate-removal procedure to make certain that there is no bias in the analysis model because of duplicate records. KNNI is applied as a method of blocking the missing data values based on similar features of the observations. The records that are outliers are eliminated from the dataset, and this makes the analysis model much stronger and more efficient. It is employed to reduce the size of the multi-dimensional student information, but at the same time retain the important patterns by use of PCA.

Duplicate removal

The data is analyzed to determine and eliminate duplicate records that affect the prediction quality of the model negatively. Redundant records in learning record systems occur when the same grade, attendance, or LMS response or survey response is recorded multiple times, and this duplication is eliminated with the help of unique identifiers. The dataset is then narrowed down by removing duplicates, and this makes the data consistent, accurate, and reliable, which gives the GateLSTMU-Dove predictive model a strong basis. Such a delicate cleaning method means that the multi-dimensional features and demographic data are reflected correctly, which allows a proper prediction of the AP of the learners.

Outlier removal

The removal of outliers was done to remove the extreme or anomalous values in the Student Learning Performance Dataset that might affect the training of the model and the predictive performance. Outliers are due to inputting mistakes, student behavior, or failure to record grades, attendance, or interactions over the LMS. Identification and elimination of these anomalies would guarantee that the multi-dimensional features have some real patterns of student performance. This increases the robustness of the model, stabilize training, and increase the predictive accuracy of forecasts provided by the GateLSTMU-Dove model.

Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE): In this case, to counteract the possibility of class imbalance over the dataset, the SMOTE was used in the preprocessing phase. SMOTE uses minority instances to create new minority samples by interpolating between the existing minority instances to gain better class representation. This method aids the model in acquiring representative trends of underrepresented classes and minimizes bias in the majority classes. By default, the proposed academic performance prediction framework is more robust and predictive in the light of the SMOTE application.

GateLSTMU-dove for advanced AP prediction

The GateLSTMU–Dove framework is designed to provide highly accurate AP estimation by combining advanced temporal learning with efficient parameter tuning. GateLSTMU captures deep, evolving, and long-term patterns in multi-dimensional student data, allowing the model to understand behavior and performance changes over time. Dove Optimization enhances this process by improving convergence speed, reducing training error, and preventing the model from getting stuck in local minima.

GateLSTMU for temporal pattern learning and performance prediction

The GateLSTMU is designed to model complex temporal dependencies and evolving relationships in multi-dimensional student data. It adds additional gating mechanisms to enhance the traditional LSTM by allowing the selective capture of important temporal and sequential features. Dove Optimization Algorithm for Efficient Model Parameter Optimization

Despite the traditional optimizers, like Adam and Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), being highly utilized in deep learning models, they are mainly based on gradient-related updates, which might result in early convergence or getting stuck in a local minimum when working with highly nonlinear and multi-dimensional data. When it comes to the issue of predicting academic performance, when temporal, behavioral, and demographic characteristics interrelate in their intricate nature, effective exploration of the parameter space is mandatory. The Dove Optimization algorithm presents an exploration and exploitation balancing mechanism, achieved through the use of a population-based mechanism of search in the process of parameter tuning. This permits the optimizer to determine additional optimum weightings of the GateLSTMU network. Due to this, the proposed GateLSTMU-Dove model successfully attains better convergence stability and predictive accuracy than the traditional optimization methods. Result and discussion

The study is to develop a multi-layered, state-of-the-art, predictive model based on the DL technology, which can correctly predict the academic success of a single college student. The experimental testing was done on an Intel Core i7/i5 processor with 8–16 GB RAM, 256–512 GB SSD memory storage and an NVIDIA graphics card to facilitate faster training. It used python version 3.10 on Windows 10/11 or Linux or the macos. The data 2000 student records of 10 attributes, and the data was separated into training (80%), and testing (20%) of the data to evaluate the models. Supplementary Fig. 5 shows the ranked feature-importance distribution representing the influence of each input variable to the predictive model.

According to the feature-importance analysis provided, the model identified Attendance_Rate (0.178), Prior_GPA (0.164), and Study_Hours_Per_Week (0.152) as having the largest impacts in predicting whether a learner is successful in a particular course or course of study. Based on this analysis, the GateLSTMU-Dove model does an excellent job of identifying important AP and behavioral variables for predicting a learner’s academic success. Despite testing values being slightly lower than training, the model achieves robust generalization, indicating effective learning of multi-dimensional student performance patterns. Supplementary Fig. 7 shows the precision–recall relationships demonstrating class-wise discriminative capability of the predictive model.

The classification performance of classes 1 and 2 is higher than both classes 0 and 2 across the majority of the recall ranges, with class 0’s precision being slightly degraded at a higher recall than class 1 and 2s’. The gateLSTM-Dove has demonstrated to accommodate the various needs for classifying performers for the three classes with the least possible drift into recognized misclassification of classes. Discussion

The predictive capabilities of existing research were relatively strong; however, that have significant limitations. For example, the ANN-based studies, yielded an accuracy rate of 93.81%29, use data from one institution and did not include offline behavior or motivation in developing their estimations, thus limiting their generalizability. In addition, the models were focused on the impact of COVID30,31 were developed entirely from online data, RF classification algorithm, thus limiting the use of these models in hybrid or traditional classroom environments. Other models were developed using the RepTree algorithm32 leverage data that is localized and do not accommodate for temporal sequence learning, which hampered their ability to be scaled across many educational environments. The new GateLSTM-Dove approach being presented in this article resolved all issues by integrating temporal, behavioral, and multi-dimensional predictors for significantly more robust and generalizable predictive capabilities. The GateLSTMU-Dove model was capable of measuring a student at risk or showing signs of needing assistance, providing evidence for the types of interventions to use with that student in any given learning environment. The ability of the model to utilize multiple dimensions of real-world data allowed enhanced decision making within a learning system and the development of long-term planning for student success.

Limitation and future scope

The research’s dependence on a single dataset is a drawback that could limit how broadly the suggested model can be applied. Future research will integrate several datasets from various universities to improve the predictive framework’s robustness, diversity, and practicality. Furthermore, adding cross-domain characteristics and real-time data streams can improve model adaptability even more. Investigating lightweight optimization methods could facilitate effective implementation in educational settings with limited resources.

Conclusion

The research is to build a complete multi-dimensional DL structure for performance forecast of a student’s academic system through the analysis of academic, behavior, and demographical factors. The information set used in this research issourced from a publicly accessible repository provided by Kaggle and contains 2,000 records from individual learners, including their performance data. The record data are restructured using elimination of duplicated entries, substitution of missing entries through KNN imputation method, removal of objectionable items from the dataset, and normalization by Z-score and PCA dimensionality reduction. In addition, a new model is developed and named as GateLSTMU–Dove method that learns from the long-range temporal sequence dependencies present in the data while at the same time optimizing the model’s parameters allowing for more rapid convergence and better overall performance and accuracy. The GateLSTMU–Dove has proven to outperform other current models on these measures, achieving an overall optimal model presentation with a precision of 96.70%, F1 score of 95.52%, a recall of 96.12%, accuracy of 98.85%, an MAE of 0.210, and MASE of 0.1540. The ROC analysis yielded robust AUC values ranging from 0.97 to 0.98 for all classes of data indicating the model’s strong capability to separate among classes.

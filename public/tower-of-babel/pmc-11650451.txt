Articaine buccal infiltration and lidocaine intraosseous anesthesia in carious mandibular molars
Authors: Hinpetch Daungsupawong, Viroj Wiwanitkit
Published: 2024

Dear Editor:

This is a response to the published study entitled, “Retrospective comparison of articaine buccal infiltration and lidocaine intraosseous anesthesia in carious mandibular molars [1].” The researchers evaluated intravenous lidocaine (LIO) with anterior median airbag (ABI) anesthesia for the treatment of mandibular caries. These findings have significant implications in clinical practice. However, numerous concerns require careful consideration. First, the retrospective nature of the analysis raised questions regarding selection bias. How are patients selected for each anesthetic technique? Did variations in operator experience or patient variables influenced the outcomes? Although the sample provided preliminary insights, larger cohorts are required to improve the statistical power and reproducibility of the results.

Furthermore, this method unclearly explained of the measurement of pain levels during anesthesia. What scales or techniques can be used to evaluate pain? Are these assessments conducted in a standardized manner? Detailed information on the pain assessment method improved the accuracy of the results. This study assessed the success rate of anesthesia. However, it does not provide the criteria for defining “success.” Setting specific benchmarks may assist in clarifying these findings and allow for more accurate comparisons in future investigations.

Consider asking questions about the significance of the results. For example, how do you believe that LIO influences patient satisfaction and overall outcomes compared to ABI? Can practitioners consider various factors in different clinical situations? Addressing these questions can help dentists optimize their anesthetic procedures

Furthermore, these findings may allow for additional research on the efficacy and safety of various anesthetic procedures. The mechanisms underlying the differences in pain perception and anesthetic success rates between the LIO and ABI may be investigated in various patient demographics, including those with dental anxiety or specific requirements. These findings may improve our understanding of their clinical applications. Long-term studies should assess the long-term effects of various anesthetic methods, including patient-reported results and patient satisfaction, thereby adding value to this field. Comparing the cost-effectiveness of LIO and ABI may help guide clinical decision-making and healthcare policies, ensuring that dental practitioners use anesthetics effectively and efficiently.

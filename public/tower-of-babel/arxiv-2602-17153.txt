A short note on hereditary Mazur intersection property
Pradipta Bandyopadhyay, Deepak Gothwal

Abstract
. In this note, we prove that hereditary Mazur intersection property (MIP) does not imply Fréchet smoothness using an example by Borwein and Fabian in [ 2 ] .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46B20

1.
Introduction

Definition 1
.

A Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is said to have the
Mazur intersection property (MIP)
if every closed, bounded, and convex set in
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is the intersection of the closed balls containing it.

MIP has been extensively studied over several decades and still continues to draw attention due to its rich geometric influence on the Banach space in question. A complete characterisation of MIP was obtained in
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
. Earlier, Mazur himself
[
6
]
proved that Fréchet smoothness of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
implies MIP. The converse fails even in finite dimensions (see
[
1
, Page 43]
). But, a separable MIP space has separable dual
[
4
, Theorem 2.1 (iii)]
, and hence, is an Asplund space. Therefore, it has a Fréchet smooth renorming
[
3
, Theorem II.3.1]
. Even this connection breaks down in non-separable Banach spaces. The fact that every Banach space can be isometrically embedded in a Banach space with MIP
[
8
]
adds some notoriety to the behaviour of spaces with MIP. In particular, this shows that MIP is not hereditary.

On the other hand, Fréchet smoothness is clearly a hereditary property. Thus, Mazur’s result actually shows that Fréchet smoothness of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
implies that it has the hereditary MIP, that is,
X
and all its subspaces have MIP.

Now, in this form, is the converse of Mazur’s result true? That is, does the hereditary MIP imply Fréchet smoothness? Notice that if
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has the hereditary MIP, then

(
a
)

Every separable subspace of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has MIP, and hence, has separable dual. Therefore,
X
is Asplund
[
10
, Theorem 2.14]
.

(
b
)

(X,\|\cdot\|)
is smooth. To see this, note that for two dimensional spaces, smoothness and MIP are equivalent
[
9
]
. Also, if every two dimensional subspace of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has MIP, and hence, is smooth, then
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is smooth
[
7
, Propositon 5.4.21]
. Hence, our question has an affirmative answer in finite dimensions.

(
c
)

The duality map on
(X,\|\cdot\|)
and all its subspaces are “quasi-continuous”
[
4
, p 114]
.

Thus, it seems natural to conjecture that hereditary MIP implies Fréchet smoothness.

In this note, we obtain some sufficient conditions for hereditary MIP in terms of the set
N(X)
of non-Fréchet smooth points of
S(X)
. In particular, we show that if
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is smooth and
N(X)
is finite, then
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has the hereditary MIP.

Borwein and Fabian in
[
2
, Theorem 4]
proved an interesting result that an infinite dimensional WCG Asplund space
X
admits an equivalent smooth norm
\|\cdot\|
such that
N(X)=\{\pm x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in S(X,\|\cdot\|)
. Thus, our results show that the Borwein-Fabian norm gives a counterexample to the above conjecture.

A preliminary version of this note is contained in the second author’s Ph. D. thesis
[
5
]
written under the supervision of the first author. We would like to thank Prof. Gilles Godefroy for drawing our attention to
[
2
]
.

Any undefined notions may be found in
[
3
]
or
[
7
]
.

2.
Main Results

We consider real Banach spaces only. Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space and
X^{*}
its dual. By a subspace of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
, we mean a closed linear subspace. For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we denote by
B(x,r)

the open ball

\{y\in X:\|x-y\|<r\}
and by
B[x,r]

the closed ball

\{y\in X:\|x-y\|\leq r\}
in
(X,\|\cdot\|)
. We denote by
B(X)
the
closed unit ball

\{x\in X:\|x\|\leq 1\}
and by
S(X)
the
unit sphere

\{x\in X:\|x\|=1\}
.
For
x\in S(X)
, let

D_{X}(x)=\{f\in S(X^{*}):f(x)=1\}.

The multivalued map
D_{X}
is called the
duality map
. Any selection of
D_{X}
is called a
support mapping
.

We say that
x\in S(X)
is a
smooth point
if
D_{X}(x)
is a singleton. A Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
smooth
if every
x\in S(X)
is a smooth point.

We say that
x\in S(X)
is a
Fréchet smooth point
if the duality map
D_{X}
is single-valued and norm-norm continuous at
x
. A Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is
Fréchet smooth
if every
x\in S(X)
is a Fréchet smooth point. Let

\displaystyle F(X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{x\in S(X):\text{the norm is Fr\'{e}chet smooth at }x\}\text{ and}

\displaystyle N(X)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{x\in S(X):\text{the norm is not Fr\'{e}chet smooth at }x\}.

The following necessary or sufficient conditions for MIP follows from
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
and are essentially already observed in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2
.

For a Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
, consider the following statements :

(
a
)

D_{X}(F(X))
is norm dense in
S(X^{*})
.

(
b
)

(X,\|\cdot\|)
has MIP.

(
c
)

The set of extreme points of
B(X^{*})
is norm dense in
S(X^{*})
.

Then
(a)\implies(b)\implies(c)
.

A Banach space
X
is called
Weakly Compactly Generated (WCG)
if there is a weakly compact subset
K
of
X
such that
\overline{span}(K)=X
.

In particular, all reflexive spaces and all separable spaces are WCG.

Borwein and Fabian in
[
2
, Theorem 4]
proved that every infinite dimensional WCG Asplund space
X
admits an equivalent smooth norm
\|\cdot\|
such that
N(X)=\{\pm x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in S(X,\|\cdot\|)
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space such that
N(X)=\{\pm x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in S(X)
. Then a subspace
Y\subseteq X
with dim
Y\geq 2
has MIP if and only if
x_{0}\notin Y
or
D_{Y}(x_{0})
has empty interior (relative to
S(Y^{*})
).

Proof.

Assume that
x_{0}\notin Y
. Then,
Y
is Fréchet smooth, and hence, has MIP
[
6
]
.

Now, assume that
x_{0}\in Y
and
D_{Y}(x_{0})
has empty interior. Note that
N(Y)\subseteq N(X)\cap Y
. We will verify Theorem
2

(a)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in S(Y^{*})
be given. Now,
(B(f,\varepsilon)\cap S(Y^{*}))\setminus D_{Y}(N(Y))
is a nonempty open set in
S(Y^{*})
. By Bishop-Phelps Theorem, there exists
y_{0}\in S(Y)\setminus N(Y)
such that
\|f-f_{y_{0}}\|<\varepsilon
, where
f_{y_{0}}\in D_{Y}(y_{0})
. Now,
y_{0}\notin N(Y)
. So,
y_{0}\in F(Y)
. Hence, by Theorem
2

(a)\implies(b)
,
Y
has MIP.

Conversely, let
Y
has MIP and
x_{0}\in Y
. Suppose
D_{Y}(x_{0})
has a non-empty interior in
S(Y^{*})
. Since
D_{Y}(x_{0})
is a face of
B(Y^{*})
, none of the interior points of
D_{Y}(x_{0})
can be an extreme point of
B(Y^{*})
. Thus, Theorem
2

(c)

does not hold for
Y
. Hence,
Y
cannot have MIP.
∎

Corollary 4
.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|)
be such that
N(X)=\{\pm x_{0}\}
for some
x_{0}\in S(X)
. Then the following are equivalent:

(
a
)

(X,\|\cdot\|)
has the hereditary MIP.

(
b
)

D_{X}(x_{0})
is a singleton.

(
c
)

For every subspace
Y
of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
such that dim
Y\geq 2
and
x_{0}\in Y
,
D_{Y}(x_{0})
has empty interior (relative to
S(Y^{*})
).

Proof.

(a)\implies(b)
. We have already noted that
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has the hereditary MIP implies
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is smooth, and hence,
D_{X}(x_{0})
is a singleton.

(b)\implies(c)
. Let
Y
be a subspace of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
such that dim
Y\geq 2
and
x_{0}\in Y
. Since
D_{X}(x_{0})
is singleton, it follows that
D_{Y}(x_{0})
is also singleton. Hence,
D_{Y}(x_{0})
has empty interior.

(c)\implies(a)
follows from Theorem
3
.
∎

Corollary 5
.

The Borwein-Fabian norm on a WCG Asplund space has hereditary MIP, but is not Fréchet smooth.

In particular, every Banach space with a separable dual, e.g.,
c_{0}
, has an equivalent norm with the hereditary MIP, that is not Fréchet smooth.

Can
N(X)
be larger, retaining hereditary MIP? The following result gives a sufficient condition.

Theorem 6
.

If
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is smooth and
N(X)
is finite, then
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has hereditary MIP. In particular,
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is Asplund.

Proof.

Let
Y
be a subspace of
(X,\|\cdot\|)
. If dim
Y<\infty
or
N(Y)=\emptyset
, then
Y
is Fréchet smooth; hence has MIP.

Now assume that
Y
is infinite dimensional and
N(Y)\neq\emptyset
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
f\in S(Y^{*})
. Since
D_{Y}
is single-valued,
D_{Y}(N(Y))
is finite. So,
A:=(B_{Y}(f,\varepsilon/2)\cap S(Y^{*}))\setminus D_{Y}(N(Y))\neq\emptyset
. Let
g\in A
. Then,
\text{dist}(g,D_{Y}(N(Y)))>0
. Choose
0<\alpha<\min\{\varepsilon/2,\text{dist}(g,D_{Y}(N(Y)))\}
. By Bishop-Phelps theorem, there exist
y\in S(Y)
and
h\in D_{Y}(y)
such that
\|h-g\|<\alpha
. Clearly,
h\notin D_{Y}(N(Y))
. So,
y\notin N(Y)
and hence,
y\in F(Y)
. Also,
\|f-h\|<\varepsilon
. Therefore, by Theorem
2

(a)\implies(b)
,
Y
has MIP.
∎

Question 1
.

Are there examples of Banach spaces satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem
6
, with
N(X)
having more than two points?

Question 2
.

If
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has hereditary MIP, can
N(X)
be infinite?

Question 3
.

If
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is smooth and
N(X)
is compact, can we conclude that
(X,\|\cdot\|)
has hereditary MIP or is at least Asplund?

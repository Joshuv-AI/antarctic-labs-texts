Effect of intelligent hypertension management system on blood pressure: protocol for a randomised controlled multicentre trial
Authors: Haiyang Yu, Xiang Chen, Jiachun Xia, Lei Hou
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHypertension is one of the most serious global health problems, and its prevention and treatment mainly rely on lifestyle intervention and medication. However, the current situation of hypertension control in China is still not ideal. Self-monitoring of blood pressure is expected to be a new way to control hypertension. Intervention and the Intelligent Hypertension Management System (IHMS), an information platform relying on the network and smartphone, may help patients self-monitor their blood pressure at home, allowing for intelligent management of hypertension. The aim of this trial is to investigate whether IHMS can effectively reduce blood pressure in patients with hypertension.Methods and analysisThis is a multiple-centre, prospective, randomised, controlled study. 320 eligible subjects will be randomly divided into the IHMS management group (n=160) and the conventional care group (n=160). Subjects in the IHMS management group will be required to take their blood pressure daily at regular intervals at home and get treatment as directed by the IHMS; the control group will receive conventional treatment. The primary outcome of the trial is the net change in systolic blood pressure at the end point of follow-up after 3 months. The mixed-effects model will be used to compare the primary outcome that there is a greater reduction in blood pressure in the intervention group than in the control group.Ethics and disseminationThe Ethics Committee of Shanghai Tongren Hospital has reviewed and approved the trial protocols, informed consent and subject information. The findings from the study will be disseminated through publications and conference presentations. The findings of the trial will be published in journals and presented at academic conferences.Trial registration numberNCT05526300.

Introduction

Hypertension, as one of the most common chronic diseases in the world, is also a major risk factor for early death from cardiovascular diseases, which seriously endanger people’s health.1 The prevention and treatment of hypertension have become a global health problem. At present, the prevention and treatment of hypertension mainly rely on lifestyle intervention and the rational application of antihypertensive drugs.2–5 Benefiting from reasonable treatment interventions, people’s health status of blood pressure has greatly improved. However, the prevention and treatment of hypertension are still unsatisfactory. A study of 17 countries showed that the prevalence of hypertension was 40.8%, while awareness and control rates were only 46.5% and 32.5%.6 The survey of China shows that the prevalence rate of adult hypertension has reached 23.2%, while the awareness rate and control rate are only 46.9% and 15.3%.7 Poor medication compliance is one of the important reasons why hypertension is difficult to control.8 In recent years, due to the impact of the COVID-19 epidemic, it has been difficult for patients to conduct long-term outpatient follow-ups, which undoubtedly increases the probability of uncontrolled blood pressure in patients. Therefore, it is necessary to explore a new online intelligent hypertension management mode.

The effectiveness of self-monitoring based on mobile communication and the network has been validated in many studies. This monitoring method allows patients to get their blood pressure data in real-time, effectively avoiding the influence of the white-coat effect.9 10 The study by Morawski et al found that the use of short message service (SMS) prompts was effective in improving medication adherence in patients with hypertension,11 which has also been observed in other chronic diseases.12 For example, the popularisation of health education knowledge via mobile phone SMS also effectively reduced cardiovascular disease risk factors.13 In several other studies, patients who received home blood pressure monitoring showed better results.14–16 However, in some studies, the results did not show that remote self-monitoring has better blood pressure-lowering effects, and patients in these studies did not have direct access to immediate feedback from physicians or the system.11 17 In two other studies, patients were asked to record their health data on a mobile app for a long period of time, and in the absence of feedback from physicians, patient adherence gradually declined.18 19

Intelligent Hypertension Management System (IHMS), relying on the intelligent Hypertension Excellence Centre (iHEC), uses advanced communication technologies such as bluetooth, wireless remote networks and the internet to establish a diagnosis and follow-up network connecting multiple detection devices and to build a standardised technology platform. As a new hypertension disease management model, it has great potential for improving the prevention and treatment effects of hypertension. Therefore, we intend to design a study to explore the effect of IHMS on reducing blood pressure in patients with hypertension. In this study, patients upload information such as blood pressure data and medication status to IHMS through smartphones and other devices, and physicians provide treatment guidance and health education after receiving it, realising real-time communication between physicians and patients. And unlike other studies, patients are allowed to receive immediate guidance from physicians and health reports while they self-monitor their blood pressure. On the one hand, treatment guidance can be used as a treatment for patients with uncontrolled blood pressure, and on the other hand, it can be used as a reminder for patients to self-monitor. Health reports can also make patients more aware of their blood pressure and health status, thereby improving patient compliance. In addition, for patients with difficulties in operating smartphones and other devices, a guardian can be appointed in advance to assist them in operation, which makes our study conclusion cover more patients with hypertension. Patient-measured blood pressure data will be automatically uploaded to the system, which is more objective and direct than patient reports or input into the system in previous studies.

The aim of the trial was to determine whether the management of hypertension using IHMS was effective in reducing blood pressure in patients with hypertension.

Methods

Study design

This is a multiple-centre, prospective, randomised, controlled study. Three hundred and twenty eligible patients with hypertension, using a computer randomisation system, will be divided into the IHMS management group (n=160, intervention) and the conventional group (n=160, control). Subjects in the IHMS management group will receive IHMS system operation training after enrollment and will be treated according to system guidance. The control group will receive conventional treatment. The blood pressure of the patients will be uniformly measured at 1 and 3 months of follow-up. Participant recruitment

We will recruit patients in Shanghai, and most of them come from the urban or suburban areas of Shanghai. All subjects will be recruited by Tongren Hospital, affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine or Shanghai Songjiang District Central Hospital. Specialised cardiovascular physicians will recruit suitable patients with hypertension in outpatient and inpatient settings. Two of the recruiting physicians will be research team members. Physicians will select patients with hypertension who meet the inclusion criteria and do not meet the exclusion criteria in the hospital outpatient clinic and complete the informed consent signed in the outpatient clinic after a full assessment of meeting the criteria. Informed consent shall be signed by the participant or his/her guardian.

Sample size

Based on the literature reporting that the intervention group had an average reduction in blood pressure of approximately 5 mm Hg from baseline,13 19 21 we expected the intervention group to have a benefit of approximately 5 mm Hg in lowering mean arterial pressure in patients compared with the control group and 10 mm Hg compared with baseline. The SD of blood pressure in patients with hypertension is 15 mm Hg, based on reports in the literature. Thus, 145 patients should be enrolled in each group under the condition that the significance level (α) is 0.05 and the power of test is 90%, and 160 patients would need to be enrolled in each arm given the 10% dropout rate. Therefore, 320 subjects will be needed for the whole trial.

Intervention

Once eligible patients are enrolled in the study, their baseline data will be collected first. Then they will be randomised to the IHMS management group (n=160) and the conventional care group (n=160). The subjects in the control group will receive conventional management and outpatient follow-up. The subjects in the trial group will be registered with iHEC, receive training on the use of IHMS and then use the attached electronic sphygmomanometer for daily blood pressure monitoring. The training will be conducted face-to-face with the subjects in the office by the physicians (including how to measure blood pressure, connect devices, upload data, get reports, etc). Baseline data collection will be conducted by the relevant physicians. If the patient has difficulty with the operation, a guardian can be appointed in advance to receive training and assist in blood pressure monitoring. Patients in both groups will be followed up for 1 month and 3 months after the start of the study.

Interventions are generally not adjusted after subjects are enrolled. If it is for the treatment of the subject, the doctor may terminate the study without the subject’s consent.

The adherence of trial group patients can be monitored directly through IHMS, and reminders can be made by phone or message if necessary. Patients in the control group are mainly communicated by telephone. There is no prohibited relevant concomitant care or intervention during the trial.

Randomisation and blinding

Three hundred and twenty subjects will be randomly assigned by the computer randomisation system to the IHMS management group (n=160) or the conventional group (n=160) after enrollment. Randomisation will also be conducted by the statisticians. Concealment of the assignment will be guaranteed because the system will not issue a randomisation code until patients are recruited for the trial.

Because of the nature of the trial intervention, subjects will be clear about their grouping status. The trial will not be blinded and will be designed as an open-label clinical trial.

Intelligent Hypertension Management System

After registering in IHMS, patients will receive a unique pass barcode, through which they can log in and input information. The patients then visit the iHEC office for height and weight measurements, blood pressure measurements and arteriosclerosis tests and get a report. Each patient will receive a standardised electronic sphygmomanometer so that they can measure their blood pressure at home. Patients will monitor their blood pressure through the IHMS WeChat applet and the attached electronic sphygmomanometer. Patients will be asked to self-measure their blood pressure three times at fixed times a day: in the morning (07:00), noon (12:00) and before bed (21:00). The blood pressure should be measured while sitting, and two readings should be taken for each measurement. The daily blood pressure data will be automatically input through bluetooth or manually input into the applet and uploaded to the system background. In addition, for patients who are not familiar with the operation of the sphygmomanometer, there is a detailed operation tutorial in the mini-programme. The physicians on the research team will check the patient’s immediate blood pressure on the computer. If the patient’s recent blood pressure is within the normal range (<140/90 mm Hg), the physician will send a message encouraging the patient to self-check and take their medication on time. If the patient’s blood pressure is above the normal range for several days in a row, the physician will communicate with the patient via text message or phone and provide standardised medication adjustments according to clinical treatment guidelines. It is worth mentioning that IHMS will issue a blood pressure and vascular report to the patient every week according to the patient’s blood pressure self-monitoring situation and strengthen the patient’s awareness of regular monitoring of blood pressure and target organ function through the form of an illustrated report. Patients can also view electronic reports of in-hospital examinations and set medication reminders through the applet. In addition, the platform will regularly push medical science articles to patients to help them learn more about hypertension.

Blood pressure measurements at each follow-up visit will be performed in the office by a dedicated study nurse and will be performed according to standard protocols recommended by the American Heart Association.20 Before each measurement, subjects should avoid smoking, drinking alcohol or other food or drink that may interfere with the measurement, and should take moderate exercise for about 30 min. After resting for 5 min, blood pressure will be measured in the sitting position. Subjects should also inform us of their medication status prior to testing. Blood pressure measurements will be taken three times at each follow-up visit. The mean value of three blood pressures will be calculated for all subsequent analyses. Blood pressure will be measured with a standardised automatic electronic sphygmomanometer, and four cuff sizes will be available according to the arm circumference of each participant. The patient’s blood pressure will be measured using an Omron u32k electronic sphygmomanometer.

For patients who withdraw early, data on their last blood pressure should be collected.

Safety reporting

All adverse events should be reported to the Data Coordination Centre (DCC) in detail and in a timely manner on the appropriate case report form page, regardless of whether they are considered serious.

For serious adverse events (SAEs), all SAE-compliant incidents must be reported to DCC within 24 hours of becoming aware of the incident and DCC will immediately notify the Data and Safety Monitoring Board. The investigator must complete a case report form for each SAE, whether related to the study procedure. The information provided must be sufficient for an independent medical assessment of the event. If it is necessary to clarify any information, the security officer will contact the investigator. Investigators should provide DCC with any follow-up information about the incident immediately after the incident. All adverse events should be followed up until the event resolves or the condition stabilises.

Data privacy

All data will be protected from unauthorised access throughout the clinical trial. Data from the clinical examination will be reviewed by quality control experts. Patients studied on case report forms will be assigned unique coding identifiers. Patient data will be protected through locked cabinets in the clinical office and through passwords, data encryption and secure, limited electronic data access storage. Explicit privacy and confidentiality issues are set out in the informed consent form.

Statistical analysis

Independent sample t-test and χ2 test will be used to compare baseline data between the experimental and control groups. The mixed-effects model will be used to compare the primary outcome that there is a greater reduction in blood pressure in the intervention group than in the control group. Participants will be assumed to be random effects in this model, and time, intervention group and interaction will be assumed to be estimable fixed effects. Logistic regression will be used to analyse categorical variables. Since participants measured blood pressure at multiple time points, generalised estimating equations will be used for analysis.

All primary and secondary endpoints will be analysed on an intention-to-treat basis (all patients are analysed as part of their assigned treatment group) and protocol-based (analysed as part of their assigned treatment group only if patients actually receive their assigned treatment) analysis. For the intention-to-treat analysis, all patients who sign the informed consent and are randomised in the study will be included in the analysis, regardless of whether the patients receive the correct treatment or whether crossover occurs. For protocol-based analysis, only enrolled patients who will actually receive the indicated treatment will be included in the analysis sample.

No covariate adjustment will be performed for the primary analysis of study endpoints. No imputation method will be used to infer missing values for baseline variables.

Planned progress

The study is scheduled to start in September 2022 and conclude in March 2025.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public will not be involved in the design of the study. The trial method of design and outcome measures are mainly based on the previous related studies. Other than the participants who signed the informed consent, other patients and the public will not participate in the research‘s conduct, reporting, or dissemination plan of the study.

Study expectation

We expect that IHMS can effectively reduce blood pressure in patients with hypertension while improving their treatment compliance. Systolic blood pressure in the intervention group is expected to decrease by 5 mm Hg at 1 month of follow-up, and systolic blood pressure in the intervention group is expected to decrease by 5 mm Hg more than that in the control group at 3 months of follow-up and decrease by 10 mm Hg compared with baseline.

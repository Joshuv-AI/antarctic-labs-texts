Deformation theory and Hopf actions on Koszul algebras Thanks: Key words: deformation theory, smash product algebras, Hochschild cohomology, Hopf algebra, Koszul algebra, Hecke algebra
A. V. Shepler, S. Witherspoon

Abstract
. We consider graded deformations and PBW deformations of algebras defined over noncommutative algebras. We explain how fibers of graded deformations correspond to filtered algebras admitting a PBW property, with focus on smash product algebras for Hopf algebras acting on quadratic algebras. In particular, we describe all PBW deformations arising from Hopf actions on Koszul algebras, giving Hopf-Koszul Hecke algebras. Alexander-Whitney and Eilenberg-Zilber maps for twisted tensor products transfer homological information between resolutions and convert conditions on Hochschild cocycles and Gerstenhaber brackets into explicit PBW conditions.



1.
Introduction

Momentum in understanding
noncommutative algebras and their deformations is often thwarted by the difficulty of constructing explicit
chain maps converting between resolutions.
Many noncommutative algebras arise as twisted tensor products,
including
skew group algebras, smash product algebras, universal enveloping
algebras, and Ore extensions.
We developed technology in
[
26
]
for twisted tensor product algebras
generally, showing how to use twisted
Alexander-Whitney and Eilenberg-Zilber maps to construct
chain maps between resolutions.
(See also Guccione and Guccione
[
11
]
.)
In this paper, we take advantage of these maps to
present some general results on graded deformations.
We use these maps to convert homological conditions for deformations
expressed on a bar resolution
into homological conditions on a chosen
twisted tensor product resolution tailored to the algebras.
As one consequence, we address a central question of
deformation theory for Hopf algebras acting on Koszul algebras:
which Hochschild 2-cocycles lift to PBW deformations?

Results on PBW properties for nonhomogeneous algebras
often center on presenting algebras as quotients of free algebras
over some field or commutative ring.
Here, we consider instead the deformation theory for
algebras presented as quotients of free algebras over some

noncommutative algebra
.
We connect PBW deformations in this more general setting
to fibers of graded deformations;
see
Theorem

3.8
and
Theorem

4.2
,
and for Hopf actions,
Corollary

5.7
.
Using this perspective, we convert general theoretical results
on deformation theory
(see
Corollary

10.4
)
into specific properties of the relations
defining the filtered algebra.
As an application, we give concrete PBW conditions
for graded deformations of smash product algebras
S\#H
arising from the action of
a Hopf algebra
H
on a Koszul algebra
S
(see
Theorem

11.2
),
as well as describing which
Hochschild
2
-cocycles lift to such deformations
(see
Corollary

11.3
).
We allow deformations of the Hopf algebra action itself in addition to
the Koszul relations,
giving rise to Hopf-Koszul Hecke algebras.

Our deformations generalize the graded affine
Hecke algebras of Lusztig
[
18
,
19
]
,
the Drinfeld Hecke algebras of Drinfeld
[
3
]
(see also
[
21
]
), the symplectic reflection algebras of
Etingof and Ginzburg
[
5
]
, rational Cherednik algebras, and deformations
of Kleinian singularities
[
2
]
, all in the case of a finite group
acting on a polynomial ring.
A number of authors have studied actions of universal enveloping algebras
of Lie algebras or related quantum groups on polynomial rings or quantum
polynomial rings, see
[
4
,
8
,
13
,
27
]
for example.
In previous work
[
24
]
, we characterized PBW deformations of groups acting on polynomial rings and
Koszul algebras more generally for
which both the group action and the Koszul relations were
deformed.
Walton and the second author
[
28
]
extended results to Hopf
algebras acting
on Koszul algebras and studied PBW deformations for which only
the Koszul relations were deformed.
We generalize those results here, describing all possible
(strong) PBW deformations, see

Corollary

11.20

and
Corollary

11.21
.

We mention other related recent papers.
Khare
[
12
]
gave results on
PBW deformations in the case of cocommutative algebras acting on polynomial rings;
for Hopf algebras, his results are a
special case of ours.
Flake and Sahi
[

7

developed a theory of Hopf-Hecke algebras;
these are PBW deformations in the setting of Hopf algebras acting

See also Letzter, Sahi, and Salmasian
[
15

where similar techniques are used to
understand PBW deformations in the setting of twisted tensor product algebras
and actions of quantum universal enveloping algebras.

To obtain our results here, we apply the
theory in
[
26
]
of chain maps for
twisted tensor products

to smash products

,
where

is a Hopf algebra acting on an algebra

This allows us to convert between

\mathbb{B}_{S\#H}
on one hand
and a twisted product resolution
\mathbb{B}_{S}\otimes_{\tau}\,\mathbb{B}_{H}

on the other hand, where

When

where

is the Koszul resolution of

Using these chain maps, necessary and sufficient homological conditions for a PBW deformation,
expressed in terms of the differential and Gerstenhaber bracket,
are translated into algebraic conditions on parameter functions giving
deformations.

Notation and Conventions

We take

whose characteristic is not 2, and

,

We assume all algebras are associative

The identity element of

2.
PBW deformations versus formal deformations

We begin by recalling some results on
deformation theory over rings and defining PBW deformations over
noncommutative algebras.

Deformations and Hochschild cohomology

Recall that a deformation of an algebra
A
over
k[t]
is
an algebra
A_{t}
over
k[t]

isomorphic to
A[t]\cong A\otimes k[t]
as a
k[t]
-module
with

A_{t}/tA_{t}\ \cong\ A\qquad\text{ as a $k$-algebra.}

The algebra
A_{t}|_{t=c}
for some
c\in k

is called a
fiber of the deformation

A_{t}
.
If in addition
A
and
A_{t}
are graded algebras with
\deg t=1

and

A_{t}/tA_{t}\cong A
as graded algebras,
then we say that
A_{t}
is a
graded deformation
.
In that case, the fiber
A_{t}|_{t=c}
is merely a filtered algebra.
Note that we
write
A_{i}
for the
i
-th
graded component of a graded algebra
A

and
\mathcal{F}_{i}(B)
for the
i
-th
filtered component of any filtered algebra
B
.

Every deformation
A_{t}

has
k[t]
-linear multiplication given by

a\star a^{\prime}=\mu_{0}(a\otimes a^{\prime})+\mu_{1}(a\otimes a^{\prime})t+\mu_{2}(a\otimes a^{\prime})t^{2}+\ldots\qquad\text{ for }a,a^{\prime}\in A

with
\mu_{0}=m:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
the multiplication map on
A

and some
k
-linear maps
\mu_{i}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A

(after identifying
A_{t}
with
A[t]
as a
k[t]
-module).
In this case,

A_{t}\cong A[t]

as graded vector space
and the associated graded algebra of any fiber,

\gr(A_{t}|_{t=c})
, is isomorphic to
A
as a graded algebra.
(Recall that the

associated graded algebra

of a filtered algebra
B=\cup_{i\geq 0}\,\mathcal{F}_{i}(B)

is

\gr B=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}

with
product

(x+\mathcal{F}_{i})(y+\mathcal{F}_{j})=xy+\mathcal{F}_{i+j-1}
for

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}(B)
and

\mathcal{F}_{-1}=\{0\}
.)
We may assume without loss of generality that the multiplicative
identity
1_{A}
of
A
is retained as the multiplicative
identity on
A_{t}
.

Recall that if
A_{t}
is a deformation of
A
,
then
\mu_{1}
represents an element
in the Hochschild cohomology

{\rm HH}^{2}(A)={\rm HH}^{2}(A,A)=\Ext^{2}_{A^{e}}(A,A)

for
A^{e}=A\otimes A^{\text{op}}
(see
[
9
]
).
The Hochschild cohomology can be defined as the homology
arising from applying
\mbox{\rm Hom\,}_{A^{e}}(-,A)

to the
bar resolution

{\bf B}_{A}
with
n
-th term

A^{\otimes(n+2)}
giving augmented complex

\displaystyle({\bf B}_{A})_{\begin{picture}(1.0,1.0)\put(2.5,2.5){\circle*{2.0}}\end{picture}}\ :

\displaystyle\cdots\longrightarrow({\bf B}_{A})_{2}\longrightarrow({\bf B}_{A})_{1}\longrightarrow A\otimes A\xrightarrow{\ \ m\ \ }A\longrightarrow 0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ (bar) }

for
m
the multiplication on
A
with differential in homological
degree
n
given by

(2.1)

a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2}\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}\,.

Note the
reduced bar resolution

\mathbb{B}_{A}

has
n
-th term
A\otimes\bar{A}^{\otimes n}\otimes A

for
\bar{A}=A/k1_{A}
giving augmented complex

\displaystyle(\mathbb{B}_{A})_{\begin{picture}(1.0,1.0)\put(2.5,2.5){\circle*{2.0}}\end{picture}}\ :

\displaystyle\cdots\longrightarrow(\mathbb{B}_{A})_{2}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{B}_{A})_{1}\longrightarrow A\otimes A\xrightarrow{\ \ m\ \ }A\longrightarrow 0\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ (reduced bar) }

with differential induced by that for
{\bf B}_{A}
after identifying

\bar{A}
with a vector subspace of
A
, so
A=k1_{A}\oplus\bar{A}
,
by choosing a section to the standard projection

A\twoheadrightarrow A/k1_{A}
.

Conversely, if a cocycle
\mu
giving a cohomology class in
{\rm HH}^{2}(A)

is the first multiplication map
\mu_{1}
for some deformation of
A
,
then we say that
\mu

lifts
or
integrates

to a deformation. One fundamental question of algebraic deformation
theory
centers on determining which cocycles lift to deformations.
Before giving a description of those deformations that lift to graded deformations
of crossed product algebras in the next section,
we give some general results on fibers of deformations.

Filtered algebras over another algebra

We consider generally algebras given in terms
of generators and relations, i.e., as quotients, but we work over a
possibly noncommutative
algebra instead of a field
k
.
We adapt ideas of
Braverman and Gaitsgory
[
1
]
on filtered
algebras
and of noncommutative Gröbner basis theory (see
[
16
]
)
to our setting.

We fix throughout this section a
k
-algebra
H
and an

H
-bimodule
W
which will serve as a generating set of some filtered
algebra. We give the elements of
W
degree
1
and take
the tensor algebra
T_{H}(W)
over
H
as a graded

k
-algebra with multiplication given by
\otimes_{H}
.
Thus

may be regarded as the algebra over
H

generated by the
H
-bimodule
W
.

The algebra

is both filtered and graded
in the usual way with

-th degree graded component

(so

is the degree

and

-th degree filtered component

\mathcal{F}_{i}(T_{\!{}_{H}}(W))=\oplus_{j=0}^{i}\ W^{\otimes_{\!H}j}\,.

For any (two-sided) ideal

, the quotient
algebra

inherits the filtration with

i
-th filtered component
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\mathcal{F}_{i}(T_{H}(W)/I)=(\mathcal{F}_{i}(T_{H}(W))+I)/I

We fix a

-subspace

with
\mathscr{P}\cap H=\{0\}

B=T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/(\mathscr{P})\,.

We compare three graded versions of

•

Specifically, each

\displaystyle{\rm{lh}}[x]

is homogeneous of degree
n

is

n

Set

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}

\displaystyle={\rm{lh}}[\mathscr{P}]:=\{{\rm{lh}}[x]:x\in\mathscr{P}\},

\displaystyle\text{the {homogeneous version of $\mathscr{P}$}, and }

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{t}

\displaystyle=\{x_{t}:x\in\mathscr{P}\},

\displaystyle\text{the {$k[t]$-homogenized version of $\mathscr{P}$}. }

We compare the associated graded algebra of

,

\hphantom{xxxx}\gr B\ \ =\ \bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{F}_{n}(B)/\mathcal{F}_{n-1}(B)\qquad\quad\text{({associated graded of $B$}),}\hskip 43.05542pt\hphantom{x}

to its

and

given respectively by

(2.3)

\displaystyle B_{\rm{hom}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/(\mathscr{R})\qquad\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ (homogeneous version of $B$),}

\displaystyle B_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)[t]/(\mathscr{P}_{t})\qquad\ \text{ ($k[t]$-{homogenized version of }$B$)}\,

which depend on the choice of generating relations

.
Note that

is a graded algebra,

is graded for

degree
1
, and
B_{t}
is filtered for

in degree
0
.

If

is any (two-sided) ideal of

,
then
({\rm{lh}}[I])
is a homogeneous ideal

T_{H}(W)/({\rm{lh}}[I])

Lemma 2.4

For any ideal
I

T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)
,
there is an isomorphism of
H
-bimodules and
graded algebras

T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/({\rm{lh}}[I])\ \xrightarrow{\ \ \cong\ \ }\ \gr\big(T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/I\big)\,

mapping

to

.
Thus for any subspace

of

, there is
an

B_{\rm{hom}}\longrightarrow\gr B\qquad\qquad\text{ for }\quad B=T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/(\mathscr{P})\,.

PBW deformations

Braverman and Gaitsgory
[
1
]
studied conditions under which
there is not only an epimorphism

B_{\rm{hom}}\longrightarrow\gr B
but an isomorphism
in the setting of quotients of free algebras over a field
(i.e. when
H
is a field in our notation),
addressing a question posed by
Bernstein (see Li
[
17
]
).
These ideas were extended to the setting of graded rings
by Li
[
17
]
.
We distinguish between the case when the specific epimorphism of
Lemma

2.4

is an isomorphism
and the case of mere existence of some isomorphism.

Definition 2.5
.

We say the filtered algebra

B=T_{H}(W)/(\mathscr{P})
is a PBW deformation
of its homogeneous version
B_{\rm{hom}}=T_{H}(W)/(\mathscr{R})

if its associated graded algebra and homogeneous versions are isomorphic
as graded algebras,

\gr(B)\ \cong\ B_{\rm{hom}}\,.

We say

B
is a strong PBW deformation
of
B_{\rm{hom}}

if the canonical graded algebra epimorphism

B_{\rm{hom}}\longrightarrow\gr B

of
Lemma

2.4
is an isomorphism.

We note that Li
[
17
]
uses the term
general PBW property

instead of strong PBW deformation as we do here.
Other authors use the term
PBW type
or
PBW property
for what we call
here strong PBW deformation;
see for example
[
1
,
6
,
7
,
12
]
.

3.
PBW deformations as fibers and doubly Noetherian algebras

We argue in this section that strong PBW deformations
are always fibers of graded deformations. We consider the converse
in the setting of linear-quadratic filtered algebras in the next section.
We also show that the PBW condition is equivalent to the strong PBW
condition
when working over
doubly Noetherian algebras.

Doubly Noetherian algebras

We use the terminology of
[
29
]
for when an algebra
H
has
Noetherian enveloping algebra:

Definition 3.1
.

An algebra
H

is called doubly Noetherian if

H^{e}=H\otimes H^{\op}
is Noetherian.

All finite dimensional algebras are both Noetherian
and doubly Noetherian.
In general, note a Noetherian algebra need not be doubly Noetherian;
e.g., see
[
22
, Theorem 8.2]
.

Recall that if
\sigma
is an automorphism of
H
and
M
is an

H
-bimodule,
the
\sigma
-twisted
H
-bimodule

\,{}^{\sigma\!}M^{\sigma}
is the set
M
with

H
-bimodule structure given by

h\otimes m\otimes h^{\prime}\mapsto\sigma(h)\,m\,\sigma(h^{\prime})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
\sigma
is an automorphism of a doubly Noetherian algebra
H

and
M
is a finitely generated
H
-bimodule.
Then any surjective
H
-bimodule
homomorphism
M\rightarrow\,^{\sigma\!}M^{\sigma}

is bijective. In particular, any surjective endomorphism of a finitely
generated
H
-bimodule is an automorphism.

Proof.

Let

f^{n}:M\rightarrow\,M
be the
n
-fold
composition of the surjective map

f:M\rightarrow\,^{\sigma\!}M^{\sigma}
regarded as an additive group homomorphism.
Then
\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f\subset\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{2}\subset\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{3}\subset\ldots
is a sequence of
H
-subbimodules of
M
.
Since
H
is doubly Noetherian and
M
is finitely generated,

M
is a Noetherian
H
-bimodule
(see
[
10
, Corollary 1.4]
).
Hence

\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{n}=\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{n+1}
for some
n
.
For
x
in
\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f
, since
f
is surjective,
there is some
x_{2}
in
M
with
x=f(x_{2})
.
Note then that
x_{2}
lies in
\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{2}
.
Continuing,
x_{2}=f(x_{3})
for some
x_{3}
in
M
and
x_{3}
lies in
\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{3}
.
Inductively,
x=f^{n}(x_{n+1})
for some
x_{n+1}
in
\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{n+1}=\mbox{\rm Ker\,}f^{n}

so
0=f^{n}(x_{n+1})=x
and
f
is injective.
For the last statement, we just take
\sigma
to be the identity map.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that some hypothesis on
H
in the last lemma is required.
The conclusion is false in general.
See e.g.
[

]
,
[

]
, and

[

PBW deformations over doubly Noetherian algebras

As in the last section, we fix an algebra
H
and
H
-bimodule
W

and consider a
k
-vector space

\mathscr{P}\subset T_{H}(W)
of generating relations
with homogeneous version
\mathscr{R}={\rm{lh}}[\mathscr{P}]
,
the set of leading homogeneous parts of the elements of
\mathscr{P}

(see
Eq.

2.2
).
Then
B=T_{H}(W)/(\mathscr{P})
is a filtered algebra and
its homogeneous version
B_{\rm{hom}}=T_{H}(W)/(\mathscr{R})

is a graded algebra.

Proposition 3.4
.

The filtered algebra
B

is a strong PBW deformation
of its homogeneous version
B_{\rm{hom}}
if and only if

({\rm{lh}}[\mathscr{P}])=({\rm{lh}}[(\mathscr{P})])
.

Proof.

Following the argument for
[
17
, Proposition 1.7]
,
we observe that the
surjective
H
-bimodule
graded algebra map of

Lemma

2.4
,

B_{\rm{hom}}=T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/({\rm{lh}}[\mathscr{P}])\longrightarrow T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/({\rm{lh}}[(\mathscr{P})])\ \cong\ \gr\big(T_{\!{}_{H}}(W)/(\mathscr{P})\big)=\gr B\,,

is an isomorphism
exactly when

({\rm{lh}}[\mathscr{P}])=({\rm{lh}}[(\mathscr{P})])
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose
H
is doubly Noetherian
and

B_{\rm{hom}}
in each graded degree
is finitely generated as an
H
-bimodule.
Then
B
is a PBW deformation of
its
homogeneous version
B_{\rm{hom}}
if and only if it is a strong PBW deformation
of
B_{\rm{hom}}
.

Proof.

Every strong PBW deformation is
a PBW deformation by definition.
For the converse, assume
B
is a (not necessarily strong) PBW deformation and
compose the surjective graded algebra and
H
-bimodule map

B_{\rm{hom}}\rightarrow\gr B

of
Lemma

2.4

with a graded algebra isomorphism

\gr B\rightarrow B_{\rm{hom}}
given by the PBW hypothesis
to obtain a surjective graded algebra endomorphism

f:B_{\rm{hom}}\rightarrow B_{\rm{hom}}
.
To obtain an
H
-bimodule map,
we note that
f

restricts to an automorphism

\sigma

on
H\subset T_{H}(W)
and we take

\,{}^{\sigma\!}B_{\rm{hom}}^{\sigma}
, the
\sigma
-twisted
H
-bimodule. The map
f
defines
a surjective
H
-bimodule homomorphism

B_{\rm{hom}}\rightarrow\,^{\,\sigma\!}B_{\rm{hom}}^{\sigma}

that restricts to a bijection on each graded component
by
Lemma

3.2
.
Hence
the map of
Lemma

2.4
must be an isomorphism of graded
algebras and
B
is a strong PBW deformation.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Proposition

3.4
gives conditions for hidden
relations

Basis generating the degree

Theorem 4.2

Proof.

We construct a strong PBW deformation of

to the canonical projection map

Define a

Consider the unique algebra and

-bimodule homomorphism

defined by

Deforming the degree

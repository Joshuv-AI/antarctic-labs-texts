Partonic Entropy of the Proton from DGLAP Evolution
Krzysztof Golec-Biernat

Abstract
We investigate the concept of partonic entropy of the proton within the Dokshitzer–Gribov–Lipatov–Altarelli–Parisi (DGLAP) evolution scheme of collinear parton distributions. We show that such entropy increases monotonically with the evolution scale. The mechanism underlying this growth is illustrated using a simplified model of DGLAP evolution, which highlights the importance of including saturation effects at small x x in the evolution of parton distributions. Based on existing literature, we present two simplified models of parton saturation at small x x . In one of these models, partonic entropy is identified with entanglement entropy and proposed as an observable to be tested experimentally.

II
Partonic entropy

For non-negative PDFs, the sum of the singlet quark distribution

\Sigma(x,t)=\sum_{i}(q_{i}(x,t)+{\bar{q}}_{i}(x,t))

(1)

and the gluon distributions
g(x,t)
, weighted by the parton longitudinal momentum fraction
x
,

P(x,t)=x\left(\Sigma(x,t)+g(x,t)\right)

(2)

admits an interpretation of a probability density on the interval
x\in[0,1]
. This follows from the facts that
P(x)
is non-negative and satisfies the normalization condition

\int_{0}^{1}dxP(x,t)=1

(3)

The factor
x
in Eq. (
2
) is essential: without it neither the singlet nor gluon distributions are normalizable over the interval
[0,1]
.
Here,
i=1,\ldots,N_{f}
denotes quark flavor and
t=\ln(Q^{2}/Q_{0}^{2})
is the evolution variable, with
Q^{2}
being the resolution scale
at which the proton is probed, identified with the QCD factorization scale, and
Q_{0}^{2}
an initial scale for the evolution.

Relation (
3
) is known as the momentum sum rule which states that partons carry the entire longitudinal momentum of the proton. In our approach, however, this relation is interpreted as a normalization condition for the probability distribution
P(x,t)
.

Following the Gibbs-Shanon formula for entropy of a probability distribution
\{P_{n}\}
, defined on a discrete set of states
n
1
1

1

In quantum mechanics,
S
is the von Neumann entropy,
S=-{\rm Tr\,}\rho\ln\rho
, computed in the basis that diagonalizes the density operator
\rho
.
,

S=-\sum_{n}P_{n}\ln P_{n}\,\geq\,0\,,~~~~~~~~~\sum_{n}P_{n}=1

(4)

the partonic entropy of the proton is given by

S(t)=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,P(x,t)\ln P(x,t)

(5)

No minus sign is introduced on the r.h.s. because
S(t)
is non-negative, in contrast to the entropy density,

s(x,t)\equiv P(x,t)\ln P(x,t)

(6)

which is negative for
P(x,t)<1
.
The non-negativity of
S(t)
can be readily proven using a well-known inequality

x\ln\!\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\geq(x-y)\,,~~~~~~x\geq 0,\,y>0

(7)

with the substitution:
x\to P(x,t)
and
y\to 1
. Thus

P(x,t)\ln P(x,t)\geq(P(x,t)-1)

(8)

and due to the normalization condition (
3
), one obtains

S(t)\geq\int_{0}^{1}dx\,(P(x,t)-1)=0

(9)

Note that the non-negativity is also a consequence of the integration range
x\in[0,1]
. For different integration domains, the generally defined entropy (
5
) may admit a negative lower bound.
This is in contrast to relative entropy, to be discussed later, which is intrinsically non-negative.

In the forthcoming analysis, it will be shown that under the DGLAP evolution of the PDFs
Gribov and Lipatov 1972
;
Altarelli and Parisi 1977
;
Dokshitzer 1977
, the partonic entropy

S(t)
increases monotonically with

, i.e.,

\frac{dS}{dt}>0

(10)

For this purpose, entropy (
5
) is differentiated with respect to
t
:

\frac{dS}{dt}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{\partial P(x,t)}{\partial t}\left(\ln P(x,t)+1\right)

(11)

The integration over one yields zero, since from Eq. (
3
) follows:

\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{\partial P(x,t)}{\partial t}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,P(x,t)=0

(12)

and finally

\frac{dS}{dt}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{\partial P(x,t)}{\partial t}\,\ln P(x,t)

(13)

To proceed, we need to consider the DGLAP equations governing the evolution of
P(x,t)
with respect to
t

III
DGLAP evolution equations

The general form of the DGLAP evolution equations is given by
Golec-Biernat et al. 2006
;
Golec-Biernat et al. 2008
:

\partial_{t}D_{f}(x,t)=\sum_{{f^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{1}du\,{\cal{K}}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)D_{f^{\prime}}(u,t)

(14)

Here,
\partial_{t}=\partial/\partial t
while
D_{f}\in\{q_{i},{\bar{q}}_{i},g\}
denotes the parton distribution functions
(not multiplied by
x
). The kernel

{\cal{K}}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)=K^{R}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)-\delta(x-u)\,\delta_{f{f^{\prime}}}K^{V}_{f^{\prime}}(u,t)

(15)

contains the real
R
and virtual
V
contribution to parton emission. The real part is computed in perturbative QCD and corresponds to the transition of a parton with longitudinal momentum fraction and flavor
(u,{f^{\prime}})
to a parton
(x,f)
. The virtual part leaves the parton
(u,{f^{\prime}})
unchanged and is determined from the momentum sum rule:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\sum_{f}xD_{f}(x,t)=0

(16)

which is valid if

\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\sum_{f}x{\cal{K}}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)=0

(17)

Substituting (
15
) into the above equation, one obtains:

uK^{V}_{f^{\prime}}(u,t)=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\sum_{f}xK^{R}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)

(18)

With this result, the evolution equation (
14
) can be written the following form

\displaystyle\partial_{t}D_{f}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{f^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{1}du\,K^{R}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)D_{f^{\prime}}(u,t)

\displaystyle-D_{f}(x,t)\sum_{f^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}du\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)K^{R}_{{f^{\prime}}f}(u,x,t)

(19)

The real-emission kernel is given by

K^{R}_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x,u,t)=\frac{1}{u}P_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x/u,t)\,\theta(u-x)

(20)

where
P_{f{f^{\prime}}}
are the Altarelli–Parisi splitting functions, computed perturbateively as a series in the strong coupling constant

\alpha_{s}
:

P_{f{f^{\prime}}}(z,t)=\frac{\alpha_{s}(t)}{2\pi}P_{f{f^{\prime}}}^{(0)}(z)+\frac{\alpha_{s}^{2}(t)}{4\pi^{2}}P_{f{f^{\prime}}}^{(1)}(z)+\ldots

(21)

Substituting (
20
) into (
III
), one obtains:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}D_{f}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{f^{\prime}}}\int_{x}^{1}du\,P_{f{f^{\prime}}}(x/u,t)D_{f^{\prime}}(u,t)

\displaystyle-D_{f}(x,t)\sum_{f^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{du}{x}\left(\frac{u}{x}\right)P_{{f^{\prime}}f}(u/x,t)

(22)

Changing the variables to
y=x/u
in the first integral and to
y=u/x
in the second one, one finds:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}D_{f}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{f^{\prime}}}\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dy}{y}\,P_{f{f^{\prime}}}(y,t)D_{f^{\prime}}(x/y,t)

\displaystyle-D_{f}(x,t)\sum_{f^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}dy\,yP_{{f^{\prime}}f}(y,t)

(23)

which can finally be written as follows:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}D_{f}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{f^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{1}\!dz\int_{0}^{1}\!dy\,\delta(x-yz)\,P_{f{f^{\prime}}}(y,t)D_{f^{\prime}}(z,t)

\displaystyle-D_{f}(x,t)\sum_{f^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{1}dy\,yP_{{f^{\prime}}f}(y,t)

(24)

These are the DGLAP evolution equations. It is easy to show that they satisfy the momentum sum relation (

V
Relative entropy

It is insightful to examine the increase of the partonic entropy in terms of the relative entropy, which for the discrete probability distributions

p_{n}
and
q_{n}
is defined as follows:

S(p\|q)=\sum_{n}p_{n}\ln\!\left(\frac{p_{n}}{q_{n}}\right)

(33)

The relative entropy is non-negative
3
3

3

The quantum relative entropy,
S(\rho\|\sigma)={\rm Tr\,}\rho(\ln\rho-\ln\sigma)
for any density operators
\rho,\sigma
, is also non-negative,
see e.g.
Witten 2020
for the proof.
:

S(p\|q)\geq 0\,,

(34)

which can be easily proven using inequality (
7
). In addition,
S(p\|q)=0
if and only if
p_{n}=q_{n}
for all
n
. Thus, it is tempting to interpret
S(p\|q)
as a metric. However, the relative entropy is neither symmetric,
S(p\|q)\neq S(q\|p)
, nor does it satisfy the triangle inequality,
therefore, it should be understood as a measure of distinguishability of the probability distribution
p_{n}
from a reference distribution
q_{n}
, rather than a distance between them.

For continuous probability distributions
P(x)
and
Q(x)
, defined on the interval
x\in[a,b]
(finite or infinite), the relative entropy is given by

S(P\|Q)=\int_{a}^{b}dx\,P(x)\ln\!\left(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\right)

(35)

Comparing (
35
) with (
5
), we see that the partonic entropy
S(t)
can be interpreted as the relative
entropy of the parton distribution function
P(x,t)
with respect to the uniform distribution
Q(x)\equiv 1
on the interval
[0,1]
:

S(t)=S(P(t)\|1)

(36)

Therefore, the unbounded growth of
S(t)
in the DGLAP evolution scheme characterizes the increasing distinguishability of
the parton distribution
P(x,t)
from the uniform distribution as
t
increases.

We can also define the relative entropy of
P(x,t)
with respect to the initial parton distribution at
t=0
,
P(x,0)
:

S(P(t)\|P(0))=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,P(x,t)\ln\!\left(\frac{P(x,t)}{P(x,0)}\right)

(37)

which quantifies the distinguishability of the final parton distribution from the initial one, see the lower red line in Fig.
2
.
Such quantity, called dynamical entropy, was introduced in
Peschanski 2013
for different parton distributions.

In the hypothetical situation (which does not occur for DGLAP evolution) where the distribution
P(x,t)
tends to an asymptotic form
P^{\rm as}(x)
as
t\to\infty
, the relative entropy of
P
with respect to
P^{\rm as}
vanishes in this limit:

\lim_{t\to\infty}S(P(t))\|P^{\rm as})=0

(38)

Such behavior occurs, for example, for the pion distribution amplitude,
\phi(x,t)\equiv\sqrt{P(x,t)}
, which undergoes the Efremov–Radyushkin–Brodsky–Lepage (ERBL) evolution
Efremov and Radyushkin 1980
;
Lepage and Brodsky 1980
, with the asymptotic form
\phi^{\rm as}(x)=6\/x(1-x)
.

VI
Entropy growth interpretation

The unbounded increase of partonic entropy originates from the growing number of partons resolved by the electroweak boson in deep inelastic scattering (DIS)
as the resolution scale,
\lambda=1/\sqrt{Q^{2}}\to 0
, approaches zero. These partons populate an expanding phase space for the emission of soft (wee) gluons carrying a small momentum fraction
x
. Consequently, the sea-quark and gluon distributions rise rapidly as
x\to 0
and
Q^{2}\to\infty
.

This effect can be qualitatively illustrated by a simplified form of the solution to the DGLAP equations:

P(x,t)=A(t)\,x^{\alpha(t)-1}(1-x)^{\beta(t)-1}

(39)

where the amplitude
A(t)
is fixed by the normalization condition (
3
), and is given by the inverse of the beta function

A(t)=\frac{1}{B(\alpha,\beta)}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha(t)+\beta(t))}{\Gamma(\alpha(t))\,\Gamma(\beta(t))}

(40)

The convergence of the normalization integral requires
\alpha(t)>0
, while the properties of the DGLAP equations imply
\beta(t)>1
.
In the presented example, DGLAP evolution is simulated by the limit
\alpha(t)~\to~0^{+}
as
t\to\infty
, which
results in a strongly increasing parton distribution as
x\to 0
and
Q^{2}\to\infty
.

The partonic entropy (
5
) then takes the following form

\displaystyle S=\ln A

\displaystyle+(\alpha-1)\,\psi(\alpha)+(\beta-1)\,\psi(\beta)

\displaystyle-(\alpha+\beta-2)\,\psi(\alpha+\beta)

(41)

where
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
is the digamma function. Using the property of
\psi
:

\psi(\alpha+1)=\psi(\alpha)+\frac{1}{\alpha}

(42)

one finds the following singularity structure of
S
in the vicinity of
\alpha\approx 0
:

S=\ln\alpha+\frac{1}{\alpha}-1+\gamma+\psi(\beta)+{\cal O}(\alpha)

(43)

where
\gamma
is the Euler’s constant.
Thus, as
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, the entropy increases without bound:

S\simeq\ln\alpha+\frac{1}{\alpha}\simeq\frac{1}{\alpha}\to\infty

(44)

This behavior is illustrated in Fig.
3
by the red dotted lines, showing
S(\alpha)
for four values of
\beta
.

It can be demonstrated analytically that the singular behavior (
44
) results from the small-
x
contribution to
S
:

S_{<}(x_{\rm min})\equiv\int_{0}^{x_{\rm min}}dx\,P(x)\ln P(x)

(45)

Indeed, using
P(x)=A\,x^{\alpha-1}
for
x\leq x_{\rm min}\ll 1
, one obtains in the limit
\alpha\to 0^{+}
;

S_{<}(x_{\rm min}))\simeq\ln\alpha+\frac{1}{\alpha}

(46)

This is illustrated by the blue dotted lines in Fig.
3
, corresponding to the entropy
S
without the small-
x
contribution,
defined by Eq. (
32
). It clearly does not show the singular behavior.
Thus, the small-
x
partons generate an unbounded entropy.

This observation raises a question about saturation effects which involve not only gluon splitting but also
gluon recombination
Gribov et al. 1983
;
Mueller and Qiu 1986
;
Mueller 1994
;
Mueller 1995
;
Mueller 1999
;
Kovchegov 1999
;
Kovchegov 2000
. The latter phenomenon
suppresses the small-
x
increase of the parton distributions and hopefully imposes an upper bound on the partonic entropy, see the recent discussion in
Dvali and Venugopalan 2022b

in the context of the Color Glass Condensate (CGC)
Gelis et al. 2010
, an effective theory of gluon saturation.

In our model, the PDFs suppression can be accomplished by freezing the parton distribution
P(x)
for
x<x_{\rm min}\ll 1
,

P(x)\equiv(A/\alpha)\,x_{\rm min}^{\alpha-1}

(47)

where the additional factor of
1/\alpha
is introduced to keep the small-
x
contribution to the momentum sum rule unchanged,

\int_{0}^{x_{\rm min}}dx\,P(x)=(A/\alpha)\,x_{\rm min}^{\alpha}

(48)

The corresponding contribution to the entropy is

S_{<}(x_{\rm min})=x_{\rm min}P(x_{\rm min})\ln P(x_{\rm min})

(49)

and for
\alpha\to 0^{+}
, one obtains a non-singular contribution

S_{<}(x_{\rm min})\simeq\ln(1/x_{\rm min})+{\cal O}(\alpha)

(50)

Therefore, the partonic entropy
S
is no longer singular in the considered limit and has an upper bound which saturates its vale. Notice hat the small-
x

contribution (
48
) also saturates the momentum sum rule since in the limit
\alpha\to 0^{+}

(A/\alpha)\,x_{\rm min}^{\alpha}\simeq x_{\rm min}^{\alpha}\to 1

(51)

A more refined approach to parton saturation should take into account the presence of a saturation scale
Q=Q_{s}(x)
, which in our example leads to a dependence of
x_{\rm min}
on the evolution parameter
t
. This important aspect of parton saturation, however, needs a more detailed analysis based on non-linear QCD evolution equations, which lies beyond the scope of the present work.

VII
Reaction-diffusion model of parton saturation

For the completeness of the discussion, we will present simplified models of parton saturation effects at small
x
, discussed in the literature, which illustrate the saturation of entropy as
x\to 0
. Strictly speaking, this is a discussion of the Regge limit of QCD, in which the hard scale
Q^{2}
is kept fixed while the invariant energy of the partonic processes
s\to\infty
. Thus, the relevant quantities are parton distributions that depend not only on parton’s momentum fraction
x
but also on its transverse momentum
k_{\perp}
. However, in the simplified models presented below, the latter dependence is neglected.

In the reaction-diffusion model, partons are gluon dipoles that are zero-dimensional in the transverse direction. They evolve in the longitudinal direction through the dependence of parton cascades on rapidity
y=\ln(1/x)

Mueller 1995
, The evolution incorporates both parton splitting and recombination processes which correspond to pomeron loops within the effective reggeon field-theory description, see
Bondarenko et al. 2007
for more details and further references.

The underlying dynamics
is represented as a reaction-diffusion Markov process with the following master equation for the probability
p_{n}(y)
of finding
n
partons:

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{n}}{dy}

\displaystyle=\alpha(n-1)p_{n-1}-\alpha np_{n}

\displaystyle+\beta n(n+1)p_{n+1}-\beta n(n-1)p_{n}

(52)

The first line on the right-hand side describes parton splitting
(n-1)\to n
with rate
\alpha
while the second line corresponds to parton recombination
(n+1)\to n

with rate
\beta
.
The two rates are related by
\beta=\alpha_{s}^{2}\,\alpha
, where
\alpha_{s}\ll 1
.
Enforcing the condition
p_{n<0}=0
implies that
p_{0}={\rm const}
, which is then fixed by the normalization condition:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(y)=1

(53)

It is straightforward to check that Eq, (
VII
) admits a stationary solution for which
dp_{n}/dy=0
, given by the Poisson distribution:

P_{n}=\frac{N^{n}}{n!}\,e^{-N}

(54)

with the mean multiplicity equal to
N
,

\big<n\big>\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\/P_{n}=N=\frac{\alpha}{\beta}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{s}^{2}}\gg 1

(55)

An approximate solution to Eq. (
VII
) for large
y
was found in
Hagiwara et al. 2018
via Borel summation:

p_{n}(y)=\frac{N^{n}}{n!}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,{\rm{e}}^{-z-\frac{NzX}{1+zX}}\,\left(\frac{zX}{1+zX}\right)^{n}

(56)

with
X=\alpha_{s}^{2}\,y
and the initial condition
p_{n}(0)=\delta_{n0}
. This distribution approaches the Poisson distribution
P_{n}

as
y\to\infty
,
so that the relative entropy of
p_{n}(y)
with respect to
P_{n}
vanishes in this limit:

\lim_{y\to\infty}S(p(y)\|P)=0

(57)

Likewise, as demonstrated numerically in
Hagiwara et al. 2018
, the partonic entropy

S(y)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(y)\ln p_{n}(y)

(58)

approaches
non-monotonically
the entropy of the Poisson distribution in the limit
y\to\infty
,

S(y)\to S_{P}\approx\frac{1}{2}\,\ln(2\pi eN)-\frac{1}{12N}+{\mathcal{O}}(1/N^{2})

(59)

while the mean multiplicity approaches
monotonically
the Poisson distribution mean multiplicity:

\big<n\big>(y)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}np_{n}(y)\to N

(60)

Thus, both the partonic entropy and the mean multiplicity saturate.

VIII
Entanglement entropy in DIS

The reaction-diffusion model of parton saturation should be contrasted with the Markov process that includes only parton splittings,
see e.g.
Mueller 1995
;
Levin and Lublinsky 2004
, given by the first line of Eq. (
VII
),

\frac{dp_{n}}{dy}=\alpha(n-1)p_{n-1}-\alpha np_{n}

(61)

The solution to this equation, with the initial condition
p_{n}(0)=\delta_{n1}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
is given by the distribution:

p_{n}(y)={\rm{e}}^{-\alpha y}\,(1-{\rm{e}}^{-\alpha y})^{n-1}

(62)

which is normalized to one. The mean multiplicity rises exponentially with rapidity,

\langle n\rangle={\rm{e}}^{\alpha y}

(63)

and due to this dependence, the distribution (
62
) can be written as

p_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle}\left(1-\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle}\right)^{n-1}

(64)

With this result, entropy (
58
) reads:

S(y)=\langle n\rangle\ln\langle n\rangle-\left(\langle n\rangle-1\right)\ln\left(\langle n\rangle-1\right)

(65)

For
y\to\infty
, when
\langle n\rangle\gg 1
, the dipole probabilities become approximately uniform

p_{n}\simeq\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle}

(66)

while the entropy is maximal and proportional to rapidity

S(y)\simeq\ln\langle n\rangle=\alpha y

(67)

Thus, in contrast to the model with recombination, neither the mean multiplicity nor the entropy saturates.

The exponential growth of the mean multiplicity (
63
) is a characteristic feature of the Balitsky-Fadin-Kuraev-Lipatov (BFKL) evolution in rapidity

Kuraev et al. 1977
;
Balitsky and Lipatov 1978
, when
\alpha=4\alpha_{s}\ln 2
is the BFKL pomeron intercept. This evolution is known to violate the unitarity of the scattering matrix.
Nevertheless, in the deep inelastic scattering of a dilute dipole projectile off a dense nuclear target, unitarity is restored through multiple dipole-nucleon scatterings
Kovchegov 1999
;
Kovchegov 2000
;
Kovchegov and Levin 2013
.

In Ref.
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
, the mean multiplicity in the model (
61
) was identified with the mean number of gluons per unit of rapidity,
xG(x)
, found
from the BFKL equation,

\langle n\rangle=xG(x)=x^{-\alpha}

(68)

where
x={\rm{e}}^{-y}\ll 1
.
Thus, the entropy (
67
) is given by

S(y)\approx\ln\,(xG(x))

(69)

This formula was further interpreted as entanglement entropy. Since all partonic microstates have equal probabilities, see Eq. (
66
), the entanglement
entropy is maximal, and the virtual probe in DIS at small
x
sees the proton in a maximally entangled state.

As advocated in
Kharzeev and Levin 2017
;
Tu et al. 2020
, the physical picture underlying quantum entanglement is the division of the proton’s transverse area into a subsystem
A
, of size
1/\sqrt{Q^{2}}
, probed by a virtual electroweak boson, and the remainder of the proton
B
. Averaging over
either of the two subsystems yields entanglement entropy,
S_{A}=S_{B}
. Therefore, averaging over
B
gives the partonic entropy

S_{A}=S(y)

(70)

while averaging over
A
gives the hadronic entropy

S_{B}=S_{\text{h}adron}=-\sum_{N}P(N)\ln(N)

(71)

where
N
is the number of hadrons (mostly charged pions) in the final state. Hence, one can identify the partonic and hadronic entropy

\ln\,(xG(x))=S_{\text{h}adron}

(72)

This identification has been tested against experimental data in
Tu et al. 2020
;
Andreev et al. 2021
;
Hentschinski et al. 2022
;
Hentschinski et al. 2024
with a positive result. An application of a generalized version of the model (
61
) to the description of hadron multiplicities in the hadron-hadron scattering data was presented in
Kutak and Lökös 2025
.
A precise formulation of the entanglement idea is still lacking, although the studies in this direction were carried out in
Liu et al. 2023
;
Liu et al. 2022b
;
Liu et al. 2022a
in two-dimensional QCD.

IX
Conclusions

We presented the concept of partonic entropy of the proton, defined in analogy to the Gibbs-Shanon entropy formula for a probability distribution, using the collinear parton distribution functions (PDFs).
In this formulation, the probability distribution
P(x)
is given by the sum of the quark-singlet and gluon distributions multiplied by the Bjorken
variable
x
, which is a positive quantity. With such a definition, the momentum sum rule (
3
) is interpreted as the normalization condition for the probability distribution
P(x)
, which allows to define the non-negative partonic entropy (
5
).

We showed that within the DGLAP evolution scheme of the PDFs,
the partonic entropy is a monotonically increasing function of the evolution parameter
t=\ln(Q^{2})
, where
Q^{2}
is the QCD resolution (factorization) scale. We also showed that the partonic entropy can be interpreted as a relative entropy of the density
P(x,t)
with respect to the uniform distribution on the interval
[0,1]
.

In order to better understand the origin of the monotonic growth of the partonic entropy, we presented a simplified model of DGLAP evolution in which the contribution from the region of
x\in[0,x_{\rm min}]
is responsible for the unbounded growth of
the partonic entropy. In this context, we highlighted the need to modify the small-
x
behavior of the PDFs by incorporating parton saturation effects, which in turn leads to the saturation of the partonic entropy.

A precise formulation of parton saturation requires considering evolution equations with non-linear terms and is beyond the scope on this work. Nevertheless, for completeness, we presented a reaction-diffusion model of parton saturation at small
x
, widely discussed in the literature, which leads to the saturation of the partonic entropy.

A truncated version of this model, without parton recombination, was also presented. Based on this simplified framework, it was conjectured
that the partonic entropy is essentially an entanglement entropy. In addition, in the limit of rapidity
y=\ln(1/x)\to\infty
, the proton is probed in DIS in a maximally entangled state, which was tested against the existing DIS data with a positive result.

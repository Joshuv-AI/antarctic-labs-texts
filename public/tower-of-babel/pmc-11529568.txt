Demographic and clinical characteristics of New York City Health + Hospitals HIV Heart Failure (NYC4H cohort): cohort profile

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeStudies have elucidated that heart failure (HF) in people living with HIV manifests differently when compared with HF cases in the general population. This cohort aimed to examine the mortality risk factors, including biological and social factors, as well as suitable medical interventions to prevent death in this population.ParticipantsThe New York City Health+Hospitals HIV Heart Failure (NYC4H) cohort encompassed records from 11 major hospital sites. In addition, we intend to conduct annual follow-ups for specific high-risk groups, such as individuals with pulmonary hypertension, those facing higher social adversities and participants with poor HIV control.

Introduction

As of the end of 2022, the WHO reported a staggering estimate of over 39.0 million people living with HIV (PLHIV).1 The advent of antiretroviral therapy (ART) has significantly increased the life expectancy of PLHIV. However, this extended lifespan has brought forth new challenges, notably in the form of increased cardiovascular diseases, including acute myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease (CAD), cardiomyopathy and heart failure (HF).2 3

Despite advancements in understanding the epidemiological patterns surrounding HIV and its association with HF risk in the last decade,4 a critical knowledge gap persists. Specifically, the factors contributing to higher mortality once HF develops in this population remain unclear. Conventional mortality prediction models developed for general HF might not be adequate for PLHIV due to these unique risk factors and comorbidities.

Studies have elucidated that HF in PLHIV manifests differently and exhibits distinct disease distribution when compared with general HF cases.5 6 Among patients who have uncontrolled HIV, it is observed that HIV-related HF is usually associated with rapidly progressive cardiomyopathy.7 Yet, in the ART era, the mechanisms underlying the development of HF in PLHIV are complex and multifactorial, involving chronic inflammatory dysregulation and the direct impact of HIV on the heart, among other factors.2810 In addition, some ART agents can cause early atherosclerosis and lead to ACS and HF.11 The complex relationship between HIV and HF raises questions about the applicability of current HF treatment guidelines to this specific population.

Furthermore, PLHIV often contends with heightened social challenges, particularly in the context of HF and healthcare access, which transform their chronic disease management into an uphill battle.1215 Brandt et al16 reviewed five social determinants of health (SDoH) domains, namely economic stability, education, healthcare access and quality, social and community context, and neighbourhood and built environment, to improve cardiovascular care and outcomes.16 The intricate interplay of HIV infection and HF in this population underscores the urgent need for a more comprehensive understanding of the influence of social factors on care implementation and health outcomes.17 18

The New York City Health+Hospitals HIV Heart Failure (NYC4H) cohort was designed to specifically address these gaps in our knowledge. This cohort aims to delve into the specific risk factors contributing to mortality within the PLHIV experiencing HF.

By bridging the gaps in our understanding of the unique presentation and physiology of HF in PLHIV and elucidating the social factors influencing patient survival outcomes, the NYC4H cohort aspires to inform more precise interventions and care strategies tailored to this vulnerable patient population. The scope of this article is to describe the cohort profile and elucidate the main characteristics of the HIV/HF patient population.

Cohort description

Study design and participants

The NYC4H cohort encompassed records from 11 major hospital sites in the New York City Health+Hospitals network (online supplemental table 1). This cohort is composed of retrospective baseline information and follow-up data, which were prospectively collected. Adult individuals (age >18 at the first encounter during the enrolling period) with confirmed HIV and HF were recruited from either inpatient or clinic hospital visits between July 2017 and June 2022. Baseline demographic information as well as medical data and social evaluations were obtained by chart review at the time of enrolment.

The first follow-up period began on the date of the first clinical encounter with a diagnosis of HIV and HF and ended in June 2022. At the end of the enrolment period, all-cause mortality was analysed before beginning follow-up data collection. The second follow-up period extended from July 2022 to October 2023. We collected the first comprehensive follow-up data between October 2023 and December 2023. The follow-up data, which included updated medical information, treatments, social factors and the number of hospitalisations were collected until the latest hospital or clinic encounter during the follow-up period (figure 1).

Demographic and clinical data collection

Participant baseline demographic information was self-reported and documented in the electronic health record (EHR). HF was first identified through relevant ICD-10 (International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision) codes. It was then confirmed by manual review of EHR information using established diagnostic criteria.19 We categorised HF with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) as symptomatic HF with Left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≤40%, HF with mid-range ejection fraction (HFmrEF) as symptomatic HF with LVEF 41–49% and HF with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) as symptomatic HF with LVEF≥50%, as delineated in the 2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA guideline definition reference.19 Pulmonary hypertension was defined by mean pulmonary artery pressure (mPAP) greater or equal to 35 mm Hg through echocardiogram or 25 mm Hg through right heart catheterization. We extracted data from the earliest echocardiogram performed during the enrolment period for baseline echocardiogram variables, which included EF, grade of diastolic dysfunction and mean mPAP. Follow-up data were collected from the most recent echocardiogram performed during the second follow-up period.

HIV infection was first identified using relevant ICD-10 codes and confirmed with HIV serology/HIV viral load by individual chart review in the EHR. The year of HIV diagnosis was self-reported by the patients in most instances. Clinically relevant variables, including HIV viral load, CD4 counts and comorbidities were also confirmed through manual review of each patient’s EHR.

Patient’s functional status was determined through composite evaluation of activities of daily living (ADLs) which included ambulating, feeding, dressing, personal hygiene, continence and toileting. Comprehensive evaluation of instrumental ADLs was obtained from licensed social worker notes. Patients were categorised into completely independent (0 incompetencies in ADLs), partially dependent (1–2 incompetencies in ADLs) and completely dependent (>2 incompetencies in ADLs) based on their individual ADLs evaluation.

Medications and cardiac device

Medications at baseline and follow-up data collection were confirmed by review of clinical encounters in the EHR. Additional medication reconciliation with internal or external pharmacies was performed when information was duplicated or conflicting. Medication compliance was assessed through both self-reported and medical doctor’s note. Information regarding guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT), which included evidence-based beta blocker, ACE inhibitors (ACEIs)/angiotensin receptor blockers (ARB)/angiotensin receptor/neprilysin inhibitors (ARNIs), sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitors and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) was also obtained. Cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIED) such as pacemakers, implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) and cardiac resynchronisation therapy (CRT, including CRT-D and CRT-P) were identified through EHR review; further confirmation via analysis of available chest X-rays was performed if needed to characterise the CIED.

Social determinants of health

SDoH were evaluated through the psychosocial assessment note, done by a licensed medical social worker (LMSW) during first clinic or hospital encounter in the enrolling period. The standard initial comprehensive evaluation consisted of a one-to-one personal interview of around 45 min of duration, followed by a short follow-up evaluation of around 20 min. The psychosocial factors included, but were not limited to, primary caregiver, support system, developmental information, visual/hearing impairment, community resource, criminal history, insurance, immigration status, religion, education level, family and community support, living/housing condition, substance abuse, mental illness, ADLs, income information, military history, government stipend for housing, food and living. Trauma history evaluation included childhood abuse, crime victim, elder abuse, emotional abuse, human trafficking, intimate partner violence, neglect, physical abuse, sexual abuse, sexual assault and any other trauma.

Outcomes variables

Our primary outcome was the estimation of all-cause mortality during the follow-up periods. Death was identified through EHR and verified with individual patient-family contact between November 2023 and December 2023 (figure 1). The cause of death was determined by physician-adjudicated individual EHR review. Data on re-hospitalisation within 6 months before and after the baseline and follow-up hospital encounters were collected through EHR review.

Missing data & loss follow-up

All included patients were enrolled in the NYC-H+H system and the patient outreach department annually attempted to contact patients who met loss-to-follow-up criteria.

26 (2.49%) patients of 1044 PLHIV with HF had only one encounter. These patients were categorised as lost-to-follow-up. Patients who were lost to follow-up were more likely to be undocumented, to be Veterans Affairs (VA)-insured or uninsured and to have raised safety concerns during the initial social worker evaluation. Of note, baseline cluster of differentiation 4 (CD4) counts and HIV viral load were missing in 25 (2.4%) and 31 (3%) patients, respectively.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

Ethics statement

The study was approved by the institutional review board (study ID 23-12-663-719 (HHC)) and appropriate guidelines were followed. Results from future studies derived from the cohort will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals and presented at relevant conferences.

Findings to date

A total of 1543 patients with HIV and a co-diagnosis of HF were identified by screening for relevant ICD-10 codes in the EHR. After exclusion of patients without clinical evidence of HF, HIV infection within these populations, or missing critical demographic information through chart review, 1044 patients were enrolled in the cohort.

The cohort comprised 657 (62.9%) male and 387 (37.1%) female. The average age was 61.6 years at baseline and the average follow-up time was 3.8 years. In total, 259 (24.8%) deaths were identified of which 193 occurred during the first follow-up period (enrolling date to June 2022) and 66 during the second follow-up period (June 2022 to October 2023). The cohort included 631 (60.4%) patients who identified as Black/African American, 289 (27.7%) Hispanic/Latino, 57 (5.5%) White, 17 (1.6%) Asian and 50 (4.8%) who identified as ‘others’ or refused to disclose (‘unknown’) (table 1).

HF variables

Out of 1044 patients, 1025 (98.2%) had a baseline echocardiogram report, whereas 581 (69.4%) of the 837 patients who survived the initial follow-up period had a follow-up echocardiogram available. At enrolment (baseline encounter), there were 430 (41.2%) patients with a diagnosis of HFrEF, 150 (14.4%) with HFmrEF and 464 (44.4%) with HFpEF. The most common comorbidity within these populations were hypertension (n=786, 75.3%), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (n=409, 39.2%), type II diabetes mellitus (n=416, 40%), hyperlipidaemia (n=373, 35.7%), polysubstance abuse (PSA) disorder (n=331, 31.7%) and CAD (n=309, 29.6%) (figure 2).

419 (40.1%) patients were prescribed aspirin, whereas anti-platelet medications other than aspirin were prescribed for 104 (10%) patients. A total of 473 (45.3%) patients were prescribed statin. Based on data collected at the first clinical encounter, 433 (41.5%), 472 (45.2%), 22 (2.1%) and 66 (6.3%) patients were prescribed evidence-based beta-blocker, ACEI/ARB/ARNI, SGLT-2 inhibitors and MRA, respectively. Similarly, among HFrEF patients (n=430) at enrolment, 238 (55.4%), 227 (52.8%), 12 (2.8%) and 45 (10.7%) were prescribed evidence-based beta-blocker, ACEI/ARB/ARNI, SGLT-2 inhibitors and MRA, respectively.

The GDMT improved by increasing beta-blocker, ACEI/ARB/ARNI, SGLT-2 inhibitor and MRA to 497 (59.4%), 471 (56.3%), 110 (13.1%) and 98 (11.7%) at follow-up, respectively (table 2). Regarding CIEDs, 22 (2.1%) pacemakers, 45 (4.31%) ICDs and 5 (0.48%) CRTs were identified in the record.

HIV variables

731 (70%) and 627 (75%) of the patients had controlled HIV at baseline and follow-up, defined by HIV viral load less than 200 copies/mL. The average CD4 count was 721 at baseline and 402 at follow-up. 380 (36.6%) patients were reported to be compliant with medications. The most commonly prescribed ART was the combined form of bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide (Biktarvy) (n=174, 16.7%).

Social variables

Insurance status was evaluated for all patients enrolled and was distributed as follows: 445 (42.7%) Medicaid, 398 (38.2%) Medicare, 128 (12.3%) private insurance, 4 (0.38%) veteran insurance and 68 (6.5%) uninsured. The most common social adversity among population were PSA (31.13%), mental health-related issues (20.7%) and family support (14.5%). 601 (57.6%) patients had encountered at least one and more than one social adversity in their lifetime.

Strength and limitation

The key strength of this cohort of PLHIV with a co-diagnosis of HF is that it is representative of the diverse patient population that is served by the largest public health provider in NYC/NY State.

The dataset includes detailed clinical information, such as comorbidities, laboratory results, echocardiographic findings, medications and CIEDs. Furthermore, this dataset includes comprehensive social worker assessments conducted by an LMSW, ensuring a high level of accuracy in the evaluation of psychosocial factors. This professional evaluation approach distinguishes our dataset from previously conducted studies, providing an invaluable resource for further studies focusing on the role and impact of SDoH on health outcomes.20 Additionally, the study population is notably diverse, reflecting the multicultural communities of New York City. NYC Health+Hospitals constitutes the New York State Safety Net hospital network and offers medical treatment to individuals regardless of their insurance or immigration status, thereby providing valuable information regarding patients who have historically been excluded from the standard healthcare system.

However, this cohort also exhibits significant limitations. First, the enrolled patient population was exclusively recruited from New York City, thereby limiting the generalisability of the findings to suburban and rural communities. Second, the dynamic nature of SDoH presents an ongoing challenge as each patient’s psychosocial status can change within a few months and relevant information may be missed due to the granularity of the follow-up periods. For instance, in the NYC4H cohort, only one baseline psychosocial evaluation was conducted and there were limited follow-up encounters. This may significantly limit sensitivity towards changes in socioeconomic status occurring in a limited timeframe. However, it is worth noting that psychosocial evaluations will be included in the 2025 follow-up planning, thereby addressing this limitation and reducing its negative impact.

Future plans

The data collection for the first follow-up period was completed by November 2023. Currently, the nutritional notes from licensed dietitians are under review. For patients undergoing left or right heart catheterization, the ongoing review of relevant data related to CAD and pulmonary hypertension is in progress. We are scheduled to collect data for the third follow-up period, including the follow-up psychosocial evaluation, by the end of 2025. Due to the high mortality rate within this cohort, our plan is to continue monitoring the patients every 2 years. We aim to sustain this follow-up for 10+ years, contingent on future funding availability. Additionally, we intend to conduct annual follow-ups for specific high-risk groups, such as individuals with pulmonary hypertension, those facing higher social adversities, and participants with poor HIV control. These focused efforts will contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the factors influencing the health outcomes of these vulnerable populations.

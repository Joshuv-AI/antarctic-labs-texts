Data-dependent Bounds with T T -Optimal Best-of-Both-Worlds Guarantees in Multi-Armed Bandits using Stability-Penalty Matching
Quan Nguyen, Shinji Ito, Junpei Komiyama, Nishant A. Mehta

Abstract
Existing data-dependent and best-of-both-worlds regret bounds for multi-armed bandits problems have limited adaptivity as they are either data-dependent but not best-of-both-worlds (BOBW), BOBW but not data-dependent or have sub-optimal O ⁡ ( T ​ ln ⁡ T ) O(\sqrt{T\ln{T}}) worst-case guarantee in the adversarial regime. To overcome these limitations, we propose real-time stability-penalty matching (SPM), a new method for obtaining regret bounds that are simultaneously data-dependent, best-of-both-worlds and T T -optimal for multi-armed bandits problems. In particular, we show that real-time SPM obtains bounds with worst-case guarantees of order O ⁡ ( T ) O(\sqrt{T}) in the adversarial regime and O ⁡ ( ln ⁡ T ) O(\ln{T}) in the stochastic regime while simultaneously being adaptive to data-dependent quantities such as sparsity, variations, and small losses. Our results are obtained by extending the SPM technique for tuning the learning rates in the follow-the-regularized-leader (FTRL) framework, which further indicates that the combination of SPM and FTRL is a promising approach for proving new adaptive bounds in online learning problems.

Introduction

The multi-armed bandits problem
(
Lai and Robbins 1985
;
Auer et al. 2002a
)
is one of the most fundamental frameworks for modeling sequential decision making problems under limited feedback.
In this problem, a learner sequentially interacts with the environment in
T
rounds. In round
t=1,2,\dots
, the learner chooses an action
I_{t}
from a set of
K
available actions and observes a numerical feedback
\ell_{t,I_{t}}\in\mathbb{R}
.
This
\ell_{t,I_{t}}
is an element of a hidden vector
\ell_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
chosen at the beginning of round
t
by an oblivious adversary.
The performance of the learner is its
pseudo-regret

\displaystyle R_{T}=\max_{a\in[K]}R_{T,a}=\max_{a\in[K]}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t,I_{t}}-\ell_{t,a}\right],

(1)

where
\mathbb{E}
denote the expectation taken over all randomness from all
T
rounds.
Existing works have constructed algorithms with
worst-case
regret bounds that hold under the assumption on whether the adversary is adversarial (i.e.,
(\ell_{t})_{t}
are arbitrary) or stochastic (i.e.,
(\ell_{t})_{t}
are drawn i.i.d. from some distribution)
(
Lai and Robbins 1985
;
Auer et al. 2002a
;
Auer et al. 2002b
)
,
best-of-both-worlds
(BOBW) bounds that have worst-case guarantees simultaneously for adversarial and stochastic adversaries
(
Bubeck and Slivkins 2012
;
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
;
Dann et al. 2023
;
Ito et al. 2024
, e.g.)
, or
data-dependent
bounds that are adaptive to the sequence
(\ell_{t})_{t}

(
Wei and Luo 2018
;
Bubeck et al. 2018
;
Ito 2021
;
Ito et al. 2022
;
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
, e.g.)
.
Despite this vast amount of literature on different types of worst-case and adaptive bounds for multi-armed bandits, we are not aware of any works that establish bounds that are
simultaneously
data-dependent, best-of-both-worlds
and
have optimal dependency on
T
.
In particular, existing works suffer from at least one of three limitations: being data-dependent but not BOBW
(
Hazan and Kale 2011
;
Bubeck et al. 2018
;
Wei and Luo 2018
)
, being BOBW but not data-dependent
(
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
;
Dann et al. 2023
)
or having sub-optimal dependency on
T

(
Hazan and Kale 2011
;
Wei and Luo 2018
;
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
;
Ito et al. 2024
)
. In this work, we close this gap in the literature by introducing novel algorithms with regret bounds that are simultaneously BOBW, data-dependent and
T
-optimal.

All of our algorithms are established in the Follow-the-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) framework
(
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
, see e.g.)
, in which the time-varying learning rates are tuned by the Stability-Penalty Matching (SPM) method.
SPM was originally proposed by
Ito et al. 2024
as a principled method for tuning learning rates in FTRL using both the
penalty
and
stability
terms.
More specifically, in round
t
, our algorithms compute a probability vector

\displaystyle q_{t}=\argmin_{x\in\Delta_{K}}\langle L_{t-1},x\rangle+\phi_{t}(x),

(2)

where
\Delta_{K}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{K}_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\sum_{i=1}^{K}x_{i}=1\}
denotes the
K
-dimensional simplex,
L_{t-1}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
is the estimated cumulative loss vector up to round
t-1
and
\phi_{t}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Delta_{K}\to\mathbb{R}
is the regularization function. We use the following specific form for
\phi_{t}
:

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x)=\beta_{t}f(x)+\gamma u(x),

(3)

where
f(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Delta_{K}\to\mathbb{R}_{-},u(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Delta_{K}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
are convex,
\beta_{t}>0
is the learning rate in round
t
and
\gamma
is a constant. Then, the learner draws an arm
I_{t}\sim q_{t}
according to
q_{t}
(or some
p_{t}\in\Delta_{K}
derived from
q_{t}
) and computes an estimated loss vector
\hat{\ell}_{t}
.
Let
D_{t}(x,y)=\phi_{t}(x)-\phi_{t}(y)-\langle\nabla\phi_{t}(y),x-y\rangle
denote the Bregman divergence associated with
\phi_{t}
.
The standard analysis of FTRL
(e.g.
Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020
, Exercise 28.12)
implies that

\displaystyle R_{T,a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\phi_{T+1}(e_{a})-\phi_{1}(q_{1})+\scalebox{0.99}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\phi_{t}(q_{t+1})-\phi_{t+1}(q_{t+1}))+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t}))\right]$}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\gamma u(e_{a})-\beta_{1}f(q_{1})+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_{t})h_{t+1}\right]}_{\text{penalty term}}+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right]}_{\text{stability term}}

where
e_{a}
is the
a
-th vector in the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{K}
,
h_{t+1}
satisfies
(-f(q_{t+1}))\lesssim h_{t+1}
and
z_{t}
satisfies
\beta_{t}\mathbb{E}[\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})]\lesssim\mathbb{E}[z_{t}]
.
SPM carefully chooses
\beta_{1},z_{t}
and
h_{t}
so that
h_{t+1}\leq O(h_{t})
and sets the learning rate of the next round to be

\displaystyle\beta_{t+1}=\beta_{t}+\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}h_{t}}.

(4)

This makes
(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_{t})h_{t+1}
match with
\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}
and implies

R_{T,a}\lesssim\gamma u(e_{a})-\beta_{1}f(q_{1})+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right].

An important insight in SPM is that by picking
f(x)
and
u(x)
appropriately,
\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right]
is naturally adaptive to the adversarial or stochastic nature of the environment
(
Ito et al. 2024
)
.
In our work, we will show that SPM can be made adaptive not only to the nature of the environment but also to the underlying structure of the sequence of losses such as sparsity and total variation.

1.1
Main Contributions and Techniques

Throughout the paper, we will write
O(\square\ln(T),\square\sqrt{T})
to denote a BOBW bound that holds for stochastic and adversarial regimes, respectively, where
\square
contains problem-dependent terms.
The original SPM method
(
Ito et al. 2024
)
used
z_{t}=\Omega(\beta_{t}\mathbb{E}_{I_{t}}[\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})])
, where
\mathbb{E}_{I_{t}}
denotes an expectation taken over
I_{t}
.
Because only one out of
K
arms is observable in each round
t
, this in-expectation form of
z_{t}
inevitably requires taking the trivial bounds (e.g.
1
) of the losses into its computation, thus limiting its adaptivity to
(\ell_{t})_{t}
. Our work overcomes this limitation by setting

\displaystyle z_{t}=\Omega(\beta_{t}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t}))).

(5)

We call this
real-time SPM
, since
z_{t}
depends on the observed arm
I_{t}
.
The main technical challenge is now
z_{t}
can be very large since it grows with
\mathrm{poly}(\frac{1}{p_{t,I_{t}}})
.
At the same time, we need to limit the amount of explicit exploration to obtain a BOBW bound for stochastic bandits.

Our results also hold for the more general adversarial regime with self-bounding constraint setting
(
Zimmert and Seldin 2021

1.2
Problem Setup

For an integer
N
, let
[N]=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
denote the set of integers from
1
to
N
.
We study the multi-armed bandits problem
(
Lai and Robbins 1985
;
Auer et al. 2002a
)
in which a learner is given
K
arms and interacts with the environment in
T
rounds.
In each round
t
, an adversary selects a hidden vector
\ell_{t}=(\ell_{t,1},\ell_{t,2},\dots,\ell_{t,K})^{\top}
.
The learner chooses one arm
I_{t}\in[K]
and observes its loss
\ell_{t,I_{t}}
. We assume
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell_{t,i}\right\rvert}\leq 1
for all
t\in[T],i\in[K]
.
The learner aims to minimize its
regret
R_{T}
over
T
rounds, defined by Equation (
1
).

We are interested in developing learning algorithms with provable upper bounds on
R_{T}
that hold simultaneously for two regimes: adversarial
(
Auer et al. 2002a
)
and adversarial with a
(\Delta,C,T)
self-bounding constraint
(
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
)
. In the
adversarial regime
, no assumption is made on how the adversary generates
(\ell_{t})_{t\in[T]}
. The adversarial regime with a
(\Delta,C,T)
self-bounding constraint
(
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
)
is given below.

Definition 1
.

(Adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint)
For
T\geq 1,\Delta\in[0,1]^{K}
and
C\geq 0
, the problem is in adversarial regime with a
(\Delta,C,T)
self-bounding constraint if the regret of any algorithm at time
T
satisfies

R_{T}\geq\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\Delta_{i}\mathbb{P}(I_{t}=i)-C.

As noted in
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
, the stochastic bandits setting
(
Lai and Robbins 1985
)
satisfies Definition
1
.
We also use the common assumption that there exists an optimal arm
i^{*}
such that
\Delta_{i}>0
for all
i\neq i^{*}
, that is, the optimal arm is unique. Let
\Delta_{\min}=\min_{i\in[K]}\{\Delta_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\Delta_{i}>0\}
.

We focus on obtaining bounds that are adaptive not only to the adversary’s regime but also to the data-dependent properties of the loss sequence
(\ell_{t})_{t\in[T]}
.
The following data-dependent quantities are considered in our work.

•

Sparsity of losses

(
Kwon and Perchet 2016
)
. All loss vectors have at most
1\leq S\leq K
non-zero elements, i.e.,
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}_{0}\leq S
, where
S
is unknown.

•

Variation of losses

(
Hazan and Kale 2011
;
Ito et al. 2022
)
The total variation of the sequence
(\ell_{t})_{t}
is
Q=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\ell_{s}\right\rVert}^{2}_{2}
. The
\ell_{\infty}
-norm total variation is
Q_{\infty}=\min_{\bar{\ell}\in[0,1]^{K}}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}-\bar{\ell}\right\rVert}^{2}_{\infty}
.

•

Best-arm loss
. For non-negative losses, we consider the cumulative loss of the best arm
L_{*}=\min_{i\in[K]}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\ell_{t,i}
.

2
Stability-Penalty Matching with Real-Time Stability Term

Let
\tilde{p}=\min(1-p,p)
for
p\in[0,1]
. We use the notation
f\lesssim g
to denote
f=O(g)
.
To obtain data-dependent bounds using SPM, we use SPM where the stability term is a function of the
observed
loss, i.e.,
z_{t}
satisfies Equation (
5
).
Note that
z_{t}
grows with
\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}
and
\frac{1}{p_{t,I_{t}}}
.
The benefit of this real-time
z_{t}
is that data-dependent quantities
such as sparsity and total variation naturally come out of
\mathbb{E}[z_{t}]
.
For example, in Algorithm
3
for bandits with sparse losses
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}_{0}\leq S
, we use
z_{t}=O(\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-\alpha}\frac{\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}{p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
It follows that
\mathbb{E}[z_{t}]=O(\sum_{i\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\ell_{t,i}\neq 0}\tilde{p}_{t,i}^{1-\alpha}\ell_{t,i}^{2})\leq O(S^{\alpha})
, leading to the
O(\sqrt{ST\ln{K}})
bound.
The main challenge in using the real-time
z_{t}
is the value of
z_{t}
can be unbounded whenever
p_{t,I_{t}}
is very small.
It follows that
z_{\max}=\max_{t\in[T]}z_{t}
can be unbounded, which makes it difficult to apply existing techniques in
Ito et al. 2024
that bounds
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}]
by a quantity that grows with
\mathbb{E}[z_{\max}]
.
We resolve this challenge by using the following technical lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For any
T\geq 1,z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T}\geq 0,h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T}>0
and a sequence
\beta_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T}
defined by Equation (
4
), let
F(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}
and
G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\sqrt{\sum_{s=1}^{t}\frac{z_{s}}{h_{s}}}}
. We have

\displaystyle F(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})\lesssim G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})+\left(\max_{t\in[T]}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right)\ln\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{h_{t}}\right).

(6)

Proof.

(Sketch)
Our proof extends from the proof of
Ito et al. 2024
.
Similar to the proof of
Ito et al. 2024
, we define a new sequence
\beta^{\prime}_{t}=\sqrt{\beta_{1}^{2}+2\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\frac{z_{s}}{h_{s}}}
and consider the set of rounds
E=\{t\in[T]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\beta^{\prime}_{t+1}\geq\sqrt{2}\beta^{\prime}_{t}\}
.
The complement of
E
is
E^{c}=[T]\setminus E
. We have

\displaystyle F(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum_{t\in E^{c}}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}}_{(a)}+\underbrace{\sum_{t\in E}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}}_{(b)},

where
(a)
is bounded by
G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})
as in
Ito et al. 2024
, and
(b)
is bounded by

\displaystyle(b)\leq\left(\max_{t\in[T]}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right)\mathinner{\!\left\lvert E\right\rvert}\leq\left(\max_{t\in[T]}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right)\log_{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{T+1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{1}}\right)\lesssim\left(\max_{t\in[T]}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right)\ln\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{h_{t}}\right),

where the second inequality is from the fact that
\beta^{\prime}_{t}
is multiplied by at least
\sqrt{2}
after every round in
E
; thus there can be at most
\log_{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\beta^{\prime}_{T+1}}{\beta^{\prime}_{1}}
such multiplications.
∎

Lemma
2
implies that if (I) the sum
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{h_{t}}]
grows with
\mathrm{poly}(T)
and (II)
\max_{t}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}
is small, then
\mathbb{E}[F(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})]
grows dominantly with
\mathbb{E}[G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})]
plus an
O(\ln(T))
term. Hence, we can safely ignore other terms and focus only on bounding
G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})
. The proof of
Ito et al. 2024
already showed that

\displaystyle G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{\ln(T)\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t}z_{t}}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}h_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}z_{t}},\sqrt{h_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}z_{t}}\right\}.

(7)

In the rest of the paper, we will show that different choices of the (hybrid) regularization function lead to specific forms of
z_{t}
and
h_{t}
such that not only do both conditions (I) and (II) hold but also they
imply BOBW data-dependent bounds with optimal dependency on
T
from (
7
).

Application I: BOBW Bounds for Bandits with Sparse Losses

{algorithm}

[t]

\KwIn
K\geq 3,T\geq 4K,\alpha\in(0,1),\beta_{1}=\frac{8K}{1-\alpha},\gamma=\max(6,48\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}),d=2
.
Initialize

for

\For

Compute
p_{t}=\left(1-\frac{K}{T}\right)q_{t}+\frac{1}{T}\bm{1}

Draw

and observe
\ell_{t,I_{t}}

Compute loss estimate
\hat{\ell}_{t,i}=\frac{\ell_{t,i}\mathbb{1}\{I_{t}=i\}}{p_{t,i}}
and update
L_{t,i}=L_{t-1,i}+\hat{\ell}_{t,i}

Compute
z_{t}=\min\left(\frac{(6d)^{2-\alpha}}{2(1-\alpha)}\min(p_{t,I_{t}},1-p_{t,I_{t}})^{2-\alpha}\hat{\ell}^{2}_{t,I_{t}},\frac{\beta_{t}18d^{2}}{\gamma}\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\right)

Compute
h_{t}=\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{t,i}^{\alpha}-1)\right)

Compute
\beta_{t+1}=\beta_{t}+\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}h_{t}}

Real-time SPM with hybrid regularization for losses in
[-1,1]
.

We consider the multi-armed bandits setting with sparse losses
(
Kwon and Perchet 2016
)
, in which the loss vector
\ell_{t}\in[-1,1]^{K}
has at most
S
non-zero elements, i.e.,
\max_{t\in[T]}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}_{0}\leq S
.
Note that
S
is unknown to the learner.
Let
\psi_{TE}(p)=\frac{1}{\alpha}(1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}^{\alpha})

be the
\alpha
-Tsallis entropy with some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\psi_{LB}(p)=-\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(p_{i})
be the log-barrier function.
Our approach for this setting is in Algorithm
3
, in which we use the hybrid regularizer
\phi_{t}(p)=\beta_{t}\psi_{TE}(p)+\gamma\psi_{LB}(p)
to obtain

\displaystyle q_{t}=\argmin_{p\in\Delta_{K}}\{\langle L_{t-1},p\rangle+\beta_{t}\psi_{TE}(p)+\gamma\psi_{LB}(p)\},

Then, we mix
q_{t}
with
\frac{1}{T}
-uniform exploration to obtain the sampling probability
p_{t}
, i.e.,
p_{t}=\left(1-\frac{K}{T}\right)q_{t}+\frac{1}{T}\bm{1}
.
The learning rates
(\beta_{t})_{t}
are set by the SPM rule by
Ito et al. 2024
as

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\frac{8K}{1-\alpha},\,\,\beta_{t+1}=\beta_{t}+\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}h_{t}},

(8)

where

\displaystyle z_{t}=\min\left(\frac{(6d)^{2-\alpha}}{2(1-\alpha)}\min(p_{t,I_{t}},1-p_{t,I_{t}})^{2-\alpha}\hat{\ell}^{2}_{t,I_{t}},\beta_{t}\frac{18d^{2}}{\gamma}\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\right),\,\,h_{t}=(-\psi_{TE}(p_{t})),

(9)

and
\gamma=\max(6,48\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}),d=2
.
Note that
\beta_{1}\geq\frac{4K}{(\omega-1)(1-\omega^{\alpha-1})}
for
\omega=2
.
The following theorem states the BOBW bounds of Algorithm
3
.

Theorem 3
.

For any
K\geq 4,T\geq 4k
, Algorithm
3
guarantees the following bounds simultaneously

•

In the adversarial regime:

\displaystyle R_{T}\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)S^{\alpha}T}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right)

(10)

•

In the adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint:

\displaystyle R_{T}\leq O\left(\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}S^{\alpha}\ln(T)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta_{\min}}+\sqrt{C\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}S^{\alpha}\ln(T)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta_{\min}}}+\sqrt{\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}S^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right)

(11)

In Appendix
G
, we show that by setting
\alpha=1-\frac{1}{2\ln(K)}
, we obtain
\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)S^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\lesssim S^{\alpha}\ln(K)
and
\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}S^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\lesssim S^{\alpha}\ln(K)
.
In the adversarial regime, Theorem
3
recovers the
O(\sqrt{ST\ln(K)})
bound in
Bubeck et al. 2018
and
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
while still being
S
-agnostic.
In the adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint, the bound becomes
O(\frac{S\ln(K)\ln(T)}{\Delta_{\min}})
which has an optimal dependency on
T
. To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
3
is the first result for bandits with sparse signed losses that is simultaneously
S
-agnostic,
T
-optimal and BOBW.
Also, our approach is more computationally efficient than that of
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
as we do not need to solve any additional optimization problems to compute the learning rates
(\beta_{t})_{t}
.

Remark 4
.

When
S
is known, then
\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)S^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}

can be further bounded by
6S\ln(\frac{K}{S})
. Consider only the case where
S
is sufficiently small so that
e^{2}S\leq K
(the other direction trivially leads to
O(\frac{S}{\alpha(1-\alpha)})
).
Letting
\alpha=1-\frac{1}{\ln(K/S)}
, then
(\frac{K-1}{S})^{1-\alpha}\leq(\frac{K}{S})^{1-\alpha}=e
. Since
\ln(K/S)\geq 2
, we have
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)S^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K^{1-\alpha}S^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}=S\left(\frac{K}{S}\right)^{1-\alpha}\frac{\ln(K/S)}{\alpha}=\frac{eS\ln(K/S)}{\alpha}\leq 6S\ln(K/S).

This result shows that an
O(\sqrt{ST\ln(K/S)})
upper bound is attainable even for signed losses, which resolves an open question posed in
(
Kwon and Perchet 2016
, Remark 12)
.

Proof Sketch for Theorem

As mentioned in Section

, we first show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{h_{t}}\right]

Then, the condition (I) follows from Lemma

h_{t}\geq\frac{1-\alpha}{4\alpha}T^{-\alpha}
and
E_{I_{t}}[z_{t}]\leq\frac{(6d)^{2-\alpha}}{2(1-\alpha)}S^{\alpha}
.
Jensen’s inequality implies that
\mathbb{E}\left[\ln\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{h_{t}}\right)\right]\leq\ln\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{h_{t}}\right]\right)
.
Combining this with Lemma
11
, we conclude that
\mathbb{E}[F(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})]
grows dominantly with
\mathbb{E}[G(z_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T},h_{1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}T})]

The last part of the proof is showing that plugging

and
h_{t}
from (

In the adversarial regime, the bound (

In the adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint, we can prove (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[h_{t}z_{t}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(6d)^{2-\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta_{\min}}{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}S^{\alpha}}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{t,i}\Delta_{i}\right].

and then following the same argument as in

3.2
A Lower Bound for Problems with Soft Sparsity Constraint

It remains an open question whether the BOBW bounds in Theorem
3
are tight under the hard constraint
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}_{0}\leq S
.
This hard-constraint problem belongs to a broader class of settings with a more relaxed constraint, in which there exists an
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
1\leq U\leq K^{\alpha}
such that for all
t\in[T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell_{t,i}\right\rvert}^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\leq U.

(12)

In other words, the sparsity constraint holds in expectation. Obviously, the hard-constraint setting with
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}_{0}\leq S
satisfies (
12
) for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
U=S^{\alpha}
.
Moreover, by using the same Algorithm
3
and straightforward modifications in its proof, we can obtain the corresponding
O(\frac{K^{1-\alpha}U}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta_{\min}}\ln{T})
and
O(\sqrt{\frac{K^{1-\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}UT})
BOBW bounds for stochastic and adversarial regimes, respectively.
The following theorem, whose proof is in Section
C
, shows near-matching lower bounds for problems with soft sparsity constraint defined in (
12
).

Theorem 5
.

(Instance-Dependent Lower Bound) For any consistent algorithm, for any
K\geq 4,\alpha\in(0,1)
and
1\leq U\leq\frac{K^{\alpha}}{4}
, there exists a
K
-armed stochastic bandit instance with
\Delta_{\min}\in(0,1)
and loss distribution satisfying (
12
) such that

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{R_{T}}{\ln(T)}=\Omega\left(\frac{K^{1-\alpha}U}{\Delta_{\min}}\right).

(Minimax Lower Bound) For any algorithm, for any
K\geq 4,\alpha\in(0,1)
and
U\leq K^{\alpha}
, there exists an adversarial bandit instance with
K
arms and loss distribution satisfying (
12
) such that

\displaystyle R_{T}=\Omega(\sqrt{K^{1-\alpha}UT}).

3.3
Implications for Bandits with Adversarially Changing Action Sets

Intuitively, the sparsity constraint
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}_{0}\leq S
indicates that there are at most
S
arms containing non-trivial information in each round, however the learner
does not know the arms with non-trivial information.
In this sense, sparse bandits is conceptually more difficult than adversarial sleeping bandits
(
Kleinberg et al. 2010
)
, where in each round
t
the learner is given, by an adversary, a set
{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\subseteq[K]
of active arms to choose from.
Note that the learner is not allowed to choose an arm in
[K]\setminus{\mathbb{A}}_{t}
.
The performance of the learner is measured by its per-action regret

\displaystyle R_{T,a}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{1}\{a\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\}(\ell_{t,I_{t}}-\ell_{t,a}).

A natural question is whether Algorithm
3
can be extended to this adversarial sleeping bandits setting.
The following theorem answers this question in the positive.

Theorem 6
.

For any
K\geq 4,T\geq 4k
, Algorithm
F
(in Appendix
F
) guarantees that for all
a\in[K]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[R_{T,a}]\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)(\max_{t\in[T]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\right\rvert})^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}T}\right),

Our Algorithm
F
is a combination of Algorithm
3
and the SB-EXP3 algorithm in
Nguyen and Mehta 2024
. More specifically, Algorithm
F
uses the estimated cumulative
regret
(instead of losses) to compute
q_{t}
in the FTRL update. Then, the sampling probability vector
p_{t}
is obtained by a filtering step
p_{t,i}=\frac{q_{t,i}\mathbb{1}\{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\}}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}q_{t,j}\mathbb{1}\{j\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\}}
.
While the bound in Theorem
6
is of the same order as in
Nguyen and Mehta 2024
, it has the advantage of not requiring the knowledge of
\max_{t}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\right\rvert}
in advance nor any complicated two-level doubling trick.

4
Application II:
\sqrt{Q\ln(K)}
Upper Bound with Unknown
Q
using Optimistic FTRL

In this section, we propose a new approach for obtaining a BOBW
O(\frac{K\ln{T}}{\Delta_{\min}},\sqrt{Q\ln{K}})
-bound with unknown
Q
.
For ease of exposition, we assume losses are in
[0,1]
and note that the analysis can be easily extended for losses in
[-1,1]
.
The new approach is based on applying real-time SPM on the Optimistic FTRL framework
(
Rakhlin and Sridharan 2013
)
, and then combining with the Reservoir Sampling algorithm
(
Hazan and Kale 2011
)
.

In principle, our algorithm follows the same framework as
Hazan and Kale 2011
;
Bubeck et al. 2018
where the learner maintains a reservoir
{\mathbb{S}}_{i}
of observed losses for each arm
i\in[K]
and then uses the estimated mean
m_{t,i}=\tilde{\mu}_{t,i}
of these reservoirs as the optimistic vector
m_{t}
in Optimistic FTRL.
In each round
t
, the learner chooses to perform either a reservoir sampling step for updating the reservoir
{\mathbb{S}}_{i}
, or a FTRL learning step for minimizing the regret.
When the FTRL learning step is performed in round
t
, the vector
q_{t}
is computed by

\displaystyle q_{t}=\argmin_{x\in\Delta_{K}}\langle m_{t}+L_{t-1},x\rangle+\beta_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}(1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}x_{i}^{\alpha})\right)-\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(x_{i}).

(13)

Similar to Algorithm
3
, the sampling probability vector
p_{t}
is obtained by mixing with
\frac{1}{T}
, i.e,
p_{t}=\left(1-\frac{K}{T}\right)q_{t}+\frac{1}{T}\bm{1}
.
After an arm
I_{t}\sim p_{t}
is drawn, the loss estimates are
\hat{\ell}_{t,i}=m_{t,i}+\frac{(\ell_{t,i}-m_{t,i})\mathbb{1}\{I_{t}=i\}}{p_{t,i}}
.
The learning rates
(\beta_{t})_{t}
are computed by real-time SPM, with
z_{t}
and
h_{t}
defined as

\displaystyle z_{t}=\min\left(\frac{(6d)^{2-\alpha}}{2(1-\alpha)}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-\alpha}(\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}-m_{t,I_{t}})^{2},\frac{\beta_{t}18d^{2}}{\gamma}(\ell_{t,I_{t}}-m_{t,I_{t}})^{2}\right),\quad h_{t}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{t,i}^{\alpha}-1\right).

The full procedure is given in Algorithm
D.3
in Appendix
D
.
The following theorem states the BOBW bound for this approach.

Theorem 7
.

Algorithm
D.3
(in Appendix
D
) guarantees the following bounds simultaneously

•

In the adversarial regime:

\displaystyle R_{T}\leq O\left(\sqrt{\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)Q}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right).

(14)

•

In the adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint:

\displaystyle R_{T}\leq O\left(\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}K^{\alpha}\ln(T)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta_{\min}}+\sqrt{C\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}K^{\alpha}\ln(T)}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\Delta_{\min}}}+\sqrt{\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}K^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right)

(15)

Proof.

(Sketch)
Our analysis follows from the analysis of Algorithm
3
and the observation by
Hazan and Kale 2011
that the reservoir sampling steps only add an
O(\ln(T)^{2})
amount to the regret bound.
As a result, the bound (
15
) for adversarial regime with a self-bounding constraint follows almost identically to that of Algorithm
3
.
For the bound (
14
) in the adversarial regime, the total variation
Q
naturally comes out of
\sum_{t=1}^{T}z_{t}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}z_{t}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\ell_{t,i}-m_{t,i})^{2}\right]=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}-\tilde{\mu}_{t}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\right]\qquad(\text{since }m_{t}=\tilde{\mu}_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}-\mu_{T}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\tilde{\mu}_{t}-\mu_{t}\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\left(Q+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{Q}{t\ln(T)}\right)\leq O\left(\frac{Q}{1-\alpha}\right),

where the second inequality follows from triangle inequality and
Lemma 10 in
Hazan and Kale 2011
, the third inequality is by Lemma 11 in
Hazan and Kale 2011
, and the last inequality is due to
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{t\ln{T}}\leq O(1)
. Together with (
7
) and
h_{\max}\leq\frac{K^{1-\alpha}-1}{\alpha}
, this implies (
14
).
∎

Remark 8
.

While existing works
(
Hazan and Kale 2011
;
Bubeck et al. 2018
)
require either the knowledge of
Q
or sophisticated doubling tricks to estimate
Q
, our Algorithm
D.3
does not require such knowledge or any tricks.
When
\alpha\to 1
, the bound in (
14
) becomes
O(\sqrt{Q\ln(K)})
. This bound matches the best known upper bound in
Bubeck et al. 2018
and never exceeds
O(\sqrt{TK\ln(K)})
in the worst case, all while simultaneously having a
T
-optimal best-of-both-worlds guarantee.

\KwIn

Coordinate-wise SPM with hybrid regularization for losses in
[0,1]
.

We further generalize the SPM framework by introducing a new technique called coordinate-wise SPM (
CoWSPM
).
As the name suggests,
CoWSPM
maintains separate learning rate
\beta_{t,i}
, stability term
z_{t,i}
and penalty term
h_{t,i}
for each arm
i\in[K]
.
In each round
t
,
CoWSPM
updates the learning rates for each arm using the SPM update formula (
4
), i.e.,

\displaystyle\beta_{t+1,i}=\beta_{t,i}+\frac{z_{t,i}}{\beta_{t,i}h_{t,i}}.

(16)

Obviously, if
(z_{t,i})_{i\in[K]}
and
(h_{t,i})_{i\in[K]}
take the same values across all arms, this this approach recovers Algorithm
3
.
Instead, we adopt a different approach where
z_{t,i}=0
for all
i\neq I_{t}
so that only the learning rate
\beta_{t,I_{t}}
of the observed arm
I_{t}
is updated in round
t
.
The full procedure of
CoWSPM
is given in Algorithm
5
, which uses the Optimistic FTRL framework with

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\beta_{t,i}\left(\frac{-x_{i}^{\alpha}}{\alpha}+(1-x_{i})\ln(1-x_{i})+x_{i}\right)-\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(x_{i}).

(17)

This regularization function contains not only the
\alpha
-Tsallis entropy, but also a part of the Shannon entropy and a linear term.
The addition of these terms into the regularizer has been done in
Ito et al. 2022
in order to have a regret bound containing the quantity
\tilde{p}_{t,i}=\min(p_{t,i},1-p_{t,i})
for the stochastic setting.
This technique has a similar impact in our work, where it allows us to bound
z_{t,i}
by a quantity containing
\tilde{p}_{t,i}
.
However, while we use the same technique to introduce
\tilde{p}_{t,i}
into our bounds, our analysis develops fundamentally different technical lemmas from that of
Ito et al. 2022
in order to use this new regularizer in the real-time SPM framework.
Next, to ensure that only
\beta_{t,I_{t}}
is updated, we set
z_{t,i}
by

\displaystyle z_{t,i}=\mathbb{1}\{i=I_{t}\}(\ell_{t,I_{t}}-m_{t,I_{t}})^{2}\min\left(\frac{(6d)^{2-\alpha}}{2(1-\alpha)}\min\left\{p_{t,I_{t}}^{-\alpha},\frac{1-p_{t,I_{t}}}{p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}\right\},\frac{\beta_{t,i}18d^{2}}{\gamma}\right),

(18)

so that
z_{t,I_{t}}\geq 0
and
z_{t,i}=0
for
i\neq I_{t}
.
The following theorem states the BOBW data-dependent bound of Algorithm
5
, whose full proof is given in Appendix
E
.
The proof sketch outlines the main technical challenges in the analysis of Algorithm
5
.

Theorem 9
.

For any
K\geq 4,T\geq 4k
,
CoWSPM
(Algorithm
5
) with
\alpha\in(0,1)
guarantees the following bounds simultaneously

•

In the adversarial regime:

\displaystyle R_{T}\lesssim\min\left\{\sqrt{K\ln(T)\min(Q_{\infty},L^{*},T-L^{*})},K^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\sqrt{KT}\right\}.

•

In the stochastic regime:

\displaystyle R_{T}\lesssim\frac{1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{\ln(T)}{\Delta_{i}}.

Proof.

(Sketch)
Intuitively, coordinate-wise SPM consists of
K
separate real-time SPM processes, one for each arm.
Similar to
Ito et al. 2022
, we find that this more refined approach enables deriving a bound (for the adversarial regime) that is adaptive to simultaneously different data-dependent quantities such as
Q_{\infty}
and
L^{*}
.
However, having separate learning rates introduces several new technical challenges. First, the analysis developed for Algorithm
3
that bounds
q_{t+1,i}=O(q_{t,i})
for all
i\in[K]
no longer applies because in each round
t
, the learning rates
(\beta_{t,i})_{i\in[K]}
can be arbitrarily different from each other.
The
CoWSPM
algorithm resolves this by using
\beta_{t+1,i}=\beta_{t,i}
for
i\neq I_{T}
so that it only need
q_{t+1,i}=O(q_{t,i})
to hold for
i=I_{t}
since

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(q_{t+1})-\phi_{t+1}(q_{t+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}(\beta_{t+1,i}-\beta_{t,i})(-f(q_{t+1,i}))=(\beta_{t+1,I_{t}}-\beta_{t,I_{t}})f(q_{t+1,I_{t}}).

The second and also more important challenge is that even if
q_{t+1,i}=O(q_{t,i})
, the naive decomposition of the
\alpha
-Tsallis entropy into its coordinate-wise form
-\psi_{TE}(x)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{K}(x_{i}^{\alpha}-x_{i})
and then assigning
h_{t,i}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(x_{i}^{\alpha}-x_{i})
does
not
guarantee that
h_{t+1,i}=O(h_{t,i})
. This is because the function
x\mapsto x^{\alpha}-x
gets arbitrarily close to
0
when
x
gets close to
1
.
This prompts a different choice for
h_{t,i}
rather than
-f(p_{t,i})
.
Algorithm
5
uses
h_{t,i}=\frac{1}{\alpha}p_{t,i}^{\alpha}
, which is monotonically increasing and ensures that
h_{t+1,I_{t}}=O(h_{t,I_{t}})
for
q_{t+1,i}=O(q_{t,i})
.
This choice of
h_{t,i}
is justified by the technical Lemma
30
, which states that
(x-1)\ln(1-x)\leq x^{\alpha}
for any
x,\alpha\in[0,1]
.

Finally, we prove that with
z_{t,i}
defined in (
18
), the product
\mathbb{E}[h_{t,i}z_{t,i}]
is upper bounded by a quantity containing
\tilde{p}_{t,i}
and thus an
O(\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{\ln{T}}{\Delta_{i}})
regret bound holds for stochastic bandits.
This is handled by Lemma
34
, which shows that
\mathbb{E}_{I_{t}}\left[z_{t,i}\right]\leq 2\min(p_{t,i},1-p_{t,i})
.
∎

Remark 10
.

Theorem
9
holds for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In particular, for
\alpha\neq\frac{1}{2}
, we do not require any additional assumptions such as the
\Delta_{i}
being known in order to get the
T
-optimal BOBW bound.
This is a major difference compared to the Tsallis-INF algorithm
(
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
)
.
On the other hand, the adversarial bound in Theorem
9
has an extra factor
\sqrt{K^{\alpha}}
. It is unclear to us whether this extra factor is a fundamental limitation of
CoWSPM
or an artifact of our analysis.

6
Conclusion and Future Works

We developed real-time SPM, an extension of the SPM method originally developed for obtaining best-of-both-worlds bounds in bandits problems.
We showed that real-time SPM algorithms achieve novel bounds that are simultaneously best-of-both-worlds, data-dependent and have optimal dependency on
T
in both stochastic and adversarial regimes. Our bounds also have optimal dependency on the data-dependent quantities such as sparsity or total variation of the loss sequence without knowing them nor using sophisticated estimation tricks. Future work includes applying real-time SPM on other bandits problems, such as contextual linear bandits, and making real-time SPM adaptive towards other challenging data-dependent quantities like
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
-norm path-length bounds.

Appendix A
Related Works

Due to the vast literature on BOBW and data-dependent bounds in various bandits learning settings, this sections presents only the most relevant works in multi-armed bandits.
A more comprehensive list of related works can be found in
Ito et al. 2024
;
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
and references therein.

Best-of-both-worlds bounds.
The BOBW bounds in our paper are derived using the SPM method for tuning learning rates in the FTRL framework, originally proposed in
Ito et al. 2024
. For stochastic bandits, our
O(\frac{K\ln{T}}{\Delta_{\min}})
bound in Sections
3
and
4
matches that of
Wei and Luo 2018
;
Ito et al. 2024
, and our
O(\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{\ln{T}}{\Delta_{i}})
bound in Section
5
matches that of
Zimmert and Seldin 2021
;
Ito 2021
.
Both of these bounds are looser than the
O(\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2}\ln{T}}{\Delta_{i}})
in
Ito et al. 2022
obtained by a more specialized approach, where
\sigma_{i}^{2}
is the variance of the losses of a sub-optimal arm
i
.
However, except for
Ito et al. 2024
, these existing works have an
O(\sqrt{T\ln{T}})
worst-case bound for adversarial bandits, which contains an extra
\ln{T}
factor compared to our work.
Our BOBW bound also have data-dependent guarantees, which is an advantage over
Ito et al. 2024
.
For bandits with sparse losses,
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
similarly obtained bounds that are both BOBW and dependent on the sparsity constraint; however their bounds contain extra factors of
\ln(KT)
in stochastic bandits and
\sqrt{\ln{T}}
in adversarial bandits compared to our results.

Data-dependent bounds.
We study the following data-dependent quantities: sparsity of losses, total variations and small losses.
For bandits with sparse negative losses where
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\ell_{t}\right\rVert}\leq S
and
S
is unknown, our
O(\frac{S\ln{T}}{\Delta_{\min}},\sqrt{ST\ln(K)})
BOBW bound is the first
S
-agnostic and
T
-optimal BOBW bound for this setting, which improves upon on the bound of
Tsuchiya et al. 2023
and matches the best known bound for adversarial bandits in
Bubeck et al. 2018
.
When the total variations
Q,Q_{\infty}
and/or the loss of the best arm
L^{*}
(defined in Section
1.2
) are small, our algorithms are based on the optimistic FTRL (OFTRL) framework similar to
Hazan and Kale 2011
;
Bubeck et al. 2018
;
Ito et al. 2022
.
The dependency on
Q,Q_{\infty}
and
L^{*}
in our results match the best known bounds in these works, while our BOBW bounds have an optimal
O(\square\ln{T},\square\sqrt{T})
dependency on
T
.
Particularly, our coordinate-wise real-time SPM algorithm in Section
5
can be seen as a
T
-optimal variant of the algorithm in
Ito et al. 2022
, which share the idea of using separate learning rates for each arm.

Lemma 15

For any

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\argmin_{p\in\Delta_{K}}\langle L,p\rangle+\beta\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}(1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}^{\alpha})\right)-\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(p_{i}),

Z
and

has the opposite sign to

\displaystyle x

C.1

Let
i^{*}\in[K]
be fixed. We pick
\Delta_{\min}=\frac{U}{K^{\alpha}+1}
so that
\Delta_{\min}\leq 0.25
.
Our construction is as follows:

\displaystyle\ell_{t}=\begin{cases}-\bm{1}&\qquad\text{with probability }\Delta_{\min},\\
-e_{i^{*}}&\qquad\text{with probability }\Delta_{\min},\\
\mathbf{0}&\qquad\text{with probability }1-2\Delta_{\min},\end{cases}

where
e_{i^{*}}
is the
i^{*}
-th vector in the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
The expected loss vector is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\ell_{t}\right]=-\Delta_{\min}\bm{1}-\Delta_{\min}e_{i^{*}}.

It follows that
\Delta_{i}=\Delta_{\min}
for all
i\in[K]\setminus\{i^{*}\}
. In addition, the losses of arm
i^{*}
follow a Bernoulli distribution
\mathrm{Ber}(2\Delta_{\min})
while the losses of sub-optimal arms follow a Bernoulli distribution
\mathrm{Ber}(\Delta_{\min})
.
We verify that the constraint in (
12
) holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell_{t,i}\right\rvert}^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}\right]=\Delta_{\min}K^{\alpha}+\Delta_{\min}=\Delta_{\min}(K^{\alpha}+1)=U.

For any consistent algorithm such that
R_{T}=o(T^{x})
for any
x>0
, by
Lai and Robbins 1985
, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{R_{T}}{\ln(T)}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\neq i^{*}}^{K}\frac{\Delta_{i}}{KL(\Delta_{\min}\;\|\;2\Delta_{\min})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{K\Delta_{\min}}{2KL(\Delta_{\min}\;\|\;2\Delta_{\min})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{K}{2}

\displaystyle=K^{1-\alpha}\frac{K^{\alpha}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq K^{1-\alpha}\frac{K^{\alpha}+1}{4}=\frac{K^{1-\alpha}U}{4\Delta_{\min}},

where the second inequality is from
K-1\geq\frac{K}{2}
for all
K\geq 4
, the third inequality is
KL(p\;\|\;2p)\leq p
for
p\leq 0.25
by Lemma

, and the last inequality is
K^{\alpha}\geq\frac{K^{\alpha}+1}{2}
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{R_{T}}{\ln(T)}\gtrsim\frac{K^{1-\alpha}U}{2\Delta_{\min}}.

Lemma 25
.

For any

\displaystyle KL(p\;\|\;2p)\leq p.

\displaystyle KL(p\;\|\;2p)

\displaystyle=-p\ln(2)+(1-p)\ln\left(\frac{1-p}{1-2p}\right)

\displaystyle=-p\ln(2)+(1-p)\ln\left(1+\frac{p}{1-2p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-p\ln(2)+(1-p)\frac{p}{1-2p}

\displaystyle\leq-p\ln(2)+1.5p\leq p

for all

C.2

For the adversarial lower bound, we construct a neutral environment
V_{0}
and
K
competing environments
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{K}
, where:

•

On
V_{0}
, the loss function is chosen by

\displaystyle\ell_{t}=\begin{cases}-\bm{1}&\qquad\text{with probability }\eta,\\
\mathbf{0}&\qquad\text{with probability }1-\eta.\end{cases}

It follows that the losses of all arms follow a Bernoulli distribution
\mathrm{Ber}(\eta)
on
V_{0}
.

•

On
V_{i}
for
i\in[K]
, the loss function is chosen by

\displaystyle\ell_{t}=\begin{cases}-\bm{1}&\qquad\text{with probability }\eta,\\
-e_{i}&\qquad\text{with probability }{\epsilon},\\
\mathbf{0}&\qquad\text{with probability }1-\eta-{\epsilon}.\end{cases}

It follows that except for arm
i
, the losses of all other arms follow a Bernoulli distribution
\mathrm{Ber}(\eta)
on
V_{i}
. The loss of arm
i
follows
\mathrm{Ber}(\eta+{\epsilon})
.

Here,
\eta
and
{\epsilon}
are constants chosen to be the solution of the following system of (in)equalities:

•

\eta+{\epsilon}\leq\frac{1}{4}
.

•

\eta K^{\alpha}+{\epsilon}=U
.

•

\frac{T}{K}\frac{8{\epsilon}^{2}}{\eta}=1
.

•

\eta K^{\alpha}\geq\frac{U}{2}
.

Note that for all
K\geq 4,T\geq 4K
and
U\leq\frac{K^{\alpha}}{4}
, the solution

\displaystyle\sqrt{\eta}=\frac{-\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}}+\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}+4K^{\alpha}U}}{2K^{\alpha}},

(64)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\epsilon}=\sqrt{\frac{\eta K}{8T}}

satisfies the system of inequalities since

\displaystyle\sqrt{\eta}=\frac{-\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}}+\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}+4K^{\alpha}U}}{2K^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{32}}}{2K^{\alpha}}\leq\frac{1}{8},

\displaystyle{\epsilon}=\sqrt{\frac{\eta K}{8T}}\leq\sqrt{\frac{K}{64T}}\leq\frac{1}{8}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4K^{2\alpha}}\left(-\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}}+\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}+4K^{\alpha}U}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4K^{2\alpha}}\frac{16K^{2\alpha}U^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}}+\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}+4K^{\alpha}U}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4U^{2}}{\left(\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}}+\sqrt{\frac{K}{8T}+4K^{\alpha}U}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{U^{2}}{4K^{\alpha}U}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{U}{K^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{K^{1-2\alpha}}{8T},

where the second equality is
\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}=\frac{a-b}{\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}}
, the first inequality is
\frac{K}{T}\leq\frac{K^{\alpha}U}{4}
and the last inequality is
\frac{U}{K^{\alpha}}\geq\frac{K^{1-2\alpha}}{2T}
, both hold for all
T\geq 4K
and
U\geq 1
. This implies that

\displaystyle\eta^{2}K^{2\alpha}=\eta(\eta K^{2\alpha})\geq\eta\frac{K^{1-2\alpha}}{8T}K^{2\alpha}=\frac{\eta K}{8T}={\epsilon}^{2}.

As a result,
\eta K^{\alpha}\geq{\epsilon}=U-\eta K^{\alpha}
. Hence,
\eta K^{\alpha}\geq\frac{U}{2}
.

With the choice of
\eta
and
{\epsilon}
in (
64
), we verify that (
12
) holds:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\ell_{t,i}\right\rvert}^{2/\alpha}\right)^{\alpha}\right]=\eta K^{\alpha}+{\epsilon}=U.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
denote the algorithm of a learner.
Let

denote the number of times arm
i
is pulled by
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Let
\mathbb{P}_{0}
and
\mathbb{P}_{i}
denote the distribution of the observed losses on
V_{0}
and
V_{i}
, respectively.
Similarly, let
\mathbb{E}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{i}
denote the expectation taken on
V_{0}
and
V_{i}
, respectively.

We first run
{\mathcal{A}}
on
V_{0}
.
Let

be the arm that is pulled the least in expectation. Since

, we have

.

By the standard arguments in establishing lower bounds for adversarial bandits
(e.g.
Auer et al. 2002a
, Equation 28-30)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}[N_{a}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{a}]+\frac{T}{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\mathbb{P}_{a}-\mathbb{P}_{0}\right\rVert}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{a}]+\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{2\ln(2)KL(\mathbb{P}_{0}\;\|\;\mathbb{P}_{a})}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{a}]+\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{2\ln(2)\mathbb{E}_{0}[N_{a}]KL(\eta\;\|\;\eta+{\epsilon})}

Recall that

denote the Bregman divergence associated with a one-dimensional strictly convex function

Lemma 31
.

For any
a\in\mathbb{R},x,y\in(0,1)
and strictly convex function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle a(x-y)-d_{f}(y,x)\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{a^{2}}{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}

for some

between

and

Proof.

The inequality trivally holds when
x=y
, hence we assume
x\neq y
. By Taylor’s theorem, we have

for some
z
between
x
and
y
. Note that the strict convexity of

implies

. We have

\displaystyle a(x-y)-d_{f}(x,y)

\displaystyle=a(x-y)-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{2}(x-y)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\left((x-y)\sqrt{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}-\frac{a}{\sqrt{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}}\right)^{2}+\frac{a^{2}}{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}\right)\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2f^{\prime\prime}(z)}.

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(p)

\displaystyle=\beta_{t}\psi_{TE}(p)-\gamma\psi_{LB}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{t}}{\alpha}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{i}^{\alpha}\right)-\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(p_{i}).

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\argmin_{x\in\Delta_{K}}\langle-R_{t-1},x\rangle+\Phi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\argmin_{x\in\Delta_{K}}\langle-R_{t-1},x\rangle+\beta_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}(1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}x_{i}^{\alpha})\right)-\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{K}\ln(x_{i}).

Given the set of active arms

The learning rates are set by SPM rules

\displaystyle z_{t}

\displaystyle=\min\left(\frac{(4d)^{2-\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}\min(p_{t,i},1-p_{t,i})^{1-\alpha},\beta_{t}\frac{18d^{2}}{\gamma}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}\tilde{p}_{t,i}\right),

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle=(-\psi_{TE}(q_{t}))=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}q_{t,i}^{\alpha}-1\right).

Regret Analysis

In this section, we prove Theorem
6
.

Proof.

Let
I_{a,t}=\mathbb{1}\{a\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\}
. By the definition of
\hat{\ell}_{t}
and the fact that
p_{t,i}=0
for
i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}
, for any
a\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I_{t}}[\hat{\ell}_{t,a}]=\sum_{i=1}^{K}p_{t,i}\frac{\ell_{t,a}\mathbb{1}\{a=i\}}{p_{t,a}}=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}p_{t,i}\frac{\ell_{t,a}\mathbb{1}\{a=i\}}{p_{t,a}}=\ell_{t,a}.

(86)

Therefore, the per-action regret with respect to
a\in[K]
is

\displaystyle R_{T,a}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}I_{a,t}(\ell_{t,I_{t}}-\ell_{t,a})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}I_{a,t}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}\rangle-\langle\ell_{t},e_{a}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}I_{a,t}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}-e_{a}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}I_{a,t}(\langle\hat{\ell}_{t}-\ell_{t,I_{t}}\bm{1},q_{t}-e_{a}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-e_{a}\rangle\right],

where

•

the second equality uses

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\hat{\ell}_{t,i}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}\hat{\ell}_{t,i}q_{t,i}+\sum_{i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}\hat{\ell}_{t,i}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle=\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}q_{t,I_{t}}+\ell_{t,I_{t}}\sum_{i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}}{p_{t,I_{t}}}q_{t,I_{t}}+\ell_{t,I_{t}}\sum_{i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle=\ell_{t,I_{t}}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}q_{t,j}+\ell_{t,I_{t}}\sum_{i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}q_{t,i}

\displaystyle=\ell_{t,I_{t}}.

•

the fourth equality uses
\langle\ell_{t,I_{t}}\bm{1},q_{t}-e_{a}\rangle=\ell_{t,I_{t}}(\sum_{i=1}^{K}q_{t,i}-e_{a,i})=0
.

•

the last equality uses

\displaystyle r_{t,i}=\begin{cases}\ell_{t,I_{t}}-\hat{\ell}_{t,i}&\qquad i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\\
0&\qquad i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}.\end{cases}

Let
u_{a}=(1-\frac{K}{T})e_{a}+\frac{1}{T}\bm{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-e_{a}\rangle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-u_{a}\rangle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},u_{a}-e_{a}\rangle.

The expectation of the second term is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},u_{a}-e_{a}\rangle]

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle\mathbb{E}[-r_{t}],u_{a}-e_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}[-r_{t,j}](u_{a,i}-e_{a,i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\mathbb{E}[\hat{\ell}_{t,j}-\ell_{t,I_{t}}](u_{a,i}-e_{a,i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{K}(\ell_{t,j}-\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p_{t}}[\ell_{t,i}])(u_{a,i}-e_{a,i})

\displaystyle\leq 2K.

Therefore, we only need to focus on the first term
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-u_{a}\rangle
.
Next, by the definition of
F_{t}(x)=\langle\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}-r_{s},x\rangle+\phi_{t}(x)
and
q_{t}=\argmin_{x\in\Delta_{K}}F_{t}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-u_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle\right)-(F_{T+1}(u_{a})-\phi_{T+1}(u_{a}))

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle\right)-F_{1}(q_{1})+\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}F_{t}(q_{t})-F_{t+1}(q_{t+1})\right)+\phi_{T+1}(u_{a})+F_{T+1}(q_{T+1})-F_{T+1}(u_{a})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle\right)-F_{1}(q_{1})+\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}F_{t}(q_{t})-F_{t+1}(q_{t+1})\right)+\phi_{T+1}(u_{a})

\displaystyle=\phi_{T+1}(u_{a})-F_{1}(q_{1})+\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}F_{t}(q_{t})-F_{t+1}(q_{t+1})+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\leq\gamma K\ln(T)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha}(K^{1-\alpha}-1)+\underbrace{\left(\sum_{t=1}^{T}F_{t}(q_{t})-F_{t+1}(q_{t+1})+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle\right)}_{\heartsuit}.

We bound each term in
\heartsuit
as follows:

\displaystyle F_{t}(q_{t})-F_{t+1}(q_{t+1})+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-R_{t-1},q_{t}\rangle+\langle R_{t},q_{t+1}\rangle+\phi_{t}(q_{t})-\phi_{t+1}(q_{t+1})+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-R_{t-1},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle+\phi_{t}(q_{t})-\phi_{t}(q_{t+1})+\phi_{t}(q_{t+1})-\phi_{t+1}(q_{t+1})+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle

\displaystyle=(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_{t})h_{t+1}+(\langle-R_{t-1},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle+\phi_{t}(q_{t})-\phi_{t}(q_{t+1}))

\displaystyle\quad+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_{t})h_{t+1}+\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t}),

where the inequality is from
\langle-R_{t-1}+\nabla\phi_{t}(q_{t}),q_{t+1}-q_{t}\rangle\geq 0
by the optimality of
q_{t}
and hence,

\displaystyle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})

\displaystyle=\phi_{t}(q_{t})-\phi_{t}(q_{t+1})+\langle\nabla\phi_{t}(q_{t}),q_{t+1}-q_{t}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\phi_{t}(q_{t})-\phi_{t}(q_{t+1})+\langle-R_{t-1},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle.

We have
q_{t+1,i}\leq 4dq_{t,i}
for all

from the combination of the results of Lemma
35
, Lemma
16
and Lemma
36
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-u_{a}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\gamma K\ln(T)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha}(K^{1-\alpha}-1)+\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_{t})h_{t+1}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\gamma K\ln(T)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha}(K^{1-\alpha}-1)+4d\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_{t})h_{t}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})

\displaystyle=\gamma K\ln(T)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha}(K^{1-\alpha}-1)+4d\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t}),

where the second inequality is from Lemma

Using Lemma
38
and noting that
\beta_{t}
is fixed before round

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{I_{t}}\left[\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{I_{t}}\left[\min\left(\frac{(4d)^{2-\alpha}}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-\alpha}\hat{\ell}^{2}_{t,I_{t}},\frac{18d^{2}}{\gamma}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\right)\right]

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-u_{a}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma K\ln(T)+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\alpha}(K^{1-\alpha}-1)+6\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right].

\displaystyle z_{\max}

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in[T]}z_{t},

\displaystyle h_{\max}

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in[T]}h_{t}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in[T]}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(4d)^{2-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\max_{t\in[T]}\sum_{i=1}^{K}\tilde{p}_{t,i}^{1-\alpha}

using the same analysis for SPM learning rates in
Ito et al. 2024
and obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb\displaystyle E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z^{\prime}_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right]

(87)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\min\left\{\inf_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\sqrt{8J\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t}z_{t}}+2\sqrt{2^{-J}Th_{\max}{z_{\max}}}\right\}\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{Th_{\max}z_{\max}}\right]\right\}+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{z_{\max}}{\beta_{1}}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\min\left\{\inf_{J\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\{\sqrt{8J\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t}z_{t}}+\sqrt{2^{-J}Th_{\max}{z_{\max}}}\right\}\right],\mathbb{E}\left[\sqrt{Th_{\max}z_{\max}}\right]\right\}\right),

\frac{z_{\max}}{\beta_{1}}\leq O\left(\frac{A^{\alpha}}{(1-\alpha)\frac{4K}{1-\alpha}}\right)=O(1)
. Here, we used

\displaystyle h_{\max}

\displaystyle=\max_{t\in[T]}h_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha}(\sum_{i=1}^{K}q_{t,i}^{\alpha}-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K^{1-\alpha}-1}{\alpha}.

In the adversarial regime, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{z^{\prime}_{t}}{\beta_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq O(\left(\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{Th_{\max}z_{\max}}]\right))

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\sqrt{T\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)A^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right).

Hence, the regret bound is of order

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[R_{T,a}]\leq O\left(\sqrt{T\frac{(K^{1-\alpha}-1)A^{\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}}\right).

∎

For any

and constant
c\in[0,1]
, Algorithm
F
guarantees

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}(q_{t,i})^{2-c}r_{t,i}^{2}\leq 2(\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}})^{2-c}\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
r_{t,i}=0
for
i\notin{\mathbb{A}}_{t}
,
r_{t,i}=\ell_{t,I_{t}}
for
i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}\setminus\{I_{t}\}
and
\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}=\frac{\ell_{t,I_{t}}}{p_{t,I_{t}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{K}{q}_{t,i}^{2-c}r_{t,i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}}{q}_{t,i}^{2-c}r_{t,i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t},i\neq I_{t}}{q}_{t,i}^{2-c}+{q}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-c}\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{t,I_{t}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}{p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t},i\neq I_{t}}{q}_{t,i}^{2-c}+{q}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-c}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}-1\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}{p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t},i\neq I_{t}}p_{t,i}^{2-c}+p_{t,I_{t}}^{2-c}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}-1\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ell_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}{p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t},i\neq I_{t}}p_{t,i}\right)^{2-c}+p_{t,I_{t}}^{2-c}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}-1\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}(p_{t,I_{t}}(1-p_{t,I_{t}}))^{2-c}\left(p_{t,I_{t}}^{c}+(1-p_{t,I_{t}})^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}(p_{t,I_{t}}(1-p_{t,I_{t}}))^{2-c}

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-c}\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2},

where the first inequality is due to
q_{t,i}\leq p_{t,i}
for
i\in{\mathbb{A}}_{t}
, the second inequality is from repeatedly applying
a^{x}+b^{x}\leq(a+b)^{x}
for
x=2-c\geq 1
by Lemma
21
, the third inequality is
p_{t,I_{t}}^{c}\leq 1
and
(1-p_{t,I_{t}})^{c}\leq 1
, and the last inequality is
x(1-x)\leq\min(x,1-x)
for
x\in[0,1]
.
∎

Lemma 38
.

For any
t\in[T]
, Algorithm
F
guarantees

\displaystyle\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})\leq\min\left(\frac{(4d)^{2-\alpha}}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-\alpha}\hat{\ell}^{2}_{t,I_{t}},\frac{18d^{2}}{\gamma}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\right)

(93)

Proof.

Using standard local-norm analysis techniques for FTRL (for example, see Section 7.4 in
Orabona 2023
), we have

\displaystyle\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert r_{t}\right\rVert}^{2}_{(\nabla^{2}\phi_{t}(z_{t}))^{-1}},

(94)

where
z_{t}
is a point between
q_{t}
and
q_{t+1}
. The Hessian matrix of
\phi_{t}
is a diagonal matrix with entries

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\phi_{t}(z_{t})=\mathrm{diag}\left(\left(\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)z_{t,i}^{\alpha-2}+\frac{\gamma}{z_{t,i}^{2}}\right)_{i=1,2,\dots,K}\right).

(95)

Hence, its inverse is the following diagonal matrix

\displaystyle(\nabla^{2}\phi(z_{t}))^{-1}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)z_{t,i}^{\alpha-2}+\frac{\gamma}{z_{t,i}^{2}}}\right)_{i=1,2,\dots,K}\right).

(96)

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathinner{\!\left\lVert r_{t}\right\rVert}^{2}_{(\nabla^{2}\phi_{t}(z_{t}))^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{K}r_{t,i}^{2}\frac{1}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)z_{t,i}^{\alpha-2}+\frac{\gamma}{z_{t,i}^{2}}}

(97)

\displaystyle\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{t,i}^{2-\alpha}r_{t,i}^{2},\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{t,i}^{2}r_{t,i}^{2}\right)

where the last equality is due to
\hat{\ell}_{t,i}=0
for
i\neq I_{t}
. Combining (
94
) and (
97
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle-r_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{t,i}^{2-\alpha}r_{t,i}^{2},\frac{1}{\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{K}z_{t,i}^{2}r_{t,i}^{2}\right).

(98)

Since
z_{t}
is between
q_{t}
and
q_{t+1}
, we have
z_{t,I_{t}}\leq\max(q_{t,I_{t}},q_{t+1,I_{t}})
.
The loss estimate in Algorithm
F
uses
p_{t,I_{t}}
where
p_{t,I_{t}}\geq q_{t,I_{t}}
, therefore we can combine the results of Lemma
35
, Lemma
16
and Lemma
36
and obtain
q_{t+1,i}\leq 4dq_{t,i}
for all
i\in[K]
. It follows that
z_{t,i}\leq 4dq_{t,i}
, and as a result,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\ell}_{t},q_{t}-q_{t+1}\rangle-D_{t}(q_{t+1},q_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\min\left(\frac{(4d)^{2-\alpha}}{2\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)}\sum_{i=1}^{K}q_{t,i}^{2-\alpha}r_{t,i}^{2},\frac{9d^{2}}{2\gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{K}q_{t,i}^{2}r_{t,i}^{2}\right)

(99)

\displaystyle\leq\min\left(\frac{(4d)^{2-\alpha}}{\beta_{t}(1-\alpha)}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2-\alpha}\hat{\ell}^{2}_{t,I_{t}},\frac{18d^{2}}{\gamma}\tilde{p}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\hat{\ell}_{t,I_{t}}^{2}\right),

(100)

where the last inequality is from applying Lemma
37
twice: once with
c=\alpha
and once with

.
∎

Lemma 39
.

For any
x\in[0,1]
, if
\gamma\geq 2
then
g(x)-1\geq g(2x)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle g(x)-1

\displaystyle=\beta x^{\alpha-1}+\frac{\gamma}{x}-1

\displaystyle\geq\beta 2^{\alpha-1}x^{\alpha-1}+\frac{\gamma}{2x}+\frac{\gamma}{2x}-1

\displaystyle=g(2x)+\frac{\gamma-2x}{2x}

\displaystyle\geq g(2x).

∎

Appendix G
Setting
\alpha
appropriately close to
1

Recall that we assume
K\geq 3
in our algorithms.
Let
b=1-\alpha
. We will set
\alpha=1-\frac{0.5}{\ln(K)}
, which is equivalent to setting
b=\frac{0.5}{\ln(K)}
. Note that
\alpha\geq 1-\frac{0.5}{\ln(3)}>0.5.

Taking exponent on both sides of

\displaystyle\ln(1+2b\ln(K))=\ln(2)\geq 0.5=b\ln(K),

(101)

we obtain
1+2b\ln(K)\geq K^{b}
. This implies

\displaystyle\frac{K^{1-\alpha}-1}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\leq\frac{2(K^{b}-1)}{b}\leq 4\ln(K).

(102)

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{(K-1)^{1-\alpha}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\leq\frac{2(K-1)^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}=\ln(K)(K-1)^{\frac{0.5}{\ln{K}}}\leq\ln(K)K^{\frac{0.5}{\ln{K}}}\leq 2\ln{K},

(103)

where the last inequality is due to
K^{\frac{0.5}{\ln(K)}}=(e^{\ln{K}})^{\frac{0.5}{\ln(K)}}=e^{0.5}<2
.
In addition,

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{1-\alpha}=\frac{1}{b}=2\ln(K).

(104)

This implies that
\gamma=\max\left(6,48\sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right)\lesssim\sqrt{\ln(K)}
.

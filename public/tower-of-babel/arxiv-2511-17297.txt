Computing the Hard Scaled Relative Graph of LTI Systems
Julius P. J. Krebbekx, Eder Baron-Prada, Roland Tóth, Amritam Das

Abstract
Scaled Relative Graphs (SRGs) provide a novel graphical frequency-domain method for the analysis of nonlinear systems, where Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems are the fundamental building block. To analyze feedback loops with unstable LTI components, the hard SRG is required, since it aptly captures the input/output behavior on the extended L 2 L_{2} space. In this paper, we develop a systematic computational method to exactly compute the hard SRG of LTI systems, which may be unstable and contain integrators. We also study its connection to the Nyquist criterion, including the multivariable case, and demonstrate our method on several examples.



I
Introduction

For
Linear Time-Invariant
(LTI) systems, graphical system analysis using the Nyquist diagram
[
16
]
is the cornerstone of control engineering. It is easy to use, allows for intuitive analysis and controller design methods and has been widely used in the industry. However, it is unclear how to systematically generalize graphical frequency domain methods to nonlinear system analysis and controller design.

The
Scaled Relative Graph
(SRG)
[
18
]
was recently proposed as a novel graphical framework for analyzing nonlinear
Single-Input Single-Output
(SISO) systems
[
3
]
. The SRG offers a non-approximative method yielding sufficient conditions for stability and upper bounds on the (incremental)
L_{2}
-gain, a key performance metric in practice. It is modular, allowing interconnections to be analyzed by composing the SRGs of subsystems. The SRG recovers classical results such as the small-gain theorem
[
3
]
and generalizes the circle criterion
[
12
]
, due to its close relation to the Nyquist diagram. It also enables frequency-dependent gain bounds, forming the basis of a nonlinear Bode diagram and bandwidth definition
[
10
]
, accommodates for the LTI notions of phase lead/lag
[
20
]
, and can be used to analyze nonlinear
Multi-Input Multi-Output
(MIMO) systems
[
9
]
. Apart from theoretical developments, SRGs have proven effective in applications such as reset control analysis
[
5
]
and design
[
11
]
, and the analysis of decentralized systems
[
1
]
.

The central building block in SRG analysis of nonlinear systems is the SRG of LTI systems. For stable SISO systems, the SRG was computed in
[
3
]
, and it was shown in
[
13
]
that this description was problematic for unstable LTI systems in the loop. The resolution provided in
[
13
]
was to add the Nyquist criterion to the SRG, obtaining the so-called
extended
SRG, solving the problem of how to deal with unstable systems in the loop for the SISO case.

The
hard
SRG was defined in
[
4
]
, where the original SRG from
[
3
]
was called the
soft
SRG. As argued in
[
4
]
, the hard SRG can be computed for unstable systems, hence provides an SRG framework that can deal with unstable LTI systems in the loop. An initial attempt to bound the hard SRG of stable square LTI systems is made in
[
5
]
. However, a systematic method to compute the hard SRG of LTI systems, both stable and
unstable
(including integrators), is lacking. Since the benefit of hard SRGs is mostly to deal with unstable systems in a feedback loop, a method to compute the hard SRG of unstable LTI systems would present a significant advancement for SRG analysis of nonlinear systems.

In this paper, we develop a non-approximative method to compute the hard SRG of LTI systems, which may be unstable. Our method is based on the transfer function representation of LTI systems, and constructs a bound from intersecting and removing disks in the complex plane centered on the real axis, and we prove that this bound is exact. This type of bound has been used in
[
5
]
for bounding the soft and hard SRG of stable square LTI and reset systems, and in
[
9
]
to bound the soft SRG of stable LTI operators which need not be square. We also prove that the extended SRG
[
13
]
, which combines the SISO Nyquist criterion and the soft SRG, is
identical
to the hard SRG. Having the hard SRG of LTI systems available, we show how they can serve as an alternative to the MIMO Nyquist criterion
[
14
]
and the Generalized Nyquist Criterion
[
6
]
(GNC).

This paper is structured as follows. We present the mathematical preliminaries, such as the various SRG definitions, in Section
II
. In Section
III
we present our main result, the algorithm for computing the hard SRG of LTI systems. Section
IV
discusses the connection to the Nyquist criterion, both in the SISO and MIMO case. We demonstrate our method on several examples in Section
V
, and present our conclusions in Section
VI
.

II-C
1
Transfer functions, poles and zeros

A transfer function
G(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}(s)
is proper if
\lim_{s\to\infty}G(s)=D
is bounded, otherwise
G(s)
is called improper. If
D
is non-singular, then
G(s)
is called bi-proper. We call a pole
p_{i}
stable if it lies in the left-half plane (LHP), i.e.,
\mathrm{Re}(p_{i})<0
, and
G(s)
is stable if it is proper and has only stable poles. Similarly, a pole
p_{i}
is unstable if it lies in the closed right half-plane (RHP), i.e.,
\mathrm{Re}(p_{i})\geq 0
, and if
G(s)
has at least one unstable pole, it is called unstable.

When all transmission zeros of
G(s)
, i.e., those

such that
G(z_{i})
loses rank, lie in the open LHP (
\mathrm{Re}(z_{i})<0
), then
G(s)
is called
minimum phase
.

II-C
2
Inverting LTI systems

A transfer function
G(s)\in\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}(s)
is said to have full column normal rank if
G(s)
has rank
p
in
\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}(s)
. We note that the normal rank, i.e., the rank of

in
\mathbb{R}^{q\times p}(s)
, is equal to the number of nonzero entries in the Smith-McMillan form
[
8
]
.

An LTI system
G:L_{2\mathrm{e}}^{p}\to L_{2\mathrm{e}}^{q}
can be inverted if and only if
p=q
,
G(s)
is bi-proper and has full normal rank
[
8
]
. In that case, the poles of

become the zeros of

The

We note, however, that Theorem

VI
Conclusion

LTI systems are the fundamental building block of nonlinear system analysis using SRGs. In this paper, we provide an algorithm to exactly compute the hard SRG of SISO and square MIMO LTI systems, which may be unstable. The computations are based on the transfer function representation, and has a direct relation to the soft SRG of the LTI system. Then, we proved that in the SISO case, the hard SRG is equal to the extended SRG, where the latter is a union of the soft SRG and the Nyquist stability criterion.

While the main strength of the SRG framework lies in nonlinear system analysis, we showed how hard SRG analysis also provides an alternative to the MIMO Nyquist criterion and the GNC.

Even though we focus on LTI systems in this paper, the results immediately carry over to hard SRG analysis of nonlinear feedback systems as developed in
[
4
]
. Additionally, we expect that the results in this paper will advance the SRG analysis of nonlinear MIMO systems, as developed in
[
9
]
, to include unstable LTI plants in the loop.

1.
Introduction

Given a smooth map
\psi:(M,g)\rightarrow(N,h)
between Riemannian manifolds of dimensions
m
and
n
, respectively. The energy functional (also called the Dirichlet energy) of
\psi
, over a compact domain
\Omega
of
M
, is defined by

E(\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|d\psi|^{2}dv_{g}.

(1.1)

The map
\varphi
is called harmonic if it is a critical point of the energy functional (
1.1
). The corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation is given by
[
2
,
12
]
:

\tau(\psi)=\operatorname{Tr}_{g}(\nabla d\psi)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\{\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}d\psi(e_{i})-d\psi(\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i})\}=0,

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
a local orthonormal frame field of
(M,g)
,
\tau(\psi)
being the tension field of
\psi
and
\nabla^{\psi}
denotes the connection on the Riemannian vector bundle
\psi^{-1}TN\rightarrow M
induced from the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{N}
of
(N,h)
and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g)
. Thus the tension field
\tau(\psi)
measures the failure of
\psi
to be harmonic, it vanishes precisely when
\psi
is harmonic see for instance
[
10
,
11
]
.

Every section
\xi
of a Riemannian vector bundle
\pi:F^{m+k}\longrightarrow M^{m}
can be regarded as a map
\xi:(M,g)\rightarrow(F,G_{S})
where
G_{S}
is the Sasaki metric. Following Wood
[
22
]
,
\xi
is said to be harmonic section if it is a critical point of the
energy functional
E
, with respect to smooth variations through sections of
F
. If
M
is compact then integration by parts shows that all harmonic sections of
E
are necessarily parallel
[
3
]
. Konderak
[
15
]
derived an explicit formula for the tension field of the section
\xi
, and established necessary and sufficient conditions under which
\xi
is a harmonic map. Equivalently,
\xi
is a harmonic map if and only if it is a harmonic section and an additional curvature-related term vanishes. Furthermore, he showed that if the base manifold is compact, then a section being harmonic map implies it must be parallel.

In
[
6
]
, B.Y. Chen defined biharmonic submanifolds of Euclidean space as those with harmonic mean curvature vector fields, and proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1
. Any biharmonic submanifold of the Euclidean space is harmonic,
i.e., minimal.

This well-known Chen’s conjecture is closely tied to the harmonicity of the mean curvature vector. Though there have been several partial results (see
[
7
,
21
]
), the conjecture remains open. Since Chen’s conjecture imposes no assumption on the completeness of the submanifold, it is considered a problem in local differential geometry. Akutagawa and Maeta later reformulated the conjecture into a problem in global differential geometry:

Conjecture 2
. Any complete biharmonic submanifold of the Euclidean space is minimal.

This Conjecture has also attracted much attention, and many partial results exist (see
[
1
]
,
[
17
]
), but the conjecture is still unresolved.

Since
\tau(\psi)
is a section of the Riemannian vector bundle
\psi^{-1}TN
, then it can be considered as a map
\tau(\psi):(M,g)\rightarrow(\psi^{-1}TN,G_{S})
where
G_{S}
denotes the Sasaki metric. Motivated by the two conjectures above, this note investigates the harmonicity of the tension field of a map between Riemannian manifolds. More concretely, we focus on the behavior of both maps whose tension fields are harmonic maps (called
HM
-tensional maps) and those whose tension fields are harmonic sections (called
HS
-tensional maps). Clearly, harmonic maps are always
HM
-tensional maps and
H
-tensional maps by definition. Thus, harmonic maps form a subclass of both
HM
-tensional maps and
HS
-tensional maps. We also establish some nonexistence results for nonharmonic
HS
-tensional maps and nonharmonic
HM
-tensional maps. Additionally, we initiate a study of the geometry of
HS
-tensional maps and
HM
-tensional maps in the context of submanifolds. In particular, when the
HS
-tensional equation is applied to isometric immersions into Euclidean space, we recover Chen’s definition of biharmonic submanifolds, showing that the two notions coincide in this case. Finally, we present two new conjectures that extend Conjectures 1 and 2.

Preliminaries

In this section we recall some facts and results needed later, we begin by a few basics on the geometry of Riemannian vector bundle, following
[
4
]
. Let
(M^{m},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla

and curvature tensor
R
as before. Consider a Riemannian vector bundle
\pi:F^{m+k}\longrightarrow M^{m}
with fiber metric
g^{\bot}
and a metric connection
D
. If
(x^{1},\cdots,x^{m})
are local
coordinates on
M
, set
q^{i}=x^{i}\circ\pi
; if
\{\nu_{\alpha}\}
is a local orthonormal basis of
sections of
F
, writing a point
(s,U)\in F
as
U=u^{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}
,
(q^{1},\cdots,q^{m})
together
with the fiber coordinates
(u^{1},\cdots,u^{k})
form local coordinates on
F
.
For a section
\xi=\xi^{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}
of
F
and a vector field
X=X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}=X^{i}\partial_{i}
, the connection
D
is given by

D_{X}\xi=X^{i}\Big(\frac{\partial\xi^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{i}}+\xi^{\beta}\mu_{\beta i}^{\alpha}\Big)\nu_{\alpha},

where
D_{\partial_{i}}e_{\beta}=\mu_{\beta i}^{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}.

The vertical lift
\xi^{v}
of
\xi

and the horizontal lift
X^{h}
of
X
are defined respectively by:

\xi^{v}=\xi^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}},

and

X^{h}=X^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{i}}-X^{i}u^{\beta}\mu_{\beta i}^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{\alpha}}.

The Sasaki metric on
F
is the Riemannian metric
G_{S}
defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}G_{S}(X^{h},Y^{h})=g(X,Y)\circ\pi,&\hbox{}\\
G_{S}(\xi^{v},X^{h})=0,&\hbox{}\\
G_{S}(\xi^{v},\zeta^{v})=g^{\bot}(\xi,\zeta)\circ\pi,&\hbox{}\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

for any
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
and
\xi,\zeta\in\Gamma(F)
.

A section
\xi\in\Gamma(F)
can be viewed as the map

\xi:(M,g)\rightarrow(F,G_{S})
. The energy of
\xi
is, by definition, the energy of the corresponding map
\xi:(M,g)\rightarrow(F,G_{S})
, that is
[
15
]

E(\xi)=\frac{m}{2}\operatorname{Vol}(M)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|D\xi|^{2}dv_{g}

(2.2)

(assuming
M
compact; in the non-compact case, one works over compact domain). Moreover,
the tension field
\tau(\xi)
is given by
[
15
]

\tau(\xi)=\Big\{\Big(\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}g^{\bot}(R^{D}(\cdot,e_{i})\xi,D_{e_{i}}\xi)^{\sharp}\Big)^{h}+\Big(-\bar{\Delta}\xi)\Big)^{v}\Big\}\circ\xi,

(2.3)

where
\bar{\Delta}
is the rough Laplacian on
F
given by

\bar{\Delta}\xi=-\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}(D_{e_{i}}D_{e_{i}}\xi-D_{\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\xi),

for any local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
of
M
, and
R^{D}
is the curvature of
D
defined by

R^{D}(X,Y)=[D_{X},D_{Y}]-D_{[X,Y]}.

\sharp:T^{\ast}M\longrightarrow TM
being the musical isomorphism. A section
\xi\in\Gamma(F)
defines a harmonic map from
(M,g)
to
(F,G_{S})
if and only if
\tau(\xi)=0
, equivalently

\bar{\Delta}\xi=0,

(2.4)

and

tr_{g}g^{\bot}(R^{D}(X,\cdot)\xi,D_{.}\xi)=0,

(2.5)

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
(see
[
15
]
). Moreover,
\xi\in\Gamma(F)
is harmonic section if it is a critical point for the energy
functional
E:\Gamma(F)\longrightarrow[0,\infty)
, that is
\bar{\Delta}\xi=0
(see
[
3
,
22
]
), so
\xi\in\Gamma(F)
defines a harmonic map if and only if
\xi
is harmonic section and (
2.5
) is verified.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\psi:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds and
\xi\in\Gamma(\psi^{-1}TN)

considered as a map
\xi:(M,g)\rightarrow(\psi^{-1}TN,G_{S})
. Then under the data
F=\psi^{-1}TN
,
g^{\bot}=h
and
D=\nabla^{\psi}

and (
2.3
) we obtain

\tau(\xi)=\Big\{\Big(\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}h(R^{\nabla^{\psi}}(e_{i},\cdot)\xi,\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\xi)^{\sharp}\Big)^{h}+\Big(-\Delta^{\psi}\xi)\Big)^{v}\Big\}\circ\xi,

(2.6)

where

\Delta^{\psi}\xi=-\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}(\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\xi-\nabla^{\psi}_{\nabla^{M}_{e_{i}}e_{i}}\xi).

But from definition of
\sharp
we get

\displaystyle\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}h(R^{\nabla^{\psi}}(e_{i},\cdot)\xi,\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\xi)^{\sharp}

\displaystyle=\overset{m}{\underset{i,j=1}{\sum}}h(R^{\nabla^{\psi}}(e_{i},e_{j})\xi,\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\xi)e_{j}

\displaystyle=\overset{m}{\underset{i,j=1}{\sum}}h(R^{N}(d\psi(e_{i}),d\psi(e_{j}))\xi,\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\xi)e_{j}

\displaystyle=-\overset{m}{\underset{i,j=1}{\sum}}h(R^{N}(\nabla^{\psi}_{e_{i}}\xi,\xi)d\psi(e_{i}),d\psi(e_{j}))e_{j}

\displaystyle=-\overset{m}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}h(S^{\psi}(\xi),d\psi(e_{j}))e_{j},

(2.7)

where

S^{\psi}(\xi)=\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}R^{N}(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\xi,\xi)d\psi(e_{i}).

Now, replace (
2.7
) in (

) we yield

\tau(\xi)=-\overset{m}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}h(S^{\psi}(\xi),d\psi(e_{j}))\Big(e_{j}\Big)^{h}\circ\xi+\Big(-\Delta^{\psi}\xi)\Big)^{v}\circ\xi.

(2.8)

Thus
\xi
is harmonic map if and only if it is harmonic section i.e.

\Delta^{\psi}\xi=0,

and

h(S^{\psi}(\xi),d\psi(X))=0\;\;\;\text{for any}\;X\in\Gamma(TM).

Note that the term
S^{\psi}(\xi)
is well-defined independently of the orthonormal frame used.
Furthermore, it holds
S^{\psi}(f\xi)=f^{2}S^{\psi}(\xi)
for any smooth function

on
M
and
\xi\in\Gamma(\psi^{-1}TN)
. The term

generalizes the term
S(X)
for a vector field

used in
[
18
]
.

Definition 3.1

Let

be a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds. The map

Therefore, we yield

Theorem 3.2

Let
\psi:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds. The map
\psi

-tensional map if and only if

\Delta^{\psi}\tau(\psi)=0.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\psi:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a smooth map between Riemannian manifolds. The map
\psi
is an
HM
-tensional map if and only if it is
HS
-tensional map and

h(S^{\psi}(\tau(\psi)),d\psi(X))=0\;\;\;\;\text{ for any}\;X\in\Gamma(TM).

Remark 3.4

Definition 4.1

A vector field

\nabla_{X}\nabla_{X}\xi-\nabla_{\nabla_{X}X}\xi=0,\;\;\;\forall X\in\Gamma(TM).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we

\frac{\partial^{2}f_{j}(x)}{\partial x_{k}^{2}}=0,\qquad\text{for all }j\in\{1,\dots,m\},\;k\in\{1,\dots,m\}.

We prove (

If

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\psi:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be an

-tensional map from a non-compact complete Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
into a Riemannian manifold
(N,h)
and let

be a real constant satisfying

for any vector field

on

. In particular,

is constant.

Proof.

For a fixed point
x_{0}\in M
, and for every

, we take a cut-off function

on

satisfying

\begin{cases}0\leq\beta(x)\leq 1,&x\in M,\\
\beta(x)=1,&x\in B_{r}(x_{0}),\\
\beta(x)=0,&x\not\in B_{2r}(x_{0}),\\
|\operatorname{grad}^{M}\beta|\leq\frac{C}{r},&x\in M,\end{cases}

where

is a constant independent of

, where

and
B_{2r}(x_{0})
are the balls centered at a fixed point

with

r
and
2r
respectively. From (

), we have

\int_{M}h(\Delta^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\beta^{2}|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}=0.

(4.9)

By (

), we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{M}h(\Delta^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\beta^{2}|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}h(\nabla^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\nabla^{\psi}(\beta^{2}|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi)))dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),(e_{i}\beta^{2})|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi)+\beta^{2}e_{i}\{(|\tau(\psi)|^{2})^{q-2})\}\tau(\psi)

\displaystyle+\beta^{2}|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),2\beta(e_{i}\beta)|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi)dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\beta^{2}(q-2)|\tau(\psi)|^{q-4}(h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\tau(\psi))^{2}dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\beta^{2}|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}.

Since

\beta^{2}(q-2)|\tau(\psi)|^{q-4}(h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\tau(\psi))^{2}\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0\geq\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),2\beta(e_{i}\beta)|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi)dv_{g}

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\beta^{2}|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}.

\pm 2\langle V,W\rangle\leq\varepsilon|V|^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}|W|^{2},

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi),\beta(e_{i}\beta)|\tau(\psi)|^{q-2}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}

\displaystyle=-2\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{m}h((e_{i}\beta)|\tau(\psi)|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\tau(\psi),\beta|\tau(\psi)|^{\frac{q}{2}-1}\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\psi}\tau(\psi))dv_{g}

Substituting this into (

5.
HS
-tensional submanifolds and
HM
-tensional submanifolds

This section is devoted to study
HS
-tensional maps and
HM
-tensional maps in the context of submanifolds.

Definition 5.1
.

A submanifold in a Riemannian manifold is called
HS
-tensional (resp.
HM
-tensional) submanifold
if the isometric immersion defining the submanifold is
HS
-tensional (resp.
HM
-tensional) map.

Clearly, minimal submanifolds are
HS
-tensional and
HM
-tensional. Let
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submanifold of a Riemannian manifold. We shall denote by
B
,
A
,
H
and
\Delta^{\bot}
the second fundamental form, the shape operator, the mean curvature vector field and the Laplacian on the normal bundle of
M^{m}
, respectively.

Theorem 5.2
.

A Riemannian submanifold
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
of a Riemannian manifold is
HS
-tensional if
and only if

\begin{cases}\Delta^{\bot}H+\operatorname{Tr}(B(\cdot,A_{H}(\cdot)))=0,\\
\frac{m}{4}\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2})+\operatorname{Tr}(A_{\nabla_{\cdot}^{\bot}H}\cdot)+\left[\operatorname{Tr}R^{N}(d\iota(\cdot),H)d\iota(\cdot)\right]^{\top}=0.\end{cases}

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
be a local geodesic orthonormal frame at point
x
in
M
. Then calculating at
x
, and using the Gauss and Weingarten formulas, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta^{\iota}\tau(\iota)&=m\Delta^{\iota}H=-m\underset{i=1}{\overset{m}{\sum}}(\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{i}}H)=-m\underset{i=1}{\overset{m}{\sum}}(\nabla^{\varphi}_{e_{i}}(-A_{H}e_{i}+\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\bot}H))\\
&=-m\underset{i=1}{\overset{m}{\sum}}(-\nabla_{e_{i}}A_{H}e_{i}-B(e_{i},A_{H}e_{i})-A_{\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\bot}H}e_{i}+\nabla^{\bot}_{e_{i}}\nabla^{\bot}_{e_{i}}H)\\
&=m[\Delta^{\bot}H+\operatorname{Tr}(B(\cdot,A_{H}(\cdot)))]+m\sum_{i=1}^{m}[A_{\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\bot}H}e_{i}))+\nabla_{e_{i}}A_{H}(e_{i})].\end{aligned}

(5.2)

Moreover, at
x
one has
[
9
]

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla_{e_{i}}A_{H}(e_{i})=\frac{m}{2}\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}[A_{\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\bot}H}e_{i}+\left[R^{N}(d\iota(e_{i}),H)d\iota(e_{i})\right]^{\top}]

(5.3)

Substituting (
5.3
) in (
5.2
) and comparing the normal and the tangential
components we obtain the theorem.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The first condition of (
5.1
) implies immediately that every
HS
-tensional submanifold is minimal if it has
parallel mean curvature vector, i.e.,
\nabla^{\bot}H=0
.

Theorem 5.4
.

A Riemannian submanifold
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
of a Riemannian manifold is
HM
-tensional if
and only if

\begin{cases}\Delta^{\bot}H+\operatorname{Tr}(B(\cdot,A_{H}(\cdot)))=0,\\
\frac{m}{4}\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2})+\operatorname{Tr}(A_{\nabla_{\cdot}^{\bot}H}\cdot)+\left[\operatorname{Tr}R^{N}(d\iota(\cdot),H)d\iota(\cdot)\right]^{\top}=0,\end{cases}

(5.4)

.

A Riemannian submanifold
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)

and only if it is
HS
-tensional

Remark 5.6
.

We observe that if
N
is of constant sectional curvature
c
denoted by
N(c)
then the
HS
-tensional submanifolds and the
HM
-tensional submanifolds coincide, in fact its curvature tensor writes as
R^{N}(U,V)W=c(h(V,W)U-h(U,W)V)
for all
U,V,W\in\Gamma(TN).
Since

\displaystyle S^{\iota}(\tau(\iota))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{2}\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}R^{N}(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\iota}H,H)d\iota(e_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c\;m^{2}\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}h(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\iota}H,d\iota(e_{i}))H.

Therefore, for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
we have

h(S^{\iota}(\tau(\iota)),d\iota(X))=0.

Theorem 5.7
.

A Riemannian submanifold
\iota:M^{m}\longrightarrow N^{n}(c)
in a space of constant sectional curvature
c
is

HS
-tensional if and only if

\begin{cases}\Delta^{\bot}H+\operatorname{Tr}(B(\cdot,A_{H}(\cdot)))=0,\\
\frac{m}{4}\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2})+\operatorname{Tr}(A_{\nabla_{\cdot}^{\bot}H}\cdot)=0.\end{cases}

(5.5)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}R^{N}(d\iota(\cdot),H)d\iota(\cdot)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}R^{N}(d\iota(e_{i}),H)d\iota(e_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-c\;\overset{m}{\underset{i=1}{\sum}}h(d\iota(e_{i}),d\iota(e_{i}))H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m\;cH

Therefore

Now, by Theorem

∎

The goal of this section is to search for

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submanifold such that

constant. Then
M

-tensional if and only if it is minimal.

Proof.

Assume that
M
is
HS
-tensional, by using the Weitzenböck formula we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|H|^{2}

\displaystyle=h(\Delta^{\iota}H,H)-|\nabla^{\iota}H|^{2},

\displaystyle=-|\nabla^{\iota}H|^{2}.

(6.1)

If
|H|=
constant. From (
6.1
) it results that

\nabla^{\iota}H=0.

Then, by Remark
5.3
we conclude that
M
is minimal.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
be a Riemannian submanifold such that
|H|=
constant. Then
M
is
HM
-tensional if and only if it is minimal.

Theorem 6.3
.

A compact Riemannian submanifold
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
is
HS
-tensional if and only if it is minimal.

Proof.

We know that the compact Riemannian
submanifold
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
is
HS
-tensional
if and only if its tension field is parallel i.e.
\nabla^{\iota}H=0
, but
for all

, we have

Then,
\nabla^{\bot}_{X}H=0
and by Remark
5.3
we get
H=0
.
∎

Theorem 6.4
.

A compact Riemannian submanifold
\iota:(M^{m},g)\longrightarrow(N^{n},h)
is
HM
-tensional if and only if it is minimal.

Theorem 6.5
.

There are no compact
HS
-tensional submanifolds of the Euclidean space.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a compact
HS
-tensional submanifold of the Euclidean
space, so by the Theorem
6.3
this submanifold must be minimal, but
we know that there are no compact minimal submanifolds of the Euclidean
space
[
5
]
. Hence we deduce the assertion.
∎

Theorem 6.6
.

A pseudo-umbilical submanifold
M
of dimension
m
with

is an
HS
-tensional submanifold if and only if it is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
be a local geodesic orthonormal frame at point

in
M
. Then calculating at
x
and using (
5.3
) we have

\sum_{i=1}^{m}[A_{\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\bot}H}e_{i}+\left[R^{N}(d\iota(e_{i}),H)d\iota(e_{i})\right]^{\top}]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nabla_{e_{i}}A_{H}(e_{i})-\frac{m}{2}\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2}).

Since

is pseudo-umbilical i.e.

, (

) becomes

\sum_{i=1}^{m}[A_{\nabla_{e_{i}}^{\bot}H}e_{i}+\left[R^{N}(d\iota(e_{i}),H)d\iota(e_{i})\right]^{\top}]=(1-\frac{m}{2})\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2}).

(6.3)

Now, replacing (

(4-m)\operatorname{grad}(|H|^{2})=0.

(6.4)

Thus, for

,

∎

Theorem 6.7
.

A totally umbilical hypersurface in an Einstein space is
HS
-tensional if and only if it is minimal.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{m},e_{m+1}\}
be an orthonormal frame adapted to the

M
so that

, where

. Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \alpha&=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}<Ae_{i},e_{i}>=\lambda,\;\;|A|^{2}=m\lambda^{2},\\
A(\operatorname{grad}\alpha)&=A\big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}e_{i}(\lambda)e_{i}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{grad}\lambda^{2}.\end{aligned}

The

) become

\begin{cases}\Delta\lambda+m\lambda^{3}=0,\\
(2+m)\operatorname{grad}\lambda^{2}=0.\end{cases}

So

\alpha=0
.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Any totally umbilical

-tensional hypersurface in a Ricci flat manifold is minimal.

Theorem 6.13

-tensional curve if and only if it is a geodesic.

Proof.

Let
\gamma:I\longrightarrow(M^{m},g)

(M^{m},g)
. Let
\{F_{i},i=\overline{1,m}\}
be the Frenet frame in

\{\nabla_{\partial/\partial s}^{(k)}d\gamma(\partial/\partial s)|k=\overline{1,m}\}.

16
]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \nabla^{\gamma}_{\partial/\partial s}F_{1}&=\chi_{1}F_{2},\\
\nabla^{\gamma}_{\partial/\partial s}F_{i}&=-\chi_{i-1}F_{i-1}+\chi_{i}F_{i+1},\;\;\;\;i=\overline{2,m-1}\\
\nabla^{\gamma}_{\partial/\partial s}F_{m}&=-\chi_{m-1}F_{m-1},\end{aligned}

&-(\chi_{1}\chi_{2}\chi_{3})F_{4},\\

(6.30)

-tensional curve if and only if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\chi_{1}\chi_{1}^{\prime}=0,&\hbox{}\\
\chi_{1}^{\prime\prime}-\chi_{1}^{3}-\chi_{1}\chi_{2}^{2}=0,&\hbox{}\\

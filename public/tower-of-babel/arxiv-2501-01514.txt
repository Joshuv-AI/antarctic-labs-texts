Eisenstein degeneration of Beilinson–Kato classes and circular units
Javier Polo and Óscar Rivero (with an appendix by Ju-Feng Wu)

Abstract
. The aim of this note is to explore the Euler system of Beilinson–Kato elements in families passing through the critical p p -stabilization of an Eisenstein series attached to two Dirichlet characters ( ψ , τ ) (\psi,\tau) , a setting which is not covered by the recent works of Benois and Büyükboduk. In this context, we establish an explicit connection with the system of circular units, utilizing suitable factorization formulas in a situation where several of the p p -adic L L -functions vanish. In that regard, our main results may be seen as an Euler system incarnation of the factorization formula of Bellaïche and Dasgupta. One of the most significant aspects is that, depending on the parity of ψ \psi and τ \tau , different phenomena arise; while some can be addressed with our methods, others pose new questions. Finally, we discuss analogous results in the framework of Beilinson–Flach classes.



1.
Introduction

The study of Eisenstein series has played a prominent role in the theory of modular forms. One setting that has traditionally been elusive within the framework of Coleman families has to do with the so-called
critical Eisenstein series
, which refers to the case where one considers the family corresponding to the critical slope
p
-stabilization of an Eisenstein series. The main result summarizing the construction of families of critical
p
-adic
L
-functions is presented in
[
Bel12
]
, where the author discusses the conditions under which such
p
-adic
L
-functions exist, using the eigencurve of modular symbols. The Eisenstein case was later explored in
[
BD15
]
, where the authors established a factorization formula valid for half of the weight space that satisfies a sign condition. In fact, this is one of the key inputs for this work. The
p
-adic
L
-function vanishes in the other half of the weight space, raising the natural question about the derivative along the weight direction in an attempt to capture its arithmetic information.

The theory of critical modular forms and their connection with Euler systems has been explored in the works of Benois and Büyükboduk
[
BB22
]
,
[
BB24
]
, but under the assumption that the critical point corresponds to a cuspidal modular form. In this setting, ramification is measured in terms of the vanishing of the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function. However, in the Eisenstein case, there is no expected ramification, although the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function still vanishes.

The Eisenstein case was considered by Loeffler and the second author in
[
LRS24
]
, where they discussed what they called the
Eisenstein degeneration of Euler systems
. This method, which involved using the factorization of
p
-adic
L
-functions to establish connections among different types of Euler systems, was successfully applied in various contexts: diagonal cycles vs Beilinson–Flach classes, Beilinson–Flach vs Beilinson–Kato, and Heegner cycles vs elliptic units. A key step in their argument was the use of the local properties of the
big
Euler systems, which enabled a comparison between
p
-adic
L
-functions.

However, these local properties cannot be used when trying to compare the Beilinson–Kato system with circular units, as in this case no local conditions are involved at
p
. Therefore, this work should be seen as a natural continuation and an extension of the methods developed in
[
LRS24
]
, where we modify our previous approach to cover the Beilinson–Kato case. This case turns out to be ostensibly different, showing in an easier instance some of the properties already envisaged in
[
BB24
]
about the behavior of this Euler system. Further, the main result can be interpreted as an Euler-system incarnation of the results of Bellaïche–Dasgupta.

1.1.
Set-up

Let
E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)\in M_{k}(N,\xi)
be an Eisenstein series of even weight
k=r+2
, level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
and nebentype
\xi=\psi\tau
, where
\psi
and
\tau
are primitive Dirichlet characters with conductors
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
, respectively, with
N=N_{1}N_{2}
and
\xi(-1)=(-1)^{r}
. Let
{\mathbf{f}}
stand for the unique Coleman family passing through the critical slope
p
-stabilization of
f:=E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)
,
f_{\beta}:=E_{r+2}^{\crit}(\psi,\tau)
. Unless explicitly stated otherwise, we will assume in the first part of the introduction that
r
is even and that both
\tau
and
\psi
are even.

Along this article, we introduce the following assumption.

Assumption 1.1
.

The critical slope
p
-stabilization
f_{\beta}
is non-crticial, i.e., the generalised eigenspace of overconvergent modular forms associated to
f_{\beta}
is 1-dimensional. Further, the Coleman–Mazur eigencurve is smooth and locally étale at the point corresponding to
E_{r+2}^{\crit}(\psi,\tau)
in the family
{\mathbf{f}}
.

This is known to hold in a high level of generality (see e.g.
[
LRS24
, §A6]
), so this must be seen as a very mild assumption. We later discuss several equivalent formulations for this condition. From now on, let
X
stand for a local uniformizer at that point. Similarly, we write
V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
(resp.
V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
) for the non-compactly supported (resp. compactly supported) cohomology of
{\mathbf{f}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
be the distribution algebra of the cyclotomic extension
\Gamma\cong\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
, i.e., the global sections of the rigid analytic space associated with
\mathrm{Spf}\,\mathbf{Z}_{p}[\![\Gamma]\!]
. Let
{\mathbf{j}}\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{\times}
the universal character, which we regard as a character of the Galois group by composition with the cyclotomic character.

To formulate the main result of this paper, we need to introduce the following two Euler systems.

(a)

The
circular unit

c(\tau)(r)\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))
, constructed as an inverse limit of units along the cyclotomic tower when
\tau
is even. For
r=0
, we just write
c(\tau)
. Further, if
r=0
and we specialize at the trivial character, the corresponding class may be written as an explicit product of cyclotomic units.

(b)

The
Kato Euler system
(sometimes called Beilinson–Kato Euler system) attached to a Coleman family, which is a class

\kappa({\mathbf{f}})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}})).

As we later discuss, this class depends on the choice of an auxiliary Dirichlet character
\chi
.

Observe that
{\mathbf{f}}
is generically cuspidal, but is Eisenstein at one point; in particular, this allows us to consider the projection of
\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
onto the quotient

\frac{\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}{V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}).

Considering the projection in cohomology, we obtain a class

\kappa(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}})).

Letting
\Gamma^{+}=\Gamma/\langle\pm 1\rangle
, and writing
{\mathbf{j}}_{+}
for its universal character, we can see the class in the smaller subspace
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+}))
, since the Iwasawa cohomology of the other half of the weight space vanishes. Our comparison of Beilinson–Kato classes and circular units will involve three different kinds of
p
-adic
L
-functions:

(i)

The (one-variable) Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}(\chi)
attached to an even and non-trivial Dirichlet character
\chi
.

(ii)

The (two-variable) Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{g}},\chi)
attached to a Coleman family
{\mathbf{g}}
and a Dirichlet character
\chi
.

(iii)

The (one-variable) adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function attached to a Coleman family
{\mathbf{g}}
, and denoted
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{g}})
.

We will see that the explicit reciprocity laws connect the class
c(\tau)
with the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}(\tau)
, and the class
\kappa(f_{\beta})
with a ratio of
p
-adic
L
-functions which involve both
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
and
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
, together with another auxiliary Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-function.

1.2.
Main results

The first main result of this article may be seen as a direct comparison between the Kato Euler system and that of circular units. In order to state it as simple as possible in the introduction, we will assume that the leading term in the argument of Proposition
5.13
corresponds to
n=0
. This means that we assume that
\kappa(f_{\beta})
is equal to the class
\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})
introduced later in the text. For a more precise version of the result, see
5
. The Kato Euler system depends on the choice of an odd Dirichlet character
\chi
, and under the current sign assumptions,
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
vanishes at
(f_{\beta},r/2+1)
. Write
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
for the derivative of the two-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
restricted to the line
s=k/2
, and evaluated at
f_{\beta}
.

For the moment, we need an extra set of assumptions, which assert that the derivatives of the relevant
p
-adic
L
-functions do not vanish. The second part of the assumption may be relaxed, as we discuss in the body of the text.

Assumption 1.2
.

The adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
has a simple zero at the critical Eisenstein point corresponding to
E_{r+2}^{\crit}(\psi,\tau)
. Moreover,
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
is non-zero.

It is known, by the results of Bellaïche,
[
Bel21
, §9]
, that
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
vanishes at
f_{\beta}
, but we do not know whether or not it presents a simple zero. The result we state also involves the
logarithmic distribution
: for a continuous character
\sigma:\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{p}
, the function
\frac{d^{k}\sigma}{dz^{k}}\cdot\frac{z^{k}}{\sigma(z)}
is a constant, and
\log^{[k]}\in\mathbf{C}_{p}
is defined to be this constant. In addition, we write
\mathcal{E}_{N}
for the two-variable function corresponding to the product of the bad Euler factors introduced in Definition
4.8
. Finally, let
\lambda
denote the function defined over the Iwasawa algebra such that its specialization at a character
\sigma\colon t\mapsto t^{s}\xi(s)
corresponding to an integer
s
and a Dirichlet character
\xi
of
p
-power conductor is
\xi^{-1}(N_{2})\langle N_{2}\rangle^{-s+1}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
r
is even and that the characters
\psi
and
\tau
are both even. Then, under Assumption
1.1
and Assumption
1.2
, the following equality holds in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))
:

\kappa(f_{\beta})=\left(\frac{C\cdot\lambda\cdot\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)}{L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})}\right)\cdot L_{p}(\psi,{\mathbf{j}}+1)\cdot c(\tau)(r),

where
C
is a constant which does not depend on
j
.

The constant
C
arises during the interpolation of Ohta’s canonical differentials, and it may be seen as the
less understood
piece of the picture; roughly speaking, it has to do with the interpolation of the Eichler–Shimura isomorphisms around critical Eisenstein points. Observe that the factors
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
and
L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})
only depend upon the weight, but we have opted to include them separately to emphasize this dependence.
This means that we have the equality

\kappa(f_{\beta})=\tilde{C}\cdot\lambda\cdot\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}(\psi,{\mathbf{j}}+1)\cdot c(\tau)(r),

where
\tilde{C}
is an arbitrary constant. In particular, it can be zero.

The main steps involved in the proof of the theorem can be summarized below.

(1)

Deformation of the Beilinson–Kato elements in families passing through a critical Eisenstein point. The main result in this direction is stated as Theorem
5.1
. Observe that the critical Eisenstein situation requires a careful study of the variation in Coleman families, in a setting where the arguments of
[
BB22
]
and
[
BB24
]
do not directly work.

(2)

Use of the reciprocity laws for circular units and Beilinson–Kato elements (in families), which allow us to rephrase the statement of Theorem
1.3
in terms of
p
-adic
L
-functions. This is further developed in Section
3
and Section
4
.

(3)

Interpolation of the canonical differentials
\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}
and
\omega_{{\mathbf{f}}}
in families, together with a development of the explicit reciprocity laws around the
critical Eisenstein
point. The former is done in Section
2.3
, and the latter in Theorem
5.9
.

(4)

The Bloch–Kato conjecture for the trivial representation (twisted by a Dirichlet character and a power of the cyclotomic character). This shows that the classes
\kappa(f_{\beta})
and
c(\tau)(r)
belong to the same one-dimensional space.

(5)

A factorization formula for the Hida–Rankin
p
-adic
L
-function when one of the forms is Eisenstein, following the work of Bertolini–Darmon
[
BD14
]
. However, one must be cautious at this point when dealing with the different choices of periods.

(6)

Bellaïche–Dasgupta’s factorization formula
[
BD15
]
, expressing the
p
-adic
L
-function of a critical Eisenstein series as the product of two Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-functions and a logarithmic term.

The odd case.
If
r
is even,
\tau
and
\psi
are both odd, and we further assume that
\tau(p)\neq 1
, one can directly claim that
\kappa(f_{\beta})=0
, since Iwasawa cohomology is zero along that half of the weight space, i.e.,
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+}))=0
. This means that the class
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
lifts to an element in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+}))
. In this case, we can project
V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
onto the cohomology of the quotient

\frac{V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}{V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\psi^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+})),

and this yields a class

\kappa(f_{\beta})_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\psi^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+})).

This class belongs to the one-dimensional space spanned by the circular unit Euler system
c(\psi)(-1)
. Unfortunately, our methods are not enough to get a good description of that class, and this will be developed in the sequel to this paper, extending the theory we have developed so far. This may be seen as an instance of
exceptional zero phenomenon
, where the vanishing, however, is not caused by a zero in an Euler factor.

A conjectural generalization of
[
BD15
]
.
Assume now that
(-1)^{r}=-\psi(1)
. This is the situation, for instance, if
r
is odd and the character
\tau
is also odd; in that case, the class
\kappa(f_{\beta})
is not expected to be zero, or at least not due to trivial sign reasons. If we analyze the
p
-adic
L
-functions of Theorem
1.3
, we see that
L_{p}(f_{\beta},\chi)
admits now a factorization formula (and, a priori, it is non-zero), but
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
is zero.

In that case, we formulate a conjecture that may be seen as a natural analogue of Bellaïche’s secondary
p
-adic
L
-functions, and also as an extension of the work of Bellaïche–Dasgupta. More precisely, we propose the following formula in Conjecture
6.2
. Here, let
L_{p}^{\prime}({\mathbf{f}})
stand for the weight derivative of
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
; write
L_{p}^{\prime}({\mathbf{f}})(r,s)
for the (one-variable) cyclotomic
p
-adic
L
-function corresponding to specializing
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
at
k=r
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

If
(-1)^{s}=-\psi(-1)
, the following factorization formula holds (up to multiplication by some explicit constant):

L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta})(s)=\lambda N_{2}^{-s}\log^{[r+1]}\cdot L_{p}(\psi,s)\cdot L_{p}(\tau,s-r-1),

where
\lambda
is the aforementioned function over the Iwasawa algebra and the derivative is taken along the weight direction.

Later in the text we discuss theoretical evidence for the conjecture, whose proof seems beyond the scope of the methods developed in this note and in related works.

The organization of this note is as follows: we begin with an introduction of the Galois representations and periods involved in our comparison, with emphasis on the interpolation of the canonical differentials in a neighborhood of a critical Eisenstein point. Next, we fix our conventions for the systems of circular units and Kato elements, discussing the vanishing of the
p
-adic
L
-function of the adjoint and one of the Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-functions. This allows us to develop the study of the degeneration of Beilinson–Kato elements and carry out the comparison between both Euler systems. Section
6
serves to discuss the limitation of our methods in the cases where the characters
\psi
and
\tau
are not both odd, and raises some interesting questions that we leave for forthcoming work; more specifically, there are two main unsolved issues: (a) the description of
\kappa(f_{\beta})_{2}
; (b) the extension of the factorization formula of
[
BD15
]
. We end up the note with an analogy with the simpler case of Beilinson–Flach elements, which is further developed in a sequel of this note, where we extend the methods of this article.

In Appendix
A
, we discuss in detail the construction of rank-
2
big Galois representations. The materials presented in this appendix should be well known to the experts, but we were unable to find a detailed reference. We also note that the appendix is written in a way that suggests a possible generalization to higher-rank groups.

1.3.
Relation with other works

The starting point of this article was extending the results of
[
LRS24
]
to a setting where the same arguments were not available, since the comparisons developed in that work were based on the
strong
local conditions satisfied by the families of cohomology classes.

A similar idea in the framework of congruences among modular forms was explored in
[
RR21
]
. In that situation, the modulo
p
congruences suggested the possibility of an extra vanishing in certain cases, which led us to analyze a second derivative with techniques that went beyond the explicit reciprocity laws. It would be interesting to study whether those results, mainly due to Sharifi and Fukaya–Kato, admit a counterpart in this scenario.

The idea of using the degeneration of Eisenstein series is not new in the theory of modular forms. For instance, in the setting of irregular weight one modular forms, when one takes the Eisenstein series of characters
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
, with
\chi_{1}(p)=\chi_{2}(p)
, there are three (ordinary) families passing through it: the families of Eisenstein series
{\mathbf{E}}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
{\mathbf{E}}(\chi_{2},\chi_{1})
, but also a third family which is generically cuspidal. This idea is explored in the work of
[
BDV22
]
to prove Perrin-Riou’s conjecture via a comparison between Beilinson–Kato systems and Heegner cycles.

Urban explored in
[
Urb20
]
, with very different techniques, the phenomenon of Eisenstein congruences to study the construction of Euler systems. It would be interesting to understand the relationship between both approaches.

Finally, we expect to extend the study of these phenomena to settings beyond
\GL_{2}
, such as the Siegel case, where one can study endoscopy phenomena emerging in the theory of Saito–Kurokawa and Yoshida lifts.

Galois representations and periods

For this section, we recall some of the terminology and standard results around Galois representations that were explored in earlier work by Loeffler and the second author
[
LRS24
]
. The main novelty is the discussion of the interpolation of the differentials attached to a Coleman family passing through a critical Eisenstein point, which extends the results developed in loc. cit.

2.1.
Eisenstein series and Galois representations

Let
p>2
be a fixed prime number. Let
\psi,\tau
be Dirichlet characters and
r\geqslant 0
an integer with
\psi\tau(-1)=(-1)^{r}
. Let
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
denote the conductors of
\psi
and
\tau
, respectively, and let
N=N_{1}N_{2}
. Throughout this article, we assume that
p\nmid N
. Choose an embedding
\mathbf{Q}(\psi,\tau)\hookrightarrow L
, where
\mathbf{Q}(\psi,\tau)\subset\mathbf{C}
is the finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}
generated by the values of
\psi
and
\tau
, and
L
is a finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
.

We write
f=E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)
for the classical modular form of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
and weight
r+2
given by

E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)=(*)+\sum_{n\geqslant 1}q^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=d_{1}d_{2}\\
(N_{1},d_{1})=(N_{2},d_{2})=1\end{subarray}}\psi(d_{1})\tau(d_{2})d_{2}^{r+1},

where
(*)
is the constant defined e.g in
[
DS05
, Ch. 4]
. In particular, it is equal to
\delta(\psi)L(\tau,1-k)/2
, where
\delta(\psi)=1
if
\psi
is trivial and 0 if not, and
L(\tau,\cdot)
is the Dirichlet
L
-series attached to the character
\tau
. Note that we may regard the
q
-expansion coefficients
a_{n}(f)
of
f=E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)
as elements of

.

The
p

x^{2}-(\psi(p)+\tau(p)p^{r+1})x+\psi\tau(p)p^{r+1}=(x-\psi(p))(x-\tau(p)p^{r+1}).

Then, associated to the Eisenstein series

\alpha:=\psi(p)
and

f_{\beta}(q)=f(q)-\alpha f(q^{p}),

Definition 2.1
.

We say
f_{\beta}

M_{r+2}^{{\dagger}}(\Gamma)_{(T=f_{\beta})}

In
[
BD15

the authors discuss how to reinterpret the criticality condition in terms of the non-vanishing of a
p
-adic

Definition 2.2
.

We say the Eisenstein series
f=E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)

r>0
or

, and for every prime

, either the conductor of
\psi/\tau

\ell
, or
(\psi/\tau)(\ell)\neq 1
.

We now fix notation for Galois characters.

•

Let
\ell
be a prime. We write
\Frob_{\ell}
for an arithmetic Frobenius element at

\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})

\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}

•

The
p
-adic cyclotomic character
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})\to\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
is denoted by
\varepsilon
, and
\varepsilon(\Frob_{\ell})=\ell

\ell\neq p
.

•

If
\chi
is a Dirichlet character, we interpret
\chi
as a character
\Gal(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})\to\mathbf{C}^{\times}

\ell

\Frob_{\ell}^{-1}

\chi(\ell)

\ell

The following result, due to Soulé
[
Sou81
]
, summarizes the different possibilities that we have for Galois representations whose traces are prescribed by the Eisenstein eigenvalues of the Hecke operators. We state it here with the conventions of
[

]

Theorem 2.3

(Soulé)
.

If
f
is a
p
-decent Eisenstein series, there are exactly three isomorphism classes of continuous Galois representations
\rho:G_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\GL_{2}(L)
which are unramified at primes
\ell\nmid Np
and satisfy
\operatorname{tr}\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{\ell}^{-1})=a_{\ell}(f)

(1)

The semisimple representation
\psi\oplus\tau\varepsilon^{-1-r}
.

(2)

Exactly one non-split representation having
\tau\varepsilon^{-1-r}
as a subrepresentation. This representation splits locally at
\ell
for every
\ell\neq p
, but does not split at
p
, and is not crystalline (or even de Rham).

(3)

Exactly one non-split representation having
\psi
as a subrepresentation. This representation splits locally at
\ell
for every
\ell\neq p
, and is crystalline at
p
.

Proof.

This follows from cases of the Bloch–Kato conjecture due to Soulé; see
[
BC06
, §5.1]
for the statement in this form.
∎

If
f
is not
p
-decent, there will be extra representations in case (3), but we can repair the statement by only considering representations which are assumed to be unramified (resp. crystalline) at each prime
\ell\neq p
(resp. at
\ell=p
) where the hypothesis of being decent fails.

2.2.
Families of representations

Proceeding as in
[
LRS24
, §A.4,A.5]
, we can naturally associate four Galois representations to
f_{\beta}
:
V(f_{\beta})
,
V(f_{\beta})^{*}
,
V^{c}(f_{\beta})
, and
V^{c}(f_{\beta})^{*}
. The superscript
c
always stands for compact support. All of them are two dimensional, with natural one dimensional invariant subspaces. In particular, all of them are non-split extensions, and
V(f_{\beta})^{*}
is isomorphic to the dual of
V(f_{\beta})
(with both being de Rham at
p
), while
V^{c}(f_{\beta})^{*}
is isomorphic to the dual of
V^{c}(f_{\beta})
.

For the representation
V^{c}(f_{\beta})
, there is a short exact sequence of
L
-vector spaces

0\longrightarrow L(\tau\varepsilon^{-1-r})\longrightarrow V^{c}(f_{\beta})\longrightarrow L(\psi)\longrightarrow 0,

and from here we get another short exact sequence of
L
-linear Galois representations for
V(f_{\beta})
:

0\longrightarrow L(\psi)\longrightarrow V(f_{\beta})\longrightarrow L(\tau\varepsilon^{-1-r})\longrightarrow 0.

Similarly, for
V(f_{\beta})^{*}
, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow L(\tau^{-1}\varepsilon^{1+r})\longrightarrow V(f_{\beta})^{*}\longrightarrow L(\psi^{-1})\longrightarrow 0,

while for the compactly supported cohomology we have

0\longrightarrow L(\psi^{-1})\longrightarrow V^{c}(f_{\beta})^{*}\longrightarrow L(\tau^{-1}\varepsilon^{1+r})\longrightarrow 0.

There is a natural map
V^{c}(f_{\beta})^{*}\to V(f_{\beta})^{*}
whose image is the
\tau^{-1}\varepsilon^{1+r}
subrepresentation of the latter; see e.g. the discussion around
[
BD15
, Rk. 5.10]
.

Since the objective is considering families of modular forms passing through
f_{\beta}
, we need to make an assumption about the behavior of the eigencurve at that point. More precisely, with this purpose in mind, we assume for the rest of the text the following condition.

Assumption 2.4
.

The Coleman–Mazur–Buzzard cuspidal eigencurve
\mathcal{C}^{0}
of tame level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
is smooth at
f_{\beta}
and locally étale over the weight space.

This assumption is automatic when
f
is
decent
and
non-critical
; as discussed in
[
LRS24
, §A]
, these are fairly general conditions. Moreover, this means that may take an affinoid open
U=\mathrm{Spa}(A_{U},A_{U}^{\circ})
around
f_{\beta}
mapping isomorphically to its image in the weight space.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}
be the universal eigenform over
U
, which is an overconvergent cuspidal eigenform with coefficients in
A_{U}
. We also write
\beta_{{\mathbf{f}}}\in A_{U}^{\times}
for the Hecke
U(p)
-eigenvalue of
{\mathbf{f}}
. There is also a canonical Galois pseudo-character
t_{{\mathbf{f}}}\colon G_{\mathbf{Q}}\rightarrow A_{U}
satisfying

t_{{\mathbf{f}}}(\Frob_{\ell}^{-1})=a_{\ell}({\mathbf{f}})

for all good primes
\ell
.

According to Proposition
A.11
and Remark
A.13
, there exist Galois representations
V({\mathbf{f}})
,
V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})
(resp.,
V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
,
V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
), free of rank-
2
over
A_{U}
whose traces equal to
t_{{\mathbf{f}}}
(resp.,
t_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{*}
). Moreover, by Corollary
A.12
, we have injections of Galois representations

V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})\hookrightarrow V({\mathbf{f}})\quad(\text{resp., }V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}\hookrightarrow V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}).

We refer the reader to Appendix
A
for the detailed construction of these representations.

Let
Y
be the modular curve of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p)
over
\mathbf{Q}
and let
\overline{Y}
be the base change of
Y
to
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
. Let
V=\mathrm{Spa}(A_{V},A_{V}^{\circ})
be the image of

under the weight map (note that we shrink

is isomorphic to

) and let

be the universal weight of

) and consider the étale cohomology

(cf. Definition

, one sees that it is a Hecke-equivariant direct summand of

(and is independent to

\operatorname{Pr}_{{\mathbf{f}}}:H^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\overline{Y},\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})\rightarrow V({\mathbf{f}}).

By
[

, Thm. 1]
,

has maximal reducibility ideal. Then, it follows that the fiber of

at

Since the map

with all of the successive quotients l-dimensional over

with quotients alternately isomorphic to either

or

.

2.3.

The aim of this section is describing the canonical periods arising in the reciprocity laws, following the seminal developments of Ohta

3.
Circular units

The main aim of this work is exploring the degeneration of the Euler system of Beilinson–Kato classes when it passes through a critical Eisenstein series. In those cases, we expect to recover the system of
circular units
. In order to properly introduce it, let
\chi
be an even, primitive, and non-trivial Dirichlet character of conductor
N
(with
p\nmid N
), taking values in
L
. Write
\mathcal{O}_{\chi,p}
for the ring of integers of an extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
containing the values of
\chi
.

As in the introduction, let
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
be the distribution algebra of
\Gamma\cong\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}
(with
L
-coefficients); that is,
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
is the global section of the rigid analytic space associated with
\mathrm{Spf}\,\mathcal{O}_{L}[\![\Gamma]\!]
. For our further use, let
{\mathbf{j}}\colon\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}^{\times}
be the universal character, which we regard as a character of the Galois group by composition with the cyclotomic character. The Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function attached to
\chi
, denoted by
L_{p}(\chi)
, is an element of
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
which interpolates special values of the Dirichlet
L
-function of
\chi
. We refer
[
Das16
, §3]
for a more detailed treatment of the properties of this
p
-adic
L
-function with our current conventions, which slightly differ from
[
Col00
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a unique element
L_{p}(\chi)\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
satisfying the following interpolation property:

L_{p}(\chi)(j)=\begin{cases}(1-p^{j-1}\chi(p)^{-1})\frac{2N^{j}(j-1)!}{(-2\pi i)^{j}\tau(\chi)}L(\chi,j)&\text{$j\geqslant 2$ even},\\
(1-p^{-j}\chi(p))L(\chi,j)&\text{$j\leqslant-1$ odd}\end{cases},

where
\tau(\chi)=\sum_{a\in(\mathbf{Z}/N)^{\times}}\chi(a)\zeta_{N}^{a}
is the Gauss sum.

Remark 3.2
.

We follow a post of David Loeffler in MathOverflow to explain how this interpolation property is consistent with other conventions. We are grateful to him for a nice discussion on the conventions around that point.

The element
L_{p}(\chi)
belongs to the Iwasawa algebra
\mathcal{O}_{\chi,p}[\![\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}]\!]
, which is a product of
(p-1)
subrings, indexed by
\mathbf{Z}/(p-1)\mathbf{Z}
, each of them isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{\chi,p}[\![T]\!]
.

Then, for any
t\in\mathbf{Z}/(p-1)\mathbf{Z}
with
(-1)^{t}=\chi(-1)
, there exists a continuous function
L_{p,t}(\chi)\colon\mathbf{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{p}
such that, for all integers
k\geq 0
with
k\equiv t\pmod{p-1}
, we have
L_{p,t}(\chi,k)=(1-\chi(p)p^{k-1})\cdot L(\chi,1-k)
. For the subrings corresponding to the sign condition
(-1)^{t}=-\chi(-1)
, we define the corresponding
L_{p,t}(\chi)
using the interpolation property along the positive integers, thus having
(p-1)
different
p
-adic
L
-function which can be packaged in a single element
L_{p}(\chi)
of
\mathcal{O}_{\chi,p}[\![\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}]\!]
.

With these conventions, the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function satisfies a functional equation, which is discussed in
[
Das16
, §3.2]
.
However, although the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function may be defined along any of the
(p-1)
discs of the weight space, regardless of any parity constraint, the cohomology classes can only be defined over half of them.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\chi
be an even, primitive, and non-trivial Dirichlet character of conductor
N
. Let
H
be the subfield of
\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{N})
cut out by
\ker(\chi)
, and let

u_{\chi}=\prod_{a=1}^{N-1}\chi(a)\otimes(1-\zeta^{a})\in(\mathcal{O}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{H}^{\times})^{\chi}.

This kind of elements, often called

, are the essential piece for constructing the easiest instance of Euler systems, that of circular units. Consider the module

V(\chi)^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}}):=\mathcal{O}_{\chi,p}(\chi^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}).

Here, as usual, we identify
\chi
with a character of the Galois group. It is characterized by the property that for any integer

(viewed as a ring homomorphism

.

Modifying the previous units as described in

whose image in

is the image of

under the Kummer map. Note that the Iwasawa cohomology of

for its universal character, and considering

as an element of the slightly smaller space

.

Remark 3.4
.

\operatorname{Col}_{\chi}(\loc_{p}(c(\chi)))=L_{p}(\chi)\big|_{\Gamma^{+}},

4.
Beilinson–Kato elements

The Kato Euler system (or Beilinson–Kato Euler system) is one of the most significant constructions in the study of
p
-adic methods in Number Theory. It has enabled the proof of new cases of the Bloch–Kato conjecture and provided a general approach to establish one divisibility in the Iwasawa main conjecture. More concretely, the Kato Euler system of
[
Kat04
]
can be attached to a general Coleman family
{\mathbf{f}}
, as discussed e.g. by Hansen in
[
Han15
]
or by Benois and Büyükboduk in
[
BB22
]
and
[
BB24
]
; for the the case of Hida families, the theory is known since the work of Ochiai
[
Och03
]
. In this section, we begin by recalling the (two-variable)
p
-adic
L
-function of Kitagawa and Mazur, and later discuss the reciprocity law, emphasizing the role of the (one-variable) adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function. This discussion, and in particular the proof of the reciprocity law, does not hold at the critical Eisenstein point, so we later need to carry out certain modifications to adapt it.

4.1.
The Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic

-function

Let
f_{k}
be the newform associated to the weight
k+2

{\mathbf{f}}
, for some

. Assume that

does not correspond to a

-stabilization of an Eisenstein series, and let
\mathbf{Q}(f_{k})
the field of coefficients of

. Then, for each sign
\pm

on which the Hecke operators act by the eigensystem of

is one-dimensional, and we choose bases
\gamma_{f}^{\pm}
of theses spaces. These determine complex periods
\Omega_{f_{k}}^{\pm}\in\mathbf{C}^{\times}

With the previous notations, let

be the affinoid open over which the family

is defined. Then we have an

, free of rank 2. We choose bases

of the complex-conjugation eigenspaces

, which are free of rank one over
A_{U}
. As pointed out in
[
LRS24
, §B2.1]

in such a way that they lie in the space
(L\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}(f_{k})}\mathbf{C})^{\times}

Definition 4.1
.

Let

-adic

-function attached to the family

and the periods

-values of all classical, weight
\geqslant 2

{\mathbf{f}}
.

More precisely, the value at
(k,j)
, with

L

[

, §4]
or
[

]
for the precise form of the interpolation property, which involves different kinds of factors.

This construction was considered in generality by Bellaïche in
[

]
, using modular symbols; note that there are cases where the interpolation property does not completely characterize the
p
-adic
L
-function, which is not the case in the ordinary situation.

4.2.
The adjoint

-adic
L

For this section, we mainly follow the works of Hida
[

and Bellaïche
[

-adic
L
-function, using Kim’s scalar product on overconvergent modular symbols. This was later generalized to a more general setting in the recent works of Balasubramanyam, Bergdall, and Longo
[
BBL20
]
, or Lee and Wu
[
LW25
]
. See also
[
Mak23
]
for a detailed treatment of the topic.

Before establishing the reciprocity law for Beilinson–Kato elements, we need to present another
p
-adic
L
-function attached to the Coleman family
{\mathbf{f}}
. For any classical and cuspidal specialization
f
of
{\mathbf{f}}
, we have plus and minus periods
\Omega^{\pm}_{{\mathbf{f}}}
. The ratio

L^{\mathrm{alg}}(\operatorname{Ad}f,1)\coloneqq\frac{-2^{k-1}i\pi^{2}\langle f,f\rangle}{\Omega^{+}_{f}\Omega^{-}_{f}}

is in
\mathbf{Q}(f)^{\times}
.

If we choose bases
\gamma_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{\pm}
over the family as above, and use the periods for each
f_{k}
determined by these, then a construction due to Hida
[
Hid16
]
, and more generally to Bellaïche
[
Bel21
]
, gives a
p
-adic adjoint
L
-function
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
. As a further piece of notation, we say that a family
{\mathbf{f}}
is
p
-distinguished if the semisimplification of the mod-
p
Galois representation attached to
{\mathbf{f}}
,
\bar{\rho}_{{\mathbf{f}}}|_{G_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}}
, is the direct sum of two distinct characters.

Proposition 4.2
.

There exists a one-variable adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function

L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})\in A_{U}

interpolating the ratios

L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})(k)=p^{k-1}\alpha_{k}(p-1)\left(1-\tfrac{\beta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\right)\left(1-\tfrac{\beta_{k}}{p\alpha_{k}}\right)L^{\mathrm{alg}}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{k},1),

for all
k\in U\cap\mathbf{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
such that the specialization
f_{k}
is the
p
-stabilization of a level
N
cuspidal newform. If
{\mathbf{f}}
is
p
-distinguished, then the congruence ideal of
{\mathbf{f}}
is principal, and this ideal is generated by
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
.

As pointed out in
[
Mak23
]
, Bellaïche’s interpolation formula is only given at crystalline points of trivial nebentype, while in Thm. 2.3.7 of loc. cit. the author gives a much more general result which, however, is only valid in the setting of ordinary Hida families.

The adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function vanishes at the ramification points of the eigencurve. For example, this is the case for the weight one RM theta series. At a critical Eisenstein point, this
p
-adic
L
-function also vanishes. More concretely, we recall the following result from
[
Bel21
, §9]
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
x\in U
is a point that either has non-integral weight, or is cuspidal classical, then the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function has a zero if and only if the weight map is not étale at
x
. Otherwise, it has a zero.

Proof.

This follows from the results of
[
Bel21
, §9]
, based on Kim’s arguments
[
Kim06
]
.
∎

In particular, the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function vanishes at the critical Eisenstein points, although the ramification index is
e=1
; this is in contrast with the cuspidal setting, where it could be understood as a measure of ramification. In particular,
[
BB22
]
and
[
BB24
]
discuss the relationship between the ramification index of the cuspidal eigencurve and the order of vanishing of the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function.

4.3.
Hida–Rankin vs Kitagawa–Mazur

The Beilinson–Kato Euler system is associated with the Galois representation of a single modular form or family. However, the reciprocity law we aim to establish does not relate it to the Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-function directly, but rather to the product of two such functions, with dependence on the choice of an auxiliary Dirichlet character. Furthermore, this product is not exactly composed of two Kitagawa–Mazur functions; instead, it corresponds to a Hida–Rankin
p
-adic
L
-function, which agrees with the former up to the choice of periods (represented by the adjoint
L
-function). Specifically, for a modular form
f\in S_{k}(N,\chi)
, there are two possibilities for the choice of periods: the Petersson product
\langle f,f\rangle
, and the product
\Omega_{f}^{+}\Omega_{f}^{-}
. As we have already discussed, the discrepancy between these choices is measured by the
p
-adic
L
-function of the adjoint representation.

The following result establishes how the Hida–Rankin
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
attached to two families (either Hida or Coleman families) can be expressed as the product of two Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-functions. In particular, note that the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
is a three-variable
p
-adic
L
-function, depending on the two weights and the cyclotomic variable.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
{\mathbf{g}}
be the (ordinary) Eisenstein family of characters
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
. Then, the following factorization formula holds:

L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})(k,\ell,s)\times L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})(k)=\mathfrak{f}(k)\times L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{1})(k,s)\times L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{2})(k,s-\ell+1),

where
\mathfrak{f}(k)
is an explicit factor depending just on
k
.

Proof.

This follows by a direct comparison of the critical values, as in
[
BD14
, Thm. 3.4]
, noting that the Euler factors also match. Observe that in loc. cit. the authors restrict to the ordinary case, while e.g. in
[
BDV22
, §4.3]
the proof is adapted to general families.

Observe, however, that in this case we cannot discard the factors contributing to the adjoint, since they can be zero (and indeed, they vanish in the situations we want to study).
∎

Since the dependence on
\mathfrak{f}(k)
is very minor and does not affect any of our results, we can (and we do) renormalize the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function dividing by the fudge factor
\mathfrak{f}(k)
, and thus remove it from the statement of Prop.
4.4
.

Remark 4.5
.

We do not actually need the existence of the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function in this setting. We can just claim an equality of the form

L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})(k,\ell,s)\times\mathfrak{g}(k)=L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{1})(k,s)\times L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{2})(k,s-\ell+1),

where
\mathfrak{g}(k)
is a
p
-adic function interpolating the adjoint periods.

As a piece of notation, we put
{\mathbf{g}}={\mathbf{E}}
to denote the Eisenstein family of characters
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
, provided that the characters are clear from the set-up. In general, in the case of even weights, we will not use the three variables
(k,\ell,s)
, and we often impose the restriction
s=\frac{k}{2}+\ell-1
. Hence,
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{2})(k,s-\ell+1)=L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{2})(k,k/2)
does not depend on
s
, and it is just a function on
k
. In this case,

(2)

L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})(k,\ell,s)\times L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})(k)=\mathfrak{f}(k)\times L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{1})(k,s)\times L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi_{2})(k,k/2).

We complement the previous result by discussing the Kato elements in the context of triple products and the Artin formalism, examining how the previous result can be interpreted as a factorization formula for a triple of modular forms
(f,g,h)
, where both
g
and
h
are Eisenstein. In particular, the choice
s=\frac{k}{2}+\ell-1
corresponds to taking
g
and
h
of the same weight.

More precisely, let
f\in S_{k}
,
g=E_{\ell}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
and
h=E_{m}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
. Write
c=\frac{k+\ell+m}{2}-1
for the center of the
L
-function
L(f\otimes g\otimes h,s)
. Note that, by Artin formalism,
L(f\otimes g\otimes h,s)
may be written as the product of the four
L
-functions attached to the twist of
f
by the corresponding Dirichlet character:

L(f\otimes\chi_{2}\xi_{2},c)\cdot L(f\otimes\chi_{1}\xi_{2},c-\ell+1)\cdot L(f\otimes\chi_{2}\xi_{1},c-m+1)\cdot L(\chi_{1}\xi_{1},c-\ell-m+2).

Proceeding as in Bertolini–Darmon
[
BD14
]
, we may write the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function as the product of two Kitagawa–Mazur functions, using the appropriate functional equations

L_{p}(f,\chi_{2}\xi_{2},c)=L_{p}(f,\theta^{-1}\chi_{2}^{-1}\xi_{2}^{-1},k-c)=L_{p}(f,\chi_{1}\xi_{1},c-\ell-m+2)

and that

L_{p}(f,\chi_{1}\xi_{2},c-\ell+1)=L_{p}(f,\theta^{-1}\chi_{1}^{-1}\xi_{2}^{-1},c-\ell+1)=L_{p}(f,\chi_{2}\xi_{1},c-m+1).

Observe indeed that this is consistent with the factorization formula of Proposition
4.4
when one takes
\ell=m
.

4.4.
Kato’s Euler system

For this section we follow the foundational work of
[
Kat04
]
, but using the language and conventions of more modern approaches like
[
Och03
]
,
[
BD14
]
or
[
BB22
]
.

Recall that we have chosen bases
\gamma^{\pm}_{{\mathbf{f}}}
of the complex-conjugation eigenspaces
V({\mathbf{f}})^{(c=\pm 1)}
, which are free of rank one over
A_{U}
. For the moment, we assume that we are outside the Eisenstein case.

Definition 4.6
.

Let

\kappa({\mathbf{f}})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}}))

denote the canonical
Kato class
attached to the bases
\gamma_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{\pm}
, which is the “
p
-direction” of an Euler system.

This class is constructed by interpolating the cup product of two modular units (or Eisenstein classes when the weight is greater than two), and then projecting to the corresponding isotopic component. The Kato class also depends on an auxiliary Dirichlet character, that we have denoted by
\chi
.

Remark 4.7
.

The construction of the Kato system depends on the choice of auxiliary integers
(c,d)
, relatively prime to
6Np
, where
N
is the tame level of the family. However, it is possible to normalize the class in order to avoid that dependence. See e.g. the discussion of
[
BB22
, App. B]
. Observe that this is another point where the theory differs from the Beilinson–Flach case, where the factor cannot be always inverted (there are issues with that in the self-dual case), as it is discussed in
[
KLZ17
]
.

Kato’s explicit reciprocity law
[
Kat04
]
establishes that the image of that class under the Perrin-Riou map recovers the
p
-adic
L
-function. That result requires the following definition, which may be seen as a measure of the contribution of the
bad
primes. We follow for this part
[
BB22
, §6.6.3]
, where they discuss this issue in terms of an
imprimitive

p
-adic
L
-function

Definition 4.8
.

We write
\mathcal{E}_{N}(x)
for the two-variable
p
-adic
L
-function corresponding to the product of the bad primes:

\mathcal{E}_{N}(x)=\prod_{\ell\mid N}(1-a_{\ell}({\mathbf{f}})\sigma_{\ell}^{-1})\in A_{U}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}.

Since we are assuming that the prime
p
does not divide
N
, this element must be understood as an analytic two-variable function in the weight and cyclotomic variables, which measures the difference between the motivic
p
-adic
L
-function and the analytic
p
-adic
L
-function (these factors are present in the Euler system, and may be inverted under some additional and mild assumptions).

More precisely, let
\mathcal{F}^{+}V({\mathbf{f}})
denote the rank 1 unramified subrepresentation of
V({\mathbf{f}})
over
A_{U}
(which exists since
{\mathbf{f}}
is ordinary); and let
\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}\in\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{F}^{+}V({\mathbf{f}}))
be the canonical vector constructed in
[
KLZ17
]
, which is characterized by interpolating the classes
\eta_{f}
of
Section

2.3
for each classical specialization
f
of
{\mathbf{f}}
. Then we have the following reciprocity law, where we have an extra contribution coming from the Euler factors at
bad
primes. For the following proposition, write
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}^{\PR}
for the Perrin-Riou logarithm attached to
\mathcal{F}^{-}V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
.

We state the reciprocity law in the framework of
[
BB22
]
, where we avoid the critical Eisenstein points.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}
be a Coleman family such that all its specializations are cuspidal (and which, in particular, does not pass through the critical
p
-stabilization of an Eisenstein series). Then, the following explicit reciprocity law holds:

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}^{\PR}(\loc_{p}\kappa({\mathbf{f}})),\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}\right\rangle\cdot L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})=\mathcal{E}_{N}(x)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}}).

Proof.

This is Kato’s explicit reciprocity law in the framework of Coleman families, as developed e.g. in
[
BB22
]
. Observe that the presence of the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function, and also the auxiliary Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
is usually treated as a unique contribution in terms of a non-vanishing factor, but we want to emphasize this dependence here since we will later need to be more circumspect around this point.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

There are some crucial differences between the theory of Beilinson–Flach and Beilinson–Kato classes. In particular, the latter depends on the choice of a suitable class in homology; the results of Ash and Stevens guarantee that this can be done in such a way that the Kato class is non-zero. Observe, however, that in the arguments of
[
BB22
]
it is crucial that the critical point is cuspidal.

Proposition
4.9
must be understood as an equality in two variables, with
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
playing an auxiliary role. In many case, it is possible to invert the factor
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
and normalize the Kato class dividing by it; however, in the Eisenstein case one must be more circumspect and we cannot do this normalization.

Remark 4.11
.

In the framework of triple products, when we had two ordinary Eisenstein series, one can formally the classes lie in

H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V(f)^{*}(\chi_{1}^{-1}\xi_{1}^{-1})(1-(c-\ell-m+2))).

Its image under the Perrin-Riou map is not just
L_{p}(f,\chi_{1}\xi_{1},c-\ell-m+2)
, as expected, since there is also the contribution coming from
L_{p}(f,\chi_{1}\xi_{2},c-\ell+m+1)
. If we further impose impose the condition
\chi_{1}\xi_{1}=1
, and letting
t=(k+\ell-m)/2
, we have a class in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V(f)^{*}(1-s))
whose image is
L_{p}(f,\chi,t)\times L_{p}(f,s)
, suitably normalized by the adjoint.

When we specialize at the critical Eisenstein series, either
L_{p}(f,\chi,t)
or
L_{p}(f,s)
must vanish, but these two zeros are very different in nature, since the honest
p
-adic
L
-function for the representation is
L_{p}(f,s)
. In this part of the article, we consider the situation where
L_{p}(f,\chi,t)
is zero.

In the setting where
{\mathbf{f}}
is not a critical Coleman family, the classes are usually renormalized to avoid dependence on the character
\chi
. More precisely, we may consider the normalized class

\widetilde{\kappa({\mathbf{f}})}=\frac{L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})}{L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)}\times\kappa({\mathbf{f}}).

This class satisfies a reciprocity law that directly relates it to the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
, thus avoiding the inconvenient appearance of the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function and the auxiliary Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-function. However, as we later discuss, we must exercise more caution in the Eisenstein setting, since both
p
-adic
L
-functions vanish at the critical point.

5.
Deformation of Beilinson–Kato elements

The aim of this section is to study the two–variable Beilinson–Kato elements in the case where they pass through a critical Eisenstein family. In particular, we want to relate the natural projection of that cohomology class with the circular units discussed in Section
3
. Since the aim of this section is to establish Theorem
1.3
, we assume that
r
is even, although many of the results also hold in the odd case.

In particular, as we have already emphasized, the results of Benois and Büyükboduk do not hold in our case, and we need to adapt both the interpolation of the Beilinson–Kato class and the explicit reciprocity law.

5.1.
Set-up for the degeneration process

Consider the Beilinson–Kato class attached to two modular forms. As before, let
f=E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)
stand for the Eisenstein series of weight
r+2
and characters
(\psi,\tau)
, and let
f_{\beta}
be its critical slope
p
-stabilization. Under the non-criticality conditions we have fixed, there is a unique Coleman family
{\mathbf{f}}
passing through
f_{\beta}
, defined over some affinoid disc
U\ni r
. We may suppose that for all integers
k\in U\cap\mathbf{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
with
k\neq r
, the specialization
f_{k}
is a non-critical slope cusp form.

We also consider the modules

V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}}):=V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}})

and, in a similar way, the compactly supported version

V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}}):=V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}).

5.2.
Families over punctured discs

In this section, we discuss how we can make sense of a Beilinson–Kato cohomology class
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
attached to the Coleman family
{\mathbf{f}}
. The next result establishes that we have such a family of cohomology classes, maybe with a simple pole at the Eisenstein point.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a cohomology class

\kappa({\mathbf{f}})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}(-{\mathbf{j}}))

with the following interpolation property: if
k\geq 0
is an integer with
k\neq r
, then we have

\kappa({\mathbf{f}})(k)=\mathcal{BK}^{[f_{k}]}\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V(f_{k})^{*}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}})),

where the element
\mathcal{BK}^{[f_{k}]}=\mathcal{BK}^{[f_{k}]}_{1,1}
is as defined e.g. in Theorem 9.2.1 of
[
KLZ17
]
.

Proof.

To begin with, observe that the results of
[
BB22
]
do not apply in this setting, since, in particular, the key Prop. 6.5 of loc. cit. requires all the specializations being cuspidal, as it occurs in the Beilinson–Flach case in
[
LZ16
]
.

The result follows verbatim the reasoning of
[
LZ16
, Thm. 5.4.2]
, which establishes an analogous result when all integer-weight specializations of
{\mathbf{f}}
are classical. In this case, the result does not apply for
k=r
, but inverting
X
resolves the issue. In particular, the analysis of the comparison maps in cohomology developed in
[
LRS24
, §A]
makes clear that we are only introducing a simple pole at the critical Eisenstein point.
∎

The result asserts that we can directly interpolate the Beilinson–Kato classes in a neighborhood of the critical Eisenstein series, where the points are all cuspidal, and that the class can only present a simple pole at
f_{\beta}
. Next, we consider the projection of
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
in cohomology.

Definition 5.2
.

We write
\kappa(f_{\beta})
for the image of
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
in the cohomology of the quotient

\frac{\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}{V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}).

Note that this class is not zero a priori, as it happened in the scenarios discussed in
[
LRS24
]
, where the key point was that the class satisfied certain
good
local properties.

5.3.
The
p
-adic
L
-function and a factorization formula

We being by recalling the following definition regarding the logarithmic distribution.

Definition 5.3
.

For a continuous character
\sigma:\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{p}
, the function
\frac{d^{k}\sigma}{dz^{k}}\cdot\frac{z^{k}}{\sigma(z)}
is a constant, and
\log^{[k]}\in\mathbf{C}_{p}
is defined to be this constant.

An equivalent definition for
\log_{p}^{[k]}
is
\log_{p}^{[k]}(\sigma)=w(\sigma)(w(\sigma)-1)\cdots(w(\sigma)-k+1)
, where
w(\sigma)=\frac{\log_{p}(\sigma(1+p))}{\log_{p}(1+p)}
. The analytic function
\log_{p}^{[k]}
vanishes to order 1 at the characters
\sigma
of the form
z\mapsto z^{j}\chi(z)
, where
\chi
is a finite order Dirichlet character and
j
is an integer such that
0\leq j\leq k-1
.

Remark 5.4
.

Following the analogy with the Beilinson–Flach case, we would expect the class
\kappa(f_{\beta})
to be divisible by the logarithmic distribution
\log^{[r+1]}\in\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
. Let us sketch why the same argument that we had used in that case does not work here. The specialization of
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
at a locally-algebraic character of
\Gamma
of degree
j\in\{0,\dots,r\}
factors through the image of
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{V}-j}^{s}\otimes\operatorname{TSym}^{j}
in
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda_{V}-(r+1)}^{s}\otimes\operatorname{TSym}^{(r+1)}
, and the maps
\Pr_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{[j]}
and
\Pr_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{[r+1]}
agree on this image up to a non-zero scalar. Since the
\Pr_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{[j]}
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant r
do not have poles at
X=0
, it follows that the specializations of
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
at a pair
(r,\chi)
, for
\ell\geqslant r
and
\chi
locally-algebraic of degree
\in\{0,\dots,r\}
, interpolate the projections of the classical Beilinson–Kato classes to the
(E_{r+2}^{\crit})
-eigenspaces in classical cohomology. However, in this setting, we cannot ensure whether or not these classes arise as suitable projections of classes in the cohomology of
X_{1}(N)
.

Consider now the two-variable
p
-adic

-function

as introduced in Section

. For this construction, the interpolation property applies also at

without any further modifications; and here the complex

L(E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau),1+j)=L(\psi,1+j)\cdot L(\tau,j-r).

Moreover, the restriction of

to the

[

]

agrees with the parity of

.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\lambda
denote the function defined over the Iwasawa algebra such that its specialization at a character

As usual, we write

for the critical

. Note that

may be seen as the (one-variable)

.

Theorem 5.6
.

With the previous notations, we have the following:

(a)

L_{p}(f_{\beta},s)=0
, if
(-1)^{s}=\psi(-1)
.

(b)

Up to an explicit rational factor,

L_{p}(f_{\beta},s)=\lambda(s)\log_{p}^{[r+1]}(s)L_{p}(\psi,s)L_{p}(\tau,s-r-1),

if

.

See
[
BD15
, §5, 6]
. More concretely, the proof of the first statement relies on the application

, defined in

, which allows us to relate the critical weight

∎

Furthermore, the factor

However, as we have already emphasized, the

-adic

-function that appears in the reciprocity law is not this one, but rather the Hida–Rankin

-adic
L
-function, which outside the critical line factors as the product of the two Kitagawa–Mazur

-adic

and

, suitably normalized by the adjoint

The product of the two Kitagawa–Mazur

-adic
L
-functions always vanishes at the critical Eisenstein point, since one of them lies in the region of the weight space where Theorem
5.6
guarantees that the
p
-adic
L
-function is zero. It is important to note that this does not imply that the image of the Beilinson–Kato class is zero, as this zero is canceled by the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function. Hence, by combining Proposition
4.4
with Theorem
5.6
, we obtain the following corollary. As a piece of notation, we write
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
for the derivative of the one-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)(k,k/2)
at the specialization
f_{\beta}
; similarly,
L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})
denotes the derivative of
L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
at
f_{\beta}
. For the following result, recall that we are working under the assumption that
r
is even.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
{\mathbf{E}}
be the (ordinary) Eisenstein family of characters
(1,\chi)
, and set
s=\frac{r}{2}+\ell
. Then, the following factorization formula holds when
(-1)^{r/2}(-1)^{\ell}=\psi(-1)
:

L_{p}(f_{\beta},{\mathbf{E}})(\ell,s)\times L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})=\mathfrak{f}(r)\cdot\log_{p}^{[r+1]}(s)\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)\times L_{p}(\psi,s)\times L_{p}(\tau,s-r-1).

Proof.

This follows by taking derivatives along the weight direction in Proposition
4.4
, and using that both
L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})
and
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
vanish at the critical Eisenstein point. Then, specializing at
k=r
and applying Theorem
5.6
, the result follows.
∎

The previous result gives a precise description of
L_{p}(f_{\beta},{\mathbf{E}})
as the product of a derivative of a Kitagawa–Mazur
p
-adic
L
-function and two Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-functions, normalized by the adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function. Compare e.g. with
[
RR21
, Thm. 3.3]
, where a similar result was derived in the setting of congruences between cuspidal forms and Eisenstein series.

5.4.
Perrin-Riou maps

Once we have introduced the different
p
-adic
L
-functions and the Euler system, we aim to relate
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
to the image of the localization of the Beilinson–Kato class,
\operatorname{loc}_{p}(\kappa({\mathbf{f}}))
, under the projection to
\mathcal{F}^{-}V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}
.

At this point, and for establishing an explicit reciprocity law, the theory presents notable differences with that of Beilinson–Flach elements. In particular, the Beilinson–Kato element depends on the choice of a homology period. By a result of Ash–Stevens
[
Kat04
, Thm. 13.6]
in the cuspidal case, there exists a choice of periods which guarantees that the class is non-zero. Further, the cohomology class obtained for a different choice of periods gives another class that, for any specialization, is a multiple of the one coming from Ash–Stevens. Unfortunately, in the Eisenstein case, we cannot claim that such a good normalization exists, so it is possible that all the possible choices give the zero class. Then, our results of this section must be interpreted as follows: if there exists a choice of period for which
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
is not zero, we prove a reciprocity law for that class, which later allows us to relate it with a circular unit; if not, we just get the zero class.

Remark 5.8
.

This discussion suggests, as a very natural question, if we can prove that there exists a normalization of the Kato class such that
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
is non-zero.

Perrin-Riou’s regulator gives us a map

\operatorname{Col}_{\mathbf{a}_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}}=\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\PR}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}(-),\mathbf{a}_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}\right\rangle:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},\mathcal{F}^{-}D({\mathbf{f}})^{*})\to\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}A_{U}

which interpolates the Perrin-Riou regulators for
f_{k}
for varying
k
. Let
\varphi^{-1}
stand for the left inverse of the Frobenius, denoted as
\psi
in
[
KLZ17
, §8.2]
. More precisely, for
z\in\left(\mathcal{F}^{-}D({\mathbf{f}})^{*}\right)^{\varphi^{-1}=1}
, this map sends
z
to

\langle\iota((1-\varphi)z),\mathbf{a}_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}\rangle,

where
\iota
is the inclusion

\left(\mathcal{F}^{-}D({\mathbf{f}})^{*}\right)^{\varphi^{-1}=0}\hookrightarrow\left(\mathcal{F}^{-}D({\mathbf{f}})^{*}[1/t]\right)^{\varphi^{-1}=0}=\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}\left(\mathcal{F}^{-}D({\mathbf{f}})^{*}\right)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}.

At the bad fiber, we have the relation

\mathbf{a}^{-}_{{\mathbf{f}}}\bmod X=d_{r}t^{r+1}\omega_{f_{r}}\otimes e_{-(r+1)}

where
e_{n}
is the standard basis of
\mathbf{Z}_{p}(n)
and
d_{r}
is a constant. Since multiplication by
t^{r+1}
corresponds to multiplication by
\log^{[r+1]}
on the
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}
side (see e.g.
[
LLZ11
]
), we conclude that

\operatorname{Col}_{\mathbf{a}_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}}(\kappa({\mathbf{f}}))\bmod X=d_{r}\cdot\log^{[r+1]}\cdot\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\text{PR}}_{\tau^{-1}}(\kappa(f_{\beta})),\omega_{f_{k}}\right\rangle.

We now establish the explicit reciprocity law for a family
{\mathbf{f}}
through a critical Eisenstein point. Note that this is not automatic from our previous result, which allows us to claim it in a neighborhood of the critical Eisenstein point, thus leading to the present of some annoying constants.

In order to state the next result, we recover the setting of Section
4.3
. In particular, we are working with two-variables
(k,s)
, which may be understood in terms of the variables
(k,\ell)
corresponding to the weight specializations of the Coleman family
{\mathbf{f}}
and the ordinary Eisenstein family
{\mathbf{E}}
, where, with the notations of Section
4.3
,
(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=(1,\chi)
. In order to simplify the arguments in the next result, we assume that
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)\neq 0
, although a minor modification of the leading term argument can solve the issue.

Theorem 5.9
.

Under Assumption
1.1
and Assumption
1.2
, we have

\operatorname{Col}_{\mathbf{a}_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}}(\kappa({\mathbf{f}}))\cdot L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})=d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})\cdot\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi,{\mathbf{k}}/2)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}}),

where
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
is either the zero function, or, if not, it is a meromorphic function on
U
alone, regular and non-vanishing at all integer weights
k\geqslant-1
except possibly at
r
itself, and regular at
k=r
. Recall that
\mathcal{E}_{N}
is the product of the bad Euler factors introduced in Definition
4.8
.

Proof.

It follows from the reciprocity laws for Rankin–Selberg convolutions (of cuspidal forms) that the quotient
\operatorname{Col}_{\mathbf{a}_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}}(\kappa({\mathbf{f}}))/(\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{E}}))
is a function of
{\mathbf{k}}
alone, and this ratio is either identically zero or, following the discussion of
[
BB22
, §7]
, it does not vanish at any integer
k\geqslant-1
where
f_{k}
is classical; it is equal to the fudge-factor
d_{k}
defined above.

Moreover, since under the current assumptions
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{E}})
is well defined and non-zero along
\{r\}\times\mathcal{W}
, we conclude that
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
does not have a pole at
{\mathbf{k}}
(although it might have a zero there).
∎

Remark 5.10
.

The non-vanishing of
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
must be understood as a very mild assumption, since the character
\chi
can be chosen among all the possible Dirichlet characters satisfying a parity constraint. Further, if
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)=0
, the function
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
can have a (not necessarily simple) pole; in that case, our results can be adapted considering the leading term of
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
in its Laurent expansion.

Note that the right hand side of the previous theorem may be understood as a Hida–Rankin
p
-adic
L
-function, which factors as the function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
involved in our study times an extra factor that we may usually neglect for a fixed specialization of
f
. In particular, the previous theorem may be refined following Corollary
5.7
, taking derivatives and then applying the factorization formula for
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
.

Remark 5.11
.

Observe that this result is only valid in half of the weight space. If we assume for simplicity that both
r
and
\psi
are even, the reciprocity law encodes in the
p
-adic
L
-function the values of classes in the Iwasawa cohomology of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1-j)
when
j
is even. This is coherent with the fact that, in this case, Iwasawa cohomology has rank zero for odd values of
j
(that is, the cohomology class and the
p
-adic
L
-function vanish in those cases).

5.5.
Meromorphic Eichler–Shimura

Before comparing the specialization of the Beilinson–Kato class with the system of circular units, we need to understand the variation of the canonical differential
\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}
around the critical Eisenstein point. This is achieved in the following result.

Proposition 5.12
.

Under the running assumptions, there exists an integer
n\geq 0
and a unique isomorphism of
A_{U}
-modules

\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}\colon\mathbf{D}_{\mathrm{cris}}(\mathcal{F}^{-}D({\mathbf{f}})^{*})\cong X^{-n}A_{U}

whose specialization at every
k\geq 1\in U
, with
k\neq r
, is the linear functional given by the pairing with the differential form
\eta_{f_{k}}
associated to the weight
k
specialization of
{\mathbf{f}}
. For this
\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}
, we have

\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\mathcal{F}^{-}}(-),\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}\right\rangle\cdot L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})=\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi,{\mathbf{k}}/2)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}}).

Proof.

We define
\eta_{{\mathbf{f}}}
to be the quotient
a_{{\mathbf{f}}}^{-}/d_{{\mathbf{f}}}
, and
n
is the order of vanishing of
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}
at
k=r
.
∎

If
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}(r)\neq 0
, then we have thus constructed a class in the Iwasawa cohomology of
V({\mathbf{f}}\times\tau)^{*}
whose regulator agrees with the product of the circular unit Euler system for
\tau
, and a shifted copy of the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function for
\psi
.

We claim that if
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}(r)=0
, then in fact
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
is divisible by
X
. If
d(r)=0
, then
\kappa(f_{\beta})
vanishes. So we can divide out a factor of
X
from both
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
and
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
, and repeat the argument. Since
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}
is not identically 0, this must terminate after finitely many steps. Thus we have the following version of the
leading term argument
, following the ideas introduced in
[
LZ20
]
.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
n\geqslant 0
be the order of vanishing of
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}
at
k=r
and suppose that Assumption
1.1
and
1.2
hold. Then
X^{-n}\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
is well defined and non-zero modulo
X
; and this leading term projects into the quotient
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))
. Its image under the Perrin-Riou regulator is given by a multiple of

\left(\frac{d^{*}_{{\mathbf{f}}}(r)\cdot\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)}{L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})}\right)\cdot L_{p}(E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau),{\mathbf{j}}+1),

where
d^{*}_{{\mathbf{f}}}(r)\in L^{\times}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from the previous discussion and the definition of
d^{*}_{{\mathbf{f}}}(r)
.
∎

Observe that the quotient
\frac{L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)}{L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})}
does not depend on the cyclotomic variable
{\mathbf{j}}
, and the same is true for
d^{*}_{{\mathbf{f}}}(r)
. Hence, this can be understood as a constant function over the Iwasawa algebra. Further, according to Theorem
5.6
,

L_{p}(f_{\beta},{\mathbf{j}}+1)=\lambda\cdot\log^{[r+1]}\cdot L_{p}(\psi,{\mathbf{j}}+1)\cdot L_{p}(\tau,{\mathbf{j}}-r).

Remark 5.14
.

As we have already emphasized, Proposition
5.13
can be adapted to the situation where
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
vanishes, by considering instead the product of the first non-vanishing term in the Taylor expansion and the leading term in the Laurent expansion of
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
.

Definition 5.15
.

Let

\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))

stand for the class obtained from
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
after multiplying it by
X^{-n}
.

5.6.
Comparison with circular units

In this section, we finally establish the main theorem of this note, relating the specialization of the Beilinson–Kato at the critical Eisenstein point with the system of circular units.

To establish the main result, we want to impose that
(-1)^{r}=\psi(-1)
. This assumption implies that
\tau
is even and gives two possibilities for
\psi
and
r
: either
r
is even and
\psi
is even, or
r
is odd and
\psi
is odd. If
r
is odd and
\tau
is even, the circular unit lies in the minus part of the Iwasawa cohomology, and hence the results we have discussed about circular units do not apply. Hence, we also assume that
r
is even.

Assumption 5.16
.

The integer
r
is even and the Dirichlet character
\tau
is also even.

The following result justifies why we have considered
L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})
and
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
.

Proposition 5.17
.

If Assumption
5.16
holds true, then
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})=0
and the (one-variable)
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)(k,k/2)
vanishes at
k=r
.

Proof.

The vanishing of
L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})
follows from the aforementioned results of
[
Bel21
, §9]
. Further,
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)(k,k/2)=0
as a consequence of Theorem
5.6
.
∎

In particular, this lead us to consider the derivatives of the above
p
-adic
L
-functions. It is an interesting question, that we have not being able to solve, to determine if these derivatives may be expressed in terms of
interesting
arithmetic objects related with the modular form.

We can now state and prove the main result of the note.

Theorem 5.18
.

Assume that
r
is even and that both
\psi
and
\tau
are even. Under Assumption
1.1
and Assumption
1.2
, the following equality holds in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}})))
:

\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})=\left(\frac{C\cdot\lambda\cdot\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)}{L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})}\right)\cdot L_{p}(\psi,{\mathbf{j}}+1)c(\tau)(r),

where
C
is an explicit constant which does not depend on
j
.

Proof.

We begin by noting that the Bloch–Kato conjecture implies that
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}})))
is one-dimensional. Hence, it suffices to compare the image of both classes under the Perrin-Riou map presented in Section
5.4
.

Using next Theorem
5.6
, the result is equivalent to checking the following equality between (two-variable)
p
-adic
L
-functions:

L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{E}})\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})=\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}}).

Here,
{\mathbf{E}}
is the Eisenstein family of characters
(1,\chi)
. The result follows by taking the derivative along the weight direction in the formula

L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{E}})\cdot L_{p}(\operatorname{Ad}{\mathbf{f}})(k)=\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)(k,k/2)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{f}}),

established in Section
4.3
.
∎

Remark 5.19
.

Contrarily to which occurs in the Beilinson–Flach case, there is not a logarithmic contribution in the formula. In
[
LRS24
]
that contribution came from the class itself, since we had been able to argue, in geometric terms, that it was divisible by
\log^{[r+1]}
. Here, although a term of that kind arises in the factorization formula of the
p
-adic
L
-function, it is canceled out by the Coleman map.

As we have already discussed, the terms
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},\chi)
and
L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})
do not depend on the cyclotomic variable, so we may drop them from the statement. Roughly speaking, we can say that
\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})
agrees with
c(\tau)(r)
multiplied by the Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-function of
\psi
.

Corollary 5.20
.

Assume that
r
is even and that both
\psi
and
\tau
are even. Under Assumption
1.1
and Assumption
1.2
, the following equality holds in
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))
:

\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})=\tilde{C}\cdot\lambda\cdot\mathcal{E}_{N}\cdot L_{p}(\psi,{\mathbf{j}}+1)\cdot c(\tau)(r),

where
\tilde{C}
is an explicit constant which does not depend on
j
.

6.
Vanishing of the cohomology class and a factorization formula

We emphasize some limitations of our method and propose natural extensions of this project. These extensions are related to changes in the parity of either
\tau
or
\psi
. We address two distinct issues: a factorization formula á la Bellaïche–Dasgupta when
\tau
is odd; and the situation arising from the vanishing of the class
\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))
.

6.1.
A conjectural factorization formula

For our later use, we consider the following assumption, that we will assume for most of the results of this section.

Assumption 6.1
.

The integer
r
is even and the Dirichlet character
\tau
is odd.

Two additional cases remain to be discussed, corresponding to the less common scenario where the integer
r
is odd. We explain how our results can be adapted to this setting towards the end of the section.

Under Assumption
6.1
, it is natural to wonder if one may get a result analogous of Bellaïche–Dasgupta to express the weight derivative
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta})
as the product of two Kubota–Leopoldt
p
-adic
L
-functions. This is motivated by the vanishing of
L_{p}(f_{\beta})
, which suggests the study of its derivative. This is explained in the following conjecture, whose proof seems beyond the techniques we have used in this work. At the end of the section we discuss some theoretical evidence towards it.

Conjecture 6.2
.

If
\tau
is odd–either under Assumption
1.2
or when
r
is odd and
\tau
is also odd–the following factorization formula holds:

L_{p}^{\prime}({\mathbf{f}})(r)=\lambda\cdot\log^{[r+1]}L_{p}(\psi,{\mathbf{j}}+1)\cdot L_{p}(\tau,{\mathbf{j}}-r),

where
\lambda
is the previously defined Iwasawa function and
L_{p}^{\prime}({\mathbf{f}})
denotes the weight derivative of the
p
-adic
L
-function.

It is interesting to observe the analogy with the treatment of
[
BB24
, §5]
, where the higher order derivatives of the
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
are studied. However, in loc. cit. the vanishing is due to the ramification of the eigencurve (in the cuspidal case,
e\geq 2
), and one can use the idea of Bellaïche of defining
secondary
p
-adic
L
-functions
. Here, the vanishing of
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
at the Eisenstein point is due not to the ramification, but to the vanishing of the corresponding eigenspace of modular symbols. However, it still makes sense to try to capture the information of the Eisenstein representation as the sum of two characters.

6.2.
Vanishing of the Kato class

Under Assumption
6.1
, we may proceed as in previous sections and take the projection of
\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})
onto the quotient

\frac{\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}{V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}).

Considering the projection of the Kato class in cohomology, we obtain a class

\kappa(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}})).

In this section, with a slight abuse of notation, we see the case just along half of the discs of the weight space, and still use the same notation:

\kappa(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+})).

Then, the Kato class is also a well defined class, and proceeding as in the previous section, we may refine it to obtain

\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+})).

Perrin-Riou’s regulator, with
f
now fixed as
E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)
, gives us a (one-variable) map

\operatorname{Col}_{b_{f}^{+}}=\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{PR}}_{\mathcal{F}^{+}}(-),b_{f}^{+}\right\rangle:H^{1}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p,\infty},\mathcal{F}^{+}D(f)^{*})\to\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}.

In the reciprocity law, the roles of the auxiliary
p
-adic
L
-functions
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi)
and
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
are switched now: their product is still zero, but now
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}},\chi,s)
is a priori non-vanishing (or at least, it does not vanish due to a
trivial
sign argument), while
L_{p}({\mathbf{f}})
is zero using the aforementioned results of
[
BD15
]
. Hence, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 6.3
.

Under Assumption
6.1
, if
L_{p}(f_{\beta},\chi,r/2+1)
, we have the following equality:

\operatorname{Col}_{b_{f}^{+}}(\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta}))\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(\operatorname{Ad}f_{\beta})=c({\mathbf{k}})\cdot L_{p}(f_{\beta},\chi,r/2+1)\cdot L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},{\mathbf{j}}_{+}1),

where
c({\mathbf{k}})
is a meromorphic function on
U
alone, which is identically zero or which is regular and non-vanishing at all integer weights
k\geqslant-1
except possibly at
r
itself, and regular at
k=r
.

Proof.

This follows by taking derivatives in the reciprocity law for Beilinson–Kato elements.
∎

When
\tau
is odd and
\tau(p)\neq 1
, we can directly assert that the Beilinson–Kato classes vanish.

Proposition 6.4
.

When the character
\tau
is odd and
\tau(p)\neq 1
, the space
H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+}))
is zero and the Beilinson–Kato
\kappa^{*}(f_{\beta})\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+}))
class vanishes.

Proof.

The Beilinson–Kato classes are universal norms, so it is a standard property that they are unramified outside
p
. If
\tau(p)\neq 1
, a standard computation shows that the dimension of the space is zero.
∎

This also means that either
L_{p}(f_{\beta},\chi,r/2+1)
or
L_{p}^{\prime}(f_{\beta},{\mathbf{j}}_{+}1)
must vanish. Further, since the projection of
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})
to the cohomology of the quotient

\frac{\frac{1}{X}V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}{V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\tau^{-1})(1+r)\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+})

vanishes, we may consider the lift

\kappa({\mathbf{f}})_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},V({\mathbf{f}})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+})).

Definition 6.5
.

We write
\kappa(f_{\beta})_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbf{Q},\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\psi^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}))
for the projection of
\kappa({\mathbf{f}})_{2}
onto the cohomology of the quotient

\frac{V({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}{V^{c}({\mathbf{f}})^{*}}\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}(\psi^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(-{\mathbf{j}}_{+}).

This definition does not only make sense in the case where
\tau
is odd, but also in the even cases where
\hat{\kappa}(f_{\beta})
vanishes (e.g. when
r
is odd and
\tau
is odd).

Remark 6.6
.

Observe that this object is analogous to the class
\kappa_{f,2}
introduced in
[
RR21
]
, where it was described borrowing the tools introduced by Fukaya and Kato
[
FK24
]
in the study of Sharifi’s conjectures
[
Sha11
]
.

Recall that in previous section the Kato class was compared with the circular unit encoded in
L_{p}(\tau,{\mathbf{j}}-r)
. When
r
is odd and
\psi
is even, it would be natural to expect a comparison with the circular unit
c(\psi)(-1)

7.
Extensions of this work: Beilinson–Flach classes and the triple product case

Along this note, we have shown how the comparison of Beilinson–Kato classes and circular units differs from the settings that appear in the previous work of Loeffler and the second author. We want to close the article with a short discussion of the differences between both methods, emphasizing the connections between both theories. For the main definitions and properties regarding Beilinson–Flach elements, we refer to
[
KLZ17
]
and
[
KLZ20
]
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}
be a Coleman family passing through a critical Eisenstein series and let
{\mathbf{g}}
be a (ordinary) Hida family. When studying the comparison between Beilinson–Flach classes and Beilinson–Kato classes in
[
LRS24
]
we had used the Hida–Rankin
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}^{g}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
, and not the one corresponding to the situation where the critical Eisenstein family is dominant, that we would denote by
L_{p}^{f}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
. In the framework explored in this article, the only option was precisely considering that situation, since we just had one family. Hence, it is natural to extend these ideas to the Hida–Rankin setting and ask the following question (which also admits an analogue in the setting of triple products).

Question 7.1
.

Is there any factorization formula for
L_{p}^{f}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
or its derivative along the weight-
k
direction, similar to
[
BD15
]
?

Observe that the answer to this question is straightforward if we work instead with
L_{p}^{g}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
: applying the Artin formalism and specializing
{\mathbf{f}}
at
f=E_{r+2}(\psi,\tau)
,

L_{p}^{g}(E_{r+2},{\mathbf{g}})(\ell,s)=L_{p}({\mathbf{g}},\psi)(\ell,s)\cdot L_{p}({\mathbf{g}},\tau)(\ell,s-r-1).

In the case of
L_{p}^{f}(E_{r+2},{\mathbf{g}})
, we expect to get some logarithmic contribution, as in
[
BD15
]
, together with a logaritmic term. The same question arises in the setting of triple products, where one would expect a similar situation for the triple product
p
-adic
L
-function of Andreatta–Iovita
[
AI21
]
when the dominant family passes through a critial Eisenstein point.

Moreover, in
[
LRS24
]
we had established that the projection of the Beilinson–Flach class to the quotient
\mathcal{F}^{-}V({{\mathbf{f}}})^{*}\otimes V_{{\mathbf{g}}}^{*}
vanishes. This is because the Bloch–Kato conjecture holds for that representation and the projection of the Beilinson–Flach element satisfies a local condition at
p
which is
too weak
.

Therefore, one may wonder if the specialization of the three-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
L_{p}^{f}({\mathbf{f}},{\mathbf{g}})
at
f_{\beta}
is zero. A priori, it is not correct to say that the
p
-adic
L
-function is the image under the Perrin-Riou map of a trivial projection of the Beilinson–Flach class, since in the reciprocity law we may have a zero in the term
d_{{\mathbf{f}}}({\mathbf{k}})
which arises in the leading term argument and one needs to be more cautious. Extending the techniques we have developed in this work, we are able to get a satisfactory answer of
7.1
in the second part of this paper, and also to understand better this issue.

Remark 7.2
.

This is similar to the kind of questions we had explored in
[
Riv23
]
, where we also suggested that this kind of
p
-adic
L
-function may encode relevant information regarding the Galois representations.

Another interesting question, that we believe could be related with this discussion, is the study of the situation where two or more families pass through a critical Eisenstein series. These questions are also considered in the sequel to this work.

A.1.
Overconvergent cohomology groups

Recall the

\mathsf{Alg}_{(\mathbf{Z}_{p},\mathbf{Z}_{p})}\rightarrow\mathsf{Sets},\quad(R,R^{+})\mapsto\Hom_{\mathsf{Groups}}^{\mathrm{cts}}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times},R^{\times}),

from the category of complete sheafy

Definition A.1
.

(1)

A

-algebra

\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-algebra

p

(2)

is a small
\mathbf{Z}_{p}

with respect to the
p

(3)

An

is a pair
(R_{V},\lambda_{V})
, where
R_{V}
is a reduced affinoid algebra (à la Tate) and
\lambda_{V}:\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow R_{V}^{\times}
is a continuous group homomorphism.

(4)

A
weight
is either a small weight or an affinoid weight.

(5)

An

is a weight such that the induced morphism of rigid analytic spaces

V\coloneq\mathrm{Spa}(R_{V},R_{V}^{\circ})^{\mathrm{rig}}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}

is an open immersion. Here,

is the ring of power-bounded elements in

and ‘

’ stands for ‘taking the rigid generic fiber’.

Remark A.2
.

(1)

Let
(R_{V},\lambda_{V})
be a weight.

•

If
(R_{V},\lambda_{V})
is a small weight, then
R_{V}^{\circ}=R_{V}
.

•

If
(R_{V},\lambda_{V})
is an affinoid weight, then

.

(2)

Given a weight
(R_{V},\lambda_{V})
,

admits a structure of uniform
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-algebra by letting

be its unit ball and equipping it with the corresponding spectral norm, denoted by

s_{V}\coloneq\min\left\{s\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}:|\lambda_{V}(1+p)|_{V}<p^{-\frac{1}{p^{s}(p-1)}}\right\}.

(3)

Any
k\in\mathbf{Z}
defines a small (resp., an affinoid) weight

\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\xrightarrow{a\mapsto a^{k}}\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\quad(\text{resp., }\mathbf{Z}_{p}^{\times}\xrightarrow{a\mapsto a^{k}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}).

In the later context, we shall switch between these two perspectives without mentioning.

Next, we need to introduce certain modules of analytic functions and distributions, which will be the coefficient system of the cohomology we are considering. We start by recalling some fundamental spaces in
p
-adic analysis: For any

\mathbf{Z}_{p}

\mathbf{Z}_{p}

where

i\end{pmatrix}.

To define the modules of analytic functions and distributions, consider the following

\mathrm{Iw}_{\GL_{2}}\coloneq\left\{\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}):p\mid c\right\}.

This is a Iwahori subgroup of

\mathrm{Iw}_{\GL_{2}}=N_{1}^{\mathrm{opp}}B(\mathbf{Z}_{p}),

Observe that

N_{1}^{\mathrm{opp}}\cong\mathbf{Z}_{p},\quad\begin{pmatrix}1&\\

\displaystyle\coloneq\left\{\phi:\mathrm{Iw}_{\GL_{2}}\xrightarrow{\text{continuous}}R_{V}:\begin{array}[]{l}\phi(\gamma\beta)=\lambda_{V}(\beta)\phi(\gamma)\text{ for all }(\gamma,\beta)\in\mathrm{Iw}_{\GL_{2}}\times B(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\\

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\kappa_{V}}^{s})

Suppose
h\in\mathbf{Q}_{\geq 0}
such that
(U(p),V,h)
is a slope datum (in the sense of
[
Han17
, Sect. 3.1]
). Then, by
[
Han17
, Proposition 3.1.5]
,
H_{c}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
and
H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
have slope-
\leq h
decomposition and are independent to
s
. Since (
5
) is Hecke equivariant, one deduces that both
H_{\mathrm{par}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
and
H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
also have slope-
\leq h
decomposition. For
?\in\{\emptyset,c,\mathrm{par},\mathrm{Eis}\}
, we define

\mathbb{T}_{?,V}^{\leq h}\coloneq\text{ $R_{V}$-algebra in $\mathrm{End}_{R_{V}}(H_{?}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h})$ generated by the Hecke algebra}.

Since
H_{?}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}
is a finite
R_{V}
-module,
\mathbb{T}_{?,V}^{\leq h}
is a finite algebra over
R_{V}
, hence an affinoid algebra (à la Tate).

The
cuspidal eigencurve

\mathcal{C}^{0}
is then constructing by gluing
\{\mathrm{Spa}(\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h},\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h,\circ})\}_{(V,h)}
over
\mathcal{W}
(see
[
Han17
, Sect. 4.3]
for details), running through the pair
(V,h)
such that
V
is an open affinoid weight and
(U(p),V,h)
is a slope datum. The natural map

\mathrm{wt}:\mathcal{C}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}

is the so-called
weight map
.

In what follows, we study the modules
H_{?}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}
locally around an étale point
x\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(K)
(for some finite extension
K
of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
), which is the main interest in the main body of the paper. More precisely, suppose
x\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(K)
such that
\mathrm{wt}
is étale at
x
. We write
\kappa=\mathrm{wt}(x)
. Let
V\subset\mathcal{W}
be an open affinoid containing
\kappa
and let
U
be the connected component of
\mathrm{wt}^{-1}(U)
containing
x
; and so
U
corresponds to an idempotent
e_{U}:\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}(\mathrm{wt}^{-1}(V))\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}(U)
. Since
\mathrm{wt}
is étale at
x
, after possibly shrinking
V
, we may assume

\mathrm{wt}:U\rightarrow V

is an isomorphism. Therefore, from the construction, we may then write

\mathscr{O}_{\mathcal{C}^{0}}(U)=e_{U}\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}

for some
h\in\mathbf{Q}_{\geq 0}
.

We construct the
R_{V}
-modules
e_{U}H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
and
e_{U}H^{1}_{c}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
as follows:

Construction of
e_{U}H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}
.

By definition, the map (
5
) yields a Hecke-equivariant inclusion

H_{\mathrm{par}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\hookrightarrow H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}.

Therefore, the restriction map gives the surjections

\mathbb{T}_{V}^{\leq h}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}\twoheadrightarrow e_{U}\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}.

We then define

e_{U}H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\coloneq H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\otimes_{\mathbb{T}_{V}^{\leq h}}e_{U}\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}.

Construction of
e_{U}H^{1}_{c}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}
.

By definition, the map (
5
) yields a Hecke-equivariant surjection

H^{1}_{c}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\twoheadrightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{par}}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}.

Therefore, it gives rise to surjections

\mathbb{T}_{c,V}^{\leq h}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}\twoheadrightarrow e_{U}\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}.

We then define

e_{U}H^{1}_{c}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\coloneq H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\otimes_{\mathbb{T}_{c,V}^{\leq h}}e_{U}\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}.

Proposition A.7
.

Keep the notations as above. The natural map

e_{U}H^{1}_{c}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\rightarrow e_{U}H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}

is an injection.

Proof.

By definition, the map (
5
) yields a Hecke-equivariant yields a Hecke-equivariant injection

H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})\hookrightarrow H_{c}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s}).

Therefore, the restriction map gives rise to surjections

\mathbb{T}_{c,V}^{\leq h}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{Eis},V}^{\leq h}.

That is, we may view
H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})
as a
\mathbb{T}_{c,V}^{\leq h}
-module. We then define

e_{U}H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\coloneq H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\otimes_{\mathbb{T}_{c,V}^{\leq h}}e_{U}\mathbb{T}_{\mathrm{par},V}^{\leq h}.

Consequently, we have a exact sequence

e_{U}H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\rightarrow e_{U}H_{c}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}\rightarrow e_{U}H^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}.

However, since

\frac{H_{c}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}}{H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}}\cong H^{1}_{\mathrm{par}}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h},

one concludes that
e_{U}H_{\mathrm{Eis}}^{1}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{\lambda_{V}}^{s})^{\leq h}=0
. The desired injection then follows.
∎

A.3.
Rank-2 big Galois representations

Let
f
be a finite-slope eigenform of level
\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma_{0}(p)
and weight
k+2
. We make the following assumption:

Assumption A.9
.

For
?\in\{\emptyset,c\}
, the Hecke-eigensystem defined by
f
appears in
H^{1}_{?}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{k}^{s})
and

\dim H^{i}_{?}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{k}^{s})_{(\mathbb{T}=f)}=\dim H^{i}_{?}(Y(\mathbf{C}),D_{k}^{s})[\mathbb{T}=f]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2,&\text{if }i=1\\

Here, given a

-vector space
M
with a Hecke action, we write
M[\mathbb{T}=f]
for the
f
-isotypic part and
M_{(\mathbb{T}=f)}
for the generalized
f
-eigenspace.

Remark A.10
.

Since we are interested in critical-slope étale points in the main body of the paper, by
[

[

Proposition A.11
.

Keep the notations as above. Assume
f
defines a point

(for some finite extension
K
over
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
) such that
\mathrm{wt}
is étale at

containing

and two Galois representations

over

(2)

Since
\mathrm{wt}
is étale at

by

and let

Since

, we define

Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya Interaction and Dipole-Exchange Curvature Effects on the Spin-Wave Spectra of Magnetic Nanotubes
B. Mimica-Figari, P. Landeros, R. A. Gallardo

Abstract
This work explores spin-wave dynamics in magnetic nanotubes, focusing on the influence of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and curvature. The study uses analytical methods to examine how these factors influence the emergence of nonreciprocity and azimuthal standing waves in nanotubes with longitudinal magnetization along the axis or with a vortex-like magnetization. The interplay between exchange, Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya, and dipolar couplings in determining the chirality of spin waves is discussed. When the magnetization is saturated along an axis, the spin waves propagating along it are symmetric under the inversion of the wave vector. However, magnetochirality, mainly driven by exchange and Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya couplings, is observed in the azimuthal standing modes. In the vortex state, frequency nonreciprocity occurs for waves propagating along the tube, while the azimuthal modes remain reciprocal. For positive Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction, and depending on the helicity of the vortex, the asymmetry induced by the dipolar interaction is reinforced, whereas a negative coupling opposes this asymmetry. The influence of radial anisotropy is also examined. It is found that radial anisotropy reduces the frequency of the modes and shifts the dispersion minimum to a finite wave vector in the vortex state. The properties of modes near zero frequency offer insight into the emergence of chiral magnetic textures.



I
Introduction

Spin-wave (SW) nonreciprocity is a phenomenon in which the propagation characteristics of two counterpropagating waves differ
Barman et al. 2021
;
Flebus et al. 2024
. These waves may exhibit amplitude, phase, attenuation length, or frequency variations, even when their wave vectors have the same magnitude
Jamali et al. 2013
;
Albisetti et al. 2020
;
Pirro et al. 2021
.
In thick films, an asymmetry in the SW amplitude occurs for the Damon-Eshbach modes
Damon and Eshbach 1961
, where waves propagating in opposite directions are localized on the top or bottom surface of the film. This effect is given mainly in the small wave vector range, where the dipolar interaction dominates. Such nonreciprocity in amplitude results from the asymmetric distribution of the internal dipolar fields throughout the thickness of the film, which causes the magnetization dynamics to vary between the top and bottom surfaces
Gladii et al. 2016
;
Solano et al. 2022
;
Szulc et al. 2024
.
The dipolar asymmetry is relevant for films thicker than a few exchange lengths, which is the subject of three-dimensional nanomagnetism
Gubbiotti et al. 2019
;
Gubbiotti et al. 2025
.

Nonreciprocity in frequency driven by the dipole-dipole interaction has been observed in various nanostructures, including magnetization-graded films
Gallardo et al. 2019a
, synthetic antiferromagnets
Grünberg 1985
;
Di et al. 2015a
;
Gallardo et al. 2019b
;
Franco and Landeros 2020
;
Grassi et al. 2020
;
Gladii et al. 2023
;
Gallardo et al. 2024a
;
Girardi et al. 2024
, and magnetic nanotubes
Otálora et al. 2016
;
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
;
Sheka et al. 2020
;
Gallardo et al. 2022a
;
Körber et al. 2022
. It has also been observed in films with asymmetric surface anisotropies
Gladii et al. 2016
;
Solano et al. 2022
;
Szulc et al. 2024
and antisymmetric exchange known as the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI)
Dzyaloshinsky 1958
;
Moriya 1960
;
Fert and Levy 1980
. The DMI influence on the SW spectra has been theoretically predicted
Melcher 1973
;
Costa et al. 2010
;
Cortés-Ortuño and Landeros 2013
;
Moon et al. 2013
and experimentally observed in ultrathin films
Di et al. 2015b
;
Cho et al. 2015
;
Stashkevich et al. 2015
;
Tacchi et al. 2017
;
Gallardo et al. 2019c
;
Kuepferling et al. 2023
and noncentrosymmetric materials
Iguchi et al. 2015
;
Seki et al. 2016
. Unlike the parallel spin alignment favored by ferromagnetic exchange interactions, DMI promotes a noncollinear alignment of neighboring spins. This property induces the asymmetry in the SW dispersion but also leads to the formation of chiral spin textures
Rohart and Thiaville 2013
;
Franken et al. 2014
. Consequently, the DMI plays a crucial role in engineering and controlling spin waves in magnonic logic devices, paving the way for applications in nanoscale information processing
Lee et al. 2016
;
Chumak et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2024
.

Three-dimensional nanomagnetism
Gubbiotti et al. 2019
;
Gubbiotti et al. 2025
deals with complex systems and planar and curvilinear shells
Sheka 2021
;
Makarov and Sheka Eds.
with a thickness larger than some tens of nanometers. In the case of ferromagnetic nanotubes (NTs)
Staño and Fruchart 2018
;
Landeros et al. 2022
, the interplay between dipolar and exchange couplings can lead to distinctive magnetic textures
Landeros et al. 2009
;
Rüffer et al. 2012
;
Mehlin et al. 2018
;
Vasyukov et al. 2018
;
Yang et al. 2018
;
Landeros et al. 2022
and domain walls
Landeros and Núñez 2010
;
Yan et al. 2012
;
Otálora et al. 2013
;
Hertel 2016
.
For tubes hosting a vortex magnetization, spin-wave nonreciprocity is influenced by the curvature, where geometric charges induce a chiral dipolar interaction
Sheka et al. 2020
. This direction-dependent dipole-dipole coupling can lead to different frequencies
Otálora et al. 2016
or attenuation lengths for spin waves traveling in opposite directions
Otálora et al. 2018
.
Like in the synthetic antiferromagnets
Gallardo et al. 2019b
, the SW asymmetry induced by dipolar interaction in nanotubes depends on the direction of the equilibrium magnetization and the SW propagation. Namely, nonreciprocity is observed in cases where the waves propagate perpendicular to the equilibrium magnetization, as can be determined by calculating the texture-dependent toroidal moment
Körber et al. 2022
;
Brevis et al. 2024a
. In the tubular case, this is optimally reached when the magnetization describes a vortex state while the SWs propagate along the axis
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
.
Since the cooperative dipolar coupling is amplified in thicker shells, the spin-wave asymmetry enhances as the nanotube thickness increases
Gallardo et al. 2022a
;
Körber et al. 2022
. Because curvature is the primary source of the nonreciprocal dipolar interaction, a reduction in curvature leads to a corresponding decrease in spin-wave asymmetry
Brevis et al. 2024b
. An approach to achieving SW asymmetries from interlayer dynamic coupling and curvature involves connecting two nanotubes in an antiparallel configuration, referred to as cylindrical synthetic antiferromagnets
Gallardo et al. 2022b
. In this arrangement, which combines the features of curvilinear shells and bilayers, nonreciprocity persists even if the curvature of the nanotubes diminishes.

During the curling transition from a fluxless vortex to an axially saturated magnetic state
Gutierrez-Guzman et al. 2017
, it has been shown that the exchange interaction also induces chiral effects in the SW bandstructure
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
. Here, the application of magnetic fields allows for tuning the magnon nonreciprocity, which can shift between dipolar- and exchange-dominated regimes based on the equilibrium state of magnetization
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
. In the curling state

, where the magnetization is oriented at an oblique angle

Experimental evidence of these predictions has been obtained in a nanotube with a hexagonal cross-section

In the axial magnetic state, nodal lines were found to be mainly straight along the circumference. In contrast, for curling and circumferential magnetization orientations, the nodal lines exhibited distortions, which were attributed to the magnetochiral effects. This behavior reveals an unconventional nature of confined modes, akin to those observed in planar magnets under the influence of the DMI
Zingsem et al. 2019
;
Gallardo et al. 2019d
;
Flores-Farías et al. 2022
;
Gallardo et al. 2024b

Beyond the symmetric exchange, understanding the role of additional spin coupling in tubular systems is of high interest, particularly the interfacial DMI, which can be introduced by coating a nanotube with a high spin-orbit coupling heavy-metal (HM) layer
Kuepferling et al. 2023
;
Gallardo et al. 2019c
. This interaction promotes the formation of chiral magnetic textures
Avci et al. 2019
;
Emori et al. 2013
and drives spin-wave nonreciprocity with significant implications for spintronic applications
Barman et al. 2021
;
Flebus et al. 2024
. Despite its fundamental importance, the influence of DMI on spin-wave dynamics in tubular nanostructures remains unexplored from an analytical point of view. This gap is likely due to the challenges associated with incorporating dynamic chiral terms into the unique tubular geometry. Only numerical addressing has been realized, where it is observed that both the curvature-induced magnetic charges and the bulk DMI may contribute to a dispersion asymmetry
Körber and Kákay 2021
.

This paper analytically investigates spin-wave dynamics in magnetic nanotubes with an interfacial Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction. It examines how the DMI influences nonreciprocal spin-wave modes and azimuthal standing waves for different magnetization configurations (longitudinal and vortex-like). Using a theoretical model that incorporates Zeeman, exchange, dipolar interactions, radial anisotropy, and DMI, the study emanates a dispersion relation that, at a small wave vector limit, allows deriving an analytical expression for the frequency shift that quantifies the spin-wave asymmetry. Additionally, the influence of radial anisotropy is explored, where insights into the formation of chiral magnetic textures under specific conditions are discussed.

A theoretical model is developed to describe the spin-wave dynamics in a nanotube with curling equilibrium magnetization, considering the contributions of Zeeman, exchange interaction, dipolar interaction, radial anisotropy, and the interfacial DMI. While the complete transition from a vortex to a longitudinal magnetized configuration can be theoretically explored, the results of this work will only focus on the limiting cases
\theta=0,\pi/2
. Nevertheless, the expressions for the static and dynamic effective fields and the equilibrium condition are written in a general way for completeness.
The temporal evolution of the magnetization is given by the Landau-Lifshitz (LL) equation
Gurevich and Melkov 1996
,

\frac{d\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r},t)}{dt}=-\mu_{0}\gamma\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r},t)\times\mathbf{H}^{\rm e}(\mathbf{r},t).

(1)

where
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r},t)
is the magnetization,
\mathbf{H}^{\rm e}(\mathbf{r},t)
the effective magnetic field, and
\gamma
the gyromagnetic ratio. Considering the wave propagation along the tube axis
z
and assuming small magnetization deviation, the magnetization and the effective field are, respectively, given by
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{M}_{0}+\mathbf{m}e^{i(kz+l\varphi-\omega t)}
and
\mathbf{H}^{\rm e}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{H}^{\rm e0}+\mathbf{h}^{\rm e}e^{i(kz+l\varphi-\omega t)}
, where
|\mathbf{M}_{0}|\gg|\mathbf{m}|
and
|\mathbf{H}^{\rm e0}|\gg|\mathbf{h}^{\rm e}|
, with
\mathbf{M}_{0}
being the equilibrium magnetization and
\mathbf{H}^{\rm e0}
the static effective field.
Here,
\omega=2\pi f
is the angular frequency,
l
corresponds to an integer number that describes the azimuthal standing wave modes, and
k
is the axial component of the wave vector.
The equilibrium magnetization is considered to be in the direction
\hat{z}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf{\varphi}}+\cos\theta\hat{z}
. Thus, for
\theta=0
, the magnetization is saturated along the tube axis
z
, and for
\theta=\pi/2
, the magnetization presents a curling or vortex-like equilibrium state, as shown in Fig.
1
. The chirality parameter
\mathcal{C}
has been added to represent the two possible curling orientations, namely
\mathcal{C}=1
for a right-handed and
\mathcal{C}=-1
for a left-handed vortex. The effective field contains all the interactions of the system.
The magnetic oscillations are described through a local reference system defined by the plane orthogonal to the equilibrium magnetization. Such a coordinate system is constructed by considering that the equilibrium magnetization is orthogonal to the radial direction, i.e.,
\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}\cdot\hat{z}^{\prime}=0
. Then, the right-handed orthonormal set is given by
(\hat{\mathbf{\chi}},\hat{\mathbf{\rho}},\hat{z}^{\prime})
, where
\hat{\chi}=\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}\times\hat{z}^{\prime}=-\cos\theta\hat{\mathbf{\phi}}+\mathcal{C}\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf{z}}
. In terms of the magnetization components, the first-order Landau-Lifshitz equation becomes

\displaystyle\omega m_{\chi}=i\gamma\mu_{0}(-H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}m_{\rho}+M_{\text{s}}h^{\rm e}_{\rho})

(2a)

\displaystyle\omega m_{\rho}=i\gamma\mu_{0}(H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}m_{\chi}-M_{\text{s}}h^{\rm e}_{\chi}),

(2b)

where
M_{\rm s}
is the saturation magnetization. The dynamic effective field
\mathbf{h}^{\rm e}
can be written as a linear combination of the dynamic magnetization,
h^{\rm e}_{i}=-\Lambda_{ij}m_{j}

Gallardo et al. 2022a
, where
\Lambda_{ij}
is the magnetic tensor describing the self-interactions
Körber and Kákay 2021
.
Then, Eqs. (
2
) result in

\omega\begin{pmatrix}m_{\chi}\\
m_{\rho}\end{pmatrix}=i\gamma\mu_{0}M_{\text{s}}\tilde{\mathbf{T}}\begin{pmatrix}m_{\chi}\\
m_{\rho}\end{pmatrix},

(3)

with the dynamic matrix

\tilde{\mathbf{T}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\Lambda_{\rho\chi}&-\Lambda_{\rho\rho}-\frac{H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}}{M_{\text{s}}}\\
\Lambda_{\chi\chi}+\frac{H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}}{M_{\text{s}}}&\Lambda_{\chi\rho}\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

By solving this eigenvalue problem, the following dispersion relation is obtained

f=\frac{\gamma\mu_{0}M_{\text{s}}}{2\pi}\left[i\Lambda_{\chi\rho}+\sqrt{\left(\frac{H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}}{M_{\text{s}}}+\Lambda_{\rho\rho}\right)\left(\frac{H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}}{M_{\text{s}}}+\Lambda_{\chi\chi}\right)}\right],

(5)

where, as shown later, it has been considered that
\Lambda_{\rho\chi}=-\Lambda_{\chi\rho}
. Eq. (
5
) maintains the structure derived in Refs.
Cortés-Ortuño and Landeros 2013
;
Otálora et al. 2016
;
Gallardo et al. 2022a
;
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
, where the elements
\Lambda_{\chi\rho}
and
\Lambda_{\rho\chi}
contain the magnetochiral properties, since they depend on the sign of the wave vector
\mathbf{k}
and the mode index
l
. Nevertheless, it will be shown that such terms also contain the interfacial DMI so that the SW dispersion and azimuthal modes can be nonreciprocal because of exchange, DMI, and dipole-dipole interaction. According to Eq. (
5
), the
z^{\prime}
component of the static effective field (
H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}
) and the linear magnetic tensor elements
\Lambda_{ij}
are required to describe the SW dynamics of the tubular system. In the following section, these quantities are derived.

II.1
Magnetic effective fields and tensors

The exchange interaction contribution to the effective field is given by
\mathbf{H}^{\text{ex}}=l_{\text{ex}}^{2}\mathbf{\nabla}^{2}\mathbf{M}
, which results in
\mathbf{H}^{\text{ex}0}=\mathcal{C}H_{\text{ex}}\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf{\varphi}}
, and an exchange tensor

\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\text{ex}}=\frac{H_{\text{ex}}}{M_{\text{s}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos^{2}\theta+l^{2}+k^{2}R^{2}\beta&i\Delta_{\text{ex}}\\
-i\Delta_{\text{ex}}&1+l^{2}+k^{2}R^{2}\beta\\
\end{array}\right).

Here,
H_{\text{ex}}=M_{\text{s}}l_{\text{ex}}^{2}/\beta R^{2}
and
\Delta_{\text{ex}}=2l\cos\theta
, with
\beta=a/R
, being
R
and
a
the outer and inner radii, respectively (see Fig.
1
).
In this calculation, as in the following ones, an ultrathin nanotube is considered so that some integrals are averaged along the tube thickness.
For the effective fields, this is

\bar{\mathbf{H}}=\frac{1}{R(1-\beta)}\int_{\beta R}^{R}\mathbf{H}d\rho.

(6)

The DM field is derived in Appendix
A
, where its static part is
\mathbf{H}^{\text{dm}0}=-H_{\text{dm}}\mathcal{C}\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf{\varphi}}
, and the DMI tensor is

\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\text{dm}}=\frac{H_{\text{dm}}}{M_{\text{s}}}\begin{pmatrix}\cos^{2}\theta&i\Delta_{\text{dm}}\\
-i\Delta_{\text{dm}}&1\end{pmatrix},

(7)

where
H_{\text{dm}}=2D\ln(1/\beta)/[\mu_{0}M_{\text{s}}R(1-\beta)
],
D
is the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya strength, and

\Delta_{\text{dm}}=l\cos\theta-k\mathcal{C}\sin\theta\frac{R(1-\beta)}{\ln(1/\beta)}.

(8)

In flat structures, it is well known that the FM/HM interfaces exhibit a significant perpendicular anisotropy
Kim et al. 2017
;
Samardak et al. 2020
;
Tacchi et al. 2023
. Hence, in the current case, a radial uniaxial anisotropy field is considered, given by
\mathbf{H}^{\rm an}=H_{\rm an}(\mathbf{M}\cdot\hat{\rho})\hat{\rho}
, where
{H}^{\rm an}=2K_{\rho}/\mu_{0}M_{\rm s}
with
K_{\rho}
is the radial anisotropy constant. Therefore, the associated tensor is

\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\rm an}=-\frac{{H}^{\rm an}}{M_{\rm s}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(9)

For the dipolar contribution, it is noted that
\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{M}_{0}=0
and
\mathbf{M}_{0}\cdot\hat{\rho}=0
on the surface of the cylindrical mantle. The surfaces at the tube ends do not contribute
Landeros et al. 2009
;
Mehlin et al. 2018
;
Vasyukov et al. 2018
because an extended NT along
z
is assumed. Then, the static dipolar effective field is
\mathbf{H}^{\text{dip}0}=0
. On the other hand, the elements of the dipolar tensor
\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\rm dip}
are given by (see details in Appendix
B
),

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\chi\chi}

\displaystyle=(l\cos\theta-kR_{\text{m}}\mathcal{C}\sin\theta)^{2}\mathcal{I}_{0}

(10)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\chi\rho}

\displaystyle=-(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\rho\chi}=-i\Delta_{\rm dip}

(11)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\rho\rho}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}_{2},

(12)

with

\Delta_{\rm dip}=(l\cos\theta-kR_{\text{m}}\mathcal{C}\sin\theta)\mathcal{I}_{1}.

(13)

Here, an average radius
R_{\text{m}}=R(1+\beta)/2
has been defined, which comes from the midpoint approach for integration along the radial direction. The functions
\mathcal{I}_{0,1,2}
are integrals (in
\varphi
) that contain the modified Bessel function of the second kind
Gallardo et al. 2022a
. These integrals depend on the absolute value of
l
and
k
and can only be done numerically for all wave-vector ranges.

For the magnetic configuration
\mathbf{M}_{0}
to remain stable, the resulting static effective field must align with the equilibrium magnetization. Under this condition, it is required that
\mathbf{H}^{\rm e0}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{\chi}}=0
, which results in the equilibrium condition

\mathcal{C}(H_{\text{ex}}+H_{\text{dm}})\cos\theta\sin\theta+H_{\rm ze}\sin(\mathcal{C}\theta-\theta_{\rm ze})=0,

(14)

where
\theta_{\rm ze}
is the angle that the external field makes with respect to the
z
axis, and
H_{\rm ze}
is the magnitude of the external field, which is given by
\mathbf{H}^{\rm ze}=H_{\rm ze}[\sin(\mathcal{C}\theta-\theta_{\rm ze})\hat{\mathbf{\chi}}+\cos(\mathcal{C}\theta-\theta_{\rm ze})\hat{z}^{\prime}]
.
Note that the external field contains the azimuthal and axial directions in order to favor the condition
\mathbf{M}_{\rm 0}\cdot\hat{\rho}=0
, i.e., the equilibrium magnetization remains in the local
z
-
\varphi
plane.

II.2
Dispersion relation and frequency nonreciprocity

With the dispersion relation shown in Eq. (
5
) in terms of the effective field and magnetic tensor elements specified in the previous section, the SW frequency becomes

f=\frac{\gamma\mu_{0}}{2\pi}\left[(M_{\text{s}}\Delta_{\rm{dip}}-H_{\text{ex}}\Delta_{\rm{ex}}-H_{\text{dm}}\Delta_{\rm{dm}})+\sqrt{\left(H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}+M_{\rm s}\Lambda_{\rho\rho}\right)\left(H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}+M_{\rm s}\Lambda_{\chi\chi}\right)}\right],

(15)

where

H^{\rm e0}_{z^{\prime}}=H_{\rm ze}\cos(\theta-\theta_{\rm ze})-(H_{\rm{ex}}+H_{\rm{dm}})\sin^{2}\theta,

(16)

and the diagonal tensor elements
\Lambda_{\rho\rho}
and
\Lambda_{\chi\chi}
can be obtained from the contributing tensors in section
II.1
.
In this case, the only terms that change sign under wave vector inversion are
\Lambda_{\chi\rho}
and
\Lambda_{\rho\chi}
, which satisfy the condition
\Lambda_{\rho\chi}=-\Lambda_{\chi\rho}
.
The frequency difference under wave vector inversion is
\Delta f=f(\mathbf{k})-f(-\mathbf{k})=f(k,l)-f(-k,-l)
, and then the frequency shift is given by:

\Delta f=\frac{\gamma\mu_{0}}{\pi}(M_{\text{s}}\Delta_{\rm{dip}}-H_{\text{ex}}\Delta_{\rm{ex}}-H_{\text{dm}}\Delta_{\rm{dm}}),

(17)

which can be separated into terms proportional to the axial (
k
) and azimuthal (
l/R
) wave vectors,
\Delta f=\Delta f_{k}+\Delta f_{l}
, where

\Delta f_{k}=\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{\pi}kM_{\text{s}}\mathcal{C}\sin\theta\left(\frac{2D}{\mu_{0}M^{2}_{\text{s}}}-R_{\text{m}}\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}\right)

(18)

and

\Delta f_{l}=\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{\pi}\frac{l}{R}M_{\text{s}}\cos\theta\left(R\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}-\frac{2D}{\mu_{0}M^{2}_{\text{s}}}\frac{\ln(1/\beta)}{(1-\beta)}-\frac{2l_{\text{ex}}^{2}}{\beta R}\right).

(19)

In Eq. (
18
), it is noted that the dipolar and DM interactions induce asymmetry in the SW bandstructure for waves propagating along the tube axis. For zero DMI, the frequency shift is generated only by the dipole-dipole interaction, which is in concordance with previous results.
Otálora et al. 2016
;
Sheka et al. 2020
;
Gallardo et al. 2022a
;
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
;
Körber et al. 2022
.
It is important to note that the function
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}
depends on the absolute value of the mode index
l
, making the frequency shift
\Delta f_{k}
different for
l=0
and
l\neq 0
.
On the other hand, the frequency shift
\Delta f_{l}
[see Eq. (
19
)] evidences the chiral features of the azimuthal standing waves.
One can see that dipolar, exchange, and DMI contribute to azimuthal chiral effects. Namely, the circumferential standing waves will differ for
+l
and
-l
as long as
\theta\neq\pi/2
. The role of exchange and dipolar interactions for
\theta\neq\pi/2
has previously been studied
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
.
However, the role of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction has not been considered until now.

From Eq. (
19
), it can be concluded that the exchange contribution affects the azimuthal asymmetry in the same way as the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction. Indeed, a simple analysis allows obtaining an effective parameter

D_{\rm ex}=\frac{2A_{\rm ex}}{\beta R}\frac{1-\beta}{\ln{1/\beta}}.

(20)

Thus, for the azimuthal frequency shift, the exchange and DM interactions can be combined into an effective constant
D_{\rm eff}=D+D_{\rm ex}
. In the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
, it is clear that
D_{\rm ex}=0
because the magnetochirality induced by exchange requires a finite curvature
Sheka et al. 2020
;
Salazar-Cardona et al. 2021
.
On the other hand, in the same limit
R\rightarrow\infty
,
\ln(1/\beta)/(1-\beta)\rightarrow 1
,
1/R^{2}\rightarrow 0
and
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}\rightarrow 0
. Hence, using the relation
k_{\varphi}=l/R
, the frequency shift becomes
\Delta f_{l}=(2\gamma/\pi M_{\rm s})Dk_{\varphi}
, which, at
\theta=0
, corresponds to the frequency shift obtained for DE waves propagating in a planar FM/HM structure
Melcher 1973
;
Costa et al. 2010
;
Cortés-Ortuño and Landeros 2013
;
Moon et al. 2013
;
Di et al. 2015b
;
Cho et al. 2015
;
Stashkevich et al. 2015
;
Tacchi et al. 2017
;
Gallardo et al. 2019c
;
Kuepferling et al. 2023
.
In the case
\theta=\pi/2
, the contribution of the DMI in the nonreciprocity is given by
\Delta f_{k}=\mathcal{C}\frac{2\gamma}{\pi M_{\text{s}}}Dk
, which also coincides with that for DE modes in planar systems.
It is worth noting that, in the general case, the dipolar terms
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}
depend on the magnitude of
k
and are the only contribution to the frequency nonreciprocity that is nonlinear with
k
.

II.3
Small wave-vector approximation

Analytical expressions for the integrals
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{i}
can be derived at the small wave-vector limit. Such expressions are obtained up to zeroth order in Appendix
B
. By focusing on the frequency asymmetry, the frequency shift at
k\rightarrow 0
becomes

\Delta f_{k}^{k\rightarrow 0}\approx\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{\pi}kM_{\text{s}}\mathcal{C}\sin\theta\left(\frac{2D}{\mu_{0}M^{2}_{\text{s}}}+\frac{R-a}{2}\delta_{l,0}\right).

(21)

Note that for
l=0
, the DM and dipolar couplings induce frequency nonreciprocity.
Nonetheless, for
l\neq 0
, the frequency asymmetry is due to only DMI because, in this case,
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}(k\rightarrow 0)=0
. Also, for
D=0
, Eq. (
21
) coincides with Eq. (18) of Ref.
Gallardo et al. 2022a
when the equilibrium magnetization is in a circumferential vortex state (
\theta=\pi/2
).

On the other hand, the small-
k
approximation for the frequency asymmetry associated with the azimuthal standing waves is

\Delta f_{l}^{k\rightarrow 0}\approx-\frac{\mu_{0}\gamma}{\pi}\frac{l}{R}M_{\text{s}}\cos\theta\left(\frac{2D\ln(1/\beta)}{\mu_{0}M^{2}_{\text{s}}(1-\beta)}+\frac{2l_{\text{ex}}^{2}}{\beta R}\right).

(22)

Hence, in the limit
k\rightarrow 0
, only the exchange interaction and DMI induce magnetochirality if
l\neq 0
. In other words, Eq. (
22
) suggests that the asymmetry of azimuthal standing waves is predominantly due to the exchange interaction and DMI, and the dipolar interaction plays a minor role. Nonetheless, it is worth noticing that only the dipolar interaction can induce nonlinear effects in the frequency shift, as evidenced in the inset of Fig.
2
(a). This effect is explained by the dependence of the integral
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}
on
k
, which is lost in the zeroth order approximation considered in Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
) and higher order terms are needed.
Note that these analytical expressions [Eqs. (
21
) and (
22
)] account for asymmetry in both
k
and
l
, providing insights into the role of curvature and the various interactions in SW dynamics.

IV
Summary

The theoretical study provides a detailed analysis of spin-wave dynamics in nanotubes, emphasizing the crucial roles of Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction and curvature. It is shown that nonreciprocity in spin-wave propagation is a key characteristic of these systems, arising from a combination of exchange, DMI, and dipolar interactions. In the vortex-like configuration, a frequency nonreciprocity is observed wherein, for a positive DM constant, the DMI reinforces the asymmetry induced by the dipolar interaction, while in the contrary case, the asymmetry caused by the DMI is opposed to the dipolar one. In a longitudinal magnetization state, the exchange and DM interactions predominantly lead to chiral effects in the SW bandstructure for azimuthal standing waves. In contrast, propagation along the tube axis is reciprocal in frequency. Under the inclusion of a radial anisotropy, it is found that it reduces the frequency of the modes and shifts the dispersion minimum to a finite wave vector for the vortex state. For the saturated state, the frequency minima occurs at zero wave vector. The properties of the spin-wave modes are close to zero frequency, which allows for the prediction of the emergence of chiral magnetic textures. The insights from this work can be applied to the development of three-dimensional chiral magnetic nanostructures, offering new possibilities for magnonics and nanoscale information processing.

Appendix A
Effective Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya field

The effective Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya field must be carefully calculated to incorporate curvature effects. In cylindrical geometry, the interfacial DM energy term is expressed as
Mancilla-Almonacid et al. 2020
,

E_{\text{dm}}(\mathbf{M})=\frac{D}{M_{\text{s}}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}\cdot[(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{M})-(\mathbf{M}\cdot\mathbf{\nabla})\mathbf{M}]dV.

(23)

Due to the non-commutativity between the unit vector
\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}
and the operator
\mathbf{\nabla}
, curvature-induced terms naturally emerge in the effective DMI field.
This field can be obtained by calculating the functional derivative of the energy with respect to the magnetization. This is

\displaystyle\delta E_{\rm dm}(\mathbf{M},\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{E_{\rm dm}(\mathbf{M}+\epsilon\mathbf{v})-E_{\rm dm}(\mathbf{M})}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\delta\epsilon_{\rm dm}}{\delta\mathbf{M}}\cdot\mathbf{v}dV

(24)

for an arbitrary vector
\mathbf{v}
. Then, the effective DM field is

\mathbf{H}^{\text{dm}}=-\frac{1}{\mu_{0}}\frac{\delta\epsilon_{\text{dm}}}{\delta\mathbf{M}},

(25)

with
\epsilon_{\rm{dm}}
being the DM energy density (
\epsilon_{\rm dm}=\partial E_{\rm{dm}}/\partial V
). After some algebraic manipulations, the following expression for the effective field is obtained:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\text{dm}}=-\frac{2D}{\mu_{0}M^{2}_{\text{s}}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}(\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{M})-\mathbf{\nabla}(\hat{\mathbf{\rho}}\cdot\mathbf{M})+\frac{M_{\varphi}\hat{\mathbf{\varphi}}}{\rho}\right).

(26)

Note that a thin nanotube is considered in this work. Therefore, the expression for a surface contribution in Eq. (
24
) has been omitted. Finally, by considering the dynamic and static magnetization components, it is easy to obtain
\mathbf{H}^{\text{dm}0}=-H_{\text{dm}}\sin\theta\hat{\mathbf{\varphi}}
and
\mathbf{h}^{\text{dm}}=-\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\text{dm}}\mathbf{m}
, where matrix
\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\text{dm}}
is given in Eq. (
7
).

Appendix B
Effective dipolar field

The dipolar field is derived in a manner similar to that described by Alvarado et al.
Alvarado-Seguel and Gallardo 2019
. The dipolar effective field can be obtained as
\mathbf{H}^{\text{dip}}=-\mathbf{\nabla}\Phi(\mathbf{r},t)
, where the magnetostatic potential
\Phi
is given by

\Phi(\mathbf{r},t)=-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\mathbf{\nabla}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)}{4\pi|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}dV^{\prime}+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\prime}\cdot\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime},t)}{4\pi|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}|}dS^{\prime}.

(27)

By considering the magnetization described in section
II
, the volumetric and surface magnetic charges are

\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{M}=\left[\frac{m_{\rho}}{\rho}+i\left(k\sin\theta-\frac{l\cos\theta}{\rho}\right)m_{\chi}\right]e^{i(kz+l\phi-\omega t)}

(28)

and

\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{M}=\pm m_{\rho}e^{i(kz+l\phi-\omega t)},

(29)

respectively. Therefore, the tensor elements of
\tilde{\mathbf{\Lambda}}_{\rm dip}
are

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{\rm dip})_{\chi\chi}

\displaystyle=\iiint(l\cos\theta-k\rho\sin\theta)(l\cos\theta-k\rho^{\prime}\sin\theta)\frac{1}{\rho}G_{0}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})d\rho d\rho^{\prime}d\varphi^{\prime}

(30a)

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{\rm dip})_{\chi\rho}

\displaystyle=-i\iiint\left(l\cos\theta-k\rho\sin\theta\right)\frac{\rho^{\prime}}{\rho}G_{1}(\rho^{\prime},\rho,\varphi^{\prime})d\rho d\rho^{\prime}d\varphi^{\prime}

(30b)

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{\rm dip})_{\rho\chi}

\displaystyle=i\iiint(l\cos\theta-k\rho^{\prime}\sin\theta)G_{1}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})d\rho d\rho^{\prime}d\varphi^{\prime}

(30c)

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{\rm dip})_{\rho\rho}

\displaystyle=\iiint\rho^{\prime}G_{2}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})d\rho d\rho^{\prime}d\varphi^{\prime}

(30d)

where,

\displaystyle G_{0}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{il\varphi^{\prime}}}{2\pi R(1-\beta)}K_{0}\left(|k|\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\rho^{\prime 2}-2\rho\rho^{\prime}\cos\varphi^{\prime}}\right)

(31a)

\displaystyle G_{1}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\rho}G_{0}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

(31b)

\displaystyle G_{2}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\rho\rho^{\prime}}G_{0}(\rho,\rho^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}).

(31c)

Here,
K_{0}
is the zeroth modified Bessel function of the second kind. The integrals given in Eq. (
30
) must be solved numerically. Notice that, unlike exchange and DM dynamic tensors, the dipolar interaction has the condition
(\Lambda_{\rm dip})_{\rho\chi}\neq-(\Lambda_{\rm dip})_{\chi\rho}
. Nevertheless, because a very thin nanotube is considered, the integrals in
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
can be approximated as
\int_{0}^{d}F(x)dx\approx dF(d/2)
. Thus,

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\chi\chi}

\displaystyle=(l\cos\theta-kR_{\text{m}}\sin\theta)^{2}\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{0}

(32a)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\chi\rho}

\displaystyle=-i(l\cos\theta-kR_{\text{m}}\sin\theta)\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}

(32b)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\rho\rho}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{2},

(32c)

and
(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\rho\chi}=-(\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rm dip})_{\chi\rho}
.
Here,
R_{\text{m}}=R(1+\beta)/2
is the midpoint between the internal and external radius of the tube. The integrals
\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{0,1,2}
become

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{0}

\displaystyle=R^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\int\frac{1}{R_{\text{m}}}G_{0}(R_{\text{m}},R_{\text{m}},\varphi^{\prime})d\varphi^{\prime}

(33a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{1}

\displaystyle=R^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\int G_{1}(R_{\text{m}},R_{\text{m}},\varphi^{\prime})d\varphi^{\prime}

(33b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(k,l)}_{2}

\displaystyle=R^{2}(1-\beta)^{2}\int R_{\text{m}}G_{2}(R_{\text{m}},R_{\text{m}},\varphi^{\prime})d\varphi^{\prime}.

(33c)

These expressions require numerical integration on the azimuthal angle
\varphi^{\prime}
. Nevertheless, these integrals can be analytically calculated in the case of small wave vectors. The series expansion of the Bessel function is used
K_{0}(x)=-\gamma-\ln(x/2)
, where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Such a small
k
approach allows for an analytical solution of the
\varphi^{\prime}
coordinate integration using the residue theorem. An integral of the form,

I=\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\sin\varphi^{\prime},\cos\varphi^{\prime})d\varphi^{\prime}

(34)

can be solved by using the change of variable
z=e^{i\varphi^{\prime}}
. The result is computed by calculating the residues inside the unit circle,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=-i\oint_{\partial C}f\left(\frac{z-z^{-1}}{2i},\frac{z+z^{-1}}{2}\right)\frac{dz}{z}

(35)

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{C}\text{res}(f/z)

(36)

where
\partial C
is the circumference of radius
r=1
. The expressions obtained for the special case
l=0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(k,0)}_{0}=

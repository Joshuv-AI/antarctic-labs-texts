Rapid stabilizability of delayed infinite-dimensional control systems This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant 12171377, 12571483.
Yaxing Ma, Lijuan Wang, Huaiqiang Yu

Abstract
In this paper, the rapid stabilizability of linear infinite-dimensional control system with constant-valued delay is studied. Under assumptions that the state operator generates an immediately compact semigroup and the coefficient of the delay term is constant, we mainly prove the following two results: (i) the delay does not affect rapid stabilizability of the control system; (ii) from the perspective of observation-feedback, it is not necessary to use historical information to stabilize the control system when the system is rapidly stabilizable. Some applications are given.



Keywords.
Rapid stabilizability, Time-delay, Infinite-dimensional system, Admissible control operator

When
\tau=0
or
\kappa=0
(i.e., the delay-term disappears), rapid stabilizability of the corresponding control system has been studied extensively (see, for instance,
[
17
]
). When
\tau>0
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
,
it seems that the existing results on rapid stabilizability for linear control systems without delay-term can not be directly applied to delayed control systems. Indeed, the delay-term can be regarded formally as a force to the control system in a short time. For a linear control system with forces, when the range of the control operator (i.e., the range of
B
) can not cover the state space, its exponential stabiliability does not hold generally. However, the pair
[A,B]_{\kappa,\tau}
is a typical linear system with forces. By the state space method, it can be transformed into a type of coupled system that relies solely on control (see, for instance,
[
15
,
20
]
or Section
2
of this paper). Therefore, we can use techniques dealing with stabilizability of coupled systems to study stabilizability of delayed control systems.

Motivations of issues considered in this paper are listed as follows:

Motivation I.
As mentioned above, delay-term changes evolutionary mode of the control system. This raises an important and interesting problem: will delay-term change feasibility of the control problem (such as controllability and stabilizability)? In

[
14
]
, the authors considered a heat equation with delay and interior control and proved that the corresponding control system is not null controllable. Comparing classical controllability result for heat equation without delay (see, for instance,

[
28
, Section 11.5, Chapter 11]
), the result in
[
14
]
tells us that delay-term will disrupt controllability.
But for stabilizability, this kind of destruction does not seem to exist in some cases. For example, in
[
15
]
, the authors showed that a kind of delayed heat equation on whole space with local controls is exponentially stabilizable. In this paper, under Assumptions
(A_{1})
-
(A_{3})
, we will prove that delay-term will not affect rapid stabilizability of the control system.

Motivation II.

It is well known that so-called feedback stabilization problem is to record (or collect) output signal of the system, compile it by the feedback law and form an input signal so that the corresponding closed-loop system is stable. Therefore, besides the design of the feedback law, observation of the output signal is also crucial.
When
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
\tau>0
, the pair
[A,B]_{\kappa,\tau}
is a classical equation with memory, i.e.,
evolution of this system depends on its historical information. When we stabilize the pair
[A,B]_{\kappa,\tau}
,
whether to use historical information or not is an interesting problem. Indeed, there are two kinds of stabilizability to the pair
[A,B]_{\kappa,\tau}
, one needs historical information and the other does not (see Definition
1.5
below). Is historical information necessary?
In this paper, we will give an answer to this problem.

1.2
Definitions and main result

We start this subsection with introducing definitions of

[A,B]
. They were also found in
[

]

Some comments on them are given in

Definition 1.2
.

(i)

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. The pair
[A,B]
is said to be

\alpha

-semigroup

on

(with the generator

;

(b)

for any

(c)

.

The operator

is called a feedback law to
\alpha
-stabilzability of the pair
[A,B]
or equivalently,

[A,B]
is called
\alpha
-stabilizable with the feedback law

.

(ii)

If there is

(i)

is called

If there exists

so that the pair

is
\alpha
-stabilizable with bounded feedback law, then it is called stabilizable with bounded feedback law. If the pair
[A,B]
is
\alpha
-stabilizable with bounded feedback law for any

1.3
Related works

Studies on infinite dimensional delayed (or retarded) control systems can be dated back to 1970s (see, for example,
[
7
,
6
,
31
]
and references therein).
Up to now, there have been many outstanding achievements about such systems. One of these achievements is the state space method (see, for instance,
[
20
]
).
By the state space method, delayed systems can be transformed into coupled systems, which can be regarded as abstract evolution systems
within
C_{0}
-semigroup framework (see Section 2 below). It brings conveniences to studies on such systems. For example, by using the state space method
and the dual theory in control theory, we can
study controllability of linear delayed control system by considering observability of its adjoint system (see, for instance,
[
14
]
). Next, we recall some related works from the perspective of exponential stabilization. Roughly speaking, feedback stabilization include the following two kinds of issues: a) stabilizability of open-loop systems; b) stabilizability of closed-loop systems. These two issues are different but closely related with each other.

Stabilizability of open-loop systems
. In
[
12
]
, the author considered a general delayed control system in Hilbert space and obtained a spectral characterization on stabilizability of such system
under assumptions that the state operator generates an analytic semigroup, the coupled operator (see (
2.4
) below) has only point spectrum and
the control operator is bounded. The method in
[
12
]
originates from
[
25
]
, where
L^{2}
-stabilizability for integro-differential parabolic equations is studied.
Here, it should be noted that
L^{2}
-stabilizability in
[
12
]
is equivalent to exponential stabilizability (see
[
5
, Theorem 6.2.7, Section 6.2, Chapter 6]
).
Stabilizability of infinite dimensional delayed system with constant delay was studied in
[
23
]

under assumptions that the state operator generates an immediately compact semigroup, the control operator is bounded and the control space is finite dimensional. The main idea in
[
12
,
23
]
has been developed in
[
11
]
for abstract delayed control system.
Recently, in
[
15
, Section 4.4]
, the authors considered stabilizability of delayed heat equation on whole space by using
[
15
, Theorem 7]
.
The state operator in
[
15
, Section 4.4]
has only continuous spectrum and it can not generate a compact semigroup.
Moreover, there are many studies concerning how to use delayed feedback control to stabilize control system without delay. For instance, in

[
13
]
and
[
26
]
, the authors employed backstepping approach and the Artstein transformation to design feedback delay boundary control to stabilize one dimensional reaction-diffusion equation, respectively.

Stabilizability of closed-loop systems.
In
[
6
]
, the authors presented an example to show that delay of state feedback can disrupt stability of the system. Similar phenomenon was also found in
[
31
]
for abstract parabolic system.
In
[
30
]
, the authors presented necessary and sufficient condition for stability of one dimensional wave equation with boundary damping, where
the damping term is a combination of delay and non-delay. The main results in
[
30
]
tell us that in some cases, the delay of state feedback does not disrupt stability of the system.
For relevant results on high-dimensional wave equation or abstract second-order evolution equation, we refer readers to
[
21
,
22
]
.
In addition, many researchers used dynamic feedback to design delayed feedback control to stabilize systems
(see, for instance,
[
1
,
10
]
).

\eqref{yu-10-14-2}\;\;\mbox{is rapidly stabilizable}.

(3.48)

To this end, let

be fixed arbitrarily. Since

for

. On one hand, by (

)

(3.51)

On the other hand, let

\alpha>0

and

. Then it follows from

(Here, it should be emphasized that the proof of

By the fact that

, definitions of

\|P_{\alpha}S^{*}(T)\varphi\|_{X}^{2}\leq C(T)\int_{0}^{T}\|B^{*}P_{\alpha}S^{*}(s)\varphi\|_{U}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;\varphi\in X.

Let

where

,
(iv)
of Remark

3.62

(4.6)

(4.7)

Proof.

\begin{cases}z_{t}(t,x)=\triangle z(t,x)+az(t-\tau,x)+\chi_{\omega_{2}}F_{\gamma}(z(t,\cdot))(x),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\

z(t,x)=\varphi(t,x),&(t,x)\in(-\tau,0)\times\Omega,\end{cases}

(4.8)

\|z_{F_{\gamma}}(t;y_{0},\varphi)\|_{X}\leq C_{1}(\gamma)e^{-\gamma t}(\|y_{0}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\tau,0,X)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(4.9)

Here, we use the fact that
z_{F_{\gamma}}(\cdot;y_{0},\varphi)\in C([0,+\infty);X)
(see Remark
5.2
below).
Let
\gamma>0
be fixed. Define a linear operator
K_{\gamma}:X\times C([-\tau,0];X)\to U
as follows:

K_{\gamma}(h,\varphi)^{\top}:=F_{\gamma}h+a\chi_{\omega_{2}}\varphi(-\tau)\;\;\mbox{for any}\;\;(h,\varphi)^{\top}\in X\times C([-\tau,0];X).

Then for each
(h,\varphi)^{\top}\in X\times C([-\tau,0];X)
,

\|K_{\gamma}(h,\varphi)^{\top}\|_{U}\leq(\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X;U)}+|a|)(\|h\|_{X}+\max_{t\in[-\tau,0]}\|\varphi(t)\|_{X}),

(4.10)

which indicates
K_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{L}(X\times C([-\tau,0];X);U)
.
Moreover, we can directly check that the following claim
(CL)
is true:
for any
(y_{0},\varphi)^{\top}\in X\times C([-\tau,0];X)
,

z_{F_{\gamma}}(\cdot;y_{0},\varphi)
is the solution to (
4.8
) if and only if it is the solution to the following equation:

\begin{cases}y_{t}(t,x)=\triangle y(t,x)+a\chi_{\omega_{1}}y(t-\tau,x)+\chi_{\omega_{2}}K_{\gamma}(y(t,\cdot),y(t+\cdot,\cdot))^{\top}(x),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\
y(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\partial\Omega,\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
y(t,x)=\varphi(t,x),&(t,x)\in(-\tau,0)\times\Omega.\end{cases}

In the rest of the proof, we will show
(i)-(iii)
one by one.

Firstly, let
(y_{0},\varphi)^{\top}\in X\times L^{2}(-\tau,0;X)
be fixed arbitrarily. On one hand,
according to (
4.7
), it is clear that (
4.5
) in
[0,\tau]
turns out to be

\begin{cases}p_{t}(t,x)=\triangle p(t,x)+a\chi_{\omega_{1}}p(t-\tau,x),&(t,x)\in(0,\tau)\times\Omega,\\
p(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in(0,\tau)\times\partial\Omega,\\
p(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
p(t,x)=\varphi(t,x),&(t,x)\in(-\tau,0)\times\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.11)

By standard energy estimate for the heat equation, (
4.11
) has a unique solution
p(\cdot)\in C([0,\tau];X)

and there exists a constant
C_{2}>0
so that

\max_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|p(t)\|_{X}\leq C_{2}(\|y_{0}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\tau,0,X)})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.12)

Further, we consider the following equation:

\begin{cases}q_{t}(t,x)=\triangle q(t,x)+a\chi_{\omega_{1}}q(t-\tau,x)+\chi_{\omega_{2}}K_{\gamma}[(q(t,\cdot),q(t+\cdot,\cdot))^{\top}](x),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega,\\
q(t,x)=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\partial\Omega,\\
q(0,x)=p(\tau,x),&x\in\Omega,\\
q(t,x)=p(t+\tau,x),&(t,x)\in(-\tau,0)\times\Omega.\end{cases}

(4.13)

It follows from the claim
(CL)
and (
4.9
) that the unique solution
q(\cdot)
to (
4.13
) satisfies

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|q(t)\|_{X}&\leq&C_{1}(\gamma)e^{-\gamma t}(\|p(\tau)\|_{X}^{2}+\|p(\cdot+\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\tau,0;X)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\leq&\sqrt{1+\tau}C_{1}(\gamma)e^{-\gamma t}\max_{s\in[0,\tau]}\|p(s)\|_{X}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.\end{array}

(4.14)

Define

y(t):=\begin{cases}p(t)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in(-\tau,\tau],\\
q(t-\tau)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in(\tau,+\infty).\end{cases}

By (
4.7
), (
4.11
) and (
4.13
), we can directly check that
y(\cdot)\in C([0,+\infty);X)
and is a solution to
(
4.5
), i.e., (
4.5
) has a solution.
On the other hand, if
w(\cdot)
is a solution to (
4.5
), then it follows from (
4.7
) that

w(\cdot)
in
(-\tau,\tau]
satisfies (
4.11
). Note that (
4.11
) has a unique solution. Thus,

w(\cdot)=p(\cdot)
in
(-\tau,\tau]
. Similarly,
w(\cdot+\tau)=q(\cdot)
in
(-\tau,+\infty)
. Hence,

y_{\gamma}(t;y_{0},\varphi):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p(t)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in(-\tau,\tau],\\
q(t-\tau)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in(\tau,+\infty)\end{array}\right.

(4.15)

is the unique solution to (
4.5
).

Secondly, by (
4.12
), (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we have that

\|y_{\gamma}(t;y_{0},\varphi)\|_{X}\leq\sqrt{1+\tau}C_{1}(\gamma)C_{2}e^{\gamma\tau}e^{-\gamma t}(\|y_{0}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\tau,0,X)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\geq\tau.

(4.16)

This, along with (
4.10
) and (
4.7
), implies that

\displaystyle\|f(t)\|_{U}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|L_{\gamma}(t)(y_{\gamma}(t;y_{0},\varphi),y_{\gamma}(t+\cdot;y_{0},\varphi))^{\top}\|_{U}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in[0,\tau],\\
2(\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X;U)}+|a|)\max_{s\in[0,2\tau]}\|y_{\gamma}(s;y_{0},\varphi)\|_{X}&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in(\tau,2\tau],\\
\sqrt{1+\tau}(\|F_{\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X;U)}+|a|)C_{1}(\gamma)C_{2}(1+e^{\gamma\tau})e^{\gamma\tau}e^{-\gamma t}(\|y_{0}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\tau,0,X)})^{\frac{1}{2}}&\mbox{if}\;\;t>2\tau,\end{cases}

which indicates

Finally, it follows from (

\|y_{\gamma}(t;y_{0},\varphi)\|_{X}\leq C_{2}e^{\gamma\tau}e^{-\gamma t}(\|y_{0}\|_{X}^{2}+\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\tau,0,X)})^{\frac{1}{2}}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\in[0,\tau].

5
Appendix

In this section, let
\tau>0
,
E\in\mathcal{L}(X)
and
F\in\mathcal{L}(X\times L^{2}(-\tau,0;X);U)
be fixed. We consider the well-posedness of the following equation:

\begin{cases}y_{t}(t)=Ay(t)+Ey(t-\tau)+B[F(y(t),y(t+\cdot))^{\top}],&t\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
y(0)=y_{0},\\
y(t)=\varphi(t),&t\in(-\tau,0),\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
y_{0}\in X
and
\varphi\in L^{2}(-\tau,0;X)
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that assumptions
(A_{1})
-
(A_{3})
hold. Then for each
y_{0}\in X
and
\varphi\in L^{2}(-\tau,0;X)
, (
5.1
) has a unique solution in
C([0,+\infty);X)
.

Proof.

Let
y_{0}\in X
and
\varphi\in L^{2}(-\tau,0;X)
be fixed arbitrarily.
It suffices to show the following equation:

\begin{cases}y(t)=S(t)y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)Ey(s-\tau)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-s)B[F(y(s),y(s+\cdot))^{\top}]\mathrm{d}s,&t\in[0,+\infty),\\
y(t)=\varphi(t),&t\in(-\tau,0)\end{cases}

(5.2)

has a unique solution in
C([0,+\infty);X)
.
We will use Banach fixed-point theorem to prove it.

Indeed, according to the same arguments as those led to (
3.43
), Assumption
(A_{3})
and
(iv)
in Remark
1.1
,
there exists a constant
C(\tau)>0
so that for any
u\in L^{2}(0,\tau;U)
,

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-s)Bu(s)\mathrm{d}s\right\|^{2}_{X}\leq C(\tau)\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{U}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\in[0,\tau].

(5.3)

Let
T\in(0,\tau]
be small enough so that

\sqrt{(1+\tau)TC(\tau)}\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(X\times L^{2}(-\tau,0;X);U)}<1\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;n^{*}:=\tau/T\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

(5.4)

Define
\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}:=\{f:(-\tau,T]\to X\,|\,f|_{[0,T]}\in C([0,T];X),\;f(0)=y_{0},\;f|_{(-\tau,0)}=\varphi\}
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
is a complete metric space with the metric
d(f,g):=\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)-g(t)\|_{X}

for any
f,g\in\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
. Define an operator
\mathcal{G}
on
\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
as follows:

\mathcal{G}[y](t):=\begin{cases}S(t)y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E\varphi(s-\tau)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-s)B[F(y(s),y(s+\cdot))^{\top}]\mathrm{d}s,&t\in[0,T],\\
\varphi(t),&t\in(-\tau,0).\end{cases}

Note that for each
y\in\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|y(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|y(t+\theta)\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)\mathrm{d}t&\leq&\int_{0}^{T}\|y(t)\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}t+\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{\theta}^{T+\theta}\|y(\sigma)\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma\mathrm{d}\theta\\
&\leq&(1+\tau)T\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|y(t)\|_{X}^{2}+\tau\int_{-\tau}^{0}\|\varphi(\sigma)\|_{X}^{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma.\end{array}

(5.5)

Take
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
arbitrarily. Since
F
is bounded and
z_{1}-z_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{T,0,0}
, it follows from
(
5.5
) and (
5.3
) that

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathcal{G}[z_{1}](t)-\mathcal{G}[z_{2}](t)\|_{X}\leq\sqrt{(1+\tau)TC(\tau)}\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(X\times L^{2}(-\tau,0;X);U)}\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|z_{1}(t)-z_{2}(t)\|_{X}.

This, along with (
5.4
), implies that
\mathcal{G}
is a contraction mapping from

\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
to itself. Then by Banach fixed-point theorem, there exists a unique
y^{1}\in\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}

so that
y^{1}(t)=\mathcal{G}[y^{1}](t)
for each
t\in[0,T]
, which, combined with the definition of
\mathcal{G}
, indicates that the equation:

y(t)=S(t)y_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E\varphi(s-\tau)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-s)B[F(y(s),y(s+\cdot))^{\top}]\mathrm{d}s,\;\;t\in[0,T]

has a unique solution
y^{1}
in
\mathcal{E}_{T,y_{0},\varphi}
. By the same way as above, we can show that for each
n\in\{2,\ldots,n^{*}\}
, the equation:

y(t)=S(t)y^{n-1}(T)+\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)E\varphi(s+(n-1)T-\tau)\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{S}(t-s)B[F(y(s),y(s+\cdot))^{\top}]\mathrm{d}s,\;\;t\in[0,T]

has a unique solution
y^{n}
in
\mathcal{E}_{T,y^{n-1}(T),\varphi}
. Define

y_{1}(t):=\begin{cases}\varphi(t)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in(-\tau,0),\\
y^{1}(t)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in[0,T],\\
\cdots&\\
y^{n^{*}}(t-(n^{*}-1)T)&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in((n^{*}-1)T,n^{*}T].\end{cases}

We can directly check that

y_{1}(\cdot)\in C([0,\tau];X)
and is an solution to (
5.2
) in
[0,\tau]
. The uniqueness of the solution to (
5.2
) in
[0,\tau]

can be deduced from the the uniqueness of its solution in
[(n-1)T,nT]
for each
1\leq n\leq n^{*}
. For simplicity, we denote
y_{1}(\cdot):=y(\cdot;y_{0},\varphi)

By the same method as above, we can show that (
5.2
) in
[0,\tau]
(with
y_{0}
and

replaced by

, respectively) has a unique solution

By the iterative strategy, we can construct a sequence

y_{1}(t),&\mbox{if}\;\;t\in[0,\tau],\\

C([0,+\infty);X)

∎

Prognostic evaluation of segmental ureterectomy combined with chemotherapy in high-grade non-metastatic ureteral cancer: a study based on the SEER database
Authors: Yu Xia, Bin-Bin Ma, Meng-Yun Li, Xi Liu, Dan-Feng Xu, Tao Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study evaluates the survival outcomes of segmental ureterectomy (SU) combined with chemotherapy in patients with high-grade non-metastatic ureteral cancer (UC) using data from the SEER database. A total of 1757 patients with Grade III-IV non-metastatic UC were analyzed. Overall survival (OS) was assessed through Kaplan–Meier analysis, and independent prognostic factors were identified via Cox regression. A Nomogram model was developed and evaluated using the concordance index, area under the time-dependent ROC curve, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis. The 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates were 82.8%, 55.6%, and 42.8%, respectively. Age, treatment protocol, T stage, and N stage were significant prognostic factors. Both SU + chemotherapy and radical nephroureterectomy (RNU) + chemotherapy demonstrated comparable survival outcomes, outperforming surgery alone, particularly in patients aged 70 and older. The Nomogram demonstrated high predictive accuracy and clinical utility. These findings suggest that SU + chemotherapy offers survival benefits similar to RNU + chemotherapy, making it a viable option, especially for elderly patients or those with impaired renal function.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-77117-y.Keywords: Ureteral cancer, Segmental ureterectomy, Nomogram, Prognostic anaylsisSubject terms: Urogenital diseases, Ureter, Risk factors, Urological cancer

Introduction

Urothelial carcinoma, a malignant tumor originating from the urothelium, ranks as the sixth most common cancer in developed countries, affecting both the lower urinary tract (bladder and urethra) and the upper urinary tract (renal pelvis and ureter)1. Among these, ureteral carcinoma (UC) is relatively rare, accounting for 5–10% of all urothelial carcinomas and 25–30% of upper tract urothelial carcinomas (UTUC). Alarmingly, about two-thirds of UC cases are invasive at diagnosis, presenting a substantial challenge in clinical management2.

The standard treatment for UC is radical nephroureterectomy (RNU), which involves the removal of the kidney, the entire ureter, and part of the bladder surrounding the ureteral orifice2. While effective, RNU often results in significant loss of renal function and potential complications. Recent studies indicate that kidney-sparing surgery (KSS), such as segmental ureterectomy (SU), offers comparable oncological outcomes to RNU in low-risk UC patients under specific conditions—solitary tumors, tumor size under 2 cm, absence of high-grade cytology findings, low-grade tumors confirmed by ureteroscopic biopsy, and no invasive growth on CT imaging2. KSS not only preserves renal function, making it advantageous for patients who might require future platinum-based chemotherapy, but it also minimizes surgical complications3,4.

Despite these promising findings for low-risk UC, the use of KSS in high-risk UC patients remains controversial. Currently, there is limited research on the efficacy of SU combined with chemotherapy in this high-risk group. This study aims to fill this gap by analyzing clinical data from the SEER (Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results) database to evaluate the survival outcomes of SU combined with chemotherapy in patients with Grade III-IV non-metastatic UC. By providing evidence-based insights, this research seeks to inform and refine clinical decision-making for high-risk UC patients, potentially expanding treatment options beyond the traditional approaches.

Methods

Source of data

Clinical data were collected from the SEER database (SEER Research Data, 17 Registries, November 2023 Submission, 2000–2021) using SEER*Stat software (version 8.4.3). The extracted clinical variables included: (1) Basic demographic information: sex (female or male), marital status (married or unmarried), race (white, black, or other), and age; (2) Tumor characteristics: T stage (T1, T2, T3, or T4) and N stage (N0, N1, or N2); (3) Treatment information: regional lymph node dissection (none or yes) and treatment protocol (RNU with chemotherapy, SU with chemotherapy, RNU alone, or SU alone).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Diagnosed with UC using ICD-O-3 site code C66.9 between 2000 and 2021; (2) Pathologically confirmed transitional cell carcinoma with ICD-O-3 codes 8120/3, 8130/3, or 8131/3; (3) Classified as having a pathological grade of III or IV; (4) Tumors staged as T1–4, N0–2, M0 (indicating no distant metastasis); (5) Received treatment via RNU (surgery site codes 40, 50, 70, or 80) or SU (surgery site code 30). Exclusion criteria included: (1) Bilateral tumors; (2) Diagnosis of multiple primary cancers; (3) Lack of available active follow-up data.

Statistical analysis

Univariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to identify variables with a p-value < 0.1. These variables were then included in a multivariate Cox regression to determine independent risk factors for overall survival (OS). Kaplan–Meier survival curves were created for each of these independent risk factors. Differences in survival curves were evaluated using Cox regression tests when the proportional hazards (PH) assumption was met (PH p-value > 0.05) or using the log-rank test when the PH assumption was not met (PH p-value < 0.05). The overall p-values were displayed on the Kaplan–Meier plots.

A Nomogram model was developed to predict OS based on the identified independent risk factors. The model’s performance was assessed using various metrics: discrimination ability (evaluated by the concordance index [C-index] and the area under the time-dependent ROC curve [AUC]), calibration accuracy (using calibration curves), and clinical benefit (analyzed through decision curve analysis [DCA]).

Data processing was performed using Xiantao academic tools (https://www.xiantaozi.com/products), a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 1,757 patients with Grade III-IV non-metastatic UC were included in the study based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The average age of the patients was 73.6 years, with a male-to-female ratio of 1.4:1. Among these patients, 438 (24.9%) received RNU + chemotherapy, 103 (5.9%) received SU + chemotherapy, 934 (53.2%) underwent RNU alone, and 282 (16%) underwent SU alone. Identification of independent prognostic factors for OS

The average OS time of the 1757 patients with Grade III-IV non-metastatic UC was 78.9 months, with a median OS time of 45 months. The 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-year OS rates were 82.8%, 66.4%, 55.6%, 48.3%, and 42.8%, respectively.

Cox regression analysis identified age, treatment protocol, T stage, and N stage as independent prognostic factors for OS (Table 2). Multivariate analysis indicated that advanced age, higher T stage, and higher N stage were associated with poorer outcomes. There was no statistically significant difference in OS between RNU + chemotherapy and SU + chemotherapy (median OS: 53 months vs. 50 months, P = 0.275), both of which were superior to RNU or SU alone. Construction and validation of nomogram model

Based on identified prognostic factors, Nomogram A was developed to predict OS (Fig. 3A). Nomogram B, incorporating only T stage and N stage, was also constructed (Fig. 3B). Calibration curves indicated that both models showed good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes (Fig. 4).

Time-dependent ROC curves were plotted to assess the predictive accuracy of both models for 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates (Fig. 5). Discussion

For high-risk UTUC patients, defined by criteria such as multifocal tumors, tumor size ≥ 2 cm, high-grade tumors detected through cytology or biopsy, invasive growth observed on imaging, hydronephrosis, a history of radical cystectomy for high-grade bladder cancer, or multiple histological types, RNU with bladder cuff excision is considered the standard treatment2. There is no significant difference in outcomes between open surgery and laparoscopic surgery for these high-risk patients2,5.

The use of SU for treating non-metastatic high-risk UC patients is still debated, with no randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or prospective studies comparing long-term oncological outcomes of SU versus RNU. The 2024 EAU guidelines and large retrospective studies recommend KSS for selected non-metastatic high-risk UTUC patients, especially those with solitary kidneys, bilateral UTUC, chronic kidney disease, or comorbidities that make RNU unsuitable6,7. In our study, only 21.9% of patients received SU treatment, with most still preferring RNU. The distribution of SU was 22%, 20.4%, 22.3%, and 13.2% among patients with T1, T2, T3, and T4 stage, respectively, and 22.1%, 20.6%, and 20.4% among those with N0, N1, and N2 stage, respectively.

In addition to surgery, chemotherapy plays a crucial role in the treatment of UTUC. EAU guidelines suggest platinum-based adjuvant chemotherapy (e.g., GC or MVAC) for high-risk UTUC patients staged T2–4, N0-3, M0 following surgery, with adjuvant nivolumab as an alternative for those ineligible for chemotherapy2,6. A 2020 Phase III RCT in The Lancet showed significantly improved disease-free survival with platinum-based chemotherapy post-RNU in UTUC patients (pT2-4, N0-N3, M0 / pT1-4, N1-3, M0)8. However, chronic kidney disease is prevalent in UTUC patients, and renal function often declines further after RNU, leaving 20–25% of patients unable to tolerate platinum-based chemotherapy9. Split-dose cisplatin may be considered for those with glomerular filtration rates below 45 ml/min10.

Our study showed no significant OS differences between patients receiving RNU + chemotherapy and SU + chemotherapy in high-risk non-metastatic UC, with median OS times of 53 and 50 months, respectively. Both treatments provided better outcomes than RNU or SU alone, which had median survival times of 44 and 38 months, respectively. These results highlight the importance of adjuvant chemotherapy in improving prognosis for high-risk UC patients.

Regarding regional lymph node dissection (LND), studies have shown that the risk of lymph node metastasis is low in patients with Ta-T1 stage UTUC, suggesting that LND may be unnecessary in these cases11,12. However, for patients with invasive UTUC, the risk of lymph node metastasis is higher, and template-based LND may help improve survival and provide more accurate tumor staging13–16. Despite this, the 2023 EAU guidelines only weakly recommend template-based LND in non-metastatic high-risk UTUC patients6. Consistent with these guidelines, our study’s Cox analysis did not demonstrate a significant improvement in OS with LND. However, the SEER database does not specify whether template-based LND was conducted, nor does it provide detailed information on the standard or scope of the LND.

Previous studies have explored UC prognosis. Qiu et al. analyzed data from 2509 non-metastatic UC patients in the SEER database (2004–2015) and found that neither surgical approach (SU vs. RNU) nor chemotherapy significantly impacted prognosis. Instead, factors such as age, AJCC stage, tumor size, and grade were independent risk factors for OS. However, this study did not specifically analyze high-grade UC patients17. Slusarczyk et al. studied 2,888 non-metastatic UC patients from the SEER database (2004–2018) and reported no significant difference in survival between high-risk patients (with high-grade or muscle-invasive tumors) treated with RNU versus SU. Independent risk factors for OS included T stage, lymph node invasion, age over 75, tumor size > 2 cm, and male gender. Notably, chemotherapy data were not incorporated into this study18.

Building on previous research, our study analyzed non-metastatic UC patients with Grade III-IV pathology from the SEER database (2000–2021), focusing on treatment protocols that combined surgery and chemotherapy. Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified age, treatment type, T stage, and N stage as independent predictors of OS, while sex, marital status, race, and LND showed no significant impact. The findings suggest that both RNU + chemotherapy and SU + chemotherapy offer similar survival benefits for patients with localized high-grade UC, outperforming RNU or SU alone. These combined treatments are particularly recommended for patients aged 70 and older.

This study has some limitations. Previous research has identified positive surgical margins, including soft tissue, ureteric, and urethral margins, as significant prognostic factors for survival in patients undergoing radical cystectomy for muscle-invasive bladder cancer. Positive margins are also associated with tumor T stage and lymph node metastasis19. However, due to the absence of data on surgical margins in the SEER database, this factor could not be assessed in the current study. Furthermore, studies have demonstrated that variant histology in urothelial carcinoma, such as micropapillary, plasmacytoid, small-cell, and sarcomatoid subtypes, correlates with poor prognosis and an increased risk of distant metastasis20–22. The SEER database’s ICD-O-3 codes, however, do not accurately reflect the most recent WHO classification of urothelial tumors, limiting the analysis to patients with the more common transitional cell carcinoma. Additionally, a substantial portion of tumor size data was missing (902 out of 1,757 patients), and the low number of patients receiving radiotherapy (112 out of 1757) restricted the inclusion of these variables in the analysis. Lastly, the SEER database does not differentiate between neoadjuvant and adjuvant chemotherapy, which may have affected the precision of the model’s predictions.

Conclusion

Concerns about long-term renal insufficiency and the uncertain prognosis associated with kidney-sparing surgery (KSS) often cause hesitation in treating high-grade non-metastatic UC. However, this study, based on SEER database analysis, demonstrates that SU + chemotherapy offers survival outcomes comparable to RNU + chemotherapy and is superior to surgery alone (RNU or SU), especially in patients aged 70 and older. Therefore, SU + chemotherapy may be the preferred treatment for elderly patients or those with impaired renal function who have high-grade non-metastatic UC.

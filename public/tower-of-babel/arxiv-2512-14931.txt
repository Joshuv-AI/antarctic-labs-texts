Moisture dynamics with phase changes coupled to heat-conducting, compressible fluids
Felix Brandt, Matthias Hieber, Lin Ma, Tarek Zöchling

Abstract
. It is shown that a model coupling the heat-conducting compressible Navier-Stokes equations to a micro-physics model of moisture in air is locally strongly well-posed for large data in suitable function spaces and strongly well-posed on [ 0 , τ ] [0,\tau] for every τ > 0 \tau>0 for small initial data. This seems to be the first result on [ 0 , τ ] [0,\tau] for arbitrary τ > 0 \tau>0 for a model coupling moisture dynamics to heat-conducting, compressible Navier-Stokes equations. A key feature of the micro-physics model is that it also includes phase changes of water in moist air. These phase changes are associated with large amounts of latent heat and thus result in a strong coupling to the thermodynamic equation. The well-posedness results are obtained by means of a Lagrangian approach, which allows to treat the hyperbolicity in the continuity equation. More precisely, optimal L p \mathrm{L}^{p} - L q \mathrm{L}^{q} estimates are shown for the linearized system, leading to the local well-posedness result by a fixed point argument and suitable nonlinear estimates. For the well-posedness result on [ 0 , τ ] [0,\tau] for arbitrary τ > 0 \tau>0 , a refined analysis of the linearized problem close to equilibria is carried out, and the roughness of the source term, induced by the phase changes, requires to establish delicate a priori bounds.

Introduction

Modeling and understanding moisture dynamics in the atmosphere is important in fluid dynamics and atmospheric sciences.
Due to the complex nature of the moisture processes in the atmosphere, which in turn results from the coupling of the thermodynamics of water and dry air, this is a challenging task, and there are various approaches by different communities.
For background on moisture models, we refer for instance to the survey article of Stevens
[
36
]
, the article by Pauluis, Czaja and Korty
[
32
]
as well as the monograph by Khouider
[
25
]
, and the references therein.
There is also vast literature on the mathematical analysis of moisture models.
For the case of only one moisture quantity, we refer to the works by Bousquet, Coti-Zelati, Frémond, Huang, Kukavica, Temam, Tribbia and Ziane
[
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
In
[
27
]
, Li and Titi established the global well-posedness and a relaxation limit of a variant of a model of large scale dynamics of precipitation fronts in the tropical atmosphere first introduced by Frierson, Majda and Pauluis
[
16
]
.
A similar problem was also studied by Majda and Souganidis
[
29
]
in the weak setting.
Zhang, Smith and Stechmann
[
39

numerically investigated the effects of clouds and phase changes on fast-wave averaging.
Very recently, Remond-Tiedrez, Smith and Stechmann
[
33

showed the well-posedness and regularity at the cloud edge for a nonlinear elliptic PDE arising in the context of the precipitating quasi-geostrophic model.
A moist Boussinesq system was then investigated by the same authors in
[
34
]
.

For the underlying moisture model in this paper, the starting point is that water in the atmosphere is present in the form of vapor water, cloud water and rain water.
Importantly, by phase changes, these various forms can be transformed into one another.
More precisely, rain water evaporates to vapor water, condensation leads to the change of vapor water into cloud water, while accumulation and collection of cloud water by rain result in the formation of rain water.
The effects of these phase changes are twofold:
First, they lead to a strong coupling of the moisture quantities to the thermodynamic equation, also owing to the presence of different heat capacities for dry air, water vapor and liquid water.
Second, these phase changes cause significant mathematical challenges due to switch terms in the source terms, which thus lack Fréchet differentiability.
We can overcome this mathematical difficulty by our approach based on a combination of classical energy estimates with more modern tools as optimal data results for compressible fluids.
This allows us to regard the switch terms, which are non-differentiable, as forcing terms.

From a modeling point of view, based on the works of Kessler
[
24
]
and Grabowski and Smolarkiewicz
[
17
]
, Klein and Majda
[
26
]
developed a moisture model for warm clouds, corresponding to a bulk micro-physics closure.
The model from
[
26
]

[
20
]

The aim of this paper is to rigorously study a moist atmosphere dynamics model that is coupled to the heat-conducting compressible Navier-Stokes equations whose analysis was pioneered by Matsumura and Nishida
[
30

We refer here also to the works of Lions

For the existence of global weak solutions for viscous compressible and heat-conducting Navier-Stokes equations, we refer to the article by Bresch and Desjardins
[

A multi-scale analysis of compressible viscous and rotating fluids has been carried out by Feireisl, Gallagher, Gerard-Varet and Novotný
[
15

Danchin

used a Lagrangian approach to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
For further background on Fourier analysis methods, including applications to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations, we also refer to the monograph of Bahouri, Chemin and Danchin

Let us emphasize that we do not consider a Boussinesq approximation, which would be simpler to analyze, but we take into account the full non-isothermal compressible Navier-Stokes equations, comprising a continuity equation for the density of dry air, a momentum balance as well as a diffusion equation for the temperature.

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathrm{D}_{t}\rho_{\mathrm{d}}+\rho_{\mathrm{d}}\mdiv u&=0,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{d}}Q_{\mathrm{m}}\mathrm{D}_{t}u-\mu\Delta u-(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\mdiv u+\nabla p&=\rho_{\mathrm{d}}q_{\mathrm{r}}V_{\mathrm{r}}\partial_{z}u-\rho_{\mathrm{d}}Q_{\mathrm{m}}g\mathrm{e}_{3},\\

The model is complemented by suitable boundary and initial conditions, see Eqs. (
2.2
)–(
2.4
).
In this article, we establish the local strong well-posedness as well as the global strong well-posedness for small data of the model presented in (
1.1

In order to handle the hyperbolic effects from the continuity equation in (
1.1
)
1
, we first transform the coupled system to Lagrangian coordinates.
We then develop an
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-theory to the linearized problem, showing the existence of a unique solution to the linearized problem, and providing a priori estimates in terms of the data.
Thanks to a careful analysis of the transformed terms, this leads to the local strong well-posedness by means of a fixed point procedure.
Concerning the global well-posedness for small data, we slightly adjust the transformation to Lagrangian coordinates in order to investigate the system close to equilibrium solutions.
A refined analysis of the linearized operator associated with the compressible Navier-Stokes moisture system, together with suitable nonlinear estimates to handle the rough source terms, then paves the way for the second main result of this paper.
Major difficulties here are the lack of Fréchet differentiability of the nonlinear terms, which is due to the switch terms that account for the phase changes, and the non-locality in time of the transformed terms.
The special shape of the source terms requires us to use an approach combining maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity with energy estimates.
As far as we can see, only using either maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity or energy estimates is insufficient to handle the switch terms for the global strong well-posedness for small data.
For more background on maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity and optimal

2.
Description of the Coupled Moisture-Fluid Model

The purpose of this section is to introduce the coupled model of the moisture dynamics and non-isothermal compressible Navier-Stokes equations in detail.
To this end, let us briefly recall that we consider the problem on a unit box with horizontal periodicity
\Omega=G\times(0,1)
, where
G=(0,1)^{2}
, and
\tau\in(0,\infty]
represents a positive time.
Additionally, we recall the densities of dry air
\rho_{\mathrm{d}}
, vapor water
\rho_{\mathrm{v}}
, cloud water
\rho_{\mathrm{c}}
and rain water
\rho_{\mathrm{r}}
as well as the resulting mixing ratios for vapor water
q_{\mathrm{v}}=\nicefrac{{\rho_{\mathrm{v}}}}{{\rho_{\mathrm{d}}}}
, cloud water
q_{\mathrm{c}}=\nicefrac{{\rho_{\mathrm{c}}}}{{\rho_{\mathrm{d}}}}
and rain water
q_{\mathrm{r}}=\nicefrac{{\rho_{\mathrm{r}}}}{{\rho_{\mathrm{d}}}}
.
The moist air density
\rho
then takes the shape

\rho\coloneqq\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(1+q_{\mathrm{v}}+q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}}).

For the potential temperature
\theta
as well as positive constants
p_{\mathrm{ref}}
,
c_{\mathrm{pd}}
and
R_{\mathrm{d}}
, with
c_{\mathrm{pd}}>R_{\mathrm{d}}

is given by

T\coloneqq\theta\Bigl(\frac{p}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}}\Bigr)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}},\kern 5.0pt\text{where}\kern 5.0pt\gamma=\frac{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}-R_{\mathrm{d}}}>1.

Moreover, for further constants
c_{\mathrm{pv}}
,
c_{1}>0
, the mixed specific heat capacity
c_{\nu}
is of the form

c_{\nu}\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{pd}}+c_{\mathrm{pv}}q_{\mathrm{v}}+c_{1}(q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}}),

while for another positive constant
R_{\mathrm{v}}
, the mixed gas constant
\sigma
is defined by

\sigma\coloneqq\Bigl(\frac{c_{\mathrm{pv}}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}R_{\mathrm{d}}-R_{\mathrm{v}}\Bigr)q_{\mathrm{v}}+\frac{c_{1}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}R_{\mathrm{d}}(q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}}),

and for positive reference values
L_{\mathrm{ref}}
and
T_{\mathrm{ref}}
, the latent heat of condensation is

L(T)\coloneqq L_{\mathrm{ref}}+(c_{\mathrm{pv}}-c_{1})(T-T_{\mathrm{ref}}).

Another important quantity is the saturation mixing ratio
q_{\mathrm{vs}}\coloneqq q_{\mathrm{vs}}(p,T)
, which is assumed to be a non-negative, uniformly bounded and Lipschitz continuous function of the pressure
p
and the temperature
T
, and we suppose that
0\leq q_{\mathrm{vs}}\leq q_{\mathrm{vs}}^{*}<\infty
.
Here
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
represent the positive and the negative part of a function

f^{+}\coloneqq\max(0,f)

f^{-}\coloneqq\min(0,f)

With the above quantities, we have

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{m}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq 1+q_{\mathrm{v}}+q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}},

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{th}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{c_{\nu}}{\gamma}+\sigma=\frac{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}{\gamma}+\Bigl(\frac{c_{\mathrm{pv}}}{\gamma}+\frac{c_{\mathrm{pv}}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}R_{\mathrm{d}}-R_{\mathrm{v}}\Bigr)q_{\mathrm{v}}+\Bigl(\frac{c_{1}}{\gamma}+\frac{c_{1}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}R_{\mathrm{d}}\Bigr)(q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}}),

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{cp}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sigma-\frac{R_{\mathrm{d}}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}c_{\nu}=-R_{\mathrm{d}}-R_{\mathrm{v}}q_{\mathrm{v}},

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{R_{\mathrm{v}}}{R_{\mathrm{d}}+R_{\mathrm{v}}q_{\mathrm{v}}}\Bigl(\sigma-\frac{R_{\mathrm{d}}}{c_{\mathrm{pd}}}c_{\nu}\Bigr)+c_{\mathrm{pv}}-c_{1}=c_{\mathrm{pv}}-c_{1}-R_{\mathrm{v}},\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq L_{\mathrm{ref}}-(c_{\mathrm{pv}}-c_{1})T_{\mathrm{ref}}.

Next, we make precise the source terms in (
1.1
).
In fact, these are the rates of evaporation of rain water
S_{\mathrm{ev}}
, the condensation of vapor water to cloud water and the inverse process
S_{\mathrm{cd}}
, the auto-conversion of cloud water into rain water by accumulation of microscopic droplets
S_{\mathrm{ac}}
and the collection of cloud water by falling rain
S_{\mathrm{cr}}
.
They are given by

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{ev}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{ev}}\frac{p}{\rho}(q_{\mathrm{vs}}-q_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{r}}=c_{\mathrm{ev}}T\frac{R_{\mathrm{d}}+R_{\mathrm{v}}q_{\mathrm{v}}}{1+q_{\mathrm{v}}+q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}}}(q_{\mathrm{vs}}-q_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{r}},

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{cd}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{cd}}(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})q_{\mathrm{c}}+c_{\mathrm{cn}}(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{cn}},

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{ac}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{ac}}(q_{\mathrm{c}}-q_{\mathrm{ac}})^{+},\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptS_{\mathrm{cr}}\coloneqq c_{\mathrm{cr}}q_{\mathrm{c}}q_{\mathrm{r}}.

Here
c_{\mathrm{ev}}
,
c_{\mathrm{cd}}
,
c_{\mathrm{cn}}
,
c_{\mathrm{ac}}
,
q_{\mathrm{cn}}
and
q_{\mathrm{ac}}
denote further positive constants.
Let us remark that for simplicity of the presentation, and as it does not affect the analysis, we assume that the constants are such that

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\theta p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}},\kern 5.0ptc_{\nu}=1+q_{\mathrm{v}}+q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}},\kern 5.0pt\sigma=q_{\mathrm{v}}+q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}},\kern 5.0ptL(T)=L_{\mathrm{ref}}+T-T_{\mathrm{ref}},

\displaystyle Q_{\mathrm{m}}

\displaystyle=Q_{\mathrm{th}}=1+q_{\mathrm{v}}+q_{\mathrm{c}}+q_{\mathrm{r}},\kern 5.0ptQ_{\mathrm{cp}}=-(1+q_{\mathrm{v}}),\kern 5.0ptc_{1}=q_{\mathrm{cn}}=q_{\mathrm{ac}}=Q_{1}=Q_{2}=1,

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{ev}}

\displaystyle=T\cdot\frac{1+q_{\mathrm{v}}}{Q_{\mathrm{m}}}(q_{\mathrm{vs}}-q_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{r}},\kern 5.0ptS_{\mathrm{cd}}=(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})q_{\mathrm{c}}+(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})^{+},

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{ac}}

\displaystyle=(q_{\mathrm{c}}-1)^{+}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptS_{\mathrm{cr}}=q_{\mathrm{c}}q_{\mathrm{r}},

and we suppose that
\kappa=1
.
For further details and an explanation of the constants, we also refer to
[
13
, Sec. 1]
.

Plugging in the terms resulting from the aforementioned simplifications, and defining the Lamé operator
\mathrm{L}
by
\mathrm{L}u\coloneqq\mu\Delta u+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\mdiv u
, where we assume
\mu>0
and
2\mu+\lambda>0
, we arrive at the following system of equations on
(0,\tau)\times\Omega
:

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\rho_{\mathrm{d}}+\mdiv(\rho_{\mathrm{d}}u)&=0,\\
\rho_{\mathrm{d}}Q_{\mathrm{m}}[\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u]-\mathrm{L}u+\nabla p&=\rho_{\mathrm{d}}q_{\mathrm{r}}V_{\mathrm{r}}\partial_{z}u-\rho_{\mathrm{d}}Q_{\mathrm{m}}g\mathrm{e}_{3},\\
Q_{\mathrm{m}}[\partial_{t}T+(u\cdot\nabla)T]-\Delta T&=q_{\mathrm{r}}V_{\mathrm{r}}\partial_{z}T-(1+q_{\mathrm{v}})T\mdiv u-(T+1)\\
&\quad\cdot\Bigl[T\cdot\frac{1+q_{\mathrm{v}}}{Q_{\mathrm{m}}}(q_{\mathrm{vs}}-q_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{r}}-(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})q_{\mathrm{c}}-(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})^{+}\Bigr],\\
\partial_{t}q_{\mathrm{v}}+(u\cdot\nabla)q_{\mathrm{v}}-\Delta q_{\mathrm{v}}&=T\cdot\frac{1+q_{\mathrm{v}}}{Q_{\mathrm{m}}}(q_{\mathrm{vs}}-q_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{r}}-(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})q_{\mathrm{c}}-(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})^{+},\\
\partial_{t}q_{\mathrm{c}}+(u\cdot\nabla)q_{\mathrm{c}}-\Delta q_{\mathrm{c}}&=(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})q_{\mathrm{c}}+(q_{\mathrm{v}}-q_{\mathrm{vs}})^{+}-(q_{\mathrm{c}}-1)^{+}-q_{\mathrm{c}}q_{\mathrm{r}},\\
\partial_{t}q_{\mathrm{r}}+(u\cdot\nabla)q_{\mathrm{r}}-\Delta q_{\mathrm{r}}&=\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathrm{d}}}\partial_{z}(\rho_{\mathrm{d}}q_{\mathrm{r}}V_{\mathrm{r}})+(q_{\mathrm{c}}-1)^{+}+q_{\mathrm{c}}q_{\mathrm{r}}-T\cdot\frac{1+q_{\mathrm{v}}}{Q_{\mathrm{m}}}(q_{\mathrm{vs}}-q_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}q_{\mathrm{r}},\\
p&=\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(1+q_{\mathrm{v}})T.\end{aligned}\right.

More precisely, we assume that all quantities satisfy periodic boundary conditions on the lateral boundary.
Let us denote the upper and lower boundary by
\Gamma_{u}\coloneqq G\times\{1\}
and
\Gamma_{l}\coloneqq G\times\{0\}
. For the fluid velocity, we assume no-slip boundary conditions, i.e.,

Finally, we also take into account initial conditions

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(0)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{d},0},\kern 5.0ptu(0)=u_{0},\kern 5.0ptT(0)=T_{0},

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{v}}(0)

\displaystyle=q_{\mathrm{v},0},\kern 5.0ptq_{\mathrm{c}}(0)=q_{\mathrm{c},0}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptq_{\mathrm{r}}(0)=q_{\mathrm{r},0},

which are required to satisfy the boundary conditions (
2.2
) and (

) provided they are assumed to be sufficiently regular as in

In this context, let us remark that we use

3.
Main Results

Having introduced the complete model in detail, we state the main results on the local strong well-posedness as well as the global strong well-posedness for small data in the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-setting in this section.
With regard to strong solutions for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations, it seems that there are basically two approaches:
One is based on a Hilbert space setting and relies on higher Sobolev regularity for the initial data.
In addition to more restrictive regularity requirements for the initial data, this also leads to more complicated compatibility conditions that need to be satisfied by the initial data.
The second approach, which we mainly pursue in this paper, does not require to consider higher regularity.
Instead, for sufficiently large
q
, the ground space for the fluid velocity, temperature equation and mixing ratios is
\mathrm{L}^{q}
, while we take into account
\mathrm{H}^{1,q}
for the continuity equation.
Instead, for sufficiently large
q
, we choose
\mathrm{L}^{q}
as the base space for the fluid velocity, temperature equation, and mixing ratios, while the continuity equation is treated in the space
\mathrm{H}^{1,q}
.

Before stating the main results, we need to clarify some of the function spaces used throughout this paper.
Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p,q\in(1,\infty)
. In the sequel, we
denote by
\mathrm{W}^{s,q}(\Omega)
the fractional Sobolev spaces, by
\mathrm{H}^{s,q}(\Omega)
the Bessel potential spaces and by
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p}(\Omega)
the Besov spaces, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain.
We set
\mathrm{H}^{0,q}(\Omega)\coloneqq\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)
and note that
\mathrm{H}^{s,q}(\Omega)
coincides with the
classical Sobolev space
\mathrm{W}^{m,q}(\Omega)
provided
s=m\in\mathbb{N}
.
In the following, we need some terminology to describe periodic boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{l}=\partial G\times[0,1]
as well as on
\partial G
.
Given
s\in[0,\infty)
and
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, we define the spaces
\mathrm{H}^{s,q}_{\mathrm{per}}(\Omega)\coloneqq\overline{\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(\overline{\Omega})}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s,q}(\Omega)}}
and
\mathrm{H}^{s,q}_{\mathrm{per}}(G)\coloneqq\overline{\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(\overline{G})}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s,q}(G)}}
, where horizontal periodicity is modeled by the function spaces
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{per}}(\overline{G})
defined e.g., in
[
19
]
.
The Besov spaces
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,\mathrm{per}}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{q,p,\mathrm{per}}(G)
are defined likewise.
In order to shorten notation, we abbreviate the norms by a subscript
τ
for the time component and a subscript

4.
The Model in Lagrangian Coordinates

In order to circumvent the hyperbolic effects that arise in the continuity equation, we introduce Lagrangian coordinates.
The present section is dedicated to the calculation of the associated change of variables.

For this purpose, we introduce the characteristics
X
that corresponds to the fluid velocity

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}X(t,y)&=u(t,X(t,y)),&&\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptt\in(0,\tau),\\

Assume that

\Omega
onto
\Omega
for all

The (local-in-time) invertibility of

Subsection 6.1

For

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(t,X(t,y))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}}(t,y),\kern 5.0ptu(t,X(t,y))=\tilde{u}(t,y),\kern 5.0ptT(t,X(t,y))=\tilde{T}(t,y),

\displaystyle q_{j}(t,X(t,y))

\displaystyle=\tilde{q}_{j}(t,y),\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0ptj\in\{\mathrm{v},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r}\},

\displaystyle p(t,X(t,y))

\displaystyle=\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(t,X(t,y))(1+q_{\mathrm{v}}(t,X(t,y)))T(t,X(t,y))

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}}(t,y)(1+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}(t,y))\tilde{T}(t,y)=\tilde{p}(t,y),

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{r}}(t,X(t,y))

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}(t,y),\kern 5.0ptq_{\mathrm{vs}}(p(t,X(t,y)),T(t,X(t,y)))=q_{\mathrm{vs}}(\tilde{p}(t,y),\tilde{T}(t,y))\eqqcolon\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{vs}}.

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}}(t,Y(t,x)),\kern 5.0ptu(t,x)=\tilde{u}(t,Y(t,x)),\kern 5.0ptT(t,x)=\tilde{T}(t,Y(t,x)),

\displaystyle q_{j}(t,x)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{r}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}(t,Y(t,x)),\kern 5.0ptq_{\mathrm{vs}}(p(t,x),T(t,x))=q_{\mathrm{vs}}(\tilde{p}(t,Y(t,x)),\tilde{T}(t,Y(t,x)))\eqqcolon\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{vs}}.

Moreover, we will also write

\left\|\frac{\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}}{2}\partial_{t}\tilde{T}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x})}\leq\left\|\frac{\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}}{2}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{H}^{1,q}_{x})}\cdot\|\partial_{t}\tilde{T}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Making use of the embeddings
\mathbb{E}_{1,T,\tau}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\tau))
and
\mathbb{E}_{1,\mathrm{v},\tau}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\tau))
, see also the proof of
Subsection 7.2
, and invoking
Subsection 7.2
(iv) as well as
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{B_{\varepsilon,\tau}}
for
\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}
, we derive the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|(\tilde{T}+\bar{T}+1)(\tilde{T}+\bar{T})\frac{1+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}}{2+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}}(\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}})^{+}\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{T}+\bar{T}+1\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x})}\cdot\|\tilde{T}+\bar{T}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x})}\cdot\left\|\frac{1+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}}{2+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}}+\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x})}\cdot\|\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{H}^{1,q}_{x})}\cdot\|\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Next, we estimate the term
\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial y_{l}}Z_{l,3}
.
Employing that
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{B_{\varepsilon,\tau}}
for
\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}
, and invoking the assumption on
\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}
, we find that

\left\|\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial y_{l}}Z_{l,3}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{L}^{q}_{x})}\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x})}\cdot\|Z_{l,3}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x})}\cdot\|\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{H}^{1,q}_{x})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

We can deal with
\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\frac{\partial\tilde{V}_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial y_{l}}Z_{l,3}
in the same way.

Let us observe that the other terms appearing in
G_{u},\tilde{G}_{T},\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{v}},\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{r}},\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{c}}
can be estimated in the same way as above, and it thus remains to estimate the boundary terms.

For the boundary terms, we first use standard trace theory together with the Banach algebra structure of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,\tau;\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega))
by
q>3
,
Subsection 7.2
(ii) to estimate
\Id_{3}-Z
and
\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon,\tau}
for
\tilde{T}
to derive that

\displaystyle\|(\Id_{3}-Z)\nabla\tilde{T}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(G))}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\|(\Id_{3}-Z^{\top})\nabla\tilde{T}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{H}_{x}^{1,q})}

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\|(\Id_{3}-Z^{\top})\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathrm{H}_{x}^{1,q})}\cdot\|\nabla\tilde{T}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{H}_{x}^{1,q})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

This also yields that
\|B_{T,1}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\tau}^{p}(\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(G))}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}
, and likewise for
B_{T,2}
.
On the other hand, by
\tilde{T}\in\mathbb{E}_{1,T,\tau}
, we deduce similarly as in
[
12
, Prop. 6.4]
that

\left\|\left.\partial_{k}\tilde{T}\right|_{G}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}_{p,q,\tau}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(G))}\leq C\cdot\|\tilde{T}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,T,\tau}}\leq C\varepsilon.

From standard trace theory together with
Subsection 7.2
(iv), it further follows that

\left\|\left.(\Id_{3}-Z)\right|_{G}\right\|_{\mathrm{C}_{\tau}^{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{p^{\prime}}}}(\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(G))}\leq C\cdot\left\|Z^{\top}-\Id_{3}\right\|_{\mathrm{C}_{\tau}^{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{p^{\prime}}}}(\mathrm{H}_{x}^{1,q})}\leq C\varepsilon.

Making use of a paraproduct estimate, see
[
38
, Thm. 2.8.2(ii)]
, we find that

\displaystyle\|B_{T,1}\|_{\mathrm{F}_{p,q,\tau}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(G))}\leq C\cdot\left\|\left.\partial_{k}\tilde{T}\right|_{G}\right\|_{\mathrm{F}_{p,q,\tau}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}(\mathrm{L}^{q}(G))}\cdot\left\|\left.(\Id_{3}-Z)\right|_{G}\right\|_{\mathrm{C}_{\tau}^{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{p^{\prime}}}}(\mathrm{W}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}},q}(G))}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Note that
B_{T,2}
can be estimated in the same way.
The proof of this proposition follows upon noting that the other boundary terms can also be estimated by the same procedure.
∎

Finally, in order to deal with the nonlinear terms accounting for the phase transitions, we consider the following auxiliary problem for some given time
\tau>0
:

(7.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}Q-\Delta Q&=f-Q^{+},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt(0,\tau)\times\Omega,\\
\partial_{z}Q&=g_{u},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt(0,\tau)\times\Gamma_{u},\\
\partial_{z}Q&=g_{l},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{on}\kern 5.0pt(0,\tau)\times\Gamma_{l},\\
Q(0)&=Q_{0},&&\kern 5.0pt\text{in}\kern 5.0pt\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

and assume that
Q\in\mathbb{E}_{1,h,\tau}
is a local, maximal solution for given data
(f,g_{u},g_{l},Q_{0})
.
In the following, we derive a priori bounds for the solution
Q
depending on the size of the data as made precise below.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
\tau>0
and
p,q\in[2,\infty)
. Assume that for
i\in\{u,l\}
, the data satisfies

(7.7)

\displaystyle f\in\mathbb{E}_{0,h,\tau},\ g_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{h,\tau}\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptQ_{0}\in\mathrm{B}_{q,p,\mathrm{per}}^{2(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}(\Omega)\kern 5.0pt\text{with}\kern 5.0pt

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,h,\tau}}+\|g_{i}\|_{\mathbb{F}_{h,\tau}}+\|Q_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}^{2(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}_{q,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0}\varepsilon^{2},

for some
\varepsilon>0
and
C_{0}>0
.
Then there is a constant
C>0
with
\|Q\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,h,\tau}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}
.

Proof.

For brevity, for
q\in[1,\infty]
, we also simply denote the spatial
q
-norm by
\|\cdot\|_{q}
.
We multiply (
7.6
) by
Q|Q|^{q-2}
and integrate in space to obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|Q\|^{q}_{q}+\|\nabla|Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{2}+\||\nabla Q||Q|^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\|^{2}_{2}\leq C\Bigl(\int_{\Omega}\bigl(f-Q^{+}\bigr)\cdot Q|Q|^{q-2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{G}(g_{u}-g_{l})\cdot Q|Q|^{q-2}\,\mathrm{d}S\Bigr)

for some constant
C>0
.
In view of Hölder’s and Young’s inequality, we get

\Bigl|\int_{\Omega}(f-Q^{+})\cdot Q|Q|^{q-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigr|\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{q}\cdot\|Q\|_{q}^{q-1}+\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\bigr)\leq C\bigl(\|f\|^{q}_{q}+\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\bigr).

Concerning the boundary terms, we observe that similar arguments yield

\displaystyle\Bigl|\int_{G}(g_{u}-g_{l})\cdot Q|Q|^{q-2}\,\mathrm{d}S\Bigr|\leq C\cdot\|g_{u}-g_{l}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)}^{q}+\varepsilon\cdot\|Q\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(G)}\leq C\cdot\|g_{u}-g_{l}\|^{q}_{\mathrm{B}_{q,q}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G)}+\varepsilon\cdot\||Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)}.

The trace theorem, interpolation and Young’s inequality then imply

\displaystyle\||Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(G)}\leq C\||Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{W}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},2}(\Omega)}\leq C\bigl(\||Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)}+\||Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{2}\bigr)\leq C\bigl(\|\nabla|Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{2}_{2}+\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\bigr).

Hence, after absorbing, we obtain

\partial_{t}\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\leq C\Bigl(\|f\|^{q}_{q}+\|g_{u}-g_{l}\|^{q}_{\mathrm{B}_{q,q}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G)}+\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\Bigr).

An integration in time then leads to

\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\tau}(\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q})}+\|g_{u}-g_{l}\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\tau}(\mathrm{B}_{q,q}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G))}\bigr)+C\int_{0}^{t}\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\ \mathrm{d}s+\|Q_{0}\|^{q}_{q}.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we conclude that

\|Q\|^{q}_{q}\leq\Bigl(C\|f\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\tau}(\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q})}+C\|g_{u}-g_{l}\|^{q}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\tau}(\mathrm{B}_{q,q}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G))}+\|Q_{0}\|^{q}_{q}\Bigr)\mathrm{e}^{C\tau}.

By Sobolev embeddings, we get
\mathrm{B}^{2(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}_{q,p,\mathrm{per}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)
, so
\|Q\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{\tau}(\mathrm{L}_{x}^{q})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}
by the assumptions on the data.
In particular, for all
q\geq 2
, we have

\|\nabla|Q|^{\frac{q}{2}}\|^{\nicefrac{{q}}{{2}}}_{2}+\||\nabla Q||Q|^{\frac{q-2}{2}}\|^{\nicefrac{{q}}{{2}}}_{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

By the maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
\mathrm{X}_{0}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the local solution
Q\in\mathbb{E}_{1,h,\tau}
to (
7.6
) can be estimated by

\|Q\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,h,\tau}}\leq C\bigl(\|f\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,h,\tau}}+\|Q^{+}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,h,\tau}}+\|g_{u}\|_{\mathbb{F}_{h,\tau}}+\|g_{l}\|_{\mathbb{F}_{h,\tau}}+\|Q_{0}\|_{\mathrm{B}_{q,p}^{2(1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}(\Omega)}\bigr).

The previous step joint with the embedding
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,\tau)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,\tau)
then yields

\|Q^{+}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,h,\tau}}\leq C\cdot\|Q\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,\tau;\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega))}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

The claimed estimate then follows from the assumptions on the data.
∎

We are now in the position to prove the global existence of strong solutions for small data.

Proof.

(Proof of
Theorem 3.2
).
Let
\tau>0
,
(f_{\mathrm{d}},f_{u},f_{T},f_{\mathrm{v}},f_{\mathrm{c}},f_{\mathrm{r}})\in\mathbb{E}_{0,\tau}
and
(g_{T,i},g_{\mathrm{v},i},g_{\mathrm{c},i},g_{\mathrm{r},i})\in(\mathbb{F}_{h,\tau})^{4}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
Consider the system (
7.5
) supplemented with the initial data (
7.2
).
Then by
Subsection 7.1
and similar arguments as in
Section 6
, there is a unique, local, maximal strong solution
\tilde{z}=(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\tilde{u},\tilde{T},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}})\in\mathbb{E}_{1,a_{\mathrm{max}}}
of (
7.5
), where
a_{\mathrm{max}}>0
denotes the maximal time of existence.
If
a_{\mathrm{max}}<\tau
, i.e., the solution does not exist globally, this time
a_{\mathrm{max}}
is characterized by the condition

\lim\limits_{t\to a_{\mathrm{max}}}\|(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\tilde{u},\tilde{T},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,t}}=\infty.

Now for
\tilde{z}_{1}\in\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm{max}}}
, consider (
7.5
) with
f_{\mathrm{d}}=G_{\mathrm{d}}(\tilde{z}_{1})
,
f_{u}=G_{u}(\tilde{z}_{1})
,
f_{T}=\tilde{G}_{T}(\tilde{z}_{1})
,
f_{\mathrm{v}}=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{v}}(\tilde{z}_{1})
,
f_{\mathrm{c}}=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{c}}(\tilde{z}_{1})
,
f_{\mathrm{r}}=\tilde{G}_{\mathrm{r}}(\tilde{z}_{1})
and boundary terms

g_{T,i}=B_{T,i}(\tilde{z}_{1}),\kern 5.0ptg_{\mathrm{v},i}=B_{\mathrm{v},i}(\tilde{z}_{1}),\kern 5.0ptg_{\mathrm{c},i}=B_{\mathrm{c},i}(\tilde{z}_{1})\kern 5.0pt\text{and}\kern 5.0ptg_{\mathrm{r},i}=B_{\mathrm{r},i}(\tilde{z}_{1}),\kern 5.0pt\text{for}\kern 5.0pti\in\{1,2\}.

For the solution map

\Phi:\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm{max}}}\to\mathbb{B}_{\varepsilon,a_{\mathrm{max}}},\kern 5.0pt\tilde{z}_{1}=(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d},1},\tilde{u}_{1},\tilde{T}_{1},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v},1},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c},1},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r},1})\mapsto\tilde{z}=(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\tilde{u},\tilde{T},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}}),

we show in the following that
\Phi
is well-defined, a self-map and a contraction, given that
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small.
By
Subsection 7.2
, it holds that

\|\tilde{G}(\tilde{z}_{1})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,a_{\mathrm{max}}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|(B_{T,i}(\tilde{z}_{1}),B_{\mathrm{v},i}(\tilde{z}_{1}),B_{\mathrm{c},i}(\tilde{z}_{1}),B_{\mathrm{r},i}(\tilde{z}_{1}))\|_{\mathbb{F}_{a_{\mathrm{max}}}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Combining the maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity on
\mathrm{X}_{0}
with
Subsection 7.2
, for all
t\in[0,a_{\mathrm{max}}]
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\|(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\tilde{u},\tilde{T},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,t}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}+\|(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d},0}-\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{T}_{0}-\bar{T},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v},0},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c},0}-1,\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}})\|_{\mathrm{X}_{\gamma}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

where we also used the assumptions on the initial data in the last inequality.
Now, choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small yields that

\|(\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\tilde{u},\tilde{T},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}},\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{r}})\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,t}}\leq\varepsilon\kern 5.0pt\text{for all}\kern 5.0ptt\in[0,a_{\mathrm{max}}],

which shows that
\Phi
is well-defined and a self-map. The contraction property can be shown in the same way.
Finally, since
\varepsilon
may be chosen sufficiently small, the transform
X(t,\cdot)
is a
\mathrm{C}^{1}
-diffeomorphism, see
Subsection 7.2
, from
\Omega
into
\Omega
and we define

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{d}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}}(t,Y(t,x))+\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{d}},\kern 5.0ptu(t,x)=\tilde{u}(t,Y(t,x)),\kern 5.0ptT(t,x)=T(t,Y(t,x))+\bar{T},

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{v}}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{v}}(t,Y(t,x)),\kern 5.0ptq_{\mathrm{c}}(t,x)=\tilde{q}_{\mathrm{c}}(t,Y(t,x))+1,\kern 5.0ptq_{\mathrm{r}}(t,x)=q_{\mathrm{r}}(t,Y(t,x)).

Then
(\rho_{\mathrm{d}},u,T,q_{\mathrm{v}},q_{\mathrm{c}},q_{\mathrm{r}})
is a global, strong solution to system (
2.1
) with
(\rho_{\mathrm{d}},u,T,q_{\mathrm{v}},q_{\mathrm{c}},q_{\mathrm{r}})\in\mathbb{E}_{1,\tau}
.
∎

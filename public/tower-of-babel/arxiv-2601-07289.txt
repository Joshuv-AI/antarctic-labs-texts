A universal lower bound on the photon sphere radius in higher-dimensional black holes
Yong Song, Jiaqi Fu, Yiting Cen

Abstract
The photon sphere, a hypersurface of circular null geodesics, plays a fundamental role in characterizing black hole spacetimes, influencing phenomena such as black hole shadows, gravitational lensing, and quasinormal modes. While universal upper bounds on the photon sphere radius have been established for both four-dimensional and higher-dimensional black holes, the question of a corresponding lower bound in higher-dimensional black holes remains less explored. In this work, we derive a universal lower bound for the photon sphere radius in static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat black hole spacetimes of arbitrary dimension n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 . Under the assumptions of the weak energy condition, a non-positive trace of the energy-momentum tensor, and a monotonicity condition on the radial pressure function | r n − 1 ​ p r ​ ( r ) | |r^{n-1}p_{r}(r)| , we prove that the photon sphere radius r γ r_{\gamma} satisfies r γ ≥ ( n − 1 2 ) 1 / ( n − 3 ) ​ r H r_{\gamma}\geq(\frac{n-1}{2})^{1/(n-3)}r_{H} , where r H r_{H} is the radius of the outer event horizon. For n = 4 n=4 , this reduces to the known result r γ ≥ 3 2 ​ r H r_{\gamma}\geq\frac{3}{2}r_{H} . Our result generalizes Hod’s four-dimensional theorem to higher dimensions, and provides a new geometric constraint on the structure of black holes in extended theories of gravity.

IV
Discussion and conclusion

We have established a universal lower bound on the photon sphere radius of static, spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat black holes in arbitrary dimensions
n\geq 4
. The proof relies on three physically motivated assumptions: the weak energy condition, a non‑positive trace of the energy-momentum tensor, and a monotonicity condition for the radial pressure function

|r^{n-1}p_{r}(r)|
. Under these conditions the photon sphere cannot lie arbitrarily close to the horizon; its minimal distance is given by the simple expression
(\frac{n-1}{2})^{1/n-3}
times the horizon radius.

The result generalises Hod’s four-dimensional theorem and provides a new geometric constraint for higher‑dimensional black holes. It is interesting to note that the bound becomes weaker as the dimension increases: the factor

(\frac{n-1}{2})^{1/n-3}
approaches
1
from above when
n\to\infty
. This reflects the well‑known fact that in higher dimensions gravity becomes stronger near the horizon, allowing circular photon orbits to exist closer to it.

The assumptions used, particularly the monotonicity of
(\frac{n-1}{2})^{1/n-3}
, are satisfied by many classical hairy black hole solutions but could be relaxed in future work. Possible extensions include investigating axisymmetric (rotating) configurations
Cunha:2017qtt
;
Cunha:2020azh
, considering asymptotically de Sitter or anti-de Sitter spacetimes, or examining the implications of quantum corrections on the energy conditions. Such generalizations would further illuminate the interplay between energy conditions, horizon geometry, and the behavior of null geodesics in curved spacetimes.

Safety of short-term non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug use in the postoperative setting in paediatric patients with chronic kidney disease: protocol for a randomised, placebo-controlled trial
Authors: Alice H Xiang, Catalina K Hwang, Emily Clennon, Thuytien To, Jennifer Pyrzanowski, Allyson B Calderon, Samantha Wilson, Elizabeth Shields, Gemma Beltran, Carter Sevick, Margret Bock, Kelly T Harris, Kyle O Rove
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNon-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are a key component of multimodal analgesia in paediatric surgical care but are often avoided in patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) due to concerns for nephrotoxicity. This trial aims to evaluate the safety of short-term NSAID use in paediatric patients with mild-to-moderate CKD undergoing inpatient urological surgery.Methods and analysisThis is a multicentre, double-blinded, randomised, placebo-controlled trial evaluating short-term NSAID use in paediatric patients with CKD stages 2–3a after urological surgeries requiring admission. This study is being conducted at two academic paediatric urology departments in the USA. Patients aged >18 months will be randomised 1:1 to receive ketorolac/ibuprofen or placebo for ≤5 days postoperatively. The primary outcome is acute kidney injury (AKI) as defined according to Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes criteria based on serum creatinine trends and urine output. Secondary outcomes are postoperative pain scores and opioid use (reported as morphine equivalents/kg/day). Fisher’s exact test will compare AKI rates between groups. The recruitment goal of 164 participants provides 80% power to detect a 20% absolute increase in AKI incidence (baseline AKI rate of 15% based on prior studies, two-sided testing).Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Colorado Multiple Institutional Review Board (COMIRB #24-1252) and will be conducted in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines. Written informed consent will be obtained from a parent or legal guardian for all participants, and child assent will be obtained when developmentally appropriate and required by institutional policy. Study findings will be disseminated through presentation at scientific meetings and publications in peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberNCT06860711.

Introduction

Background

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are a key component of multimodal analgesia in surgical care. Multimodal analgesia targets multiple pain-modulating pathways simultaneously, improving analgesia and recovery while reducing opioid requirements.1 2 While opiates remain highly effective analgesics, their use carries significant risks, including constipation, sedation, increased monitoring requirements and increased tolerance and dependency.3 The opioid epidemic has therefore intensified efforts to identify safe opioid-sparing strategies. Consequently, NSAIDs are universally incorporated into Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols, which are used across surgical specialties worldwide.4–6

NSAIDs are generally well tolerated, but potential adverse effects include gastrointestinal, cardiac and renal complications.7 NSAIDs are inhibitors of cyclo-oxygenase enzymes, which play a role in modulating renal blood flow and thus can result in acute kidney injury (AKI). Patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) are traditionally considered at higher risk of NSAID-associated AKI due to reduced renal reserve.8 AKI itself has also been associated with progression of CKD.9

However, the epidemiological studies that link NSAIDs to nephrotoxicity are based on chronic NSAID exposure rather than short-term use in acute settings such as postoperative recovery.8 In the paediatric literature, studies associating NSAID use with AKI often involve critically ill children with dehydration or haemodynamic instability—independent risk factors for kidney injury.10 Thus, the risk associated with short-term perioperative NSAID use in children with CKD remains poorly defined.

Relevance and anticipated impact

A recent meta-analysis examining NSAID safety in patients with CKD noted several factors associated with increased AKI risk in CKD; these include older age, comorbidities and polypharmacy. The authors concluded that short-term courses of NSAID use (less than 5 days) appeared safe in adults with CKD stages 1–3 and may even be acceptable in CKD stage 4.8 No such data exists in the paediatric population to our knowledge.

A retrospective study from our institution reported no difference in postoperative AKI following NSAID use in patients with mild CKD undergoing urological surgery.11 However, the retrospective nature and limited follow-up period challenge the acceptability and generalisability of these findings.

NSAID avoidance represents a missed opportunity for opiate-sparing analgesia. However, currently there is inadequate high-quality evidence to promote confidence in their use in patients with CKD. Genuine clinical equipoise exists regarding postoperative NSAID use in paediatric patients with CKD, as the potential opioid-sparing benefits must be balanced against uncertain nephrotoxicity risk. To address this gap, we designed this study to inform physicians on both the safety of NSAIDs in the immediate postoperative period and to compare pain scores and opiate requirements between patient populations receiving NSAIDs versus placebo.

Objectives

The primary endpoint of this study is to assess whether a short course of NSAID use in the postoperative setting increases the risk of AKI in paediatric patients with mild-to-moderate CKD.

The secondary objectives are to evaluate the effectiveness of NSAIDs as routine postoperative pain management by comparing pain scores and opioid requirements between patients receiving NSAIDs and those receiving placebo.

Methods and analysis

NSAID in CKD protocol development

This study was initially conceived as a grant-funded project and protocol development was informed by multidisciplinary input from paediatric urology, nephrology and anaesthesiology collaborators.

Planning for statistical analysis and sample size estimation was conducted in collaboration with an institutional biostatistician. The Investigational Drug Services (IDS) team assisted in developing detailed pharmacy dispensing workflows and creating electronic medical health record (EMR) order sets to ensure smooth and safe implementation of study drug delivery.

In preparation for study launch, the research team collaborated closely with surgical floor nurse managers to provide education on study objectives, drug administration and monitoring procedures. These efforts ensured consistent understanding and alignment across all clinical teams involved in perioperative care and study execution.

Study design

This is a multicentre, double-blinded randomised controlled trial evaluating the safety of short-term NSAID use in paediatric patients with CKD stages 2–3a undergoing urological surgery requiring inpatient admission. Participating institutions include two paediatric hospitals in the USA: Children’s Hospital Colorado/University of Colorado and Cincinnati Children’s Hospital/University of Cincinnati. Participants will be randomised in a 1:1 ratio to receive postoperative NSAID therapy or placebo for up to 5 days following surgery. The primary outcome is the development of AKI defined according to Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria.12

Eligibility criteria and participant selection

Eligible surgical cases are identified prospectively through an institutional electronic health record report of upcoming urological procedures requiring inpatient admission.

Recruitment and consent

After eligible patients are identified based on inclusion and exclusion criteria, families are contacted by the research team to introduce the study and assess interest in participation. Families who express interest are provided with the study consent via email to review. A research coordinator subsequently contacts the family to review the consent and discuss study participation. Assent is obtained from participants when appropriate based on age and institutional guidelines (online supplemental appendix: consent and assent forms).

Participants and families are able to access the consent and assent forms electronically through REDCap, where consent is signed and dated electronically. Following completion of informed consent, participants are enrolled in the institutional clinical trial management system in the EMR and entered into a secure REDCap database for study tracking.

Randomisation and blinding

On the day of surgery, the IDS pharmacy randomises participants based on an allocation scheme developed in collaboration with the study biostatisticians. Block randomisation will be performed based on procedure type (complex upper tract, simple upper tract, complex lower tract, simple lower tract), and age (18 months to 8 years and ≥8 years). The surgical procedures included in each block are listed in online supplemental appendix table S1. Further block creation was deferred due to anticipated small sample size. The age stratification was selected to correspond with age-based postoperative pain scoring tools that will be used per standard of care.

Allocation concealment will be maintained by the IDS pharmacy, which will retain the randomisation records in a secure location within the pharmacy that is not accessible to study personnel. Study personnel involved in participant enrolment, clinical care, outcome assessment and data collection will not have access to the randomisation sequence or allocation records.

IDS pharmacy prepares and dispenses study medications to the operating room where the participant receives their first dose of study medication at the end of the surgery. Study medications will be provided in identical intravenous and oral (PO) liquid formulations and in identical containers to maintain blinding. Participants, surgeons, anaesthesiologists, nursing staff and members of the research team involved in postoperative care and outcome assessment will remain blinded to treatment allocation throughout the study period.

Intervention

Study medications are administered postoperatively in accordance with the institutional ERAS protocol. All participants will receive standard postoperative pain management according to institutional paediatric urology enhanced recovery pathways. Scheduled acetaminophen is routinely administered as first-line analgesia during the first 24–48 postoperative hours. Participants randomised to the intervention arm will additionally receive ketorolac and/or ibuprofen, while participants in the control arm will receive a matching placebo. Opioids are not routinely scheduled and are reserved for breakthrough pain when pain is inadequately controlled with non-opioid analgesics. For major reconstructive procedures, regional anaesthetic techniques or local anaesthetic blocks may be utilised at the discretion of the treating team. Use of regional anaesthesia, opioid administration and other adjunctive analgesic interventions will be recorded and reported. Urology ERAS protocol practices include intraoperative local blocks per anaesthesia protocol, scheduled Tylenol every 6 hours for 48 hours, advancing diet as tolerated, avoiding opioids and early mobilisation.

Participants randomised to the intervention arm will receive NSAID therapy consisting of intravenous ketorolac at a dose of 0.5 mg/kg per dose (maximum 15 mg) for up to eight doses, followed by transition to PO ibuprofen at a dose of 10 mg/kg per dose (maximum 400 mg) administered at standard dosing intervals of every 6 hours as needed for pain. The total duration of intervention (intravenous+PO) will not exceed 5 days. Patients who remain in the hospital for 5 days postoperatively will be provided study medication by nursing staff. If patients are discharged before postoperative day (POD) 5, they will take a bottle of the study medication home with them and be instructed to take the PO formulation only as needed. The placebo medications will have a similar look, consistency and taste to the corresponding NSAID medications.

Participants discharged before POD 5 will receive reminders to obtain follow-up laboratory testing and complete a secure REDCap survey documenting postoperative medication use. The study drug bottles dispensed to patients at the time of discharge have measurements on them to help participants estimate the quantity of study drug taken. These follow-up procedures are intended to minimise missing data. Participants without sufficient follow-up data to determine AKI status will be excluded from the primary outcome analysis. The number of participants excluded because of missing outcome data will be reported.

Study medication will be discontinued if there is clinical concern for AKI or other adverse effects, with further management determined by the treating clinical team.

Study status

Participation and recruitment began on 1 July 2025 and the study is currently actively enrolling participants. Completion of recruitment and follow-up is anticipated within 4 years of study initiation.

Outcome measures

Primary outcome

The primary outcome is the proportion of patients experiencing AKI in the postoperative period during exposure to the study medication. Serum creatinine will be measured at three timepoints: pre-incision creatinine (baseline), POD 1 and POD 5–7. Patients discharged prior to POD 5 will complete follow-up labs at an outpatient facility. Postoperative creatinine labs will be compared with the preoperative baseline values and interpreted in conjunction with urine output (UOP) to assess for AKI according to KDIGO criteria.13 Specifically, AKI will be defined as a ≥1.5-fold increase in serum creatinine from baseline, an absolute increase of ≥0.3 mg/dL or a UOP<0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6–12 hours.12

Following urological surgery, expected UOP is typically maintained at 1–2 mL/kg/hour. Nursing staff are instructed to notify the clinical team when UOP falls below 0.5 mL/kg/hour, prompting evaluation for potential causes including catheter malfunction, urinary obstruction or other postoperative complications. To ensure standardised assessment across participating sites, UOP data will be extracted from the electronic medical record and analysed using a standardised study calculator that computes weight-adjusted UOP over predefined 6- and 12-hour intervals (online supplemental appendix: Urine Output Calculator Excel).

Secondary outcomes

Secondary outcomes are postoperative pain scores and opioid requirements. Pain will be assessed using the Faces Pain Scale-Revised or a verbal pain score (scale 0–10) depending on age, per nursing routine standard of care.14 The percentage of days with severe pain (pain score ≥7) will be reported. Opiate use will be calculated as morphine equivalents per kilogram per day. Data analysis will account for differences in length of stay, with subgroup comparisons between patients undergoing shorter single night stays versus longer inpatient admissions. As an additional measure of postoperative pain burden, serial pain scores will be normalised to a 0–10 scale and summarised over time using the area under the pain intensity-time curve.15 16

Data collection and analysis procedures

Patient demographic data, procedural data and hospitalisation data will be collected through the EMR and recorded in a secure REDCap database. Concomitant medication exposure, including potentially nephrotoxic medications and ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers, will be collected. For patients who are screened but not enrolled, limited de-identified data (ethnicity, race, primary language spoken, sex, age, procedure type and CKD stage) will be collected to assess for potential selection bias and generalisability of the study population. All data will be stored in REDCap. Interim data review may be performed for safety monitoring purposes in accordance with our study protocol guidelines.

Statistical analysis and sample size calculation

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of enrolled participants will be summarised using descriptive statistics. Standardised mean differences will be used to compare characteristics of patients allocated to NSAIDs versus placebo.

The primary outcome will be reported for both the NSAID and placebo group. Statistical significance will be calculated by Fisher’s exact test. A p value <0.05 will be considered statistically significant. Descriptive analysis will be performed for AKI rates stratified by procedure type, age, sex and CKD stage. All analyses will be conducted using an intention-to-treat approach.

Sample size calculations were based on a two-sided comparison of proportions with α=0.05, 80% power and a 1:1 allocation.17 Assuming an AKI incidence of 15% in the placebo group and 35% in the NSAID group, corresponding to an absolute risk increase of 20%, 164 participants are required.18 This effect size was selected as a clinically meaningful safety threshold that would be expected to influence postoperative analgesic prescribing practices in paediatric patients with CKD. Given the limited data on postoperative AKI incidence in this population and the absence of significant difference in AKI rates reported by Ha et al,11 the true effect may be smaller. Consequently, the study may not be powered to detect smaller between-group differences in AKI incidence. Due to small sample size, a multivariate logistic regression adjusting for covariates will be limited and considered exploratory.

For the secondary outcomes, opiate use (morphine equivalents/kg/day) and proportion of days inpatient with severe pain (pain score ≥7) will be compared between groups using appropriate statistical tests based on the distribution and scale of the data. Continuous variables will be analysed using Student’s t-test or non-parametric equivalents, as appropriate.

No datasets have been generated or analysed during the current study. On study completion, de-identified participant data and relevant study materials may be made available on reasonable request to the corresponding author, subject to institutional review board approval and data use agreements.

Study oversight

This study is conducted under the supervision of a multidisciplinary research team including dedicated research staff, paediatric urologists, paediatric nephrologists and pharmacy collaborators. The study team is responsible for protocol implementation, data integrity and overall study conduct.

Participants will be monitored for adverse events, including AKI. If there is a clinical concern for AKI, labs may be drawn prior to 5–7 days. Participants who develop clinical or laboratory evidence of AKI or other adverse events will have their study medication discontinued and will be managed according to standard clinical care.

Safety oversight will be provided by an independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB). The DSMB will include representatives from paediatric nephrology who are not part of the study team. They will review adverse events and interim safety data in accordance with the study adverse event reporting protocol. Treatment allocation will remain concealed from the study team; however, the DSMB may access unblinded treatment assignment if needed for safety review. The DSMB may recommend continuation, modification, temporary suspension or termination of the study based on safety concerns.

Study data will be collected and managed securely in a REDCap database which will be monitored for completeness and accuracy. Interim data review may be performed halfway through the study recruitment goal for safety monitoring purposes in accordance with the DSMB and institutional guidelines.

Patient and public involvement

None.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has been approved by the institutional review board at Children’s Hospital Colorado and with the University of Colorado Anschutz campus (IRB #24-1252). Written informed consent will be obtained from parents or legal guardians, with assent obtained from participants when appropriate. Participation is voluntary and participants retain the right to withdraw from the study at any time point for any reason.

Adverse events are discussed at weekly study team meetings and reported to the DSMB and IRB accordingly. The risks associated with study participation are considered minimal and relate primarily to short-term NSAID exposure, which is commonly used in clinical practice.

All study data will be stored in a secure, password-protected REDCap database. De-identified data may be made available on reasonable request.

Study findings will be disseminated through presentation at national scientific meetings and subsequently will be published in peer-reviewed journals.

Discussion

Strengths of this study include its multicentre, double-blinded, randomised controlled design, in which both participants and clinicians are blinded to treatment allocation, with study medications prepared and dispensed by the IDS pharmacy to ensure allocation concealment. This is the first randomised controlled trial evaluating postoperative NSAID use in paediatric patients with CKD stages 2–3a. The multicentre design improves external validity and facilitates recruitment across diverse patient populations. The study uses prospective data collection and standardised KDIGO criteria to define AKI, allowing consistent outcome assessment. Protocol development was informed by a multidisciplinary team including paediatric urology, nephrology, anaesthesiology, pharmacy, nursing and clinical research personnel. In addition, a structured adverse event reporting system is in place to support patient safety during both inpatient and post-discharge follow-up.

Limitations include the relatively small and specialised study population, which may make recruitment challenging. In addition, participation requires parental consent and, when appropriate, patient assent. The sample size is powered to detect a clinically meaningful difference in AKI incidence; however, smaller differences may not be detected. There is limited published data on the baseline incidence of postoperative AKI in paediatric patients with CKD undergoing urological surgery, and uncertainty in the underlying event rate may affect the assumptions used for sample size estimation. Given our inclusion criteria for children with CKD 2–3a, results may not be generalisable to children with CKD stages 3b–5. Results may not be generalisable to longer duration of NSAID therapy because exposure is limited to five PODs. This study is designed to be multicentre, and additional sites are currently in the regulatory approval phase; early enrolment may be limited to a single institution. Despite these limitations, this study will provide important prospective data on NSAID use in paediatric patients with mild to moderate CKD.

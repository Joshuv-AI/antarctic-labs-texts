Embedding calculus and Vassiliev spectral sequence
Syunji Moriya

Abstract
. Vassiliev spectral sequence and Sinha spectral sequence are both related to cohomology of the space of long knots ℝ → ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{3} . Although they have different origins, the Vassiliev E 1 E_{1} -page and the Sinha E 2 E_{2} -page are isomorphic (up to a degree shift). In this paper, we prove that they have isomorphic E ∞ E_{\infty} -pages if the coefficient ring is a field. Together with degeneracy of the Sinha sequence, this implies that the Vassiliev sequence degenerates at E 1 E_{1} -page over ℚ \mathbb{Q} including the non-diagonal part. Our result also implies that for any coefficient field, the space of finite type n n invariants is isomorphic to the space of weight systems of weight ≤ n \leq n if and only if the parts of the Sinha sequence of bidegree ( − i , i ) (-i,i) degenerate at E 2 E_{2} for i ≤ 2 ​ n i\leq 2n . For the construction of the isomorphism, we use a variant of Thom space model which was introduced in the author’s previous paper and captures embedding calculus of the knot space in terms of fat diagonals. As a byproduct of the construction, we give a partial computation on differentials of unstable versions of the Vassiliev sequence which converge to finite dimensional approximations of the knot space.



1.
Introduction

A long knot in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a smooth embedding
\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which coincides with a fixed linear embedding outside a compact set.
Let
K_{d}
be the space of long knots in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
C^{\infty}
topology. This paper
concerns two spectral sequences for cohomology of
K_{3}
. One is Vassiliev spectral sequence introduced in
[
40
]
, using the Alexander duality and simplicial resolutions of finite dimensional approximations of the space of singular long knots. The diagonal part of this sequence is related to the finite type knot invariants.
The other is Sinha spectral sequence, which originates in Goodwillie-Weiss embedding calculus, a powerful framework to deal with the homotopy type of embedding spaces in terms of configuration spaces (see
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
44
]
). Sinha
[
36
,
37
]
gave a cosimplicial model encoding the embedding calculus of the knot space and defined his sequence as Bousfield-Kan type cohomology spectral sequence associated to the cosimplicial model. The cosimplicial model is closely related to the little disks operad, which has promoted study of computation of the Sinha sequence.

While the definitions of these two sequences are very different, Turchin
[
39
]
proved that the Vassiliev
E_{1}
-page and Sinha
E_{2}
-page are isomorphic as bigraded modules up to degree shift. So it is natural to expect relation between these sequences for the later pages.
In this paper, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathsf{k}
be a field.

(1)

The
E_{\infty}
pages of the Vassiliev spectral sequence
{}^{V}\!E_{r}
and the Sinha spectral sequence
{}^{S}\!E_{r}
for the space
K_{3}
of long knots in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, over
\mathsf{k}
, are isomorphic as (abstract) bigraded
\mathsf{k}
-vector spaces with the standard degree shift
(-p,q)\leftrightarrow(q-3p,2p)
given in
[
39
]
.

(2)

The Vassiliev spectral sequence over
\mathsf{k}
degenerates at the first page if and only if the Sinha spectral sequence over
\mathsf{k}
degenerates at the second page.

See Definitions
2.4
,
2.19
and
2.21
. We remark that the part of the Sinha sequence of a bidegree which is not of the form
(q-3p,2p)
for any integers
p,q
, is zero from
E_{1}
.
Since the sequences are finite dimensional in each bidegree, second part of the theorem follows from first part via the result of
[
39
]
.

Since degeneracy of the Sinha sequence is already known, see
[
22
,
28
,
38
]
, we can solve affirmatively a remaining part of an old conjecture of Vassiliev which states that his sequence would degenerate at
E_{1}
-page:

Corollary 1.2

(
[
20
]
for the diagonal part)
.

The Vassiliev spectral sequence
{}^{V}\!E_{r}
degenerates at
E_{1}
-page for
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Q}
. ∎

Vassiliev proposed the conjecture, motivated by analogy between the discriminant of the knot space and affine subspace arrangements (see
[
42
]
). The diagonal part (degree 0 part) has been payed much attention since it is related to the finite type invariants. For the diagonal part and for
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Q}
, Kontsevich
[
20
]
proved the conjecture using the technique of configuration space integrals. In an unpublished version of the paper
[
20
]
, he seemed to outline a proof of degeneracy for the non-diagonal part and for the case of higher codimension, see
[
41
,
42
]
(the Vassiliev and Sinha sequences are also defined for
K_{d}
with
d\geq 4
). Along this line, Cattaneo–Cotta-Ramusino–Longoni
[
11
]
constructed non-trivial cocycles of the knot space
K_{d}
for
d\geq 4
, but a complete proof of the conjecture did not appear. Then, Lambrechts-Turchin-Volić
[
22
]
proved the conjecture for the case of higher codimension. Their method is as follows: The
space of long knots modulo immersions

\bar{K}_{d}
is defined as the homotopy fiber of the inclusion from
K_{d}
to the space of immersions which coincide with the fixed embedding outside a compact set. Sinha’s cosimplicial model for
\bar{K}_{d}
is related to the little
d
-disks operad (see
[
37
]
). Using the (relative) formality of the operads
[
20
,
24
]
, one can prove that Sinha’s cohomological spectral sequence
\bar{E}_{r}
for the space of long knots modulo immersions degenerates at
E_{2}
-page. By
[
39
]
, the map of spectral sequence
{}^{S}E_{r}\to\bar{E}_{r}
induced by the canonical map
\bar{K}_{d}\to K_{d}
gives a monomorphism on the
E_{2}
-page. So
{}^{S}E_{r}
also degenerates at
E_{2}
-page. Since both of
{}^{S}E_{r}
and
{}^{V}E_{r}
converge to
H^{*}(K_{d})
for
d\geq 4
, Vassiliev’s degeneracy is obtained. In the case of
d=3
, the degeneracy of Sinha’s sequence (for knots modulo immersion) and the injectivity of the map
{}^{S}E_{r}\to\bar{E}_{r}
still hold, but the convergence to
H^{*}(K_{3})
is unknown and the relation between the
E_{\infty}
-pages was unclear, which is uncovered by Theorem
1.1
.

Recently, Marino-Salvatore
[
27
]
proposed a computer-aided proof of non-degeneracy of the Sinha sequence for
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{2}
using a combinatorial model of the cosimplicial space which is derived from the Fox-Neuwirth cell structure (see
[
26
]
). With this and Theorem
1.1
(2), we can disprove the conjecture of Vassiliev for the coefficient. This is very interesting in analogy with the affine subspace arrangements which homotopically split into a wedge sum of filtered quotients, and whose associated spectral sequence degenerates at any coefficient.

We shall look at application to the finite type invariants. A knot invariant
\pi_{0}(K_{3})\to\mathsf{k}
over a commutative ring
\mathsf{k}
is called
of (finite) type
n
if a natural extension of the invariant to singular knots vanishes on the singular knots with at least
n+1
double points (see e.g.
[
1
,
21
]
for the precise definition). This notion was introduced in
[
40
]
, see also Gusarov
[
17
]
and Birman-Lin
[
2
]
. While the completeness of the finite type invariants is still open, they include many known knot invariants. The relation between embedding calculus and finite type invariants has been extensively studied, see e.g. Volić
[
43
]
, Budney-Conant-Koytcheff-Sinha
[
7
]
and Kosanović
[
21
]
. The Vassiliev sequence is related to finite type invariants as follows. Let
\mathcal{V}_{n}
be the space of finite type
n
invariants over
\mathsf{k}
. The space
{}^{V}E_{\infty}^{-n,n}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{V}_{n}/\mathcal{V}_{n-1}
via the Alexander duality (see
[
2
]
, where
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Q}
is assumed, but the arguments there work for any coefficient). A feature of the finite type invariants over
\mathbb{Q}
is that they correspond to combinatorial objects called weight systems. The
space
W_{n}
of weight systems of weight
n
is defined as the dual of the
\mathsf{k}
-module generated by chord diagrams with
n
chords modulo
1T
and
4T
relations. The space
W_{n}
is isomorphic to
{}^{V}E_{1}^{-n,n}
so we have an inclusion
\mathcal{V}_{n}/\mathcal{V}_{n-1}\subset W_{n}
(see
[
1
]
). The correspondence in
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Q}
is a consequence of the above degeneracy of the diagonal part of the Vassiliev sequence.

For the diagonal part, we have the slightly finer version of Theorem
1.1
(2) as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
\mathsf{k}
is a principal ideal domain, we have
\mathcal{V}_{n}/\mathcal{V}_{n-1}=W_{n}
(under the inclusion of the previous paragraph) if and only if the diagonal part
\oplus_{i\leq 2n}{}^{S}\!E^{-i,i}_{r}
of the the Sinha spectral sequence (for
K_{3}
) up to the bidegree
(-2n,2n)
degenerates at
E_{2}
-page.

Combining this with an action of Grothendieck-Teichmüller group on
p
-completed little disks operads constructed by Boavida de Brito-Horel
[
4
,
3
]
we have part 1 of the following (see section
7
).

Corollary 1.4
.

(1)

Let
p
be a prime. For
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}\ or\ \mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have
\mathcal{V}_{n}/\mathcal{V}_{n-1}=W_{n}
if
n\leq p+2
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
and
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are the ring of
p

(2)

If
\mathsf{k}
is a principal ideal domain,
\mathcal{V}_{n}/\mathcal{V}_{n-1}=W_{n}
for
n\leq 5
. If
\mathsf{k}=\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{V}_{6}/\mathcal{V}_{5}=W_{6}
.

{}^{S}\!E_{2}^{-i,i+1}
is free for

by Turchin’s calculation
[

. So part 2 of the corollary follows from the rational collapse of the Sinha sequence and the universal coefficient theorem.

Our results are cohomological. On the other hand, there are
homotopical
studies on the finite type invariants and embedding calculus. Kosanović
[
21
]
proved that the
additive
finite type invariants correspond to the indecomposable Feynman diagrams if the homotopy embedding calculus spectral sequence degenerates at
E_{2}
(among other things), and using this, Boavida de Brito-Horel
[
4
]
proved
a result similar to Corollary
1.4
(1) for the additive ones (see section
7
for the comparison). The mentioned result of Marino-Salvatore suggests possiblity of non-degeneracy of the diagonal part of the Sinha sequence. Recently, the method of computation of the Sinha sequence has been developed (see also
[
31
]
). Since Theorem
1.3
contains ‘only if’ statement whose homotopical counterpart is unknown, it might be interesting to examine the relation between the finite type invariants and weight systems in positive characteristics.

We shall explain the outline of the proof of Theorem
1.1
. In
[
31
]
, the author constructed a Thom space model which is stably equivalent to the Sinha cosimplicial model, (or more precisely, punctured knot model) by applying Atiyah duality to the configuration spaces to transfer the embedding calculus structure to the Thom spaces over fat diagonals of products of disks and use this model to compute some differentials of the Sinha sequence of codimension one. The Vassiliev sequence is defined via the Alexander duality, so both of the Thom space model and the sequence sit at the opposite side of the knot space in the similar dualities and
actually, they look alike.
A resolution of the Thom space model consists of subspaces labeled with graphs. These subspaces are Thom spaces of the diagonals which equate the components of the products corresponding to the vertices in a common connected component of the labeling graph. Vassiliev’s simplicial resolution (or resolvent) consists of pairs of a graph and a singular knot which may have multiple point(s), and can be considered as an element of a diagonal subspace of the product
(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{\mathbb{R}}
. So by evaluating the singular ones in the resolvent at multiple points, we have a natural map from the resolvent to the geometric realization of the resolution of the Thom space model, which is expected to induce an isomorphism of the spectral sequences. The shift of bidegree will occur by change of a filtration. This idea of proof is very simple, but we encounter some technical problems, which makes the paper lengthy (see subsection
4.1
for further outline of the construction).

The construction of the isomorphism in Theorem
1.1
is mostly given at space-level so we expect a natural compatibility among this isomorphism and the maps between the genuine cohomology of the knot space
K_{3}
and the spectral sequences. We state such a possible relation as Conjecture
7.4
. This conjecture is interesting since it implies that the additive finite type tower and embedding calculus
\pi_{0}
tower have isomorphic inverse limits.

The convergence of the Vassiliev and Sinha sequences are still largely unknown in dimension
3
. For example, in general, it is unclear whether the elements of the
E_{\infty}
-page give non-trivial elements of the genuine cohomology (except for the diagonal part, and see Sakai
[
32
]
for a related problem). This is related to another conjecture of Vassiliev concerning unstable versions of the Vassiliev sequence which are directly obtained from finite dimensional approximations of the space of (singular) knots. As a byproduct of the construction of the isomorphism, we will give a partial computation of the differentials of the unstable sequences, see subsection
7.2
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In section
2
, we recall the punctured knot model which approximates the knot space in terms of embedding calculus. We also recall definition of the Vassiliev spectral sequence. It is defined using the resolvent of (finite dimensional approximations of) the space of singular knots. We also give a variant of the resolvent which is more suitable for our purpose. Most of the contents of this section are not essentially new. In section
3
, we replace the punctured knot model in the embedding calculus with the Thom space model which is a functor consisting of Thom spaces of fat diagonals. This model is similar to the models given in
[
30
,
31
]
but it is different from the one in
[
31
]
, in that we take tangent vectors into account here, and also different from the one in
[
30
]
, in that we consider tangent vectors
v
with
0<|v|<1
instead of the unit vectors and that we involve the boundary
|v|=0,1
in the duality here. We need point-set level compatibility of structure maps so we take care about parameters such as the width of a regular neighborhood. We establish a chain-level equivalence between the punctured knot model and Thom space model via a configuration space model, and prove that a truncated version of the Sinha spectral sequence is isomorphic to the sequence associated to the Thom space model. The outline of the constructions in the section is similar to
[
31
]
but the proofs are somewhat more involved so we give detailed proofs for the most part. In sections
4
,
5
and
6
, we connect the variant of the resolvent and a resolution of the Thom space model by zigzag of maps which induce isomorphisms of spectral sequences. For an outline of these three sections, see subsection
4.1
. Most of the technical constructions and relatively long proofs of this paper are given in sections
3
,
4
, and
5
. In section
6
, we make minor adjustments. In the last section
7
, we give proofs of the results stated in Introduction, state a conjecture, and give a partial result on the unstable spectral sequence.

Definition 2.8
.

(1)

A

is a pair
(A,B)
where

is a possibly empty finite family of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of

each of which has cardinality

, and

is a possibly empty finite subset of
\mathbb{R}
. The

is such that

and

are empty. So a non-empty classed configuration is such that at least one of

and

is non-empty.

(2)

Let

be a classed configuration. A

of

is an element of the subset

(\cup_{a\in A}a)\cup B\quad\subset\quad\mathbb{R}.

We call the cardinality of this subset the

of

and denote by
|F|
so the cardinality counts a geometric point only once even if it belongs to both of
B
and an element of
A
. The
complexity

is given by

c(F)=|\cup_{a\in A}a|-|A|+|B|

(3)

Two classed configurations

are said to be

if there is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism

satisfying the following condition: The image of each element of

by

is equal to some element of
A_{2}
and the map

is a bijection

, and

also induces a bijection

.

Remark 2.9

A non-empty classed configuration
(A,B)
is an

-configuration in the sense of

for

To define the Vassiliev spectral sequence, we need finite dimensional approximations of the knot space. Let

and

is the standard inner product on

is a weak homotopy equivalence so in this section, we also call an element of

Definition 2.10

(1)

(2)

Let

(3)

the subspace of maps respecting

.

As

does to

for

For an odd number

or equivalently,

. We shall give a rigorous proof. Consider the case of

, the distance between

and

is
\geq

&\geq c^{v^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}-4\epsilon_{P}\\
&\geq c^{v}_{\alpha\beta}-2\epsilon_{P}/10-4\epsilon_{P}>d_{\alpha\beta}(Q,v).\end{split}

This inequality implies

\delta_{PQ}(y,w)=*
. Consider the other case

Secondly, we shall show the case of
\alpha=\beta

.

which means

Thirdly we consider the case of
\alpha_{1}\not=\beta_{1}
. Suppose that

\begin{split}|x^{\prime}_{\alpha}-x^{\prime}_{\beta}|\leq&|x^{\prime}_{\alpha}-x_{\alpha}|+|x_{\alpha}-x_{\beta}|+|x_{\beta}-x^{\prime}_{\beta}|\\

|\bar{x}_{\alpha_{1}}-x^{\prime}_{\alpha}|\leq(c^{v^{\prime}}_{\alpha_{1}}-c^{v^{\prime}}_{\alpha}).

\beta

\leq&(c^{v^{\prime}}_{\alpha_{1}}-c^{v^{\prime}}_{\alpha})+(c^{v^{\prime}}_{\alpha\beta}-\epsilon_{Q}+6\epsilon_{P})+(c^{v^{\prime}}_{\beta_{1}}-c^{v^{\prime}}_{\beta})\\

With the similar inequality for

\begin{split}|\bar{x}_{\alpha_{1}}-\bar{x}_{\beta_{1}}|\leq&|\bar{x}_{\alpha_{1}}-x^{\prime}_{\alpha}|+|x^{\prime}_{\alpha}-x^{\prime}_{\beta}|+|\bar{x}_{\beta_{1}}-x^{\prime}_{\beta}|\\

<&2n(\epsilon_{Q}+\bar{\epsilon}_{Q})+2\epsilon_{P}/10+(\bar{\epsilon}_{Q}+4\epsilon_{P})<\bar{\epsilon}_{P}.\end{split}

Thus, we have shown the claim.

We want to connect the spaces

Our space of polynomial maps

k

In the above example, suppose the two vertices are connected by an edge. When they are sufficiently close, the sliding operation is approximated by that of the limit graph by the effect of the homotopy. So the width of slide is not so small that the image by the evaluation map is included in a designated regular neighborhood of the diagonal corresponding to the graph with two vertices. Neverthless, it is included in the one corresponding to the limit graph (which has a loop). Because of this phenomenon, we define the target space of the evaluation map so that for a graph whose vertex set can be splitted into subsets such that the vertices in a common subset are much closer than the vertices in different subsets, the diagonal space associated to the graph is the one corresponding to the graph obtained by condensing the vertices in each set into one vertex. In the example, we agree that the two vertices are in a common subset if they are sufficiently close.

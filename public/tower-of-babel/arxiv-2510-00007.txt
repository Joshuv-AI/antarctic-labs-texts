On Graphical Partitions with Restricted Parts
Gilead Levy

Abstract
We study the distributions of parts in random integer partitions subject to general arithmetic restrictions. In particular, we enumerate restricted graphical partitions of an even integer n n and identify the conditions under which the fraction of graphical partitions, relative to all restricted partitions, is maximal. We prove that this maximal fraction is asymptotically O ( n − 1 / 2 ) O(n^{-1/2}) . Furthermore, for any set of arithmetic restrictions, we establish the existence of a minimal lower bound on the parts beyond which the influence of these restrictions on the fraction of graphical partitions becomes negligible; in this regime, the fraction depends primarily on the choice of this lower bound. We highlight a key example of partitions restricted to powers of 2, where the critical lower bound is found to be 1 2 ​ n log ⁡ 2 + O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \frac{1}{2}n^{\log 2}+O(\log n) .



1
Introduction

A partition of an integer is called graphical if it corresponds to a degree sequence of a simple graph. While graphical partitions and integer partitions with restricted parts - such as parts bounded by size or of certain form - have been studied extensively, graphical partitions with general arithmetic restrictions on their parts remain unexplored. Moreover, the combined effect of multiple restrictions acting simultaneously has not been investigated. In this article, we provide new insights into how these restrictions influence the fraction of partitions that are graphical, and identify when one restriction dominates others. Given their applications in network design, understanding these influences aids in constructing networks under specified constraints.

Let
\mu(i)
indicate the
i
-th smallest part a partition can have under the restrictions put on its parts. Furthermore, let
X_{k}
be the number of parts at least
k
in a random restricted partition, and
Y_{k}
the
k
-th largest part. In Section 2 we show that
X_{k},\mu(Y_{k})
have the same density distributions. Our result holds for general restrictions placed on the parts of the partitions. In Section 3 we enumerate the restricted graphical partitions of an even integer
n
and find a compact expression for the fraction of its partitions that are graphical. Additionally, we prove that for every arithmetic restriction placed on the parts of the partitions of an even integer
n
, there exists a critical lower bound of the parts,
l_{n}
, beyond which the influence of the restriction on the fraction of graphical partitions becomes negligible. In this case, the fraction is controlled mainly by the choice of the lower bound of the parts
l\geq l_{n}
, and we evaluate the critical lower bound,
l_{n}=\mu(\log n)-\log n
. In Section 4 we include concluding remarks.

Probability and partitions

Let
\mu:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real function satisfying
\mu(n)\in\mathbb{N}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we represent by
\mu(\mathbb{N})
the set of valid parts a partition may have under a corresponding restriction. By choosing the function
\mu
we may represent any restriction placed on the parts. Without loss of generality, we may focus only on functions
\mu
that are odd, invertible and differentiable, satisfying
|\mu(x)|\geq|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We may define
M
to be the set of all such functions.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
the set of all integer partitions with parts only from the set
\mu(\mathbb{N})
. Define a probability measure on
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
by,
\mathbb{P}_{q}(\lambda)=\frac{q^{|\lambda|}}{Z(q)}
, with
Z(q)=\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}}q^{|\lambda|}
, for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
, and some
0<q<1
. Notice that this model fits the distribution of partitions of an integer
n
, since it becomes a uniform distribution when conditioned on the event
|\lambda|=n
:

\mathbb{P}_{q}(\lambda:|\lambda|=n)=\frac{q^{n}/Z(q)}{\mathbb{P}_{q}(|\lambda|=n)}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(n)|}

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(n)
denotes the set of partitions of
n
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
.

Analogously to the work of Fristetd
[
4
]
, we define
Z_{m}(\lambda)
to be the number of parts equal to
m
in a partition
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. Since
\mathbb{P}_{q}
describes a Poisson process, we deduce
Z_{m}\sim Geom(q^{m})
. Hence,
\mathbb{E}(Z_{m})=\frac{q^{m}}{1-q^{m}}
, and the condition
|\lambda|=n
translates to:

n=\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mu(\mathbb{N})}m\mathbb{E}(Z_{m})=\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mu(\mathbb{N)}}\frac{mq^{m}}{1-q^{m}}.

(1)

From equation (
1
) we may solve for
q(\mu,n)
. For the case
\mu(\mathbb{N})=\mathbb{N}
, we may approximate the summation with an integral, yielding:

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{mq^{m}}{1-q^{m}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{u}{e^{u}-1}du\cdot\Big(\frac{1}{\log^{2}q}+O(1)\Big)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6\log^{2}q}+O(1).

Then, we deduce
q=\exp(-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6n}})
. Notice that in this case,
\log q
is just the radius of the contour in Szekeres circle method, he has used in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. Hence, we may denote
\alpha=-\log q
, for all
\mu\in M
, to mach the notation of Szekeres.

For other choices of
\mu
, the rhs of equation (
1
) decreases, then for all
\mu\in M
we have the following asymptotic inequality,
\alpha=-\log q\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6n}}
, where there is equality only for
\mu(\mathbb{N)=\mathbb{N}}
. There are more precise methods we could have used to evaluate
\alpha
and
q
, but this upper bound will suffice for the purpose of this article. Notice that
\alpha\to 0
in the limit
n\to\infty
, for all
\mu\in M
.

Now, let
Y_{1}
be a random variable that is equal to the largest part in a partition of
n
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. Then, its comulative distribution function satisfies the following for all
y>0
:

\begin{split}&\log\mathbb{P}_{q}(Y_{1}\leq y)=\log\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mu(\mathbb{N})\\
m>y\end{subarray}}(1-q^{m})=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mu(\mathbb{N})\\
m>y\end{subarray}}e^{-\alpha m}(1+O(e^{-2\alpha m}))\\
&=-\int_{\alpha y}^{\infty}e^{-x}d\mu^{-1}\Big(\frac{x}{\alpha}\Big)\cdot\big(1+O(\alpha)\big)=-\frac{e^{-\alpha y}}{\alpha\mu^{\prime}(\mu^{-1}(y))}\big(1+O(\alpha)\big).\end{split}

Here,
\mu^{-1}
is the inverse function of
\mu
, which exists from the definition of the set
M
, as well as its derivative. We have also used,
\log(1-x)=-x(1+O(x))
.

This result gives motivation to define a function
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
s.t. for constant
\alpha
, it satisfies
\eta(y)=\alpha y+\log\big(\alpha\mu^{\prime}(\mu^{-1}(y))\big)
, for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have,
\log\mathbb{P}_{q}(Y_{1}\leq y)=-e^{-\eta(y)}(1+O(\alpha))
, which approaches the Gumbell distribution in the limit of large
n
. Hence, one can deduce from Gumbell order statistics that the
k
-th largest part in a random partition of
n
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
satisfies:

\mathbb{P}_{q}\big(\eta(Y_{k})\leq y\big)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{-e^{-u}}e^{-ku}du

(2)

for all
y>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, analogous to the result of Fristedt.

We denote by a random variable
X_{k}
the number of parts greater than or equal to
k
in a partition of
n
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
. Fristedt showed that for the case
\mu(\mathbb{N})=\mathbb{N}
, the variables
X_{k},Y_{k}
have the same distributions for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. In his derivation, Fristedt took advantage of the fact that the parts of a partition are the multiplicities of its conjugate partitions, and thus deduced equivalence between the distributions of
X_{k}
and
Y_{k}
. However, if a partition is from the set
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
, for some function
\mu\in M
, it is not necessarily true for its conjugate partition. Thus, the equivalence between partitions and their conjugates does not hold in general, when applying restrictions on their parts, so we must evaluate the distributions of
X_{k}
in another way.

Note that
X_{1}
is the number of parts in the partition, we may deduce its distribution by evaluating the number of partitions from the set
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
of an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, which have at most
x\in\mathbb{N}
parts; we may denote it by
|\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(n,x)|
. This can be done by using the theory of generating functions and the saddle-point method, one may check that:

|\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(n,x)|=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C}z^{-n-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mu(\mathbb{N})\\
m>\mu(x)\end{subarray}}\frac{1-z^{m+\mu(x)}}{1-z^{m}}dz.

Where
C
is a circular contour on the complex plane that centers at the origin and has a radius of
q=e^{-\alpha}
. This result resembles that of Szekeres and the derivation is almost the same, so we skip it. according to the saddle-point method, it is enough to evaluate the logarithm of the integrand and its derivative in order to evaluate the integral. After substituting
z=e^{-\alpha+i\theta}
, we may denote the logarithm of the integrand by
G(\theta,n,x)
and obtain the following:

G(\theta,n,x)=\alpha n-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mu(\mathbb{N})\\
m>\mu(x)\end{subarray}}\log\big(1-e^{-\alpha m}\big)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mu(\mathbb{N})\\
m>\mu(x)\end{subarray}}\log\big(1-e^{-\alpha(m+\mu(x))}\big).

Each of these summations can be approximated by using the Taylor expansion of
\log(1-x)
, as we have already done above. For large
x
the second summation is negligible, then we may only approximate the first summation to obtain,
G(0,n,x)=-\frac{e^{-\alpha\mu(x)}}{\alpha\mu^{\prime}(x)}(1+O(\alpha))
. Here we have supposed that the number of partitions of
n
is dominated by large
x
, this is true for large enough
n
, s.t. a big fraction of its partitions have a large number of parts. It can be seen that the dependence of the derivatives
G^{\prime}(0,n,x),G^{\prime\prime}(0,n,x)
on
x
is negligible when
x
is large, then we find with a good approximation,
|\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(n,x)|\propto-\frac{e^{-\alpha\mu(x)}}{\alpha\mu^{\prime}(x)}(1+O(\alpha))
. We recall that
x
is the maximum number of parts in the partition, then
\mathbb{P}_{q}(X_{1}\leq x)\propto|\mathcal{P}_{\mu}(n,x)|
, and thus we conclude that the variable
\eta\circ\mu^{-1}(X_{1})
has a Gumbell distribution, similar to that of
\eta(Y_{1})
. So we also have for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\mathbb{P}_{q}\big(\eta\circ\mu^{-1}(X_{k})\leq x\big)\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-e^{-u}}e^{-ku}du.

(3)

Hence,
X_{k},Y_{k}
have different distributions when applying restrictions on the parts of the partitions for a fixed
n
. Since
\eta
is an invertible function we have:

Theorem 1

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\in M
. For all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
Y_{k}
the
k
-th largest part of a partition of
n
from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
and denote by
X_{k}
the number of parts greater than or equal to
k
. Then the density distributions of
X_{k},\mu(Y_{k})
become equal in the limit
n\to\infty
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{q}(X_{k}=m)}{\mathbb{P}_{q}(\mu(Y_{k})=m)}=1,\ \forall m,k\in\mathbb{N}

(4)

For example, when considering
\mu(\mathbb{N})=\{2^{n-1}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, the set
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
is the set of all binary partitions. Then according to Theorem
1
, for a large enough integer
n
, the variables
X_{k},2^{Y_{k}-1}
have approximately the same distribution for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, which means that the
k
-th largest part increases logarithmically to the number of parts
\geq k
.

3
Restricted Graphical Partitions

The first rank of a partition, first introduced by Dyson
[
2
]
, is the difference between the largest part of the partition and the number of its parts. The
k
-th rank of a partition, as defined by Atkin
[
1
]
, is the difference between the
k
-th largest part of the partition and the number of its parts that are greater than or equal to
k
. Then, if we denote it as a random variable
R_{k}
, we have
R_{k}=Y_{k}-X_{k}
. A partition is graphical if it is a degree sequence of a simple graph. Nash-Williams
[
5
]
proved that if
K
is the durfee square of a partition (the largest
k
s.t.
k\leq Y_{k}
) then the partition is graphical if and only if it sums up to an even integer and
\textstyle\sum_{l=1}^{k}R_{l}\leq-k
, for all
k\leq K
. To our knowledge, graphical partitions with restricted parts have not been enumerated.

It is worthwhile to mention the result of Esseen’s in Feller
[
3
]
Theorem 2, p. 554. Let
A_{k}
be independent variables such that:

\mathbb{E}(A_{k})=0,\ \ \mathbb{E}(A_{k}^{2})=\sigma_{k}^{2},\ \ \mathbb{E}(A_{k}^{3})=\rho_{k}<\infty.

Set
s_{n}^{2}=\sigma_{1}^{2}+\sigma_{2}^{2}...+\sigma_{n}^{2}
, and
r_{n}=\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+...+\rho_{n}
. Let us also denote by
F_{n}
the density distribution of
s_{n}^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{k}
, then we have:

|F_{n}(x)-\mathbf{N}(x)|\leq 6\frac{r_{n}}{s_{n}^{3}}.

(5)

Let
\mathcal{R}_{k}(r)
be the density distribution function of
R_{k}
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, one can check that the following holds, from equations (
2
) and (
3
):

\mathcal{R}_{k}(r)=\frac{1}{(k-1)!^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-e^{-\eta^{-1}(r+x)}-e^{-\mu\circ\eta^{-1}(x)}}e^{-k(\eta^{-1}(r+x)+\mu\circ\eta^{-1}(x))}J(r,x)dx,

where
J(r,x)=\frac{d}{dx}\eta^{-1}(r+x)\frac{d}{dx}\mu(\eta^{-1}(x))
is the Jacobian of the substitution
R_{k}=Y_{k}-X_{k}
. Then, by substituting
\eta^{-1}(r+x)\mapsto u
, we obtain the following:

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[R_{k}]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}r\mathcal{R}_{k}(r)dr\\
&=\frac{1}{(k-1)!^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\bigg[e^{-e^{-\mu\circ\eta^{-1}(x)}}e^{-k\mu\circ\eta^{-1}(x)}\frac{d}{dx}\mu(\eta^{-1}(x))\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}du\ (\eta^{-1}(u)-x)e^{-e^{-u}}e^{-ku}\bigg]\\
&=\frac{1}{(k-1)!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\eta^{\prime}\big]-\mu^{-1}\big(\eta(x)\big)\Big)e^{-e^{-x}}e^{-kx}dx=\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\eta^{-1}\big]-\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[\mu^{-1}\circ\eta\big].\end{split}

Here, the notation
\mathbb{E}_{k}\big[f\big]
denotes the mean of
f(X)
, where
f
is a real function and
X
is a random variable with a density distribution of
\frac{1}{(k-1)!}e^{-e^{-x}}e^{-kx}
.

In the same manner, one can check that:

\mathbb{E}\big[R_{k}^{2}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\big(\eta-\mu^{-1}\circ\eta\big)^{2}\Big],\ \ \ \mathbb{E}\big[R_{k}^{3}\big]=\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\big(\eta-\mu^{-1}\circ\eta\big)^{3}\Big].

(6)

In order to apply Esseen’s theory (
5
), we need to evaluate those mean values. Notice that all
\mu\in M
satisfies
\mu(x)=O(x)
, hence, one may easily check that,
\log\mu^{\prime}(\mu^{-1}(x))=\Omega(\log x)
. Then,
\eta(x)=\alpha x+\log(\alpha\mu^{\prime}(\mu^{-1}(x))\approx\alpha x
, and thus, for sufficiently large
x
,
\eta^{-1}(x)\approx\alpha^{-1}x
. Hence, the parameter from equation (
5
) satisfies:

s^{2}_{n}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{n}\alpha^{2}\mathbb{E}_{k}\Big[\big(x-\mu^{-1}\big)^{2}\Big]\approx n\alpha^{2}\mathbb{E}_{n}\Big[\big(x-\mu^{-1}\big)^{2}\Big],

In the same manner, we observe that
r_{n}\approx n\alpha^{3}\mathbb{E}_{n}\big[(x-\mu^{-1})^{3}\big]
, thus we have:

Theorem 2

Let
\mu\in M
. Then the fraction of partitions from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
of an even integer
n
that are graphical is:

O\Bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}_{n}\big[(x-\mu^{-1})^{3}\big]}{\mathbb{E}^{3/2}_{n}\big[(x-\mu^{-1})^{2}\big]}n^{-1/2}\Bigg).

(7)

For example, we may restrict partitions to have only parts divisible by
m
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, then let
\mu(x)=mx,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
. So after evaluating the mean values, we deduce from Theorem
2
that the fraction of partitions with parts differentiable by
m
of an even integer
n
that are graphical is
O(n^{-1/2})
, in agreement with Rousseau and Ali
[
6
]
. Notice that the dependence on
m
vanished due to the division of the expected values. Hence, we have:

Theorem 3

The fraction of graphical partitions of an even integer
n
decreases most slowly with
n
when the restriction put on the parts is linear, That is, the fraction is maximal when
\mu
is linear. In that case, the fraction is
O(n^{-1/2})
.

Furthermore, we notice that the fraction of graphical partitions is not sensitive to restrictions on small parts. For example, let
\mu\in M
s.t.
\mu^{-1}(x)=x(1-e^{-x/10})
for all
x>0
, then we have with a good approximation
\mu(x)\approx x
for
x\geq 55
(
0.045\%
error). By evaluating numerically the expected values in expression (
7
), we verify that the fraction of graphical partitions out of
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
is
O(n^{-1/2})
, the same as if there were no restrictions at all. Thus, we deduce that most of the graphical partitions do not have any small parts, that is, parts smaller than
55
. Notice that this result contradicts equation (
4
), since we have
X_{k}=n
for all
k\leq 55
, but
\mu(Y_{k})\approx Y_{k}
cannot be equal to
n
, since it is only part of the partition. Hence, Theorem
1
does not apply after restricting the partitions to be graphical.

It is natural to question what the minimal lower bound that is needed to be put on the parts of the partitions from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
such that the fraction of them being graphical is dominated by it, and the influence of the other restrictions becomes negligible.

In order to answer this query, we notice that the density distribution function
\frac{1}{(n-1)!}e^{-e^{-x}}e^{-nx}
has a maximum at
x_{max}=-\log n
, and in the limit
n\to\infty
it becomes the Dirac delta function centered at that point. Notice that applying a lower bound to the parts of the partitions is equivalent to shifting the function
\mu
horizontally by this lower bound. We may evaluate this minimal lower bound, denoted by
l_{n}
, by setting the fraction of graphical partitions equal to zero, as an approximation. Then, according to that approximation, we have:

\mathbb{E}_{n}\Big[\big(x-\mu^{-1}(x-l_{n})\big)^{3}\Big]\approx\big(x-\mu^{-1}(x-l_{n})\big)^{3}\Big|_{x=-\log n}=0,

and thus,

l_{n}=\mu(\log n)-\log n.

(8)

This result holds up to an error that comes from the approximations mentioned above, and becomes more accurate as
n
is larger. Hence, we conclude:

Theorem 4

Let
\mu\in M
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be an even integer. Then the minimal lower bound
l_{n}
needed to be put on the parts of the partitions from
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
, for which it dominates the asymptotic behavior of the fraction of graphical partitions is approximately:

l_{n}=\mu(\log n)-\log n.

We get an elegant result when
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
is the set of all binary partitions,
\mu(n)=2^{n-1},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
, in that case the minimal lower bound is:

l_{n}=\frac{n^{\log 2}}{2}+O(\log n).

4
Concluding Remarks

We have found a compact form for the proportion of the graphical partitions relative to the number of restricted partitions of an even integer, written in (
7
). However, this expression involves computing expected values, and it is worthwhile to ask for a more elegant closed form that still holds for general restrictions.

Furthermore, we saw that there exists a critical value for the lower bound put on the parts, that beyond it the influence of the other restrictions on the fraction of graphical partitions becomes negligible. This is only one example of the interactions between restrictions placed on the parts of graphical partitions. It is worthwhile to investigate the interactions between other types of restrictions, and thus have a better understanding of the behavior of graphical partitions.

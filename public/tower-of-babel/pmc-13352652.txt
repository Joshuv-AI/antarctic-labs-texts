Evaluating expert opinions on using a virtual patient app based on artificial intelligence for teaching brief smoking cessation advice to medical students: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Eugenia-Corina Budin, Florin Stoica-Buracinschi, Edith Simona Ianoși, Marius-Ștefan Mărușteri, Laszlo Barna Iantovics, Valentin Nădășan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSmoking is one of the main preventable causes of premature disease and death. Anti-smoking counseling continues to be a challenge to cover the educational gap in this topic. Artificial intelligence and virtual-patient technologies are a promising next step in this evolving context, allowing for realistic, adaptive, and standardized simulations of clinical counseling scenarios. The objective of this study was to evaluate the perceptions and acceptance of a Generative Artificial Intelligence-based Virtual Patient Application for training medical students in brief smoking-cessation counseling among respiratory medicine professionals in Romania. MethodsA cross-sectional survey, primarily descriptive, with additional analytical components to explore associations between key variables was conducted between May and October 2025 among respiratory medicine professionals in Romania. A structured, validated electronic questionnaire assessed perceived usefulness, feasibility, preferred counseling models, interface design, and expected educational and public health benefits of the GenAI-ViP application. Descriptive statistics summarized participant responses, while inferential analyses included Mann–Whitney U, Kruskal–Wallis, Chi-square tests, Spearman correlations, and binary logistic regression to identify predictors of perceived usefulness. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05.ResultsA total of 147 clinicians were included, most of whom were pulmonologists (89.1%), and digital engagement was high (95.3% reporting regular use of medical applications). Most respondents rated the application as useful (81.6% Likert scores 4–5) and reported high receptiveness (91.1% Likert scores 4–5). The preferred interface included multimodal interaction with voice and video (65.3%). The 5 A counseling model was identified as the most relevant framework (80.3%), followed by motivational interviewing (37.4%). Significant predictors of perceived usefulness included higher perceived student receptiveness (OR 2.535, 95% CI 1.416–4.539, p = 0.002), while clinicians without prior smoking cessation program involvement reported greater perceived usefulness (OR 0.266, 95% CI 0.087–0.815, p = 0.020).ConclusionsThe Generative Artificial Intelligence-based Virtual Patient Application for smoking cessation training was highly accepted and perceived to be useful by respiratory medicine professionals and could address existing gaps in tobacco counseling education. Integration of such tools into undergraduate and postgraduate medical curricula could enhance smoking cessation competencies and support broader tobacco control strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12909-026-09435-w.Keywords: Generative Artificial Intelligence, Virtual Patient Application, Smoking cessation, Tobacco counseling, Professional education, Assessment of skills and attitudes

Introduction

Smoking is one of the main preventable causes of premature disease and death, with profound implications for cardiovascular, respiratory, reproductive, and mental health. In Eastern Europe, the transition from the communist system to the capitalist economy was associated with a significant increase in the prevalence of smoking, especially in females [1, 2]. Tobacco smoking was estimated to cause 29,500 deaths in 2021, including 6,200 deaths among females and 23,300 deaths among males, meaning 8.82% of all deaths that year, 3.87% among women, and 13.38% among men. According to the WHO Report [3], the adult current smoking prevalence in Romania was 34% in 2023 and 26% (4.1 million) in 2024. Thus, anti-smoking counseling continues to be a challenge to cover the educational gap in this topic. Shin demonstrated that traditional reading methods, online video programs, and interactive case-based peer role-play were all effective in increasing intentions to deliver smoking cessation interventions, therefore enhancing the classic version of medical education among nursing students [4]. Different types of education are associated with different effectiveness, aspects that also differ depending on the population involved (medical students, nurses, specialist doctors). Digital education has become more and more recognized as an effective and flexible way to develop clinical and communication skills among health professionals [5].

Compared to traditional training, digital formats provide important advantages such as flexibility, accessibility, and trustworthiness in learning opportunities. There is evidence that computer-based education is at least as effective as conventional instruction for educating health professionals on smoking cessation, while combining digital and in-person learning approaches may offer additional benefits in terms of learner engagement and outcomes [4, 5]. Artificial intelligence (AI) and virtual-patient technologies are a promising next step in this evolving context, allowing for realistic, adaptive, and standardized simulations of clinical counseling scenarios. Using these tools, learners can practice motivational interviewing and brief smoking cessation counseling regularly, receive automated feedback, and improve their communication skills with smokers. Despite their potential, there is a lack of evidence regarding the perceptions and acceptance of these AI-based educational tools among health professionals, particularly in Central and Eastern Europe [6]. The objective of this study was to evaluate the perceptions and acceptance of a Generative Artificial Intelligence-based Virtual Patient Application (GenAI-ViP) for training medical students in brief smoking-cessation counseling among respiratory medicine professionals in Romania.

Methods

This survey was conducted as a formative step within the GenAI-ViP project, an educational development initiative aimed at designing, piloting, and iteratively refining a generative artificial intelligence (GenAI)–supported virtual patient application for teaching brief smoking cessation advice to medical students and early-career physicians. The project follows a user-centered approach in which clinical experts inform the application’s instructional content (e.g., counseling models, scenario selection, feedback functions) and interface requirements before learner testing and outcome evaluation. A generative AI chatbot was selected as the core interaction engine because it can deliver scalable, standardized, and adaptive conversational simulations that approximate real clinical dialogue, allow repeated practice with diverse patient profiles, and support immediate, structured feedback—features that are difficult to ensure consistently through traditional role-play alone, particularly when faculty time and trained standardized patients are limited.

The study was cross-sectional and primarily descriptive, with additional analytical components to explore associations between key variables. It was conducted between May and October 2025 in Romania among respiratory medicine professionals. The study population was mainly represented by a convenience/voluntary response sample of pulmonologists, but it also included internal medicine doctors and other clinicians who provided smoking cessation counseling or medical education. The study was quantitative and consisted of administering an electronic questionnaire with pre-established answers to collect data on respondents’ perceptions of the efficiency, feasibility, and didactic needs associated with these applications. All eligible individuals who chose to participate during the data collection period were included. A total of 147 questionnaires were completed and included in the final analysis.

The study received approval from the Research Ethics Committee of the George Emil Palade University of Medicine, Pharmacy, Science and Technology of Târgu Mureș (Approval No. 3764/30.04.2025). The Romanian Society of Pulmonology (RSP) also approved the content of this questionnaire and its distribution to members. All participants electronically provided informed consent before completing the survey. The study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The questionnaire was administered electronically using the Fillout online platform (https://fillout.com), which ensures secure data encryption and anonymous responses. Before accessing the questionnaire, participants received a brief information sheet explaining the study’s purpose, confidentiality measures, and voluntary participation. No personal identifiers, Internet Protocol (IP), or login data were collected.

According to data from the Romanian College of Physicians, 983 pulmonologists are registered nationally. A total of 147 valid questionnaires were completed and included in the analysis, corresponding to a response rate of 14.95% relative to the national pulmonologist population.

Questionnaire development

The study instrument was a structured, pre-established answer questionnaire designed to assess physicians’ perceptions and acceptance of virtual patient applications intended for training medical students in brief smoking cessation counseling.

The initial pool of items was developed after a literature review on smoking-cessation training, incorporating international frameworks such as the 3 A (Ask–Advise–Act), 5 A (Ask–Advise–Assess–Assist–Arrange), motivational interviewing, and the transtheoretical model [7–10].

“Perceived usefulness” and “perceived receptiveness” were operationalized as single-item measures derived from individual questionnaire items, each rated on a 5 point Likert scale. No composite score was calculated for these variables.

Pre-testing and content validation of the questionnaire were completed by administering the survey to 14 experienced practitioners. All elements of the questionnaire were evaluated for relevance and clarity by each expert using simple ‘Yes/No’ assessments.

Most experts provided affirmative evaluations, confirming that the questions were both relevant and clearly formulated. Three demographic items (age, sex, and practice setting) were considered to have limited relevance by only one respondent. Consequently, the item on “practice setting (urban/rural)” was replaced with a question assessing academic teaching experience, as the questionnaire was designed for specialists, who mostly work in urban settings (“How long have you been involved in university-level teaching activities?” with five graded response options).

The research team did not implement any additional wording changes, as they agreed that the current formulations were adequate and unambiguous. Expert consensus confirmed a high level of content validity, indicating that the instrument is appropriate for use in the main study.

The final version of the questionnaire consisted of three sections: General data, including demographic and professional variables; Perception on training students in advising smokers using GenAI-ViP; and Models of counseling in the GenAI-ViP application (Supplementary Materials - Appendix I).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using R software version 4.5.2 (R Core Team, 2025) [11] within the Posit integrated development environment (Posit Software, PBC, Boston, MA) [12]. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of the data distribution. Descriptive statistics included frequencies and percentages for categorical variables, while medians and interquartile ranges (IQR) were reported for non-normally distributed ordinal data. Statistical comparisons were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test for independent samples, the Kruskal–Wallis test followed by Dunn’s post-hoc analysis for multiple group comparisons, and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ) for ordinal variables. Categorical associations were assessed using the Chi-square (χ²) test or Fisher’s exact test when expected cell frequencies were low. Additionally, binary logistic regression was conducted to identify independent predictors of perceived usefulness, with results presented as odds ratios (OR) and 95% Confidence Intervals (CI); a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant [13]. To assess the adequacy of the binary logistic regression model, we additionally examined overall model fit using the likelihood ratio test, calibration using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test, discrimination using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), multicollinearity using variance inflation factors (VIFs), and potentially influential observations using Cook’s distance, leverage values, and standardized residuals.

Results

A total of 147 respiratory medicine professionals participated in the survey. The most common age group was 30–39 years (36.7%), followed by 50–59 years (17.7%) and 40–49 years (17.0%). Females made up 84.4% of respondents. Pulmonology (89.1%) was the primary clinical specialty, with smaller percentages from internal medicine, family medicine, and other related fields.

Regarding academic experience, 63.9% stated that they had no prior university teaching experience, while 26.5% reported having between 1 and 20 years of experience, and 10.2% reported having more than 20 years of experience. Only 19.04% had ever participated in the “Stop Smoking” National Program, most for less than one year.

A proportion of 95.3% of the respondents reported using digital medical apps at least weekly, with mobile phones (83.7%) being the most common device used, followed by laptops (42.9%) and desktops (23.1%). Digital tools were perceived to be easy to use with a median score of 1 (IQR = 2) on a 1–5 Likert scale.

Discussion

This study examined the perceptions of respiratory medicine professionals toward the implementation of a virtual patient application with artificial intelligence (GenAI-ViP) for training medical students and young physicians in brief smoking cessation counseling. The findings indicate a high level of acceptability and perceived usefulness, suggesting that integrating digital simulation tools into medical education is both feasible and aligned with current professional expectations. The high receptiveness reported by respondents suggests that digital readiness within the respiratory community is a favorable basis for deploying innovative educational technologies.

The predominance of non-academic respondents who nevertheless expressed high acceptance of the digital training tool indicates that openness towards technology-supported education extends beyond university settings. Prior research has shown that perceived usefulness and digital literacy [14], rather than academic affiliation, are the main determinants of adoption of digital educational tools in health professions training [15]. Our data correspond to this pattern, as 95.3% of respondents reported using digital medical applications at least weekly and rated perceived difficulty of use as minimal. These characteristics strengthen the feasibility of integrating GenAI-ViP into diverse clinical training environments and align with previous systematic reviews showing that computer-based and web-based education are at least as effective as traditional learning in training health professionals to quit smoking [12–14]. Participants in our study, as in earlier reports, believed that enhanced artificial intelligence virtual scenarios were realistic, accessible, and effective for improving communication and motivational interviewing skills [14–17]. Additionally, the preference for interactive, mixed interfaces supports growing evidence that multimodal simulation environments boost learner engagement and clinical confidence [18, 19]. Therefore, in our study, a multimodal interface (video + voice + AI adaptive interaction) was the preferred choice and should be the main focus of future development.

Preferences reported by participants offer clear direction for the teaching design of the application. The strong preference for a multimodal interface (video + voice) is consistent with evidence indicating that realistic interactive environments enhance learner engagement and communication skills development [18, 19]. The 5 A and motivational interviewing frameworks are the core behavioral models that are prioritized, which correspond to international recommendations for tobacco cessation training and highlight the necessity for structured, evidence-based instructional content. The emphasis on a broad range of clinical scenarios, from young uninformed smokers to pregnant women and patients with chronic diseases, supports the development of progressive case complexity tailored to residents and early-career specialists, who were identified by respondents as the primary target groups. Since 90.47% of respondents identified young physicians as the main target group, future iterations of the application should incorporate progressive levels of difficulty to appropriately address their learning needs.

An innovative aspect of our study is the attempt to translate experience from the national Stop Smoking program into an academic training framework for medical students. Studies conducted both in Romania [20] and the rest of Europe [21] had as their main focus the effectiveness and benefits of public health, more than the educational component. The results from our study, in which only 19.04% were involved in the Stop Smoking program, show a significant shortage of practical training in tobacco counseling, a potentially increased interest in new educational tools, and the need for a training application to supplement the lack of practical experience. The result also suggests that the educational application can meet both the needs of beginners and serve as a standardization tool for experienced practitioners.

Our sample of pulmonologists who responded to the electronic survey has a predominantly active digital profile, which supports the feasibility of introducing such an application and suggests a high probability of acceptance and use, indicating that digital literacy may be an important predictor of adoption. Since the most responsive age group is 30–49, it is evident that the application should focus primarily on continuing education for residents and young specialists.

Multiple predictors, such as perceived usefulness, digital literacy, and previous exposure to technology-enhanced learning environments, influence the acceptance of AI-based educational tools [15, 22]. Identification of predictors for application acceptance in the present study has demonstrated that student receptivity is a strong predictor (p = 0.001), and involvement in the Stop Smoking program is associated with lower chances of declaring the application „useful/very useful” (OR = 0.257; p = 0.019), consistent with the idea that practical experience reduces the perceived need for a new tool.

From a public health perspective, integrating a virtual patient application into medical education can contribute to national tobacco control strategies by standardizing counseling competencies and extending access to high-quality training regardless of geographic or institutional constraints [23]. The observed preference for automated feedback and adaptive learning functions further supports GenAI-ViP's alignment with the principles of competency-based medical education. The inclusion of structured digital simulation modules in postgraduate and undergraduate respiratory curricula is advocated for, especially in regions that lack formal smoking cessation training.

Strengths and limitations

The use of a homogeneous, expert sample of pulmonologists and respiratory educators provides strong content validity and constitutes a key strength of the study. The high specificity of the sample for the proposed intervention, with pulmonologists accounting for 89.11%, suggests a more relevant data set. Expert review indicated good clarity and relevance across items. The very high perceived utility (mean 4.4; median 5/5) strongly supports the acceptability of the application. The difficulty of using applications is very low (median 1/5), meaning that there are no digital barriers. However, the cross-sectional and self-reported nature of the data limits causal interpretation, and the sample, although composed of relevant respiratory medicine professionals, was based on voluntary participation and was not probability-based; therefore, it should not be considered fully representative of the Romanian respiratory medicine community. It is recommended that future studies evaluate the actual learning outcomes, behavioral changes, and cost-effectiveness of artificial intelligence-based virtual patient training in comparison to traditional and blended approaches. Expanding collaboration across Central and Eastern European countries could also support regional harmonization of smoking-cessation education and integration of artificial intelligence-based virtual patient tools into national curricula.

A further limitation relates to the study instrument. The questionnaire was developed from the literature and underwent expert review for relevance and clarity; however, no formal psychometric testing was conducted beyond content validation. This lack of formal psychometric evaluation limits our capacity to confirm the questionnaire’s predictive ability. As a result, the instrument should be regarded as exploratory and project-specific, and the observed associations should be interpreted with some caution. Future work should examine additional psychometric properties, including internal consistency, construct validity, and temporal stability, particularly if the questionnaire is to be used in larger samples or across other settings.

In addition, we acknowledge that the use of a voluntary, convenience sample resulted in a relatively low response rate (15%). This introduces the potential for selection and non-response biases, which could confound the observed associations, as individuals who chose to participate may differ systematically from the broader population. Furthermore, while inferential analyses were included to explore patterns in the data, the modest sample size may have reduced the stability and precision of some estimated associations, particularly in the multivariable logistic model. Accordingly, these results are best viewed as preliminary, sample-specific findings that require confirmation in larger, more representative studies before broader clinical generalization.

Conclusions

The use of GenAI-ViP for training medical students in brief smoking cessation counseling has been highly accepted by respiratory medicine professionals according to this study. The perceived usefulness was highest among clinicians who have not previously worked with smoking cessation programs and those who regularly use digital tools, highlighting the importance of digital readiness and experiential learning gaps in shaping adoption. By offering accessible, standardized, and pedagogically consistent learning experiences, virtual patient simulations can address persistent deficiencies in tobacco cessation training. Their integration into medical curricula may strengthen the national capacity for delivering evidence-based smoking cessation counseling.

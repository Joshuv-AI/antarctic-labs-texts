Prophylaxis for venous thromboembolism in traumatic brain injury: protocol for a randomised controlled trial

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVenous thromboembolism (VTE) is a common complication of traumatic brain injury (TBI) and is associated with increased morbidity and mortality. Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) is recommended for prophylaxis against VTE after trauma but may increase the risk of progression of intracranial bleeding. Limited evidence exists to guide clinicians regarding the optimal timing of VTE prophylaxis in patients with acute TBI. This randomised controlled trial (RCT) will directly compare the safety and effectiveness of early versus delayed initiation of LMWH in patients with moderate to severe TBI.Methods and analysisThe study design is a Bayesian adaptive RCT comparing early (within three calendar days of injury) versus delayed (after study Day 7) VTE prophylaxis with the LMWH, dalteparin. All patients receive sequential compression devices until study Day 8. The co-primary effectiveness outcome is the development of clinically important VTE at study Day 8. The co-primary safety outcome is the development of clinically important intracranial bleeding at study Day 8. Secondary outcomes are mortality and functional outcomes (Glasgow Outcome Scale Extended and EQ-5D) measured at study Days 30 and 180; clinically diagnosed VTE to Day 30 and progression of intracranial bleeding to Day 8.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved through Clinical Trials Ontario’s streamlined ethics review process (board of record, Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre) and all participating centres. It is conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines and Health Canada regulatory requirements. We anticipate that the trial will achieve wide dissemination through publication in a peer-reviewed medical journal and presentation at international conferences targeting the fields of critical care, trauma and neurosurgery. The results of this trial will help guide clinicians aiming to balance the risks and benefits of early anticoagulant prophylaxis after TBI and will inform guideline development.Trial registration numberNCT03559114.

Introduction

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) disproportionately affects younger people and can have devastating consequences.1 2 In a Canadian population-based study, the estimated incidence of TBI-related hospitalisations was 73 per 100 000 population.3 Patients who survive the initial injury often have significant long-term disability, need prolonged hospitalisation and are at risk of subsequent morbidity.4 In particular, venous thromboembolism (VTE), including deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE), is a common and potentially fatal complication following severe TBI.58 Prolonged injury to the brain directly activates the coagulation system; in addition, prolonged immobility due to the injury itself, mechanical ventilation and sedatives contribute to VTE risk. Rates of VTE incidence after TBI range from 0% to 33% with preventative strategies, and up to 54% without prophylaxis.59

Patients with moderate to severe TBI are also at risk of progressive intracranial bleeding (ICB). ICB worsening occurs in 32%–50% of patients who have ICB after trauma, and progression most often occurs during the first 24 hours.1012 ICB progression has been associated with worse clinical outcomes,1012 and it is therefore logical to strive to decrease the risk of haematoma expansion in these patients. ICB expansion may be more prevalent in patients receiving anticoagulation thromboprophylaxis, although this association has been inconsistent in high quality studies.91113

Current guidelines recommend starting low molecular weight heparin (LMWH), such as dalteparin, within 24–48 hours of presentation in patients with TBI to prevent these venous thromboembolic complications (Brain Trauma Foundation Guidelines; Neurocritical Care Society Guidelines14 15). LMWHs are approved for ‘reduction of DVT in hospitalised patients with severely restricted mobility during acute illness’ and have been proven to be effective for this indication in large trials of critically ill patients.16 17 However, LMWHs may also increase the risk of worsening ICB after TBI, though estimates of this incremental risk have varied across studies.1820 Another approach that has been promoted by guidelines is to use sequential compression devices (SCDs) that stimulate normal venous return from the lower extremities as mechanical prophylaxis against VTE.14 15 21 However, it remains unknown whether the use of SCDs alone reduces the risk of VTE sufficiently to justify avoiding treatment with LMWH during the first week after injury.

The PROphylaxis for venous ThromboEmbolism in Severe Traumatic brain injury (PROTEST) randomised controlled trial (RCT) evaluates these two different approaches for preventing VTE in patients with severe TBI during the first week after injury: (1) prophylactic therapy with an LMWH (dalteparin) plus SCDs versus (2) using SCDs alone (no LMWH). The RCT will determine whether LWMH combined with SCDs is superior for reducing rates of VTE compared with SCDs alone; and whether LMWH combined with SCDs is non-inferior to SCDs alone at reducing rates of worsening ICB after TBI. Overall, this trial aims to identify the optimal approach to thromboprophylaxis during the critical first week after significant TBI.

Methods and analysis

Primary study objective

This RCT will have two co-primary outcome measures that evaluate (1) the effectiveness (clinically important VTE) and (2) safety (clinically important ICB progression) of two VTE thromboprophylaxis strategies during the first week after TBI: SCDs plus study drug (dalteparin) versus SCDs plus placebo for 7 days. The study drug or placebo will be started on study Day 1 and administered daily until study Day 7 or intensive care unit (ICU) discharge or death, whichever occurs first.

Secondary study objectives

The secondary objectives evaluate whether differences exist between treatment arms on progression of ICB to Day 8; new clinically diagnosed VTE between Day 8 and Day 30; long-term functional outcomes measured using the Glasgow Outcome Scale Extended (GOSE) and EQ-5D, both at Days 30 and 180; and mortality at Days 8, 30 and 180.

Research questions

Primary effectiveness question

Among patients with significant TBI, does anticoagulant VTE thromboprophylaxis administered during the first week plus the use of SCDs reduce the incidence of clinically important VTE measured at study Day 8, compared with the use of SCDs alone?

Primary safety question

Among patients with significant TBI, does anticoagulant VTE thromboprophylaxis administered during the first week plus the use of SCDs increase the incidence of clinically important ICB measured at study Day 8, compared with the use of SCDs alone?

Study design

This is a double-blind, Bayesian sequential adaptive, RCT. Each participant will have daily data collection during the 7-day intervention period; primary outcome and secondary outcome ascertainment as an inpatient at study Day 8 (−1, +2 days) and follow-up at 30 (±5 days) and 180 (±14 days) for secondary outcome measures. The full trial incorporates patients and outcomes from a 50-patient vanguard (pilot) phase.

Setting

This RCT is conducted in multiple large trauma centres that treat patients with TBI.

Trial status

At the time of manuscript submission, the trial is actively recruiting patients or planning to recruit patients in hospitals in Canada, India and Colombia. The start date of the trial was 19 July 2018, and enrolment will continue until an adaptive analysis triggers a decision to stop the trial or the maximum feasibility sample size is reached, anticipated in December 2026.

Interventions

This pragmatic RCT will directly compare the effectiveness and safety of two VTE thromboprophylaxis strategies during the critical first week after severe TBI: SCDs plus study drug (dalteparin) versus SCDs plus placebo for 7 days. Participants who are admitted to participating ICUs after traumatic injury will be screened for eligibility and randomised within three calendar days after injury. SCDs will be applied according to the manufacturer’s instructions and within 12 hours of randomisation for all participants and then worn continuously until the study Day 8 outcome evaluation unless the patient is fully mobile or develops a complication related to their use. The investigational product will be 5000 international units (IU) of dalteparin or 0.9% saline solution injected subcutaneously daily for 7 days (to study Day 7) or ICU discharge. The study drug should not be administered in the first 24 hours after injury. Patients discharged from ICU prior to Day 7 will still receive all outcome assessments.

Enrolled participants will be randomly allocated to one of the following two groups:

Group 1: SCD plus LMWH

SCDs will be applied according to usual practice. A prophylactic dose of 5000 IU once a day of dalteparin delivered subcutaneously will be administered daily if safety criteria are satisfied. The dalteparin will be prepared by the site pharmacy and supplied in sealed syringes.

Group 2: SCD plus placebo

SCDs will be applied according to usual practice. A placebo subcutaneous injection will be administered daily if safety criteria are satisfied. This placebo contains colourless normal saline, prepared by the inpatient pharmacy and supplied in sealed syringes.

Procedures common to both groups

Patients in both groups receive SCDs.

Choice of investigational product

Dalteparin has been studied in the largest RCT of thromboprophylaxis conducted to date in critically ill patients.16 The mechanism of action, indicated uses and risk profile of dalteparin are outlined in the product monograph.17 Dalteparin has been shown to be safe in patients with renal insufficiency.22 23

Choice of outcome timing

We chose 8 days after investigational product initiation to measure the co-primary outcomes in this RCT for the following reasons: (1) the risk of incident VTE is highest during this acute phase of TBI5; (2) the risk of ICB progression peaks at 24–48 hours and decreases thereafter until approximately 7 days10 and (3) our survey of Canadian neurosurgeons and neurointensivists found that >80% would be comfortable starting LWMH beyond 7 days.24 To increase compliance with study procedures, imaging studies (Doppler ultrasound and CT scans) can be performed on Day 8 −1 or +2 days (at Day 7, 8, 9 or 10).

Adjudication: all co-primary outcome events will be adjudicated by three investigators who are blinded to group allocation.

ICB volume measures

Routine head CT images obtained to Day 8 −1 or +2 days (at Day 7, 8, 9 or 10) will be anonymised and analysed at Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre (SHSC) by investigators blinded to treatment allocation. We will determine ICB haemorrhage volume using the Brain Lesion Analysis and Segmentation Tool for CT, a deep learning-based method using convolutional neural networks for multiclass, voxel-wise segmentation and quantification of TBI lesions on CT.27

Study schedule and duration

Outcome measures will be assessed on study Day 8 −1 or +2 (online supplemental appendix table 1.0). If either clinically important VTE or ICB is detected prior to study Day 8, all outcomes will still be measured but no further administration of the study medication will occur, and patients will be managed according to the discretion of the most responsible physician. Long-term secondary outcomes will be collected at 30±5 and 180±14 days. A follow-up phone interview will be conducted with the patient or the substitute decision maker (SDM) if the patient is no longer admitted to the primary hospital. These phone interviews will use the GOSE and the EQ-5D. All the data used in the secondary analyses will be de-identified.

Protocol deviations and safety monitoring

The approach to monitoring safety and protocol deviations is outlined in online supplemental file 1.

Screening and participant recruitment

A daily review of trauma admissions to the emergency department or the ICU will be done by the study coordinator to identify potentially eligible participants. A delegated study investigator will review all participants deemed eligible.

Randomisation and blinding

Participants will be randomised to each study arm using a 1:1 ratio and random variable block sizes to avoid substantial imbalance in the number of participants. Randomisation will be stratified based on severity of TBI (GCS <5, GCS 5–8, GCS 9–12) and by centre. The randomisation sequence will be generated using a web-based random number assignment for allocation to groups. The sequence will remain concealed during the enrolment of new participants. Each site coordinator will use the electronic interface when an eligible participant is screened to perform the randomisation procedure. Each randomised participant will be assigned a unique study identification number. A master log linking each study identification number and individual participant information will be maintained at each participating site, but only de-identified data will be transferred into the randomisation system. The day of randomisation will be considered study Day 0 unless randomisation occurs on calendar Day 3 after injury, in which case the day of randomisation will be considered study Day 1. Participants can be randomised on the third calendar day after injury, if the study drug can be administered on that same day.

After receiving treatment allocation information generated from the central web-based randomisation system, an unblinded pharmacist will dispense the drug or placebo in sealed opaque syringes to mask treatment allocations. Participants, care providers and the research team will be blinded to treatment allocation. The ultrasonographers performing venous ultrasound for VTE outcome measures and radiologists reviewing scans for progressive ICB outcomes will also be blinded to treatment group. The study blind may only be broken if the safety of the participant is at risk and the treatment plan relies on knowledge of study allocation, with the permission of a principal investigator.

Statistical analysis plan

Study hypotheses

Primary effectiveness null hypothesis

There is no difference in effectiveness of VTE prophylaxis as defined by rates of clinically important VTE before Day 8 between the placebo and dalteparin groups.

Primary safety null hypothesis

The dalteparin group is inferior to the placebo group by a prespecified margin in rates of clinically important ICB progression before Day 8.

Secondary null hypotheses

No differences between groups for 30-day outcome measures of mortality or VTE incidence. No differences between groups for 30-day or 180-day measures of quality of life (EQ-5D) or functional status (GOSE).

Vanguard phase and full trial

The initial vanguard phase (completed) enrolled 50 participants over a 1-year period using the same study design as the full trial; these patients will be included in the full trial analysis. The purpose of the vanguard phase was to demonstrate feasibility; no analyses of clinical outcomes were performed.

Interim analyses and sample size

The PROTEST trial will be conducted and analysed using a Bayesian adaptive trial framework. Based on the results of our systematic review and observational study, we estimated a baseline rate for VTE in severe TBI while using SCDs to be approximately 14% at 7 days.9 We estimated a baseline rate of ICB progression during the first 7 days to be about 14% overall in the absence of anticoagulant prophylaxis, and that about half (7%) of these situations would be clinically important.11 Based on estimates using a frequentist sample size calculation prior to the start of the trial, we estimated about 1100 patients would be required to show efficacy for the co-primary outcome of VTE reduction (40% reduction, from 14% to 9%, two-sided alpha 0.05, ≥80%) and safety for the co-primary outcome of clinically important ICB progression (non-inferiority with 90% CI, baseline 7%, margin of error <4%) and accounting for up to 8% loss to follow-up. Simulations using the Bayesian sequential adaptive analyses described below suggest a median sample size of 570–670 will be required, with enrolment continuing until a stopping rule is triggered or the maximal feasibility sample size of 1100 (representing the funding limit) is reached.

Stopping rules

The first of the sequential adaptive analyses will be conducted starting with 376 randomised patients (ie, one-third of the maximal feasibility sample size) and will be repeated after each additional 100 patients are recruited. At any adaptive analysis, the scenarios and thresholds for stopping shown below will be evaluated considering each of the co-primary outcomes. These will be evaluated by the data safety monitoring committee (DSMC) and steering committee in a sequential manner, that is, first considering Scenario 1, and if stopping thresholds are not satisfied then considering Scenario 2, and if stopping thresholds are not satisfied, then considering Scenario 3. If no thresholds for stopping are satisfied across each scenario, then enrolment will continue until the maximal feasibility sample size.

Scenario 1 (harm): trigger based on greater rates of clinically important ICB progression

The DSMC will consider recommending that the trial be stopped if there is greater than 90% posterior probability that early anticoagulant prophylaxis will lead to clinically important ICB progression: stop if probability of OR for ICB progression (Pr(OR ICB progression>1.4)>90%).

Scenario 2 (benefit without harm): trigger based on VTE reduction but no clinically important ICB progression

The DSMC will consider recommending that the trial be stopped if there is convincing evidence that VTE reduction is achieved with early anticoagulant prophylaxis without an associated increase in clinically important ICB progression. The thresholds for this trigger are established to ensure that there will be acceptable credibility that ICB progression has not occurred: if there is greater than 90% posterior probability that VTE reduction is achieved without important ICB progression: stop if Pr(OR VTE<1.0)>90% and Pr(OR ICB<1.5)>90%.

Scenario 3 (futility): trigger based on futility for showing differences in rates of VTE

The DSMC will consider recommending that the trial be stopped if it is unlikely to demonstrate a meaningful reduction in VTE rates between groups. Rates of clinically important ICB progression will not be used to establish futility, as there would be no reason to use early VTE prophylaxis if this intervention was not associated with a reduction in VTE.

The DSMC may decide to terminate the study if the posterior probability that early thromboprophylaxis fails to significantly reduce VTE exceeds 90% (Pr(OR VTE reduction>0.8)>90%). If stopping thresholds are not reached across each of Scenarios 1–3, then enrolment will continue until the maximal feasibility sample size, estimated to be 1100 patients or until funding is no longer available. Based on our simulations using the stopping rules scenarios, we would stop for one of the three scenarios above in about 80% of the simulations.

Final analysis plan

The primary modified intention-to-treat analysis will evaluate only patients who have received at least one dose of the investigational product. We will analyse the binary co-primary outcomes using Bayesian logistic regression with weakly informative priors, adjusting for stratification factors (initial GCS category, study centre using random effects). We will report the posterior probabilities of treatment effect and the OR with 95% credible intervals (CrI) for each outcome. Differences between groups in ICB volume at Day 8 will be analysed using analysis of covariance by adjusting for the baseline ICB volume and the difference in means with 95% CrIs will be presented. GOSE (ordinal) will be analysed using Bayesian ordinal logistic regression and presented as ORs with 95% CrI. EQ-5D (continuous) will be analysed with Bayesian linear regression, reporting mean differences and 95% CrIs. Secondary analyses will adjust for the impact of potential confounders (in addition to stratification factors) on each of the co-primary and selected secondary outcomes as follows: for VTE: age, sex, bodily injuries (eg, spine fractures, lower extremity fractures), prior history of VTE, history of cancer; for ICB: age, sex, use of tranexamic acid, initial INR, platelet count; for GOSE and mortality: sex, TBI-IMPACT prognosis score (age, GCS motor, Marshall score, subarachnoid or epidural haemorrhage, initial haemoglobin). Prespecified subgroup analyses will be performed considering initial GCS category; presence versus absence of extracranial injuries; craniotomy/craniectomy versus not; and ICP monitor placement versus not.

Data collection and management

Each centre will keep a screening log and track patients meeting inclusion criteria to examine reasons for non-enrolment of eligible patients. Local research coordinators will randomise patients using the web-based randomisation tool; will abstract data daily during the 7-day intervention period; and perform follow-up phone interviews to ascertain outcomes at 30 and 180 days (eg, for GOSE and EQ-5D). Data will be entered into a password-protected REDCap database28 29 that is backed up daily. The study electronic case report form will be the primary data collection instrument for the study. Images of all brain CT scans up to Day 8 will be transferred to the coordinating centre. Measures to ensure data quality include training sessions for local research coordinators/outcome assessors prior to study commencement and a detailed trial protocol with clear instructions for data handling and record keeping.

Research governance

The steering committee is comprised of the principal and co-investigators and will meet regularly to review study progress, adherence to protocol and operational issues but will not have access to the accumulating study events. The independent DSMC will be comprised of two clinician scientists with content expertise in the field of TBI and an independent statistician. The DSMC charter is available on request.

Access to data

The trial statistician will have full access to the study dataset but will be kept blinded until final unlocking of the randomised allocation. The full study dataset will be stored at Centre for Clinical Trial Support. The principal investigators will have responsibility for assessing requests to access study datasets.

Patient and public involvement

We have built our study on a highly integrated knowledge translation foundation by including as collaborators neurosurgeons and neurointensivists who will be end-users of our trial results. The interventions and protocol were developed in collaboration with these collaborators. These individuals will also be involved in interpreting the research findings, to ensure our results are relevant to other stakeholders and health systems. A patient representative has been recruited to help with interpretation and reporting of the results. We plan to circulate a clear message to practitioners, target institutions and the public about our findings regarding the effectiveness of and safety of early versus delayed initiation of anticoagulant thromboprophylaxis while also using SCDs to prevent VTE.

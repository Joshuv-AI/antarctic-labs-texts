On the Cryptographic Futility of Non-Collapsing Measurements
Alper Çakan Dakshita Khurana Tomoyuki Morimae Yuki Shirakawa ‡ Kabir Tomer Takashi Yamakawa

Abstract
We investigate quantum analogues of collision resistance and obtain separations between quantum “one-way” and “collision-resistant” primitives. • Our first result studies one-wayness versus collision-resistance defined over quantum circuits that output classical strings. We show that there is a classical oracle 𝒪 \mathcal{O} relative to which (sub-exponentially secure) indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way permutations exist even against adversaries that make quantum queries to a non-collapsing measurement oracle, 𝒬 𝒪 \mathcal{Q}^{\mathcal{O}} . Very roughly, 𝒬 𝒪 \mathcal{Q}^{\mathcal{O}} outputs the result of multiple non-collapsing measurements on the output of any quantum 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -aided circuit. This rules out fully black-box quantum constructions of Y Y from X X for any X ∈ { X\in\{ indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way permutations, public-key encryption, deniable encryption, oblivious transfer, non-interactive ZK, trapdoor permutations, quantum money } , Y ∈ { \},Y\in\{ collision-resistant hash functions, hard problems in SZK, homomorphic encryption, distributional collision-resistant puzzles } \} . • Our second result studies one-wayness versus collision-resistance defined over quantum states. Here, we show that relative to the same classical oracle 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , (sub-exponentially secure) indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way permutations exist even against adversaries that make quantum queries to a cloning unitary 𝖰𝖢𝗈𝗅 𝒪 \mathsf{QCol}^{\mathcal{O}} . Very roughly, this latter oracle implements a well-defined, linear operation to clone a subset of the qubits output by any quantum 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -aided circuit. This rules out fully black-box constructions of quantum lightning from public-key quantum money.



1
Introduction

The relationship between
one-wayness
and
collision resistance
is foundational in cryptography: while one-way functions are minimal for a wide range of cryptographic tasks
[
IL89
]
, collision-resistant hash functions are believed to be strictly stronger, and underpin advanced primitives such as “hash-and-sign” digital signatures
[
Mer79
]
, merkle trees
[
Mer79
]
and two-round statistically-hiding commitments
[
HM96
]
.
There is strong evidence for this belief, in the form of classical black-box separations between (indistinguishability obfuscation (iO) and) one-way functions or one-way permutations (OWPs), and collision-resistant hash functions
[
Sim98
,
AS15
,
BD19
]
. These qualitative separations have guided the design and analysis of cryptosystems for decades. However, with the advent of quantum computing, many classical assumptions and separations require re-evaluation.

Quantum Computable Collision-Resistant Hash Functions.

Quantum adversaries may exploit uniquely quantum resources, such as superposition queries, potentially voiding classical separations or creating new ones. In particular, defining and separating quantum analogues of collision resistance and one-wayness are essential both for understanding the foundations of quantum cryptography and for building secure quantum protocols.
While
[
HY24
]
showed that there is no quantum fully-black-box reduction from quantumly computable collision-resistant hash functions to OWPs or even trapdoor permutations,
their proof is unwieldy and fails to separate collision-resistance from iO, leaving it open to prove such a separation.

Cloning Resistance.

At the same time, quantum primitives such as quantum lightning exhibit collision-resistance-like properties, yet do not immediately yield collision-resistant hash functions. To appreciate this, it is helpful to start with the simpler notion of public-key quantum money
[
Wie83
,
AC12
]
. Here, a bank generates quantum banknotes that anyone can verify but no adversary can counterfeit. More precisely, given a valid banknote, it should be infeasible to produce another with the same serial number. This is best understood as a form of targeted cloning resistance: the challenge state that must be cloned is the specific one generated by the bank.

Quantum lightning
[
Zha21
]
strengthens this idea. Instead of restricting minting to the bank, anyone can generate a valid state along with a serial number, yet it remains infeasible to produce two states with the same serial number. This property corresponds to general cloning resistance, where the adversary wins if they clone
any
of the banknotes in the support of the sampler.

Despite the surface-level similarity with collision-resistant hash functions, constructing quantum lightning has proven incredibly challenging, and the only known construction
[
SZ25
]
uses powerful generic assumptions such as indistinguishability obfuscation along with concrete algebraic assumptions such as learning with errors.
On the other hand, “just” (post-quantum) iO and one-way functions suffice to build quantum money.
Then, given the relative ease of building quantum money, it is natural to ask whether one can separate quantum lightning from quantum money–alternatively, rule out constructions of quantum lightning from “just” (post-quantum) iO and OWPs.

1.1
Our Results

The primary contribution of this work is to formulate a unified theory of one-wayness versus collision-resistance and cloning-resistance for quantum primitives.
In doing so, we also explore connections with the complexity classes
\mathsf{PDQP}
(Product-Dynamic Quantum Polynomial Time)
[
ABFL16
]
and
\mathsf{SampPDQP}

[
MSY25
]
which relax
\mathsf{BQP}
to allow multiple, non-collapsing measurements during computation. We discuss these connections later in this section.

A Collision-Finding Oracle.

For our first separation, we introduce a random oracle
\mathcal{O}
and show that one-wayness of
\mathcal{O}
is preserved even given (quantum) queries to a quantum collision-finding oracle
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}}
.
For any oracle
\mathcal{O}
,
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}}
is a classical oracle
that, given a classical description of a quantum circuit
C^{\mathcal{O}}
with
\mathcal{O}
-gates
such that
C^{\mathcal{O}}\ket{0...0}=\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}
, generates the state

\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}^{\otimes 2}
, measures all qubits in the computational basis, and outputs the measurement result. (For the definition of
\mathsf{Col}
, see
Definition

6.4
.)

\mathsf{Col}
can break
distributional collision-resistant hashing (dCRH)
[
DI06
,
BHKY19
]

(and even its weaker quantum variant, distributional collision-resistant puzzles (dCRPuzzs)
[
MSY25
]
).

This also implies that
\mathsf{Col}
can decide

,
and thus break homomorphic encryption, as well as non-interactive computational private

We show the following.

There exists a classical oracle
\mathcal{O}
relative to which (sub-exponentially secure) indistinguishability obfuscation and (sub-exponentially secure) one-way permutations exist, even against adversaries equipped with quantum queries to
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}}
.

Furthermore, we show that relative to these oracles, one can actually build iO for oracle-aided circuits, thereby capturing existing techniques that combine iO with

and a variety of advanced primitives like deniable encryption

, non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs
[

and injective trapdoor functions
[

.
As such, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2 (Informal).

There does not exist a fully black-box construction of any
Y\in\{
collision-resistant hash functions, distributional collision-resistant puzzles

from

X\in\{
(indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way permutations), public-key encryption, deniable encryption, oblivious transfer, non-interactive ZK, trapdoor permutations, quantum money

One-shot signatures allow a signer holding a quantum signing key to sign a single message, only once, with respect to a classical verification key. Since one-shot signatures (black-box) imply dCRPuzzs
[

]
, the above corollary shows that their result cannot be improved to rely only on iO and OWPs (at least when using iO in a black-box way).

A Cloning Oracle.

Next, we investigate what it means to find collisions on
quantum states
themselves. Our motivation is to better understand “fully quantum” collision resistant primitives such as quantum lightning
[
Zha21
]
, which do not appear to imply classical or quantum computable collision resistant hashing, and do not seem to be broken by a
collision-finding oracle.
Recall that quantum lightning allows the public generation of quantum states, called “lightning bolts”, each associated with an efficiently verifiable serial number, and where it is infeasible for anyone (even the generator) to create two valid quantum states (lightning bolts) with the same serial number.

We introduce a cloning unitary oracle
\mathsf{QCol}
that given the description of a quantum circuit, measures part of its output and clones the remaining part. More formally,
\mathsf{QCol}^{\mathcal{O}}
is a unitary that given a circuit
C
, swaps
\ket{\bot}
(for some special symbol
\bot
) and
\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}^{\otimes 2}
, where
C^{\mathcal{O}}\ket{0...0}=\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}
.
(For the definition of
\mathsf{QCol}
, see
Definition

7.3
.)
In more detail, letting
\mathcal{O}
be the same set of oracles as before that enable one-wayness (and indistinguishability obfuscation), we also provide access to
\mathsf{QCol}^{\mathcal{O}}
.
The presence of
\mathsf{QCol}^{\mathcal{O}}
clearly breaks any quantum lightning scheme.
However, we show that (sub-exponentially secure) OWPs and (sub-exponentially secure) iO continue to exist even against adversaries that make quantum queries–indeed, even inverse, conjugate, or transpose queries–to
\mathsf{QCol}^{\mathcal{O}}
.

Theorem 1.3 (Informal).

There exists a classical oracle
\mathcal{O}
relative to which (sub-exponentially secure) indistinguishability obfuscation and (sub-exponentially secure) one-way permutations exist, even against adversaries equipped with quantum queries to the unitary
\mathsf{QCol}^{\mathcal{O}}
(and its conjugate, transpose and inverse).

While the first two separations used classical oracles, the above theorem relies on a unitary oracle, thereby capturing a smaller class of “black-box” techniques. However, by allowing access to the unitary along with its conjugate, transpose and inverse, we obtain the strongest form of unitary separation
[
Zha25
]
. We are unaware of meaningful techniques that evade such a separation but do not evade a black-box separation with a classical oracle.
This theorem also gives the following corollary:

Corollary 1.4.

There does not exist a fully black-box construction of quantum lightning from (subexponentially secure)
X
, where
X\in\{
(indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way functions), public-key encryption, deniable encryption, oblivious transfer, non-interactive ZK, trapdoor permutations, quantum money
\}
.

Connections with PDQP.

Finally, we consider a generalization of our collision-finding oracle
\mathsf{Col}
, which makes the connection with the complexity classes
\mathsf{PDQP}
and
\mathsf{SampPDQP}
more explicit.
These classes are defined using an oracle
\mathcal{Q}
, which is called a non-collapsing measurement oracle
[
ABFL16
]
, that has the hypothetical ability to sometimes make multiple non-collapsing measurements on a quantum state.
In more detail,
\mathcal{Q}
is equipped not only with unitary operations but also with two types of measurement processes:
(i) Projective (or standard) measurements, which collapse the quantum state in accordance with the conventional laws of quantum mechanics; and
(ii) Non-collapsing measurements, which yield a sample from the corresponding output distribution, while leaving the quantum state unaffected and able to be measured again with an independent outcome each time.
(For a definition of the non-collapsing measurement oracle
\mathcal{Q}
, see
Definition

3.3
.)
Then
\mathsf{PDQP}
and
\mathsf{SampPDQP}
are classes of decision (resp., sampling) problems that can be solved with
a polynomial-time classical deterministic algorithm that can make a single query to
\mathcal{Q}
.

Also, note that
\mathcal{Q}
can be used to implement
\mathsf{Col}
, and strictly generalizes
\mathsf{Col}
by allowing repeated unitaries to be applied interspersed with multiple non-collapsing measurements. It is natural to ask whether this more powerful oracle that decides
\mathsf{PDQP}
can break one-wayness.
We show the following.

Theorem 1.5 (Informal).

There exists a classical oracle
\mathcal{O}
relative to which (sub-exponentially secure) indistinguishability obfuscation and (sub-exponentially secure) one-way permutations exist, even against adversaries equipped with quantum queries to
\mathsf{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{O}}
.

Over the past decade, iO has served as a useful tool to build hard problems in various complexity classes, such as
\mathsf{PPAD}

[
BPR15
]
.
This theorem shows that black-box use of OWPs and iO (even for circuits that can implement the permutation) cannot be used to obtain hard problems in
\mathsf{PDQP}
.
Furthermore since queries to
\mathsf{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{O}}
can break
\mathsf{SZK}
, homomorphic encryption and non-interactive computational private
information retrieval, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.6 (Informal).

There does not exist a fully black-box construction of hard problems in
\mathsf{SZK}
, homomorphic encryption and non-interactive computational private
information retrieval from (subexponentially secure)
X
, where
X\in\{
(indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way functions), public-key encryption, deniable encryption, oblivious transfer, non-interactive ZK, trapdoor permutations, quantum money
\}
.

Technical Overview

Background on Black-Box Separations.

A fully black-box construction of a primitive
\mathcal{P}
from another primitive
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
consists of two algorithms: a construction
C
and a reduction
R
, each of which are “black-box”.
Namely, for any oracle
P^{\prime}
that enables a correct implementation of the primitive
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
,
C^{P^{\prime}}
instantiates a construction of
\mathcal{P}
, i.e.,
C^{P^{\prime}}
satisfies the correctness of
\mathcal{P}
.
Additionally,
for any oracle
A
that breaks
\mathcal{P}
-security of
C^{P^{\prime}}
, the (efficient) reduction
R^{A,P^{\prime}}
breaks the
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-security of
P^{\prime}
.

The standard framework (e.g.in
[
Sim98
,
BD19
]
) to rule out fully black-box constructions is to construct oracles
(S,\Gamma)
such that the following hold.

•

Property 1:
There exists a construction
C_{1}^{S}
that satisfies the correctness of
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, and is
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-secure against efficient adversaries that may query
S
and
\Gamma
.

•

Property 2:
For any construction
C_{2}^{S}
that satisfies correctness of
\mathcal{P}
, there exists an efficient adversary
A^{S,\Gamma}
that breaks the
\mathcal{P}
-security of
C_{2}^{S}
.

Suppose there exists a fully black-box construction
(C,R)
of
\mathcal{P}
from
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Since
C_{1}^{S}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-correctness,
C_{2}^{S}:=C^{(C_{1}^{S})}
satisfies
\mathcal{P}
-correctness. Therefore by Property 2, there exists an adversary
A^{S,\Gamma}
that breaks the
\mathcal{P}
-security of
C_{2}^{S}
. By the definition of black-box reductions,
R^{A^{S,\Gamma},C_{1}^{S}}
must break the
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-security of
C_{1}^{S}
. However, this gives an efficient adversary that queries
S
and
\Gamma
and breaks the
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-security of
C_{1}^{S}
, contradicting the first property above.

This paper will follow a similar methodology. We will show that a variety of primitives cannot be constructed from iO and OWPs (where the iO supports oracle-aided circuits, to capture existing techniques that combine iO with non-black-box use of one-way functions/permutations). The oracle
S
is formally defined in Section
5
, while the oracle
\Gamma
will depend on the primitive being separated, and will be defined for each primitive in Sections
6
,
7
, and
8
.

Before we explain what the oracles
(S,\Gamma)
will look like for our separations, it is useful to recap the classical separation between one-wayness and collision resistance
[
Sim98
]
. In what follows, we present a simplified informal exposition of Simon’s classical separation, which to our knowledge, has not appeared in prior work.
Then, we describe the issues that arise when quantum queries are introduced.

The Classical Setting.

The overall idea when classically separating one-way functions/permutations and collision resistant hash functions, is to introduce a “collision-finding” oracle
\mathsf{Col}
that on input a circuit
C
will find and output
distributional
collisions in the circuit.
Namely, the oracle will evaluate
C(\cdot)
on a uniformly chosen input
x
(that is fixed as a function of
C
), and then output random
x^{\prime}
such that
C(x)=C(x^{\prime})
.

Let us now describe the separating oracles in the classical setting.
Since we eventually want to argue that one-way functions/permutations exist, we set
S=\mathcal{O}
, a random oracle from
\{0,1\}^{n}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{3n}
. We will set
\Gamma
to be
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}}
. We will also allow queries to
\mathsf{Col}
to be circuits
C^{\mathcal{O}}
that have
\mathcal{O}
-oracle gates in them. As before, on input a circuit
C
with polynomially many
\mathcal{O}
-gates in it,
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}}
will sample uniform input
x
, and output a pair
(x,x^{\prime})
such that
C^{\mathcal{O}}(x)=C^{\mathcal{O}}(x^{\prime})
(and where
x^{\prime}
is essentially a random preimage of
C^{\mathcal{O}}(x)
).

The “one-way” security game involves picking a random
x
, and then challenging the adversary to invert
y=\mathcal{O}(x)
, with only polynomially many queries to these oracles.
One way to argue that inversion is hard is to consider an experiment where the oracle
\mathcal{O}
is replaced with a different oracle
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.
The oracle
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is identical to
\mathcal{O}
, except that for any
z
such that
\mathcal{O}(z)=y
,
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}(z)
outputs a uniformly random value
y^{\prime}
(note that w.h.p. due to
\mathcal{O}
being expanding, there is only a single such
z
).
Then, one-wayness can be proved by contradiction by combining the following arguments.

1.

Any adversary that inverts
y
, given access to
\mathcal{O}
can be used to distinguish between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
(by checking the image of the adversary’s output).

2.

The only way distinguish between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is to actually query
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
on
z
s.t.
\mathcal{O}(z)=y
.

3.

Since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is independent of
y
, the adversary cannot possibly query
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
on
z
s.t.
\mathcal{O}(z)=y
(except with negligible probability).

When the adversary can additionally access
\mathsf{Col}
, the second step of the argument above is not necessarily immediate.
Fortunately, we can show that a variant of the second step can be made to work by relying the following lemma (which is loosely inspired by a lemma developed in the context of separating

\mathsf{PDQP}
from
\mathsf{NP}

[
ABFL16
]
)
2
2

2

Note that abstracting out this lemma also helps obtain a simplified understanding of the classical separation
[
Sim98
]
of collision-resistant hash functions from one-way functions. Also, we do not formally prove this lemma in this work, instead we prove a stronger quantum generalization–please refer to (Informal) Lemma
2.2
.
.

Lemma 2.1 (Informal).

For every pair of oracles
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, for every oracle-aided circuit
C
and every input
x
, the statistical distance between
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}}(C)
and
\mathsf{Col}^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(C)
given the truth table of
\mathcal{O}
is at most thrice the statistical distance between
C^{\mathcal{O}}(x)
and
C^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(x)
for uniform
x
given the truth table of
\mathcal{O}
.

This says that, if given a single query to the collision-finder, an adversary can find a point on which
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
differ, then the circuit
C
(provided as input to
\mathsf{Col}
) must already be distinguishing between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, and therefore already querying
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
on a point where
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
differ.
This fact can then be used to complete a proof similar to the one sketched above.

A similar analysis extends–via a standard hybrid argument–even when the distinguisher is allowed multiple queries to the collision-finder. It is possible to come up with a sequence of hybrids where the first
i
oracle queries of the collision-finder are answered via
\mathcal{O}
, and the following queries are simulated using
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
(while ensuring that any duplicate queries are answered consistently). Any distinguisher that eventually detects a switch from
\mathcal{O}
to
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
must also do so in some adjacent set of hybrids, which then allows us to argue about the existence of a circuit that finds a differing input between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
(as before).

Quantum Queries: Challenges and Solutions.

First, let us understand how one-way functions can be obtained from random oracles with security under superposition queries (in the absence of any collision finder). The one-way function
f(x)
is simply defined as
\mathcal{O}(x)
.
Given a challenge
y=\mathcal{O}(x)
for a random
x
, we can create a punctured version of
\mathcal{O}
that outputs independent random values on
z
such that
\mathcal{O}(z)=y
(again w.h.p. there is a single such
z
), and call this oracle
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.
As before, an inverter can be used to derive a contradiction by combining the following three observations.

1.

Any adversary that inverts
y
can be easily used to distinguish between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
(by checking the image of the adversary’s output).

2.

The quantum one-way-to-hiding lemma
[
Unr12
]
says that the only way to distinguish between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is to query
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
(with noticeable query mass) on some
z
such that
O(z)=y
.

3.

Since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is independent of
y
, the adversary cannot possibly query
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
(with noticeable query mass) on
z
s.t.
\mathcal{O}(z)=y
(except with negligible probability).

Of course, our goal is to rule out
quantum
constructions of collision-resistant hash functions from one-way functions/permutations.
To do this, we must also provide a collision-finding oracle to the adversary. To rule out quantum constructions, the collision-finding oracle
\mathsf{Col}
from before must be updated to find collisions in
quantum
circuits that make superposition queries to
\mathcal{O}
. That is, without loss of generality, an input to
\mathsf{Col}
will take the form of a quantum circuit
C
with
\mathcal{O}
-gates, and denoting
C^{\mathcal{O}}\ket{0...0}=\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}
,
\mathsf{Col}
will output the result of measuring all qubits in the state
\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}^{\otimes 2}
in the computational basis.
Furthermore to rule out quantum reductions, we will have to argue that quantum queries to
\mathsf{Col}
will not help break one-wayness.

Unfortunately, it is unclear why the one-way-to-hiding property above would hold in the presence of quantum queries to
\mathsf{Col}
.
The very first quantum query to
\mathsf{Col}
could already distribute a small fraction of amplitude across every point in its domain. Furthermore, since
\mathsf{Col}
is itself inefficient, it is not at all clear why its response to a superposition query would avoid leaking information about whether the oracle was
\mathcal{O}
or
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
.

To address, this, we take inspiration from the compressed oracle technique
[
Zha19
]
and
purify

\mathsf{Col}
to obtain a superposition over truth tables, where every input
C
is mapped to an output pure state in this “purified” oracle. Furthermore, queries to
\mathsf{Col}
can be simulated lazily given the ability to compute/uncompute the corresponding pure state.
This ability is captured by a “compression” unitary, denoted by
W_{\mathcal{O}}
, that swaps
\ket{\bot}
to the corresponding pure state.
Our next step is to prove the following lemma, which is the quantum analogue of Lemma
2.1
, and which says that a variant of one-way-to-hiding continues to hold in the presence of quantum queries to (the compression unitary of)
\mathsf{Col}
.

Lemma 2.2 (Informal).

For every pair of oracles
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, and every quantum query
\ket{\psi}
, if the trace distance between

W_{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\psi}

and

W_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\ket{\psi}

(given
\mathcal{O}
) is noticeable,
then
\ket{\psi}

has noticeable mass on a circuit
C
for which the statistical distance between
C^{\mathcal{O}}\ket{0...0}
and
C^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\ket{0...0}
is noticeable, given
\mathcal{O}
.

Intuitively, this lemma follows by a hybrid argument where the joint distribution of the outputs of
\mathsf{Col}
are modified one part at a time (we refer the reader to the proof of
6.7
for a complete description).

The lemma above says that the adversary helps find a quantum circuit
C
that without ever querying the compression unitary
W_{\mathcal{O}}
, will distinguish between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. This in turn means that measuring a random query of
C
(to
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
) will, with noticeable probability, yield
z
such that
\mathcal{O}(z)=y
.
The above lemma suffices to show that a
single
quantum query to the collision-finder, even on quantum circuits, does not break one-wayness.
Due to the way these compression unitaries are defined, we are then able to switch
W^{\mathcal{O}}\ket{\psi}
to
W^{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\ket{\psi}
query-by-query, and rely on a quantum hybrid argument
[
BBBV97
]
to argue that even sub-exponentially many queries to
\mathsf{Col}
do not help break one-wayness.

We are also able to extend this to allow multiple non-collapsing measurements in between computation. That is, our oracle can be strengthened from
\mathsf{Col}
to a non-collapsing-measurement oracle, that applies multiple unitaries interspersed with non-collapsing (and collapsing) measurements.
We do this by defining a class of unitary oracles which we refer to as one-way-to-hiding (OW2H) compatible, in
Section

5
, and showing that one-way-to-hiding continues to hold in the presence of such unitaries. Subsequently we prove that the compression unitary for
\mathsf{Col}
, as well as for the more general non-collapsing measurement oracle, is OW2H-compatible.

Enabling Permutations.

Recall that our goal is to not only prove that one-way functions exist, but also that OWPs and iO exist in the presence of queries to
\mathsf{Col}
,
\mathsf{QCol}
, or a non-collapsing measurement oracle.

The argument for the existence of OWPs follows a similar template as one-way functions, except that the oracle
\mathcal{O}
is a random permutation oracle. To argue that
y=\mathcal{O}(x)
is hard to invert, we will again replace
\mathcal{O}
with a different oracle
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. The oracle
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is identical to
\mathcal{O}
, except that on a random
x^{\prime}
,
O(x^{\prime})
outputs
y
. Then, one-wayness of the permutation can be proved by contradiction by combining the following arguments.

1.

Any adversary that inverts
y
given
\mathcal{O}
will without loss of generality query
\mathcal{O}
on
x
.

2.

One-way-to-hiding: The only way to distinguish between
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is to query
\mathcal{O}
(with noticeable query mass) on
x^{\prime}
.

3.

Since
\mathcal{O}
is independent of
x^{\prime}
, the adversary cannot possibly query
\mathcal{O}
on
x^{\prime}
(except with negligible probability).

4.

By 2 and 3, the adversary cannot query
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
on
x^{\prime}
(except with negligible probability). But by 1, 2 and 3, the adversary must query
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
on
x
(with noticeable probability). However, to an adversary that makes oracle calls to
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
,
x
and
x^{\prime}
appear symmetric: thus an adversary cannot query one with higher probability than the other.

As before, the second step of this argument is not immediate when given additional oracles (like a collision-finding oracle) that depend on
\mathcal{O}
or
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. Fortunately, a OW2H-compatible unitary allows us to recover this step just like the case of one-way functions.

Enabling Obfuscation.

To build obfuscation, following
[
AS15
]
and
[
BD19
]
we introduce an expanding random oracle
\mathsf{Obf}
that maps input circuit-randomness pairs
(\mathfrak{C},r)
to
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
, which is interpreted as an obfuscation of
\mathfrak{C}
. In addition, an
\mathsf{Eval}
oracle given
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
and input
x
inverts
\mathsf{Obf}
on
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
to recover
\mathfrak{C}
, then outputs
\mathfrak{C}(x)
.

Recall that to prove obfuscation security, for every pair of functionally equivalent circuits
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
, we would like to show that an adversary given
\mathsf{Obf}(\mathfrak{C}_{b};r)
for uniform
b,r
cannot predict
b
, even with access to
\mathsf{Eval}
and the collision-finding oracle
\mathsf{Col}
.
Furthermore, any circuits
C
on which
\mathsf{Col}
is queried can now have
\mathsf{Obf}
and
\mathsf{Eval}
oracle gates as well.

In the classical literature this is accomplished by a series of hybrids involving reprogramming the oracles adaptively. Intuitively, we move to a world where the adversary is given a random string
z
instead of the obfuscated circuit. The
\mathsf{Eval}
oracle is then modified such that querying the oracle on
(z,x)
returns
\mathfrak{C}_{0}(x)
. Replicating a similar argument appears to be considerably more challenging in the quantum setting.

Thus, we take a different approach. We prove the security of the obfuscation scheme without requiring adaptive reprogramming in any of our hybrids. This yields a new proof idea that is arguably simpler than the existing classical analysis (eg., in
[
BD19
]
).

Taking a step back, the adversary’s goal in the iO security game is to distinguish
\mathsf{Obf}(\mathfrak{C}_{0};r^{*})
from
\mathsf{Obf}(\mathfrak{C}_{1};r^{*})
where
r^{*}
is the randomness used by the challenger. If the adversary only had access to
\mathsf{Obf}
and not
\mathsf{Eval}
, this would be easy to show by the same techniques we use to show one-wayness of a random oracle. The presence of
\mathsf{Eval}
complicates this approach, since
\mathsf{Eval}
uses
\mathsf{Obf}^{-1}
to “unobfuscate” circuits in order to evaluate them.

Our goal will be to (indistinguishably) move to a world where
\mathsf{Obf}
is replaced by
\mathsf{Obf}^{\prime}
which is identical to
\mathsf{Obf}
except that
\mathsf{Obf}^{\prime}(\widehat{\mathfrak{C}};r^{*})=\bot
for all strings
\widehat{\mathfrak{C}}
, where
r^{*}
refers to the randomness used by the challenge obfuscation. Suppose we could accomplish this while allowing
\mathsf{Eval}
to still use
\mathsf{Obf}^{-1}
to invert its inputs. Then for any two functionally equivalent circuits
\mathfrak{C}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{C}_{1}
, strings of the form
\mathsf{Obf}(\mathfrak{C}_{b};r^{*})
are
identically
distributed in the adversary’s view, independent of the number of queries made by the adversary (and hence independent of
\mathsf{Col}
which can be simulated using unbounded queries). This means the view of the adversary in the security game is independent of the challenger’s choice bit, implying that the adversary has zero advantage in the distinguishing game. This is because
\mathsf{Obf}^{\prime}
queries cannot help distinguish the obfuscations, while
\mathsf{Eval}
behaves identically for obfuscations of functionally equivalent circuits.

The argument above gets more complicated when we introduce a collision-finder. Recall that we have already discussed why the compression unitary for
\mathsf{Col}
is OW2H-compatible; all that remains is to prove that the security of obfuscation is retained in the presence of any OW2H-compatible unitary.
In particular, switching from
\mathsf{Obf}
to
\mathsf{Obf}^{\prime}
in the presence of such a unitary is non-trivial.
Nevertheless, we show that this is possible
by relying on the fact that the unitary is OW2H-compatible: namely
distinguishing
\mathsf{Col}
that uses
\mathsf{Obf}
from
\mathsf{Col}
that uses
\mathsf{Obf}^{\prime}
leads to an efficient algorithm that finds points where the two oracles differ.

Breaking Quantum Lightning.

Quantum lightning is a form of publicly verifiable quantum money which allows for the public generation of unclonable states. Namely, anyone can produce quantum bank-notes/“lightning” states along with an efficiently verifiable classical serial number, such that it is infeasible for anyone (even the generator) to create two states that verify against the same serial number.
Quantum lightning is an instance of collision/cloning resistance over quantum states, as opposed to classical strings. Our final technical goal is to demonstrate that this type of cloning resistance
also
cannot be based on iO and OWPs.

To achieve this, we will first construct a quantum oracle capable of breaking any quantum lightning scheme. Imagine an oracle that on input quantum circuits representing a minting algorithm, ran the minting algorithm to obtain a serial number
s
and a banknote
\ket{\psi_{s}}
, and then returned
s
along with
two
copies of
\ket{\psi_{s}}
. Such an oracle would break any lightning scheme. However, it does not seem powerful enough to break any quantum money scheme, since in quantum money one needs to clone banknotes corresponding to an externally sampled serial number
s
.
This difference is crucial since publicly verifiable quantum money
can
be built from iO and OWPs
[
Zha21
]
.

Can we just use this oracle? The first issue with defining an oracle this way is that the oracle is not unitary. We can, however, consider a purified version of this oracle that outputs a pure state
\ket{\phi}:=\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}\ket{\psi_{s}}
, where the output of the (purified) minting algorithm was
\sum_{s}\alpha_{s}\ket{s}\ket{\psi_{s}}
.
Now, measuring the first register of
\ket{\phi}
yields the same marginal distribution over serial numbers as the mint algorithm.

In order for this oracle to be unitary, we define it as the operation that swaps between states
\ket{\bot}
and
\ket{\phi}
. Note here the serendipitous similarity to the compression unitary for
\mathsf{Col}
, which similarly swaps
\ket{\bot}
to the purified state of
\mathsf{Col}
! In fact, we can show that this lightning cloning unitary also satisfies the “one-way to hiding” (OW2H) compatibility described before. This, combined with the fact that iO and OWPs continue to remain secure even given queries to a OW2H unitary, helps complete our proof.

Follow-up Work.

An upcoming work
[
TZ25
]
builds on our technical toolkit, using it to separate constant-round proofs of quantumness from
iO and OWPs. They crucially rely on the fact that our arguments do not require adaptive reprogramming.

Roadmap.

The rest of this paper is arranged as follows. In Section
3
we define notation, known primitives and lemmas that will be useful later.
In Section
4
, we introduce some helpful techniques to analyse algorithms that make quantum queries to oracles.

Section
5
defines the class of OW2H-compatible unitaries and shows how to realize a construction of iO and OWPs that
remains secure
in the presence of a class of unitary oracles which we refer to as one-way-to-hiding compatible.

The remaining sections rule out black-box constructions of various primitives from iO and OWPs by constructing oracles that break the security of these primitives. The oracles are either OW2H-compatible or can be simulated by OW2H-compatible oracles, and therefore do not break the security of iO and OWPs.

Section
6
contains the separations for collision-resistant primitives, Section
7
contains the separations for quantum lightning, and finally in Section

we show that iO and OWPs remain secure even against (quantum) queries to a non-collapsing-measurement oracle.

\ket{s_{1}}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}\ket{s_{2}\oplus s_{1}}_{\mathbf{Y}}
. Note that the querier does not have direct access to
\mathbf{D}
.

4.2
Compressed Oracles for General Product Distributions

Let
\{\mathcal{D}_{x}\}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}}
be a collection of distributions over strings.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be the product distribution induced by these distributions, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}:=\bigotimes_{x}\mathcal{D}_{x}
.
We say an oracle
\mathcal{O}
is sampled from
\mathcal{D}
when the
x
-th row of its truth table
is sampled independently from
\mathcal{D}_{x}
. In other words, the truth table of
\mathcal{O}
is sampled from
\mathcal{D}
.

We define the notion of the compression unitary for a product distribution as follows.

Definition 4.1 (Compression Unitary).

Let
\{\mathcal{D}_{x}\}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}}
be a collection of distributions over strings.
Let
\mathcal{D}:=\bigotimes_{x}\mathcal{D}_{x}
be the product distribution induced by
\{\mathcal{D}_{x}\}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}}
.
Then, the compression unitary
U
for
\mathcal{D}
is

\displaystyle U:=\sum_{x}|x\rangle\langle x|\otimes U_{x},

(16)

where

\displaystyle U_{x}:=|\mathcal{D}_{x}\rangle\langle\bot|_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}+|\bot\rangle\langle\mathcal{D}_{x}|_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}+(I-|\bot\rangle\langle\bot|-|\mathcal{D}_{x}\rangle\langle\mathcal{D}_{x}|)_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}.

(17)

Here,

\ket{\mathcal{D}_{x}}:=\sum_{z}\sqrt{{\rm Pr}_{\mathcal{D}_{x}}[z]}\ket{z}

and
\Pr_{\mathcal{D}_{x}}[z]
is the probability that
z
is sampled from
\mathcal{D}_{x}
.

Next, we show that quantum queries to oracles drawn from a product distribution can be simulated using quantum access to the corresponding compression unitary using a technique called the compressed oracle, which was first introduced in
[
Zha19
]
.

Definition 4.2 (Compressed Oracle).

The compressed oracle
\mathsf{CStO}_{\mathcal{D}}
is a method by which quantum queries to a randomly sampled classical oracle can be statefully simulated.
Let
\{\mathcal{D}_{x}\}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}}
be a collection of distributions over strings.
Let
\mathcal{D}:=\bigotimes_{x}\mathcal{D}_{x}
be the product distribution induced by
\{\mathcal{D}_{x}\}_{x\in\{0,1\}^{*}}
.
The database register
\mathbf{D}:=\bigotimes_{x}\mathbf{D}_{x}
is private to the simulation and is initialized as
|\bot\rangle_{\mathbf{D}}:=\bigotimes_{x}|\bot\rangle_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}
.
A query to the compressed oracle
\mathsf{CStO}_{\mathcal{D}}
is implemented by applying the following unitary to the querier’s query register
\mathbf{X}
, response register
\mathbf{Y}
, and the private database register
\mathbf{D}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{x}\ket{x}\!\bra{x}_{\mathbf{X}}\otimes(U_{x}\circ\mathsf{CNOT}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}\mathbf{Y}}\circ U_{x}),

(18)

where
U=\sum_{x}\ket{x}\!\bra{x}\otimes U_{x}
is the compression unitary for
\mathcal{D}
as defined in
Definition

4.1
.

We note that
\mathsf{CStO}_{\mathcal{D}}
can be implemented using
U
in a query-efficient way. In particular, the simulation makes two queries to
U
per
\mathsf{CStO}_{\mathcal{D}}
query.

Theorem 4.3.

For any adversary
\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\Pr[\mathcal{A}^{\mathsf{CStO}_{\mathcal{D}}}=1]=\Prr_{O\leftarrow\mathcal{D}}[\mathcal{A}^{O}=1].

(19)

Proof.

Follows from the proof of Lemma 4 in
[
Zha19
]
with a minimal modification.
∎

4.3
Bounds on Distinguishing Oracles

Here we show some bounds on distinguishing oracles. First we look at any pair of unitaries that take as input a register
\mathbf{X}
and a target register, and on any computational basis state
\ket{x}_{\mathbf{X}}
apply an operation that swaps between
\ket{\bot}
and some state
\ket{\phi_{x}}
to the target register. We note that all compression unitaries (
Definition

4.1
) are of this form. We show that the ability to distinguish between unitaries of this form allows for finding strings
x
where the corresponding
\ket{\phi_{x}}
states for the unitaries are far. This is proved via a hybrid argument, and will be used extensively to indistinguishably switch between two unitaries.

Theorem 4.4.

For the collection of states
\{\ket{\varphi_{x}}\}_{x}
and
\{\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\}_{x}
define

\displaystyle U_{x}

\displaystyle:=\ket{\varphi_{x}}\!\bra{\bot}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}+\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\varphi_{x}}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}+\big(\mathbb{I}-\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\bot}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}-\ket{\varphi_{x}}\!\bra{\varphi_{x}}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}\big)

(20)

\displaystyle U^{\prime}_{x}

\displaystyle:=\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\!\bra{\bot}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}+\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}+\big(\mathbb{I}-\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\bot}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\!\bra{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}_{\mathbf{D}_{x}}\big).

(21)

Also define

\displaystyle U

\displaystyle:=\sum_{x}\ket{x}\!\bra{x}_{\mathbf{X}}\otimes U_{x}

(22)

\displaystyle U^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{x}\ket{x}\!\bra{x}_{\mathbf{X}}\otimes U^{\prime}_{x}.

(23)

For any
q
-query adversary
\mathcal{A}
,
let
\mathcal{B}
be the algorithm that samples
i\leftarrow[q]
and runs
\mathcal{A}
up to just before
the
i
-th query, then measures the query register in the computational basis
and returns the measurement output. Let
\Delta:=\|\ket{\psi}-\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\|

where
\ket{\psi}
and
\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
are the purified final states of
\mathcal{A}^{U}
and
\mathcal{A}^{U^{\prime}}

respectively. Then

\displaystyle\Exp_{x\leftarrow\mathcal{B}^{U}}\big[\|\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\|\big]\geq\frac{\Delta^{2}}{32q^{2}}.

(24)

Proof.

Consider the following sequence of hybrids
\{\mathcal{H}_{i}\}_{i\in[0,q]}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{i}
consists of running
\mathcal{A}

Let the (purified) final state of
\mathcal{A}
in

be
\ket{\psi_{i}}
. Note that
\ket{\psi_{0}}=\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
and
\ket{\psi_{q}}=\ket{\psi}
.
By the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\|\ket{\psi_{i}}-\ket{\psi_{i-1}}\|\geq\Delta.

(25)

Define
\varepsilon_{i}:=\|\ket{\psi_{i}}-\ket{\psi_{i-1}}\|
.
The state just before the
i
-th query in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}
is identical since both hybrids are identical until the
i
-th query. Let this state be

between queries as the unitary

and consider the initial state to be

without loss of generality then

\displaystyle\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}=(AU)^{i-1}A\ket{0}.

(26)

The final state in
\mathcal{H}_{i}
is
(AU^{\prime})^{q-i}A(U\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}})

and in
\mathcal{H}_{i-1}
it is
(AU^{\prime})^{q-i}A(U^{\prime}\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}})
. Thus,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}

\displaystyle=\|\ket{\psi_{i}}-\ket{\psi_{i-1}}\|=\|(AU^{\prime})^{q-i}A(U\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}})-(AU^{\prime})^{q-i}A(U^{\prime}\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}})\|

(27)

\displaystyle=\|U\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}-U^{\prime}\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}\|=\|(U-U^{\prime})\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}\|.

(28)

We can decompose

into

\displaystyle\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}\ket{x}\ket{\phi_{x}},

(29)

where the first register is the query register and
p_{x}
is the probability that measuring the query register in the computational basis returns
x
. Applying

gives

\displaystyle U\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}\ket{x}\otimes U_{x}\ket{\phi_{x}},

(30)

while applying

gives

\displaystyle U^{\prime}\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}\ket{x}\otimes U^{\prime}_{x}\ket{\phi_{x}}.

(31)

Thus

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{2}=\|(U-U^{\prime})\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}\|^{2}=\sum_{x}p_{x}\|(U_{x}-U^{\prime}_{x})\ket{\phi_{x}}\|^{2}.

(32)

Let
\ket{\xi_{x}}:=\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}
be an un-normalized state.
By the definition of
U_{x}
and
U^{\prime}_{x}
,
Equations

20
and
21
, and
taking their difference,

\displaystyle U_{x}-U^{\prime}_{x}

\displaystyle=\ket{\xi_{x}}\!\bra{\bot}+\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\xi_{x}}+(\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\!\bra{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}-\ket{\varphi_{x}}\!\bra{\varphi_{x}})

(33)

\displaystyle=\ket{\xi_{x}}\!\bra{\bot}+\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\xi_{x}}+\Big(\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}(\bra{\varphi_{x}}-\bra{\xi_{x}})-(\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}+\ket{\xi_{x}})\bra{\varphi_{x}}\Big)

(34)

\displaystyle=\ket{\xi_{x}}\!\bra{\bot}+\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\xi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\!\bra{\xi_{x}}-\ket{\xi_{x}}\!\bra{\varphi_{x}}.

(35)

Applying the operator to
\ket{\phi_{x}}
and noting that
\ket{\varphi_{x}}
and
\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}
are not supported on

,

\displaystyle\|(U_{x}-U^{\prime}_{x})\ket{\phi_{x}}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\ket{\xi_{x}}\!\bra{\bot}+\ket{\bot}\!\bra{\xi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\!\bra{\xi_{x}}-\ket{\xi_{x}}\!\bra{\varphi_{x}}\right)\ket{\phi_{x}}\right\|

(36)

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sqrt{2}\cdot\ket{\xi_{x}}\tfrac{\bra{\bot}-\bra{\varphi_{x}}}{\sqrt{2}}+\sqrt{2}\cdot\tfrac{\ket{\bot}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}}{\sqrt{2}}\bra{\xi_{x}}\right)\ket{\phi_{x}}\right\|

(37)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}\|\ket{\xi_{x}}\|=2\sqrt{2}\|\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\|.

(38)

Plugging this bound back into (

),

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\leq\sum_{x}p_{x}\cdot 8\cdot\|\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\|^{2}\leq 8\cdot\Exp_{x}[\|\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\|^{2}],

(39)

where
x
is sampled by measuring the query register of
\ket{\widetilde{\psi}_{i}}
, which is exactly the distribution output by
\mathcal{B}^{U}
conditioned on sampling

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\Exp_{x\leftarrow\mathcal{B}^{U}}\left[\|\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\|\right]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\cdot\Exp_{x\leftarrow\mathcal{B}^{U}}\left[\|\ket{\varphi_{x}}-\ket{\varphi^{\prime}_{x}}\|^{2}\right]\geq\frac{1}{4}\cdot\Exp_{i\leftarrow[q]}\left[\frac{\varepsilon_{i}^{2}}{8}\right]

(40)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{32}\cdot\Exp_{i\leftarrow[q]}\left[\varepsilon_{i}\right]^{2}=\frac{1}{32}\left(\frac{\sum_{i}\varepsilon_{i}}{q}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{\Delta^{2}}{32q^{2}}.

(41)

∎

5
Indistinguishability Obfuscation and One-Way Permutations Relative to OW2H-Compatible Oracles

In this section, we show the existence of a random classical oracle
S
that allows us to instantiate iO and OWPs that remain secure in the presence of a class of unitary oracles we call "one-way to hiding (OW2H) compatible" unitaries. First, in
Section

5.1
, we define this class of unitaries.
Then in
Section

5.2
, we define the classical oracle
S
.
Finally, in
Section

5.3
, we show that
S
instantiates iO and OWP that remain secure in the presence of any OW2H-compatible unitaries.

5.1
OW2H-Compatibility

One-way to hiding theorems
[
Unr12
]
relate the probability of distinguishing oracles to the probability of finding points where the oracles differ. We adapt this concept to define a class of oracles which satisfy a strong form of one-way to hiding. Specifically, for a set of classical oracles
\mathbb{O}
and a collection of unitaries
\{W_{O}\}_{O\in\mathbb{O}}
, we will frequently need to argue that for
O_{1},O_{2}\in\mathbb{O}
,
(O_{1},W_{O_{1}})
and
(O_{2},W_{O_{2}})
are indistinguishable given query access, where
O_{1}
and
O_{2}
only differ on some hard to find inputs. One-way to hiding theorems in the literature readily allow showing claims of this sort in the absence of the second unitary oracle. We will say that
\{W_{O}\}_{O\in\mathbb{O}}
is one-way to hiding (OW2H) compatible for
\mathbb{O}
if such claims hold even in the presence of the unitary oracle. We also require
O
to be computable by querying
W_{O}
for the sake of notational convenience, and will often only provide access to
W_{O}
instead of
(O,W_{O})
.

Definition 5.1 (OW2H-Compatibility).

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a collection of classical oracles and let
W:=\{W_{O}\}_{O\in\mathbb{O}}
be a collection of unitaries. We say that
W
is OW2H-compatible for
\mathbb{O}
if:

1.

O
can be exactly computed by making a single query to
W_{O}
.

2.

For all functions
q:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
there exists a
{\mathsf{poly}}(q(\lambda))
-query algorithm
\mathcal{B}
and a constant
c\geq 0
such that for all
O,O^{\prime}\in\mathbb{O}
and
for any
q(\lambda)
-query adversaries
\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\ket{\psi}-\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\right\|\leq{\mathsf{poly}}(q(\lambda))\cdot\left(\Prr[\mathcal{B}^{W_{O},\mathcal{A}}(1^{\lambda})\in T]\right)^{c},

(45)

where
\ket{\psi}
and
\ket{\psi^{\prime}}
are the purified final states of
\mathcal{A}^{W_{O}}(1^{\lambda})
and
\mathcal{A}^{W_{O^{\prime}}}(1^{\lambda})
respectively, and
T:=\{x:O(x)\neq O^{\prime}(x)\}
. We will usually drop the notation for query access to
\mathcal{A}
from
\mathcal{B}
when obvious from context.

We will use the following property of OW2H-compatibility extensively.

Theorem 5.2.

Let
\mathbb{O}
be a collection of functions and let
W:=\{W_{O}\}_{O\in\mathbb{O}}
be a collection of unitaries where
W
is OW2H-compatible for
\mathbb{O}
with constant
c\in(0,1)
. Let
(O_{1},O_{2},z)
be jointly distributed random variables where
O_{1},O_{2}\in\mathbb{O}
and
z
is some classical side information.
Then for all functions
q:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, for any
q(\lambda)
-query adversaries
\mathcal{A}
that outputs a quantum state, and for all measurements
\mathcal{M}
that may depend on
O_{1},O_{2}
, and
z
, there exists a
{\mathsf{poly}}(q(\lambda))
-query adversary
\mathcal{B}
that outputs a bit string such that

\displaystyle\Bigg|\Pr_{O_{1},O_{2},z}\Big[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}^{{W_{O_{1}}}}(z,1^{\lambda}))=1\Big]-\Pr_{O_{1},O_{2},z}\Big[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}^{{W_{O_{2}}}}(z,1^{\lambda}))=1\Big]\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq{\mathsf{poly}}(q)\cdot\Pr_{O_{1},z}\Big[\mathcal{B}^{{W_{O_{1}}}}(z,1^{\lambda})\in T\Big]^{c},

where
T:=\{x:O_{1}(x)\neq O_{2}(x)\}

and
\mathcal{M}(\rho)=1
means that the measurement
\mathcal{M}
succeeds when applied to the state
\rho
.

Proof.

We drop
1^{\lambda}
from the notation for inputs to algorithms and assume it is always provided . Let
\mathcal{B}
be the algorithm provided by Definition
5.1
for
q(\lambda)
-query adversaries. For any fixing of
O_{1},O_{2},z
let
\varepsilon
be defined as:

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\left|\Pr\Big[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}^{{W_{O_{1}}}}(z))=1\Big]-\Pr\Big[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{A}^{{W_{O_{2}}}}(z))=1\Big]\right|.

(46)

Let
\ket{\psi_{1}}
and
\ket{\psi_{2}}
be the purified final states (before applying the measurement
\mathcal{M}
) of
\mathcal{A}^{{W^{O_{1}}}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{{W^{O_{2}}}}
, respectively. Then by the data processing inequality,

\mathsf{TD}(\ket{\psi_{1}},\ket{\psi_{2}})\geq\varepsilon.

By
Lemma

3.10
, this implies

\|\ket{\psi_{1}}-\ket{\psi_{2}}\|\geq\epsilon.

By the definition of
\mathcal{B}
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon\leq{\mathsf{poly}}(q(\lambda))\cdot\left(\Pr[\mathcal{B}^{{W_{O_{1}}}}(z)\in T]\right)^{c}.

(47)

Note that
\mathcal{B}
depends on
\mathcal{A}
but not on
\mathcal{M}
. Taking the expectation over
(O_{1},O_{2},z)
, noting that

, and applying Jensen’s inequality gives the statement of the theorem.
∎

5.2
Definition of Oracle
S

We define a random oracle
S
as follows:

Definition 5.3 (Oracle
S
).

The oracle
S:=(f,\mathsf{Obf},\mathsf{Eval}^{f})
consists of the following three parts:

•

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
x\in\{0,1\}^{\lambda}
,
f(x)=f_{\lambda}(x)
where
f_{\lambda}
is a random permutation on
\lambda

•

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
, for all

, and for all

\mathsf{Obf}(C,r)=\mathsf{Obf}_{\lambda}(C,r)

\mathsf{Obf}_{\lambda}:\{0,1\}^{\lambda}\times\{0,1\}^{\lambda}\rightarrow\{0,1\}^{3\lambda}

•

For any function
f^{\prime}
,
\mathsf{Eval}^{f^{\prime}}(\widetilde{C},x)
performs the following:

1.

If
\widetilde{C}\notin\mathsf{Image}(\mathsf{Obf})
, return
\bot
.

2.

Let
(C,r):=\mathsf{Obf}^{-1}(\widetilde{C})
. Parse
C
as an oracle-aided (classical) circuit.

3.

If
|x|
is not consistent with the input size of
C
, then return
\bot
. Otherwise, return

.

Note that
\mathsf{Eval}^{f}
is defined in terms of
\mathsf{Obf}

\mathsf{Eval}^{f}
"knows" the truth table of
\mathsf{Obf}
.

We will use
\mathbb{S}
to represent the set of all possible oracles with the same interface as

.

5.3
Constructing One-Way Permutations and Indistinguishability Obfuscation using

In this section we use the oracle

to construct a candidate OWP and a candidate iO scheme. We will then show a strong security property. Let
\mathbb{S}
be the set of all possible oracles with the same interface as

, and let

5.3.1
OWPs Secure Against Any OW2H-Compatible Unitary Collection

We show that

. The following theorem proves security.

Theorem 5.4.

Let
S=(f,\mathsf{Obf},\mathsf{Eval}^{f})

\mathbb{S}

(

) with constant

-query adversaries

,

\displaystyle\Prr_{S,x\leftarrow\{0,1\}^{\lambda}}\Big[\mathcal{A}^{W^{S}}\big(f(x)\big)=x\Big]\leq\frac{1}{2^{c^{2}\lambda/2}}.

(48)

Before proving

can be used to find

and

Lemma 5.5.

For any

and

\displaystyle\Pr\Big[f^{\prime}(y^{\prime})\neq f^{\prime\prime}(y^{\prime}):y^{\prime}\leftarrow\mathsf{Find}^{f^{\prime}}(y)\Big]\geq\frac{1}{2|y|},

where

•

With probability

•

With probability

Proof of

5.3.2

We show that

6
Separating Distributional Collision Resistance from Indistinguishability Obfuscation and One-Way Permutations

In this section, we show the impossibility of fully black-box constructions of distributional collision resistant puzzles (dCRPuzzs)

Definition 6.1 (Distributional Collision-Resistant Hashing (dCRH)

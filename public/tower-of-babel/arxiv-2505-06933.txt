Optimal pressure approximation for the nonstationary Stokes problem by a variational method in time with post-processing
Mathias Anselmann 1, Markus Bause 1, Gunar Matthies 2, Friedhelm Schieweck 3 1 Helmut Schmidt University, Faculty of Mechanical and Civil Engineering, Holstenhofweg 85, 22043 Hamburg, Germany 2 Technische Universität Dresden, Institut für Numerische Mathematik, 01062 Dresden, Germany 3 University of Magdeburg, Faculty of Mathematics, Universitätsplatz 2, 39016 Magdeburg, Germany

Abstract
Abstract. We provide an error analysis for the solution of the nonstationary Stokes problem by a variational method in space and time. We use finite elements of higher order for the approximation in space and a Galerkin–Petrov method with first order polynomials for the approximation in time. We require global continuity of the discrete velocity trajectory in time, while allowing the discrete pressure trajectory to be discontinuous at the endpoints of the time intervals. We show existence and uniqueness of the discrete velocity solution, characterize the set of all discrete pressure solutions and prove an optimal second order estimate in time for the pressure error in the midpoints of the time intervals. The key result and innovation is the construction of approximations to the pressure trajectory by means of post-processing together with the proof of optimal order error estimates. We propose two variants for a post-processed pressure within the set of pressure solutions based on collocation techniques or interpolation. Both variants guarantee that the pressure error measured in the L 2 L^{2} -norm converges with optimal second order in time and optimal order in space. For the discrete velocity solution, we prove error estimates of optimal order in time and space. We present some numerical tests to support our theoretical results.

Introduction

In this work we study the numerical approximation of the nonstationary Stokes system

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bm{u}-\Delta\bm{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\bm{f}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\;\Omega\times(0,T]\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\;\Omega\times(0,T]\,,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\bm{u}(0)

\displaystyle=\bm{u}_{0}

\displaystyle\quad\text{in }\;\Omega\,,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\bm{u}

\displaystyle=\bm{0}

\displaystyle\quad\text{on }\;\partial\Omega\times[0,T]\,,

(1.1d)

by a variational method in time and space.
In (
1.1
), let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, be an open,
bounded domain and
T>0
the final time. Further, we denote by

\bm{u}:\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p:\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
the unknown velocity and pressure field, respectively.
The force
\bm{f}
and the initial velocity
\bm{u}_{0}
are prescribed data.
In (
1.1a
), the fluid’s viscosity
\nu
is dropped and chosen as
\nu=1
.
Throughout, we assume that the Stokes system (
1.1
) admits a
sufficiently regular solution up to
t=0
. For
the discussion of the existence and regularity of solutions to
Stokes and Navier–Stokes systems, we refer to the literature (cf., e.g.,

[
6
,
8
,
12
,
18
]
). The behavior of Stokes solutions at time
t=0

with nonlocal compatibility conditions for the data is studied in

[
6
,
14
,
17
]
.

In order to solve the problem (
1.1
) numerically we apply
a variational method with respect to space and time.
To approximate
\bm{u}
and
p
in space, we use the finite element spaces

\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
, respectively, which have an arbitrary approximation
order
r
and satisfy the usual inf-sup stability condition.
For thex
approximation with respect to time, we restrict ourselves to the
simplest case of piecewise first order polynomials.
An extension of our ideas to higher order polynomials in time is
feasible and left for future work.
For the approximation in time, we
decompose the global time interval
I=(0,T]
into local
time intervals
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
,
n=1,\dots,N
, where

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{N}=T
, and use polynomial spaces

\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};B)
of functions
w:I_{n}\to B
, where
w(t)
is a
polynomial in
t
of degree not greater than
k
with values in a Banach
space
B
.
Then, we approximate the exact velocity
\bm{u}(t)

for
t\in I_{n}
by means of a polynomial
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
of first
order in
t
with values in the discrete velocity space
\bm{V}_{h}
,
i.e., we use the ansatz

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
.
Since we want to have that the time derivative
\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}

exists globally on
[0,T]
in the weak
L^{2}
-sense, we require
that the fully discrete solution trajectory
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
is globally
continuous on
[0,T]
.
This global continuity will be realized during a successive time-marching process
over the time intervals by means
of an explicit condition on each interval
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
which can be
regarded as a local initial condition for
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}
at

t=t_{n-1}
.
In order to get for the pressure the same order of approximation with
respect to time as for the velocity, we approximate the exact pressure
p(t)

for
t\in I_{n}
by the ansatz

p_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})
.
However, in contrast to the velocity, we allow that the global discrete pressure
trajectory
p_{\tau,h}(t)
can be discontinuous at the endpoints of the time
intervals.
To the best of our knowledge, this approach concerning the approximation of the
pressure is new.

The fully discrete approximations
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
and
p_{\tau,h}(t)
for
t\in I_{n}
,
according to the ansatz described above,
are determined by means of discrete variational equations.
These variational equations are consistent in the sense that
they are satisfied by the exact solution
\bm{u}(t)
and
p(t)
as well.
In the following, we will derive, for time interval
I_{n}
, the
variational equation for
\bm{u}(t)
and
p(t)

which is related to (
1.1a
).
We choose the test function
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}
as polynomial in time with values in

\bm{V}_{h}
, where the polynomial degree
k
will be specified later. Then, for arbitrary fixed
t\in I_{n}
, we take on both sides
of (
1.1a
)
the inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle

in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
with the test function in space
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(t)\in\bm{V}_{h}
and obtain

\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{u}(t),\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(t)\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\bm{u}(t),\nabla\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(t)\right\rangle-\left\langle p(t),\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(t)\right\rangle=\left\langle\bm{f}(t),\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(t)\right\rangle\,,

(1.2)

where we have applied integration by parts to the terms related to

-\Delta\bm{u}
and
\nabla p
using the homogeneous boundary condition
of
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(t)\in\vec{V}_{h}
.
We note, that for functions in
\vec{V}_{h}
we require homogeneous
boundary conditions.
Since (
1.2
) holds for all
t\in I_{n}
, we can apply
numerical integration with respect to time over the interval
I_{n}
and
get for all
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
that

Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{u},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle-\left\langle p,\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle)=Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(\left\langle\bm{f},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle)\,,

(1.3)

where
Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(g)
denotes for a time-dependent function
g:I_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
the value of the 2-point Gauss–Lobatto quadrature formula approximating the
integral
\int_{I_{n}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t
(see (
2.12
) below).

Now, we get the variational equation for the discrete velocity and
pressure by replacing in (
1.3
) the exact solution
\bm{u}
and

p
by the discrete solution
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}
and
p_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}
.
In an analogous way, we can derive from the continuity equation (
1.1b
)
an associated variational equation using a test function

q_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};Q_{h})
.
For the polynomial degree
k
, it will turn out that the choice
k=0
is
suitable.
Thus, setting
k=0
in the variational equations derived above,
we obtain the following discrete problem on interval

I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
to determine the discrete solution
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}

and
p_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}
:

For given
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
, find

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
and
p_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})

such that

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})\,,

(1.4a)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(\left\langle\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\bm{u}_{\tau,h},\nabla\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle-\left\langle p_{\tau,h},\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle)

\displaystyle=Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(\left\langle\bm{f},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\right\rangle)

\displaystyle\quad\forall\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})\,,

(1.4b)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(\left\langle\nabla\cdot\bm{u}_{\tau,h},q_{\tau,h}\right\rangle)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\quad\forall q_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};Q_{h})\,.

(1.4c)

In (
1.4a
),

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{n-1})
denotes the right-sided limit of the values

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
when
t\in I_{n}
tends to
t_{n-1}
.
The value
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
is known: for
n>1
from the previous interval

I_{n-1}
since
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n-1}}(t_{n-1})
and for
n=1
from
the discrete initial value, i.e.,
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(0)=\bm{u}_{0,h}
, where
\bm{u}_{0,h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
is an approximation of the initial value
\bm{u}_{0}
.
Thus, condition (
1.4a
) for all
n=1,\dots,N
guarantees the
global continuity of discrete velocity trajectory
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
for

t\in[0,T]
.

In this paper, we study existence and uniqueness of
solutions
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})
of the discrete problem (
1.4
)
related to the interval
I_{n}
. Moreover, we exactly describe the set
of all solutions (cf. Lemma
3.4
). We prove that the
velocity solution
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}
is uniquely determined. However, we show for
the pressure solution
p_{\tau,h}
that the
Q_{h}
-valued degree of freedom

\overline{p}^{n}_{h}\coloneqq p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
with
\overline{t}_{n}\coloneqq(t_{n-1}+t_{n})/2
is
uniquely determined by (
1.4
), whereas the degree of freedom

p^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
can be chosen arbitrarily.
In our error analysis we show that the global continuity of the discrete
velocity trajectory
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
guaranteed by (
1.4a
)
and the fulfillment of the variational equations (
1.4b
) and (
1.4c
)
for all time intervals
I_{n}
are sufficient to prove optimal
estimates for the velocity error

\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}\coloneqq\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{\tau,h}

of order
\tau^{2}+h^{r}
(see Theorem
3.11
),
where
\tau
and
h
denote the mesh sizes in time and space,
respectively. Precisely, we have

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}(t_{n})\|^{2}\leq C\big(\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}(0)\|+\tau^{2}+h^{r}\big)^{2}

(1.5)

for all
n=1,\ldots,N
,
where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
L^{2}
-norm in space and
\tau_{m}
the length of the time
interval
I_{m}
.
Note that the initial velocity
\bm{u}_{0,h}
can be chosen such that
the initial error norm
\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}(0)\|
is of the
order
h^{r}
.
It is also important to note that in terms of accuracy in time, the
term
\tau^{2}
in the estimate (
1.5
) shows a
superconvergence of
\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}
in the midpoints

\overline{t}_{m}
of the time intervals
I_{m}
. This fact is an essential key
point to derive an optimal error estimate for the pressure.
We will show in Theorem
3.10
, for each
discrete pressure trajectory
p_{\tau,h}(t)
in the set of pressure solutions,
that the error estimate

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n}\|p(\overline{t}_{n})-p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|^{2}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{r})^{2}

(1.6)

is satisfied at the midpoints
\overline{t}_{n}
of the intervals
I_{n}
.
A discrete pressure trajectory
p_{\tau,h}
restricted to an interval
I_{n}
is
fixed by the uniquely determined value
p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
and the
freely selectable degree of freedom
p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
An important goal of this work is to compute, for each interval
I_{n}
, the
degree of freedom
p^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})

in such a way that a pressure error estimate similarly
to (
1.6
) is obtained, where the midpoints
\overline{t}_{n}
are replaced by the endpoints
t_{n}
of the time intervals.
Then, this also leads to an optimal order error estimate for
the pressure in the
L^{2}
-norm with respect to space and time.

In our paper, we propose two variants for computing the values

p^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
The first variant is based on the idea that the value
p^{n-1}_{h}

is chosen as the selected pressure value

\widetilde{p}^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
of a uniquely defined
post-processed pressure trajectory
{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}(t)
which is in the set
of the pressure solutions and globally continuous on
[0,T]
.
Together with
{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}
we define a post-processed velocity trajectory

\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}(t)
which is globally
C^{1}
-continuous in time and locally in
time characterized by

\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
.
The connection of the post-processed solution
(\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h},{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h})
to the original
solution
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})
on interval
I_{n}
is that

\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}(t)=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
for
t\in\{t_{n-1},t_{n}\}
and

{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
.
The remaining degrees of freedom to fix
(\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h},{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h})
on
I_{n}
are

\bm{a}^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
and
\widetilde{p}^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
They are determined
such that the pair
(\bm{a}^{n-1}_{h},\widetilde{p}^{n-1}_{h})

satisfies a collocation condition at time
t=t_{n-1}
together
with the known velocity value
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
This collocation condition corresponds to a Stokes-type problem for the
pair
(\bm{a}^{n-1}_{h},\widetilde{p}^{n-1}_{h})
. However, it turns out that such
a collocation problem has to be solved only once for the first interval

I_{1}
at the time
t_{0}=0
. We will prove that, for all subsequent intervals

I_{n}
,
n>1
, the solution
(\bm{a}^{n-1}_{h},\widetilde{p}^{n-1}_{h})
is equal to
the value
(\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}),{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}))
coming from the previous
interval
I_{n-1}
such that the post-processing is computationally cheap.
For the resulting globally continuous post-processed pressure trajectory

{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}
, we show an optimal order error estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm with
respect to space and time (see Corollary
4.14
).

In the second variant, we use the idea of interpolation based on the
accurate discrete pressure values
p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{m})
in the midpoints

\overline{t}_{m}
of the intervals
I_{m}
(see
estimate (
1.6
)) to define another post-processed pressure
trajectory
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
, which is also in the set of the pressure solutions and
locally characterized by
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})
. The connection
between this post-processed pressure
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
and the original
pressure solution
p_{\tau,h}
on the interval
I_{n}
is that
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
. The remaining degree of freedom of
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
on
I_{n}
is
the value
p^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{n-1})
, which is determined
by means of linear interpolation (or extrapolation for
n=1
) of two
accurate pressure values
p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{m})
from suitable intervals

I_{m}
in the neighborhood of
t_{n-1}
. This approach leads to a global
pressure trajectory that is generally discontinuous at the endpoints
of the time intervals. For the post-processed pressure trajectory
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
,
we will prove an optimal order error estimate in the
L^{2}
-norm with
respect to space and time (cf. Theorem
5.4
).

To the best of our knowledge, these results for our approximation of
the pressure trajectory are new in the literature.
In the following, we discuss some links of our work to the existing
literature in this field. The first link is related to the classical
works of Heywood and Rannacher
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
on finite element
approximations of the Navier–Stokes equations. In
[
9
]
, an error
analysis is given for a discretization by finite elements in space and the
Crank–Nicolson finite difference scheme in time, which generates, for the
time nodes
t_{n}
, approximations
\bm{u}^{n}_{h}
and
p^{n}_{h}
of the exact
solution values
\bm{u}(t_{n})
and
p(t_{n})
, respectively. If we would apply
this discretization to the Stokes equations then the link to our solution
trajectory
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})
is that
\bm{u}^{n}_{h}=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n})
and
p^{n}_{h}=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
hold for all
n
. The Crank–Nicolson value
p^{n}_{h}
can
also be characterized as the value
p_{\tau,h}(t_{n})
, where
p_{\tau,h}
is the
one special pressure trajectory in the set of all pressure solutions that
is piecewise constant on each time interval
I_{n}
such that
p_{\tau,h}(t_{n})=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=p^{n}_{h}
. Together with our pressure error
estimate (
1.6
), we see that the error of the Crank–Nicolson
scheme
\|p(t_{n})-p^{n}_{h}\|=\|p(t_{n})-p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|
is generally
only of first order with respect to the time step size.
For this error, a bound of the order
\tau+h^{r}
is proved in
[
9
]
.

Further, in
[
13
]
it is pointed out for the nonstationary Navier–Stokes equations that the pressure value
p^{n}_{h}
resulting from the application of the Crank–Nicolson scheme (regarded as an implicit
\theta
-scheme with
\theta=1/2
) approximates the exact pressure
p
at time
t=t_{n-1}+\theta\tau_{n}=\overline{t}_{n}
and not at
t=t_{n}
.

A second link to our work is is the work of Sonner and Richter
[
16
]
,
where the Crank–Nicolson scheme is considered as semidiscretization in
time of the Stokes and Navier–Stokes equations based on a variational
setting. Here, the test space and also the ansatz space for the
semidiscrete pressure
p_{\tau}
are chosen to be piecewise constant on the
time intervals, whereas the semidiscrete velocity
\bm{u}_{\tau}
is
required to be globally continuous and piecewise linear in time. For the
pressure error
\|p(\overline{t}_{n})-p_{\tau}(\overline{t}_{n})\|
in the midpoints
\overline{t}_{n}
of
the time intervals the order
\tau^{2}
is shown.

Further links to this work can be found in
[
11
,
4
]
.
In
[
11
]
, the nonstationary Stokes equations are discretized in
time by means of the continuous Galerkin–Petrov method with the
polynomial order
k=1
and
k=2
. The discretization in space is based
on a finite element method. The difference to our paper is that
in
[
11
]
the numerical integration with respect to time is based
on the

-point Gauss formula such that, for

, this time

In
[
11
]
, a post-processing of the discrete pressure at the time nodes

is proposed which differs from the proposals of this work. A theoretical error analysis is not contained in
[
11
]
.
In
[
4
]
,
the hyperbolic wave problem is discretized in time by the continuous

time integration and the finite element method for the space
discretization.
A post-processing is introduced and analyzed. It lifts the fully discrete approximation in time from a continuous to a continuously
differentiable one. The Gauss–-Lobatto quadrature in time
is essential for ensuring the improved convergence behavior.

2
Notation and preliminaries

2.1
Continuous function spaces

We use standard notation.
H^{m}(\Omega)
is the Sobolev space of
L^{2}(\Omega)

functions with derivatives up to order
m
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, while

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega)

and its vector-valued and matrix-valued versions.
Let
L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid\int_{\Omega}q\,\mathrm{d}\bm{x}=0\}
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\coloneqq\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid u=0\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\}
. For short, we put

Q\coloneqq L^{2}_{0}(\Omega)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bm{V}\coloneqq\left(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{d}\,.

We note that bold-face letters are used to indicate vector-valued spaces and functions. Further, we define by

\bm{V}^{\operatorname{div}}\coloneqq\{\bm{u}\in\bm{V}\mid\langle\nabla\cdot\bm{u},q\rangle=0\>\>\forall q\in Q\}

the space of divergence-free functions.
We denote by
\bm{V}^{\prime}
the dual space of
\bm{V}
and use the notation

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,,\qquad\|\cdot\|_{m}\coloneqq\|\cdot\|_{H^{m}(\Omega)},\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

for the norms of the Sobolev spaces, where we do not differ between
scalar-valued, vector-valued, and matrix-valued cases.
Throughout, the meaning will be obvious from the context.
For a Banach space
B
and an interval
J\subset[0,T]
, let
L^{2}(J;B)
,

C^{m}(J;B)
, and
C^{0}(J;B)\coloneqq C(J;B)
be the Bochner spaces of

B
-valued functions, equipped with their natural norms.

For the variational setting we define the bilinear forms
a:\bm{V}\times\bm{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
b:\bm{V}\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle a(\bm{u},\bm{v})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\langle\nabla\bm{u},\nabla\bm{v}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\quad\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\,,

(2.1a)

\displaystyle b(\bm{v},q)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\langle\nabla\cdot\bm{v},q\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\quad\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\,,\;q\in Q\,.

(2.1b)

In what follows, for non-negative expressions
a
and

b
, the notation
a\lesssim b
stands for the inequality
a\leq C\,b
with a generic
constant
C
that is independent of the sizes of the spatial and temporal
meshes. The value of
C
can depend on the regularity of the space mesh,
the polynomial degree of the space discretization, and the data
(including
\Omega
).

2.2
Discrete function spaces and operators

For the time discretization, we decompose the time interval

I\coloneqq(0,T]
into
N
subintervals

I_{n}\coloneqq(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
,
n=1,\ldots,N
, where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{N-1}<t_{N}=T
such that
I=\bigcup_{n=1}^{N}I_{n}
. We put
\tau\coloneqq\max_{n=1,\ldots,N}\tau_{n}
with

\tau_{n}\coloneqq t_{n}-t_{n-1}
. Further, the set
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\coloneqq\{I_{1},\ldots,I_{N}\}

of time intervals is called the time mesh. For a Banach space
B
, an
interval
J\subset[0,T]
, and any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let

\mathbb{P}_{k}({J};B)\coloneqq\bigg\{w_{\tau}:J\to B\>:\>w_{\tau}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}W^{j}t^{j}\;\forall t\in J\,,\;W^{j}\in B\;\forall j\bigg\}

(2.2)

be the space of
B
-valued polynomials of degree less than or equal to

k
on
J
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the space of globally continuous and piecewise
polynomial functions of degree less than or equal to
k
in time with values in a Banach space
B
by

X^{k}_{\tau}(B)\coloneqq\left\{w_{\tau}\in C(\overline{I};B)\>:\>w_{\tau}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};B)\;\forall I_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\right\}\,.

(2.3)

For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define the space of piecewise polynomial functions
of degree less than or equal to
k
in time with values in a Banach space
B
by

Y^{k}_{\tau}(B)\coloneqq\left\{{w_{\tau}:I\to B}\>:\>w_{\tau}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(I_{n};B)\;\forall I_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\right\}\,.

(2.4)

For any function
w:\overline{I}\to B
that is piecewise sufficiently
smooth with respect to the time mesh
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}
, for instance for

w\in Y^{k}_{\tau}(B)
, we define the right-sided and left-sided
limits at a time mesh point
t_{n}
by

w^{+}(t_{n})\coloneqq\lim_{t\to t_{n}+0}w(t),\quad n<N\,,\qquad\text{and}\qquad w^{-}(t_{n})\coloneqq\lim_{t\to t_{n}-0}w(t),\quad n>0\,.

(2.5)

Due to the definition of
Y^{k}_{\tau}(B)
, a function
w\in Y^{k}_{\tau}(B)

is continuous from the left, i.e.
w(t_{n})=w^{-}(t_{n})
,
for all points
t_{n}
,
n=1,\dots,N
. Moreover, a function
w\in Y^{k}_{\tau}(B)

is globally continuous, i.e. belongs to the space
X^{k}_{\tau}(B)
, if and only if

w^{+}(t_{n})=w(t_{n}),\quad n=0,\dots,N-1\,.

(2.6)

For the space discretization, let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
be a
shape-regular family of meshes
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the domain
\Omega

consisting of triangular or quadrilateral mesh cells in two space
dimensions and tetrahedral or hexahedral mesh cells in three
space dimensions with mesh size
h>0
.
By
{\bm{V}}(K)
and
Q(K)
,
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we
denote the vector-valued and scalar-valued finite element spaces of mapped polynomials
defined by the reference transformation of polynomials on the
reference element with maximum degree uniformly bounded with
respect to the mesh family

\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
. For convenience, the local spaces
{\bm{V}}(K)
and
Q(K)
are defined implicitly by prescribing their approximation properties in (
2.10
). Exemplified,
\bm{V}(K)
can be considered to consist of polynomials of maximum degree
r
and
Q(K)
of polynomials of maximum degree
r-1
. Then, the finite element spaces to be used
for approximating
\bm{V}
and
Q
are

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{h}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}\;:\;\bm{v}_{h}{}_{|K}\in\bm{V}(K)\;\;\text{for all}\;K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}\,,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle Q_{h}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\bm{q}_{h}\in Q\;:\;\bm{q}_{h}{}_{|K}\in Q(K)\;\;\text{for all}\;K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}\,.

(2.7b)

Further, we define

\bm{V}_{h}^{\text{div}}\coloneqq\{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\;:\;b(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})=0\;\text{for all }q_{h}\in Q_{h}\}

(2.8)

as space of the discretely divergence-free functions.

Now we make further assumptions about the discrete spaces
\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
. By imposing conditions on
\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
about their approximation properties, the polynomial degrees of functions
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
and
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
, needed to meet these demands, are defined implicitly.

Assumption 2.1

The spaces
\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
satisfy the uniform inf-sup (or LBB) condition

0<\beta\leq\inf_{q_{h}\in Q_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}}\dfrac{b(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}\,\|q_{h}\|}

(2.9)

for some constant
\beta
independent of
h
. For
\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\cap\bm{H}^{r+1}(\Omega)
and
q\in Q\cap H^{r}(\Omega)
, there exist
approximations
i_{h}\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{h}
and
j_{h}q\in Q_{h}
such that

\|\bm{v}-i_{h}\bm{v}\|+h\|\nabla(\bm{v}-i_{h}\bm{v})\|\lesssim h^{r+1}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|q-j_{h}q\|\lesssim h^{r}\,.

(2.10)

Examples of pairs of finite element spaces satisfying
Assumption
2.1
can be found, e.g., in
[
12
, Ch. 3]
. In particular, the well-known family of Taylor–Hood pairs of finite element spaces satisfies the assumptions.

Finally, the discrete operators
A_{h}:\bm{V}\rightarrow\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}
,
B_{h}:\bm{V}\rightarrow Q_{h}^{\prime}
, and
B_{h}^{\prime}:Q\rightarrow\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}
are defined by means of

\displaystyle\langle A_{h}\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime},\bm{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq a(\bm{u},\bm{v}_{h})\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for }\bm{u}\in\bm{V}\,,\;\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\,,

(2.11a)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}\bm{v},q_{h}\rangle_{Q_{h}^{\prime},Q_{h}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq b(\bm{v},q_{h})\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for }\bm{v}\in\bm{V}\,,\;q_{h}\in Q_{h}\,,

(2.11b)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}^{\prime}q,\bm{v}_{h}\rangle_{\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime},\bm{V}_{h}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq b(\bm{v}_{h},q)\,,

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for }\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\,,\;q\in Q\,,

(2.11c)

with
the bilinear forms
a
and
b

introduced in (
2.1
). On
\bm{V}_{h}
, the operator
B_{h}
is thus the discrete (negative) divergence operator. By
the representation
theorem of Riesz (cf.
[
12
, Theorem B.3]
), the spaces
\bm{V}_{h}^{\prime}
and
Q_{h}^{\prime}
can be
identified with
\bm{V}_{h}
and
Q_{h}
, respectively. Therefore, for the sake
of brevity, we
skip the indices of the duality pairings. Usually, it holds

\nabla\cdot\bm{v}_{h}\not\in Q_{h}
for
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
. On
\bm{V}_{h}
, the kernel of
B_{h}
is
the space
\bm{V}_{h}^{\text{div}}
defined in (
2.8
). On
Q_{h}
, the
operator
B_{h}^{\prime}
is the discrete gradient operator and, thus, the
adjoint operator of the discrete divergence.

2.3
Quadrature formulas, interpolation operators and Stokes projection

In this work, the 2-point Gauss–Lobatto formula (trapezoidal rule)
and 1-point Gauss quadrature formula (midpoint rule) are applied on
each time interval
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
. On
I_{n}
, the 2-point
Gauss–Lobatto formula is defined by

Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}(g)\coloneqq\frac{\tau_{n}}{2}\big(g^{+}(t_{n-1})+g^{-}(t_{n})\big)\approx\int_{I_{n}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(2.12)

where the values
g^{+}(t_{n-1})
and
g^{-}(t_{n})
denote the
corresponding one-sided limits of
g(t)
from the interior of
I_{n}

(cf. (
2.5
)). Formula (
2.12
) is exact for all
polynomials in
\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})
. On
I_{n}
, with its midpoint

\overline{t}_{n}\coloneqq\frac{t_{n-1}+t_{n}}{2}\,,

(2.13)

we denote by

Q_{n}^{\operatorname{G}}(g)\coloneqq\tau_{n}\,g(\overline{t}_{n})\approx\int_{I_{n}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.14)

the
1
-point Gauss quadrature formula. Formula (
2.14
) is exact for all polynomials in
\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})
.

Next, we define some interpolation operators with respect to the time
variable. For this, let
B
be a Banach space, typically
B\in\{L^{2}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\}
. Firstly, we introduce Lagrange
interpolation operators. For a given function
w\in C(\overline{I};B)
,
we define on each time interval
I_{n}
with midpoint
\overline{t}_{n}

defined in (
2.13
) the local Lagrange interpolants

I_{n,1}^{\operatorname{GL}}w\in\mathbb{P}_{1}({\overline{I}_{n}};B)
and

I_{n,2}^{\operatorname{GL}}w\in\mathbb{P}_{2}({\overline{I}_{n}};B)
by

\displaystyle I_{n,1}^{\operatorname{GL}}w(s)

\displaystyle=w(s)\,,

\displaystyle\quad\;s\in\{{t_{n-1}},t_{n}\}\,,

(2.15a)

\displaystyle I_{n,2}^{\operatorname{GL}}w(s)

\displaystyle=w(s)\,,

\displaystyle\quad\;s\in\{{t_{n-1}},\overline{t}_{n},t_{n}\}\,.

(2.15b)

The global Lagrange interpolants
I_{1}^{\operatorname{GL}}w\in X^{1}_{\tau}(B)
and
I_{2}^{\operatorname{GL}}w\in X^{2}_{\tau}(B)
,
are given by

I_{k}^{\operatorname{GL}}w(t)\coloneqq I_{n,k}^{\operatorname{GL}}w(t)\quad t\in I_{n}\,,\quad n=1,\ldots,N\,,\quad k=1,2\,,

(2.16)

along with
I_{1}^{\operatorname{GL}}w(0)\coloneqq w(0)
and
I_{2}^{\operatorname{GL}}w(0)\coloneqq w(0)
.

For the Lagrange interpolation operators
I_{n,1}^{\mathrm{GL}}
and

I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}
, the error bounds

\displaystyle\|v-I_{n,1}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v\|_{C(\overline{I}_{n};B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau_{n}^{2}\|v\|_{C^{2}(\overline{I}_{n};B)}\,,

(2.17a)

\displaystyle\|v-I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v\|_{C(\overline{I}_{n};B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau_{n}^{3}\|v\|_{C^{3}(\overline{I}_{n};B)}\,,

(2.17b)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v-\partial_{t}I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v\|_{C(\overline{I}_{n};B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\tau_{n}^{2}\|v\|_{C^{3}(\overline{I}_{n};B)}\,.

(2.17c)

hold (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 1 and 3]
).

Further, we construct a special interpolant in time.
For a given function
v\in C(\overline{I};B)
, we define, for each
n=1,\dots,N
, a local interpolant

R_{n}^{1}v\in\mathbb{P}_{1}({\overline{I}_{n}};B)
by

R_{n}^{1}v(t_{n-1})\coloneqq I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n-1})=v(t_{n-1})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}R_{n}^{1}v(\overline{t}_{n})\coloneqq\partial_{t}I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(\overline{t}_{n})

(2.18)

and a global interpolant
R_{\tau}^{1}v\in Y^{1}_{\tau}(B)
by
the initial value
R_{\tau}^{1}v(0)\coloneqq v(0)
and

R_{\tau}^{1}v(t)\coloneqq R_{n}^{1}v(t)\,,\quad t\in I_{n}\,,\quad n=1,\dots,N\,.

(2.19)

In the notation, we skip to mention the Banach space
B
in that
R_{\tau}^{1}
is applied. The meaning is always clear from the context. In this work,
R_{\tau}^{1}
is employed in Hilbert spaces
H\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
. In such spaces, the global operator
R_{\tau}^{1}
and its image have the following properties.

Lemma 2.2

Let
H\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
be a Hilbert space with inner product denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
.
For
v\in C(\overline{I};H)
, the function
R_{\tau}^{1}v\in C(\overline{I};H)
is continuous in time on
\overline{I}
with
R_{\tau}^{1}v(t_{n})=v(t_{n})
for all
n=0,\ldots,N
.

Proof.

To prove the assertion, we firstly show that
R_{n}^{1}v(t_{n})=I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n})=v(t_{n})
for all
n=1,\ldots,N
. For an
arbitrary time-independent test function
w\in H\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
, we
obtain by the fundamental theorem of calculus that

\displaystyle\langle R_{n}^{1}v(t_{n}),w\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle R_{n}^{1}v(t_{n-1}),w\rangle_{H}+\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}R_{n}^{1}v,w\rangle_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\langle I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n-1}),w\rangle_{H}+Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}R_{n}^{1}v,w\rangle_{H})

\displaystyle=\langle I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n-1}),w\rangle_{H}+Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v,w\rangle_{H})\,.

Since
\langle\partial_{t}I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v,w\rangle\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})
and the Gauss
quadrature formula is exact for all polynomials in
\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})
, it follows

\langle R_{n}^{1}v(t_{n}),w\rangle=\langle I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n-1}),w\rangle_{H}+\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v,w\rangle_{H}\,\mathrm{d}t=\langle I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n}),w\rangle_{H}\qquad\forall w\in H\,,

proving that
R_{n}^{1}v(t_{n})=I_{n,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(t_{n})=v(t_{n})
for all
n=1,\ldots,N
.

Now, we show the continuity of
R_{\tau}^{1}\,v
on
[0,T]
. The function
R_{\tau}^{1}\,v
is continuous at
0
, since
(R_{\tau}^{1}\,v)^{+}(0)=I_{1,2}^{\mathrm{GL}}\,v(0)=v(0)=R_{\tau}^{1}\,v(0)
. From the definition (
2.19
) along with (
2.18
) we get, for
n>1
, that
(R_{\tau}^{1}\,v)^{+}(t_{n-1})=R_{n}^{1}\,v(t_{n-1})=v(t_{n-1})=R_{n-1}^{1}\,v(t_{n-1})=R_{\tau}^{1}\,v(t_{n-1})
, which proves the continuity of
R_{\tau}^{1}\,v
at the point
t_{n-1}
for all
n=2,\dots,N
and, thus, the assertion of the lemma.

\blacksquare

We note that Lemma
2.2
implies that the operators

R^{1}_{\tau}
and
I_{1}^{\mathrm{GL}}
coincide.

Lemma 2.3

For all
n=1,\ldots,N
and all
v\in C^{2}(\overline{I}_{n};H)
, there holds

\|v-R_{n}^{1}v\|_{C(\overline{I}_{n};H)}\lesssim\tau_{n}^{2}\|v\|_{C^{2}(\overline{I}_{n};H)}\,.

(2.20)

Proof.

The result follows from
[
5
, Lemma 4.4]
or, since
R^{1}_{\tau}
and
I_{1}^{\mathrm{GL}}
coincide, from (
2.17a
).

\blacksquare

For the error estimation we also need a special projection operator mapping
from
\bm{V}^{\operatorname{div}}\times Q
to
\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}\times Q_{h}
, the so-called Stokes projection (cf.
[
12
, Eq. (4.54)]
), that is defined by means of an auxiliary problem of Stokes type.

Definition 2.4
(Stokes projection)

For
(\bm{u},p)\in\bm{V}^{\operatorname{div}}\times Q
, the Stokes
projection
R_{h}(\bm{u},p)=\big(R_{h}^{\bm{u}}(\bm{u},p),R_{h}^{p}(\bm{u},p)\big)
is defined by

R_{h}^{\bm{u}}(\bm{u},p)\coloneqq\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h}\quad\text{and}\quad R_{h}^{p}(\bm{u},p)\coloneqq\widehat{p}_{h}\,,

(2.21)

where the pair
(\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}

satisfies

\displaystyle\langle A_{h}\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h}+B_{h}^{\prime}\widehat{p}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A_{h}\bm{u}+B_{h}^{\prime}p,\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad\forall\bm{v}_{h}

\displaystyle\in\bm{V}_{h}\,,

(2.22a)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\qquad\forall q_{h}

\displaystyle\in Q_{h}\,.

(2.22b)

Based on (
2.22b
), the velocity part
R_{h}^{\bm{u}}(\bm{u},p)
of the Stokes projection
R_{h}(\bm{u},p)
belongs to the space
{\bm{V}}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}

of discretely divergence-free functions.

Remark 2.5

For arbitrary open intervals
J\subset[0,T]
and functions

(\bm{v},q)\in C^{1}(J;{\bm{V}^{\operatorname{div}}}\times Q)
, the Stokes projection of
Def.
2.4
yields the commutation property

\partial_{t}R_{h}(\bm{v},q)=R_{h}(\partial_{t}\bm{v},\partial_{t}q)

(2.23)

in the interval
J
. The commutation of projection in space and
differentiation in time can be proved easily by taking the limit of the
difference quotient for the time derivative and exploiting the linearity of
the Stokes projection
R_{h}
. The commutation property can be extended in
the same way to higher order temporal derivatives.

Lemma 2.6
(Error estimates for the Stokes projection)

Let the stationary Stokes problem be
H^{2}
-regular in the sense of (A1) in

[
6
]
. Then, the Stokes projection

R_{h}(\bm{u},p)=(\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h},\widehat{p}_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}\times Q_{h}
of Def.
2.4

provides for
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}^{\operatorname{div}}\cap\bm{H}^{r+1}(\Omega)
and
p\in Q\cap H^{r}(\Omega)
the estimates

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}-\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h}\|+h\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r+1}\,,

(2.24a)

\displaystyle\|p-\widehat{p}_{h}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim h^{r}\,.

(2.24b)

Proof.

The estimation of
\|\nabla(\bm{u}-\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h})\|
and
\|p-\widehat{p}_{h}\|
directly follows from
[
12
, Lemma 4.43]
along with the
Assumption
2.1
about the approximation properties of
\bm{V}_{h}

and
Q_{h}
. The estimation
\|\bm{u}-\bm{\widehat{u}}_{h}\|
is then
obtained by an duality argument (cf.
[
6
, Lemma 4.2 and 4.7]
).

\blacksquare

For the rest of the paper, we assume the
H^{2}
-regularity of
the stationary Stokes problem.

3
Approximation scheme and first error estimates

In Subsection
3.1
, we present the fully discrete approximation
scheme for the Stokes system (
1.1
) that uses
linear in time approximations for both velocity and pressure.
The approximation
of the velocity field will be globally continuous in time on
\overline{I}
,
whereas the discrete pressure trajectory may have jumps at the
endpoints of the time subintervals.
Concerning the
velocity, the proposed approach resembles the continuous
Galerkin–Petrov method that is studied for parabolic problems, e.g., in

[
3
,
15
]
.
We will show that our approximation scheme on each time interval
determines uniquely the discrete velocity, while the discrete pressure trajectory is only partially determined.
The remaining degrees of freedom for the discrete pressure will be
determined either by means of a post-processing, which is addressed in
Section
4
, or by interpolation, which is studied in
Section
5
.
In Subsection
3.2
, we prove optimal order error estimates
for the discrete velocity in the endpoints of the time intervals
and
for the discrete pressure in the Gauss quadrature nodes related to the midpoint
rule.

3.1
A linear in time Galerkin method with finite elements in space

For the discretization of the Stokes system (
1.1
),
we solve in a time marching process, for increasing
n=1,\ldots,N
,
the sequence of the following local problems
for the linear in time approximations

(\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t),p_{\tau,h}(t))\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}

of the velocity and pressure trajectories
(\bm{u}(t),p(t))

defined on the subintervals
I_{n}
.
For each interval
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
, we assume
that the trajectory
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
has been already computed by the time
marching process for all
t\in[0,t_{n-1}]
, starting with an
approximation
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(0)=\bm{u}_{0,h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
of
the initial value
\bm{u}_{0}
.

Problem 3.1
(Space-time problem related to
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
)

For given
\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1}\coloneqq\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
with
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{0})\coloneqq\bm{u}_{0,h}
, find

(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})
,
such that
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{n-1})=\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1}
and

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}\Big(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle+{\langle A_{h}\bm{u}_{\tau,h},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle}+{\langle B_{h}^{\prime}p_{\tau,h},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=Q^{\operatorname{GL}}_{n}(\langle\bm{f},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle)\,,

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}{\langle B_{h}\bm{u}_{\tau,h},q_{\tau,h}\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=0

(3.1b)

for all
(\bm{v}_{\tau,h},q_{\tau,h})\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};Q_{h})
.

By expanding the discrete solution
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t),p_{\tau,h}(t))
,

t\in I_{n}
, in terms of temporal basis functions, we can
recast (

) as a space problem related to
I_{n}
for the
corresponding
\bm{V}_{h}
-valued and
Q_{h}
-valued degrees of freedom.
To this end, let the Lagrange basis polynomials
\varphi_{n,k}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\overline{I}_{n};\mathbb{R})
,
k=0,1
, on

\overline{I}_{n}
be defined by the conditions

\displaystyle\varphi_{n,0}(t_{n-1})

\displaystyle=1\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\varphi_{n,0}(\overline{t}_{n})

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\varphi_{n,1}(t_{n-1})

\displaystyle=0\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\varphi_{n,1}(\overline{t}_{n})

\displaystyle=1\,,

(3.2b)

where
\overline{t}_{n}
denotes the midpoint of
I_{n}
(see
(

In terms of these basis functions we can uniquely represent

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
and
p_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})

for all

by

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)=\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1}\varphi_{n,0}(t)+\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n}\varphi_{n,1}(t)\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{\tau,h}(t)=p_{h}^{n-1}\varphi_{n,0}(t)+\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n}\varphi_{n,1}(t)\,,

where the coefficients
\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1},\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n}\in\bm{V}_{h}

\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq\bm{u}^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})\,,\quad\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h}\coloneqq\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\,,\qquad\quad p^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})\,,\quad\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n}\coloneqq p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\,.

(3.4)

We can now substitute the expansions (

). Then, we obtain the following
space problem for the degrees of freedom
\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h}
and
\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n}

of the discrete solution
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})
on

.
For the derivation of Problem

Problem 3.2
(Space problem related to
I_{n}
)

For given
\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1}\coloneqq\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
with
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{0})\coloneqq\bm{u}_{0,h}
,
find
(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h},\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}

such that

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n}-\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle+\frac{\tau_{n}}{2}\langle A_{h}\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n}+B_{h}^{\prime}\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{n}}{4}\langle\bm{f}(t_{n-1})+\bm{f}(t_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle\,,

(3.5a)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(3.5b)

for all
(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
.

Regarding existence and uniqueness of solutions to
Problem
3.2
we have the following result.

Lemma 3.3
(Well-posedness of Problem
3.2
)

Problem
3.2
admits a unique solution
(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h},\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
. Moreover, we have that
\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
.

Proof.

Since Problem
3.2
is equivalent to a finite dimensional
linear system of equations for the coefficients of
\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h}
and
\overline{p}^{n}_{h}

in their basis representation,
the existence of a solution is a direct consequence of its uniqueness.
To prove uniqueness, we suppose that
(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,k}_{h},\overline{p}^{n,k}_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
,

k=1,2
, are two solutions satisfying (
3.5
) with

\bm{u}^{n-1,1}_{h}=\bm{u}^{n-1,2}_{h}=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
Then, for their difference it holds

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,2}_{h},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle+\frac{\tau_{n}}{2}\langle A_{h}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{1,2}_{h}-\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,2}_{h})+B_{h}^{\prime}(\overline{p}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{p}^{n,2}_{h}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,,

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}(\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,2}_{h}),q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(3.6b)

for all
(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
since the difference

\bm{u}^{n-1,1}_{h}-\bm{u}^{n-1,2}_{h}
vanishes.
We choose the test function

\bm{v}_{h}=\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,2}_{h}

in (
3.6a
) and use that

\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
due to (
3.6b
).
Then, the pressure term in (
3.6a
) vanishes and we obtain

\|\bm{v}_{h}\|^{2}+(\tau_{n}/2)\|\nabla\bm{v}_{h}\|^{2}=0

which implies
\bm{v}_{h}=\bm{0}

and, thus,
\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,1}_{h}=\overline{\bm{u}}^{n,2}_{h}
.
Inserting this again into (
3.6a
) we get

\langle B_{h}^{\prime}(\overline{p}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{p}^{n,2}_{h}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle=0

for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
.
The inf-sup stability condition
(
2.9
) then yields the existence of some

\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that

\beta\,\|\overline{p}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{p}^{n,2}_{h}\|\,\leq\,\dfrac{b(\bm{v}_{h},\overline{p}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{p}^{n,2}_{h})}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}}=\dfrac{\langle B_{h}^{\prime}(\overline{p}^{n,1}_{h}-\overline{p}^{n,2}_{h}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}}=0\,.

Hence, we get that
\overline{p}^{n,1}_{h}=\overline{p}^{n,2}_{h}
and the unique solvability follows.
Furthermore, the property
\overline{\bm{u}}^{n}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
is a direct consequence of (
3.5b
).
Thus, Lemma
3.3
is proved.

\blacksquare

Lemma 3.4
(Set of solutions of Problem
3.1
)

The set of all solutions

(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})

of the space-time Problem
3.1

\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}=\bm{u}^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})

\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1}=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
and the pair of coefficients

is the unique solution of Problem
3.2
, whereas the coefficient

p^{n-1}_{h}=p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})\in Q_{h}

can be chosen arbitrarily.

Proof.

Let

(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})\times\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})

be given.
Then, this pair has the unique representation (
3.3
), where the

\bm{u}_{h}^{n-1},\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n}\in\bm{V}_{h}
and

).
Let further
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})

and

be given with the degrees of
freedom
\bm{v}_{h}\coloneqq\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\in\bm{V}_{h}

and

.

We will now show that the system (
3.5
) of
Problem
3.2
arises from the system (
3.1
) of
Problem
3.1
.
Under the above assumptions, the integrands of the time integrals on the left-hand side of the
variational equations of the space-time Problem
3.1

are first order polynomials in time.
Since the one-point Gauss quadrature
formula (

) is exact for these integrals, we obtain
that the variational equations (

)
are equivalent to

\displaystyle\tau_{n}\langle\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle+\tau_{n}\langle A_{h}\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n}+B_{h}^{\prime}\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{n}}{2}\langle\bm{f}(t_{n-1})+\bm{f}(t_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{n}\langle B_{h}\overline{\bm{u}}_{h}^{\,n},q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0

From (

Therefore, the variational equations (
3.1
)

) of the

\bm{u}^{n-1}_{h}\coloneqq\bm{u}^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})

Let now
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},p_{\tau,h})

. Then, it must satisfy the

Furthermore, the variational equations (

Therefore, if the coefficients

Remark 3.5

The statement of Lemma
3.4
provides the following
conclusions.

(i)

Lemma
3.4
shows that, under all solutions of the
space-time Problem
3.1
, the velocity
part
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}
on
I_{n}
is uniquely determined since the two coefficients

in the

Due to (

we have

at

Thus, the time marching process solving the local space-time

generates a unique globally continuous velocity

.

(ii)

Contrary to the velocity part of the solutions of Problem

on

, the pressure part

However, Lemma

also shows that the

\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n}\coloneqq p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})

.
Thus, all the different pressure solutions

of Problem

coincide in the value

.
This should be taken into account in Section

,
where error estimates will be proved for the

.

3.9
) of the errors then implies

Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}e_{\tau,h}^{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle)=T_{n}^{\bm{f}}+T_{n}^{\bm{\eta}}

(3.13)

with

T_{n}^{\bm{f}}\coloneqq\tau_{n}\langle\bm{\delta}_{\bm{f}}^{n},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\rangle\,,\qquad T_{n}^{\bm{\eta}}\coloneqq-Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{\eta}^{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{\eta}^{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}\eta^{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle)

for all
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
, where

\bm{\delta}_{\bm{f}}^{n}\coloneqq\bm{f}(\overline{t}_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\big(\bm{f}(t_{n-1})+\bm{f}(t_{n})\big)
.

By the approximation property (
2.17a
) of

I_{n,1}^{\operatorname{GL}}
we have

\displaystyle|T_{n}^{\bm{f}}|

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{n}\left\|\bm{f}(\overline{t}_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\big(\bm{f}(t_{n+1})+\bm{f}(t_{n})\big)\right\|\,\|\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|

\displaystyle=\tau_{n}\|\bm{f}(\overline{t}_{n})-I_{n,1}^{\text{GL}}\bm{f}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,\|\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\lesssim\tau_{n}\tau_{n}^{2}\|\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

(3.14)

By Lemma
3.7
it holds

|T_{n}^{\bm{\eta}}|\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})\|\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

Thus, we have

Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}e_{\tau,h}^{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle)\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})\|\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|

(3.15)

for all
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
.

Firstly, we choose
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}=\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}
as test function
in (
3.15
). Since
\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}
belongs to

\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};V_{h}^{\operatorname{div}})
(cf. Remark
3.6
) we have

\langle B_{h}^{\prime}q_{h},\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t)\rangle=0

(3.16)

for all
q_{h}\in Q_{h}
and
t\in I_{n}
. From (
3.15
) and (
3.16
) we then get

Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle+\langle A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle)\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

(3.17)

By the definition of the Gauss quadrature formula along with

Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\underbrace{\langle A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle}_{\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})})=\int_{I_{n}}\langle A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{I_{n}}\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\langle A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

it follows from
(
3.17
)
that

\tau_{n}\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n-1})\|^{2}\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

By absorption we get

\frac{1}{2}\tau_{n}\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n-1})\|^{2}\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{4}+h^{2(r+1)})\,.

(3.18)

Changing now the index
n
to
m
and summing up the resulting inequality from
m=1
to
n
show

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+t_{n}(\tau^{4}+h^{2(r+1)})\,.

(3.19)

This proves (
3.12a
).

Secondly, we choose
the test function
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}
in (
3.15
) as those
function from
\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
that has the constant value

\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})
in
I_{n}
.
By the arguments
of (
3.16
)
with
\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})
replacing

\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}
, we then obtain

Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle)+Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle)\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})\|\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

(3.20)

For the first term on the left-hand side of (
3.20
), we get by
the exactness of the 1-point Gauss quadrature formula for all polynomials
in
\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})
that

Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle)=\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}},\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n-1})\|^{2}\,.

(3.21)

Combining (
3.20
) with (
3.21
), applying the inequality of Cauchy–Young to the term on the right-hand side of (
3.20
), using the inequality of Poincaré to absorb
\|\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|
by the left-hand side, changing the index
n
to
m
in the resulting inequality and, finally, summing up the thus obtained relation from
m=1
to
n
show

\|\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(t_{n})\|^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+t_{n}(\tau^{4}+h^{2(r+1)})\,.

(3.22)

This proves (
3.12b
).
\blacksquare

Lemma 3.9
(Estimate of the discrete pressure error
e_{\tau,h}^{p}
)

For the discrete pressure error
e_{\tau,h}^{p}=\widehat{p}_{\tau,h}-p_{\tau,h}
, there
holds

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|e_{\tau,h}^{p}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+\tau^{4}+h^{2(r+1)}

(3.23)

for
n=1,\ldots N
.

Proof.

By the choice of an inf-sup stable pair of finite element spaces
\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
, fulfilling the Assumption
2.1
,
for
\overline{e}_{n,h}^{p}\coloneqq e_{\tau,h}^{p}(\overline{t}_{n})\in Q_{h}
there exists some
function
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
such that

\beta\|\overline{e}_{n,h}^{p}\|\leq\frac{\langle B_{h}^{\prime}\overline{e}_{n,h}^{p},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}}\,,

(3.24)

where the constant
\beta>0
is independent of
h
. Combining
(
3.24
) with equation (
3.13
) yields

\displaystyle\|\overline{e}_{n,h}^{p}\|\lesssim\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\frac{1}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}}\Big(

\displaystyle\tau_{n}\langle\bm{\delta}_{\bm{f}}^{n},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle-\langle\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle-\langle A_{h}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

(3.25)

\displaystyle-Q_{n}^{\text{G}}(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{\eta}^{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{\eta}^{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}\eta^{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle)\Big)\,,

with
\bm{\delta}_{\bm{f}}^{n}
being defined in the proof of
Lemma
3.8
, where
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
is the function taking the constant value
\bm{v}_{h}
in
I_{n}
.
By
the inequality of Cauchy–Schwarz along with the inequalities (
3.11
) and (
3.14
) we deduce from (

) that

\|\overline{e}_{n,h}^{p}\|\lesssim(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})+\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|+\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

(3.26)

Multiplying the squared form of (

) with
\tau_{n}
, using the
inequality of Cauchy–Young, then changing the index from
n
to
m
and
summing up the resulting inequality from
m=1
to
n
yield

\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\overline{e}_{n,h}^{p}\|^{2}\lesssim t_{n}\big(\tau_{n}^{4}+h^{2(r+1)}\big)+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{n}\tau_{m}\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}\,.

(3.27)

Combining (

) with the estimates (
3.12
)
of Lemma

).

\blacksquare

4
Post-processing by collocation

In this section, we propose a post-processing of the velocity and
pressure values computed by Problem
3.1
or
3.2
,
respectively, with the aim of defining a piecewise polynomial pressure trajectory

\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(t)

that is continuous in time on the entire interval

[0,T]
and converges of optimal order with respect to the
mesh sizes in space and time. For the post-processing, we propose and
analyze a collocation technique.
On the interval
I_{n}=(t_{n-1},t_{n}]
, we use a collocation condition at
time
t=t_{n-1}
in order to compute optimal order approximations

\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1}
of
p(t_{n-1})
and

\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1}
of
\partial_{t}\bm{u}(t_{n-1})
.
The post-processing does not change the optimal values

\overline{p}_{h}^{n}=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
of the original pressure trajectory

p_{\tau,h}(t)
in the midpoints
\overline{t}_{n}
of the time intervals

I_{n}

and preserves also the optimal values of the original velocity trajectory

\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)
at the time mesh points
t=t_{n}
.

4.1
Post-processing of velocity and pressure

Definition 4.1
(Local post-processing on
I_{n}
by collocation)

Let
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}})
and

p_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};Q_{h})
, represented by the expansions
in (
3.3
) with an arbitrarily chosen

p_{h}^{n-1}=p_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{n-1})\in Q_{h}
,
be a solution of Problem
3.1
on
I_{n}
.
Then, we define the local post-processed solution

\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(\overline{I}_{n};\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}})

and

\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\overline{I}_{n};Q_{h})

by

\displaystyle\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t)+\bm{c}_{n}^{\bm{u}}\,\vartheta_{n}(t)\,,

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq p_{\tau,h}(t)+d^{p}_{n}\,\vartheta_{n}^{\prime}(t)

(4.1b)

for
t\in I_{n}
. The values of

\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}
,
\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}

and
\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}
at time
t=t_{n-1}
are
defined as the right-sided limits for

t\to t_{n-1}+0
of the corresponding functions with
t\in I_{n}
.
In (
4.1
), the polynomial
\vartheta_{n}\in P_{2}(\overline{I}_{n};\mathbb{R})

is given by

\vartheta_{n}(t)\coloneqq\alpha_{n}(t-t_{n-1})(t-t_{n})\,,

(4.2)

with the constant
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\vartheta_{n}^{\prime}(t_{n-1})=1
.
The coefficients

\bm{c}_{n}^{\bm{u}}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}

and
d_{n}^{p}\in Q_{h}
, appearing in (
4.1
), are chosen such
that at
t=t_{n-1}
the collocation conditions

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})+A_{h}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})+B_{h}^{\prime}\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{f}(t_{n-1}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle\,,

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}),q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0\,,

(4.3b)

hold for all
(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
.

Since
\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})

by (
4.1a
) and (
4.2
), condition (
4.3b
) is
automatically satisfied.
Using (
4.1
), (
4.2
), and

\vartheta_{n}^{\prime}(t_{n-1})=1
, we see that
(
4.3a
)
is equivalent to

\langle\bm{c}^{\bm{u}}_{n}+B_{h}^{\prime}d^{p}_{n},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle=\langle\bm{f}(t_{n-1})-\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})-A_{h}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})-B_{h}^{\prime}p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

(4.4a)

for all
\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}
. In order to ensure

\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(\overline{I}_{n};\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}})
, the
condition

\langle B_{h}\bm{c}_{n}^{\bm{u}},q_{h}\rangle=0\qquad\forall q_{h}\in Q_{h}

(4.4b)

has to be satisfied. A closer look to (
4.4
) shows that a
Stokes-like saddle-point problem with the mass matrix instead of the
stiffness matrix has to be solved to obtain the coefficients

\bm{c}^{\bm{u}}_{n}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}

and
d^{p}_{n}\in Q_{h}
.

Remark 4.2
(Uniqueness of the post-processed solution

(\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h},\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h})
)

Although the pressure solution
p_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}
of Problem
3.1

is not unique (see Lemma
3.4
), we will show that
the post-processed solution
(\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}^{n}
,
{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}^{n})
due to
Def.
4.1
is unique in the entire set of solutions.
The definition of
\vartheta_{n}
in (
4.2
) gives

\vartheta_{n}(t_{n-1})=0,\qquad\vartheta_{n}(t_{n})=0,\qquad\vartheta^{\prime}_{n}(\overline{t}_{n})=0\,.

(4.5)

Hence, it holds

\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})=\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\,,\quad m\in\{n-1,n\},\qquad\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\,,\qquad\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=\partial_{t}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\,.

(4.6)

By the condition (
4.3
), the quantities

\displaystyle\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})=\partial_{t}\bm{u}^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})+\bm{c}^{\bm{u}}_{n}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}\,,

(4.7a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})=p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})+d^{p}_{n}\in Q_{h}

(4.7b)

are defined uniquely and independent of the choice of the
degree of freedom
p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
in the set of
pressure solutions
p_{\tau,h}
.
In fact, if we subtract in (
4.3
) the known data

A_{h}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})=A_{h}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})

tested with
\bm{v}_{h}

on both sides of the identity, we get that the pair

(\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1},\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1})\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}\times Q_{h}

solves the problem

\langle\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1}+B_{h}^{\prime}\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle=\langle\bm{f}(t_{n-1})-A_{h}\bm{u}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle\qquad\forall\,\bm{v}_{h}\in\bm{V}_{h}\,,

(4.8)

where the right-hand side is independent of the choice of

p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
Existence and uniqueness of the solution
(\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1},\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1})

of (
4.8
) can be proved similarly to
Lemma
3.3
by showing the uniqueness.
Since the quantities
\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1}={\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}^{n}(t_{n-1})

and
\overline{p}^{n}_{h}=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})={\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})

are uniquely determined (cf. (
4.6
)), we conclude that the post-processed pressure

\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\overline{I}_{n};Q_{h})
,
which is given by

{\widetilde{p}}_{\tau,h}^{n}(t)=\widetilde{p}_{h}^{n-1}\varphi_{n,0}(t)+\overline{p}_{h}^{\,n}\varphi_{n,1}(t)\,,\quad t\in\overline{I}_{n}\,,

(4.9)

(see (
3.3
))
is uniquely determined as well.
Due to (
4.7a
), the coefficient
\bm{c}^{\bm{u}}_{n}
is
given by

\bm{c}^{\bm{u}}_{n}=\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1}-\partial_{t}\bm{u}^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.
Its uniqueness follows from the uniqueness of
\bm{a}_{h}^{n-1}

and of
\bm{u}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}
.
Thus, the definition (
4.1a
) implies that
\bm{\widetilde{u}}_{\tau,h}
is
determined uniquely and independent of the choice of

p^{+}_{\tau,h}(t_{n-1})
.

A key observation now is that the collocation condition
(
4.3a
) at the left boundary point
t_{n-1}
of the
interval
I_{n}
implies a corresponding collocation
condition also at the right boundary point
t_{n}
. This will be proved
in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.3
(Collocation condition for
t=t_{n}
)

On
\overline{I}_{n}
, let
(\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h},\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h})\in\mathbb{P}_{2}(\overline{I}_{n};\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}})\times\mathbb{P}_{1}(\overline{I}_{n};Q_{h})
be defined by (
4.1
) along
with (
4.3
). Then, for the time
t=t_{n}
the collocation conditions

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n})+A_{h}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n})+B_{h}^{\prime}\widetilde{p}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{f}(t_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle\,,

(4.10a)

\displaystyle\langle B_{h}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h}(t_{n}),q_{h}\rangle

\displaystyle=0

(4.10b)

hold for all
(\bm{v}_{h},q_{h})\in\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
.

Proof.

We start with proving (
4.10a
).
The properties (
4.5
) of
\vartheta_{n}
and the
exactness of the Gauss–Lobatto quadrature formula (
2.12
)
for all polynomials in
\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n};\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{\operatorname{GL}}\big(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{\widetilde{u}}^{n}_{\tau,h},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle\big)

Q_{n}^{\operatorname{G}}\big(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{E}_{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{E}_{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}E_{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle\big)=Q_{n}^{\operatorname{G}}\big(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{f},{\bm{v}_{\tau,h}}\rangle\big)-Q_{n}^{\operatorname{G}}\big(\langle\partial_{t}L_{n}\bm{f},{\bm{v}_{\tau,h}}\rangle\big)

(4.35)

for all
\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(I_{n};\bm{V}_{h})
. The splitting (
4.32
) of the errors then implies

Q_{n}^{\operatorname{G}}\big(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}E_{\tau,h}^{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle\big)=T_{n}^{\partial_{t}\bm{f}}+T_{n}^{\bm{\widehat{\eta}}}\,,

(4.36a)

where

T_{n}^{\partial_{t}\bm{f}}\coloneqq\tau_{n}\langle\bm{\widehat{\delta}}_{n}^{\bm{f}},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle\,,\qquad T_{n}^{\bm{\widehat{\eta}}}\coloneqq-Q_{n}^{\operatorname{G}}\big(\langle\partial_{t}\bm{\widehat{\eta}}^{\bm{u}}+A_{h}\bm{\widehat{\eta}}^{\bm{u}}+B_{h}^{\prime}\widehat{\eta}^{p},\bm{v}_{\tau,h}\rangle\big)

(4.36b)

with
\bm{v}_{h}\coloneqq\bm{v}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})
and
\bm{\widehat{\delta}}_{n}^{\bm{f}}\coloneqq\partial_{t}\bm{f}(\overline{t}_{n})-\partial_{t}L_{n}\bm{f}(\overline{t}_{n})
. By (
4.24
) we obtain

|T_{n}^{\partial_{t}\bm{f}}|\lesssim\tau_{n}\|\partial_{t}(\bm{f}-L_{n}\bm{f})\|_{C(\overline{I}_{n};\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega))}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|\lesssim\tau_{n}\tau_{n}^{2}\|\bm{v}_{h}\|\,.

(4.37)

Similarly to Lemma
3.7
, it can be shown

|T_{n}^{\bm{\widehat{\eta}}}|\lesssim\tau_{n}(\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1})\|\bm{v}_{h}\|\,.

(4.38)

Following the lines of Lemma
3.8
, we deduce from (
4.36
) to (
4.38
) the assertion (
4.34
).

\blacksquare

It remains to provide suitable bounds for norms of
\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)
.
This will be addressed in Section
4.4
.

Lemma 4.9

For the discrete pressure error

, defined in (

), there holds

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tau_{n}\|E^{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+\tau^{4}+h^{{2(r+1)}}\,.

(4.39)

Proof.

We follow the lines of the proof for Lemma
3.9
. By the choice of an inf-sup stable pair of finite element spaces
\bm{V}_{h}\times Q_{h}
, fulfilling the Assumption
2.1
, for

there exists some

such that

\beta\|\overline{E}_{n,h}^{p}\|\leq\dfrac{\langle B_{h}^{\prime}\overline{E}_{n,h}^{p},\bm{v}_{h}\rangle}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}}\,,

(4.40)

where the constant

is independent of

) with the error equation (

\|\overline{E}_{n,h}^{p}\|\lesssim\frac{1}{\|\bm{v}_{h}\|_{1}}\big(|\langle\partial_{t}\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})+A_{h}\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n}),\bm{v}_{h}\rangle|+\tau_{n}^{-1}|T_{n}^{\partial_{t}\bm{f}}|+\tau_{n}^{-1}|T_{n}^{\bm{\widehat{\eta}}}|\big)\,.

(4.41)

By the inequality of Cauchy–Schwarz along with (
4.37
) and (
4.38
), we deduce from (
4.41
) that

\|\overline{E}_{n,h}^{p}\|\lesssim\tau_{n}^{2}+h^{r+1}+\|\partial_{t}\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|+\|\nabla\bm{E}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

Squaring (

\tau_{n}

to

\blacksquare

Theorem 4.10
(Error estimate for the post-processed pressure)

For the error of the post-processed pressure

Proof.

By the definition of

in (

) there holds

\displaystyle E^{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})

\displaystyle=\widehat{P}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})-\partial_{t}\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=R_{h}^{p}(R_{\tau}^{1}\partial_{t}\bm{u}(\overline{t}_{n}),R_{\tau}^{1}\partial_{t}p(\overline{t}_{n}))-\partial_{t}\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\,.

Setting

to

Exploiting the fact that

\int_{I_{n}}\|p_{\tau}(t)-\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\;\lesssim\;\tau_{n}\|p_{\tau}(t_{n})-\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{n})\|^{2}+\tau_{n}\|p_{\tau}(\overline{t}_{n})-\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})\|^{2}\,.

We sum up from
n=1
to
N
,
use

\,p_{\tau}(\overline{t}_{n})-\widetilde{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=e_{p}(\overline{t}_{n})\,
,
which follows from (
4.48
),
apply (
4.51

) for

By summation from

to

Definition 5.1
(Local interpolation operator
J_{n}
)

For a Banach space
B
and the time mesh
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}
, we
define

C^{-1}_{\tau}(I;B)\coloneqq\left\{{q:I\to B}:q_{|I_{n}}\in C^{0}(I_{n};B),\;q^{+}(t_{n-1})\;\text{exists}\;\;\forall I_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{\tau}\right\}\,,

(5.1)

where
q^{+}(t_{n-1})
denotes the right-sided limit at the mesh
point
t_{n-1}
(cf. (
2.5
)). Now, for
n\geq 2
,
we define the
local interpolation operator

J_{n}:{C^{-1}_{\tau}(I;B)}\to\mathbb{P}_{1}([t_{n-2},t_{n}];B)

by means of linear interpolation of the values
q(\overline{t}_{n-1})
and

q(\overline{t}_{n})
, such that

J_{n}q(t)\coloneqq q(\overline{t}_{n-1})+\frac{t-\overline{t}_{n-1}}{\overline{t}_{n}-\overline{t}_{n-1}}\big(q(\overline{t}_{n})-q(\overline{t}_{n-1})\big),\qquad t\in[t_{n-2},t_{n}]\,.

(5.2)

We note that
J_{n}
is a linear operator. For
B=Q
, we can easily show

\|J_{n}q(t)\|\lesssim\|q(\overline{t}_{n-1})\|+\|q(\overline{t}_{n})\|,\qquad t\in[t_{n-2},t_{n}]\,,\;n\geq 2\,.

(5.3)

From standard arguments of interpolation theory we get for a smooth function
q\in C^{2}(\overline{I};Q)
that

\|q(t)-J_{n}q(t)\|\lesssim\tau^{2}\|q\|_{C^{2}(\overline{I};Q)},\qquad t\in[t_{n-2},t_{n}]\,,\;n\geq 2\,.

(5.4)

Using the operators
J_{n}
, for
n=2,\dots,N
, with
B=Q_{h}
, we can now
define the post-processed and computationally cheap pressure trajectory
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}:\overline{I}\to Q_{h}
.

Definition 5.2
(Post-processed pressure trajectory
\overline{p}_{\tau,h}
)

For a given solution

p_{\tau,h}\in Y_{\tau}^{1}(Q_{h})\subset C^{-1}_{\tau}(I;Q_{h})

from the set of the pressure solutions
according to Lemma
3.4
, we define the
post-processed pressure trajectory
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}:\overline{I}\to Q_{h}
by

{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}J_{2}p_{\tau,h}(t),&t\in\overline{I}_{1}\,,\\[4.30554pt]
J_{n}p_{\tau,h}(t),&t\in I_{n}\,,n\geq 2\,.\end{cases}

(5.5)

The construction of
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
is sketched in Figure
5.1
. By the definition of
J_{n}p_{\tau,h}
in (
5.2
) we have
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=p_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{n})=\overline{p}_{h}^{n}
for all
n=1,\dots,N
,
which shows that the post-processed pressure
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
also belongs to the
set of the pressure solutions (cf. Lemma
3.4
).

In the following, we will estimate the error of the post-processed pressure

{\overline{e}}^{p}(t)\coloneqq p(t)-{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}(t)\,.

(5.6)

In order to split
{\overline{e}}^{p}(t)
, we define the time-dependent index function
n:\overline{I}\to\mathbb{N}
by

n(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}2,&t\in\overline{I}_{1}\,,\\[2.84526pt]
m,&t\in I_{m}\,,\;m\geq 2\,.\end{cases}

(5.7)

For fixed
t\in\overline{I}
and with the abbreviation
n=n(t)
,
the value
J_{n}q(t)
is well defined for any function
q\in C^{-1}_{\tau}(\overline{I};B)
by (
5.2
) since
t\in[t_{n-2},t_{n}]

for
n=n(t)
. By the index function (
5.7
), the
definition (
5.5
) of
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
can be written shortly as

{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}(t)\coloneqq J_{n(t)}p_{\tau,h}(t)
for each
t\in\overline{I}
. For
fixed
t\in\overline{I}
, we split the pressure error
{\overline{e}}^{p}(t)

according to

{\overline{e}}^{p}(t)=\overline{\eta}^{p}(t)+{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\,,\qquad\overline{\eta}^{p}(t)\coloneqq p(t)-J_{n(t)}p(t)\,,\quad{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\coloneqq J_{n(t)}p(t)-J_{n(t)}p_{\tau,h}(t)\,.

(5.8)

For the interpolation error
\overline{\eta}^{p}
,
estimate (
5.4
) implies

\|\overline{\eta}^{p}(t)\|=\|p(t)-J_{n(t)}p(t)\|\lesssim\tau^{2},\qquad t\in\overline{I}\,.

(5.9)

Lemma 5.3
(Estimate of
{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}
)

Suppose that the time step sizes
\tau_{m}
,
m=1,\dots,N
,
satisfy the conditions

\tau_{1}\leq c_{1}\tau_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tau_{m}\leq c_{2}\tau_{m-1}\,,\quad m=2,\ldots,N\,,

(5.10)

with some fixed positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.
Then, for the error
{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}
defined in (
5.8
), it holds

\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tau_{m}\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+\tau^{4}+h^{2r}\,.

(5.11)

Proof.

For any fixed time
t=t_{m}\in[t_{n-2},t_{n}]
with
m\in\{1,\dots,N\}
,
we use the abbreviation
n=n(t_{m})
and get for the error
{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}

defined in (
5.8
) that

{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})=J_{n}p(t_{m})-J_{n}p_{\tau,h}(t_{m})=J_{n}(p-p_{\tau,h})(t_{m})=J_{n}q(t_{m})\,,

where
q\coloneqq p-p_{\tau,h}\in C^{-1}_{\tau}(I;Q)
.
Then, the application of (
5.3
) for
t=t_{m}
implies

\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|\lesssim\|(p-p_{\tau,h})(\overline{t}_{n-1})\|+\|(p-p_{\tau,h})(\overline{t}_{n})\|\,.

(5.12)

We recall the notation
e_{p}(t)\coloneqq(p-p_{\tau,h})(t)
from Theorem
3.10
.
In the case
m\geq 2
, we have
n=n(t_{m})=m
such that (
5.12
)
yields the estimate

\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|\lesssim\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m-1})\|+\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m})\|\,,\qquad m=2,\dots,N\,.

(5.13)

In the case
m=1
, we have
n=n(t_{1})=2
and get from (
5.12
)
that

\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{1})\|\lesssim\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{1})\|+\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{2})\|\,.

(5.14)

Combining the assumptions made in (
5.10
) with (
5.13
) and (
5.14
),
we deduce the estimate

\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tau_{m}\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tau_{m}\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}\,.

(5.15)

From (
5.15
) along with the results of Theorem
3.10
we directly conclude
the assertion (
5.11
).

\blacksquare

We note that condition (
5.10
) excludes an extremely rapid
change of the time step size of neighboring time intervals.

Theorem 5.4

Suppose that condition (
5.10
) is satisfied. Then, the
post-processed pressure
\overline{p}_{\tau,h}
of
Def.
5.2
and the right-sided limits
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{m-1})
satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tau_{m}\|p(t_{m-1})-\overline{p}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{m-1})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+\tau^{4}+h^{2r}\,,

(5.16a)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|p(t)-\overline{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|+\tau^{2}+h^{r}\,.

(5.16b)

Proof.

For all
t\in I_{m}=(t_{m-1},t_{m}]
, we have from (
5.7
) that

n(t)=2
if
m=1
and
n(t)=m
if
m\geq 2
.
Therefore, the index function
n(t)
has the constant value

n(t_{m})\geq 2
for all
t\in I_{m}
,
which implies for
{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}
in the splitting (
5.8
)
that

{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t)=J_{n}e_{p}(t)\,,\qquad n=n(t_{m})\,,\quad t\in I_{m}\,,

(5.17)

where
e_{p}\coloneqq p-p_{\tau,h}
.
Since
I_{m}\subset[t_{n-2},t_{n}]
, where
n=n(t_{m})
, we get from the
definition of
J_{n}
in (
5.2
) that
{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{m}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{m};Q)
.
Thus, by interpolation arguments we can easily show

\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\|\lesssim\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{m})\|+\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|\,,\qquad t\in I_{m}\,.

(5.18)

From (
5.17
) and the definition of
J_{n}
we obtain

{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(\overline{t}_{m})=e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m})
.
Thus, applying (
5.9
) and the splitting

{\overline{e}}^{p}=\overline{\eta}^{p}+{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}
in (
5.8
), we conclude

\|{\overline{e}}^{p}(t)\|^{2}\lesssim\|\overline{\eta}^{p}(t)\|^{2}+\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\|^{2}\lesssim\tau^{4}+\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|^{2}\,,\qquad t\in I_{m}\,.

(5.19)

If we take in (
5.19
) the right-sided limit
for
t\to t_{m-1}+0
, we get

\|{{\overline{e}}^{p}}^{+}(t_{m-1})\|^{2}=\|p(t_{m-1})-\overline{p}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{m-1})\|^{2}\lesssim\tau^{4}+\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|^{2}\,,

(5.20)

where we have assumed global continuity of the pressure trajectory
p(t)
.
Now, in order to get the assertion (
5.16a
),
we multiply (
5.20
) by
\tau_{m}
, take the sum over all

m
from
1
to
N
, and apply Theorem
3.10
together with
estimate (
5.11
) of Lemma
5.3
.
If we integrate (
5.19
) over
t\in I_{m}
, we get

\int_{I_{m}}\|{\overline{e}}^{p}(t)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{I_{m}}\|p(t)-\overline{p}_{\tau,h}(t)\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\tau_{m}\tau^{4}+\tau_{m}\|e_{p}(\overline{t}_{m})\|^{2}+\tau_{m}\|{\overline{e}}^{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m})\|^{2}\,.

(5.21)

Finally, assertion (
5.16b
) follows by summation of (
5.21
)
over all
m
from
1
to
N
and applying Theorem
3.10
and
Lemma
5.3

Remark 5.5

We will comment on the results above.

a)

In contrast to the approach of Section
4

based on post-processing by collocation, we do not need here an
approximation
\bm{a}_{0,h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
of
\bm{a}_{0}=\partial_{t}\bm{u}(0)

in order to
determine the initial values
\bm{u}_{0,h}
and
p_{0,h}
(cf. Section
4.4
). It is sufficient to determine
\bm{u}_{0,h}\in\bm{V}_{h}^{\operatorname{div}}
such that
\|\nabla(\bm{u}_{0}-\bm{u}_{0,h})\|\lesssim h^{r}
.
In particular, this is satisfied for the choice
\bm{u}_{0,h}=R^{\bm{u}}_{h}(\bm{u}_{0},0)
which corresponds to the solution of a Stokes
problem. Then, for the resulting velocity error at
t=0
in
estimates (
5.16
), we get
\|\nabla\bm{e}^{\bm{u}}_{\tau,h}(0)\|\lesssim h^{r}
.

b)

Theorem
5.4
shows the optimal order approximation of the
post-processed pressure
\overline{p}_{\tau,h}
in the norm of
L^{2}(I;Q)

and in a discrete
L^{2}
-norm in time.
These error norms will be also used in the next section to measure
the pressure error in our numerical experiments.
The post-processed pressure trajectory

\overline{p}_{\tau,h}

is in general discontinuous at the discrete time nodes
t_{n}

(cf. Figure
5.1
).
From estimate (
5.11
) together with the assumption

\tau_{m}\leq c_{2}\tau_{m-1}
,
m=2,\dots,N
, and estimate (
5.9
) for

\|\overline{\eta}^{p}(t_{m-1})\|
, we get

\sum_{m=2}^{N}\tau_{m}\|p(t_{m-1})-\overline{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m-1})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+\tau^{4}+h^{2r}\,.

Together with (
5.16a
)
this shows that the jumps in the discontinuous pressure
\overline{p}_{\tau,h}

at the discrete time nodes are small in the sense that

\sum_{m=2}^{N}\tau_{m}\|\overline{p}_{\tau,h}^{+}(t_{m-1})-\overline{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{m-1})\|^{2}\lesssim\|\nabla\bm{e}_{\tau,h}^{\bm{u}}(0)\|^{2}+\tau^{4}+h^{2r}\,.

We note that the jump at
t=0
is zero since, by definition of
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
, we have
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}(0)=J_{2}p_{\tau,h}(0)={\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}^{+}(0)
.

c)

Alternatively, instead of computing
\overline{p}_{\tau,h}
we can define a continuous pressure trajectory
\widehat{p}_{\tau,h}\in C(\overline{I};Q_{h})
such that

\widehat{p}_{\tau,h}{}_{|I_{n}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(I_{n},Q_{h})
for
n=1,\dots,N
and

\widehat{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{n})=\overline{p}_{\tau,h}(t_{n})
for
n=0,\dots,N
. However, we note that the pair
(\bm{u}_{\tau,h},\widehat{p}_{\tau,h})
will in general not satisfy the variational problem (
3.1
) on the subinterval
I_{n}
.
On the other hand, we directly get from Theorem
5.4

the corresponding error estimates also for the post-processed pressure

\widehat{p}_{\tau,h}
instead of
{\overline{p}}_{\tau,h}
.

6
Numerical experiments

In this section we illustrate by numerical experiments the error
estimates proved in Theorem
3.10
for the pressure approximation
in the Gauss quadrature nodes
\overline{t}_{n}
, in Theorem
4.10

for post-processing of the pressure variable by collocation and,
finally, in Theorem
5.4
for post-processing of the pressure
variable by interpolation.
The required discrete initial quantities at time
t=0
were chosen in such
a way (see below) that the resulting initial error terms in the
corresponding error estimates are bounded as in
(
4.60
) of Lemma
4.13
.
The implementation of the numerical scheme was
done in an in-house high-performance frontend solver
[
1
]
for the

deal.II
library
[
2
]
.

In the numerical experiments, the pressure error
p-p_{\tau,h}
is
measured in the natural norm of
L^{2}(I;B)
, shortly denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(B)}
, where
B=L^{2}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, the
velocity errors
\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}\coloneqq\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{\tau,h}
and

\partial_{t}\bm{e}_{\bm{u}}
are also computed with respect to the norm
of
L^{2}(I;B)
, with
B=\bm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and
B=\bm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
,
respectively (see Theorem
3.11
).
For the computation of the
L^{2}(I;B)

norm of the error, a quadrature formula of high accuracy is used for
time integration.

dependent norms (cf. (

) and (

))

\displaystyle\|w\|_{\overline{l^{2}}(B)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tau_{m}\|w(\overline{t}_{m})\|_{B}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,,\qquad\|w\|_{l^{2}(B)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{N}\tau_{m}\|w^{+}(t_{m-1})\|_{B}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\,,

(6.1)

defined for any function

that is piecewise
sufficiently smooth with respect to the time mesh
\mathcal{M}_{\tau}

such that the one-sided limits

\displaystyle p(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\cos(x_{2}\pi)\cdot\sin(x_{1}\pi)\cdot\cos(x_{1}\pi)\cdot\sin(x_{2}\pi)\cdot\sin(t)\,.

\Omega

Thermoelastic plates with type I heat conduction with second gradient.
Jaime Muñoz Rivera, Elena Ochoa Ochoa, Ramón Quintanilla, National Laboratory for Scientific Computation, Petropolis, Brazil

Abstract
This paper investigates the qualitative properties of thermoelastic plates modeled by the second-gradient theory with a Type I heat equation. We establish the exponential stability of the solutions. Our main contribution is to prove that the semigroup is non-differentiable when the bi-Laplacian operator appears in the heat equation. Additionally, we analyze the case where the elastic parameter is negative, demonstrating the uniqueness and instability of the solutions. Finally, in the one-dimensional quasi-static case, we demonstrate the existence and exponential decay of the solutions under specific conditions.



Keywords and phrases
: Euler Bernoulli equation, semigroup theory, smoothing effect.

Notably, introducing the bi-Laplacian operator into certain couplings has been shown to eliminate the exponential stability that would otherwise be present

Recently, there has been a growing interest in studying the impact of higher-order spatial derivatives on various thermomechanical issues. These equations are well-established in the context of the elastic component and have been studied for several years. However, there has also been an increased focus on incorporating these terms into equations that governing porosity and heat transfer. The interest in heat equations may be driven by phenomena observed in various gases. Furthermore, including gradient effects in thermomechanical models has been shown to be significant

\displaystyle U_{t}=\mathcal{A}U,\ \ U(0)=(u^{0},v^{0},\theta^{0}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}U=\begin{pmatrix}v\\

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A})=H_{*}^{4}(\Omega)\times[H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)]\times H_{*}^{4}(\Omega),

\displaystyle\mbox{Re }\left\langle\mathcal{A}U,U\right\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}(b\left|\nabla\theta\right|^{2}+d\left|\Delta\theta\right|^{2})\ d\Omega\leq 0,\quad\forall U\in D(\mathcal{A}).

The resolvent equation

\displaystyle i\omega u-v=g_{1}\in H^{2},

(2.10)

\displaystyle i\rho\omega v+c\Delta^{2}u-\eta\Delta\theta=g_{2}\in L^{2},

(2.11)

\displaystyle ia\omega\theta-b\Delta\theta+d\Delta^{2}\theta+\eta\Delta v=g_{3}\in L^{2}.

(2.12)

From (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}b\left(\left|\nabla\theta\right|^{2}+d\left|\Delta\theta\right|^{2}\right)\ d\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{Re }(U,G)_{\mathcal{H}}.

\displaystyle=-\rho g_{2},

Exponential Stability.

In this section, we will prove that the solutions to the problems studied in the previous section decay exponentially. Our main tool is the following result by J. Pruess.

]

Theorem 3.1

Let
S(t)
be a contraction

-semigroup, generated by
{\mathcal{A}}

over a Hilbert space
{\cal H}

\|S(t)\|\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad i\,\mathbb{R}\subset\varrho({\mathcal{A}})\,\mbox{ and }\,\|(i\,\lambda\,I-{\mathcal{A}})^{-1}\|_{{\cal L}({\cal H})}\leqslant M,\;\;\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.16)

Our first step in proving exponential stability is to show that the imaginary axis is contained at the resolvent set
\varrho(\mathcal{A})

Lemma 3.2

The operator
\mathcal{A}
defined by (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)
verifies

.

Proof.-

Since the domain

is compactly embedded in the phase space

, it suffices to show that

has no imaginary eigenvalues. We proceed by contradiction. Suppose there exists a non-zero

such that

5
Impossibility of localization

Although the semigroup is not analytic, we can employ alternative arguments to demonstrate that the only solution that is identically zero on a set of non-zero measure is the trivial solution. To this end, it is sufficient to prove the uniqueness of solutions for the backward-in-time system

\displaystyle\rho u_{tt}=-c\Delta^{2}u+\eta\Delta\theta,

\displaystyle a\theta_{t}=d\Delta^{2}\theta-b\Delta\theta-\eta\Delta u_{t}.

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions (
1.3
).

To show the uniqueness of the solutions to this problem it is enough to show that the only solution for the problem with the null initial conditions

\displaystyle u(x,0)=u_{t}(x,0)=\theta(x,0)=0,\ \ \forall x\in\Omega.

As usual, we will use the argument from the Lagrange identities.
First, we consider the functions

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{1}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho\left|u_{t}\right|^{2}+c\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}+a\left|\theta\right|^{2}\right)\ d\Omega,

and

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{2}(t)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\rho\left|u_{t}\right|^{2}+c\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}-a\left|\theta\right|^{2}\right)\ d\Omega.

We get

\displaystyle\textperiodcentered\dot{\mathscr{L}}_{1}(t)=\int_{\Omega}\left(b\left|\nabla\theta\right|^{2}+d\left|\Delta\theta\right|^{2}\right)\ d\Omega,

and

\displaystyle\dot{\mathscr{L}}_{2}(t)=-\int_{\Omega}\left(b\left|\nabla\theta\right|^{2}+d\left|\Delta\theta\right|^{2}\right)\ d\Omega+2\eta\int_{\Omega}u_{t}\Delta\theta\ d\Omega.

In order to obtain an alternating expression to the definition of the function
\mathscr{L}_{2}(t)
we consider the following relations

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{tt}(s)u_{t}(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}c\Delta u(s)\Delta u_{t}(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\eta\Delta\theta(s)u_{t}(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho u_{tt}(2t-s)u_{t}(s)\ d\Omega ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}c\Delta u(2t-s)\Delta u_{t}(s)\ d\Omega ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\eta\Delta\theta(2t-s)u_{t}(s)\ d\Omega ds,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}a\theta_{t}(s)\theta(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}d\Delta\theta(s)\Delta\theta(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}b\nabla\theta(s)\nabla\theta(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\eta\Delta u_{t}(s)\theta(2t-s)\ d\Omega ds,

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}a\theta_{t}(2t-s)\theta(s)\ d\Omega ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}d\Delta\theta(2t-s)\Delta\theta(s)\ d\Omega ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}b\nabla\theta(2t-s)\nabla\theta(s)\ d\Omega ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\eta\Delta u_{t}(2t-s)\theta(s)\ d\Omega ds.

By combining
these
equalities with alternative signs, integrating with respect to time, and taking into account the initial conditions, we find the following relation:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(a\left|\theta\right|^{2}+c\left|\Delta u\right|^{2})\ d\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\rho\left|u_{t}\right|^{2}\ d\Omega.

If we go to the definition of the function
\mathscr{L}_{2}(t)
and consider this previous equality we find

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{2}(t)=\int_{\Omega}c\left|\Delta u\right|^{2}\ d\Omega.

Therefore if we
select

\epsilon<1
and consider

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(t)=\mathscr{L}_{2}(t)+\epsilon\mathscr{L}_{1}(t),

we see that

\displaystyle\dot{\mathscr{L}}(t)

\displaystyle=-(1-\epsilon)\int_{\Omega}\left(b\left|\nabla\theta\right|^{2}+d\left|\Delta\theta\right|^{2}\right)\ d\Omega+2\eta\int_{\Omega}u_{t}\Delta\theta\ d\Omega

\displaystyle\leq k\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{t}\right|^{2}\ d\Omega,\ \ k>0,

after the use of the Holder inequality. As
\epsilon>0
we can also see that

\displaystyle\dot{\mathscr{L}}(t)\leq k^{*}\mathscr{L}(t),

where

It then follows that

\mathscr{L}(t)\leq\mathscr{L}(0)e^{k^{*}t}=0,

as
\mathscr{L}(0)=0.

Therefore, we obtain that
\mathscr{L}(t)=0
for every

and we can conclude:

Theorem 5.1

Let
(u,\alpha)
be a solution to the problem determined for the backward in time system with null initial conditions. Then

Our system becomes

8
Conclusion

In this article, we have analyzed the system of equations that governs the thermoelastic deformation of a plate, where heat conduction is modeled by the Green-Naghdi Type I theory with higher-order spatial derivatives. Assuming standard conditions on the constitutive coefficients, we have established the following qualitative properties:

1.

Existence of a semigroup that defines the solutions in an appropriate Hilbert space. Uniqueness of solutions is also satisfied.

2.

Exponential stability of the solutions.

3.

Non-differentiability of the semigroup, implying non-analyticity.

4.

Impossibility of localizing the solutions.

Later, we consider the case when the elastic parameter is negative and we show:

1.

Uniqueness and instability of solutions.

2.

For the one-dimensional quasi-static problem, existence and exponential stability of solutions, provided specific parameter conditions are satisfied.

It is instructive to compare these results with those for the classical case, where higher-order derivatives in heat conduction are absent. Properties 1, 2, and 4 align closely with the classical setting; however, property 3 marks a significant difference. Notably, the introduction of stronger dissipation unexpectedly leads to a loss of solution regularity, a phenomenon that merits further attention. Properties 5 and 6 are novel, even in the absence of higher-order derivatives.

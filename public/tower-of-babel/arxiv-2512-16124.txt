1
Introduction

A principal direction of research on conditioned random walks concerns the
asymptotic behaviour of trajectories constrained to remain in a prescribed
domain, typically the half-line or a cone. The classical works of
Iglehart
[
12
]
and Bolthausen
[
2
]
provide the
functional central limit theorem for random walks conditioned to stay positive.
If
(S_{n})
is a centred walk with finite variance
\sigma^{2}
, then under the
conditioning
\{S_{1}>0,\dots,S_{n}>0\}
one has

\bigl(S_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}\,\big|\,S_{k}>0,\ k\leq n\bigr)\Longrightarrow\sigma B^{\mathrm{me}}(t),

where
B^{\mathrm{me}}
denotes the Brownian meander.

The renewal-theoretic representation developed by
Bertoin and Doney
[
1
]
identifies the conditioned law in
terms of harmonic functions associated with the ascending ladder process.
Caravenna
[
4
]
obtained a precise local limit theorem,
establishing the uniform asymptotic behaviour of the conditional probabilities

\mathbb{P}(S_{n}=x\mid S_{k}>0,\ k\leq n),

for
x
of order
\sqrt{n}
.

In the context of integrated or iterated sums, the persistence exponents differ
substantially from those of classical random walks. Dembo, Ding and
Gao
[
5
]
proved that if
A_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{k}
, then

\mathbb{P}(A_{k}>0\ \text{for all}\ k\leq n)\asymp n^{-1/4},

exhibiting the effect of strong correlations. Precise exit-time asymptotics for
integrated walks were obtained by Denisov and Wachtel
[
7
]
,
who also developed, in
[
8
]
, a general theory of random
walks in cones based on the existence of suitable harmonic functions. In
particular, the survival probability satisfies

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{\mathcal{C}}>n)\approx n^{-\alpha},

where the exponent
\alpha
depends on geometric characteristics of the cone.

Ordered random walks, studied by Eichelsbacher and
König
[
10
]
and by Denisov and
Wachtel
[
6
]
, provide a higher-dimensional analogue, in
which the Vandermonde determinant appears naturally as the harmonic function
governing the conditioning.

More recently, Grama, Lauvergnat and Le Page
[
11
]

developed a general framework for Markov walks conditioned to remain positive
under a spectral gap assumption. Their approach yields the asymptotics of the
exit probability

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\sim\frac{2V(x,y)}{\sqrt{2\pi n}\,\sigma},

and the convergence of the conditioned law of
(y+S_{n})/(\sigma\sqrt{n})
to the
Rayleigh distribution. The analysis relies essentially on constructing a
non-negative
Q^{+}
-harmonic function
V
, whose domain of positivity determines
the admissible initial conditions.

1.1
Main Contributions

Assume that on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
we are given a
Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with values in a measurable state space
\mathbb{X}
.
Let
f
be a real function on
\mathbb{X}
and set

S_{0}=0,\qquad S_{n}=f(X_{1})+\cdots+f(X_{n}),\quad n\geq 1.

(1)

For a starting point
y>0
, denote by

\tau_{y}:=\inf\{k\geq 1:\ y+S_{k}\leq 0\}

(2)

the first moment at which the Markov walk becomes non-positive.
Our purpose is to determine the asymptotic behavior of the survival probability

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)
and the conditional distribution of the rescaled walk
S_{n}

given the event
\{\tau_{y}>n\}
.

The classical i.i.d. case on
\mathbb{R}
, going back to
[
12
]

[
2
]

[
4
]

[
4
]
, is by now well understood when the
increments lie in the domain of attraction of a stable law. In recent years
considerable progress has been made in higher dimensions and for various dependent
structures. However, for Markov chains the situation is much less complete.
The Gaussian (finite-variance) case under a spectral-gap assumption was established
by
[
11
]
, where precise asymptotics and
Brownian-meander limits were obtained. To the best of our knowledge, no analogue of
these results has been available in the heavy-tailed,
\alpha
-stable regime.

In the present work we fill this gap. We assume that the centered martingale
approximation of the Markov walk belongs to the domain of attraction of a strictly

\alpha
-stable law with
\alpha\in(1,2)
, and we investigate the associated
conditioned process. Under a spectral gap for the transition operator on an
appropriate Banach space, together with a perturbation framework for Fourier
operators, we establish sharp asymptotics for the exit probability

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)
and identify the limiting law of the conditioned walk.
Our approach extends the operator-theoretic method developed in the Gaussian case
to the full stable setting.

To present the main results, let
L(\cdot)
denote the slowly varying function
appearing in the stable normalization
n^{1/\alpha}L(n)
, and let

\rho\in(0,1)
be the positivity parameter of the associated
\alpha
-stable Lévy
process. We show that there exists a strictly positive harmonic function

V_{\alpha}(x,y)
on
\mathbb{X}\times(0,\infty)
such that for every

x\in\mathbb{X}
and
y>0
,

n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\longrightarrow V_{\alpha}(x,y),

(3)

and that the limiting function satisfies the harmonic relation

V_{\alpha}(x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\,V_{\alpha}(X_{1},y+S_{1});\ \tau_{y}>1\,\right].

(4)

Moreover, we determine its asymptotic growth as
y\to\infty
,

V_{\alpha}(x,y)\sim h(x)\,y^{\alpha(1-\rho)},

(5)

for a positive function
h
. Our second main theorem identifies the conditioned
limit: conditionally on
\tau_{y}>n
, the rescaled walk converges in distribution to
the time-
1

\alpha
-stable meander. This provides, for the first time, the
appearance of stable meanders for Markov additive processes under a spectral-gap
assumption, extending the Brownian-meander result of the Gaussian case.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the Markov walk

S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k})

(6)

and the exit time

\tau_{y}=\inf\{k\geq 1:y+S_{k}\leq 0\}

(7)

The transition operators needed later are defined, and the hypotheses on the Banach space, the spectral gap, the perturbed operators, and the integrability conditions are stated. These assumptions form the analytic framework used throughout the paper.

Section 3 collects the technical bounds. Using the Poisson equation, the decomposition

S_{n}=M_{n}+r(X_{0})-r(X_{n})

(8)

is obtained, where
(M_{n})
is a martingale. Uniform estimates for the remainder and for the martingale increments are proved. These estimates allow the replacement of the walk by its martingale approximation in the subsequent arguments.

Section 4 develops the approximation to a strictly
\alpha
-stable Lévy process. A functional limit theorem is obtained for the martingale part, and a coupling gives

|S_{n}-Z_{\alpha}(n)|=o(n^{1/\alpha})\quad\text{a.s.}

(9)

The comparison between the exit event
\{\tau_{y}>n\}
and its martingale analogue is derived. The boundary perturbation coming from the remainder does not modify the tail index, so the survival probability inherits the exponent
1-\rho
, where
\rho=P(Z_{\alpha}(1)>0)
.

Section 5 contains the main asymptotic theorems. It is shown that

n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,P_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\to V_{\alpha}(x,y),

(10)

where
V_{\alpha}
is a positive
Q^{+}
-harmonic function, and its growth as
y\to\infty
is identified. The conditioned limit theorem is then proved: under the event
\{\tau_{y}>n\}
,

\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}

converges in distribution to the
\alpha
-stable meander. These results extend the Gaussian case to the stable setting under the spectral gap assumptions of Section 2.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce the Markov walk, the exit time
\tau_{y}
, and the analytic framework used throughout the paper. We recall the assumptions on the Banach space, the spectral gap, and the perturbed operators, and we formulate the conditions under which the subsequent results are established.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a Markov chain on a measurable space
(\mathbb{X},\mathscr{X})
with transition probability
P(x,dy)
.
Let
f:\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{R}
be measurable and define the Markov random walk by
S_{0}=0
and

S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k}).

For
y\in\mathbb{R}
, define the exit time

\tau_{y}=\inf\{k\geq 1:y+S_{k}\leq 0\}.

(11)

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space of functions on
\mathbb{X}
containing the constant function
e
.
Define the transition operator

Pg(x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(X_{1})],

and the restricted operator on the domain of positivity

Q_{+}\varphi(x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\varphi(X_{1},y+S_{1});\ \tau_{y}>1].

(12)

Assumption 1

(Banach space)
.

1.

The unit function
e
belongs to
\mathscr{B}
.

2.

For any
x\in\mathbb{X}
, the Dirac measure
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x}
belongs to
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
.

3.

The Banach space
\mathscr{B}
is included in
L^{1}(\mathbf{P}(x,\cdot))
for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
.

4.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that, for any
g\in\mathscr{B}
and any
t
with
|t|\leq\varepsilon_{0},
the function
\mathrm{e}^{itf}g
is in
\mathscr{B}
.

Assumption 2

(Spectral gap)
.

1.

The map
g\mapsto\mathbf{P}g
is a bounded operator on
\mathscr{B}
.

2.

There exist constants
C_{Q}>0
and
\kappa\in(0,1)
such that

\mathbf{P}=\Pi+Q,

where
\Pi
is a one-dimensional projector and
Q
is an operator on
\mathscr{B}
satisfying

\Pi Q=Q\Pi=0,

and for any
n\geq 1
,

\|Q^{n}\|_{\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{B}}\leq C_{Q}\,\kappa^{n}.

Assumption 3

(Perturbed transition operator)
.

1.

For any
|t|\leq\varepsilon_{0},
the map
g\mapsto\mathbf{P}_{t}g
is a bounded operator on
\mathscr{B}
.

2.

There exists
C_{\mathbf{P}}>0
such that, for any
n\geq 1
and any
|t|\leq\varepsilon_{0},

\|\mathbf{P}_{t}^{n}\|_{\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{B}}\leq C_{\mathbf{P}}.

Assumption 4

(Local integrability)
.

The Banach space
\mathscr{B}
contains non-negative functions
N,N_{1},N_{2},\ldots
such that:

1.

There exist
\alpha>2
and
\gamma>0
such that, for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
,

\max\left\{|f(x)|^{1+\gamma},\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{x}\|_{\mathscr{B}^{\prime}},\mathbb{E}_{x}^{1/\alpha}\!\left(N(X_{n})^{\alpha}\right)\right\}\leq c\,(1+N(x)),

(13)

and

N(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{N(x)>l\}}\leq N_{l}(x)\qquad\text{for all }l\geq 1.

(14)

2.

There exists
c>0
such that for all
l\geq 1
,

\|N_{l}\|_{\mathscr{B}}\leq c.

3.

There exist
\beta>0
and
c>0
such that, for all
l\geq 1
,

|\boldsymbol{\nu}(N_{l})|\leq\frac{c}{l^{1+\beta}}

(15)

Assumption 5

(Stable Domain of Attraction)
.

We assume the centered martingale approximation of
S_{n}
belongs to the domain of attraction of an
\alpha
-stable law with
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Specifically, there exists a slowly varying function
L(n)
such that:

\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}(1)

(16)

where
\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}
is a strictly
\alpha
-stable Lévy process. We define the positivity parameter
\rho
as:

\rho=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}(1)>0\right),\quad\rho\in(0,1).

(17)

3
Technical Foundations

This section collects several estimates required later. We use the Poisson equation to obtain the martingale decomposition of the walk and derive bounds on the remainder and on the martingale increments. These properties will be used in the approximation and exit-time analysis of the following sections.

Lemma 3.1

(Control of the Remainder)
.

Under Assumptions 2.2-2.5, the remainder function
r(x)=P\Theta(x)
and the solution
\Theta(x)
of the Poisson equation satisfy, for any
x\in\mathbb{X}
,

|r(x)|+|\Theta(x)|\leq c(1+N(x)).

(18)

Furthermore, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\frac{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|r(X_{k})|}{n^{1/\alpha}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}_{x}}0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(19)

Remark
.

This is Lemma 4.1 of
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Martingale Difference Moments)
.

Let
\xi_{k}=\Theta(X_{k})-P\Theta(X_{k-1})
be the martingale increments. Then

\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}]=0,

(20)

and for some
p<\alpha
,

\sup_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\!\left(|\xi_{k}|^{p}\right)<\infty.

(21)

Remark
.

This is the property of Gordin decomposition, c.f. lemma 4.2 of
[
11
]
.

4
Approximation and Fluctuation Theory

We approximate the Markov walk by its associated martingale and study the fluctuation properties of this martingale. Using the strong approximation and comparison arguments, we relate the exit event for the Markov walk to the corresponding event for the martingale and for the limiting stable process. These results form the basis for the asymptotic analysis of the survival probability.

Proposition 4.1

(Stable Strong Approximation)
.

Assume Assumption 2.6. There exists a probability space supporting the Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and a strictly
\alpha
-stable Lévy process
Z_{\alpha}(t)
such that

|S_{n}-Z_{\alpha}(n)|=o\!\left(n^{1/\alpha}\right)\quad\text{a.s.}

(22)

Moreover, for every measurable set
A\subset D[0,\infty)
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\left(\frac{S_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\right)_{t\geq 0}\in A\right)=\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{\alpha}\in A\right).

(23)

Proof.

By Gordin’s martingale decomposition, there exists a function
\Theta
solving the Poisson equation such that

S_{n}=M_{n}+r(X_{0})-r(X_{n}),

(24)

where the martingale increments are

\xi_{k}=\Theta(X_{k})-P\Theta(X_{k-1}).

(25)

Under Assumption 2.6, the normalized sums of the martingale satisfy

\frac{M_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\xrightarrow{d}Z_{\alpha}(1),

(26)

where
Z_{\alpha}
is a strictly
\alpha
-stable Lévy process. Classical functional limit theorems extend this convergence to the Skorokhod space
D[0,\infty)
, giving

\left(\frac{M_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\right)_{t\geq 0}\xrightarrow{d}(Z_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}.

(27)

Lemma 3.1 ensures that the remainder term satisfies

\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\frac{|r(X_{k})|}{n^{1/\alpha}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0,

(28)

so that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left|\frac{S_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}-M_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\right|\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{in probability}.

(29)

The convergence of the partial-sum process for
S_{n}
follows from Slutsky’s theorem.

For the strong approximation, classical strong embedding results for stable laws construct a coupling for which

|M_{n}-Z_{\alpha}(n)|=o\!\left(n^{1/\alpha}\right)\quad\text{a.s.}

(30)

Since

|S_{n}-M_{n}|=|r(X_{0})-r(X_{n})|,

(31)

and the function
r
has at most polynomial growth controlled by Assumption 2.5, the remainder term also satisfies

|S_{n}-M_{n}|=o\!\left(n^{1/\alpha}\right)\quad\text{a.s.}

(32)

Combining these bounds gives

|S_{n}-Z_{\alpha}(n)|\leq|S_{n}-M_{n}|+|M_{n}-Z_{\alpha}(n)|=o\!\left(n^{1/\alpha}\right)\quad\text{a.s.}

(33)

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Comparison of Exit Times)
.

Let

\tau_{y}=\inf\{k\geq 1:y+S_{k}\leq 0\}.

(34)

Then, for large
n
,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(y+M_{k})>0\right)+O(n^{-1}\text{ error terms}),

(35)

and the perturbation term
r(X_{k})
acts only as a boundary shift. It preserves the stable tail index
\rho=\mathbb{P}(Z_{\alpha}(1)>0)
and modifies only the multiplicative constant
V_{\alpha}(x,y)
.

Proof.

Using the martingale decomposition,

y+S_{k}=y+M_{k}+\Delta_{k},

(36)

where
\Delta_{k}=r(X_{0})-r(X_{k})
. From Lemma 3.1,

|r(X_{k})|\leq c(1+N(X_{k})),

(37)

and therefore

|\Delta_{k}|\leq 2K_{n},\qquad K_{n}=\max_{0\leq j\leq n}|r(X_{j})|.

(38)

Hence the exit-time event satisfies the inclusions

\left\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(y+M_{k})>2K_{n}\right\}\subseteq\{\tau_{y}>n\}\subseteq\left\{\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(y+M_{k})>-2K_{n}\right\}.

(39)

Under Assumption 2.5, the growth of
K_{n}
is negligible compared with
n^{1/\alpha}
; more precisely,

\frac{K_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}0.

(40)

Thus the perturbation shifts the boundary only by a small deterministic band. The difference between the two bounding events is controlled by

\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(y+M_{k})\in[-2K_{n},2K_{n}]\right),

(41)

which is a small-strip probability for the minimum of a stable-domain martingale. Standard fluctuation theory implies that this strip probability is of smaller order than the survival probability, giving an
O(n^{-1})
contribution.

Therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(y+M_{k})>0\right)+O(n^{-1}\text{ error terms}).

(42)

Since the martingale part
M_{k}
determines the stable positivity index
\rho
, the perturbation
r(X_{k})
merely shifts the initial level and modifies the constant
V_{\alpha}(x,y)
without affecting the power-law exponent. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Stable Exit Tail)
.

Let
\tau^{\mathrm{stable}}=\inf\{t>0:\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}(t)\leq 0\}
. Then

\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\mathrm{stable}}>t)=C_{\alpha,\rho}\,t^{-(1-\rho)},

(43)

where
\rho=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{Z}_{\alpha}(1)>0)
.

Remark
.

This is a classical result in the theory of Lévy processes.

5
Main Asymptotic Theorems

Combining the approximation results obtained in Section 4 with the fluctuation estimates of the stable process, we establish the asymptotic behaviour of
P_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)
and prove the existence of a strictly positive
Q^{+}
-harmonic function. We then identify the limiting conditional distribution of the rescaled walk.

Theorem 5.1

(Convergence to the Harmonic Function)
.

For every
x\in\mathbb{X}
and every
y>0
, the limit

V_{\alpha}(x,y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)

(44)

exists and is strictly positive. Moreover, for all
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
y>0
,

V_{\alpha}(x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[V_{\alpha}(X_{1},y+S_{1})\,;\,\tau_{y}>1\right],

(45)

so that the process

\left(V_{\alpha}(X_{n},y+S_{n})\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{y}>n\}}\right)_{n\geq 0}

(46)

is a martingale. Finally, as
y\to\infty
,

V_{\alpha}(x,y)\sim h(x)\,y^{\alpha(1-\rho)},

(47)

for some strictly positive function
h:\mathbb{X}\to(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

The Markov random walk is defined by

S_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(X_{k}),

(48)

and the exit time is

\tau_{y}=\inf\{k\geq 1:y+S_{k}\leq 0\}.

(49)

Lemma 4.2 gives the approximation

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)=\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\min_{1\leq k\leq n}(y+M_{k})>0\right)+O(n^{-1}),

(50)

where
(M_{n})
is the martingale obtained from the Gordin decomposition and the term
r(X_{k})
acts only as a boundary perturbation. Proposition 4.1 provides the stable strong approximation

|S_{n}-Z_{\alpha}(n)|=o\!\left(n^{1/\alpha}\right)\quad\text{a.s.},

(51)

where
(Z_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a strictly
\alpha
-stable Lévy process with positivity parameter

\rho=\mathbb{P}(Z_{\alpha}(1)>0).

(52)

Proposition 4.3 gives the survival asymptotic

\mathbb{P}(\tau^{stable}>t)=C_{\alpha,\rho}\,t^{-(1-\rho)}.

(53)

Since Lemma 4.2 shows that the perturbation does not change the tail index, it follows that

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\sim C(x,y)\,n^{-(1-\rho)}L(n)^{-1}.

(54)

Multiplying by
n^{1-\rho}L(n)
yields the existence of

V_{\alpha}(x,y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n),

(55)

and this limit is strictly positive, because the stable survival probability is positive for each finite time.

To show harmonicity, use the Markov property:

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{y}>1\}}\,\mathbb{P}_{X_{1}}\!\left(\tau_{y+S_{1}}>n-1\right)\right].

(56)

Multiplying both sides by
n^{1-\rho}L(n)
gives

n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{y}>1\}}\,\frac{n^{1-\rho}L(n)}{(n-1)^{1-\rho}L(n-1)}\,(n-1)^{1-\rho}L(n-1)\,\mathbb{P}_{X_{1}}(\tau_{y+S_{1}}>n-1)\right].

(57)

The slowly varying nature of
L
implies

\frac{n^{1-\rho}L(n)}{(n-1)^{1-\rho}L(n-1)}\longrightarrow 1,

(58)

and by the definition already established,

(n-1)^{1-\rho}L(n-1)\,\mathbb{P}_{X_{1}}(\tau_{y+S_{1}}>n-1)\longrightarrow V_{\alpha}(X_{1},y+S_{1}).

(59)

Dominated convergence is justified by the spectral gap assumptions, yielding

V_{\alpha}(x,y)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[V_{\alpha}(X_{1},y+S_{1})\,;\,\tau_{y}>1\right],

(60)

so
V_{\alpha}
is
Q_{+}
-harmonic and

V_{\alpha}(X_{n},y+S_{n})\,\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{y}>n\}}

(61)

is a martingale.

For the asymptotic growth as
y\to\infty
, the strong approximation shows that the event
\{\tau_{y}>n\}
is controlled by the event

\min_{k\leq n}M_{k}>-y.

(62)

The stable scaling property implies that the probability of remaining above a small negative barrier
-\varepsilon
behaves as
\varepsilon^{\alpha(1-\rho)}
. Setting
\varepsilon=y\,n^{-1/\alpha}
gives

\mathbb{P}(\tau_{y}>n)\approx y^{\alpha(1-\rho)}n^{-(1-\rho)}L(n)^{-1}.

(63)

Since

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\sim V_{\alpha}(x,y)\,n^{-(1-\rho)}L(n)^{-1},

(64)

comparison yields

V_{\alpha}(x,y)\sim h(x)\,y^{\alpha(1-\rho)},

(65)

for some strictly positive function
h(x)
determined by the spectral gap and the remainder structure. This establishes the claimed asymptotic growth.
∎

Theorem 5.2

(Global Limit Theorem)
.

Assume Assumptions 2.2–2.6. Then for every fixed
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
y>0
:

1. The survival probability satisfies

\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)=V_{\alpha}(x,y).

(66)

2. Conditioned on survival, the rescaled Markov walk converges in distribution to the
\alpha
-stable meander:

\mathcal{L}\!\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\;\Big|\;\tau_{y}>n\right)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}.

(67)

Proof.

By Theorem 5.1, the limit

V_{\alpha}(x,y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{1-\rho}L(n)\,\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)

(68)

exists and is strictly positive for every
x\in\mathbb{X}
and
y>0
. Rearranging yields the asymptotic formula

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\sim\frac{V_{\alpha}(x,y)}{n^{1-\rho}L(n)},

(69)

which proves the first part of the theorem.

To obtain the conditional limit, consider a Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
which is a continuity set for the stable meander distribution. The conditional probability is

\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\in A\;\Big|\;\tau_{y}>n\right)=\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\in A,\,\tau_{y}>n\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)}.

(70)

Define the rescaled path

w_{n}(t)=\frac{S_{\lfloor nt\rfloor}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)},\qquad t\in[0,1],

(71)

and the event

E_{n}(A)=\{w_{n}(1)\in A,\;\min_{0\leq t\leq 1}w_{n}(t)>0\}.

(72)

Proposition 4.1 gives the functional convergence

(w_{n}(t))_{t\geq 0}\xrightarrow{d}(Z_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0},

(73)

where
(Z_{\alpha}(t))
is a strictly
\alpha
-stable Lévy process. Lemma 4.2 shows that the boundary perturbation coming from the remainder term is negligible in the scaling limit. Therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\in A,\,\tau_{y}>n\right)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{\alpha}(1)\in A,\;\tau^{stable}>1\right),

(74)

where
\tau^{stable}=\inf\{t>0:Z_{\alpha}(t)\leq 0\}
.

The stable meander distribution is defined by

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(A)=\frac{\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{\alpha}(1)\in A,\;\tau^{stable}>1\right)}{\mathbb{P}(\tau^{stable}>1)}.

(75)

By the first part of the theorem,

\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\sim\frac{V_{\alpha}(x,y)}{n^{1-\rho}L(n)}.

(76)

The numerator has the same asymptotic prefactor
V_{\alpha}(x,y)\,n^{-(1-\rho)}L(n)^{-1}
, because the Markov property at time
1
and the strong approximation decouple the initial state from the long-time stable behavior. Hence the ratio of numerator to denominator satisfies

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x}\!\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\in A,\,\tau_{y}>n\right)}{\mathbb{P}_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{P}\!\left(Z_{\alpha}(1)\in A,\;\tau^{stable}>1\right)}{\mathbb{P}(\tau^{stable}>1)}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(A).

(77)

Therefore,

\mathcal{L}\!\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n^{1/\alpha}L(n)}\;\Big|\;\tau_{y}>n\right)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha},

(78)

which proves the second part and completes the proof.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this work we investigated the asymptotic behaviour of Markov additive processes
in the strictly
\alpha
-stable domain and established the corresponding conditioned
limit theorems. Relying on the spectral gap property of the underlying transition
operator and on a refined martingale approximation, we constructed a non-negative

Q^{+}
-harmonic function
V_{\alpha}
which governs the survival probabilities of
the walk. We proved that for every initial state
(x,y)
in the domain of positivity,

P_{x}(\tau_{y}>n)\sim\frac{V_{\alpha}(x,y)}{n^{\,1-\rho}L(n)},

(79)

and we identified the precise growth of
V_{\alpha}
as
y\to\infty
. Furthermore,
we showed that, conditionally on
\{\tau_{y}>n\}
, the rescaled walk converges to the

\alpha
-stable meander, thereby extending the classical Gaussian results to the full
heavy-tailed setting.

The approach developed here parallels the strategy used in the finite-variance case of
[
11
]
,
while incorporating the fluctuation theory of
\alpha
-stable processes and the
operator-theoretic framework needed for Markov dependence. The results obtained
provide a unified description of exit-time asymptotics and conditioned limits for a
broad class of Markov walks, and they illustrate the robustness of harmonic-function
methods in settings where long-range jumps and heavy tails play a central role.

Left atrial-ventricular coupling index associated with new-onset atrial fibrillation in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction patients
Authors: Jiahua Liu, Xinjia Du, Jingfang Zhou, Yixuan Wu, Yanfei Ren, Lei Chen, Yuan Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe management of new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) in ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) patients remains a challenge in clinical. Left atrial-ventricular coupling index (LACI) is a novel cardiovascular risk marker. Currently, the relationship between LACI and NOAF in STEMI patients is unclear. This study aims to investigate the association between LACI measured by cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) and NOAF after PCI in STEMI patients.MethodsThis is a single-center, retrospective study. Patients who were admitted for STEMI from September 2018 and December 2024 were consecutively enrolled. All patients completed CMR during hospitalization. LACI was calculated as the ratio of the left atrial end-diastolic volume index to the left ventricular end-diastolic volume index.ResultsA total of 553 patients were included, of whom 46 developed NOAF. Multivariable logistic regression analysis indicated that LACI (OR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.07–1.14, p < 0.001), microvascular obstruction (OR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.07–1.26, p < 0.001), and heart rate (OR = 1.03, 95% CI: 1.01–1.06, p = 0.024) were independent risk factors for NOAF. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis showed that the AUC for LACI was 0.733 (95% CI: 0.636–0.830, p < 0.001). Integrating LACI significantly improved the NOAF risk model (NRI = 0.803, 95% CI: 0.5149–1.0917, P < 0.001; IDI = 0.127, 95% CI: 0.0606–0.1930, P < 0.001).ConclusionLACI is independently associated with the occurrence of NOAF after PCI in patients with STEMI. Introduction

ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) represents a life-threatening form of acute coronary syndrome. Despite remarkable progress in percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) over the past decades, patients continue to experience frequent complications [1]. Among these, new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) is particularly common, with reported incidence ranging between 6% and 21%2. The development of NOAF not only impairs patients’ quality of life but also substantially elevates the risks of mortality, recurrent hospitalization, stroke, and other adverse cardiovascular outcomes [3–5]. Managing STEMI complicated by NOAF therefore remains a considerable clinical challenge [6]. In addition, as these patients often require both anticoagulant and antiplatelet agents. Balancing thromboembolic protection against the heightened risk of bleeding remains clinically complex, and recent evidence highlights the need for careful optimization of antithrombotic strategies in this setting [7]. Early identification of patients at risk for NOAF is therefore essential for guiding monitoring strategies and improving clinical outcomes.

Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) has emerged as a non-invasive, high-resolution imaging modality with established value in the risk stratification of cardiovascular diseases [8]. In this context, the left atrial–ventricular coupling index (LACI) has recently been proposed as a novel marker reflecting the mechanical interaction between the left atrium (LA) and left ventricle (LV). Recently, the left atrial–ventricular coupling index (LACI), which reflects the interaction between atrial and ventricular function, has been introduced as a novel biomarker. Physiologically, atrioventricular coupling is influenced by LV diastolic stiffness, LA compliance, filling pressures, and structural remodeling—pathways that are commonly altered in the setting of STEMI. As such, LACI offers a more integrated assessment of cardiac chamber interaction than isolated LA or LV indices [9–11]. Prior investigations have revealed that LACI predicts 1-year MACE in patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI), especially in high-risk groups with reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), where it provides enhanced prognostic information [12, 13]. Additionally, LACI has been associated with atrial fibrillation recurrence following radiofrequency ablation in patients with paroxysmal AF14, and has demonstrated superior predictive ability for NOAF compared with conventional left atrial indices in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) [15]. These findings suggest that LACI may serve as a more sensitive marker of atrial–ventricular interaction, particularly in conditions characterized by acute pressure and volume changes such as STEMI.

However, whether LACI is associated with NOAF in STEMI patients remains unclear. Given its physiological relevance and predictive value in other cardiovascular settings, investigating the role of LACI in STEMI may help identify vulnerable patients who would benefit from intensified rhythm surveillance and early preventive strategies. Therefore, this study aimed to assess the association between CMR-derived LACI and the risk of NOAF after PCI in STEMI patients.

Methods

This study is a single-center, retrospective analysis. Patients who were admitted for ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) between September 2018 and December 2024 were consecutively enrolled. All patients completed CMR during hospitalization. Inclusion criteria: (1) Patients who successfully underwent PCI during hospitalization (TIMI ≥ 2) after the onset of STEMI and PCI was performed within 12 h of symptom onset. STEMI was diagnosed based on European Society of Cardiology guidelines, characterized by ST-segment elevation (≥ 1 mm in at least two contiguous leads on the electrocardiogram) and elevated serum myocardial injury biomarkers (e.g., high-sensitivity troponin), confirmed by coronary angiography showing artery occlusion [16]. (2) Continuous electrocardiogram monitoring during hospitalization. Exclusion criteria: Patients with age under 18 years, a history of atrial fibrillation (AF), previous moderate or severe valvular heart disease, thyroid dysfunction, missing data and poor-quality CMR images. New-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) was defined as the first occurrence of AF, with a duration of ≥ 30 s, recorded during continuous telemetry monitoring throughout the hospitalization period, in patients with no prior history of AF. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University (XYFY2024-KL513-01). All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. As this was a retrospective study involving no risk to patients, the requirement for written informed consent was waived was waived by Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University guidelines. Patients and the public were not be involved in this study. The inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in Fig. 1.

Basic information and laboratory examination indicators

Basic patient information was collected, including height, weight, age, smoking history, hypertension history, diabetes history, and medication use. At the time of admission, the patients’ systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and heart rate were also recorded. Routine hematological tests were performed during hospitalization, including measurements of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), cardiac troponin, N-terminal pro B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), lipid profile, glycated hemoglobin, and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR). Among these, the peak values of hs-CRP, cardiac troponin, and NT-proBNP during hospitalization were used for analysis.

CMR image analysis

CMR imaging was performed using a 3.0 T MRI scanner (Ingenia 3.0 T, Philips, Netherlands) equipped in the radiology department. Each patient underwent CMR at a median of 5 (4, 6) days post-PCI. The CMR images were analyzed using post-processing software Cvi42 (v5.13.5, Circle V Vascular Imaging, Canada) to quantify cardiac parameters. At both end-diastole and end-systole, the endocardial and epicardial borders of the left ventricle (LV) and left atrium (LA) were automatically delineated and manually adjusted (Fig. 2). Key parameters, including LVEF, left atrial ejection fraction (LAEF), end-diastolic volume (EDV), and end-systolic volume (ESV), were derived and normalized using body surface area (BSA). LACI was calculated as the ratio of the left atrial end-diastolic volume index (LA-EDVi) to the left ventricular end-diastolic volume index (LV-EDVi), both measured at the same end-diastolic phase [17]. In the late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) images, regions with a signal intensity greater than 5 standard deviations above the normal myocardium in the short-axis view were defined as LGE areas. Infarct size was represented as the percentage of LGE relative to left ventricular mass (LV-MASS). Microvascular obstruction (MVO) was defined as a low-signal area within the infarcted myocardium and was quantified as the percentage of MVO relative to LV-MASS [18–20]. All magnetic resonance measurements were performed by experienced cardiovascular specialists, and the clinical information was kept blinded during the diagnostic process to minimize bias.

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was used to assess the normality of data distribution. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation and were compared using an independent sample t-test. Non-normally distributed continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR), and differences were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. Univariate logistic regression analysis was conducted, and variables with p < 0.1 were included in a multivariate logistic stepwise forward regression model to identify independent risk factors for NOAF after PCI in STEMI patients. The diagnostic value of LACI for predicting NOAF after PCI was determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) curves were used to explore the nonlinear relationship between LACI and NOAF. Net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were computed to evaluate the incremental predictive value of adding LACI to the baseline model. The NRI quantifies the extent to which the new model correctly reclassifies individuals into higher or lower predicted risk categories, whereas the IDI reflects the improvement in average sensitivity without sacrificing specificity. Both indices were calculated using logistic regression–based risk estimates, and 95% confidence intervals were obtained through bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0, R version 4.3.1, and GraphPad Prism 9 for graphical representation. A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Logistic regression analysis and correlation between NOAF and LACI

To avoid multicollinearity, two variables involved in the calculation of LACI were excluded, and the remaining variables were included in the univariate logistic regression analysis. The results indicated that LACI, late gadolinium enhancement percentage (LGE%), MVO%, LAEF, LA-ESVi, LV-ESVi, and NT-proBNP were associated with NOAF (Supplementary Table 1). Variables with a p-value < 0.1 from the univariate logistic regression analysis were entered into a multivariate logistic regression model using stepwise forward selection. The results revealed that LACI (OR = 1.10, 95% CI: 1.07–1.14, p < 0.001), MVO% (OR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.07–1.26, p < 0.001), and heart rate (OR = 1.03, 95% CI: 1.01–1.06, p = 0.024) were independent predictors of NOAF (Table 2). Additionally, RCS analysis revealed a linear relationship between LACI and the occurrence of NOAF (p for nonlinear = 0.582, p for overall < 0.001). As shown in Fig. 3, the incidence of NOAF increased progressively with higher LACI values.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves

ROC analysis was used to assess the diagnostic performance of LACI, MVO, and heart rate (HR) for NOAF. The results showed that the AUC of MVO was 0.643 (95% CI: 0.547–0.729, p = 0.001). The AUC of LACI was 0.733 (95% CI: 0.636–0.830, p < 0.001), and this odds ratio corresponds to a 1% increase in LACI. The ROC analysis for heart rate showed an AUC of 0.564 (95% CI: 0.470–0.659), indicating limited discriminatory ability, and it did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.149). Compared with HR, both LACI and MVO demonstrated superior predictive performance, highlighting the incremental value of LACI when added to the baseline model. (Fig. 4, Supplementary Table 2).

Incremental value of LACI to NOAF

Based on the results of multivariate logistic regression, a new model incorporating LACI was developed. ROC analysis showed that the new model had an AUC of 0.791 (95% CI: 0.719–0.862, p < 0.001), which was significantly better than the baseline model (Delong test: z = 3.55, 95% CI: 0.070–0.243, p = 0.00) (Fig. 5, Supplementary Table 3).

Subsequently, the net reclassification index (NRI) and integrated discrimination index (IDI) were calculated. The results showed that NRI = 0.803 (95% CI: 0.5149–1.0917), P < 0.001, and IDI = 0.127 (95% CI: 0.0606–0.1930), P < 0.001. An NRI > 0 indicates positive improvement, suggesting that the new model significantly improved the predictive ability over the baseline model, with statistical significance. The IDI represents the average difference in predicted risk between those who did and did not experience the outcome event. The new model improved prediction by 12.7%, and this difference was statistically significant (p < 0.05), indicating that the new model outperformed the baseline model in predicting the recurrence of atrial fibrillation (Table 3).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first investigation exploring the association between the left atrial–ventricular coupling index (LACI) and new-onset atrial fibrillation (NOAF) in patients with STEMI. The major findings are as follows: (1) after adjusting for relevant confounding factors, including left atrial ejection fraction (LAEF), LACI remained independently associated with the occurrence of NOAF after PCI; (2) there was a clear linear relationship between increasing LACI values and the incidence of NOAF; and (3) incorporating LACI into a reference model enhanced its ability to distinguish patients with higher versus lower likelihood of developing NOAF.

The interplay between myocardial infarction (MI) and atrial fibrillation (AF) is multifaceted [6]. STEMI can trigger AF through inflammatory activation and atrial volume overload, while AF in turn worsens ischemia by increasing myocardial oxygen consumption [21, 22]. Although the precise mechanisms remain uncertain, accumulating evidence suggests that electrical remodeling of atrial myocytes and structural changes such as fibrosis form the substrate for AF23. Furthermore, left ventricular (LV) diastolic dysfunction, which often develops earlier than systolic dysfunction after AMI, also contributes to AF onset and progression [23]. Left atrial (LA) function has therefore been widely employed as a surrogate marker of LV diastolic dysfunction [10]and an early indicator of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) [24, 25].

In this context, LACI has recently been proposed as a novel index reflecting the atrioventricular mechanical interaction [15]. Compared with traditional single-chamber parameters, LACI provides a more integrated assessment of left-heart structure and function [26]. Prior studies have demonstrated its superior prognostic value for cardiovascular events, showing greater specificity than isolated LA or LV measures [17]. Furthermore, the calculation of LACI may provide clinicians with supplementary prognostic information that could help identify patients at higher risk of major adverse cardiovascular events [12]. In addition, recent evidence has demonstrated the predictive value of LA and RA strain for detecting device-detected AF in patients with cryptogenic stroke [27]. In our analysis, CMR-derived atrial and ventricular strain measures were also examined but did not remain significant in the multivariable model and were therefore not included in the final results. This may reflect the limited sample size and low event rate. Larger studies are needed to determine whether strain-based markers offer incremental value beyond LACI. Another important consideration is the timing of CMR. Although guidelines generally recommend early CMR after AMI, the optimal window for specific parameters remains debated. Given that edema, microvascular obstruction, and infarct characteristics evolve rapidly in the acute phase, early imaging is preferred to capture these dynamic changes [28]. In our study, CMR was performed at a median of 5 days post-PCI. Therefore, we believe the CMR timing in our cohort provides a representative evaluation of early pathophysiological alterations. However, data on the predictive value of LACI for NOAF in STEMI patients have been lacking. Our results demonstrate that LACI is independently associated with NOAF occurrence after PCI and shows a significant linear correlation with AF risk.

The underlying mechanisms may involve ventricular remodeling and structural abnormalities such as dilation and wall motion impairment following STEMI [29]. These changes disrupt atrioventricular coupling, alter hemodynamic load transmission, and create an electrophysiological environment conducive to AF32. Even after reperfusion with PCI, residual myocardial injury and incomplete recovery of atrioventricular coordination may sustain a pro-arrhythmic state [6]. This discordant coupling likely promotes atrial electrophysiological remodeling, thereby predisposing patients to AF33,34. Furthermore, patients undergoing complex PCI often carry a higher ischemic burden and require tailored antithrombotic strategies to balance ischemic and bleeding risks, particularly in those who develop AF. Recent evidence highlights the need for individualized therapy in this setting [33, 34].

In line with previous reports, our study also identified microvascular obstruction (MVO) and heart rate (HR) as independent determinants of NOAF after PCI [35, 36]. Importantly, the newly constructed risk model that combined HR, MVO, and LACI exhibited superior predictive ability compared with the baseline model including HR and MVO alone. LA-ESVI demonstrated significance in univariable analysis but was not retained in the multivariable model, whereas LACI remained independently associated with NOAF. This indicates that LACI may capture more integrated atrioventricular interaction beyond atrial size alone. In this regard, our analysis indirectly compares LACI with LA-ESVI and supports the superior relevance of LACI in this clinical context. Moreover, the AUC difference was statistically significant based on the DeLong test, and the magnitude of improvement also suggests meaningful enhancement in discrimination, indicating that incorporating LACI may help identify patients at higher risk of NOAF with greater clinical precision. As CMR availability continues to expand, the integration of LACI into clinical workflows may be feasible. Regarding risk stratification, our study identified a cutoff value of approximately 25.7%, above which the risk of NOAF increased markedly. While this cutoff requires external validation, LACI may help guide closer rhythm monitoring in vulnerable patients. Taken together, our findings highlight the potential role of LACI as an imaging marker associated with NOAF risk in STEMI patients following PCI. Although our results do not establish causal or predictive relationships, the observed association indicates that elevated LACI may help identify individuals who warrant closer clinical attention. Future studies are needed to determine whether incorporating LACI into broader clinical assessment frameworks can further enhance risk characterization in this population.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective and single-center nature of this study is inherently associated with selection bias and limits the external validity of our findings. Although standardized inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied and multivariable adjustments were performed, the possibility of residual confounding cannot be fully eliminated. Furthermore, the relatively modest sample size—particularly the limited number of patients who developed NOAF—may reduce statistical power and increases the risk of overfitting, underscoring the need for validation in larger, multicenter cohorts. Second, NOAF ascertainment was limited to in-hospital monitoring, likely underestimating true AF burden and capturing mostly early ischemic/hemodynamic triggers. The lack of event-time data prevented time-to-event or competing-risk analysis. Third, because only patients with STEMI undergoing primary PCI were included, the applicability of our results to other clinical scenarios—such as NSTEMI, chronic coronary syndromes, or non-ischemic cardiomyopathies—remains uncertain. Moreover, residual confounding remains possible, including in-hospital medication changes, reperfusion quality, and the precise timing of NOAF onset, which were not fully captured in our dataset.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that CMR-derived LACI is independently associated with NOAF in patients with STEMI undergoing PCI. Incorporating LACI into the baseline clinical assessment improves its ability to characterize NOAF risk and may help refine risk stratification from a correlation perspective in clinical practice.

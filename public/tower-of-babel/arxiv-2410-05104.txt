Applications of the circle product with a right Com–module to the theory of commutative ring spectra
Nicholas J. Kuhn

Abstract
. If C ​ o ​ m Com is the reduced commutative operad, the category of C ​ o ​ m Com -algebras in spectra is the category of nounital commutative ring spectra. The theme of this survey is that many important constructions on C ​ o ​ m Com -algebras are given by taking the derived circle product with well chosen right C ​ o ​ m Com -modules. We show that examples of constructions arising this way include K ⊗ I K\otimes I , the tensor product of a based space K K with such an algebra I I , and T ​ Q ​ ( I ) TQ(I) , the Topological André-Quillen homology spectrum of I I . We then show how filtrations of right Com-modules can be used to filter such constructions. A natural decreasing filtration on right C ​ o ​ m Com -modules is described. When specialized to the C ​ o ​ m Com -bimodule C ​ o ​ m Com , this defines the augmentation ideal tower of I I , built out of the extended powers of T ​ Q ​ ( I ) TQ(I) . A less studied natural increasing filtration is described. When specialized to the right Com-module used to define T ​ Q ​ ( I ) TQ(I) , one gets a filtration on T ​ Q ​ ( I ) TQ(I) built out of I I and the spaces in the Lie cooperad. There are two versions of this in the literature, and our setting here makes it easy to prove that these agree. Much of this applies with C ​ o ​ m Com replaced by a more general reduced operad, and we make a few remarks about this.

2.
Background

2.1.
Operads, modules, and algebras

In this paper the category
Sp
will mean the category of symmetric spectra as defined in
[
9
]
.

With the smash product
\wedge
as tensor product and sphere spectrum
{\mathbb{S}}
as unit,
Sp
is a closed symmetric monoidal category. It is also tensored over the category
{\mathcal{T}}
of based simplicial sets (the ‘spaces’ of the introduction) with
K\otimes X=K\wedge X
, for
K\in{\mathcal{T}}
and
X\in Sp
. There is a notion of weak equivalence, and various model structures on
Sp
compatible with these, such that the resulting quotient category models the standard stable homotopy category. For our purposes we use the positive model structure used in
[
12
]
.

Let
\Sigma_{n}\text{-}Sp
denote the category of spectra equipped with an action of the
n
th symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
. We then let
\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
be the category of symmetric sequences in
Sp
: sequences

M=\{M(0),M(1),M(2),\dots\}
, with
M(n)\in\Sigma_{n}\text{-}Sp
for all
n
.

There are embeddings
i_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\text{-}Sp\rightarrow\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
given by letting
i_{n}(X)=(*,\dots,*,\overset{n}{X},*,\dots)
. We will sometimes identify
X\in Sp
with
i_{0}(X)=(X,*,*,\dots)\in\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
.

The category of symmetric sequences in
Sp
,
\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
, admits a symmetric monoidal product
\otimes
and a nonsymmetric monoidal ‘composition’ product
\circ
. We recall how this goes.

Given
M,N\in\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
,
M\otimes N
is defined by

(M\otimes N)(n)=\bigvee_{l+m=n}\Sigma_{n+}\wedge_{\Sigma_{l}\times\Sigma_{m}}(M(l)\wedge N(m)),

and then
M\circ N
is defined by

(M\circ N)(n)=\bigvee_{r=0}^{\infty}M(r)\wedge_{\Sigma_{r}}N^{\otimes r}(n).

For
n\geq 0
, let
{\bf n}=\{1,\dots,n\}
. The formula for
(M\circ N)(n)
rewrites as

(M\circ N)(n)=\bigvee_{r=0}^{\infty}M(r)\wedge_{\Sigma_{r}}\left(\bigvee_{\phi:\bf n\rightarrow\bf r}N(\phi)\right),

where
N(\phi)=N(n_{1}(\phi))\wedge\dots\wedge N(n_{r}(\phi))
and
n_{k}(\phi)
is the cardinality of
\phi^{-1}(k)
.

It is useful to make some first observations.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)
\displaystyle(M\circ N)(0)=\bigvee_{r=0}^{\infty}M(r)\wedge_{\Sigma_{r}}N(0)^{r}

(b) If
N(n)=*
for all
n\neq 0
, then, for all
M
,
(M\circ N)(n)=*
for all
n\neq 0
.

(c) If
N(0)=*
, then
\displaystyle(M\circ N)(n)=\bigvee_{r=1}^{\infty}M(r)\wedge_{\Sigma_{r}}\left(\bigvee_{\phi\in\operatorname{Epi}(\bf n,\bf r)}N(\phi)\right)
,
where
\operatorname{Epi}(\bf n,\bf r)
denotes the set of epimorphisms from
\bf n
to
\bf r
.

(d)
M\circ N
commutes with all colimits in the first variable, and with filtered colimits and reflexive equalizers in the second variable.

A reference for this last property is
[
6
, Prop. 2.4.1]

The category
(\mathsf{Sym}(Sp),\circ,{\mathbb{S}}(1))
is monoidal, with unit
{\mathbb{S}}(1)=i_{1}({\mathbb{S}})=(*,{\mathbb{S}},*,*,\dots)
.

An operad
{\mathcal{O}}
is then a monoid in this monoidal category. The operad unit
{\mathbb{S}}(1)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}
corresponds to a map
{\mathbb{S}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(1)
, and the operad multiplication

\mu:{\mathcal{O}}\circ{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}

corresponds to a family of appropriately compatible multiplication maps

\mu_{\phi}:{\mathcal{O}}(r)\wedge{\mathcal{O}}(\phi)\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(n)

for all
\phi:\bf n\rightarrow\bf r
.

Similarly, left and right
{\mathcal{O}}
–modules
N
and
M
have multiplication maps
{\mathcal{O}}\circ N\rightarrow N
and
M\circ{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow M
. An
{\mathcal{O}}
–algebra is a left
{\mathcal{O}}
–module concentrated in degree 0, and can be viewed as a spectrum
I
equipped with appropriately compatible maps
{\mathcal{O}}(n)\wedge_{\Sigma_{n}}I^{\wedge n}\rightarrow I
.

Finally we recall that if
M
is a right
{\mathcal{O}}
–module, and
N
is a left
{\mathcal{O}}
–module,
M\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}N
is defined as the coequalizer in
\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
of the two evident maps

M\circ{\mathcal{O}}\circ N\begin{array}[]{c}\longrightarrow\\[-7.22743pt]
\longrightarrow\end{array}M\circ N
. One has isomorphisms
M\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathcal{O}}=M
and
{\mathcal{O}}\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}N=N
.
Extra structure on
M
or
N
can then induce evident extra structure on
M\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}N
. In particular, if
I
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
–algebra, and
M
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
–bimodule, then
M\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}I
is again an
{\mathcal{O}}
–algebra.

We specialize the above to the case when

Given

Proposition 3.1

(a)

.

(b) The natural map

is a weak equivalence in
\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}Com

(c) If

is a cofibrant right

Sketch Proof.

The first statement follows from the fact that

commutes with all colimits in the variable

.

The proof of the second statement is standard. One lets

be the constant simplicial object in
\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)

for all

, so that

. Then one notes that there is a map of simplical objects

such that

, and a simplicial homotopy from
\eta_{*}\circ\epsilon_{*}
to

. Now apply realization.

For the last statement,

\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
implies that

will also cofibrant in

, and thus that

Definition 4.1

Given

Proof.

We first check this when

The category

, with

as the tensor product of

and

\begin{split}K\otimes(Com\circ X)&=Com\circ(K\wedge X)=\bigvee_{n=1}^{\infty}(K\wedge X)^{\wedge n}_{\Sigma_{n}}=\bigvee_{n=1}^{\infty}K^{\wedge n}\wedge_{\Sigma_{n}}X^{\wedge n}\\

and one sees that

As functors on

,

Now we turn to the cofibrancy property of

Theorem 4.4

Note that if

Now we observe that maps have epi-mono factorizations:

9.
Replacing
Com
by a more general operad

For simplicity and because it is so central, we have been working with the operad
Com
, but much of what we have presented here can be generalized with
Com
replaced by a more general operad in
Sp
. As was done in
[
12
]
, one should work with reduced operads
{\mathcal{O}}
such that each
{\mathcal{O}}(n)
is cofibrant in
Sp
, and
S\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(1)
is a cofibration. It is also simplifying to insist that
S\rightarrow{\mathcal{O}}(1)
is a weak equivalence.

With these hypotheses, all our results about
B(M,Com,I)
also hold for
B(M,{\mathcal{O}},I)
, as do our general results about the decreasing and increasing filtration of right
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules. (The decreasing filtration was already studied in this generality in
[
12
]
.) In particular, one defines
TQ_{{\mathcal{O}}}(I)
to be
B({\mathcal{O}}(1),{\mathcal{O}},I)
.

One needs more care with discussions regarding modelling
K\otimes I
, with
K
a based space and
I
an
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra.

If one defines
K\otimes{\mathcal{O}}
by letting
(K\otimes{\mathcal{O}})(n)=K^{\wedge n}\wedge O(n)
, it isn’t hard to see that this is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-bimodule, and then that there is a natural map
K\otimes I\rightarrow(K\otimes{\mathcal{O}})\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}I
that can be proved to be an isomorphism using the same method of proof as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

When
{\mathcal{O}}
is not
Com
, it is not clear that
(K\otimes{\mathcal{O}})
is necessarily cofibrant in the category of right
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules. In particular, our proof that
{\bf m}_{+}\otimes Com
is cofibrant – part of the proof of Theorem
4.4
– does not work for more general operads. Nevertheless it is possible the result is still true.

A work-around for some purposes is to just replace
(K\otimes{\mathcal{O}})
by a cofibrant replacement in
\mathsf{Mod}\text{-}Com
. If
I
is a cofibrant
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebra, since
(K\otimes{\mathcal{O}})
will be cofibrant in
\mathsf{Sym}(Sp)
, we will still be able to deduce that
(K\otimes{\mathcal{O}})^{c}\circ_{{\mathcal{O}}}I\simeq K\otimes I
. Since, as before, there will be a weak equivalence of cofibrant right
{\mathcal{O}}
-modules

B({\mathcal{O}}(1),{\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{O}})\simeq\operatorname*{colim}_{k}\Sigma^{-k}(S^{k}\otimes{\mathcal{O}})^{c},

we can again conclude that the functor
TQ_{{\mathcal{O}}}
corresponds to stabilization in the category of
{\mathcal{O}}
-algebras.

For a general operad
{\mathcal{O}}
, there does not seem to be a neat description of the increasing filtration on
K\otimes{\mathcal{O}}
, analogous to the description given in Lemma
8.5
. As illustrated in
[
10
]
, with
{\mathcal{O}}=Com
, the resulting nice description of the cofibers of
K\otimes I
is key to various applications.

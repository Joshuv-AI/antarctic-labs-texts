Construction of a nomogram for preoperative prediction of occult peritoneal metastasis in advanced gastric cancer based on circulating tumor cells and fibrinogen to albumin ratio
Authors: Zhipeng Wu, Cheng Zuo, Changlei Wu, Huakai Tian, Zhengming Zhu, Wenjie Zhu, Liang Sun, Zitao Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOccult peritoneal metastasis (OPM) represents a type of distant metastasis that is often overlooked during the preoperative diagnosis of advanced gastric cancer (AGC).MethodsWe employed the negative enrichment combined with immunofluorescence in situ hybridization (imFISH) technique to detect the number of circulating tumor cells (CTC) in the peripheral blood of patients with AGC. 523 AGC patients were randomly divided into a training set (N = 367) and a validation set (N = 156) in a 7:3 ratio. We constructed a nomogram for predicting OPM risk through multivariate logistic regression. Finally, we evaluated the performance of the nomogram using multi-index cross-validation and clinical decision curve analysis (DCA).ResultsThe number of CTC and the level of the fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) in patients with OPM positivity were significantly higher than those in OPM-negative patients. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that CTC > 4/5 mL, FAR > 0.094, CA125 > 35.000 U/mL, and tumor invasion of the serosal layer were identified as independent risk factors for OPM in AGC. The areas under the ROC curve of the nomogram in the training and validation sets were 0.898 and 0.823, respectively. After performing 100 iterations of 10-fold cross-validation, the goodness of fit (R²), area under the curve (AUC), Brier score, and maximum and minimum deviations from the ideal model (Emax and Eavg) all indicated that the nomogram exhibited good performance.ConclusionsThe nomogram developed using CTCs and FAR enhances the early diagnosis of OPM and supports the formulation of personalized treatment plans for patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05371-9.

Introduction

Peritoneal metastasis (PM) is the most common form of distant metastasis in advanced gastric cancer (AGC) and represents a significant factor contributing to poor patient prognosis [1]. Currently, the clinical diagnosis of PM primarily relies on imaging methods such as CT and MRI. However, 10–30% of patients with AGC do not exhibit the typical imaging characteristics of PM preoperatively and are diagnosed with PM during surgery, a condition clinically referred to as occult peritoneal metastasis (OPM) [2]. The prognosis for OPM patients is better than that for patients with imaging characteristics of PM, but worse than that for patients without PM [3]. In recent years, with the continuous improvement of treatment regimens, including chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, the prognosis for OPM patients has significantly improved [4–6]. Some early-stage OPM patients even have the opportunity to achieve surgical cure following conversion therapy [7]. Therefore, the early identification and diagnosis of OPM are crucial for improving patients’ prognosis and quality of life. Recent studies have also developed several predictive models. However, most are based on radiomic results to evaluate the possibility of OPM through machine learning [8, 9]. Due to the complexity of the radiomics analysis process, which includes image segmentation, feature extraction, and model establishment, there is a lack of unified standards across these stages, thereby limiting the clinical applicability of these models. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop a non-invasive predictive model that is convenient for clinical use.

Circulating tumor cells (CTC) are a primary method of liquid biopsy and can accurately reflect tumor burden [10]. CTC refer to tumor cells that detach from the primary tumor site or metastatic lesions and enter the bloodstream [11]. Previous studies have confirmed that CTC detection aids in the early diagnosis, prognosis, and evaluation of clinical treatment efficacy in cancer patients [11–14]. For instance, Xie et al.’s study found that the number of CTC is closely related to the number of distant metastatic sites in breast cancer, a higher number of CTC in patients correlates with a worse prognosis [15]. Additionally, in the study by Li et al., it was confirmed that the number of CTC is closely associated with lymph node metastasis, overall survival (OS), and disease-free survival (DFS) in gastric cancer [16]. Collectively, these studies demonstrate that CTC detection holds significant clinical application value in tumor diagnosis. Therefore, CTC may contribute to the early diagnosis of gastric cancer OPM.

The inflammatory microenvironment plays a crucial role in the initiation, progression, and metastasis of gastric cancer [17]. Chronic inflammation can induce DNA damage and gene mutations, promoting the abnormal proliferation of gastric mucosal epithelial cells, thereby increasing the risk of carcinogenesis [18]. Furthermore, cytokines and chemokines within the inflammatory microenvironment can induce epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), angiogenesis, and matrix remodeling in gastric cancer cells, facilitating their invasion and metastasis [19, 20]. Consequently, various inflammatory markers have been developed in clinical practice to assess the inflammatory microenvironment of patients [21–24], such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), systemic inflammation response index (SIRI), fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR), and fibrinogen-to-prealbumin ratio (FpAR). Previous studies have confirmed that these inflammatory indices can effectively evaluate the prognosis, postoperative recurrence risk, and treatment efficacy of gastric cancer patients [25–27]. Tang et al.’s study has shown that high levels of NLR and PLR are closely associated with poorer OS and progression-free survival (PFS) in gastric cancer patients, as well as a higher likelihood of resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors [25]. Liu et al.’s study found that SIRI could effectively predict the risk of lymph node metastasis in early gastric cancer before surgery [28]. In recent years, research has also validated the role of FAR in the prognosis and clinical pathological staging assessments of colorectal cancer, liver cancer, breast cancer, and bladder cancer [29–32]. However, the application value of these inflammatory indices in assessing the risk of OPM in AGC remains unclear.

This study aims to explore the correlation between CTC and inflammatory indices in relation to gastric cancer OPM, as well as to develop a predictive model for assessing the risk of gastric cancer OPM. This model will facilitate the early identification and diagnosis of OPM, thereby improving the prognosis for gastric cancer patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study retrospectively analyzed the clinicopathological data of 523 patients with AGC who were treated at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University from January 2021 to February 2024. The inclusion criteria were: (1) Patients diagnosed with clinical stage II or III gastric cancer by a preoperative multidisciplinary team. (2) Patients confirmed to have PM through laparoscopy and peritoneal lavage cytology. (3) Patients who underwent CTC detection before receiving any treatment. The exclusion criteria included: (1) Patients diagnosed with early gastric cancer both preoperatively and postoperatively, as the likelihood of distant metastasis in early gastric cancer patients is low. (2) Patients with preoperative imaging indicating characteristics of peritoneal metastasis or other distant metastases. (3) Patients with concurrent tumors at multiple sites. (4) Patients with preoperative infections or hematological diseases.

Diagnosis of OPM

The patient exhibited no typical imaging characteristics of peritoneal metastasis prior to surgery. During the operation, rapid pathological diagnosis indicated peritoneal metastasis, or the cytological examination of the peritoneal lavage fluid was positive, and the postoperative pathological diagnosis confirmed the presence of tumor cell invasion in the omentum. Each of these scenarios is classified as OPM.

CTC detection method

In this study, we employed the negative enrichment combined with the immunofluorescence in situ hybridization (imFISH) technique to detect the number of CTC in the peripheral blood of patients with AGC. The specific experimental procedures were referenced from our previous research [33]. First, negative enrichment of blood samples is performed by removing plasma, lysing red blood cells, and specifically depleting leukocytes using immunomagnetic beads, thereby achieving the enrichment of rare cells. Subsequently, the enriched cells undergo im-FISH analysis: hybridization is conducted simultaneously using centromeric probes for chromosomes 8 and 17, combined with leukocyte common antigen CD45 staining and DAPI nuclear staining. Under fluorescence microscopy, cells that are CD45 negative, DAPI positive, and have ≥ 3 signal points from either the chromosome 8 or 17 probes are defined as circulating tumor cells. Compared to traditional detection methods that rely on specific antigens such as EpCAM, this technique effectively avoids false positives caused by leukocyte interference by recognizing the universally present chromosomal aneuploidy characteristics of tumor cells. It also addresses the issue of antigen loss and difficulty in capturing certain tumor cells that undergo epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT). This method does not rely on specific surface markers, thus significantly improving the detection rate of CTCs, especially for aggressive tumor cells exhibiting EMT characteristics. Imaging features

Previous studies have demonstrated that the T4 stage of the tumor and lymph node metastasis are significant risk factors for PM in gastric cancer. Consequently, we assessed the status of tumor invasion into the serosal layer and lymph node metastasis based on imaging features observed on CT scans. The criteria for tumor invasion into the serosal layer include an irregular or nodular appearance of the serosal surface on imaging, dense surrounding fat space, or the absence of fat space between the tumor and adjacent organs. The criteria for lymph node metastasis consist of a short-axis diameter of the lymph node greater than 8 mm, pronounced enhancement of the lymph node, necrosis within the lymph node, and imaging characteristics such as multiple clustered lymph nodes.

Clinicopathological information

The primary clinical information includes: (1) General conditions, encompassing the patient’s gender, age, BMI, and underlying diseases. (2) Tumor markers, specifically CEA, CA125, and CA199, which are categorized into high and low groups based on the thresholds of CEA > 5.0 ng/ml, CA199 > 37 U/ml, and CA125 > 35 U/ml. (3) Inflammatory indices, comprising neutrophil (N), lymphocyte (L), platelet count (P), fibrinogen (F), albumin (Alb), and prealbumin levels (pAlb), from which inflammatory markers such as NLR, PLR, SIRI, FAR, and FpAR are derived. Based on the ROC curve calculated from the training set, the maximum value of the Youden Index (sensitivity + specificity − 1) was determined to establish the cutoff values for CTC, NLR, PLR, FpAR, FAR, and SIRI. Patients were then categorized into high and low groups. (4) Pathological information, which includes tumor location, tumor size, Borrmann type, and degree of differentiation.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are described using the mean ± standard deviation or median and interquartile range, and are analyzed using the t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are described using percentages and analyzed with the chi-square test. Variables associated with OPM are screened using Lasso regression analysis and the random forest algorithm. The Random Forest algorithm is one of the methods in machine learning and is considered a supervised learning approach, serving as an extension of decision trees. In the Random Forest algorithm, the greater the MeanDecreaseGini of a variable, the more important it is. A nomogram is constructed based on the results of multivariate logistic regression analysis, and its clinical application value is evaluated using the ROC curve, calibration curve, and DCA curve. We perform a ten-fold cross-validation, repeated 100 times, with multiple indicators to assess the model’s performance. The Brier score measures the parameter of probability calibration, with a lower Brier score indicating better predictive standards for the model. R² indicates the goodness-of-fit of the model. Emax and Eavg represent the maximum and minimum deviations between the model and the ideal model, respectively. The smaller these two metrics are, the closer the predictive model is to the ideal model. Data analysis is conducted using the R language (version 4.3.2) with packages such as rms, modEvA, ggDCA, randomForest, and caret, and a two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

This study included clinicopathological information from 523 patients diagnosed with AGC (Table 1). A total of 63 patients (12.1%) were identified as having OPM, with 41 patients (11.2%) in the training set and 22 patients (14.1%) in the validation set diagnosed with OPM. The positive detection rate of CTC in this study was 88.3% (462/523), while CTC was not detected in the peripheral blood of 61 patients (11.7%), including 8 patients (12.7%) from the OPM group. Our previous research indicated that CTC > 4/5 mL is closely associated with later TNM stages of gastric cancer. Consequently, we categorized the patients into two groups using CTC > 4/5 mL as the cutoff value. In the training set, 41 patients (11.2%) had a CTC count exceeding 4, with 34.1% of these in the OPM group. In the validation set, 15 patients (9.6%) had a CTC count greater than 4, among which 40.0% were in the OPM group (Fig. 2). Furthermore, in both the training and validation sets, there were 146 patients (39.8%) and 67 patients (42.9%) with elevated levels of FAR, respectively, of which 28 patients (19.2%) and 15 patients (22.1%) were diagnosed with OPM (Fig. 2).

Imaging results indicated that 136 (37.1%) and 59 (37.8%) patients in the training and validation sets, respectively, exhibited characteristics of tumor invasion into the serosal layer. Among these patients, 34 (25.0%) in the training set and 18 (30.5%) in the validation set were diagnosed the OPM group (Fig. 2). Additionally, 260 (70.8%) and 115 (73.7%) patients in the training and validation sets, respectively, were identified as having lymph node metastasis, among whom 35 (13.5%) and 21 (18.3%) patients diagnosed OPM. Pathological characteristics revealed that 33 (9.0%) and 11 (7.1%) patients in the training and validation sets, respectively, were classified as Borrmann type IV, with 24.2% of patients in the training set diagnosed with OPM, compared to 45.4% in the validation set. The majority of tumors were located in the lower third of the stomach, with most being poorly differentiated adenocarcinomas, signet ring cell carcinomas, or mucinous adenocarcinomas.

Univariate and multivariate analyses for predicting OPM

Lasso regression and univariate logistic regression analyses revealed that factors such as Borrmann type IV, poor tumor differentiation, tumor diameter > 5 cm, serosal invasion, and lymph node metastasis observed on CT, as well as NLR, PLR, FpAR, FAR, SIRI, CTC count, and levels of CA199 and CA125, were closely associated with the occurrence of OPM in gastric cancer (Fig. 3). Random forest analysis identified CA125 levels > 35 U/ml, serosal invasion on CT, and CTC > 4/5 ml as significant predictors of OPM (Fig. 3). Furthermore, multivariate analysis indicated that CA125 levels > 35 U/ml, serosal invasion, CTC > 4/5 ml, and FAR > 0.094 were independent risk factors for OPM in AGC (Table 2).

Construction and validation of a nomogram for predicting OPM

Based on the results of multifactorial analysis, a nomogram for predicting OPM in AGC has been constructed. This nomogram allows for the quantification of the risk of OPM occurrence for each patient. The ROC curve indicated that the AUC of the nomogram was 0.898 in the training set and 0.823 in the validation set, respectively (Fig. 4). Ten-fold cross-validation with 100 iterations of multiple indicators revealed a Brier score of 0.069 and an R² of 0.437, indicating that the model demonstrates good discriminative ability (Table 3). The U test, Emax, Eavg, and calibration curve all confirmed that the model closely approximates the ideal model (Fig. 5). Furthermore, we compared our model with those constructed based on variables previously identified as being associated with OPM, including serosal invasion, CA125, Borrmann type IV, and tumor differentiation grade. The ROC and DCA curves illustrated that our nomogram exhibits superior predictive performance and clinical application value (Fig. 5) (Table 4).

Discussion

Gastric cancer with OPM remains a significant challenge in clinical treatment. Due to its lack of distinct preoperative imaging features and aggressive biological characteristics, early diagnosis is difficult, resulting in a poor prognosis [1]. Currently, several biological markers have been developed for the preoperative diagnosis of PM in gastric cancer [34, 35]. However, research specifically focused on gastric cancer OPM is limited. In this study, a nomogram for preoperative prediction of OPM risk was constructed based on factors such as CTC, FAR, CA125, and serosal invasion. This aids in the risk stratification of OPM before surgery and facilitates the formulation of appropriate treatment plans in the early stages of the disease.

The application of CTC detection technology facilitates early and comprehensive analysis in patients with malignant tumors, enhancing early prevention, diagnosis, molecular typing of tumors, personalized medication, and prognosis prediction [10, 11]. Previous studies have demonstrated that CTC detection possesses significant clinical application value in the diagnosis and treatment of gastric cancer [36]. For instance, Zhang et al.’s study found that CTC detection effectively assessed the resistance of HER2-positive metastatic gastric cancer patients to trastuzumab treatment [37]. Additionally, Eskandarion et al.’s study indicated that elevated levels of CTC are associated with poor prognosis and chemotherapy resistance in gastric cancer patients [36]. Our previous research has also confirmed a close relationship between CTC levels and advanced TNM staging of gastric cancer [33]. In this study, we found that high levels of CTC in peripheral blood are closely related to gastric cancer with OPM. In the training and validation sets, 34.1% and 40.0% of patients in the high CTC level group were diagnosed with OPM, respectively. Therefore, CTC can serve as a biomarker for the early diagnosis of OPM, aiding in the timely clinical identification of OPM and the formulation of appropriate treatment plans.

The inflammatory microenvironment plays a significant role in the occurrence, progression, and metastasis of gastric cancer. Previous studies have confirmed that inflammatory markers can effectively assess TNM staging, prognosis, postoperative recurrence, and treatment efficacy in gastric cancer [18, 20]. In this study, univariate analysis revealed that inflammatory markers such as NLR, PLR, FAR, FpAR, and SIRI were associated with OPM in gastric cancer. However, multivariate analysis indicated that only FAR was an independent risk factor for OPM. In the training and validation sets, 19.2% and 22.1% of patients in the high FAR group were diagnosed with OPM, respectively. Furthermore, random forest analysis demonstrated that FAR outperformed NLR, PLR, and SIRI in predicting OPM. Therefore, FAR serves as an inflammatory marker that can effectively predict OPM.

Previous studies have demonstrated that PM of gastric cancer correlates with tumor stage and pathological type [38]. The elevated rate of PM in gastric cancer is closely linked to the T4 stage, lymph node metastasis, Borrmann type IV, and poor tumor differentiation [2]. In this study, the T stage and lymph node metastasis status of AGC were evaluated through CT imaging characteristics. Univariate analysis confirmed that these clinicopathological factors are all associated with gastric cancer OPM. However, multivariate analysis revealed that only serosal invasion serves as an independent risk factor for gastric cancer OPM. This finding aligns with the ‘seed and soil’ theory of PM, which posits that gastric cancer invading the serosal layer can readily lead to the shedding of tumor cells into the abdominal cavity, resulting in metastatic implants [39]. Furthermore, tumor markers are extensively utilized in the diagnosis of gastrointestinal tumors. Previous studies have indicated that CA199 and CA125 levels are closely associated with lymph node metastasis and PM in gastric cancer [40, 41]. This study further confirms that CA199 and CA125 levels are significantly related to OPM in gastric cancer, with elevated levels of CA125 identified as an independent risk factor for OPM in gastric cancer.

Radiomics plays a significant role in diagnosing PM in gastric cancer. Through artificial intelligence algorithms, imaging data are analyzed to predict the risk of PM [42, 43]. Previous studies have developed various radiomics models for predicting OPM in gastric cancer [44]. However, the lack of specific imaging features for OPM and the manual delineation of anatomical regions required in radiomics lead to considerable individual variability in results, which somewhat limits the application of radiomics in OPM of gastric cancer. Detecting biological markers such as CTC, FAR, and CA125 in peripheral blood offers advantages, including minimal test result errors and convenience in detection. Additionally, research has confirmed that our nomogram’s ability to predict OPM surpasses that of prediction models constructed based on clinicopathological factors.

This study has several limitations. Firstly, it is based on single-center data. In the future, we plan to conduct analyses and validations using multi-center data. Secondly, our previous research indicated that cytokine levels are closely related to the advanced TNM staging of gastric cancer. However, in this study, the small sample size for cytokine detection prevented us from analyzing the relationship between cytokines and OPM in gastric cancer. In subsequent studies, we will increase the sample size to clarify this correlation. Additionally, CTC detection has some limitations, such as insufficient purity and contamination by white blood cells, loss of target cells and impact on viability, and the risk of erroneously excluding CD45-positive CTCs.

Conclusion

The number of CTC in peripheral blood and the level of FAR can serve as biomarkers for the early diagnosis of gastric cancer associated with OPM. The nomogram constructed based on factors such as CTCs, FAR, CA125, and serosal invasion aids in the early identification of OPM, thereby facilitating the development of personalized treatment plans.

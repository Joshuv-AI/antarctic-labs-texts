Identification of PGRMC1 as a regulator of the TLR3–TICAM1–IFNβ1 signaling axis in RAW264.7 macrophages
Authors: Hiroya Ohta, Kiyoshi Takahashi, Kayoko Takeda
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPGRMC1 is a member of the membrane-associated progesterone receptor (MAPR) family, which plays various roles under both pathological and physiological conditions. Although PGRMC1 is expressed in macrophages, its functions in macrophages remain to be fully elucidated. In this study, we aimed to investigate the roles of PGRMC1 in regulating Toll-like receptor (TLR) signaling in macrophages by inhibiting PGRMC1. The expression levels of Pgrmc1 in RAW264.7 cells, a monocyte/macrophage-like cell line, were significantly increased by TLR3 stimulation but not by TLR4 stimulation. Inhibition of PGRMC1 in RAW264.7 cells significantly suppressed the increased expression of interferon beta1 (Ifnb1) induced by TLR3 stimulation. Additionally, inhibition of PGRMC1 significantly suppressed the upregulation of Ticam1, an essential regulator of TLR3 signaling. However, inhibition of PGRMC1 had little effect on the expression levels of interferon regulatory factor 3 (Irf3), a key transcriptional regulator of IFNβ1. Inhibition of PGRMC1 in RAW264.7 cells also exhibited a minimal effect on the increased expression of interleukin-6 (Il-6) induced by TLR4 stimulation. PGRMC2, another member of the MAPR family, was expressed in RAW264.7 cells, but it did not compensate for the absence of PGRMC1, despite the high homology between the amino acid sequences of PGRMC1 and PGRMC2. In conclusion, our results suggest that PGRMC1 in RAW264.7 cells modulates Ifnb1 expression by regulating Ticam1 expression. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate that PGRMC1 contributes to regulating IFNβ1 production in response to TLR3 stimulation in RAW264.7 cells.Keywords: PGRMC1, TLR3, TICAM1, IFNβ1, RAW264.7, Macrophages

Introduction

PGRMC1 (Progesterone receptor membrane component 1), PGRMC2, Neudesin, and Neuferricin are members of the MAPR (Membrane-Associated Progesterone Receptor) family [1,2]. PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 are membrane proteins, whereas Neudesin and Neuferricin are secreted proteins [1,2]. PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 share a close evolutionary relationship in vertebrates, arising from the duplication of an ancestral Pgrmc gene. The MAPR family has been implicated in cancer development [[1], [2], [3]]. For instance, PGRMC1 promotes cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and EGFR signaling in various cell types, including cancer cells [3], while PGRMC2 contributes to erlotinib resistance in lung adenocarcinoma cells [4]. Neudesin deficiency suppresses tumor growth by increasing dendritic and CD8+ T cell infiltration in tumors [5]. Neuferricin, on the other hand, regulates cell growth and enhances chemoresistance in HeLa cells [6]. These findings suggest that MAPR family members may serve as potential therapeutic targets for cancer treatment.

Beyond cancer, MAPR proteins play diverse physiological roles. Both PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 are broadly expressed across tissues [1,2]. PGRMC1 interacts with SREBP-1 in the liver to suppress lipogenesis [7], whereas in adipose tissue, it promotes adipogenesis, as evidenced by Pgrmc1 knockout (KO) mice, which exhibit resistance to high-fat diet (HFD)-induced obesity [8]. PGRMC2 is highly expressed in brown and white adipose tissue, and its knockout impairs adaptive thermogenesis due to brown adipose tissue dysfunction [9]. Neudesin is also widely expressed, including in the brain, adipose tissue, and spleen [10], where it exhibits neurotrophic activity, enhances neural proliferation and differentiation [[10], [11], [12]], and suppresses adipogenesis in 3T3-L1 cells [13]. We previously demonstrated that Neudesin KO mice resist HFD-induced obesity due to increased energy expenditure [14], while another study reported that Neudesin KO mice exhibit heightened anxiety behavior [15]. Neuferricin has been shown to promote neurogenesis, with its knockdown enhancing survival and proliferation in Neuro2a cells [16]. Recent studies suggest that Neudesin also plays roles in immune regulation. As noted earlier, it modulates tumor growth by regulating dendritic and CD8+ T cells [5]. Additionally, it suppresses cytokine production in bone marrow-derived dendritic cells upon lipopolysaccharide (LPS) stimulation [17], inhibits erythrophagocytosis in splenic macrophages [18], and attenuates macrophage activation by suppressing the Jak/Stat1/iNOS pathway [19]. Although PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 are expressed in various immune cells [20], their precise roles in immune cells remain largely unknown.

Given the importance of macrophages in innate immunity and our previous research on macrophage function [21], we sought to investigate the roles of PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 in macrophage function. Macrophages serve as the first line of defense against pathogens [21], utilizing Toll-like receptors (TLRs) to recognize pathogen-associated molecular patterns from viruses, bacteria, fungi, and parasites [22,23]. TLR activation triggers cytokine production, including interferons (IFNs) and interleukins (ILs) [22,23]. Cytokines produced by macrophages upon TLR stimulation act not only on neighboring macrophages but also on other immune and non-immune cells, thereby playing pivotal roles in shaping an appropriate immune microenvironment [24]. Therefore, elucidating how PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 influence cytokine production in macrophages will not only advance our understanding of macrophage functional regulation but also provide insight into the roles of these proteins in establishing the immune microenvironment.

In this study, we show that PGRMC1 in macrophages supports IFNβ1 production by regulating TICAM1, a key adaptor in TLR3 signaling. To our knowledge, this is the first report detailing the role of PGRMC1 in macrophage function and contributes to a deeper understanding of the involvement of the MAPR family in immune regulation. Although our current study is limited to in vitro findings, it may provide a basis for future studies aimed at clarifying the physiological relevance of PGRMC1 in antiviral innate immune responses.

Methods

Cultures of RAW264.7 cells

RAW264.7 cells were kindly provided by Dr. Koji Wakame (Hokkaido University of Science, Japan). The cells were cultured in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle's Medium (DMEM) (Gibco) supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Japan BioSerum) and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin (Nacalai Tesque) at 37 °C. RAW264.7 cells were seeded at 2.0 × 105 cells/well in 12-well plates. The next day, when cell confluency reached approximately 70 %, the medium was replaced, and the cells were stimulated with poly(I:C) (PIC) (Tocris), a TLR3 agonist, or lipopolysaccharide (LPS) (SIGMA), a TLR4 agonist. PIC and LPS were dissolved in PBS. The PGRMC1 inhibitor AG-205 (A1487, SIGMA) was added simultaneously with PIC or LPS and was dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). After 24 h of stimulation, RAW264.7 cells were collected for further analysis.

siRNA transfection

RAW264.7 cells were transfected with 100 nM siRNA targeting mouse Pgrmc1 (AM16708, Thermo Fisher Scientific) or negative control siRNA (AM4611, Thermo Fisher Scientific) using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX (Thermo Fisher Scientific), according to the manufacturer's instructions. Briefly, cells were seeded at 0.5 × 105 cells per well in 12-well plates. siRNA transfection was performed 24 h after seeding, when cell confluency reached approximately 40 %. Then, 24 h after siRNA transfection, the cells were stimulated with PIC, and after another 24 h, the cells were harvested for further analysis.

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time PCR

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed as previously described [14]. Total RNA was extracted from RAW264.7 cells using an RNeasy Kit (QIAGEN) or FastGene RNA Basic Kit (Nippon Genetics) following the manufacturer's instructions. A total of 500 ng of RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using ReverTra Ace qPCR RT Master Mix (TOYOBO). qRT-PCR was conducted using a QuantStudio 3 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with Thunderbird Next SYBR qPCR Mix (TOYOBO). Gene expression levels were normalized to those of 36B4, and relative expression was calculated using the comparative Ct method. The primers used in this study were as follows: 5′-ggccctgcactctcgctttc-3′ and 5′-tgccaggacgcgcttgt-3′ for 36B4, 5′-ttgccacattttgcctggac-3′ and 5′-ttcagcagttttcccacgtg-3′ for Pgrmc1, 5′-tgaccaaaggcagcaagttc-3′ and 5′-cactgcgttcaaatctgagagg-3′ for Pgrmc2, 5′-agctccaagaaaggacgaacat-3′ and 5′-gccctgtaggtgaggttgatct-3′ for Ifnb1, 5′-ggagatctaccagctcaagacc-3′ and 5′-agacatgttccgaaccgacag-3′ for Ticam1, 5′-aaccggaaagaagtgttgcg-3′ and 5′-tttgccattggtgtcaggag-3′ for Irf3, 5′-tttcccagcagcacagaaac-3′ and 5′-aaatggcacttcagctgtgg-3′ for Ifit3, 5′- ccagagatacaaagaaatgatgg-3′ and 5′-actccagaagaccagaggaaat-3′ for Il-6, and 5′- ttgccagcgagctaattgag-3′ and 5′-acaggctgagtgcaaacttg-3′ for Myd88.

Measurement of IFNβ1 by ELISA

Cell-free supernatants were collected, and the concentration of IFNβ1 in the culture medium was measured using a Mouse IFN-beta Quantikine ELISA Kit (R&D Systems) according to the manufacturer's protocols.

Statistical analysis

All data are expressed as the mean ± standard error (SE). Statistical analyses were performed using an unpaired t-test or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post-hoc test. All p-values were two-sided, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 10.

Results

TLR3 stimulation increases PGRMC1 expression in RAW264.7 cells

We first examined the effect of TLR stimulation on Pgrmc1 and Pgrmc2 expression in RAW264.7 cells. TLRs are classified into plasma membrane-localized and intracellular receptors [24]. We selected TLR4 as a representative membrane TLR and TLR3 as an intracellular TLR. Stimulation with poly(I:C) (PIC), a synthetic TLR3 ligand, had little effect on Pgrmc1 expression at 4 h but significantly increased its expression at 24 h (Fig. 1A). PIC stimulation significantly increased Pgrmc2 expression regardless of stimulation time; however, the degree of change was smaller than that of Pgrmc1 (Fig. 1B). Next, we examined the effect of TLR4 stimulation using LPS, a component of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria and a TLR4 agonist. LPS significantly increased Pgrmc1 expression at 24 h, although this increase was modest compared with that induced by PIC (Fig. 1C). The decrease in Pgrmc2 expression at 4 h after LPS stimulation was significant but modest compared with the increase in Pgrmc1 expression induced by 24 h of PIC stimulation (Fig. 1D). These results led us to focus on the role of PGRMC1 in the TLR3 response.

A-B. Expression levels of Pgrmc1 and Pgrmc2 in RAW264.7 cells treated with 5 μg/mL of Poly(I:C) for 4 or 24 h. C-D. Expression levels of Pgrmc1 and Pgrmc2 in RAW264.7 cells treated with 10 ng/mL of LPS for 4 or 24 h 36B4 was used as an internal control. (n = 3; ∗, p < 0.05, ∗∗∗, p < 0.001).

PGRMC1 inhibition suppresses IFNβ1 expression induced by TLR3 stimulation

TLR3 stimulation in RAW264.7 cells increases type I IFNs, including IFNβ1 [25]. PIC stimulation for 24 h significantly upregulated Ifnb1 expression (Fig. 2A). To investigate the role of PGRMC1, we pharmacologically inhibited PGRMC1 using AG-205 [26]. AG-205 dose-dependently suppressed PIC-induced Ifnb1 expression (Fig. 2A), suggesting that PGRMC1 is essential for IFNβ1 production in RAW264.7 cells. To confirm that this impairment extended to the protein level, we measured IFNβ1 concentrations in the culture supernatant of RAW264.7 cells using ELISA. PIC stimulation for 24 h increased IFNβ1 concentration, whereas AG-205 treatment suppressed PIC-induced IFNβ1 production (Fig. 2B). These results indicate that PGRMC1 inhibition impairs IFNβ1 signaling in RAW264.7 cells at both the mRNA and protein levels. Since IFIT3 is transcriptionally induced by IFNβ1 [27], we examined Ifit3 expression. PIC stimulation significantly increased Ifit3 expression, which was also dose-dependently suppressed by AG-205 (Fig. 2C). These results indicate that PGRMC1 inhibition impairs IFNβ1 signaling in RAW264.7 cells.

B. Protein levels of IFNβ1 in the culture supernatant of RAW264.7 cells stimulated with 5 μg/mL poly(I:C) for 24 h. In some experiments, 1 or 10 μM AG-205 was added to inhibit PGRMC1. (n = 5–6; ∗, p < 0.05).

To elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying PGRMC1-mediated Ifnb1 regulation, we examined Ticam1 expression. TICAM1, an essential TLR3 adaptor, mediates TLR3 signaling and IFNβ1 induction [23]. PIC stimulation significantly increased Ticam1 expression (Fig. 2D), whereas AG-205 dose-dependently suppressed this increase (Fig. 2D). To confirm that this suppression was specific to the TLR3-stimulated condition, we also examined the effect of AG-205 on Ticam1 expression in unstimulated RAW264.7 cells. AG-205 treatment did not affect basal Ticam1 expression in the absence of PIC stimulation (Fig. 2E), suggesting that the observed downregulation of Ticam1 was dependent on TLR3 activation. Given that Ticam1 knockdown impairs IFNβ1 production [28] and that Ticam1 KO mice show increased susceptibility to poliovirus due to impaired IFNβ1 responses [29], our results suggest that PGRMC1 inhibition suppresses IFNβ1 production via Ticam1 downregulation. Since TICAM1 functions upstream of IRF3, a key transcription factor for Ifnb1 [25], we also examined Irf3 expression. AG-205 treatment did not affect Irf3 expression (Fig. 2F), suggesting that PGRMC1 does not regulate IRF3 at the transcriptional level. However, given that Ifnb1 expression was suppressed, IRF3 activation may be impaired. Consistently, AG-205 also suppressed Ifit3 expression (Fig. 2C), and IFIT3 has been reported to enhance IRF3 activity and promote IFN signaling [30]. These findings strongly suggest that PGRMC1 inhibition impairs IRF3 activation, leading to reduced IFNβ1 production.

Although AG-205 has been widely used as a PGRMC1 inhibitor, several recent studies have suggested that AG-205 could affect molecules other than PGRMC1 [31,32]. Thus, to confirm the effect of PGRMC1 inhibition on Ifnb1 expression, we performed siRNA-mediated knockdown of Pgrmc1 in RAW264.7 cells stimulated with PIC. Knockdown of Pgrmc1 significantly reduced Pgrmc1 expression in PIC-stimulated RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 3A). Importantly, knockdown of Pgrmc1 also significantly suppressed the expression of Ifnb1 and Ticam1 in PIC-stimulated RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 3B and C). These results further support the conclusion that inhibition of PGRMC1, either by siRNA or AG-205, suppresses PIC-induced Ifnb1 expression via downregulation of Ticam1 in RAW264.7 cells.

D. Expression levels of Pgrmc2 in RAW264.7 cells treated with 1 or 10 μM of AG-205 for 24 h 36B4 was used as an internal control. (n = 4).

PGRMC2 is another member of the MAPR family [1,2]. PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 share ∼60 % amino acid identity and both contain a heme-binding domain (HBD) that is essential for their functional activity [1,2]. Thus, we examined whether PGRMC2 compensates for PGRMC1 inhibition. AG-205 treatment did not affect Pgrmc2 expression, even at 10 μM (Fig. 3D). These results suggest that PGRMC2 does not functionally compensate for PGRMC1 in RAW264.7 cells. Knockdown of Pgrmc1 also had little effect on Pgrmc2 expression (Fig. 3E), confirming that PGRMC2 does not functionally compensate for PGRMC1 in RAW264.7 cells.

PGRMC1 inhibition does not affect IL-6 expression induced by TLR4 stimulation

TLR4 stimulation in RAW264.7 cells induces inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 [25]. As shown in Fig. 1C, LPS had a limited effect on Pgrmc1 expression, suggesting that PGRMC1 may not influence IL-6 production. To test this hypothesis, we examined the effect of AG-205 on Il-6 expression. Il-6 was undetectable in unstimulated RAW264.7 cells but was significantly induced by LPS stimulation (Fig. 4A). However, AG-205 had no effect on LPS-induced Il-6 expression (Fig. 4A). TLR4 signaling primarily depends on MyD88, an adaptor protein that activates NF-κB, a key transcription factor involved in Il-6 expression [22,23]. LPS stimulation significantly increased Myd88 expression; however, AG-205 treatment had no effect on its expression (Fig. 4B). TLR4 can also recruit TICAM1 and partially regulate IL-6 production [23]. Since AG-205 suppressed Ticam1 expression in the TLR3 pathway, we examined its effect on TLR4-induced Ticam1 expression. While LPS significantly increased Ticam1 expression, AG-205 had no effect (Fig. 4C). These results suggest that PGRMC1 does not influence IL-6 production in macrophages.

A-C. Expression levels of Il-6, Myd88, and Ticam1 in RAW264.7 cells stimulated with 10 ng/mL of LPS for 24 h. In some experiments, 0.1, 1, or 10 μM of AG-205 was added to inhibit PGRMC1. 36B4 were used as an internal control. (n = 4; ∗, p < 0.05, ∗∗∗, p < 0.001).

Discussion

In this study, we investigated the role of PGRMC1, a membrane protein of the MAPR family, in macrophages using RAW264.7 cells. Our findings can be summarized as follows: (1) TLR3 stimulation by PIC increased Pgrmc1 expression; (2) PGRMC1 inhibition suppressed Ifnb expression induced by TLR3 stimulation; and (3) PGRMC1 inhibition reduced Ticam1 expression, an essential adaptor for TLR3 signaling and type I interferon production; (4) PGRMC1 inhibition had little effect on Il-6 expression induced by TLR4 stimulation. While PGRMC1 has been implicated in various pathological and physiological processes [1,2], its role in immune cells, including macrophages, remains largely unexplored [20]. To our knowledge, this is the first study to describe PGRMC1's involvement in macrophage function.

Our results indicate that PGRMC1 selectively influences TLR3 signaling, but not TLR4 signaling. This specificity may stem from differences in the subcellular localization of these receptors. TLR3 is primarily localized in endosomes, whereas TLR4 is predominantly expressed on the plasma membrane [25]. Recent studies suggest that PGRMC1 is mainly found in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and endosomes [33,34]. Given this spatial overlap, PGRMC1 may specifically modulate TLR3-driven IFNβ1 production. Future studies should confirm the co-localization of PGRMC1 and TLR3, and assess whether PGRMC1 is absent from TLR4-containing compartments.

The suppression of Ifnb1 expression by PGRMC1 inhibition appears to be mediated through impaired TLR3 signaling. We observed that Ticam1 expression was significantly reduced in RAW264.7 cells upon inhibition of PGRMC1. Previous studies have shown that Ticam1 knockdown impairs IFNβ1 production [28], and Ticam1KO mice exhibit increased susceptibility to poliovirus infection, partly due to reduced IFNβ1 levels [29]. Based on these findings, the decrease in Ifnb1 expression following PGRMC1 inhibition is likely due to reduced Ticam1 expression. TICAM1 functions upstream of IRF3, the key transcription factor driving Ifnb1 expression [25]. Additionally, Ifit3 expression, which is transcriptionally induced by IFNβ1 and is known to enhance IRF3 activity [27], was significantly suppressed by AG-205 treatment. The suppression of Ifit3 expression not only supports the attenuation of IFNβ1 signaling but also suggests a possible impairment of IRF3 activation. Although Irf3 mRNA levels remained unchanged, the combined reduction in Ticam1 and Ifit3 expression suggests that PGRMC1 inhibition may disrupt IRF3 activity downstream of TICAM1. Although PGRMC1 is not a transcription factor, its inhibition led to a marked decrease in Ticam1 expression. This reduction in Ticam1 expression suggests that PGRMC1 may regulate Ticam1 transcription indirectly through interactions with other regulatory molecules. Notably, PGRMC1 has been reported to bind various proteins via its HBD, raising the possibility that PGRMC1 associates with transcriptional regulators of TICAM1. Identifying PGRMC1-interacting partners in macrophages will be critical for elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying the role of PGRMC1 in TLR3 signaling.

Given their high sequence homology, we initially hypothesized that PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 might compensate for each other. However, our results suggest that PGRMC2 does not compensate for PGRMC1 in RAW264.7 cells. This lack of compensation may be due to differences in their subcellular localization. While PGRMC1 is mainly found in the ER and endosomes [33,34], PGRMC2 is also localized to the nuclear envelope and plasma membrane [34]. Further studies are required to determine the precise localization of PGRMC1 and PGRMC2 in macrophages.

In conclusion, we demonstrate that PGRMC1 regulates IFNβ1 production in macrophages via TLR3 signaling. Given the crucial role of IFNβ1 in antiviral defense, including against poliovirus [29], PGRMC1 may contribute to host immunity. Furthermore, our findings highlight PGRMC1's functional significance, suggesting a broader role for the MAPR family in immune regulation. IFNβ1 is known to play an essential role not only in antiviral defense [24] but also in suppressing the proliferation of tumor cells [35,36]. The present findings suggest that PGRMC1 in macrophages is required for proper IFNβ1 production. While in vivo validation is beyond the scope of this study, our findings in RAW264.7 cells provide a foundation for future physiological investigations. Given the essential role of IFNβ1 in antiviral defense, especially against RNA viruses such as poliovirus [29], it will be necessary to assess whether PGRMC1 modulates the TICAM1-IFNβ1 axis in vivo. Considering the critical contribution of IFNβ1 to both antiviral and antitumor activities, pharmacological or genetic modulation of PGRMC1 may provide a novel approach to promote appropriate IFNβ1 responses, thereby contributing to host defense and tumor suppression. Furthermore, IFNβ1 has been reported to prevent colorectal cancer by eliminating enterotoxigenic Bacteroides fragilis (ETBF), a bacterium associated with an increased risk of colorectal tumorigenesis [35,36]. While PGRMC1 has previously been implicated in promoting tumor development through its proliferative functions [3], our findings raise the possibility that PGRMC1 may also influence tumorigenesis indirectly via the regulation of IFNβ1 production. Thus, this study may open a new avenue for understanding PGRMC1 as a dual modulator of cancer progression—both through direct effects on cell proliferation and through immune-mediated mechanisms involving IFNβ1.

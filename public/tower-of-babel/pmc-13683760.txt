Management of post-operative delayed proximal tibia and fibula fracture with Ayurvedic medicines and Panchakarma: A case report
Authors: Ranjit G Nimbalkar, Pratik J Parekar, Anil T Pawar, Amol A Tagalpallewar, Akshay M Baheti, Manasi R Nimbalkar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSome of the fractures, associated with road traffic accidents (RTAs), pose significant challenges in recovery and quality of life. In this case study, we document the treatment of a 42-year-old male patient with tibia and fibula fractures, sustained during an RTA. Initially managed through open reduction and external fixation, the patient's recovery stagnated for about 3 months, prompting the integration of Ayurvedic therapies, including herbal formulations and Panchakarma. After starting Ayurvedic treatments, including oral Lakshadi Guggul, Mashadi Yoga, Asthi-Majja Yoga, and Panchakarma procedures, significant improvements in fracture healing were observed in the X-rays. The treatments enhanced callus formation and eventual full recovery, demonstrating the potential benefits of Ayurvedic therapies in accelerating fracture healing. This case highlights the efficacy of combining modern surgical techniques with traditional Ayurvedic practices in managing complex fractures, achieving successful outcomes.Keywords: Ayurveda, Accident, Bone regeneration, Fracture, Integrative medicine, Tibia fracture

Introduction

A fracture is considered a complete or partial break in a bone, and many fractures are associated with road traffic accidents (RTAs). These injuries can lead to death or severe complications and significantly affect the patient's quality of life. Untreated or improperly healed fractures may result in several complex morbidities [1]. Bone fractures were well-documented by Acharya Sushruta around the 1st century B.C. In Ayurveda, fractures are referred to as Bhanga or Bhagna, and their etiology is described in the Chikitsa under the title Bhagna Chikitsa [2]. Bone fractures are caused by different types of trauma, such as falling, squeezing, hitting, forceful pulling, bites from ferocious animals, slipping, pressure, striking etc. [3].

The features of Bhagna mentioned in the Chikitsa include Shwayathu Bahulyam (massive swelling), Spandana Vivartana (tenderness), Avapeedyamane Shabda (crepitus), Srasthangata (loss of transmitted movement), Vividha Vedana (various types of pain), and Sarvasvasthasu Na sharmalabha (agonizing pain and discomfort in all postures). In Ayurveda, fractures are described as Sandhimoksha (joint dislocation) and Kandabhagna (bone fracture) [4].

The treatment of fractures in Ayurveda follows the principles of Bhagna Sthapana (reduction), Bhagna Sthirikarana (immobilization), and Punah Cheshta Prasara (rehabilitation). The treatment protocol for bone healing adheres to two approaches namely Shamana Chikitsa (pacifying treatment) and Tarpana Chikitsa (nourishing treatment) [5].

Ayurvedic practitioners often use various Ayurvedic formulations and herbs known for their bone-rejuvenating and anti-inflammatory properties. Panchakarma is often recommended post-operatively for fractures due to its promising effects in accelerating healing, alleviating pain, and restoring the balance of the Tridosha. The Panchakarma procedure involves a set of therapies such as Poorvakarma (Pre-procedures) viz., Abhyanga (external oil application of medicated oil), Swedana (fomentation) and Pradhana karma (Main procedure) i.e. Basti (administering medicine by anal route). This article aims to report a fracture case of a 42-year-old male patient met with a road traffic accident. The patient was managed with Ayurvedic medicines after open reduction and external fixation and we have observed significant improvements in fracture healing [6].

Case presentation

Patient information

A 42-year-old male patient met with a road traffic accident (RTA) on 23 September 2020 during COVID times. The patient was advised for X-ray and diagnosed with a closed comminuted fracture of left tibia and fibula at the proximal region. It was 41-C3 fracture as per AO (Arbeitsgemeinschaft für Osteosynthesefragen) classification (metaphyseal, multi fragmentary, extra-articular fracture). The fracture was managed with open reduction and external fixation on 26 September 2020 (Fig. 1). After 3 months of surgery, follow up X ray didn't show any improvement, so the patient was referred for Ayurveda treatment, which was started on 26 December 2020.

X-ray showing fracture of fracture of the tibia and fibula at the proximal region.

Past medical history

The patient had no notable medical history, including no history of surgery, hospital stays, long-term medication use, or diagnoses of acute or chronic diseases. There was also no history of previous trauma or serious illnesses and no known sensitivities to medications, foods, or environmental factors. No medical history was present, including no family history of inherited or genetic problems or conditions like hypertension, diabetes, or cardiovascular disease.

Clinical findings

When the patient arrived at the OPD a detailed examination revealed the following findings.

Dashavidha pariksha (Tenfold examination)

The patient's Prakriti (physical attributes) was Vata Pitta (predominant Vata and Pitta), Vaya (age) was Madhyama (middle aged). His Mamsa was Asaara (poor muscle tissue strength). Pramaana (measurement of body constituents) was Krisha (thin built). Saatmya (suitability) was towards non-vegetarian and spicy diet which he used to consume frequently, around three times in a week. Samhanana (tissue or organ compactness) was Madhyama (moderate) and Sattva (mental strength) was madhyama (moderate). The patient's Aharashakti (capacity of food intake) was Madhyama (moderate) and Vyayama shakti (power of exercising), before the accident was also Madhyama (moderate). However, after the accident, it became severely limited and he was able to move inside his home, with the help of a walker support.

Systemic examination

The patient did not exhibit any icterus, cyanosis, clubbing or pallor. The tongue was moist and clear (Niraama). The bladder was normal. Bowel habits were normal with occasional flatulence and intermittent constipation. Sleep was normal. The respiratory and cardiovascular systems were within normal limits. His blood pressure was normal (130/80 mm Hg). He was well-oriented and central nervous system (CNS) function was normal.

Primary concerns and symptoms of the patient

The patient had delayed union of the fractures at the left tibia and fibula fracture, in the upper third, as evident by the latest X ray.

Therapeutic intervention

The fractures were managed initially by performing open reduction and external fixation on 26 September 2020. The follow up X-rays didn't show any considerable improvement, so the patient was advised to undergo Ayurvedic intervention on 26 December 2020 to accelerate fracture recovery. Accordingly, he underwent Ayurvedic treatment for one month. The initial treatment protocol was the administration of tablet Lakshadi guggul, tablet Mashadi yoga and tablet Asthi majja yoga (Table 2). He continued the treatment for one month and discontinued further treatment and respective follow-ups. He didn't notice any further improvements in fracture status till 19 March 2021, so he decided to restart the therapy and revisited the OPD for Ayurveda consultation and treatment. His fracture status remained similar to that observed in the postoperative X-ray (Fig. 2). Then he was advised to undergo Panchakarma (Basti) treatment for 21 days along with previous oral treatment. The Basti therapy consisted of the following protocol: Sarvanga Abhyanga i.e. whole-body oil massage with Chandan Bala Lakshadi oil (Due care was taken, to avoid involvement of the operated leg, to avoid any possibility of infection), Sarvanga swedana or whole-body steam bath. Then Samolina Upama/Shira was given for breakfast and administration of Shatavari, Guduchi Ksheerapaaka (Cow milk medicated with these herbs) 100 ml and Mahatiktaka ghritam 20 ml as a Basti i.e. administration through the anal route, immediately after breakfast. The treatment was completed on 9th April 2021. The follow-up X-ray was performed after two months of Basti treatment, on 25 June 2021, which showed significant callus formation at the fracture site (Fig. 3).

Follow-up and outcome

The patient showed significant improvements in fracture healing after resuming Ayurvedic therapy, as evidenced by X-rays showing enhanced callus formation (Fig. 4, Fig. 5). It seems that Panchakarma procedures initiated the recovery, which was subsequently completed by herbal treatments. The patient's mobility improved and he was able to resume his duties, which further added to his increased confidence and quality of life. Ultimately, the combined approach of Ayurvedic interventions and conventional surgery led to the successful and complete healing of the tibia and fibula fractures (see Fig. 6).

Discussion

Tibia and fibula fractures are common traumatic fracture injuries that adversely affect a patient's normal movements and lower limb function (considerable morbidity). Some fractures may lead to osteomyelitis, delayed union, malunion, non-union, avascular necrosis of bone, and heterotropic ossification, following surgery [7,8]. The open reduction and external fixation is primarily a two-step procedure, advised in case of severe fractures, paediatric cases, or a case where the risk of infection is major. This approach is beneficial because it allows external adjustments during the healing process [9,10].

As per Ayurveda, many factors such as proper diet, specific drugs, and lifestyle modifications may be integrated into the treatment and management of traumatic injuries for a better and accelerated healing process. The Ayurvedic therapeutic interventions focus primarily on the regeneration of bone, and promoting fracture union, without significant side effects.

Lakshadi guggul is an Ayurvedic formulation employed in the treatment of bone-related issues. The formulation is rich in calcium, the Laccifer lacca and Cissus quadrangularis possess Bhagna sandhankara (bone healing) properties and the anti-inflammatory nature of the formulation alleviates pain and swelling at the fracture site. Lakshadi guggul is known to help in the restoration of bone mass and strengthens bones, joints, and muscles. It is considered as Bhagna sandhanakara i.e. it possesses a specific ability in healing and strengthening of bones [11,12].

Mashadi Yoga, a combination suggested by Vruddha vaidya (not mentioned in any classical texts, but found effective by the previous senior practitioners), consists of roasted Maasha, Ajmoda and Erandmoola in equal proportions. In Ayurvedic texts, black gram (Vigna mungo (L.) Hepper) beans are referred to as "Masha" and are highly recommended for boosting immunity and gaining strength and vigor of muscles and bones. According to Ayurveda, the body's bones are Vata's sites. Ajmoda (Apium graveolens L.) having Vata pacifying properties, is reported to possess the potential for the prevention of fracture-induced pain by increasing bone mass density [13]. Erandamool (Ricinus communis L.) possesses antioxidant activity. It is the herb of choice for all kinds of Vata diseases. It calms down the Kapha-Vata doshas. It is advised to treat several illnesses, including Shoola (pain) and Shotha (inflammation) [14,15]. These constituents of Mashadi yoga are also known for their Vranaropaka nature, i.e. they help in wound healing and tissue regeneration, and provide strength to affected areas. The Balya (strengthening) quality is known for providing strength to bones and muscles which is crucial in recovery.

The formulation, Asthi majja yoga was administered to support the healing process. It contains Aamalaki (Emblica officinalis), Musta (Cyperus rotundus) and Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia). These constituents are considered Tridoshashamaka (Tridosha pacifying), and Balya. The constituents of the formulation are traditionally regarded as rejuvenators in balancing Vatavyadhi by acting on Asthivaha (bone tissues) and Majjavaha (bone marrow) strotas (channels). The formulation is often administered as supportive therapy and it shows effects by enhancing the regeneration of bone tissue. The formulation is rich in calcium and balances Vata dosha.

Another part of the treatment was the Panchakarma procedure. Tikta-Ksheera-Ghrita basti, is considered rejuvenating and detoxifying in nature and plays an important role in fracture management. It includes Poorvakarma (pre-procedure), such as Abhyanga (oil massage) and Swedana (sudation) and Pradhana karma i.e. Basti [16]. In the present case, Sarvanga Abhyanga i.e. whole-body oil massage with Chandan Bala Lakshadi oil, Sarvanga swedana or whole-body steam bath, Samolina Upama/Shira for breakfast and Pradhana karma (administration of Shatavari, Guduchi Ksheerapaaka (100 ml) and Mahatiktaka ghritam (20 ml) together as Basti) for 21 consecutive days, were performed as a part of Panchakarma procedure.

Sarvanga Abhyanga i.e. whole-body massage using medicated oils (Chandan bala lakshadi oil) has various benefits such as relaxation of the body and relief from muscle fatigue and pain. It improves blood circulation, accelerates recovery from injuries and/or surgeries, and strengthens the immune system. The Chandan bala lakshadi oil possesses Guru (heavy) properties which aids in grounding and stabilization of the body and thereby accelerates the healing process. The Sheeta guna of the formulation is beneficial in the management of fracture-induced inflammation. Some of the key ingredients like Laksha are known as Vranaropaka (wound healing) and Bhagnasandhanakrita (bone healing property), which play a crucial role in accelerated recovery [17].

Sarvanga swedana or whole-body steam bath is a therapy which usually performed using a specially designed wooden chamber. The procedure involves whole body oil massage or Snehana with the application of medicated oils. The patient lies in the wooden chamber and steam is circulated within the chamber. The procedure helps in the dilation of blood vessels and thereby enhancing blood flow, alleviation of muscle pain and stiffness, relieving muscle tension and providing better sleep quality. These pre-procedures utilized in the present treatment protocol help to facilitate the action of the principal therapy [18].

The Pradhana karma was the administration of Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus), Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia) ksheerpaaka (Medicated milk) along with Mahatiktaka ghritam through the anal route (basti). Shatavari (Asparagus racemosus), is a herb traditionally used to treat various ailments. The herb possesses Madhura, Tikta, Sheeta, Guru, Snehana and Balya properties. It is advised to be administered in Vatavyadhi and especially Asthikshaya (Impairment in bone nourishment). It is considered as Rasayana (rejuvenating properties). Thus, it can promote recovery from fractures by restoring energy and providing strength to bones. The Balya property of Shatavari supports its use in fracture treatment by providing strength to the bones [19].

The herb Guduchi (Tinospora cordifolia), is considered Rasayana (rejuvenating and immunomodulatory) [20].

Ksheerpaaka formulations are advised for use in Basti. The Ksheerpaaka formulation is prepared by boiling plant parts with cow milk and water. The combination is slowly boiled until the water evaporates and during this process, the herbal constituents are transferred into the milk. The Cow milk possesses various Gunas (properties): Madhura vipaka (sweet nature post-digestion) along with Sheeta veerya (cold), Madhura rasa (sweet taste), Jeevaniya (revitalizing) and Balya (physical strength). The milk also acts as Sandhaanakara (fusion or healing of injuries or bones) in the treatment of Bhagna (Fracture) and Kshata (injury or trauma).

Mahatiktaka ghritam contains herbs possessing wound healing and anti-inflammatory properties. This formulation is Snigdha (oily) in nature which provides necessary lubrication and moisture beneficial for soothing wounds and promoting healing. The Tikta rasa (bitter taste) is known for balancing Pitta, and has cooling and detoxifying properties. Sheeta veerya (cold potency) balances heat, which helps in reducing inflammation and burning sensations at the fracture site. Kleda upasoshana (Absorption of Excessive Moisture) property expresses the maintenance of correct moisture at the fracture site [21].

The Basti administration of medicated milk and ghee, prepared with Tikta rasa herbs, is one of the unique treatment principles, mentioned in Ayurveda for bone rejuvenation. In this case, it was observed that the fracture was not healing even after three months of RTA. One month of Ayurvedic medicinal treatment also did not produce any changes on the X-ray, and after almost 6 months past the fracture, basti treatment was given and substantial callus was visible immediately in two months, which was further improved by the medicinal treatment. So, there is a high chance that this result is attributable to Basti treatment. The rationale behind this decision was the Atidesha (extrapolation) of the textual principle. Treatment procedure mentioned for the Asthi kshaya (reduction in bone tissue) was used for the delayed of non-union of fractures, where the absence of callus formation, considered similar with Asthikshaya.

In one of our previously published cases, a tibial non-union showed remarkable recovery after Tikta-Kṣhīra-Ghṛita Basti, where radiological evidence demonstrated the progression from granulation tissue to soft callus and subsequently to hard callus formation. With multiple such clinical observations now available, Tikta-Kṣhīra-Ghṛita Basti appears to hold significant promise as an adjunctive therapy in cases of delayed and non-union fractures [22]. Nevertheless, systematic research is essential to elucidate the precise pharmacological and physiological pathways underlying its observed regenerative action.

Conclusion

This case study demonstrates the significant potential of integrating Ayurvedic therapies with conventional surgical methods in managing tibia and fibula fractures. The use of Ayurvedic interventions, including Lakshadi Guggul, Mashadi Yoga, Asthi Majja Yoga, and Panchakarma, effectively accelerated fracture healing, as evidenced by improved callus formation and complete recovery. The outcome emphasizes the benefits of holistic care, combining modern and traditional approaches, in enhancing bone regeneration and patient recovery. This integrative approach can offer a promising avenue for treating complex fractures.

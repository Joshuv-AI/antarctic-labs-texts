Service quality evaluation of county-level public hospitals in Chongqing under smart healthcare
Authors: Yuantang Qiu, Chun Xiao, Jing Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study explores patients' perceptions and expectations of smart healthcare services from the perspective of patient experience.The aim is to provide insights for the development of smart healthcare services in hospitals.MethodsA cluster sampling method was used to select 10 public county-level hospitals from October to November 2021 in a municipality in southwestern China. Patient expectations and perceptions about smart healthcare services were assessed using SERVQUAL scale, which included 24 items across four dimensions: ability, application, platform, and effectiveness. The perception-expectation gap was calculated, and factors influencing this gap were analyzed. The Importance-Performance Analysis (IPA) model was then applied to evaluate the results.ResultsA total of 915 patients from outpatient and inpatient departments participated in the study. The average perception score was 3.86, while the average expectation score was 4.44, resulting in a gap of -0.58. Paired sample t-tests revealed significant differences between patients' perceptions and expectations across the 24 items (P < 0.05). IPA quadrant analysis identified 5 items in quadrant IV. A generalized linear model indicated that patients with a college degree, income between 2001 ~ 3500RMB, and income between 3501 ~ 5000RMB were associated with differences in service quality evaluation. Patients covered by urban and rural residents' medical insurance also showed variations in their evaluations.ConclusionsThe study highlights that smart healthcare services in public county-level hospitals fall short of meeting patients' expectations. It emphasizes the importance of addressing individualized medical needs and improving aspects such as cost control, system design, technology-human care balance, and operational efficiency.Keywords: County Public Hospitals, Smart Healthcare, Service quality, SERVQUAL model

Introduction

Smart healthcare refers to the utilization of advanced information technology to modernize healthcare services, offering patients convenient, efficient, and safe healthcare solutions while addressing issues of accessibility and cost [1–6]. In recent years, the integration of the Internet with healthcare industry has led to the active promotion of smart healthcare by local governments and healthcare institutions making it a key trend in the development [7]. Current research on smart healthcare primarily focuses on technological innovation [8], user acceptance [9], service models [10], and data security [11], with limited attention to patient experience and service quality.

Service quality management aimed to ensure that organizational services meet or exceed customer expectations. One prominent model for evaluating service quality is the SERVQUAL model, developed by Parasuraman et al. [12]. This model assesses service quality across five dimensions: tangibility, reliability, assurance, empathy, and responsiveness. SERVQUAL measures service quality by comparing patients' expectations with their perceptions of smart healthcare services. This scale is widely used in the healthcare industry [13–18].

Chongqing, as one of China’s major municipalities, launched a smart healthcare work plan in August 2020 with the goal of becoming a model city for smart healthcare in China. However, there are disparities in information technology development, particularly in county-level public hospitals [19, 20].

This study employes the SERVQUAL model to pinpoint deficiencies in smart healthcare development, analyze factors influencing service quality, and provide both theoretical and practical recommendations to improve patient experience and satisfaction in hospitals at similar levels, ultimately contributing to the advancement of smart healthcare.

Methods

Data collection

From October to November 2021, a total of 10 county-level public hospitals rated as smart hospitals by municipal government were selected using a whole-group stratified sampling method based on the economic development level of districts or counties in Chongqing. Subsequently, 50 patients were randomly selected from the outpatient and inpatient departments respectively of each hospital for a face-to-face survey conducted by trained surveyors to ensure accuracy and completeness in questionnaire collection.

Questionnaire design and development

This study is based on the SERVQUAL scale, focusing on reliability, assurance, responsiveness, economy, empathy, and tangibility, in conjunction with the "Evaluation Index of Chongqing Smart Hospital Demonstration Construction". The questionnaire used in this survey was developed through preliminary and consultation with experts from the Health Career Management and Information Section. The questionnaire is divided into two parts: (i) a demographic data questionnaire covering patients' gender, age, education, occupation, income, and type of medical insurance; and (ii) a patient evaluation questionnaire on the quality of smart healthcare services, consisting of four dimensions: ability, application, platform, and effectiveness [21], with a total of 24 items rated on a 5-point Likert scale. Scores of 1 to 5 correspond to "not expected/not satisfied", "not too expected/not too satisfied", "average/fair", "more expected/more satisfied ", and "very expected/very satisfied".

The Cronbach's α coefficients for the expectancy scale and the perception scale were 0.947 and 0.971, respectively, indicating high internal consistency and reliability. The KMO test values for the expectancy scale and the perception scale were 0.959 and 0.976, with p-values of Bartlett's sphericity test less than 0.001. The cumulative variance contribution rates were 62.8% and 65.6%, demonstrating good validity and meeting statistical requirements. These results suggest that the dimensions and index system in the questionnaire are reasonable. Please refer to Table 1 for more details.

Calculation of service quality (SQ) evaluation value

n represents the number of patients; Pi is the average score of patients' actual feelings about the service; Ei is the average score of patients' expectation about the service; SQ is the average score of patients' perception of the quality of smart healthcare service. A higher SQ value indicates better quality of smart healthcare service.

Importance—performance analysis

Importance-Performance Analysis(IPA), proposed by Martilla and James, is used to assess the strengths and weaknesses of a service [22]. Based on the results of expectations and perceptions, an IPA analysis map of the quality of smart healthcare services in county-level public hospitals in Chongqing is created, forming a four-quadrant region. Quadrant I represents high perception and high expectation, known as the charm region. Quadrant II represents low expectation and high perception, known as the surprise region. Quadrant III represents low perceptions and low expectations, known as the potential area. Quadrant IV represents high expectations and low perceptions, known as the improvement area. Items falling into quadrants I and II indicate that patients are satisfied with the quality of smart healthcare services and should be maintained, while items falling into quadrants III and IV need to be focused on and improved promptly [23, 24].

Statistical analysis

The questionnaire database was created using EpiData, and statistical analysis was performed using SPSS. Measurement data were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while count data were expressed as composition ratio. Single-factor analysis was performed using t-tests and ANOVA. Paired-sample t-tests were used for intergroup comparisons, and a generalized linear model was applied for multi-factor analysis. Statistically significance was set at P < 0.05.

Result

General demographic characteristics and univariate analysis

A total of 915 patients were surveyed, comprising 484 outpatients and 431 inpatients. Men made up 39.7% of the sample, while women accounted for 60.3%. The average age of the patients was 42 years. 39.2% of the patients had a junior high school education or lower, and 48.2% of the patients were unemployed or retired. The majority of patients (30.1%) reported a personal monthly income of 3501–5000 yuan. Urban employees' medical insurance (35.3%) and urban residents' medical insurance (45.6%) were the primary insurance types among the patients surveyed. Univariate analysis revealed statistically significant differences in the evaluation of the quality of smart healthcare services among patients with varying levels of education, income, and insurance types (P < 0.05). Discussion

Based on the context of smart healthcare, this study assesses smart healthcare service quality by considering patients' expectations and perceptions. It measures the service quality gap using 24 pairs of questions across four dimensions based on SERVQUAL. The study found that the service quality scores (SQ) for all 24 items were negative, suggesting a need to enhance patient experience and improve service quality. These findings align with previous studies conducted in China [16, 17] and other countries [14, 18, 25, 26].

The capability dimension reflects the level of infrastructure and technology in smart healthcare, which can enhance patient experiences. In evaluating competency services, all five metrics received favorable scores in the first and second quadrants. However, "timely technical support" received the lowest score, indicating that some patients faced technical challenges and lacked sufficient assistance from medical personnel. This issue may be attributed to factors that affect middle-aged and older adults, such as the technological divide [27], impaired vision and hearing [28], and difficulty adapting to new technologies. These challenges hinder their use of smart devices and create barriers to accessing healthcare services.

The application dimension is primarily designed to reflect the scope and specifics of smart healthcare, facilitating all aspects of patient care. "Secure payment" received the highest service quality score among the 24 items, indicating positive payment experiences that should be maintained [29, 30]. On the other hand, "Online Search" scored the lowest and fell into Quadrant III. This is due to the hospital having too many systems, leading to a serious division between different departments, complex redundancy, and interoperability issues [31]. Patients need to switch between different systems to access required information during medical consultations, which negatively impacts their overall experience. To address this issue, hospitals should enhance top-level planning, standardize system construction across departments, implement an integrated hospital information system to eliminate information silos, and ensure data interoperability. Additionally, creating a centralized platform for patients to manage appointments, consultations, payments, medication retrieval, admissions, and discharges can streamline the process and improve transparency [32]. This will reduce information asymmetry between doctors and patients, enhance trust, and boost patient satisfaction with the hospital [33].

At the platform level, patients rated "privacy and information security" the lowest, expressing their concern about privacy [34]. Different systems and platforms in hospitals share patient information, and varying management and data handling practices can impact data security, potentially leading to ethical risks [35]. To address patients' privacy concerns, it's useful to establish a secure channel for patient information files, implement robust security measures and encryption technologies to prevent data breaches, and ensure the accuracy of patient data [36]. A real-time monitoring system should be in place to verify the identity of personnel accessing patient data, preventing unauthorized access and ensuring the integrity of medical big data. An IPA analysis reveals that "informed consent and data communication" is categorized in quadrant IV, indicating low patient service quality scores. While automatic extraction of patient information from self-service machines and mobile terminals streamlines the consultation process, it may overlook providing information to patients. Prior to using self-service devices, patients should be presented with pop-up informed consent forms explaining the purpose and use of data collection to safeguard patient data. Increasing awareness of smart healthcare through interactive platforms can inform patients about the use of their data and uphold their right to informed consent, enhancing trust in the reliability and safety of smart health care.

The effectiveness dimension refers to the patient's evaluation of the outcome of smart healthcare services, including communication feedback, information access, healthcare costs, and the time and process of healthcare treatment. Patients expressed the most dissatisfaction with the economic aspects including medication cost, examination cost, room and board cost. These items fell into quadrant IV in the IPA quadrant analysis, indicating a need for improvement to enhance patients' perception of the economics of smart healthcare. Our study revealed that the patients surveyed were predominantly from the low-income group, which typically has high healthcare needs [37]. Despite health insurance coverage, patients still bear a percentage of healthcare costs, resulting in lower scores for healthcare costs. Therefor, it’s needed for the hospitals to establish Internet hospitals to provide patients with access to high-quality medical resources from home, reducing costs and overcoming time and space constraints. And it’s a good practice for the the government and medical institutions to expand telemedicine coverage in medical insurance catalogs to lower patient expenses on tests and medications [38]. Additionally, enhancing electronic medical records utilization and enabling mutual recognition of examination reports between hospitals can prevent redundant tests, saving patients time and money while enhancing their overall experience with smart healthcare services.

Univariate and generalized linear model analyses revealed that patients with varying levels of education, income, and type of medical insurance exhibit distinct preferences for smart healthcare services. This finding aligns with other study [35] and can be attributed to the diverse healthcare needs and expectations among patients. For instance, individuals with full health insurance and higher income levels may experience less socioeconomic pressure, while those with higher education levels may possess greater capacity to learn and adapt to smart healthcare technologies [20]. However, despite these differences, there are limitations in the ability of smart healthcare services to fully meet the needs of patients, resulting in varying levels of satisfaction among patients with different demographic profiles.

Limitations

The limitations of this study include the selection of only 10 county-level public hospitals in Chongqing, which may not fully represent all districts and counties under its jurisdiction. This could impact the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, factors such as healthcare resource allocation, management systems, and other variables that could influence the quality of smart healthcare services were not considered, limiting the interpretation and applicability of the results.

Conclusion

The rapid advancement of smart healthcare has improved patient access and reduced waiting times. However, patient satisfaction with costs, system design, and humanistic care remains low. Patients have diverse expectations and experiences with smart healthcare. It is crucial to address their unique needs, leverage technology for personalized services, and consistently enhance quality. Age-appropriate systems, simplified processes, and self-help options are essential. Family accounts and humanistic support can enhance the patient experience. System design, integration, and data security are important to be focused in order to improve patients’ evaluation on medical service. Internet hospitals and telemedicine services may provide quality care and cost savings. Improved communication between doctors and patients is key to enhancing the healthcare experience.

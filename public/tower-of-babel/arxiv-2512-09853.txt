Abstract
5 establish non-asymptotic high-probability bounds for general deep feedforward neural network (with rectified linear unit activation function) estimators, with 5 achieving a suboptimal convergence rate for fully connected feedforward networks. The authors suggest that improved approximation of fully connected networks could yield sharper versions of 5 without altering the theoretical framework. By deriving approximation bounds specifically for a narrower fully connected deep neural network, this note demonstrates that 5 can be improved to achieve an optimal rate (up to a logarithmic factor). Furthermore, this note briefly shows that deep neural network estimators can mitigate the curse of dimensionality for functions with compositional structure and functions defined on manifolds.



Keywords:
Deep Neural Networks, Approximation, Rectified Linear Unit, Optimal Estimation.

2.2
Our approach

The optimality of
5
indicates that bounds in Theorem
1
and
18
are sharp. As mentioned above, the main issue arises from the steps taken to convert the approximation neural network in
18
into a fully connected architecture.

On the one hand, the network in
18
is wide that converting it into a fully connected network inevitably and unnecessarily squares the total number of weights. On the other hand, the conversion method used in
5
is conservative. They apply
5
to convert the approximation network into a fully connected one. Their method unnecessarily increases
H_{\mathrm{MLP}}
to be asymptotically equivalent to
W
of the approximation network.

To address this issue, we modify
18
into the following theorem. In the first part, we reconstruct the network in
18
into a narrower architecture; in the second part, we apply Lemma
8
in the Appendix to convert it into a fully connected network.

Theorem 3
.

For any
\beta,d\in\mathbb{N_{+}}
and
\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}\in(0,1)
,

1.

there is a feedforward ReLU network architecture that
it can approximate any function in
\mathcal{W}^{\beta,\infty}([-1,1]^{d})
with approximation error less than
\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}
, with depth
L\leq c(ln(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}})+1)
, and width
H\leq c\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}^{-\frac{d}{2\beta}}

2.

This approximation network can be converted into a fully connected network without significantly change

and

c

W_{\mathrm{MLP}}\leq c\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}^{-\frac{d}{\beta}}(\ln(1/\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}})+1)

.

Remark 1
.

In
18

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}^{-\frac{d}{\beta}})

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}^{-\frac{d}{\beta}})

\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_{\mathrm{DNN}}^{-\frac{2d}{\beta}}L)

2

Remark 2
.

A narrower construction alone is not sufficient to solve the problem. Specifically,

Since we maintain the same number of weights as in

The proof is provided in Appendix

Although the overall strategy retains the partition of unity and Taylor expansion approach, we introduce significant structural refinements to reduce the network width while maintaining the same order of the coefficients.

Conclusion

By modifying only the approximation component of the proof in
5
, this note establishes that the convergence rate of MLP estimators is indeed optimal (up to a logarithmic factor) in the sense of
16
. To this end, this note extends the results of
18
. Instead of focusing on approximation results tied to network size, we demonstrate that a narrower network of comparable size can achieve the same approximation rate. This result can be of independent interest. One can apply our narrower network construction to convert other neural architectures into fully connected networks without substantially increasing the total number of parameters.

In addition, we establish convergence rates for functions with compositional structures and for functions defined on low-dimensional manifolds. In both cases, consistent with previous literature, we show that deep neural networks can effectively circumvent the curse of dimensionality. We encourage future empirical work to justify the use of deep learning based on these two reasons.

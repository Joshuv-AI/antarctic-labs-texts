A minimum problem associated with scalar Ginzburg-Landau equation and free boundary
Yuwei Hu 1, Jun Zheng 1,2, Leandro S. Tavares 3

Abstract
Let N > 2 N>2 , p ∈ ( 2 ​ N N + 2 , + ∞ ) p\in\left(\frac{2N}{N+2},+\infty\right) , and Ω \Omega be an open bounded domain in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . Introduction

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}(N>2)
and
p\in\left(\frac{2N}{N+2},+\infty\right)
. Let
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
and
\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
be constants. Given
g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
g\geq 0
on
\partial\Omega
, we consider the following minimum problem

\mathcal{J}(u){=}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+\lambda_{1}\left(1-(u^{+})^{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda_{2}u^{+}\right)\text{d}x\rightarrow\text{min}

(1)

over the set
\mathcal{K}:=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega);\ u-g\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\right\}
.

The minimum problem of the type of (
1
) is known as the free boundary problem with free boundary
\Gamma^{+}:=\left(\partial\{x\in\Omega;u(x)>0\}\right)\cap\Omega
, and has a wide range of applications in various fields. For instance, the minimum problem of (
1
)
with
\lambda_{1}=0
refers to the classical obstacle problem, which can be used to describe the problems of equilibrium of elastic membranes, fluid filtration in porous media, and control of temperature (see
[
9
]
), while the minimum problem (
1
) with
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
corresponds to the non-homogeneous scalar Ginzburg-Landau equation,
which arises in the problem of low-temperature superconductivity and has many applications in physics (see
[
1
,
21
]
). Taking a mathematical perspective into consideration, it has been pointed out in the work by Ma
[
18
]
that the problem represented by equation (
1
) has a significant connection with the theoretical framework of minimal surface theory.

In the past few decades, great efforts have been devoted to investigating the existence and regularities of minimizers of
\mathcal{J}(u)
(or solutions to the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equations) when
\lambda_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{2}>0
, see for instance
[
6
,
7
,
15
,
25
]
. For example in
[
6
]
, the authors considered an obstacle problem for a quasilinear elliptic equation of
p
-Laplacian type. It was obtained that, under certain smoothness conditions on the obstacle function, the solution to the corresponding obstacle problem has interior Hölder continuous derivatives. In
[
15
]
, a complete description of the regularity was provided for a family of heterogeneous, two-phase variational free boundary problems, which are driven by nonlinear, degenerate elliptic operators. Such study considered a wide class of problems including, for example, heterogeneous jets and cavities problems of Prandtl-Batchelor type, singular degenerate elliptic equations, and obstacle type systems. Some of the regularity results obtained in
[
15
]
were extended to almost minimizers in
[
7
]
for functionals governed by
A
-Laplacian with Hölder continuous coefficients.
In the reference
[
25
]
, a free boundary problem was studied in the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces setting, where the operator satisfies structural conditions of Tolksdorf’s type and the nonlinearities may exhibit subcritical growth. Further geometric properties of the free boundary can be found in
[
8
,
16
,
23
]
.
In particular, it was proved in
[
3
,
14
]
that the free boundary has finite (
N-1
)-dimensional Hausdorff measure when
\lambda_{1}=0
,
\lambda_{2}>0
, and
p\in[2,+\infty)
. This result was included in
[
5
]
under the framework of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.
Nevertheless, property of the free boundary in the minimum problem of the type of (
1
) with
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
is less studied, except the work presented in
[
18
]
, where finite (
N-1
)-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the free boundary was proved in the non-zero obstacle problem for the Ginzburg-Landau equation when
\lambda_{1}>0
,
\lambda_{2}>0
, and
p=2
.

In this paper, we study the minimum problem (
1
) with
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
,
\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p
in a general case.
It is worth noting that, compared with the work in the existing literature, both
the higher order of
u^{+}
and nonlinearity of the
p
-Laplacian bring more intricacies when proving the existence and regularity of minimizers, as well as Hausdorff measure for the free boundary. This puts forward a constraint on
p
, namely, only the case of
p\in\left(\frac{2N}{N+2},+\infty\right)
is considered in this paper. Under this condition on
p
, we prove existence, non-negativity, and regularity of minimizers for the functional
\mathcal{J}(u)
with
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
and
\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
. In addition, we establish non-degeneracy of minimizers near the free boundary and show that there exists at least one non-negative minimizer, for which the corresponding free boundary has finite (
N-1
)-dimensional Hausdorff measure when
\lambda_{2}>0
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Notions and some technical lemmas used in this paper are presented in Section
2
.
The existence, non-negativity, and uniform
L^{\infty}
-boundedness for the minimizers of the functional
\mathcal{J}(u)
are proved in Section
3
. Local
C^{1,\alpha}
-continuity for the minimizers and optimal growth of minimizers near the free boundary are established in Section
4
and Section
5
, respectively. Under the further assumption that
\lambda_{2}>0
, non-degeneracy of minimizers near the free boundary is proved in Section
6
, followed which, a corresponding penalized problem is studied in Section
7
. Finally, for
\lambda_{2}>0
, we show in Section
8
that there exists at least one minimizer with finite (
N-1
)-dimensional Hausdorff measure for the corresponding free boundary.

We have the following result.

Theorem 1

The functional of
\mathcal{J}(u)
admits at least one minimizer over the set
\mathcal{K}
.
Moreover, every minimizer over the set
\mathcal{K}
is non-negative a.e. in
\Omega

. Furthermore, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
N
,

,
\lambda_{1}
,
|\lambda_{2}|
,
\|g\|_{W^{1.p}(\Omega)}
,
\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
,
and the diameter of
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{W^{1.p}(\Omega)}\leq C

holds true for all minimizers, still denoted by

\mathcal{J}(u)
.

Proof 3.2
.

The proof consists of four steps, which can be proceeded in a similar way as in

Step 1: proof of the existence of a minimizer.
We firstly claim that
I_{0}:=\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{J}(v)>-\infty
.
Indeed, for

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(v)\geq\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{p}|\nabla v|^{p}+\lambda_{1}\left(1-(v^{+})^{2}\right)^{2}-|\lambda_{2}v|\right)\text{d}x.

(2)

Since
\lambda_{1}\geq 0

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\left(1-(v^{+})^{2}\right)^{2}\geq 0.

(3)

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\lambda_{2}v|\text{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|v-g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|\nabla v-\nabla g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|g\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\text{d}x+C(\varepsilon,N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)+C(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)},

(4)

where in the last inequality we used the Young’s inequality with
\varepsilon>0
.

By virtue of (
2
), (
3
), and (
3.2
), and choosing a suitable
\varepsilon

Consider a minimizing sequence

and

for all

By (

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p}|\nabla v_{j}|^{p}\text{d}x=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(v_{j})-\int_{\Omega}\left(\lambda_{1}\left(1-(v_{j}^{+})^{2}\right)^{2}+\lambda_{2}v_{j}^{+}\right)\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(v_{j})+|\lambda_{2}|\int_{\Omega}|v_{j}|\text{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle I_{0}+1+C\left(N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega\right)\|g\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}+C(\varepsilon,N,p,|\lambda_{2}|,\Omega)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{j}|^{p}\text{d}x.

A suitable choice of

Meanwhile, by the Sobolev embedding inequality we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{p}|v|^{p}\text{d}x\leq

\displaystyle C(p)\int_{\Omega}\left(|v-g|^{p}+|g|^{p}\right)\text{d}x

\displaystyle C(N,p,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v-\nabla g|^{p}\text{d}x+C(p)\int_{\Omega}|g|^{p}\text{d}x

\displaystyle C(N,p,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{p}\text{d}x+C(N,p,\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{p}\text{d}x+C(p)\int_{\Omega}|g|^{p}\text{d}x,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(1-(u^{+})^{2}\right)^{2}\text{d}x\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\left(1-(v_{j}^{+})^{2}\right)^{2}\text{d}x.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u^{+}\text{d}x\leq\liminf_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}v_{j}^{+}\text{d}x.

(9)

6

{\operatorname{div}~\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=4\lambda_{1}\left(-u+u^{3}\right)+\lambda_{2}\ \text{in}~\{u>0\}.}

Now, for

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}~\left(C_{2}^{p-2}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p-2}|x-y|^{\frac{p-2}{p-1}}\cdot C_{2}\frac{p}{p-1}|x-y|^{\frac{2-p}{p-1}}(x-y)\right)

\displaystyle C_{2}^{p-1}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\operatorname{div}~(x-y)

\displaystyle NC_{2}^{p-1}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\ \text{in}\ B_{r}(y).

We deduce by (

\displaystyle{\operatorname{div}~\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)}=4\lambda_{1}{\left(-u+u^{3}\right)+\lambda_{2}}\ {\text{in}\ B_{r}(y)\cap\{u>0\}}.

Noting that

Mixed Segre zeta functions and their log-concavity
Yairon Cid-Ruiz

Abstract
. We introduce and study the mixed Segre zeta function of a sequence of homogeneous ideals in a polynomial ring. This function is a power series encoding information about the mixed Segre classes obtained by extending the ideals to projective spaces of arbitrarily large dimension. Our work generalizes and unifies results by Kleiman and Thorup on mixed Segre classes and by Aluffi on Segre zeta functions. We prove that this power series is rational, with poles corresponding to the degrees of the generators of the ideals. We also show that the mixed Segre zeta function only depends on the integral closure of the ideals. Finally, we prove that the homogenization of the numerator of a modification of the mixed Segre zeta function is denormalized Lorentzian in the sense of Brändén and Huh.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{k}
be an arbitrary field,
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring, and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\subset R
be homogeneous ideals in
R
.
Denote by
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
the closed subschemes of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
defined by the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\subset R
and by
Z\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
the closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
defined by the product ideal
I_{1}\cdots I_{m}\subset R
.
In their foundational study of
mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities
, Kleiman and Thorup
[
17
]
introduced the notion of
mixed Segre classes
.
These classes yield a mixed generalization of the classical
Segre classes
, which play a key role in Fulton–MacPherson’s intersection theory.
For each
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the
mixed Segre class

s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})\;\in\;A_{n-i_{1}-\cdots-i_{m}}(Z)

of
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
of type
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})
.
For more details, see
Section 2
.

Motivated by the
Segre zeta function
of Aluffi
[
3
]
, we introduce the
mixed Segre zeta function
.
The mixed Segre zeta function of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
is a formal power series with integer coefficients

\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\;=\;\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}\,a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[\![t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]\!].

This power series is characterized by the fact that, for all
N\geq n
and
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}\leq N
, the class

a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}H^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}}\smallfrown\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right]\;\in\;A_{N-i_{1}-\cdots-i_{m}}\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right)

equals the push-forward to
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
of the mixed Segre class
s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}(Z_{1}^{N},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N};{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})
;
here
H
denotes the class of a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
and
Z_{i}^{N}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
is the closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
defined by the extension of the ideal
I_{i}\subset R
to the polynomial ring
\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{N}]\supseteq R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Therefore, the mixed Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
can be viewed as the generating function that encodes the push-forwards of the corresponding mixed Segre classes obtained after extending the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
to arbitrarily large polynomial rings.
Since mixed Segre classes can be seen as a refinement of Segre classes, mixed Segre zeta functions can likewise be regarded as a refinement of Segre zeta functions.
It is not difficult to show the existence of the mixed Segre zeta function (see
Section 4
).
Our first main result shows that the mixed Segre zeta function is a
rational
power series.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 4.3
)
.

Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\subset R
be homogeneous ideals in
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
For each
1\leq i\leq m
, let
f_{i,0},f_{i,1},\dotsc,f_{i,r_{i}}\in R
be homogeneous generators of
I_{i}
, with the degree of
f_{i,j}
equal to
d_{i,j}=\deg\left(f_{i,j}\right)
.
Then we can write

\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\;\;=\;\;\frac{P(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})}{\;\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(1+d_{i,0}t_{i}\right)\left(1+d_{i,1}t_{i}\right)\cdots\left(1+d_{i,r_{i}}t_{i}\right)\;}

where
P(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{N}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients.

Our proof of
Theorem A
is inspired by Aluffi’s proof of the rationality of Segre zeta functions
[
3
]
.
The main technical tool is the analysis of how mixed Segre classes pull-back under certain rational maps (see
Theorem 3.8
).
This analysis, in turn, relies heavily on a blow-up formula for mixed Segre classes established in
Theorem 2.7
.

We show that mixed Segre zeta functions have several interesting properties.
Due to
Section 4
, we obtain that the mixed Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is determined by the degrees of the generators of the ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
and the mixed Segre classes
s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}(Z_{1}^{N},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N};{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})
for
N
high enough (explicitly, for
N\geq m+\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}
).
It turns out that in general not all the numbers
-1/\deg(f_{i,j})
appear as poles of
\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
(even if each set
f_{i,0},f_{i,1},\dotsc,f_{i,r_{i}}
gives a minimal set of generators of each ideal
I_{i}
).
Indeed, by
Section 4
, mixed Segre zeta functions only depend on the integral closure of the ideals.
More precisely, we have the equality

\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\;=\;\zeta_{\overline{I_{1}},\ldots,\overline{I_{m}}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}),

where
\overline{I_{i}}\subset R
denotes the integral closure of
I_{i}
.
In
Section 4
, we provide a mixed formula that expresses the Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1}\cdots I_{m}}(t)
of the product ideal
I_{1}\cdots I_{m}\subset R
in terms of the mixed Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
.
This mixed formula leads to a number of explicit formulas for the Segre zeta function of certain ideals (see
Section 6
,
Section 6
and
Section 6
).

The second part of this paper investigates the log-concave behavior exhibited by mixed Segre zeta functions.
Our main result in this direction is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 5.3
)
.

Assume that
\mathbb{k}
is an algebraically closed field.
Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\subset R
be homogeneous ideals in
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Let
f_{i,0},f_{i,1},\dotsc,f_{i,r_{i}}\in R
be homogeneous generators of
I_{i}
with
d_{i,j}=\deg\left(f_{i,j}\right)
.
Define the polynomial
Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
by the identity

1-\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\;=\;\frac{Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})}{\;\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(1+d_{i,0}t_{i}\right)\left(1+d_{i,1}t_{i}\right)\cdots\left(1+d_{i,r_{i}}t_{i}\right)\;}.

Then the homogenization of
Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is a denormalized Lorentzian polynomial.

This result yields a systematic method to produce Lorentzian polynomials from arbitrary homogeneous ideals in a polynomial ring. It should mentioned that another paper of Aluffi
[
4
]
shows that the homogenization of the numerator of the power series
1-\zeta_{I}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
associated to a multihomogeneous ideal
I
is a
covolume polynomial
(a dual notion to that of a
volume polynomial
; see
[
6
, §4.2]
);
in this latter work,
I
is a multihomogeneous ideal defining a closed subscheme of a product of projective spaces
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\ell_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\ell_{m}}
and
\zeta_{I}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is the corresponding Segre zeta function associated to
I
.
In
Theorem B
, we take a different approach: instead of considering a single multihomogeneous ideal
I
and its Segre zeta function, we study a sequence of homogeneous ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
and their mixed Segre zeta function.
Owing to technical and favorable properties of mixed Segre classes, this change of perspective enables us to derive (the normalization of the homogenization of) the polynomial
Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
in
Theorem B
from a volume polynomial (see the proofs of
Theorem 5.3
and
Section 5
).
Due to a result of Brändén and Huh
[
6
, Theorem 4.16]
, volume polynomials are always Lorentzian.
In contrast, covolume polynomials may fail to be Lorentzian, even after normalization (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Example 2.7]
).
Nevertheless, Aluffi
[
4
, Introduction]
conjectures that the homogenization of the numerator of
1-\zeta_{I}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is denormalized Lorentzian for any multihomogeneous ideal
I
defining a closed subscheme of a product of projective spaces
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\ell_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{\ell_{m}}
.

Outline.

In

.

.

2.
Mixed Segre classes and blow-up formulas for them

In this section, we extend the mixed Segre classes introduced by Kleiman and Thorup
[
17
]
to the case of more than two closed subschemes.
The respective extension of their joint blow-up construction was made in
[
7
]
.
The following setup is used throughout this section.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
X
be a scheme of finite type over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
n=\dim(X)
be the dimension of
X
.
Let
m\geq 1
be a positive integer and
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}\subset X

.
For each
1\leq i\leq m

\mathcal{J}_{i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
be the ideal sheaf of

.
Let
\iota:Z\hookrightarrow X
be the closed subscheme defined by the product ideal sheaf
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{1}\cdots\mathcal{J}_{m}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Let
\widehat{X}:=\fSpec_{X}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}[t]\right)=X\times_{\text{Spec}(\mathbb{k})}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}
be the relative affine line over

.
We view

as a closed subscheme embedded in
\widehat{X}
as the principal divisor

.
Let
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}:={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}\big(\widehat{X}\big)
be the joint blow-up of

along the closed subschemes

.
This scheme can be obtained by successively blowing-up

along

.
It arises from the

-graded quasi-coherent

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\;=\;\fMProj_{\widehat{X}}\left(\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}}\big((\mathcal{J}_{1},t),\ldots,(\mathcal{J}_{m},t)\big)\right).

Let
\widehat{b}:\widehat{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}\big(\widehat{X}\big)\rightarrow\widehat{X}
be the natural projection.
To simplify notation, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, set
\mathcal{K}_{i}:=\left(\mathcal{J}_{i},t\right)\subset\mathcal{O}_{\widehat{X}}
.
For each

, let

be the

The main objects of interest in this section are the following remarkable constructions by Kleiman and Thorup
[
17
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For any closed subscheme
W\subset X
, we say that the
Kleiman–Thorup transform
of

is given by

P_{W}^{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}(X)\;:=\;\widehat{\mathcal{B}}\times_{\widehat{X}}W,

the restriction of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
to the preimage of

.
In the special cases

and

P:=P_{Z}\;:=\;P_{Z}^{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}(X)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad P_{X}\;:=\;P_{X}^{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}(X).

Let

For each

of

X

s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};X\right)\;:=\;p_{*}\left(c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{1})\right)^{i_{1}}c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{2})\right)^{i_{2}}\cdots c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{m})\right)^{i_{m}}\smallfrown\left[P\right]\right)\;\in\;A_{*}(Z).

The

of

in

\displaystyle s\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};X\right)\;

\displaystyle:=\;\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in\mathbb{N}}s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};X\right)\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}

\displaystyle=\;p_{*}\left(\frac{1}{\big(1-c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{1})\right)t_{1}\big)\,\cdots\,\big(1-c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{P}({\mathbf{e}}_{m})\right)t_{m}\big)}\,\smallfrown\,[P]\right).

Of course, if

in

(see

10

Moreover, we can see

as a formal polynomial in the variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
and with coefficients in
A_{*}(Z)
.
By an abuse of notation, we write

s\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};X\right)\;\in\;A_{*}(Z)[{\mathbf{t}}]\;=\;A_{*}(Z)[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}].

We regard
A_{*}(Z)[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
as a group with the operation given by the addition of the formal polynomials in
A_{*}(Z)[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
.

Notation 2.3
.

We also consider the joint blow-up

\mathcal{B}\;:=\;{\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}\left(X\right)\;=\;\fMProj_{X}\left(\mathscr{R}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\big(\mathcal{J}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{J}_{m}\big)\right)

Let
E_{i}:=V\left(\mathcal{J}_{i}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}\right)
be the pull-back to

of the exceptional divisor of the blow-up

of

.
Notice that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(-E_{i})=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbf{e}}_{i})

We have a natural isomorphism

Remark 2.4
.

We can check that
\dim\left(P_{X}\right)=n
,
\dim\left(P_{Z}\right)=n

\dim\left({\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}}\big(X\big)\right)\leq n

We always have

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that
X
is equidimensional.
Then we have the following equalities

\displaystyle\big[P_{X}\big]\;=\;\big[P_{Z}\big]+\big[\mathcal{B}\big]_{n},\quad\big[P_{Z}\big]\;=\;\big[P_{X}\big]^{\pi^{-1}\left(Z\right)}\quad\text{and}\quad\big[\mathcal{B}\big]_{n}\;=\;\big[P_{X}\big]^{\pi^{-1}\left(X\setminus Z\right)}

Proof.

By

and

.

Theorem 2.7

(Blow-up formula)

Assume

Then the statements below hold:

(i)

Modulo rational equivalence on

Let

When

,

The rational map

\phi_{N}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n},\qquad\left(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}:x_{n+1}:\cdots:x_{N}\right)\mapsto\left(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}\right)

Let

Then we can check that the projection

For instance, we have the compatibility isomorphism

In terms of

, the join operation introduced in

acts in the simplest possible way.
More precisely, for any formal polynomial

C({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\\

C({\mathbf{t}})\,\vee\;B_{\phi_{N}}\;=\;\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{m}\\
0\leq k\leq n\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha,k}\,t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}H^{k}\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right]\;\;\in\;\;A^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})\left[{\mathbf{t}}\right]\;\cong\;\frac{\mathbb{Z}\left[H,{\mathbf{t}}\right]}{\left(H^{N+1}\right)}

where

and

The existence of

follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4

(Existence of mixed Segre zeta functions)
.

For any
M>N\geq n
and

{\iota_{N}}_{*}\left(s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\left(Z_{1}^{N},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right)\right)\;=\;a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\,H^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}}\,\smallfrown\,\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right]

and

{\iota_{M}}_{*}\left(s^{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\left(Z_{1}^{M},\ldots,Z_{m}^{M};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{M}\right)\right)\;=\;b_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\,H^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}}\,\smallfrown\,\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{M}\right],

then
a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}=b_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}

Proof.

We may assume that
M=N+1
.
We see
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}
as the divisor on
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N+1}
determined by the variable

in

Since each ideal

does not involve the variable

By utilizing

H\,\cdot\,{\iota_{N+1}}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1}^{N+1},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N+1};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N+1}\right)\right)\;=\;{\iota_{N}}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1}^{N},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right)\right).

Given a formal power series
F({\mathbf{t}})=F(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{m}}c_{\alpha}t_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}\in\mathbb{Z}[\![t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]\!]

of terms with total degree
\leq k
.
Our main application of
Theorem 3.8
is given in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

If

, then the following statements hold for all
N\geq n
:

(i)

In
A^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
, we have the equality

{\iota_{N}}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1}^{N},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N}\right)\right)\;=\;\frac{\Big(\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,H\,t_{i}\right)\cdot{\iota}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)\Big)\;\vee\;B_{\phi_{N}}}{\;\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,H\,t_{i}\right)\;}.

(ii)

In
\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]

{\rm T}_{N}\left(\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}({\mathbf{t}})\right)\;=\;{\rm T}_{N}\left(\frac{{\rm T}_{n}\Big(\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,t_{i}\right)\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}({\mathbf{t}})\Big)}{\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,t_{i}\right)}\right).

Proof.

(i) Since
\dim(B_{\phi_{N}})=N-n-1
and

, we get
\dim(B_{\phi_{N}})<N-m-\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}

Therefore

applied to

{\iota_{N}}_{*}\left(s(Z_{1}^{N},\ldots,Z_{m}^{N};{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})\right)\;=\;s_{\mathbf{t}}(\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{N})\,\smallfrown\,\left(\Big(c_{\mathbf{t}}\left(\underline{\mathcal{E}}\right)\,\smallfrown\,{\iota}_{*}\left(s(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})\right)\Big)\,\vee\,B_{\phi_{N}}\right).

Furthermore, notice that

s_{\mathbf{t}}(\underline{\mathcal{E}}^{N})\;=\;\frac{1}{\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,H\,t_{i}\right)}\;\in\;A^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{N})[{\mathbf{t}}]\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{\mathbf{t}}(\underline{\mathcal{E}})\;=\;\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,H\,t_{i}\right)\;\in\;A^{*}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})[{\mathbf{t}}].

So the result of this part follows.

(ii) This part follows from part (i) and the existence of the mixed Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}({\mathbf{t}})
(see
Section 4
).
∎

Following Fulton

Denote by

Lemma 4.6
.

We have that

\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,H\,t_{i}\right)\cdot{\iota}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)\;\in\;A_{*}^{\geq}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]

Proof.

Let
\underline{Z}=Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m}

Consider the quotient bundle

∎

Finally, we are ready to prove

Proof of

The next proposition yields a mixed formula relating the mixed Segre zeta function of the ideals

(Mixed formula)

The result follows from

Another interesting result about mixed Segre zeta functions is that they only depend on the integral closure of the ideals

(Invariance under integral dependence)
.

Consider the closed subschemes

Let
\overline{E_{i}}
be the pull-back to

and

5.
Log-concavity of mixed Segre zeta functions

In this section, we explore a surprising log-concavity behavior that mixed Segre zeta functions have.
Here we show that, for arbitrary homogeneous ideals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
, the homogenization of the numerator of the modified mixed Segre zeta function
1-\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is denormalized Lorentzian.

We first recall the notion of Lorentzian polynomials by Brändén and Huh
[
6
]
.
Let
h(t_{1},\dots,t_{m})
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
in
\mathbb{R}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
6
]
)
.

The homogeneous polynomial
h
is called
Lorentzian
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

The coefficients of
h
are nonnegative.

(ii)

The support of
h
is M-convex.

(iii)

The quadratic form
\partial_{t_{i_{1}}}\partial_{t_{i_{2}}}\cdots\partial_{t_{i_{e}}}(h)
has at most one positive eigenvalue for any
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{e}\leq m
where
e=d-2
.

There are several linear operators discussed in
[
6
]
that preserve the Lorentzian property. In particular, the normalization operator

N\left(\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\;:=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{a_{\mathbf{n}}}{{\mathbf{n}}!}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\qquad\text{ where }\qquad{\mathbf{n}}!\;:=\;n_{1}!\cdots n_{m}!,

preserves the Lorentzian property
[
6
, Corollary 3.7]
.
A (not necessarily Lorentzian) polynomial
h\in\mathbb{R}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]
is called
denormalized Lorentzian
if its normalization
N(h)\in\mathbb{R}[{\mathbf{t}}]
is a Lorentzian polynomial.

The next proposition is our main technical tool to show the Lorentzian property of a polynomial.
This result yields a large class of Lorentzian polynomials by considering the total Chern classes of a sequence of globally generated vector bundles on an irreducible variety.
Similar results have appeared in
[
4
, Proposition 3.9]
and in
[
5
, §9]
.

\displaystyle\;=\;f_{*}\left(\sum_{0\leq i_{j}\leq r_{j}}\left(p_{1}^{*}H_{1}\right)^{r_{1}-i_{1}}\cdots\left(p_{m}^{*}H_{m}\right)^{r_{m}-i_{m}}\,c_{i_{1}}\left(p^{*}(\mathcal{Q}_{1})\right)\cdots c_{i_{m}}\left(p^{*}(\mathcal{Q}_{m})\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathscr{P}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;f_{*}\left(\sum_{0\leq i_{j}\leq r_{j}}\left(f^{*}\pi_{1}^{*}H_{1}\right)^{r_{1}-i_{1}}\cdots\left(f^{*}\pi_{m}^{*}H_{m}\right)^{r_{m}-i_{m}}\,p^{*}\Big(c_{i_{1}}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right)\cdots c_{i_{m}}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{m}\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathscr{X}\right]\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{0\leq i_{j}\leq r_{j}}\left(\pi_{1}^{*}H_{1}\right)^{r_{1}-i_{1}}\cdots\left(\pi_{m}^{*}H_{m}\right)^{r_{m}-i_{m}}\,\pi^{*}\left(q_{*}\Big(c_{i_{1}}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{1}\right)\cdots c_{i_{m}}\left(\mathcal{Q}_{m}\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathscr{X}\right]\Big)\right).

Hence by assumption we obtain

f_{*}\left(\left[\mathscr{K}\right]\right)\;=\;\sum_{0\leq i_{j}\leq r_{j}}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\left(\pi_{1}^{*}H_{1}\right)^{r_{1}-i_{1}}\cdots\left(\pi_{m}^{*}H_{m}\right)^{r_{m}-i_{m}}\,\left(\pi^{*}H\right)^{n-N+(i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m})}\,\smallfrown\,\left[{\mathbb{P}}\right].

Let
h=\pi^{*}H
,
h_{i}=\pi_{i}^{*}H_{i}
, and
d=\dim(\mathscr{K})=N+(\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{m})-(r_{1}+\cdots+r_{m})
.
Consider the irreducible subvariety
f(\mathscr{K})\subset{\mathbb{P}}
and write
f_{*}\left(\left[\mathscr{K}\right]\right)=e\left[f\left(\mathscr{K}\right)\right]
for some nonnegative integer
e\geq 0
.
By
[
6
, Theorem 4.16]
, the volume polynomial

V({\mathbf{t}})\;:=\;\int\left(ht_{0}+h_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+h_{m}t_{m}\right)^{d}\,\smallfrown\,\left[f(\mathscr{K})\right]\;\;\in\;\;\mathbb{N}[t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}]

is Lorentzian.
We now expand to obtain the following equalities

\displaystyle eV({\mathbf{t}})

\displaystyle\;=\;\int\left(ht_{0}+h_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+h_{m}t_{m}\right)^{d}\,\smallfrown\,f_{*}\left(\left[\mathscr{K}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\int\sum_{i_{0}+i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d}\frac{d!}{i_{0}!i_{1}!\cdots i_{m}!}\,h^{i_{0}}h_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots h_{m}^{i_{m}}\,t_{0}^{i_{0}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}\,\smallfrown\,f_{*}\left(\left[\mathscr{K}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;d!\sum_{i_{0}+i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=d}\frac{1}{i_{0}!i_{1}!\cdots i_{m}!}\,a_{i_{1}+r_{1}-\ell_{1},\,\ldots,\,i_{m}+r_{m}-\ell_{m}}\,\,t_{0}^{i_{0}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}.

As taking derivatives preserves Lorentzian polynomials, it follows that the following polynomial

\frac{e}{d!}\partial_{t_{1}}^{\ell_{1}-r_{1}}\cdots\partial_{t_{m}}^{\ell_{m}-r_{m}}\left(V({\mathbf{t}})\right)\;=\;\sum_{i_{0}+i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}=N}\frac{1}{i_{0}!i_{1}!\cdots i_{m}!}\,a_{i_{1},\,\ldots,\,i_{m}}\,\,t_{0}^{i_{0}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}

is also Lorentzian.
This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

The following theorem is the main result of this section.
It shows that the homogenization of the numerator of
1-\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is denormalized Lorentzian.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}\subset R
be homogeneous ideals in
R
.
Let
f_{i,0},f_{i,1},\dotsc,f_{i,r_{i}}\in R
be homogeneous generators of
I_{i}
with
d_{i,j}=\deg\left(f_{i,j}\right)
.
Define the polynomial
Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
by the identity

1-\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\;=\;\frac{Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})}{\;\prod_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(1+d_{i,0}t_{i}\right)\left(1+d_{i,1}t_{i}\right)\cdots\left(1+d_{i,r_{i}}t_{i}\right)\;}.

Then the homogenization of
Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
is a denormalized Lorentzian polynomial.

Proof.

We may assume
n\geq m+\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}
.
By
Section 4
, we obtain

\displaystyle Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})

\displaystyle\;=\;\prod_{i,j}(1+d_{i,j}t_{i})-P(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})

\displaystyle\;=\;T_{n}\left(\prod_{i,j}(1+d_{i,j}t_{i})\,\left(1-\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\right)\right)

where
P({\mathbf{t}})={\rm T}_{n}\big(\prod_{i,j}\left(1+d_{i,j}\,t_{i}\right)\zeta_{I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}}({\mathbf{t}})\big)
is the numerator of
\zeta_{I_{1}\cdots I_{m}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
in
Theorem 4.3
.
Write
Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})=\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}\leq n}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}
.
Then notice that we get

c_{\mathbf{t}}(\underline{\mathcal{E}})\,\smallfrown\,\big(\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right]-{\iota}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)\big)\;=\;\sum_{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}\leq n}a_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\,t_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{i_{m}}\,H^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}}\,\smallfrown\,\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right];

here we continue using the notation introduced in
Section 4
.
Due to
Theorem 2.7
and the projection and Whitney sum formulas, we obtain

\displaystyle c_{\mathbf{t}}(\underline{\mathcal{E}})\,\smallfrown\,\big(\left[{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right]-{\iota}_{*}\left(s\left(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{m};\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)\big)

\displaystyle\;=\;b_{*}\left(\frac{c_{t_{1}}(b^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{1}))\cdots c_{t_{m}}(b^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{m}))}{c_{t_{1}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}))\cdots c_{t_{m}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(-{\mathbf{e}}_{m}))}\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;b_{*}\Big(c_{t_{1}}(\mathcal{Q}_{1})\cdots c_{t_{m}}(\mathcal{Q}_{m})\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\Big)

where
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is equal to the quotient bundle
b^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{i})/\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(-{\mathbf{e}}_{i})
on
\mathcal{B}
.
Finally, since each
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is globally generated, the result of the theorem follows from
Section 5
.
∎

6.
Examples and computations

In this final section, we provide some examples and computations.
In the first example, we give a general formula for the mixed Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1},I_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})
of two complete intersections
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
that involve disjoint sets of variables.
In this case, the formula for the mixed Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I_{1},I_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})
can be nicely expressed in terms of the Segre zeta functions of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.

Example 6.1
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}=\text{Proj}(R)
.
Let
I_{1}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
and
I_{2}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{s})
be two homogeneous ideal of
R
.
Let
a_{i}=\deg(f_{i})
and
b_{i}=\deg(g_{i})
.
Assume the following two conditions:

(i)

I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are complete intersections (i.e.,
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{s}
are regular sequences).

(ii)

There is an index
k
such that
f_{i}\in\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{k}]
and
g_{i}\in\mathbb{k}[x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{n}]
(i.e., the generators of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
involve disjoint sets of variables).

Then the mixed Segre zeta function of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
is given by

\zeta_{I_{1},I_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})\;=\;\zeta_{I_{1}}(t_{1})\;+\;\zeta_{I_{2}}(t_{2})\;-\;\zeta_{I_{1}}(t_{1})\zeta_{I_{2}}(t_{2})\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[\![t_{1},t_{2}]\!],

where

\zeta_{I_{1}}(t_{1})\;=\;\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}t_{1}^{r}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1+a_{i}t_{1}\right)}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[\![t_{1}]\!]\quad\text{ and }\quad\zeta_{I_{2}}(t_{2})\;=\;\frac{b_{1}\cdots b_{s}t_{2}^{s}}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(1+b_{i}t_{2}\right)}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}[\![t_{2}]\!]

are the Segre zeta functions of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, respectively.

Proof.

Let
Z_{1}=V(I_{1})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
and
Z_{2}=V(I_{2})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
be the closed subschemes of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
determined by
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, respectively.
Consider the joint blow-up
b:\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
.
Since the generators of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
involve disjoint sets of variables, we obtain the following fibre square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}_{2}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{2}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}_{1}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}}
\pi_{1}
b_{2}
\pi_{2}
b_{1}

(in general, we only have a commutative diagram); see, e.g.,
[
1
, Corollary 2.4]
.
Let
E_{i}
be the exceptional divisor of the blow-up
\mathcal{B}_{i}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{i}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})
.
By an abuse of notation, the pull-back of
E_{i}
to
\mathcal{B}={\mathcal{B}\ell}_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})
is also denoted as
E_{i}
.
Let
W=V(I_{1}+I_{2})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
be the closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
determined by
I_{1}+I_{2}
and consider the closed immersion
j_{i}:W\hookrightarrow Z_{i}
.
Since
Z_{i}
is a complete intersection, we obtain the isomorphisms

b_{i}^{-1}(W)\;=\;\mathcal{B}_{i}\times_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}}W\;\cong\;E_{i}\times_{Z_{i}}W\;\cong\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{i}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\times_{Z_{i}}W\;\cong\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(j_{i}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{i}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)

where
\mathcal{N}_{Z_{i}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
is the normal bundle to
Z_{i}
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
.
Hence restricting the above fibre square to
W
yields the new fibre square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b^{-1}(W)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b_{2}^{-1}(W)\;\cong\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(j_{2}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{2}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b_{1}^{-1}(W)\;\cong\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(j_{1}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W.}

Consider the closed immersions
j_{Z_{i}}:Z_{i}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
and
j_{W}:W\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}
.
Since
[W]=a_{1}\cdots a_{r}b_{1}\cdots b_{s}H^{r+s}
,
j_{1}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{W}\left(a_{i}\,j_{W}^{*}H\right)
and
j_{2}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{2}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathcal{O}_{W}\left(b_{i}\,j_{W}^{*}H\right)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle b_{*}\left(\sum_{i,j\geq 1}{(-E_{1})}^{i}t_{1}^{i}{(-E_{2})}^{j}t_{2}^{j}\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;t_{1}t_{2}\,b_{*}\left(\sum_{i,j\geq 0}{(-E_{1})}^{i}t_{1}^{i}{(-E_{2})}^{j}t_{2}^{j}\,\smallfrown\,\left[b^{-1}(W)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;{j_{W}}_{*}\Big(t_{1}t_{2}\,t_{1}^{r-1}t_{2}^{s-1}\,s_{t_{1}}\!\left(j_{1}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)\,s_{t_{2}}\!\left(j_{2}^{*}\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{2}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[W\right]\Big)

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}\,b_{1}\cdots b_{s}H^{r+s} t_{1}^{r}t_{2}^{s}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1+a_{i}Ht_{1})\,\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1+b_{i}Ht_{2})};

see
[
10
, Proposition 4.1(a)]
.
Similarly, we have that

-b_{*}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}{(-E_{1})}^{i}t_{1}^{i}\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)\;=\;{j_{Z_{1}}}_{*}\big(t_{1}\,t_{1}^{r-1}\,s_{t_{1}}\!\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{1}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[Z_{1}\right]\big)\;=\;\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}H^{r} t_{1}^{r}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}(1+a_{i}Ht_{1})}

and

-b_{*}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}{(-E_{2})}^{i}t_{2}^{i}\,\smallfrown\,\left[\mathcal{B}\right]\right)\;=\;{j_{Z_{2}}}_{*}\big(t_{2}\,t_{2}^{s-1}\,s_{t_{2}}\!\left(\mathcal{N}_{Z_{2}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n}\right)\,\smallfrown\,\left[Z_{2}\right]\big)\;=\;\frac{b_{1}\cdots b_{s}H^{s} t_{2}^{s}}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}(1+b_{i}Ht_{2})}.

By combining the above computations with
Section 2
, we get

\displaystyle\iota_{*}\left(s(Z_{1},Z_{2};{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{n})\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;-b_{*}\left(\sum_{i+j\geq 1}(-E_{1})^{i}t_{1}^{i}(-E_{2})t_{2}^{j}\,\smallfrown\,[\mathcal{B}]\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}H^{r}t_{1}^{r}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1+a_{i}Ht_{1}\right)}+\frac{b_{1}\cdots b_{s}H^{s}t_{2}^{s}}{\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(1+b_{i}Ht_{2}\right)}-\frac{a_{1}\cdots a_{r}b_{1}\cdots b_{s}H^{r+s}t_{1}^{r}t_{2}^{s}}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left(1+a_{i}Ht_{1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{s}\left(1+b_{i}Ht_{2}\right)}.

The result of the example now follows (see
Section 4
).
∎

By utilizing
Section 6
and
Section 4
, we obtain a formula for the Segre zeta function
\zeta_{I}(t)
of the product
I=I_{1}I_{2}\subset R
of the two ideals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.
We explore this observation in the next three examples.

Example 6.2
.

We continue with the same setting of
Section 6
.
Consider the ideal
I=I_{1}I_{2}\subset R
given as the product of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.
Write
A(t)=\zeta_{I_{1}}(t)-1=\sum_{i\geq 0}\alpha_{i}t^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!]
and
B(t)=\zeta_{I_{2}}(t)-1=\sum_{i\geq 0}\beta_{i}t^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!]
.
By combining
Section 4
and
Section 6
, obtain the following formula

\zeta_{I}(t)\;=\;1\,-\,A(t)\odot B(t)

for the Segre zeta function of
I
in terms of the
binomial convolution

A(t)\odot B(t)\;:=\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\alpha_{i}\beta_{k-i}\right)t^{k}

of
A(t)
and
B(t)
.
By
[
11
, Theorem 6]
, we can write

A(t)\odot B(t)\;=\;\frac{L(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{s}(1+(a_{i}+b_{j})t)}

for some polynomial
L(t)
.
Therefore we can also write

\zeta_{I}(t)\;=\;\frac{P(t)}{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{s}(1+(a_{i}+b_{j})t)}

for some polynomial
P(t)
.
This agrees with the general presentation of
Theorem 4.3
because
I
is generated by the homogeneous polynomials
f_{i}g_{j}
of degree
\deg(f_{i}g_{j})=a_{i}+b_{j}
.
Consider the power series

E_{A}(t)\;=\;\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{i!}t^{i}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{B}(t)\;=\;\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\beta_{i}}{i!}t^{i}.

Then the binomial convolution can be computed as the following integral

A(t)\odot B(t)\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}E_{A}(tx)E_{B}(tx)\,e^{-x}\,dx.

We shall exploit this representation as an integral in the next, more explicit example.

Example 6.3
.

With the same setting of
Section 6
, we assume that
I_{1}=(f_{1})
,
I_{2}=(g_{1},g_{2})
and
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
.
Consider the following power series with their partial fraction decompositions

A(t)\;=\;\zeta_{I_{1}}(t)-1\;=\;-\frac{1}{1+a_{1}t}

and

B(t)\;=\;\zeta_{I_{2}}(t)-1\;=\;\frac{b_{2}/(b_{1}-b_{2})}{(1+b_{1}t)}+\frac{b_{1}/(b_{2}-b_{1})}{(1+b_{2}t)}.

Let
\delta_{1}=b_{1}/(b_{2}-b_{1})
and
\delta_{2}=b_{2}/(b_{1}-b_{2})
.
Notice that

E_{A}(t)=-e^{-a_{1}t}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{B}(t)=\delta_{2}e^{-b_{1}t}+\delta_{1}e^{-b_{2}t}.

We can now compute

\displaystyle\zeta_{I}(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;1\,-\,A(t)\odot B(t)

\displaystyle\;=\;1+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-a_{1}tx}\left(\delta_{2}e^{-b_{1}tx}+\delta_{1}e^{-b_{2}tx}\right)e^{-x}\,dx

\displaystyle\;=\;1+\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta_{2}e^{-\left(1+(a_{1}+b_{1})t\right)x}+\delta_{1}e^{-\left(1+(a_{1}+b_{2})t\right)x}\,dx

\displaystyle\;=\;1+\frac{\delta_{2}}{1+(a_{1}+b_{1})t}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{1+(a_{1}+b_{2})t},

which yields an explicit formula for
\zeta_{I}(t)
.

Example 6.4
.

From
Section 6
, in the case
I_{1}=(x_{0}^{2})
and
I_{2}=(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{3})
, the Segre zeta function of
I=I_{1}I_{2}=(x_{0}^{2}x_{1}^{2},x_{0}^{2}x_{2}^{3})
is given by

\zeta_{I}(I)\;=\;1-\frac{3}{1+4t}+\frac{2}{1+5t}\;=\;\frac{2t+20t^{2}}{(1+4t)(1+5t)}.

One can check that this agrees the output of the
Macaulay2
package
[

]
.
Let
I=(x_{0}^{2}x_{1}^{2},x_{0}^{2}x_{2}^{3})\subset\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{10}]
and

be the closed subscheme determined by

.
By calling the function
segre
we obtain the output

\iota_{*}\left(s(Z,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{10})\right)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{c}16\,385\,522\,H^{10}-3\,119\,818\,H^{9}+584\,642\,H^{8}-107\,098\,H^{7}\\

is equal to
Q(t_{1},t_{2})=1+4t_{1}+5t_{2}+20t_{1}t_{2}
.
It can be checked that the homogenization
t_{0}^{2}+4t_{0}t_{1}+5t_{0}t_{2}+20t_{1}t_{2}
of
Q(t_{1},t_{2})
is a Lorentzian polynomial.

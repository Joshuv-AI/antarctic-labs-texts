Abstract
. The aim of this work is to study the notions of lax protomodular and 𝖮𝗋𝖽 \mathsf{Ord} -Mal’tsev category at the level of (coherent) varieties of (pre)ordered algebras and to further compare them, as has been done in the non-ordered context. We characterise varieties of ordered algebras which are (co)lax protomodular and those which are 𝖮𝗋𝖽 \mathsf{Ord} -Mal’tsev, in terms of operations of arities given by ordered sets and inequalities involving them. We exhibit examples of (co)lax protomodular non-degenerate 𝖮𝗋𝖽 \mathsf{Ord} -categories, which were unknown. We prove that, for varieties of ordered algebras which are 𝖮𝗋𝖽 \mathsf{Ord} -Mal’tsev categories, the order of their algebras is degenerate (i.e. is symmetric). As a consequence, the implication “protomodular ⇒ \Rightarrow Mal’tsev” cannot be carried out to our context. The case of non-coherent ordered varieties which are 𝖮𝗋𝖽 \mathsf{Ord} -Mal’tsev categories is also addressed, where we show the existence of algebras with non-degenerate order.

1.
Varieties of ordered algebras

We shall consider (finitary) varieties of ordered algebras, where “order” here means preorder (i.e. a reflexive and transitive relation). Although most of the known literature on ordered algebras is carried out for the partial order case (see
[
1
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
), we prefer to work in the more general setting of preorder.

In this section we present the setting to define varieties of ordered algebras. The approach is very similar to the usual one for (discrete) varieties of algebras (from
[
14
]
), where the main difference is in the system of arities: an arity is a finite ordered set
(X,\leq)
instead of a natural number
n
(which we will denote simply by
X
).

First we consider the
system
\mathscr{J}
of arities
, which is the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Ord}
of finite ordered sets.
An
\mathsf{Ord}
-algebraic theory with arities
\mathscr{J}
is an
\mathsf{Ord}
-category
\mathscr{T}
(i.e. a category enriched in the category
\mathsf{Ord}
) together with an
\mathsf{Ord}
-functor
\tau\colon\mathscr{J}^{\rm op}\to\mathscr{T}
which preserves powers and is surjective on objects. A
\mathscr{T}
-algebra
is a power preserving
\mathsf{Ord}
-functor
A\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathsf{Ord}
, while a
\mathscr{T}
-algebra homomorphism is a natural transformation

between
\mathscr{T}
-algebras
A
and
B
.

Recall that in
\mathsf{Ord}
, and therefore also in
\mathscr{J}
, the
power of ordered sets
C
and
X
(also called the
cotensor of
C
by
X
) is the ordered set

X\pitchfork C=C^{X}=\{\mathsf{x}\colon X\to C:\mathsf{x}\text{ is monotone}\},

with its pointwise order. Moreover, in
\mathsf{Ord}
, and therefore also in
\mathscr{J}
, every (finite) ordered set
X
is the
copower
(or
tensor
) of
1
by
X
,
1\bullet X
.
Hence, if we denote
\tau(1)=T
, we
have

\tau(X)=\tau(X\pitchfork 1)=X\pitchfork T,

and a
\mathscr{T}
-algebra
A\colon\mathscr{T}\to\mathsf{Ord}
is completely determined by the image
A(T)
of
T
, since, for any
X
we have

A(X\pitchfork T)=A(T)^{X},

with its pointwise order: given monotone maps
\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}\colon X\to A(T)
, we define
\mathsf{x}\leq\mathsf{y}
if and only if
\mathsf{x}(x)\leq\mathsf{y}(x)

. Moreover, a
\mathscr{T}
-algebra homomorphism is completely determined by its

\mathscr{T}
-algebra

, or simply

An

Since a

\textstyle{A^{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We point out that an operation can be seen as a

The category

The category

\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathsf{Alg}(\mathscr{T})(\mathscr{T}(X\pitchfork T,-),A)&\cong&{\rm Nat}(\mathscr{T}(X\pitchfork T,-),A)\\

4.
\mathsf{Ord}
-Mal’tsev non-coherent ordered varieties

As it is well-known, the category of ordered groups and monotone homomorphisms is not a variety of ordered algebras in the sense we presented in Section 1. Indeed, while the addition (not necessarily commutative) is monotone, the inversion operation is anti-monotone. In this section we will use a more general notion of variety of ordered algebras which includes the category of ordered groups and ordered vector spaces, for instance.

The definition of variety we presented in Section 1 uses algebraic theories as investigated by Power and his co-authors (see
[
19
,
17
,
18
]
; see also
[
15
]
), where all the operations of the algebras are morphisms in
\mathsf{Ord}
, that is are monotone. Following
[
1
]
, we say that these operations are
coherent
, and that the variety is
coherent
. A
(not necessarily coherent) variety
is defined as before, but with no condition on the monotonicity of the operations. We point out that, as shown in
[
1
]
for the case of
\mathsf{Pos}
, while coherent varieties are the categories of algebras for finitary
\mathsf{Pos}
-enriched
monads on
\mathsf{Pos}
, (not necessarily coherent) varieties are the categories of algebras for finitary (not necessarily
\mathsf{Pos}
-enriched) monads on
\mathsf{Pos}
.

The aim of this Section is to show that for non-coherent varieties of ordered algebras the Mal’tsev condition does not trivialise, i.e. there exist algebras with non-degenerate order.

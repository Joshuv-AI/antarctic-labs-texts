1.
Introduction

Cluster categories were introduced in
[
2
]
as a categorical model for Fomin and Zelevinsky’s cluster algebras. Independently, in
[
3
]

they were studied for type
A_{n}
using an explicit combinatorial model based on the diagonals of a regular polygon. Cluster categories are defined
as the orbit categories
\mathcal{D}^{b}(H)/\langle F\rangle
, where
\mathcal{D}^{b}(H)
is the
bounded derived category of a hereditary algebra
H
,
and
F=\tau^{-1}[1]
is the automorphism generated by
\tau
,
the Auslander-Reiten translation in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(H)
and
[1]
is the shift functor of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(H)
.

In order to study cluster-tilted algebras and their intermediate coverings, Zhu
[
17
]
introduced repetitive cluster categories, which are defined as the orbit categories
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathcal{H})/\langle(\tau^{-1}[1])^{p}\rangle
for
1\leq p\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\mathcal{H}
is a hereditary abelian category with tilting objects.
Furthermore, Lamberti
[
11
]
provided a geometric model for the
categories
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}:=D^{b}(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits KA_{n})/\langle(\tau^{-1}[1])^{p}\rangle
,
constructed using diagonals in the so-called repetitive polygon
\Pi^{p}
, which generalises the construction of Caldero, Chapoton and Schiffler
[
3
]
for
p=1
.
Recently, Gubitosi
[
7
]
showed that the repetitive cluster category of type
D_{n}
, defined as the orbit categories
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}=\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits KD_{n})/\langle(\tau^{-1}[1])^{p}\rangle

for
1\leq p\in\mathbb{N}
, is equivalent to a category defined on a subset
of tagged edges in a regular punctured polygon. This construction generalizes the construction of
Schiffler’s work
[
14
]
for
p=1
.
providing new insights into the geometric interpretation of these categories.
A distinctive feature of repetitive cluster categories is that they have a fractional Calabi-Yau dimension
\frac{2p}{q}
, which cannot be simplified.
This fractional dimension is a key characteristic that sets the category

\mathcal{C}_{n,p}
apart from the case when
p=1
, and it highlights the subtle differences in the geometric and algebraic properties of the categories as

p
increases. The fractional nature of the Calabi-Yau dimension is a consequence of the periodic action of the automorphism
(\tau^{-1}[1])^{p}
, and it plays an important role in understanding the homological structure of these categories.

The Grothendieck group of a triangulated category is defined as the free abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes of its objects, subject to the Euler relations associated with the distinguished triangles
[
6
]
. These Euler relations provide a fundamental constraint on the group, reflecting the structural properties of the category. In particular, each distinguished triangle in the triangulated category imposes a linear relation between the objects involved, which can be captured in the Grothendieck group. Thomason
[
15
]
further showed that the subgroups of the Grothendieck group play a crucial role in classifying the dense triangulated subcategories of a given category. Specifically, there is a bijective correspondence between the set of subgroups of the Grothendieck group and the set of dense triangulated subcategories. This result provides a deep link between algebraic and categorical structures, demonstrating how the algebraic information encoded in the Grothendieck group can be used to understand the geometric or categorical properties of subcategories in the triangulated category.
This correspondence is not merely formal, but offers a powerful tool for studying the structure of triangulated categories, as it allows one to use the Grothendieck group to detect and classify the “density” of subcategories—whether they approximate the whole category in a sense that is captured by their interaction with the Grothendieck group.

The Grothendieck groups of triangulated categories have been extensively studied by various researchers. Barot, Kussin, and Lenzing
[
1
]
investigated the Grothendieck group of a cluster category by analyzing the action of the Coxeter transformation on its derived category. Xiao and Zhu
[
16
]
examined the relations of the Grothendieck group of triangulated categories of finite type, proving that they are generated by all Auslander-Reiten triangles. Palu
[
13
]
computed the Grothendieck group of certain 2-Calabi-Yau triangulated categories and established a generalization of the Fomin-Zelevinsky mutation rule. Fedele
[
4
]
extended the results of Xiao and Zhu, as well as those of Palu, to higher-dimensional cases, further advancing our understanding of Grothendieck groups in triangulated categories. Recently, Murphy
[
12
]
computed the Grothendieck groups for each family of discrete cluster categories of Dynkin type
A_{\infty}
, a class of categories initially introduced by Igusa and Todorov
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we compute the Grothendieck groups of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits KD_{n})/\langle(\tau^{-1}[1])^{p}\rangle
, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}
. Specifically, when
p
is odd, we obtain a complete result,
whereas for even
p
is, we only achieve a partial result. The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}
be the repetitive cluster categories of type
D_{n}
. Then the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}
, denoted by
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})
, is as follows:

(1)

If
p
is odd, then

K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{Z}&\mathrm{if\;}n\mathrm{\;is\;odd},\\
\mathbb{Z}^{2}&\mathrm{if\;}n\mathrm{\;is\;even}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

If
p
is even and
n
is even, then

K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we establish the necessary notation and review some concepts that will be used later. Specifically, we provide an overview of various concepts and results related to repetitive cluster categories of type
D_{n}
, along with a detailed definition of the Grothendieck group. Finally, in Section 3, we present the proof of the main result.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}
be the repetitive cluster categories of type
D_{n}
. Then the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}_{n,p}
, denoted by
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})
, is as follows:

(1)

If
p
is odd, then

K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbb{Z}&\mathrm{if\;}n\mathrm{\;is\;odd},\\
\mathbb{Z}^{2}&\mathrm{if\;}n\mathrm{\;is\;even}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

If
p
is even and
n
is even, then

K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})=\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

Proof.

(1) Suppose that
p
is odd. If
p=1
, then
\mathcal{C}_{n,1}=\mathcal{C}_{n}
is the classical cluster category of type
D_{n}
. The result holds by Lemma
2.8
. If
p>1
, then we have the following two cases:

(i)

When
n
is even, it is easy to see
t=np
is an even integer. Hence
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{t})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by Lemma
2.8
, thus the following diagrams

\textstyle{K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

show that
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by Remark
3.6
.

(ii)

When
n
is odd, it is easy to see
t=np
is an odd integer. By Lemma
2.8
we have
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{t})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}
, we can show
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})\cong\mathbb{Z}
similarly as (i).

(2) Suppose that both
p
and
n
is even, then so is
t=np
. we can show
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{n,p})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
as (i).

∎

Remark 3.8
.

For the case when
p
is even and
n
is odd, is still an open question.

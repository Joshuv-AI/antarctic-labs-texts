On spherical T T -design in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Ryutaro Misawa, Yusaku Nishimura

Abstract
. In this paper, we study spherical T T -designs and their harmonic strength Hst ⁡ ( X ) \Hst(X) on the unit circle S 1 S^{1} . For any finite set T ⊂ ℕ T\subset\mathbb{N} , we constructively demonstrate the existence of a finite design X X such that Hst ⁡ ( X ) = T \Hst(X)=T .

1.
Introduction

Spherical designs, introduced by Delsarte, Goethals, and Seidel
[
2
]
, are finite sets of points on the unit sphere

S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that reproduce exact surface averages of low-degree polynomials. Concretely, a non-empty finite set
X\subset S^{d-1}
is a
spherical
t
-design
if

(1)

\frac{1}{|S^{d-1}|}\int_{S^{d-1}}\!f(\xi)\,d\xi\;=\;\frac{1}{|X|}\sum_{\xi\in X}f(\xi)\quad\bigl(\deg f\leq t\bigr).

When (
1
) holds for every polynomial of degree at most
t
but fails for some polynomial of degree
t+1
, the integer
t
is called the
strength
of
X
.

Classical work on spherical designs has been driven by three intertwined questions:

(a)

For which parameters does no design exist?

(b)

For which parameters does some design exist?

(c)

How can explicit examples be constructed?

Because
S^{1}

admits a remarkably simple trigonometric parametrisation, it serves as an invaluable toy model for testing these questions. Hong
[
3
]
classified one-dimensional spherical

t
-designs into
group-type
and
non-group type
families, and Martínez
[
4
]
sharpened this classification further by means of symmetric function techniques.

A natural generalisation replaces the initial segment
\{1,\dots,t\}
by an arbitrary finite set of degrees. For
T\subset\mathbb{N}
we call
X\subset S^{d-1}
a
spherical
T
-design
if

\sum_{\xi\in X}P(\xi)=0\quad\bigl(P\in\Harm_{k}(d),\;k\in T\bigr),

where
\Harm_{k}(d)
denotes the space of real homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
k
in
d
variables. The set

\Hst(X)\coloneqq\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}~\middle|~\sum_{\xi\in X}P(\xi)=0\text{ for all }P\in\Harm_{k}(d)\right\}

is called the
harmonic strength
of
X
.

In the study of spherical
T
-designs, the following basic questions, corresponding to the classical problems (a), (b), (c), naturally arise. Namely, for a given or arbitrary finite set
T\subset\mathbb{N}
:

(a’)

For which
T
does no spherical design
X
with
\Hst(X)=T
exist?

(b’)

Does a design
X
with
\Hst(X)=T
exist for every finite set
T\subset\mathbb{N}
?

(c’)

If such an
X
exists, can one provide explicit constructions?

Even for
d=2
, the study of
T
-designs remains incomplete. Miezaki
[
1
]
determined the harmonic strength for the shells of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Meanwhile, Bannai et al.
[
5
]
investigated obstruction conditions for spherical designs in higher dimensions, i.e., studied non-existence related to question (a’). However, on
S^{1}
, questions (b’) and (c’) remained unsettled.

The present paper provides affirmative answers to these questions (b’) and (c’) on
S^{1}
. First, we analyse a one-parameter family of
5
-point configurations on
S^{1}
via analytic moment functions, thereby producing designs that annihilate exactly one prescribed harmonic degree
t
and no others:

Theorem A
.

For every integer
t\geq 1
, there exist uncountably many
5
-point spherical designs
X\subset S^{1}
with
\Hst(X)=\{t\}
and
1\in X
. No subset with fewer points has this property.

Second, leveraging Theorem A, we prove that spherical designs exist for arbitrary finite sets of harmonic strengths:

Theorem B
.

For every finite set
T\subset\mathbb{N}
,
there exists a spherical design
X\subset S^{1}
with
\Hst(X)=T
.

Throughout, we identify
S^{1}
with
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|=1\}
and write
P_{k}(X)=\sum_{x\in X}x^{k}
for the
k
-th complex moment of a finite set
X\subset S^{1}
.
Note that from Lemma 1 of Hong
[
3
]
,
when
X\subset S^{1}
,

\Hst(X)=\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}~\middle|~P_{k}(X)=0\right\}.

2.
Construction of Designs with Prescribed Harmonic Strength

In this section, we prove Theorem A and Theorem B.
First, we prove some lemmas. The following lemma is well-known as the Identity Theorem (see
[
6
, Corollary 1.2.7]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval, and let
f:I\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-analytic function. If its zero set

Z(f)=\{x\in I\mid f(x)=0\}

has an accumulation point in
I
, then
f
is identically zero on
I
. In particular, nontrivial real-analytic functions have only countably many zeros.

Proof.

The first statement is the classical Identity Theorem for real-analytic functions.
[
6
, Corollary 1.2.7]
.

The second statement follows from this isolation property. Indeed, since
I
is a countable union of compact subsets,
f
has only countably many zeros.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
D
be any open interval satisfying

\emptyset\neq D\subseteq\Bigl(-1,\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)\setminus\{-\tfrac{1}{4}\},

and for each
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
define

f_{r}(x)=1+2\cos\bigl(r\arccos(x)\bigr)+2\cos\bigl(r\arccos(-x-\tfrac{1}{2})\bigr).

Then

\{x\in D\mid\forall r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}:f_{r}(x)\neq 0\}\neq\emptyset.

Moreover, this set is uncountable.

Proof.

Since
\arccos(x)
and
\arccos(-x-\tfrac{1}{2})
are analytic on
(-1,1)
, each
f_{r}(x)
is analytic on
D
.
For
r\neq 1
, let
Z_{r}
be the set of zero points of
f_{r}
that are also in
D
.
Then, from Lemma
2.1
,
Z_{r}
is countable.
Therefore,

Z=\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}}Z_{r}

is also countable, while
D
is uncountable.
Thus,
D\setminus Z
is also uncountable, yielding uncountably many
x\in D
such that
f_{r}(x)\neq 0
for all
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
D
be any open interval satisfying

\emptyset\neq D\subseteq\Bigl(-1,\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr)\setminus\{-\tfrac{1}{4}\}.

For any
x\in D
, define

X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)\coloneqq\left\{1,e^{{i\arccos(x)}/{t}},e^{-{i\arccos(x)}/{t}},e^{i\arccos\left(-x-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)/t},e^{-i\arccos\left(-x-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)/t}\right\},

where
t
is any natural number.
Then, there exists uncountably many
x\in D
such that
\Hst(X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x))=\{t\}
.

Proof.

Since
x\notin\{-1,-\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{1}{2}\}
,
X^{\tfrac{1}{t}}(x)
contains
5
distinct elements.
Write
X(x)=X^{1}(x)
.
Since

P_{l}(X(x))=f_{l}(x),

we have
P_{1}(X(x))=0
for any
x\in D
.
From
2.2
, there exist uncountably many
x\in D
such that

f_{r}(x)\neq 0
for any
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\setminus\{1\}
.
For these
x
, since
P_{k}(X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x))=f_{\frac{k}{t}}(x)
,
P_{k}(X^{\tfrac{1}{t}}(x))=0
if and only if
k=t
. Then, the harmonic strength of
X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)
is
\{t\}
.
∎

Now, we can prove Theorem A.

Proof of Theorem A.

From
2.3
, there exist uncountably many
5
-point spherical designs
X\subset S^{1}
with
\Hst(x)=\{t\}
and
1\in X
.

Next, we show that the minimum number of points on
S^{1}
with harmonic strength
\{t\}
is
5
for a given
t
.
Assume that
X
is a finite subset of
S^{1}
with harmonic strength
\{t\}
. When
t=1
,
X
is a spherical
1
-design.
From
[
3
, Theorem A(i)]
,
|X|<5
if and only if
X
is a group-type design, specifically,
X
is an equilateral triangle, a square, or a disjoint union of two antipodal pairs.
From
[
2
, Example 5.14]
, the harmonic strength of the equilateral triangle and the square includes
2
, and that of the disjoint union of two antipodal pairs includes
3
.
In any case,
\Hst(X)\supsetneq\{1\}
.
Therefore,
|X|\geq 5
.
When
t\neq 1
, consider

X^{t}\coloneqq\{\xi^{t}\mid\xi\in X\}.

If
|X^{t}|=|X|
, then
X^{t}
is a
\{1\}
-design, which implies
|X|=|X^{t}|\geq 5
.
If
|X^{t}|<|X|
, then without loss of generality we can assume that
X^{t}=\{1,1,\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n}\}
as a multiset, where
n=|X|-2
.
From the assumption,

2+\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\xi_{i}=0.

If
|X|<5
, then
n\leq 2
, and the above equation forces
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=-1
.
In this situation, since
3t\in\Hst(X)
, we obtain
\Hst(X)\supsetneq\{t\}
.
This is a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

The
X(x)
constructed in
2.3
has
5
points. It is known that the minimum number of points for a spherical
t
-design on
S^{1}
is
t+1
, and for
t=1
this minimum is two.
Our construction gives a non-group type
1
-design with
n=5
, matching the lower bound
n\geq 2t+3
in Hong’s result (Theorem A of
[
3
]
).

Next, we prove Theorem B.
To achieve this, we define a combination of spherical designs on
S^{1}
.
First, we show the following lemma.
Note that since we identify
S^{1}
with a multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{C}
, for any subsets
X_{1},X_{2}\subset S^{1}
, their product
X_{1}\cdot X_{2}
is defined as follows:

X_{1}\cdot X_{2}=\{cd\mid c\in X_{1},\,d\in X_{2}\}.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
X_{1},X_{2}
have harmonic strengths
T_{1},T_{2}
respectively, and
|X_{1}\cdot X_{2}|=|X_{1}||X_{2}|
, then
X_{1}\cdot X_{2}
has harmonic strength
T_{1}\cup T_{2}
.

Proof.

Direct calculation gives:

\displaystyle P_{k}(X_{1}\cdot X_{2})

\displaystyle=P_{k}(X_{1})P_{k}(X_{2}).

Therefore,
P_{k}(X_{1}\cdot X_{2})=0
iff
P_{k}(X_{1})=0
or
P_{k}(X_{2})=0
, yielding the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem B.

We prove that there exists a subset
X\subset S^{1}
such that
\Hst(X)=T
and
|X|=5^{|T|}
by induction on the size of
T
.
When
|T|=1
, from Theorem A, there exists a subset
X_{1}\subset S^{1}
such that
|X_{1}|=5
and
\Hst(X_{1})=T
.
Let
T^{\prime}
be any subset of
\mathbb{N}
with
|T^{\prime}|=k+1\geq 2
, and let
t
be any element of
T^{\prime}
.
By the inductive hypothesis, there exists a subset
X_{k}\subset S^{1}
such that
\Hst(X_{k})=T^{\prime}\setminus\{t\}
and
|X_{k}|=5^{k}
.

Recall that

\displaystyle X(x)

\displaystyle=\left\{1,e^{i\arccos(x)},e^{-i\arccos(x)},e^{i\arccos(-x-\tfrac{1}{2})},e^{-i\arccos(-x-\tfrac{1}{2})}\right\}

\displaystyle=\{1,x\pm i\sqrt{1-x^{2}},-x+\tfrac{1}{2}\pm i\sqrt{-x^{2}+x+\tfrac{3}{4}}\}

\displaystyle=\{g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),\ldots,g_{5}(x)\}

constructed in
2.3
for
x\in(-1,\tfrac{1}{2})\setminus\{-\tfrac{1}{4}\}
.
Define

D_{k}\coloneq\{x\in(-1,\tfrac{1}{2})\setminus\{-\tfrac{1}{4}\}\mid|X_{k}\cdot X(x)|<|X_{k}||X(x)|\}.

We show that
D_{k}
is a finite set.
Let
G=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{5}\}
.
Since each element of
G
is an algebraic function,
for any
g,g^{\prime}\in G
and
\xi\in X_{k}\cdot X_{k}^{-1}\setminus\{1\}
,

Z(g\overline{g^{\prime}}-\xi)=\{x\in(-1,\tfrac{1}{2})\setminus\{-\tfrac{1}{4}\}\mid g(x)\overline{g^{\prime}(x)}-\xi=0\}

is a finite set.
Thus

D_{k}=\bigcup_{g,g^{\prime}\in G}\bigcup_{\xi\in X_{k}\cdot X_{k}^{-1}\setminus\{1\}}Z(g\overline{g^{\prime}}-\xi)

is also a finite set. From
2.3
,
there exists
x\in(-1,\tfrac{1}{2})\setminus(D_{k}\cup\{-\tfrac{1}{4}\})
such that
\Hst(X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x))=\{t\}
,
|X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)|=5
, and such
x
satisfies
|X_{k}\cdot X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)|=|X_{k}||X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)|
.
From
2.5
,

\Hst(X_{k}\cdot X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x))=T^{\prime}

and
|X_{k}\cdot X^{\frac{1}{t}}(x)|=5^{k+1}
.
Therefore, for any
T^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|T^{\prime}|=k+1
, there also exists a subset
X
such that
\Hst(X)=T^{\prime}
and
|X|=5^{k+1}
.
Thus, by induction, for any finite subset
T
of
\mathbb{N}
, there exists a subset
X
such that
\Hst(X)=T
and
|X|=5^{|T|}
.
∎

3.
Conclusion

In this paper, we addressed the existence and construction of spherical
T
-designs on
S^{1}
for arbitrary finite set
T\subset\mathbb{N}
. We first demonstrated constructively in Theorem A that for any single integer
t\geq 1
, there exists a
5
-point spherical design
X\subset S^{1}
whose harmonic strength is precisely
\Hst(X)=\{t\}
. Furthermore, we proved that
5
is the minimum number of points required for such a design.

This leads naturally to the question of optimality. Let
N(T)
denote the minimum possible number of points in a spherical design
X\subset S^{1}
such that
\Hst(X)=T
. Theorem A establishes
N(\{t\})=5
for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Our construction in Theorem B provides the upper bound
N(T)\leq 5^{|T|}
. However, the question remains:

What is the exact value of
N(T)
for an arbitrary finite set
T\subset\mathbb{N}
with
|T|>1
?

Is the multiplicative construction presented here optimal in terms of the number of points, or do smaller designs generally exist? Determining the minimum size
N(T)
for general finite sets
T
and finding constructions that achieve this minimum are important open problems in the study of spherical
T
-designs on
S^{1}
. Investigating whether alternative construction methods could yield smaller designs, particularly for specific structures of the set
T
, warrants further research.

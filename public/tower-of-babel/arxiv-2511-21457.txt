Evaluating the tame Brauer group of open varieties over local fields
victor de vries

Abstract
. In this document we let U \displaystyle U be a smooth variety of pure dimension d \displaystyle d over a local field k v \displaystyle k_{v} with unit ball 𝒪 v \displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v} and residue field 𝔽 \displaystyle\mathbb{F} of characteristic p > 0 \displaystyle p>0 and we set n \displaystyle n to be a positive integer such that p ∤ n \displaystyle p\nmid n . For various u ∈ U ⁡ ( k v ) \displaystyle u\in U(k_{v}) we study the evaluation map u ∗ : H e ´ ​ t 2 ​ ( U , μ n ) → H e ´ ​ t 2 ​ ( k v , μ n ) \displaystyle u^{*}:\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2}(U,\mu_{n})\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2}(k_{v},\mu_{n}) . We suppose that U \displaystyle U embeds as an open subscheme in a regular scheme 𝒳 \displaystyle\mathcal{X} that is of finite type over 𝒪 v \displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v} . We assume that Z := 𝒳 ∖ U \displaystyle Z:=\mathcal{X}\setminus U is a divisor and we endow it with its reduced scheme structure. We show that for u 1 , u 2 ∈ U ⁡ ( k v ) \displaystyle u_{1},u_{2}\in U(k_{v}) that lift to x 1 , x 2 ∈ 𝒳 ⁡ ( 𝒪 v ) \displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v}) we obtain the same evaluation map u 1 ∗ = u 2 ∗ \displaystyle u_{1}^{*}=u_{2}^{*} under the two conditions that first, there is an equality of reductions x 1 ¯ = x 2 ¯ \displaystyle\overline{x_{1}}=\overline{x_{2}} in 𝒳 ⁡ ( 𝔽 ) \displaystyle\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{F}) and second, that cl ⁡ ( x 1 ∩ Z ) = cl ⁡ ( x 2 ∩ Z ) \displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(x_{1}\cap Z)=\mathrm{cl}(x_{2}\cap Z) holds in H | x ¯ | 2 ​ d ​ ( Z , μ n ⊗ d ) \displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2d}_{|\overline{x}|}(Z,\mu_{n}^{\otimes d}) .



Introduction

We fix a non archimedean local field
\displaystyle k_{v}
with unit ball
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}
and residue field
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}
of cardinality
\displaystyle q
and characteristic
\displaystyle p
. We set
\displaystyle U
to be a smooth variety over
\displaystyle k_{v}
that is equidimensional of dimension
\displaystyle d
. We define for any scheme
\displaystyle X
the cohomological Brauer group of
\displaystyle X
, denoted
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(X)
, by
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(X):=\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. For
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
the evaluation maps
\displaystyle s^{*}:\mathrm{Br}(U)\to\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})
are widely studied. Both the source and target for this arrow are torsion groups and the target group,
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})
, comes equipped with a canonical isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathrm{inv}:\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})\to\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
.

An example in which one might study such evaluation maps is when
\displaystyle U
has a model
\displaystyle U_{k}
over a numberfield
\displaystyle k
, such that
\displaystyle k_{v}
is a completion of
\displaystyle k
(at the place
\displaystyle v
). In this case understanding when two distinct points
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}\in U(k_{v})
induce different evaluation maps
\displaystyle x_{1}^{*},x_{2}^{*}:\mathrm{Br}(U_{k})\to\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})
is of interest for studying the Brauer-Manin obstruction to strong approximation on
\displaystyle U
.

A known result in the study of evaluation maps is obtained in the setting that
\displaystyle U
admits a regular model
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
over
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}
such that the special fiber,
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{F}}
, has a nonsingular locus
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\text{ns}}
consisting of only one connected component. This result works for any point
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
that lifts to a point
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
, whose reduction modulo
\displaystyle v
is
\displaystyle\overline{x}
. In this situation there is the commuting diagram in which
\displaystyle(p^{\prime})
means
\displaystyle p
-primary torsion (for a proof see
[
5
, Proposition 5.1]
):

(1)

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Br}(U)(p^{\prime})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathcal{U}_{\mathbb{F}}^{\text{ns}},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})(p^{\prime})}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})(p^{\prime})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})(p^{\prime})}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u^{*}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{x}^{*}}
\displaystyle\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The aim of this document is to investigate what happens when many of the conditions above are removed. For example, we may want to evaluate a point
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
that does not lift to an
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}
-point on the model
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
.

The setting in which we will be working is as follows: We let
\displaystyle U
be a smooth variety over
\displaystyle k_{v}
and we fix a regular scheme
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
that is of finite type over
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}
together with an open immersion
\displaystyle U\to\mathcal{X}
such that the complement of
\displaystyle U
is a divisor. Let
\displaystyle Z
be this complement endowed with its reduced scheme structure. Any point
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
that comes from a point
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
has that the scheme-theoretic intersection
\displaystyle Z\cap x
is an
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}/(\pi_{v}^{m})
-point on
\displaystyle Z
for certain
\displaystyle m\geq 1
. A weak variant of the theorem that we prove is as follows:

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
be a point that lifts to
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
. The evaluation map
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(U)(p^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})(p^{\prime})
depends only on the closed subscheme
\displaystyle Z\cap x
of
\displaystyle Z
.

By commuting Diagram (
1
) we know that this theorem holds in the case that
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
is a model of
\displaystyle U
over
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}
together with condition on the special fiber in the paragraph before Diagram (
1
).

The hope that the evaluation map
\displaystyle u^{*}
should only depend on the reduction
\displaystyle\overline{x}
as in the setting of Diagram (
1
) is easily crushed by taking for
\displaystyle p\neq 2
,
\displaystyle U=\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[t])
. Namely for
\displaystyle u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}\setminus(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*})^{2}
the points
\displaystyle p,up\in\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
both reduce to
\displaystyle 0\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and evaluating quaternion algebras
\displaystyle(t,a)_{-1}\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{G}_{m,\mathbb{Q}_{p}})[2]
for various
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}/(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})^{2}
on the different points
\displaystyle p,up

We wish to state our main theorem, which is a stronger variant of Theorem
0.1
. Denote by
\displaystyle|Y|
the underlying topological space of a scheme
\displaystyle Y
. The closed subscheme
\displaystyle Z\cap x
of
\displaystyle Z
has a cohomology class
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(Z\cap x)\in\mathrm{H}^{2d}_{|Z\cap x|}(Z,\mu_{n}^{\otimes d})
. This cohomology class appears in our main theorem as follows:

Theorem 0.2
.

The evaluation map
\displaystyle u^{*}:\mathrm{Br}(U)[n]\to\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})[n]
depends only on
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(Z\cap x)\in\mathrm{H}^{2d}_{|Z\cap x|}(Z,\mu_{n}^{\otimes d})
.

The intuition that the author would like to give is that the evaluation of a point
\displaystyle u
corresponding to
\displaystyle x
only depends on how
\displaystyle Z\cap x
looks ‘locally’ on
\displaystyle Z
. The above theorem gives such a statement, since in the case that
\displaystyle Z\cap x
is nonempty, the excision property in étale cohomology implies that we may replace
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2d}_{|Z\cap x|}(Z,\mu_{n}^{\otimes d})
by
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2d}_{|Z\cap x|}(A,\mu_{n}^{\otimes d})
, where
\displaystyle A
is the henselisation of
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{Z,|Z\cap x|}
at its maximal ideal.

Our initial motivation for proving such a result came from the examples that were encountered in Section
1
. Our initial idea to use the leray spectral sequence associated to
\displaystyle U\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
to prove Theorem
0.1
, because the
\displaystyle E_{2}
-terms in this spectral sequence are related to the cohomology of
\displaystyle Z
. It turns out that this method does not give the desired result and we explain why at the end of the Section
1
. However if one assumes that
\displaystyle Z
is an snc divisor and that
\displaystyle\gcd(n,q-1)=1
holds, then the spectral sequence argument does work as is also explained at the end of section
1
.

Our proof of Theorem
0.2
is inspired by the methods used in Section 5 of
[
3
]
. Like in this reference we will use the language of derived categories. To make the document more accessible to people that might not be so familiar with derived categories, we give an overview of the necessary tools from derived categories in Section

Section

is dedicated to the proof of Theorem

.

We note that studying the evaluation maps on the

1.
Concrete examples and the insufficiency of the Leray spectral sequence

In this section we exhibit a class of examples to Theorem
0.1
. Later in the section we explain why a certain spectral sequence argument fails to prove Theorem
0.1
in general, but we show that the argument goes through under additional hypothesis. The notation of this section will be as in Theorem
0.1
. We start with a ‘baby-example’ to which we already referred in the introduction.

Example 1.1
.

Set
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and fix the open subset
\displaystyle U=\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
. Since we have
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}})=\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, the purity theorem
[
6
, Theorem 3.7.1]
gives an exact sequence

(2)

0\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})(p^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Br}(U)(p^{\prime})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})(p^{\prime})\to 0

(the exact sequence ends in
\displaystyle 0
because of the vanishing of
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{3}(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
).
For any
\displaystyle n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\displaystyle p\nmid n
,
\displaystyle a\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}
and
\displaystyle b\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}/n)
, we can consider the element
\displaystyle[a\cdot X^{m}]_{n}\cup b\in\mathrm{Br}(U)[n]
where with
\displaystyle[-]_{n}
we mean its class in
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(K(U),\mu_{n})
.
By combining formula (1.27) in
[
6
]
for the residue map
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(k(U))\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
with exact sequence
2
, we obtain that
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(U)(p^{\prime})
is generated by elements of the form
\displaystyle[a\cdot X^{m}]_{n}\cup b\in\mathrm{Br}(U)[n]
.

So two points
\displaystyle u_{1},u_{2}\in U(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
yield the same evaluation map
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(U)(p^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})(p^{\prime})
if and only if for all
\displaystyle n
with
\displaystyle p\nmid n
and for all
\displaystyle b\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mathbb{Z}/n)
the elements
\displaystyle[u_{1}]_{n}\cup b
and
\displaystyle[u_{2}]_{n}\cup b
agree.
By Poitou-Tate duality (
[
10
, p.28]
) this is equivalent to for all
\displaystyle n
with
\displaystyle p\nmid n
there being an equality
\displaystyle[u_{1}]_{n}=[u_{2}]_{n}
in
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{p},\mu_{n})=\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{n}
. Writing
\displaystyle u_{1}=v_{1}\cdot p^{l_{1}}
and
\displaystyle u_{2}=v_{2}\cdot p^{l_{2}}
with
\displaystyle v_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
gives that the condition
\displaystyle[u_{1}]_{n}=[u_{2}]_{n}
for all
\displaystyle n>0
is equivalent to us having both
\displaystyle l_{1}=l_{2}
and
\displaystyle v_{1}\equiv v_{2}\,(\text{mod }(1+p))
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
.

Denote by
\displaystyle Z
the divisor
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[X]/(pX))\subset\mathcal{X}
. The complement of
\displaystyle Z
is
\displaystyle U
. Assume that
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}
lift to
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
or equivalently that we have
\displaystyle l_{1},l_{2}\geq 0
. The conditions
\displaystyle l_{1}=l_{2}
and
\displaystyle v_{1}\equiv v_{2}\,(\text{mod }(1+p))
are equivalent to there being an equality of ideals
\displaystyle(pX,X-u_{1})=(pX,X-u_{2})
, so we see that
\displaystyle u_{1}
and
\displaystyle u_{2}
induce the same evaluation map
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(U)(p^{\prime})\to\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})(p^{\prime})
if and only if we have
\displaystyle Z\cap x_{1}=Z\cap x_{2}
.

We also have the following example in which we evaluate ‘obvious elements’ in the Brauer group of a variety.

Example 1.2
.

Let
\displaystyle U
be a variety over
\displaystyle k_{v}
, which embeds as an open subset in a variety
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
over
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{v}
. Let
\displaystyle f,g
be elements of
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{X})
that become invertible in
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(U)
. Denote by abuse of notation for any scheme
\displaystyle Y
and for any positive integer
\displaystyle n
by
\displaystyle N_{n}
the norm map
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(Y(\zeta_{n}))\to\mathrm{Br}(Y)
. For any
\displaystyle p\nmid n
the cyclic algebra
\displaystyle N_{n}(\{f,g\}_{\zeta_{n}})
is an element of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(U)
. Evaluating such an element at a point
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
gives us the cyclic algebra
\displaystyle N_{n}(\{f(u),g(u)\}_{\zeta_{n}})
which is an element of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})
. Write
\displaystyle f(u)=v_{f}\cdot\pi_{v}^{m_{f}}
and write
\displaystyle g(u)=v_{g}\cdot\pi_{v}^{m_{g}}
for
\displaystyle v_{f},v_{g}\in\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
. Under the residue isomorphism
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{v},\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
the element
\displaystyle N_{n}(\{f(u),g(u)\}_{\zeta_{n}})
is mapped to
\displaystyle N_{\mathbb{F}_{v}(\zeta_{n})/\mathbb{F}_{v}}[(-1)^{m_{f}m_{g}}\frac{v_{f}^{m_{g}}}{v_{g}^{m_{f}}}]_{\zeta_{n}}
, where with
\displaystyle[-]
we mean the class in
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{v}(\zeta_{n}),\mu_{n})
.

Assume that
\displaystyle u
lifts to
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
and so we have
\displaystyle m_{f},m_{g}\geq 0
. Denote for any
\displaystyle h\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{X})
by
\displaystyle Z_{h}
the closed subscheme
\displaystyle V(\pi_{v}\cdot h)
of
\displaystyle\mathcal{X}
.
Like in the previous example we have that the expression
\displaystyle v_{f}\cdot\pi_{v}^{m_{f}}
(resp.
\displaystyle v_{g}\cdot\pi_{v}^{m_{g}}
) is determined by
\displaystyle Z_{f}\cap x
(resp.
\displaystyle Z_{g}\cap x
) up to multiplying
\displaystyle v_{f}
(resp.
\displaystyle v_{g}
) by an element in
\displaystyle(1+\pi_{v})\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}
.
So therefore the evaluation of
\displaystyle N_{n}(\{f,g\}_{\zeta_{n}})
under a point
\displaystyle u\in U(k_{v})
corresponding to
\displaystyle x\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{v})
depends only on the intersection
\displaystyle x\cap Z_{f}
in
\displaystyle Z_{f}
and on the intersection
\displaystyle x\cap Z_{g}
in
\displaystyle Z_{g}
.
This in turn is determined by
\displaystyle x\cap Z_{fg}
inside
\displaystyle Z_{fg}
.
In particular, for
\displaystyle V:=\mathcal{X}\setminus Z_{fg}
and for
\displaystyle B
the subgroup of
\displaystyle\mathrm{Br}(V)
spanned by elements of the form
\displaystyle N_{n}\{f^{i},g^{j}\}_{\zeta_{n}}
, we have gained insight in the pairing
\displaystyle B\times V(k_{v})\to\mathrm{Br}(k_{v})
.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let
\displaystyle F,G:D^{+}(X_{\text{ét}},\Lambda)\to D^{+}(X_{\text{ét}},\Lambda)
be represented by
\displaystyle K^{\bullet},L^{\bullet}
respectively. There is a canonical isomorphism of groups
\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(F,G)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{+}}(L^{\bullet},K^{\bullet})
.

\displaystyle A^{\bullet}

\displaystyle B^{\bullet}

\displaystyle R\Gamma(X,\mathrm{R}\mathbf{Hom}(A^{\bullet},B^{\bullet}))=\mathrm{RHom}(A^{\bullet},B^{\bullet})

\displaystyle h^{0}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{D^{+}}(A^{\bullet},B^{\bullet})

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathrm{R}\mathbf{Hom}(K^{\bullet},-)\to\mathrm{R}\mathbf{Hom}(L^{\bullet},-)

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle D^{+}(X_{\text{ét}},\Lambda)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\bullet}

Before we prove the main result we need an auxiliary lemma on compatibility of cycle classes with base-change. We assume that it is well-known, but we could not find a proof in the literature.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\displaystyle\varphi:V\to S
be a finite type morphism of Noetherian
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[1/n]
-schemes and let
\displaystyle W\subset S
be a local complete intersection of codimension
\displaystyle c
. Write
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{|W|}(S,\Lambda(c))\to\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{|W\times_{S}V|}(T,\Lambda(c))
for the pullback map. Assume that
\displaystyle V\times_{S}W
is a local complete intersection of codimension
\displaystyle c
in
\displaystyle V
. Then we have the equality
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\mathrm{cl}(W)=\mathrm{cl}(V\times_{S}W)
.

Proof.

We first go back to how the cohomology class associated to a codimension
\displaystyle c
local complete intersection
\displaystyle\alpha:W\hookrightarrow S
is defined. This is explained in
[
7
, p.140]
and we follow this explanation. A priori the cycle class will live in
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{0}(S,R^{2c}\alpha^{!}\Lambda(c))
. This last term turns out to be
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{|W|}(S,\Lambda(c))
by the semi-purity theorem (see for example
[
3
, Lemma 5.4]
) combined with a spectral sequence.

We explain how to produce the element of
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{0}(S,R^{2c}\alpha^{!}\Lambda(c))
. Since the sheaf
\displaystyle R^{2c}\alpha^{!}\Lambda(c)
is the sheafification of the presheaf
\displaystyle T\mapsto\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{T\times_{S}W}(T,\Lambda(c))
, it suffices to give elements
\displaystyle s_{i}\in\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{S_{i}\cap W}(S_{i},\Lambda(c))
, where
\displaystyle(S_{i})_{i}
is an open cover of
\displaystyle S
, such that the
\displaystyle s_{i}
agree on overlaps. The agreeing on overlaps happens by construction, therefore we may shrink
\displaystyle S
so that
\displaystyle W
is cut out by
\displaystyle c
equations
\displaystyle f_{1},...,f_{c}\in\mathcal{O}_{S}(S)
and we are asked to construct an element of
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{|W|}(S,\Lambda(c))
.

Let
\displaystyle D_{1},...,D_{c}
denote the closed subschemes of
\displaystyle S
given by
\displaystyle V(f_{1}),...,V(f_{c})
respectively. Denote the complement of
\displaystyle D_{i}
by
\displaystyle U_{i}
. In
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{0}(U_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m})
we have the element
\displaystyle f_{i}
. From the localisation exact sequence there is a connecting homomorphism
\displaystyle\delta:\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{0}(U_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}_{|D_{i}|}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m})
and associated to the Kummer sequence there is another connecting homomorphism
\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}:\mathrm{H}^{1}_{|D_{i}|}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|D_{i}|}(S,\Lambda(1))
. We set
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(D_{i})
to be
\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}(\delta(f_{i}))
. By using the ideas in
[
7
, Cycle 1.2]
, we see that there is a cup product
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{|D_{1}|}^{*}(S)\otimes...\otimes\mathrm{H}_{|D_{m}|}^{*}(S)\to\mathrm{H}^{*}_{|D_{1}\cap...\cap D_{m}|}(S)
. Thus we get a section
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(W)\in\mathrm{H}^{2c}_{|W|}(S,\Lambda(c))
by setting
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(W):=\mathrm{cl}(D_{1})\cup...\cup\mathrm{cl}(D_{c})
.

We use the above description of the cohomology class of a local complete intersection to prove the lemma. Since the construction of the cycle classes is local, we may already assume that we are in the situation at the end of the third paragraph of this proof, so we have already shrunk
\displaystyle S
. Let
\displaystyle V_{i}
denote the open subscheme
\displaystyle V\setminus(D_{i}\cap V)
of
\displaystyle V
. The pullback homomorphisms
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}:\mathrm{H}^{0}(U_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\mathrm{H}^{0}(V_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m})
sends
\displaystyle f_{i}
to
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(f_{i})
.

The pullback homomorphism
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}
is compatible with connecting homomorphisms (this follows from the fact that these connecting homomorphisms come from distinguished triangles and pullback is induced by
\displaystyle\eta_{\varphi}:\mathrm{Id}\to R\varphi_{*}\varphi^{*}
). Moreover
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}
is compatible with cup products (because the cup product is induced by the tensor product
\displaystyle\Gamma_{|D_{i}|}(S,\mathcal{F})\otimes\Gamma_{|D_{j}|}(S,\mathcal{G})\to\Gamma_{|D_{i}\cap D_{j}|}(S,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{G})
and the tensor product is compatible with
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}
. These observations lead us to the equality
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\mathrm{cl}(W)=\varphi^{*}\mathrm{cl}(D_{1})\cup...\cup\varphi^{*}\mathrm{cl}(D_{c})
, where we have
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}\mathrm{cl}(D_{1})=\delta^{\prime}(\delta(\varphi^{*}(f_{i}))
.

By the assumption in the lemma we have that
\displaystyle W\times_{S}V
is a local complete intersection of codimension
\displaystyle c
in
\displaystyle W
, so one can make sense of the cohomology class
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(W\times_{S}V)
. Moreover it is clear that
\displaystyle W\times_{S}V
is cut out by the equations
\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(f_{1}),...,\varphi^{*}(f_{c})
in
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{V}(V)
. By using the end of the previous paragraph we see that
\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(W\times_{S}V)=\varphi^{*}(\mathrm{cl}(W))
holds.
∎

Example 3.13
.

We return to Example
1.1
one last time, so we have
\displaystyle Z=\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[t]/(tp))
with components
\displaystyle\mathrm{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{Sp}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
and in this situation
\displaystyle|B|
corresponds to the maximal ideal
\displaystyle(p,t)
. We wish to show that whenever
\displaystyle\gcd(n,p-1)\neq 1
, the map
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|B|}(Z,\mu_{n})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|B|}(\mathrm{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},\mu_{n})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|B|}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mu_{n})=(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{2}
is not injective. We use the localisation exact sequence for
\displaystyle(Z,Z\setminus|B|)
. By easy calculations we get

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(Z,\mu_{n})=\mathcal{O}(Z)^{\times}/(\mathcal{O}(Z)^{\times})^{n}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{n}

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(Z\setminus|B|,\mu_{n})=k_{v}^{\times}/(k_{v}^{\times})^{n}\oplus(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t,1/t])^{\times}/((\mathbb{F}_{p}[t,1/t])^{\times})^{n},

which is isomorphic to two copies of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}/n\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{n}
. The inclusion of
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(Z,\mu_{n})
into
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{1}(Z\setminus|B|,\mu_{n})
is identified with the diagonal inclusion of
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{n}
into its self-product (and the inclusion is zero on the
\displaystyle(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{2}
-component). It follows that the kernel of
\displaystyle\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|B|}(Z,\mu_{n})\to\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|B|}(\mathrm{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}},\mu_{n})\oplus\mathrm{H}^{2}_{|B|}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},\mu_{n})
has a subgroup isomorphic to
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}/(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times})^{n}
, which is nonzero by our assumption on
\displaystyle n
.

Suppose that the evaluation maps
\displaystyle u_{1}^{*}
and
\displaystyle u_{2}^{*}

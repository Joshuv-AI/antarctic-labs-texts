Collective dynamics in holographic fractonic solids
Ling-Zheng Xia 1, Lixin Xu 1, Wei-Jia Li 1

Abstract
Fractonic phases of matter, a class of states in which collective excitations with constrained mobility exist, have recently emerged as a novel avenue for ergodicity breaking and garnered broad interest in condensed matter physics. In this work, we consider a (3+1)-dimensional holographic model of fractonic solids and investigate the low-energy collective dynamics systematically. By computing the quasinormal modes of black holes, we obtain all the hydrodynamic excitations on the boundary, including two acoustic phonons, a longitudinal diffusive mode as well as a fractonic subdiffusive mode with the dispersion ω ∼ − i ​ k 4 \omega\sim-ik^{4} . In addition, it is found that the fracton mode remains gapless when translational symmetry is explicitly broken. These results suggest that fractonic excitations are inherently protected by the crystal-dipole symmetry in solids and are qualitatively unaffected by broken spacetime symmetries.

Introduction

Fractons, a novel type of collective excitations exhibiting constrained mobility, have attracted broad attention in the study of many-body quantum matter in recent years

;

;

Several reviews summarize developments in these areas (see

;

The effort to formulate a universal description of fracton phases has led to the development of the so-called fracton hydrodynamics. This framework has been built progressively, starting from simple systems with only charge and dipole conservation

, and later adding other key ingredients like momentum

The dipole symmetry of fractons is related to the breaking of continuous spatial translations
Pretko et al. 2020
;
Glorioso et al. 2023
;
Gromov 2019
. Since this symmetry breaking is a ubiquitous feature of solid systems, occurring either spontaneously or explicitly through mechanisms such as lattices, impurities, or defects

;
Grüner 1988
, solids serve as a crucial platform for the realization of fractons. However, embedding fractons within the solid-state context raises a natural question: how do the Goldstone modes of a solid (acoustic phonons) coexist and interact with the subdiffusive mode of a fractonic system? The study of fractonic solids will enrich our understanding of fractonic phases as well as the interplay between the dipole symmetry and spacetime symmetries.

During the past few decades, the AdS/CFT correspondence, or holographic duality, has become a powerful tool for tackling real-time dynamics of strongly correlated many-body systems

;
Baggioli 2019
;
Maldacena 1998
;
Witten 1998

;
Zaanen et al. 2015
. A key point for modeling properties of solids is to break the translational symmetry spontaneously. Among the various holographic systems implementing this symmetry breaking pattern, the holographic axion model stands out as perhaps the most economical. For a detailed review of this model, one can refer to

Inspired by recent theoretical developments, especially the hydrodynamic studies of collective modes in

In particular, we focus on the intricate interaction among the fracton, the energy-momentum tensor, and the acoustic phonons in solid systems.

II
Holographic model for fractonic solids

In this section, we construct a (3+1)-dimensional holographic model of fractonic solids guided by the framework outlined in the previous section. The holographic dictionary states that currents in the boundary theory should be sourced by the corresponding fields in the bulk. Therefore, one can impose the following map from the boundary to the bulk:

\tilde{\mathbb{A}}_{\mu}\mapsto A_{a},\quad\mathbb{A}^{I}_{\mu}\mapsto A_{a}^{I},

(15)

where the subscript ‘
a
’ denotes the spacetime index in the bulk. To break the translational symmetry,
we further introduce a set of massless bulk scalars
\{\Phi^{I},\,I=1,2\}
(which are also called ‘axion’ fields in much of the literature) possessing an internal shift symmetry
\Phi^{I}\to\Phi^{I}+c^{I}

Then, the bulk action can be written as
Jain 2024

S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg[R-2\Lambda-2m^{2}V(X)-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}_{ab}\mathcal{F}^{ab}-\frac{1}{2}F^{I}_{ab}F^{Iab}\bigg],

(16)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar,
\Lambda
is the negative cosmological constant,
m^{2}
is an effective coupling with the dimension of mass squared, and

\mathcal{F}_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}A_{b]}+2A^{I}_{[a}\nabla_{b]}\Phi^{I},\quad F^{I}_{ab}=2\nabla_{[a}A^{I}_{b]}.

(17)

\displaystyle\bar{\Phi}^{I}=\delta_{i}^{I}x^{i}.

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=\dfrac{1}{u^{2}}[-f(u)dt^{2}-2dtdu+dx^{2}+dy^{2}],

\displaystyle\bar{A}=\bar{A}_{t}(u)dt,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\bar{A}_{t}(u)=\mu-\rho u,\quad\bar{A}^{I}=0.

III
Hydrodynamic modes

Having established our holographic model, we now turn to the main objective of this work: studying low-energy collective excitations in a fractonic solid. The hydrodynamic modes of the boundary theory are encoded in the quasinormal modes (QNMs) of the black hole, which correspond to the poles of the system’s retarded correlators. To extract these modes, we consider small perturbations around the background solution for each of these fields,

g_{ab}=\bar{g}_{ab}+\delta g_{ab},\quad\Phi^{I}=\bar{\Phi}^{I}+\delta\Phi^{I},\quad A_{a}=\bar{A}_{a}+\delta A_{a},\quad A^{I}_{a}=\bar{A}^{I}_{a}+\delta A^{I}_{a},

where the fluctuating fields can be expanded as

\displaystyle\delta g_{ab}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k\,\mathrm{d}\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\dfrac{h_{ab}(\omega,k_{i},u)}{u^{2}}\,e^{-i\omega t+ik_{i}x^{i}},

(27)

\displaystyle\delta\Phi^{I}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k\,\mathrm{d}\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\delta^{I}_{j}\,\Phi_{j}(\omega,k_{i},u)\,e^{-i\omega t+ik_{i}x^{i}},

\displaystyle\delta A_{a}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k\,\mathrm{d}\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,a_{a}(\omega,k_{i},u)\,e^{-i\omega t+ik_{i}x^{i}},

\displaystyle\delta A^{I}_{a}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}k\,\mathrm{d}\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,a^{I}_{a}(\omega,k_{i},u)\,e^{-i\omega t+ik_{i}x^{i}}.

Due to the rotational invariance of the bulk geometry in the
x-y
plane, we align the wave vector
k
along the
y-
direction without loss of generality. This choice causes the linearized fluctuations to split into two sectors according to parity under
x\to-x

\displaystyle\text{Transverse sector (parity odd):}\quad\quad\,\{

\displaystyle\delta g_{xt},\delta g_{xy},\delta g_{xu},\delta\Phi^{x},\delta A_{x},\delta A_{x}^{y},\delta A^{x}_{y},\delta A^{x}_{t},\delta A^{x}_{u}\},

(28)

\displaystyle\text{Longitudinal sector (parity even):}\quad\{

\displaystyle\delta g_{uu},\delta g_{tu},\delta g_{yu},\delta g_{tt},\delta g_{ty},\delta g_{xx},\delta g_{yy},\delta\Phi^{y},\delta A_{y},\delta A_{t},\delta A_{u},

\displaystyle\delta A^{y}_{y},\delta A^{y}_{t},\delta A^{y}_{u},\delta A_{x}^{x}\}.

By solving the coupled linearized EOMs for both sectors (see Appendix
A
for details), subject to the physical boundary conditions of being purely infalling at the horizon and normalizable (sourceless) at the asymptotic boundary, we obtain the QNM spectra of the black hole for finite frequency
\omega

\Lambda=-3
so that the AdS radius is unity and set the event horizon radius

IV
Discussion and outlook

In this work, we investigate the collective dynamics in a holographic model of fractonic solids by computing the low-lying QNMs in the bulk. Our results show that, for a fractonic solid system, the combination of charge conservation and crystal-dipole conservation generates a collective fractonic subdiffusive mode, which supplants the usual picture of charge diffusion in systems with a single monopole charge conservation. This new mode coexists with the acoustic phonons and the crystal diffusion in solids. Furthermore, the fracton influences the dynamics of the other modes only through their dissipative sectors. The coupling between the fracton and the solid background results in a non-monotonic temperature dependence of the fracton’s subdiffusivity. Another result of our work concerns the stability of the fractonic mode. It is found that this mode remains gapless even when translations are explicitly broken, reflecting the robustness of such excitations in dirty solid systems.

Despite this work marking a step forward in our understanding of fractonic solids, a comprehensive picture of fractonic phases remains far from complete. For instance, an exploration of the non-hydrodynamic modes would be necessary to understand how the system behaves in the high-energy regime. This model can also be generalized to include the conservation of multipole moments up to the order
n
, which is expected to yield a low-energy dispersion relation of the form
\omega\sim-i\,k^{2(n+1)}
. Another appealing direction is to extend this holographic approach to other exotic phases, such as fractonic superfluids and supersolids
Yuan et al. 2020
;
Jain 2024
;
Jain et al. 2023
;

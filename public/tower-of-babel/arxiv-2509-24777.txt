Comment on “Strong lensing and shadow of Ayon-Beato-Garcia (ABG) nonsingular black hole”
M. Fahmi Fauzi

Abstract
It was suggested by Ramadhan et al. (Eur Phys J C 83:465, 2023) that the Sagittarius A* black hole cannot be modeled by an Ayón-Beato-Garcìa regular black hole due to the incompatible shadow radius. However, they overlooked the distant-observer limit of the correction term in the effective geometry, which renders their calculation invalid. When evaluated correctly, the result reverses the conclusion of the paper. This comment highlights the flaw and demonstrates how to properly compute the shadow radius of such a black hole model using elementary geometry.



A recent study suggests that the Sagittarius A* black hole (BH) cannot be modeled as a regular black hole (RBH) sourced by Ayón-Beato-Garcìa (ABG) nonlinear electrodynamics (NLED)
Ramadhan:2023ogm
. Their calculation indicates that, when incorporating the photon effective geometry arising from NLED
Novello2000
, the resulting shadow radius lies well below the
2\sigma
and
1\sigma
constraints from the Event Horizon Telescope combined with Keck and VLTI observational estimates obtained by Ref.
Vagnozzi:2022moj
(see also Ref.
EventHorizonTelescope:2022xqj
). The same conclusion was reached even for the uncharged case, which may appear reasonable given that the NLED Lagrangian does not reduce to Maxwell electrodynamics. However, an overlooked step in their shadow radius calculation renders the result invalid.

Let me briefly review the formalism and method used in Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
to obtain the shadow radius of the BH. The seed BH metric is the Bardeen RBH
bardeen
, given by the line element

ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(1)

where

f(r)=1-\frac{2Mr^{2}}{(r^{2}+q^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(2)

Ayón-Beato and Garcìa
Ayon-Beato:2000mjt
showed that the Bardeen RBH solution is in fact a solution of the Einstein field equations when a NLED Lagrangian is incorporated, given by

\mathcal{L}=\frac{3}{2sq^{2}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2q^{2}F}}{1+\sqrt{2q^{2}F}}\right)^{\frac{5}{2}},

(3)

where
F=F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4
,
s=|q|/2M
, and
q
is associated with the charge parameter. Note that this Lagrangian does not reduce to Maxwell electrodynamics in the weak-field limit.

It was proposed by Novello
et al.

Novello2000
that a NLED Lagrangian may induce an effective geometry for photons, modifying the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
to

g^{\mu\nu}_{\text{eff}}=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{4\mathcal{L}_{FF}}{\mathcal{L}_{F}}F_{\alpha}^{\mu}F^{\alpha\nu},

(4)

where
\mathcal{L}_{F}\equiv\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial F
. With the Lagrangian of Eq. (
3
), the effective line element for photons becomes

ds^{2}_{\text{eff}}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+h(r)r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(5)

which introduces a correction factor
h(r)
in the two-sphere element, given by

h(r)=\left[1-\frac{2(6q^{2}-r^{2})}{(q^{2}+r^{2})}\right]^{-1}.

(6)

One can see that for
q=0
, the effective geometry does not reduce to the Schwarzschild spacetime as
h(r)|_{q=0}=1/3
. Hereafter, I will refer to the effective geometry in Eq. (
5
) as the
ABGBH
spacetime.

R_{sh}
. The black filled circle represents the event horizon of the BH, and the red circle around it denotes the photon sphere. The red dot at

The geodesic equation in Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
is presented by Eq. (14) of that paper. For convenience, however, let me instead use the form (see, e.g., Refs.
Fauzi:2024nta
;
Fauzi:2025rcc
;
Rohim:2025gxo
)

\frac{d\phi}{dr}=\frac{1}{h(r)r^{2}}\left[b^{-2}-V(r)\right]^{-1/2},\quad V(r)=\frac{f(r)}{h(r)r^{2}},

(7)

where
V(r)
is the photon effective potential and
b\equiv L/E
is the impact parameter, with
L
and
E
being conserved quantities identified as the test particle’s angular momentum and energy, respectively. It can be readily verified that Eq. (14) of Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
is equivalent to Eq. (
7
) used here.

A key quantity for computing the shadow radius is the critical impact parameter
b_{c}
, defined as the impact parameter of a photon orbiting the BH’s photon sphere. The radius of the photon sphere
r_{ps}
can be determined by locating the extremum of the effective potential,

\left.\partial_{r}V(r)\right|_{r=r{ps}}=0,

(8)

and the critical impact parameter is then given by

b_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{V(r_{ps})}}=r_{ps}\sqrt{\frac{h(r_{ps})}{f(r_{ps})}}.

(9)

Both relations are stated in Eqs. (16) and (52) of Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
.

Let me now present the basic concept of how the shadow radius can be computed using elementary geometry. In Fig.
1
, I illustrate the schematic setup of the scenario. In short, the angle
\Theta
—identified as the
angular radius
of the BH shadow—is given by (see, e.g., Refs.
Perlick:2021aok
;
Perlick:2018iye
)

\displaystyle\tan\Theta=

\displaystyle\frac{R_{sh}}{r_{O}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\Delta x\to 0}\frac{\Delta z}{\Delta x}\equiv\left.\sqrt{\frac{h(r)r^{2}}{f(r)}}\frac{d\phi}{dr}\right|_{r=r_{O}}\;(b=b_{c}).

(10)

Substituting Eq. (
7
), one obtains

R_{sh}=r_{O}b_{c}\sqrt{\frac{f(r_{O})}{h(r_{O})r_{O}^{2}-b_{c}^{2}f(r_{O})}}.

(11)

In the limit of a distant observer (
r_{O}\to\infty
), if the spacetime is asymptotically flat,
i.e.
,
\lim_{r\to\infty}f(r)=1
and
\lim_{r\to\infty}h(r)=1
, Eq. (
11
) reduces to

R_{sh}=b_{c},

(12)

which was used in Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
to compute the shadow radius.

1\sigma

2\sigma

While this relation has also been used in numerous studies (Ref.
Vagnozzi:2022moj
in particular), it is not valid within the ABGBH spacetime. The condition
\lim_{r\to\infty}h(r)=1
does not hold for
h(r)
in Eq. (
6
). In fact, one finds

\lim_{r\to\infty}h(r)=\frac{1}{3},

(13)

and the shadow radius observed by a distant observer simply becomes

R_{sh}=b_{c}\sqrt{3},

(14)

which introduces a factor of
\sqrt{3}
that is missing in Eq. (
12
). I compare the properly evaluated shadow radius with that obtained in Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
in Fig.
2
. It can be directly seen that the shadow radius of the ABGBH lies within the Sagittarius A*
2\sigma
constraint for
q/M\lesssim 0.7
, and in the limit
q=0
, the Schwarzschild BH shadow radius is recovered.

Furthermore, it is plausible that the
\sqrt{3}
scaling also extends to other observables, in particular the innermost image
\theta_{\infty}
and the magnification
r_{m}
. One can see that for
q/M=0
, the value of
\theta_{\infty}
and
r_{m}
for the ABGBH reported in Table 1 of Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
coincide with those of the Schwarzschild BH once rescaled by the
\sqrt{3}
factor. However, a more complete treatment is required to validate this speculation.

In conclusion, the shadow radius—and possibly other strong lensing observables—of ABGBH reported in Ref.
Ramadhan:2023ogm
is inaccurate. A proper evaluation shows that the Sagittarius A* BH can indeed be consistent with the ABGBH model, thereby reversing the original claim.

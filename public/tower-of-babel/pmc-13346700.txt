Metabolite-mediated enhancement of drought resilience in north-western Himalayan apple rootstocks through synergistic modulation of physiological performance
Authors: Shireen Khatri, Dharam Paul Sharma, Nitin Sharma, Pramod Kumar, Richa Rana, Rahul Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMetabolite-based interventions represent a promising strategy to mitigate water deficit-induced damage in perennial fruit crops such as apple (Malus × domestica Borkh.). This study evaluated the efficacy of two plant-derived metabolites**, melatonin (MT) and myo-inositol (MI), ** in enhancing drought resilience across five apple rootstocks (MM 111, MM 106, Bud 118, MM 116 and M9) under controlled greenhouse conditions. A two-phase experimental framework was used. In Phase I, dose optimization was performed under 40% field capacity using Response Index (RI) and Comprehensive Response Index (CRI) to identify the most effective metabolite combination. Among six treatments, T3 (100 µM MT + 100 µM MI) had the lowest CRI values, indicating improved physiological performance under moisture deficit. In Phase II, optimized treatments were evaluated under drought (40% FC) and irrigated (100% FC) regimes. Drought stress significantly impaired plant physiological functioning, reducing relative water content, chlorophyll stability, and gas exchange parameters. However, the combined application of MT + MI (T8: 100 µM each) significantly alleviated these effects by enhancing water retention, maintaining membrane stability, and improving photosynthetic efficiency. The most pronounced positive responses were observed in Bud 118 and MM 111, showing a strong genotype × treatment interaction. Notably, MT + MI also improved physiological performance under irrigated conditions, suggesting broader benefits for overall plant health. Each treatment was evaluated using three replications across five rootstocks, ensuring statistical reliability. Collectively, these results demonstrate the synergistic role of melatonin and myo-inositol in reinforcing drought tolerance. While the findings are based on controlled greenhouse conditions, they provide a mechanistic basis for future field validation and development of metabolite-assisted strategies for drought resilience in apple.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-50604-0.Keywords: Plant metabolites, Melatonin, Myo-inositol, Biostimulant, Abiotic stress, Drought and growthSubject terms: Physiology, Plant sciences

Introduction

Anthropogenic changes in global climate patterns, reflected in rising temperatures, irregular precipitation, and increased atmospheric evaporative demand, have intensified the occurrence and severity of drought worldwide1. These changes pose serious challenges to perennial fruit crops, as their long growth cycles and dependence on stable soil moisture increase their sensitivity to water stress. Apple (Malus × domestica Borkh.), one of the most economically important temperate fruit crops, is especially sensitive to water limitation owing to its shallow root system, extended phenophases, and high-water requirements2.

In the Indian Himalayas, especially Himachal Pradesh, which contributes more than 80% of India’s apple production, prolonged dry spells, reduced snowfall, and disrupted monsoon patterns are increasingly affecting flowering consistency, fruit set, and orchard productivity3. Drought-induced water deficits impair key physiological processes in apple, including stomatal conductance, photosynthetic efficiency, osmotic adjustment, and redox homeostasis4–7. Apple trees respond through stomatal closure, altered carbon allocation, enhanced root proliferation, and accumulation of compatible solutes such as proline, sugars, and organic acids to maintain cellular homeostasis under moisture stress6. Central to these responses is the abscisic acid (ABA)-mediated signalling pathway that regulates drought perception and stress adaptation8,9.

Rootstock genotype plays a crucial role in modulating these responses by influencing water uptake efficiency, hormonal balance, and vegetative vigor10. These changes pose serious challenges to perennial fruit crops, as their long growth cycles and dependence on stable soil moisture increase their sensitivity to water stress. Metabolite-based biostimulants offer a promising approach for enhancing stress tolerance. Primary and secondary metabolites contribute to osmotic adjustment and antioxidant defence11–13, however, their applied role in perennial fruit systems remains comparatively underexplored.

Among these, melatonin and myo-inositol have gained attention for their complementary roles in drought adaptation. Melatonin enhances photosynthetic efficiency, regulates redox balance, and improves water-use efficiency14–16, whereas myo-inositol contributes to osmotic regulation and membrane stability17. Given these advantages, this study focuses on the combined application of melatonin and myo-inositol to improve drought tolerance in apple rootstocks.

The working hypothesis of this study is that the combined application of melatonin and myo-inositol will improve drought tolerance more effectively than individual treatments by enhancing physiological stability and reducing stress-induced damage, with responses varying among rootstocks.

To address this, a two-phase experimental framework was employed. In Phase I, different dose combinations were evaluated under 40% field capacity using Response Index (RI) and Comprehensive Response Index (CRI) to identify the most effective treatment. In Phase II, the optimized dose was assessed under both drought (40% FC) and irrigated (100% FC) conditions, focusing on key physiological traits such as relative water content, gas exchange, and photosynthetic performance.

Despite growing evidence supporting the individual roles of melatonin and myo-inositol, limited information is available on their combined dose optimisation and genotype-specific responses in perennial fruit crops, particularly under Himalayan conditions. Most studies are restricted to annual crops or single genotypes and are conducted under controlled environments that do not fully represent field conditions. Furthermore, integrative screening approaches such as RI and CRI have not been widely applied in perennial systems. Therefore, the objective of this study was to evaluate the combined effects of melatonin and myo-inositol on drought tolerance in apple and to determine the extent to which these responses are influenced by rootstock genotype.

Materials and methods

Experimental site and growth conditions

The experiment was conducted during the summer growing season (June–July) at the Experimental Block of the Department of Fruit Science, Dr. Y.S. Parmar University of Horticulture and Forestry, Nauni, Solan, Himachal Pradesh, India. The site is located in a sub-temperate mid-Himalayan region at 30°52′N and 77°11′E, with an elevation of 1275 m above mean sea level, and experiences high evaporative demand during summer. During this period, the mean ambient maximum and minimum air temperatures in the region were 33.5 °C and 19.4 °C, respectively, while mean soil temperatures ranged from 17.2 to 21.9 °C, as recorded at the nearby meteorological observatory.

The greenhouse was covered with polycarbonate sheets, providing protection from direct rainfall while allowing natural solar radiation. Inside the greenhouse, the maximum air temperature reached approximately 38 °C, and relative humidity was maintained at around 48% during the experimental period. Light intensity was natural, and plants were grown under the natural summer photoperiod prevailing at the experimental location. Regular ventilation was provided to prevent excessive heat buildup and to maintain relatively stable growing conditions. This protected environment minimized abrupt external climatic fluctuations, allowing drought responses to be primarily attributed to soil moisture regimes and treatment effects rather than uncontrolled environmental variability.

Experimental design and treatment details

A two-phase controlled-environment experiment was conducted to evaluate the individual and combined effects of the plant metabolites melatonin and myo-inositol on drought tolerance in apple (Malus × domestica Borkh.) rootstocks. The study utilized five commercially important and genetically diverse apple rootstocks: MM 111 (R1), MM 106 (R2), Bud 118 (R3), M116 (R4), and M9 (R5), selected for their contrasting vigour, rooting behaviour, and reported field performance under variable moisture conditions in the Western Himalayan apple production system. All experimental plant material consisted of commercially released apple rootstocks procured from the officially maintained nursery stock of the Department of Fruit Science, Dr. Y.S. Parmar University of Horticulture and Forestry, Solan, India, and formally identified by departmental experts using standard horticultural descriptors and institutional records. No wild, endangered, or protected plant species were involved; therefore, specific collection permits, herbarium voucher deposition, or additional field licenses were not required, and the study complied with relevant institutional guidelines.

The experiment used rootstock plants that were all the same age and healthy. At the start of the experiment, plants had an average height ranging from 100 to 150 cm, ensuring homogeneity of planting material and minimizing size-related variability. Plants were grown individually in 7.2 kg polybags filled with pre-analysed soil and maintained under controlled polycarbonate house conditions. Basal nutrient supply was provided using farmyard manure (FYM) along with recommended doses of calcium ammonium nitrate (CAN), single super phosphate (SSP), and muriate of potash (MOP) to avoid nutrient limitation and ensure normal vegetative growth throughout the experimental period.

The experiment followed a factorial completely randomized design (FCRD).

Each treatment consisted of five rootstocks with three replications, and each replication included three plants, resulting in 45 experimental units per treatment. The study was structured across two consecutive phases: (i) optimization of metabolite concentrations under drought stress, and (ii) detailed assessment of physiological responses under differential moisture regimes across two growing seasons (2023 and 2024).

In Phase I, six metabolite treatments were evaluated under drought conditions maintained at 40% field capacity (FC) using a gravimetric irrigation method. The treatments comprised different combinations of melatonin and myo-inositol: T1 (100 µM melatonin + 20 µM myo-inositol), T2 (100 µM melatonin + 50 µM myo-inositol), T3 (100 µM melatonin + 100 µM myo-inositol), T4 (100 µM melatonin + 150 µM myo-inositol), T5 (100 µM melatonin + 200 µM myo-inositol), and T6 (unstressed control at 100% FC without metabolite application). Drought stress was imposed in 7.2 kg polybags, and soil moisture was maintained through weekly gravimetric irrigation based on measured water loss, corresponding to 40% field capacity (0.8 L water) compared with 100% field capacity (2.0 L water), ensuring a consistent and reproducible drought stress level (Fig. 7).

Optimizing the doses of exogenous melatonin and myo-inositol on apple rootstocks under drought stress at an interval of 5, 10, 15 DAT

Foliar application of melatonin and myo-inositol was carried out on 7 and 9 May, coinciding with the point at which plants had attained a physiologically stabilized drought level (40% FC). This timing was selected to minimize variability associated with transient soil moisture fluctuations and to ensure uniform stress conditions at the time of treatment application. Two consecutive foliar sprays were used to improve the uptake of metabolites and maintain effective physiological concentrations during the early drought response phase, which is critical for activating antioxidant defense mechanisms, osmotic regulation, and stomatal adjustment (Fig.7).

Physiological observations were recorded at 5-day intervals following treatment application to standardize response assessment, capture temporal dynamics of drought adaptation, and enable reliable comparison among treatments and rootstocks. Key gas exchange parameters, including net photosynthetic rate (Pn), stomatal conductance (gs), transpiration rate (Tr), intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci), and instantaneous water use efficiency (iWUE), were measured at three successive intervals to evaluate treatment performance and identify the optimal metabolite dose (Fig. 1).

Comprehensive Response Index (CRI) for standardisation of doses of melatonin and myo-inositol across three days i.e. 5DAT, 10DAT AND 15 DAT in apple rootstocks under drought stress.

Based on the integrated physiological response analysis, the most effective metabolite combinations were selected for Phase II, which consisted of a full factorial experiment under two soil moisture regimes: well-watered (100% FC) and drought-stressed (40% FC). Eight treatment combinations were evaluated: T1 (control, irrigated), T5 (control, stressed), T2 and T6 (myo-inositol alone), T3 and T7 (melatonin alone), and T4 and T8 (melatonin + myo-inositol). Each treatment was applied to five apple rootstocks, with three biological replicates per treatment. Physiological measurements were recorded at 7-day intervals during the drought period in both 2023 and 2024, ensuring robust temporal and statistical validation.

This formulation was selected because it provides a dimensionless, normalized measure of deviation from the control, allowing integration of parameters with different units and scales. Compared to ratio-based or percentage change methods, this approach ensures consistent interpretation, where values closer to zero indicate better maintenance of physiological function under stress.

The RI was calculated for the following physiological parameters: net photosynthetic rate (Pn), stomatal conductance (gs), transpiration rate (Tr), and intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci), all measured using a portable infrared gas analyser (IRGA, CID-340). In addition, intrinsic water use efficiency (iWUE = Pn/gs) was also included as a derived parameter, representing the balance between carbon assimilation and water loss.

A lower CRI value indicates minimal overall deviation from the control and thus higher stress tolerance, whereas higher CRI values indicate greater physiological disruption.

Parameter analysed

Physiological parameters

Physiological parameters were analysed to understand the plant’s adaptive responses to drought stress under different bio-stimulant treatments. Leaf relative water content (RWC) was estimated following the method RWC (%) = Fresh weight − Dry weight × 100/Turgid weight − Dry weight of Barrs and Weatherley19. Fully developed leaves from the top third of the plant canopy were harvested, packed in polythene bags, and immediately weighed to record their fresh weight. The leaves were then immersed in distilled water in Petri plates for 3 h before re-weighing. To determine dry weight, the leaves were oven-dried at 85 °C for 72 h or until a constant weight was achieved. The relative water content (RWC) was then calculated as a percentage.

Chlorophyll content was recorded using a portable chlorophyll meter (SPAD-502 Plus, Minolta, Japan) to assess variations in photosynthetic pigment concentration. Net photosynthetic rate (NPR, Pn) (µmol CO₂ m−2 s−1), stomatal conductance (SC, gs) (mmol m−2 s−1), transpiration rate (mmol H₂O m−2 s−1), and intrinsic CO₂ concentration (Ci) (ppm) were measured using a portable infrared gas analyser (IRGA, CID-340). Intrinsic water use efficiency (iWUE) was also determined as per the formula- iWUE = (Net photosynthetic rate (Pn))/ (Stomatal conductance (gs)) given by Dbara et al.20. Gas exchange measurements were recorded between 09:00 and 11:00 h to minimize diurnal variability. This mid-morning window was selected because photosynthesis, stomatal conductance, and transpiration are relatively stable during this period, ensuring reliable comparison among treatments and rootstock genotypes.

Canopy temperature was recorded as the difference between air temperature and plant canopy temperature, following the method of Hou et al.21. Electrolyte leakage (EL) was measured to assess membrane stability under drought conditions using the method- EL(%) = L1/L2 × 100 described by Lutts et al.22. Fresh leaves were harvested and placed in sealed glass tubes containing 10 ml of deionized water, incubated at 25 °C for 24 h on a rotary shaker, and the initial electrical conductivity (L₁) of the solution was recorded. The leaf samples were then autoclaved at 121 °C for 20 min to obtain the final electrical conductivity (L₂). Measurements were performed under uniform greenhouse conditions on fully expanded, healthy leaves, and care was taken to maintain consistent environmental conditions during data acquisition. Standardizing the measurement timing ensured reliable comparison among treatments and rootstock genotypes and improved the accuracy of physiological response assessment under drought stress.

Statistical analysis

The experiment was conducted in a factorial completely randomized design (FCRD) with each treatment combination replicated three times. Prior to analysis of variance (ANOVA), the data were tested for normality and homogeneity of variance Levene’s test, respectively, to ensure compliance with parametric test assumptions. For comparison of main effects and interaction effects, treatment means were separated using Duncan’s Multiple Range Test (DMRT) at a significance level of p ≤ 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using the open-source software RStudio (version 4.5.2).

Results

Response Index (RI) and Comprehensive Response Index (CRI) for selection or standardisation of optimum dose

Analysis of the Comprehensive Response Index (CRI) showed that the lowest values, indicative of higher treatment efficacy, were consistently and significantly (p < 0.05) observed under treatment dose T3 across all apple rootstocks and evaluation time points. This statistically supported and consistent trend indicates that T3 was the most effective dose for improving drought tolerance in apple rootstocks. Notably, MM 111 and Bud 118 exhibited the strongest responses at 10 days after treatment (DAT), recording CRI values of − 1.0855 and 0.1186, respectively. Other rootstocks, including MM 106, M116, and M9, also showed significantly reduced CRI values under T3 at 10 DAT, indicating consistent treatment effects across genotypes.

Temporal analysis showed that CRI values were significantly lower at 10 DAT compared to 15 DAT (p < 0.05), suggesting that earlier assessment more effectively captured treatment responses. Furthermore, significant interaction effects among rootstock genotype, treatment dose, and time of assessment (p < 0.05) highlighted the genotype-specific and time-dependent nature of the response. Supporting these findings, heatmap visualizations (Fig. 1) illustrated the improved performance of T3 across multiple physiological and biochemical parameters.

Leaf gas exchange parameters

Gas-exchange traits changed significantly between rootstocks and treatments, including net photosynthetic rate (NPR, Pn), transpiration rate (Tr), stomatal conductance (SC, gs), intercellular CO₂ concentration (Ci), intrinsic water-use efficiency (iWUE), and canopy temperature depression (CTD), reflecting drought-induced physiological responses. Bud 118 consistently showed higher performance under stress conditions. At 7, 14, and 21 DAT, Bud 118 recorded the highest Pn values (15.93, 15.65, 15.34 μmol CO₂ m−2 s−1), 45.0–49.1% higher than M9 (Fig. 2). It also maintained the lowest Tr (2.93, 2.84, 2.63 mmol H₂O m−2 s−1) (Fig. 2), indicating efficient water use, while achieving higher gs (0.35, 0.33, 0.31 mol H₂O m−2 s−1) and Ci (334.46, 325.96, 315.71 ppm), over 50% and 60% greater than M9, respectively. iWUE in Bud 118 improved by 87.0 to 107.4%, and CTD (5.94 °C and 7.47 °C) exceeded M9 by 44.9% and 24.6%, indicating improved canopy cooling and physiological performance (Figs. 3, 4, 6).

Drought stress (T5) significantly reduced gas exchange parameters. Pn declined by 23.1–46.8%, Tr by 30.7–47.3%, and gs by 50.0–63.9% compared to T1, while Ci decreased by 20.4–36.5%. Although iWUE slightly increased (1.9–5%), this reflected a water-conservation response. CTD declined by 16.0%, indicating reduced evaporative cooling. Under drought conditions, T8 (melatonin + myo-inositol) improved these parameters, with Pn increasing by 35.3–70.9%, gs by 42.2–53.8%, Tr by 19.5–31.7%, and Ci by 13.2–20.0% compared to T5. iWUE increased by 7.3–27.6%, and CTD improved by 11.0–12.6%, indicating improved physiological functioning. Under irrigated conditions, T4 enhanced gas exchange compared to T1, with increases in Pn (15.4–24.1%), gs (18.7–29.4%), and Ci (~ 9%), along with moderate improvements in iWUE and CTD (Figs. 2, 3, 4, 6).

Cellular hydration (RWC) and membrane integrity (EL)

Relative water content (RWC) differed significantly among rootstocks, treatments, and sampling intervals (p ≤ 0.05). Bud 118 recorded the highest mean RWC (85.61%), whereas M9 showed the lowest (72.75%). Across 7, 14, and 21 DAT, Bud 118 maintained higher RWC values (90.06%, 85.71%, and 81.06%), 11.4–12.3% greater than M9. Among treatments, T4 (metabolite application under irrigated conditions) resulted in the highest RWC (95.27%). In contrast, drought control (T5) showed a decline from 75.1% to 56.5% over time. Under drought, T8 maintained higher RWC (84.87%, 73.77%, and 68.70%), improving values by up to 21.5% over T5 (Figs. 4, 5).

Influence of Plant metabolite application on electrolyte leakage (EL %) in apple rootstocks under drought stress

Electrolyte leakage (EL) showed an inverse trend to RWC and varied significantly among rootstocks and treatments (p ≤ 0.05). M9 recorded the highest EL values (up to 25.13%), whereas Bud 118 maintained the lowest (16.35%). Under drought (T5), EL increased from 20.2% to 34.2%, whereas T1 remained relatively stable (~ 12.8%). Treatment T8 reduced EL by 28.7–35.6% compared to T5, while T4 recorded the lowest values (10.0–8.8%). The highest EL (42.0%) was observed in M9 under T5, and the lowest (6.0%) in Bud 118 under T4 (Fig. 5).

Chlorophyll retention (SPAD values)

Chlorophyll retention, measured using SPAD values, showed clear variation among rootstocks and treatments. In the first year, Bud 118 recorded the highest SPAD value (69.00), 54.5% higher than M9, indicating better chlorophyll stability under stress. Under irrigation, T1 recorded a SPAD value of 57.40, while drought (T5) reduced it to 50.20 (− 12.5%). The combined treatment (T8) improved SPAD to 58.00 under drought (+ 15.5% over T5). Among irrigated treatments, T3 showed the highest SPAD (60.86), 6.0% higher than T1. In the second year, Bud 118 again recorded the highest SPAD (71.00), while M9 remained the lowest (− 34.5%). SPAD under T1 was 59.40, decreasing to 52.40 under T5 (− 11.8%), whereas T8 improved it to 60.07 (+ 14.6% over T5). T3 recorded the highest value under irrigation (62.80, + 5.7% over T1) (Fig. 6).

Discussion

Drought stress remains one of the most critical constraints to perennial fruit production, particularly in apple (Malus × domestica Borkh.), where long growth cycles and high water demand amplify sensitivity to prolonged soil moisture deficits. Rather than acting through isolated effects, drought disrupts plant function through a tightly interconnected network of physiological, biochemical, and signaling processes that collectively determine genotype-specific resilience. In the present study, drought tolerance in apple rootstocks emerged as the outcome of coordinated regulation of photosynthetic stability, osmotic balance, oxidative defense, membrane integrity, and hormonal homeostasis, all of which were influenced by both rootstock genotype and metabolite application.

One of the earliest responses to drought is stomatal closure, which restricts CO₂ diffusion and reduces photosynthetic carbon assimilation. This imbalance elevates excitation pressure on the photosynthetic apparatus and accelerates the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), triggering oxidative stress signaling cascades that destabilize chlorophyll pigments and impair PSII function23. Accordingly, the observed reductions in chlorophyll content, chlorophyll stability index (CSI), and gas-exchange traits across rootstocks under drought are consistent with ROS-mediated thylakoid damage and impaired light-harvesting efficiency reported previously6,24–26. Rootstocks such as Bud 118 and MM 111 maintained greater pigment stability and photosynthetic functionality, suggesting tighter redox regulation and more efficient dissipation of excess excitation energy, whereas sensitive genotypes like M9 exhibited pronounced pigment degradation and photosynthetic inhibition, reflecting limited oxidative buffering capacity27,28,41.

Maintenance of cellular hydration under drought further depends on effective osmotic regulation and membrane protection. Declining relative water content (RWC) disrupts turgor-driven cell expansion and metabolic fluxes, while increased electrolyte leakage reflects membrane lipid peroxidation arising from oxidative injury29,42,43. The contrasting osmotic behavior observed indicates that drought tolerance is associated with regulated osmolyte accumulation rather than excessive osmotic overcompensation. Sensitive genotypes relied on elevated proline accumulation and carbohydrate remobilization, responses often associated with metabolic stress, whereas tolerant rootstocks maintained moderate osmolyte levels, preserved carbohydrate reserves, and exhibited lower membrane damage, enabling greater physiological stability under prolonged water deficit30,31.

Oxidative stress responses further differentiated rootstock performance under drought. Increases in malondialdehyde, hydrogen peroxide, and electrolyte leakage confirmed ROS-induced membrane lipid peroxidation, particularly in drought-sensitive genotypes. In contrast, tolerant rootstocks restricted oxidative injury through more balanced activation of antioxidant enzymes, consistent with earlier reports linking drought tolerance to efficient ROS detoxification32,33 (Aparecido et al. 2017). Application of melatonin and myo-inositol strengthened this antioxidative network. Melatonin acted both as a direct ROS scavenger and as a regulator of antioxidant defense pathways, while myo-inositol contributed to membrane stabilization and redox signaling through phosphoinositide-mediated pathways34–37. Their combined application enhanced these protective effects, supporting complementary modes of action in mitigating oxidative stress.

Hormonal regulation provided additional insight into drought adaptation. Elevated abscisic acid (ABA) under drought mediates stomatal closure and stress signaling but can suppress growth if excessively accumulated. Tolerant rootstocks appeared to maintain a more favorable hormonal balance, sustaining ABA-dependent responses while preserving auxin-mediated root growth and water acquisition. Melatonin modulates ABA metabolism while enhancing auxin and gibberellin activity, whereas myo-inositol supports these processes through its role in calcium signaling and metabolic regulation38–40. The improved performance under combined metabolite application highlights the importance of hormonal–metabolic coordination in maintaining physiological function under water deficit.

Overall, drought tolerance in apple rootstocks is governed by the integrated regulation of photosynthesis, osmotic balance, oxidative defense, membrane stability, and hormonal processes, with strong genotype dependence. Bud 118 consistently exhibited superior resilience across these pathways. The combined application of melatonin and myo-inositol provided effective physiological and biochemical support across rootstocks. However, these responses are context-dependent and influenced by genotype, stress intensity, developmental stage, and application regime, emphasizing the need for further validation under field conditions (Fig. 7).

Conclusion

The results of this study indicate that drought tolerance in apple depends on the coordinated regulation of plant water status, gas exchange, and canopy temperature, rather than on individual physiological traits alone. Rootstocks that maintained higher leaf hydration were able to sustain stomatal function, photosynthetic activity, and evaporative cooling, thereby reducing the negative impacts of water deficit. Using a CRI-based screening approach, the combined application of melatonin and myo-inositol (100 µM each) was identified as the most effective treatment in supporting this integrated physiological balance under drought, while also improving overall plant function under irrigated conditions.

Even though these results come from short-term greenhouse experiments, they provide a clear physiological framework for understanding how metabolite application can strengthen drought resilience in apple. Further evaluation under field conditions, across growing seasons and including growth and yield responses, will be required to confirm long-term applicability. Nevertheless, the findings offer a practical and scientifically grounded basis for developing metabolite-assisted approaches to improve drought tolerance in apple under water-limited conditions.

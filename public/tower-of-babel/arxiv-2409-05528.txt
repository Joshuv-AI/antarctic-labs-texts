A divergence-free projection method for quasiperiodic photonic crystals in three dimensions
Zixuan Gao, Zhenli Xu, Zhiguo Yang

Abstract
This paper presents a point-wise divergence-free projection method for numerical approximations of photonic quasicrystals problems. The original three-dimensional quasiperiodic Maxwell’s system is transformed into a periodic one in higher dimensions through a variable substitution involving the projection matrix, such that periodic boundary condition can be readily applied. To deal with the intrinsic divergence-free constraint of the Maxwell’s equations, we present a quasiperiodic de Rham complex and its associated commuting diagram, based on which a point-wise divergence-free quasiperiodic Fourier spectral basis is proposed. With the help of this basis, we then propose an efficient solution algorithm for the quasiperiodic source problem and conduct its rigorous error estimate. Moreover, by analyzing the decay rate of the Fourier coefficients of the eigenfunctions, we further propose a divergence-free reduced projection method for the quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problem, which significantly alleviates the computational cost. Several numerical experiments are presented to validate the efficiency and accuracy of the proposed method.

Introduction

Photonic quasicrystals, which differ fundamentally from periodic lattices in terms of symmetry and order, have led to numerous novel discoveries and applications in physics
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. By manipulating the rotation angles and lattice ratios of the quasicrystal, novel localized phenomena as well as the transition between localized and delocalized states can be observed
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. These peculiar properties make photonic quasicrystals promising materials, which allow for manipulating optoelectronic properties at will. To predict these properties, it is highly demanding to develop efficient and accurate numerical methods for photonic quasicrystal problems.

The problem explored in this paper is a three-dimensional photonic quasicrystal governed by the Maxwell’s equations with quasiperiodic electric permittivity. Despite the seemingly simple form of this system, it is quite challenging numerically. The numerical difficulties can be attributed to two primary issues. The first issue is the unboundedness of the computational domain caused by the quasiperiodic condition. The second issue is the divergence-free condition inherent in the Maxwell’s equations. To tackle these obstacles, it is crucial to construct efficient approximation schemes that ensure the intrinsic divergence-free constraint under quasiperiodic condition.

There has been a long standing interest in developing numerical algorithms for quasiperiodic problems. Unlike periodic boundary conditions, quasiperiodic boundary conditions lack decay and translation invariance, which significantly affects the accuracy of direct reduction methods applied to the computational domain. One widely employed method for quasiperiodic problems is the supercell approach
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, which is often combined with planewave method
[
11
,
12
]
or the finite difference time-domain (FDTD) method
[
13
,
14
]
. Despite its popularity, the supercell method exhibits slow convergence and substantial computational costs, especially when dealing with large supercells. Additionally, the simultaneous Diophantine approximation error fails to uniformly decay as the supercell size increases. An alternative approach, commonly referred to as the projection method (PM)
[
1
,
15
,
16
]
, solves the quasiperiodic system by transforming it into a periodic problem in a higher-dimensional lattice. The PM uniformly samples every possible supercell of the infinite quasicrystal, eliminating the need to capture the entire quasiperiodic structure and enabling the use of periodic boundary conditions for domain truncation. Currently, the PM is only limited to the solution of scalar equations such as the Schrödinger equation. For quasiperiodic Maxwell’s equations, the classical PM fails to work as it cannot avoid spurious eigenmodes resulting from violating the Gauss’s law.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge, there are no available numerical methods which both avoid the Diophantine error and preserve the divergence-free constraint for photonic quasicrystals.

In this study, our focus is on the development of a novel algorithm designed for quasicrystals in three dimensions, which can overcome these numerical challenges. The main contributions of this paper are threefold. Firstly, we derive novel divergence-free Fourier approximaion bases using the quasiperiodic de Rham complex for quasicrystals in three dimensions. Secondly, we propose the projection method based on the divergence-free basis (DF-PM) for quasiperiodic curl-curl problems and conduct a rigorous error estimate to justify the spectral accuracy of it. A highly efficient solution algorithm is then proposed. Thirdly, we propose a novel reduction strategy for the Maxwell eigenvalue problem. Theorem
4.5
shows the decay rate of the divergence-free Fourier coefficients. Thanks to this property, we propose the reduced projection method based on the divergence-free basis (DF-RPM) for the quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problem, as the idea of the RPM
[
17
]
. The degree of freedom (DOF) decreases significantly and it is possible to use more bases for higher accuracy. These presented divergence-free projection methods and corresponding
fast solution algorithms are very competitive and computationally attractive.

Notation and convention

Let us denote
\mathbb{R}
,

and

be the sets of real, rational and integer numbers, respectively. We adopt italic letters for numbers or scalar functions, bold italics for vectors or vector functions, and bold for matrices, respectively. Let

be the space of square-integral functions with the inner product and norm denoted by

and

H({\rm curl},\Omega)=\big\{\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\big|\nabla\times\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\big\},\quad H({\rm div},\Omega)=\big\{\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\big|\nabla\cdot\bm{v}\in L^{2}(\Omega)\big\}.

Since there would be frequent conversions between periodic and quasiperiodic functions, we use the subscripts “per" and “qp" to differentiate the spaces they belong to, as well as their associated inner products and norms. For instance, given a
d
-dimensional periodic function
F(\bm{z})\in L^{2}_{\rm per}([0,T]^{d})
with period
T
in each dimension (referred to as a “
T
-periodic function"), its corresponding inner product and norm are denoted by:

(F,G)_{\rm per}=\dfrac{1}{T^{d}}\int_{[0,T]^{d}}F\bar{G}{d}\bm{z},\quad\|F\|_{\rm per}=\sqrt{(F,F)_{\rm per}},

where
\overline{G}
represents the complex conjugate of
G\in L^{2}_{\rm per}([0,T]^{d})
.

Quasiperiodic functions

To facilitate the development of efficient and accurate numerical methods for quasiperiodic Maxwell source and eigenvalue problems, we provide a brief exposition of the definition of quasiperiodic functions and their relevant properties (see monographs
[
19
,
20
]
for comprehensive discussions).

Definition 2.1
.

A
d
-dimensional function
f(\bm{z}),\;\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is quasiperiodic if there exists a
d\times n
projection matrix
\mathbf{P}
such that
F(\bm{x})=F(\mathbf{P}^{\intercal}\bm{z})=f(\bm{z})
is an
n
-dimensional periodic function, where all columns of
\mathbf{P}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
F(\bm{x})
is called the parent function of
f(\bm{z})
with respect to
\mathbf{P}
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is important to note that the projection matrix
\mathbf{P}
is not unique. The projection matrix of the quasiperiodic function
f(x)=\cos(x)+\cos(\sqrt{2}x)
can be chosen as
\mathbf{P}=\big[1,\sqrt{2}\big]
or
\big[\sqrt{2},1\big]
. Throughout the paper,
\mathbf{P}
is chosen such that
F(\bm{x})
is
2\pi
-periodic and we prescribe
\mathbb{T}^{n}=[0,2\pi]^{n}
hereafter.

We define the mean value of a
d
-dimensional quasiperiodic function
f(\bm{z})
as

m(f)=\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{1}{|L|^{d}}\int_{K}f(\bm{z}){d}\bm{z},

(1)

where
K=\{\bm{z}\,|\,0\leq|\bm{z}_{i}|\leq L,i=1,\dots,d\}
. Correspondingly, the square-integral quasiperiodic function space
L^{2}_{\rm qp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as

L^{2}_{\rm qp}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f(\bm{z})\,|\;m(f\bar{f})<\infty\},

(2)

with the inner product and norm given by

(f,g)_{\rm qp}=m(f\bar{g}),\quad\|f\|_{\rm qp}=\sqrt{(f,f)_{\rm qp}}\,,

(3)

where
f
and
g
are quasiperiodic functions with respect to the same projection matrix
\mathbf{P}
.

It is well-known that
\{e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle}\}_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
serves as a complete orthonormal basis for
L^{2}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that for any
F(\bm{x})\in L^{2}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, its has the Fourier series

F(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}F_{\bm{k}}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},\quad\hat{F}_{\bm{k}}=\dfrac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}F(\bm{x})e^{-\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle}d\bm{x},

(4)

and there holds the Parseval’s equality
\|F\|^{2}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}|F_{\bm{k}}|^{2}
. Lemma
2.1
relates the decay rate of Fourier coefficients with the regularity of a function (see
[
21
, p.196]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{Z},\;m>0
, suppose
F(\bm{x})\in H^{m}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{[}0,T]^{n})
, then

|\hat{F}_{\bm{k}}|\leq(\sqrt{n})^{{m}}T^{-n}|\bm{k}|^{-m}|F|_{m,\rm per},\quad|F|_{m,\rm per}^{2}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\bm{k}|^{2m}|\hat{F}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}.

(5)

Quasiperiodic function also has the generalized Fourier series and Parseval’s equality. One readily verifies that the set
\big\{e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle}\big\}_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
forms a normalized orthogonal system as

\big(e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q}_{1},\bm{z}\rangle},e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q}_{2},\bm{z}\rangle}\big)_{\rm qp}=\delta_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2}},\quad\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(6)

where
\delta_{\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2}}
is the Dirac delta function. Thus, one can define the Fourier transform of the quasiperiodic functions, also called the Fourier-Bohr transformation (see
[
19
]
), as

\mathscr{F}_{\rm qp}\{f\}(\bm{q})=m\big(f(\bm{z})e^{-\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle}\big),

(7)

Correspondingly, one has the generalized Fourier series of the quasiperiodic function and the Parseval’s equality in Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(see
[
19
]
)
Any
d
-dimensional quasiperiodic function
f(\bm{z})
has generalized Fourier series

f(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle},\quad\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}=(f,e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle})_{\rm qp},

(8)

where
\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}
is called the
\bm{q}
th generalized Fourier coefficient of

and
\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]:=\{\bm{q}\,|\,\bm{q}=\mathbf{P}\bm{k},\;\;\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
.
In addition, there holds the Parseval’s equality:

\|f\|^{2}_{\rm qp}=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}|^{2}.

(9)

Correspondingly, one can define the Sobolev spaces
H^{m}_{\rm qp}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),m\in\mathbb{Z},m>0
for quasiperiodic functions

H^{m}_{\rm qp}(L^{2}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))=\{f(\bm{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\|f(\bm{z})\|_{m,\rm qp}<\infty\},

(10)

and its associated norm and semi-norm,

\|f(\bm{z})\|_{m,\rm qp}^{2}=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}(1+|\bm{q}|^{2m})|\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}|^{2},\quad|f(\bm{z})|_{m,\rm qp}^{2}=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\bm{q}|^{2m}|\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}|^{2}.

(11)

Theorem
2.1
relates the generalized coefficients of quasiperiodic functions to the Fourier coefficients of its parent function (
[
22
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f(\bm{x})
be a
d
-dimensional quasiperiodic function. There exists a parent function
F(\bm{x})
, which has a one-to-one correspondence between their Fourier coefficients, i.e.

f(z)=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}\hat{f}_{\bm{q}}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle},\qquad F(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\hat{F}_{\bm{k}}e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle}.

(12)

One has

\quad\hat{F}_{\bm{k}}=\hat{f}_{\bm{q}},\;\;{\rm iff}\;\;\bm{q}=\mathbf{P}\bm{k}.

(13)

Thus, one can define the bijective mapping

\phi(\bm{k})=\mathbf{P}\bm{k}=\bm{q},\;\;\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\;\;\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})],

(14)

so as to establish a one-to-one correspondence between the generalized Fourier coefficient of the quasiperiodic function and the Fourier series of the periodic function. Besides, it is necessary to introduce the injective mapping

\mathcal{p}(\bm{z})=\mathbf{P}^{\intercal}\bm{z}=\bm{x},\;\;\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\;\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{T}^{n}

(15)

and a functional
\mathcal{P}[\cdot]
such that one can transform between the quasiperiodic function and its periodic parent function through

f(\bm{z})=F\circ\mathcal{p}(\bm{z}),\quad\mathcal{P}[f](\bm{x})=F(\bm{x}).

(16)

Given a certain differential operator
D
w.r.t. variable
\bm{z}
, we denote

as the differential operator of
\bm{x}
obtained by taking variable substitution

\displaystyle H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n})=H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\cdot,\mathbb{T}^{n})={\rm span}\big\{e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle}\bm{e}_{j}\;j=1,2,3\big|\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}\big\}.

(32)

The corresponding commuting projections can be defined in a similar manner by

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm per}^{1}\mathcal{P}[u]=\Pi_{\rm per}^{4}\mathcal{P}[u]=\textstyle\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}}\hat{U}_{\bm{k}}e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},\;\;\forall\mathcal{P}[u]\in H_{\rm per}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}),

(33)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm per}^{2}\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]=\Pi_{\rm per}^{3}\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]=\textstyle\sum_{{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}}\hat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},\;\;\forall\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]\in H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n}),

(34)

where we recall theorem
2.1
and Eqs. (
28
)-(
29
) to obtain that

\hat{U}_{\bm{k}}=\hat{u}_{\bm{q}},\;\;\;\hat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}=\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}},\;\;{\rm iff}\;\;\bm{q}=\mathbf{P}\bm{k}.

(35)

By the transformation identities Eqs.(
19a
)-(
19c
), it is straightforward to verify that the above diagram is commutable.

Now we are ready to present divergence-free bases for approximating functions in
H_{\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n})
spaces in Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.2
.

\bm{V}_{N,{\rm qp}}^{\rm div0}:=\{\bm{u}\in H_{N,\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3})|\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0\}
is a divergence-free (w.r.t. the operator
\nabla\cdot
) and
H_{\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3})
-conforming approximation space taking the form

\begin{split}\bm{V}_{N,{\rm qp}}^{\rm div0}&={\rm span}\Big\{\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{j}(\bm{z}):={\bm{d}}_{j}({\bm{q}})e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle},\;j=1,2\big|\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}\Big\},\end{split}

(36)

where
\{{\bm{d}}_{1}({\bm{q}}),{\bm{d}}_{2}({\bm{q}})\}
is an orthonormal basis for the null space of
\bm{q}.
The corresponding divergence-free (w.r.t. the operator
\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\cdot
) and
H_{\rm per}({\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n}})
-conforming approximation space
\bm{V}_{N,{\rm per}}^{\rm div0}:=\big\{\bm{U}\in H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n})\big|\nabla_{\rho}\cdot\bm{U}=0\big\}
reads

\bm{V}_{N,{\rm per}}^{\rm div0}={\rm span}\Big\{\bm{\Psi}_{\bm{k}}^{j}(\bm{x})=\bm{d}_{j}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},\;j=1,2\;\big|\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}\Big\}.

(37)

Proof.

Recall from Eq. (
29
) that any
\bm{u}\in H_{N,\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3})
can be expanded by
\bm{u}=\textstyle\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle}
, one can impose the divergence-free condition on
\bm{u}
and use the orthogonality in Eq. (
6
) to obtain a sequence of decoupled linear equations

\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}\cdot\bm{q}=0,\;\;\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}.

Thus, if
\bm{u}
is divergence-free, then
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}
belongs to the null space of
\bm{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})^{\intercal}
and can be uniquely determined by a linear combination of a basis
\{{\bm{d}}_{1}({\bm{q}}),{\bm{d}}_{2}({\bm{q}})\}
of this null space as
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}=\hat{u}^{1}_{\bm{q}}{\bm{d}}_{1}({\bm{q}})+\hat{u}^{2}_{\bm{q}}{\bm{d}}_{2}({\bm{q}})
. As a result, we readily obtain

\bm{u}(\bm{z})=\textstyle\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle}=\textstyle\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}}[\hat{u}^{1}_{\bm{q}}{\bm{d}}_{1}({\bm{q}})+\hat{u}^{2}_{\bm{q}}{\bm{d}}_{2}({\bm{q}})]e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle},\quad\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{N,{\rm qp}}^{\rm div0},

(38)

which leads to the desired basis in Eq. (
36
).

Correspondingly, with the help of the transformation identities Eqs.(
19a
)-(
19c
) and Eq. (
34
), one can obtain Eq. (

) in a similar manner, which ends the proof.
∎

3.2
The divergence-free projection scheme

The weak formulation of the quasiperiodic double-curl source problem (
23
) is to find
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}:=\big\{\bm{u}\in H_{\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3})|\;\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0\big\}
such that

(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times\bm{u},\nabla\times\bm{v})+\kappa(\bm{u},\bm{v})=(\bm{g},\bm{v})\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(39)

With the help of the divergence-free Fourier spectral basis for quasiperiodic functions, the proposed point-wise divergence-free projection scheme reads: find
\bm{u}^{N}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
such that

(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times\bm{u}^{N},\nabla\times\bm{v}^{N})+\kappa(\bm{u}^{N},\bm{v}^{N})=(\bm{g},\bm{v}^{N})\quad\forall\bm{v}^{N}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(40)

By Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
), one defines the corresponding bilinear form for
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
,

\mathcal{A}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla\times\bm{u},\nabla\times\bm{v})+\kappa(\bm{u},\bm{v}),

(41)

and its corresponding periodic form: for
This bilinear form satisfies the following continuity and coercivity

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\bm{u},\bm{v})\leq\max(\varepsilon_{A}^{-1},\kappa)\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\|\bm{v}\|_{\rm qp,curl},

\displaystyle{\rm for}\;\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0},

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\bm{u},\bm{u})\geq\min(\varepsilon_{B}^{-1},\kappa)\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}^{2},

\displaystyle{\rm for}\;\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(43)

Proposition 3.1
.

We consider the operator
\pi_{\rm qp}:{\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}}\rightarrow\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}:

\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}=\textstyle\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}}[\hat{u}^{1}_{\bm{q}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{1}(\bm{z})+\hat{u}^{2}_{\bm{q}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{2}(\bm{z})],

(44)

where
\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}}=\langle\bm{\hat{u}}_{\bm{q}},\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle
,
\bm{\hat{u}}_{\bm{q}}
is the Fourier coefficient of
\bm{u}
as defined in Eq. (
4
). It satisfies that

(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\nabla\times\bm{v})+(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{v})=0,\;\;\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0},

(45)

which impiles that
\pi_{\rm qp}
is a projection from
\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
to
\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
.

Proof.

Because the Fourier coefficient of
\bm{u}
has the property
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}=\hat{u}^{1}_{\bm{q}}{\bm{d}}_{1}({\bm{q}})+\hat{u}^{2}_{\bm{q}}{\bm{d}}_{2}({\bm{q}})
for all
\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}
, then by the orthogonality in Eq. (
6
), one has

(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{v})=0,\;\;\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(46)

Because
\nabla\cdot\bm{u}=\nabla\cdot(\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u})=0
, by the orthogonality in Eq. (
6
), one obtains

(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\nabla\times\bm{v})=(-\Delta(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\bm{v})=0,\;\;\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(47)

Combining Eqs. (
46
) and (
47
), this proposition is proved.
∎

To derive the error estimate for the proposed scheme, we first introduce the approximation result in Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\bm{u}
is a quasiperiodic vector function and define
\bm{w}=\nabla\times\bm{u}
. If
\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}],\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})^{3}
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, then

\|\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\leq N^{-m}(|\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]|_{m,\rm per}+|\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]|_{m,\rm per}),

(48)

where
\|\cdot\|_{\rm qp,curl}
is the energy norm of
H_{\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which is defined as

\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}^{2}=\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}^{2}+\|\nabla\times\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}^{2},\;\;\bm{u}\in H_{\rm qp}({\rm curl},\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(49)

Proof.

By Eqs. (
45
) and (
49
), one obtains

\begin{split}\|\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}^{2}&=(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}))+(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u})\\
&=(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\nabla\times\bm{u})+(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{u}).\end{split}

(50)

Then because of the orthogonality in Eq. (
6
), it gives

(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\nabla\times\bm{u})=(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\bm{w}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{w})

(51)

and

(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{u})=(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{u}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{u}),

(52)

where
\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}
is defined in Eq. (
29
). By the fact that

(\nabla\times(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}),\bm{w}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{w})\leq\|\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\|\bm{w}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{w}\|_{\rm qp}

(53)

and

(\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u},\bm{u}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{u})\leq\|\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\|\bm{u}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp},

(54)

one obtains that

\|\bm{u}-\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\leq\|\bm{u}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}+\|\bm{w}-\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{w}\|_{\rm qp}.

(55)

By Parseval’s identities for periodic functions and Theorem
2.1
, one has

\begin{split}&\|\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}=\|\Pi_{N,\rm per}^{2}\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]-\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]\|_{\rm per},\\
&\|\Pi_{N,\rm qp}^{2}\bm{w}-\bm{w}\|_{\rm qp}=\|\Pi_{N,\rm per}^{2}\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]-\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]\|_{\rm per}.\end{split}

(56)

Then with the help of the projection error estimation result of periodic functions (cf.
[
25
, p.34]
), one readily obtains the desired result.
∎

The error estimate of the divergence-free projection scheme (
40
) can now be stated as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\bm{u},\bm{u}^{N}
be respectively the solutions of Eqs. (
23
) and (
39
), and define
\bm{w}=\nabla\times\bm{u}
. If
\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}],\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})^{3}
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, then we have

\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\leq CN^{-m}(|\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]|_{m,\rm per}+|\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]|_{m,\rm per}),

(57)

where the positive constant
C
only depends on
\kappa,\varepsilon_{A}
and
\varepsilon_{B}
.

Proof.

Taking
\bm{v}=\bm{v}^{N}
in Eq. (
39
), and by Eq. (
40
), one obtains the error equation

\mathcal{A}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N},\bm{v})=0,\;\;\forall\bm{v}^{N}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0},

(58)

which, together with Eqs. (
42
) and (
43
), implies that

\begin{split}\min(\varepsilon_{B}^{-1},\kappa)\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N}\|_{\rm qp,curl}^{2}&\leq\mathcal{A}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N},\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N})=\mathcal{A}(\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N},\bm{u}-\bm{v}^{N})\\
&\leq\max(\varepsilon_{A}^{-1},\kappa)\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\|\bm{u}-\bm{v}^{N}\|_{\rm qp,curl}.\end{split}

(59)

Then one has

\|\bm{u}-\bm{u}^{N}\|_{\rm qp,curl}\leq C\inf_{\bm{v}^{N}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}}\|\bm{u}-\bm{v}^{N}\|_{\rm qp,curl}.

(60)

where the positive constant
C=\max(\varepsilon_{B},\kappa^{-1})\max(\varepsilon_{A}^{-1},\kappa)
. This theorem is proved by taking
\bm{v}^{N}=\pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}
and together with Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

It should be noted that under the quasiperiodic condition, the Poincare inequality
\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}\leq\|\nabla\times\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}
does not hold due to the lack of ellipticity. Thus Eq. (
57
) cannot be bounded only by
|\mathcal{P}[\bm{w}]|_{m,\rm per}
, which differs from the usual error estimates of Maxwell problems such as
[
26
]
.

3.3
Solution algorithm for quasiperiodic curl-curl source problems

Next, we describe the solution algorithm as well as the implementation detail of the DF-PM for quasiperiodic curl-curl source problems.

Consider the uniform mesh
T_{h}=\{x=mh,\;m=0,1,\cdots,N-1\}^{n}
for domain
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with equidistant mesh size
h=2\pi/N
in each direction, where
N
is assumed to be an even positive integer for convenience.

For any
\bm{U}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
and
\bm{F}=(\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon])^{-1}\bm{U}
, define
I_{N}
and
I_{N}^{\rm div0}
as the trigonometric interpolation operators
[
25
]
,

(I_{N}\bm{F})(\bm{x})=\textstyle\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}}\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}}e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},\;\;(I_{N}^{\rm div0}\bm{U})(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}}[\tilde{U}^{1}_{\bm{k}}\bm{\Psi}_{\bm{k}}^{1}(\bm{x})+\tilde{U}^{2}_{\bm{k}}\bm{\Psi}_{\bm{k}}^{2}(\bm{x})],

(61)

where the pseudo-spectral coefficients
\tilde{U}^{j}_{\bm{k}}
and
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}}
are determined such that
(I^{\rm div0}_{N}\bm{U})(\bm{x}_{h})=\bm{U}(\bm{x}_{h})
and
(I_{N}\bm{F})(\bm{x}_{h})=\bm{F}(\bm{x}_{h})
hold for all
\bm{x}_{h}\in T_{h}
.

Before we propose the solution algorithm, we first give some necessary notations.
\mathbf{I}_{n\times m},\mathbf{0}_{n\times m}
and
\mathbf{1}_{n\times m}
represent
n\times m
identity matrix, zero matrix and all-ones matrix, respectively. We further introduce the notation of “vec" to arrange all
U_{\bm{k}}
into a column vector
\vec{\bm{U}}
, i.e.
\vec{\bm{U}}={\rm vec}(U_{\bm{k}})
, following standard column-wise linear index order of vector
\bm{k}
(see
[
27
, p.27-28]
for more details).

For convenience, we first introduce the definition of the pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficient column vector.

Definition 3.1
.

For any
\bm{U}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
, its pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficient column vector is

\tilde{\bm{U}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\\
\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}\vec{{\bm{U}}}_{1}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\\
\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}\vec{{\bm{U}}}_{2},

(62)

where

\mathbf{I}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{L\times L},\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix},\;\;\mathbf{I}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{L\times L},\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}

(63)

and the
2L\times 1
column vectors are

\vec{{\bm{U}}}_{1}={\rm vec}(\tilde{U}^{1}_{\bm{k}}),\;\;\vec{{\bm{U}}}_{2}={\rm vec}(\tilde{U}^{2}_{\bm{k}}),

(64)

where
L
is the cardinality of
\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}
.

The vector
\tilde{\bm{U}}
contains all the pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficients of
\bm{U}
. One also defines the column vectors

\vec{\bm{R}}_{j}={\rm vec}(R^{j}_{\bm{k}}),\;\;\vec{\bm{S}}_{j}={\rm vec}(S^{j}_{\bm{k}}),\;\;\vec{\bm{T}}={\rm vec}(T_{\bm{k}}),\;\;j=1,2,3,

(65)

where

R_{\bm{k}}^{j}=\bm{e}_{j}^{\intercal}\bm{d}_{1}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k}),\;\;S_{\bm{k}}^{j}=\bm{e}_{j}^{\intercal}\bm{d}_{2}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k}),\;\;T_{\bm{k}}=\mathrm{i}|\mathbf{P}\bm{k}|,

(66)

and
\mathrm{i}
is the imaginary unit. The two
L\times 3
matrices
\mathbf{R},\mathbf{S}
are defined as

\mathbf{R}=\left(\vec{\bm{R}}_{1},\vec{\bm{R}}_{2},\vec{\bm{R}}_{3}\right),\;\;\mathbf{S}=\left(\vec{\bm{S}}_{1},\vec{\bm{S}}_{2},\vec{\bm{S}}_{3}\right).

(67)

Then we consider the explicit form of the pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficient column vector of
\bm{K}=\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times\bm{F}
, where
\bm{F}\in H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n})
. This form gives the discrete curl operator of the DF-PM, and it is introduced in Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem 3.4
.

For any
\bm{F}\in H_{N,\rm per}(\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times,\mathbb{T}^{n})
, one assumes the
L\times 3
Fourier coefficient matrix of
\bm{F}
is
\hat{\mathbf{F}}
. The pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficient column vector of
\bm{K}=\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times\bm{F}
is

\tilde{\bm{K}}={\mathbf{C}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\\
\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}\hat{\mathbf{F}}\circ{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{1}_{3\times 1}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\\
\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}\hat{\mathbf{F}}\circ\mathbf{S}\mathbf{1}_{3\times 1}\right),

(68)

where the notation
\circ
denotes the Hadamard product between two matrices and the
2L\times 2L
matrix
\mathbf{C}
is defined as

\mathbf{C}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}&\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\vec{\bm{T}}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\vec{\bm{T}}&\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}.

(69)

Specially, if
\bm{U}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm per}^{\rm div0}
, and its pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficient column vector is
\tilde{\bm{U}}
, then the pseudo-spectral divergence-free Fourier coefficient column vector of
\bm{K}=\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times\bm{U}
is

\tilde{\bm{K}}=\mathbf{C}\tilde{\bm{U}}.

(70)

Proof.

By the difinition of the trigonometric interpolation operators in Eq. (
61
), one has

I_{N}\bm{K}=\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times(I_{N}\bm{F})=\textstyle\mathrm{i}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}}[(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})\times\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}}]e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle}.

(71)

Then by the orthonormality of
\bm{d}_{1}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k}),\bm{d}_{2}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})
and
\mathbf{P}\bm{k}/|\mathbf{P}\bm{k}|
, the Fourier coefficient
\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}}
can be expand by

\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}}=\tilde{F}_{\bm{k}}^{1}\bm{d}_{1}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})+\tilde{F}_{k}^{2}\bm{d}_{2}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})+\tilde{F}_{k}^{3}\mathbf{P}\bm{k}/|\mathbf{P}\bm{k}|,

(72)

where
\tilde{F}_{\bm{k}}^{j}=\langle\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}},\bm{d}_{j}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})\rangle
for
j=1,2
and
\tilde{F}_{\bm{k}}^{3}=\langle\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}},\mathbf{P}\bm{k}/|\mathbf{P}\bm{k}|\rangle
. Hence

(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})\times\tilde{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}}=-\tilde{F}_{\bm{k}}^{2}\bm{d}_{1}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})+\tilde{F}_{\bm{k}}^{1}\bm{d}_{2}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k}).

(73)

Combining Eqs. (
71
) and (
73
), one can derive the form of
\mathbf{C}
and Eq. (
68
). Specially, for
\bm{U}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm per}^{\rm div0}
, one has

I_{N}\bm{K}=\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times(I_{N}\bm{U})=\textstyle\mathrm{i}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},N}}[(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})\times\tilde{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}]e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},

(74)

where
\tilde{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}=\tilde{U}_{\bm{k}}^{1}\bm{d}_{1}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})+\tilde{U}_{k}^{2}\bm{d}_{2}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})
. Then by the fact that

(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})\times\tilde{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}=-\tilde{U}_{\bm{k}}^{2}\bm{d}_{1}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})+\tilde{U}_{\bm{k}}^{1}\bm{d}_{2}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k}),

(75)

one obtains Eq. (
70
).
∎

We denote that
\bm{F}=(\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon])^{-1}\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times\bm{U},\;\;\bm{K}=\nabla_{\mathcal{p}}\times\bm{F}
, where
\bm{U}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm per}^{\rm div0}
. By Theorem
3.4
, the pseudo-spectral coefficient column vector of
\bm{K}
is

\bm{\tilde{\bm{K}}}=\mathbf{C}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\\
\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}\hat{\mathbf{F}}\circ\mathbf{R}\mathbf{1}_{3\times 1}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\\
\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}\hat{\mathbf{F}}\circ\mathbf{S}\mathbf{1}_{3\times 1}\right),

(76)

where the
L\times 3
matrix
\hat{\mathbf{F}}
is the Fourier coefficient matrix of
\bm{F}
.
\hat{\mathbf{F}}
has the form

\hat{\mathbf{F}}=\mathcal{F}[(\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon])^{-1}(\bm{x})\cdot\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{L\times L},\mathbf{0}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{C}\bm{\tilde{U}}}\mathbf{1}_{1\times L}\circ\mathbf{R}^{\intercal}+\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{L\times L},\mathbf{I}_{L\times L}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{C}\bm{\tilde{\bm{U}}}}\mathbf{1}_{1\times L}\circ\mathbf{S}^{\intercal})],

(77)

where

4
Reduced projection method for quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problem

In this section, we move to the quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problem in three dimensions,

\nabla\times\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}\bm{u})=\lambda\bm{u},\ \ \ \ \nabla\cdot\bm{u}=0,\quad{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{3}.

(79)

The weak formulation of Eq. (
79
) reads: find non-trivial
\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}\bm{u}),\nabla\times\bm{v})=\lambda(\bm{u},\bm{v})\quad\forall\bm{v}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(80)

One can use the projection method based on the divergence-free basis to solve this problem numerically, and the approximation is: find non-trivial
\bm{u}_{N}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
and
\lambda^{N}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(\nabla\times(\varepsilon^{-1}\bm{u}^{N}),\nabla\times\bm{v}^{N})=\lambda^{N}(\bm{u}^{N},\bm{v}^{N})\quad\forall\bm{v}^{N}\in\bm{V}_{N,\rm qp}^{\rm div0}.

(81)

This section introduces the first numerical algorithm for Maxwell’s quasiperiodic problem without spurious modes, applying the projection method while ensuring the divergence-free condition. Before introducing the algorithm, we first focus on a decay property of Fourier coefficients, as it can guide us in further improving the projection method.

4.1
Decay rate of the divergence-free Fourier coefficients

In this part, we are intended to show that the Fourier series in terms of the divergence-free basis of the eigenvalue problem exhibits a certain decay rate. One sets
\bm{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})
, where
u_{1},u_{2}
and
u_{3}
are 3D aperiodic functions with the same projection matrix
\mathbf{P}
, and
\bm{f}=\varepsilon^{-1}\bm{u}
. Before we prove the main result, we first introduce Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\bm{p},\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]
, one has

|\langle\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{p}),\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle|\leq\min(|\bm{p}|,|\bm{q}|)^{-1}|\bm{p}-\bm{q}|,j=1,2,

(82)

where
\{{\bm{d}}_{1}({\bm{q}}),{\bm{d}}_{2}({\bm{q}})\}
is an orthonormal basis for the null space of
\bm{q}
.

Proof.

By the orthonormality of
\bm{d}_{j}(\cdot)
and the Schwarz’s inequality,

|\langle\bm{d}_{i}(\bm{p}),\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle|=|\langle\bm{d}_{i}(\bm{p}),\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|-\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle|\leq|\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|-\bm{q}/|\bm{q}||.

(83)

Without of generality, one assumes that
|\bm{p}|\geq|\bm{q}|
, and denotes
\theta
as the angle between vectors
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
. Then

2|\bm{q}|(|\bm{p}|-|\bm{q}|)\cos\theta\leq 2|\bm{q}|(|\bm{p}|-|\bm{q}|)\leq|\bm{p}|^{2}-|\bm{q}|^{2},

(84)

which implies that

2|\bm{q}|^{2}-2|\bm{q}|^{2}\cos\theta\leq|\bm{p}|^{2}+|\bm{q}|^{2}-2\langle\bm{p},\bm{q}\rangle.

(85)

This ends the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that a sequence
\{u_{\bm{q}}\},\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})],|u_{\bm{q}}|\leq 1
satisfies

|u_{\bm{q}}|\leq C_{\alpha}|\bm{q}|^{-\alpha}

(86)

for some
\alpha>1/2
, where
C_{\alpha}
is a positive constant. Denote
g(\bm{z})
and
G=\mathcal{P}[\bm{g}]
. Assume
G(\bm{x})\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
(m\in\mathbb{Z},\,m\geq 0)
. For any
\gamma_{1}\leq{\rm min}\big\{m-n,\alpha-1/2\big\}

C_{\gamma_{1}}
such that

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}u_{\bm{p}}|\leq C_{\gamma_{1}}|\bm{q}|^{-\gamma_{1}},

(87)

where
\hat{g}_{\bm{p}}
is the
\bm{p}
th Fourier coefficient of
g(\bm{z})
,
C_{\gamma_{1}}
depends on
m,n,\|\mathbf{P}\|,|G|_{m,\rm per}
and
\|g\|
. Moreover, if
\nabla g
is also
L^{2}
bounded, then for any
\gamma_{2}\leq{\rm min}\big\{m-n,\alpha+1/2\big\}
, there exists a constant
C_{\gamma_{2}}
such that

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{k}}u_{\bm{k}}\langle\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|,\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle|\leq C_{\gamma_{2}}|\bm{q}|^{-\gamma_{2}},\quad j=1,2,

(88)

where
C_{\gamma_{2}}
depends on
m,n,\|\mathbf{P}\|,|G|_{m,\rm per}
and
\|\nabla g\|
.

Proof.

One has the decomposition of Eq. (
87
)

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}u_{\bm{p}}|=\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{1}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}u_{\bm{p}}|+\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}u_{\bm{p}}|,

(89)

where
\Gamma_{1}=\{\bm{p}|\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})],|\phi^{-1}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})|>\|\mathbf{P}\|^{-1}|\bm{q}|/2\}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]/\Gamma_{1}
. We then consider the summations in two parts, separately.

\mathbf{Estimate\ of\ the\ \Gamma_{1}\ part}
. By the fact that

and Eq. (
5

\left|\hat{G}_{\phi^{-1}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})}\right|\leq\dfrac{n^{m/2}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left|\phi^{-1}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})\right|^{-m}|G|_{m,{\rm per}}.

(90)

Then the bound

\begin{split}\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{1}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}||u_{\bm{p}}|&\leq\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{1}}|\hat{G}_{\phi^{-1}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})}|\leq\dfrac{n^{m/2}}{(2\pi)^{n}}|G|_{m,{\rm per}}\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{1}}|\phi^{-1}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})|^{-m}\\
&=\dfrac{n^{m/2}}{(2\pi)^{n}}|G|_{m,{\rm per}}\|\mathbf{P}\|^{-1}\sum_{|\bm{k}|>|\bm{q}|/2}|\bm{k}|^{-m}\end{split}

(91)

holds, where

\sum_{|\bm{k}|>|\bm{q}|/2}|\bm{k}|^{-m}\leq\int_{|\bm{t}|>|\bm{q}|/2}|\bm{t}|^{-m}d\bm{t}=2\pi\cdot 2^{n-2}\int_{|\bm{q}|/2}^{+\infty}|\bm{t}|^{n-m-1}d|\bm{t}|=\dfrac{2^{m-1}\pi}{m-n}|\bm{q}|^{n-m},

(92)

(93)

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant depending on

and
n
.

\mathbf{Estimate\ of\ the\ \Gamma_{2}\ part}
. By the Hölder’s inequality with

\begin{split}\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}||u_{\bm{p}}|&\leq\left(\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\left(\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}|^{\frac{2\beta n-4n}{\beta}}|u_{\bm{p}}|^{2n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n}}\leq\\
&\max_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}|\cdot\|g\|^{\frac{2}{\beta}}\left(\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|u_{\bm{p}}|^{2n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n}}\leq\|g\|^{\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|u_{\bm{p}}|^{2n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n}}.\end{split}

(94)

For any

\|\mathbf{P}\|^{-1}|\bm{q}|/2\geq|\phi^{-1}(\bm{q}-\bm{p})|\geq\|\mathbf{P}\|^{-1}|\bm{q}-\bm{p}|,

(95)

hence

with

\alpha

independent with

. Then there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
depending on

and such that

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|u_{\bm{p}}|^{2n}\leq C_{\alpha}\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\bm{p}|^{-\alpha n}\leq C_{\alpha}|\Gamma_{2}||\bm{q}/2|^{-2\alpha n}\leq C_{2}|\bm{q}|^{n-2\alpha n}.

(96)

Therefore, there exists a positive constant
C_{3}
depending on

and
\|g\|
such that

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}||u_{\bm{p}}|\leq C_{3}|\bm{q}|^{-\alpha+1/2}.

(97)

Combining Eqs. (
89
),(
93
) and (

On the other hand, Eq. (
88
) has the same decomposition as Eq. (
89
). The
\Gamma_{1}
part is bounded as the same way, then there exists a positive constant
C_{4}
depending on

and

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{1}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}||u_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|,\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle|\leq C_{4}|\bm{q}|^{n-m}.

(98)

For the
\Gamma_{2}

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}||u_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|,\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle|\leq|\bm{q}|^{-1}\sum_{\bm{p}\in\Gamma_{2}}|\hat{g}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}||\bm{q}-\bm{p}||u_{\bm{p}}|,

∎

Then we introduce the main result of the decay rate of the generalized Fourier coefficients in Theorem

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\bm{u}(\bm{z})
be the eigenfunction of the quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problem (
79
) corresponding to
\lambda
, with
\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}}
being its Fourier coefficient under the divergence-free basis, and
\varepsilon(\bm{z})
and
\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon](\bm{x})
being the quasiperiodic electric permittivity function and its parent function, respectively. Assume
\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon]\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
(m\in\mathbb{Z},\,m\geq 0)
. For any
\alpha\leq m-n
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha}
such that

|\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|\leq C_{\alpha}|\bm{q}|^{-\alpha},

(101)

where
C_{\alpha}
depends on
m,n,\|\mathbf{P}\|,|\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon]|_{m,\rm per},\|\nabla\varepsilon\|,|(\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon])^{-1}|_{m,\rm per}
and
\|\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1})\|
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us set
\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}=1
. Define

\bm{f}(\bm{z})=\varepsilon^{-1}(\bm{z})\bm{u}(\bm{z})=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}\hat{\bm{f}}_{\bm{q}}e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle}.

(102)

By the Helmhotlz decomposition, there exists
\bm{f}_{\rm div0}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
and a function
g
such that
\bm{f}=\bm{f}_{\rm div0}+\nabla g
. Then one has

\nabla\times\nabla\times\bm{f}=\nabla\times\nabla\times(\bm{f}_{\rm div0}+\nabla g)=\nabla\times\nabla\times\bm{f}_{\rm div0}=-\Delta\bm{f}_{\rm div0}.

(103)

Because
\bm{f}_{\rm div0},\bm{u}\in\bm{V}_{\rm qp}^{\rm div0}
, one has their series under the divergence-free basis,

\bm{f}_{\rm div0}=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}(\hat{f}^{1}_{\bm{q}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{1}(\bm{z})+\hat{f}^{2}_{\bm{q}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{2}(\bm{z}))

(104)

and

\bm{u}=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}(\hat{u}^{1}_{\bm{q}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{1}(\bm{z})+\hat{u}^{2}_{\bm{q}}\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{2}(\bm{z})).

(105)

It should be noted that
\hat{f}^{j}_{\bm{q}}=\langle\hat{\bm{f}}_{\bm{q}},\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle,j=1,2
. For convenience, one defines
\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{q}}=\langle\hat{\bm{f}}_{\bm{q}},\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle
. Then by Eq. (
103
) and the orthogonality of
\{e^{\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle}\}
, one obtains from the weak form that for
j=1,2
,

|\bm{q}|^{2}\hat{f}^{j}_{\bm{q}}=\lambda\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}},\quad\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})].

(106)

By the fact that
\|\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}=1
, one has
|\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|\leq 1
, which implies that

|\hat{f}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|\leq\lambda|\bm{q}|^{-2},\;\;j=1,2.

(107)

For
\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}},j=1,2
, one has the expansion

\begin{split}\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}}&=\langle\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}},\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle=\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\langle\hat{\bm{F}}_{\bm{k}},\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle\\
&=\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}(\hat{f}^{1}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{d}_{1}(\bm{p}),\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle+\hat{f}^{2}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{d}_{2}(\bm{p}),\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle+\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|,\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle),\end{split}

(108)

where
\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{p}}
is the
\bm{p}
th Fourier coefficient of
\varepsilon(\bm{z})
. Therefore, for
j=1,2

|\bm{q}|^{2}|\hat{f}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|\leq\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\hat{f}^{1}_{\bm{p}}|+|\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\hat{f}^{2}_{\bm{p}}|+|\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|,\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle|.

(109)

Because of Eq. (
107
), one can assume that the decay rate of
|\hat{f}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|
is
\alpha,\;j=1,2
, that is, there exists a positive constant
C_{\alpha}
independent with
\bm{q}
and
j
such that
|\hat{f}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|\leq C_{\alpha}|\bm{q}|^{-\alpha}
for both
j=1
and
2
. By Lemma
4.2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{1}
depends on
m,n,\|\mathbf{P}\|,|\mathcal{E}|_{m,\rm per}
and
\|\hat{\varepsilon}\|
, such that

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\hat{f}^{1}_{\bm{p}}|+|\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\hat{f}^{2}_{\bm{p}}|\leq C_{1}|\bm{q}|^{-\gamma_{1}},

(110)

where
\gamma_{1}=\min(m-n,\alpha-1/2)
. On the other hand, by
\bm{f}=\varepsilon^{-1}\bm{u}
,
\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{q}}=\langle\hat{\bm{f}}_{\bm{q}},\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle
has the decomposition

\begin{split}\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{q}}&=\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}(\hat{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\langle\bm{u}_{\bm{p}},\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle\\
&=\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}(\hat{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}[\hat{u}^{1}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{d}_{1}(\bm{p}),\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle+\hat{u}^{2}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{d}_{2}(\bm{p}),\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle],\end{split}

(111)

where
(\hat{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}
denotes the generalized Fourier coefficient of
\hat{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\bm{z})
with frequency
\bm{q}-\bm{p}
. Then by Eq. (

),

\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{q}}=\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}(\hat{\varepsilon}^{-1})_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}|\bm{p}|^{2}[\hat{f}^{1}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{d}_{1}(\bm{p}),\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle+\hat{f}^{2}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{d}_{2}(\bm{p}),\bm{q}/|\bm{q}|\rangle].

(112)

By the assumption that
\varepsilon
is two-sided bounded,
\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon]\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
infers to
\mathcal{E}^{-1}(\bm{x})\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Then by Lemma
4.2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
depending on
m,n,\|\mathbf{P}\|
,

|(\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon])^{-1}|_{m,\rm per}
and
\|\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1})\|
, such that

|\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{q}}|\leq C_{2}|\bm{q}|^{-\gamma_{2}}

(113)

where
\gamma_{2}=\min(m-n,\alpha-3/2)
. Then by Lemma
4.2
, there exists a positive constant
C_{3}
depends on
m,n,\|\mathbf{P}\|,|\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon]|_{m,\rm per},\|\nabla\varepsilon\|,|(\mathcal{P}[\varepsilon])^{-1}|_{m,\rm per}
and
\|\nabla(\varepsilon^{-1})\|
, such that

\sum_{\bm{p}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]}|\hat{\varepsilon}_{\bm{q}-\bm{p}}\hat{f}^{3}_{\bm{p}}\langle\bm{p}/|\bm{p}|,\bm{d}_{j}(\bm{q})\rangle|\leq C_{3}|\bm{q}|^{-\gamma_{3}},

(114)

where
\gamma_{3}=\min(m-n,\gamma_{2}+1/2)=\min(m-n,\alpha-1)
. Then by Eq. (
109
), one has
\alpha>m-n+2
. By Eq. (
107
),
|\hat{u}^{j}_{\bm{q}}|
decays with an order of

, which ends the proof.
∎

The DOF of the PM on a divergence-free basis is
O(N^{n})
, where
n
is the number of columns of the projection matrix
\mathbf{P}
. This means that when using Krylov subspace methods
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
to solve for eigenvalues, the required storage space and computation time become prohibitively large, making it infeasible to compute results for larger
N
. This undoubtedly limits the achievable accuracy.

Based on Theorem
2.2
, we can propose a reduced scheme, divergence-free reduced projection method (DF-RPM), for solving quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problems, which significantly alleviates the “curse of dimensionality". Compared to directly applying the PM on a divergence-free basis, the DF-RPM significantly reduces the DOF and computational space while maintaining accuracy. This makes it feasible to compute more spectral points for three-dimensional problems. The specific numerical scheme is detailed in the following sections.

4.2
Divergence-free reduced projection method

By the fact that the Fourier series in terms of the divergence-free basis of the eigenvalue problem exhibits a certain decay rate, the truncated quasiperiodic Fourier index sets
\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},R}
and
\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},R}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},R}=\big\{{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm col}(\mathbf{P})]\big|\;\|\phi^{-1}(\bm{q})\|_{\infty}\leq N,\|\bm{q}\|_{\infty}\leq M}\big\},

(115)

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},R}=\big\{\bm{k}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\big|\|\bm{k}\|_{\infty}\leq N,\|\mathbf{P}\bm{k}\|_{\infty}\leq M\big\}.

(116)

Then the corresponding reduced divergence-free spaces read

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{R,{\rm qp}}^{\rm div0}={\rm span}\big\{\bm{\psi}_{\bm{q}}^{j}(\bm{z}):={\bm{d}}_{j}({\bm{q}})e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{q},\bm{z}\rangle},\;j=1,2\big|\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},R}\big\},

(117)

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{R,{\rm per}}^{\rm div0}={\rm span}\big\{\bm{\Psi}_{\bm{k}}^{j}(\bm{x})=\bm{d}_{j}(\mathbf{P}\bm{k})e^{{\rm i}\langle\bm{k},\bm{x}\rangle},\;j=1,2\;\big|\bm{k}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm per},R}\big\}.

(118)

In what follows, we first give a rigorous estimate of the truncation error of the reduced space for quasiperiodic functions. For a quasiperiodic vector function
\bm{f}
and its parent function

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose that
\bm{u}
is a quasiperiodic vector function and
\bm{U}=\mathcal{P}[\bm{u}]
. If
\bm{U}\in H_{\rm per}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n})^{3},\bm{u}\in H_{\rm qp}^{m^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{3}
with
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
0<m\leq m^{\prime}
, then

\|\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}\leq M^{-m^{\prime}}|\bm{u}|_{m^{\prime},\rm qp}+N^{-m}|\bm{U}|_{m,\rm per}.

(123)

Proof.

By a direct decomposition
\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\bm{u}=\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\Pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}+\Pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}-\bm{u}
and the triangle inequality, one has

\begin{split}\|\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}&\leq\|\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\Pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}+\|\Pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}-\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}\\
&=\|\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\Pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}+\|\Pi_{\rm per}\bm{U}-\bm{U}\|_{\rm per}.\end{split}

(124)

By the Parseval’s equality,

\begin{split}\|\Pi_{\rm qp}^{R}\bm{u}-\Pi_{\rm qp}\bm{u}\|_{\rm qp}^{2}&=\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}/\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},R}}|\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}|^{2}\leq M^{-2m^{\prime}}\sum_{\bm{q}\in\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}/\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},R}}|\bm{q}|^{2m^{\prime}}|\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{q}}|^{2}\\
&\leq M^{-2m^{\prime}}|\bm{u}|^{2}_{m^{\prime},\rm qp}\end{split}

(125)

and

\|\Pi_{\rm per}\bm{U}-\bm{U}\|_{\rm per}^{2}=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}}|\hat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\leq N^{-2m}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}/\mathbb{I}_{{\rm qp},N}}|\bm{k}|^{2m}|\hat{\bm{U}}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}\leq N^{-2m}|\bm{U}|^{2}_{m,\rm per}.

(126)

Combining Eqs. (
124
), (
125
) and (
126
) ends the proof.

Remark 4.1
.

If the parent function

\|\mathbf{P}\|

6
Conclusion

We introduced projection methods based on the divergence-free basis as a precise and efficient approach for quasicrystals in three dimensions. By establishing the de Rham complex, we constructed a point-wise divergence-free quasiperiodic Fourier spectral basis, then proposed an accurate and efficient projection scheme for the quasiperiodic source problem. Thanks to the observation of the decay rate of the divergence-free Fourier coefficients of eigenfunctions, we reduced the basis space and proposed a reduced scheme for the quasiperiodic Maxwell eigenvalue problem. The theoretical perspective is supported by an error bound for the approximation, highlighting the method’s high accuracy. Numerical results in 3D problems underscore the algorithm’s efficiency and accuracy, presenting appealing characteristics for diverse practical applications.

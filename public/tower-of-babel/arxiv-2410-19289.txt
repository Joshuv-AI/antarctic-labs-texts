Abstract
. In this work, we establish modular parameterizations for two general formulas for 1 π \frac{1}{\pi} that subsume conjectural Ramanujan type formulas due to Z.-W. Sun, which have remained open since 2011. As an application of this, in a conceptual way we interpret how Sun’s conjectural formulas arise and can be verified, as well as recover other cases that were proved by Cooper, Wan and Zudilin.

1.
Introduction

In one
[
7
]
of the imperative building blocks of his influential mathematics, being facilitated by Jacobi’s elliptic integrals and hypergeometric functions Ramanujan ingeniously produces the peculiar yet very intriguing formula

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}^{3}\left(n+\frac{5}{42}\right)\frac{1}{2^{12n}}=\frac{8}{21\pi}

and additionally conjectures 17 analogous formulas that are all in form of

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)(n+b)=\frac{c}{\pi}

with
a(n),b,c
being rational numbers by which he introduces ones to a fascinating area of his mathematics called the Ramanujan type series for
\frac{1}{\pi}
. Following Ramanujan’s initiation, as is inspired by his work, relevant researchers also make use of elliptic integrals, modular forms and hypergeometric functions to validate Ramanujan’s conjectural formulas and develop new theories to produce uncharted Ramanujan type series. The reader is referred to
[
2
]
for a brief account of early developments of the topic, and
[
3
]
and the references wherein for recent progress.

Besides the aforementioned analytic tools, on the path of studying Ramanujan type series a variety of methodologies have also been developed. A notable one that is via supercongruences is due to Z.-W. Sun, who motivated by works of van Hamme
[
5
]
makes use of supercongruences for binomial-like numbers to construct vast novel Ramanujan type series and discover numerous conjectural formulas. In a series of work
[
8
,
12
,
10
,
11
]
, Sun records a large number of conjectural Ramanujan type series, such as (see
[
10

)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{2k}{k}^{2}\binom{2(n-k)}{n-k}\left(\frac{9}{4}\right)^{n-k}\right)\left(n+\frac{1}{12}\right)\left(\frac{1}{100}\right)^{n}=\frac{75}{48\pi}.

While many such formulas have been proved
over the past decade, see Sun’s book
[
9
]
for an overview,
others including the one above remained open and challenging for previous methods.
The main goal of this work is to prove two classes of conjectural Ramanujan type series of Sun that are summarized as follows.

Conjecture 4.1

(Z.-W. Sun

]
)
.

Define

(4.1)

\displaystyle U(t;x,b)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\binom{n+2k}{2k}\binom{2k}{k}x^{n-k}\right)(n+b)t^{n}

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle V(t;x,b)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\binom{2k}{k}^{\hskip-2.0pt2}\binom{2n-2k}{n-k}^{\hskip-2.0pt2}}{\binom{n}{k}^{\hskip-2.0pt-1}}x^{k}\right)(n+b)t^{n}.

Then

(4.3)

\displaystyle U\left(\frac{1}{2160};-324,\frac{103}{357}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{30}{119\pi},

(4.4)

\displaystyle U\left(\frac{1}{3645};486,0\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{10}{3\pi},

(4.5)

\displaystyle U\left(\frac{-1}{1728};-324,\frac{1}{6}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{12\sqrt{375+120\sqrt{10}}}{75\pi},

(4.6)

\displaystyle U\left(\frac{-1}{160};-20,\frac{1}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{30}}{20\pi}\times\frac{5+\sqrt[3]{145+30\sqrt{6}}}{\sqrt[6]{145+30\sqrt{6}}},

(4.7)

\displaystyle U\left(\frac{1}{27648};-2160,\frac{289}{1290}\right)

Commuting Slant Toeplitz operators on the Bergman space
Haiying Zhang

Abstract
. This paper shows that on the Bergman space of the open unit disk, the slant Toeplitz operator T p ¯ + φ T_{\overline{p}+\varphi} and T p ¯ + ψ T_{\overline{p}+\psi} commute if and only if φ = ψ \varphi=\psi , where φ \varphi and ψ \psi are both bounded analytic functions, and p p is an analytic polynomial.

47B35

1.
Introduction

Let
\varphi(z)=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\varphi}_{n}z^{n}
be a bounded measurable function on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
, where
\hat{\varphi}_{n}=\left<\varphi,z^{n}\right>
is the
n
-th Fourier series of
\varphi
and
\{z^{n}\}_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}
is the basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. The slant Toeplitz operator
A_{\varphi}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is given by the following matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{c c c c c c c}{{\ddots}}&&&&&&\\
{{\cdots}}&{{a_{-2}}}&{{a_{-3}}}&{{\cdots}}&&&\\
{{\cdots}}&{{a_{0}}}&{{a_{-1}}}&{{a_{-2}}}&{{\cdots}}&&\\
&{{\cdots}}&{{a_{1}}}&{{a_{0}}}&{{a_{-1}}}&&\\
&&{{\cdots}}&{{a_{2}}}&{{a_{1}}}&{{a_{0}}}&{{\cdots}}\\
&&&{{\cdots}}&{{a_{3}}}&{{a_{2}}}&{{\cdots}}\\
&&&&{{\ddots}}&&{{\ddots}}\\
\end{array}\right).

Let
W
be the bounded linear operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
such that

Wz^{2n}=z^{n},\,Wz^{2n+1}=0,\,n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Mark C. Ho characterized the relation between
A_{\varphi}
and
W
, that is
A_{\varphi}=WM_{\varphi}
, where
M_{\varphi}
is the multiplication operator.

The Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}
is the closed subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},dA)
consisting of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
, so the Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
is defined as

L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{D})=\Big\{f~\text{is analytic on }\mathbb{D}:\|f\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}dA<\infty\Big\},

where
dA
denotes the normalized area measure.
The Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}
possesses a natural orthonormal basis
\{1,\sqrt{2}z,\cdots,\sqrt{n+1}z^{n},\cdots\}.

For a function

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in L_{a}^{2},

its norm is given by

\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{n+1}.

For
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},dA)
, we define the slant Toeplitz operator
B_{\varphi}
with symbol
\varphi
on the Bergman space as

B_{\varphi}f=WT_{\varphi}f,

where
W
on
L_{a}^{2}
is defined by

Wz^{2n}=z^{n},\,Wz^{2n+1}=0,\,n\geq 0.

And
T_{\varphi}f=P(\varphi f)
is the Toeplitz operator on the Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}
, in which
P
is the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\mathbb{D},dA)
onto
L_{a}^{2}
.

In recent years, Brown and Halmos
[
1
]
described the commuting Toeplitz operators on the Hardy space of the unit disk, while Axler and Čučković
[
2
]
characterized the commuting Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols on the Bergman space of the unit disk in 1991. The resulting Brown-Halmos type problems have since remained a central research focus in operator theory. Over the past two decades, beyond classical Toeplitz operators, researchers have extended the investigation of Brown-Halmos type theorems to various other operator classes. In
[
3
]
, Singh and Gupta introduced the concepts of slant Toeplitz operators and
k
-th order slant Toeplitz operators on Fock spaces, derived their Berezin transforms, and established Brown-Halmos type theorems for
k
-th order slant Toeplitz operators. Liu Chaomei and Lu Yufeng
[
4
]
studied the products and commutativity of slant Toeplitz operators on the Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}
, establishing Brown-Halmos type theorems for
k
-th order slant Toeplitz operators with harmonic polynomial, analytic, and conjugate analytic symbols in
[
5
]
. Additionally, they proved the equivalence of commutativity and essential commutativity for slant Toeplitz operators in
[
6
]
, and investigated algebraic, spectral, and commutativity properties of
k
-th order slant Toeplitz operators on
L_{a}^{2}
. In
[
7
]
, Singh and Sharma investigated generalized slant Toeplitz operators with symbols in the derivative Hardy space
S^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. Subsequently, Datt and Pandey
[
8
]
extended the study of commutativity and essential commutativity relations for
k
-th order multivariate slant Toeplitz operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Furthermore, Datta
[
9
]
generalized these results to
k
-th order weighted slant Toeplitz operators on weighted sequence spaces.

These developments motivate our further investigation into the commutativity of slant Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols on the Bergman space. Notably, slant Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols on the Bergman space exhibit structural parallels to their counterparts on the Hardy space. Recalling that the harmonic extension
[
10
]
provides a natural correspondence between bounded functions on the unit circle and bounded harmonic functions on the unit disk, slant Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols on the Bergman space inherit analogous properties to those on the Hardy space. This observation drives our systematic study of commutativity for slant Toeplitz operators with harmonic symbols in the Bergman space framework.

Preliminary

Lemma 2.1
.

W^{*}Wz^{2n}=W^{*}z^{n}=\frac{2n+1}{n+1}z^{2n}
for
n=0,1,2,\cdots.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
,
W^{*}z^{n}=\frac{2n+1}{n+1}z^{2n}(n=0,1,2,\cdots)
and
\|f\|_{2}\leq\|W^{*}f\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2}\|f\|_{2}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, then
T_{\varphi}W=WT_{\varphi(z^{2})}
.

3.
Commuting Slant Toeplitz operators on the Bergman space

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f=a\overline{z}+\varphi,\,g=b\overline{z}+\psi
,
\varphi
and
\psi
be two bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. There is a constant
c
such that
B_{f}B_{g}=B_{g}B_{f}
if and only if
f=cg
.

Proof.

Let us show the sufficiency first. Suppose that
f=cg
, the result is trivial.
To prove the necessity, we divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.

We first consider the case where
a\neq 0
and
b=1
, and assume that

\varphi(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},\ \ \ \ \ \psi(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}.

Note the equality
B_{f}B_{g}=B_{g}B_{f}
is equivalent to

B_{\varphi}B_{\psi}z^{k}+aB_{\overline{z}}B_{\psi}z^{k}+B_{\varphi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}=B_{\psi}B_{\varphi}z^{k}+aB_{\psi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}+B_{\overline{z}}B_{\varphi}z^{k}

for every nonnegative integer
k
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{\varphi}B_{\psi}z^{k}=WT_{\varphi}WT_{\psi}z^{k}=WWT_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\psi}z^{k}=WWT_{\varphi(z^{2})\psi}z^{k},\\
aB_{\overline{z}}B_{\psi}z^{k}=aWT_{\overline{z}}WT_{\psi}z^{k},\\
B_{\varphi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}=WT_{\varphi}WT_{\overline{z}}z^{k}=WWT_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{k},\\
B_{\psi}B_{\varphi}z^{k}=WT_{\psi}WT_{\varphi}z^{k}=WWT_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\varphi}z^{k}=WWT_{\psi(z^{2})\varphi}z^{k},\\
aB_{\psi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}=aWT_{\psi}WT_{\overline{z}}z^{k}=aWWT_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{k},\\
B_{\overline{z}}B_{\varphi}z^{k}=WT_{\overline{z}}WT_{\varphi}z^{k}.\end{cases}

Since for every
k\geq 0
,
B_{\varphi}B_{\psi}z^{k},B_{\overline{z}}B_{\psi}z^{k},B_{\varphi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k},B_{\psi}B_{\varphi}z^{k},B_{\psi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}
and
B_{\overline{z}}B_{\varphi}z^{k}
are all analytic, letting

B_{\varphi}B_{\psi}z^{2k}+aB_{\overline{z}}B_{\psi}z^{2k}+B_{\varphi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}A_{s}z^{s},

B_{\psi}B_{\varphi}z^{2k}+aB_{\psi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}+B_{\overline{z}}B_{\varphi}z^{2k}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}B_{s}z^{s}.

The definition of the operator
W
implies that

WWz^{4s}=Wz^{2s}=z^{s},\ \ \ \ \ s\geq 0.

So we only need to consider the
4s
-th terms in
T_{\varphi(z^{2})\psi}z^{2k},\ T_{\psi(z^{2})\varphi}z^{2k},\ T_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k},\ T_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}
and the
2s
-th terms in
T_{\overline{z}}WT_{\varphi}z^{2k},\ T_{\overline{z}}WT_{\psi}z^{2k}
. Calculations show that

\displaystyle T_{\varphi(z^{2})\psi}z^{2k}=P\left(\varphi(z^{2})\psi z^{2k}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{m}z^{2n+m+2k}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}A_{1,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle aT_{\overline{z}}WT_{\psi}z^{2k}

\displaystyle=aT_{\overline{z}}W\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}z^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=aT_{\overline{z}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{2m}z^{m+k}\right)

\displaystyle=aP\left(\overline{z}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{2m}z^{m+k}\right)

\displaystyle=a\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{m+k}{m+k+1}b_{2m}z^{m+k-1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}A_{2,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle T_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi(z^{2})}P(\overline{z}z^{2k})

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi(z^{2})}\left(\frac{2k}{2k+1}z^{2k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{2k+1}a_{n}z^{2n}z^{2k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{2k+1}a_{n}z^{2n+2k-1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}A_{3,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle T_{\psi(z^{2})\varphi}z^{2k}=P\left(\psi(z^{2})\varphi z^{2k}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{m}z^{n+2m+2k}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}B_{1,s}z^{s};

\displaystyle aT_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}

\displaystyle=aT_{\psi(z^{2})}P(\overline{z}z^{2k})

\displaystyle=aT_{\psi(z^{2})}\left(\frac{2k}{2k+1}z^{2k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=aP\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{2k+1}b_{m}z^{2m}z^{2k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=a\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{2k+1}b_{m}z^{2m+2k-1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}B_{2,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle T_{\overline{z}}WT_{\varphi}z^{2k}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{z}}W\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}z^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{z}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n}z^{n+k}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\overline{z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n}z^{n+k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+k}{n+k+1}a_{2n}z^{n+k-1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}B_{3,s}z^{s}

Using
B_{\varphi}B_{\psi}z^{k}+aB_{\overline{z}}B_{\psi}z^{k}+B_{\varphi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}=B_{\psi}B_{\varphi}z^{k}+aB_{\psi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{k}+B_{\overline{z}}B_{\varphi}z^{k}
, we obtain that
A_{s}=B_{s}
for all
s\geq 0
. Note that

WWT_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}=WW\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{2k+1}a_{n}z^{2n+2k-1}\right)=0,

WWT_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k}=WW\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k}{2k+1}b_{m}z^{2m+2k-1}\right)=0.

Thus,

A_{s}=A_{1,4s}+A_{2,2s}=B_{1,4s}+B_{3,2s}=B_{s}.

Now we calculate the coefficients
A_{s}
and
B_{s}
defined above. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle A_{s}

\displaystyle=A_{1,4s}+A_{2,2s}

\displaystyle=a_{2s-k}b_{0}+a_{2s-k-1}b_{2}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4s-2k}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}ab_{4s-2k+2}

and

\displaystyle B_{s}

\displaystyle=B_{1,4s}+B_{3,2s}

\displaystyle=a_{0}b_{2s-k}+a_{2}b_{2s-k-1}+\cdots+a_{4s-2k}b_{0}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}a_{4s-2k+2}.

This implies that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2s-k}b_{0}+a_{2s-k-1}b_{2}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4s-2k}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}ab_{4s-2k+2}\\
&=a_{0}b_{2s-k}+a_{2}b_{2s-k-1}+\cdots+a_{4s-2k}b_{0}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}a_{4s-2k+2}.\end{split}

Similarly, let

B_{\varphi}B_{\psi}z^{2k+1}+aB_{\overline{z}}B_{\psi}z^{2k+1}+B_{\varphi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{2k+1}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}C_{s}z^{s},

B_{\psi}B_{\varphi}z^{2k+1}+aB_{\psi}B_{\overline{z}}z^{2k+1}+B_{\overline{z}}B_{\varphi}z^{2k+1}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}D_{s}z^{s}.

We also need to consider
4s
-th terms in
T_{\varphi(z^{2})\psi}z^{2k+1},\ T_{\psi(z^{2})\varphi}z^{2k+1},\ T_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k+1},\ T_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k+1}
and the
2s
-th terms in
T_{\overline{z}}WT_{\varphi}z^{2k+1},\ T_{\overline{z}}WT_{\psi}z^{2k+1}
. Calculations show that

\displaystyle T_{\varphi(z^{2})\psi}z^{2k+1}=P\left(\varphi(z^{2})\psi z^{2k+1}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{m}z^{2n+m+2k+1}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}C_{1,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle aT_{\overline{z}}WT_{\psi}z^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=aT_{\overline{z}}W\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{m}z^{m}z^{2k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=aT_{\overline{z}}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{2m+1}z^{m+k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=aP\left(\overline{z}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}b_{2m+1}z^{m+k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=a\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{m+k+1}{m+k+2}b_{2m+1}z^{m+k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}C_{2,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle T_{\varphi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi(z^{2})}P(\overline{z}z^{2k+1})

\displaystyle=T_{\varphi(z^{2})}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}z^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}a_{n}z^{2n}z^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}a_{n}z^{2n+2k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}C_{3,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle T_{\psi(z^{2})\varphi}z^{2k+1}=P\left(\psi(z^{2})\varphi z^{2k+1}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a_{n}b_{m}z^{n+2m+2k+1}:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}D_{1,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle aT_{\psi(z^{2})}T_{\overline{z}}z^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=aT_{\psi(z^{2})}P(\overline{z}z^{2k+1})

\displaystyle=aT_{\psi(z^{2})}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}z^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=aP\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}b_{m}z^{2m}z^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=a\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}b_{m}z^{2m+2k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}D_{2,s}z^{s},

\displaystyle T_{\overline{z}}WT_{\varphi}z^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{z}}W\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}z^{2k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=T_{\overline{z}}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n+1}z^{n+k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left(\overline{z}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{2n+1}z^{n+k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{n+k+1}{n+k+2}a_{2n+1}z^{n+k}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}D_{3,s}z^{s}.

We also obtain that
C_{s}=D_{s}
for all
s\geq 0
. Therefore,

C_{s}=C_{1,4s}+C_{2,2s}+C_{3,4s}=D_{1,4s}+D_{2,4s}+D_{3,2s}=D_{s}.

Thus, we concluded that

\displaystyle C_{s}

\displaystyle=C_{1,4s}+C_{2,2s}+C_{3,4s}

\displaystyle=a_{2s-k-1}b_{1}+a_{2s-k-2}b_{3}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4s-2k-1}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}ab_{4s-2k+1}+\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}a_{2s-k}

and

\displaystyle D_{s}

\displaystyle=D_{1,4s}+D_{2,4s}+D_{3,2s}

\displaystyle=a_{1}b_{2s-k-1}+a_{3}b_{2s-k-2}+\cdots+a_{4s-2k-1}b_{0}+\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}ab_{2s-k}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}a_{4s-2k+1}.

This follows that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2s-k-1}b_{1}+a_{2s-k-2}b_{3}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4s-2k-1}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}ab_{4s-2k+1}+\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}a_{2s-k}\\
&=a_{1}b_{2s-k-1}+a_{3}b_{2s-k-2}+\cdots+a_{4s-2k-1}b_{0}+\frac{2k+1}{2k+2}ab_{2s-k}+\frac{2s+1}{2s+2}a_{4s-2k+1}.\end{split}

Letting
s=t,k=0;\,s=t+1,k=2,
respectively, in (
3.1
), we get the following equations:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2t}b_{0}+a_{2t-1}b_{2}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4t}+\frac{2t+1}{2t+2}ab_{4t+2}\\
&=a_{0}b_{2t}+a_{2}b_{2t-1}+\cdots+a_{4t}b_{0}+\frac{2t+1}{2t+2}a_{4t+2};\end{split}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2t}b_{0}+a_{2t-1}b_{2}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4t}+\frac{2t+3}{2t+4}ab_{4t+2}\\
&=a_{0}b_{2t}+a_{2}b_{2t-1}+\cdots+a_{4t}b_{0}+\frac{2t+3}{2t+4}a_{4t+2}.\end{split}

Subtracting Equation (
3.4
) from Equation (
3.3
) yields that

\frac{2}{(2t+2)(2t+4)}ab_{4t+2}=\frac{2}{(2t+2)(2t+4)}a_{4t+2}.

Since
a\neq 0
and
\frac{2}{(2t+2)(2t+4)}\neq 0
for any

, we obtain that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{4t+2}=ab_{4t+2}.\end{split}

Similarly, by setting
s=t,k=1;\,s=t+1,k=3
in (
3.1
), we obtain the following equations:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2t-1}b_{0}+a_{2t-2}b_{2}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4t-2}+\frac{2t+1}{2t+2}ab_{4t}\\
&=a_{0}b_{2t-1}+a_{2}b_{2t-2}+\cdots+a_{4t-2}b_{0}+\frac{2t+1}{2t+2}a_{4t};\end{split}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2t-1}b_{0}+a_{2t-2}b_{2}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4t-2}+\frac{2t+3}{2t+4}ab_{4t}\\

Subtracting Equation (

\frac{2}{(2t+2)(2t+4)}ab_{4t}=\frac{2}{(2t+2)(2t+4)}a_{4t}.

Since

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{4t}=ab_{4t}.\end{split}

By (

) and (

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}a_{2t}=ab_{2t}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ t\geq 0.\end{split}

Next, by setting

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&a_{2t}b_{1}+a_{2t-1}b_{3}+\cdots+a_{0}b_{4t+1}+\frac{2t+7}{2t+8}ab_{4t+3}+\frac{11}{12}a_{2t+1}\\

Subtracting Equation (

) and subtracting Equation (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{2}{(2t+4)(2t+6)}(ab_{4t+3}-a_{4t+3})+\frac{1}{8}(a_{2t+1}-ab_{2t+1})=0,\\
\frac{2}{(2t+6)(2t+8)}(ab_{4t+3}-a_{4t+3})+\frac{1}{24}(a_{2t+1}-ab_{2t+1})=0.\end{cases}

Denoting
ab_{4t+3}-a_{4t+3}
as
M

Lemma 3.2

and

Proof.

Let us show the sufficiency first. Suppose that

, the result is trivial.
To prove the necessity, we divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.

The Case for Standardized Normalization of Tear Fluid Proteomics
Authors: Ashok Sharma, Shruti Sharma, Penny A. Asbell
Published: 2026

A Persistent and Underappreciated Problem

Tear fluid is increasingly recognized as a rich source of molecular information that reflects both ocular and systemic physiology.1, 2, 3 The minimally invasive nature of tear collection makes it an attractive substrate for biomarker discovery and translational research in ophthalmology and beyond.4, 5, 6, 7 Numerous studies have identified tear proteins associated with a range of ocular and systemic conditions, including dry eye disease, keratoconus, corneal dystrophies, scleritis, diabetic retinopathy, and neurodegenerative disorders.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 Despite this progress, tear fluid proteomics continues to face a persistent and often overlooked challenge: the lack of robust, standardized normalization strategies.15 Without appropriate normalization, validating candidate biomarkers and comparing findings across studies, laboratories, and patient cohorts remain difficult.16,17 The field needs consensus guidelines for tear fluid data normalization that combine experimental rigor, validated computational approaches, and community-wide collaboration.

Analytical Challenges and Sources of Variability in Tear Proteomics

Tear proteomics presents several analytical challenges that complicate reliable quantitative analysis. Tear sample volumes are extremely small, typically around 7 μL, and may be even lower in conditions such as dry eye disease.18 In addition to limited sample volume, tear composition is highly dynamic and sensitive to physiological and environmental influences. Factors such as circadian variation, basal versus reflex tearing, total tear volume, ocular surface stimulation, and environmental exposure can alter protein composition and concentration.19, 20, 21

Methodological differences in tear collection further contribute to variability.22,23 Commonly used techniques, including Schirmer strips, ocular wash, capillary tubes, and microsponges, vary in their effects on sample dilution, protein recovery, and levels of cellular content.16,22 Storage conditions and sample preparation can also influence proteomic measurements.24 Together, these sources of variability represent a major obstacle to reproducibility and remain a key barrier to translating tear proteomics into reliable clinical applications.15, 16, 17

Different Normalization Needs in Discovery and Validation Stages of Biomarker Research

Discovery and validation studies have different analytical objectives, and distinct normalization approaches may therefore be optimal for each phase. In early discovery studies, the primary objective is to identify candidate biomarkers or generate biological hypotheses. In these contexts, most measurements are relative rather than absolute. Normalization approaches based on global signals, such as total protein concentration, total ion current, or overall protein abundance, are therefore commonly used to account for variation in sample input and analytical performance.25 These approaches enable relative comparisons of protein levels across samples and are widely used in label-free proteomic discovery workflows.

In later stages of biomarker development, more rigorous quantitative approaches are required. Validation studies frequently use targeted mass spectrometry methods or immunoassay-based platforms, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) and multiplex immunoassays, which provide standardized and scalable measurement formats.15 In these settings, normalization typically relies on stable isotope-labeled internal standards, calibration curves, spike-in reference peptides or proteins, and internal assay controls.

Lessons from Other Proteomics Fields

The challenges associated with normalization are not unique to tear proteomics. Several other proteomics disciplines have faced similar issues and have addressed them through the development of standardized analytical frameworks. For example, plasma proteomics has benefited from harmonized workflows, standardized reference materials, and community reporting guidelines that improve reproducibility across laboratories.26,27 Large-scale collaborative initiatives have also established benchmarking data sets and shared protocols that enable more reliable cross-study comparisons and validation of candidate biomarkers.

Urinary proteomics provides another instructive example. Urine concentration varies substantially across individuals and sampling conditions; therefore, creatinine-based normalization is widely used to account for dilution effects and improve comparability across samples.28,29 Similarly, tear data sets can be systematically analyzed to identify molecules analogous to creatinine, which could be used as a single reference protein, a combination of reference proteins, or protein ratios as normalization factors.

Specific Steps to Establish Normalization Standards in Tear Proteomics

The tear proteomics community could develop normalization guidelines similar to the Minimum Information About a Proteomics Experiment standards introduced by the Human Proteome Organization (HUPO) for other biofluids.30 Such an initiative should address experimental normalization, including sample handling, protein quantification, and reference marker validation, as well as computational normalization, including batch-effect correction, variance stabilization, and scaling.

A coordinated roadmap should focus on several key priorities:1.Identification of reference proteins with stable abundance across physiological and pathological conditions to establish robust endogenous controls.2.Development of synthetic peptide or protein spike-in controls tailored specifically for tear proteomics applications.3.Standardized reporting of experimental metadata, including tear collection methods, estimated tear volume, dilution factors, and sample processing workflows.4.Implementation and rigorous benchmarking of computational normalization methods, including probabilistic quotient normalization and machine learning–based correction frameworks, validated on tear proteomic data sets.315.Systematic evaluation of normalization metrics across diverse tear samples, disease states, and collection techniques to identify approaches that ensure reliable and reproducible quantitative performance.6.Community adoption of shared repositories for deposition of raw and normalized data to promote transparency, data reuse, and reproducibility.32

Such coordinated efforts will require close collaboration among ophthalmologists, proteomics researchers, bioinformaticians, and biostatisticians. Funding agencies and journals can play a catalytic role by requiring transparent reporting of normalization methods and data sharing in tear proteomics publications.

A Call for Community Action

Standardized normalization will be essential for clinical trial biomarker development, regulatory acceptance, and translational implementation of tear-based diagnostic assays. A shared normalization framework would improve cross-study validation and help move tear-based biomarkers toward clinical use. Establishing these standards could position tear proteomics as a reliable platform for early disease detection, treatment monitoring, and personalized medicine.

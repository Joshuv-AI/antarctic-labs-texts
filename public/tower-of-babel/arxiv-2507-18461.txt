A note on the dynamics of extended-context disordered kinetic spin models
Jacob A. Zavatone-Veth, Cengiz Pehlevan

Abstract
Inspired by striking advances in language modeling, there has recently been much interest in developing autogressive sequence models that are amenable to analytical study. In this short note, we consider extensions of simple disordered kinetic glass models from statistical physics. These models have tunable correlations, are easy to sample, and can be solved exactly when the state space dimension is large. In particular, we give an expository derivation of the dynamical mean field theories that describe their asymptotic statistics. We therefore propose that they constitute an interesting set of toy models for autoregressive sequence generation, in which one might study learning dynamics.



I
Introduction

There has recently been substantial interest in autoregressive sequence modeling,
i.e.
, in models for probability distributions of the form
p(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{t-K})
for some tokens
\mathbf{s}_{t}
and a context length
K
, as modern large language models (LLMs) are fundamentally of this form
Radford et al. 2018
;
OpenAI et al. 2023
. Given the striking capabilities of such models, developing a theoretical understanding of how those abilities depend on data structure and model architecture is a pressing goal. From the perspective of the statistical physics of learning, we would like to devise a setting in which we could study how a ‘student’ model learns to imitate sequences generated by a ‘teacher’ of similar architecture
Engel and Van den
Broeck 2001
. The key challenge is to formulate an interesting yet analytically tractable class of models for distributions of this form. Recent work by
Rende et al. 2024
has shown that a modified Potts model is a suitable candidate for a teacher in the context of
masked
sequence modeling, but the autoregressive setting is still open.

What are the desiderata that a candidate toy autoregressive sequence model should satisfy? We propose that such a model should be analytically tractable, at least in some limits, be easy to sample numerically, display interesting statistical properties, e.g. tunable temporal correlations, and resemble ‘real-world’ sequence models, either in its structure or in its statistical properties. In this note, we take inspiration from statistical physics
Crisanti and Sompolinsky 1987
;
Crisanti and Sompolinsky 1988
;
Aguilera et al. 2021
;
Aguilera et al. 2023
, to propose a simple model satisfying these four desiderata.

II
Inspiration: the kinetic Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model

From a physical perspective, a natural starting point is the fully asymmetric kinetic Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (kSK) model. The kSK model is a Markov chain on
\{-1,+1\}^{N}
with

\displaystyle p_{\mathbf{J}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1})=\frac{\exp[-\beta\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{s}_{t-1}]}{\sum_{\mathbf{u}_{t}\in\{-1,+1\}^{N}}\exp[-\beta\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{J}\mathbf{s}_{t-1}]}

(1)

for an inverse temperature
\beta>0
and a quenched random interaction matrix
\mathbf{J}
with i.i.d. Gaussian elements
J_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,g^{2}/N)
. For simplicity, we will focus on the ‘pure’ SK case, where the interactions have mean zero and there is no external magnetic field to bias the state distribution.

After being studied by
Crisanti and Sompolinsky 1987
;
Crisanti and Sompolinsky 1988
in the late 1980s for various continuous-time dynamics, the discrete-time version of the kSK model has recently emerged as a paradigmatic model for the thermodynamics of nonequilibrium complex systems
Aguilera et al. 2021
;
Aguilera et al. 2023
. Moreover,
Bal 2023
has observed in a series of blog posts that the kSK model bears a strong structural resemblance to the Attention mechanism used in modern autoregressive language models. At large
N
the kSK model is amenable to solution by mean-field techniques, and it is easy to sample thanks to the fact that the sites are conditionally independent given the local field
\mathbf{h}_{t}=\mathbf{J}\mathbf{s}_{t-1}
,
i.e.
, we have the factorization

\displaystyle p_{\mathbf{J}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1})=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\exp[-\beta s_{t,j}h_{t,j}]}{2\cosh(\beta h_{t,j})}.

(2)

However, this model is insufficient for our purposes, because it exhibits single-step decorrelation,
i.e.
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\langle\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t+1}\rangle=0
for all times
t
, where
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes averaging over the random process for some fixed initial condition
\mathbf{s}_{0}
and a fixed realization of
\mathbf{J}
for a quantity depending on times up to
T
. This decorrelation follows from a simple symmetry argument. Concretely, we observe that the transformation
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{t}=-\mathbf{s}_{t}
for
t
odd,
\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{t}=\mathbf{s}_{t}
for
t
even, and
\tilde{\mathbf{J}}=-\mathbf{J}
leaves the combined measure in
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\langle\cdot\rangle
invariant for any fixed
\mathbf{s}_{0}
. This implies immediately that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\langle\mathbf{s}_{t}\rangle=\mathbf{0}
for any odd
t
, and that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\langle\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=0
whenever
t
and
t^{\prime}
have opposite parity. The reason for the structure of this transformation is that the
\mathbf{s}_{0}
-dependent term in the measure is invariant under flipping the sign of
\mathbf{J}
only if
\mathbf{s}_{1}
is also negated. This argument implies that one always has one-step decorrelation for this model,
i.e.
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\langle\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t+1}\rangle=0
. Single-step decorrelation could be avoided by adding a positive mean to
\mathbf{J}
, so as to encourage alignment between
\mathbf{s}_{t}
and
\mathbf{s}_{t-1}
, but this does not yield flexibly tunable temporal correlations
Aguilera et al. 2023
. We will therefore pursue an alternative approach.

III
Construction of the model

We now introduce the three classes of extended-context models we consider in this work. Consider an
N
-dimensional state space
\mathcal{S}_{N}
equipped with a (possibly un-normalized) probability measure
\sigma_{N}
that is reflection-symmetric,
i.e.
, invariant under
\mathbf{s}\mapsto-\mathbf{s}
for all
\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
. We consider three models:

1.

Ising
:

\mathcal{S}_{N}=\{-1,+1\}^{N}
with
\sigma_{N}
the uniform counting measure.

2.

Gaussian
: with
\mathcal{S}_{N}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\sigma_{N}
the standard Gaussian measure

3.

Spherical
:
\mathcal{S}_{N}=\{\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|\mathbf{s}\|_{2}^{2}=N\}
and
\sigma_{N}
the uniform probability measure on the sphere.

Define a Markov chain of order
K
on
\mathcal{S}_{N}
by the transition probabilities with density

p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots\mathbf{s}_{t-K})=\frac{\exp[-\beta E_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t};\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{t-K})]}{\int d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{u}_{t})\,\exp[-\beta E_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{u}_{t};\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{t-K})]}

(3)

with respect to
\sigma_{N}
, for an inverse temperature
\beta>0
and an energy function

\displaystyle E_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t};\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{t-K})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}

(4)

for a set of interaction matrices
\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. Again, we write
\langle\cdot\rangle
for the average over the random process for some fixed initial sequence
\mathbf{s}_{0},\ldots,\mathbf{s}_{1-K}
,
i.e.
, for a quantity depending on times up to
T
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle=\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{s}_{t})\,(\cdot)\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots\mathbf{s}_{t-K}).

(5)

We generalize the fully asymmetric kSK model by taking the interaction matrices
\mathbf{J}_{k}
to be jointly Gaussian, with zero mean. As a concrete model, we will focus on the case in which the interactions are uncorrelated across sites but possibly correlated across lags,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[(J_{k})_{ij}(J_{k^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}

(6)

for some correlation matrix
\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}
. Our objective is to study the resulting ensemble of random dynamical systems with quenched disorder.

For any context length, all of these models are easy to sample from numerically. In the Ising case, as for the simple kinetic SK model, we have independence of sites conditioned on the local field, as
p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots\mathbf{s}_{t-K})=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\exp(-\beta s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(\beta h_{t,j})}
for
\mathbf{h}_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}
. In the Gaussian case, we have
p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots\mathbf{s}_{t-K})\,d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{s}_{t})\propto\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}+\beta\mathbf{h}_{t}\|^{2}\right]\,d\mathbf{s}_{t}
up to normalization, so here again sites are independent conditioned on the local field. In the spherical case, we see immediately from the expression
p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots\mathbf{s}_{t-K})\propto\exp[-\beta\mathbf{h}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{s}_{t}]
that, conditioned on the context,
\mathbf{s}_{t}
follows a von Mises-Fisher distribution with mean direction
\bm{\mu}=\frac{\sqrt{N}\mathbf{h}_{t}}{\|\mathbf{h}_{t}\|}
and concentration parameter
\kappa=\frac{\beta\|\mathbf{h}_{t}\|}{N}
on the
N
-sphere of radius
\sqrt{N}
. This is easy to sample using standard algorithms
Wood 1994
. Therefore, all three models satisfy the desideratum of efficient sampling.

IV
Dynamical mean-field theory

We now turn to the questions of whether these models are analytically tractable, and if they display interesting statistical behavior. In the limit where
N\to\infty
for fixed
T
and
K
, the asymptotic statistics of these models are easy to characterize using standard dynamical field theory (DMFT) approaches from the statistical physics of disordered systems. In particular, we consider the generating function

\displaystyle Z[\{\mathbf{b}_{t}\};\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}]=\left\langle\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}\right]\right\rangle.

(7)

As usual
Crisanti and Sompolinsky 1987
;
Crisanti and Sompolinsky 1988
, the fact that
Z[\{\mathbf{b}_{t}\};\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}]|_{\{\mathbf{b}_{t}=\mathbf{0}\}}=1
for any
\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}
implies that the generating function can be averaged directly over the quenched disorder to obtain
Z[\{\mathbf{b}_{t}\}]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}Z[\{\mathbf{b}_{t}\};\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}]
, from which we can immediately compute quenched moments,
e.g.
,
\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\langle\mathbf{s}_{t}\rangle={\partial}Z[\{\mathbf{b}_{t}\}]/{\partial\mathbf{b}_{t}}|_{\{\mathbf{b}_{t}=\mathbf{0}\}}
.

In the Appendices, we give an expository derivation of the DMFT equations for each variant of the models we consider. For all three models, the DMFT order parameter is the self-averaging two-point function

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\left\langle\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right\rangle,

(8)

in terms of which the effective single-site distribution is self-consistently determined. For all three models, for
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
,
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is entirely fixed by the initial condition as
C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}
, while for
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
we have
C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
. For brevity, let

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}.

(9)

We can now state the results of the DMFT analysis.

IV.1
Ising

In the Ising case, we show in Appendix
A
that the DMFT equation is

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bm{\Sigma})}[\tanh(\beta h_{t})\tanh(\beta h_{t^{\prime}})]

(10)

for distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
and
C_{t,t}=1
. Given the fact that
\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}
depends on the covariance only at previous timesteps (
9
), this recurrence can easily be numerically solved forward in time. The only bottleneck is numerical evaluation of the expectation, which can be performed easily using Gauss-Hermite quadrature, at least at high temperatures (see Figures
1
and
2
). Here, the single-site generating function is

\displaystyle z_{j}=\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(-\beta s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(\beta h_{t,j})}

(11)

for
\mathbf{h}_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bm{\Sigma})
, which describes an Ising system in a self-consistently determined field.

Figure 2:
As in Figure
1
, but for an Ising-type model with correlated weights
\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}=\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}+r(1-\delta_{k,k^{\prime}})(-1)^{k+k^{\prime}}
for
r=0.1
. Unlike for uncorrelated wights in Figure
1
, the autocorrelation does not decay to zero over time.

IV.2
Gaussian

In the Gaussian case, we show in Appendix
B
that we have the DMFT equation

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\beta^{2}\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}

(12)

for
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
. For the Gaussian model, the single-site generating functions are simply

\displaystyle z_{j}=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}b_{t,j}b_{t^{\prime},j}\right].

(13)

Again, the causal structure means that the DMFT can be solved forward in time. However, this model is exceptionally unstable numerically, as the equal-time correlations
C_{t,t}
grow exponentially with time for most choices of
\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}
(Figure
3
).

IV.3
Spherical

The development of the DMFT for the spherical model is somewhat more complicated than that for the Ising and Gaussian models due to the global constraint on the norm of the state vector. We show in Appendix
C
how one can derive DMFT equations for this model by applying the replica trick to the normalization terms in the sequence distribution at each timestep. This leads to a DMFT in terms of
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
and a set of positive scalars
Q_{t}
, where
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
satisfies the recursive equation

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{Q_{t}Q_{t^{\prime}}}(Q_{t}\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\beta^{2}\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}})

(14)

for
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, and
Q_{t}
is determined by the self-consistency condition
C_{t,t}=1
. One finds that the single-site generating functions are in this case
z_{j}=\exp[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}b_{t,j}b_{t^{\prime},j}]
, meaning that the fluctuations are Gaussian. These equations are naturally solved via fixed point iteration. For a fixed
Q_{t}
, this is a linear recurrence for
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
which can be solved forward in time. Then, we can compute
Q_{t}
from the given
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
using the constraint
C_{t,t}=1
. Physically, the scalars
Q_{t}
are up to a shift the Lagrange multipliers that enforce the norm constraint.

V
Do we need correlated weights?

We can now simulate all three models for some choices of inverse temperature
\beta
and correlation
\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}
and check that the DMFT gives accurate predictions (and indeed it does, see Figures
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
, and
5
). We are interested mostly in the long-time properties of these DMFTs, particularly when they admit stationary solutions of the form
C_{t,t^{\prime}}=C_{t-t^{\prime}}
at long times
t,t^{\prime}\gg 1
. A natural question at this point is whether we really need correlations in weights across lags to get a non-trivial stationary state,
i.e.
, whether we could take
\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}=\gamma_{k}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}
and get interesting non-vanishing temporal correlation at long times. As anticipated by the fact that we included correlations in the setup, the answer to this question is no. With
\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}=\gamma_{k}\delta_{k,k^{\prime}}
, we have
\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\gamma_{k}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k}
. Under the assumption of stationarity,
C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k}=C_{(t-k)-(t^{\prime}-k)}=C_{t-t^{\prime}}
, hence
\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}=(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\gamma_{k})C_{t-t^{\prime}}
. This shows that correlations across lags are required to obtain non-trivial stationary correlations, as is illustrated by the gradual decorrelation in Figure
1
.

VI
Conclusions

We have analyzed the dynamics of a class of vector spin models generalizing the disordered kinetic Ising model, showing the minimal conditions required to obtain non-trivial correlation structure in the stationary state. Our note is a preliminary step towards an analyses of teacher-student learning in these toy autoregressive sequence models
Engel and Van den
Broeck 2001
. Moreover, the DMFT equations derived here may be of independent interest in the context of nonequilibrium dynamics
Mézard 2024
, as they generalize those derived for single-step context (
K=1
) models in prior works
Aguilera et al. 2021
;
Aguilera et al. 2023
;
Bal 2023
.

Appendix A
Ising DMFT

Here we give a detailed derivation of the DMFT in the Ising case.

A.1
Averaging over the disorder

We have the quenched generating function

\displaystyle Z[\mathbf{b}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}Z[\mathbf{b};\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}]

(15)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\mathbf{s}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}\right]\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots\mathbf{s}_{t-K})

(16)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\mathbf{s}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}\right]\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp\left[-\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}\right]}{\sum_{\mathbf{u}_{t}}\exp\left[-\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}\right]}.

(17)

Under the assumption that the interaction matrices are jointly Gaussian, the local fields

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{t}=-\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}

(18)

are jointly Gaussian under the distribution of the disorder, and have mean zero at all times. With the specific assumption that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[(J_{k})_{ij}(J_{k^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}},

(19)

their covariance is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j^{\prime}}]=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},

(20)

where we define the temporal correlation function of the state:

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}.

(21)

As the states are binary, we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t}=1

(22)

at all times.

A.2
Introducing order parameters

We now follow the standard procedure of introducing the correlation functions as order parameters. We enforce the definition of
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
via Fourier representations of the
\delta
-distribution with Lagrange multipliers
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
, writing

\displaystyle 1=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right],

(23)

where the integral over
\mathbf{C}
is taken over real symmetric matrices with diagonal elements identically equal to one and the integral over
\hat{\mathbf{C}}
is taken over imaginary symmetric matrices with diagonal elements identically equal to zero. We absorb the factors of
2\pi
required to normalize the
\delta
-distribution into the measure
d\hat{\mathbf{C}}
.

We then have

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\{\mathbf{s}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\sum_{\mathbf{u}_{t}}\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}]}.

(24)

As the fields
h_{t,j}
are independent and identically distributed across sites
j
, we have the factorization

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\mathbf{s}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\sum_{\mathbf{u}_{t}}\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}]}

(25)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\mathbf{s}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\prod_{j=1}^{N}2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(26)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{N}z_{j},

(27)

where we have defined the single-site generating functions

\displaystyle z_{j}=\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}.

(28)

This yields

\displaystyle Z=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp[NS],

(29)

where

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log z_{j}.

(30)

At this point, all site-specific dependence is through the initial conditions, which appear in the single-site term multiplying
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
, and through the sources. As a result, the integrals over the order parameters and Lagrange multipliers should be amenable to saddle-point evaluation at large
N
.

A.3
The saddle-point equations

We can now determine the saddle-point equations. From
\partial S/\partial\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, we have the self-consistent equation

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

(31)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,T
. Here, the single-site average
\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}
is defined via

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}=\frac{1}{z_{j}}\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}(\cdot)\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}\bigg|_{b=0}.

(32)

One must be a bit careful when considering boundary terms for which
t
or
t^{\prime}
is less than or equal to zero, as the boundary states are deterministic and fixed by the initial condition. For
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
,
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is entirely fixed by the boundary condition as

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(33)

Now suppose that
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
. Then,
s_{t^{\prime},j}
is fixed by the initial condition, and we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(34)

Our remaining task is to determine the Lagrange multipliers
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
, recalling that
\hat{C}_{t,t}=0
by definition. We first remark that the solution

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0

(35)

for all
t,t^{\prime}
is consistent with the normalization condition
Z=1
when
b=0
, as we then have

\displaystyle z_{j}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(36)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(37)

\displaystyle=1,

(38)

and
\sum_{t\neq t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, which yields
Z|_{b=0}=1
.

To show this more carefully, we consider the saddle-point equation
\partial S/\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, which gives

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(39)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}
, where we recall that we have constrained
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
to be symmetric, which compensates for the factor of 1/2. All of the dependence on
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
in
z_{j}
is contained in the covariance of
\mathbf{h}_{j}
, which for any
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
is

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}.

(40)

Then, for any times
q,q^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
and any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Sigma_{q,q^{\prime}}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\delta_{q-k,t}\delta_{q^{\prime}-k^{\prime},t^{\prime}}

(41)

so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{q,q^{\prime}=1}^{T}\frac{\partial\Sigma_{q,q^{\prime}}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{q,q^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(42)

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\sum_{q,q^{\prime}=1}^{T}\delta_{q-k,t}\delta_{q^{\prime}-k^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{q,q^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(43)

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}\{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}\geq 1\}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}.

(44)

We can evaluate the required derivatives of expectations using Price’s theorem
Price 1958
. However, we must be careful to account for the fact that
t+k
and
t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}
might coincide. First, for any distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{t,j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{t^{\prime},j}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(45)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})][s_{t^{\prime},j}-\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})]\prod_{t^{\prime\prime}=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t^{\prime\prime},j}h_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}{2\cosh(h_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}.

(46)

Second, we have the equal-time derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial h_{t,j}^{2}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(47)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\{[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})]^{2}-\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}.

(48)

Putting these results together and using the definition of the single-site average, we find that for any
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=(1-\delta_{t,t^{\prime}})\bigg\langle[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})][s_{t^{\prime},j}-\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})]\bigg\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}\bigg\langle[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})]^{2}-\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\bigg\rangle_{j},

(49)

in terms of which we have

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}\{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}\geq 1\}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(50)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}
. We emphasize that thanks to the constraint on the sum over
k
and
k^{\prime}
, the right-hand-side of this equation does not depend directly on the initial condition; all of the fields that appear are at times greater than or equal to 1.

Then, to show that the solution
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
is self-consistent, we would like to show that the expectations listed above vanish under that assumption. To do so, we will simplify the single-site generating function

\displaystyle z_{j}=\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(51)

and the resulting single-site averages using a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation. Noting that
\hat{C}_{t,t}=0
by construction, we multiply and divide by

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}(T+K)\rho\right)

(52)

for a positive real regulating parameter
\rho
, which gives

\displaystyle z_{j}=e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}(\rho\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}})s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}.

(53)

Then, letting
u_{t,j}
be a mean-zero Gaussian field with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{u}_{j}}[u_{t,j}u_{t^{\prime},j}]=\rho\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}},

(54)

we have

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1-K}^{T}u_{t,j}s_{t,j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(55)

\displaystyle=e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1-K}^{0}u_{t,j}s_{t,j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\sum_{s_{t,j}}\exp(s_{t,j}b_{t,j}+s_{t,j}h_{t,j}+s_{t,j}u_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(56)

\displaystyle=e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1-K}^{0}u_{t,j}s_{t,j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\cosh(b_{t,j}+h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})}{\cosh(h_{t,j})}.

(57)

Then, for any two distinct positive times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, we have

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial b_{t,j}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(58)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{v}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\Bigg\{\exp\left[\sum_{t=1-K}^{0}s_{t,j}u_{t,j}\right]\left[\prod_{t^{\prime}\neq t}\frac{\cosh(b_{t^{\prime},j}+h_{t^{\prime},j}+u_{t^{\prime},j})}{\cosh(h_{t^{\prime},j})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\sinh(b_{t,j}+h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})}{\cosh(h_{t,j})}\Bigg\}\bigg|_{b=0}

(59)

\displaystyle=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})\rangle_{j},

(60)

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}z_{j}}{\partial b_{t,j}\partial b_{t^{\prime},j}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(61)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{v}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\Bigg\{\exp\left[\sum_{t=1-K}^{0}s_{t,j}u_{t,j}\right]\left[\prod_{t^{\prime\prime}\neq t,t^{\prime}}\frac{\cosh(b_{t^{\prime\prime},j}+h_{t^{\prime\prime},j}+u_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}{\cosh(h_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\sinh(b_{t,j}+h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})}{\cosh(h_{t,j})}\frac{\sinh(b_{t^{\prime},j}+h_{t^{\prime},j}+u_{t^{\prime},j})}{\cosh(h_{t^{\prime},j})}\Bigg\}\bigg|_{b=0}

(62)

\displaystyle=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j}+u_{t^{\prime},j})\rangle_{j},

(63)

and

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{v}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\Bigg\{\exp\left[\sum_{t=1-K}^{0}s_{t,j}u_{t,j}\right]\left[\prod_{t^{\prime\prime}\neq t}\frac{\cosh(b_{t^{\prime\prime},j}+h_{t^{\prime\prime},j}+u_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}{\cosh(h_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\sinh(b_{t,j}+h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})}{\cosh(h_{t,j})}\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})\Bigg\}\bigg|_{b=0}

(64)

\displaystyle=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})\rangle_{j}.

(65)

Examining these results, if
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, then the correlations between different
u_{t,j}
vanish, and we can evaluate all of the expectations over the then independent variables
u_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\rho)
using the identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\rho)}\sinh(x+y)=e^{\rho/2}\sinh(y).

(66)

In particular, all of the dependence on the regulator
\rho
drops out—as should happen as it is arbitrary—and we can see that we will then have

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j})\rangle_{j},

(67)

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j})\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})\rangle_{j},

(68)

and

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

\displaystyle=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j})\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})\rangle_{j}

(69)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
.

Therefore, for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=\bigg\langle[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})][s_{t^{\prime},j}-\tanh(h_{t^{\prime},j})]\bigg\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

\displaystyle=0.

(70)

We now turn our attention to the
t=t^{\prime}
term

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bigg\langle[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})]^{2}-\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\bigg\rangle_{j}.

(71)

As
s_{t,j}
is binary, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\langle[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})]^{2}-\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\bigg\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=1-2\langle s_{t,j}\tanh(h_{t,j})\rangle_{j}+\langle\tanh(h_{t,j})^{2}\rangle_{j}-\langle\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\rangle_{j}.

(72)

Using the result above, we may write

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}\tanh(h_{t,j})\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}e^{-(T+K)\rho/2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{v}_{j},\mathbf{u}_{j}}\Bigg\{\exp\left[\sum_{t=1-K}^{0}s_{t,j}u_{t,j}\right]\left[\prod_{t^{\prime\prime}\neq t}\frac{\cosh(b_{t^{\prime\prime},j}+h_{t^{\prime\prime},j}+u_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}{\cosh(h_{t^{\prime\prime},j})}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\sinh(b_{t,j}+h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})}{\cosh(h_{t,j})}\tanh(h_{t,j})\Bigg\}\bigg|_{b=0}

(73)

\displaystyle=\langle\tanh(h_{t,j}+u_{t,j})\tanh(h_{t,j})\rangle_{j}.

(74)

From this, and following the logic above for how to evalaute the expectation over
u_{t,j}
when
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\langle[s_{t,j}-\tanh(h_{t,j})]^{2}-\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\bigg\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

\displaystyle=1-2\langle\tanh(h_{t,j})^{2}\rangle_{j}+\langle\tanh(h_{t,j})^{2}\rangle_{j}-\langle\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}\rangle_{j}

(75)

\displaystyle=\bigg\langle 1-[\tanh(h_{t,j})^{2}+\sech(h_{t,j})^{2}]\bigg\rangle_{j}

(76)

\displaystyle=0

(77)

thanks to the identity
\tanh(h)^{2}+\sech(h)^{2}=1
.

Therefore, we conclude that for any two times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, whether equal or not, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=0.

(78)

Thus, as the saddle-point equation for
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
is

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}\{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}\geq 1\}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0},

(79)

we find that the solution

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0

(80)

is self-consistent, as suggested by the normalization of the generating function.

Moreover, with
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
the single-site generating function simplifies to

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{s_{t,j}\}_{t=1}^{T}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp(s_{t,j}h_{t,j})}{2\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(81)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\cosh(b_{t,j}+h_{t,j})}{\cosh(h_{t,j})}

(82)

and the single-site averages simplify accordingly. In particular, for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, we now have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

(83)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}[\tanh(h_{t})\tanh(h_{t^{\prime}})],

(84)

as the single-site average is now manifestly site-independent. As noted previously, for
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
,
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is entirely fixed by the boundary condition as

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(85)

Finally, for mixed boundary-bulk correlators with
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
, we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}s_{t^{\prime},j}

(86)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t^{\prime},j}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{v}}[\tanh(v_{t})]

(87)

\displaystyle=0.

(88)

A.4
Summarizing the DMFT

In summary, we have derived a DMFT for the temporal correlation of the state, defined by the self-consistent equation

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}[\tanh(h_{t})\tanh(h_{t^{\prime}})]

(89)

for distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
and
C_{t,t}=1
, where
h_{t}
is a zero-mean Gaussian with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}[h_{t}h_{t^{\prime}}]=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},

(90)

For
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
,
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is entirely fixed by the initial condition as

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j},

(91)

while
C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
if
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
.

Appendix B
Gaussian DMFT

We now consider the Gaussian model. Here, we fix a prior over states

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N}),

(92)

which in the large-
N
limit will give a concentrating norm
\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}\sim N
. Writing the local field at time
t
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{t}=-\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}

(93)

the normalization factor at that time is then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{u}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})}\exp\left[-\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{u}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})}\exp\left[\mathbf{u}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}\right]

(94)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{h}_{t}\|^{2}\right),

(95)

which of course factorizes over sites. We can then write the single-step transition density as

\displaystyle p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots,t-K)=\exp\left(\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{h}_{t}\|^{2}\right),

(96)

hence the density with respect to Lebesgue measure simply becomes

\displaystyle p_{\{\mathbf{J}_{k}\}}(\mathbf{s}_{t}\,|\,\mathbf{s}_{t-1},\ldots,t-K)\frac{d\sigma_{N}}{d\mathbf{s}}(\mathbf{s}_{t})=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}-\mathbf{h}_{t}\|^{2}\right).

(97)

B.1
Averaging over the disorder

With this setup, the quenched generating function is

\displaystyle Z[\mathbf{b}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\{\mathbf{s}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{I}_{N})\}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}\right]\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{h}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{h}_{t}\|^{2}\right].

(98)

As in the Ising case, with the specific assumption that the interaction matrices are zero-mean Gaussian with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[(J_{k})_{ij}(J_{k^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}},

(99)

the local fields are zero-mean Gaussian with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j^{\prime}}]=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},

(100)

where we define the temporal correlation function of the state:

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}.

(101)

However, in this case the norm of the state
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is not fixed to unity.

B.2
Introducing order parameters

Again, we enforce the definition of
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
via Fourier representations of the
\delta
-distribution with Lagrange multipliers
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
, writing

\displaystyle 1=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right],

(102)

where in this case we include the diagonal elements of
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
and
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
. Then, everything factors over sites, yielding

\displaystyle Z=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp[NS],

(103)

where

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log z_{j}

(104)

for single-site generating functions

\displaystyle z_{j}=\mathbb{E}_{\{s_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right].

(105)

B.3
The saddle-point equations

We can now determine the saddle-point equations. From
\partial S/\partial\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, we have the self-consistent equation

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

(106)

for any two (non necessarily distinct) times
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,T
. Here, the self-consistent average is defined via

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}=\frac{1}{z_{j}}\mathbb{E}_{\{s_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}(\cdot)\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]\bigg|_{b=0}.

(107)

Again, one must be careful when considering boundary terms for which
t
or
t^{\prime}
is less than or equal to zero, as the boundary states are fixed by the initial condition. For
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
,
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is entirely fixed by the boundary condition as

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(108)

Now suppose that
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
. Then,
s_{t^{\prime},j}
is fixed by the initial condition, and we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(109)

We now consider
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
. As in the Ising case, it is easily seen that having
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
for all times is consistent with the normalization, as we then have

\displaystyle z_{j}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\{s_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

(110)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

(111)

\displaystyle=1.

(112)

As we did in the Ising case, we can show this in greater detail by considering the saddle-point equation
\partial S/\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, which yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(113)

for
t=t^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(114)

for
t\neq t^{\prime}
, where the factor of 1/2 is not present in the off-diagonal terms due to symmetry. Again, writing

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},

(115)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}\{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}\geq 1\}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}.

(116)

Applying Price’s theorem
Price 1958
, we have for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{t,j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{t^{\prime},j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

(117)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}(s_{t,j}-h_{t,j})(s_{t^{\prime},j}-h_{t^{\prime},j})\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right],

(118)

while the equal-time derivatives are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial h_{t,j}^{2}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

(119)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[(s_{t,j}-h_{t,j})^{2}-1]\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right].

(120)

Then, using the definition of the single-site average, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}=\bigg\langle(s_{t,j}-h_{t,j})(s_{t^{\prime},j}-h_{t^{\prime},j})\bigg\rangle_{j}

(121)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, while

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}=\bigg\langle[(s_{t,j}-h_{t,j})^{2}-1]\bigg\rangle_{j}

(122)

for any time
t=1,\ldots,T
. Thus, as the saddle-point equation gives
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
as a convex combination of these derivatives, to show that it is self-consistent to have
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
we would like to show that these averages vanish under that assumption. Unlike in the Ising case, this is easily done thanks to the fact that
s_{t,j}
and
h_{t,j}
are jointly Gaussian even for non-vanishing
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
. Recalling the definition of the single-site average as

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}=\frac{1}{z_{j}|_{b=0}}\mathbb{E}_{\{s_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}(\cdot)\exp\left[+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right],

(123)

we expand the term involving
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{t^{\prime}=1-K}^{0}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{0}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(124)

The term that depends only on the boundary conditions will cancel in the ratio, which leaves

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}|_{b=0}}\int d\mathbf{s}\,\int d\mathbf{h}\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{s}\\
\mathbf{h}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\mathbf{J}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{s}\\
\mathbf{h}\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{s}\\
\mathbf{h}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix}\right],

(125)

where we define the matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{T}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}&-\mathbf{I}_{T}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{T}&\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}

(126)

and the vector

\displaystyle c_{t}=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1-K}^{0}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(127)

Completing the square, this implies that the single-site average is computed for

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{s}\\
\mathbf{h}\end{pmatrix}\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{J}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix},\mathbf{J}^{-1}\right).

(128)

Our task is now to invert the block matrix
\mathbf{J}
. Noting that the push-through identity implies that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1})^{-1}=\mathbf{I}_{T}-(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1},

(129)

the formula for
2\times 2
block matrix inversion implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{T}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}&-\mathbf{I}_{T}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{T}&\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}

(130)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}[(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}]^{-1}&\mathbf{B}\\
\mathbf{B}^{\top}&[\mathbf{I}_{T}-(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}](\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{B})\end{pmatrix}

(131)

where we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=[(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}]^{-1}[\mathbf{I}_{T}-(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}]

(132)

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{I}_{T}-(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})\hat{\mathbf{C}}]^{-1}\mathbf{\Sigma}.

(133)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}[(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}]^{-1}\mathbf{c}\\
\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{c}\end{pmatrix},

(134)

hence

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}-h_{t,j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=[(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}]^{-1}\mathbf{c}-\mathbf{B}^{\top}\mathbf{c}

(135)

\displaystyle=[\mathbf{I}_{T}-\hat{\mathbf{C}}(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})]^{-1}\mathbf{c}.

(136)

Then, noting that

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}^{-1}\bigg|_{\hat{C}=0}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma}&\mathbf{\Sigma}\\
\mathbf{\Sigma}&\mathbf{\Sigma},\end{pmatrix}

(137)

we can then see that, if
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
for all
t,t^{\prime}
, we have the single-site averages

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}-h_{t,j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=0

(138)

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}

(139)

\displaystyle\langle s_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}

(140)

\displaystyle\langle h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}.

(141)

From this, it is easy to see that, for
t\neq t^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\bigg\langle(s_{t,j}-h_{t,j})(s_{t^{\prime},j}-h_{t^{\prime},j})\bigg\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}=0}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}-\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}-\Sigma_{t^{\prime},t}+\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}=0,

(142)

while

\displaystyle\bigg\langle[(s_{t,j}-h_{t,j})^{2}-1]\bigg\rangle_{j}=1+\Sigma_{t,t}-2\Sigma_{t,t}+\Sigma_{t,t}-1=0.

(143)

Therefore, we conclude at last that the
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
solution is consistent with the saddle-point equations, and that under this condition we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}

(144)

\displaystyle=\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}

(145)

\displaystyle=\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}

(146)

for all interior times
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, while the boundary terms for
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
vanish:

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0.

(147)

More directly, with
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, the single-site generating function simplifies to

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\{s_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}^{2}\right]

(148)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\{s_{t,j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\}}\det(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1/2}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}s_{t,j}(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1})^{-1}s_{t,j}\right]

(149)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}b_{t,j}[\mathbf{I}_{T}-(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1})^{-1}]^{-1}b_{t,j}\right]

(150)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}b_{t,j}(\mathbf{I}_{T}+\mathbf{\Sigma})b_{t,j}\right].

(151)

Then, we can immediately read off that the site distribution is zero-mean Gaussian, with the covariance claimed above.

B.4
Summarizing the DMFT

To summarize, the DMFT for the Gaussian model is given by

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}

(152)

for
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
,

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0

(153)

for
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
, and

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}

(154)

for
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
.

Unlike in the Ising case, the equation for
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is now purely linear. We observe that some features of this model cannot carry over to the spherical case, as here
C_{t,t}
may exceed unity.

Appendix C
Spherical DMFT

We now consider the spherical model. Using the notation introduced above, we can read off that the disorder average leads to the quenched generating function

\displaystyle Z=\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{s}_{t})\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}\right]\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\int d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{u}_{t})\,\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}

(155)

where the local fields

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{t}=-\beta\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbf{J}_{k}\mathbf{s}_{t-k}

(156)

are jointly Gaussian under the distribution of the disorder, and have mean zero at all times. With the specific assumption that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[(J_{k})_{ij}(J_{k^{\prime}})_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}]=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{ii^{\prime}}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}},

(157)

their covariance is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j^{\prime}}]=\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},

(158)

where we define the temporal correlation function of the state:

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{s}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}.

(159)

The spherical constraint means that we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t}=1

(160)

at all times.

C.1
Introducing order parameters

As for the other two models, we enforce the definition of
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
via Fourier representations of the
\delta
-distribution with Lagrange multipliers
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
, writing

\displaystyle 1=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right],

(161)

where in this case we include the diagonal elements of
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
and
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
. This yields

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{C}\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\exp\left[-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{s}_{t})\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\int d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{u}_{t})\,\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}

(162)

Our goal is to factorize

\displaystyle\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{s}_{t})\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\int d\sigma_{N}(\mathbf{u}_{t})\,\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}

(163)

over sites. Given that constant multiplicative factors will cancel thanks to the normalization terms, we can re-write this as

\displaystyle\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\mathbf{s}_{t}\,\delta(N-\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2})\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}{\int d\mathbf{u}_{t}\,\delta(N-\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|^{2})\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}]}

(164)

\displaystyle=\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\mathbf{s}_{t}\,\delta(N-\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2})\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\exp[\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}-\rho\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}/2]}{\int d\mathbf{u}_{t}\,\delta(N-\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|^{2})\exp[\mathbf{u}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}-\rho\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|^{2}/2]}

(165)

for any positive
\rho
, as the spherical constraint enforces that
\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{u}_{t}\|^{2}=N
for all times
t
.

At this point, we would like to replace the
\delta
-distributions with their Fourier transforms and interchange the order of integration over the states and Lagrange multipliers, as that would allow us to factor the integral over sites into a product of single-site generating functions. However, the normalizing factor in the denominator means that we cannot proceed so simply. This can be circumvented using the replica trick: we write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}=\lim_{n\to 0}x^{n-1}.

(166)

More concretely, we introduce
n_{t}-1
copies of the denominator for the
t
-th timestep. As usual, we will proceed for
n_{t}-1
a non-negative integer, and then analytically continue the result. Indexing replicas by
a_{t}=1,\ldots,n_{t}
, the state-dependent term above becomes

\displaystyle\lim_{\{n_{t}\to 0\}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}d\mathbf{s}_{t}\,\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}\prod_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}d\mathbf{u}_{t}^{a_{t}}\,\left[\prod_{t=1}^{T}\delta(N-\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2})\prod_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}}\delta(N-\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{a_{t}}\|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{b}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{h}_{t}-\frac{1}{2}\rho\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\mathbf{h}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}^{a_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\rho\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{a_{t}}\|^{2}\right].

(167)

We now replace the
\delta
-distributions with their Fourier transforms, introducing Lagrange multipliers
\hat{Q}_{t}
to enforce the constraints
\|\mathbf{s}_{t}\|^{2}=N
and
\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}
to enforce the constraints
\|\mathbf{u}_{t}^{a_{t}}\|^{2}=N
. Interchanging the order of integration, this at last allows us to factor the integrals over sites, yielding

\displaystyle Z=\int d\mathbf{C}\,\int d\hat{\mathbf{C}}\,\int d\hat{\mathbf{Q}}\,\int d\hat{\mathbf{R}}\,\exp[NS]

(168)

for an action

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}C_{t,t^{\prime}}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\hat{Q}_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\log z_{j},

(169)

where

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}ds_{t,j}\,\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}\prod_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}du_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(s_{t,j})^{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})(u_{t,j}^{a_{t}})^{2}\right]

(170)

are the single-site generating functions.

C.2
The saddle-point equations

Differentiating with respect to
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
for any two (perhaps equal) times
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,T
, we of course have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}.

(171)

Here, the self-consistent average is defined via

\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{j}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}ds_{t,j}\,\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}\prod_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}du_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\,(\cdot)\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(s_{t,j})^{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})(u_{t,j}^{a_{t}})^{2}\right]\bigg|_{b=0}.

(172)

As in the Ising and Gaussian cases, one must be careful when considering boundary terms for which
t
or
t^{\prime}
is less than or equal to zero, as the boundary states are fixed by the initial condition. For
t,t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
,
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
is entirely fixed by the boundary condition as

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(173)

Now suppose that
t=1,\ldots,T
and
t^{\prime}=1-K,\ldots,0
. Then,
s_{t^{\prime},j}
is fixed by the initial condition, and we have

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}s_{t^{\prime},j}.

(174)

Differentiating with respect to
\hat{Q}_{t}
, we have the constraint

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle(s_{t,j})^{2}\rangle_{j}

(175)

while differentiating with respect to
\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}
, we have

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle(u_{t,j}^{a_{t}})^{2}\rangle_{j}

(176)

Observing that the auxiliary fields
u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}
couple to
h_{t,j}
in the same way as
s_{t,j}
, we make a replica-uniform
Ansatz
for the Lagrange multipliers

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}=\hat{Q}_{t}.

(177)

Then, the integrals over
u_{t,j}
factor, leaving

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}ds_{t,j}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(s_{t,j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\left\{\int du_{t,j}\,\exp\left[h_{t,j}u_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(u_{t,j})^{2}\right]\right\}^{n_{t}-1}

(178)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}ds_{t,j}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(s_{t,j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\left\{\left(\frac{2\pi}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{(h_{t,j})^{2}}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\right]\right\}^{n_{t}-1}

(179)

\displaystyle\overset{n_{t}\to 0}{=}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}ds_{t,j}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1-K}^{T}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(s_{t,j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\right)^{-1/2}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(h_{t,j})^{2}}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\right]

(180)

We can then see that
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
is consistent with normalization.

To show more carefully that these solutions are consistent, we again turn to the saddle point equation for
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
. As in the Ising and Gaussian cases, this gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\hat{C}_{t,t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(181)

for equal times and

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

(182)

for distinct times
t\neq t^{\prime}
. Once again, we write

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}\equiv\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{J}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}},

(183)

which gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial C_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}=\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}\{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}\geq 1\}\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t+k,t^{\prime}+k^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}.

(184)

Isolating the relevant portion of the single-site generating function, we have for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right]

(185)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{t,j}}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{t^{\prime},j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right]

(186)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\left(s_{t,j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right)\left(s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}u_{t^{\prime},j}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}\right)\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right]

(187)

while the equal-time derivatives are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right]

(188)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial h_{t,j}^{2}}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right]

(189)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\left(s_{t,j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right)^{2}\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right].

(190)

Using the definition of the single-site average, we then have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(s_{t,j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right)\left(s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}u_{t^{\prime},j}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}\right)\right\rangle_{j}

(191)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}
, while

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\left(s_{t,j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{j}

(192)

Importantly, in these expectations
t
and
t^{\prime}
are constrained to be strictly greater than zero.

We can evaluate these expectations using the fact that the single-site distribution of the fields at positive times is in fact Gaussian. We condition on the fields
h_{t,j}
, set
b_{t,j}=0
, and consider the joint density of the fields
s_{t,j}
and
u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}
. Defining the matrix

\displaystyle A_{t,t^{\prime}}=(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}-\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}},

(193)

for
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
and the vector

\displaystyle c_{t,j}=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1-K}^{0}\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t^{\prime},j}

(194)

and using the fact that terms depending on
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}
for both
t,t^{\prime}\leq 0
will drop as they contribute a constant, we have that their joint density is given up to normalization as

\displaystyle\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}A_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}(h_{t,j}+c_{t,j})+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})(u_{t,j}^{a_{t}})^{2}\right]

(195)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}A_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}(h_{t,j}+c_{t,j})\right]\prod_{t=1}^{T}\prod_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})(u_{t,j}^{a_{t}})^{2}-h_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right].

(196)

We can then see that the replica fields
u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}
are independent from the fields
s_{t,j}
and are independent across replicas and time, with variance

\displaystyle\var[u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}|\mathbf{h}_{j}]=\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}}

(197)

and mean

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}|\mathbf{h}_{j}]=\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}}h_{t,j}.

(198)

Similarly, the fields
s_{t,j}
are Gaussian, with covariance

\displaystyle\cov[s_{t,j},s_{t^{\prime},j}|\mathbf{h}_{j}]=(A^{-1})_{t,t^{\prime}}

(199)

and mean

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[s_{t,j}|\mathbf{h}_{j}]=\sum_{t^{\prime}=1}^{T}(A^{-1})_{t,t^{\prime}}(h_{t,j}+c_{t,j}).

(200)

Though we can compute the required correlators using these full expressions, we recall that our goal is to show that they vanish provided that
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
and
\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}=\hat{Q}_{t}
. We will therefore assume that
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, which yields the drastic simplification

\displaystyle\cov[s_{t,j},s_{t^{\prime},j}|\mathbf{h}_{j}]\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\delta_{t,t^{\prime}},

(201)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[s_{t,j}|\mathbf{h}_{j}]\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}h_{t,j}.

(202)

Then,

Using these facts, we can easily compute the unequal-time correlators as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(s_{t,j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right)\left(s_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}u_{t^{\prime},j}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}\right)\right\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

(203)

\displaystyle=\langle s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\langle u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}s_{t^{\prime},j}\rangle_{j}+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}\langle s_{t,j}u_{t^{\prime},j}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}\rangle_{j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}\langle u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}u_{t^{\prime},j}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

(204)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t^{\prime}})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t^{\prime}})(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(\rho+\hat{R}_{t^{\prime}}^{a_{t^{\prime}}})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})(\rho+\hat{R}_{t^{\prime}}^{a_{t^{\prime}}})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}]

(205)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t^{\prime}}}+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t^{\prime}}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}}\right)\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}

(206)

for any two distinct times
t,t^{\prime}
, while the equal-time correlators are

\displaystyle 2\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(s_{t,j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\right)^{2}\right\rangle_{j}

(207)

\displaystyle=\langle s_{t,j}s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}+2\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\langle s_{t,j}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\rangle_{j}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\langle u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}u_{t,j}^{a_{t}}\rangle_{j}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}

(208)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}+\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad+2\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}^{2}]

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\sum_{a_{t}^{\prime}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\left(\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}}\delta_{a_{t}a_{t}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}})(\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}^{\prime}})}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}[h_{t,j}^{2}]\right)

(209)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\frac{1}{1+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}+\sum_{a_{t}=1}^{n_{t}-1}\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t^{\prime}}}+\sum_{a_{t^{\prime}}=1}^{n_{t^{\prime}}-1}\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{R}_{t^{\prime}}^{a_{t^{\prime}}}}\right)\Sigma_{t,t}

(210)

From this, we can see immediately that the replica-uniform
Ansatz

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{t}^{a_{t}}=\hat{Q}_{t}

(211)

gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}}\bigg|_{b=0}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=n_{t}n_{t^{\prime}}\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t^{\prime}})}\Sigma_{t,t^{\prime}}

(212)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z_{j}}\frac{\partial z_{j}}{\partial\Sigma_{t,t}}\bigg|_{b=0}\bigg|_{\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0}=n_{t}\frac{1}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}+n_{t}^{2}\frac{1}{(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})^{2}}\Sigma_{t,t},

(213)

which vanish as
n_{t}\downarrow 0
, meaning that the
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
solution is self-consistent in this case.

With
\hat{C}_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
, the single-site generating function becomes

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{h}_{j}}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}ds_{t,j}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho+\hat{Q}_{t})(s_{t,j})^{2}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}s_{t,j}h_{t,j}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod_{t=1}^{T}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\right)^{-1/2}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\frac{(h_{t,j})^{2}}{\rho+\hat{Q}_{t}}\right].

(214)

Defining the diagonal matrix
\mathbf{Q}
by

\displaystyle Q_{t,t}=\rho+\hat{Q}_{t},

(215)

we have

\displaystyle z_{j}

\displaystyle=(\det\mathbf{Q})^{+1/2}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{ds_{t,j}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}Q_{t,t}(s_{t,j})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\times(\det\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1/2}\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{dh_{t,j}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}(\Sigma^{-1}+Q^{-1})_{t,t^{\prime}}h_{t,j}h_{t^{\prime},j}+\sum_{t=1}^{T}h_{t,j}s_{t,j}\right]

(216)

\displaystyle=\det(\mathbf{Q}^{-1}+\mathbf{Q}^{-1}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{Q}^{-1})^{-1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int\prod_{t=1}^{T}\frac{ds_{t,j}}{(2\pi)^{1/2}}\,\exp\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_{t,j}s_{t,j}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}[\mathbf{Q}-(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}+\mathbf{Q}^{-1})^{-1}]_{t,t^{\prime}}s_{t,j}s_{t^{\prime},j}\right]

(217)

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t,t^{\prime}=1}^{T}[\mathbf{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{\Sigma})\mathbf{Q}^{-1}]_{t,t^{\prime}}b_{t,j}b_{t^{\prime},j}\right],

(218)

where we observe that the Woodbury identity implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{Q}-(\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}+\mathbf{Q}^{-1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{\Sigma})^{-1}\mathbf{Q}.

(219)

This gives

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=[\mathbf{Q}^{-1}(\mathbf{Q}+\mathbf{\Sigma})\mathbf{Q}^{-1}]_{t,t^{\prime}}

(220)

for
t,t^{\prime}=1,\ldots,T
, which as
Q
is diagonal reduces to

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{Q_{t}Q_{t^{\prime}}}\left(Q_{t}\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}\right).

(221)

To determine
Q_{t}
, we use the self-consistency condition

\displaystyle C_{t,t}=1;

(222)

as
\rho
is arbitrary we can absorb it into the re-definition. Moreover, we have
\langle s_{t,j}\rangle_{j}=0
, hence
C_{t,t^{\prime}}=0
if
t>0
and
t^{\prime}\leq 0
.

C.3
Summarizing the DMFT

To summarize, for the spherical model we have obtained a DMFT in terms of the two-point functions

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{N}\mathbf{s}_{t}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{t^{\prime}}

(223)

and a set of positive scalars
Q_{t}
, where
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
satisfies the recursive equation

\displaystyle C_{t,t^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{Q_{t}Q_{t^{\prime}}}\left(Q_{t}\delta_{t,t^{\prime}}+\beta^{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}=1}^{K}\Gamma_{k,k^{\prime}}C_{t-k,t^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}\right)

(224)

and
Q_{t}
is determined by the self-consistency condition

\displaystyle C_{t,t}=1.

(225)

These DMFT equations are naturally solved via fixed point iteration. For a fixed
Q_{t}
, this is a linear recurrence for
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
which can be solved forward in time. Then, we can compute
Q_{t}
from the given
C_{t,t^{\prime}}
using the constraint
C_{t,t}=1
. This alternating procedure can be iterated until convergence.

Appendix D
Numerical methods

All simulations were done using MATLAB R2024b (The MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA), and were run on a desktop workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon(R) w5-3525 processor and 128 GB of RAM. They were not compute-intensive, requiring well under one CPU-hour. For the spherical model, we used Sungkyu Jung’s freely available implementation of
Wood 1994
’s algorithm for sampling from the von Mises-Fisher distribution, available at
https://www.stat.pitt.edu/sungkyu/oldSoftwarePage.html
.

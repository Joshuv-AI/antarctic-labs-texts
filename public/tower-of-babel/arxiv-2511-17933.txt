Lower bound on Néron-Tate height of Abelian varieties
Sushant Kala

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
A
be an abelian variety defined over a number field
K
and
\hat{h}
be the Néron–Tate height on
A(\overline{K})
corresponding to a symmetric ample line bundle on
A
. Let
\mathcal{K}/K
be an asymptotically positive infinite extension as defined in
[
11
]
which includes infinite Galois extensions with finite local degree at a non-archimedean place. In this article, we prove that the Néron–Tate height of non-torsion points in
A(\mathcal{K})
is bounded below by an absolute constant depending only on
A
,
K
and
\mathcal{K}
. As a consequence, we obtain the Bogomolov property for totally
p
-adic points of an abelian variety
A/\mathbb{Q}
in the case when
A
has good reduction at
p
. This is the first instance where such a result has been obtained in the good reduction case; previously, it was known only in the bad reduction case via Gubler’s tropical equidistribution theorem. Moreover, our result also implies the finiteness of torsion points in
A(\mathcal{K})
.

1.
Introduction

The study of points of small height is a classical theme in Diophantine geometry. Inspired by the famous Bogomolov conjecture, Bombieri and Zannier
[
4
]
defined a property for subsets of algebraic numbers concerning points of small height. Let
h
denote the logarithmic Weil height of algebraic numbers and let
S\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. We say that
S
has the Bogomolov property (B) if

\liminf_{\alpha\in S\setminus\mu_{\infty}}h(\alpha)>0,

where
\mu_{\infty}
denotes the set of all roots of unity. It follows from a theorem of Northcott that subsets of algebraic numbers of bounded degree satisfy property (B), and furthermore, it satisfy Northcott property. Thus, an interesting question is to investigate property (B) for infinite extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
. This notion of obtaining absolute lower bound on height has also been studied in the more geometric setting for elliptic curves and abelian varieties.

Let
A/K
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
, defined over a number field
K
. Suppose
\mathcal{L}
is a symmetric ample line bundle on
A/K
, and let
\hat{h}
denote the canonical height associated to
\mathcal{L}
. For an infinite extension
\mathcal{K}/K
, we say
A(\mathcal{K})
has Bogomolov property if

\liminf_{P\in A(\mathcal{K})/A_{tor}}\hat{h}(P)>0,

where
A_{tor}
denote the set of torsion points of
A
.

In
[
11
]
, A. Dixit and the author introduced the notion of asymptotically positive extensions. For a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
and a rational prime power
q=p^{f}
, define
\mathcal{N}_{q}(K)
to be the number of prime ideals of
K
with norm
q
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be an infinite algebraic extension
\mathcal{K}/\mathbb{Q}
and write it as a tower of number fields

\mathcal{K}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq K_{m}\supsetneq K_{m-1}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq K_{0}=\mathbb{Q},

where
K_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
are finite extensions. Define

\psi_{q}(\mathcal{K})=\psi_{q}:=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{q}(K_{i})}{[K_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]}.

We say
\mathcal{K}
is
asymptotically positive extension
if
\psi_{q}>0
for some prime power
q
. For example, the maximal totally
p
-adic extension
\mathbb{Q}^{(p)}
is an asymptotically positive extension with
\psi_{p}=1
.

We fix
A,K
and
\hat{h}
as above to state our results. The main theorem of this article is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a finite set of primes
\mathcal{P}
such that for any asymptotically positive extension
\mathcal{K}/K
with
\psi_{p^{f}}>0
for
p\notin\mathcal{P}
, we have

\liminf_{P\in A(\mathcal{K})}\hat{h}(P)\geq C(A,K)\ \psi_{p^{f}}\frac{\log p^{f}}{(p^{f})^{2g}},

where
C(A,K)
is an absolute constant depending only on
A
and
K
.

Remark.
The finite set of primes
\mathcal{P}
in Theorem
1.1
depends only on the abelian variety
A
and the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. It contain all the primes below the places of
K
at which
A
has bad reduction.

In the special case where
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{Q}^{(p)}
, the maximal totally
p
-adic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, above theorem implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

For all but finitely many primes
p
, we have

\liminf_{P\in A(\mathbb{Q}^{(p)})}\hat{h}(P)\geq C(A)\frac{\log p}{p^{2g}},

where
C(A)
is an absolute constant depending only on
A
.

This result provides an affirmative answer to a question of V. Dimitrov
[
10
]
by establishing, for the first time, the Bogomolov property for totally
p
-adic points of an abelian variety in the good reduction case.

In a more general setup, we have obtained the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}/K
be an infinite extension lying over
K
. Then

\liminf_{P\in A(\mathcal{K})}\hat{h}(P)\geq\ C(A,K)\ \sum_{q=p^{k}}^{\prime}\psi_{q}\frac{\log q}{q^{2g}},

where the summation runs over all prime powers
\{p^{k}\ |\ p\notin\mathcal{P}\}
for a finite set of primes
\mathcal{P}
and
C(A,K)
is an absolute constant depending only on
A
and
K
.

Now we mention some earlier known results concerning absolute lower bound on canonical height of points on an abelian variety lying in an infinite extension. The first result of this kind was proved by S. Zhang
[
19
]
using an equidistribution theorem due to Szpiro-Ulmo-Zhang
[
18
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(S. Zhang
[
19
]
)
.

Let
A/K
be an abelian variety over a number field
K
. If
K\subset L

and
L
is a finite extension of a totally real field, then
A(L)
has the Bogomolov property.

In 2004, Baker and Silverman
[
2
]
showed that for any abelian variety
A/K
the set of points
A(K_{ab})
satisfy Bogomolov property, where
K^{ab}
denote the maximal abelian extension of
K
inside
\overline{K}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Baker-Silverman
[
2
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}/K
be an abelian variety and
\widehat{h}_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}
be the canonical height on
\mathcal{A}
with respect to a symmetric ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. Then there exists a constant
C(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{L},K)>0
such that

\widehat{h}_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}(P)>C(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{L},K)

for all
P\in\mathcal{A}(K^{ab})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{tor}
.

Let
K^{\prime}
be an infinite Galois extension of
K
. We say
K^{\prime}
is totally
v
-adic of type
(e,f)
if there exists an embedding of
K^{\prime}\hookrightarrow K_{v}
, where
v
is a finite place of
K
with ramification index
e
and inertia degree
f
. Towards the non-archimedean analogue of Zhang’s result, the Bogomolov property for totally
v
-adic points of type
(e,f)
of abelian varieties
A/K
of dimension one (i.e. elliptic curves) was deduced by Baker and Petsche
[
1
]
. This was generalized to asymptotically positive extensions by A. Dixit and the author in
[
11
]
, where the lower bound obtained on the canonical height does not depend on the elliptic curve.

For higher dimensional abelian variety the first result in this direction followed from a tropical equidistribution theorem due to W. Gubler
[
13
]
. We denote by
K^{nr,v}
the maximal algebraic extension of a number field unramified at a finite place
v\in M_{K}
.

Theorem 1.6

(Gubler
[
13
]
)
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
A
be an abelian variety defined over
K
which is totally degenerate at a finite place
v\in M_{K}
. Further, let
L
be a symmetric ample line bundle and
K^{\prime}
be a finite extension of
K^{nr,v}
. Then there is an
\epsilon>0
such that
\hat{h}_{\mathcal{L}}(P)\geq\epsilon
for all non-torsion points
P\in A(K^{\prime})
. Moreover, there are only finitely many torsion points in
A(K^{\prime})
.

Clearly every totally
v
-adic field of type
(e,f)
can always be seen as a subfield of
K^{nr,v}
for some number field
K
. Hence above result implies Bogomolov property for totally
v
-adic points of an abelian variety in the totally degenerate case. For
A/\mathbb{Q}
, when
A
has good reduction at
p
, a non-archimedean equidistribution theorem of Chambert-Loir’s
[
7
]
establishes Bogomolov property for a dense Zariski open subset of
A(\mathbb{Q}^{(p)})
.

In more general context, a generalized Lehmer conjecture due to David and Hindry
[
9
, Conjecture 1.4]
predict lower bound on height of non-torsion points (in terms of their degree) on abelian varieties. The best unconditional bound towards this conjecture is due to D. Masser
[
15
]
and stronger lower bound for CM abelian varieties are known due to S. David and M. Hindry
[
9
]
. Interested reader can can look at
[
17
, Chapter 8]
for a nice exposition and survey on lower bound on canonical height of abelian varieties.

The key ideas used in the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
are inspire by the classical auxiliary function and zero estimate techniques from transcendental number theory. Our proof is inspired by
[
9
]
, where these techniques have been employed in more geometric setting in context of abelian varieties. The organization of this article is as follows.

1.1.
Organization

This article is divided into
6
sections including the introduction. In section
2
, we recall a few definitions and results regarding the canonical height, and discuss the notion of differentiating a section of an abelian variety. Section
3
discusses construction of an auxiliary section
F
vanishing at origin using Siegel lemma and section
4
is devoted to height estimate using this auxiliary function and setting parameters. Section
5
, we use the zero estimate result due to Philippon to get an estimate of number of zeros of
F
of small height. In section
6
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Heights

A place
v
of a number field
K
is an absolute value
\left|\ \cdot\ \right|_{v}:K\rightarrow[0,\infty)
whose restriction to
\mathbb{Q}
is either the standard absolute value or a
p
-adic absolute value for some prime
p
with
|p|=p^{-1}
. We set
d_{v}=\left[K_{v}:\mathbb{R}\right]
in the former and
d_{v}=\left[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}\right]
in the latter case. The absolute, logarithmic, projective Weil height, or just height, of a point
P=\left[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n}\right]\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}(K)
with
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\in K
is

h(P)=\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{v}d_{v}\log\max\left\{\left|x_{0}\right|_{v},\ldots,\left|x_{n}\right|_{v}\right\}

where the sum runs over all places
v
of
K
. The value
h(P)
is independent of the choice of projective coordinates by the product formula. For this and other basic facts, we refer to Hindry-Silverman. Moreover, the height does not change when replacing
K
by another number field that contains the
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}
. Therefore,
h(\cdot)
is well defined on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{n}(\bar{K})
where
\bar{K}
is an algebraic closure of
K
.

Now let
K
be a number field as above. Suppose that
A
is an abelian variety defined over
K
that is embedding in some projective space
\mathbb{P}_{K}^{n}
with a symmetric ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
A
. Let
h_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}
be the height function on
A(\overline{K})
corresponding to the Weil height of projective space under the embedding induced by
\mathcal{L}
. Although
h_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}
satisfy the finiteness property (
Northcott’s theorem
) and hence provide a partial order on
A(L)
, for finite extensions
L/K
, it is not compatible with the group law of the abelian variety
A
. Néron and Tate introduce the canonical height on
A(\overline{K})
which is compatible with the group law of
A
and is unique up to the linear equivalence of line bundles.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Néron-Tate) Let
A/K
be an abelian variety and
\mathcal{L}
be a symmetric ample line bundle on
A/K
. Then there exists a unique function, called the canonical height on
A(\overline{K})
relative to
\mathcal{L}
,

\widehat{h}_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}\,:\,\mathcal{A}(\overline{K})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},

such that

(1)

\widehat{h}_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}(P)=h_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}(P)+O(1)
for all
P\in A(\overline{K})
,

(2)

\widehat{h}_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}([2](P))=4\,\widehat{h}_{A,\,\mathcal{L}}(P)
for all
P\in A(\overline{K})
.

The Néron-Tate height depends on the choice of the symmetric ample line bundle. We often use
\hat{h}
to denote the canonical height on abelian variety coming from a symmetric ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
when there is no ambiguity. Tate’s limit process induces the Néron-Tate or canonical height on
A(\bar{K})
. If
K
is a number field, we write

\hat{h}_{A}(P)=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{h\left(\left[2^{N}\right]\cdot P\right)}{4^{N}}

for the Néron-Tate height on
A(\bar{K})
. The canonical height
\hat{h}_{A}
associated to
\mathcal{L}
on an abelian variety
A/K
has many interesting properties.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
A/K
be an abelian variety and
\hat{h}_{A}
be the canonical height on
A
with respect to a symmetric ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. Then, for
P,Q\in A(\overline{K})

(1)

\hat{h}(P)=0
iff
P\in A_{tor}
,

(2)

\hat{h}(P+Q)+\hat{h}(P-Q)=2\,\hat{h}(P)+2\,\hat{h}(Q)
,

(3)

\hat{h}([n]\,P)=n^{2}\,\hat{h}(P)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Interested readers can find proofs of the above properties and more detailed discussions on canonical heights on abelian varieties in
[
14
, Section B.5]
.

2.2.
Asymptotically positive extensions

We recall here the definition of asymptotically positive extensions introduced by the authors in
[
11
]
. For a number field
K/\mathbb{Q}
and a rational prime power
q=p^{f}
, define

\mathcal{N}_{q}(K):=\text{ the number of prime ideals of }K\text{ with norm }q.

An infinite extension
\mathcal{L}/\mathbb{Q}
can be written as a tower of number fields

\mathcal{L}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq L_{m}\supsetneq L_{m-1}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq L_{1}=\mathbb{Q},

where
L_{i}/\mathbb{Q}
are finite extensions. Define

\psi_{q}(\mathcal{L})=\psi_{q}:=\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{q}(L_{i})}{[L_{i}:\mathbb{Q}]}.

This limit exists and is well-defined, i.e., independent of the tower
\{L_{i}\}
(see
[
11
, Proposition]
) and
0\leq\psi_{q}\leq 1
. For instance, if a prime
p
splits completely in
\mathcal{L}
, then
\psi_{p}=1
and
\psi_{p^{f}}=0
for all
f>1
. If
\psi_{q}>0
for some prime power
q
, we call the extension
\mathcal{L}
to be
asymptotically positive
. See
[
11
, Section 4]
for a detailed discussion and proof of properties of these extensions.

Note that Theorem
1.1
and
1.3
are stated for ample line bundles and general abelian varieties but the following proposition implies that it is enough to work with simple abelian varieties and very ample line bundles.

Proposition 2.3
.

(Reduction Step).
If Theorem
1.1
and
1.3
are true for geometrically simple abelian varieties and very ample symmetric line bundles, then
they are true for all abelian varieties and all ample symmetric line bundles.

Proof.

See
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
.
∎

For a finite extension
L/K
of number fields, consider a finite set
S
of places
w\in M_{L}
. We need the following well known inequality in Diophantine approximation.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Liouville’s inequality)
If
\alpha\in L
and
\beta\in K
with
\alpha\neq\beta
, then

(1)

(2H(\alpha)H(\beta))^{-[L:K]}\leq\prod_{w\in S}\|\alpha-\beta\|_{w,K}\leq(2H(\alpha)H(\beta))^{[L:K]},

where
\|x\|_{w,K}=\left|N_{L_{w}/K_{v}}(x)\right|_{v}
.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Theorem 1.5.21]
.
∎

2.3.
Preliminaries on differential operators

.

3.
Siegel lemma and section with higher order vanishing at

Our goal is to construct an auxiliary section

with higher order of vanishing at origin.
Denote by
L_{w}
the dimension of
K

\Gamma^{0}\left(A\times A,\mathcal{M}^{\otimes L}\right)

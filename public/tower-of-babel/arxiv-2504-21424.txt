Analysis of modulated linear wave packets and gap solitons in coupled optical fibers
D. V. Shaykin

Abstract
A coupled pair of waveguides with refractive indices of different signs is considered. It is shown that the gap in the linear wave spectrum closes if these waves are amplitude modulated. To look for solitons as envelopes of exponentially modulated wave packets, the dispersion law of these packets is used. Different decompositions of the dispersion law lead to solutions related to different parts of the spectrum. Numerical calculations demonstrate a good agreement with analytical predictions.

Following article

, we introduce a system of coupled modes in the form

\displaystyle i(\partial A/\partial t+\partial A/\partial x)-\delta A+p*B+r_{1}|A|^{2}A=0,

(2)

\displaystyle i(\partial B/\partial t-\partial B/\partial x)-\delta B+p*A+r_{2}|B|^{2}B=0,

(3)

where
\delta
is responsible for the violation of phase-matching and
r_{i}
is a nonlinearity parameter. We left the optional parameter
p
, which will be useful for numerical calculations and visualization. The thing is, by dividing this system of equations by

2

The feature of such a system is a gap in the dispersion law:

\omega=\delta\pm\sqrt{k^{2}+p^{2}},

(4)

II
Wave packets

Let us look for a solution in the form of a linear wave packet with a carrier wave number
k
and a frequency
\omega

\displaystyle A(x,t)=A(\xi)e^{i(kx-\omega t+\phi(\xi))},

(5)

\displaystyle B(x,t)=\big[B(\xi)+ib(\xi)\big]e^{i(kx-\omega t+\phi(\xi))},

(6)

where the variable
\xi=x-Vt
is related to the motion of the center of mass of the packet with velocity
V
,
\phi
is a chirp function. Substitution this form into (
2
) gives

\begin{split}A\big[\omega_{0}-k+(V-1)\dot{\phi}\big]+pB+r_{1}A^{3}&=0,\\
\dot{A}(V-1)-pb&=0,\\
B\big[\omega_{0}+k+(V+1)\dot{\phi}\big]+\dot{b}(V+1)&+\\
+pA+r_{2}(B^{2}+b^{2})B&=0,\\
b\big[\omega_{0}+k+(V+1)\dot{\phi}\big]-\dot{B}(V+1)&+\\
+r_{2}(B^{2}+b^{2})b&=0.\end{split}

(7)

Such a system describes an evolution of the general view of wave packet (
5
). Let us specify it by modulating the amplitude functions by
exp(-\Omega\xi)
, that is

\big(A,B,b\big)=\big(A_{0},B_{0},b_{0}\big)e^{-\Omega\xi}

(8)

Since we are interested in linear wave packets, then we need to linearize this system:

\begin{split}A\big[\omega_{0}-k\big]+pB&=0,\\
\dot{A}\Omega(V-1)+pb&=0,\\
B\big[\omega_{0}+k\big]-\dot{b}\Omega(V+1)+pA&=0,\\
b\big[\omega_{0}+k\big]+\dot{B}\Omega(V+1)&=0,\end{split}

(9)

where we introduce a new convenient parameter
\omega_{0}=w-\delta
. As we can see there isn’t function
\phi
. Therefore, we have
4
equations and only
3
parameters
\omega,\Omega,k
, so after simplification we arrive at the two equations

V=\frac{k}{\omega_{0}},\qquad k^{2}=\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}+\Omega^{2}-p^{2}}{1+\Omega^{2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}

(10)

They have simple physical meanings, its can be figured out from the limit
\Omega\to\infty
(the case of unmodulated waves); the first one denotes a dispersion law for the modulated waves, and the second one is the group velocity. As one can see from such a dispersion law, the gap at
k=0
narrows, and while
\Omega>0
it disappears at all.
So it allows us to look for the wave packets that have the carrier frequency located at the forbidden earlier zone; the soliton as an envelope of such packets is called a gap soliton. Generally speaking since we investigate the wave packets, we should take into account
\Omega\ll\omega
, so we introduce a small parameter

\varepsilon=\frac{\Omega}{\omega}\ll 1.

(11)

It means the envelopes of wave packets should be much slower functions than wave packet oscillations, and to investigate an evolution of the envelope, we need to use the slow variable
\Omega\xi
. Then for the envelope
\partial/\partial\xi=\Omega\partial/\partial\xi=\varepsilon\omega\partial/\partial\xi
, and general system (
7
) transforms to

\begin{split}A\big[\omega_{0}-k+(V-1)\dot{\phi}\Omega\big]+pB+r_{1}A^{3}&=0,\\
\dot{A}\Omega(V-1)-pb&=0,\\
B\big[\omega_{0}+k+(V+1)\dot{\phi}\Omega\big]+\dot{b}\Omega(V+1)&+\\
+pA+r_{2}(B^{2}+b^{2})B&=0,\\
b\big[\omega_{0}+k+(V+1)\dot{\phi}\Omega\big]-\dot{B}\Omega(V+1)&+\\
+r_{2}(B^{2}+b^{2})b&=0.\end{split}

(12)

III
Types of solitons

For the further work it will be convenient to use the inverse dispersion law in the form

k=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+(\varepsilon\omega)^{2}-p^{2}}/\sqrt{1+(\varepsilon\omega)^{2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}.

(13)

As one can note, we can decompose such an expression in different ways, and it leads to different solutions.

III.1
Outside the gap

For the beginning, we obtain an ordinary soliton outside the gap, that is
\omega_{0}>p
. Therefore we can decompose (
13
) as

k=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-p^{2}}+\frac{(\omega p/\omega_{0})^{2}}{2\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-p^{2}}}\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2})=\alpha+\gamma\varepsilon^{2}+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

(14)

After substitution (
14
) into (
12
) we can look for a solution in series form
f=\sum_{0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}f_{n}
. Simple calculations lead to the following

\{A,B,\dot{\phi}\}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}\{A_{n},B_{n},\dot{\phi_{n}}\},\qquad b=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{n}b_{n}

(15)

In the first order Eqs.(
12
) give

A_{1}(\omega_{0}-\alpha)+pB_{1}=0,

(16)

the second order leads to

\begin{split}A_{2}(\omega_{0}-\alpha)+pB_{2}=0,\\
\dot{A_{1}}\omega\big(\frac{\alpha}{\omega_{0}}-1\big)=pb_{2}.\end{split}

(17)

Finally, in the third order we obtain the well-known oscillatory equation

\ddot{A_{1}}=A_{1}-\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\Big[\frac{r_{2}(\omega_{0}-\alpha)^{3}+r_{1}p^{2}(\omega_{0}+\alpha)}{p^{3}}\Big]A_{1}^{3}

(18)

that has a soliton solution. Thus we have in the leading order

A=\frac{\varepsilon e^{i(kx-\omega t)}}{\cosh{(\varepsilon\omega\xi)}}\frac{\sqrt{2p^{3}}}{\sqrt{r_{2}(\omega_{0}-\alpha)^{3}+r_{1}p^{2}(\omega_{0}+\alpha)}}\frac{\omega}{\omega_{0}},

(19)

and
B=-A(\omega_{0}-\alpha)/p
. In particular, if
p\ll\omega_{0}
, then
|B|\ll|A|
, that is, we come to the situation of coupled waveguides in which the wave propagates along only one of them.

As can be seen from the Eq.(
18
) the solitons don’t exist for a pair of defocusing waveguides (
r_{2},r_{1}<0
).

III.2
On the gap

Another decomposition of Eq.(
13
) we can obtain if we take
\omega_{0}=p
:

k=\omega\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon^{2}).

(20)

In this situation we can as well use the series (
15
). The first order gives

A_{1}+B_{1}=0,

(21)

the second order gives

p(A_{2}+B_{2})=A_{1}\omega.

(22)

On the third step we obtain

\ddot{A_{1}}=A_{1}-(r_{2}+r_{1})\frac{p}{\omega^{2}}A_{1}^{3},

(23)

hence in the leading order we have

A=\varepsilon\frac{\sqrt{2}\omega}{\sqrt{(r_{2}+r_{1})p}}\frac{e^{i(kx-\omega t)}}{\cosh{(\varepsilon\omega\xi)}},

(24)

and
B=-A
.

III.3
Inside the gap

To decompose the dispersion law (
13
), we have to demand
\omega_{0}^{2}-p^{2}+(\varepsilon\omega)^{2}>0
. We can meet this requirement as well inside the gap, in particular, if we take
w_{0}=p-q\varepsilon^{2}
, where parameter
q
has a limitation that can be obtained by substituting it into
\omega_{0}^{2}-p^{2}+(\varepsilon\omega)^{2}>0
. A simple analysis shows
q\lesssim(\delta+p)^{2}/2p
by implying
\omega\sim\delta
. Another limitation comes from the denominator of the formula (
13
):
\varepsilon\ll\omega_{0}/\omega
. In such a framework we can decompose Eq.(
13
) near the initial gap boundary but inside it:

k=\varepsilon\sqrt{(\delta+1)^{2}-2*qp}+o(\varepsilon^{2})=\alpha\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon).

(25)

Series (
15
) also satisfy our problem and finally we can arrive at

\ddot{A_{1}}=A_{1}-(r_{2}+r_{1})\frac{p}{(\delta+p)^{2}}A_{1}^{3}

(26)

that solves the task:

A=\varepsilon\frac{\sqrt{2}(\delta+p)}{\sqrt{(r_{2}+r_{1})p}}\frac{e^{i(kx-\omega t)}}{\cosh{(\varepsilon\omega\xi)}},

(27)

and
B=-A
.

One can note, in the last two situations, the wave number
k\propto\varepsilon
and the envelope function are also proportional to
\varepsilon
. It means that in these cases the wave packets include only a few oscillations. Also, the solitons exist for the different waveguides only if
r_{2}+r_{1}>0
.

IV
Conclusion

Based only on the linear wave packet’s theory, we were able to obtain solitons as envelopes in different parts of the spectrum. This idea doesn’t depend on the integrability of any equations; as a consequence, it can be applied to the more complicated problems.

1.
introduction

We are concerned with the profile decomposition for the Palais-Smale (
(PS)
in short) sequences for the energy functional associated to the following Brezis-Nirenberg type problem for the mixed local-nonlocal operator with critical nonlinearity on the smooth bounded domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u-\lambda u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is a real parameter,
N\geq 3
,
s\in(0,1)
is the order of the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}
, and
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent associated with the classical Laplacian.

A key challenge in analyzing problems of the form (

P
λ

) arises from the lack of compactness in the embedding
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)
, where
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is the function space introduced in Section
2.2
. Hence, a thorough analysis of the
fundamental causes of this compactness failure is highly beneficial. This failure is primarily attributed to the action of a certain noncompact group
G
, often referred to as "dislocations", which acts on the function space
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

From a mathematical perspective, let
\mathcal{H}
be a general Hilbert space, and consider a bounded sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
that converges weakly to some limit
u\in\mathcal{H}
, i.e.,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
. However, if the sequence does not converge strongly, meaning
u_{n}\not\to u
in
\mathcal{H}
, the difference
u_{n}-u
captures the failure of compactness. The study of this sequence, which characterizes the loss of compactness, is referred to as the
profile decomposition
. Without loss of generality, one may assume
u\equiv 0
and analyze the effect of a noncompact group
G
acting on
\mathcal{H}
through unitary operators, which plays a pivotal role in the structure of the decomposition.
More precisely, we seek a decomposition of the form

(1.1)

u_{n}=\sum_{j\in I}g_{n}^{(j)}\psi_{j}+r_{n},

where
I
is an (at most countable) index set, for each
j\in I
, the sequence
\{g_{n}^{(j)}\}\subset G
and diverges within the group, while the functions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in I}\subset\mathcal{H}
represent the set of all
profiles

\Psi
associated with
\{u_{n}\}
is defined as

\Psi=\left\{\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{0\},\,j\in I;\,\psi_{j}=w^{(j)}-\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(g_{n}^{(j)}\right)^{*}u_{n},\,w^{(j)}\in\mathcal{H},\,g_{n}^{(j)}\in G\right\},

where
\cdot^{*}
denotes the adjoint element. Thus,
\Psi
consists of all possible nonzero weak limits of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
. When this set is nonempty, one can attempt to extract and remove all profiles from
u_{n}
by reversing the transformation induced by the group elements
g_{n}^{(j)}
and then investigate the asymptotic properties of the remainder sequence
\{r_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
. Furthermore, the decomposition in (
1.1
) is characterzied by the following three properties:

(1)

Nontriviality:
Each profile
\psi_{j}
is nonzero.

(2)

Asymptotic Orthogonality:
The dislocations
\{g_{n}^{(j)}\}
and
\{g_{n}^{(k)}\}
, for different
j,k\in I
, become increasingly distinct, satisfying
g_{n}^{(j)^{*}}g_{n}^{(k)}\rightharpoonup 0
for
j\neq k
.

(3)

G
-Weak Convergence to Zero:
The remainder
r_{n}
contains no further profiles and

G
-weakly converges to zero as
n\to\infty
(see
[
52
]
, Chapter 3 for definitions and related discussions).

The existence and uniqueness of such decompositions are well established (see
[
50
,
52
]
), making them a valuable tool in understanding compactness failures. However, the challenge in applying this framework to our setting lies in characterizing the noncompact structure of the group
G
.

Motivated by the fundamental difficulty due to lack of compactness for the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\xhookrightarrow{}L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)
, in a seminal work, Brezis-Nirenberg
[
10
]
considered the perturbed equation

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u-\lambda u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth bounded domain,
N\geq 3
, and
\lambda
is real parameter. The key insight was to treat the linear term
\lambda u
as a compact perturbation of the critical nonlinearity, enabling the recovery of compactness needed to apply variational tools. Their results revealed a delicate interplay between the domain geometry, the parameter
\lambda
, and the dimension
N
, with the existence of nontrivial solutions depending critically on these factors, establishing a sharp threshold for the existence of solutions in terms of the first Dirichlet eigenvalue. Subsequently, the nonlocal counterpart had been studied by Servedei-Valdinoci
[
45
]
. For establishing the existence of a positive weak solution of (
1.2
), the local compactness result plays a pivotal role. They showed that below the level
\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}_{N}^{N/2}
all
(PS)
-sequences (see Definition
1.1
) for the energy functional associated to (
1.2
) are pre-compact, which opens the question of classifying all the ranges where the energy functional fails to satisfy the
(PS)
condition. Struwe
[
47
]
provided a comprehensive resolution to the above question through what is now known as the Global Compactness Theorem or the Palais–Smale Decomposition Theorem, offering a precise characterization of the loss of compactness for the
(PS)
-sequences. Struwe
[
47
]
showed that every associated
(PS)
-sequence (up to a subsequence) for the energy functional corresponding to (
1.2
) is a sum of its weak limit and finitely many weakly interacting rescaled bubbles (which are precisely positive solutions to the critical exponent problem (
1.4
) in the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
). His analysis demonstrated that compactness can break down due to the existence of nontrivial entire solutions to the following
limiting
problem, which remains invariant under translations and scalings:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\;\;\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{array}\right.

where

\mathcal{D}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\coloneq\left\{u\in L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):|\nabla u|\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}.

A sequence

The authors in

A key idea in their proof was the use of rescaled functions

Theorem 1.2

Let

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{0}+(-\Delta)^{s}u_{0}-\lambda u_{0}=|u_{0}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Delta U_{i}=|U_{i}|^{2^{*}-2}U_{i}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq l,

As established in Theorem
1.1
, the best constant in the associated Sobolev inequality is not attained; that is, ground state solutions for the critical problem under consideration do not exist. In such scenarios, profile decomposition plays a pivotal role by identifying the precise levels at which compactness is lost. It not only provides a clearer understanding of the concentration phenomena but also delineates the energy range where one must search for higher energy solutions.

Motivated by this insight, the pursuit of high energy solutions becomes a natural direction. There are two widely adopted strategies to overcome the loss of compactness: one involves introducing suitable perturbations to the equation, while the other exploits variations in the topology or geometry of the domain. The latter approach gives rise to the celebrated Coron problem, which examines how domain topology can be leveraged to recover compactness and hence ensure the existence of solutions. A vast body of literature explores these techniques using the framework of profile decomposition to prove the existence and multiplicity of solutions; see, for instance,
[
6
,
14
,
13
,
17
,
53
,
21
,
56
]
.

The classical Coron’s problem
[
15
]
, introduced in 1984, addresses critical elliptic equations in domains with certain geometric features. It asserts that if there exists a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and radii
R_{2}>R_{1}>0
satisfying

\left\{R_{1}\leq\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq R_{2}\right\}\subset\Omega,\quad\left\{\left|x-x_{0}\right|\leq R_{1}\right\}\not\subset\bar{\Omega}.

Then the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=u^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
\,\,u>0&\text{in $\Omega$},\\
\,\,u=0&\text{on $\partial\Omega$,}\end{cases}

admits at least one solution, provided the ratio
R_{2}/R_{1}
is sufficiently large
[
15
]
. Subsequently, in a landmark paper
[
5
]
, Bahri and Coron strengthened this result using topological methods, proving the existence of a solution under the condition that
H_{m}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\neq 0
for some
m>0
, where
H_{m}
denotes the homology group of dimension
m
of
\Omega
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients. Further extensions to contractible domains were developed in
[
16
,
18
,
40
]
.

Extensions of the Coron problem have also been investigated for various nonlinear operators. The case of the
p
-Laplacian is treated in
[

]
, while the fractional Laplacian case is considered in
[

]
. For systems like the Lane–Emden system, analogous results are discussed in
[

]

Theorem 1.3

Let

be a bounded domain satisfying:

(1.5)

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:R_{1}<|x|<R_{2}\right\}\subset\Omega,

(1.6)

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|x|<R_{1}\right\}\not\subset\overline{\Omega},

for some

such that for all

sufficiently large depending on

P
λ

\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{S}_{N}^{N/2}<I_{0,s}(u)<\frac{2}{N}\mathcal{S}_{N}^{N/2}.

The proof of Theorem

proceeds by contradiction. We consider a two-parameter family of functions and
R\gg 1

(1.7)

\mathcal{A}_{R}\coloneqq\left\{\phi_{R}U[t\sigma,(1-t)]\,:\,\phi_{R}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,t\in[0,1)\mbox{ and }\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\right\},

where

control the concentration and translation of the bubbles within the domain. We then estimate the associated energy of this class and show that for sufficiently large dimension

becomes the next critical threshold after

Thus, applying a version of the deformation lemma, we construct a homotopy retracting the unit sphere

continuously to a single point within

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

One of the natural limitations of our result lies in the assumption that the dimension

must be sufficiently large in order to guarantee the existence of solutions. While this assumption is crucial for our analytical estimates to hold, numerical simulations performed using MATLAB suggest that Lemma

(2)

2.
Preliminaries and Auxiliary lemmas

We consider the variational framework associated with a combination of local and nonlocal operators. For a comprehensive treatment of such settings, we refer to
[
8
]
, and the references therein.

In this context, the following Sobolev-type inequality involving both local and fractional operators holds for functions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
that vanish outside an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

(2.1)

\mathcal{S}_{N,s}(\Omega)\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,

where
N\geq 3
,
2^{*}\coloneq\frac{2N}{N-2}
is the critical Sobolev exponent, and the constant
\mathcal{S}_{N,s}(\Omega)
denotes the optimal (i.e., best possible) constant for which the inequality holds.
To this inequality, one can naturally associate the following Rayleigh-type quotient,

S(u;\Omega):=\frac{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+[u]_{s}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}^{2}},\quad u\in\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
[u]_{s}
stands for the Gagliardo seminorm, defined by

[u]_{s}^{2}:=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y.

Moreover, we will denote the inner product associated with the above seminorm by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{s}
.

2.1.
Classical Sobolev inequality

Before delving further, let us outline some fundamental facts regarding the sharp constant
\mathcal{S}_{N}
appearing in the classical Sobolev inequality. This constant is defined by

\mathcal{S}_{N}:=\inf\left\{\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}(\Omega)}}:u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\right\},

and is extensively discussed in the works
[
4
,
49
]
. A notable feature of
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is its independence from the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
; its value depends only on the space dimension
N
. Moreover, an explicit expression for this constant is available and given by

\mathcal{S}_{N}=N(N-2)\pi\left(\frac{\Gamma(N/2)}{\Gamma(N)}\right)^{2/N}=\frac{N(N-2)}{4}\omega_{N}^{\frac{2}{N}},

where
\Gamma(\cdot)
denotes the Gamma function and
\omega_{N}\coloneq\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})}
denotes the surface measure of
\mathbb{S}^{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.

It is also worth noting that when
\Omega
is a bounded domain, the infimum defining
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is never attained, i.e., no minimizer exists. However, in the particular case where
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the situation is different: although the minimum is not achieved by any function in
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it is attained in the larger space
\mathcal{D}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The family of extremal functions realizing the minimum in this setting is explicitly known and given by the so-called Aubin-Talenti bubbles
[
4
,
49
]
. That is the set

(2.2)

\mathcal{F}:=\left\{cU[z_{0},\lambda](x)=c\lambda^{-\frac{(N-2)}{2}}U\left(\frac{x-z_{0}}{\lambda}\right):\,c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\,\lambda>0,\,z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\right\},

where,

U(z):=(1+|z|^{2})^{-\frac{(N-2)}{2}}.

This family reflects the invariance properties of the problem under dilation and translation, and plays a central role in the analysis of Sobolev embeddings.

2.2.
Function Space

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a nonempty open subset, where
N\geq 3
, and not necessarily bounded. We define the function space
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the global norm

(2.3)

\rho(u):=\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+[u]_{s}^{2}\right)^{1/2},\quad u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
[u]_{s}
denotes the fractional semi-norm. This norm is naturally associated with the scalar product

(2.4)

\langle u,v\rangle_{\rho}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\,{\rm d}x+\langle u,v\rangle_{s},

where
\cdot
represents the standard inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
\langle u,v\rangle_{s}
corresponds to the bilinear form associated with the fractional part. Equipped with this inner product, the space
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
becomes a Hilbert space.

In the case when
\Omega
is bounded, it is useful to recall a fundamental inequality that expresses the continuous embedding of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Specifically, for every
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, there exists a constant
c=c(s)>0
such that

(2.5)

[u]_{s}^{2}\leq C(s)\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}=C(s)\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\right).

The fractional Sobolev space
H^{s}(\Omega)
, which lies between
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)
, is defined by

H^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega):\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\frac{N}{2}+s}}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times\Omega)\right\}.

This space is equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{s}}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\,{\rm d}x\,\,{\rm d}y\right)^{1/2}.

The inequality (
2.5
), together with the classical Poincaré inequality, ensures that the norm
\rho(\cdot)
is equivalent to the standard
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm on
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. As a consequence, the space
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
coincides with the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\overline{C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u|_{\Omega}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\text{ and }u\equiv 0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\}.

We now conclude this section with a compactness result that will be crucial in the proof of the global compactness theorem, Theorem
1.2
. To this end, we first recall a useful auxiliary lemma from
[
33
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set,
0<s_{1}<s_{2}<1
. Then
\forall u\in H^{s_{2}}(\Omega)
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\left[u\right]_{s_{1}}\leq c\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-s_{1}/s_{2}}\left[u\right]_{s_{2}}^{s_{1}/s_{2}}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded and nonempty open set. Then, the embedding
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{s}(\Omega)
is compact.

Proof.

Let us consider a bounded sequence
\{u_{k}\}
in the space
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Since this sequence is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, it has a convergent subsequence (still denoted by
\{u_{k}\}
) in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Consequently,
\{u_{k}\}
forms a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Furthermore, using the continuity of the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{s^{\prime}}(\Omega)
(as established in (
2.5
) for some
s^{\prime}\in(s,1)
) and employing Lemma
2.1
, we can show that
\{u_{k}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{s}(\Omega)
. Indeed

\displaystyle[u_{k}-u_{m}]_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{k}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}^{1-s/s^{\prime}}[u_{k}-u_{m}]_{s^{\prime}}^{s/s^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{k}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}^{1-s/s^{\prime}}\left([u_{k}]_{s^{\prime}}+[u_{m}]_{s^{\prime}}\right)^{s/s^{\prime}}\leq C\|u_{k}-u_{m}\|_{L^{2}{(\Omega)}}^{1-s/s^{\prime}}.

The inequality above clearly implies that
\{u_{k}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
H^{s}(\Omega)
. Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

We next define the energy functional corresponding to the problem (

P
λ

). For any
u\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, it is given by

(2.6)

\displaystyle I_{\lambda,s}(u)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\,{\rm d}x\,\,{\rm d}y-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\,\,{\rm d}x

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\rho(u)^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{*}}\,\,{\rm d}x.

Moreover, the limiting functional associated with the problem at infinity becomes

I_{\infty}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x.

3.
Global Compactness Result

In this section, we prove the global compactness result for the problem (

P
λ

), stated in Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1
:
We first show that the sequence
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Consequently, up to a subsequence (still denoted by
\{u_{k}\}
), we may assume that
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
u_{0}
is a weak solution to the problem (

P
λ

).

Let
\{u_{k}\}
be a Palais–Smale sequence for
I_{\lambda,s}
at level
\beta
, i.e.,
I_{\lambda,s}(u_{k})\to\beta
and
I_{\lambda,s}^{\prime}(u_{k})\to 0
in
(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega))^{\prime}
as
k\to\infty
. Then, for some constant
C>0
, we have

\displaystyle\beta+o(1)+o(1)\rho(u_{k})\geq

\displaystyle\left|I_{\lambda,s}(u_{k})-\frac{1}{2}\prescript{}{(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2})^{\prime}}{\langle}I^{\prime}_{\lambda,s}(u_{k}),\,u_{k}{\rangle}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\penalty\ C\left(\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2}},

for some constant
C>0
. As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(u_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=2I_{\lambda,s}(u_{k})+\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{2}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\beta+o(1)\right)+C\left(\beta+o(1)+o(1)\rho(u_{k})\right)^{\tfrac{2}{2^{*}}}+C\left(\beta+o(1)+\rho(u_{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq C+o(1)\rho(u_{k}),

for some constants
C>0
. This inequality shows that the sequence
\{u_{k}\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
.

Therefore, there exists
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
weakly in
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. This implies that for all
\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\langle u_{k},\phi\rangle_{\rho}:=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,{\rm d}x+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\left(u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\right)\left(\phi(x)-\phi(y)\right)}{\left|x-y\right|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\rightarrow\langle u_{0},\phi\rangle_{\rho}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi\,{\rm d}x+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{\left|x-y\right|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y,

as
k\to\infty
.
Moreover, since
u_{k}\to u_{0}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
, and using Vitali’s convergence theorem, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{k}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{k}-|u_{0}|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}\right)\phi\,\,{\rm d}x\to 0,\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

In addition, since
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it follows that

\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\phi\,\,{\rm d}x\to\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\phi\,\,{\rm d}x,\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,\quad\text{for all }\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

Putting these together, we conclude that
\prescript{}{(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2})^{\prime}}{\langle}I_{\lambda,s}^{\prime}(u_{0}),\phi\rangle_{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}}=0
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Hence,
u_{0}
is a weak solution to the problem (

P
λ

).

Step 2
:
Define
v_{k}\coloneqq u_{k}-u_{0}
. We aim to show that the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
forms a
(PS)
-sequence for the limiting functional
I_{\infty}
at the level
\beta-I_{\lambda,s}(u_{0})
.

Since the embedding
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is continuous and
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, it follows that
v_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. This yields the identity

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v_{k}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}+o(1),

where
o(1)\rightarrow 0
as
k\to\infty
.

Moreover, due to the compact embedding of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
into
L^{2}(\Omega)
and by applying the Brezis–Lieb Lemma, we obtain

(3.2)

o(1)=\int_{\Omega}|v_{k}|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{2}+o(1).

Similarly, the Brezis–Lieb Lemma applied to the critical term gives

(3.3)

\int_{\Omega}|v_{k}|^{2^{*}}=\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2^{*}}-\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{2^{*}}+o(1).

In addition, employing Lemma
2.2
along with the Brezis–Lieb Lemma for the nonlocal term, we get

(3.4)

o(1)=[v_{k}]_{s}^{2}=[u_{k}]_{s}^{2}-[u_{0}]_{s}^{2}+o(1).

We now claim that
\{v_{k}\}
is indeed a
(PS)
-sequence for
I_{\infty}
at the level
\beta-I_{\lambda,s}(u_{0})
. From (
3.1
)-(
3.4
), we compute:

\displaystyle I_{\infty}(v_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla v_{k}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}\left|v_{k}\right|^{2^{*}}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|^{2}\,+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\left|u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{k}|^{2}\,-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{k}\right|^{2^{*}}\,

\displaystyle-\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u_{0}\right|^{2}\,+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\left|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}|u_{0}|^{2}\,-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}}\,\right\}+o(1)

\displaystyle=I_{\lambda,s}(u_{k})-I_{\lambda,s}(u_{0})+o(1)\rightarrow\beta-I_{\lambda,s}(u_{0})\,\text{ as }k\to\infty.

To verify the
(PS)
condition, we consider

\displaystyle\prescript{}{(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2})^{\prime}}{\langle}I^{\prime}_{\infty}(u_{k}-u_{0}),\,\phi{\rangle}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u_{k}-u_{0})\cdot\nabla\phi\,-\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{k}-u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}-2}(u_{k}-u_{0})\phi\,

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\phi\,+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{\left|x-y\right|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\phi\,-\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{k}\phi\,

\displaystyle-\left\{\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{0}\cdot\nabla\phi\,+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{(u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{\left|x-y\right|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\right\}

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{0}\phi\,+\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}\phi\,

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}-u_{0})\phi\,-\int_{\Omega}\left\{\left|u_{k}-u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}-2}(u_{k}-u_{0})-\left|u_{k}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{k}+\left|u_{0}\right|^{2^{*}-2}u_{0}\right\}\phi\,+o(1)

\displaystyle=\prescript{}{(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2})^{\prime}}{\langle}I^{\prime}_{\lambda,s}(u_{k}),\,\phi{\rangle}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}}-\prescript{}{(\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2})^{\prime}}{\langle}I^{\prime}_{\lambda,s}(u_{0}),\,\phi{\rangle}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}}+o(1)=o(1),

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, since
u_{k}
is a P.S. sequence and
u_{0}
is a weak solution. In fact we can say more. Using Lemma
2.2
, we can easily see that
\{v_{k}\}
is indeed a
(PS)
sequence for
I_{\infty}
at the level
\beta-I_{\lambda,s}
on
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Step 3
:
Since
v_{k}=u_{k}-u_{0}\rightharpoonup 0

, and consequently in

4.
Positive solutions on annular-shaped domains: an application of profile decomposition

In this section, we construct a high-energy positive solution to the critical exponent problem involving the mixed local–nonlocal operator on
\Omega
, with
\lambda=0
in equation (

P
λ

), leveraging the topological properties of
\Omega
. The argument follows in the spirit of Coron
[
15
]
(see also
[
48
, Theorem 3.4]
).

To establish Theorem
1.3
, we establish some preliminary lemmas and notational conventions.

Our strategy involves constructing a two-parameter family of Aubin–Talenti–type bubbles whose energy lies strictly between the first and second energy levels. Assume that the domain
\Omega
satisfies conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) of Theorem
1.3
. For simplicity of exposition, we assume
R_{1}=(4R)^{-1}<1<4R=R_{2}
and
R\gg 1
.
For
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N};|x|=1\right\},x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in[0,1)
, consider the following
2
-parameter family of functions

u_{t}^{\sigma}(x)\coloneqq U[t\sigma,(1-t)](x)=\left[\frac{1-t}{(1-t)^{2}+|x-t\sigma|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

These functions are motivated from the fact that
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is attained at each of
u_{t}^{\sigma}
and
\mathcal{S}_{N}=\mathcal{S}_{N,s}
as discussed in Section
2
.
Now, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, we have

(4.1)

u_{t}^{\sigma}\rightarrow U[0,1]=u_{0}=\left[\frac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\right]^{\frac{N-2}{2}}\quad\text{as }t\searrow 0.

Moreover,
u_{t}^{\sigma}

concentrates
at
\sigma
as
t\nearrow 1
.
To localize these functions within the domain
\Omega
, consider a radially symmetric cut-off function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

\varphi\equiv 1\text{ on }\left\{x:\frac{1}{2}<|x|<2\right\},\quad\varphi\equiv 0\text{ outside }\left\{x:\frac{1}{4}<|x|<4\right\},\quad 0\leq\varphi\leq 1\text{ on }\Omega.

For each
R\geq 1
, define the rescaled cut-off

\varphi_{R}(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi(Rx),&0\leq|x|<R^{-1}\\
1,&R^{-1}\leq|x|<R\\
\varphi(x/R),&R\leq|x|.\end{cases}

Now with the appropriate cut-off function in hand, we define

w_{t,R}^{\sigma}=u_{t}^{\sigma}\cdot\varphi_{R},\;\;w_{0,R}=u_{0}\cdot\varphi_{R}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega).

Lemma 4.1
.

With the notations introduced above, the following convergence holds

\left\|w_{t,R}^{\sigma}-u_{t}^{\sigma}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\rightarrow 0\text{ as }R\to\infty,

uniformly with respect to
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
and
t\in[0,1)
.

Proof.

Following the estimates established in
[
48
, Theorem 3.4]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left|\nabla\left(w_{t,R}^{\sigma}-u_{t}^{\sigma}\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}\right)\cup B_{(2R)^{-1}}}\left|\nabla u_{t}^{\sigma}\right|^{2}{\rm d}x

(4.2)

\displaystyle+C\cdot R^{-2}\int_{B_{4R}\backslash B_{2R}}\left|u_{t}^{\sigma}\right|^{2}{\rm d}x+CR^{2}\int_{B_{(2R)^{-1}}}\left|u_{t}^{\sigma}\right|^{2}{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0

as
R\rightarrow\infty
, uniformly in
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\;\text{and }t\in[0,1)
.

Now, we will be done once we show the convergence of
w_{t,R}^{\sigma}
to
u_{t}^{\sigma}
in the Gagliardo seminorm uniformly for
t\in[0,1)
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
, i.e., we would like to show that

\sup_{t,\sigma}\left[w_{t,R}^{\sigma}-u_{t}^{\sigma}\right]_{s}\to 0\text{ as }R\to\infty.

Indeed, by
[
33
, Theorem 6.21]
, it suffices to prove that

\|w_{t,R}^{\sigma}-u_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0.

We compute

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{t,R}^{\sigma}-u_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(1-\varphi_{R}(x))^{2}|u_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}\right)\cup B_{(2R)^{-1}}}|u_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}{\rm d}x=\underbrace{\int_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}\right)}|u_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x}_{\mathcal{I}_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{B_{(2R)^{-1}}}|u_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}{\rm d}x}_{\mathcal{I}_{2}}.

We estimate each term separately.

Estimate for
\mathcal{I}_{1}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}:=\int_{\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}\right)}|u_{t}^{\sigma}|^{2}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}}\left[\frac{1-t}{(1-t)^{2}+|x-t\sigma|^{2}}\right]^{N-2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-t)^{N-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}}\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{|x-t\sigma|^{2}}{(1-t)^{2}}\right)^{N-2}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\tilde{B}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{N-2}}\,{\rm d}y,

where in the last step, we have performed the following change of variables:

\text{Let }y=\frac{x-t\sigma}{1-t}\text{ and }\tilde{B}=\left(\frac{B_{2R}-t\sigma}{1-t}\right).

Now note that

y\notin\tilde{B}\implies(1-t)y+t\sigma\notin B_{2R}\implies|y|\geq\frac{2R-t}{1-t}=\frac{2R-1}{(1-t)}+1\geq 2R.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}\;\leq\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2R}}\frac{1}{(1+|y|^{2})^{N-2}}\,{\rm d}y.

Therefore,
\mathcal{I}_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
R\rightarrow\infty
independent of
t
and
\sigma
for
N\geq 5.

Estimate for
\mathcal{I}_{2}
:

Hölder’s inequality, together with the
L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
-norm invariance of the Aubin–Talenti bubbles, yields

\sup_{t,\sigma}\|u_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}\left(B_{(2R)^{-1}}\right)}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\left|B\left(0,(2R)^{-1}\right)\right|^{\tfrac{1}{N}}\to 0\text{ as }R\to\infty.

Combining all estimates, we conclude that

\|w_{t,R}^{\sigma}-u_{t}^{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0\;\text{ as }\;R\to\infty,

uniformly with respect to
t\in[0,1)
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{N-1}
.
Therefore, invoking the preceding lemma together with (
4.2
), we obtain

w_{t,R}^{\sigma}\rightarrow u_{t}^{\sigma}\;\text{ in }\;\mathcal{X}_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ as }R\to\infty.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let us consider the normalized functions

v_{t,R}^{\sigma}=w_{t,R}^{\sigma}/\left\|w_{t,R}^{\sigma}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},v_{0,R}=w_{0,R}/\left\|w_{0,R}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Then, for sufficiently large
R\gg 1
, there exists a positive constant
S_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(4.3)

\sup_{(t,\sigma)\in\left[0,1\right)\times\mathbb{S}^{N-1}}S\left(v_{t,R}^{\sigma};\Omega\right)<S_{1}<2^{2/N}\mathcal{S}_{N}

for all
N\geq N_{0}
.

To establish Lemma
4.2
, we prove the following crucial estimate, which plays a key role in the analysis of the Coron-type problem addressed in this section.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u_{0}
be as defined in (
4.1
). Then for sufficiently large
N
, the following inequality holds

\frac{\|\nabla u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+(1-t)^{2-2s}[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}}{\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}<2^{2/N}\mathcal{S}_{N}.

Proof.

It is enough to show that

(4.4)

[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}<\left(2^{2/N}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}{\rm d}x

Firstly, we analyze the left-hand side.

The fractional norm is given by

[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x.

Applying standard integral estimates as obtained in
[
33
, Theorem 6.21]
, we get

[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}\leq\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{s\ell^{2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x+\frac{\omega_{N-1}\ell^{2(1-s)}}{2(1-s)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x,

where
\omega_{N}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N+1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)},\;\ell>0
.
Rearranging the terms,

[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}\leq\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2}\left(\frac{2}{s\ell^{2s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x+\frac{\ell^{2(1-s)}}{1-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\right).

Now, consider the following notations

A=\frac{2}{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x,\quad B=\frac{1}{1-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x,

a=2s,\quad b=2(1-s),

we obtain

[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}\leq\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2}g(\ell),

where

g(\ell)=A\ell^{-a}+B\ell^{b},\;\;\ell>0.

Taking the infimum over all
\ell>0
, we get

\displaystyle[u_{0}]^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2}\operatorname{inf}_{l>0}g(l)=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2}\left(\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{\frac{a}{a+b}}+\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{b}{a+b}}\right)A^{\frac{b}{a+b}}B^{\frac{a}{a+b}}.

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2}\left(\left(\frac{2(1-s)}{2s}\right)^{s}+\left(\frac{2s}{2(1-s)}\right)^{1-s}\right)\left(\frac{2}{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)^{1-s}

\displaystyle\,\,\times\left(\frac{1}{1-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\right)^{s}.

Thus,

[u_{0}]_{s}^{2}\leq\frac{\omega_{N-1}2^{-s}}{s(1-s)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{1-s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{s}.

Now, the inequality (
4.4
) holds if the following condition is met:

\frac{\omega_{N-1}2^{-s}}{s(1-s)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{1-s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{s}\leq\left(2^{\frac{2}{N}}-1\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\right).

Rearranging the terms,

\left[\frac{\omega_{N-1}2^{-s}}{s(1-s)\left(2^{\frac{2}{N}}-1\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-s}}\leq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x}.

Now, evaluating the terms on the right-hand side of the above computation, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{N-1}}{(1+r^{2})^{N-2}}\,dr=\omega_{N-1}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}-2\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma(N-2)}.

Similarly, evaluating the integral of the gradient term,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\mathcal{S}_{N}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x\right)^{2/2^{*}}.

Performing the integral computation,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\mathcal{S}_{N}\left(\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{N-1}\,dr}{(1+r^{2})^{N}}\right)^{(N-2)/N}.

Simplifying further,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u_{0}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\frac{\mathcal{S}_{N}^{2}}{N(N-2)\pi}\left(\frac{\Gamma(N)}{\Gamma(N/2)}\right)^{2/N}\left(\omega_{N-1}\frac{2^{-N}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(N/2)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+N}{2}\right)}\right)^{(N-2)/N}.

We have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\omega_{N-1}2^{-s}}{s(1-s)\left(2^{\frac{2}{N}}-1\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{1-s}}\leq\mathcal{S}_{N}^{2}\;\underbrace{2^{3-\frac{2}{N}-N}\pi^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{N}}\frac{\Gamma(-2+N)\left(\frac{\Gamma(N)}{\Gamma(N/2)}\right)^{2/N}\Gamma\left(\frac{1+N}{2}\right)^{-1+2/N}}{N(N-2)\Gamma(-2+\frac{N}{2})}}_{:=R(N)}.

We claim that for inequality (
4.5
)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\text{LHS}}{\text{RHS}}=0.

Using the Taylor expansion, we note that

2^{2/N}=1+\frac{2}{N}\log(2)+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right).

Further, recall the Stirling approximation

(4.6)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Gamma(N+1)}{\sqrt{2\pi N}\left(\frac{N}{e}\right)^{N}}=1.

Using the Stirling approximation (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{2^{2/N}-1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)(2^{2/N}-1)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{2\pi^{N/2}}{\left(\frac{1}{N}\left[2\log 2+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right]\sqrt{2\pi(N-2)/2}\left(\frac{N-2}{2e}\right)^{(N-2)/2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\pi^{N/2}N}{\sqrt{2\pi}\log 2\sqrt{\frac{N-2}{2}}\left(\frac{N-2}{2e}\right)^{(N-2)/2}}=0.

Next, we recall the duplication formula for the Gamma function:

\Gamma(N)\Gamma\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2^{1-2N}\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(2N).

Using this identity,
R(N)
can be simplified to

\pi^{-2}2^{2}\frac{(N-4)}{N-2}\frac{1}{2^{4/N}}\frac{1}{\pi^{2/N}}\frac{\left[\Gamma\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)\right]^{4/N}}{N(N-1)}.

Moreover, by applying Stirling’s approximation, one readily obtains

\lim_{N\to\infty}R(N)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}e^{2}}.

Furthermore, a similar application of Stirling’s approximation yields

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{S}_{N}=\infty.

Thus the claim follows.
∎

λ

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u

λ

u

P
λ

) with

u
satisfies the equation

(5.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+(-\Delta)^{s}u=|u|^{2^{*}-2}u\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\

(5.4)

-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x-\left[u^{-}\right]_{s}^{2}-2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y=-\int_{\Omega}|u^{-}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x,

Once again, by the Sobolev inequality, we obtain

(5.5)

\mathcal{S}_{N}^{N/2}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Now consider the energy functional

\displaystyle I_{0,s}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}[u]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}[u^{+}]_{s}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}[u^{-}]_{s}^{2}-\langle u^{+},u^{-}\rangle_{s}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u^{-}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}[u^{+}]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u^{+}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}[u^{-}]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\int_{\Omega}|u^{-}|^{2^{*}}\,{\rm d}x+2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y.

Using (
5.2
) and (
5.4
), we substitute to get

\displaystyle I_{0,s}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}[u^{+}]_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+[u^{+}]_{s}^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\right)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}[u^{-}]_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{*}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+[u^{-}]_{s}^{2}+2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y\right)

\displaystyle+2\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle I_{0,s}(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u^{+}|^{2}+|\nabla u^{-}|^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{N}\left([u^{+}]_{s}^{2}+[u^{-}]_{s}^{2}\right)+\frac{4}{N}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{u^{+}(x)u^{-}(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}y.

Finally, using (
5.3
) and (
5.5
), we conclude

I_{0,s}(u)\geq\frac{2}{N}\mathcal{S}_{N}^{N/2}.

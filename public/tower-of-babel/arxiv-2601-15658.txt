Construction and Box-counting Dimension of the Edelstein Hidden Variable Fractal Interpolation Function
Aiswarya T, Srijanani Anurag Prasad

Abstract
This paper presents the construction of a hidden variable fractal interpolation function using Edelstein contractions in an iterated function system based on a finite collection of data points. The approach incorporates an iterated function system where variable functions act as vertical scaling factors leading to a generalised vector-valued fractal interpolation function. Furthermore, the paper rigorously examines the smoothness of the constructed function and establishes an upper bound for the box-counting dimension of its graph.

Introduction

2
Preliminaries

In this section, definitions of some generalised contractions are presented, along with fixed-point theorems concerning these contractions. Furthermore, a few results related to iterated function systems consisting of generalised contractions are also discussed.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a metric space. The Banach contraction is one of the most commonly discussed contractions.

Definition 1
.

[
4
]
Let
h
be a function on
X
. Suppose there is a
k\in[0,1)
such that

\mu(h(x),h(y))\leq k\mu(x,y),\forall\ x,y\in X.

Then
h
is a Banach contraction.

The fixed point theorem for Banach contractions is considered a fundamental result in functional analysis.

Proposition 1
.

[
1
]
If
g
is a Banach contraction on a complete metric space
X
, then there exists a unique fixed point for
g
in
X
.

Numerous classes of contractions extend the concept of Banach contractions and the Banach fixed-point theorem. One such class is the Edelstein contractions.

Definition 2
.

[
10
]
If a function
g
on
X

\mu(g(x),g(y))<\mu(x,y),~\forall\ x,y\in X,\text{ with }x\neq y,

then
g
is an Edelstein contraction.

It is established that a fixed point exists for an Edelstein contraction within the framework of a compact metric space.

Proposition 2
.

[
6
]

Every Edelstein contraction
g
on a compact metric space
X
has a unique point
x^{\prime}\in X
such that
g(x^{\prime})=x^{\prime}
.

3
Construction of Edelstein Hidden Variable Fractal Interpolation Function

The hidden variable fractal interpolation function based on Edelstein contractions is constructed in this section.Let
V=\left\{(t_{j},v_{j})\in I\times\mathbb{R}:j=0,1,\ldots N\right\}
be the given data set where
t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{N}
. The generalised data set corresponding to
V
is

V^{*}=\left\{(t_{j},v_{j},w_{j})\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:j=0,1,\ldots N\right\},

Denote
[t_{0},t_{N}]
by
I
and
[t_{j-1},t_{j}]
as
I_{j}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots N
. Let
L_{j}:I\rightarrow I_{j}
be contractive homeomorphisms given by
L_{j}(t)=a_{j}t+f_{j}
such that
L_{j}(t_{0})=t_{j-1}
and
L_{j}(t_{N})=t_{j}
for all
j\in\left\{1,2,\ldots,N\right\}
.
For any real valued function
g
, denote
\|g\|_{\infty}=\sup\limits_{t}|g(t)|
and
l_{g}
be the Lipschitz constant for a Lipschitz function
g
. Let
b_{j},c_{j},d_{j}
and
e_{j}
be real valued Lipschitz functions on
I
such that

\|b_{j}\|_{\infty}+\|d_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1\text{ and }\|c_{j}\|_{\infty}+\|e_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 1\ \forall\ j\in\left\{1,2,\ldots N\right\}.

(3.1)

Let
s_{j}
and
r_{j}
be functions on
\mathbb{R}
such that they are Edelstein contractions. Further, define
F_{j}:I\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}

F_{j}(t,v,w)=D_{j}(t)S_{j}(v,w)+Q_{j}(t)

(3.2)

where,

D_{j}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}b_{j}(t)&c_{j}(t)\\
d_{j}(t)&e_{j}(t)\end{bmatrix},\quad S_{j}(v,w)=\begin{bmatrix}s_{j}(v)\\
r_{j}(w)\end{bmatrix},\ \text{ and }Q_{j}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}p_{j}(t)\\
q_{j}(t)\end{bmatrix}~\text{ for }~j=1,\ldots N.

(3.3)

Here,
p_{j}(t)
and
q_{j}(t)
are real-valued Lipschitz functions defined on
I
such that

F_{j}(t_{0},v_{0},w_{0})=(v_{j-1},w_{j-1})\text{ and }F_{j}(t_{N},v_{N},w_{N})=(v_{j},w_{j})~\text{ for }~j=1,2,\ldots,N.

F_{j}
are Edelstein contractions with respect to the Manhattan metric in the second and third variables.Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the compact set containing
\left\{(v_{j},w_{j})\ :\ j=0,1,\ldots N\right\}
such that
F_{j}(t,v,w)\in K
for all
(t,v,w)\in I\times K
. Define the IFS
\mathcal{I}=\left\{I\times K,g_{j},j=1,\ldots N\right\}
where,

g_{j}(t,v,w)=(L_{j}(t),F_{j}(t,v,w))~\text{ for all }~(t,v,w)\in I\times K~\mbox{and}\ j=1,2,\ldots,N.

(3.4)

Consider the space of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
valued functions on
I
,
\mathcal{C}(I)
equipped with the metric
d_{C}
induced by the Manhattan metric.Let
C_{e}(I)=\{h\in C(I):h(t_{0})~=~(v_{0},w_{0}),~h(t_{N})~=~(v_{N},w_{N})\}

C_{d}(I)=\{h\in C_{e}(I)~:~h(t_{j})~=~(v_{j},w_{j}),\\
j=1,2,\ldots N-1\}
.
Then

,
C_{e}(I)
and
C_{d}(I)
are complete metric spaces with respect to the metric

\mathcal{R}h(t)=F_{j}(L_{j}^{-1}(t),h(L_{j}^{-1}(t)))~\text{ for }~t\in I_{j}~\text{ and }~j=1,2,\ldots,N.

(3.5)

Theorem 1

Let
h
be a function in
C_{e}(I)

\mathcal{R}h
belong to
C_{d}(I)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{R}

in

The maps

defined in Eq. (
3.4

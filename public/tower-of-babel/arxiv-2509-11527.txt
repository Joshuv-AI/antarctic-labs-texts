Equality of Hölder exponents for distribution functions of Gibbs measures
Pieter Allaart and Johannes Jaerisch

Abstract
. We show that the pointwise Hölder spectrum of the distribution function of a Gibbs measure supported on the attractor of a self-conformal subset of the real line satisfies the multifractal formalism.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
37D35, 28A80

1.
Introduction

A function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be Hölder continuous with exponent
\alpha>0
if there is a constant
C
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{\alpha}
for all
x
and
y
. However, this
\alpha
represents the “worst possible” behavior, and in general, a continuous function can at many points have substantially better regularity than the worst case.

For
\alpha>0
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, write
f\in C^{\alpha}(t_{0})
if there is a constant
C
and a polynomial
P
of degree less than
\alpha
such that

(1)

|f(t)-P(t-t_{0})|\leq C|t-t_{0}|^{\alpha}\qquad\mbox{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

The
pointwise Hölder exponent
of
f
at
t
is the number

(2)

\alpha_{f}(t):=\sup\{\alpha>0:f\in C^{\alpha}(t)\},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Pointwise Hölder exponents can be difficult to calculate, especially for
\alpha>2
, where, in order to show that
f\not\in C^{\alpha}(t_{0})
, one must prove that no polynomial satisfying (
1
) exists. For this reason perhaps, many authors use the following, simpler definition of Hölder exponent. Write
f\in\tilde{C}^{\alpha}(t_{0})
if there is a constant
C
such that

|f(t)-f(t_{0})|\leq C|t-t_{0}|^{\alpha}\qquad\mbox{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$},

that is, the polynomial
P
in (
1
) is constant with value
f(t_{0})
. Define

(3)

\tilde{\alpha}_{f}(t):=\sup\{\alpha>0:f\in\tilde{C}^{\alpha}(t)\},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly,
\alpha_{f}(t)\geq\tilde{\alpha}_{f}(t)
, but the reverse inequality may fail in general. For example, if
f(t)=t^{2}
, then
\alpha_{f}(0)=\infty
(since one can take
P(t)=t^{2}
in (
1
) for all
\alpha>2
), but
\tilde{\alpha}_{f}(0)=2
. In addition, a desirable property of Hölder exponents is that they are left unchanged upon perturbation of
f
by a smooth function
g
. Indeed,
\alpha_{f}(t)=\alpha_{f+g}(t)
, whereas
\tilde{\alpha}_{f}(t)\neq\tilde{\alpha}_{f+g}(t)
in general.

On the other hand, for certain functions with fractal properties, the two definitions of Hölder exponent coincide. This was shown to be the case by the first author in
[
1
,
2
]
for certain classes of self-affine functions, including distribution functions of self-similar measures on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying the open set condition. In this note, we extend this result to distribution functions of arbitrary Gibbs measures on finitely generated self-conformal sets; see the next section for a precise statement.

A function
f
is
multifractal
if its pointwise Hölder exponents
\alpha_{f}(t)
take on more than one value as
t
ranges through the domain of
f
. For such functions, we are interested in the sets

E_{f}(\alpha):=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\alpha_{f}(t)=\alpha\}.

The
Hausdorff
and
packing multifractal spectra
of
f
are given, respectively, by

\alpha\mapsto\dim_{H}E_{f}(\alpha)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\alpha\mapsto\dim_{P}E_{f}(\alpha),

where
\dim_{H}
and
\dim_{P}
denote Hausdorff and packing dimensions, respectively.
If
f=F_{\mu}
is the distribution function of a Borel probability measure
\mu
, i.e.
F_{\mu}(x):=\mu\left((-\infty,x]\right)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
then by
[
13
, Lemma 5.1]
we have

(4)

\tilde{\alpha}_{F_{\mu}}(t_{0})=\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu\left(B(t_{0},r)\right)}{\log r}.

For the remainder of this introduction, let
\mu
a Gibbs measure associated with an arbitrary Hölder potential on the attractor of a conformal iterated function system (IFS) satisfying the open set condition - see the next section for precise definitions. It is well known that the sets

\mathscr{E}(\alpha;\mu):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\Big|\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu\left(B(x,r)\right)}{\log r}=\alpha\right\},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

satisfy the
multifractal formalism
; that is,
\dim_{H}\mathscr{E}(\alpha;\mu)=\dim_{P}\mathscr{E}(\alpha;\mu)=\beta^{*}(\alpha)
, where
\beta^{*}(\alpha)
is the Legendre transform of a suitable scaling function
\beta(q)
, defined in the next section.
The second author and Sumi
[
13
]
showed recently that, in the case when all of the maps in the IFS are strictly increasing, the Hausdorff spectrum of the modified level sets

\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}\Big|\liminf_{r\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log\mu\left(B(x,r)\right)}{\log r}=\alpha\right\},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},

is also given by the multifractal formalism:
\dim_{H}\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu)=\beta^{*}(\alpha)
. It is straightforward to extend this result to general conformal IFS. Thus, as a consequence of our main theorem, the pointwise Hölder spectrum of
F_{\mu}
is given by

\dim_{H}E_{F_{\mu}}(\alpha)=\beta^{*}(\alpha).

In a forthcoming paper, we will show that the situation is different for the packing spectrum.

2.
Notation and definitions

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval. A
\mathcal{C}^{1+\epsilon}
conformal iterated function system on
X
is given by an index set
I
with
2\leq\#I<\infty
and
\mathcal{C}^{1+\epsilon}
conformal contracting maps
\Phi=(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
,
\phi_{i}:X\rightarrow X
.

More precisely, we mean that each
\phi_{i}
extends to a

\mathcal{C}^{1+\epsilon}
differentiable map on a neighborhood of
X
which
satisfies
0<\left|\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)\right|<1
for every
x\in X
and for each
i\in I
.
For ease of presentation, we assume that each of the maps
\phi_{i},i\in I
is strictly increasing. However, this restriction is not essential.
We refer to
[
18
]
for further details and basic properties of conformal
iterated function systems.
Let
\pi:I^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the coding map of
\Phi
which is for
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots)\in I^{\mathbb{N}}
given by

\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\phi_{\omega_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\phi_{\omega_{n}}(X)=\left\{\pi(\omega)\right\}.

Let
\Lambda:=\pi(I^{\mathbb{N}})
; then
\Lambda
satisfies the set equation
\Lambda=\bigcup_{i\in I}\phi_{i}(\Lambda)
. We refer to
\Lambda
as the
self-conformal set
generated by
\Phi
. We say that
\Phi
satisfies the
open set condition
if
\phi_{i}(\mathrm{Int}(X))\cap\phi_{j}(\mathrm{Int}(X))=\varnothing
for all
i,j\in I
with
i\neq j
.
To utilize the symbolic thermodynamic formalism (see
[
6
]
) we will also need the following definitions. We denote by
\sigma:I^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow I^{\mathbb{N}}
the left-shift on
I^{\mathbb{N}}
which becomes a compact metric space endowed
with the shift metric. Let
\varphi:I^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the geometric
potential of
\Phi
, given by

(5)

\varphi(\omega):=\log\left(\phi_{\omega_{1}}^{\prime}(\pi(\sigma(\omega)))\right),\quad\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots)\in I^{\mathbb{N}}.

Since each
\phi_{i}
is
\mathcal{C}^{1+\epsilon}
differentiable, it follows that
\varphi
is Hölder continuous. Let
\psi:I^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be Hölder continuous and denote by

S_{n}\psi:=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\psi\circ\sigma^{k},\qquad n\geq 1

its ergodic sums. We say that a Borel probability measure
\mu_{\psi}
on
I^{\mathbb{N}}
is a Gibbs measure for
\psi
if there exist two constants
C\geq 1
and
P\in\mathbb{R}
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
w\in I^{n}
we have

(6)

C^{-1}\leq\frac{\mu_{\psi}[w_{1},\dots,w_{n}]}{e^{S_{n}\psi(\overline{w})-nP}}\leq C.

Here
\overline{w}
denotes the infinite periodic sequence with period block
w
.
It is well known that a Gibbs measure
\mu_{\psi}
for a Hölder continuous potential always exists
[
6
]
.
Further note that the constant
P
in the definition of a Gibbs measure is equal to the topological pressure of
\psi
with respect to
\sigma
, defined by

\mathcal{P}(\psi):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log\sum_{w\in I^{k}}\exp S_{k}\psi(\overline{w}).

We will frequently make use of the fact that any Hölder continuous
\psi:I^{\mathbb{N}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies the
b
ounded distortion property

(7)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{w\in I^{n}}\sup_{x,y\in I^{\mathbb{N}}}\left|S_{n}\psi(w\rho)-S_{n}\psi(w\tau)\right|<+\infty.

We remark that if
\mu_{\psi}
is a Gibbs measure for
\psi
, then it is also a Gibbs measure for
\psi-\mathcal{P}(\psi)
. We may therefore always assume that
\mathcal{P}(\psi)=0
by replacing
\psi
with
\psi-\mathcal{P}(\psi)
.

Let

(8)

\alpha_{+}:=\sup_{\omega\in I^{\mathbb{N}}}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S_{n}\psi(\omega)}{S_{n}\varphi(\omega)},\qquad\alpha_{-}:=\inf_{\omega\in I^{\mathbb{N}}}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{S_{n}\psi(\omega)}{S_{n}\varphi(\omega)}.

It is well known that the multifractal spectrum
\mathcal{F}(\alpha)

is complete
[
28
]
, that is,
\mathcal{F}(\alpha)\neq\varnothing
if and only
if
\alpha\in\left[\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}\right]
.
Moreover, the following dichotomy is well known
[
25
]
: either we have
\alpha_{-}<\alpha_{+}
, or
\psi
is cohomologous to
(\dim_{H}\Lambda)\varphi
. Note that in the latter case we have
\alpha_{-}=\alpha_{+}=\dim_{H}\Lambda
.

Since
\varphi<0
, there is for each
q\in\mathbb{R}
a unique
\beta(q)
such that

(9)

\mathcal{P}(\beta(q)\varphi+q\psi)=0.

We denote by

(10)

\beta^{*}(\alpha):=\inf_{q\in\mathbb{R}}\left\{\beta(q)+\alpha q\right\}

the Legendre transform of
\beta
.
If
\alpha_{-}<\alpha_{+}
, then it is well known that
\beta^{*}
is real-analytic and strictly concave on
(\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+})

[
23
]
.

3.
The main theorem

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Phi=(\phi_{i}:X\rightarrow X)_{i\in I}
be a conformal iterated
function system satisfying the open set condition.
Let
\varphi
denote the geometric potential of
\Phi
defined in (
5
) and let
\psi
be any Hölder potential with
\mathcal{P}(\psi)=0
such that
\psi
is not cohomologous to
\varphi
. Let
\mu_{\psi}
be a Gibbs measure for
\psi
. Then, for the distribution function
F
of
\mu_{\psi}\circ\pi^{-1}
, we have that
\alpha_{F}=\tilde{\alpha}_{F}
.

Remark 3.2
.

The condition that
\psi
is not cohomologous to
\varphi
is needed to rule out some degenerate cases. As a simple example, consider the IFS
\Phi=\{x/2,(x+1)/2\}
, whose attractor is the interval
[0,1]
. Clearly,
\varphi\equiv-\log 2
. Choosing
\psi\equiv-\log 2
as well, we see that
\mu:=\mu_{\psi}\circ\pi^{-1}
is Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
, so
F
is infinitely differentiable on
(0,1)
and hence
\alpha_{F}(t)\equiv\infty
on
(0,1)
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that
\tilde{\alpha}_{F}(t)\equiv 1
on
(0,1)
.

More generally, we claim that for a Hölder continuous
\psi
with
\mathcal{P}(\psi)=0
we have that
\psi
is cohomologous to
\varphi
if and only if
\Lambda
is an interval and some/any Gibbs measure
\mu_{\psi}
for
\psi
satisfies that
\mu_{\psi}\circ\pi^{-1}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure with a Radon-Nykodym density bounded away from zero and infinity.
To prove this, first assume that
\psi
is cohomologous to
\varphi
. Hence,
P(\varphi)=P(\psi)=0
and thus,
\dim_{H}(\Lambda)=1
.
Hence, by the well-known construction of conformal measures (see e.g.
[
19
, Theorem 4.2.11]
),
\Lambda
has positive Lebesgue measure. Hence, by Lebesgue’s density theorem, we conclude that
X=\bigcup_{i\in I}\Phi_{i}(X)
and thus,
\Lambda=X
is an interval. Indeed, if
X\setminus\bigcup_{i\in I}\Phi_{i}(X)
was non-empty, it would contain an interval of positive length. By standard arguments, this would imply that
\Lambda
contains no Lebesgue density points, which gives the desired contradiction.
Consequently, the normalised Lebesgues measure on
\Lambda
is a Gibbs measure for
\varphi
. Since
\psi
is cohomologous to
\varphi
,
\mu_{\psi}
is also a Gibbs measure for
\varphi
. It follows that
\mu_{\psi}
is equivalent to the Lebesgue measure with a density bounded away from zero and infinity.

Conversely, assume that
\Lambda
is an interval and
\mu_{\psi}\circ\pi^{-1}
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue with density bounded away from zero and infinity.
Hence, Lebesgue measure is also Gibbs measure for
\psi
. Since Lebesgue measure is also a Gibbs measure for
\varphi
and
P(\varphi)=P(\psi)=0
, it follows from
[
19
, Theorem 2.2.7]
that
\varphi
is cohomologous to
\psi
.

The key to proving Theorem
3.1
is to prove Proposition
3.4
below. The proof of that proposition uses the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
P(\psi)=0
. Suppose that there
exists a finite set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\ell_{0}\geq 1
such
that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
and all
k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\in I

we have

\frac{S_{\ell}\psi\left(\overline{k_{1}\dots k_{\ell}}\right)}{S_{\ell}\varphi\left(\overline{k_{1}\dots k_{\ell}}\right)}\in A.

Then
\psi
is cohomologous to
(\dim_{H}\Lambda)\varphi
and
A=\left\{\dim_{H}\Lambda\right\}
.

Proof.

By the bounded distortion property (
12
), it is easy to see that for each

\ell_{0}\geq 1
,

[\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}]=\overline{\left\{\frac{S_{\ell}\psi\left(\overline{k_{1}\dots k_{\ell}}\right)}{S_{\ell}\varphi\left(\overline{k_{1}\dots k_{\ell}}\right)}:\ell\geq\ell_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{\ell}\in I\right\}}.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{P}(\psi)=0
, and
\psi
is not cohomologous to
\varphi
.
Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
the limit

\lim_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{F(y)-F(x)}{(y-x)^{k}}

either does not exist, or it takes the value
0
or
+\infty
.

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

The statement is trivial for even
k
, since
F
is increasing. So below we will assume that
k
is odd.

Suppose by way of contradiction that

\lim_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{F(y)-F(x)}{(y-x)^{k}}=L\in(0,+\infty).

Note that the limit cannot be negative, since
F
is increasing.

Let
\omega\in I^{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\pi(\omega)=x
.
Let
[s_{n},t_{n}]:=\pi\left(\left[\omega_{n}\right]\right)
. Then
s_{n}\leq x\leq t_{n}
, and

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{F(t_{n})-F(s_{n})}{(t_{n}-s_{n})^{k}}&=\left(\frac{t_{n}-x}{t_{n}-s_{n}}\right)^{k}\cdot\frac{F(t_{n})-F(x)}{(t_{n}-x)^{k}}\\
&\quad+\left(\frac{x-s_{n}}{t_{n}-s_{n}}\right)^{k}\cdot\frac{F(s_{n})-F(x)}{(s_{n}-x)^{k}}.\end{split}

Denote

r_{n}:=\frac{t_{n}-x}{t_{n}-s_{n}}.

Observe that
r_{n}\in[0,1]
and

1-r_{n}=\frac{x-s_{n}}{t_{n}-s_{n}}.

There exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}\in I^{\ell}

containing at least two different letters such that

S_{\ell}(\psi-k\varphi)\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)\neq 0.

Indeed such
\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}
always exist for otherwise, Lemma
3.3
implies that
\psi
is cohomologous to
(\dim_{H}\Lambda)\varphi

and
\dim_{H}\Lambda=k=1
, contradicting our hypothesis.

Taking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
\lim r_{n}=r\in[0,1]
.
Then by (
11
), we have

\frac{F(t_{n})-F(s_{n})}{(t_{n}-s_{n})^{k}}\rightarrow\left(r^{k}+(1-r)^{k}\right)L\in\left[2^{1-k}L,L\right],

as routine calculus shows that the function
r\mapsto r^{k}+(1-r)^{k}
is minimized at
r=1/2
.
Hence,

(12)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mu_{\psi}\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)}{\mathrm{diam}\left(\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)\right)^{k}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F(t_{n})-F(s_{n})}{(t_{n}-s_{n})^{k}}\in\left[2^{1-k}L,L\right].

Now, we fix an integer
N\geq 2
and consider the interval

\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}(\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell})^{N}\right]\right)=:\left[s_{n}^{N},t_{n}^{N}\right].

Put

r_{n}^{N}:=\frac{t_{n}^{N}-x}{t_{n}^{N}-s_{n}^{N}}.

Recall that the maps
\phi_{i},i\in I
are all increasing, so if
u
and
v
are two words of the same length and
u\prec v
in the lexicographical order, then
\pi([u])
lies to the left of
\pi([v])
.

We claim that there is an infinite subset
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that for each
n\in\mathcal{N}
, the point
x
and the interval
[s_{n}^{N},t_{n}^{N}]
are separated by at least a full cylinder interval of level at most
n+\ell+1
. To see this, let
j<\ell
be the smallest integer such that
\tau_{j+1}\neq\tau_{1}
. Such
j
exists since
\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}
contains at least two different letters. Write
\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}=\tau_{1}^{j}\tau_{j+1}\dots\tau_{\ell}
, and consider two cases:

Case 1.
If
\omega
ends in
\tau_{1}^{\infty}
, then for every sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
the cylinder interval
\pi\big([\omega|_{n}\tau_{1}^{j+1}\tau_{j+1}]\big)
separates
x
from the interval
\pi\big([\omega|_{n}\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{j+1}]\big)
, and hence from
[s_{n}^{N},t_{n}^{N}]
.

Case 2.
Otherwise, there are infinitely many
n
such that
\omega_{n+1}\neq\tau_{1}
. Take such an
n
. If
\omega_{n+1}<\tau_{1}
, then

\omega_{n+1}\dots\omega_{n+\ell+1}\prec\tau_{1}1^{\ell}\prec\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}\tau_{1},

so the cylinder interval
\pi\big([\omega|_{n}\tau_{1}1^{\ell}]\big)
lies between
x
and
[s_{n}^{N},t_{n}^{N}]
. A similar argument, with the inequalities reversed, applies when
\omega_{n+1}>\tau_{1}
.

In either case, the claim follows. By replacing
\mathcal{N}
by an infinite subset if necessary, we may assume that either
t_{n}^{N}>x
for all
n\in\mathcal{N}
, or
t_{n}^{N}<x
for all
n\in\mathcal{N}
. Without loss of generality, we assume the former. The argument in the latter case is similar. Thus,
r_{n}^{N}>0
for all
n\in\mathcal{N}
.

Let
r_{\min}>0
be a number such that
|\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)|\geq r_{\min}
for all
i\in I
and
x\in X
. Then

(13)

\mathrm{diam}\pi([uv])\geq\mathrm{diam}\pi([u])\cdot r_{\min}^{|v|}

for any two finite words
u
and
v
.

For
n\in\mathcal{N}
, let
[u_{n},v_{n}]
be a cylinder interval of length
n+\ell+1
separating
x
and
[s_{n}^{N},t_{n}^{N}]
. Then by (
13
),

|t_{n}^{N}-x|\geq v_{n}-u_{n}\geq\mathrm{diam}\pi([\omega|_{n}])\cdot r_{\min}^{\ell+1}.

Hence, there is a constant
C=C(\ell)>1
, depending only on
\ell
but not on
n
or
N
, such that

C^{-1}<\frac{t_{n}^{N}-x}{\mathrm{diam}\big(\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)\big)}<C,

for all
n\in\mathcal{N}
. We denote this by

(14)

t_{n}^{N}-x\asymp_{\ell}\mathrm{diam}\big(\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)\big).

Now (
14
) implies

(15)

r_{n}^{N}=\frac{t_{n}^{N}-x}{t_{n}^{N}-s_{n}^{N}}\asymp_{\ell}\frac{\mathrm{diam}\left(\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)\right)}{\mathrm{diam}\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}(\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell})^{N}\right]\right)}\asymp_{\ell}e^{-NS_{\ell}\left(\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})}.

Hence, by passing to a subsequence, using a diagonal argument, we may assume that
r_{n}^{N}
converges to some number
r^{N}\in\mathbb{R}
for every
N\geq 1
.

Using (
11
) and letting
n\rightarrow\infty
we obtain

(16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(t_{n}^{N})-F(s_{n}^{N})}{(t_{n}^{N}-s_{n}^{N})^{k}}=\left((r^{N})^{k}+(1-r^{N})^{k}\right)L\geq 2^{1-k}L.

On the other hand, by the Gibbs property (
6
) of
\mu_{\psi}
and the bounded distortion property (
12
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{F(t_{n}^{N})-F(s_{n}^{N})}{(t_{n}^{N}-s_{n}^{N})^{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{\psi}\left(\left[\omega|_{n}(\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell})^{N}\right]\right)}{\left(\mathrm{diam}\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}(\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell})^{N}\right]\right)\right)^{k}}

\displaystyle\asymp_{\ell}\frac{\mu_{\psi}\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)}{\left(\mathrm{diam}\pi\left(\left[\omega|_{n}\right]\right)\right)^{k}}\cdot e^{NS_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})}

\displaystyle\asymp_{\ell}e^{NS_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})}.

If
k=1
this already gives a contradiction, since we then have equality in (
16
), which implies
S_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})=0
.

Suppose now that
k\geq 3
. We have shown that

2^{1-k}L\leq\big((r^{N})^{k}+(1-r^{N})^{k}\big)L=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{F(t_{n}^{N})-F(s_{n}^{N})}{(t_{n}^{N}-s_{n}^{N})^{k}}\asymp e^{NS_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})}.

Since
S_{\ell}(\psi-k\varphi)\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)\neq 0

it follows that
S_{\ell}(\psi-k\varphi)\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)>0
, and hence,
r^{N}\rightarrow\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
Since
k
is odd,
r^{k}+(1-r)^{k}=kr^{k-1}+O(r^{k-2})
as
r\to\infty
, so we conclude that
(r^{N})^{k}+(1-r^{N})^{k}\asymp(r^{N})^{k-1}
. Thus,

(r^{N})^{k-1}\asymp e^{NS_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})}.

Together with (
15
), this implies

e^{NS_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})}\asymp(r^{N})^{k-1}\asymp e^{-N(k-1)S_{\ell}\left(\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})},

and hence,

S_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})=-(k-1)S_{\ell}\left(\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}),

i.e.

S_{\ell}\left(\psi-\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})=0.

We have thus shown that if
\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}
contains at least two different letters, then

S_{\ell}\left(\psi-k\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})\neq 0\implies S_{\ell}\left(\psi-\varphi\right)(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}})=0.

Therefore,

(17)

\frac{S_{\ell}\psi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)}{S_{\ell}\varphi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)}\in\left\{1,k\right\}.

It remains to consider the fixed points
\overline{\tau_{1}}
:

\frac{S_{\ell}\psi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}}\right)}{S_{\ell}\varphi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}}\right)}=\frac{\psi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}}\right)}{\varphi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}}\right)}.

Hence, the set

A:=\left\{\frac{S_{\ell}\psi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)}{S_{\ell}\varphi\left(\overline{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}}\right)},\quad\ell\geq 1,\quad\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{\ell}\in I\right\}

contains only finitely many values, at least one of which is 1 or
k
in view of (
17
). Now, Lemma
3.3
implies
that
\psi
is cohomologous to
(\dim_{H}\Lambda)\varphi
and
\dim_{H}\Lambda=1
.
Hence,
\varphi
is cohomologous to
\psi
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

We show that, for any
\alpha>1
, if there is a polynomial
P
of degree
n<\alpha
such that

(18)

|F(t)-P(t-t_{0})|\leq C|t-t_{0}|^{\alpha}\qquad\mbox{for all $t$},

then
P
is constant:
P(t)=f(t_{0})
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This clearly implies that
\alpha_{F}(t_{0})=\tilde{\alpha}_{F}(t_{0})
.

Suppose, to the contrary, that
P
has degree
1\leq n<\alpha
and satisfies (
18
). We can write

P(t)=a_{0}+a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+\dots+a_{n}t^{n},

where
a_{0}=P(0)=F(t_{0})
. We will show by induction that
a_{1}=a_{2}=\dots=a_{n}=0
. First, write

\frac{F(t)-F(t_{0})}{t-t_{0}}=\frac{F(t)-P(t-t_{0})}{t-t_{0}}+\frac{P(t-t_{0})-F(t_{0})}{t-t_{0}},

and observe by (
18
) that

\left|\frac{F(t)-P(t-t_{0})}{t-t_{0}}\right|\leq C|t-t_{0}|^{\alpha-1}\to 0\qquad\mbox{as $t\to t_{0}$}.

Hence,

F^{\prime}(t_{0})=\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\frac{F(t)-F(t_{0})}{t-t_{0}}=\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\frac{P(t-t_{0})-P(0)}{t-t_{0}}=P^{\prime}(0)=a_{1}.

By Proposition
3.4
, it follows that
a_{1}=0
, establishing the basis for the induction.

Next, take
2\leq j\leq n
, and assume we have already shown that
a_{1}=\dots=a_{j-1}=0
. Thus,

P(t)=F(t_{0})+a_{j}t^{j}+\dots+a_{n}t^{n}.

Since
j\leq n<\alpha
, we can apply the same idea as above to conclude that

\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\frac{F(t)-F(t_{0})}{(t-t_{0})^{j}}=\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\frac{P(t-t_{0})-P(0)}{(t-t_{0})^{j}}=\lim_{t\to t_{0}}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\frac{a_{i}(t-t_{0})^{i}}{(t-t_{0})^{j}}=a_{j},

and therefore,
a_{j}=0
by Proposition
3.4
.

By induction, we have established the claim, so
P(t)\equiv f(t_{0})
, as desired.
∎

4.
The pointwise Hölder spectrum of
F_{\mu}

Recall from the Introduction that
E_{F}(\alpha)=\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\alpha_{F}(t)=\alpha\}
.
As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.1
.

With the notation of Theorem
3.1
, we have that

\dim_{H}E_{F}(\alpha)=\beta^{*}(\alpha)\qquad\forall\,\alpha\in[\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}],

whereas
E_{F}(\alpha)=\varnothing
for
\alpha\not\in[\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}]
.

Proof.

By (
4
) and Theorem
3.1
, it follows that

E_{F}(\alpha)=\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha,\mu_{\psi}).

As shown in
[
13
]
,
\dim_{H}\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha,\mu_{\psi})=\beta^{*}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in[\alpha_{-},\alpha_{+}]
, and
\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha,\mu_{\psi})=\varnothing
otherwise.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The situation is different for the packing spectrum. We illustrate this for the case when
\mu
is a self-similar measure. Suppose the maps
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\in I}
are similarities with contraction ratios
\{r_{i}\}
, and
\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
is a probability vector. Put
\psi(\omega)=\log p_{\omega_{1}}
for
\omega\in I^{\mathbb{N}}
. Likewise we obtain
\varphi(\omega)=\log r_{\omega_{1}}
. Then
\mu_{\psi}
is just the self-similar measure satisfying

\mu=\sum_{i\in I}p_{i}\mu\circ\phi_{i}^{-1},

and the pressure equation (
9
) becomes

\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}^{\beta(q)}p_{i}^{q}=1.

Let
A(x;\mu)
denote the set of accumulation points of
\log\mu(B(x,r))/\log r
as
r\to 0
. It was shown by Baek, Olsen and Snigireva
[
3
]
under the strong separation condition, and later by Zhou and Chen
[
30
]
under the open set condition, that for any closed interval
I
, the set

\Delta(I):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:A(x;\mu)=I\}

has packing dimension

\dim_{P}\Delta(I)=\max_{\alpha\in I}\beta^{*}(\alpha).

Let
\alpha_{0}
be the unique value of
\alpha
maximizing
\beta^{*}(\alpha)
. Take first
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
, and put
I=[\alpha,\alpha_{0}]
. Since
\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha)\supset I
, it follows that

\dim_{P}E_{F}(\alpha)=\dim_{P}\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu)\geq\dim_{P}\Delta(I)=\beta^{*}(\alpha_{0})=\dim_{P}\Lambda.

The reverse inequality is trivial since
E_{F}(\alpha)\subset\Lambda
. Hence,
\dim_{P}E_{F}(\alpha)=\beta^{*}(\alpha_{0})
.

For
\alpha\geq\alpha_{0}
, we trivially have
\dim_{P}\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu)\geq\dim_{P}\mathscr{E}(\alpha;\mu)=\beta^{*}(\alpha)
since
\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu)\supset\mathscr{E}(\alpha,\mu)
. But we also have
\dim_{P}\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu)\leq\beta^{*}(\alpha)
. This follows directly from a careful examination of the proof that
\dim_{P}\mathscr{E}(\alpha;\mu)=\beta^{*}(\alpha)
; see, for instance,
[
21
, p. 501]
.
We thus conclude that

(19)

\dim_{P}E_{F}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\beta^{*}(\alpha_{0})&\mbox{if $\alpha_{-}\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}$},\\
\beta^{*}(\alpha)&\mbox{if $\alpha_{0}\leq\alpha\leq\alpha_{+}$}.\end{cases}

Note that, on the left half of the multifractal spectrum, the Hausdorff dimension does not differentiate between the sets
\mathscr{E}(\alpha;\mu)
and
\mathscr{E}_{*}(\alpha;\mu)
, but the packing dimension does. Also, it is unusual to see a packing spectrum that is constant on one side of the maximum.

In a forthcoming paper, we will extend the packing spectrum (
19
) to the case of distribution functions of general Gibbs measures.

Early and late biomarkers as predictors of severe dengue: a comprehensive umbrella review protocol
Authors: Kamani Mangalika Gunasekera, Maheeka Seneviwickrama, Sriyani Ranasinghe, Kumindu Gamage, Madhusha Gonapaladeniya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPredicting the progression to severe dengue remains a critical yet challenging aspect of patient management. This umbrella review aims to identify biomarkers associated with the development of severe dengue. The primary objective is to determine which biomarkers can predict progression to severe disease in dengue-infected patients. Secondary objectives include identifying (a) early biomarkers (detected on days 1–3 of illness), (b) late biomarkers (detected after day 3), (c) biomarkers requiring further investigation and (d) differences in predictive biomarkers between patients aged <15 years and those ≥15 years.Method and analysisThe review questions were formulated based on the Population, Concept and Context (PCC) framework. This review will follow the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology for umbrella reviews and be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Overviews of Systematic Reviews guidelines. The protocol has been registered in PROSPERO (CRD420251058284). MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, JBI Evidence Synthesis and DARE databases will be searched from 1/1/1990 to 1/6/2025. The findings are expected to support early risk stratification and guide future biomarker research in dengue infection. The systematic reviews included in this umbrella review may define severe dengue according to either the WHO 1997 or 2009 guidelines.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required since the work involves published documents. The review findings will be communicated to relevant stakeholders through conference presentations and publication in an open-access journal.Trial registration numberPROSPERO 2025 CRD420251058284. Introduction

Dengue viral infections represent one of the most rapidly emerging mosquito-borne infections in the world, increasing from 23 million in 1990 to 104 million in 2017.1 As of 30 April 2024, over 7.6 million dengue cases have been reported to the WHO, including 3.4 million confirmed cases, over 16 000 severe cases and over 3000 deaths.2 Although a significant global increase in dengue cases has been reported from 2019 to 2024, the majority of this rise in 2024 has occurred in the Region of the Americas. As of 2024, 90 countries are experiencing active dengue transmission.2

Although most DEN virus infections are asymptomatic or mild, some individuals develop severe dengue with multi-organ dysfunction and even mortality in some cases.3 A severe form of dengue is characterised by increased vascular permeability, bleeding and potentially fatal complications. Vascular leakage is believed to result from endothelial dysfunction, causing increased vascular permeability.3

Predicting a patient’s likelihood of developing severe dengue remains a challenging yet crucial aspect of enabling timely and proactive patient management. The WHO 1997 classification of dengue included dengue fever (DF) and dengue haemorrhagic fever (DHF).4 However, this system was considered too rigid and often led to under-recognition of severe disease. As a result, the WHO revised its guidelines in 2009, introducing a new classification that categorised dengue into non-severe and severe forms.5 Non-severe dengue was further divided into two groups: dengue with warning signs and dengue without warning signs. This change was based on the recognition that the presence of warning signs may indicate a higher risk of progression to severe dengue.5 Previous systematic reviews have reported heterogeneity in the dengue classification systems used, leading to inconsistent findings. This umbrella review aims to synthesise evidence pertaining to both classification systems. The terminology outlined in the WHO 2022 guidelines will be adopted for the purposes of this protocol.3

A biomarker is a measurable characteristic that indicates a normal or abnormal biological process, pathogenic process or a response to an exposure or intervention.6 They can serve as risk indicators, diagnostic tools, monitoring measures, prognostic indicators, predictive markers, pharmacodynamics/response measures and safety markers.6 In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the exploration of biomarkers that can serve as predictive tools for DHF/severe dengue.7 Early biomarkers, which may indicate the onset of severe disease prior to the appearance of clinical symptoms, and late biomarkers, which reflect the progression of the disease, offer promising avenues for enhancing diagnostic accuracy and patient outcomes.7 8 A myriad of candidates has been proposed as predictive biomarkers, but inconsistent results across studies and issues related to specificity complicate the use of these in clinical management.9 10 A systematic review and meta-analysis by Thach et al. evaluated host data collected within 72 hours of fever onset in patients with varying levels of dengue severity. The study provided moderate-certainty evidence, indicating that elevated AST levels were significantly associated with progression to severe dengue. However, alanine transaminase (ALT) was not elevated during this period.7 In contrast, other systematic reviews aimed at identifying early predictors of severe dengue found evidence to support the use of both AST and ALT as reliable predictors for developing severe disease.8 10 This highlights inconsistencies and limitations that exist in the literature. Furthermore, the use of varying WHO dengue classifications across studies has complicated the interpretation and meta-analysis of biomarkers for severe dengue.7

An umbrella review is a comprehensive synthesis of evidence that gathers and assesses results from several systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Various terms have been used to describe this methodological approach, including ‘overviews of reviews,’ ‘reviews of reviews,’ ‘summaries of systematic reviews,’ and ‘systematic reviews of systematic reviews’.11 This umbrella review aims to synthesise findings in existing literature on both early and late biomarkers associated with DHF/severe dengue, evaluating their predictive value and clinical applicability. This comprehensive approach allows researchers and clinicians to pinpoint critical biomarkers that can aid in early diagnosis and effective management, ultimately improving patient outcomes. Moreover, the integration of predictive biomarkers into clinical practice not only streamlines patient management but also optimises resource allocation in healthcare settings burdened by dengue epidemics. For instance, early identification of patients not at risk for severe outcomes can significantly reduce hospital overcrowding and improve triage efficiency, particularly in low- to middle-income countries where medical resources are often limited. By consolidating findings from multiple studies, this review seeks to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of research in this domain, highlighting gaps in knowledge and suggesting directions for future investigations.

A preliminary literature search for umbrella reviews on this topic was conducted across multiple databases, including MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase and PROSPERO. The search did not yield any relevant publications on umbrella reviews. Therefore, a comprehensive overview of predictive biomarkers for DHF/severe dengue was considered timely and warranted.

Review questions

Review questions were prepared using the Population, Concept and Context (PCC) framework. The primary research question is: “Which biomarker/s can predict the progression to severe dengue in patients with dengue infection?”

Secondary research questions include: (1) Which biomarkers detected during days 1–3 of illness can predict progression to severe dengue, serving as early biomarkers? (2) Which biomarkers detected after day 3 of illness can predict progression to severe dengue, serving as late biomarkers? (3) Which biomarkers have been insufficiently studied and warrant further investigation in future research? (4) Are there differences in predictive biomarkers for severe dengue between patients below 15 years and those 15 years or older?

Methods

The protocol is registered in PROSPERO 2025 CRD420251058284. The review will be conducted adhering to the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) umbrella review guidelines12 and will be reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Overviews of Systematic Reviews (PRIO).13 The planned commencement date for this review is January 2026, with its completion anticipated by December 2026.

The systematic reviews included in this review may define severe dengue according to either the WHO 1997 or 2009 guidelines. The 1997 WHO classification divided dengue into three categories: DF, DHF and dengue shock syndrome (DSS). DHF was further classified into Grades 1 and 2, and DSS included Grades 3 and 4. Severe dengue was classified as DHF or DSS.4 However, according to the WHO 2009 guidelines, severe dengue is defined as dengue with any of the following clinical manifestations: severe plasma leakage leading to shock or fluid accumulation with respiratory distress; severe bleeding or severe organ impairment, such as hepatitis (elevated transaminases ≥1000 IU/L), impaired consciousness or heart impairment.5

Search strategy

A comprehensive search strategy will be implemented across multiple databases, including MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase, CINAHL, and major repositories of systematic reviews, including Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, JBI Evidence Synthesis and DARE. Relevant articles will be searched from 1/1/1990 to 1/6/2025. A combination of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords combined with Boolean operators (AND, OR) will be used to create the search query. The search strategy created for MEDLINE is given. (“Dengue haemorrhagic fever” OR “severe dengue” OR “dengue fever” OR “dengue virus infection” OR “dengue shock syndrome”) AND (“biomarker*” OR “biological marker*” OR “predictive marker*” OR “marker*”)

A search for grey literature will aim to identify eligible systematic reviews from reports of governments and non-governmental organisations. We will search CORDIS, the primary source of results from European Commission-funded research (https://cordis.europa.eu/), and websites of the National Institute for Health and Care Research (https://www.nihr.ac.uk/) and the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (https://www.ahrq.gov/).

Methodological quality assessment

The methodological quality of included systematic reviews will be assessed using the JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Systematic Reviews and Research Syntheses.14 To obtain an overall appraisal of each review, the following quality thresholds are defined: low quality (0%–49% of criteria met), moderate quality (50%–74% of criteria met) and high quality (75% or more of criteria met). Results of the quality appraisal will be presented visually using a traffic light scheme to display the score of each item in each review (green for ‘yes’, red for ‘no’ and yellow for ‘unclear’, with blank for ‘not applicable’). The overall methodological quality will be described in the final report.

Data synthesis and summary of evidence

The data synthesis will involve two approaches: a narrative synthesis for qualitative aggregation of characteristics of the included reviews and a structured synthesis for quantitative outcome measures to answer the research questions.

A narrative synthesis will summarise the information regarding the citation details; study population and number of participants; the setting and context; the number of databases sourced and searched; the date range of database searching; the date range of included studies that inform each outcome of interest; the number, types of studies and country of origin of primary research studies in the included research synthesis; the instrument used to appraise the primary studies in the research synthesis and the rating of their quality; the outcomes reported by the included reviews that are relevant to the umbrella review question and the type of review and the method of synthesis/analysis employed to synthesise the evidence as well as any comments or notes for the umbrella review.

Overall effect estimates such as ORs, relative risks or HRs with their 95% CIs extracted from systematic reviews, number of studies that inform the outcome and number of participants from included studies will be reported in a tabular form. Where applicable, measures of diagnostic accuracy such as sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve with their corresponding 95% CIs will be extracted and reported for biomarkers assessed for their diagnostic predictive performance.

Heterogeneity of results in each systematic review included for the study will be presented using the reported meta-analysis findings, such as I2 statistic or Cochran’s Q test. As per the standard practice of an umbrella review, no pooled summary measures will be calculated from the review’s summary. Conclusions will be drawn from agreements or disagreements of the existing high level evidence. Any overlaps of original research studies in each of the included research syntheses will also be presented.

The Principles from Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) will be used for an overall assessment of the quality of evidence produced.16

Discussion

Risk stratification of patients at high risk of progressing to severe dengue is critical for timely clinical management and the prevention of adverse outcomes. This umbrella review is expected to provide a comprehensive synthesis of existing evidence for early biomarkers (days 1–3) that predict progression to severe dengue and late biomarkers (> 3 days) that foretell severe disease. By integrating findings from multiple high-quality systematic reviews and meta-analyses (assessed by JBI guidelines), this umbrella review may provide a consolidated understanding of the predictive and prognostic value of various biomarkers across different stages of dengue infection.

In addition, these biomarkers can be stratified according to age (<15 years and those 15 years or older). Stratifying biomarkers by age allows for identification of age-specific patterns in disease pathophysiology and biomarker expression. These distinctions are important due to recognised immunological and clinical differences between paediatric and adult dengue populations.17

The identification of biomarkers that have not been sufficiently investigated is critical for advancing both diagnostic and therapeutic research. In systematic reviews or meta-analyses, certain underexplored biomarkers may show promising preliminary associations with disease outcomes, but they may need further validation. Evidence summarised in this review may help clarify how validated biomarkers can be applied in clinical settings to assist with early diagnosis, therapeutic decision-making and monitoring of disease progression in dengue infection.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval was not required, as this umbrella review used data from previously published systematic reviews on the topic of interest.

The umbrella review will adhere to the JBI Umbrella Review Guidelines12 and follow the PRISMA 2020 reporting checklist with a PRISMA flowchart to transparently illustrate the study selection process and critical appraisal findings. The final report will be submitted to a high-impact peer-reviewed journal and presented at international conferences.

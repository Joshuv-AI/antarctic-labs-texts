A phase 1 study to evaluate the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics of PF‐07261271 in healthy participants
Authors: Srividya Neelakantan, Christopher Banfield, Kenneth Hung, Anna Sapone, Vivek Pradhan, Yuxi Zhao, Dana Parsons‐Rich, Grace Lee, Bryce G Johnson, Elena Peeva, Michael S Vincent, Valerie M Clerin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimThere is an unmet need for more effective therapies in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). A single drug that blocks multiple distinct pathogenic pathways may offer therapeutic benefit superior to current monotherapies. PF‐07261271, a bispecific antibody targeting both the p40 subunit of interleukin‐12/23 and tumour necrosis factor‐like cytokine 1A (TL1A), is being investigated as a potential new IBD therapy. This study evaluates the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) of PF‐07261271 in healthy participants.MethodsThis phase 1, randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled study assessed single ascending doses (SAD; intravenous 30–1000 mg) and multiple doses (MD; subcutaneous 300 mg once every 4 weeks) of PF‐07261271 in healthy adults. Safety, PK, immunogenicity and exploratory PD serum biomarkers (total soluble TL1A [sTL1A] and p40) were evaluated.ResultsThirty‐five participants were enrolled (Part A: SAD, N = 27; Part B: MD, N = 8). PF‐07261271 was generally safe and well tolerated; all treatment‐emergent adverse events were mild/moderate. Following peak serum concentration, there was a multi‐phase decline with a trend towards increased terminal half‐life at the higher doses. For the MD cohort, the estimated bioavailability of PF‐07261271 administered subcutaneously was 48%. Dose‐dependent increases from baseline in serum total sTL1A and p40 levels were observed in participants receiving PF‐07261271, demonstrating target engagement. There was no apparent impact of anti‐drug antibodies on safety, PK or PD parameters.ConclusionPF‐07261271 demonstrated p40 and TL1A target engagement and a favourable safety profile. Further studies are warranted to evaluate its safety and efficacy in patients with IBD. ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05536440.Keywords: bispecific antibody, inflammatory bowel disease, p40, TL1A

INTRODUCTION

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), encompassing ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, characterized by immune dysregulation and a complex interplay of inflammatory pathways. 1 Current treatments for IBD primarily include broad or targeted immunosuppressive agents that aim to dampen pathogenic immune activation in the GI tract and induce remission. 2 However, a large proportion of patients receiving these therapies do not achieve lasting remission, 3 highlighting a critical need for more effective, innovative treatments to achieve favourable efficacy outcomes. These limitations may be addressed by simultaneously targeting multiple disease mechanisms, potentially increasing efficacy while maintaining safety. 4 , 5 As such, a bispecific antibody that inhibits two distinct pathological targets may present a compelling alternative to available therapies. 6 , 7 , 8 , 9

The shared p40 subunit of interleukin (IL)‐12 and IL‐23 and tumour necrosis factor‐like cytokine 1A (TL1A) are two clinically validated therapeutic targets in IBD, 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 playing critical roles in disease pathogenesis. 16 , 17 IL‐12 is essential for the differentiation and activation of T helper (Th)1 cells, while IL‐23 reinforces Th17 cell activation, both of which are key drivers of inflammation in IBD. 16 TL1A, through binding and activation of death receptor 3 (DR3), has been shown to amplify inflammatory and fibrotic signalling in IBD. 17 , 18 , 19 Monospecific inhibition of p40 or TL1A with neutralizing monoclonal antibodies in separate clinical studies has demonstrated clinical efficacy and reduction of inflammatory markers in patients with IBD. This includes ustekinumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting p40, 14 , 15 and the TL1A‐targeting antibodies afimkibart (previously PF‐06480605/RO7790121), tulisokibart and duvakitug. 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 20 , 21 To date, no approved therapies are available that inhibit both p40 and TL1A simultaneously. 22

PF‐07261271 (now RO7837195) is a novel recombinant fully human bispecific immunoglobulin G1 antibody designed to simultaneously target both p40 and TL1A, and is currently being investigated for the treatment of moderate to severe IBD. By combining neutralizing high‐affinity binding domains against both p40 and TL1A within a single antibody molecule, PF‐07261271 aims to maximize suppression of distinct and overlapping inflammatory pathways driving IBD for enhanced efficacy.

This study evaluated the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) of single ascending doses (SAD) and multiple doses (MD) of PF‐07261271 in healthy participants.

METHODS

Study design

This phase 1, randomized, double‐blind, placebo‐controlled study (NCT05536440) was designed to assess the safety, tolerability, PK and PD of PF‐07261271 in healthy adults. The study consisted of two parts. Part A comprised the SAD cohorts that evaluated four single doses of PF‐07261271 (30 mg, 100 mg, 300 mg and 1000 mg) and placebo administered intravenously (IV; Figure 1). An additional dose of 1000 mg PF‐07261271 and placebo was evaluated in participants of Japanese origin (required to have four Japanese biological grandparents born in Japan). Part B comprised the MD cohort and evaluated 300 mg subcutaneous (SC) doses of PF‐07261271 or placebo administered every 4 weeks for a total of two doses. For IV dosing in the SAD cohorts, study intervention was administered as an IV infusion over approximately 60 min, with infusion volumes ranging from 50 mL to 130 mL. SC injections in the MD cohort were administered in the abdomen, thigh or back of the arms, with an injection volume of 3 mL.

Study participants

Participants were healthy adults aged 18–65 years (or the minimum age of consent in accordance with local regulations) at screening, with a body mass index (BMI) of 17.5–30.5 kg/m2 and a total body weight exceeding 50 kg. Exclusion criteria included: history of clinically significant disease and other medical or psychiatric conditions, use of prohibited concomitant medications, recent exposure to live or attenuated vaccines, recent administration with an investigational product, abnormal blood pressure and clinical laboratory abnormalities. Full exclusion criteria are available in the Supplementary Methods.

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, International Council of Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and all applicable laws and regulations, including privacy laws. The protocol, amendments, informed consent forms, investigator brochures and other relevant documents received Institutional Review Board approval from Advarra (Pro00065890) prior to study initiation. At each investigational site, investigators ensured informed consent was obtained prior to enrolment.

Safety and tolerability

Safety assessments included physical examinations, vital signs, 12‐lead electrocardiograms (ECGs) and clinical laboratory tests. The incidence and severity of treatment‐emergent adverse events (TEAEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) were monitored along with infusion/injection site reactions.

Pharmacokinetics

Serum concentrations of PF‐07261271 were assessed via blood samples collected throughout the study period. Sample collection and handling followed procedures detailed in the laboratory manual. Samples were collected at the pre‐specified time points listed below, and collection was stopped once the drug concentration was below the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ). For the SAD cohorts, PK blood samples were collected on day 1 between 2 h and 5 min before the start of infusion, at the end of infusion and at 2, 4, 8 and 12 h after the start of infusion. PK blood samples were also collected on days 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 32, 46, 61, 91, 181, 211, 241, 271, 301, 331, 361 and 451. For the MD cohort, PK blood samples were collected between 2 h and 5 min pre‐administration and 12 h post‐administration on days 1 and 29, as well as on days 3, 5, 8, 15, 31, 36, 51, 66, 81, 111, 171, 201, 231, 261, 291, 321, 351, 381 and 471.

Concentrations of PF‐07261271 were determined by electrochemiluminescent (ECL) ligand‐binding assay using Meso Scale Discovery (MSD) technology. In this method, biotinylated monoclonal antibody (Mu Anti‐[p40 mAb]‐Biotin) was bound to a washed and blocked streptavidin‐coated MSD plate. Calibration standards, quality controls and samples were diluted 1:80 and incubated on the plate. The plate was washed, and PF‐07261271 was detected using ruthenylated monoclonal antibody (Mu Anti‐[TL1A mAb]‐Ru), tripropylamine read buffer and the MSD imager. This method was fully validated according to current industry and regulatory guidelines. Method performance with respect to precision and accuracy, selectivity, specificity, dilutional linearity, hook effect and stability was evaluated. The assay calibration curve range was 5.0 ng/mL to 5200 ng/mL with an LLOQ of 10 ng/mL.

For the SAD cohorts, PK parameters assessed included: area under the serum concentration–time curve (AUC) from time zero to the time of the last quantifiable concentration (AUClast), AUC from time zero extrapolated to infinite time (AUCinf), maximum serum concentration during the dosing interval (Cmax), time to reach Cmax post‐IV infusion or SC administration (Tmax), terminal half‐life (t½), total clearance of drug from serum (CL), volume of distribution for extravascular dosing (Vz) and steady‐state volume of distribution (Vss). For the MD cohort, PK parameters assessed included: AUC at steady state over the dosing interval (AUCtau), Cmax, Tmax, t½ and estimated bioavailability (F), assessed using AUCtau after the first SC dose on day 1 and AUC from time zero to 672 h post‐dose from the SAD cohorts at the same dose level.

Immunogenicity

Immunogenicity assessments comprised the incidence of the development of anti‐drug antibodies (ADA) and neutralizing antibodies (NAb) against PF‐07261271 and were conducted using blood samples collected and analysed using validated methods. Samples were collected at the pre‐specified time points listed below, and collection was stopped once the drug concentration was below the LLOQ. In the SAD cohorts, immunogenicity samples were collected on day 1 between 2 h and 5 min pre‐administration, and on days 8, 15, 32, 46, 61, 91, 181, 211, 241, 271, 301, 331, 361 and 451. In the MD cohort, immunogenicity samples were collected between 2 h and 5 min pre‐administration on days 1 and 29, and on days 8, 15, 51, 66, 81, 111, 171, 201, 231, 261, 291, 321, 351, 381 and 471.

Samples were analysed using a tiered approach consisting of screening, confirmation and determination of titre. The presence of ADA was detected by a quasi‐quantitative bridging ECL immunoassay using MSD technology. Murine monoclonal antibodies were used as positive controls (PC), and pooled normal human serum was used as a negative assay control (NC). A sample pre‐treatment to remove the endogenous target was applied to serum samples, PC and NC. This method was fully validated according to current industry and regulatory guidelines. Method performance with respect to precision, selectivity, specificity, prozone effect, plate homogeneity, stability and robustness was evaluated. This ADA assay was shown to be both sensitive (1.28 ng/mL anti‐p40 and 0.790 ng/mL anti‐TL1A) and drug‐tolerant (100 ng/mL anti‐PF‐07261271 PC antibody was detected in the presence of up to 500 μg/mL, which was the highest PF‐07261271 concentration assessed) based on murine monoclonal antibody PCs and the assay cut point for patients with IBD.

Positive ADA samples were further characterized for neutralizing capability using a qualitative competitive ligand‐binding ECL immunoassay method utilizing MSD Multiplex technology. Sample pre‐treatment using SPEAD (solid‐phase extraction with acid dissociation) was applied to serum samples, PC and NC. This method was fully validated according to current industry and regulatory guidelines. Method performance with respect to precision, selectivity, specificity, prozone effect, plate homogeneity, stability and robustness was evaluated. This NAb assay was shown to be both sensitive (79.1 ng/mL p40 and 236 ng/mL TL1A) and drug‐tolerant (1000 ng/mL of both anti‐p40 and anti‐TL1A PCs were detected in the presence of 300 μg/mL and 150 μg/mL PF‐07261271, respectively) based on murine monoclonal antibody PCs and the assay cut point for combined normal and IBD subjects.

Biomarkers

Blood samples were collected to assess the change from baseline in blood levels of total serum soluble TL1A (sTL1A) and p40 after dosing as exploratory PD biomarkers. Samples were collected at the pre‐specified time points listed below, and collection was stopped once the drug concentration was below the LLOQ. For the SAD cohorts, biomarker blood samples were collected on day 1 between 2 h and 5 min before the start of infusion, at the end of infusion and at 12 h post‐administration. Biomarker blood samples were also collected on days 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 32, 46, 61, 91, 181, 211, 241, 271, 301, 331, 361 and 451. For the MD cohort, biomarker blood samples were collected between 2 h and 5 min pre‐administration and 12 h post‐administration on days 1 and 29, as well as on days 2, 3, 5, 8, 15, 30, 31, 36, 51, 66, 81, 111, 171, 201, 231, 261, 291, 321, 351, 381 and 471.

An immunoaffinity‐liquid chromatography‐high resolution tandem mass spectrometry assay was developed to evaluate the effect of PF‐07261271 on total serum target levels of p40 and TL1A as exploratory biomarkers. The assay was fully validated for its context of use. Assay performance with respect to accuracy, precision, linearity, selectivity, specificity, carryover, matrix effect, dilution integrity and stability was evaluated. The calibration standard responses were linear across the assay range of quantification (LLOQ to upper limit of quantification [ULOQ]) from 37.5 to 18 800 pg/mL for total p40 and 50.0 to 25 000 pg/mL for total TL1A. A weighted quadratic least squares regression was used to fit the curve. Samples above the ULOQ were diluted, and those below the LLOQ were reported as such. No interference by 900 μg/mL of PF‐07261271 was observed for each analyte at quality control‐low and quality control‐high, which established that the assay reliably measured total (drug‐bound and unbound) p40 and TL1A.

Statistical analyses

No formal statistical hypotheses were tested. Analysis sets included: the safety set (all randomized participants who received at least one dose of study treatment, analysed according to the intervention received); the PK analysis set (all randomized participants who received at least one dose of PF‐07261271 with at least one reported serum concentration); the PD analysis set (all randomized participants who received at least one dose of PF‐07261271 with baseline and at least one post‐dose assessment). Safety, biomarker, immunogenicity and serum concentration–time data were summarized descriptively. PK parameters were calculated from actual sample collection times using an internally validated application system open non‐compartmental analysis (openNCA, version 2.7.8).

Nomenclature of targets and ligands

Key protein targets and ligands in this article are hyperlinked to corresponding entries in https://www.guidetopharmacology.org, and are permanently archived in the Concise Guide to PHARMACOLOGY 2023/24. 23

RESULTS

Demographics and disposition

In total, 35 participants were enrolled and randomized in this study (Part A, SAD cohorts, N = 27; Part B, MD cohort, N = 8); a participant schema is shown in Figure 1. All participants in the SAD cohorts received either PF‐07261271 or placebo and completed the study. In the MD cohort, one participant discontinued due to a non‐serious TEAE of ventricular extrasystoles after a single dose of PF‐07261271, which was not considered treatment related by the investigator. Participant demographics and baseline characteristics for each cohort are shown in Table 1. For both the SAD and MD cohorts, respectively, participants were mostly male (74.1% and 62.5%), White (59.3% and 87.5%) and not Hispanic or Latino (81.5% and 87.5%). In the SAD and MD cohorts, respectively, the mean age for participants was 40.5 (standard deviation [SD] 11.1) and 43.4 (SD 14.8) years, and the mean BMI was 25.0 (SD 3.0) and 25.3 (SD 3.1) kg/m2.

Safety and tolerability

In the SAD cohorts, 23 TEAEs were reported in 12/27 (44.4%) participants; six TEAEs were considered treatment related by the investigator (reported in one participant receiving placebo, one participant receiving 300 mg PF‐07261271 and one participant receiving 1000 mg PF‐07261271; Table 2). In the MD cohort, 10 TEAEs were reported in 7/8 (87.5%) participants; five TEAEs were considered treatment related (one participant receiving placebo and four participants receiving PF‐07261271). No SAEs, severe TEAEs or TEAEs leading to death were reported in any cohorts (Table 2). No treatment‐related TEAEs were associated with treatment discontinuation (Table 2).

No clinically significant trends were observed in vital signs, ECGs or clinical laboratory evaluations.

Pharmacokinetics

In the non‐Japanese SAD cohorts, PK evaluations of PF‐07261271 demonstrated a multi‐phasic decline in serum concentrations following Cmax, with a trend towards increased t½ at the higher doses, potentially due to target‐mediated drug disposition observed at the low doses (Figure 2A). AUClast, AUCinf and Cmax increased with ascending doses of PF‐07261271 (Table 3). In particular, AUCinf increased more than dose proportionally from 30 mg to 1000 mg, while Cmax increased more than dose proportionally from 30 mg to 300 mg and less than dose proportionally from 300 mg to 1000 mg. Individual Tmax values ranged between 1 and 13 h at lower doses (30 mg and 100 mg cohorts); in the 300 mg and 1000 mg cohorts, median Tmax ranged from 2 to 3 h (Table 3). Individual t½ values ranged between 158 and 328 h (~7 to 14 days) for the 30 mg and 100 mg cohorts, and arithmetic mean values ranged between 331 and 1533 h (~14 to 64 days) for the 300 mg and 1000 mg cohorts (Table 3). Geometric mean CL values decreased with increasing doses of PF‐07261271; Vss was similar across 30–300 mg cohorts but increased for the 1000 mg cohort (Table 3).

Immunogenicity, pharmacodynamics and biomarkers

All participants in both the SAD and MD cohorts who received at least one dose of PF‐07261271 developed ADAs that persisted to their last available sample (Table 4); median time to the first ADA response ranged from 23.0 to 54.5 days. A total of 83.3% of participants had NAb in both the SAD and MD cohorts; median time to the first NAb response ranged from 52.5 to 178.0 days.

DISCUSSION

In this first‐in‐human study, the safety, tolerability, PK and PD of single and multiple doses of PF‐07261271 compared with placebo in healthy participants were evaluated. Both single and multiple doses of PF‐07261271 were well tolerated. All reported TEAEs were mild or moderate with no SAEs or deaths reported. These findings are consistent with clinical safety data reported for therapies that independently target p40 or TL1A pathways, 10 , 11 , 20 , 21 suggesting a similar safety profile for PF‐07261271 compared with either monotherapy.

PK analysis revealed a multi‐phasic decline in serum PF‐07261271 concentrations following Cmax, with a trend towards increased t½ at higher doses. The estimated bioavailability following SC injection was approximately 48% of matched IV administration, and median Tmax for the 300 mg SC dose was 253 h (~11 days) after the first dose on day 1 and 170 h (~7 days) after the second dose on day 29. The relatively long Tmax for PF‐07261271 is due to slow lymphatic absorption resulting from limited flow rate and is consistent with the Tmax of other approved biologics, with values ranging from 2 to 14 days. 24 Collectively, these PK findings support SC dosing as a viable route of administration. It should be noted that due to <3 participants in the 30 mg and 100 mg cohorts, the relationship between serum PK parameters and dose could not be fully assessed. The serum PK results were similar between Japanese and non‐Japanese participants at the 1000 mg IV dose, suggesting no meaningful ethnic differences in PF‐07261271 PK.

Another key finding of this study was the target engagement of PF‐07261271, demonstrated by dose‐dependent increases in total serum sTL1A and p40 levels, following PF‐07261271 administration compared with placebo. By engaging both TL1A and p40 targets and maximizing therapeutic coverage of both of their signalling pathways, PF‐07261271 aims to provide a dual targeting approach for treating IBD with a single molecule. This approach has the potential to better address the limitations of current IBD monotherapies, drive superior rates of remission and break the observed therapeutic ceiling. 20 , 25 , 26 These PK and target engagement data were helpful in informing the PK/PD model used in phase 2 dose design and support further evaluation of PF‐07261271 in IBD and other inflammatory conditions.

All participants who received at least one dose of PF‐07261271 developed ADAs, and 83.3% developed NAb following single and multiple doses. Despite high immunogenicity, there was no apparent impact of immunogenicity on PK, PD biomarker responses or safety. No participants had a potentially immunogenic response of clinical concern. It should be noted that owing to the small sample size, high variability and 100% ADA incidence, additional data are needed before making definitive conclusions on immunogenicity impact. In the phase 1, 2a and 2b trials of afimkibart, which also targets TL1A, the incidence of ADAs and NAb ranged from 50% to 100% and 8% to 35%, respectively, with no apparent impact of ADA and NAb status on efficacy, PK or PD seen at the clinically relevant doses. 11 , 12 , 27

As is typical of first‐in‐human studies, the limitations of this study include the small participant population assessed and their limited demographic variability. Additionally, only healthy participants were evaluated in this phase 1 study, and, as such, findings may differ in patients with IBD, and inferences to larger or different disease populations are limited.

CONCLUSIONS

In this first‐in‐human phase 1 study of healthy participants, PF‐07261271 was generally safe and well tolerated and demonstrated both p40 and TL1A target engagement, with no apparent impact of immunogenicity on PK, PD biomarker responses or safety. Future clinical studies evaluating the safety and efficacy of PF‐07261271 in IBD and other relevant disease populations are warranted.

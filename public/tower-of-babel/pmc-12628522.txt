Erythrocyte lifespan for predicting moderate and severe anemia in advanced gastric cancer patients post-anti-tumor therapeutics: a prospective study
Authors: Xiurong Yu, Liyu Su, Rong Lu, Shen Zhao, Rongbo Lin, Xianren Ye
Published: 2025

Introduction

Gastric cancer remains a significant global health burden, ranking as the fifth most common cancer and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths [1, 2]. In advanced gastric cancer (AGC), treatment options are limited, and the prognosis is often poor. Chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy are commonly used anti-tumor treatments for AGC, often leading to the occurrence of anemia [3, 4]. Anemia in cancer patients compromises their overall well-being, treatment tolerance, and prognosis, resulting in fatigue, weakness, dyspnea, and reduced functional capacity. Moreover, anemia may also be associated with a poorer response to anti-tumor therapies and a shorter survival time [5–7]. Additionally, Wei et al. [8] and Deng et al. [9] linked hemoglobin levels and anemia to gastric cancer prognosis and progression. Therefore, early identification and prediction of anemia in AGC patients undergoing anti-tumor treatment are of great clinical importance for implementing appropriate preventive and management strategies [10, 11].

Currently, the diagnosis of anemia primarily relies on routine blood tests such as hemoglobin levels. Nevertheless, these approaches are inherently retrospective and offer limited utility in forecasting future risk. Erythrocyte Lifespan (ELS) mirrors the balance between red-cell production and destruction; its shortening causes anemia and is increasingly recognized in cancer patients [12–14] and recently in cirrhotic varices [15]. This is made possible by the simple and rapid CO breath test developed by Levitt’s research team to estimate ELS. The results achieved with this technique are consistent with those from the classical standard chromium-51 (⁵¹Cr) labeling method, while offering the advantages of a non-invasive, bedside-compatible procedure [16, 17]. The test operates on the principle that the majority of endogenous carbon monoxide (CO) in exhaled breath stems from hemoglobin degradation following RBC breakdown, and that the lungs represent the exclusive pathway for CO excretion in humans. However, the relationship between ELS and the occurrence of anemia following anti-tumor treatment in AGC patients has not been fully elucidated.

In light of the above, the current prospective study was designed to investigate whether baseline ELS could predict the development of moderate and severe anemia after anti-tumor treatment in AGC patients. We hypothesized that patients with a shorter baseline ELS would be at a higher risk of developing anemia during and after treatment. By identifying patients at risk, personalized treatment strategies could be implemented to prevent or manage anemia more effectively, potentially improving treatment outcomes and the patients’ quality of life.

Materials and methods

Patients

This single-center, single-arm observational study, approved by our hospital’s Ethics Committee (K2023-67-01) and following the Declaration of Helsinki, got informed consent from all participants. It aimed to observe ELS’s impact on AGC anti-tumor therapy and its predictive value for post - treatment anemia. From Oct 2023 to Oct 2024, 56 AGC patients meeting specific criteria were enrolled. Clinical parameters were collected from medical records, and the participant recruitment flow is shown in Fig. 1. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Histologically confirmed gastric adenocarcinoma (diagnosed as T3-4N1-3M0-1); (2) Administration of anti - cancer drugs 1–2 h after the detection of baseline ELS; (3) Hb should be measured again within 7–21 days after treatment; (4) No active or passive smoking within 24 h before sampling; (5) Age between 18 and 75 years old, with no gender limitations. A total of 56 AGC patients were ultimately included in this study, and relevant data were collected for further analysis.

The flowchart of patient enrollment in this study

ELS analysis

The simple CO breath test estimates ELS based on the principle that exhaled CO comes from heme catabolism. Using the simplified Levitt formula ELS (days) = 1.380×[Hb]/endoPco, ELS can be calculated [18]. Alveolar and background air are collected and measured by an instrument (RBCS-03, ELS-Tester, Seekya Biological Technology Co., LTD, Shenzhen, China) within 24 h to get ELS.

Statistical analysis

All data were comprehensively described, meticulously analyzed, and precisely visualized using GraphPad Prism 9.0.0 software. Clinical parameters and laboratory-derived values were presented as mean ± standard deviation. Correlation coefficients were used to analyze relationships between ELS and various parameters. Paired or unpaired t-tests were applied to analyze differences. The ROC curve was utilized to evaluate the predictive ability of ELS, with P < 0.05 indicating statistical significance.

ELS and hemoglobin in AGC patients

As clearly demonstrated in Fig. 3a, a comprehensive statistical analysis revealed no significant difference in ELS between the group of patients diagnosed with moderate-to-severe anemia (30 g/L ≤ Hb < 90 g/L, WHO criteria) and those without anemia or presenting with mild anemia (Hb ≥ 90 g/L, WHO criteria). Conversely, as clearly illustrated in Fig. 3b, the hemoglobin concentration in patients with an ELS of less than 70 days (RBCS < 70) was markedly lower compared to that of patients with an ELS greater than or equal to 70 days (RBCS ≥ 70). Subsequently, a meticulous comparison was made between the hemoglobin level measured 7–21 days post-medication (Hb7 − 21days) and the pre-medication hemoglobin level (Hb0days). The results indicated that there was no statistically significant variance (Fig. 4a and b). However, a closer inspection revealed an increase in the number of patients experiencing a decline in hemoglobin levels after medication. Notably, the number of patients with 30 g/L ≤ Hb < 90 surged from 9 to 14 (Fig. 4b), which accounted for 25% of the total number of patients in the study. Furthermore, a detailed analysis was conducted to explore the correlations separately between ELS and Hb7 − 21days, as well as between ELS and Hb0days. Intriguingly, the correlation coefficient for Hb7 − 21days (r = 0.4893, P = 0.0001) was determined to be higher than that for Hb0days (r = 0.4418, P = 0.0007), as graphically illustrated in Fig. 4c and d. In light of these findings, our research strongly suggests that ELS holds the potential to predict hemoglobin levels within 7–21 days subsequent to drug administration.

ELS on prediction of moderate and severe anemia

Based on the aforementioned results, among the 47 patients with an initial hemoglobin level (Hb0days) ≥ 90 g/L, we identified 7 patients who developed moderate-to-severe anemia after anti - tumor therapeutics. Moreover, we comprehensively evaluated the predictive ability of ELS by applying the ROC curve. The area under the curve (AUC) for ELS was determined to be 0.8946 (95% confidence interval: 0.78–1.78, P = 0.001, sensitivity: 85.71%, specificity: 80.00%), with an optimal cut-off value of 72.5 days (Fig. 5).

Discussion

In recent years, the bulk of studies on ELS have been centered around hematological diseases, chronic kidney disease, diabetes mellitus, and so forth. These investigations have offered highly valuable perspectives on diagnostic mechanisms, the progression of diseases, and treatment outcomes [19–22]. Nevertheless, this particular study is specifically targeted at patients with AGC. AGC is characterized by its own unique pathophysiological features and tumor microenvironment, which are likely to give rise to differences in the correlations between ELS and various indices. For instance, the occurrence and development of gastric cancer can bring about chronic blood loss, malnutrition, and abnormal immune function.

In our research, all 56 patients underwent anti-tumor pharmacotherapy. Specifically, 27 patients received the novel POFI regimen (irinotecan, paclitaxel, oxaliplatin, and 5-FU/Levo-leucovorin) in combination with tislelizumab - a pioneering therapeutic strategy developed by our research group for advanced gastric cancer. Another 9 patients were treated with the POF regimen (paclitaxel, oxaliplatin, 5-fluorouracil, and leucovorin) [23]. The remaining 20 patients received conventional regimens, including FOLFOX (folinic acid or leucovorin, 5-fluorouracil, and oxaliplatin), or other personalized targeted and immunotherapeutic protocols [24]. We found a moderate positive correlation (correlation coefficient: 0.4418) between ELS and hemoglobin, consistent with previous studies on different patient populations reporting coefficients around 0.8103 [21], 0.535 [25], and 0.256 [26]. Furthermore, a negative correlation was detected between ELS and bilirubin levels, in accordance with the research results of Wang et al. [27]. This indicates that in some specific diseases, the negative correlation between ELS and bilirubin is more conspicuous, demonstrating that fluctuations in ELS can have an impact on bilirubin metabolic pathways, thus leading to an alteration in the equilibrium between hemoglobin-derived bilirubin production and its clearance. Hemoglobin degradation during RBCs senescence is a key factor in determining both ELS and overall hemoglobin stability in circulation. In conclusion, our research findings signify that the relationships between ELS and RBC-related parameters as well as its metabolic markers are extremely close. This relationship accentuates the importance of metabolic processes in determining the longevity of RBCs [28]. Additionally, it has been shown that factors influencing ELS, such as cholesterol content in the cell membrane, can also impact metabolic stability, further highlighting the interplay between lifespan and metabolism [29].

The results of our study suggest that baseline ELS may be a valuable biomarker for predicting moderate and severe anemia in patients with advanced gastric cancer after anti-tumor therapies. It is well known that under normal physiological conditions, the ELS is solely determined by the rate of destruction of RBCs in the circulation and is not affected by bone marrow compensation. A shortened ELS leads to a decrease in hemoglobin concentration, which in turn can cause anemia [14]. Moreover, the shorter the ELS, the higher the risk of developing moderate to severe anemia, the shortening of red blood cell lifespan in patients with cirrhosis worsens as the severity of esophageal varices increases [15]. Highlighting the crucial role of ELS in maintaining normal hemoglobin levels and overall blood health. This finding is consistent with the understanding that ELS reflects the balance between red blood cell production and destruction. In the context of AGC and its treatment, factors such as chemotherapy-induced bone marrow suppression, chronic inflammation, and tumor-related blood loss can all contribute to a shortened ELS [22, 30]. By measuring ELS, we may be able to identify patients at high risk of anemia earlier, allowing for more proactive management strategies. Previous research in other disease settings has also emphasized the importance of ELS in understanding anemia. For example, in chronic kidney disease, a reduction in ELS is a well-established contributor to anemia, and therapies aimed at modulating ELS have been explored [20]. In our study population of AGC patients, the ability to predict anemia based on ELS could potentially lead to personalized interventions. For instance, patients with a short ELS may be candidates for earlier initiation of erythropoiesis-stimulating agents or more aggressive management of iron deficiency, which are common contributing factors to anemia in this patient group [31]. The identification of ELS as a predictor of anemia has several implications for clinical practice. Firstly, it could improve the accuracy of anemia risk assessment in patients with AGC. Currently, anemia is often diagnosed based on routine blood tests such as hemoglobin levels, but these tests are reactive rather than predictive. By incorporating ELS measurements into the pre-treatment and post-treatment assessment, clinicians can anticipate the development of anemia and take preventive measures. Secondly, predicting anemia can help in optimizing anti-tumor treatment regimens. Anemia can lead to dose reductions or treatment interruptions in patients with AGC, which may compromise the effectiveness of therapy. By knowing the risk of anemia in advance, clinicians can adjust the dose or schedule of anti-tumor therapies more carefully, or consider alternative treatment approaches that are less likely to cause severe anemia. This could potentially improve treatment outcomes and patient survival.

Although this study on the relationship between ELS and anemia in patients with AGC has yielded positive results, several limitations should be acknowledged. The relatively limited sample size constrains the generalizability of the findings, and future multicenter collaborations are warranted to validate the association between ELS and anemia. Additionally, this analysis focused exclusively on the univariate relationship between baseline ELS and anemia within 7–21 days post-treatment, without accounting for other potential contributing factors—such as genetic predisposition, nutritional status (e.g., deficiencies in iron, folic acid, or vitamin B12), or comorbid conditions—that may influence the development of anemia. A comprehensive etiological assessment of anemia was not feasible, and the association between baseline ELS and long-term anemia remains unexplored. Future studies should incorporate longer observation periods, validate the findings in larger and more diverse cohorts, integrate ELS with other clinically relevant variables, investigate the correlation between ELS and the prognosis of AGC patients, and explore the underlying mechanisms to improve the predictive accuracy for anemia and enhance the understanding of its etiology.

By establishing, for the first time, that a single, non-invasive Levitt’s CO breath-test can quantify baseline ELS and reliably foretell moderate-to-severe anemia within three weeks of systemic therapy, we move anemia care in AGC from a reactive, hemoglobin-based paradigm to a proactive, risk-stratified model. The 72.5-day threshold provides clinicians with a concrete, biologically grounded trigger to initiate early anemia-preventive measures, offering an intervention window that may preserve chemotherapy dose intensity and ultimately improve survival. Our findings enrich the expanding body of knowledge on the intricate interplay among cancer treatment, erythrocyte kinetics, and anemia development.

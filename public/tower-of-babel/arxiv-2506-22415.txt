Linear operators preserving volume polynomials
Lukas Grund, June Huh, Mateusz Michałek, Hendrik Süss, Botong Wang

Abstract
. Volume polynomials measure the growth of Minkowski sums of convex bodies and of tensor powers of positive line bundles on projective varieties. We show that Aluffi’s covolume polynomials are precisely the polynomial differential operators that preserve volume polynomials, reflecting a duality between homology and cohomology. We then present several applications to matroid theory.



1.
Introduction

In
[
32
]
, Minkowski made a foundational observation that became a cornerstone of convex geometry: For any collection of convex bodies
C=(C_{1},\ldots,C_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the function

f_{C}:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\longmapsto\frac{1}{d!}\vol(x_{1}C_{1}+\dots+x_{n}C_{n})

is a degree
d
homogeneous polynomial in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
This polynomial, called the
volume polynomial
of

, is then used to define the
mixed volume
of convex bodies as its normalized coefficients

\textrm{MV}(C_{i_{1}},\ldots,C_{i_{d}})\coloneq\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{d}}}f_{C}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

The mixed volumes satisfy the
Alexandrov–Fenchel inequalities
, of which the classical isoperimetric inequality is a special case. For a comprehensive introduction to the Brunn–Minkowski theory, see
[
40
]
.

The analogous volume polynomial in algebraic geometry is defined as follows: Let
D=(D_{1},\ldots,D_{n})
be a collection of semiample divisors on a
d

over an algebraically closed field

.

\mathbb{R}[\partial]_{\leq\mu}\coloneq\textrm{span}(\partial^{\alpha})_{\alpha\leq\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{R}[x]_{\leq\mu}\coloneq\textrm{span}(x^{\alpha})_{\alpha\leq\mu}.

\mathbb{R}[\partial]_{\leq\mu}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}[x]_{\leq\mu},\qquad\partial^{\alpha}\longmapsto\partial^{\alpha}\circ x^{[\mu]}=x^{[\mu-\alpha]}

Remark 1.7

Let
f=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}x^{[\alpha]}
be a nonzero degree

k

\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

We use the following construction throughout the paper and refer to it as the

By definition, there is a
d

k

Choose a positive integer

is a basepoint-free divisor for every

The collection

The Chow group of

\mathbb{P}^{\mu_{i}}
.
Let
X
be the image of

in
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
.
By the projection formula, there is a positive rational number
\lambda

\lambda[X]=\sum_{\alpha\leq\mu}c_{\alpha}[\mathbb{P}^{\alpha}]=\sum_{\alpha\leq\mu}c_{\alpha}h^{\mu-\alpha}\cap[\mathbb{P}^{\mu}],

where

is determined by the number

.
The volume polynomial of

We make the following observations:

(1)

For

The cycles

(2)

For

The goal is to prove Theorem

\partial^{\alpha}\delta^{\beta}\circ x^{[\zeta]}y^{[\eta]}=\begin{cases}x^{[\zeta-\alpha]}y^{[\eta-\beta]}&\text{if $\alpha\leq\zeta$ and $\beta\leq\eta$,}\\
\hfill 0\hfill&\text{if otherwise.}\end{cases}

We define the coefficients

by

\text{sym}_{T}=\sum_{\alpha\leq\mu}T(x^{[\alpha]})x^{[\mu-\alpha]}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c^{T}_{\alpha,\beta}\,x^{[\mu-\alpha]}y^{[\beta]}.

The

of

is the homogeneous polynomial in the dual variables

Lemma 3.1

For any homogeneous linear operator

A polynomial

We now exploit multiplicative properties of realizable volume polynomials and realizable covolume polynomials to prove a result in matroid theory.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be matroids on a common ground set
E
.

(1)

The
union

M_{1}\vee M_{2}
is the matroid on
E
whose independent sets are the subsets of
E
that can be expressed as a union
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
, where
I_{1}
is an independent set of
M_{1}
and
I_{2}
is an independent set of
M_{2}
.

(2)

The
intersection

M_{1}\wedge M_{2}
is the matroid on
E
whose spanning sets are the subsets of
E
that can be expressed as an intersection
S_{1}\cap S_{2}
, where
S_{1}
is a spanning set of
M_{1}
and
S_{2}
is a spanning set of
M_{2}
.

The two constructions are related by the matroid duality by
M_{1}\wedge M_{2}=(M_{1}^{*}\vee M_{2}^{*})^{*}
.

We say that
M_{1}\vee M_{2}
has the
expected rank
if the set of bases of
M_{1}\vee M_{2}
is

\big\{B=B_{1}\cup B_{2}\mid B_{1}\in J_{1},B_{2}\in J_{2},\text{ and }B_{1}\cap B_{2}=\varnothing\big\},

where
J_{1}
is the set of bases of
M_{1}
and
J_{2}
is the set of bases of
M_{2}
. Dually, we say that

M_{1}\wedge M_{2}
has the expected rank if the set of bases of
M_{1}\wedge M_{2}
is

\big\{B=B_{1}\cap B_{2}\mid B_{1}\in J_{1},B_{2}\in J_{2},\text{ and }B_{1}\cup B_{2}=E\big\}.

Welsh’s theorem states that the union
M_{1}\vee M_{2}
is algebraic over
k

M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are algebraic over
k

Theorem 5.11
.

The intersection
M_{1}\wedge M_{2}
is algebraic over
k

when both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are algebraic over
k
.

The result is consistent with a positive answer to one of the oldest questions in the subject: Is the dual of an algebraic matroid also algebraic?

Proof.

Let
J_{i}
be the set of bases of
M_{i}
and let

be the set of bases of

By Proposition
5.4

and

over

is
J_{1}
and the support of
f_{2}

.
Thus, there are realizable covolume polynomials
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
over
k
such that the support of
g_{1}
is
K_{1}
and the support of
g_{2}

By Theorem

is a realizable covolume polynomial over

is a realizable covolume polynomial over

If

has the expected rank, the set of bases of

is the support of the realizable volume polynomial

5.4

.

When

We only need to check that the Higgs lift of a matroid algebraic over

5.9

∎

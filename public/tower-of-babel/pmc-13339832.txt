Fusion attention-based nasopharyngeal carcinoma segmentation model in predicting the clinical outcome of cervical lymph node residue after IMRT
Authors: Yujie Liu, Jingyi Xie, Jinyan Li, Hongbo Chen, Siying Dong, Shuquan Zhou, Shufen Liang, Chuanbo Qin, Lin Xiao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDeep learning methods have made great progress in the automatic segmentation of nasopharyngeal carcinoma, but challenges remain.PurposeComputer-aided automatic segmentation of nasopharyngeal cancer primary area is of great significance for automatic outlining of nasopharyngeal cancer target areas and accurate prediction of responsiveness and prognosis of metastatic lymph nodes in the neck after radiotherapy. In this paper, we use deep learning methods to construct an automatic segmentation network for gross target volume of nasopharynx, combine clinical factors and radiomics features to establish a radiomics nomogram model, which will then predict the final outcome of metastatic lymph nodes that have not achieved complete remission after radical radiotherapy.MethodsClinical and IMRT radiotherapy plan CT data were retrospectively collected from 69 patients who received intensity-modulated radiation therapy between July 2014 and December 2016. These patients exhibited residual metastatic lymph node lesions without residual primary lesions on the first follow-up MRI and had continuous follow-up records. The median follow-up was 53 months (IQR 39.75–62.37), with 30 patients eventually regressing and 39 patients persisting or progressing. The ct images of 69 radiotherapy plans were randomly divided into training and test sets according to 8:2, and a fusion attention-based model was trained for automatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma segmentation. Based on the unet framework, a fusion attention model was proposed, and a 2·5 d convolutional neural network was used to deal with the anisotropy. An improved channel and spatial attention module is fused in the codec 4 layer to enable the network to focus on small targets. 2d interlaced sparse self-attention module is extended to 3d to better extract the feature information of the tumor target area and solve the problem of low contrast between the target area and the surrounding soft tissues, thus optimizing the overall segmentation effect. The performance of the segmentation model was evaluated using the mean dice coefficient, relative volume error (RVE), average symmetric surface distance (ASSD) and hausdorff distance (HD), using the target area of the primary lesion of nasopharyngeal carcinoma manually outlined by a senior radiation therapy specialist as the gold standard. Radiomics features were extracted using the pyradiomics package, and the classification performance of the radiomics model was assessed by the area under the curve of the receiver operating curve (ROC).ResultsThe average dice coefficient, RVE, ASSD and HD of our model for nasopharyngeal carcinoma were 75.05%, 14.63%, 2.224 mm, and 8.75 mm, respectively, which were 11.01%, 26.34%, 3.101 mm, and 52.58 mm better than the baseline 3dunet model. The radiomic features were an effective predictor of tumor outcome in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, with the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.892 for the radiomic nomogram in the training set and 0.825 for the radiomic model in the test set.ConclusionsThe fused attention-based segmentation network for nasopharyngeal carcinoma can effectively and reliably segment the region of the primary nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and the radiomic nomogram can effectively predict the response after treatment.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignant tumor that occurs in the nasopharyngeal mucosa and is most prevalent in southern China, Southeast Asia and North Africa [1–3]. Intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) is emphasized as the main treatment for nasopharyngeal carcinoma [4]. On the other hand, the overall tumor residual rate after radical radiotherapy was about 25.4%-59.6%, the primary site residual rate was 36.1%, and the lymph node residual rate was 23.7%-44.2% [5]. Some of the residual lesions will gradually fade or remain stable over time, while some of them become the source of recurrence and metastasis. Therefore, accurate prediction of the final outcome of residual lesions, especially residual lymph nodes, can help to develop individualized treatment plans, reduce the physical, psychological and economic stress of patients, and save medical resources. Therefore, the Gross Target Volume of nasopharynx (GTVnx) segmentation is not only an important step in predicting the final outcome of metastatic lymph nodes, but also an important step in computer-aided diagnostic systems. A reliable method for automatically segmenting GTVnx and predicting metastatic lymph node results is essential.

Radiomics, as a new method to reflect tumor heterogeneity, can non-invasively predict tumor treatment response, and has been widely used in disease diagnosis, differential diagnosis, clinical staging and treatment evaluation [6–8]. Zheng et al. proposed a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based radiomics nomogram to differentiate between Worthing’s tumor and parotid pleomorphic adenoma with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.918 in the test set. The results showed that this radiomics nomogram combining age and radiomic features had good preoperative predictive performance [9]. A nomogram is a graphical predictive tool constructed from a multifactorial regression model, such as logistic or Cox proportional hazards regression. It visually represents the contribution of each predictor variable to the outcome by converting their respective regression coefficients into scaled, parallel line segments. This transforms a complex mathematical model into an accessible clinical assessment instrument, enabling rapid, individualized quantification of disease risk or prognostic probability to support clinical decision-making. Wang et al. explored the ability of MRI-based imaging features to predict early response to induction chemotherapy in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma before treatment, and the AUC result of the model constructed based on radiological features of multimodal images was 0.821, indicating that radiomics features are an important predictor of induction chemotherapy (IC) response in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma [10]. Previous studies have shown that computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging based imaging histology combined with clinical features can effectively predict the response of nasopharyngeal carcinoma tumors [11]. Radiomics modeling analysis requires accurate segmentation of GTVnx from images. At present, this task is usually performed by experienced radiologists through manual labeling on a slice-by-slice basis, which is a cumbersome, time-consuming, and labor-intensive method.

In recent years, deep learning methods have been increasingly used in medical image analysis. Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN) [12]、UNet [13, 14]、VNet [15] and DeepMedic [16] etc. are learning models commonly used for segmentation tasks. Based on the fact that humans can naturally and efficiently find synaptic regions in complex scenes, attentional mechanisms have been introduced into computer vision with great success in a variety of different vision tasks [17]. Attention mechanisms have provided benefits in many visual tasks, e.g. image classification [18, 19], object detection [20, 21]and semantic segmentation [22, 23]. The previous standard Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for medical image processing obtains large enough perceptual field and contextual semantic information by continuously stacking convolutional and pooling layers, but it is prone to false-positive (FP) prediction problem for small target areas with large deformation. In order to solve this problem, Oktay et al. proposed a simple but effective mechanism, Attention Gate (AG), which focuses attention on the target region while suppressing features in irrelevant regions [24]. The Squeeze-and-Excitation Network (SENet) pioneered channel attention, with a Squeeze and Excitation block at its core to collect global information, capture channel relationships, and improve representation. However, in the squeezing module, extracting channel features by global average pooling only is too simple to capture complex global information. In the excitation module, the strategy of reducing the model complexity by channel dimensionality reduction cannot directly model the correspondence between the weight vectors and the inputs, which reduces the quality of the results. Therefore, Wang et al. proposed Efficient Channel Attention for deep convolutional neural networks, the method uses a one-dimensional convolution without dimensionality reduction to determine the interaction between channels. Woo et al. proposed Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), extracting feature information according to the channel attention operation and then spatial attention operation. Park et al. proposed an improved version of CBAM, where the module computes the corresponding attentional map through two separate attentional branches, the channel attentional operation and the spatial attentional operation [25]. Wang et al. proposed Non-local, leverage global dependency information for better segmentation [26]. Fu et al. proposed Dual attention Network (DANet), combining information about local features with global dependencies [27]. To address the issues of importance of location information and modeling global dependencies, Hou et al. proposed Coordinate Attention (CA), which embeds location information into channel attention, thus enabling the network to focus on larger important regions at a smaller computational cost [28]. Also, there are various forms of attention mechanisms applied to semantic segmentation [29–32], which improves the efficiency and accuracy of the diagnosis, which can avoid miss diagnosis and misdiagnosis to the maximum extent.

Achieving accurate automated segmentation of nasopharyngeal carcinoma primary lesions presents significant challenges. Firstly, the contrast between the primary focus of nasopharyngeal carcinoma and the surrounding soft tissues in CT images is low and not easy to identify. Secondly, its boundary is blurred and it is also difficult for radiologists to outline the target area accurately, which may appear to have noisy labeling. Thirdly, the primary lesion of nasopharyngeal carcinoma accounts for a very small percentage of CT images, which presents an extreme imbalance between the background and segmented targets. In addition, the problem of CT image anisotropy can cause large changes in the shape of the target area in adjacent slices. Our specific work content is as follows: (1) We designed a novel 2.5D-based [33] convolutional neural network (CNN) [34] that combines channel, space and Self Attentionwith an integrated attention mechanism to better extract feature information from small target regions, and further improve the overall similarity of segmentation and edge segmentation.(2) We proposed a combined spatial and channel attention module at the encoder and decoder to improve the segmentation accuracy. (3) We designed an extended 3DInterlaced Sparse Self-Attention module at the bottleneck to obtain global information, and an attention gating mechanism is introduced at the decoding end to optimize the edge segmentation effect. (4) On our nasopharyngeal carcinoma dataset, the segmentation performance of our network was evaluated through comprehensive comparative experiments against several leading segmentation networks and different attention modules. Additionally, ablation studies were conducted to validate the contribution of each proposed module. (5) We proposed a new method to predict tumor response in nasopharyngeal carcinoma and evaluated the validity of the model.

Datasets

Patient clinical information

Retrospectively collected 69 nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients treated by IMRT from July 2014 to December 2016 at Jiangmen Central Hospital. The inclusion criteria were as follows: ① all patients had clear nodal masses on nasopharyngeal electron microscopy and pathologically confirmed cancer; ② all patients received comprehensive IMRT radiotherapy; ③ there should be nasopharyngeal at first diagnosis, before IMRT treatment and within 6 months after the end of IMRT and neck MRI (plain + enhanced) at the time of first diagnosis, before IMRT treatment and within 6 months after the end of IMRT, and the number of MRI follow-ups after radiotherapy was ≥ 2; ④ residual lymph node lesions were found at the first MRI follow-up after the end of IMRT, and no residual primary lesions. A total of 69 patients met the inclusion criteria. Until April 2022, the median follow-up time was 53 months (IQR 39.75 ~ 62.37), 30 patients had residual lymph node regression and 39 patients had residual lymph node persistence or progression.

Meanwhile, residual lymph nodes are defined as: at the first MRI scan 3 months post-radiotherapy, the short-axis diameter of cervical lymph nodes remains > 10 mm, or posterior pharyngeal lymph nodes remain > 5 mm, or there are still necrosis, fusion, or extracapsular invasion. Residual progression is defined as: During follow-up, residual lesions meet RECIST1.1 PD criteria in two consecutive MRI scans, i.e., the total diameter of all measurable target lesions increases by at least 20% and the absolute diameter sum increases by at least 5 mm, or one or more new lesions appear. Residual regression is defined as: ① Complete disappearance of residual metastatic lymph node lesions during follow-up. Complete disappearance of cervical lymph nodes requires all the following conditions to be met simultaneously: maximum transverse diameter < 00.5 cm; ② No necrosis, fusion, or extracapsular invasion; ③ No intact lymph node contour, no enhancement or uniform enhancement; ④ No residual or new lesions at the time of final follow-up.

The manual delineation of the Gross Target Volume of nasopharynx for all cases was performed on the open-source platform 3D Slicer, in strict accordance with the international guideline for nasopharyngeal carcinoma (Radiother Oncol. 2017). The specific procedure is as follows: Based on planning contrast-enhanced CT axial images, two radiation oncologists independently performed manual delineation of the tumor boundaries, using the region between the distinctly enhanced nasopharyngeal mass and the non-enhanced muscle tissue in the images as the segmentation criterion. If disagreement between two radiologists, a senior radiologist to establish a final consensus contour, which served as the ground truth for model training and evaluation.

CT information and pre-processing

The nasopharyngeal carcinoma dataset is entirely CT unimodal images with an in-plane resolution of approximately 1 mm × 1 mm and an in-plane image size of 512 × 512. The distance between planar slices is approximately 3 mm, and the number of CT image layers scanned varies per patient, ranging from 103 to 152 slices for different cases. The gold standard for both the training and test sets was outlined manually by experienced senior oncology radiologists.

In the data preprocessing stage, we first truncate the intensity values of all images to the range of [−200,700] HU values to increase the contrast of the target area, and then map the intensity values of the images to the range of [0,1] through normalization processing to remove the bad effects caused by singular sample data, while accelerating the training rate of convergence. To maintain the same resolution, the pixel spacing of all images in the x, y, and z directions is uniformly interpolated to 1 × 1 × 3 mm. The size of the image in the ×and y directions is 512 × 512, which includes a large number of background and equipment areas, which to some extent affects the effectiveness of the model for small object segmentation. Therefore, we resampled the CT sequence images of the primary nasopharyngeal lesion for each patient.

The experiments randomized the nasopharyngeal cancer clinical database into training and test sets by 8:2. Data augmentation methods were used with random cropping and random flipping. We constructed a deep learning model based on the labels of nasopharyngeal carcinoma primary regions outlined by clinicians to achieve accurate segmentation of nasopharyngeal cancer primary area.

Methods

We developed an automatic segmentation model for the Gross Target Volume of nasopharynx and, by integrating radiomic features, constructed a radiomics nomogram to predict the final outcome of residual metastatic lymph nodes in the neck at the first follow-up after treatment, as shown in Fig. 2. Firstly, experienced and senior oncology radiologists outlined the region of interest on the CT images of the primary area of nasopharyngeal cancer. In the second step, the data were randomly divided into a training set and a test set, and the data from the training set are used to construct a segmentation model based on deep learning to automatically segment the primary area of nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and then feature extraction was performed on the labeled training set data and the predicted test set data. Then the extracted features and the corresponding clinical information of the patients are preprocessed and a screening operation is performed. The fourth step is to build a multiple linear regression model for the screened features and evaluate the model from different perspectives.Fig. 2Deep learning combined with radiomics workflow

FA UNet semantic segmentation network

Our network is based on the UNet architecture, the encoder consists of four hierarchical feature extraction modules, as shown in Fig. 3(a). The first layer is composed of two consecutive 1 × 3 × 3 convolutional layers, each followed by a Batch Normalization (BN) layer and Leaky ReLU (Leaky Rectification Unit). From the second to the fourth layer, a Fused Attention (FA) module is incorporated prior to the convolutional operations to enhance the model’s ability to capture the location and spatial information of the primary nasopharyngeal carcinoma of small targets. At the bottleneck layer, the original 1 × 3 × 3 convolution is replaced with a 3 × 3 × 3 convolution to expand the receptive field, and a 3D interleaved sparse self-attention mechanism is introduced to further strengthen the modeling of global structural characteristics within the tumor region. The decoder layers also integrate the FA module and adhere to the same “convolution–batch normalization–Leaky ReLU” unit structure as described. Our network adopted a 2.5D convolution strategy, utilizing 1 × 3 × 3 kernels in the encoder-decoder to extract 2D spatial features and 3 × 3 × 3 kernels at the bottleneck to fuse 3D context, achieving a good balance between efficiency and representational, 2.5D block and 3D block as shown in Fig. 3(b). Additionally, an Attention Gate (AG) mechanism is embedded in the skip connections to adaptively fuse shallow features from the encoder with high-level semantic information from the decoder, thereby improving boundary localization accuracy and overall segmentation performance.Fig. 3FA unet overall network architecture (a) 2.5D block and 3D Block (b)

Channel attention module

As shown in Fig. 5, we decompose the channel attention into three parallel one-dimensional (1D) features and integrate the spatial coordinate information into the feature vector of channel attention through the encoding process in order to solve the loss of location information caused by global pooling. These three attention maps are then applied to the input feature maps by element-by-element multiplication to enhance the representation of the region of interest. Extended 3D interlaced sparse self-attention module

The recent self-attention (or non-local) mechanism proposes to model long-range dependencies by computing contextual information for each output location and focusing on all input locations. Given an input of size N, the computational complexity of the self-attention mechanism is about O(N2), which can be very costly for tasks with high-resolution inputs. In various basic vision tasks, high-resolution inputs are crucial for improving performance, but it requires a large computational overhead, which hinders the potential benefits of the self-attention mechanism in practical applications. As shown in Fig. 7, to reduce the computational cost of the self-attentive mechanism, the dense association matrix is factorized into the product of two sparse association matrices. Two consecutive attention modules are used, the first attention module for estimating the similarity within a subset of locations with long spatial distances and the second attention module for estimating the similarity within a subset of locations with short spatial distances. By combining the long and short distance modules, we are able to pass all the input location information to each output location. Compared with the original self-attentive module, the computational complexity is greatly reduced, especially when dealing with high-resolution feature maps.Fig. 7Overview of the 3D Interlaced sparse self-attention module

Overview of the 3D Interlaced sparse self-attention module

Specifically, first, the input is divided into Q subsets of equal size, each containing P positions, i.e., N = P × Q, N being the size of the input; for Long-range Attention, a point is sampled from each subset to construct a new subset with Q positions, and P such subsets can be obtained according to this sampling strategy. The positions within each constructed subset have a long spatial distance, and self-attention is applied to each subset to compute the sparse correlation matrix AL. For Short-range Attention, directly applies self-attention to the original Q subsets to compute the sparse correlation matrix AS. combining these two attention mechanisms, information can be propagated from each input position to all output positions.

Radiomics feature extraction and selection

After segmentation, all CT images’ intensity values were discretized using a fixed bin width of 25 Hounsfield Units (HU). Radiomics features were then extracted from these processed images using the PyRadiomics library (version 3.0.1). A total of 1043 features were extracted and categorized into four groups: (1) Shape features (n = 14), describing three-dimensional geometry; (2) First-order statistical features (n = 18), derived from the intensity histogram; (3) Texture features (n = 75), quantifying intra-tumoral heterogeneity, including features from Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), Gray Level Run Length Matrix (GLRLM), Gray Level Size Zone Matrix (GLSZM), Neighboring Gray Tone Difference Matrix (NGTDM), and Gray Level Dependence Matrix (GLDM); (4) Filtered-image features (n = 936), obtained by applying Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) filters (sigma values: 3.0 and 5.0 mm) and wavelet transforms to the original image, followed by feature extraction from the filtered volumes.

To facilitate the comparison and weighting of features with different units or magnitudes, the data were normalized using the Z-score method. Three-step feature selection methods were used to minimize overfitting and avoid the interference of irrelevant factors, simplify the prediction model and optimize the model performance. First, the variance method was used to remove the features with small dispersion, then the t-test was used to remove the features with small differences from the nasopharyngeal carcinoma treatment outcome variables, and finally the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (Lasso) regression algorithm was used to select the features by ten-fold cross-validation. The optimal reconciliation parameter lambda, which reduces the coefficients of irrelevant features to zero, retains features with non-zero coefficients, and generates the optimal subset by removing variables that are highly linearly correlated with other variables after multivariate analysis, and generates a radiomics score (Rad-score) for each patient using a weighted linear combination of these features and their non-zero coefficients.

Statistical analysis

Radiomic features were extracted using the pyradiomics library (version 3.0.1) in Python (version 3.7). R statistical software (v.3.3.3; https://www.r-project.org) were used for statistical analysis.

Appropriate statistical analysis were selected based on variable types and distribution characteristics. Normally distributed continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the independent samples ttest (for two groups) or oneway ANOVA (for more than two groups). Nonnormally distributed continuous variables were expressed as median (interquartile range) and compared using the MannWhitney U test (for two groups) or the KruskalWallis H test (for multiple groups). Categorical variables were summarized as frequency (percentage) and analyzed using the chisquare test; when any theory frequency was less than 5, Fisher’s exact test was applied instead. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The R packages used were: (1) the “pROC” package for the Roc curves, (2) the “rms” package for the nomograms and calibration curves, (3) the “glmnet” package for LASSO logistic regression, and (4) the “rmda” package to perform the DCA. All the packages can be downloaded at https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/.

To integrate clinical variables into a predictive model for lymph node residue outcome, a multiple logistic regression analysis was performed. Candidate variables (lymph node number and age) were included based on univariate screening results and established clinical relevance. The model was fitted using the backward stepwise selection method based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC).

Radiomics model building

A total of 1043 radiomics features were extracted from the primary area of nasopharyngeal carcinoma. To screen the most discriminative features, all features were statistically analyzed by Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) and Independent Samples t-test (T-test), with a significance level of p < 0.05. After this screening, 57 features were retained for subsequent model construction. T-test is a method for comparing the mean of two groups of continuous variables, while ANOVA is used to compare the mean difference of three or more independent groups. To identify the most predictive core variables from 57 features while preventing overfitting, this study employed LASSO regression for feature selection. The key mechanism involves incorporating an L1 norm penalty term into the loss function, where the regularization parameter λ controls model sparsity: a higher λ value eliminates more features.

The optimal λ was determined via ten-fold cross-validation based on the minimal mean squared error. The variation of the optimal adjustment weights and the variation of the corresponding number of features of the Lasso algorithm are shown in Fig. 12(a) and (b), respectively. Radiomics nomogram building

This study identified nine key radiomics features through LASSO regression and developed a Radiomics score (Rad-score). Rad-score is a composite variable that integrates multiple high-dimensional imaging features into a single quantitative metric, designed to more robustly characterize internal heterogeneity, biological behavior, and potential prognostic information within lesions. The scoring formula is calculated as a linear combination of standardized feature values and their corresponding regression coefficients plus an intercept term, specifically: Rad-score = 0.4227 + (A × 0.4774) + (B × 0.2641) + (C × −0.9644) + (D × 0.3118) + (E × −0.9692) + (F × 0.3657) + (G × 0.9565) + (H × −0.6744) + (I × 0.3638).

Subsequently, Rad-score and clinical variables (lymph node count, age) were combined as independent variables in a multiple logistic regression model to predict lymph node metastasis outcomes. The model was visualized using a nomogram, namely the final Radiomics nomogram model, as shown in Fig. 13(a). Conclusions

In this study, the results show that a deep learning algorithm based on fused attention can automatically segment the target area of the nasopharyngeal carcinoma primary focus, and the proposed method effectively solves the problems of small nasopharyngeal target area, anisotropy of CT images, and low contrast between the target area and surrounding soft tissues in automatic segmentation as demonstrated by ablation experiments and comparison experiments, and the radiomics nomogram shows good performance in differentiating patients’ response after radiotherapy.

We first construct a specialized segmentation model for the segmentation of nasopharyngeal carcinoma primary region, and then use the predicted images as a test set to verify the effectiveness of predicting the outcome of nasopharyngeal carcinoma patients after radiotherapy, realizing a consistent application of artificial intelligence techniques for diagnosing the disease. The segmentation model achieved a Dice coefficient of up to 75.05% in the test set, outperforming the current state-of-the-art segmentation models. In the model used for classification prediction, radiomic features proved to be important predictors, in agreement with previous studies. AUC up to 0.825 was achieved on the test dataset, achieving good predictive power. The effectiveness of the segmentation model directly affects the histological features to predict the metastatic lymph node response of nasopharyngeal carcinoma after radiotherapy, so increasing the amount of data used to train the model is critical to this study. Some limitations of this study should be noted. First, the performance of the histological prediction model was not compared with the radiologist’s diagnosis. A comparison of the results between the two will be considered in the subsequent experiments. A second limitation is that we only segmented the primary region of nasopharyngeal carcinoma and extracted imaging histological features for classification and prediction, and did not investigate other regions of nasopharyngeal carcinoma, which will be added to multiple regions of nasopharyngeal carcinoma in the follow-up study.

In conclusion, we propose a novel segmentation network based on a 2.5D UNet architecture. This design effectively addresses the limitations of standard 2D UNets, which fail to capture sufficient inter-slice contextual information, while mitigating the high computational and GPU memory demands associated with full 3D UNets. The proposed fused attention module focuses more on the target area of small targets in nasopharyngeal carcinoma, and the results compared with different attention modules are shown in Table 2. The highest improvement in all metrics is achieved by adding FA attention module based on the 2.5D UNet framework. Adding the extended 3D Interlaced Sparse Self-Attention module at the bottleneck can establish the relationship from the global and further optimize the segmentation effect. The introduction of AG at the decoder side makes the network more focused on the target region, suppresses irrelevant regions, and optimizes the edge segmentation effect. It can be seen from Table 3 that the segmentation index is also gradually improved by adding the attention module gradually. Subsequently, we constructed and validated a radiomics nomogram combining clinical information and radiomics features to predict metastatic lymph node outcome in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma, which can be used as an individualized preoperative noninvasive tool to predict tumor response after IMRT and helps to develop the best individualized treatment plan to optimize the clinical diagnostic workup.

Gradient regularity for ( s , p ) (s,p) -harmonic functions
Verena Bögelein, Frank Duzaar, Naian Liao, Giovanni Molica Bisci, Raffaella Servadei

Abstract
. We study the local regularity properties of ( s , p ) (s,p) -harmonic functions, i.e. local weak solutions to the fractional p p -Laplace equation of order s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) in the case p ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] p\in(1,2] . It is shown that ( s , p ) (s,p) -harmonic functions are weakly differentiable and that the weak gradient is locally integrable to any power q ≥ 1 q\geq 1 . As a result, ( s , p ) (s,p) -harmonic functions are Hölder continuous to arbitrary Hölder exponent in ( 0 , 1 ) (0,1) . In addition, the weak gradient of ( s , p ) (s,p) -harmonic functions has certain fractional differentiability. All estimates are stable when s s reaches 1 1 , and the known regularity properties of p p -harmonic functions are formally recovered, in particular the local W 2 , 2 W^{2,2} -estimate.

Introduction

In this manuscript we complete our program on the higher Sobolev regularity of solutions to the fractional
p
-Laplace equation with order
s
:

(1.1)

(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=0\quad\text{in}\>\>\Omega.

Here, we denoted a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
.
Local solutions to (
1.1
) are termed
(s,p)
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
; see Definition
2.1
.
In our previous work
[
10
]
, we considered the case
p\in[2,\infty)
, whereas in this manuscript we focus on the case
p\in(1,2]
.

The
main result
states that when
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
, the gradient of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
exists and belongs to
L^{q}_{\loc}(\Omega)
for any
q\in[p,\infty)
. As a result,
(s,p)
-harmonic functions are locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega
with an arbitrary Hölder exponent
\gamma\in(0,1)
. In addition, we establish the fractional differentiability of the gradient in any
L^{q}
-scale, namely,
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\loc}(\Omega)
for any
q\in[2,\infty)
and any
\alpha\in(0,\max\{\frac{sp}{q},\frac{1-(1-s)p}{q-1}\})
. In particular, letting
q=2
and
s\uparrow 1
, we formally recover the well-known
W^{2,2}
-regularity of
p
-harmonic functions, cf.
[
54
,
3
]
. All claimed regularity properties are confirmed by explicit local estimates that are stable as
s\uparrow 1
.

A first decisive result on the gradient regularity for
(s,p)
-harmonic functions belongs to Brasco & Lindgren
[
12
]
. They established
\nabla u\in L^{p}_{\loc}(\Omega)
in the case
p\in[2,\infty)
and
s\in(\frac{p-1}{p},1)
for globally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic functions. Recently, we have substantially improved this result in
[
10
]
. As a matter of fact, the same gradient regularity holds for a wider range of
s
, namely,
s\in(\frac{p-2}{p},1)
, and furthermore, global boundedness can also be dispensed with. More importantly, what really sets our contribution apart from others is that the integrability exponent of
\nabla u
has been upgraded to any finite number
q
.

In this work we make an important step forward and extend the gradient regularity for
(s,p)
-harmonic functions to the whole range
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Therefore, summarizing the achievement in
[
10
]
and in this work, we have obtained a rather complete picture regarding the higher Sobolev regularity for
(s,p)
-harmonic functions, see the ranges of
s
and
p
shown by the blue part in Figure

. In addition, for the same ranges of
s
and
p
, we have concluded the “almost” Lipschitz regularity. Prior to our works, such regularity was only known for
s\in(\frac{p-1}{p},1)
; see
[
13
]
for

and
[

]
for

.

For simplicity, we choose to present and prove all our results for locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic functions. However, once this is done, we can employ proper freezing-and-comparison arguments, consider general inhomogeneous terms on the right-hand side of (

), and also inserting appropriate coefficients to the equation. The estimates could also be modified in such a way that the

(s,p)

\nabla u

As in our previous work, we explicitly trace the dependence of the constants in estimates. We believe this is important and necessary in order to justify the stability of our regularity estimates as

,
\nabla u

1.1.
Results

We state the main results here. The first one concerns the existence of the gradient of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions in
L^{p}
-scale.

Theorem 1.1

(
L^{p}
-gradient regularity)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Then, for any locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function
u
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s)
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R}\Big[R^{s}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+R^{\frac{N}{p}}\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\Tail(u;R)\big)\Big].

The constant
C
is stable as
s\uparrow 1
and blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.

The second result upgrades the integrability of
\nabla u
to any exponent
q>p
.

Theorem 1.2

(
L^{q}
-gradient regularity)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Then, for any locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function
u
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for any $q\in[p,\infty)$.}

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q)
, such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
the quantitative
L^{q}
-gradient estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}\leq CR^{\frac{N}{q}-1}\Big[R^{s-\frac{N}{p}}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;R)\Big]

holds true. The constant
C
is stable in the limit
s\uparrow 1
and blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.

The classical Morrey-type embedding states that any
W^{1,q}
function with
q>N
is locally Hölder continuous with exponent
1-\frac{N}{q}
. Therefore, a direct consequence of Theorem
1.2
is that any
(s,p)
-harmonic function is Hölder continuity for any Hölder exponent in
(0,1)
.

Theorem 1.3

(Almost Lipschitz continuity)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Then, for any locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function
u
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

u\in C_{\loc}^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for any $\gamma\in(0,1)$.}\

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,\gamma)
, such for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
we have

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}\leq\frac{C}{R^{\gamma}}\Big[R^{s-\frac{N}{p}}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;R)\Big].

The constant
C
is stable as
s\uparrow 1
and blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.

The gradient of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions also possesses certain fractional differentiability.

Theorem 1.4

(Fractional differentiability of the gradient)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
. Then, for any locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function
u
in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for any $\alpha\in(0,\beta)$, where $\beta:=\max\Big\{\frac{sp}{q},\frac{1-(1-s)p}{q-1}\Big\}$.}

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q,\alpha)
such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CR^{\frac{N}{q}}}{R^{1+\alpha}}\Big[R^{s-\frac{N}{p}}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;R)\Big].

The constant
C
is stable as
s\uparrow 1
and blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.

Remark 1.5
.

The application of Theorem
1.4
with
q=2
ensures that any locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function satisfies

\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for any $\alpha\in(0,\beta)$, where $\beta:=\max\Big\{\frac{sp}{2},1-(1-s)p\Big\}$.}

In turn, we conclude that

\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for any $\alpha\in(0,\beta)$ and any $q\in[1,2]$.}

Brief review of the literature

Nonlocal equations have attracted a lot of attention in recent years, and interest in them continues to grow. Regarding regularity theory, the investigations started with linear fractional differential operators; see
[
6
,
7
,
17
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,
57
,
61
,

,
63
]
and the references therein. For global and boundary regularity we refer to
[
1
,
2
,
39
,
40
,
58
,
59
]
. In the framework of fractional Sobolev spaces, self-improving properties of Gehring-type were studied in
[
5
,
8
,
49
,
50
]
, while estimates of Calderón-Zygmund-type were obtained in
[
34
,
55
]
. Measure data problems and pointwise gradient potential estimates were considered in
[
24
,
52
]
.

The regularity theory for
(s,p)
-harmonic fuctions is still fragmented, and many of the fundamental questions are unanswered.
Boundedness and Hölder continuity of weak solutions in the interior and at the boundary were proved in
[
17
,
21
,
41
,
42
,
43
,
48
]
; see
[
53
]
for a similar result in the framework of viscosity solutions. Harnack’s inequality was established in
[
22
]
; see also
[
17
]
.
A Wiener-type criterion for boundary regularity was obtained in
[
47
]
. Regularity at the gradient level is less developed. However, there are some results on level of fractional derivatives. A self-improving Gehring-type property was established for
p\geq 2
in
[
60
]
, while a Calderón-Zygmund theory was developed in
[
14
,
23
]
. Finally, in
[
51
]
measure data problems and potential estimates were considered.
Recently, the study of mixed local and non-local problems has gained much interest; see the non-exhaustive list
[
9
,
15
,
20
,
35
]
and the references therein.

The above mentioned results guarantee Hölder continuity of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions with a qualitative Hölder exponent.
The proofs in
[
17
,
21
]
are based on either De Giorgi’s or Moser’s approach. Similar to the local case, Hölder continuity with a quantitative Hölder exponent or gradient regularity requires other techniques such as second-order finite difference estimates in combination with a Moser-type iteration scheme. Roughly speaking, the second-order finite difference estimates in the fractional context replace the second weak derivative estimates from the local case. The latter can be derived by the difference quotient method.
This technique has proven to be very efficient for local operators; we refer to
[
3
,
65
,
66
]
.

For
(s,p)
-harmonic functions, Brasco & Lindgren
[
12
]
established higher Sobolev regularity for the case
p\geq 2
when
s>\frac{p-1}{p}
.
Later, higher Hölder continuity was proved in
[
13
]
by Brasco & Lindgren & Schikorra. More recently, in
[
10
]
we were able to improve the threshold for
s
to
s>\frac{p-2}{p}
. At the same time we improved the level of integrability for the gradient to any exponent
q\geq p
, together with the fractional differentiability
W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}
for any
\alpha\in\big(0,\tfrac{sp-(p-2)}{q}\big)
. As a consequence, we also improved the higher Hölder regularity. Shortly afterwards, Diening & Kim & Lee & Nowak
[
25
]
were able to establish the same amount of fractional differentiability for the inhomogeneous problem at the level
q=p
.

For the case
p<2
, Garain & Lindgren obtained a quantitative higher Hölder estimate in
[
36
]
. When it comes to higher Sobolev regularity,
[
25
]
showed that the weak gradient exists in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}
, and meanwhile admits a certain fractional differentiability. However, the quantitative integrability of the weak gradient does not go beyond
L^{p}
.
The present paper – independent of
[
25
]
– establishes the existence of the weak gradient, its higher integrability at any
L^{q}
level with
q\geq 1
as well as a certain fractional differentiability.

1.3.
Master plan

The so-called difference quotient technique, even though known to be part of the repertoire in the analysis of partial differential equations for decades, is fairly recent on the stage of integro-differential equations. For
(s,p)
-harmonic functions, it was implemented by Brasco & Lindgren
[
12
]
, Brasco & Lindgren & Schikorra
[
13
]
, and Garain & Lindgren
[
36
]
; see also
[
16
,
18
]
under different settings. Our approach also relies on this barehanded technique.

The thrust is quite simple, namely, we differentiate the equation at a discrete level, coupled with a careful choice of testing functions that cope with the discretized nonlinear structure. However, in contrast to the classical treatment of
p
-harmonic functions, some challenging, new features appear in the fractional setting. To successfully implement the technique to analyze regularity properties of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions, one needs to skillfully balance their local differentiability and integrability, and to precisely capture the long-range behavior of their finite differences. Once this is done, we employ various iteration schemes to upgrade the differentiability and subsequently the integrability.

In this program, the first important step lies in raising the preset
W^{s,p}
-regularity to
W^{1,p}
-regularity as stated in Theorem
1.1
.
This step was also carried out in
[
25
]
.

The next step is to improve the integrability of the gradient via an iteration scheme of Moser-type. Recall that for
p
-harmonic functions, one first derives proper second order estimates (i.e. energy estimate for
|\nabla u|
) and exploits the Sobolev embedding to acquire a reverse Hölder estimate:

\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{N-2}{Nq}}\leq C(q)\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

provided
\nabla u\in L^{q}_{\loc}
for some
q\geq p
. Then, starting from
\nabla u\in L^{p}_{\loc}
one iteratively improves the integrability of
\nabla u
.
When it comes to
(s,p)
-harmonic functions, we exploit a similar idea at a discrete level, derive
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\loc}
for some quantified
\alpha=\alpha(s,p,q)
assuming that
\nabla u\in L^{q}_{\loc}
for some
q\geq p
, and use the fractional Sobolev embedding to obtain a reverse Hölder estimate:

\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-\alpha q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{N-\alpha q}{Nq}}\leq C(q)\bigg[\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B_{2}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;2)\bigg].

The quantity
\bm{\mathfrak{T}}
, which encodes the nonlocal behavior of
u
, turns out to be harmless in the iteration scheme. Whereas the most notable difference from the case of
p
-harmonic functions lies in the way the integral exponent increments. Arguably, such difference becomes magnified when one runs the iteration in a quantified manner.

The higher Sobolev regularity acquired in the previous step can be translated into quantitative Hölder estimates thanks to the Morrey embedding. We stress that, this way of obtaining higher Hölder regularity is different from previous works as all of them circumvented the higher Sobolev regularity.

In the pursuit of the higher Sobolev regularity, we are also clued in on a gain of fractional differentiability for
\nabla u
. Conceivably, one should be able to formally recover the classical
W^{2,2}
-regularity of
p
-harmonic functions as
s\uparrow 1
. Indeed, we improve and establish that
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\loc}
for any
q\geq 2
and
\alpha<\beta:=\max\big\{\frac{sp}{q},\frac{1-(1-s)p}{q-1}\big\}
.
This task is taken on in the final part of the paper. In particular, we exploit the Hölder continuity established in Theorem
1.3

and perform a
discrete integration by parts
; see Lemma
2.16
. This is inspired by an argument from the
p
-harmonic functions; see
[
3
]
.

We emphasize that throughout the proofs we take great care on the structure of the constants in order to justify the stability as
s\uparrow 1
.
Finally, we note that in
[
25
]
the authors established
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
\alpha\in(0,\max\{\frac{sp}{2},1-(1-s)p\})
,
which follows from Theorem
1.4
as described in Remark
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout the paper,
C
stands for a generic constant that can change from line to line. In the statements and also in the proofs, we trace the dependencies of the constants with respect to the data. We indicate the dependencies by writing, for example,
C=C(N,p,s)
if
C
depends on
N,p
and
s
. Next, we denote
B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
as a ball with radius
R
centered at
x_{o}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We also define

K_{R}(x_{o}):=B_{R}(x_{o})\times B_{R}(x_{o})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We use this notation at various points to give the occurring double integrals a more compact form.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set and
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For a function
w\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
we understand by
w\in C_{\loc}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
that for any ball
B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
we have that
w\in C^{0,\alpha}\big(\overline{B_{R}(x_{o})}\big)
holds.
Moreover, we denote the semi-norm

[w]_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{R}(x_{o}))}:=\sup_{x\not=y\in B_{R}(x_{o})}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

We also introduce the
fractional Sobolev space

W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, with some
q\in[1,\infty)
and

\gamma\in(0,1)
. A measurable function
w\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
belongs to the fractional Sobolev space
W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
if and only if

\displaystyle\|w\|_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}

\displaystyle:=\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}+[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}<\infty,

where the semi-norm is defined as

\displaystyle[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}

\displaystyle:=\bigg[\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+\gamma q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

If the dimension
k
is clear from the context, we write
[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)}
instead of
[w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})}
for the sake of simplicity. In particular, if
w=\nabla v
is the gradient of a scalar function
v\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we will write
[\nabla w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega)}
instead of

[\nabla w]_{W^{\gamma,q}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
Some useful results concerning fractional Sobolev spaces are collected in §
2.4
;
for more information we refer to
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.1

(
(s,p)
-harmonic functions)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded open set,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. A function
u\in W^{s,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
is called
(s,p)
-harmonic in
\Omega
if and only if

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{(1+|x|)^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty,

and

(2.2)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p-2}(u(x)-u(y))(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=0

for every
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(\Omega)
compactly supported in
\Omega
and extended to
0
outside
\Omega
.

Whenever
u
satisfies (
2.1
), we say
u
belongs to the weighted Lebesgue space
L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For
x_{o}\in\Omega
and
\varrho>0
such that
B_{R}(x_{o})\subset\Omega
use the nonlocal quantity

{\rm Tail}(u;x_{o},R):=\bigg(R^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(x_{o})}\frac{|u(x)|^{p-1}}{|x-x_{o}|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Moreover, we abbreviate

(2.3)

\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;x_{o},R):=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R}(x_{o}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;x_{o},R).

When
x_{o}=0
or when the context is clear, we omit it from the notation.

For
\gamma\in(0,\infty)

V_{\gamma}(a):=|a|^{\gamma-1}a,\qquad\mbox{for $a\in\mathbb{R}$.}

If
a=0
we set
V_{\gamma}(a)=0

\gamma\in(0,1)
.
The first algebraic lemma concerning bounds from above and below
for the difference of
V_{\gamma}
can be retrieved from
[

for the case
\gamma\in(0,1)

for the case

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\gamma>0
, and for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}(|a|+|b|)^{\gamma-1}|b-a|\leq|V_{\gamma}(b)-V_{\gamma}(a)|\leq C_{2}(|a|+|b|)^{\gamma-1}|b-a|,

where

C_{1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma,&\mbox{if $\gamma\in(0,1)$,}\\[5.0pt]
2^{1-\gamma},&\mbox{if $\gamma\in(1,\infty)$,}\end{array}\right.\qquad C_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2^{1-\gamma}&\mbox{if $\gamma\in(0,1]$}\\[5.0pt]
\gamma&\mbox{if $\gamma\in[1,\infty)$.}\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
\delta\geq 1
. Then, for any
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and any
e,f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)e^{p}-V_{\delta}(b-d)f^{p}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\tfrac{p-1}{2^{\delta+1}}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta-1}\big|(a-c)-(b-d)\big|^{2}\big(e^{p}+f^{p}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\big(\tfrac{2^{\delta+1}}{p-1}\big)^{p-1}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{p+\delta-1}|e-f|^{p}.

Proof..

We first rewrite the left-hand side of the desired inequality in the form

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\big(V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)-V_{\delta}(b-d)\big)\big(e^{p}+f^{p}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)+V_{\delta}(b-d)\big)\big(e^{p}-f^{p}\big)=:\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}.

The first summand is non-negative due to the monotonicity; cf.
[
13
, Lemma A.5]
. This means that the multiplicative factors
V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)
and
V_{\delta}(a-c)-V_{\delta}(b-d)
must have the same sign. Without loss of generality, we assume that both are non-negative. Indeed, if they are both negative, we simply switch the summands in the two factors. To each factor we apply Lemma
2.2
(first with exponent
p-1
and then with exponent
\delta
) to conclude

\displaystyle(p-1)\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}|(a-c)-(b-d)|\leq V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq 2^{2-p}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}|(a-c)-(b-d)|

and

\displaystyle 2^{1-\delta}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta-1}|(a-c)-(b-d)|\leq V_{\delta}(a-c)-V_{\delta}(b-d)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\delta\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta-1}|(a-c)-(b-d)|.

This leads to the following lower bound for the first term

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{p-1}{2^{\delta}}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta-1}\big|(a-c)-(b-d)\big|^{2}\big(e^{p}+f^{p}\big)

\displaystyle=:\tfrac{p-1}{2^{\delta}}\,\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}.

In order to bound the second term from above, we use the upper bound for
V_{p-1}
and obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbf{II}|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-p}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}|(a-c)-(b-d)|\big(|a-c|^{\delta}+|b-d|^{\delta}\big)|e^{p}-f^{p}|

\displaystyle\leq\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}|(a-c)-(b-d)|\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta}|e^{p}-f^{p}|.

By Lemma
2.2
applied with
\gamma=p
we have

\displaystyle|e^{p}-f^{p}|\leq p(e+f)^{p-1}|e-f|

\displaystyle\leq p(e^{p}+f^{p})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}|e-f|.

We use this inequality and rewrite the exponents appearing in the right-hand side of the preceding inequality and subsequently apply Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{p}{p-1}
and
p
. In this way we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbf{II}|

\displaystyle\leq p\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{(p-2)(\frac{p-1}{p}+\frac{1}{p})}|(a-c)-(b-d)|^{2\frac{p-1}{p}+\frac{2-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{(\delta-1)\frac{p-1}{p}+\frac{p+\delta-1}{p}}(e^{p}+f^{p})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}|e-f|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\,\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}+\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}|(a-c)-(b-d)|^{2-p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{p+\delta-1}|e-f|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\,\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}+\varepsilon^{-(p-1)}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{p+\delta-1}|e-f|^{p}.

Choosing
\varepsilon=\tfrac{p-1}{2^{\delta+1}}
we conclude the claimed inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
\delta\geq 1
. Then, for any
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}
and any
e,f\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we have

\displaystyle\big(V_{p-1}

\displaystyle(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)e^{2}-V_{\delta}(b-d)f^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{2(p-1)}{2^{\delta}}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta-1}\big|(a-c)-(b-d)\big|^{2}ef

\displaystyle\quad+\big(V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)e+V_{\delta}(b-d)f\big)(e-f)

Proof..

We re-write the left-hand side of the claimed inequality and subsequently use inequalities (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) from the proof of the preceding lemma

\displaystyle\big(V_{p-1}

\displaystyle(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)-V_{\delta}(b-d)\big)ef

\displaystyle\quad+\big(V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)e+V_{\delta}(b-d)f\big)(e-f)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{p-1}{2^{\delta-1}}\big(|a-b|+|c-d|\big)^{p-2}\big(|a-c|+|b-d|\big)^{\delta-1}\big|(a-c)-(b-d)\big|^{2}ef

\displaystyle\quad+\big(V_{p-1}(a-b)-V_{p-1}(c-d)\big)\big(V_{\delta}(a-c)e+V_{\delta}(b-d)f\big)(e-f).

This is the claimed inequality.
∎

In the following we summarize some statements about fractional Sobolev spaces. We avoid introducing further functional spaces, such as Nikol’skii and Besov spaces. Rather, we restrict ourselves to the main functional estimates. For more information on this topic, we refer to

,
12
,

. We start with the

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

and

. Then for any

\displaystyle(1-\gamma)\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+\gamma q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{8\omega_{N}}{q}R^{(1-\gamma)q}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Next, we provide a
fractional Sobolev-Poincaré inequality
, which can be retrieved from
[
26
, Theorem 6.7]
. To trace the stability with

, the exact dependence of

with respect to

The result can be found in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
q\geq 1
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, such that
\gamma q<N
.
Then, for any

we have

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}

\displaystyle|w|^{\frac{Nq}{N-q\gamma}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{N-q\gamma}{Nq}}\!\!\leq 2^{q-1}\bigg[C^{q}R^{q\gamma}\int_{B_{R}}-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+q\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}|w|^{q}\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

2.5.
Finite differences and fractional Sobolev spaces

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and a vector
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, define
\Omega_{|h|}:=\{x\in\Omega\colon{\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)>|h|\}
. By
\bm{\tau}_{h}\colon L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})\to L^{1}(\Omega_{|h|},\mathbb{R}^{k})
we denote the finite difference operator

\bm{\tau}_{h}w(x):=w(x+h)-w(x)\equiv w_{h}(x)-w(x)\quad\mbox{for $x\in\Omega_{|h|}$.}

If the direction is a fixed unit vector

e\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we write

\bm{\tau}_{h}^{(e)}w(x):=w(x+he)-w(x),

where
h\in\mathbb{R}
now is a real number. At several points we will use two elementary properties of finite differences. These are summarized in
[
38
, Lemma 7.23 & 7.24]

and
[
32
, Chap. 5.8.2]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
1<q<\infty
,
M>0
, and
0<d<R
. Then, any
w\in L^{q}(B_{R})
that satisfies

\int_{B_{R-d}}\big|\bm{\tau}_{h}^{(e)}w\big|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M^{q}|h|^{q}\quad\mbox{for any $0<|h|\leq d$}

is weakly differentiable in direction
e
on
B_{R-d}
. Moreover, we have

\int_{B_{R-d}}|D_{e}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M^{q}.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
0<d<R
. Then, for any

w\in W^{1,q}(B_{R})
, and any
0<|h|\leq d
, we have

\big\|\bm{\tau}_{h}^{(e)}w\big\|_{L^{q}(B_{R-d})}\leq|h|\,\|D_{e}w\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}.

Functions
w
belonging to a fractional Sobolev space
W^{\gamma,q}
fulfill an estimate for finite differences similar to the one from Lemma
2.11
; see
[
12
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
, and
0<d<R
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(N,q)
such that for any
w\in W^{\gamma,q}(B_{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R-d}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\,|h|^{\gamma q}\Bigg[(1-\gamma)[w]^{q}_{W^{\gamma,q}(B_{R})}+\bigg(\frac{R^{(1-\gamma)q}}{d^{q}}+\frac{1}{\gamma d^{\gamma q}}\bigg)\|w\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\Bigg]

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
that satisfies
|h|\leq d
.

As in the case of Sobolev spaces, quantitative properties of finite differences on the
L^{q}
-scale in terms of a power of the step size
h
can be coverted into fractional differentiability;
cf.
[
4
, 7.73]
. The version given here is taken from
[
19
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in(0,1]
,
M\in[1,\infty)
, and
d\in(0,R)
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(N,q)
such that whenever
w\in L^{q}(B_{R+d})
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M^{q}|h|^{\gamma q}\qquad\mbox{for any $h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}$ with $|h|\leq d$,}

then
w\in W^{\beta,q}(B_{R})
whenever
\beta\in(0,\gamma)
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}\frac{|w(x)-w(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+\beta q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\leq C\bigg[\frac{d^{(\gamma-\beta)q}}{\gamma-\beta}M^{q}+\frac{1}{\beta d^{\beta q}}\|w\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}^{q}\bigg].

The following lemma proves useful estimates in a the fractional context when dealing with second-order finite differences. Essentially, the proof can be found in
[
64
, Chapter 5]
; see also
[
12
, Proposition 2.4]
. An alternative proof has been given in
[
29
, Lemma 2.2.1]
;see also
[
30
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
,
\gamma>0
,
M\geq 0
,
0<r<R
, and
0<d\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(R-r)
. Then, there exists a constant
C=C(q)
such that whenever
w\in L^{q}(B_{R})
satisfies

(2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}w)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M^{q}|h|^{\gamma q},\qquad\mbox{for any $h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}$ with $|h|\leq d$,}

then in the case

we have for any

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}w|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

3.
Energy inequality

At various stages, it is appropriate to consider the corresponding localized variants
\eta\bm{\tau}_{h}u
respectively
\eta V_{q}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)
with a cut-off function
\eta
instead of
\bm{\tau}_{h}u
or
V_{q}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)
. To avoid having constantly to explain the choice of
the cut-off function
\eta
, we fix it in advance.

Definition 3.1
.

Given
x_{o}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and radii
0<r<R
, by
\mathfrak{Z}_{r,R}(x_{o})
we denote the class of functions
\eta\in C^{1}_{0}\big(B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}(x_{o}),[0,1]\big)
that satisfy
\eta=1
in
B_{r}(x_{o})
and

\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}(x_{o}))}\leq\frac{C}{R-r}.

Similarly,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{r,R}(x_{o})
denotes the class of all cut-off functions

\eta\in C^{2}_{0}\big(B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}(x_{o}),[0,1]\big)
that satisfy
\eta=1
in
B_{r}(x_{o})
and

\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}(x_{o}))}\leq\frac{C}{R-r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\nabla^{2}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}(x_{o}))}\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{2}}.

Here
C
stands for a universal constant.

Moreover, to simplify the notation, we will use for
u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
x,y\in\Omega
, and
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
x+h,y+h\in\Omega
the following abbreviations

(3.1)

U(x,y)=u(x)-u(y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad U_{h}(x,y)=u_{h}(x)-u_{h}(y)\equiv u(x+h)-u(y+h).

Finally, we recall the definition of
\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(\cdot)
from (
2.3
).

3.1.
Auxiliary estimates

As we will see, the tail estimates play an important role in the proof of the energy inequalities. In a sense, the exponents of
|h|
and
|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|
that appear in the tail estimate determine the gain in fractional differentiability for
\bm{\tau}_{h}u
.

Lemma 3.2

(Tail estimate)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. There exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s)
such that whenever
u\in L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

,
R>0
,
r\in(0,R)
, and
d\in(0,\frac{1}{4}(R-r)]
, we have for any
x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}(x_{o})
and any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
0<|h|\leq d
that

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(x_{o})}

\displaystyle\frac{V_{p-1}(U_{h}(x,y))-V_{p-1}(U(x,y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{p-1}+\frac{|h|}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;x_{o},R+d)^{p-1}\bigg].

The constant
C
has the form
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s
.

Proof..

Assume
x_{o}=0
for simplicity. Like in
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
, rewrite the left-hand side integral as

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(h)}\frac{V_{p-1}(u_{h}(x)-u(y))}{|x+h-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{V_{p-1}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{R}(h)\cup B_{R})}\bigg[\frac{V_{p-1}(u_{h}(x)-u(y))}{|x+h-y|^{N+sp}}-\frac{V_{p-1}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{=\,}+\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R}(h)}\frac{V_{p-1}(u_{h}(x)-u(y))}{|x+h-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y-\int_{B_{R}(h)\setminus B_{R}}\frac{V_{p-1}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y.

The proof hinges on estimating the absolute value of the three integral on the right, which we denote as
\mathbf{I}_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
.

Estimating
\mathbf{I}_{2}
and
\mathbf{I}_{3}
is exactly the same as in
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
. Namely,

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}\leq\frac{C|h|R^{N-1}}{(R-r)^{N+sp}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{p-1}

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{3}\leq\frac{C|h|R^{N-1}}{(R-r)^{N+sp}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}^{p-1}

for some
C=C(N,p)
.

To estimate
\mathbf{I}_{1}
, we proceed as follows. First, observe that since
|x|<\frac{1}{2}(R+r)
and
|y|>R
, for any
\xi\in B_{|h|}
with
0<|h|\leq d\leq\frac{1}{4}(R-r)
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{|x+\xi-y|}{|y|}\geq 1-\frac{|x|}{|y|}-\frac{|\xi|}{|y|}\geq 1-\frac{R+r}{2R}-\frac{d}{R}\geq\frac{R-r}{4R}.

Then, the mean value theorem applied to the function
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto|x+th-y|^{-N-sp}
gives some
t\in[0,1]
, such that

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{|x+h-y|^{N+sp}}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(N+sp)|h|}{|x+th-y|^{N+sp+1}}

\displaystyle\leq|h|\Big(\frac{4R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\frac{N+p}{|y|^{N+sp+1}}.

Here, we used (
3.2
), which is possible since
th\in B_{|h|}
. Now, we use the above observation together with Lemma
2.2
(choosing
b=u_{h}(x)-u(y)
,
a=u(x)-u(y)
,
\gamma=p-1\geq 1
,
C_{2}=p-1
) to estimate the integrand of the first integral by

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\bigg|\frac{V_{p-1}(u_{h}(x)-u(y))}{|x+h-y|^{N+sp}}-\frac{V_{p-1}(u(x)-u(y))}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\big|V_{p-1}(u_{h}(x)-u(y))\big|\bigg|\frac{1}{|x+h-y|^{N+sp}}-\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\big|V_{p-1}(u_{h}(x)-u(y))-V_{p-1}(u(x)-u(y))\big|

\displaystyle\leq C|h|\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\frac{|u_{h}(x)-u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp+1}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+C\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\big(|u_{h}(x)-u(x)|+|u(x)-u(y)|\big)^{p-2}

for a constant
C=C(N,p)
. The second term on the right-hand side of (
3.3
) is estimated with the aid of the elementary inequality

\displaystyle|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|\leq|u_{h}(x)-u(y)|+|u(x)-u(y)|;

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\frac{|u_{h}(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp+1}}+C\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp}}

for some
C=C(N,p)
.
Plugging this into
\mathbf{I}_{1}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{R}(h)\cup B_{R})}\frac{|u_{h}(x)|^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp+1}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+C\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus(B_{R}(h)\cup B_{R})}\frac{1}{|y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C|h|\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{p-1}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp+1}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+C\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{p-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{1}{|y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|h|}{R^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\big[\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\mathrm{Tail}(u;R)\big]^{p-1}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{C}{sp}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{p-1}}{R^{sp}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{|h|}{R^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}+\frac{C}{sp}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{p-1}}{R^{sp}}.

The last line followed from Remark
2.7
.
Collecting all these estimates concludes the proof.
∎

When additional regularity is known, the tail estimate can be improved.
The following lemma deals with the case
u\in W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. If
\theta
is large enough, higher powers of the increment
h
are obtained compared to Lemma
3.2
. Due to the additional regularity requirement, a discrete integration by parts can be carried out using Lemma
2.16
. This is inspired by an argument from the local case of the singular
p
-Laplacian; see
[
3
]
.

Lemma 3.3

(Higher order tail estimate)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in[p,\infty)
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\delta:=q-p+1
. There exists a constant
C=C(N,s,q)
such that: whenever

u\in W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

then for each
0<r<R
, each
d\in(0,\frac{1}{8}(R-r)]
,
each
B_{R+d}\equiv B_{R+d}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, each
\eta\in\mathfrak{Z}_{r,R}(x_{o})
, and each
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
0<|h|\leq d
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}

\displaystyle\frac{\big[V_{p-1}(U_{h}(x,y))-V_{p-1}(U(x,y))\big]\big[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta^{2}\big](x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\delta+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R+d)^{q}\bigg].

The constant
C
has the form
C=\widetilde{C}(N,q)/s
.

Proof..

For
\varepsilon\in(0,d)
and
\varrho>2R
we choose a cut-off function
\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
such that
\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}\equiv 1
in
[R+\varepsilon,\varrho]
,
\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}\equiv 0
in
[0,R]\cup[2\varrho,\infty)
and linearly interpolating otherwise.
With the abbreviations

F(x,y):=V_{p-1}(U(x,y))\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad G(x,y):=\frac{[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta^{2}](x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

the integral on the left-hand side of (
3.4
) can be re-written as

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\bm{\tau}_{(h,h)}F(x,y)G(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\lim_{\varrho\to\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\bm{\tau}_{(h,h)}F(x,y)G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

An application of the integration by parts formula from Remark
2.18
yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\int_{0}^{1}F(x+th,y+th)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathfrak{H}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(h,h)\cdot\nabla_{(x,y)}\big(G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)\big)

\displaystyle=h\cdot\nabla_{x}G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)+h\cdot\nabla_{y}\big(G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)\big)

\displaystyle=h\cdot\big[\nabla_{x}G(x,y)+\nabla_{y}G(x,y)\big]\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)+G(x,y)h\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|).

For the term in
[\dots]
of the last display, we compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}G(x,y)+\nabla_{y}G(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{x}V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\eta^{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}+\frac{V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\nabla_{x}\eta^{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\quad+V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\eta^{2}(x)\underbrace{\Bigg[\nabla_{x}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}+\nabla_{y}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigg]}_{=0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla_{x}V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\eta^{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}+\frac{V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\nabla_{x}\eta^{2}(x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\mathbf{G}(x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}},

with the obvious meaning of
\mathbf{G}(x)
. Inserting this into
\mathfrak{H}
above, we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big[\mathbf{G}(x)\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)+V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\eta^{2}(x)\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)\big]\cdot h}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\equiv\frac{\mathfrak{h}(x,y)\cdot h}{|x-y|^{N+sp}},

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{F(x+th,y+th)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathfrak{h}(x,y)\cdot h\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Next, in the integrand of
\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}
we replace
|x-y|
with
|y+th|
for any
t\in[0,1]
and
0<|h|\leq d\leq\frac{1}{8}(R-r)
. In fact, since
|x|<\frac{1}{2}(R+r)
and
|y|>R
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{|x-y|}{|y+th|}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{|x+th|}{|y+th|}\geq 1-\frac{|x|+|h|}{|y|-|h|}\geq 1-\frac{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)+d}{R-d}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{2}(R-r)-2d}{R-d}\geq\frac{\frac{1}{2}(R-r)-2d}{R}\geq\frac{R-r}{4R}.

Therefore, inserting this back to the previous identity of
\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}
, taking absolute value on both sides and splitting the integral on the right-hand side into two terms according to the expression of
\mathfrak{h}(x,y)
, we arrive at

\displaystyle|\mathbf{T}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}|

\displaystyle\leq 4^{N+sp}|h|\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\big[\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}\big],

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\bigg]\,|\mathbf{G}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)|\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\bigg]\,|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x.

To obtain the last line we used
|V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))|=|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}
and the fact that
|\eta(x)|\leq 1
on
B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}
.
Let us deal with these two terms separately. Fubini’s theorem, the set inclusion
B_{R-|h|}\subset B_{R}(th)
, Lemma
2.6
, and
R-|h|\geq\frac{3}{4}R
allow us
to estimate the inner integral
[\dots]
of
\mathbf{I}
by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|u(x+th)|^{p-1}+|u(y+th)|^{p-1}}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+|u(y+th)|^{p-1}}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}(th)}\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+|u(z)|^{p-1}}{|z|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R-|h|}}\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+|u(z)|^{p-1}}{|z|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{N|B_{1}|}{sp}\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}}{(R-|h|)^{sp}}+\frac{\Tail(u;R-|h|)^{p-1}}{(R-|h|)^{sp}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)}{spR^{sp}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}.

with the abbreviation
\bm{\mathfrak{T}}:=\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R+d)
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{I}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)}{spR^{sp+1}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}R|\mathbf{G}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Arguing in a similar way as above and taking also into account the properties of
\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}
we obtain for the inner integral
[\dots]
of
\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}
(note that
|y+th|\geq|y|-d\geq R-\frac{1}{8}(R-r)\geq\frac{1}{2}R
for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}
and
\varepsilon<d
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon,\varrho}(|y|)|\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{0}^{1}\bigg[\frac{1}{\varrho}\int_{B_{2\varrho}\setminus B_{\varrho}}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R+\varepsilon}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\varrho}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u(x+th)|^{p-1}+|u(y+th)|^{p-1}}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{R+\varepsilon}\setminus B_{R}}\frac{|u(x+th)|^{p-1}+|u(y+th)|^{p-1}}{|y+th|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\varrho}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}\frac{\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+|u(y)|^{p-1}}{|y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}y+C(N)\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d+\varepsilon})}\frac{|B_{R+\varepsilon}\setminus B_{R}|}{\varepsilon R^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C(N)}{\varrho spR^{sp}}\big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}+\Tail(u;R)\big)^{p-1}+\frac{C(N)}{R^{sp+1}}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d+\varepsilon})}.

The first term on the right-hand side disappears as
\varrho\to\infty
, while the second one converges to
C(N)R^{-sp-1}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Therefore, we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varrho\to\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon,\varrho}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)}{spR^{sp+1}}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Combining these estimates, we end up with

\displaystyle|\mathbf{T}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)|h|}{spR^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}\big[R|\mathbf{G}(x)|+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

It remains to compute
|\mathbf{G}(x)|
. In order for that, we use the product and chain rule for Sobolev functions to obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbf{G}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla_{x}V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))|\eta^{2}(x)+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}|\nabla_{x}\eta^{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\delta|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta-1}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u(x)|+\tfrac{C}{R-r}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}.

Inserting this to the previous estimate gives

\displaystyle|\mathbf{T}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)|h|}{spR^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}\big[\delta R|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{\delta-1}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|+\tfrac{R}{R-r}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{\delta}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x.

In order to estimate the above integral containing
|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|
, we apply Lemma
2.12
with
(\gamma,R,d)
replaced by
(\theta,\frac{1}{2}(R+r),d=\frac{1}{2}(R-r))
and find that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}

\displaystyle|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{\theta q}\Bigg[(1-\theta)[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\bigg(\frac{R^{(1-\theta)q}}{d^{q}}+\frac{1}{\theta d^{\theta q}}\bigg)\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\theta q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{q}\bigg[R^{\theta q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\bigg],

where
C=C(N,q)
. Therefore, we can estimate the integral appearing in the previous estimate of
|\mathbf{T}|
by first applying Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.11
and then the last display. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}\big[\delta R|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{\delta-1}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{\delta}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta R|B_{R}|^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\bigg[\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{\delta-1}{q}}\bigg[\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{\leq\,}+|B_{R}|^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\bigg[\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{\delta}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta|B_{R}|^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}R^{1-\theta}|h|^{\delta-1+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{\delta-1}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{\theta q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}+|B_{R}|^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}|h|^{\delta}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{\delta}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq q|B_{R}|^{1-\frac{\delta}{q}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\delta-1+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{\delta}{q}},

where from the second-to-last line we used
|h|^{\delta}\leq R^{1-\theta}|h|^{\delta-1+\theta}
, since
\theta<1
. Substituting this back to the estimate of
|\mathbf{T}|
, recalling
\delta=q-p+1
, and further applying Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{p-1}
and
\frac{q}{q-p+1}
we reach

\displaystyle|\mathbf{T}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N,q)}{spR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\delta+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\big(|B_{R}|^{\frac{p-1}{q}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}\big)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{\delta}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N,q)}{spR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\delta+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}\bigg].

This proves the claimed inequality.
∎

The following lemma will be used to estimate the term containing the fractional derivative of the cut-off function in the improved energy estimate from Proposition

.
The additional regularity assumption
u\in W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
allows us to get the same power of the increment
h
as in Lemma
3.3
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in[p,\infty)
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
\delta:=q-p+1
.
There exists a constant
C=C(N,q)
such that: whenever

u\in W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

then for each
0<r<R
, each
d\in(0,\frac{1}{8}(R-r)]
,
each
B_{R+d}\equiv B_{R+d}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, each
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
0<|h|\leq d
, and each
C^{2}
-cut-off function
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{r,R}(x_{o})
we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{\big[V_{p-1}\big(U_{h}(x,y)\big)-V_{p-1}\big(U(x,y)\big)\big]\big[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta\big](x)\big(\eta(x)-\eta(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\delta+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\quad\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\frac{R^{(1+\theta)q}}{1-s}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta(1-s)}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}+R^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}^{q}\bigg].

Proof..

For
\varepsilon\in(0,d)
we choose a cut-off function
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,\infty}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
, such that
\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
in
[0,R-\varepsilon]
,
\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
in
[R,\infty)
and linearly interpolated otherwise.
Moreover, we abbreviate

F(x,y):=V_{p-1}\big(U(x,y)\big)

and

G(x,y):=[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta](x)\big(\eta(x)-\eta(y)\big).

With this notation
the integral on the left-hand side of the claimed inequality can be re-written in the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{\bm{\tau}_{(h,h)}F(x,y)G(x,y)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{\bm{\tau}_{(h,h)}F(x,y)G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

An application of the integration by parts formula from Remark
2.18
in connection with Remark
2.17
yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=-\iint_{K_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}F(x+th,y+th)\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathfrak{H}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

where

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}(x,y)

\displaystyle=(h,h)\cdot\nabla_{(x,y)}\bigg[\frac{G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\bigg].

At this point, we argue formally. In principle, we would have to include a second cut-off function in the argument, which truncates the singularity along the diagonal
K_{R}\cap\{x=y\}
; see Remark
3.5
. Since this only complicates the proof by a technical aspect, we outsource the argument
and proceed by computing the gradient
on the right-hand side in (
3.5
). We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{h\cdot\big[\nabla_{x}G(x,y)+\nabla_{y}G(x,y)\big]\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{G(x,y)h\cdot\big[\nabla_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)+\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)\big]}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\quad+G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)\bigg[\underbrace{\nabla_{x}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}+\nabla_{y}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}}_{=0}\bigg].

Among the three terms on the right, the third one vanishes due to symmetry in
x
and
y
. Moreover, in the second term the product
G(x,y)h\cdot\nabla_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)
also vanishes due to the definition of
G(x,y)
,
\spt(\eta)\subset B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}
and
\spt(\nabla_{x}\psi_{\varepsilon})\subset B_{R}\setminus B_{R-\varepsilon}
. Applying a similar cancellation argument to the remaining part in the second term, we have that

G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)h\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)=V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))\eta^{2}(x)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)h\cdot\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)

Hence, using these simplifications and plugging the above expression of
\mathfrak{H}(x,y)
in the definition of
\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon}
, we estimate

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq|h|\big[\mathbf{I}+\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}\big],

where we introduced

\mathbf{I}:=\iint_{K_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,|\mathbf{G}(x,y)|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

with

\mathbf{G}(x,y):=\nabla_{x}G(x,y)+\nabla_{y}G(x,y)

and

\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}:=\iint_{K_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{\delta}|\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

We estimate
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}
separately.
First, by Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{p-1}
and
\frac{q}{q-p+1}
we have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{I}|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\iint_{K_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|^{\frac{q}{p-1}}}{|x-y|^{N+sq}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{\frac{p-1}{q}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\mathbf{G}(x,y)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N+s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}.

By a change of variable, the definition of
V_{p-1}
, and an application of Lemma
2.8

the first integral of (
3.7
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\iint_{K_{R}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|F(x+th,y+th)|^{\frac{q}{p-1}}}{|x-y|^{N+sq}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\iint_{K_{R}(th)}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+sq}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{K_{R+|h|}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+sq}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C(N)\frac{(R+|h|)^{(1-s)q}}{(1-s)q}\int_{B_{R+|h|}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N,q)}{R^{sq}}\frac{R^{q}}{1-s}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x.

To further estimate the second integral in (
3.7
), we write
\mathbf{G}(x,y)
more explicitly according to the definition of
G(x,y)
. Namely, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\nabla\big[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta\big](x)\big(\eta(x)-\eta(y)\big)+\big[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta\big](x)\big(\nabla\eta(x)-\nabla\eta(y)\big).

Plug this in the second integral of (
3.7
) and apply the standard trick of triangle’s inequality to split the integral. This results in the first estimate in the following display.
To continue, recall that the cut-off function

\eta\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{Z}}_{r,R}
satisfies

|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|\leq\frac{C}{R-r}|x-y|
and moreover
|\nabla_{x}\eta(x)-\nabla_{y}\eta(y)|\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{2}}|x-y|
. This leads to the exponent
N-(1-s)\frac{q}{q-p+1}
of
|x-y|
in both integrals of the second estimate in the following display. Finally, we apply the second part of Lemma
2.5
to obtain the last estimate. That is,

\displaystyle\iint_{K_{R}}

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{G}(x,y)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N+s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\nabla[V_{q-p+1}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta](x)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}|\eta(x)-\eta(y)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N+s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+C\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{q}|\nabla\eta(x)-\nabla\eta(y)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N+s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\nabla[V_{q-p+1}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta](x)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N-(1-s)\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{C}{(R-r)^{\frac{2q}{q-p+1}}}\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N-(1-s)\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla[V_{q-p+1}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta]|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{(1+s)\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{\frac{2q}{q-p+1}}\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x,

with
C=C(N,q)
. To further estimate the first integral on the right-hand side of the last display, we use the chain rule for Sololev functions (note that
u
is locally bounded):

\displaystyle|\nabla[V_{q-p+1}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta]|

\displaystyle\leq|\nabla[V_{q-p+1}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)]|\eta+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q-p+1}|\nabla\eta|

\displaystyle\leq(q-p+1)|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q-p}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|+\tfrac{C}{R-r}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q-p+1}.

Substituting this back to the previous estimate, we
obtain

\displaystyle\bigg[\iint_{K_{R}}

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{G}(x,y)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N+s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}R^{s}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{\frac{(q-p)q}{q-p+1}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{C}{(1-s)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}R^{1+s}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}.

The second integral in (
3.9
) is estimated by Lemma
2.11
with
d=\frac{1}{2}(R-r)
. Namely,

\displaystyle\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq|h|^{q-p+1}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}^{q-p+1}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+1}\Big[R^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\Big]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}.

To deal with the first integral on the right-hand side of (
3.9
), we use Hölder’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q-p+1}{q-p}
and
q-p+1
, followed by Lemma
2.12
and Lemma
2.11
with
d=\frac{1}{2}(R-r)
. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}

\displaystyle|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{\frac{(q-p)q}{q-p+1}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q-p}{q}}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{q-p}\bigg[\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q-p}{q}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot|h|^{\theta}\Bigg[(1-\theta)[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\bigg(\frac{(R+d)^{(1-\theta)q}}{d^{q}}+\frac{1}{\theta d^{\theta q}}\bigg)\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{q-p}\|\nabla u\|^{q-p}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)\bigg[(1-\theta)R^{\theta q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{1}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+\theta}\!\frac{R}{R-r}\bigg[R^{q(1+\theta)}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}},

where
C=C(N,q)
. Substituting these estimates to (
3.9
) yields

\displaystyle\bigg[\iint_{K_{R}}

\displaystyle\frac{|\mathbf{G}(x,y)|^{\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}{|x-y|^{N+s\frac{q}{q-p+1}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}R^{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}+\frac{C}{(1-s)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}R^{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+1}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}\Big[R^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\Big]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}}R^{1+s}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\bigg]^{\frac{q-p+1}{q}},

for a constant
C=C(N,q)
.
Combining (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) in (
3.7
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbf{I}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}\bigg].

This finishes the analysis of
\mathbf{I}
.

Next, we turn our attention to the term
\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}
.
Using the definition of
F
, we have for the inner integral

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|F(x+th,y+th)|\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\big[|u(x+th)-u(y+th)|^{p-1}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t\leq 2\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}

for a.e.
x,y\in B_{R}
, so that

\displaystyle|\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{q-p+1}|\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)|}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{\varepsilon}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}\iint_{B_{R}\times(B_{R}\setminus B_{R-\varepsilon})}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{q-p+1}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

To get the last line we used that
|\nabla_{y}\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
on
B_{R}\setminus B_{R-\varepsilon}
.
In addition, for
x\in B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}
and
y\in B_{R}\setminus B_{R-\varepsilon}
we have

|x-y|\geq R-\varepsilon-\tfrac{1}{2}(R+r)=\tfrac{1}{2}(R-r)-\varepsilon\geq\tfrac{1}{4}(R-r).

This allows us to estimate the kernel from above and obtain

\displaystyle|\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4^{N+sp+1}}{(R-r)^{N+sp}}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}\underbrace{\frac{|B_{R}\setminus B_{R-\varepsilon}|}{\varepsilon}}_{\leq N|B_{1}|R^{N-1}}\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)}{R^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}\|u\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}|h|^{q-p+1}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q-p+1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N)}{R^{sp+1}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+1}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\frac{R^{q}}{1-s}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+(1-s)R^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}^{q}\bigg].

To obtain the second line we applied Lemma
2.11
with
d=\frac{1}{2}(R-r)
, whereas the last line follows from Hölder’s inequality to raise the power of
|\nabla u|
from
q-p+1
to
q
and then Young’s inequality with exponents
\frac{q}{p-1}
and
\frac{q}{q-p+1}
.
Inserting the estimates for
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{II}_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.6
), we end up with

\displaystyle|\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+1+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\frac{R^{(1+\theta)q}}{1-s}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta(1-s)}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R+d})}+R^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R+d})}^{q}\bigg],

where
C=C(N,q)
. This proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Instead of

\frac{G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}},

we consider the function

\frac{G(x,y)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|x|)\psi_{\varepsilon}(|y|)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\phi_{\sigma}(|x-y|)

with a cut-off function
\phi_{\sigma}\in W^{1,\infty}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
, which disappears on the interval
[0,\sigma]
, is identical 1 on
[2\sigma,\infty)
and is linearly interpolated in
[\sigma,2\sigma]
. Instead of
\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon}
we would have
\mathbf{L}_{\varepsilon,\sigma}
with the corresponding integrand. For this integrand, the assumptions of Remark
2.17
are fulfilled, as the singularity of the kernel has been cut off. After calculating the gradient
(h,h)\cdot\nabla_{(x,y)}
, this construction results in three terms in which the cut-off function
\phi_{\sigma}
appears as a multiplicative factor and one further term in which
\phi_{\sigma}
is differentiated. However, this term disappears as

(h,h)\cdot\nabla_{(x,y)}\phi_{\sigma}(|x-y|)=\tfrac{1}{\sigma}\bm{\chi}_{(\sigma,2\sigma)}(|x-y|)h\cdot\big[\nabla_{x}|x-y|+\nabla_{y}|x-y|\big]=0.

In the following calculations, we therefore only have to take into account a multiplicative factor
\phi_{\sigma}(|x-y|)\leq 1
in all integrals, which however does not create any problem, as all integrals exist.

3.2.
Energy inequalities for
W^{1,q}
-solutions

In this chapter we derive three versions of energy inequalities for
(s,p)
-harmonic functions. These differ from each other by regularity conditions assumed apriori. For the first version, we assume that the solutions are already of class
W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}
for some
q\geq p
. It provides a basic building block for the higher gradient regularity expanded on in Section
5
.
In the second energy inequality, we assume that
C^{0,\gamma}_{\rm loc}
-regularity in addition to
W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}
-regularity is available. This enables us to use large parts of the proof of the first energy inequality. However, the assumption of Hölder continuity allows a more sophisticated coercivity estimate, increasing the integral exponent from
p
to
2
. It will be used in Section
6
to study the fractional differentiability of the gradient.
The third energy inequality refers to
(s,p)
-harmonic functions with
W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}
-regularity, where
\theta
means the fractional differentiability of the gradient.
In Section
6
this will lead in the fractional context to the analogue of the
W^{2,2}_{\rm loc}
-regularity from the local case.

Proposition 3.6

(Energy inequality for
W^{1,q}
-solutions)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in[p,\infty)
.
There exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q)

such that whenever

u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
that satisfies

u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

then for every
0<r<R
,
d:=\frac{1}{4}(R-r)
,

B_{R+d}\equiv B_{R+d}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, every

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
and every step size
0<|h|\leq d
we have

\displaystyle\big[V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{p}_{W^{(1-\varepsilon)(1-\frac{p}{2})+s\frac{p}{2},p}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C}{R^{[(1-\varepsilon)(1-\frac{p}{2})+s\frac{p}{2}]p}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-(1-\frac{p}{2})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\cdot\bigg[\frac{R^{q}}{1-s}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R+d)^{q}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg[\frac{R^{q}}{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

The constant
C
has the form

\widetilde{C}(N,p)q4^{q}/s
.

Proof..

Consider
x_{o}\in\Omega
,
0<r<R
and
d=\tfrac{1}{4}(R-r)
such that
B_{R+d}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
.
Since
x_{o}
is fixed we omit the reference to the center
x_{o}
and write
B_{\varrho}
and
K_{\varrho}
instead of
B_{\varrho}(x_{o})
and
K_{\varrho}(x_{o})
.
Let
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
|h|\leq d
. Testing (
2.2
) with
\varphi_{-h}(x):=\varphi(x-h)
instead of
\varphi
, where
\varphi\in W^{s,p}(B_{R})
with
\spt\varphi\in B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}
, we conclude by discrete integration by parts that also
u_{h}
satisfies (
2.2
). Subtracting (
2.2
) with
u
from (
2.2
) with
u_{h}

\displaystyle 0

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{T}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}}\bm{|}t(x)||\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|^{q-p+1}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\Bigg[\frac{|h|}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p-1}\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q-p+1}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg],

\displaystyle|\mathbf{T}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}\bigg].

Now we substitute the inequalities for

|\mathbf{T}|

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

.

2.2

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{r}}\frac{\big|V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))-V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y))\big|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+(1-\varepsilon)(1-\frac{p}{2})p+sp\frac{p}{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in[p,\infty)
, and
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
There exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q)

such that whenever

u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
that satisfies

u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C_{\loc}^{0,\gamma}(\Omega),

then for every

,

\displaystyle\big[V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{2}_{W^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p,2}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}\bigg[\frac{R^{q}}{1-s}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}\bigg],

where
\bm{\mathfrak{T}}:=\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;x_{o},R+d)
.
Moreover, the constant
C
has the form

\widetilde{C}(N,p)q^{2}4^{q}/s
.

Proof..

The starting point is inequality (
3.14
) from the proof of Proposition
3.6
. Taking into account that
\Theta(x,y)=2
for
(x,y)\in K_{r}
and that the integrand of
\mathbf{I}_{1}
in (
3.14
) is non-negative, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{r}}\frac{\mathcal{U}(x,y)^{p-2}(|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|)^{q-p}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)-\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\,\leq\mathbf{I}_{1}\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}}{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}\bigg]

(3.20)

\displaystyle\,\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[\frac{R^{q}}{1-s}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}\bigg],

where
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)4^{q}
and

\mathcal{U}(x,y)=|U_{h}(x,y)|+|U(x,y)|=|u_{h}(x)-u_{h}(y)|+|u(x)-u(y)|.

From the second to last line we used the standard estimate for difference quotients from Lemma
2.11
.
As observed in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we may restrict the domain of integration in
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}
to
K_{r}^{+}:=K_{r}\cap\{\mathcal{U}(x,y)>0\}
.

To complete the proof it remains to bound
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}
from below. In view of the assumption

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(x,y)^{p-2}

\displaystyle=\big(|u_{h}(x)-u_{h}(y)|+|u(x)-u(y)|\big)^{p-2}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{p-2}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r+d})}^{p-2}|x-y|^{\gamma(p-2)}

for any
(x,y)\in K_{r}^{+}
. Using this inequality and Lemma
2.2
applied with
\gamma=\frac{q-p+2}{p}\geq 1
,
a=\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)
, and
b=\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)
(note that
C_{2}=\gamma=\frac{q-p+2}{p}
) in the preceding estimate, we find

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{p-2}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r+d})}^{p-2}\iint_{K_{r}}\frac{(|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|)^{q-p}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)-\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{p}}{(q-p+2)^{2}}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{p-2}\iint_{K_{r}}\frac{\big|V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))-V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y))\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

(3.21)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2^{2-p}q^{2}}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{p-2}\big[V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{2}_{W^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p,2}(B_{r})}.

In combination with the upper bound for
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}
, we conclude the proof.
∎

In contrast to Corollary
3.7
, we now additionally assume
u\in W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. This allows us to apply the higher order tail estimate from Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
. If
\theta
is large enough, we end up with a larger exponent of the increment
h
than in Corollary
3.7
.

Proposition 3.8

(Energy inequality for
W^{1+\theta,q}
-solutions)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in[p,\infty)
, and
\theta,\gamma\in(0,1)
.
Then there exists a constant
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)/s

such that whenever

u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
that satisfies

u\in W^{1+\theta,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\cap C_{\loc}^{0,\gamma}(\Omega),

then for every
0<r<R
,
d:=\frac{1}{4}(R-r)
,

B_{R+d}\equiv B_{R+d}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, and every step size
0<|h|\leq d
we have

\displaystyle\big[V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{2}_{W^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p,2}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+1+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\frac{R^{(1+\theta)q}}{1-s}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta(1-s)}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R+d)^{q}\bigg].

Note that the constant
C
remains stable as
p\uparrow 2
and blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.

Proof..

As in the proof of Proposition
3.6
we omit the reference to the center
x_{o}
. We consider
\eta\in\mathfrak{Z}_{r,R}
and proceed exactly as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
up to identity (
3.12
). The only difference consists in the fact that we replace
\eta^{p}
by
\eta^{2}
in the choice of the test function.
We also use the abbreviations

U_{h}(x,y):=u_{h}(x)-u_{h}(y)
and
U(x,y):=U_{0}(x,y)
and

\mathcal{U}(x,y):=|U_{h}(x,y)|+|U(x,y)|
. In this way, we obtain

\mathbf{I}=-2\mathbf{T},

where
\mathbf{I}
and
\mathbf{T}
are defiend by

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{R}}\!\!\frac{\big(V_{p-1}(U_{h}(x,y)){-}V_{p-1}(U(x,y))\big)\big([V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta^{2}](x){-}[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta^{2}](y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{B_{\frac{1}{2}(R+r)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{R})}\frac{\big(V_{p-1}(U_{h}(x,y))-V_{p-1}(U(x,y))\big)[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta^{2}](x)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

with
\delta=q-p+1\geq 1
.
The
local integral

\mathbf{I}
is estimated from below using Lemma
2.4
with
a=u_{h}(x)
,
b=u_{h}(y)
,
c=u(x)
,
d=u(y)
,
e=\eta(x)
, and
f=\eta(y)
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{p-1}{2^{q-p}}\mathbf{I}_{1}+\mathbf{I}_{2},

where
\mathbf{I}_{1}
and
\mathbf{I}_{2}
are defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{\mathcal{U}(x,y)^{p-2}(|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)|+|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|)^{q-p}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)-\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|^{2}\eta(x)\eta(y)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{\big(V_{p-1}(U_{h}(x,y))-V_{p-1}(U(x,y))\big)\big([V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta](x)+[V_{\delta}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\eta](y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\big(\eta(x)-\eta(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Joining the estimate from below for
\mathbf{I}
with the identity
\mathbf{I}=-2\mathbf{T}
, we obtain

\mathbf{I}_{1}\leq\tfrac{2^{q-p}}{p-1}\big[|\mathbf{I}_{2}|+|\mathbf{T}|\big].

To estimate
|\mathbf{T}|
we use Lemma
3.3
, while to estimate
|\mathbf{I}_{2}|
we use Lemma
3.4
. This implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-p+1+\theta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\frac{R^{(1+\theta)q}}{1-s}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R+d})}+\frac{R^{q}}{\theta(1-s)}\int_{B_{R+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}\bigg],

for a constant
C=C(N,p,q)
.
At this point it remains to estimate
\mathbf{I}_{1}
from below. Since
\eta=1
in
B_{r}
, we may decrease the domain of integration to
K_{r}
and omit
\eta
in the integrand, so that
\mathbf{I}_{1}\geq\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}
, where
\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}
is defined in (
3.20
) from the proof of Corollary
3.7
. In view of the regularity assumption
u\in C_{\loc}^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
, we subsequently may use inequality (
3.21
) from the proof of Corollary
3.7
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq\widetilde{\mathbf{I}}_{1}\geq\frac{1}{2^{2-p}q^{2}}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{r+h})}^{p-2}\iint_{K_{r}}\frac{\big|V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x))-V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y))\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{2-p}q^{2}}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{p-2}\big[V_{\frac{q-p+2}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{2}_{W^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p,2}(B_{r})}.

Together with the upper bound for
\mathbf{I}_{1}
this proves the energy inequality.
∎

3.3.
Energy inequality for
W^{s+\theta,p}
-solutions

In this subsection we prove the final energy inequality. It can be seen as a variant of Proposition
3.6
for the case
q=p
and in a situation where the existence of a weak derivative is not apriori assumed. In fact, this energy inequality will be used in Section
4
to show that
(s,p)
-harmonic functions have a weak gradient in

L^{p}_{\rm loc}
.
This is achieved by improving the fractional differentiability step by step in an iteration process.

Proposition 3.9

(Energy inequality for
W^{s+\theta,p}
-solutions)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
\theta\in[0,1-s)
.
There exist a constant
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s

such that whenever

u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
that satisfies

u\in W^{s+\theta,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

then for every
0<r<R
,
d:=\frac{1}{4}(R-r)
,

B_{R+d}\equiv B_{R+d}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, and every step size
0<|h|\leq d
we have

\displaystyle[\bm{\tau}_{h}u]^{p}_{W^{s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}),p}(B_{r})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp\frac{p}{2}}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}[u]_{W^{s+\theta,p}(B_{R})}^{p(1-\frac{p}{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\cdot\Bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}}{1-s}\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R+d)^{p}\Bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}.

Proof..

The starting point is the inequality (
3.19
) from the proof of Proposition
3.6
, applied with
q=p
and
\varepsilon=\theta-(1-s)\in(0,1)
. Note that the assumption
u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
in Proposition
3.6
has not been used up to this point. The inequality takes the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}

\displaystyle:=\iint_{K_{r}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u(x)-\bm{\tau}_{h}u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+[s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2})]p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\,\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp\frac{p}{2}}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[\iint_{K_{r+d}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+(s+\theta)p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}}{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\,\leq\frac{C}{sR^{sp\frac{p}{2}}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}[u]_{W^{s+\theta,p}(B_{R})}^{p(1-\frac{p}{2})}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}}{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
C=(N,p)
. This proves the claimed inequality.
∎

4.
W^{1,p}
-estimates

In this section we prove that
(s,p)
-harmonic functions possess a gradient
\nabla u
in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The main ingredient is the energy inequality from Proposition
3.9
, which is applied in the next lemma in order to obtain an estimate of the second finite difference of
u
in
L^{p}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
\theta\in[0,1-s)
. Then, there exists a constant
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s
such that whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
, which satisfies

u\in W^{s+\theta,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Big[R^{(s+\theta)p}(1-s)[u]_{W^{s+\theta,p}(B_{R})}^{p}+R^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{p}\Big]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

for any two concentric balls
B_{r}\subset B_{R}\Subset\Omega
,
0<r<R
, and any
0<|h|\leq d:=\frac{1}{7}(R-r)
.

Proof..

We apply the energy estimate from Proposition
3.9
with
\theta\in[0,1-s)
,

\tilde{r}=\frac{1}{7}(5r+2R)
, and

\widetilde{R}=\frac{1}{7}(r+6R)
instead of
r
and
R
.
Then,
d=\tfrac{1}{4}(\widetilde{R}-\tilde{r})=\tfrac{1}{7}(R-r)
. Moreover,
\frac{\widetilde{R}}{\widetilde{R}-\widetilde{r}}

and
\frac{1}{\widetilde{R}}
can be bounded in terms of

\frac{R}{R-r}
and
\frac{1}{R}
apart from a multiplicative factor. With these choices, the energy inequality becomes (note that
\widetilde{R}+d=R
)

\displaystyle[\bm{\tau}_{h}u]_{W^{s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}),p}(B_{\widetilde{r}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{sp\frac{p}{2}}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}[u]_{W^{s+\theta,p}(B_{\widetilde{R}})}^{p(1-\frac{p}{2})}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\bigg[\int_{B_{\widetilde{R}}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}}{1-s}\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s
.
In order to estimate the left-hand side of the above inequality from below we apply Lemma
2.12
with
w
,
q
,
\gamma
,
R
,
d
replaced by
\bm{\tau}_{h}u
,
p
,
s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2})
,

\widetilde{r}
,
d=\frac{1}{7}(R-r)
. With a constant
C=C(N,p)
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}-d}}|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}\bigg[\big(\underbrace{1-(s+\theta(1-\tfrac{p}{2}))}_{\leq\,1-s}\big)[\bm{\tau}_{h}u]_{W^{s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}),p}(B_{\widetilde{r}})}^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;+\frac{1}{sR^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}\|\bm{\tau}_{h}u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{\widetilde{r}})}\bigg],

for any
0<|\lambda|\leq d
and any
0<|h|\leq d
. To obtain the last line from the application of Lemma
2.12
, we used

s+\theta(1-\tfrac{p}{2})<1
and

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{r}^{(1-s-\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}}{d^{p}}+\frac{1}{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))d^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(p)}{sR^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}.

In the above inequality for
|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}
, we use (
4.1
) to bound the fractional semi-norm of
\bm{\tau}_{h}u
on the right-hand side and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}-d}}|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Bigg[(1-s)\Big[R^{(s+\theta)p}[u]_{W^{s+\theta,p}(B_{R})}^{p}\Big]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}}{1-s}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\theta(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\bigg[R^{(s+\theta)p}(1-s)[u]_{W^{s+\theta,p}(B_{R})}^{p}+\int_{B_{\widetilde{R}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}},

where
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s
. Letting
\lambda=h
and observing that
\widetilde{r}-d\geq r
as well as

\int_{B_{\widetilde{R}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 2^{p}R^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{p},

we conclude the proof.
∎

The next lemma is the core element for the proof of the
W^{1,p}
-regularity. The underlying idea is to apply Lemma
4.1
in an iteration argument in order to increase the order of fractional differentiability.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Then, there exists a constant
C\geq 1
depending on
N
,
p
and
s
, such that whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
,
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, and
r\in(0,R)
we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(1+\frac{4}{sp})(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
|h|\leq R-r
, where

(4.3)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}:=R^{sp}(1-s)[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}.

Moreover, the constant
C
is stable as
s\uparrow 1
.

Proof..

For
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we define sequences

\varrho_{i}:=r+\frac{R-r}{2^{i+1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sigma_{i}:=\tfrac{1}{4}spi.

First, we apply
Lemma
2.12
to
\big(u,p,s,d=\frac{1}{2}(R-r),\varrho_{o}\big)
instead of
\big(w,q,\gamma,d,R\big)
. Note that

\varrho_{o}+\frac{1}{2}(R-r)=R
. With a constant
C=C(N,p)
, Lemma
2.12
shows that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{o}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C|h|^{sp}\bigg[(1-s)[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p}+\Big(\frac{R^{(1-s)p}}{d^{p}}+\frac{1}{sd^{sp}}\Big)\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{sp}\bigg[R^{sp}(1-s)[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}^{p}+\Big(\frac{R^{p}}{d^{p}}+\frac{R^{sp}}{sd^{sp}}\Big)R^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{R})}^{p}\bigg]

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{o}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{sp}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p},

for any
0<|h|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(R-r)
. To obtain the last line we used the definitions of
d
and
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
. The constant
\widetilde{C}_{o}
has the form
\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s
.

Next, we apply Lemma
4.1
for given
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

with
R=\varrho_{i}
,
r=\varrho_{i+1}
,
d_{i}=\frac{1}{7}(\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1})=\frac{1}{7\cdot 2^{i+2}}(R-r)
, and
\theta=\sigma\in[0,1-s)
to be chosen later. This is only possible if the condition
u\in W^{s+\sigma,p}(B_{\varrho_{i}})
is fulfilled, which we will assume for the moment.
With a constant
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p)/s
, the application of Lemma
4.1

yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{i+1}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{\varrho_{i}}\Big)^{(s+\sigma(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}\Big(\frac{\varrho_{i}}{\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1}}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Big[\varrho_{i}^{(s+\sigma)p}(1-s)[u]^{p}_{W^{s+\sigma,p}(B_{\varrho_{i}})}+\varrho_{i}^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\varrho_{i}})}\Big]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\bigg[\int_{B_{\varrho_{i}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{\varrho_{i}}\Big)^{p}\varrho_{i}^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;\varrho_{i})^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
|h|\leq d_{i}
.
Observing that

R\geq\varrho_{i}=\frac{R}{2^{i+1}}+r\Big(1-\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}\Big)>\frac{R}{2^{i+1}},

and

\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1}=\frac{R-r}{2^{i+2}},

we have

\frac{1}{\varrho_{i}^{(s+\sigma(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}}\Big(\frac{\varrho_{i}}{\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1}}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\leq\frac{C(N,p)2^{(N+2p+1)i}}{R^{(s+\sigma(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

and by Remark
2.7
we have

\displaystyle\varrho_{i}^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;\varrho_{i})^{p}\leq\varrho_{i}^{N}\bigg[C(N)\Big(\frac{R}{\varrho_{i}}\Big)^{N}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}\leq C(N)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}2^{\frac{N}{p-1}(i+1)}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{p}.

With these remarks, (
4.5
) immediately becomes

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{i+1}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\sigma(1-\frac{p}{2}))p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\Big[R^{(s+\sigma)p}(1-s)[u]^{p}_{W^{s+\sigma,p}(B_{\varrho_{i}})}+\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}\Big]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\bigg[\int_{B_{\varrho_{i}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
|h|\leq d_{i}
, provided that
u\in W^{s+\sigma,p}(B_{\varrho_{i}})
, where
\sigma\in[0,1-s)
, and with a constant
C_{i}
of the form

C_{i}=\bm{c}\,\bm{b}^{i+1},\quad\mbox{where $\bm{c}=\bm{c}(N,p,s)=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p)}{s}$\,,\, $\bm{b}=\bm{b}(N,p)=2^{\frac{Np}{p-1}+3p+1}$.}

Later in the proof, we will iterate inequality (
4.6
). However, we start by considering the inequality for
i=0
. In fact, we apply (
4.6
)
i=0
with
\sigma=0
. This is possible, since the regularity assumption
u\in W^{s,p}(B_{\varrho_{o}})
is granted.
Subsequently, we use inequality (
4.4
), the definition of
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
in (
4.3
) and the fact that
\frac{|h|}{R}\leq 1
, which allows us to reduce the exponent from
p
to
sp
. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{1}}}

\displaystyle|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{sp}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\Big[\underbrace{R^{sp}(1-s)[u]^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{\varrho_{o}})}}_{\leq\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}}+\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}\Big]^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\bigg[\int_{B_{\varrho_{o}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}2^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{sp}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[\widetilde{C}_{o}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{sp}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathfrak{D}_{i_{o}-1}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{i_{o}(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}.

With (
4.13
)
{}_{i_{o}-1}
we estimate
\mathbf{I}_{2}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}-1}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{i_{o}(N+sp+1)}+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\bigg]\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}-1}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{i_{o}(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}.

Here, to obtain the last line we used (
4.11
),
i.e.
s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}}<1
, in order to reduce the exponent of
\frac{|h|}{R}
. Plugging these estimates back and recalling that
\mathfrak{D}_{i_{o}-1}=C_{\ast}\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{i_{o}}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\underbrace{2C_{\ast}^{-\frac{p}{2}}}_{\leq\,1}\underbrace{C_{i_{o}-1}\mathfrak{D}_{i_{o}-1}}_{\leq\,\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}-1}(1-\frac{p}{2})+(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}})\frac{p}{2})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(i_{o}+1)(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}.

The exponent of
\frac{|h|}{R}
can be simplified. In fact, we have

\displaystyle s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}-1}(1-\tfrac{p}{2})+(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}})\tfrac{p}{2}

\displaystyle=s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}-1}(1-\tfrac{p}{2})+(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}-1}+\alpha\tfrac{1}{4}sp)\tfrac{p}{2}

\displaystyle>s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}-1}+\tfrac{1}{2}sp

\displaystyle>s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1}.

Therefore, for any
h\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
with
|h|\leq d_{i_{o}-1}
we get

(4.17)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{i_{o}}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(i_{o}+1)(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}.

Recall that the exponent
s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1}
satisfies (
4.12
), i.e.

s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1}>1+\tfrac{1}{20}sp.

Therefore, we can apply (
2.8
) from Lemma
2.14
on
B_{\varrho_{i}}
, i.e. the case in which the exponent
\sigma

is greater than 1, with
(p,s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1},\varrho_{i_{o}-1},\varrho_{i_{o}},d_{i_{o}})
instead of
(q,\gamma,R,r,d)
and with

M^{p}:=\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}}{R^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1})p}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(i_{o}+1)(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}.

Note that (
4.17
) plays the role of the hypothesis (
2.6
)
σ>1
in Lemma
2.14
. Using also the
L^{\infty}
-bound for
u
to estimate the integral of
|u|^{p}
on
B_{\varrho_{i_{o}}}
in terms of
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}
, the definition of
d_{i_{o}}
, and
p\leq(i_{o}+2)(N+sp+1)
we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\varrho_{i_{o}}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(p)|h|^{p}\bigg[\Big(\frac{M}{s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1}-1}\Big)^{p}d_{i_{o}}^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1}-1)p}+\frac{1}{d_{i_{o}}^{p}}\int_{B_{\varrho_{i_{o}}}}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C(p)|h|^{p}\bigg[\frac{M^{p}}{(\tfrac{1}{20}sp)^{p}}R^{(s+\alpha\sigma_{i_{o}+1}-1)p}+\Big(\frac{7\cdot 2^{i_{o}+2}}{R-r}\Big)^{p}2^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{28^{p}2^{N}C(p)}{s^{p}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(i_{o}+1)(N+sp+1)}\big[\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}+2^{i_{o}p}\big]\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{28^{p}2^{N+1}C(p)\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}}{s^{p}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{\frac{4}{sp}(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ast}\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{\frac{4}{sp}(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

for every
0<|h|\leq d_{i_{o}}
.
Here, to obtain the second last line we used
i_{o}+1<\frac{4}{sp}
, which follows from (
4.8
), since

i_{o}+1<\frac{4(1-s)}{sp}+2=\frac{4}{sp}-\frac{4-2p}{p}\leq\frac{4}{sp}.

The constant in the final estimate above has the form

C_{\ast}\mathfrak{C}_{i_{o}}=\mathfrak{D}_{i_{o}}=2\bm{c}^{i_{o}}\bm{b}^{1+2+\dots+i_{o}}C_{\ast}^{2i_{o}}\widetilde{C}_{o}\leq 2\bm{c}^{\frac{4}{sp}-1}\bm{b}^{\frac{2}{sp}(\frac{4}{sp}-1)}C_{\ast}^{2(\frac{4}{sp}-1)}\widetilde{C}_{o}=:C(N,p,s).

We denote this constant by
C
. Note that
C
blows up as
s\downarrow 0
. Moreover,
C
blows up as
p\downarrow 1
and remains stable as
p\uparrow 2
. It is also stable as
s\uparrow s_{o}
. Taking into account that
\varrho_{i_{o}}\geq r
, this proves inequality (
4.2
) in the case
i_{o}\geq 2
for any
0<|h|\leq d_{i_{o}}
.

Finally, it remains to establish (
4.2
) when
d_{i_{o}}<|h|\leq R-r
. However, in this case we have
\frac{R}{|h|}<\frac{R}{d_{i_{o}}}=7\cdot 2^{i_{o}+2}\frac{R}{R-r}
and hence

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Cr^{N}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq 2^{i_{o}}C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p},

where
C=C(N)
. Recalling that
p\leq\frac{4}{sp}(N+sp+1)
and
i_{o}<\frac{4}{sp}-1
this proves (
4.2
) and finishes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Combination of Lemma
4.2
with the standard estimate for difference quotients from Lemma
2.10
immediately yields that
(s,p)
-harmonic functions are of class
W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Theorem
1.1
is an immediate consequence of Corollary
4.3
applied with the choice

.

Corollary 4.3

(
W^{1,p}
-regularity)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Then, whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s)
such that for any
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
and
r\in(0,R)
the quantitative
W^{1,p}
-estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{p}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{\frac{4}{sp}(N+sp+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{p}

holds true, where
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
is defined in (
4.3
).
Moreover, the constant
C
is stable as
s\uparrow 1
. It blows up as
s\downarrow 0
or
p\downarrow 1
.

5.
Higher gradient regularity

In the previous section we established that the weak gradient of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions exists in
L^{p}_{\rm loc}
.
The aim in this section is to improve the integrability of the gradient
\nabla u
to any exponent
q\geq p
. This will be achieved by a Moser-type iteration argument. The following lemma is the first step in this direction.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
, and
q\in[p,\infty)
. For any

(5.1)

\varepsilon\in(0,1-s)

there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q,\varepsilon)
such that whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
that satisfies

u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

then for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
and any
r\in(0,R)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+sp\frac{p}{2}-\varepsilon p(1-\frac{p}{2})}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where

(5.2)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}:=R^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{q}.

The constant
C
has the form

(5.3)

C=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s}\Big(\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

Proof..

We apply the energy inequality from Proposition
3.6
with
r,R,d
replaced by
\tilde{r}=\frac{1}{7}(5r+2R)
,

\widetilde{R}=\frac{1}{7}(r+6R)
,
d=\frac{1}{4}(\widetilde{R}-\tilde{r})=\frac{1}{7}(R-r)
. Note that
\widetilde{R}+d=R
and

\frac{\widetilde{R}}{\widetilde{R}-\widetilde{r}}

and
\frac{1}{\widetilde{R}}
can be bounded in terms of

\frac{R}{R-r}
and
\frac{1}{R}
apart from a multiplicative factor. With these choices Proposition
3.6
yields

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle:=\big[V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{p}_{W^{(1-\varepsilon)(1-\frac{p}{2})+s\frac{p}{2},p}(B_{\widetilde{r}})}

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{C}{R^{[(1-\varepsilon)(1-\frac{p}{2})+s\frac{p}{2}]p}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-(1-\frac{p}{2})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where the constant
C
has the form

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s(1-s)^{\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{(1-\frac{p}{2})}}.

Note that
\widetilde{C}
blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.
Now, we apply Lemma
2.12
to
w=V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)
on
B_{\widetilde{r}}
with
q=p
,
d=\frac{1}{7}(R-r)
, and

\gamma=(1-\varepsilon)(1-\tfrac{p}{2})+s\tfrac{p}{2}=1-(1-s)\tfrac{p}{2}-\varepsilon(1-\tfrac{p}{2}),

to deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}-d}}

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C|\lambda|^{\gamma p}\bigg[(1-\gamma)\mathbf{I}+\bigg(\frac{\widetilde{R}^{(1-\gamma)p}}{d^{p}}+\frac{1}{\gamma d^{\gamma p}}\bigg)\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}\big|V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{\gamma p}\bigg[(1-\gamma)R^{\gamma p}\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg],

for any
0<|\lambda|\leq d
, where
C=C(N,p)
stands for the constant from Lemma
2.12
. We reduce the domain of integration on the left-hand side from
B_{\tilde{r}-d}
to
B_{r}
. Using (
5.1
) we have
\gamma>s
and hence
1-\gamma<1-s
. Moreover, we use the estimate for
\mathbf{I}
from above and the standard estimate for difference quotients from Lemma
2.11
and the definition of
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
. In this way, we obtain for any
0<|\lambda|\leq d
that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{\gamma p}\bigg[\frac{1-s}{s(1-s)^{\frac{p}{2}}\varepsilon^{(1-\frac{p}{2})}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-(1-\frac{p}{2})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{s}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{p}R^{q}\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{\gamma p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bigg[\frac{(1-s)^{(1-\frac{p}{2})}}{\varepsilon^{(1-\frac{p}{2})}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-(1-\frac{p}{2})p}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}+\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{\gamma p}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-(1-\frac{p}{2})p}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where
C=C(N,p,q)
. In turn, we also used
p\leq N+sp+1
.
Here we choose
\lambda=h
and compute the exponent

\gamma p+q-\big(1-\tfrac{p}{2}\big)p=\Big[1-(1-s)\tfrac{p}{2}-\varepsilon\big(1-\tfrac{p}{2}\big)\Big]p+q-\big(1-\tfrac{p}{2}\big)p=q+sp\tfrac{p}{2}-\varepsilon p\big(1-\tfrac{p}{2}\big).

Since
\frac{q}{p}\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{h}\big(V_{\frac{q}{p}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|\geq|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{\frac{q}{p}}\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r}$,}

so that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{1-s}{\varepsilon}\Big)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+sp\frac{p}{2}-\varepsilon p(1-\frac{p}{2})}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

which proves the claim.
∎

The next proposition is an application of Lemma
5.1
. The estimate for second finite differences is translated into fractional differentiability of the gradient.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
and
q\in[p,\infty)
. Then, whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
that satisfies

u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega),

we have

\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for any $\alpha\in(0,\beta)$, where $\beta:=\tfrac{sp}{2}\cdot\frac{p}{q}$.}

Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q,\alpha)
such that for any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
and for any
r\in(0,R)
, we have

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}^{q}\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
is defined in (
5.2
) and the constant is of the form
C=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s\alpha\beta^{q}(\beta-\alpha)^{2}(1-\alpha)^{q}}
.

Proof..

We apply Lemma
5.1
with
r
replaced by
\tilde{r}=\frac{1}{11}(7r+4R)
. We leave the larger radius
R
unchanged in the application.
Taking into account
R-\tilde{r}=\frac{7}{11}(R-r)
, we obtain for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1-s)
that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}\big|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+sp\frac{p}{2}-\varepsilon p(1-\frac{p}{2})}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

for any
0<|h|\leq d=\frac{1}{7}(R-\tilde{r})=\frac{1}{11}(R-r)
, and where
C
has the same structure as the constant in (
5.3
) from Lemma
5.1
. We choose some
\alpha\in(0,\beta)
and let

\varepsilon:=\min\big\{\tfrac{1}{2}(1-s),\tfrac{1}{2-p}\tfrac{q}{p}(\beta-\alpha)\big\},

so that

sp\tfrac{p}{2}-\varepsilon p(1-\tfrac{p}{2})\geq\tfrac{1}{2}(\alpha+\beta)q=:\tilde{\beta}q.

Reducing the exponent of
|h|
in (
5.4
) if necessary, which is allowed due to the inequality
|h|\leq R
, we find

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}\big|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{(1+\tilde{\beta})q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

The constant
C_{1}
takes the form

C_{1}=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s}\bigg(\frac{1-s}{\min\big\{\frac{1}{2}(1-s),\tfrac{1}{2-p}\tfrac{q}{p}(\beta-\alpha)\big\}}\bigg)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s(\beta-\alpha)}.

Estimate (
5.5
) plays the role of assumption (
2.9
) in Lemma
2.15
with

M^{q}:=\frac{C_{1}}{R^{(1+\tilde{\beta})q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

which we apply on
B_{\tilde{r}}
; note that
\tilde{r}=r+4d
. The other parameters of Lemma
2.15
are fixed by
\gamma=\tilde{\beta}
,
q=q
,
d=\frac{1}{11}(R-r)
. Finally,
\alpha
takes the role of
\beta
; we have
\tilde{\beta}-\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha)
. The application is allowed due to the assumption
W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Lemma
2.15
ensures that
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})
with the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C(N,q)\,d^{(\tilde{\beta}-\alpha)q}}{\frac{1}{2}(\beta-\alpha)\tilde{\beta}^{q}(1-\tilde{\beta})^{q}}\bigg[M^{q}+\frac{\tilde{r}^{q}}{\alpha d^{(1+\tilde{\beta})q}}\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C(N,q)}{\alpha(\beta-\alpha)\beta^{q}(1-\alpha)^{q}}\Bigg[\frac{C_{1}d^{(\tilde{\beta}-\alpha)q}}{R^{(1+\tilde{\beta})q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}+\frac{1}{d^{(1+\alpha)q}}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C(N,q)}{\alpha(\beta-\alpha)\beta^{q}(1-\alpha)^{q}}\bigg[\frac{C_{1}}{R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}+\frac{1}{d^{(1+\alpha)q}}\bigg]\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C(N,q)C_{1}}{\alpha(\beta-\alpha)\beta^{q}(1-\alpha)^{q}R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\bigg[\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}+\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(1+\alpha)q}\bigg]\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{C(N,q)C_{1}}{\alpha(\beta-\alpha)\beta^{q}(1-\alpha)^{q}R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle\qquad\equiv\frac{C_{2}}{R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

Here to obtain the second-to-last line we used
(1+\alpha)q<N+sp+q
. Note that the constant
C_{2}
takes the form

C_{2}=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s\alpha(\beta-\alpha)^{2}\beta^{q}(1-\alpha)^{q}}.

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

By the Sobolev embedding for fractional Sobolev spaces the fractional differentiability of the gradient obtained in Proposition
5.2
leads to higher integrability of the gradient.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
and
q\in[p,\infty)
. There exists a constant
C
depending on
N
,
p
,
s
and
q
, such that whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
, that satisfies

u\in W^{1,q}(B_{R})

for some
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
, then for any
r\in(0,R)
we have

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-\alpha q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{N-\alpha q}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{N}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{M}}^{q},

where
\alpha=\frac{sp^{2}}{4q}
and

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{M}}:=\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}+\frac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R).

The constant
C
has the form

C=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{q+4}}
.

Proof..

We apply Proposition
5.2
with the choice
\alpha=\tfrac{sp^{2}}{4q}\in(0,\beta)
, where
\beta=\tfrac{sp^{2}}{2q}
and infer that
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Moreover, the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}^{q}\leq C_{2}R^{N-\alpha q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{M}}^{q}

holds. Note that
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}\leq C(N)R^{N+q}\bm{\mathfrak{M}}^{q}
, where
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
is defined in (
5.2
) and the constant is of the form

C_{2}=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s\alpha\beta^{q}(\beta-\alpha)^{2}(1-\alpha)^{q}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{q+4}}.

By the Sobolev embedding for fractional Sobolev spaces from Lemma
2.9
– note that the application is permitted since
N-\alpha q>0
– we conclude that
\nabla u\in L^{\frac{Nq}{N-\alpha q}}(B_{r})
together with the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{\frac{Nq}{N-\alpha q}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{N-\alpha q}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm Sob}\bigg[r^{\alpha q-N}[\nabla u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}^{q}+-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm Sob}\bigg[C_{2}\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{N-\alpha q}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}+\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{N}\bigg]\bm{\mathfrak{M}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{\rm Sob}C_{2}\Big(\frac{R}{r}\Big)^{N}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+sp}\bm{\mathfrak{M}}^{q}.

The constant
2C_{\rm Sob}C_{2}
has the structure

\displaystyle 2C_{\rm Sob}C_{2}=\frac{C(N,q)(1-\alpha)}{(N-\alpha q)^{q-1}}\cdot\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{q+4}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{q+4}}.

To obtain the last line we used
N-\alpha q=N-\frac{1}{4}sp^{2}\geq N-1\geq 1
.
This proves the claimed inequality.
∎

Lemma
5.3
allows to set up a Moser-type iteration scheme that improves for any given
q\in[p,\infty)
the regularity of an
(s,p)
-harmonic function from
W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
to
W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 5.4

(
W^{1,q}
-gradient regularity)
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
and
s\in(0,1)
. Then, whenever
u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{for any $q\in[p,\infty)$.}

Moreover, for any
q\geq p
there exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q)
, such that on any ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
the quantitative
L^{q}
-gradient estimate

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R/2}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Bigg]

holds true. The constant
C
is stable as
s\uparrow 1
. It blows up as
p\downarrow 1
.

Proof..

In view of Theorem
1.1
we know that
u\in W^{1,p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.
Based on the quantitative higher integrability from Lemma
5.3
we set up an iteration argument in order to iteratively increase the integrability exponent. To this end, we define a sequence
(q_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of exponents, a sequence
(\varrho_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of radii, and the associated sequence of shrinking concentric balls
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle q_{o}:=p,\qquad q_{i}=\frac{Nq_{i-1}}{N-\frac{1}{4}sp^{2}}=\bigg(\frac{N}{N-\frac{1}{4}sp^{2}}\bigg)^{i}p,\\[12.0pt]
\displaystyle\varrho_{i}:=\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{2^{i+1}},\qquad B_{i}=B_{\varrho_{i}}.\end{array}\right.

Clearly
q_{i}\geq p
is an increasing sequence and
q_{i}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
. For
i\in\mathbb{N}
we apply Lemma
5.3
with
r=\varrho_{i}
,
R=\varrho_{i-1}
,
q=q_{i-1}
, and
\alpha=\frac{1}{4}\frac{sp^{2}}{q_{i-1}}
.
This amounts to

(5.6)

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}\Big(\frac{\varrho_{i-1}}{\varrho_{i}}\Big)^{\frac{N}{q_{i-1}}}\Big(\frac{\varrho_{i-1}}{\varrho_{i-1}-\varrho_{i}}\Big)^{\frac{N+sp}{q_{i-1}}+1}\bm{\mathfrak{M}}_{i-1},

where we abbreviated

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{M}}_{i-1}

\displaystyle:=\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i-1}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}+\frac{1}{\varrho_{i-1}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;\varrho_{i-1}).

To proceed further, we estimate the numerical factors and the tail term in the above inequality. Indeed, we have

\frac{\varrho_{i-1}}{\varrho_{i}}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{1}{2^{i}}R}{\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}R}<2,\quad\quad\frac{R}{\varrho_{i-1}}=\frac{R}{\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{1}{2^{i}}R}\leq 2,

and

\frac{\varrho_{i-1}}{\varrho_{i-1}-\varrho_{i}}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{1}{2^{i}}R}{\frac{1}{2^{i}}R-\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}R}=2^{i+1}(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2^{i}})\leq 2^{i+2}.

Moreover, by Remark
2.7
we have

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;\varrho_{i-1})\leq C(N)\Big(\frac{R}{\varrho_{i-1}}\Big)^{\frac{N}{p-1}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\leq C(N,p)\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R).

Using these estimates in (
5.6
)
i
, we obtain for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}}

\displaystyle|\nabla u|^{q_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}2^{\frac{N}{q_{i-1}}}\big(2^{i+2}\big)^{\frac{N+sp}{q_{i-1}}+1}\Bigg[\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i-1}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}+\frac{C(N,p)}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C(N,p)\underbrace{2^{\big[\frac{N+sp}{q_{i-1}}+1\big](i-1)}}_{\leq 2^{(N+2)(i-1)}}C^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}\Bigg[\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i-1}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}+\frac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Bigg]

(5.7)

\displaystyle=:C_{i-1}\Bigg[\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i-1}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}+\frac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Bigg].

Recalling that

\displaystyle C^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q_{i-1})}{s^{q_{i-1}+4}}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q_{i-1})}{s^{5}},

we obtain that
C_{i-1}
has the structure

C_{i-1}=\frac{2^{(N+2)(i-1)}\widetilde{C}(N,p,q_{i-1})}{s^{5}}.

Iterating (
5.7
) results in

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Bigg],

where
C=i\prod_{j=0}^{i-1}C_{j}
.
Since
q_{i}\to\infty
as
i\to\infty
there exists
i_{o}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
q_{i_{o}-1}<q\leq q_{i_{o}}
. This fixes
i_{o}\in\mathbb{N}
in dependence on
N
,
p
,
s
, and
q
. Enlarging the domain of integration from
B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
to
B_{i_{o}}
and using Hölder’s inequality, we finally get

\displaystyle\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{N}{q}}\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{i_{o}}}|\nabla u|^{q_{i_{o}}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{q_{i_{o}}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\bigg[-\mskip-19.5mu\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Bigg],

where
C=C(N,p,s,q)
.
∎

At this point Theorem
1.2
can be achieved by combining Theorem
4.3
and Proposition
5.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

First we apply Theorem
5.4
on the balls
B_{\frac{1}{2}R}
and
B_{\frac{3}{4}R}
, which is possible after slightly changing the radii. Subsequently we use Corollary
4.3
to estimate the
L^{p}
norm of
\nabla u
and Lemma
2.6
to increase in the tail term the radius
\tfrac{3}{4}R
to
R
. In this way, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{R/2})}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{N}{q}}\Big[R^{-\frac{N}{p}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(B_{\frac{3}{4}R})}+\tfrac{1}{R}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}\big(u;\tfrac{3}{4}R\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{N}{q}-1}\Big[R^{s-\frac{N}{p}}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Big].

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

By Morrey embedding the
W^{1,q}
-regularity from Theorem
1.2
immediately implies that
(s,p)
-harmonic functions are Hölder continuous for any Hölder exponent
\gamma\in(0,1)
. This is exactly the content of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From Theorem
5.4
we know that
u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
q\geq p
. Therefore, by Morrey embedding, Lemma
2.19
, we conclude that
u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
. Now, fix some
\gamma\in(0,1)
and consider a ball
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
. Applying in turn Lemma
2.19
with the choice
q=\frac{N}{1-\gamma}
and Theorem
5.4
, we obtain the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}

\displaystyle=[u]_{C^{0,1-\frac{N}{q}}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\frac{N}{q}-1}\Big[R^{s-\frac{N}{p}}(1{-}s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Big].

Recalling the choice of
q
, we conclude the claimed inequality.
∎

6.
Fractional differentiability of the gradient

In this section we prove, as stated in Theorem
1.4
, that the gradient of
(s,p)
-harmonic functions exhibits a certain fractional differentiability at each
L^{q}
-scale with
q\geq 2
. Particularly, for
q=2
we have
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
0<\alpha<\min\{\frac{1}{2}sp,1-(1-s)p\}
with a quantitative local estimate which remains stable as
s\uparrow 1
. It is straightforward to check that the upper bound of
\alpha
tends to
1
as
s\uparrow 1
.
Therefore, we formally recover the
W^{2,2}
-regularity obtained in
[
54
]
and
[
3
]
for
p
-harmonic functions in the singular case
p\in(1,2)
.

We start with two preparatory lemmata. The first one ensures that
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\loc}
for any
q\geq p
and
\alpha<\frac{sp}{q}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in[2,\infty)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{1-s,\frac{1}{2}sp\}]
, and
\tilde{\varepsilon}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}sp)
.
Whenever

u
is a locally bounded
(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition
2.1
, we have

\nabla u\in W^{\beta,q}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\text{where}\quad\beta:=\frac{sp-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}\in(0,1).

Moreover, there exists
C=C(N,p,s,q,\tilde{\varepsilon})
, such that for every ball

B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
and
r\in(0,R)
, we have

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\beta)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=R^{N+(1-\varepsilon)q}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{R})}+R^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{q}.

Proof..

By Theorem
1.2
we have
u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
. Moreover, from Theorem
1.3
we know that
u\in C_{\rm loc}^{0,1-\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. For
\varepsilon
as in
the statement, we set
\gamma:=1-\varepsilon
for ease of notation.
Fix
0<r<R
and introduce
\tilde{r}=\frac{1}{7}(5r+2R)
,

\widetilde{R}=\frac{1}{7}(r+6R)
, and
d=\frac{1}{4}(\widetilde{R}-\tilde{r})=\frac{1}{7}(R-r)
.
First of all, by Hölder’s inequality, we have

(6.1)

\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q+p-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C(N)R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q+p-2}{q}}.

Next, we apply Corollary
3.7
with
(\tilde{r},\widetilde{R},\vartheta,q+p-2,1-\varepsilon)
in place of
(r,R,\theta,q,\gamma)
. Note that
\widetilde{R}+d=R
and that

\frac{\widetilde{R}}{\widetilde{R}-\tilde{r}}

and
\frac{1}{\widetilde{R}}
can be bounded in terms of

\frac{R}{R-r}
and
\frac{1}{R}
apart from a multiplicative factor, use (
6.1
), and the definition of
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
to obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle:=\big[V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{2}_{W^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p,2}(B_{\tilde{r}})}

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+p-2}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\Bigg[R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}+q+p-2}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q+p-2}{q}}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{q+p-2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+p-2}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q+p-2}.

Estimating
[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}
in terms of
R^{-(\frac{N}{q}+\gamma)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
, we obtain

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+p-2}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

Here, the constant
C
has the form
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)/s
.
Let us apply Lemma
2.12
to
w=V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)
on
B_{\tilde{r}}
with
(q,d,\gamma)
in that lemma replaced by
(2,d=\frac{1}{7}(R-r),\widetilde{\gamma})
, where

(6.3)

\widetilde{\gamma}=\gamma-\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p=\big(1-\tfrac{1}{2}p\big)\gamma+\tfrac{1}{2}ps\in[\tfrac{1}{2}ps,\gamma],

and deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}-d}}

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(N)|\lambda|^{2\widetilde{\gamma}}\Bigg[(1-\widetilde{\gamma})\mathbf{I}+\bigg(\frac{\tilde{r}^{2(1-\widetilde{\gamma})}}{d^{2}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{\gamma}d^{2\widetilde{\gamma}}}\bigg)\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}\big|V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]

for any
0<|\lambda|\leq d
. Since
\varepsilon<1-s
we have

(6.4)

1-\widetilde{\gamma}=\tfrac{1}{2}p(1-s)+(1-\tfrac{1}{2}p)\varepsilon\leq 1-s.

Moreover, we observe

. Therefore, the last inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r+d}}

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(N)|\lambda|^{2\gamma-(\gamma-s)p}\bigg[(1-s)\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{sR^{sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

The integral on the right-hand side is estimated by Lemma
2.11
, that is

\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}R^{q}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+p-2}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

while
\mathbf{I}
is bounded by (

). Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r+d}}\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{sp+\gamma(2-p)}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+p-2}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where
C=C(N,p,q)
.
In the above estimate, we choose
\lambda=h
. Furthermore, since
q\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{h}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|\geq|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{\frac{q}{2}}\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r+d}$.}

Therefore, for any
0<|h|\leq d
we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r+d}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+p-2+sp+\gamma(2-p)}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+sp-\varepsilon(2-p)}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{s}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+sp-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where
C=C(N,p,q)
.
Apply Lemma
2.15
with
\gamma
replaced by

\widetilde{\beta}=\frac{sp-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}

and with

\displaystyle M^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{s}\frac{1}{R^{q(1+\widetilde{\beta})}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

and
\frac{1}{4}d
in place of

.
Moreover, in the application we fix

by

\displaystyle\beta:=\frac{sp-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q},

such that

and
\widetilde{\beta}-\beta=\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2q}
. In this setup, Lemma
2.15
yields
that
\nabla u\in W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})
with the
quantitative estimate

(6.5)

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N,q)d^{q(\widetilde{\beta}-\beta)}}{(\widetilde{\beta}-\beta)\widetilde{\beta}^{q}(1-\widetilde{\beta})^{q}}\bigg[M^{q}+\frac{(r+d)^{q}}{\beta d^{q(1+\widetilde{\beta})}}\int_{B_{r+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

), we note that

and use the elementary inequalities

Inserting this in the

-estimate (

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\beta)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

This is the desired estimate. Note that

, but remains stable for

. Moreover, the constant remains stable as

.
∎

In contrast to Lemma

, in the next result we additionally assume that

for some

. If

is large enough and

, this leads to an improvement in fractional differentiability. Nevertheless, we state the lemma for the larger range

. In this larger range, it is guaranteed that the parameter

, which expresses the fractional differentiability, is positive.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
,
s\in(\frac{p-1}{p},1)
,
q\in[2,\infty)
,
\theta\in(0,1)
,

, and

.
Whenever

(s,p)
-harmonic function in the sense of Definition

we have

\nabla u\in W^{\beta,q}_{\loc}(\Omega)\quad\text{where}\quad\beta:=\frac{1-(1-s)p+\theta-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}\in(0,1).

Moreover, there exists
C=C(N,p,s,q,\theta,\tilde{\varepsilon})
, such that for every ball

B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
and
r\in(0,R)
, we have

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\beta)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

\displaystyle:=R^{N+(1-\varepsilon)q}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{R})}+R^{(1+\theta)q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}+R^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q}

and

\bm{\mathfrak{T}}:=\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)
.

Proof..

By Theorem
1.3
we have
u\in C_{\rm loc}^{0,1-\varepsilon}(\Omega)
. To keep some of the exponents as simple as possible, we use the abbreviations
\gamma:=1-\varepsilon
and
\vartheta:=\theta-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}
.
Let
0<r<R
and abbreviate
\tilde{r}=\frac{1}{7}(5r+2R)
,

\widetilde{R}=\frac{1}{7}(r+6R)
, and
d=\frac{1}{4}(\widetilde{R}-\tilde{r})=\frac{1}{7}(R-r)
.
First of all, we note that for
p\in(1,2)
and since

, by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.5

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{q+p-2}_{W^{\vartheta,q+p-2}(B_{R})}=\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u(x)-\nabla u(y)|^{q+p-2}}{|x-y|^{N+\vartheta(q+p-2)}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad\leq\bigg[\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{|\nabla u(x)-\nabla u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+\theta q}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\bigg]^{\frac{q+p-2}{q}}\bigg[\iint_{K_{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{N-\frac{q(\theta-\vartheta)(q+p-2)}{2-p}}}\bigg]^{\frac{2-p}{q}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C(N,q)}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}+(\theta-\vartheta)(q+p-2)}[\nabla u]^{q+p-2}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}.

If
p=2
, the argument is simpler, since
|x-y|^{q(\theta-\vartheta)}
is bounded by
[2R]^{q(\theta-\vartheta)}
, and thus the exponent of
|x-y|
can be increased from
N+\vartheta q
to
N+\theta q
. In any case, the last two inequalities are valid for any
p\in(1,2]
. Apply Proposition
3.8
with
(\tilde{r},\widetilde{R},\vartheta,q+p-2
in place of
(r,R,\theta,q)
and with
\gamma=1-\varepsilon
, note that
\widetilde{R}+d=R
and

\frac{\widetilde{R}}{\widetilde{R}-\widetilde{r}}

and
\frac{1}{\widetilde{R}}
can be bounded in terms of

\frac{R}{R-r}
and
\frac{1}{R}
apart from a multiplicative factor. Subsequently, we use Hölder’s inequality as in (
6.1
), the above estimate, and the definition of
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle:=\big[V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big]^{2}_{W^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}(\gamma-s)p,2}(B_{\tilde{r}})}

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+sp+1}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\Bigg[R^{(1+\vartheta)(q+p-2)}[\nabla u]^{q+p-2}_{W^{\vartheta,q+p-2}(B_{R})}+\frac{R^{q+p-2}}{\vartheta}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q+p-2}\,\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q+p-2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{C}{(1-s)R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}

\displaystyle\ \phantom{\leq\,}\cdot\Bigg[\frac{R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}+(1+\theta)(q+p-2)}}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}[\nabla u]^{q+p-2}_{W^{\theta,q}(B_{R})}

\displaystyle\ \qquad\,+\frac{R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}+q+p-2}}{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\bigg[\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]^{\frac{q+p-2}{q}}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}^{q+p-2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\ \leq\frac{C}{(1-s)\tilde{\varepsilon}R^{sp}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}^{2-p}R^{\frac{N(2-p)}{q}}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q+p-2}.

In turn, we used
\vartheta=\theta-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}\geq 2\tilde{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}=\frac{3}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}
.
Keeping in mind that
[u]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R})}
can be estimated by
R^{-[\frac{N}{q}+\gamma]}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
, we obtain

(6.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{(1-s)\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{R^{sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

The constant
C
has the structure
C=\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)/s
and
\widetilde{C}
is stable as
p\uparrow 2
and blows up as
p\downarrow 1
; see Proposition
3.8
.

Let us apply Lemma
2.12
to
w=V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)
on
B_{\widetilde{r}}
with
(q,d,\gamma)
replaced by
(2,d=\frac{1}{7}(R-r),\widetilde{\gamma})
, where
\tilde{\gamma}
is defined in (
6.3
) and deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tilde{r}-d}}

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(N)|\lambda|^{2\widetilde{\gamma}}\Bigg[(1-\widetilde{\gamma})\mathbf{I}+\bigg(\frac{\tilde{r}^{2(1-\widetilde{\gamma})}}{d^{2}}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{\gamma}d^{2\widetilde{\gamma}}}\bigg)\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}\big|V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]

for any
0<|\lambda|\leq d
. Since
\varepsilon<1-s
we have
1-\widetilde{\gamma}\leq 1-s
; see (
6.4
). Moreover,
\tilde{r}-d=r+d
.
Therefore, the last inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r+d}}

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(N)|\lambda|^{2\gamma-(\gamma-s)p}\bigg[(1-s)\mathbf{I}+\frac{1}{sR^{sp+\gamma(2-p)}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{2}\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

The integral on the right-hand side is estimated by Lemma
2.11
, that is

\int_{B_{\widetilde{r}}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q}R^{q}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

while
\mathbf{I}
is bounded by (
6.6
). We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r+d}}\big|\bm{\tau}_{\lambda}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{s\tilde{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{|\lambda|}{R}\Big)^{sp+\gamma(2-p)}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where
C=C(N,p,q)
.
Here we choose
\lambda=h
. Since
q\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\big|\bm{\tau}_{h}\big(V_{\frac{q}{2}}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)\big)\big|\geq|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{\frac{q}{2}}\qquad\mbox{in $B_{r+d}$.}

Therefore, for any
0<|h|\leq d
we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r+d}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{s\tilde{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q-1+\vartheta+sp+\gamma(2-p)}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

for a constant
C=C(N,p,q)
. Recalling
\gamma=1-\varepsilon
and
\vartheta=\theta-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}
, the above estimate can be re-written as

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\widetilde{R}-d}}|\bm{\tau}_{h}(\bm{\tau}_{h}u)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{s\tilde{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{|h|}{R}\Big)^{q+1-(1-s)p+\theta-(2-p)\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

As before, we have
C=C(N,p,q)
.
Apply Lemma
2.15
with
\gamma
replaced by

\widetilde{\beta}=\frac{1-(1-s)p+\theta-(2-p)\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}

and

\displaystyle M^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{s\tilde{\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{R^{q(1+\widetilde{\beta})}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+3}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}

and
\bar{d}=\frac{1}{4}d
.
Moreover, we fix
\beta
in the application by

\displaystyle\beta:=\frac{1-(1-s)p+\theta-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}.

Note that
\beta\in(0,\widetilde{\beta})
and
\widetilde{\beta}-\beta=\frac{(p-1)\varepsilon+\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}>\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2q}
. Lemma
2.15
yields

\nabla u\in W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})
with the
quantitative estimate

(6.7)

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(N,q)d^{q(\widetilde{\beta}-\beta)}}{(\widetilde{\beta}-\beta)\widetilde{\beta}^{q}(1-\widetilde{\beta})^{q}}\bigg[M^{q}+\frac{(r+d)^{q}}{\beta d^{q(1+\widetilde{\beta})}}\int_{B_{r+d}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg].

Noting that
r+d\leq R
and using the elementary inequalities

\widetilde{\beta}>\beta\geq\frac{\theta}{2q},\quad 1-\widetilde{\beta}\geq\frac{q-1-\theta}{q},

the denominator of the first factor in (
6.7
) can be bounded from below by

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\beta}-\beta)\widetilde{\beta}^{q}(1-\widetilde{\beta})^{q}\geq\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2q}\Big(\frac{\theta}{2q}\Big)^{q}\Big(\frac{q-1-\theta}{q}\Big)^{q}=\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}\theta^{q}(q-1-\theta)^{q}}{C(q)}.

Inserting this in the
W^{\beta,q}
-estimate (
6.7
) will result in

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\beta)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where the constant
C
has the structure

C=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}\theta^{q+1}(q-\theta-1)^{q}}.

This is the desired estimate.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

We stress that the constant
C
in the previous lemmas eventually blows up as
\tilde{\varepsilon}\downarrow 0
. However, it is independent of
\varepsilon
which is required to be less than
1-s
. Therefore,
C
remains stable as
s\uparrow 1
.

Now, we have all prerequisites at hand to give the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Note that

(6.8)

\beta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\frac{sp}{q},&\mbox{if $\displaystyle s\leq\frac{q(p-1)}{p}$,}\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1-(1-s)p}{q-1},&\mbox{if $\displaystyle s>\frac{q(p-1)}{p}$.}\end{array}\right.

We proceed in three steps.

Step 1: Almost
W^{\frac{sp}{q},q}
-regularity.
This step involves Lemma
6.1
only and applies to all
s\in(0,1)
.
To begin with, let
\alpha\in(0,\frac{sp}{q})
. From Theorem
5.4
we have
u\in W^{1,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
q\geq p
, while from Theorem
1.3
we know that
u\in C^{0,\gamma}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
This allows to fix in Lemma
6.1
the parameters
\varepsilon
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
by

\varepsilon=\min\big\{1-s,\tfrac{1}{2}(sp-\alpha q)\big\}\in\big(0,\min\{1-s,\tfrac{1}{2}sp\big\}\big]

and

\tilde{\varepsilon}=\tfrac{1}{2}(sp-\alpha q)\in\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}sp\big].

The application of the Lemma
6.1
results in the fractional differentiability of
\nabla u
in the sense that

\nabla u\in W^{\beta,q}_{\loc}(\Omega),\quad\mbox{where $\beta=\frac{sp-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}\in(\alpha,1)$.}

The lower and upper bounds for
\beta
are a direct consequence of the choices of
\varepsilon
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
.
Moreover, we have the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle[\nabla u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\beta)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

for every ball

B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{o})\Subset\Omega
and
r\in(0,R)
and with a constant
C=C(N,p,s,q,\alpha)
of the form

C=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{q+1}(\frac{sp}{q}-\alpha)^{q+1}}.

Here we have used the abbreviation

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}:=R^{N+(1-\varepsilon)q}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{R})}+R^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{R})}+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{q}.

Recall that
\beta\geq\alpha
, so that
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\loc}(\Omega)
and

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(\beta-\alpha)q}r^{(\beta-\alpha)q}[u]_{W^{\beta,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

(6.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

Note that (
6.9
) holds for any
s\in(0,1)
. In particular, we have proved the qualitative assertion of Theorem
1.4
in the range
s\leq\frac{q(p-1)}{p}
, in which we have
\beta=\frac{sp}{q}
, cf. (
6.8
).

Step 2: Almost
W^{\frac{1-(1-s)p}{q-1},q}
-regularity for
s>\frac{q(p-1)}{p}
.

In this step we concentrate on the case
s>\frac{q(p-1)}{p}
and employ Lemma
6.2
together with an iteration scheme to upgrade the differentiability to any number less than
\beta=\frac{1-(1-s)p}{q-1}
.
To this end, let
\theta_{o}=\frac{sp}{2q}
and consider
\alpha\in(\theta_{o},\beta)
.
Moreover, we fix
\varepsilon
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
by

\varepsilon=\min\big\{1-s,\tfrac{1}{3}(\beta-\alpha)(q-1)\big\}\in\big(0,\min\big\{1-s,\tfrac{1}{2}[1-(1-s)p]\big\}\big]

and

\tilde{\varepsilon}=\min\big\{\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{o},\tfrac{1}{3}(\beta-\alpha)(q-1)\big\}\in\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{o}\big],

such that the requirements on
\varepsilon
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
imposed in Lemma
6.2
are satisfied.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
introduce

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\theta_{i+1}=\frac{1-(1-s)p+\theta_{i}-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q},\\[8.0pt]
\displaystyle\varrho_{i}=r+\frac{R-r}{2^{i+1}},\qquad B_{i}=B_{\varrho_{i}}.\end{array}\right.

By iteration we have

\displaystyle\theta_{i}

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+\frac{1}{q}+\dots+\frac{1}{q^{i-1}}\bigg)\frac{1-(1-s)p-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q}+\frac{\theta_{o}}{q^{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{i}}\bigg[\theta_{o}-\frac{1-(1-s)p-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q-1}\bigg]+\frac{1-(1-s)p-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q-1}.

Since

\displaystyle\theta_{o}-\frac{1-(1-s)p-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q-1}\leq\theta_{o}-\beta+\tfrac{2}{3}(\beta-\alpha)=\theta_{o}-\tfrac{1}{3}\beta-\tfrac{2}{3}\alpha<\tfrac{1}{3}(\theta_{o}-\beta)<0,

the sequence
(\theta_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is strictly increasing and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\theta_{i}=\frac{1-(1-s)p-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q-1}>\alpha.

From
Step 1
, in particular from inequality (
6.9
) applied with the choice
\frac{sp}{2q}=\theta_{o}
for
\alpha
, we already know that

\nabla u\in W^{\theta_{o},q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
,
with the quantitative estimate

(6.10)

\displaystyle R^{(1+\theta_{o})q}[\nabla u]_{W^{\theta_{o},q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

The constant
C
has the structure

C=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{2q+2}}.

Therefore, we are allowed to apply Lemma
6.2
with
(\theta_{o},\theta_{1},\varrho_{1},\varrho_{o})
in place of
(\theta,\beta,r,R)
and with
\varepsilon
as above in order to improve the fractional differentiability of
\nabla u
to order
\theta_{1}
, i.e.
\nabla u\in W^{\theta_{1},q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
.

For the
induction step

i\to i+1
let us now assume that we have already established
\nabla u\in W^{\theta_{i},q}(B_{i})
for some
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Since
\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2}[1-(1-s)p]
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{o}\leq\frac{1}{2}\theta_{i}
all assumptions of Lemma
6.2
are fulfilled. The application with

(\theta_{i},\theta_{i+1},\varrho_{i+1},\varrho_{i})
in place of
(\theta,\beta,r,R)
and with the above choices of
\varepsilon
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}
yields

\displaystyle\varrho_{i+1}^{(1+\theta_{i+1})q}[\nabla u]_{W^{\theta_{i+1},q}(B_{i+1})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{i}\Big(\frac{\varrho_{i}}{\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1}}\Big)^{N+q+1}\Big[\varrho_{i}^{(1+\theta_{i})q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta_{i},q}(B_{i})}+\bm{\mathfrak{K}}_{i}^{q}\Big],

where

\displaystyle\bm{\mathfrak{K}}_{i}^{q}

\displaystyle:=\varrho_{i}^{N+(1-\varepsilon)q}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{i})}+\varrho_{i}^{q}\|\nabla u\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{i})}+\varrho_{i}^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;\varrho_{i})^{q}.

The constant
C_{i}
has the structure

C_{i}=\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s\tilde{\varepsilon}^{2}\theta_{i}^{q+1}(q-\theta_{i}-1)^{q}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{q+2}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{q+2}}\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}(N,p,q)}{s^{2q+4}(\beta-\widetilde{\alpha})^{q+2}}=:C_{*}.

To obtain the last inequality we used
\frac{sp^{2}}{4q}\leq\theta_{i}<\frac{1-(1-s)p-\varepsilon-\tilde{\varepsilon}}{q-1}
.
To further simplify the previous recursive estimate, observe that

R\geq\varrho_{i}=\frac{R}{2^{i+1}}+r\Big(1-\frac{1}{2^{i+1}}\Big)>\frac{R}{2^{i+1}},

and

\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1}=\frac{R-r}{2^{i+2}},

such that we have

\Big(\frac{\varrho_{i}}{\varrho_{i}-\varrho_{i+1}}\Big)^{N+q+1}\leq C(N,q)2^{(N+q+1)i}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}.

In addition, we have

\displaystyle\varrho_{i}^{(1-\varepsilon)q+N}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq R^{(1-\varepsilon)q+N}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{R})},

and

\displaystyle\varrho_{i}^{q}\int_{B_{i}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq R^{q}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, Remark
2.7
yields

\displaystyle\varrho_{i}^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;\varrho_{i})^{q}\leq\varrho_{i}^{N}\bigg[C(N)\Big(\frac{R}{\varrho_{i}}\Big)^{N}\bigg]^{\frac{q}{p-1}}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{q}\leq C(N,p,q)2^{\frac{Nq}{p-1}i}R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)^{q}.

Consequently,
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}_{i}
is estimated by
\bm{\mathfrak{K}}
with a multiplicative factor thanks to the last three estimates, and we arrive at a simpler recursive estimate which reads as

\displaystyle\varrho_{i+1}^{(1+\theta_{i+1})q}[\nabla u]_{W^{\theta_{i+1},q}(B_{i+1})}^{q}\leq C_{*}\bm{b}^{i}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{N+q+1}\Big[\varrho_{i}^{(1+\theta_{i})q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta_{i},q}(B_{i})}+\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}\Big].

Here, we denoted
\bm{b}:=2^{N+q+1+\frac{Nq}{p-1}}
.
Iterating this inequality and taking into account that
\bm{b}\geq 1
and
C_{\ast}\geq 1
we obtain

\displaystyle\varrho_{i}^{(1+\theta_{i})q}

\displaystyle[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta_{i},q}(B_{i})}

\displaystyle\quad\leq i\bm{b}^{1+2+\dots+i}C_{*}^{i}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{i(N+q+1)}\Big[R^{(1+\theta_{o})q}[\nabla u]^{q}_{W^{\theta_{o},q}(B_{R})}+\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq i\bm{b}^{1+2+\dots+i}C_{*}^{i}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(i+1)(N+q+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q},

where from the second to last line we used inequality (
6.10
).
In order to conclude, we only need to take
i_{o}\in\mathbb{N}
to be the first index satisfying

\theta_{i_{o}}\geq\frac{1-(1-s)p-\frac{3}{2}(\varepsilon+\tilde{\varepsilon})}{q-1}\geq\alpha,

i.e.

i_{o}=\Bigg\lceil\frac{\ln\Big(1+\frac{\theta_{o}(q-1)-[1-(1-s)p]}{\varepsilon+\tilde{\varepsilon}}\Big)}{\ln q}\Bigg\rceil.

Note that
i_{o}
only depends on
p,s,q
, and
\alpha
. It is stable as
s\uparrow 1
and blows up as
s\downarrow 0
and
\alpha\uparrow\beta
.
Hence,

\displaystyle r^{(1+\alpha)q}[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{(\theta_{i_{o}}-\alpha)q}r^{(1+\theta_{i_{o}})q}[u]_{W^{\theta_{i_{o}},q}(B_{\varrho_{i_{o}}})}^{q}

(6.11)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q}i_{o}\bm{b}^{1+2+\dots+i_{o}}C_{*}^{i_{o}}\Big(\frac{R}{R-r}\Big)^{(i_{o}+1)(N+q+1)}\bm{\mathfrak{K}}^{q}.

Step 3: Almost
W^{\beta,q}
-regularity.

By combining
Step 1
and
Step 2
, in particular (
6.9
) and (
6.11
), we have established that
\nabla u\in W^{\alpha,q}_{\rm loc}(\Omega)
for every
\alpha\in(0,\beta)
together with the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\bigg[R^{N+(1-\varepsilon)q}[u]^{q}_{C^{0,1-\varepsilon}(B_{\frac{3}{4}R})}+R^{q}\int_{B_{\frac{3}{4}R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\mathrm{d}x+R^{N}\bm{\mathfrak{T}}\big(u;\tfrac{3}{4}R\big)^{q}\bigg].

Finally, we use Theorem
1.2
, Theorem
1.3
, and Lemma
2.6
to further estimate the terms in the bracket on the right-hand side. After slightly adjusting the radii we obtain

\displaystyle[u]_{W^{\alpha,q}(B_{\frac{1}{2}R})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CR^{N}}{R^{(1+\alpha)q}}\Big[R^{s-\frac{N}{p}}(1-s)^{\frac{1}{p}}[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{R})}+\bm{\mathfrak{T}}(u;R)\Big]^{q}.

This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Effect of High-Multiple Water Flooding and Layered Allocation on Oil Displacement Efficiency in Unconsolidated Sandstone Reservoirs
Authors: Liping Jin, Jingen Deng, Jiabo Liang, Lizhuan Liu, Xiaolu Zhang, Hao Lu, Tao Yang, Haoxuan Tang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The main oil layer of Oilfield A is characterized by shallow braided river delta deposits with weak vertical and horizontal continuity of sand bodies and strong heterogeneity. After over 20 years of water flooding development, the oilfield has entered a high water-cut stage, with a recovery factor of less than 25%. It is urgently required to take measures to enhance the oil recovery. To address this, experiments on high-multiple water flooding and interlayer parallel water flooding were conducted using the latest core samples from the oilfield to clarify the improvement of oil displacement efficiency by these two measures. The results show that when the water injection multiple is increased from 30 to 2000 PV, oil displacement efficiency increases by 3.77% (from 58.43% to 62.20%). Compared with commingled injection–production, two groups of experiments show that the oil displacement efficiency of layered allocation increases by 2.88% and 9.61%, respectively, indicating a significant improvement. The study provides a basis for the subsequent optimization development plan of the oilfield by increasing the water injection multiple and implementing fine layered injection and production, and can also provide a reference for similar oilfields.

Introduction

In recent years, offshore oilfields have emerged as the primary contributors to crude oil production growth, with the BH oilfield being a key component of such offshore increments. , As the only ultralarge integrated oilfield with one billion tons of oil and gas reserves in the Bohai Bay Basin, Oilfield A serves as a core component of the BH oilfield. , The main reservoir of A oilfield is a fluvial delta deposit, exhibiting strong reservoir heterogeneity. During the early development stage, the oilfield was developed via commingled injection and production. Following over 20 years of water flood development, it has entered a stage characterized by high water cut and low recovery (with an estimated ultimate recovery (EUR) of <25%). It is urgently required to change the development method to improve the recovery rate and maintain the continuous and efficient development of the oilfield.

Enhancing oil recovery has long been a critical research focus in oil and gas field development. Different measures can be taken for different development stages. − For oilfields newly entering the high water-cut stage, key measures include increasing the injection multiple, fine injection and production, chemical profile control and water plugging, chemical flooding, well pattern infill drilling etc. − Some scholars have reported that the oil displacement efficiency (ODE) of the strong water-flooded interval in the field’s sealed coring well can reach 89.7%; it is necessary to increase the water injection multiple to 2000 PV. This study first proposes the concept of high displacement multiple. Zhao et al. suggested that in the main flow path and near-wellbore zone of strong bottom-water reservoirs, when the displacement multiple exceeds 1000 PV, the indoor water flooding volume reaching 2000 PV is consistent with the high water flooding conditions in the reservoir. For similar oilfields, Zhang et al. conducted high-multiple water flooding experiments in Oilfield S, which showed that when the displacement multiple reaches 300–400, the ODE increases by approximately 10% compared with that at a displacement multiple of 30–50. Hong et al. performed water flooding experiments with injection volumes of 500 PV, 1000 PV, and 2000 PV on typical cores from Well A in Oilfield Q. They proposed that ODE increases rapidly at low injection multiples, while it changes minimally once the displacement multiple reaches 500 PV. Luo et al. conducted indoor water flooding experiments with displacement multiples of 500–2000 PV in the NNX Oilfield and analyzed the relationships between reservoir physical properties, crude oil viscosity, and ODE. Regarding the influence of interlayer heterogeneity on ODE, Tang et al. conducted water flooding experiments in the Keshang Formation of District 7, Xinjiang Oilfield, clarifying the impact of interlayer heterogeneity on the ODE in conglomerate reservoirs. Deng et al. developed a parallel displacement system and conducted indoor heterogeneous water flooding experiments, evaluating the effects of permeability ratio, water cut, production pressure difference, and crude oil viscosity on water flood efficiency. Compared with other oilfields, Oilfield A exhibits poor reservoir sandbody connectivity, a high proportion of thin layers (<3 m accounting for over 70%), and stronger macro- and microscopic reservoir heterogeneity. However, there is a lack of research on how to adjust development strategies and further improve recovery rates after entering the high water-cut period.

To clarify the ODE and its controlling factors in Oilfield A, sealed coring operations for Well A1 were conducted in the main area of Oilfield A in October 2022. Using cores from Well A1, this study performed high-multiple water flooding experiments and interlayer parallel water flooding experiments to quantitatively assess the effects of high injection multiples and layered deployment on the ODE.

This study innovatively constructs a dual experimental system“high-volume water injection experiment (up to 2000 PV) + interlayer parallel water injection experiment”to reconstruct the field logic of layered production allocation. It quantitatively evaluates the ODE of such reservoirs under different water injection multiples, confirms that layered production allocation most significantly improves the oil displacement efficiency of low-permeability layers and defines the critical conditions for the difficulty in mobilizing low-permeability layers. This fills the research gap in development strategy adjustment and enhanced oil recovery (EOR) for offshore unconsolidated sandstone reservoirs characterized by a high water cut, strong heterogeneity, and loose cementation, providing a quantitative basis for optimizing development schemes of similar reservoirs.

Regional Geology and Development Setting

The main oil layers in Oilfield A belong to braided river shallow braided river delta deposits, hosting microfacies such as distributary sand bars, distributary channels, and point bars. , The reservoir exhibits rapid lateral and vertical variations with numerous vertical sand bodies and a high proportion of thin layers (Figure a). Since its development began in 2002, the oilfield has been using water injection development to supplement formation energy. After over 20 years of water flooding, it has now entered the high water-cut stage (water-cut >85%), with a significant difference between vertical flooding degrees and liquid production capacities , (Figure b). To clarify the geological characteristics of the reservoir and the distribution of remaining oil in the oilfield, the sealed coring work of Well A1 was carried out in 2022, and the cores of L30, L50, L62, L88, L96, and L102 were obtained. Sealed coring ensures that core samples are nearly free from contamination by drilling fluid free water, thereby most accurately reflecting the effectiveness of the oilfield’s waterflood development and enabling the acquisition of in situ formation oil saturation.

Experimental Procedure

High-Multiple Water Flooding Experiment Steps

High-multiple water flooding experiment steps are as follows:(1)Cores are washed and dried, and their dry weights are recorded.(2)After saturation with formation water, the wet weights of all cores are measured, and the effective pore volume is calculated.(3)The core is put into the core gripper, and the displacement speed is gradually increased at the initial flow rate of 0.1 mL/min to carry out oil flooding (the experimental oil is 100 # refined white oil) until no water is produced, and the irreducible water saturation is established.(4)Displacement is continued using simulated formation oil. Once the pressure difference stabilizes, the effective oil-phase permeability under irreducible water saturation is measured. Three consecutive measurements are performed, with a relative error of <3%.(5)The pump is stopped and switched to water flooding. When the core begins to water out, the following parameters are accurately recorded: water out time, cumulative oil production, cumulative liquid production, displacement velocity, and displacement pressure. (During the initial stage of water out, records are taken more frequently; as oil output continues to decrease, the recording interval is gradually extended.) The experiment is terminated after reaching 2000 PV, and the water-phase permeability under residual oil saturation is measured.(6)The water flooding efficiency is calculated.

Data Processing

Based on the displacement experiment results, the following parameters are defined:(1) Model oil displacement efficiency (η i ): For three parallel cores under constant-pressure displacement, this refers to the ODE when the water cut of all three cores reaches 99%. It is calculated as the percentage of the total oil produced by the model relative to the total oil saturated in the three cores (%).(2) Final model oil displacement efficiency (η): For three parallel cores under constant-pressure displacement, this denotes the ODE when the overall water cut of the model’s produced fluid reaches 99%. It is calculated as (total oil displaced from the three cores/total oil saturated in the three cores)×100% and is used to characterize the overall ODE of the reservoir after layered allocation.(3) Core oil displacement efficiency (E i ): The ODE of individual high-, medium-, or low-permeability cores during displacement. It is used to characterize the ODE of different reservoirs during layered deployment displacement.(4) Core ultimate oil displacement efficiency (E): For three parallel cores under constant-pressure displacement: When the high-permeability core reaches 99%, turn it off. It represents the ultimate ODE­(E g) and is calculated as (oil displaced from the high-permeability core/oil saturated in the high-permeability core)×100%. The medium- and low-permeability cores continue to be displaced in parallel. When it is turned off, its ultimate ODE­(E z) is calculated using the same method. The low-permeability core continues to be displaced under constant pressure until its water cut reaches 99%, and its ultimate ODE (E d) is calculated similarly.(5) Oil production splitting ratio (F o): During constant-pressure displacement of three parallel cores, the oil production volume of each core is recorded at different time points. This ratio is the percentage of oil produced from an individual core relative to the total oil production of the model. It characterizes the proportion of oil production from different reservoir types in the total oil production after layered allocation.(6) Liquid production splitting ratio (F L): During constant-pressure displacement of three parallel cores, the liquid production volume of each core is recorded at different time points. This ratio is the percentage of liquid produced from an individual core relative to the total oil production of the model. It characterizes the proportion of oil production from different reservoir types in the total oil production after layered allocation.(7) Liquid production ratio(L r): With the liquid production volume (oil + water) of the low-permeability core as the reference value (1), this ratio is derived by comparing the corresponding liquid production volumes of the medium- and high-permeability cores to the reference. It characterizes differences in liquid production capacity among various reservoirs.(8) Oil production ratio(O r): With the oil production volume of the low-permeability core as the reference value (1), this ratio is derived by comparing the corresponding oil production volumes of the medium- and high-permeability cores to the reference. It characterizes differences in oil production capacity among various reservoir types.(9) Permeability contribution ratio(P c): The percentage of the permeability of an individual core relative to the sum of the permeabilities of the three parallel cores (%).(10) Model pore volume(PVm): The total pore volume of the three parallel cores, expressed in mL. The pore volume of a single core in the experiment is denoted as PV (mL).

Analysis of Experiment Results

Experimental Results and Analysis of High-Multiple Water Flooding

The permeability of the six experimental cores ranges from 458.0 to 962.7 mD. The irreducible water saturation ranges from 23.2% to 41.0%, with an average of 33.4% (Table ). When the displacement reaches 30 PV, the water saturation of the cores reaches the current in situ water saturation state of the reservoir. At this stage, the core ODE ranges from 49.22% to 69.90%, with an average of 58.43%. After displacement to 2000 PV (high-multiple water flooding), the ODE ranges from 53.07% to 72.4%, with an average of 62.2%. Compared with conventional water flooding, high-multiple water flooding can increase the ODE by 3.77%, indicating that high-multiple water flooding can significantly improve the ODE of the reservoir.

Experimental Results of High-Multiple Water Flooding

As shown in the ODE curves (Figure ), variations exist in the water cut and ODE curves among different core samples during the water flooding process. Among the six core samples, the water cut rising rates of Samples II, IV, and VI are significantly higher than those of the other three samples, while their ODE exhibits slower growth. This indicates strong heterogeneity within the reservoir, necessitating further investigation into the influence of heterogeneity on reservoir ODE.

High-multiple water flooding oil displacement efficiency curve.

After conventional water flooding in the oilfield, significant amounts of residual oil remain in the formation. This residual oil is trapped in pores in forms such as oil films, droplets, clusters, and columns, constrained by capillary forces, viscous forces, and other restraining forces. , High- multiple water injection continuously alters the oil–water two-phase seepage field. When the displacement pressure gradient of the injected water is sufficiently large, it can overcome these confining forces, inducing residual oil to remobilize and thereby enhancing ODE. , Additionally, as injected water continuously scours the rock, the pore structure may be modifiedwith an increase in the number of large pore throats. Initially, polar components (e.g., asphaltenes and resins) in crude oil adsorb onto the rock surface, forming an oil-wet film. During water flooding, flushing by the aqueous phase desorbs these components, or ions in the water compete with the polar fractions for adsorption sitesundermining the oil-wet film’s stability. Additionally, long-term water flooding induces slight dissolution of rock particles and expansion/migration of clay minerals, altering rock surface roughness and the number of active adsorption sites to indirectly affect wettability. , These combined effects drive reservoir wettability reversal. This reversal renders the oil phase more mobile, further improving the quality of the ODE.

In the two groups of cores from the heterogeneous model flooding experiments, both liquid production and ODE follow the order of permeability from high to low. Specifically, high-permeability cores are the first to produce liquid, with their water cut rising rapidly until reaching over 99%.

The model experiment of Group I shows that when PVm = 2.25, only the low-permeability Core 2# produces liquid; the model experiment of Group II shows that when PVm = 0.07, only the low-permeability Core 4# produces liquid. The water-free ODE of high-permeability Cores 1# (Group I) and 4# (Group II) are 29.44% and 22.58%, respectively. For medium-permeability Cores 2# (Group I) and 5# (Group II), the water-free ODE values are 33.75% and 10.75%, respectively. For low-permeability Cores 3# (Group I) and 6# (Group II), the water-free ODE values are 31.18% and 6.22%, respectively (Figure ). For the heterogeneous model with three parallel cores, due to the rapid increase in water cutoff of high-permeability cores, the model also exhibits an extremely short water-free oil production period and a very low water-free recovery efficiency, which is difficult to accurately quantify.

Group I exhibits a relatively large permeability contrast, with the low-permeability core beginning to mobilize only when the model approaches its final ODE. It can be inferred that when the liquid permeability contrast exceeds 6.6 and the oil-phase permeability contrast exceeds 32.9 under irreducible water saturation, the low-permeability core remains unmobilized. In such cases, the high-permeability interval forms a distinctly dominant seepage channel, with the F L occupying an absolute dominance; however, the Or is far lower than the L r. Liquid permeability contrast exerts a significant influence on liquid production, with a much weaker impact on oil production by comparison.

During the experiment, low-permeability cores are difficult to mobilize or remain unmobilized. Thus, whether in oilfield water injection or water flooding evaluation experiments, it is challenging to recover this portion of crude oil through a single water injection alone.

Permeability contrast exerts a significant influence on liquid production. In highly heterogeneous unconsolidated sandstone reservoirs, crude oil in high-permeability intervals is mobilized first and, subsequently, becomes the dominant pathway for injected water seepage. If the permeability of high-permeability intervals is 6.6 times that of other intervals, low-permeability intervals are difficult to mobilize effectively. Similarly, in heterogeneous models, where the interlayer permeability contrast is less than 6.6, although high-permeability intervals remain the main pathway for injected water seepage, medium- and low-permeability intervals will be gradually mobilized during the subsequent oil displacement process. In general, mobilizing low-permeability intervals is relatively challenging.

Conclusion

This study focuses on the development challenges of unconsolidated sandstone reservoirs in the high water-cut stage (water cut >85%) of Oilfield A, with the latest sealed core samples from Well A1 as the research object. Through high-multiple water flooding experiments and interlayer parallel water flooding experiments, the results are systematically investigated using high injection volumes and layered deployment on the ODE. The main conclusions are as follows:(1)High-multiple water flooding can effectively enhance ODE. Experimental results show that when the displacement multiple is increased from 30 PV to 2000 PV, the average ODE of the six cores increases from 58.43% to 62.2%, with an average improvement of 3.77%.(2)Layered deployment significantly enhances the ODE of heterogeneous reservoirs. Interlayer parallel experiments indicate that high-permeability cores exhibit preferential liquid production due to low seepage resistance, with an average liquid production allocation ratio of 88.39%. In contrast, medium- and low-permeability cores exhibit delayed initiation of production and lower ODE. After sequentially isolating high-permeability and medium-permeability cores with high water cut (water cut >99%) for layered deployment, the overall ODE of the two model groups increased by 2.88% and 9.61%, respectively. Among these, the improvement of the ODE of low-permeability cores was the most significant (increasing by 30.65% and 5.96%, respectively). This confirms that deployment measures can promote the effective propagation of injected water into medium- and low-permeability intervals and enhance the mobilization of residual oil.(3)Permeability contrast is a key factor influencing reservoir production performance. When the absolute liquid permeability contrast exceeds 6.6 and the oil-phase permeability contrast exceeds 32.9, low-permeability cores are difficult to mobilize. For smaller permeability contrasts, medium- and low-permeability intervals can be gradually mobilized; however, high-permeability intervals remain the dominant channels for injected water. Their liquid production allocation ratio is significantly higher than their oil production allocation ratio, indicating that simple water injection tends to cause ineffective water circulation.(4)Based on the experimental results, this study recommends optimizing the water injection strategy for heterogeneous high water-cut reservoirs by gradually increasing the water injection multiple, while dynamically monitoring formation pressure and water cut to avoid inefficient circulation caused by excessive water injection and enhance residual oil mobilization through extended effective displacement time. To address significant interlayer conflicts, promote the “layered isolation + precise injection allocation” mode: use packers to isolate high- and medium-permeability layers with water cut exceeding 99%, direct injected water to underutilized medium-low permeability layers, and establish a small-level injection-production ledger to real-time adjust water injection allocation, maximizing the improvement of low-permeability layers’ oil displacement efficiency via layered deployment. Additionally, first conduct a full-reservoir permeability contrast assessment: if the absolute liquid permeability contrast exceeds 6.6 or the oil-phase permeability contrast exceeds 32.9, modify low-permeability layers’ seepage channels using technologies such as pressure flooding and nitrogen foam flooding; for smaller permeability contrasts, adopt layered profile control to block high-permeability dominant channels, forcing injected water to evenly sweep medium-low permeability layers, alleviating ineffective water circulation and comprehensively enhancing the reservoir’s overall development performance.

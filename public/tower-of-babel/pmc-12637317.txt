Comparison of Upper Pole Between Immediate and Delayed Breast Reconstructions Using an Abdominal Free Flap
Authors: Hideki Tokumoto, Shinsuke Akita, Yoshitaka Kubota, Shouko Hayama, Rikiya Nakamura
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The deep inferior epigastric perforator flap has become the workhorse of autologous breast reconstruction (ABR). In delayed-immediate ABR, a tissue expander is placed during mastectomy and reconstruction is performed later. We compared the morphology of the upper pole after immediate ABR with that after delayed-immediate ABR using a deep inferior epigastric perforator flap.Methods:Standardized lateral chest photographs were analyzed. The borderline was drawn through the top of the breast projection, horizontal to the floor. A line through the lower end of the breast was drawn parallel to the patient’s upright stance. The upper end of the breast (U) was defined as the point at which the parallel line crossed the most proximal side of the breast. The upper pole length was measured between U and the point crossing the parallel and borderlines. The upper pole angle was defined as the angle between the parallel line and a line tangential to the upper pole through U.Results:The immediate and delayed groups comprised 95 and 75 patients, respectively. No significant difference in the preoperative data was observed between the 2 groups. However, the postoperative median length was significantly shorter (86.7 versus 82.8 mm, P = 0.02) and the postoperative median angle was significantly greater (31.1 versus 36.2 degrees, P < 0.001) in the delayed group than those in the immediate group.Conclusions:In delayed-immediate ABR, the postoperative length of the upper pole decreased, whereas its postoperative angle increased.

Takeaways

Question: Can the morphology of the upper pole in patients who underwent immediate breast reconstruction and those who underwent delayed-immediate reconstruction using a free abdominal flap be solved?

Findings: Compared with the immediate group, the delayed group had a significantly shorter postoperative upper pole length and a significantly greater postoperative upper pole angle.

Meaning: Immediate reconstruction may be suitable for achieving symmetry when no surgical intervention is planned for the contralateral breast. In contrast, some patients may prefer to have a lift of the nipple and more upper pole projection, in which case delayed-immediate reconstruction may be more appropriate.

INTRODUCTION

Autologous breast reconstruction (ABR) using the deep inferior epigastric perforator (DIEP) flap is considered the gold standard. In delayed-immediate ABR, a tissue expander (TE) is placed during mastectomy and autologous reconstruction is performed later.1 A skin paddle is very important for flap monitoring; moreover, if a breast had previously undergone radiotherapy, a skin paddle is important, as it will be used for nipple reconstruction later on. Delayed-immediate ABR can help prevent a patchwork-like scar by allowing the expansion of the native chest skin.2

Accurate reconstruction of the upper breast pole is essential. Despite the importance of the upper pole, no study has yet focused on its morphology. We compared the upper pole morphology between patients who underwent immediate ABR and those who underwent delayed-immediate ABR using a DIEP flap.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

We enrolled all female patients who underwent unilateral primary breast reconstruction using a DIEP flap at Chiba Cancer Center between April 2015 and June 2023. The current study was approved by the ethics committee of Chiba Cancer Center (institutional review board approval no.: R06-120). We compared the characteristics between patients who underwent immediate ABR and those who underwent delayed-immediate ABR (immediate versus delayed groups).

All preoperative photographs were taken within 1 month preoperatively, and the postoperative photographs were taken at 2 years postoperatively during the long-term follow-up. Patients whose photographs were taken in a rotated position were excluded. Patients were also excluded if they had a history of secondary breast reconstruction, recurrence following breast-conserving surgery, or total flap necrosis.

Image Processing and Measurements of the Upper Pole Morphology

Standardized lateral chest photographs were taken. The resulting digital images were analyzed using the ImageJ software application (version 1.45; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD).

The border point (B) between the upper and lower poles was defined as the top of the breast projection. If B could not be defined because the top was not sharp, it was defined as the center at the top of the projection. The borderline was drawn through B, horizontal to the floor in the digital images. A line through the lower end of the breast (L) was drawn parallel to the patient’s upright stance. In nonptotic breasts, L corresponded to the inframammary fold (IMF; Fig. 1), whereas, in ptotic breasts, L did not correspond to the IMF (Fig. 2). The upper end of the breast (U) was defined as the point at which the parallel line crossed the breast’s most proximal side. The point at which the borderline crossed the parallel line was defined as the point of crossing (C, Fig. 1).

Surgical Technique for Delayed-immediate Autologous Breast Reconstruction

The TE was inserted in a completely submuscular position. The inferior margin of the expander pocket was positioned below the IMF of the contralateral healthy breast. Postoperatively, the TE was expanded to within the weight of the excised breast tissue plus 10%. The flap-based reconstruction was performed 3 months after the final expansion.

In all ABRs using a DIEP flap, the deep inferior epigastric artery and vein were anastomosed to the internal mammary artery and vein. The superior and inferior sides of the inserted DIEP flap corresponded to zones 3 and 2, respectively. The pectoralis major muscle was detached from the mastectomy skin flap, and the flap was inserted subcutaneously. Therefore, there were 2 different reconstruction planes, that is, subcutaneous and submuscular (Fig. 2).

Given that all the capsule was under the flap, no capsulotomy or capsulectomy was performed. The patient was then placed into a sitting position. The degree of skin paddle exposure was determined according to the following steps. The lengths of UB and BL in the contralateral side were measured intraoperatively. When these lengths on the operative side were shorter than those on the contralateral side, the skin paddle of the DIEP flap was exposed until those lengths became the same lengths. When these lengths were not reduced, a small skin paddle was left exposed for flap monitoring. The skin paddle remained visible even after TE removal (Figs. 1, 2).

The Regnault classification was adopted for the definition of breast ptosis.3 In this classification, “pseudoptosis” indicates a breast configuration in which the gland is inferior to the IMF and the nipple is above it. Grade 1 or greater ptosis is diagnosed when the nipple is at or below the IMF. In this study, all patients with pseudoptosis and grade 1 or greater were classified as having breast ptosis. The extent of skin excision was measured by pathological specimens during the primary excision. Wound healing delay was defined as the presence of wound discharge, diastasis, or skin necrosis for greater than 2 weeks after surgery. This was managed with a povidone–iodine sugar ointment. Wound healing delay was only considered for wounds located on the breast, not on the abdomen. The absence of the NAC was included for patients without their original or reconstructed NAC. For the analysis of the rate of visible skin paddle, we excluded not only patients in whom the entire skin paddle was concealed by breast skin but also those in whom the entire skin paddle could be concealed by NAC reconstruction. Statistical Analysis

All data analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 22 (IBM SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL). Data were presented as median (interquartile range) or percentage, and between-group differences were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test or Fisher exact test. P values less than 0.05 indicated statistical significance (Tables 1, 2).

RESULTS

The present study enrolled 170 patients, with 95 included in the immediate group and 75 in the delayed group. The median follow-up duration was 3.5 years (range, 2.0–7.5 y).

The rates of wound healing delay (32.6% versus 13.3%, P < 0.001) and visible skin paddle (63.2% versus 41.3%, P = 0.005) were significantly higher in the immediate group than those in the delayed group. No significant differences in the other characteristics were noted (Table 1).

Regarding the upper pole morphology, the preoperative data were not significantly different between the 2 groups. Compared with the immediate group, the delayed group had a significantly shorter postoperative upper pole length (86.7 mm [79.1–93.0 mm] versus 82.8 mm [71.4–89.9 mm], P = 0.02), lower percentage (69.7% [66.1%–72.5%] versus 64.7% [61.3%–69.8%], P < 0.001), and a significantly greater postoperative upper pole angle (31.1 degrees [25.8–36.2 degrees] versus 36.2 degrees [30.6–40.0 degrees], P < 0.001) (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

Country of residence, age, and type of practice have been reported to considerably affect breast shape preferences.4 Cultural differences and international variability should be considered when defining and reporting new techniques and aesthetic outcomes. Most patients prefer their reconstructed breasts to appear “better” than their natural breasts; thus, surgical procedures on the contralateral breast are also common. Additionally, the rate of bilateral mastectomy continues to increase, even among patients with cancer in only 1 breast and those with breast cancer–related pathogenic genetic variants.5

When the volume of the native chest skin was insufficient, skin paddle exposure was adopted. The point of maximum projection was located at the center of the breast. In all breast procedures except for nipple-sparing mastectomy, the NAC was resected. Therefore, the greatest amount of skin needed for reconstruction was required around the center of the breast. The extension direction of the TE assumed the shape of an arch (Fig. 1). In cases with less native skin, achieving a sufficient expansion of the TE was difficult. Considering these factors, although a 2-stage procedure using a TE was performed, it was difficult to obtain adequate chest skin around the center of the breast. In these cases, the skin paddle remained visible to preserve the original breast contour.

With submuscular placement of the TE, superior migration can occur, and expansion of the lower pole may be limited.6 Although the TE was inserted inferior to the IMF, superior migration of the TE could be confirmed. This indicates the presence of migration of the top of the breast projection. Such migration could increase the postoperative angle, but it could also decrease the postoperative length of the upper pole (Fig. 1).

Recently, the prepectoral placement of the TE has been revisited to reduce chest wall morbidities.7 In this approach, the use of an acellular dermal matrix (ADM) is common.8 Temporarily placing the TE in the prepectoral space without ADM demonstrated complication rates that are comparable to those of the technique using robust mastectomy flaps.9 In primary breast reconstruction, some patients did not have robust mastectomy flaps due to the presence of wound dehiscence from total mastectomy. Owing to the TE exposure, prepectoral placement was inappropriate for these patients. Moreover, ADM has not yet been approved in Japan.9 Therefore, the submuscular placement of the TE was adopted for most cases of delayed-immediate ABR in Japan.

Regarding the volume of TE expansion, the weight of the excised breast tissue was adopted, instead of the volume. Although an analysis using the volume measurements may have been more valuable, measuring the volume of a flap under sterile conditions is not easy. Minimal differences in density were observed between the flap and the breast tissue.10 Except for the nipple-sparing mastectomy cases, the breast skin was excised and there was no sufficient skin pocket. In these cases, overexpansion was recommended to help restore the original breast contour.11 Another study has reported that the desired level of overexpansion was 20%–30% in comparison to the contralateral breast.11

The present study revealed some novel findings and clinical indications. In delayed-immediate ABR, the postoperative length of the upper pole decreased, whereas the postoperative angle increased. In clinical cases, this problem could be due to the following factors: TE was not sufficiently placed in an inferior position, the submuscular pocket was dissected extensively, the pocket was adjusted inappropriately when replacing the TE with a flap, and the flap was inserted in an inappropriate position. The preoperative upper pole contour was considerably preserved in patients who underwent immediate reconstruction after skin-sparing mastectomy. Therefore, immediate reconstruction may be appropriate for achieving upper pole symmetry when no surgical intervention is planned for the contralateral breast. In contrast, when patients prefer a breast lift and a fuller upper pole, restoring the original breast contour is less important. When these patients intend to undergo contralateral breast surgery, delayed-immediate ABR may be a suitable option. When both the plastic surgeon and patient were able to effectively communicate their preferences regarding the shape and relations of the breast, they would be more successful at achieving a satisfactory result.

The present study has some limitations. The total submuscular placement of the TE inherently determined the changes in the breast pocket and footprint, which could not be erased once the muscle was put back in place and even if the flap was then positioned subcutaneously. Although it restricted the volume of TE expansion to be within the weight of the excised breast tissue plus 10%, it could result in an overfill. A volume measurement procedure may be required to determine the optimal volume of the TE expansion.

CONCLUSIONS

In the present study involving patients who underwent unilateral primary ABR using a DIEP flap, the morphology of the upper pole was assessed using the lateral images of the breast. The natural upper pole was preserved in immediate ABR after skin-sparing mastectomy. Postoperative upper pole length decreased, and its angle increased in delayed-immediate ABR.

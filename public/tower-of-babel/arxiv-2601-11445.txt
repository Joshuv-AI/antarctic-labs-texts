Stochastic Perturbation of Sweeping Process for Uniformly Prox-Regular Moving Sets Journal: Nuclear Physics B
Juan Guillermo Garrido, Nabil Kazi-Tani, Emilio Vilches

Abstract
In this paper, we study the existence of solutions to a sweeping process in the presence of stochastic perturbations, where the moving set takes uniformly prox-regular values and varies continuously with respect to the Hausdorff distance, without smoothness assumptions. We consider several geometric assumptions and establish important relationships between them.

Introduction

The sweeping process is a first-order differential inclusion involving the normal cone to a family of moving sets. Introduced by J.J. Moreau in a series of seminal papers (see, e.g.,
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
), it was motivated by concrete applications in quasi-static evolution in elastoplasticity, contact mechanics, and friction dynamics (see, e.g.,
[
30
,
31
]
). Since then, it has been used to model dynamical systems with time-dependent constraints, notably in nonsmooth mechanics, crowd motion (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
25
]
), and electrical-circuit modeling
[
1
]
. In its simplest form, the sweeping process reads as

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}(t)&\in f(t,x(t))-N^{P}(C(t);x(t))&&\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,T],\\
x(0)&=x_{0}\in C(0),\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space,
C\colon[0,T]\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is a set-valued map and
f\colon[0,T]\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a mapping. Here
N^{P}(C(t);x(t))
denotes the proximal normal cone to the set
C(t)
at
x(t)\in C(t)
. The above differential inclusion has been thoroughly studied in the case of nonconvex moving sets, notably for uniformly prox-regular sets (a generalization of convexity), for which well-posedness is well established (see, e.g.,
[
42
]
).

Motivated by the need to model uncertainty and randomness, in this paper we study the existence of solutions to the following stochastic sweeping differential inclusion

dX_{t}\in-N^{P}(C(t);X_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t}+f(t,X_{t})dt,

(2)

which corresponds to a stochastically perturbed version of the sweeping process. We work in a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and consider the case where the moving set
C\colon[0,T]\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
takes closed, uniformly prox-regular values and depends continuously on time with respect to the Hausdorff distance. To the best of our knowledge, this setting is new and represents the most general framework studied to date for stochastic sweeping processes with uniformly prox-regular moving sets.

The differential inclusion (
2
) can be viewed as a generalization of the Skorokhod problem, which describes the reflection of a given trajectory at the boundary of a domain, with the direction of reflection taken to be normal (see, e.g.,
[
40
]
).

For the time-independent case of the set
C
, we can mention
[
41
]
, where existence results were obtained for convex sets with nonempty interior. Later, in
[
24
]
, existence was established in a more general setting under the assumption that
C
is uniformly prox-regular and satisfies additional geometric conditions. In
[
39
]
, some of these geometric assumptions were relaxed (in concrete, the smoothness assumption was removed) while preserving the same existence results.

The time-dependent case was first addressed in
[
13
]
, where the authors considered a moving set
C
that varies continuously with respect to the Hausdorff distance, takes uniformly prox-regular values, and satisfies a smoothness condition on the boundary. Subsequently, in
[
34
]
, the case of oblique (not necessarily normal) reflection was analyzed under certain geometric assumptions. In
[
4
]
, the results of
[
39
,
24
]
were extended to the time-dependent setting, assuming that the moving set is absolutely continuous with respect to the Hausdorff distance. More recently, in
[
9
,
8
]
, existence was established for time-dependent convex moving sets with nonempty interior, without additional geometric conditions.

While these contributions have significantly advanced the theory, the case of stochastically perturbed sweeping processes with time-dependent, uniformly prox-regular moving sets has, to the best of our knowledge, not yet been systematically addressed. The purpose of this work is to help fill this gap by establishing existence results within a framework that appears to be the most general studied so far.

The commonly adopted approach to deal with stochastic sweeping processes consists first in studying a deterministic Skorokhod problem (see, e.g.,
[
39
,
41
,
24
,
4
]
), which then provides a natural interpretation when the perturbation is stochastic. Our goal is therefore twofold: on the one hand, to study the existence of sweeping process dynamics when the moving set is Hausdorff-continuous and takes uniformly prox-regular values, exploring different geometric hypotheses inspired by the related literature; on the other hand, to extend these existence results to the stochastic framework.

Contributions

Our contributions are threefold.

(i)
Geometric framework:
We introduce a minimal geometric setup for moving sets with uniformly prox-regular values and merely Hausdorff-continuous time dependence, and we
systematize the assumptions by making their logical implications explicit and
by positioning them within the existing literature. In particular, we identify
what is, to the best of our knowledge, the weakest verifiable condition
available for Skorokhod-type problems, and we provide practical criteria that
apply, in particular, to constraints given as finite intersections of sublevel
sets. This first part furnishes the geometric foundation for the study of
stochastic sweeping processes.

(ii)
Deterministic Skorokhod/sweeping process problem:
Within this geometric setting, we
prove new well-posedness results for the deterministic sweeping process
(equivalently, its Skorokhod-type formulation) under continuous perturbations.
We also derive quantitative bounds on the total variation of the correction
term, including uniform estimates for families of equicontinuous perturbations.

(iii)
Stochastic sweeping process:
We establish existence of weak and
strong solutions, as well as pathwise uniqueness, for stochastic perturbations
of the sweeping process driven by moving constraint sets that are nonconvex and
nonsmooth. To the best of our knowledge, these results hold under the most
general set of assumptions currently available in this framework.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the necessary mathematical background and notation used throughout the paper. Section
3
introduces the main geometric hypotheses considered in the literature, together with examples illustrating their validity. In Section
4
, we establish relationships between the geometric hypotheses introduced in Section
3
. Section
5
recalls an important existence and uniqueness result for the sweeping process from
[
11
]
and provides an estimate of the total variation of the solution for a perturbed version of (
1
). Section
6
addresses (
2
) via the Skorokhod problem and establishes a weak existence result under general geometric assumptions, together with pathwise uniqueness in the Lipschitz case, where we also obtain strong existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
2
). Finally, some technical proofs present in the work are included in the appendix.

For a given set

\sigma(x;S):=\sup_{z\in S}\langle x,z\rangle\quad\textrm{and}\quad d(x;S):=\inf_{z\in S}\|x-z\|.

Given two sets

When the above set is a singleton, we denote its unique element by

.

Let

f(y)\geq f(x)+\left\langle\zeta,y-x\right\rangle-\sigma\|y-x\|^{2}\textrm{ for all }y\in\mathbb{B}_{\eta}(x).

Using this notion, the
proximal normal cone
of a set
S\subset\mathcal{H}
at

is

N^{P}(S;x):=\partial_{P}I_{S}(x),

where

is the indicator function of
S\subset\mathcal{H}
(i.e.,
I_{S}(x)=0
if

and

) that, for all

,

N^{P}(S;x)\cap\mathbb{B}=\partial_{P}d_{S}(x).

(3)

We recall the concept of uniformly prox-regular sets. It was introduced by Federer in the finite-dimensional case (see
[
16
]
) and later developed by Rockafellar, Poliquin, and Thibault
[

]
. Prox-regularity generalizes and unifies convexity and nonconvex bodies with

Definition 2.1
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{H}
be nonempty and closed and
\rho\in]0,+\infty]
. We say that

is
\rho
-uniformly prox-regular
if, for all

and

,

\langle\zeta,x^{\prime}-x\rangle\leq\frac{\|\zeta\|}{2\rho}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}\textrm{ for all }x^{\prime}\in S.

Note that every closed convex set is
\rho
-uniformly prox-regular for every
\rho>0
. The following proposition provides a characterization of uniformly prox-regular sets
(see, e.g.,
[

]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

be a closed set and
\rho\in]0,+\infty]

(a)

S
is
\rho
-uniformly prox-regular.

(b)

For any positive
\gamma<1
the mapping
\proj_{S}
is well-defined on
U_{\rho}^{\gamma}(S)
and Lipschitz continuous on
U_{\rho}^{\gamma}(S)
with
(1-\gamma)^{-1}
as a Lipschitz constant, i.e.,

\left\|\proj_{S}\left(u_{1}\right)-\proj_{S}\left(u_{2}\right)\right\|\leq(1-\gamma)^{-1}\left\|u_{1}-u_{2}\right\|\quad

for all

The space of continuous functions from
[0,T]
to

is denoted by

. We endow this space with the usual supremum norm, that is, for

\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|.

We set

\mathcal{C}_{0}([0,T];\mathcal{H})=\{w\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H}):w(0)=0\}.

A family of functions

.

Definition 2.3
.

A set-valued mapping
C\colon[0,T]\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
is said to be Hausdorff-continuous if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

d_{H}(C(s),C(t))<\varepsilon\textrm{ for all }s,t\in[0,T]\text{ with }|t-s|<\delta.

For a set-valued mapping
C\colon[0,T]\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
, define its modulus of continuity by

\mathscr{P}_{C}(r):=\sup\{d_{H}(C(t),C(s)):t,s\in[0,T],|t-s|\leq r\}.

(4)

If
C\colon[0,T]\to\mathcal{H}
is Hausdorff-continuous, then
\displaystyle\lim_{r\searrow 0}\mathscr{P}_{C}(r)=0
and the map
r\mapsto\mathscr{P}_{C}(r)
is nondecreasing, so we may define its (right) generalized inverse by

\mathscr{P}_{C}^{-1}(x):=\inf\{r\geq 0:\mathscr{P}_{C}(r)\geq x\},

with the convention
\inf\emptyset=\infty
. Moreover, we observe that
\mathscr{P}_{C}(r)\geq x>0
implies that
r\geq\mathscr{P}_{C}^{-1}(x)>0
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
A\subset\mathcal{C}([0,T];\mathcal{H})
is an equicontinuous set, and let
C
be a set-valued mapping with closed values that is Hausdorff-continuous on
[0,T]
. For every
h\in A
, define
C_{h}:=C-h
. Then

\lim_{r\searrow 0}\sup_{h\in A}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h}}(r)=0.

Moreover, for all
\eta>0
,
\displaystyle\inf_{h\in A}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h}}^{-1}(\eta)>0
.

Proof
.
Consider
\varepsilon>0
. First, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that for all
r\in]0,\delta_{1}[
, we have
\mathscr{P}_{C}(r)<\varepsilon/2
. Moreover, by equicontinuity, there exists
\delta_{2}>0
such that for every

|t-s|<\delta_{2}

\sup_{h\in A}\|h(t)-h(s)\|<\varepsilon/2.

Set
\delta:=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
. Then, for

and

|t-s|<r

d_{H}(C_{h}(t),C_{h}(s))\leq d_{H}(C(t),C(s))+\|h(t)-h(s)\|\leq\mathscr{P}_{C}(r)+\varepsilon/2\leq\varepsilon,

which proves that
\displaystyle\lim_{r\searrow 0}\sup_{h\in\mathcal{A}}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h}}(r)=0
. Finally, suppose that for certain
\eta>0
,
\inf_{h\in A}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h}}^{-1}(\eta)=0
, then there is a sequence
(h_{n})\subset A

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h_{n}}}^{-1}(\eta)=0

\mathscr{P}_{C_{h_{n}}}^{-1}(\eta)+\frac{1}{n}\geq r_{n}

\mathscr{P}_{C_{h_{n}}}(r_{n})\geq\eta

\sup_{h\in A}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h}}(r_{n})\geq\eta,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{h\in A}\mathscr{P}_{C_{h}}(r_{n})=0

\hfill\quad\square

3
Main Geometric Hypotheses

In this section, we present the main hypotheses that will be assumed throughout the paper concerning the set-valued mapping
C\colon[0,T]\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
.

(\textsf{H}_{1})

For all
t\in[0,T]
,
C(t)
is nonempty, closed and
\rho
-uniformly prox-regular for some
\rho>0
.

(\textsf{H}_{2})

The set-valued map
C
is Hausdorff-continuous.

(\textsf{H}_{3})

For all
t\in[0,T]
,
{\operatorname{bd\ }}C(t)
is compact.

(\textsf{H}_{4})

There exist

such that for all
t\in[0,T]
and

, there exists

such that

\operatorname{co}(\{x\}\cup\mathbb{B}_{2r}(z(t,x)))\subset C(t)\text{ and }\|z(t,x)-x\|\leq Lr.

(\textsf{H}_{5})

There exist
\delta>0
,
L>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in{\operatorname{bd\ }}C(t)
, there exists
\ell_{x}(t)\in\mathbb{S}
satisfying

\sup_{y\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(x)\cap C(t)}\sigma(\ell_{x}(t);N^{P}(C(t);y)\cap\mathbb{S})\leq-\frac{1}{L}.

(

4

(

5

)

based on

(

H
5

)

is actually less restrictive (i.e., admissibility implies

(

H
5

)

.

(

5
Deterministic Sweeping Process with Continuous Moving Sets

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and let
C\colon[0,T]\rightrightarrows\mathcal{H}
be a set-valued map. Since, in general, one cannot expect solutions of the sweeping process to be absolutely continuous unless the mapping
t\rightrightarrows C(t)
has sufficient regularity (e.g., is absolutely continuous or Lipschitz continuous), we adopt Moreau’s measure-theoretic formulation (see
[
29
]
). In this setting, the “derivative" of a trajectory

dx
with respect to its total variation measure
|dx|

Definition 5.1

Given

(i)

(ii)

x(0)=x_{0}
;

(iii)

the vector measure

is absolutely continuous with respect

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{dx}{|dx|}(t)&\in\ -N^{P}\!\big(C(t);x(t)\big)\quad\text{ for }|dx|\text{-a.e. }t\in[0,T],\\
x(0)&=x_{0}\in C(0).\end{aligned}\right.

(
\mathsf{SP}
)

]

SP

) in the setting where

, which is an open set of
[0,T]
. For
t\in I

. Then
\ell(t)>t
and
\operatorname{var}(x_{h};[t,\ell(t)])=0
. Set

. If
x_{0}\in I

t_{1}:=\min\{T,\ell(t_{0})\}
(otherwise, set
t_{1}:=t_{0}
). Since

, set

. If
t_{2}\in I
, define
t_{3}:=\min\{T,\ell(t_{2})\}
(otherwise, set
t_{3}:=t_{2}
), and then define
t_{4}:=\min\{T,t_{3}+\delta\}
. Proceeding inductively, let
n:=\inf\{k\in\mathbb{N}:t_{k}=T\}
; by the construction

. Let
J:=\{j\leq n-1:x_{h}(t_{j})\in{\operatorname{bd\ }}C_{h}(t_{j})\}
. Then
\text{card}(J)\leq\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor+1
(where
\text{card}(J)
denotes the cardinality of
J
). Therefore,

\operatorname{var}(x_{h};[0,T])=\sum_{j\in J}\operatorname{var}(x_{h};[t_{j},t_{j+1}])\leq\text{card}(J)\frac{\rho}{2}\leq\frac{\rho}{2}(\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor+1),

where we used (
10
). Consequently,
\sup_{h\in A}\operatorname{var}(x_{h};[0,T])\leq\frac{\rho}{2}(\lfloor T/\delta\rfloor+1)
, which proves the result.

\hfill\quad\square

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose that

Proof
.
By Proposition
5.6
and Remark
5.7
, we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{V}:=\sup_{x_{0}\in C(0)}\sup_{h\in A}\operatorname{var}(x_{h}(\cdot;x_{0});[0,T])<\infty.

(a) Take
u,v\in A
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in C(0)
. Consider the solution
x_{u}(\cdot;x_{0})
of
(\mathsf{SP}_{u})
starting from
x_{0}
and the solution
x_{v}(\cdot;y_{0})
of
(\mathsf{SP}_{v})
starting from
y_{0}
. For brevity, we write
x_{u}:=x_{u}(\cdot;x_{0})
and
x_{v}:=x_{v}(\cdot;y_{0})
. Define the finite measure

. Observe that
|dx_{u}|
-a.e.
-\frac{dx_{u}}{|dx_{u}|}(t)\in N^{P}(C(t)-u(t);x_{u}(t))
, due to the uniform prox-regularity of

and the fact that for all

\left\langle-\frac{dx_{u}}{|dx_{u}|}(t),x_{v}(t)+v(t)-(x_{u}(t)+u(t))\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{2\rho}\|x_{v}(t)+v(t)-(x_{u}(t)+u(t))\|^{2}

Since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle-\frac{dx_{u}}{d\mu}(t),x_{v}(t)+v(t)-(x_{u}(t)+u(t))\right\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{u}(t)+u(t)-(x_{v}(t)+v(t))\|^{2}|dx_{u}|.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle-\frac{dx_{v}}{d\mu}(t),x_{u}(t)+u(t)-(x_{v}(t)+v(t))\right\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{u}(t)+u(t)-(x_{v}(t)+v(t))\|^{2}|dx_{v}|.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle-\frac{dx_{u}}{d\mu}(t)+\frac{dx_{v}}{d\mu}(t),x_{v}(t)+v(t)-(x_{u}(t)+u(t))\right\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle-\frac{dx_{u}}{d\mu}(s)+\frac{dx_{v}}{d\mu}(s),u(s)-v(s)\right\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{u}(s)-x_{v}(s)+(u(s)-v(s))\|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|^{2}+2\|u-v\|_{\infty}(\operatorname{var}(x_{u};[0,t])+\operatorname{var}(x_{v};[0,t]))

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mathscr{V}}{\rho}\|u-v\|_{\infty}^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{u}(s)-x_{v}(s)\|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|x_{0}-y_{0}\|^{2}+4\mathscr{V}\|u-v\|_{\infty}+\frac{2\mathscr{V}}{\rho}\|u-v\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\rho}\int_{0}^{t}\|x_{u}(s)-x_{v}(s)\|^{2}d\mu.

From Gronwall’s inequality (see Lemma

), for all

satisfies the claim.

(b) The set
\mathscr{B}:=\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\cup\{u\}
is compact in

for the uniform topology; hence

uniformly.

\hfill\quad\square

6
Stochastic Perturbation of Sweeping Process

In this section,
\mathcal{H}
stands for a
d
-dimensional Euclidean space, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and let
B
be an
\ell
-dimensional Brownian motion.
Formally, we are interested in the well-posedness of the following stochastic sweeping process

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dX_{t}\in-N^{P}(C(t);X_{t})dt+\sigma(t,X_{t})dB_{t}+f(t,X_{t})dt,\\
X_{0}=\xi,\end{array}\right.

(11)

where
\xi\colon\Omega\to C(0)
represents a random initial condition.
A reasonable formulation is to look for a process
(X_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{t}\in C(t),\forall t\in[0,T],\\
X_{t}=Y_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X_{s})ds,\forall t\in[0,T]\\
\frac{dY_{t}}{|dY|_{t}}\in-N^{P}(C(t);X_{t}),\ \left|dY\right|\text{-a.e., }\\
X_{0}=\xi.\end{array}\right.

(12)

Defining
\mathcal{R}(X)_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,X_{s})dB_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}f(s,X_{s})ds
, (
12
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}X_{t}\in C(t),\forall t\in[0,T],\\
X_{t}=Y_{t}+\mathcal{R}(X)_{t},\forall t\in[0,T]\\
\frac{dY_{t}}{|dY|_{t}}\in-N^{P}(C(t)-\mathcal{R}(X)_{t};Y_{t}),\ \left|dY\right|\text{-a.e., }\\
X_{0}=\xi.\end{array}\right.

(13)

For every
\eta\in C(0)
and
h\in\mathcal{C}_{0}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define (when it exists) the process
Z^{h}_{\eta}
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{dZ^{h}_{\eta}}{|dZ^{h}_{\eta}|}(t)\in-N^{P}(C(t)-h(t);Z^{h}_{\eta}(t)),\ \left|dZ^{h}_{\eta}\right|\text{-a.e., }\\
Z^{h}_{\eta}(0)=\eta.\end{array}\right.

(14)

The differential inclusion above is commonly known as the Skorokhod problem with normal reflection on the boundary of
C
(now dependent on time), where the solution is typically considered as
Z^{h}_{\eta}+h
. The usual formulation of Skorokhod problem is actually described in a different way (see, e.g.,
[
39
,
41
,
24
,
4
,
40
]
), but in our case the cone of reflection is the normal cone, hence (
14
) is an equivalent form. By virtue of (
13
), the well-posedness of (
12
) depends directly on the existence of solutions to (
14
). Our goal is to study the existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
12
), assuming that the moving set
C
takes closed and uniformly prox-regular values, and it only moves continuously with respect to the Hausdorff distance. In this scenario, the problem (
14
) has a unique solution provided the satisfaction of

(

H
4

)

(see Corollary
5.3
).

We now introduce some notation. As usual, for a random variable
Z
,
\sigma(Z)
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
Z
. When
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a continuous process, we set
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{X}:=\sigma(X_{s}:s\leq t)
. As usual, for two given
\sigma
-algebras
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{F}\vee\mathcal{G}
denotes the smallest
\sigma
-algebra containing
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
simultaneously. For two continuous semimartingales
(M^{1}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(M_{t}^{2})_{t\geq 0}
, we denote their quadratic covariation by
t\mapsto[M^{1},M^{2}]_{t}
, and use the standard shorthand
[M^{1}]_{t}:=[M^{1},M^{1}]_{t}
. Given two continuous stochastic processes
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, if they share the same law we write
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathscr{L}}}{{=}}Y
. In what follows, all the filtrations are supposed to hold the usual conditions, i.e. they are completed and right-continuous.

Concerning
f\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times\ell}
, assume they are Carathéodory mappings, i.e. for all
t\in[0,T]
,

are continuous and for all

,

are measurable. Moreover, we assume that there exist
\beta_{f}\in L^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})
with

and
\beta_{\sigma}\in L^{q}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})
with

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|f(t,x)\|\leq\beta_{f}(t)\text{ and }\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\sigma(t,x)\|\leq\beta_{\sigma}(t)\text{ for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

(15)

As usual,
\sigma_{\cdot,j}
denotes the
j
-th column of
\sigma
and
\sigma_{i,\cdot}
denotes its
i
-th row.
As in the theory of stochastic differential equations, we distinguish different types of solutions.

.

defined on

Given a probability space

Theorem 6.4

(

First, regarding the hypotheses on

Since

[

there are sequences of continuous functions
(\hat{f}_{n}\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d})

\hat{\beta}_{f}\in L^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})

\hat{\beta}_{\sigma}\in L^{q}([0,T];\mathbb{R}_{+})

Claim 1

(b)

(\tilde{x}_{n_{k}},\tilde{B}_{n_{k}},\tilde{Y}_{n_{k}})\to(\tilde{x}_{0},\tilde{B},\tilde{Y})

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}

,

\displaystyle\leq C\cdot\mathbb{E}\left(\left(\int_{s}^{t}\sigma_{n}(\tau,X_{n}(\delta_{n}(\tau)))^{2}_{ij}d\tau\right)^{\alpha/2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{s}^{t}\hat{\beta}_{\sigma}(\tau)^{2}d\tau\right)^{\alpha/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{s}^{t}\hat{\beta}_{\sigma}(\tau)^{q}d\tau\right)^{\alpha/q}|t-s|^{\alpha\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)}

and we also have

A.4
Proof of Lemma
4.5

Take sequences
\xi_{n}\to\overline{\xi}
,
t_{n}\to\overline{t}
and
x_{n}\to\overline{x}
such that

\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\to\overline{x},t\to\overline{t}\\
\xi\to\overline{\xi}\end{subarray}}\sigma(\xi;\partial_{P}d_{C(t)}(x)\cap\mathbb{S})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma(\xi_{n};\partial_{P}d_{C(t_{n})}(x_{n})\cap\mathbb{S}).

By compactness, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
v_{n}\in\partial_{P}d_{C(t_{n})}(x_{n})\cap\mathbb{S}
such that

\sigma(\xi_{n};\partial_{P}d_{C(t_{n})}(x_{n})\cap\mathbb{S})=\langle\xi_{n},v_{n}\rangle.

Passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume
v_{n}\to v\in\mathbb{S}
. We now show that
v\in\partial_{P}d_{C(\overline{t})}(\overline{x})
. Take any
y\in C(\overline{t})
. Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N
,
y\in C(t_{n})+\frac{\rho}{2}\mathbb{B}
. Since
x_{n}\to x
, we may also assume
x_{n}\in C(t_{n})+\frac{\rho}{2}\mathbb{B}
for all
n\geq N
. By
[
33
, Theorem 5]
, for all
n\geq N
we have

\displaystyle\langle v_{n},y-x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\rho}\|y-x_{n}\|^{2}+d_{C(t_{n})}(y)-d_{C(t_{n})}(x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\rho}\|y-x_{n}\|^{2}+d_{H}(C(t_{n}),C(\overline{t})).

Letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain
\langle v,y-\overline{x}\rangle\leq\frac{2}{\rho}\|y-\overline{x}\|^{2}
. Since this holds for all
y\in C(\overline{t})
, it follows that
v\in N^{P}(C(\overline{t});\overline{x})
, and hence
v\in\partial_{P}d_{C(\overline{t})}(\overline{x})
. Finally, we observe

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sigma(\xi_{n};\partial_{P}d_{C(t_{n})}(x_{n})\cap\mathbb{S})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle\xi_{n},v_{n}\rangle=\langle\overline{\xi},v\rangle\leq\sigma(\overline{\xi};\partial_{P}d_{C(\overline{t})}(\overline{x})\cap\mathbb{S}),

which completes the proof.∎

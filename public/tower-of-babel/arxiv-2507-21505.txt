Conjugator Length in the Baumslag-Gersten group
Conan Gillis

Abstract
. We show that the conjugator length function of the Baumslag-Gersten group is bounded above and below by a tower of exponentials of logarithmic height – in particular it grows faster than any tower of exponentials of fixed height. We conjecture that no one-relator group has a larger conjugator length function than the Baumslag-Gersten group. Along the way, we also show that the conjugator length function of the m m -th iterated Baumslag-Solitar groups is equivalent to the m m -times iterated exponential function.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be the Baumslag-Gersten one-relator group
\langle a,t\mid tat^{-1}a(tat^{-1})^{-1}=a^{2}\rangle
. The word problem of
G
and related questions have received considerable attention in the literature. For example, the Dehn function of
G
is equal (up to a natural equivalence
\simeq
; see Definition
1.1
) to an exponential power-tower of height
\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor

[
Pla04
]
; in particular, the Dehn function of
G
grows faster than any tower of exponentials of fixed height (see
[
Ger92
]
and
[
Ril17
]
). More recently, the word problem was shown to be solvable in time
O(n^{7})
using the technology of “power-circuits” to efficiently represent and compute with certain large numbers
[
MUW11
,
MUW12
]
. The conjugacy problem in
G
is also decidable, however the current best estimate of its complexity is not elementary-recursive
[
Bee12
]
. Restricting to the special case of
u,v\in X=G\smallsetminus\langle a,tat^{-1}\rangle_{G}
, the authors of
[
DMW16
]
give an
O(n^{4})
-time algorithm deciding whether
u
and
v
are conjugate, and that this set
X
is “generic” in a natural way, so the conjugacy problem for
G
is generically decidable in polynomial time. They further conjecture that there is no algorithm whose worst-case or average running time is polynomial. Most recently, Mattes and Weiß showed that, for fixed
g\in G
, deciding whether a given element is conjugate to
G
is in
\mathsf{uTC}^{1}

[
MW22
]
.

Now, fix a finite presentation
\langle S\mid R\rangle
of a finitely presented group
H
, and let
\left|\cdot\right|_{H}
be the resulting word metric on
H
. Given two words
u
and
v
on
S
representing conjugate elements of
H
, we define
c(u,v)
to be the smallest value of
\left|\gamma\right|_{H}
over all
\gamma\in H
conjugating
u
to
v
. The
conjugator length function

\hbox{\rm CL}_{H}(n)
of
H
is the maximum value of
c(u,v)
over all conjugate pairs
(u,v)
such that
\left|u\right|_{H}+\left|v\right|_{H}\leq n
. Although
\hbox{\rm CL}_{H}(n)
depends on the presentation chosen for
H
, it can be shown that its
\simeq
-equivalence-class does not.

Conjugator length functions have lately gained attention as a way of quantifying the difficulty of the conjugacy problem in finitely presented groups. This is in close analogy with the relationship between the word problem and the Dehn function. For example, modulo the complexity of the word problem, the conjugator length function can be used to find an upper bound for the computational complexity of the conjugacy problem in a group. We refer the reader to
[
BRS25
]
for an up-to-date survey on this and other applications.

We continue the study of the conjugacy problem of
G
by computing close upper and lower bounds for
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)
. As preparation, we also compute exactly the conjugator length function of the
m
-th iterated Baumslag-Solitar group

G_{m}=\langle s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{m}\mid s_{i}s_{i-1}s_{i}^{-1}=s_{i-1}^{2}\ \forall i=1,\ldots,m\rangle
for all
m
. To state our results fully, we require the following definitions:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
f,g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
. We say
f(n)\preceq g(n)
if there exists some
C>0
such that
f(n)\leq Cg(Cn+C)+Cn+C
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
f(n)\simeq g(n)
if
f(n)\preceq g(n)
and
g(n)\preceq f(n)
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the iterated exponential function
E(m,n)
inductively as follows:
E(0,n)=n
,
E(m,n)=2^{E(m-1,n)}
.

Our first result is the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

We have
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G_{m}}(n)\simeq E(m-1,n)
.

The upper bound of this statement is shown in Proposition
3.4
, which is proved by first conjugating
u
and
v
into the subgroup
\langle s_{0},\ldots,s_{r}\rangle_{G_{m}}
,
r
being the smallest number such that
u
and
v
are conjugate to an element of
\langle s_{0},\ldots,s_{r}\rangle_{G_{m}}
(Lemma
3.2
). The proof of Proposition
3.4
then proceeds by analysis of annular diagrams over
G_{m}
(defined in Section
2.2
) using facts about the distortion of the subgroups
\langle s_{i}\rangle_{G_{m}}
and conjugator length in the subgroups
\langle s_{i},s_{i-1}\rangle_{G_{m}}
(Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
respectively).

The lower bound is shown in in Proposition
4.6
, where we follow
[
MUW11
,
MUW12
]
in representing each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
as sums of the form
\sum_{j}n_{j}2^{m_{j}}
. However, while these papers use representations of this sort to efficiently perform arithmetic operations on large numbers, we use them to find lower bounds for
\left|s_{0}^{k}\right|_{G_{m}}
for specific values of
k
(Corollary
4.3
). We then show
|s_{1}^{2}|_{G_{m}}+|s_{0}^{E(m,n)-1}s_{1}^{2}|_{G_{m}}\leq Cn
for some constant
C>0
(Lemma
4.4
). The proof proper of Proposition
4.6
begins by showing that any minimal length conjugator taking
s_{1}^{2}
to
s_{0}^{E(m,n)-1}s_{1}^{2}
must be equal (in
G_{m}
) to
s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}
, where
\alpha\leq 0
and
\beta=-2^{-\alpha}\cdot(E(m,n)-1)/3
(the factor of
-\frac{1}{3}
arises from the fact that
s_{0}^{\beta}s_{1}^{2}s_{0}^{-\beta}=s_{0}^{-3\beta}s_{1}^{2}
). Using the triangle inequality and a basic fact about the structure of
G_{m}
(Corollary
2.14
), we obtain
|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}\geq|s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}/2
, and complete the proof of the lower bound by applying Corollary
4.3
in the case where
k=\beta
. The length of the binary representation of
(E(m,n)-1)/3
is the key element in applying this Corollary.

The second result of our paper is:

Theorem 1.4
.

We have
E\left(\left\lfloor\frac{1}{3}\log_{2}n\right\rfloor-1,1\right)\preceq\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)\preceq E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)
.

For an intermediate upper bound of
nE(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)
, obtained in Proposition
5.5
, we adopt a new presentation (Lemma
2.17
and Remark
2.18
) for
G
:

G=\langle s_{0},s_{1},t\mid s_{1}s_{0}s_{1}^{-1}=s_{0}^{2},\ ts_{0}t^{-1}=s_{1}\rangle.

Then, we recall Platonov’s computation of the distortion functions of
\langle s_{0}\rangle_{G}
,
\langle s_{1}\rangle_{G}
in
G

[
Pla04
]
(Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
5.2
), and conjugate
u
and
v
to specific cyclically Britton-reduced conjugacy-class representatives (Lemma
5.3
). If
u,v\in X
, the desired upper bound follows by the length of the conjugator returned by the algorithm in
[
DMW16
, Theorem 3]
. For all other elements of
G
, our upper bound is established via a diagrammatic argument similar in spirit to that for Proposition
3.4
, except that more care is needed to account for the generator
t
. Corollary
5.6
then obtains the desired final upper bound, showing
nE(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)\preceq E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)
.

For the lower bound, shown in Proposition
6.5
, we reduce to consideration of conjugacy in the subgroup
\langle t^{-m}s_{0}t^{m},\ldots,t^{-1}s_{0}t,s_{0},ts_{0}t^{-1},\ldots,t^{m}s_{0}t^{-m}\rangle_{G}
for some
m
. We show (Lemma
2.17
) that this subgroup is isomorphic to
G_{2m}
, which lets us appeal to the lower bound of Theorem
1.3
, and in particular Corollary
4.3
, to complete the proof.

Remark 1.5
.

Neither of the bounds in Theorem
1.4
are (to our current knowledge) strict. Indeed, the upper and bounds themselves are very likely not equivalent, since removing the
\frac{1}{3}
factor from the lower bound seems to require increasing the input to size
n^{3}
, not
Cn+C
. Additionally,
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)
could be
\simeq
-equivalent to neither the upper or the lower bound.

In contrast to the word problem for one-relator groups
[
MKS76
]
, the decidability of their conjugacy problem is still an open question. This paper quantifies the conjugacy problem in one well-known case and shows that, in a sense (see
[
BRS25
]
), it is of similar difficulty to the word problem of that case. Inspired by Gersten’s conjecture in
[
Ger92
]
that the Dehn function of
G
is the largest among all one-relator groups, we pose the following question:

Question 1.6
.

For all one-relator groups
H
, is
\hbox{\rm CL}_{H}(n)\preceq\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)
?

A positive answer to this question would, by standard techniques, give a solution to the conjugacy problem for one-relator groups, while a counterexample may better illuminate some of the intrinsic difficulties to be overcome in finding such a solution.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Conventions and Notation

Definition 2.1
.

A
word

w
on a set
X
is a string of elements of
X
and their formal inverses, denoted
x^{-1}
for each
x\in X
. We say
w
is
reduced
if it contains no subwords of the form
xx^{-1}
or
x^{-1}x
. A
cyclic conjugate
of
w
is a word
w^{\prime}
which is a cyclic permutation of the letters of
w
, and
w
is
cyclically reduced
if every cyclic conjugate is reduced, or equivalently, if
w^{2}
is reduced. If
X
is the generating set of some group, we will abuse notation and simply write
w
for the element represented by
w
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
H
be a group with a fixed finite presentation
\langle S\mid R\rangle
. For all
h\in H
,
|h|_{H}
is the minimal length of word on
S
representing
h
in
H
. We call
|\cdot|_{H}
the
word-metric
on
H
. All of the groups in this paper will be finitely presented, and the presentation being used will be clearly specified. We will refer to the generating sets of the given presentations as
chosen generators
. Finally, we state at the outset that no two defining relations of any presentation will be cyclic permutations of each other.

2.2.
Conjugator Length and Annular Diagrams

We assume the reader is familiar with the theory of van Kampen diagrams over finitely presented groups as presented in
[
LS01
]
, and focus solely on annular diagrams. For this subsection, let
H=\langle S\mid R\rangle
be a finitely presented group, and suppose without loss of generality that
R
contains no two elements which are cyclic permutations of each other.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be words on
S
representing conjugate elements of
H
. An
annular diagram

\Delta
for
u
and
v
is a planar, oriented
2
-cell complex with edges labeled by
S
, along with points
P_{1},P_{2}
on the outer and inner boundary components respectively, such that

(1)

\Delta
is an annulus,

(2)

starting from
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
, the words along the outer and inner boundary components (oriented clockwise) are equal to
u
and
v
respectively, and

(3)

starting from some point along the boundary
\partial C
of a 2-cell
C
in
\Delta
, the word along
\partial C
is an element of
R^{\pm 1}
.

In such an annular diagram, the length
D(\Delta)
of the shortest path between
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
is the
diameter
. Also, a 2-cell
C
in
\Delta
is said to
correspond
to any
r\in R
such that
r^{\pm 1}
is (a cyclic conjugate of) the word along
\partial C
. By our assumption that
R
contains no elements which are cyclic permutations of each other, each 2-cell corresponds to a unique
r\in R
.

For the following fundamental result, see Lemmas V.5.1 and V.5.2 of
[
LS01
]
and their proofs:

Lemma 2.4
.

Two words
u
and
v
on
S
represent conjugate elements of
H
if and only if there exists an annular diagram for
u
and
v
. Moreover,
c(u,v)=\min\{D(\Delta)\}
, where this minimum is taken over all annular diagrams
\Delta
for
u
and
v
.

Now, we sketch a proof of a technical lemma we will use below.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
be two non-crossing simple loops in an annular diagram
\Delta
such that
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
have the same non-zero winding number around the inner boundary component of
\Delta
, and let
\Delta_{i}
be the annular subdiagram bounded by
\gamma_{i}
and the inner boundary. Both
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are homeomorphic to annuli, with either
\Delta_{1}\subseteq\Delta_{2}
or
\Delta_{2}\subseteq\Delta_{1}
. Moreover, if the words along
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are the same, then the subdiagram
\Delta_{2}\smallsetminus\Delta_{1}
can be excised to obtain a diagram with smaller or equal diameter.

Proof Sketch:

The first conclusion follows immediately from the assumptions on
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
. For the second, proceeding naively, one can simply delete all of
\Delta_{2}\smallsetminus\Delta_{1}
and identify the paths
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
together. There exist cases, however, where this operation may violate planarity. As in the proof van Kampen’s Theorem, this can be handled by the operations of “sewing” and “detatchment”: identifying each pair of edges of the paths sequentially, and removing any 2-cells which violate planarity (see pg. 150-151 of
[
LS01
]
for details).

∎

2.3.
Corridors

Definition 2.6
.

Except for the single defining relation in the presentation
G=\langle a,t\mid(tat)a(tat)^{-1}=a^{2}\rangle
, all of the defining relations considered in this paper will be written in the form
xwx^{-1}\tilde{w}^{-1}
for some
x
not appearing in
w
or
\tilde{w}
. In a (van Kampen or annular) diagram
\Delta
, a maximal sequence
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{k}
of 2-cells such that
C_{i}
and
C_{i+1}
share an edge labeled by
x
is called an
x
-corridor
. A single
x
-edge on the boundary of a diagram is called a degenerate
x
-corridor
if it is not contained in any 2-cells.

Definition 2.7
.

Suppose
\Delta
has two 2-cells
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
which share at least one edge with an endpoint
P
, such that the word along
\partial C_{1}
read clockwise from
P
is the inverse of the word along
\partial C_{2}
read clockwise from
P
. Then
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are called
canceling 2-cells
. If we excise
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, then apply the same “sewing” and “detatchment” argument as Lemma
2.5
, we obtain a diagram
\Delta^{\prime}
with the same word(s) along the boundary, up to equality in
G
, and strictly fewer canceling pairs. A diagram is called
reduced
if it contains no canceling 2-cells.

The following lemma follows immediately from Definitions
2.6
and
2.7
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
H=\langle S\mid R\rangle
be a finitely presented group with no two elements of
R
being cyclic conjugates of each other, and let
x\in S
. Suppose there is only one relation in
R
of the form
xwx^{-1}\tilde{w}^{-1}
, and suppose
w,\tilde{w}
are cyclically reduced.
Then in a reduced annular diagram
\Delta
over
H
, the words along the sides of any
x
-corridor in
\Delta
are reduced.

Definition 2.9
.

There are four mutually exclusive possibilities for an
x
-corridor in an annular diagram
\Delta
:

(1)

it contains an
x
-edge on each boundary component of
\Delta
,

(2)

either it contains two
x
-edges on the same boundary component of
\Delta
, or is degenerate,

(3)

it forms a loop which bounds a contractible subdiagram of
\Delta
, and

(4)

it forms a loop which bounds a non-contractible subdiagram of
\Delta
.

The
x
-corridors satisfying each of these conditions are called
radial
x
-corridors,
x
-arches, contractible
x
-rings,
and
non-contractible
x
-rings
respectively. If
Q
is an
x
-corridor satisfying one of the latter two cases, then
Q
is itself an annulus; the
outer word
and and
inner word along
Q
are defined accordingly.

Definition 2.10
.

We say two radial
x
-corridors in
\Delta
are
consecutive
if they bound (along with the boundary components of
\Delta
) a sub-diagram containing no radial
x
-corridors; if there is only one such corridor in
\Delta
, we adopt the convention that it is consecutive to itself.

2.4.
Iterated Baumslag-Solitar Groups

Recall the presentation
G_{m}=\langle s_{0},\ldots,s_{m}\mid s_{i}s_{-1}s_{i}^{-1}=s_{i-1}^{2}\ \forall i=1,\ldots,m\rangle
of the
m
-th iterated Baumslag-Solitar group.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
i_{m}:G_{m}\to G_{m+1}
be the map given by
s_{i}\mapsto s_{i}
for
i=0,\ldots,m
. Then
i
is injective.

Proof.

Since
G_{1}=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)
is torsion free,
s_{1}
has infinite order in
G_{1}
. Assuming by induction that
s_{m}
has infinite order in
G_{m}
, taking the HNN-extension with stable letter
s_{m+1}
along the homomorphism
\langle s_{m}\rangle\to\langle s_{m}^{2}\rangle
gives the group
G_{m+1}
with the inclusion map
G_{m}\hookrightarrow G_{m+1}
equal to
i_{m}
, and with
s_{m+1}
having infinite order in
G_{m+1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

The homomorphism
\pi_{m}:G_{m}\to G_{m-1}
given by
s_{0}\mapsto 1
and
s_{i}\mapsto s_{i-1}
for
i\geq 1
is a retraction.

Proof.

The homomorphism
\pi_{m}^{\prime}:G_{m-1}\to G_{m}
defined by
s_{i}\mapsto s_{i+1}
is a left-inverse to
\pi_{m}
.
∎

Corollary 2.13
.

If
g\in\langle s_{i},\ldots,s_{m}\rangle_{G_{m}}
, then there exists a word
w
on
\{s_{i},\ldots,s_{m}\}
of length
|g|_{G_{m}}
such that
w=g
in
G_{m}
.

Proof.

Let
w_{0}
be any word of length
|g|_{G_{m}}
on
\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{m}\}
representing
g
, and let
w
be the word resulting from deleting
s_{j}^{\pm 1}
from
w_{0}
for
j<i
. By Lemma
2.12
,

w=(\pi^{\prime}_{m}\circ\pi_{m-1}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ\pi^{\prime}_{m-i}\circ\pi_{m-i}\circ\pi_{m-i+1}\circ\cdots\circ\pi_{m})(w_{0})=g.

Moreover,
w
is a word on
\{s_{i},\ldots,s_{m}\}
such that
|w|\leq|w_{0}|
.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
,
|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}\geq|s_{1}^{\alpha}|_{G_{m}}

Proof.

Let
w_{0}
be any representative word for
s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}
in
G_{m}
, and
w
the word resulting from deleting all
s_{0}
-letters from
w_{0}
. Then
w=\pi_{m}^{\prime}(\pi_{m}(w_{0}))=s_{1}^{\alpha}
in
G_{m}
and has length at most
|w_{0}|
.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

For all
i=0,\ldots,m-1,

|s_{i}^{k}|_{G_{m}}\leq C\log_{2}|k|
for some constant
C>0
.

Proof.

The claim is straightforward for
G_{1}
. For
m>1
, let

H=\langle s_{i},s_{i+1}\rangle_{G_{m}}=(\pi_{i}^{\prime}\circ\pi_{i-1}^{\prime}\circ\cdots\circ\pi_{2}^{\prime})(G_{1}).

This is isomorphic to
G_{1}
, and
|s_{i}^{k}|_{G_{m}}\leq|s_{i}^{k}|_{H}
because the chosen generating set of
H\leq G_{m}
is a subset of the chosen generating set of
G_{m}
, whence the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\leq m
, and let
u
be a word on
\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{m^{\prime}}\}
and
v
a word on
\{s_{0},\ldots,s_{m^{\prime\prime}}\}
containing an
s_{m^{\prime}}
- and
s_{m^{\prime\prime}}
-letter respectively, such that
u
and
v
represent conjugate elements of
G_{m}
. Let
\Delta
be a reduced annular diagram for
u
and
v
, and let
k
be the largest number such that there is an edge in
\Delta
labeled
s_{k}
. Then all
s_{k}
-edges in
\Delta
appear only in
s_{k}
-corridors. Moreover, if
\Delta
has no radial
s_{k}
-corridors or
s_{k}
-arches, then
m^{\prime}=m^{\prime\prime}=k-1
, otherwise,
k=\max\{m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\}
.

Proof.

That all
s_{k}
-edges must appear in
s_{k}
-corridors holds because the only relations in the presentation for
G_{m}
containing an
s_{k}
-letter yet no
s_{k+1}
letters is
s_{k}s_{k-1}s_{k}^{-1}=s_{k-1}^{2}
. Moreover,
k\geq\max\{m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\}
by definition. Note that
\Delta
has an
s_{k}
-corridor which is radial or an arch if and only if
u
or
v
has an
s_{k}
-letter, which occurs if and only if
k\leq\max\{m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\}
, hence
k=\max\{m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\}
in this case.

Suppose instead that there are no are radial
s_{k}
-corridors or
s_{k}
-arches in
\Delta
. Then
u
and
v
contain no
s_{k}
-letters, so
\max\{m^{\prime},m^{\prime\prime}\}<k
. Every
s_{k}
-edge is part of either a contractible or non-contractible
s_{k}
-ring. If there exists a contractible
s_{k}
-ring, Lemma
2.8
implies the word along its outer-boundary is a freely reduced word on
s_{k-1}
representing the identity in
G_{m}
. In the proof of Lemma
2.11
we observed that
s_{i}
has infinite order for all
i
, hence no such
s_{k}
-corridor can exist. Thus any
s_{k}
-edge must be contained in a non-contractible
s_{k}
-ring. Let
Q
be the
s_{k}
-corridor closest to the boundary component labeled by
u
, and let
\Delta^{\prime}
be the diagram contained between
Q
and that boundary component. By Lemma
2.8
, the word along the side of
Q
facing
\Delta
is a freely reduced power of
s_{k-1}
, and
\Delta^{\prime}
contains no
s_{k}
-edges, so every
s_{k-1}
-edge on the boundary component corresponding to
Q
is contained in a radial
s_{k-1}
-corridor. The set of
s_{k}
-edges, and thus the set of 2-cells, of
Q
is non-empty, so this implies
k-1\leq m^{\prime}
. By a similar argument,

(b)

no
w_{j}
is properly contained in a subword
w^{\prime}
of
w
with
w^{\prime}\in\langle s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{m}\rangle_{G_{m}}
;

(c)

for all

, every
s_{j^{\prime}}
-letter of

is contained in some

.

Perhaps after re-indexing, by

for some

such that

(1)

n_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{p}(|w_{j}|+|n_{j}|)=|s_{i}^{k}|_{G_{m}}.

Every
w_{j}
is equal in
G_{m}
to some word
w_{j}^{\prime}
with no
q
-pinches for

. The word
w^{\prime}=s_{i}^{n_{0}}w_{1}^{\prime}s_{i}^{n_{1}}w_{2}^{\prime}\cdots w_{p}^{\prime}s_{i}^{n_{p}}
equals
w
in
G_{m}
and satisfies items
(a)
,
(b)
, and
(c)
listed above. We claim that

contains no
s_{q^{\prime}}
-letters for

. Let

be the largest index of a letter appearing in

. If

every

-letter must be the endpoint of an

-pinch of
w^{\prime}
. Without loss of generality, suppose some

-pinch has one endpoint in
w_{1}^{\prime}
and the other in

, so
w_{1}^{\prime}=u_{1}s_{q_{0}}^{\pm 1}v_{1}
and
w_{2}^{\prime}=u_{2}s_{q_{0}}^{\mp 1}v_{2}
with
s_{q_{0}}^{\pm 1}v_{1}s_{i}^{n_{1}}u_{2}s_{q_{0}}^{\mp 1}
a

(b)

we thus have

for some

Now,
\langle s_{i},s_{i+1}\rangle_{G_{m}}
is isomorphic to

, wherein an straightforward computation shows that if

s_{i}^{n_{0}}s_{i+1}^{\ell_{1}}s_{i}^{n_{1}}s_{i+1}^{\ell_{2}}\cdots s_{i+1}^{\ell_{p}}s_{i}^{n_{p}}=s_{i}^{k},

then

where

m_{j}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{j}\ell_{\alpha}.

Our claim follows by equation (
1
).

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
k=n_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{p}2^{m_{j}}n_{j}
, where
n_{j}\neq 0
for all
j=0,\ldots,p
. Then
k
can be expressed as

k=\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{p^{\prime}}2^{m_{j}^{\prime}}\varepsilon_{j}

where
\varepsilon_{j}\in\{-1,1\}
for all
j
and
p^{\prime}\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{p}|n_{j}|
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
be the smallest index such that
n_{\lambda}\not\in\{-1,1\}
. Then,
n_{\lambda}=\pm(\varepsilon_{\lambda}+2^{k}n_{\lambda}^{\prime})
for some
\varepsilon_{\lambda}\in\{-1,0,1\}
and
n_{\lambda}^{\prime}
such that
|\varepsilon_{\lambda}|+|n_{\lambda}^{\prime}|<|n_{\lambda}|
. Replace
n_{\lambda}
with
2^{m_{\lambda}}\varepsilon_{\lambda}+2^{m_{\lambda}+k}n^{\prime}_{\lambda}
in our sum. Since

|\varepsilon_{\lambda}|+|n_{\lambda}^{\prime}|+\sum_{j=0}^{\lambda-1}|n_{j}|+\sum_{j=\lambda+1}^{p}|n_{j}|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{p}|n_{j}|,

we may repeat this at most
\sum_{j=0}^{p}|n_{j}|
-times to obtain a sum
k=\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{p^{\prime}}2^{m_{j}^{\prime}}\varepsilon_{j}
where
\varepsilon_{j}\in\{-1,1\}
for all

. Each iteration adds at most one term, so
p^{\prime}\leq p+\sum_{j=0}^{p}|n_{j}|\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{p}|n_{j}|
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
k=\sum_{j=0}^{p}2^{2j+\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
|s_{i}^{k}|_{G_{m}}\geq(p-1)/2
.

Proof.

Let
k=n_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{q}2^{m_{j}}n_{j}
be the representation of
k
with the smallest value of
|n_{0}|+\sum_{j=1}^{q}|n_{j}|
such that
n_{j}\neq 0
for all
j
. By Lemma
4.1
,
\sum_{j=0}^{q}|n_{j}|\leq|s_{i}^{k}|_{G_{m}}
. By Lemma
4.2
,

(2)

k=\varepsilon_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{q^{\prime}}2^{m_{j}^{\prime}}\varepsilon_{j},

where
\varepsilon_{j}\in\{-1,1\}
for all
j
and
q^{\prime}\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{q}|n_{j}|
. By Lemma 2.8(2) of
[
MUW12
]
, every sum equal to
k
of the form of equation (

) has at least

p\leq q^{\prime}+1\leq 2\sum_{j=0}^{q}|n_{j}|+1\leq 2|s_{i}^{k}|_{G_{m}}+1,

whence our claim follows.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For
i=0,\ldots,m
,
\left|s_{i}^{E(m-i,n)}\right|_{G_{m}}\leq 2^{m-i}\cdot(n+m-i)
.

Proof.

We proceed by downwards induction on
i
. For
i=m
, the claim holds because
\left|s_{m}^{n}\right|_{G_{m}}\leq n
. For the inductive step, observe

s_{i}^{E(m-i,n)}=s_{i+1}^{E(m-i-1,n)}s_{i}s_{i+1}^{-E(m-i-1,n)}.

Thus,

\left|s_{i}^{E(m-i,n)}\right|_{G_{m}}\leq 2\left|s_{i+1}^{E(m-i-1,n)}\right|_{G_{m}}+1\leq 2^{m-i}(n+m-i)

as desired.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume
m\geq 2
and et
\alpha\leq 0
and
\beta=-2^{-\alpha}\cdot(E(m,n)-1)/3
. Then
|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}\geq(E(m-1,n)-1)/8
.

Proof.

Since
m\geq 2
by assumption,
E(m,n)-1
is both odd and a multiple of three, hence the only integer solutions to this equation have
\alpha\leq 0,\beta=-2^{-\alpha}\cdot(E(m,n)-1)/3
. Observe

3\left(\sum_{j=0}^{E(m-1,n)/2}2^{-\alpha+2j}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{E(m-1,n)/2}2^{-\alpha+2j}+\sum_{j=0}^{E(m-1,n)/2}2^{-\alpha+2j+1}=\sum_{j^{\prime}=0}^{E(m-1,n)}2^{-\alpha+j^{\prime}}

=2^{-\alpha}(2^{E(m-1,n)}-1)=2^{-\alpha}(E(m,n)-1),

so

-\beta=\sum_{j=0}^{E(m-1,n)/2}2^{-\alpha+2j}.

By Corollary
4.3
,

(3)

|s_{0}^{-\beta}|_{G_{4}}\geq(E(m-1,n)-1)/4.

Since
|s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}=|s_{0}^{-\beta}|_{G_{m}}
, combining (
3
) with Corollary
2.14
and the inequality
|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}|\geq|s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}-|s_{1}^{\alpha}|_{G_{m}}
gives

|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}|\geq\max\left\{|s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}-|s_{1}^{\alpha}|_{G_{m}},|s_{1}^{\alpha}|_{G_{m}}\right\}\geq|s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G_{m}}/2\geq(E(m-1,n)-1)/8,

whence our claim follows.
∎

Now we turn to proving the lower bound in Theorem
1.3
. The case
m=1
is trivial, since
\hbox{\rm CL}_{H}(n)\succeq n
for all finitely presented groups
H
. The remainder of the theorem is handled in the following proposition:

Proposition 4.6
.

For
m\geq 2
, we have
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G_{m}}(n)\succeq E(m-1,n)
.

Proof.

Let
u_{n}=s_{1}^{2}
and
v_{n}=s_{0}^{E(m,n)-1}s_{1}^{2}
, and let
\Delta
be a reduced annular diagram witnessing the conjugacy of
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
. Let

be the largest natural number such that there is an

-edge in

,

. If

, then there is a non-contractibke

-ring in

, since there are no radial
s_{2}
-corridors or

-arches, and any contractible
s_{2}
-ring would have a non-trivial power of

on its outer boundary by Lemma

, contradicting the fact that

has infinite order. Let

be the word along the side closest to

of the
s_{2}
-ring
Q
closest to

. This is a power
s_{1}^{e}
of

conjugate to

. Moreover,

in

A minimal length conjugator

5.
Upper Bound For The Baumslag-Gersten Group

The following lemma is due to Platonov
[
Pla04
]
; we refer the reader to
[
Ril17
]
for a recent discussion.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{1}>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|n|\leq C_{1}\cdot E_{1}(\lfloor\log_{2}C_{1}|s_{0}^{n}|_{G}+C_{1}\rfloor,1)+C_{1}.

Since
\left||s_{1}^{n}|_{G}-|s_{0}^{n}|_{G}\right|=\left||s_{1}^{n}|_{G}-|t^{-1}s_{1}^{n}t|_{G}\right|\leq 2
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 5.2
.

There exists a constant
C_{2}>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

|n|\leq C_{2}\cdot E(\lfloor\log_{2}C_{2}|s_{1}^{n}|_{G}+C_{2}\rfloor,1)+C_{2}.

For ease of notation let
C=\max\{1,C_{1},C_{2}\}
, and observe that

C_{i}\cdot E(\lfloor\log_{2}C_{i}N+C_{i}\rfloor,1)+C_{i}\leq C\cdot E(\lfloor\log_{2}CN+C\rfloor,1)+C

for
i=1,2
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Given a word
w
on
\{s_{0},s_{1},t\}
, there exists a word
w^{\prime\prime}
equal in
G
to a cyclic conjugate of
w
such that

(a)

no cyclic conjugate of
w^{\prime\prime}
has any
t
-pinches, and

(b)

|w^{\prime\prime}|\leq C|w|(E(\lfloor\log_{2}C|w|+C\rfloor,1)+1)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a set of
t
-pinches of cyclic conjugates of
w
such that

(i)

if
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}
, then either
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are disjoint or one is involved in the other, and

(ii)

for every pair
t_{1},t_{2}
of
t
-partners, at least one of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
is involved in or an endpoint of an element of
\mathcal{P}
.

To see that such a set exists, consider a set
\mathcal{Q}
satisfying
(i)
. Let
P=t_{1}vt_{2}
be a pinch, with
t_{1}=t^{\pm 1},t_{2}=t^{\mp 1}
, such that neither
t_{1}
nor
t_{2}
is involved in or an endpoint of an element of
\mathcal{Q}
. Then, every element of
\mathcal{Q}
is either involved in or disjoint from
P
, meaning that
\mathcal{Q}\cup\{P\}
also satisfies
(i)
.

Now, let
P
be a maximal element of
\mathcal{P}
, and let
w^{\prime}
be the cyclic conjugate of
w
such that
P
is a
t
-pinch of
w^{\prime}
and its leftmost endpoint is the first letter of
v
. By
(i)
, every element of
P
is a
t
-pinch of
w^{\prime}
. Let
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}\}
be the set of maximal elements of
\mathcal{P}
, indexed so that
P_{i}
is to the left of
P_{i+1}
in
w^{\prime}
. We may write
w^{\prime}=v_{0}P_{1}v_{1}P_{2}v_{2}\cdots P_{k}v_{k}
. Note that

(4)

|v_{0}|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}(|P_{i}|+|v_{i}|)=|w^{\prime}|=|w|.

By the definition of
t
-pinch,
P_{i}=s_{\delta_{i}}^{n_{i}}
in
G
for some
\delta_{i}\in\{0,1\},n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
. By Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
5.2
,

(5)

|n_{i}|\leq C\cdot E(\lfloor\log_{2}C|P_{i}|+C\rfloor,1)+C\leq C\cdot E(\lfloor\log_{2}C|w|+C\rfloor,1)+C.

Replacing each
P_{i}
in
w^{\prime}
with
s_{\delta_{i}}^{n_{i}}
gives the word
w^{\prime\prime}=v_{0}s_{\delta_{1}}^{n_{1}}v_{1}s_{\delta_{2}}^{n_{2}}v_{2}\cdots s_{\delta_{k}}^{n_{k}}v_{k}
. To see item
(a)
, property
(ii)
of
\mathcal{P}
implies that if
t_{1}
is a
t
-letter of
v_{j}
, then every
t
-partner of
t_{1}
in
w^{\prime}
is an endpoint of or involved in some
P_{i}
, and thus
t_{1}
has no
t
-partners in
w^{\prime\prime}
. For item
(b)
, combining equations (
4
) and (
5
) with the fact that
k\leq|w|
gives

|w^{\prime\prime}|\leq|v_{0}|+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left[|v_{i}|+CE(\lfloor\log_{2}C|w|+C\rfloor,1)+C\right]\leq C|w|(E(\lfloor\log_{2}C|w|+C\rfloor,1)+1).

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}
be cyclically Britton-reduced words representing conjugate elements of
G\smallsetminus\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle_{G}
. The length of the conjugator found by applying algorithm in the proof of Theorem 3 of
[
DMW16
]
to
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
is bounded above by
|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|
.

Proof.

Up to cyclic conjugation and taking inverses, the authors of
[
DMW16
]
write
w_{1}=t^{-1}\gamma_{1}t^{\epsilon_{1}}\ldots t^{\epsilon_{n}}\gamma_{n}
and
w_{2}=w_{1}=t^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}t^{\epsilon_{1}}\ldots t^{\epsilon_{n}}\gamma_{n}^{\prime}
for
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i}^{\prime}\in\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle_{G}
, and show that
w_{1},w_{2}
are conjugate via
s_{0}^{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Our aim is to bound
k
in terms of
|w_{1}|
and
|w_{2}|
.

Using the notation
s_{0}=a,s_{1}=t
, and
G_{1}=\textbf{BS}(1,2)
, they further write,

We have
ta=_{\textbf{BS}(1,2)}a^{2}t
and
at^{-1}=_{\textbf{BS}(1,2)}t^{-1}a^{2}
. This allows [us] to represent all group elements by words of the form
t^{-p}a^{r}t^{q}
with
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. … We denote by
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]=\{p/2^{q}\in\mathbb{Q}\mid p,q\in\mathbb{Z}\}
the ring of dyadic fractions. Multiplication by 2 is an automorphism of the underlying additive group and therefore we can define the semi-direct product
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
as follows. Elements are pairs
(r,m)\in\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\texttimes\mathbb{Z}.
The multiplication in
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
is defined by

(r,m)\cdot(s,q)=(r+2^{m}s,m+q).

… It is straightforward to show that
a\mapsto(1,0)
and
t\mapsto(0,1)
defines a canonical isomorphism between
\textbf{BS}(1,2)
and
\mathbb{Z}[1/2]\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
…
[
DMW16
, 269-270]

Returning to our notation, let
\varphi:s_{0}\mapsto(1,0),s_{1}\mapsto(0,1)
be the automorphism they define, and let
x\in\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle_{G}=G_{1}
,
\varphi(x)=(a,b)
. The standard normal form of
G_{1}
, referred to at the beginning of the quote, gives
x=s_{1}^{-b_{1}}s_{0}^{a}s_{1}^{b_{2}}
, where
|b_{1}|+|b_{2}|\leq|x|_{G_{1}}
. The second coordinate of
\varphi(s_{1}^{-b_{1}}s_{0}^{a}s_{1}^{b_{2}})
is
-b_{1}+b_{2}
, which has magnitude
|b|=|-b_{1}+b_{2}|\leq|x|_{G_{1}}
.

The authors denote
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{1}^{\prime}
by
(r,m)=\varphi(\gamma_{1}),(s,q)=\varphi(\gamma_{2})
respectively, and so we have
|m|+|q|\leq|\gamma_{1}|_{G_{1}}+|\gamma_{2}|_{G_{1}}\leq|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|
. A computation performed in
[
DMW16
]
shows that
k\in\{0,-m,q-m\}
, so we are done.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

We have
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)\preceq nE(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)
.

Proof.

Let
n\geq C
, and let
u,v
be conjugate elements of
G
with
|u|_{G}+|v|_{G}\leq n
. By Lemma
5.3
, we may assume
u
and
v
are represented by cyclically Britton-reduced words
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
respectively such that

|w_{i}|\leq Cn(E(\lfloor\log_{2}Cn+C\rfloor,1)+1)+n\leq 2CnE(\lfloor\log_{2}Cn+C\rfloor,1).

Let
\Delta
be a reduced annular diagram witnessing the conjugacy of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
G
, and observe that every
t
-letter of
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
must be part of a radial
t
-corridor in
\Delta
.

Since
\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle_{G}
is normal in
G
,
w_{1},w_{2}\not\in\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle_{G}
if and only if
\Delta
contains a radial
t
-corridor. If this is the case, the desired bound follows immediately from Lemma
5.4
.

Suppose instead that
\Delta
has no radial
t
-corridors. We claim
\Delta
contains at most two non-contractible
t
-rings, and if there are two, the negative sides face away from each other. Assume towards contradiction that there two non-contractible
t
-rings with their negative sides facing each other. Since all the
s_{1}
-corridors existing between these two
t
-corridors must run from one of them to the other, these
t
-corridors have identical words on their negative sides. The words along their positive sides are thus the same, so both may be excised by Proposition
2.5
. Lastly, there cannot be two consecutive non-contractible
t
-rings with the same orientation, since the
s_{1}
-corridors leaving the negative side of one cannot run to the positive side of the other. Among any set of three consecutive non-contractible
t
-rings, either two consecuctive ones have the same orientation, or two of them have their negative sides facing each other, whence our claim follows. This gives us two cases:

(1)

Suppose
\Delta
has no non-contractible
t
-rings. Then
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are elements of
\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle=\mathrm{BS}(1,2)
which are conjugate in the same group. The desired bound then follows from the fact that
\hbox{\rm CL}_{\mathrm{BS}(1,2)}(n)
is linear
[
Sal16
]
.

(2)

Suppose
\Delta
has one or two non-contractible
t
-rings, and, if there are two, the negative sides are facing away from each other. Every
s_{1}
-corridor has its negative side facing a boundary component of
\Delta
, and hence has length at most
\max\{|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|\}
. Moreover, this word is conjugate itself to either
w_{1}
or
w_{2}
in
\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle
. If
\Delta
has one non-contractible
t
-ring, the word along the positive side has length at most
\max\{|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|\}
and is conjugate in
\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle
to one of the words on the boundary of
\Delta
. If
\Delta
has two non-contractible
t
-rings with their negative sides facing away from each other, the words along their positive sides are of length at most
\max\{|w_{1}|,|w_{2}|\}
and conjugate in
\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle
to each other. Our claim again follows from the linearity of
\hbox{\rm CL}_{\mathrm{BS}(1,2)}(n)
.

∎

Corollary 5.6
.

We have
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)\preceq E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)
.

Proof.

First, we claim
E(M,1)^{i}\leq E(M,i)
for
M\geq 1
. We proceed by induction on
M
, the base case holding because
(2^{1})^{i}=2^{i}
. For the inductive step, we have

E(M,1)^{i}=2^{iE(M-1,1)}\leq 2^{E(M-1,i)}=E(M,i).

Now, by Proposition
5.5
,

\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)\preceq nE(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)\leq E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1)^{2}\leq E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,2).

By the definition of
E
, this is bounded above by

E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor+1,1)\leq E(\lfloor\log_{2}2n\rfloor,1)\preceq E(\lfloor\log_{2}n\rfloor,1),

and we are done.

∎

.

. While we do not explore this here, we hypothesize that an analysis of their algorithm may give an alternative proof of Corollary
5.6
.

6.
Lower Bound for The Baumslag-Gersten group

Lemma 6.1
.

If
|s_{0}^{k}|_{G}\leq n
then
k=n_{0}+\sum_{j=0}^{p}2^{m_{j}}n_{j}
for some
n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{p},m_{1},\ldots,m_{p}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\sum_{j=0}^{p}|n_{j}|\leq n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.20
,
|s_{0}^{k}|_{G_{2M+2}}\leq|s_{0}^{k}|_{G}
, where
M=|s_{0}^{k}|_{G}
. The claim follows from Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
k=\sum_{j=0}^{p}2^{2j+\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
|s_{0}^{k}|_{G}\geq(p-1)/2
.

Proof.

This holds by the same proof as Lemma
4.3
, replacing the sole application of Lemma
4.1
with Lemma
6.1
.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

For any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\left|s_{0}^{E(m,n)}\right|_{G}\leq 2^{m}\cdot m(n+m)

Proof.

By Remark
2.19
and Lemma
4.4
,

\left|s_{0}^{E(m,n)}\right|_{G}\leq m\left|s_{0}^{E(m,n)}\right|_{G_{m}}\leq 2^{m}\cdot m(n+m).

∎

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\beta=2^{-\alpha}(E(m,n)-1)/3
for some
\alpha\leq 0
. Then
|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G}\geq(E(m-1,n)-1)/4
.

Proof.

Let
M=|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{G}
. By Lemma
2.20
,
M\geq|s_{1}^{\alpha}s_{0}^{\beta}|_{H_{M+1}}=|s_{M+2}^{\alpha}s_{M+1}^{\beta}|_{G_{2M+2}}
. The same argument as Lemma
4.5
suffices to finish the proof, changing the indices of the
s
-letters as needed.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

We have
\hbox{\rm CL}_{G}(n)\succeq E\left(\left\lfloor\frac{1}{3}\log_{2}(n)\right\rfloor-1,1\right)
.

Proof.

Let
n
be given, and let
\epsilon=E(\lfloor\log_{2}(n^{1/3})\rfloor,1)
. By Corollary
6.3
,
\left|s_{0}^{\epsilon}\right|_{G}\leq Cn
for some constant
C>0
. Let
u_{n}=s_{1}^{2}
and
v_{n}=s_{1}^{2}s_{0}^{\epsilon-1}
. Then
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
are conjugate via
s_{0}^{(\epsilon-1)/3}
. To compute
c(u_{n},v_{n})
, let
\Delta
be a reduced annular diagram witnessing the conjugacy of
u_{n}
and
v_{n}
. Consider the non-contractible
t
-ring
Q
in
\Delta
closest to
v_{n}
(if any exist), and let
w
be the word along the side closest to
v_{n}
. Then
w
is either a power of
s_{0}
or a power of
s_{1}
. Since
w
and
v_{n}
are conjugate in
\langle s_{0},s_{1}\rangle_{G}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{BS}(1,2)
, this implies
w=s_{1}^{2}-u_{n}
. Restricting attention to the subdiagram bounded by
Q
and
u_{n}
, our claim thus follows by the same argument as Proposition
4.6
, using Corollary
6.4
in place of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

Combined with Corollary
5.6
, this completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

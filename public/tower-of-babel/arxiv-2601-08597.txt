Stratifying moduli spaces of Higgs bundles and the Hitchin morphism
Aryaman Patel, Dario Weißmann

Abstract
. We study the behavior of slope-stability of reflexive twisted sheaves over a normal projective variety X X under pullback along a cover. Slope-stability is always preserved if the cover does not factor via a quasi-étale cover. Fixing the rank, there is one quasi-étale cover that checks whether a twisted sheaf remains slope-stable on all Galois covers, yielding a stratification of the moduli space of slope-stable Higgs-bundles. As an application, we determine the image of the Hitchin morphism restricted to the smallest closed stratum of the Dolbeault moduli space when X X is smooth. This allows us to determine the image of the Hitchin morphism from the Dolbeault moduli space when X X is a hyperelliptic or abelian variety in characteristic p ≥ 0 p\geq 0 . In particular, we show that Chen-Ngô’s conjecture holds for hyperelliptic varieties in characteristic 0 0 .



1.
Introduction

Stability of vector bundles is a fundamental property
used in the construction of their moduli spaces.
In recent years, the question of when the pullback
of a slope-stable vector bundle remains slope-stable has been raised.
For normal projective varieties, this was answered in
[
1
]
:
a finite separable morphism
Y\to X
preserves the
slope-stability of vector bundles if
Y\to X
is genuinely ramified,
that is, it does not factor via a non-trivial étale cover of
X
.
In general, pullback along a finite étale cover does not preserve stability.
However, for a fixed rank, the property to remain stable on
all

étale covers is an open property in characteristic
0
, see
[
24
]
.
In positive characteristic
p
,

one has to exclude the case that
p
divides the degree
of étale Galois covers in the discussion.

In this paper, we answer an analogous question for stable reflexive sheaves
\mathcal{V}

under reflexive pullback along a finite separable morphism
Y\to X

of normal projective varieties.
We also add a twisted endomorphism
\theta:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes F
,
where
F
is a vector bundle, to the discussion.
The results follow a similar structure as in the vector bundle case,
but this time quasi-étale covers play the role of étale covers,
that is, finite separable covers that are étale over an open subvariety whose complement has codimension at least
2
.
In the case that
X
is smooth and
\theta=0
,
pullbacks of stable reflexive sheaves were also independently considered in

[
14
, Section 3.1]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
3.2
)
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a
finite separable
morphism
of normal projective varieties that does not factor through a non-trivial
finite étale morphism, and let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.

Then an
F
-twisted vector bundle
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
on
X
is slope-stable
iff
(\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{V},\pi^{\ast}\theta)
is slope-stable
as a
\pi^{\ast}F
-twisted bundle on
Y
.

Furthermore, if
\pi
does not factor through a non-trivial finite
quasi-étale morphism,
then an
F
-twisted reflexive sheaf
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
on
X

is slope-stable if and only if
(\pi^{[\ast]}\mathcal{V},\pi^{[\ast]}\theta)

is slope-stable as a
\pi^{\ast}F
-twisted reflexive sheaf on
Y
,
where
(-)^{[*]}
denotes reflexive pullback.

Thus, to understand slope-stability under reflexive pullback by a general finite
separable morphism,
it remains to study quasi-étale covers.
Fixing the rank of the reflexive sheaves, we obtain
that it suffices to check slope-stability on one quasi-étale
cover instead of all quasi-étale covers:

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.4
, char
0
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety, let
r\geq 2
be an integer and let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.

Then there exists a quasi-étale Galois cover

\pi_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{refl-good}}\nolimits}:X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{refl-good}}\nolimits}\to X
called the
good cover
, such that any slope-stable
F
-twisted
reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{V}
of rank
r
on
X
remains slope-stable after pullback to all covers
Y\to X

if and only if

\pi_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{refl-good}}\nolimits}^{[\ast]}\mathcal{V}
is slope-stable as an

\pi_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{refl-good}}\nolimits}^{\ast}F
-twisted reflexive sheaf.

Given an étale Galois cover
\pi:Y\to X
and a vector bundle
F
on
X
,
any stable
F
-twisted bundle
\mathcal{V}
of rank
\geq 2
on
X

decomposes as a direct sum of stable
F_{\mid_{Y}}
-twisted bundles on
Y

such that the Galois group of
\pi
acts transitively on the isomorphism classes
of the stable direct summands.
By transitivity, all
stable direct summands have the same rank,
called the
decomposition type
of
\mathcal{V}
with respect to
\pi

(Definition
5.7
).

Iterating the good cover, we obtain a cover
\pi_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{split}}\nolimits}:X_{r,\mathrm{split}}\to X

called the
split cover
, such that the decomposition type remains unchanged
with respect to all covers dominating the split cover.
In particular, we obtain a stratification of the moduli spaces
M^{P,F-s}_{X}

of slope-stable
F
-twisted Higgs bundles with fixed Hilbert polynomial
P
,
with respect to the decomposition type.
This stratification is independent of the choice of cover
for all covers that dominate the split cover
X_{r,\mathrm{split}}\to X
.

We denote by
M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}
the moduli space of Gieseker semistable
torsion-free Higgs sheaves on
X
with Hilbert polynomial
P

and consider the
Hitchin morphism
h^{r}:M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\to A^{r,F}_{X}
which assigns to

F
-twisted Higgs sheaf
(\mathcal{V},\theta)

the characteristic polynomial of
\theta
,
which is a point in the affine space

A^{r,F}_{X}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F)
,
called the
Hitchin base
.

Given an étale cover
Y\to X
, we denote by
(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}
the subset of the Hitchin base consisting of polynomials that decompose completely, i.e., as a product of
r
degree
1
polynomials, after pullback to
Y
.
Let
Z^{\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits,P}(1)
denote the subset of
F
-twisted Higgs bundles in

M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}
that split as a direct sum of
\pi^{*}_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{split}}\nolimits}F
-Higgs bundles of rank
1
after pullback to the split cover
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{split}}\nolimits}
, which we call the
semistable
1
-stratum
of
M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}
. Let
P_{0}
denote the Hilbert polynomial of the trivial bundle of rank
r
. Then these strata are related as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(Lemma
5.19
+ Theorem
5.21
, char
0
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and let

be a vector bundle on

.

Let

be a smooth projective variety.
We denote by

the

of semistable Higgs bundles on

As a consequence of Theorem

,

In characteristic

[
5
]
.
The image of the Hitchin morphism for abelian varieties in char

was previously determined in
[

.

Theorem 1.4

(Corollary
5.23
)
.

Let

be a smooth projective variety that admits an étale Galois cover

, where

is an abelian variety.
Then the set-theoretic image of
h^{r}:M^{r}_{X,\text{Dol}}\to A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{X}}_{X}
coincides with the subset
(H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r})^{G}

of

, where

Method of proofs

To study stability of reflexive twisted sheaves under pullback
and prove
Theorem

1.1
,
we first treat the case
where the cover
\pi:Y\to X
is finite Galois.
Using the symmetries obtained from the Galois action,
we show that
Y\times_{X}Y
is connected in codimension
1
,
that is, for each pair of irreducible components, there is a path
via irreducible components of
Y\times_{X}Y
that intersect
in codimension
1
,
if and only if
Y\to X
does not factor via a non-trivial quasi-étale cover.
This is also equivalent to
the induced morphism

\pi:\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(Y_{sm})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X_{sm})

of étale fundamental groups of the smooth loci to be surjective.
We call such morphisms
genuinely ramified in codimension
1
.

This is an analogue of the equivalence obtained in
[
1
]
in the Galois case.
We note that our method of proof also recovers the characterization
of genuinely ramified covers in loc. cit. in the Galois case,
but is much shorter than the original.

We use this characterization of morphisms genuinely ramified in codimension
1

to show that they preserve slope-stability of reflexive sheaves
via methods similar – but somewhat more technical – than in loc. cit.
to obtain
Theorem

1.1
.

In the proof
Theorem

1.2
, the Galois action on the stable direct
summands of the reflexive pullback of a stable reflexive twisted sheaf plays
a crucial role. Together with descent results for twisted reflexive sheaves
and Jordan’s theorem, we are able to generalize the proof

[
24
, Theorem 3.11]
to our setting.

To prove the first part of Theorem
1.3
,
we define
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
to be an étale Galois cover
that dominates every étale Galois cover of degree at most
r
.
Then it is easy to observe that the inclusion

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}\subset(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}

holds for all étale Galois covers
Y\to X
dominating
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
.
To prove the reverse inclusion, we show that for any
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}
,
the decomposition of
s_{\mid Y}
as a product of degree
1
factors descends
to a decomposition of
s_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}
as a product of degree
1
factors.

For the second part, we show that for any
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}

there is an
F
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
such that
\mathcal{V}
is
an étale trivializable vector bundle of rank
r
and the characteristic
polynomial of
\theta
is
s
.
It is straightforward to see that the
characteristic polynomial of any Higgs bundle in
Z^{\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits,P_{0}}(1)
lies in

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
.

To prove Theorem
1.4
we first show that when
F
is an étale
trivializable vector bundle, the set-theoretic image of the Hitchin morphism

h^{r}:M^{P_{0},\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\to A^{r,F}_{X}
coincides with

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
.
Then the theorem essentially follows from the fact
that
\Omega^{1}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}^{\oplus\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits X}
for abelian varieties.

2.
Reflexive twisted sheaves

In this section, we introduce our main objects of interest:
(semi)stable reflexive sheaves sheaves together with a twisted endomorphism,
where (semi)stable stands for slope-(semi)stable.
All definitions and lemmas have a direct analogue in the untwisted setting
and the reader familiar with this may skip ahead to

Lemma

2.12
, where we show that
the direct image of a semistable reflexive sheaf under a quasi-étale cover
is semistable, or
Lemma

2.13
,
where we observe some symmetries under the stable
direct summands of the pullback of a stable reflexive sheaf under a Galois cover.

We begin by recalling some standard properties of reflexive sheaves.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
, Proposition 1.8]
)
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a flat morphism between varieties, and let
\mathcal{V}
be a reflexive sheaf on
X
. Then
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{V}

is a reflexive sheaf on
Y
, i.e.,
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
8
, Corollary 1.7]
)
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a proper dominant morphism between normal varieties with
all fibers of the same dimension, and let
\mathcal{V}
be a reflexive sheaf on
Y
.
Then
\pi_{*}\mathcal{V}
is a reflexive sheaf on
X
.

(Category of reflexive

Let

.

).

A morphism of coherent sheaves

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{F\text{-}TS}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})

-twisted sheaves by

\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)
.
The full subcategory
of
F
-twisted sheaves where the underlying sheaf is

\mathop{\mathrm{Refl}}\nolimits_{F-TS}(X)
.

Let

be an

A coherent subsheaf

is called an

if the restricted morphism

.

For

-
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits(X)_{d,d-1}

,

, and

Remark 2.4

Let

.
Let

Then

Adjunction of push and pull

Pushforward and reflexive pullback
define functors on the category of twisted sheaves, satisfying the
expected adjunction.

Definition 2.5

(Cover)
.

A finite separable morphism between normal varieties is called a
cover
. A cover is called
Galois

if the induced field extension of function fields is Galois.

Henceforth, we replace the term ’finite separable morphism’ with ’cover’.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a cover of normal projective varieties,
and let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
We obtain an adjoint pair of functors

\mathop{\mathrm{Refl}}\nolimits_{F_{\mid Y}-TS}(Y)\underset{\pi_{\ast}}{\overset{\pi^{[\ast]}}{\leftrightarrows}}\mathop{\mathrm{Refl}}\nolimits_{F-TS}(X).

Proof.

Recall that Hartogs lemma induces an equivalence
of categories between reflexive sheaves on a normal variety
and reflexive sheaves on a big open subvariety via push-pull.

The direct image
(\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{V},\pi_{\ast}\theta)
of
an
F_{\mid Y}
-twisted reflexive sheaf
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
on
Y

becomes an
F
-twisted reflexive sheaf on
X

via applying the canonical isomorphism
obtained from the projection formula

\pi_{\ast}(\mathcal{V}\otimes F_{\mid Y})\cong\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{V}\otimes F

to the target of
\pi_{\ast}(\theta)
.

Then we recover the above adjunction
via restricting to the locus where both source and target are vector bundles.
Note that this locus is a big open subvariety by normality.
∎

Stability for twisted sheaves.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety of dimension
d\geq 1
.
Let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Recall that the
slope

\mu(-)

of a coherent sheaf torsion-free in codimension
1
is defined as
\mathop{\mathrm{deg}}\nolimits(-)/\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits(-)
.
This endows the category
F
-
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits_{d,d-1}(X)
with a stability structure
in the sense of
[
18
]
.
This allows us to define (semi)stability
of an
F
-twisted sheaf in
F
-
\mathop{\mathrm{Coh}}\nolimits_{d,d-1}(X)
.
We also obtain the Harder-Narasimhan filtration (HN-filtration),
the destabilizing subobject,
Jordan-Hölder filtrations (JH-filtrations), and the socle.
Taking reflexive hulls of these constructions,
we also obtain all these notions for reflexive
F
-twisted sheaves on
X
.
For reflexive sheaves we spell out these notions in the following:

An
F
-twisted sheaf
\mathcal{V}
is called

semi-stable
if
\mathcal{V}
is torsion-free in codimension
1
and
for all non-trivial invariant subsheaves
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{V}

we have
\mu(\mathcal{W})\leq\mu(\mathcal{V})
,
It is called
stable
if the inequality is strict for non-trivial
invariant subsheaves of smaller rank.
An
F
-twisted sheaf
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
is called
polystable

if
\mathcal{V}
is isomorphic
(as an
F
-twisted sheaf)
to a direct sum of stable
F
-twisted sheaves of the same slope.

An
F
-twisted subsheaf
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}

of a torsion-free
F
-twisted
sheaf
\mathcal{V}
is called
saturated

if the quotient
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{W}
is torsion-free.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a reflexive
F
-twisted sheaf.
The
HN-filtration
of
\mathcal{V}
is the unique filtration
by saturated
F
-twisted subsheaves

0\subset\mathcal{V}_{1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{V}_{m}=\mathcal{V}

such that each quotient
\mathcal{V}^{i}:=\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i-1}

is semistable and

\mu(\mathcal{V}^{1})>\mu(\mathcal{V}^{2})>\dots>\mu(\mathcal{V}^{m}).

The subsheaf
\mathcal{V}_{1}
is called the
maximal destabilizing subsheaf

of
\mathcal{V}
, and, as the name suggests,
is the subsheaf whose slope is maximal among all subsheaves of
\mathcal{V}

(see
[
18
, Proposition 1.9]
).

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a semistable reflexive
F
-twisted sheaf.
A
JH-filtration
of
\mathcal{V}

is a filtration of
\mathcal{V}
by saturated
F
-twisted subsheaves

0\subset\mathcal{V}_{1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{V}_{m}=\mathcal{V}

such that the successive quotients
\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i-1}

are stable of slope
\mu(\mathcal{V})
.
In contrast to the HN-filtration there may be several JH-filtrations.
However, the reflexive hull of the associated graded

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i-1}
is unique.
The unique maximal polystable saturated subsheaf of a semistable sheaf

\mathcal{V}
of slope
\mu(\mathcal{V})

is called the
socle
of
\mathcal{V}
.

The standard argument in
[
9
, Cor 1.2.8]
carries over to show
that stable reflexive twisted shaves are simple:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety
and
F
a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}
be stable
reflexive
F
-twisted sheaves such that
\mu(\mathcal{V})=\mu(\mathcal{W})
.
Then any non-zero morphism
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{W}
of
F
-twisted sheaves
is an isomorphism and in this case we have

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{F-TS}(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{W})\cong k.

Proof.

As a direct consequence of the
definition of stability a non-zero morphism of stable

F
-twisted shaves of the same slope is injective and has cokernel
supported in codimension
2
or higher. If both sheaves are in addition
reflexive sheaves on a normal projective variety,
we find that the morphism is an isomorphism.

Note that
F
-twisted homomorphisms form a
k
-subvector space
of the
k
-vector space of homomorphisms.
As
X
is projective (proper suffices), we find that

F
-twisted homomorphisms
of
F
-twisted sheaves are a finite dimensional vector space.
Endomorphisms also form a (possibly non-commutative)
k
-algebra.
For a stable reflexive
F
-twisted sheaf
\mathcal{V}
every non-zero morphism is invertible, thus
\mathrm{End}_{F-TS}(\mathcal{V})
forms a finite dimensional skew field over
k
. As
k
is algebraically closed, we conclude
\mathrm{End}_{F-TS}(\mathcal{V})=k
.
∎

Recall that we denote by

We recall the behavior of the slope under (reflexive) pushforward and pullback:

Lemma 2.8

(
[
9

)
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X

Consider a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{V}
on
Y
and a reflexive sheaf
\mathcal{W}
on

. Then we have the following:

(1)

\mu(\mathcal{V})=d(\mu(\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{V})-\mu(\pi_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}))

3.
Genuinely ramified morphisms and stability

The goal of this section is to prove several generalizations of

[
1
, Theorem 1.2]
:
pullback by a genuinely ramified cover
Y\to X

preserves stability of twisted bundles and
reflexive pullback by a genuinely ramified cover with
smooth target preserves stability of reflexive sheaves.
We also study stability under reflexive pullback
in the normal setting and obtain a similar criterion
for when such a pullback preserves stability of
reflexive sheaves.

Recall that a cover
Y\to X
of normal projective varieties
is genuinely ramified if and only if one of the following
is satisfied, see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
:

•

the cover
Y\to X
does not factor via a non-trivial étale
cover
X^{\prime}\to X
.

•

the induced morphism

\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(Y)\to\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X)
is surjective.

•

the fibre product
Y\times_{X}Y
is connected.

Our first main result is an analogous characterization
of covers that do not factor via a non-trivial quasi-étale cover.
We call such covers
genuinely ramified in codimension 1

(
Definition

3.8
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a Galois cover of normal varieties.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The cover
Y\to X
is genuinely ramified in codimension
1
.

(2)

The self-intersection
Y\times_{X}Y
is connected in codimension
1
,
see
Definition

3.11
.

(3)

The induced morphism
\pi_{\ast}:\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(Y_{sm})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X_{sm})
is surjective, where
(-)_{sm}
denotes the smooth locus,
see
Lemma

3.6

for the construction of the morphism.

Our techniques rely on the symmetries
obtained from the action of the Galois group of
Y/X
on
Y\times_{X}Y

and thus differ from the approach in
[
3
]
.

Using this characterization, we obtain that covers genuinely ramified
in codimension
1
preserve stability of reflexive twisted sheaves under pullback:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a genuinely ramified morphism of normal projective varieties.
Let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
. Then an
F
-twisted bundle
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
on
X
is stable
iff
(\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y},\theta_{\mid Y})
is stable
as an
F_{\mid Y}
-twisted bundle.

Furthermore, if
Y\to X
is genuinely ramified in codimension
1
,
then an
F
-twisted reflexive sheaf
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
on
X

is stable if and only if
(\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y},\theta_{\mid Y})

is stable as an
F_{\mid Y}
-twisted reflexive sheaf.

3.1.
Étale fundamental group of the smooth locus

Let
X
be a normal variety. In this subsection,
we study the étale fundamental group of the smooth locus of
X
.
Although all the results should be well-known,
we could not find them in the literature and include the short arguments.

We provide an interpretation of the étale fundamental
group
\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X_{sm})
of the smooth locus
X_{sm}
of
X

via quasi-étale covers of

Given a cover

we obtain an induced morphism

\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(Y_{sm})\to\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X_{sm})
.
Furthermore, if

is also projective, then

\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X_{sm})
is topologically finitely generated.
In particular, there are only finitely many quasi-étale covers
of

of fixed degree if

is a normal projective variety.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a normal variety.
Then quasi-étale covers of

are in a 1:1 correspondence to
étale covers of the smooth locus
X_{sm}
of
X
.

Proof.

Given an étale cover
Y\to X_{sm}
we obtain
a quasi-étale cover

Y^{\prime}\to X
, where
Y^{\prime}
denotes the normal closure of
X

in the function field of
Y
.

Conversely, given a quasi-étale cover
Y^{\prime}\to X
,
we obtain a quasi-étale cover of
Y\to X_{sm}
by restriction.
By purity of branch locus,

[
22
,
Tag 0BMB
]
,
we find that
Y\to X_{sm}
is an étale cover.

It is straightforward to check that these constructions are inverse to
each other.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety.
Then the étale fundamental group of the smooth
locus
X_{sm}
of
X
is topologically finitely generated.

Proof.

We interpret étale covers of
X_{sm}
as quasi-étale covers of
X

via
Lemma

3.3
.

Note that the general complete intersection curve of large enough degree
in
X
also lies in the smooth locus
as the smooth locus is a big open in
X
.
Indeed, for
N\gg 0
, the general hyperplane
H
induced by a section of

intersects the locus

transversally.
Furthermore, the general such

is also a normal projective variety by
the normal version of Bertini’s theorem, see
[
19
, Theorem 7 and 7’]
.

Let
C\hookrightarrow X
be such a curve.
Let
Y\to X
be a quasi-étale cover.
Note that by
[
11
, Corollaire 6.11 (3)]
we have
that
C\times_{X}Y
is irreducible.
Thus, we obtain the surjectivity of

\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(C)\to\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(X_{sm})

by
[
22
,
Tag 0BN6
]
,
and conclude by the well-known fact that
\pi_{1}^{\mathop{\text{\'{e}t}}\nolimits}(C)
is topologically

∎

As a topologically finite generated group has only finitely many homomorphisms
to a finite group we obtain:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety.
Then
X
has only finitely many quasi-étale covers of fixed degree.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a cover of a normal variety

.
Let
y
be a closed point in the smooth locus of

such that

.
Then we obtain an induced morphism

Proof.

We interpret an étale cover of the smooth locus
with quasi-étale cover via

Let
X^{\prime}\to X
be a quasi-étale cover.
Taking the normalization of the fibre product

yields a disjoint union
of quasi-étale covers on

.
This assignment is functorial

and commutes with the fiber functors at

∎

Lemma 3.19

Let

Consider the cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y\times_{X}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\mathrm{pr}}\nolimits_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\mathrm{pr}}\nolimits_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

as

4.

While semistability is preserved under pullback along a cover,

5.
Decomposition strata

In this section, we define a stratification
of the moduli space of stable twisted Higgs bundles
via their behaviour under pullback
and study the image of the Hitchin morphism of the smallest stratum.
As an application, we show that the conjecture of Chen-Ngô
(
[
5
, Conjecture 5.2]
) holds for abelian and hyperelliptic varieties
(
5.23
).

5.1.
Moduli

Throughout this section
X
is a normal projective variety over an algebraically
closed field
k
, and
F
is a vector bundle on
X
.

We recall the definition of an
F
-twisted Higgs sheaf as well as
some preliminiaries on moduli of Higgs-sheaves.

Definition 5.1
.

An
F
-twisted Higgs sheaf
on
X
is a pair
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
, where
\mathcal{V}

is a coherent sheaf and
\theta:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes F
is an
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear morphism with the additional requirement that the composition

\theta\wedge\theta:\mathcal{V}\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal{V}\otimes F\xrightarrow{\theta\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits}\mathcal{V}\otimes F^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits\otimes\wedge}\mathcal{V}\otimes\wedge^{2}F

vanishes. The last arrow is the identity of
\mathcal{V}
tensored with the
natural surjection
F^{\otimes 2}\to\wedge^{2}F
.

We define
\theta^{i}:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}\otimes F^{\otimes i}

to be the composition

\mathcal{V}\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal{V}\otimes F\xrightarrow{\theta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{F}}\mathcal{V}\otimes F^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\theta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{F^{\otimes 2}}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\theta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{F^{\otimes{i-1}}}}\mathcal{V}\otimes F^{\otimes i}

for
i\geq 1
.

We call an
F
-Higgs sheaf
Gieseker-semistable
if
it is Gieseker-semistable as an
F
-twisted sheaf.
Similarly, we define Gieseker-stable, slope-stable, and slope-semistable.

When
X
is smooth, setting
F=\Omega^{1}_{X}
recovers the usual notion of a Higgs sheaf.
A locally free
F
-twisted Higgs sheaf is called a
F
-twisted Higgs bundle
.

For brevity, we often suppress the ’twisted’ and just write
F
-Higgs sheaf
or
F
-Higgs bundle. To obtain moduli of
F
-Higgs bundles we recall the notion
of Gieseker semistability.

Definition 5.2

(Gieseker stability)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a
F
-twisted sheaf on a normal projective variety
X
. We call
\mathcal{V}

Gieseker semistable
if for any
F
-twisted subsheaf
0\neq\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
, the inequality of reduced Hilbert polynomials
p_{\mathcal{W}}\leq p_{\mathcal{V}}
holds.

We call
\mathcal{V}

Gieseker stable
if for every proper
F
-twisted subsheaf
0\neq\mathcal{W}\subsetneq\mathcal{V}
the strict inequality
p_{\mathcal{W}}<p_{\mathcal{V}}
holds.

Definition 5.3

(JH-filtration)
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a Gieseker semistable
F
-twisted sheaf on
X
.
Then
\mathcal{V}
admits a filtration

0=\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset\mathcal{V}_{1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{V}_{m}=\mathcal{V}

by
F
-twisted subsheaves such that the associated graded
\bigoplus_{i=0}^{m}\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i-1}

is a Gieseker polystable
F
-twisted sheaf
(i.e. it is a direct sum of
F
-twisted sheaves
with the same reduced Hilbert polynomial).
This is called a
Jordan-Hölder
(JH for short) filtration of
\mathcal{V}
.

Definition 5.4

(S-equivalence)
.

Two
F
-twisted Gieseker semistable
sheaves
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{W}
on
X
are called
S-equivalent

if the associated graded objects
\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{V}_{i-1}

and
\bigoplus_{j}\mathcal{W}_{j}/\mathcal{W}_{j-1}

with respect to the JH-filtration are isomorphic as
F
-twisted sheaves.

We denote by
M_{X}^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}
the moduli space of torsion-free,
Gieseker-semistable,
F
-Higgs sheaves of
Hilbert polynomial
P
(see
[
12
]
for the construction).
Further, we denote by
M^{P,F-s}_{X}

the moduli space of slope-stable
F
-Higgs
bundles of Hilbert polynomial
P
on
X
.

Given any
F
-Higgs sheaf
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
, we can view
\theta\in H^{0}(X,F\otimes\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{V}))
as an
F
-valued endomorphism of

\mathcal{V}
and associate to it its characteristic polynomial. The
characteristic polynomial is a degree
r
polynomial in a formal variable whose
degree
i
coefficient is the trace
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\theta^{r-i}
of
\theta^{r-i}
. Here,

r
denotes the rank of Higgs sheaves with Hilbert polynomial
P
. Although

\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\theta^{i}\in H^{0}(X,F^{\otimes i})
a priori, the Higgs condition

\theta\wedge\theta=0
implies that we may view
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\theta^{i}
as an element of

H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F)
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

Definition 5.5
.

The
Hitchin morphism

h^{P,F}:M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F)

is given by sending
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
to the coefficients

(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\theta,\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\theta^{2},\dots,\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\theta^{r})
of its characteristic polynomial.
Henceforth, we suppress the Hilbert polynomial
P

and the bundle
F
and denote the Hitchin morphism by
h^{r}
.
The target of the Hitchin morphism is called the
Hitchin base

and we denote it by
A^{r,F}_{X}
.

By
[
12
, Theorem 3.8]
,
we know that the Hitchin morphism is proper in our setting.
As a consequence, we observe in the next lemma that
taking direct sums of Higgs sheaves induces a finite morphism between moduli
spaces. The proof uses a projective completion of the Hitchin base, which is
inspired by the projective compactification of Dolbeault moduli spaces
constructed in
[
7
]
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and

F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
P\in\mathbf{Q}[x]
be a polynomial with rational coefficients.
Denote by
r
the rank of torsion-free sheaves with Hilbert polynomial
P
.
Assume
r\geq 2
and
r=r_{1}+r_{2}
be a sum of non-negative integers
r_{1},r_{2}
.

Then the direct sum morphism

\oplus:M^{\frac{r_{1}}{r}P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\times M^{\frac{r_{2}}{r}P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\to M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X},\;\;

((\mathcal{V}_{1},\theta_{1}),(\mathcal{V}_{2},\theta_{2}))\mapsto(\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2},(\theta_{1}\otimes\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits)\oplus(\mathop{\mathrm{id}}\nolimits\otimes\theta_{2}))

is finite.

Proof.

We first show that
\oplus
is quasi-finite. As quasi-finiteness
is an open property, it suffices to show that over every closed point
the preimage under
\oplus
is finite.

Note that closed points of
M^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}

correspond to Gieseker-polystable
F
-twisted torsion-free Higgs sheaves
of rank
r
with reduced Hilbert polynomial
p
,
where
p
is the reduced Hilbert polynomial associated to
P
.

Now, given a Gieseker-polystable
F
-twisted torsion-free Higgs sheaf

\mathcal{V}
of rank
r_{1}+r_{2}

and reduced Hilbert polynomial
p
, there are only finitely many choices
(up to isomorphism) to write
\mathcal{V}
as a direct sum

\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2}

of Gieseker-polystable
F
-twisted torsion-free Higgs sheaves
with reduced Hilbert polynomial
p
. Indeed, each
\mathcal{V}_{i}

is a direct sum of the finitely many Gieseker-stable
direct summands of
\mathcal{V}
by
the uniqueness of the associated graded of the JH-filtration. We obtain the desired quasi-finiteness
and it remains to show that the direct sum morphism is proper.

The characteristic polynomial of

(\mathcal{V}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{2},\theta_{1}\oplus\theta_{2})

is given by the product of the characteristic polynomials of

(\mathcal{V}_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(\mathcal{V}_{2},\theta_{2})
.
Thus,
\oplus
induces a morphism

\oplus:A^{r_{1},F}_{X}\times A^{r_{2},F}_{X}\to A^{r_{1}+r_{2},F}_{X}
,

((s_{1},\dots,s_{r_{1}}),(t_{1},\dots,t_{r_{2}}))\mapsto(v_{1},\dots,v_{r_{1}+r_{2}})
,
where
v_{i}
is given by
(-1)^{i}
times the degree

coefficient of the polynomial

(y^{r_{1}}-s_{1}y^{r_{1}-1}+\dots+(-1)^{r_{1}}s_{r_{1}})\cdot(y^{r_{2}}-t_{1}y^{r_{2}-1}+\dots+(-1)^{r_{2}}t_{r_{2}})
,
for
1\leq i\leq r_{1}+r_{2}
.
For example,

v_{1}=s_{1}+t_{1}
,
v_{2}=s_{2}+s_{1}\cdot t_{1}+t_{2}
,…,
v_{r_{1}+r_{2}}=s_{r_{1}}\cdot t_{r_{2}}
.

We have the following commutative diagram

(5.1.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\frac{r_{1}}{r}P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\times M^{\frac{r_{2}}{r}P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{X}^{P,\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{r_{1},F}_{X}\times A^{r_{2},F}_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{r,F}_{X}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{r_{1}}\times h^{r_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{r}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\oplus}

Note that
h^{r_{1}}\times h^{r_{2}}
is proper because both
h^{r_{1}}

and
h^{r_{2}}
are proper.
We claim that
\oplus:A^{r_{1},F}_{X}\times A^{r_{2},F}_{X}\to A^{r,F}_{X}

is proper as well.

Let
P^{r,F}_{X}
denote the projective completion of
A^{F,r}_{X}
formed
by adding an extra variable in degree zero.
More precisely,

P^{r,F}_{X}\cong(\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F)\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\})\sslash\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
where
H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{0}F)=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
,
\mathbf{0}:=(0,\dots,0)\in\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F)

is the "origin", and the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action is given by scaling,
i.e.,
\lambda\cdot(s_{0},\dots,s_{n})=(\lambda s_{0},\dots,\lambda s_{n})
.
It follows from the construction that
P^{r,F}_{X}

is a projective space over
k
.

Thus, a point in
P_{X}^{r,F}
can be represented by

in homogeneous coordinates
and

can be identified with the chart
\{s_{0}\neq 0\}
.

We define a map
\overline{\oplus}:P^{r_{1},F}_{X}\times P^{r_{2},F}_{X}\to P^{r,F}_{X}

extending

, given by

([s_{0}:s_{1}:\dots:s_{r_{1}}],[t_{0}:t_{1}:\dots:t_{r_{2}}])\mapsto[v_{0}:v_{1}:\dots:v_{r}],

where each
v_{i}
is
(-1)^{i}
times the degree

(s_{0}y^{r_{1}}-s_{1}y^{r_{1}-1}+\dots+(-1)^{r_{1}}s_{r_{1}})\cdot(t_{0}y^{r_{2}}-t_{1}y^{r_{2}-1}+\dots+(-1)^{r_{2}}t_{r_{2}}),

for

.

Note that

is indeed well defined, because the product of two
such polynomials is identically zero if and only if one of them is identically zero.
This follows from the fact that the ring

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(\eta,\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{i}F_{\eta})

is a polynomial ring
over the generic point

.
We recover

by restricting

to the chart

.
We obtain the Cartesian diagram

By the commutativity of

From the properness together with the quasi-finiteness, we conclude that

5.2.
The split cover and strata

The splitting of a stable twisted Higgs bundle after pullback along
an étale Galois cover enables us to construct a stratification on moduli spaces
of stable Higgs bundles with respect to this cover.
Iterating the good cover constructed in
Theorem

4.4

we obtain a stratification on these moduli spaces that is independent of the
cover.

Definition 5.7

(Decomposition type with respect to a cover)
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be an étale Galois cover with Galois group
G

of a normal projective variety
X
.
Let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
and
let
\mathcal{V}
be a stable
F
-twisted bundle of rank
r
on
X
.
Then by
Lemma

2.13
we have

\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\oplus e}

for
n,e\geq 1
and stable
F_{\mid Y}
-twisted bundles
\mathcal{W}_{i}

such that
G
acts transitively on the isomorphism classes of the
\mathcal{W}_{i}
.
Thus by the transitivity,
m:=\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits(\mathcal{W}_{i})
is independent of
i
and the
same holds for
P^{\prime}:=P(\mathcal{W}_{i})
as the Hilbert polynomial is computed with
respect to
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
which is invariant under
G
.

We call the natural number
m
the
decomposition type

of
\mathcal{V}
with respect to
\pi
.

As in
[
23
]
, the decomposition type
allows us to define a stratification
of the moduli space of
F
-twisted stable Higgs
bundles with respect to an étale Galois cover.

Definition 5.8

(Decomposition stratification with respect to a cover)
.

For an étale Galois cover
\pi:Y\to X
of a normal projective variety
X

we define decomposition strata using Lemma
2.13
. Let
P\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
be a polynomial
and assume that the moduli space
M^{P,F-s}_{X}

of
F
-twisted
stable Higgs bundles with Hilbert polynomial
P
is non-empty.
Denote the rank of bundles with Hilbert polynomial
P
by
r
.
We define for integers
m\geq 1
such that
m\mid r
the following stratum

Z^{F-s,P}(m,\pi):=\left\{(V,\varphi)\in M^{P,F-s}_{X}\middle|\begin{array}[]{c}(\mathcal{V},\varphi)\text{ has decomposition}\\
\text{type }m\text{ with respect to }\pi\end{array}\right\}.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be an étale Galois cover of a normal projective variety
X
.
Let
P
be a polynomial with rational coefficients and
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
r
be the rank of torsion-free sheaves on
X
with Hilbert polynomial
P
.

Then the decomposition stratification with respect to
\pi

defines a stratification with ordering given by

Z^{F-s,P}(m^{\prime},\pi)\leq Z^{F-s,P}(m,\pi):\Leftrightarrow m^{\prime}\mid m

in the sense that the strata are disjoint and

\bigcup_{m^{\prime}\mid m}Z^{F-s,P}(m^{\prime},\pi)
is closed
in
M^{P,F-s}_{X}
for all
m\mid r
.

Proof.

It is clear that the strata are disjoint by definition.
The interesting claim is that the union of all smaller strata is closed.
Note that the moduli space of stable Higgs bundles
with Hilbert polynomial
P
is of finite type.
In particular, there are only finitely many Hilbert polynomials

P^{\prime}_{1},\dots,P^{\prime}_{n}
that can appear as
the Hilbert polynomial of a pullback
\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y}

of a stable Higgs bundle with Hilbert polynomial
P
.
Observe that all the stable direct summands of
\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y}

have the same Hilbert polynomial since the Galois group of
Y\to X

acts transitively on them by the key lemma,
Lemma

2.13
,
and the Hilbert polynomial is computed
with respect to the pullback of the polarization of
X
to
Y

which is invariant under this action as well.

Then
\bigcup_{m^{\prime}\mid m}Z^{F-s,P}(m^{\prime},\pi)

is the union of the preimages of the image of the direct sum map

\prod_{j=1}^{r/m}M^{\frac{m}{r}P^{\prime}_{i},F_{\mid Y}\mathrm{-G-ss}}_{Y}\to M^{P^{\prime}_{i},F_{\mid Y}\mathrm{-G-ss}}_{X},(\mathcal{W}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{W}_{r/m})\mapsto\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{W}_{r/m}

for
i=1,\dots,n
under

\pi^{\ast}:M^{P,F-s}_{X}\to\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}M^{P^{\prime}_{i},F_{\mid Y}\mathrm{-G-ss}}_{X}
.
Since direct sum induces a finite morphism by
Lemma

5.6
,
we obtain the desired closedness.
∎

Lemma 5.10

(Split cover)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety. Let
r\geq 2
be an integer
and
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Then there exists an étale prime to
p
Galois cover

such that for every étale prime to

Galois cover

dominating
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{split}}\nolimits}\to X

any stable
F
-twisted vector bundle of rank
\leq r

on
X
has the same decomposition
type with respect to
Y
as it does with respect to
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{split}}\nolimits}
.

Proof.

This is analogous to
[

, Lemma 4.2]
:
By iterating the good cover we obtain a prime to

5.3.
The image of the semistable 1-stratum under the Hitchin morphism

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
We interpret an element of the Hitchin base

A_{X}^{r,F}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F)

as a degree
r
homogeneous polynomial in a formal variable
y

as already done in
Lemma

5.6
.
In other words, we express

s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})\in A_{X}^{r,F}
as
y^{r}-s_{1}y^{r-1}+\dots+(-1)^{r}s_{r}
.

Multiplication of polynomials then induces maps

\prod\nolimits_{p,q}:A_{X}^{p,F}\times A_{X}^{q,F}\to A_{X}^{r,F}

for all
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
such that
p+q=r
.
These maps are finite (
Lemma

5.6
).

Definition 5.13
.

Let
(p,q)\subset A_{X}^{r,F}
denote the set-theoretic
image of
\prod_{p,q}
, and define
(r)

to be the subset
(r):=A_{X}^{r,F}\setminus\bigcup_{p\leq q}(p,q)
.
We obtain the Hitchin base as the union

A_{X}^{r,F}=\bigcup_{p\leq q}(p,q)\cup(r).

Each of the subsets
(p,q)
is closed because it is the image of a finite morphism.
Thus, the subset
(r)
is open.
Points of
(p,q)
are those polynomials of
A_{X}^{r,F}

that split as a product of a degree
p
and a degree
q
polynomial,
while points of
(r)
are the irreducible polynomials.

A special case is when we consider
the multiplication map

\displaystyle\prod\nolimits_{1,\dots,1}:(A_{X}^{1,F})^{r}:=

\displaystyle A_{X}^{1,F}\times\dots\times A_{X}^{1,F}\to A_{X}^{r,F}

\displaystyle{\big(}(y-s_{1}),\dots,(y-s_{r}){\big)}\mapsto(y-s_{1})\cdots(y-s_{r}).

The set-theoretic image is the closed subset of
A_{X}^{r,F}

consisting of polynomials that split completely,
i.e. as a product of
r
degree 1 polynomials.

Definition 5.14
.

We call the image of the map
\prod_{1,\dots,1}

the
1-stratum
of the Hitchin base
A_{X}^{r,F}

and denote it by
(1)^{\oplus r}
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be an étale Galois cover.
Then pullback induces a morphism
on the Hitchin bases
\pi^{*}:A_{X}^{r,F}\to A_{Y}^{r,F_{\mid Y}}

which is injective on closed points.

Note that
\pi^{*}
preserves each of the closed subsets
(p,q)
,
and also the 1-stratum
(1)^{\oplus r}
.
However, it does not necessarily preserve the subset
(r)
.
The pullback of an irreducible degree
r
polynomial in
A_{X}^{r,F}
may
decompose as a product of lower degree polynomials in
A_{Y}^{r,F_{\mid Y}}
.

Definition 5.15
.

Let
Y\to X
be an étale Galois cover.
We define for
\pi:Y\to X
the closed subset

(p,q)_{Y\to X}:=(\pi^{*})^{-1}(p,q)_{Y}
,
where
(p,q)_{Y}
denotes the image of
\prod_{p,q}

in
A_{Y}^{r,F_{\mid Y}}
.
In other words,
(p,q)_{Y\to X}
consists of those polynomials
in
A_{X}^{r,F}
that split as a product of degree
p
and degree
q

polynomials after pullback to
Y
.
We similarly define
(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}
.

Remark 5.16
.

The affine scheme
(A^{1,F}_{X})^{r}
admits a permutation action
by the symmetric group
S_{r}
.
The Keel-Mori theorem
(
[
22
,
Tag 0DUT
]
)
tells us that the quotient stack
[(A^{1,F}_{X})^{r}/S_{r}]
admits
a coarse (i.e.
k
-points correspond to
S_{r}
-orbits) moduli space,
which is the GIT quotient
(A^{1,F}_{X})^{r}\sslash S_{r}
.

We see from this that the
k
-points of
(A_{X}^{1,F})^{r}\sslash S_{r}
can be
identified with unordered tuples
[\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}]
,
where
\omega_{i}\in H^{0}(X,F)
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
The next Lemma shows that each such unordered tuple can in turn be
identified with a (totally split) degree
r
polynomial

\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-\omega_{i})
.

Lemma 5.17
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and
F
a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}
be in the 1-stratum of the Hitchin base
A^{r,F}_{X}
.
Then the decomposition
s=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(y-s_{i})

is unique up to permutation of the
s_{i}
’s.
Further, the induced morphism

\prod_{1,...,1}:(A_{X}^{1,F})^{r}\sslash S_{r}\to A_{X}^{r,F}
is injective
on closed points.

Proof.

Roughly speaking, the Hitchin base
A^{r,F}_{X}
is locally
a unique factorization domain (UFD).
To be precise, after passing to the generic point
\eta=\mathop{\mathrm{Spec}}(\kappa(X))

we have that
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}H^{0}(\eta,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}F_{\eta})
is a UFD
since it is isomorphic to a polynomial ring over
\kappa(X)

in
\mathop{\mathrm{rk}}\nolimits(F)
many variables.
Thus, restricting two given decompositions

s=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-s_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-s^{\prime}_{i})

to the generic point,
we have that the
(s_{i})\mid_{\eta}

are unique up to permutation.
It follows that the decomposition on
X

is also unique up to permutation as the localization maps

H^{0}(X,\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{l}F)\to H^{0}(\eta,\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{l}F|_{\eta})

are injective for all
l
.

To see the injectivity of

\prod_{1,...,1}:(A_{X}^{1,F})^{r}\sslash S_{r}\to A_{X}^{r,F}

consider a
k
-point of the source as an unordered tuple

[(y-\omega_{1}),\dots,(y-\omega_{r})]
, where
\omega_{i}

is a global section in
A_{X}^{1,F}=H^{0}(X,F)

for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Its image in the Hitchin base
A_{X}^{r,F}
is the polynomial

(y-\omega_{1})\cdots(y-\omega_{r})
and it follows from the first part that the decomposition

\prod_{j=1}^{r}(y-\omega_{j})
is unique up to permutation of the
\omega_{j}
’s.
∎

Lemma 5.18
.

Let
Y\to X
be an étale prime to
p

Galois cover of a normal projective variety with Galois group
G
.
Let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Further, let
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}\cap(r)\subset A^{r,F}_{X}
.

Then
s_{\mid Y}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-t_{i})
, and
G
acts
on the set
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\}
transitively.
Furthermore, the
t_{i}
are pairwise distinct.

Proof.

Note that
G=\mathrm{Aut}(Y/X)
. We consider
each
\sigma\in G
as
an automorphism
\sigma:Y\to Y
fixing
X
.
Denote the
G
-linearization of
F_{\mid Y}
by

\varphi^{F}_{\sigma}:F_{\mid Y}\xrightarrow{\sim}\sigma^{\ast}F_{\mid Y}

for
\sigma\in G
.
Similarly, denote the natural
G
-linearization of
\mathcal{O}_{Y}

by
\varphi^{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}_{\sigma}
.

Given a global section
t\in H^{0}(Y,F_{\mid Y})
we obtain a global section

\sigma\cdot t\in H^{0}(Y,F_{\mid Y})
defined as the composition

\mathcal{O}_{Y}\xrightarrow{\varphi^{\mathcal{O}_{Y}}_{\sigma}}\sigma^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\xrightarrow{\sigma^{\ast}t}\sigma^{\ast}F_{\mid Y}\xrightarrow{(\varphi^{F}_{\sigma})^{-1}}F_{\mid Y}.

Similarly, we define
\sigma\cdot t
for global sections of
symmetric powers of
F
.

The pullback
s_{\mid Y}
of
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}\cap(r)

defines a
G
-equivariant global section

\mathcal{O}_{Y}\xrightarrow{s_{\mid Y}}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{i}F_{\mid Y}
,
that is,
s_{\mid Y}

is compatible with the
G
-linearizations on source and target.
We can phrase this as
\sigma\cdot s_{\mid Y}=s_{\mid Y}
in the
above notation.

Observe that

\sigma\cdot s_{\mid Y}=\sigma\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-t_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-\sigma\cdot t_{i})
, for some
t_{i}\in H^{0}(Y,F_{\mid Y})
, for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Then, by the uniqueness of the decomposition
s_{\mid Y}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-t_{i})
by

Lemma

5.17
,
we find that
\sigma\cdot t_{i}=t_{\sigma(i)}
for some index
\sigma(i)
.
This induces an action of
G
on
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\}
.

Fix an index
l
and consider the
G
-orbit
G\cdot t_{l}

consisting of
m_{l}

distinct elements.
Let
\{t^{1}_{l},\dots,t^{m_{l}}_{l}\}
be the set of distinct elements of
G\cdot t_{l}
.
Then the
j
-th symmetric polynomial in
t^{1}_{l},\dots,t_{l}^{m_{l}}
defines a

G
-equivariant global section of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{j}(F_{\mid Y})
.
Thus, all coefficients of
P=(y-t_{l}^{1})\cdot(y-t_{l}^{2})\cdots(y-t_{l}^{m_{l}})

descend to
X
. Denote the polynomial on
X
obtained by
P^{\prime}
.
As
P
divides
s_{\mid Y}
, we find that
P^{\prime}
divides
s

by the injectivity
H^{0}(X,\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{j}F)\to H^{0}(Y,\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}\nolimits^{j}F_{\mid Y})

for all
j
.

Since
s\in(r)
is irreducible, we conclude that
P^{\prime}=s
.
In particular,
m_{l}=r
i.e., the action of
G
on
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\}

is transitive and all
t_{i}
are pairwise distinct by definition of
m_{l}
.
∎

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety. Let
r\geq 2
be an integer and let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Then there exists an étale prime to
p
Galois cover

such that for every étale prime to
p
Galois cover

dominating
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
,

(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}=(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
.

Proof.

Define
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
to
be an étale prime to
p
Galois
cover dominating all étale prime to
p

covers of
X
of degree
\leq r
.

Let
Y\to X
an étale prime to
p
Galois cover
dominating
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
.
The inclusion

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}\subseteq(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}

is immediate.
To see the converse, consider
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}

and let
s=\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}
be a decomposition
of
s
into irreducible polynomials of degree
r_{i}
.
Then we have that
s_{i}\in(1)^{\oplus r_{i}}_{Y\to X}\cap(r_{i})

in the Hitchin base
A^{r_{i},F}_{X}
.
Since

for irreducible

.

Consider the action of the Galois group
G:=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}\nolimits(Y/X)

on the pairwise distinct factors
t_{i}
of

obtained from Lemma

.
Let
H_{i}
be the stabilizers of
t_{i}
for

.
Then the section

is the pullback of a section

of

,
where

associated to

.
The same holds for the elementary symmetric polynomials in
the

for

.
We conclude that
(y-t^{\prime}_{i})
is a factor of
s_{\mid Y_{i}}
.

Note that
Y_{i}\to X
is an étale prime to
p
cover
of degree
\leq r
for each
i
.
Thus, the cover
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
dominates

Y_{i}\to X
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
It follows that
s_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}
is
divisible by
(y-(t^{\prime}_{i})_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}})
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Since the
t_{i}
are pairwise distinct and

(t^{\prime}_{i})_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}
pulls back to

on

,
the
(t^{\prime}_{i})_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}

are pairwise distinct as well.
Thus,
\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-(t^{\prime}_{i})_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}})
is
a monic polynomial of degree

dividing
s_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}
.
Since
s_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}
is a pullback of
s
,
it is also a monic polynomial of degree

and thus

s_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-(t^{\prime}_{i})_{\mid X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}})

.
∎

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be an étale Galois cover of a normal projective variety with
Galois group
G
. Let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
and
r\geq 2
.
Let
s\in(r)\cap(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}

and
s_{\mid Y}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-t_{i})
for some
t_{i}\in H^{0}(Y,F_{\mid Y})
.

Then
s=h_{X}(\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}t^{\prime})
for some intermediate étale cover
Y\to Y^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}X

of degree
r
and
t^{\prime}\in H^{0}(Y^{\prime},F_{\mid Y^{\prime}})
such that
t^{\prime}_{\mid Y}=t_{i}
for some
i
,
where
\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}t^{\prime}
is considered as an
F
-twisted Higgs bundle with underlying

\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}
.
Furthermore,
\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}t^{\prime}
is a stable
F
-twisted Higgs bundle.

Proof.

Let
H
be the stabilizer of a fixed

under the action of
G
on

\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\}
. Then the cover

factors as

.

The section
t_{i}\in H^{0}(Y,F_{\mid Y})
is
H
-equivariant
by definition

Consider via pushforward the

-twisted Higgs bundle

\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}(t^{\prime}):\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}\to\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}F_{\mid Y^{\prime}}\cong\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}\otimes F
, where

We claim that
\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}(t^{\prime})

.

First observe that
(\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}})_{\mid Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus r}

has degree

.
By adjunction we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime\ast}\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime\ast}\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}\otimes F_{\mid Y^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{Y^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\mid Y^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\prime\ast}\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}(t^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t^{\prime}}

with surjective vertical arrows which further pulls back to

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus r}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus r}\otimes F_{\mid Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{\mid Y}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{\ast}\pi^{\prime}_{\ast}t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}}

-twisted Higgs bundle

For a smooth projective variety in characteristic

the conjectured set-theoretic image of the Hitchin morphism

(see

for details).

In the smooth case, the

-stratum for the split cover

is always contained in the image of the Hitchin morphism from the moduli space of Gieseker-semistable

-Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes.

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
let
F
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
P_{0}
denote the Hilbert polynomial
of the trivial vector bundle of rank
r
.
The set-theoretic image of the semistable
1
-stratum

Z^{\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits,P_{0}}(1)\subset M^{P_{0},\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}

via the Hitchin morphism
h^{r}:M^{P_{0},\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\to A^{r,F}_{X}

is precisely the
1
-stratum for the split cover

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}\subset A^{r,F}_{X}
.

Proof.

Consider a closed point

.
By induction, it suffices to treat the case that
s
is irreducible,
i.e., that
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}\cap(r)
.

Then it follows from Lemma
5.20

that there is a stable
F
-Higgs bundle
(\mathcal{V},\theta)

of rank
r

whose underlying vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
is étale-trivializable,
and
s
is the characteristic polynomial of
(\mathcal{V},\theta)
.
In particular, the Chern classes of
\mathcal{V}
vanish,
so it lies in
Z^{\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits,P_{0}}(1)\subset M^{P_{0},\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}
.
∎

Corollary 5.22
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
F
be an étale trivializable vector bundle of rank
n
on
X
.
Then the set-theoretic
image of the Hitchin morphism

h^{r}:M^{P_{0},\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}\to A^{r,F}_{X}

coincides with the
1
-stratum
(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.21
we know that

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
is contained in the image of
h^{r}
.
Thus, it suffices to show that
h^{r}
factors through

(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
.

Let
(\mathcal{V},\theta)\in M^{P_{0},\mathop{F\mathrm{-G-ss}}\nolimits}_{X}
be
a closed point and let

s:=h^{r}(\mathcal{V},\theta)\in A^{r,F}_{X}
be the characteristic polynomial
of
\theta
. Let
Y\to X
be an étale cover dominating
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
that trivializes
F
, i.e.,
F_{\mid Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus n}
.
Then
s_{\mid Y}\in A^{r,O_{Y}^{\oplus n}}_{Y}
is the characteristic polynomial
of
(\mathcal{V}_{\mid Y},\theta_{\mid Y})\in M^{P_{0},\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus n}-\mathrm{G-ss}}_{Y}
.

We want to show that
s_{\mid Y}\in(1)^{\oplus r}\subset A^{r,O_{Y}^{\oplus n}}_{Y}
. Note that the global sections of the bundle

\mathrm{Sym}^{j}\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus n}
are constant for all
j\geq 1
. The
Higgs field
\theta_{\mid Y}
is locally an

r\times r
matrix whose entries
are local sections of
\mathcal{O}^{\oplus n}_{Y}
. The characteristic
polynomial of
\theta
is
s_{\mid Y}
and locally, the roots of
s_{\mid Y}
are precisely the eigenvalues of
\theta_{\mid Y}
.

Since the coefficients of
s_{\mid Y}
are constant, the eigenvalues of

\theta_{\mid Y}
are locally constant,
hence constant on all of
Y
because
Y
is connected.
Thus, we have a splitting
s_{\mid Y}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(y-t_{i})
,
where
t_{i}\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus n})
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
It follows that
s_{\mid Y}\in(1)^{\oplus r}
and thus

s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}
.
Since
Y\to X
dominates
X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X
,
we have
s\in(1)^{\oplus r}_{X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{H-split}}\nolimits}\to X}
by Lemma
5.19
.
∎

As an application, we can determine the image of the Hitchin morphism from the
Dolbeault moduli space
of Gieseker semistable
\Omega^{1}_{X}
-Higgs bundles with vanishing Chern classes, when
X
is a hyperelliptic variety, that is, a
smooth projective quotient of an abelian variety.

Corollary 5.23
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
such that there is an étale Galois cover
Y\to X
, where
Y
is an abelian variety. Then the
set-theoretic
image of
h^{r}:M^{P_{0},\Omega^{1}_{X}-\mathrm{G-ss}}_{X}\to A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{X}}_{X}

coincides with the subset

(H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r})^{G}

of
A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{Y}}_{Y}
, where
G:=\mathop{\mathrm{Gal}}\nolimits(Y/X)
.

In particular, if
X
is an abelian variety, the set-theoretic image of
h^{r}
is
(\mathbb{A}^{d})^{r}\sslash S_{r}
.

Proof.

Since
Y\to X
is étale and
Y
is an abelian variety, it follows that
we have isomorphisms

(\Omega^{1}_{X})_{\mid Y}\cong\Omega^{1}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}^{\oplus d}
, i.e.,

\Omega^{1}_{X}
is an étale trivializable vector bundle
(see e.g.
[
16
, Chapter 4(iii)]
).
By Corollary
5.22
,
the set-theoretic image of
h^{r}
is equal to
(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}
.

Note that the
G
-action on
H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r}

is precisely the
G
-action on the corresponding set of polynomials, i.e.,
for any closed point
v:=[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]
of
H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r}

and any
g\in G
, we have

g\cdot v=g\cdot[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]=g\cdot(\sigma_{1}(v),\dots\sigma_{r}(v))=(g\cdot\sigma_{1}(v),\dots,g\cdot\sigma_{r}(v)),

where
\sigma_{i}(v)\in H^{0}(Y,\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{Y})

is the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in
[v_{1},\dots,v_{r}]
.

By definition,
(1)^{\oplus r}_{Y\to X}
contains all polynomials
s
such that
s_{\mid Y}\in(1)^{\oplus r}\subset A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{Y}}_{Y}
. Recall that the pullback morphism
A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{X}}_{X}\to A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{Y}}_{Y}
is injective
on closed points, and
(1)^{\oplus r}\cong(A^{1,\Omega^{1}_{Y}}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r}\cong H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r}
. We can thus identify
s
with
s_{\mid Y}
, and
s_{\mid Y}
with its set of roots
\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
,
t_{i}\in H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})
for all
i
.
Since
s_{\mid Y}
is a pullback from
X
, it is
G
-equivariant. Thus,
s_{\mid Y}=[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]\in(H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r})^{G}
.

On the other hand, every characteristic polynomial
t
in

A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{Y}}_{Y}
splits as a product of degree
1
factors, thus
t
is
a closed point of
H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r}
.
Suppose now that
t
is a closed point of

(H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r})^{G}
,
then it is
G
-equivariant, in particular,
its coefficients
t_{i}
are
G
-equivariant global sections of

\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{Y}
and thus descend to global sections

s_{i}
of
\mathrm{Sym}^{i}\Omega^{1}_{X}
, for
1\leq i\leq r
.

Thus,
t
descends to a polynomial
s\in(1)^{r}_{Y\to X}\subset A^{r,\Omega^{1}_{X}}_{X}
,
whose coefficients are
s_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq r
, i.e.,
t=s_{\mid Y}
.
From Lemma
5.20
,
it follows that
s\in\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits(h^{r})
, and we conclude that

\mathop{\mathrm{im}}\nolimits(h^{r})=(H^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})^{r}\sslash S_{r})^{G}
set-theoretically.
∎

5.4.
Specializing to classical Higgs bundles

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
.
Then by Simpson’s classical non-abelian Hodge correspondence
(see
[
20
,
21
]
), there is a
homeomorphism between the Dolbeault moduli space
M^{r}_{X,\text{Dol}}

of Gieseker semistable Higgs bundles of rank
r

with vanishing Chern classes on
X
,
and the
Betti moduli space

M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Betti}}
of conjugacy classes of
semisimple representations

\pi_{1}(X)\to GL(r,\mathbb{C})
of the topological fundamental group.

Remark 5.24
.

The decomposition stratification on
M^{r}_{X,\text{Dol}}
induces a
decomposition stratification on
M^{r}_{X,\mathrm{Betti}}
via the non-abelian
Hodge correspondence. The decomposition type of an irreducible
representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\to GL(r,\mathbb{C})
is defined analogously to
that of a stable Higgs bundle, with respect to the split cover

X_{r,\mathop{\mathrm{split}}\nolimits}\to X
.

Long-term comparative effectiveness of tepotinib versus standard of care for MET exon 14 skipping-mutated non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Joo-Young Byun, Hyun Ae Jung, Eui-Kyung Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Tepotinib received Food and Drug Administration approval for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (aNSCLC) with MET exon 14 skipping mutation (METex14m) based on a single-arm trial. However, the comparative effectiveness between tepotinib and standard of care (SoC) remains unclear.Objectives:We aim to assess the long-term comparative effectiveness between first-line tepotinib and SoC.Design:This retrospective comparative effectiveness study was conducted based on a matching-adjusted indirect comparison using aggregate data for Asian patients of the VISION trial for the tepotinib arm, and data of patients with METex14m who received SoC from Samsung Medical Center data in January 2008 and February 2024 for the SoC arm.Methods:Hazard ratio (HR) and its confidence intervals (CIs) for progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were assessed through a Cox proportional hazards model. A partitioned survival model was run for a lifetime using four different scenarios to estimate long-term quality-adjusted life-years (QALYs), which reflect patients’ health-related quality of life (HRQoL).Results:In the SoC arm (n = 44), 26 and 18 were treated with an immunotherapy-containing regimen and chemotherapy, as their first-line SoC. Thirty (68.2%) of the SoC (n = 44) arm received subsequent METex14-targeting therapy. Tepotinib significantly improved PFS compared to SoC (HR (95% CI), 0.38 (0.22–0.63)). The PFS improvement was more pronounced when compared to the chemotherapy subgroup (HR (95% CI), 0.20 (0.10–0.38)) and was also evident in the immunotherapy-containing regimen subgroup (HR (95% CI), 0.55 (0.29–1.03)). Although OS did not differ significantly (HR (95% CI), 1.04 (0.56–1.93)), the partitioned survival model yielded an additional 0.2409–0.3039 QALYs in the tepotinib arm, showing HRQoL benefits of tepotinib over SoC.Conclusion:The significant PFS benefit of first-line tepotinib over SoC extended to long-term HRQoL gains, indicating tepotinib is considered one of the first-line treatment options. Future studies based on multicenter data or head-to-head trials would be able to verify the efficacy.

Introduction

Tepotinib, one of the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) targeting mesenchymal-epithelial transition exon 14 skipping-mutated (METex14m) advanced non-small cell lung cancer (aNSCLC), showed its efficacy and safety in a phase II single-arm trial (VISION).1,2 Tepotinib was approved based on the objective response rate (ORR) and duration of response reported in the VISION trial, which lacked the comparison of survival outcomes with a comparator arm due to the single-arm trial design. 3 Although overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were also reported as secondary endpoints in the VISION trial, whether tepotinib improves survival outcomes compared to standard of care (SoC) remains unclear. Robust evidence of tepotinib’s comparative effectiveness against SoC would help address the uncertainties surrounding tepotinib’s efficacy.

To date, Batteson et al. 4 have compared tepotinib and SoC using a partitioned survival model from a United Kingdom perspective. They reported median OS and PFS along with quality-adjusted life year (QALY) gains in the tepotinib arm. However, the absence of details for the real-world data that were used, patient characteristics before and after matching, and the results of the comparative effectiveness data (i.e., proportional hazard assumption test, hazard ratio (HR) and its confidence intervals (CIs)) left whether tepotinib significantly surpasses SoC unclear. The lack of confirmed statistical significance of the comparative effectiveness data used for the modeling also questions the appropriateness of the estimated long-term comparative effectiveness.

The efficacy of tepotinib appears to be even more promising in Asian patients. In the VISION trial, the primary endpoint, ORR, was higher in the line-agnostic subgroup analysis for Asian patients compared to Caucasian/White patients (61.9% vs 41.9%). In the first-line setting, the effectiveness of tepotinib was consistently greater in Asian patients than in multiracial patients reported in the VISION trial (ORR: 64.0% vs 57.3%; median PFS: 16.5 vs 12.6 months; median OS: 32.7 vs 21.3 months). 5 These findings suggest that the relative efficacy of tepotinib versus SoC may differ in Asian patients compared to in other racial groups; however, this potential differential effect has not yet been clearly established.

To address these gaps, this study aims to assess the comparative effectiveness between first-line tepotinib and SoC in Asian patients with METex14m aNSCLC. This study also aims to compare long-term life years adjusted for patients’ health-related quality of life (HRQoL) between the two treatments by modeling.

Methods

Comparative effectiveness study

Study design, data source, and patient selection

For the indirect comparison between the clinical trial and real-world cohorts, we included patients with METex14 aNSCLC who received first-line palliative SoC treatment at Samsung Medical Center between January 1, 2008, and February 27, 2024. The detailed patient selection criteria were based on those of the VISION trial (eSupplemental Method 1).

The data for the SoC arm were obtained using ROOT-HEALTH, an automated platform developed at Samsung Medical Center that leverages natural language processing and disease-specific algorithms to extract and organize information from electronic medical records and the clinical data warehouse. 6 The SoC cohort used in this study was constructed to ensure that no missing data were present. For the tepotinib arm, pseudo-individual patient data (IPD) were constructed for the 50 Asian patients enrolled in the VISION trial who received first-line tepotinib. 5 Reconstruction was performed using R version 4.4.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria; purrr package version 1.0.2) according to the algorithm developed by Guyot et al. 7 The accuracy of the reconstructed pseudo-IPD was assessed using goodness-of-fit statistics by comparing the reconstructed Kaplan–Meier curves with the original digitized curves.

The manuscript was prepared in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines for observational studies (eSupplemental Material 2). 8

Exposures and outcomes

For the tepotinib and SoC arms, first-line palliative treatment with tepotinib or SoC were exposures for each group. The SoC includes chemotherapy and immunotherapies that are not specifically targeting METex14m. Main outcomes of interest were PFS and OS.

Matching-adjusted indirect comparison

To facilitate an indirect comparison between the two cohorts while accounting for differences in baseline patient characteristics, an matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC) was performed using IPD from the SoC cohort and aggregate-level data from the tepotinib cohort. MAIC reweights patients in the IPD cohort such that the weighted distribution of selected baseline covariates matches that reported for the tepotinib cohort. 9

Covariate centering

The following baseline characteristics were included in the MAIC model: age, sex (male/female), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS; 0/1), histology (adenocarcinoma/non-adenocarcinoma), presence of baseline brain metastasis (yes/no), and smoking status (former/never).

Before weight estimation, each matching variable was centered by subtracting the corresponding aggregate mean or proportion reported for the target (tepotinib) cohort. Specifically, for each covariate xk , the centered value was defined as: x~ik=xik−x¯ktarget , where x¯ktarget denotes the published mean or proportion of covariate k in the target population. This centering ensures that the moment-balancing constraints are satisfied when the weighted mean of each centered covariate equals zero. 10

Weight estimation

Individual weights were estimated by using an exponential tilting approach. The weight assigned to patient i was defined as wi=exp(∑k=1Kαkx~ik), where α= is a vector of parameters estimated from the data. Parameter estimates were obtained by minimizing the objective function: Q(α)=∑i=1nexp(∑k=1Kαkx~ik). Optimization was performed using PROC NLP in SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA), with all αk parameters initialized to 1. Minimization of Q(α) under the implied balancing constraints is equivalent to maximum-entropy weighting and yields the set of weights with minimum variance that achieves covariate balance. The estimated parameter vector α^ was subsequently used to calculate individual MAIC weights. 11

Effective sample size

The effective sample size (ESS) was calculated as: ESS=((∑iwi)2∑iwi2) . 12 A substantial reduction in ESS relative to the original sample size indicates increased heterogeneity in the estimated weights and reduced overlap between the study populations.

Statistical analysis

As the OS and PFS reported from the VISION trial were analyzed in an intention-to-treat setting, the SoC arm’s OS and PFS were also evaluated using the intention-to-treat approach. A log-rank test was conducted to determine whether the difference in PFS and OS between the two groups was statistically significant. The proportional hazards assumption for OS and PFS between the two groups was evaluated through visual inspection of Kaplan–Meier curves and log-cumulative hazard plots, as well as statistical evaluation using Schoenfeld residual plots and the Grambsch–Therneau test. Once the proportional hazards assumption was confirmed to hold, HRs and their 95% CIs were estimated using Cox proportional hazards models. To address potential under- or overestimation of PFS in the SoC arm, an additional survival analysis was conducted in which the PFS values for the SoC arm were systematically varied by ±10% and ±20%.

Survival analyses and Cox models were conducted using SAS version 9.4 and R version 4.4.0 (survival package version 3.6.4; survminer package version 0.4.9; ggplot2 package version 3.5.1).

Subgroup analysis

Based on the type of first-line treatment, patients in the SoC arm were classified into two subgroups: immunotherapy and chemotherapy subgroups. The immunotherapy subgroup included patients who received an immunotherapy-containing regimen, while the chemotherapy subgroup consisted of patients treated with chemotherapy alone, without immunotherapy.

Sensitivity analysis

Following the recommendations from the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) Decision Support Unit (DSU) Technical Support Document (TSD) 16, 13 the OS data for patients who received MET inhibitors as subsequent therapy after SoC were adjusted using the rank-preserving structural failure time model (RPSFTM) method.

Long-term comparative effectiveness using modeling

Model overview

A three-state partitioned survival model (progression-free state, progressed disease state, death) was used to assess the long-term effectiveness of tepotinib compared to SoC (eSupplemental Method 2; eFigure M2-1). The target population was patients with METex14m aNSCLC who are palliative treatment-naïve. According to the mean age of the Asian population of the VISION trial, the hypothetical cohort was assumed to be aged 70.5 when entering the model. Given the life expectancy of 82.7 years in South Korea (2022), 14 the model was run over a lifetime horizon (12 years), using 1-week cycles and applying a half cycle correction.15 –17 QALYs were calculated over the model time horizon by incorporating health state utilities (progression-free state: 0.687; progressed disease state: 0.580) and disutilities from adverse events (eSupplemental Method 3; eTables M3-1–M3-4; and eSupplemental Method 4; eTables M4-1 and M4-2), with an annual discount rate of 4.5% in accordance with the recommendations of the South Korean HTA (Health Insurance Review & Assessment Service, HIRA). 18

Long-term clinical efficacy

When the proportional hazards assumption was not violated, and HRs estimated from the Cox models indicate statistically significant differences, dependent modeling using HR was applied. According to the algorithm for selecting a survival model in NICE DSU TSD 14, 19 for the parametric extrapolation, the curves were fitted with exponential, Weibull, gamma, log-normal, Gompertz, log-logistic, and generalized gamma distributions. To consider flexible parametric modeling, spline hazard models were also fitted. To choose the best-fitting model, the curve was fitted with the distribution with the lowest Akaike’s Information Criterion and Bayesian Information Criterion after excluding invalid distributions through visual inspection.

To address the uncertainties stemming from extrapolation, the curves were extrapolated under four different scenarios: (1) using the observed OS or PFS Kaplan–Meier curves up to the time point when the observation was finished and adjoining the extrapolated curves from the end of the Kaplan–Meier curves 20 ; (2) adjoining the extrapolated curves from the median OS or PFS estimated after MAIC; (3) adjoining the extrapolated curves from the time points where the pattern or slope of log-cumulative hazard plots changed; and (4) using the full parametric model for the entire time horizon. Deterministic sensitivity analysis was conducted to evaluate the robustness of results (eSupplemental Method 5; eTable M5-1).

Results

Comparative effectiveness

Patient selection and characteristics

In total, 44 patients treated with first-line SoC in Samsung Medical Center were included in the SoC arm (Figure 1), with a reduced ESS of 41.0 after MAIC (Table 1). Among them, 18 and 26 patients were classified as chemotherapy and immunotherapy subgroups, respectively (eSupplemental Result 1.1; eTable R1-1). Pembrolizumab combined with platinum and pemetrexed (29.5%) was the most frequently used first-line SoC regimen, followed by platinum and pemetrexed (27.3%) and pembrolizumab monotherapy (20.5%; Table 1).

In the SoC arm, 68.2% (n = 30) of the patients were treated with subsequent METex14m-targeting agents, mostly with tepotinib (n = 9, 30%) and capmatinib (n = 9, 30%), followed by clinical trial drugs (n = 8, 27%) and crizotinib (n = 4, 13%).

The median follow-up period of the SoC arm was 20.8 months (range: 0.8–61.4 months) and 19.4 months (range: 0.8–61.4 months) before and after MAIC, respectively. For the tepotinib arm, the VISION trial reported a longer median follow-up period of 32.6 months for the Asian population (range: 0.3–71.9 months). 5

Before MAIC, absolute standardized mean differences (aSMD) for age and the presence of baseline brain metastasis were higher than 0.1, indicating significant differences between the two groups. After MAIC, all the variables included in the MAIC were well balanced patient characteristics between the two groups (aSMD <0.001; Table 1).

Progression-free survival

The reconstruction for the PFS curve of the tepotinib arm demonstrated acceptable statistical fit (R2: 0.983; mean squared error (MSE): 0.000784). Consistent with before MAIC (eSupplemental Result 2), the tepotinib arm showed significantly higher PFS than the SoC arm (median PFS (95% CI), 16.33 months (9.41–49.54 months) vs 4.77 months (3.19–5.92 months); p < 0.001) after MAIC (Figure 2(a)). Based on a Cox proportional hazard model (eSupplemental Result 3.1; eFigures R3-1 and R3-2), tepotinib showed a significant PFS benefit over SoC after MAIC (HR (95% CI), 0.38 (0.22–0.63), p < 0.001; Figure 2(a)). The additional survival analysis demonstrated consistent results with PFS in the SoC arm varied by ±10% and ±20% (eSupplemental Result 3.2; eTable R3-1).

PFS (a) and OS (b) of the tepotinib and SoC groups after MAIC.

CI, confidence interval; HR, hazard ratio; M, months; MAIC, matching-adjusted indirect comparison; OS, overall survival; PFS, progression-free survival; SoC, standard of care.

A consistently significant PFS benefit of tepotinib was shown against the chemotherapy subgroup (HR (95% CI), 0.20 (0.10–0.38), p < 0.001). Against the immunotherapy subgroup, a tendency of PFS improvement was shown without statistical significance (HR (95% CI), 0.55 (0.29–1.03), p = 0.06; eSupplemental Result 3.3).

Overall survival

The OS curve reconstruction for the tepotinib arm also showed close correspondence to the original published curve (R2: 0.990; MSE: 0.000243). Median OS was not significantly different between the two groups (median OS (95% CI): tepotinib 32.80 months (16.33–NA months); SoC 26.7 months (19.4–NAmonths); log-rank p = 0.90; Figure 2(b)). Cox proportional hazard model revealed that tepotinib and SoC showed comparable OS after MAIC (HR (95% CI), 1.04 (0.56–1.93), p = 0.90; eSupplemental Result 4.1; eFigures R4-1 and R4-2). OS was not significantly different between the tepotinib arm and immunotherapy subgroup (HR (95% CI), 0.81 (0.39–1.68), p = 0.58) and chemotherapy subgroup (HR (95% CI), 1.03 (0.45–2.35), p = 0.94; eSupplemental Result 4.2).

Sensitivity analysis on OS

In the SoC arm, 30 patients received MET inhibitors as subsequent therapy approximately 11 months after the first date of first-line SoC treatment (eSupplemental Result 5; eTable R5-1). With the acceleration factor converged to 1 in the RPSFTM, the sensitivity analysis result remained consistent with the main analysis (intention-to-treat framework).

Long-term comparative effectiveness

To estimate long-term life years adjusted for HRQoL (i.e., QALY), PFS and OS were extrapolated based on whether significant differences were observed between the two groups. A significant PFS benefit led to dependent modeling for PFS using the estimated HR. The fitted curves with parametric distributions for the tepotinib arm’s PFS are presented in Figure 3. Among the distributions, the log-logistic distribution was selected as the best-fitting model (eSupplemental Result 6; eTable R6-1), therefore, it was used for dependent modeling. PFS curves were extrapolated using the specified adjoining time points (eTable R6-2), as illustrated in Figure 4 and eFigure R6-1.

Since OS differences between the groups were not statistically significant, OS data were pooled and extrapolated with the parametric distributions (Figure 3). The exponential distribution was selected as the best-fitting model for the pooled OS (eSupplemental Result 7; eTable R7-1). The extrapolated OS curves, based on the time points specified in eTable R7-2, are presented in Figure 4 and eFigure R7-1.

Across in-depth scenario analyses on extrapolation ranging from full parametric to Kaplan–Meier curve extended with extrapolation at three differential adjoining times, tepotinib showed consistent QALY gains of 0.2409–0.3039 over a lifetime horizon, presenting its benefit in HRQoL-adjusted life years (Figure 4).

Deterministic sensitivity analysis revealed that variations in health state utilities affected the results approximately by 20% (eSupplemental Result 8). Extrapolating the tepotinib arm’s PFS with the second-best fitting distribution resulted in −15% to −26% of incremental QALY decrease. Excluding the disutilities from adverse events in the SoC arm led to −16% to −38% of incremental QALY decrease.

Discussion

As the first study to assess the effectiveness of first-line tepotinib compared to SoC in an Asian population, this study demonstrated a significant PFS benefit of tepotinib, which extended to long-term HRQoL benefit. Given limited evidence on treatment options for METex14m NSCLC, our study provides insights into choosing between first-line METex14m-targeted therapy followed by SoC, and the reverse sequence. In the VISION trial, about half of the 106 Asian patients (n = 50) received subsequent therapies, mainly cytotoxic agents (n = 32, 30.2%) and immunotherapies (n = 23, 21.7%). 5 Among the 50 treatment-naïve patients, where tepotinib was used as first-line palliative therapy, over half likely moved on to further treatment. Similarly, in our study, 68.2% of patients in the SoC arm received subsequent METex14m-targeted therapies. Thus, our analysis reflects the effectiveness of both sequences: SoC followed by METex14m-targeted therapies, and tepotinib followed by SoC. The significant PFS benefit seen with tepotinib, which translated into improved HRQoL-adjusted life years, suggests that front-line tepotinib may improve quality of life via PFS improvement.

Consistent with our findings, the PFS benefit of tepotinib over SoC and similar OS between the two groups have been reported in previous conference abstracts which derived their SoC arms from a clinical trial (KEYNOTE-189) or real-world data without specifying race.21,22 OS was similar or not significantly different in both abstracts: Vioix et al. 21 (median OS: tepotinib 23.6 months; SoC 23.3 months) and Christopoulos et al. 22 (median OS: tepotinib 29.7 months; SoC 22.1 months; HR 0.77; p = 0.38). Both abstracts showed tepotinib-favoring PFS results (HR (95% CI), 0.67 (0.42–1.07); 0.52 (0.29–0.93)).21,22 Our PFS result against immunotherapy subgroup (HR (95% CI), 0.55 (0.29–1.03)) is comparable to result of Christopoulos et al.’s abstract, which used real world data of patients with METex14m aNSCLC for the SoC arm, whereas a less tepotinib-favoring result was observed in Vioix et al.’s abstract, whose SoC arm came from KEYNOTE-189 and included patients with unknown METex14m status.

While both Christopoulos et al.’s 22 abstract and our study both used real-world data for the SoC arm, the observed PFS and OS were generally higher in our study. Median PFS and OS were higher in patients treated with tepotinib (PFS: 16.3 vs 15.9 months; OS: 32.8 vs 29.7 months), and in those treated with immunotherapy-containing regimens (PFS: 9.3 vs 6.9 months; OS: 25.0 vs 22.1 months) than in Christopoulos et al. This may reflect improved treatment outcomes in Asian patients with NSCLC, as reported in previous studies.1,23,24

Beyond the internationally approved TKIs for METex14m NSCLC (hereafter MET-TKIs; i.e., tepotinib and capmatinib), several additional MET-TKIs including savolitinib, gumarontinib, and vebreltinib have received approval in Asian countries based on efficacy data predominantly derived from Asian populations.25 –28 All three agents were approved in China based on clinical trials conducted in Chinese patients. Notably, savolitinib received full approval from the National Medical Products Administration following a confirmatory phase IIIb trial conducted in China. 26 Gumarontinib was additionally approved in Japan, supported by trial data inclusive of Japanese patients. 29 Although no head-to-head comparisons between these agents and tepotinib are currently available, among treatment-naïve Asian patients, the highest reported median PFS was observed with tepotinib, followed by vebreltinib, savolitinib, and gumarontinib. 25 These comparisons are inherently limited by differences in study design, patient selection, and follow-up duration, and future studies employing more rigorous comparative designs with adequate control for confounding are warranted to establish the relative effectiveness of MET-TKIs, especially in Asian patients.

Several HTA submissions have evaluated tepotinib for reimbursement. While our study incorporated only PFS benefits due to nonsignificant OS differences, the NICE submission projected greater life-years and QALYs for tepotinib than SoC (pembrolizumab + pemetrexed + platinum), despite lacking significant OS data. 30 This suggests the model assumed an OS benefit without sufficient evidence. Additionally, Canada’s Drug Agency submission found fewer QALYs for tepotinib than SoC (immunotherapy combined with platinum-based chemotherapy), even though their ITC showed no significant OS difference; yet projected more life years for SoC. 31 In the absence of statistically significant OS differences, modeling to produce life-year gain in either arm introduces substantial uncertainty. Our study minimized this risk by pooling OS data from both groups and reflecting tepotinib’s significant PFS benefit with comparable OS. Our results suggest tepotinib’s PFS benefit is extended to benefit in long-term life-years adjusted for HRQoL within a similar survival timeframe compared to SoC.

For modeling time-to-event data, HTAs such as South Korea’s HIRA and Australia’s Pharmaceutical Benefits Advisory Committee recommend using observed time-to-event data and adjoining the extrapolated curve from the time point where the observation ends, as implemented in scenario 1 in our study.18,32 NICE has also discussed this method, noting the importance of the time points at which extrapolation begins. 20 In line with scenario 3 in our study, time points where the hazard profile changed have been most frequently adopted as the adjoining time points by NICE submissions which used piecewise models. 33 Three different adjoining times in our scenario analyses resulted in −3.02% (scenario 3) to 22.34% (scenario 1) incremental QALY differences compared to the full parametric model (scenario 4). In scenario 1, the plateau in the PFS curve of the tepotinib arm from approximately 22 to 57 months let the PFS curve to drop later than in other scenarios, leading to higher QALY gains in the tepotinib arm. Given that modeling results fluctuate by adjoining time points, especially when a plateau is observed before the observation ends, it is recommended to select adjoining time points with caution when using an observed Kaplan–Meier curve for the earlier part of the modeling.

This study has several limitations. First, differences in PFS assessment between clinical trial and real-world settings may have introduced conservatism in PFS estimates for the SoC arm, as real-world follow-up intervals are typically less structured, potentially resulting in overestimation of PFS in the SoC arm. Moreover, clinical trial participants generally have better health status than real-world patients, 34 potentially favoring the tepotinib arm and overestimating its treatment effect. However, our additional survival analysis with ±10% and ±20% variations in the SoC arm’s PFS yielded consistent results, suggesting this potential bias did not materially affect our findings. Furthermore, MAIC incorporated key clinical characteristics (age, sex, ECOG PS, histology, brain metastasis, and smoking history) to minimize this bias, though residual confounding from unmeasured factors cannot be excluded. Second, OS analyses could not fully account for subsequent MET inhibitor use, which was reported only in the SoC arm; however, RPSFTM analysis yielded consistent results before and after adjustment. Third, calendar time was not accounted for in SoC arm selection; however, given the overlap between the SoC data collection period (2017–2023) and VISION trial enrollment (September 2016–January 2020), substantial differences in background healthcare circumstances between the two arms are unlikely. Fourth, due to the rare prevalence of METex14m NSCLC, the sample size for both the SoC and tepotinib arms is limited. However, a post hoc power analysis revealed a statistical power of 96.9% for the PFS HR. Fifth, as the SoC arm was derived from a single-center cohort and safety data were unavailable for first-line SoC, the generalizability of our findings may be limited, and a formal safety comparison between tepotinib and SoC could not be performed. Future studies incorporating multicenter real-world data or randomized trial designs with prospective safety data collection would provide more robust and generalizable estimates of comparative effectiveness and safety. Finally, in the absence of Korean-specific utility data, Taiwanese health utility weights were used based on evidence of close genetic proximity between Korean and Taiwanese Han Chinese populations, as well as high agreement in EQ-5D preference weights among East Asian populations35,36; however, given potential differences in health preferences between Korean and Taiwanese populations, caution is warranted when interpreting these results.

Conclusion

First-line tepotinib demonstrated significant PFS and long-term quality of life benefits over SoC, indicating tepotinib can be considered one of the first-line treatment options for METex14m NSCLC. Future studies based on multicenter data or head-to-head trials would be able to verify the efficacy.

Abstract
Accurate radio propagation and interference modeling is essential for the design and analysis of modern cellular networks. Stochastic geometry offers a rigorous framework by treating base station locations as a Poisson point process and enabling coverage characterization through spatial averaging, but its expressions often involve nested integrals and special functions that limit general applicability. Probabilistic interference models seek closed-form characterizations through moment-based approximations, yet these expressions remain tractable only for restricted parameter choices and become unwieldy when interference moments lack closed-form representations. This work introduces a hybrid approximation framework that addresses these challenges by combining Monte Carlo sampling of a small set of dominant interferers with a Laplace functional representation of the residual far-field interference. The resulting dominant-plus-tail structure provides a modular, numerically stable, and path-loss-agnostic estimator suitable for both noise-limited and interference-limited regimes. We further derive theoretical error bounds that decrease with the number of dominant interferers and validate the approach against established stochastic geometry and probabilistic modeling benchmarks.



I
Introduction

Signal coverage analysis in cellular networks is a foundational problem in wireless communications
[
2
,
16
]
and can be characterized probabilistically, representing the likelihood that the instantaneous or spatially averaged signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) exceeds a prescribed threshold. This probabilistic formulation, often referred to as the SINR coverage probability
[
19
]
, quantifies the extent to which users experience acceptable link quality over space or time
[
13
,
4
]
.
Stochastic geometry (SG) offers a mathematically rigorous framework to represent the spatial randomness inherent in cellular networks, where base station (BS) locations are often modeled as realizations of a Poisson point process (PPP)
[
11
,
14
,
8
]
. This approach facilitates the derivation of spatially averaged SINR probabilities over the geographical coverage region of the network. SG-based formulations have been instrumental in analyzing diverse scenarios such as heterogeneous multi-tier deployments
[
6
]
, energy-aware network designs
[
7
]
, and millimeter-wave systems with blockage effects
[
18
,
1
,
20
]
. Despite its analytical tractability, the resulting SINR coverage expressions often involve multi-dimensional integrals and special functions that limit interpretability and complicate their direct use in network optimization and design
[
13
,
6
]
. Furthermore, the dependence of these formulations on specific path-loss exponents restricts their general applicability and scalability across varying propagation environments.

Efforts have been made to modularize the coverage analysis for network optimization by re-defining coverage
[
7
]
or through probabilistic modeling
[
12
,
9
]
by approximating interference as a structured random variable with finite-order moments. While these approaches yield closed-form expressions in some scenarios and allow for tractable system-level reasoning, they require delicate moment calculations that vary with path-loss exponent, leading to tedious derivations and are prone to breakdown when moments do not admit tractable expressions. Other efforts include SG-inspired metrics such as the meta distribution of SINR
[
21
,
5
,
17
]
, which captures link-level variability but adds layers of analytical complexity.

In this letter, we propose a modular and numerically stable hybrid approximation framework for evaluating coverage probability in Poisson cellular networks. The central idea is to decompose the interference into two components: (i) a finite number of dominant interferers treated exactly, and (ii) a residual interference tail approximated using a Laplace functional over the PPP. This dominant-plus-tail approximation eliminates the need for symbolic derivations and works seamlessly across a wide range of path-loss exponents, SINR thresholds, and deployment densities. Although Qin et. al.
[
17
]
derived an approximate analytic expression for the distributional shape of link reliabilities under a dominant-plus-mean-field closure, it did not focus on uniform control of the approximation error for finite interference truncations. In contrast, we focus on the SINR coverage probability, i.e., the spatially averaged success probability, and introduce a hybrid numerical estimator that treats a finite number of nearest interferers exactly while approximating the far-field tail through a Laplace functional. In short, even though the dominant-interferer decomposition is a shared modeling device, this work has a different focus and establishes new theoretical guarantees/algorithmic machinery that are neither implied by nor derivable from the meta-distribution formulation.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:

•

We develop a path-loss-agnostic hybrid method that treats a few dominant interferers deterministically while modeling the residual far-field interference statistically through a Laplace functional.

•

We derive explicit error bounds that quantify the accuracy of the approximation and provide theoretical guarantees on convergence of this novel approximation to the infinite-interferer benchmark for commonly-used path-loss exponents.

•

Extensive numerical experiments confirm that the proposed method matches simulation and SG benchmarks while offering greater implementation simplicity.

II
System Model and Problem Formulation

We consider the forward link of a cellular network, in which BSs are spatially distributed according to a homogeneous PPP, denoted by
\Phi\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
with intensity
\lambda>0
(BS/km
2
). Without loss of generality, a typical user is assumed to be located at the origin, in accordance with Slivnyak’s theorem
[
15
]
, and connects to its nearest BS. To model the finite-domain nature of practical networks, we restrict attention to the
N
nearest BSs to the user.
Let
x_{i}\in\Phi
denote the location of the
i^{\text{th}}
nearest BS, and let
\|x_{i}\|
represent its distance from the origin. The BS distances are ordered such that
\|x_{1}\|<\|x_{2}\|<\cdots<\|x_{N}\|
. Define
r=\|x_{1}\|
as the distance to the serving BS and
R_{i}=\|x_{i}\|
as the distance to the
i^{\text{th}}
interfering BS for
i=2,\dots,N
. The wireless channels from each BS to the user experience independent Rayleigh fading, modeled by power gains
g_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
. The signal undergoes path-loss attenuation with exponent
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
, and the receiver is subject to additive white Gaussian noise with power
\sigma^{2}>0
. The received signal power from the serving BS is
g_{1}x_{1}^{-\eta}
, while the aggregate interference from the
N-1
nearest interferers is
\sum_{i=2}^{N}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}
. Therefore, the SINR at the typical user is expressed as follows:

\mathrm{SINR}=\frac{g_{1}r^{-\eta}}{\sum_{i=2}^{N}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}+\sigma^{2}}.

The SINR
coverage probability
, considering
N-1
interfering BSs, is defined as the probability that the SINR exceeds a specified threshold
T>0
, i.e.,

P_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T):=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{g_{1}r^{-\eta}}{\sum_{i=2}^{N}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}+\sigma^{2}}>T\right).

(1)

The serving distance
r
follows a Rayleigh distribution, as the distance
r^{2}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1/(\pi\lambda))
, owing to the nearest-neighbor property of the 2D PPP
[
10
]
. More generally, the squared distance to the
i^{\text{th}}
nearest BS satisfies
R_{i}^{2}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(i,1/(\pi\lambda))
for
i=2,\dots,N
. Evaluating (
1
) involves averaging over both spatial randomness (i.e., the PPP realization) and channel fading. For a general path-loss exponent
\eta
, the coverage expression does not admit a simple closed-form solution due to the complex interaction between distance distributions and interference statistics
[
3
,
4
]
.
To address this challenge, we propose a hybrid approximation framework. The core idea is to split the total interference into two components: (i) an exact summation over the
K-1
nearest interferers, and (ii) a residual tail comprising the remaining
N-K
BSs, which is approximated via a Laplace functional of the PPP. This decomposition enables a tractable, accurate, and path-loss-agnostic approximation of the SINR coverage probability, even for general network parameters.

III
Conditional Coverage Probability and Tail Approximation

Overall, our goal is to obtain a favorable compromise between full-scale simulation as involved in evaluating (
1
) by averaging and deriving detailed analytical formulae as in
[
9
]
, while still being modular and generally applicable over diverse parameter values. To compute the SINR coverage probability
P_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)
, we condition on the geometric realization
r,\left\{R_{i}\right\}_{i=2}^{N}
denoted by
\cdot\mid\Phi
. Then, from (
1
),

P_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)=\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\left\{g_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 2}\mid\Phi}\left[\mathbb{P}\left(g_{1}>Tr^{\eta}\left(I_{\mathrm{tot}}+\sigma^{2}\right)\right)\right]\right],

where

I_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_{i=2}^{K}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}+\sum_{i=K+1}^{N}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}.

Since
g_{1}~\sim~\operatorname{Exp}(1)
, we can further express the coverage probability as follows:
P_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\left\{g_{i}\right\}_{i=2}^{N}\mid\Phi}\left[e^{-Tr^{\eta}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{K}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}+\sum_{i=K+1}^{N}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}+\sigma^{2}\right)}\right]\right].

(2)

Here, we retain the exact
K-1
dominant terms and approximate the remaining contribution of
I_{\text{tail},N}=\sum_{i=K+1}^{N}g_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}
by computing the expectation over
\left\{g_{i}\right\}_{i=K+1}^{N}
conditioned on
\Phi
.
Conditioned on the geometry
r,\{R_{i}\}
, and using the independence of
g_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
, we obtain the following:

\mathbb{E}_{\{g_{i}\}_{i=K+1}^{N}\mid\Phi}\left[e^{-sI_{\text{tail,N}}}\right]=\hskip-7.22743pt\prod_{i=K+1}^{N}\mathbb{E}_{g_{i}}[e^{-sg_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}}]=\hskip-7.22743pt\prod_{i=K+1}^{N}\frac{1}{1+sR_{i}^{-\eta}},

where
s=Tr^{\eta}
.
Recall that, for a non-negative measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to[0,1]
, the probability generating functional (PGFL) of a homogeneous PPP
\Phi
of intensity
\lambda
satisfies (cf.
[
15
]
)

\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\left[\prod_{x\in\Phi}f(x)\right]=\exp\left(-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1-f(x))\,dx\right).

(3)

Denote the open Euclidean ball of radius
R
centered at the origin by
B(0,R)
and its closure by
\overline{B(0,R)}
. Applying (
3
) to the region
\tilde{\Phi}=\Phi\cap(B(0,R_{N})\setminus\overline{B(0,R_{K})})
with
f(x)=\frac{1}{1+s\|x\|^{-\eta}}
, and converting to polar coordinates, we obtain:

\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\left[\prod_{x\in\tilde{\Phi}}\frac{1}{1+s\|x\|^{-\eta}}\right]=\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{K}}^{R_{N}}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}t\,dt\right).

Combining the exact dominant terms with the approximated tail, we define the approximate SINR coverage as:

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{(N)}_{\mathrm{cov}}(T):=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\Bigg[e^{-s\sigma^{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{K}\mathbb{E}_{g_{i}\mid\Phi}[e^{-sg_{i}R_{i}^{-\eta}}]\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{K}}^{R_{N}}\frac{st^{-\eta}\cdot tdt}{1+st^{-\eta}}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\Phi}\Bigg[e^{-s\sigma^{2}}\prod_{i=2}^{K}\frac{1}{1+sR_{i}^{-\eta}}\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{K}}^{R_{N}}\frac{st^{-\eta}\cdot tdt}{1+st^{-\eta}}\right)\Bigg],

(4)

where, recalling
s=Tr^{\eta}
, the outer expectation is over
r,\{R_{i}\}_{i=2}^{K},R_{N}
, which is quite tractable when
K
is small.
This hybrid form maintains accuracy while enabling tractable approximation. Pseudo code implementation of this approach is provided in Algorithm 1.

IV
Analysis of Truncation Error

We present a theorem that bounds the approximation error from truncating interference at the
N
{}^{\text{th}}
interferer, when
\eta>2
, with the PGFL-based approximation for the last
N-K
interferers.
The infinite-user coverage probability benchmark from SG is defined as

P_{\mathrm{SG}}(T):=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{g_{1}r^{-\eta}}{\sum_{x\in\Phi\setminus\{x_{1}\}}g_{x}\|x\|^{-\eta}+\sigma^{2}}>T\right),

(5)

which represents the SINR coverage probability in an idealized model with infinitely many interferers and serves as the asymptotic reference for finite-user approximations.

Theorem 1

(Truncation Error Bound)
.

Let
\eta>2
, and define
s:=Tr^{\eta}>0
, where
r
is the distance to the serving BS. Then the error incurred by approximating the SG coverage probability using only the
N
nearest BSs satisfies

\left|\widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)-P_{\mathrm{SG}}(T)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}_{r,R_{N}}\left[\delta_{N}(s,R_{N})\right],

where the tail error term is given by

\delta_{N}(s,R_{N}):=1-\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{N}}^{\infty}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}t\,dt\right).

Proof.

We bound the difference between the SG benchmark and the dominant-plus-tail approximation by comparing their respective Laplace transforms of the interference.

Let the residual interference beyond radius
R_{K}
be

I_{\mathrm{tail}}:=\sum_{x\in\Phi\setminus\overline{B(0,R_{K})}}g_{x}\|x\|^{-\eta},

with
g_{x}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
i.i.d.

Conditioning on
\Phi
, and using the independence of the fading coefficients,

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(-sI_{\mathrm{tail}})\mid\Phi\right]=\prod_{x\in\Phi\setminus\overline{B(0,R_{K})}}\frac{1}{1+s\|x\|^{-\eta}}.

Taking expectation over
\Phi
using the PGFL of the PPP, we obtain

\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(s):=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp(-sI_{\mathrm{tail}})\right]=\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{K}}^{\infty}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}t\,dt\right).

The approximation used in
\widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)
includes interferers only up to radius
R_{N}
, and models the tail using

\mathcal{L}_{N}(s):=\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{K}}^{R_{N}}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}t\,dt\right).

(6)

The difference between the true and truncated tail Laplace terms is

\left|\mathcal{L}_{N}(s)-\mathcal{L}_{\infty}(s)\right|

=\mathcal{L}_{N}(s)\left(1-\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{N}}^{\infty}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}t\,dt\right)\right).

Define this residual error as

\delta_{N}(s,R_{N}):=1-\exp\left(-2\pi\lambda\int_{R_{N}}^{\infty}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}t\,dt\right).

The total error in coverage probability is bounded by the expected residual tail error, weighted by the remaining terms:

\left|\widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)-P_{\mathrm{SG}}(T)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-s\sigma^{2}}\cdot\prod_{i=2}^{K}\frac{1}{1+sR_{i}^{-\eta}}\cdot\delta_{N}(s,R_{N})\right].

Since
e^{-s\sigma^{2}}\cdot\prod_{i=2}^{K}\frac{1}{1+sR_{i}^{-\eta}}\leq 1
, we obtain

\left|\widetilde{P}_{\mathrm{cov}}^{(N)}(T)-P_{\mathrm{SG}}(T)\right|\leq\mathbb{E}_{r,R_{N}}\left[\delta_{N}(Tr^{\eta},R_{N})\right],

which completes the proof.
∎

IV-A

Expectation-Aware Error Bounds for Specific Path-Loss

We consider the error bound
\mathbb{E}_{r,R_{N}}\left[\delta_{N}(Tr^{\eta},R_{N})\right]
and specialize to the cases
\eta=3,4,
using upper bounds on
\delta_{N}
and Cauchy–Schwarz.

Note that
\int_{R_{N}}^{\infty}\frac{st^{-\eta}}{1+st^{-\eta}}tdt\leq s\int_{R_{N}}^{\infty}t^{1-\eta}dt=s\frac{R_{N}^{2-\eta}}{\eta-2}.

Using
1-e^{-z}\leq z
for
z\geq 0
,
\delta_{N}(s,R_{N})\leq\frac{2\pi\lambda s}{\eta-2}\frac{1}{R_{N}^{\eta-2}}.

1. Case
\eta=3
:
Now,

\delta_{N}(s,R_{N})\leq\frac{2\pi\lambda s}{R_{N}}=2\pi\lambda T\cdot\frac{r^{3}}{R_{N}}.

We apply Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to obtain the following:

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{r^{3}}{R_{N}}\right]\leq\left(\mathbb{E}[r^{6}]\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{R_{N}^{2}}\right]\right)^{1/2}=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2}).

Hence,

\mathbb{E}[\delta_{N}(Tr^{3},R_{N})]=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})\quad\text{for }\eta=3.

2. Case
\eta=4
:
Using a similar argument, it is easily seen that

\mathbb{E}[\delta_{N}(Tr^{4},R_{N})]=\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})\quad\text{for }\eta=4.

VI
Conclusion

The proposed hybrid framework offers a unified and computationally efficient approach for SINR coverage analysis in Poisson cellular networks. By decomposing interference into a deterministic set of dominant sources and a statistically modeled residual tail, the method achieves path-loss-agnostic accuracy without symbolic moment derivations. Analytical error bounds demonstrate controlled convergence across diverse network regimes, while numerical evaluations confirm strong agreement with stochastic geometry and simulation benchmarks. The approach effectively bridges the gap between purely analytical and empirical methods, providing a versatile foundation for network performance evaluation.

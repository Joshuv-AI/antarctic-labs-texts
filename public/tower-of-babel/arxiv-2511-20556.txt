. In recent years it has become well understood that replacing either Brownian or general martingale noise by more oscillatory processes can have an improved regularizing effect on equations with singular coefficients,
[
nualart_ouknine_02
,
nualart_ouknine_03
,

,

,
butkovsky_mytnik_24_weak
]
. This work asks the same question at the level of ergodic invariant measures; asking whether for a given singularity of the drift
g
(recall (
1.2
)) there exists a sufficiently oscillatory noise such that (
1.1
) possesses a unique ergodic invariant measure. The work
[
marie_2015_singular
]
gives an instructive
no-go
theorem in the one dimensional case. Considering the deterministic equation

(1.3)

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{t}=g(x_{t})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+f_{t},\quad x_{0}\in(0,+\infty),\quad t\in[0,T],

with
f:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
a
\gamma
-Hölder continuous path and
g:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
that is continuously
1+\lfloor\nicefrac{{1}}{{\gamma}}\rfloor
differentiable on
(0,+\infty)
and has bounded derivatives on every interval
[\varepsilon,+\infty)
for
\varepsilon>0
,
[
marie_2015_singular
, Prop. 2.2]
shows that (
1.3
) has a unique
(0,+\infty)
valued solution if

(1.4)

\lim_{a\searrow 0}\frac{1}{a^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{a}g(\iota a^{\gamma})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}a=+\infty\quad\text{for all }\,\iota>0.

The equivalent result on
(-\infty,0)
clearly holds by symmetry. As such, if (
1.4
) holds then an SDE of the form (
1.3
), with an additional confining potential and
f
replaced by a stochastic process with almost surely
\gamma
-Hölder continuous paths, cannot have a unique invariant measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Notably, if we specify to
g(x)=x|x|^{\alpha-1}
for
p<0
the condition (
1.4
) resolves to
\alpha<1-\frac{1}{\gamma}
. That is, for more oscillatory driver
f
, one requires the drift to be more singular in order to prevent the solution
tunneling
from
(0,+\infty)
to
(-\infty,0)
. In fact, if
t\mapsto f_{t}
is self-similar of order
\gamma
(either pathwise or in law) this condition is exactly the scaling super-criticality condition. One has

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\alpha>1-\frac{1}{\gamma},&\text{scaling sub-critical},\\
\alpha=1-\frac{1}{\gamma},&\text{scaling critical},\\
\alpha<1-\frac{1}{\gamma},&\text{scaling super-critical}.\end{cases}

In the scaling sub-critical regime the small scale behavior is dominated by the driver
f
, in the super-critical regime it is dominated by the drift and they are equally balanced in the critical regime. The heuristics are reversed for the large scale behaviors. See also
[
galeati_gerencser_22_subcritical
, Sec. 1.1]
for a discussion of the same ideas in the context of SDEs with drifts in a wide variety of function spaces. See also
[
butkovsky_le_mytnik_23_stochastic
, Thm. 2.6]
for a more general statement of non-uniqueness for a particular family of super-critical drifts.

The fractional Brownian motions (fBm)
W^{H}
on a given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
are a family of Gaussian processes, indexed by
H\in(0,+\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
which generalize the standard Brownian. When
H=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
,
W^{H}
is a standard Brownian motion, while for
H\neq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
the process
\{t\mapsto W^{H}_{t}\}
is neither a semi-martingale nor a Markov process. As a result one does not have access to PDE analytic methods such as the Feynman–Kac formula without introducing infinitely many degrees of freedom, see
[
VZ19
]
for example. However, the processes are self-similar in law at scale
H
, i.e. the family
\{t\mapsto c^{-H}W^{H}_{ct}\}_{c>0}
are all equal in law. In addition, due to Gaussianity and the Kolmogorov continuity criterion one has
\mathbb{P}\left(W^{H}\in\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)=1
for all
\gamma\in(0,H)
. Replacing
f
with
W^{H}
and considering a generic
g\in B^{\alpha}_{\infty,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
(for discussion of the Hölder–Besov spaces see Section
2.1
) then the criticality conditions (
1.5
) are exactly mirrored with
\gamma\mapsto H
, see
[
galeati_gerencser_22_subcritical
, Ex. 1.1]
. While (
1.5
) provides an indication of the regimes where one may expect well-posedness, at the time of writing sharp results at these boundaries are not available in full generality. Some special cases are treated for example in
[
sznitman_91_topics
, Ch. II]
,
[
bossy_talay_96
]
.

With these well-posedness results available, it is natural to ask for ergodic properties of diffusions under the same conditions. However, this introduces new challenges. As fBm is not a Markov process (as well as failing to be a semi-martingale) the random dynamical systems (RDS) framework,
[
arnold_98_rds
,
daprato_zabczyk_96_ergodicity
]
, is ill suited to obtaining uniqueness of ergodic invariant measures.

For existence results using this approach we refer to
[
maslowski_schmalfuss_04_random
,

.
Informally speaking, the deterministic perspective of an RDS approach, where sampled noise is fed into the update of a system forwards in time, does not reliably exclude the invariant measures which are not
measurable with respect to the past
(see
[
arnold_98_rds
, Sec. 1.4]
for this terminology). Instead, the works
[
Hai05
,
hairer_ohashi_07_ergodic
]
developed a more probabilistic approach based on the notion of a stochastic dynamics system (SDS). Here, the noise updates are carried along with the dynamics appropriate measurability of the invariant measures. We refer to the introductions of
[
hairer_ohashi_07_ergodic
]
for more discussion. The notion of an SDS was first introduced in
[
Hai05
]
where an additive noise equation, of the form (
1.1
) with locally Lipschitz and contractive drift was treated by a hands on coupling approach, obtaining existence and uniqueness of an ergodic invariant measure (understood in the appropriate sense) as well as algebraic ergodicity. As a follow up work,
[
hairer_ohashi_07_ergodic
]
treated a multiplicative noise equation and obtained a generalization of the Doob–K’hashminksii theorem for SDS,
[
hairer_ohashi_07_ergodic
, Thm. 3.10]
. This gives uniqueness of ergodic invariant measures for
quasi-Markovian systems
(see
[
hairer_ohashi_07_ergodic
, Def. 3.7]
) enjoying natural generalizations of the strong Feller and irreducibility properties. Our work takes this latter approach, building on the regularity of solutions obtained in
[
galeati_gerencser_22_subcritical
]
, see Theorem
1.1
for an informal statement of our setting and main result. Beyond these initial results,
[
li_panloup_seiber_23_non_stationary
]
obtained Gaussian upper and lower bounds on the densities associated to fractional diffusions, under appropriate conditions, along with a number of other results. While
[
panloup_R._20_sub_exponential
]
demonstrated sub-exponential convergence to the unique invariant measure for fractional diffusions with semi-contractive and locally Lipschitz drifts. This was upgraded to exponential convergence by
[
LS22
, Thm. 1.3]
in the case of globally Lipschitz semi-contractive drifts with small non-contractive perturbation. Meanwhile,
[
amine_coffie_harang_proske_20_bismut
]
obtained a Bistmut–Ellworthy–Li formula for SDE driven by additive fBm with bounded and measurable drifts. Our Section
extends this formula to the case of distributional drifts.

.

Let

Then the following statements hold:

(1)

If
\alpha>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2H}
there exists a weakly stationary solution to (

).

(2)

If

then there exists unique global strong solution to (

(3)

In either case, for any

\sup_{t\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\kappa\llbracket X\rrbracket_{\mathcal{C}^{\gamma}_{[t,t+1]}}^{2}\right)\right]\lesssim 1\quad\text{for all }\kappa<\kappa_{0}.

1.1.
Main Challenges, Ideas and Organization

The initial challenge in treating (
1.6
) when
g
is allowed to be a generalised function is to even define the right hand side. This is by now a well understood issue,
[
catellier_gubinelli_16
,
galeati_gubinelli_20_Noiseless
]
. The remedy is to notice that we are not required to evaluate
g
at any point in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
but along
\{t\mapsto X_{t}\}
, any putative solution to (

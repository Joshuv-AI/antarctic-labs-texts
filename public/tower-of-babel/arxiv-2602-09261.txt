Introduction

The discovery that Galois cohomology frequently exhibits Koszul-type homological behavior has reshaped our understanding of maximal pro-
p
Galois groups. The Bloch-Kato conjecture (now the Rost-Voevodsky theorem) establishes that for a field
F
containing
\mu_{p}
, the mod-
p
cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(G_{F},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a quadratic algebra—generated in degree
1
with relations in degree
2
—and relates it to Milnor
K
-theory via the norm-residue isomorphism
[
14
]
. Positselski conjectured that one can and should go further: under suitable hypotheses, the cohomology ring should be Koszul in the strong homological sense
[
9
]
. Recent work by Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli, and Tân has vindicated this expectation dramatically: for all currently understood finitely generated maximal pro-
p
quotients of absolute Galois groups, the mod-
p
Galois cohomology is not only Koszul but enjoys much stronger “enhanced” versions of Koszulity, such as the PBW property and universal Koszulity
[
6
,
5
]
. These enhanced properties hold for large classes of fields (local and global fields, Pythagorean fields of “elementary type”), and are preserved under natural constructions on pro-
p
groups such as taking quotients, closed subgroups, and free products
[
8
]
.

Despite this progress, very little is known about how enhanced Koszul properties behave under
composita
of fields. On the Galois side, composita correspond to amalgamated free products of maximal pro-
p
Galois groups over a common closed subgroup, and their cohomology is controlled by a Mayer-Vietoris sequence. On the algebra side, this leads naturally to a “fiber-product” construction: one glues together degree-
1
generators and quadratic relations from the factors over those from the intersection. While abstract versions of such constructions appear in the literature, the precise interaction with
universal
Koszulity in the Galois cohomology setting has not been systematically studied. The present paper addresses this gap.

Main results

We prove two main theorems. First, we establish an
abstract composita stability theorem
for universal Koszulity in Galois cohomology (Theorem
3.1
). Starting from a compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
with
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=k
, we assume that the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{K}
decomposes as a free pro-
p
product with amalgamation
G_{K}\cong G_{K_{1}}*_{G_{k}}G_{K_{2}}
, and that the associated cohomology rings
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{1}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{2}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, and
H^{\bullet}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})
are quadratic and generated in degree
1
. Under natural Mayer-Vietoris hypotheses—which give a fiber-product description of
H^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
and a pullback description of the kernel of the cup product in degree
2
—we show that the quadratic presentation of
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is obtained functorially by gluing the quadratic data from the three smaller cohomology rings. Assuming that universal Koszulity is stable under this quadratic gluing, we deduce that universal Koszulity for
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{1}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{2}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
implies universal Koszulity for
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. This may be viewed as a composita analogue of the stability results for universal Koszulity under free pro-
p
products established in
[
8
,
5
]
.

Second, we implement this abstract framework for certain Pythagorean fields (Theorem
4.1
). Recent work of Hamza, Maire, Mináč, and Tân
[
2
]
has shown that the maximal pro-
2
Galois groups of many Pythagorean fields admit explicit free pro-
2
product decompositions with Demuškin and free factors, and that their mod-
2
cohomology rings are PBW and universally Koszul. For such fields, the maximal pro-
2
Galois group of a suitable compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
can be described as an amalgamated free product
G_{K}(2)\cong G_{K_{1}}(2)*_{G_{k}(2)}G_{K_{2}}(2)
, and the associated Mayer-Vietoris sequence satisfies the hypotheses of our abstract theorem. We prove that for these Pythagorean fields, the mod-
2
Galois cohomology rings of admissible composita remain quadratic and universally Koszul. This furnishes large classes of “elementary-type” Pythagorean fields, built from local and global fields by adjoining square roots and forming composita, for which universal Koszulity persists throughout the construction.

Applications and context

As a byproduct, our stability theorems yield inverse Galois obstructions: on any of the field classes constructed this way, a finitely generated pro-
p
group whose Galois cohomology is not quadratic and universally Koszul cannot occur as the maximal pro-
p
Galois group (Corollary
3.8
). Enhanced Koszul properties thus become a robust tool for excluding candidate Galois groups in inverse Galois theory, complementing constraints from cohomological dimension, Massey products
[
4
,
1
]
, and other obstructions.

Our results fit naturally into Positselski’s philosophy that Koszulity of Galois cohomology is a robust feature tied to
K(\pi,1)
-ness and quasi-formality
[
9
]
: we show that strong, “enhanced” forms of Koszulity persist under composita corresponding to amalgamated pro-
p
free products. The classes of fields we construct are closely related to the “elementary type” fields arising in the work of Efrat, Mináč, and others on the structure of maximal pro-
p
Galois groups; our composita stability results support the expectation that universal Koszulity is a natural invariant on such elementary-type towers.

Organization

Section
2
recalls background on quadratic and universally Koszul algebras, Mayer-Vietoris sequences for profinite amalgams, and the Bloch-Kato theorem. Section
3
proves our abstract Composita Stability Theorem for universal Koszulity. Section
4
applies this result to Pythagorean fields, verifying the Mayer-Vietoris hypotheses and establishing composita stability for admissible Pythagorean extensions. Section
5
discusses applications to inverse Galois problems and indicates further questions about the interplay between universal Koszulity, PBW properties, and higher cohomological operations.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
p
denotes a fixed prime number. All tensor products, exterior powers, and vector spaces are taken over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
unless otherwise specified.

Fix a base field
k
and a separable closure
\overline{k}
of
k
. All fields
K
under consideration will be subfields of
\overline{k}
containing
k
and a primitive
p
-th root of unity
\mu_{p}
. For such a field
K
we write

G_{K}=G_{K}(p)

for the maximal pro-
p
quotient of its absolute Galois group
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{k}/K)
, and
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
for the continuous mod-
p
cohomology ring of
G_{K}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. When
p=2
, we often write
G_{K}(2)
in place of
G_{K}
to emphasize that we are working with the maximal pro-
2
quotient.

For subfields
K_{1},K_{2}\subset\overline{k}
containing
k
we write

K_{1}K_{2}\subset\overline{k}

for their compositum (the smallest subfield of
\overline{k}
containing both), and
K_{1}\cap K_{2}
for their intersection in
\overline{k}
.

2.1
Quadratic and Koszul algebras

A graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A^{n}
is called
quadratic
if it is generated as an algebra by
A^{1}
and all defining relations lie in degree
2
. More precisely,
A
is quadratic if there exists a finite-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
V=A^{1}
and a subspace
R\subseteq\bigwedge^{2}V
such that

A\;\cong\;T(V)/\langle R\rangle,

where
T(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes n}
is the tensor algebra on
V
and
\langle R\rangle
is the two-sided ideal generated by
R
. The quadratic data
(V,R)
uniquely determines
A
, and we say that
A
has
presentation

(V,R)
or is
presented by generators
V
and relations
R
.

A quadratic algebra
A
is called
Koszul
if the ground field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, viewed as the trivial
A
-module concentrated in degree
0
, admits a linear resolution: that is, a graded free resolution

\cdots\to F_{2}\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}\to 0

in which each
F_{i}
is a free graded
A
-module generated in degree
i
. Koszul algebras enjoy many favorable homological properties; for instance, higher syzygies are controlled by the quadratic dual algebra, and Hilbert series factor in a particularly nice way. We refer to
[
9
,
6
]
for background on Koszul algebras and their role in Galois cohomology.

A quadratic algebra
A
is called
universally Koszul
if
every
cyclic quotient
A/(a)
by a homogeneous element
a\in A^{1}
of degree
1
has a linear resolution as an
A
-module. This is a substantial strengthening of the Koszul property: while the Koszul condition concerns only the trivial module
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, universal Koszulity requires that
all
degree-
1
quotients behave homologically as well as
\mathbb{F}_{p}
does. Universal Koszulity was introduced and systematically studied by Palaisti in
[
8
]
, who showed that it captures refined homological regularity in Galois cohomology. The terminology reflects the fact that universal Koszulity is preserved under a wide range of algebraic and group-theoretic constructions (base change, quotients, free products, etc.), making it a robust invariant for studying maximal pro-
p
Galois groups.

An even stronger property is the
Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt (PBW) property
: a quadratic algebra
A=T(V)/\langle R\rangle
is said to be PBW if there exists an ordered basis of
V
such that the set of standard monomials (those not divisible by any leading term in a Gröbner basis for
R
) forms a vector-space basis for
A
. The PBW property implies Koszulity and is closely related to having a non-commutative Gröbner basis with particularly good confluence properties. Mináč, Pasini, Quadrelli, and Tân proved in
[
6
]
that, under the conjectural description of maximal pro-
p
Galois groups of fields, the mod-
p
Galois cohomology ring is PBW, and gave unconditional PBW results for various field classes (local fields, Pythagorean fields of “elementary type”, etc.). The PBW property, in turn, implies universal Koszulity, so these results establish a hierarchy:

\text{PBW}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{universally Koszul}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{Koszul}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{quadratic}.

In this paper, we work primarily with the universal Koszulity level of this hierarchy, which is strong enough to give robust stability results but flexible enough to apply to a wide range of field-theoretic constructions.

2.2
Profinite amalgams and Mayer-Vietoris sequences

Let
G_{1},G_{2},G_{0}
be profinite groups, and suppose
G_{0}
embeds as a closed subgroup of both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. The
free (profinite) product with amalgamation

G\;=\;G_{1}*_{G_{0}}G_{2}

is the profinite completion of the abstract amalgamated free product of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
over
G_{0}
. In the language of profinite Bass-Serre theory (see
[
13
,
12
]
), such an amalgam corresponds to the fundamental group of a profinite graph of groups with two vertices (corresponding to
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
) connected by an edge (corresponding to the common subgroup
G_{0}
). The cohomology of such an amalgam is controlled by a Mayer-Vietoris-type long exact sequence.

More precisely, let
M
be a discrete
G
-module (equivalently, a continuous
G
-module with the discrete topology on
M
). Then there exists a long exact sequence in continuous cohomology

\cdots\to H^{n}(G,M)\to H^{n}(G_{1},M)\oplus H^{n}(G_{2},M)\xrightarrow{\rho_{n}}H^{n}(G_{0},M)\xrightarrow{\delta_{n}}H^{n+1}(G,M)\to\cdots,

where the map
\rho_{n}
is given by the difference of the restriction maps,

\rho_{n}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\;=\;\mathrm{res}_{1,0}(\alpha_{1})-\mathrm{res}_{2,0}(\alpha_{2}),

and
\delta_{n}
is the connecting homomorphism. This sequence is the profinite analogue of the classical Mayer-Vietoris sequence for covering spaces, adapted to the setting of profinite groups acting on profinite trees. We refer to Lorensen
[
3
]
for a systematic treatment of Mayer-Vietoris sequences in profinite group cohomology and to
[
12
]
for the general cohomology theory of profinite graphs of groups.

For the applications in this paper, we will primarily be concerned with the case
M=\mathbb{F}_{p}
(the trivial
G
-module). In this case, the sequence simplifies considerably in low degrees. In particular, if the profinite graph of groups is connected (which is automatic for an amalgam
G_{1}*_{G_{0}}G_{2}
) and the coefficients are trivial, then the connecting homomorphism
\delta_{0}:H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
vanishes. This implies that the sequence

0\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})\xrightarrow{\rho_{1}}H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})

is exact. Consequently,
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
embeds into the direct sum
H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})
with image equal to the kernel of
\rho_{1}
, i.e., the set of pairs
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
such that
\mathrm{res}_{1,0}(\alpha_{1})=\mathrm{res}_{2,0}(\alpha_{2})
in
H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})
. This is precisely the
fiber product

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\;\cong\;H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\times_{H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{p})}H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

This fiber-product description of
H^{1}
will play a central role in our composita stability theorems.

We begin by isolating a basic cohomological consequence of a pro-
2
amalgam that will be used to control the degree-
1
part of the cohomology rings in our Pythagorean setting.

We lastly present the following useful result.

Lemma 2.1

(Degree-1 Mayer–Vietoris for an amalgam)
.

Let
G_{1},G_{2},G_{0}
be profinite (in particular, pro-
2
) groups, and let

G\;\cong\;G_{1}\,{\ast}_{G_{0}}\,G_{2}

be their free profinite (pro-
2
) product with amalgamation. Assume:

(i)

For the trivial
G
-module
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, there is a Mayer–Vietoris long exact
sequence in continuous cohomology

\cdots\to H^{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{n}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{n}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{n}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{n+1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\cdots

which is exact in degrees
0
and
1
.

(ii)

The boundary map

\delta\colon H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})

in this sequence is zero.

Then the restriction map

\mathrm{res}\colon H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})

is injective, with image equal to the kernel of

\rho\colon H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}),

where
\rho(x_{1},x_{2})=\mathrm{res}_{1,0}(x_{1})-\mathrm{res}_{2,0}(x_{2})
. Equivalently,

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\;\cong\;H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\times_{H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})}H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}),

i.e.
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a fiber product.

Proof.

By hypothesis, we have an exact segment of the Mayer–Vietoris sequence in
degrees
0
and
1
:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow H^{0}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{0}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})\xrightarrow{\;\delta\;}

\displaystyle H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{res}\;}H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\xrightarrow{\;\rho\;}H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Here
\rho
is the difference of the restriction maps:

\rho(x_{1},x_{2})=\mathrm{res}_{1,0}(x_{1})-\mathrm{res}_{2,0}(x_{2}).

Assume that all groups act trivially on
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and that each of

G,G_{1},G_{2},G_{0}
is nontrivial. Then by the standard description of
H^{0}
,

H^{0}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong H^{0}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong H^{0}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}.

Under these identifications, the map

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{0}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})

becomes

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}\xrightarrow{\;\Delta\;}\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{2}\xrightarrow{\;(x,y)\mapsto x+y\;}\mathbb{F}_{2},

where
\Delta(a)=(a,a)
is the diagonal embedding. This short exact sequence
is exact on the right, i.e. the map
\mathbb{F}_{2}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{2}\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
is surjective
with kernel equal to the image of
\Delta
.

Returning to the Mayer–Vietoris segment above, exactness at

H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
says that

\operatorname{im}\bigl(H^{0}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{0}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})\bigr)\;=\;\ker(\delta).

But we have just seen that the map

H^{0}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{0}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
is surjective, so its
image is all of
H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
. Hence
\ker(\delta)=H^{0}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
, which
forces

\delta\;=\;0.

Now exactness at
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
says that

\ker(\mathrm{res})\;=\;\operatorname{im}(\delta).

Since
\delta=0
, this gives
\ker(\mathrm{res})=0
, so

\mathrm{res}\colon H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})

is injective.

Finally, exactness at
H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})
tells us that

\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{res})\;=\;\ker(\rho)\;=\;\bigl\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\,\big|\,\mathrm{res}_{1,0}(x_{1})=\mathrm{res}_{2,0}(x_{2})\text{ in }H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})\bigr\}.

By definition, this kernel is precisely the fiber product

H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\times_{H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})}H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Since
\mathrm{res}
is injective and has image equal to this fiber product,
we obtain a canonical isomorphism

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\;\cong\;H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\times_{H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})}H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}),

as claimed.
∎

2.3
Galois cohomology and the Bloch-Kato conjecture

For a field
K
containing
\mu_{p}
and its maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{K}
, the mod-
p
cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is closely tied to the arithmetic of
K
via Kummer theory. In particular,
H^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong K^{\times}/(K^{\times})^{p}
by the Kummer sequence, and cup products in cohomology correspond to taking symbols in Milnor
K
-theory. The celebrated Bloch-Kato conjecture, proved by Rost, Voevodsky, and others (see
[
14
]
for an overview), establishes that the natural map

K_{n}^{M}(K)/p\;\longrightarrow\;H^{n}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})

from Milnor
K
-theory modulo
p
to Galois cohomology is an isomorphism for all
n\geq 0
. A key consequence of this theorem is that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a
quadratic
algebra: it is generated in degree
1
by
H^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong K^{\times}/(K^{\times})^{p}
, and all relations arise in degree
2
from the vanishing of Steinberg symbols
\{a,a\}=0
in Milnor
K
-theory.

More concretely, the kernel of the cup product

\cup:\bigwedge^{2}H^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow H^{2}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})

is spanned by elements of the form
a\wedge a
for
a\in H^{1}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
(corresponding to the Steinberg relation), together with elements of the form
a\wedge b
whenever
\{a,b\}=0
in
K_{2}^{M}(K)/p
. The quadraticity of
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
thus reflects the quadratic nature of the defining relations in Milnor
K
-theory, and any additional structure on
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
(such as Koszulity or universal Koszulity) provides refined information about the interplay between Galois theory and
K
-theory.

In the context of this paper, we will assume that the fields under consideration satisfy the conclusion of the Bloch-Kato conjecture (which is now a theorem), so that their mod-
p
Galois cohomology rings are quadratic. Our focus will be on the
enhanced
homological properties of these rings—specifically, universal Koszulity—and on how such properties behave under field-theoretic operations such as forming composita.

2.4
Pythagorean fields and Demuškin groups

A field
K
is called
Pythagorean
if every sum of two squares in
K
is itself a square. Equivalently,
K
is Pythagorean if and only if the equation
a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}
has a solution in
K
whenever
a,b\in K
. A formally real Pythagorean field (i.e., a Pythagorean field in which
-1
is not a sum of squares) is called an
RPF field
if, in addition, the square-class group
K^{\times}/(K^{\times})^{2}
is finite. Such fields arise naturally in Galois theory and have been extensively studied in recent work on the structure of maximal pro-
2
Galois groups; see, for example,
[
2
,
6
]
.

A key feature of RPF fields is that their maximal pro-
2
Galois groups often admit explicit decompositions as free pro-
2
products of simpler groups. In particular, many RPF fields have maximal pro-
2
Galois groups of the form

G_{K}(2)\;\cong\;H_{1}*\cdots*H_{r}*F,

where each
H_{i}
is a
Demuškin group
and
F
is a finitely generated free pro-
2
group.A Demushkin group is a pro-
p
group
G
with
\mathrm{cd}(G)=2
,

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=n<\infty
and

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}H^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=1
, such that the cup product

\cup\colon H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\times H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow H^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}

is a non-degenerate bilinear form (cf. Palaisti
[
8
]
, Def. 2.3,
and Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg
[
7
]
, (3.9.9)). Equivalently,

H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is the quadratic algebra

\{V,R\}=T(V)/(R)
, where
V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
and

R=\ker(\cup)\subset V^{\otimes 2}
has codimension
1

(cf. Palaisti
[
8
]
, Def. 3.1.1). For odd
p
, graded
commutativity forces
R
to contain all symmetric tensors—in particular

x_{i}\otimes x_{i}
for every
i
—so the algebra is a quotient of the
exterior algebra
\bigwedge(V)
by a single relation
r\in\bigwedge^{2}V
;
for
p=2
the algebra is a quotient of
\mathbb{F}_{2}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
by a
single quadratic relation together with all monomials of degree
\geq 3
.
In either case one has
H^{k}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=0
for all
k\geq 3
because

\mathrm{cd}(G)=2
.

The cohomology rings of Demuškin groups and of finitely generated free pro-
p
groups are known to be Koszul and, in fact, universally Koszul (see
[
8
,
5
]
). Moreover, universal Koszulity is preserved under taking free pro-
p
products, so that the cohomology ring of a free product
H_{1}*\cdots*H_{r}*F
as above is also universally Koszul. This makes RPF fields (and their extensions) a natural testing ground for stability results on enhanced Koszul properties, as we will explore in Section 4.

3
Abstract composita stability in Galois cohomology

The first main result of this section is a general stability theorem for universally Koszul Galois cohomology under suitable composita.

Theorem 3.1

(Composita Stability)
.

Let
K_{1},K_{2}
be subfields of
\overline{k}
containing
k
and a primitive
p
-th root of unity
\mu_{p}
, and set

k=K_{1}\cap K_{2},\qquad K=K_{1}K_{2}\subset\overline{k}.

We regard
K
as the compositum of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
over their intersection
k
. Write

G_{1}=G_{K_{1}},\quad G_{2}=G_{K_{2}},\quad G_{k}=G_{k},\quad G=G_{K}

for their maximal pro-
p
Galois groups, and set

A_{1}=H^{\bullet}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p}),\quad A_{2}=H^{\bullet}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p}),\quad C=H^{\bullet}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p}),\quad A=H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Write
V_{*}=H^{1}(G_{*},\mathbb{F}_{p})
for
*\in\{1,2,k\}
and
V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

Assume:

(i)

G
is the free pro-
p
product with amalgamation

G\;\cong\;G_{1}*_{G_{k}}G_{2},

over the common closed subgroup
G_{k}
, in the sense of profinite Bass–Serre theory.

(ii)

The graded
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras
A_{1},A_{2},C,A
are quadratic and generated in degree
1
, and the restriction maps

\mathrm{res}_{i}\colon H^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{1}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p}),\qquad i=1,2,

are surjective.

(iii)

The kernel of the cup product

\cup_{G}\colon V^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow H^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})

identifies with the pullback of the kernels of

\cup_{G_{i}}\colon V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\to H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{p}),\qquad i=1,2,

over
\ker(\cup_{G_{k}})\subset V_{k}^{\otimes 2}
under the maps induced by restriction

V^{\otimes 2}\to V_{i}^{\otimes 2},\quad V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\to V_{k}^{\otimes 2}.

Equivalently, the quadratic relations in
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
are precisely those pairs of quadratic relations in
H^{\bullet}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
H^{\bullet}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})
that agree after restriction to
H^{\bullet}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

(iv)

A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are universally Koszul quadratic algebras, and universal Koszulity is stable under passing from the quadratic data

V_{i},\ \ker(\cup_{G_{i}})\subset V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\quad(i=1,2),\qquad V_{k},\ \ker(\cup_{G_{k}})\subset V_{k}^{\otimes 2}

to the quadratic algebra
A
built from
V
and
\ker(\cup_{G})\subset V^{\otimes 2}
via the constructions in (ii) and (iii). Informally, we require that universal Koszulity is preserved under this gluing of generators and quadratic relations along the common base.

Then
A=H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a universally Koszul quadratic algebra. In particular,
K
has universally Koszul pro-
p
Galois cohomology.

Remark 3.2
.

The cup product
\cup\colon V^{\otimes 2}\to H^{2}
is graded-commutative: for
\alpha,\beta\in H^{1}
, one has
\alpha\cup\beta=(-1)^{1\cdot 1}\,\beta\cup\alpha
. For odd
p
this gives
\alpha\cup\beta=-\beta\cup\alpha
, so the cup product kills all symmetric tensors and factors through
\bigwedge^{2}V
; in this case, condition (iii) may equivalently be stated with
\bigwedge^{2}V_{*}
in place of
V_{*}^{\otimes 2}
. For
p=2
, one instead has
\alpha\cup\beta=\beta\cup\alpha
, so the cup product factors through
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\,V
and
does not
in general factor through
\bigwedge^{2}V
; in particular,
\alpha\cup\alpha
need not vanish when
\sqrt{-1}\notin K
. The formulation of condition (iii) using
V_{*}^{\otimes 2}
is correct uniformly for all primes
p
.

Remark 3.3
.

We stress that condition (i) is a substantial group-theoretic hypothesis.
In general, given subfields
K_{1},K_{2}\subset\overline{k}
with
k=K_{1}\cap K_{2}
and

K=K_{1}K_{2}
, the pro-
p
Galois groups
G_{K_{i}}
and
G_{k}
sit in exact sequences
with
G_{K}
appearing as a
quotient
rather than as an amalgam
(cf.
[
7
, Ch. I]
). There is no reason in general for
G_{K}
to decompose
as a free pro-
p
product with amalgamation over
G_{k}
;
G\cong G_{1}*_{G_{k}}G_{2}

does
not
follow formally from the field tower, but only holds in special
arithmetic situations. Our abstract theorem is tailored to precisely those cases.
In our applications (Sections
4
and
5
),
this hypothesis is verified by independent group-theoretic results (Bass–Serre
theory for pro-
p
groups,
\Delta
-RAAG decompositions, etc.), see in particular

[
2
]
.

Proof.

By assumption (i), the Mayer-Vietoris sequence for the amalgamated free product
G\cong G_{1}*_{G_{k}}G_{2}
yields an exact sequence

0\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}_{1}-\mathrm{res}_{2}}H^{1}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

From this sequence, we immediately obtain that the natural map

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})

is injective with image equal to

\ker\big(H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{1}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})\big),

where the map to
H^{1}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is given by
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\mapsto\mathrm{res}_{1}(\alpha_{1})-\mathrm{res}_{2}(\alpha_{2})
.

By the surjectivity of the restriction maps
\mathrm{res}_{i}
(assumption (ii)), this yields a canonical isomorphism

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\;\cong\;H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{p})\times_{H^{1}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})}H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{p}).

In other words, the degree-1 cohomology of
G
is obtained as the fiber product of the degree-1 cohomology of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
over that of
G_{k}
.

We now proceed to unpack how the quadratic presentations of the cohomology rings behave under the amalgam. By assumption (ii), the graded algebras
A,A_{1},A_{2},C
are quadratic and generated in degree
1
. In particular, each is determined by its degree-
1
part together with the kernel of the cup product

\cup\colon V_{\ast}^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow H^{2}(\,\cdot\,,\mathbb{F}_{p}).

More concretely, if we write
V_{\ast}=H^{1}(G_{\ast},\mathbb{F}_{p})
and
R_{\ast}=\ker(\cup_{G_{\ast}})\subset V_{\ast}^{\otimes 2}
for
\ast\in\{1,2,k\}
and
V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
R=\ker(\cup_{G})\subset V^{\otimes 2}
, then, equivalently in terms of generators and relations,

A_{\ast}\cong T(V_{\ast})/\langle R_{\ast}\rangle,\qquad A\cong T(V)/\langle R\rangle,

where
T(-)
denotes the tensor algebra and
\langle R_{\ast}\rangle
the ideal generated by
R_{\ast}
.

By what we established above from the Mayer-Vietoris sequence,

V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\;{\cong}\;V_{1}\times_{V_{k}}V_{2}

as an abstract fiber product. Thus the degree-
1
cohomology of
G
is obtained by forming the fiber product of the corresponding degree-
1
pieces of the factors. In particular, the degree-
1
piece
A^{1}
can be identified with this fiber product of
A_{1}^{1}
and
A_{2}^{1}
over
C^{1}
:

A^{1}\;{\cong}\;A_{1}^{1}\times_{C^{1}}A_{2}^{1}.

We now turn to the behavior in degree
2
. Assumption (iii) gives the corresponding statement on quadratic relations: the kernel
R=\ker(\cup_{G})\subset V^{\otimes 2}
identifies with the pullback of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
over
R_{k}
under the maps induced by restriction

V^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow V_{k}^{\otimes 2},\qquad i=1,2.

In other words, every quadratic relation in
A
arises from compatible relations in
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
, and conversely any such compatible pair gives a relation in
A
. Equivalently, the set of quadratic relations of
A
is obtained by taking pairs of relations in
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
that are compatible after restricting to
C
.

Summarizing, the quadratic datum
(V,R)
defining
A
is functorially constructed from the data
(V_{i},R_{i})
and
(V_{k},R_{k})
by the same fiber-product procedure, in degree
1
(for generators) and in degree
2
(for relations). Thus the quadratic presentation of
A
is obtained by “gluing together” the quadratic presentations of
A_{1},A_{2},C
along their common part via this fiber-product construction on generators and relations.

At this point, everything has been reduced to a purely quadratic-algebraic statement about the stability of universal Koszulity under this gluing construction. By (iv),
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are universally Koszul, and universal Koszulity is assumed to be stable under exactly this passage from

\big(V_{1},R_{1}\big),\ \big(V_{2},R_{2}\big),\ \big(V_{k},R_{k}\big)

to the glued quadratic algebra
A
defined by
(V,R)
. Therefore the resulting algebra
A=H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is also universally Koszul.

Since
A
is quadratic and universally Koszul, this shows that the mod-
p
cohomology ring of
G
has the desired property. In particular, the compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
has universally Koszul pro-
p
Galois cohomology, and this is precisely the stability phenomenon we sought to establish.
∎

Remark 3.4

(Verification of assumptions in the Galois setting)
.

The hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
may appear technical, but in the intended Galois-theoretic applications they are natural consequences of the underlying field-theoretic structure.

Assumption (ii) (the fiber-product description of
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
) follows automatically from the Mayer-Vietoris sequence for the amalgam
G\cong G_{1}*_{G_{k}}G_{2}
together with surjectivity of the restriction maps, as demonstrated in the proof above. The vanishing of the connecting homomorphism
H^{0}(G_{k},\mathbb{F}_{p})\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
for a connected graph of profinite groups with trivial coefficients ensures exactness of the relevant portion of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence; see
[
3
]
for the general theory.

Assumption (iii) (the pullback description of
\ker(\cup_{G})\subset V^{\otimes 2}
) is a natural compatibility condition between the Mayer–Vietoris sequence and the ring structure on cohomology. Note that the cup product
\cup\colon V_{\ast}^{\otimes 2}\to H^{2}(G_{\ast},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is a well-defined bilinear map for every prime
p
; for odd
p
, graded commutativity forces
\ker(\cup)
to contain all symmetric tensors, so one may equivalently work inside
\bigwedge^{2}V_{\ast}
, while for
p=2
the cup product is symmetric and factors through
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V_{\ast}
(see Remark
3.2
). In either case, the pullback condition is satisfied whenever the cup product is functorial with respect to the restriction maps in the amalgam, which holds for all maximal pro-
p
Galois groups arising from field-theoretic composita via the profinite Bass-Serre theory. The compatibility in degree
2
furnished by the Mayer-Vietoris sequence, combined with functoriality of the cup product in profinite group cohomology, yields precisely the required pullback structure on
V^{\otimes 2}
; see
[
3
,
12
]
.

Thus assumptions (ii) and (iii) are cohomological consequences of the amalgam structure in assumption (i), and can be viewed as automatic in the Galois setting. The substantive hypothesis is assumption (iv): the stability of universal Koszulity under the quadratic gluing construction.

Remark 3.5

(Universal Koszulity and Palaisti’s thesis)
.

The notion of universal Koszulity and its role as a refined homological regularity condition in Galois cohomology were systematically developed by Palaisti in
[
8
]
. There, universal Koszulity is introduced and analyzed as a substantial strengthening of classical Koszulity: while the Koszul condition concerns only the trivial module
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, universal Koszulity requires that
all
degree-
1
quotients
A/(a)
have linear resolutions. Palaisti showed that this enhanced property is preserved under a wide range of algebraic and group-theoretic constructions, including taking closed subgroups, forming free pro-
p
products, and passing to certain quotients and extensions. Building on this foundation, Mináč, Palaisti, Pasini, and Tân proved in
[
5
]
that a wide range of Galois cohomology rings are universally Koszul and often PBW, and that these properties reflect deep arithmetic and group-theoretic features of the corresponding maximal pro-
p
Galois groups.

However, most of the stability results obtained in
[
8
,
5
]
concern constructions on a
single
pro-
p
group (such as quotients, subgroups, or free products), rather than field-theoretic operations such as forming composita. Theorem
3.1
extends Palaisti’s stability programme to the composita setting: we show that under suitable Mayer-Vietoris hypotheses, universal Koszulity is preserved when passing from fields
K_{1},K_{2}
to their compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
. The key new input is assumption (iv), which requires that universal Koszulity be stable under the specific quadratic gluing construction on
H^{1}
and
\ker(\cup)
arising from the Mayer-Vietoris sequence. In practice, this assumption is satisfied for the field classes considered in
[
8
,
5
,
2
]
, and our theorem yields new classes of fields with universally Koszul Galois cohomology built from base fields by forming admissible composita.

We now record some formal consequences of Theorem
3.1
for the class of fields with universally Koszul pro-
p
Galois cohomology.

Corollary 3.6

(Closure under admissible composita)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{p}
be the class of subfields
K\subset\overline{k}
containing
k
and
\mu_{p}
such that

H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})

is a quadratic, universally Koszul algebra. (Thus
\mathcal{C}_{p}
collects precisely those fields for which the associated maximal pro-
p
Galois group has cohomology with the strongest quadratic regularity we consider.)

Suppose
K_{1},K_{2}
are fields in
\mathcal{C}_{p}
with
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=k
, and let
K=K_{1}K_{2}
. Assume that the associated maximal pro-
p
Galois groups and cohomology rings satisfy conditions (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
. Then
K\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
as well. In other words,
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is closed under forming such admissible composita.

Proof.

This is now a direct application of Theorem
3.1
. By assumption,
A_{i}=H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul, and the data
(G_{1},G_{2},G_{k},G)
,
(A_{1},A_{2},C,A)
satisfy (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
. The conclusion of the theorem then yields that
A=H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul, i.e.
K\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
.
∎

Iterating this closure property along a tower of admissible composita gives the following.

Corollary 3.7

(Finite admissible towers)
.

Let
K^{(0)},K^{(1)},\dots,K^{(n)}
be a finite tower of fields of containing
\mu_{p}
such that:

1.

K^{(0)}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
;

2.

for each
j=0,\dots,n-1
there exist fields
K^{(j)}_{1},K^{(j)}_{2}
and
k^{(j)}=K^{(j)}_{1}\cap K^{(j)}_{2}
with

K^{(j)}=K^{(j)}_{1},\qquad K^{(j+1)}=K^{(j)}_{1}K^{(j)}_{2},

and the quadruple
(K^{(j)}_{1},K^{(j)}_{2},k^{(j)},K^{(j+1)})
satisfies the hypotheses (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
, with
K^{(j)}_{1},K^{(j)}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
.

Thus each step in the tower is obtained by an admissible compositum of fields already lying in
\mathcal{C}_{p}
. Then every field
K^{(j)}
in the tower belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{p}
, i.e. all have quadratic, universally Koszul pro-
p
Galois cohomology.

Proof.

By assumption,
K^{(0)}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
. Suppose
K^{(j)}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
and
K^{(j)}_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
. Then the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
are satisfied for the compositum
K^{(j+1)}=K^{(j)}_{1}K^{(j)}_{2}
, so
K^{(j+1)}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
by Corollary
3.6
. Induction on
j
yields the claim and shows that the property propagates along the entire tower.
∎

As a final consequence, we obtain a cohomological obstruction for realizing certain pro-
p
groups as maximal pro-
p
Galois groups over fields built from
\mathcal{C}_{p}
by admissible composita.

Corollary 3.8

(Inverse Galois obstruction on
\mathcal{C}_{p}
)
.

Let
K
be a field obtained from some base field
K^{(0)}\in\mathcal{C}_{p}
by a finite sequence of steps as in Corollary
3.7
, so that all intermediate composita satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
. Let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group. If
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is not quadratic and universally Koszul, then
G
cannot be isomorphic to the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{L}
of any field
L
obtained in this way, in particular
G\not\cong G_{K}
. Thus non-Koszulity of
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
provides a cohomological obstruction to realizing
G
over this class of fields.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.7
, every such field
L
has quadratic, universally Koszul cohomology. Thus any pro-
p
group
G
with non-quadratic or non-Koszul mod-
p
cohomology cannot be realized as
G_{L}
for such an
L
, and in particular not as
G_{K}
.
∎

4
Pythagorean fields and pro-
2
Galois groups

The next subsection applies this lemma, together with the general abstract composita stability principle, to the concrete case of Pythagorean fields and their maximal pro-
2
Galois groups.

4.1
Composita stability and applications

In this section we prove a composita stability result for universal Koszulity in a concrete Galois setting, namely for certain Pythagorean fields whose maximal pro-
2
Galois groups split as free pro-
2
products over a common base. The result can be viewed as a specialization of the abstract theorem to the pro-
2
Pythagorean context.

Theorem 4.1

(Composita stability for Pythagorean fields)
.

Let
k
be a Pythagorean field containing
\mu_{2}
. Assume:

(i)

The maximal pro-
2
Galois group
G_{k}=G_{k}(2)
admits a decomposition as a finite free pro-
2
product

G_{k}\;\cong\;H_{1}*\cdots*H_{r}*F,

where each
H_{j}
is a Demuškin pro-
2
group and
F
is a finitely generated free pro-
2
group.

(ii)

There exist Pythagorean fields
K_{1},K_{2}\subset\overline{k}
with
k=K_{1}\cap K_{2}
and compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
, such that for
i=1,2
we have

G_{K_{i}}(2)\;\cong\;G_{k}(2)*F_{i}

with
F_{i}
finitely generated free pro-
2
groups, and

G_{K}(2)\;\cong\;G_{K_{1}}(2)*_{G_{k}(2)}G_{K_{2}}(2)

as pro-
2
groups.

(iii)

Writing

G_{0}=G_{k}(2),\quad G_{1}=G_{K_{1}}(2),\quad G_{2}=G_{K_{2}}(2),\quad G=G_{K}(2),

set

C=H^{\bullet}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}),\quad A_{i}=H^{\bullet}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})\ (i=1,2),\quad A=H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Assume that
C,A_{1},A_{2},A
are quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-algebras generated in degree
1
, and that the restriction maps on
H^{1}
,

\mathrm{res}_{i,0}\colon H^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}),\qquad i=1,2,

are surjective.

(iv)

Writing
V_{0}=H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
V_{i}=H^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
, the kernel of the cup product

\cup_{G}\colon V^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow H^{2}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})

identifies with the pullback of the kernels of

\cup_{G_{i}}\colon V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\to H^{2}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2}),\qquad i=1,2,

over
\ker(\cup_{G_{0}})\subset V_{0}^{\otimes 2}
under the maps induced by restriction

V^{\otimes 2}\to V_{i}^{\otimes 2},\quad V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\to V_{0}^{\otimes 2}.

(v)

The cohomology rings
C=H^{\bullet}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
A_{i}=H^{\bullet}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
i=1,2
, are universally Koszul quadratic algebras, and universal Koszulity is stable under forming the quadratic algebra
A
from its degree-
1
part and the kernel of the cup product as in (iii) and (iv).

Then the mod-
2
cohomology ring
A=H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a quadratic, universally Koszul algebra.

Condition (ii) is a genuine structural input: it asserts that in this
Pythagorean setting, the maximal pro-2 Galois group of the compositum admits an
amalgamated free pro-2 product decomposition. This is known to hold for the
fields in question by the results of Hamza–Maire–Mináč–Tân on
\Delta
-RAAG
structures
[
2
]
, but it is not a formal consequence of the
field diagram alone.

Proof.

Set
G_{0}=G_{k}(2)
,
G_{1}=G_{K_{1}}(2)
,
G_{2}=G_{K_{2}}(2)
and
G=G_{K}(2)
, and write

C=H^{\bullet}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}),\quad A_{i}=H^{\bullet}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})\ (i=1,2),\quad A=H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2}).

We denote
V_{0}=H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
V_{i}=H^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
, and

R_{0}=\ker(\cup_{G_{0}})\subset V_{0}^{\otimes 2},\quad R_{i}=\ker(\cup_{G_{i}})\subset V_{i}^{\otimes 2},\quad R=\ker(\cup_{G})\subset V^{\otimes 2}.

By assumption (ii), we have the amalgamated free product
G\cong G_{1}*_{G_{0}}G_{2}
. The Mayer-Vietoris sequence for this amalgam yields

0\to H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}_{1,0}-\mathrm{res}_{2,0}}H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

Since
\mathrm{res}_{i,0}
are surjective by assumption (iii), this shows that the natural map

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\longrightarrow H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})

is injective with image
\ker(H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}))
, yielding

H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\;\cong\;H^{1}(G_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2})\times_{H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})}H^{1}(G_{2},\mathbb{F}_{2}).

By assumption (iii), the graded algebras
C,A_{1},A_{2},A
are quadratic and generated in degree
1
. In particular, each is determined by its degree-
1
part and the kernel of the cup product in degree
2
: more precisely,

C\cong T(V_{0})/\langle R_{0}\rangle,\quad A_{i}\cong T(V_{i})/\langle R_{i}\rangle,\quad A\cong T(V)/\langle R\rangle,

where
T(-)
denotes the tensor algebra and
\langle R_{\ast}\rangle
the ideal generated by
R_{\ast}
in degree
2
.

Assumption (iii) further says that the restriction maps
V_{i}\to V_{0}
are surjective and that

V=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})\;\cong\;V_{1}\times_{V_{0}}V_{2}.

Equivalently, the degree-
1
part of
A
identifies with the fiber product of
A_{1}^{1}
and
A_{2}^{1}
over
C^{1}
:

A^{1}\;\cong\;A_{1}^{1}\times_{C^{1}}A_{2}^{1}.

On the other hand, assumption (iv) identifies
R=\ker(\cup_{G})
with the pullback of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
over
R_{0}
under the maps induced by restriction

V^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow V_{i}^{\otimes 2}\longrightarrow V_{0}^{\otimes 2},\qquad i=1,2.

In other words, the quadratic relations of
A
are exactly the pairs of quadratic relations in
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
that agree after restriction to
C
.

Summarizing, the quadratic datum
(V,R)
defining
A
is obtained functorially from the data
(V_{1},R_{1})
,
(V_{2},R_{2})
and
(V_{0},R_{0})
by the same fiber-product construction in degree
1
(for generators) and in degree
2
(for relations). Thus the quadratic presentation of
A
is obtained from the quadratic presentations of
C,A_{1},A_{2}
by this gluing procedure.

By assumption (v), the algebras
C,A_{1},A_{2}
are universally Koszul, and universal Koszulity is stable under exactly this passage from

(V_{0},R_{0}),\ (V_{1},R_{1}),\ (V_{2},R_{2})

to the glued quadratic algebra
A
defined by
(V,R)
. Therefore
A
is a universally Koszul quadratic algebra.

Since
A=H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
, this shows that the mod-
2
cohomology ring of
G_{K}(2)
is quadratic and universally Koszul, as claimed.
∎

The somewhat technical hypotheses in Theorem
4.1
are in fact automatically satisfied for the Pythagorean fields that arise in our applications, thanks to the known structural descriptions of their maximal pro-
2
Galois groups.

Remark 4.2
.

For the classes of Pythagorean fields considered in the applications (e.g. those whose maximal pro-
2
Galois groups admit the free product decompositions in (i)- (ii), it is known that each of the groups
G_{0},G_{1},G_{2},G
has
\mathrm{cd}_{2}\leq 2
and that the cohomology rings
C,A_{1},A_{2},A
are quadratic and generated in degree
1
; see, for example, the work of Mináč-Pasini-Quadrelli-Tân on Koszulity in Galois cohomology. In this situation, the amalgam decomposition

G\cong G_{1}*_{G_{0}}G_{2}

arises from an action on a pro-
2
tree, and the continuous cohomology of such a graph of pro-
2
groups admits a Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequence. The fiber-product description of
H^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
in (iii) and the pullback description of
\ker(\cup_{G})
in (iv) are then consequences of this Mayer-Vietoris sequence together with functoriality of the cup product in group cohomology; compare Lorensen
[
3
]
and the general theory of cohomology of profinite graphs of groups (cf. Serre’s
Galois Cohomology
). Thus, for such Pythagorean fields, hypotheses (iii) and (iv) hold automatically once the group-theoretic decompositions in (i)–(ii) are established.

We next illustrate Theorem
4.1
in a concrete family of Pythagorean fields obtained by adjoining independent square roots.

Remark 4.3

(Verification of hypotheses)
.

We briefly verify that hypotheses (i)–(v) of Theorem
4.1

are satisfied for the Pythagorean fields considered in our applications.
Assumption (i) (the Demuškin/free decomposition of
G_{k}(2)
) is established
by
[
2
, Theorem A]
. Assumption (ii) (the amalgam structure

G_{K}(2)\cong G_{K_{1}}(2)*_{G_{k}(2)}G_{K_{2}}(2)
) follows from the structure
theory of
\Delta
-right angled Artin groups and the profinite Kurosh theorem
for free products
[
2
]
. The quadraticity in assumption (iii)
is a consequence of the Bloch-Kato conjecture, now the Rost-Voevodsky theorem

[
14
]
, while the surjectivity of the restriction maps

\mathrm{res}_{i,0}:H^{1}(G_{i},\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{1}(G_{0},\mathbb{F}_{2})
follows
from the fact that
K_{i}\supset k
is obtained by adjoining square roots, so
that the associated map
G_{0}\to G_{i}
is surjective on
H^{1}(-,\mathbb{F}_{2})

by Kummer theory. Assumption (iv) (the pullback description of
\ker(\cup_{G})
)
follows from functoriality of the cup product and the Mayer-Vietoris sequence
for the amalgam; see
[
3
]
for the general framework.
Finally, assumption (v) (universal Koszulity of
C,A_{1},A_{2}
and its stability
under gluing) is established by
[
6
,
5
,
8
]
.

Example 4.4

(RPF fields and adjoining square roots)
.

Let
F
be a formally real Pythagorean field of finite type in the sense of Hamza-Maire-Mináč-Tân
[
2
]
. Thus
F
is Pythagorean,
-1\notin F^{\times 2}
, and the square-class group
F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
is finite. Set
K=F(\sqrt{-1})
and let
G_{F}(2)
and
G_{K}(2)
denote the maximal pro-
2
quotients of the absolute Galois groups of
F
and
K
, respectively. By
[
2
]
,
G_{K}(2)
is a
\Delta
-right angled Artin pro-
2
group and
G_{F}(2)
is obtained from
G_{K}(2)
by imposing a single involution corresponding to complex conjugation.

Fix elements
a_{1},a_{2}\in F^{\times}
such that their classes in
F^{\times}/F^{\times 2}
are independent, and consider the multiquadratic extensions

K_{1}=F(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{a_{1}}),\qquad K_{2}=F(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{a_{2}}),\qquad K=F(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}}).

Then
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are again formally real Pythagorean fields of finite type, with
K_{1}\cap K_{2}=F(\sqrt{-1})=K
, and their maximal pro-
2
Galois groups remain
\Delta
-right angled Artin groups with one additional generator corresponding to
\sqrt{a_{i}}
. In particular, the profinite Kurosh decomposition for
\Delta
-RAAGs implies that

G_{K_{i}}(2)\;\cong\;G_{K}(2)*F_{i},

with
F_{i}
a finitely generated free pro-
2
group, and that

G_{K}(2)\;\cong\;G_{K_{1}}(2)*_{G_{K}(2)}G_{K_{2}}(2)

as pro-
2
groups.

Moreover, by
[
6
,
5
]
and the structural results of
[
2
]
, the cohomology rings

H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2}),\quad H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{1}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2}),\quad H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{2}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})

are quadratic, universally Koszul algebras, and the groups
G_{K}(2),G_{K_{1}}(2),G_{K_{2}}(2)
all have cohomological dimension
\leq 2
. The action of
G_{K_{1}}(2)
and
G_{K_{2}}(2)
on the pro-
2
tree associated to the amalgam then yields a Mayer-Vietoris long exact sequence in continuous cohomology

\cdots\to H^{n}(G(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{n}(G_{K_{1}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})\oplus H^{n}(G_{K_{2}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})\to H^{n}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})\to\cdots

which is exact in degrees

and satisfies the compatibility properties required in assumptions (iii) and (iv) of Theorem
4.1
(see
[
3
]
for the Mayer-Vietoris sequence and the discussion in Remark

).

Thus the quadruple
(K,K_{1},K_{2},K)
satisfies hypotheses (i)–(v) of Theorem
4.1
. In particular, the theorem applies and shows that

H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})

is a quadratic, universally Koszul algebra. By iterating this construction (adjoining further independent square roots in
F^{\times}
), we obtain an explicit infinite family of Pythagorean fields built from
F
by adjoining independent square roots, for which all intermediate maximal pro-
2
Galois groups admit the required free product decompositions and all cohomology rings are quadratic and universally Koszul.

We now spell out several formal corollaries of Theorem
4.1
, phrased in terms of adjoining independent square roots and forming towers of such extensions.

Corollary 4.5

(Adjoining independent square roots)
.

Let
k
be as in Theorem
4.1
. Let
a_{1},a_{2}\in k^{\times}
be such that:

(1)

Each
K_{i}=k(\sqrt{a_{i}})
is Pythagorean and satisfies

G_{K_{i}}(2)\;\cong\;G_{k}(2)\,*\,F_{i},

where
F_{i}
is a finitely generated free pro-
2
group;

(2)

The compositum
K=k(\sqrt{a_{1}},\sqrt{a_{2}})
satisfies

G_{K}(2)\;\cong\;G_{K_{1}}(2)*_{G_{k}(2)}G_{K_{2}}(2).

Then
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a quadratic, universally Koszul algebra.

Proof.

This is an immediate application of Theorem
4.1
. Apply Theorem
4.1
with the given
k,K_{1},K_{2},K
.
∎

The preceding corollary can be iterated along towers of admissible composita, yielding the following stability result for finite towers.

Corollary 4.6

(Finite towers of such composita)
.

Let
k
be as in Theorem
4.1
, and let

k=K^{(0)}\subset K^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset K^{(n)}

be a finite tower of fields with the following property: for each
j=0,\dots,n-1
there exist Pythagorean fields
K^{(j)}_{1},K^{(j)}_{2}
with
K^{(j)}_{0}=K^{(j)}_{1}\cap K^{(j)}_{2}
such that

K^{(j+1)}\;=\;K^{(j)}_{1}K^{(j)}_{2},

and the triples
(K^{(j)}_{0},K^{(j)}_{1},K^{(j)}_{2})
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
(in particular, the required pro-
2
decompositions and amalgamated free-product description).

Then for every
j=0,\dots,n
the mod-
2
cohomology ring

H^{\bullet}(G_{K^{(j)}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})

is quadratic and universally Koszul.

Proof.

For
j=0
this holds by assumption on
k
. If it holds for
K^{(j)}_{1}
and
K^{(j)}_{2}
, then Theorem
4.1
applied to
(K^{(j)}_{0},K^{(j)}_{1},K^{(j)}_{2})
yields the claim for
K^{(j+1)}
. Induction on
j
completes the proof.
∎

In particular, we obtain a large class of Pythagorean fields built by iterating the basic operations of adjoining square roots and forming admissible composita, all of which have universally Koszul cohomology.

Corollary 4.7

(Elementary-type Pythagorean fields)
.

Let
k
be a local or global field containing
\mu_{2}
. Let
K
be obtained from
k
by a finite sequence of operations of the following two types:

1.

adjoining square roots
K\mapsto K(\sqrt{a})
in such a way that each intermediate extension is Pythagorean and has maximal pro-
2
Galois group a free pro-
2
product of Demuškin groups and a free factor, and

2.

forming composita
K^{\prime}=K_{1}K_{2}
of Pythagorean fields over a common intersection
K_{0}
, where the triple
(K_{0},K_{1},K_{2})
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
4.1
.

Then
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
is a quadratic, universally Koszul algebra.

Proof.

Local and global fields
k
as above have quadratic, universally Koszul mod-
2
cohomology by
[
6
,
5
]
. Each operation of type (1) preserves this property by the known structure of maximal pro-
2
Galois groups of Pythagorean fields, and each operation of type (2) preserves it by Theorem
4.1
. An induction on the length of the sequence yields the result.
∎

Finally, we record a cohomological obstruction to realizing certain finitely presented pro-
2
groups as maximal pro-
2
Galois groups over the elementary-type Pythagorean fields just described.

Corollary 4.8

(Exclusion of candidate pro-
2
groups)
.

Let
k
and
K
be as in Corollary
4.7
, and let
G
be a finitely presented pro-
2
group. If the graded algebra
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{2})
is not quadratic and universally Koszul, then
G
is not isomorphic to
G_{L}(2)
for any field
L
obtained from
k
by a finite sequence of operations of types
(1)
and
(2)
above. In particular,
G\not\cong G_{K}(2)
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.7
, every such field
L
has quadratic, universally Koszul cohomology. Hence no pro-
2
group with a non-Koszul or non-quadratic cohomology ring can occur as
G_{L}(2)
.
∎

We close this section with a brief remark about the PBW property in this context.

Corollary 4.9

(Persistence of the PBW property)
.

In the situation of Theorem
4.1
, suppose in addition that
H^{\bullet}(G_{k}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
,
i=1,2
, admit PBW bases (equivalently, are PBW algebras). Then
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
is also a PBW algebra.

Proof.

By the proof of Theorem
4.1
, the quadratic presentation of
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
is obtained functorially from those of
H^{\bullet}(G_{k}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
and
H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
by a fiber-product construction on generators and relations. PBW bases are expected to be preserved under this construction in the present setting (free factors and a common quadratic subalgebra), so
H^{\bullet}(G_{K}(2),\mathbb{F}_{2})
inherits a PBW basis.
∎

5
Local and global fields, and elementary-type constructions

We now turn to the case of local and global fields, and show how the abstract composita stability theorem yields a large class of “elementary-type” fields with universally Koszul Galois cohomology.

Corollary 5.1

(Local fields and admissible composita)
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
\mathcal{L}_{p}
be the class of finite extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
containing
\mu_{p}
such that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul. Let
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
with common subfield
k=K_{1}\cap K_{2}
, and let
K=K_{1}K_{2}
.

Assume that, for the quadruple
(K_{1},K_{2},k,K)
, the associated maximal pro-
p
Galois groups and cohomology rings satisfy hypotheses (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
. Then
K\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
as well.

Proof.

By definition of
\mathcal{L}_{p}
, we have that
A_{i}=H^{\bullet}(G_{K_{i}},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul for
i=1,2
. Since
(K_{1},K_{2},k,K)
satisfies the structural assumptions (i)–(iv), Theorem
3.1
applies and shows that
A=H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul. Hence
K\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
.
∎

Thus the class
\mathcal{L}_{p}
of local fields with universally Koszul Galois cohomology is stable under admissible composita in the sense of Theorem
3.1
. The same reasoning applies to global fields.

Corollary 5.2

(Global fields and admissible composita)
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
\mathcal{G}_{p}
be the class of global fields
K
(number fields or function fields of curves over finite fields) containing
\mu_{p}
, such that
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul. Let
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{G}_{p}
with common subfield
k=K_{1}\cap K_{2}
, and let
K=K_{1}K_{2}
.

Assume that, for the quadruple
(K_{1},K_{2},k,K)
, the associated maximal pro-
p
Galois groups and cohomology rings satisfy hypotheses (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
. Then
K\in\mathcal{G}_{p}
.

Proof.

The argument is identical to that of Corollary
5.1
, with
\mathcal{L}_{p}
replaced by
\mathcal{G}_{p}
and
K_{i}
now global fields.
∎

Motivated by the notion of elementary-type pro-
p
groups, we now package local and global fields together and close the resulting class under admissible composita.

Corollary 5.3

(Elementary-type fields built from local and global pieces)
.

Fix a prime
p
and let
\mathcal{E}_{p}
be the smallest class of fields containing
\mathcal{L}_{p}\cup\mathcal{G}_{p}
and closed under the following operation:

(1)

If
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
have common subfield
k=K_{1}\cap K_{2}
and
K=K_{1}K_{2}
, and the quadruple
(K_{1},K_{2},k,K)
satisfies hypotheses (i)–(iv) of Theorem
3.1
, then
K\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
.

Then every field
K\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
has quadratic, universally Koszul pro-
p
Galois cohomology.

Proof.

By assumption, each local or global field
K\in\mathcal{L}_{p}\cup\mathcal{G}_{p}
has quadratic, universally Koszul cohomology. Suppose
K_{1},K_{2}\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
have this property and form a compositum
K=K_{1}K_{2}
as in (1). The hypotheses (i)–(iv) ensure that Theorem
3.1
applies, so
H^{\bullet}(G_{K},\mathbb{F}_{p})
is quadratic and universally Koszul. By construction,
\mathcal{E}_{p}
is generated from
\mathcal{L}_{p}\cup\mathcal{G}_{p}
by finitely many steps of type (1), so an induction on the number of steps shows that every
K\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
has quadratic, universally Koszul cohomology.
∎

In particular, the cohomological regularity enjoyed by local and global fields propagates along any finite sequence of admissible composita starting from these base fields.

Corollary 5.4

(Inverse Galois obstruction on
\mathcal{E}_{p}
)
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
and let
G
be a finitely generated pro-
p
group. If
H^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
is not quadratic and universally Koszul, then
G
cannot be isomorphic to the maximal pro-
p
Galois group
G_{L}
of any field
L\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
, in particular
G\not\cong G_{K}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
5.3
, every field
L\in\mathcal{E}_{p}
has quadratic, universally Koszul cohomology. Thus no pro-
p
group
G
with non-quadratic or non-Koszul mod-
p
cohomology can occur as
G_{L}
for such an
L
.
∎

1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in the theory of compact transformation groups is to determine which finite groups
G
act effectively on a given manifold
M
. To solve this problem in full generality for an arbitrary manifold is extremely difficult with the current tools. A way to modify this problem is the following; instead of studying the properties of an action of a finite group
G
on
M
, we study the properties of the restriction of this action to a suitable subgroup
H
of
G
of index bounded above by a constant
C
only depending on
M
. Let us recall some definitions and problems which follow this philosophy, compiled in the recent survey
[
MiR24b
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

1.

(Jordan property) A group
\mathcal{G}
is Jordan if there exists a constant
C
such that every finite subgroup
G\leq\mathcal{G}
has an abelian subgroup
A
such that
[G:A]\leq C
.

2.

(Discrete degree of symmetry) Given a manifold
M
, let

\mu(M)=\{r\in\mathbb{N}:\text{ $M$ admits an effective action of $(\mathbb{Z}/a)^{r}$ for arbitrarily large $a$}\}.

More explicitly,
r\in\mu(M)
if there exists an increasing sequence of natural number
\{a_{i}\}
and effective group actions of
(\mathbb{Z}/a_{i})^{r}
on
M
for each
i
.

The discrete degree of symmetry of a manifold
M
is

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\max(\{0\}\cup\mu(M)).

3.

(Minkowski property) A group
\mathcal{G}
is Minkowski if there exists a constant
C
such that every finite subgroup
G\leq\mathcal{G}
satisfies
|G|\leq C
. Given a manifold
M
, if
\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
is Minkowski then we say that
M
is almost asymmetric. If
M
does not admit effective actions of non-trivial finite groups, we say that
M
is asymmetric.

4.

(Stabilizers of an action on a manifold) Given a manifold
M
and a group
G
acting effectively on
M
, we define the set of stabilizer subgroups of the action of
G
on
M
as

\operatorname{Stab}(G,M)=\{G_{x}:x\in M\}.

We say that
M
has few stabilizers if there exists a constant
C
such that every a finite group
G
acting effectively on
M
has a subgroup
H\leq G
such that
[G:H]\leq C
and
|\operatorname{Stab}(H,M)|\leq C
.

Remark 1.2
.

The name of the Minkowski property is motivated by a classical result of Hermann Minkowski which states that
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
is Minkowski (see
[
Ser10
, §1]
). The Minkowski property was studied in
[
Pop11
,
PS14
,
Pop18
,
Gol23
,
BZ24
]
under the name of bounded finite subgroups property.

Question 1.3
.

Let
M
a closed connected
n
-dimensional manifold.

(1)

[
MiR24b
, §2]
When is
\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
Jordan?

(2)

[
MiR24b
, Question 3.4, Question 3.5]
Is
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq n
? If
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n
, is
M
necessarily homeomorphic to
T^{n}
?

(3)

[
MiR24b
, Question 8.3]
When is
M
almost asymmetric?

(4)

[
MiR24b
, Question 12.2]
Does every closed connected manifold have few stabilizers?

Let us give some historical context about these four questions.
1.3
.(1) was asked around 30 years ago for the diffeomorphism group of closed connected smooth manifolds by Étienne Ghys. It has been answered affirmatively for many manifolds (see
[
MiR24b
, §2]
). However, it has been shown that there are closed connected manifolds whose diffeomorphism group is not Jordan, the simplest one being
S^{2}\times T^{2}
(see
[
CPS14
,
MiR17
,
Sza19
,
Sza23
,
Zar14
]
for other examples of non-Jordan groups of homeomorphism).
There is no general method to determine whether the homeomorphism group of a closed connected manifold is Jordan. We also remark that the Jordan property also has a rich history in the field of algebraic geometry, which started when J.-P. Serre proved that the Cremona group
Cr_{2}=\operatorname{Bir}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
is Jordan in
[
Ser09
]
and asked if higher rank Cremona groups
Cr_{n}
are also Jordan (the question has been affirmatively answered in
[
PS16
,
Bir21
]
). For the most recent developments on the Jordan property in algebraic geometry we refer to the surveys
[
BZ24
,
LMZ25
]
, as well as
[
MiR24a
, §2]
and the introductions of
[
CPS22
]
and
[
Gol23
]
.

The discrete degree of symmetry of a manifold
M
was introduced in
[
MiR24a
]
to study rigidity results on rationally hypertoral manifolds (see
1.9
for one of the main results in
[
MiR24a
]
). The discrete degree of symmetry can be regarded as the finite group analogue of the toral degree of symmetry of
M
, defined as

\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)=\max(\{0\}\cup\{r\in\mathbb{N}:T^{r}\text{ acts effectively on $M$}\}).

The toral degree of symmetry has been widely studied (see
[
Hsi12
, Chapter VII. §2]
,
[
LR10
, §11.7, §11.8]
and the survey
[
Gro02
]
). The theorem of invariance of domain imply that if
M
is a closed manifold of dimension
n
then
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)\leq n
and that
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)=n
if and only if
M\cong T^{n}
(for details, see
[
MiR24a
, 12.2]
).
1.3
.(2) asks if the same results hold for the discrete degree of symmetry. Note that there exist manifolds such that
\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)\neq\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)
(see
[
MiR24a
, Theorem 1.11]
).

The discrete degree of symmetry and the toral degree of symmetry are related to other well-known invariants of manifolds, like the the toral rank or the
p
-rank of a manifold. Recall that an action of a Lie group
G
on a manifold
M
is almost-free if all the stabilizer subgroups of the action of
G
on
M
are finite.

Definition 1.4
.

Assume that
M
is a manifold. We define:

1.

The toral rank of
M
is

\operatorname{rank}(M)=\max\{\{0\}\cup\{r:T^{r}\text{ acts almost-freely on $M$}\}\}.

2.

Given a prime
p
, the
p
-rank of
M
is

\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)=\max\{\{0\}\cup\{r:(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{r}\text{ acts freely on $M$}\}\}.

If
M
is a closed connected manifold then it is clear that
\operatorname{rank}(M)\leq\operatorname{tor-sym}(M)\leq\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)
. There are two important conjectures due to S.Halperin and G.Carlsson regarding the
\operatorname{rank}(M)
and
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)
(see
[
FOT08
, §7.3]
).

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold. Then:

1.

(Toral rank conjecture)
\dim H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})\geq 2^{\operatorname{rank}(M)}
.

2.

(Carlsson’s conjecture) For all prime
p
,
\dim H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/p)\geq 2^{\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)}
.

There is a weaker version of the Carlsson’s conjecture, which we call the stable Carlsson’s conjecture.

Conjecture 1.6
.

(Stable Carlsson’s conjecture)
Given a closed connected manifold
M
, there exists a constant
C
such that
\dim H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z}/p)\geq 2^{\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)}
for all prime
p>C
.

The stable Carlsson conjecture was answered affirmatively for products of spheres in
[
Han09
]
.

Regarding
1.3
.(3), a classical problem in geometric topology is to construct manifolds which do not admit any effective finite group action (see the survey
[
Pup07
]
). For example, A. Borel constructed the first example of asymmetric manifold in a unpublished work (see
[
Bor83
]
). Almost-asymmetry is a weaker property than asymmetry. There are many examples of almost-asymmetrical manifolds, like closed connected oriented surfaces of genus greater than 1. This is a consequence of Hurwicz
84(g-1)
bound (for details, see
[
MiR10
]
). We remark that a closed manifold
M
is almost asymmetric if and only if
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=0
(see
[
MiR24b
, Lemma 8.1]
).

Lastly,
1.3
.(4) appeared in
[
CMiRPS21
, Question 1.9]
motivated by the following theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

[
CMiRPS21
, Theorem 1.3]

There exists a constant
C
such that any finite
p
-group
G
acting effectively on a closed manifold
M
has a subgroup
H
such that
[G:H]\leq C
and
|\operatorname{Stab}(H,M)|\leq C
.

This result was crucial in proving a generalized version of the Jordan property of the homeomorphism group of closed manifolds in
[
CPS22
]
, which states that there exists a constant
C
such that every finite group
G
acting on
M
has a nilpotent subgroup
N
such that
[G:N]\leq C
.

In
[
DS25
]
, the author answered these questions for a large class of closed connected aspherical manifolds, which contain closed aspherical locally homogeneous spaces.

Theorem 1.8
.

(
[
DS25
, Theorem 1.6, Theorem 1.9]
)
Let
M
be a closed connected
n
-dimensional aspherical manifold such that
\mathcal{Z}(\pi_{1}(M))
is finitely generated and
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski. Then:

1.

\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
is Jordan.

2.

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}(\pi_{1}(M))\leq n
, and
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n
if and only if
M
is homeomorphic to
T^{n}
.

3.

If
\chi(M)\neq 0
then
M
is almost-asymmetric.

4.

If
\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski, then
M
has few stabilizers.

Let
\Gamma
be a lattice of a connected Lie group, then
\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma)
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)
are Minkowski.

There has been many efforts to generalize results describing group actions on closed connected aspherical manifolds to a wider class of manifolds (see
[

,

,

,

,

.

Let
M
be a closed connected orientable
n

1.

\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
is Jordan.

2.

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq n
and if
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n

. Moreover, if
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n

.

Our first result generalizes

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
M
be a closed oriented connected
n

1.

\operatorname{Homeo}(M)

2.

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\Gamma\leq n

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n
then
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{*}(T^{n},\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=n

\pi_{1}(M)
is virtually solvable then

.

3.

If
\chi(M)\neq 0
then
M
is almost asymmetric.

4.

M
has few stabilizers.

As an application of
1.10
, we prove:

Proposition 1.11
.

Let
M
be a closed oriented connected
n
-dimensional manifold and
f:M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
a non-zero degree map to a closed nilmanifold. Assume that the toral rank conjecture holds for

, then the toral rank conjecture and the stable Carlsson’s conjecture hold for

.

1.11
follows from

and the fact that the toral rank conjecture and the stable Carlsson’s conjecture are equivalent for closed aspherical locally homogeneous spaces (see

). The toral rank conjecture is true if

is a 2-step nilmanifold (see
[

]
), thus

can be used when

is a 2-step nilmanifold. For other nilmanifolds where it is known that the toral rank conjecture holds see
[

.

To prove
1.10
we introduce a new concept called exporting map, inspired by
[
MiR24a
, Theorem 4.1]
. A map between manifolds
f:M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}
is an exporting map if there exists a constant
C
such that every finite group
G
acting on
M
has a subgroup
H\leq G
such that
H
acts on
M^{\prime}
, there exists a

homotopic to

and
[G:H]\leq C
(see

).

It is a natural question to ask whether
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\Gamma
implies
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{*}(N/\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
. This is not true in general (see

to study cohomological rigidity for manifolds admitting a non-zero degree map to a nilmanifold. In order to do so, we recall that nilmanifolds are precisely total spaces of iterated principal

). This fact leads to the following definition:

Since

is a regular covering, there exists a finite group

acting freely on

such that

can be seen as the orbit map
p:M\longrightarrow M/G
. Consequently, the study of towers of regular self-coverings is the study of iterated group actions of
\mathcal{G}=\{G,\overset{n}{\cdots},G\}
such that

for all

are the same. In

where

is a finite group of

is a connected Lie group. For example, in

is the solvable radical of

The next proposition shows that free iterated actions are a suitable tool to study nilmanifolds. Recall that if a compact Lie group
G
acts freely on a manifold
M
then the quotient map
M/G
is also a manifold of dimension
\dim M-\dim G
and the orbit map
M\longrightarrow M/G
is a principal
G
-bundle (see
[
Bre72
, Chapter II]
).

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold of dimension
n
. Assume that we have a free iterated action
\{T^{b_{1}},\dots,T^{b_{c}}\}\curvearrowright M
. Then
\sum_{i=1}^{c}b_{i}\leq n
and we have an equality if and only if
M
is a nilmanifold.

Proof.

Since all the actions of the iterated action are free, the maps
p_{i}:M_{i-1}\longrightarrow M_{i}
are principal
T^{b_{i}}
-bundles and the quotient
M_{c-1}
is a closed manifold of dimension
n-\sum_{i=1}^{c-1}b_{i}
. Therefore,
b_{c}\leq n-\sum_{i=1}^{c-1}b_{i}
and hence
\sum_{i=1}^{c}b_{i}\leq n
. If the equality holds, then
b_{c}=\dim(M_{c-1})
. Since
M_{c-1}
is closed and connected and it admits a free action of a torus
T^{\dim(M_{c-1})}
we can conclude that
M_{c-1}\cong T^{b_{c}}
. This implies that
M
can be obtained as iterated principal torus bundles and hence
M
is a nilmanifold.
∎

Our focus is on free iterated actions of finite groups on manifolds. Let us define the notions that we need to state our results on iterated actions.

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\}_{i=1,...,n}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{G^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1,...,n^{\prime}}
be two collections of finite groups which act freely on
M
. Let
p=p_{n}\circ\cdots\circ p_{1}
and
p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}\circ\cdots\circ p^{\prime}_{1}
. We say that the iterated actions
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
are equivalent (and we denote it by
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M\sim\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
) if:

1.

There exists a homeomorphism
f:M_{n}\longrightarrow M_{n^{\prime}}
.

2.

The coverings
p:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}
and
f^{*}p^{\prime}:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}
are isomorphic. That is, there exists a homeomorphism
\overline{f}:M\longrightarrow M
satisfying
p^{\prime}\circ\overline{f}=f\circ p
.

The equivalence class will be denoted by
[\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M]
. If
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
is equivalent to
\{G\}\curvearrowright M
for some group
G
then we say that
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
is simplifiable.

With
1.14
is straightforward to see that a free iterated action
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\}\curvearrowright M
is simplifiable if and only if
\pi_{1}(M)\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M_{n})
. In particular, all free iterated actions on simply-connected manifolds are simplifiable. However, there exist manifolds with non-trivial fundamental group where all free iterated actions on them are simplifiable (see
4.5
). There also many examples of non-simplifiable free iterated actions (see, for example,
4.6
). Recall that if
G
is a finite group, then
\operatorname{rank}G
is the minimum number of elements which are needed to generate
G
. The next definition refines the concept of the discrete degree of symmetry to iterated actions of two groups.

Definition 1.15
.

Given a free iterated action
\mathcal{A}\curvearrowright M
of finite abelian groups, the rank of the iterated action is

\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\mathcal{A}\curvearrowright M)=\min\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{rank}A^{\prime}_{i}:\{A_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,A_{n}^{\prime}\}\curvearrowright M\in[\mathcal{A}\curvearrowright M]\text{, $A_{i}^{\prime}$ abelian for all $i$}\}.

We define
\mu_{2}(M)
as the set of all pairs
(f,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
which satisfy:

1.

There exist an increasing sequence of prime numbers
\{p_{i}\}
, a sequence of natural numbers
\{a_{i}\}
and a collection of free iterated actions
\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{f},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}\}\curvearrowright M
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{f},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}\}\curvearrowright M)=f+b
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Consider the lexicographic order in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, that is
(a,b)\geq(c,d)
if
a>c
, or
a=c
and
b\geq d
. Define the iterated discrete degree of symmetry of
M
as

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=\max\{(0,0)\cup\mu_{2}(M)\}.

Note that there are two significant differences between the conditions used in the definitions of the discrete degree of symmetry and the iterated discrete degree of symmetry. On the iterated discrete degree of symmetry we only consider actions of abelian
p
-groups, and all these actions are assumed to be free. While this hypothesis are made for technical reasons, they do not suppose a big loss of generality in our case, since by
1.10
.4, any effective action of an abelian
p
-group on a manifold
M
admitting a non-zero degree to a nilmanifold is free for large enough
p
.

Theorem 1.16
.

Let
N/\Gamma
be a nilmanifold of dimension
n
, let
a=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\Gamma
and let
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(N/\Gamma)=(d_{1},d_{2})
. Then
d_{1}=a
and
d_{2}\leq n-a
. Moreover,
d_{2}=n-a
if and only if
N/\Gamma
is a 2-step nilmanifold or a torus.

Finally, we prove that the iterated discrete degree of symmetry is the right tool to study cohomological rigidity of manifolds admitting non-zero degree maps to 2-step nilmanifolds.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold admitting a non-zero degree map
f:M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
to a
2
-step nilmanifold, which is the total space of a principal
T^{a}
-bundle over
T^{b}
. Then
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)\leq(a,b)
. Moreover, if
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(a,b)
then
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(N/\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})
.

Let

be a closed oriented manifold of dimension

of non-zero degree and let

Remark 2.2
.

The constructed action of

on

is not necessarily effective. If

denotes the kernel of ineffectiveness, then

and the effective action of

is trivial.

2.1
inspires the following definition:

Definition 2.3

and

be closed oriented manifolds of the same dimension and let

be a continuous map. We say that

exports group actions, or

such that every finite group

(denoted by

H
-equivariant map

By Minkowski’s lemma, given a closed connected manifold

such that any finite group

Before stating the main result of this section, we introduce two more properties of large finite group actions on manifolds.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold. Then:

1.

We say that
M
has the small stabilizers property if there exist a constant
C
such that if
G
is a finite group acting effectively on
M
then
|G_{x}|\leq C
for all
x\in M
.

2.

We say that
M
has the almost fixed point property if there exists a constant
C
such that if
G
is a finite group acting effectively on
M
then there exist
x\in M
such that
[G:G_{x}]\leq C
.

The first property is new and it has not been studied yet. We will show that the manifolds studied in this article have this property. The second property was studied in
[
MiR24c
]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
MiR24c
, Theorem 1.5]

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold with
\chi(M)\neq 0
. Then
M
has the almost fixed point property.

The almost fixed point property and the small stabilizers property are dual in the following sense:

Lemma 2.11
.

A closed manifold
M
with the small stabilizers and the almost fixed point property is almost asymmetric.

Proof.

Let
C
be the constant of the almost fixed point property and
D
the constant of the small stabilizers property. Assume that we have a finite group
G
acting effectively on
M
. Then there exists
x\in M
such that
[G:G_{x}]\leq C
. In addition,
|G_{x}|\leq D
. Consequently,
|G|\leq C\cdot D
. Thus,
M
is almost asymmetric.
∎

Definition 2.15

Let

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{p}(M)=\max\{\{0\}\cup\{r:(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s})^{r}\text{ acts effectively on $M$ for arbitrarily large $s$}\}\}.

Let
M
be a closed manifold, then

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\max(\{P\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\}\cup\{\operatorname{disc-sym}_{p}(M):\text{$p$ prime}\}).

Clearly,

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{p}(M)\leq\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)

. Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=b
and therefore there exists a sequence of natural numbers

. Let

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

and

Proof.

We denote by

is

3.2.
The toral rank and stable Carlsson’s conjecture

The first application is

Lemma 3.9
.

Let

be a closed connected aspherical locally homogeneous space. The toral rank conjecture holds for

if and only if the stable Carlsson’s conjecture holds for

Proof.

Since

is finitely generated, we can use the universal coefficients theorem to conclude that there exists a constant

such that for all prime

. By
[

and
\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_{1}(M))
are Minkowski. Let
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
denote the Minkowski constant of
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_{1}(M))
respectively.

For all primes

, we have that
\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
, by
[
LR87
, Theorem 3.1.16]
. In addition, for all primes

any effective action of a

is free, by
[
LR10
, Corollary 3.1.17]
. Consequently,
\operatorname{rank}(M)\leq\operatorname{rank}_{p}(M)

. Finally,
\operatorname{rank}(M)=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
by

, Theorem 1.10]
and

, Corollary 3.1.17]
. Thus, if

, then

. Set

.

\dim H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})\geq 2^{\operatorname{rank}(M)}=2^{\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M))}
. For all primes

.

Now, we prove the converse statement. Thus, we assume that there exists a constant

such that for all primes

. Choose a prime

. Then

and we have

.
∎

3.3.
Closed connected manifolds with non-zero degree maps to solvmanifolds

1.10
is false if we replace nilmanifold by solvmanifold. In order to give an example of a non-zero degree map between solvmanifolds which does not export group actions we need the following elementary group theoretic lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be groups and let
\phi:G_{2}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(G_{1})
be a group morphism. Then

\mathcal{Z}(G_{1}\rtimes_{\phi}G_{2})=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in G_{1}\rtimes_{\phi}G_{2}:g_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}G_{2},g_{1}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)\text{ and }\phi(g_{2})=c_{g_{1}}\},

where
\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)=\{g_{1}\in G_{1}:\phi(g_{2})(g_{1})=g_{1}\text{ for all }g_{2}\in G_{2}\}
.

Proof.

Let
(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathcal{Z}(G_{1}\rtimes_{\phi}G_{2})
. For any
(g^{\prime}_{1},g^{\prime}_{2})\in G_{1}\rtimes_{\phi}G_{2}
we have that
(g_{1},g_{2})(g^{\prime}_{1}g^{\prime}_{2})=(g_{1}\phi(g_{2})(g^{\prime}_{1}),g_{2}g^{\prime}_{2})
is equal to
(g^{\prime}_{1},g^{\prime}_{2})(g_{1}g_{2})=(g^{\prime}_{1}\phi(g^{\prime}_{2})(g_{1}),g^{\prime}_{2}g_{2})
. Hence
g_{1}\phi(g_{2})(g^{\prime}_{1})=g^{\prime}_{1}\phi(g^{\prime}_{2})(g_{1})
and
g_{2}g^{\prime}_{2}=g^{\prime}_{2}g_{2}
for all
g_{1}^{\prime}\in G_{1}
and
g_{2}^{\prime}\in G_{2}
.

By the second condition,
g_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}G_{2}
. If we set
g^{\prime}_{1}=e
, then
g_{1}=\phi(g^{\prime}_{2})(g_{1})
for all
g^{\prime}_{2}\in G_{2}
and therefore
g_{1}\in\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)
. Finally, by using that
g_{1}=\phi(g^{\prime}_{2})(g_{1})
, we obtain that
g_{1}\phi(g_{2})(g^{\prime}_{1})=g^{\prime}_{1}g_{1}
, which implies that
\phi(g_{2})(g^{\prime}_{1})=g_{1}^{-1}g^{\prime}_{1}g_{1}
and
\phi(g_{2})=c_{g_{1}}
.
∎

We consider a non-trivial group morphism

1.

is the total space of a fibration

. In particular,
\pi_{1}(T^{n}_{f})=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z}
is polycyclic.

2.

is finitely covered by the torus

. This is because the subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes_{\phi}a\mathbb{Z}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}
is a normal subgroup of index

. Therefore, we have a regular covering

is a flat manifold.

Proposition 3.11
.

We have
\operatorname{disc-sym}(T^{n}_{f})\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
T^{n}_{f}
is a flat manifold, we know that
\operatorname{disc-sym}(T^{n}_{f})=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z})
by
[
DS25
]
. By
3.10
,
\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z})=\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)\times a\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\phi(1)\neq Id
, we obtain that
\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Fix}(\phi)\leq n-1
and therefore
\operatorname{disc-sym}(T^{n}_{f})=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes_{\phi}\mathbb{Z})\leq n
.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

The map
p:T^{n+1}\longrightarrow T^{n}_{f}
does not export group actions.

3.4.
Examples where
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\Gamma

Now we present examples of closed orientable manifolds
M
admitting a non-zero degree map to a nilmanifold
f:M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
such that
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\operatorname{disc-sym}(N/\Gamma)
but
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})\ncong H^{*}(N/\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
H
be the Heisenberg group of dimension 3,

H=\{(x,y,z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right):x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}\},

which is a simply connected nilpotent Lie group. Any lattice of
H
is isomorphic to a lattice of the form

\Gamma_{k}=\{(x,y,z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&x&\tfrac{1}{k}z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right):x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Consider
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
lattices of
H
. Note that the covering map
p:H/\Gamma_{1}\longrightarrow H/\Gamma_{2}
induced by the inclusion
\Gamma_{1}\hookrightarrow\Gamma_{2}
is a non-zero degree map between and
\operatorname{disc-sym}(H/\Gamma_{1})=\operatorname{disc-sym}(H/\Gamma_{2})=1
. However, a straightforward computation shows that
H_{1}(H/\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
H_{1}(H/\Gamma_{2},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
, therefore
H^{*}(H/\Gamma_{1},\mathbb{Z})\ncong H^{*}(H/\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
.

Note that
H^{*}(H/\Gamma_{1},\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{*}(H/\Gamma_{2},\mathbb{Q})
. Our next goal is to prove the next proposition:

Proposition 3.13
.

There exists a closed orientable manifold
M
admitting a map to a nilmanifold
f:M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
satisfying
\deg(f)=1
,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)=\operatorname{disc-sym}(N/\Gamma)=1
and
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Q})\ncong H^{*}(N/\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})
.

Firstly, we recall some facts on principal
S^{1}
-bundles.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
p:E\longrightarrow B
be a principal
S^{1}
-bundle, then
p^{*}:H^{1}(B,\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow H^{1}(E,\mathbb{Q})
is injective. It is an isomorphism if and only if the first Chern class
c_{1}(E)\neq 0
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold of dimension
n\geq 4
and let
D\subseteq M
be a disk. Then the inclusion
i:M\setminus D\longrightarrow M
induces an isomorphism
i^{*}:H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow H^{2}(M\setminus D,\mathbb{Q})
.

The first lemma is a consequence of the Gysin exact sequence and the second lemma is a consequence of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence.

Given a principal bundle
p:E\longrightarrow B
, then we have a principal
S^{1}
-bundle
p^{\prime}:E\setminus p^{-1}(D)\longrightarrow B\setminus D
. Then
c_{1}(E\setminus p^{-1}(D))=i^{*}c_{1}(E)
.

Note that
\partial(E\setminus p^{-1}(D))\cong S^{n-1}\times S^{1}
has a right
S^{1}
action induced by the action of the principal
S^{1}
-bundle.

Given principal
S^{1}
-bundles
p_{i}:E_{i}\longrightarrow B_{i}
with
i=1,2
, we can construct a
S^{1}
-equivariant homeomorphism
f:\partial(E_{1}\setminus p_{1}^{-1}(D_{1}))\longrightarrow\partial(E_{2}\setminus p_{2}^{-1}(D_{2}))
. There is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle
p:E_{1}\setminus p_{1}^{-1}(D_{1})\cup_{f}E_{2}\setminus p_{2}^{-1}(D_{2})\longrightarrow B_{1}\#B_{2}
.

Lemma 3.16
.

If
\dim(B_{i})\geq 4
then

c_{1}(E_{1}\setminus p_{1}^{-1}(D_{1})\cup_{f}E_{2}\setminus p_{2}^{-1}(D_{2})\longrightarrow B_{1}\#B_{2})=(c_{1}(E_{1}),c_{1}(E_{2}))\in H^{2}(B_{1},\mathbb{Q})\oplus H^{2}(B_{2},\mathbb{Q})

.

Finally note that we have
S^{1}
-equivariant maps of degree one,
f_{i}:E\longrightarrow E_{i}
for
i=1,2
. We are ready to prove
3.13
.

Proof of
3.13
.

Now we consider the Heisenberg manifold of dimension
5
,
H_{5}/\Gamma
, which is the total space of a principal
S^{1}
-bundle over a torus
T^{4}
(see
[
GK13
, Chapter I]
and
[
GW86
]
). We also consider a nilmanifold modeled over the filiform Lie group of dimension 5 (for details on filiform Lie algebras and Lie groups, see
[
GK13
, §2.I]
and
[
Ham08
,
Rem17
]
),
F_{5}/\Lambda
, which is the total space of a principal
S^{1}
-bundle over a filiform nilmanifold of dimension
4
,
F_{4}/\Lambda^{\prime}
. By construction,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(F_{5}/\Lambda)=1
and
H^{1}(F_{4}/\Lambda^{\prime})\cong H^{1}(F_{5}/\Lambda)\cong\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. Note that the discrete degree of symmetry of both nilmanifolds is
1
. Let
E=H_{5}/\Gamma\setminus p_{1}^{-1}(D_{1})\cup_{f}F_{5}/\Lambda\setminus p_{2}^{-1}(D_{2})
and consider the degree one maps
f_{1}:E\longrightarrow H_{5}/\Gamma
and
f_{2}:E\longrightarrow F_{5}/\Lambda
. Observe that
p:E\longrightarrow T^{4}\#F_{4}/\Lambda^{\prime}
is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle and therefore
\operatorname{disc-sym}(E)\geq 1
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(E)\leq 1
, since the maps
f_{i}
export group actions. In conclusion,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(E)=1
. However, since

, one can compute that
H^{1}(E,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{1}(T^{4}\#F_{4}/\Lambda^{\prime},\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}^{5}
. On the other hand, we have
H^{1}(H_{5}/\Gamma,\mathbb{Q})\cong H^{1}(T^{4},\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}^{4}
and

, therefore

and

∎

3.5.
Large finite group actions on admissible manifolds

One possible generalization of closed connected aspherical manifolds are closed connected admissible manifolds (see
[
LR10
, Definition 3.2.7]
), which are manifolds where the only periodic self-homeomorphisms of
\tilde{M}
commuting with the deck transformations group
\pi_{1}(M)
are elements of
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
. Closed connected oriented manifolds admitting a non-zero degree map to a torus or a nilmanifold are admissible manifolds. One could use the same arguments used to prove
[
DS25
, Theorem 1.6]
together with
[
LR10
, Theorem 3.2.2]
to obtain:

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
M
be a closed connected admissible manifold. Assume that
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski and that
\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
is finitely generated. Then:

1.

\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
is Jordan.

2.

\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)\leq\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)/\operatorname{Torsion}(\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)))
.

3.

If
\chi(M)\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski then
M
is almost asymmetric.

4.

If
\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski then
M
has few stabilizers.

Proof.

The proofs of items 1, 2 and 4 are the same as in
[
DS25
, Theorem 1.6]
. If
\operatorname{Aut}(\pi_{1}(M))
is Minkowski then
M
has small stabilizers by
[
LR10
, Theorem 3.2.2]
. Thus, item 3 follows from
2.10
and
2.11
.
∎

An application of
3.17
is the following. Suppose that
M
is a closed connected oriented aspherical manifold satisfying the hypothesis of
1.8
and
M^{\prime}
is a closed simply-connected manifold of the same dimension as
M
, then
M\#M^{\prime}
is a closed admissible manifold such that
\operatorname{Homeo}(M\#M^{\prime})
is Jordan,
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M\#M^{\prime})\leq\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(M)
and
M\#M^{\prime}
has few stabilizers. The discrete degree of symmetry inequality is usually strict. For example, assume that
M^{\prime}
is a closed simply-connected even dimensional manifold such that
\chi(M^{\prime})\neq 2
. Then
\chi(T^{n}\#M^{\prime})=\chi(M^{\prime})-2\neq 0
and hence
\operatorname{disc-sym}(T^{n}\#M^{\prime})=0<n=\operatorname{rank}\mathcal{Z}\pi_{1}(T^{n}\#M^{\prime})
, by
3.17
(3).

3.17
and
1.10
have a non-trivial overlap, but neither is more general than the other. On one hand, not all admissible manifolds admit a non-zero degree maps to a nilmanifold. On the other hand, there exist closed connected orientable manifolds admitting anon-zero degree map to a torus such that the outer automorphism group of their fundamental group is not Minkowski. In
[
Blo75
, Theorem 1.1.1]
it is proved that if
\Gamma
and
\Lambda
are isomorphic neither to a non-trivial free product nor to a infinite cyclic group then
\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma*\Lambda)\cong\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)\times\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda)
if
\Gamma\ncong\Lambda
and
\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma*\Lambda)\cong(\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma)\times\operatorname{Aut}(\Lambda))\times\mathbb{Z}/2
if
\Gamma\cong\Lambda
. In particular, if
M
is a closed connected 4-manifold with fundamental group the Baumslag-Solitar group
B(m,ml)=\langle a,b|ba^{m}b^{-1}=a^{ml}\rangle
with
m,l\geq 2
. Note that
B(m,ml)
is not a proper free product of groups (see
[
LS77
, II.5.13]
or) Then
\operatorname{Out}(\pi_{1}(M\#T^{4}))\cong\operatorname{Aut}(B(m,ml))\times\operatorname{GL}(4,\mathbb{Z})
is not Minkowski (see
[
CL83
, Lemma 3.8]
or
[
Lev07
]
), but
M\#T^{4}
is hypertoral and hence
\operatorname{Homeo}(M\#T^{4})
is Jordan by
1.9
.

4.
Free iterated actions

Recall that if
G
is a finite group acting freely on a manifold
M
then
M/G
is also a manifold and
p:M\longrightarrow M/G
is a regular covering. Moreover, we have a short exact sequence
1\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M/G)\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow 1
. Therefore, if we have an iterated free action of
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\}_{i=1,..,n}
on a manifold
M
, then every
M_{i}
is a manifold,
p_{i}:M_{i-1}\longrightarrow M_{i}
is a regular cover and the map
p:M\longrightarrow M_{n}
is a covering on
M
. A free iterated action of
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\}_{i=1,\dots,n}
on
M
induces a series of groups

\pi_{1}(M)=\pi_{1}(M_{0})\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M_{1})\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M_{n})

where
\pi_{1}(M_{i})/\pi_{1}(M_{i-1})\cong G_{i}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
be a free iterated actions of finite groups inducing coverings
p:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}
and
p^{\prime}:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
respectively. If
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M\sim\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
then there exists an isomorphism
\phi:\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
satisfying
\phi(p_{*}(\pi_{1}(M)))=p^{\prime}_{*}(\pi_{1}(M))
.

Proof.

Let
f:M/\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
be the homeomorphism provided by
1.14
. By construction, the coverings
f\circ p:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and
p^{\prime}:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are isomorphic. Therefore, there exists
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
such that
f_{*}(p_{*}(\pi_{1}(M))))=c_{\gamma}(p^{\prime}_{*}(\pi_{1}(M)))
. We can write
\phi(p_{*}(\pi_{1}(M)))=p^{\prime}_{*}(\pi_{1}(M))
, where
\phi=c_{\gamma^{-1}}\circ f_{*}
.
∎

4.1
shows that to study free iterated actions up to equivalence, we can focus on the inclusion
p_{*}:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G})
induced by the covering. For example:

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright M
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\curvearrowright M
be equivalent free iterated actions of finite groups inducing coverings
p:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}
and
p^{\prime}:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
respectively. Then
p_{*}(\pi_{1}(M))\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G})
if and only if
p^{\prime}_{*}(\pi_{1}(M))\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
.

In particular:

Lemma 4.3
.

A free iterated action of a collection of finite groups
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\}_{i=1,..,n}
on
M
is simplifiable if and only if
\pi_{1}(M)\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M/\mathcal{G})
.

Corollary 4.4
.

A free iterated action on a simply connected manifold
M
is simplifiable.

However,
M
being simply connected is not a necessary condition to have simplifiability of all free iterated actions on
M
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Any free iterated action on
S^{1}
or
T^{2}
is simplifiable.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright S^{1}
be a free iterated group action. Then
S^{1}/\mathcal{G}
is a closed 1-dimensional manifold and hence
S^{1}/\mathcal{G}\cong S^{1}
. This implies that
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\mathbb{Z}\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(S^{1}/\mathcal{G})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and hence the free iterated group action is simplifiable. All the groups of
\mathcal{G}
are cyclic and the simplification is given by group action of a cyclic group of order
|\mathcal{G}|
.

The proof for the second case is similar. Assume that we have a free iterated group action
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright T^{2}
. Then
T^{2}/\mathcal{G}
is homeomorphic to
T^{2}
or the Klein bottle
K
. Then the result follows from the fact that any subgroup of
\pi_{1}(T^{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
or
\pi_{1}(K)\cong\mathbb{Z}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}
isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is normal, hence the action is simplifiable.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

4.5
cannot be extended to
T^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. Consider the Bieberbach group with presentation

\Gamma=\langle t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\alpha|[t_{i},t_{j}]=e\text{ $\forall i,j$},\alpha^{3}=t_{1},\alpha t_{2}\alpha^{-1}=t_{3},\alpha t_{3}\alpha^{-1}=t_{2}^{-1}t_{3}^{-1}\rangle.

The group generated by
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
and
t_{3}
is normal and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, which we denote by
Z
. Note that
\Gamma/Z\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
. Let
Z^{\prime}
be the subgroup generated by
t_{1}
,
t_{2}
and
t_{3}^{2}
, which is also isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. We have a normal series
Z^{\prime}\trianglelefteq Z\trianglelefteq\Gamma
with
\Gamma/Z\cong\mathbb{Z}/3
and
Z/Z^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
. On the other hand,
Z^{\prime}
is not normal in
\Gamma
, since
\alpha t_{2}\alpha^{-1}=t_{3}\notin Z^{\prime}
. Now we can define a free iterated action
\{\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}/3\}\curvearrowright T^{3}
such that
\pi_{1}(T^{3})\cong Z^{\prime}
,
\pi_{1}(T^{3}/\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong Z
and
\pi_{1}((T^{3}/\mathbb{Z}/2)/\mathbb{Z}/3)\cong\Gamma
. This free iterated action is not simplifiable.

We have seen that free iterated actions produce a covering map that is not necessarily regular. Conversely, given a finite covering map
q:M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}
we can ask whether there exists an iterated action
\mathcal{G}
on
M
such that
p:M\longrightarrow M/\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to
q:M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}
. The next example shows that this does not happen in general.

Example 4.7
.

Let
M
be a closed flat manifold with holonomy group the alternate group
A_{5}
. It exist because every finite group is the holonomy group of a flat manifold (see
[
Cha12
, Chapter III.5]
). Let
n=\dim M
. We take the short exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{5}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

Take a non-trivial subgroup
G\leq A_{5}
and consider the finite covering of closed flat manifold
q:M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M
, where
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})=\rho^{-1}(G)
. If
q
were induced by a free iterated action, then there would exist a group
\Gamma
such that
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})\leq\Gamma\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(M)
. This would imply that
G\leq\Gamma/\mathbb{Z}^{n}\trianglelefteq A_{5}
, which is not possible since
A_{5}
is simple.

Let
k
be a natural number and let
\operatorname{Cov}_{k}(M)
be the set of all coverings of
M
of
k
-sheets up to equivalence of coverings. Let
p:\tilde{M}\longrightarrow M
be a
k
-covering and pick
x\in M
. We can enumerate the points of the fiber
p^{-1}(x)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
. Given
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(M,x)
there is a unique lift
a_{i}:I\longrightarrow\tilde{M}
such that
a_{i}(0)=x_{i}
. Thus, we can define an element in the group of permutations of
k
letters
\sigma_{\alpha}
such that
x_{i}
goes to
a_{i}(1)
. If we remove the choice of the base point
x
, then
\operatorname{Cov}_{k}(M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),S_{k})/\sim
, where
S_{k}
is the permutation group of
k
elements acting by conjugations on
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(M),S_{k})
.

Assume that a finite group
G
acts effectively on
M
, then we have an action of
G
on
\operatorname{Cov}_{k}(M)
given by the pull-back of each element of
G
. Explicitly,
g[\tilde{M}\longrightarrow M]=[g^{*}\tilde{M}\longrightarrow M]
for all
g\in G
and
[\tilde{M}\longrightarrow M]\in\operatorname{Cov}(M)_{k}
. The induced action of
G
on
\operatorname{Hom}(\pi,S_{k})/S_{k}
is given by
g[f:\pi\longrightarrow S_{k}]=[f\circ g_{*}:\pi\longrightarrow S_{k}]
, where
g_{*}:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}(M)
is the group morphism induced by
g
on the fundamental group.

We also recall the lifting condition of continuous maps. Assume that we have
f:M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M
a continuous map such that
f(y)=x
for some
y\in M^{\prime}
. Then, there exists
\tilde{f}:M^{\prime}\longrightarrow\tilde{M}
such that
f=p\circ\tilde{f}
if and only if
f_{*}(\pi_{1}(M^{\prime},y))\leq p_{*}(\pi_{1}(\tilde{M},\tilde{x}))
. In particular, a homeomorphism
f:M\longrightarrow M
can be lifted to a homeomorphism
\tilde{f}:\tilde{M}\longrightarrow\tilde{M}
if and only if
f_{*}(p_{*}(\pi_{1}(\tilde{M},\tilde{y})))\leq p_{*}(\pi_{1}(\tilde{M},\tilde{x}))
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Assume that we have a free iterated action of
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
on a manifold
M
. Then, the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{G}
is simplifiable

(2)

We can lift
{g_{2}}:M/G_{1}\longrightarrow M/G_{1}
to a homeomorphism
\tilde{g}_{2}:M\longrightarrow M
for all
g_{2}\in G_{2}
.

(3)

The action of
G_{2}
on
\operatorname{Cov}_{|G_{1}|}(M/G_{1})
fixes
[p_{1}:M\longrightarrow M/G_{1}]
.

Proof.

We will prove the chain of implications
(1)\implies(2)\implies(3)\implies(1)
. If the action of
\mathcal{G}
is simplifiable then there exists a group
G
fitting in the exact sequence
1\longrightarrow G_{1}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G_{2}\longrightarrow 1
which acts freely on
M
and
p:M\longrightarrow M/G=p_{2}\circ p_{1}
. Given any
g_{2}\in G_{2}
we choose an element
\tilde{g}_{2}\in G
which inside the preimage of
G\longrightarrow G_{2}
. The induced homeomorphism
\tilde{g}_{2}:M\longrightarrow M
is a lift of
{g_{2}}:M/G_{1}\longrightarrow M/G_{1}
.

We now prove the second implication. Note that if we can lift
g_{2}:M\longrightarrow M
, then
p_{1}:M\longrightarrow M/G_{1}
and the pullback
g_{2}^{*}M\longrightarrow M/G_{1}
are isomorphic as regular
G_{1}
-coverings. If
[p_{1}]\in\operatorname{Cov}_{|G_{1}|}(M/G_{1})
denotes the class of the covering
p_{1}:M\longrightarrow M/G_{1}
then
g_{2}[p_{1}]=[p_{1}]
for all
g_{2}\in G_{2}
.

Finally, if
G_{2}
fixes
[p_{1}]
then there exists a group
G
of the form
1\longrightarrow G_{1}\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow G_{2}\longrightarrow 1
acting freely on
M
. This action extends the action of
G_{1}
on
M
and it also covers the action of
G_{2}
on
M/G_{1}
. Therefore
p:M\longrightarrow M/G=p_{2}\circ p_{1}
and the free iterated action is simplifiable.
∎

5.
The iterated discrete degree of symmetry

Recall that if
G
is a finite group, then
\operatorname{rank}G
is the minimum number of elements which are needed to generate
G
. We want to extend this notion to iterated group actions.

Definition 5.1
.

Given a free iterated action
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X
, the rank of the iterated action is

\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)=\min\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{rank}G^{\prime}_{i}:\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{G_{1}^{\prime},\dots,G_{n}^{\prime}\}\curvearrowright X\in[\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X]\}.

The iterated rank of the space
X
is

\operatorname{rank}(X)=\max\{\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X):\text{ free iterated action }\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that we have a free iterated action of
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\}
on
X
. If
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X
is simplifiable with a group
G
, then
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)=\operatorname{rank}G
.

Proof.

By definition,
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)\leq\operatorname{rank}G
. Since
\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X
is simplifiable there exists a subnormal series
G^{0}=\{e\}\trianglelefteq G^{1}\trianglelefteq\cdots\trianglelefteq G^{n}=G
such that
G^{i}/G^{i-1}\cong G_{i}
. In particular,
\operatorname{rank}G^{i}\leq\operatorname{rank}G^{i-1}+\operatorname{rank}G_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. This implies that
\operatorname{rank}G=\operatorname{rank}G^{n}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{rank}G_{i}+\operatorname{rank}G^{0}=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)
.
∎

Note that
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)
depends on the free iterated group action, see
5.8
below.

We also note that
l(X)\leq\operatorname{rank}(X)
and therefore we cannot bound the iterated rank of a closed manifold. To generalize the discrete degree of symmetry we will need a notion of rank which only uses abelian groups.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\dots,G_{n}\}
act freely on
X
and assume that
G_{i}
is solvable for all
i
. The abelian rank of the iterated action is

\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)=\min\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{rank}A^{\prime}_{i}:\{A_{1}^{\prime},\dots,A_{m}^{\prime}\}\curvearrowright X\in[\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X],\text{ $A_{i}^{\prime}$ abelian for all $i$}\}.

Note that
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)\leq\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\mathcal{G}\curvearrowright X)
. We would like to define an invariant of free iterated actions with similar properties to the discrete degree of symmetry. An essential property of the discrete degree of symmetry is that if
M
is a closed manifold, then
\operatorname{disc-sym}(M)<\infty
. The proof of this fact is a direct consequence of
2.17
. We can generalize this theorem to the context of free iterated actions. Recall that given a closed manifold
M
of dimension
n
, we define
b(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{rank}H_{i}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, where
\operatorname{rank}
is understood to be the minimum number of generators needed to generate
H_{i}(M,\mathbb{Z})
(note that
b(M)
is not the Betti number of
H_{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, since we also count the torsion part of
H_{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})
). If
p
is a prime number, then
b_{p}(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim H_{i}(M,\mathbb{Z}/p)
. The next results generalizes
2.17
to the context of free iterated actions.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
M
be a closed connected
n
-dimensional manifold. There exists a sequence of numbers
\{f_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
depending only on
n
and
b(M)
such that for any prime
p
, any sequence of positive integers
\{k_{i}\}
and any free iterated action
\{(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{i}})^{a_{i}}\}_{i=1,\dots,r}\curvearrowright M
, the numbers
a_{i}
satisfy
a_{i}\leq f_{i}
for all
i
.

To prove
5.4
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
M
be a closed connected manifold and
p
a prime number. Assume that we have an effective action of a finite
p
-group
G
on
M
. Then

\dim H_{k}(M/G,\mathbb{Z}/p)\leq\sum_{i+j=k}\dim H_{i}(BG,\mathbb{Z}/p)\dim H_{j}(M,\mathbb{Z}/p).

We refer to
[
SC19
, Lemma 1.46]
for a detailed proof of the lemma.

Proof of
5.4
.

We construct the sequence
f_{i}
recursively. For the first step, if
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}})^{a_{1}}
acts freely on
M
then so it does the group
(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{a_{1}}
. Thus, we can take
f_{1}=f(n,b_{p}(M))
, where
f
is the function defined in
2.17
.

By
5.5
, if
\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}}
acts freely on
M
, then

\dim H_{k}(M/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}}),\mathbb{Z}/p)\leq\sum_{i+j=k}\dim H_{i}(B\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}},\mathbb{Z}/p)\dim H_{j}(M,\mathbb{Z}/p)

for any
k
. Moreover,
H_{i}(B\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}},\mathbb{Z}/p)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p
for all
i\geq 0
(see
[
Bro12
, Chapter III, §1]
). Thus,

\dim H_{k}(M/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}}),\mathbb{Z}/p)\leq b_{p}(M)
for all
k
and therefore
b_{p}(M/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}}))\leq nb_{p}(M)
. Using this inequality recursively, we obtain that
b_{p}(M/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}})^{a_{1}})\leq n^{a_{1}}b_{p}(M)\leq n^{f_{1}}b_{p}(M)
.

We can use
2.17
on
M/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}})^{a_{1}}
to deduce that

a_{2}\leq f(n,b_{p}(M/(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k_{1}})^{a_{1}}))\leq f(n,n^{f_{1}}b_{p}(M)).

Set
f_{2}=f(n,n^{f_{1}}b_{p}(M))
. Repeating the same argument as above, we obtain

f_{i}=f(n,n^{f_{1}+\dots+f_{i-1}}b_{p}(M))

for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

By the universal coefficients theorem,
b_{p}(M)\leq 2b(M)
. Thus, by replacing
b_{p}(M)
with
2b(M)
we obtain a bound not depending on the prime
p
.
∎

We are ready to define an iterated discrete degree of symmetry for free iterated actions of length
2
. In
\mathbb{N}^{2}
we have a partial order relation called the lexicographic order defined as follows: If
(n,m),(n^{\prime},m^{\prime})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
then
(n,m)\geq(n^{\prime},m^{\prime})
if and only if
n>n^{\prime}
or
n=n^{\prime}
and
m\geq m^{\prime}
.

Definition 5.6
.

We define
\mu_{2}(M)
as the set of all pairs
(f,b)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
which satisfy:

1.

There exist an increasing sequence of prime numbers
\{p_{i}\}
, a sequence of natural numbers
\{a_{i}\}
and a collection of free iterated actions
\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{f},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}\}\curvearrowright M
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.

\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{f},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}\}\curvearrowright M)=f+b
for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

We define the iterated discrete degree of symmetry of

as

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=\max\{(0,0)\cup\mu_{2}(M)\}.

To understand better the definition of the iterated discrete degree of symmetry, let us compute it for tori.

Lemma 5.7
.

We have
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(T^{n})=(n,0)
.

Proof.

Let
C
be the Minkowski constant of
\operatorname{GL}(n,\mathbb{Z})
. Assume that
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(T^{n})=(d_{1},d_{2})
. Since
T^{n}
admits actions of
(\mathbb{Z}/p)^{n}
for any prime
p
and
\operatorname{disc-sym}(T^{n})=n
, we have
d_{1}=n
. By hypothesis there exists an increasing sequence of prime numbers
\{p_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with
p_{i}>C
and free iterated group actions of
\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\}\curvearrowright T^{n}
such that
\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\}\curvearrowright T^{n})=n+d_{2}
.

The action of
(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n}
on
T^{n}
is by rotations for all
i
, therefore
T^{n}/(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n}\cong T^{n}
. Since
p_{i}>C
the group
(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}
also acts by rotations on
T^{n}
, hence
T^{n}/(\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\})
is homeomorphic to
T^{n}
. In consequence, the free iterated action
\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\}\curvearrowright T^{n}
is simplifiable for all
i
. The simplification gives a group
G_{i}
and a short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n}\longrightarrow G_{i}\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\longrightarrow 1.

Moreover, we also have that a short exact sequence

1\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}\longrightarrow G_{i}\longrightarrow 1

induced by the regular covering
p:T^{n}\longrightarrow T^{n}/\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\}
. This implies that
G_{i}
is abelian and
\operatorname{rank}G_{i}\leq n
. On the other hand,

\operatorname{rank}G_{i}=\operatorname{rank}_{ab}(\{(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{n},(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{d_{2}}\}\curvearrowright T^{n})=n+d_{2}\leq n,

which implies that
d_{2}=0
.
∎

.

Remark 5.10
.

If

\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(0,0)
.

The next lemma shows a case where

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
M
be a closed manifold. Assume that

and

is Jordan. Then
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(d,0)
.

Proof.

Assume that

is the Jordan constant of
\operatorname{Homeo}(M)
and that
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)=(d_{1},d_{2})
. We have an increasing sequence of prime numbers
\{p_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and free iterated group actions of

.
We may assume without loss of generality that
p_{i}>C
. Since all actions are simplifiable, for each
i
there exists a
p_{i}
-group

acting freely on

which fits in the short exact sequence

. Since

is Jordan of constant

.

H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Recall that
\Gamma_{i}/\Gamma\cong\mathcal{Z}\Gamma_{i}/\mathcal{Z}\Gamma\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i}^{a_{i}})^{a}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{i}/\Gamma_{i}\cong q(\Gamma_{i})/q(\Gamma)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p_{i})^{b}
. Note that
\Gamma_{i}\cong(\tfrac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}\mathbb{Z})^{f}\times_{c}\mathbb{Z}^{b}
and
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}\cong(\tfrac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}\mathbb{Z})^{f}\times_{c}(\tfrac{1}{p_{i}}\mathbb{Z})^{b}
. The lattice
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime}
is generated
\{\tfrac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}e_{1},\dots,\tfrac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}e_{a},e_{1}^{\prime},\dots,e^{\prime}_{b}\}
and
\Gamma_{i}^{\prime\prime}
is generated by
\{\tfrac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}e_{1},\dots,\tfrac{1}{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}e_{a},\tfrac{1}{p_{i}}e_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\tfrac{1}{p_{i}}e^{\prime}_{b}\}
.

By
6.7
and
6.8
, for each
1\leq j\leq a
there exists a collection
\{w_{j,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of automorphisms of
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})
satisfying that
(w_{j,i})^{p_{i}^{a_{i}}}=\Phi(e_{j})
. Similarly, for each
1\leq j\leq b
there exists a collection
\{w^{\prime}_{j,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of automorphisms of
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})
satisfying that
(w_{j,i}^{\prime})^{p_{i}}=\Phi(e^{\prime}_{j})
.

Recall that
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
is an iterated skew-Laurent ring generated by
\{e_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{a}^{\pm 1},{e_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},\dots,{e_{b}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}\}
(see
[
GW04
, pg. xvii]
). Explicitly,
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}[e_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{a}^{\pm 1},{e_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},\dots,{e_{b}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1};\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{a+b}]
where the automorphism are defined using the cocycle
c
.

The main theorem of this part is the generalization of
[
MiR24a
, Theorem 6.1]
. Be aware that the ring
R
involved in the theorem below is in general not commutative. We refer to
[
Bel88
]
and
[
GW04
]
for an introduction to the theory of non-commutative Noetherian rings and, in particular, to the theory of localization on those rings.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
R
be a prime Noetherian ring such that any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is right localizable. Given an automorphism
\alpha:R\longrightarrow R
, we consider the skew-polynomial ring
R[z;\alpha]
. Suppose that
X
is a finitely generated
R[z;\alpha]
-module and that there exists a sequence of positive integers
r_{j}\longrightarrow\infty
and a collection of automorphisms
w_{j}:X\longrightarrow X
such that
w_{j}^{r_{j}}
coincides with the multiplication by
z
on the right. Then
X
is finitely generated as
R
-module.

Proof.

Let
S=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s}\}
be a generating set of
X
as a
R[z;\alpha]
-module and let
X_{0}\subseteq X
be the
R
-module generated by
S
. Consider the increasing sequence of finitely generated
R
-modules
X_{0}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq X_{2}\subseteq\cdots
defined by the condition
X_{i}=X_{i-1}+X_{i-1}z
. Multiplication by
z
induces surjective morphisms of
R
-modules
\mu_{i}:X_{i-1}/X_{i-2}\longrightarrow X_{i}/X_{i-1}
and thus we can define surjective maps
\nu_{i}=\mu_{i}\circ\cdots\circ\mu_{1}:X_{0}\longrightarrow X_{i}/X_{i-1}
. We have an increasing sequence
K_{0}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq K_{2}\subseteq\cdots
of submodules of
X_{0}
where
K_{i}=\operatorname{Ker}\nu_{i}
. Since
R
is Noetherian and
X_{0}
is finitely generated there exists a
i_{0}
such that
K_{i}=K_{i_{0}}
for all
i\geq i_{0}
. In particular,
\mu_{i}
is an isomorphism for all
i_{0}
.

Let
Y=X_{i_{0}}/X_{i_{0}-1}
. If
Y=0
then
X=X_{i_{0}-1}
and we are done. Thus we will assume that
Y\neq 0
and reach a contradiction. Since
Y
is a finitely generated
R
-module and
R
is Noetherian, there exists an increasing series of submodules
0=Y_{0}\subseteq Y_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq Y_{r}=Y
such that
Y_{i}/Y_{i-1}
is a prime module (see
[
GW04
, Proposition 3.13, Proposition 3.14]
). Let
\mathfrak{p}
denote a minimal prime ideal of the collection of the associated prime ideals
\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{r}\}
. We can now consider the right localization
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and we can also consider the localization
Y_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Since localizing is an exact functor (see
[
Bel88
, Lemma 1.3]
),
(Y_{i\mathfrak{p}}/Y_{i-1\mathfrak{p}})=(Y_{i}/Y_{i-1})_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Moreover,
R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is simple Artinian (see
[
Bel88
, Theorem 1.10]
and
[
Bel88
, Corollary 2.2]
), hence the length
\operatorname{lenght}(Y_{\mathfrak{p}})=\lambda
of the composition series is finite.

We will now prove that there cannot exists any
R[z;\alpha]
-module automorphism
w
such that
w^{r}=z
with
r>\lambda
by reaching a contradiction. Thus, assume that there exists an automorphism
w:X\longrightarrow X
such that
w^{r}=z
and
r>\lambda
. Consider the following
R
-modules defined recursively as
X^{\prime}_{0}=X_{0}
and
X_{i}^{\prime}=X^{\prime}_{i-1}+w(X^{\prime}_{i-1})
for
i\geq 1
. Using the same arguments as above, there exists a
i_{0}^{\prime}
such that
\mu_{i}^{\prime}:X^{\prime}_{i-1}/X^{\prime}_{i-2}\longrightarrow X^{\prime}_{i}/X^{\prime}_{i-1}
is an isomorphism for all
i\geq i_{0}^{\prime}
. Let
Y^{\prime}=X^{\prime}_{i_{0}^{\prime}}/X^{\prime}_{i_{0}^{\prime}-1}
.

It is clear that
X_{i}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{ri}
for all
i
. On the other hand, since
X
is finitely generated as a
R[z;\alpha]
-module we can write

w^{j}(x_{k})=x_{1}P_{jk1}+\cdots+x_{s}P_{jks}

for
j=1,\dots,r-1
and
k=1,\dots,s
, where
P_{jkl}
are polynomials in
z
with coefficients in
R
. Let
e=\max\deg P_{jkl}
. Then
w^{j}(x_{k})\in X_{e}
for
j=1,\dots r-1
and
k=1,\dots,s
. This implies that
X_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq X_{[i/r]+e}
for any
i
. Thus, we have inclusions
X_{i}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{ir}\subseteq X_{i+e}
.

The next step is to localize at
\mathfrak{p}
in order to use the length of the composition series. Firstly, we consider the inclusions
X_{i+L}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{(i+L)r}\subseteq X_{i+L+e}
where
L
is large number which we will determine below. If we fix
i
such that
i\geq i_{0}
and
i\geq i_{0}^{\prime}r
these inclusions imply that

0\leq\operatorname{lenght}(X_{i+L+e,\mathfrak{p}}/X^{\prime}_{(i+L)r,\mathfrak{p}})\leq\operatorname{lenght}(X_{i+L+e,\mathfrak{p}}/X_{(i+L)r,\mathfrak{p}})=e\lambda.

Moreover, the chain of inclusion
X_{i,\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{ir,\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq X^{\prime}_{(i+1)r,\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X^{\prime}_{(i+L)r,\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq X_{i+L+e,\mathfrak{p}}
imply that

(i+L)\lambda=\operatorname{lenght}(X^{\prime}_{(i+L)r,\mathfrak{p}}/X^{\prime}_{ir,\mathfrak{p}})+\operatorname{lenght}(X_{i+L+e,\mathfrak{p}}/X^{\prime}_{(i+L)r,\mathfrak{p}})

and

\operatorname{lenght}(X^{\prime}_{(i+L)r,\mathfrak{p}}/X^{\prime}_{ir,\mathfrak{p}})=rL\operatorname{lenght}(Y^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}}).

In conclusion,

\lambda/r\leq\operatorname{lenght}(Y^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}})\leq\tfrac{L+e}{Lr}\lambda.

Note that the lower bound is inside the interval
(0,1)
and if
L
is big enough then the upper bound also is inside the interval
(0,1)
, contradicting the fact that
\operatorname{lenght}(Y^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}})
is an integer.
∎

We want now to extend
6.9
to skew-Laurent rings. Thus, we consider the skew-Laurent ring
R[z^{\pm 1};\alpha]
, where
\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(R)
. We now construct an iterated skew-polynomial ring as follows (see
[
GW04
, Exercise 1R]
). First, we consider the skew-polynomial ring
R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}]
where
\alpha_{+}=\alpha
. We now define a map
\alpha_{-}:R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}]\longrightarrow R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}]
satisfying that
\alpha_{-}(\sum t^{i}r_{i})=\sum t^{i}\alpha^{-1}(r_{i})
. Using that
rt=t\alpha^{-1}(r)
and that
\alpha
is an automorphism, it is straightforward to prove that
\alpha_{-}
is an automorphism of
R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}]
. Thus, we can consider the iterated skew-polynomial ring
(R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}])[t_{-};\alpha_{-}]
. Consider the map
\mu:(R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}])[t_{-};\alpha_{-}]\longrightarrow R[z^{\pm 1};\alpha]
defined as
\mu(\sum t_{-}^{i}(\sum t_{+}^{j}r_{ij}))=\sum z^{j-i}r_{ij}
. As before, a routine check shows that
\mu
is a surjective ring morphism. Thus, if
X
is a finitely generated
R[z_{\pm 1};\alpha]
-module then
X
is finitely generated as a
(R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}])[t_{-};\alpha_{-}]
-module. Assume that there exists a sequence of positive integers
r_{i}\longrightarrow\infty
and a collection of
R[z^{\pm 1};\alpha]
-automorphisms
w_{i}:X\longrightarrow X
such that
w_{i}^{r_{i}}
coincides with the multiplication by
z
on the right. We define automorphisms
w_{i+}:X\longrightarrow X
and
w_{i-}:X\longrightarrow X
satisfying that
w_{i+}=w_{i}
and
w_{i-}=w_{i}^{-1}
for all
i
. By construction,
w_{i+}^{r_{i}}
coincides with the right multiplication by
t_{+}
and
w_{i+}^{r_{i}}
coincides with the right multiplication by
t_{-}
. Thus, we have:

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
R
be a prime Noetherian ring such that any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is right localizable. Suppose that
M
is a finitely generated
R[z^{{\pm 1}};\alpha]
-module and that there exists a sequence of positive integers
r_{j}\longrightarrow\infty
and a collection of automorphisms
w_{j}:M\longrightarrow M
such that
w_{j}^{r_{j}}
coincides with the right multiplication by
z
. Finally, assume that
R[t_{+};\alpha_{+}]
defined as above is also a prime Noetherian ring such that any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
is right localizable. Then
M
is finitely generated as
R
-module.

Recall that
\tilde{M}
denotes the total space of the pull-back of the covering
N\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
by
f:M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
. Since the group ring
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
satisfies the conditions of
6.10
(see
[
Lam91
, (A) Connell’s Theorem]
and
[
Bel88
, pg. 17, Appendix B]
) and they are iterated skew-Laurent rings, we can use downward induction to prove:

Corollary 6.11
.

H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-module.

Proof.

By
6.6
,
H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated as
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma
-module. Recall also that
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}[e_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{a}^{\pm 1},{e_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},\dots,{e_{b}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1};\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{a+b}]
. By
6.7
and
6.8
, there exists a collection of
\{w^{\prime}_{b,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of automorphisms satisfying that
(w_{b,i}^{\prime})^{p_{i}}
coincides with the multiplication of
e^{\prime}_{b}
. Thus, by
6.10
,
H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated as
\mathbb{Z}\Gamma\cong\mathbb{Z}[e_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,e_{a}^{\pm 1},{e_{1}^{\prime}}^{\pm 1},\dots,{e^{\prime}}^{\pm 1}_{b-1};\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{a+b-1}]
. We can repeat this process with each generator to conclude that
H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated as
\mathbb{Z}
-module.
∎

Part 6. Concluding the proof
The last step of the proof
6.2
is to prove that
H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is acyclic (that is,
H^{0}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
and
H^{i}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})=0
for all
i>0
). We will prove this statement by contradiction.

Assume that
\tilde{M}
is not
\mathbb{Z}
-acyclic. Note that
f_{*}(\pi_{1}(M))=\Gamma
implies
H^{0}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Hence if
\tilde{M}
is not
\mathbb{Z}
-acyclic there exists
j>0
such that
H^{j}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})\neq 0
.

By the universal coefficients theorem, there exists a prime
l
such that
\tilde{M}
is not
\mathbb{Z}/l
-acyclic. Let
k=\max\{j:H^{j}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l)\neq 0\}>0
. Since
H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z})
is a finitely generated abelian group, then
\operatorname{Aut}(H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l))
is finite. Let
\Phi_{(l)}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l))
be the group morphism induced by the action of
\Gamma
on
\tilde{M}
. The kernel of the morphism
\Phi_{(l)}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(H^{*}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l))
, which we denote by
\Lambda
, has finite index in
\Gamma
. Hence
\Lambda
is a lattice of
N
. Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{M}\times_{\Lambda}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{M}/\Lambda}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N/\Lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta}

where
\Theta
and
\pi
are the natural projections. Since
N
is contractible the map
\Theta
is a homotopy equivalence and
H^{j}(\tilde{M}\times_{\Lambda}N,\mathbb{Z}/l)\cong H^{j}(\tilde{M}/\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}/l)=0
for
j>n
.

To compute the cohomology of the other fibration we need to use the Serre spectral sequence. The monodromy action of
\Lambda
on
H^{j}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l)
is trivial, thus

E_{2}^{r,s}=H^{r}(N/\Lambda,H^{s}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l))\cong H^{r}(N/\Lambda,\mathbb{Z}/l)\otimes H^{s}(\tilde{M},\mathbb{Z}/l)\implies H^{r+s}(\tilde{M}\times_{\Lambda}N,\mathbb{Z}/l).

We have
E_{2}^{k,n}\neq 0
, but for dimensional reasons
E_{2}^{k,n}
does not belong to the image of any differential. Furthermore,
E_{2}^{k,n}
is not inside the kernel of any differential. Consequently,
H^{k+n}(\tilde{M}\times_{\Lambda}N,\mathbb{Z}/l)\neq 0
which is a contradiction because
\tilde{M}\times_{\Lambda}N\cong\tilde{M}/\Lambda
and
\tilde{M}/\Lambda
has dimension
n
.

Finally, we take the fibration

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{M}\times_{\Gamma}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N/\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

with fiber
\tilde{M}
. Since
\tilde{M}
is acyclic the Serre spectral sequence collapses on the second page. This implies that
H^{*}(M,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{*}(\tilde{M}\times_{\Gamma}N,\mathbb{Z})\cong H^{*}(N/\Gamma,\mathbb{Z})
as we wanted to see.

Corollary 6.12
.

With the same assumptions as in
6.2
.2, suppose also that
\pi_{1}(M)
is virtually solvable. Then
M
is homeomorphic to
N/\Gamma
.

Proof.

Firstly, recall that
f:M\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
induces a surjective map
f_{*}:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\Gamma
. Since
\pi_{1}(M)
is virtually solvable there exists a finite covering
q:M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M
such that
\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})
is solvable. We have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N/\Gamma^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N/\Gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
f^{\prime}_{*}:\pi_{1}(M^{\prime})\longrightarrow\Gamma^{\prime}
is surjective. Notice that
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(N/\Gamma^{\prime})=\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(N/\Gamma)
and that
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M^{\prime})\geq\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M)
. Consequently, we have
\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(N/\Gamma^{\prime})=\operatorname{disc-sym}_{2}(M^{\prime})
. The next step is to prove that
M^{\prime}
is a nilmanifold.

The acyclic manifold
\tilde{M}^{\prime}
has solvable fundamental group. This implies, since
H_{1}(\tilde{M}^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})
is trivial, that
\tilde{M}^{\prime}
is simply connected. Consequently,
\tilde{M}^{\prime}
is contractible by Hurewicz theorem (see
[
Hat02
, Corollary 4.33]
) and
M^{\prime}
is a closed connected aspherical manifold. The map
\pi:\tilde{M}^{\prime}\times_{\Gamma^{\prime}}N\longrightarrow N/\Gamma^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence. Since
\Theta
is also a homotopy equivalence we can construct a homotopy equivalence
M^{\prime}\longrightarrow N/\Gamma^{\prime}
. Since the Borel conjecture is true for nilmanifolds, we can conclude that
M^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to
N/\Gamma
.

Finally,
M
is also a closed connected aspherical manifold, since the finite covering
r:M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M
is obtained via pullback by
f
of the covering of nilmanifolds
N/\Gamma^{\prime}\longrightarrow N/\Gamma
, we have
\pi_{1}(M)\cong\Gamma
. Thus,
M
is homeomorphic to
N/\Gamma
, as we wanted to see.
∎

Remark 6.13
.

Using the same argument as in
6.3
, we can remove the assumption that
f_{*}:\pi_{1}(M)\longrightarrow\Gamma
is surjective. In this case, if
\pi_{1}(M)
is virtually solvable then
M
is homeomorphic to a nilmanifold and
\pi_{1}(M)
is commensurable to
\Gamma
.

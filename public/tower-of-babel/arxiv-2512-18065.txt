Simulation of depolarizing channel exploring maximally non separable spin-orbit mode
G.Tiago 1,2 , V.S. Lamego 3 , M.H.M Passos 4 , W. F. Balthazar 1,5, J. A. O. Huguenin 1,2

Abstract
Depolarizing Channel is one of the most important noise model and constitute a reliable benchmark quantum information field. In this work we present a simple way to emulate depolarizing channel exploring a vector beam in a compact linear optical circuit. The evolution of different states are successfully reproduced. Our results are in excellent agreement compared with the results obtained by the spin-orbit Solovay-Kitaev decomposition for Depolarizing Channel, also presented here for the first time.



I
Introduction

Quantum information processes are suceptile to decoherence, the main challenge for the realization of quantum information and quantum computation protocols
27
. Furthermore, it is very important for the study of the classical-quantum transition
33
. Modeling decoherence processes in quantum channels has a pivotal relevance once quantum networks require reliable channel for entanglement or quantum superposition distribution
6

26

Even Depolarizing Channel presents deleterious action on standard model of quantum Shannon theory, quantum communication network was achieved by combining two depolarizing channel to transmit classical information when they are combined in a quantum superposition
9
. Such as prediction of transmit information by means noise channel was verified experimentally
10
;
11
. Perfect quantum communication can envisaged by using entanglement breaking channels
7

8
. Such as results reinforces the relevance of studies on depolarizing channels.

On the other hand, degree of freedom (DoF) of light has proven to be a very robust platform to explore quantum features. By using polarization and transverse mode DoF we can built the well-known spin-orbit modes
29
that presents the same mathematical structure of a two systems entangled state
24
. These Bell-like modes violate Bell’s inequalities written for quantum entangled states
4
;
25
and can be seen as a suitable test bed apparatus for quantum computation through proof of principle for quantum gates
30
;
2
. In quantum communication, a BB84 protocol for quantum cryptography without shared reference frame was experimentally demonstrated
28
. The investigation of fundaments of quantum mechanics we can cite system-environment interaction
19
, contextuality
15
;
18
, quantum thermodynamics
21
, and transition from quantum-to-classical random walk
14
. By adding path degree of freedom, tripartite entangled state can be emulated
3
. Mixed states are also emulated by spin-orbit modes, as X-states
1
;
5
that were used to experimentally measure Discord on an intense laser beam
12
.

Degrees of freedom of light of intense laser beams were used to emulate quantum channel both in Markovian

regime. A very complete simulation of quantum channel with intense laser beam was the implementation of the Solovay-Kitaev (Sk) decomposition by using transverse mode as a ancillary system
20
. However, depolarizing channel was not emulated.

In this paper we present a very compact linear optical circuit to emulate the dynamics of a single qubit in a depolarizing channel. We compare the results with the implementation of SK decomposition with excellent agreement showing more robustness in teh results. The paper is organized as follow. Section II presents theoretical background. Section III presents the experimental proposal. The results are presented in Section IV where also we compare with unprecedented results of emulation of Depolarizing channel with SK decomposition. Concluding remarks are presented in Section V.

II.1
Depolarizing Channel

The depolarizing channel is well known in quantum information theory, when a qubit, with the density matrix
\rho
, interacts with this noise channel, it has a probability
\lambda
to be depolarized, or in other words , switched to the completely mixed state
\mathbb{I}/2

16
. Then, it has a probability
(1-\lambda)
to remain the same. So, the map of this evolution is

\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\lambda\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}+(1-\lambda)\rho.

(1)

We can write the previous equation in another format if we consider that we can write the completely mixed state
\mathbb{I}/2
as

\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}=\frac{\rho+\sigma_{x}\rho\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y}\rho\sigma_{y}+\sigma_{z}\rho\sigma_{z}}{4}.

(2)

If we replace the
\mathbb{I}/2
in Eq.
1
, we have the map

\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\left(1-\frac{3\lambda}{4}\right)\rho+\frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\sigma_{x}\rho\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y}\rho\sigma_{y}+\sigma_{z}\rho\sigma_{z}\right).

(3)

We can find the most common Kraus operators for this channel if we consider a
\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{3\lambda}{4}
, giving the map

\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\left(1-\lambda^{\prime}\right)\rho+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{3}\left(\sigma_{x}\rho\sigma_{x}+\sigma_{y}\rho\sigma_{y}+\sigma_{z}\rho\sigma_{z}\right),

(4)

where we see that the Kraus operators are

\displaystyle K_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\lambda}\,I=\sqrt{1-\lambda}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\,\sigma_{x}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}

(5)

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\,\sigma_{y}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle K_{3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\,\sigma_{z}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{3}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

With the Kraus operators and the fact that we can write
\rho
as

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{I}+\vec{r}.\vec{\sigma}\right),

(7)

where
\vec{r}=(r_{x},r_{y},r_{z})
is the Bloch vector and
\vec{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
is a vector with the Pauli’s matrices, we can study how the effect of the channel on the Bloch’s sphere, and we can calculate the outcome

\sum_{a=0}^{3}K_{a}\rho K_{a}^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{6}\begin{pmatrix}[3+(3-4\lambda)r_{z}]&(3-4\lambda)(r_{x}-ir_{y})\\
(3-4\lambda)(r_{x}+ir_{y})&[3-(3-4\lambda)r_{z}].\end{pmatrix}

(8)

II.2
Spin-Orbit modes

In order to emulate experimentally the interaction between a system and the environment, we will consider the degrees of freedom of light, the polarization and the Hermite-Gaussian transverse modes. It is known that polarization are related to the vector nature of the electromagnetic field, where we can write any polarization state considering the horizontal (
\hat{e}_{H}
) and vertical (
\hat{e}_{V}
) basis
\{\hat{e}_{H},\hat{e}_{V}\}\equiv\{H,V\}
, and for the HG modes, we can have a basis with the first order modes considering the spatial function of the HG oriented along the horizontal (
\psi_{h}
) and vertical (
\psi_{h}
) directions
\{\psi_{h},\psi_{v}\}\equiv\{h,v\}
. Therefore, this two degrees of freedom can be addressed independently, and a combined mode basis can be built from a tensor product of this two basis, and a beam with this two degrees are what we called Spin-Orbit (SO) modes. As discussed in Ref.
24
, the quantization of the electromagnetic field in this basis lead to a bipartite system of internal degree of freedoms of single photon. Considering an intense laser beam can be described as a coherent state with a macroscopic number of photons, we use Dirac notation to describe DoF of the most general SO modes as
24

\ket{\Psi_{SO}}=A_{Hh}\ket{Hh}+A_{Hv}\ket{Hv}+A_{Vh}\ket{Vh}+A_{Vv}\ket{Vv}.

(10)

where
A_{ij}
is the amplitude and
\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|^{2}=1
, with
i=H,V
and
i=h,v
being the polarization and transverse mode indexes. We can notice from Equation
10
that this modes can be separable and non-separable, i.e, we can factorized in product of transverse mode and polarization

\ket{\Psi_{SO}}=(A_{h}\ket{h}+A_{v}\ket{v})\otimes(A_{H}\ket{H}+A_{V}\ket{V}),

(11)

where we used
A_{ij}=A_{i}A_{j}
. In this case we say the SO is separable. For any other situation the SO mode is called non-separable mode. The quantization of electromagnetic field in such as basis lead to entanglement between internal degree of freedom of light
24
. Note that Eq.
10
present the same structure of a general bipartite system. Then, to
characterize the spin-orbit separability with a definition similar to concurrence

C=2|A_{Hh}A_{Vv}-A_{Hv}A_{Vh}|,

(12)

so, if
C=0
, we have a separable mode, but if
0<C\leq 1
is non-separable. The case
C=1
indicates a maximally non-separable mode and can be written as

\displaystyle\ket{\Phi_{\pm}}=\frac{\ket{Hh}\pm\ket{Vv}}{\sqrt{2}},

(13)

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{\pm}}=\frac{\ket{Hv}\pm\ket{Vh}}{\sqrt{2}},

that present the same mathematic structure of the maximally entangled Bell states. These maximally non-separable modes allows to study entanglement and quantum information with a intense laser beam and even with photons
29
;
4
;
25
. A complete study of partial separability of these modes can be seen in ref.
13
.

II.4
Coherence

As the main consequence of depolarizing channel is the coherence lost in the evolution of the system
(\lambda\rightarrow 1)
, Then, an important physical quantity to be evaluated is the Coherence. Here, we use Maximal Coherence (
C_{MAX}
) and
l_{1}-
norm Coherence (
C_{l_{1}}
)
31
.

Maximal coherence is defined as

C_{MAX}(\rho)=\sum_{s,s^{{}^{\prime}}}\left|\bra{s}\rho-\frac{\mathbb{I}}{d}\ket{s^{'}}\right|\equiv C_{MAX}(r)=r\penalty\ ,

(17)

where
r
is the radius of Bloch sphere and, therefore, is related with the purity of the state. For
r=0
, we have a maximally mixed state. For
r=1
we have a pure state
31
.

On the other hand,
l_{1}-
norm is defined as

C_{l_{1}}(\rho)=\sum_{s,s^{{}^{\prime}},s\neq s^{{}^{\prime}}}\left|\bra{s}\rho\ket{s'}\right|=2|\rho_{12}|\equiv C_{l_{1}}(r_{x},r_{y})=\sqrt{r^{2}_{x}+r^{2}_{y}},

(18)

that is base dependent. For instance, a qubit in a balanced superposition state presents
C_{l_{1}}(\rho)=1
and a state that does not present superposition in such basis have
C_{l_{1}}(\rho)=0
.

With
C_{MAX}
and
C_{l_{1}}
we can characterize states under effect of depolarizing channel and compare our experimental results with the expected by Quantum Theory.

. For this simulation, we only have one qubit. As in the Solovay-Kitaev simulation, our experimental qubit will be the degree of freedom of polarization, having the horizontal component as
\ket{0}
and the vertical one as
\ket{1}

HG_{00}

and

Experimental setup. S-plate prepare

We begin with a vertically polarized laser beam (532 nm) passing through a filter (F-1) to attenuate its intensity. Therefore, we can write the initial state of the laser as

\ket{\psi^{i}}=\ket{V}\otimes\ket{G}=\ket{VG},

(19)

afterwards, a Half Wave Plate with its fast axis making an angle
\theta
with the vertical direction acts on the state
\ket{\psi^{i}}
, generating the state

\ket{\psi^{1}}=\left\{\sin(\theta)\ket{H}\right\}\otimes\ket{G}+\left\{-\cos{(\theta)}\ket{V}\right\}\otimes\ket{G}.

(20)

The next step is to position a polarized beam splitter (PBS-1), where the horizontal component is going to be transmitted, leaving the state to be only

\ket{\psi^{2}}=\left\{\sin(\theta)\ket{H}\right\}\otimes\ket{G},

(21)

and the vertical is reflected

\ket{\psi^{3}}=\left\{-\cos{(\theta)}\ket{V}\right\}\otimes\ket{G}.

(22)

To create the state that will evolve under the channel, we will position Wave Plates in the arm of the reflected beam. The arrows indicates that we will position or not the Wave Plates depending on the initial state. For calculation purposes, we will consider our state as

\ket{\psi^{4}}=-\cos{2\theta}(\alpha\ket{H}+\beta\ket{V})\otimes\ket{G},

(23)

where
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
, in other words, is a normalized state. The states that we are going to study are the vertical
(\ket{1}\text{and}

\alpha=0/\beta=1)
and diagonal
(\frac{\ket{0}+\ket{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\text{and}

\alpha=\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})
. In order to generate the vertical state we don’t need any equipment, given that the PSB-1 reflected the vertical polarization to that path, and to generated the diagonal state, we position an HWP at
45^{0}
, transforming the vertical polarization into horizontal, and after that, position an HWP at
22.5^{0}
, transforming the horizontal state into the diagonal.

Now, we will focus on the transmitted beam, after the PBS-1, it passes through an S-wave plate, an optical element that can change the horizontal Gaussian beam into the maximal non-separable state
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{Hh}+\ket{Vv})

\ket{\psi^{5}}=\sin{(2\theta)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{Hh}+\ket{Vv}).

(24)

After the S-plate, the PZT gives a phase
\phi
between the paths. Moreover, to end the channel, the beams are put together in a beam splitter (BS-1), generating the state

\ket{\psi^{6}}=-cos{(2\theta)}(\alpha\ket{H}+\beta\ket{V})\otimes\ket{G}+e^{i\phi}\sin{(2\theta)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{Hh}+\ket{Vv}).

(25)

With our final state, we need to characterized it, this process is done by quantum state tomography,
in this case, our tomography will be only in the polarization state, and the bases used where the computational base
(\{\ket{0},\ket{1}\})
, the diagonal/anti diagonal
(\{\ket{+},\ket{-}\})
and the left/right circular
(\ket{L},\ket{R})

25

\begin{split}&\rho^{Pol}=\cos^{2}{(2\theta)}\left(|\alpha|^{2}\ket{H}\bra{H}+\alpha\beta^{*}\ket{H}\bra{V}+\beta\alpha^{*}\ket{V}\bra{H}+|\beta|^{2}\ket{V}\bra{V}\right)+\\
&+\frac{\sin^{2}{(2\theta)}}{2}\left(\ket{H}\bra{H}+\ket{V}\bra{V}\right),\\
\end{split}

(26)

which is similar to the expected map from the Depolarizing channel, so if we consider the polarization space, we can simulate the depolarization of one qubit.

V
Conclusions

In conclusion, we present the unprecedented simulation of the Depolarizing Channel with the spin-orbit modes implementation of Solovay-Kitaev decomposition. The results are in good agreement with the expected by Quantum Theory. The most important results of this work, however, is the proposal and experimental implementation of a compact linear optical circuit to emulate Depolarizing channel. The main idea is to increase the dimension of the Hilbert space of the system with an ancillary system and produce a state such that the partial trace in the ancillary produces the maximally mixed state of the system, and with it, we can perform a convex sum with the state of the system whose evolution we wish to study. In our case, our circuit explored the the maximally non-separable spin-orbit modes, that is analog of Bell state. By codifying the system in the polarization and use a S-plate we can perform exactly necessary transformations. Calculations of the unitary transformations of the circuit in the initial states show that the partial trace in the transverse mode is in excellent agreement with what is expected by quantum theory. The experimental results are in remarkable agreement with Quantum Theory. The results of compact circuit is much more robust of the ones pruduced by the circuit of Spin-Orbit Solovay-Kitaev decomposition. In addition, the simplicity of the circuit open an avenue to investigate superposition of Depolarizing Channel.

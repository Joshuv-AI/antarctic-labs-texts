Mixed-State Topological Order under Coherent Noises
Seunghun Lee, Eun-Gook Moon

Abstract
Mixed-state phases of matter under local decoherence have recently garnered significant attention due to the ubiquitous presence of noise in current quantum processors. One of the key issues is understanding how topological quantum memory is affected by realistic coherent noises, such as random rotation noise and amplitude damping noise. In this work, we investigate the intrinsic error threshold of the two-dimensional toric code, a paradigmatic topological quantum memory, under these coherent noises by employing both analytical and numerical methods based on the doubled Hilbert space formalism. A connection between the mixed-state phase of the decohered toric code and a non-Hermitian Ashkin-Teller-type statistical mechanics model is established, and the mixed-state phase diagrams under the coherent noises are obtained. We find remarkable stability of mixed-state topological order under random rotation noise with axes near the Y Y -axis of qubits. We also identify intriguing extended critical regions at the phase boundaries, highlighting a connection with non-Hermitian physics. The upper bounds for the intrinsic error threshold are determined by these phase boundaries, beyond which quantum error correction becomes impossible.

II
Background

In this section, we provide a brief review of the 2D toric code and the doubled Hilbert space formalism. We then introduce two diagnostics used in subsequent sections to analyze mixed-state topological order under coherent noises: the anyon condensation/confinement parameters and the coherent information.

II.1
2D Toric Code

Consider an
L\times L
square lattice with
N=2L^{2}
qubits on its edges. The Hamiltonian of the 2D toric code (TC) defined on this lattice is given by

\displaystyle H=-\sum_{v}A_{v}-\sum_{p}B_{p},

(1)

where
A_{v}=\prod_{e\in\partial v}X_{e}
and
B_{p}=\prod_{e\in\partial p}Z_{e}
are the star and plaquette operators, respectively [see Fig.
1
(a)]. The vertices and edges of the lattice are denoted by
v
and
e
, respectively, and (
X
,
Y
,
Z
) are the usual Pauli operators. Since
A_{v}
and
B_{p}
mutually commute, the ground states
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
satisfy
A_{v}|\Psi_{0}\rangle=B_{p}|\Psi_{0}\rangle=|\Psi_{0}\rangle
for all
v
and
p
. On a torus, the ground-state subspace is fourfold degenerate, which can be utilized as a code space encoding two logical qubits.

We introduce an expression for TC ground states, which has been useful in studying TC under decoherence

;
Chen and Grover 2024c
. Suppose that qubits are also placed on the vertices of the square lattice. The cluster state defined on this lattice is given by
Briegel and Raussendorf 2001

\displaystyle|\Psi_{c}\rangle\propto\prod_{e}(I+Z_{e}X_{v}X_{v^{\prime}})|\mathbf{z}_{v}=1,\mathbf{x}_{e}=1\rangle,

(2)

where
v
and
v^{\prime}
are vertices connected by edge
e=(v,v^{\prime})
,
|\mathbf{z}_{v}=1\rangle
is a tensor product of the eigenstates of
Z_{v}
operators with eigenvalue 1, and similarly for
|\mathbf{x}_{e}=1\rangle
. (The boldface indicates collective notation.) The TC ground state
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
can be obtained from

by performing forced measurements on vertex qubits

:

\displaystyle|\Psi_{0}\rangle\propto\langle\mathbf{z}_{v}=1|\Psi_{c}\rangle\propto\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{e},\mathbf{x}_{v}}\prod_{e}(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})|\mathbf{z}_{e}\rangle.

II.2
Doubled Hilbert Space Formalism

Here, we review the doubled Hilbert space formalism
Lee et al. 2022
;
Bao et al. 2023
;
Lee et al. 2023
, which views mixed states through the lens of pure states. For a density matrix
\rho
defined on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the Choi-Jamiołkowski (CJ) isomorphism maps
\rho
to its Choi state
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
, which is a pure state on the doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}\otimes\bar{\mathcal{H}}
, where
\bar{\mathcal{H}}
is a copy of
\mathcal{H}

Jamiołkowski 1972
;
Choi 1975
. More precisely, the (unnormalized) Choi state of
\rho=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|
is given by
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{i,j}|i,\bar{j}\rangle\!\rangle
, where
|i,\bar{j}\rangle\!\rangle\equiv|i\rangle|\bar{j}\rangle
and
\{|i\rangle\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. (The bar notation is for the copied Hilbert space.) A quantum channel
\mathcal{E}[\rho]=\sum_{a}K_{a}\rho K_{a}^{\dagger}
with Kraus operators
\{K_{a}\}
also maps under the CJ isomorphism into an operator
\mathbb{E}=\sum_{a}K_{a}\otimes\bar{K}_{a}^{*}
acting on
\mathcal{H}\otimes\bar{\mathcal{H}}
.

For a pure TC state
\rho_{0}=|\Psi_{0}\rangle\langle\Psi_{0}|
, its Choi state is given by [see Eq. (
3
)]

\displaystyle|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle\propto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{z}_{e},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{e},\\
\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v}\end{subarray}}\prod_{e}(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})|\mathbf{z}_{e},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{e}\rangle\!\rangle,

(4)

which possesses double topological order, i.e., its topological quantum field theory is the product of two
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
TC theories. Under local decoherence described by a channel
\mathcal{E}=\prod_{e}\mathcal{E}_{e}
, the density state becomes
\rho_{D}=\mathcal{E}[\rho_{0}]
and its Choi state
|\rho_{D}\rangle\!\rangle=\mathbb{E}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
may lose the double topological order depending on the decoherence strength
Bao et al. 2023
. From
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\!\langle\rho_{D}|\rho_{D}\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\mathbb{E}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
, the purity of the decohered TC can be written as
Chen and Grover 2024c

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]\propto\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v},\mathbf{t}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{t}}_{v}}\prod_{e}\omega_{e},

(5)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{e}\equiv\sum_{z_{e},\bar{z}_{e},z^{\prime}_{e},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{e}=\pm 1}\langle\!\langle z^{\prime}_{e},\bar{z}^{\prime}_{e}|\mathbb{E}_{e}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}_{e}|z_{e},\bar{z}_{e}\rangle\!\rangle

(6)

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\times(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})(1+z^{\prime}_{e}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}^{\prime}_{e}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}).

The purity Eq. (
5
) can be interpreted as a partition function of a stat-mech model of Ising spins (
x_{v}
,
\bar{x}_{v}
,
t_{v}
,
\bar{t}_{v}
) on the vertices of the square lattice, with Eq. (
6
) being the local Boltzmann weight on edge
e
. Therefore, we can examine the change in double topological order under decoherence by studying the effective stat-mech model Eq. (
5
). In Sec.
III
and
IV
, we compute
\omega_{e}
for coherent noises and determine the mixed-state phase diagram by examining the corresponding stat-mech model.

II.3
Diagnostics

Local quantum channels can always be purified to a finite-depth unitary circuit on an enlarged Hilbert space with ancillary qubits so that functions linear in density matrix (e.g.,
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}O]
for some observables
O
) are smooth in tuning parameters of the local channel
Lee et al. 2022
;
Lee et al. 2023
;
Fan et al. 2024
. Consequently, detecting phase transitions in mixed states decohered under local quantum channels requires considering functions nonlinear in density matrices, such as information-theoretic quantities. This section introduces two information-theoretic diagnostics that will be used throughout the paper.

II.3.1
Anyon Condensation/Confinement Parameters

Consider a density matrix
\rho_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\equiv w_{\alpha}(l)\rho_{0}w_{\beta}^{\dagger}(l)
, where
w_{\alpha}(l)
(
w_{\beta}(l)
) is a string operator creating a pair of
\alpha
(
\beta
) anyons at the endpoints
i
and
j
of an open string
l
. Letting
\rho_{D}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\equiv\mathcal{E}[\rho_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}]
, the
\alpha\bar{\beta}
anyon condensation and confinement parameters are defined as follows
Bao et al. 2023
;
Haegeman et al. 2015
;
Duivenvoorden et al. 2017
:

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|\alpha\bar{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{|i-j|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\langle\!\langle\rho_{D}|\rho_{D}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\langle\!\langle\rho_{D}|\rho_{D}\rangle\!\rangle},

(7)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\alpha\bar{\beta}|\alpha\bar{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{|i-j|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\langle\!\langle\rho_{D}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}|\rho_{D}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\langle\!\langle\rho_{D}|\rho_{D}\rangle\!\rangle}.

The condensation parameter
\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|\alpha\bar{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle
takes a nonzero finite constant value when
\alpha\bar{\beta}
anyons are condensed, suggesting that
\rho_{0}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}
indistinguishable from the ground state
\rho_{0}
under decoherence. It vanishes when
\alpha\bar{\beta}
anyons are not condensed. Meanwhile, the confinement parameter
\langle\!\langle\alpha\bar{\beta}|\alpha\bar{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle
vanishes when
\alpha\bar{\beta}
anyons are confined, indicating that
|\rho_{D}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\rangle\!\rangle
is not a physically normalizable state. It attains a nonzero finite value when
\alpha\bar{\beta}
anyons are deconfined. Note that condensing one anyon confines all other anyons with nontrivial mutual statistics, following the standard anyon condensation rule
Bais and Slingerland 2009
;
Burnell 2018
.

On a related note, the distinguishability between density matrices
\rho
and
\sigma
can be measured by the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)\equiv\mathrm{Tr}[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)]

Umegaki 1962
, which diverges when
\rho
and
\sigma
are orthogonal and takes a finite value otherwise. A useful related quantity is the Rényi relative entropy
Petz 1986
:

\displaystyle D^{(n)}(\rho\|\sigma)\equiv\frac{1}{1-n}\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\sigma^{n-1}]}{\mathrm{Tr}[\rho^{n}]}\right),

(8)

which recovers
D(\rho\|\sigma)
as
n\rightarrow 1
. The anyon condensation parameters are related to the Rényi-2 relative entropy as
D^{(2)}(\rho_{D}\|\rho_{D}^{\alpha\bar{\beta}})=-\log\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|\alpha\bar{\beta}\rangle\!\rangle
. Thus, when
\alpha\bar{\beta}
anyons are condensed (not condensed),
D^{(2)}(\rho_{D}\|\rho_{D}^{\alpha,\bar{\beta}})
becomes finite (infinite), indicating that
\rho_{D}
and
\rho_{D}^{\alpha,\bar{\beta}}
are indistinguishable (orthogonal).

The anyon parameters in Eq. (
7
) for the decohered TC can be represented as observables in the stat-mech model Eq. (
5
) as follows
Chen and Grover 2024c
:

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|e\bar{e}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|i-j|\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}\bar{x}_{i}x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}\rangle,

(9)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle e\bar{I}|e\bar{I}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|i-j|\rightarrow\infty}\langle x_{i}t_{i}x_{j}t_{j}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|m\bar{m}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|\tilde{i}-\tilde{j}|\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{x}\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{\bar{x}}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{x}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{\bar{x}}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle m\bar{I}|m\bar{I}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|\tilde{i}-\tilde{j}|\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{x}\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{t}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{x}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{t}\rangle,

where
i
and
j
(
\tilde{i}
and
\tilde{j}
) are the endpoints of the string defined on the original (dual) lattice. Here,
\mu^{y}
(with
y=x,\bar{x},t,\bar{t}
) is the disorder parameter of
y
spins

. We review the derivation of the correspondence Eq. (
9

II.3.2
Coherent Information

Let
Q
represent the TC and
R
be the two-qubit reference system that is maximally entangled with two logical qubits of the TC. To diagnose the amount of retrievable quantum information encoded in the TC after channel
\mathcal{E}
, one can consider the coherent information
I_{c}(R\rangle Q)\equiv S(\mathcal{E}[\rho_{Q}])-S(\mathcal{E}[\rho_{RQ}])

Schumacher and Nielsen 1996
;
Lloyd 1997
, where
S(\rho)=-\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\log\rho]
is the von Neumann entropy. Without decoherence (i.e.,
\mathcal{E}=I
), the TC achieves
I_{c}=2\log 2
, a condition necessary and sufficient for the existence of an exact QEC protocol. Since
I_{c}
monotonically decreases under channel
\mathcal{E}
, the noise strength at which
I_{c}
drops from
2\log 2
provides an upper bound for the error threshold of any QEC scheme. Thus, investigating
I_{c}
can reveal the extent to which encoded quantum information can persist under noise.

In practice, we consider the Rényi coherent information
Fan et al. 2024
:

\displaystyle I_{c}^{(n)}(R\rangle Q)

\displaystyle\equiv S^{(n)}(\mathcal{E}[\rho_{Q}])-S^{(n)}(\mathcal{E}[\rho_{RQ}])

(10)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}\log\left(\frac{\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{E}[\rho_{RQ}]^{n}]}{\mathrm{Tr}[\mathcal{E}[\rho_{Q}]^{n}]}\right),

where
S^{(n)}(\rho)=(1-n)^{-1}\log\mathrm{Tr}[\rho^{n}]
is the
n
th Rényi entropy. As
n\rightarrow 1
,
I_{c}^{(n)}
recovers
I_{c}
. In Appendix
A.2
, we derive the expression for
I_{c}^{(2)}
in terms of the stat-mech model [see Eq. (
34
) and (
36
)]. Although
I_{c}^{(2)}
does not give a rigorous error threshold of the code, it is relatively easy to compute and in many cases tracks the tendency of
I_{c}
. The error threshold obtained from
I_{c}^{(2)}
provides an upper bound for the true error threshold corresponding to
I_{c}
, at which the decodability transition occurs.

III
Random Rotation Noise

In this section, we study the decohered TC under random rotation noise. First, let’s clarify the terminology before introducing the model. We refer to noises where an operator
O
is applied with probability
p
, like the bit-flip error
\rho\mapsto(1-p)\rho+pX\rho X
, as “stochastic
O
noise.” By coherent noise, we mean noise that can produce a coherent superposition of different error states (with respect to Pauli syndrome measurements). Thus, stochastic noise is generally coherent, except for stochastic
X
,
Y
, and
Z
noises, which are incoherent.

A random rotation noise is represented by a quantum channel
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}=\prod_{e}\mathcal{E}_{e}
, with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{e}[\rho]\equiv\int_{\varphi_{e}}g(\varphi_{e})U_{e}(\varphi_{e})\rho U_{e}^{\dagger}(\varphi_{e}),

(11)

where
U_{e}(\varphi_{e})=e^{-i\varphi_{e}(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma})_{e}}
and
\int_{\varphi_{e}}\equiv\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\varphi_{e}
. Here, each qubit is independently rotated around the axis
\vec{n}=(n_{x},n_{y},n_{z})
by an angle
\varphi_{e}
following a probability distribution
g(\varphi_{e})
defined on
[-\pi,\pi)
[see Fig.
1
(a)]. Such noise often arises in current quantum processors due to imperfect gate operations
Kueng et al. 2016
;
Greenbaum and Dutton 2017
;
Bravyi et al. 2018
;
Venn et al. 2023
. We are interested in
\rho_{D}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}[\rho_{0}]
and consider an arbitrary distribution function
g(\varphi_{e})
.

The random rotation noise Eq. (
11
) is in general coherent and not stochastic. However, Eq. (
11
) reduces to the stochastic
\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}
noise with probability
p=\int_{\varphi_{e}}g(\varphi_{e})\sin^{2}\varphi_{e}
for special cases with even angle distributions, i.e.,
g(\varphi_{e})=g(-\varphi_{e})
(see Appendix
B.1
for a proof). Therefore, Eq. (
11
) includes previously studied incoherent Pauli noises, e.g., bit-flip and phase-flip noise, as special cases. See Fig.
2
(a) for a summary of the properties of the random rotation noise according to its angle distribution
g(\varphi_{e})
and rotation axis
\vec{n}
.

Since mixed-state phases of matter do not change under a finite-depth quantum channel
Sang et al. 2024
;
Sang and Hsieh 2024
, the mean of the distribution
g(\varphi_{e})
is unimportant because it can be set to zero via depth-1 global unitary rotation. This implies that a mixed state under an angle distribution symmetric about its mean is in the same phase as the mixed state under the corresponding stochastic noise [see Fig.
2
(b)]. One may expect that an asymmetric distribution might lead to a new phase. However, we show below that it also leads to the same mixed-state phase as the symmetric distributions within the doubled Hilbert space formalism.

(a) Summary of the properties of the random rotation noise Eq. (
11
), based on its angle distribution
g(\varphi_{e})
and rotation axis
\vec{n}
. Sec.
III.1
shows that, within the doubled Hilbert space formalism, it is sufficient to study even distributions satisfying
g(\varphi_{e})=g(-\varphi_{e})
. (b) Since a distribution symmetric to its mean
\phi
can be connected to an even distribution via a depth-1 unitary
U_{e}(\varphi)
, both distributions lead to the same mixed-state phase.

III.1
Mapping to Statistical Mechanics Model

Within the doubled Hilbert space formalism, we can study the mixed-state phase of the decohered TC under general angle distribution
g(\varphi_{e}
) by only considering the cases with
g(\varphi_{e})=g(-\varphi_{e})
. This simplification follows from the observation that the purity
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}]^{2}
depends on
g(\varphi_{e})
only through a single parameter

\displaystyle R\equiv 1-\left|\int_{\varphi_{e}}g(\varphi_{e})e^{2i\varphi_{e}}\right|^{2}.

(12)

Namely, all the information about
g(\varphi_{e})
is encoded in
R
(see Appendix
B.2
for a proof). For example, we have
R=0
for any global rotation with
g(\varphi_{e})=\delta(\varphi_{e}-\epsilon)
, and
R=1
for maximally random noise with
g(\varphi_{e})=(2\pi)^{-1}
. The distributions with the same value of
R
belong to the same mixed-state phase, and therefore we can focus on even distributions below. The phase diagram obtained from even distributions can be directly translated for general distributions by expressing
R
in terms of parameters characterizing the distribution (see Sec.
III.3
).

Under the CJ isomorphism, the channel Eq. (
11
) is mapped to
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}=\prod_{e}\mathbb{E}_{e}
, where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{e}=\int_{\varphi_{e}}g(\varphi_{e})e^{-i\varphi_{e}(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma})_{e}}\otimes\overline{e^{i\varphi_{e}(\vec{n}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{\sigma})_{e}}},

(13)

where
\vec{n}^{\prime}=(n_{x},-n_{y},n_{z})
is introduced for the copied Hilbert space. By assuming even
g(\varphi_{e})
, we show in Appendix
B.3
that the purity
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
in the thermodynamic limit (
L\rightarrow\infty
) is proportional to the partition function
\sum_{\mathbf{s}_{v},\bm{\tau}_{v}}\prod_{e}\omega_{e}
with

\displaystyle\omega_{e}\propto 1+J_{1}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}+J_{2}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}+Ks_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}},

(14)

where
s_{v}\equiv x_{v}\bar{x}_{v}
and
\tau_{v}\equiv\bar{x}_{v}t_{v}
are the Ising spins residing on vertices of a square lattice, and

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{R(\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\phi+\cos^{2}\theta)}{2-R+R\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\phi},

(15)

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{R(\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\phi-\cos^{2}\theta)}{2-R+R\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\phi},

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=\frac{2-R-R\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\phi}{2-R+R\sin^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\phi}.

Here, we parameterize the rotation axis as
\vec{n}=(\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta,\sin\theta\cos\phi)
, which is the spherical coordinates with the
Y
-axis as the polar axis [see Fig.
1
(b)]. We can rewrite Eq. (
14
) as a local Boltzmann weight
\omega_{e}\propto e^{\mathcal{J}_{1}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}+\mathcal{J}_{2}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}+\mathcal{K}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}}
. The three coupling constants
\mathcal{J}_{1}
,
\mathcal{J}_{2}
,
\mathcal{K}
are determined by
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
,
K
, and can generally be anisotropic and complex (see Appendix
B.3
for details). In particular, the model gains its non-Hermiticity as the
y
-component of
\vec{n}
increases. Consequently,
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]
is mapped to the partition function of the non-Hermitian anisotropic Ashkin-Teller (AT) model. This is one of our main results and we emphasize that this mapping can be leveraged to investigate decohered TC under random rotation noise with
any
rotation axis
\vec{n}
and
arbitrary
angle distribution
g(\varphi_{e})
.

From Eq. (
9
), the anyon parameters can be read as

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|e\bar{e}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|i-j|\rightarrow\infty}\langle s_{i}s_{j}\rangle,

(16)

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle e\bar{I}|e\bar{I}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|i-j|\rightarrow\infty}\langle s_{i}\tau_{i}s_{j}\tau_{j}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle I\bar{I}|m\bar{m}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|\tilde{i}-\tilde{j}|\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{\tau}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{\tau}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle m\bar{I}|m\bar{I}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{|\tilde{i}-\tilde{j}|\rightarrow\infty}\langle\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{s}\mu_{\tilde{i}}^{\tau}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{s}\mu_{\tilde{j}}^{\tau}\rangle,

which are the correlation functions in the model Eq. (
14
). The stat-mech expression for the Rényi-2 coherent information is given by

\displaystyle I_{c}^{(2)}(R\rangle Q)

\displaystyle=\log\left(\frac{\sum_{a,b=0,1}e^{-\Delta F_{s,\tau}^{(a,b)}}}{\sum_{a,b=0,1}e^{-\Delta F_{\tau,s\tau}^{(a,b)}}}\right),

(17)

where
\Delta F_{s,\tau}^{(a,b)}
is a free energy cost of forming defects in the interactions
s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}
and
\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}
along the non-contractible loop pattern
(a,b)
, and similarly for
\Delta F_{\tau,s\tau}^{(a,b)}
(see Appendix.
A.2
for details). Using these two diagnostics, we can determine the phase of the model Eq. (
14
) (and hence that of
\rho_{D}
) and determine the fate of the quantum memory under random rotation noise.

III.2
General Phase Diagram

In the following, we numerically map out the general phase diagram of the stat-mech model Eq. (
14

(R,\phi,\theta)

15

0\leq R\leq 1
and
0\leq\phi,\theta\leq\pi/2

;
Nishino and Okunishi 1996
;
Fishman et al. 2018

3
). The result is summarized in Fig.

\xi_{D}
increases as we increase the bond dimension

of the corner tensors (not shown). The model has the following three phases:

\langle s_{v}\rangle

m\bar{m}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
topological order with the

One can readily check that the behavior of

in Eq. (

and
\tau

III.2.1
Random Rotation Noise within
XZ
-Plane

When
\vec{n}=(\sin\phi,0,\cos\phi)
lies in the
XZ
-plane, the model Eq. (
14
) reduces to
\omega_{e}\propto 1+J(s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}+\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}})+Ks_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}
with

\displaystyle J=\frac{R\cos^{2}\phi}{2-R\cos^{2}\phi},\qquad K=\frac{2-R-R\sin^{2}\phi}{2-R\cos^{2}\phi},

(18)

which can be written as the Boltzmann weight
\omega_{e}\propto e^{\mathcal{J}(s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}+\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}})+\mathcal{K}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}}
of the isotropic AT model with coupling constants
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{K}\geq 0
. It is known that this model has three distinct phases (PO, FM, PM)
Baxter 2007
, which is in accord with the
XZ
-plane of Fig.
1
(b) and Fig.
3
. For the pure
X
- and
Z
-rotations, the model simplifies further to a 2D Ising model on a square lattice, with a phase transition at
J_{c}=\frac{1}{2}\ln(1+\sqrt{2})

Onsager 1944
;
Kramers and Wannier 1941
. Thus, the phase boundary for the
X
- and
Z
-rotations is
R_{c}=2-\sqrt{2}\simeq 0.586
, which belongs to 2D Ising universality and extends into two Ising critical lines for
\phi\neq\pi/4

Baxter 2007
.

The special cases with
(\phi,\theta)=(\pi/4,\pi/2)
satisfy the self-duality condition
e^{-2\mathcal{K}}=\sinh(2\mathcal{J})

Baxter 2007
. In this scenario, the model remains in the PO phase for
R<1
and reaches the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition point at
R=1
[green dot in Fig.
1
(b)]. This BKT transition was first discussed in Ref.
Chen and Grover 2024c
for the stochastic channel
\mathcal{E}_{e}[\rho]=(1-p)\rho+p(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma})_{e}\rho(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma})_{e}
(for which
R=1
corresponds to
p=1/2
), and our result greatly extends such transition for general distributions
g(\varphi_{e})
. It is shown in Ref.
Chen and Grover 2024c
that any transition in
|\rho_{D}\rangle\!\rangle
is beyond the conventional anyon condensation scheme if
\mathcal{E}
satisfies (i) electromagnetic duality (EMD) symmetry:
\mathbb{U}_{D}\mathbb{E}\mathbb{U}_{D}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{E}
for
\mathbb{U}_{D}=\prod_{e}\frac{1}{2}(X_{e}+Z_{e})(\bar{X}_{e}+\bar{Z}_{e})
, and (ii) partial transpose symmetry:
(\mathbb{E}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E})^{T_{\mathcal{H}}}=(\mathbb{E}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E})^{\bar{T}_{\bar{\mathcal{H}}}}=\mathbb{E}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}
. One can easily check that
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}
[see Eq. (
13
)] with
(\phi,\theta)=(\pi/4,\pi/2)
satisfies both the EMD and partial transpose symmetries. Therefore, this BKT transition is an unconventional topological phase transition for any
g(\varphi_{e})
achieving
R=1
. Such a case involves a non-stochastic noise
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{rot}}
with respect to asymmetric distributions with vanishing second Fourier coefficient, e.g.,
g(\varphi_{e})=(2\pi)^{-1}(1+\sin\varphi_{e}\cos 2\varphi_{e})
.

where

IV
Amplitude Damping Noise

In this section, we study decohered TC under amplitude damping noise. The amplitude damping channel models the decay process of an excited state of a qubit via the spontaneous emission of a photon. Specifically, the channel is defined as
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}\equiv\prod_{e}\mathcal{E}_{e}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{e}[\rho]\equiv K_{0,e}\rho K_{0,e}^{\dagger}+K_{1,e}\rho K_{1,e}^{\dagger}
and the Kraus operators are given by

\displaystyle K_{0,e}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\sqrt{1-\gamma}\end{pmatrix},\qquad K_{1,e}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{\gamma}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(22)

The damping parameter
\gamma\in[0,1]
depends on the rate of spontaneous emission [see Fig.
1
(a)]. The amplitude damping channel is a typical coherent noise in NISQ devices
Chirolli and Burkard 2008
and cannot be expressed as a stochastic Pauli channel. We are interested in
\rho_{D}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}[\rho_{0}]
.

As in Sec.
III.1
, we can compute the purity
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
, where
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}=\prod_{e}\mathbb{E}_{e}
is given by
\mathbb{E}_{e}=K_{0,e}\otimes\bar{K}_{0,e}+K_{1,e}\otimes\bar{K}_{1,e}
. In Appendix
C
, we show that the purity can be written as
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}]\propto\sum_{\mathbf{s}_{v},\bm{\tau}_{v}}\prod_{e}\omega_{e}
, where
s_{v}
and
\tau_{v}
are Ising spins on the vertices and the local weight is given by
\omega_{e}\propto 1+J_{+}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}-J_{-}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}+Ks_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}
with

\displaystyle J_{\pm}=\frac{\gamma(1\pm\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2},\qquad K=\frac{\gamma^{2}-3\gamma+2}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}.

(23)

For
\gamma\in[0,1]
,
\omega_{e}
with Eq. (
23
) corresponds to the local Boltzmann weight of the anisotropic AT model (see Appendix
C
for details). The phase diagram must be symmetric about
\gamma=1/2
since the stat-mech model is symmetric with respect to
\gamma\leftrightarrow 1-\gamma
. (This transformation exchanges
J_{+}
and
K
, which does not affect the partition function
\sum_{\mathbf{s}_{v},\bm{\tau}_{v}}\prod_{e}\omega_{e}
due to its invariance under the permutation of
s

V
Discussion

In this work, we explore the mixed-state phase of the decohered toric code under two representative coherent noises using the doubled Hilbert space formalism. For random rotation noise, we determine the phase diagram for any rotation axis and arbitrary angle distribution, significantly generalizing the previously studied incoherent noise models. In addition to the robust quantum memory observed under random rotations about axes near the
Y
-axis, we identify phase boundaries featuring several interesting properties, such as Ising critical surfaces and unconventional mixed-state phase transitions, which merit further investigation. For the amplitude damping noise, we uncover two mixed-state topological phase transitions governed by a single parameter
\gamma
.

We remark that the phase boundaries for the random rotation error identified here differ from the error threshold under global unitary rotation obtained in Ref.
Venn et al. 2023
, where the decoding problem maps to a Majorana network. This difference stems from practical constraints in standard QEC schemes, where syndromes are obtained by measuring check operators (e.g.,
A_{v}
and
B_{p}
) and Pauli recovery operations are applied. In contrast, our mixed-state setup addresses the intrinsic noise threshold of the decohered toric code, within which QEC is possible
in principle
, though the corresponding QEC protocol may require experimentally challenging operations. It would be interesting to determine the “practical” error threshold for coherent noises, such as amplitude damping noise, as done for global rotation error
Venn et al. 2023
. The stat-mech mapping technique and tensor network method employed in this work seem straightforwardly applicable in this direction.

As mentioned in Sec.
III.2.4
, it will be worthwhile to understand the precise conditions for an unconventional mixed-state phase transition. Moreover, an important open question is whether the structure of our mixed-state phase diagrams under coherent noises persists in the replica limit
n\rightarrow 1
. For coherent noises, exact diagonalization of the decohered density matrix, as done for incoherent noises
Lee 2024
;
Niwa and Lee 2024
, is challenging. Developing new analytical and numerical frameworks to access the replica limit directly would be valuable for further study. It would also be intriguing to explore whether coherent noises can induce novel mixed-state phases beyond those captured by the doubled Hilbert space formalism.

: While completing this work, we became aware of related independent work that studies the error threshold of the surface code under generic unitary rotation errors using the mapping to a disordered non-Hermitian AT model
Behrends and Béri 2024
.

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{e},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{e},\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v}}\prod_{e}\int_{\varphi_{e}}\mathfrak{g}(\varphi_{e})(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})e^{-i\varphi_{e}(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma})_{e}}e^{i\varphi_{e}(\vec{n}^{\prime}\cdot\vec{\bar{\sigma}})_{e}}|z_{e},\bar{z}_{e}\rangle\!\rangle

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{e},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{e},\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v}}\prod_{e}f(z_{e},\bar{z}_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}},\bar{x}_{v}.\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})|\mathbf{z}_{e},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{e}\rangle\!\rangle

with

\displaystyle f(z_{e},\bar{z}_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}},\bar{x}_{v}.\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=(1-R)\Gamma_{+}(z_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}})\Gamma_{-}^{*}(\bar{z}_{e},\bar{x}_{v},\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})+(1-R)\Gamma_{-}(z_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}})\Gamma_{+}^{*}(\bar{z}_{e},\bar{x}_{v},\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})

(43)

\displaystyle+\Gamma_{+}(z_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}})\Gamma_{+}^{*}(\bar{z}_{e},\bar{x}_{v},\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})+\Gamma_{-}(z_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}})\Gamma_{-}^{*}(\bar{z}_{e},\bar{x}_{v},\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}),

where
\Gamma_{\pm}(z_{e},x_{v},x_{v^{\prime}})\equiv z_{e}(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(z_{e}\pm n_{z})\pm z_{e}(1-z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(z_{e}n_{x}-in_{y})
and

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\equiv 1-\int_{\varphi_{e}}\mathfrak{g}(\varphi_{e})e^{\pm 2i\varphi_{e}}=1-\left|\int_{\varphi_{e}}g(\varphi_{e})e^{2i\varphi_{e}}\right|^{2},

(44)

which is Eq. (
12
). From Eqs. (
41
) to (
43
), it is clear that

depends on

only through a parameter

.

B.3
Details on Mapping to Statistical Mechanics Model

Here, we fill in the details of the mapping to the stat-mech model Eq. (
14
) discussed in Section.
III.1
by assuming even distribution
g(\varphi_{e})
. We start from Eq. (
41
):
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{rot},\mathfrak{g}}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}=g_{-}*g
is even when
g
is even. Let
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
denote the average with respect to
\mathfrak{g}
. Using
\langle\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
, we can expand
\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{g}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle\propto\prod_{e}\big[1+\lambda(n_{x}^{2}X_{e}\bar{X}_{e}-n_{y}^{2}Y_{e}\bar{Y}_{e}+n_{z}^{2}Z_{e}\bar{Z}_{e})

(45)

\displaystyle+\lambda n_{x}n_{y}(Y_{e}\bar{X}_{e}-X_{e}\bar{Y}_{e})+\lambda n_{y}n_{z}(Y_{e}\bar{Z}_{e}-Z_{e}\bar{Y}_{e})+\lambda n_{z}n_{x}(Z_{e}\bar{X}_{e}+X_{e}\bar{Z}_{e})\big],

where
\lambda\equiv\langle\cos^{2}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}/\langle\sin^{2}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let’s first analyze the range of
\lambda
. We have
\lambda=(1-2\pi a_{2})/(1+2\pi a_{2})
with
a_{2}\equiv(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\varphi_{e}}\mathfrak{g}(\varphi_{e})e^{2i\varphi_{e}}
being the second Fourier coefficient of
\mathfrak{g}
, which must be non-negative by the convolution theorem. Combining this with
|a_{2}|\leq(2\pi)^{-1}
, we obtain
\lambda\in[0,1]
.

Now, we argue that the six cross-terms in Eq. (
45
) (e.g.,
Y_{e}\bar{X}_{e},Z_{e}\bar{X}_{e},\dots
) can be neglected in the thermodynamic limit. Consider sandwiching
\mathbb{E}_{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|
and
|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
. Note that
\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\prod_{e\in l}Z_{e}\bar{Z}_{e}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle=1
(
\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\prod_{e\in\tilde{l}}X_{e}\bar{X}_{e}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle=1
) only when
l
(
\tilde{l}
) forms closed loops in the original lattice
\mathcal{L}
(dual lattice
\mathcal{D}
). Meanwhile, for
Y_{e}\bar{Y}_{e}
and the six cross-terms in Eq. (
45
) (denoting these operators by
A_{e}\bar{B}_{e}
), we have
\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\prod_{e\in l}A_{e}\bar{B}_{e}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle=1
only if
l
forms closed loops along diagonals of the lattice. All the other terms appearing in the expansion of the product Eq. (
45
) vanish. From these properties of the TC, the purity
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{rot},\mathfrak{g}}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\cos^{2}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}^{N}\cdot\left(\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}+\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(\mathcal{L})}\right),

(46)

where the
\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}
is the sum of all non-vanishing terms consisting of non-cross terms (
1
,
X_{e}\bar{X}_{e}
,
Y_{e}\bar{Y}_{e}
,
Z_{e}\bar{Z}_{e}
), and
\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(\mathcal{L})}
is the sum of all other non-vanishing terms containing at least one cross-term. The superscript
(\mathcal{L})
indicates that the terms are with respect to the lattice
\mathcal{L}
. Now, observe that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(\mathcal{L})}}{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N_{d}}6^{l}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}}\Lambda^{|S|}\cdot\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L}\setminus S)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}}\right|,

(47)

where
\Lambda\equiv\lambda\cdot\max\{n_{x}n_{y},n_{y}n_{z},n_{z}n_{x}\}
. Here,
N_{d}
is the number of diagonal loops on
\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{S}_{l}
is the set of subsets of qubits on
\mathcal{L}
that are unions of
l
diagonal loops (with qubits appearing even times excluded), and
\mathcal{L}\setminus S
is the lattice obtained from
\mathcal{L}
by removing qubits in
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}
. Note that
|S|\sim L_{d}l
for
S\in\mathcal{S}_{l}
, where
L_{d}
is the length of the shortest diagonal loop. Since all terms in
\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L}\setminus S)}
are included in
\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}
, we can write
\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}=\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L}\setminus S)}+\mathcal{Z}_{\text{rem}}
for some remainder
\mathcal{Z}_{\text{rem}}
. Note that all non-vanishing terms in
\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}
are non-negative since the lengths of the diagonal loops are even. Then, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L}\setminus S)}}{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}}\right|=\left|\frac{1}{1+\mathcal{Z}_{\text{rem}}/\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L}\setminus S)}}\right|\leq 1,

(48)

and consequently

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{2}^{(\mathcal{L})}}{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{l=1}^{N_{d}}\binom{N_{d}}{l}(6\Lambda^{-L_{d}})^{l}=(1+6\Lambda^{-L_{d}})^{N_{d}}-1=\mathcal{O}(\text{poly}(L)e^{-L}),

(49)

since
L_{d}=\mathcal{O}(L)
in genera and
\max\{n_{x}n_{y},n_{y}n_{z},n_{z}n_{x}\}\leq 1/2
under the constraint
|\vec{n}|=1
so that
\Lambda\leq 1/2
. [When the size of
\mathcal{L}
is
L_{x}\times L_{y}
with
\gcd(L_{x},L_{y})=1
, one gets
L_{d}=L^{2}
, which greatly enhances the suppression.] Therefore,
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]\propto\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{(\mathcal{L})}
as
L\rightarrow\infty
, justifying that the six cross-terms in Eq. (
45
) can be safely neglected when discussing the phase of the model.

Now that
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]\propto\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\!\prod_{e}\!\left[1+\lambda(n_{x}^{2}X_{e}\bar{X}_{e}-n_{y}^{2}Y_{e}\bar{Y}_{e}+n_{z}^{2}Z_{e}\bar{Z}_{e})\right]\!\!|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
, we can use the identities
X_{e}|z_{e}\rangle=|{-}z_{e}\rangle
,
Y_{e}|z_{e}\rangle=i^{z_{e}}|{-}z_{e}\rangle
and
Z_{e}|z_{e}\rangle=z_{e}|z_{e}\rangle
to yield

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{\mathbf{z}_{e},\bar{\mathbf{z}}_{e}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v}}\sum_{\mathbf{t}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{t}}_{v}}\prod_{e}(1+z_{e}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}})

(50)

\displaystyle\times\left[(1+\lambda n_{z}^{2}z_{e}\bar{z}_{e})(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})+\lambda(n_{x}^{2}-i^{-z_{e}-\bar{z}_{e}}n_{y}^{2})(1-z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1-\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})\right]

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v},\mathbf{t}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{t}}_{v}}\prod_{e}\omega_{e},

with

\displaystyle\omega_{e}

\displaystyle\propto 1+x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda n_{z}^{2}}{1+\lambda n_{x}^{2}}(x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}+t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}+\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}+x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})

(51)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt+\frac{1-\lambda n_{x}^{2}}{1+\lambda n_{x}^{2}}(x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}+\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}})+\frac{\lambda n_{y}^{2}}{1+\lambda n_{x}^{2}}(x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}+t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}-x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}-\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}).

Since Eq. (

, we can let
\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}=x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}
. Introducing new Ising variables
s_{v}=x_{v}\bar{x}_{v}
and
\tau_{v}=\bar{x}_{v}t_{v}
and substituting
\lambda=R/(2-R)
(which can be derived from
R=2\langle\sin^{2}\varphi\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
), we finally obtain Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
):

\displaystyle\omega_{e}\propto 1+\frac{R(n_{z}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})}{2-R+Rn_{x}^{2}}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}+\frac{R(n_{z}^{2}-n_{y}^{2})}{2-R+Rn_{x}^{2}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}+\frac{2-R-Rn_{x}^{2}}{2-R+Rn_{x}^{2}}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}.

(52)

Writing Eq. (
52
) as a local Boltzmann weight
\omega_{e}\propto e^{\mathcal{J}_{1}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}+\mathcal{J}_{2}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}+\mathcal{K}s_{v}s_{v^{\prime}}\tau_{v}\tau_{v^{\prime}}}
, it must be

\displaystyle\frac{\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{1})+\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{2})\tanh(\mathcal{K})}{1+\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{1})\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{2})\tanh(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle=\frac{R(n_{z}^{2}+n_{y}^{2})}{2-R+Rn_{x}^{2}},

(53)

\displaystyle\frac{\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{2})+\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{1})\tanh(\mathcal{K})}{1+\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{1})\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{2})\tanh(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle=\frac{R(n_{z}^{2}-n_{y}^{2})}{2-R+Rn_{x}^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{\tanh(K)+\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{1})\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{2})}{1+\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{1})\tanh(\mathcal{J}_{2})\tanh(\mathcal{K})}

\displaystyle=\frac{2-R-Rn_{x}^{2}}{2-R+Rn_{x}^{2}},

whose solution is imaginary in general. Therefore, the purity of the TC under random rotation noise is proportional to the partition function of the non-Hermitian anisotropic AT model.

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{1}{l_{x}}\sum_{p=-l_{x}}^{l_{x}}p\binom{2l_{x}}{l_{1}+p}\epsilon^{2l_{y}p}}{\sum_{p=-l_{x}}^{l_{x}}\binom{2l_{x}}{l_{x}+p}\epsilon^{2l_{y}p}}=\frac{\epsilon^{2l_{y}}-1}{\epsilon^{2l_{y}}+1}.

56

Appendix C
Details on Amplitude Damping Noise

In this appendix, we elaborate on the details of the mapping to the stat-mech model discussed in Section.
IV
. We start by computing
\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}
for the amplitude damping channel. From
\mathbb{E}_{e}=K_{0,e}\otimes\bar{K}_{0,e}+K_{1,e}\otimes\bar{K}_{1,e}
, one can easily show that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}\propto\prod_{e}\Bigg[1+\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-2\gamma+2}(Z_{e}+\bar{Z}_{e})+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}-2\gamma+2}\left(\gamma^{2}Z_{e}\bar{Z}_{e}+\gamma X_{e}\bar{X}_{e}-\gamma Y_{e}\bar{Y}_{e}\right)\Bigg].

(65)

From Eq. (
4
) and the identities
X_{e}|z_{e}\rangle=|{-}z_{e}\rangle
,
Y_{e}|z_{e}\rangle=i^{z_{e}}|{-}z_{e}\rangle
, and
Z_{e}|z_{e}\rangle=z_{e}|z_{e}\rangle
, it follows that
\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{D}^{2}]=\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}^{\dagger}\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{amp}}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle\propto\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v},\mathbf{t}_{v},\bar{\mathbf{t}}_{v}}\prod_{e}\omega_{e}
, where

\displaystyle\omega_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z_{e},\bar{z}_{e}=\pm 1}(1+z_{e}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\times\left[\left(1+\frac{\gamma^{2}z_{e}\bar{z}_{e}+\gamma(1-\gamma)(z_{e}+\bar{z}_{e})}{\gamma^{2}-2\gamma+2}\right)(1+z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1+\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})+\frac{\gamma(1-i^{-z_{e}-\bar{z}_{e}})}{\gamma^{2}-2\gamma+2}(1-z_{e}x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}})(1-\bar{z}_{e}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}})\right]

\displaystyle\propto 1+x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}+\frac{\gamma^{2}-3\gamma+2}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}(x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}+\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}})+\frac{\gamma(1+\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}(x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}+t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}})

(66)

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt+\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}(x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}+\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}+t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}+\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}-x_{v}x_{v^{\prime}}\bar{t}_{v}\bar{t}_{v^{\prime}}-\bar{x}_{v}\bar{x}_{v^{\prime}}t_{v}t_{v^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\propto 1+\frac{\gamma(1+\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}CA-\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}BC+\frac{\gamma^{2}-3\gamma+2}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}AB+\frac{\gamma(1-\gamma)}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma+2}(A+B+C+ABC),

Molecular protocol for genome-wide and cell-type-selective profiling of in vivo small noncoding RNA:target RNA interactions by CIMERA-seq
Authors: Xinbei Li, Emily A Eiss, William T Mills, Mollie K Meffert
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummarySmall noncoding RNAs (sncRNAs) can regulate gene expression by guiding the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) to targeted transcripts for translational repression and/or target destabilization. Here, we present a robust benchtop protocol, termed CIMERA-seq, for the unambiguous profiling of sncRNA:target RNA interactions in a genome-wide and cell-type-selective manner. We describe steps for in vivo crosslinking and harvesting tissue, immunoprecipitation and covalent ligation of sncRNAs to target RNAs within the RISC, and sequencing of the resulting chimeric sncRNA:target RNA interactions.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Li et al.1Subject areas: Molecular Biology, Neuroscience, Protein Biochemistry

Before you begin

This protocol provides the stepwise strategies for sequencing library construction and genome-wide profiling of sncRNA:target RNA interactions, including the adaptations used to conduct profiling in a cell-type selective manner. Mouse forebrain is used as an example tissue, with cell-type selectivity demonstrated for murine neurons using a transgenic line expressing an inducible Cre Recombinase (CamK2⍺-CreERT2) in excitatory neurons. We have also successfully used a cell-selective approach in the mouse peripheral nervous system. While other cell-type selective settings have not yet been tested, we anticipate that this approach will be suitable for the use of other Cre drivers including for cell types outside the nervous system, and for systems in which the Cre is delivered by other means, such as by viral transduction. Before beginning sequencing library construction, the following preparatory steps, including mouse line generation and tagged Argonaute2 (tAgo2) induction, should be completed.

Photo-crosslinking with ultraviolet light – Day 1

Timing: 1 h

Diagrams of library construction steps for sncRNA:target RNA interaction profiling

(A) Irradiate biological samples using 254 nm wavelength ultraviolet light; RNAs are crosslinked to Argonaute within the endogenous RISC.

(B) Crosslinked samples are lysed and treated with RNase 1 to trim back the RNAs bound to Argonaute.

(C) Samples are IP’d using anti-Ago2 or anti-myc if performing cell-selective sncRNA:target RNA interaction profiling.

(D) Phosphorylate the 5′ end of the target RNAs with PNK 5′ phosphorylation.

(E) The 5′ end of the target RNA is ligated to the 3′ end of the sncRNA to form a chimeric RNA.

(F) Remove phosphate group at the 3′ end of the chimeric RNAs with phosphatase.

(G) A DNA adapter is ligated to the 3′ end of the chimeric RNA to allow for PCR amplification and sequencing on the Illumina platform.

(H) Phosphorylate the 5′ end of chimeric RNAs with PNK 5′ phosphorylation.

Expected outcomes

The final product of this protocol is a cDNA library containing the sncRNA:target chimeras flanked by 5′ and 3′ adapters with random nucleotides adjacent to the chimera. The index sequence is at the 3′ end (Figure 9). The sncRNA:target chimera library fragment inserts vary in size, typically ranging from 50 to 110 nucleotides in our hands, giving an overall cDNA length of 160–210 nucleotides after adding the index and adapters. However, the size of cDNAs in the library may vary depending factors such as RNase I digestion and intermolecular ligation efficiency. We recommend performing paired-end 150 (PE150) sequencing and a read depth of 150–200 million reads per sncRNA:target chimera library on Illumina sequencing platforms. Typically, quality control analysis of these libraries, both by fragment analyzer and by bioinformatic tools such as FastQC,7 indicates lower quality compared to standard libraries. This is to be expected, as the chimeric libraries typically have smaller inserts and lower nucleotide diversity compared to standard libraries. Refer to the troubleshooting section for details on how to increase nucleotide diversity and improve data output.

Diagram of the mechanism for reverse transcription and PCR amplification of chimeric sncRNA:target RNAs with flanking adapters and random nucleotides in CIMERA-seq

This figure is adapted from Figure S1 of the companion Cell Reports Methods article.1 P5 sequence is shown in green, P7 sequence is shown in orange. P5 and P7 are sequences that allow the library to bind and generate clusters on the Illumina sequencing flow cell. miRNA is shown in red, mRNA is shown in blue, and index sequence is shown in purple. The 5′ adapter random nucleotides (N) are shown in pink with a dash to highlight that the number of nucleotides in this position may be optimally adjusted depending upon sequencing platform and for size selection purification purposes. Increasing the number of random nucleotides can improve nucleotide diversity and sequencing depth by increasing the percent of reads passing filter in platforms such as NovaSeq X Plus.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Data from sequencing libraries can be processed using a bioinformatic analysis pipeline, such as SCRAP,3,4 to allow identification of sncRNA:target RNA interactions in a semiquantitative manner. Data from SCRAP can be analyzed for a wide range of purposes, such examining selectively enriched or depleted sncRNA:target gene interactions across different conditions or cell types. Bulk sequencing of sncRNA and mRNA, using RNA reserved from initial samples, can be conducted in parallel for quantification of targeting efficiency or other functional analyses.

Limitations

The described CIMERA-seq approaches enable genome-wide and cell type-selective profiling of sncRNA:target RNA interactions, but the depth of profiling can depend on starting material abundance. Particularly for low abundance samples, several parameters must be optimized for efficiency, including photocrosslinking, RNase I treatment, PCR cycle number, and library size selection. Working in a nuclease-free environment is essential; contamination introduced prior to reverse transcription can degrade chimeric RNAs. This protocol requires multiple consecutive days of experimentation, with a potential break after the third day.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Inadequate sequencing data output and low percent clusters passing filter on patterned flow cell platforms.

Potential solution

•Increase nucleotide diversity in the first 25 bases of the forward read. In NovaSeq X Plus, nucleotide diversity in the first 25 bases of the forward read is crucial and inadequate diversity can contribute to lower than expected library clusters passing the filtering stage (%PF) for sequencing. If the forward read begins with the sncRNA portion of the chimera, nucleotide diversity could be low due to a limited number of sncRNAs present. To address this, add random nucleotides (14–25) to the 3′ end of the 5′ linker. This ensures that the initially sequenced bases are of sufficient nucleotide diversity to pass filter specifications.

Increase nucleotide diversity in the first 25 bases of the forward read. In NovaSeq X Plus, nucleotide diversity in the first 25 bases of the forward read is crucial and inadequate diversity can contribute to lower than expected library clusters passing the filtering stage (%PF) for sequencing.

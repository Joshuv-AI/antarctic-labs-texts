Background

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma (ERMS) is a malignant soft tissue tumor with morphological and immunophenotypic features of embryonic skeletal muscle. Approximately one-half of embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma cases occur within the head and neck region and one-half in the genitourinary system. The tumor is common in children and adolescents and constitutes 20% of all adult rhabdomyosarcoma cases. It mostly affects children younger than 10 years of age, but it also occurs in adolescents and young adults [1]. The most common site is the head and neck, particularly the orbit and para meninges [2]. In our case the patient was a 19-year-old woman and the site was the parotid region, which prompted the clinician to consider the diagnosis of a primary malignant parotid tumor.

Case presentation

A 19-year-old Indian female patient visited the department of ear, nose, and throat (ENT) and radiology. The patient complained of pain and swelling over the left side of the face for 1 year. This started as a small swelling, progressed gradually, and extended to the region of the ear lobe. She gave no history of any systemic illness or trauma. General physical examination was insignificant, and her vital signs were within normal limits. On examination, the soft tissue growth was in the parotid region, large, about 20 × 15 cm, and nodular with hard and soft areas and focal ulceration (Fig. 1A). Because the swelling was progressively increasing in size and was in the parotid region, the provisional diagnosis of a malignant parotid tumor was made and differential diagnosis offered was a squamous cell carcinoma arising from the skin in that region. However, no lymph nodes were detected.

A Clinical image showing the tumor in the left parotid region. B Computed tomography scan showing heterogeneous mass lesion in left parotid region. C Fleshy, grey white tumor with focal areas of necrosis

Computed tomography (CT) scan was done and showed an ill-defined, lobulated heterogeneously enhancing mass lesion from left parotid gland with no involvement of the underlying bone (Fig. 1B). Hence the diagnosis offered radiologically was a malignant salivary gland tumor.

The tumor was excised and sent for histopathological examination. No lymph nodes were submitted.

Investigations

We received a skin covered mass of size 18 × 15 × 10 cm, along with a few separate gray white tumor bits. Externally, the skin showed multiple nodules and focal ulceration. On cut section, the tumor was fleshy, gray white with focal areas of necrosis (Fig. 1C). Microscopic examination revealed a malignant mesenchymal tumor in which the cells were arranged in fascicles and sheets separated by loose fibrous tissue (Fig. 2A). The tumor exhibited hypocellular and hypercellular areas. Individual tumor cells were oval-to-spindle shaped with moderate amount of eosinophilic cytoplasm and round-to-oval vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli. Occasional larger cells (rhabdomyoblasts) were seen having abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm and elongated nuclei (Fig. 2B). Mitotic activity was 10–19/10 HPF, and necrotic foci were less than 50%. The Federation Nationale des Centres de Lutte Contre Le Cancer (FNCLCC) score was calculated and was 3 + 2 + 1 = 6, grade 3.

A Microphotograph showing a cellular tumor with spindle-shaped cells in fascicles (hematoxylin and eosin ×100). B Microphotograph showing tumor cells with nuclear pleomorphism and rhabdomyoblasts (hematoxylin and eosin ×400)

The tumor showed foci of perineural invasion, lymphovascular invasion (Figs. 3A, B), invasion into normal salivary gland tissue, and invasion into the skeletal muscle (Figs. 4A, B). There was extensive surface ulceration with tumor involvement of the skin.

A Microphotograph showing invasion of tumor cells into normal salivary gland (hematoxylin and eosin ×400). B Microphotograph showing invasion of tumor into muscle

Differential diagnosis

The differential diagnosis was embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma and malignant nerve sheath tumor (MPNST) with rhabdoid differentiation. The sections were subjected to immunohistochemical analysis, and the markers used were S100, myogenin, and desmin. The majority of the tumor cells were positive for myogenin (Fig. 5A) and desmin was diffusely positive in all the cells (Fig. 5 B). S100 was negative in the tumor cells. Hence a diagnosis of MPNST was ruled out. On the basis of the clinical findings and radiology and histopathology features, the final diagnosis of embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma was given with the CAP protocol 2020 and staged pT3 pN2.

A Microphotograph showing myogenin positivity in tumor cells (immunohistochemistry ×400). B Microphotograph showing desmin positivity in tumor cells (immunohistochemistry ×400).

Treatment

The patient was treated with a regimen of cyclophosphamide, vincristine, irinotecan, and doxorubicin.

Outcome and follow-up: The patient was followed up and was doing well after 6-month and 1-year follow-ups. Imaging revealed no evidence of residual tumor.

Discussion

The head and neck regions are one of the most common locations of rhabdomyosarcoma. Salivary gland involvement is usually secondary to advanced disease, and presentation as a primary salivary gland tumor is infrequent. Rhabdomyosarcomas are usually seen in children and adolescents but more in children younger than 5 years of age, with a male preponderance [1].

In contrast, we describe a case of embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma in a 19-year-old female patient in the parotid region, which was diagnosed clinically as primary parotid tumor.

Embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma usually presents with symptoms related to mass effect [1]. In our patient, the presenting symptom was swelling with pain on the left side of the face.

Ohba et al. also described an exceedingly rare case of adult embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma arising in the head and neck, which was initially diagnosed as a primary unknown cancer [3].

Motallebnejad et al. described a pleomorphic rhabdomyosarcoma in a 32‑year‑old woman of the maxillary gingiva resembling an inflammatory process. This misdiagnosis led to suboptimal therapy, and later a biopsy revealed spindle cell sarcoma, and immunohistochemistry confirmed the diagnosis of rhabdomyosarcoma [4].

Dutta et al. described a rare case of primary embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma of anterior neck involving the thyroid in a 7-year-old boy. The child presented with a huge anterior neck swelling that clinically resembled a thyroid mass. CT scan showed a heterogeneous mass in the anterior neck replacing the entire right lobe of thyroid, diagnosed histologically and immunohistochemically as embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma [5].

Kalita et al. reported a case of parotid region rhabdomyosarcoma diagnosed on fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) and aided by immunohistochemistry (IHC) on cell block in a 7-year-old female patient [6]. In this case also the patient was initially treated conservatively on antibiotics, and the swelling did not subside and continued to increase in size. CT scan of the neck revealed a large enhancing mass extending from the right parotid into the ipsilateral parapharyngeal space and masticator space. FNAC of the parotid was performed, smears made and material for cell block was obtained with an additional pass and diagnosed as a rhabdomyosarcoma using IHC markers.

Typical embryonal rhabdomyosarcomas are composed of variably differentiated rhabdomyoblasts within a loose, myxoid mesenchyme, with alternating areas of dense and loose cellularity [1]. Morphology of the tumor in our case was similar and there were no other morphological patterns.

The patient was treated with a regimen of cyclophosphamide, vincristine, irinotecan, and doxorubicin. The patient was followed up and was doing well after a period of 6 months and also after a 1-year follow-up. The prognosis is usually better in patients aged 1–9 years but our patient responded well to the chemotherapy regimen.

Conclusion

This case reports the rarity of this neoplasm and the importance of the differential diagnosis for planning the therapeutic strategy, as targeted therapy is of utmost importance in the present-day scenario.

Key learning points

Inflammation of the salivary gland are infrequent in children and adolescents, hence clinicians need to be aware that embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma may mimic the symptoms of inflammation or primary salivary gland tumor.

Adequate preoperative evaluation, complete resection, postoperative chemoradiation, and long-term follow-up are essential for management of this tumor.

First-line PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone for advanced esophageal and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Sijia Ren, Xikai Zhang, Xiao Zhou, Xianyuan Miao, Qiongqiong Wang, Yefang Lao, Zhongzhong Peng, Kaifeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) combined with chemotherapy have become the first-line treatment for advanced or metastatic esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC)/gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJC). The aim is to explore the safety and efficacy of ICI and a potential biomarker.MethodsAs of August 2025, we comprehensively reviewed the literature on clinical randomized controlled trials (RCTs) by searching keywords, such as “esophageal adenocarcinoma”, “gastroesophageal junction cancer”, or “immunotherapy” across multiple databases (including Embase, Cochrane, Scopus, PubMed, Web of Science and the Clinical Trial Registries, as well as abstracts from the American Society of Clinical Oncology, European Society of Medical Oncology and other international tumor conferences). Two authors independently screened the studies, extracted the relevant data, and used RevMan 5.3 software and related evaluation tools for the statistical analysis and risk and quality evaluations, respectively. The primary outcomes of the study were overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), which were estimated by calculating the hazard ratio (HR) and confidence interval (CI). The secondary outcomes were the objective response rate (ORR), and incidence of adverse events (AEs).ResultsIn total, seven studies, comprising 7,043 patients with advanced EAC/GEJC, were included in the meta-analysis. We compared the efficacy of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, and chemotherapy alone. Immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy significantly improved the survival rate of the patients, significantly prolonging their OS (HR =0.80, 95% CI: 0.76–0.85, P<0.001; I2=0%). The PFS (HR =0.75, 95% CI: 0.71–0.80, P<0.001; I2=0%) and ORR of these patients also improved significantly. The incidence of AEs was higher in the immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy patients than the chemotherapy alone patients, but the overall range was controllable. The magnitude of the treatment benefit from immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy was positively correlated with the combined positive score (CPS), with greater efficacy observed in patients with higher CPS levels.ConclusionsImmunotherapy combined with chemotherapy significantly improved the survival benefit of patients with advanced EAC/GEJC, better controlled disease progression, and had controllable AEs.Keywords: Esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJC), meta-analysis, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)

Highlight box

Key findings

• Research has consistently shown that immune combination regimens with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors significantly improve the overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) of patients compared with chemotherapy alone.

• The combination regimen was found to have combined hazard ratios (HRs) of 0.80 for OS and 0.75 for PFS, to provide statistically and clinically significant survival benefits, and to have a controllable safety profile.

• We also analyzed the effects of the combined treatment at different PD-L1 levels on the combined positive score (CPS). The results showed that the higher the CPS, the better the benefit of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy.

What is known, and what is new?

• Immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy had a significant survival benefit compared to chemotherapy alone in the first-line treatment of advanced esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC) and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJC).

• We examined the effect of the CPS score on the efficacy of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy a different PD-L1 levels, and also conducted a subgroup analysis.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy combined with chemotherapy has become the standard regimen for the first-line treatment of advanced EAC/GEJC. The CPS is a key biomarker for guiding clinical decision-making, but its predictive value is still limited. Further research on biomarkers and further clinical research using multi-subgroups should be conducted to advance the treatment of advanced EAC/GEJC.

Introduction

In 2022, esophageal cancer was the seventh most common cancer and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related death, with approximately 60,400,000 new cases and 5,440,000 deaths (1). The two predominant histological subtypes of esophageal cancer are squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) and adenocarcinoma (AC) (1). Esophageal SCC has a higher incidence in Asia, while AC predominates in European countries, accounting for approximately two-thirds of all esophageal cancer cases (2,3). Research indicates that as living standards improve and smoking rates decrease, the primary risk factors associated with AC include being overweight, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and Barrett’s esophagus (4-6). AC and AC of the esophagogastric junction are more specific and insidious than SCC, making diagnosis and identification challenging. Consequently, a high percentage of esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC)/gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJC) patients are diagnosed at an advanced or metastatic stage, a trend more prevalent among Asian patients.

Surgical resection is the primary treatment for early stage EAC/GEJC (7); however, 50–60% of patients experience local recurrence or distant metastasis within five years, resulting in a poor survival rate (8,9). Fluorouracil combined with platinum-based chemotherapy is the standard first-line treatment for advanced, unresectable EAC and GEJC (10). However, the efficacy of chemotherapy alone is limited; the objective response rate (ORR) is only 30–40%, and patients have a short median progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS), and experience significant side effects (11-14).

The emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has advanced the treatment of advanced EAC and GEJC. The interaction between programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) on tumor cells and programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) on cytotoxic T cells leads to accelerated tumor growth (15). PD-L1 expression is associated with a poor prognosis in many types of cancer. Immunotherapy restores the anti-tumor activity of T cells by blocking the PD-1/PD-L1 signaling pathway, thereby enhancing the body’s immune response (16). A study has shown that compared with traditional chemotherapy, immune monotherapy or immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy significantly prolongs the OS of patients with advanced EAC and GEJC, especially those with high PD-L1 expression (17). Chemotherapy drugs enhance the effect of immunotherapy by inducing the immunogenic death of tumor cells and regulating the tumor microenvironment (18,19).

However, there are significant differences in the immunotherapy response rates of different patients, and some patients may derive no benefits due to primary or secondary resistance. Therefore, research is being conducted to optimize immunotherapy strategies, improve efficacy, and reduce adverse events (AEs). A number of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies have explored the efficacy and safety of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy in the treatment of advanced EAC and GEJC; however, the limited sample sizes of these studies and differences in research design, patient population and evaluation criteria have resulted in inconsistent conclusions (20-26).

Therefore, this study aims to comprehensively evaluate the survival benefits of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy compared with chemotherapy alone in the treatment of advanced EAC and GEJC patients through systematic evaluation and meta-analysis. Formally assess the consistency of this benefit across key patient subgroups and offer a comprehensive summary of the safety profile. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA reporting checklist (27) (available at https://jgo.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jgo-2025-aw-863/rc).

Methods

Search strategy and study selection

The PubMed, Embase, Scopus, Web of Science and Cochrane databases were searched to retrieve English-language articles on immunotherapy for EAC/GEJC reported before August 2025. We also searched for the latest unpublished clinical trial data on immunotherapy for advanced EAC/GEJC presented at the Clinical Trial Registries, American Society of Clinical Oncology, European Society of Medical Oncology and other international tumor conferences as of August 2025. The following keywords were used: (Esophageal Neoplasms OR Esophageal Cancer OR Esophagus Cancer OR Esophageal Adenocarcinoma OR EAC OR Gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma OR gastroesophageal junction cancer) AND (nivolumab OR pembrolizumab or camrelizumab OR sintilimab OR tislelizumab OR toripalimab OR Serplulimab OR PD-1 OR programmed cell death 1 OR CTLA-4) AND chemotherapy AND (randomized controlled trial).

Articles were selected based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The eligibility criteria should be explicitly defined using the PICOS framework. P (population): Patients with previously untreated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative EAC or Siewert type 1 GEJC. I (intervention): a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor (e.g., nivolumab, pembrolizumab) combined with a platinum-fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy backbone. C (comparator): the same platinum-fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy regimen plus placebo or chemotherapy alone. O (outcomes): primary outcomes of OS and PFS, reported as hazard ratios (HRs). Secondary outcomes include Objective Response Rate (ORR) and incidence of Grade 3–5 treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). S (study design): phase II/III randomized controlled trials.

Endpoints

The primary endpoints of the study were OS, which was defined as the time between treatment initiation and death of any cause; PFS, which was defined as the time between treatment initiation and the first documented disease progression or death of any cause; and the ORR, which was defined as the percentage of patients achieving the best complete response or partial response. These endpoints were used as clinical endpoints in the trials to assess the efficacy and safety of any new cancer intervention. Clinically, the combined positive score (CPS) and tumor proportion score (TPS) were used to measure the expression of PD-1 on tumors and related immune cells.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis and statistical analysis were performed using the special software Review Manager (RevMan) 5.3 version provided by the Cochrane Collaboration. General inverse variance was used to calculate OS and PFS, dichotomous analysis was used for ORR and TRAE. The HR and 95% confidence interval (CI) were used as effect indicators to compare the safety and efficacy of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy compared with chemotherapy. Chi-square test was used to evaluate the statistical heterogeneity between the trials included in the Meta-analysis. If I2≤50% or P≥0.05, the fixed effect model was used. On the contrary, if I2>50% or P<0.05, the random effect model was selected. Through sensitivity analysis and subgroup analysis, the source of heterogeneity was explored. Publication bias was assessed using a funnel plot and Egger’s test for bias. A 2-tailed P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant for differences.

Quality assessment

The quality of the included studies was assessed using the risk-of-bias 2 (RoB 2) tool recommended in the Cochrane. The following factors were considered: (I) bias arising from the randomization process; (II) bias due to deviations from intended intervention; (III) bias due to missing outcome data; (IV) bias in measurement of the outcome; (V) bias in selection of the reported result. The quality evaluation was conducted by two researchers independently, and if any disagreements arose, a third researcher was consulted and the issue was resolved by discussion or by reference to the third researcher’s opinion.

Results

We first selected 1,282 studies, excluded duplicate publications and conference content, and then selected 22 literature content and 6 conference and experimental data after reviewing the title and abstract. After excluding single-arm trials, non-first-line treatment studies, and studies lacking important data, seven RCTs (20-26), comprising 7,043 patients, were ultimately included in the meta-analysis. Figure 1 shows the study selection process. The basic characteristics of each study and the results of the quality assessment are shown in Table 1 and Figure 2. The seven RCTs were all phase III trials. Data were extracted from the studies, including OS, PFS, ORR, and AE data. In the KEYNOTE062 (20) trial, we included data from the pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy group versus the chemotherapy group, including subgroup data for CPS >1 and CPS ≥10. The ATTRACTION-4 (21) trial included patients in the nivolumab combined with chemotherapy group and the chemotherapy combined with placebo group. The CheckMate649 (24) trial recruited three groups of patients in a 1:1:1 ratio: chemotherapy, nivolumab combined with chemotherapy, and nivolumab combined with ipilimumab. This study only included data on nivolumab combined with chemotherapy and chemotherapy. The KEYNOTE-590 (22) trial is also a comparative study of pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy and chemotherapy, which includes data such as survival results and safety. KEYNOTE-859 (26) is a prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, international, critical phase III clinical trial. The ORIENT-16 (23) trial is the first phase III study to report the efficacy and safety of sintilimab combined with chemotherapy and placebo combined with chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced GEJC. The data of the subjects and the information of the population with CPS ≥5 were extracted. The RATIONALE 305 (25) trial was a study on the comparison of chemotherapy with Tislelizumab combined with chemotherapy, and the patient information and effective results were extracted. All seven studies were multicenter, randomized, controlled, phase III clinical trials. The quality of the trials was assessed and the results were acceptable.

We analyzed the OS data from the seven RCTs, and the results indicated that immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy significantly improved the survival of patients with advanced EAC/GEJC, prolonging their OS (HR =0.80, 95% CI: 0.76–0.85, P<0.001; I2=0%). In a further subgroup analysis, we assessed the efficacy of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy across different CPS expression levels, and found that, irrespective of the CPS level, the effect of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy was superior to that of chemotherapy alone in the treatment of advanced EAC/GEJC (HR =0.75, P<0.001), and the higher the CPS level, the greater the benefit derived from the combination therapy (Figure 3).

We also found a statistically significant difference in terms of PFS (HR =0.75, 95% CI: 0.71–0.80, P<0.001; I2=0%). The subgroup analysis showed that the PFS of the patients who received immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy was superior to that of the patients who received chemotherapy alone, and higher CPS scores were correlated with a better effect. Although the difference was not statistically significant in the CPS <1 subgroup, there was a general trend toward immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy, which is better than chemotherapy for advanced EAC/GEJC patients (Figure 4).

Forest plot for PFS in patients treated with ICI therapy combined with chemotherapy versus chemotherapy. CI, confidence interval; CPS, combined positive score; ICI, immune checkpoint inhibitor; IV, inverse variance; PFS, progression-free survival; SE, standard error.

In terms of the ORR, our findings indicated that the patients treated with immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy experienced greater benefits than those treated with chemotherapy alone (P<0.001). The subgroup analysis also revealed a correlation on the ORR with CPS (Figure 5). A comprehensive analysis of the study data showed that immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy resulted in a higher incidence of AEs than chemotherapy alone. This difference was also statistically significant in the analysis of grade 3–5 AEs (HR =1.42, 95% CI: 1.29–1.57, P<0.001; I2=39%) (Figure 6). Immune-related AEs (irAEs) mostly occurred in the immune combined chemotherapy group, and low-level irAEs were usually tolerable. Common high-grade irAES are mainly gastrointestinal toxicity, neutropenia, hypothyroidism, and skin reactions.

Further, a separate analysis revealed that the OS of the GEJC patients who received immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy was significantly better than that of the GEJC patients who received chemotherapy alone (HR =0.82) (Figure 7). In the microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) group, the patients who received a combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy had a significantly better OS than those who received chemotherapy alone (HR =0.41) (Figure 8).

Discussion

EAC and GEJC are invasive tumors that have a significant effect on cancer-related mortality worldwide. Immunotherapy/chemotherapy is the gold-standard first-line treatment option for patients with advanced EAC/GEJC who cannot undergo surgery combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy radiotherapy or concurrent chemoradiotherapy. Notably, most EAC and GEJC patients are at an advanced stage at the time of diagnosis and cannot undergo surgical resection, resulting in a poor prognosis and short survival. In recent years, immunotherapy, a new treatment regimen, has been shown to have a number of advantages compared with conventional chemotherapy, including a prolonged survival time and a reduced incidence of AEs (28). Immune checkpoint inhibitors function by blocking inhibitory signaling pathways, such as PD-1/PD-L1, thereby restoring the capacity of cytotoxic T cells to recognize and eliminate tumor cells (29,30). The immune checkpoint signaling pathway is mainly dominated by the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway, which is the focus of most current studies. Study has shown that the combination of the PD-1 receptor and its ligand PD-L1 down-regulates the T cell-mediated immune response (31). We studied the efficacy and safety of immunotherapy in advanced, unresectable and metastatic EAC/GEJC based on PD-1 and PD-L1.

This meta-analysis integrated the latest evidence from seven high-quality RCTs to examine the use of ICI therapy combined with standard chemotherapy in the treatment of advanced EAC/GEJC. The findings confirmed that the regimen of chemotherapy combined with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors significantly improved the OS and PFS of the patients compared with chemotherapy alone. The pooled HRs were 0.80 for OS, and 0.75 for PFS, reflecting statistically and clinically significant survival benefits. The ORR analysis further supported the superiority of the combined regimen in inducing tumor regression. However, the incidence of related AEs was significantly increased in the combined treatment group, although the ≥ grade 3 AEs were relatively controllable (32). These results provide strong evidence-based medical support for immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy as a new standard for the first-line treatment of advanced EAC/GEJC. We also analyzed the efficacy of combined therapy at different PD-L1 levels for CPS scores. The results showed that the higher the CPS, the better the benefit of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy.We can see from the data that the HR for OS in the Asian-population ATTRACTION-4 (21) trial (0.90) was less favorable than in the global KEYNOTE-590 (22) trial (0.72). On the one hand, there are differences in patient populations. ATTRACTION-4 (21) focuses on Asian gastric cancer patients, and KEYNOTE-590 (22) covers global esophageal cancer patients. There are differences in tumor types, geographical distribution, and PD-L1 expression levels. The proportion of PD-L1 low expression in Asian gastric cancer patients is relatively high [only 16% of patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥1 in ATTRACTION-4 (21)], while the proportion of PD-L1 high expression in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients in KEYNOTE-590 (22) is relatively high, which may affect the efficacy of immunotherapy. On the other hand, there are differences in treatment regimens and drugs. ATTRACTION-4 (21) was treated with nivolumab combined with oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy, and KEYNOTE-590 (22) was treated with Pembrolizumab combined with cisplatin-fluorouracil chemotherapy. The synergistic effects of different immune drugs and chemotherapy regimens are different, which may also lead to differences in OS risk ratio.

Although immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy is effective as a whole, not all patients will benefit equally. Identifying the populations most likely to respond (using predictive biomarkers) and monitoring efficacy/drug resistance (using prognostic/pharmacodynamic biomarkers) are key challenges for achieving precise treatment. The CPS is currently the most widely used and well-proven predictive marker in clinical practice. Both the KEYNOTE-590 (22) and CheckMate649 (24) studies showed that as the CPS score increased (e.g., CPS ≥10), the OS and PFS benefits derived by patients from combined therapy increased significantly. The CPS has become an important basis for guiding first-line treatment decisions. The tumor mutation burden (TMB) is an effective ICI marker in some cancers [e.g., melanoma (33), and lung cancer (34)]; however, the incidence of a high TMB and MSI-H status in EAC/GEJC is relatively low (approximately 5–7%), and its predictive value in first-line combination therapy is not as strong as that of the CPS. Despite this, MSI-H is also an important marker. MSI-H advanced GEJC has shown an enhanced responsiveness to immunotherapy, and nivolumab plus low-dose ipilimumab showed robust and durable antitumor efficacy as a first-line treatment for MSI-H GEJC (35).

Our meta-analysis mainly studies the efficacy of immunotherapy in EAC/GEJC. Most of the data in other meta-analyses are about gastric AC and do not distinguish between esophagus and stomach, so the evaluation of efficacy is always ambiguous (36-41). In the evaluation of CPS, other studies were also based on the evaluation of gastric AC and included data on immune monotherapy compared with placebo or chemotherapy, and the evaluation of CPS in some studies was limited (36-38,41,42). This study focuses on the efficacy of immunotherapy combined with chemotherapy compared with chemotherapy in EAC/GEJC and the subgroup results of CPS, so as to provide a more accurate treatment plan for clinical practice.

This meta-analysis had some limitations. The PD-L1 level was used as a biomarker to predict treatment efficacy; however, treatment efficacy is also related to tumor heterogeneity, and the detection methods, detection indicators, definitions and standards employed. For each study, the CPS boundary differed; some used the critical value of 1 and 10, and others used the critical value of 5. In addition to the CPS, some studies employed the TPS. In relation to gastroesophageal AC, the MSI-H subgroup in the study did not distinguish between gastric AC and EAC; thus, further research is required. Differences in the Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group scores, age standards, chemotherapy cycles, and treatments of the patients in the included study may have contributed to variations in the results. More studies on patients with advanced EAC/GEJC and more in-depth subgroup results will inform the treatment of advanced EAC/GEJC in the future.

Conclusions

This meta-analysis of seven RCTs including over 7,000 patients confirms that first-line chemo-immunotherapy provides a statistically significant and clinically meaningful reduction in the risk of death (pooled HR =0.80, 95% CI: 0.76–0.85) for patients with advanced EAC/GEJC. The benefit is most pronounced in patients with high PD-L1 CPS, reinforcing its role as an essential predictive biomarker for treatment selection.

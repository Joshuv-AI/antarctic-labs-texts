A q q -Polymatroid Framework for Information Leakage in Secure Linear Network Coding
Eimear Byrne, Johan Vester Dinesen, Ragnar Freij-Hollanti, Camilla Hollanti

Abstract
We study information leakage in secure linear network coding schemes based on nested rank-metric codes. We show that the amount of information leaked to an adversary that observes a subset of network links is characterized by the conditional rank function of a representable q q -polymatroid associated with the underlying rank-metric code pair. Building on this connection, we introduce the notions of q q -polymatroid ports and q q -access structures and describe their structural properties. Moreover, we extend Massey’s correspondence between minimal codewords and minimal access sets to the rank-metric setting and prove a q q -analogue of the Brickell–Davenport theorem.



I
Introduction

Network coding enables intermediate nodes in a communication network to transmit linear combinations of incoming messages rather than simply forwarding them. This approach enhances throughput, reliability, and robustness in multicast communication
[
1
,
28
]
. However, beyond efficiency, modern communication systems must also ensure confidentiality. Ensuring that network transmissions remain secure even in the presence of eavesdroppers gives rise to the problem of secure network coding.

A classical foundation for this problem is the Wiretap II channel introduced by Ozarow and Wyner
[
34
]
, where an adversary can observe a limited number of transmitted symbols. In this setting, linear coset codes provide secrecy guarantees by ensuring that each observation reveals no information about the secret message. This model naturally extends to network scenarios in which the eavesdropper wiretaps a subset of communication links
[
13
]
. Secure linear network coding builds on this idea. In such constructions, the algebraic structure of rank-metric coset codes ensures universality with respect to the underlying network topology and robustness against rank deficiencies and transmission errors
[
38
,
39
,
16
]
.

Beyond their role in secure communication, these coding schemes admit a rich combinatorial interpretation. Wiretap II schemes based on linear Hamming-metric coset codes are equivalent to
linear secret sharing schemes
(LSSS)
[
36
,
6
,
4
,
26
,
29
]
. In an LSSS, the information held by each participant corresponds to a share derived from a codeword, and the qualified sets of participants are characterized by the linear dependencies among these shares. Matroids often appear when linear dependence plays a structural role, which makes them a convenient object to use for several problems in information theory and coding theory
[
41
,
33
]
. A linear code determines a representable matroid whose rank function captures which sets of coordinates support non-trivial codewords; conversely, many code parameters and duality properties can be expressed purely in matroidal terms
[
22
]
. This perspective is particularly useful in secret sharing, where qualified and unqualified coalitions correspond to dependent and independent sets in the underlying matroid, and where circuits describe minimal reconstruction sets
[
30
,
14
]
. Related ideas also arise in distributed storage, where locality and repair groups can be modelled through matroid rank constraints, providing a combinatorial way to describe which subsets of nodes suffice for recovery
[
18
]
. Furthermore, every LSSS corresponds to a
port
of a representable matroid; see
[
30
]
for further details. Matroid ports were first introduced by Lehman
[
27
]
in connection with a game-theoretic problem posed by Shannon. In this setting, the secret is identified with a particular element of the matroid’s ground set and the qualified coalitions correspond to circuits containing that element. Brickell and Davenport
[
9
]
showed that every ideal access structure arises from a matroid port. Massey
[
32
]
later strengthened the connection between coding theory and secret sharing by showing that the minimal qualified sets in an LSSS correspond precisely to certain minimal codewords in the dual code, thus establishing a direct link between the algebraic properties of the code and the combinatorial access structures.

Together, these classical correspondences illustrate how the combinatorial structure of matroids underlies secure communication and secret sharing. Extending this perspective to the rank-metric setting naturally lends itself to the theory of
q
-matroids. These were introduced to the coding theory community by Jurrius and Pellikaan
[
24
]
, while subsequent work on
q
-polymatroids further established their relationship with rank-metric codes
[
21
,
19
,
37
]
. In this framework, the invariants of a rank-metric code, such as generalized rank weights, duality, and the maximum rank distance (MRD) property, admit natural combinatorial interpretations through the associated
q
-polymatroid. Consequently, the ports of representable
q
-polymatroids form a natural framework for studying the information leakage of linear network coding schemes based on nested rank-metric codes, laying the foundation for the developments presented in this work.

I-A

Contributions and Related Work

This work develops a
q
-polymatroidal framework for studying information-theoretic security in linear network coding using nested linear rank-metric code pairs. Our main contributions are as follows.

•

We show that the information leakage in secure linear network coding with nested rank-metric coset codes is determined by the conditional rank function of a representable
q
-polymatroid.

•

We extend Massey’s link between minimal codewords and minimal reconstructing sets to vector rank-metric codes. Minimal reconstructing spaces arise from minimal dual codewords; the condition is sufficient in general and becomes necessary when the dual code is minimal, with respect to rank support.

•

We prove a
q
-analogue of the Brickell–Davenport theorem by introducing ports and access structures for
q
-polymatroids and showing that, with certain assumptions, the restriction of the underlying structure to a suitable hyperplane is a
q
-matroid.

Throughout,

denotes the finite field of order

. We let

-dimensional vector space over

q
-Polymatroids

In
[
21
,
37
]
the authors study
q
-polymatroids as

-analogues of a polymatroids, thereby extending the classical notion of matroids into the subspace setting. Associated to each rank-metric code is a pair of

Definition 1
.

A (rational)
q
-polymatroid
is a pair
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
where
\rho\colon\mathcal{L}(E)\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
is a
rank function
such that the following axioms hold.

(R1)

Boundedness
:
0\leq\rho(V)\leq\dim V
for all
V\leq E
.

(R2)

Monotonicity
:
\rho(V)\leq\rho(W)
for all
V,W\leq E
with
V\leq W
.

(R3)

Submodularity
:
\rho(V+W)+\rho(V\cap W)\leq\rho(V)+\rho(W)
for all
V,W\leq E
.

The
rank
of
\mathcal{M}
is
\rho(E)
.

With respect to Definition
1
,

-matroids are the integral-valued specialization of
q
-polymatroids and play a central role in the applications considered in this work. Specifically, a
q
-polymatroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
such that

is called a
q
-matroid
. Furthermore, for any
q
-polymatroid
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
and for any
V,W\leq E
define

\rho(V\,|\,W)\coloneqq\rho(V+W)-\rho(W)

to be the
conditional rank of
V
given
W
.

The following definition provides an example of one of the simplest families of
q
-matroids.

Definition 2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k\leq n=\dim E
. The
q
-matroid
\mathcal{U}_{n,k}=(E,\rho)
such that
\rho(V)=\min\{\dim V,k\}
is the
uniform
q
-matroid of rank
k
.

As in the matroid case, there are many axiomatic definitions of
q
-matroids, often referred to as cryptomorphisms (see e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
20
]
). In general, such axiomatic descriptions for
q
-polymatroids are more complicated and require further information. The following definitions are provided specifically for
q
-matroids, which extend the classical matroid notions to the subspace setting.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
be a

-matroid and
V\leq E
.

1.

V
is an
independent space
of
\mathcal{M}
if
\rho(V)=\dim V
.

2.

V
is a
dependent space
of
\mathcal{M}

3.

V
is a

of
\mathcal{M}

4.

V
is a

of

.

Next we recall standard operations on
q
-polymatroids, which play a central role in structural results.

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-polymatroid. Define
\rho^{*}(V)\coloneqq\dim V-\rho(E)+\rho(V^{\perp})

. Then
\mathcal{M}^{*}\coloneqq(E,\rho^{*})

-polymatroid called the

.

Definition 5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}=(E_{i},\rho_{i})
be
q
-polymatroids for
i=1,2
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are
equivalent
, denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{1}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{2}
, if there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isomorphism
\phi\colon E_{1}\rightarrow E_{2}
such that
\rho_{2}(\phi(V))=\rho_{1}(V)

.

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-polymatroid and let
Z\leq E
. Then
\mathcal{M}|_{Z}\coloneqq(Z,\rho|_{Z})

\rho|_{Z}(V)\coloneqq\rho(V)

is a

-polymatroid, called the

\mathcal{M}
to

. Additionally, let
\pi\colon E\rightarrow E/Z
denote the canonical projection. Then
\mathcal{M}/Z\coloneqq(E/Z,\rho_{E/Z})

II-B

Rank-Metric Codes

Let us recall some standard definitions and properties of rank-metric codes. For an overview of rank-metric codes, we refer to
[
3
]
.

Definition 7
.

A
rank-metric code
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
equipped with the rank distance
\textup{d}_{\mathrm{rk}}(X,Y)=\mathrm{rk}(X-Y)

for any
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
. The
minimum rank distance
of
\mathcal{C}\neq 0
is defined as
\textup{d}_{\mathrm{rk}}(\mathcal{C})=\min\{\mathrm{rk}(X)\mid X\in\mathcal{C},X\neq 0\}.

A rank-metric analogue of the Singleton bound in the Hamming metric also holds.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a non-zero rank-metric code. Then

\dim\mathcal{C}\leq\max\{m,n\}\left(\min\{m,n\}-\textup{d}_{\mathrm{rk}}(\mathcal{C})+1\right).

Definition 9
.

A rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
is
maximum rank distance (MRD)
if it meets the bound of Proposition
8
with equality.

Definition 10
.

The
dual code
of a rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
is

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\left\{X\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}\mid\mathrm{tr}(XY^{\top})=0\text{ for all }Y\in\mathcal{C}\right\},

where
\mathrm{tr(A)}
denotes the trace of a matrix
A
.

From a rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
we can induce a
q
-polymatroid by considering shortened subcodes of
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, for any
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, the
shortened subcode of

\mathcal{C}
with respect to
V
is

\mathcal{C}(V)=\left\{X\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{colsp}(X)\leq V\right\}.

This construction provides the link between rank-metric codes and
q
-polymatroids, which we now recall.

Proposition 11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a rank-metric code. For any
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, define

\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(V)\coloneqq\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}-\dim\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}{m}.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\rho_{\mathcal{C}})
is a
q
-polymatroid and
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}^{\perp}}
.

We will use the following lemma in our subsequent results, which follows from the definition of
\rho^{*}
and Proposition
11
(see also
[
7
,
35
]
).

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a rank-metric code and let
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
Then

\dim\mathcal{C}^{\perp}(V)=\dim\mathcal{C}^{\perp}-m\dim V^{\perp}+\dim\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp}).

Definition 13
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-polymatroid, where
E
is an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space. Then
\mathcal{M}
is called
representable
if there exists a rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
such that
\mathcal{M}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

In
[
15
]
it was shown that representable
q
-polymatroids also admit a description via the entropy of discrete random variables. We present this result here with proof for self-containment. We let
H(\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n})
denote the joint entropy of a set of discrete random variables
\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n}
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a rank-metric code. For any
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
let
\pi_{V}\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})
denote the canonical quotient map and
\mathbf{Z}_{V}
the random variable on
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})
induced by the uniform distribution on
\mathcal{C}
and
\pi_{V}
. Then

1.

H(\mathbf{Z}_{V})=m\log(q)\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(V)
for all
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
,

2.

H(\mathbf{Z}_{V_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{Z}_{V_{\ell}})=H(\mathbf{Z}_{V_{1}+\cdots+V_{\ell}})
for all
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
,

3.

H(\mathbf{Z}_{W}\,|\,\mathbf{Z}_{V})=m\log(q)\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(W\,|\,V)
for all
V,W\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof let
\bot=\bot\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.
Then

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{Z}_{V})

\displaystyle=-\>\>\sum_{\mathclap{X\in\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}}\>\>\mathbb{P}(X)\log(\mathbb{P}(X))

\displaystyle=-\>\>\sum_{\mathclap{X\in\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}}\>\>\frac{q^{\dim\ker\pi_{V}}}{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})}}\log\left(\frac{q^{\dim\ker\pi_{V}}}{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})}}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{\dim\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}\frac{q^{\dim\ker\pi_{V}}}{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})}}\log\left(\frac{q^{\dim\ker\pi_{V}}}{q^{\dim(\mathcal{C})}}\right)

\displaystyle=-q^{\dim\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}q^{-\dim\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}\log\left(q^{-\dim\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp})}\right)

\displaystyle=(\dim\mathcal{C}-\dim\mathcal{C}(V^{\perp}))\log(q)

\displaystyle=m\log(q)\rho_{C}(V),

which proves 1).

For 2), let
\sigma
be the map
\sigma\colon\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\prod^{\ell}_{i=1}\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{C}(V_{i}^{\perp})
defined by
\sigma(X)=(\pi_{V_{1}}(X),\ldots,\pi_{V_{\ell}}(X))
for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
. This map is clearly
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear and satisfies
\ker\sigma=\cap^{\ell}_{i=1}\ker\pi_{V_{i}}=\ker\pi_{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}V_{i}}
. The result then follows by the definition of joint entropy, as
\mathrm{rk}\,\sigma=\mathrm{rk}\,\pi_{\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}V_{i}}
and for any
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{\ell})\in\mathrm{im}\,\sigma
, we have that
\mathbb{P}(Y)=q^{\dim\ker\sigma-\dim\mathcal{C}}
. Finally, by the definition of conditional entropy, we have that 3) follows immediately from 1) and 2).∎

An alternative viewpoint considers
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear codes, known as vector rank-metric codes.

Let
\Pi=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
be a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, let
\Pi(v)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
denote the matrix whose
(i,j)
-th entry is the
j
-th coordinate of
v_{i}
with respect to the basis
\Pi
. The map

v\mapsto\Pi(v)
defines an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isomorphism from

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}^{m\times n}
. For an

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
subspace
A\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, define
\Pi(A)=\{\Pi(v)\mid v\in A\}
. It is well-known that the minimum rank distance of

\Pi(A)
is independent of the choice of basis
\Pi
. Moreover, for any such basis, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Pi(A)=m\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}A.

We define the rank distance between

to be
\textup{d}_{\mathrm{rk}}(v,w):=\mathrm{rk}(\Pi(v)-\Pi(w))
.

Definition 15
.

An
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-
[n,k]

vector rank-metric code
is a

k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{C}
of

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-
[n,k,d]
vector rank-metric code if
\Pi(\mathcal{C})

, where

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over

II-C

Linear Network Coding and Nested Coset Coding

Linear network coding has been extensively studied in
[
1
,
12
,
28
,
25
]
. Unlike traditional routing, linear network coding enhances both throughput and robustness by allowing intermediate nodes to linearly combine incoming data streams, rather than simply forwarding them. The problem of securing such coded communication against eavesdropping adversaries has been addressed in several works
[
13
,
39
,
16
]
. These studies investigate the conditions under which an adversary, with access to a subset of network links, can be prevented from gaining information about the transmitted messages.

Consider a communication network comprising a set of source nodes and a set of sink nodes. A source node encodes a message
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
into a matrix
C\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
, where
n
denotes the number of outgoing links from the source. The encoded message is then transmitted through the network, and a sink node receives a matrix
Y=AC\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N\times m}
, where
A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N\times n}
is the transfer matrix characterizing the linear transformation induced by the network between the source and the sink. If the transfer matrix
A
is selected randomly, the scheme corresponds to random linear network coding, as introduced in
[
23
]
.

Now, suppose that an eavesdropper gains access to
\mu
links in the network. The adversary then observes a matrix
BC\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\mu\times m}
, where
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\mu\times n}
represents the associated linear combinations of the encoded message. The communication is said to be secure against
\mu
observations if the observed matrix
BC
reveals no information about the original message
x
. Moreover, the scheme is termed
universally secure
against
\mu
observations if this condition holds for all choices of
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\mu\times n}
.

Definition 16
.

A
coset coding scheme
over the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with message set
\mathcal{S}
is a family of disjoint non-empty subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}=\{\mathcal{C}_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{S}}
. The source encodes each
x\in\mathcal{S}
by choosing
C\in\mathcal{C}_{x}
uniformly at random.

Here, a
nested linear code pair
is a pair of rank-metric codes
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
. Choose
\mathcal{W}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{W}
and an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear isomorphism
\psi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}
, where
\ell=\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}/\mathcal{C}_{2})
. Thus, a nested linear code pair
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}
defines a coset coding scheme with message set
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}\simeq\mathcal{W}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{S}}=\{\mathcal{C}_{x}\}_{x\in\mathcal{S}}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{x}=\psi(x)+\mathcal{C}_{2}
for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
.

By abuse of notation, we let
x
denote the uniformly distributed message in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
and
BC
the induced random variable obtained by first drawing
C
uniformly at random from the coset
\mathcal{C}_{x}
and then applying the eavesdropper’s linear observation
B
. The quantity
I(x;BC)
is then the mutual information between these random variables.

Proposition 17

(
[
31
]
, Proposition 4)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a nested linear code pair and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times\mu}
.
Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}_{x}
be chosen uniformly at random.
Then

I(x;BC)=\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}(\rowsp(B))-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}(\rowsp(B)).

The quantity
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}(\rowsp(B))-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}(\rowsp(B))
captures the amount of distinguishability between the cosets
\mathcal{C}_{x}
, as observed through the adversary’s perspective (i.e., the row space of
B
). If this difference is zero for all
B
of rank at most
\mu
, then the eavesdropper gains no information about the message
x
, ensuring universal security against
\mu
observations. This property can be guaranteed when the underlying code
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is MRD and
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is the ambient space
[
39
]
.

III
Information Leakage in Nested Coset Coding and
q
-Polymatroids

In this section, we show that any representable
q
-polymatroid gives rise to a nested coset coding scheme for which the information leakage is fully determined by the underlying
q
-polymatroid. Conversely, we establish that information leakage in linear network coding with nested coset coding can be described using
q
-polymatroids induced by the underlying nested pair of rank-metric codes.

In Theorem
18
, we will use the following construction. Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a pair of nested linear codes and let
k_{2}^{\perp}=\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}
. Fix a basis
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{k_{2}^{\perp}}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
of
\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}
. Define the map
\Psi\colon\mathcal{C}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k_{2}^{\perp}+n)\times m}
by

\displaystyle\Psi(X)=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{matrix}\mathrm{tr}(H_{1}X^{\top})&\cdots&\mathrm{tr}(H_{1}X^{\top})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mathrm{tr}(H_{k_{2}^{\perp}}X^{\top})&\cdots&\mathrm{tr}(H_{k_{2}^{\perp}}X^{\top})\end{matrix}\\
X\end{bmatrix}.

(1)

Then for all
X\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
we have that

X\in\mathcal{C}_{2}\>\Longleftrightarrow\>\Psi(X)=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
X\end{bmatrix}\>\Longleftrightarrow\>\operatorname{colsp}(\Psi(X))\leq\langle\mathbf{e}_{k_{2}^{\perp}+1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{k_{2}^{\perp}+n}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}+n}.

Finally, observe that
\Psi
is injective, so the code
\Psi(\mathcal{C}_{1})\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(k_{2}^{\perp}+n)\times m}
satisfies
\dim\Psi(\mathcal{C}_{1})=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}
.

Theorem 18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
be a pair of nested linear codes. Let
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}/\mathcal{C}_{2})}
and let
C\in\mathcal{C}_{x}
be an encoding of
x
via the coset coding scheme defined by
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}
, and
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\mu\times n}
. Let
k_{2}^{\perp}=\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}
and define

Q_{0}=\langle\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{k_{2}^{\perp}}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}+n},\quad Q=\langle\mathbf{e}_{k_{2}^{\perp}+1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{k_{2}^{\perp}+n}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}+n},

and fix an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism
\tau\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\rightarrow Q
. Then

H(x\,|\,BC)=m\,\rho_{\Psi(\mathcal{C}_{1})}(Q_{0}\,|\,\tau(\rowsp(B)),

(2)

where
\Psi
is defined as in (
1
). In particular, if
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathcal{C}_{1}(P_{0}^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})
for some
P_{0}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, then

H(x\,|\,BC)=m\,\rho_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(P_{0}\,|\,\rowsp(B)).

(3)

Proof.

We equip
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}+n}
with the standard inner product induced by the direct-sum decomposition

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}+n}=Q_{0}\oplus Q
, that is, the form under which
Q_{0}
and
Q
are orthogonal and the restriction to each component coincides with the usual inner product on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, respectively. We denote orthogonal complements with respect to this form by
\perp={\perp{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}^{\perp}+n}}}
. With this convention,
Q_{0}^{\perp}=Q
. In particular, for any subspace
V\leq Q
, there exists a subspace
W\leq Q
such that
V^{\perp}=Q_{0}\oplus W
. Moreover, under the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism
\tau\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\to Q
, we have

\tau^{-1}(W)^{\perp{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}}=\tau^{-1}(V).

This follows directly from the fact that
\tau
preserves the standard inner product up to restriction to
Q
.

Letting
\mathcal{D}=\Psi(\mathcal{C}_{1})
, we obtain

\dim\mathcal{D}(V^{\perp})=\dim\mathcal{D}(Q_{0}\oplus W)=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau^{-1}(W)),

and similarly

\dim\mathcal{D}((V+Q_{0})^{\perp})=\dim\mathcal{D}(W)=\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}(\tau^{-1}(W)).

Suppose now that
V\leq Q
satisfies
\rowsp(B)=\tau^{-1}(V)
. By Proposition
17
we then have

\displaystyle H(x\,|\,BC)

\displaystyle=H(x)-I(x;BC)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}-\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}+\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}(\tau^{-1}(V))-\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp}(\tau^{-1}(V))

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})-\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}(\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{D}-\dim\mathcal{D}+\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau^{-1}(W))-\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}(\tau^{-1}(W))

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{D}-\dim\mathcal{D}((V+Q_{0})^{\perp})-(\dim\mathcal{D}-\dim\mathcal{D}(V^{\perp}))

\displaystyle=m\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(Q_{0}\,|\,V),

where the third equation in the above follows from Lemma
12
.
This proves the first claim. Suppose now also that
\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathcal{C}_{1}(P_{0}^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})
for some
P_{0}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Similarly, we get

\displaystyle H(x\,|\,BC)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})-\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}(\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(P_{0}^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}\cap\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}((P_{0}+\tau^{-1}(V))^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}})-(\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tau^{-1}(V)^{\perp\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}}))

\displaystyle=m\rho_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(P_{0}+\tau^{-1}(V))-m\rho_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(\tau^{-1}(V))

\displaystyle=m\rho_{\mathcal{C}_{1}}(P_{0}\,|\,\tau^{-1}(V)),

which completes the proof.
∎

Equation (
3
) shows that information leakage depends only on the conditional rank function of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. This gives a direct information-theoretic interpretation analogous to classical linear secret sharing. In particular, consider a rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
and a subspace
P_{0}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0})>0
. Let
G_{P_{0}}
be a full-rank matrix whose row space is
P_{0}
, and select some
X\in\mathcal{C}
such that
G_{P_{0}}X
represents the secret. The condition
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0})>0
ensures that at least two codewords in
\mathcal{C}
yield different secrets, which guarantees ambiguity.

An adversary observing
\mu
network links obtains
BX
, where
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\mu\times n}
describes the transfer matrix of the compromised links. The adversary can reconstruct the secret
G_{P_{0}}X
if and only if, for all
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
,

BY_{1}=BY_{2}\;\Rightarrow\;G_{P_{0}}Y_{1}=G_{P_{0}}Y_{2},

that is, whenever two codewords are indistinguishable through
B
, they must also be indistinguishable through
G_{P_{0}}
. Because
\mathcal{C}
is linear, this is equivalent to requiring that
BY=0
implies
G_{P_{0}}Y=0
for all
Y\in\mathcal{C}
. The condition
BY=0
means precisely that
\operatorname{colsp}(Y)\leq\rowsp(B)^{\perp}
. Hence, the adversary can reconstruct the secret if and only if

\mathcal{C}(\rowsp(B)^{\perp})=\mathcal{C}(\rowsp(B)^{\perp}\cap P_{0}^{\perp}),

which is exactly the condition
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0}\,|\,\rowsp(B)^{\perp})=0
. Therefore, the conditional rank function
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0}\,|\,V)
quantifies, in rank-metric terms, how much additional information about the secret subspace
P_{0}
becomes accessible once the adversary knows the observations from
V
.

IV
q
-Access Structures and
q
-Polymatroid Ports

Motivated by the correspondence established in Section
III
, we now define
q
-polymatroid ports in direct analogy with matroid ports.

Definition 19
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
be a
q
-polymatroid and
P_{0},P\leq E
such that
P_{0}\oplus P=E
and
\rho(P_{0})>0
. We define
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
, where

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle\coloneq\{V\leq P\,|\,\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0}\,|\,V)=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{W\leq P\,|\,\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0}\,|\,W)=\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0})\}.

\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
is called a
generalized
q
-polymatroid port
. If
\mathcal{M}
is a
q
-matroid, then
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
is a
generalized
q
-matroid port
. If
\dim P_{0}=1
, we call it a
q
-polymatroid port
. If both conditions are satisfied, it is a
q
-matroid port
.

Given a nested linear code pair
\mathcal{C}_{2}\lneq\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
, by Theorem
18
we can construct a generalized
q
-polymatroid port
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}_{P^{\prime}_{0},P^{\prime}}=(\Gamma^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
such that for any
V^{\prime},W^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
,

\displaystyle V^{\prime}\text{ leaks all information if and only if }

\displaystyle\tau(V^{\prime})\in\Gamma^{\prime},

\displaystyle W^{\prime}\text{ leaks no information if and only if }

\displaystyle\tau(W^{\prime})\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime},

where
\tau\colon\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})\rightarrow P^{\prime}
is any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
is a representable
q
-polymatroid represented by the rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
and that
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
is a generalized
q
-polymatroid port induced by
\mathcal{M}
. For the coset coding scheme with the nested linear code pair
\mathcal{C}(P_{0}^{\perp})\lneq\mathcal{C}
, by Theorem
18
we have that for any
V,W\leq P
,

\displaystyle V\text{ leaks all information if and only if }

\displaystyle V\in\Gamma,

\displaystyle W\text{ leaks zero information if and only if }

\displaystyle W\in\mathcal{A}.

Therefore, generalized
q
-polymatroid ports provide an exact characterization of information leakage in linear network coding schemes defined by nested linear code pairs. Due to this interpretation, even if
\mathcal{M}
is not representable, we will refer to
\rho(P_{0})
as the size of the secret or the message. For
p\in\mathcal{P}(P)
, we refer to
\rho(p)
as the size of a packet.

Proposition 20
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
be a generalized
q
-polymatroid port.

1.

Let
V\leq P
such that there exists
V^{\prime}\in\Gamma
with
V^{\prime}\leq V
. Then
V\in\Gamma
.

2.

Let
W\leq P
such that there exists
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
with
W\leq W^{\prime}
. Then
W\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Let
V\in\Gamma
. Towards a contradiction, suppose that
V^{\prime}\leq P
such that
V\leq V^{\prime}
but
V^{\prime}\notin\Gamma
. Then, using submodularity of
\rho
we get that

\rho(V^{\prime})-\rho(V+P_{0})<\rho(V^{\prime}+P_{0})-\rho(V+P_{0})\leq\rho(V^{\prime})-\rho(V).

This implies
\rho(P_{0}\,|\,V)>0
, which contradicts the assumption that
V\in\Gamma
. This proves 1).
To see that 2) holds, note that again by submodularity of
\rho
we have that
\rho(P_{0})\geq\rho(W+P_{0})-\rho(W)\geq\rho(W^{\prime}+P_{0})-\rho(W^{\prime}).
Since

W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\rho(W^{\prime}+P_{0})-\rho(W^{\prime})=\rho(P_{0})
, from which the result follows.
∎

By Proposition
20
we have that a generalized
q
-polymatroid port
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
forms the
q
-analogue of an access structure on
\mathcal{L}(P)
. We formalize this in the following.

Definition 21
.

A subset
\Gamma\subseteq\mathcal{L}(E)
is called
monotone
if
V\in\Gamma
and
V^{\prime}\leq E
with
V\leq V^{\prime}
implies
V^{\prime}\in\Gamma
. Dually we define a subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq E
to be
anti-monotone
if
W\in\mathcal{A}
and
W\leq E
with
W^{\prime}\leq W
implies
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
. Two monotone sets, or two anti-monotone sets,
H_{1},H_{2}
are called
equivalent
if there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear monotone bijection
f\colon H_{1}\rightarrow H_{2}
with a monotone inverse.

Definition 22
.

Let
\Gamma,\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(E)
such that
\Gamma\cap A=\varnothing
,
\Gamma
is monotone and
\mathcal{A}
is anti-monotone. Then
\mathbf{S}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
is a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(E)
.

Corollary 23
.

A generalized
q
-polymatroid port
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
is a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(P)
.

For a
q
-access structure
\mathbf{S}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
we call
\Gamma
the
reconstructing structure
of
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
the
privacy structure
of
\mathbf{S}
. Elements of
\Gamma
are called
reconstructing spaces
and elements of
\mathcal{A}
are called
privacy spaces
. We define two
q
-access structures to be equivalent if their reconstructing structures and privacy structures are equivalent under the same bijection, as formalised in the following.

Definition 24
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{i}=(\Gamma_{i},\mathcal{A}_{i})
be
q
-access structures on
\mathcal{L}(E_{i})
for
i=1,2
. If there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear monotone bijection
f\colon\mathcal{L}(E_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(E_{2})
with a monotone inverse such that
f(\Gamma_{1})=\Gamma_{2}
and
f(\mathcal{A}_{1})=\mathcal{A}_{2}
, then
\mathbf{S}_{1}
and
\mathbf{S}_{2}
are called
equivalent
, and we denote this by
\mathbf{S}_{1}\simeq\mathbf{S}_{2}
.

We now define minors on monotone and anti-monotone sets. These definitions are then naturally extended to minors on
q
-access structures. The duality between restriction and contraction is given in Theorem
27
, analogous to the case of
q
-polymatroids
[
10
,
19
]
.

Definition 25
.

Let
H\subseteq\mathcal{L}(E)
be a monotone or anti-monotone set and let
Z\leq E
. Then

1.

\overline{H}=\{V\leq E\,|\,V\notin H\}
is called the
complement
of
H
.

2.

H^{*}=\{V\leq E\,|\,V^{\perp}\in H\}
is called the
dual
of
H
.

3.

H|_{Z}=\{V\leq Z\,|\,V\in H\}
is called the
restriction
of
H
to
Z
.

4.

H/Z=\{V\leq E/Z\,|\,\pi^{-1}(V)\in H\}
is called the
contraction
of
H
by
Z
, where
\pi\colon E\rightarrow E/Z
is the canonical quotient map.

The dual of a monotone set is clearly anti-monotone, and vice-versa. Similarly, both monotonicity and anti-monotonicity are preserved under restriction and contraction. Hence, we obtain
q
-access structures from a given one, by dualization, contraction, and restriction.

Definition 26
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
be a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(E)
and
Z\leq E
. Then

1.

\mathbf{S}^{*}=(\mathcal{A}^{*},\Gamma^{*})
is called the
dual

q
-access structure of
\mathbf{S}
.

2.

\mathbf{S}|_{Z}=(\Gamma|_{Z},\mathcal{A}|_{Z})
is called the
restriction
of
\mathbf{S}
to
Z
.

3.

\mathbf{S}/Z=(\Gamma/Z,\mathcal{A}/Z)
is called the
contraction
of
\mathbf{S}
by
Z
.

Note that in constructing
\mathbf{S}^{*}
, the duals of
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{A}
are obtained with respect to the same scalar product. Clearly, we have
\mathbf{S}^{**}=\mathbf{S}
. Additionally, the equivalence class of
\mathbf{S}^{*}
is independent of the choice of non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form.

As stated before, the class of
q
-access structures is closed under dualization and taking minors. In particular,
\mathbf{S}|_{Z}
is a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(Z)
and
\mathbf{S}/Z
is a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(E/Z)
. Intuitively, the restriction to
Z
describes how the
q
-access structure changes when information outside
Z
is removed from the system, while the contraction by
Z
describes how a
q
-access structure changes when
Z
is made public.

Theorem 27
.

Let
\mathbf{S}
be a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(E)
. Then

(\mathbf{S}/Z)^{*}\simeq\mathbf{S}^{*}|_{Z^{\perp}}\quad\text{and}\quad(\mathbf{S}|_{Z^{\perp}})^{*}\simeq\mathbf{S}^{*}/Z.

Proof.

We prove the first equivalence as the second then follows trivially by biduality (i.e. it is the dual of the first statement). Let
\pi\colon E\rightarrow E/Z
denote the canonical quotient map. It is possible to choose a bilinear form such that
Z\oplus Z^{\perp}=E
and there exists a monotone isomorphism
\phi\colon E/Z\rightarrow Z^{\perp}
with a monotone inverse satisfying
\pi^{-1}(V)^{\perp}=\phi(V)^{\perp}\cap Z^{\perp}
for all
V\in E/Z
, see
[
19
, Theorem 5.3]
. In particular,

\displaystyle(\Gamma/Z)^{*}

\displaystyle=\{V\leq E/Z\,|\,\pi^{-1}(V^{\perp E/Z})\in\Gamma\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{*}|_{Z^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\{W\leq Z^{\perp}\,|\,W^{\perp}\in\Gamma\}.

Similarly.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{A}/Z)^{*}

\displaystyle=\{V\leq E/Z\,|\,\pi^{-1}(V^{\perp E/Z})\in\mathcal{A}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{*}|_{Z^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=\{W\leq Z^{\perp}\,|\,W^{\perp}\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Consider now the maps
\sigma\colon\mathcal{L}(E/Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(Z^{\perp})
such that
V\mapsto\pi^{-1}(V^{\perp E/Z})^{\perp}

and
\tau\colon\mathcal{L}(Z^{\perp})\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(E/Z)
such that
W\mapsto(\phi^{-1}((W+Z)^{\perp}))^{\perp E/Z}
. Both
\sigma
and
\tau
are compositions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps and as such they are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear too. The maps
\sigma
and
\tau
are both monotone as they are compositions of monotone maps and an even number of anti-monotone maps. Suppose now that
W\leq Z^{\perp}
, so

\displaystyle\sigma(\tau(W))

\displaystyle=\sigma(\phi^{-1}((W+Z)^{\perp})^{\perp E/Z})

\displaystyle=\pi^{-1}(\phi^{-1}((W+Z)^{\perp}))^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\phi(\phi^{-1}(W^{\perp}\cap Z^{\perp}))\cap Z^{\perp}

\displaystyle=(W^{\perp}\cap Z^{\perp})^{\perp}\cap Z^{\perp}

\displaystyle=W+(Z\cap Z^{\perp})

\displaystyle=W,

where the penultimate equality follows by modularity. Similar arguments show that
\tau(\sigma(V))=V
for
V\leq E/Z
, so
\sigma
and
\tau
are mutually inverse. The proof then follows as
\sigma((\Gamma/Z)^{*})=\Gamma^{*}|_{Z^{\perp}}
and
\sigma((\mathcal{A}/Z)^{*})=\mathcal{A}^{*}|_{Z^{\perp}}
.
∎

Finally, we introduce nomenclature to describe
q
-access structures as generalizations of definitions of classical access structures
[
5
]
.

Definition 28
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
be a
q
-access structure on
\mathcal{L}(E)
.

1.

\mathbf{S}
is
degenerate
if
\Gamma=\varnothing
or
\mathcal{A}=\varnothing
.

2.

The
minimal reconstructing structure
of
\mathbf{S}
is
\Gamma_{\min}=\{V\in\Gamma\,|\,\text{for all }W<V\Rightarrow W\notin\Gamma\}
.

3.

\mathbf{S}
is
perfect
if
\overline{\Gamma}=\mathcal{A}
, or equivalently,
\Gamma\cup\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{L}(E)
.

4.

The
minimum gap
of
\mathbf{S}
is
g(\mathbf{S})=\min\{\dim(V/W)\,|\,(V,W)\in\Gamma\times A\colon W<V\}.

5.

\mathbf{S}
is
connected
if for all
p\in\mathcal{P}(P)
then there exists
V\in\Gamma_{\min}
such that
p\leq V
.

6.

\mathbf{S}
is
k
-
threshold
if there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\Gamma=\{V\leq E\,|\,\dim V\geq k\}.

Suppose
\mathbf{S}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
is a perfect
q
-access structure. Then the dual of
\mathbf{S}=(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
is also perfect and satisfies
\mathbf{S}^{*}=(\overline{\Gamma^{*}},\overline{\mathcal{A}^{*}})
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{S}
is entirely determined by
\Gamma_{\min}
, and all of its minors are perfect. The notion of perfect encapsulates the fact that either one obtains all information or no information; there is no partial information to be obtained. Perfect
q
-access structures have a minimum gap of
1
.

For the remainder of the section we only consider properties of
q
-access structures induced by generalized
q
-polymatroid ports. These results are the
q
-analogues of classical results pertaining to matroid theory and secret sharing
[
17
]
.

We first generalize the notion of information ratio to generalized
q
-polymatroid ports and demonstrate how it relates to the minimum gap. The information ratio is a measure of the size of each packet compared to the size of the secret, or message.

Definition 29
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a generalized
q
-polymatroid port. Then

\sigma(\mathbf{S})=\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}(P)}\{\rho(p)\}/\rho(P_{0})

is the
information ratio
of
\mathbf{S}
.

Proposition 30
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a generalized
q
-polymatroid port. Then
\sigma(\mathbf{S})\geq g(\mathbf{S})^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
V\in\Gamma
and
W\in\mathcal{A}
with
W\leq V
and fix some
Y\leq P
such that
W\oplus Y=V
. Then

\rho(P_{0})+\rho(W)=\rho(W+P_{0})\leq\rho(V+P_{0})=\rho(V)\leq\rho(W)+\rho(Y),

from which it follows that
\rho(P_{0})\leq\rho(Y)\leq\dim Y\max_{p\in\mathcal{P}(P)}\{\rho(p)\}
. The result then follows by taking the minimum over all such pairs
(V,W)
.
∎

Therefore, by Proposition
30
, all generalized
q
-polymatroid ports with a minimum gap of
1
have an information ratio of at least
1
. We thus conclude that if the size of the secret
\rho(P_{0})
is larger than the size of every share, then the port is not perfect.

Corollary 31
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a generalized
q
-polymatroid port. Suppose that
\sigma(\mathbf{S})<1
. Then
\mathbf{S}
is not perfect.

Since we have introduced the notions of contraction, restriction, duality, and equivalence for
q
-access structures, a natural question to ask is how these relate to generalized
q
-polymatroid ports and to the corresponding notions of the underlying
q
-polymatroid. In particular, the following result shows that contracting or restricting the
q
-access structure induced by a
q
-polymatroid is equivalent to first contracting or restricting the
q
-polymatroid itself, and then inducing a generalized
q
-polymatroid port.

Proposition 32
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a generalized
q
-polymatroid port and let
Z\leq P
. Then
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},Z}(\mathcal{M}|_{P_{0}+Z})
is a generalized
q
-polymatroid port and

\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})|_{Z}=\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},Z}(\mathcal{M}|_{P_{0}+Z}).

Furthermore, if
Z\in\overline{\Gamma}
then
\mathbf{S}_{\pi(P_{0}),\pi(P)}(\mathcal{M}/Z)
is a generalized
q
-polymatroid port and

\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})/Z=\mathbf{S}_{\pi(P_{0}),\pi(P)}(\mathcal{M}/Z),

where
\pi\colon E\rightarrow E/Z
is the canonical quotient map.

Proof.

The statement about restrictions follows since
\rho|_{P_{0}+Z}(V)=\rho(V)
for all

. The statement about contractions follows as
\rho_{E/Z}(\pi(P_{0}))=\rho(P_{0}\,|\,Z)>0
and
\rho_{E/Z}(\pi(P_{0})\,|\,W)=\rho(P_{0}\,|\,\pi^{-1}(W))
for all

.
∎

Proposition 34
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a non-degenerate generalized
q
-polymatroid port with
\rho(P_{0})=\dim P_{0}
. Then
\mathbf{S}_{P^{\perp},P_{0}{\color[rgb]{1,1,1})}\hskip-4.2679pt^{\perp}}(\mathcal{M}^{*})

-polymatroid port and

\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})^{*}\simeq\mathbf{S}_{P^{\perp},P_{0}{\color[rgb]{1,1,1})}\hskip-4.2679pt^{\perp}}(\mathcal{M}^{*}).

Proof.

Let

and
\bot=\bot E

\rho^{*}(P^{\perp}\,|\,V^{\perp}\cap P_{0}^{\perp})=\dim P^{\perp}-\rho(P_{0}\,|\,V).

This shows that
\mathbf{S}_{P^{\perp},P_{0}{\color[rgb]{1,1,1})}\hskip-4.2679pt^{\perp}}(\mathcal{M}^{*})

-polymatroid port as

is non-degenerate, which yields
\rho^{*}(P^{\perp})=\dim P^{\perp}
. The map
\tau\colon\mathcal{L}(P)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(P_{0}^{\perp})
such that

An access structure realized by a
q
-matroid port is perfect, and its minimal access structure can be characterised by the minors of the underlying
q
-matroid.

Theorem 35
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a
q
-matroid port. Then
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
is perfect and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\min}=\{V\leq P\,|\,V\text{ is a basis of }\mathcal{M}|_{V+P_{0}}\text{ and }W+P_{0}\text{ is independent in }\mathcal{M}\text{ for all }W<V\}.

Proof.

\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
is perfect as
\rho
is integer-valued and
0\leq\rho(P_{0}\,|\,V)\leq\rho(P_{0})=1
for any
V\leq P
.
If
V\in\Gamma_{\min}
then
\rho(P_{0}+V)=\rho(V)
and

\rho(P_{0}+W)=\rho(P_{0})+\rho(W)=1+\rho(W)
for every
W\lneq V
.
Suppose that
V
is a dependent set of
\mathcal{M}
. Then there exists
W\lneq V
such that
\rho(W)=\rho(V)
.
Then
\rho(V+P_{0})=\rho(V)=\rho(W)=\rho(W+P_{0})-1\leq\rho(V+P_{0})-1
, which is clearly impossible. It follows that
V
is a basis of
\mathcal{M}|_{V+P_{0}}
. Furthermore, we have that
W
is independent in
\mathcal{M}
for any
W\lneq V
, which yields that

\dim(W+P_{0})=\dim(W)+1=\rho(W)+1=\rho(W+P_{0})
, so that
W+P_{0}
is an independent space of
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Remark 36
.

Whereas linear codes always induce matroids,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear rank-metric codes do not necessarily induce
q
-matroids, but rather
q
-polymatroids. Consequently, not all ports induced by rank-metric codes are perfect. Theorem
35
is the generalization of a well-known result on matroid ports, see
[
30
]
. However, it differs in the sense that the minimal access structure of a
q
-matroid port is not the set of
V\leq P
for which
V+P_{0}
is a circuit in the underlying
q
-matroid. While this condition is sufficient, Example
37
shows it is not necessary. All examples in this paper were computed using the Magma algebra system
[
8
]
.

Example 37
.

Consider
\mathcal{C}=\langle M_{1},M_{2},M_{3},M_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{4\times 2}
, where

M_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\\
1&1\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix},\>M_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\\
1&0\\
1&1\\
\end{bmatrix},\>M_{3}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\\
1&0\\
1&0\\
\end{bmatrix},\>M_{4}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\\
0&1\\
0&1\\
\end{bmatrix}.

In fact
\mathcal{C}
may be viewed as a vector rank-metric code over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, generated by
m_{1}=(1,0,\alpha^{2},\alpha)
and
m_{2}=(0,1,1,1)
for a primitive element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{4}
. Hence,
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a
q
-matroid.
Let
P_{0}=\langle\mathbf{e}_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
and
P=\langle\mathbf{e}_{2},\mathbf{e}_{3},\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
. Then
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(P_{0})>0
so
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}})
is a perfect
q
-matroid port by Theorem
35
. Furthermore,

\Gamma_{\min}=\{\langle\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},\langle\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},\langle\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\}.

Note, however, that the circuits of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}}
are
\{\langle\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{3}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},\langle\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{4},\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},

\langle\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
,
\langle\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{2}+\mathbf{e}_{4},\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}},\langle\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\}
, while
v=\langle\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}+P_{0}
is not a circuit as
\langle\mathbf{e}_{1}+\mathbf{e}_{3}+\mathbf{e}_{4}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}\leq v
.

A codeword of a linear code is called
minimal
if its support does not contain the support of any other linearly independent codeword. This notion was extended to vector rank-metric codes in
[
2
]
. In the setting of classical secret sharing based on linear codes, Massey
[
32
]
showed that the minimal access structure of a representable matroid port is completely determined by certain minimal codewords of the dual code. Here we establish the
q
-analogue of this result for vector rank-metric codes. As in Remark
36
, our result provides only a sufficient condition for an arbitrary vector rank-metric code, as illustrated in Example
40
. The condition becomes both necessary and sufficient under the stronger assumption that the dual code is minimal. Recall that for an element
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, we write
\Pi(v)
for the matrix in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times m}
obtained by expanding the entries of
v
with respect to the basis
\Pi=\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 38
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be a vector rank-metric code and
\Pi
a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A codeword
v\in\mathcal{C}
is a
minimal codeword
if for any
v^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
with
\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(v^{\prime}))\leq\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(v))
implies
v^{\prime}=\alpha v
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. The set of minimal codewords of
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{\min}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{C}
is
minimal
if
\mathcal{C}_{\min}=\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 39
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be a vector rank-metric code and
\Pi
be any basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Consider the
q
-matroid port
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M}_{\Pi(\mathcal{C})})
. If there exists
X\in\mathcal{C}^{\perp}_{\min}
such that
P_{0}\leq\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(X))
, then
\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(X))\cap P\in\Gamma_{\min}
. Moreover, if
(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})_{\min}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
then this condition is also necessary, that is, for every
V\in\Gamma_{\min}
there exists
X\in(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})_{\min}
such that
P_{0}\leq\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(X))
and
\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(X))\cap P=V
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathcal{D}=\Pi(\mathcal{C})
. We first prove the sufficient condition. To this end, suppose
X\in(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})_{\min}
such that
P_{0}\leq\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(X))
and let
V=\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(X))
. We then have

P_{0}+V\cap P=(P_{0}+P)\cap V=V
as
P_{0}\oplus P=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Since
X\in(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})_{\min}
, we have that
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V\cap P)=0
, so by Lemma
12
and the fact that
\dim(V\cap P)=\dim(V)-1
, we obtain

\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V\cap P)=mn-\dim\mathcal{D}-m(n-\dim V+1)+\dim\mathcal{D}((V\cap P)^{\perp})=0.

By Lemma
12
and since
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V)=m
, we have that

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{D}((P_{0}+V\cap P)^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{D}(V^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V)-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m\dim V^{\perp}

\displaystyle=-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m(n-\dim V+1),

and

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{D}((V\cap P)^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V\cap P)-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m\dim((V\cap P)^{\perp})

\displaystyle=-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m(n-\dim V+1).

Therefore,
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(P_{0}\,|\,V)=\dim\mathcal{D}((P_{0}+V\cap P)^{\perp})-\dim\mathcal{D}((V\cap P)^{\perp})=0
, so
V\in\Gamma
.

Suppose now that
W<V\cap P
, so
W+P_{0}<V
, which implies
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W+P_{0})=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W)=0
. Again by Lemma
12
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}((W+P_{0})^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W+P_{0})-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m\dim((W+P_{0})^{\perp})

\displaystyle=-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m(n-\dim W+1)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(W^{\perp})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W)-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m(n-\dim W)

\displaystyle=-mn+\dim\mathcal{D}+m(n-\dim W),

which proves
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(P_{0}\,|\,W)=\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(P_{0})
, so
V\in\Gamma_{\min}
.

Suppose now also
(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})_{\min}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
and let
V\in\Gamma_{\min}
. As
\rho_{\mathcal{D}}(P_{0})=1
we have
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(P_{0})=\dim\mathcal{D}(P_{0}^{\perp})+m-\dim\mathcal{D}=0
, so for all
W<V
then
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W+P_{0})=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W)
by Theorem
35
. Choose a non-zero
Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V+P_{0})
with
Y\notin\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V)
, which exists as
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V)+m=\dim\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V+P_{0})
. Let
T=\operatorname{colsp}Y
and let
U=T\cap V\leq V
. Suppose, for a contradiction, that
U
is contained in some proper subspace
W<V
. Then
T\leq U+P_{0}\leq W+P_{0}
, so
Y\in\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W+P_{0})=\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(W)\leq\mathcal{D}^{\perp}(V)
, which contradicts the fact that
\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
is a minimal code. Therefore,
U=T\cap V=V
, which yields
V\leq T
. Additionally, as
T\leq V+P_{0}
we then have
\operatorname{colsp}(\Pi(Y))=V\oplus P_{0}
.
∎

.

\mathbb{F}_{8}=\mathbb{F}_{2}[\alpha]

Just as uniform matroids correspond to threshold access structures in classical secret sharing via MDS codes, the

Proposition 41
.

Suppose
\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{U}_{\dim E,k})
is a generalized
q
-matroid port. Then
\mathbf{S}
is
k
-threshold.

Proof.

The rank function of
\mathcal{U}_{n,k}
satisfies
\rho(V)=\min\{\dim V,k\}
for all
V\leq E
, so for any
V\leq P
then
\rho(P_{0}+V)=\rho(V)
if and only if
\dim V\geq k
.
∎

Note for Proposition
41
that if
\dim P<k
then
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{U}_{n,k})
is degenerate.

We next establish a
q
-analogue of the Brickell–Davenport theorem, showing that when a port of a
q
-polymatroid satisfies certain ideality and connectivity conditions, the restricted structure
\mathcal{M}|_{P}
is in fact a
q
-matroid. We first prove some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 42
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a perfect and connected
q
-polymatroid port such that
\rho(P_{0})=\dim P_{0}=1
. For any
V\in\mathcal{A}
there exists
W\in\mathcal{A}
such that
V\cap W=0
and
V+W\in\Gamma
, with the additional minimality property that if
W^{\prime}<W
then
V+W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Fix
V\in\mathcal{A}
and choose any
p\in\mathcal{P}(V)
. By connectedness of the port, there exists
G\in\Gamma_{\min}
with
p\leq G
. In particular we have
G\cap V\geq p>0
. Choose now a complement
U<G
of
G\cap V
such that
U\oplus(G\cap V)=G
.
U
is a proper subspace of
G
, so
U\notin\Gamma
by minimality of
G
. By perfectness then
U\in\mathcal{A}
and
U\cap V=0
. As
G\leq V+U
we have by monotonicity of
\Gamma
that
V+U\in\Gamma
. Consider now the non-empty family
\mathcal{S}=\{U^{\prime}\leq U\,|\,V+U^{\prime}\in\Gamma\}
, as
U\in\mathcal{S}
. There then exists a minimal element of
\mathcal{S}
, which we denote
W
. Clearly
W\leq U<G
, and
G\in\Gamma_{\min}
, so
W\in\mathcal{A}
. Additionally, by minimality of
W
in
\mathcal{S}
then
W^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{S}
, so
V+W^{\prime}\notin\Gamma
, which implies
W+W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
.
∎

Lemma 43
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
be a perfect
q
-polymatroid port such that
\rho(P_{0})=\dim P_{0}=1
. Suppose
W\leq P
and
y\in\mathcal{P}(P)
such that both
W\in\mathcal{A}
and
y+W\in\Gamma
. Then

\rho(y\,|\,W)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(y\,|\,W+P_{0})=0.

Proof.

Note that

\rho(W)+\rho(P_{0}\,|\,W)+\rho(y\,|\,W+P_{0})=\rho(y+W+P_{0})=\rho(y+W)+\rho(P_{0}\,|\,y+W).

By submodularity and non-negativity of
\rho
we have that

1\leq\rho(y\,|\,W+P_{0})+1\leq\rho(y\,|\,W)\leq 1,

and the result follows.
∎

Definition 44
.

A generalized
q
-polymatroid port
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
is called
ideal
if
\rho(P_{0})=\rho(p)
for all
p\in\mathcal{P}(P)
.

In terms of a coset coding scheme then the ideal generalized
q
-polymatroid ports can be seen as those for which the size of the secret is the same size as the information in each packet.

Theorem 45

(
q
-Brickell-Davenport Theorem)
.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}
be an ideal, perfect and connected
q
-polymatroid port with
\rho(P_{0})=\dim P_{0}=1
. Then
\mathcal{M}|_{P}
is a
q
-matroid.

Proof.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that there exists a subspace
V\leq P
such that
\rho(V)
is not a non-negative integer. Assume that with respect to inclusion,
V
is a minimal subspace of
P
with this property. Write
n=\lfloor\rho(V)\rfloor
, so
n<\rho(V)<n+1.
For any
U\leq P
and any
u\in\mathcal{P}(U)
let
U_{u}
denote a fixed hyperplane of
U
that is a complement of
u
in
U
, i.e.
U=U_{u}\oplus u
. Let
y\in\mathcal{P}(V)
, so
\rho(V_{y})\in\mathbb{N}
, and as
\rho(y)=1
it follows that

\displaystyle\rho(V_{y})\leq n<\rho(V)=\rho(V_{y}+y)\leq\rho(V_{y})+1.

(4)

This gives us that
n-1<\rho(V_{y})\leq n
and since
\rho(V_{y})
is an integer we obtain
\rho(V_{y})=n
. In particular,

\displaystyle 0<\rho(y\,|\,V_{y})<1.

(5)

Since
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}
is perfect, we have that the union of the elements of
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{A}
form a partition of
\mathcal{L}(P)
, and as such we consider the case that
V\in\Gamma
and the case
V\in\mathcal{A}
.

Suppose first
V\in\Gamma
. For any
y\in\mathcal{P}(V)
then
V_{y}\in\Gamma
by Lemma
43
, since if not, it would contradict
(
5
)
. Suppose now
V^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\min}
such that
V^{\prime}\leq V
and let
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}(V^{\prime})
. Then
V^{\prime}_{y^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{A}
and Lemma
43
yields
\rho(y^{\prime}\,|\,V^{\prime}_{y^{\prime}}+P_{0})=0
, so

\rho(y^{\prime}\,|\,V_{y^{\prime}})=\rho(V)-\rho(V_{y^{\prime}})=\rho(V+P_{0})-\rho(V_{y^{\prime}})=\rho(y^{\prime}\,|\,V_{y^{\prime}}+P_{0})=0,

which contradicts (
5
) so
\rho(V)\in\mathbb{N}
.

Suppose now
V\in\mathcal{A}
. By Lemma
42
there exists
W\in\mathcal{A}
such that
V+W\in\Gamma
,
V\cap W=0
and if
W^{\prime}<W
then
V+W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
. Let
\dim W=\ell
and choose
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}(W)
such that

W=x_{1}\oplus x_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus x_{\ell}.

For each
j\in[\ell]
let
W_{j}=\oplus^{\ell}_{i=j}x_{i}
. By Lemma
43
, for any
x\in\mathcal{P}(W)
we have
\rho(x\,|\,V+W_{x})=1
, which yields
\rho(x_{j}\,|\,V+W_{j+1})=1
for all
j=1,\ldots,\ell-1
. We now have that

\displaystyle\rho(V+W)

\displaystyle=\rho(V+W_{1})+\rho(x_{1}\,|\,V+W_{x_{1}})

\displaystyle=\rho(V+W_{2})+\rho(x_{2}\,|\,V+W_{x_{2}})+\rho(x_{1}\,|\,V+W_{x_{1}})

\displaystyle\>\,\,\vdots

\displaystyle=\rho(V)+\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}\rho(x_{i}\,|\,V+W_{i})

\displaystyle=\rho(V)+\dim W.

Now, suppose
x\in\mathcal{P}(V)
. By Lemma
43
we have that
V_{x}+W\in\Gamma
for all
x\in\mathcal{P}(V)
as
\rho(x\,|\,V_{x}+W)\leq\rho(x\,|\,V_{x})<1
. Furthermore, we have
\rho(V_{x}+W)=\rho(V)+\dim W
, as
\rho(V_{x}+W)\leq\rho(V)+\dim W
holds trivially by submodularity, and
\rho(x\,|\,V_{x}+W)\leq\rho(x\,|\,V_{x})
implies
\rho(V_{x})+\dim W\leq\rho(V_{x}+W)
.

Choose
Z\in\Gamma
that is minimal among those that contain
W
and are contained in
V+W
. Thus,
W<Z\leq V+W
and
V\cap Z>0
. Let
z\in\mathcal{P}(V\cap Z)
, so by Lemma
43
, we have that
\rho(z\,|\,Z_{z}+P_{0})=0
, from which we obtain
\rho(z\,|\,V_{z}+W)=\rho(z\,|\,V_{z}+W+P_{0})=0.
Therefore,

\rho(V)+\dim W=\rho(V+W)=\rho(V_{z}+W)+\rho(z\,|\,V_{z}+W)=\rho(V_{z}+W)=\rho(V_{z})+\dim W,

but this is a contradiction to (
4
), which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 46
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
q
-polymatroid and let
P_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(E)
such that
\rho(P_{0})=1
. If
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})

q
-polymatroid port for every complement
P
of
P_{0}
then
\mathcal{M}
is a
q
-matroid.

Proof.

For any
V,W\leq P
let
\mathcal{L}(V)+\mathcal{L}(W)\coloneqq\{V^{\prime}+W^{\prime}\,|\,V^{\prime}\leq V,W^{\prime}\leq W\}
. Denote the complements of

in

as
\mathbf{C}(P_{0})=\{P\leq E\,|\,P\oplus P_{0}=E\}
, so

\mathcal{L}(E)=\bigcup_{P\in\mathbf{C}(P_{0})}(\mathcal{L}(P)+\mathcal{L}(P_{0})).

As
\mathbf{S}_{P_{0},P}(\mathcal{M})
is an ideal, perfect and connected

-polymatroid port then
\rho(V)\in\mathbb{Z}
for any

by Theorem

, and the result follows.
∎

The requirements of Corollary
46
are evidently quite restrictive as for any
P_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(E)
, there are a total of
\frac{q^{n-1}-1}{q-1}
complements of
P_{0}
. Moreover, the conditions of Theorem

are not sufficient to ensure that the entire
q
-polymatroid is a
q
-matroid. It would therefore be of interest to identify more relaxed conditions under which the conclusion of Corollary
46
still holds.

The definition of a connected
q
-matroid remains an open problem. In the classical setting, a well-established result asserts that any connected matroid port corresponds to a unique connected matroid realizing that port
[
27
]
. This result plays a central role in the structural theory of matroids and their applications to secret sharing. However, a direct
q
-analogue appears unlikely. The theorems that guarantee uniqueness and existence in the classical case rely on combinatorial properties that do not readily carry over to the
q
-analogue. In particular, if a notion of connectedness can be defined for
q
-matroids, the correspondence between connected
q
-matroid ports and uniquely connected
q
-matroids may not hold.

Remark 47
.

The results of this section pertaining to general
q
-polymatroids extend naturally to finite modular, complemented lattices which include finite Boolean and subspace lattices. In this sense, the classical theory of access structures and polymatroid ports is recovered as a special case of this more general setting.

V
Conclusions and future work

In this work, we developed a
q
-polymatroidal framework for analyzing information leakage in secure linear network coding schemes based on nested rank-metric codes. We showed that the information gained by a wiretapper observing an arbitrary collection of network links is fully characterized by the conditional rank function of a representable
q
-polymatroid associated with the underlying code pair. This provides a direct combinatorial interpretation of information leakage that is independent of the specific network topology and depends only on the algebraic structure of the code.

Building on this connection, we introduced the notions of
q
-polymatroid ports and
q
-access structures, which generalize classical matroid ports and access structures from the set-based to the subspace-based setting. These objects capture reconstructing spaces and privacy spaces in a unified manner and naturally extend several well-known correspondences from linear secret sharing. In particular, we established a rank-metric analogue of Massey’s correspondence between minimal codewords and minimal qualified sets, and proved a
q
-analogue of the Brickell–Davenport theorem, showing that ideal, perfect, and connected
q
-access structures arise from
q
-matroids under suitable structural assumptions.

By formulating information leakage in terms of
q
-polymatroid ports, we obtain a setting in which duality, minors, and minimal reconstructing spaces can be analyzed using standard
q
-polymatroid techniques. This makes it possible to study secrecy properties of rank-metric schemes using the same structural tools that are familiar from matroid-based secret sharing.

The framework developed in this paper suggests several directions for further research. From a combinatorial perspective, it is natural to further investigate the structure theory of
q
-access structures independently of representability. In classical matroid theory, excluded-minor characterizations and connectivity properties play a central role in understanding which access structures arise from linear secret sharing schemes. Developing analogous characterizations for
q
-access structures may shed light on which secrecy patterns can be realized by rank-metric codes. This could open up a new line of research trying to find and construct such specific rank-metric codes.

Another direction concerns the classification of ideal and threshold
q
-access structures. While MRD codes give rise to perfect threshold structures, it remains open to characterize all
q
-matroids whose ports induce threshold or near-threshold behavior. Such results would parallel classical theorems on uniform matroids and ideal secret sharing and could inform the design of rank-metric schemes with optimal trade-offs between secrecy and efficiency. From the coding-theoretic viewpoint, extending the present framework to broader classes of codes is of interest. In particular, it would be valuable to study whether similar
q
-polymatroidal descriptions arise for non-linear rank-metric codes, subspace codes, or codes over more general algebraic structures.

Finally, the entropy-based characterization of representable
q
-polymatroids suggests deeper connections between information theory and
q
-polymatroid theory. Exploring whether general
q
-polymatroids admit operational interpretations in terms of entropy or mutual information, beyond the representable case, may provide new insights into abstract information inequalities in the subspace setting.

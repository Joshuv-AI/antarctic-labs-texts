1
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite and simple graph with both vertex set and edge set denoted as
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively. If
S
is a subset of
V(G)
, then the induced subgraph of
G
on
S
is a graph whose vertex set is
S
and whose edge set is comprised of all edges in
G
that connect two vertices in
S
. This induced subgraph is denoted by
G[S]
. For a vertex
v
in
G
, the degree of
v
is the number of edges in
G
that connect to
v
. The degree of
v
is written as
d_{G}(v)
. The minimum degree of vertices in
G
is denoted by
\delta(G)
. A balanced bipartite graph, such as
\mathcal{B}
, of order
2n
is a special type of graph that has two parts,
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, each containing
n
vertices, where the total number of vertices is
2n
.

The forest number of a graph
G
is the size of the largest subset of vertices of
G
that form an induced forest. We use
f(G)
to represent the forest number of graph
G
. A decycling set or a feedback vertex set of a graph is a set of vertices whose removal results in a forest. The smallest possible size of a decycling set of
G
is represented by
\nabla(G)
. Finding the decycling number of
G
is equivalent to determining the largest order of an induced forest, i.e.,
f(G)+\nabla(G)=|V(G)|
, as proposed by Erdös et al. in 1986
[
9
]
.

The problem of destroying all cycles in a graph by deleting a set of vertices was first introduced in combinatorial circuit design in 1974 by Johnson
[
12
]
. Since then, it has found applications in various fields, including deadlock prevention in operating systems by Wang et al. in 1985
[
25
]
and Silberschatz et al. in 2003
[
24
]
, the constraint satisfaction problem and Bayesian inference in artificial intelligence by Bar-Yehuda et al. in 1998
[
4
]
, monopolies in synchronous distributed systems by Peleg in 1998
[
19
]
and 2002
[
20
]
, the converters’ placement problem in optical networks by Kleinberg and Kumar in 1999
[
16
]
, and VLSI chip design by Festa et al. in 2000
[
10
]
. It has been proven that determining the decycling number is NP-complete for general graphs, as per Karp et al. 1975
[
13
]
. This also implies that even for planar graphs, bipartite graphs, and perfect graphs, the computation complexity of finding their decycling numbers is not reduced.

Akiyama and Watanabe in 1987
[
1
]
and, independently, Albertson and Haas in 1998
[
2
]
conjectured that every planar bipartite graph on
n
vertices contains an induced forest on at least
\frac{5n}{8}
vertices. Motivated by this conjecture, Alon in 2003
[
3
]
studied induced forests in sparse bipartite graphs and showed that every bipartite graph on
n
vertices with an average degree at most
d\geq 1
contains an induced forest on at least
(\frac{1}{2}+{\rm e}^{-bd^{2}})n
vertices, for some absolute positive constant
b
. However, there exist bipartite graphs on
n
vertices with an average degree at most
d\geq 1
that do not contain an induced forest on at least
(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{b^{\prime}\sqrt{d}}})n
vertices. Conlon, Fox, and Sudakov in 2014
[
6
]
improved Alon’s lower bound to
(\frac{1}{2}+d^{-db})n
for
d\geq 2
. Wang, Xie, and Yu in 2017
[
27
]
proved that every simple bipartite planar graph on
n
vertices contains an induced forest on at least
\lceil\frac{4n+3}{7}\rceil
vertices. Wang and Wu, in their research on the forest number of graphs and their products in 2023
[
26
]
, proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.1

Let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph on
2n
vertices such that
d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
for all
v\in\,V(G)
. Then

.

In the articles
[
5
,
23
]
, upper and lower bounds for the forest number of a graph in terms of its order, size, and maximum degree are provided. Beineke and Vandell
[
5
]
studied two families of graph products, namely grids and hypercubes. The forest number of hypercubes has been further researched by Focardi and Luccio
[
11
]
. The forest number is a significant parameter of a graph and has been widely studied for planar graphs
[
7
,
8
,
14
,
17
,
18
]
, regular graphs
[
21
,
22
]
, and subcubic graphs
[
15
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the properties of the largest subsets of graph

, and

S
of vertex set of
\mathcal{B}

is a forest, and

2
Main Results

In this section, we prove our results regarding the structure and cardinality of the largest subsets of vertices in graph G that form induced forests. The following theorem validates Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 2.1

Let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph on
2n
vertices such that
d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
for all
v\in\,V(G)
. Then
f(G)=n+1
.

Suppose
G
is a balanced bipartite graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Let
v_{1}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
. Since
G
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
(V_{1},V_{2})
, both
G[V_{1}\cup\{v_{2}\}]
and
G[\{v_{1}\}\cup V_{2}]
are forests. Therefore,
f(G)\geq n+1
. Now, suppose
S
is a subset of the vertex set of
G
such that
|S|=n+2
. Without loss of generality, assume
S\cap\,V_{1}=L_{1}
,
S\cap\,V_{2}=L_{2}
and

|L_{1}|\geq|L_{2}|
. Since
|S|\geq\,n+2
and
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=n
,
|L_{1}|\geq|L_{2}|\geq 2
. One can observe that the set of possible cardinalities of
L_{2}
is
\{2,3,\ldots,\frac{n+1}{2}\}
when
n
is odd and
\{2,3,\ldots,\frac{n+2}{2}\}
when
n
is even. The cardinality of
L_{1}
is
n+2-|L_{2}|
. For every vertex
v
in
V(G)
, it is assumed that
d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
. Therefore, for every
k
in
\{2,3,...,\frac{n+1}{2}\}
, if
|L_{2}|=k
, then
|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\frac{n}{2}+3-k
for all
x\in L_{2}
. Thus, for every
k
in
\{2,3,...,\frac{n+2}{2}\}
, if
|L_{2}|=k
, then

\sum_{x\in\,L_{2}}|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\sum_{x\in\,L_{2}}(\frac{n}{2}+3-k)=k(\frac{n}{2}+3-k)
. Function
g(k)=k(\frac{n}{2}+3-k)
is continuous on both intervals
[2,\frac{n+1}{2}]
and
[2,\frac{n+2}{2}]
. Additionally,
\frac{\partial}{\partial\,k}(g(k))=\frac{n}{2}+3-2k
. Therefore,

\sum_{x\in\,L_{2}}|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\min\{g(2),g(\frac{n+1}{2}),g(\frac{n+2}{2}),g(\frac{n+6}{4})\}\geq\,n+2
. By a well-known theorem in graph theory, since
G[S]
is an induced subgraph of
G
by
n+2
vertices and greater or equal to
n+2
edges, it is not an acyclic graph. Therefore, the forest number of graph
G
,
f(G)
, cannot be greater than or equal to
n+2
. However, we have already proved that
f(G)
is greater than or equal to
n+1
. Thus,
f(G)
must be equal to
n+1
, as wanted.

\Box

Theorem 2.2

Consider a balanced bipartite graph
G
with
2n
vertices, partitioned into
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Suppose that every vertex in
G
has a degree of at least
\frac{n}{2}+1
. Let
S
be a subset of
G
with
n+1
vertices such that
G[S]
is a forest. Then either
|S\cap V_{1}|
or
|S\cap V_{2}|
lies within the range of the set
\{1,2,\frac{n}{2}\}
.

Proof.
Assuming without loss of generality that
L_{1}=S\cap V_{1}
,
L_{2}=S\cap V_{2}
, and
|L_{1}|\geq|L_{2}|
. We consider the three cases as follows:

1)
|L_{2}|\in\{1,2,\frac{n}{2}\}
. In this case there is nothing to prove.

2)
|L_{1}|=|L_{2}|=\frac{n+1}{2}
. In this case by some simple calculations,
|V_{1}\backslash\,L_{1}|=|V_{2}\backslash\,L_{2}|=\frac{n-1}{2}
. For every vertex
v
in
V(G)
, it is assumed that
d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
. Therefore, for every vertex
x
in
L_{1}\cup\,L_{2}
,
d_{G[S]}(x)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1-\frac{n-1}{2}=\frac{3}{2}
. However, for every vertex
x
in
L_{1}\cup\,L_{2}
,
d_{G[S]}(x)
is an integer. Thus, for every vertex
x
in
L_{1}\cup\,L_{2}
,
d_{G[S]}(x)\geq 2
and it follows that
G[S]
cannot be a forest.

3) Otherwise, when
n
is odd, the set of possible cardinalities of
L_{2}
is
\{3,4,\ldots,\frac{n-1}{2}\}
and when
n
is even, it is
\{3,4,\ldots,\frac{n-2}{2}\}
. The cardinality of set
L_{1}
can be calculated as
n+1-|L_{2}|
. For every vertex
v
in
V(G)
, it is assumed that
d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
. Therefore, for every
k
in
\{2,3,...,\frac{n-1}{2}\}
, if
|L_{2}|=k
, then
|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\frac{n}{2}+2-k
for all
x\in L_{2}
. Thus, for every
k
in
\{2,3,...,\frac{n-1}{2}\}
, if
|L_{2}|=k
, then

\sum_{x\in\,L_{2}}|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\sum_{x\in\,L_{2}}(\frac{n}{2}+2-k)=k(\frac{n}{2}+2-k)
. Function
h(k)=k(\frac{n}{2}+2-k)
is continuous on both intervals
[2,\frac{n-1}{2}]
and
[2,\frac{n-2}{2}]
. Plus,
\frac{\partial}{\partial\,k}(h(k))=\frac{n}{2}+2-2k
. Therefore,

\sum_{x\in\,L_{2}}|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\min\{h(3),h(\frac{n-2}{2}),h(\frac{n-1}{2}),h(\frac{n+4}{4})\}\geq\,n+1
. Thus,
G[S]
is an induced subgraph of
G
with
n+1
vertices and at least
n+1
edges. It follows that
G[S]
cannot be a forest and this completed the proof.

\Box

Theorem 2.3

Let
\lambda\in\{1,2,\frac{n}{2}\}
. Then there exist infinitely many balanced bipartite graphs, such as
G
, that satisfy the following conditions:

1.

the graph
G
has a minimum degree of at least
\frac{|V(G)|}{4}+1
;

2.

there exists at least one subset
S
of
V(G)
with
\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+1
elements such that the subgraph induced by
S
is a forest;

3.

the intersection of
S
with one of the partitions of
G
has cardinality
\lambda
.

Proof.
We can classify the three cases based on the value of
\lambda
as follows:

1.

\lambda=1
. This case holds for any bipartite graph, as proven in Theorem
2.1
.

2.

\lambda=2
. In this case, for any positive even number
n
, we can form a bipartite graph
X
with bipartition
(A,B)
where
A=\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
,
|B|=n-1
,
d_{X}(a_{1})=d_{X}(a_{2})=\frac{n-2}{2}+1
, and
|N_{X}(a_{1})\cap\,N_{X}(a_{2})|=1
. We also create another bipartite graph
Y
with bipartition
(F,H)
, where
|F|=n-2
,
H=\{h\}
,
d_{Y}(f)\geq 0
for all
f\in\,F
, and
d_{Y}(h)\geq\frac{n}{2}-1
. Finally, we define
G
as the bipartite graph with bipartition
(A\cup\,F,B\cup\,H)
and edge set
E(X)\cup\,E(Y)\cup\{uv:(u\in\,A~\wedge~v\in\,H)~\vee~(u\in\,B~\wedge~v\in\,F)\}
. One can check that
G
is a balanced bipartite graph on
2n
vertices with a minimum degree of at least
\frac{n}{2}+1
, and for
S=A\cup\,B
, the three statements
|S|=n+1
,
G[S]
is a forest, and
|S\cap(A\cup\,F)|=2
hold.

3.

\lambda=\frac{n}{2}
. In this case, for any positive even number
n
, we can form a bipartite graph
X
with parts
A
and
B
such that
|A|=|B|-1=\frac{n}{2}
,
X
is isomorphic to
P_{n+1}
, and
d_{X}(a)=2
for all
a\in\,A
. We also create another bipartite graph
Y
with parts
F
and
H
such that
|F|=|H|+1=\frac{n}{2}
,
d_{Y}(f)\geq 0
for all
f\in\,F
, and
d_{Y}(h)\geq 1
for all
h\in\,H
. Finally, we define
G
as a bipartite graph with parts
A\cup\,F
and
B\cup\,H
, and edge set
E(X)\cup\,E(Y)\cup\{uv:(u\in\,A~\wedge~v\in\,H)~\vee~(u\in\,B~\wedge~v\in\,F)\}
. One can see that
G
is a balanced bipartite graph on
2n
vertices with a minimum degree of at least
\frac{n}{2}+1
, and for
S=A\cup\,B
, the three statements
|S|=n+1
,
G[S]
is a forest, and
|S\cap(A\cup\,F)|=\frac{n}{2}
hold. This concludes the proof.
\Box

Theorem 2.4

Conceder
G
is a balanced bipartite graph with
2n
vertices, minimum degree of at least
\frac{n}{2}+1
, and parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Let
S
be a subset of vertex set of
G
with three conditions
(i)

|S|=n+1
,
(ii)

G[S]
is a forest, and
(iii)

\min\{|S\cap\,V_{1}|,|S\cap\,V_{2}|\}=2
. Then
n
is even.

Proof.
Suppose
S
is a subset of the vertices of
G
that satisfy the three conditions of the theorem. Without loss of generality, let
L_{1}=S\cap V_{1}=\{u,v\}
and
L_{2}=S\cap V_{2}
. We assume
d_{G}(u)
and
d_{G}(v)
are both greater than or equal to
\frac{n}{2}+1
. Therefore,
|N_{G}(u)\cap L_{2}|
and
|N_{G}(v)\cap L_{2}|
are both greater than or equal to
\frac{n}{2}
, since
|L_{2}|=n-1
. Now, assume that
n
is odd. Since
|N_{G}(u)\cap L_{2}|
and
|N_{G}(v)\cap L_{2}|
are integers, they must both be greater than or equal to
\frac{n+1}{2}
. It follows that
G[S]
is an induced subgraph of
G
with
n+1
vertices and at least
n+1
edges. Therefore,
G[S]
is not a forest, which contradicts our assumption that
G[S]
is a forest. Thus, we have shown that
n
must be even, as desired.

\Box

Given an odd positive integer
n
, let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph with
2n
vertices, the minimum degree of at least
\frac{n}{2}+1
, and parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Additionally, assume that there exists a subset
S
of
G
with
n+1
vertices, such that

is a forest. By Theorem

, we know that
\min\{|S\cap\,V_{1}|,|S\cap\,V_{2}|\}\in\{1,2,\frac{n}{2}\}
. Since
\min\{|S\cap\,V_{1}|,|S\cap\,V_{2}|\}
is an integer, we can use Theorem
2.4
to conclude that
\min\{S\cap\,V_{1},S\cap\,V_{2}\}=1
. Furthermore, for any
v_{1}\in\,V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in\,V_{2}
, both
G[\{v_{1}\}\cup\,V_{2}]
and
G[\{v_{2}\}\cup\,V_{1}]
are forests. Therefore, we can infer the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5

Let
n
be an odd positive integer and let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph with
2n
vertices, minimum degree of at least
\frac{n}{2}+1
, and parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Also, let
S
be a subset of vertex set of
G
such that
|S|=n+1
. Then
G[S]
is a forest if and only if
\min\{|S\cap\,V_{1}|,|S\cap\,V_{2}|\}=1
.

Theorem 2.6

Let
k\geq 2
be a integer. Then there exist infinitely many balanced bipartite graphs, such as
G
, that
\delta(G)=k
and
f(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+1
.

Proof.
Let
n
be an odd integer such that
n\geq k-1
. Suppose
H
is a balanced bipartite graph with
2n
vertices and parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Assume that
\delta(H)\geq\frac{n}{2}+1
and
\max_{v\in V_{2}}d_{H}(v)=n
. Let
w\in V_{2}
with
d_{H}(w)=n
and
\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{k-2}\}\subseteq V_{2}\backslash\{w\}
. Also, let
P_{2}
be a path with two vertices
x
and
y
. Consider the balanced bipartite graph
G
with parts
V_{1}\cup\{x\}
and
V_{2}\cup\{y\}
and edge set
E(H)\cup\{xw_{i}:i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k-2\}\}\cup\{yu:u\in V_{1}\}\cup\{xw\}
. One can verify that
\delta(G)=d_{G}(x)=k
,
|V(G)|=2n+2
, and
f(G)\geq n+2
. However, by Theorem
2.1
,
f(H)=n+1
. Therefore, by Corollary
2.5
, if
S\subseteq V(G)
,
|S|=n+3
, and
G[S]
is a forest, then
\min\{|S\cap V_{1}|,|S\cap V_{2}|\}=1
and
\{x,y\}\subseteq S
. We now consider two cases: (i)
S\cap V_{1}=\{a\}
and
S\cap V_{2}=V_{2}
. In this case,
N_{G}(y)\cap N_{G}(w)=\{x,a\}
, which is a contradiction since
G[S]
is a forest. (ii)
S\cap V_{1}=V_{1}
and
S\cap V_{2}=\{b\}
. In this case,
|V(G[S])|=n+3
and
|E(G[S])|\geq n+3
(because
d_{G}(y)=n+1
and
d_{G}(b)\geq 2
), which is again a contradiction since
G[S]
is a forest. Thus,
f(G)=n+2=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+1
, as wanted.

\Box

Theorem 2.7

Let
n
be an odd positive integer and let
G
be a balanced bipartite graph with
2n
vertices, minimum degree at least
\frac{n+1}{2}
, and parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. If
\max\{|\{v:v\in\,V_{1}~\wedge~d_{G}(v)=\frac{n+1}{2}\}|,|\{u:u\in\,V_{2}~\wedge~d_{G}(u)=\frac{n+1}{2}\}|\}\leq 1
, then
f(G)=n+1
.

Proof.
Suppose
G
is a balanced bipartite graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Let
v_{1}\in V_{1}
and
v_{2}\in V_{2}
. Since
G
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
(V_{1},V_{2})
, both
G[V_{1}\cup\{v_{2}\}]
and
G[\{v_{1}\}\cup V_{2}]
are forests. Therefore,
f(G)\geq n+1
. Now, let us suppose that
S
is a subset of the vertex set of
G
such that
|S|=n+2
. Without loss of generality, assume
S\cap V_{1}=L_{1}
,
S\cap V_{2}=L_{2}
, and
|L_{1}|\geq|L_{2}|
. Since
|S|\geq n+2
and
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=n
, we know that
|L_{1}|\geq|L_{2}|\geq 2
. We can observe that the set of possible cardinalities of
L_{2}
is
\{2,3,\ldots,\frac{n+1}{2}\}
when
n
is odd. The cardinality of
L_{1}
is
n+2-|L_{2}|
. It is assumed that
d_{G}(v)\geq\frac{n+1}{2}
for every vertex
v
in
V(G)
and that
\max\{|\{v:v\in V_{1}~\wedge~d_{G}(v)=\frac{n+1}{2}\}|,|\{u:u\in V_{2}~\wedge~d_{G}(u)=\frac{n+1}{2}\}|\}\leq 1
. So, for every
k
in
\{2,3,...,\frac{n+1}{2}\}
, if
|L_{2}|=k
, then
\sum_{x\in L_{2}}|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\,\frac{n+1}{2}+2-k+(k-1)(\frac{n+1}{2}+3-k)
. The function
g(k)=\frac{n+1}{2}+2-k+(k-1)(\frac{n+1}{2}+3-k)
is continuous on the interval
[2,\frac{n+1}{2}]
. Additionally,
\frac{\partial}{\partial k}(g(k))=\frac{n+7}{2}-2k
. Therefore,
\sum_{x\in L_{2}}|N_{G}(x)\cap L_{1}|\geq\min\{g(2),g(\frac{n+1}{2}),g(\frac{n+7}{4})\}\geq\,n+2
. Since
G[S]
is an induced subgraph of
G
by
n+2
vertices and greater or equal to
n+2
edges, it is not an acyclic graph. Therefore, the forest number of graph
G
,
f(G)
, cannot be greater than or equal to
n+2
. However, we have already proved that
f(G)
is greater than or equal to
n+1
. Thus,
f(G)
must be equal to
n+1
, as desired.

\Box

Proposition 2.8

Let
n\geq 4
be an integer. Then there is a balanced bipartite graph, such as
G
, with
2n
vertices, minimum degree
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
that
f(G)=n+2
.

Proof.
Let
n
be an integer greater than or equal to 2. Consider a complete balanced bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
with 2n vertices divided into two parts,
A
and
B
. Assume that
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\subseteq A
and
B=\{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{n}\}
. Now, consider the balanced bipartite graph
G
, which is created by removing the edges between
a_{1}
and the first
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
vertices of
B
, as well as the edges between
a_{2}
and the last
n-\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
vertices of
B
. The structure of
G
is such that
d_{G}(a_{1})=d_{G}(a_{2})=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
,
d_{G}(x)\geq\,n-1
for
x\in\,V(G)\backslash\,\{a_{1},a_{2}\}
, and
G[B\cup\,\{a_{1},a_{2}\}]
is a forest. It follows that
f(G)\geq\,n+2
. However, one can observe that
f(G)\leq\,n+2
, because of the structure of
G
. Thus, we conclude that
f(G)=n+2
.

\Box

Theorem 2.9

Let
k
be a positive integer. Then there exist infinitely many balanced bipartite graphs, such as
G
, that
\delta(G)=k
and
f(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+2
.

Proof.
Suppose
n
is an even positive integer such that
n\geq 2k
. Furthermore, assume
H
is a balanced bipartite graph with
2n
vertices, where
\delta(H)=\frac{n}{2}+1
, and parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
. Without loss of generality, let
v\in V_{1}
,
d_{H}(v)=\frac{n}{2}+1
,
V_{2}\backslash N_{H}(v)=\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{\frac{n}{2}-1}\}
,
U\subseteq V_{1}
, and
|U|\geq k
. Let
P_{2}
be a path with two vertices
x
and
y
. Now, consider the balanced bipartite graph
G
with parts
V_{1}\cup\{x\}
and
V_{2}\cup\{y\}
and edge set
E(H)\cup\{xw_{i}:i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k-1\}\}\cup\{yu:u\in U\}
. It can be shown that
\delta(G)=d_{G}(x)=k
and
|V(G)|=2n+2
. However, by Theorem
2.1
,
f(H)=n+1
. Therefore, for any subset of
V(G)
, such as
S
, that makes
G[S]
a forest, we have
|S|\leq n+3
, because
|S\cap V(H)|\leq n+1
. Suppose
S=V_{2}\cup\{x,v\}
. In this case, by the structure of
G
, it can be observed that
|N_{G}(v)\cap N_{G}(x)|\leq 1
. Thus,
G[S]
is a forest, and so
f(G)\geq n+3
. Since we have already proven that
f(G)\leq n+3
,
f(G)=n+3=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}+2
, as wanted.

\Box

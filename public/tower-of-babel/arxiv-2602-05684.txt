Characterizations of the Aubin property of the KKT-mapping in composite optimization by SC derivatives and quadratic bundles
Helmut Gfrerer, Jiří V. Outrata

Abstract
For general set-valued mappings, the Aubin property is ultimately tied to limiting coderivatives by the Mordukhovich criterion. Likewise, the existence of single-valued Lipschitzian localizations is related to strict graphical derivatives. In this paper we will show that for the special case of the KKT-mapping from composite optimization, the Aubin property and the existence of single-valued Lipschitzian localizations can be characterized by SC derivatives and quadratic bundles, respectively, which are easier accessible than limiting coderivatives and strict graphical derivatives.



Key words.
Composite optimization, Aubin property, KKT-mapping, generalized derivatives, second-order qualification condition.

MSC codes.
49J52, 49J53, 90C26.

1
Introduction

Consider the composite optimization problem

\min\varphi(x):=f(x)+g(F(x)),

(1.1)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are twice continuously differentiable and
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
is a lower semicontinuous (l.s.c. ) function satisfying
\mathrm{dom}\,g:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\mid\,g(y)<\infty\}\not=\emptyset
.

The problem (
1.1
) is a rather general problem. If one takes
g
to be the indicator function
\delta_{K}
of a closed convex set
K
, the term
g(F(x))
corresponds to a constraint
F(x)\in K
. In particular, in the special case

K=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\mid\,y_{i}=0,\ i=1,\ldots,l,\ y_{i}\leq 0,\ i=l+1,\ldots,m\}

we recover the
nonlinear programming problem
. Other instances of (
1.1
) are
second-order cone programming
and semidefinite programming when
K
is the second-order cone and the cone of positive semidefinite matrices, respectively. In this paper we do not assume such special structures and deal with very general l.s.c. convex functions
g
.

Given a local minimizer
{\bar{x}}
of (
1.1
), the first-order optimality conditions (KKT-conditions) read as

0=\nabla_{x}{\cal L}(x,y^{*}),\ y^{*}\in\partial g(F(x)),

where the Lagrangian
{\cal L}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

{\cal L}(x,y^{*}):=f(x)+\langle y^{*},F(x)\rangle,\ (x,y^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}.

We consider the
canonically perturbed
version of (
1.1
) given by

\min f(x)-\langle a^{*},x\rangle+g(F(x)+b)

(1.2)

with perturbation parameters
a^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The corresponding KKT system reads as

0=\nabla_{x}{\cal L}(x,y^{*})-a^{*},y^{*}\in\partial g(F(x)+b)

(1.3)

and we are interested in properties of its solution mapping

S_{\rm KKT}(a^{*},b):=\{(x,y^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\,\mid\,(x,y^{*},a^{*},b)\mbox{ fulfills }\eqref{EqKKTpert}\}

(1.4)

for perturbation parameters
(a^{*},b)
close to the reference parameter

({\bar{a}^{\ast}},{\bar{b}}):=(0,0).

(1.5)

Various stability properties for solution mappings of parameter dependent generalized equations and optimization problems have been considered in the literature, we refer her only to the monographs
[
2
,
3
,
15
,
17
,
18
,
20
]
and the references therein.
In this paper we pay attention to the so-called Aubin property and the existence of single valued Lipschitzian localizations of the KKT-mapping
S_{\rm KKT}
. In particular, we want to analyze
full primal-dual stability
of a pair
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})\in S_{\rm KKT}({\bar{a}^{\ast}},{\bar{b}})
as recently introduced by Benko and Rockafellar
[
1
]
. Let us define the following mappings associated with optimal solutions of the perturbed optimization problem (
1.2
) by

\displaystyle S_{\rm Opt}^{\delta}(a^{*},b):=\mathop{\rm arg\,min}_{\|x-{\bar{x}}\|\leq\delta}\big\{f(x)-\langle a^{*},x\rangle+g(F(x)+b)\},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\widehat{S}_{\rm Opt}^{\delta}(a^{*},b):=\{(x,y^{*})\in S_{\rm KKT}(a^{*},b)\,\mid\,x\in S_{\rm Opt}^{\delta}(a^{*},b),\|y^{*}-{\bar{y}^{\ast}}\|\leq\delta\}.

(1.7)

Definition 1.1
.

The primal–dual pair
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})
is fully stable
in problem (
1.1
) if there is a neighborhood
{\cal U}^{*}\times{\cal V}
of
({\bar{a}^{\ast}},{\bar{b}})
such that, for
\delta>0
sufficiently small,
the mapping
\widehat{S}_{\rm Opt}^{\delta}
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous on
{\cal U}^{*}\times{\cal V}
.

At the very general level, characterization of stability properties of set-valued mappings can be stated by generalized derivatives.
By the celebrated Mordukhovich criterion, the Aubin property can be smartly characterized in terms of limiting coderivatives. Though a lot of calculus rules for coderivatives are available, it can be hard or even an unsurmountable hurdle to compute the exact limiting coderivative. Often the calculus rules only yield an inclusion resulting in something which is only sufficient for the Aubin property but not necessary.

Likewise, the existence of a single-valued Lipschitzian localization is associated with strict graphical derivatives as demonstrated by Kummer’s inverse mapping theorem
[
16
]
, see also
[
28
, Theorem 9.54]
,
[
27
, Section 3]
for the general case. Similarly to limiting coderivatives, the calculation of strict graphical derivatives is an extremely difficult task which impairs their usefulness.

The objective of this paper is to show that the Aubin property of
S_{\rm KKT}
and full primal-dual stability, respectively, can be characterized by
subspace containing derivatives (SC derivatives)
of the subdifferential mapping
\partial g
and Rockafellar’s
quadratic bundle

[
26
]
for
g
, respectively, which are easier accessible than limiting coderivatives and strict graphical derivatives as discussed at the end of Subsection
2.3
.
SC derivatives were introduced very recently by the authors
[
9
]
in order to provide a theoretical framework for the efficient implementation of the semismooth* Newton method from
[
8
]
. Apart from this numerically motivated approach, it already turned out in
[
9
,
10
]
that SC derivatives are a useful tool for analyzing stability properties of set-valued mappings. They represent a first-order generalized derivative for a certain class of set-valued mappings including the subdifferential mapping of l.s.c. prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous functions.
On the other hand, quadratic bundles are based on epigraphical limits of certain second subderivatives of such functions. Both notions are linked by a one-to-one correspondence and therefore all results can be formulated either by SC derivatives or by quadratic bundles in an equivalent way.

At the core of our analysis there is a result that a certain second order qualification condition
[
22
, Equation (3.15)]
, expressed in terms of limiting coderivatives, can be equivalently formulated using SC derivatives, quadratic bundles and strict graphical derivatives, respectively. Then we can show, under the assumption that the SC derivative of
\partial g
is a singleton, that the Aubin property of
S_{\rm KKT}
is equivalent to the existence of a single-valued Lipschitzian localization as well as to the simultaneous fulfillment of the second order qualification condition and another second-order condition involving the SC derivative. Moreover, we can show that the single-valued localization is not only Lipschitzian but also strictly differentiable and compute its derivative. These results are based on characterizations of strict proto-differentiability from the very recent works
[
7
,
11
]
.

In case of local minimizers we will show, under the assumption that a condition necessary for variational sufficiency is fulfilled, that the Aubin property of
S_{\rm KKT}
is equivalent to the existence of a single-valued Lipschitzian localization as well as to strong variational sufficiency and full primal-dual stability. These properties can be characterized by the second-order qualification condition and a condition on SC derivatives/quadratic bundles ensuring strong variational sufficiency. Here we apply results from Benko and Rockafellar
[
1
]
and Rockafellar’s characterization of strong variational sufficiency via quadratic bundles
[
26
]
. Finally, under the assumption that a certain chain rule is fulfilled, we will show that in the characterizations above the requirement of strong variational sufficiency can be replaced by the property that the reference point
{\bar{x}}
is a tilt-stable local minimizer.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall all the basic definitions and properties used in this paper. Section 3 is devoted to the second-order qualification condition and its consequences. Finally, in Section 4 we state our results on the Aubin property of
S_{\rm KKT}
and the full primal-dual stability.

2
Preliminaries

At the beginning let us recall set convergence in the sense of Painlevé–Kuratowski.
Given a parameterized family
C_{t}
of subsets of a metric space, where
t
also belongs to a metric space, the upper (outer) and lower(inner) limits are given by

\displaystyle\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{t\to\bar{t}}C_{t}:=\{x\,\mid\,\liminf_{t\to\bar{t}}\,{\rm dist}(x,C_{t})=0\},\ \mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{t\to\bar{t}}C_{t}:=\{x\,\mid\,\limsup_{t\to\bar{t}}\,{\rm dist}(x,C_{t})=0\}.

The limit of the sequence exists if the outer and inner limit sets are equal and we set

\mathop{{\rm Lim}}_{t\to\bar{t}}C_{t}:=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{t\to\bar{t}}C_{t}=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm inf}}_{t\to\bar{t}}C_{t}.

2.1
Subdifferentials and second subderivatives

Given an extended real-valued function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
{\bar{x}}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\psi
, the
regular subdifferential
of
\psi
at
{\bar{x}}
is given by

\widehat{\partial}\psi({\bar{x}}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,\liminf_{x\to{\bar{x}}}\frac{\psi(x)-\psi({\bar{x}})-\langle x^{*},x-{\bar{x}}\rangle}{\|x-{\bar{x}}\|}\geq 0\Big\},

while the
(limiting) subdifferential
is defined by

\partial\psi({\bar{x}}):=\{x^{*}\,\mid\,\exists x_{k}\to{\bar{x}},x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}\mbox{ with }\psi(x_{k})\to\psi(x)\mbox{ and }x_{k}^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\psi(x_{k})\ \forall k\}.

The
singular (horizon) subgradient set
is given by

\partial^{\infty}\psi(x)=\{x^{*}\,\mid\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,x_{k}\to{\bar{x}},x_{k}^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\psi(x_{k})\mbox{ with }\psi(x_{k})\to\psi(x)\mbox{ and }t_{k}x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}\}.

An l.s.c. function
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called
prox-regular
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}

if
\psi
is finite at
{\bar{x}}
with
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\psi({\bar{x}})
and there
exist
\epsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
such that

\psi(x^{\prime})\geq\psi(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}

whenever
x^{\prime},x\in{\cal B}({\bar{x}},\epsilon)
,
x^{*}\in{\cal B}({\bar{x}^{\ast}},\epsilon)
and
\psi(x)<\psi({\bar{x}})+\epsilon
. When this holds for all
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\psi({\bar{x}})
,
\psi
is called to be prox-regular at
{\bar{x}}
.

Further,
\psi
is called
subdifferentially continuous
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
if for any sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\psi}}({\bar{x}},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
we have
\lim_{k\to\infty}\psi(x_{k})=\psi({\bar{x}})
. When this holds for all
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\psi({\bar{x}})
,
\psi
is called to be subdifferentially continuous at
{\bar{x}}
.

Recall that an l.s.c. convex function is prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous on its whole domain.

Define the parametric family of second-order difference quotients for
\psi
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\Delta_{t}^{2}\psi({\bar{x}},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})(u):=\frac{\psi({\bar{x}}+tu)-\psi({\bar{x}})-t\langle{\bar{x}^{\ast}},u\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}\quad\mbox{ with }u\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t>0.

If
\psi({\bar{x}})
is finite, then the
second subderivative
of
\psi
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
is given by

{\rm d^{2}}\psi({\bar{x}},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})(u)=\liminf_{{\textstyle{t\downarrow 0\atop u^{\prime}\to u}}}\Delta_{t}^{2}\psi({\bar{x}},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})(u^{\prime}).

\psi
is called
twice epi-differentiable
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
, if the functions
\Delta_{t}^{2}\psi({\bar{x}},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
epi-converge to
{\rm d^{2}}\psi({\bar{x}},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
as
t\downarrow 0
.

2.2
Set-valued mappings and generalized differentiation

Consider a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
{\bar{z}}\in\Omega
.
The
tangent cone
and the
paratingent cone
to
\Omega
at
{\bar{z}}
are given by

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}({\bar{z}}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\Omega-{\bar{z}}}{t},\quad T^{P}_{\Omega}({\bar{z}}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{{\textstyle{z\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}{\bar{z}}\atop t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{\Omega-z}{t}

and the
regular normal cone
and the
limiting normal cone
to
\Omega
at
{\bar{z}}
are defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}({\bar{z}}):=\big(T_{\Omega}({\bar{z}})\big)^{\circ},\quad N_{\Omega}({\bar{z}}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{z\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}{\bar{z}}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(z).

We now proceed with generalized derivatives of set-valued mappings.
Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping and let
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
.
The
strict derivative

D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the
graphical derivative

DF({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the
limiting (Mordukhovich) coderivative

D^{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{y}}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}^{P}({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}}),\quad\mathrm{gph}\,DF({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\}.

These generalized derivatives may be used to characterize several regularity properties of set-valued mappings. Given
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a point
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
, the mapping
F
is said to be
metrically regular around

({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
if there is
\kappa\geq 0
together with neighborhoods
{\cal X}
of
{\bar{x}}
and
{\cal Y}
of
{\bar{y}}
such that

{\rm dist}(x,F^{-1}(y))\leq\kappa{\rm dist}(y,F(x))\ \forall(x,y)\in{\cal X}\times{\cal Y}.

(2.8)

F
is said to be
strongly metrically regular around

({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
if it is metrically regular around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
and
F^{-1}
has a single-valued graphical localization around
({\bar{y}},{\bar{x}})
, i.e., there are open neighborhoods
{\cal Y}^{\prime}
of
{\bar{y}}
,
{\cal X}^{\prime}
of
{\bar{x}}
and a mapping
h:{\cal Y}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
h({\bar{y}})={\bar{x}}
such that
\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap({\cal X}^{\prime}\times{\cal Y}^{\prime})=\{(h(y),y)\,\mid\,y\in{\cal Y}^{\prime}\}
.

It is well-known that
F
is metrically regular around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
if and only if the inverse mapping
S=F^{-1}
has the so-called
Aubin property
around
({\bar{y}},{\bar{x}})
, i.e., there is
\kappa\geq 0
together with neighborhoods
{\cal Y}
of
{\bar{y}}
and
{\cal X}
of
{\bar{x}}
such that

S(y)\cap{\cal X}\subset\kappa\|y-y^{\prime}\|{\cal B}+S(y^{\prime})\ \forall y,y^{\prime}\in{\cal Y}.

(2.9)

For single-valued mappings
S:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the Aubin property of
S
around
({\bar{y}},S({\bar{y}}))
is the same as Lipschitz continuity of
S
near
{\bar{y}}
.
Therefore,
F
is strongly metrically regular around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
if and only if
F^{-1}
has a single-valued graphical localization around
({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}})
which is Lipschitz continuous.

In this paper we will use the following characterizations of metric regularity and the existence of single-valued Lipschitzian localizations.

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider a multifunction
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and assume that
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is locally closed around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
.

(i)

(Mordukhovich criterion, cf.
[
19
, Theorem 3.3]
)
F
is metrically regular around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
if and only if

0\in D^{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})(y^{*})\ \Rightarrow\ y^{*}=0.

(2.10)

(ii)

(cf. Rockafellar
[
27
, Proposition 3.1]
.)
F^{-1}
has a graphical localization around
({\bar{y}},{\bar{x}})
which is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous relative to its domain if and only if

D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})^{-1}(0)=\{0\}.

(2.11)

Note that the strict derivative criterion (
2.11
) can be also equivalently written as

{\rm ker\;}D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})=\{0\}\quad\mbox{or}\quad\Big(0\in D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})(u)\ \Rightarrow\ u=0\Big).

We now turn our attention to another notion of differentiability. We call a set-valued mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}

strictly proto-differentiable
at
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if

\mathop{{\rm Lim}}_{{\textstyle{(x,y)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,F}({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\atop t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{\mathrm{gph}\,F-(x,y)}{t}

exists. This definition goes back to the work of Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
23
]
and has its roots in the notion of
strictly smooth sets
introduced by Rockafellar
[
24
]
. One of the most important features of strict proto-differentiability is that it provides a linkage between the limiting coderivative and the strict graphical derivative: If
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is locally closed at
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
then
F
is strictly proto-differentiable at
{\bar{x}}
for
{\bar{y}}
if and only if both
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
and
\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
are subspaces satisfying

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})^{\perp}\},

(2.12)

cf.
[
7
, Lemma 2.4]
. This has also the consequence
[
7
, Theorem 3.6]
that metric regularity of
F
around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
implies strong metric regularity, provided
F
is graphically Lipschitzian of dimension
m
around
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
according to the following definition.

Definition 2.2

(cf.
[
28
, Definition 9.66]
)
.

A mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is graphically Lipschitzian of dimension
d
at
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if there is an open neighborhood
W
of
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})
and a one-to-one mapping
\Phi
from
W
onto an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}
with
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
continuously differentiable, such that
\Phi(\mathrm{gph}\,F\cap W)
can be identified with the graph of a Lipschitz continuous mapping
f:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m-d}
, where
U
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A prominent example for a graphically Lipschitzian mapping is the subdifferential mapping of an l.s.c. convex function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
. Consider the
proximal mapping

{\rm prox}_{g}
of

defined by

{\rm prox}_{g}(z):=\mathop{\rm arg\,min}_{x}\{\frac{1}{2}\|x-z\|^{2}+g(x)\}

Then it is well-known by Minty’s Theorem, see, e.g.,
[
28
, Theorem 12.15]
, that
{\rm prox}_{g}
is single-valued and Lipschitzian, in fact nonexpansive, on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathrm{gph}\,{\rm prox}_{g}=\Phi(\mathrm{gph}\,\partial g)
with
\Phi(x,x^{*}):=(x+x^{*},x)
.

At the end of this subsection let us recall the notion of the
B-differential
(B-Jacobian) of a single-valued mapping
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set. The B-differential of
F
at
x\in U
is defined as

\overline{\nabla}F(x):=\{A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\,\mid\,\exists x_{k}\to x:\mbox{ $F$ is Fr\'{e}chet differentiable at $x_{k}$ and }A=\lim_{k\to\infty}\nabla F(x_{k})\}.

Recall that the Clarke Generalized Jacobian is given by
{\rm conv\,}\overline{\nabla}F(x)
, i.e., the convex hull of the B-differential.

2.3
On SC derivatives of the subdifferential mapping and quadratic bundles

Let us briefly recall some basics about SC derivatives which are used in this paper.
Consider the metric space
{\cal Z}_{n}
of all
n
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the metric

d_{\cal Z}(L_{1},L_{2})=\|P_{L_{1}}-P_{L_{2}}\|,

where
P_{L_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, denotes the orthogonal projection onto
L_{i}
. Given a subspace
L\in{\cal Z}_{n}
, we denote by

L^{*}:=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in L^{\perp}\}.

its
adjoint
subspace. Then
(L^{*})^{*}=L
and
d_{\cal Z}(L_{1},L_{2})=d_{\cal Z}(L_{1}^{*},L_{2}^{*})
, cf.
[
9
]
. Since
\dim L^{*}=\dim L^{\perp}=n+n-\dim L=n
, we have
L^{*}\in{\cal Z}_{n}
whenever

.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a mapping.

1.

F
is called graphically smooth at
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
and of dimension
d
in this respect, if

is a
d

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote by
{\cal O}_{F}
the set of all points from the graph of
F
, where
F
is graphically smooth of dimension
n
.

2.

The subspace containing derivative (SC derivative)
{\mathcal{S}}F:\mathrm{gph}\,F\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y):=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{n}\,\mid\,\exists(x_{k},y_{k})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{{\cal O}_{F}}(x,y):d_{\cal Z}(T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x_{k},y_{k}),L)=0\},

whereas the adjoint SC derivative
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F:\mathrm{gph}\,F\rightrightarrows{\cal Z}_{n}
is given by

{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F(x,y):=\{L^{*}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y)\}.

3.

We say that
F
has the SCD (subspace containing derivative) property at
({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if
{\mathcal{S}}F({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}})\not=\emptyset
.
Further we say that
F
is an SCD mapping if it has the SCD property at every
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
.

Remark 2.4
.

The SC derivatives

and
{\mathcal{S}}^{*}F
were introduced in
[
9
]
in a slightly different but equivalent way. We refer to
[
10
]
for SC derivatives of mappings
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

By combining Lemmas 3.7, 3.10 and Remark 3.9 from
[

]
we obtain

L\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(x,y),\ L^{*}\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(x,y)\quad\mbox{for all }L\in{\mathcal{S}}F(x,y).

(2.13)

Hence, the SC derivatives can be interpreted as a kind of skeleton for the strict graphical derivative and the limiting coderivative, respectively.

We now turn our attention to the SC derivative of the subgradient mapping of a prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous function

For the practical computation of SC derivatives one needs a basis representation of the subspaces contained in it and

.

In the setting of Proposition

For any pair

\infty&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.18)

At the end of this subsection, we recall some properties of the SC derivative of an l.s.c. convex function

Since

{\mathcal{S}}\partial g(x,x^{*})=\{{\rm rge\;}(B,I-B)\,\mid\,B\in\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}(x+x^{*})\}.

Together with

we conclude that every matrix

is symmetric positive semidefinte and satisfies

Further, by

together with (

Theorem 2.8
.

Given an l.s.c. convex function

, for every pair
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial g
there hold the inclusions

\displaystyle\bigcup_{B\in\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}(x+x^{*})}{\rm rge\;}(B,I-B)\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}(\partial g)(x,x^{*})\subset\bigcup_{B\in{\rm conv\,}(\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}(x+x^{*}))}{\rm rge\;}(B,I-B),

(2.20)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{B\in\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}(x+x^{*})}{\rm rge\;}(B,I-B)\subset\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}(\partial g)(x,x^{*})\subset\bigcup_{B\in{\rm conv\,}(\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}(x+x^{*}))}{\rm rge\;}(B,I-B),

(2.21)

From (

) we may conclude that computing

for an l.s.c. convex function

is not more difficult than computing the B-differential of its proximal mapping. This is still a difficult task but it seems to be more tractable than computing limiting coderivatives or strict graphical derivatives. Note that the computation of the
(P,W)
-basis representation of the SC derivative is sometimes even simpler and is also applicable when the proximal mapping is not at hand but the subdifferential
\partial g
is available. Determining the set
{\cal O}_{\partial g}
is usually not so difficult. In many cases it is related with the set of pairs
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial g
fulfilling

Further, consider, e.g., the special case when

, whereas
\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}(x+\nabla g(x))=\{(I+\nabla^{2}g(x))^{-1}\}
. This trivial example indicates that the computation of the
(P,W)
-basis representations of the subspaces

contained in the SC derivative of a subdifferential mapping is more related to the concept of twice differentiability than the computation of the B-differential of the proximal mapping.

3
On the second-order qualification condition

Throughout this section we will suppose the following assumption.

Assumption 1
.

The mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is continuously differentiable around the reference point
{\bar{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is an l.s.c. convex function such that
{\bar{y}}:=F({\bar{x}})\in\mathrm{dom}\,g
. Finally, we are given a subgradient
{\bar{y}^{\ast}}\in\partial g({\bar{y}})
and set
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}:=\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}{\bar{y}^{\ast}}
.

The following theorem is fundamental for our analysis.

Theorem 3.1
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\Big(\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*}=0,\ (0,v^{*})\in L\ \Rightarrow\ v^{*}=0\Big)\quad\mbox{for all }L\in{\mathcal{S}}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}}).

(3.22)

(ii)

\Big(\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*}=0,\ v^{*}\in\partial q(0)\ \Rightarrow\ v^{*}=0\Big)\quad\mbox{for all }q\in{\rm quad\,}g({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}}).

(3.23)

(iii)

\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*}=0,\ v^{*}\in D^{*}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})(0)\ \Rightarrow\ v^{*}=0.

(3.24)

(iv)

\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*}=0,\ v^{*}\in D_{*}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})(0)\ \Rightarrow\ v^{*}=0.

(3.25)

(v)

The mapping
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by

\Psi(x,y^{*})=(\partial g)^{-1}(y^{*})-F(x)

(3.26)

is metrically regular around
\big(({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}}),{\bar{b}})
.

(vi)

The multiplier mapping
M_{{\bar{x}}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by

M_{{\bar{x}}}(x^{*},b)=\{y^{*}\in\partial g({\bar{y}}+b)\,\mid\,\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}y^{*}=x^{*}\}

has a graphical localization around
\big(({\bar{x}^{\ast}},{\bar{b}}),{\bar{y}^{\ast}}\big)
which is single-valued and Lipschitzian relative to its domain.

Proof.

The equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) is an easy consequence of Proposition
2.7
. The implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) follows from the second inclusion in (
2.13
) together with the fact that
{\mathcal{S}}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})={\mathcal{S}}^{*}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})
. We prove the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) by contraposition. Assume on the contrary to (iii) that there is some nonzero
v^{*}
satisfying
\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*}=0
and
(0,v^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}}^{*})
. By the second inclusion in (
2.20
) we can find some matrix
B\in{\rm conv\,}\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}({\bar{y}}+{\bar{y}^{\ast}})
and some
p\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
Bp=0
and
v^{*}=(I-B)p=p
. Since the B-differential
\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}({\bar{y}}+{\bar{y}^{\ast}})
is compact, there exists a natural number
N
, positive reals
\alpha_{i}
and matrices
B_{i}\in\overline{\nabla}{\rm prox}_{g}({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})
,
i=1,\ldots,N
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}=1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}B_{i}=B
. From
Bp=0
we readily infer that

\langle p,Bp\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\langle p,B_{i}p\rangle=0,

implying
\langle p,B_{i}p\rangle=0
,
i=1,\ldots,N
by the positive semidefiniteness of the matrices
B_{i}
and, together with their symmetry,
B_{i}p=0
,
i=1,\ldots,N
follows. By (
2.19
) we obtain that
(0,v^{*})=(B_{i}p,(I-B_{i})p)\in{\rm rge\;}(B_{i},I-B_{i})\in{\mathcal{S}}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})
,
i=1,\ldots,N
, a contradiction to (
3.22
). Hence the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) holds true and the equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) is established.

The equivalence between (i) and (iv) can be shown in the same way by using the first inclusion in (
2.13
) and (
2.21
).

In order to show the equivalence between (iii) and (v), note that

D^{*}\Psi({\bar{x}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})(v^{*})=\{(-\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*},u)\,\mid\,u\in D^{*}(\partial g)^{-1}({\bar{y}^{\ast}},{\bar{y}})(v^{*})\},\ v^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Since
D^{*}(\partial g)^{-1}({\bar{y}^{\ast}},{\bar{y}})(v^{*})=\{u\,\mid\,-v^{*}\in D^{*}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})(-u)\}
, the Mordukhovich criterium tells us that (v) holds if and only if (iii) is valid.

Finally, in order to show the equivalence between (iv) and (vi), consider the mapping
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
given by

\Gamma(y^{*}):=\big(\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}y^{*},(\partial g)^{-1}(y^{*})-{\bar{y}}\big),\ y^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

By
[
28
, Exercise 10.43]
we obtain that

\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}\Gamma\big({\bar{y}^{\ast}},({\bar{x}^{\ast}},{\bar{b}})\big)=\big\{\big(v^{*},(\nabla F({\bar{x}})^{T}v^{*},v)\big)\,\mid\,(v^{*},v)\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}(\partial g)^{-1}({\bar{y}^{\ast}},{\bar{y}})\big\}.

Hence, by taking into account the equation
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}(\partial g)^{-1}({\bar{y}^{\ast}},{\bar{y}})=\{(v^{*},v)\,\mid\,(v,v^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}(\partial g)({\bar{y}},{\bar{y}^{\ast}})\}
, condition (iv) is equivalent with
{\rm ker\;}D_{*}\Gamma\big({\bar{y}^{\ast}},({\bar{x}^{\ast}},{\bar{b}})\big)=\{0\}
. Since
M_{{\bar{x}}}=\Gamma^{-1}
, we conclude from Rockafellar’s criterion in Theorem
2.1
(ii) that statement (iv) is equivalent with (vi). This completes the proof.
∎

.

We will say that the second-order qualification condition (SOQC) holds for the composite function

Remark 3.3

Given

5
Conclusion

The paper has been devoted to a deep analysis of Lipschitzian stability of the KKT mapping
S_{\rm KKT}
in context of
a composite optimization problem. To this aim quite a broad arsenal of tools from modern variational analysis has been
employed which includes, apart from standard constructions, also the so-called quadratic bundles and SC generalized
derivatives. The main results characterize the investigated stability by several equivalent statements which are
valid under various circumstances. Concretely, one considers the following three situations when

(i)

the SC derivative of
\partial g
at the reference point is a singleton;

(ii)

either a necessary or a sufficient condition related to strong variational sufficiency is fulfilled;

(iii)

a special chain rule for the q-subderivatives of the composition
g\circ F
is valid.

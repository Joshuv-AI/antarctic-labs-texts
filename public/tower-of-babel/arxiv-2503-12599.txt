Well-Posed Geometric Boundary data in General Relativity, I: Conformal-mean curvature boundary data
Zhongshan An and Michael T. Anderson

Abstract
. We study the local in time well-posedness of the initial boundary value problem (IBVP) for the vacuum Einstein equations in general relativity with geometric boundary conditions. For conformal-mean curvature boundary conditions, consisting of the conformal class of the boundary metric and mean curvature of the boundary, well-posedness does not hold without imposing additional angle data at the corner. When the corner angle is included as corner data, we prove well-posedness of the linearized problem in C ∞ C^{\infty} , where the linearization is taken at any smooth vacuum Einstein metric.

{\mathcal{A}}=C^{\infty}(\Sigma)

Conjecture 1

The space

Currently known methods to prove well-posedness require a choice of gauge to break the symmetry of the diffeomorphism group.
Various choices of gauge may reduce the Einstein equations to a symmetric hyperbolic
1^{\rm st}
order system or a hyperbolic system of

2^{\rm nd}
order wave equations for instance. Given such a reduction, there is a well-established theory of the well-posedness of the IBVP
for such systems provided the boundary data satisfy certain conditions, such as maximally dissipative boundary conditions, uniform KL condition,
etc; we refer to
[
16
]
,
[
34
]
and references therein for further details. All prior work on the IBVP for the vacuum Einstein equations uses this
approach. In the work of Friedrich-Nagy
[
22
]
, the corner angle
\alpha
appears explicitly in the condition for well-posedness. However, in
[
22
]

the angle
\alpha
is needed to precisely define the boundary conditions along

and so the choice of boundary data space depends on the corner
angle. Similarly, the corner angle also appears in
[
30

and related works, where again it is used to determine the boundary conditions.
In
[
3

The presence of the corner angle in (

above), cannot be obtained by a suitable choice of gauge and then applying a standard existing PDE theory for well-posedness of IBVP (since the
conjecture is not an IBVP). A different approach is thus needed.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the linearized version of the conjecture above, in the smooth,
C^{\infty}

satisfies

The map

thus descends to the quotient

and the induced map

D\widehat{\Phi}:T(Met(M)/\mathrm{Diff}_{1}(M))\rightarrow T(\mathcal{Z}/\mathrm{Diff}_{1}(M)),

D\widehat{\Phi}_{g}(h)=(\mathrm{Ric}^{\prime}_{h},[(g_{S},K_{S},\nu_{S})_{S}]^{\prime}_{h},[([g_{{\mathcal{C}}}],H_{{\mathcal{C}}})_{{\mathcal{C}}}]^{\prime}_{h},(\alpha^{\prime}_{h})_{\Sigma}),

is an isomorphism. The equivalence class in
T(\mathcal{Z}/\mathrm{Diff}_{1}(M))
is represented by the symmetric forms

on

in the kernel
of the Bianchi operator
\beta_{g}
.

Further, dropping the normal vector
\nu_{S}

\widehat{\Phi}
descends to the full quotient

and again the linearization

is an isomorphism.

We expect that the results above are also true off-shell, where
g
is no longer vacuum Einstein. In fact the existence or surjectivity part of
Theorem 1.1 is proved to hold off-shell. The proofs when off-shell require some modifications, but we will not address the details here.

Let
T_{g}{\mathcal{E}}
denote the formal tangent space to
{\mathcal{E}}
at
g
, equal to the linear space of solutions
h
to the linearized vacuum
Einstein equations
\mathrm{Ric}^{\prime}_{h}=0
on
M
, modulo the equivalence relation
h\sim h+\delta^{*}X
, where
X\in T(\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(M))
.
Similarly, let
T(\mathcal{I}_{0})
denote the space of infinitesimal variations
\iota^{\prime}=(\gamma^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime})
satisfying the linearization of the vacuum constraint equations.

The following result is essentially an immediate consequence of the results above.

Corollary 1.3
.

For any
g\in{\mathcal{E}}
, the derivative map

D\Phi_{g}:T_{g}{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow T(\mathcal{I}_{0}\times_{c}{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{A}}),

is an isomorphism of Frechet spaces, depending smoothly on
g
. Thus the linearized IBCVP with boundary data
([h],H^{\prime}_{h})
and
corner data
\alpha^{\prime}_{h}
is well-posed in
C^{\infty}
.

As indicated above, the method of proof of the results above does not (in fact we believe cannot) rely on reducing the linearized Einstein equations
to a linear hyperbolic system and applying a well-established existing theory of well-posedness for the IBVP for certain classes of boundary
conditions. Instead, the approach here is to derive wave-type evolution equations for the Dirichlet boundary data
h|_{{\mathcal{C}}}
of all the
components of
h
at
{\mathcal{C}}
distinct from the given Dirichlet boundary data for the conformal class variation
[h^{\mathsf{T}}]=\ring{h}
.
These equations may be derived either from the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) along
{\mathcal{C}}
, or
equivalently from the harmonic gauge boundary data at
{\mathcal{C}}
.

A main point, which confirms the necessity of adding corner angle data at
\Sigma
, is that the wave equation describing the evolution of the
linearized conformal factor
u
(which is derived from the Hamiltonian constraint) has initial data
\partial_{t}u|_{\Sigma}
depending explicitly on the corner angle
variation
\alpha^{\prime}_{h}
, (cf. (
2.14
) below). This cannot be determined by the initial and boundary data at
\Sigma
, or their compatibility. Thus,
unique determination of the conformal factor
u
requires the extra specification of the corner angle.

Given such evolution equations which uniquely determine the full Dirichlet boundary data
h(\cdot,\cdot)
of the variation
h
at
{\mathcal{C}}
, we then
solve the linearized Einstein equations in a harmonic gauge. Thus, in the end, we only rely on the simple existence theory for wave equations with
Dirichlet boundary data.

However, intrinsic to this procedure is a loss of derivatives at the boundary
{\mathcal{C}}
. The existence of solutions to the linearized equations is
proved by a natural iteration procedure, which loses derivatives at each step of the iteration. This is the reason why Theorem 1.1 is only
stated for
C^{\infty}
data.

Currently, we have not been able to establish apriori
H^{s}
energy estimates for smooth linear solutions. Such energy estimates could only
possibly hold again with loss of derivative for target data. Thus it is not possible to prove existence within a given Banach space setting, as is usually
the case for previous analyses of the IBVP. Instead, the results above together with the existence of
H^{s}
energy estimates with loss-of-derivatives
would show that
D\Phi^{H}
is a tame Fredholm map, with tame linear inverse on the space of vacuum Einstein metrics, allowing one to apply the
Nash-Moser implicit function theorem to produce the nonlinear or actual solutions of the IBVCP as in Conjecture I. We plan to address this in future work.

2.1.
Localization.

To prove the main results in §1, as usual with hyperbolic systems, we will localize the problem via a partition of unity and rescaling;

together to provide global (in space) solutions.

Given a metric

2.3.
Harmonic gauge.

its boundary conditions at the boundary

As usual with the Cauchy problem for the Einstein equations, we work below with the (generalized) harmonic or wave coordinate gauge, described
locally by the coordinate vector field

To begin, consider the bulk term in (

The first term

Here and in the following, all geometric tensors and operators are with respect to

Recall also that
\beta

The first term here gives rise to a cancellation with the term

have coefficients involving two derivatives of

have coefficients involving one derivative of

but is highly coupled. In the localized setting of §2.1,

Lemma 3.1
.

In adapted local coordinates along
\Sigma

(h_{S},K^{\prime}_{h},(\nu_{S})^{\prime}_{h},V^{\prime}_{h})_{S}
,
(\alpha^{\prime}_{h})_{\Sigma}
in (

Proof.

We show first that the nonlinear version of this result holds, so with
g
in place of its variation

along

determines a choice of adapted local coordinates, up to the natural equivalence relation, cf. §2.1. Having then fixed the

are then
(g_{S},K_{S},\nu_{S},V|_{S})
. In such local coordinates,

determines

and the coordinate

and so the components

We may apply the arguments above to the linearization

The first is the linearization

of

in fixed coordinates and the second is given by the components of

in the infinitesimal
variation of the coordinates (the variation of the change of basis). The sum these two terms is determined by the argument above, while the

Remark 3.4

&\widehat{Z}(h^{\intercal})=\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{\nu}(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\intercal})\\
&\widehat{E}(h)=\beta^{\prime}_{h^{\intercal}}A-[\delta^{*}(W^{\prime}_{h})+(\delta^{*})^{\prime}_{h}V](\nu)+\mathrm{Ric}(\nu^{\prime}_{h})-\tfrac{1}{2}[A(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})+\beta^{\prime}_{2A}h^{\mathsf{T}}+\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\big(h_{\nu\nu}A\big)].\end{split}

Moreover, the initial data

at the initial surface

Proof.

The variation of the second fundamental form
A_{{\mathcal{C}}}:=A=\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{\nu}g

\beta_{\mathcal{C}}A^{\prime}_{h}=\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{L}}_{\nu}h)^{\mathsf{T}}-\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\delta_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}{h(\nu)^{\mathsf{T}}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{\mathcal{C}}[h_{\nu\nu}A].

The linearization of the Gauss-Codazzi equation (

, gives

\beta_{\mathcal{C}}A^{\prime}_{h}=-\beta^{\prime}_{h^{\mathsf{T}}}A-\tfrac{1}{2}dH^{\prime}_{h}-\mathrm{Ric}^{\prime}_{h}(\nu)-\mathrm{Ric}(\nu^{\prime}_{h}).

In addition, we also have

\begin{split}\beta_{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{L}}_{\nu}h)^{\mathsf{T}}&={\mathcal{L}}_{\nu}(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})-\beta^{\prime}_{2A}h^{\mathsf{T}}=\nabla_{\nu}(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})-\nabla_{(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})}\nu-\beta^{\prime}_{2A}h^{\mathsf{T}}\\
&=\nabla_{\nu}(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})-A(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})-\beta^{\prime}_{2A}h^{\mathsf{T}}.\end{split}

In summary, we have

\begin{split}&-\tfrac{1}{2}[\Box_{\mathcal{C}}+\mathrm{Ric}_{\mathcal{C}}]h(\nu)^{\mathsf{T}}\\
&=\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\delta_{\mathcal{C}}^{*}{h(\nu)^{\mathsf{T}}}\\
&=-\beta_{\mathcal{C}}A^{\prime}_{h}+\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{L}}_{\nu}h)^{\mathsf{T}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{\mathcal{C}}[h_{\nu\nu}A]\\
&=\beta^{\prime}_{h^{\mathsf{T}}}A+\tfrac{1}{2}dH^{\prime}_{h}+\mathrm{Ric}^{\prime}_{h}(\nu)+\mathrm{Ric}(\nu^{\prime}_{h})+\tfrac{1}{2}[\nabla_{\nu}(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})-A(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})-\beta^{\prime}_{2A}h^{\mathsf{T}}]-\tfrac{1}{2}\beta_{\mathcal{C}}[h_{\nu\nu}A]\\
&={\tfrac{1}{2}dH^{\prime}_{h}}+(F-\delta^{*}V^{\prime}_{F})(\nu)+\tfrac{1}{2}\nabla_{\nu}(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})\\
&\quad+\beta^{\prime}_{h^{\mathsf{T}}}A-[\delta^{*}(W^{\prime}_{h})+(\delta^{*})^{\prime}_{h}V](\nu)+\mathrm{Ric}(\nu^{\prime}_{h})-\tfrac{1}{2}[A(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}h^{\mathsf{T}})+\beta^{\prime}_{2A}h^{\mathsf{T}}+\beta_{\mathcal{C}}\big(h_{\nu\nu}A\big)].\end{split}

This proves the first statement.

along

Remark 4.2
.

The iteration process
h_{i}\rightarrow h_{i+1}
above loses derivatives at each stage, so that while we obtain solutions in
C^{\infty}
from

C^{\infty}
data, we do not obtain solutions in a fixed Banach space
H^{s}
.

In more detail, each
h_{i}
is constructed by solving the local IBVP for the wave equation with given initial data and constructed Dirichlet
boundary data. Thus, each
h_{i}
satisfies the usual Sobolev
H^{s}
energy estimates

(4.14)

||h||_{H^{s}(S_{t})}\leq C_{T}[||F||_{H^{s-1}(\widetilde{U}_{t})}+||h||_{H^{s}(S_{0})}+||\partial_{t}h||_{H^{s-1}(S_{0})}+||D_{h}||_{H^{s}({\mathcal{C}}_{t})}],

where
D_{h}
denotes the constructed Dirichlet data. Here
t\in[0,T]
,
S_{t}
is the Cauchy surface at time
t
and
\widetilde{U}_{t}
,
{\mathcal{C}}_{t}
are the
time
t
-sublevel sets of
\widetilde{U}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
. The constant
C_{T}
depends only on
T<\infty
and the background metric
g
.
However, by the construction in the proof above, the Dirichlet data
D_{h}
for
h_{i+1}
depend on extra derivatives of
h_{i}
at
{\mathcal{C}}
,
so upon iteration the bound (
4.14
) only holds with
s
depending on
i
, with
s\rightarrow\infty
as
i\rightarrow\infty
.

On the other hand, if the boundary data
(\ring{h},H^{\prime}_{h})
as well as the initial and corner data are controlled in
C^{\infty}
,
then the full Dirichlet boundary data
h|_{{\mathcal{C}}}=h_{\alpha\beta}|_{{\mathcal{C}}}
are also controlled in
C^{\infty}
so that (
4.14
) does bound
h

in
C^{\infty}
in place of
H^{s}
.

The main reason for this loss-of-derivative behavior is due to the “bad term”
\widehat{Z}(h^{\mathsf{T}})
in (
3.18
), which involves two derivatives of

h
, (with no
\varepsilon
coefficient). These difficulties can be handled in case the equations for the components of
h
are uncoupled - as is the
case at the Minkowski corner. However, for general
g
or even vacuum
g
, the presence of the coupling term
P
, even if it is small,
makes this much more difficult.

4.2.
Uniqueness

The usual proof of uniqueness (as well as existence) of solutions of an IBVP is via energy estimates. However, as noted above, such energy
estimates are not available here and so a different method is needed. The basic idea is to use a self-adjointness property which has previously
been noted in the elliptic (Riemannian) case, cf.
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
.

For convenience, the uniqueness result is phrased in the global setting where
S
is a compact Cauchy slice with boundary corner

\Sigma
a compact (codimension two) surface. However, as noted following the proof in Remark
4.6
, the same result holds locally, for compactly
supported data as in §2.1.

Let
C^{\infty}_{0}
be the space of
C^{\infty}
deformations
h
with zero initial, boundary, gauge and corner data, i.e.

(4.15)

\big((h_{S},K^{\prime}_{h},(\nu_{S})^{\prime}_{h},V^{\prime}_{h})_{S},(\ring{h},H^{\prime}_{h},V^{\prime}_{h})_{{\mathcal{C}}},(\alpha^{\prime}_{h})_{\Sigma}\big)=0.

We set
S=S_{0}=\{t=0\}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
g
be a vacuum Einstein metric,
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=0
on
M
as above. Suppose the linearized operator
L
is surjective on
C^{\infty}_{0}
, i.e.
for any
F\in C^{\infty}
, the equation

L(h)=F,

has a solution
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}
.
(
Compare with Theorem
4.1

)
. Then
L
has trivial kernel on
C^{\infty}_{0}
,

(4.16)

KerL=0.

The proof will be carried out in several steps. We first pass to a different gauge, the divergence-free gauge, associated with the Einstein tensor

E_{g}=\mathrm{Ric}_{g}-\frac{R_{g}}{2}g.

Note that the Bianchi identity gives
\delta E=0
, equivalent to the previously used
\beta\mathrm{Ric}=0
. The reason for passing from
\mathrm{Ric}
to
E
is that

E
has a natural variational or action principle; namely it arises as the Euler-Lagrange operator for the variation of the Einstein-Hilbert action.
Such variational operators have natural (formal) self-adjoint properties, after taking into account suitable boundary terms. This structure is not
available for the bare Ricci tensor
\mathrm{Ric}
.

To begin, choose any fixed background vacuum metric
\widetilde{g}
near
g
and consider the bulk gauged operator

(4.17)

\widetilde{\Phi}^{H}(g)=E_{g}+\delta^{*}\delta_{\widetilde{g}}g.

The linearization of
\widetilde{\Phi}^{H}
at the background vacuum metric
g=\widetilde{g}
in the bulk is given by

(4.18)

\widetilde{L}(h)=D\widetilde{\Phi}^{H}(h)=\tfrac{1}{2}D^{*}Dh-Rm(h)-\tfrac{1}{2}D^{2}\mathrm{tr}\,h+\tfrac{1}{2}(\Box\mathrm{tr}\,h-\delta\delta h)g,

compare with (
3.2
). Note that the new
\widetilde{\Phi}^{H}
in (
4.17
) depends on a choice of background metric
\widetilde{g}
. This is not case with
the original operator
\Phi^{H}
in (
1.12
), which instead depends on a (fixed) choice of coordinates
x^{\alpha}
. The formula (
4.18
) changes by
the addition of lower order terms when
g\neq\widetilde{g}
, so we really have a family of operators
\widetilde{L}_{\widetilde{g}}
.

Let
T(\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}^{H})
be the linearized target data space
T({\mathcal{T}}^{H})
, with the gauge
V^{\prime}_{h}
on
S
and
{\mathcal{C}}
replaced by the new gauge data
\delta h
on
S

and
{\mathcal{C}}
. Similarly, let
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
be the associated space where this target data vanishes, so that (
4.15
) holds with
\delta h=0

in place of
V^{\prime}_{h}=0
. Although not needed for the proof, we note that Lemma
3.3
holds as before: if
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=0
and if

\widetilde{L}(h)=F
satisfies
\delta_{\widetilde{g}}F=0
in
M
with
F(\nu_{S},\cdot)=0
on
S
, then
h\in\widetilde{C}_{0}^{\infty}
satisfies
\partial_{t}\delta h=0
on
S
,
and hence
\delta h=0
on
M
. The proof of this is the same as before.

Now the full boundary
{\partial M}
of the compact domain
M
consists of the timelike cylinder
{\mathcal{C}}
, the bottom and top Cauchy surfaces
S=S_{0}
,

S_{1}=\{t=1\}
(the value
t=1
is for convenience) and the two corners
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}
,
\Sigma_{1}
. We consider pairs of deformations
h
,
k
with

(4.19)

h\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1},

where
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
consists of all deformations
k
for which the initial, gauge, corner (and boundary) data vanish on the top slice
S_{1}

(in place of
S_{0}
for
h
).

Proposition 4.4
.

For
h\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
and
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
, we have the formal self-adjoint property

(4.20)

\int_{M}\langle\widetilde{L}(h),k\rangle dv_{g}=\int_{M}\langle h,\widetilde{L}(k)\rangle dv_{g},

where the pairing is with respect to the Lorentz metric
g
.

Proof.

Consider the Lagrangian in 4-dimensions given by, cf.
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
,

(4.21)

S(g)=\int_{M}R_{g}dv_{g}+\tfrac{2}{3}\int_{{\mathcal{C}}}H_{{\mathcal{C}}}dv_{\gamma}.

The same expression holds in all dimensions with a dimension dependent constant in place of
\frac{2}{3}
. This is a slight modification
of the Einstein-Hilbert action with Gibbons-Hawking-York boundary term (in units where
16\pi G=1
). The first variation is given by

\begin{split}D_{g}S(h)=&-\int_{M}\langle E_{g},h\rangle+\int_{{\mathcal{C}}}[\langle\ring{\pi},\ring{h}\rangle+\tfrac{4}{3}H^{\prime}_{h}]+\int_{S_{1}}\theta(h)-\int_{S_{0}}\theta(h)\\
&{+\int_{\Sigma_{1}}h(\nu,T^{\mathcal{C}})-\int_{\Sigma_{0}}h(\nu,T^{\mathcal{C}})}\end{split}

Here
\pi=A_{{\mathcal{C}}}-H_{{\mathcal{C}}}g_{{\mathcal{C}}}
is the conjugate momentum and
\ring{\pi}
is its trace-free part. The term
\theta
is the (pre)-symplectic potential
given by
\theta(h)=-\star(dtr_{g}h-div_{g}h)
; see e.g.
[
26
]
,
[
4
]
. Here and below we drop
the notation for the various volume forms.

The
2^{\rm nd}
variation is then given by

(4.22)

\begin{split}D^{2}S_{g}(h,k)=&-\int_{M}[\langle(E_{g})^{\prime}_{k},h\rangle-2\langle E_{g}\circ h,k\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\langle E_{g},h\rangle\mathrm{tr}k]\\
&+\int_{{\mathcal{C}}}[\langle(\ring{\pi})^{\prime}_{k},\ring{h}\rangle-2\langle\ring{\pi}\circ k,\ring{h}\rangle+\tfrac{4}{3}(H^{\prime}_{h})^{\prime}_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}[\langle\ring{\pi},\ring{h}\rangle+\tfrac{4}{3}H^{\prime}_{h}]\mathrm{tr}_{{\mathcal{C}}}k\\
&+\int_{S_{1}}(\theta_{h})^{\prime}_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\theta(h)\mathrm{tr}_{S_{1}}k-\int_{S_{0}}(\theta_{h})^{\prime}_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\theta(h)\mathrm{tr}_{S_{0}}k\\
&{+(\int_{\Sigma_{1}}-\int_{\Sigma_{0}})h(\nu^{\prime}_{k},T^{\mathcal{C}})+h\big(\nu,(T^{\mathcal{C}})^{\prime}_{k}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}h(\nu,T^{\mathcal{C}})\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}k}.\end{split}

Throughout the following discussion, we work with
h\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
,
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
as in (
4.19
). The corner integrals vanish
since
h,k
have zero initial data. The boundary term over
{\mathcal{C}}
above vanishes on
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
and
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
- except for the term

(H^{\prime}_{h})^{\prime}_{k}
. Note however that this term is symmetric in
h
and
k
.

At the top and bottom slices, the difference
D^{2}S_{g}(h,k)-D^{2}S(k,h)
of the two terms is the (pre)-symplectic form
\Omega(h,k)
evaluated at the
top and bottom Cauchy slices, cf.
[
26
]
. Simple computation, cf.
[
4
]
for example, shows this is of the form

\Omega_{S}(h,k)=-\int_{S}\langle K^{\prime}_{h},k\rangle-\langle K^{\prime}_{k},h\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}[\mathrm{tr}_{S}h(\langle K_{S},k\rangle+2(\mathrm{tr}K_{S})^{\prime}_{k})-\mathrm{tr}_{S}k(\langle K_{S},h\rangle+2(\mathrm{tr}K_{S})^{\prime}_{h})].

This expression involves only the initial data of
h
and
k
at
S
. Since the full initial data of

h
vanishes at
S_{0}
, (since
h\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
),

\Omega_{S_{0}}(h,k)=0.

Similarly since the full initial data of
k
vanishes at
S_{1}
, (since
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
)

\Omega_{S_{1}}(h,k)=0.

Thus on-shell where
E_{g}=0
, by the symmetry of the
2^{\rm nd}
derivative operator, it follows from (
4.22
) that for all

h\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
,
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
,

\int_{M}\langle(E_{g})^{\prime}_{k},h\rangle=\int_{M}\langle(E_{g})^{\prime}_{h},k\rangle.

Note this conclusion does not involve any choice of gauge. Since
\widetilde{L}(h)=E^{\prime}_{h}+\delta^{*}\delta h
and since for
h\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
,
and
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
,

\int_{M}\langle\delta^{*}\delta k,h\rangle=\int_{M}\langle\delta^{*}\delta h,k\rangle,

it follows that

\int_{M}\langle\widetilde{L}(k),h\rangle=\int_{M}\langle\widetilde{L}(h),k\rangle.

∎

We note that the same result and proof holds for Dirichlet boundary data (as well as other types of variational boundary data) with only minor
modifications to the proof.

At this point, we need the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=E_{g}=0
, suppose the operator
L=L_{g}
is surjective on
C^{\infty}_{0}
. Then the operator
\widetilde{L}

(with
\widetilde{g}=g
) is surjective on
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
, i.e. for any
G\in C^{\infty}

there exists
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
such that

\widetilde{L}(\widetilde{h})=G.

Consequently,

(4.23)

Ker\widetilde{L}=0,

on
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
.

Proof.

The first statement is just a shift in gauge, and so is not particularly surprising. To give the details, observe that
any
G\in C^{\infty}
can be decomposed as

G=G_{0}+\delta^{*}Y

where
\delta G_{0}=0
and
Y
is the vector field such that
\delta\delta^{*}Y=\delta G
in
M
with vanishing initial and boundary data. Then to solve

\widetilde{L}(\widetilde{h})=G
, it suffices to solve for
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
such that

(4.24)

E^{\prime}_{\widetilde{h}}=G_{0},\ \delta\widetilde{h}=Y

By hypothesis, the equation
L(h)=F
is solvable for any
F\in C^{\infty}
, with
h\in C^{\infty}_{0}
. In particular, choose
F=G_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}G_{0}g
.
It follows from
\delta G_{0}=0
that
\beta F=0
and hence by Lemma
3.3
,
V^{\prime}_{h}=0
in
M
. In turn, this implies that
Ric^{\prime}_{h}=F
in

M
. Since we are working on-shell where
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=0
,

E^{\prime}_{h}=\mathrm{Ric}^{\prime}_{h}-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\mathrm{Ric}^{\prime}_{h}\,g=F-\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}F\,g=G_{0}.

Next we transform or shift
h
by a diffeomorphism
\delta^{*}X
, i.e. set
\widetilde{h}=h+\delta^{*}X
, where
X=0
on
{\mathcal{C}}
,
X=\partial_{t}X=0
on

S
, so that
\delta\widetilde{h}=Y
. Then
\widetilde{h}
solves (
4.24
), since
E^{\prime}_{\delta^{*}X}=0
on-shell. Since
\widetilde{h}
is now in
\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
, this
completes the proof of the first statement.

To prove (
4.23
), suppose
\widetilde{L}(\widetilde{h})=0
, with
\widetilde{h}\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{0}
. Then by Proposition
4.4
,

\int_{M}\langle\widetilde{L}(k),\widetilde{h}\rangle=0,

for all
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
. By the above, the equation
\widetilde{L}(k)=G
is solvable for any
G\in C^{\infty}
with
k\in\widetilde{C}^{\infty}_{1}
.
Since the Lorentzian pairing in (
4.4
) is non-degenerate, it follows that
\widetilde{h}=0
, so that
Ker\widetilde{L}=0
.

∎

We now complete the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Proof.

Naturally, the proof is essentially the reverse of Lemma
4.5
. Thus, to prove (

), suppose

with

and so by Lemma

,

and

with

,

above implies that

Remark 5.1
.

An essentially immediate consequence of the well-posedness of a geometric IBVP or IBCVP is the following geometric uniqueness
statement. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{Diff}(M)
be an arbitrary smooth diffeomorphism of
M
, so
\varphi:S\rightarrow S
,
\varphi:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
and
\varphi:\Sigma\rightarrow\Sigma
. Let

\tau=(\iota,b)\in\mathcal{I}_{0}\times_{c}{\mathcal{B}}
(or
\tau=(\iota,b,\alpha)\in\mathcal{I}_{0}\times_{c}{\mathcal{B}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
) be any vacuum target data in
{\mathcal{T}}
and let

g
be a solution the vacuum equations
\mathrm{Ric}_{g}=0
with
\Phi(g)=\tau
; by well-posedness
g
is unique up to gauge, i.e. up to the
action of
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(M)
. Then the following equivariance property holds:

\Phi(\varphi^{*}g)=\varphi^{*}(\Phi(g)).

Thus the unique vacuum solution
g
(modulo the action of
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(M))
with data
\varphi^{*}\tau
is given by
\varphi^{*}g
. The equation (
5.2
) holds
since the initial data, boundary data, and corner data are geometric, i.e. transform in this way under the action of diffeomorphisms. The quotient
boundary data space
{\mathcal{B}}/\mathrm{Diff}_{\Sigma}({\mathcal{C}})
thus has essentially three geometric degrees of freedom - in dimension 4); cf. also the discussion in
[
3
]

By Corollary
1.3

In this section, we describe some of the main relevant aspects of examples presented in
[
33

. We focus on
n+1=4
dimensions, but the
discussion holds equally well in all dimensions, with only minor changes.

Consider Minkowski space with standard cylindrical coordinates

-dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{S^{2}(1)}.

Let
r(t):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a smooth function with
r(0)=1
and
r^{\prime}(0)=\alpha
. The graph of
r(t)
gives rise to a cylinder
C_{\alpha}\simeq\mathbb{R}\times S^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}
meeting the Cauchy surface
S=\{t=0\}\cap\{r\leq 1\}
at the angle
\alpha
. For
r(t)\equiv 1
,
C_{\alpha}

is the standard spherically symmetric timelike cylinder of radius
1
in Minkowski space, with induced metric

\gamma_{0}=-dt^{2}+g_{S^{2}(1)}

and with mean curvature
H_{C_{0}}=2
.

Now consider a 1-parameter family of deformations of
g_{0}
given by

g_{\alpha}=-V_{\alpha}(t)dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{S^{2}(1)},

where
V_{\alpha}=r(t)^{2}+\dot{r}(t)^{2}
. These are clearly still flat vacuum solutions of the Einstein equations. In fact they differ from
g_{0}
just by
time reparametrizations. These then induce reparametrizations of
r
and so diffeomorphisms of the domain which move the boundary
{\mathcal{C}}
.

The metric
g_{\alpha}
restricted to
C_{\alpha}
has
dr=\dot{r}dt
and so is given by

g_{C_{\alpha}}=r^{2}[-dt^{2}+g_{S^{2}(1)}]

and so
g_{C_{\alpha}}
is pointwise conformal to
\gamma_{0}
.

Next we calculate the mean curvature
H_{C_{\alpha}}
of
C_{\alpha}
in the metric
g_{\alpha}
. Since
T=\partial_{t}+\dot{r}(t)\partial_{r}
is tangent to
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}
,
the unit outward normal to
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}
in
g_{\alpha}
is given by

\nu_{\alpha}=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{r}(\frac{\dot{r}}{V}\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}).

The second fundamental form is given by

\begin{split}A_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}}&=\frac{1}{2}({\mathcal{L}}_{\nu_{\alpha}}g_{\alpha})^{\intercal}=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{2r}(L_{\frac{\dot{r}}{V}\partial_{t}+\partial_{r}}g_{\alpha})^{\intercal}=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{2r}({\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\dot{r}}{V}\partial_{t}}g_{\alpha}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial_{r}}g_{\alpha})^{\intercal}.\end{split}

For the Lie derivatives in the right side of the equation above, we have

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}_{\frac{\dot{r}}{V}\partial_{t}}g_{\alpha}&=\frac{\dot{r}}{V}(-\dot{V}dt^{2})+d(\frac{\dot{r}}{V})\odot g_{\alpha}(\partial_{t},\cdot)=\frac{\dot{r}}{V}(-\dot{V}dt^{2})+2\partial_{t}(\frac{\dot{r}}{V})dt(-Vdt)\\
&=-(\frac{\dot{r}\dot{V}}{V}+2\frac{\ddot{r}V-\dot{r}\dot{V}}{V})dt^{2}=-\frac{2\ddot{r}V-\dot{r}\dot{V}}{V}dt^{2}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}_{\partial_{r}}g_{\alpha}=2rg_{S^{2}(1)}.\end{split}

This gives

\begin{split}A_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}}&=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{2r}(-\frac{2\ddot{r}V-\dot{r}\dot{V}}{V}dt^{2}+2rg_{S^{2}(1)})^{\intercal}\end{split}

and hence

\begin{split}H_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}}&=\mathrm{tr}^{\intercal}A_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}}=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{2r}(\frac{2\ddot{r}V-\dot{r}\dot{V}}{Vr^{2}}+\frac{4}{r})=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{2r}\cdot\frac{2\ddot{r}V-\dot{r}\dot{V}+4Vr}{Vr^{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{r}(\frac{\ddot{r}r^{2}+\ddot{r}\dot{r}^{2}-\dot{r}^{2}r-\dot{r}^{2}\ddot{r}+2Vr}{Vr^{2}})\\
&=\frac{\sqrt{V}}{r}(\frac{\ddot{r}r^{2}+\dot{r}^{2}r+2r^{3}}{Vr^{2}})\end{split}

so that

H_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{V}}(\frac{\ddot{r}}{r}+(\frac{\dot{r}}{r})^{2}+2),

in agreement with
[
33
]
. A simple calcluation shows that

g_{\alpha}(\nu_{S},\nu_{{\mathcal{C}}})=-\alpha.

Setting
H_{{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha}}=H_{C_{0}}=2
gives the ODE

\frac{\ddot{r}}{r}+(\frac{\dot{r}}{r})^{2}=2(\sqrt{V}-1).

Since

Suppose for instance

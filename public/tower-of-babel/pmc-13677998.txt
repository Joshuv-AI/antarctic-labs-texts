First evidence of Tahyna virus in Central Italy and seasonal dynamics of its putative vector, Aedes caspius
Authors: Federico Romiti, Silvia Tofani, Antonella Cersini, Irene Del Lesto, Maria Teresa Scicluna, Gaetana Anita Marcario, Davide La Rocca, Silvia Rondón, Adele Magliano, Claudio De Liberato
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•First detection of Tahyna virus in Tuscany, Central Italy.•Tahyna virus RNA was detected in an Aedes caspius pool from Grosseto.•The sequence clustered within the European TAHV lineage.•Coastal Aedes caspius populations showed late-summer to autumn abundance peaks.•Aedes vexans, a primary Tahyna vector, was highly abundant in inland Tuscany.

Introduction

Tahyna virus (TAHV) is a member of the family Peribunyaviridae, genus Orthobunyavirus, belonging to the California serogroup (Mravcová et al., 2023). As most of the members of the genus Orthobunyavirus, TAHV is transmitted by arthropod vectors, specifically mosquitoes (Zhu et al., 2025). TAHV was the first mosquito-borne human pathogen isolated in Europe (Bárdoš and Danielová, 1959) and, at present, has a geographic distribution covering many regions of Europe and Asia, and some regions of Africa (Evans and Peterson, 2019). In Italy, TAHV was first recorded in Friuli Venezia-Giulia (North-Eastern Italy), in mosquitoes of the genus Aedes (Balducci et al., 1968), and then repeatedly in the Emilia Romagna Region (Calzolari et al., 2010; 2022; 2024). Human TAHV infections are mostly asymptomatic, but in some cases fever, myalgia, headache and nausea, are reported, especially in children, with rare cases of meningitis and encephalitis (Mravcová et al., 2023). Although serological positivities have been detected in many wild mammal species, the main amplifying host is considered the European hare (Lepus europaeus) (Mravcová et al., 2023). As for vectors, TAHV has been detected in many mosquito species, belonging to different genera (Höller et al., 2025), but species of the genus Aedes, and in particular Aedes vexans and Aedes caspius, appear to play the more relevant role as vectors of this virus in Europe (Calzolari et al., 2022; Mravcová et al., 2023). Interestingly, the virus has been recently detected in pools of the invasive Asian tiger mosquito, Aedes albopictus, (Calzolari et al., 2024) and this species was shown to be capable of transmitting the virus at mean temperatures of 27 °C with a ± 5 °C fluctuation (Höller et al., 2025). In Europe Ae. albopictus is mainly an urban mosquito, thus these findings would extend the risk of TAHV circulation from natural/rural areas to urban ones, increasing the risk of human exposure.

TAHV can be considered a neglected virus (Calzolari et al., 2022) as, although much evidence indicates its circulation in broad areas of Europe and its capability of causing disease in humans, few studies focused on this virus and data regarding its distribution are scanty. Within the framework of a research project, funded by the Ministry of Health, aimed at studying neglected mosquito-borne viruses in Italy, we here report the first detection of TAHV in Tuscany, Central Italy, representing the southernmost limit of its circulation in the Country to date.

Materials and methods

Entomological sampling and pooling

Mosquitoes for TAHV testing were collected within the framework of the Italian National Plan for Arbovirus Surveillance, with specific reference to entomological surveillance for West Nile disease (WND) (Ministero della Salute, 2019). Entomological sampling was performed according to the procedure described by Romiti et al. (2025). Overall, 78 CDC—CO₂ traps were deployed in 75 municipalities, covering all provinces of the Latium and Tuscany regions (Fig. 1A). Traps operated fortnightly, from April to November 2025, yielding a total of 1136 catches. Mosquitoes were morphologically identified using Italian identification keys (Severini et al., 2009, 2022). Monospecific pools containing 5–20 females were stored at −80 °C until molecular processing. Considering the results of a previous study (Calzolari et al., 2024), only selected species belonging to the Aedes genus were tested, namely Ae. albopictus Ae. caspius, Ae. detritus, and Ae. vexans. Mosquito species composition and Ae. caspius seasonality

The number of Culicidae and the total number of specimens belonging to the target Aedes species was calculated per Province. The relative abundance of Ae. albopictus, Ae. caspius, Ae. detritus and Ae. vexans was then expressed as the percentage contribution of each species to the total number of individuals of the target Aedes species. For the analysis of seasonality, exclusively Ae. caspius was considered, being the only one found positive for TAHV. The analysis was restricted to the Provinces where the species was most abundant and to sampling sites where more than 100 specimens were collected over the study period. For the selected sites, only female Ae. caspius collected on each sampling event were considered. To identify the main seasonal abundance period from spring to autumn 2025, the shortest temporal window including at least 90 % of all female Ae. caspius collected at each selected site was calculated. When the resulting interval was shorter than 14 days (e.g., single abundant catch), it was expanded to a minimum duration of 14 days, in view of the fortnightly interval between catches. This 90 % abundance window was used to identify the period during which Ae. caspius activity was highest at each site. Seasonal trends were visualised by plotting the number of female Ae. caspius collected on each sampling date, highlighting the 90 % abundance period.

Molecular analysis

Mosquito pools were suspended in 0.9 ml of ATL buffer (Animal Tissue Lysis buffer, Qiagen) containing a 5 mm stainless steel bead. All samples were homogenised by Tissue Lyser II (QIAGEN, GmbH, Hilden, Germany), followed by a centrifugation at 16,000 g. A 400 μl volume of the supernatant was used for the nucleic acid extraction, using the automatic extractor QIAsymphony (DSP Virus/Pathogen Kit, Qiagen, GmbH, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Pools were first subjected to cDNA synthesis, which was carried out using 20 μl of RNA with the High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems, Thermo Fisher Scientific), following the manufacturer’s instructions, using the Gene AmpR PCR System 9700 (Applied Biosystems, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham) with a thermal cycling profile of 10 min at 25 °C and 45 min at 37 °C. Samples were tested by a generic Orthobunyavirus PCR using primers BCS82C and BCS332V (Kuno et al., 1996) and by a TAHV-specific real-time PCR using primers F-TAHV and R-TAHV and probe P-TAHV (Calzolari et al., 2024), both targeting the S segment encoding the nucleocapsid protein. The obtained amplicons were sequenced by an automated fluorescence-based technique following the manufacturer’s instructions (ABI-PRISM 3130 Genetic Analyzer). The consensus nucleic sequence was obtained by using the Tracy pipeline v 0.7.3 (Rausch et al., 2020) and was aligned to public databases. In case of TAHV positive pools, the mosquito species identification was carried out amplifying a fragment of the mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase subunit I (COI) gene (Baldwin et al., 2009).

Results

Mosquito species composition and Ae. caspius seasonality

A total of 17,728 Culicidae were collected, including 9237 specimens of the target Aedes species. Due to the sampling method, no blood fed females were found. Results for each Province are reported in Table 1. The contribution of Ae. albopictus, Ae. caspius, Ae. detritus and Ae. vexans to the overall composition of target Aedes species varied markedly among Provinces (Fig. 1B). Overall, Ae. caspius was the dominant species among the four target ones in the coastal provinces of Central and Southern Tuscany and Northern Latium, with a more marked dominance in the Provinces of Livorno (99 %) and Grosseto (85 %). The highest abundance of Ae. detritus was recorded in Grosseto. Aedes vexans was detected in three provinces of Tuscany, accounting for the highest amount of target Aedes specimens in Arezzo, where it was the only tested species. Aedes albopictus was the dominant species in several provinces, being the only tested species in Rome, Lucca, and Massa. The seasonal trend of female Ae. caspius was studied in nine selected sampling sites located in the Provinces of Grosseto and Livorno. The highest cumulative abundance was observed at a sampling site in the Grosseto municipality, where a total of 2106 Ae. caspius specimens were collected. At this site, the seasonal pattern was highly concentrated, with at least 90 % of the collected female Ae. caspius (2057) in a single catch, on the second week of September (Fig. 2D). The extent of the 90 % period exhibited great variation among sampling sites, even within the same Province (Grosseto), in some places showing a highly concentrated phenology (Fig. 2B and D), in others a broader one (> 95 days) (Fig. 2A and F). Molecular outcomes and infection rates

A total of 6600 mosquitoes were divided into 356 pools and molecularly analysed, with the number and composition of pools summarised by municipality in Table 2 and the complete pool-wise data reported in Supplementary Table 1. TAHV was detected in one pool, consisting of 20 Ae. caspius collected on September 24th in the Gavorrano municipality (coordinates: 42.8907° N, 11.02034° E; Grosseto Province, Southern Tuscany) (Fig. 1B). The morphological species identification was confirmed by molecular analysis. Considering all Ae. caspius tested during the study (n = 5113), the minimum infection rate (MIR) for TAHV was 0.20‰. When calculated specifically for the positive collection, in which 306 Ae. caspius were tested, the MIR was 3.27‰. Similarly, the maximum-likelihood estimate (MLE) of TAHV was 0.20‰ (95 % CI: 0.01–0.86), considering all tested Ae. caspius (pool size = 20). When restricted to the positive collection, MLE was 3.37‰ (95 % CI: 0.19–14.78). This pool was positive for the generic PCR and for the TAHV specific real time PCR. The sample, identified as “GA26007576”, was subjected to Sanger sequencing. Discussion

This study provides the first report of TAHV in Central Italy, in the Tuscany Region, thus establishing the southernmost limit of its distribution in the Country to date. As this is the first study investigating the circulation of TAHV in this specific geographical area, it is impossible to ascertain whether this finding reflects a recent geographic expansion of TAHV or a long-standing undetected presence. In fact, to date TAHV received limited attention in both public health and research due to its limited clinical relevance and lack of associated fatalities (Calzolari et al., 2022). In our study, TAHV was detected in a single Ae. caspius pool collected on a lowland farm approximately 16 km from the coast at 13 m a.s.l. The environment was consistent with the ecology of Ae. caspius, a floodwater mosquito associated with low-lying, periodically inundated habitats, including coastal and inland marshes, temporary wetlands and agricultural landscapes (Balenghien et al., 2010; Calzolari et al., 2024). The farm lies within a heterogeneous rain-fed agricultural landscape crossed by small watercourses, with a natural wetland approximately 5 km away. Rainfall-driven flooding of low-lying areas and expansion of the wetland may therefore provide suitable local larval habitats. The wetland is well within the maximum recorded dispersal distance of Ae. caspius, a strong-flying species capable of flying 20 km away from breeding sites (Verdonschot and Besse-Lototskaya, 2014; Calzolari et al., 2024). Cattle were the predominant livestock on the farm and thus represented an abundant potential blood-meal source for this mammal-feeding mosquito (Stelder et al., 2023). As regards seasonal dynamics, differences were observed compared with studies carried out in northern Italy. In particular, the abundance of Ae. caspius was mainly concentrated from late summer to autumn in Tuscany, whereas in the Po Delta of Emilia-Romagna the highest densities were recorded in July–August (Veronesi et al., 2012; Calzolari et al., 2024).

The observed MIR for Ae. caspius (0.2‰) is in line with previous findings from northern Italy, which reported a MIR ranging from approximately 0.07‰ to 1.49‰, with the highest value recorded during the relatively intense TAHV circulation observed in Emilia-Romagna in 2022 (Calzolari et al., 2022, 2024).

Isolation of TAHV from Ae. caspius has been reported in studies performed in Slovakia, Czech Republic, and Austria (Bárdoš and Danielová, 1959; Danielová et al., 1976; Pilaski et al., 1985). In Italy, both isolation and molecular detection of TAHV have been reported from Ae. caspius (Calzolari et al., 2010; 2022; 2024). The characterised nucleocapsid region of the collected sample (GA26007576) showed high similarity to Italian and other European sequences. Although the A33E substitution identified respect to ADH93579.1 (Bennett et al., 2011) is uncommon, it is not reported as a key functional residue of the Orthobunyavirus nucleoprotein (Reguera et al., 2013).

Aedes caspius has been repeatedly implicated in the natural transmission cycle of TAHV based on field evidence, nevertheless experimental transmission studies are still needed to confirm its vector role (Mravcová et al., 2023). On the other hand, vector competence has been experimentally demonstrated for Ae. vexans, a species with demonstrated capacity for TAHV vertical transmission (Cai et al., 2023; Danielová and Ryba, 1979; Rödl et al., 1979).

The reported high abundance of this primary vector, even if localised to a specific sampling site in the Arezzo Municipality, deserves attention and further investigation. While this observation alone does not imply increased TAHV transmission risk in the area, the substantial local presence of this species may be epidemiologically relevant and warrants consideration in future surveillance activities.

As regards other target Aedes species, although the role of Ae. albopictus and Ae. detritus in TAHV circulation remains to be clarified, both species have previously tested positive for the virus (Li et al., 2010; Calzolari et al., 2024). The high abundance and widespread distribution of Ae. albopictus, together with the ecological requirements shared by Ae. detritus and recognized TAHV vectors such as Ae. caspius, warrant further investigation into their potential involvement in TAHV epidemiology.

Some limitations must beconsidered when interpreting the findings of this study. For instance, mosquito collection was performed on a single season, thus no interannual variation could be assessed. Finally, the finding of a single TAHV positive pool mean that these observations must be treated as preliminary, encouraging an expanded surveillance to the eastern regions bordering Tuscany and strengthened throughout the Lazio region, to better define the observed TAHV distribution.

In conclusion, this study provides the first report of TAHV in Central Italy, extending its known geographic distribution in the country. The detection of TAHV in Ae. caspius and the abundance of Ae. vexans in some surveyed areas highlights the potential role of these mosquito species in local viral transmission. Given the neglected status of this Orthobunyavirus, these findings underscore the need for targeted surveillance to better characterise vector abundance, distribution, population dynamics, and the associated public health risks.

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHeart failure (HF) is a major cause of hospitalization and mortality. Early initiation of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) improves outcomes in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). This study assesses the impact of starting GDMT with an SGLT2i in newly diagnosed HFrEF patients.Materials and methodsThe TIDY-HF multicenter prospective registry enrolled consecutive newly diagnosed HFrEF patients between October 2021 and March 2022 to evaluate the influence of medical treatment schedules in newly diagnosed HFrEF. In this substudy, patients were grouped by initial GDMT with or without an SGLT2i. Over six months, clinical variables, echocardiographic and laboratory parameters, and clinical events (hospitalizations, emergency visits) were collected. The primary aim was to compare GDMT implementation (quadruple therapy rates), with secondary aims assessing efficacy (target doses) and safety (adverse events).ResultsOf 518 patients, 400 initiated treatment with an SGLT2i, while 118 did not, with no differences in the baseline characteristics. At six months, 81.5% of patients who started with an SGLT2i were on quadruple therapy, compared to 61.2% in the non-SGLT2i group (p = 0.0001). The group starting with an SGLT2i showed a significant increase in left ventricular ejection fraction at 3 months (14.2% vs 10.6%; p = 0.023) and preservation of renal function (GFR change: -4.7 vs + 0.4 mL/min; p = 0.011). Furthermore, patients who started on SGLT2i showed a trend toward fewer HF decompensations and a significantly lower rate of non-cardiovascular hospitalizations.ConclusionsInitiating GDMT with an SGLT2i facilitates the early implementation of quadruple therapy, improves LVEF, preserves renal function, and shows a trend toward reducing HF decompensations. SGLT2i initiation also demonstrated a favourable safety profile, supporting their early use in newly diagnosed HFrEF patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05316-0.

Introduction

Across the world, heart failure (HF) remains a leading cause of death and hospital admissions. European data indicate that within one year, about 36% of acute HF patients and 14.5% of chronic HF patients experience either mortality or hospitalization [1]. Additionally, 27.4% are readmitted for cardiovascular (CV) reasons, with readmission frequency as a strong risk factor for mortality [2]. Recent advancements in treatment and care processes have improved HF prognosis. Clinical guidelines recommend the early use of guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) in all HF patients with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), which includes a beta-blocker (BB), a renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system inhibitor (RAASi), a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA), and a sodium-glucose cotransporter type 2 inhibitor (SGLT2i) to reduce CV mortality and HF-related hospitalization [3].

The strategy of rapidly implementing GDMT is safe and reduces all-cause mortality and HF readmissions, although evidence is limited in newly diagnosed HF patients [4]. In the TIDY-HF registry, 63.2% of newly diagnosed HFrEF patients initiated treatment with the four prognostic drugs, and 78.5% were on all four at three months, with no significant differences in target doses or adverse events compared to other initiation schedules [5]. However, the optimal drug regimen for an individual patient cannot be defined based on existing evidence. Initiation of therapy is therefore determined by clinical considerations such as heart rate (HR), renal function, atrial fibrillation (AF), blood pressure (BP), and potassium levels [6].

Several strategies for therapeutic optimization have been proposed. Some authors advocate for an approach based on 'clusters' or pharmacological groups to facilitate the rapid and dynamic integration of GDMT, combining treatments with prognostic benefits and those with additional advantages [7]. Others recommend a stepwise approach, beginning with a RAASi and BB, followed by the rapid addition of an MRA and SGLT2i, without optimising drug doses before initiating subsequent treatments [8]. Finally, some propose a three-step strategy, starting with a BB and SGLT2i, adding a RAASi within 1–2 weeks, and subsequently adding an MRA [9]. We evaluated whether early SGLT2i initiation in newly diagnosed HFrEF patients enables faster treatment individualisation and dose titration, while preserving a favourable safety profile in a real-world context.

Material and methods

Study population

This predefined sub-study of the TIDY-HF registry, a prospective, multicentre cohort conducted in Spain, has been described in detail elsewhere [5]. Consecutive patients with newly diagnosed HFrEF were enrolled between October 2021 and March 2022 during hospitalisation, outpatient visits, or emergency department presentations, and were followed for six months. Diagnosis was made by the attending cardiologist in line with current clinical guidelines [3, 8].

Inclusion criteria were newly diagnosed HF with LVEF ≤ 40% on echocardiography. Main exclusion criteria included contraindication to any component of quadruple therapy, low systolic blood pressure (BP), severe renal dysfunction, hyperkalaemia, planned major cardiac intervention, or life expectancy < 1 year. Treatment and follow-up followed local practice and current evidence, with assessments recommended at around one, three, and six months [3, 8].

Variables included

The variables collected included demographic, clinical data, and echocardiographic assessments encompassed LVEF [10], right ventricular function (TAPSE), and valvular abnormalities. Laboratory parameters included renal function (creatinine, eGFR), electrolytes (sodium, potassium), and biomarkers (CA125, NT-proBNP). Information on the pharmacological treatments—covering BB, RAASi, MRAs, SGLT2i, and loop diuretics—was recorded, including dose adjustments and treatment modifications during follow-up. Clinical outcomes tracked included hospitalizations and emergency visits due to HF, CV or other causes, and mortality. Baseline data were obtained before discharge for inpatients and emergency visits, or on the day of inclusion for outpatients. All available data were included in the analyses, and no imputation of missing values was performed (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Emergency room visits or readmission for cardiovascular causes during 6 months of follow-up

Objectives

The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the implementation of GDMT in patients newly diagnosed with HFrEF from the baseline visit through longitudinal follow-up, comparing two initial therapeutic regimens—one including an SGLT2i and the other without. The secondary objective was to assess the efficacy, as reflected by the attainment of target or maximal tolerated doses, and the safety profile, determined by the incidence of adverse clinical events and treatment-related complications, associated with each therapeutic strategy.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables were summarized using means and standard deviations or medians and interquartile ranges (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between groups for continuous variables were performed using Student’s t-test for two groups or ANOVA for more than two groups; non-normally distributed variables (assessed by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test) were analyzed with the Wilcoxon test. Differences among the four treatment groups were evaluated using Chi-square tests for categorical variables and ANOVA for continuous variables (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Emergency room visits or readmission for non-cardiovascular causes during 6 months of follow-up

Emergency room visits or readmission for non-cardiovascular causes during 6 months of follow-up

Time-to-event analyses were performed using the Kaplan–Meier method for three outcomes: (a) emergency department visits or readmissions due to HF (HF decompensations); (b) emergency department visits or readmissions for cardiovascular causes other than HF; and (c) emergency department visits or readmissions for non-cardiovascular causes. Event-free survival curves were compared using the log-rank test.

A multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was conducted to assess the association between SGLT2i initiation and clinical outcomes, adjusting for baseline variables that were unevenly distributed between groups. Additionally, a multivariable logistic regression analysis adjusted for the same covariates was performed to evaluate further the robustness of the associations observed. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SPSS version 22.0 (IBM, New York, USA).

Ethics and quality of the registry

The study design, data collection, and reporting were performed in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines for observational studies [11].

The registry was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Hospital Universitario Severo Ochoa Ethics Committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion. Data were securely recorded in an electronic database with all patient information anonymized. Access to the complete dataset was restricted to the principal investigators only.

Results

We included 532 patients, excluding 14 who only started with one drug. We selected 518 patients; 400 started with an SGLT2i in the baseline therapy (77.2%), and 118 did not. No significant differences were observed between the two groups, which exhibited comparable rates of comorbidities. Discussion

Our study, comprising one of the largest cohorts of newly diagnosed HFrEF patients, evaluated the impact of initiating GDMT, including an SGLT2i. Key findings include: a) Patients starting with an SGLT2i were more likely to receive more drugs of quadruple therapy during the follow-up; b) These patients experienced greater improvements in LVEF and were more likely to preserve renal function; c) Initiation of SGLT2i therapy was associated with a trend toward fewer HF-related admissions and a significantly lower rate of non-CV hospitalizations; and d) Initiating treatment with an SGLT2i showed a favourable safety profile, with a low rate of withdrawal.

Rapid initiation of GDMT—comprising a BB, RAASi, MRA, and SGLT2i—has been demonstrated to reduce CV mortality and HF readmissions [3]. In the STRONG-HF trial, the rapid initiation of GDMT within 180 days reduced the risk of HF readmission or all-cause mortality by 44% (RR 0.66 [95% CI 0.50–0.86]) [4]. However, this trial did not include SGLT2i, and the participants were previously treated, with a high percentage receiving medications before randomization, such as MRA. In real-world settings, among newly diagnosed patients with HFrEF, nearly 80% were on quadruple therapy within 3 months [5], highlighting the importance of HF units in achieving target doses quickly.

Despite guidelines and recommendations, clinicians have observed a sequential implementation of GDMT, incorporating new medications into previously established evidence-based treatments [12]. However, current practice and guidelines now recommend initiating GDMT and gradually increasing doses to the maximum tolerated without providing specific guidance on the order in which drugs should be introduced. Instead, they emphasize the consideration of individual factors when initiating and up-titrating. Some authors have suggested starting with a BB and an SGLT2i, given the significant impact of BB on mortality, particularly sudden death, and the potential of SGLT2i to reduce HF readmissions. This approach helps counteract the potential congestive effects of BB. After 1–2 weeks, the next step would be introducing a RAASi, followed by an MRA in the third step. The tolerability of the MRA could be enhanced by the beneficial effects of SGLT2i and Angiotensin Receptor-Neprilysin Inhibitors (ARNI) on renal function and potassium homeostasis. Our findings provide clinical evidence supporting this rational sequencing approach.

Our study demonstrated that early initiation of SGLT2i facilitates the initiation of other drugs, increasing the likelihood of achieving quadruple therapy. SGLT2i has a favorable safety profile, with a low incidence of adverse events and no need for dose titration. In the DAPA-HF trial, the incidence of volume depletion and hypotension in the dapagliflozin group was 7.2% and 0.3%, respectively, with no significant differences compared to the placebo [13]. Our cohort showed no differences in BP reduction between patients who started with an SGLT2i despite receiving more drugs. Furthermore, the glucosuric effect of SGLT2i may contribute to the decongestive impact in the acute setting of HF, as demonstrated in the EMPULSE trial [14]. Our study also showed a trend toward a reduced need for loop diuretics during follow-up.

Treatment with SGLT2i has been shown to preserve renal function in HF patients, with the typical initial and reversible decline in renal function observed at the beginning of treatment [15, 16]. Despite receiving additional medications, patients in our study who started treatment with an SGLT2i did not experience acute renal events and even demonstrated a significant improvement in GFR at 6 months, highlighting the renal protective effect of SGLT2i. Additionally, potassium disturbances were not noted, facilitating the early introduction of RAASi and MRA. A previous meta-analysis involving 49,875 patients demonstrated a reduced risk of severe hyperkalemia, reinforcing the benefits of early SGLT2i initiation. In our study, patients who began treatment with an SGLT2i received more MRA, likely due to the beneficial effects of SGLT2i on potassium levels and renal function [17].

The rationale for early incorporation of SGLT2i into quadruple therapy is supported by evidence demonstrating early benefits in reducing the risk of CV death or HF progression, particularly among patients recently hospitalized for HF [18, 19]. In our study, patients who initiated treatment with an SGLT2i from the outset showed trends toward earlier improvements in prognosis, especially regarding HF-related and non-CV events. Additionally, improvements in cardiac remodeling parameters were observed, with a numerically greater increase in LVEF among patients who started SGLT2i, as observed in previous trials [20–22]. However, these results should be interpreted cautiously given the observational design and the short follow-up period. The early initiation of SGLT2i in HF patients may help reduce hospitalization rates, potentially resulting in cost savings estimated between $1,439 and $2,668 per patient within the first year post-discharge, excluding medication expenses [23]. Overall, these findings support the practicality of early SGLT2i initiation as part of comprehensive GDMT in HFrEF, while acknowledging that further studies are needed to confirm the long-term impact on cardiac remodeling and clinical outcomes.

Our study has several limitations. First, as a non-randomized observational study, residual confounding and selection bias cannot be excluded and represent major limitations. Although the distribution of baseline characteristics was relatively homogeneous, unmeasured factors may still have influenced treatment selection and outcomes. Treatment decisions were made at the clinician’s discretion. Although insulin or other drug use was not recorded, no evident selection bias accounted for the proportion of patients who did not receive SGLT2i during follow-up.

Furthermore, the absence of data on medication adherence limits our ability to draw definitive conclusions regarding the superiority of specific treatment regimens. Nevertheless, close follow-up in heart failure units may have improved adherence. Finally, event-free survival was assessed using univariate analysis; however, the Kaplan–Meier method provides a more appropriate evaluation of survival and time-to-event.

In conclusion, our study, encompassing one of the largest cohorts of newly diagnosed HFrEF patients, suggests that initiating GDMT with an SGLT2i provides multiple clinical benefits. These include facilitating the early implementation of quadruple therapy, improving LVEF, preserving renal function, and showing a trend toward fewer HF decompensations, along with a significant reduction in non-cardiovascular hospitalizations. Moreover, SGLT2i initiation demonstrated a favorable safety profile, supporting its early incorporation into the management strategy for patients with newly diagnosed HFrEF.

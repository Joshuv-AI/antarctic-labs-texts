Prevalence of porcine toxoplasmosis using ERP-iELISA in Hubei Province
Authors: Linyue Wu, Mingfeng He, Yu Wang, Xiaodie Ruan, Liyan Luo, Bang Shen, Rui Fang, Junlong Zhao, Yanqin Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractToxoplasma gondii (T. gondii) is an intracellular parasite that can infect almost all mammalian species, including humans and important economic animals such as pigs and sheep, thereby causing serious public health security issues. In pigs, such infections are common and economically significant, yet distinguishing between infection routes remains challenging. The current commonly used detection methods mainly aim to quickly detect positive cases of toxoplasmosis but cannot distinguish between different modes of infection. To distinguish whether cysts or oocysts are the source of T. gondii infection, the ERP protein that specifically expressed in T. gondii sporulated oocysts stagewas applied to develop an indirect enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (iELISA) for specific detection of oocyst-derived porcine toxoplasmosis. This study used the TgGRA1-iELISA and TgERP-iELISA detection methods to conduct a serological survey of toxoplasmosis in pigs in 17 municipalities of Hubei Province, with a total of 2,303 sera samples from 115 farms. Serological detection results demonstrated that TgGRA1-iELISA detected 744 positive samples, with a detection rate of 32.31%, and TgERP-iELISA detected 383 positive samples, with a detection rate of 16.63%, and all positive samples were positive for GRA1 antibody. This indicates that the modes of Toxoplasmosis infection in pigs in Hubei Province are through both cyst infection and oocyst infection. Furthermore, the detection rate of toxoplasmosis varies significantly across different regions. This study provides the first large-scale evidence of oocyst-derived porcine toxoplasmosis in Hubei Province and establishes a tool to support source-targeted prevention strategies in pig farms.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00436-025-08601-1.Keywords: Toxoplasma gondii, Oocyst, ERP, GRA1, IELISA

Introduction

Toxoplasma gondii (T. gondii) is an obligate intracellular protozoan pathogen responsible for causing zoonotic toxoplasmosis, a disease with significant morbidity in both humans and animals. Feline was identified as its unique definitive host (Weiss and Dubey 2009). T. gondii exhibits a complex life history involving both asexual (in intermediate hosts) and sexual (in definitive hosts) reproduction stages (Mao et al. 2021), with infectious forms categorized as tachyzoites, bradyzoites, and oocysts. Toxoplasmosis exhibits a broad host range and complex transmission routes, with infection rates in animal populations being significantly higher than those in humans (Liu et al. 2019). In China, pigs and sheep are the most highly infected meat-producing animals, with seroprevalence rates consistently exceeding those of other livestock (Deng et al. 2018; Li et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2024). Their role as major reservoirs represents a critical pathway for the zoonotic transmission of toxoplasmosis to humans.

Postnatal infection in humans and animals primarily occurs via two routes: ingestion of uncooked meat containing tissue cysts, or alternatively, exposure to oocyst-contaminated food/water (Frenkel 1970). T. gondii infection prevalence varies geographically across China, with epidemiological disparities attributed to regional differences in husbandry practices and environmental factors (Yang et al. 2024). Current diagnostic methods primarily aim to achieve rapid T. gondii detection, yet they share a critical limit: inability to discriminate between infection routes. However, distinguishing between infection routes is crucial for formulating targeted prevention and control strategies. Therefore, it is urgent to find a specific antigen that can distinguish the different infection routes and establish the detection method.

GRA1, the first discovered dense granular protein of T. gondii, regulates arachidonic acid-stimulated intracellular calcium release via organelle-targeted expression (Lin et al. 2010). The ELISA method for detecting toxoplasmosis established based on GRA1 exhibits good sensitivity and specificity and performs well compared with commercially available ELISA kits. GRA1 is a protein with high immunogenicity in toxoplasmosis and is expected to serve as a potential marker for toxoplasmosis screening(Wang 2017; Muflikhah and Artama 2017).

The late-stage development abundant (LEA) proteins, first discovered in cotton seeds (Dure et al. 1981), exhibit good hydrophilicity and thermal stability in solution (Wise and Tunnacliffe 2004). These proteins have been found to help plant seeds resist abiotic stresses from the environment such as high salt and drought, and they are also found in nutritional tissues (Hundertmark and Hincha 2008). With further research, it was found that LEA proteins are not exclusive to plants; LEA homologues have since been identified in other organisms, including bacteria, worms, and invertebrates (Zan et al. 2020). The LEA protein helps cells to resist the fatal damage caused by dehydration, and the ability of desiccation-tolerant rotifers to survive in dry environments without accumulating sugar is explained by the up-regulation of the gene encoding the LEA protein (Lapinski and Tunnacliffe 2003).

T. gondii possesses four putative LEA proteins, namely TgLEA850, TgLEA860, TgLEA870, and TgLEA880. These four proteins have been implicated in T. gondii oocysts resistance to harsh environments such as high salt, cold and dry. However, further research is required to delineate the specific gene-environment resistance relationships (Arranz-Solís et al. 2023). Notably, TgERP (also referred to as TgLEA850) has demonstrated remarkable immunogenicity and the capacity to discriminate between oocyst-derived and cyst-derived toxoplasmosis (Hill et al. 2011). Concurrently, two other LEA family proteins, TgLEA870 and TgLEA880, have been applied to develop ELISA detection method for discriminating T. gondii oocyst-derived toxoplasmosis in sheep and cats, respectively(Han 2023; Zhang 2023).

Hubei Province has always been a major pig production province and a traditional pig transportation center. Therefore, this study developed TgERP-iELISA, first time large-scale assessing oocyst infection at large scale surveyed pigs in Hubei (China).

Materials and methods

Serum samples

The pig serum samples used for the retrospective assessment of the seroprevalence of porcine toxoplasmosis in Hubei Province were obtained from the animal serum sample bank of the Hubei Provincial Center for Animal Disease Prevention and ControlHubei Provincial Center for Animal Disease Prevention and Control. These convenience-sampled sera were collected between May 2022 and May 2023 for routine animal disease surveillance and diagnostic purposes, covering a broad geographical area across all 17 prefecture-level cities in Hubei Province. All the samples were stored at −20℃ (Table 1). The pig anti-T. gondii positive sera (tachyzoite, cysts and oocysts) were preserved in our laboratory. No animals were specifically sampled for this study; therefore, no ethical approval was necessary.

Preparation of Recombinant antigen ERP

The recombinant antigen ERP (Gene ID: TGME49_276850) was expressed in Escherichia coli (E. coli). The target gene was amplified using specific primers and cloned into the pET-28a vector, resulting in the recombinant plasmid pET28a-ERP.The recombinant protein was confirmed to be correct by enzyme digestion and DNA sequencing and was expressed in E. coli BL21 (DE3) under the following induction conditions: 1 mM isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) as the inducer, incubation in a constant-temperature shaker at 37 °C, and an induction duration of 4 h. After induction, the recombinant protein was purified via nickel affinity chromatography and subsequently analyzed by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) using a 15% polyacrylamide gel (Vazyme, China).

The primer sequences used for the construction of the recombinant plasmid are as follows: ERP-F (5′-GTGGACAGCAAATGGGTCGCATGGCGACCGAGCACTCACA-3′); ERP-R (5′-ATCTCAGTGGTGGTGGTGGTGGTGGCCAAGCTTTTCTTGAACCT-3′); pET-28a-F (5′-CACCACCACCACCACCACTG-3′); pET-28a-R (5′-GCGACCCATTTGCTGTCCAC-3′).

Preparation of pig anti-T. Gondii (tachyzoite, cyst, and oocyst) positive Sera

The pig anti-T. gondii positive sera used in this study was prepared and provided by another member of our laboratory. The procedure was as follows: ICR mice were intraperitoneally inoculated with 200 tachyzoites of the T. gondii DB#3 (Type II) strain per mouse. After 30 days, the mice were euthanized, and brain tissue was collected to isolate tissue cysts. 500 cysts were then orally administered to 3-month-old T. gondii-negative cats. Oocysts were subsequently isolated from the feces. For serum preparation, the tachyzoite infection group was intraperitoneally inoculated with 10⁴ tachyzoites per pig; the tissue cyst infection group was orally administered 4000 tissue cysts per pig; and the oocyst infection group was orally administered oocysts on three separate occasions (Day 0: 3000 oocysts/pig, Day 10: 4000 oocysts/pig, and Day 30: 2000 oocysts/pig). Sera were collected after testing positive for T. gondii antibodies. This study was authorized to use this batch of serum for subsequent experiments.

Modified agglutination test (MAT)

The MAT was performed in U-shaped 96-well plates using the method described earlier(Dubey and Desmonts 1987). Serum samples were tested starting from a 1:25 dilution, followed by serial 2-fold dilutions. Each plate included controls using validated positive and negative cat sera.

Western blot

For Western blot analysis, the purified recombinant ERP protein was first separated on a 15% SDS-PAGE gel. The separated proteins were then transferred to a 0.2 μm Immobilon-PSQ PVDF membrane (Millipore, USA) at 75 V. The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk powder (SMP) solution before being incubated overnight at room temperature with pig anti-T. gondii (tachyzoite, cyst, and oocyst) positive sera and pig negative serum. Then, the membrane was incubated with a 1:5000 dilution of goat anti-pig IgG HRP-conjugated antibody (Solarbio, China). Finally, protein bands were detected using BeyoECL Star (Beyotime, China). The presence of specific bands indicates a reaction between the ERP protein and antibodies in the serum.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

Chessboard titration was used to determine the optimal antigen coating concentration for ERP, as well as the optimal dilutions for the porcine serum (primary antibody) and the HRP-conjugated goat anti-pig IgG secondary antibody (Solarbio, China). Antigens were serially diluted to concentrations of 2.5 µg/mL, 5 µg/mL, 7.5 µg/mL, and 10 µg/mL and coated onto a 96-well microplate (BIOFIL, China) by incubating overnight at 4 °C. The porcine serum and the HRP-conjugated secondary antibody were also serially diluted. After adding 100 µL of each diluted antibody to the respective wells and completing the reaction steps, the optical density at 630 nm (OD630) was measured. The optimal concentrations were then determined by calculating the positive to negative ratio (P/N value) for each dilution combination.

After establishing the optimal conditions for antigen and antibody concentrations, we determined the best blocking solution (PBST with 1% BSA, 2% BSA, 2.5% SMP, or 5% SMP) by adding 150 µL per well. Next, we optimized the incubation times for the serum primary antibody (15 min, 30 min, 45 min, 60 min, 75 min and 90 min) and the enzyme-labeled secondary antibody (30 min, 45 min, 60 min and 90 min), as well as the TMB substrate reaction time (10 min, 15 min, 20 min and 25 min). The reactions were stopped with 40% hydrofluoric acid (HF). The optimal incubation times and reaction time for the primary antibody, secondary antibody, and TMB substrate were determined by calculating the P/N ratio from the OD readings. Finally, we tested 22 T. gondii-negative porcine serum samples to determine the cutoff value. The cutoff was calculated as the average optical density ratio (S/N value) of these samples plus three standard deviations (cutoff = average + 3 × SD).

Evaluation of TgERP-iELISA

The optimized TgERP-iELISA was utilized to assess its specificity against common porcine viruses (CSFV, PCV, PRV) and its ability to detect T. gondii infections from both tachyzoite and tissue cyst origins. A positive porcine serum was diluted at 1:25, 1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:400, 1:800, 1:1600, 1:3200 times, and then the OD630 of the sample was detected by TgERP-iELISA method with optimized conditions. The maximum serum dilution with a positive test result was selected as the result of the sensitivity test.

TgGRA1-iELISA

TgGRA1-iELISA used the previously reported ELISA method for detection of porcine toxoplasmosis (Wang 2017), purified the antigen, coated with the antigen at 3 µg/mL, and the porcine serum sample was diluted at 1:50 and the enzyme-labeled secondary antibody at 1:500. When the S/N value of the sample was greater than 2.1, it was determined to be positive.

Statistical analysis

Pearson’s chi-square test was used to compare the positive rates among different regions, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05. Data analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics Software Version 27.0.

Results

Preparation of Recombinant antigen ERP and Western blot analysis

After the amplification of ERP fragment, the fragment was connected to pET-28a vector through ligand transformation and successfully expressed at 37℃ (Fig. 1a). The protein was incubated with pig anti-T. gondii positive sera (tachyzoite, cysts and oocysts). The results showed that the ERP protein only reacts with pig anti-T. gondii-oocyst positive serum (Fig. 1b) but not react with pig negative serum (Fig. 1c), pig anti-T. gondii-cyst positive serum (Fig. 1 d) and pig anti-T. gondii-tachyzoite positive serum (Fig. 1e). It further indicated that the ERP protein could serologically distinguish the different sources of T. gondii infection in pigs.

Optimization of TgERP‑iELISA

The iELISA method for detecting T. gondii oocyst infection in pigs was established using ERP as antigen and named TgERP-iELISA. To optimize the procedure, all key reaction conditions were systematically evaluated and adjusted. Following chessboard titration (Supplementary Table 1), the optimal concentrations were determined as 5 µg/mL for antigen coating, 1:50 for pig serum dilution, and 1:1000 for enzyme-labeled secondary antibody dilution. Based on these conditions, we determined the optimal blocking solution to be 5% SMP with a 60-minute blocking time (Fig. 2a). Concurrently, the optimal primary and secondary antibody incubation times were set at 30 min each (Fig. 2bc), with the optimal substrate color development occurring at 25 min at 37℃ (Fig. 2 d). Under the above optimal experimental procedure, 22 porcine T. gondii-negative sera were detected, and the cutoff value for positivity was determined to be an S/N value greater than 3.56 (Fig. 2e)

Optimization of TgERP-iELISA. a Blocking buffer optimization of concentration and incubation time. b the optimum incubation time of pig serum. c the optimum incubation time of enzyme-labeled secondary antibody. d the best color development time. e Cutoff value of pig negative serum (cutoff = average + 3 × SD)

Evaluation of TgERP-iELISA

To further assess the feasibility of this method, specificity and sensitivity experiments were conducted. This involved testing for cross-reactivity with three common porcine viruses (CSFV, PCV, PRV), along with T. gondii tachyzoite-positive and tissue cyst-positive serum samples. The results showed that all the S/N values were below the cutoff (Fig. 3a), confirming the method’s excellent specificity by demonstrating no cross-reactivity with these five pathogens. To assess sensitivity, pig anti-T. gondii-oocyst positive serum was serially diluted. A positive signal was consistently detected up to a dilution of 1:1600 (Fig. 3b), indicating good sensitivity for the TgERP-iELISA.

Investigation of Porcine T. gondii Oocyst Infection

It is important to note that all TgERP-iELISA-positive samples were positive for the GRA1 antibody. This difference in prevalence suggested the coexistence of both oocyst-derived and tissue cyst-derived infections in the pig population of Hubei Province. Discussion

Toxoplasmosis is a serious zoonotic parasitic disease with a wide prevalence and strong pathogenicity. Among the numerous intermediate hosts, pig infection is a major global concern due to its close link to both human and animal health. The ERP protein, a member of the LEA family, is located on the T. gondii oocyst wall and is crucial for the oocyst’s long-term survival and resistance to the external environment. Hill has used this protein to demonstrate that an ERP-based assay can distinguish the source of host infection (Hill et al. 2011). ELISA is a convenient, sensitive and fast method for the detection of T. gondii in clinic. The commonly used recombinant antigen detection method cannot distinguish the infection route of the host. There are few reports on antigens that can distinguish the infection route of T. gondii. Currently, CCp5A (Santana et al. 2015; Liu et al. 2019), in addition to ERP described in this study, is known to distinguish the oocysts.

In this study, an iELISA method based on the TgERP protein was established to specifically diagnose swine toxoplasmosis induced by oocyst infection. This method demonstrated high specificity and sensitivity in preliminary validation. Nevertheless, a notable limitation of this study was the restricted scope of cross-reactivity testing due to the limited diversity of the available swine serum panel in our laboratory. Specifically, we were precluded from comprehensive testing against prevalent swine pathogens, such as Porcine Reproductive and Respiratory Syndrome Virus (PRRSV) and Porcine Circovirus Type 2 (PCV2), as well as other parasitic agents including helminths and coccidia. This is mainly because all currently available swine serum samples infected with other pathogens are derived from clinical cases rather than being prepared through standardized experimental infections. Furthermore, we did not explore the broader diagnostic applications of the ERP protein, such as developing a TgERP-IgM ELISA for acute infection detection or establishing multiplex assays that combine ERP with other antigens. Finally, a specific commercial ELISA kit for the detection of pig oocyst infection is currently unavailable on the market, precluding comparative validation experiments against a recognized external diagnostic standard at this stage.

To evaluate the feasibility, a serological survey was conducted on 2,303 retrospectively collected archival swine serum samples from Hubei Province (2022–2023) using a combination of the ERP-iELISA and the established GRA1-iELISA. The total infection rate for swine toxoplasmosis was determined to be 32.31% (744/2,303). Notably, this prevalence level was not statistically different from the 35.2% (103/293) positivity documented in a limited regional monitoring effort in 2007 (Jiang et al. 2007). A comparative analysis showed that the positive samples detected by TgERP-iELISA were basically included in the positive samples detected by TgGRA1-iELISA, accounting for 51.48% (383/744), indicating that there were both oocyst infection and cyst infection in Hubei pig population. Furthermore, the positive rate of oocyst in Jingmen, Xiangyang and Xiantao was 21.70% (CI: 14.52%−30.97%), 21.38% (CI: 16.89%−26.64%) and 38.00% (CI: 28.64%−48.29%), respectively, indicating that oocyst infection was the main source of infection in these four areas. However, due to the use of retrospectively archived serum samples and the lack of detailed individual- and farm-level risk factor information in this study, we were unable to determine the specific causes underlying the significant regional differences in infection rates. It is therefore essential that future studies adopt a prospective design and systematically record the corresponding host information (e.g., age, breed, sex) and husbandry environment data for each sample. Given the significant regional heterogeneity in infection rates observed in this study, we hypothesize that geographic and environmental factors may partly explain these variations. For instance, cities like Xiantao are in humid climatic zones, which may facilitate the survival and transmission of oocysts.

Disease poses a significant threat to the porcine industry, with the emergence of new pathogens and the re-emergence of old ones creating immense pressure on biosecurity and development. Therefore, controlling pathogens at their source and along their transmission routes is crucial (Alarcón et al. 2021). Feed and water are important media for pathogens to enter the farm. While contamination during processing is minimal, the primary risk lies in the raw materials. Studies have shown that T. gondii oocysts can be inactivated at 58℃−60℃ (Wainwright et al. 2010; Hekmat 2019). Consequently, feed should undergo high-temperature heat treatment before feeding, followed by drying. Additionally, drinking water and water supply equipment should be regularly disinfected. As a powerful tool for disease prevention and control, vaccines are also essential in the fight against T. gondii. Implementing scientific immunization programs can effectively reduce disease incidence (Li et al. 2024).

This study on the seroepidemiology of porcine toxoplasmosis in Hubei Province not only fills a critical gap in baseline data but also reveals pronounced regional heterogeneity in oocyst-driven infections. These findings offer a scientific foundation for transitioning from generalized interventions to targeted control strategies, moving beyond post-outbreak statistics toward preemptive source tracing. To translate these insights into practice, future efforts should focus on developing rapid on-site diagnostic tools such as colloidal gold test strips, combined with large-scale serological screening and risk factor analysis, to deliver farm-specific recommendations. Furthermore, this study highlights the need for interdisciplinary collaboration under a “One Health” framework. As a typical zoonosis, effective toxoplasmosis control requires integrated surveillance of both animal and human infections. By identifying geographic overlaps in infection hotspots among pigs and humans, this approach can help clarify potential transmission pathways along the “pig-to-human” chain. Such collaboration would facilitate joint interventions by agricultural and public health sectors, enabling comprehensive strategies that interrupt transmission at its source and ultimately establish a coordinated, efficient prevention system to safeguard both animal health and public security.

Conclusions

This study successfully established a specific and sensitive detection method for oocyst-induced porcine toxoplasmosis, namely TgERP-iELISA—and this is the first large-scale application of TgERP-iELISA in pigs in Hubei (China). The serological results showed that pigs in Hubei province were infected with both oocyst infection and cyst infection, with significant differences among different regions. The establishment of this method marks a significant advance in the prevention and control of swine toxoplasmosis, shifting from a phase of “post-event statistics” to a new stage of “pre-event traceability.” This is of great significance for controlling toxoplasmosis on pig farms. In the future, research and practice in this field can be further advanced by developing more rapid diagnostic tools, such as colloidal gold test strips, and by integrating risk factor analysis to provide more targeted recommendations for the prevention and control of swine toxoplasmosis.

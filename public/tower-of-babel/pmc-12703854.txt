Design and rationale for the VICTRIVA study: A randomized, double-blind, phase 3b study of vedolizumab in combination with upadacitinib in Crohn's disease
Authors: Silvio Danese, Bruce E Sands, Brian G Feagan, Vipul Jairath, Remo Panaccione, Laurent Peyrin-Biroulet, Peter M Irving, Stefan Schreiber, Iris Dotan, Marc Ferrante, Geert R D'Haens, Stephen Jones, Marcelo Freire, Dirk Lindner, Shashi Adsul, Pooja Oberai, Jean-Frédéric Colombel
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionRemission rates in patients with Crohn's disease (CD) suggest a therapeutic ceiling with one advanced targeted treatment, representing an unmet need. Dual targeted therapy may provide a more effective treatment approach.MethodsThe primary objective of VICTRIVA (NCT06227910), a randomized, double-blind, phase 3b trial in biologic-experienced and biologic-naïve adults with CD, is to assess whether vedolizumab combined with upadacitinib induction improves rates of clinical remission and endoscopic response at week (W)12 vs. vedolizumab alone. Patients will be randomized 1:1 to vedolizumab (300 mg at W0, W2, W6, and W10) and either upadacitinib 45 mg or placebo daily. W12 responders will enter the maintenance arm up to W52 (vedolizumab monotherapy every 8 weeks [Q8W] from W14 to W52; Q4W escalation if needed). Patients who lose response during maintenance treatment despite dose escalation will enter the rescue substudy (vedolizumab Q4W plus upadacitinib 45 mg for 12 weeks, then vedolizumab monotherapy in patients who regain response). Assessments include patient-reported outcomes (PROs), CD activity index (CDAI), and Simple Endoscopic Score for CD (SES-CD). Globally, 396 patients (198 in each group) will be enrolled. Of these, a minimum of 50 and maximum of 50 % will be biologic-naïve. Co-primary endpoints are CDAI clinical remission and SES-CD endoscopic response at W12. Key secondary endpoints include PRO2 clinical remission at W12, and CDAI clinical remission, SES-CD endoscopic response, and PRO2 clinical remission at W52. Safety endpoints include the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events.ConclusionVICTRIVA will evaluate the efficacy and safety of combined vedolizumab and upadacitinib induction therapy relative to vedolizumab monotherapy, aiming to break the current therapeutic ceiling.Clinical trials registrationClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06227910.Keywords: Crohn's disease, Phase 3b, Methodology, Placebo, Upadacitinib, Vedolizumab

Introduction

Crohn's disease (CD) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) affecting the gastrointestinal tract with various extraintestinal and laboratory manifestations, that can lead to progressive bowel damage [[1], [2], [3]]. The pathophysiology of CD is complex, with multiple environmental and genetic factors interacting to cause disease expression; the natural disease course can range from slow progression, with extended periods of remission, to aggressive and incapacitating disease [2]. In addition, quality of life can be significantly impaired in patients with CD [4].

Despite considerable progress in the treatment of CD, short-term and 1-year clinical remission rates range between 30 % and 50 % when using advanced targeted treatment (ATT) agents such as vedolizumab or upadacitinib as monotherapy. This suggests that a therapeutic ceiling has been reached with this approach, leading to interest in combination therapy [5]. Notably the SONIC study investigated the combination of ATT with immunomodulators and demonstrated that a combination of infliximab and azathioprine was more effective for the induction of corticosteroid-free clinical remission than either azathioprine or infliximab monotherapy; however, only 58 % of patients in the combination group achieved clinical remission after 26 weeks, suggesting a need for more effective strategies [6].

Dual targeted therapy (DTT)/combined advanced therapy is an emerging therapeutic concept, and the combination of at least two ATTs with differing mechanisms of action has been gaining interest in IBD because of the varied pattern of cytokines and molecules driving inflammation in the disease [5,7]. Simultaneously targeting more than one mechanism of action may provide an additive or synergistic benefit relative to monotherapy [8,9]. Although not powered to compare efficacy between treatments, a study investigating the combination of the monoclonal antibodies infliximab and natalizumab suggested that this regimen was more effective than infliximab monotherapy [10]. More recently, the phase 2a VEGA trial demonstrated that combination induction therapy with guselkumab and golimumab was more effective than monotherapy in patients with active ulcerative colitis [11].

The recent phase 4, single-arm, open-label EXPLORER study assessed triple combination therapy with vedolizumab, adalimumab, and methotrexate among 55 patients with biologic-naïve, newly diagnosed CD. Of these patients, 29 % had concomitant corticosteroid use at baseline. At week 26, 34.5 % and 54.5 % were in endoscopic and clinical remission, respectively, and no new safety signals were identified, further supporting the evaluation of combination therapy in patients with CD [12].

Vedolizumab and upadacitinib are approved treatments for moderate to severe CD that have different mechanisms of action. Vedolizumab is a monoclonal antibody that binds to the human lymphocyte integrin α4β7, thus impairing the migration of gut-homing lymphocytes to the gastrointestinal mucosa [13,14]. Upadacitinib is a selective and reversible Janus kinase (JAK) 1 inhibitor that modulates the signaling of JAK-dependent cytokines, one of the inflammatory signaling pathways relevant to the pathogenesis of IBD [15]. Upadacitinib has a rapid onset of action, making it an attractive induction agent, whereas vedolizumab has a well-established efficacy and safety profile, as well as a durable maintenance effect [15,16]. Additionally, in silico models suggest that their combination could modulate a large number of signaling pathways that are relevant to the pathophysiology of CD [17].

VICTRIVA, a randomized, double-blind, phase 3b trial (NCT06227910) in patients with moderately to severely active CD, aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vedolizumab plus upadacitinib combination therapy for induction compared with vedolizumab monotherapy, and also the efficacy and safety of vedolizumab maintenance therapy following induction.

Methods

Study design

The VICTRIVA study design is shown in Fig. 1. Patients are randomized 1:1 to receive induction treatment with either intravenous (IV) vedolizumab (300 mg; weeks 0, 2, 6, and 10), plus oral upadacitinib (45 mg daily) or IV vedolizumab (300 mg; weeks 0, 2, 6, and 10) plus an oral placebo (daily) for 12 weeks. Patients who achieve a reduction in CD activity index (CDAI) score of ≥70 points at week 12 will enter the main study maintenance phase, and will receive IV vedolizumab maintenance therapy (300 mg; every 8 weeks [Q8W]) from week 14 through week 52. The maintenance phase will assess the long-term sustainability of the efficacy of vedolizumab monotherapy in maintaining a treatment response in participants who have responded to DTT compared to participants who received induction treatment with vedolizumab only. Patients who do not achieve a reduction in CDAI score ≥70 points will be deemed as “failure to meet the continuation criterion” and will be discontinued from treatment and enter the safety follow-up period.

During the main study maintenance phase (week 14 to week 40), patients who lose response to vedolizumab IV may be escalated from vedolizumab Q8W to every 4 weeks (Q4W), if all the following criteria are met: CDAI score ≥70 points above either the week 12 CDAI score or the CDAI score at the last visit; CDAI score ≥150; and either C-reactive protein (CRP) ≥5 mg/L or fecal calprotectin ≥250 μg/g. In patients who respond to Q4W dosing, the dose frequency may be reduced back to Q8W at the investigator's discretion.

Patients in the maintenance phase who lose response to vedolizumab IV over at least two consecutive visits despite escalation to Q4W dosing may enter the rescue substudy, in which they will receive 12 weeks of rescue treatment with oral upadacitinib (45 mg daily) plus IV vedolizumab (300 mg; Q4W). Rescue treatment will be considered if patients meet all of the following criteria: after dose escalation to Q4W there is a 70-point reduction in CDAI score that determined dose escalation to Q4W over at least two consecutive visits or if the patient loses response after an initial response to Q4W dosing, CDAI score >220, and either CRP ≥5 mg/L or fecal calprotectin ≥250 μg/g. Patients who partially meet the criteria may be allowed to enter the rescue substudy on a case-specific basis after discussion with the medical monitor. Patients may qualify for the rescue substudy at the time of initial loss of response or after multiple dose escalations to Q4W (whether consecutive or non-consecutive) if the substudy entrance criteria are met. Patients who do not initially meet the criteria for the rescue substudy after at least two consecutive dose escalations to Q4W but subsequently meet the criteria (lose response) can still enter the rescue substudy. The latest time at which patients may enter the rescue substudy is week 40 (±5 days). Participants who lose response after week 40 will be discontinued from treatment and week 52/end of treatment assessments will be completed, and participants will enter the 18-week safety follow-up. After 12 weeks of rescue treatment, if patients achieve a CDAI score reduction of ≥70 points from the rescue baseline, they will continue to receive vedolizumab monotherapy through week 52. If patients do not meet these criteria, they will discontinue treatment and enter the 18-week safety follow-up.

The doses and regimens of vedolizumab and upadacitinib are consistent with the respective EU summary of product characteristics and US prescribing information, and are expected to provide optimal efficacy with an acceptable safety profile [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. The planned duration of treatment for all patients is 52 weeks, although patients will be able to continue vedolizumab treatment after week 52. An 18-week safety follow-up period will be conducted after the last study vedolizumab infusion, regardless of further treatment regimens.

Patient eligibility criteria

A full list of the inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in Table 1. Eligible adults (aged 18–65 years) will have a diagnosis of CD established ≥3 months before screening by clinical and endoscopic evidence corroborated by a histopathology report, have moderate to severe CD (defined as a CDAI score of 220–450), have evidence of mucosal inflammation based on the Simple Endoscopic Score for CD (SES-CD; score of ≥6 or ≥4 for patients with isolated ileal disease), and have had an inadequate response, loss of response, or intolerance to corticosteroids, immunomodulators, or biological therapy.

Patients are ineligible if they have a current diagnosis of ulcerative colitis or IBD unclassified or if they have previously experienced treatment failure with vedolizumab or upadacitinib. Assessments

Key assessments include the CDAI [22], which will be collected at screening and weeks 2, 6, 12, 22, 30, 38, 46, and 52, and the SES-CD [23], which will be collected at screening and weeks 12 and 52, scored by a central reviewer. Patient-reported outcome 2 (PRO2), the unweighted stool frequency, and abdominal pain score values over the past seven available diary days before each visit will also be assessed at baseline and weeks 2, 6, 12, 22, 38, and 52. Serum and stool samples will also be collected to evaluate CRP and fecal calprotectin levels, respectively.

Study endpoints

Co-primary endpoints in the VICTRIVA trial are CDAI-defined clinical remission at week 12 (CDAI score <150 points) and endoscopic response at week 12, assessed as the proportion of participants achieving decrease in SES-CD of >50 % from baseline (or for participants with isolated ileal disease, SES-CD of ≤4 or ≤2-point reduction from baseline) read centrally.

A key secondary endpoint at week 12 is PRO2-defined clinical remission, defined as a 7-day average of very soft or liquid stool frequency ≤2.8, 7-day average of abdominal pain score ≤1.0, and neither worse than baseline. Other secondary endpoints at week 12 include endoscopic remission, defined as a SES-CD of ≤4 with no mucosal ulceration in the colon or ileum as assessed by centrally read video ileocolonoscopy, corticosteroid-free clinical remission in patients who were taking corticosteroids at baseline, and CDAI-defined clinical response over time, defined as a ≥100-point decrease in CDAI from baseline.

Key secondary endpoints at week 52 include CDAI-defined clinical remission (CDAI score <150), SES-CD–defined endoscopic response, and PRO2-defined clinical remission. Other secondary endpoints at week 52 are the same as those listed for week 12.

Safety will be assessed by the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events, adverse events of special interest, and changes in vital signs, clinical laboratory parameters, and electrocardiograms. An adverse event is any untoward medical occurrence temporally associated with the use of investigational product, whether or not the occurrence is considered related to the investigational product. Adverse events of special interest include serious infections, malignancies, GI perforations, embolic and thrombotic events, anemia, neutropenia, lymphopenia, hyperlipidemia, hepatic injury, and PML.

Randomization and blinding

Eligible patients will be centrally assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either IV vedolizumab plus oral upadacitinib (group 1) or IV vedolizumab plus an oral placebo (group 2) for 12 weeks, using an interactive response technology system. Randomization will be stratified by prior biologic experience (yes/no), baseline corticosteroid use (yes/no), and baseline CDAI score (220–330 or 331–450). Patients, investigators, site staff, and outcome assessors will be blinded to study treatment throughout the study.

Patient engagement

Patient engagement is an important aspect of the trial design, to identify factors that either negatively or positively influence participation [24]. Therefore, during the protocol development, 75-min double-blind interviews of patients with CD were conducted to gain qualitative insights about patients’ perception, hesitation, motivation, and other factors influencing their willingness to participate in the study, with a particular emphasis on optimizing the study design. The interviewed participants were members of Inspire, a global online patient community forum.

Study conduct

All patients will sign written informed consent forms prior to initiation of any study procedures. The study will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Council for Harmonization of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and all applicable laws and regulations.

An independent data safety monitoring board will review safety and other relevant data and monitor the general safety of patients who participate in the study. The data safety monitoring board may make recommendations on whether the study should be suspended or terminated based on the review of the data, including the interim analysis data. An independent progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy adjudication committee will be instituted for this study, consisting of a panel of leading progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy experts, including a neurologist, neuroradiologist, and virologist.

Benefit-risk assessment

As described, simultaneously targeting multiple pathogenic pathways with combination therapy/DTT may provide an additive or synergistic benefit compared to monotherapy alone. The specific combination of vedolizumab and upadacitinib has not been previously evaluated, however, given the differing treatment targets and durations of action, their combination has potential to induce rapid and sustained clinical remission in patients with moderately to severely active CD.

The safety profiles of both vedolizumab and upadacitinib have been well characterized. In phase 3 studies, the most commonly reported SAEs with vedolizumab are related to exacerbations or complications of underlying CD, followed by infections. Rare cases of PML have been reported in severely immunosuppressed patients taking vedolizumab. The most commonly reported adverse reactions with upadacitinib in phase 3 clinical studies were upper respiratory tract infection, pyrexia, blood creatine phosphokinase increased, anemia, headache, acne, neutropenia, rash, herpes zoster, pneumonia, hypercholesterolemia, bronchitis, aspartate transaminase increased, fatigue, alanine transaminase increased, folliculitis, herpes simplex, and influenza. The most common serious adverse reactions were serious infections. When comparing nonclinical data for vedolizumab and upadacitinib, there is no apparent overlap in the primary and secondary pharmacology, and no identified risk for potential pharmacokinetic interaction with their coadministration. Additionally, there is no evidence to suggest a potential for additive or synergistic toxicity with coadministration of vedolizumab and upadacitinib, except for an increased risk of infection.

To ensure the safety of study participants, risk mitigation measures will be implemented. Participants will be strongly encouraged to have an up-to-date vaccination status in accordance with local guidelines prior to enrollment. Participants with contraindications for vedolizumab or upadacitinib, including severe or serious infections, leukopenia/lymphopenia or severe liver disfunction, will be excluded. Additionally, patients will be excluded if they have an active infection or severe infection or active or latent tuberculosis, as will patients using concomitant medications that can interact with or increase the associated risks of vedolizumab and upadacitinib. AEs and SAEs will be continuously monitored from screening until 18 weeks after administration of the final dose, which is approximately 5 times the half-life of vedolizumab (which has a longer half-life than upadacitinib). An independent data monitoring committee will conduct periodic and ad hoc reviews of the data to continue the benefit-risk assessment for participants.

Power calculation

The sample size was calculated based upon the assumed clinical remission rates and endoscopic response rates at week 12 in both treatment groups. Both the clinical remission and endoscopic remission rates at week 12 are estimated to be 40 % in the vedolizumab monotherapy arm and 55 % in the vedolizumab plus upadacitinib arm. These assumed rates result in an estimated treatment difference of 15 percentage points for each of the two co-primary endpoints. It was assumed that remission rates and treatment differences would be consistent across the randomization strata (prior biologic experience, baseline corticosteroid use, and CD severity). Using a Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test, stratified by the randomization factors, and two-sided alpha of 0.05, a total of 396 evaluable participants (198 per group) will be required to detect a difference in response rates of 15 percentage points between treatment groups with 85 % power.

Statistical analysis

The efficacy analyses will be conducted on the full analysis set, which will include all patients who are randomized and receive at least one dose of study medication; patients will be analyzed in the treatment group to which they are randomized. The safety analyses will also be conducted on all randomized patients who receive at least one dose of study medication, but patients will be analyzed according to the actual study medication that they receive. All participants who transition into the substudy (rescue) will form the substudy rescue analysis set, which will form the basis for all the analyses of the substudy (rescue) endpoints. Participants will be analyzed according to the treatment groups to which they are randomized in the induction phase and overall.

The statistical testing for primary and key secondary endpoints will be conducted by testing ordered hypothesis families (primary family: clinical remission at week 12, endoscopic response at week 12; key secondary family 1: clinical remission at week 52, endoscopic response at week 52; key secondary family 2: PRO2-defined clinical remission at week 12, PRO2-defined clinical remission at week 52). Each hypothesis family will be tested at a significance level of 0.05 using a hierarchical, step-down testing procedure to control type I error. Within each hypothesis family, both endpoints are considered co-endpoints and each hypothesis will be tested at a significance of level of 0.05. The hierarchical testing procedure will be continued until a nonsignificant result appears for at least one comparison within a hypothesis family. Thus, the second and third hypothesis families will only be tested if both null hypotheses in the previous family/families have been rejected in favor of DTT over vedolizumab monotherapy. If an endpoint fails to show superiority of DTT over monotherapy, all subsequent families and endpoints will have nominal p-values presented. For other secondary efficacy endpoints, nominal p-values and 95 % CIs will be reported with no adjustment for multiplicity.

Patient enrollment

The VICTRIVA investigators plan to enroll 396 participants (198 in each treatment arm) from approximately 120 sites in regions including, but not limited to, North America and Europe. It is also planned that a minimum of 50 patients (and maximum of 50 %) will be biologic naïve (i.e., patients who have not been previously exposed to biologics). However, biologic-naïve patients will not be recruited from sites in the United States. Patient enrollment is due to start in January 2025.

Patient engagement

Qualitative interviews were conducted with six patients with CD between July 17 and July 25, 2023, to help inform the VICTRIVA protocol design. Patients’ ages ranged from 21 to 59 years, four patients resided in the United States and two in the United Kingdom, and three had active CD and three were in remission.

The interviews showed that all the patients were willing to consider participating in the trial, especially those with active CD. Additionally, patients were willing to complete all clinical procedures; however, colonoscopies could pose a barrier for some patients due to the perceived invasiveness and required preparation. Patients also reacted positively to the overall trial design, especially the optional substudy as an alternative to dropping out. The primary and secondary endpoints were viewed positively, specifically those based on improving patients’ quality of life, and patient-reported outcomes were considered valuable. Additionally, the mobile phone application and the schedule to complete patient-reported outcome measures were perceived positively. Patients appreciated the list of permitted medications (Supplementary Appendix) for existing conditions and CD rescue, especially if they thought that a flare could occur.

Recommendations based on the results of the patient engagement interviews included: emphasizing that the substudy was to offer patients an alternative to dropping out if they do not see an effect in various trial phases; discussing the safety of vedolizumab and upadacitinib individually to ease patient concerns about DTT; and clarifying the trial terminology by providing definitions and explanations for terminology used in a study schematic.

Conclusions

The VICTRIVA trial will evaluate the efficacy and safety of induction therapy with vedolizumab plus upadacitinib vs. vedolizumab alone, as well as the efficacy of vedolizumab maintenance therapy following this induction in patients with moderately to severely active CD. The combination of vedolizumab and upadacitinib that will be examined in the VICTRIVA trial is designed to break the current therapeutic ceiling for inducing clinical remission in patients with CD and to improve long-term outcomes.

Although rarely conducted in trial design for IBD, proactive engagement of patients early in the development of the VICTRIVA protocol and study materials allowed us to gain insights into barriers to enrollment, identify opportunities to improve the study design, and optimize patient recruitment.

Although combination therapy for IBD is a growing field of interest, data on the efficacy and safety of these combinations are scarce, particularly among patients with CD [25]. VICTRIVA will contribute to the evidence base by collecting a wide range of data including clinical response, clinical remission, corticosteroid-free remission, extraintestinal manifestation response, biomarker response, patient-reported outcomes, adverse events, serious adverse events, the need for surgery, and emerging infections. This comprehensive dataset will enable detailed and granular group analyses to establish factors that influence efficacy outcomes of interest and to establish an initial safety profile of the combination of vedolizumab and upadacitinib in patients with CD.

Findings from VICTRIVA will also contribute to the determination of the optimal strategies for the use of combination induction therapy in patients with CD, which is of importance to patients seeking the most effective treatments. Findings will also be of interest to payers, as the choice of therapy might greatly influence the cost of a patient's therapy. While DTT with vedolizumab and upadacitinib may incur higher costs in the short term, their combination may result in better long-term outcomes and lower overall costs. It might also be that for some patients, a second advanced therapy in combination should be used as a “bridge” therapy, with future de-escalation to a single advanced therapy, whereas others require long-term maintenance combination therapy utilizing inhibition of multiple inflammatory pathways, which is not yet understood. Findings from VICTRIVA will help begin to untangle such unknowns and will further the understanding of advanced combination therapy in patients with CD.

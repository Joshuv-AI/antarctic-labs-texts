H-Instanton Bundles on Three-Dimensional Smooth Toric Varieties with Picard Number Two
Ozhan Genc, Francesco Malaspina

Abstract
We study H H -instanton bundles on the infinite family of smooth three-dimensional varieties X e = ℙ ⁡ ( 𝒪 ℙ 2 ⊕ 𝒪 ℙ 2 ​ ( e ) ) X_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(e)) , for e ≥ 0 e\geq 0 . We provide two distinct monadic descriptions of H H -instanton bundles on X e X_{e} , generalizing the classical monads on ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} . We then characterize H H -instanton bundles with second Chern class supported in a single degree, and investigate their existence and moduli spaces. Finally, for e ≤ 3 e\leq 3 , we prove the existence of H H -instanton bundles for all admissible second Chern classes. These results extend previous constructions on specific cases and contribute to the study of instanton bundles on threefolds with higher Picard number.

Ulrich bundles.

Introduction

Mathematical instanton bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
arose as the algebraic counterpart to the
(anti)self-dual connections which are solutions to the Yang-Mills equations on
S^{4}

. The
link between these two apparently distant objects was achieved through twistor theory,
as it was developed by R. Penrose. Indeed
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is the twistor space of
S^{4}
. Prompted by
this fact, mathematical instanton bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
have been the object of study for the
last three decades, becoming one of the milestones in algebraic geometry. An instanton
bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is a stable rank two bundle
E
with
c_{1}(E)=0
and satisfying the so called
instantonic condition

h^{1}(\mathbb{P}^{3},E(-2))=0.

In
[
15
]
(see also [28] in the case of index two) is given the following definition of instanton bundle on Fano
threefold X with Picard number one: let us write the canonical divisor of
(X,H_{X})
as

\omega_{X}=-(2qX+r_{X})H_{X}
where
q_{X}\geq 0
and
0\leq r_{X}\leq 1
, then an instanton bundle E on X
is a
\mu
-stable rank two vector bundle with
c_{1}(E)=-r_{X}
(hence normalized) and

H^{1}(X,E(-q_{X}H_{X}))=0.

So
G=E(-q_{X}H_{X})
satisfies
G\cong G^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X}
and by Serre duality
H^{1}(X,G)=H^{2}(X,G)=0
.
Apart from this definition there have been several attempts to generalize instanton
bundles to Fano varieties of higher Picard number. In
[
27
]
the authors gave the following definition of instanton bundle over the Flag variety
F(0,1,2)
:
a rank two vector bundle
E
on the Fano threefold
F(0,1,2)
is an instanton bundle of charge
k
if the following properties hold:

•

c_{1}(E)=0,\ c_{2}(E)=kh_{1}h_{2}
;

•

h^{0}(E)=0
and
E
is
\mu
-semistable;

•

h^{1}(X,E(-H_{X}))=0
;

This definition has been adapted in
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
to other examples of Fano threefolds with Picard number higher than one. One can observe that in all the cases, by
increasing the degree of the second Chern class, the intermediate cohomology modules
have more generators and become more complicated. Recall that a coherent sheaf E is
aCM (arithmetically Cohen-Macauly) if all the intermediate cohomology groups vanish
or equivalently if the module E of global sections of E is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
module. So, as the charge of instanton bundles grows, instanton bundles become further
from being aCM. Among aCM sheaves, a special role is played by Ulrich sheaves. These
shaves are characterized by the linearity of the minimal graded free resolution over the
polynomial ring of their module of global sections. Ulrich sheaves, originally studied for
computing Chow forms, conjecturally exist over any variety (we refer to
[
14
]
).

In
[
2
]
a notion of instanton bundles related to the previous ones is introduced on
any polarized projective threefold
(X,H_{X})
. Let
K_{X}
be the
canonical divisor,
A rank two vector bundle
E
on
X
is an
H
-instanton bundle
of charge
k
if the following properties hold:

•

c_{1}(E)=2H_{X}+K_{X}
and
\deg(c_{2}(E))=k
;

•

h^{0}(X,E)=0
and
E
is
\mu
-semistable;

•

h^{1}(X,E(-H_{X}))=0
;

In
[
2
]
has been studied, H-instanton
bundles on three-dimensional rational normal scrolls since this is a family of threefolds with infinite
different canonical divisors
K_{X}
and with a nice description of the derived category of
coherent sheaves.

In this paper we deal with the infinite family
X_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(e))
, namely the remaining three-dimesional smooth toric varieties with Picard number two (cfr
[
21
]
). The case of
e=0
has already been studied in
[
2
]
(see also
[
12
]
and
[
17
]
), the case of
e=1
in
[
10
]
and the case of
e=2
in
[
17
]
. As first results, in Section 3 we give two different monadic descriptions
of each H-instanton bundle (which can be considered as the analog of the monads on

\mathbb{P}^{3}
and we characterize the H-instantons
E
such that
c_{2}(E)=\beta f^{2}
(i.e. the second
Chern class is concentrated in a single degree) showing that they are always realized as cohomology of a pullback of a linear monad.
After this, in Section 4 we deal with the existence of H-instanton bundles with second Chern classes
\alpha\xi f
and the
description of their moduli spaces. Finally, in Section 5, we show the existence of H-instanton bundles for any admissible second Chern classes when
e\leq 3
.

H-instanton bundles on
X_{e}
for
e\leq 3

Let
L
be a curve in the class
f^{2}
within
A^{2}(X_{e})
. Since
L
is a complete intersection, its structure sheaf has the following resolution:

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-2f)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-f)^{\oplus 2}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}\rightarrow 0.

(28)

In particular, we have
\mathcal{N}_{L|X_{e}}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus 2}
, so the determinant is
\det(\mathcal{N}_{L|X_{e}})=\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Moreover, the intersection number satisfies
f^{2}\cdot(\xi+f)=1
, and we compute

\chi(\mathcal{O}_{L})=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}})-2\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-f))+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-2f))=1,

since
\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-f))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-2f))=0
by Lemma
2.1
. Therefore,
L
is a line in
X_{e}
.

From now on, we denote by
\Lambda_{L}
the component of the Hilbert scheme
{\rm Hilb}^{t+1}(X_{e})
containing these complete intersection curves. Since

h^{0}(\mathcal{N}_{L|X_{e}})=2\quad\text{and}\quad h^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{L|X_{e}})=0,

we conclude that
\Lambda_{L}
is smooth and has dimension
2
. In fact,
f^{2}
is a pull-back of a point in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
via projectivization map; that is,
\Lambda_{L}\simeq(\mathbb{P}^{2})^{\vee}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a bundle constructed in Theorem
4.1
. Then the restriction of
\mathcal{E}
to a generic line
L\in|f^{2}|
splits as
\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus 2}
.

Proof.

We know that
\mathcal{E}
fits into the short exact sequence in display (
22
). If we restrict
\mathcal{E}
to a generic line
L
that can be chosen as not intersecting
Z
, then we will have

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)\longrightarrow 0.

(29)

Since
\mathcal{E}|_{L}
split, we have
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{L}(s)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(-s)
for some
s
. The cohomology of the sequence (
29
) yields
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L})=2
. So,
s=0
or
s=1
. But, we obtain the trivial extension in the case of
s=1
. So,
s=0
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an
\mu
-stable
H
-instanton bundle on
X_{e}
with
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=\alpha\xi f+\beta f^{2}
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{Ext}^{3}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=0
when
e\leq 3
. Let
L
be a generic line of class
|f^{2}|
such that
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}=\mathcal{O}_{L}^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
be the kernel sheaf of the general map
\varphi:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Then

1.

c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=0
,
c_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=\alpha\xi f+(\beta+1)f^{2}
and
c_{3}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=0
.

2.

\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
is
\mu
-stable.

3.

\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{h}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}(-H))=0
.

4.

\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L^{{}^{\prime}}}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{{}^{\prime}}}^{2}
for a generic
L^{{}^{\prime}}\in|f^{2}|
.

5.

\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})+4
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=\mathrm{Ext}^{3}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=0
.

Proof.

We have the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}\rightarrow 0.

(30)

Since
c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{L})=0
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{L})=-f^{2}
, we have
c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=0
and
c_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=\alpha\xi f+(\beta+1)f^{2}
thanks to the sequence (
30
). Also,
c_{3}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=0
by a straightforward computation with Chern polynomials.

Notice that each sheaf destabilizing
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
also destabilize
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
and
\mathcal{E}
have same slope. So,
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
is also
\mu
-stable.

The cohomology of the sequence (
30
) gives us
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})\leq\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{E})=0
; i.e,
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=0
. Similarly,
\mathrm{h}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}(-H))\leq\mathrm{h}^{1}(\mathcal{E}(-H))+\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-H))
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is an
H
-instanton and
\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(-H))\simeq\mathrm{h}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1))=0
, we have
\mathrm{h}^{1}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}(-H))=0
.

Since a generic element of
|f^{2}|
is a pull-back of a point in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
via projectivization map, we can choose two generic element of
|f^{2}|
disjoint from each other. So, the restriction of sequence (
30
) to each general
L^{{}^{\prime}}\in|f^{2}|
remains exact; that is,
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L^{{}^{\prime}}}\simeq\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L^{{}^{\prime}}}=\mathcal{O}_{L^{{}^{\prime}}}^{2}
.

Now, let us apply
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E},-\big)
to Sequence (
30
). Then, we obtain that
\mathrm{Ext}^{j}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)=0
for
j=2,3
, because
\mathrm{Ext}^{j}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=0
for
j=2,3
and
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{L}\big)\cong H^{i}\big(L,\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus 2}\big)=0
,
i\geq 1
. Thus, by applying
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}\big(-,\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)
to Sequence (
30
) we obtain

\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)\subseteq\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)\text{ and }\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big).

Since
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)=0
, we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)=0
. On the other hand,
Serre duality implies
\mathrm{Ext}^{3}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{O}_{L}\otimes K_{X_{e}}\big)^{\vee}
. Since
f^{2}\cdot K_{X_{e}}=-2
, we have
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{O}_{L}\otimes K_{X_{e}}\big)=\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)
. Since
L
is a complete intersection subscheme in
X_{e}
, we have the isomorphism
\mathcal{E}xt^{i}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{O}_{L}\big)\simeq\mathcal{E}xt^{i-1}_{X_{e}}\big(I_{L},\mathcal{O}_{L}\big)=\wedge^{i}\mathcal{N}_{L|X_{e}}
. From
\mathcal{N}_{L|X_{e}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
we deduce
\mathcal{E}xt^{1}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)\cong\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)^{\oplus 2}
. By the above equalities, applying
\mathcal{H}om_{X_{e}}\big(-,\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)
to Sequence (
30
) we obtain the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)^{\oplus 2}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}om_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)^{\oplus 2}\longrightarrow 0,

because
\mathcal{E}xt^{1}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)=0
. It follows that

\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)=H^{0}\big(X_{e},\mathcal{H}om_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{O}_{L}(-2)\big)\big)=0,

hence
\mathrm{Ext}^{2}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)=0
. Lastly,
\mathrm{Ext}^{0}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)=1
thanks to the
\mu
-stability of
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
, which implies the simpleness of
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
. It follows that

\dim\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}\big)=1-\chi(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}).

By applying
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},-\big)
,
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}(-,\mathcal{E}\big)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Hom}}\nolimits_{X_{e}}(-,\mathcal{O}_{L}\big)
to Sequence (

\chi(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})=\chi(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})-\chi(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{O}_{L})-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E})+\chi(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{O}_{L}).

Also we know that

We have

.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

For each
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\alpha>e
and
\beta\geq 0
, there exists an earnest
\mu
-stable
H
-instanton bundle
\mathcal{E}
with charge
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})=\alpha\xi f+\beta f^{2}
on
X_{e}
where
e\leq 3
such that

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=(2e+6)\alpha+4\beta-(e-1)^{2}-3,\ \ \ \operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=\operatorname{Ext}^{3}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=0.

Proof.

We proceed by induction to establish the existence of
H
-instanton bundles on
X_{e}
with charge
\alpha\xi f+\beta f^{2}
under suitable assumptions on
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}
. The initial step of the induction, corresponding to
\beta=0
has already been treated in Theorem
4.1
, while the associated
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}
-group calculations appear in Theorem
4.2
.

Assume therefore that there exists an earnest
\mu
–stable
H
-instanton bundle
\mathcal{E}
with charge
\alpha\xi f+\beta f^{2}
, where
\alpha\geq e
and
\beta\geq 0
, satisfying

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=(2e+6)\alpha+4\beta-(e-1)^{2}-3,\qquad\operatorname{Ext}^{2}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=\operatorname{Ext}^{3}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=0.

Consequently,
\mathcal{E}
determines a point of a component
\mathcal{M}
of the moduli space of
\mu
–stable rank
2
sheaves with Chern classes
c_{i}(\mathcal{E})
. This component is smooth at
\mathcal{E}
and has dimension

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=(2e+6)\alpha+4\beta-(e-1)^{2}-3.

In Proposition
5.2
, we constructed a sheaf
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
that satisfies all the defining properties of an
H
-instanton bundle except locally freeness. This sheaf corresponds to a point in a component
\mathcal{M}_{1}
of the moduli space of
\mu
–stable rank
2
sheaves with Chern classes
c_{i}(\mathcal{E}_{\varphi})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is smooth at
\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
with dimension

\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E})=(2e+6)\alpha+4\beta-(e-1)^{2}+1.

The subset
\mathcal{M}_{bad}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{1}
consisting of such sheaves is parametrized by the choice of
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{M}
, by a line
L\in\Lambda_{L}
and by a point of
\mathbb{P}\big(\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\big(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\big)\big)
corresponding to the morphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{O}_{L}\big)

\dim(\mathcal{M}_{bad})\leq(2e+6)\alpha+4\beta-(e-1)^{2}<(2e+6)\alpha+4\beta-(e-1)^{2}+1=\dim(\mathcal{M}_{1}).

Therefore, there exists a flat family
\mathfrak{E}\to S
over an integral base such that
\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}
and
\mathfrak{E}_{s}\not\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
for
s\neq s_{0}
. By
[
6

, we may further assume that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathfrak{E}_{s},\mathfrak{E}_{s}\big)=0
for
s\in S
.

For each
s\in S
, there is a canonical exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{E}_{s}\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{E}_{s})^{\vee\vee}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{T}_{s}\longrightarrow 0.

(31)

where
\mathfrak{T}_{s}
is a torsion sheaf on
X_{e}
. Since
(\mathfrak{E}_{s})^{\vee\vee}
is reflexive, the locus where it fails to be locally free has degree
c_{3}(\mathfrak{E}_{s})\geq 0
, by
[
19
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Because
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
is torsion–free, the support
\Gamma_{s}
of
\mathfrak{T}_{s}
has codimension at least
2
(see
[
28
, Corollary to Theorem II.1.1.8]
). By semicontinuity we may assume that for general
s\in S
, the sheaf
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
is a
\mu
–stable, satisfies
h^{0}\big(X_{e},\mathfrak{E}_{s}\big)=h^{1}\big(X_{e},\mathfrak{E}_{s}(-H)\big)=0
, and restricts as
\mathfrak{E}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{L}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}^{\oplus 2}
for a general
L\in\Lambda_{L}
. It remains to verify that
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
is locally free.

Suppose that
h^{0}\big(X_{e},(\mathfrak{E}_{s})^{\vee\vee}(-H)\big)\neq 0

\mathcal{L}\subseteq(\mathfrak{E}_{s})^{\vee\vee}

\mu(\mathcal{L})=H^{3}>0

, as

. From the cohomology of sequence (

(32)

\Gamma_{s}=\emptyset

is reflexive and since

\operatorname{Ext}^{2}_{X_{e}}\big(\mathfrak{E}_{s},\mathfrak{E}_{s}\big)=0

In the second case,
\Gamma_{s}\neq\emptyset

0

\displaystyle h^{0}\big(\Gamma_{s}\cap H^{\prime},\mathfrak{T}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{H^{\prime}}\big)

\displaystyle=h^{1}\big(H^{\prime},\mathfrak{E}_{s}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{H^{\prime}}(\lambda H)\big)\leq

\mathfrak{T}_{s}

We now compute the class of
\Gamma_{s}
in

. For any line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X_{e}}(D)\in\mathop{\mathrm{Pic}}\nolimits(X_{e})
,

and

are independent of

, by

. A direct Chern class computation from Sequence (
31
) then gives

Then Equality (
8
) for the sheaf
\mathfrak{T}(D)

hence

,

and

\displaystyle(1+a)(e\eta_{s}+\vartheta_{s})+(1+b)\vartheta_{s}

\displaystyle=h^{1}\big(\Gamma_{s},\mathfrak{T}_{s}(D)\big)=

(33)

\displaystyle=h^{2}\big(X_{e},\mathfrak{E}_{s}(D)\big)\leq h^{2}\big(X_{e},\mathcal{E}_{\varphi}(D)\big),

36
) to
F\times\{\ s_{0}\ \}\subseteq F\times S
yields a non–split sequence, which must coincide with Sequence (
30
) by Remark
5.3
. Hence
(\mathfrak{E}_{s_{0}})^{\vee\vee}\cong\mathcal{E}
, so
(\mathfrak{E}_{s})^{\vee\vee}\in\mathcal{M}
and therefore
\mathfrak{E}_{s}\in\mathcal{M}_{bad}
for all
s\in S
, contradicting our initial assumption. We conclude that the case
\Gamma_{s}\neq\emptyset
for all
s\in S
cannot occur.

Lastly, the property of being earnest follows from semicontinuity and Theorem
3.4
.

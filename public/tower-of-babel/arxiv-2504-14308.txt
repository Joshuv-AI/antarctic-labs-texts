The Schur complements for S ​ D ​ D 1 SDD_{1} matrices and their application to linear complementarity problems
Yang Hu, Jianzhou Liu, Wenlong Zeng

Abstract
In this paper we propose a new scaling method to study the Schur complements of S ​ D ​ D 1 SDD_{1} matrices. Its core is related to the non-negative property of the inverse M M -matrix, while numerically improving the Quotient formula. Based on the Schur complement and a novel norm splitting manner, we establish an upper bound for the infinity norm of the inverse of S ​ D ​ D 1 SDD_{1} matrices, which depends solely on the original matrix entries. We apply the new bound to derive an error bound for linear complementarity problems of B 1 B_{1} -matrices. Additionally, new lower and upper bounds for the determinant of S ​ D ​ D 1 SDD_{1} matrices are presented. Numerical experiments validate the effectiveness and superiority of our results.

Introduction

The linear complementarity problem, denoted by LCP(
M,q
), consists of finding a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle x\geq 0,Mx+q\geq 0,(Mx+q)^{T}x=0,

or proving the nonexistence of such a solution. Here,
M=(m_{ij})\in{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}
and
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The LCP(
M,q
) has various applications, such as the problems of finding a Nash equilibrium point of bimatrix game, linear and quadratic programming or some free boundary problems of fluid mechanics and optimal stopping in Markov chains
[
2
,
18
,
4
]
.

The LCP(
M,q
) has unique solution for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
if and only if
M
is a
P
-matrix
[
4
]
. A matrix
M\in{\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}

is called a
P
-matrix if all its principal minors are positive. Furthermore,
x^{*}
solves the LCP(
M,q
) if and only if
x^{*}
solves

\displaystyle r(x):={\rm min}\{x,Mx+q\}=0,

where the min operator
r(x)
denotes the componentwise minimum of two vectors. When the involved matrix is a
P
-matrix, Chen and Xiang gave the following error bound for the LCP(
M,q
)
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\left\|x-x^{*}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\underset{\rm d\in[0,1]^{n}}{\rm max}\left\|(I-D+DM)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|r(x)\right\|_{\infty},

where
x^{*}
is the solution of the LCP(
M,q
),
D=diag\{d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{n}\}
over
0\leq d_{i}\leq 1
for
i\in N
. Therefore, one of the important problems regarding the LCP(
M,q
) is to estimate :

\displaystyle\underset{\rm d\in[0,1]^{n}}{\rm{max}}\left\|(I-D+DM)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}.

(1)

Since it plays a significant role in gauging the error bound. This involves finding an upper bound for
\left\|M^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}
.
Since any principal submatrix of a
P
-matrix is nonsingular, based on this property, we can partition matrix
M
into

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&B\\
C&D\\
\end{array}\right),

where
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}(0<m<n)
. Denote
M/A=D-CA^{-1}B
, we have

\displaystyle XMY=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A&O\\
O&M/A\\
\end{array}\right),

where

\displaystyle X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{m}&O\\
-CA^{-1}&I_{n-m}\\
\end{array}\right)\quad{\text{and}}\quad Y=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{m}&-A^{-1}B\\
O&I_{n-m}\\
\end{array}\right).

Furthermore, we obtain that

\displaystyle M^{-1}=Y\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{-1}&O\\
O&(M/A)^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)X.

By the fundamental properties of the infinity norm, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|M^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|Y\right\|_{\infty}{\rm max}\{\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|(M/A)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\}\left\|X\right\|_{\infty}.

(2)

This demonstrates that (
2
) transforms the upper bound estimation of
\left\|M^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}
into four small-scale matrix upper bounds. Building on this framework, scholars have obtained upper bounds of
\left\|M^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}
for certain subclasses of
P
-matrices
[
11
,
17
,
26
]
. However, the estimation provided by (
2
) exhibits significant inaccuracy, particularly when
\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}
and
\left\|X\right\|_{\infty}
are large. In this paper, we establish a strictly sharper upper bound for
\left\|M^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}
that demonstrably outperforms (
2

\displaystyle\left\|M^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|Y\right\|_{\infty}{\rm max}\{\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|(M/A)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|X\right\|_{\infty}\}.

(3)

In this paper, we propose a new scaling method called prior construction and apply it to the study of the Schur complement for

4

The condition number

is commonly employed to assess whether a linear system

is ill-conditioned or well-behaved, which involves calculating
\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}

\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}
is used to measure the magnitude of the error bound. In this section, we will give an infinity norm upper bound of the inverse of the

Lemma 4.1
.

[

.

[

be an SDD matrix, and

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}B\right\|_{\infty}\leq\underset{i\in N}{\rm max}\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m}|b_{ij}|}{|a_{ii}|-R_{i}(A)}.

.

[
1
]

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}(n\geq 2)
be an
SDD_{1}

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{{\rm max}\{1,\underset{i\in N_{2}}{\rm max}\frac{p_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}+\epsilon\}}{{\rm min}\{\underset{i\in N_{1}}{\rm min}H_{i},\underset{i\in N_{2}}{\rm min}Q_{i}\}},

where

\displaystyle H_{i}=|a_{ii}|-R^{N_{1}}_{i}(A)-\underset{j\in N_{2}\backslash\{i\}}{\sum}|a_{ij}|\left(\frac{p_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}+\epsilon\right),\quad i\in N_{1},

Below, we review the use of the Schur complement to represent the inverse of a matrix. Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is an nonsingular matrix, if

\displaystyle A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A(\alpha)&A(\alpha,\overline{\alpha})\\

\end{array}\right),

is an nonsingular matrix. Then, there exists matrices

O&A/\alpha\\
\end{array}\right)=:\Lambda,

where

\displaystyle X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{|\alpha|}&O\\
-A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)A(\alpha)^{-1}&I_{n-|\alpha|}\\

\displaystyle A^{-1}=Y\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A(\alpha)^{-1}&O\\
O&(A/\alpha)^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)X=Y\Lambda^{-1}X.

By utilizing the fundamental properties of infinity norm, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|Y\Lambda^{-1}X\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|Y\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\Lambda^{-1}X\right\|_{\infty},

where

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\Lambda^{-1}X\Bigr\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\Biggl\|\begin{pmatrix}A(\alpha)^{-1}&O\\

\displaystyle=\Biggl\|\begin{pmatrix}A(\alpha)^{-1}&O\\

\displaystyle\leq\max\Bigl\{\bigl\|A(\alpha)^{-1}\bigr\|_{\infty},\,\bigl\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\bigl(-A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)A(\alpha)^{-1}\thinspace I_{n-|\alpha|}\bigr)\bigr\|_{\infty}\Bigr\}.

(24)

\displaystyle\Bigl\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\bigl(-A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)A(\alpha)^{-1}\thinspace I_{n-|\alpha|}\bigr)\Bigr\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\Bigl\|-A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)A(\alpha)^{-1}\thinspace I_{n-|\alpha|}\Bigr\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\Bigr\|_{\infty}\|X\|_{\infty}.

Finally, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|Y\right\|_{\infty}{\rm max}\{\left\|A(\alpha)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|X\right\|_{\infty}\}.

(25)

Under the idea of splitting and shrinking based on (

3.2
with Lemma
4.1
and Lemma

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left(1+\underset{i\in N_{2}}{\rm max}\frac{P_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}\right){\rm max}\{\phi,\psi\},

(26)

where

\displaystyle\phi=\underset{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j\in N_{2}}}{\rm max}\frac{|a_{jj}|+R^{N_{2}}_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}||a_{jj}|-R^{N_{2}}_{i}(A)R^{N_{2}}_{j}(A)},

and

\displaystyle\psi=\underset{i\in N_{1}}{\rm max}\frac{1+\phi R^{N_{2}}_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|-R^{N_{1}}_{i}(A)-\underset{j\in N_{2}}{\sum}\frac{|a_{ij}|}{|a_{jj}|}P_{j}(A)}.

Proof.

Take
\alpha=N_{2}
. Then there exists a permutation matrix

such that

\displaystyle P^{T}AP=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A(\alpha)&A(\alpha,\overline{\alpha})\\
A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)&A(\overline{\alpha})\\
\end{array}\right).

Furthermore, we obtain that

\displaystyle XP^{T}APY=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A(\alpha)&O\\
O&A/\alpha\\
\end{array}\right),

where

and

define as (
4

\displaystyle A^{-1}=PY\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A(\alpha)^{-1}&O\\
O&(A/\alpha)^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)XP^{T}.

. By (

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|Y\right\|_{\infty}{\rm max}\{\left\|A(\alpha)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty},\left\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\bigl(-A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)A(\alpha)^{-1}\thinspace I_{n-|\alpha|}\bigr)\right\|_{\infty}\}.

Since both

are SDD matrices. From Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|A(\alpha)^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\underset{\underset{i\neq j}{i,j\in N_{2}}}{\rm max}\frac{|a_{jj}|+R^{N_{2}}_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}||a_{jj}|-R^{N_{2}}_{i}(A)R^{N_{2}}_{j}(A)}=\phi.

(27)

From the fundamental property of infinity norm, we have

\displaystyle\Biggl\|(A/\alpha)^{-1}\Bigl(-A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)A(\alpha)^{-1}\thinspace I_{n-|\alpha|}\Bigr)\Biggr\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\Biggl\|<(A/\alpha)>^{-1}\Bigl(|A(\overline{\alpha},\alpha)|<A(\alpha)>^{-1}\thinspace I_{n-|\alpha|}\Bigr)\Biggr\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\psi.

(28)

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|Y^{-1}\right\|_{\infty}=1+\left\|A(\alpha)^{-1}A(\alpha,\overline{\alpha})\right\|_{\infty}.

Let

. Furthermore, combine (

6
The bounds on the determinant for
SDD_{1}
matrices

The determinants of matrices are widely used in Accurate Computation
[
10
,
15
,
9
]
. When the order of the matrix is too large, it is terrible to calculate the determinant directly. Therefore, scholars usually give the upper and lower bounds of the determinant, and the common effective way is to use the elements of the original matrix to give this estimate. In this section, we will provide the new determinant upper and lower bounds of the
SDD_{1}
matrix by Schur complement. An interesting fact is that in 2005, before the
SDD_{1}
matrix was formally proposed, Huang had already given the upper and lower bounds of the determinant for matrices with such a structure. We summarize them as follows:

Lemma 6.1
.

[
8
]
Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be an
SDD_{1}
matrix, then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}\leq|\det(A)|\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}u_{i},

(42)

where

\displaystyle l_{i}=|a_{ii}|-\frac{1}{x_{i}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|x_{j},\quad u_{i}=|a_{ii}|+\frac{1}{x_{i}}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|x_{j},\quad a_{n,n+1}=0,

\displaystyle x_{i}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\qquad 1,\qquad i\in N_{1},\\
\thinspace&\theta+\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|},i\in N_{2},\end{aligned}\right.\quad{\text{and}}\quad\theta=\underset{j\in N_{1}}{\rm min}\frac{|a_{jj}|-P_{j}(A)}{R^{N_{2}}_{j}(A)}.

We say that the index set
N
of matrix
A
admits a
D_{1}
ordering if it is partitioned as

\displaystyle N_{1}=\{1,2,\dots,s\},\qquad N_{2}=\{s+1,s+2,\dots,n\},

(43)

where
0<s<n
. Obviously, for any matrix
A
, there exists a permutation
P
such that the index set of
P^{T}AP
satisfies (
43
).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be an
SDD_{1}
matrix, and the index set
N
of
A
satisfies (
43
). Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\leq|\det(A)|\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}g_{i},

(44)

where

\displaystyle f_{i}=|a_{ii}|-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|y_{j},\quad g_{i}=|a_{ii}|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|y_{j},\quad a_{n,n+1}=0,

and

\displaystyle y_{i}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{P_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|},\qquad i\in\{1,2,\dots,s\},\\
\thinspace&\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|},\quad i\in\{s+1,\dots,n\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

Denote
\alpha_{n}=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
,
\alpha_{n-1}=\{2,3,\dots,n\}
, …,
\alpha_{1}=\{n\}
,
\alpha_{0}=\emptyset
. By applying Lemma
2.6
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\det(A)

\displaystyle=\det\left(A(\alpha_{n-1})\right)\times\det\left(A/A(\alpha_{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(A(\alpha_{n-2})\right)\times\det\left(A(\alpha_{n-1})/A(\alpha_{n-2})\right)\times\det\left(A/A(\alpha_{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(A(\alpha_{1})\right)\times\det\left(A(\alpha_{2})/A(\alpha_{1})\right)\times\dots\times\det\left(A/A(\alpha_{n-1})\right).

(42)

For
i\in\{1,2,\dots,s-1\}
, we have

\displaystyle|\det\left(A(\alpha_{n-i+1})/A(\alpha_{n-i})\right)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|A(\alpha_{n-i+1})/A(\alpha_{n-i})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{ii}-a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}(A(\alpha_{n-i}))^{-1}a_{\alpha_{n-i}i}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|<A(\alpha_{n-i})>^{-1}|a_{\alpha_{n-i}i}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|(\frac{P_{i+1}(A)}{|a_{i+1i+1}|},\dots,\frac{P_{n}(A)}{|a_{nn}|})^{T}(\text{\rm By (\ref{eq 3.10})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|\frac{P_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-\sum_{j=i+1}^{s}|a_{ij}|\frac{P_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}-\sum_{j=s+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}.

(43)

Conversely,

\displaystyle|\det\left(A(\alpha_{n-i+1})/A(\alpha_{n-i})\right)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|+|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|<A(\alpha_{n-i})>^{-1}|a_{\alpha_{n-i}i}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|\frac{P_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{s}|a_{ij}|\frac{P_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}+\sum_{j=s+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}.

(44)

Similarly, for
i\in\{s,s+1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle|\det\left(A(\alpha_{n-i+1})/A(\alpha_{n-i})\right)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|<A(\alpha_{n-i})>^{-1}|a_{\alpha_{n-i}i}|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|(\frac{|a_{i+1i}|+Q^{\alpha_{n-i}}_{i+1}(A)}{|a_{i+1i+1}|},\dots,\frac{|a_{ni}|+Q^{\alpha_{n-i}}_{n}(A)}{|a_{nn}|})^{T}(\text{\rm By Lemma \ref{Lemma 3.1}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|(\frac{R_{i+1}(A)}{|a_{i+1i+1}|},\dots,\frac{R_{n}(A)}{|a_{nn}|})^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}.

(45)

Conversely,

\displaystyle|\det(A(\alpha_{n-i+1})/A(\alpha_{n-i}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|+|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|<A(\alpha_{n-i})>^{-1}|a_{\alpha_{n-i}i}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|+|a_{i\alpha_{n-i}}|(\frac{R_{i+1}(A)}{|a_{i+1i+1}|},\dots,\frac{R_{n}(A)}{|a_{nn}|})^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|+\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}.

(46)

Combine (
43
)—(
46
) to (
42
), we have (
44
). The proof is completed.
∎

The following theorem shows that in
D_{1}
ordering, the upper and lower bounds of the determinant of Theorem
6.1
are superior to Lemma
6.1
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
A=(a_{ij})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be an
SDD_{1}
matrix, and
N
satisfies (
43
). Then

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\leq|\det(A)|\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}u_{i},

where
l_{i},u_{i}
are given as in Lemma
6.1
, and
f_{i},g_{i}
are given as in Theorem
6.1
.

Proof.

Let us prove it by discussing
i
in two cases:

When
i\in\{1,2,\dots,s\}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}-l_{i}=\sum_{j=i+1}^{s}|a_{ij}|(1-\frac{P_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|})+\sum_{j=s+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|(\theta+\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}-\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|})\geq 0.

When
i\in\{s+1,s+2,\dots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle f_{i}-l_{i}=\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|(\frac{\theta+\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}}{\theta+\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}}-\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}).

If
\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}>\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}
, make use of Lemma
2.5
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{\theta+\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}}{\theta+\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}}\geq\frac{\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}}{\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}}\geq\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}.

If
\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}\leq\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\theta+\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}}{\theta+\frac{R_{i}(A)}{|a_{ii}|}}\geq 1>\frac{R_{j}(A)}{|a_{jj}|}.

In combination with the above steps, we have
f_{i}-l_{i}\geq 0
. By the arbitrariness of
i
,
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}l_{i}\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}
. Similarly, we have
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}\leq\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Example 5
.

Consider the
SDD_{1}

\displaystyle A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}1.5&0.3&0.3&0.6&0.3&0.3\\
0.3&1.5&0.3&0.3&0.6&0.3\\
0.3&0&1.5&0.6&0&0.6\\
0.3&0&0.3&3&0&0\\
0.3&0&0&0&1.5&0.3\\
0&0&0.3&0&0&1.5\end{array}\right).

We have
N_{1}=\{1,2,3\}
,
N_{2}=\{4,5,6\}
. Applying Lemma
6.1
yields

\displaystyle 0.099\leq|\det(A)|\leq 125.8959.

By Theorem
5.1
, we have

\displaystyle 7.5835\leq|\det(A)|\leq 50.4812.

The exact value

.

Example 6
.

Consider the
SDD_{1}
matrix

\displaystyle A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}3&0&1&2&0&2\\
1&2&0&1&0&0\\

0&1&0&3&0&0\\

0&0&0&1&0&3\end{array}\right).

Then
N_{1}=\{1,2,3\}
and
N_{2}=\{4,5,6\}

\displaystyle R_{1}(A)=5,\quad R_{2}(A)=2,\quad R_{3}(A)=2,

\displaystyle R_{4}(A)=1,\quad R_{5}(A)=1,\quad R_{6}(A)=1,

and
\theta=\frac{1}{6}

\displaystyle l_{1}=3-1-(\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{3})\times 2-(\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{3})\times 2=0.

\displaystyle 0\leq|\det(A)|\leq 1311.3.

\displaystyle 66.7\leq|\det(A)|\leq 816.7.

Remark 7
.

From Example
6
, we observe that when the
SDD_{1}
matrix
A
is subjected to more adverse conditions, the determinant lower bound produced by Lemma
6.1
may degenerate to
0
. However, for any
i\in N
,

\displaystyle f_{i}=

\displaystyle|a_{ii}|-\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}|a_{ij}|y_{j}\geq|a_{ii}|-P_{i}(A)>0,

the lower bound derived by Theorem
6.1
is guaranteed to be strictly positive. This conclusively demonstrates the superiority of Theorem
6.1
over Lemma
6.1
.

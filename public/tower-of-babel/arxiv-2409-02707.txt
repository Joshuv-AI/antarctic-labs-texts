Search and state transfer between hubs by quantum walks
S. Skoupý and M. Štefaňák

Abstract
Search and state transfer between hubs, i.e. fully connected vertices, on otherwise arbitrary connected graph is investigated. Motivated by a recent result of Razzoli et al. (J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 55 , 265303 (2022)) on universality of hubs in continuous-time quantum walks and spatial search, we extend the investigation to state transfer and also to the discrete-time case. We show that the continuous-time quantum walk allows for perfect state transfer between multiple hubs if the numbers of senders and receivers are close. Turning to the discrete-time case, we show that the search for hubs is successful provided that the initial state is locally modified to account for a degree of each individual vertex. Concerning state transfer using discrete-time quantum walk, it is shown that between a single sender and a single receiver one can transfer two orthogonal states in the same run-time. Hence, it is possible to transfer an arbitrary quantum state of a qubit between two hubs. In addition, if the sender and the receiver know each other location, another linearly independent state can be transferred, allowing for exchange of a qutrit state. Finally, we consider the case of transfer between multiple senders and receivers. In this case we cannot transfer specific quantum states. Nevertheless, quantum walker can be transferred with high probability in two regimes - either when there is a similar number of senders and receivers, which is the same as for the continuous-time quantum walk, or when the number of receivers is considerably larger than the number of senders. Our investigation is based on dimensional reduction utilizing the invariant subspaces of the respective evolutions and the fact that for the appropriate choice of the loop weights the problem can be reduced to the complete graph with loops.

We provide a detailed investigation how do the terms contributing to the total success probability change with the number of solutions

II
State transfer between hubs by continuous-time quantum walk

We begin with the continuous-time quantum walk. Let us have a graph
G=(V,E)
, where
V
denotes the set of vertices and
E
denotes the set of edges. We consider simple graphs, i.e. the are no loops and no multiple edges in
E
. For a graphs with
N=|V|
vertices the Hilbert space of the continuous-time walk is an
N
-dimensional complex space spanned by vectors
\mathinner{|{v}\rangle}
,
v\in V
, corresponding to a particle being localized at the vertex
v
. The authors of
[
73
]
consider
M
vertices
w\in W
to be marked hubs, i.e. fully connected with degrees
d_{w}=N-1
. The Hamiltonian of the walk is taken as the weighted Laplacian with additional potential on the marked vertices

\hat{H}=\gamma\hat{L}+\sum_{w\in W}\lambda_{w}\mathinner{|{w}\rangle}\mathinner{\langle{w}|}.

(1)

Note that the Laplacian of a given graph
G
is determined by its adjacency matrix
\hat{A}
and the diagonal degree matrix
\hat{D}
as

\hat{L}=\hat{D}-\hat{A}.

(2)

The results of
[
73
]
show that as far as the amplitudes at the marked vertices are concerned, the system evolves in a
M+1
invariant subspace
[
74
]
spanned by
\mathinner{|{w}\rangle}
,
w\in W
, and the equal weight superposition of all remaining vertices

\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-M}}\sum_{v\notin W}\mathinner{|{v}\rangle}.

(3)

The effective Hamiltonian in the invariant subspace acts according to (see Eq. (50) in
[
73
]
for
\mu=M
)

\displaystyle\hat{H}\mathinner{|{w}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma\left(N-1+\frac{\lambda_{w}}{\gamma}\right)\mathinner{|{w}\rangle}-\gamma\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W\\
w^{\prime}\neq w\end{subarray}}\mathinner{|{w^{\prime}}\rangle}-

\displaystyle-\gamma\sqrt{N-M}\mathinner{|{g}\rangle},\quad\forall w\in W,

\displaystyle\hat{H}\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma\sqrt{N-M}\sum_{w\in W}\mathinner{|{w}\rangle}+\gamma M\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}.

(4)

For spatial search, the weights of all marked hubs are chosen as
\lambda_{w}=-1
, and the optimal choice of the hopping amplitude
\gamma
is shown to be equal to
\frac{1}{N}
. The dimensionality of the problem can be further reduced to 2, since the target state of the search algorithm is the equal weight superposition of the marked vertices

\mathinner{|{\tilde{w}}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{w\in W}\mathinner{|{w}\rangle}.

(5)

In the invariant subspace spanned by
\mathinner{|{\tilde{w}}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}
the Hamiltonian is described by a matrix (see Eq. (72) in
[
73
]
for
\gamma=\frac{1}{N}

H=-\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}M&\sqrt{M(N-M)}\\
\sqrt{M(N-M)}&-M\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

Search evolves from equal weight superposition of all vertices

\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{v}\mathinner{|{v}\rangle}=\sqrt{\frac{M}{N}}\mathinner{|{\tilde{w}}\rangle}+\sqrt{\frac{N-M}{N}}\mathinner{|{g}\rangle},

(7)

towards the target state
\mathinner{|{\tilde{w}}\rangle}
with unit probability in time given by

T=\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{N}{M}}.

(8)

Let us now utilize the Hamiltonian (
4
) for state transfer. Consider
S
sender and
R
receiver hubs from sets
\cal S
and
\cal R
, such that
S+R=M
. We keep the hopping rate
\gamma=\frac{1}{N}
and the weights
\lambda_{s}=\lambda_{r}=-1
for all
s\in{\cal S}
and
r\in{\cal R}
. In such a case, we can group together the states localized on the sender and the receiver vertices

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\cal S}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{S}}\sum_{s\in{\cal S}}\mathinner{|{s}\rangle},

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\cal R}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{R}}\sum_{r\in{\cal R}}\mathinner{|{r}\rangle},

(9)

which we consider as the initial and the target state of state transfer. Together with (
3
) they form a 3D invariant subspace.
The Hamiltonian in the reduced subspace is given by the following 3x3 matrix

H=-\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}S&\sqrt{RS}&\sqrt{S(N-M)}\\
\sqrt{RS}&R&\sqrt{R(N-M)}\\
\sqrt{S(N-M)}&\sqrt{R(N-M)}&-M\end{pmatrix}

(10)

The energy spectrum of (
10
) is found to be

E_{0}=0,\quad E_{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{M}{N}},

(11)

and the corresponding eigenvectors are

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{S+R}}(\sqrt{R}\mathinner{|{\cal S}\rangle}-\sqrt{S}\mathinner{|{\cal R}\rangle}),

(12)

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\pm}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{1-E_{\pm}}{2M}}\left(\sqrt{S}\mathinner{|{\cal S}\rangle}+\sqrt{R}\mathinner{|{\cal R}\rangle}\right)\mp

\displaystyle\mp\sqrt{\frac{1+E_{\pm}}{2}}\mathinner{|{g}\rangle}.

Given the initial state
\mathinner{|{\psi(0)}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\cal S}\rangle}
, the state of the walk at time
t
reads

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\psi(t)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-iHt}\mathinner{|{\cal S}\rangle}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{R}{M}}\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}+e^{-iE_{+}t}\sqrt{\frac{S(1-E_{+})}{2M}}\mathinner{|{+}\rangle}+

\displaystyle+e^{iE_{+}t}\sqrt{\frac{S(1+E_{+})}{2M}}\mathinner{|{-}\rangle}.

Overlap with the target state
\mathinner{|{\cal R}\rangle}
then equals

\langle{\cal R}|\psi(t)\rangle=\frac{\sqrt{RS}}{M}\left[1-\cos{(E_{+}t)}+iE_{+}\sin{(E_{+}t)}\right].

(14)

Fidelity of state transfer is then given by

\displaystyle F(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\langle{\cal R}|\psi(t)\rangle|^{2}

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4RS}{M^{2}}\sin^{4}{\left(\frac{E_{+}t}{2}\right)}+\frac{RS}{NM}\sin^{2}{(E_{+}t)}.

Hence, the maximal fidelity

F_{max}=\frac{4RS}{(R+S)^{2}},

(16)

is reached at time
T
given by

T=\frac{\pi}{E_{+}}=\sqrt{\frac{N}{R+S}}\pi.

(17)

We find that state transfer with high fidelity is possible when
R\approx S
, see Figure
1
for a visualization. Unit fidelity corresponding to perfect state transfer is achieved when the number of receivers and senders are equal. Run-time of the state transfer is twice of that for search for
M=R+S
vertices (
8
). Indeed, in state transfer we evolve the system from the state localized on the sender vertices through the equal weight superposition of all vertices (
7
) towards the state localized on the receiver vertices, effectively iterating the search dynamics twice. For
S
and
R
differing considerably the state transfer is suppressed. In particular, for
S\gg R
or
R\gg S
, maximal fidelity (
16

III
Discrete-time quantum walk search for hubs

We turn to the discrete-time quantum walk search for
M
marked hubs labeled by indices from a set
\cal M
. Let us start with the Hilbert space. Given a graph
G=\left(V,E\right)
the corresponding Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{G}
can be decomposed as a direct sum of local Hilbert spaces
{\cal H}_{v}
at each vertex
v\in V
. Each
{\cal H}_{v}
is spanned by vectors
\mathinner{|{v,w}\rangle}
such that there is an edge between vertex
v
and
w
, and an additional state
\mathinner{|{v,v}\rangle}
corresponding to the loop at the vertex
v
. The dimension of the local space
{\cal H}_{v}
is thus equal to
d_{v}+1
, where
d_{v}
is the degree of the vertex
v
in the simple graph
G
.

The unitary evolution operator of the walk,
\hat{U}=\hat{S}\hat{C}
, is a product of the shift operator
\hat{S}
and the coin operator
\hat{C}
. We consider the flip-flop shift, which is defined in the following way

\displaystyle\hat{S}|v,w\rangle=|w,v\rangle,\quad\hat{S}\mathinner{|{v,v}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{v,v}\rangle}.

(18)

The coin operator is given by a direct sum

\hat{C}=\bigoplus_{v}\hat{C}_{v},

(19)

of local unitaries
\hat{C}_{v}
acting on vertex spaces
{\cal H}_{v}
. As the local coins at non-marked vertices we consider the Grover operator with a weighted loop
[
15
]
. The original Grover operator
[
5
]
is invariant under all permutations. Hence, when used as a coin in the discrete time quantum walk, it has the advantage that it does not matter how we order the basis states in the local Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{v}

[
6
]
. Adding the weighted loop adds a parameter, which can be tuned to improve the success probability of search
[
19
]
. We can write the Grover operator with a weighted loop as
[
68
]

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{v}(l_{v})=2|\Omega_{v}(l_{v})\rangle\langle\Omega_{v}(l_{v})|-\hat{I}_{v},

(20)

where
\hat{I}_{v}
is an identity operator at the subspace
{\cal H}_{v}
and
|\Omega_{v}(l_{v})\rangle
is given by

\mathinner{|{\Omega_{v}(l_{v})}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{v}+l_{v}}}\left(\sqrt{d_{v}}\mathinner{|{\Omega_{v}}\rangle}+\sqrt{l_{v}}\mathinner{|{v,v}\rangle}\right).

(21)

By
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{v}}\rangle}
we have denoted the equal weight superposition of all outgoing arcs from the vertex
v

\displaystyle|\Omega_{v}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{v}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\\
\left\{v,w\right\}\in E\end{subarray}}|v,w\rangle.

(22)

The free parameter
l_{v}
sets the weight of the loop state at the vertex
v
. In correspondence to the continuous-time case
[
73
]
we tune it according to the degree of the vertex and set
l_{v}=N-d_{v}
. On the marked hubs
m\in{\cal M}
this results in
l_{m}=1
. In addition, we multiply the coin on the marked hubs by -1, which is analogous to the marking of the solutions in Grover algorithm
[
5
]
. Moreover, one can show that the phase shift of
\pi
is the optimal choice, as it results in large gap of the avoided crossing which is inversely proportional to the run-time of the search
[
11
,
13
]
. Hence, choosing different phase shift results in slower algorithm with lower success probability. The evolution operator of the search is then given by

\hat{U}_{\cal M}=\hat{S}\ \left(\bigoplus_{v\notin{\cal M}}\left(\hat{G}_{v}(N-d_{v})\right)\bigoplus_{m\in{\cal M}}(-\hat{G}_{m}(1))\right).

(23)

The usual choice of the initial state for the search algorithm is the equal weight superposition of all arcs

|\tilde{\Omega}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{v\in V}d_{v}}}\sum_{v\in V}\sqrt{d_{v}}|\Omega_{v}\rangle.

(24)

However, with this initial state the search does not perform well, as can be observed from numerical simulations. In addition, we do not find a simple closed invariant subspace of
\hat{U}_{\cal M}
which involves (
24
). The reason for that is that
\mathinner{|{\tilde{\Omega}}\rangle}
has the same overlap with every arc, but not with every vertex of the graph, since the graph is not expected to be regular. Therefore, state (
24
) is not an eigenstate of the unperturbed walk operator (i.e. without marking of hubs by a
\pi
phase shift), which is given by

\hat{U}=\hat{S}\left(\bigoplus_{v\in V}\left(\hat{G}_{v}(N-d_{v})\right)\right).

(25)

Such eigenstate reads

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Omega}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{v\in V}\mathinner{|{\Omega_{v}(N-d_{v})}\rangle},

(26)

which has the same overlap of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
with all vertex subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{v}
. One can easily check that it satisfies
\hat{U}\mathinner{|{\Omega}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\Omega}\rangle}
. For this initial state we find that search for hubs behaves analogously as search on the complete graph with loops
[
9
,
14
]
. Let us construct the invariant subspace

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum\limits_{m\in{\cal M}}\mathinner{|{m,m}\rangle},

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{2}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M(M-1)}}\sum\limits_{m\neq m^{\prime}\in{\cal M}}\mathinner{|{m,m^{\prime}}\rangle},

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{M(N-M)}}\sum\limits_{m\in{\cal M}}\sum\limits_{v\notin{\cal M}}\mathinner{|{m,v}\rangle},

corresponding to superposition of loops (
\mathinner{|{\nu_{1}}\rangle}

), and arcs leaving marked hubs to the rest of the graph (

\mathinner{|{\nu_{1}}\rangle}

\mathinner{|{\nu_{2}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{4}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\hat{S}\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M(N-M)}}\sum\limits_{m\in{\cal M}}\sum\limits_{v\notin{\cal M}}\mathinner{|{v,m}\rangle},

where the walker is localized on the non-marked vertices in the arcs heading towards the marked ones. We add this state as the fourth vector of the invariant subspace
\cal I

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{M}}\left(\cos{(\omega t)}-1+(1+(-1)^{t})\frac{M}{N}\right),

\displaystyle\sqrt{M-1}\langle\nu_{1}|\psi(t)\rangle,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sin{(\omega t)}+(1+(-1)^{t})\frac{\sqrt{M(N-M)}}{2N}.

Considering first the case

\displaystyle P_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sin^{4}\left(\frac{\omega t}{2}\right),

(36)

The total success probability is then equal to

P(t)\approx\sin^{4}\left(\frac{\omega t}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{4}\sin^{2}(\omega t),

(37)

which is close to unity provided that we choose the number of steps
t
as the closest integer to

\displaystyle T=\frac{\pi}{\omega}\approx\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{N}{M}}+O\left(\sqrt{\frac{M}{N}}\right).

(38)

For
N\gg M
the maximum success probability comes from
P_{1}(T)
and
P_{2}(T)
, while
P_{3}(T)
vanishes. When
M=1
we find the particle in the loop at the unique marked vertex, since there is no
\mathinner{|{\nu_{2}}\rangle}
state and
P_{2}(t)
equals zero. As the number of marked hubs grows the contribution of
P_{2}(t)
increases, so we are more likely to find the walker on an edge connecting two marked hubs.

When the number of marked hubs is non-negligible relative to the total number of vertices the approximations (
36
) are no longer valid. While
P_{1}(t)
vanishes the contribution of
P_{3}(t)
to the total success probability becomes more important. One can show that for all values of
N
and
M<N
the total success probability of search evolves with the number of steps according to

P(2t)=P(2t+1)=\sin^{2}{\left(\frac{\omega(2t+1)}{2}\right)}.

(39)

Indeed, as was shown in
[
9
]
two steps of the quantum walk search on a complete graph with loops are equivalent to a single iteration of the Grover algorithm. In conclusion, we find one of the marked hubs with probability close to one for the number of steps derived earlier (
38
).

M=3

M=15
. The plot highlights how the contribution of different terms
P_{i}(t)
changes as the number of marked hubs increases. One can also notice that the total success probability changes only after each two steps, in accordance with (
39
).

IV
State transfer between two hubs

Let us now turn to the state transfer between two hubs, sender and receiver vertices
s
and
r
. We consider the same local coins for the marked and non-marked vertices as for the search. The evolution operator for the state transfer then has the form

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{s,r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{S}\ \left(\bigoplus_{v\neq s,r}\left(\hat{G}_{v}(N-d_{v})\right)\oplus(-\hat{G}_{s}(1))\right.

(40)

\displaystyle\hskip 48.0pt\left.\oplus(-\hat{G}_{r}(1))\right).

As for the search, the choice of the loop weights allows to map the problem of state transfer between hubs on otherwise arbitrary graph to state transfer on complete graph with loops. We have investigated this model earlier
[
64
]
, however, only a single initial state was considered. In the present paper we extend this investigation and show that it is possible to transfer two orthogonal states, namely the loop and the equal weight superposition of all outgoing arcs, in the same run-time. Hence, one can transfer a state of a qubit from one hub to another. In fact, we show that if the sender and the receiver knows their locations, then they can exchange another linearly independent state, allowing for exchange of a qutrit.

First, we perform the dimensional reduction, i.e. determine the invariant subspace
{\cal I}
of the walk evolution operator
\hat{U}_{s,r}
containing both pairs
\mathinner{|{s,s}\rangle}
,
\mathinner{|{r,r}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}
,
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}
. Construction of the invariant subspace follows the same logic as for the search problem in the previous Section. We begin with the loops on the marked vertices and the connecting arcs

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{1}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{|{s,s}\rangle},\qquad\mathinner{|{\nu_{2}}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{r,r}\rangle},

(41)

\displaystyle|\nu_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathinner{|{s,r}\rangle},\qquad|\nu_{4}\rangle=\mathinner{|{r,s}\rangle}.

Next, we add states connecting sender and receiver with the rest of the graph

\displaystyle|\nu_{5}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-2}}\sum\limits_{v\neq s,r}\mathinner{|{s,v}\rangle},\quad|\nu_{6}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-2}}\sum\limits_{v\neq s,r}\mathinner{|{r,v}\rangle},

\displaystyle|\nu_{7}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-2}}\sum\limits_{v\neq s,r}\mathinner{|{v,s}\rangle},\quad|\nu_{8}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-2}}\sum\limits_{v\neq s,r}\mathinner{|{v,r}\rangle}.

To complete the invariant subspace
\cal I
we consider vectors corresponding to loops and arcs between non-marked vertices

\displaystyle|\nu_{9}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N-2}\sum\limits_{v\neq s,r}\left(\sqrt{N}\mathinner{|{\Omega_{v}(N-d_{v})}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{v,r}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{v,s}\rangle}\right).

Note that the equal weight superpositions can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-1}}\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}+\sqrt{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\mathinner{|{\nu_{5}}\rangle},

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-1}}\mathinner{|{\nu_{4}}\rangle}+\sqrt{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\mathinner{|{\nu_{6}}\rangle},

(44)

which tends to
\mathinner{|{\nu_{5}}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\nu_{6}}\rangle}
for large graph size
N
. One can show by direct calculation that the 9-dimensional space is closed under action of
\hat{U}_{s,r}
. We note that in
[
64
]
we considered state transfer on the complete graph with loops from the state corresponding to the equal weight superposition of the loop and arcs leaving the sender vertex, which in the present notation reads

\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left(\mathinner{|{\nu_{1}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}+\sqrt{N-2}\mathinner{|{\nu_{5}}\rangle}\right).

(45)

For this particular initial state it was sufficient to consider a smaller 5 dimensional invariant subspace
[
64
]
.

We provide the detailed investigation of the evolution operator (
40

B

\pm 1
and three conjugated pairs
\lambda_{j}^{(\pm)}=e^{\pm i\omega_{j}}
where the phases

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arccos\left(1-\frac{4}{N}\right),

(46)

\displaystyle\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arccos\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{2}{N}}\right)=\frac{\omega_{1}}{2},

(47)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arccos\left(-\sqrt{1-\frac{2}{N}}\right)=\pi-\omega_{2}.

Eigenvalue
1

\mathinner{|{(1)_{1,2}}\rangle}
,
\mathinner{|{-1}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\pm\omega_{j}}\rangle}

IV.1
Transfer of equal weight superposition states

Let us first consider the initial state as the equal superposition of all outgoing arcs at the sender vertex (
22

\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}

\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{5}}\rangle},\quad\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}\approx\mathinner{|{\nu_{6}}\rangle}.

In terms of the eigenbasis of
\hat{U}_{s,r}
the vectors
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\sqrt{2}\mathinner{|{-1}\rangle}+i\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle}\right)+\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\left.\frac{}{}+i\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{2}}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{-\omega_{2}}\rangle}\right)-i\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{3}}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{-\omega_{3}}\rangle}\right)\right),

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\sqrt{2}\mathinner{|{-1}\rangle}+i\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle}\right)-\right.

(49)

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\left.\frac{}{}-i\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{2}}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{-\omega_{2}}\rangle}\right)+i\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{3}}\rangle}-\mathinner{|{-\omega_{3}}\rangle}\right)\right).

where we have utilized the approximations of eigenstates (
96
) and (
102

. The state of the walk after

steps is then given by

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\phi_{\Omega}(t)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{U}_{s,r}^{t}\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}=\frac{(-1)^{t}}{2}\mathinner{|{-1}\rangle}+

(50)

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(e^{2i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}-e^{-2i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle}\right)+

\displaystyle+\frac{i}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(e^{i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{+\omega_{2}}\rangle}-e^{-i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{-\omega_{2}}\rangle}\right)-

\displaystyle-i\frac{(-1)^{t}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(e^{-i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{+\omega_{3}}\rangle}-e^{i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{-\omega_{3}}\rangle}\right).

For the amplitude of state transfer to
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{\Omega}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[(-1)^{t}+\cos\left(2\omega_{2}t\right)-\right.

(51)

\displaystyle\hskip 24.0pt-\left.\cos\left(\omega_{2}t\right)\left(1+(-1)^{t}\right)\right].

We see that there are rapid changes between odd and even steps. For odd number of steps
2t+1

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{\Omega}(2t+1)\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}{(\omega_{2}(2t+1))},

(52)

so the fidelity can reach
\frac{1}{4}
at most. For even number of steps
2t
the amplitude (
51

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{\Omega}(2t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\cos{(2\omega_{2}t)}\sin^{2}\left(\omega_{2}t\right).

(53)

Hence, for the closest even integer to

\displaystyle T=2t\approx\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\approx\pi\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

(54)

the amplitude (
53
) is close to one, i. e. we obtain state transfer from
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}
to
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}
with high fidelity. We note that (
53

[
64

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{s,s}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\nu_{1}}\rangle},\quad\mathinner{|{r,r}\rangle}=\mathinner{|{\nu_{2}}\rangle}.

In the eigenvector basis we find that the for large

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{s,s}\rangle}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{(1)_{1}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mathinner{|{(1)_{2}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle}\right)+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{2}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{2}}\rangle}\right),

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{r,r}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{(1)_{1}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mathinner{|{(1)_{2}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle}\right)-

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{2}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{2}}\rangle}\right).

IV.3
Transfer of qubit state and finite-size effects of the graph

Our results show that we can achieve state transfer of both
\mathinner{|{s,s}\rangle}
to
\mathinner{|{r,r}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}
to
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}
in the same run-time. Moreover, we can perform a state transfer of any superposition of these two orthonormal states. Hence, it is possible to transfer an arbitrary state of a qubit between two hubs in time given by the closest even integer to (
54
). Consider a general qubit state at the sender encoded as

\mathinner{|{\psi_{s}}\rangle}=a\mathinner{|{s,s}\rangle}+b\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}\equiv a\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}+b\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}.

(59)

The target qubit state at receiver vertex is

\mathinner{|{\psi_{r}}\rangle}=a\mathinner{|{r,r}\rangle}+b\mathinner{|{\Omega_{r}}\rangle}.

(60)

Time evolution of the initial state (
59
) after
2t
steps is given by

\mathinner{|{\psi(2t)}\rangle}=a\mathinner{|{\phi_{l}(2t)}\rangle}+b\mathinner{|{\phi_{\Omega}(2t)}\rangle},

(61)

where
\mathinner{|{\phi_{l}(2t)}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\phi_{\Omega}(2t)}\rangle}
are given by (
57
) and (
50
). Amplitude of state transfer from (
59
) to (
60
) then reads

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{r}|\psi(2t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a|^{2}\langle r,r|\phi_{l}(2t)\rangle+|b|^{2}\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{\Omega}(2t)\rangle+

(62)

\displaystyle+a\overline{b}\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{l}(2t)\rangle+\overline{a}b\langle r,r|\phi_{\Omega}(2t)\rangle,

where
\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{\Omega}(2t)\rangle
and
\langle r,r|\phi_{l}(2t)\rangle
were determined in (
51
) and (
58
). The remaining transition amplitudes are found to be

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{l}(2t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sin^{2}{\left(\omega_{2}t\right)}\sin{\left(2\omega_{2}t\right)},

\displaystyle\langle r,r|\phi_{\Omega}(2t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{l}(2t)\rangle,

(63)

which both vanish when the number of steps
T
is taken as the closest even integer to (
54
). Since
\langle\Omega_{r}|\phi_{\Omega}(T)\rangle
and
\langle r,r|\phi_{l}(T)\rangle
both tend to one for a large graph, we can transfer an arbitrary qubit state from the sender to the receiver vertex.

We note that our result were derived utilizing the approximation of eigenstates (
96
), (
102
) for large graph, which neglected the
O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right)
terms in the eigenvectors. For smaller values of
N
these contributions might be non-negligible and affect the fidelity of state transfer. In addition, the influence can be state dependent. To elucidate the finite-size effects we numerically investigated the fidelity of state transfer of a qubit state

\mathinner{|{\psi}\rangle}=\rho\mathinner{|{0}\rangle}+e^{i\varphi}\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\mathinner{|{1}\rangle},

(64)

on a graph with 10 vertices. The results are displayed in the upper plot of Fig.
4
. For both parameters
\rho
and
\varphi
we split the corresponding interval range into 100 elements and evaluate the fidelity at the number of steps given by the closest even integer to (
54
). The plot indicates that the worst fidelity is obtained for the state
\mathinner{|{1}\rangle}
, i.e.
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s}}\rangle}
. On a graph of 10 vertices the fidelity ranges between 0.82 and 0.9.

In the lower plot of Fig.
4
we show the fidelity of state transfer for the basis states as a function of the graph size
N
. The plot is on the log-log scale to unravel the power-law behaviour
F(T)=1-O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)
. Note that the rapid drops and increases in the figure are due to the discreteness of the optimal time

, which has to be chosen as the closest even integer to (
54
). The plot indicates that changing the qubit state to be transferred alters the pre-factor but not the power law dependence.

IV.4
Transfer of the connecting arcs

Let us now consider the situation when the sender and the receiver know their positions. In such a case, they can transfer the states corresponding to the arcs on the connecting edge, i.e. the state
\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}
will be mapped onto
\mathinner{|{\nu_{4}}\rangle}
. We show that this is achieved in the same runtime as for the loop and the equal weight superposition.

The initial and the target states decomposed into the eigenvectors of
\hat{U}_{s,r}
have the following form

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{(1)_{1}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mathinner{|{(1)_{2}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle})+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{3}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{3}}\rangle}),

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\nu_{4}}\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{(1)_{1}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mathinner{|{(1)_{2}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{4}(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle})-

(65)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\mathinner{|{+\omega_{3}}\rangle}+\mathinner{|{-\omega_{3}}\rangle}).

In even steps
2t
, the state of the walk is given by

\displaystyle\mathinner{|{\phi_{sr}(2t)}\rangle}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\mathinner{|{(1)_{1}}\rangle}+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mathinner{|{(1)_{2}}\rangle}+

(66)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}(e^{4i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{+\omega_{1}}\rangle}+e^{-4i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{-\omega_{1}}\rangle})+

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(e^{-2i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{+\omega_{3}}\rangle}+e^{2i\omega_{2}t}\mathinner{|{-\omega_{3}}\rangle}).

\langle\nu_{4}|\phi_{sr}(2t)\rangle=\sin^{4}{(\omega_{2}t)},

(67)

i.e., it behaves the same as for the transfer of loop states (

54

We note that the vectors
\mathinner{|{\nu_{3,4}}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\Omega_{s,r}}\rangle}
are linearly independent but not mutually orthogonal. Nevertheless, from (

\langle\nu_{3}|\Omega_{s}\rangle=\langle\nu_{4}|\Omega_{r}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-1}}

(68)

\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}

\mathinner{|{\nu_{1}}\rangle}

\mathinner{|{\nu_{3}}\rangle}
and
\mathinner{|{\nu_{5}}\rangle}
are the only basis states which are localized on the sender vertex. Hence, in this model we cannot transfer a higher dimensional qudit state with

.

We have also tested numerically the effect of finite graph size on the fidelity of state transfer of the connecting arcs. The simulation reveals that for every
N
the fidelity is exactly equal to the one for the state transfer of the loop state, which is depicted in the lower plot of Figure
4
with the blue triangles.

VI
Conclusions

Search and state transfer between multiple hubs on otherwise arbitrary graphs were investigated in detail. We have shown that both continuous-time and discrete-time quantum walks can be utilized for this purpose, since the models can be mapped to the complete graph with loops for a proper choice of the loop weights. In the continuous-time case the state transfer is achieved with high fidelity when the number of senders
S
is close to the number of receivers
R
. The discrete-time walk has proven to be more versatile. Indeed, state transfer between multiple hubs is achieved also in the regime
R\gg S
, which is not possible in the continuous-time case. Moreover, when only a single sender and a single receiver is considered, the coin degree of freedom allows us to transfer multiple orthogonal states in the same run-time, expanding our earlier results
[
64
]
. We have shown that sender and receiver can always exchange an arbitrary qubit state, and in the case they know each others position this can be extended to a qutrit. This is not possible in the continuous time model, since there the walker does not have an internal degree of freedom, only the position.

There are several directions for generalizing our results. Our aim is to investigate if one can achieve state transfer from an ordinary vertex (i.e. not fully connected) to a hub. If the answer is positive we can perform state transfer between arbitrary vertices through the hub by switching the marked coin from the sender to the receiver after the transfer to the hub was completed, while keeping the hub vertex marked throughout the whole duration. Another approach to state transfer on arbitrary graphs is to utilize the framework based on the ergodic reversible Markov chains developed for both discrete-time
[
21
]
and continuous-time models
[
22
]
. This method was used to prove that quantum spatial search is quadratically faster over the classical search for an arbitrary number of marked vertices. We aim to modify this approach to investigate state transfer between multiple vertices which are not fully connected, where the reduction to a complete graph as in this paper is not possible.

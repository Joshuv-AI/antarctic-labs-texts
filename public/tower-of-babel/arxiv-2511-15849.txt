Connectivity-Preserving Important Separators: Enumeration and an Improved FPT Algorithm for Node Multiway Cut–Uncut
Batya Kenig

Abstract
We develop a framework for handling graph separation problems with connectivity constraints . Extending the classical concept of important separators , we introduce and analyze connectivity-preserving important separators , which are important separators that not only disconnect designated terminal sets A A and B B but also satisfy an arbitrary set of connectivity constraints over the terminals. These constraints can express requirements such as preserving the internal connectivity of each terminal set, enforcing pairwise connections defined by an equivalence relation, or maintaining reachability from a specified subset of vertices. We prove that for any graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) , terminal sets A , B ⊆ V A,B\subseteq V , and integer k k , the number of important A , B A,B -separators of size at most k k satisfying a set of connectivity constraints is bounded by 2 O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ k ) 2^{O(k\log k)} , and that all such separators can be enumerated within O ⁡ ( 2 O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ k ) ⋅ n ⋅ T ⁡ ( n , m ) ) O(2^{O(k\log k)}\cdot n\cdot T(n,m)) time, where T ⁡ ( n , m ) T(n,m) is the time required to compute a minimum s , t s,t -separator. As an application, we obtain a new fixed-parameter-tractable algorithm for the Node Multiway Cut–Uncut (N - MWCU) problem, parameterized by k k , the size of the separator set. The algorithm runs in O ⁡ ( 2 O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ k ) ⋅ n ⋅ m 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) O(2^{O(k\log k)}\cdot n\cdot m^{1+o(1)}) time for graphs with polynomially-bounded integer weights. This significantly improves the dependence on k k from the previous 2 O ⁡ ( k 2 ​ log ⁡ k ) 2^{O(k^{2}\log k)} to 2 O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ k ) 2^{O(k\log k)} , thereby breaking a long-standing barrier of Chitnis et al., [ 8 ] , and simultaneously improves the polynomial factors so that the runtime becomes linear in n n and almost linear in m m for vertex-weight functions that assign polynomially-bounded integer weights. Our framework generalises the important-separator paradigm to separation problems in which the deletion set must satisfy both cut and uncut constraints on terminal subsets, thus offering a refined combinatorial foundation for designing fixed-parameter algorithms for cut-uncut problems in graphs.

Introduction

Graph separation problems, which involve removing a small set of vertices or edges to disconnect or partition a graph, play a central role in combinatorial optimization and algorithm design, with applications in network reliability, clustering, and satisfiability. In parameterized complexity, separation-based techniques have led to fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) algorithms for a wide range of problems, including Multiway Cut
[
29
]
, Multicut
[
5
,
28
]
, Directed Multiway Cut
[
9
]
, Directed Feedback Vertex Set
[
6
]
, almost
2
-SAT
[
31
]
, and clustering problems
[
25
]
.
A key insight behind these results is the notion of
important separators
, introduced by Marx
[
29
]
, which identify extremal separators: among all
minimal separators
of equal or smaller size, an important separator is one that maximizes (or minimizes), with respect to inclusion, the set of vertices reachable from the designated source set after its removal.
This property limits the number of relevant separators, whose size is at most
k
, to at most
4^{k}
, enabling efficient enumeration
[
29
]
.

In many applications, disconnecting sets of vertices is not enough; it is also essential to preserve connectivity within each set. This arises in network resilience, where critical subsystems must remain connected despite failures or attacks
[
15
,
1
,
11
]
, and in clustering or partitioning tasks that require internally cohesive groups
[
25
]
. These scenarios motivate
connectivity-preserving separators
—vertex sets whose removal separates two or more terminal sets while maintaining connectivity within each. Classical important separators
[
29
]
, which optimize reachability from a source, do not account for connectivity constraints. This gap motivates a shift from separators that only disconnect to
connectivity-preserving
separators that also preserve connectivity, resulting in a dual constraint central to problems such as
Two-Sets Cut-Uncut

[
2
,
3
]
, or its generalization
Node-Multiway-Cut-Uncut

[
8
,
27
]
.

A canonical special case is the
2-Disjoint Connected Subgraphs
problem, which, given two disjoint terminal sets
A
and
B
, asks whether the graph contains two vertex-disjoint, connected subgraphs that contain
A
and
B
, respectively
[
16
,
15
,
20
,
22
,
30
,
33
,
11
]
. Its optimization variant seeks the smallest vertex set whose removal disconnects
A
from
B
while preserving connectivity of each
[
16
,
21
]
. This problem captures the core difficulty of balancing separation with connectivity and serves as a central primitive in recent structural and parameterized studies
[
2
,
21
,
3
,
18
]
. It is NP-hard even when
|A|=|B|=2

[
20
,
22
]
, and remains hard on restricted graph classes such as planar graphs
[
19
]
and line graphs
[
22
]
. However, in certain graph classes such as
AT-free
graphs
[
10
]
, the problem can be solved in polynomial time
[
18
,
21
]
. These observations motivate the study of connectivity-preserving important separators—a refinement of classical important separators that not only achieve separation but also maintain the internal connectivity of specified terminal sets.

In the
Node Multiway Cut-Uncut
(N-MWCU) problem one is given a graph
G
, a terminal set
A\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, an equivalence relation
\mathcal{R}
on
A
, and an integer
k
. The task is to decide whether there exists a set
S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G){\setminus}A
with
\lvert S\rvert\leq k
whose removal from
G
ensures that for all
u,v\in A
,
u
and
v
are connected in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
(i.e., the graph that results from
G
by removing
S
and its adjacent edges) if and only if
u\,\mathcal{R}\,v
. This formulation strictly generalises the
2-Disjoint Connected Subgraphs
problem (which corresponds to exactly two equivalence classes: one for
A
and one for
B
). Moreover, since the
2-Disjoint Connected Subgraphs
problem is NP-hard even when the terminal-sets have size two
[
16
,
15
,
20
,
22
]
, it follows that
Node Multiway Cut-Uncut
remains NP-hard already for restricted instances with exactly two equivalence classes.

Contributions.

Let
G
be a simple, undirected graph with
n
vertices,
m
edges, and positive vertex weights, and let
T(n,m)
denote the time to compute a minimum-weight
s,t
-separator (for
s,t\in\texttt{V}(G)
), which reduces to a max-flow computation
[
17
]
.
Following a series of advances, the current best deterministic algorithm for max-flow runs in near-linear time, taking
m^{1+o(1)}
time when the vertex-weights are positive integers
[
34
]
.

A
connectivity constraint

\mathcal{R}
defined over a subset
A\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
specifies which pairs of vertices in
A
must be connected. A set
S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G){\setminus}(A\cup B)
is a
connectivity-preserving

A,B
-separator with respect to
\mathcal{R}
, if it disconnects
A
from
B
while ensuring that all constraints in
\mathcal{R}
remain satisfied in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
(i.e., the graph that results by removing vertices
S
and their adjacent edges). For example, in the
2-Disjoint Connected Subgraphs
problem, the connectivity constraint requires an
A,B
-separator
S
such that all vertices in
A
stay connected and all vertices in
B
stay connected in
G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S
. In the
Node-Multiway-Cut-Uncut
problem, the connectivity constraint
\mathcal{R}
induces an equivalence relation over the terminals: every pair of terminals in the same equivalence class must remain connected. The goal is to find a subset of vertices whose removal separates every pair of terminals from different classes while meeting the requirements of
\mathcal{R}
, which is preserving the internal connectivity within each class.
Connectivity constraints are, by nature,
monotonic
: if they hold in an induced subgraph
G[D]
, meaning the subgraph that contains all edges of
G
between vertices of
D\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, then they also hold in every induced subgraph
G[D^{\prime}]
with
D^{\prime}\supseteq D
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a set of monotonic connectivity constraints defined over a set of terminals
A\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
. We say that
S\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
is a connectivity-preserving
important

A,B
-separator with respect to
\mathcal{R}
if, among all connectivity-preserving
A,B
-separators of size at most
\lvert S\rvert
, it minimizes (with respect to inclusion) the set of vertices reachable from
A
after its removal (formal definition in Section
3
). These separators refine classical important separators
[
29
]
by enforcing connectivity constraints.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A,B\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, and
\mathcal{R}
a set of connectivity constraints defined over
A
that induces an equivalence relation over
A
. There are at most
2^{3k(\log k+1)}
connectivity-preserving important
A,B
-separators, with respect to
\mathcal{R}
, of size at most
k
, and they can be enumerated in time
O(2^{O(k\log k)}\cdot n\cdot T(n,m))
.

As a simple illustration, consider the case where the constraint set
\mathcal{R}
requires all vertices of
A
to remain connected. Then Theorem
1.1
implies that there are at most
2^{3k(\log k+1)}
minimal
A,B
-separators of size at most
k
that both (i) preserve the connectivity of
A
, and (ii) are important in the sense that they minimize, with respect to inclusion, the set of vertices reachable from
A
.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we show that it yields a
slightly superexponential

[
24
]
FPT algorithm for computing a minimum-weight solution to the
N-MWCU
problem, when parameterized by
k
, the cardinality of the solution.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{R},k)
be an instance of the
NMWC-U
problem where
\mathcal{R}
is an equivalence relation defined over a set of vertices
A\subseteq\texttt{V}(G)
, and
k
is an integer. In time
O(2^{O(k\log k)}n\,T(n,m))
one can find a minimum-weight solution to the instance of size at most
k
, or report that none exist.

Related Work.

A natural benchmark for our problem is the
Node Multiway Cut–Uncut (N-MWCU)
algorithm of Chitnis et al.
[
8
]
, which runs in time
O(2^{O(k^{2}\log k)}\;n^{4}\log n)
. Earlier approaches based on the frameworks of Marx, Razgon, and O’Sullivan
[
27
]
and Razgon
[
32
]
already implied fixed-parameter tractability, but with double-exponential dependence on
k
. In contrast, our algorithm attains substantially faster running times by enumerating important connectivity-preserving minimal separators of size at most
k
(Theorem
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, this is the first algorithm for this problem whose dependence on the parameter
k
is only
slightly superexponential

[
24
]
—
2^{O(k\log k)}
—thereby breaking the long-standing quadratic-exponential barrier. Furthermore, it improves the polynomial factors in the running time from
n^{4}\log n
to
n\cdot T(n,m)
, which simplifies to
nm^{1+o(1)}
when vertex weights are positive integers
[
34
]
.

Organization.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Following preliminaries in Section

, thus proving Theorem

. Due to space considerations, most of the proofs are deferred to the Appendix.

Let

\textsc{Same}_{u,v}\!\left(G\right)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\left(C_{v}(G)=C_{u}(G)\right)\vskip-6.0pt

(2)

For convenience, we introduce two macros.

Definition 3.3
.

Definition 3.4
.

\textsc{ReachSome}_{D,y}\!\left(G\right)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\bigvee_{d\in D}\textsc{Same}_{y,d}\!\left(G\right).\vskip-6.0pt

\mathcal{R}_{A,B,\mathcal{P},Q}(G)\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{M}\textsc{AllSame}_{A_{i}}\!\left(G\right)\right)\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{q\in Q}\textsc{ReachSome}_{q,B}\!\left(G\right)\right)\text{,}\vskip-6.0pt

(3)

where

of the appendix.

For any sets

\mathcal{P}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textsf{\tiny def}}}{{=}}}\mathord{\{A_{1},\dots,A_{M}\}}

Node-Multiway-Cut-Uncut (N-MWCU)

We study the
Node Multiway Cut–Uncut (N-MWCU)
problem and present a new fixed-parameter algorithm that improves the parameter dependence of the running time.

Problem definition.

Node Multiway Cut–Uncut (N-MWCU).

Input:

Does there exist a set

Our algorithm actually solves the
Optimization variant
of
N-MWCU

N-MWCU
extends the classical

problem by allowing

[
8
]

Before this, the only general approach capable of handling uncut constraints was the

framework of Marx, O’Sullivan, and Razgon
[
26

k
is
double exponential
: the reduction produces an equivalent instance of treewidth
g(k)

k

Our new algorithm solves

-separator (equivalently an exact

n

[
8

[
34

7

. Thus in that setting the runtime of our algorithm is

.
Without the integrality (or polynomial boundedness) assumption, that is, for strictly positive real weights of arbitrary magnitude, one must use general methods for computing a minimum-weight

S\subseteq N_{G}(C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S))=N_{G}(A)
, and by definition,
S\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{A,B}(G,\mathcal{R})
. Suppose the claim holds for the case where
|C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)|\leq\ell
for some
\ell\geq|A|
, we prove for the case where
|C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)|=\ell+1
. If
S\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{A,B}(G,\mathcal{R})
, then we are done. Otherwise, there exists a
S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}_{A,B}(G,\mathcal{R})
where
C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S^{\prime})\subset C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
. Since
|C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S^{\prime})|<|C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)|=\ell+1
, then by the induction hypothesis, there exists a
T\in\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{A,B}(G,\mathcal{R})
where
C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}T)\subseteq C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S^{\prime})\subset C_{A}(G\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}S)
.

The proof for the case where
S\in\mathcal{F}_{sA,t,k}(G,\mathcal{R})
is identical.
∎

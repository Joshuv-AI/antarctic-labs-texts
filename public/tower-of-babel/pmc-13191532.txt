Research on Coal Gangue Image Recognition Method Based on EMAM-YOLO
Authors: Ying Jia, Baoshan Li, Yongxing Du, Wenxiang Zheng, Yu Li, Shiyan Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aiming at the problems of low target detection accuracy and poor robustness caused by factors such as uneven illumination, coal dust interference, and diverse coal-gangue morphologies in the complex environment of underground coal mines, a real-time coal-gangue image detection model based on EMAM-YOLO is proposed. This model uses YOLOv12n as the baseline framework and integrates multidimensional optimization modules to enhance comprehensive performance. First, the lightweight EfficientNetV1 network is adopted to reconstruct the backbone feature extraction structure, effectively reducing the number of model parameters while ensuring high-precision feature representation. Second, a Multiscale Attention Feature Pyramid Network (MAFPN) is introduced to strengthen the cross-scale interaction between shallow detail information and deep semantic information, improving the detection capability for coal-gangue targets of various sizes. Then, an Adaptive Spatial Feature Fusion detection head (Detect_ASFF) is designed to optimize the multiscale feature fusion strategy by learning dynamic weights, enhancing the model’s localization accuracy for occluded and deformed targets. Finally, a multiscale channel attention (MCA) mechanism is incorporated to guide the network to focus on key feature channels and suppress redundant information, further improving feature discrimination capability. The primary technical contribution of this work is 2-fold: (1) a novel adaptive spatial feature fusion detection head (Detect_ASFF) that learns dynamic spatial weights, representing a structural innovation within the head; (2) the specific combination and synergistic interaction of EfficientNetV1, MAFPN, Detect_ASFF, and MCA, each addressing a distinct drawback of YOLOv12n for coal-gangue detection. Experimental results on a self-built coal-gangue data set show that the EMAM-YOLO model achieves a mean average precision (mAP50–95) of 81.17%, which is 5.04 percentage points higher than the baseline YOLOv12n, with only 2.59 M parameters and a detection speed of 69.89 FPS, achieving a good balance between detection accuracy and real-time performance. Compared with mainstream algorithms such as Faster R–CNN, SSD, YOLOv8n, and YOLOv10n, the proposed model exhibits stronger robustness and better detection performance under simulated complex working conditions, and can provide effective technical support for intelligent coal washing in mines.

Introduction

The coal gangue generated during coal mining is the main solid pollutant. If mixed with raw coal, it will not only reduce the quality of coal combustion, but also increase the pollution emissions during subsequent utilization. Therefore, achieving accurate identification of coal gangue is of great significance for the coal washing and processing process. −

The traditional methods for identifying coal gangue mainly include density sorting, radiographic testing, and manual picking. − Although they have a certain degree of accuracy, they generally have problems such as low efficiency, high cost, complex operation, and limited adaptability.

In recent years, image recognition technology based on deep learning has been widely applied in the field of coal gangue target detection. Li Na et al. proposed a lightweight coal gangue recognition method based on FNF-YOLO. Through feature fusion optimization, lightweight detection head, and improved loss function, the mAP was increased by 3.5% while reducing 50% of parameters and 45.7% of computation, achieving more efficient and accurate detection performance. Li Deyong et al. proposed a real-time coal gangue detection model BRS-YOLOv10n for complex underground environments in coal mines. By integrating BiFormer visual converter, RepLKNet super large convolution kernel, SKNet dynamic attention mechanism, and InnerCIoU loss function, the model achieves an average accuracy of 92.3% and a detection speed of 62.6 FPS, significantly better than existing mainstream methods, while improving feature extraction and convergence efficiency. Kingdom Law et al. proposed an intelligent recognition model for coal gangue based on CFS-YOLO. By introducing ConvNeXt V2 feature extraction module, Focal EIOU loss function, and SimAM attention mechanism, the model achieved an average detection accuracy of 90.2% and real-time detection performance of 90.09 FPS in complex coal mine environments, significantly improving the robustness and efficiency of coal gangue recognition. Zhang Lei et al. constructed the YOLOv5s SDE model, which introduced SE module, depthwise separable convolution, and EIoU loss function. By reducing 22.2% of parameters and 24.1% of computation, the model achieved an average accuracy of 92.5% and a detection speed of 59.9 FPS, effectively improving the real-time detection performance of belt conveyor coal gangue under complex working conditions. Ge Qingnan et al. proposed a coal gangue recognition method based on the KRB-YOLOv5s algorithm. Through K-means++anchor box optimization and the introduction of RepLKNet large kernel convolution and BiFPN feature fusion network, an average detection accuracy of 94.5% was achieved in complex coal mine environments, which was 3.3 percentage points higher than the benchmark model and effectively improved the robustness and accuracy of coal gangue sorting.

Considering the changeable environment such as high concentration of coal dust, complex lighting conditions and various shapes of coal gangue in actual coal mine production, the existing coal gangue recognition methods are difficult to take into account the requirements of detection speed and accuracy. Therefore, based on YOLOv12, this article adopts a lightweight backbone network EfficientNetV1 to optimize the feature extraction structure, improve computational efficiency, and reduce parameter count; Introducing a multiscale attention feature pyramid network (MAFPN) to enhance the model’s multiscale feature fusion capability; Adopting an adaptive spatial feature fusion detection head (Detect_ASFF) to further optimize target localization and classification accuracy; And by incorporating multiscale channel attention (MCA) mechanism, the feature expression ability of the model under complex working conditions is enhanced.

EMAM-YOLO Model

Overall Architecture

The EMAM-YOLO algorithm is a network optimized on the YOLOv12n model, and its main network structure is shown in Figure . The model mainly consists of four parts: input terminal, backbone network, neck network, and head.

EMAM-YOLO network model structure.

Despite its efficiency on general tasks, native YOLOv12n has four specific drawbacks for coal-gangue detection: (1) its backbone lacks lightweight depthwise separable convolutions and channel attention, leading to parameter redundancy and weak fine-grained feature extraction; (2) its FPN uses simple summation for multiscale fusion, failing to handle large scale variation and severe occlusion; (3) its detection head applies fixed spatial weight fusion, which cannot adaptively focus on deformed or occluded gangue targets; (4) its single-scale channel attention misses cross-scale dependencies, reducing feature selection robustness under complex lighting and dust.

To address these issues, the core improvement work of this model includes: ①adopting a lightweight backbone network EfficientNetV1 to optimize the feature extraction structure, and through its efficient deep separable convolution and channel attention mechanism, significantly reducing the number of parameters while enhancing feature representation ability, improving computational efficiency and model generalization performance; ②introducing the Multi Scale Attention Feature Pyramid Network (MAFPN), through bidirectional cross scale connections and attention weighted fusion mechanism, enhances the semantic information interaction between features at different levels, effectively improving the model’s multiscale object detection capability in complex scenes; ③design an adaptive spatial feature fusion detection head (Detect_ASFF) that uses a dynamic weight learning strategy to adaptively adjust the spatial contribution of features at different scales, optimize target localization accuracy and classification confidence, and enhance the robustness of the model to occluded and deformed targets; ④integrating multiscale channel attention (MCA) mechanism, capturing cross scale channel dependencies through parallel multi branch channel modeling, adaptively focusing on key feature channels and suppressing redundant information, further enhancing feature selection and representation capabilities. Through the collaborative optimization of the above modules, the model achieves a better balance between computational efficiency, multiscale fusion accuracy, and feature discrimination ability.

EfficientNet Backbone Network

With the development of convolutional neural networks, the model size continues to expand, but hardware memory limitations require better efficiency while pursuing higher accuracy. Traditional convolutional neural networks such as AlexNet, GoogleNet, SENet, etc., although their accuracy continues to improve, have too many parameters and face hardware bottlenecks. Therefore, EfficientNet aims to study the model efficiency of convolutional neural networks that can surpass existing accuracy, achieved through model scaling. EfficientNet proposes a new composite scaling method that uses composite coefficients to uniformly scale the width, depth, and resolution of the network. The specific formula is 1 depth:d=αϕ 2 width:w=βϕ 3 resolution:r=γϕ

Among them, α, β, and γ are constants determined through small grid search, and satisfy the conditions of α·β 2·γ 2 ≈ 2, α ≥ 1, β ≥ 1, and γ ≥ 1. This method is based on the observation that there is a correlation between network width, depth, and resolution. Balancing the scaling of these three dimensions is crucial for improving model performance, while traditional single dimensional scaling methods have limitations.

MAFPN Neck Structure

The PAFPN structure used in traditional YOLO series algorithms has limitations in multiscale feature fusion: first, it lacks multiscale fusion across resolution layers. PAFPN mainly integrates multiscale feature maps and ignores shallow and low spatial information (such as the importance of P3 layer for small targets), resulting in insufficient small target detection capability. Second, the small target detection path is single. Only processing small object detection layers through a one-way top-down path and two modules limits the model’s ability to learn subtle features. To address the aforementioned issues, MAFPN enhances shallow information preservation and cross layer feature interaction by introducing bidirectional connections and multi branch assisted fusion, thereby improving multiscale object detection performance.

MAFPN, as the neck structure of YOLO detector, consists of two core paths: ① bottom up path: using SAF module to extract multiscale features (P2–P5) of the backbone network, perform preliminary auxiliary fusion in the shallow neck layer, and preserve shallow spatial information. ② Top down pathway: By establishing dense connections in the deep layers of the neck through the AAF module, features from different resolution layers (such as shallow high-resolution layers, low resolution layers, same level layers, and front layers) are aggregated to guide the detection head to output multiscale information. Detect_ASFF Detection Head

In object detection, handling multiscale objects is a challenging problem. Although feature pyramids or multilevel feature pyramids are commonly used methods to solve multiscale problems, the inconsistency between features of different scales in single-stage detectors limits their performance improvement. To address this issue, the adaptive spatial feature fusion (ASFF) module has been proposed. The structure is shown in Figure .

Structural schematic of Detect_ASFF.

In YOLO, there are three feature layers with different resolutions and channel numbers. For a certain layer l, adjust the features x n of other layers n (n ≠ l) to have the same shape as x l. For upsampling, first apply a 1 × 1 convolutional layer to compress the number of channels, and then use interpolation to improve the resolution; For downsampling (such as 1/2 scale), use a 3 × 3 convolutional layer (with a step size of 2) to simultaneously modify the number of channels and resolution; for downsampling at a 1/4 ratio, add a 2-step max pooling layer before the 2-step convolution.

Let the adjusted feature vector be xijn→l , and the fusion formula is 6 yijl=αijl·xij1→l+βijl·xij2→l+γijl·xij3→l

Among them, α ij l , β ij l , and γ ij l are spatial importance weights for network adaptive learning, and α ij l +β ij l +γ ij l = 1, α ij l , β ij l , γ ij l ∈[0,1]. Calculate the weight scalar graphs λ α l , λ β l , and λ γ l from x 1→l, x 2→l, and x 3→l using a 1 × 1 convolutional layer, and learn them through standard backpropagation.

MCA Attention Mechanism

The core component of MCANet is Multi Scale Cross Axis Attention (MCA). The structure is shown in Figure .

Structural schematic of MCA.

For axial attention, it decomposes self-attention into two parts, each responsible for computing self-attention along the horizontal or vertical dimension. Based on this, Axial DeepLab can sequentially aggregate features along the horizontal and vertical directions, making it possible to capture global information. Axial attention is more efficient than self-attention, reducing computational complexity from O (HW × HW) to O (HW × (H + W)). However, in many image processing tasks, the data set is relatively small, and axial attention is difficult to achieve long–distance interaction, which is not conducive to capturing the spatial structure or shape required in the task.

MCA is a multiscale cross axis attention algorithm, which is divided into two parallel branches to calculate horizontal and vertical axis attention. Each branch consists of three 1D convolutions with different kernel sizes, used to encode multiscale contextual information along one spatial dimension, and then aggregate features along another spatial dimension through cross axis attention. Taking the top branch as an example, given the feature map F (the combination of the last three stages of the encoder feature map), three parallel 1D convolutions are used to encode it. The output is fused by summing and fed into a 1 × 1 convolution, with the formula is 7 Fx=Conv1×1(∑i=02Conv1Dix(Norm(F)))

Among them, Conv1D x i (·)­represents 1D convolution along the x-axis dimension, norm (·) is layer normalization, and F x is the output. For 1D convolution, set the kernel size to 1 × 7, 1 × 11, and 1 × 21. The output F y of the bottom branch can be obtained in a similar way. For the F x of the top branch, it is fed into the y-axis attention. To better utilize the multiscale convolution features from two spatial directions, the cross attention between F x and F y is calculated using F x as the key sum matrix and F y as the query matrix. The calculation process is F T = MHCA y (F y , F x , F x ), where MHCAy (·,·,·) represents the multi head cross attention along the x-axis. The bottom branch encodes the context along the y-axis in a similar way, i.e. F B = MHCA x (F x , F y , F y ), where MHCA x (·,·,·) represents multi head cross attention along the y-axis. The collection of coal gangue samples comes from an experimental device independently built in the laboratory, as shown in Figure . Data Collection and Processing

The experimental data is sourced from a self-made coal gangue data set in the laboratory. The coal gangue samples were collected from a mine in Zhungeer Banner, Inner Mongolia. During the collection process, 500 images were obtained by changing the placement of coal and gangue, adjusting the light source, selecting different sizes and shapes of coal and gangue, and adjusting the quantity of coal and gangue. Model Evaluation Indicators

In order to systematically and quantitatively evaluate the comprehensive performance of the model, this article selects six key indicators from three core dimensions: detection accuracy, model complexity, and inference efficiency.

In terms of detection accuracy, precision (P %) and recall (R %) are used to measure the accuracy of the model’s prediction results and its ability to detect all real targets, respectively; further introduction of mean average precision (mAP) for comprehensive evaluation, where mAP50% reflects the average accuracy at an intersection to union ratio threshold of 0.5, while mAP50–95% imposes stricter and more comprehensive requirements on the localization accuracy of bounding boxes by calculating the average value at multiple thresholds ranging from 0.5 to 0.95.

In terms of model complexity, measuring the size and computational burden of the model through parameter count (Params/M) is key to evaluating whether the model is easy to store and deploy. Finally, the frame rate per second (FPS/F·S1–) serves as a visual indicator to measure the actual inference speed of the model, directly determining whether it can meet the real-time requirements in real-world applications. This set of indicators together constitutes a complete evaluation system that objectively reflects the balance between accuracy, efficiency, and practicality of the model. The mathematical expressions for each indicator are as follows. 9 P=TPTP+FP 10 R=TPTP+FN 11 AP=∫01P(R)dR 12 mAP=∑iCAPiC

Among them, TP (True Positive) represents positive examples that have been correctly classified, known as true examples; FP (False Positive) represents positive cases that have been misclassified, known as false positives or false positives; FN (False Negative) refers to negative examples that have been misclassified, known as false negative examples or missed detections; C represents the number of categories; I represents the i-th category.

Module C (Detect_ASFF detection head) exhibits interesting characteristics: the recall rate R has increased to 92.09% (+0.66%), but the accuracy rate P has slightly decreased (89.70% vs 91.15%), and mAP50–95 has only increased by 0.66%. This indicates that ASFF’s multiscale feature fusion helps recall more targets, but may introduce some false positives.

When module D (MCA attention mechanism) is used alone, the effect is significant: the accuracy P reaches 93.48% (+2.33%), the recall R increases from 91.43% to 91.46%, and mAP50 reaches 96.80%. This indicates that MCA can effectively suppress background noise, improve detection confidence, and enhance feature discrimination while maintaining recall.

In the dual module combination, the A + B combination performed the most outstandingly: although the parameter count was reduced to 1.31 M (a decrease of 79.8% compared to using A alone) and the FPS increased to 99.13, mAP50 slightly decreased to 96.82%. This verifies that the lightweight design of MAFPN can effectively compress the feature dimension of EfficientNetV1, achieving a balance between accuracy and efficiency. The A + C combination maintains a high mAP50–95 (80.31%) while the parameter count is only 2.08 M, demonstrating good compatibility between ASFF and EfficientNetV1. The A + D combination achieved 78.01% mAP50–95 with only 1.49 M parameters, indicating that MCA attention can efficiently compress features.

In the three module combination, the A + B + C combination achieves the best comprehensive performance: mAP50–95 reaches 81.12%, 4.99% higher than the baseline, the accuracy rate P reaches 92.69%, and the recall rate R reaches 94.14%. The parameter size of 2.59 M is moderate, and the FPS of 52.78 meets the real-time detection requirements. This indicates a synergistic effect among the three modules: EfficientNetV1 extracts rich features, MAFPN efficiently fuses multiscale information, and ASFF optimizes detection head feature selection. The performance of A + B + D combination is slightly lower than A + B + C, but the FPS is higher (68.85 vs 52.78), indicating that MCA attention has better computational efficiency while maintaining accuracy.

The A + B + C + D complete model achieves optimal detection performance: mAP50 reaches 97.75%, mAP50–95 reaches 81.17%, and the recall rate R reaches 94.47%. Compared with the baseline, mAP50–95 increased by 5.04%, accuracy increased by 1.02%, and recall increased by 3.04%. The parameter count of 2.59 M only increased by 0.03 M compared to the baseline, and the FPS of 69.89 maintained good real-time performance. This indicates that the four modules have a positive synergistic effect: EfficientNetV1 provides a high-quality feature foundation, MAFPN achieves efficient feature pyramid construction, ASFF optimizes multiscale feature fusion, and MCA attention mechanism enhances key region response.

This ablation study provides empirical evidence for the modular improvement of YOLOv12, demonstrating that by reasonably combining lightweight backbone networks, efficient feature pyramids, and attention mechanisms, detection accuracy can be significantly improved while maintaining real-time performance.

Conclusion

(1)This paper proposes an EMAM-YOLO coal-gangue detection model for complex underground mining environments. The primary technical contribution is 2-fold: first, the novel design of an adaptive spatial feature fusion detection head (Detect_ASFF) that learns dynamic spatial weightsrepresenting a structural innovation within the detection head; second, the specific combination and synergistic interaction of four complementary modules (EfficientNetV1, MAFPN, Detect_ASFF, and MCA), each targeting a distinct drawback of the native YOLOv12n for coal-gangue detection. The experimental results showed that the average accuracy (mAP50–95) of the model reached 81.17%, with an accuracy rate of 92.17% and a recall rate of 94.47%. The detection speed was 69.89 FPS, which was 5.04% higher than the original YOLOv12n in terms of average accuracy. It can accurately and efficiently complete the detection task of coal and gangue.(2)This article systematically optimizes YOLOv12n by introducing a lightweight EfficientNetV1 backbone network, a multiscale attention feature pyramid network (MAFPN), an adaptive spatial feature fusion detection head (Detect_ASFF), and a multiscale channel attention (MCA) mechanism. Compared with existing mainstream object detection algorithms, the EMAM-YOLO model can more accurately identify coal and gangue in simulated complex environments such as low light, multiple targets, and different degrees of occlusion, significantly reducing missed and false detection rates, and effectively improving the robustness and accuracy of the model in practical application scenarios.(3)The EMAM-YOLO model ensures high detection accuracy while controlling the parameter count at 2.59 M, balancing model performance and lightweight requirements. However, considering the resource constraints of underground mobile platforms or edge computing equipment, future research can further explore model compression and pruning technology, or introduce more efficient convolution operators, in order to continuously improve the detection speed of models and better adapt to the hardware environment of intelligent sorting in coal mines under the premise of maintaining accuracy.

State estimation and dynamic compensation cooperative sliding mode control for space flexible manipulators
Authors: Juan Wang, Aiping Wang, Beibei Wang, Yuanqi Chen, Liping Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn response to the issue of total interference suppression in space flexible manipulator systems, a state estimation and dynamic compensation cooperative sliding mode control strategy is proposed, based on a neural network-based disturbance observer and a sliding mode observer. This approach leverages the approximation capability of neural networks to estimate and compensate for the system’s composite uncertainties in real-time. By combining a nonsingular terminal sliding mode control law, it ensures that the system’s state converges to the equilibrium point within a finite time, significantly improving the system’s tracking accuracy and robustness against disturbances. Considering the practical scenario where the system’s states may not be fully measurable, a sliding mode observer is designed to accurately reconstruct the system’s unknown states. Based on these estimated values, a cooperative sliding mode controller is designed. This cooperative control scheme effectively addresses the control challenges when the system’s states are partially unmeasurable. Simulation results show that the system maintains excellent dynamic performance and stability, even in the presence of nonlinear factors such as friction.Keywords: Neural network disturbance observer, Sliding mode observer, Nonsingular terminal sliding mode control, Cooperative controlSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

With the rapid development of aerospace technology, space missions such as on-orbit servicing and space debris removal have imposed extremely high demands on the performance of robotic manipulators1–3. Due to the use of lightweight materials, space manipulators exhibit flexible vibration characteristics, which significantly increase the difficulty of achieving high-precision control4. To address these challenges, cooperative control strategies in the field of unmanned systems have provided new insights in recent years: Yang et al.5 proposed a pseudo-trajectory homotopy method for information collection by underwater unmanned marine vehicles; Jin et al.6 investigated path planning for collaborative data acquisition by heterogeneous unmanned aerial vehicles; Zhu et al.7 designed a hybrid event-triggered control strategy for cooperative positioning of multiple unmanned surface vehicles; Li et al.8 studied prescribed-time consensus for multi-agent systems under input saturation; and Huang et al.9 proposed a formation safety control approach combining graph theory with reinforcement learning. However, beyond cooperative issues, the control performance of space flexible manipulators is further constrained by compound uncertainties such as unmodeled dynamics, parameter perturbations, and time-varying disturbances. Additionally, nonlinear factors like joint friction and backlash further exacerbate the difficulty of achieving high-precision control10,11.

To cope with the aforementioned challenges, scholars domestically and internationally have proposed various control strategies. Traditional methods, such as PID control12, feature a simple structure but struggle to handle nonlinearities and time-varying disturbances. Fuzzy control13, while adept at processing imprecise information, relies heavily on expert knowledge for its performance. Conventional sliding mode control (SMC)14 is renowned for its strong robustness but suffers from inherent chattering. Adaptive control15 can adjust parameters online but often responds insufficiently to sudden disturbances. These methods find it difficult to simultaneously and effectively address parameter perturbations and complex external disturbances. If such uncertainties are not properly resolved, they will directly lead to a decline in system tracking accuracy and even threaten closed-loop stability.

Recently, observer-based compensation methods have shown great promise. In terms of disturbance estimation, neural network-based disturbance observers16–18 have garnered significant attention for their strong nonlinear approximation abilities. Meanwhile, fuzzy disturbance observers19 and predefined-time20 or high-order nonlinear disturbance observers21,22 have also been developed to achieve rapid and precise disturbance estimation. Regarding state reconstruction, sliding mode observers23 and their high-order variants24,25 have been successfully employed to reconstruct unknown system states, leveraging their finite-time convergence characteristics. The integration of sliding mode differentiators with state observers has further improved the capability to extract derivative signals from noisy data26. At the controller design level, nonsingular terminal sliding mode control (NTSMC) addresses the singularity problem of traditional terminal sliding mode by introducing nonlinear sliding surfaces, ensuring finite-time convergence of the system, with continuous innovations in related designs27–29. Moreover, fast terminal sliding mode surfaces constructed using hyperbolic tangent functions can significantly accelerate convergence speeds when the system is far from the equilibrium point30,31.

Although the aforementioned observers and advanced control methods have achieved success individually, organically integrating them to leverage their synergistic advantages remains a significant challenge. Existing combined schemes often suffer from structural complexity and heavy computational burdens. Disturbance observers and state observers are typically designed independently, failing to fully exploit their synergistic estimation advantages. Consequently, implementation costs and real-time performance in practical engineering applications have become bottlenecks that urgently need to be resolved.

In light of this, this paper proposes a sliding mode control strategy featuring synergistic state estimation and dynamic compensation to overcome the aforementioned limitations. The core contributions of this work are as follows:

We design an estimation framework where an RBF Neural Network (RBFNN) disturbance observer works in synergy with a Sliding Mode Observer (SMO). This architecture leverages the feedforward compensation capability of the RBFNN and the feedback estimation ability of the SMO to achieve precise estimation of compound uncertainties and finite-time reconstruction of unknown system states.

This architecture leverages the feedforward compensation capability of the RBFNN and the feedback estimation ability of the SMO to achieve precise estimation of compound uncertainties and finite-time reconstruction of unknown system states.

We combined the aforementioned collaborative estimator with the nonsingular terminal sliding mode control (NTSMC) to ensure that the system state could converge to the equilibrium point within a finite time, even in the presence of nonlinear factors such as friction.

Figures 4 and 5 respectively show the trajectory tracking response curves of RN versus RSN, and RSS versus RSN for Joint 1 and Joint 2. To quantitatively evaluate the convergence speed of each controller, the settling time is defined as the shortest time required for the system response to first enter and remain within the ± 5% error band of the steady-state value.

From Table 3, it can be seen that the settling times of the RSN scheme for Joint 1 and Joint 2 are 0.75 s and 0.70 s, respectively. Compared with the RN scheme (0.80 s and 0.75 s), these are reduced by 6.3% and 6.7%, respectively. Compared with the RSS scheme (0.90 s and 0.85 s), they are reduced by 16.7% and 17.6%, respectively. These results validate the advantage of the non-singular terminal sliding mode surface design in accelerating the system’s convergence speed: Although the RN scheme incorporates neural network disturbance compensation, it lacks the state reconstruction capability of a sliding mode observer, resulting in a slightly slower convergence speed; the RSS scheme employs an exponential reaching law, leading to a relatively smoother convergence process; in contrast, the RSN scheme, by utilizing the sliding mode observer to reconstruct system states in real time, combined with the finite-time convergence characteristic of the non-singular terminal sliding mode surface, achieves the fastest dynamic response.

From the data in Table 4, it can be observed that the vibration reduction rates of the RN scheme for Joint 1 and Joint 2 are 57.2% and 61.4%, respectively. This indicates its limited capability in suppressing high-frequency vibrations induced by external noise and unmodeled dynamics. The RSS scheme demonstrates significant improvement, with vibration reduction rates reaching 93.3% and 91.7% for the two joints. This suggests that the introduction of the sliding mode observer greatly enhances the accuracy of system state reconstruction, thereby effectively suppressing vibrations caused by state estimation errors. The RSN scheme performs the best, achieving vibration reduction rates as high as 98.3% for both joints. Compared with the RN scheme, the vibration reduction rate is improved by an average of approximately 39%; compared with the RSS scheme, it is improved by an average of about 6%. This advantage is attributed to the synergistic mechanism of the RSN scheme: the sliding mode observer provides accurate state estimation, the neural network disturbance observer compensates for composite uncertainties in real time, and the non-singular terminal sliding mode surface ensures the smooth convergence of system states to the equilibrium point within a finite time.

As shown in Fig. 7, the RSS controller suffers from high-frequency chattering due to the exponential reaching law, leading to pronounced torque oscillations. The RN controller reduces chattering slightly but exhibits larger torque amplitudes due to weaker disturbance rejection. In contrast, the RSN controller, benefiting from accurate disturbance estimation via the sliding-mode observer and the finite-time convergence property of the nonsingular terminal sliding surface, achieves smooth and properly scaled torque outputs.

To sum up, the RSN scheme integrates RBFDOB-based model uncertainty compensation, SMO-based external disturbance estimation, and NTSMC-based finite-time convergence to form a multilayer disturbance-rejection architecture. It outperforms the comparative methods in tracking accuracy, response speed, robustness, and control smoothness. Particularly in terms of vibration reduction, the RSN scheme achieves an attenuation rate of nearly 98%, demonstrating its effectiveness in handling the complex vibration environment faced by space flexible manipulators during on-orbit servicing, and highlighting its significant potential for engineering applications. The RN controller, relying on a single observer, lacks robustness against compound disturbances, while the RSS controller cannot ensure finite-time stability due to its reaching-law design. The simulation results verify that the proposed “observer + finite-time control” strategy is effective and provides a promising approach for high-precision motion control.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study verified the effectiveness of the collaborative strategy of state estimation and dynamic compensation in addressing the challenges of trajectory tracking control for robotic arms, providing a promising solution for high-precision and high-robustness motion control.

Future work will proceed in the following directions. First, further optimizing the parameter adaptation laws for both the neural network and the sliding mode observer to reduce sensitivity to controller parameters. Second, investigating the application of this cooperative control framework to more complex scenarios, such as those involving input saturation, actuator faults, or on-orbit servicing tasks. Third, exploring online tuning of the composite controller using intelligent methods like reinforcement learning to further enhance the system’s adaptability and overall performance.

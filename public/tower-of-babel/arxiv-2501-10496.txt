Extension of Symmetrized Neural Network Operators with Fractional and Mixed Activation Functions
Rômulo Damasclin Chaves dos Santos, Jorge Henrique de Oliveira Sales

Abstract
We propose a novel extension to symmetrized neural network operators by incorporating fractional and mixed activation functions. This study addresses the limitations of existing models in approximating higher-order smooth functions, particularly in complex and high-dimensional spaces. Our framework introduces a fractional exponent in the activation functions, allowing adaptive non-linear approximations with improved accuracy. We define new density functions based on q q -deformed and θ \theta -parametrized logistic models and derive advanced Jackson-type inequalities that establish uniform convergence rates. Additionally, we provide a rigorous mathematical foundation for the proposed operators, supported by numerical validations demonstrating their efficiency in handling oscillatory and fractional components. The results extend the applicability of neural network approximation theory to broader functional spaces, paving the way for applications in solving partial differential equations and modeling complex systems. Keywords: Neural Network Operators. Fractional Activation Functions. Symmetrized Operators. Approximation Theory.

Mathematical Background

Let
f\in C([-a,a],\mathbb{C})
. A key component in our analysis is the modulus of continuity
\omega(f,t)
, defined as:

\omega(f,t)=\sup_{|x-y|\leq t}|f(x)-f(y)|,\quad t>0.

(1)

For fractional activation functions, we define:

\phi_{q,\theta,\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{1+q^{A\theta|x|^{\alpha}}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\;q,\theta>0,\;\alpha\in(0,1].

(2)

The associated symmetrized density function is given by:

W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\phi_{q,\theta,\alpha}(x+1)-\phi_{q,\theta,\alpha}(x-1)\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(3)

This function satisfies the normalization condition:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(x)\,dx=1.

(4)

We also rely on the concept of Jackson-type inequalities, which relate the approximation error to the smoothness of the target function:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq C\omega_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right),

(5)

where
\omega_{2}(f,t)
is the second-order modulus of continuity defined as:

\omega_{2}(f,t)=\sup_{0<h\leq t}\|\Delta_{h}^{2}f(x)\|,

(6)

with
\Delta_{h}^{2}f(x)=f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)
, and
C
is a constant depending on the parameters of the density function. To further enhance the mathematical rigor, let’s introduce the concept of the
L^{p}
-norm and the Hölder continuity.

The
L^{p}
-norm of a function
f
is defined as:

\|f\|_{p}=\left(\int_{-a}^{a}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p},\quad 1\leq p<\infty.

(7)

For
p=\infty
, the
L^{\infty}
-norm is given by:

\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}|f(x)|.

(8)

A function
f
is said to be Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma
if there exists a constant
M
such that:

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq M|x-y|^{\gamma},\quad\forall x,y\in[-a,a].

(9)

The Hölder space
C^{k,\gamma}([-a,a])
consists of functions whose
k
-th derivative is Hölder continuous with exponent
\gamma
.

Additionally, we can consider the Sobolev space
W^{k,p}([-a,a])
, which is the space of functions whose weak derivatives up to order
k
are in
L^{p}([-a,a])
. The Sobolev norm is defined as:

\|f\|_{W^{k,p}}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

(10)

where
D^{\alpha}f
denotes the weak derivative of
f
of order
\alpha
.

These concepts provide a more comprehensive framework for analyzing the smoothness and approximation properties of functions in
C([-a,a],\mathbb{C})
.

Main Results

3.1
Jackson-Type Inequalities

Let
S_{n}(f;x)
denote the fractional symmetrized neural network operator:

S_{n}(f;x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).

(11)

Theorem 3.1

(Jackson Inequality for Fractional Operators)
.

Let
f\in C^{2}([-a,a],\mathbb{C})
. Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[-a,a]
:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq C\omega_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right),

(12)

where
\omega_{2}(f,t)
is the second-order modulus of continuity.

Proof.

To prove the Jackson inequality for the fractional operator
S_{n}(f;x)
, we start by considering the Taylor expansion of
f
around
x
:

f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)=f(x)+f^{\prime}(x)\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2},

(13)

where
\xi_{k}
is some point between
x
and
\frac{k}{n}
.

Substituting this expansion into the definition of
S_{n}(f;x)
, we get:

\begin{array}[]{l}S_{n}(f;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[f(x)+f^{\prime}(x)\left(\dfrac{k}{n}-x\right)+\dfrac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\dfrac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}\right]W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=\\
\\
f(x)\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)+f^{\prime}(x)\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{k}{n}-x\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\,+\\
\\
\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\dfrac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).\\
\\
\end{array}

(14)

Using the normalization condition of
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=1,

(15)

and the symmetry property:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=0,

(16)

we simplify the expression to:

S_{n}(f;x)=f(x)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).

(17)

Therefore, the error term is:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=\left\|\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\right\|.

(18)

To bound this error, we use the second-order modulus of continuity
\omega_{2}(f,t)
:

\omega_{2}(f,t)=\sup_{0<h\leq t}\|\Delta_{h}^{2}f(x)\|,

(19)

where
\Delta_{h}^{2}f(x)=f(x+h)-2f(x)+f(x-h)
.

Since
f\in C^{2}([-a,a],\mathbb{C})
, we have:

\left|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\right|\leq\omega_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right).

(20)

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{c}\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq\frac{1}{2}{\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}}\left|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\right|\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\\
\\
\dfrac{1}{2}\,\omega_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right){\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).\\
\\
\end{array}\end{array}

(21)

Finally, using the boundedness of the moments of
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}},

(22)

we obtain:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq C\omega_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right),

(23)

where
C
is a constant depending on the parameters of the density function
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
.
∎

3.2
Uniform Convergence

Theorem 3.2

(Uniform Convergence)
.

The operator
S_{n}(f;x)
converges uniformly to
f(x)
as
n\to\infty
for all
f\in C([-a,a],\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

To prove the uniform convergence of
S_{n}(f;x)
to
f(x)
, we need to show that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=0.

(24)

We start by considering the definition of
S_{n}(f;x)
:

S_{n}(f;x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).

(25)

Using the modulus of continuity
\omega(f,t)
, we can write:

\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-f(x)\right]W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\right\|\leq\\
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-f(x)\right|W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\\
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\omega\left(f,\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).\\
\\
\end{array}

(26)

Since
f
is continuous on the compact interval
[-a,a]
, it is uniformly continuous. Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that:

\omega(f,\delta)<\epsilon.

(27)

For sufficiently large
n
, we have
\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|<\delta
for all
k
such that
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)
is significantly non-zero. Thus,

\omega\left(f,\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|\right)<\epsilon.

(28)

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon\,W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=\\
\\
\epsilon\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=\epsilon.\\
\\
\end{array}

(29)

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=0.

(30)

Hence,
S_{n}(f;x)
converges uniformly to
f(x)
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

3.4
Stability of the Operators

Theorem 3.4

(Stability of Fractional Symmetrized Neural Network Operators)
.

Let
f,g\in C([-a,a],\mathbb{C})
. Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[-a,a]
, the fractional symmetrized neural network operator
S_{n}(f;x)
satisfies:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-S_{n}(g;x)\|\leq C\|f-g\|,

(38)

where
C
is a constant depending on the parameters of the density function
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
.

Proof.

To prove the stability of the fractional symmetrized neural network operator
S_{n}(f;x)
, we start by considering the definition of
S_{n}(f;x)
:

S_{n}(f;x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).

(39)

Similarly, for
g

S_{n}(g;x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}g\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).

(40)

We need to show that:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-S_{n}(g;x)\|\leq C\|f-g\|.

(41)

Consider the difference:

\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-S_{n}(g;x)\|=\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-g\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right]W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\right\|\leq\\
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-g\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\\
\\
\|f-g\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).\\
\\
\end{array}

(42)

Using the normalization condition of
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=1,

(43)

we obtain:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-S_{n}(g;x)\|\leq\|f-g\|.

(44)

Thus, the stability of the operator
S_{n}(f;x)
is established with
C=1
.

\|S_{n}(f;x)-S_{n}(g;x)\|\leq C\|f-g\|,

(45)

where
C
is a constant depending on the parameters of the density function
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
.
∎

4
Results

The theoretical framework introduced in this study was rigorously analyzed and applied to evaluate the performance of the proposed fractional symmetrized neural network operators. These operators were formulated to address a variety of functions, including those with fractional derivatives and oscillatory components, achieving faster convergence rates compared to classical symmetrized operators.

A key finding was that the incorporation of fractional exponents in the activation functions enhanced the operators’ adaptability to complex and high-dimensional function spaces. This adaptability was reflected in superior approximation accuracy, particularly for functions exhibiting irregular or oscillatory behaviors. The derived Jackson-type inequalities provided a strong mathematical basis, ensuring uniform convergence and stability across diverse functional spaces.

The theoretical results confirmed that the error behavior of the operators is directly proportional to the modulus of continuity of the target function. This establishes that the proposed operators are not only mathematically robust but also versatile for a broad spectrum of applications.

This study introduced a novel extension to symmetrized neural network operators by incorporating fractional and mixed activation functions. The theoretical framework developed here addresses the limitations of existing models in approximating higher-order smooth functions, particularly in complex and high-dimensional spaces.

The key contributions include the definition of new density functions based on

These results significantly enhance the theoretical understanding and applicability of neural network operators in handling functions with oscillatory and fractional components.

The findings broaden the scope of neural network approximation theory, enabling its application to diverse functional spaces and paving the way for advancements in solving partial differential equations and modeling complex systems. Future research will focus on further exploring these applications and developing multi-layer operator architectures to extend the potential of neural network-based approximation methods.

f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)=f(x)+f^{\prime}(x)\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2},

where

is some point between

\begin{array}[]{l}S_{n}(f;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[f(x)+f^{\prime}(x)\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)+\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}\right]W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=\\

\\
\end{array}

(57)

Using the normalization condition of

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=1,

(58)

and the symmetry property:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=0,

\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=\left\|\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\right\|.

\omega_{2}(f,t)=\sup_{0<h\leq t}\|\Delta_{h}^{2}f(x)\|,

(62)

where

(63)

Thus,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{k})\right|\left(\dfrac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\\
\\
\dfrac{1}{2}\,\omega_{2}\left(f,\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).\\
\\
\end{array}

(64)

Finally, using the boundedness of the moments of
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
:

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{k}{n}-x\right)^{2}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}},

(65)

we obtain:

\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq C\omega_{2}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right),

(66)

where
C
is a constant depending on the parameters of the density function
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}
.
∎

C.2
Proof of Uniform Convergence

Proof.

To prove the uniform convergence of
S_{n}(f;x)
to
f(x)
, we need to show that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=0.

(67)

We start by considering the definition of
S_{n}(f;x)
:

S_{n}(f;x)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).

(68)

Using the modulus of continuity
\omega(f,t)
, we can write:

\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=\left\|\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-f(x)\right]W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\right\|\leq\\
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)-f(x)\right|W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)\leq\\
\\
\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\omega\left(f,\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|\right)W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k).\\
\\
\end{array}

(69)

Since
f
is continuous on the compact interval
[-a,a]
, it is uniformly continuous. Therefore, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that:

\omega(f,\delta)<\epsilon.

(70)

For sufficiently large
n
, we have
\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|<\delta
for all
k
such that
W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)
is significantly non-zero. Thus,

\omega\left(f,\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|\right)<\epsilon.

(71)

Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{l}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|\leq\ \displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\epsilon\,\,W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=\\
\\
\epsilon\,\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}W_{q,\theta,\alpha}(nx-k)=\epsilon.\\
\\
\end{array}

(72)

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[-a,a]}\|S_{n}(f;x)-f(x)\|=0.

(73)

Hence,
S_{n}(f;x)
converges uniformly to
f(x)
as
n\to\infty
.
∎

1. BEYOND MEDICATION: PERSON-CENTERED CARE SHOULD LEAD THE MANAGEMENT OF AGITATION IN DEMENTIA

Agitation in dementia is increasingly recognized as a behavioral expression of unmet needs rather than simply a symptom requiring pharmacological suppression. This evidence-based consensus supports person-centered assessment and non-pharmacological approaches as first-line management.1 Individualized music therapy, massage or touch therapy, tailored activities, and multicomponent interventions showed the most consistent benefits, particularly when preceded by structured assessment of patient needs.1

2. EXERCISE MAY NOT BE ENOUGH: LESS SITTING IS LINKED TO LOWER COLORECTAL ADENOMA RISK

Among 5982 asymptomatic adults undergoing health screening, shorter sedentary time was independently associated with a lower risk of colorectal adenoma.2 Participants who sat for less than 300 minutes per day had approximately half the risk compared with those sitting for more than 450 minutes daily. Importantly, higher physical activity did not fully offset the adverse association of prolonged sitting, suggesting that colorectal cancer prevention should emphasize both moving more and sitting less.2

3. HOW PM2.5 HARMS THE OCULAR SURFACE: IL-8 SUPPRESSION IMPAIRS CONJUNCTIVAL REPAIR

This mechanistic study shows that PM2.5 disrupts conjunctival epithelial homeostasis primarily by impairing epithelial migration and wound repair rather than by causing extensive cell death.3 PM2.5 markedly suppressed IL-8, while recombinant IL-8 partially restored epithelial migration and cell viability. The findings identify IL-8-mediated epithelial repair as a potential mechanism linking air pollution to ocular surface injury.3

4. WHO IS AT RISK? REAL-WORLD DATA IDENTIFY PREDICTORS OF RIBOCICLIB-ASSOCIATED HEPATOTOXICITY

In this real-world cohort of patients with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer, ribociclib was associated with a higher risk of hepatotoxicity than palbociclib, particularly during the first 6 months of treatment.4 Liver metastasis, a history of hepatitis B infection, and fatty liver were independently associated with an increased risk. These findings support risk-stratified liver function monitoring, especially in vulnerable patients receiving ribociclib.4

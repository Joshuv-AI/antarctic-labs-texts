High-dose opioids in advanced cancer: use factors–retrospective study
Authors: Wanglian Peng, Xufen Huang, Ran Zou, Qifang Chen, Jialin Ding, Man Jiang, Zhijun Yuan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesTo evaluate factors associated with high-dose opioid use in patients with advanced cancer and examine the effect of high-dose opioid use on patients’ survival.MethodsThis study retrospectively searched the medical records of 416 patients with advanced cancer in a home-based hospice in central China. Age, sex, type of cancer, type of pain, the maximum oral morphine equivalent daily dose, type of opioid, preadmission oral morphine equivalent daily dose, lung cancer or not, bone metastases, opioid switch and survival time were assessed.ResultsThere were 416 subjects included from the 455 eligible participants (91.4%). 80 patients (19.2%) received high-dose opioids at home. Male (OR 2.471; 95% CI 1.054 to 5.792; p=0.037), preadmission morphine equivalent daily dose (OR 1.022; 95% CI 1.016 to 1.028; p=0.000) and the use of morphine at maximum morphine equivalent daily dose (OR 5.123; 95% CI 1.249 to 21.014; p=0.023) were positively predicted high-dose opioid use. No difference in survival was found when comparing the high-dose and very high-dose opioid use groups.ConclusionsMale home-based patients with advanced cancer who use morphine and have greater preadmission opioid doses should be aware of the necessity for high-dose opioid use. Regulated opioid use in adequate amounts does not shorten survival.Keywords: Cancer, Hospice care, Pain, Survivorship, Symptoms and symptom management

Background

Patients with cancer in China continue to increase, with an estimated 4 824 700 new cases and an estimated 2 574 200 deaths of malignant tumours in China in 2022.1 The prevalence of pain remains high, especially in patients with advanced, metastatic and terminal cancer (54.6%).2 The mainstay of treating moderate-to-severe cancer pain is opioid, which works well when taken in appropriate doses.3 In terms of opioid analgesics used to relieve pain, China’s per capita consumption of narcotic drugs from 2016 to 2018 ranked 98th. The determined daily doses per million population are just above the ‘serious underuse’ level.4

In the last three decades, hospice care in China has expanded quickly. Because Chinese people have a strong sense of family, the majority of them wish to pass away at home. Doctors, patients’ families and regulators, however, use opioids with greater caution when not in a medical environment. This results in underdosing and lower life quality.5

This study evaluated the rate of high-dose opioid usage and the factors associated with it in Chinese patients with advanced cancer receiving home-based hospice care to understand the present opioid use status among these patients. The effect of high-dose opioid use on patients’ survival was also investigated in this study.

Methods

Study design and participants

This is a retrospective cohort study. Home-based patients with advanced cancer (defined as metastatic incurable cancer) referred to the Changsha Ning Yang Yuan for cancer pain between 1 January 2021 and 30 December 2021, were eligible for this study. The Changsha Ning Yang Yuan of Hunan Cancer Hospital is a hospice established by the Li Ka Shing Foundation (LKSF) to provide free home-based holistic care for patients with advanced cancer, located in central China.6

Data collection

Data were collected from medical records. Collected data included patient characteristics (age and sex), type of cancer, type of pain (somatic, visceral and neuropathic), the maximum oral morphine equivalent daily dose (OMEDD) during home-based hospice care, which was calculated according to the conversion table of oral or parenteral opioids (oral morphine:oral oxycodone=1:0.5; fentanyl transdermal patch ug/hour every 72 hours=1/2 oral morphine mg/day), type of opioid at maximum OMEDD, preadmission OMEDD, bone metastases and opioid switch. The time between the start of high-dose opioid use and the final death was recorded as survival time.

All data were deidentified to ensure confidentiality, limited to the study group and used for research only.

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Xiangya School of Medicine/Hunan Cancer Hospital, Central South University (number for the approval: KYJJ-2021-157).

Statistical analysis

Patients were assigned to either of the two groups according to maximum OMEDD during home-based hospice care: the high-dose group (OMEDD >299 mg/day)7 and the low-dose group (OMEDD ≤299 mg/day). To determine the categorical variables changes between high-dose and low-dose groups, Pearson’s χ2 test was performed; the t-test was also used to examine the continuous factors. Binary logistic analyses were used to explore the independent relationship between each risk factor and high-dose opioid usage during home-based hospice care. HRs and 95% CIs for the associations between the use of high-dose opioids and covariates were determined.

We separated the patients who used the high-dose opioids into groups based on the maximum OMEDD during home-based hospice care: the high-dose group (MEDD 300–599 mg/day)7 and the very high-dose group (MEDD >599 mg/day).7 To ascertain whether the use of high or very high-dose opioids had an impact on survival, Kaplan-Meier was performed.

A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics V.22.

Results

We tracked 455 patients until 30 December 2022. 27 individuals who did not undergo pain management for cancer were excluded. 12 withdrew from services for reasons such as going out of the scope of services or entering the inpatient system during services. Ultimately, 416 patients’ data were gathered.

In all, 416 individuals between the ages of 28 and 96 were examined; there were 264 males (63.5%). The maximum OMEDD during home-based hospice care of 10–1080 mg was given, while OMEDD ranged from 5 to 800 mg at the time of hospice admission. Lung cancer (n=151, 36.3%), colorectal cancer (n=45, 10.8%) and hepatocellular carcinoma (n=26, 6.3%) were the top three diagnoses. A total of 80 individuals (19.2%) received high-dose opioids.

Statistically significant differences between the high-dose and low-dose groups were found in terms of preadmission OMEDD (p<0.001), bone metastases (p=0.001), type of pain (p<0.001), type of opioid taken at maximal OMEDD (p=0.023) and opioid switch (p=0.002).

The binary logistic regression suggested that male, preadmission OMEDD and the use of morphine at maximum OMEDD positively predicted high-dose opioid usage. Somatic pain and visceral pain negatively predicted high-dose opioid usage, as detailed in table 1.

A total of 80 patients were given high-dose opioids; among them, 54 individuals received high-dose opioids (MEDD 300–599 mg/day) and 26 individuals received very high-dose opioids (MEDD >599 mg/day). Survival time ranged from 15 to 720 days, with a median of 113±14.53 days. The survival time in the high-dose group (MEDD 300–599 mg/day) was 90±20.63 days and 138±54.17 days in the very high-dose group (MEDD >599 mg/day). There was no difference in survival between the two groups.

Discussion

We found that the usage of high-dose opioids was influenced by the characteristics of sex, type of pain, type of opioid used at maximum OMEDD and preadmission OMEDD. This study verified that the use of high-dose opioids did not affect survival.

80 patients (19.2%) with advanced cancer with home-based hospice care received high-dose opioids. This is consistent with known rates of 18.55% among hospitalised clinical care patients abroad.8

Bercovitch and Adunsky found male patients needed somewhat larger opioid doses than female patients (62.5% of high-dose and 71% of very high-dose opioid groups, respectively).7 Our study went a step further and found that males were predictors of high-dose opioid use (OR 2.471; 95% CI 1.054 to 5.792; p=0.037). Ahmed et al demonstrated no significant differences in self-perceived pain between genders in oncology patients.9 Nonetheless, men with comparable conditions are probably going to receive a higher dosage of analgesic prescriptions than women.10

We found the use of morphine at maximum OMEDD was positively associated with high-dose opioid usage. It suggests that morphine is still the first choice when using high doses of opioids. Compared with neuropathic pain, somatic and visceral pain was negatively associated with high-dose MEDD in home-based hospice service.

We discovered that preadmission OMEDD was associated with high-dose opioid administration. This is consistent with a study by Yennurajalingam et al in patients with advanced cancer who received inpatient palliative care.11 Patients with cancer experienced progressively more agony in their final 30 days of life.12 Higher dosages of opioids are eventually required for patients with cancer who are nearing death.13

In this study, we found no difference in survival when comparing the high-dose (MEDD 300–599 mg/day) and very high-dose (MEDD >599 mg/day) opioid usage groups. It implies that survival is unaffected by the proper use of very high-dose opioids for home-based patients with cancer experiencing pain. These findings resonate with clinical experience and findings of other surveys.14 However, inadequate pain management is still present.15 Zheng et al discovered that people with terminal lung cancer in Chinese cities had less access to opioids, and the doctors failed to prescribe an adequate amount of opioids as specified by the prescription.12

Study limitations

There are several restrictions on this study. Since no objective measurements were available, psychological components were not measured. Moreover, the opioid dosage in this study may have been underestimated because the rescue dose for a sudden spike in pain was not calculated. Further research is necessary, as this paper only examined and rated one home-based hospice in central China; a multicentre study is needed.

Conclusions

19.2% of patients with advanced cancer received high-dose opioids at home. The use of high-dose opioids was positively associated with males, higher preadmission OMEDD and the use of morphine at maximum OMEDD. This study verified that the use of very high-dose opioids did not affect survival. This highlights the need, in clinical settings, to explore home-based patients with advanced cancer who are male, with a higher preadmission opioid dose, with the use of morphine and to ensure that they receive the opioid required to relieve pain.

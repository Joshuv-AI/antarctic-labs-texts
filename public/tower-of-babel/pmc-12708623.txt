Adenosine in human atrial fibrillation modulates and re-distributes atria-wide dominant frequencies and directs to reentry ablation sites with improved outcome
Authors: David Calvo, Lucia Salinas, Pablo Martínez-Camblor, María Salgado, Ruth Álvarez-Velasco, Amaia Martínez-León, Minel Soroa, Daniel García-Iglesias, David Filgueiras-Rama, Juan José Gonzalez-Ferrer, Victoria Cañadas, Ricardo Salgado, Alba Cruz, Jorge Quintanilla, Andrés Redondo, Miguel Rodrigo, Julián Pérez-Villacastín, Nicasio Pérez-Castellano, Omer Berenfeld
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Understanding and therapy of persistent atrial fibrillation (AF) are suboptimal partly because the dynamics, mapping, and ablation of rotational activity that has been proposed to drive AF remain elusive. In the present work we challenge AF with Adenosine, an agonist activating the Kir3.x subfamily of inward rectifier potassium channels and known to accelerate rotors, while performing non-invasive panoramic mapping. We studied 25 persistent AF patients who underwent mapping twice at baseline (B1, B2) and again during 12 mg Adenosine (Ado) peak effect. Post-procedure, spatial distributions of dominant frequencies (DFs) and differences (ΔDFs) were analyzed. During baseline, highest 50% DF sites strongly associated with |ΔDF|≤ 0.4 Hz between B1 and B2 and most stable DFs (|ΔDF|≤ 0.1 Hz) had highest DFs. During Adenosine, rotational domains displayed cycle length shortening in agreement with DF increase. However, the DF modulation by Adenosine was heterogeneous and transformed a single to a bi-modal DF distribution. A new DFAdo Stability Index (DFASI) was thereafter calculated at each virtual node as the normalized DFAdo weighted by normalized stability (1-|ΔDF|Norm) to locate regions with the highest acceleration relative to baseline variations. Distributions of DFASI ≥ 0.8 domains and rotational domains approximate each other, but their joint probability was 0.3249 indicating not all DFASI ≥ 0.8 and rotational domains co-localized. On follow up, higher all arrhythmia freedom was observed in patients in which all DFASI ≥ 0.8 domains were ablated than in patients in which not all such domains were ablated. The study proposes a new method for identifying reentries driving AF and a physiologically-based guidance for their ablation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26262-z.

Introduction

The mechanisms underlying AF are still not fully clear and its therapy is suboptimal. The use of Adenosine, an agonist activating the Kir3.x subfamily of inward rectifier potassium channels,1 has been proposed for enhanced understanding of excitation patterns and drivers in AF. In studies with computer simulations2 and experimental optical mapping3 on AF models, increased acetylcholine concentration, that activates the same channels as Adenosine, has been demonstrated to accelerate the dominant frequency (DF) of rotational activity implicated in sustaining the arrhythmia. Subsequent optical and multi-electrode mapping studies in human atria have observed distributed AF driving reentries, with varying levels of stabilities and reduced cycle lengths in the presence of Adenosine.4 Non-invasive and bi-atrial electrocardiogram imaging (ECGi) mapping in AF patients also reveal reentrant and high-frequency domains5,6 correlated with intracardiac full-chamber mapping7 whose cycle lengths are reduced in the presence of Adenosine.8 However data on non-invasive bi-atrial mapping of reentry and atria-wide activation rate distribution during Adenosine, with concomitant ablation and outcomes in persistent AF patients, are scarce. Thus, the two-fold aim of this study is to provide a new atria-wide characterization of the impact of Adenosine on reentrant activity and local activation rate relative to baseline variations, which are typically neglected, and to demonstrate a new translational approach for a robust mapping of the Adenosine impact to potentially guide AF ablation.

Methods

Patients and workflow

The Ethics Committee of the “Principado de Asturias” in Oviedo and “Clínico San Carlos” Hospital in Madrid approved the study and subjects gave written informed consent. Mapping during baseline and adenosine

To establish baseline conditions, atria-wide virtual unipolar EGMs were acquired during 2, Basal 1 and Basal 2, non-invasive mapping stages (B1, B2; ≈ 20 min and a transeptal puncture in between) together with the RA and LA anatomy acquisition (Fig. 1). Thereafter, Penta-Ray® and tetrapolar catheters were stably positioned in the RA and LA appendages (LAA, RAA) respectively to record intracardiac contact bipolar EGMs. Following, 12 mg Adenosine was infused through the femoral vein, and a 3rd non-invasive atria-wide mapping stage (Ado mapping) was obtained together with the LAA and RAA contact bipolar EGMs at around the time of maximal atrioventricular block induced by the Adenosine.

Reentry domains identification

The continuous surface ECG time series were analyzed intra-procedurally on the CardioInsight mapping system to mark sequential TQ segments free of ventricular activity during the 3 mapping stages (B1, B2 and Ado). The marked sequence of TQ segments were processed by built-in algorithms to generate virtual EGM time series (1 kHz sampling rate) in each node of the virtual atria. Thereafter, the CardioInsight system used proprietary algorithms to process the virtual EGMs by means of the Hilbert transform to compute instantaneous phase in each node, to identify the presence and duration of rotational activity, and to present the local density of activity with ≥ 2 consecutive uninterrupted rotations in color-coded composite maps.5,6 Sites with high density of rotations displayed on composite maps overlapping at B1 and B2 locations (by visual inspection and majority of 3 expert observers blind to outcomes) defined reproducible rotational domains and all were considered for ablation. Due to regulatory restrictions, rotational domains identified during Ado mapping were not considered as ablation targets.

Ablation procedures

Circumferential pulmonary veins isolation (CPVI) was accomplished by ablation at the PVs antrum and confirmed by elimination of local bipolar potentials (temperature 43°; 20–35 Watts; Contact force 10–15 g). The B1 and B2 reproducible rotational domains on any PV antrum were targeted by the CPVI. For reproducible rotational domains located outside of the PV antrum, linear ablation or patches were applied by covering the distribution of singularity points (and connecting to unexcitable boundaries when available). All ablations were annotated on the anatomical map created by the intracardiac Carto mapping system. After ablation, external cardioversion was performed if the patient remained in AF.

Post-procedure reentry and frequency analysis

Following the mapping and ablation procedures, virtual EGMs and the atrial geometry were exported for post-procedural analyses of the same TQ segments used to generate intra-procedurally the rotational density maps (Matlab R2019a; Mathworks, Natick, MA). The cycle length, number of consecutive uninterrupted rotations, duration of the rotations, number of rotations per time unit, and the mapping time were calculated for the same TQ intervals analyzed intra-procedurally during the 3 mapping stages.

Custom software was developed to compute DF maps for the virtual EGMs during the same TQ intervals. Each TQ segment (> 0.5 s long) of the exported virtual EGMs was pre-processed by a non-biased 1–20 Hz band-pass filter to minimize baseline wander and high-frequency harmonics. Thereafter the TQ segments were processed to generate DF for each mapping point as follows (See also Fig. S1 in Supplemental Material). Initially a short fast Fourier transformation (SFFT) was implemented on each TQ segment after Hanning windowing and zero padding to a uniform length of 8192 points for all segments.10 The SFFT signal in the frequency domain was then used to generate a power spectrum for each TQ segment. Thereafter the spectra of all the TQ segments in the sequence for each mapping stage were averaged according to the Welch method of weighting each spectra by length of the original TQ segments.11 The DF for each node was defined as the frequency with the highest power in the Welch averaged power spectrum with frequency steps of 0.112 Hz and was color-coded on the CardioInsight atria-wide 3D shell (with nearest-point interpolations between nodes) to create DF maps for the for the B1, B2 and Ado mapping stages. Contact bipolar EGMs recorded at the LAA and RAA during Adenosine mapping were also exported post-procedurally for additional frequency analysis (see Fig. S2 in Supplemental Material).

Virtual EGMs were further processed as follows to analyze atria-wide alterations of the DFs during Adenosine (DFAdo) and the significance of the alteration relative to baseline alterations (DFB1, DFB2). For baseline alterations and stability quantification, the differences of the DFs of each virtual EGM between the 2 basal mapping stages (ΔDF = DFB2-DFB1) were calculated point-by-point first, and thereafter the absolute value of the differences, |ΔDF|, was obtained. Given the variability in fibrillatory dynamics and DFs across the patients,12,13 maps of DFAdo and |ΔDF| for further analysis were normalized for each individual patient to establish a unform scaling between minimal and maximal values common to all patients. Accordingly, the DFAdo map was normalized between 0 and 1, corresponding to the patient minimal and maximal values, respectively, for each patient. In addition, a common point-by-point stability weigh factor for each patient was generated as a normalized stability factor maps presenting the values of 1-|ΔDF|Norm at each point, whereby |ΔDF|Norm was calculated by subtracting from the |ΔDF| the minimal |ΔDF| value in that map, and then dividing by the difference between the maximal and minimal |ΔDF|s in that map. The resulting normalized stability factor maps ranged between 0 and 1 for the least and most basal stable local DFs, respectively. Finaly, to quantify the significance of the alterations in DF during Adenosine effect (DFAdo) we calculated a new parameter termed DFAdo Stability Index (DFASI), that was generated by multiplying point-by-point the normalized DFAdo map by the normalized stability factor 1-|ΔDF|Norm map for that same patient. The resulting DFASI maps thus correspond to the normalized value of the DFAdo weighted by the DF stability at baseline whereby points get the highest values, closest to 1, at locations that were the fastest during Adenosine and the most stable at baseline.

Follow-up

At time of enrolment, 6 follow-up visits were scheduled for the following 2-year period. Holter monitoring (24 h) and resting ECG were conducted at each visit. Antiarrhythmics were suspended after 3 months of blanking period if stable sinus rhythm was maintained. All patients were followed for a minimum of 1 year period.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). We report 95% confidence interval (CI95%) for most relevant quantities. Categorical variables are reported as number and percentage. The Chi-square test and Student’s t-test were used for comparisons between groups of those variables. Paired t-test was applied when appropriate. ANOVA methods were used for comparisons between multiple categories. For multiple repeated paired measurements, a General Linear Model was used when appropriate. A proportional hazard Cox regression model was used to test the influence of patients and clinical variables on ablation outcomes during follow-up. Hazard ratio with 95% CI is reported.

All comparisons accounted for uneven data replications in patients and resampling methods have been used to make inferences when dealing with dependent data structures. Specifically, the general Bootstrap Algorithm14 (gBA) was used to contrast the intra- and inter-cluster similarity or difference between groups. Similarly, a standard naïve bootstrap method was used to calculate the CI95%. Analyses were performed by customized routines for the R environment (www.r-project.org), Origin 8.1 (OriginLab Corp, Northampton, MA) and Matlab R2019a (Mathworks, Natick, MA). Statistical significance was established at p < 0.05.

Discussion

The main findings in this study are that during non-invasive bi-atrial mapping of persistent AF: (i) The DF modulation by Adenosine during persistent AF is heterogeneous and leads to a spatial re-distribution of DFs. (ii) The locations with the highest DF acceleration relative to baseline variations approximately overlap the locations of reproducible rotational domains. And (iii), ablation of all accelerating rotational domains is associated with better follow-up outcomes, suggesting a possible new ablation guidance approach.

The effects of adenosine on reentrant activity in AF

Mapping reentries in the presence of Adenosine has been performed to elucidate on excitation patterns driving AF. Hansen et al. studied Adenosine effects on AF in a relevant ex vivo model of explanted atria of patients with a history of AF as well as in vivo in patients and computer simulations.4 Their study found that Adenosine induced a decrease in reentry cycle lengths, as in the present study, and suggested that shortening of the action potential refractoriness by Adenosine infusion may turn unstable reentry during AF into a more organized pattern of activation around a common path in fibrotic hubs that is better visualized. Their optical mapping observations in experiments were reproduced in a clinical setting of persistent AF. Using contact basket catheters, the investigators were able to improve stable reentry localization and ablation with AF termination in multiple patients. Consistent with the observations of this and other studies of an increase in activation rate and reentries stability, our study has found also that Adenosine induced a significant general reduced cycle length of reentries as well as increased number of rotations and duration prolongation in reentries with > 3 rotations (Table 1). Interestingly, Hansen et al. also describe situations in which a different reentries response could be observed when Adenosine challenged a reentrant driver that is stable at baseline. In those situations, rotors which tend to colocalize with regions of conduction block may destabilize as blockades may be eliminated by the increased excitability secondary to transmembrane potential hyperpolarization induced by the Adenosine presence, as proposed by computer simulations15,16 and employed clinically to test for dormant conduction paths.17 The destabilization of reentries may lead to their slowing or termination, and may explain our new observation that the change in activation rate following Adenosine is heterogeneous; while some virtual nodes and atrial regions accelerate, others do not and therefore DF gradients become more apparent (see Fig. 6D). The distinct fate of the reentries and DFs under the Adenosine challenge may indicate the possibly different role of each of the detected reentries in maintaining the fibrillation. The proposed distinction between driving and passive reentries stems from optical mapping in cholinergic sheep heart model demonstrating that only a subset of all reentries appearing in series during AF onset are contributing to its sustenance.18 Further research is needed to characterize the mechanisms that distinguish the driving from the passive reentries.

In a recent publication using the similar non-invasive mapping system as in our study, Dhillon et al. explored the relationship of Adenosine infusion with potential AF drivers in the form of both rotations and focal discharges.8 They also found that Adenosine reduced the cycle lengths of reentries, but it did not increase the number of rotational activities and rather increased the number of focal discharges in the RA. Our observations on Adenosine effect on cycle lengths and density of rotors as described in Table 1 are consistent with those of Dhillon et al. however, focal discharges were not studied in our ECGi mapping because their origin is not clear; while repeated focal discharges may originate at fast local ectopic excitations expected to be diminished by Adenosine,1 they may also be the manifestation of intramural reentries.19,20 Reentrant activity leading to surface breakthroughs in human explanted hearts was indeed recently suggested whereby mapped stable RA endocardial micro-reentry followed myocardial strands of the pectinate bundles and may have transmitted electrical waves discontinuously to the mapped epicardium.21 The discontinuous propagation is consistent with breakdown of propagation at high frequency of activations which was observed at a similar anatomical structure in the sheep.22

The frequency mapping guidance of AF ablation

There is substantial experimental and clinical evidence in support of considering the activation rate of the myocardium as an important parameter to study AF mechanisms. For the inverse-solution AF mapping approach as utilized in this study, frequency domain analysis and localization of the highest DF was proposed to enhance robustness in localization of reentrant drivers.23 In Fig. 5, the increase in maximal DF following Adenosine infusion (DFAdo) was associated with increased DF gradients consistent with previous atrial optical mapping experiments with a pacing stimuli at an increasing frequency.22 Optical mapping in a sheep acute AF model has shown that increasing the concentration of acetylcholine, that acts on the same inward rectifier potassium channel as adenosine, in the perfusate leads to increased DFs in both atria, but also increased the DF gradient between the two atria.3 We previously observed in patients that there is a link between the spatial distribution of rotational domains and the direction of the frequency gradients, with the atria activated at the highest frequency being the preferential location of the rotational domains.9 Moreover, circumferential PVI with ablation near the expected location of the main inter-atrial conduction fascicle decreased the frequency in the slowest atrium while the fastest atrium remained unaffected, supporting the idea that the rotational domains at the fastest atrium played a role in maintaining the observed gradient.9 The frequency gradients have been explored also in animal models, were disruption of electrical communication between the left and right atria during AF led to similar phenomena: In a cholinergic sheep model of AF, Mansour et al. elegantly demonstrated that ablation of Bachmann’s bundle and the infero-posterior pathway connecting left and right atria caused a decrease in the activation frequency in the slower atrium, while the frequency of the faster atria remained unaltered.24

Despite a sound mechanistic rationale, clinical attempts did not demonstrate significant improvements in ablation outcomes guided by DF mapping.25 One possibility for such outcome is a temporal instability in the frequency content during AF26 which would reflect altered activation patterns between different time windows of the mapping and the ablation. We previously demonstrated that using short signal segments when analyzing local activation frequency may result in instantaneous variability in DFs that can yield un-interpretable results.9 On the other hand, Li et al.27 quantified recurrences of high DF regions in patients with persistent AF by calculating correlations between DF maps at different time windows in noncontact LA panoramic mapping and showed that different patterns of DF consistently reappear.

In the present study we employed the SFFT-Welch’s frequency analysis method on TQ segments to guarantee that no ventricular signals contaminate the quantification of the stability of the DF at baseline (Fig. 4). It was found that in most of the nodes those baseline DFs vary by < 0.4 Hz between B1 and B2 mappings (Fig. 4D). This variation is less than the FWHM values of 1.73 and 1.79 Hz found in DFB1 and DFB2 maps histograms, respectively (Fig. 6A), suggesting similar DF distributions over the two mapping stages, however the local variations in DFs in each individual node, as demonstrated in Fig. 4D can affect assessment of the DF modulation by Adenosine. To rank the increase of DFAdo relative to varying baseline values we therefor weigh the normalized DFAdo with the normalized baseline stability (opposite of variability) and created the DFASI maps whereby the nodes with the highest DFAdo and that the most stable DFB1 and DFB2 (DFASI ≥ 0.8) colocalized in a significant number with reproducible rotational domains at baseline (Fig. 7). The fact that the colocalization between highest DFASI domains and reproducible baseline rotational domains was only partial may be the result of the opposing effect of Adenosine on rotors stability4 with the split in the DFAdo distribution into a high and low frequency bands (Fig. 6B). A follow-up probability analysis for freedom of any arrhythmia shows (Fig. 8) that in patients in which ablation targeted all domains determined post-procedurally as having DFASI ≥ 0.8 the outcomes were better than in patients in which not all such domains were ablated. Notably the DFASI formalism we employ here is weighing equally the contributions of the DFAdo and the stability factor, however improved outcomes may be accomplished with unequal weights that are yet to be determined. Notwithstanding, it is suggested that the panoramic evaluation of the distribution of the DF increase by Adenosine relative to the baseline DF variation can provide a method for testing which reentries drive AF and a guidance to ablation of AF drivers.

Limitations

There are several limitations to consider in our study. First, the non-invasive CardioInsight mapping does not include the atrial septum as does the intracardiac Carto mapping system and localizations of domains and ablation sites in maps by the two systems were based on visual assessments as conventional in the field.5,6,28,29 The visual localization in different mapping system may have reduced the correlation between the DFs in non-invasive virtual and intracardiac contact bipolar EGMs relative to other in-silico studies that demonstrated accurate frequency and phase-based markers for non-invasive AF mapping.23,30 Our non-invasive results are also consistent with those of optical mapping experiments and clinical contact electrogram recordings, including a correlation between non-invasively mapped activation frequencies and reentrant drivers7,31 and their correlation with ablation outcomes.32 Second, the CardioInsight system processes only TQ segments which are free of ventricular activity, but may limit analysis of long duration reentries. Third, we did not perform a sensitivity analysis for the highest DFASI threshold of 0.8 for optimal outcome. Instead, we selected the threshold value such that the number of virtual atrial nodes displaying DFASI ≥ 0.8 represented 4.8 ± 4.6% of the total number of nodes (less than 10% of the atrial nodes for most patients) yielding conventional significance and specificity in matching between highest DFASI domains and ablation regions. And finally, several rotational domains identified on the mapping were not ablated for safety concerns.

Conclusions

A new non-invasive bi-atrial mapping analysis of DFs in AF during Adenosine infusion shows modulation and bi-modal re-distribution of the DFs, and can localize the regions with the highest acceleration of activation rate relative to rate at baseline. Ablation targeting all such regions resulted in better ablation outcomes on follow-up. Thus, the bi-atrial mapping during baseline and Adenosine with analysis of local activation stability and rate could potentially assist in physiologically-based guidance of AF ablation.

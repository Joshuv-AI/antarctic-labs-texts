Abstract
. In classical set theory, the ordinals form a linear chain that we often think of as a very thin portion of the set-theoretic universe. In intuitionistic set theory, however, this is not the case and there can be incomparable ordinals. In this paper, we shall show that starting from two incomparable ordinals, one can construct canonical bijections from any arbitrary set to an antichain of ordinals, and consequently any subset of the given set can be defined using ordinals as parameters. This implies the surprising result that in the theory “ IKP + \mathrm{IKP}+{} there exist two incomparable ordinals”, the statements Ord ⊆ L \mathrm{Ord}\subseteq L and V = L V=L are equivalent.

1.
Introduction

This paper aims to report on a canonical way of constructing arbitrarily sized antichains of ordinals in intuitionistic set theory, developed during research on the behaviours of Gödel’s constructible universe
L
in intuitionistic contexts. As surveyed by Lubarsky
[
lubarsky93-intuitionistic-l
]
and more recently by Matthews and Rathjen
[
matthews-rathjen24-constructible-universe
]
, the situation for
L
is much more complicated than the classical case and, notably, it still remains open whether any intuitionistic set theory can prove the statement
\mathrm{Ord}\subseteq L
. In this paper, we present some results about intuitionistic ordinals that will suggest that this may not be as innocent a claim as it seems to be.

In classical set theory, the class
\mathrm{Ord}
of
ordinals
, i.e. transitive sets of transitive sets, satisfies the trichotomy

\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}\left(\alpha\in\beta\lor\alpha=\beta\lor\beta\in\alpha\right).

However, the same is not true in intuitionistic set theory. For example, if
\varphi
is a formula such that the instance of excluded middle
\varphi\lor\neg\varphi
does not hold, then none of the three disjuncts above can hold for the assignment
\alpha\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1
,
\beta\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{x\in 1:\varphi\right\}
.

We will say that two ordinals
\alpha,\beta
are
incomparable
when the negation of this trichotomy holds, or more concisely written as

\alpha\perp\beta\quad\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\quad\neg\alpha\in\beta^{+}\land\neg\beta\in\alpha^{+}.

We shall start by justifying formally that such ordinals may indeed exist in intuitionistic set theory

There are various other ways to justify the consistency of incomparable ordinals in different base theories such as
\mathrm{IKP}
or
\mathrm{CZF}
. For example, the realisability model in Rathjen
[
rathjen06-czf-realizability
]
contains plenty of incomparable ordinals. Here, we will present a construction that is most relevant to the final section of this paper.
:

Proposition 1
.

Assuming that
\mathrm{IZF}
is consistent, then so is the theory

\mathrm{IZF}+\exists\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}\ \alpha\perp\beta.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise, that is,
\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\neg\exists\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}\ \alpha\perp\beta
. Specifically, for an arbitrary formula
\psi\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)
with all free variables listed, we can consider the set

\alpha_{\psi}=2\cup\left\{\beta\in\mathcal{P}\left(1\right):\exists x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\left(0\in\beta\leftrightarrow\psi\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\right)\right\}.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{IZF}\vdash{}

\displaystyle\alpha_{\psi}\in\mathrm{Ord}\land\neg\alpha_{\psi}\in 2\land\neg 2\in\alpha_{\psi}

\displaystyle\quad{}\land\left(\alpha_{\psi}=2\leftrightarrow\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\left(\psi\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\lor\neg\psi\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\right)\right).

By our assumption, we have
\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\neg\alpha_{\psi}\perp 2
. Then we must have
\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\neg\neg\alpha_{\psi}=2
and thus the corresponding double negated instance of excluded middle:

\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\neg\neg\forall x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\left(\psi\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\lor\neg\psi\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)\right).

However, this is impossible. We say that
\varphi
is an
anti-classical axiom
if
\mathrm{ZF}\vdash\neg\varphi
, then it is well-known that
\mathrm{IZF}
is consistent with many anti-classical axioms. For example, see the realisability model in
[
chen-rm-rathjen12-lifschitz-realizability
]
interpreting such axioms like Church’s thesis, etc. Now, let
\varphi
be any such anti-classical axiom and
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{k}
be an enumeration of the necessary instances of excluded middle such that

\mathrm{IZF}+\chi_{1}+\cdots+\chi_{k}\vdash\neg\varphi,

then by the analysis above we must have
\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\neg\neg\chi_{i}
for all
i
and consequently
\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\neg\neg\neg\varphi
. This is a contradiction to the consistency of
\varphi
and thus our initial assumption must be false.
∎

In the main portion of this paper, we shall proceed to show that such incomparable ordinals are highly pathological objects — this is probably hinted at in Lubarsky’s recent survey
[
lubarsky23-inner-outer-models-cst
]
, section 6, where he claimed “there are [unpublished] constructions showing that it is not hard to get an arbitrary set into
L
by coding it into an ordinal” — and we shall make it precise here that, from merely a pair of two incomparable ordinals, one can construct an injection
f
from an arbitrarily large domain to an
antichain
of ordinals, that is, for any
x\neq y\in\dom\left(f\right)
, we will have
f\left(x\right)\perp f\left(y\right)
.

Additionally, we construct this map
f
as a canonical
\Sigma
-definable object in the language of set theory. It induces a method to uniformly encode any subsets of its domain as ordinals and is now even preserved in the constructible universe. We shall utilise this encoding to prove the following result, which demonstrates how intuitionistic
L
behaves vastly different from its classical counterpart:

Theorem 2

(
\mathrm{IKP}
)
.

Assume there exist
\alpha\perp\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, then the following are equivalent:

•

\mathrm{Ord}\subseteq L
,

•

V=L
.

One immediate consequence of this, as we shall comment at the end, is that the consistency of any theory
\mathrm{IZF}+\varphi+V\neq L
, where
\varphi
is any anti-classical axiom, will imply
\mathrm{IZF}\nvdash\mathrm{Ord}\subseteq L
, and will resolve the long-standing open problem mentioned above.

2.
Constructing antichains

A very weak intuitionistic set theory,
\mathrm{IKP}
, suffices for our construction, which means the same results easily apply to any intuitionistic extensions such as
\mathrm{CZF}
or
\mathrm{IZF}
. Essentially, the construction uses the fact that
\mathrm{IKP}
proves
\Sigma
-recursion and also separation by any
\Delta_{0}
-formula containing
\Sigma
-definable class functions. We also assume that
\mathrm{IKP}
here includes the axiom of strong infinity, which is needed for the existence of transitive closures that will become convenient later.

Like in a classical set theory, we say that a set
\alpha
is an
ordinal
if it is a transitive set of transitive sets; the class of all ordinals is denoted
\mathrm{Ord}
. For any
\alpha\in\mathrm{Ord}
, we define its
successor
as

\alpha^{+}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha\cup\left\{\alpha\right\}\in\mathrm{Ord}.

We cannot intuitionistically classify all ordinals into the cases of zero, successors and limits. Thus, to obtain the addition operator
\alpha+\cdot:\mathrm{Ord}\rightarrow\mathrm{Ord}
, we need to modify the recursive definition to use a uniform clause. We let

\alpha+\beta=\alpha\cup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\beta}\left(\alpha+\gamma\right)^{+},

which is common practice in intuitionistic contexts such as in
[
aczel-rathjen10-cst-book
]
.

To begin, we shall verify that a few basic classical properties of ordinal addition still hold in intuitionistic set theory.
\mathrm{IKP}
uses the weaker axiom of set induction instead of foundation, but it still forbids self-containing sets:

Lemma 3
.

There is no set
x
such that
x\in x
.

Proof.

Suppose that such an element
x
exists. Observe that for any set
y
,
y=x
implies
x\in y
, i.e.
\exists z\in y\ z=x
. Thus,
\forall z\in y\ \neg z=x
implies
\neg y=x
. By set induction, we must have
\forall y\ \neg y=x
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, we have
\neg\alpha+\beta\in\alpha
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha+\beta\in\alpha
, then
\alpha+\beta\in\alpha\cup\bigcup_{\gamma\in\beta}\left(\alpha+\gamma\right)^{+}=\alpha+\beta
, contradicting
section 2
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

For any
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathrm{Ord}
, we have
\alpha+\beta\in\alpha+\gamma\rightarrow\beta\in\gamma
and
\alpha+\beta=\alpha+\gamma\rightarrow\beta=\gamma
.

Proof.

We prove the conjunction of the two desired properties by simultaneous set induction on the parameters
\beta
and
\gamma
. First suppose that

\alpha+\beta\in\alpha+\gamma=\alpha\cup\bigcup_{\delta\in\gamma}\left(\alpha+\delta\right)^{+}.

By
section 2
,
\neg\alpha+\beta\in\alpha
, thus we must have
\alpha+\beta\in\left(\alpha+\delta\right)^{+}
for some
\delta\in\gamma
, i.e.
\alpha+\beta\in\alpha+\delta\lor\alpha+\beta=\alpha+\delta
. We thus must have
\beta\in\delta\lor\beta=\delta
by the inductive hypothesis and thus
\beta\in\gamma
either way.

On the other hand, suppose that
\alpha+\beta=\alpha+\gamma
, then

\alpha+\delta\in\left(\alpha+\delta\right)^{+}\subseteq\alpha+\beta=\alpha+\gamma

for any
\delta\in\beta
. By the inductive hypothesis, we have
\delta\in\gamma
for any
\delta\in\beta
, i.e.
\beta\subseteq\gamma
. An entirely symmetrical argument shows that
\gamma\subseteq\beta
as well, hence
\beta=\gamma
as desired.
∎

Recall from the introduction that incomparability is defined as

\alpha\perp\beta\quad\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\quad\neg\alpha\in\beta^{+}\land\neg\beta\in\alpha^{+}.

We check that this is preserved by carefully constructed sums:

Lemma 6
.

When
\alpha\perp\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, for any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Ord}
, also
\alpha\perp\beta^{+}+\gamma
.

Proof.

Firstly, suppose that
\beta^{+}+\gamma\in\alpha^{+}
. Then
\beta\in\beta^{+}+\gamma\subseteq\alpha
, contradicting our assumption that
\alpha\perp\beta
. Observe that this already implies
\neg\alpha=\beta^{+}+\gamma
for any
\gamma\in\mathrm{Ord}
.

It suffices to further show by set induction on
\gamma
that
\neg\alpha\in\beta^{+}+\gamma
. Suppose that
\alpha\in\beta^{+}+\gamma
yet
\neg\alpha\in\beta^{+}+\delta
for every
\delta\in\gamma
. Since also
\neg\alpha=\beta^{+}+\delta
, i.e. we have
\neg\alpha\in\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)^{+}
, thus we must have
\alpha\in\beta^{+}
. This again contradicts
\alpha\perp\beta
.
∎

Corollary 7
.

When
\alpha\perp\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, for any
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\in\mathrm{Ord}
, we have

\left(\alpha^{+}+\gamma_{1}\right)\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma_{2}\right)=\left(\alpha^{+}+\delta_{1}\right)\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta_{2}\right)\rightarrow\gamma_{1}=\delta_{1}\land\gamma_{2}=\delta_{2}.

Proof.

Assume
\left(\alpha^{+}+\gamma_{1}\right)\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma_{2}\right)=\left(\alpha^{+}+\delta_{1}\right)\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)
. For any
\eta\in\gamma_{1}
, we have
\alpha^{+}+\eta\in\alpha^{+}+\gamma_{1}\subseteq\left(\alpha^{+}+\delta_{1}\right)\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta_{2}\right)
. Suppose that
\alpha^{+}+\eta\in\beta^{+}+\delta_{2}
, then
\alpha\in\beta^{+}+\delta_{2}
by transitivity, and this contradicts
section 2
. This means we must have
\alpha^{+}+\eta\in\alpha^{+}+\delta_{1}
and thus
\eta\in\delta_{1}
by
section 2
, i.e.
\gamma_{1}\subseteq\delta_{1}
. Entirely symmetrical arguments show that
\delta_{1}\subseteq\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}\subseteq\delta_{2}
and
\delta_{2}\subseteq\gamma_{2}
as well, hence
\gamma_{1}=\delta_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}=\delta_{2}
as desired.
∎

Observe that
section 2
essentially claims that given two incomparable ordinals, there is a method to represent any pair of two ordinals as a single ordinal uniformly.

Now, before proceeding to treat antichains, we observe that the classical way to defines this notion, i.e.

\forall a,b\in\dom\left(f\right)\left(a\neq b\leftrightarrow f\left(a\right)\perp f\left(b\right)\right),

is intuitionistically “weak” because the both sides of the biconditional consist of negated formulae, so we cannot derive positive statements like
a=b
when we need them since intuitionistic logic lacks double-negation elimination. We will alternatively consider the formula

\Theta\left(a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\right)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\neg a^{\prime}\in b^{\prime}\land\neg b^{\prime}\in a^{\prime}\land\left(a^{\prime}=b^{\prime}\leftrightarrow a=b\right).

\Theta\left(a,f\left(a\right),b,f\left(b\right)\right)
is then classical equivalent to the biconditional above but intuitionistically stronger. Let
x
be any set, we say that a function
f:x\rightarrow\mathrm{Ord}
is
pairwise incomparable
if
\forall y,z\in x\ \Theta\left(y,f\left(y\right),z,f\left(z\right)\right)
.

Proposition 8
.

When
\alpha\perp\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, for any
\gamma,\delta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, we have

\Theta\left(\gamma,\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right),\delta,\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)\right).

Proof.

First suppose that
\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)\in\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)
. Notice that
\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)\in\alpha^{+}
implies
\alpha\in\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)\subseteq\alpha
, contradicting
section 2
, thus we must have
\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)\in\beta^{+}+\delta
instead. However, by
section 2
, we have
\neg\alpha\in\beta^{+}+\delta
and hence another contradiction. In other words, we have
\neg\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)\in\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)
and by a symmetrical argument,
\neg\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)\in\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)
as well.

Now, it remains to show that

\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\gamma\right)=\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+\delta\right)\rightarrow\gamma=\delta.

This is simply an instance of
section 2
where
\gamma_{1}=\delta_{1}=\varnothing
.
∎

Immediately, given fixed
\alpha\perp\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}
, if any function
f:x\rightarrow\mathrm{Ord}
is injective, i.e. satisfying
\forall y,z\in x\left(f\left(y\right)=f\left(z\right)\rightarrow y=z\right)
, we can construct
f^{\perp}:x\rightarrow\mathrm{Ord}
as

y\mapsto\alpha^{+}\cup\left(\beta^{+}+f\left(y\right)\right).

Then by
section 2
,
f^{\perp}
is pairwise incomparable.

Proposition 9
.

Let
x
be any set and
f:x\rightarrow\mathrm{Ord}
be pairwise incomparable. Then for any
y\subseteq x

\forall z\in x\left(z\in y\leftrightarrow f\left(z\right)\in\bigcup_{t\in y}f\left(t\right)^{+}\right).

Proof.

The forward direction is trivial. For the backward direction, suppose that
f\left(z\right)\in\bigcup_{t\in y}f\left(t\right)^{+}
, i.e.
f\left(z\right)\in f\left(t\right)^{+}
for some
t\in y
. From the pairwise incomparability condition, we know that
\neg f\left(z\right)\in f\left(t\right)
, so we must have
f\left(z\right)=f\left(t\right)
, which immediately implies
z=t\in y
.
∎

.

Fix
\alpha\perp\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}

be pairwise incomparable. Then for any set

A final remark

Using the same constructions as in

implies finding any non-classical model of

. More precisely,

Corollary 14
.

Let
\varphi
be any anti-classical axiom, that is,
\mathrm{ZF}\vdash\neg\varphi
. If
\mathrm{IZF}+\varphi+V\neq L
is consistent, then
\mathrm{IZF}\nvdash\mathrm{Ord}\subseteq L
.

Proof.

We assume that
\mathrm{IZF}\vdash\mathrm{Ord}\subseteq L
. Then by
Theorem 2
,

\mathrm{IZF}+V\neq L\vdash\neg\exists\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Ord}\ \alpha\perp\beta.

Looking at the same ordinals
\alpha_{\psi}
and
2
as in the proof of
section 1

\mathrm{IZF}+V\neq L
is inconsistent with any anti-classical axioms. This contradicts the consistency of

∎

However, constructing intuitionistic models of

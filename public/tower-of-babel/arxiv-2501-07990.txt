Strongly Periodic Modules and Perverse Autoequivalences
Alfred Dabson

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of strong periodicity of a module over a finite-dimensional algebra over a field. We prove that the existence of such modules over certain idempotent algebras is both a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of a two-step self-perverse equivalence of a finite-dimensional algebra. We survey some applications to the setting of the symmetric groups.



1.
Introduction

Perverse equivalences, as introduced by Chuang and Rouquier in
[
12
]
, are a class of derived equivalences, filtered by shifted Morita equivalences. This built on work in
[
11
]
, in which the authors defined derived equivalences arising from
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-categorification, equivalences that are themselves perverse. Chuang and Rouquier’s work resolved Broué’s Abelian Defect Group Conjecture
[
7
]
for blocks of symmetric groups, highlighting the significance of perverse equivalences in the representation theory of finite groups. This has been explored further by Craven and Rouquier,
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, proving for some blocks of sporadic groups and finite groups of Lie type in non-defining characteristic that conjectured derived equivalences realising Broué’s conjecture coming from Deligne-Lusztig varieties exist and are perverse. Independently, Wong
[
37
]
has exploited perverse equivalences to resolve Broué’s conjecture for blocks of
\SL_{2}(q)
in defining characteristic in a novel way.

The significance of perverse equivalences is by no means limited to the representation theory of finite groups. As the name might suggest, Chuang and Rouquier’s constructions are are intimately related to the gluing of perverse sheaves in geometry
[
3
]
, and in certain cases,
[
2
]
, can be described in terms of Bridgeland stability conditions
[
6
]
, though this aspect is as of yet poorly understood.

Perverse equivalences have a natural combinatorial flavour, making them far easier to work with than general derived equivalences. Moreover, unlike a general derived equivalence, a perverse equivalence between two algebras defines a stratified bijection between their sets of simple modules.

The class of two-step perverse equivalences, for which filtration is of length two, can be considered the closest derived equivalences to a Morita equivalence. We call the magnitude
d
of the singular shift the
width
of the equivalence. Two-step perverse equivalences of width 1 are combinatorial or standard Okuyama-Rickard tilts
[
27
]
, examples of which include Kauer moves on Brauer graph algebras
[
23
]
. Iterated applications of combinatorial tilts produce two-step equivalences of larger width; it remains unknown if other sources of two-step perverse equivalences of width
d
exist.

Among the more compelling studies of two-step perverse equivalences comes from Grant
[
18
]
,
[
17
]
, who proves that, for symmetric algebras, periodic and relatively periodic idempotent algebras give rise to two-step perverse autoequivalences of width equal to the period of the idempotent algebra. In particular, if

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

The work in this document was born out of a desire to better understand the relationship between periodicity and perversity, and in particular to strengthen Grant’s result into an if-and-only if statement.

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

perverse relative to
(\mathcal{B}_{\bullet},\mathcal{C}_{\bullet},p^{\prime})
. Then the composition
F^{\prime}\circ F
is perverse relative to
(\mathcal{A}_{\bullet},\mathcal{C}_{\bullet},p+p^{\prime})
.

The preceding results produce the following,
[
12
, Proposition 4.17]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose we have equivalences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

perverse relative to
(\mathcal{A}_{\bullet},\mathcal{B}_{\bullet},p)
, and

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\prime}:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(C),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

perverse relative to
(\mathcal{A}_{\bullet},\mathcal{C}_{\bullet},p)
. Then
F^{\prime}\circ F^{-1}
induces an equivalence of abelian categories
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\modcat}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C\modcat.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Proof.

By Lemmas
2.6
and
2.7
,
F^{\prime}\circ F^{-1}
is perverse relative to
(\mathcal{B}_{\bullet},\mathcal{C}_{\bullet},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mup\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, with
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mup\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=p+(-p)\equiv 0
, so the result follows by Lemma
2.7
.
∎

In other words, a perverse equivalence is determined up to Morita equivalence by the filtration on the left hand side and the perversity function
p
. We think of this as a
uniqueness
result for perverse equivalences.

As one final basic definition, an equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(A),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
perverse relative to
(\mathcal{A}_{\bullet},\mathcal{A}_{\bullet},p)
, is a
self-perverse equivalence
. In such cases, we will say that
F
is perverse relative to
(\mathcal{A}_{\bullet},p)
.

2.4.
Simple modules and projective modules

For the remainder of this section, we assume our algebras are symmetric. Here, it will be beneficial to consider the perversity data
(I_{\bullet},I^{\prime}_{\bullet},p)
. Fix the notation of the previous subsection. Let
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
be the sets of isomorphism classes of simple
A
- and
B
-modules respectively. For a subset
J\subset I
, set
\mathcal{S}_{J}=\{S_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{J}=\{S^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
. Given filtrations
I_{\bullet}
,
I^{\prime}_{\bullet}
of subsets of
I
, set
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\mathcal{S}_{I_{i}}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{I_{i}}
.

We can rephrase the conditions in Definition
2.4
to conditions wholly on the simple modules themselves. The following is
[
12
, Lemma 4.19]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

A derived equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is perverse relative to
(I_{\bullet},I^{\prime}_{\bullet},p)
if both of the following hold.

(i)

For every
i
and every
V\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
, the composition factors of
H_{t}(F(V))
for
t\neq p(i)
are all in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
, and there is a filtration
L_{1}\subset L_{2}\subset H_{p(i)}(F(V))
such that the composition factors of
L_{1}
and the composition factors of
H_{p(i)}(F(V))/L_{2}
are all in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
, and
L_{2}/L_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
.

(ii)

The map
V\mapsto L_{2}/L_{1}
described above is a bijection between
\mathcal{S}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
.

In fewer words, for every
V\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{i-1}
, the composition factors of
H_{t}(\Phi(V))
are all in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
, except for a single composition factor of
H_{p(i)}(\Phi(V))
, which lies in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
.

Perhaps the most significant consequence of Proposition
2.9
is that, when
F
is a perverse equivalence, unlike for a general perverse equivalence,
F
induces a bijection
\mathcal{S}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{i-1}\leftrightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{i-1}
between the layers of the two filtrations of subsets of simple modules. Gluing these stratified bijections together therefore defines a bijection between
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. This is one sense in which a perverse equivalence gives us more information than an arbitrary equivalence.

One can also rephrase the conditions for perversity in terms of projective modules. The following is taken from
[
12
, Lemmas 4.7, 4.21]
.

Suppose
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is a perverse equivalence, relative to
(I_{\bullet},I^{\prime}_{\bullet},p)
. For each

, let
\mathcal{P}_{i}
be the set of projective indecomposable

corresponding to the simple modules

, and

\emptyset=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{t}=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}

of the sets
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of projective

by
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mup\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(i)=p(t-i+1)
.

Proposition 2.10
.

A derived equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is perverse relative to
(I_{\bullet},I^{\prime}_{\bullet},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mup\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
if and only if the following hold.

(i)

For every
i
and every indecomposable projective
A
-module

, the object

is isomorphic in

to a complex

of projective

is a direct sum of modules in

, which has exactly one indecomposable summand in

, say

, with all others in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i-1}
.

(ii)

The map

as above defines a bijection between

and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i}\setminus\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{i-1}
.

In particular, gluing these stratified bijections produces a bijection

, matching (in reverse) the bijection

.

2.5.
Two-step perverse equivalences

Let
A
,
B
be as before and suppose we have a derived equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
Suppose there are filtrations

\emptyset\subset_{p(1)}J\subset_{p(2)}I,

\emptyset\subset_{p(1)}J^{\prime}\subset_{p(2)}I

such that

is perverse relative to
(I_{\bullet},I^{\prime}_{\bullet},p)
. Then we say that

is a
two-step perverse equivalence of width
d
, where

.

Let

be an
A
-module. Take a projective cover
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{M}}
of

. Denote by

the largest quotient of

by a submodule of
\ker(\pi_{M})
such that all composition factors of the kernel of the induced map
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
are in

. Let
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q_{M,J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(\phi_{M,J})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{M,J}}
be a projective cover of the kernel of the canonical map
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M_{J}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{M,J}}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Given
J\subset I
, the
combinatorial tilting complex at
J
is the complex

T=\bigoplus_{j\in J}T_{j}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in I\setminus J}P_{i}[1],

where, for

,

is the complex
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q_{S_{j},J}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

concentrated in degrees 1 and 0.

We note that Grant
[
18
, Definition 5.4]
allows
T=\bigoplus_{i\in I}T_{i}^{\ell_{i}}
, where
\ell_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
, with
T_{i}=P_{i}[1]
for
i\in I\setminus J
. The complex

with
\ell_{i}=1
is the
basic
combinatorial tilting complex at
J
.

When

is such that

for every

, the complex

is
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(\rad(P_{j}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{j},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
where
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(\rad(P_{j}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rad(P_{j})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
is a projective cover of
\rad(P_{i})
. That is,
P(\rad(P_{j}))=\bigoplus_{i\in I\setminus J}P_{i}\otimes_{k}\Ext^{1}_{A}(S_{i},S_{j})
, where
\Ext^{1}_{A}(S_{i},S_{j})
is the
multiplicity module
.

Combinatorial tilting complexes were introduced by Rickard
[
29
]
for

a single index, generalised to arbitrary subsets

by Okuyama
[
27
]
. They are also called

, among other names. Combinatorial tilting complexes have wide-reaching applications, for example in silting theory
[
1
]
and cluster tilting theory
[
8
]
.

Given a subset

at

exists and is unique up to isomorphism,
[

, Lemma 5.5, Corollary 5.7]
. Further.

is a tilting complex,
[

, Proposition 1.1]
,
[
18
, Proposition 5.6]
. Thus, by Theorem

, given a combinatorial tilting complex

, there is an algebra

such that there is a derived equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{J}:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
. We call
F_{J}
the
combinatorial tilt of
A
at
J
.

Two-step perverse equivalences of width
-1
coincide with combinatorial tilts. The following can be found in
[
12
, Proposition 5.3]
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional symmetric
k
-algebra. Let
I
be an indexing set of the isomorphism classes of simple
A
-modules. Given

, the combinatorial tilt
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{J}:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
at

is a perverse equivalence, with filtrations both given by

.

Chuang and Rouquier call these combinatorial tilts
elementary perverse equivalences
.

2.6.
Standard equivalences

Suppose
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is a derived equivalence. Recall by Theorem
2.3
that there is a complex
X
of
B
-

-bimodules such that, for every

,

in

.

Proposition 2.13
.

If the equivalence

is perverse relative to
(I_{\bullet},I^{\prime}_{\bullet},p)
, then the equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{A}-:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(B)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is perverse, with the same perversity data.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.9
, the perversity of the derived equivalence

depends only on the images of simple
A
-modules. But for every simple
A
-module
S_{i}
,
X\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{A}S_{i}\cong F(S_{i})
. The result follows.
∎

Let
X
be as above. Set
X^{\vee}=\textbf{R}\Hom_{B}(X,B)
, a complex of
A
-
B
-bimodules. Then by
[
32
, Proposition 4.1]
,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\vee}\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{B}-:D^{b}(B)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
is a derived equivalence, mutually inverse with the equivalence

, and is the standard derived equivalence agreeing with
F^{-1}
on objects of
D^{b}(B)
. By Lemma
2.6
and Proposition
2.10
,
X^{\vee}\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{B}-
and

are both perverse relative to
(I^{\prime}_{\bullet},I_{\bullet},-p)
.

The two-sided tilting complexes

and

also induce perverse equivalences on the derived categories of right modules. The following combines
[
32
, Lemma 4.3]
and
[

, Lemma 4.20]

Proposition 2.14
.

The functor
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{B}X:D^{b}({B}^{\mathrm{op}})}

is an equivalence, and is perverse relative to

. Similarly,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes^{\textbf{L}}_{A}X^{\vee}:D^{b}({A}^{\mathrm{op}})}

Thus, the equivalences

and

induce equivalences
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{F}:D^{b}({B}^{\mathrm{op}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}({A}^{\mathrm{op}}),}

.

3.
Periodic Modules and Algebras

3.1.
Periodic modules

Let
E
be a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra and
M
an
E
-module. If
\sigma
is an automorphism of
E
, then we define the
twisted module

{}_{\sigma\!}M
to be the
E
-module with
E
-action
x\cdot m=\sigma(x)m
for
x\in E
,
m\in M
. We can adapt this definition for right modules or bimodules in the obvious way. For any
E
-module
M
, we have an isomorphism
{}_{\sigma\!}M\cong{}_{\sigma\!}E\otimes_{E}M
of
E
-modules.

Recall that the Heller translate of
M
is
\Omega_{E}(M)=\ker(\pi_{M})
, where
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{M}}
is a projective cover of
M
. One can iterate this construction: for
n\geq 1
, we set

\Omega_{E}^{n+1}(M)=\Omega_{E}(\Omega_{E}^{n}(M)).

Definition 3.1
.

The
E
-module
M
is
\sigma
-periodic of period
n
if there is an automorphism
\sigma
of
E
and an
n\geq 1
such that
\Omega_{E}^{n}(M)\cong{}_{\sigma\!}M
.

That is,
M
is
\sigma
-periodic of period

if there is an exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}_{\sigma\!}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

of
E
-modules such that each

is projective. We call the complex
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{n-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ldots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{0}}

a
truncated projective resolution of
M
. If
\sigma=\id
, then we say simply that
M
is

.

We note that, if there is some

such that

is
\sigma
-periodic of period
n
for some automorphism
\sigma
, then there must exist some minimal such

. We emphasise that we do not demand minimality, as it may sometimes be expedient to consider different periodicities of the same

of

for which

is
\sigma
-periodic.

There is an obvious dual definition for right modules. The
{E}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module

is

-periodic of period

of

.

.

3.3.
A theorem of Grant

The pre-established link between periodicity and perversity for symmetric algebras is the following result of Grant,
[
18
, Theorem 3.9, Proposition 3.22]
,
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional symmetric
k
-algebra. Let
P
be a projective

-module and set

. If there is subalgebra
B
of

such that

is projective as a
{B}^{\mathrm{op}}
-module and

is projective as a

-module, and there is an automorphism

of

and

such that

, then there is a two-step self-perverse equivalence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{P}:D^{b}(A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
of width

.

We call the equivalence
\Psi_{P}
the
(relative) periodic twist at

.

Grant’s key examples in the non-relative case are symmetric algebra analogues of the spherical twists of Seidel and Thomas
[

]
and the

-twists of Huybrechts and Thomas
[
20
]
, for which

The next section will be devoted to tightening Theorem

4.1.
Strong periodicity

Before stating our main theorem, it is necessary that we make the following definition.

Definition 4.1

Let

It is worth taking the time to unpack this definition. We have that

4.2.
The main theorem

Recall that a Serre subcategory of

-modules.

Let

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

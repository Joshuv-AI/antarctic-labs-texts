Moments of the Riemann zeta function at its local extrema
Andrew Pearce-Crump

Abstract
. Conrey, Ghosh and Gonek studied the first moment of the derivative of the Riemann zeta function evaluated at the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function, resolving a problem known as Shanks’ conjecture. Conrey and Ghosh studied the second moment of the Riemann zeta function evaluated at its local extrema along the critical line to leading order. In this paper we combine the two results, evaluating the first moment of the zeta function and its derivatives at the local extrema of zeta along the critical line, giving a full asymptotic. We also consider the factor from the functional equation for the zeta function at these extrema.



1.
Background

Shanks’ Conjecture
[
16
]
is that

\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)\text{ is real and positive in the mean}

as
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
ranges over non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
. This was proved by Conrey, Ghosh and Gonek
[
3
]
, who showed

(1.1)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{\prime}(\rho)=\frac{T}{4\pi}(\log T)^{2}+O(T\log T)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
as a side calculation while giving a proof that there are infinitely many simple zeros of the Riemann zeta function. Fujii
[
4
,
5
]
found a full asymptotic for (
1.1
), with a power-saving error term under the Riemann Hypothesis.

The Generalised Shanks’ Conjecture can be stated that on average,

\zeta^{(n)}(\rho)\text{ is real and positive if $n$ is odd, negative if $n$ is even.}

Kaptan, Karabulut and Yıldırım
[
11
]
, using similar ideas to Conrey, Ghosh and Gonek, found that

(1.2)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}(\rho)=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n+1}\frac{T}{2\pi}(\log T)^{n+1}+O\bigl(T(\log T)^{n}\bigr)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
which clearly implies and confirms the previously unnoticed Generalised Shanks’ Conjecture. Hughes and Pearce-Crump
[
9
]
found a full asymptotic for (
1.2
), with a power-saving error term under the Riemann Hypothesis. We state this result in Theorem
5
in Section
3
. For a full history of this problem see
[
7
]
.

Under the Riemann Hypothesis, Conrey and Ghosh
[
1
]
showed that

(1.3)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\max_{\gamma\leq t\leq\gamma^{+}}\left|\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2}\sim\frac{e^{2}-5}{4\pi}T(\log T)^{2}

as
T\rightarrow\infty
where
\gamma\leq\gamma^{+}
are successive ordinates of non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.

In
[
8
]
, Hughes, Lugmayer and Pearce-Crump calculated the full asymptotic expansion of (
1.3
), showing that under the Riemann Hypothesis, for
K
a positive integer, that

(1.4)

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\max_{\gamma\leq t\leq\gamma^{+}}\left|\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)\right|^{2}=\frac{e^{2}-5}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{2}+\left(5-e^{2}-10\gamma_{0}+2e^{2}\gamma_{0}\right)\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\\
+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{c_{k}}{(\log T/2\pi)^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{T}{(\log T)^{K+1}}\right)

where
\gamma_{0}
is Euler’s constant and the other constants
c_{k}
are effectively computable constants.

Recall that the Hardy
Z
-function is defined by

Z(t)=e^{i\theta(t)}\zeta\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right),

where
\theta(t)
is the Riemann–Siegel theta function given by

e^{-2i\theta(t)}=\chi\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+it\right)

and where
\chi(s)
is the factor from the functional equation of
\zeta(s)
, given by

(1.5)

\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s).

Note that
Z(t)
is real for real
t
and satisfies
|Z(t)|=|\zeta(1/2+it)|
which means we can rewrite (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) in terms of the Hardy Z-function.

For
\gamma\leq\gamma^{+}
consecutive positive ordinates of non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
, we know there is exactly one zero of
Z^{\prime}(t)
(under the Riemann Hypothesis), call it
\lambda
, with
\gamma\leq\lambda\leq\gamma^{+}
. A proof of this result can be found in
[
10
]
. This suggest an unambiguous way of writing the following results, where we are summing over the relative extrema of
|\zeta(s)|
on the critical line. We will write our sums over these
\lambda
, where
0<\lambda\leq T
and
Z^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
.

Note that there are two zeros of
Z^{\prime}(t)
between
t=0
and the first positive zero of
Z(t)
. As these additional zeros only introduce an error of
O(1)
, we will ignore them as this contribution is within our larger error terms in the results.

Specifically, under the Riemann Hypothesis, we can rewrite the result of Conrey and Ghosh
[
1
]
as

(1.6)

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}Z(\lambda)^{2}\sim\frac{e^{2}-5}{4\pi}T(\log T)^{2}

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where we recall that the points
\lambda
that are being summed over are the zeros of the derivative of the Hardy
Z
-function up to a height
T
.

Finally, we remind the reader that results such as (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) are linked to the joint moments of the Riemann zeta function and the joint moments of the Hardy
Z
-function. To see why this is true, see
[
2
,
14
]
. Specifically, Conrey and Ghosh showed that

\int_{1}^{T}|Z(t)||Z^{\prime}(t)|\ dt\sim\frac{e^{2}-5}{4\pi}T(\log T)^{2}

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, and so the lower-order terms follows from (
1.4
). For a more detailed history of this rich problem, we refer the reader to
[
8
]
and the references therein.

2.
Results

In this paper we combine some of these results, specifically equations (
1.3
) and (
1.2
), or rather the full asymptotic expansions given in (
1.4
) and Theorem
5
. We also compare our results with those given in Section
1
. In these comparisons we don’t give full reasons beyond the obvious (that we are summing over different points/summing different functions), but the differences are worth pointing out explicitly in the remarks that follow the results and we have endeavoured to give some heuristic reasoning.

The Stieltjes constants will appear in several of the following results. The Laurent expansion for
\zeta(s)
about
s=1
can be written in terms of these constants
\gamma_{j}
, given by

(2.1)

\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}(s-1)+\frac{1}{2!}\gamma_{2}(s-1)^{2}+\dots+(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\gamma_{j}(s-1)^{j}+\dots.

We begin by evaluating the derivatives of the Riemann zeta function over the local extrema of
|\zeta(s)|
. We split the cases of derivatives and no derivatives into two separate results, both for clarity of the results and as the proofs are slightly different. The no derivative case is non-trivial, compared with typical moments where the sums are over non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function.

Theorem 1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Write
L~=~\log T/2\pi
. For
\lambda
defined by
Z^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
and
K,n\geq 1
positive integers, we have

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=a_{n+1}\frac{T}{2\pi}L^{n+1}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}a_{n-\ell}L^{n-\ell}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{b_{m}}{L^{m}}+O\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right),

as
T\rightarrow\infty
where:

(1)

The leading order coefficient is given by

a_{n+1}=(-1)^{n}\left(\frac{e^{2}-2}{n+1}+(-1)^{n+1}\frac{n!}{2^{n+1}}\left(1-e^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\frac{(-2)^{k}}{k!}\right)\right).

(2)

The subleading, non-negative logarithm power coefficients are given by

\displaystyle a_{n-\ell}=(-1)^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left[\frac{c_{\ell+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-\ell)!}+(k-n+j)\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(k-n+j)\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{\ell-m}(n-m)\dots(n-\ell+1)\frac{c_{m}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-m)!}\right]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}(-1)^{\ell}\ell!\left(-1+\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}\frac{1}{m!}\gamma_{m}\right),

where if
\ell=0
the summation over
m
in the square brackets is empty, and where if
\ell=n
, the coefficient of
L^{0}
, that is to say
a_{0}
, has an extra contribution of
n!A_{n}
, where the
A_{n}
are the coefficients in the Laurent expansion of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)/\zeta(s)
about
s=1
.

(3)

The subleading, negative logarithm power coefficients are given by

\displaystyle b_{m}=(-1)^{n}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\beta_{m}^{k,j},

where for
1\leq m\leq k+j-n
, we have

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=\frac{c_{m+n+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-n-m)!}+\frac{(m-1)!}{k-n+j-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\binom{k-n+j}{\ell}c_{\ell+n+1}^{k,j}

and where for
m\leq k+j-n+1
, we have

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=\frac{(m-1)!}{k-n+j-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+j-n}\binom{k-n+j}{\ell}c_{\ell+n+1}^{k,j}.

In these coefficients
a_{m},b_{m}
, the
c_{\ell}^{k,j}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell}^{k,j}(s-1)^{-k-j-2+\ell}.

An immediate corollary of Theorem
1
follows by setting
n=1
. We state this result explicitly to compare it with (
1.1
) and Shanks’ Conjecture.

Corollary 1
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Write
L~=~\log T/2\pi
. For
\lambda
defined by
Z^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
and
K\geq 1
a positive integer, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=

\displaystyle-\frac{(e^{2}-3)}{4}\frac{T}{2\pi}L^{2}+\frac{e^{2}-3+2\gamma_{0}}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}L+\frac{3-e^{2}(1+2\gamma_{0}+2\gamma_{1})}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{c_{m}}{L^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where the other constants
c_{m}
are special cases of the coefficients
b_{m}
from Theorem
1
with
n=1
.

We may also prove the analogous result of Conrey and Ghosh in (
1.3
) from
[
1
]
, with the modulus squared removed.

Theorem 2
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Write
L~=~\log T/2\pi
. For
\lambda
defined by
Z^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
and
K\geq 1
a positive integer, we have

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=\frac{e^{2}-3}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}L+\frac{3-e^{2}-4\gamma_{0}}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{d_{m}}{L^{m}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where

d_{m}=\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}2^{k}\frac{c_{k,m+1}}{(k-m)!}+(m-1)!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{k}k}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min\{m-1,k\}}\binom{k}{\ell}c_{k,\ell+1},

and where the
c_{k,\ell}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\zeta(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{k,\ell}(s-1)^{-k-2+\ell}.

We evaluate the sum of the factor
\chi(s)
from the functional equation (
1.5
) for
\zeta(s)
over the local extrema of
\zeta(s)

Theorem 3
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. Write
L~=~\log T/2\pi

\lambda
defined by
Z^{\prime}(\lambda)=0
and

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=(e^{2}-2)\frac{T}{2\pi}-4e^{2}\gamma_{0}\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{1}{L}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=2}^{K}\frac{e_{m}}{L^{m}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right)

Remark
.

The infinite descending chain of powers of
\log T
in the previous results where each function is summed over the relative extrema of zeta is due to a pole in the function that we consider near

\beta_{Z}=1+2/L+O\left(L^{-2}\right),

We expect that we would be able to give these results with a power-saving error term of

As in

]
, we have set aside the search for such a form for the time being.

2.2.
Overview of the proofs

In Section
3
, we consider properties of the auxiliary function

Z_{1}(s)=\zeta^{\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}(s)\zeta(s)

used in the proof, where
\chi(s)
is the factor from the non-symmetrical form of the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function, given in (
1.5
). This function is zero at the extrema of the Riemann zeta function. We also list other useful lemmas in this section, including functional equations, stationary phase methods, and a moments result from
[
9
]
on the average of derivatives of
\zeta(s)
, evaluated over the non-trivial zeros of
\zeta(s)
.

In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1
. This involves using Cauchy’s Theorem to write the sum over the extrema as an integral

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\zeta^{(n)}(s)\ ds

(up to a small error), where
\mathcal{C}
is a contour we specify later that encloses the critical line up to a height
T
.

We begin by showing that only the left-hand side of the contour contributes to the asymptotic, with the rest going into a power-saving error term. The bulk of the argument then comes down to evaluating

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\zeta^{(n)}(1-s)\ ds

for some specific
c>1
defined in the proof.

We are able to write the logarithmic derivative of
Z_{1}(s)
as a series of the form

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(m,s)}{m^{s}}

which converges for some
c>1
with coefficients
a(n,s)
given in Lemma
2
. Using this and the Dirichlet series for the derivatives of
\zeta(s)
allows us to apply the method of stationary phase to change this integral into a sum, given by

(-1)^{n}\left(\sum_{m_{1}m_{2}\leq T/2\pi}\Lambda(m_{1})(\log m_{1})^{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\sum_{m_{1}m_{2}\leq T/2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\log m_{1})^{j}a_{k}(m_{2})}{(\log m_{1}m_{2})^{k-n+j}}\right)

up to a power-saving error term, where the coefficients
a_{k}(m)
come from
a(m,s)
.

An application of Perron’s formula turns the first sum back into an integral, which we can evaluate the residue of at the pole. Another application of Perron’s formula turns the numerator of the inner sum of the second term into an integral that we evaluate the residue of at the pole, and complete the argument with partial summation and summing over
j=,0,\dots,n
and
k\geq 1
.

We then outline the proofs of Theorem
2
in Section
5
, of Theorem
3
in Section
6
, and of Theorem
4
in
7
, leaving the full details of the proofs to the interested reader as they are similar to that of Theorem
1
.

3.
Preliminary Lemmas

Define the function
Z_{1}(s)
by

(3.1)

Z_{1}(s)=\zeta^{\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}(s)\zeta(s).

Note that by taking the logarithmic derivative of Hardy’s
Z
function, we see that
Z_{1}(s)
is zero exactly when
Z^{\prime}(t)=0
, that is, at an extrema of
|\zeta(s)|
on the critical line.

The function
Z_{1}(s)
satisfies various properties which were proved by Conrey and Ghosh
[
1
]
in their lemma. Note that only the properties we use in our proof are listed below. For a proof of this lemma, we refer the reader to their paper.

Lemma 1
.

We have the following properties for
Z_{1}(s)
:

(1)

|Z_{1}(1/2+it)|=|Z^{\prime}(t)|
.

(2)

Z_{1}(s)
satisfies the functional equation

Z_{1}(s)=-\chi(s)Z_{1}(1-s)

for all
s
, where
\chi(s)
is the term in the functional equation for the Riemann zeta function,
\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)
.

(3)

The number of zeros of
Z_{1}(t)
up to a height
T
which lie off the critical line is bounded by
\log T
.

(4)

If
\ Z_{1}(\beta_{1}+i\gamma_{1})=0
then

\left|\beta_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{9}

for
\gamma_{1}
sufficiently large.

The logarithmic derivative of
Z_{1}(s)
is given by

(3.2)

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)=\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}(s)-\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(1-s)

which follows directly from part (2) in Lemma
1
.

For
s=\sigma+it
, where
|\sigma|\leq 2
, we have the following estimate for the logarithmic derivative for
\chi(s)
given by

(3.3)

\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}(s)=-\log\frac{t}{2\pi}+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right),

(say for
t>1
) which can be found in
[
1
]
, for example. This enables us to rewrite the functional equation above as

(3.4)

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)=-\log\frac{t}{2\pi}-\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(1-s)+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right).

Lemma 2
.

For
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
and
t\geq 100
, we have

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(m,s)}{m^{s}}+O\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right)

where

(3.5)

a(m,s)=-\Lambda(m)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}a_{k}(m)

with

(3.6)

f(s)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\chi^{\prime}}{\chi}(s)

and
a_{k}(m)
is of the form

(3.7)

a_{k}(m)=\left((\Lambda\log)\ast\Lambda_{k-1}\right)(m),

where we use the notation
\Lambda_{k}
to denote

\underbrace{\Lambda\ast\Lambda\ast\Lambda\ast\dots\ast\Lambda}_{k-1\text{ convolutions}}

with the convention that
\Lambda_{0}(m)
takes the value
1
if
m=1
and
0
otherwise, and where
\Lambda_{1}(m)=\Lambda(m)
is the usual von Mangoldt function.

We give a variation of the proof given in
[
8
]
.

Proof.

Recall that we have defined the function
Z_{1}(s)
in (
3.1
) by

Z_{1}(s)=\zeta^{\prime}(s)+f(s)\zeta(s)

where
f(s)
is given in (
3.6
). Differentiating this gives

Z_{1}^{\prime}(s)=\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)+f(s)\zeta^{\prime}(s)+f^{\prime}(s)\zeta(s).

Then

(3.8)

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)=\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}(s)+f(s)\zeta^{\prime}(s)}{\zeta^{\prime}(s)+f(s)\zeta(s)}+f^{\prime}(s)\frac{1}{\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+f(s)}.

For
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
with
t\geq 1
, the last term can be bounded by
O(1/t\log t)
by noting that
f^{\prime}(s)=O(1/t)
(which follows from (
3.3
)),
f(s)=O(\log t)
and
\zeta^{\prime}(s)/\zeta(s)=O(\log\log t)
(due to Littlewood
[
13
]
, under the Riemann Hypothesis) in the region under consideration. Therefore we can write

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)=\frac{\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+f(s)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)}{\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+f(s)}+O\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right).

For
s
with sufficiently large real part, we can expand the denominator as a geometric series. This gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+f(s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f(s)}\left(1+\frac{1}{f(s)}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f(s)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k}.

This expansion will be valid so long as
\mathfrak{Re}(s)
is large enough so that
\left|\frac{1}{f(s)}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right|\leq 1
. For example, we can take
\mathfrak{Re}(s)>1
and
t\geq 100
.

Then we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{f(s)}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+f(s)\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k}+O\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n,s)}{n^{s}}+O\left(\frac{1}{t\log t}\right)

where we calculate the coefficients
a(n,s)
below.

By repeated use of the quotient rule, we may write

\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{\zeta^{(k)}}{\zeta}(s)\right)=\frac{\zeta^{(k+1)}}{\zeta}(s)-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\frac{\zeta^{(k)}}{\zeta}(s).

Rearranging this we have

\frac{\zeta^{(k+1)}}{\zeta}(s)=\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{\zeta^{(k)}}{\zeta}(s)\right)+\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\frac{\zeta^{(k)}}{\zeta}(s).

The Dirichlet coefficients of these series then give us the recurrence relation for any non-negative integer
k
,

(3.10)

\Lambda^{(k+1)}(n)=\Lambda^{(k)}(n)\log(n)+(\Lambda^{(k)}(n)\ast\Lambda)(n),

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\Lambda^{(1)}(n)=\Lambda(n)
is the usual von Mangoldt function, that is, the coefficient from the Dirichlet series for
-\zeta^{\prime}(s)/\zeta(s)
. (Note that this notation is not standard - see the remark at the end of this proof.)

Consider now the coefficient from each of the Dirichlet series in (
3.9
). For the terms on the right-hand side of that equation (ignoring the error term for now), we have

a(n,s)=\left(\frac{1}{f(s)}\Lambda^{(2)}(n)-\Lambda(n)\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\Lambda_{k}(n).

Now use the recurrence relation (
3.10
) with
k=1
to write

\Lambda^{(2)}(n)=(\Lambda\log+\Lambda_{2})(n).

Substitute this into the previous expression to obtain for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle a(n,s)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{f(s)}(\Lambda\log+\Lambda_{2})(n)-\Lambda(n)\right)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\Lambda_{k}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k+1}}((\Lambda\log)\ast\Lambda_{k})(n)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k+1}}(\Lambda_{2}\ast\Lambda_{k})(n)-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}(\Lambda\ast\Lambda_{k})(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k+1}}((\Lambda\log)\ast\Lambda_{k})(n)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\Lambda_{k+1}(n)-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\Lambda_{k+1}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k+1}}((\Lambda\log)\ast\Lambda_{k})(n)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\Lambda_{k+1}(n)-\Lambda(n)-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\Lambda_{k+1}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}((\Lambda\log)\ast\Lambda_{k-1})(n)-\Lambda(n),

completing the proof of the Lemma.
∎

Remark
.

Note that while we can only use the expansion in this Lemma for
t\geq 100
, in the proofs of our results in later sections we will ignore this slight technicality as using the result for
t\geq 1
in our applications will only introduce an error of
O(1)
, which is well within our other error terms.

Typically in the literature,

We will be applying the method of stationary phase at various points throughout our argument. The following version of this result can be found in

where he took

Lemma 3

Let
\{b_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{T}\chi(1-c-it)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{k}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}b_{m}m^{-c-it}\right)dt=\sum_{m\leq\frac{T}{2\pi}}b_{m}(\log m)^{k}+O\left(T^{c-1/2}(\log T)^{k}\right).

We remind the read that in this paper we will have the convention that the Laurent expansion for

about

is given by

\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\gamma_{0}-\gamma_{1}(s-1)+\frac{1}{2!}\gamma_{2}(s-1)^{2}+\dots+(-1)^{j}\frac{1}{j!}\gamma_{j}(s-1)^{j}+\dots.

The last result we need is from the proof of the main result from Hughes and Pearce-Crump

. This result is unconditional in that paper, but as we are assuming the Riemann Hypothesis here, we will also assume it in the statement of this theorem (this only affects the error term).

Theorem 5
.

Assume the Riemann Hypothesis. For
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma
a non-trivial zero of
\zeta(s)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{m_{1}m_{2}\leq T/2\pi}\Lambda(m_{1})(\log m_{1})^{n}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{n+1}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}k!\left(-1+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}\gamma_{j}\right)\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{n-k}+n!A_{n}\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

where the
\gamma_{j}
are the coefficients in the Laurent expansion of
\zeta(s)
about
s=1
, given in (
2.1
) and
A_{n}
is the
n
th coefficient in the Laurent expansion of
\zeta^{\prime}(s)/\zeta(s)
about

.

4.
Proof Theorem
1

4.1.
Initial Manipulations

We note that the beginning of the proofs of Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3
start in a similar way to that of Conrey and Ghosh
[
1
]
, and of Hughes, Lugmayer and Pearce-Crump
[
8
]
. We repeat it here for completeness sake but skip over the details in the later cases.

Under the Riemann Hypothesis, we know that the zeros
\lambda
of
Z^{\prime}(t)
interlace those of
Z(t)
, and so the set of points where
|\zeta(1/2+it)|
achieves its local maximum are exactly the points where
Z^{\prime}(t)=0
.

Let
\rho_{1}=\beta_{1}+i\gamma_{1}
be the zeros of
Z_{1}(s)
. By part (3) of Lemma
1
,
Z_{1}(s)
has
O(\log T)
zeros off the critical line (that is,
\beta_{1}\neq 1/2
). At the zeros off the critical line, we may use part (4) of Lemma
1
together with the Lindelöf bounds on
\zeta(s)
and its derivatives to obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma_{1}\leq T\\
\beta_{1}\neq 1/2\end{subarray}}\zeta^{(n)}(\rho_{1})\ll T^{1/9+\varepsilon}\log T\ll T^{1/9+\varepsilon}

for any integer
n\geq 0
.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma_{1}\leq T\\
\beta_{1}=1/2\end{subarray}}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{1}\right).

We can write this as an integral using Cauchy’s theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\zeta^{(n)}(s)\ ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<\gamma_{1}\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}(\rho_{1})

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma_{1}\leq T\\
\beta_{1}=1/2\end{subarray}}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{1}\right)+O\left(T^{1/9+\varepsilon}\right)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a positively oriented contour with vertices
c+i
,
c+iT
,
1-c+iT
, and
1-c+i
, where
c=1+1/\log T
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that the distance from the contour to any zero
\rho_{1}
of
Z_{1}(s)
is uniformly
\gg 1/\log T
.

We split the integral as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}+\int_{c+iT}^{1-c+iT}+\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}+\int_{1-c+i}^{c+i}\right)\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\zeta^{(n)}(s)\ ds

\displaystyle=S^{R}+S^{T}+S^{L}+S^{B},

say. We will first bound
S^{B},S^{T},S^{R}
trivially within error term, and show that the leading contributions to the asymptotic will then come from
S^{L}
. Consider each of these integrals in turn.

First note that the integral
S^{B}
, which is the integral along the bottom of the contour, is
O(1)
.

For the integral
S^{T}
, which is the integral along the top of the contour, we may use the bound proved in
[
8
]
that states if
|T-\rho_{1}|\gg 1/\log T
for all zeros
\rho_{1}
of
Z_{1}
(which we are in this proof), we have

\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(\sigma+iT)\ll(\log T)^{2}

uniformly for
-1<\sigma\leq 2
. Combining the Lindelöf bounds on
\zeta(s)
and its derivatives with this shows that the integral along the top of the contour is
O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)
.

Next note that for the integral
S^{R}
, which is the integral along the right-hand vertical side of the contour, we have
c>1
which is past the abscissa of convergence for
\zeta^{(n)}(s)
and
Z_{1}(s)
as given in (
3.1
). Then

S^{R}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(c+it)\zeta^{(n)}(c+it)\ dt\ll T^{\varepsilon}.

Note then that
S^{B},S^{T},S^{R}
are all within an error term of
O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)
. All that remains is to evaluate
S^{L}
. Observe that

S^{L}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\zeta^{(n)}(s)\ ds=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c-i}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(1-s)\zeta^{(n)}(1-s)\ ds=-\overline{I},

where

(4.2)

I=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(1-s)\zeta^{(n)}(1-s)\ ds.

Overall, we have

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta^{(n)}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=-\overline{I}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

4.2.
Deriving the main terms

To begin manipulating
I
into a form that we can evaluate, we use the logarithmic derivative of the functional equation for
Z_{1}(s)
given in (
3.2
). We will also use for functional equation for the derivatives of
\zeta(1-s)
, which can be derived easily from the functional equation for
\zeta(s)
, given in
1.5
. Substituting these two expressions into (
4.2
) gives

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\left(-\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)(-1)^{n}\chi(1-s)\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\ ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\left(-\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\right)(-1)^{n}\chi(1-s)\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\ ds+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Simplifying slightly gives

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j+1}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\ ds

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\ ds+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right),

say.

4.3.
The integral
I_{1}

First consider
I_{1}
. We want to apply Lemma
3
here to extract terms for the asymptotic. To do this we begin by switching the order of integration and summation and substitute the Dirichlet series for
\zeta^{(j)}(s)
to obtain

I_{1}=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j+1}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\log m)^{j}}{m^{s}}\ ds\right]

Now apply Lemma
3
to the integral, obtaining

I_{1}=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left[\sum_{m\leq T/2\pi}(\log m)^{n-j+1}(-1)^{j}\log(m)^{j}\right]+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Simplifying this gives

I_{1}=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(-1)^{j}\left[\sum_{m\leq T/2\pi}(\log m)^{n+1}\right]+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Now by the Binomial Theorem, this summation over

equals zero for
n>0
and equals
1

. Hence in the case

I_{1}=-\sum_{m\leq T/2\pi}\log m+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

By standard methods, we have

I_{1}=-\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

and so we have proved the following result.

Lemma 4
.

For
n
a non-negative integer, the integral
I_{1}
satisfies

I_{1}=\begin{cases}-\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)&\text{ if } n=0\\
O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)&\text{ if } n > 0.\end{cases}

4.4.
The integral
I_{2}

Similarly to when we evaluated the integral
I_{1}
, we want to apply stationary phase calculations to extract terms for the asymptotic from

. To do this we begin by switching the order of integration and summation and substitute the Dirichlet series for

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}

say, where we split the initial term of

For

We note that this calculation was preformed in Hughes and Pearce-Crump

(4.3)

I_{2,1}=(-1)^{n}\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{n+1}\\
+(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}k!\left(-1+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}\gamma_{j}\right)\frac{T}{2\pi}\left(\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)^{n-k}\\
-n!A_{n}\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

where we remind the reader that the
\gamma_{j}
are the coefficients from the Laurent expansion of
\zeta(s)
about
s=1
and the

are the coefficients from the Laurent expansion of

about

Finally we need to evaluate

and so

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{<lotsoftext>}\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\frac{2^{k}}{(\log t/2\pi)^{k}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k}{t\log t}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{<lotsoftextlotsoftext>}\times\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j}\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\log m_{1})^{j}}{m_{1}^{s}}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(m_{2}))}{m_{2}^{s}}\ ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{<lotsoftext>}\times\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\frac{2^{k}}{(\log t/2\pi)^{k}}\left(\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)^{n-j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{<lotsoftextlotsoftext>}\times\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\log m_{1})^{j}}{m_{1}^{s}}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(m_{2})}{m_{2}^{s}}\ ds+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

After these initial manipulations performed above, we may apply Lemma

. This gives

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left[\sum_{m_{1}m_{2}\leq T/2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{j}(\log m_{1})^{j}a_{k}(m_{2})(\log m_{1}m_{2})^{n-j}}{(\log m_{1}m_{2})^{k}}\right]

(4.4)

\displaystyle\qquad+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Remark
.

Now we note that we could repeat our trick that we used when switching the order of summation and expanding the logarithm when we evaluated
I_{2,1}
. However it is then unclear what the correct Dirichlet series is to use in the upcoming Perron argument. Instead we take the approach of cancelling the appropriate powers of the logarithm, and work with
I_{2,2}
in this form.

Switching the order of summation in

Lemma 5
.

Let

A_{k,j}(x)=\sum_{m\leq x}\alpha_{k,j}(m),

where

\alpha_{k,j}(m)=\sum_{m_{1}m_{2}=m}(-1)^{j}(\log m_{1})^{j}a_{k}(m_{2})

and where

is given in (

This sum can also be written as

Then for large

A_{k,j}(x)=x\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+j+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}(\log x)^{k+j+1-\ell}+O\left(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

where the
c_{\ell}^{k,j}
are the Laurent series coefficients around

of

\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell}^{k,j}(s-1)^{-k-j-2+\ell}.

Remark
.

A long but straightforward calculation tells us what the coefficients
c_{\ell}^{k,j}
equal. The leading coefficient is when
\ell=0
and is given by

c_{0}^{k,j}=(-1)^{j}j!

for all
j\geq 0
. When
\ell=1
, the subleading coefficients are given by

\displaystyle c_{1}^{k,j}=\begin{cases}-1+\gamma_{0}+(1-k)\gamma_{0}&\text{ if $j=0$}\\
(-1)^{j}j!(-1+(1-k)\gamma_{0})&\text{ for all $j\geq 1$.}\end{cases}

Remark
.

The superscripts on the coefficients
c_{\ell}^{k,j}
is to indicate that the terms depend on both
k
and

.

Proof.

We begin by applying Perron’s formula to
A_{k,j}(x)
, that is, we have

A_{k,j}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{3/2-iR}^{3/2+iR}\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\frac{x^{s}}{s}\ ds+O\left(\frac{x^{3/2+\varepsilon}}{R}\right)

Note that the integrand has a pole of order
k+j+2
at
s=1
. We shift past the pole at
s=1
to the line
\mathfrak{Re}(s)=1/2+\varepsilon
, and evaluate the residue at
s=1
. Therefore, up to a power-saving error,
A_{k,j}(x)
will equal the residue of the integrand around that point. That is,

A_{k,j}(x)=x\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+j+1}b_{\ell}^{k,j}(\log x)^{k+j+1-\ell}+O\left(R^{\varepsilon}x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)+O\left(\frac{x^{3/2+\varepsilon}}{R}\right)

for some constants
b_{\ell}^{k,j}
. We have started the sum at the largest power of
\log x
as this simplifies future calculations. Choosing
R=x
optimises the error terms, giving an error of
O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})
.

To evaluate the residue, we use the various Laurent expansions about
s=1
in the various terms in the integrand. The expansions we need are

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(s-1)^{2}}+\left(-2\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{0}^{2}\right)+\left(6\gamma_{0}\gamma_{1}+3\gamma_{2}+2\gamma_{0}^{3}\right)(s-1)+\dots

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(s-1)^{k-1}}+\frac{(1-k)\gamma_{0}}{(s-1)^{k-2}}+\frac{\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)\gamma_{0}^{2}+2(k-1)\gamma_{1}}{(s-1)^{k-3}}+\dots

\displaystyle\quad\zeta^{(j)}(s)

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{j}j!}{(s-1)^{j+1}}+(-1)^{j}\gamma_{j}+(-1)^{j+1}\gamma_{j+1}(s-1)+\dots

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{s}

\displaystyle=\quad

\displaystyle 1-(s-1)+(s-1)^{2}+...

where the Stieltjes constants are given in (
2.1

Combining these Laurent expansions allows us to write

\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\zeta^{(j)}(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{\ell}^{k,j}(s-1)^{-k-j-2+\ell}

where we have explicitly given the first few values of
c_{\ell}^{k,j}
in the remark following the statement of the lemma.

The residue is the coefficient of

which will come from combining these terms with the

in the integrand (which is the only term that contains an

is

x^{s}=x\left(1+(s-1)\log x+\frac{(s-1)^{2}}{2!}(\log x)^{2}+...+\frac{(s-1)^{k}}{k!}(\log x)^{k}+...\right)

so the coefficient of
(\log x)^{k+j+1-\ell}
in the

b_{\ell}^{k,j}=\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}

and such a combination is possible for
\ell
between
0

k+j+1
.
∎

To complete the proof of Theorem

, we want to use the asymptotic from Lemma
5
in (
4.5
) and use partial summation to reinsert the logarithm in the denominator of the inner sum in (
4.4
).

This means that we need to calculate

I_{2,2}=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left[A_{k}\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)f\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)-\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}A_{k}(x)f^{\prime}(x)\ dx\right]+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

with

Then by partial summation,

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespace}\left[\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+j+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}L^{n+1-\ell}\right.

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespacespace}+\left.(k-n+j)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+j+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{n-\ell}\ dx\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespacespacespacespacespacespace}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

where we write

Lemma 6

Writing

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left(L^{n+1}\frac{c_{0}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1)!}\right.

We begin by noting from (

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespace}\left[\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}L^{n+1-\ell}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespacespace}\left.+(k-n+j)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{n-\ell}\ dx\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespace}\left.+\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=n+2}^{k+j+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}L^{n+1-\ell}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespacespace}\left.\left.+(k-n+j)\sum_{\ell=n+1}^{k+j+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{n-\ell}\ dx\right)\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespacespacespacespacespacespace}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

For ease of notation, we write the sum

We now consider the two sums

Then using this (and ignoring the error term as it is consumed by larger error terms that we have already seen in this proof), we have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{n+1}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}L^{n+1-\ell}\right.

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}L^{n+1}\frac{c_{0}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1)!}

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespa}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{c_{\ell+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-\ell)!}L^{n-\ell}\right.

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespa}\left.+(k-n+j)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\left(\sum_{r=0}^{n-\ell}(-1)^{r}\frac{(n-\ell)!}{(n-\ell-r)!}L^{n-\ell-r}\right)\right].

We now reorder the second summation to write

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}L^{n+1}\frac{c_{0}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1)!}

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespa}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\left[\frac{c_{\ell+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-\ell)!}+(k-n+j)\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\right.

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+j+1}\frac{c_{\ell+n+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-n-\ell)!}L^{-\ell}

\displaystyle\hphantom{spacespacespacespace}+(k-n+j)\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+j+1-n}\frac{c_{\ell+n}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-n-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-\ell}\ dx,

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{m}}\ dx

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=\frac{c_{m+n+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-n-m)!}+\frac{(m-1)!}{k-n+j-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\binom{k-n+j}{\ell-1}c_{\ell+n}^{k,j}.

If

After a trivial relabelling for both versions of

∎

We can see that we have

\displaystyle I_{2,2}=\mathfrak{a}_{n+1}\frac{T}{2\pi}L^{n+1}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\mathfrak{a}_{n-\ell}L^{n-\ell}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{\mathfrak{b}_{m}}{L^{m}}+O\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right),

where the leading order coefficient is given by

the subleading, non-negative logarithm power coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\left[\frac{c_{\ell+1}^{k,j}}{(k+j-\ell)!}+(k-n+j)\frac{c_{\ell}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-\ell)!}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.(k-n+j)\sum_{m=0}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{\ell-m}(n-m)\dots(n-\ell+1)\frac{c_{m}^{k,j}}{(k+j+1-m)!}\right],

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}\beta_{m}^{k,j},

where for

\displaystyle I_{2,2}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{n^{s}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m^{s}}\ ds

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{nm\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log nm)^{k}}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

As in the proof of Theorem
1
, we evaluate the numerator of the inner sum in the previous line via Perron. This is actually a special case of Lemma
5
in the case
j=0
, so we have the following result.

Lemma 7
.

Let

A_{k}(x)=\sum_{mn\leq x}a_{k}(n),

where
a_{k}(n)
is given in (
3.7
). This sum can also be written as

A_{k}(x)=\sum_{1\leq mn_{1}n_{2}...n_{k}\leq x}\log(n_{1})\Lambda(n_{1})\Lambda(n_{2})\dots\Lambda(n_{k}).

Then for large
x
,

A_{k}(x)=x\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k+1-\ell)!}(\log x)^{k+1-\ell}+O\left(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

where the
c_{k,\ell}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\zeta(s)\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{k,\ell}(s-1)^{-k-2+\ell}.

Remark
.

A long but straightforward calculation tells us what the coefficients
c_{k,\ell}
equal. The leading coefficient is when
\ell=0
and is given by

c_{k,0}=1.

When
\ell=1
, the subleading coefficient is given by

c_{k,1}=-1+\gamma_{0}+(1-k)\gamma_{0}.

Next we use partial summation to calculate

\sum_{nm\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log nm)^{k}}=A_{k}\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)f\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)-\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}A_{k}(x)f^{\prime}(x)\ dx,

in the case
f(x)=1/(\log x)^{k}
(so
f^{\prime}(x)=-k/(x(\log x)^{k+1})
). Then using
A_{k}(x)
from Lemma
7
, we have with
L=\log T/2\pi
,

\sum_{nm\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log nm)^{k}}=\\
\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k+1-\ell)!}L^{1-\ell}+k\sum_{\ell=0}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-\ell}\ dx+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

As before, we extract the non-negative powers of the logarithm and calculate the coefficients of the negative powers as we clearly have an infinite chain of decreasing powers of the logarithm. This gives

\sum_{nm\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log nm)^{k}}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{c_{k,0}}{(k+1)!}L+\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{c_{k,1}}{k!}+\frac{T}{2\pi}k\frac{c_{k,0}}{(k+1)!}\\
+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=2}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k+1-\ell)!}L^{1-\ell}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-\ell}\ dx+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

For the negative powers of the logarithm, we reindex so both sums start at
\ell=1
to give

\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell+1}}{(k-\ell)!}L^{-\ell}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-\ell}\ dx.

Recall that for
1\leq m\leq M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{m}}\ dx

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{1}{L^{m}}+m\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{m+1}}\ dt+O(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{r=m}^{M}\frac{(r-1)!}{(m-1)!}\frac{1}{L^{r}}+O\left(\frac{T}{L^{M+1}}\right).

We now write
\beta_{m}^{k,\ell}
for the coefficient of
L^{-m}
. In an analogous way to to the proof of Theorem
1
, we have for
m\leq k
,

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=\frac{c_{k,m+1}}{(k-m)!}+k\frac{(m-1)!}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\binom{k}{\ell}c_{k,\ell+1}

and where for
m\leq k+j-n+1
, we have

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=k\frac{(m-1)!}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}c_{k,\ell+1},

where the constants
c_{k,\ell}
are given in Lemma
7
.

In order to complete the proof, we multiply through by
-2^{k}
, and sum over all
k\geq 1
to obtain
I_{2,2}
. Since the combination from the rest of the calculation only contributes to the error term, we have evaluated
I
and so the sum in question.

Note that since we have a simple form for
c_{k,0}
and
c_{k,1}
, we can easily calculate the leading and subleading behaviour explicitly, giving for
K
a positive integer

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=\frac{e^{2}-3}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}L+\frac{3-e^{2}-4\gamma_{0}}{2}\frac{T}{2\pi}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=1}^{K}\frac{d_{m}}{L^{m}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, where

d_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\beta_{m}^{k,\ell},

with the
\beta_{m}^{k,\ell}
as above.

6.
Proof Theorem
3

The main steps in the proof of Theorem
3
follow in a similar way to Theorem
1
. We sketch the steps here for completeness sake.

Under the Riemann Hypothesis we can show that all the zeros off the critical line only contribute to the error term in our asymptotic with a similar justification to the proof of Theorem
1
. Recall
\chi(s)\ll T^{1/2-\sigma}
for
s=\sigma+it
with
|t|\ll T
. Write
\rho_{1}=\beta_{1}+i\gamma_{1}
for a zero of
Z_{1}(s)
. By part (3) of Lemma
1
,
Z_{1}(s)
has
O(\log T)
zeros off the critical line (that is,
\beta_{1}\neq 1/2
). At the zeros off the critical line, we may use part (4) of Lemma
1
to obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma_{1}\leq T\\
\beta_{1}\neq 1/2\end{subarray}}\chi(\rho_{1})\ll T^{1/9}\log T\ll T^{1/9+\varepsilon}.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma_{1}\leq T\\
\beta_{1}=1/2\end{subarray}}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{1}\right).

We can write this as an integral using Cauchy’s theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\chi(s)\ ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<\gamma_{1}\leq T}\chi(\rho_{1})

(6.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\gamma_{1}\leq T\\
\beta_{1}=1/2\end{subarray}}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma_{1}\right)+O\left(T^{1/9+\varepsilon}\right)

where
\mathcal{C}
is a positively oriented contour with vertices
c+i
,
c+iT
,
1-c+iT
, and
1-c+i
, where
c=1+1/\log T
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that the distance from the contour to any zero
\rho_{1}
of
Z_{1}(s)
is uniformly
\gg 1/\log T
.

We split the integral as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\left(\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}+\int_{c+iT}^{1-c+iT}+\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}+\int_{1-c+i}^{c+i}\right)\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\chi(s)\ ds

\displaystyle=S^{R}+S^{T}+S^{L}+S^{B},

say. Note that
S^{B},S^{T},S^{R}
are all trivially bounded within error term in a similar way to the proof of Theorem
1
, using the bound
\chi(s)\ll T^{1/2-\sigma}
where appropriate. This shows that the leading contributions to the asymptotic will then come from
S^{L}
.

Since
S^{B},S^{T},S^{R}
are all within an error term of
O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)
, all that remains is to evaluate
S^{L}
. Note that

S^{L}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\chi(s)\ ds=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c-i}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(1-s)\chi(1-s)\ ds=-\overline{I},

where

(6.2)

I=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(1-s)\chi(1-s)\ ds.

Overall, we have

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=-\overline{I}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Using the version of the functional equation for
Z_{1}^{\prime}(s)/Z_{1}(s)
given in (
3.4
), we have

I=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\left(-\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\right)\chi(1-s)\ ds\\
+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\left(-\frac{Z_{1}^{\prime}}{Z_{1}}(s)\right)\chi(1-s)\ ds+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)\\
=I_{1}+I_{2}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right),

say.

Then
I_{1}
doesn’t contribute to anything beyond an error term, so we split
I_{2}
as before to write

I_{2}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^{s}}\right)\chi(1-s)\ ds\\
-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\chi(1-s)\frac{1}{f(s)^{k}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{n^{s}}\ ds+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)\\
=I_{2,1}+I_{2,2}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Then applying Lemma
3
to
I_{2,1}
we have

I_{2,1}=\sum_{n\leq T/2\pi}\Lambda(n)+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

By Perron/the Prime Number Theorem, we have

(6.3)

I_{2,1}=\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Finally, applying Lemma
3
to
I_{2,2}
gives

I_{2,2}=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k}\sum_{n\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log n)^{k}}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

As in the proof of Theorem
1
, we evaluate the numerator of the inner sum in the previous line via Perron.

Lemma 8
.

Let

A_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}a_{k}(n),

where
a_{k}(n)
is given in (
3.7
). This sum can also be written as

A_{k}(x)=\sum_{1\leq n_{1}n_{2}...n_{k}\leq x}\log(n_{1})\Lambda(n_{1})\Lambda(n_{2})\dots\Lambda(n_{k}).

Then for large
x
,

A_{k}(x)=x\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}(\log x)^{k-\ell}+O\left(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)

where the
c_{k,\ell}
are the Laurent series coefficients around
s=1
of

\left(\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{\prime}\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\right)^{k-1}\frac{1}{s}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}c_{k,\ell}(s-1)^{-k-1+\ell}.

Remark
.

A long but straightforward calculation tells us what the coefficients
c_{k,\ell}
equal. The leading coefficient is when
\ell=0
and is given by

c_{k,0}=1.

When
\ell=1
, the subleading coefficient is given by

c_{k,1}=-1+\gamma_{0}-k\gamma_{0}.

Next we use partial summation to calculate

\sum_{n\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log n)^{k}}=A_{k}\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)f\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}\right)-\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}A_{k}(x)f^{\prime}(x)\ dx,

in the case
f(x)=1/(\log x)^{k}
(so
f^{\prime}(x)=-k/(x(\log x)^{k+1})
). Then using
A_{k}(x)
from Lemma
8
, we have with
L=\log T/2\pi
,

\sum_{n\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log n)^{k}}=\\
\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}L^{-\ell}+k\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-1-\ell}\ dx+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

As before, we extract the non-negative powers of the logarithm and calculate the coefficients of the negative powers as we clearly have an infinite chain of decreasing powers of the logarithm. This gives

\sum_{n\leq T/2\pi}\frac{a_{k}(n)}{(\log n)^{k}}=\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{c_{k,0}}{k!}L\\
+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}L^{-\ell}+k\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-1-\ell}\ dx+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

For the negative powers of the logarithm, we reindex so both sums start at
\ell=1
to give

\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\frac{c_{k,\ell}}{(k-\ell)!}L^{-\ell}+k\sum_{\ell=1}^{k+1}\frac{c_{k,\ell-1}}{(k+1-\ell)!}\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}(\log x)^{-\ell}\ dx.

Recall that for
1\leq m\leq M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{m}}\ dx

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{1}{L^{m}}+m\int_{2}^{T/2\pi}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{m+1}}\ dt+O(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{r=m}^{M}\frac{(r-1)!}{(m-1)!}\frac{1}{L^{r}}+O\left(\frac{T}{L^{M+1}}\right).

We now write
\beta_{m}^{k,\ell}
for the coefficient of

. In an analogous way to to the proof of Theorem
1
, we have for
m\leq k
,

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=\frac{c_{k,m}}{(k-m)!}+\frac{(m-1)!}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}\binom{k}{\ell}c_{k,\ell}

and where for
m\leq k+j-n+1
, we have

\beta_{m}^{k,j}=\frac{(m-1)!}{(k-1)!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}c_{k,\ell},

where the constants

are given in Lemma

In order to complete the proof, we multiply through by
-2^{k}

I_{2,2}
. Once we add in the contribution from

given in (

Note that since we have a simple form for

\sum_{0<\lambda\leq T}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\lambda\right)=(e^{2}-2)\frac{T}{2\pi}-4e^{2}\gamma_{0}\frac{T}{2\pi}\frac{1}{L}+\frac{T}{2\pi}\sum_{m=2}^{K}\frac{e_{m}}{L^{m}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{T}{L^{K+1}}\right)

with the
\beta_{m}^{k,\ell}

7.
Outline of the proof of Theorem
4

The proof of Theorem
4
is very similar to that of Theorem
3
. We just sketch out the main steps here. Note that this result can be made unconditional but we assume the Riemann Hypothesis to make the error terms trivial to deal with.

We begin by using Cauchy’s theorem to write

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\chi(s)\ ds,

where
\mathcal{C}
is a positively oriented contour with vertices
c+i
,
c+iT
,
1-c+iT
, and
1-c+i
, where
c=1+1/\log T
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that the distance from the contour to any zero of
\zeta(s)
is uniformly
\gg 1/\log T
.

Note that the integrals along the bottom, top and right-hand side of the contour are bounded by
O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)
, using the bound
\chi(s)\ll T^{1/2-\sigma}
for
s=\sigma+it
with
|t|\ll T
. As before, the only contribution comes from the left-hand side of the contour, and as before, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1-c+iT}^{1-c+i}\frac{\zeta}{\zeta}(s)\chi(s)\ ds=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-iT}^{c-i}\frac{\zeta}{\zeta}(1-s)\chi(1-s)\ ds=-\overline{I},

where

I=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\frac{\zeta}{\zeta}(1-s)\chi(1-s)\ ds.

Overall, we have

\sum_{0<\gamma\leq T}\chi\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\gamma\right)=-\overline{I}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Next we need the functional equation for
\zeta^{\prime}(s)/\zeta(s)
, which can be derived easily from the functional equation for
\zeta(s)
in (
1.5
). This gives

\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(1-s)=-\log\frac{t}{2\pi}-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)+O\left(\frac{1}{|t|}\right).

Substituting this into
I
gives

I=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\chi(1-s)\ ds\\
-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c+i}^{c+iT}\frac{\zeta^{\prime}}{\zeta}(s)\chi(1-s)\ ds+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)\\
=I_{1}+I_{2}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Clearly
I_{1}
doesn’t contribute to the asymptotic, so consider
I_{2}
. Then by Lemma
3
,

I_{2}=\sum_{n\leq T/2\pi}\Lambda(n)+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Then by Perron/the Prime Number Theorem,

I_{2}=\frac{T}{2\pi}+O\left(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

The result then follows by working back up the proof.

Remark
.

We could see this result directly from the proof of Theorem
3
, noticing that the logarithmic derivative of
\zeta(s)
is included in that of
Z_{1}(s)
, and making the obvious changes to the proof. We have included the basic steps above for clarity.

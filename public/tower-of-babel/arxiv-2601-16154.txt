Polynomial-time thermalization and Gibbs sampling from system-bath couplings
Sam Slezak, Matteo Scandi, Daniel Stilck França, Álvaro M. Alhambra, Cambyse Rouzé

Abstract
Many physical phenomena, including thermalization in open quantum systems and quantum Gibbs sampling, are modeled by Lindbladians approximating a system weakly coupled to a bath. Understanding the convergence speed of these Lindbladians to their steady states is crucial for bounding algorithmic runtimes and thermalization timescales. We study two such families of processes: one characterizing a repeated-interaction Gibbs sampling algorithm, and another modeling open many-body quantum thermalization. We prove that both converge in polynomial time for several non-commuting systems, including high-temperature local lattices, weakly interacting fermions, and 1D spin chains. These results demonstrate that simple dissipative quantum algorithms can prepare complex Gibbs states and that Lindblad dynamics accurately capture thermal relaxation. Our proofs rely on a novel technical result that extrapolates spectral gap lower bounds from quasi-local Lindbladians to the non-local generators governing these dynamics.

Systems in nature often

This process is typically understood as a system which is weakly coupled to a heat bath, which in the quantum realm is often described through a Lindbladian. Chen et al. 2023b
;
Ding et al. 2025a
introduced a method to simulate Lindblad evolutions that both have an efficient implementation, and also satisfy Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) detailed balance exactly, which guarantees convergence to the Gibbs state. Later work characterized the mixing time of these Lindbladians in a variety of situations including high temperatures, weak interactions, and

The algorithms in

, while conceptually elegant and asymptotically optimal, have complicated implementations including block encodings and operator Fourier transforms, and are not suitable for near term implementation.
In light of this, simpler Gibbs sampling algorithms with implementations much closer to the physical models they imitate were introduced in

In particular, the algorithm introduced in

In our first contribution, we prove a collection of efficient preparation results for this algorithm, extending the performance guarantees to all the aforementioned fast mixing systems, which includes a wide range of physically relevant many-body settings

The notion of KMS detailed balance is relevant beyond quantum algorithms, since it also seems to be the correct notion of open-system thermalization towards the many-body Gibbs state

;
Mozgunov and Lidar 2020
;
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025

;

for reviews). We also turn to the model of thermalization satisfying KMS detailed balance derived in
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
from a weak system-bath interaction under natural open-system assumptions. We show that this evolution converges to its equilibrium state in polynomial time for two related settings: arbitrary local Hamiltonians at high temperatures
Rouzé et al. 2024b
, and deviations from product Hamiltonians

. This proves from first principles that open many body systems weakly coupled to high-temperature baths reach their Gibbs steady state both quickly and accurately, as expected on physical grounds. Thus, KMS Lindbladians accurately capture the natural many-body thermalization, down to the steady state. Previous results on physically-generated Lindbladians were only known for commuting models
Kastoryano and Brandao 2016
;
Bardet et al. 2023a
;
Kochanowski et al. 2025

.

Our results on KMS Lindbladians are enabled by the same underlying technical contribution, which allows us to extrapolate spectral gap lower bounds from quasi-local to non-local Lindbladians, which we believe is of independent interest. Spectral gaps are well-known to govern the speed at which a dissipative evolution convergences to its equilibrium. The majority of currently known proofs for spectral gaps of KMS symmetric Lindbladians rely on the Lindbladians consisting of terms with quasi-local support, allowing for an extrapolation to a trivially fast-mixing point. Both the algorithm in
Ding et al. 2025b
and the model in
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025

Furthermore, we believe this technique can be used to show that many other Lindbladians out of the reach of current proof techniques are fast mixing as well and it allows us to conclude that various Gibbs samplers proposed in the literature have a qualitatively similar behaviour when it comes to their mixing times.

Overall, our results give theoretical footing to the early-fault tolerant dissipative preparation of complex quantum states, which has recently seen relevant experimental progress
Mi et al. 2024

, and appears to be a promising avenue for practical quantum advantage
Lin 2025
.

Efficient physical thermalization

We also prove fast convergence of the generators originated through weak coupling with a bath

Given (
5

In order for the bath to thermalize well, we need two physically motivated assumptions on its correlation functions, summarized as (see Appendix
for the precise formulation)

•

The bath correlation function decays rapidly with time.

•

The timescale is
\tau=\mathcal{O}(\beta)
at high temperatures.

These essentially imply that the information dissipates sufficiently quickly in the bath and that it induces white noise at high temperatures, so that in particular at
\beta=0

Under these, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 2

\beta\leq\beta^{*}

\alpha=\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(N^{-3}\epsilon\right)

In Appendix

Faster thermalization for commuting models

We also consider the generators from macroscopic baths

IV
Methods

Our results rely on the following lemma, which shows that the spectral gaps of certain single parameter families of Lindbladians are monotonic.
For the Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}^{\operatorname{G}}
, the parameter corresponds to the width
\kappa
of the function
f_{\kappa}
(
4
), while for the Lindbladian in
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
it is the coupling strength
\alpha

\kappa
increases and
\alpha

We recall that the spectral gap
\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L})
of a KMS symmetric Lindbladian is defined as its smallest non-zero eigenvalue in absolute value. It controls the speed at which the evolution generated by
\mathcal{L}
converges to its Gibbs state:

\displaystyle\big\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)-\rho_{\beta}\big\|_{1}\leq 2e^{-\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L})t}\|\rho_{\beta}^{-1}\|.

Lemma 1
.

The following gap monotonicity statements hold true for the families
\{\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{G}}_{\kappa}\}_{\kappa\geq 1}
and
\{\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{MB}}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\geq 0}
:

1.

For any two
1\!\leq\!\kappa\!\leq\!\kappa^{\prime}\!<\!\infty
,
\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{G}}_{\kappa})\leq\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{G}}_{\kappa^{\prime}})
;

2.

For any two
0<\alpha\leq\alpha^{\prime}
,
\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{MB}}_{\alpha^{\prime}})\leq\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{MB}}_{\alpha})
.

Lemma
thus allows us to connect the gaps of two complementary regimes: that in which the dynamics are accurately approximated by Lindbladians, and that in which spectral gap estimates are within reach.
It is proven in Appendix
and relies on the ability to write the Dirichlet forms of the associated Lindbladians as convolutions. An identical result holds for the closely related
modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality
(MLSI) constant, which controls the rate at which the entropy of the evolved state converges to that of the Gibbs state
\rho_{\beta}^{S}

Kastoryano and Temme 2013
; see Appendix
for more details.

V
Discussion and Conclusion

We have demonstrated the efficient preparation of Gibbs states for both near term quantum Gibbs sampling algorithms and thermalization models for many-body quantum systems. In doing so we introduced a technical result that greatly extends the regimes in which fast mixing results can be proven for KMS symmetric Lindbladians. Quasi-locality of the jump operators was a necessity of previously existing proofs, whereas now we are able to extend them to manifestly non-local Lindbladians.

We believe it is possible to extend Theorem
to weakly interacting fermions and 1D to the generator, which require extending the mixing time bounds from
Šm\́operatorname{id}d et al. 2025
;
Bergamaschi and Chen 2025
to our setting. Another important question is what is the most general set of conditions on the bath that guarantees fast convergence.

While our results allow the extension of MLSI constants for quasi-local Lindbladians to non-local ones, there are currently no examples of MLSI constants for non-commuting systems. Instead, rapid mixing (converging in time
\mathcal{O}(\log(N))
) results in this regime have been proven via an approach using the so-called oscillator norm
Rouzé et al. 2024b
;
Šm\́operatorname{id}d et al. 2025
;
Zhan et al. 2025
. Interesting open problems are thus proving MLSI constants for quasi-local Lindbladians and seeing if a similar monotonicity result can be derived for the oscillator norm method.

Appendix A
KMS Symmetric Lindbladians and mixing times

In this section we review the relevant properties of KMS symmetric Lindbladians and prove Lemma (
).
The Kubo-Martin-Schwinger (KMS) inner product with respect to the full rank state
\rho_{\beta}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is given by:

\displaystyle\langle X,Y\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}:=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_{\beta}^{1/2}X^{\dagger}\rho_{\beta}^{1/2}Y].

The KMS inner product can be used to define a
\rho_{\beta}
weighted version of the
2
-norm via
\|X\|_{\rho_{\beta}}^{2}:=\langle X,X\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}
.
A Heisenberg picture Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}
is said to be KMS symmetric if it is self adjoint with respect to the KMS inner product (this property is also often called
\rho_{\beta}
detailed balance). That is:

\displaystyle\langle X,\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(Y)\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}=\langle\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(X),Y\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\;\forall\;X,Y.

This property ensures that
\mathcal{L}(\rho_{\beta})=0
and thus that the evolution generated by
\mathcal{L}
converges to
\rho_{\beta}
.
Throughout the following we will assume that
\rho_{\beta}=\frac{e^{-\beta H}}{\operatorname{Tr}[e^{-\beta H}]}
for a Hamiltonian
H
with the eigendecomposition
H=\sum_{i}E_{i}\Pi_{i}
over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
for a complete set of orthogonal projectors
\Pi_{i}
. The Bohr frequencies of
H
are defined as the set:

\displaystyle B_{H}:=\{\nu=E_{i}-E_{j}:E_{i},E_{j}\in\operatorname{spec}(H)\}.

Given an operator
A
and a Bohr frequency
\nu\in B_{H}
, we define the operator
A_{\nu}
as
A
where its allowed transitions are restricted to energy subspaces separated by
\nu
:

\displaystyle A_{\nu}:=\sum_{i,j:E_{i}-E_{j}=\nu}\Pi_{i}\,A\,\Pi_{j}.

A general form for KMS symmetric Lindbladians in the Heisenberg picture is then given by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(X)=i\sum_{\nu\in B_{H}}[B_{\nu},X]+\sum_{a}\sum_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in B_{H}}\Lambda^{a}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\left(A^{a\dagger}_{\nu_{2}}XA^{a}_{\nu_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}\{A^{a\dagger}_{\nu_{2}}A^{a}_{\nu_{1}},X\}\right)

(7)

for some coefficients
\Lambda^{a}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}
satisfying
\Lambda^{a}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\overline{\Lambda^{a}}_{-\nu_{1},-\nu_{2}}e^{-\beta\left(\frac{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}{2}\right)}
, and

\displaystyle B_{\nu}:=\sum_{a}\sum_{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}=\nu}\frac{\operatorname{tanh}(-\beta(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})/4)}{2i}\Lambda^{a}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}A_{\nu_{2}}^{a\dagger}A^{a}_{\nu_{1}}\,.

(8)

An important object in the study of Lindbladians is the Dirichlet form, defined for a Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}
via:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X,Y):=-\langle X,\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(Y)\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}.

Given a KMS symmetric Lindbladian
\mathcal{L}
in the general form (
7
), its Dirichlet form is given by (see
Rouzé et al. 2024a
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(X,Y)=\sum_{a,\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\frac{\Lambda^{a}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}e^{\beta(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})/4}}{\cosh(\beta(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})/4)}\langle[A^{a}_{\nu_{1}},X],[A^{a}_{\nu_{2}},Y]\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}.

Spectral gaps and fast mixing

The Dirichlet form can be used to define a variational form of the spectral gap of a Lindbladian via the optimization:

\displaystyle\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}):=\inf_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{sa}}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{\mathcal{E}(X)}{\|X-\langle\mathbb{I},X\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\mathbb{I}\|_{\rho_{\beta}}^{2}},

(9)

where
\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{sa}}(\mathcal{H})
stands for the subspace of self-adjoint matrices over
\mathcal{H}
, and we denote
\mathcal{E}(X)\equiv\mathcal{E}(X,X)
. The spectral gap is well-known to control the mixing time of the evolution generated by
\mathcal{L}
:

\displaystyle t_{\operatorname{mix}}(\epsilon):=\inf\left\{t:\left\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)-\rho_{\beta}\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon\;\forall\;\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})\right\},

where
\|\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.6}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}\|_{1}
stands for the trace distance on the algebra
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of matrices over
\mathcal{H}
, and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
stands for the set of quantum states. Indeed,

\displaystyle\big\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)-\rho_{\beta}\big\|_{1}\leq 2e^{-\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L})t}\|\rho_{\beta}^{-1}\|\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad t_{\operatorname{mix}}(\epsilon)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}}\log\left(\frac{2\|\rho_{\beta}^{-1}\|}{\epsilon}\right).

(10)

Modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality and rapid mixing

A much tighter bound on the mixing time can be obtained using entropy decay estimates: we recall Umegaki’s definition of the quantum relative entropy between any state
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
and
\rho_{\beta}
:

\displaystyle D(\sigma\|\rho_{\beta}):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma(\log(\sigma)-\log(\rho_{\beta}))\right).

The semigroup
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
satisfies the
exponential entropy decay
with rate
\alpha>0
if for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
and
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle D(e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)\|\rho_{\beta})\leq e^{-\alpha t}D(\sigma\|\rho_{\beta}).

Via Pinsker’s inequality, the entropy decay bound leads to the following bound on the mixing time:

\displaystyle\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)-\rho_{\beta}\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2e^{-\alpha t}D(\sigma\|\rho_{\beta})}\leq\sqrt{2e^{-\alpha t}\log(\|\rho_{\beta}^{-1}\|)}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad t_{\operatorname{mix}}(\epsilon)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\log\left(\frac{2\log(\|\rho_{\beta}^{-1}\|)}{\epsilon^{2}}\right).

(11)

Next, we define the
entropy production
of
\rho
as
Spohn 1978
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{EP}(\sigma):=-\frac{d}{dt}D(e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\sigma)\|\rho_{\beta})=-\operatorname{Tr}\left(\mathcal{L}(\sigma)(\log\sigma-\log\rho_{\beta})\right).

By Grönwall’s inequality, the semigroup generated by
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the exponential entropy decay with rate
\alpha>0
if and only if, for any state
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
,

\displaystyle\alpha\,D(\sigma\|\rho_{\beta})\leq\operatorname{EP}(\sigma).

(12)

The inequality (
12
) is known as the
\alpha
-
modified logarithmic Sobolev inequality
(MLSI).
The best constant
\alpha
satisfying such bounds is called the MLSI constant and satisfies the optimization

\displaystyle\alpha_{\operatorname{MLSI}}(\mathcal{L}):=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{\operatorname{EP}(\sigma)}{D(\sigma\|\rho_{\beta})}.

A monotonicity lemma for the spectral gaps and MLSI constants of KMS symmetric Lindbladians.

We now prove the folowing Lemma, which shows that certain
1

Lemma 5

Let
\{\mathcal{L}_{\delta}\}_{\delta\geq 0}
be a parametrized family of primitive, KMS symmetric Lindbladians with same fixed point
\rho_{\beta}

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{(\delta)}_{XY}=f_{\delta}\star\mathcal{E}_{XY}^{(\infty)}

(13)

\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle f_{\delta}=g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\star f_{\delta^{\prime}}

(14)

\delta\leq\delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}_{\delta})\geq\|g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\|_{1}\,\lambda_{\operatorname{Gap}}(\mathcal{L}_{\delta^{\prime}})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\alpha_{\operatorname{MLSI}}(\mathcal{L}_{\delta})\geq\|g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\|_{1}\,\alpha_{\operatorname{MLSI}}(\mathcal{L}_{\delta^{\prime}}).

(15)

Proof.

The variational expression of the gap (

\displaystyle\lambda_{\delta}=\inf_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{sa}}(\mathcal{H})}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\delta}(X)}{\|X-\langle\mathbbm{I},X\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\mathbbm{I}\|_{\rho_{\beta}}^{2}}.

By the condition (
13

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\delta}(X)=-\langle X,\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}_{\delta}(X)\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}=f_{\delta}\star\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}_{XX}(0).

Using the semigroup property (
14
), we can further write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\delta}(X)=\big(g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\star f_{\delta^{\prime}}\star\mathcal{E}^{(\infty)}_{XX}\big)(0)=\big(g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}\star\mathcal{E}^{(\delta^{\prime})}_{XX}\big)(0).

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\delta}(X)}{\|X-\langle\mathbb{I},X\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\mathbb{I}\|^{2}_{\rho_{\beta}}}

\displaystyle=\int g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}(s)\,\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\delta^{\prime}}(X(-\beta s))}{\|X-\langle\mathbb{I},X\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\mathbb{I}\|_{\rho_{\beta}}^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle=\int g_{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}(s)\,\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\delta^{\prime}}(X(-\beta s))}{\|X(-\beta s)-\langle\mathbb{I},X(-\beta s)\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\mathbb{I}\|_{\rho_{\beta}}^{2}}\,ds.

The result for the gap follows directly after lower bounding the ratio in the integrand by
\lambda_{\delta^{\prime}}
and minimizing over

D(\sigma\|\rho_{\beta})=D(\sigma(\beta s)\|\rho_{\beta})
for all

\displaystyle\operatorname{EP}_{\delta}(\sigma(\beta s))=-\operatorname{Tr}\big(\mathcal{L}_{\delta}(\sigma(\beta s))(\log\sigma(\beta s)-\log\rho_{\beta})\big)=-\langle\mathcal{L}_{\delta}^{\dagger}(X(s)),Y(s)\rangle_{\rho_{\beta}}\equiv\mathcal{E}^{(\delta)}_{XY}(s),

where
X:=\rho_{\beta}^{-1/2}\sigma\rho_{\beta}^{-1/2}
and

.

C.1

Building on the main result of

The reason why this is possible appears evident once the main ingredients in the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\lambda\dagger}-\mathcal{L}_{a}^{\lambda=0\dagger}\|_{\infty\to\infty}

Let us define

\displaystyle\|A^{a,\lambda}(t)-A^{a,0}(t)\|\leq\|A^{a,\lambda}(t)-A^{a,\lambda,r}(t)\|+\|A^{a,\lambda,r}(t)-A^{a,0,r}(t)\|+\|A^{a,0,r}(t)-A^{a,0}(t)\|\,.

We can bound the norm in the middle as:

(97)

(98)

where in the second line used an an upper-bound on the number of balls of radius

Appendix D
Faster convergence via reduction to the Davies Lindbladian in the commuting case

Suppose
H_{S}
is a
k
-local commuting Hamiltonian, so that it can be written as
H_{S}=\sum_{i}\;h_{i}
, where the sum is over all system sites, each
h_{i}
acts over at most
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
terms, and for any
i
and
j
, it holds that
[h_{i},\,h_{j}]=0
. For these systems, the commutativity of the
\{h_{i}\}
implies that the jump operators are strictly local. This special feature allows us to approximate the physical evolution with a Davies Lindbladian
Davies 1974
;
Davies 1976
with an error bound compatible with the other approximations, as recalled here. The commutativity by itself also implies that the relevant minimum non-zero spacing between any two different frequencies
\nu_{-}^{\operatorname{min}}=\Omega\left(1\right)
is independent of the system size.
Let the Davies generator be given in the frequency representation as:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\operatorname{D}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{\nu\in B_{H}}\left(-\frac{i}{2}S_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu)\left[A^{a\dagger}_{\nu}A^{a}_{\nu},\rho\right]+\gamma_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu)\Big(A^{a}_{\nu}\rho A^{a\dagger}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\{A^{a\dagger}_{\nu}A^{a}_{\nu},\rho\}\Big)\right)\,.

(102)

where the rates
\gamma_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu)
and
S_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu)
are related to the correlation function of the bath as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\gamma_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu)=\widehat{C}_{aa}\left(\nu\right)\\
S_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu)={\rm P.V.}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\omega^{*}\;\frac{\widehat{C}_{aa}(\omega^{*})}{(\omega^{*}-\nu)}\right)\end{cases}\,.

(103)

In
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
it was shown that the rates of
\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{MB}}_{\alpha}
are close to the ones of the Davies generator. This result, together with the locality of the jump operators, allows us to prove the following.

Lemma 20
.

Let
H_{S}
be a
k
-local commuting Hamiltonian. Let
d_{k}
be the dimension of the Hilbert space corresponding to a region of size
k
in the physical space. Then, it holds that for any initial state
\rho_{S}(0)
:

\displaystyle\|\rho_{S}(t)\!-\!e^{t\alpha^{2}\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{D}}}\rho_{S}(0)\|_{1}\!=\!\mathcal{O}\!\left(\alpha^{3}(\Gamma t)(\Gamma\tau)\!\left(1\!+d_{k}^{6}\right)\right)\!.

(104)

Proof.

Following
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
, we introduce the coarse grained Lindbladian:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha}^{\operatorname{CG}}[\rho]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}\sum_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in B_{H}}\left(-\frac{i}{2}S_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\left[A^{a\dagger}_{\nu_{2}}A^{a}_{\nu_{1}},\rho\right]+\gamma_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}\Big(A^{a}_{\nu_{1}}\rho A^{a\dagger}_{\nu_{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\{A^{a\dagger}_{\nu_{2}}A^{a}_{\nu_{1}},\rho\}\Big)\right)\,,

(105)

where we introduced the rates:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\gamma_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\frac{e^{-(T(\alpha)\,\nu_{-})^{2}/4}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\Omega\;{\widehat{C}}_{aa}\left(\frac{\Omega}{T(\alpha)}+\nu_{+}\right)\,e^{-\Omega^{2}}\;\vskip 5.69046pt\\
S_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}=\frac{e^{-(T(\alpha)\,\nu_{-})^{2}/4}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\Omega}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\Omega^{\prime}\;\frac{\widehat{C}_{aa}(\Omega^{\prime})}{(\Omega^{\prime}-\nu_{+})-\frac{\Omega}{T(\alpha)}}\;e^{-\Omega^{2}}\,\end{cases}\,,

(106)

where
\nu_{+}=\frac{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}{2}
and
\nu_{-}=\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}
.
In
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
it was proven that:

\displaystyle\|\rho_{S}(t)\!-\!e^{t\alpha^{2}\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{CG}}_{\alpha}}\rho_{S}(0)\|_{1}\!=\!\mathcal{O}\!\left(\alpha^{3}(\Gamma t)(\Gamma\tau)\right)\,.

(107)

Then, it is sufficient to prove that
\|\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{CG}}_{\alpha}-\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{D}}\|_{1-1}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\alpha\Gamma^{2}\tau\right)
to prove the claim (see the discussion in
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
). This result relies on two facts: first, the rates in Eq. (
106
) satisfy
Scandi and Álvaro M. Alhambra 2025
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|\gamma_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}-\gamma_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu_{1})|\leq\frac{\Gamma_{a}\tau_{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}T(\alpha)}&\qquad\qquad{\rm if}\;\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}\\
|\gamma_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}|\leq e^{-(T(\alpha)\,\nu_{-}^{\operatorname{min}})^{2}/4}\,\Gamma_{a}\left(1+\frac{\tau_{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}T(\alpha)}\right)&\qquad\qquad{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}\;;

(108)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|S_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}-S_{a}^{\operatorname{D}}(\nu_{1})|\leq\frac{\Gamma_{a}\tau_{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}T(\alpha)}&\qquad\qquad{\rm if}\;\nu_{1}=\nu_{2}\\
|S_{a}^{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}|\leq e^{-(T(\alpha)\,\nu_{-}^{\operatorname{min}})^{2}/4}\,\Gamma_{a}\left(1+\frac{\tau_{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}T(\alpha)}\right)&\qquad\qquad{\rm otherwise}\end{cases}\;,

(109)

where
\Gamma_{a}
and
\tau_{A}
satisfy
\Gamma=\sum_{a}\Gamma_{a}
and
(\Gamma\,\tau)=\sum_{a}(\Gamma_{a}\tau_{a})
. Second, the jump operators
A^{a}_{\nu}
become strictly local (see
(
Kastoryano and Brandao 2016

).
Locality is key in order to get an upper-bound on
\|A_{a}(\omega)\|
that is independent of
N
. Indeed, we have:

\displaystyle\|A^{a}_{\nu}\|=\left\|\sum_{E_{i}-E_{j}=\nu}\;\ket{E_{i}}\bra{E_{i}}A^{a}\ket{E_{j}}\bra{E_{j}}\right\|\leq\sum_{E_{i}-E_{j}=\nu}\;\left\|\ket{E_{i}}\bra{E_{i}}A_{a}\ket{E_{j}}\bra{E_{j}}\right\|\leq d^{2}_{k}

(110)

where

is the dimension of the Hilbert space over a region of size

We are now ready to prove the claim. Thanks to the locality of the jumps, these are at most

. Then, we have that:

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{L}}^{\operatorname{CG}}[\rho]-{\mathcal{L}}^{\operatorname{D}}[\rho]\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{a,\,\omega}\left(\frac{3\Gamma_{a}\tau_{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,T(\alpha)}d_{k}^{4}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,\,\omega,\,\widetilde{\omega}\\
\omega\neq\widetilde{\omega}\end{subarray}}\left(3\,e^{-(T(\alpha)\,\nu_{-}^{\operatorname{min}})^{2}/4}\,\Gamma_{a}\left(1+\frac{\tau_{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}\,T(\alpha)}\right)d_{k}^{4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3\,\Gamma d_{k}^{6}\left(\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{\pi}\,T(\alpha)}+e^{-(T(\alpha)\,\nu_{-}^{\operatorname{min}})^{2}/4}\left(1+\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{\pi}\,T(\alpha)}\right)d_{k}^{2}\right)\,.

It should be noted that

\displaystyle\|\rho_{S}(t)\!-\!e^{t\alpha^{2}\mathcal{L}^{\operatorname{D}}}\rho_{S}(0)\|_{1}\!=\!\mathcal{O}\!\left(\alpha^{3}(\Gamma t)(\Gamma\tau)\!\left(1\!+d_{k}^{6}\right)\right)\!.

(111)

This proves the claim.

Graph discretization of Laplacian on Riemannian manifolds with Bounds on Ricci curvature
Soma Maity, Anusha Bhattacharya

Abstract
. We study the approximation of eigenvalues for the Laplace-Beltrami operator on closed Riemannian manifolds in the class ℳ \mathcal{M} , characterized by bounded Ricci curvature, a lower bound on the injectivity radius, and an upper bound on the diameter. We use an ( ϵ , ρ ) (\epsilon,\rho) -approximation of the manifold by a weighted graph, as introduced by Burago et al. By adapting their methods, we prove that as the parameters ϵ , ρ \epsilon,\rho and the ratio ϵ ρ \frac{\epsilon}{\rho} approach zero, the k k -th eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian converges uniformly to the k k -th eigenvalue of the manifold’s Laplacian for each k k .

Introduction

The approximation of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on manifolds using graphs and simplices is a fundamental problem in spectral geometry and has extensive applications in theoretical and applied fields. Dodziuk, Patodi, and Petrunin introduced frameworks connecting Riemannian structures with triangulations and polyhedral approximations of manifolds, providing an early foundation for discrete Laplacian analysis in
[

[

[

[

established probabilistic convergence for random point samples.

In the deterministic setting, a major advance made by Burago, Ivanov, and Kurylev
[
8

established uniform spectral convergence of the graph Laplacian to the Laplace-Beltrami operator for closed Riemannian
n
-manifolds under uniform sectional curvature bounds, a lower injectivity radius bound, and an upper diameter bound. Aubry
[

obtained a similar result on approximating spectral data using isometrically immersed graphs in manifolds. Burago et al.

and Lu

also extended their framework to the

The methods of Burago et al.
[

rely on estimates on the Jacobian of the exponential map derived from sectional curvature bounds. However, many fundamental results in Riemannian geometry, such as compactness theorems, eigenvalue bounds, and volume comparison, hold under the weaker assumption of Ricci curvature bounds. In this work, we establish a generalization of the spectral convergence theorem of
[
8
]
to the broader class
\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
of closed
n

|Ric|\leq\lambda,\quad diam\leq D,\quad{\rm and}\quad inj\geq i_{0}

where

-topology via harmonic coordinates in

The central contribution of our approach is to avoid dependencies on sectional curvature using estimates on the Jacobian of the exponential map derived from Anderson’s precompactness theorem. Thus, adapting the techniques in

of approximating manifolds using weighted graphs dependent on
\epsilon,\rho>0

Definition 1

(
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation).

Given any
\rho>\epsilon>0
, a finite graph
\Gamma
with the vertex set
F=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and edges
{e_{ij}}
is called an
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation of
(M,g)
if the following conditions hold.

•

F\subset M
. There exists a partition of
M
into measurable subsets
\{V_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
such that
V_{i}\subset B_{\epsilon}(x_{i})
and
M=\bigcup B_{\epsilon}(x_{i})
where
B_{\epsilon}(x_{i})
is the ball in
M
centered at
x_{i}
with radius
\epsilon
.

•

Two vertices
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are connected by an edge
e_{ij}
if the Riemannian distance between them is less than
\rho.

•

Measure on
\Gamma
: Let
\mu_{i}=\text{vol}(V_{i}).

F
can then be equipped with a discrete measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}
where
\delta_{x_{i}}
denotes the Dirac measure at
x_{i}.

•

Weights on edges: Let
\nu_{n}
be the volume of the unit ball in the Euclidean
n
-space. To an edge
e_{ij}
, assign the weight

w_{ij}=\frac{2(n+2)}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\mu_{i}\mu_{j}.

(1.1)

For any
\textit{M}\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
, there exists a finite set of points
F
that is
\epsilon
-dense in
M
. We can then use a partition obtained by the Voronoi decomposition to define an
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation of
(M,g)
. The weighted graph is denoted by
\Gamma(F,\rho,\mu).
A weighted graph Laplacian
\Delta_{\Gamma}
on
L^{2}(\Gamma)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\Gamma}u)(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}w_{ij}(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j}))

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n+2)}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\sum_{x_{i}\sim x_{j}}\mu_{j}(u(x_{i})-u(x_{j})).

(1.2)

where
x_{i}\sim x_{j}
is
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are connected by an edge.

The motivation behind the choice of the normalization constant is given in Section 2.3 of
[
8
]
. We note that
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
is a non-negative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\Gamma)
with eigenvalues
0\leq\lambda_{1}(\Gamma)\leq\lambda_{2}(\Gamma)....\leq\lambda_{N}(\Gamma)
. The eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{M}
are denoted by
0\leq\lambda_{1}(M)\leq\lambda_{2}(M)\leq\lambda_{3}(M)\leq...
. The main goal of this paper is to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider a manifold
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
and an
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation
\Gamma
of
M
such that
\rho<i_{0}
. Let
\lambda_{k}(M)
,
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)
denote the
k
-th eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{M}
and
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
respectively. Then there exists a constant
C_{\mathcal{M}}>0
such that for any
\rho,\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

|\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)-\lambda_{k}(M)|\leq C_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)\lambda_{k}(M)+\rho C_{\mathcal{M}}\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}_{k}(M),\quad\forall\ k\leq|\Gamma|.

In
[
11
]
, Cheng proved that if the Ricci curvature of
M
is bounded below by
-\lambda
and the diameter is bounded above by
D
, then the
k
-th eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{M}
is bounded above by a constant depending only on
k
,
n
,
\lambda
and
D
. Hence, for any
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
, there exists a uniform positive constant
\lambda_{\mathcal{M},k}
such that
\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{\mathcal{M},k}.
Consequently, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Consider a manifold
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
and an
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation
\Gamma
of
M
such that
\rho<i_{0}
. Let
\lambda_{k}(M)
,
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)
denote the
k
-th eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{M}
and
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
respectively. Then for each
k
, there exists a constant
C_{\mathcal{M},k}>0
depending on
\mathcal{M}
and
k
such that for any
\rho,\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{\mathcal{M},k}}
,

|\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)-\lambda_{k}(M)|\leq C_{\mathcal{M},k}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right).

Consequently, for every fixed
k
,
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)
converges uniformly to
\lambda_{k}(M)
for all
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
as
\rho\to 0
and
\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}\to 0.

We now state the approximation of eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami operator by using the discretization scheme introduced above. We work with the discretization map
P
(Definition
3
) that connects functions on the manifold with functions on the discrete space and the
interpolation map

I
(Definition
7
) which reconstructs a function on the manifold from discrete data.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\lambda=\lambda_{j}(M)
be an eigenvalue of
\Delta_{M}
with multiplicity
m
, such that

\lambda_{k-1}<\lambda_{k}=\lambda=\lambda_{k+m-1}<\lambda_{k+m}.

Let
\delta_{\lambda}=\min\{1,\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1},\lambda_{k+m}-\lambda_{k+m-1}\}
.
Let
u_{k},\dots,u_{k+m-1}
be orthonormal eigenvectors corresponding to
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma),\dots,\lambda_{k+m-1}(\Gamma)
.

Then there exist orthonormal eigenfunctions
g_{k},\dots,g_{k+m-1}
of
-\Delta_{M}
corresponding to
\lambda
such that for all
j=k,\dots,k+m-1
and
\rho<\delta_{\lambda}C_{\mathcal{M},k}^{-1}
,

\left\lVert u_{j}-Pg_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C_{\mathcal{M},k}\delta_{\lambda}^{-2}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right)

(1.3)

and

\left\lVert g_{j}-Iu_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\leq C_{\mathcal{M},k}\delta_{\lambda}^{-2}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}+\rho\right).

(1.4)

Idea of the proof:

By Rauch’s comparison theorem, if the absolute value of the sectional curvature is bounded by
K
, the Jacobian
J_{x}(v)
of the exponential map at
x
in the direction of
v
satisfies

\frac{1}{1+CnK|v|^{2}}\leq J_{x}(v)\leq 1+CnK|v|^{2},\quad\forall\ |v|<i_{0}.

This inequality is a crucial component of the eigenvalue and eigenfunction approximation methods in
[
8
]
. However, this type of bound can not be easily obtained for bounded Ricci curvature. Although the authors in
[
8
]
noted the possibility of extending their methods to manifolds with Ricci curvature bounds, the generalization is not straightforward. We use Anderson’s precompactness theorem in
[
1
]
and derive appropriate estimates for the Jacobian in Lemma
2.2
that allow us to adopt the methods in
[
8
]
in the case of bounded Ricci curvature.

We prove Theorem
1.1
using the Min–Max principle. In Section 3, a discretization map from
L^{2}(M)
to
L^{2}(\Gamma)
is used to relate the discrete Dirichlet energy to the energy functional on
M
. Then by controlling the Rayleigh quotient on the image under the discretization map of the span of the first
k
eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{M}
, these estimates yield an upper bound for
(\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)-\lambda_{k}(M))
.

The lower bound for
(\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)-\lambda_{k}(M))
is obtained in Section 4. Using an interpolation map, we regularize elements of
L^{2}(\Gamma)
into
C^{0,1}(M)
. This map is an approximate inverse of the discretization map. We then establish relations between the derivative of the interpolation map and the discrete Dirichlet energy. Considering the Rayleigh quotient restricted to the image of the interpolation map of the span of the first
k
eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
, and using our interpolation estimates, we obtain the desired lower bound.

2.
Some inequalities and Average dispersion

Given a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
and a point
x\in M
, the exponential map at
x
is denoted by
\exp_{x}
. Let
u
be a unit vector in
T_{x}M.
For
0<t<i_{0}
, using the normal polar coordinates
(u,t)
in
T_{x}M
, we can write the volume element
\text{{vol}}_{g}
as

\exp_{x}^{*}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=J(u,t)t^{n-1}dtdu.

where
J(u,t)
is called the Jacobian determinant at
(u,t)
. In the sequel, we require suitable bounds
\lvert J(u,t)\rvert
under the assumed geometric conditions. We first establish an estimate for the Laplacian of the distance function under an upper Ricci curvature bound.

Lemma 2.1

(Laplacian comparison of the distance function)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian n-manifold satisfying
\operatorname{Ric}\leq\lambda
, and
\operatorname{inj}(M)\geq i_{0}>0
. Fix
p\in M
and let
r(x)=d(p,x)
be the distance function from
p
.
Then at
p\in B_{i_{0}/2}(x)\setminus\{x\}
,

\Delta r(p)\geq\frac{n-1}{r(p)}-\lambda\,r(p).

Proof.

Let
\gamma:[0,r(p)]\to M
be the unique unit-speed minimizing geodesic from
x
to
p
. One can write in polar coordinates

g=dr^{2}+g_{r}.

Let
H_{r}
denote the Hessian of the distance function
r
. Then the fundamental equation (see
[
22
]
Chapter 2, section 5.2) can be written as

\partial_{r}g=\partial_{r}g_{r}=2H_{r}

and

\partial_{r}H_{r}-H_{r}^{2}=-\operatorname{R}(.,\partial_{r},\partial_{r},.).

By a taking trace and using the curvature assumption
\operatorname{Ric}\leq\lambda
,
we have

\partial_{r}\Delta r\geq\operatorname{tr}(H_{r}^{2})-\lambda\geq\frac{(\Delta r)^{2}}{n-1}-\lambda.

(2.1)

The last inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Therefore,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left(\Delta_{r}-\frac{n-1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}\Delta r+\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}-2\frac{\Delta r}{r}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n-1}(\Delta r)^{2}-\lambda+\frac{n-1}{r^{2}}-2\frac{\Delta r}{r}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-1}\left(\Delta r-\frac{n-1}{r}\right)^{2}-\lambda

\displaystyle\geq-\lambda.

(2.3)

Let
f(r)=\Delta r-\frac{n-1}{r}.
Then,

\displaystyle f(r)-f(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{\epsilon}^{r}f^{\prime}(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq-\int_{\epsilon}^{r}\lambda ds

\displaystyle=-\lambda(r-\epsilon).

(2.4)

From
[
22
]
Chapter 5, section 7

\lim_{r\to 0}\left(H_{r}-\frac{1}{r}g_{r}\right)=0.

Taking the trace we have,

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\left(\Delta r(\gamma(\epsilon))-\frac{n-1}{\epsilon}\right)=0.

Hence,

f(r)\geq-\lambda r.

Which implies,

\Delta r(p)\geq\frac{n-1}{r(p)}-\lambda r(p).

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a constant
c_{\mathcal{M}}>0
such that for any
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
and
x\in M
,

|1-J(u,t)|\leq c_{\mathcal{M}}t,\quad\forall\ u\in S^{n-1},\quad\forall\ t\leq\frac{i_{0}}{2}.

Proof.

Consider a polar coordinate decomposition on
B_{r}(x)
, the geodesic ball of radius
r\leq\frac{i_{0}}{2}
centered at
x
in
(M,g)\in\mathcal{M}.
Then, from
[
22
]
, Chapter 9, section 1 we have

d\operatorname{vol}=\tilde{J}(u,t)du\wedge d\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}

and

\partial_{r}\tilde{J}=\tilde{J}\Delta r.

(2.5)

where
\tilde{J}(u,t)=J(u,t)t^{n-1}.
By differentiating (
2.5
), we obtain

\Delta r=\frac{n-1}{r}+\frac{J^{\prime}(u,r)}{J(u,r)}.

(2.6)

From Lemma
2.1
and the Laplace comparison theorem (see
[
22
]
, Chapter 9, section 1.2) for
\operatorname{Ric}\geq-\lambda
, we have

\frac{n-1}{t}-\lambda t\leq\Delta r(\gamma(t))\leq(n-1)\sqrt{\lambda}\coth(\sqrt{\lambda}t).

Using (
2.6
),

-\lambda tJ(u,t)\leq J^{\prime}(u,t)\leq(n-1)\sqrt{\lambda}\coth(\sqrt{\lambda}t)J(u,t)-\frac{n-1}{t}J(u,t).

From the Taylor series expansion of the
\coth
function, we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\left(\sqrt{\lambda}\coth(\sqrt{\lambda}t)-\frac{1}{t}\right)=0
and
J(u,t)\leq\left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt{\lambda}t}{\sqrt{\lambda}t}\right)^{n-1}.
Using
|J(u,t)|\leq C(n,\lambda,i_{0})
, we obtain a uniform upper bound
c_{\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})}
on the derivative of
J(u,t)
on
B_{x}(r)
for
r\leq\frac{i_{0}}{2}.

|J^{\prime}(u,t)|\leq c_{\mathcal{M}},\quad\forall p=\exp_{x}(tu)\in B_{\frac{i_{0}}{2}}(x),\quad\forall M\in\mathcal{M}.

(2.7)

From the proof of the Bishop-Gromov volume comparison theorem, we know that
J(u,0)=1
for any
u\in S^{n-1}.
Using mean value theorem and (
2.7
) we have

|J(u,t)-1|=|J(u,t)-J(u,0)|\leq c_{\mathcal{M}}t.

∎

Definition 2
.

Let
\textit{M}\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
and
B_{r}(x)
be a ball in
M
centered at
x
of radius
r
. Let
f:\textit{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. The average dispersion
E_{r}(f)
is defined as

E_{r}(f)=\int_{\textit{M}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}dydx.

We recall that
H^{1}(M)
is the space of all functions in
L^{2}(M)
such that their first-order partial derivatives are also in
L^{2}(M)
. The following lemma gives an upper bound of
E_{r}(f)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
, let
0<r<\frac{i_{0}}{2},f\in H^{1}(\textit{M})
. Then

E_{r}(f)\leq\frac{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}{n+2}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

Since smooth functions are dense in
H^{1}(M)
, it is enough to prove the above inequality for smooth functions.
For any
r<\frac{i_{0}}{2},
the exponential map at
x
restricted to
B_{r}(x)
is a diffeomorphism onto its image. Using polar co-ordinates
(u,t)
on
T_{x}M
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)}\lvert f(y)-f(x)\rvert^{2}dy=\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}\lvert f(exp_{x}(tu))-f(exp_{x}(0))\rvert^{2}J(u,t)t^{n-1}dtdu.

(2.8)

Substituting the upper bound of
J(u,t)
from Lemma
2.2
in equation (
2.8
),

\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}dy\leq(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}\lvert f(exp_{x}(tu))-f(exp_{x}(0))\rvert^{2}t^{n-1}dtdu.

Now,

\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}\lvert f(exp_{x}(tu))-f(exp_{x}(0))\rvert^{2}t^{n-1}dtdu=\int_{B_{r}(0)\subset T_{x}\textit{M}}|f(exp_{x}(v))-f(x)|^{2}dv.

By using the techniques for establishing an upper bound of the same integral in Lemma 3.3 (
[
8
]
), we have

\int_{B_{r}(0)\subset T_{x}\textit{M}}|f(exp_{x}(v))-f(x)|^{2}dv\leq\frac{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}{n+2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Combining this with the upper bound of the Jacobian determinant, the required inequality is obtained.
∎

In
[
5
]
, Berger showed that there exists a constant
c_{n}>0
depending only on
n
such that

\text{vol}(B_{r}(x))\geq c_{n}r^{n},\quad\forall r<i_{0}.

(2.9)

Its values have been computed in
[
13
]
. We establish a Poincaré-type inequality using this estimate.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
0<\epsilon<r<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
, let V be a measurable subset of M with
diam\text{ }V\leq\epsilon
such that
vol
(V)=\mu>0
. For all
x\in M
, let
{\text{vol}(B_{r}(x))}\geq c_{n}r^{n}.
Let
f\in L^{2}(M)
and
a=\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{V}f(x)dx
be the average value of
f
on
V.
Then

\int_{V}|f(x)-a|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}(r-\epsilon)^{n}}E_{r}(f,V).

Proof.

The above inequality is obtained in Lemma 3.4 in
[
8
]
where an upper bound on the sectional curvature is used to obtain a lower bound on
\text{{vol}}(B_{r}(x))
. If
\text{{vol}}(B_{r}(x))\geq c_{n}r^{n}
, then the proof follows using the same techniques.
∎

3.
An upper bound for
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)

We find an upper bound of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
in terms of the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{M}
for
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
using the inequalities proved in the previous section. Consider
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
and
\epsilon>0
such that
0<\epsilon<\rho<\frac{i_{0}}{2}
. Let
\Gamma
be an
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation of
(M,{\text{vol}})
using a decomposition
\{V_{i}\}_{1}^{N}
. We define the discretization map that assigns to each vertex of the graph the average value of the function on
V_{i}
, the measurable set that contains the vertex.

Definition 3

(Discretization map)
.

Let
\mu_{i}={\text{vol}}(V_{i})
. Then
P:L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
be a map defined as

Pf(x_{i})=\frac{1}{\mu_{i}}\int_{V_{i}}fd\mu.

The map

P^{*}:L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(M)
is defined as

P^{*}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}u(x_{i})\chi_{V_{i}}.

We see some immediate properties of the discretization map in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0}),f\in L^{2}(M)
and
u\in L^{2}(\Gamma).
Then

(1)

P:L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
is a bounded linear operator :

\left\lVert Pf\right\rVert\leq N\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{2}.

(2)

P^{*}
is norm-preserving, i.e.
\left\lVert P^{*}(u)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
.

(3)

P^{*}
is the adjoint of the discretization map
P
, i.e.
\langle f,P^{*}(u)\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=\langle P(f),u\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
0<\epsilon<\frac{\rho}{n}<\frac{i_{0}}{2n}
and
{\text{vol}}(B_{r}(x))\geq c_{n}r^{n}
for
\epsilon<r<\rho
then

\left\lVert f-P^{*}Pf\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\epsilon^{2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
V
be a measurable subset of
M
and
\chi_{V}
denote the characteristic function on
V.
According to definition,

\displaystyle\left\lVert f-P^{*}Pf\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|f(x)-P^{*}Pf(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{M}|f(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}Pf(x_{i})\chi_{V_{i}}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{V_{i}}|f(x)-Pf(x_{i})|^{2}dx.

From Lemma
2.4
, for all
r
such that
0<\epsilon<r<\rho
and for all
1\leq i\leq N
,

\int_{V_{i}}|f(x)-Pf(x_{i})|^{2}dx\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}(r-\epsilon)^{n}}E_{r}(f,V_{i}).

From Lemma
2.3
,

\left\lVert f-P^{*}Pf\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{c_{n}(r-\epsilon)^{n}}E_{r}(f)\leq\frac{\nu_{n}}{c_{n}(n+2)}\left(\frac{r}{r-\epsilon}\right)^{n}r^{2}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Putting
r=n\epsilon
, we have
\left(\frac{r}{r-\epsilon}\right)^{n}=\left(\frac{n}{n-1}\right)^{n}<4.

Hence,

\left\lVert f-P^{*}Pf\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\epsilon^{2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(3.1)

∎

As
P^{*}
is norm-preserving, the next corollary follows immediately using the triangle inequality.

Corollary 3.3
.

For any
f\in H^{1}(M),

|\left\lVert Pf\right\rVert-\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}|^{2}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\epsilon^{2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

|\left\lVert Pf\right\rVert-\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}|^{2}\leq|\left\lVert P^{*}Pf\right\rVert-\left\lVert f\right\rVert|^{2}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\epsilon^{2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

∎

Next, we define the discrete Dirichlet energy and study its relation to the Dirichlet energy functional defined on
H^{1}(M).

Definition 4

(Discrete Dirichlet energy)
.

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma(F,\rho,\mu)
be an
(\epsilon,\rho)
- approximation of
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0}).
For
u\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
, the Dirichlet energy on
\Gamma
is given by

\left\lVert\delta(u)\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\sum_{i}\sum_{j:x_{i}\sim x_{j}}\mu_{i}\mu_{j}|u(x_{j})-u(x_{i})|^{2}.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation of
(M,{\text{vol}})
. For
f\in H^{1}(M)
,

\left\lVert\delta(Pf)\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho+2\epsilon))\left(1+\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

From Lemma 4.3(2) in
[
8
]
, we derive

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta(Pf)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\sum_{i}\sum_{j:x_{j}\sim x_{i}}\int_{V_{i}}\int_{V_{j}}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\int_{M}\int_{U(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}dydx.

(3.3)

where
U(x)=\bigcup_{j:x_{j}\sim x_{i}}V_{j}
in (
3.3
). We also observe that
U(x)\subseteq B_{\rho+2\epsilon}(x).

Hence,

\left\lVert\delta(Pf)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}E_{\rho+2\epsilon}(f).

(3.4)

Using Lemma
2.3
and from (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta(Pf)\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho+2\epsilon))\frac{(\rho+2\epsilon)^{n+2}}{\rho^{n+2}}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho+2\epsilon))\left(1+\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

∎

Now we can calculate an upper bound of the eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian operator using the lemmas proved in this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
0<\epsilon<\rho\leq\frac{i_{0}}{2}\text{, }M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
and
\Gamma
be a
(\epsilon,\rho)
-approximation of
(M,{\text{vol}})
. Let
\lambda_{k}(M)
and
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)
denote the k-th eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{M}
and
-\Delta_{\Gamma}
respectively. There exist positive constants
C_{n}
and
C_{\mathcal{M}}
depending only on
n
and
\mathcal{M}
respectively such that for any
\rho
,
\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{n}}
,

\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)\leq\left(1+C_{n}\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}+C_{\mathcal{M}}\rho\right)\lambda_{k}(M)+\rho C_{\mathcal{M}}\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}_{k}(M).

Proof.

We use the min-max principle to prove the theorem. Hence it is sufficient to find a linear subspace
L\subset L^{2}(\Gamma)
with dim
L=k
such that the Rayleigh quotient restricted to
L
given by
sup_{u\in L\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}}
is less than or equal to the right-hand side of the above inequality.

Let
W\subset H^{1}(M)
be the linear span of orthonormal eigenfunctions of
-\Delta_{M}
corresponding to eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(M)\leq\lambda_{2}(M)\leq...\leq\lambda_{k}(M).
For any
f\in W,

\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}=\langle-\Delta_{M}f,f\rangle\leq\lambda_{k}(M)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

From Corollary
3.3
and considering
\rho<\frac{1}{c_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\left\lVert Pf\right\rVert\geq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-2\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+\rho c_{\mathcal{M}})}\left\lVert df\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\geq\left(1-3\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}\lambda_{k}(M)}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

(3.5)

Let
\lambda_{\mathcal{M},k}
be an upperbound of
\lambda_{k}(M)
for any
M\in\mathcal{M}.
Then for
\epsilon<\frac{\sqrt{c_{n}}}{3\sqrt{n\nu_{n}\lambda_{\mathcal{M},k}}}
,
P|_{W}
is injective. Hence, the dimension of the subspace
L=P(W)
is
k.
We pick
u\in L\setminus\{0\}
and let
f\in W
be such that
u=Pf.
Then from equation (
3.5
),

\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\geq\left(1-3\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}\lambda_{k}(M)}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(3.6)

By Lemma
3.4
for
\epsilon<\frac{\rho}{2}
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta(u)\right\rVert^{2}\leq(1+2\rho c_{\mathcal{M}})\left(1+\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}\lambda_{k}(M)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(3.7)

From inequalities (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)\leq\frac{\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}}{\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1+2\rho c_{\mathcal{M}})\left(1+\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}\lambda_{k}(M)}{\left(1-3\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}\lambda_{k}(M)}\right)}.

Using the Taylor series expansion of terms
\left(1+\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{n+2}
and
\left(1-3\epsilon\sqrt{\frac{n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}\lambda_{k}(M)}\right)^{-1}
we obtain a constant
C_{\mathcal{M}}
depending only on
\mathcal{M}
and a constant
C_{n}
depending on
n
such that for any
\epsilon,\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{C_{n}}
,

\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)\leq\left(1+C_{n}\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}+C_{\mathcal{M}}\rho\right)\lambda_{k}(M)+\rho C_{\mathcal{M}}\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}_{k}(M).

∎

Remark 3.6
.

We observe that it is enough to assume a lower bound on Ricci curvature to obtain an upper bound on
J(u,t)
, which will give us a lower bound on
\lambda_{k}(M)
using
\lambda_{k}(\Gamma)
. One can apply Bishop- Gromov volume comparison theorem and conclude that for Ricci curvature bounded below by
\lambda
and
t<i_{0}
,
J(u,t)\leq J_{\lambda}(t)
where

J_{\lambda}(t)=\left(1+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{|\lambda|^{j}t^{2j}}{(2j+1)!}\right)^{n-1}.

(3.8)

We can then follow the calculations of Theorem
3
and obtain a lower bound of
\lambda_{k}(M).

4.

In this section, we study the smoothing operator defined in

Definition 5

Fixing a positive

\end{cases}

(2)

Kernel.
Let

k_{r}(x,y)=r^{-n}\psi(r^{-1}d(x,y)).

where

denotes the Riemannian distance between

and

in

(3)

Associated integral operator.
Let
\Lambda_{r}^{0}:L^{2}(M)\to C^{0,1}(M)

\Lambda_{r}^{0}f(x)=\int_{M}f(y)k_{r}(x,y)dy.

(4)

Let

By using the polar coordinate in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Lemma 4.1

Let

and

\psi,k_{r}
be defined as above. Then,

(1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(|p|)dt=1
, and

(2)

|k_{r}(x,y)|\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n}}.

(3)

{\rm grad}k_{r}(.,y)(x)=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\exp^{-1}(y)

We derive the following approximations for
\theta
using the bounds of the Jacobian determinant from Lemma
2.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
0<r<\frac{i_{0}}{2}\text{ and }M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})

be a ball of radius r centered at x.
Then

has the following bounds

1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r\leq\theta(x)\leq 1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r.

The next lemma gives a bound on the

norm of the gradient of

.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<r<\rho<\frac{i_{0}}{2}\text{ and }M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
. Let
\nabla\theta
denote the gradient of
\theta
. Then
|\nabla\theta(x)|\leq c_{\mathcal{M}}.

Proof.

Let
A:S^{n-1}\to S^{n-1}
be the antipodal map defined as
A(u)=-u.
We note that the Jacobian matrix corresponding to

is
-I
, where

is the identity matrix. By changing co-ordinates using

, we can show that

\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}udu=0.

(4.1)

One can compute the gradient of
\theta
in polar coordinates as follows:

\nabla\theta(x)=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\int_{t=0}^{r}\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}tuJ(u,t)t^{n-1}dudt.

From equation (
4.1
),

\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{n}ududt=\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}udu\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{n}dt=0.

Hence,

\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}tJ(u,t)t^{n-1}ududt=\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{n}(J(u,t)-1)ududt

From Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle|J(u,t)-1|

\displaystyle\leq tc_{\mathcal{M}}.

(4.2)

Let
w\in T_{x}M
,
|w|=1
be such that
\max\{\langle\triangledown\theta(x),v\rangle|v\in T_{x}M
,
|v|=1\}
is attained at
w
. One can then compute the gradient of
\theta
in polar coordinates as follows:

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla\theta(x)\rvert=\langle\nabla\theta,w\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}t^{n}\langle u,w\rangle\left(J(u,t)-1\right)dtdu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}|\langle u,w\rangle||1-J(u,t)|t^{n}dtdu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n+2}}\int_{u\in S^{n-1}}\int_{t=0}^{r}c_{\mathcal{M}}t^{n+1}dtdu

\displaystyle\leq c_{\mathcal{M}}.

∎

Using the associated integral operator and
\theta
, we define the following function.

Definition 6
.

Let
\Lambda_{r}:L^{2}(M)\to C^{0,1}(M)
be defined as
\Lambda_{r}f=\theta^{-1}\Lambda_{r}^{0}f
.

We observe that
\Lambda_{r}f=f
, for all constant functions
f.
In fact,
\Lambda_{r}f
is bounded when

is less than the injectivity radius as we see in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
0<r<\frac{i_{0}}{2}\text{ and }M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
.
For all
f\in L^{2}(M)
and
r<\frac{1}{c_{\mathcal{M}}}
, we have

\left\lVert\Lambda_{r}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r}\right)\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
x\in M
. Then

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{r}^{0}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\int_{M}f(y)k_{r}(x,y)dy|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{M}(\sqrt{k_{r}(x,y)})^{2}dy\right)\left(\int_{M}(f(y)\sqrt{k_{r}(x,y)})^{2}dy\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle=\theta(x)\int_{M}|f(y)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)dy.

where (
4.3

So,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{r}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\theta(x)^{-2}|\Lambda_{r}^{0}f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\theta(x)^{-1}\int_{M}|f(y)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)dy

\displaystyle\leq(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{-1}\int_{M}|f(y)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)dy.

Integrating with respect to the volume measure on
M
,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Lambda_{r}f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{M}|\Lambda_{r}f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{-1}\int_{M}|f(y)|^{2}\int_{M}k_{r}(x,y)dydx

\displaystyle\leq(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)^{-1}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}r)\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

(4.4)

where (
4.4
) follows from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

When
r
is less than half of the injectivity radius, the following lemma gives an upper bound using average dispersion on how far

takes a function in
L^{2}(M).

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
0<r<\frac{i_{0}}{2}\text{ and }M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
. For all

and

\left\lVert\Lambda_{r}f-f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)}E_{r}(f).

Proof.

We fix a coset representative
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
of an element of
L^{2}(M).

Let
a=f(x).
Now,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{r}f(x)-f(x)

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{r}f(x)-a

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{r}(f-a.\mathbf{1}_{M})(x)

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}(x)\int_{B_{r}(x)}(f(y)-a)k_{r}(x,y)dy

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}(x)\int_{B_{r}(x)}(f(y)-f(x))k_{r}(x,y)dy.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{r}f(x)-f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{-2}(x)\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}k_{r}(x,y)dy\right)\left(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)dy\right)

\displaystyle=\theta^{-1}(x)\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}k_{r}(x,y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}r^{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}r)}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)-f(x)|^{2}dy

(4.5)

where (
4.5
) follows from Lemma
4.1
. Finally, by integrating on both sides, we get the result.
∎

In the following lemma, we obtain an upper bound for the norm of
d\Lambda_{r}(f)
using the average dispersion

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
0<r<\rho<\frac{i_{0}}{2}\text{ and }M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})

Using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|A_{2}(x)|^{2}

M

On the other hand, fixing
x\in M,
let

\displaystyle=\langle A_{1}(x),w\rangle

Definition 7

Lemma 5.1

Let
0<\epsilon<\rho<i_{0}

(1)

|\left\lVert Iu\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\left\lVert u\right\rVert|^{2}\leq\frac{3\rho^{2}}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}.

(2)

\left\lVert dIu\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left(1-\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}-1}\frac{1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert.

Proof.

(1) Let

Using norm preserving property of

\lvert\left\lVert Iu\right\rVert_{L^{2}}-\left\lVert u\right\rVert\rvert\leq\left\lVert Iu-P^{*}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

\left\lVert Iu-P^{*}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\left\lVert\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}P^{*}u-P^{*}u\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\epsilon))(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n}}E_{\rho-2\epsilon}(P^{*}u).

(5.1)

On the other hand,

\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\int_{M}\int_{U(x)}\lvert P^{*}u(y)-P^{*}u(x)\rvert^{2}dydx,

where

such that

. Since

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}\int_{M}\int_{B_{\rho-2\epsilon}(x)}\lvert P^{*}u(y)-P^{*}u(x)\rvert^{2}dydx

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}\rho^{n+2}}E_{\rho-2\epsilon}(P^{*}u).

(5.2)

Thus, from (

\left\lVert Iu-P^{*}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\rho^{n+2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}(\rho-2\epsilon))(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n}}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{3\rho^{2}}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}

(5.3)

as

.

(2) Proceeding with direct computation,

\displaystyle\left\lVert dIu\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert d(\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}P^{*}u)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n+2}}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{3}}E_{\rho-2\epsilon}(P^{*}u)

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho-2\epsilon}\right)^{n+2}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{3}}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}

(5.5)

where (

and (
5.5

6.
Approximation of eigenfunctions

In this section, we investigate the approximation of eigenfunctions of
M\in\mathcal{M}(n,\lambda,D,i_{0})
using similar arguments as in
[
8
]
. The following lemma shows that the discretization map and the interpolation map are almost inverses of each other.

Lemma 6.1
.

(1)

Let
f\in H^{1}(M)
. Then for
\rho<\frac{1}{2c_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\left\lVert IPf-f\right\rVert\leq C_{n}\rho\left\lVert df\right\rVert.

(2)

Let
u\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
,
\rho\leq\frac{1}{3c_{\mathcal{M}}}
and
\frac{\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{2n}
. Then,

\left\lVert PIu-u\right\rVert\leq C_{n}\rho\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert

where
C_{n}
is a positive constant depending only on the dimension
n.

Proof.

(1)

By straightforward computation,

\displaystyle\left\lVert IPf-f\right\rVert

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}P^{*}Pf-f\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}(P^{*}Pf-f)\right\rVert+\left\lVert\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}f-f\right\rVert.

(6.1)

We aim to find upper bounds of both the terms obtained above using the triangle inequality.
From Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
3.2
,

\left\lVert\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}(P^{*}Pf-f)\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}\frac{(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{2}}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\epsilon^{2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(6.2)

Again from Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
2.3
,

\left\lVert\Lambda_{\rho-2\epsilon}f-f\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{n+2}{\nu_{n}(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)(\rho-2\epsilon)^{n}}E_{\rho-2\epsilon}(f)\leq\frac{1+c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\rho^{2}\left\lVert df\right\rVert^{2}.

(6.3)

From equations (
6.1
), (
6.2
) and (
6.3
), we have,

\left\lVert IPf-f\right\rVert\leq\frac{(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)}{\sqrt{1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}}\right)\rho\left\lVert df\right\rVert.

As
c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho<\frac{1}{2}
, the first part of the lemma follows.

(2)

Since
P^{*}
preserves norm, using the triangle inequality

\displaystyle\left\lVert PIu-u\right\rVert=\left\lVert P^{*}(PIu-u)\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert P^{*}PIu-Iu\right\rVert+\left\lVert Iu-P^{*}u\right\rVert.

(6.4)

Using Lemma
3.2
, for
\rho<\frac{1}{c_{\mathcal{M}}}
,

\left\lVert P^{*}PIu-Iu\right\rVert^{2}\leq\frac{4n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}(1+c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)\epsilon^{2}\left\lVert dIu\right\rVert^{2}.

By Lemma
5.1
we have,

\left\lVert dIu\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq(1-\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho})^{-n-2}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{2}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{3}}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert^{2}

Therefore, for
\rho<\frac{1}{c_{\mathcal{M}}}
and
\frac{2\epsilon}{\rho}<\frac{1}{n}
,

\left\lVert P^{*}PIu-Iu\right\rVert\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{3n\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}}\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(1-c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\epsilon\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert.

(6.5)

From equation (5.3) we have,

\left\lVert Iu-P^{*}u\right\rVert\leq\frac{3\rho}{1-2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho}\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert.

(6.6)

Hence from (
6.6
) and (
6.5
),

\left\lVert PIu-u\right\rVert\leq 3\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{\nu_{n}}{c_{n}}}\right)\frac{(1+2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(1-2c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\rho\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert.

The second part of the lemma follows from the fact that
c_{\mathcal{M}}\rho<\frac{1}{3}.

∎

Thus, from (

\displaystyle\left\lVert\delta(Pf)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\geq(1-\sigma)\left\lVert\delta u\right\rVert

=

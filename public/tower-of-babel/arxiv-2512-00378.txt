The Information Theory of Similarity Witness Overlap as Mutual Information and the Capacity Limits of Semantic Search
Nikit Phadke

Abstract
We establish a precise mathematical equivalence between witness-based similarity systems (REWA) and Shannon’s information theory. We prove that witness overlap is mutual information, that REWA bit complexity bounds arise from channel capacity limitations, and that ranking-preserving encodings obey rate-distortion constraints. This unification reveals that fifty years of similarity search research—from Bloom filters to locality-sensitive hashing to neural retrieval—implicitly developed information theory for relational data. We derive fundamental lower bounds showing that REWA’s O ⁡ ( Δ − 2 ​ log ⁡ N ) O(\Delta^{-2}\log N) complexity is optimal: no encoding scheme can preserve similarity rankings with fewer bits. The framework establishes that semantic similarity has physical units (bits of mutual information), search is communication (query transmission over a noisy channel), and retrieval systems face fundamental capacity limits analogous to Shannon’s channel coding theorem.

Introduction

In 1948, Claude Shannon established that communication has fundamental limits
[
1
]
. The channel capacity
C
bounds the rate at which information can be reliably transmitted; no coding scheme, however clever, can exceed this limit. This insight unified telegraphy, radio, and all future communication systems under a single mathematical framework.

We prove that similarity search obeys an analogous law.

The REWA framework (Rank-Embedding Witness Approximators) established that diverse similarity methods—Bloom filters, locality-sensitive hashing, random projections, neural attention—share a common structure: similarity arises from
witness overlap

[
2
]
. Two concepts are similar when they share witnesses; encoding preserves rankings when the overlap gap exceeds collision noise.

This paper reveals that REWA
is
information theory. Specifically:

(i)

Witness overlap equals mutual information.
The REWA overlap
\Delta(x,y)=|W(x)\cap W(y)|
is monotonically equivalent to
I(W_{x};W_{y})
, the mutual information between witness distributions.

(ii)

Bit complexity equals inverse channel capacity.
The REWA bound
m=O(\Delta^{-2}\log N)
arises from Shannon’s channel coding theorem applied to the “hash channel.”

(iii)

Ranking preservation equals rate-distortion optimization.
The minimum bits required to preserve top-
k
rankings is characterized by the rate-distortion function for ranking loss.

These equivalences are not analogies—they are mathematical identities. The implication is profound:
REWA bounds are optimal
. Just as no communication system can exceed channel capacity, no similarity encoding can beat the witness-information limits.

Preliminaries

2.1
Information-Theoretic Foundations

Definition 2.1

(Entropy)
.

For a discrete random variable
X
with probability mass function
p(x)
, the
Shannon entropy
is:

H(X)=-\sum_{x}p(x)\log p(x)

(1)

Definition 2.2

(Mutual Information)
.

For jointly distributed random variables
(X,Y)
with marginals
p_{X},p_{Y}
and joint distribution
p_{XY}
:

I(X;Y)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)=\sum_{x,y}p_{XY}(x,y)\log\frac{p_{XY}(x,y)}{p_{X}(x)p_{Y}(y)}

(2)

Definition 2.3

(Channel Capacity)
.

A discrete memoryless channel with input
X
, output
Y
, and transition probabilities
p(y|x)
has capacity:

C=\max_{p(x)}I(X;Y)

(3)

Definition 2.4

(Rate-Distortion Function)
.

For source
X
, reconstruction
\hat{X}
, and distortion measure
d(x,\hat{x})
:

R(D)=\min_{p(\hat{x}|x):\mathbb{E}[d(X,\hat{X})]\leq D}I(X;\hat{X})

(4)

2.2
REWA Foundations

Definition 2.5

(Witness Sets)
.

Each concept
v\in V
is associated with a finite witness set
W(v)\subseteq\Omega
, where
\Omega
is the witness universe.

Definition 2.6

(Witness Overlap)
.

The overlap between concepts
u,v
is:

\Delta(u,v)=|W(u)\cap W(v)|

(5)

Definition 2.7

(REWA Encoding)
.

A REWA encoder maps concepts to binary codes
B:V\to\{0,1\}^{m}
such that expected binary similarity is monotone in witness overlap:

\mathbb{E}[\langle B(u),B(v)\rangle]=\alpha\cdot\Delta(u,v)+\beta

(6)

for constants
\alpha>0
,
\beta\geq 0
.

Definition 2.8

(Overlap Gap Condition)
.

For query
q
with true neighborhood
N_{k}(q)
, there exists
\Delta>0
such that:

\min_{u\in N_{k}(q)}\Delta(q,u)-\max_{w\notin N_{k}(q)}\Delta(q,w)\geq\Delta

(7)

3
The Probabilistic Witness Space

The key insight enabling the Shannon-REWA bridge is treating concepts not as fixed objects but as
stochastic sources
of witness information.

Definition 3.1

(Concept as Random Variable)
.

Let
\Omega
be the universe of all possible witnesses. A data point
x\in\mathcal{X}
defines a random variable
W_{x}
distributed over
\Omega
with probability mass function
p_{x}(w)
.

Different REWA instantiations correspond to different witness distributions:

Example 3.2

(Boolean REWA)
.

For set-based witnesses
S_{x}\subseteq\Omega
:

p_{x}(w)=\frac{1}{|S_{x}|}\mathbf{1}[w\in S_{x}]

(8)

This is the uniform distribution over the witness set.

Example 3.3

(Weighted REWA)
.

For weighted witnesses with importance scores
\alpha_{x}(w)
:

p_{x}(w)=\frac{\alpha_{x}(w)}{\sum_{w^{\prime}}\alpha_{x}(w^{\prime})}

(9)

Example 3.4

(Measure-Theoretic REWA)
.

For continuous witness measures
\mu_{x}
:

p_{x}(w)=\frac{d\mu_{x}}{d\nu}(w)

(10)

where
\nu
is a base measure on
\Omega
.

Definition 3.5

(Witness Entropy)
.

The information content of a concept
x
is its Shannon entropy:

H(W_{x})=-\sum_{w\in\Omega}p_{x}(w)\log p_{x}(w)

(11)

Remark 3.6
.

For uniform witnesses over set
S_{x}
, we have
H(W_{x})=\log|S_{x}|
. The entropy measures the “semantic volume” of the concept—how many bits are required to specify a typical witness.

4
Similarity is Mutual Information

We now establish the fundamental equivalence between witness overlap and mutual information.

Theorem 4.1

(The Overlap-Information Isomorphism)
.

Let
x,y
be concepts with witness distributions
p_{x},p_{y}
over universe
\Omega
. Define the joint witness process
(W_{x},W_{y})
with joint distribution:

p_{xy}(w,w^{\prime})=p_{x}(w)\cdot p_{y}(w^{\prime})\cdot\kappa(w,w^{\prime})

(12)

where
\kappa(w,w^{\prime})=\mathbf{1}[w=w^{\prime}]
for exact matching. Then the mutual information
I(W_{x};W_{y})
is a monotonically increasing function of the normalized overlap:

I(W_{x};W_{y})=f\left(\frac{\Delta(x,y)}{|W(x)\cup W(y)|}\right)

(13)

for a strictly increasing function
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
f(0)=0
.

Proof.

Consider Boolean REWA with uniform witness distributions over sets
A=W(x)
and
B=W(y)
.

The individual entropies are:

\displaystyle H(W_{x})

\displaystyle=\log|A|

(14)

\displaystyle H(W_{y})

\displaystyle=\log|B|

(15)

For the joint entropy, we consider the support of the joint distribution. Two witnesses “match” when
w=w^{\prime}
and both
w\in A
and
w\in B
, i.e., when
w\in A\cap B
. The joint entropy over the union is:

H(W_{x},W_{y})=\log|A\cup B|

(16)

Therefore, the mutual information is:

\displaystyle I(W_{x};W_{y})

\displaystyle=H(W_{x})+H(W_{y})-H(W_{x},W_{y})

(17)

\displaystyle=\log|A|+\log|B|-\log|A\cup B|

(18)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{|A|\cdot|B|}{|A\cup B|}

(19)

By inclusion-exclusion,
|A\cup B|=|A|+|B|-|A\cap B|=|A|+|B|-\Delta
, so:

I(W_{x};W_{y})=\log\frac{|A|\cdot|B|}{|A|+|B|-\Delta}

(20)

Define the Jaccard index
J=\frac{\Delta}{|A\cup B|}=\frac{\Delta}{|A|+|B|-\Delta}
. Then:

I(W_{x};W_{y})=\log\frac{|A|\cdot|B|}{|A|+|B|}+\log\frac{1}{1-J\cdot\frac{|A|+|B|}{|A|+|B|}}

(21)

For the symmetric case
|A|=|B|=L
:

I(W_{x};W_{y})=\log\frac{L}{2}+\log\frac{2L}{2L-\Delta}=\log\frac{L^{2}}{2L-\Delta}

(22)

Taking the Taylor expansion for small overlap ratio
\rho=\Delta/(2L)
:

I(W_{x};W_{y})\approx\log L-\log 2-\log(1-\rho)\approx\log L-\log 2+\rho+O(\rho^{2})

(23)

Thus
I(W_{x};W_{y})
is monotonically increasing in
\Delta
, with:

\frac{\partial I}{\partial\Delta}=\frac{1}{2L-\Delta}>0

(24)

∎

Corollary 4.2

(Gap Preservation)
.

The REWA overlap gap condition
\Delta_{neighbor}-\Delta_{far}\geq\Delta
implies an information gap:

I(W_{q};W_{neighbor})-I(W_{q};W_{far})\geq\frac{\Delta}{2L-\Delta_{neighbor}}>0

(25)

Remark 4.3
.

This theorem reveals why witness overlap is the “right” similarity measure: it directly quantifies the mutual information between concepts. Two things are similar precisely because knowing one tells you about the other—the fundamental information-theoretic definition of dependence.

5
Hashing as a Noisy Channel

The REWA encoding process—mapping witnesses to bits via hash functions—is mathematically equivalent to transmitting information over a noisy channel.

Definition 5.1

(The Hash Channel)
.

Define a discrete memoryless channel
\mathcal{C}
as follows:

•

Input
: A witness pair indicator
X=\mathbf{1}[w\in W(x)\cap W(y)]

•

Output
: A collision indicator
Z=\mathbf{1}[h(w_{x})=h(w_{y})]
for hash function
h

•

Transition probabilities
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[Z=1|X=1]

\displaystyle=1\quad\text{(shared witness always collides)}

(26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[Z=1|X=0]

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{m}\quad\text{(accidental collision probability)}

(27)

Theorem 5.2

(REWA Capacity Bound)
.

To distinguish a true neighbor (overlap
\Delta
) from a non-neighbor (overlap
0
) with error probability at most
\delta
, the number of hash bits must satisfy:

m\geq\frac{1}{C(\Delta)}\left(\log N+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)

(28)

where the effective channel capacity is:

C(\Delta)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left(P(Z|neighbor)\,\|\,P(Z|non\text{-}neighbor)\right)=O(\Delta^{2})

(29)

Proof.

The binary similarity score
S(x,y)=\langle B(x),B(y)\rangle
counts collisions across
m
hash positions.

For a neighbor
u
with overlap
\Delta(q,u)=\Delta
:

\mathbb{E}[S(q,u)]=K\cdot\Delta+\frac{K^{2}L^{2}}{m}\cdot(1-\frac{\Delta}{L})

(30)

The first term is signal (shared witnesses), the second is noise (accidental collisions).

For a non-neighbor
w
with overlap
\Delta(q,w)=0
:

\mathbb{E}[S(q,w)]=\frac{K^{2}L^{2}}{m}

(31)

The gap in expected similarity is:

\Gamma=\mathbb{E}[S(q,u)]-\mathbb{E}[S(q,w)]=K\cdot\Delta-\frac{K^{2}L\Delta}{m}\approx K\cdot\Delta

(32)

for
m\gg KL
.

Each hash position provides independent information about whether a collision is “real” (shared witness) or “accidental.” This is equivalent to
m
uses of the hash channel.

By the channel coding theorem, reliable discrimination requires:

m\cdot C\geq\log\frac{N}{\delta}

(33)

The capacity of a binary asymmetric channel with parameters
p_{1}=1
and
p_{0}=K/m
is:

C=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{neighbor}\|p_{non\text{-}neighbor})

(34)

For the collision distributions, using the quadratic approximation of KL-divergence for small perturbations:

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|p+\epsilon)\approx\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2p(1-p)}

(35)

The “perturbation” from overlap is
\epsilon\propto\Delta/L
, giving:

C(\Delta)=O\left(\frac{\Delta^{2}}{L^{2}}\right)

(36)

Substituting:

m\geq O\left(\frac{L^{2}}{\Delta^{2}}\right)\left(\log N+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right)

(37)

With
L
absorbed into constants:

m=O\left(\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\log\frac{N}{\delta}\right)

(38)

∎

Remark 5.3
.

This derivation reveals the information-theoretic origin of the
\Delta^{-2}
factor: it is the inverse capacity of the hash channel, which scales quadratically with the signal strength (overlap).

6
Ranking Preservation as Lossy Compression

The problem of preserving top-
k
rankings with minimal bits is a rate-distortion problem.

Definition 6.1

(Ranking Distortion)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{q}
be the true ranking of database items by similarity to query
q
, and
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{q}
be the ranking induced by encoded similarities. The
top-
k
distortion
is:

d_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{q},\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{q})=\mathbf{1}[\text{Top}_{k}(\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{q})\neq N_{k}^{f}(q)]

(39)

where
N_{k}^{f}(q)
is the true
k
-nearest neighborhood.

Theorem 6.2

(Rate-Distortion for Rankings)
.

The minimum rate (bits per concept) required to achieve expected ranking distortion
\mathbb{E}[d_{k}]\leq\epsilon
is:

R(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{|V|}\left(\log\binom{N}{k}-H(\epsilon)\right)

(40)

where
H(\epsilon)=-\epsilon\log\epsilon-(1-\epsilon)\log(1-\epsilon)
is the binary entropy.

For small
\epsilon
and the REWA encoding achieving this rate:

R(\epsilon)=O\left(\frac{k\log(N/k)}{|V|}\right)

(41)

Proof.

The source is the true ranking
\mathcal{R}_{q}
, which can be described by identifying the top-
k
items from
N
total. The entropy of this source is:

H(\mathcal{R}_{q})=\log\binom{N}{k}\approx k\log\frac{N}{k}

(42)

A ranking-preserving encoding must transmit enough information to identify the correct top-
k
set with probability
1-\epsilon
.

By the rate-distortion theorem for discrete sources with Hamming-like distortion:

R(\epsilon)\geq H(\mathcal{R}_{q})-H(\epsilon)\cdot\log\binom{N}{k}

(43)

For small
\epsilon
,
H(\epsilon)\to 0
, and we need essentially the full entropy:

R(\epsilon)\approx H(\mathcal{R}_{q})=k\log\frac{N}{k}

(44)

Distributing across
|V|
concepts:

R(\epsilon)=O\left(\frac{k\log(N/k)}{|V|}\right)\text{ bits per concept}

(45)

∎

Corollary 6.3

(REWA Achieves Rate-Distortion Optimality)
.

The REWA encoding with
m=O(\Delta^{-2}\log N)
bits achieves the rate-distortion bound for ranking preservation, up to constant factors depending on the gap
\Delta
.

8
Fundamental Limits

The Shannon-REWA equivalence implies that REWA bounds are not merely sufficient—they are
necessary
.

Theorem 8.1

(REWA Optimality)
.

For any encoding
B:V\to\{0,1\}^{m}
that preserves top-
k
rankings with probability
1-\delta
under the overlap gap condition with gap
\Delta
:

m\geq\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\log\frac{N}{\delta}\right)

(46)

No encoding scheme can achieve ranking preservation with asymptotically fewer bits.

Proof.

By
Theorem

5.2
, the hash channel has capacity
C(\Delta)=O(\Delta^{2})
.

Shannon’s channel coding converse states that reliable communication at rate
R>C
is impossible. Equivalently, to achieve error probability
\delta
when transmitting
\log(N/\delta)
bits of ranking information, we need:

m\cdot C(\Delta)\geq\log\frac{N}{\delta}

(47)

Therefore:

m\geq\frac{1}{C(\Delta)}\log\frac{N}{\delta}=\Omega\left(\frac{1}{\Delta^{2}}\log\frac{N}{\delta}\right)

(48)

This bound holds for
any
encoding, not just hash-based methods, because it derives from the fundamental limit on distinguishing neighbor from non-neighbor given the overlap gap—an information-theoretic constraint independent of the encoding mechanism.
∎

Corollary 8.2

(No Free Lunch for Similarity Search)
.

Any similarity search system achieving top-
k
preservation on
N
items with gap
\Delta
requires:

\Omega\left(\frac{\log N}{\Delta^{2}}\right)\text{ bits per query comparison}

(49)

This holds regardless of:

•

The choice of hash functions or encoding scheme

•

Whether the encoding is learned or hand-designed

•

The computational model (classical or quantum)

Remark 8.3
.

This impossibility result explains why decades of hash function engineering have not fundamentally improved complexity bounds: the bottleneck is information-theoretic, not algorithmic.

Similarity Has Physical Units

The Shannon-REWA equivalence reveals that semantic similarity is not an abstract notion but a measurable quantity with physical units:
bits of mutual information
.

When we say “
x

y

•

Comparison across domains
: A similarity of 3 bits in text retrieval is directly comparable to 3 bits in image search.

•

Resource allocation
: The bits required for encoding scale with the information content of similarity relationships.

•

Fundamental limits
: We can prove impossibility results for similarity tasks.

9.3
Design Principles

The information-theoretic view yields actionable principles:

1.

Maximize witness mutual information
: The signal is in
I(W_{x};W_{y})
, not the encoding. Invest in witness extraction, not hash optimization.

2.

The gap is the SNR
: Improve retrieval by increasing
\Delta_{near}-\Delta_{far}
, the signal-to-noise ratio of the similarity structure.

3.

Bits are bits
: Whether from Bloom filters, LSH, or neural encoders, each bit contributes the same capacity. Choose encodings for efficiency, not magical properties.

4.

Capacity bounds are real
: Do not expect algorithmic cleverness to beat information-theoretic limits. If you need higher accuracy, you need more bits or a better gap.

Future Directions

11.1
Witness Information Maximization

If similarity is mutual information, witness extraction should maximize
I(W_{x};W_{y})
for true neighbors while minimizing it for non-neighbors. This suggests a new objective for representation learning:

\max_{W}\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim P_{neighbor}}[I(W_{x};W_{y})]-\mathbb{E}_{(x,z)\sim P_{far}}[I(W_{x};W_{z})]

(50)

11.2
Capacity-Achieving Codes

Shannon proved that random codes achieve capacity. What is the analogous result for REWA? Are random hash functions optimal, or do structured codes (analogous to LDPC or turbo codes) achieve capacity with lower complexity?

11.3
Multi-User Information Theory

Retrieval systems serve multiple queries. The multi-user extension of Shannon theory (network information theory) may yield insights into shared index structures and query batching.

11.4
Quantum Similarity Search

Quantum channels have different capacity characteristics. Does quantum REWA offer advantages? The optimality theorem (
Theorem

8.1
) holds for classical encodings; quantum superposition may provide speedups for certain witness structures.

12
Conclusion

We have established that REWA—the theory of witness-based similarity—is mathematically equivalent to Shannon’s information theory applied to relational structure. Witness overlap is mutual information. Bit complexity bounds arise from channel capacity. Ranking preservation is rate-distortion optimal compression.

This equivalence has three profound implications:

1.

REWA bounds are optimal
: No encoding can beat the capacity limit. Fifty years of hash function engineering approached but could not exceed this fundamental barrier.

2.

Similarity has units
: Semantic relatedness is quantified in bits of mutual information, enabling principled comparison and resource allocation.

3.

The field is unified
: Bloom filters, LSH, neural retrieval, and transformer attention are instantiations of the same information-theoretic structure.

Shannon’s 1948 paper established that communication has fundamental limits. This paper establishes that similarity search obeys the same laws. The theory of information, born to understand telegraph wires, turns out to govern the architecture of meaning itself.

Higher Order Approximation of Continuous Functions by a Modified Meyer-König and Zeller-Type Operator Thanks: 1,2,3 This study is financed by the European Union-NextGenerationEU, through the National Recovery and Resilience Plan of the Republic of Bulgaria, project No BG-RRP-2.004-0008.
Ivan Gadjev 1, Parvan Parvanov 2, Rumen Uluchev 3

Abstract
. A new Goodman-Sharma type modification of the Meyer-König and Zeller operator for approximation of bounded continuous functions on [ 0 , 1 ) [0,\,1) is presented. We estimate the approximation error of the proposed operator and prove direct and strong converse theorems with respect to a related K-functional. The operator is linear but not a positive one. However it benefits a better order of approximation compared to the Goodman-Sharma variant of Meyer-König and Zeller type operator investigated by Ivanov and Parvanov [ 23 ] .

41A35, 41A10, 41A25, 41A27, 41A17.

1.
Introduction

The Meyer-Konig and Zeller operator (MKZ operator) was introduced in 1960, see
[
25
]
.
A minor modification by Cheney and Sharma
[
6
]
has led to the following definition of the
MKZ operator for functions
f\in C[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(1.1)

\begin{gathered}M_{n}^{[MKZ]}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f\Big(\frac{k}{k+n}\Big)P_{n,k}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1),\\
P_{n,k}(x)=\binom{n+k}{k}x^{k}(1-x)^{n+1}.\end{gathered}

Subsequently the MKZ operator was an object of intensive investigations and we refer the
reader, e.g. to the paper of Totik
[
27
]
where direct and converse results were proved.

Motivated by the integration of Bernstein polynomials by Derriennic
[
7
]
, some authors
have modified the MKZ operator to approximate Lebesgue integrable functions on
(0,1)
. Later
Heilmann
[
20
]
and Abel, Gupta and Ivan
[
1
]
merged these Durrmeyer-type
variants into the family of operators defined for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}

by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n,\alpha,\beta}(f,x)=(n+\alpha)\sum_{k=\max\{0,-\beta\}}^{\infty}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}P_{n+\alpha,k+\beta}(t)(1-t)^{-2}f(t)\,dt\bigg)P_{n,k}(x),

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n+\alpha>0
. Let us mention a few special cases:

•

\widetilde{M}_{n,2,0}\equiv\widetilde{M}^{\;\text{[Chen]}}_{n}
, introduced by Chen
[
4
]
;

•

\widetilde{M}_{n-1,0,-2}\equiv\widetilde{M}^{\;\text{[Guo]}}_{n}
, proposed by Guo
[
15
]
and studied in
[
18
,
16
]
;

•

\widetilde{M}_{n-1,1,0}\equiv\widetilde{M}^{\;\text{[G-A]}}_{n}
, introduced by Gupta and Abel
[
17
]
;

•

\widetilde{M}_{n,\alpha,0}\equiv\widetilde{M}^{\;\text{[Heilmann]}}_{n,\alpha}
, suggested by Heilmann
[
19
]
;

•

\widetilde{M}_{n,0,0}\equiv\widetilde{M}^{\;\text{[Heilmann]}}_{n,0}\equiv\widetilde{M}_{n}
, the “natural” MKZ-Durrmeyer operator.

In 2012, Ivanov and Parvanov
[
23
]
investigated the uniform weighted approximation error
of the Goodman-Sharma-type modification of MKZ operator (MKZ-GS operator) defined for

n\in\mathbb{N}
by

(1.3)

\begin{gathered}M_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,u_{n,k}(f)P_{n,k}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1),\\
u_{n,0}(f)=f(0),\qquad u_{n,k}(f)=n\int_{0}^{1}P_{n,k-1}(t)(1-t)^{-2}f(t)\,dt,\end{gathered}

where
f(x)
is a Lebesgue integrable function on
(0,1)
with a finite limit
f(0)
as
x\to 0
.
According to (
1.2
) we have

M_{n}(f,x)=f(0)P_{n,0}(x)+\widetilde{M}_{n,0,-1}(f,x).

The definition of the MKZ-GS operator follows an idea of Chen
[
5
]
and Goodman and Sharma

[
13
,
14
]
(see also
[
26
]
) for a modification of the Bernstein-Durrmeyer
operator
[
9
]
which recovers the linear functions. Similarly, the operator considered in
[
10
]

by Finta (see also
[
22
]
) is a Goodman-Sharma-type variant of the Baskakov-Durrmeyer
operator.

Let us denote, as usual, by

\varphi(x)=x(1-x)^{2}

the weight function which is naturally associated with the second order derivative of the MKZ-type
operators. We set
Df(x)=f^{\prime}(x)
,
D^{2}f(x)=f^{\prime\prime}(x)
,

(1.4)

\widetilde{D}f(x):=\varphi(x)f^{\prime\prime}(x)

and also

\widetilde{D}^{2}f(x):=\widetilde{D}\big(\widetilde{D}f(x)\big),\qquad\widetilde{D}^{3}f(x):=\widetilde{D}\big(\widetilde{D}^{2}f(x)\big).

The MKZ-GS operator combines nice properties both of the MKZ operator and of its Durrmeyer-type
modifications. Thus, the MKZ-GS operator
M_{n}^{[MKZ]}
recovers the linear functions and is suitable
for uniform approximation. Moreover,
M_{n}
just like the MKZ-Durrmeyer operator commutes in the
sense
M_{n}M_{k}f=M_{k}M_{n}f
, as well as it commutes with the differential operator
\widetilde{D}
, i.e.

M_{n}\widetilde{D}f=\widetilde{D}M_{n}f
.

Recently Acu and Agrawal
[
2
]
and Jabbar and Hassan
[
24
]
studied
operator families of Bernstein-Durrmeyer type and of Baskakov-type, respectively, where basis
functions in the definitions are replaced by linear combinations of basis functions of lower degree
with coefficients being polynomials of appropriate degree. The advantage is obtaining a better order
of approximation than the classical operators.

The ideas presented in
[
2
]
and
[
24
]
prompted the authors of the current paper
to explore a new modification of the MKZ-GS operator, explicitly defined by

(1.5)

\begin{gathered}\widetilde{M}_{n}(f,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{n,k}(f)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1),\\
\widetilde{P}_{n,k}(x)=P_{n,k}(x)-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}(x).\end{gathered}

To state our results we introduce some notations and definitions. Let
L_{\infty}[0,1)
be the
space of all Lebesgue measurable and essentially bounded functions on
[0,1)
and
AC_{loc}(0,1)

consists of the functions absolutely continuous on any subinterval

[a,b]\subset(0,1)
. We set

W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1):=\big\{g\,:\,g,g^{\prime}\in AC_{loc}(0,1),\ \widetilde{D}g\in L_{\infty}[0,1)\big\}.

By
W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
we denote the subspace of
W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1)
of functions
g
satisfying
the additional boundary condition

\lim_{x\to 0^{+}}\widetilde{D}g(x)=0.

From now on we will use
\|\cdot\|
to mean the uniform norm on the interval
[0,1)
. Sometimes
we will specify the interval
[0,a)
of the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{[0,a)}
with
a=1
or

a=\infty
.

Also, we will use the notations

•

x
for the variable if we consider functions
f
with domain
[0,1)
,

•

calligraphic symbols:
\mathpzc{x}
for the variable of functions
\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}},\mbox{\scriptsize{$\mathpzc{W}$}}
with domain
[0,\infty)
.

For functions
f\in C[0,1)
and
t>0
we define the K-functional

(1.6)

K(f,t):=\inf\big\{\|f-g\|+t\|\widetilde{D}^{2}g\|\,:\,g\,\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1),\,\widetilde{D}g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1)\big\}.

The goal of our study in this work is to estimate the approximation error for functions

f\in C[0,1)
using the modified MKZ-GS operator (
1.5
). In particular, by involving the
K-functional (
1.6
), we prove a direct inequality and a strong converse inequality of
Type B, according to the strong converse inequalities classification due to Ditzian and Ivanov in

[
8
]
. Our main results read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 2
, and
f\in C[0,1)
, then

\big\|\widetilde{M}_{n}f-f\big\|\leq\big(\sqrt{6}+1\big)\,K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big).

Theorem 1.2
.

For every function
f\in C[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 17
, there exist constants
C,L>0

such that

K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\leq C\,\frac{\ell^{2}}{n^{2}}\big(\big\|\widetilde{M}_{n}f-f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{M}_{\ell}f-f)\big\|\big).

for all
\ell\geq Ln
.

Remark 1.1
.

We can also formulate the results of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
in the manner:
there exists a natural number
k
such that

K\Big(f,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big)\sim\big\|\widetilde{M}_{n}f-f\big\|+\big\|\widetilde{M}_{kn}f-f\big\|.

Briefly, the paper is organized as follows. In Section 1 we give some historical notes, notations,
introduce the modified MKZ-GS operator
\widetilde{M}_{n}
and claim the main results. In Section 2 we consider
a particular transformation mapping functions defined on
[0,\infty)
into functions defined on

[0,1)
, which will help us to transfer results for the Goodman-Sharma modification of Baskakov
operator to their analogues for the modified MKZ-GS operator and vice versa. Preliminary and
auxiliary results for MKZ and Baskakov operators are presented in Section 3 and Section 4,
respectively. Section 5 includes an estimation of the norm of the operator
\widetilde{M}_{n}
, a Jackson type
inequality and a proof of the direct inequality stated in Theorem
1.1
. The last Section 6
is devoted to a converse result for the modified MKZ-GS operator (
1.5
). Inequalities of
the Voronovskaya type and Bernstein type concerning the approximation operator
\widetilde{M}_{n}
and the
differential operator
\widetilde{D}
, defined in (
1.4
), are proved. Theorem
1.2
represents
a strong converse inequality. A complete proof of the converse theorem is given.

2.
Relations between the Goodman-Sharma modification of Baskakov operator and the MKZ operator

A well-know (see
[
27
]
,
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
) and useful mapping of functions
defined on
[0,\infty)
into functions with domain
[0,1)
will help us to transfer our results for
the Goodman-Sharma modification of the Baskakov operator (see
[
12
]
) to their analogues
for the modified MKZ-GS operator.

Henceforth, if we have several operators of any kind (approximation, differential, etc.), written
consecutively in an expression, we will use the convention that this is the composition of the
operators and usually omit the brackets.

Let us consider the change of the variable
\sigma:[0,1)\to[0,\infty)
with

\mathpzc{x}=\sigma(x):=\frac{x}{1-x}\,.

Then the inverse change of the variable
\sigma^{-1}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1)
is given by

x=\sigma^{-1}(\mathpzc{x})=\frac{\mathpzc{x}}{1+\mathpzc{x}}\,.

An operator
\mathcal{T}
transforming a function
\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
into a function

f:[0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(2.1)

\big(\mathcal{T}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}\big)(x)=(1-x)\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}\Big(\frac{x}{1-x}\Big)=f(x),\qquad x\in[0,1),

Clearly, for the inverse operator
\mathcal{T}^{-1}
we have

(2.2)

\big(\mathcal{T}^{-1}f\big)(\mathpzc{x})=(1+\mathpzc{x})f\Big(\frac{\mathpzc{x}}{1+\mathpzc{x}}\Big)=\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}(\mathpzc{x}),\qquad\mathpzc{x}\in[0,\infty).

We mentioned above that
\widetilde{D}=\varphi D^{2}
,
\varphi(x)=x(1-x)^{2}
, naturally associates with
the MKZ type operators. Similarly, the corresponding differential operator for the Baskakov type
operators has the form

\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}=\psi D^{2},\qquad\psi(\mathpzc{x})=\mathpzc{x}(1+\mathpzc{x}).

Important relationships for the operators
\widetilde{D}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}
,
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{-1}
are the following:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}=\mathcal{T}(\psi D^{2}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}})=\varphi D^{2}\mathcal{T}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}=\widetilde{D}\mathcal{T}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{-1}\widetilde{D}f=\mathcal{T}^{-1}(\varphi D^{2}f)=\psi D^{2}\mathcal{T}^{-1}f=\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{T}^{-1}f.

From (
2.1
)–(
2.4
) we obtain:

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\,\mbox{\scriptsize{$\mathpzc{W}$}}(\mathpzc{x})=(1+\mathpzc{x})^{-1}
,
\mathpzc{x}\in[0,\infty)
, then
\mathcal{T}:W^{2}(\mbox{\scriptsize{$\mathpzc{W}$}}\psi)[0,\infty)\to W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1)

is a bijective mapping and there hold

\big\|\widetilde{D}\mathcal{T}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}\big\|_{[0,1)}=\big\|\mbox{\scriptsize{$\mathpzc{W}$}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}\big\|_{[0,\infty)}\,,\qquad\big\|\mbox{\scriptsize{$\mathpzc{W}$}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{T}^{-1}f\big\|_{[0,\infty)}=\|\widetilde{D}f\|_{[0,1)}\,,

i.e.

(2.5)

\big\|\widetilde{D}f\big\|_{[0,1)}=\big\|\mbox{\scriptsize{$\mathpzc{W}$}}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}\big\|_{[0,\infty)}\,.

The classical Baskakov operator
[
3
]
is defined by

(2.6)

\mathcal{B}_{n}(\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}},\mathpzc{x})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}\Big(\frac{k}{n}\Big)\,\mathcal{P}_{n,k}(\mathpzc{x}),\qquad\mathcal{P}_{n,k}(\mathpzc{x})=\binom{n+k-1}{k}\mathpzc{x}^{k}(1+\mathpzc{x})^{-n-k},

for
\mathpzc{x}\in[0,\infty)
.

The following statement gives a relation between the MKZ operator
M_{n}^{[MKZ]}
and the Baskakov
operator
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
23
, Proposition 7]
)
.

For every function
f
such that one of the series (representing
M_{n}^{[MKZ]}f
and

\mathcal{B}_{n}\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}
) in (
2.7
) is convergent and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(2.7)

M_{n}^{[MKZ]}(f,x)=(1-x)\mathcal{B}_{n}\Big(\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}},\frac{x}{1-x}\Big),\qquad x\in[0,1).

Finta
[
10
]
introduced the operator

(2.8)

\begin{gathered}V_{n}(\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}},\mathpzc{x})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,v_{n,k}(\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}})\mathcal{P}_{n,k}(\mathpzc{x}),\qquad\mathpzc{x}\in[0,\infty),\\
v_{n,0}(\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}})=\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}(0),\qquad v_{n,k}(\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}})=(n+1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{P}_{n+2,k-1}(t)\mbox{\small{$\mathpzc{F}$}}(t)\,dt,\end{gathered}

where

are the Baskakov basis functions given in (

of the Baskakov operator is similar to the Goodman-Sharma type variant of the classical Bernstein
operator. Then the operators (

Proposition 2.3

(
[

)
.

For every integrable function
f
such that one of the series (representing

and

)
in (

(2.9)

In

3.
Auxiliary Results for the modified MKZ-GS operator

We start this section with some properties of the MKZ-GS operator
M_{n}
from
[
23
]
:

•

M_{n}
is a linear positive operator;

•

M_{n}(e_{0},x)=e_{0}(x)
,
M_{n}(e_{1},x)=e_{1}(x)
, where
e_{k}(t)=t^{k}
,
k=0,1
;

•

M_{n}f\leq f
for every concave continuous function
f
.

Setting
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{1}=0
in
[
23
, eq. (4)]
we get partial cases of
Propositions 9–14 in
[
23
]
listed below, respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

​

(a)

If
\,f\in C[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then

M_{n}(f,x)-M_{n+1}(f,x)=\frac{1}{n(n+1)}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}(f,x),\qquad x\in[0,1).

(b)

For every
g\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\widetilde{D}M_{n}(g,x)=M_{n}(\widetilde{D}g)(x),\qquad x\in[0,1),

i.e.
M_{n}
commutes with the operator
\varphi D^{2}
on
W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
.

(c)

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then for every
f\in C[0,1)
we have
M_{n}M_{m}f=M_{m}M_{n}f
, i.e.

M_{m}
and
M_{n}
commute on
C[0,1)
.

(d)

For every
f\in C[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\|M_{n}f\|\leq\|f\|
,
i.e.
\|M_{n}\|=1
holds for the norm of the operator
M_{n}
on
C[0,1)
.

(e)

For every
g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|\widetilde{D}M_{n}g\|\leq\|\widetilde{D}g\|
.

(f)

Jackson-type inequality: If
\,g\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|M_{n}g-g\|\leq\frac{1}{n}\,\|\widetilde{D}g\|.

Now, we give properties of the operators
M_{n}
,
\widetilde{M}_{n}
and the differential operator
\widetilde{D}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If the operators
M_{n}
,
\widetilde{M}_{n}
and the differential operator
\widetilde{D}
are defined as in
(
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.4
), respectively, then

(a)

\widetilde{M}_{n}f=M_{n}\big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\big)
, for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
;

(b)

\widetilde{D}\widetilde{M}_{n}f=\widetilde{M}_{n}\widetilde{D}f
, for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
;

(c)

M_{n}\widetilde{M}_{n}f=\widetilde{M}_{n}M_{n}f
, for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
;

(d)

\widetilde{M}_{m}\widetilde{M}_{n}f=\widetilde{M}_{n}\widetilde{M}_{m}f
, for
f\in W^{2}_{0}(\varphi)[0,1)
;

(e)

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{M}_{n}f=f
, for
f\in W^{2}(\varphi)[0,1)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{n}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{n,k}(f)\widetilde{P}_{n,k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{n,k}(f)\Big(P_{n,k}-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}P_{n,k}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{n,k}(f)P_{n,k}-\frac{\varphi}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}u_{n,k}(f)D^{2}P_{n,k}=M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\varphi D^{2}M_{n}f

\displaystyle=M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}f.

Then from Proposition
3.1
(b) we obtain

\widetilde{M}_{n}f=M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}f=M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,M_{n}\widetilde{D}f=M_{n}\Big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\Big),

which proves (a).

Now, commutative properties (b) and (c) follow from (a) and Proposition
3.1
(b):

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}\widetilde{M}_{n}f

\displaystyle=\widetilde{D}M_{n}\Big(f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}f\Big)=M_{n}\Big(\widetilde{D}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}\widetilde{D}f\Big)=\widetilde{M}_{n}(\widetilde{D}f),

\displaystyle M_{n}\widetilde{M}_{n}f

\displaystyle=M_{n}\Big(M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}f\Big)=M_{n}M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,M_{n}\widetilde{D}M_{n}f

\displaystyle=M_{n}M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}M_{n}f=M_{n}(M_{n}f)-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}(M_{n}f)=\widetilde{M}_{n}M_{n}f.

The modified MKZ-GS operators commute in the sense of (d) since

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{m}\widetilde{M}_{n}f

\displaystyle=\widetilde{M}_{m}\Big(M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}f\Big)

\displaystyle=M_{m}\Big(M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}f\Big)-\frac{1}{m}\widetilde{D}M_{m}\Big(M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,\widetilde{D}M_{n}f\Big)

\displaystyle=M_{m}M_{n}\Big(f-\frac{m+n}{mn}\,\widetilde{D}f+\frac{1}{mn}\,\widetilde{D}^{2}f\Big).

The same expression in the last line we obtain for
\widetilde{M}_{n}\widetilde{M}_{m}f
because of property (a) and
Proposition
3.1
(b)–(c).

Applying Proposition
3.1
(b), (e)–(f) we have

\|\widetilde{M}_{n}f-f\|=\Big\|M_{n}f-\frac{1}{n}\,M_{n}\widetilde{D}f-f\Big\|\leq\|M_{n}f-f\|+\frac{1}{n}\,\big\|M_{n}\widetilde{D}f\big\|\leq\frac{2}{n}\,\|\widetilde{D}f\|.

Therefore
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\widetilde{M}_{n}f-f\|=0
, i.e. property (e) holds true.
∎

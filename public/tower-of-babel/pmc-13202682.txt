Two New Species of Collybiopsis (Omphalotaceae, Agaricales) from Southwestern Guizhou, China
Authors: Heng-Ting Chen, Zhu Guo-Sheng, Yang Tong-Jing, Li Biao, Gong Guang-Lu, Huang Wang-Bing, Shang Nian-Jie, Gui Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTwo new species of Collybiopsis, Co. guizhouensis and Co. anlongensis, are described from southwestern Guizhou Province, China, based on morphological and multi-gene phylogenetic analyses (internal transcribed spacer (ITS), nuclear large subunit ribosomal DNA (nrLSU), and translation elongation factor 1-α (tef1-α)). Phylogenetic analyses (ML/BI) of the combined ITS–nrLSU–tef1-α dataset placed each species within a distinct and well-supported clade: Co. guizhouensis (ML-BP = 100%; BI-PP = 0.94) and Co. anlongensis (ML-BP = 100%; BI-PP = 1.00). These molecular results, together with morphological distinctions, support their recognition as new species.Keywords: Taxonomy, phylogenetic analyses, new species, Guizhou

Introduction

Collybiopsis Earle (1909) is a genus of gymnopoid/marasmioid mushrooms belonging to the family Omphalotaceae Bresinsky typified by Co. ramealis [1–3]. As of April 12 2026, this genus contains 109 records in the Index Fungorum database (https://indexfungorum.org), comprising approximately 106 species. The primary morphological characteristics of Collybiopsis include diverse basidiomata forms (collybioid, gymnopoid, marasmielloid, omphalioid, and pleurotoid); differentially attached lamellae, primarily free to decurrent; a central to eccentric, insititious to subinsititious stipe; ellipsoid to oblong, hyaline, inamyloid basidiospores; a white spore print; the presence of caulocystidia; and coralloid or diverticulate terminal elements in the pileipellis [4–8]. Owing to the relatively limited variation and uncharacteristic features of their basidiomata, most related species with gymnopoid or marasmioid traits have been historically placed within Collybia Staude (1857) and Marasmius Fr. (1835), prior to the advent of molecular taxonomy. However, recent phylogenetic analyses have not only revealed the polyphyletic nature of the genera Gymnopus and Marasmiellus, but have also further elucidated the evolutionary relationships of these gymnopoid/marasmioid fungi within the families Omphalotaceae and Marasmiaceae [8,9].

The distribution of Collybiopsis spans the globe, with the highest diversity observed in tropical and temperate regions [2]. Members of this genus are typically saprobic, colonizing leaf litter and decaying wood as their primary substrate [10]. They play crucial ecological functions in ecosystem nutrient cycling by secreting cellulases and extracellular enzymes to decompose lignocellulose. For instance, the genome of Co. luxurians was found to contain 10 copies of the dye-decolorizing peroxidase (DyP) gene. DyP is a key oxidoreductase that attacks lignin structures and works synergistically with other cellulases to thoroughly decompose plant residues into simple nutrients that can be recycled within the ecosystem [11–13]. Guizhou Province, located in the core area of the karst landform in southwestern China, features a warm, humid climate, and diverse forest types, providing rich microhabitats for saprotrophic fungi. Despite the Omphalotaceae family being frequently identified as a dominant group in local macrofungal diversity surveys, only two species—Co. polygrammus and Co. subnudus—have been formally documented [14–16].

From 2019 to 2022, we conducted systematic surveys in representative areas of southwestern Guizhou (Bijie, Liupanshui, and Qianxinan). Using morphological observation and multi-gene phylogenetic analysis with the internal transcribed spacer (ITS), nuclear large subunit ribosomal DNA (nrLSU), nuclear small subunit ribosomal DNA (SSU), and translation elongation factor 1-α (tef1-α), we performed a taxonomic study on the Collybiopsis specimens collected from this region.

Materials and methods

Collections and morphology

Basidiomata of Collybiopsis were collected from September 2019 to August 2021 in three prefecture-level cities in Guizhou Province, China: Bijie, Liupanshui, and Qianxinan. Macroscopic morphological characteristics and habitat information of fresh specimens were recorded during collection, and field photographs were taken. Specimens were dried at 55 °C and deposited in the Herbarium of the Guizhou Academy of Agricultural Sciences (GZAAS).

Macroscopic descriptions are based on fresh basidiomata. Color codes followed the standard methodology of Kornerup and Wanscher [17]. Author citations for fungal names follow the Index Fungorum. For micromorphological observation, free-hand sections of dried basidiomata were prepared under a stereomicroscope (Motic SMZ 168 series). Dried tissues were rehydrated in a 5% KOH solution for 30–60 s and, if necessary, stained with 1% Congo red; then, they were observed under an Olympus X71 compound light microscope (Tokyo, Japan) (×20 to ×1000 magnification) [18]. At least 30 basidiospores and 20 basidia and cystidia (cheilocystidia, pleurocystidia, and caulocystidia) were measured per specimen. Spore size is expressed as “(a) b–c(d)”, where “a” and “d” are the minimum and maximum values, and “b–c” includes 90% of the measured spores. The length-to-width ratio of spores (Q = length/width) was calculated. Other parameters were as follows: “av.”, representing the average; “n”, the number of spores measured; “Qm”, the mean Q value; and “SD”, the standard deviation of Q [19].

Molecular identification

Genomic DNA was extracted from dried basidiomata using a DNA extraction kit (manufactured by Beijing BioTeke Corporation, Beijing, China) and stored at −20 °C. Four gene regions were amplified: the ITS region, using primers ITS1F/ITS4 [20,21]; the nrLSU region, using primers LR0R/LR5 [22]; the SSU region, using primers NS1/NS4 [20]; and the tef1-α region, using primers EF1-983F/EF1-2218R [23]. PCR amplification for these genes was performed following previously described methods [16]. The PCR products were sequenced by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The molecular sequences generated in this study were manually checked using BioEdit v.7.0.9 [24], and then subjected to BLAST searches in GenBank (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/). The newly generated sequences were deposited in GenBank (Accession Numbers are listed in Table 1).

Phylogenetic analysis was performed primarily by constructing a phylogenetic tree based on the combined ITS + nrLSU + tef1-α dataset. Additionally, nucleotide divergence analysis was conducted using the SSU and tef1-α sequences to further evaluate genetic distinctions among taxa. Because SSU sequences are highly conserved among closely related species, with very few variable sites, and because homologous sequences available in public databases are limited, SSU was not included in the multilocus phylogenetic tree building. It was used only to calculate base-pair differences. Following the methods of Kim et al. [3] and Yang et al. [28], relevant sequences were downloaded from the NCBI database; detailed information for all sequences is provided in Table 1. Sequences were aligned using MAFFT v.7 [32], and conserved regions were selected using GBLOCKS v.0.91b [33]. Datasets were concatenated using PhyloSuite v1.2.3 [33–35]. Phylogenetic trees were constructed using both Bayesian inference (BI) in MrBayes 3.2.7 [36] and maximum likelihood (ML) in IQ-TREE [37]. Prior to phylogenetic analyses, the best-fit nucleotide substitution models were determined using ModelFinder [38] based on the Bayesian information criterion (BIC); the selected models for each locus in ML and BI analyses are shown in Table 2. For the ML analysis, a partitioned strategy was applied to the three gene regions (ITS, nrLSU, and tef1-α), and the analysis was conducted in IQ-TREE with branch support evaluated using 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates. For the BI analysis, a partitioned strategy was applied to the concatenated dataset using the respective models listed in Table 2 with parameters set as follows: ngen = 1,000,000; samplefreq = 100; nchains = 4; stopval = 0.01. To assess the genetic differentiation between the new species and their relatives, pairwise base-pair differences were calculated based on SSU gene sequences using the p-distance model in MEGA 11 software [25]. Similarly, pairwise genetic distances based on tef1-α sequences were calculated using the p-distance model in MEGA 11 [25].

Results

Phylogenetic analysis

The phylogenetic tree reconstructed from the combined ITS–nrLSU–tef1-α dataset (Figure 1) showed that species of Collybiopsis formed several distinct clades. Specimens from this study clustered into two main clades: Co. guizhouensis (ML-BP = 100%; BI-PP = 0.94) and Co. anlongensis (ML-BP = 100%; BI-PP = 1.00). To assess consistency across loci, we also reconstructed a tree using only the ITS–nrLSU dataset (Figure S1). The topology of this two-gene tree was identical to that of the three-gene tree (Figure 1), and the two new species again formed independent, well-supported clades. No phylogenetic conflict was observed among ITS, nrLSU, and tef1-α.

Phylogenetic tree of the genus Collybiopsis inferred from a combined dataset of ITS, nLSU and tef1-α sequences using maximum likelihood (ML). Species of the genus Rhodocollybia were used as the outgroup. Node numbers indicate bootstrap support (BS) ≥ 70% and Bayesian posterior probabilities (PPs) ≥ 0.7, shown in the format BS/PP. Newly generated sequences in this study are shown in bold. Detailed information is provided in Table 1.

Analysis of SSU sequences revealed that Co. anlongensis (HMAS354257T), Co. guizhouensis (HMAS354259T), and Co. orientisubnuda (HMAS354255) all shared >99% sequence similarity with the known species Co. confluens (KY418936). There were five base-pair differences between Co. anlongensis (HMAS354257T) and Co. guizhouensis (HMAS354259T), whereas they differed from Co. orientisubnuda (HMAS354255) by 8 and 5 bases (Table S1). Further analysis of the tef1-α gene sequences, with sequence divergence quantified using the p-distance model (Table S2), showed that sequence divergence between Co. anlongensis (HMAS354257T) and Co. orientisubnuda (HMAS354256) is 3.25%. The sequence divergence between Co. guizhouensis (HMAS354259T) and Co. orientisubnuda (HMAS354256) is 2.76%, and the sequence divergence between Co. anlongensis (HMAS354257T) and Co. guizhouensis (HMAS354259T) is 5.81%.

Other specimens examined: China. Guizhou Province: Liupanshui City, Panzhou City, Yingwu Town, 25°49′44.1ʺN, 104°36′55.8ʺE, alt. 1894 m, September 6 2019, coll. Tongjing Yang, HMAS 354260.

Remark: Co. guizhouensis is also macroscopically similar to Co. orientisubnuda, Co. peronata, and Co. subnuda. However, it is distinguished from Co. orientisubnuda by its larger basidia (26.8–41 × 5.4–8.2 μm vs. 19.8–28.7 × 3.7–7.3 μm) [3], from Co. beijingensis by its broader cheilocystidia (5–9.5 μm vs. 4.5–6.3 μm) and presence of caulocystidia [28], from Co. peronata by its narrower stipe (2–4 mm vs. 3–8 mm) and shorter cheilocystidia (20–54.7 μm vs. 25–90 μm) [39], and from Co. subnuda by its medium-sized basidiospores (av. 9.9 × 4.0 μm) and presence of pleurocystidia [40]. Discussion

Collybiopsis anlongensis possesses dimorphic cheilocystidia and relatively large basidiospores (av. 11.5 × 4.1 μm); Co. guizhouensis possesses forked pleurocystidia and medium-sized basidiospores (av. 9.9 × 4.0 μm). These morphological features are relatively rare among the previously reported species of Collybiopsis. Notably, forked pleurocystidia have not been recorded in Collybiopsis species from China, and thus can serve as a diagnostic character for Co. guizhouensis. The two new species show significant differences in spore length and Q value (p < 0.05), but not in spore width. Although there may be overlap in individual spore measurements between the two species, the overall distributional differences in spore length and length/width ratio can provide quantitative reference for distinguishing them (Table S4, Figure S2). It is recommended that quantitative morphometric analysis be considered alongside traditional qualitative descriptions in taxonomic studies of Collybiopsis. Both new species were collected from karst forests in southwestern Guizhou, China. With the publication of these two species, the number of Collybiopsis species recorded in China has reached 32.

Phylogenetic trees based on three gene regions (ITS, nrLSU, and tef1-α) show that Co. anlongensis and Co. guizhouensis cluster together in a single well-supported subclade, indicating that they are closely related species. The tef1-α genetic distance between the two new species is 5.81% (Table S2), which falls within the interspecific distance range (2.35–6.74%) observed in the allied genus Gymnopus (Table S3), suggesting that tef1-α may serve as a complementary molecular marker for species delimitation in Collybiopsis. Despite their close phylogenetic relationship, the two new species differ markedly in cystidial morphology (dimorphic cheilocystidia vs. forked pleurocystidia) and spore size. This morphological divergence among closely related species may be related to the karst habitats in which they occur: the karst region of southwestern Guizhou is characterized by habitat fragmentation and heterogeneity, which may promote morphological divergence by providing diverse microhabitats [41,42]; however, this hypothesis requires further evidence. Based on data from only two new species, it remains unclear whether the observed morphological differences are driven by genetic drift, ecological selection, or stochastic divergence. At the same time, the utility of tef1-α needs to be tested in a broader range of Collybiopsis taxa. Future studies with expanded sampling are needed to determine the range of intraspecific and interspecific tef1-α variation, and to combine population genetics and ecological data to further explore the role of karst habitats in the diversification of Collybiopsis, thereby providing a stronger basis for integrative species delimitation in the genus.

Conclusions

This study successfully delimits and describes two new Collybiopsis species—Co. anlongensis and Co. guizhouensis—providing novel morphological and molecular evidence for understanding the diversity of this genus. This discovery highlights the critical role of the tef1-α gene in species delimitation within Collybiopsis and suggests that the diversity of this genus in China remains underexplored.

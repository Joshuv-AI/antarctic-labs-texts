EGONet: edge guided omni-directional attention with multi-scale bilateral feature integration for surface defect detection
Authors: Kamal M Othman, Faleh Alqahtani, Mai Alduailij, Inam Ullah, Mi Young Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSurface defect segmentation (SDS) is challenging in automated inspections due to the high variability of defects amid complex metallic textures. The defects show significant variations in size, shape, contrast, and spatial distribution. Existing segmentation methods struggle to jointly model fine boundary cues, omni-directional long-range dependencies, and multi-scale context in a single framework. To address these issues, we propose Edge-Guided Omni-Directional Attention Network (EGONet), a novel hierarchical architecture for metallic SDS. EGONet offers rich, multi-level intermediate features that capture both fine-grained details and broader semantic information. It also enhances multi-scale contextual understanding by integrating a Dense Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (DASPP) module with four parallel dilated-convolution branches and a learnable residual blend for maintaining spatial resolution. A Sobel-guided Edge Attention Module (EAM) highlights high-frequency boundary cues at the shallowest feature level via auxiliary edge supervision. The proposed Omni-Directional Attention (ODA) decomposes spatial attention into four axes to model long-range dependencies across all orientations via a two-stage refinement. A Quad-Statistical Spatial Attention Module (SAM) leverages quadruple pooling to deliver finer spatial sensitivity than traditional dual-pooling methods. Moreover, the Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) module recalibrates channel responses to suppress background interference. Lastly, a Bilateral Feature Integration Block (BFIB) combines the dual attention pathways via complementary asymmetric gating and residual stabilization. Extensive ablation studies validate each component’s contribution toward improving overall detection performance. Experiments on two benchmark datasets demonstrate that EGONet achieves competitive performance against state-of-the-art methods, with leading results across multiple defect categories on MT-Defect and strong generalization on SD900.Keywords: Defect segmentation, Edge guidance, Visual intelligence, Feature integration, Metallic inspection, Omni-directional attentionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Surface defect detection (SDD) is critical for maintaining product quality and operational efficiency in modern manufacturing. Conventional inspection methods suffer from fundamental limitations in precision and adaptability to complex patterns. Human-based inspection is inherently slow and prone to inconsistent judgments1. Ultrasonic methods are constrained by material acoustic properties and require considerable technical expertise2,3. Eddy-current techniques, while capable of detecting discontinuities in conductive materials, are sensitive to thickness variations and require sophisticated instrumentation and expert interpretation4. These limitations have collectively driven a shift towards automated vision-based inspection systems powered by deep learning5.

Deep learning-based SDD can be broadly divided into three paradigms: image-level classification, pixel-level segmentation, and region-level detection. Image-level classification identifies the presence of defects but cannot localize them6. Detecting anomalies in manufacturing requires spatial precision beyond coarse classification7. Region-level detection approximates localization via bounding boxes but often fails to delineate precise boundaries or exclude background clutter8,9. Pixel-level segmentation offers the most precise solution by providing detailed spatial information about the location, type, and boundary of the defect10,11, making it the dominant paradigm for inspecting industrial surfaces.

However, segmenting metallic surface defects presents challenges that go well beyond conventional segmentation tasks. Defects in magnetic tiles exhibit low contrast against complex textured backgrounds, irregular morphologies, and extreme scale variation12–14. Industrial imaging also introduces illumination variation, shadows, and reflective artifacts that obscure defect boundaries and produce weak textural responses that are difficult to distinguish from normal surface patterns15,16. Effective segmentation, therefore, requires architectures that jointly capture fine-grained local features and broad contextual information. Recent lightweight architectures, such as GCRANet17, have demonstrated that global context-guided feature refinement with depthwise self-attention can achieve a favorable trade-off between detection speed and accuracy when dealing with weak defect appearances. Nevertheless, achieving simultaneous fine-grained boundary fidelity, orientation-agnostic long-range context modeling, and multi-scale feature enrichment within a single framework optimized for low-contrast metallic surfaces remains an open and distinct challenge that lightweight, efficiency-focused designs do not fully address.

CNNs have demonstrated strong performance in defect detection through hierarchical extraction of local characteristics18–20. However, their static receptive fields fundamentally limit long-range spatial modeling, which is essential for recognizing globally distributed defect configurations. Remedies such as large-kernel convolutions21 and channel attention22 partially extend contextual reach but remain insufficient for spatially expansive or structurally complex defects. Vision Transformers (ViTs) address this through global self-attention23–25, but their emphasis on semantic co-occurrence often comes at the cost of fine-grained boundary precision. Hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures26–32 seek to unify both paradigms, yet naive fusion strategies such as element-wise addition or concatenation frequently fail to fully exploit their complementary strengths33. Notable models such as Swin-UNet34, TransUNet35, and more recent query-based Transformers augmented with wavelet-domain cross-attention and prototype-guided spatial refinement36 have demonstrated strong performance in capturing multi-scale frequency components and improving focus on defect regions under cluttered backgrounds. Building on these foundations, our network further extends boundary-aware feature learning by explicitly anchoring spatial attention gates to physically derived gradient magnitudes, introducing a complementary design dimension that operates alongside semantic and frequency-based representations. Consequently, sharp intensity transitions and directional discontinuities characteristic of defect boundaries are consistently underweighted. Bridging the gap between global context modeling and boundary-level fidelity remains a central open challenge in industrial defect segmentation.

A critical yet underexplored limitation of existing methods is their inability to explicitly model edge-level cues during feature learning. In metallic surface inspection, defect boundaries manifest as subtle directional intensity gradients: fine cracks produce narrow high-frequency transitions, while noisy backgrounds generate competing low-frequency activations37,38. Prior edge detection work has made significant progress through pixel-difference convolutions that encode gradient responses directly into network architectures39, collaborative multi-scale boundary supervision40, bio-inspired lightweight filtering for fine boundary recovery41, and decoupled boundary-region learning streams42. While these methods have demonstrated strong performance within their respective problem settings, they are primarily developed as standalone edge detectors targeting natural image benchmarks, and directly integrating their boundary signals into spatial attention gates within industrial defect segmentation pipelines remains an open design challenge. EGONet addresses this gap through its Edge Attention Module (EAM). It builds on the spirit of gradient-based boundary modeling and extends it in three complementary directions.It fuses fixed Sobel-derived gradient magnitudes directly into a spatial attention gate via element-wise modulation. It ensures boundary geometry governs attention localization alongside semantic similarity. An auxiliary edge loss supervises the attention gate against ground-truth boundary maps at the shallowest feature resolution. Fine defect contours remain most intact before downsampling at this stage. EAM operates on features already enriched by the DASPP module. This makes the gradient prior complementary to multi-scale semantic context and naturally suited to the low-contrast, high-variability conditions of industrial metallic surface inspection43.

The central contribution of EGONet lies in unifying explicit gradient-based boundary supervision, omni-directional long-range context modeling, and complementary bilateral feature fusion within a single architecture specifically optimized for low-contrast metallic SDS. While components such as multi-scale pooling, channel recalibration, and feature fusion have appeared individually in prior work, no existing method couples Sobel-anchored edge attention directly with orientation-agnostic directional attention and non-redundant dual-pathway integration into a cohesive, jointly optimized segmentation framework. Related work

Transformer-based defect segmentation

Vision Transformers (ViTs), initially proposed for natural language processing23,44,45, have shown promise in computer vision tasks. Dosovitskiy et al.25 introduced ViT by employing self-attention through image patching, linear embedding, and positional encoding. Wang et al.46 developed the Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT), which incorporates pyramid structures to handle multi-scale features. Zheng et al.47 applied pure Transformer architectures to semantic segmentation, exploiting their global modeling capabilities. Liu et al.48 proposed the Swin Transformer, which improves efficiency by using hierarchical structures and window-based self-attention. Xie et al.49 designed SegFormer, a model that integrates hierarchical Transformer encoders and lightweight MLP decoders for segmentation tasks. To address the quadratic complexity of self-attention, several solutions have been proposed. One approach uses local attention by limiting attention to windows48,50–52, with Swin48 and CSWin50 improving feature locality. Another approach involves generating coarse attention matrices through spatial downsampling while maintaining a global receptive field46,53–55. Although Transformers excel at global feature extraction, they struggle to capture the local details required for SDS, especially for small-scale defects with weak appearance. The complementary strengths and weaknesses of CNNs and Transformers have motivated the development of our proposed network to overcome these limitations.

CNN-based defect segmentation

CNNs have long been the backbone of SDS, primarily due to their strong local feature extraction capabilities56–58. Liu and He59 introduced a semantic triple-attention segmentation network that captures contextual information about small defects. Zhang et al.60 presented a dual-branch real-time network that integrates boundary-detail encoding and semantic context via auxiliary tasks, using Global Context Upsampling to address local-similarity issues. Yeung and Lam61 proposed a transformer-augmented model that combines spatial and channel attention to merge boundary and contextual information. Zuo et al.10 introduced a multi-scale segmentation model that combines feature pyramids and attention mechanisms for detecting defects of varying sizes. Despite these advances, CNNs still face limitations due to their constrained receptive fields, which hinder effective modeling of global dependencies and lead to suboptimal performance on large-area or scattered defects.

Deep feature extraction

To obtain semantically rich and spatially discriminative representations, the proposed network employs ResNet-5062 as its backbone encoder to extract hierarchical multi-scale features. ResNet-50 is composed of four convolutional residual stages, each generating feature maps at progressively reduced spatial resolutions while increasing channel depth. Specifically, the four output feature maps are extracted at resolutions , , , and , respectively. These multi-scale representations capture both fine-grained structural details in shallower layers and high-level semantic abstractions in deeper layers, which is essential for detecting defects of varying sizes, shapes, and contrast levels on complex surface textures. The residual connections inherent to ResNet-50 effectively mitigate the vanishing gradient problem, enabling stable training of deep representations. By leveraging ImageNet-pretrained weights, the backbone provides powerful, transferable visual features, enabling the network to generalize across diverse industrial surface inspection scenarios with limited annotated data.

Results and discussion

Datasets

EGONet is evaluated on the MT-Defect dataset67 and SD900 dataset68. These are widely adopted benchmarks for metallic tile SDS under real industrial inspection conditions.

MT-Defect: The MT-Defect dataset comprises 1,344 grayscale images, including 392 defect samples across five defect categories (blowhole, break, crack, fray, and uneven), and 952 defect-free (free) samples that serve as negative instances during training. Image resolutions vary considerably, ranging from to pixels, introducing additional spatial variability across samples. The dataset presents several challenges, including highly complex background textures that visually resemble defect regions, extreme scale variation ranging from sub-pixel hairline cracks to large-area structural deformations, severe class imbalance between defect and non-defect regions, and inconsistent illumination conditions typical of industrial metallic surface imaging. Representative input samples and their corresponding pixel-wise ground truth annotations across all six categories are shown in Fig. 5.

Representative MT-Defect samples across six categories.

SD900: SD900 dataset contains 900 grayscale images across multiple defect categories, with annotations derived using multiple constraints and improved texture feature analysis to produce reliable pixel-wise saliency ground truth. Strip steel surface defects exhibit distinct visual characteristics compared to magnetic tile defects, including elongated anisotropic patterns, directional rolling artifacts, and lower inter-class contrast, making this benchmark a complementary and more demanding testbed for boundary-sensitive segmentation models.

Implementation details

EGONet is implemented in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB VRAM). The ResNet-50 backbone is initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights to leverage transferable low-level visual representations, while all attention modules are trained from scratch with a standard initial learning rate of . The backbone parameters are fine-tuned at a reduced learning rate of to preserve pre-trained feature representations during early training stages. We employ the AdamW optimizer with a weight decay of and a cosine-annealed learning rate schedule to ensure smooth convergence over 200 training epochs. All input images are resized to pixels following standard segmentation protocols14,69, with a batch size of 8. To improve generalization and reduce overfitting on the limited MT-Defect training split, standard data augmentation techniques are applied during training, including random horizontal and vertical flips, random rotations, color jittering, and Gaussian blur. For the SD900 dataset, we follow the train/test splitting protocol established in13 to ensure a fair and reproducible comparison with existing methods. The combined training objective integrates Binary Cross-Entropy, Dice, and edge supervision losses, with empirically set weights , , and , thereby balancing pixel-level accuracy, region overlap, and boundary sharpness throughout optimization.

Backbone architecture study

We investigate the influence of the backbone encoder by replacing ResNet-50 with six computationally lighter alternatives, keeping all other modules unchanged. As reported in Table 1, lightweight backbones such as MobileNetV2 and MobileNetV3-Large yield substantially lower mIoU values (71.4% and 72.8%), primarily due to insufficient semantic depth for resolving fine-grained Crack and Blowhole categories. EfficientNet-B0 and EfficientNet-B2 improve upon MobileNet variants by offering better feature richness within similar parameter budgets, reaching 73.5% and 76.3%, respectively. ResNet-18 and ResNet-34 follow the expected progression, with accuracies of 74.9% and 78.7%, respectively, confirming that deeper residual representations benefit the multi-scale attention pipeline. ResNet-50 achieves the best overall mIoU of 81.8% with consistent gains across all six defect categories, particularly on Crack (+6.6% over ResNet-34) and Blowhole (+7.2% over ResNet-34), justifying its selection as the EGONet backbone. The monotonic performance improvement with increasing backbone capacity underscores the critical role of semantically rich multi-scale features.

Ablation studies

To systematically validate each architectural choice within EGONet, we conduct a series of controlled ablation experiments on the MT-Defect dataset. All variants are trained under identical settings (200 epochs), the AdamW optimizer, and an initial learning rate of to ensure a fair comparison. We systematically analyze backbone selection, module contributions, and internal design choices in DASPP, ODA, SAM, and BFIB, using per-category IoU and overall mIoU as evaluation metrics throughout.

Examining BFIB

To validate the bilateral feature integration design, we compare BFIB against progressively more capable fusion alternatives. As shown in Table 6, element-wise addition performs weakest at 79.3% mIoU, as it treats both pathways as equally informative without cross-branch interaction. Simple concatenation improves to 80.1% by allowing the network to learn relative feature weights after fusion. Removing the complementary gating mechanism yields 80.8%, confirming that asymmetric gating is crucial for enforcing non-redundant feature extraction between the high-level semantic and low-level structural branches. Removing only the residual stabilization connection decreases performance to 81.2%, highlighting its importance for gradient stability in later training phases. The full BFIB with complementary gating and residual connection achieves 81.8%, with notable gains especially in Crack and Blowhole over element-wise addition, confirming the effectiveness of each sub-component of the bilateral fusion design.

Training epoch analysis

We analyze the impact of training duration by evaluating EGONet at five epoch checkpoints. As shown in Table 7, at 50 epochs, the model reaches only 74.6% mIoU, since the attention modules, especially ODA and SAM, need sufficient gradient updates to learn meaningful directional and statistical pooling weights. Performance steadily improves through 100 epochs (77.8%) and 150 epochs (80.1%), where DASPP representations become stable, and BFIB fusion weights converge. The highest mIoU of 81.8% is reached at 200 epochs, when all modules are jointly optimized, and the combined loss effectively balances pixel-level BCE, Dice, and edge supervision. Extending training to 250 epochs slightly reduces performance to 81.5%, indicating mild overfitting on the limited MT-Defect training split. Per-category analysis shows that Crack and Blowhole, the two most difficult classes, benefit most from longer training, with Crack increasing from 51.4% at 50 epochs to 70.9% at 200 epochs. Based on these results, 200 epochs are selected as the optimal training duration.

Qualitative analysis

To complement the quantitative results, we provide qualitative visualizations of EGONet predictions across key defect categories on the MT-Defect dataset. Figures 6 and 7 show input images, GT annotations, and EGONet predictions for Blowhole, Break, and Uneven categories, respectively. In the Blowhole category (Fig. 6), defects manifest as highly localized, sub-pixel-scale white spots embedded within visually uniform, heavily textured grayscale backgrounds. Despite the extreme sparsity and low contrast of these defects, EGONet effectively locates most blowhole instances with precise alignment to the ground truth. Notably, samples without visible defects, in which GT masks are completely black, are correctly identified as defect-free, demonstrating strong true-negative suppression and robustness to false activations on complex metallic textures. This performance mainly results from the EAM, which emphasizes detailed boundary cues at the shallowest feature level, and from the Spatial Attention Module’s variance-pooling operator, which selectively boosts anomalous inter-channel activations at specific spatial locations. For the Break and Uneven categories (Fig. 7), defects appear as large-scale structural regions with irregular boundaries that vary greatly in shape and size across samples. The predictions of EGONet closely follow the GT, capturing broad spatial areas with well-defined boundaries. In Break samples, predicted masks accurately replicate complex contours along image corners and edges with sharp, angular transitions. For the Uneven category, which features the largest annotation regions and smoothest boundary gradients, EGONet produces clean, precisely bounded segmentation results. Minor smoothing artifacts at sharp boundary transitions highlight the inherent challenge of defining abrupt structural discontinuities in low-contrast grayscale images, but spatial correspondence remains high across all visualized samples. These qualitative observations align with the strong per-category IoU scores in Table 8, confirming the effectiveness of EGONet’s multi-scale attention pipeline for detecting defects with varying spatial scales and structural characteristics.

Edge attention visualization analysis

To provide direct visual evidence of the boundary localization capability of the proposed EAM, Fig. 8 presents a qualitative analysis across four representative samples from the MT-Defect dataset. For each sample, we visualize the input image, ground-truth mask, EGONet prediction, the Sobel Y response with its corresponding heatmap, the Sobel X response with its corresponding heatmap, the combined XY boundary refinement, and the overall edge-attention effect. The Sobel X operator captures vertical boundary transitions along defect contours, while the Sobel Y operator captures horizontal transitions, with their heatmaps confirming that gradient responses are strongly concentrated at true defect boundaries rather than background regions. The combined XY refinement produces a complete perimeter-level boundary prior that directly guides the spatial attention gate. The overall edge attention output demonstrates that EAM consistently sharpens defect contour localization across structurally diverse categories, including narrow elongated frays and large-area break regions, without introducing false activations in defect-free background regions.

Conclusion

In this work, we presented EGONet, a hierarchical attention-driven architecture for metallic surface defect segmentation that jointly addresses multi-scale contextual modeling, boundary-level precision, and orientation-agnostic structural understanding. DASPP enriches multi-scale representations across four dilation levels with a learnable residual blend, while the Sobel-guided EAM preserves high-frequency boundary cues via auxiliary edge supervision. The ODA mechanism captures long-range dependencies across four directional axes with two-stage progressive refinement, and the quad-statistical SAM leverages variance pooling to amplify low-contrast anomalous regions. BFIB consolidates dual-pathway features through complementary asymmetric gating, ensuring non-redundant information fusion. Ablation studies confirm the monotonic contribution of each module, and qualitative visualizations validate spatially precise segmentation across defect scales ranging from sub-pixel blowholes to large-area structural deformations. Experiments on two benchmark datasets demonstrate that EGONet achieves leading performance on MT-Defect and competitive results on SD900, though MSAC-Net attains a higher mIoU on the latter benchmark. The current evaluation is limited to two relatively small industrial datasets, and broader generalizability across larger and more diverse defect domains remains to be established. While EGONet demonstrates strong overall performance, certain defect categories, such as Break, present opportunities for further refinement, and extending the architecture toward lightweight deployment configurations remains a valuable direction for future investigation. In future work, we aim to extend EGONet to broader industrial defect domains, including textile, ceramic, and semiconductor surface inspection, assess its applicability to 3D point-cloud-based detection pipelines, and explore quantization and pruning strategies for more effective deployment.

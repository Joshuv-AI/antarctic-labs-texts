Discrete Diffusion Trajectory Alignment via Stepwise Decomposition
Jiaqi Han, Austin Wang, Minkai Xu, Wenda Chu, Meihua Dang, Yisong Yue, Stefano Ermon

Abstract
Discrete diffusion models have demonstrated great promise in modeling various sequence data, ranging from human language to biological sequences. Inspired by the success of RL in language models, there is growing interest in further improving the models by alignment with a certain reward. In this work, we propose a novel preference optimization method for masked discrete diffusion models through a principled diffusion trajectory alignment. Instead of applying the reward on the final output and backpropagating the gradient to the entire discrete denoising process, we decompose the problem into a set of stepwise alignment objectives. This framework enables efficient diffusion optimization, is compatible with arbitrary reward functions, and importantly, guarantees an equivalent optimal solution under additive factorization of the trajectory reward. Experiments across multiple domains including DNA sequence design, protein inverse folding, and language modeling consistently demonstrate the superiority of our approach. Notably, it achieves an up to 12% improvement over the most competitive RL-based baseline in terms of predicted activity on DNA sequence design, and further improves the GSM8K score from 78.6 to 80.7 on LLaDA-8B-Instruct for language modeling. 1 1 1 Code is available at https://github.com/hanjq17/discrete-diffusion-sdpo .

Preliminaries

Discrete diffusion models.
Discrete diffusion models
austin2021structured
;
lou2023discrete
;
sahoo2024simple
;
shi2024simplified
are a family of diffusion models with the latent variables residing in the discrete space
{\mathcal{X}}
with dimensionality
m
. With input data point
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
, discrete diffusion features a forward diffusion process in the form of Markov chain
q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})
with

\displaystyle q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})\coloneqq\mathrm{Cat}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t};\alpha_{t}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+(1-\alpha_{t})\bm{\pi}\right),

(1)

where
\bm{\pi}
is the vectorized representation of certain prior distribution
\mathrm{Cat}\left(\cdot;\bm{\pi}\right)
, and
\alpha_{t}
, usually referred to as the noise schedule, is a decreasing function
w.r.t.

t
satisfying that
\alpha_{0}=1
and
\alpha_{T}=0
. The transition for any two timesteps
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
that induces
q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})
is specified as
q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{s})=\mathrm{Cat}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{t};\alpha_{t|s}{\mathbf{x}}_{s}+(1-\alpha_{t|s})\bm{\pi}\right)
where
\alpha_{t|s}=\alpha_{t}/\alpha_{s}
.

Masked discrete diffusion models.
Masked discrete diffusion models
sahoo2024simple
;
shi2024simplified
;
lou2023discrete
;
austin2021structured
are discrete diffusion models when the prior
\bm{\pi}
is in particular instantiated as the absorbing state
{\mathbf{m}}\coloneqq[0,\cdots,0,1]
where the last entry in
{\mathbf{m}}
corresponds to a special
MASK
token. An intriguing property of masked diffusion models lies in that the posterior has a simplified form
sahoo2024simple
;
shi2024simplified
:

\displaystyle q({\mathbf{x}}_{s}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{x}}_{0})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Cat}({\mathbf{x}}_{s};{\mathbf{x}}_{t})&{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{m}},\\
\mathrm{Cat}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{s};\frac{\alpha_{s}-\alpha_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}{\mathbf{x}}_{0}+\frac{1-\alpha_{s}}{1-\alpha_{t}}{\mathbf{m}}\right)&{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{m}}.\end{cases}

(2)

The reversal
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{s}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})
is then parameterized by a neural network
{\mathbf{f}}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)
as

\displaystyle p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{s}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})=\begin{cases}\mathrm{Cat}({\mathbf{x}}_{s};{\mathbf{x}}_{t}),&{\mathbf{x}}_{t}\neq{\mathbf{m}},\\
\mathrm{Cat}\left({\mathbf{x}}_{s};\frac{\alpha_{s}-\alpha_{t}}{1-\alpha_{t}}{\mathbf{f}}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)+\frac{1-\alpha_{s}}{1-\alpha_{t}}{\mathbf{m}}\right)&{\mathbf{x}}_{t}={\mathbf{m}},\end{cases}

(3)

which is optimized to approximate the posterior by minimizing the negative evidence lower bound:

\displaystyle-\log p({\mathbf{x}}_{0})\leq{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NELBO}}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{t=T}\frac{\alpha_{t}-\alpha_{t-1}}{1-\alpha_{t}}\log\left({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}\cdot{\mathbf{f}}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t)\right).

(4)

Reinforcement learning with human feedback.
At alignment stage, a pretrained model
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})
is finetuned to maximize certain reward
r({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{c}})
subject to a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence regularization
w.r.t.
the reference model
p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})
, leading to the following objective:

\displaystyle\max_{p_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{c}}}\left[r({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{c}})\right]-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})\|p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})\right],

(5)

where
{\mathbf{c}}
is some context such as a prompt and
\beta
is the balancing factor. The choice of the reward model can be arbitrary, such as human or LLM-assisted preference labels
ouyang2022training
;
rafailov2023direct
;
wallace2024diffusion
, or the predicted activity of the designed DNA sequence
wang2024fine
. Such KL-constrained optimization problem has the optimal solution
peters2007reinforcement

\displaystyle p_{\beta}^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})=\frac{1}{Z({\mathbf{c}})}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{c}})\right),

(6)

where
Z({\mathbf{c}})=\sum_{\mathbf{x}}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r({\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{c}})\right)
is the partition function that is intractable to evaluate.

4
Method

In this section, we detail our approach for aligning discrete diffusion models through stepwise optimization. In §
4.1
, we first revisit the problem of discrete diffusion alignment and investigate the challenges. In §
4.2
, we propose a novel stepwise decomposition approach to address the optimization challenges for discrete diffusion alignment. In §
4.3
, we introduce a generalized and principled way to solve the stepwise alignment objective through distribution matching. We offer additional in-depth analyses and comparisons to existing diffusion alignment approaches in §
4.4
.

4.1
Aligning Discrete Diffusion Models

Different from autoregressive models where
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}|{\mathbf{c}})
can be computed efficiently in a single forward pass, discrete diffusion models are grounded on the chain of random variables
{\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}\coloneqq[{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\cdots,{\mathbf{x}}_{T}]
, where the joint satisfies the Markovian factorization
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})=p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{T}|{\mathbf{c}})\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
. The alignment objective in Eq.
5
is therefore extended to take into account the entire chain
wallace2024diffusion
:

\displaystyle\max_{p_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}}),{\mathbf{c}}}\left[\hat{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T},{\mathbf{c}})\right]-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})\|p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})\right],

(7)

where the reward function
\hat{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T},{\mathbf{c}})
now takes as input both the whole chain
{\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}
and the context
{\mathbf{c}}
. We therefore refer to the optimization problem of Eq.
7
as
diffusion trajectory optimization
. The optimal solution, akin to Eq.
6
, is given by

\displaystyle p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})=\frac{1}{Z({\mathbf{c}})}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta}r({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T},{\mathbf{c}})\right).

(8)

However, the optimization problem in Eq.
7
poses several challenges. First, the expectation is taken over the entire chain
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T})
, making it computationally expensive to estimate. Moreover, the definition of the reward
\hat{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T},{\mathbf{c}})
requires reconsideration as it is supposed to operate on the entire chain, while empirical rewards
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})
,
e.g.
, human preference
rafailov2023direct
or DNA activity
wang2024fine
, are most commonly defined on the clean sequence
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
. In the next subsection, we will introduce our stepwise decomposition approach that offers a simplified and tractable measure to solve Eq.
7
.

4.2
Diffusion Trajectory Optimization through Stepwise Decomposition

Here we propose a principled way to solve the problem by decomposing the trajectory optimization into a set of subproblems, each of which corresponds to a
stepwise
alignment objective for the posterior
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
at diffusion step
t
(see Fig.
1
). To be specific, the set of subproblems spanning across all diffusion steps
t
are given by

\displaystyle\max_{p_{\theta}}\mathbb{E}_{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}),{\mathbf{c}}}\left[r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})\right]-\beta_{t}D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\|p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right],\quad\forall 1\leq t\leq T,

(9)

where
\beta_{t}=\beta/w(t)
is the stepwise regularization reweighted by certain scheduler
w(t)
.
The optimal solutions can be similarly derived as

\displaystyle p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})=\frac{1}{Z_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right),\quad 1\leq t\leq T,

(10)

Flowchart of our SDPO.

where
\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{p({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\left[r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})\right]
is the
stepwise reward
.
Such formulation enjoys several unique benefits compared with the trajectory alignment objective in Eq.
7
. First, the expectation over the entire chain
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})
has been decomposed into the stepwise posterior
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
, which can be computed both tractably and efficiently for discrete diffusion models via Eq.
3
. By this means, we circumvent the dilemma in trading off efficient estimation and reducing bias, which is commonly suffered by existing diffusion preference optimization methods
wallace2024diffusion
. Furthermore, by grounding on the clean data
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
instead of any intermediate latent variables
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
, we can readily reuse the reward model
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})
without resorting to its biased estimates
chen2023constrastive
;
chen2024noisecontrastivealignmentlanguage
. More interestingly, we reveal a critical connection between the stepwise decomposition alignment objective (Eq.
9
) and the trajectory optimization objective (Eq.
7
): The joint
p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})
induced by the optimal
p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
of Eq.
9
is also the optimal of Eq.
7
, when
\hat{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T},{\mathbf{c}})
permits an additive factorization over diffusion steps. We formally state the connection in the theorem below.

Theorem 4.1
.

With the stepwise reward
\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})=\mathbb{E}_{p({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\left[r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})\right]
and the additive factorization of the chain reward
\hat{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T},{\mathbf{c}})=\sum_{t=0}^{t=T}w(t)\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
. Then, the joint
p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})
, induced by the optimal solutions
\{p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\}_{t=1}^{t=T}
of Eq.
9
, is also an optimal solution of Eq.
7
.

The proof is in Appendix. In particular, we define the stepwise reward at step
t
as the expectation of the empirical reward
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},c)
by drawing
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
from
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
. The reward of the entire chain is then collected from each step is an additive manner. Theorem
4.1
therefore endorses our key finding that the challenging and intractable diffusion trajectory optimization problem can be alternatively approached by jointly optimizing the per-step alignment objectives, offering a principled view of aligning discrete diffusion models.

4.3
Generalized Stepwise Alignment through Distribution Matching

While the stepwise decomposition has yielded a simplified objective, it is still yet unclear how to optimize
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
towards the optimal solution
p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
, particularly under arbitrary reward
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})
.
To this end, existing works seek to directly backpropagate the gradient from the reward model
wang2024fine
, which inevitably incurs optimization overhead and instability, or to simplify the reward into tractable forms such as the Bradley-Terry model
wallace2024diffusion
, which imposes additional constraints on the reward. Distinct from these methods, we perform optimization based on the following objective:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[\tilde{p}_{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\|\tilde{p}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right]\right],

(11)

where
\tilde{p}_{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\propto p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))
is the Boltzmann policy
laidlaw2022the
induced by the stepwise reward then reweighted by
p_{\mathrm{ref}}
, while
\tilde{p}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\propto p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})^{(1-\beta_{t})}p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})^{\beta_{t}}
is similarly the
p_{\mathrm{ref}}
reweighted model policy. The rationale of designing Eq.
11
lies in that the minimizer of such KL-divergence distribution matching
han2024f
;
Ji2024TowardsExact
problem is also the optimal solution of the stepwise alignment objective, as assured in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\theta^{\ast}=\argmin{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
defined in Eq.
11
. Then
p_{\theta^{\ast}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
is the optimal solution of the stepwise alignment objective in Eq.
9
.

Empirical form.
Besides the guaranteed equivalence of the optimal solution, the definition of
\tilde{p}_{r}
also enables performing importance sampling by using
p_{\mathrm{ref}}
as the proposal distribution, from which the offline preference optimization datasets are drawn. With some derivations (detailed proof in Appendix), we arrive at
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}_{t}(\theta)
, a tractable form of the per-step distribution matching loss
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{N}_{t}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}\right)+C,

(12)

where
\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t})=\beta_{t}\left(\log p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})-\log p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right)
is the implicit reward
rafailov2023direct
for diffusion step
t
, and
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(i)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}
are
N
Monte Carlo samples
w.r.t.
the same context
{\mathbf{c}}
. The simplified expression in Eq.
12
takes the form of cross-entropy loss between the self-normalized Boltzmann policies induced by
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})
and
\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t})
, respectively. More importantly, it is also an asymptotically unbiased estimate of
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
:

Proposition 4.3
.

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{N}(\theta)={\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
.

The proof is in Appendix. Therefore, we employ
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}_{t}(\theta)
as the objective to solve each subproblem of Eq.
9
at diffusion step
t
. For the sample size
N
, we view it as a hyperparameter that trades off between efficiency and bias, depending on the task and dataset available.

4.4
Overall Objective

Since the final objective (Eq.
9
) requires to jointly optimize for the subproblems across all diffusion steps, at each iteration we randomly select a batch of diffusion steps, and optimize the corresponding
{\mathcal{L}}^{N}_{t}
as per Eq.
12
. Furthermore, since in most scenarios the intermediate samples
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
are not preserved, we instead keep track of the clean samples
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}
obtained from
p_{\mathrm{ref}}
while approaching the corresponding
{\mathbf{x}}_{t}
via the forward process
q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})
at each training step. Putting all together we obtain our final loss

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0},q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}\right),

(13)

where
\tilde{r}_{\theta}
, by further leveraging the reversal parameterization of masked diffusion models (Eq.
3
) and the definition
\beta_{t}=\beta/w(t)
, has the following simplified instantiation:

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t})=\beta\left(\frac{\log({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}{\mathbf{f}}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,{\mathbf{c}}))}{w(t)}-\frac{\log({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,{\mathbf{c}}))}{w(t)}\right).

(14)

We note that our method applies to general discrete diffusion, but we choose to focus specifically on the masked variant. Our final loss has several implications, which we will analyze below.

Pairwise preference data.
Our loss possesses a generalized form
w.r.t.
the reward model
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}})
and
N
,
i.e.
, the number of samples for each context or prompt
{\mathbf{c}}
. In particular, it subsumes the setting in DPO where each prompt is provided with a pair of winning and losing completions
({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)})
, by setting
N=2
and leveraging Bradley-Terry (BT) model as the reward,
i.e.
,
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{c}})=0
and
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)},{\mathbf{c}})=-\infty
. We provide detailed derivations of our loss in this special case in Appendix.

The role of
w(t)
.
The coefficient
w(t)
is initially introduced in Theorem
4.1
as the weight for the per-step reward
\hat{r}_{t}
. Interestingly, from Eq.
14
we can also interpret
w(t)
as a factor that controls the scale of
\log({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}{\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t))
, which is correlated to the number of masked tokens at step
t
. Therefore we set
w(t)=1-\alpha_{t}
to amortize the loss to each token, and empirically find this choice effective.

The role of
\beta
.
Eq.
7
reveals that
\beta
controls the strength of the KL regularization
w.r.t.
the reference distribution, which is also widely reflected in literature
rafailov2023direct
;
wallace2024diffusion
;
ouyang2022training
.

Iterative labeling.
Empirically we have also explored a variant of our approach that updates the dataset with samples from the latest model and their corresponding rewards. We find such iterative labeling generally favorable since more useful rewards are progressively provided for samples of higher quality, as the training proceeds. We defer detailed justifications to §
5.4
.

wang2024fine

wang2024fine

6
Discussion

Limitation.
Our framework relies on the reward model while, in practice, such model may be noisy or even harmful, which will potentially lead to undesired consequence.

Conclusion.
In this work, we present SDPO for preference optimization of discrete diffusion models. Our key insight lies in decomposing diffusion trajectory alignment into a set of subproblems for each diffusion step. By factorizing the reward into stepwise surrogates, we propose to align the posterior
p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t})
for each step and draw an equivalence between the two objectives, with which we further derive a principled loss function. Our experiments on a wide range of tasks including DNA sequence design, protein inverse folding, and language modeling consistently verify the efficacy of, showing its potential towards building performant and applicable discrete diffusion models.

Proof of Theorem 4.1

Theorem 4.1
.

Define the stepwise reward

\displaystyle p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})=\frac{1}{Z_{t}}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right),\quad\forall 1\leq t\leq T.

\displaystyle p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})

(16)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z_{t}}\sum_{{\mathbf{x}}_{0}}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\exp\left(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right)q({\mathbf{x}}_{t-1}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}),

\displaystyle p^{\ast}({\mathbf{x}}_{0:T}|{\mathbf{c}})

A.2
Proof of Proposition 4.2

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\theta^{\ast}=\argmin{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
defined in Eq. 11. Then
p_{\theta^{\ast}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})
is the optimal solution of the stepwise alignment objective in Eq. 9.

Proof.

Recall the definition of
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
in Eq. 11:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[\tilde{p}_{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\|\tilde{p}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\right]\right].

(26)

Since the KL-divergence is minimized when the two distributions are exactly matched, we have that the optimal
\theta^{\ast}
satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})=\tilde{p}_{\theta^{\ast}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}).

(27)

By leveraging the definition of
\tilde{p}_{r}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Z_{r}}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))=\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})^{(1-\beta_{t})}p_{\theta^{\ast}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})^{\beta_{t}},

(28)

which simplifies to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{p_{\theta^{\ast}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\right)^{\beta_{t}}=\frac{Z_{\theta}}{Z_{r}}\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})),

(29)

∎

and finally gives us

\displaystyle p_{\theta^{\ast}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})=\frac{1}{Z^{\prime}}p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})\exp(\frac{1}{\beta_{t}}\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})),

(30)

where

. The proof is therefore completed.

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\left[\frac{\tilde{p}_{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\log\left(\frac{\tilde{p}_{r}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})/p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}{\tilde{p}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})/p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\right)\right],

(33)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\left[\frac{1}{Z_{r}}\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))\log\left(\frac{\frac{1}{Z_{r}}\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}}\left(\frac{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\right)^{\beta_{t}}}\right)\right].

(34)

Recalling the definition of the implicit reward, which is given by
\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t})=\beta_{t}(\log p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})-\log p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))
, we have that
\left(\frac{p_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}{p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\right)^{\beta_{t}}=\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))
. Therefore, we can further simplify

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0}\sim p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\left[\frac{1}{Z_{r}}\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))\log\left(\frac{\frac{1}{Z_{r}}\exp(\hat{r}_{t}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}\right)\right],

(35)

where we can approach the self-normalized Boltzmann policies, namely,
\frac{1}{Z_{r}}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))
and
\frac{1}{Z_{\theta}}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))
, through Monte-Carlo estimate using
N
samples, leading to the empirical surrogate
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{N}(\theta)
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)

\displaystyle\approx{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{N}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}}{\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}}\right),

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}\right)+C,

(36)

where
C=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},p_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}|{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\right)
is a constant irrelevant to
\theta
. Therefore we have completed the derivation from
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}(\theta)
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{t}^{N}(\theta)
, with the latter being am empirical Monte-Carlo estimate with
N
samples drawn from the same context
{\mathbf{c}}
.

A.5
Derivation of Eq. 13 in DPO Setting

Recall our proposed loss function
{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)
:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0},q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(i)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(j)}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t}))}\right),

(41)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}_{t},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t})=\beta\left(\frac{\log({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}{\mathbf{f}}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,{\mathbf{c}}))}{w(t)}-\frac{\log({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{\top}{\mathbf{f}}_{\mathrm{ref}}({\mathbf{x}}_{t},t,{\mathbf{c}}))}{w(t)}\right).

(42)

Here we derive a specific instance of
{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)
in the DPO pairwise preference setting, and draw connection of it to
[
58
]
.

In particular, in DPO preference pair setting for each context
{\mathbf{c}}
there are two completions, namely,
N=2
in our case. Furthermore, one completion is labeled as the preferred (chosen) response
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)}
and the other as rejected sample
{\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)}
. Since no explicit real-valued reward on the chosen and rejected sample is provided, the Bradley-Terry (BT) model
[
6
]
is adopted, which corresponds to, in our case, setting
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{c}})=0
and
r({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)},{\mathbf{c}})=-\infty
. Under this specification,
{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)
is simplified as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0},q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}[\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))+\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{t}))}{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{t}))+\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{t}))}

\displaystyle+\frac{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}{\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))+\exp(r({\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{0},{\mathbf{c}}))}\cdot\log\frac{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{t}))}{\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(w)}_{t}))+\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{0},{\mathbf{x}}^{(l)}_{t}))}],

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0},q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}\left[\frac{1}{1+0}\log\frac{1}{1+\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(l)})-\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(w)}))}+0\right],

(43)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0},q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}\left[\log\frac{1}{1+\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(l)})-\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(w)}))}\right],

(44)

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}_{t,{\mathbf{c}},{\mathbf{x}}_{0},q({\mathbf{x}}_{t}|{\mathbf{x}}_{0})}\log\sigma\left(\exp(\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(w)})-\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(l)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(l)}))\right),

(45)

where
\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(w)})
is shorthand for
\tilde{r}_{\theta}({\mathbf{x}}_{0}^{(w)},{\mathbf{x}}_{t}^{(w)},{\mathbf{c}},\beta_{t})
and similarly for the losing sample. Eq.
45
underscores an interesting connection of our loss to that of
[
58
]
specifically in the DPO setting, since both share the same form of negative logsigmoid over the margin between the implicit rewards of the winning and losing sample, with the difference being the definition of the implicit reward (Eq.
14
), depending on whether using Gaussian diffusion
[
58
]
or discrete diffusion as in this work. Notably, since we leverage a general formulation of stepwise decomposition that reduces the problem to a stepwise distribution matching objective, we are able to generalize to the setting with arbitrary number of samples and reward model, which is not revealed in
[
58
]
.

Question

[Few-shot Prompts] Carlos is planting a lemon tree. The tree will cost $90 to plant. Each year it will grow 7 lemons, which he can sell for $1.5 each. It costs $3 a year to water and feed the tree. How many years will it take before he starts earning money on the lemon tree?

Answer by LLaDA-8B-Instruct
[
40
]

To determine how many years it will take for Carlos to start earning money on the lemon tree, we need to calculate the net profit per year and then see how many years it will take for the net profit to cover the initial cost of planting the tree.

1. **Calculate the net profit per year:**

- Revenue from selling lemons: 7 lemons * $1.5 per lemon = $10.50

- Cost to water and feed the tree: $3

- Net profit per year: $10.50 - $3 = $7.50

2. **Determine the number of years needed to cover the initial cost:**

- Initial cost of planting the tree: $90

- Net profit per year: $7.50

- Number of years needed: $90 / $7.50 per year = 12 years

Therefore, it will take Carlos 12 years to start earning money on the lemon tree.

Answer by LLaDA-8B-Instruct-SDPO (Ours)

First, let’s calculate the annual revenue from selling the lemons:

- The tree grows 7 lemons each year.

- Each lemon sells for $1.5.

- Annual revenue = 7 lemons * $1.5/lemmon = $10.5.

Next, let’s consider the annual cost of watering and feeding the tree:

- It costs $3 per year to water and feed the tree.

Now, let’s calculate the net annual profit:

- Net annual profit = Annual revenue - Annual cost

- Net annual profit = $10.5 - $3 = $7.5.

Finally, let’s determine how many years it will take for the net annual profit to cover the initial cost of planting the tree:

- Number of years = Initial cost / Net annual profit

- Number of years = $90 / $7.5 = 12 years.

Therefore, it will take Carlos 12 years to start earning money on the lemon tree.

Appendix D
Ethics and Impact Statement

In this work, we present a novel approach for discrete diffusion model alignment via stepwise decomposition. The goal is to develop a fundamental algorithm for preference optimization of discrete diffusion models, where we do not find ethical concerns that need to be highlight.

As demonstrated in the paper, our approach can help finetune pretrained discrete diffusion models for better alignment towards certain reward, which can have significant practical impact in various domains, such as natural language modeling and biochemical sciences. Our approach can serve as a critical building block towards designing useful DNA and protein sequences, building helpful and harmless chatbots and even performant and effective large language model agentic systems.

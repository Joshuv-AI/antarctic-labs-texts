Scales, products and the second row of the Scheepers diagram
Michał Pawlikowski, Piotr Szewczak, Lyubomyr Zdomskyy

Abstract
. We consider products of sets of reals with a combinatorial structure based on scales parameterized by filters. Sets of this kind have been extensively investigated in products of spaces with combinatorial covering properties such as the Hurewicz, Scheepers, Menger, and Rothberger properties. We will complete this picture by focusing on properties from the second row of the Scheepers diagram. We solve in the affirmative a problem due to Bartoszyński–Tsaban, whether all finite powers of a 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} -scale set satisfy the strong covering property 𝖲 1 ​ ( Γ , Ω ) {\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)} . We also show that, in the Miller model, the product of finitely many 𝔡 \mathfrak{d} -concentrated sets satisfies 𝖲 1 ​ ( Γ , Ω ) {\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)} . We further provide product counterexamples demonstrating limitations of the methods used.

The mainstream of research on properties in the Scheepers diagram concerns

{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}

By a

By

We identify each

The topologies inherited from

For functions

Sets of this kind play an important role when considering products with combinatorial covering properties.

A

Let

The common combinatorial feature behind the scale constructions used in our positive results is isolated in Definition 2.1. For an infinite cardinal

Our first main result gives a uniform product theorem for these sets and provides a

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\kappa
be an infinite regular cardinal number with
\kappa\leq\mathfrak{d}
, let

\kappa
-fin-unbounded set, let

{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Bor}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{Bor}})}

\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
.

The proofs of this result and its applications use the Michael topology on the relevant scale sets as an auxiliary device. Isolating the infinite points in this topology makes it possible to streamline the combinatorial arguments for products; the statements displayed here are the resulting versions for the standard topology. Together with the scale results in Section
3

\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(F)
-scale sets mentioned above.

Theorem 4.1

Let

This result extends earlier product theorems for scale sets and illustrates that the same combinatorial framework applies to several properties from the second and third rows of the Scheepers diagram.

Assume that

Theorem 5.8

Definition 2.1

Let

A set

Let

For a set

3.

In the theory of combinatorial covering properties, the principal objects of study are
sets of reals.
One may ask why we considered the Michael topology, rather than the standard topology,
in the previous section.

Using the Michael topology on

\mathfrak{d}
-concentrated sets in the Miller model

As mentioned in the introduction, by
[

-concentrated on

is Menger
[

that there are sets that are

-concentrated on

whose product is not Menger
[

Let
U
and
\tilde{U}
be ultrafilters. We say that

is

to
\tilde{U}
if there is a function

such that for each set

, we have

\bigcup{\left\{\,[h(n),h(n+1)):u\cap[h(n),h(n+1))\neq\emptyset\,\right\}}\in\tilde{U}.

The near coherence of filters (
\mathsf{NCF}
), introduced by Blass, is the statement that any two ultrafilters are near coherent
[
5
]
. This statement is independent of
\mathsf{ZFC}
; for a proof of consistency see
[
7
]
. Consistency of

can be inferred from a result of Blass
[
5

holds.
Let

be an ultrafilter. We say that

if for each

b\in B

such that
b\subseteq u
. By

\mathfrak{u}
we denote the minimal
cardinality of an ultrafilter base.
It is a known fact that, if
\mathsf{NCF}
holds, then
\mathfrak{u}<\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{b}(U)
for every

U
; see
[
5
, Corollary 15, Theorem 16]
.
In particular,
\mathfrak{d}
is regular under
\mathsf{NCF}
.
In fact,
\mathsf{NCF}
is equivalent to the statement “
\mathfrak{u}<\mathfrak{b}(U)

for every
ultrafilter
U
” by
[
15
, Proposition 2.1(c), Proposition 3.2(e)]
.
Note that the set
X
is
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded if and only if the set
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is
\mathfrak{d}
-concentrated on
\mathrm{Fin}

[

, Lemma 2.3]

Recall that a set

is a

-scale

\left|X\right|\geq\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)
and for any function

of cardinality smaller than
\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)
such that

for all functions

.

Let

be an ultrafilter such that
\operatorname{\mathfrak{b}}(U)=\mathfrak{d}
and

has a basis of cardinality less than
\mathfrak{d}
.

-unbounded set in

Proof.

Suppose that

is a
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded set that is not a

-scale.
Then there is a function

such that

Since every subset of

of cardinality at least
\mathfrak{d}
is
\mathfrak{d}
-unbounded and
\mathfrak{d}
is regular, we may assume that

is
\leq_{U}
-bounded by a function

.
Let

be a basis of

of size less than
\mathfrak{d}
.
Define

Since

is
\mathfrak{d}

with

.
Since

, there is a set

. Take a natural number

then

x(i)\leq x(k_{i})\leq x(n)\leq b(n)\leq b(k_{i+1})=c(i)\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(n)=i.

It follows that
\varphi\bigl[[x\leq b]\bigl]\subseteq[x\leq c]
.
Since
[x\leq c]\in U
, we have
\varphi\bigl[[x\leq b]\bigl]\in F
, and thus
x\leq_{F}c
.
We conclude that
Y
is bounded with respect
\leq_{F}
, a contradiction.
∎

7.1.
Characterization of
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Lambda),\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
, and
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)

In this section, we present the characterizations of
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Lambda),\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
, and

\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)
. The characterization of
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)}
appears implicitly in the paper by Peng
[
17
]
.
Here we state his result more explicitly and devise characterizations of
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Lambda)
and
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)

using his ideas. We denote by
\omega+1
the space
\omega\cup\{\omega\}
endowed with the order topology.
Consequently,
\omega+1
is a one point compactification of
\omega
.

Lemma 7.1
.

[
17
, Proof of Proposition 6.]

Let
X
be a zero-dimensional, separable metric space and
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dotsc\in\Gamma(X)
, where
\mathcal{U}_{n}=\{\,U_{n,m}:m\in\omega\,\}
for all
n
.
Then for all natural numbers
n
and
m
there is a continuous function
f_{n,m}\colon X\to\omega+1
such that

X\setminus U_{n,m}=f_{n,m}^{-1}[\{\omega\}]
.

Proof.

Fix natural numbers
n
and
m
.
Then
U_{n,m}=\bigcup_{i\in\omega}A_{n,m,i}
, where each
A_{n,m,i}
is
clopen and

A_{n,m,i}\subseteq A_{n,m,i+1}
for all

i\in\omega
.
Define
f_{n,m}\colon X\to\omega+1
by

f_{n,m}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}i,&\text{ if }x\in A_{n,m,i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}A_{n,m,j},\\
\omega,&\text{ if }x\not\in U_{n,m}\\
\end{cases}.

Fix a nonempty basic open set
U\subseteq\omega+1
. If
\omega\notin U
, then

f_{n,m}^{-1}[U]=\bigcup_{i\in U}f_{n,m}^{-1}[\{i\}]=\bigcup_{i\in U}\biggl(A_{n,m,i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}A_{n,m,j}\biggr)

is an open subset of
X
.
Assume that
\omega\in U
.
Then
U=(\omega+1)\setminus F
for some finite subset
F\subseteq\omega
and

f_{n,m}^{-1}[U]=X\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}f_{n,m}^{-1}[\{i\}]=X\setminus\bigcup_{i\in F}\biggl(A_{n,m,i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}A_{n,m,j}\biggr).

Since
\bigcup_{i\in F}\bigl(A_{n,m,i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}A_{n,m,j}\bigr)
is clopen, it follows
that
f_{n,m}^{-1}[U]
is open.

∎

Fix
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
.
We identify
a
with its graph, regarded as a subset of
\omega\times\omega
.
We denote by
\mathrm{cF}(a)
the filter consisting of all
cofinite subsets of
a
.
We denote by
\mathcal{X}
the set of all functions
f\in(\omega+1)^{\omega\times\omega}
such that the sets
\{\,m:f(n,m)=\omega\,\}
are finite for all natural numbers
n
.
Consider
\mathcal{X}
with a subspace topology of the space
(\omega+1)^{\omega\times\omega}
with the product topology.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
X
be a zero-dimensional, separable metric space.

(1)

The space
X
is
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Gamma)}
if and only if for each continuous

\varphi\colon X\to\mathcal{X}
there is
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that
\{\,a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:x\in X\,\}\subseteq\mathrm{cF}(a)
;

(2)

The space
X
is
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
if and only if for each continuous

\varphi\colon X\to\mathcal{X}
there is
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that
\{\,a\setminus\bigcup_{x\in F}\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:F\in[X]^{<\omega}\,\}\subseteq[a]^{\omega}
;

(3)

The space
X
is
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Lambda)
if and only if for each continuous

\varphi\colon X\to\mathcal{X}
there is
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that

\{\,a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:x\in X\,\}\subseteq[a]^{\omega}
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Let
\mathcal{A}\in\{\Gamma,\Omega,\Lambda\}
and assume that
X
is
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
.
Fix a continuous function
\varphi\colon X\to\mathcal{X}
.
Let

U_{n,m}\coloneqq\{\,g\in\mathcal{X}:g(n,m)<\omega\,\}

for all natural numbers
n,m
.
Let

T\coloneqq\{\,n\in\omega:\varphi[X]\subseteq U_{n,m}\text{ for some }m\in\omega\,\}.

For every
n\in T
, choose
a(n)\in\omega
such that
\varphi[X]\subseteq U_{n,a(n)}
.
For every
n\in\omega\setminus T
, the family

\{\,U_{n,m}\cap\varphi[X]:m\in\omega\,\}

is a
\gamma
-cover of
\varphi[X]
. Indeed, since
\varphi[X]\subseteq\mathcal{X}
, for every
g\in\varphi[X]
we have

g\in U_{n,m}
for all but finitely many
m
, and, by the definition of
T
,

U_{n,m}\cap\varphi[X]\neq\varphi[X]
for every
m\in\omega
.

If
\omega\setminus T
is infinite, enumerate it as
\omega\setminus T=\{\,n_{k}:k\in\omega\,\}
.
Since the set
\varphi[X]
is
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathcal{A})
, there are
a(n_{k})\in\omega
for all
k\in\omega
such that

\{\,U_{n_{k},a(n_{k})}\cap\varphi[X]:k\in\omega\,\}\in\mathcal{A}(\varphi[X]).

If
\omega\setminus T
is finite, define
a(n)
arbitrarily for all
n\in\omega\setminus T
.
Thus,
a\in\omega^{\omega}
is defined.

(1) Assume that
\mathcal{A}=\Gamma
.
For each
x\in X
, we have
\varphi(x)\in U_{n,a(n)}
for every
n\in T
and for all but finitely many
n\in\omega\setminus T
.
Consequently,

\{\,a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:x\in X\,\}\subseteq\mathrm{cF}(a).

(2) Assume that
\mathcal{A}=\Omega
.
Let
F\in[X]^{<\omega}
.
If
T
is infinite, then

\{\,(n,a(n)):n\in T\,\}\subseteq a\setminus\bigcup_{x\in F}\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}.

If
T
is finite, then
\omega\setminus T
is infinite and

\{\,U_{n,a(n)}\cap\varphi[X]:n\in\omega\setminus T\,\}\in\Omega(\varphi[X]).

Since every finite subset of a space is contained in infinitely many members of an
\omega
-cover, there are infinitely many
n\in\omega\setminus T
such that
\varphi(x)\in U_{n,a(n)}
for all
x\in F
.
In either case,

\{\,a\setminus\bigcup_{x\in F}\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:F\in[X]^{<\omega}\,\}\subseteq[a]^{\omega}.

(3) Assume that
\mathcal{A}=\Lambda
.
Fix
x\in X
.
If
T
is infinite, then

\{\,(n,a(n)):n\in T\,\}\subseteq a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}.

If
T
is finite, then
\omega\setminus T
is infinite and

\{\,U_{n,a(n)}\cap\varphi[X]:n\in\omega\setminus T\,\}\in\Lambda(\varphi[X]),

and therefore
\varphi(x)\in U_{n,a(n)}
for infinitely many
n\in\omega\setminus T
.
In either case,

\{\,a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:x\in X\,\}\subseteq[a]^{\omega}.

(
\Leftarrow
)
Let
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dotsc\in\Gamma(X)
, where
\mathcal{U}_{n}=\{\,U_{n,m}:m\in\omega\,\}
for all
n
.
By Lemma
7.1
for all natural numbers
n
and
m
there is a continuous function

f_{n,m}\colon X\to\omega+1
such that
X\setminus U_{n,m}=f_{n,m}^{-1}[\{\omega\}]
.
Then the map
\varphi\colon X\to\mathcal{X}
, defined by
\varphi(x)(n,m)\coloneqq f_{n,m}(x)
, is continuous.

(1) There is
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that

\{\,a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:x\in X\,\}\subseteq\mathrm{cF}(a).

Fix
x\in X
.
We have
x\in U_{n,a(n)}
for all but finitely many
n
. Thus,

\{\,U_{n,a(n)}:n\in\omega\,\}\in\Gamma(X).

(2) There is
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that

\{\,a\setminus\bigcup_{x\in F}\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:F\in[X]^{<\omega}\,\}\subseteq[a]^{\omega}.

Let
F
be a finite subset of
X
.
Then
a\setminus\bigcup_{x\in F}\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}
is an infinite subset of
a
.
Thus, there is
n
such that
\varphi(x)(n,a(n))<\omega
for all
x\in F
, and hence
F\subseteq U_{n,a(n)}
.
It follows that

\{\,U_{n,a(n)}:n\in\omega\,\}\in\Omega(X).

(3) There is
a\in{\omega^{\omega}}
such that

\{\,a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}:x\in X\,\}\subseteq[a]^{\omega}.

Fix
x\in X
.
Then
a\setminus\varphi(x)^{-1}\{\omega\}
is an infinite subset of
a
, and thus there are infinitely many
n
such that
\varphi(x)(n,a(n))<\omega
.
We have
x\in U_{n,a(n)}
for infinitely many
n
.
It follows that

\{\,U_{n,a(n)}:n\in\omega\,\}\in\Lambda(X).\qed

7.2.
Properties
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
and
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{O})}

The following fact is an easy and useful observation about spaces all of whose finite powers satisfy
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)}
.
Surprisingly, it is not mentioned in the literature on combinatorial covering properties.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
X
be a space. The space
X^{k}
satisfies
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{O})}
for each natural number
k\geq 1
if and only if
X^{k}
satisfies
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)}
for each natural number
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove that if a space
X^{k}
is
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{O})}
for each natural number
k\geq 1
, then it is
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
.
Let
\mathcal{U}_{0},\allowbreak\mathcal{U}_{1},\allowbreak\dotsc\in\Gamma(X)
. Let us reindex this sequence as
\mathcal{U}_{k,m}
where
k\geq 1
and
m
are natural numbers.
Let
\mathcal{U}_{k,m}^{\prime}:=\{\,U^{k}:U\in\mathcal{U}_{k,m}\,\}
. Then for all natural numbers
k\geq 1
and
m
,
\mathcal{U}_{k,m}^{\prime}\in\Gamma(X^{k})
.
Since
X^{k}
is
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\mathrm{O})}
for each natural
k\geq 1
, it follows that for each natural number
k\geq 1
, there exist sets
U_{k,m}\in\mathcal{U}_{k,m},m\in\omega
such that

{\left\{\,U_{k,m}^{k}:m\in\omega\,\right\}}
is a cover of
X^{k}
.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}:={\left\{\,U_{k,m}:k,m\in\omega\,\right\}}
. Then
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
is an
\omega
-cover of
X
.
Indeed, take any finite
F=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k-1}\}\subseteq X
. Then there exists
m\in\omega
such that
(x_{0},x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k-1})\in U_{k,m}^{k}
.
Thus,
F\subseteq U_{k,m}\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
.
∎

This fact allows us to easily prove a more basic version of Theorem
2.2
(2) where we take
Y
to be a one-point space.

7.3.
Michael topology

Note that all results in which the space is endowed with the Michael topology hold when we consider any topology in which the neighborhoods of
\mathrm{Fin}
are the same as in the standard topology on
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
.

Let
C
be the standard middle-third Cantor set in the real line, and let
C_{\mathrm{S}}
denote the set
C
endowed with the topology induced by the Sorgenfrey line
[
21
]
.
Using the standard homeomorphism between
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
and
C
, we introduce the Sorgenfrey topology on
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
and denote the resulting space by
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}_{\mathrm{S}}
.
Since the points of
\mathrm{Fin}
correspond to the left endpoints of the intervals appearing in the construction of
C
, their open neighborhoods in
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}
,
{{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}_{\mathrm{M}}}
, and
{\operatorname{P}(\omega)}_{\mathrm{S}}
are the same as in the usual real-line topology.
Moreover, for every
X\subseteq{[\omega]^{\omega}}
, the Michael topology on
X\cup\mathrm{Fin}
is finer than the Sorgenfrey topology.
Thus, the identity map from
{(X\cup\mathrm{Fin})_{\mathrm{M}}}
to
(X\cup\mathrm{Fin})_{\mathrm{S}}
is continuous.
Consequently, all results above for the Michael topology transfer to the Sorgenfrey topology, since the covering properties under consideration are preserved by continuous images.
For example, one can prove the following result.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal number with
\kappa\leq\mathfrak{d}
, let
X
be a
\kappa
-fin-unbounded set, let
k
be a natural number, and let
Y
be a set satisfying
{\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{Bor}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{Bor}})}
. Then the space
(X\cup\mathrm{Fin})_{\mathrm{S}}^{k}\times Y
satisfies
\mathsf{S}_{1}(\Gamma,\Omega)
.

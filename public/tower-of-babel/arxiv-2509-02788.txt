Some Maker-Breaker games on graphs/hypergraphs
Patrick Bennett, Alan Frieze, Wesley Pegden

Abstract
We consider some biased Maker-Breaker games. Starting with the complete k k -uniform hypergraph on n n vertices, at each turn Maker claims one edge, and then Breaker claims b b edges. Maker’s goal is to obtain a set of edges having some increasing property. We consider the case where Maker’s desired property is minimum degree m m , or having a full-rank incidence matrix, or having a Berge Hamilton cycle or having a perfect matching or a Hamilton ℓ \ell -cycle.

Introduction

In this paper we consider some Maker-Breaker games played on the edges of the complete
k
-uniform hypergraph
H=H_{n,k}
, sometimes referred to as the
board
. There are two players, Maker and Breaker, who take turns in acquiring the edges of
H
. In one turn Maker acquires one edge and in one turn Breaker acquires
b

. Maker’s aim is to acquire a copy of some particular class of hypergraph and Breaker’s aim is to prevent this. There will be a value
b^{*}
, which we call the
threshold bias
, such that Maker wins if
b\leq b^{*}
and Breaker wins if
b>b^{*}
. We discuss estimates for
b^{*}
for a variety of games.

The study of biased Maker-Breaker games begins with Chvátal and Erdős
[
3
]
. There are many results in this area and the book by Hefetz, Krivelevich, Stojaković and Szabó
[
7
]
provides a very nice introduction to the area.

We first consider the Minimum Degree Game, which is one of the most fundamental of games. Here Maker’s goal is to build a hypergraph with minimum degree
m
. (The degree of a vertex

is the number of edges that contain

[
6
]
asymptotically estimated the threshold bias. We generalize their result to

Theorem 1
.

Suppose first that
m=o(\log n)
. Let
b=\frac{\beta N}{\log n}
where

. Then for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, Maker wins the Minimum Degree Game (i.e. Maker gets minimum degree

) if

, and Breaker wins if
\beta\geq 1+\varepsilon
. In general, if
m=\Omega(\log n)
, we only claim that Maker wins if
b\leq\frac{\gamma N}{km+\log n}
for
\gamma<1
.

Next we consider the
Matrix Rank Game
. We use the same board

but we view it as an

over the boolean field

gives rise to a column

and a 0 everywhere else. We view the set of

of “full rank”, i.e.

Theorem 2
.

Let

. Then for any fixed

\beta\geq 1+\varepsilon

Next we consider the
Berge Hamiltonicity Game

(v_{1},e_{1},v_{2},e_{2},\ldots,v_{n-1},e_{n},v_{n})

Theorem 3
.

Let
b=\frac{\beta N}{\log n}
. Then for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, Maker wins the BHC Game if
\beta\leq 1-\varepsilon
and Breaker wins if
\beta\geq 1+\varepsilon

Our next theorem concerns the Hamilton

-cycle Game in

whose union is

Theorem 4

Fix

with

. Let

. Then, Maker wins the Hamilton

-cycle Game if

The lower bound on the threshold bias is rather weak. For an upper bound we can take

2
Minimum Degree Game

2.1
Breaker strategy

Suppose that
b\geq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)N}{\log n}
. We will show that Breaker can take every edge incident with some particular vertex, which means that Breaker actually wins all of the games listed in Theorems

3
. Breaker’s strategy here will have two phases.

Breaker’s goal in Phase 1 is to build a set

s:=n/\log n

S
are incident with any of Maker’s edges, and Breaker has taken every edge which is incident with two vertices of
S
. We follow a strategy based on that of Chvátal and Erdős
[
3
]
for graphs.

To analyze Phase 1, suppose Breaker has some current set
S
with the required properties (in the beginning
S
is empty). We will show that Breaker can increase the size of
S
by one each turn. Assuming that Breaker has succeeded so far in growing
S
by one vertex at each turn, but

, there have only been
|S|=o(n)
turns so far and so Maker has only touched

that are untouched by Maker. Breaker takes every edge containing

and
y

and another vertex from
S
. Breaker can do all of this in one turn since

b>(1+2|S|)\binom{n}{k-2}
. Breaker then adds
x,y
to
S
. On Maker’s turn, Maker can touch at most one vertex from
S
, and so playing by this strategy Breaker can keep growing
S
by one vertex each turn. Thus, Phase 1 succeeds.

In Phase 2, Breaker’s goal is to take every remaining edge from some vertex in
S
. Phase 2 will be an instance of the Box Game of Chvátal and Erdős
[
3
]
. In the Box Game, we have
x
boxes, each containing
y
balls. Two players, BoxMaker and BoxBreaker, take turns where BoxMaker takes
b
balls per turn and BoxBreaker only takes one ball. BoxMaker wins if they take every ball in some box. Theorem 2.1 in
[
3
]
implies that BoxMaker wins if

. For our present Minimum Degree Game, Breaker will play the role of BoxMaker. For each vertex
v\in S
we have a “box” consisting of all the edges containing
v
, so each box has at most
N
elements. Since
b\geq N/\log s\sim N/\log n
, Breaker wins.

-expander
if for all disjoint sets of vertices

and

|Y|<\alpha|X|
and
|X|\leq r
, then there is an edge,

|e\cap X|=1
and
e\cap Y=\emptyset

Definition 2

For a hypergraph

is an edge such that either
{\mathcal{H}}\cup e
has a longer (Berge) path than
{\mathcal{H}}
or
{\mathcal{H}}\cup e
is (Berge) Hamiltonian.

TwinGuard-Sec: a federated blockchain-enabled AI framework for standardized security and privacy in cross-domain digital twin ecosystems over 6G
Authors: Mrim M Alnfiai, Reemiah Muneer Alotaibi, Faiz Abdullah Alotaibi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe fast rate of cross-domain Digital Twins (DT) ecosystem growth in 6G-based scenarios poses unresolved security and privacy issues into the scope of existing frameworks. This study examines the inherent constraints of existing methods and presents TwinGuard-Sec, a new federated blockchain-based AI system expressly aimed at providing a set of standardized security and privacy of data in a heterogeneous realm of DT. The methodology comprises a dual-layered systematic architectural framework, comprising an AI-governed threat intelligence unit and zero-knowledge identity verifications and a distributed ledger technology layer that is domain-interoperable with lightweight consensus algorithms ensuring synchronous operation in real time. The framework fills three essential gaps in research including: absence of standardized cross-domain security protocols, inadequate privacy preserving mechanisms applied to sensitive inter-organizational data sharing and lack of scalable consensus algorithms to be used in DT-specific needs. We show on the rigorous test of a comprehensive 6G virtual twin testbed that includes 50 distributed nodes and five application domains (smart mobility, e-health, industrial IoT, smart cities, and autonomous systems) that the performance is significantly improved: 27.4% increase in threat detection accuracy (reaching 95.0% vs. 76.4% base) can be improved, better privacy preservation with a differential privacy parameter = 0.94 (62% improvement), 21.2% reduction in latency down to 147 ms. The system achieves Precision = 0.968, Recall = 0.959, and F1-score = 0.963 (macro-average), with AUC-ROC = 0.989 across eight attack categories. These results confirm that TwinGuard-Sec is an innovative means of ensuring the safety of cross-domain DT coordination, equipping it with both theoretical frameworks and implementation channels of the next generation intelligent infrastructure systems.Keywords: Digital twins, Federated learning, Blockchain, 6G networks, Cross-domain security, Privacy preservation, IoT security, Zero-knowledge proofsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The advanced Digital Twin (DT) technologies that have spread across various fields have at last really altered the ways we model, monitor, and control complicated cyber-physical systems in the sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks. As shown in Fig. 1, cross-domain digital twin ecosystems through applications such as Smart Factory, Intelligent healthcare, Connected Vehicles, and Smart Grids have three main challenges: the inability to support interoperability across heterogeneous systems, distributed architecture security vulnerability, and privacy issues in data sharing across domains. The joint occurrence of these problems in 6G-enabled settings necessitates holistic solutions that can both meet security needs, privacy protection, and provide a consistent cross-domain interoperability across various application-based digital twin ecosystems and has resulted in the proposal of a TwinGuard-Sec framework as an integrated federated blockchain-based AI system that meets security and privacy needs and facilitates interoperability uniformly across domains.

Overview of cross-domain digital twin ecosystem challenges and the proposed TwinGuard-Sec framework addressing security, privacy, and interoperability requirements across 6G-enabled environments.

Recent developments in federated learning of IoT settings have shown a great potential of balancing data privacy and collaborative learning between distributed systems1. Decentralizing blockchain-based federated learning systems have become one of the potential solutions to reliable adaptation of Internet of Things ecosystems with particular focus on the cross-domain FL workflows across organizational boundaries. Nevertheless, the current solutions do not consider the specific security needs of DT ecosystems which use 6G infrastructure. The introduction of metaverse technologies has only increased the necessity of the synergistic combination of semantic communication, 6G networks, and edge learning functions2. Studies demonstrate that blockchain-based interoperability is highly important in supporting transparent and secure data interactions among heterogeneous DT networks.

Moreover, overall surveys concerning digital twin networks have outlined limitation of architecture, technological disconnect and open questions that specifically arise in the context of security and privacy in vehicular digital twin applications3. Recent federated learning studies have also examined the uses and issues of smart building space, including how digital twins and 5G/6G networks may be used to support cross-domain fault and privacy-sensitive analytics4.

Recent advances in 6G security architectures have explored privacy-preserving mechanisms for cross-domain IoT. Li et al. (IEEE IoT Journal, 2024) proposed a privacy framework achieving ε = 1.32 differential privacy with 178 ms latency, though lacking blockchain integrity and adaptive threat detection. Zhang et al. (IEEE TNSM, 2023) presented intelligent security architecture with edge intelligence and blockchain, achieving 88.4% detection accuracy but without zero-knowledge authentication (7.8 bits leakage vs. 3.2 in TwinGuard-Sec) and using standard PBFT consensus (223 ms vs. 147 ms). TwinGuard-Sec advances these works by integrating AI-governed threat intelligence, privacy-preserving federated learning, zero-knowledge proofs, and optimized consensus achieving 95.0% detection accuracy, ε = 0.94 privacy, and 147 ms latency with full ISO/IEC 27010 and 3GPP compliance.

When used with 6G and IoT technologies, there are rare opportunities to deploy edge intelligence, but a notable difficulty lies in the promotion of interoperability between cross-domain systems with the strong security posture5. Federated learning architectures have achieved recent advancements that center around privacy-aware AI specifications grounded on lightweight efficient and secure cloud-edge-end collaboration designs6. Digital twin technologies are implemented into these architectures to verify update or guaranteeing protection against cross-domain data transmission, but scalability remains to be an issue in heterogeneous edge protocols. Privacy-preserving computing, digital twins, and 5G/6G communications have been named the enabling technologies here as fundamental to cross platform and cross-domain data integration through the industrial metaverse paradigm7. Quantum-resistant frameworks [10.1038/s41598-025-21648-5] achieve 91.7% with post-quantum crypto. Multi-sensor auth [10.3390/s24186143] provides 94.3% biometric accuracy. Healthcare privacy [10.1007/s11277-024-11192-2] uses homomorphic encryption (847 ms).

The reviews of digital twin data management have clearly highlighted that data are in the middle of digital twin ecosystems and that integration frameworks with the capability to align cross-domain data are required8. Cloud-dominated and blockchain-enabled solutions have been demonstrated to solve these issues of integration, albeit there is still much work on standardization. The development of blockchain uses in smart city has been researched on cross-domain authentication schemes and the application of drone coordination with blockchains and with the use of federated learning principles9.

Scientometric evaluation of applications of digital twin in sustainable development has found tendencies toward cross domain integration and human-AI co-operations, specifically, layered Digital Twin Learning Ecosystems contributing to blockchain-facilitated federated learning competencies10. Adaptive threat detection and decision-making based on AI use in the blockchain-enabled smart ecosystems is an essential research area today that bears directly on the security posture of cross-domain DT networks.

In spite of these developments, present solutions have a host of critical issues including: (1) inexistence of unified security frameworks that complete cross-domain DT coordination, (2) inadequate privacy preservation apparatuses to offer sensitive data sharing across the organizational barriers, (3) inadequate scalability of the available blockchain consensus algorithms to synchronize massive real-time DT and (4) non-existence of adaptive mechanisms of detecting threats that are specific to the attackers in the context of a DT. Such constraints serve as the reason to create a complete framework that would consider the security, privacy, and standardization needs at the same time.

To overcome these challenges, the paper presents TwinGuard-Sec, which is a new federated blockchain-based AI architecture that will be dedicated to supporting the challenges with standardized security and privacy practices in cross-domain digital twin ecosystems on 6G infrastructure. Our key contributions are: (1) a layered architecture that combines threat intelligence that is controlled by AI as well as domain-interoperable distributed ledger technology, (2) zero-knowledge identity proofs in privacy-preserving cross-domain authentication, (3) lightweight blockchain consensus that are optimized to synchronize distributed ledgers in real-time, and (4) comprehensive evaluations that show that we have a higher rate of accuracy in detection of attacks, faster latency reduction, and compliant with standards.

Core technical novelty

While TwinGuard-Sec integrates multiple established technologies, four components represent fundamental technical innovations: (1) Domain-Adaptive Federated Threat Intelligence introduces domain-weighted gradient aggregation dynamically adjusting learning rates based on cross-domain trust metrics and historical attack patterns, achieving 16.6% higher detection accuracy than standard federated averaging by accounting for domain-specific threat landscapes; (2) Hybrid Zero-Knowledge Cross-Domain Authentication Protocol enables unlinkable authentication across organizational boundaries without centralized identity providers through domain-blind attribute proving, achieving 212% larger anonymity sets than existing approaches; (3) Lightweight Adaptive Consensus for Real-Time DT Synchronization combines reputation-weighted validator selection with domain-based voting optimization, reducing consensus latency by 34.7% while maintaining Byzantine fault tolerance; (4) Cross-Domain Trust-Based Data Sharing Framework computes continuous trust scores with temporal decay and historical reputation analysis, reducing information leakage by 74% compared to traditional role-based access control. Ablation studies demonstrate removing any novel component significantly degrades system performance, confirming substantial advancement beyond technology integration.

Related work

The topography of safe digital twin ecosystems has changed considerably following the advent of 6G networks and interoperability at the cross domain. The current section makes a detailed study of the already available solutions including their strengths and weaknesses with regard to their roles in resolving complex security and privacy issues which are part of federated DT environments.

Blockchain-enabled federated learning in IoT

According to a recent study by Javed et al.1, a thorough framework of blockchain-facilitated federated learning in IoT-based applications was proposed, with the key aspect being credible adaptation and standardization. Their concept utilizes permissioned blockchain systems to support cross-domain FL processes, but the small scale is evident when used in large scale DT ecosystems. The piece of work creates significant underpinnings towards secure FL deployment but does not provide the real-time responsiveness that 6G supported DT synchronisation needs.

Aloudat et al.2 investigated the synergistic learning of semantic communication, 6G networks, and edge learning, in metaverse spaces. Their questionnaire identifies blockchain-enabled interoperability as one of the key enablers to transparent cross-domain operations, but the mechanisms mentioned do not put particular focus on the security needs of industrial DT applications. The study offers useful information on 6G integration issues but does not give many solutions to cross-domain privacy protection.

Digital twin network security and privacy

Pan et al.3 have carried out a comprehensive survey of digital twin networks, uncovering the key architecture component, technologies, and applications as well as noting severe open future problems in security and privacy. Their study shows that there are significant vulnerabilities in car digital twin security and cross-domain interaction protocols. The survey underscores the importance of strong frameworks that deal with any unsolicited exposure of data in cross-domain interactions, which is the direct cause of creating TwinGuard-Sec.

Berkani et al.4 analyzed the application of federated learning to smart building environment and proved that cross-domain fault detection via Fed-CNN models contacting with digital twins and 5G/6G networks is possible. Although their methodology has potential in creating automation, their security measures are not at advanced levels to coordinate multi-domain DT on a heterogeneous infrastructure.

Edge intelligence and cross-domain integration

He et al.5 examined the implementation of the IoT and 6G in edge intelligence use cases, discovering the most important issues in facilitating cross-domain cross-system cooperation. Their article has pointed to the development of digital twins beyond mere virtualization to intricate cross-domain collaborative models. Nevertheless, the suggested solutions do not cover the security implications of cross-domain data sharing sufficiently and do not provide the standardized privacy protection measures.

Zhan et al.6 provided a review of federated learning architecture with an excellent focus on privacy-aware AI by using lightweight cloud-edge-end cooperation. Their framework includes digital twin technologies to check updates and utilize cross-domain data transmission protection, and still, the problems of scaling remain present over heterogeneous edge ecosystems. The study gives architectural significant findings but lacks on important real-time security needs.

Lightweight federated learning architectures for edge-cloud collaboration (IEEE Access, 2022, 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3161132) propose hierarchical aggregation reducing communication overhead through gradient compression, achieving 82.6% accuracy with 40% cost reduction. While effective for bandwidth-constrained edges, this approach focuses on single-domain deployments without cross-organizational coordination, relies on gradient sparsification rather than formal differential privacy, lacks blockchain audit mechanisms, and employs statistical outlier analysis rather than adaptive AI-driven threat intelligence. TwinGuard-Sec extends these concepts by integrating differential privacy (ε = 0.94), blockchain immutability, and AI-governed detection (95.0% accuracy) while maintaining comparable efficiency through optimized consensus (147 ms latency).

Industrial metaverse and cross-platform integration

Zhang et al.7 reviewed facilitating technologies to industrial metaverse applications that include privacy-preserving computing, digital twins, and 5G/6G communication as the basic unit of integration of cross platform data and cross domain data. They display significant vulnerabilities of the universal security architectures, indicating the necessity of efficient privacy protection systems in multi-domain settings. Ouedraogo et al.8 present a review of the data management in digital twins, underlining the central role of data in the ecosystems of the digital twins and the need to integrate the models that would facilitate the harmonization of cross-domain data sources. Cloud-based and blockchain-enabled solutions are identified as promising solutions in their piece of work, but standardization is an important issue that demands novel solutions.

Recent reports further defined the interplay of blockchain, artificial intelligence and 6G networks as enabling systems to support secure and intelligent cross-domain ecosystems. The article by Liu et al.11 suggested an AI-based blockchain architecture which guarantees effective orchestration of trust and end-to-end security over 6G-enabled spaces. Cui et al.12 have given a comprehensive description of AI and communication integration of 6G systems, determining distributed intelligence, semantic learning, and privacy-preserving mechanisms as being a key to sustainable interoperability. In the 6G wireless communications article, Zuo et al.13 provide an extensive overview of the research on blockchain-AI fusion with a specific focus on decentralized intelligence in an attempt to form an autonomous decision-making in a heterogeneous network. Equally, Huang et al.14 proposed a 6G self-evolution electronic framework that employs a digital twin network system with federated AI and blockchain consensus to attain adaptive and self-secured cross-domain coordination. Overall, these developments justify the design logic of the suggested TwinGuard-Sec framework, which is advocated to integrate AI-governed threat intelligence with blockchain-developed synchronization of universal and privacy of digital twins environments on 6G infrastructures.

Blockchain applications in smart cities

Rejeb et al.9 studied the use of blockchains in smart cities basing on co-word analysis and BERTopic models, including cross-domain authentication in smart parks and blockchain-based drone coordination based on the federated learning framework. Their work power is the possibility of having blockchain-powered coordination but insufficient is the level of security that is necessary when using a blockchain in critical infrastructure. In a study of the digital twins in sustainable development, Bhatia and Kumar10 performed a scientometric study revealing the tendencies to cross-domain integration and human-AI cooperation. Their contribution highlights stratified Digital Twin Learning Ecosystems that augment blockchain enabled federated learning features that deliver valuable knowledge on the implementation of the DT technologies.

Recent advances in ethical AI and adaptive security explore standardization paralleling digital twin concerns. Community-centered protocols for ethical AI in healthcare (Nature Digital Medicine, 2024) establish trustworthy deployment principles including transparency and privacy, though focusing on single-domain clinical settings without cross-organizational federated learning. Stress-adaptive cyber defense frameworks (IEEE TDSC, 2024) employing cognitive-physiological synchronization achieve 86.7% detection by correlating network patterns with system stress, though designed for single-domain IoT without cross-domain threat intelligence sharing. Secure intelligent fuzzy blockchain frameworks (Computers & Security, 2024) achieve 89.4% detection using fuzzy logic with blockchain, though employing PoW consensus with high latency (284 ms) unsuitable for real-time DT synchronization. TwinGuard-Sec synthesizes insights while introducing novel cross-domain coordination mechanisms achieving superior performance (95.0% vs. 89.4%, 147 ms vs. 284 ms) with comprehensive standards alignment.

Research gaps and limitations

Although individual components have been developed quite significantly, the available studies have considerable limitations which include: (1) poor incorporation of security, privacy, and standardization criteria into single frameworks, (2) a lack of scalability in blockchain consensus mechanisms to synchronize real-time DTs, (3) a lack of cross-domain privacy preservation systems specially crafted to handle DT attacks, and (4) the absence of adaptive threat detection systems. Such constraints manifest the necessity of the detailed frameworks, such as TwinGuard-Sec, that could manage a range of issues at once at the same time and be adjusted to the new standards. Recent works on smart city blockchain15, 6G enabling technologies16, metaverse interoperability17, edge-cloud integration18, blockchain-based data sharing19, multimodal IoT communication20, sustainable smart cities21, multi-LLM orchestration22, human activity recognition with IoT23, zero-trust policy management24, intelligent transportation systems25, blockchain-based federated learning for IoV26, AI-based DDoS detection27, distributed cloud privacy28, AI in personalized systems29, manufacturing ecosystem architectures30, PBFT consensus optimization31, decentralized identity verification32 all underscore the pressing need for a unified, standards-compliant, privacy-preserving framework such as TwinGuard-Sec.

The recent benchmark frameworks of 2024–2025 present quantitative information on the optimization of federated, blockchain and AI-driven security. Taj et al.33 suggested a Quantum-Resistant Security Framework in an IoT-enabled Metaverse setting that attains 89.3% threat-detection fidelity and an average privacy budget of and average inference latency of 198 ms. Tang et al.34 proposed an Industrial IoT (LMAAS-IIoT) Large-Model-as-a-Service architecture that achieved 90.7% detection accuracy, , and end-to-end delay of about 203 ms with 15 domain settings. Whereas these findings show advancements in the ability to combine blockchain-based intelligence with scalable edge architecture, both articles also have moderate privacy leakage and low cross-domain interoperability compared to the proposed TwinGuard-Sec model, which works with 95.0% detection rates, , and 147 ms of latency in 20 domains. This quantitative analysis underscores the better trade off with privacy, scalability, and computational efficiency of TwinGuard-Sec which makes it a next-generation standard of secure 6G-aided digital-twin systems.

Proposed methodology

The following section discusses the overall design and deployment of TwinGuard-Sec, which is a federated blockchain-based AI model that can resolve the severe security and privacy risks of cross-domain digital twin ecosystems on 6G networks. The suggested design incorporates the state-of-the-art cryptography protocols, simplified consensus algorithms, as well as, AI-based threat monitoring to guarantee the high security level and, at the same time, be interoperable with diverse fields.

Lightweight blockchain consensus mechanism

The lightweight consensus mechanism of TwinGuard-Sec employs a novel design tailored for real-time DT synchronization. The protocol is a consensus system that is based on both Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance (pBFT) and proof-of-stake concepts, but designed to have an extremely low-latency consensus, as would be found in 6G environments.

Complexity: AI O(n log n + m2), ZK O(C2 + C log C) for C = 12,347, consensus O(n2) optimized to O(n log n). state machine: S = {IDLE,PROPOSE,PREPARE,COMMIT,FINALIZE}. Safety theorem: no two blocks at same round; Liveness: progress with ≥ 2n/3 honest stake.

The lightweight hybrid pBFT-PoS achieves superior real-time performance: standard pBFT (250-400 ms), Raft (150-300 ms but CFT-only), pure PoS (8-15 s), Tendermint (180-350 ms), HotStuff (200-420 ms), Algorand (4.5-8 s). At n = 50 validators, TwinGuard-Sec achieves 147 ms representing 52.9% faster than standard pBFT and 46.6% faster than Tendermint while maintaining Byzantine resilience, instant finality, and high energy efficiency. Experimental validation shows latency scaling: n = 10 (89 ms, 38.6% improvement), n = 25 (134 ms, 32.4%), n = 50 (147 ms, 46.8%), n = 75 (214 ms, 41.7%), n = 100 (289 ms, 40.6%). Critical design choices: reputation-weighted selection, domain-based vote optimization, adaptive timeouts (3 s baseline, 2 s trusted), pipelined consensus, and optimistic execution. The experimental assessment employs a complex 6G-virtual twin testbed which has 50 distributed nodes which are strategically placed in five different application fields namely smart mobility, e-health, industrial IoT, smart cities, and autonomous systems. The domains have 10 DT instances with varying computational capabilities and network connectivity patterns that effectively model the provision of a real-world deployment environment and operational constraints.

It provides 500 + million network flow traces and packet-level traced samples designated by benign and malicious activity, and makes it possible to benchmark intrusion detection on a large scale.

Limitations and bias

Although the proposed TwinGuard-Sec framework is highly accurate, has a high scalability and close to complete compliance to standards, it has a number of limitations and potential biases to be considered with the aim of offering a balanced evaluation towards the current scope and reproducibility of the framework.

Speaking of data, the datasets of attacks utilized whether CICIoT2023, TON-IoT, or IoT-23, are mostly synthetic and semi-realistic. Even though these datasets represent a broad spectrum of typical network abnormalities, they are not representative of the complex adversarial nature of 6G infrastructure (in reality). As an example, an insider threat and adaptive poisoning case was simulated with approximation of 4, 200 labeled events per dataset which can bias the model towards common attack signature. This will be addressed in future as live 6G traffic will be integrated in SmartCity-NYC and Industrial IoT-Lab testbeds to increase diversity (), temporal variability, and attack realism.

Another limitation is that which is imposed by the experimental setup. The federated training was introduced onto an NVIDIA DGX-A100 cluster with an equal ratio of network bandwidth (10 Gbps) and negligible packet loss (less than 0.1%). Although the use of this controlled configuration does provide consistency across trials, it may overestimate the communication delays present in actual heterogeneous 6G deployments. Likewise, consensus mechanism values that use propagation intervals which are constant, in reality, the propagation latency may have more variability due to the presence of link interference, link congestion, and multi-hop routing.

The performance that is observed is also affected by environmental assumptions. Simulations were performed in the frame of static latency distributions and fixed node participation, which mean that the digital twins should be continuously active. Nevertheless, in actual deployments the connectivity is usually intermittent and conditions of loads vary and the device level power management is often adaptively managed. These variables may slightly decrease throughput and stability in relation to the controlled outcomes that CIRDNOC records. Moreover, the synthetic enhancement of telemetry data did not simulate some impairments in the physical-layer, including terahertz path loss, beam misalignment, and multipath fading, which can also aggravate performance in 6G at large scale.

In the future, these limitations will be reduced by a time-stamped progression of extensions. Experiments in the future will combine real-world settings that have a 6G testbed with heterogeneous edges configuration and adjustable bandwidth, use the concept of adversarial data generation in the effort to at least capture unknown forms of attacks, and test asynchronous model updates to model the uneven participation of nodes. To determine the robustness of a longitudinal evaluation based on actual IoT-DT data, the adaptive learning ability, and equity among different node constructions, a longitudinal assessment with actual IoT-DT data will also be carried out. With this in mind, the subsequent phase of TwinGuard-Sec development is to offer a more realistic, entirely generalizable solution and deployment-ready framework of secure cross-domain digital twins ecosystems.

TwinGuard-Sec attains phenomenal performance benefits such as 27.4% improvement in threat detection accuracy, better privacy protection with a differential privacy parameter of and a 21.2% latency decrease without sacrificing its complete ability to meet international standards such as ISO/IEC 27010 and 3GPP security baselines by use of its innovative dual-layered architecture of integrating AI-governed threat intelligence with domain-interoperable distributed ledger technology.

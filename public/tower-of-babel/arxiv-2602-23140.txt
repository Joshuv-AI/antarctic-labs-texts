Modular interpretation of the Weil–Petersson metric asymptotics for abelian varieties
Yanbo Fang, Andres Gomez

Abstract
. As a first step towards a refined description of the asymptotic of the Weil–Petersson metric on the moduli space of polarized Calabi-Yau manifolds we investigate the concrete case of abelian varieties by linking such asymptotic with the multi-scale collapsing limits of the parametrized flat tori, as explicitly classified by Odaka.



1.
Introduction

Motivated by the problem of studying the possible
geometric
compactifications of the moduli space of polarized Calabi–Yau manifolds, we explore the relations between the collapsing of the Ricci flat Kähler metrics along degenerating families of polarized Calabi-Yau manifolds and the asymptotic behavior of the Weil–Petersson metric at infinity on their moduli space. In this paper, we first make progress on this question by investigating the concrete case of principally polarized abelian varieties.

In order to justify our approach, let us elaborate on what we mean by a
geometric compactification
. By Yau’s celebrated solution of the Calabi conjecture, for any compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
with
c_{1}(X)=0
there exists a
unique
Ricci-flat Kähler metric
\omega_{\scriptscriptstyle{CY}}
in the Kähler class
[\omega]
. Therefore, given a polarized degenerating family of Calabi-Yau manifolds
(\mathcal{X}^{*},\mathcal{L}^{*})\rightarrow\Delta^{*}_{t}
, as
t\to 0
we approach the boundary of moduli with a fixed Kähler class and the complex structure degenerates. If we denote by
\omega_{\scriptscriptstyle{CY,t}}
the unique Ricci-flat Kähler metric on
X_{t}
in the class
2\pi c_{1}(\mathcal{L}|_{X_{t}})
, we say that a compactification is
geometric
if the boundary of the compactified moduli space parametrizes the metric limit of
\omega_{\scriptscriptstyle{CY,t}}
as
t\to 0
. In the concrete case of principally polarized abelian varieties, where
\omega_{\scriptscriptstyle{CY}}
is a flat Kähler metric on a complex torus, these limits were classified by Odaka in
[
Odaka2019
]
. There he explicitly determines the Gromov–Hausdorff limits of principally polarized abelian varieties and provides a moduli theoretic framework to understand such limits.

Following this perspective, the Weil–Petersson metric is a natural candidate for studying the geometry at infinity on the moduli space of polarized Calabi-Yau manifold because, by definition, the Weil–Petersson metric is induced by the variation of the Ricci-flat Kähler metric on the Kähler class fixed by the polarization as the complex structure varies (see Section
for more details); along this lines, it has been proved by Wang
[
Wang97
]
and Tosatti
[
Tosatti15
]
that on a one-parameter degeneration of polarized Calabi-Yau’s the origin lies at finite Weil–Petersson distance if and only if the central fiber of the degeneration is a Calabi-Yau variety with at worst canonical singularities, which implies that the polarized Ricci-flat Kähler metrics have uniformly bounded diameter and are uniformly volume non-collapsed. In contrast to this set-up, the degenerations we are interested are always at infinite Weil–Petersson distance and the Ricci-flat Kähler metric will collapse, phenomena that is already present for abelian varieties as explicitly classified by Odaka.

The main theorem of this paper is the following result that morally says that the asymptotic behavior of the Weil–Petersson metric on the moduli of principally polarized abelian varieties is encoded in the multi-scale collapsing geometry of the (canonical) flat Kähler metric along degenerating sequences of abelian varieties.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A_{g},\mathcal{g}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{652080}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{652080}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{569828}{229375}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{569828}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle W\!P$}}}}}})
be the moduli space of complex
g
-dimensional principally polarized abelian varieties endowed with its canonical Kähler structure given by the Weil–Petersson metric. Then for every non-degenerate direction at infinity in
A_{g}
, there exists an integer
g^{\prime}
with
0\leq g^{\prime}<g
such that the Weil–Petersson metric converges in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff sense, to a Weil–Petersson type metric on

(1)

(A_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathscr{T}_{g-g^{\prime}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{>0}},\,\mathcal{g}^{\prime}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{652080}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{652080}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{569828}{229375}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{569828}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle W\!P$}}}}}}+\mathcal{g}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1041256}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle trWP$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1041256}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle trWP$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{907753}{229375}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle trWP$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{907753}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle trWP$}}}}}}),

where

•

the moduli space
A_{g^{\prime}}
is naturally identified with a boundary stratum of the constant-volume Gromov–Hausdorff compactification
\bar{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{GH}_{\mathrm{vol}}}
of
A_{g}
, and
\mathcal{g}^{\prime}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{652080}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{652080}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{569828}{229375}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle W\!P$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{569828}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle W\!P$}}}}}}
denotes the usual Weil–Petersson metric on
A_{g^{\prime}}
;

•

\mathscr{T}_{g-g^{\prime}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{>0}}
denotes the moduli space of real
(g-g^{\prime})
-dimensional flat tori of arbitrary positive diameter, which at any fixed diameter
\mathscr{T}_{g-g^{\prime}}^{\,{c}}
is naturally realized as a boundary stratum of the constant-diameter Gromov–Hausdorff compactification
\bar{A}_{g}^{\mathrm{GH}_{\mathrm{diam}}}
of
A_{g}
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{g}^{\prime\prime}_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1041256}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle trWP$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{1041256}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle trWP$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{907753}{229375}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle trWP$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{907753}{229376}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle trWP$}}}}}}
is a “tropical Weil–Petersson type” metric on
\mathscr{T}_{g-g^{\prime}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{>0}}
, defined via the variation of the limiting flat metrics.

A few remarks are in order.

Remark 1.2
.

Moduli spaces like
A_{g}
, that can be realized as locally symmetric spaces, naturally admit several distinct compactification frameworks. Consequently, identifying which of these compactifications carries a modular geometric interpretation is one of the guiding questions of our investigation. Theorem
1.1
suggest that the Weil–Petersson metric can indeed shed light on this problem: as the abelian varieties degenerate, their canonical flat metrics collapse, and this collapse is reflected in the asymptotic geometry of the Weil–Petersson metric.

Remark 1.3
.

The theorem’s formulation requires a precise notion of a
non-degenerate direction at infinity in
A_{g}
, which we give in Definition
. Roughly speaking this says that, given a sequence
\tau_{n}\in A_{g}
after choosing lifts
\tau_{n}=X_{n}+iY_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{g}
contained in a Siegel set, one may assume that
Y_{n}
has exactly
g-g^{\prime}
eigenvalues tending to infinity, while the remaining
g^{\prime}
eigenvalues stay uniformly bounded above and below. Non-degeneracy then amounts to ruling out the possibility that a sub-collection of the diverging eigenvalues separates into different scales, which would force an additional splitting and hence a lower-rank limit. (Cf. Theorem 3.1 in
[
Odaka2019
]
for an algebraic characterization of this condition).

Remark 1.4
.

One immediate consequence of Theorem
1.1
is that along any such non-degenerate direction,
A_{g}
collapses in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff sense to the lower–dimensional space
A_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathscr{T}_{g-g^{\prime}}^{\scriptscriptstyle{>0}}
. From a Riemannian viewpoint, this behavior is expected given that it is classical that
A_{g}
is a complete non-compact Riemannian orbifold with finite volume.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In §2, we introduce the necessary preliminary results and fix the notation and conventions used throughout. We first recall, in §2.1, the basic theory of moduli of complex abelian varieties and fix conventions for the Siegel upper half space together with the relevant reduction theory. Next, in §2.2, in the setting of the Siegel upper half-space, we recall the Lie theoretic notion of
boundary component
for a Hermitian symmetric domain and the algebraic and differential-geometric data naturally associated with it. Next, in §2.3 we revisit the work of
[
Odaka2019
,
OO2021
]
on the Gromov–Hausdorff compactifications of
A_{g}
. The core of the paper is §3. In §3.1 we recall the definition of the Weil–Petersson metric and the standard derivation of its explicit formula on
A_{g}
, which serves as the starting point for our analysis in the remainder of the paper. Finally In §3.1 we prove the main theorem after developing the necessary technical preliminaries.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties.

In this section we recall some classical notions about complex abelian varieties and set up our notation on the Siegel upper half space and the Siegel reduction theory, we follow closely
[
Chai84
]
. For a detailed treatment and all of the proofs, we refer to
[
Namikawa80
,
Hulek1993
]
.

Recall that
A_{g}
, the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties of complex dimension
g
, is given the structure of complex analytic space by means of the identification

A_{g}\simeq\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g},

where
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
is the
Siegel upper half-space
, namely
\mathfrak{S}_{g}=\{\tau\in\mathrm{Sym}(g,{\mathbb{C}})|\,\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0\}
and
\Gamma=\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})/\{\pm 1\}
is an arithmetic subgroup of the group
\mathrm{Sp}(2,\mathbb{R})/\{\pm 1\}
of holomorphic automorphism of
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
, whose action is given by

M=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}:\tau\to M\cdot\tau=(A\tau+B)(C\tau+D)^{-1},\quad\tau\in\mathfrak{S}_{g},M\in\mathcal{G}.

Notice that, as an analytic space,
A_{g}
has the structure of a complex orbifold, or V-manifold, due to the presence of abelian varieties with extra automorphism.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Gamma(n)
denote the principal congruence subgroup of level
n
,

(2)

\Gamma(n):=\{\,M\in\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{Z})\mid M\equiv I_{2g}\ (\mathrm{mod}\ n)\,\}.

We define the moduli space of complex
g
-dimensional principally polarized abelian varieties with level-
n
structure as the quotient

(3)

A_{g}(n):=\Gamma(n)\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}.

For
n\geq 3
, the group
\Gamma(n)
is torsion-free; hence the action on
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
is free and properly discontinuous, and
A_{g}(n)
is a smooth complex manifold. Equivalently, the finite cover
A_{g}(n)\to A_{g}
provides uniformizing charts near the orbifold points of
A_{g}
.

We now recall some basic notions from Siegel reduction theory that will be used throughout. For a point
\tau=X+iY\in\mathfrak{S}_{g}
we denote the
Jacobi decomposition
of
Y
as
Y=LDL^{t}
where

L=\begin{pmatrix}1&&&&\\
l_{12}&1&&\text{\Large{0}}&\\
l_{13}&l_{23}&1&&\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
l_{1g}&l_{2g}&\cdots&l_{g-1,g}&1\end{pmatrix},\quad D=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},\cdots,d_{g}).

With respect to this decomposition we have that a Siegel sets
\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)
is be given by

(4)

\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)=\{X+i\,Y\in\mathfrak{S}_{g}\mid|x_{ij}|<u,|l_{ij}|<u,\,1<ud_{1},\,d_{i}<ud_{i+1}\text{ for all }i,j\}.

Additionally, it is straightforward to check that a subset of the inequalities in (
4
) define the Siegel sets on
\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g,\mathbb{R})
for the action of the discrete subgroup
\mathrm{GL}(g,\mathbb{Z})
. More specifically we have that with respect to the Jacobi decomposition
Y=LDL^{t}
for a point
Y\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g,\mathbb{R})
a Siegel set
\mathfrak{F}_{g}^{+}(u)
is given by

(5)

\mathfrak{F}^{+}_{g}(u)=\{Y\in\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g,\mathbb{R})\mid|l_{ij}|<u,\,1<ud_{1},\,d_{i}<ud_{i+1}\text{ for all }i,j\}.

Expression that will be needed later in our analysis.

Finally let us recall that for a suitable choice of
u\gg 1
the Siegel set
\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)
is a
coarse fundamental domain
for the action of
\Gamma
in the sense that they satisfy the following properties:

i.

\bigcup_{u>0}\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)=\mathfrak{S}_{g}
.

ii.

\Gamma\cdot\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)=\mathfrak{S}_{g}
if
u
is large enough.

iii.

For all
u>0
the set
\{\gamma\in\Gamma\mid\gamma\,\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)\cap\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)\neq\varnothing\}
is finite.

Analogously,
\mathfrak{F}_{g}^{+}(u)
will be a coarse fundamental domain for the action of
\mathrm{GL}(g,\mathbb{Z})
.

2.2.
Boundary components of
\mathfrak{S}_{g}

In this subsection we introduce the Lie-theoretic concepts underlying the notion of boundary component for a Hermitian symmetric domain. Our main goal is to describe the boundary components of the Siegel upper half space and the
algebraic data naturally attached to them. As this material is technical and notation-heavy, we do not provide a comprehensive exposition but introduce only the necessary notions and results that we will need in later sections. For a detailed treatment and all of the proofs, we refer to
[
Namikawa80
]
.

Recall that as a Hermitian symmetric space, the Siegel upper half-space is given by
\mathfrak{S}_{g}=Sp(2g,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{U}(g)
. Here, the unitary group
\mathrm{U}(g)
, which is the stabilizer of the basepoint
iI_{g}\in\mathfrak{S}_{g}
, is embedded in
\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{R})
by
A+iB\to\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
-B&A\end{pmatrix}
. The Harish-Chandra embedding

\Phi:\mathfrak{S}_{g}\to\mathfrak{D}_{g},\quad\Phi(\tau)=(\tau-iI_{g})(\tau+iI_{g})^{-1},

gives a realization of
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
as a bounded domain
\mathfrak{D}_{g}
in
\mathrm{Sym}_{g}({\mathbb{C}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{g(g+1)/2}
, where

\mathfrak{D}_{g}=\{Z\in\mathrm{Sym}_{g}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,I_{g}-Z\bar{Z}>0\}.

The boundary components of
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
are defined as an stratification of the topological closure
\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{g}=\{Z\in\mathrm{Sym}_{g}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,I_{g}-Z\bar{Z}\geq 0\}
. A distinguished collection of proper boundary components is obtained by fixing
0\leq g^{\prime}<g
and requiring

\bigg\{Z\in\overline{\mathfrak{D}}_{g}\,\bigg|\,Z=\begin{pmatrix}Z^{\prime}&0\\
0&I_{g-g^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix},\text{ and }Z^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{D}_{g^{\prime}}\bigg\}.

Furthermore, each boundary component is a
\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{R})
-translate of one of the
distinguished
boundary components. It is clear that each
distinguished
boundary component can be identified with
\mathfrak{D}_{g^{\prime}}
for
0\leq g^{\prime}<g
, and by abuse of notation we identify them with their corresponding unbounded realization
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
.

Associated with each boundary component
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
we have its normalizer subgroup
P_{g^{\prime}}
in
\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{R})
. Each
P_{g^{\prime}}
is a maximal real parabolic subgroup and can be realized as the normalizer of the isotropic-co-isotropic flag
I\subset I^{\perp}
, where
I=\langle e_{g^{\prime}+1},\dots,e_{g}\rangle
. Let us fix
g=g^{\prime}+g^{\prime\prime}
throughout, a direct computation will show that
P_{g^{\prime}}
consist of matrices

P_{g^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A^{\prime}&0&B^{\prime}&*\\
*&u&*&*\\
C^{\prime}&0&D^{\prime}&*\\
0&0&0&(u^{t})^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\!\in\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{R})\,\middle|\begin{pmatrix}A^{\prime}&B^{\prime}\\
C^{\prime}&D^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\!\in\mathrm{Sp}(2g^{\prime},\mathbb{R}),\,u\in\mathrm{GL}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})\right\}.

Moreover, relative to the filtration
I\subset I^{\perp}
we have the Levi decomposition
P_{g^{\prime}}=N_{g^{\prime}}\rtimes L_{g^{\prime}}
, where
L_{g^{\prime}}
is the Levi factor given by the direct product
L=\mathrm{Sp}(I^{\perp}/I)\,\mathrm{GL}(I)=\mathrm{Sp}(2g^{\prime},\mathbb{R})\,\mathrm{GL}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})
, and
N_{g^{\prime}}
is the unipotent radical of
P_{g^{\prime}}
which is given in terms of the matrices

N_{g^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I_{g^{\prime}}&0&0&n\\
m^{t}&I_{g^{\prime\prime}}&n^{t}&b\\
0&0&I_{g^{\prime}}&-m\\
0&0&0&I_{g^{\prime\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\,\middle|\,n^{t}m+b=m^{t}n+b^{t}\right\}.

Finally, following standard terminology we will reefer to
L_{g^{\prime}h}=\mathrm{Sp}(2g^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
as the Hermitian factor and to
L_{g^{\prime}l}=\mathrm{GL}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})
as the linear factor.

From a more geometric perspective it is worth noting that, as differentiable manifolds, the decomposition
P_{g^{\prime}}\cong N_{g^{\prime}}\,L_{g^{\prime}h}\,L_{g^{\prime}l}
is a diffeomorphism. Furthermore, this decomposition gives the existence of a diffeomorphism

(6)

\mathfrak{S}_{g}\cong P_{g^{\prime}}/(\mathrm{U}(g)\cap P_{g^{\prime}})\cong N_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R}),

which we call the
horospherical
decomposition of
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
relative to
P_{g^{\prime}}
. To see this we first need to recall that
P_{g^{\prime}}
acts transitively on
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
and that the Levi factor is stable under the Cartan involution corresponding to
\mathrm{U}(g)
. Then we observe that

\mathrm{U}(g)\cap P_{g^{\prime}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}A^{\prime}&0&B^{\prime}&0\\
0&u&0&0\\
-B^{\prime}&0&A^{\prime}&0\\
0&0&0&u\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{Sp}(2g,\mathbb{R})\,\middle|\,A^{\prime}+iB^{\prime}\in\mathrm{U}(g^{\prime}),\,u\in\mathrm{O}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})\right\},

which identifies the boundary component
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
with the symmetric space of
L_{g^{\prime}h}=\mathrm{Sp}(2g^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
and the cone
\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})
with the symmetric space of
L_{g^{\prime}l}=\mathrm{GL}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})
. Finally, with respect to this decomposition we define the map

(7)

\pi_{g^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{S}_{g}\to\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}\!\times\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R}),\,\tau=\!\begin{pmatrix}\tau^{\prime}&\tau^{\prime\prime\prime}\\
{}^{t}\tau^{\prime\prime\prime}&\tau^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}\longmapsto(\tau^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime}-{}^{t}Y^{\prime\prime\prime}(Y^{\prime})^{-1}Y^{\prime\prime\prime}).

The geometric significance of the decomposition of Equation (
6
) is that it defines a coordinate system of the quotient
\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}
adapted to the boundary component
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
. To see this we have the following result that essentially tell us that for a
suitable neighborhood
of
\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}
the action of
\Gamma
is equivalent to the action of
P_{g^{\prime}}\cap\Gamma
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Fix
0\leq g^{\prime}<g
, and let
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}:=\Gamma\cap\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}
. Then, there exist a
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}
-invariant neighborhood
\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
such that the inclusion
\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{g}
induces a holomorphic embedding

\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}.

Proof.

The existence of such a
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}
-invariant neighborhood is a consequence of the Piatetskii-Shapiro cylindrical topology (see
[
AMRT
]
, Chap. III, §1.).
∎

In practice, we can described such invariant neighborhood in terms of the Siegel sets as follows. Fix a
u>0
such that
(\Gamma\cap L_{g^{\prime}h})\cdot\mathfrak{F}_{g^{\prime}}(u)=\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
, for all
1\leq g^{\prime}\leq g
and for
r>0
sufficiently large, set

(8)

\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}^{r}:=\left\{\tau\in\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)\,|\,d_{g^{\prime}+1}>r\right\}.

This will define a family of
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}
-invariant neighborhoods of
\mathfrak{F}_{g^{\prime}}(u)\subset\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
.
Moreover, we notice that if we have a sequence
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)
we conclude that
\tau_{n}\to\tau^{\prime}
, for some
\tau^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}_{g^{\prime}}(u)
if and only if

\pi_{g^{\prime}}(\tau_{n})\to\tau^{\prime}
and
d_{g^{\prime}+1}(\tau_{n})\to\infty
.

The relevance of this result to our analysis is that to study the asymptotic behavior of the Weil–Petersson metric near the boundary components of
\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}
we can instead work on the neighborhoods
\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}
modulo the action of the simpler subgroup
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}
. Before we proceed, let us introduce the following notation that we will use throughout the paper.

Notation:
Fix
0\leq g^{\prime}<g
, and let
P_{g^{\prime}}
be the parabolic subgroup associated with the
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}
boundary component of
\mathfrak{S}_{g}
. Then relative to the arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma
we denote by

\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}=\Gamma\cap P_{g^{\prime}},\quad\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}:=\Gamma\cap N_{g},\quad\mathtt{L}_{g^{\prime}}=\Gamma\cap L_{g^{\prime}}.

Moreover, for any congruence subgroup
\Gamma(n)
we have the analogous notation, i.e.,
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}(n),\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}(n)
, and
\mathtt{L}_{g^{\prime}}(n)
.

To understand the quotient
\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}
, notice that
N_{g^{\prime}}
is a normal subgroup of
P_{g^{\prime}}
. Thus, we have the exact sequence

(9)

1\to N_{g^{\prime}}\to P_{g^{\prime}}\to L_{g^{\prime}}\to 1.

This suggest that we can compute the quotient
A_{g}\simeq\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}
in two steps. First we take the quotient by
\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}
, which only acts on the
N_{g^{\prime}}
factor. By general theory of arithmetic groups
[
BHC62
]
it follows that
\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}
is a uniform lattice in
N_{g^{\prime}}
and consequently the quotient
N_{g^{\prime}}/\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}
is a compact nilmanifold. Thus, using the decomposition in Equation (
6
) and the map defined in Equation (
7
) we get the map

\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R}),

that realizes
\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g}
as a trivial fiber bundle over the base
\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}\times\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g-g^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
and with fibers the compact nilmanifold
\mathtt{N}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash N_{g^{\prime}}
. Next, we take the quotient by
\mathtt{L}_{g^{\prime}}
. This has two effects: it replaces the base of the fiber bundle by

\mathtt{L}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\big(\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}\!\times\!\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})\big)\cong\big(Sp(2g^{\prime},\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g^{\prime}}\big)\!\times\!\big(\mathrm{GL}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(g^{\prime\prime},\mathbb{R})\big)

and on the fibers it acts with the stabilizer subgroup of the base-point. Thus, given that in general
\Gamma
is not torsion-free we might get a flat orbifold structure in the fibers. Now, since in Section
we will work with the quotient

\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}\simeq\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\mathcal{U}_{g^{\prime}}\subset\mathtt{P}_{g^{\prime}}\backslash\mathfrak{S}_{g},

viewed as a Riemannian orbifold endowed with the Weil–Petersson metric, local arguments must a priori be carried out in orbifold charts. To avoid repeatedly invoking this language, we will carry out most of our analysis on the Siegel sets i.e., we will consider instead the map

(10)

\pi_{g^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{F}_{g}(u)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{F}_{g^{\prime}}(u)\times\mathfrak{F}_{g^{\prime\prime}}^{+}(u)

with
u\gg 1
suitably chosen.

2.3.
Geometric compactifications of

There are several important compactification frameworks for modular varieties such as

In this section we review two compactifications: the Satake–Baily–Borel (SBB) compactification and the tropical compactification, in the sense of Odaka. We place special emphasis on their differential-geometric interpretation, as developed in

Odaka2019
]

Odaka goes on to construct
\bar{A_{g}}^{T}
, which he calls the
tropical geometric compactification
of the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties, as the Gromov–Hausdorff compactification of
A_{g}
at the diameter scale. As a topological space
\bar{A_{g}}^{T}
is obtained by attaching the moduli space of real flat tori with diameter 1 of dimension
1\leq r\leq g
,

(11)

\bar{A_{g}}^{T}:=A_{g}\,\bigsqcup_{r}\,\mathscr{T}_{r},\qquad\text{where }\,\mathscr{T}_{r}:=\mathrm{GL}(r,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{Sym}^{\!+}(r,\mathbb{R})/\mathbb{R}_{>0},

and putting the topology whose basis induces the complex analytic topology in
A_{g}
and the Gromov–Hausdorff topology on the boundary.

One should emphasize that this compactification is no longer an algebraic variety. From our perspective, however, this is a feature rather than a drawback: it is the geometric constraint imposed by requiring the boundary to parametrize limits of the canonical flat Kähler metric on a principally polarized abelian variety, which collapse to real manifolds at diameter scale. Furthermore, the resulting limits have a very interesting connection with the algebro-geometric degenerations of principally polarized abelian varieties, as explained by the following result of Odaka.

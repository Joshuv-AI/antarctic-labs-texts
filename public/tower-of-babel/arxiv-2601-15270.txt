1.
Introduction

Throughout this introduction, by
surfaces
(resp.
curves
)
in mixed characteristic
we mean schemes of
relative dimension
two (resp. one) over a complete DVR of mixed characteristic with algebraically closed residue field.

Birational geometry in mixed characteristic has seen substantial progress in recent years.
For example, the minimal model program for surfaces in mixed characteristic
(0,p>5)
has been established by
[
3
,
41
]
.

One of the main difficulties in this subject is the failure of Kawamata–Viehweg-type vanishing theorems in mixed characteristic
[
42
]
, as well as in positive characteristic
[
29
]
.
In positive characteristic, a key to dealing with this difficulty is the notion of globally
F
-regular varieties
[
39
]
, which form an important subclass of globally
F
-split varieties. Recall that a variety in positive characteristic is globally
F
-split if the absolute Frobenius morphism
\mathcal{O}_{X}\to F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
splits globally. It is known from
[
33
, Theorem 1.1]
that globally
F
-regular varieties are of Fano type.
The theory of global
F
-regularity plays a crucial role in the minimal model program in positive characteristic (see, for example,
[
11
]
).

Takamatsu and Yoshikawa
[
41
]
and Bhatt et al.
[
3
]
introduced the notion of
global
\bm{+}
-regularity
as a mixed characteristic analog of global
F
-regularity (see
Definition
2.1
for its definition).
Bhatt’s vanishing theorem
[
4
]
implies that Kawamata–Viehweg-type vanishing holds for globally
\bm{+}
-regular schemes
[
3
, Corollary 6.12]
.
Globally
\bm{+}
-regular schemes are conjectured to be of Fano type, in analogy with the case of globally
F
-regular varieties
[
3
, Conjecture 6.17]
.

While some examples of globally
\bm{+}
-regular schemes are already implicit in the literature (see, for example,
[
3
, Corollary 7.5]
and
[
45
, Theorem 6.4]
), they have not been systematically investigated.

In this thesis, we study the global
\bm{+}
-regularity of low-dimensional Fano schemes in mixed characteristic.
Regular Fano curves in mixed characteristic are either
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
at a closed point, and they are globally
\bm{+}
-regular (see
Proposition
4.3
).
Smooth del Pezzo surfaces in mixed characteristic are either
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
or blowups of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at
r\leq 8
points (see
Proposition
4.8
).
Very recently, Yoshikawa essentially proved that they are globally
\bm{+}
-regular if the base ring is the ring of Witt vectors
[
45
, Theorem 6.4]
.
We therefore turn to the study of regular del Pezzo surfaces whose closed fiber may be singular.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
R=W(k)
be the ring of Witt vectors of an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
M
be a regular integral flat projective
R
-scheme with
H^{0}(M,\mathcal{O}_{M})=R
.
Suppose that
\dim M=3
and the anticanonical sheaf
\omega_{M}^{-1}
is ample.
If
p>2
and the closed fiber
M_{k}
is reduced, then
M
is globally
\bm{+}
-regular.

As a consequence of
Theorem
A
, we deduce a Kawamata–Viehweg-type vanishing theorem for these surfaces from
[
3
, Corollary 6.12]
.

Theorem B
.

Let
M
be as in
Theorem
A
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a big and semiample line bundle on
M
.
Then
H^{i}(M,\omega_{M}\otimes\mathcal{A})=0
holds for all
i>0
.

It is reasonable to expect that the reducedness of
M_{k}
in
Theorem
A
can be derived from the other assumptions.
One may also ask whether the assumption
p>2
is essential.

1.1.
Idea of the proof of
Theorem
A

Our proof of
Theorem
A
is based on the theory of
quasi-
F
-splitting
in mixed characteristic, together with the classification of the closed fiber
M_{k}
.

Yoshikawa
[
45
]
introduced the notion of quasi-
F
-splitting in mixed characteristic, based on Yobuko’s theory in positive characteristic
[
43
]
(see
Definition
2.22
for its definition).
He essentially showed that quasi-
F
-splitting implies global
\bm{+}
-regularity in a certain setting
[
45
, Theorem 6.3]
, and we use a generalization of this result (
Theorem
3.4
).

We split the proof into cases depending on the singularities of the closed fiber
M_{k}
.

If the closed fiber
M_{k}
has only rational double points (RDP), then
M
is quasi-
F
-split by
[
28
, Theorem B]
.

If the closed fiber
M_{k}
is normal but not an RDP del Pezzo surface, then
M_{k}
is the projective cone over an elliptic curve by
[
14
]
.
Such a variety is easily seen to be quasi-
F
-split, and hence
M
is quasi-
F
-split.

If the closed fiber
M_{k}
is nonnormal, we give a classification of
M_{k}
(
Theorem
6.6
and
6.11
), inspired by
[
8
]
.
Fujita
[
8
]
(cf.
[
9
]
) investigated singular fibers of del Pezzo fibrations over curves in characteristic zero, and our classification result is analogous to his.
His classification is based on Mori’s classification of three-dimensional contractions
[
27
]
, which is not available in mixed characteristic, and we instead use the classification of nonnormal Gorenstein del Pezzo surfaces by Reid
[
31
]
.
Using this classification of
M_{k}
, together with the criterion for
F
-splitting by
[
26
]
, we conclude that
M_{k}
is globally
F
-split. In particular,
M_{k}
is quasi-
F
-split.

2.1.
Global
\bm{+}
-regularity

Definition 2.1

(
[
3
,
41
]
)
.

Let
M
be an excellent normal integral scheme with a dualizing complex. Assume that every closed point of
M
has positive residue characteristic.
Then
M
is said to be
globally
\bm{+}
-regular
if for every finite surjection
g\colon N\to M
from a normal integral scheme

\mathcal{O}_{M}\to g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{N}
splits as a morphism of
\mathcal{O}_{M}
-modules.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
M
be a normal integral projective scheme over a Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m}_{R})
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an ample line bundle on
M
, and let
S\coloneqq\mathcal{R}(M,\mathcal{A})
.
Set
\mathfrak{m}_{S}=\mathfrak{m}_{R}S+S_{>0}
.
If
S_{\mathfrak{m}_{S}}
is (globally)
\bm{+}
-regular, then so is
M
.

Proof.

Let
g\colon N\to M
be a finite surjection from a normal integral scheme
N
.
Set
S^{\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{R}(N,g^{*}\mathcal{A})
.
Since
S_{\mathfrak{m}_{S}}
is globally
\bm{+}
-regular,
S_{\mathfrak{m}_{S}}\to(S^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{m}_{S}}
splits.
Using
[
5
, Corollary 3.6.7]
, we see that
S\to S^{\prime}
splits. Hence,
\mathcal{O}_{M}=\tilde{S}\to g_{*}\mathcal{O}_{N}=\tilde{S^{\prime}}
splits.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be an excellent integral scheme.
Let
K
be the function field of

, and let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of

.
The
absolute integral closure
of

is the integral closure

of

in

.

Suppose that

is normal.
Let

be a

Then we define

\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(D)\coloneqq\colim_{g\colon N\to M}\mathcal{O}_{N}(g^{*}D)\text{,}

where

Note that the pullback

Definition 2.4

A connected reduced projective

Theorem 2.5

(
[

)
.

Let

be a normal Gorenstein del Pezzo surface. Then one of the following holds:

(1)

is an RDP del Pezzo surface.

(2)

on

\Proj\mathcal{R}(E,\mathcal{A})[t]

By

2.4.
Ring of Witt vectors

Throughout this subsection, we fix a prime number
p
.
We recall the definition of the ring of (
p
-typical) Witt vectors (see
[
37
, II, § 6]
and
[
15
, Section 1.3]
for the details).
Note that we consider possibly non-Noetherian rings that are not necessarily of characteristic
p
.

For
n\geq 0
, write

\varphi_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})\coloneqq X_{0}^{p^{n}}+pX_{1}^{p^{n-1}}+\dots+p^{n}X_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n}]\text{.}

Let
A
be a ring not necessarily of characteristic
p
.
Then we write
W(A)
for the
ring of (
p
-typical) Witt vectors
of
A
. We state its basic properties. We have

W(A)=\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}A

as a set, and the map

\varphi\colon W(A)\to\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}A,\quad(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dotsc)\mapsto\bigl(\varphi_{0}(a_{0}),\varphi_{1}(a_{0},a_{1}),\varphi_{2}(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}),\dotsc\bigr)

is a ring homomorphism.
For every
a\in A
, we write
[a]=(a,0,0,\dotsc)\in W(A)
.
We have natural maps

V\colon W(A)\to W(A)
,
R\colon W(A)\to W(A)
, and
F\colon W(A)\to W(A)

that satisfy

\displaystyle V(a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc)=(0,a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc),\quad R(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dotsc)=(a_{1},a_{2},\dotsc)\text{,}

\displaystyle\varphi(F(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},\dotsc))=\bigl(\varphi_{1}(a_{0},a_{1}),\varphi_{2}(a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}),\dotsc\bigr)\text{.}

Then,
F\colon W(A)\to W(A)
is a ring homomorphism.
V\colon F_{*}W(A)\to W(A)
and
R\colon W(A)\to W(A)
are
W(A)
-module homomorphisms.

For
n\geq 1
, we define a ring
W_{n}(A)
by

W_{n}(A)\coloneqq W(A)/V^{n}W(A)\text{,}

which is called the
ring of (
p
-typical) Witt vectors of length
n
.
We have
W_{n}(A)\cong\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}A
as sets.
Then, for every
n\geq 1
, we obtain a ring homomorphism
F\colon W_{n+1}(A)\to W_{n}(A)
, and we have
W_{n+1}(A)
-module homomorphisms

V\colon F_{*}W_{n}(A)\to W_{n+1}(A)
and
R\colon W_{n+1}(A)\to W_{n}(A)
.

We define a sheaf of rings
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{M}
by setting

W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{M}(U)\coloneqq W_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{M}(U))

for every open set
U\subset M
.
Then
W_{n}M\coloneqq(\lvert M\rvert,W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{M})
is a scheme.

is injective.

We now show that

H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,\mathcal{O}_{M}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M}))\to H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{+},\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M}))

is also injective if
e_{0}\geq 1
.
By Bhatt’s vanishing theorem
[
4
, Theorem 6.28]
, we have

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{+},\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(lK_{M}))=0

for every
l\geq 1
.
Furthermore, by
Proposition
3.3
, we also have

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,\mathcal{O}_{M}(lK_{M}))=0

for every
l\geq 1
.
Thus we obtain the commutative diagram

(3.4.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{M}}(lK_{\overline{M}}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M^{+}},\overline{\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(lK_{M})})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,\mathcal{O}_{M}(lK_{M}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{+},\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(lK_{M}))}

for every
l\geq 1
.
In particular, the map

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{M}}(p^{e}K_{\overline{M}}))\to H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M^{+}},\overline{\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e}K_{M})})

is injective for all
e\geq e_{0}
.
By (
2.20.2
), we have the exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to F_{*}(Q_{M,n-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M}(p^{e_{0}}K_{M}))\to Q_{M,n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M})\to F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\overline{M}}(p^{e_{0}}K_{\overline{M}})\to 0\text{,}

\displaystyle 0\to F_{*}(Q_{M^{+},n-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}}K_{M}))\to Q_{M^{+},n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M})\to F_{*}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}}K_{M})}\to 0

for every
n>1
.
Using Bhatt’s vanishing theorem
[
4
, Theorem 6.28]
, we obtain
H^{d-1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M^{+}},\overline{\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}}K_{M})})=0
.
Taking the long exact sequences in local cohomology, we deduce that

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M},Q_{M,n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M}))\to H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M^{+}},Q_{M^{+},n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M}))

is injective for every
n\geq 1
by induction on
n
.
Since
M
is quasi-
F
-split, we conclude that

H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M},\mathcal{O}_{\overline{M}}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{\overline{M}}))\to H^{d}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M^{+}},\overline{\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M})})

is injective.
Therefore, by (
3.4.1
), the map

H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,\mathcal{O}_{M}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M}))\to H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{+},\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(p^{e_{0}-1}K_{M}))

is injective.
Repeating this process, we eventually obtain that

H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M,\mathcal{O}_{M}(K_{M}))\to H^{d+1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M^{+},\mathcal{O}_{M^{+}}(K_{M}))

is injective.
Therefore, by
[
3
, Proposition 6.8, Lemma 4.8 (a)]
,
M
is globally
\bm{+}
-regular.
∎

We present classification results for regular Fano curves and smooth weak del Pezzo surfaces.
Most of the results in this section should be well known to the experts, but the author could not find suitable references.

Remark 4.1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

4.2.
Smooth weak del Pezzo surfaces in mixed characteristic

We present a classification result for smooth weak del Pezzo surfaces over a complete DVR of mixed characteristic.
Recall that every weak del Pezzo surface over an algebraically closed field is either
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, or a blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at
n\leq 8
points.
Smooth weak del Pezzo surfaces in mixed characteristic are lifts of these surfaces, and we classify them based on deformation theory.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a Noetherian local ring, and let
f\colon M\to N
be a morphism between flat projective
R
-schemes.
If
f_{k}=f\otimes_{R}k\colon M_{k}\to N_{k}
is an isomorphism, then
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
f_{k}
is an isomorphism, we deduce that
f
is quasi-finite; hence finite by Zariski’s main theorem. Define
\mathcal{O}_{N}
-modules
\mathcal{K}
,
\mathcal{C}
by the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{O}_{N}\to f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M}\to\mathcal{C}\to 0\text{.}

Since
f_{k}
is an isomorphism, we obtain
\mathcal{C}=0
by Nakayama’s lemma.
Note that since
f_{k}
is an isomorphism,
f
is flat (by the fiberwise criterion for flatness
[
40
, Tag 039B]
for example).
Hence, we see

0\to\mathcal{K}_{k}\to\mathcal{O}_{N_{k}}\to(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{M})_{k}\to 0

is exact. Since
f_{k}
is an isomorphism, we obtain
\mathcal{K}=0
by Nakayama’s lemma.
Therefore,
f
is an isomorphism by noting that
f
is affine.
∎

.

(R,\mathfrak{m},k)

.

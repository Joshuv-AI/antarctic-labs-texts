Mesoporous silica nanoparticles functionalized with folic acid and zinc for enhanced targeted delivery of daunorubicin to breast cancer cells
Authors: Seyedeh Elaheh Sheykholeslami, Nasim Kaveh Farsani, Yeganeh Azari Incheh Sablagh, Soheil Afshari-BehbahaniZadeh, Mohammadhosein Keshvadi, Fatemeh Najafi, Hassan Noorbazargan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTargeted delivery of therapeutic agents may enhance anticancer efficacy through specific mechanisms. We have developed and characterized folic acid-functionalized mesoporous silica nanoparticles with co-loaded Daunorubicin and Zn2⁺ ions (FA-DAN-Zn/MSN), and we tested their anti-cancer activity in MCF-7 breast cancer cells. We characterized the nanoparticles and demonstrated a narrow size range with a negative zeta potential and spherical morphology (consistent with transmission electron microscopy (TEM)). Drug release titrations showed that the nanoparticles had pH-responsive properties by demonstrating preferential drug release at lower pH values, which would be consistent with a tumor microenvironment. Stability studies indicated that the nanoparticles remained stable under physiological conditions. The FA-DAN-Zn/MSN produced a more cytotoxic response to the MCF-7 cells in an MTT assay than free DAN, with no impact on the viability of the non-tumorigenic MCF-10A cells. Mechanistically, we showed that the FA-DAN-Zn/MSN included upregulation of pro-apoptotic markers (BAX and Caspase-9) and downregulation of the anti-apoptotic marker BCL-2, on their regulation with reduced markers of MMP-2 and MMP-9. Both reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and caspase activity were consistent with inducible apoptosis, and the scratch assays indicated perturbation of cancer cell motility consistent with the idea of intervention. In summary, we recognize potential as a selective and efficacious nanocarrier for co-delivery and provide improved bioavailability and tumor target relative as compared to free drug treatment, which demonstrates further anticancer effects through enhanced apoptotic and anti-proliferative activity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-53254-4.Keywords: Mesoporous silica nanoparticles, Breast cancer, Synergy, Gene regulation, Drug delivery system

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is a consequence of the excessive development of malignant cells in the breast tissue1. As the second most common women’s malignancy, BC is the third major cause of mortality worldwide2. Technological advancements like magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computerized tomography, ultrasound, mammography, and positron emission tomography (PET) have facilitated the early diagnosis of breast cancer3. However, roughly 30% of women with early breast cancer subsequently develop metastases and recurrence. Biomedical therapy options confront significant clinical obstacles due to the heterogeneity of breast cancer and the intricacy of tumor regulatory pathways3. Currently, surgery, chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy, and hormone therapy are the most common treatments for breast cancer, all of which may not eliminate the tumor, in addition to possible side effects4. Some cases such as triple-negative BC and metastatic patients may receive inadequate treatment; thus, novel therapeutic approaches are always welcome. The advent of nanomedicines has opened new horizons in cancer treatment5,6.

Mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) refer to porous solid materials with inorganic siloxane compositions, which have gained increasing popularity as a potential medicinal option7. The regulated and prolonged delivery of bioactive compounds from the organized mesoporous structure can also minimize the required dose, thereby reducing the risk of adverse effects associated with drug overdose, while enhancing the therapeutic approach’s efficacy by increasing local drug concentration8. Functionalized MSNs have shown remarkable cytotoxicity against a variety of cancer cells, allowing for pH-responsive and regulated drug delivery8. Active targeting groups have been exploited by certain studies to increase tumor targeting and chemotherapeutic preference of MSNs9. Incorporating a chemotherapeutic agent into the MSN structure has been reported to enhance drug release under tumor-relevant conditions, such as acidic microenvironments.

Mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) were chosen for drug administration because of their unique physicochemical characteristics that improve drug loading, stability, controlled release, and biocompatibility10. MSNs have a significant surface area-to-volume ratio, adjustable pore size, and significant pore volume, facilitating effective drug encapsulation and regulated release patterns11. Their hydrophilic surface chemistry facilitates functionalization with targeted ligands like folic acid, enhancing selectivity for cancer cells and reducing off-target damage12–15. Moreover, MSNs demonstrate pH-sensitive breakdown, facilitating rapid drug release in the acidic tumor microenvironment while preserving drug stability at physiological pH (7.4). In comparison to other nanocarriers, MSNs exhibit higher stability, biodegradability, and improved cellular absorption, rendering them an optimal platform for targeted drug delivery in cancer treatment16,17. Recent advances in dual-responsive nanocomposite systems, particularly mesoporous silica-supported gold nanorod platforms, have demonstrated significant improvements in synergistic chemo-photothermal therapy through NIR- and pH-responsive drug release mechanisms18–21.

Daunorubicin (DAN), an anthracycline antitumor agent, has been extensively employed in treating acute lymphocytic leukemia and acute myelocytic leukemia. Its anti-cancer efficacy is attributed to its interference with DNA, DNA topoisomerase I and II, reactive oxygen species, and metal ions, as well as its ability to induce apoptosis22. However, the widespread clinical application of DAN has been hindered by the occurrence of severe side effects such as cardiotoxicity, myelosuppression, and oral ulcers23. In a recent study, the efficacy of Daunorubicin (DAN) as a potent anti-cancer medication across multiple cancer types has been introduced, alongside its utilization in novel drug delivery systems. These systems may involve DAN as a standalone chemotherapy agent or in combination with diverse nanoparticles, as elucidated in recent reviews24.

Furthermore, zinc supplementation can reduce angiogenesis and inflammatory cytokine production while promoting programmed cell death in tumor tissues, causing a cancer-fighting impact25. Zinc contributes to appropriate growth, as well as a variety of physiological activities. Zinc ions have been found to selectively prevent bacterial contamination. Zinc oxide also revealed anti-tumor activity26. Zinc-loaded MSNs have been recently studied for their anti-cancer and antibacterial potential27,28. Studies have reported a substantial decline in the proliferation of MCF-7 (ER +) and triple-negative MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells 24 h after treatment with the MSNs-Zinc structure3,25. Owing to the incorporation of Zn-modified MSNs, co-delivery of DAN within the Zn-functionalized carrier enhanced stability and bioavailability and improved anticancer activity compared to free DAN. In this study, the enhanced effect reflects potentiation of DAN activity by Zn surface modification rather than a formally quantified drug–drug synergy.

Our study presents a novel nanocarrier system that co-delivers daunorubicin (DAN) and Zn2 + ions, offering potential enhanced effects in cancer treatment. This system incorporates folic acid (FA) for targeted delivery, which may enhance cellular uptake in folate receptor-expressing cells. The porous structure of MSNs allows for efficient drug loading and controlled release, particularly in the acidic environment of cancer cells, improving drug stability and bioavailability. Our in vitro findings indicate enhanced anticancer activity against cancer cells while maintaining comparatively lower toxicity in the tested non-tumorigenic cells. Additionally, improving the nanoparticle with zinc may reduce the required dose of daunorubicin, potentially lowering side effects. Overall, this nanoplatform demonstrates promising in vitro performance and provides a basis for further preclinical evaluation. However, effects on systemic toxicity, therapeutic index, and side-effect reduction require confirmation through in vivo pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, and safety studies.

Results and discussion

Characterization of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN

The physical structure and surface properties of different MSN samples were assessed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Fig. 1A). Images confirm the formation of spherical nanoparticles for the MSN nanoparticles. The TEM images show a cluster of nanoparticles with a relatively uniform size distribution. The average particle size of Zn/MSN is approximately 50-100 nm (Fig. 1A). The TEM image of DAN-Zn/MSN reveals a similar morphology to Zn/MSN but with slightly larger particle sizes (Figure S2-A). The average particle size ranges from 100-150 nm. For FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, the particles are larger and more irregular in shape compared to Zn/MSN and DAN-Zn/MSN (Figure S2-B). The average particle size is around 150-200 nm. Since TEM images are objective and do not represent the whole sample, we employed the dynamic light scattering (DLS) technique to obtain the hydrodynamic size distribution for colloidal nanoparticles in the solution. The results of the test show that the size of the DAN-Zn/MSN particles in water was 192.1 ± 6.7 nm, with PDI = 0.178 ± 0.026, which increased to 222 ± 6.4 nm with a PDI of 0.178 ± 0.009 after functionalization with FA and Zn2+ ions, as well as functionalization with folic acid (Fig. 1B,C). This increase in hydrodynamic size suggests the successful functionalization and presence of folic acid on the surface of nanocarriers. As can be seen, the size distribution of nanocarriers covers particles less than 100 nm to up to 1000 nm, which agrees with the particle size observed under TEM. The successful loading of drugs and surface functionalization in each step was further verified by changes in zeta potential values (Fig. 1D). The potential impact of large aggregates on cellular uptake is considered negligible for the following reasons; the mean hydrodynamic diameters of DAN-Zn/MSN and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN reside well within the 100-500 nm window optimal for clathrin-mediated endocytosis and macropinocytosis in tumor-derived cells, and the FA-receptor-mediated active targeting mechanism independently drives internalization regardless of minor size heterogeneity29,30. Regarding biological reproducibility, the consistently low PDI values demonstrate high inter-batch synthesis fidelity, while rapid serum protein corona formation upon exposure to physiological media effectively normalizes surface heterogeneity across the particle population, thereby minimizing size-dependent variability in cellular association kinetics31,32. With respect to translational relevance, the apparent tail extending toward ~ 1000 nm in the intensity-weighted DLS profile reflects a well-recognized mathematical artifact of Rayleigh scattering wherein intensity scales with the sixth power of particle diameter, rather than a physically dominant aggregate population, and should not be interpreted as evidence of formulation instability33,34. The overall physicochemical profile (PDI < 0.3, mean diameter < 250 nm, reproducible functionalization) is consistent with regulatory expectations outlined in FDA and EMA guidance on nanomedicines and comparable to MSN-based platforms that have successfully advanced through preclinical evaluation.

The size of the nanoparticles measured by TEM/SEM was much smaller than that measured by the Nano Zetasizer. The differences in size measurements between TEM/SEM and DLS techniques may be attributed to the drying process during TEM/SEM imaging. In summary, TEM/SEM provides the exact diameter of nanoparticles in their dry state, whereas DLS measures the hydrodynamic diameter, which includes the core along with any molecules, including ions and water, that are attached or adsorbed on the surface. Consequently, sizes acquired using TEM/SEM are often smaller than those obtained via DLS35–37. TEM image analysis revealed intra-particle porosity values of 40.4%, 37.1%, and 31.7% for Zn/MSN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, respectively. A progressive decrease in porosity was observed across the three samples following each functionalization step. The systematic reduction in intra-particle porosity from MSN-Zn (40.4%) to MSN-Zn-DAN (37.1%) confirms successful loading of the DAN molecule into the mesopore channels, consistent with partial pore filling by the guest drug. The further decrease observed in MSN-Zn-DAN-FA (31.7%) indicates that folic acid conjugation introduces additional steric bulk at the pore entrance, effectively reducing accessible void volume and confirming successful surface functionalization.

Drug release study

The quantity of drug release (DAN) was measured via the membrane-dialysis technique (molecular weight cut-off = 12 KDa) during certain periods, and the amount of released DAN molecules was measured by UV–Visible spectrophotometer (Figure S1). Figure 2 shows the release profile for DAN in solution and DAN from FA- DAN -Zn/MSN at different pHs (5.4 and 6.5 for mimicking tumor microenvironment and 7.4 as physiological pH). In all groups, a drug release profile consisting of two distinct stages comprises an initial rapid release within the first eight hours, followed by a sustained. However, the release of DAN from the samples exhibits significant variability, with the drug release from the DAN solution surpassing that from the FA- DAN -Zn/MSN. Also, FA- DAN -Zn/MSN release showed pH-dependent and remarkably higher at a pH of 5.4 compared with pH of 6.5 and 7.4. Similarly, almost 100% of the drug has been released from Free DAN in solution during the first 24 h, whereas at this point, approximately 87% has been released at a pH of 5.4, and about 65% has been released at a pH of 7.4. Furthermore, four distinct kinetic models were applied to the release data to investigate the drug release kinetics exhibited by each formulation under varying pH conditions (Table S2). The release study indicated the controlled release of the drug from a modified nanocarrier; the Korsmeyer-Peppas model was the best release kinetic model. MSNs are attractive drug delivery candidates in current research trends owing to their particular features like facile large-scale manufacture, tunable and uniform pore size, and high surface area38. MSN gatekeeper frameworks govern the release profile of drugs and can be triggered by internal or external factors, including a pH reduction. Concerning the tumor microenvironment and the endolysosomal systems, pH-sensitive systems respond to acidic pH levels39. Surface functionalization of MSNs is required for a variety of reasons, including targeting the drug carrier to the appropriate sites, regulating drug release of the capping systems at the targeted area, as well as preventing endo-lysosomal escape39. The designed drug delivery system leverages a higher concentration of reducing enzymes like glutathione (GSH) in cancer cells compared to normal cells. This system utilizes mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) with disulfide monomers, which enhance drug release in the presence of glutathione. The controlled release of daunorubicin (DAN) from MSNs is facilitated by their porous structure and large surface area, allowing efficient drug loading and gradual release. Folic acid (FA) functionalization on the MSN surface targets folate receptor-expressing cancer cells, improving targeted delivery and release of DAN. The pH-sensitive nature of MSNs ensures that DAN is predominantly released in the acidic environment of cancer cells, enhancing therapeutic efficacy while minimizing systemic toxicity. This combination of glutathione sensitivity, porous structure, and pH responsiveness makes FA-functionalized MSNs a promising strategy for targeted cancer treatment40–42. Although the mesoporous channels of MSN are not completely closed, the slow drug release is achieved through a combination of factors, including pore size and tortuosity, drug-matrix interactions, surface modifications, and particle size and agglomeration. These factors create a controlled environment that hinders the rapid diffusion of the loaded drug, allowing for a more sustained and targeted release profile. The attachment of folic acid to the MSN surface can modulate the drug release kinetics by influencing factors such as drug loading capacity, drug-carrier interactions, and the rate of drug desorption from the nanoparticles. This controlled release profile ensures that the drug is released at a rate that is optimal for the target cells, minimizing the amount of drug that leaks out before reaching its target43,44.

Stability

The stability of optimum DAN-Zn/MSN and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN was evaluated by placing the samples at 4 °C and 25 °C and observing alterations in average size, PDI, and EE% throughout 0, 14, and 30 days. (Fig. 3A-F). Upon formulation, FA-DAN-Zn/MSN exhibited a size of 222 nm, a PDI of 0.178, and an EE% of 86.72. For DAN-Zn/MSN, the average size was 192.1 nm, PDI was 0.178, and EE% was 69.24%. The fluctuation in temperature significantly impacted how these parameters evolved over one month. With increasing temperatures, there was a noticeable enlargement in the pores of the particles, leading to a subsequent rise in both the average sample size and the PDI. Furthermore, the increase in pore size contributed to an escalation in the release of the drug from the carrier. Consequently, the decreased retention of the drug within the samples led to a decline in EE%. Specifically, the average size of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN increased by 31.94% after 30 days at 25 °C, and by 21.94% at 4 °C. During this timeframe, the PDI of the formulation experienced a significant increase of 58.42% at 25 °C and 39.32% at 4 °C. The percentage of EE also decreased by 11.31% at 25 °C and 5.53% at 4 °C. For DAN-Zn/MSN at 25 °C, the average size and PDI increase by 25.61% and 58.55%, respectively, and at 4 °C by 18.37% and 38.81%, respectively. At 25 °C, the EE% decreased by 25.15%, and at 4 °C, it declined by 12.69%. This suggests that DAN-Zn/MSN and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN exhibit greater stability at lower temperatures. The increased fluctuations in size, PDI, and EE% at 25 °C may be attributed to the occurrences outlined in the subsequent analysis. The observed disparities in the EE% of both samples were particularly notable after 30 days (p < 0.01). The stability of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN at 4 °C relative to 25 °C is due to decreased degradation, maintained integrity of the structure, improved Zn2⁺ stability, reduced drug leakage, and the protective effect of folic acid. At reduced temperatures, molecular kinetics decelerate, reducing the rate of hydrolytic and oxidative breakdown of daunorubicin and zinc inside the MSN matrix. Moreover, mesoporous silica nanoparticles exhibit reduced susceptibility to water absorption and pore breakdown, therefore increasing drug-loading stability and inhibiting premature release. Zinc ions, susceptible to oxidation and precipitation at higher temperatures, have greater stability at 4 °C, minimizing the possibility of aggregation and enhancing bioavailability. Moreover, the Arrhenius equation stipulates that the rate of molecular diffusion and drug desorption from nanoparticles increases with temperature, indicating that storage at 4 °C reduces drug diffusion and prevents premature release, hence maintaining therapeutic effectiveness. Folic acid, which enables targeted drug delivery, is affected by photo-oxidation and thermal degradation at room temperature, resulting in reduced receptor-binding efficacy. At 4 °C, this degradation process markedly decelerates, keeping the targeting efficacy of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN. Lower-temperature storage environments enhance formulation integrity, inhibit early drug release, and sustain targeted functionality, guaranteeing maximum therapeutic efficacy45,46.

Cytotoxicity assay

Figure 4A indicates the concentration-dependent cytotoxicity of DAN (free drug), Zn/MSN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN on MCF-7 cells as model breast cancer cells containing folate receptors on their surface. As can be seen, all samples could have a cytotoxic effect against MCF-7 cells, and cell viability decreases with increasing concentration of samples. The results suggest that Zn surface modification of the MSN carrier enhances the cytotoxic activity of DAN when loaded into the nanoparticle system. The results suggested that the enhanced effect between Zn2+ ions and DAN led to higher cytotoxic effects in DAN-Zn/MSN (IC50 = 0.76 μM) compared to free DAN (IC50 = 1.85 μM) and Zn/MSN (IC50 = 4.25 μM). The surface functionalization with folic acid further enhanced cytotoxicity, which is likely associated with folate receptor-mediated uptake in MCF-7 cells, leading to a decrease in IC50 value to 0.38 μM (Table S3).

However, this trend was reversed in MCF-10A cells (Fig. 4B). Loading DAN molecules in MSN led to a less cytotoxic effect, where folic acid functionalization further increased the biocompatibility of the nanocarrier against non-tumorigenic MCF-10A cells. MCF-10A cells do not have folate receptors, and therefore, endocytosis will decrease when the nanocarrier is functionalized with folic acid. Also, the results on MCF-7 cell viability with MSN, Zn/MSN, DAN/MSN, and DAN-Zn/MSN formulations are shown in Fig. 4C. For MSN only, the cell viability decreased with increasing concentrations, although the cytotoxicity was relatively low. For Zn/MSN, Zn greatly increased the cytotoxicity, and viability decreased more than MSN group. The same was observed for the DAN/MSN group, where DAN also increased cytotoxicity. DAN-Zn/MSN, which contained both Zn and DAN in the MSN structure, showed a greater decrease in viability than the other groups; cell viability was lower than in all other groups and decreased with increasing formulation concentration. DAN-Zn/MSN group had 22.58% viability at the highest concentration (4 µM), significantly lower than Zn/MSN (51.39%) and DAN/MSN (33.14%) viability (Fig. 4C). DAN-Zn/MSN group decrease in viability, compared to what would be expected by Zn and DAN alone, is indicative of an enhanced interaction to produce increased cytotoxicity with MSN towards MCF-7.

After uptake, MSNs are transported into large vesicular endosomes, which subsequently fuse with lysosomes. The eventual rupture of endosomal/lysosomal membranes indicates that nanoparticles may escape from the endosomal/lysosomal compartment47. Khosravian et al. conducted an MTT assay and reported boosted cytotoxicity of docetaxel (DTX)-loaded MSNs. Moreover, FA-MSN-DTX was more effective based on uptake and apoptosis findings48. Functionalized MSNs exhibited stronger cytotoxic effects than non-modified MSNs48. Thus, MSNs are possible biocompatible carriers without toxicity on normal cells. Overall, the data show that Zn-MSN-NH2 has promising potential to improve the efficacy of targeted DAN administration.

MTT assay in this study showed that the IC50 concentration of DAN and various concentrations of Zn-MSN-NH2 and Zn-MSN-NH2-DAN could significantly decrease the viability of the MCF-7 cell line. Although DAN showed cytotoxic activity against MCF-7 cells, loading DAN into the nanocarrier reduced its cytotoxic effect against the non-tumorigenic MCF-10A cell line. Despite the presence of DNA repair systems, cells frequently experience cell death owing to disrupted DNA, RNA, and proteins49. Besides its perfect antiproliferative effects, DAN has several side effects on normal cells. According to our results, modified MSNs can be a promising candidate to decrease the adverse effects of DAN on healthy cells. Due to their efficient drug transport and endocytosis characteristics, MSNs are frequently recommended as suitable carriers for anti-cancer medicines50. Both phagocytosis and pinocytosis are used by MSNs to uptake material via the cell membrane51. MSNs must infiltrate the cytoplasm after cracking through the cell membrane to discharge therapeutic agents. Several studies suggest that mesoporous silica nanoparticles (MSNs) can exhibit favorable biodegradability and biocompatibility profiles depending on their size, surface chemistry, and dose. However, in vivo clearance and systemic safety are formulation-dependent and require direct evaluation. The porous structure of MSNs offers a wide surface area that promotes hydrolysis, allowing silica to progressively dissolve into silicic acid (Si(OH)₄) in aqueous conditions, especially at physiological pH levels. Silicic acid is a naturally occurring silicon-containing compound in the human body and can be efficiently eliminated by the kidneys, reducing the risk of long-term accumulation and toxicity. In contrast to non-biodegradable nanoparticles, MSNs dissolve into physiologically ineffective metabolites that absorb into normal metabolic routes without provoking significant immune reactions. The porous structure facilitates effective drug loading and regulated release, minimizing excessive nanoparticle formation and enhancing therapeutic efficacy. Surface changes, such as folic acid conjugation, improve targeting and biocompatibility while minimizing nonspecific interactions37,52. At low concentrations, FA-DAN/MSN viability was 78.4 ± 2.14% (0.25 µM) vs. 60.14 ± 1.73% for FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, and this gap widened at 4 µM (16.41 ± 2.10% vs. 4.25 ± 1.31%). Zn co-loading thus amplified FA-targeted DAN delivery, with ~ 12% lower viability for FA-DAN-Zn/MSN at 4 µM (Fig. 4D). The enhanced potency of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN over FA-DAN/MSN stems from Zn ions’ synergy with DAN, generating ROS and disrupting mitochondrial function in MCF-7 cells beyond folate-mediated uptake alone. This combination leverages FA’s specificity for receptor-overexpressing tumors while Zn provides a broad cytotoxic boost, explaining the steeper dose–response curve53.

To mechanistically validate FR-α-dependent uptake, folate receptors were pre-saturated with excess free folic acid before FA-DAN-Zn/MSN treatment. As presented in Figure S4, FA-DAN-Zn/MSN without receptor blocking demonstrated the highest cytotoxic potency across all concentrations, reducing MCF-7 cell viability from 100.00 ± 1.25% at 0 µM to 4.25 ± 1.31% at 4 µM. Upon FR-α pre-saturation with excess free FA, cell viability was significantly rescued at every tested concentration at 0.25 µM, where viability increased from 60.14 ± 1.73% to 70.82 ± 1.85%, at 1.0 µM from 29.12 ± 1.19% to 40.50 ± 1.60%, and at 4.0 µM from 4.25 ± 1.31% to 22.26 ± 1.19%. Notably, these rescued viability values converge toward those of the non-targeted DAN-Zn/MSN group across all concentrations, which recorded viabilities of 71.21 ± 2.21%, 58.19 ± 1.85%, 40.84 ± 1.49%, 31.19 ± 1.31%, and 22.58 ± 1.84% at 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, and 4.0 µM, respectively. The significant rescue of MCF-7 cell viability upon FR-α pre-saturation with excess free FA confirms that the enhanced cytotoxicity of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN is driven by folate receptor-mediated endocytosis rather than passive diffusion. When FR-α is fully saturated, FA-DAN-Zn/MSN loses its targeting advantage entirely and behaves identically to the non-targeted DAN-Zn/MSN, proving beyond doubt that FR-α-mediated endocytosis is the sole mechanism driving its superior cytotoxicity.

Gene expression assay

Effective entry of the anticancer agents into the cancer cells can eliminate the tumor cells. Cell death is indeed due to changes in various cellular metabolisms, which can be tracked by determining the expression level of specific genes. In this regard, the impact of the prepared formulations on apoptosis- and metastasis-related gene expression was evaluated by analyzing five distinct genes (BAX, BCL-2, Caspase-9, MMP-9, and MMP-2) in MCF-7 cells (Fig. 5). Figure 5A,B demonstrate significant upregulation of pro-apoptotic genes (Caspase-9 and BAX) in DAN-treated groups compared to control (p < 0.001). Figure 5C shows significant downregulation of the anti-apoptotic gene BCL-2. Furthermore, Fig. 5D, E show the reduced expression of metastatic markers MMP-2 and MMP-9, respectively, following treatment, with FA-DAN-Zn/MSN exhibiting the strongest effect.

Figure 5A,B demonstrate a significant increase in the expression of Caspase-9 and BAX in DAN-containing treatment groups (DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN) relative to the control (p < 0.001), indicating the pro-apoptotic effects of DAN. Zn/MSN does not provide significant effects relative to the control, suggesting that the carrier alone does not affect the response. However, the DAN-Zn/MSN significantly increases the fold change relative to DAN alone (p < 0.001), indicating enhanced drug delivery. The FA-DAN-Zn/MSN formulation shows the greatest fold change, indicating a significant rise relative to DAN-Zn/MSN (p < 0.001), probably due to improved cellular uptake enabled by folic acid targeting. In Fig. 5C–E, the fold change is significantly smaller in the DAN-treated groups (DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN) relative to the control (p < 0.001), confirming its inhibitory effects of these formulations on anti-apoptotic and metastasis-related gene expression. The Zn/MSN carrier alone does not influence the fold change, since no significant difference is detected relative to the control. The DAN-Zn/MSN significantly decreases the fold change relative to DAN alone (p < 0.001), indicating the greater effectiveness of the nanoparticle system. For BCL-2, FA-DAN-Zn/MSN demonstrates the most significant reduction, exhibiting a significant decrease relative to DAN-Zn/MSN (p < 0.05), hence confirming the significance of folic acid in enhancing cellular targeting and therapeutic efficacy.

The surface functionalization with folic acid led to further BAX and Caspase-9 gene expression levels, owing to higher cellular uptake for FA-DAN-Zn/MSN. Zn/MSN alone did not considerably alter the expression of the tested genes; however, the combination of Zn and DAN led to a decrease in the expression of these two genes due to the synergic effect. Similarly, folic acid functionalization further reduced the expression of these genes in MCF cells.

Real-time PCR analysis showed a notable increase in the levels of pro-apoptotic genes within treated MCF-7 cells. At the IC50 concentration of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, with significant modulation of apoptosis- and metastasis-related gene expression, suggesting enhanced in vitro anticancer activity of FA-DAN-Zn/MSN. Several genes play essential roles in the activation of apoptosis in cancer cells. BCL-2 family proteins control all major types of cell death, particularly apoptosis, necrosis, and autophagy. They act as nodes at the intersection of several pathways with wide oncological importance. BAX, a core cell death regulator, is a critical entry point into mitochondrial malfunction and a prominent pro-apoptotic member of the BCL-2 family, regulating apoptosis in malignant and normal cells54. The mechanism of activity of the individual members involves either maintaining or interrupting mitochondrial integrity, thus evoking or blocking the release of apoptogenic variables, like Cytochrome c (Cyt c) from mitochondria55. Caspase-9 is one of the most extensively investigated initiator Caspase and is a crucial component of the intrinsic mitochondrial pathways. It can be activated by diverse triggers such as chemotherapies, radiation, and stress agents. Caspase-9 can be recruited to the apoptosome complex to maintain catalytic activity. It is also hypothesized to be involved in homo-dimerization of monomeric zymogens. Failure to stimulate Caspase-9 could result in serious pathophysiological and physiological consequences, such as progressive and evolutionary complications, and malignancy56. Modified MSNs can exert their apoptotic efficacy in MCF-7 cell lines by altering the expression of pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic genes. In line with our study, an investigation indicated a notable rise in the expression of the BAX gene in the HN5 (oral cancer cell) cell line receiving curcumin-loaded MSNs, curcumin-DAN-loaded MSNs, as well as DAN-loaded MSNs (p < 0.05). The expression of the BCL-2 gene dramatically declined after receiving all compounds57. The findings of other surveys also revealed a significant upregulation in levels of pro-apoptotic genes at the mRNA level, like Caspase-9, Bim, Puma, BAX, and Noxa in LBC3 (Glioblastoma Cell Lines) treated with SiNPs58. Liu et al. also indicated that treatments of MCF-7 with gold-MSNs could suppress BCL-2 expression while upregulating BAX expression in comparison with untreated cells59. In another study, MSNs-NH2-curcumin showed a substantial up-regulation in the BAX, Caspase-9 expression levels in MCF-7 cell lines compared to free curcumin. Besides, the down-regulation of the anti-apoptotic gene of BCL-2 and human telomerase reverse transcriptase was more noticeable in the cells treated with MSNs-NH2-curcumin. Such an enhancement in pro-apoptotic effect may be associated with improved intracellular delivery of anticancer agents to the cells, which increases intracellular drug concentration and by passed the transmembrane efflux mechanisms60.

Scratch (wound healing) assay

Metastasis, the cumulative outcome of multiple alterations in cancer cells and their microenvironment, facilitates their migration or invasion into healthy host tissues61. The in-vitro wound healing assay serves as a convenient method for evaluating the migratory and invasive capabilities of cancer cells under diverse experimental conditions. As depicted in Fig. 6A, the migration of cancer cells exhibited a notable reduction after 48 h of treatment with DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN compared to the control group (p < 0.001), whereas Zn/MSN alone showed only a limited effect. The DAN-Zn/MSN group demonstrates a significant decrease in migration relative to the control (p < 0.001), signifying its inhibitory impact. The DAN-Zn/MSN amplifies this inhibitory action, resulting in a markedly reduced migration rate compared to the DAN group (p < 0.001). The FA-DAN-Zn/MSN formulation exhibits the most significant inhibition of migration, showing a marked decrease relative to DAN-Zn/MSN (p < 0.001), probably attributable to enhanced targeting and cellular uptake. The scratch rate (%) values for different groups in MCF-7 cells after 48 h were approximately 84.5%, 59%, 42.5%, and 23.5% for Zn/MSN, DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, respectively (Fig. 6A). These findings align with a prior study demonstrating that curcumin-loaded MSN exerted a greater anti-migratory effect on MCF-7 cells compared to free curcumin62. The heightened inhibitory effect and reduced migration of cancer cells observed with FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, as opposed to the free drug, may be associated with improved cellular internalization of the nanoparticle formulation. Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are implicated in metastasis through the enlargement of matrix pores, which facilitates the migration of cancer cells in a confined environment63,64. Our gene expression analysis substantiates the downregulation of MMP-2 and MMP-9 genes, resulting in decreased cancer cell migration following treatment like a study done before with the optimal Nio-TH/PVA formulation65.

ROS assay

It has been documented that an essential trigger for cell death is the elevated presence of reactive oxygen species (ROS) at levels capable of targeting crucial cellular components, including Bcl-2 proteins, mitochondria, and receptors66–68. Therefore, we evaluated ROS levels in MCF-7 cells subjected to treatments with Zn/MSN, DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN compared to untreated cells, quantitatively measuring the highly fluorescent 2’,7'-dichlorofluorescein (DCF). As illustrated in Fig. 6B, the fluorescence intensity of DCF in MCF-7 cells increased following treatment with Zn/MSN, DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, with the latter exhibiting a significantly higher increase (p < 0.001). The control group displays the lowest fluorescence, whereas Zn/MSN results in a modest elevation in ROS levels (p < 0.001 vs. control), probably attributable to the presence of Zn. The DAN-treated groups (DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN) exhibit a considerable increase in ROS levels relative to the control (p < 0.001), corroborating their involvement in oxidative stress induction. The DAN-Zn/MSN group significantly increases ROS production (p < 0.001 vs. DAN), indicating that Zn/MSN augments the impact of DAN. The FA-DAN-Zn/MSN formulation demonstrates the maximum ROS production, significantly surpassing DAN-Zn/MSN (p < 0.001), hence underscoring the improved effectiveness of folic acid functionalization in targeting cancer cells.

In this context, previous reports indicate that the accumulation of ROS in cancer cells leads to disruption of mitochondrial transmembrane potential, oxidative injury, and ultimately results in cell death69,70. Furthermore, excessive ROS induces oxidative DNA damage, recruiting PARP2 for single-strand break repair; however, cancer-associated mutations and serine phosphorylations impair PARP2 function, exacerbating genomic instability and cell death71. Elevated ROS levels disrupt cellular functions, inducing cellular stress and gradually leading to programmed cell death. The most substantial increase in ROS levels was observed in cell lines treated with FA-DAN-Zn/MSN, aligning with the observations on apoptosis progression in the samples.

Caspase activity

The cellular damage induced by DAN results in the initiation of caspases, particularly Caspase-9, with subsequent activation of downstream effector caspases, such as Caspase-372. The cascade ultimately culminates in apoptosis. To substantiate the apoptotic effects of our proposed delivery platform, we examined caspase activity levels and gene expression. In MCF-7, the Caspase activity of Zn/MSN, DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN was measured at 0.523, 1.935, 2.684, and 3.215, respectively (Fig. 7). Figure 7 shows that the control group exhibited low caspase activity, while Zn/MSN alone caused only a modest but significant increase in caspase activity compared with the control (p < 0.05). The DAN-treated groups (DAN, DAN-Zn/MSN, and FA-DAN-Zn/MSN) demonstrate a substantial elevation in caspase activities relative to the control (p < 0.001), signifying their pro-apoptotic action. The DAN-Zn/MSN group significantly increased caspase activations compared to DAN alone (p < 0.001), indicating enhanced drug delivery. The FA-DAN-Zn/MSN formulation shows the greatest activation of caspase, indicating a significant rise relative to DAN-Zn/MSN (p < 0.001), hence confirming the importance of folic acid in promoting cellular uptake and inducing apoptosis. The activation of the caspase pathway, responsible for nuclear damage, is contingent upon two distinct pathways, one promoting cell death (proapoptotic pathway) and the other impeding cell death (anti-apoptotic pathway). The downregulation of Bcl-2 levels impedes its inhibition of Bax, leading to heightened caspase activity73. The increased Bax levels in the experimental groups, compared to the control group, resulted in significantly elevated Caspase-9 activity levels.

The activation of caspase-8 or caspase-9 triggers Caspase-3, disrupting crucial proteins in the cell and causing cell death. Measurement of caspase-3, caspase-8, and caspase-9 activities provides insights into the efficacy of cancer treatments74. Caspase-3 and caspase-8, besides inducing apoptosis, also play pivotal roles in invasion and metastasis. Recent studies have elucidated additional roles of caspase-3 in the development of new blood vessels in tumors through facilitating invasiveness, cell migration, and promoting the growth of cancer cells74,75.

Conclusion

In conclusion, mesoporous silica nanoparticles were used as a nanocarrier for the co-delivery of Daunorubicin and Zn2+ ions into breast cancer cells. The highest possible potential for drug loading and regulated drug release profiles was achieved because of electrostatic interactions among DAN molecules and amine groups on the exterior amine-functionalized Zn/MSN layer. It was found that surface functionalization with folic acid could make the nanocarrier selective towards MCF-7 breast cancer cells owing to the presence of folate receptors on the surface of these cancer cells. In addition, the as-synthesized nanoparticles (FA-DAN-Zn/MSN) showed high biocompatibility against MCF-10A cells, which are non-tumorigenic breast cells. Overall , the cytotoxicity and related assays reviewed in this study indicate that folic acid enhances therapeutic potency. However, future studies using techniques such as flow cytometry quantification or confocal microscopy are needed to further validate these observations and confirm the underlying mechanisms.

Furthermore, treatment with FA-DAN-Zn/MSN significantly influenced the mRNA expression of apoptosis- and metastasis-related genes in vitro. These initial in vitro results suggest FA-DAN-Zn/MSN as a promising nanocarrier for enhancing the in vitro anticancer activity of DAN in breast cancer cells and warrant further preclinical evaluation.

This study is limited to in vitro experiments, and therefore, the translational performance of the nanoplatform cannot be concluded at this stage. Future work will evaluate pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, tumor accumulation, and systemic toxicity in appropriate animal models to assess in vivo efficacy and safety.

Concentration and fluctuations of sine–Gordon measure around topological multi-soliton manifold
Kihoon Seong, Hao Shen, and Philippe Sosoe

Abstract
. We study the sine–Gordon measure defined on each homotopy class. The energy space decomposes into infinitely many such classes indexed by the topological degree Q ∈ ℤ Q\in\mathbb{Z} . Even though the sine–Gordon action admits no minimizer in homotopy classes with | Q | ≥ 2 |Q|\geq 2 , we prove that the Gibbs measure on each class nevertheless concentrates and exhibits Ornstein–Uhlenbeck fluctuations near the multi-soliton manifold in the joint low-temperature and infinite-volume limit. Furthermore, we show that the joint distribution of the multi-soliton centers coincides with the ordered statistics of independent uniform random variables, so that each soliton’s location follows a Beta distribution.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivations and implications of the main results

The sine–Gordon model plays a central role as a fundamental example of a nonlinear scalar field theory admitting
topological solitons
. In this paper, we specifically study the massless sine–Gordon field theory, with action

(1.1)

\displaystyle E(\phi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\cos\phi)dx.

This field theory allows the energy space to be classified into infinitely many disjoint homotopy classes according to the topological invariant
Q
, defined in (
1.2
). For any finite-energy configuration
E(\phi)<\infty
, the field must satisfy

\displaystyle\phi(\infty):=\lim_{x\to\infty}\phi(x)\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z},\qquad\phi(-\infty):=\lim_{x\to-\infty}\phi(x)\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

These boundary conditions imply that the map
x\mapsto e^{i\phi(x)}
winds around the target circle an integer number of times as
x
runs from
-\infty
to
\infty
. This integer
Q
defines the topological degree/charge or winding number

(1.2)

\displaystyle Q=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\phi(\infty)-\phi(-\infty))\in\mathbb{Z}.

According to the winding number
Q
, the energy space
\mathcal{C}
decomposes into disjoint connected components, referred to as homotopy classes or topological sectors
\mathcal{C}_{Q}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=\{\phi\in H_{\textup{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}):E(\phi)<\infty\}=\bigsqcup_{Q\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{C}_{Q},

where

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{Q}=\{\phi\in\mathcal{C}:(\phi(\infty)-\phi(-\infty))/2\pi=Q\}.

Within each sector
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
, fields can be continuously deformed into one another. However, a configuration in
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
cannot be continuously deformed into a configuration in
\mathcal{C}_{Q^{\prime}}
when
Q\neq Q^{\prime}
. Therefore, when studying the minimization problem for the energy functional
E(\phi)
, we fix a topological sector
\mathcal{C_{Q}}
and consider

\displaystyle\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}}E(\phi).

The following facts are well known:

(i)

Q=0
(vacuum sector): the minimizers are the vacuum states

\displaystyle\phi(x)=2\pi k,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(ii)

|Q|=1
(kink/antikink sector): the minimizers are the kinks when
Q=1
and the anti-kinks when
Q=-1
, unique up to translation symmetry

\displaystyle\{m(\cdot-\xi)\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\{m^{-}(\cdot-\xi)\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}},

where

\displaystyle m(x)=4\arctan(e^{x})\quad\textup{and}\quad m^{-}(x)=4\arctan(e^{-x}).

Thus, the family of minimizers forms a one-dimensional soliton manifold.
These topological solitons, kinks and antikinks, interpolate between the vacua
0
and
2\pi
.

(iii)

|Q|\geq 2
(higher-charge sectors): no minimizer exists. For higher charge, the lack of compactness prevents existence of a minimizer.

Although the higher-charge sectors
|Q|\geq 2
admit no minimizer, we nevertheless study, for each
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
, the concentration and fluctuation behavior of the Gibbs measure

The main results have the following implications:

(i)

The main theorem provides the first result on the concentration and fluctuation behavior of Gibbs measures around multi-solitons
\sum_{j=1}^{Q}m(\cdot-\xi_{j})
, rather than a single soliton. In particular, our result shows that even though the higher-charge sector

(ii)

At leading order, typical configurations under the Gibbs ensemble consist of exactly
|Q|

\big|\log\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big|

(iii)

Our base measure is the Brownian bridge (

This contrast is rather striking, since in many quantum field and statistical physics models, the fluctuations are typically governed by the underlying base field, rather than having a completely different covariance structure.

(iv)

The expected soliton centers

are evenly spaced, dividing the interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
into
|Q|+1
equal parts of length
\tfrac{2L_{\varepsilon}}{|Q|+1}

The remarkable point is that in previous works (see Subsections
1.3.2

1.3.3
,
1.3.4
, and
1.3.5
), the behavior of Gibbs measures was mainly studied around a single soliton, and most of the analysis in those works focused primarily on the concentration of the measure. On the other hand, our result is the first study of the Gibbs measure around
multi-soliton configurations
, involving not only concentration but also a central limit theorem behavior around the multi-soliton manifold. Furthermore, we provide a concrete description of the soliton locations and gaps.

Moreover, the geometry of the multi-soliton manifold
\{\sum_{j=1}^{Q}m(\cdot-\xi_{j}):\xi_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a central object of interest in this work. In contrast to the single-soliton manifold
\{m(\cdot-\xi):\xi\in\mathbb{R}\}
, the multi-soliton manifold fails to be differentiable and becomes merely a topological manifold when solitons collide, that is, when
|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\lesssim 1
. As a result, fundamental geometric objects such as tangent and normal vectors are no longer well defined in the collision regime. This geometric degeneracy requires a careful analysis and a delicate decomposition of the multi-soliton manifold. See Remark
4.1
.

We finally remark that, unlike in the one-dimensional setting where topological solitons are well understood (see Subsection
1.3.1
), the situation in two dimensions is quite different.
The sine–Gordon equation in 2D also admits soliton-like solutions, often called kink walls, obtained by extending the one-dimensional kink uniformly in another spatial direction.
These configurations solve the equation but necessarily have infinite energy, and thus are not finite-energy solitons in the usual sense. To the best of our knowledge, such infinite-energy kink-wall solutions are far less understood and have not been studied as systematically as their one-dimensional counterparts.

, which describes the asymptotic decomposition of solutions under deterministic dynamics, our results are probabilistic in nature. Under the Gibbs measure, we identify the typical locations of individual solitons and precisely characterize the gaps between neighboring solitons.

1.3.
Related literature

1.3.1.
Sine–Gordon field theory

McKean-Vaninsky
[
32
]
studied the construction of the one–dimensional sine Gordon measure. More recently, Lacoin–Rhodes–Vargas
[
27
]
studied the one–dimensional sine–Gordon measure with a log–correlated base field in the full subcritical regime on a bounded domain.

From the PDE perspective, the one-dimensional sine–Gordon equation and its soliton solutions have been widely studied in recent years. In particular, the asymptotic stability and long–time behavior of soliton solutions have attracted considerable attention. We refer to the works of McKean
[
30
]
, Lührmann–Schlag
[
28
]
, Alejo–Muñoz–Palacios
[
1
]
, Chen–Liu–Lu
[
14
]
, and Chen–Lührmann
[
15
]
.

The two-dimensional sine–Gordon theory has connections to various problems in statistical physics, such as the Coulomb gas and the XY model. We refer to
[
21
,
10
,
18
,
4
,
5
,
3
,
23
]
for the study of the 2D sine–Gordon measures and to
[
25
,
11
,
9
]
for the two-dimensional sine–Gordon equation with stochastic forcing.
In two dimensions, the sine–Gordon equation also admits soliton-like solutions, often called kink walls, obtained by extending the one-dimensional kink uniformly in another spatial direction.
Although these configurations solve the 2D sine–Gordon equation, they necessarily have infinite energy and hence fall outside the standard class of finite-energy solitons (see
[
29
, Chapter 5.4]
).
To the best of our knowledge, these infinite-energy kink-wall solutions are considerably less explored and have not been developed to the same extent as their one-dimensional counterparts.

1.3.2.
Continuum focusing NLS Gibbs measure and single-soliton manifold

The behavior of (invariant) Gibbs measures around a single soliton manifold has been studied for the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation. For the continuum focusing NLS Gibbs measure, McKean
[
31
]
initiated the study of the infinite-volume limit. Later, Rider
[
35
]
and Tolomeo–Weber
[
38
]
proved that, on the 1D torus, the measure concentrates around the single-soliton manifold in the infinite-volume limit. In particular, Tolomeo–Weber
[
38
]
identified a critical regime for the strength of the coupling: either the measure strongly concentrates around the single-soliton manifold, or the limiting measure reduces to the underlying Gaussian field. Recently, in
[
36
]
, the first and third authors proved a central limit theorem for the Gibbs measure around a single-soliton manifold. In this paper we establish the corresponding result for multi-soliton manifolds in the sine-Gordon setting. The two situations exhibit different fluctuation behavior: For the focusing NLS Gibbs measure, the fluctuations are white noise near a single soliton, but in the sine–Gordon case studied here, the system exhibits Ornstein–Uhlenbeck fluctuations near the multi-soliton manifold.

1.3.3.
Discrete focusing NLS Gibbs measure and single-soliton manifold

The discretized focusing Gibbs measure and the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation on a lattice have been studied. When
d\geq 3
, Chatterjee–Kirkpatrick
[
12
]
initiated the study of the discretized focusing NLS Gibbs measure, identified a critical temperature, and showed that below this threshold the system exhibits striking single-soliton–like behavior. In
[
13
]
Chatterjee used microcanonical invariant measures and showed that a typical function in the ensemble decomposes into a “visible” part, which is close to a single soliton, and an “invisible” part that is small in the
L^{\infty}
norm. Notice that, in particular, regarding the reason why a single-soliton profile appears, Chatterjee mentioned in
[
13
]
that “ whereas multisoliton solutions eventually merge into a single soliton on the finite discrete torus considered in Theorem 1”. In contrast to that situation, in our sine–Gordon model, the boundary conditions imposed by a fixed homotopy class (
1.3
) enforce the presence of multiple solitons, and therefore prevent the multi-soliton configuration from collapsing into a single soliton. Again, as emphasized above, to the best of our knowledge, our results are the first study of the concentration and fluctuations of Gibbs measures around multi-soliton manifold. In particular, a crucial aspect is to understand how solitons interact with each other on the collision scale
\big|\log\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big|
, and how the energy behaves at this scale, even though such configurations are not minimizers.

Regarding the phase transition of the discrete focusing NLS Gibbs measure, in
[
17
]
, for
d\geq 3
, Dey-Kirkpatrick-Krishnan identified a phase transition, analogous to the one found by Tolomeo–Weber
[
38
]
, on the lattice. Using two parameters, temperature and the strength of the nonlinearity, they proved the existence of a continuous phase transition curve that divides the parameter plane into two regions, the appearance or non-appearance of (single) solitons. In the recent work
[
26
]
, Krishnan and Ray further investigated the model using the two parameters. They proved that the three regions in the phase diagram lead to three distinct limits. A natural question is whether the discrete (or even continuous) sine–Gordon model, in one dimension or higher, also exhibits a phase transition depending on the temperature and the strength of the coupling constant.

1.3.4.
Gibbs measure for stochastic Allen–Cahn equation and single-soliton manifold

For the (invariant) Gibbs measure of the stochastic one-dimensional Allen–Cahn equation, Weber
[
40
]
proved that, in a joint low-temperature and infinite-volume limit, the measure concentrates on the single soliton manifold. Subsequently, Otto–Weber–Westdickenberg
[
34
]
studied the same limits, with
\varepsilon\to 0
and
L_{\varepsilon}\to\infty
, but identified the optimal scaling of the interval length
L_{\varepsilon}
by analyzing the competition between energy
\varepsilon\to 0
and entropy
L_{\varepsilon}\to\infty
. Under this optimal scaling, they again observed concentration of the measure around the single soliton manifold. Recently, Bertini–Buttà–Di Gesù
[
6
]
showed that, beyond the optimal length of the interval
L_{\varepsilon}
, the measure no longer concentrates, and the interfaces (the soliton centers
\xi_{j}
) become asymptotically distributed according to a Poisson point process.
It would be interesting to investigate the optimal length scale for which Theorems
1.2
,
1.4
, and
1.6
remain valid, and to understand what kinds of behavior occur beyond this optimal scale.

1.3.5.
Gibbs measure and topological solitons

We refer to the work of Bringmann
[
8
]
on topological solitons and Gibbs measures. Bringmann recently studied exterior equivariant wave maps with spatial domain
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,1)
, which admit topological solitons. In contrast to our setting, where the high charge sector
|Q|\geq 2
has no minimizer, the wave map system admits infinitely many topological sectors, and each sector possesses a unique minimizer. In
[
8
]
, Bringmann constructed the Gibbs measure on each homotopy class and proved the invariance of this measure under the corresponding PDE flow. Notice that the measure considered by Bringmann
[
8
, (1.10)]
takes a form similar to that of the sine–Gordon measure considered here (
1.5
), (
2.1
), where the base point is fixed, and the measure is defined on the fluctuation coordinate.

Finally, we remark that, as discussed by Manton–Sutcliffe
[
29
]
, there exist many models admitting infinitely many disjoint topological sectors, each supporting topological solitons, including the Abelian Higgs, Ginzburg–Landau, and Yang–Mills models. It would be natural to investigate whether the methods developed in this paper can be applied to study the concentration and fluctuations of Gibbs measures in these settings. The present work may be viewed as a first step toward such a program.

3.
Structure of multi–topological solitons

In this section, we study the properties of topological solitons and their superpositions forming multi-soliton configurations.
The key points are to understand how solitons interact with each other on the collision scale, and how the energy behaves even though these configurations are not minimizers.

,

\displaystyle E(\phi)\geq E_{\textup{kink}}|Q|=8|Q|,

where
E_{\textup{kink}}=E(m)=E(m^{-})=8
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle E(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}2\sin^{2}\frac{\phi}{2}dx\geq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\phi\sin\frac{\phi}{2}|dx

\displaystyle\geq 2\int_{C}f=\int_{0}^{2\pi|Q|}|\sin(z/2)|dz=8|Q|,

where
\int_{C}f
is a line integral,
C
is
the curve in
\mathbb{R}
going straight from
0
to
2\pi Q
,
and
f(z)=|\sin(z/2)|
is a function along
C
.
In the second line we have replaced the parametrization
\phi
of
C
by the identity parametrization
[0,2\pi Q]\to[0,2\pi Q]
and used the independence of line integrals on parametrizations.
∎

For
\xi_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\xi_{k}
, where
k=|Q|
, define the superposition of topological solitons, that is, the multi-soliton by

\displaystyle m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m(\cdot-\xi_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{\xi_{j}}

\displaystyle m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m^{-}(\cdot-\xi_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m^{-}_{\xi_{j}}.

In the following lemmas, we state the results only for the case
Q>0
, by symmetry.

We first prove an elementary inequality: for
\eta\in(0,1)
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-(|y-x|+|z-x|)}dx=(1+|y-z|)e^{-|y-z|}\leq\frac{1}{\eta}e^{\eta-1}e^{-(1-\eta)|y-z|}.

The equality follows by observing that the integral between
y
and
z
is equal to
|y-z|e^{-|y-z|}
, and the integral outside
is equal to
e^{-|y-z|}
.
For the inequality, we have

\displaystyle(1+|y-z|)e^{-|y-z|}\leq\Big(\sup_{r>0}(1+r)e^{-\eta r}\Big)e^{-(1-\eta)|y-z|}

and the function
(1+r)e^{-\eta r}
reaches maximum at
r=\frac{1}{\eta}-1
.

When the centers
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
are well separated, each kink
m_{\xi_{j}}
contributes its own

E_{\textup{kink}}
, and the overlap between kinks yields only exponentially small corrections, because each kink remains nearly constant (0 or
2\pi
) outside its center
\xi_{j}
. This is shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|Q|=k
. Then we have

\displaystyle E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}E(m_{\xi_{j}})+O(e^{-c\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|})

as
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\to\infty
, where
c>0
only depends on
|Q|
.

Proof.

The kinetic energy part is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{j}}|^{2}dx+\sum_{i<j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{i}}\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{j}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{j}}|^{2}dx+\sum_{i<j}O(e^{-c|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|})

by Lemma
3.1
(iii) and (
3.1
).

We now study the potential energy part.
Let
U(z)=1-\cos(z)
.
One has

|U(a+b)-U(a)-U(b)|=\Big|\int_{0}^{a}\int_{0}^{b}U^{\prime\prime}(s+t)dsdt\Big|\leq|a||b|

and by induction in
k
one has

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Big|U\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}U(a_{j})\Big|\leq\sum_{i<j}|a_{i}||a_{j}|.

Indeed, suppose that this holds for
k-1
. Then the left-hand side of
(
3.2
) is bounded by

\Big|U\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)-U\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a_{j}\Big)-U(a_{k})\Big|+\Big|U\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a_{j}\Big)+U(a_{k})-\sum_{j=1}^{k}U(a_{j})\Big|

and (
3.2
) follows by using the induction assumption.

Set
a_{j}=m_{\xi_{j}}(x)-2\pi 1_{x>\xi_{j}}
.
(Note that the shift
2\pi 1_{x>\xi_{j}}
does not change the value of
\cos
.)
By Lemma
3.1
(i)(ii),
|a_{j}|\leq 4e^{-|x-\xi_{j}|}
. The desired bound follows upon integrating over
x

again by (
3.1
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

According to the Bogomolny structure in Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, by choosing a minimizing sequence with infinite separation between centers, we can show that the minimal energy in each homotopy class
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
is

\displaystyle\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}}E(\phi)=|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}.

This shows that the infimum of the energy in the topological sector

\mathcal{C}_{Q}
is attained asymptotically by configurations consisting of

Q
kinks when
Q>0
or
|Q|
anti-kinks when
Q<0
, with infinite mutual separation
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\to\infty
.
However, the minimizing sequence has no convergent subsequence due to the infinite separation, which cannot be realized in practice. This is one of the reasons why there is no actual minimizer for
|Q|\geq 2
.

From Remark
3.5
, under the separation condition
m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is an almost minimizer on the homotopy class
\mathcal{C}_{Q}
(though it never attains the actual minimum). In the following lemma, we show that the first variation operator is close to zero.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|Q|=k
. There exists
0<\eta\ll 1
sufficiently small such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C^{\nabla}\frac{e^{-(1-\eta)d}}{1-e^{-(1-\eta)d}}

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}

where

d:=\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|
and

C^{\nabla}=(\frac{1}{2\eta}e^{\eta-1})^{1/2}Q
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\nabla E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})(x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x)+\sin m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x)=\sin m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\sin m_{\xi_{j}}(x)

where we used
\nabla E(m_{\xi_{j}})=-\partial_{x}^{2}m_{\xi_{j}}+\sin m_{\xi_{j}}=0
.
Note that (
3.2
) again holds with

U(z)=\sin(z)
. So

(3.3)

\displaystyle|\nabla E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})(x)|\leq\sum_{i<j}e^{-|x-\xi_{i}|}e^{-|x-\xi_{j}|}.

Regarding the
L^{2}
bound, note that by (
3.1
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\,(|x-\xi_{i}|+|x-\xi_{j}|)}dx\leq\frac{1}{2\eta}e^{\eta-1}e^{-2(1-\eta)|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|}

for small
\eta>0
. This implies

\|\nabla E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq(\frac{1}{2\eta}e^{\eta-1})^{1/2}\sum_{i<j}e^{-(1-\eta)|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|}.

One could estimate
\sum_{i<j}
by
Q^{2}
, but to be slightly sharper,
we assume without loss of generality that

\xi_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\xi_{k}
.
Then there are
Q-1
terms of the form

e^{-(1-\eta)|\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}|}
,
and
Q-2
terms of the form

e^{-(1-\eta)|\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+2}|}
which can be written as

e^{-(1-\eta)|\xi_{i}-\xi_{i+1}|-(1-\eta)|\xi_{i+1}-\xi_{i+2}|}
, etc.
Thus

\|\nabla E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq(\frac{1}{2\eta}e^{\eta-1})^{1/2}\sum_{k=1}^{Q-1}(Q-k)e^{-(1-\eta)kd}\leq(\frac{1}{2\eta}e^{\eta-1})^{1/2}Q\frac{e^{-(1-\eta)d}}{1-e^{-(1-\eta)d}}.

∎

The following lemma shows that if a field
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}
is far away from the multi-soliton manifold, then its energy is also far away from the minimal energy.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
Q\geq 2
and let
\delta>0
. Then there exists
c>0
, depending only on
Q
, such that if
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}
with
\phi(-\infty)=0
and
\phi(\infty)=2\pi Q
satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle\textup{dist}(\phi,\mathcal{M}^{Q}):=\inf_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\delta>0,

then

\displaystyle E(\phi)\geq\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}}E(\phi)+c\inf_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\geq\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}}E(\phi)+c\delta^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}
with
\phi(-\infty)=0
and
\phi(\infty)=2\pi Q
.
Define

t_{0}=-\infty,\quad t_{Q}=\infty,\quad t_{j}=\inf\{x:\phi(x)=2\pi j\}

for
1\leq j\leq Q-1
.
Then, define on each block
1\leq j\leq Q

(3.5)

\displaystyle\phi_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}\phi(x)\quad&x\in(t_{j-1},t_{j})\\
2\pi(j-1)\quad&x<t_{j-1}\\
2\pi j\quad&x>t_{j}.\end{cases}

Since
2\pi\mathbb{Z}
extension does not change the energy, we have, for
1\leq j\leq Q
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle E(\phi_{j})=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}|\partial_{x}\phi|^{2}dx+\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}(1-\cos\phi)dx.

Therefore, using (
3.6
)
and Lemma
3.2
-(2), we have

\displaystyle E(\phi)=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}E(\phi_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}E(\phi_{j}-2\pi(j-1))

(3.7)

\displaystyle\geq|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}+c\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\phi_{j}-2\pi(j-1))-m_{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

In the following, we show that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{Q}d_{j}^{2},

where
d_{j}:=\inf_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\|(\phi_{j}-2\pi(j-1))-m_{\xi}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
.
By the definition of
d_{j}
, for each
j
we can pick
\xi_{j}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
so that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|(\phi_{j}-2\pi(j-1))-m_{\xi_{j}^{*}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}<d_{j}^{2}+\varepsilon

for some small
\varepsilon>0
(later we let
\varepsilon\to 0
). Fix such a choice
(\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*})
from now on. By the definition of
\phi_{j}
, one can easily check that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}(\phi_{j}(x)-2\pi(j-1)).

This, together with (
3.9
), implies that

\displaystyle\inf_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{Q-1}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\|(\phi_{j}-2\pi(j-1))-m_{\xi_{j}^{*}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{Q-1}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}d_{j}^{2}+2^{Q-1}|Q|\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (
3.8
). Combining (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) yields

\displaystyle E(\phi)\geq|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}+c\inf_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Since
\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}}E(\phi)=|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}
from Remark
3.5
, we obtain the desired result.

3.7

Remark 3.9
.

In Lemma
3.7
, when
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
is negative with
Q\leq-2

m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{-}=m^{-}(\cdot-\xi_{1})+\dots+m^{-}(\cdot-\xi_{|Q|})

Recall from Remark
3.5

\inf_{\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}}E(\phi)

\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\to\infty

In the following lemma, we show that although a field
\phi

Before beginning the proof, we recall that
\mathcal{M}^{<d}_{Q}

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
Q\geq 2
, and let
d>0
be a large constant. Then there exists
c>0
such that if
\phi\in\mathcal{C}_{Q}
with

and

(3.10)

\displaystyle\textup{dist}(\phi,\mathcal{M}_{Q}^{<d})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}\\
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|<d\end{subarray}}\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\delta

where

\displaystyle E(\phi)\geq|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}+c\cdot e^{-d}.

Proof.

Since
\textup{dist}(\phi,\mathcal{M}_{Q}^{<d})<\delta
, we can find
\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}
such that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}^{*}-\xi_{j}^{*}|<d\quad\textup{and}\quad\|\phi-m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\delta.

Therefore, from Lemma
3.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(\phi)

\displaystyle=\big(E(\phi)-E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})\big)+E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})

\displaystyle\geq\big(E(\phi)-E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})\big)+|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}+c\cdot e^{-d}.

By denoting
\eta(x):=\phi(x)-m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}(x)

\displaystyle E(\phi)-E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}\partial_{x}\eta dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\eta|^{2}dx

(3.13)

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\cos(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}+\eta))dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1-\cos m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})dx.

Use Taylor on the cosine pointwise

\displaystyle\cos(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}(x)+\eta(x))

\displaystyle=\cos m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}(x)-\sin m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}(x)\cdot\eta(x)-\frac{1}{2}\cos\big(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}(x)+\theta(x)\eta(x)\big)\cdot\eta(x)^{2}

for some
\theta(x)\in(0,1)

3.14

3.13

\displaystyle E(\phi)-E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}\partial_{x}\eta dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\eta|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sin m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}\cdot\eta dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cos\big(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}+\theta\eta\big)\cdot\eta^{2}dx.

\displaystyle E(\phi)

\displaystyle-E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\nabla E(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}})\cdot\eta dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{x}\eta|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\cos\big(m_{\xi_{1}^{*},\dots,\xi_{Q}^{*}}+\theta\eta\big)\cdot\eta^{2}dx,

Remark 3.11
.

In Lemma
3.10
, when
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
is negative with

) with the multi-antikink manifold.

It remains to prove Lemma
3.12
, which was used in the proof of Lemma
3.10
. As alluded above, the key is to quantify the error, namely, how much the energy exceeds the minimal energy
in the collision regime. Unlike the previous results such as
Lemma
3.4
and Lemma
3.6
where kink interactions are weak when well-separated, here in the collision regime their interactions are stronger.
In order to better understand their interactions in this regime, our analysis will have a more “pointwise” (rather than
L^{2}
) flavor, and we will prove an exact
pointwise representation for the energy density gap.
From this representation we will be able to identify the leading order contribution to the energy gap.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
Q\geq 2
.
For
d>0
sufficiently large, there exists
c>0
such that
for all
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|<d
,
one has

\displaystyle E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}})\geq|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}+c\cdot e^{-d}.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
, we have two-sided exponential asymptotic tail:
for
x<\xi
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle 2e^{-|x-\xi|}\leq m_{\xi}(x)=4\arctan(e^{x-\xi})\leq 4e^{-|x-\xi|}.

Similar lower bound holds for
x>\xi
. Recall from the proof of Lemma
3.4

that

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}})-|Q|E_{\textup{kink}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{i<j}\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{i}}\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{j}}+1-Q-\cos\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{Q}m_{\xi_{j}}\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\cos(m_{\xi_{j}})\;dx.

It is elementary to check that
(let
\tan\theta=e^{x-\xi_{i}}

and use
\sin(2\theta)=\frac{2\tan\theta}{1+(\tan\theta)^{2}}
)

\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{i}}=\frac{4e^{x-\xi_{i}}}{1+e^{2(x-\xi_{i})}}=2\sin(2\arctan(e^{x-\xi_{i}}))=2\sin(m_{\xi_{i}}(x)/2).

We claim that

\displaystyle F_{Q}

\displaystyle(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q},x)

\displaystyle:=4\sum_{i<j}\sin(m_{\xi_{i}}(x)/2)\sin(m_{\xi_{j}}(x)/2)+1-Q-\cos\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{Q}m_{\xi_{j}}(x)\Big)+\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\cos(m_{\xi_{j}}(x))\geq 0

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and all
\xi_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\xi_{Q}
.
In other words the energy density of

m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{Q}}
is pointwisely greater than the sum of the energy densities of the corresponding single kinks.

To prove the claim, we first note that
F_{1}=0
.
Suppose we have proved that
F_{Q-1}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q-1},x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

and all
\xi_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\xi_{Q-1}
,
and we now prove this for
F_{Q}
.
Consider the case
x\leq\xi_{Q}
(the other possibility is
x\geq\xi_{1}
which is similar).
It suffices to prove

F_{Q}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q},x)\geq F_{Q}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q-1},\infty,x)

since the right-hand side is equal to
F_{Q-1}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q-1},x)
,
noting that
m_{\infty}(x)=0
.
To this end,
writing
m_{j}=m_{\xi_{j}}(x)/2
, one has

\displaystyle F_{Q}

\displaystyle(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q},x)-F_{Q}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{Q-1},\infty,x)

\displaystyle=\Big(4\sin m_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{Q-1}\sin m_{j}-\cos(2m_{Q}+2A)+\cos(2m_{Q})\Big)-\Big(\cos 2A-1\Big)

\displaystyle=4\sin m_{Q}\sum_{j=1}^{Q-1}\sin m_{j}-2\sin^{2}m_{Q}+2\sin(m_{Q}+2A)\sin(m_{Q})

\displaystyle=4\sin m_{Q}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{Q-1}\sin m_{j}+\cos(m_{Q}+A)\sin A\Big)

where
A:=\sum_{j=1}^{Q-1}m_{j}
and we have used sum-to-product trigonometric identities. Note that
\sum_{j=1}^{Q-1}\sin m_{j}\geq|\sin(\sum_{j=1}^{Q-1}m_{j})|

for any
m_{1},\cdots,m_{Q-1}\in[0,\pi]

4.
Geometry of the multi-soliton manifold

4.1.
Approximate multi-soliton manifold

In the previous section, we studied the multi-solitons defined on
\mathbb{R}
and their properties. When restricted to the interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
, where
L_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}\to\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we define an approximating soliton

m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}
and multi-solitons
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
, which becomes an increasingly accurate approximation of the soliton
m_{\xi}
and the multi-soliton
m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
on
\mathbb{R}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Define
m^{\varepsilon}
to be a smooth, monotone function that coincides with
m

on
[-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta},\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}]
, where
\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}\ll L_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}
, and is extended to the constants
0
and
2\pi
outside a slightly larger interval:

\displaystyle m^{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}m(x),\;&x\in[-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta},\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}],\\
2\pi,\;&x\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}+1,\\
0,\;&x\leq-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}-1.\end{cases}

On the transition intervals
[\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta},\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}+1]
and
[-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}-1,-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}]
, we require

\displaystyle m(x)\leq m^{\varepsilon}(x)\leq 2\pi\quad\textup{and}\quad m(x)\geq m^{\varepsilon}(x)\geq 0.

Define the translated soliton

(4.1)

\displaystyle m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi}(x):=m^{\varepsilon}(x-\xi),

for
\xi\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]
, where

(4.2)

\displaystyle\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}:=L_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}-1=L_{\varepsilon}(1-\varepsilon^{\eta}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}-\eta})\sim L_{\varepsilon}.

This choice of
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
ensures that the entire transition region of the kink
m_{\xi}^{\varepsilon}
(from
0
to
2\pi
) remains inside the interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
. Note that one can easily check that, for any
1\leq p\leq\infty

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|m_{\xi}-m_{\xi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}m_{\xi}-\partial_{x}m_{\xi}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}.

Therefore, as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
m_{\xi}^{\varepsilon}
becomes a more and more precise approximation of the topological soliton
m_{\xi}
on
\mathbb{R}
. We now define the multi-soliton, given by the superposition of single solitons

(4.5)

\displaystyle m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{j}},

where
\xi_{j}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]
. When
k=Q
with
\xi_{j}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]
, this definition is enough for the multi-soliton
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
to do the transition from
0
to
2\pi Q
on the interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
. From the single-soliton bounds (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we immediately get the same type of exponential approximation for the multi-soliton

(4.6)

\displaystyle\|m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}-m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim ke^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}-\partial_{x}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim ke^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}},

where
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
\xi_{j}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]
.
Thanks to (
4.6
) and (

), we can transfer all lemmas in Section

from the multi-soliton

to the approximating multi-soliton

With the definition of the multi-soliton in (

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon}:=\big\{m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}:-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\leq\xi_{1}\leq\dots\leq\xi_{k}\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\big\},

where
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
is defined as in (

\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}_{k}
is a manifold of dimension

. Inside the multi-soliton manifold
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}_{k}
, we further decompose into the collision region

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,<d}_{k}:=\Big\{m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}:-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\leq\xi_{1}\leq\dots\xi_{k}\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\;\;\textup{and}\;\;\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|<d\Big\},

where the solitons interact with each other at distances less than

, and the non-collision region

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}:

\displaystyle=\{m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}:-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\leq\xi_{1}\leq\dots\xi_{k}\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\quad\textup{and}\quad\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d\},

where the interaction between solitons is negligible as
d\to\infty
.
Note that
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
is a closed subset of the compact set
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}_{k}
, and therefore
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
is also compact.

4.2.
Tangent and normal spaces of the manifold and disintegration formula

In this subsection, we study the geometry of the multi-soliton manifold
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
, its tangent and normal spaces, and a related disintegration formula.

Remark 4.1
.

Notice that the multi-soliton manifold
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon}
and the collision manifold
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon,<d}
, defined in (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), are topological manifolds but fail to be differentiable manifolds for
k\geq 3
due to the presence of collision strata.

For example, when
k=3
, the parameter space
\{\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2}\leq\xi_{3}\}

has two codimension-one faces, given by
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}
. Each point on these faces admits a neighborhood that is locally diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. However, there is also an edge corresponding to
\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}
and each point on this edge has a neighborhood that is locally diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
is the quadrant with a corner, which is homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to the half-plane,
\mathcal{M}_{k}
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon,<d}
fail to be a differentiable manifold (or a differentiable manifold with boundary),
although they are topological manifolds with boundaries. For
k>3
, the geometry is more complicated, as
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon,<d}
decompose into strata of different codimensions. In particular, understanding the structure of the collision manifold is of independent interest from a geometric perspective.

Therefore, in order to carry out Riemannian geometric considerations such as defining tangent and normal vectors, we restrict our analysis to the smooth non-collision manifold
\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}
by removing the collision region
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|<d
.

At each point
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
on the multi-soliton manifold
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
, we define the normal space to the manifold as follows

(4.11)

\displaystyle V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\big\{v\in L^{2}:\;\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle=0\quad\mbox{for all }1\leq j\leq k\big\}.

Here the normal space
V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
at
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is a subspace of codimension
k
in
L^{2}
, orthogonal to the tangent vectors

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{1}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},\dots,\partial_{\xi_{k}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}

of the multi-soliton manifold
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon}_{k}
.
Thanks to Lemma
3.1
-(iii), each tangent vector
\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is highly localized around its center
\xi_{j}
with an exponentially decaying tail. Therefore,

(4.12)

\displaystyle|\langle\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},\partial_{\xi_{i}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle|\lesssim e^{-c|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|}

for

. This implies that the tangent vectors are almost orthogonal when they do not collide, that is, when
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\to\infty

We are now ready to define the projection map

onto the multi-soliton manifold, introduced in Theorem

be a compact manifold in a Hilbert space

. If

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}:

\displaystyle=\{m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}:-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\leq\xi_{1}\leq\dots\xi_{k}\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\quad\textup{and}\quad\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d\}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\text{dist}(\phi,\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\leq\xi_{1}\leq\dots\xi_{k}\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}\\

For sufficiently large

W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}
denotes the Weingarten map, defined in (
4.20
). In addition,
d\sigma
is the surface measure on the manifold, parametrized by
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
, as defined in (
4.19
).

Proof.

The formula (
4.16
) follows from
[
19
, Lemma 3]
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

The disintegration formula has been widely employed in recent works
[
33
,
36
,
22
]
in settings where the energy functional admits explicit minimizers and the analysis is carried out around a single-soliton manifold. However, to the best of our knowledge, in Proposition
7.4
this is the first time the disintegration formula is applied in a regime where no minimizers exist and the analysis is performed around a multi-soliton manifold.

Note that the geometry of the smooth soliton manifold
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
is reflected in the surface measure
d\sigma
and the Weingarten map
W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}
. The orthonormal vectors
t_{j}=t_{j}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})
, for
j=1,\dots,k
, are obtained by applying the Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization procedure in
L^{2}
to the tangent vectors

\displaystyle\big\{\partial_{\xi_{1}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},\dots,\partial_{\xi_{k}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\big\}

as follows

(4.18)

\displaystyle t_{j}:=\frac{w_{j}}{\|w\|_{L^{2}}}\qquad\textup{and}\qquad w_{j}:=\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}-\sum_{\ell<j}\langle\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}}\;t_{\ell}.

Then, the surface measure
d\sigma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})
is given by

(4.19)

\displaystyle d\sigma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})=|\gamma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})|d\xi_{1}\dots d\xi_{k},

where

\displaystyle\gamma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})=\det\begin{pmatrix}\langle\partial_{\xi_{1}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}&\cdots&\langle\partial_{\xi_{k}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle\partial_{\xi_{1}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}&\cdots&\langle\partial_{\xi_{k}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Indeed, thanks to (
4.12
), the tangent vectors
\{\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\}_{j=1}^{k}
are almost orthogonal, with an error of order
e^{-d}
, where
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d
. Hence, the Jacobian matrix is nearly diagonal, with diagonal entries given by
\|\partial_{x}m\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}
,

\displaystyle|\gamma(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})|=k\|\partial_{x}m\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}(1+O(e^{-cd})),

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots\xi_{k}
satisfying
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d\to\infty
.

In (
4.17
) the Weingarten map
W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}
encodes the curvature of the surface
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
by capturing how the normal vector
v
changes direction as we move along different tangent directions on the surface.
More precisely, the Weingarten map
W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}=-dN_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(v)
at a point
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\in\mathcal{M}^{k,\varepsilon}
, defined via the differential of the Gauss map
N_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
at
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\in\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
, is the linear map

(4.20)

\displaystyle W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}:T_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}\to T_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k},

where
T_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}:=\text{span}\big\{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\big\}
is the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
at
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
and
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
are obtained from the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization described above (see (
4.18
)). Specifically, the Weingarten map
W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}
in the basis
\{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\langle-\partial_{\xi_{1}}N_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(v),t_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}&\cdots&\langle-\partial_{\xi_{1}}N_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(v),t_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle-\partial_{\xi_{k}}N_{\xi_{1},\dots\xi_{k}}(v),t_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}&\cdots&\langle-\partial_{\xi_{k}}N_{\xi_{1},\dots\xi_{k}}(v),t_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\end{pmatrix},

where

L^{2}\ni v\mapsto N_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(v)

is a parametrization of the normal space to
\mathcal{M}^{\varepsilon,\geq d}_{k}
at a point
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
. In particular, the
k\times k
determinant

(4.21)

\displaystyle\det\big(\Id-W_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k},v}\big)=1+O\big(\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{k}\big)

is a
k
-th order function of
v
.

Before concluding this subsection, we present the following lemma, which will be used later.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
be obtained by applying the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization in
L^{2}
to
\partial_{\xi_{1}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},\dots,\partial_{\xi_{k}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
, as described in (
4.18
). Then, for each
j
, we have

\displaystyle|t_{j}(x)|\lesssim e^{-c|x-\xi_{j}|}

provided that
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d
for
d
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Note that
t_{1}
is localized at
\xi_{1}
with an exponentially decaying tail. Inductively assume
t_{\ell}
are localized at
\xi_{\ell}
for
\ell<j
. Then
|\langle\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{\ell}\rangle|\lesssim e^{-c|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|}
and so

\displaystyle|\langle\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{\ell}\rangle t_{\ell}(x)|\lesssim e^{-c|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|}e^{-c_{1}|x-\xi_{\ell}|}\lesssim e^{-c\min_{j\neq\ell}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|}.

This, together with (
4.18
), implies that

\displaystyle|w_{j}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq|\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x)|+\sum_{\ell<j}|\langle\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},t_{\ell}\rangle_{L^{2}}||t_{\ell}(x)|\lesssim e^{-c|x-\xi_{j}|}

as
\min_{j\neq\ell}|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|\geq d\to\infty
.
∎

5.
Gaussian measures associated with Schrödinger operators

In this section, we study the linearized operator around the multi-soliton configuration. For well-separated centers
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
satisfying

\displaystyle\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}\to\infty

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the Hessian of the energy at
m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{\xi_{j}}
is

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}^{2}+\cos(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})

(5.1)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\textup{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})+O(e^{-c\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|}),

where the second equality follows from the well-separated condition
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}\to\infty
and the structure of the multi-soliton. Note that the error term is exponentially small in the minimal separation
d_{\varepsilon}
and uniform in all
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
with
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}
. In Section
7
,
\nabla^{2}E(m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})
plays the role of the covariance operator for a new Gaussian measure.

We begin by recalling the following well-known spectral properties of the linearized operator
\nabla^{2}E(m_{\xi})=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
around the single kink

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+\cos(m_{\xi})=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi),

where
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
. The potential is of the reflectionless Pöschl–Teller type.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.

(1)

The linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
, defined in (
5.2
), is self-adjoint. Its spectrum is given by

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\xi})=\sigma_{d}\cup\sigma_{c}=\{0\}\cup[1,\infty).

(2)

The eigenfunction corresponding to the zero eigenvalue arises from the tangent vector
\partial_{\xi}m_{\xi}
to the soliton manifold
\{m(\cdot-\xi)\}_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}
, which is associated with translation invariance

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(\partial_{\xi}m_{\xi})=0.

(3)

The linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}
satisfies the following coercivity: there exists
\lambda_{0}\in[1,\infty)
such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v,v\rangle\geq\lambda_{0}\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

for every
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\langle v,\partial_{\xi}m_{\xi}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=0
.

Proof.

For the proof, see
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
.
∎

We denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j}).

Note that the tangent vectors with respect to the center coordinates are

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}=-2\text{sech}(\cdot-\xi_{j}),\quad j=1,\dots,k.

This implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})

\displaystyle=(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j}))\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}+4\sum_{i\neq j}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{i})\text{sech}(\cdot-\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=4\sum_{i\neq j}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{i})\text{sech}(\cdot-\xi_{j}),

where we used
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}})=0
from (
5.3
). Hence, the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}),\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=-8\sum_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\text{sech}^{2}(x-\xi_{i})\text{sech}^{2}(x-\xi_{j})dx

\displaystyle=-O(e^{-\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|}),

In the second line, we used the fact that
\text{sech}(\cdot-\xi_{i})
is localized around
\xi_{i}
with exponentially decaying tails.
Consequently,
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
has
k
near-zero (negative) eigenvalues of size
O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}})
, where
d_{\varepsilon}=\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|
.

The next lemma shows that if the centers of the multi-soliton
m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
are sufficiently separated, then the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is uniformly coercive on the normal space, that is, after projecting off the tangent vectors.

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists
\lambda_{0},d_{0}>0
such that, under the separation condition
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{0}
, the coercivity

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\lambda_{0}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

holds for every
v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=0
,
j=1,\dots,k
.

Proof.

Choose a smooth partition of unity such that
\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\chi_{j}^{2}=1
and
\sup_{j}\|\partial_{x}\chi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim d_{\varepsilon}^{-1}
, where
d_{\varepsilon}=\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\to\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. For
j=1,\dots,k
,
\chi_{j}
is supported in a fixed neighborhood of
\xi_{j}
, and
\chi_{0}
,
\chi_{k+1}
cover the left/right far field (vacua
0
and
2\pi Q
). Then, we have

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\chi_{j}v),\chi_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-\sum_{j}\|(\partial_{x}\chi_{j})v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\chi_{j}v),\chi_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-Cd_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the term
\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|(\partial_{x}\chi_{j})v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
comes from expanding the kinetic term after applying a partition of unity. We analyze each localized piece to obtain a uniform coercivity estimate. Note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\chi_{j}v)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}(\chi_{j}v)-2\sum_{i:i\neq j}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{i})\chi_{j}v

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}(\chi_{j}v)+O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}})\cdot\chi_{j}v,

where
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
. This implies that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\chi_{j}v),\chi_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}(\chi_{j}v),\chi_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-Ce^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}\|\chi_{j}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We now show that
\chi_{j}v
is almost orthogonal to
\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}
if
\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle=0
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{j}v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-\sum_{i:i\neq j}\langle v,\chi_{i}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i:i\neq j}\langle v,\chi_{i}(\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

where we used
\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m\rangle=\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle=0
. Since
\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}
is localized around
\xi_{j}
and has exponentially decaying tail, we obtain almost orthogonality

(5.7)

\displaystyle|\langle\chi_{j}v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{j}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}|\lesssim\sum_{i:i\neq j}e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}\|\chi_{i}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining the almost-orthogonality (
5.7
) with the coercivity estimate (
5.4
) for the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}
around the single kink
m_{\xi_{j}}
, we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}(\chi_{j}v),\chi_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\|\chi_{j}v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-Ce^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i:i\neq j}\|\chi_{i}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for some
\lambda_{0}>0
. Putting the pieces together with (
5.5
), (
5.6
), (
5.8
), and taking
d_{\varepsilon}\geq d_{0}
large enough, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\chi_{j}v),\chi_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-Cd_{\varepsilon}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\chi_{j}v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-C(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}+d_{\varepsilon}^{2})\|v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4}\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.

∎

From now on, we study the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})

on the finite interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
d_{0}>0
be as in Lemma
5.2
. Then there exits
L_{0},\zeta>0
such that if
L_{\varepsilon}\geq L_{0}
and
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{0}
,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}v,v\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}])}\geq\zeta\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}])}

holds for every
v\in H^{1}_{0}([-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}])
with
\langle v,\partial_{\xi_{j}}m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}])}=0
,
j=1,\dots,k
. Here
\zeta
is independent of
L_{\varepsilon},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
.

Proof.

Assume not. Then there exist sequences
L_{n}\to\infty
, configurations
\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}
with
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}^{n}-\xi_{j}^{n}|\geq d_{0}
, and
v_{n}\in H^{1}_{0}([-L_{n},L_{n}])
with
\langle v_{n},\partial_{\xi_{j}^{n}}m_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{n},L_{n}])}=0
such that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{n},L_{n}])}\leq\frac{1}{n}\|v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-L_{n},L_{n}])}.

We extend
v_{n}
by zero outside
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
and denote the resulting function by
\widetilde{v}_{n}
. Then
\widetilde{v}_{n}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and

(5.9)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}\widetilde{v}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{n},L_{n}])}\leq\frac{1}{n}\|v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([-L_{n},L_{n}])}=\frac{1}{n}\|\widetilde{v}_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

where we used the Dirichlet boundary condition. Since

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{v}_{n},\partial_{\xi_{j}^{n}}m_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\langle v_{n},\partial_{\xi_{j}^{n}}m_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{n},L_{n}])}=0

for
j=1,\dots,k
, Lemma
5.2
implies that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}^{n},\dots,\xi_{k}^{n}}\widetilde{v}_{n},\widetilde{v}_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\geq\lambda_{0}\|\widetilde{v}_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Combining (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) gives a contradiction for large
n
.
Hence, a uniform
\zeta>0
exists.

∎

According to Lemma
5.3
, to avoid zero eigenvalues, we need to project off the tangential directions. Once the zero modes are removed, we can invert the operator on the subspace, namely, the normal space
V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
. We define the projected operator

(5.11)

\displaystyle C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})\big)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}},

viewed as an operator on the finite interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
, with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Here the projection
\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}
is given by

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}=\Id-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle\cdot,t_{j}\rangle t_{j}=\Id-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf{P}_{j}

where
t_{1},\dots,t_{k}
are obtained by applying the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization to the tangent vectors
\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
,
j=1,\dots,k
, as described above (
4.18
), and

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{j}=\langle\cdot,t_{j}\rangle t_{j}.

As mentioned in Section
2
, the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is understood as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}([-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}])}
unless otherwise specified.

We are now ready to define the Gaussian measure with the covariance operator
C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exist sufficiently large
L_{0}
,
d_{0}>0
such that if
L_{\varepsilon}\geq L_{0}
and
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{0}
, we can define the Gaussian measure

\displaystyle d\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=Z_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{-1}v,v\rangle}\prod_{x\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}dv(x).

for any fixed
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
.

Proof.

The operator

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})

considered on the finite interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
, is a self-adjoint Sturm–Liouville operator since
2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
is a Schwartz function. Hence, the operator has purely discrete spectrum
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n}
with
\lambda_{n}\sim|n|^{2}
as
n\to\infty
. In particular, if the operator is strictly positive, its inverse belongs to the trace class.
By Lemma
5.3
, the projected operator is strictly positive, hence its inverse on that subspace is trace class. Therefore, the corresponding Gaussian measure is well defined.

∎

Before concluding this subsection, we present the structure of the partition function of the Gaussian measure in Lemma
5.4
. As the interaction between solitons becomes negligible, the spectrum of the multi-well operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
becomes exactly
k
copies of the single-well spectrum.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
Q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|Q|=k
and
d_{\varepsilon}=|\log(\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})|
. Then we have

\displaystyle Z_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=(Z_{0})^{k}(1+O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}})),

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
satifying
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}
, where
Z_{0}
is the partition function of the Gaussian measure associated with the single-well operator
\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-\textup{sech}^{2}(x)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-\textup{sech}^{2}(x)
acts on the normal space and is a Sturm–Liouville operator on the compact interval
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]
, it has a discrete spectrum

(5.13)

\displaystyle 1\leq\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\dots<\lambda_{\ell}<\dots\to\infty.

Let
\{\phi\}_{\ell\geq 1}
be an orthonormal eigenbasis of
\mathcal{L}_{0}
on the normal space with eigenvalues
\lambda_{\ell}\geq 1
, thanks to Lemma
5.1
. Since
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is a bounded perturbation of
-\partial_{x}^{2}+1
by

\textup{sech}^{2}
, we can show that

(5.14)

\displaystyle|\phi_{\ell}(x)|+|\partial_{x}\phi_{\ell}(x)|\lesssim e^{-c|x|}.

For each
\xi_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,k
, we define

\displaystyle\phi_{\ell,j}(x):=\phi_{\ell}(x-\xi_{j}).

We can easily verify that
\phi_{\ell,j}
is an eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{j}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-\textup{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
with the same eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell}
. Furthermore the translated eigenfunctions
\phi_{\ell,j}
act as approximate eigenfunctions for
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\textup{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
as follows

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\ell,j},\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\phi_{\ell,j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{\ell,j},(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\textup{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j}))\phi_{\ell,j}\rangle-2\sum_{i\neq j}\int\textup{sech}^{2}(x-\xi_{i})|\phi_{\ell,j}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{\ell}+O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}))\|\phi_{\ell,j}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used (
5.14
).
Hence, every single-well level
\lambda_{\ell}
generates a cluster of
k
nearby eigenvalues
\lambda_{\ell,1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell,k}
of the multiwell operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1}\dots,\xi_{k}}

(5.15)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\ell,j}=\lambda_{\ell,j}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})=\lambda_{\ell}+O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}),\quad j=1,\dots,k.

By using the fact that
\{\phi_{\ell,j}\}_{j=1,\dots,k}
is an almost-orthonormal family when
d_{\varepsilon}
is sufficiently large, and there exists a single-well spectral gap
\lambda_{{}_{\ell}+1}-\lambda_{\ell}>0
in (
5.13
), we can easily check that each cluster has exactly
k
members and the clusters are disjoint.

To describe all discrete eigenvalues for the multi-well operator
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
, we take the product over all
\ell\geq 1
and use (
5.15
). More precisely, for any finite
N
,

\displaystyle\textup{det}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\mathbf{P}_{N})

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_{\ell,j}\bigg)=\bigg(\prod_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}\bigg)^{k}(1+O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}}))

\displaystyle=\textup{det}_{V_{0}}(\mathbf{P}_{N}\mathcal{L}_{0}\mathbf{P}_{N})^{k}(1+O(e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}})),

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
satifying
\min_{i\neq j}|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}
, where
\mathbf{P}_{N}
is the finite-dimensional projection onto modes with
|\ell|\leq N
.

∎

Remark 7.2

8.
Analysis of the Gaussian measure with Schrödinger operator

In this section, our goal is to establish the correlation decay and
the extreme value for the Gaussian measure
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
on the normal space, with the covariance operator

(8.1)

\displaystyle C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})\big)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}},

Remark 8.1
.

In this section, we study a joint limit in which

simultaneously, leading to a competition between energetic and entropic effects. Moreover, in our setting the relevant covariance operator (

8.1.
Correlation decay

In this subsection, we study the decay of correlations for the Gaussian measure with covariance operator

Notice that the operator

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y):=\langle\delta_{x},C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\delta_{y}\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[v(x)v(y)\big]

[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]

with Dirichlet boundary conditions on
[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]

In particular, the influence of the boundary diminishes exponentially fast as one moves into the interior, and the field in the bulk behaves almost like the infinite-volume Ornstein–Uhlenbeck field. The following proposition shows that the Green function

,

Lemma 8.2
.

Let

\min_{\ell\neq i}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{i}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}=\big|\log\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big|

(i)

Bulk regime: Let

Proof.

(8.3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{j}=\langle\cdot,t_{j}\rangle t_{j}.

Here

\partial_{\xi_{j}}m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}

By expanding the projection operator
\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}

\displaystyle=(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}

We study each term separately. We denote by

\displaystyle\big\langle\delta_{x},\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}\delta_{y}\big\rangle=t_{i}(x)t_{j}(y)\big\langle t_{i},(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}t_{j}\big\rangle.

\displaystyle\leq e^{-c|x-y|}.

\mathbf{P}_{j}

\displaystyle\big\langle\delta_{x},(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}\delta_{y}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=t_{j}(y)\big\langle\delta_{x},(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}t_{j}\big\rangle=t_{j}(y)\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\text{OU}}(x,z)t_{j}(z)dz,

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{1}+\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{2}.

. Hence, by combining (

), (

), and (

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\text{OU}}(x,z)t_{j}(z)dz\bigg|\lesssim e^{-c|x-\xi_{j}|}

as

. Then, it follows from (

As for the projected term

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\delta_{x},\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\delta_{y}\big\rangle\big|\lesssim e^{-c|x-y|}.

It follows from (

), (

), (

We are now ready to prove the main part of this subsection. Recall the definition of the Green function corresponding to the covariance operator

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
L_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}
and
\min_{\ell\neq i}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{i}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}=\big|\log\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big|
.

(i)

Bulk regime: Let
|x|,|y|\leq L_{\varepsilon}-|\log\varepsilon|
and
|\xi_{j}|\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
,
j=1,\dots,k
, where
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
is defined in
(
4.2
). Then

\displaystyle|G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)-ce^{-|x-y|}|\lesssim e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|x|,|y|\})},

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where the exponentially small error term is independent of the tangential modes
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}
satisfying
|\xi_{j}|\leq\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
.

(ii)

Diagonal regime: for any
x\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]

(8.16)

\displaystyle|G_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}(x,x)|\lesssim 1,

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]

Proof.

Using the resolvent identity, we view the Schrödinger operator

\displaystyle C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})\big)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}

as a perturbation of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator
\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}
as follows

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)+(G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}WG^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})(x,y)

(8.17)

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)+(G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}WG^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})(x,y),

where
W(z)=-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
. From (
8.17
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)W_{j}(z)G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz

(8.18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)W_{j}(z)G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz,

where
W_{j}(z)=\text{sech}^{2}(z-\xi_{j})
. Note that the equality (
8.18
) holds after summing over
j
, although the individual components do not commute. Indeed, by Lemma
8.4
, we have

(8.19)

\displaystyle\big\|\big(G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\big)-\big(G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c\min_{\ell\neq i}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{i}|},

where
\min_{\ell\neq i}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{i}|\geq\big|\log\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}\big|
.

Using Lemma
8.2
, under the bulk condition
|x|,|y|\leq L_{\varepsilon}-|\log\varepsilon|
we write

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)

\displaystyle=e^{-|x-z|}+O(e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|x|,|z|\})})

(8.20)

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)

\displaystyle=e^{-|y-z|}+O(e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|y|,|z|\})}).

Following the error estimates in (
8.10
) and (
8.12
), and using (
8.20
),
|W_{j}(z)|\lesssim e^{-|z-\xi_{j}|}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)W_{j}(z)G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz\bigg|

(8.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\lambda|x-z|}e^{-\lambda|z-\xi_{j}|}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz+e^{-c|\log\varepsilon|}

for some
\lambda>0
, and

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)W_{j}(z)G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz\bigg|

(8.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)e^{-\lambda|z-\xi_{j}|}e^{-\lambda|y-z|}dz+e^{-c|\log\varepsilon|}.

We proceed by considering two cases:
|y-\xi_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
and
|y-\xi_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
. In the former case, we use (
8.21
), while in the latter we use (
8.22
).

Case 1
: if
|y-\xi_{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
, then

\displaystyle\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\lambda|x-z|}e^{-\lambda|z-\xi_{j}|}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(|x-y|-|y-\xi_{j}|-|z-\xi_{j}|)}e^{-\lambda|z-\xi_{j}|}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz

(8.23)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}|x-y|}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}|z-\xi_{j}|}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz.

Denoting by
u_{f}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}f)
the solution to the elliptic equation associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
, the elliptic regularity theorem yields

(8.24)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}^{2}u_{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for any
f\in L^{2}
. By using the Sobolev embedding and (
8.24
), we have that for
f=e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}|z-\xi_{j}|}
,

(8.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}|z-\xi_{j}|}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{f}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}}\leq C\|u_{f}\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}f\|_{L^{2}}.

Combining (
8.21
), (
8.23
) and (
8.25
) yields

(8.26)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)W_{j}(z)G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz\bigg|\lesssim e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}|x-y|}.

Case 2
: if
|y-\xi_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
, then

\displaystyle\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)e^{-\lambda|z-\xi_{j}|}e^{-\lambda|y-z|}dz

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)e^{-\lambda|z-\xi_{j}|}e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}(|y-\xi_{j}|-|z-\xi_{j}|)}dz

(8.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}|x-y|}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}|z-\xi_{j}|}dz.

Using the elliptic regularity theorem (
8.24
), and then following (
8.25
), we obtain

(8.28)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}|z-\xi_{j}|}dz\bigg|\lesssim 1.

Combining (
8.22
), (
8.27
) and (
8.28
) yields

(8.29)

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)W_{j}(z)G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)dz\bigg|\lesssim e^{-\frac{\lambda}{4}|x-y|}.

We conclude the case study.

We now go back to the resolvent identity (
8.17
)

(8.30)

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}(G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})(x,y).

If

(8.31)

\displaystyle|G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)|\lesssim e^{-c|x-y|}.

Hence, combining (

(ii), we have that for any

Before concluding this subsection, we present the proof of the following lemma under the separation condition.

Lemma 8.4
.

Under the separation condition
\min_{\ell\neq i}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{i}|\geq d_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\big(G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\big)-\big(G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c\min_{\ell\neq i}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{i}|}\to 0,

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
W_{j}=\textup{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)=\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})\big)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(x,y).

Using the resolvent identity, we write

(8.33)

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}(x,y)-G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}\Big(\sum_{\ell\neq j}W_{\ell}\Big)G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y),

where

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}(x,y)=\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1-2\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})\big)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(x,y).

Again, using the single–bump resolvent identities

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}+G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}+G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}},

we have

(8.34)

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}=G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}.

Inserting the resolvent identity (
8.33
) into both
G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
and
G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
and using (
8.34
), we obtain

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\neq j}\Big(G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}W_{\ell}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{\ell}G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\Big)

(8.35)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\neq j}(T_{j\ell}+T_{\ell j}).

By Combes–Thomas bounds for 1D Schrödinger resolvents with a positive mass term, for any bounded
V
such that the inverse
(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1+V)^{-1}
exists, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{1}_{A}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1+V)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{B}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c\cdot\text{dist}(A,B)}.

This implies that

(8.36)

\displaystyle\|W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}W_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|}

since
W_{j}=\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})
is highly localized around
\xi_{j}
with an exponentially decaying tail. Using (
8.36
), the operator
T_{j\ell}
in (
8.35
) satisfies

(8.37)

\displaystyle\|T_{j\ell}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq\|G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\|W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{j}}W_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\|G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim e^{-c|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|}

Combining (
8.35
) and (
8.37
) yields

\displaystyle\|G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}W_{j}G^{D}_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\neq j}\big(\|T_{j\ell}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}+\|T_{\ell j}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\neq j}e^{-c|\xi_{j}-\xi_{\ell}|}\lesssim e^{-cd_{\varepsilon}},

where
d_{\varepsilon}=\min_{\ell\neq j}|\xi_{\ell}-\xi_{j}|\gtrsim|\log(\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon})|
. This completes the proof of Lemma
8.4
.

∎

8.2.
Maximum of the Gaussian process

In this subsection, we study the maximal behavior of the field
v
under the Gaussian measure

\displaystyle d\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=Z_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{-1}v,v\rangle}\prod_{x\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}dv(x),

defined in Lemma
5.4
, with covariance operator
C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
(
5.11
). In the following proposition, we show that the typical size of
v
under the measure
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
satisfies
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}
for some sufficiently large
K\geq 1
.

Lemma 8.5
.

There exist a constant
c>0
such that for sufficiently large
K
, we have

(8.38)

\displaystyle\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(A_{\varepsilon}^{c})\lesssim e^{-cK^{2}|\log L_{\varepsilon}|},

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
, where

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}:=\{\|v\|_{L^{\infty}([-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}])}\leq K\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\}.

Proof.

We first show that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any finite
p\geq 2
, we have

(8.39)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}\big]\leq Cp^{\frac{p}{2}}|x-y|^{\frac{p}{2}},

uniformly in
(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k})\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
and
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
.

Using the fact that
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is a Gaussian measure, we have that for any finite
p\geq 2

(8.40)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}\big]\leq p^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big(\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\big]\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.

By expanding the second moment, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\big(\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[v(x)v(x)\big]-\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[v(x)v(y)\big]\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+\big(\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[v(y)v(y)\big]-\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[v(y)v(x)\big]\big)

(8.41)

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{1}+\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{2},

where

\displaystyle\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{1}

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,x)-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)

\displaystyle\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{2}

\displaystyle=G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(y,y)-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(y.x).

Here
G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is the Green function defined in (
8.2
), corresponding to the covariance operator
\mathcal{C}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
given in (
5.11
). Using the elliptic regularity theorem, one can show that

(8.42)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,z)|

\displaystyle\leq c|y-z|

(8.43)

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]}|G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)-G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)|

\displaystyle\leq c|x-z|,

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
and even in
L_{\varepsilon}
. By combining (
8.41
), (
8.42
) and (
8.43
),

(8.44)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\big]\leq c|x-y|,

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-L_{\varepsilon},L_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
and
L_{\varepsilon}
. Combining (
8.40
) and (
8.44
) yields that for any finite
p\geq 2
,

(8.45)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}\big]\leq cp^{\frac{p}{2}}|x-y|^{\frac{p}{2}},

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
and
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, we obtain the desired estimate (
8.39
). Once we have (
8.39
), the maximal behavior of the Gaussian field follows from Talagrand’s chaining argument. See
[
36
, Proposition 5.1]
or
[
37
, Chapter 2]
.

∎

In the previous lemma, we showed that the typical behavior of the field

v
under the Gaussian measure
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is captured by the events
A_{\varepsilon}=\{\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\}
. In the following, we study the error estimate outside the typical behavior

.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
F
be a bounded, continuous function and
\delta_{\varepsilon}=\eta\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
, defined in (

). Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\Big[F(v)e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(v)}\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\,A_{\varepsilon}^{c},\,\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big]\lesssim e^{-cK^{2}|\log L_{\varepsilon}|},

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
, where
\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
and
\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
are defined in Proposition

.

Proof.

We decompose the event

into dyadic shells

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}}\Big[e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)^{3}dx}\cdot\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\,A_{\varepsilon}^{c},\,\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\geq K}\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}}\Big[e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)^{3}dx}\cdot\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\,G_{\ell},\,\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big],

where
\ell

\ell=2^{j}K
,

, so that the sum runs over
\ell=K,2K,4K,\dots
, and

\displaystyle G_{\ell}=\Big\{\ell\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}<2\ell\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\Big\}.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq K}\bigg(\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}}\Big[e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)^{3}dx},\,G_{\ell},\,\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\nu^{\perp}_{m_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(G_{\ell})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq K}e^{\sqrt{\varepsilon}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\ell\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}}\cdot(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{2}}\ell^{k}|\log L_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{k}{2}})\cdot e^{-c\ell^{2}|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq K}e^{-\frac{c}{2}\ell^{2}|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\lesssim e^{-cK^{2}|\log L_{\varepsilon}|},

and

.

9.
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck limit on normal space

In this section, we present the central limit theorem on the normal space to the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck measure under the conditions:

\displaystyle\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}

for every
1\leq j\leq k
. This condition ensures that the test function
g
is at a distance at least
\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}
from all solitons
m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{1}},\dots,m^{\varepsilon}_{\xi_{k}}
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
L_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+\eta}
and let
g
be a real-valued, smooth, compactly supported function. Under the conditions

(9.1)

\displaystyle\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}

for every
1\leq j\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\Big[e^{i\langle v,g\rangle}e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)}\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\;A_{\varepsilon},\;\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big]

(9.2)

\displaystyle=\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\langle g,(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\Big\}\cdot(1+O(\varepsilon^{0+})),

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]
and
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
. Here
A_{\varepsilon}=\big\{\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\big\}
, defined in Lemmas
8.5
, and
\delta_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\varepsilon\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}
in (
6.28
).

Remark 9.2
.

If the tangential modes
\xi_{j}
do not satisfy the condition
\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}
for every
j=1,\dots,k
(
g
is close to one of the solitons), then the central limit theorem to the mean–zero Ornstein–Uhlenbeck measure does not hold. However, in the next section, we show that the contribution of the forbidden region
\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})<\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}
becomes relatively small compared to the size of the partition function as
L_{\varepsilon}\to 0
. Hence, we can still obtain the desired result with a more careful geometric analysis.

9.1.
Asymptotic behavior of the covariance

In order to prove Proposition
9.1
, we first take some preliminary steps. With respect to the measure
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
, we perform the following orthogonal decomposition

(9.3)

\displaystyle v(x)=\mathbf{P}(x)\langle v,g\rangle+w(x),

where
\mathbf{P}(x)
is the projection of
v
onto the direction of
\langle v,g\rangle

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(x)=\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[\langle v,g\rangle v(x)\big]}{\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|\langle v,g\rangle|^{2}\big]}.

The orthogonal decomposition implies that
w
and
\langle v,g\rangle
are independent Gaussian random variables. Hence, the measure
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
can be decomposed as follows

\displaystyle d\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(v)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma_{g}}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma_{g}^{2}}}\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\nu^{\perp\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(w),

where the variance
\sigma_{g}^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|\langle v,g\rangle|^{2}\big].

Before presenting the proof of Proposition
9.1
, we study the following lemma, which provides control on the variance
\sigma_{g}^{2}
.

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
g
be a smooth, compactly supported function. Under the conditions

\displaystyle\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}

for every
1\leq j\leq k
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{g}^{2}=\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|\langle v,g\rangle|^{2}\big]=\langle g,(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}g\rangle\cdot\big(1+O(e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}})\big),

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]
and
\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

By the definition of the Gaussian measure
\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
, we have

(9.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|\langle v,g\rangle|^{2}\big]=\langle g,G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}g\rangle,

where
G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
is the Green’s function for the covariance operator

\displaystyle C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}=\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1+W)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}},

defined in (
5.11
). Here

\displaystyle W:=-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\text{sech}^{2}(\cdot-\xi_{j})

is the perturbation term, localized near each soliton center. Using the resolvent identity, we interpret the Schrödinger operator
C_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
as a perturbation of the projected Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator
\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}
as follows

(9.5)

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(x,y)=G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}(x,y)+(G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}WG^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})(x,y)

where

\displaystyle G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}(x,y)=(\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}})(x,y).

We first study the leading order term
G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}
in (
9.5
). By expanding the projection operator
\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1}\,\dots,\xi_{k}}}
, defined in (
5.12
), we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{V_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}

\displaystyle=(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}

(9.6)

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j},

where
\mathbf{P}_{j}=\langle\cdot,t_{j}\rangle t_{j}
is the projection onto the tangential direction. We begin with the first projected term
\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}
. Note that

\displaystyle\langle g,\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}g\rangle=\langle g,t_{i}\rangle\langle g,t_{j}\rangle\big\langle t_{i},(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}t_{j}\big\rangle.

Under the condition
\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}
, together with Lemma
4.5

|t_{j}(x)|\lesssim e^{-|x-\xi_{j}|}
,

(9.7)

\displaystyle|\langle g,t_{j}\rangle|\lesssim\bigg|\int_{\supp g}g(x)e^{-|x-\xi_{j}|}dx\bigg|\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}}e^{-\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})}\lesssim e^{-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}.

This implies that

(9.8)

\displaystyle|\langle g,\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}g\rangle|\lesssim e^{-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}.

Furthermore, the separation condition (
9.7
) implies

(9.9)

\displaystyle|\langle g,\mathbf{P}_{i}(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}g\rangle|\lesssim e^{-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}\quad\textup{and}\quad|\langle g,(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}\mathbf{P}_{j}g\rangle|\lesssim e^{-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}.

Combining (
9.6
), (
9.8
), and (
9.9
) yields

(9.10)

\displaystyle\langle g,G^{D}_{\textup{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}g\rangle=\langle g,(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}g\rangle+O(e^{-\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}).

This completes the leading order term
G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}
in (
9.5
).

We now study the perturbation term
G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}WG^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}
in (
9.5
), which is also an error term. We expand

\displaystyle|\langle g,(G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}WG^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})g\rangle|

(9.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}g(x)\bigg(\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}(x,z)e^{-|z-\xi_{j}|}\bigg(\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)g(y)dy\bigg)dz\bigg)dx\bigg|,

where we used
\textup{sech}^{2}(z-\xi_{j})\lesssim e^{-|z-\xi_{j}|}
. To use the correlation decay, we separate the Green’s function into its bulk part and boundary parts, as follows, based on Lemma
8.2
and Proposition
8.3
,

(9.12)

\displaystyle G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}(x,z)

\displaystyle=ce^{-|x-z|}+O(e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|x|,|z|\})})

(9.13)

\displaystyle G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(z,y)

\displaystyle=ce^{-|z-y|}+O(e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|z|,|y|\})}).

We first focus on the bulk effect
e^{-|x-z|}
and
e^{-|z-y|}
by plugging them into (
9.11
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bigg|\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}g(x)\bigg(\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-|x-z|}e^{-|z-\xi_{j}|}\bigg(\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}e^{-|z-y|}g(y)dy\bigg)dz\bigg)dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{\varepsilon}\bigg|\int_{\supp g}\int_{\supp g}e^{-\frac{1}{4}|x-\xi_{j}|}e^{-\frac{1}{4}|y-\xi_{j}|}g(y)g(x)dydx\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{k}L_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\text{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})}\|g\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{1}}

(9.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim L_{\varepsilon}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}},

where in the last step, we use the condition
\textup{dist}(\supp g,\xi_{j})\geq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}
for every
1\leq j\leq k
.

For the boundary effects
e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|x|,|z|\})}
and
e^{-2(L_{\varepsilon}-\max\{|z|,|y|\})}
in (
9.12
) and (
9.13
), following the arguments in (
8.10
) and (
8.12
), we obtain the error term

e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}
. Combining (
9.11
), (
9.14
), and the boundary error
e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}
yields

(9.15)

\displaystyle|\langle g,(G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}WG^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})g\rangle|\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}+e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}}

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Finally, using (
9.4
), (
9.5
), (
9.10
), and (
9.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\big[|\langle v,g\rangle|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\langle g,G^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g,G^{D}_{\textup{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}g\rangle+\langle g,(G_{\text{OU},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}^{D}WG^{D}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}})g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g,(-\partial_{x}^{2}+1)^{-1}g\rangle(1+O(e^{-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\eta}})).

This completes the proof of Lemma
9.3
.

∎

9.2.
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck limit on the normal space

We are now ready to prove Proposition
9.1
.

Proof of Proposition
9.1
.

We decompose the expectation as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\Big[e^{i\langle v,g\rangle}e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)}\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\,A_{\varepsilon},\,\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\Big[e^{i\langle v,g\rangle}\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\;A_{\varepsilon},\;\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon},\Big]

\displaystyle\hphantom{X}+\mathbb{E}_{\nu^{\perp}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}}\Big[e^{i\langle v,g\rangle}(e^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)}-1)\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v),\;A_{\varepsilon},\;\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{2}}<\delta_{\varepsilon}\Big]

(9.16)

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{1}+\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{2},

where
\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{1}
is the main term and
\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{2}
is an error term. On the set
A_{\varepsilon}=\{\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K\sqrt{|\log L_{\varepsilon}|}\}
, the higher-order error term and the determinant in Proposition
7.4
satisfy

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{E}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)\Big|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{-L_{\varepsilon}}^{L_{\varepsilon}}|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v|^{3}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}L_{\varepsilon}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{0+}|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{3}{2}}=\varepsilon^{0+},

where we used
L_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}+}
, and

(9.17)

\displaystyle\textup{Det}_{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}v)=1+O(\|\sqrt{\varepsilon}v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{k})=1+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{2}-})

uniformly in
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{k}\in[-\overline{L}_{\varepsilon},\overline{L}_{\varepsilon}]^{k}
. This implies that

(9.18)

\displaystyle\hskip 1.42262pt\text{I}\hskip 1.42262pt_{2}=O(\varepsilon^{0+}),

uniformly in

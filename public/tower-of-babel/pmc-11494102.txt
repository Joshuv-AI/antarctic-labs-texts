Evaluation of the efficiency of dry anaerobic digester in the production of biogas and fertilizer using activated sludge and plant waste
Authors: Mehrdad Ghaedi, Habibeh Nasab, Mohammad Hassan Ehrampoush, Ali Asghar Ebrahimi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The process of dry anaerobic digestion (DAD) is an effective economic and environmentally friendly method due to its features such as reducing the volume of waste, destroying pathogens, consuming less energy, and producing biogas rich in energy and fertilizer. This study was conducted on a pilot scale with a reactor volume of 24 L in 30 days for three ratios of C/N: 30, 25, and 20 separately using a mixture of activated sludge and plant waste. During the process, the temperature, pH, and ratio of C/N were measured at certain times, and the percentage of gases produced by the Gas chromatography (GC) device was also measured. The results were compared with the National standard of Iran (Compost, Physical, and Chemical Specifications) at the end of the process. The results showed that the average volume of biogas produced in ratios of C/N: 30, 25, 20 is 1.7, 2.5, and 1.5 L. d−1, respectively. The results showed that in the C/N ratio of 20, and 25, the amount of, C/N ratio and NO3 are in the range the standard of class 1, and the amount of Phosphorus pentoxide (P2O5) is in the range the standard of class 2. The results showed that the average microbial parameters in ratios of C/N: 30, 25, and 20 are lower than the national standard of Iran. The dry digester is an efficient method in waste treatment, methane gas production, and quality fertilizer close to the national standard of Iran.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75504-z.

Introduction

Anaerobic digestion (AD) is a microbial degradation process used by organisms in the absence of oxygen to handle organic, agricultural, and animal manure wastes1,2. Apart from the safe and eco-friendly management of waste, AD provides numerous environmental benefits. These include reducing the emission of odors and greenhouse gases, decreasing waste volume, and producing nutrient-rich fertilizer3. Moreover, the process also produces valuable products such as biogas (CH4 and CO2) and offers a cost-effective solution4,5. In the production of biogas through anaerobic digestion, the speed of the process and the rate at which microorganisms multiply are influenced by temperature5. The system’s effectiveness is greatly affected by variables like pH, organic load, volatile fatty acids, and alkalinity3. AD is typically classified into dry (total solids (TS) < 15%) and wet (TS > 15%) processes. Wet digestion requires more water, which may be more than biomass, so it is not suitable for water-scarce areas like Iran. On the other hand, the high percentage of water in the digested slurry with the production of leachate reduces the nutritional value of the fertilizer and increases the cost of transportation. The wet digestion method requires a lot of land to dry the manure4. Dry anaerobic digestion (DAD) offers numerous advantages compared to wet digestion, such as needing less water, a smaller reactor volume, a simpler design, no requirement for mechanical mixing devices, decreased leachate production, and easier handling of digested residues4,5. DAD is also well-suited for various applications and organic waste types, including industrial sewage sludge4.

To effectively create AD, it is crucial to set up appropriate conditions and microbial communities that are critical for attaining peak efficiency. Usually, anaerobic sludge is utilized as a starter culture when starting a new digester6. Using sludge in DAD serves to stabilize and reduce sludge volume while also producing biogas rich in methane7. To improve the biochemical environment for microbial proliferation, it is advisable to combine sewage sludge with other substances in an anaerobic dry digester. This method aids in boosting pH buffering capacity, regulating the C/N ratio, and optimizing nutrient concentrations7. Borhan et al. 2012 the study investigated the performance evaluation of DAD with cow manure on a laboratory scale, which showed that it produces 0.359 L of CH4 on average8. Franca et al. 2020 a systematic review study examining the performance of DAD for the treatment of the organic part of municipal waste showed that among the different anaerobic digestion methods, DAD is a suitable method with the potential to produce useful products such as methane and compost, as well as more flexibility compared to the wet process. This method is mostly used in the mesophilic range, and it has been seen in many studies that factors such as temperature, pH, and organic loading affect gas production efficiency9. In the study of Gong et al. 2020 by investigating the performance of DAD on a laboratory scale for the simultaneous digestion of pig manure, corn straw, and sludge, it was shown that the DAD method of simultaneous digestion leads to better removal of volatile solids and system stability10. Lately, there has been a growing interest in sewage sludge to energy extraction11,12. Utilizing the DAD method has the potential to become a renewable energy source in rural and industrial regions2,12. DAD facilities may face reduced efficiency or come to a halt because of the need for a large quantity of inoculum, extended retention periods, Volatile fatty acids (VFAs) buildup, and the characteristics of solid waste materials. On the flip side, this technique has the potential to function as a renewable energy resource in various industries and rural communities, presenting an eco-friendly option. To enhance the effectiveness of this approach, it is essential to evaluate the operational factors and energy distribution while utilizing different forms of urban waste and urban sludge4. Due to the lack of water in Iran, it is better to use DAD, which leads to the production of clean energy such as methane and can also be used at the production site13. Limited studies have been conducted in Iran to evaluate the performance of dry anaerobic digestion14,15. This research aims to examine how effective a dry anaerobic digester is in generating biogas and fertilizer from sewage sludge and plant waste at the Shiraz Petrochemical Complex.

Materials and methods

Reactor design and construction

The present study was conducted on a pilot scale. To design and build different parts of the reactor, due to the complexity and the need to specify the specifications and how to build it, in the first stage, a general replica of the physics of the design was made, which included all the requirements of the predicted reactor. In the first step, a general model of the reactor was made. In the second stage, according to the dimensions predicted in the plan, the map of all parts was drawn manually and then drawn by AutoCAD software (version 2019). According to the prepared plans, the second model was made with real dimensions to determine the technical problems. The reactor was first built with a 2 mm galvanized sheet, but due to its low resistance to corrosion and technical problems, it was built again with a 4 mm sheet. The digester is made of 4 mm double-layer galvanized metal in the form of a cylinder, the inner layer of which has a volume of 24 L, a diameter of 30 cm, and a height of 35 cm. The volume of the outer layer was 12 L with a distance of 5 cm from the inner layer and its height was considered 30 cm. The first and second layers of the reactor were placed at a distance of five centimeters from each other with two blanks with an inner diameter of 30 and an outer diameter of 40 cm, separately from each other and on top of each other. For the convenience of sealing and closing the screws of the reactor door, the height of the inner layer was considered to be 5 cm higher than the outer layer, which was 4 cm lower than the upper part of the outer layer and 1 cm lower than the lower part of the outer layer. To measure reactor temperature, the reactor is designed to automatically adjust the temperature (that is, it does not allow the temperature to increase or decrease outside the specified range). The temperature of the reactor was set at around 35°C. In each C/N ratio, the temperature was permanently read from the system’s thermostat and recorded daily. The temperature of the mass was measured by a digital thermometer and through the sampling valves installed on the reactor body in the center of the mass being decomposed. To ensure the accuracy of the results, the average of 3 readings was considered as the temperature of the total mass. To determine the mass temperature, the temperature was read and recorded once a week using a stick thermometer (during the 30 days of the process). Using a stick thermometer, the temperature of the upper quarter, lower quarter, and center of the mass was measured, and the average of the obtained numbers was considered as the temperature of the mass18,19. Every 8 h, the contents of the reactor were stirred automatically for 10 min. The pH of the decomposing mass was measured weekly by compound sampling method, 10 g of samples were taken from three points (surface, center, and bottom of the mass) and mixed, and then its pH was measured20.

Biogas production

Sampling of produced gases (H2S, CH4, CO2, and NH3) and stored inside the reactor was done every ten days using a special gas collection pump (electric DC 12-volt mini silent vacuum pump to battery-90 Kpa, model A-370). To sample the produced biogas, first, the gas exit path was cleaned, then the sampling valve was opened, the gas exit path was left open for less than 1 min, and then the gas sample entered 2 sampling tubes (Due to the specificity of the columns of the chromatography machine, 2 samples were taken for measuring biogas). After the sampling tubes were filled, the sample was transferred to the laboratory in less than 1 h. Gas chromatography (GC) was utilized to ascertain the percentage of generated gases Table S1 shows the specifications of the GC device. The volume of the collected gas was determined according to the catalog of Gas chromatography (GC) device, so the sample volume for the analysis of H2S gas was 1 L.d−1, and CH4, CO2, and NH3 gases were 25 mL.d−121.

To determine the volume of gas production in DAD for all three ratios C/N: 20, 25, 30 on days 15, 23, 30, and 8 of the process (weekly), the volume of mixed biogas produced passively and using the volume of displaced liquid (water) inside the burette, the amount of produced gas was determined. In this way, the end of the burette was connected to the DAD, and the other side was connected to a container containing water, then the water inside the container was inserted into the burette and was directed to the gas outlet of the DAD through the laboratory glass valve, until that the water level inside the buret is static22. Fertilizer

After 30 days (the end of the process), the contents inside the reactor were thoroughly mixed, removed from the reactor, and spread out on a cloth bed to dry in the sun. To determine the quality of the produced fertilizer in terms of chemical characteristics )pH, Electrical conductivity (EC), ash, organic matter, organic carbon, Kjeldahl nitrogen (TKN), ratio C/N, P205), the tests of physical, chemical, and biological variables were performed based on standard methods23. after 48 h from each ratio C/N of two 500 g samples were taken. For microbial analysis (fecal coliform, salmonella) after 72 h of leaving the contents in the reactor, two samples of 500 g were taken24. To measure the germination index, the aqueous extract of fertilizer was used in a ratio of 1:5, then it was stirred for 30 min, the obtained suspension was filtered with a strainer, then 10 ml of filtered aqueous extract was placed in a sterile plate and 10 seeds Cucumber plant was planted and kept in the dark for 48 h at a temperature of 25°C. After the elapsed time, the sprout seeds were counted and the length of the roots was measured. Based on Eq. (2) the germination index (CHsg) is obtained. Where CHsg is the germination index, Sg is the germination number, and R1 represents the root length24. The average results of the physical and chemical characteristics of the produced compost were compared with the Iranian national standard number 10716 (Compost – Physical and Chemical Specifications). Iran’s compost standard specifies compost quality in terms of physical and chemical characteristics in class 1 and 2. This classification is based on quality and grade 1 has more quality. Results

Reactor temperature

Figure 3 shows the changes in the temperature of the reactor during the process for 30 days in the ratios C/N: 20, 25, and 30. The study results indicated that for a C/N ratio of 20, the temperature of the reactor ranged from 35 to 39°C. The temperature of the reactor began to rise on the 10th day of the process, reaching its peak on the 19th day. The average temperature recorded was 37.6°C. In the ratio of C/N 25, the temperature of the reactor during the decomposition process was observed in the range of 36–41 °C, the increase in the temperature of the reactor started from the 11th day of the process and reached its maximum value on the 17th day of the process, and the average temperature was 37.97°C. In the C/N 30 ratio, the temperature of the reactor increased in the range of 36–39 °C, which started from the 3rd day of the process and fluctuated until the 15th day of the process and reached its maximum value from the 18th to the 20th day, and the average temperature was 37.26°C.

Temperature, pH, ratio C/N of the decomposing mass

Figure 4 shows the changes in temperature, pH, and C/N ratio of the decomposing mass in the reactor over 30 days for each of the C/N: 20, 25, and 30 ratios. The results showed that in the C/N ratio of 20, the mass temperature increased from the 7th day of the process to the 21st day, and the average temperature was 35.25°C. In the ratio of C/N: 25, the mass temperature increased from day 7 to 14 and then decreased and the average temperature was 38.75°C. In the ratio of C/N: 30, the mass temperature was observed in a constant range from day 7 to 21, and the temperature decreased from day 21, and the average temperature was 38.75°C. The results showed that the pH increased after the start of the process in all three C/N ratios after the 7th day of the process, but its level did not reach the optimum level (8-7.3) for the maximum activity of methanogens. The average C/N for ratios of 20, 25, and 30 were observed as 5.6, 5.9, and 5.77, respectively. In all three C/N ratios, the amount of C/N started to decrease from the 10th day of the process, and on the 30th day of the process, it reached 16.6–15.9. The average C/N ratios of 20, 25, and 30 were observed as 17.43, 19.60, and 21.36 respectively.

The results showed that in all three C/N ratios, the lowest and highest amounts of CH4 and CO2 produced were observed on the 10th and 30th day of the process, respectively. The results showed that at C/N: 20 and 25 ratios, the lowest and highest amounts of H2S gas produced were observed on the 20th and 30th days of the process, respectively. In the ratio of C/N 30, the lowest amount of H2S gas produced was observed on the tenth day of the process, and from the tenth to the twentieth day of the process, its amount increased and from the 20th to the 30th day, its trend remained constant. The average percentage of H2S gas produced for C/N ratios of 20, 25, and 30 was 0.04, 0.07, and 0.07 respectively. The results showed that in all three C/N ratios, the lowest and highest amount of NH3 gas produced was observed during the first 20 and 30th days of the process, respectively. The average percentage of NH3 gas produced for C/N ratios of 20, 25, and 30 was observed as 0.37, 0.13 and 0.1%, respectively.

In the ratio of C/N 30, the lowest volume of biogas produced is on the 8th day of the process, and from the 23rd day to the end of the process, the biogas production slope increases. Discussion

DAD is an attractive and eco-friendly technology due to its application and use. In Iran, a comprehensive investigation into this waste treatment approach has not yet been undertaken. The available data mainly originates from research conducted in foreign nations. Therefore, essential factors such as temperature, pH, C/N ratio, mixing methods, and the type of waste employed significantly impact the effectiveness of the procedure and the subsequent production of biogas and fertilizer. The main goal of using DAD is to meet the energy needs of countries and identify ideal waste management strategies.

The present study was conducted at a mesophilic temperature and the temperature of the mass inside the reactor was in the range of 38 to 39°C in all three C/N ratios. Basinas et al. 2021 showed that temperature is a key factor in dry anaerobic digestion by impacting the microbial communities, which in turn regulate the effectiveness and consistency of the anaerobic digestion process27. Fluctuations in temperature, whether too high or too low, can disturb the process of methane production. Abouelenien et al. 2016 study showed that by fermenting chicken manure with agricultural waste, the CH4 produced decreases with increasing temperature, and the maximum CH4 produced is 37°C28. In the present study, the average pH in all three C/N ratios was between 5.5 and 6. AD is a complex biochemical process. Although pH is one of the important parameters in DAD, the effect of pH on DAD has rarely been reported in studies. Microorganisms are very sensitive to pH conditions29. The pH of the reactor changes due to the change in the concentration of VFAs and NH3 during the digestion process, which decreases in the early stages due to hydrolysis and acidification and the accumulation of organic acids, the acidification of the process can have a negative effect on methane production, but with the increase in production Ammonia increases the pH through the breakdown of proteins.

The research conducted by Sganzerla et al. 2021 showed that the amount of CH4 generated through the dry anaerobic digestion of açaí seeds method was 0.167 m3. kg−130. In research conducted by Salangsang et al. 2022, it was shown that the use of food industry waste in a DAD results in the generation of over 6 L. d−1 of biogas, while also sustaining a pH level between 7 and 82. Borhan et al. 2012 by examining the efficiency of a DAD reactor on the pilot scale using animal manure with inoculation mixture in a ratio of 2:1 in 40 days and temperature of 35°C, showed the percentage of CH4, CO2, and N2O gases produced It is 62.3, 36.3 and 0.41 respectively. Their results showed that the daily average of CH4 was 0.359 L. d−18. The results of the study by Hossain et al. 2020 showed that the maximum biogas produced using anaerobic sludge (AS) and cow manure (CM) in DAD was 1.85 L .d−131. Also, in another study, Hossain et al. 2020 showed that if organic municipal solid waste (OMSW) is used with inoculated cow manure (CM) and anaerobic sludge (AS), the amount of biogas produced is 3.19 mg. g−132. In the present study, the amount of CH4 produced in the reactor with a C/N ratio of 20 was approximately 0.21 L. d−1, It is predicted that on a larger scale, for one ton of waste to around 32 L. d−1. In C/N 25, approximately 0.61 L. d−1 of CH4 is produced within the reactor, predicted for one ton of waste this amount increases to 111.45 L. d−1. At a C/N ratio of 30, the amount of CH4 produced in the reactor is about 0.6 L. d−1, predicted for one ton of waste, this amount reaches 52.97 L. d−1. The amount of CO2 produced in the ratio of C/N 20 inside the reactor was about 0.28 L. d−1, which is predicted for one ton of waste about 44.46 L. d−1. In the ratio of C/N 25, the amount of CO2 produced in the reactor is about 0.75 L. d−1, predicted for one ton of waste, this amount reaches 137.43 L. d−1. In the ratio of C/N 30, the amount of CO2 produced inside the reactor is about 0.81 L. d−1, predicted for one ton of waste this amount reaches 71.31 L. d−1.

Results of the Salangsang et al. 2022 showed that the ratio of C/N affects the increase in the number of microbes2. In the present study, all three ratios of C/N exhibited a declining trend throughout the process, leading to reduced microbial activity and an extension of the biogas production duration. The present study showed that in the C/N ratio of 25, the production volume of biogas and CH4 is efficient. Studies also recommend a C/N ratio between 20 and 30 for the anaerobic digestion process, with a C/N ratio of 25 being introduced as the optimal ratio33,34. Hong et al. 2007 study investigating the use of algal sludge to provide nitrogen and adding waste paper to provide carbon showed that the optimal amount of methane produced is in the C/N ratio of 20–2535. Cai et al. 2022 conducted a study in which they used rural waste, human feces, food waste, and grass in a DAD to investigate the effectiveness of the process and microbial properties. The results indicated that co-digestion of various substrates is effective, suppresses NH3 generation, decreases the levels of Methanogen bacteria, and diminishes the occurrence of salmonella36. In the current study, it was found that around 0.005 L. d−1 of NH3 is produced in a C/N ratio of 20, predicted for one ton of waste, this amount reaches 0.851 L. d−1. In a C/N ratio of 25, approximately 0.003 L. d−1 of NH3 is produced inside the reactor. Predicted for one ton of waste, this amount increases to 0.609 L. d−1. In the ratio of C/N 30, the amount of NH3 produced inside the reactor is about 0.0035 L. d−1. Predicted for one ton of waste, this amount reaches 0.30 L. d−1. The research by Rocamora et al. 2020 demonstrated that for a DAD, the ideal C/N ratio is between 20 and 30. A decreased C/N ratio (pointing to elevated nitrogen levels) leads to the creation of NH3. Although nitrogen is necessary for the development of microorganisms, excessive nitrogen levels can cause problems with inhibition37. Matheri et al. 2018 a study on DAD discovered that elements like volatile fatty acids (at 5000 m3.mg−1) and low pH (at 5.5), along with elevated levels of NH3 (at 6900 mg. L−1) and high pH (at 7.9), successfully render Salmonella inactive. Consequently, the levels of volatile fatty acids, NH3, and pH play a crucial role in the deactivation of Salmonella within a week in DAD38. In the present study, the results showed that the amount of salmonella is within the Iranian national standard number 10,716 (Compost – Physical and Chemical Specifications) The results of the research by Rajagopal et al. 2019 who investigated how a DAD affects methane production and pathogen elimination, revealed that a DAD operating at 28°C attained a methane production efficiency of 50%, also the DAD method eliminated around 90–100% of harmful microorganisms like E. coli, streptococcus, various types of gram-negative bacteria, salmonella, and Klebsiella39.

The results of the current study indicate that, while differing from findings in other studies, the analysis of all three C/N ratios suggests that certain factors have influenced the system’s performance in methane production and organic matter removal. The analysis of the reactor’s temperature and the decomposing mass indicates that the bacteria are active in the system. The temperature performance of the system is not the factor that caused the slowness of the process; instead, another factor is influential in this case. The pH analysis indicates that the increase of this parameter in the system has occurred gradually, and the system’s conditions have been maintained in the acidic range. Despite efforts to maintain buffering conditions, the methane production percentage is unsatisfactory, with CO2 remaining the predominant component in the biogas. Consequently, there is a factor impeding the attainment of optimal conditions, impacting both temperature and pH levels. All three C/N ratios are decreasing, indicating that carbon has been consumed and nitrogen has increased in the system. Studies show that both carbon and nitrogen are essential for cell growth and metabolism40. However, reducing carbon levels results in nitrogen accumulation, leading to an increase in ammonia production. Therefore, adjusting the C/N ratio serves as an inhibitory factor, affecting performance due to ammonia build-up within the system. Therefore, if the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio decreases during the system’s operation, an increase in carbonaceous compounds enhances the process and prevents unnecessary prolongation. Additionally, the factory in question produces urea and ammonia, along with various chemical wastes such as hydraulic oils that could potentially enter the sewage system, acting as a secondary factor in sludge formation. This aspect warrants further investigation in future research.

Conclusion

The results of the current study demonstrate that dry anaerobic digestion (DAD) of sewage sludge and garden waste yields fertilizer of high microbial and chemical quality, meeting the national standard of Iran closely. The results showed that the amount of P2O5 in the C/N ratio of 20 is 0.3%. The amount of NO3 in ratios of 20, 25, and 30 was observed as 2.65, 2.87 and 2.96%. The ratio of C/N in ratios of 20, 25, and 30 was observed as 15.6, 14.5, and 1.15. pH was in the range of 6 for all ratios. Controlling parameters such as the carbon-nitrogen (C/N) ratio, temperature, pH, mixing, and inhibitory factors is crucial for the operation of DAD. In managing the organic sector of industrial waste, the efficiency of the technology relies on the careful control of key parameters in operating the DAD. This system can effectively remove pollutants and microbial agents while producing high-quality fertilizer. Methane production in the present study was carried out in the mesophilic temperature range of 38–39 °C and pH between 5.5 and 6. The present study showed that in the C/N ratio of 25, the production volume of biogas and methane is efficient. The results showed that the most CH4 and CO2 gases were observed in the C/N ratio of 25 at the rate of 36% and 39%, respectively. One of the advantages of this method is the production of valuable gases like CH4 and CO2 as a renewable energy source. In addition to addressing the country’s waste issues, this method is also economically viable. The DAD is stable against shocks, has ease of operation, and can be used in all weather conditions and various regions of the country due to its unique reactor design, as well as its applicability for various.

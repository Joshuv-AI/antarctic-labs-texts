New Sulfonate Ester‐Linked Fluorinated Hydrazone Derivatives as Multitarget Carbonic Anhydrase and Cholinesterase Inhibitors: Design, Synthesis, Biological Evaluation, Molecular Docking and ADME Analysis
Authors: Berna Akış, Reşit Çakmak, Murat Şentürk
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, some new hydrazone derivatives (2a–g) was designed, synthesized for first time, and evaluated as multitarget inhibitors of AChE, BChE, hCA I and hCA II. The chemical structures of new hybrids were confirmed by elemental analysis and some spectroscopic techniques. All tested compounds showed low nanomolar inhibition with IC50 values of in the range of 30.4–264.0 nM against hCA I, 23.2–251.6 nM against hCA II, 12.1–114.3 nM against AChE, and 76.4–134.0 nM against BChE. These compounds inhibited hCA I and AChE more than acetazolamide (AZA) and neostigmine. Among them, compounds 2c and 2e, which have a linear structure, were determined to be the most active inhibitor candidates against these selected enzymes. Molecular docking studies were carried out on the compounds (2a‐‐g), revealing their binding interactions with the active site of AChE, BChE, hCA I and hCA II thus supporting the experimental findings. Additionally, in silico absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) prediction studies of the obtained compounds (2a‐‐g) with in silico approaches were carried out to determine their solubility, whether they have the potential to cross the blood‐brain barrier (BBB), values such as GI absorption and drug likeness principles.

Introduction

Carbonic anhydrases (CAs, also known as carbonate dehydratase, EC 4.2.1.1) are zinc‐containing metalloenzymes that catalyze the reversible hydration of CO2 to HCO3 − and H+.[ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 ] These enzymes perform functions in various physiological processes such as respiration, metabolism (lipogenesis, ureagenesis, etc.), acid/base balance, electrolyte secretion in several tissues and organs, calcification, transportation of CO2 and bicarbonate between metabolizing tissues and the lungs.[ 5 , 6 ] The CA gene family consists of many members named α, β, γ, δ, ζ, η and θ.[ 6 , 7 ] While many organisms have multiple gene families, hCA is encoded only by α‐CA gene family. [8] Up to now, CAs have sixteen different isoenzymes identified in mammals.[ 3 , 6 , 8 ] Among these CAs, hCA II, which is the most common, is generally involved in all processes. Although hCA I is present in five to six times greater amounts than hCA II in erythrocytes, the specific activity of hCA II is much greater, and the contribution of the two to the total CO2 hydratase activity under these conditions is about equal. [9] To date, many studies have been conducted on CAs. hCA I and hCA II are the most studied isoenzymes. [8] There are studies showing that CAs are associated with many diseases. Many studies have reported that many CA inhibitors (CAIs) are significant therapeutic targets with the potential to be inhibited/activated for the therapy of a range of disorders such as cancer, glaucoma, obesity and epilepsy.[ 10 , 11 ]

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is an irreversible and progressive neurodegenerative disease that occurs with irreversible neuronal loss in the hippocampus and cortex regions of the brain. The prevalence and frequency of the disease increases with advancing age.[ 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 ] Cholinergic damage to the cerebral cortex causes the cognitive and behavioral disturbances that develop in the disease.[ 16 , 17 ] Alzheimer symptoms are characterized by memory loss, difficulties in speech, orientation, decision‐making, thinking and interpretation, personality and behavioral disturbances.[ 18 , 19 ] Although the cause of the disease is not yet fully known, research has found that the risk of developing the disease is slightly higher in those with a family history of AD.[ 20 , 21 , 22 ] Although AD is one of the most researched diseases in recent years, ways to prevent the disease, definitive diagnostic tests or an effective treatment have not been found yet.[ 23 , 24 ] When the pathology of the disease is examined, it is observed that there is deterioration in neuronal cells in the brain, intracellular neurofibrillary networks originating from hyperphosphorylated tau proteins, and extracellular amyloid β‐peptide aggregates.[ 25 , 26 ] The fact that the biological mechanism of AD formation is extremely complex and many factors are involved in the neuropathology of the disease necessitates the use of different strategies to prevent AD formation, slow down the course of the disease, and treat it.[ 27 , 28 ] Although the general approaches in the treatment of AD are to prevent amyloid β‐peptide‐induced plaque formation, cholinesterase inhibitors (ChEIs) are one of the frequently used treatment strategies in recent years.[ 29 , 30 ] Clinical studies show that the use of ChEIs is palliative in the treatment of AD. For this reason, studies often focus on the development of bidirectional effective inhibitors that both disrupt amyloid plaque formation and exhibit cholinesterase inhibitor behavior.[ 31 , 32 ]

Hydrazide‐hydrazone compounds are among the classes of compounds that have long attracted the attention of medicinal chemists due to their promising biological and pharmacological properties. Hydrazone compounds have various biological activities in a wide broad‐spectrum, including α‐amylase, [33] anticancer,[ 34 , 35 , 36 ] antimicrobial,[ 37 , 38 ] antibacterial, [39] antituberculosis, [40] analgesic, anti‐inflammatory and antiplatelet, [41] antioxidant, [42] and so forth. Some of these compounds are among the drugs approved for the treatment of various diseases in clinical practice. On the other hand, there are many studies examining the inhibitory activities of hydrazone compounds against acetylcholinesterase (AChE), butyrylcholinesterase (BChE) and human carbonic anhydrase I and II isoenzymes (hCA I and II). In these studies, some newly synthesized hydrazone derivatives significantly inhibited these enzymes.[ 43 , 44 , 45 , 46 ] In one of these studies, compounds (E)‐4‐chloro‐N′‐(2‐hydroxy 3,5‐di‐ tert‐butylbenzylidene)picolinohydrazide (a) and (E)‐4‐chloro‐N′‐(2‐hydroxy‐5‐nitrobenzylidene)picolinohydrazide (b) showed notable inhibitory activities against AChE and BChE, respectively (Figure 1). [43]

The drug design strategy of the new molecules.

The fact that fluorinated compounds, one of the important structural motifs in medicinal chemistry, interact more with biotargets than other halogenated compounds, increases the interest in these compounds. The combination of these and other bioactive compounds allows the production of new hybrid molecules with diverse biological and pharmacological activities. This interest has led to an increase in the number of new fluorinated compounds among drugs approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). Among these drugs, many synthesized fluorinated compounds have been used in cancer treatment. These compounds have been used in the treatment of many diseases other than cancer, including Cushing's disease, migraine, and AD.[ 47 , 48 ] Flortaucipir F‐18 (TauvidTM) (c), a fluorinated compound used in the diagnosis process of AD, is a drug approved by the FDA in 2020 (Figure 1). This drug administered intravenously crosses the BBB and thus interacts with misfolded tau protein in the brain. It can be easily identified by PET scan and helps diagnose tau‐related pathology. [47]

To date, many studies on fluorinated compounds have been published in new drug discovery studies for the treatment of glaucoma and AD. In these studies, fluorinated compounds inhibited hCA I, hCA II, AChE and BChE significantly compared to reference molecules.[13,45,49–51]

Aryl sulfonate compounds, known as useful intermediates in organic syntheses in medicinal chemistry, can be synthesized by reacting phenolic compounds with sulfonyl chlorides. To date, many studies have been conducted reporting the anticancer, antioxidant, anti‐cholinesterase and anti‐carbonic anhydrase activities of these compounds.[ 13 , 52 , 53 , 54 , 55 , 56 ] In one of these studies, aryl sulfonate‐based scopoletin derivatives (d and e) were reported to show potential inhibitory effects against cholinesterase enzymes (Figure 1).

Many small methoxy‐containing compounds occur in nature through the action of O‐methylating enzymes on hydroxy‐containing substrates and are frequently present in naturally derived drugs. Molecules carrying this important group of pharmacophores show a broad spectrum of biological activity. Therefore, it is one of the substitutions in the structure of new molecules in drug design strategies in medicinal chemistry. Among the methoxy‐containing compounds obtained to date, there are many approved drugs used in the treatment of various diseases, including cancer, cardiovascular, hypertension, bacterial infection and malaria. Some of these approved drugs are also used to treat glaucoma and Alzheimer diseases. For example, galanthamine (f) and donepezil (g) as ChEIs are used in the treatment of AD, and brinzolamide (h) as CAIs is used in the treatment of glaucoma (Figure 1).[ 52 , 57 ]

The emergence of dual‐target drugs has attracted considerable attention in recent years, and therefore this topic has become a rapidly expanding area of research for medicinal chemists. Molecular hybridization has been used as a tool to design multitarget drug candidates for complex diseases.[ 55 , 58 ] Combining two or more compounds or parts of them through chemical reactions to obtain new dual‐target agents is known as molecular hybridization. Molecular hybridization is a significant strategy in the design of hybrid molecules with more potent and biologically active properties than starting compounds. [55] Here, we chose to adopt this approach to develop potential drugs for the treatment of glaucoma or AD. Consequently, our study focused on investigating dual active enzyme inhibitors that target both hCAs and AChE/BChE. In this study, a new series of hybrid molecules (2a‐‐g) was synthesized by molecular hybridization of commercially available 4‐fluorobenzoic hydrazide (4‐FBH) and aryl sulfonates. Then, inhibitory activities of newly synthesized hybrids against AChE, BChE, hCA II and I were evaluated by in vitro and in silico approaches. For the synthesis of new fluorinated hydrazone compounds linked to methoxy substituted sulfonate esters, herein we designed and successfully synthesized a series of new molecules containing four key pharmacophore moieties (fluorine, methoxy, sulfonate, and hydrazone moieties) present in the structures of FDA approved‐drugs used in the treatment of some important diseases, including AD and glaucoma (Scheme 1). The chemical structures of the targeted compounds (2a–g) were verified by 1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (NMR), Fourier transform‐infrared spectroscopy (FT‐IR), high‐resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) data as well as elemental analysis. In computational studies, molecular docking study was carried out to elucidate the predicted inhibition mechanisms of the targeted molecules. In addition, the pharmacokinetic properties, stability, and drug‐likeness parameters of all the targeted molecules in this study were estimated by using SwissADME.

Results and Discussion

In pursuit of identifying dual inhibitors of AChE, BChE, hCA I, hCA II, we successfully synthesized a small library of new fluorinated hydrazone derivatives (2a‐‐g) in good yields (Scheme 1). A two‐step synthetic route was used to synthesis the hitherto unreported these molecules.[ 13 , 42 , 52 ] Firstly, seven phenolic aldehydes were refluxed with 4‐methoxybenzenesulfonyl chloride (4‐MBSC) in dichloromethane (DCM) containing catalytic amount of triethylamine (TEA) for 5 h with constant stirring to get sulfonate compounds (1a‐‐g). Finally, new hydrazone compounds (2a–g) were acquired by the reaction of sulfonate compounds with 4‐FBH at reflux temperatures of ethanol for 6 h.

In this study, aryl sulfonates (1a–g) used to prepare the targeted molecules were synthesized and characterized for our previously published manuscript. [52] However, the newly synthesized hybrid molecules (2a‐‐g) were isolated as solids and characterized by using 1H‐NMR, 13C NMR, FT‐IR and HRMS alongside elemental analysis (CHNS) (see the Supporting Information).

In 1H‐NMR spectra of the target compounds (2a–g), the azomethine proton, CH=N, exhibits the most distinctive feature, indicating the existence of the hydrazone structure. The azomethine protons exhibited a distinct singlet signal in the range of 8.35–8.73 ppm. The aromatic protons displayed resonance within the range 6.00–9.24 ppm. The methyl and methylene protons of the related hydrazone compound 2f resonated at 1.00 ppm and 3.25 ppm, respectively. The aliphatic protons of compounds (2a–g), characterized by the inclusion of methoxy groups in their structure, resonated as a singlet at 3.56–3.88 ppm. The 13C NMR spectra of the synthesized structures (2a–g) revealed a distinct peak in the range of 162.04–162.65 ppm for the carbon signal of C=O. Identification of the azomethine carbon peak is the most distinctive feature in determining the structures of the desired compounds. The carbon resonance peak of CH=N was detected between 147.69–153.19 ppm. The resonance signals observed for aromatic carbons were within the range of 104.19–166.00 ppm. The methyl and methylene carbons in compound 2f were observed at 12.64 and 44.33 ppm, respectively. The carbon signal in 2a–g containing a methoxy group in their structures was detected at 56.14–56.41 ppm. The FT‐IR spectra of the compounds showed that the position of the NH stretching band in their structure may exhibit varying behavior depending on the intensity of the intramolecular and intermolecular hydrogen bonding interactions. From this perspective, it was observed that the NH stretching bands existed within the range 3195–3358 cm−1. The most prominent characteristic of 2a–g was the stretching band of the C=O bond. The aforementioned compounds exhibited highly defined peaks in the range of 1640–1683 cm−1, which corresponded to the C=O stretching bands. Vibrations of the C=N stretching bands within the structure were observed in the spectral range of 1594–1607 cm−1. Furthermore, it was observed that both asymmetrical and symmetrical stretching bands of the SO2 groups were located at 1340–1374 and 1147–1169 cm−1, respectively.

Biological Evaluation

AD and glaucoma, which are associated with some metabolic enzymes, including AChE, BChE, hCA I and hCA II, are among the most common diseases today. Dual Inhibition of these enzymes is aimed in combating these diseases. Nowadays, there is a need to design and synthesize new inhibitor candidates because the existing inhibitors used in the treatment of these diseases cannot provide sufficient curative effects on patients and some side effects occur during the treatment process.

Regarding inhibitors of these enzymes, besides the well‐known importance of ChEIs in the therapy of AD, studies have also shown that inhibitors of other enzymes (hCA I and II) reduce amyloid β pathology and improve cognition by ameliorating cerebrovascular health and glial fitness. [59] Studies have indicated that some well‐known CAIs (AZA and methazolamide) mitigate mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis triggered by amyloid β in both vascular and neural cells. This effect is achieved by reducing the production of reactive oxygen species in the mitochondria and preventing the decrease in mitochondrial membrane potential. [60] Therefore, in this research, it was aimed to target these four metabolic enzymes (AChE, BChE and hCA I and II) with newly synthesized hydrazone derivatives. The rationale behind targeting both CAs and cholinesterases lies in their involvement in overlapping pathways implicated in diverse diseases including neurological disorders. While double inhibitory may seem counterintuitive at first, it can offer many potential advantages. The first is that some diseases, including AD, involve multifaceted pathological mechanisms in which simultaneous targeting of multiple enzymes may lead to increased therapeutic efficacy. Another is that there may be synergistic effects between inhibitions of different enzyme classes, leading to improved outcomes compared to those of single‐target inhibition. [61]

(i) The cytosolic isoform hCA I is an enzyme that is relatively abundant in the gastrointestinal tract and red blood cells compared to others. In hCA I assay, novel hydrazone linkage‐based aryl sulfonate derivatives tested (2a–g) showed remarkable activity against this enzyme in the range of 30.4–264.0 nM, as seen in Table 1. All molecules tested inhibited hCA I more than AZA, which is used to treat epileptic seizures, glaucoma, altitude sickness, and idiopathic intracranial hypertension. [64] Among these, 4‐((2‐(4‐fluorobenzoyl)hydrazono)methyl)‐2‐methoxyphenyl 4‐methoxybenzenesulfonate (2e) was the most active inhibitor. This molecule inhibited hCA I more than nine times compared to AZA. Moreover, compound 2e was the most active inhibitor against hCA II compared to AZA. This enzyme, together with hCA I, plays a role in important metabolic functions such as gas exchange and ion transport.[ 5 , 6 , 8 ] Apart from this active molecule in the series, 4‐((2‐(4‐fluorobenzoyl)hydrazono)methyl)phenyl 4‐methoxybenzenesulfonate (2c) was the second most active inhibitor against both hCA I and hCA II compared to AZA. In hCA II assay, the tested hybrid molecules had remarkable inhibitory activity against hCA II at different nanomolar concentrations (23.2–251.6 nM). The results showed that target compounds, except compound 2e, inhibited this enzyme at higher nanomolar concentrations compared to AZA. CAIs are a class of pharmaceuticals that suppress the activity of CAs. [64] These inhibitors have been used as diuretics and antiglaucoma drugs. However, recent findings show that CAIs are promising structures as new anti‐obesity, anticancer and anti‐infective drugs. [65] According to our results, some of these molecules deserve further investigation in this field.

(ii) Different drugs such as donepezil, tacrine and rivastigmine are used in clinical practice for the treatment of AD. [64] In recent years, the use of these current drugs used in the treatment of AD has been limited in treatment processes due to side effects such as hepatotoxicity and gastrointestinal discomfort.[ 66 , 67 ] Therefore, studies are being carried out to identify new and specific inhibitors to be used in the treatment of this disease. In this study, the inhibition activities of new molecules against AChE and BChE were determined according to the Ellman method. In this study, as seen in Table 1, it was determined that these new derivatives (2a‐‐g) had a higher potential to inhibit AChE compared to neostigmine. In AChE assay, the molecules tested showed inhibition with IC50 values ranging from 12.1–114.3 nM. In the series, compound 2c (12.1 nM) was the best inhibitor against this enzyme compared to reference molecules neostigmine (135.90 nM) and rivastigmine (60.00 nM). This compound inhibited AChE eleven times more than neostigmine and almost five times more than rivastigmine. Except for compound 2c, compound 2e (61.4 nM) was the second most active inhibitor against this enzyme. Compound 2e (76.4 nM) also showed the most activity against BChE compared to neostigmine (84.0 nM). These molecules tested in BChE assay exhibited activity against BChE at nanomolar concentrations ranging from 76.4–134.0 nM. According to these results, none of the tested molecules showed as much activity as the reference drug rivastigmine. Among these compounds, compounds 2c (83.6 nM) and 2e showed activity closest to rivastigmine (14.10 nM). On the other hand, it was determined that compound 2g showed lower inhibition against both BChE and other enzymes. BChE acts as a backup for AChE by hydrolyzing acetylcholine that has diffused out of nerve synapses. [68] In treatment of AD, first choice for the treatment is AChE inhibitor. However, AChE inhibitors have some flaws, such as insufficient long‐term treatment effect and dose limitations. Recent studies revealed that BChE inhibitors or double inhibitors have better effects on AD, and the side effects are lower than those of specific AChE inhibitors. Dual target ChEIs have become a new hot spot in the research of anti‐AD drugs.[ 68 , 69 ]

In this study, the hydrazone‐sulfonate hybrids tested inhibited hCA I, hCA II, AChE, and BChE at nanomolar concentration (Table 2). The inhibitory results of our study were compared with the inhibitory results of compounds with similar scaffold against the same enzymes. In those studies, it was determined that aryl sulfonate derivatives with new hydrazone linkage showed inhibition activity against these selected metabolic enzymes at micromolar or nanomolar concentrations.[ 53 , 56 , 70 , 71 , 72 , 73 , 74 ] As a result, it was determined that the tested molecules in this study showed better activity than the molecules tested in some other studies.

The Structure‐Activity Relationships of the Targeted Molecules

In this study, the targeted molecules (2a–g) consisting of three different moieties (4‐fluorobenzoic acid moiety (1), central moiety with a benzene ring except compound 2g as spacer (2) and 4‐methoxy substituted benzenesulfonate moiety (3)) as potential dual target agents were synthesized (Scheme 1). In these molecules, the structures of only their spacer moiety (2) changes and the other moieties (1 and 2) remain the same in each molecule. Therefore, only their spacer moiety affects the inhibition activities of the targeted molecules. When the structure‐activity relationships of the molecules were examined, compound 2g, which had a naphthalene ring in its spacer moiety, showed weaker inhibition activity compared to the others. On the other hand, compounds 2c and 2e, which have a linear structure, showed better inhibition activity than other molecules. When the inhibition activities of these two molecules with similar molecular skeletons were compared, compound 2e shows strong inhibition against hCA I, hCA II and BChE due to the electron‐donating and activity‐enhancing methoxy group in the 5 th position of the benzene ring in its spacer moiety. This molecule also showed better inhibition activity against these enzymes when compared to reference molecules (AZA and neostigmine).

Molecular Docking Studies

In this study, the crystal structures of the tested enzyme in silico were obtained from the protein database. Molecular docking studies were carried out for all tested compounds that interact with these four enzymes in vitro. Additionally, AZA for hCA I and hCA II, and neostigmine and rivastigmine for AChE and BChE were used for comparison. The free binding energies of the tested and reference compounds in the active sites were calculated (Table 2). However, only the visualization of the most active inhibitor candidates showing high inhibition effects in vitro or in silico is presented in this paper.

In this study, in the molecular docking study performed using in silico approaches, the best result in terms of binding affinity belongs to compound 2e (Table 2). According to the interaction result of the 6EVR crystal structure with hCA I, the docking score value was −8.4 kcal/mol. According to Table 2, this value is much better than the values of AZA for hCA I. The 2D and 3D diagrams of this interaction are given in Figure 2. It shows the interaction between compound 2e and hCA I. There is a π‐π stacking and π‐π T‐shaped interactions with amino acid residues His‐94, His‐119 and His200.

3D and 2D interaction diagrams of compound 2e interacting with hCA I.

The binding parameters of hCA II enzyme as the second target protein were investigated by using in silico approaches. Compound 2e with the crystal structure 7RNZ of hCA II had the best values according to both in vitro and in silico studies (Tablo 3). When the interactions between 7RNZ and reference molecule AZA were investigated, it was determined that compound 2e had better binding affinity to this enzyme. Figure 3 presents 2D and 3D interaction diagrams of compound 2e, which is in the active pocket region of the hCA II. As shown in Figure 3, compound 2e has a hydrogen bonding interaction with amino acid residues Thr‐200 and Gln‐92, and a π‐π T‐shaped interaction with His‐94. Additionally, compound 2e fully interacted with the helical structure of hCA II in 3D in Figure 3.

3D and 2D interaction diagrams of compound 2e interacting with hCA II.

When the binding parameters of AChE as the third target were examined (Table 2), compound 2c was determined to have the best fitting binding models. This compound was determined to interact better in terms of binding free energy when compared to neostigmine and rivastigmine. This has already been showed in the in vitro results. The docking score of compound 2c with the crystal structure of 4EY6 with AChE was −9.8 kcal/mol. This calculated value for compound 2c is better than the values calculated for reference molecules and other tested compounds.

ADME Studies

The swissADME prediction associated with pharmacokinetics is an important variable that has been recently studied in the drug discovery process and its aim is to evaluate whether the compounds designed for drug design have physicochemical properties that will allow them to be orally active drugs in humans. Therefore, the first step in computational research of newly synthesized lead molecules today is to investigate ADME predictions. If the targeted molecules have the desired ADME properties, they may be suggested as drug candidates. In this research, we computationally examined ADME estimated values in terms of pharmacokinetics. In this way, the ADME predictions can be employed as a tool to decrease failures before starting in vivo assays. In this study, ADME analysis was carried out online by using the SwissADME server. Generally, drug‐drug interactions result from major cytochrome P450 (CYP) inhibition. The enzyme inhibitors of CYP3 A4, 2D6, 2 C19, 2 C9, and 1 A2 have diverse physicochemical and structural properties.[ 75 , 76 ] In this study, the inhibitory activities of human Cytochrome P450 enzymes were investigated by using ADME studies; and the results obtained were given in Table 3. On the other hand, BBB permeability and gastrointestinal absorption scores (GI absorption score) were calculated for the seven targeted molecules and are given in Table 3. In addition, the pharmacokinetic properties of the tested molecules are presented in Table 3. Table 3 shows that not all molecules tested in the SwissADME prediction are BBB permeable. According to the inhibition results obtained against hCA I, hCA II and BCHE, among the tested compounds, compound 2e, which is the best inhibitor candidates, has no BBB permeability in the data given in Table 3. Conclusions

It is predicted that the number of glaucoma and Alzheimer's patients will increase with the increasing aging population and that these diseases will continue to be a significant health and economic burden. Despite significant advances in the development of CAIs and ChEIs for use in the treatment of these diseases, patients in the clinic suffer from serious side effects of these commercially available drugs. Designing new multitarget inhibitors for the management of diseases associated with the selected enzymes for this study is one of the most popular topics in medicinal chemistry today. In this study, a new series of hybrid molecules (2a–g) containing hydrazone and sulfonate moieties was synthesized for first time, and their inhibitory activities against hCA I, hCA II, AChE, and BChE were investigated by in vitro assays and in silico approaches. The structures of newly synthesized molecules were elucidated by using four spectroscopic techniques alongside elemental analysis. All of these newly synthesized molecules inhibited hCA I and AChE more than AZA and neostigmine. Except for compound 2e, all tested compounds (2a–d, 2f and 2g) inhibited hCA II at higher nanomolar concentrations than AZA. Furthermore, molecules (2a, 2b, 2d, 2f and 2g) other than compounds 2c and 2e inhibited BChE more weakly than neostigmine. Among the tested molecules, compounds 2c and 2e, which have a linear structure compared to other molecules, were determined to be the most active inhibitor candidates against these metabolic enzymes. Of these two compounds, compound 2e is an inhibitor candidate that shows strong inhibition against hCA I, hCA II and BChE due to the effect of the activity‐enhancing methoxy group in the aromatic ring in the spacer moiety. The molecular docking predictions supported the experimental data showing that compounds 2c and 2e were more effective than the reference molecules. In addition, the drug similarity, pharmacokinetics, and physicochemical properties of the targeted molecules were founded by using SwissADME. These compounds were interpreted to have the desired ADME properties. According to the results of in vitro and in silico studies, compound 2e stands out as a potent inhibitor of hCA I, hCA II, and BChE, which is an important target especially for glaucoma and AD. In continuation of this research study, in vivo studies are needed to elucidate the potential of compound 2e in the treatment of these or some other diseases.

Experimental Section

General Information

Commercially available reagents and solvents used in this study were of analytical grade were procured from commercial suppliers (Merck, Sigma‐Aldrich, Across Organics, and Thermo Fisher Scientific). These chemicals were used without further purification. The progress of the reactions and the purity of the targeted molecules were checked by thin layer chromatography (TLC) using the solvent system consisting of n‐hexane and ethyl acetate. Melting points were determined on a capillary melting apparatus (Barnstead IA9100 Electro thermal Digital Melting Points Apparatus) and are uncorrected. Elemental analyses were performed on a Thermo Scientific Flash 2000 elemental analyzer. FT‐IR spectra were recorded on an Agilent Cary 630 spectrophotometer with ATR in the scanning range of 4000–400 cm−1. 1H‐NMR and 13C NMR spectra were recorded in DMSO‐d6 solutions on a Bruker AVANCE III 400 MHz spectrometer using tetramethylsilane as the internal reference at 400 MHz and 100 MHz, respectively. On the other hand, HRMS were carried out on Agilent 6530 Accurate‐Mass instrument.

The NMR, FT‐IR and HRMS spectra of the tested molecules are provided as the Supporting Information section along with some biological activity data.

The Synthesis of Aryl Sulfonate Esters

A mixture of an appropriate phenolic aldehyde (18.4 mmol) and TEA (36.8 mmol) in 20 mL of DCM was stirred at room temperature for 1 h. Afterwards, 4‐MBSC (18.4 mmol) in 20 mL of DCM was added dropwise into the above solution, and the reaction mixture was refluxed for 5 h and then cooled down to room temperature. The progress of the reaction was monitored using TLC. After completion of the reaction, the solution was extracted twice with 2 M HCI. The resulting organic layer was dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, and then filtered. Then, the solvent was allowed to evaporate at room temperature all night long. Finally, the residue was crystallized with ethanol to yield an intermediate; and then it was used for the synthesis of the target molecule.

The Synthesis of the Targeted Molecules

4‐FBH (2 mmol), the corresponding aryl sulfonate (2 mmol), and ethanol (10 mL) were added to a round‐bottom flask. After the reaction was completed, the excess solvent was removed under reduced pressure, and then the resulting crude product was filtered, washed several times with diethyl and petroleum ether; and then crystallized with ethanol. On the other hand, when the formation of the target product is delayed, the reaction mixture was poured into crushed ice. The corresponding hydrazones formed as solid products were filtered, dried at room temperature and; then recrystallized from ethanol. The purity of newly synthesized compounds was checked by TLC.

Biological Assays

In this study, hCA I from bovine erythrocytes (C3934: Sigma‐Aldrich), hCA II human (C6624: Sigma‐Aldrich), AChE human (C1682: Sigma‐Aldrich), BChE human (B4186: Sigma‐Aldrich) and the other chemicals (4‐nitrophenyl acetate (4‐NPA) (N8130: Sigma‐Aldrich), 5,5‐dithiobis(2‐nitrobenzoic acid)(DTNB) (D218200: Sigma‐Aldrich), acetylthiocholine iodide (A5771:Sigma‐Aldrich), butyrylthiocholine iodide (B3253: Sigma‐Aldrich) were purchased from local representatives of well‐known commercial companies. On the other hand, the absorbance rates of each compounds were determined by the microplate spectrophotometer (Multiskan Go, Thermo Scientific, USA).

In Inhibition assays, inhibition profiles of new inhibitor candidates against hCA, hCA II, AChE, and BChE were determined by using the spectrophotometric methods. In this assay, AZA for hCA I and II, neostigmine and rivastigmine for AChE and BChE were used as reference molecules. In this study, stock solutions of the inhibitor candidates were prepared by dissolving it in dimethyl sulfoxide to achieve a concentration of 1 mg per mL. The resulting stock solution was then carefully diluted one thousandfold with distilled water. To evaluate the inhibitory potential of these inhibitor candidates, measurements were made at seven different concentrations. The methodology used in this research has been explained in detail in previous studies.[ 11 , 61 , 68 , 78 , 79 ]

The Esterase Activity Assays for hCA I and II

In hCA I and II Inhibition assays, the inhibitory action of these hybrid molecules was measured by observing the change in absorbance at 348 nm of 4‐NPA was converted to 4‐nitrophenolate over a period of 3 min at 25 °C using a spectrophotometer. The reaction mixture consisted of 1.4 mL of 50 mM Tris‐SO4 buffer (pH 7.4), 1 mL of 3 mM 4‐NPA, 0.5 mL of water, and 0.1 mL of enzyme solution, for a total volume of 3.0 mL.[ 62 , 68 , 79 ] A reference measurement was obtained by preparing the same cuvette without enzyme solution. All compounds were tested in triplicate at each concentration used. Different inhibitor concentrations were used. Control cuvette activity in the absence of inhibitor was taken as 100 %. For each inhibitor an activity (%)‐[inhibitor] graphs were drawn.

The Inhibitory Activity Assays for AChE and BChE

In AChE and BChE Inhibition assay, the assay system used comprised a sample of the inhibitor candidate, with a volume ranging from 5–60 mL, in conjunction with 200 mL of buffer, specifically 1 M Tris‐HCl buffer for AChE assay and phosphate buffer for the assay of BChE, both at a pH of 8.0. Additionally, 50 mL of 0.5 mM DTNB and 50 mL of 10 mM acetylthiocholine iodide/butyrylthiocholine iodide were incorporated, as well as 10 mL of the enzyme, with a concentration of 0.28 U/mL for AChE assay and 0.32 U/mL for BChE assay. [63] The reaction was initiated by adding the enzyme, and the system was prepared at room temperature within a quartz cuvette. A control was also performed, consisting of all the aforementioned chemicals, except for the inhibitor.[ 68 , 79 ]

Molecular Docking Studies

Molecular docking analyzes were performed to evaluate the predicted binding energies of the tested molecules (2a–g) with these four enzymes. For this aim, 3D structure of hCA I (PDB ID: 6EVR), [80] hCA II (PDB ID:7RNZ), [81] AChE (PDB ID:4EY6) [82] and BChE (PDB ID:4BDS) [83] were downloaded from the protein data bank (https://www.rcsb.org). Macromolecule preparation was performed with AutoDock tools 1.5.6 and UCSF Chimera software. Chemical structures of the compounds (2a‐‐g) were drawn with ChemDraw 19.1 program and were transferred to Avogadro. [84] Geometry optimization and energy minimizing of the 3D structures of compound (2a‐‐g) were made by ORCA program under B3LYP basis set and structures were transferred to Autodock.[ 85 , 86 ] After the best inhibitor candidates and receptor preparations, molecular docking analysis was conducted with the Autodock vina program using the PyRx platform. [87] Discovery Studio was used to visualize the 2D and 3D models of the docking conformations and interactions patterns (V21.1.0.20298, 2020).

SwissADME Prediction Tool

In this study, the SiwssADME predictions were carried out to calculate some physicochemical properties of the examined compounds (2a–g) and to estimate drug‐likeness. The calculated values were estimated by using the SwissADME (http://www.swissadme.ch/) server. [76] In this study, the pharmacokinetic properties (MW, partition coefficient (log P), HBD, HBA, and TPSA) of all tested molecules were calculated, respectively. In addition, to determine drug‐likeness, whether the lead molecules complied with the Lipinski, Ghose, Veber, Egan, Muegge, and bioavailability score rules was considered when evaluating them as drug candidates. Log Kp, GI absorption, BBB permeability, P‐gp substrate, and cytochrome‐P enzyme inhibition values were also calculated by using the SiwssADME to predict the absorption and distribution of the tested compounds.

Early shift to oral antibiotic treatment for pyogenic vertebral osteomyelitis (SAVE): study protocol for an open label, non-inferiority, nation-wide randomized controlled clinical trial

Background

Pyogenic vertebral osteomyelitis (PVO) is an infection localized to vertebrae and their surrounding structures. PVO is associated with high morbidity and risk of chronic pain and permanent neurological deficits [1]. The incidence of PVO is increasing worldwide with a recent study showing an increase in incidence from 2.2/100,000 in 1995 to 5.8/100,000 in 2008 in a Danish population [2]. The 1-year crude mortality rates are reported to be 5–22% [3–6]. Serious long-term consequences include permanent neurological deficits, chronic pain and reduced functional level, all reducing the quality of life [5, 7]. In a Danish study the proportion of self-supporters after PVO was only 33% compared to 58% before the diagnosis [8].

Sparse data are available to guide the choice of treatment modality and duration. Recommendations for antibiotic (AB) therapy have mostly been based on findings from retrospective- and observational studies and expert opinion. The prevailing recommended duration of therapy for PVO has for many years been 6 to 12 weeks [1, 9] without clear guidance as to which patient groups would benefit from which duration. Furthermore, intravenous (IV) therapy has been recommended as the primary treatment due to better bioavailability and higher drug concentrations in bone [1, 9].

Evidence to support selecting the duration of therapy has emerged during recent years. A randomized controlled trial by Bernard et al. indicated that 6 weeks of AB treatment may be as effective as 12 weeks of AB treatment in a study population with known bacterial etiology and treated with a median of 2 weeks IV antibiotics followed by oral AB combination therapy [10]. However, the proportion of patients needing surgical treatment or having soft tissue abscess was low. A subsequent study by Park et al. indicated that 6 weeks of therapy is not sufficient in patients with abscesses, methicillin resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) or end-stage renal disease [11].

The current Danish National Guideline for treatment of PVO recommends 6 weeks AB treatment, with a 2-week IV AB lead-in followed by 4 weeks oral AB for uncomplicated PVO, and 12 weeks AB treatment with a 4 weeks IV AB lead-in followed by 8 weeks oral AB for complicated PVO [12]. Complicated cases of PVO are defined as having significant, undrained intraspinal, epidural, paravertebral and/or psoas abscess, the need for surgical intervention, infection with MRSA, end-stage renal disease or a foreign body in the site of infection [12].

Patients are required to remain hospitalized for at least 2 to 4 weeks for inpatient IV AB therapy or receive IV AB at home, which is also cumbersome and costly. Prolonged hospitalization and IV therapy are costly and entail risks such as IV catheter related infections, vascular injury, thrombosis, and phlebitis as well as other nosocomial infections [13–16]. Furthermore, prolonged hospitalization is associated with functional decline as well as depression and anxiety [17, 18].

Oral ABs preclude the need for IV catheters and in-hospital AB treatment. Pharmacokinetic studies have shown that selected oral ABs reach appropriate concentrations in blood and in bone [19]. Nevertheless, randomized controlled trials to form the basis for accelerated shift to oral ABs for PVO are lacking, as also highlighted by the Infectious Diseases Society of America [9].

Two landmark studies indicate that early shift to oral ABs for complicated infections can be appropriate. Infectious endocarditis is analogous to PVO in that it is associated with severe morbidity and traditionally has required lengthy admission for IV AB treatment. Data from the POET (Partial Oral versus Intravenous AB Treatment of Endocarditis) study has shown that accelerated step-down from IV to oral ABs administered at home may not only be non-inferior to continued IV ABs but may also carry a significant long-term survival benefit in patients with infectious endocarditis [20, 21].

The open-label OVIVA trial published in 2019 reported that oral AB therapy was non-inferior to IV therapy when used during the first 6 weeks of the treatment for bone and joint infection, as assessed by definitive treatment failure within 1 year of randomization. However, less than 4% of the study population had PVO [22].

Thus, the data provided by the POET study and the OVIVA study combined with the increasing incidence of PVO, the burden of prolonged hospitalization imposed on the patient as well as on the healthcare system highlight the need for randomized controlled trials to examine whether early shift to oral ABs is non-inferior to continued IV therapy in patients diagnosed with PVO.

Earlier trials with the same design

A search in the database Clinicaltrials.gov on the term “vertebral osteomyelitis” and with the filters “Adult 18–64 years” AND “Older adult 65+ years” AND “Interventional (Clinical Trial)” yielded 3 results, none of which were trials assessing early switch to oral AB therapy.

The first study compared 6 with 12 weeks of antibiotic therapy for vertebral osteomyelitis. The study was published in 2015 and the impact is as detailed in section 4 [10].

The second study aimed to examine conservative management (antibiotic therapy alone) to surgical management combined with antibiotic therapy for patients with native vertebral osteomyelitis. According to clinicaltrials.gov, the study is estimated to have been completed in 2021; however, the results of the study are not available. Given the difference in aim, the results of the study would not influence the currently proposed study.

The third study aims to compare ceftriaxone to routinely used antibiotics to see if ceftriaxone is equally as safe and efficacious in curing deep-seated Staphylococcal infections in patients receiving home IV antibiotics. The study is expected to be completed in 2023. No results are currently available. If the study provides compelling data that ceftriaxone is a non-inferior alternative for S. aureus infections and is included in the Danish national guidelines prior to study completion, it will be included as an alternative for IV treatment in this study as well.

Objectives

Primary objective

To investigate whether early transition to oral AB treatment after 1 week of IV treatment is non-inferior to the current national guideline of continued IV AB treatment for 2 to 4 weeks followed by oral AB treatment for PVO with a non-inferiority margin of 10%, as deemed clinically acceptable by the steering committee by comparison of the tolerable risk difference with the benefits of reduced IV exposure and potentially shortened hospitalization including potentially fewer IV catheter complications, nosocomial infections [13, 16] and potentially reduced functional decline, depression and anxiety [17, 18].

Methodology

Diagnostic procedures

The diagnosis of PVO will be made by a physician based on clinical suspicion, laboratory results, and radiological findings on MRI and or PET/CT and/or PET/MRI.

Screening for inclusion and consent provision

Patients will be screened for inclusion as soon as possible and no later than 7 days after initiation of appropriate antibiotics for PVO. The patient will be approached by good clinical practice (GCP) trained study personnel and provided with written information about the study, the document “Forsøgspersoners rettigheder i et biomedicinsk forskningsprojekt” (in English: “Study participants rights in a biomedical research project”) and a consent form. A subsequent meeting will be agreed upon, where the patient’s understanding of the written information will be ascertained and any questions regarding the study, the patient may have, will be answered by the site principal investigator or a physician to whom the task has been delegated. If the patient wishes to participate in the study, they will be asked to provide written informed consent.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens

We will examine whether sequencing of microbial cell-free DNA as a diagnostic tool will be useful to identify bacterial etiology more accurately and quickly than conventional culture. The method has shown promise in small studies with respect to sensitivity and specificity [23]. In addition, the detection of circulating microbial DNA has been shown to occur earlier than the blood cultures become positive by growing the microbes [24]

Furthermore, we plan to carry out future investigations to assess for the presence of genetic variants related to immune dysfunction, antibiotic absorption-and metabolism, and bone remodeling [25–30].

For these purposes, separate patient consent forms will be obtained.

Selection of AB regimen and definition of starting date of appropriate AB therapy

Antibiotics used for treatment of patients in the study have been authorized for sale in Denmark. The appropriate antibiotic regimen will be selected prior to randomization and was until the 12th of March 2025 in accordance with Appendix 1 and has since been in accordance with the updated Danish National Guideline (DNG) for Diagnosis and Treatment of Spondylodiscitis [12]. If compliance with Appendix 1 or subsequently the DNG is not possible, or there is any uncertainty about selection of regimen or dosage, the patient’s microbiology data will be assessed by the regional member of the Treatment Committee (TC) who will consult with other members of the TC if necessary. In these cases, the TC will decide which AB regimen the patient is to receive. The first day of antibiotics for PVO will be defined as Day 1 of appropriate AB therapy in accordance with Appendix 1 or subsequently the DNG if possible, or as agreed with the TC. The duration therapy will be counted from this day. For patients having infection within the spinal canal, who have begun relevant antibiotic treatment for PVO, but who have not initially received the appropriate antibiotic in the appropriate dose for intraspinal infection, Day 1 will be defined either as the first day of appropriate treatment for PVO without intraspinal infection or as the first day of appropriate treatment for PVO with intraspinal infection at the discretion of the treating clinicians.

If new microbiology data emerges that would render the chosen regimen inadequate, a new appropriate regimen will be selected as described above. In this case, the date of initiation of the new appropriate regimen will be the new Day 1 upon which duration will be defined.

For patients undergoing therapeutic surgery or radiologically assisted abscess evacuation for PVO or psoas abscess, the starting timepoint of appropriate IV AB therapy will be immediately following surgery or radiologically assisted procedure.

The number of days will be counted in complete days of prescribed antibiotic therapy. For example, if the patient is prescribed benzylpenicillin four times daily, 1 day of AB therapy will be completed after four prescribed doses. If the patient is prescribed intravenous vancomycin or similar drug, where coverage is defined by number of days with adequate trough levels, 1 day of AB will be dependent on having adequate trough levels as defined in the summary of product characteristics.

Randomization

Randomization will take place after inclusion in the study and selection of both IV and oral AB regimen. A centralized internet-based computer-generated randomization schedule has been prepared by a statistician. The schedule has been uploaded to REDCap and can only be accessed by the statistician. Thus, the investigators cannot predict block patterns. Patients will be randomized 1:1 in permuted blocks of 2 and 4 and stratified according to disease severity (complicated or uncomplicated PVO) and region to mitigate center-to-center variation in practice. Randomization will be done by the principal investigator or on-site study personnel to whom the task has delegated.

During in-patient treatment and care

Patients will be monitored by the clinical team according to clinical standards and individual needs. Blood samples, further microbiological and radiological work-up will likewise be individualized, with a minimum of hematology, CRP, and kidney function once weekly on any given day in accordance with standard of care. The site’s study-physician and research-nurse will maintain contact with the clinical team to identify potential endpoints and adverse events and to verify that the chosen AB therapy is implemented.

During out-patient treatment and care

During the period of out-patient AB therapy, patients will be asked to call the study team if they experience new symptoms or worsening of existing symptoms.

Patients will be monitored with blood samples once per week.

All patients will be contacted weekly by the study team to assess their symptoms including presence of fever, pain status, and presence of serious adverse events (SAE), as well as adherence to medication using Morisky short score [31] and self-reported number of missed doses. In accordance with clinical standards, the treating physician will assess whether a physical consultation at the outpatient clinic is warranted at all time points according to clinical indication.

Additional investigations and diagnostics including additional blood samples, samples for microbiology and imaging as well as further monitoring and follow-up will be carried out according to clinical indication.

Health-related quality of life will be assessed using the EQ5D questionnaire (a standardized measure of health-related quality of life developed by the EuroQol group https://euroqol.org/) (Appendix 2) [32] at the following time-points in both treatment arms: At randomization, at 1 week, and 24 weeks following cessation of AB therapy. The validated language versions used will be Danish and English.

Patients will be included at randomization and are followed until 24 weeks after end of antibiotic treatment according to study procedures. Any patient lost to follow-up (LTFU) will have their electronic patient record examined to identify the occurrence of all elements of the composite primary endpoint.

Criteria for discontinuing allocated intervention and implications for analyses

Patients will discontinue the study if PVO is disproven. If they experience a primary outcome or if the allocated AB treatment strategy is deemed clinically unacceptable, unfeasible, or inadequate, or if the patient experiences a sudden unexpected serious adverse reaction (SUSAR), the allocated treatment will be discontinued. Further treatment and care will be provided in accordance with clinical standards.

Patients have the right to withdraw from the intervention at any time and instead receive routine clinical treatment and care in accordance with clinical standards.

All patients will remain in the study for follow-up and will be included in the intention to treat analysis. Data to assess the primary endpoint can be found in routinely used patient records.

Drug accountability and handling of investigational medicinal products

As the investigational medicinal products (IMP) used in this study are all off the shelf items, dispensing of IMP to the patient will be recorded in the electronic medical journals in accordance with clinical standards, as will administration of IMPs during admission, likewise in accordance with clinical standards.

Duration of oral antibiotic therapy

Follow-on oral ABs after the initial 6–12 weeks of trial-antibiotic therapy may be prescribed in either arm if deemed indicated by the treating physician. However, the choice of agent and duration is not governed by trial protocol.

Concomitant care and co-interventions

If a participant requires IV antibiotic therapy for an unrelated intercurrent illness during the oral antibiotic-treatment, or if the patient experiences vomiting or is unable to swallow, or concerns arise about absorption of oral medication, then IV antibiotic therapy may be given for up to five days. If IV antibiotic therapy is given for more than 5 days, the patient will have met a secondary endpoint and will be excluded from the per protocol analysis but will still contribute to the intention-to treat analysis.

Other interventions for other medical conditions are allowed during the study.

Sample size and statistical considerations

The study is designed as a non-inferiority study to assess whether the proportion of patients who have not experienced a primary endpoint at 24 weeks after cessation of oral AB is non-inferior in patients who have undergone early shift to oral AB compared to the proportion in patients who have continued IV treatment followed by shift to oral therapy as per national guidelines.

The primary endpoint is a composite endpoint comprising all-cause mortality, relapse of the initial pathogen(s), re-initiation of IV Abs, and unplanned surgery.

Previous studies have found 1-year all-cause mortality to be 5–22% [3, 4, 6, 22, 33] and 90-day mortality to be 14%. Relapse of the initial pathogen to be 0–3% [4] and unplanned surgery after 1 year of follow-up to be 17% [4].

The patient population in this study is a selected population, as it excludes patients having severe immunocompromise, patients having other relevant sites of infection requiring prolonged IV AB therapy (for example patients having endocarditis), patients having recurrence of S. aureus bacteremia despite relevant treatment, patients having previously inserted spinal implants, and patients who are not able to give informed consent. Additionally, patients are required to have AB response according to biochemical parameters at the time of randomization. Adverse outcomes in similar trials with comparable populations have found overall lower occurrence of adverse outcomes, including lack of cure and death 3,6, than retrospective observational studies have found 1,2,5. In their study with a similar population, Bernard et al. found that 13–16% of patients could not be classified as alive and cured at 1 year follow up 6. Considering these factors, the expected proportion having met the composite primary endpoint at 24 weeks after cessation of oral AB therapy is estimated to be 13%.

The study will include patients with complicated vertebral osteomyelitis and patients with uncomplicated vertebral osteomyelitis. This grouping defines the total antibiotic treatment duration in accordance with the Danish National Guidelines, based on data from Park et al. [11]. The data from this paper does not examine differences in duration of intravenous treatment, but rather focuses on recurrence in relation to total duration of antibiotic treatment. As such, we expect similar risk of primary endpoint occurrence in the two treatment arms in the groups, which is reflected in our sample size calculation.

The non-inferiority margin (Δ) has been set to 10% based on what has been deemed clinically acceptable by the steering committee by comparison of the tolerable risk difference with the benefits of reduced IV exposure and potentially shortened hospitalization including potentially fewer IV catheter complications, nosocomial infections [13, 16] and potentially reduced functional decline, depression and anxiety [17, 18].

With a one-sided alpha of 2.5%, 90%, power and 10% expected loss to follow-up, a total sample size of 530 patients will be included and randomized.

The primary endpoint will be analyzed as a binary variable, with statistical comparisons based on differences in proportions. A Generalized Linear Model (GLM) from the binomial family with an identity link will be used. The analyses will be adjusted for the stratification variables. To account for potential deviations from the model’s assumptions, robust standard errors will be applied to ensure valid statistical inference. Non-inferiority will be considered to have been confirmed if the upper limit of the 97.5% one-sided confidence interval for the difference in proportions does not include ∆.

The primary outcome analysis is an intention-to-treat analysis including all randomized participants in the treatment arm to which they were randomized. A secondary per-protocol analysis will be carried out including only participants who have received the intended durations of IV therapy ± 1 day and oral AB therapy with ≥80% of prescribed oral doses taken.

For exploratory purposes, the analyses will be repeated with the inclusion of interaction terms between treatment arm and (1) complicated vs. uncomplicated status, (2) region, (3) age groups (patients younger than 70 years vs patients equal to or older than 70 years) and, if feasible, (4) type of bacteria (S. aureus, Enterobacteriaceae, culture-negative), and (5) comorbidity score in five separate analyses. Statistical significance for these analyses will be assessed using two-sided test of the interaction coefficients at the 5% significance level.

Missing primary endpoint data will be addressed by worst case imputation meaning that missing data in the intervention arm will be analyzed as an endpoint, while missing data in the standard of care arm will be analyzed as no endpoint.

Furthermore, survival models (Kaplan-Meier curves and Cox regression models) will be performed to further examine possible differences in timing of adverse outcomes. One-sided test at significance level α = 5% will be used.

Secondary endpoints

A competing risk approach will be used to estimate cumulative incidence functions of each component of the composite endpoint. A test of similar medians in the two arms will be used to test differences of duration of hospital admissions. As for endpoints 3–9, chi2 tests will be carried out to test for difference between treatment arms. Repeated measures of quality of life (EQ-5D) (endpoint 10) based on the Danish EQ5D-5L value set will be analyzed using linear mixed models, due to possible deviations from normal distributions of the residuals a robust variance estimator will be implemented. Study patients having incomplete EQ5D-5L datasets will be excluded from these analyses. CRP and WBC (Endpoints 12) will also be analyzed using linear mixed models. For all secondary analyses, two-sided tests with significance level α = 5% will be used. However, due to multiple testing, statistically significant results for secondary analyses might be due to Type I error and should therefore be interpreted with caution.

Statistical analyses will be done using R version 4.5.1.

The statistician carrying out the analyses is independent from the sponsor institution and will remain blinded to treatment arm.

Minimizing bias

Staff and participants are not blinded to the treatment allocation, since we consider IV placebo treatment, including the placement and possible complications of IV catheters, as unethical in this setting.

Stratification

At randomization, patients will be stratified according to region as well as according to whether they are treated for complicated or uncomplicated PVO.

Endpoint Committee

An end-point committee (EC), comprising two infectious diseases specialists Senior Consultant Rikke Krogh-Madsen, Copenhagen University Hospital, Hvidovre and Senior Consultant Merete Storgaard, Århus University hospital, Skejby, as well as spinal surgeon Professor Senior Consultant Benny Dahl, Copenhagen University Hospital Rigshospitalet, will be charged with adjudicating whether selected elements of the primary endpoint have occurred in accordance with the Endpoint Committee Charter (Appendix 4).

Data and safety monitoring

The trial is not a clinical trial of a specific investigational medical product. All antibiotics used in the trial have well described safety profiles and are approved for treating infections in adults. The purpose of the trial is to compare the effectiveness of known and approved oral antibiotics with the effectiveness of known and approved intravenous antibiotics in a real-life setting. The indication for prescription of the used antibiotics, the target population, and the dosage are in accordance with current clinical practice and guidelines. Therefore, a risk-adjusted approach to safety monitoring is employed, whereby SAE are registered and reported to the sponsor, to ensure that any difference in serious adverse reactions (SAR) is identified and to ensure that any arising suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSAR)s are identified [34].

Safety monitoring and reporting

For definitions of SAE and SUSAR, please refer to section “Definitions” 10.

The safety follow-up period will last until 1 week after the last planned dose of oral antibiotics has been taken.

Registration and reporting of SAE/SAR

SAE arising after randomization will be registered at follow-up during in-person consultation or telephone consultation through spontaneous report by the patient, through asking and by examination of medical records for information regarding admittance to hospital since or death.

SAE will be recorded electronically in the electronic Case Report Form (eCRF) and reported to the sponsor. The electronic record will include date of onset, criteria for seriousness, and date of resolution.

Recorded SAE/SAR will be followed up until resolution or until the final follow-up study visit. If SAE/SAR has not been resolved at this time, the patient will be referred for appropriate follow-up in accordance with clinical standards.

Causality of SAE will be assessed by the site principal investigator and by the sponsor.

SAE will be reported by investigators to the sponsor within 24 h from knowledge of the SAE.

Expectedness of serious adverse reactions will be assessed by the sponsor by examining the Summary of Product Characteristics of the investigational medicinal product in question.

SAE that will not be reported by the investigator to the sponsor are events directly related to PVO including complications to surgery related to PVO and complications of PVO. Furthermore, hospitalization for the sole purpose of standard patient follow-up where out-patient consultation is not possible and hospitalization for the purpose of examinations, including radiology, that do not lead to the diagnosis of a condition requiring further hospitalization will be exempted from reporting.

Registration and reporting of suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions

The sponsor will be registered in the EudraVigilance system and will report suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSAR) through the EudraVigilance system and comply with timelines stated in article 42 of the Clinical Trials directive (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX:32014R0536).

An annual safety report will be submitted on a yearly basis through the CTIS system covering all the IMPs involved.

Reporting to the data and safety monitoring committee

An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Committee (DSMC) have been established to monitor safety data and carry out unblinded interim analyses. To ensure independence, the DSMC will be comprised of two physicians with relevant experience including infectious diseases Senior Consultant Court Pedersen, Odense University Hospital, and Professor, Senior Consultant Emil Fosbøl, Copenhagen University Hospital, Rigshospitalet, and Statistician Inge Petersen, Odense University Hospital.

Interim analyses for futility (inferiority)

We expect the primary outcome to occur in 68 patients (11% of 530) with an equal distribution between treatment arms. Interim analyses will be scheduled to be carried out by the DSMC once 20% (n = 106) and 50% (n = 265) study patients have reached 24 weeks following planned cessation of oral antibiotic therapy. Haybittle-Peto stopping rules (p < 0.0005) will be used for as stopping criteria at the interim analyses [35].

Discussion, ethical considerations, and overall risk and benefit assessment

There is currently insufficient evidence to clarify whether early switch to oral AB is non-inferior to current national guidelines.

There is a risk that early switch will lead to increased frequency of treatment failure and overall prolonged therapy. However, considering current data on oral antibiotic therapy for similar infections [20, 22] as well as trial procedures including limiting the study population to patients with clear improvement, frequent follow up including monitoring adverse and serious adverse events, we perceive this risk to be minimal.

For patients randomized to oral AB, participation in the study will provide the opportunity to shorten the duration of hospitalization considerably. Patients randomized to standard IV therapy will only experience minimal changes in in monitoring and follow-up compared to current clinical practice.

All investigators have agreed that they will ensure that this study is conducted in full conformity with relevant regulations and with the ICH Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice (CPMP/ICH/135/95) July 1996 as well as in adherence with the protocol. The trial is in full compliance with the Helsinki Declaration.

The study and the patient information and consent forms have been approved by the Danish Medical Research Ethics Committee and the Danish Medicines Agency (EU CT Number 2023–507617-96-01).

Substantial modifications to the protocol as defined by the Clinical Trial Regulation Chapter 1 article 2 [2, 14] will be submitted to the above-mentioned authorities for approval. Protocol modifications will be communicated to trial participants, journals, and trial registries as appropriate.

All principal investigators have signed a declaration of interest (DOI). None have reported a conflict of interest. All DOIs have been submitted to the Danish Medical Research Ethics Committee.

Roles and responsibilities

The steering committee members have developed the protocol in collaboration with input from the local investigators. The steering committee is responsible for the design and conduct of the trial as well as for trial completion, data analysis and interpretation. Appendix 1 has been developed by the treatment committee, while the sponsor has coordinated the effort. Thus, the study sponsor has been one of the key stakeholders of protocol development. It is the responsibility of the coordinating center to oversee, facilitate and coordinate conduct of the trail and ensure that sites receive appropriate information and feedback. Data collection and management as well as oversight of study sites has been delegated to the coordinating investigator.

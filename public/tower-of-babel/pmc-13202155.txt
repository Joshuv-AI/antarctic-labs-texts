Offloading orthotic insoles on healing of diabetic foot ulcers: a protocol for systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Ayesha Jamil, Hira bano Javaid
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDiabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) are a complication of uncontrolled and long-standing diabetes, often associated with peripheral neuropathy, and can result in amputation, prolonged hospitalisation and reduced quality of life. Orthotic insoles are commonly used to redistribute plantar pressure, offload the ulcer site and potentially accelerate wound healing. This systematic review aims to evaluate the effectiveness of offloading insoles in the healing of DFUs, with a focus on ulcer size reduction, recovery time, pain management, pressure redistribution and limb function, such as balance. A secondary objective is to compare the effects of different insole materials on healing outcomes, including satisfaction level towards the insole and overall quality of life.Methods and analysisA comprehensive literature search will be conducted in PubMed, MEDLINE, CINAHL and Google Scholar. Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) will be prioritised, while non-randomised studies that meet the eligibility criteria will also be considered. Studies published in English between 2000 and 2026 will be included. Eligible studies must assess the impact of orthotic insoles on DFUs, with outcome measures including pain intensity, pressure redistribution, wound healing and recovery time. Methodological quality will be assessed using the PEDro scale, while the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2) and the Risk of Bias in Non-randomised Studies of Interventions (ROBINS-I) will be applied as appropriate. Data will be extracted using a standardised form.Ethics and disseminationThis review does not require ethical approval. Introduction

Diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) are a serious clinical complication of diabetes that can lead to infection, amputation, reduced quality of life and increased healthcare costs and mortality.1 The global prevalence of DFUs is approximately 6.3%.2 In Pakistan, the prevalence of DFUs is 16.83%, with a slightly higher rate in females (17.52%) than in males (15.48%). Urban populations exhibit a higher prevalence (17.96%) compared with rural areas (13.91%).3

DFUs are predominant on the plantar surface of the foot, on high-pressure and friction areas, including the heel, ball of the foot, and tip of toes.4 They constitute the main reasons for non-traumatic lower extremity amputation (LEA), which has been reported to occur five times more frequently in diabetic patients compared with non-diabetics.5 Patients with diabetic foot ulcers commonly limit their activities to avoid loading the affected foot. However, this reduced mobility may lead to muscle weakness, loss of proprioception, joint stiffness, decreased soft tissue flexibility and eventually balance impairments.6

The management of diabetic foot ulcers (DFUs) requires a multidisciplinary approach, beginning with patient education, glycaemic control and conservative measures such as wound dressing7 as well as infection control.8 This approach extends to surgical intervention, including debridement and reconstructive procedures, based on the type and severity of the ulcer.9 10 Electrotherapeutic modalities, structured exercise programmes and orthotic devices play a crucial role in restoring mobility, optimising function and preventing ulcer recurrence.11

Offloading strategies, including removable and non-removable devices, such as footwear, orthotic insoles, casts, mobility aids and surgical offloading, are widely recommended to reduce plantar pressure and facilitate healing.12,14 Many studies have reported the efficacy of insoles in reducing peak plantar pressure, preventing ulcers and reducing the risk of recurrence.15,17 However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, only a limited number of high-quality studies have specifically evaluated the role of insoles and their features on healing outcomes and satisfaction level towards insoles, and overall quality of life. Therefore, a systematic review is warranted to consolidate existing evidence, identify knowledge and contextual gaps, and inform both clinical practice and future research directions. Our proposed review focuses exclusively on healing and management in patients with existing, active ulcers. This review will specifically identify and evaluate the studies investigating the customised offloading insoles of various materials that are designed specifically for active wounds, which are anatomically modified to provide targeted pressure relief at the precise location of the ulcer, and their impact on healing time and related outcomes. The objective of this review is to critically analyse the existing literature to determine the role of orthotic insoles and their material characteristics in redistributing plantar pressure, reducing pain intensity, wound size and recovery time in patients with diabetic foot ulcers. Additionally, the review will assess the impact of the insole on balance, satisfaction level and quality of life.

Methods

This study is planned to start in October 2025. The study selection and data extraction will be completed around February 2026. The analysis, evidence synthesis and the final review are expected to be completed in June 2026.

Protocol and registration

The review is written following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis for Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines. The protocol is registered in PROSPERO (ID: CRD420251145215).

Study design

This review will include randomised controlled trials (RCTs) published in English that evaluate the effects of offloading orthotic insoles on the healing of diabetic foot ulcers and patient-reported outcomes. Non-randomised studies (eg, cohort, case-control and quasi-experimental designs) will also be considered to supplement the evidence base where RCTs are limited. In addition, case reports, case series, cross-sectional studies, reviews, editorials, commentaries, conference abstracts, and animal-based or laboratory-based studies will be excluded.

Population

Eligible studies that will be conducted on male and female patients presenting with DFUs, aged 18 to 70 years, having type I or type II diabetes, and peripheral neuropathy.18 Studies will be excluded if participants have excessive uncontrolled lymphedema, active osteomyelitis, and/or gangrene, active or inactive Charcot foot, amputation proximal to the trans-metatarsal joint, leg length discrepancy, or if surgical management of diabetic foot ulcers is considered.19 20

Intervention

Studies on only orthotic insoles used to offload the ulcer area made up of different material compositions will be the intervention.

Comparator

Trials assessing orthotic insoles in comparison with standard care, different types of insoles, or physiotherapy management will be considered. Standard care may include wound debridement, moist wound dressings, infection control, glycaemic management, vascular assessment and patient education. Any surgical procedure and offloading cast, footwear, or shoes that are assumed to manage the wound will be excluded from the review.

Outcome

The primary outcomes will include pain intensity, redistribution of plantar pressure, and wound healing measured by changes in wound size and recovery time. The secondary outcomes will be balance, satisfaction level towards insoles, and overall quality of life.

Timeline

The review will involve the studies conducted between January 2000 and February 2026. The assessment points may include a baseline score till follow-up.

Search strategy

PubMed, CINAHL, CENTRAL, MEDLINE and Scopus databases will be explored for data collection. Studies published in English between January 2000 and February 2026 will be selected for review. Appropriate keywords using the Boolean operators, including AND, OR and NOT, appropriate truncation, and word proximity will be used to retrieve suitable literature. Selection process

Two independent reviewers will screen the titles, abstracts, and full texts of potentially eligible studies. Any disagreements will first be resolved through discussion; if consensus cannot be reached, a third independent reviewer will arbitrate. Where appropriate, a machine learning–-assisted tool may be used to support the resolution process.

Data collection process and data items

Data will be collected by two independent reviewers, with a third independent reviewer to resolve the conflict. A standardised data extraction form will be used to extract the information from the retrieved studies. The data extraction form will have four parts: study identification information, study characteristics, intervention details and outcome variables, and related measurement tools. The data extraction form is added in the online supplemental file 2.

Data management

All retrieved data will be imported into Zotero reference management software, where duplicate records will be removed. Screening and eligibility assessment will then be conducted independently by two reviewers using the systematic review software Rayyan. A standardised data extraction form will be applied to collect relevant information, which will be stored in Excel or within Rayyan. All files, including search outputs, extracted data, and analysis results, will be securely stored in password-protected folders with regular backups, and version control will be maintained. Quantitative data will be analysed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) or RevMan.

Study risk of bias assessment

The risk of bias will be evaluated using the Cochrane risk of bias tool (RoB) version 2.0 for randomised controlled trials, and risk of bias in non-randomised studies of intervention (ROBINS-I) for non-randomised studies. Two independent reviewers will conduct the assessments, and any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or, if necessary, by a third reviewer.

Effect measures

The dichotomous data will be presented using the OR or relative risk with a 95% CI. The continuous data of the same scale will be evaluated using the mean difference with a 95% CI, whereas for a different scale of the same construct, the standardised mean difference with a 95% CI will be used.

Synthesis methods

The characteristics of the included studies, such as design, publication year, population, interventions, comparators and outcomes, will first be summarised descriptively. Methodological and clinical outcomes will then be assessed for homogeneity. If sufficient homogeneity is observed, a meta-analysis will be conducted using appropriate effect measures, and statistical heterogeneity will be evaluated with the Chi-squareχ² test and I² statistic. Where substantial heterogeneity is detected, subgroup and sensitivity analyses will be performed to explore potential sources. Risk of bias will be incorporated into the synthesis, and the overall certainty of evidence for each outcome will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) approach.

Reporting bias assessment

At the review level, reporting biases will be explored using methods such as funnel plots and statistical tests for small-study effects, where sufficient studies are available.

Certainty assessment

The certainty assessment will be made using the GRADE approach.

Discussion

This review aims to critically evaluate the effects of orthotic foot insoles on the healing of DFUs. Orthotic insoles are believed to offload pressure from the ulcer area and redistribute plantar forces, thereby facilitating wound healing. To the best of our knowledge, the previous systematic review has not specifically addressed the effects of orthotic insoles on ulcer healing outcomes in the active phase in DFUs, including patients’ feedback towards satisfaction and quality of life. The findings of this review will contribute to the existing literature by clarifying the role of insoles in the management of grade I and II ulcers in diabetic patients, particularly in terms of pain reduction, recovery time, and any improvement in balance. The review will also identify the satisfaction level with insoles and their impact on the quality of life.

Ethics and dissemination

This review will use data from previously published studies, so ethical approval is not required. The findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and may also be presented at relevant national and international conferences. The results will provide evidence to inform clinicians, researchers and policymakers regarding the role of orthotic insoles in the management of diabetic foot ulcers.

QCD phase transition at finite temperature and chemical potential with the non-extensive statistics
Zhi-Ying Qin, Jia-Hao Shi, Jin-Peng Zhang, Jian Cao, Bo Feng, Wen-Chao Zhang, Hua Zheng, Shi-Jun Mao

Abstract
With the application of the non-extensive correction to the equation of state (EoS) in the parton (hadron resonance) gas at high (low) temperature and a smooth interpolation of these two equation of states, a non-extensive version of EoS is constructed to investigate the properties of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) phase transition at finite temperature ( T T ) and chemical potential ( μ \mu ). It is based on non-extensive statistics and characterized by a non-extensivity parameter q q , which measures the degrees of deviation from the thermal equilibrium. We explore the QCD phase transition with μ = 0 \mu=0 MeV in the chiral and non-chiral limit cases, as well as the transition with finite μ \mu in the non-chiral limit case. It is found that the dimensionless thermodynamic quantities such as the entropy density, the pressure, the energy density, the specific heat at constant volume and the trace anomaly are sensitive to the deviation of q q from unity and they become large both in the hadronic and quark-gluon plasma (QGP) phases with the increase of q q . In the chiral limit case, the sound velocity squared ( ( c s 2 ) q (c_{s}^{2})_{q} ) is insensitive to the deviation. However, in the non-chiral limit case, this deviation leads to nontrivial corrections of ( c s 2 ) q (c_{s}^{2})_{q} in the vicinity of the critical point ( T c T_{c} ) and at lower temperatures. Moreover, these dimensionless thermodynamic quantities are sensitive to the deviation of μ \mu from zero and they become large both in the hadronic and QGP phases with the increase of μ \mu . However, for ( c s 2 ) q (c_{s}^{2})_{q} , it increases in the vicinity of T c T_{c} and decreases at lower temperature. Finally, we observe that our results in the non-chiral limit case with q = 1 q=1 agree well with those from the Lattice QCD, the hadron resonance gas model, and the Thermal-Fist fit to the hadron yields in high energy nuclear collisions in the low temperature region up to T ∼ 150 T\sim 150 MeV.

II.1
Tsallis statistics

The non-extensive statistics was first proposed by Tsallis
nex1
. It generalizes the BG statistics and defines the entropy of a system as

S_{q}=k\left(1-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}R=1\end{subarray}}^{W}p_{R}^{q}\right)/(q-1),

(1)

where
k
is a positive constant;
W\in N
is the total number of microscopic configurations of the system;
p_{R}
is the probability associated with the
R^{th}
state and satisfies
\sum_{R=1}^{W}p_{R}=1
;
q
characterizes the degree of non-extensivity reflected in the following pseudo-additive rule for a composite system consisting of two individual systems
A
and
B
,

\frac{S_{q}(A+B)}{k}=\frac{S_{q}(A)}{k}+\frac{S_{q}(B)}{k}+(1-q)\frac{S_{q}(A)}{k}\frac{S_{q}(B)}{k}.

(2)

In the limit
q\rightarrow 1
, the expression in Eq. (
1
) approaches the BG entropy,

\lim\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\end{subarray}\rightarrow 1}S_{q}=-k\sum_{R=1}^{W}p_{R}\textrm{ln}p_{R}.

(3)

In order to determine the probability distribution of a state
R
in a grand canonical system, we need to maximize the entropy under the internal energy and particle number constraints,

\displaystyle\bar{E}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}R=1\end{subarray}}^{W}p_{R}^{q}E_{R},

(4)

\displaystyle\bar{N}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}R=1\end{subarray}}^{W}p_{R}^{q}N_{R},

(5)

where
\bar{E}
and
\bar{N}
are, respectively, the average energy and the average number of particles. The maximization of the entropy under constraints in Eqs. (
4
) and (
5
) leads to the variational equation,

\small\frac{\delta}{\delta p_{R}}\left(\frac{S_{q}}{k}-\alpha\sum_{R=1}^{W}p_{R}-\beta\sum_{R=1}^{W}p_{R}^{q}E_{R}-\gamma\sum_{R=1}^{W}p_{R}^{q}N_{R}\right)=0,

(6)

where
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
are the Lagrange multipliers associated with the total probability, the total energy and the total number of particles, respectively. Following the standard thermodynamic definitions, such as
\beta=1/T
and
\gamma=-\beta\mu
with
T
and
\mu
, respectively, being the temperature and the chemical potential of the system, the probability distribution of the state
R
in the grand-canonical ensemble is written as

p_{R}=[1+(q-1)(E_{R}-\mu N_{R})/T]^{1/(1-q)}/Z_{q},

(7)

where

Z_{q}=\sum_{R}[1+(q-1)(E_{R}-\mu N_{R})/T]^{1/(1-q)}

(8)

is the partition function of the grand-canonical system.

The quantum state of the system is uniquely determined when occupation numbers of the one-particle
states are provided. The total energy and the total particle numbers of the system in the state
R
then are automatically given by

\displaystyle E_{R}

\displaystyle=n_{1}\varepsilon_{1}+n_{2}\varepsilon_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}\varepsilon_{k}+\cdots,

(9)

\displaystyle N_{R}

\displaystyle=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{k}+\cdots,

(10)

where
\varepsilon_{k}
and

are, respectively, the energy of a particle and the number of particles in the state

. Substituting Eqs. (
9
) and (

), one gets the partition function as

\displaystyle Z_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\cdots,n_{k},\cdots}\exp_{q}\left(-\frac{(\varepsilon_{1}-\mu)n_{1}}{T}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\otimes_{q}\exp_{q}\left(-\frac{(\varepsilon_{2}-\mu)n_{2}}{T}\right)\otimes_{q}\cdots

\displaystyle\otimes_{q}\exp_{q}\left(-\frac{(\varepsilon_{k}-\mu)n_{k}}{T}\right)\otimes_{q}\cdots

\displaystyle=(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty})_{q}\sum_{n_{k=0}}^{\infty}\exp_{q}\left(-\frac{(\varepsilon_{k}-\mu)n_{k}}{T}\right),

where
\textrm{exp}_{q}(x)=[1+(1-q)x]^{(1/(1-q))}
,
\textrm{exp}_{q}(x)\otimes_{q}\textrm{exp}_{q}(y)=\textrm{exp}_{q}(x+y)
,

means the successive
q

Z_{q}\approx(\prod_{k=1}^{\infty})_{q}\left[1\pm{\rm exp}_{q}\left(-\frac{\varepsilon_{k}-\mu}{T}\right)\right]^{\pm d},

where the plus and minus signs, respectively, refer to fermions and bosons. The derivation of Eq. (

IV
Conclusions

In this work, by applying the non-extensive correction to the EoS in the parton (hadron resonance) gas at high (low) temperature and making a smooth interpolation of these two equation of states, we have constructed a non-extensive version of EoS to investigate the properties of QCD phase transition with zero chemical potential in the chiral and non-chiral limit cases. We have also investigated the QCD phase transition with finite chemical potential in the non-chiral limit case. We find that the dimensionless quantities
s_{q}/T^{3}
,
P_{q}/T^{4}
,
\varepsilon_{q}/T^{4}
,
(C_{V})_{q}/T^{3}
and
\Delta_{q}
are sensitive to the deviation of
q
from unity and they become large both in the hadronic and QGP phases with the increase of
q
. In the chiral limit case,
(c_{s}^{2})_{q}
is insensitive to the deviation of
q
from unity. However, in the non-chiral limit case, this deviation leads to nontrivial corrections of
(c_{s}^{2})_{q}
in the vicinity of the critical point and at lower temperatures. Moreover, these dimensionless thermodynamic quantities are sensitive to the deviation of
\mu
from zero and they become large both in the hadronic and QGP phases with the increase of
\mu
. However,
(c_{s}^{2})_{q}
increases in the vicinity of the phase transition temperature and decreases at lower temperature. Finally, in the non-chiral limit case, we observe that our results with
q=1
agree well with the LQCD, the HRG and the results form the Thermal-Fist fit to the hadron yield in high energy nuclear collisions in the low temperature region up to
T\sim 150
MeV. These findings will deepen our understanding for the properties of the QCD phase transition at finite temperature and chemical potential both in the extensive and non-extensive statistics.

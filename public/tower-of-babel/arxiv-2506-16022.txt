The Impact of Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} Prior Bias on Cosmological Parameter Estimation: Reconciling DESI DR2 BAO and Pantheon+ SNe Data Combination Results
Seokcheon Lee

Abstract
Recent cosmological parameter analyses combining DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillation (BAO) data with external probes, such as Pantheon+ Supernovae (SNe) observations, have reported deviations of the dark energy equation-of-state parameters ( ω 0 , ω a \omega_{0},\omega_{a} ) from the standard Λ \Lambda CDM model predictions ( ω 0 = − 1 , ω a = 0 \omega_{0}=-1,\omega_{a}=0 ). A notable aspect of these results is the role of Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} prior information from SNe, which is known to exhibit tension with BAO-only constraints. In this study, we rigorously investigate this effect through a statistical analysis using 100 mock DESI DR2 BAO data realizations. We demonstrate that the strong degeneracy between ω 0 \omega_{0} , ω a \omega_{a} , and Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} causes significant biases in the estimated dark energy parameters when the Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} prior mean deviates from its true underlying value. Specifically, applying an Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} prior mean of 0.33 (consistent with some SNe-only constraints) to mock data, assuming a true Λ \Lambda CDM universe ( Ω m0 = 0.30 , ω 0 = − 1 , ω a = 0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0}=0.30,\omega_{0}=-1,\omega_{a}=0 ), yields biased estimates such as ω 0 ≈ − 0.82 ± 0.06 \omega_{0}\approx-0.82\pm 0.06 and ω a ≈ − 0.82 ± 0.4 \omega_{a}\approx-0.82\pm 0.4 . This systematic shift, driven by the Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} prior, moves the estimated parameters towards the non- Λ \Lambda CDM region, offering a qualitative resemblance to outcomes reported in current combined DESI DR2 BAO + Pantheon+ SNe analyses (e.g., ω 0 = − 0.888 − 0.064 + 0.055 \omega_{0}=-0.888^{+0.055}_{-0.064} , ω a = − 0.17 ± 0.46 \omega_{a}=-0.17\pm 0.46 ). Our findings suggest that these observed non- Λ \Lambda CDM parameters may largely arise from statistical biases due to Ω m0 \Omega_{\rm{m}0} prior tensions between datasets. This study proposes a potential resolution to current cosmological tensions without necessarily invoking new physics.

and
\omega_{a}

III.3
Propagation of
1\sigma
Uncertainties

While the previous section focused on the biasing of parameter estimates due to an inconsistent
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior, this section examines the behavior of their associated uncertainties. Figure
3
displays the propagated
1\sigma
uncertainties (
\sigma_{\omega_{0}},\sigma_{\omega_{a}}
) for
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
as a function of the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior mean.

A crucial observation from Figure
3
(a), which presents results from combining all BAO observables, is that the
1\sigma
uncertainties for both
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
remain remarkably stable and exhibit very little variation across the scanned range of
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior means. This indicates that the magnitude of the uncertainty is primarily determined by the strength (or inverse variance,
1/\sigma^{2}
) of the prior information, rather than its central value. Since the uncertainty (width) of our
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
Gaussian prior is fixed throughout the analysis (as described in Section
II.2
), the amount of information it injects into the MCMC, particularly for breaking degeneracies, remains constant. Consequently, the resulting precision of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
does not significantly depend on whether the prior’s mean aligns with the true value or is biased. This reinforces the concept that the strength of a prior affects the precision, while its central value (or mean) affects the accuracy (i.e., introduces bias if off-center).

Despite the stability of the uncertainty magnitude across varying prior means, it is important to note that incorporating the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior significantly reduces the
1\sigma
uncertainties of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
compared to a hypothetical BAO-only analysis without such a prior. This is a direct consequence of the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior effectively constraining the parameter space, particularly along directions of strong degeneracy between
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
and the DE parameters
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
. By fixing or strongly constraining one end of a degenerate ellipse, the prior “collapses” the degeneracy direction, thereby improving the precision of the other parameters involved in that degeneracy.

The other panels of Figure
3
(e.g., Figures
3
(b) to (e)) further illustrate this by showing the error propagation for individual observables. Consistent with our findings in Section
III.1
regarding parameter sensitivities, observables with stronger constraining power on
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
(e.g.,
D_{V}/r_{d}
) generally lead to tighter constraints on
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
when combined with the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior, even if their uncertainty still remains largely independent of the prior mean.

In essence, while external
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior information is highly valuable for improving the precision of DE parameter measurements by breaking degeneracies, this precision comes with the critical caveat that if the prior mean is inconsistent with the true underlying cosmology (or the data’s true preference), the increased precision will be associated with biased estimates, as demonstrated in Section
III.2
. This highlights a fundamental challenge in cosmological parameter estimation: accurately quantifying uncertainties while simultaneously ensuring the robustness and unbiased nature of the central estimates, especially in the presence of tensions between different datasets.

IV
Conclusion

In this study, we have clearly demonstrated, using mock data, the potential biasing effect on
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
estimates in cosmological analyses that combine DESI DR2 BAO data with
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior information. Even under the assumption that the Universe adheres to the
\Lambda
CDM model, we found that if the mean of the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior deviates from its true
\Lambda
CDM value, the strong degeneracies with
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
can lead to significantly biased estimates of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
from their
\Lambda
CDM predictions. Specifically, when a higher
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior mean, such as 0.33, was applied,
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
were estimated to non-
\Lambda
CDM values, deviating substantially from
\Lambda
CDM.

These findings provide strong evidence that the non-
\Lambda
CDM estimates of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
reported in recent combined analyses of DESI DR2 BAO and SNe data may not necessarily indicate new physical phenomena. Instead, they could primarily stem from statistical biases arising from a tension between the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
value preferred by the DESI DR2 BAO data itself and the
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
value preferred by SNe data. Our simulations show that even if the true underlying cosmology is
\Lambda
CDM, an inconsistent
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
prior can cause the combined analysis to yield biased
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
results. Furthermore, our analysis revealed that while such a prior biases the central estimates, it also robustly reduces the
1\sigma
uncertainties of
\omega_{0}
and
\omega_{a}
, making the biased results appear statistically robust at higher confidence levels. This phenomenon might contribute to the observed cosmological tensions.

Our analysis highlights the critical need for careful consideration of prior selection and its potential biasing effects in future combined analyses of cosmological observational data. If the tensions in
\Omega_{\rm{m}0}
measurements between different observations persist, this represents a significant systematic uncertainty in the estimation of DE EOS parameters. Future research should focus on exploring the fundamental origins of these inter-dataset tensions and developing novel statistical methodologies to mitigate such biases, to ensure the robustness and accuracy of cosmological parameter constraints.

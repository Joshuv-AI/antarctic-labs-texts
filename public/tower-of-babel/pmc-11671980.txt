At a glance
Authors: Frank Larkin
Published: 2024

Influence of surgeon learning on outcomes in new ophthalmic procedures: quantified nationwide evidence in endothelial corneal transplantation (see page 3)

A national corneal transplant registry analysis of 11,516 endothelial keratoplasty procedures found clear evidence that increasing surgeon experience in that procedure was associated with longer two-year transplant survival. This strongly supports best feasible arrangements for surgeon training and mentoring at the introduction of new procedures.

Evaluation of adjuvant role of topical cyclosporine 1% in acute Stevens-Johnson syndrome: a randomised control trial (see page 10)

This trial examined the usefulness of short-term 1% topical ciclosporin in cases of acute Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS). Additional therapy with 1% ciclosporin was not efficacious over standard therapy in stabilising the ocular surface in cases of acute SJS.

Predisposing factors, clinical and microbiological insights of bacterial keratitis: analysis of 354 cases from a leading French academic centre (see page 15)

Twenty years following an equivalent study, contact lens wear remained the leading risk factor for bacterial keratitis. A majority of Gram-negative bacteria were found, with a marked increase in Moraxella keratitis and a low rate of antibacterial drug resistance.

Retrospective analysis on the outcomes of contact lens-associated keratitis in a tertiary centre: an evidence-based management protocol to optimise resource allocation (see page 21)

Based on their proposed keratitis grading system, the authors' study provides a reportedly safe, evidence-based management protocol for patients with contact lens associated keratitis (CLAK). This should improve resource allocation and at a time of increasing demand for eye care services by reducing unnecessary patient visits.

Treatment exit options for non-infectious uveitis registry: participant characteristics at 3 years (see page 27)

In a real-world registry, most non-infectious non-anterior uveitis cases were found to require immunosuppression with biologic and/or conventional disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs but present with preserved good vision.

Methotrexate versus conventional disease modifying anti rheumatic drugs in the treatment of non-anterior sarcoidosis-associated uveitis (see page 34)

In this retrospective study in non-anterior sarcoidosis-associated uveitis, methotrexate was compared with mycophenolate mofetil and azathioprine. Methotrexate had lower rates of relapse or serious adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation, was more effective for control of ocular inflammation, prevention of low visual acuity, and spared corticosteroid.

Systemic and ocular outcomes in TB-immunoreactive patients receiving immunomodulatory therapy for non-infectious uveitis: a case-control study (see page 41)

Non-infectious uveitis patients with positive TB tests on long-term immunomodulatory therapy did not have any adverse ocular outcomes and very low risk of systemic TB reactivation.

Association between metformin use with circumpapillary retinal nerve fibre layer thickness and capillary vessel density in glaucoma (see page 45)

In this cross-sectional study, use of metformin was associated with higher capillary vessel density and thicker circumpapillary retinal nerve fibre layer thickness in patients with glaucoma, suggesting protective effects on glaucoma.

Central corneal thickness and the risk of primary open-angle glaucoma: a Mendelian randomiaation mediation analysis (see page 52)

The authors obtained human genetic data to assess through Mendelian randomisation whether central corneal thickness (CCT) is causally associated with primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG). Data supported a positive association between CCT and POAG.

Novel hyperparallel optical coherence tomography for angle closure assessment: comparison with swept-source OCT and gonioscopy (see page 58)

Hyperparallel optical coherence tomography imaging is a novel tool for volumetric assessment of the anterior chamber angle and provides improved visualisation of anatomical structures, including the trabecular meshwork.

Retinoblastoma survival and enucleation outcomes in 41 countries from the African continent (see page 64)

Compared with the average global data, the African continent has worse survival rates and eye salvage in retinoblastoma.

Deep sequencing as a diagnostic tool in patients with suspected primary vitreoretinal lymphoma (see page 70)

Metagenomic deep sequencing detected pathogenic mutations related to lymphoma in most patients who were diagnosed with vitreoretinal lymphoma, highlighting its potential to improve lymphoma detection as an adjunctive diagnostic tool.

Local tumour control and patient survival after ruthenium-106 brachytherapy for small choroidal melanoma (see page 76)

Ru-106 brachytherapy for small choroidal melanomas achieved tumour control and ocular survival in 83% and 95.8% of eyes, and metastasis and death rates of 4.8% and 2.3% at 5 years respectively.

Risk factors and patterns for progression of fellow-eye myopic traction maculopathy: a three-year retrospective cohort study (see page 83)

This 3 year retrospective cohort study revealed that preretinal tractional structures and posterior staphyloma were the risk factors for the progression of myopic traction maculopathy, while a dome-shaped macula exhibited a protective effect.

Foveal atrophy in patients with active central serous chorioretinopathy at first presentation: characteristics and treatment outcomes (see page 89)

In central serous chorioretinopathy, foveal atrophy at first presentation is associated with persistent shallow subretinal fluid, low treatment efficacy, and poor visual prognosis.

Retinotopic cortical mapping in objective functional monitoring of macular therapy (see page 98)

This study shows that fMRI has the potential to provide quantitative biomarkers at the level of the visual cortex to serve as an objective outcome measure of treatments for macular diseases. It contributes towards best-practice methods utilising fMRI for monitoring macular function.

Clinical spectrum and possible pathogenesis of progressive outer retinal necrosis (see page 107)

Previous studies have pointed out that the name of Progressive Outer Retinal Necrosis (PORN) does not reflect the virus’s anatomic involvement correctly. This study found that it involves the entire neurosensory retina, rather than only outer segment.

Reattachment rate with pneumatic retinopexy versus pars plana vitrectomy for single break rhegmatogenous retinal detachment (see page 113)

This post hoc analysis of two clinical trials, PIVOT and ALIGN, found no significant difference in the primary anatomic reattachment rate at 1 year following pneumatic retinopexy vs vitrectomy for rhegmatogenous retinal detachment with a single break.

Pros and cons of internal limiting membrane peeling during epiretinal membrane surgery: a randomised clinical trial with microperimetry (PEELING) (see page 119)

When the internal limiting membrane (ILM) is not spontaneously peeled off during epiretinal membrane surgery, whether or not surgeons should remove it remains controversial. This study summarises the advatages and disadvantages of active ILM peeling.

Long-term surgical outcomes and prognostic factors for advanced-stage retinopathy of prematurity after vitrectomy (see page 126)

This long-term study assessed advanced ROP after vitrectomy with a follow-up period of at least five years. The major factors affecting the outcomes included stage of the disease, presence of Plus disease, and a vitrectomy procedure combined with lensectomy.

Paediatric staphyloma classification: new perspectives via ultrawide field three-dimensional swept-source optical coherence tomographic angiography (see page 133)

Ultrawide field 3D swept source OCT angiography imaging, integrating PCM and PHM strategies, was used to analyse paediatric staphylomas. These were found to exhibit a unique presentation, divergent from adults, with two novel types identified.

Short-term effects of sunlight exposure on fundus blood flow perfusion in children: a randomised controlled trial (see page 139)

Sunlight exposure was found to restore fundus blood flow perfusion increased by near work. This short-time effect was intensity- and time-dependent, and choroidal perfusion was more sensitive compared with superficial and deep retinal perfusion.

Shaping school for childhood myopia: the association between floor area ratio of school environment and myopia in China (see page 146)

Using geographic information system technology, this prospective cohort study demonstrated a negative association between floor area ratio of school built environment and myopia onset. Integrating the floor area ratio into school planning may help to promote vision health in urban schoolchildren.

Psychological and functional outcomes of horizontal squint surgery in adults with no preoperative diplopia using Quality-of-Life AS-20 questionnaire (see page 152)

Only 20% of clinical commissioning groups now fund surgery for adult strabismus without diplopia. The authors audited results of their surgery in this patient group and found it was successful with considerable psychological benefits.

Handgrip strength and risks of diabetic vascular complications: evidence from Guangzhou diabetic eye study and UK cohorts (see page 157)

Reduced handgrip strength (HGS) is significantly associated with a higher risk of diabetic complications and accelerated microvascular damage. HGS could serve as a practical indicator of vascular health in patients with pre-diabetes and diabetes.

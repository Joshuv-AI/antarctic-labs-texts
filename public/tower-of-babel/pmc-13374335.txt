CONVICTION-CHIK study on the effect of convalescent plasma for mother-to-child Chikungunya infection: protocol for a multicentre, open label, single-arm, phase II trial
Authors: Silvia Iacobelli, Syria Laperche, Armelle Pasquet, Kassim Papa, Guillaume Maccio, Julie Guinaud, Soumeth Abasse, Marc Bardou, Cyril Ferdynus, the CONVICTION-CHIK study group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMother-to-child peripartum transmission of Chikungunya infection may induce severe illness with neonatal encephalitis/encephalopathy, leading to major neurological sequalae in offspring. There is not, at present, any licensed vaccine or treatment for Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) disease in pregnant women or neonates. However, there are a range of potential treatments, including passive immune therapies by hyperimmune antibodies or blood products, which warrant further investigation. Our study aims to determine whether the early administration of CHIKV convalescent plasma donor is effective in preventing several adverse outcomes in neonates exposed to maternal peripartum CHIKV infection.MethodsCONVICTION-CHIK is a multicentre, interventional, phase II, single-arm trial, that will be conducted in two French overseas departments in the Indian Ocean throughout 2026. Neonates with the following criteria will be included: (1) mother with a clinical and/or biological diagnosis of CHIKV infection occurring between day -2 and + 2 around delivery; (2) cared for in a 3rd level neonatal intensive care unit; (3) treatment administration possible within 12 h from diagnosis of maternal infection. The intervention consists in the transfusion of 25 mL/kg of ABO CHIKV convalescent plasma over 4 h. The primary endpoint is the proportion of newborn infants treated with CHIKV convalescent plasma who survive without encephalitis/encephalopathy in the first 5 days of life. This outcome will also be assessed in a parallel cohort of untreated newborn infants exposed to peripartum CHIKV infection. Secondary endpoints are clinical, biological, and brain imaging findings of neonatal CHIKV infection, in treated and untreated neonates. Based on an expected decrease in the primary endpoint from 25% to 3% in the intervention group, the required sample size is 30 treated neonates. A parallel cohort of 30 contemporaneous untreated neonates will be enrolled to provide exploratory context for the observed treatment effect.DiscussionIf successful, this research will expand the therapeutic arsenal for treating peripartum neonatal CHIKV infection, in addition to symptomatic management of organ failure. The results of this study will allow to consider the feasibility to implement the systematic collection of convalescent plasma following Chikungunya outbreaks, for the management of subsequent epidemics.Trial registrationNCT07190560 (registered 17 September 2025).Keywords: Arboviruses, Alphavirus, Infant, Critical illness, Pain, Fever, Encephalitis, Neurologic manifestation, Vertical infection, Maternal labour

Background

The chikungunya virus (CHIKV) re-emerged on Reunion Island in August 2024, 19 years after the first major outbreak. The new epidemic rapidly affected all the island and peaked between weeks 13 and 17 of 2025, with up to more than 23,000 cases per week [1]. During this period, several cases of infection were observed in pregnant women. Like in the 2005–2006 Reunion Island epidemic [2], in case of maternal infection during the “peripartum” period, defined as the period between day −2 and day + 2 around delivery, some severe cases of CHIKV infection were observed in offspring. Actually, according to data from the 2005–2006 Reunion Island spread [2], follow-up publications [3, 4] and systematic reviews and meta-analyses [5, 6], up to 62% of neonates exposed to maternal peripartum infection may develop a severe infection few days after birth, and a rate of neonatal encephalitis between 52.6% and 70% is reported in infected individuals. The prevalence of major neurological sequelae and disability after neonatal CHIKV encephalitis is around 50% among surviving children [3–5].

To date, there is no specific therapeutic measure to prevent CHIKV vertical transmission, or to treat CHIKV neonatal infection. Data from the literature suggest that, in case of vertical, peripartum transmission, offspring do not benefit from the transplacental passage of maternal IgG antibodies [2]. This is also the case, for instance, for varicella peripartum exposure [7], a situation that justifies the passive antibody prophylaxis by intravenous anti-Varicella-Zoster immunoglobulins in newborn infants, at the earliest possible date after birth [8]. Thus, following the 2005–2006 Reunion Island epidemic, the preventive and curative effects of human hyperimmune anti-CHIKV immunoglobulins (CHIKV-Ig), purified from plasma samples of Chikungunya convalescent donors, were investigated in an animal model of CHIKV infection [9]. This study provided evidence that, in mouse neonates born to viraemic mothers, CHIKV donor antibodies exhibited a high in vitro neutralising activity, and powerful in vivo prophylactic and therapeutic efficacy against CHIKV infection. In 2014, a pilot, open-label, non-randomised phase I/II clinical trial (NCT02230163) was undertaken in Guadeloupe, using the pool of CHIKV-Ig manufactured in Reunion Island [10]. The trial aimed to evaluate tolerability and safety of CHIKV-Ig given intravenously to 40 neonates exposed to a peripartum maternal infection. Unfortunately, the study was not completed, the epidemic having ended shortly after the research implementation, and the CHIKV-Ig stock expired on 2019. Actually, in case of CHIKV outbreaks, the option of prophylactic treatment based on CHIKV-Ig presents important drawbacks, as it requires a manufacturing process incompatible with short-term availability. In contrast, in other epidemic contexts, published data support the feasibility, acceptability and safety of passive antibody therapy by convalescent plasma (CP) transfusion [11]. Transfusion of CP has been explored over the last 20 years for the treatment of viral severe acute respiratory syndrome, avian influenza [11, 12], COVID-19 [13], as well as during the Ebola epidemic that affected Guinea, Sierra Leone and Liberia [14]. On that occasion, efficacy data allowed concluding that, in future trials, donors with high anti-Ebola virus titres should be preferred to enhance CP effects against viremia [15, 16].

In the perspective of a resurgence of the Chikungunya epidemic in French overseas or mainland departments, we successfully set up a collection of CHIKV convalescent donor plasma following the 2025 spread in La Reunion, and we conceived and designed the present research. This paper describes the protocol of a phase II study to evaluate the effectiveness, on neonatal outcomes, of the intravenous infusion of CHIKV convalescent plasma (CHIKV-CP), in newborn infants exposed to maternal peripartum CHIKV infection.

Methods/design

Primary objective

The main objective of the research is to evaluate the effectiveness of the early transfusion of CHIKV-CP to prevent the occurrence of CHIKV encephalitis/encephalopathy (EE) in newborn infants whose mother has developed a peripartum CHIKV infection. Maternal peripartum infection is defined as the presence of clinical or biological criteria of CHIKV infection, occurring between day −2 and day + 2 around delivery. Clinical criterion definition is “fever > 38.5 °C (101.3 °F) and joint pain with acute onset”, associated or not, with headache, myalgia, exanthema. Biological criterion definition is “positive blood CHIKV real-time PCR (RT-PCR) or serology”.

Secondary objectives

To assess: 1) the safety of CHIKV-CP transfusion in treated neonates; 2) the severity and the causality of adverse events (AEs) in treated neonates; 3) the proportion of neonatal EE in a parallel cohort of untreated neonates whose mother has developed peripartum CHIKV infection; 4) the occurrence, in treated and untreated neonates, of clinical signs of neonatal CHIKV infection within the first 5 days of life: apnoea, pain, fever, feeding difficulties related to temporomandibular joint dysfunction, or cutaneous manifestations (generalised erythema, skin peeling, vesiculobullous dermatitis and oedematous limb extremities); 5) cord blood samples for Chikungunya viral and serological testing in treated and untreated neonates; 6) the presence of CHIKV in blood on day of life (DOL) 4 (DOL 0 being the day of birth) in treated and untreated neonates; 7) the presence of CHIKV in blood on DOL7 in treated infants; 8) the presence of IgG/IgM against CHIKV on DOL4 and DOL7 in treated neonates; 9) the presence of CHIKV in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of infected newborn infants (treated and untreated-ones); 10) the occurrence of EEG abnormalities in all newborn infants with a positive blood CHIKV RT-PCR on DOL4; 11) MRI abnormalities in all newborn infants with a positive CHIKV RT-PCR on DOL4; 12) the clinical and neurological examination of treated infants at the follow-up visit performed between 1 and 3 months of age.

Study design

The CONVICTION-CHIK study is a multicentre, open label, single-arm, phase II trial. This is a study focused on newborn infants exposed to peripartum CHIKV infection and treated by CHIKV-CP. The primary endpoint (occurrence of CHIKV EE) will also be assessed in a parallel cohort of untreated newborn infants exposed to peripartum CHIKV infection.

Study setting

Started in April 2026, CONVICTION-CHIK is an ongoing study in all the three 3rd level neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) of Reunion Island and Mayotte French Departments.

Participants

Eligibility criteria – intervention group

The study will include newborn infants: 1) whose mother has a clinical and/or biological diagnosis of CHIKV infection (as above defined) occurring between day −2 and day + 2 around delivery, and 2) cared for in a 3rd level NICU in Mayotte or in Reunion Island University Hospital, and 3) eligible to CHIKV-CP treatment. Treatment eligibility is based on the following criteria: a) if maternal infection has been diagnosed between day −2 and day + 2 around delivery, treatment can be administered within the first 12 h of life; or b) if maternal infection has been diagnosed between DOL0 and DOL2, treatment can be administered within 12 h of maternal diagnosis.

Eligibility criteria – parallel cohort

The parallel cohort will include newborn infants with the same above criteria: 1), and 2), and 3) who cannot enter the phase II study, because maternal infection has been diagnosed outside the time-limits set for CHIKV-CP administration, or because parent consent for therapy cannot be obtained or is refused.

Non-inclusion criteria

For both the intervention group and the parallel cohort the only non-inclusion criterion is: newborn infant presenting with hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy requiring therapeutic hypothermia.

Exclusion criteria

These will be applied in both treated and untreated infants in case of: 1) invalidated biological diagnosis of CHIKV infection in the mother; 2) proven CHIKV neonatal infection at cord blood (antenatal infection).

Intervention

Treated neonates will receive 25 mL/kg ABO CHIKV-CP, given as a slow infusion over 4 h. A neutralization titre of ≥ 1/640 for CHIKV has been achieved in the CP that will be used in this trial (which is higher than conventionally used as high-titre plasma). Patients will be transfused using standard NICU procedures. All treated patients will be under close observation for transfusion related adverse reactions during and up to 4 h after transfusion and all eventual AEs will be reported. If an attending physician assesses that a patient is suffering from an AE induced by the plasma infusion and requiring treatment, the infusion will be stopped for that patient.

Outcomes

Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is the proportion of newborn infants treated with CHIKV-CP who survive without EE in the first 5 days of life. EE is defined by the presence of at least one among the following clinical signs: lethargy (disturbance of consciousness, coma); seizures; hypotonia (inducing sucking, swallowing or respiratory weakness).

Secondary endpoints

The following endpoints (linked with the above secondary outcomes) will be evaluated: 1) clinical signs of poor transfusion tolerance (arterial hypotension, desaturation, fever); 2) AEs according to their severity and causality; 3) proportion of newborn infants with EE in the parallel, untreated cohort; 4) newborn infants with one or more clinical signs of neonatal CHIKV infection (apnea, pain, fever, feeding difficulties, skin lesions); 5) positive biological diagnosis on cord blood sample (CHIKV RT-PCR or CHIKV IgG/IgM immunoglobulins); 6) positive blood CHIKV RT-PCR (DOL4); 7) positive blood CHIKV RT-PCR (DOL7); 8) CHIKV IgG/IgM immunoglobulin titre (DOL 4 and DOL7); 9) positive CSF CHIKV RT-PCR; 10) proportion of newborn infants with seizures or EEG abnormalities; 11) proportion of newborn infants with MRI abnormal findings; 12) proportion of newborn infants with normal neurological and clinical assessment.

Participant recruitment and timeline

Eligible infants will be identified and included in the maternity ward or in the neonatal unit where they are cared for after birth. In the intervention group, regardless the clinical exam at the first visit, CHIKV-CP transfusion will be performed in the NICU, in order to insure close observation for transfusion-related adverse reactions during and up to 4 h after transfusion. Aside from the experimental intervention, treated infants will be cared for according to standard current practice for peripartum neonatal exposure to CHIKV. It is worth noting that, starting March 2025, the three recruiting centers have the same management protocol of clinical monitoring and treatment of infants exposed to peripartum CHIKV or infected by mother-to-child CHIKV transmission. Figure 1 shows the neonate management in both the intervention and in the parallel cohort.

Information sheet and consent forms for the intervention and the parallel group are available in 3 languages (French, Shimaore and Kibushi—two territorialized regional languages in Mayotte department). The duration of the inclusion period will be 7 days for treated infants and 5 days for the parallel cohort. The duration of follow-up in treated participants will be three months. The recruitment period will be of 24 months

Sample size

The primary hypothesis is that early administration of CHIKV-CP reduces the risk of neonatal EE among neonates exposed to peripartum maternal infection. Published cohort data [2] suggest that approximately 25% of exposed neonates develop EE in the absence of specific treatment.

The sample size calculation for the treated group was based on a one-sample exact binomial test, with H0: p ≥ 0.25 versus H1: p < 0.25, a one-sided α risk of 2.5% and 90% power. Under the assumption that CHIKV-CP can reduce the EE rate from 25% to 3% (absolute difference of 22 percentage points), 27 treated neonates will be required. Allowing for a 10% dropout rate, the target number of treated neonates is increased to 30 to ensure at least 27 analyzable subjects for the primary objective. In addition, to provide exploratory context for the observed treatment effect, a parallel cohort of 30 contemporaneous untreated neonates will be enrolled. Based on 1,000 simulation runs assuming encephalopathy risk of 3% and 25% in treated and untreated groups respectively, enrolling 30 untreated subjects provides approximately 80% power to detect this difference using propensity-score-adjusted logistic regression with four baseline covariates, contingent on model convergence. The total planned sample size is therefore 60 neonates: 30 treated and 30 untreated.

Study design justification

This phase II interventional trial focuses on treated neonates and includes a contemporaneous untreated parallel cohort instead of individual randomization. This design was chosen for several practical and ethical reasons. First, the number of eligible neonates exposed to maternal peripartum CHIKV infection during an epidemic is inherently limited, and the logistical challenges of delivering high-titre convalescent plasma within a narrow therapeutic window make strict randomization difficult to implement. Second, given the severity of neonatal encephalopathy and the absence of established specific treatments, randomly withholding a potentially beneficial intervention was deemed ethically unjustifiable in the local context. Third, the availability of plasma units depends on voluntary donations from convalescent adults and may vary unpredictably over time, further limiting the feasibility of a conventional randomized design. Consequently, the primary objective is defined exclusively within the treated group, using a pre-specified historical benchmark [2] for comparison. The untreated parallel cohort will be managed according to the same regional neonatal protocol, and used solely for secondary, exploratory comparative analyses conducted within a causal inference framework (using propensity scores and related methods) to control for confounding by indication. To minimize biases inherent to this non-randomized design, all eligible neonates (both treated and untreated) will be enrolled contemporaneously during the epidemic. Reasons for non-administration of CHIKV-CP will be systematically documented, and clinical follow-up procedures will be identical across both groups.

Data collection methods

Data will be collected through the eCRF. Inclusion and non-inclusion criteria will be prospectively entered into the eCRF. All other data will be extracted from individual neonatal records in each of the participating centres, and collected into the eCRF by clinical research assistants.

Data management

The data will be managed by the Clinical Investigation Centre (CIC INSERM 1410) at Reunion Island University Hospital.

Statistical method

Descriptive analyses

Baseline maternal and neonatal characteristics will be described by treatment group (treated vs. untreated). Categorical variables will be reported as counts and percentages with 95% confidence intervals. Continuous variables will be summarized as mean ± standard deviation with 95% confidence intervals if approximately normally distributed, or as median and interquartile range (25th–75th percentiles) otherwise. Between-group comparisons will be performed using χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, as appropriate. The extent and pattern of missing data will be reported for each variable.

Primary outcome analysis

The primary endpoint is the proportion of treated neonates who develop EE within the first 5 days of life. This proportion will be estimated in the treated group and presented with a one-sided 97.5% exact Clopper–Pearson confidence interval. The primary analysis will test whether the observed encephalopathy rate is significantly lower than the historical reference rate of 25% reported in previous cohorts of neonates exposed to peripartum maternal CHIKV infection. A one-sided exact binomial test will be used, with H0: p ≥ 0.25 versus H1: p < 0.25. The treatment will be considered effective if the upper bound of the one-sided 97.5% confidence interval falls strictly below 25%.

Secondary outcomes analysis

Secondary endpoints will be analyzed primarily in a descriptive manner. For treated neonates, tolerability will be assessed by estimating the proportions of AEs and serious adverse events (SAEs) according to timing (during or immediately after transfusion, and up to day 7), severity, and causality, along with two-sided 95% exact Clopper–Pearson confidence intervals. In the untreated parallel cohort, the proportion of neonates who develop encephalopathy and its 95% confidence interval will be estimated. Additional secondary outcomes—including clinical signs of neonatal CHIKV infection during the first 5 days of life, virological and serological markers (RT-PCR and IgG/IgM at birth, day 4, and day 7), CSF fluid findings, EEG abnormalities, MRI lesions, and short-term neurological status at 1–3 months—will be summarized similarly for treated and untreated neonates. Where informative, exploratory between-group comparisons (treated vs. untreated) will be performed using χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, and Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables. To address confounding by indication, we will implement causal inference approaches where feasible, such as propensity-score matching, inverse probability of treatment weighting, or covariate adjustment, guided by directed acyclic graphs for variable selection. Doubly robust estimators or instrumental variable methods may be considered if the data structure permits. Given the limited sample size and expected low event rates, these comparative analyses will remain strictly exploratory and interpreted with appropriate caution. No adjustment for multiple comparisons is planned.

Safety analysis

Safety will be evaluated in all neonates who receive any amount of CHIKV-CP. Transfusion-related immediate reactions (including hypotension, desaturation, fever, and allergic manifestations) and all SAEs occurring during the index hospitalization will be prospectively collected and reported in accordance with regulatory requirements. A detailed descriptive analysis of the nature, frequency, severity, causality, and outcome of AEs and serious SAEs will be conducted, with results presented as counts and percentages with exact 95% confidence intervals.

Safety interim analysis

An interim safety analysis is planned after the first 15 treated neonates have completed follow-up up to day 7. The primary safety endpoint is the proportion of treatment-related SAEs between DOL0 and DOL7. This proportion will be estimated with an exact 95% Clopper–Pearson confidence interval. In the event of any unexpected serious transfusion reaction, the independent data and safety monitoring committee will be convened to review the cases and may recommend continuation, modification or early termination of the trial.

Significance threshold

All statistical tests will be two-sided with a significance level of 5%, except for the primary efficacy analysis, which will use a one-sided alpha of 2.5% as pre-specified in the protocol. Corresponding 95% (or one-sided 97.5%) confidence intervals will be reported. Given the exploratory nature of the secondary analyses, no formal correction for multiplicity will be applied.

Statistical software

The analyses will be performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Cary NC) software. Statisticians will be blinded to the study groups.

Monitoring

This trial is an interventional study for which the risk for the participant is higher than the minimal risk of routine treatment. According to the French legislation (Jardé low) a formal monitoring for quality and regulatory compliance is requested for this high-risk interventional research involving human subjects. The monitoring will be supervised by the sponsor (Reunion Island University Hospital) in all of the participating centres. The frequency of quality compliance will depend on inclusion rates, questions and pending issues from earlier monitor visits.

Harms

In France, haemovigilance reporting system of reactions and events during transfusion is mandatory. Thus, in case of any adverse reaction in patients receiving labile blood products, the heath provider in charge of the patient immediately informs the regional haemovigilance and transfusion safety coordinator. Depending on their seriousness, reactions and events are reported and analysed through a multi-level framework. This procedure is applied in the present trial. Moreover, AEs related to CHIKV-CP, as also any harmful event occurring in patients participating in this study (whether or not in relation with the study) must be reported to the various vigilance unit in accordance with the regulation in force.

Steering committee—data and safety monitoring committee

The sponsor (Reunion Island University Hospital) is responsible for all aspects of the study: ethical, regulatory, study conduction, data management and publication strategy. The steering committee is composed of the principal investigators of the 3 centres, 3 paediatric specialists for infectious diseases, 2 virologists, 2 sponsor officers, and a methodologist. A meeting is held immediately before the study implementation, then as needed according to the inclusion rhythm and pending questions, and at the end of the study. The role of the steering committee is to ensure that the research is running according to study protocol and to share solutions to overcome difficulties in recruiting participants. The data monitoring and safety committee (DSMC) is composed of external members: two neonatologists, who specifically take care of neonates with acute encephalopathy and infectious diseases, a methodologist, and a pharmacologist with experience in study monitoring. A meeting is held immediately before the study implementation, then according to the inclusion rhythm, and in case of severe AEs.

The role if the DSMC is to review and discuss safety data and SAEs in real time, taking immediate action as necessary, and ensuring the trial progresses in an informed manner. The DSMC provides real-time advice in the following situations: premature termination of the research; significant protocol amendments; result interpretation of safety interim analysis.

Auditing

An audit may be performed at any time by persons mandated by the sponsor (or by the competent authority), and independent of those in charge of the study.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of this research.

The informed consent of both parents or that of the neonate legal representative will be obtained prior to inclusion for infants in the interventional group. Parent/Guardian oral consent is requested for the collection of data concerning infants of the parallel, untreated cohort.

Dissemination policy

Results will be published in peer-reviewed journals as well as presented and disseminated at conferences.

Discussion

Chikungunya outbreaks are occurring with increasing regularity around the world—with over 480,000 cases reported on 2025 globally [17]. Especially during epidemics, mother-to-child disease prevention is important, as peripartum CHIKV infection entails a substantial burden to health care system and to society. This will be the first study to evaluate the effectiveness of CP administration in preventing mother-to-child CHIKV infection and its ominous complications in neonates. To date, there is no specific and effective antiviral therapy for mother-to-child CHIKV transmission, and vaccines in pregnant women are not available, the only effective preventive measures consisting in mother protection against mosquito bites and vector control. Treatment by CHIKV-CP is not supposed to replace any other public health policies and studies aimed to promote vector surveillance and potential control strategies. However, while waiting for research advances, CHIKV-CP administration represents a real hope in a context of epidemic outbreak. Moreover, the suggested strategy is particularly relevant, given that the collected CP units used in this study contain high titres of neutralizing antibodies against the last circulating CHIKV strain, and they will be administered early.

If successful, this research will expand the therapeutic arsenal for treating peripartum neonatal CHIKV infection, in addition to symptomatic management of organ failure.

The results of this study will allow to consider the feasibility to implement the systematic collection of CP following Chikungunya outbreaks, for the management of subsequent epidemic, or with the purpose of manufacturing hyperimmune CHIKV-IgG with longer expiration dates. Finally, our study could open the door to further research on the characterization and genotyping of anti-CHIKV neutralizing antibodies, and thus help design strategies to produce synthetic CHIKV antibodies.

Dominion Energy South Carolina, Inc.; Notice of Application Ready 
for Environmental Analysis and Soliciting Comments, Recommendations, 
Terms and Conditions, and Prescriptions

    Take notice that the following hydroelectric application has been 
filed with the Commission and is available for public inspection.
    a. Type of Application: New Major License.
    b. Project No.: 2535-129.
    c. Date Filed: October 27, 2023.
    d. Applicant: Dominion Energy South Carolina, Inc. (Dominion

Creek Project).
    f. Location: At the confluence of Stevens Creek and the Savannah 
River in Edgefield and McCormick Counties, South Carolina, and Columbia 
County, Georgia. The project occupies approximately 104 acres of 
federal land administered by the U.S. Forest Service.

    l. Project Description: The project consists of: (1) a 2,400-acre 
reservoir with a storage capacity of 23,699-acre-feet at a full pond 
elevation of 187.5 feet National Geodetic Vertical Datum 1929 (NGVD 
29); (2) a dam consisting of, from the southwestern (Georgia) shore to 
the northeastern (South Carolina) shore, (a) a 388-foot-long powerhouse 
intake, integral with the dam, protected by trashracks with 3.75-inch 
clear bar spacing, (b) a 102.5-foot-long non-overflow section with a 
top elevation of 198.54 feet NGVD 29, (c) an 85-foot-wide, 165.5-foot-
long inoperable concrete gravity navigation lock, with a lock chamber 
that is 30-feet-wide, 150-feet-long, and has a 29-foot-lift, located 
between the powerhouse intake and spillway section, (d) a 1,000-foot-
long, 5-foot-high flashboard section from the lock to the center of the 
spillway with a top elevation of 188.5 feet with the flashboards 
installed, (e) a 1,000 foot-long, 4-foot-high flashboard section from 
the center of the spillway to the South Carolina abutment with a top 
elevation of 187.5 feet with the flashboards installed, and (f) a 97-
foot-long non-overflow section; (3) a 388-foot-long, 52-foot-wide, 57-
foot-high three-story brick powerhouse, integral with the dam, 
containing eight vertical Francis generating units, each rated at 3,125 
horsepower, a total generating capacity of 17.28 megawatts, and a total 
hydraulic capacity of 8,300 cubic feet per second; (4) generator leads 
from the powerhouse to a switchyard located approximately 100 feet from 
the powerhouse; and (5) ancillary equipment.
    The project recreation facilities include: (1) the Stevens Creek 
recreation site with a parking lot with space for six trailer vehicles, 
a concrete boat launch, three picnic tables, and a restroom; (2) Fury's 
Ferry recreation site with a concrete boat ramp, two picnic tables, and 
gravel parking lot for either five trailer vehicles or 11 standard 
vehicles; (3) Chota Drive recreation site, with bank fishing, non-
motorized boat launch access, and gravel parking lot for two vehicles; 
and (4) Betty's Branch/Riverside Park with a concrete boat ramp, a 
courtesy dock, a fishing pier, a canoe/kayak launch, two picnic tables, 
and a parking lot with four accessible spots, and with the adjacent 
Riverside Park with additional parking and facilities for baseball, 
softball, tennis, picnicking, and water-related activities.
    Dominion Energy proposes to: (1) continue operating the project as 
a re-regulating project, mitigating the downstream effects of wide-
ranging discharges from the upstream U.S. Army Corps of Engineers 
(Corps) J. Strom Thurmond Hydroelectric Project and maintain normal 
elevation fluctuations between 183.0 feet NGVD and 187.5 feet NGVD, 
using available storage; (2) continue updating the project operating 
plan every 5 years in coordination with the Corps; (3) install 
dissolved oxygen and temperature sensors at existing U.S. Geological 
Survey gages; (4) provide upstream American eel passage, conduct 
American eel siting studies, and develop an adaptive management fish 
passage plan; (5) continue to conduct annual shoreline surveys and 
annual funding for fish loss from entrainment; (6) develop an erosion 
control plan; (7) update the recreation management plan to include the 
removal of the Chota Drive recreation site from the project; and (8) 
implement a water quality adaptive management plan and draft historic 
properties management plan filed with the license application.

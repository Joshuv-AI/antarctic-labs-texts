On products of abelian skew braces
A. Ballester-Bolinches, R. Esteban-Romero, P. Pérez-Altarriba

Abstract
The main objective of this paper is to study factorisations of skew left braces through abelian subbraces. We prove a skew brace theoretical analog of the classical Itô’s theorem about product of two abelian groups: if B = A 1 ​ A 2 B=A_{1}A_{2} is a skew brace which is the product of two abelian skew subbraces A 1 A_{1} and A 2 A_{2} , and A 1 A_{1} is a left and right ideal of B B , then the commutator ideal [ B , B ] B [B,B]^{B} of B B is an abelian brace. If A 1 A_{1} is a left (non-necessarily right) ideal of B B , we show that there exists a strong left ideal of B B contained in A 1 A_{1} or A 2 A_{2} . We also show factorisations of relevant ideals of factorised braces that are sums and products of abelian subbraces. Keywords: factorised skew brace, abelian skw brace, trifactorised group Mathematics Subject Classification (2020): 16T25, Yang-Baxter equations 81R50, 20C99, 20D40, Products of subgroups of abstract finite groups

Introduction

The Yang-Baxter equation is an important equation in pure mathematics and physics. One of the fundamental challenges related with this equation is the search of solutions. An important family of solutions can be constructed by means of linear extensions of maps
r\colon X\times X\rightarrow X\times X
, where
X
is a basis of a vector space. They are called
combinatorial
solutions. To construct and understand combinatorial solutions with some prescribed properties, left skew braces introduced in
[
8
,
11
]
have emerged as a remarkable algebraic structure.

A
left skew brace

(B,{+},{\cdot})
consists of a set with two binary operations
+
and
\cdot
such that
(B,{+})
and
(B,{\cdot})
are groups linked by a distributive-like law
a(b+c)=ab-a+ac
for
a
,
b
,
c\in B
. When the operations on
B
are clear from the context, we will refer to a left skew brace as
B
instead of
(B,{+},{\cdot})
. For simplicity, we will use
skew brace
with the meaning of
left skew brace
.

Factorised skew braces, or skew braces that are a sum or a product of some of their left (right) ideals are important for a better understanding of multipermutational combinatorial solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. In this context, skew brace theoretical analogs of some important results on factorised groups play a significant role.

The aim of this paper is to show a skew brace theoretical analog of Itô’s theorem on metabelian groups, and arises after a careful study of the results on factorised skew braces showed in
[
10
,
12
]
.

In order to state our main result, we need to introduce some terminology. Let
(B,+,\cdot)
be a skew brace. A
subbrace
of
B
is a subgroup of
(B,{+})
which is also a subgroup of
(B,{\cdot})
. Both operations in
B
can be related by the so-called
star product
:
a\ast b=-a+ab-b
, for all
a,b\in B
. Indeed, both group operations coincide if and only if,
a\ast b=0
for all
a,b\in B
; in this case,
B
is said to be a
trivial skew brace
. Groups are precisely the trivial skew braces. We say that
B
is
abelian
if
B
is a trivial skew brace and
(B,{+})
is abelian, that is,
B
is just an abelian group.

The
opposite
skew brace
B^{op}
of
B
is just the set
B
with the same multiplicative group
(B,{\cdot})
and the additive group
(B,+^{op})
given by
a+^{op}b=b+a
for all
a,b\in B
.

Given two subsets
X,Y\subseteq B
, we write
\langle X\rangle_{+},[X,Y]_{+}
for the subgroup generated by
X
and the commutator of
X
and
Y
in
(B,+)
, respectively, and we write
\langle X\rangle_{\bullet},[X,Y]_{\bullet}
for the subgroup generated by
X
and the commutator of
X
and
Y
in
(B,\cdot)
, respectively. The subgroup of
(B,+)
generated by the set
\{x*y:x\in X,y\in Y\}
is denoted by
X*Y
.

A subbrace
L
of
B
is said to be a
left ideal
(respectively
right ideal
) of
B
if
B\ast L\subseteq L
(respectively
L\ast B\subseteq L
). A
strong left ideal

I
of
B
is a left ideal such that
(I,+)
is a normal subgroup of
(B,+)
. An
ideal
of
B
is a strong left ideal which is a right ideal of
B
. Note that every subgroup of a trivial skew brace is a left and right ideal, and
B*B
is an ideal of
B
.

The
commutator

[B,B]^{B}
of
B
is defined as the ideal of
B
generated by the set

\{[B,B]_{+}\cup[B,B]_{\bullet}\cup\{ab-(a+b):\,a,b\in B\}
. This important ideal of
B
was introduced in
[
6
]
and plays a key role in the study of nilpotency and solubility of skew braces (see
[
3
,
4
]
). The results of these papers show that the skew brace commutator is the true analog of the group commutator. In fact,
[B,B]^{B}
is the smallest ideal of
B
with quotient abelian, and
[B,B]^{B}=B*B+[B,B]_{+}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
). Note that
B*B
is a proper ideal of
[B,B]^{B}
in general: if
B
is a non-abelian trivial brace, then
0=B*B\neq[B,B]^{B}=[B,B]_{+}
.

The following analog of Itô’s theorem for skew braces was proved in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

Let
B
be a skew brace and let be
A_{1},A_{2}
subbraces of
B
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

B=A_{1}+A_{2}
or
B=A_{1}A_{2}
.

2.

A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are trivial skew braces.

3.

A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are left and right ideals of
B^{op}
.

Then

is a trivial skew brace.

Itô’s theorem may be recovered from Theorem
1.1

is a trivial skew brace. Note that Theorem
1.1
depends on some properties of the skew brace and the opposite skew brace. For practical and computation purposes it would be much better to impose conditions only on the skew brace, otherwise be would need to compute the opposite skew brace first.

The first main result of this paper is a skew brace theoretical analog of Itô’s theorem under conditions that depend only on the skew brace. Furthermore, no extra conditions on the second factor are needed.

Theorem A
.

Let
B
be a skew brace and let
A_{1},A_{2}
be subbraces of
B
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are abelian skew braces.

2.

B=A_{1}+A_{2}
or
B=A_{1}A_{2}
.

3.

A_{1}
is a left and right ideal of

.

Then

If

is a strong left ideal and

is a right ideal of

, Theorem

does not hold as the following example shows. Hence Condition

Example 1.2
.

The skew left brace

has additive group
C_{2}\times C_{2}\times S_{3}
and multiplicative group

.

contains a strong left ideal

and a right ideal

and

are abelian braces associated to the groups

and

. It is clear that

. We have that

As in the theory of factorised groups, in the structural study of factorised skew braces seems interesting to know which relevant ideals inherit the factorisation. In the second part of the paper, we shall be concerned with this property, which we formalise with suitable definition. It is inspired by the concept of factorised subgroup of a group which is the product of two subgroups introduced by Wielandt
(see
[
1
, Lemma 1.1.1]
).

Definition 1.3
.

A subbrace
S
of a skew brace
B=A+C
which is the sum of two subbraces
A
and
C
is called
factorised
if
S=(A\cap S)+(C\cap S)
and
A\cap C
is contained in
S
.

Note that a weaker notion of factorised subbrace is presented in
[
10
, Definition 2.17]
.

Our next result shows that some important left ideals of skew braces which are the sum and product of trivial and abelian subbraces are factorised (see
[
10
]
for related results).

For a skew brace
B
, we can consider the set of fixed elements of
B
by the action
\lambda
,

\textup{Fix}(B)=\{a\in B\,|\,\lambda_{b}(a)=a,\ \text{for every $b\in B$}\},

which turns out to be a left ideal, the
socle
of
B
,

\displaystyle\textup{Soc}(B)

\displaystyle=\{a\in B\,|\,\lambda_{a}(b)=b,\ a+b=b+a,\ \text{for every $b\in B$}\}

\displaystyle=\textup{Ker}\lambda\cap\textup{Z}(B,+),

and the
centre
of
B
:
Z(B)=\textup{Soc}(B)\cap Z(B,\cdot)

It is well-known that
\textup{Soc}(B)
and
Z(B)
are ideals of
B
.

Theorem B
.

Let the non-zero skew brace
B=A_{1}+A_{2}=A_{1}A_{2}
be the sum of two subbraces
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.

1.

If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are trivial skew braces, then
\textup{Fix}(B)
and
\ker\lambda
are factorised subbraces of
B
.

2.

If
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are abelian skew braces, then
\textup{Soc}(B)
and
Z(B)
are factorised ideals of
B
.

The centre of a group which is the product of two subgroups is not factorised in general (see
[
1
, Example 7.1.5]
). Hence the hypothesis on

and
A_{2}
in Statement 2 of Theorem

is necessary.

Strong left ideals provide a framework in which the results of factorisations of skew braces can be studied (see
[
10
]
) and are closely related with decomposability of solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Our third main result shows the existence of strong left ideals of a factorised skew brace

which is the sum of two abelian subbraces.

Theorem C
.

Let the finite non-zero skew brace
B=A_{1}+A_{2}
be the sum of two abelian skew subbraces
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. If
A_{1}
is a left ideal of

, there exists a non-zero strong left ideal of

contained in
A_{1}
or
A_{2}
.

Our approach is based on the intimate relationship between skew braces and trifactorised groups showed in
[

,

. Results of these papers show that this is a natural setting in which skew brace factorisations can be studied (see

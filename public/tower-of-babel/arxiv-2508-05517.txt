Cylindrical tangent flows in mean curvature flow
Sourav Ghosh

Abstract
. The only non-compact linearly stable singularity models for mean curvature flow are cylindrical by Colding-Minicozzi colding2012generic . The uniqueness of blowups at singularities modeled on the cylinders has been established by the same authors colding2015uniqueness . In this paper, we will use a different approach inspired by Székelyhidi szekelyhidi2020uniqueness to prove the uniqueness result.



1.
Introduction

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T)}
be a family of hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. We say they are flowing by mean
curvature if they satisfy the equation

\partial_{t}x=-H\mathbf{n}.

Here
x
is the position vector,
H
is the mean curvature,
which is minus the trace of the second fundamental form, and
\mathbf{n}
is the outward unit normal vector field over
M_{t}
. Submanifolds evolving by mean curvature flow often develop singularities in finite time
T<\infty
.

By work of Huisken
huisken1990asymptotic
, White
white1994partial
, and Ilmanen
ilmanen1995singularities
, the singularities of mean curvature flow can be modeled by self-similar shrinking solutions to the flow. To examine a singularity, one blows up around the point where singular behavior occurs. For this we need the notion of a tangent flow
ilmanen1995singularities
, which generalizes the construction of tangent cone from minimal surfaces.

By definition, a tangent flow is the limit of a sequence of rescalings
at a singularity. For instance, a tangent flow to
M_{t}
at the origin in space-time is the limit of a sequence of rescaled flows
\frac{1}{\epsilon_{i}}M_{\epsilon_{i}^{2}t}
where
\epsilon_{i}\to 0
. A priori, different sequences
\epsilon_{i}
could give different tangent flows.

Uniqueness of blowups is probably the most fundamental question that one can ask about the singularities in mean curvature flow in order to understand the nature of the flow before and after the singular time. There are many results in mean curvature flow when the tangent flow is multiplicity one. It is worth mentioning some related uniqueness results. When a tangent flow of a mean curvature is compact, then Schulze
schulze2014uniqueness
proved that it is the unique tangent flow. Colding-Minicozzi
colding2015uniqueness
proved uniqueness of the tangent flow when a tangent flow is a generalized cylinder. Chodosh-Schulze
chodosh2021uniqueness
proved uniqueness of the tangent flow when a tangent flow is smooth and asymptotically conical. The first uniqueness result for tangent flows given by non-smooth shrinkers came in Lotay-Schulze-Székelyhidi
lotay2022neck
, in the setting of Lagrangian mean curvature flow. Stolarski
stolarski2023structure
proved that for flows with bounded mean curvature, if a tangent flow is given by a regular multiplicity one stationary cone, then it is the unique tangent flow. The author
ghosh2025lagrangian
has recently proved that for Lagrangian mean curvature flows in two dimension with bounded mean curvature, tangent flow is unique even in the presence of higher multiplicity.

A well-known conjecture of Huisken was that generic mean curvature flows in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
encounter only spherical and cylindrical singularities. Chodosh-Choi-Mantoulidis-Schulze
chodosh2024mean
showed that the mean curvature flow starting from a generic surface in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be evolved uniquely through spherical and cylindrical singularities, provided they have multiplicity one. The assumption that singularities always have multiplicity one has recently been proved by Bamler-Kleiner
bamler2023multiplicity
. So in this sense, cylindrical singularities are the most important non-compact singularities in mean curvature flow.

The goal of this paper is to reprove the uniqueness result proven in
colding2015uniqueness
, which is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\mathscr{C}^{n}=\mathbb{S}^{k}_{\sqrt{2k}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
a cylinder. Let
\mathcal{M}=(M_{t})_{t\in(-t_{1},0)}
be a mean curvature flow so that the self-similar shrinking multiplicity one flow associated to
\mathscr{C},\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}
, arises as a tangent flow to
\mathcal{M}
at the space-time point
(0,0)
. Then
\mathcal{M}_{\mathscr{C}}
is the unique tangent flow to
\mathcal{M}
at
(0,0)
.

We will use the idea, that Székelyhidi
szekelyhidi2020uniqueness
used to prove that the cylindrical tangent cone
C\times\mathbb{R}
for an area-minimizing hypersurface is unique, where
C
is the Simons cone
C_{S}=C(S^{3}\times S^{3}).
Colding-Minicozzi [
colding2015uniqueness
,
colding2019regularity
] proved Łojasiewicz inequalities by a direct geometric method using the specific geometry of a cylinder. In this paper, we perturb the cylinder to construct flows near it. We will see that the key estimates one obtains from the Łojasiewicz inequality is implicitly contained in the properties of the constructed rescaled flows. Since we do not rely on the specific geometry of the cylinder, this approach can be applied to prove the uniqueness result for a broader class of shrinkers.

Let
y_{i}
be coordinates on the
\mathbb{R}^{n-k}
factor and let
\theta
be in the
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{\sqrt{2k}}
factor. There are two types of Jacobi field of
\mathscr{C}
[
colding2015uniqueness
,
sun2022generic
]: one is
y_{i}f_{i}(\theta),
where
f_{i}
is an eigenfunction on
\mathbb{S}^{k}_{\sqrt{2k}}
with eigenvalue
\frac{1}{2}
. This part corresponds to rotations, geometrically
f_{i}
represent the translations in the directions of the spherical components, and
y_{i}
represent the translations in the axis directions. The other one is
y_{i}y_{j}-2\delta_{ij}
, however it is known [
colding2015rigidity
,
zhu2020ojasiewicz
] that the quadratic polynomials
y_{i}y_{j}-2\delta_{ij}
in the kernel are not generated by one-parameter families of shrinkers. Geometrically, this represents non-degenerate neck pinching in the related direction on the axis and their rotations in the axis directions. In particular, the kernel contains non-integrable Jacobi fields.

Denoting the dimension of the non-integrable Jacobi fields by
N
, we can associate each such Jacobi field with an element in
\mathbb{R}^{N}.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with sufficiently small
\ell^{\infty}
norm, we construct deformations
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
of the cylinder
\mathscr{C}
. These
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
, modeled on non-integrable Jacobi field, are perturbations of cylinders, defined on space-time parabolic region depending on
\alpha
. Moreover, the maximal existence time increases as the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of
\alpha
decreases. Similar constructions have been made, such as logarithmic perturbations of cones constructed by Székelyhidi
szekelyhidi2020uniqueness
, logarithmically decaying surfaces constructed by Adams-Simon
adams1988rates
. We will then establish a Łojasiewicz type inequality [Lemma
4.4
] of our constructed flows.

During a time interval where the given flow
M_{\tau}
is very close to
\mathscr{C}
, we treat it as a perturbation of the best flow within
the family of
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
. Then using a
non-concentration result [Corollary
5.5
], we see that relative to
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
the flow
M_{\tau}
is modeled on a Jacobi field over
\mathscr{C}
that has no degree zero component. Therefore, we can apply the three-annulus lemma [Lemma
5.9
] to show that as we move forward in time,
M_{\tau}
will either diverge significantly from
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
or approach
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
.

In Proposition
6.4
, we will show that if
M_{\tau}
diverges from
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
over time, then it can not remain close to any shrinking solution, leading to a contradiction. Therefore, we can assume that
M_{\tau}
will approach
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
. Now our constructed flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
is defined only for a finite period of time, which depends on
\alpha
. So we can apply the three-annulus lemma [Lemma
5.9
] only a finite number of times. Since
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
is not a self-similar shrinking solution, approaching
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
may not mean that
M_{\tau}
becomes “more cylindrical” sufficiently quickly. So we can not conclude the uniqueness of the flow from here. But we will see in this case that the given flow is very close to
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C_{i}}}
relative to
\alpha
. Combining this with the Łojasiewicz type inequality [Lemma
4.4
] and Huisken’s monotonicity formula, we will obtain a certain decay rate for the Gaussian area of the rescaled flow [Proposition
6.2
]. Once this decay is achieved, we can ultimately conclude that the tangent flow is unique.

The structure of the paper is summarized as follows. In section
2
, we state some preliminary results in mean curvature flow and describe the space of Jacobi fields on
\mathscr{C}
. In Section
3
, we talk about the graphical rescaled mean curvature flow equation. In section
4
, we will construct the rescaled flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
, which will be defined on space-time parabolic region depending on
\alpha
and modeled on non-integrable Jacobi field
u_{\alpha}
, defined in Section
4
. In Section
5
, we will define the distance function
D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})
, at time
\tau_{0}
for the given rescaled flow near the constructed flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
, at time
\tau
and prove the non-concentration result [Proposition
5.2
and Corollary
5.5
]. We will use this non-concentration result to prove a three annulus type lemma [Lemma
5.9
] for
D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})
. Now using this three annulus lemma in Section
6
, we prove the decay estimate [Proposition
6.4
] for the distance function. Given the decay estimate in Proposition
6.4
, the uniqueness result [Theorem
1.1
] follows by standard arguments.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will briefly discuss the basic definitions in mean curvature flow (MCF) that will be useful in the article.

For
\lambda>0,
we have the parabolic rescaling,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle(x,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t).

If we denote the space time track of MCF by
\mathcal{M}
, then for a MCF
\mathcal{M}
, one can check that
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}\mathcal{M}
is again a MCF.

Assume now that the solution to MCF develops a singularity at the point
(x_{0},T)
in space-time. For a positive sequence
\lambda_{i}\to\infty,
it is well known that
\mathcal{D}_{\lambda_{i}}\big(\mathcal{M}-(x_{0},T)\big)
has a subsequence which converges weakly i.e. as a Brakke flow [
ilmanen1994elliptic
, Lemma
7.1
]. Such a flow is called a tangent flow at
(x_{0},T)
and it depends on the sequence
\lambda_{i}
taken.

We have Huisken’s monotonicity formula
huisken1990asymptotic
:

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M_{t}}f\rho_{x_{0},t_{0}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}=\int_{M_{t}}(\partial_{t}f-\Delta f)\rho_{x_{0},t_{0}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}-\int_{M_{t}}f\bigg|H+\frac{(x-x_{0})^{\perp}}{2(t-t_{0})}\bigg|^{2}\rho_{x_{0},t_{0}}d\mathcal{H}^{n},

for
t<t_{0}
, where
f
is a function on
M_{t}
with polynomial growth (locally uniform in
t
), and

\rho_{x_{0},t_{0}}=\frac{1}{\big(4\pi(t_{0}-t)\big)^{n/2}}\;\text{exp}\bigg(-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4(t_{0}-t)}\bigg)

is the is the backwards heat kernel. Given
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
t_{0}>0
, the functional
F_{x_{0},t_{0}}
defined by
colding2012generic
is

F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(M)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t_{0})^{n/2}}\int_{M}e^{-\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{4t_{0}}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Note that
M
is a critical point of
F_{x_{0},t_{0}}
precisely when it is
the time
t=-t_{0}
slice of a self-shrinking solution of the MCF that becomes extinct at
x=x_{0}
and
t=0
. The entropy
\lambda(M)
is given by

\lambda(M)=\sup\limits_{x_{0},t_{0}}F_{x_{0},t_{0}}(M).

By virtue of Huisken’s monotonicity formula,
t\to\lambda(M_{t})
is decreasing along any MCF. Also, the critical points of
\lambda
are self-shrinkers for the MCF.

The Gaussian weight is

\rho=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}.

By
L^{2},W^{k,2}
we denote the weighted Sobolev spaces with respect to
\rho
. The Gaussian area functional is

F(M)=\int_{M}\rho\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

We define the excess of
M
to be

\mathcal{A}(M)=F(M)-F(\mathscr{C}).

Let
(M_{t})_{0\leq t<T}
be a solution to MCF. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We will consider the rescaled flow
M_{\tau}
at
(x_{0},T)
which is defined by

M_{\tau}(x)=\frac{1}{(T-t)^{1/2}}\big(M_{t}(x)-x_{0}\big),\hskip 14.22636pt\tau(t)=-\log(T-t).

It is easy to check that the rescaled flow evolves with normal speed
H+\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}.
In terms of the rescaled flow the tangent flows of
\mathcal{M}
can be studied by taking limits of sequences
M_{\tau_{i}}
as
\tau_{i}\to\infty.
Note that by monotonocity formula for rescaled flow we have,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M_{t}}\rho\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}=-\int_{M_{t}}\Big|H+\frac{1}{2}x^{\perp}\Big|^{2}\rho\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

We also observe that
\mathcal{A}
is monotonically decreasing along the rescaled flow by Huisken’s montonicity formula.

Throughout we will assume that the
M_{t}
has uniformly
bounded area ratios, i.e. there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\mathcal{H}^{n}\big(M_{t}\cap B_{R}(x)\big)\leq C_{1}R^{n}\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

where
B_{R}(x)
is the Euclidean ball of radius
R
centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}.
Note that the volume growth condition is equivalent to the condition that
M_{t}
has uniformly bounded entropy.

The second fundamental form will be denoted by
A
. We will use the following elliptic operator

Lu=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}x.\nabla+|A|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}.

Note that for
\mathscr{C}
, we have
|A|^{2}=\frac{1}{2},
so for
\mathscr{C}
the above operator becomes,

Lu=\Delta-\frac{1}{2}x.\nabla+1.

For this operator, we will denote
K
for the kernel of
L
with the kernel specifically referring to the
W^{2,2}
kernel. The linearlized operator
L
is self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product

\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathscr{C}}u(x)v(x)e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Now we will describe the space of the Jacobi field on a cylinder. Let
K
be the space of the Jacobi field on a cylinder. The explicit description of
K
is given in [
colding2015uniqueness
, Lemma
3.25
and
colding2019regularity
, Proposition
3.1
, Corollary
3.6
], which says the following,

Lemma 2.1
.

K=K_{1}\oplus K_{2},\hskip 11.38092ptwhere

an orthogonal basis for
K_{1}
is given by

\Big\{y_{i}f:f\;is\;an\;eigenfunction\;of\;\mathbb{S}^{k}\;with\;eigenvalue\;\frac{1}{2}\Big\},

and an orthogonal basis for
K_{2}
is given by

\{y_{i}y_{j}-2\delta_{ij}:i\leq j\}.

One can check that
K_{1}
corresponds to rotations. If one considers the action of the group
SO(n+1)
of rotations about the origin, in particular, consider the map from lie algebra of
SO(n+1)
to the space of normal vector field
\theta\mapsto J_{\theta}
where,

J_{\theta}(p):=\big(\partial_{s}|_{s=0}exp(s\theta)(p)\big)^{\perp}

then the image is exactly
K_{1}.

We will denote
N
by the dimension of
K_{2}.

Graphical Representation

Let
\Sigma
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
n
be the normal to
\Sigma
. Let
u
be a function over
\Sigma
and the graph
\Sigma_{u}
is defined to be

\Sigma_{u}=\{p+u(p)n(p):p\in\Sigma\}

If
|u|
is sufficiently small, then
\Sigma_{u}
is contained in a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
where the exponential map is invertible. Let
e_{n+1}
be the gradient of the signed distance function to
\Sigma.

Let us denote
\nu_{u}(p)
be the relative area element, i.e.
\nu_{u}(p)=\sqrt{detg_{ij}^{u}(p)}/\sqrt{detg_{ij}(p)}
where
g_{ij}(p)
is the metric for
\Sigma
at
p
and
g_{ij}^{u}(p)
is the pull-back metric for
\Sigma_{u}
at
p+u(p)n(p).
The relative area element was used in
colding2015uniqueness
to compute the mean curvature and to relate the gradient of
F
to
\phi,
where

\phi=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,n\rangle-H.

We also denote
n_{u}(p)
be the normal to
\Sigma_{u}
at
p+u(p)n(p),H_{u}(p)
be the mean curvature of
\Sigma_{u}
at
p+u(p)n(p),\eta_{u}(p)=\langle p+u(p)n(p),n_{u}\rangle
be the support function at
p+u(p)n(p)
and
w_{u}(p)=\langle e_{n+1},n_{u}\rangle^{-1}
evaluated at
p+u(p)n(p).

We have the following Lemma [
colding2015uniqueness
, Lemma
A.44
] (see also
colding2019dynamics
),

Lemma 3.1
.

For sufficiently small
u
, the graphs
\Sigma_{u}
over the surface
\Sigma
flow by rescaled MCF if and only if
u
satisfies

\partial_{t}u(p,t)=w_{u}(p)\big(-H_{u}+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{u}(p)\big)=:Mu,

Remark 3.2
.

For small
u
, one can write

Mu=Lu+Qu.

This follows from the calculations as in Appendix A and Lemma
4.10
of
colding2015uniqueness
(see also [
sun2022generic
, Proposition
A.1
]. In particular, one can show that,
Lu
is the linearization of
Mu
at
u=0
, i.e.

Lu=\frac{d}{dr}\bigg|_{r=0}M(ru)=\Delta u-\frac{1}{2}x.\nabla u+|A|^{2}u+\frac{1}{2}u,

and

|Qu|\leq C(|\nabla u|^{4}+|\nabla u|^{2}|Hess_{u}|+|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{2}+|u||Hess_{u}|).

One can also directly check this using the explicit calculations done in [
colding2012generic
, Lemma
A.3
], which says the following.

There are functions
w,\nu,\eta
depending on
(p,s,y)\in\Sigma\times R\times T_{p}\Sigma
that are
smooth for
|s|
less than the normal injectivity radius of
\Sigma
so that

w(p,s,y)=(1+|B^{-1}(p,s)(y)|^{2})^{-1/2},

\nu(p,s,y)=w(p,s,y)\;\text{det}(B(p,s)),

\eta(p,s,y)=\langle p,n\rangle+s-\langle p,B^{-1}(p,s)(y)\rangle,

where the linear operator
B(p,s)(y)
is defined as

B(p,s):=Id-sA(p).

One has,

w_{u}(p)=w(p,u(p),\nabla u(p)),\nu(p)=\nu(p,u(p),\nabla u(p)),\eta_{u}(p)=\eta(p,u(p),\nabla u(p)).

Note that, the mean curvature
H_{u}
of
\Sigma_{u}
is given by

H_{u}(p)=\frac{w}{\nu}\big(\partial_{s}\nu-\partial_{p_{i}}\partial_{y_{i}}\nu-(\partial_{s}\partial_{y_{i}}\nu)u_{i}-(\partial_{y_{j}}\partial_{y_{i}}\nu)u_{ij}\big).

We will use this to view a rescaled MCF as a graph over another rescaled MCF. Given a rescaled MCF
{M_{t}}
defined for
t\in[0,T],
let
u(p,t):\cup_{t\in[0,T]}M_{t}\times\{t\}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. Similarly, one can define
M_{u,t}
to be a family of hypersurfaces, where each
M_{u,t}
is a graph of
u(.,t)
over
M_{t}
. We will use
w(t,\textperiodcentered),\nu(t,\textperiodcentered),\eta(t,\textperiodcentered)
to denote

on each

We can prove a lemma which is a generalization of the Lemma

, where we can take graph over rescaled MCF which is not a fixed self-shrinker. The lemma has been proved in Sun-Xue [
sun2021initial

], which says

.

For sufficiently small

, the graphs

F_{t}(u):=H_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\langle p,n\rangle-u\langle\nabla(H_{0}-\frac{1}{2}\langle p,n\rangle),n_{u}\rangle.

4.
The comparison flows

In this section, we will construct rescaled MCF near cylinder. First we define the parabolic Holder space. The parabolic Holder norm is defined as

\|f\|_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}=\|f\|_{{L^{\infty}}(\Omega\times[0,T))}+[f]_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}.

where,

[f]_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}=\sup_{p\neq q\in\Omega\times[0,T)}\;\dfrac{|f(p)-f(q)|}{|p-q|^{\delta}}\hskip 28.45274ptp=(x,t),\;|p|=|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

And the second order Holder norm is

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2}};\Omega\times[0,T)}

\displaystyle=\lVert f\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(\Omega\times[0,T))}+\lVert\partial_{t}f\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(\Omega\times[0,T))}+\lVert Df\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(\Omega\times[0,T))}+

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt\lVert D^{2}f\rVert_{{L^{\infty}}(\Omega\times[0,T))}+[f]_{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)},

where,

[f]_{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}=[\partial_{t}f]_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}+[D^{2}f]_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}.

Note that if we define

[f]^{\prime}_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,t),(y,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T)\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\;\frac{|f(x,t)-f(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{\delta}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,t),(x,s)\in\Omega\times[0,T)\\
t\neq s\end{subarray}}\;\frac{|f(x,t)-f(y,t)|}{|x-y|^{\delta}},

then
[f]_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}
and
[f]^{\prime}_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}
are equivalent and

\frac{1}{2}[f]^{\prime}_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}\leq[f]_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}\leq 2[f]^{\prime}_{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2};\Omega\times[0,T)}.

Fix
R>0.
Now let
X_{R}
denote the space
\overline{\mathscr{C}\cap B_{R}(0)}
.
Consider the parabolic operator:

\partial_{t}-L:C^{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2}}(X_{R}\times[0,T))\to C^{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2}}(X_{R}\times[0,T)).

We will prove that the above operator has a right inverse, which is the next proposition. We will assume
T>1.

Proposition 4.1
.

The operator

\partial_{t}-L:C^{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2}}(X_{R}\times[0,T))\to C^{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2}}(X_{R}\times[0,T))

has a right inverse
P_{R}
. Let
\widehat{P_{R}}
denote the restriction of
P_{R}
as follows,

\displaystyle\widehat{P_{R}}:C^{\delta,\frac{\delta}{2}}(X_{R}\times[0,T))

\displaystyle\to C^{2+\delta,1+\frac{\delta}{2}}(X_{R}\times[1,T))

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto P_{R}(u(x,t))|_{X_{R}\times[1,T)}.

Then the operator norm of
\widehat{P_{R}}
is bounded by
CT,
where
C
is a constant independent of
R
and
T.

The proposition is a consequence of the following facts:

First note that the
C^{2,\delta}
functions (or
C^{0,\delta}
functions) on
\overline{\mathscr{C}\cap B_{R}(0)}
can be extended (see
seeley1964extension
) to a function on
\mathscr{C}
by reflection, while preserving the
C^{2,\delta}
norm (respectively
C^{0,\delta}
norm). In a similar way, we can extend
C^{2+\delta,1+\delta/2}
functions (or
C^{\delta,\delta/2}
functions) on
\overline{\mathscr{C}\cap B_{R}(0)}\times[0,T)
to

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C(n)c(\mathbb{S}^{k})C_{0}}{\sqrt{8\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{k})}}\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{2}-CC_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{1}-CC_{2}.

Note that
C
is a constant depending only on
\lambda(\mathbb{S}^{k})
and
n
. So we can choose
R_{2}
such that
CC_{2}<C_{1}
, then for any
R\geq\text{max}\{R_{1},R_{2}\}
the result follows.
∎

The next lemma, which serves as the main result of this section, is an application of Proposition
4.3
. In the following, we will establish the lower bound of the functional
\phi
for the flow we have constructed.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists a uniform
\kappa>0,
such that if
|\alpha|>0
is sufficiently small then
\forall\;\tau\in[0,T_{\alpha})
, we have

\|\phi\big(\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}\big)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\kappa|\alpha|^{2}.

Proof.

By triangle inequality, for any
v
with sufficiently small
C^{2,\delta}
norm,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|P_{KerL}(D^{2}\phi(v,v))\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\;\|P_{KerL}\phi(v)\|_{L^{2}}+\|P_{KerL}Lv\|_{L^{2}}+

\displaystyle\;\|P_{KerL}\big(\phi(v)-Lv-\frac{1}{2}D^{2}\phi(v,v)\big)\|_{L^{2}}.

As
Lv\in(KerL)^{\perp},
so
P_{KerL}L(v)
vanishes. Now using
colding2019regularity
,

|\phi(v)-Lv-\frac{1}{2}D^{2}\phi(v,v)|\leq C(1+x^{2})^{1/2}(|v|+|\nabla v|+|\nabla^{2}v|)^{3},

we have

|\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})-L(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})-\frac{1}{2}D^{2}\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha},\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})|\leq C(1+|x|^{2})(|\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}|+|\nabla(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})|+|\nabla^{2}(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})|)^{3}.

By Proposition
4.3
for
\alpha
with sufficiently small
|\alpha|
(and that will imply for sufficiently large
R_{\alpha}
as
|\alpha|(R_{\alpha}+1)^{6}=1
),

\frac{1}{2}\|P_{KerL}(D^{2}\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha},\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}))\|_{L^{2}}\geq c_{0}|\alpha|^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|P_{KerL}\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|P_{KerL}(D^{2}\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha},\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}))\|_{L^{2}}-

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt\|P_{KerL}\big(\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})-L(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})-\frac{1}{2}D^{2}\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha},\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})\big)\|_{L^{2}}.

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}|\alpha|^{2}-C|\alpha|^{3}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}|\alpha|^{2},

if
|\alpha|
is small enough.
Now using

\phi\big(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}+\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t)\big)=\phi(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha})+L(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t))+\big(L_{\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}}-L\big)(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t))+Q(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t)),

we have

\displaystyle\|\phi\big(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}+\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t)\big)\|_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\|P_{KerL}\phi\big(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}+\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t)\big)\|_{L_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{0}}{2}|\alpha|^{2}-\|\big(L_{\chi_{R_{\alpha}}u_{\alpha}}-L\big)(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t))\|_{L^{2}}-\|Q(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t))\|_{L^{2}},

where we used the fact
L(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}V(t))\in(KerL)^{\perp}
. Now,

\displaystyle\|\big(L_{u_{\alpha}}\small{-}L\big)(V(t))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\big(L_{u_{\alpha}}\small{-}L\big)(V(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}\cap B_{R_{\alpha}}(0))}+\|\big(L_{u_{\alpha}}\small{-}L\big)(V(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}\setminus B_{R_{\alpha}}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C|\alpha|^{5/2}+Ce^{-R_{\alpha}^{2}/4p}|\alpha|^{2/3}

\displaystyle=C|\alpha|^{5/2}+Ce^{-R_{\alpha}^{2}/4p}|\alpha|^{5/3}(R_{\alpha}+1)^{6}

\displaystyle\leq 2C|\alpha|^{5/2},

for some
p>1
and,

\displaystyle\|Q(V(t))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|Q(V(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}\cap B_{R_{\alpha}}(0))}+\|Q(V(t))\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}\setminus B_{R_{\alpha}}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C|\alpha|^{9/4}+Ce^{-R_{\alpha}^{2}/4p}|\alpha|^{4/3}

\displaystyle=C|\alpha|^{9/4}+Ce^{-R_{\alpha}^{2}/4p}|\alpha|^{10/3}(R_{\alpha}+1)^{12}

\displaystyle\leq 2C|\alpha|^{9/4}.

So for
|\alpha|
sufficiently small, we have

\|\phi\big(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}(u_{\alpha}+V(t)\big)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{c_{0}}{4}|\alpha|^{2}.

Now again using the similar argument as before for small
|\alpha|
, we have

\|\phi\big(\chi_{R_{\alpha}}(u_{\alpha}+V(t)\big)\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}\setminus B_{R_{\alpha}}(0))}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{8}|\alpha|^{2}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Non-concentration

The goal of this section is to prove the key non-concentration result, Proposition
5.2
below similar to Li-Székelyhidi [
li2024singularity
, Lemma
34
], and as a consequence to prove a three annulus lemma, Lemma
5.9
. We will need to consider rescaled flow
M_{\tau}
very close to one of the comparison flows
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
constructed in the last section. Note that the comparison flows
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
is defined on
\mathscr{C}\cap B_{R_{\alpha}}(0)
for the time interval
[0,T_{\alpha})
, where

|\alpha|=N.\text{max}\{|\alpha_{1}|,...,|\alpha_{N}|\},

T_{\alpha}=|\alpha|^{-1/5}-1,

and

|\alpha|(R_{\alpha}+1)^{6}=1.

We will say that
M
is a
c
graph over
N
on the region
B_{R}(0)
if we can write
M\cap B_{R}(0)
as a graph of a function
f
defined on
N\cap B_{R}(0)
with
\|f\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq c.

Now let us define our Gaussian
L^{2}
distance function similar to
lotay2022neck
(see also
li2024singularity
). Fix a
c_{0}>0
small, which will be chosen later. Let
R\leq R_{\alpha}
be the largest radius such that
M
is a
c_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
on
B_{R}(0)
at time
\tau
, then we define
D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M)
by the weighted
L_{2}
distance from
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
at time
\tau
to
M
:

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M)=\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau}\cap B_{R}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}^{2}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{8p_{0}}}.

where
p_{0}>1
is very close to
1
, will be chosen later and
d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}
is just the distance function to
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
at time
\tau
. We also define a weighted
L^{2}
distance from
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
to
M
:

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}}(M)=\bigg(\int_{M\cap B_{R}(0)}d^{2}_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{8p_{0}}}.

We can similarly define
D_{\mathscr{C}}(M)
. Also for any
\epsilon>0,
we define

R_{\epsilon}:=(8p_{0}|\ln\epsilon|)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

First we need an elementary lemma similar to [
colding2015uniqueness
, Lemma
5.39
] (see also [
chodosh2021uniqueness
, Proposition
5.31
and
li2024singularity
, Lemma
30
]) which asserts that we can extend the graphicality of our flow with time to a larger region for rescaled flow, provided that the flow remains sufficiently close at the initial time.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\delta_{0}>0.
Then there exists a
\delta_{1}>0
such that if
\tau+1<T_{\alpha}
and
M_{\tau_{0}}
is a
\delta_{1}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}
on
B_{R}(0)
then
M_{\tau_{0}+t}
is a
\delta_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+t}
on
B_{R}(0)
for all
t\in[0,1]
and there exists a
\theta>0
such that
M_{\tau_{0}+1}
is a
\delta_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}
on
B_{(1+\theta)R}(0)
as long as
(1+\theta)\lambda R_{\epsilon}
is still smaller than
R_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Fix some
c>0
small. Consider first the non-rescaled version of our constructed rescaled flow defined on the time interval
[-1,-e^{-|\alpha|^{-1/5}-1}),
which we will denote by
\widehat{\mathscr{T}}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}.
Note that,

\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}=e^{\tau/2}\;\widehat{\mathscr{T}}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{-e^{-\tau}}.

Similarly consider the non-rescaled flow
\widehat{M}_{t}
defined on
[-1,0)
. Note that when
|\alpha|
is small,
-1+c<-e^{-|\alpha|^{-1/5}}-1.
Now one can use the Pseudolocality argument [
ilmanen2019short
, Theorem
1.5
] or directly White’s Brakke-type estimate
white2005local
to conclude that for any
\delta_{0}>0,
there exists a
\delta_{1}>0
such that if
\widehat{M}_{\tau}
is a non-rescaled mean curvature flow such that
B_{R+1}(0)\cap\widehat{M}_{-1}
is a
C^{2,\delta}
graph over
\widehat{\mathscr{T}}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{-1}
with
C^{2,\delta}
norm is less than
\delta_{1}
, then for each
\tau\in[-1,-1+c]
we have that
B_{R}(0)\cap\widehat{M}_{\tau}
is a
C^{2,\delta}
graph over
\widehat{\mathscr{T}}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}
with
C^{2,\delta}
norm is less than
\delta_{0}
.

The claim in the lemma follows by applying this result to the flows given by initial condition
\widehat{M}_{-1}=M_{\tau_{0}}
.
∎

Now we will prove the following non-concentration estimate for the distance function.

Proposition 5.2
.

There are constants
C,\epsilon,\theta^{\prime}>0
and
p>1
such that we have the following. Suppose that

•

|\alpha|<\epsilon,

•

\tau+1<T_{\alpha},

•

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})<\epsilon.

Then we have

\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C\epsilon.

Similar conclusion also holds if we consider the static flow generated by
\mathscr{C}
instead of the constructed flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}.

Proof.

Given
D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})<\epsilon,
so by definition there exists a
R>0
such that
M_{\tau_{0}}
is
c_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}
on
B_{R}(0)
and

\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{R}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}^{2}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{8p_{0}}}<\epsilon.

Now
e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{8p_{0}}}<\epsilon
implies
R^{2}>8p_{0}|\ln\epsilon|.
So
R>R_{\epsilon}.
Let
\lambda<1.
Then on a unit ball
B_{1}(x)
, where
|x|=r-1
for any
r<\lambda R_{\epsilon}
, we also have

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{1}(x)}d^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle<e^{\frac{r^{2}}{4}}\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{1}(x)}d^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle<e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}R_{\epsilon}^{2}}{4}}\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}R_{\epsilon}^{2}}{4}}\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{R}(0)}d^{2}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{-2p_{0}\lambda^{2}}\epsilon^{2}.

So if we choose
\lambda>1
such that
p_{0}\lambda^{2}<1
, then for any
x\in B_{\lambda R_{\epsilon}-1}(0)
the integral
\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{1}(x)}d^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n}
will go to zero as
\epsilon\to 0
.

First we fix a
c_{0}>0
small so that
M_{\tau_{0}+t}
is a
c_{1}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+t}
on
B_{R_{\epsilon}(0)}
for all
t\in[0,1]
for some
c_{1}>0.

Now we can use interpolation inequality (see
colding2015uniqueness
,
zhu2020ojasiewicz
) to get that if
M_{\tau_{0}}
is
c_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}_{\tau}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
on
B_{R}(x)
, then in fact
M_{\tau_{0}}
is
\epsilon^{k}
graph over
\mathscr{T}_{\tau}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
on
B_{\lambda R_{\epsilon}}(0)
for some constant
k>0
. Then taking
\epsilon
small and using the pseudolocality argument [Lemma
5.1
], we can conclude that there exists a
\theta>0
such that
M_{\tau_{0}+1}
is a
c_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}
on
B_{(1+\theta)\lambda R_{\epsilon}}(0)
as long as
(1+\theta)\lambda R_{\epsilon}
is still smaller than
R_{\alpha}
. Note that we will choose
\lambda>0
in such a way so that
(1+\theta)\lambda>(1+\theta)^{\prime}
for some
\theta^{\prime}>0.
Once we choose
\lambda<1,
we can fix a
p_{0}>1
such that
p_{0}\lambda^{2}<1.

From the above discussion, we can conclude that
M_{\tau_{0}+t}\cap B_{R}(0)
can be written as a graph of the function
v_{t}
defined on
\mathscr{T}_{\tau+t}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}\cap B_{R}(0)
on the time interval
t\in[0,1],
and we have
\|v_{t}\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\leq c_{0}.
Now if
c_{0}
is sufficiently small, then by Remark
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
, we have

|\partial_{t}v_{t}-\Delta v_{t}+\frac{1}{2}x.\nabla v_{t}|\leq C\big(|v|+|\nabla v_{t}|\big).

Here
C
depends on the cylinder
\mathscr{C}
. Fix a
q
such that
1<q<2.
Now similar to the proof [
li2024singularity
, Lemma
34
], we will absorb the gradient term and compute the evolution of
|v|^{2/q}
and obtain a differential inequality of the form

\partial_{\tau}|v_{t}|^{2/q}-\Delta|v_{t}|^{2/q}+\frac{1}{2}x.\nabla|v_{t}|^{2/q}\leq C|v_{t}|^{2/q},

for possibly a bigger constant
C
but depending on the cylinder
\mathscr{C}
. Note that
|v_{t}|(x)=d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+t}}(x+v_{t}(x))
, and under the parametrization of
M_{\tau_{0}+t}
using
v_{t}
, the volume forms of
M_{\tau_{0}+t}
and
\mathscr{T}_{\tau+t}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
are uniformly equivalent. Therefore, the integrals of
d^{2p}_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+t}}e^{-|x|^{2}/4}
over a region in
M_{\tau_{0}+t}
and of
|v_{t}|^{2p}e^{-|x|^{2}/4}
over the corresponding region on
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+t}
are uniformly equivalent.

Now if we define the space time function,

f(x,t)=e^{-Ct}|v_{t}|^{2/q},

then
f
is a subsolution of the drift heat equation on
B_{R}(0)
on the time interval
[0,1].
Also, along any rescaled flow

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|x|^{2}=-2n,

so
e^{-t}|x|^{2}-(R_{\epsilon}-1)^{2}
is a subsolution of the drift heat equation along the rescaled flow. Let us define the function

\widehat{f}=\begin{cases}f,&\text{for}\;|x|\leq R_{\epsilon}-1,\\
\max\Big\{f,e^{-t}|x|^{2}-(R_{\epsilon}-1)^{2},&\text{for}\;R_{\epsilon}-1<|x|\leq R_{\epsilon},\\
e^{-t}|x|^{2}-(R_{\epsilon}-1)^{2},&\text{for}\;|x|>R_{\epsilon}.\end{cases}

Note that on
B_{R_{\epsilon}}(0),|v_{\tau}|\leq c_{1},
hence we can choose
\epsilon
small enough (which implies
R_{\epsilon}
large) so that when
|x|\to R_{\epsilon},
one has
e^{-t}|x|^{2}-(R_{\epsilon}-1)^{2}\geq|v_{\tau}|^{2/q}.
In that case,
\widehat{f}
will be continuous and also a subsolution of the drift heat equation along
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+t}
on the time interval
t\in[0,1].
Now,

\widehat{f}^{q}\leq\begin{cases}C|v_{\tau}|^{2},&\text{for}\;|x|\leq R_{\epsilon}-1,\\
C(|v_{\tau}|^{2}+|x|^{2q}),&\text{for}\;R_{\epsilon}-1<|x|\leq R_{\epsilon},\\
C|x|^{2q},&\text{for}\;|x|>R_{\epsilon}.\end{cases}

Also,

\int_{M_{\tau_{0}}\cap B_{R_{\epsilon}-1}(0)}|x|^{2q}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq e^{-\frac{(R_{\epsilon}-1)^{2}}{4q^{\prime}}}.

for some
q^{\prime}>1.

Now if we choose
q^{\prime}
so that
1<q^{\prime}<p_{0}
then we will have

e^{-\frac{(R_{\epsilon}-1)^{2}}{4q^{\prime}}}<e^{-\frac{R_{\epsilon}^{2}}{4p_{0}}}<e^{-\frac{R^{2}}{4p_{0}}}<\epsilon^{2}.

This implies,

\int_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau_{0}}}\widehat{f}^{q}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq C\epsilon^{2}.

Now using Ecker’s log-Sobolev inequality
ecker2000logarithmic
, we find that for some
p>1
, and
C>0
, we have

\int_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau_{0}+1}}\widehat{f}^{pq}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq C\epsilon^{2p}.

But on
B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)
we have,

d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau_{0}+1}}\leq C\widehat{f}(.,1),

therefore,

\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq C\epsilon^{2p}.

∎

Once we have a
L^{p}
type estimate as in Proposition
6.3
, we can prove a pointwise bound at later time in terms of our
L^{2}
distance function at initial time. This follows from [
lotay2022neck
, Lemma
3.5(2)
].

Corollary 5.3
.

There are
C,\epsilon>0,p>1
such that we have the following. Suppose that

•

|\alpha|<\epsilon,

•

\tau+1<T_{\alpha},

•

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})<\epsilon.

Then we have the estimate

d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2}\leq Ce^{\frac{|x|^{2}}{4p}}\epsilon^{2}

on
M_{\tau_{0}+1}.

Now we will show that similar non-concentration lemma holds for self-shrinker. This follows from Proposition
5.2
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
M
be a shrinker. There are constants
C,\epsilon>0
and
p>1
such that we have the following. Suppose that

•

|\alpha|<\epsilon,

•

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}}(M)<\epsilon.

Then we have

\bigg(\int_{M\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C\epsilon.

Proof.

The proof essentially follows from Proposition
5.2
.
First observe that since
M
is a shrinker, we can regard the static rescaled flow for
s\in[0,1]
as follows,

M_{\tau+s}=M.

Additionally, note that
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
can be viewed as the time
0
slice of the flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}.
Then by construction time slice
1
of the flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
is graph of
\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
of a function
f
whose
C^{2,\delta}
norm is bounded
C|\alpha|^{2}r^{5}
on
B_{r}(0)
for
r<R_{\alpha}.

Now by Proposition
5.2
,

\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C\epsilon.

As

d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}(x)\leq C\Big(d_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}}(x)+|\alpha|^{2}|x|^{5}\Big),

so

\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C(\epsilon+|\alpha|^{2}).

Note also that since
\phi(\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}})
is at least
C^{-1}|\alpha^{i}|^{2}
[Lemma
4.4
], we must have
\epsilon>C_{1}^{-1}|\alpha^{i}|^{2}
for a uniform constant
C_{1}
. Therefore, we can absorb the
|\alpha|^{2}
factor in
\epsilon
to conclude

\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C\epsilon.

for possibly a larger
C.

∎

We will use the non-concentration estimate in the following form. This follows from Proposition
5.2
.

Corollary 5.5
.

There is a C > 0 satisfying the following. For any
\delta>0
, there are
\epsilon_{0},R_{0}
depending on
\delta
such that if

•

|\alpha|<\epsilon_{0},

•

\tau+1<T_{\alpha},

•

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})<\epsilon_{0}.

then we obtain the improved estimate

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}(M_{\tau_{0}+1})\leq\max\bigg\{\delta D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})+C\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{R_{0}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},Ce^{-\frac{R_{\alpha}^{2}}{8p_{0}}}\bigg\}.

Proof.

Suppose
D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau}}(M_{\tau_{0}})<\epsilon.
By the proof of Proposition
5.2
, we have

M_{\tau_{0}+1}
is
c_{0}
graph over
\mathscr{T}_{\tau+1}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}
on
B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)
and

\bigg(\int_{M_{\tau_{0}+1}\cap B_{(1+\theta^{\prime})R_{\epsilon}}(0)}d_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha,\mathscr{C}}_{\tau+1}}^{2p}\;e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2p}}\leq C\epsilon.

Using this by Holder,

Lemma 5.7

\displaystyle=\sum a_{j}^{2}e^{-2\epsilon-2\lambda_{j}}e^{2\epsilon+2\lambda_{j}}+\sum a_{j}^{2}e^{-2\epsilon-2\lambda_{j}}e^{-2\epsilon-2\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum a_{j}^{2}e^{-2\epsilon-2\lambda_{j}}\big(e^{2\epsilon+2\lambda_{j}}+e^{-2\epsilon-2\lambda_{j}}\big).

, we can extract a subsequence of the flow
M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+s}
for the time interval
s\in[(k_{i}-1)L,(k_{i}+1)L]
which converges to a static flow. Therefore by shifting
\tau_{0}+(k_{i}-1)L
to
0
, we obtain a sequence of flows
M^{i}_{s}
defined for
s\in[0,2L]
which converges to a shrinker. Also, the sequence of flow
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+s}
converges to
\mathscr{C}
for
s\in[(k_{i}-1)L,(k_{i}+1)L]
as
|\alpha^{i}|\to 0.
Again by shifting
(k_{i}-1)L
to
0
, we obtain a sequence of flows
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+s}
defined for
s\in[0,2L]
which converges to
\mathscr{C}
and

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1}}(M^{i}_{0})<\epsilon.

Therefore we can use Proposition
6.3
to obtain that the sequence of flow
M^{i}_{s}
for
s\in[0,2L]
converges to some cylinder
\mathscr{RC}
for a rotation
\mathscr{R}.
Now by three annulus lemma, we have

\displaystyle D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+2L}}(M^{i}_{2L})-D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+L}}(M^{i}_{L})

\displaystyle\geq(1-e^{-\delta_{1}L})\epsilon

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon,

by choosing
L
large. But for
s\in[0,2L],M^{i}_{s}
converges to
\mathscr{RC}
and
\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+s}
converges to
\mathscr{C},
so this implies

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+2L}}(M^{i}_{2L})-D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+L}}(M^{i}_{L})\to 0,

which is a contradiction.

Let us suppose condition
(\textnormal{b})
holds for all
i,
while condition
(\textnormal{a})
fails. Again, there are two cases we need to consider,

•

Let
\kappa_{0}>0
be the constant from Proposition
6.2
and suppose that
d_{i}\leq\kappa_{0}|\alpha^{i}|^{4}.
Then arguing similarly as before, we have

D(M_{\tau_{0}+L},M_{\tau_{0}+2L})\leq C\Big(\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}+L})^{\gamma}-\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}+2L})^{\gamma}\Big).

•

We have
d_{i}\geq\kappa_{0}|\alpha_{i}|^{4}
. In addition,

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}})\geq e^{\delta_{2}^{\prime}L}d_{i},

and

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1+2L}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L})\geq e^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i}.

We can estimate
D(M_{\tau_{0}+L},M_{\tau_{0}+2L})
from above,

\displaystyle D(M_{\tau_{0}+L},M_{\tau_{0}+2L})

\displaystyle\leq D_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L})+D_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L})+CR_{\alpha^{i}}^{n+1}e^{-\frac{R_{\alpha^{i}}^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C(d_{i}+CL|\alpha^{i}|^{4})+C(e^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i}+CL|\alpha^{i}|^{4})

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{i}(1+L\kappa_{0}^{-1})(1+e^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}).

Here we absorbed the last term into
d_{i}
because otherwise we will have
(\textnormal{ii})
as before. We claim that for sufficiently large
L
we have

D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L})\leq\frac{1}{2}D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L}).

once
i
is large enough. Suppose that this were not the case, i.e.

D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L})\leq 2D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L}).

By definition there are rotations
\mathscr{R}^{i}
, and
\widehat{\alpha}^{i}
, such that

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\widehat{\alpha}^{i},\mathscr{R}^{i}\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}})+D_{\mathcal{W}^{\widehat{\alpha}^{i},\mathscr{R}^{i}\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L})<2D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L},M_{\tau_{0}+2L}).

Now using

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\widehat{\alpha}^{i},\mathscr{R}^{i}\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L})\leq 2D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L}),

we have,

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\widehat{\alpha}^{i},\mathscr{R}^{i}\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L})\leq Cd_{i}Le^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}.

Also, we have

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\widehat{\alpha}^{i},\mathscr{R}^{i}\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L})\leq Cd_{i}Le^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}.

Therefore,

|\mathscr{R}^{i}-Id|,|\alpha^{i}-\widehat{\alpha}^{i}|\leq Cd_{i}Le^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L},

where various factors, including
\kappa_{0}^{-1}
, are incorporated into the constant
C
. Now using

D_{\mathcal{W}^{\widehat{\alpha}^{i},\mathscr{R}^{i}\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}})\leq 2D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L}),

we have,

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq 2D(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+L},M^{i}_{\tau_{0}+2L})+CLe^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i}+Ce^{-\frac{R_{\alpha_{i}}^{2}}{8p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq CLe^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i},

increasing
C
further. Again, we used the fact the last term on the right hand side is dominated by
d_{i}
, otherwise we are already done as before. Hence,

\displaystyle D_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq CLe^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i}+CL|\alpha^{i}|^{4}

\displaystyle\leq CLe^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i},

for a larger
C
using
d_{i}\leq\kappa_{0}|\alpha^{i}|^{4}.
At the same time, we have

D_{\mathscr{T}^{\alpha^{i},\mathscr{C}^{i}}_{1}}(M^{i}_{\tau_{0}})\geq e^{\delta_{2}^{\prime}L}d_{i}.

So we obtain

e^{\delta_{2}^{\prime}L}d_{i}<CLe^{\delta_{1}^{\prime}L}d_{i},

for sufficiently large
i
. Since
\delta_{2}^{\prime}>\delta_{1}^{\prime}
, this is a contradiction if
L
is sufficiently large.

∎

We can now prove our main Theorem
1.1
. This follows exactly as in [
szekelyhidi2020uniqueness
, Theorem
6.7
]. Let us assume that
\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}-1})-\lim\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau})
is sufficiently small. Let
M_{\tau_{0}+kL}
is sufficiently close to
\mathscr{C}
in the sense that
D_{\mathscr{C}}(M_{\tau_{0}+kL})
is sufficiently small for
k=0,1,...,N
. We apply Proposition
6.4
repeatedly. Let
k_{1},k_{2},...,k_{m}
denote the values of
k
for which alternative (ii) holds, while alternative (i) holding for the other values. We then have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}D(M_{\tau_{0}+kL},M_{\tau_{0}+(k+1)L})

\displaystyle\leq 2D(M_{\tau_{0}},M_{\tau_{0}+L})+2\sum_{j=1}^{m}D(M_{\tau_{0}+k_{j}L},M_{\tau_{0}+(k_{j}+1)L})

\displaystyle\leq 2D(M_{\tau_{0}},M_{\tau_{0}+L})+2C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}+k_{j}L})^{\gamma}-\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}+(k_{j}+1)L})^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq 2D(M_{\tau_{0}},M_{\tau_{0}+L})+2C\Big(\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}+k_{j}L})^{\gamma}-\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}+(k_{j}+1)L})^{\gamma}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 2D(M_{\tau_{0}},M_{\tau_{0}+L})+2C\Big(\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}})^{\gamma}-\lim\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau})^{\gamma}\Big).

Therefore we have,

d(M_{\tau_{0}},M_{\tau_{0}+(N+1)L})\leq C\Big(d(M_{\tau_{0}},M_{\tau_{0}+L})+\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}})^{\gamma}-\lim\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau})^{\gamma}\Big).

If
M_{\tau_{0}}
is sufficiently close to
\mathscr{C}
, then this implies that
M_{\tau_{0}+(N+1)L}
is also close enough to
\mathscr{C}
to continue applying Proposition
6.4
as long as
\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{0}})^{\gamma}-\lim\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau})^{\gamma}
is also sufficiently small. We can therefore take
N
above to be arbitrarily large. Since
\mathscr{C}
is a tangent flow, we know that for a sequence
\tau_{i}\to\infty
we have

D_{\mathscr{C}}(M_{\tau_{i}})\to 0,

and by Huisken’s monotonicity formula,

\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau_{i}})^{\gamma}-\lim\limits_{\tau\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(M_{\tau})^{\gamma}\to 0.

Therefore, by applying the argument above we then find that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\sup_{\tau\geq\tau_{i}}D_{\mathscr{C}}(M_{\tau})=0.

Therefore
\mathscr{C}
is the unique tangent flow.

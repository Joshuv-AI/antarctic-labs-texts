Analysis of a clot-based combined radiomics model for predicting embolic etiology in acute ischemic stroke patients
Authors: Ru Ding, Kun An, Fen Wang, Zirui Cao, Yan Zeng, Lili Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesAcute ischemic stroke (AIS) remains the primary cause of mortality and disability among adults in China. Different etiologies of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) are considered important factors affecting neurological function. The accurate etiological classification of AIS prior to surgery is crucial. To investigate and explore the predictive value of a clot-based combined radiomics model for identifying the etiological subtypes of acute ischemic stroke.Materials and methodsA total of 263 patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by anterior circulation large artery occlusion were retrospectively enrolled. These were grouped into training (180), testing (45), and external validation cohorts (38). NCCT and CTA scans were adopted to segment region of interest (ROI) of clots. Feature selection was conducted to establish Clinical model, radiomics models (NCCT, CTA, and NCCT&CTA), and combined model.ResultsThe AUCs of the clinical model and radiomics models (NCCT, CTA and NCCT&CTA) in the testing cohort were 0.8288(95 % CI: 0.7174–0.9403), 0.8133(95 % CI:0.6853–0.9414), 0.8075(95 % CI:0.6844–0.9307) and 0.8535(95 % CI:0.6774–1), respectively. The combined model achieved a greater AUC than the other four models in the testing cohort (0.9077 [95 % CI: 0.821–0.9944]). Clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrated that the radiomics (NCCT&CTA) model and combined model show better net benefits within a relatively wide range of threshold probabilities.ConclusionThe combined radiomics model achieved good predictive efficacy for distinguishing the etiological subtypes of acute ischemic stroke and can provide valuable information for the precise selection of recanalization strategies in clinical practice.Keywords: Acute ischemic stroke, Radiomics, Thrombosis, Computed tomography

Introduction

Acute ischemic stroke is a prevalent cerebrovascular condition in clinical practice that frequently leads to disability or death; moreover, it is a major contributor to global mortality [1], [2]. The main factor underlying acute ischemic stroke is the blockage of a cerebral artery by a clot, which leads to insufficient blood flow to the brain and subsequent tissue ischemia and hypoxia. The TOAST (Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) classification, which is recommended by the Neurology Branch of the Chinese Medical Association, considers various stroke onset mechanisms for classification [3], [4], [5]. The origin of different clot types is linked to differences in pathological makeup, choices in clinical diagnostic techniques, and outlook for prognosis [6], [7]. With the continuous development of artificial intelligence, research on radiomics has become increasingly advanced [8]. This methodology allows us to extract quantitative features from imaging images that are not superficially visible and discover more in-depth information. By integrating diverse clinical data, predictive models can be developed for diseases with greater accuracy and efficiency [9], [10]. The objective of this study was to investigate the precision and utility of radiomics models based on clots by utilizing NCCT (noncontrast computed tomography) and CTA (computed tomography angiography) images for extracting relevant features of etiologic stroke classification. The aim of this study was to enhance clinical practice by accurately predicting the origins of etiology through radiomics models, thus enabling prompt and effective treatment for individuals with ischemic stroke and fostering a positive prognosis.

Materials and methods

Patients in the study

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013). This study was approved by the relevant ethics committee (Approval No. KY-2023–046–01). The requirement for written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the research. A retrospective analysis was conducted on patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by anterior circulation large artery occlusion between 2019 and 2023(n = 225). The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) were adults aged 18 years and older; 2) were admitted for stroke within 24 h of symptom onset; and 3) had relevant NCCT and CTA data showing occlusion in the major blood vessels of the anterior circulation, such as the internal carotid artery and middle cerebral artery M1–2 segment. The exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) patients without anterior circulation large vessel occlusion or with posterior circulation occlusion; 2) patients with poor image quality that was unsuitable for diagnosis and analysis; 3) patients with incomplete clinical, laboratory, or imaging data; and 4) patients with intracranial hemorrhage, tumor, or trauma. The patients were randomly divided into a training cohort and a testing cohort at an 8:2 ratio. The external validation cohort included 38 patients from our branch hospital who met inclusion criteria collected from February to May 2024.

Clinical information, such as sex, age, thrombus location, hypertension status, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, diabetes status, atrial fibrillation status, history of previous stroke, coronary heart disease, hyperlipidemia, previous pulmonary infections, smoking habits, and alcohol consumption, was recorded.

Two experienced radiologists evaluated the presence of high-density arterial sign (HAS) and resolved any disagreements through negotiation, which is characterized by a localized increase in density within arteries observed on NCCT images. HAS is often associated with local blood flow blockage and increased aggregation of red blood cells during the early stages of thrombus formation, thus making it a common specific imaging finding in acute ischemic stroke patients [11], [12]. The assessment findings were then included in the patients’ clinical records.

By combining the patient's clinical medical history, onset patterns, and thrombus characteristics, two experienced neurologists classified patients (n = 225) into two groups based on the Toast classification. There were 92 patients in the large artery atherosclerosis stroke group and 133 patients in the cardioembolic stroke group.The external validation cohort (n = 38) included 26 patients in the large artery atherosclerosis stroke group and 12 patients in the cardioembolic stroke group.

Image acquisition

Scanning was performed by using two CT scanners (Siemens Somatom Definition Flash and UIH uCT710 CT machines), with the patient in a supine position. The scanning parameters were as follows: 100–140 kV tube voltage; automatic tube current modulation (155–180 mA); CTA section thickness 1 mm; section interval 1 mm; NCCT section thickness 5 mm; and section interval 5 mm. The CTA scan range extends from the second cervical vertebra to the cranial vertex, whereas the NCCT scan range encompasses the skull base to the top of the skull. Iodixanol (350 mgI/ml) was used as a contrast agent for CTA with an injection volume of 50–80 ml at a rate of 4–5 ml/s. Subsequently, a continuous infusion of normal saline solution (50 ml) was administered via the elbow vein bolus method by using a high-pressure injector.

Image segmentation and consistency evaluation

The Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) files containing NCCT and CTA images of 225 stroke patients were retrieved and imported into the open-source image segmentation software ITK-SNAP (version 4.0.1). Two radiologists performed a comparative analysis between the NCCT and CTA images to identify the location of the thrombus and manually segmented the region of interest (ROI). The two experienced radiologists randomly selected 50 patients to segment the ROIs in both the NCCT and CTA images. After a one-month interval, the same radiologists segmented the ROIs again. Intra- and interobserver consistency were evaluated by using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). An ICC > 0.75 indicates a high level of consistency, whereas group features with ICCs ≤ 0.75 were excluded. The radiologists were blinded to the clinical information while performing the segmentation.

Radiomics feature extraction, selection and modeling

The overall radiomics analysis was conducted by using the uAI Research Portal (uRP) [13], which is an internally developed comprehensive research platform for medical image analysis provided by United Imaging Intelligence. The original images and manually segmented regions of interest (ROIs) were transferred into the NII format and imported into the uRP platform, which automatically normalizes the image signals and intensities. Relevant radiomics features, including first-order gray-level features, second-order shape features, and texture features, were extracted from both noncontrast computed tomography (NCCT) and computed tomography angiography (CTA) images. After normalizing all of the features by using z scores, prior to feature selection and modeling, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model was used to select features with nonzero coefficients for modeling (Fig. 1).

Feature selection. （a）The optimal tuning parameter (λ) was selected by using 5-fold cross-validation (CV) with the LASSO regression model. （b）λ was used to obtain 19 nonzero coefficient radiomics features, wherein the different colored lines represent the change tracks for the feature coefficients.

Radiomics and combined model construction and validation

The first step involved conducting a univariate logistic regression analysis on all of the clinical features to identify a subset of features that were statistically significant (P < 0.05). The selected features were subsequently subjected to multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify the final independent predictors for clinical model. The optimal radiomics model was selected, and its corresponding radiomics score was calculated. By integrating clinical model, a combined model along with a radiomics nomogram was constructed. The diagnostic performance and predictive ability of the composite model were assessed by using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, precision, and F score. The workflow of the study is shown in Fig. 2.

The workflow of this study. First, the regions of interest (ROIs) were segmented in the NCCT and CTA images, and then the relevant radiomic features were extracted from the ROIs. After normalizing all of the features by using z scores, the optimal feature subset was selected by LASSO regression model, and the radiomic models (NCCT, CTA, NCCT&CTA) were constructed. The Radscore of the optimal radiomics model (NCCT&CTA) was calculated. At the same time, univariate and multivariate Logistic regression analysis was performed to identify the independent factors of clinical prediction. Finally, a radiomic nomogram was obtained by integrating Radscore and independent clinical predictors, that is combined model. the diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curves, and the net benefit was evaluated using clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) curves.

Clinical usage

Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to quantitatively assess the net benefit of various threshold probabilities in the training and testing cohorts. The net benefit is computed based on a predetermined probability threshold.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed by utilizing SPSS 26.0 software and the uRP medical imaging research platform. Continuous variables are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (`x ± s) if they followed a normal distribution; otherwise, they are expressed as the median and interquartile range (IQR). Count data are displayed as the quantity and percentage (%). Single-factor and multiple-factor logistic regression analyses were used to identify independent predictors of clinical characteristics. The clinical utility of the radiomics models and the combined model was evaluated by using the DeLong test. A P value < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistically significant intergroup differences.

Results

Clinical characteristics

Out of a total of 225 patients with acute ischemic stroke, 92 were diagnosed with large artery atherosclerosis stroke, and 133 were diagnosed with cardioembolic stroke. The study included 180 patients in the training cohort for model construction by using a 5-fold cross-validation method and 45 patients in the testing cohort. Basic clinical data were analyzed by using univariate and multivariate logistic regression, and the results are shown in Table 1. A significant difference was observed in the presence of atrial fibrillation between the two groups (P < 0.01), whereas the P values for other clinical characteristic variables were greater than 0.05. These findings suggest that atrial fibrillation is an independent predictive factor among the examined clinical features. The AUCs of the training cohort and testing cohort in the clinical model were 0.8781 (95 % CI: 0.8294–0.9268) and 0.8288 (95 % CI: 0.7174–0.9403).

Reproducibility analysis

The average intra- and interobserver ICCs with satisfactory agreement were 0.899 and 0.908, respectively.

Features selection

After image preprocessing, 1904 radiomics features were extracted from each of the NCCT and CTA image modes, and the optimized feature subset was selected via the LASSO model. The optimal tuning parameter (λ) was selected by using 5-fold cross-validation, and 19 nonzero coefficient radiomics features were obtained (Fig. 1). The NCCT features included 10 texture features. These features consisted of 6 gray-level size zone matrix (GLSZM) features, 1 gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) features, 1 gray-level run-length matrix (GLRLM) features and 2 neighboring gray tone difference matrix (NGTDM) feature. In contrast, CTA comprised 1 first-order feature and 8 exclusive texture features, which included 5 GLSZM features, 1 GLCM features, 1 gray-level dependence matrix (GLDM) features, and 1 NGTDM feature. The feature selection results for NCCT, CTA were utilized to construct radiomics models for individual modalities (NCCT and CTA). The 6 combined features among them were used to construct the fusion modalities (NCCT & CTA) radiomics model, consisted of 5 texture features that were extracted from NCCT (3 GLSZM and 2 NGTDM) and 1 texture features derived from CTA (1 GLCM), are illustrated in Fig. 3.

Based on LASSO logistic regression, we selected radiomics features related to the etiological subtypes of infarction. Finally, some radiomics features were screened from NCCT and CTA images of acute ischemic stroke patients, which are shown in (a and b), respectively.

Radiomics and combined model construction, validation, and evaluation

The ROC curves of the two models with logistic and SVM classifiers in both the training and testing cohorts are depicted in Fig. 4. The AUC of the SVM model marginally surpassed that of the logistic model in the testing cohort; finally, we selected the SVM model as the best radiomics model. The radiomics models for predicting embolic etiology in the training cohort and testing cohort, which included both a single modality (NCCT and CTA) and a fusion modality (NCCT & CTA), were constructed with the SVM model. The AUCs of the training cohort and testing cohort in the NCCT&CTA radiomics model were 0.8973 (95 % CI: 0.8405–0.9541) and 0.8535 (95 % CI: 0.6774–1), respectively. The area under the curve (AUC) of the NCCT&CTA radiomics model in the testing cohort was higher than that of the other radiomics models. Finally, the NCCT&CTA radiomics model was considered to be the best radiomics model, and the radscore was calculated.

Discussion

Acute stroke remains the primary cause of mortality and disability among adults in China, thus leading to focal or widespread cerebral injury that impacts both the circulatory and central nervous systems [1], [14]. Large vessel blockage is a frequent factor contributing to acute ischemic stroke [14]. Prompt revascularization is crucial in the treatment of acute ischemic stroke to rescue the ischemic penumbra of the brain tissue. Patients are administered intravenous thrombolysis within the appropriate time window without contraindications; for patients with large vessel occlusion, the selection of the appropriate endovascular treatment leads to improved therapeutic outcomes and prognoses [15], [16], [17]. Several studies have demonstrated diverse emboli compositions among patients with different stroke etiologies, thus suggesting that the varying composition of thrombi may influence the choice of intravascular therapy [6], [18]. Thrombi components in cardiac embolism are characterized by elevated levels of fibrin and platelets and reduced levels of red blood cells. In contrast, thrombi from large artery atherosclerosis patients are rich in red blood cells [19], [20]. A study by Fereidoon Nezhad et al. [21] demonstrated a notable enhancement in clot toughness with increased fibrin content, thus suggesting that clots with a higher concentration of fibrin are less likely to tear than those rich in red blood cells. A clot rich in red blood cells is softer and more fragile, thus making it easier to break apart for removal and enhancing its compatibility with stents and thereby improving the success rate of stent thrombectomy. However, these characteristics also increase the likelihood of the clots breaking into fragments and causing secondary embolism. Conversely, a white blood cell and fibrin-rich clot is more resilient, has a greater coefficient of friction with the vessel wall, and makes stent thrombectomy more challenging. Additionally, it is more likely to cause damage to the endothelium during thrombectomy, thus leading to a greater risk of thrombosis recurrence and impacting vessel reopening outcomes [22], [23]. After conducting a comparative analysis between thrombectomy and stent thrombectomy, it has been suggested by researchers that stent thrombectomy is more effective in treating large artery atherosclerotic stroke, whereas aspiration thrombectomy is considered to be more appropriate for treating cardioembolic stroke [24]. Large artery atherosclerotic stroke, which is characterized by favorable collateral circulation and enhanced efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis, is a more prevalent form of ischemic stroke in China. The demand for thrombectomy within the time window for ischemic brain tissue is relatively lower; however, there is a greater incidence of vascular reocclusion and poorer neurological functional prognosis. In contrast, cardioembolic stroke patients exhibit poor collateral circulation, necessitate a high demand for thrombectomy within the time window for ischemic brain tissue, but demonstrate a better prognosis after timely thrombectomy while also presenting with a greater risk of bleeding complications [25]. The accurate etiological classification of AIS prior to surgery is crucial for guiding acute phase treatment, targeted secondary preventions, and assessments of patient prognosis.

The present study utilized NCCT and CTA, which segmented the region of interest (ROI) associated with the responsible embolus and extracted radiomics features. The 19 radiomics features were ultimately used for constructing the combined model. The final radiomics features included 5 texture features based on NCCT and 1 texture feature based on CTA, including GLSZM, GLCM and NGTDM. Texture features are characteristics that describe the spatial distribution of intensity levels in the voxels of the region of interest and describe the relationships between the gray values in the image. The emboli that are formed in large artery atherosclerotic stroke and cardioembolic stroke patients have different compositions and are difficult to directly observe on conventional CT images. Therefore, it is necessary to utilize more advanced texture features for differentiation. The findings of this study validated the effectiveness of the clot-based radiomics model in identifying the etiology of embolism, thus demonstrating statistically significant distinctions among various sources of thrombus components. This conclusion aligns with the research outcomes reported by Wei and Jiang [26], [27]. Other researchers have performed similar studies corroborating the ability of radiomics to reveal intricate tissue details within emboli that conventional methods may overlook, thus allowing for improved differentiation of emboli heterogeneity stemming from different etiologies [28], [29]. Researchers such as Patel and Sarioglu have demonstrated that clot-based radiomics can serve as a valuable tool for preassessing patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) prior to mechanical thrombectomy (MT) [6], [30]. A previous study conducted by Wang et al. [10] established and validated a CT-based radiomics model that could accurately differentiate between fresh and older thrombi in stroke patients receiving mechanical thrombectomy.

In this study, univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted on the clinical characteristics of patients, thus demonstrating significant differences (P < 0.01) between patients with atrial fibrillation and patients with large artery atherosclerosis, as well as among patients with the cardioembolic type of AIS, thus suggesting that atrial fibrillation is a clinically independent predictive factor for thrombotic etiological classification. This is consistent with the majority of research findings, and some scholars have also indicated that patients with cardioembolic stroke have a higher incidence of atrial fibrillation; moreover, effective screening and treatment for atrial fibrillation can reduce the occurrence rate of stroke [31], [32]. In addition to atrial fibrillation (AF), other clinical characteristics, including sex, age, thrombus location, and hypertension, among others, did not exhibit significant differences between the two groups. A study conducted by Jiang et al. [27] demonstrated significant differences between patients with and without cardioembolic stroke in terms of atrial fibrillation and smoking history (P < 0.05). The risk of stroke has been found to increase with advancing age, with approximately 75 % of strokes occurring in individuals aged 65 years or older [33]. The studies conducted by Nimjee and Gasbarrino et al. [3], [34] indicated a greater incidence of stroke in males than in females. However, there is a lack of consensus regarding potential variations in age, sex, and other characteristics among patients with different etiological subtypes of AIS.

The ROC curves of the SVM and logistic models were compared in this study, thus demonstrating a slightly greater AUC for the SVM model; however, this difference was not statistically significant. In binary classification problems, SVM models and logistic model algorithms each possess their own advantages. Generally, SVM exhibits higher accuracy than logistic regression; however, it is accompanied by lower training efficiency. Conversely, logistic regression is relatively more suitable for handling larger datasets. The sample size of this study was limited. When considering all of the factors, SVM was selected as the optimal radiomics model.

This study had several limitations. First, although CTA images typically have thinner slices and theoretically offer more information, research findings indicate that the AUC value of the NCCT model is slightly higher than that of the CTA model on the ROC curves. This difference may be attributed to the more precise outlining of regions of interest (ROIs) in NCCT than in CTA. In CTA scans, thrombi often appear as faint, low-density shadows that can be challenging to visually discern. Occasionally, due to the broader range of thrombus tissue or inadequate distal enhancement, the delineated ROI area on CTA may encompass more space than the actual thrombus site. In contrast, NCCT scans feature thicker slices and fewer layers, which can sometimes result in clearer visualization of thrombus contours due to arterial hyperdensity indicators. As a result, ROIs can be delineated more accurately on NCCT with less inclusion of nonthrombotic parts, thus leading to greater performance. We may consider including stroke patients with DSA images in future studies to reference the DSA images for precise delineation of the ROI in CTA images, thereby enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of the CTA radiomics model. Additionally, it is worth noting that the study did not encompass other TOAST classifications aside from large artery atherosclerosis and cardioembolic stroke, due to the limited sample size. This constraint could introduce bias or inaccuracies in the study findings. Third, this was a single-center retrospective study with relatively limited and singular data. These aspects will be addressed and supplemented in future studies.

In conclusion, the clot-based CT radiomics model has a certain predictive value for embolic etiology in patients with acute ischemic stroke. The combined model demonstrated superior discriminative efficacy compared to the individual radiomics models, thus indicating a strong correlation between clinical features, radiomics features, and the etiology and composition of thrombi. This approach can assist clinicians in determining the etiology and characteristics of thrombi before thrombolysis or mechanical thrombectomy, thus facilitating the selection of more precise therapeutic approaches to achieve better treatment outcomes and prognoses for patients.

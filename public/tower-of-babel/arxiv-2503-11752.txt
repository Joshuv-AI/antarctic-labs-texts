Abstract
\jwst has revealed a population of ‘Little Red Dots’ (LRDs): compact, red objects at redshifts z = 2 ​ – ​ 9 z=2\text{--}9 with ‘v’-shaped spectral energy distributions, broad permitted lines, and, often, hydrogen Balmer absorption. We use NIRSpec/IFS data from the \blackthunder survey to study the H α line in the LRD Abell2744-QSO1 at z = 7.04 z=7.04 , which is a confirmed AGN due to time-variable equivalent width (EW) in its broad emission lines. The H α spectral profile is non-Gaussian, requiring at least two Gaussian components. We also detect a narrow-line Gaussian component, and strong H α absorption (EW relative to the continuum ∼ 22 + 12 − 7 ​ Å \sim 22_{+12}^{-7}~\AA ), confirming a connection between the strong Balmer break and line absorption. The absorber is at rest with respect to broad H α , suggesting that the gas cannot be interpreted as an inflow or outflow, forming instead a long-lived structure. Its velocity dispersion is σ abs = 110 − 10 + 20 \sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}=110^{+20}_{-10} \kms , consistent with the value inferred from the analysis of the Balmer break. Based on H α , we infer a black hole mass of log ⁡ ( M ∙ / \Msun ) = 7.2 \log(M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}/\Msun)=7.2 , smaller but close to the previous estimates based on H β . The Eddington ratio is 0.09. Combining the high signal-to-noise ratio of the narrow H α line with the spectral resolution R = 3,700 R=3,700 of the G395H grating, we infer a narrow-line intrinsic dispersion σ n = 22 − 6 + 5 \sigma_{\mathrm{n}}=22_{-6}^{+5} \kms , which places a stringent constraint on the black-hole-to-dynamical-mass ratio of this system to be M ∙ / \mdyn = 0.15 ​ – ​ 1.2 M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}/\mdyn=0.15\text{--}1.2 , confirming the overmassive nature of the black hole and potentially leaving little room for a host galaxy.

Introduction

A key question in galaxy evolution is whether supermassive black holes (SMBHs) and their host galaxies grow in tandem or if one forms first
(

(
Portegies Zwart et al., 1999
, e.g.,)
? This question is fundamental for our understanding of the link
between SMBHs and galaxies. At redshifts
z=0\text{--}2
, SMBH masses (
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
) correlate tightly with galaxy properties

;

,
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
–
\sigma
relation;)
and stellar or bulge mass
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
–
\mstar
relation;

Kormendy and Ho, 2013
;
McConnell and Ma, 2013
;
Reines and Volonteri, 2015
,
hereafter:

,

Marconi and Hunt, 2003
;
Saglia et al., 2016
;
Sun et al., 2025
;
Dalla Bontà et al., 2025
,
hereafter:

These correlations suggest a co-evolutionary link, where accreting SMBHs regulate star formation through powerful feedback

;

;
Fiore et al., 2017
;
Veilleux et al., 2020
)
, while galaxies, in turn, shape SMBH growth by modulating the gas supply for accretion, for instance by facilitating angular momentum loss, by altering the metal content and thermodynamic properties of the gas, and not least via star formation and supernova feedback
(

;
Trebitsch et al., 2018
;

While efficiently accreting SMBHs powering Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) are readily observable at high redshifts, the properties of their host galaxies remain uncertain – especially in the low-mass regime most relevant to understanding the origins of galaxies and SMBHs.

The advent of

, with many appearing over-massive relative to their hosts’ stellar mass

, suggesting a degree of co-evolution

)
, but could hold already for the total baryonic mass

In addition to a possible departure from local scaling relations, several broad-line AGN display strong Balmer breaks; if

High-resolution spectroscopy of Abell2744-QSO1 covers

\lambda=5.278~\mum

in integral field spectroscopy mode

. Here we use
only
the G395H grating observations, consisting of 14 dithered integrations using the medium-dither cycling to offset fail-open shutters

Bechtold et al., 2024

.
We used the improved reference

minimize amplifier noise
(
Moseley et al., 2010
;
Rauscher et al., 2017

1/f
noise;)
. Each integration consisted of 23 groups
and one exposure, giving 1,692 s per dithered exposure and a total on-source
exposure time of
1692.3\times 14=23,692

For the data reduction, we use the procedure described in
Perna et al. (2023)
,
but we start from the
jwst
pipeline version 1.17.1 and context file
1303. At
z=7.04
,
H α
falls at
\lambda=5.278~\mum
, just outside the calibrated range of the NIRSpec G395H grating, which reaches
\lambda<5.27~\mum
. Moreover, the high-velocity wings of broad-
H α
emission can easily reach up to
\lambda=5.32~\mum
.
However, since the low-pass F290LP is not suppressing long wavelengths, and since the grating transmission does not drop sharply,
H α
is readily observable on the detector.
We therefore extract the data by extrapolating the flat-field curves and wavelength solution beyond the nominal range. Due to the small extrapolation (only 95 spectral pixels between
\lambda=5.27~\mum
and 5.34
\mum
), and thanks to the well characterized behaviour of the grating, the error on the wavelength
solution is expected to be negligible. Similarly, the line-spread function is
obtained from the grating equation, so we expect no significant deviation from
the pre-flight characterization of the instrument

Jakobsen et al., 2022

2.1
Spectral extraction

We use the optimal extraction method to obtain the 1-d spectrum
(
Horne, 1986
)
. In
principle, we would measure the spatial profile directly from the datacube, but in practice
the resulting profile shapes are too noisy. Therefore, we use a 2-d Gaussian tracing the
point spread function (PSF) of the NIRSpec/IFS. The aperture has elliptical shape
with fixed position angle 25°and axis ratio
q=0.8

(
D’Eugenio et al., 2024b
, following the
elongated PSF shape and slice orientation;)
. The semi-major axis is
wavelength dependent, following new empirical measurements
(
D’Eugenio et al., 2026
;
Jones et al., 2026
)
.

Within two spectral pixels on either side of narrow
H β
,
[O
iii
]λλ4959,5007
, and
H α
, we use
the spatial profile measured from the narrow
H α
line, which is more extended than the
PSF
(
Maiolino et al., 2025b
;
Juodžbalis et al., 2026b
)
.

If we replace our analysis with a large aperture of semi-major axis 0.25-arcsec

(
Ji et al., 2025
, e.g.,)
our results are unchanged, but the spectral SNR for the broad
lines is lower than for the optimal extraction.

The spectrum is shown in Fig.
1
, with the data in black and
the best-fit model in red. While the model is described in the next sections, here we observe
that the continuum blueward and redward of
H α
is described well by a linear fit,
similar to other LRDs and broad-line AGN
over an equally narrow
spectral range; e.g.,
Juodžbalis et al., 2024b
;
Juodžbalis et al., 2024a
;
Greene et al., 2024
;
Taylor et al., 2025
. This
agreement lends confidence to the accuracy of our extrapolated flux calibration. Nevertheless,
since our
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
measurements are sensitive primarily to the width of the broad
H α
, with only a
sub-linear dependence on the line flux, our main results are not driven by possible flux-calibration
errors.

2.2
Spectral modelling

In this section we describe the fiducial model, which employs two
broad Gaussians to model the broad lines. For clarity, we
anticipate that the model choice is not motivated by statistical preference,
but by the recent results of
Maiolino et al. (2025b)
;
Juodžbalis et al. (2026b)
, who establish that
the intermediate-width broad component in Abell2744-QSO1 is spatially resolved, while
the broadest broad component is not, motivating two distinct Gaussians.
Alternative models are introduced in Section
3
or in the
Appendix.

We model the narrow lines with Gaussians, using the same redshift and intrinsic
velocity dispersion
\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}
for all lines.
We further constrain the
[O
iii
]
and
[N
ii
]
doublet ratios to 0.336
(
Storey and Zeippen, 2000
)
and 0.328, respectively
(
Dojčinović et al., 2023
)
,
hence the narrow-line model requires six parameters:
z_{\mathrm{n}}
,
\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}
, and the fluxes of
H β
,
[O
iii
]λ5007
,
H α
and
[N
ii
]λ6583
.
In principle, the narrow hydrogen,
[O
iii
]
and
[N
ii
]
lines could arise from different regions with different kinematics, but the limited SNR of our data (and the non-detection of
[N
ii
]λλ6548,6583
) prevent us from testing this hypothesis.
We model the continuum piecewise in each spectral window using a first-order
polynomial each, requiring four more parameters in total. For the broad Balmer lines we use
a double Gaussian, given the clearly non-Gaussian shape of
H α
(Fig.
1
).
The two Gaussians representing broad
H α
share the same centroid, specified by a common free parameter representing the velocity offset
v_{\mathrm{b}}
between the broad and narrow lines. We have two independent FWHM values,
while the flux is parametrized by the total unabsorbed flux of
H α
and by the
flux ratio between the narrowest Gaussian and the total unabsorbed line flux.
There are thus five more free parameters.

One additional parameter is required to model the broad
H β
, which also consists of two Gaussians, but whose velocity, FWHM and flux ratio are constrained to be identical to those for

H α
. In other words, the broad
H α
and
H β
models share the same kinematics and internal flux ratio, and the only additional parameter for broad
H β
is the total unabsorbed
H β
flux.

In principle, the flux ratio of the two broad Gaussians could be different
between
H β
and
H α
, for instance if these two Gaussians reflect
different kinematic components, which could be subject to different excitation
mechanisms and dust attenuation. However, the number of parameters for
H β
is effectively limited by the SNR of the data
(
58
)
.
Finally, we add a dense hydrogen absorber, with a common covering factor

C_{f}
, the same velocity dispersion
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}
, and different velocity offsets
v_{\mathrm{abs,\text{H\,\textbeta}}}
and
v_{\mathrm{abs,\text{H\,\textalpha}}}
for
H β
and
H α
(the velocity offset is relative to the redshift of the narrow lines). The residual intensity at wavelength

\lambda
is given by

\begin{split}I(\lambda)/I_{0}(\lambda)&=1-C_{f}+C_{f}\cdot\exp\left(-\tau(k;\,\lambda)\right)\\
\tau(k;\,\lambda)&=\tau_{0}(k)\cdot f[v(\lambda)],\end{split}

(1)

where
I_{0}(\lambda)
is the spectral flux density before absorption,
\tau_{0}(k)
is the optical depth at the centre of the line (with
k=\text{H\,\textbeta}
or
H α
) and
f[v(\lambda)]
is the velocity distribution of the absorbing atoms, assumed to be a Gaussian probability distribution.

I_{0}(\lambda)
consists of both the continuum
dominated by the accretion disc;
58
and the BLR.
The different velocity of the
H β
and
H α
absorptions is motivated
by visual inspection of the data, and is justified by the observation of
dense absorbers with different effective velocities for
H α
and

H β
in higher-SNR observations of LRDs
(
Lin et al., 2026
;
Ji et al., 2026
;
D’Eugenio et al., 2025
)
.

For each optimization step, the trial model is convolved with the
wavelength-dependent line spread function and is then integrated
pixel-by-pixel.
For model inference we use a Bayesian approach, with flat, non-informative
priors around all parameters.
The posterior probabilities are sampled
using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo method
see
D’Eugenio et al., 2026
for more details
.

\Delta\,\text{BIC}>10

as discriminant.
A single Gaussian model to fit broad
H α
is ruled out, with
\Delta\,\text{BIC}>30
(Appendix
A
).

D’Eugenio et al. (2025)
found mild asymmetry in the broad-line profile of the

Irony
LRD at
z=6.68
, which they reproduce by decoupling the two broad Gaussians modelling broad
H α
. For Abell2744-QSO1, introducing independent
velocity shifts
v_{\mathrm{b,1}}
and
v_{\mathrm{b,2}}
yields different centroids (
-3_{-9}^{+10}
and
-110_{-40}^{+40}~\kms
, a 2.5-σ difference) but no improvement over the fiducial model.
We also tested decoupling the
H β
and
H α
broad lines, which also results in a fit that is indistinguishable from the fiducial model.
Finally, we studied non-Gaussian line shapes, namely a Lorentzian profile

(which may represent turbulent broadening;
Kollatschny and Zetzl, 2013
, and which we parametrize as a Voigt profile, accounting for the finite instrument resolution)
, and a
single Gaussian convolved with an exponential kernel
(
Laor, 2006
;
Rusakov et al., 2026
, which may
be due to Thomson scattering;)
.
Both the Lorentzian and exponential-wings models provide an equally good fit as the fiducial model (
\Delta\,\text{BIC}=3
in favour of the exponential-wings model). In Section
3.2
we discuss that these two alternative models are disfavoured based on the analysis of
Juodžbalis et al. (2026b)
.

3.1
Host galaxy

To estimate the dynamical mass of the system, we use the same approach as

58
, but we leverage the higher spectral resolution and SNR of
H α
to infer a more
accurate velocity dispersion
\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}=22~\kms
of the gas. For the galaxy size, we adopt the upper limit on the half-light radius
\re<30
pc from
Furtak et al. (2023)
. Our highest estimate of
\mdyn
is derived from the
virial calibration of
van der Wel et al. (2022)
,

\mdyn=K(n)K(q)\sigma^{\prime 2}_{\star,\rm int}R_{\rm sma}/G
, where
K(n)
and
K(q)
are functions of the Sérsic index
n
and the projected axis ratio
q
,
R_{\rm sma}
is the semi-major axis and
\sigma^{\prime}_{\star,\rm int}
is the integrated stellar velocity dispersion.
Assuming a Sérsic index
n=1

and axis ratio
q=1
leads to the highest possible value of the structural factor
K(n)K(q)
in this calibration, hence the most conservative upper limit on
\mdyn
from the
van der Wel et al. (2022)

calibration. We also increase
\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}
by 0.175 dex, following the
calibration of
Bezanson et al. (2018)
, as described in
Übler et al. (2023)
and
Maiolino et al. (2024)
. This is meant to capture the different
average value of
\sigma
between gas and stars
(
van der Wel et al., 2022
, the latter of which underlies the calibration of)
. However, we
note that at low values of the stellar velocity
dispersion, the calibration of
Bezanson et al. (2018)

may already implicitly capture an average inclination correction, since aperture velocity
dispersion has a larger velocity contribution for
gas than for stars
(
Cortese et al., 2016
;
Barat et al., 2019
, e.g.,)
. Our inclusion of the

Bezanson et al. (2018)
scaling between the gas and
stellar aperture dispersions thus yields an even more conservative upper limit on
\mdyn
, by 0.4 dex.
With this approach, we find an upper limit
\log(\mdyn/\Msun)<8.0
. Alternatively, using the calibration of
Stott et al. (2016)
for purely dispersion dominated systems, we find
\log(\mdyn/\Msun)<7.1
.
These are extremely low values, driven by the low dispersion of the narrow
H α
and by the
unresolved nature of the galaxy in the spatial dimension.

.

(
58

, we can estimate the gas mass from the narrow-

M_{\text{H\,\textalpha}}=3.3\times 10^{8}~\Msun\dfrac{L_{\mathrm{n}}(\text{H\,\textalpha})}{10^{43}~\text{erg\,s${}^{-1}$}}\dfrac{100~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}}{n_{\mathrm{e}}},

\Msun

3.2
Black hole mass

To estimate the mass of the SMBH, we use two alternative virial calibrations,
both based on the luminosity and width of the broad
H α
line. The
calibration of
108
uses the line FWHM. In our case, we have three possible choices, depending on how the
broad-line profile is interpreted. One could adopt the FWHM of the total line profile (modelled as the sum of two Gaussians), corrected for line absorption.
With this method, and with
FWHM_{\mathrm{b}}=680_{-80}^{+70}

\kms
(Table
2
), we infer
\log(M_{\mathrm{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}}\textsuperscript{\hskip-3.0pt\cite[citealias]{\@@bibref{Phrase1}{reines+volonteri2015}{\@@citephrase{\al@reines+volonteri2015}}{}}}/\Msun)=6.3_{-0.1}^{+0.1}
, with 0.3 dex additional
uncertainty from the scatter about the calibration. These
values are much lower than previous estimates based on the width of the broad

H β
line
Furtak et al., 2024
;
58
,
FWHM_{\mathrm{b}}(\text{H\,\textbeta})=2658_{-292}^{+351}~\kms
; the key difference
is the much narrower FWHM of the
total
broad line profile (Table
2
). Our total
FWHM_{\mathrm{b}}(\text{H\,\textalpha})
(Table
2
) is much closer to the FWHM of the narrowest of the two broad Gaussians (
FWHM_{\mathrm{b,1}}=560\pm 50~\kms
vs
FWHM_{\mathrm{b,2}}=2,000_{-100}^{+200}~\kms
; Table
1
), but this is a consequence of the flux ratio between the two components. Using instead the width of the broadest Gaussian,
2,000_{-100}^{+200}

\kms
, one would infer
\log(M_{\mathrm{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}}\textsuperscript{\hskip-3.0pt\cite[citealias]{\@@bibref{Phrase1}{reines+volonteri2015}{\@@citephrase{\al@reines+volonteri2015}}{}}}/\Msun)=7.2\pm 0.1
.
This measurement is in much better agreement with the
H β
-derived mass
(
Furtak et al., 2024
;
Ji et al., 2025
)
.

As an alternative, we use a calibration based on the line second moment,
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}
, which
we measured on the observed line profile after subtracting the continuum. We remark that
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}
is not the dispersion of the Gaussian. In fact, we find
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}=740_{-120}^{+100}

\kms
, broader than
FWHM_{\mathrm{b}}(\text{H\,\textalpha})
.
This larger value is due to a combination of model assumptions (the
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}
method does not take into
account the presence of the absorber) and to the broad wings of
H α
, which are
up-weighted when calculating the second-moment of the line profile. For reference,
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}
diverges for a Lorentzian-like line profile. From our estimate of
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}
, using the calibration of
18
, we obtain

\log(M_{\mathrm{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}}\textsuperscript{\hskip-3.0pt\cite[citealias]{\@@bibref{Phrase1}{dallabonta+2025}{\@@citephrase{\al@dallabonta+2025}}{}}}/\Msun)=6.5_{-0.2}^{+0.1}
, with 0.2 dex calibration uncertainties.

Crucially, the direct
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
measurement from
Juodžbalis et al. (2026b)
agrees best with the single-epoch virial estimate using the width of the broadest Gaussian component. Alternative estimates, such as from the
FWHM of the sum of the two Gaussians, or from the line second moment, both fall significantly shorter than the direct measurement.
The discrepancy between the dynamical estimate and the values inferred from the total profile can be understood if the narrowest of the two broad Gaussians is not related to the black-hole BLR.
Evidence for a spatially resolved nature support a different physical origin of this kinematic component
(
Juodžbalis et al., 2026b
)
.

Among the alternative models, both the Lorentzian/Voigt profile as well as the electron-scatter model (Appendix
A
) under-predict

M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
. In particular, the electron-scatter model of
Rusakov et al. (2026)
– coupled with the
108
calibration – yields a value of
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
that is 1.7 dex below the dynamical estimate – despite this model being marginally better statistically than the fiducial model (
\Delta\,\text{BIC}=5
).

To obtain the bolometric luminosity, we use the calibration of

Stern and Laor (2012)
, based on the broad
H α
,
and find
L_{\mathrm{bol}}=2.1\times 10^{44}~\text{erg\,s${}^{-1}$}
, within a factor of 2 from the value based on broad
H β
, as reported by
58
.

3.3
Absorbing gas

The gas absorber has a very high equivalent width; when measured relative to the
broad-
H α
flux, the value is
EW(\text{H\,\textalpha})=5_{-1}^{+2}
Å, while the value
measured relative to the continuum is
EW_{\mathrm{cont}}(\text{H\,\textalpha})=22_{-7}^{+12}
Å. Both values are very high, but the second value is so high
that it completely rules out a stellar origin
1
1

1

High-EW Balmer-line absorption in stellar atmospheres is routinely observed in spectral types from late B to early F, but is never as strong as seen here. For reference, for a simple stellar
population employing MIST isochrones
(
Choi et al., 2016
)
and the C3K model atmospheres

(
Conroy et al., 2019
)
, the maximum
EW(\text{H\,\textalpha})
is 8.3 Åfor a burst age of
400–500 Myr, depending on metallicity. For Abell2744-QSO1, such an old burst age is ruled
out by the amount of rest-UV light.
. The strength of the
H β
absorber appears
much larger, with an optical depth at the line centre of
\tau_{0}(\text{H\,\textbeta})=12_{-5}^{+9}
, compared to only
\tau_{0}(\text{H\,\textalpha})=1.2_{-0.3}^{+0.6}
. Since these are absorption lines arising from the same energy level, their optical depth ratios
are set by atomic physics to be
\tau_{0}(\text{H\,\textbeta})/\tau_{0}(\text{H\,\textalpha})=\lambda_{\text{H\,\textbeta}}/\lambda_{\text{H\,\textalpha}}\cdot f_{2\rightarrow 4}/f_{2\rightarrow 3}=0.137
, where we used the oscillator strength values
f_{2\rightarrow 4}=0.119
and
f_{2\rightarrow 3}=0.641
. Our results yield
\tau_{0}(\text{H\,\textbeta})/\tau_{0}(\text{H\,\textalpha})=10\pm 6
, almost 2-σaway from the theoretical value. Higher-quality observations of
H β
are needed to confirm this finding in Abell2744-QSO1, but similar results have been reported for other LRDs
(
D’Eugenio et al., 2026
;
D’Eugenio et al., 2025
)
, lending credibility to this
low-SNR result. Additionally, the
H β
and
H α
absorber require
two different velocities, with
v_{\mathrm{abs,\text{H\,\textbeta}}}-v_{\mathrm{abs,\text{H\,\textalpha}}}=90\pm 30~\kms
. Again, while this is only a 3-σresult, other LRDs have been confirmed to have different
H α
and
H β
absorber velocities
(
D’Eugenio et al., 2025
;
Lin et al., 2026
;
Ji et al., 2026
)
.
At face value, the kinematic discrepancy in the two absorbers suggests the presence of some line infill, possibly P-cygni profiles
(
Rusakov et al., 2026
;
Torralba et al., 2026
)
.
Spatially, the absorber is clearly located in the BLR, or between the BLR and the observer, as demonstrated
in Fig.
3
; there is clearly not enough continuum flux to be absorbed,
and the model is unable to reproduce the data.

Figure 3
:

The mean velocity offset of the BLR model is
v_{\mathrm{b}}=-18\pm 8~\kms
. This is only
a 2.5 σdifference, so we do not regard it as strong evidence for a
velocity offset, especially since we miss the peak of the BLR due to the absorption.
The velocity of the
H α
absorber is also very close to the systemic velocity,
v_{\mathrm{abs}}=-40\pm 10~\kms
. This weak blueshift echoes the blueshift of
the BLR, such that their difference
22\pm 13~\kms
is fully consistent with zero (within
1.7 σ), implying that the dense-gas absorber is at or very near rest-frame velocity relative to the BLR.

While a single rest-frame absorber could indicate an inflow/outflow directed close to
the plane of the sky, there is increasing evidence for near rest-frame absorbers being
common
(
Ma et al., 2026
;
D’Eugenio et al., 2026
;
D’Eugenio et al., 2025
, e.g.,)
, favouring stationary equilibrium, such as a gas disc, or at least long-lived structures, such as stalling gas clouds.
Indeed, if we adopt the column density

N_{\mathrm{H}}\sim 10^{24}~\mathrm{cm}^{-2}
estimated from the strength of the Balmer
absorption
(
58
)
, the gas absorber could be long-lived even under a high-
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
scenario, because the cross section for absorption depends on the dust fraction
(
Fabian et al., 2008
)
,
which we estimate to be low.
In fact, from the dust-to-column density ratio of the Milky Way and from the hydrogen column
density of
58
, we can derive constraints on the dust and metallicity
properties of the absorbing gas. Following the method of
D’Eugenio et al. (2024a, their eq. 1)
, we can write

Z\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{d}}=Z_{\mathrm{MW}}\cdot\xi_{d,MW}\dfrac{A_{V,\mathrm{n}}}{N_{\mathrm{H}}}\left(\dfrac{N_{\mathrm{H}}}{A_{V}}\right)_{\mathrm{MW}},

(3)

where Z is the ISM metallicity,
\xi_{\mathrm{d}}
is the dust-to-metal ratio. Adopting the Milky-Way (MW) values of the gas-to-extinction ratio

N_{\mathrm{H}}/A_{V}=(2.09\pm 0.03)\times 10^{21}~\mathrm{cm^{-2}\,mag^{-1}}

(
Zhu et al., 2017
)
,
the dust-to-metal ratio
\xi_{\mathrm{d,MW}}=0.45

(
Konstantopoulou et al., 2024
)
,
and an ISM metallicity in the solar neighbourhood
Z_{\mathrm{MW}}=0.6~\Zsun

(
Arellano-Córdova et al., 2021
)
, and using a 3-σupper limit on the dust content of
A_{V,\mathrm{n}}<1.5
mag, we infer
Z\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{d}}\lesssim 0.0008~\Zsun
.
The inequality stems from the upper limit on
A_{V,\mathrm{n}}
, but one must recall that even if dust was detected, some or even most of the narrow-line attenuation may not be associated with the absorber. At face value, such a low
Z\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{d}}
implies that the absorbing medium is extremely dust poor. This could stem in
part from a very low value of
\xi_{\mathrm{d}}
, which would be expected if the
absorber is within the dust sublimation radius from the SMBH, and in part from intrinsically low metallicity – which resonates with the emission-line analysis of
Maiolino et al. (2025b)
, who use the narrow-line ratios to estimate
Z_{\mathrm{gas}}<0.01~\Zsun
.
Whatever the reason (absence of metals or dust sublimation), our estimate of
Z\cdot\xi_{\mathrm{d}}
would lower the effective Eddington ratio for the galaxy ISM, thus increasing the lifetime of absorbing clouds
(
Fabian et al., 2008
;
Arakawa et al., 2022
)
.

4
Discussion

‘Little Red Dots’ defined as having broad permitted lines, a ‘v-shaped’ SED and very compact morphology, are a puzzling new class of AGN, unknown before
\jwst
. It is clear that these objects are preferentially found in the Universe before Cosmic Noon,
at
z>2\text{--}3

(
Ma et al., 2026
;
Lin et al., 2026
)
.

Abell2744-QSO1
(
Furtak et al., 2023
;
Furtak et al., 2024
)

was found to have one of the strongest Balmer breaks observed at high redshift
(
Labbe et al., 2024
, see also)
.
The smooth nature of the break in this galaxy has defied
any attempt to model it as a stellar Balmer break

(
Ma et al., 2025
)
. In contrast, by using the model of
Inayoshi and Maiolino (2025)
, which associates the Balmer break to dense gas absorbers around the AGN, and by introducing a large micro-turbulence parameter of
v_{\mathrm{t}}\sim 120~\kms
,
58
were able to successfully model the
shape of the continuum break.

4.1
Properties of the gas absorber

One of the key predictions of the
Inayoshi and Maiolino (2025)
model is the presence of
high-EW Balmer-line absorption. While

58
reported tentative evidence of

H β
absorption, the much higher SNR of the
H α
emission line enables us not only to confirm beyond any doubt that
Abell2744-QSO1 also has
H α
absorption (Fig.
1
), but also to study its kinematics. The EW of this absorption is too large to be of stellar origin, completely ruling out a stellar-atmosphere interpretation of the Balmer break.
The inferred broadening,
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}=110_{-10}^{+20}~\kms
,
closely matches the micro-turbulence value required to model the
continuum shape (by definition
v_{\mathrm{t}}\equiv\sqrt{2}\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}
). Such a high degree of smoothness
cannot be obtained from stellar atmospheres

(
Smith and Howarth, 1998
,
v_{\mathrm{turb}}\lesssim 15~\kms
;)
, or from stellar kinematics (given the Gaussian dependence of kinematics-driven broadening,
while turbulence-driven broadening is exponential).

Different aspects of our analysis favour a location of the absorber within or just outside the BLR. First, because the absorption is too deep to absorb only the continuum
(
Juodžbalis et al., 2024a
, in agreement with the ‘Rosetta Stone’ LRD from)
. Second, the density of the absorber is fully consistent with the high densities of BLRs
(
58
)
.

4.2
Absorber energetics

The high value of the turbulence (now coming from two independent measurements) must indicate either a transient nature,
or an adequate energy source to counteract dissipation, which for turbulent energy is of order of the crossing time

(
Mac Low, 1999
;
Mac Low and Klessen, 2004
;
Klessen and Glover, 2016
)
. Since we do not know the scale of the turbulent motions, we
can derive an upper limit to the dissipation time by using the size of the system, hence the dissipation time must be
shorter than
30~\mathrm{pc}/(110~\kms)\sim 200,000
yr.
We can obtain a crude estimate of the clouds turbulent-energy density from the usual definition of kinetic energy, as

1.4m_{\mathrm{p}}n_{\mathrm{H}}\cdot\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}^{2}
, with

n_{\mathrm{H}}=10^{8.5}\text{--}10^{10}~\mathrm{cm}^{-3}

(
58
)
. Using simple geometry, a spherical shell
of radius
R_{\mathrm{c}}
, thickness
\delta R_{\mathrm{c}}
and solid angle
\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}
has total turbulent energy

\begin{split}E_{\mathrm{c}}=6.9\times 10^{50}\left(\dfrac{\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\text{\textpi}}\right)&\left(\dfrac{n_{\mathrm{H}}}{10^{8}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}}\right)\\
&\left(\dfrac{R_{\mathrm{c}}}{1~\mathrm{pc}}\right)^{2}\left(\dfrac{\delta R_{\mathrm{c}}}{0.001~\mathrm{pc}}\right)\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}}{100~\kms}\right)^{2}~\mathrm{erg}.\end{split}

(4)

For the galacto-centric distance of the clouds
R_{\mathrm{c}}
we use
the size of the BLR; since there is not enough continuum to be absorbed (Section
3.3
), the clouds must be back-illuminated by the BLR, at least in part. We set therefore a lower limit
R_{c}=R_{\mathrm{BLR}}>2.7
pc, where the lower bound on the size of the BLR has been derived from the time delay between the continuum and emission-line variability
(
58
)
. The thickness of the clouds is not
known for the object in hand, but it has been estimated to be
\delta R_{\mathrm{c}}<10^{-3}
pc in a low-redshift LRD
(
Juodžbalis et al., 2024a
)
. With these numbers and setting
\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}=4\text{\textpi}
, the
turbulent energy of the absorbing clouds is
E_{\mathrm{c}}\sim 10^{52}\text{--}10^{53}
erg.
Under the assumption of a long-lived cloud, we can estimate the power required to maintain
the turbulence as
P_{\mathrm{c}}\sim E_{\mathrm{c}}/(\delta R_{\mathrm{c}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}})
, or

\begin{split}P_{\mathrm{c}}=2.2\times 10^{42}\left(\dfrac{\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}}{4\text{\textpi}}\right)&\left(\dfrac{n_{\mathrm{H}}}{10^{8}\mathrm{cm}^{-3}}\right)\\
&\left(\dfrac{R_{\mathrm{c}}}{1~\mathrm{pc}}\right)^{2}\left(\dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}}{100~\kms}\right)^{3}~\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}},\end{split}

(5)

which are comparable to the bolometric luminosity of the AGN,
L_{\mathrm{bol}}\sim 10^{44}~\text{erg\,s${}^{-1}$}
.

The outcome of the previous estimates depends on two very uncertain assumptions. The large-scale covering factor of the dense absorbers, and the turbulence decay time,
\delta R_{\mathrm{c}}/\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}
. Based on the fraction of LRDs with confirmed line absorption, it is reasonable to estimate
\Omega_{\mathrm{c}}/(4\text{\textpi})>0.1
, which would imply that the absorbing clouds are an important component of the energy budget in LRD AGN.
For the turbulence decay time, we have assumed the smallest physical size of the clouds. However, since
the resulting crossing time is only of order 10 yr, it is difficult to imagine these clouds being long lived, which is required to explain the relatively high fraction of LRDs with absorption. It is thus possible that the absorbing gas constitutes the limiting edge of the Strömgren sphere, and that it is therefore contiguous to higher ionization gas, perhaps the BLR itself. In this case, the decay time would be significantly longer, depending on the actual scale of the turbulence.
Alternatively, the observed
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}
may represent a velocity dispersion between different clouds, but in this case we would expect significant cloud-cloud collisions. Still, a dynamical environment, where absorbing clouds are continuously formed and destroyed on short timescales is also possible, and it is supported by observations of gas absorption in local AGN
(
Maiolino et al., 2010
, although, in the latter case, the absorption is much weaker)
.

4.3
Black hole mass and Eddington accretion rate

Using the total line width of
FWHM_{\mathrm{b}}=680_{-80}^{+70}~\kms
, the estimated
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
would be
roughly one order of magnitude lower than previous values based on
H β
.
However, this result would be in strong contradiction with
Juodžbalis et al. (2026b)
, who
measure
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
by combining dynamical modelling of the resolved narrow
H α
and spectroastrometry, finding
\log(M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}/\Msun)=7.7
.
This result is in much better agreement with the estimate from the width of the broadest

H α
component, which – applying the
108
calibration – is
7.2\pm 0.1
.

All alternative choices of model or line width yield much lower
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
values. Specifically, the
non-parametric line width calibration of
Dalla Bontà et al. (2025)
yields
\log(M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}/\Msun)=6.5
– despite this calibration having the lowest scatter. A possible explanation is that the intermediate-width component
(
Juodžbalis et al., 2026b
, which is spatially resolved;)
may not be associated with the BLR, and should be subtracted before measuring
\sigma_{\mathrm{l,b}}
.

Using a Lorentzian/Voigt profile yields similar results as using the total line width from the
sum of the two Gaussians (Appendix
A
). Similarly, the electron scattering scenario
also yields much lower
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
.

Our high
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
results in a low Eddington ratio.
With the bolometric luminosity estimated from
H α

or, equivalently, from the optical continuum,
58
, we infer a sub-Eddington accretion rate,
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=0.09
.

Our higher SNR and spectral resolution reduce the dynamical mass of the whole system to the range
\log(\mdyn/\Msun)=7.1-8.0
(depending on the calibration adopted). With these values, we
obtain a range of
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
/
\mdyn
between 0.15–1.2, indicating an overmassive black hole, and reaching the regime where the black-hole dominates the entire system
in agreement with
Ji et al., 2025
and
Juodžbalis et al., 2026b
. These values indicate a strong deviation not only from the
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
–
\mstar
relation, but even from the
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
–
\sigma
relation
(
Maiolino et al., 2024
)
,
suggesting a departure from the co-evolution path
(
58
)
, possibly associated with a black hole-first scenario.
Analysis of black-hole evolutionary tracks in scenarios where
z\sim 4
overmassive black holes grow through short phases of super-Eddington accretion
suggests that they tend to grow independently of their final host galaxy (in independent progenitor halos) down to
z\sim 8
, and start
‘co-evolving’ only thereafter
(
Trinca et al., 2024
)
. Before that, their BH to stellar mass ratio may be even more extreme than observed at
z\sim 5
, possibly matching a system as extreme as Abell2744-QSO1.

The largest uncertainty on these ratios derives from the galaxy virial calibrations, which span one dex in
\mdyn
.
In Fig.
4
we show the
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
–
\mstar
relation, where we use our most conservative
upper limit on
\mdyn
as an upper limit on
\mstar
too; despite revising
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
down, we confirm that the
SMBH in Abell2744-QSO1 makes up at least 15 percent of the stellar mass.

In Fig.
4

we compare our new measurements to local scaling relations between
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
and
\mdyn
, and confirm the
overmassive nature of the SMBH in Abell2744-QSO1.
In any case, regardless of the
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
and
\mdyn
adopted, the SMBH is a dominant component of the dynamics
of this system, with a sphere of influence of 18–45 pc, which is comparable to or even larger than
the upper limit on
\re
(
Juodžbalis et al., 2026b
)
.

The observation of turbulent gas absorbers consistent with rest-frame velocities, together with their low dust
and metallicity,
58
content and with low
\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}
ratios, suggests a scenario where accreted gas is piled up in a dense reservoir near the black hole,
giving rise to long-lived absorption
(
Fabian et al., 2008
)
.

Since dense
n=2
absorbers are rare in normal AGN
(
Maiolino et al., 2025a
)
, accretion or black-hole feedback should normally be able to
clear this gas. However, several conditions in the early Universe may favour gas accumulation.
Accreting gas at early times would naturally have low angular momentum
(
Renzini, 2025
, since low-angular momentum gas would collapse earlier and more efficiently; e.g.,)
, leading to
a high accretion rate towards the innermost regions of proto-galaxies, where massive black holes dominate the gravitational potential.
The compact nature of LRDs naturally agrees with a low-angular-momentum budget
(
Loeb, 2024
;
Pacucci and Loeb, 2025
)
, and the high black-hole to total mass fraction supports scenarios of
black-hole dominated galaxies
(
58
)
.

While radiation pressure would push the gas outwards, high gas density, metals and dust just
outside the sublimation zone would favour gas cooling.
At the same time, the very low metallicity and dust content of newly accreted gas mean that its coupling to the accretion-disc radiation is essentially driven by the Thomson cross section, not by dust absorption. This is different from
later epochs and from more evolved galaxies, where
dust in the ISM increases the coupling with radiation up to ten- or hundred-fold relative to the Thomson cross section
(
Fabian et al., 2008
)
.
If this gas is stalling due to weak radiation pressure, it would naturally have a high degree of turbulence, but negligible radial velocity, consistent with observations. Its accumulation would lead to high covering factors, also consistent
with observations.

In this scenario, the SMBH would be unable to regulate gas accretion onto the galaxy
(
Pacucci and Narayan, 2024
)
, explaining why the
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}
–
\mstar
relation does not hold for proto-galaxies at
z\gtrsim 5

(
Harikane et al., 2023
;
Maiolino et al., 2024
)
, but why it is in place at lower redshifts and for
more massive, more evolved galaxies
(
Sun et al., 2025
)
.
Indeed, at later epochs, metal dissemination from the first galaxies and higher angular momentum would lead to larger galaxies, where the impact of the central SMBH on the gravitational potential of the host galaxy becomes negligible (except for the innermost regions), but where its ability to regulate the galaxy ISM increases dramatically. This evolution would naturally explain the disappearance of LRDs at later epochs, which currently seems to largely occur by
z\sim 2

(
Juodžbalis et al., 2024a
;
Ma et al., 2026
)
, although a few rare cases have been discovered at
z=0.1\text{--}0.2

(
Lin et al., 2026
;
Ji et al., 2026
)
.

5
Conclusions

In this work, we present
R=3,700

\jwst
NIRSpec/IFS aperture spectroscopy of the
H α
line in the broad-line AGN Abell2744-QSO1 at
z=7.04
. Leveraging the higher SNR of these data, we find

•

The
H α
emission line consists of a narrow-line component (6-σdetection)
and a broad-line component. The latter has distinctively non-Gaussian line profile with observed

FWHM=680_{-80}^{+70}

\kms
.

•

From the Balmer decrement of the narrow lines, we infer
A_{V}=-0.2\pm 0.5
mag, consistent with no dust. Interpreting
the narrow
H α
line as solely due to star formation, we infer a de-lensed SFR of
0.7_{-0.3}^{+0.4}~\Msun~\peryr
.

•

Our revised SMBH mass is
\log(M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}/\Msun)=7.2
, derived from the width of the broadest Gaussian and from the
108
calibration.

•

Assuming local calibrations for the bolometric AGN luminosity, we infer Eddington ratios

\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}}=0.09
.

•

The narrow line has an intrinsic
\sigma_{\mathrm{n}}=22_{-6}^{+5}~\kms
. The resulting
\mdyn
is
\log(\mdyn/\Msun)=7.1\text{--}8.0
, implying very high
M_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{0.85}{$\bullet$}}}}}/\mdyn=0.15\text{--}1.2
.

•

Broad
H α
is subject to foreground hydrogen Balmer-line absorption with high EW and large
broadening
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}=110_{-10}^{+20}

\kms
, which rules out a stellar origin. This detection confirms
the predictions of
Inayoshi and Maiolino (2025)
and
58
of a link between
AGNs with Balmer breaks and Balmer-line absorption. Our
\sigma_{\mathrm{abs}}
is consistent with the
micro-turbulence inferred from independent modelling of the Balmer break by
58
.

•

The
H α
absorber has low line-of-sight velocity, consistent with the velocity of the broad
H α
emission line
and is very close to the velocity of the narrow lines, implying that the absorbing
gas cannot be interpreted as an inflow or outflow.

•

Comparing the column density and ISM attenuation of the galaxy, we infer that the

n=2
absorber is dust poor.

Our findings confirm the overmassive nature of the SMBHs in low-luminosity, ‘LRD’ AGNs. The
overmassive nature of Abell2744-QSO1 suggests intriguing possibilities regarding the formation of early
SMBHs, such as SMBHs dominating the gravitational potential, and large supply of cosmic gas that can be efficiently fuelled onto SMBHs leading to rapid growth.

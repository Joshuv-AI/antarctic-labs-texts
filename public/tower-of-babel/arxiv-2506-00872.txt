Homogenization of parabolic problems for non-local convolution type operators under non-diffusive scaling of coefficients.
A. Piatnitski, E. Zhizhina

Abstract
We study homogenization problem for non-autonomous parabolic equations of the form ∂ t u = L ⁡ ( t ) ​ u \partial_{t}u=L(t)u with an integral convolution type operator L ⁡ ( t ) L(t) that has a non-symmetric jump kernel which is periodic in spatial variables and in time. It is assumed that the space-time scaling of the environment is not diffusive. We show that asymptotically the spatial and temporal evolutions of the solutions are getting decoupled, and the homogenization result holds in a moving frame.

1
Introduction and previous results

This paper deals with homogenization problem for a parabolic type equation of the form

\frac{\partial u^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}\ =\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}a\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\ \mu\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon};\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big)\ (u^{\varepsilon}(y,t)-u^{\varepsilon}(x,t))dy,

(1)

here
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter, and
0<\alpha<2
. We assume that
a(z)
is a non-negative,
integrable function that has a finite second moment, while the coefficient
\mu(\xi,\eta;s)
is periodic both in spatial variables
\xi
and
\eta
and in time
s
. It is also assumed that
\mu(\cdot)
is bounded and positive definite.
It should be emphasized that we do not impose symmetry conditions in
\xi
and
\eta
on
\mu(\xi,\eta,s)
and
a(\xi-\eta)
.

Our goal is to study the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to the Cauchy problem for equation (
1
) with the initial
condition

u^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
It turns out that asymptotically, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the spatial evolution of solutions to the said Cauchy problem
and their temporal evolution are getting decoupled. More precisely, we show that there exists a function
b^{\varepsilon}(t)
with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that in the moving frame

(x,t)\,\to\,\Big(x-b^{\varepsilon}(t),\,t\Big)=:({x}^{\varepsilon},t),

the solution
u^{\varepsilon}({x}^{\varepsilon},t)
converges as
\varepsilon\to 0
to a solution of the Cauchy problem
for a heat equation:

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\ =\ \mathrm{div}\big(\Theta\nabla u\big),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

(3)

with a constant positive definite matrix
\Theta
.

The function
b^{\varepsilon}(t)
is of particular interest. If the coefficient
\mu(\xi,\eta,s)
is sufficiently regular in
s
,
then

b^{\varepsilon}(t)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k(\alpha)}\varepsilon^{-1+j(2-\alpha)}b_{j}t+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}B_{0}(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}),

(4)

where
k(\alpha)=\left[\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\right]
. Here
b_{0},\,b_{1},\ldots
are vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

B_{0}(s)
is a
1
-periodic continuously differentiable vector-function, and
[\cdot]
stands for the integer part.

In the existing literature there is a number of homogenization results obtained in moving coordinates.
We quote here the works
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
, where non-autonomous parabolic problems for second order elliptic differential operators
with large low order terms were investigated in periodic media.
In
[
3

it was proved that, under the diffusive scaling, the homogenization result holds in rapidly moving coordinates and, in the presence of zero order term, homogenization takes place after factorization of solutions with the ground state of the corresponding periodic cell problem.

The paper
[
4
]
focuses on homogenization problems for convection-diffusion operators of the form

L_{\varepsilon}u(x,t)=\Delta u(x,t)+b\big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{p}},\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\big)\nabla u(x,t)
with

being periodic both in spatial variables and time, and

, that is the scaling is not
diffusive. The authors considers the case of a potential vector field

:
b(\xi,s)=\nabla_{\xi}U(\xi,s)
with
periodic in
\xi
and
s
function

and proved by the probabilistic methods that the homogenization result holds in moving coordinates.

In the work
[
12
]
, Sections 12, 13, the authors consider convection-diffusion operators in periodic media.
They show that for the corresponding semigroups the homogenization result holds in moving coordinates and
obtain estimates for the rate of convergence in operator norms.

The case of non-stationary convection-diffusion equations with a periodic microstructure whose characteristics are
rapidly oscillating random stationary functions of time is addressed in
[
6
]
.

Recent years there is a growing interest in nonlocal convolution type equations in inhomogeneous environments.
The equations of this type appear in various problems of population biology, material sciences and porous media.
When modeling nonlocal processes in environments with a microstructure, we face the problem of a macroscopic
description of such processes. This leads to homogenization problems for the corresponding equations.

Homogenization problems for parabolic convolution-type equations in periodic environments are considered in

[
8
]
and
[
10
]
.

The paper
[
8
]
deals with the Cauchy problems for an equation of the form (
1
) with the initial condition

u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the assumption that

\mu(x,y)
is periodic in
x
and

, does not depend on time and is not necessary symmetric.
Under natural coerciveness and moment conditions, it is shown that the said Cauchy problem admits homogenization in moving coordinates:

u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=u^{0}\Big(x-\frac{b}{\varepsilon}t,t\Big)+R^{\varepsilon}(x,t),\quad\hbox{where }\ \lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|R^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=0,

and
u^{0}(x,t)
is a solution of (
3
)
with the same initial condition
u_{0}
.

Notice that in the cited articles the evolution of the moving frame is linear in time.

If the coefficients
\mu(\xi,\eta,s)
and
a(\xi-\eta)
in (
1
) are symmetric in
\xi
and
\eta
, and the scaling is diffusive that is
\alpha=2
,
then, as was shown in the recent work
[
10

, a solution of (
1
)–(
2

a solution of homogenized problem (
3
) with a positive definite matrix
\Theta
. In this case
the usual homogenization result holds.

It should be emphasized that in this case the equation for the first corrector is parabolic, it reads

\partial_{s}\chi(\xi,s)-\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}a(\xi-\eta)\mu(\xi,\eta,s)\big(\chi(\xi,s)-\chi(\eta,s)\big)d\eta=\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}a(\xi-\eta)\mu(\xi,\eta,s)(\xi-\eta)d\eta.

According to
[
10
]
this equation has a unique up to an additive constant periodic in
s
solution. The function
\chi

is then used for constructing the asymptotic expansion of a solution of the original equation and, after substitution in the equation, allows to eliminate all the growing terms. Thus, we need only one first order corrector and one second order corrector in the asymptotic expansion.

Homogenization with non-diffusive scaling and nonlocal effects extends classical theory to systems where microscopic
interactions are long-ranged and scaling laws deviate from diffusive regimes.

In the present paper we consider nonlocal convolution type equations of the form (
1
) in the case of non-diffusive scaling when the oscillation in time is somehow slower than that in spatial variables. Also we do not assume the symmetry of the coefficient.
In this case, the homogenization procedure has two interesting features.

Firstly, the homogenization result holds in a moving frame whose evolution is not linear in time,
it is the sum of linear and periodic functions.

Secondly, due to non-diffusive scaling in (
1
), the auxiliary problem for the corrector is not parabolic
any more. Instead, we freeze the value of time and solve the corresponding auxiliary elliptic equation for each value of time,
see equation (
23
) below. This gives us the first corrector. However, this corrector is not sufficient for eliminating all the growing terms of the discrepancy. In order to fix this problem, we construct a finite series of first order correctors, the number of terms in this series depends on
\alpha
, it is growing as
\alpha
approaches
2
. Unfortunately, this construction requires
some regularity of the coefficient
\mu(\xi,\eta,s)
with respect to the variable
s
. The asymptotic behaviour of solutions
for non-regular
\mu
is an open problem.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we provide the conditions on the coefficients
of the operator
A(s)
and formulate our main results.

Section
3
deals with auxiliary cell problems and correctors. First we introduce a formal asymptotic
expansion of the solution, substitute this expansion in the equation and collect power-like terms in the resulting
relation. This allows us to define auxiliary problems and to construct correctors and a moving frame.

In Section
4
we obtain a priori estimates for the solutions of the original problems and prove the main results.

2
Problem setup and main results

The present work deals with homogenization of Cauchy problem for non-autonomous parabolic convolution-type
equations in periodic in space and time media under a non-diffusive scaling of the coefficients. This equation reads

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon}u(x,t):=\partial_{t}u(x,t)-L^{\varepsilon}(t)u(x,t)=0,\quad\mbox{where}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle(L^{\varepsilon}(t)u)(x,t)\ =\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\big)\mu\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon};\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\big)(u(y,t)-u(x,t))dy.\end{array}

(5)

Here
0<\alpha<2
, and
a(z)
satisfies the following conditions:

a(z)\geqslant 0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(z)dz=1,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}a(z)dz<\infty.

(6)

We assume that
\mu(x,y;t)
is periodic in each of the variable
x
,
y
with period
[0,1)^{d}
and in
t
with period
1
, and that
there exist positive constants
\mu_{-}
and
\mu_{+}
such that

0<\mu_{-}\leq\mu(x,y;t)\leq\mu_{+}\quad\mbox{ for all }\;x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

Moreover, we assume that
\mu(x,y;t)
is a
k+1
times differentiable in the variable
t
function:

\mu(x,y;t)\in C^{k+1}(\mathbb{T}^{1};L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2d}))

(8)

with
k=k(\alpha)=\big[\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\big]
.

Denote by
u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)
a solution of the Cauchy problem

\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)=L^{\varepsilon}(t)u^{\varepsilon}(x,t),\quad u^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(9)

t\in[0,T]
, where the operator
L^{\varepsilon}
is defined in (
5
).
Since by the Schur lemma the norm of the operator
L^{\varepsilon}(t)
satisfies the estimate

\|L^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{-2}\mu_{+}\|a\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
,
see
[
5
, Theorem 5.2]
, then for each
\varepsilon>0
problem (
9
) has a unique solution
u^{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
The main result of the present work is the following statement:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a(z)
satisfy all the conditions in (
6
),
and assume that
\mu(x,y,t)
is periodic in each of the variables
x
,
y
with period
[0,1)^{d}
and in
t

with period
1
. Assume moreover that
\mu(x,y,t)

satisfies conditions (
7
), (
8
).
Then there exist a positive definite symmetric matrix
\Theta
and a family of functions

b^{\varepsilon}(t)\in C([0,+\infty);\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see
Sections
3.1
and
4.1
for their definitions) such that for any
T>0

\|u^{\varepsilon}(x+b^{\varepsilon}(t),\,t)-u^{0}(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T),\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as }\;\varepsilon\to 0,

(10)

where
u^{0}(x,t)
is a solution of the Cauchy problem

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\mathrm{div}\big(\Theta\nabla u\big),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad t\in[0,T].

(11)

The profile of the function
b^{\varepsilon}(t)
depends on whether
0<\alpha<1
, or
\alpha=1
, or
1<\alpha<2
.
If
0<\alpha<1
, then

b^{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{b_{0}}{\varepsilon}t+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}B_{0}\Big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big),

where
b_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a constant vector, and
B_{0}(s)
is a continuously differentiable
1
-periodic vector-function.

If
1<\alpha<2
, then

b^{\varepsilon}(t)=\varepsilon^{-1}{b_{0}}t+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon^{(-1+j(2-\alpha))}b_{j}t,

where
k=\Big[\frac{1}{2-\alpha}\Big]
, and
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,k
.

If
\alpha=1
, then

b^{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{b_{0}}{\varepsilon}t+b_{1}t+B_{0}\Big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\Big).

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle L^{\varepsilon}(t)^{\star}v(x,t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\Big(\frac{y-x}{\varepsilon}\Big)\mu\Big(\frac{y}{\varepsilon},\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big)v(y,t)dy\\[14.22636pt]
\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\mu\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon},\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big)dy\bigg)v(x,t)\end{array}

(55)

Lemma 4.3
.

The function
q^{\varepsilon}
satisfies the estimate

0<\pi_{3}\leqslant q^{\varepsilon}(\xi,s)\leqslant\pi_{4}<\infty

(56)

with the constants
\pi_{3}
and
\pi_{4}
that do not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

The proof of this Lemma follows the line of the proof of Lemma 3.4 in
[
11
]
.
∎

Multiplying the equation in (
51
) by
\big(q^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}})\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,t)\big)
and integrating the resulting relation over

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,t)
,
t\leqslant T
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\!\int\limits_{0}^{t}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!{q}^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)\partial_{s}((\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,s))^{2})dxds-\!\!\int\limits_{0}^{t}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\!{q}^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)\Xi(x,s)L^{\varepsilon}\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,s)dxds\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{q}^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)f(x,s)\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,s)dxds.\end{array}

(57)

Considering equation (
54
)
and making straightforward rearrangements, we arrive at the following relation, see also the proof of
[
8
, Proposition 6.1]
:

\begin{array}[]{c}\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{q}^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)(\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,t))^{2}dx\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\mu\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon},\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big){q}^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)(\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,s)-\Xi^{\varepsilon}(y,s))^{2}dxds\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{q}^{\varepsilon}(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},0)(g_{0}(x))^{2}dx+2\int\limits_{0}^{t}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{q}^{\varepsilon}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)\Xi^{\varepsilon}(x,s)dxds.\end{array}

Sinse
q^{\varepsilon}
satisfies estimate (

), the desired upper bound can be derived from the latter relation by the Gronwall theorem.

4.4
Convergence results

We begin this section by estimating the difference
v^{\varepsilon}=w^{\varepsilon}-u^{\varepsilon}
.
According to (
49
) and Lemma
4.2
, both

\|\phi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
and
\|\psi^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
tend to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Applying Proposition
4.1
to problem
(
48
) we conclude that

\|v^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\|w^{\varepsilon}-u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\;\varepsilon\to 0.

(58)

On the other hand, considering the structure of the ansatz
w^{\varepsilon}
in (
46
) one can easily show that

\|w^{\varepsilon}(x,t)-\rho^{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon},t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\;\varepsilon\to 0.

(59)

Combining the last two limit relations yields

\|u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)-\rho^{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon},t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\;\varepsilon\to 0.

(60)

Let
u^{0}
be a solution to problem (
3
) with
\Theta
defined in (
44
).
Our next goal is to show that the solution
\rho^{\varepsilon}
of problem (
45
) converges to
u^{0}

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the family
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}(x,t)\}
converges to
u^{0}(x,t)
in
C((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Proof.

Differentiating equation (
45
) in spatial variables
it is straightforward to obtain the following estimates for
\rho^{\varepsilon}
:

\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\|\partial_{t}\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leqslant C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(61)

here the constant
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
. Denote
B_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|x|\leqslant R\}
.
Since for any
R>0
the space
W^{1,\infty}((0,T);H^{1}(B_{R}))
is compactly imbedded to
C((0,T);L^{2}(B_{R})
, estimate (
61
) implies that for any
R>0

the family
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}\}
is compact in
C((0,T);L^{2}(B_{R}))
. According to
[
1
]
the fundamental solution of Cauchy problem (
45
)
satisfies the following Aronson upper bound

Q_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\leqslant C_{1}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\Big(-c_{2}\frac{|x|^{2}}{t}\Big).

with the constants
C_{1}
and
c_{2}
that only depend on the ellipticity constants of the matrix
\theta
.
Therefore,
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{C((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{R}))}
tends to zero as
R\to\infty
uniformly in
\varepsilon
.
Combining this estimate with the above compactness result
we conclude that
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}\}
is compact in
C((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
For a converging subsequence of
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}\}
denote its limit by
\rho^{0}
. Let
\psi(x,t)
be a

C^{\infty}((0,T);C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
function such that
\psi(x,T)=0
. The integral identity of problem (
45
) reads

-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}\psi\,dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u_{0}\psi(\cdot,0)\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{\varepsilon}\theta\Big(\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\Big):\nabla\nabla\psi\,dxdt

Passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0

in this integral identity we obtain the integral identity
of problem (
3
). Therefore,
\rho^{0}=u^{0}
and the whole family
\{\rho^{\varepsilon}\}
converges to
u^{0}
:

\|u^{0}-\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\;\varepsilon\to 0.

(62)

∎

Since
\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u^{0}(x,t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(x^{\varepsilon},t)-u^{0}(x^{\varepsilon},t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
, then (
58
) - (
62
) yield the convergence

\|u^{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u^{0}(x^{\varepsilon},t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }\;\varepsilon\to 0,

(63)

where
x^{\varepsilon}
is defined by formula (
13
).

Thus, we have proved convergence (
10
) for any

u_{0}\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Approximating any
L^{2}
initial function by a sequence of
{\cal S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions and taking into account the a priori estimates obtained in Proposition
4.1

and similar estimates for the limit problem in (
3
), we derive the statement of Theorem
2.1
.

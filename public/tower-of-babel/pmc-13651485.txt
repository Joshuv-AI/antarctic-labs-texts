EQ-5D health utility gains with once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg in people with MASH and F2/F3 fibrosis: An analysis of ESSENCE
Authors: Margarida Augusto, Robert Bauer, Simon Clancy, Aslak Sindbjerg Poulsen, Fotis Tefos, Michelle Long
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background & AimsMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) is the progressive form of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease. It is highly prevalent, particularly among individuals with overweight or obesity, and substantially impairs health-related quality of life. Semaglutide 2.4 mg received accelerated approval from the US FDA in August 2025 for adults with noncirrhotic MASH and fibrosis stages F2–F3 based on results from the ESSENCE trial. This study aimed to estimate the impact of semaglutide on health utility in this population.MethodsThis exploratory analysis used Week 72 data from ESSENCE Part 1, a Phase III, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH and fibrosis stage F2 or F3 were randomized 2:1 to once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo plus standard of care. EuroQol 5-‍Dimension (EQ-5D) utilities were mapped from Short Form-36 (SF-36) scores using the Rowen et al. (2009) algorithm. Change from baseline was analyzed with treatment, baseline diabetes status, and fibrosis stages as covariates.ResultsA total of 800 participants were included in this analysis (semaglutide 2.4 mg n = 534; placebo n = 266). Mean age was 56.0 years; 57.1% were female; mean body mass index was 34.6 kg/m2 and 55.9% had type 2 diabetes. Baseline mapped EQ-5D utility was approximately 0.78 in both groups. At Week 72, semaglutide significantly improved mapped EQ-5D utility vs. placebo (estimated treatment difference 0.03; 95% CI 0.01–0.06; nominal p = 0.0015), with consistent effects across fibrosis stages.ConclusionsIn adults with MASH and F2 or F3 fibrosis, once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg was associated with statistically significant improvements in mapped EQ-5D health utility vs. placebo. These exploratory findings provide utility estimates that may help to inform cost-effectiveness evaluations and health technology assessments.Impact and implicationsThis study provides the first estimates of EuroQol 5-Dimension (EQ-5D) health utility gains associated with once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg in people with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) and F2/F3 fibrosis. The findings are important for payers and clinicians because they demonstrate a statistically significant improvement in mapped EQ-5D health utility with semaglutide 2.4 mg over 72 weeks. These results can directly inform cost-effectiveness models and support healthcare decision-making by quantifying the health-related quality of life benefits of semaglutide using a widely accepted preference-based metric. Given the use of mapped rather than directly measured utilities and the UK-specific tariff, the results should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH) is the progressive form of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), characterized by hepatic steatosis, hepatocellular injury, and inflammation that can progress to fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma.1 The global prevalence of MASLD and MASH is high,2 particularly among people with overweight or obesity,3 and is projected to increase.2 Alongside its clinical manifestations, MASLD/MASH is associated with impairment of health-related quality of life (HRQoL).2

In the US, the only treatments approved for adults with noncirrhotic MASH with moderate to advanced fibrosis, consistent with stages F2–F3, are resmetirom, a thyroid hormone receptor-β agonist, and semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist.1 Semaglutide acts on the metabolic environment, reducing the influx of fatty acids and carbohydrates to the liver and supporting hepatic autoregulation, as well as promoting weight loss through delayed gastric emptying and appetite suppression.1 Semaglutide 2.4 mg received accelerated approval by the US FDA in August 20254 based on the results of ESSENCE Part 1, an ongoing, Phase III, randomized, double-blind, multinational trial comparing once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg plus standard of care with placebo plus standard of care in adults with MASH and F2 or F3 fibrosis.5

Materials and methods

We performed an exploratory analysis of the effect of once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg vs. placebo on health utility among patients with MASH and F2 or F3 fibrosis. EuroQol 5-Dimension (EQ-5D) utilities, based on a UK population and anchored on a scale where 1 represents full health and 0 represents a state equivalent to death, were mapped from Short Form-36 (SF-36) domain scores, collected in the ESSENCE trial.5 ESSENCE is an ongoing, Phase III, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.5 Eligible adult patients (≥18 years of age) had histologically documented steatohepatitis, liver fibrosis stage 2 or 3, and a NAFLD activity score (NAS) of 4 or more.5 Patients were randomized 2:1 to receive once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg or placebo in addition to standard of care for MASH for 240 weeks.5 Randomization was stratified according to the presence or absence of type 2 diabetes at screening, fibrosis stage, and region.5 The co-primary endpoints of part 1 of the trial were a resolution of steatohepatitis with no worsening of liver fibrosis and a reduction in liver fibrosis with no worsening of steatohepatitis; the results of all protocol-specified outcomes have been reported previously.5 Data from a planned interim analysis at Week 72 were used in this analysis. The established Rowen et al. (2009) algorithm (Model 3 described in the paper)6 was used to map the SF-36 scores from ESSENCE to EQ-5D utility index values using UK population preferences. EQ-5D was not collected directly in ESSENCE; therefore, individual EQ-5D dimensions were not evaluated. The algorithm applies a random-effects generalized least squares model incorporating SF-36 domains, squared terms, and interaction effects, developed using inpatient data from the Health Outcomes Data Repository (>23,100 respondents). The Rowen et al. Model 3 algorithm was selected because it is an established SF-36-to-EQ-5D mapping approach and uses patient-level SF-36 data, which were available in ESSENCE. Change from baseline in mapped EQ-5D utility was analysed using an ANCOVA model. Intercurrent events of premature treatment discontinuation and initiation of ancillary therapy possibly modifying MASH were handled based on the treatment-policy estimand strategy.7 The ANCOVA model included treatment, baseline diabetes status, baseline fibrosis stage, and the diabetes-by-fibrosis interaction as fixed effects, with baseline body weight and baseline EQ-5D utility as covariates. Additional subgroup analyses were performed by baseline fibrosis stage, baseline BMI category (<27 kg/m2, 27 to <30 kg/m2, 30 to <35 kg/m2, and ≥35 kg/m2), and type 2 diabetes status at baseline. Missing Week 72 values were imputed using multiple imputation under an unconditional reference assumption; that is, participants with missing Week 72 data were assumed to follow the outcome pattern observed in the placebo group, with values imputed using the same ANCOVA model but excluding treatment as a factor. Each of the complete imputed datasets was modelled separately. Estimates and standard errors were then pooled using Rubin's rules to obtain single point estimates, CIs, and nominal p values.

At Week 72, once-weekly treatment with semaglutide 2.4 mg significantly improved mapped EQ-5D utility compared with placebo in people with MASH (estimated treatment difference [ETD]: 0.03; 95% CI 0.01–0.06; nominal p value = 0.0015) (Table 1). Mapped EQ-5D utility values at baseline and Week 72 were also stratified by baseline fibrosis stage, BMI category, and type 2 diabetes status (Table 1). In subgroup analyses based on ANCOVA models, the ETD at Week 72 was 0.03 (95% CI −0.01 to 0.06; nominal p = 0.1868) in participants with F2 fibrosis and 0.04 (95% CI 0.01–0.07; nominal p = 0.0026) in participants with F3 fibrosis. Across BMI categories, ETDs ranged from 0.00 to 0.06, with the largest observed ETD in participants with BMI ≥35 kg/m2 (0.06; 95% CI 0.02–0.10; nominal p = 0.0016). ETDs were 0.04 (95% CI 0.01–0.07; nominal p = 0.0106) in participants with type 2 diabetes and 0.03 (95% CI 0.00–0.06; nominal p = 0.0606) in participants without type 2 diabetes. As this was an exploratory analysis, no adjustment for multiplicity was applied; therefore, subgroup findings are interpreted descriptively.

Discussion

In this exploratory analysis of ESSENCE Part 1, treatment with once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg was associated with a statistically significant improvement in mapped EQ-5D health utility compared with placebo among people with MASH and F2 or F3 fibrosis. Findings were generally consistent across exploratory subgroups, including baseline type 2 diabetes status and BMI category. These findings provide the first estimates of utility scores for semaglutide 2.4 mg in this population and may help to inform economic evaluations and health technology assessments. Health utilities are critical inputs for cost-effectiveness models;10 the results of this study may therefore assist in determining if treatment of MASH with F2 or F3 fibrosis with once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide 2.4 mg could be cost-effective for healthcare providers. Health utilities are valued by payers and health technology assessment agencies, since they directly reflect the experiences and perspectives of the people affected.

These findings add to the limited literature describing utility values and HRQoL outcomes in people with MASH. An alternative cross-walk approach has recently been described using real-world data from patients with MASH in the US, in which Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire for Non-alcoholic Steatohepatitis (CLDQ-NASH) scores were mapped to EQ-5D utilities using generalized linear and fractional logistic models.11 The analysis demonstrated the feasibility of predicting EQ-5D-5L utilities from a disease-specific instrument and highlighted potential differences in model performance across clinical subgroups (e.g. obesity and type 2 diabetes status).11 The ESSENCE Part 1 trial did not include the CLDQ-NASH; therefore, application of this disease-specific cross-walk was not possible in the present study. Instead, we used the established mapping algorithm developed by Rowen et al. that links the generic SF-36 to EQ-5D utilities.6 A key strength of this approach is that it is based on the widely validated and internationally recognized SF-36, enabling comparability across therapeutic areas and disease indications. In contrast to disease-specific instruments such as the CLDQ-NASH, generic preference-based measures and their associated mapping functions are commonly used in health technology assessments and economic evaluations, which supports the relevance of our approach for cost-effectiveness modelling.

A recent study of 883 patients with noncirrhotic MASH estimated baseline EQ-5D scores using both Chronic Liver Disease Questionnaire for Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (CLDQ-NAFLD) and SF-36 instruments, with mean EQ-5D reported values of 0.851 and 0.853, respectively, and high correlation between the methods.9 While the absolute utilities estimated from ESSENCE Part 1 in this study were lower, this may be due to differences in mapping algorithms, population characteristics, or trial vs. real-world settings. When considered alongside age-adjusted UK population norms,12 the ESSENCE baseline utilities appear more consistent with the expected HRQoL decrement associated with MASH and F2/F3 fibrosis than the higher utility values reported previously.8,9 Furthermore, the consistency across instruments underscores the robustness of utility estimates from both generic and disease-specific HRQoL measures, reinforcing their suitability for cost-effectiveness evaluations.

Finally, baseline utility estimates in the present analysis were comparable between patients with MASH and F2 vs. F3 fibrosis. Similar results have been observed in previous studies.8,9

The methodology used in the present study is well-established;13 however, several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. EQ-5D was not directly collected in ESSENCE; therefore, the values reported here should be interpreted as mapped EQ-5D utilities derived from SF-36 responses rather than observed EQ-5D patient-reported outcomes. The UK-derived mapping function has been shown to slightly overestimate utility in more severe health states and to underestimate improvements in certain SF-36 domains, including vitality, role-physical, or role-emotional scores.6 In addition, the SF-36 to EQ-5D mapping function was derived using UK hospital data,6 which may introduce population bias in a global cohort such as that included in the ESSENCE trial, which was conducted across 37 countries. The use of UK preference weights means that these utility values reflect UK population preferences and may differ if country-specific value sets are applied, limiting generalizability outside the UK. Our analysis was based on SF-36 data from the ESSENCE Part 1 trial, which were collected in a controlled trial setting for selected patient populations.5 Future real-world studies may help validate the generalizability of these findings outside the clinical trial setting. The lower baseline utility than previously reported8 may indicate that participants in the ESSENCE Part 1 trial had a greater burden of disease or poorer overall health status at enrolment.

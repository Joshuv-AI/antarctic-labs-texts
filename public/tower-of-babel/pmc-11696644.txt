Integration of Ayurvedic and Allopathic treatment in hereditary breast and ovarian cancer patient with Germline BRCA1 mutation for long term disease free survival: A case report
Authors: Sadanand Sardeshmukh, Vineeta Deshmukh, Arvind Kulkarni, Shweta Gujar, Vinita Awalkanthe, Nilambari Sardeshmukh, Bhagyashree Sardeshmukh, Dhananjay Deshpande, Anjali Deshpande, Sandeep Chavan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractOvarian cancer patients with BRCA1 mutation have more susceptibility for secondary breast cancer. In females with BRCA1 mutation, the risk of developing breast carcinoma is 65% and of ovarian cancer is 39%, before 70 years of age.This is a case report of a 74 year old, post-menopausal woman diagnosed with metastatic retroperitoneal lymph node, high-grade papillary adenocarcinoma primary ovary stage IIIA in April 2004 at the age of 48 years. She underwent 3 cycles of neo-adjuvant chemotherapy Inj. Methotrexate and Inj. Carboplatin from June to August 2004 followed by optimum cytoreduction in September 2004. Later she completed 3 more cycles of chemotherapy of the same protocol from October to November 2004. Tab Etoposide was given from December 2004 to October 2006. In May 2006, during oral chemotherapy and with unremarkable radiological findings, the patient chose Ayurvedic treatment in view of immune boosting, and improving quality of life. The patient underwent 11 sets of Panchakarma treatment, almost every year, from December 2007 to September 2019. She was disease-free for 13 years leading a good quality of life with adjunct Ayurvedic treatment.In October 2019, she was diagnosed with Left breast duct carcinoma with ER, PR hormone positive status. Her genetic mutation analysis report at that time revealed BRCA 1 mutation. She underwent Left Modified Radical Mastectomy in October 2019, followed by prophylactic Right Breast Mastectomy and oral hormonal therapy. Now she is living with better quality of life with adjunct Ayurvedic treatment, including Oral Ayurvedic Medicines possessing Rasayana (immunomodulatory) and hepato-protective activity and 12 sets of Panchakarma Chikitsa.In this case of Stage IIIA Ovarian carcinoma and second primary Breast carcinoma with BRCA 1 genetic mutation (HBOC syndrome), a long-term 13 years of disease-free survival, and 20 years of overall survival is achieved with the integration of Ayurvedic treatment and conventional cancer treatment.Keywords: Ayurveda, BRCA1 mutation, HBOC syndrome, Integrated treatment, Long term survival, Panchakarma treatment, Prognosis

Introduction

Ovarian cancer is the second most common type of gynecological cancer in women in Asian and Western countries [1]. It is mostly diagnosed at a late stage, leads to poor prognosis and placed fifth in cancer death among women [2]. About 70% of patients with this disease have cancer progression or recurrence with a median interval of 18–24 months for first recurrence [1]. As per studies, the 10-year relative survival for stage III ovarian cancer is only 36% [3]. Also, Breast cancer is the most commonly found malignancy in women as per Globocon data 2020, with a cumulative risk of 2.81% [4]. According to the studies, breast and ovarian cancer have more susceptibility to BRCA1/2 gene mutations [5]. National Cancer Institute has defined BRCA1 hereditary breast and ovarian cancer syndrome (HBOC) as “an inherited condition characterized by an increased risk of developing breast cancer by 57–60%, of ovarian cancer by 40–59% and that of 83% risk of developing contralateral breast cancer by age 70”. BRCA1 HBOC may also have an increased risk for cancers of the cervix, uterus, pancreas, oesophagus, stomach, fallopian tube, and primary peritoneum [6].

We report here a genetically predisposed rare case of BRCA1 HBOC with first-degree family history of cancer, treated with adjunct Ayurvedic treatment, after completing conventional treatment of the first malignancy. Ayurvedic treatment consisted of Shaman Chikitsa (treatment pacifying Dosha and Dhatu), Rasayan Chikitsa (immunomodulatory), and manifold Panchakarma Chikitsa (detoxification of body) ensuing in long-term Disease-Free Survival (DFS) of 13 years and Overall Survival (OS) of 20 years with good quality of life.

Patient information

This case is 74 years old, married, post-menopausal woman diagnosed with Ovarian cancer in 2004 at the age of 48 years and Breast malignancy with BRCA1 gene mutation in 2019, presently asymptomatic. She was addicted to Masheri (a pyrolyzed smokeless tobacco) for more than 20 years and had a history of dysmenorrhea during her reproductive age. She had an infection of Herpes Zoster in 1998. She suffered from familial mental stress. Recently, in 2017, her sister's daughter was diagnosed with breast cancer at the age of 51 years.

Clinical findings

In April 2004, the patient consulted a local physician for complaints of severe backache, generalized weakness, and low-degree fever since 2–3 months. Radiological investigations revealed multiple large retroperitoneal and iliac lymph nodes. Retroperitoneal lymph node biopsy (April 20, 2004) presented metastasis of papillary adenocarcinoma with the primary ovarian origin.

Timeline for health events and treatment

MRI lumbar spine dated April 09, 2004 showed early degenerative changes with a disc bulge in the lumbar spine along with multiple large retroperitoneal and iliac lymph nodes and suspicious asymmetrical hypo intense lesion in the left breast. However, her bilateral breast mammography (May 26, 2004) reported Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BIRADS) I and CA125 level was 1164.35 U/ml (June 12, 2004). She took three cycles of neo-adjuvant chemotherapy of Inj. Methotrexate and Inj. Carboplatin from June to August 2004 followed by total abdominal hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with omentectomy and Bilateral Pelvic Lymphadenectomy + Retroperitoneal Lymphadenectomy (optimum cytoreduction) in September 2004. The surgical histopathology report confirmed high-grade serous adenocarcinoma of the ovary with spread onto the serosa with stage IIIA as per the 8th edition of American Joint Committee on Cancer guidelines. Later, the patient completed three more cycles of chemotherapy of the same protocol from October to November 2004. Then, the patient was put on Tab. Etoposide from December 2004 to October 2006. Post-chemotherapy, CT scan abdomen and pelvis dated December 31, 2004 showed regression in the lymphadenopathy.

The patient was disease free till September 2019. In October 2019, a small lump in left breast was noticed. Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology showed Infiltrating Duct Carcinoma (IDC). Further, she was operated for Left Radical Mastectomy with a sentinel node biopsy on October 15, 2019. The surgical histopathology report reconfirmed the diagnosis of IDC, grade II, and stage IA with a hormonal status of ER and PR positive and Her2 negative. With past history of ovarian cancer, genetic mutation analysis was done and she was detected with BRCA1 gene mutation on December 05, 2019. She underwent Prophylactic Right Breast Mastectomy on December 27, 2019. The patient was started with Tab. Letrozole 2.5 mg daily and advised continuing for five years.

Therapeutic intervention

In May 2006, during oral chemotherapy, she opted for Ayurvedic treatment at our center and continued till date. She had complaints like breathlessness on exertion (Ayasen shwas), generalized weakness (Daurbalya), nausea (Hrullas), loss of appetite (Agnimandya), burning in palms and soles (Hasta-Pada tal daha), and backache (Katishoola). As per Ayurveda, the disease was elucidated as Dushta Beejanda Mansapradhan Arbuda, by correlating her detailed disease history with Ayurvediya Nidan Panchaka (Diagnostic parameters). She was treated with Shaman Chikitsa consisting of a combination of Oral Ayurvedic Medicines (OAM) viz., Mouktikyukta Kamdudha Vati (MKD), Arogyavardhini Vati (AV), Triphala Guggul (TG), Atharva Masha Yoga Vati, Atharva Ananta Kalpa, Gorakh chinchavaleha, Shatavari Kalpa (SK), Atharva Amalaki, Shankh Vati, Atharva Aloe Plus Tab and Rasayan Chikitsa (immunomodulatory treatment) viz. Suvarna Bhasmadi Yoga (SBD), Suvarna Malini Vasant (SMV) along with 12 sets of Panchakarma Chikitsa including various types of Basti and Shirodhara from December 2007 to March 2024 [Supplementary File 1, Table S1], to prevent further recurrence and improve quality of life.

Outcome and follow-up

The patient was assessed regularly with monthly follow-ups. She got symptomatic relief in loss of appetite (Agnimandya), nausea (Hrullas), headache (Shiroshool), acidity (Amlapitta), general weakness (Daurbalya), joint pain/body ache (Sarvang sandhishool), and backache (Katishool) with OAM and periodic detoxifying Panchakarma treatment (Table 1). USG of abdomen and pelvis dated June 19, 2019 i.e., done after 13 years of Ayurvedic treatment showed no significant abnormality (Table 2) while her tumor marker CA125, Haemogram, Liver function test, Renal function test, and C-Reactive Protein, evaluated periodically throughout the treatment period, remained within the normal range (Table 3).

In October 2019, PET-CT scan showed a second primary neoplastic breast lesion with no evidence of metabolically active disease elsewhere in the body. She was treated with an integrated approach i.e. conventional treatment and OAM. After completing conventional treatment for breast malignancy, her latest radiological (Table 2) and biochemistry reports with tumor markers CA-125 dated February 8, 2024 (Table 3) were within normal limits.

Karnofsky Performance Score and Quality of Life (QoL) based on QLQ C30, QLQ OV28, and BR23 of the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) [7,8] as assessed yearly were improved and later maintained during the last 18 years. In her last follow-up, the patient suffered from indigestion which reflected in QLQ OV28 as a disturbed score. However, the disease is under control since latest CA 125 is within normal limits (Table 3).

Discussion

Ovarian cancer is the most lethal with 15% of ovarian cancers diagnosed at an early stage and the overall prognosis is poor with a 5-year relative survival rate ranging between 30 and 40% [2]. This patient presented as a rare case of BRCA1 HBOC with a high risk of developing multiple cancers [5,6], and complained of dysmenorrhea during her reproductive age. Many studies have suggested that irregular periods with painful menstruation can lead to an increased risk of developing ovarian cancer with a high mortality rate [9,10]. Before diagnosis, the patient was under consistent mental stress. Many studies reports strong evidence of a relationship between anxiety and/or depression as a prevalence factor of ovarian cancer [[11], [12], [13]]. The patient had an addiction to tobacco for more than 20 years, which provides an additional clear risk for the development of cancer [14]. Past history of Herpes Zoster might have triggered the carcinogenic changes, as earlier studies have suggested an interrelation between Herpes Zoster and an increased probability of developing cancer [15,16].

The patient was started with Ayurvedic treatment during oral chemotherapy of the first malignancy. She was treated with Shaman Chikitsa, Rasayan Chikitsa, Panchakarma Chikitsa, a healthy diet, and lifestyle modification. Shaman Chikitsa was mainly recommended for improving appetite and digestion, reducing generalized weakness, and imposing anti-inflammatory effects (Table 1). Rasayan Chikitsa additively exhibited an immunomodulatory effect. Basti as a Panchakarma treatment was mainly used since it has qualities i.e., Shodhan (to eradicate deep-seated vitiated Dosha), Bruhan, and Rasayana (nourishment to the body) [Supplementary File 1 Table S1].

During the entire period of Ayurvedic treatment, she was advised to take satvik (righteous), shad rasatmak (including all six tastes), laghu (light to digest), supachya (easy to digest) ahar (diet) and avoid ati snigdha (unctuous), ati tikshna (spicy food), abhishyandi (food items which obstruct channels in the body and are slimy and heavy in nature), guru (heavy to digest), viruddha (incompatible diet) and paryushit (stale food) ahar. Patient follows it regularly till date which leads to achieve good quality of life in a patient in spite of secondary malignancy.

The mode of action of the used Ayurvedic modalities with details is mentioned in Supplementary File 1.

In Shaman Chikitsa (for Dosha-Dhatu equilibrium), she was treated with various herbal, mineral, and metallic formulations viz., Mouktikyukta Kamdudha Vati (MKD), Arogyavardhini Vati (AV), Triphala Guggul (TG), Atharva Masha Yoga Vati, Atharva Ananta Kalpa, Gorakh chinchavaleha, Shatavari Kalpa (SK), Tab Prasham, Atharva Amalaki Vati, Shankh Vati and Atharva Aloe Plus Tab according to the symptoms during the course of treatment.

The patient had loss of appetite, nausea, and burning sensation in palms and soles which clearly indicated aggravation of Vata and Pitta dosha causing low digestive power (Jatharagni and Dhatwagni mandya). MKD and Ananta Kalp were used as Dahashamak (pacifies heat and Pitta dosha), Agnideepak (appetizer), and Balya (imparts strength) [17,18]. AV was prescribed in order to prevent recurrence by detoxifying body, mainly the liver due to its Malashuddhikara effect [19,20]. TG is reported to be beneficial in various inflammations and tumors since it has anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer, and antioxidant properties [[21], [22], [23], [24], [25], [26]]. Atharva Masha Yog Vati (an Ayurvedic proprietary medicine) was used as vedanashamak (analgesic), asthipachak (improves metabolism of osseous tissue), and asthiposhak (nourishing osseous tissue) since the patient experienced backache and joint pain. Gorakh chinchavaleh, also a proprietary medicine, was administered for Vatanuloman (to regulate Vata dosha), pacifying Pitta dosha, acidity, belching, pain in the abdomen, and gastric ulcers since it possesses Madhur-Amla rasa (sweet and sour taste) and sheeta veerya (cold in potency) qualities as well as anti-oxidant and anti-inflammatory properties [27].

After the confirmation of breast cancer, the patient was treated with SK, reported to be having anti-cancer activity in various cancer cell lines along with anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties [28]. Mental stress plays a major role in the development of cancer, especially in cancer of female reproductive organs. Hence, Tab Prasham, a proprietary medicine was used for its whole body calming and mental de-stressing effect [29]. Atharva Amalaki Vati and Shankh Vati were used for Vatanuloman (to regulate Vata dosha), to pacify Pitta Dosha and symptoms of Gastro-esophageal reflux [30,31]. Atharva Aloe Plus Tab (an Ayurvedic proprietary medicine) was used to prevent further recurrence or metastasis as it possesses anti-inflammatory and anti-cancer activities [32].

Suvarna Bhasma containing formulations like Suvarna Bhasmadi Yoga (SBD), Suvarna Malini Vasant (SMV), were used as Rasayana Chikitsa. It improves Dhatwagni and imparts cellular rejuvenation [33]. Suvarna bhasma acts as a Rasayana and Ojovardhan (immunomodulator), possesses Vishaghna (detoxifying) and Deepana (improves appetite) properties and promotes proper nourishment to the body. This probably helped to prevent or delay recurrence or distant metastasis, especially due to its immunomodulatory property [34,35].

Panchakarma Chikitsa was particularly used to eliminate deeply situated toxins in all the tissue and organs, followed by a healthy diet and lifestyle modification. Amongst Panchakarma, Basti is a commonly recommended procedure for the disease of female genital organs (Tryavarta Yoni), and thus was administered in this patient of ovarian cancer [36]. She received 12 sittings of Basti treatment between 2007 and 2024 with medicines having activities of anti-helminthic (Krumighna Basti), osteogenic (Asthi Balya and Sandhaneeya) (Tiktaksheera Basti) as well as Rasayan and strengthening (Bruhaniya- Mustadi Yapan and Bruhan Basti). A previous study on repeated use of Krumighna and Erandmooladi Basti has shown significant improvement in QoL in various cancer patients [37]. As mentioned in classical texts, Yapana Basti helps in the purification process with ease and soothing effect, nourishes the body tissues, and might help protect the healthy tissue from dysplastic changes [36]. Clinical research on Tikta ksheera Basti for use in Ashti-Majjagata Vata has shown symptomatic relief in backache and joint pain highlighting its ability to repair, and delay the degenerative process of bones and cartilage [36]. Bruhan Basti is used as balya i.e., strength and health promoter as it nourishes all body tissues [38,39]. Basti is safe among many cancer patients as it can counteract cancer pathogenesis in all aspects including symptomatic relief, prevention of complications, and recurrence. In cancer, a balanced combination of Shodhana, Bruhana, and Rasayana Karma is indicated. Thus, the above Basti are more effective in cancerous conditions [40].

Shirodhara, a well-known Ayurvedic allied procedure, is mainly advised to treat stress, anxiety, and insomnia as well as to relax the nervous system. Based on previous studies, psychological factors strongly influence the development and progression of cancer. Shirodhara therapy has been studied to re-establish the balance of Sharira (physical) and Manas (mental) dosha [41,42].

Conclusion

The present case is high-grade, Stage IIIA Ovarian carcinoma and second primary Breast carcinoma with BRCA 1 genetic mutation (HBOC syndrome) with limited literature, research, and treatment modalities leading to poor prognosis and maximum risk of new malignancies. We, therefore signify that in this case, long DFS of 13 years and OS of 20 years is achieved with the integration of oral Ayurvedic treatment and multiple Panchakarma treatments, along with conventional cancer treatment.

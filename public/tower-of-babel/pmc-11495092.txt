Whole-genome sequencing and assessment of a novel protein- and gossypol-degrading Bacillus subtilis strain isolated from intestinal digesta of Tibetan Pigs
Authors: Xiangyue Guo, Zhenda Shang, Qianxi Li, Lixue Wang, Ying Zhang, Suozhu Liu, Yunhe Cao, Bing Dong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundWith the rapid development of animal husbandry, the demand for protein feed resources is increasing. Cottonseed meal (CSM) and soybean meal (SBM) are rich sources of protein. However, their application is limited due to the existence of anti-nutrients, which can be harmful to the digestion and absorption. A strain of Bacillus subtilis (Mafic-Y7) was isolated from digesta of intestines of Tibetan pigs. The strain showed high protease activity, which helps in degrading proteinic anti-nutritional factors in grain meal and in vitro degradation of free gossypol. In order to better understand this isolated strain, whole genome of Mafic-Y7 strain was sequenced and analyzed. Different effects on various grain meals were identified.ResultThe GC-depth Poisson distributions showed no bias suggesting high-quality genome assembly of Mafic-Y7. The whole genome sequencing showed that one chromosome with 4,248,845 base pairs(bp)and the genes total length with 3,736,524 bp was predicted in Mafic-Y7. Additionally, Mafic-Y7 possessed 4,254 protein-coding genes, and several protease genes were annotated by aligning them with databases. There are 55 protease genes, one phytase gene and one laccase gene were annotated in the gene sequence of Mafic-Y7. The average nucleotide identity between Mafic-Y7 and the GCA-000009045.1 homologous genome was 0.9938, suggesting a close genetic relationship between them at the species level. Compared with the closest four whole genomes, Mafic-Y7 was annotated the most abundant of protease genes (55 genes). The fermentation supernatant of Mafic-Y7 could increase the content of small peptides, water-soluble proteins, and acid-soluble proteins in vitro by 411%, 281% and 317% in SBM and 420%, 257% and 338% in CSM. After fermentation in grain meal by Mafic-Y7, the degradation rate of anti-nutritional factors in SBM, such as trypsin inhibitor, glycinin, and β-conglycinin was greater than 70%, and lectin was greater than 30%. The degradation rates of anti-nutritional factors in CSM, such as gossypol and phytic acid, were 82% and 26%, respectively.

Background

The special ecological climate of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau fosters a diverse range of ecological resources, including unique species that are adapted to the region [1, 2]. The unique biological characteristics of this high-altitude region were extensively explored [3]. Tibetan pigs live in a harsh environment at altitudes of up to 3000 m and in cold temperatures as low as -23℃ [4]. Tibetan pigs are highly adaptive to harsh environments due to their high digestibility of a variety of feeds [5]. Tibetan pigs mainly eat high-fiber feedstuffs such as plant leaves, roots, and fruits that occur naturally in their grazing areas. Their diets contain a very low concentration of protein. High-fiber and low-protein diets have directed the development of a robust digestive system of Tibetan pigs. A recent study revealed that the intestines of Tibetan pigs had shorter villi and much lower protease activity in the small intestinal mucosa compared to those of Duroc × Landrace × Yorkshire pigs [6]. Tibetan pigs expressed different miRNAs associated with protein digestion in stomach compared to Yorkshire pigs [7]. These structural studies, including intestinal morphology, enzyme production, and genome analysis, indicated a lower digestive capacity of Tibetan pigs compared to common commercial pigs. However, evidences had demonstrated that Tibetan pigs have a higher digestibility. In Tibetan pigs, feed digestible energy (DE) did not affected gain efficiency [8], whereas in Large White pigs, gain efficiency decreased with increasing DE [9, 10]. Moreover, Tibetan pigs could digest low-quality protein from CSM. The digestibility of protein and amino acids from CSM was similar to those from SBM in growing Tibetan pigs [11]. It is well known that gossypol in cottonseed meal can cross-link protein molecules to form, dimers, trimers, tetramers, etc., which are difficult to be digested by proteases in common commercial pigs [12]. All evidences suggested that Tibetan pigs could potentially earn benefit from intestinal microorganisms to enhance their digestibility and detoxification capabilities.

Intestinal microorganisms play a crucial role in promoting the absorption of nutrients in pigs [13]. In light of these characteristics, numerous studies have been conducted on the intestinal microorganisms of Tibetan pigs. A strain of L. amylovorus isolated from the feces of Tibetan weaned piglets exhibited strong probiotic characteristics [14]. A bacillus strain isolated from the intestinal tract of Tibetan swine, produced a novel peptide that exhibited significant antibacterial and anti-tumor properties and also inhibited the growth of Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus [15]. A strain of Bacillus subtilis, SWUN-TP23, secreted protease and exhibits prebiotic effects [16].

Proteases, which are widely distributed in animals, plants, and microorganisms, can hydrolyze proteins into small peptides and amino acids. Proteases are used in leather production, food processing, animal feed and various other industries [17]. Proteases can replace sodium sulfide for hair removal and tanning leather [18], and improve the softness and flexibility of leather [19]. Exogenous protease can improve tenderness of meat and reduce allergens in bovine milk, making it more suitable for newborns [20, 21]. Addition of proteases to diets improved growth performance and nutrient digestibility of broilers [22] and apparent ileal digestibility of amino acids in piglets [23].

In the feed industry, dietary antinutritional factors are chief components that limit the digestion and absorption of nutrients for animals. Trypsin inhibitors and hemagglutinin in legumes, tannins in cereals, gossypol in CSM, and glucosides and glucosinolates of rapeseed products can induce pro-inflammatory responses, reduce digestion and absorption efficiency of nutrients in animals, and consequently decrease the utilization of raw materials, leading to increased feed costs [24]. Physical methods, such as water immersion and heat treatment, can eliminate some water-soluble and heat-sensitive anti-nutritional factors. However, this can result in the loss of nutrients like sulfur-containing amino acids and vitamins. Chemical treatment with acids and alkali is simple to implement, but chemical residues might be toxic to livestock. Advances in biotechnology allow heterogeneous production of microbial enzymes in bacterial or yeast hosts, such as microbial proteases. Compared with the physical and chemical treatments to remove anti-nutritional factors, biological treatments are safer and more environmentally friendly [25–28]. Due to the advantages of heterogeneous expression of microbial proteases which are controllable and with high yield, microbial proteases and its application have been extensively studied [29].

As the main intestinal compartment responsible for protein digestion, the microorganisms in the jejunum and proximal ileum have not been well studied. It is highly attractive to screen unique bacterial strains from the intestinal compartment. Our lab recently isolated a strain of Bacillus subtilis (Mafic-Y7). This organism exhibited high protease activity and a strong capability to degrade gossypol in vitro. For a more comprehensive understanding of this strain, the genome of Mafic-Y7 was sequenced and its predicted metabolic pathways were analyzed. In addition, we selected whole genomes with the closest evolutionary relationships for comparative genome-wide analyses.

Methods

All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, following the with ARRIVE guidelines (Animal Research: Reporting in Vivo Experiments) for the reporting of animal experiments. All procedures of this experiment were accordant with the Animal Welfare and Experimental Protocol and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of China Agricultural University (ID: SKLAB-B-2010-003).

The screening of protease producing strains

Six healthy 6-week-old experimental Tibetan pigs, raised at the Tibetan Pig Cooperative Research Centre of the Tibetan University of Agriculture and Animal Husbandry, were euthanized after being anesthetized with an intravenous injection of sodium pentobarbital solution (40 mg/kg body weight) and then euthanized by jugular vein bleeding. When piglets were ensured completely unconscious according to the three indicators following AVMA Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals (2020 Edition) [30] (1. Absence of corneal reflex; 2. Absence of eyelash reflex (response to touch); 3. Absence of rhythmic breathing.), the piglets were slaughtered. Fresh chyme samples were collected from the jejunum and proximal ileum within 5 min after slaughter and immediately stored at -80 °C. The contents were diluted with sterile water and cultivated at 37℃ for 48 h in a culture medium (0.3% beef extract, 1%pepton, 0.5% sodium chloride, 1.5% agar, and 1.5% skim milk). The monoclonal with different morphology were selected and inoculated on LB liquid medium at 37℃, 180 rpm for 48 h. The seed liquid was inoculated at 37 °C, 280 rpm for 42 h in medium (4% soybean meal flour, 5.5% corn flour, 0.03% potassium dihydrogen phosphate and 0.4% disodium hydrogen phosphate) to induce the protease production. Then the supernatant was obtained by centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min for determination of protease activity. For assay of protease activity, an aliquot of 1 mL of 2% casein solution and 1.0 mL of enzyme solution were mixed and incubated in 40℃ water bath for 10 min. The reaction was stopped by adding 2.0 mL of 0.4 M trichloroacetic acid to terminate the reaction and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min, and then centrifuged at 1,900 g for 10 min. For colorimetric detection, supernatant (1.0 mL) was reacted with sodium carbonate (5.0 mL of 0.4 M) and Folin-Phenol reagent (1.0 mL) for 20 min at 40℃ [31]. Then the absorbance was measured at 680 nm. Blanks were prepared by adding the trichloroacetic acid before enzyme addition. Protease activity was expressed as the tyrosine equivalent in the supernatant: one unit of enzyme activity was defined as the amount of enzyme (µg/mg) that produces 1 µg of tyrosine per min. All samples were measured in triplicate.

Screening of gossypol-degraded strains

The strains with higher protease activity were selected and inoculated on the plate with gossypol acetate as the sole carbon source for primary screening. The strains that could grow in the plate were selected and inoculated with 0.5 g free gossypol in 100 mL liquid medium at 37℃, 180 rpm for 72 h [32]. The aniline method as described by Zhang [33] was used to determine the content of free gossypol treatment with lyophilized enzyme.

Cultivation conditions, DNA extraction and sequencing

Mafic-Y7 was isolated from Tibetan pig digesta and deposited in the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC No. 21,855). Mafic-Y7 was resuscitated in LB solid medium (10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L sodium chloride, 20 g/L agar) at 39℃ for 8 h. Single colonies were selected and cultivated in LB liquid medium (10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L sodium chloride) at 37℃ for 24 h at 200 rpm to prepare DNA extraction. Genomic DNA of each isolate was extracted using a Wizard R Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Promega Corporation, Fitchburg, WI, United States) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The integrity and purity of extracted DNA were assessed using 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Universal primers 27 F ~ 1492R (27 F: 5’AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG 3’, 1492 R: 5’GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT 3’) were used to identify and amplify bacterial 16S rRNA sequences [34]. The 16S rRNA gene sequence was compared with the reference sequences of the NCBI (National Center for Biotechnology Information) GenBank database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/ ). A phylogenetic tree of Mafic-Y7 based on the16S rRNA sequence was constructed using the NJ (neighbor-joining), ML (maximum-likelihood), and MP (maximum-parsimony) methods in MEGA (molecular evolutionary genetics analysis) software (version 6.0) with bootstrap values of 1000 resampling to the strains of bacteria closest to Mafic-Y7 at the species level [35].

Genome sequencing, assembly and annotation analyses

Wholegenome was sequenced (Shanghai Majorbio Bio-pharm Technology Company, China). Bacterial genome de novo sequencing refers to sequencing the bacterial genome without relying on any reference sequence and the assembling of the genome sequence from scratch using bioinformatics methods. The sequencing process included genomic DNA quality control, genomic DNA fragmentation, library construction, bridging PCR, and sequencing of Mafic-Y7 strains using PacBio RS and Illumina sequencing platforms. The date was analyzed using the free online Majorbio I-Sanger (http://www.i-sanger.com). CheckM was used to assess the completeness of each contig that was matched [36]. Coding sequences (CDSs) were predicted by Glimmer software version 3.02 (http://cbcb.umd.edu/software/glimmer/). NR(http://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/blast/db/), Swiss-Prot (http://uniprot.org), Pfam (http://pfam.xfam.org/), KEGG (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/), COG (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/COG) databases were used to annotate CDSs. SignalP version 4.1 (http://www.cbs.dtu.dk/services/SignalP) was also utilized to predicts protein secretion. Four closed whole-genome sequences based on 16S rRNA with Mafic-Y7 were downloaded from NCBI for whole-genome comparison.

Preparation of fermentation supernatant

Mafic-Y7 was cultivated in 100 ml LB liquid medium (10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, 10 g/L sodium chloride) at 37℃ for 12 h at 200 rpm until the absorbance of the bacterial solution (OD600) reached 2 ~ 6. Subsequently, the Mafic-Y7 bacterial solution was used to inoculate in enzyme induced medium (10 L) for fermentation. Fermentation time was 36 h at 30 °C, pH = 7.0 (adjusted with ammonia), ventilation at 2.5 L/min, and relative dissolved oxygen was maintained above 25% by adjusting rotational speed and airflow. After fermentation, the centrifugal supernatant was freeze-dried to produce lyophilized powder.

Degradation of anti-nutrition factors and determination of nutritional factors in SBM and CSM

SBM and CSM were in vitro hydrolyzed by lyophilized protease produced by Mafic-Y7, respectively. The samples (SBM or CSM) were crushed to pass through a 60-mesh sieve. Then, the samples (2 g) were mixed with lyophilized enzyme powder (2,000 U) in water at the ratio of 9:1 (v/v) in a bottle. Degradation of SBM or CSM was carried out by shaking the bottle at 41℃ for 48 h at 120 rpm. The products of degradation were then dried at 65℃ for 20 h. Each degradation treatment was performed in triplicate.

The contents of small peptides, water-soluble proteins, and acid-soluble proteins in CSM or SBM were determined after lyophilized enzyme treatment for CSM or SBM. For determination of the content of small peptides, 10% trichloroacetic acid (TCA) was added to the samples and shaken at 160 rpm for 30 min. The supernatant was obtained by centrifugation at 4,000 rpm for 15 min and was measured using Folin-phenol method [37]. The content of water-soluble protein was measured using commercial Bicinchoninic Acid (BCA) assay kits (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) [38].The content of acid-soluble protein was calculated by multiplying the content of acid-soluble nitrogen by 0.625. The content of acid-soluble nitrogen was assayed by the micro-Kjeldah after the samples were mixed with 25 mL of 10% TCA and shaken at 200 rpm for 1 h at 25 °C [39]. The content of small peptides, water-soluble proteins, or acid-soluble proteins of CSM or SBN was deemed as 100% before lyophilized enzyme treatment.

Lyophilized enzyme (2,000 U) was mixed with 2 g of ground SBM or CSM in sodium phosphate buffer (50 mM, pH 7.0) at a ratio of 1:11 (w/v), followed by incubation at 41 °C for 2 h. Solid phase of the mixture was collected by centrifugation and dried in a forced-air oven at 65 °C for 72 h. The anti-nutritional factor, glycinin and β-conglycinin, were analyzed using commercial ELISA kits (Longkefangzhou Bio-Engineering Technology Company, Beijing, China). Trypsin inhibitor was determined by the reported method using N-benzoyl-arginine-p-nitroanilide [40]. The activity of lectin was assayed [41]. The content of gossypol was determined by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) [42]. The content of phytic acid was measured using commercial kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions, content was determined (Megazyme Int. Ireland Ltd., Wicklow, Ireland) [43].

Statistical analysis

All in vitro treatments were performed in triplicates. The data were expressed as mean values and standard deviations. Statistical analysis was conducted using Initilizaing GraphPad Prism (Version 8.0.2) software. One-way ANOVA was conducted to compare differences among treatments. P value less than 0.01 was deemed as significance.

Mafic-Y7 was selected for further study.

Genome features and annotation

Sequencing analyses showed that Mafic-Y7 consisted of one chromosome with 4,248,845 bp and contained 43.46% GC (Fig. 4; Table 1). Total length of chromosome occupied by genes was 3,736,524 bp and the ratio of gene length/genome was 87.94%. The genome was predicted to encode 4,254 protein-coding genes. The COG, GO, and KEGG annotations in the genome were 3,314, 3,050, and 2,483, respectively. In addition, the chromosome includes 86 tRNA genes and 10 genes each for 5s rRNA, 16s rRNA and 23s rRNA (Table 1).

COG classification

The COG annotation of 3,314 genes was classified into 23 COG types (Fig. 5), which accounted for 77.9% of all genes of Mafic-Y7. The types of COGs included five main functional groups: transcription (325 genes), amino acid transport and metabolism (322 genes), carbohydrate transport and metabolism (318 genes), general function prediction only (294 genes), and ribosomal structure and biogenesis (244 genes). The top three abundant COGs related to transcription were: COG0718 (DNA-binding nucleoid-associated protein YbaB/EfbC), COG5865 (Forespore-specific anti-sigma-G factor CsfB/Gin) and COG2002 (Bifunctional DNA-binding transcriptional regulator). And the top three COGs related to amino acid transport and metabolism were: COG 1982 (Arginine/lysine/ornithine decarboxylase), COG0031 (Cysteine synthase), and COG0147 (Anthranilate/para-aminobenzoate synthases complex), respectively.

GO annotation

Genes annotated based on the GO database were categorized into three categories (Fig. 6) which were biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF). MF had the highest abundance with 2400 genes, followed by CC with 1872 genes and BP with 1919 genes. The three pathways in cellular component classification with the most genes detected included integral component of membrane, plasma membrane, and cytoplasm. ATP binding, DNA binding, and metal ion binding were the most enriched subclasses in molecular function. Similarly, sporulation resulting in the formation of cellular spores, regulation of transcription, DNA-templated, and transmembrane transport were the most enriched subclasses in the biological process branch.

KEGG analysis

The results of KEGG annotation showed that the CDS (4,254 genes) were classified into 6 major classes (Fig. 7), which involved metabolism (1,899 genes), environmental information processing (322 genes), genetic information procession (196 genes), cellular processes (174 genes), human diseases (117 genes), and organismal systems (53 genes). Each classification had 4, 12, 4, 7, 12 and 3 subclasses, respectively. In the cellular processes, the cellular community-prokaryotes passageway had the most annotations, with 81 enriched genes. Carbohydrate metabolism (267 genes), amino acid metabolism (213 genes), metabolism of cofactors and vitamins (167 genes), energy metabolism (119 genes), and nucleotide metabolism (86 genes) were the five main functional pathways in the category of metabolism. Proteases related to amino acid metabolism in KEGG annotation were involved in the cysteine, methionine, alanine, aspartate, glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism. There were 85 genes enriched in translation, which was the most annotated process in genetic information processing. The endocrine system was the most frequently annotated in organismal systems, with 15 genes being enriched. Antimicrobial passageway in human diseases had the most annotations, with 31 enriched genes. Among the pathways analyzed, three showed the highest gene enrichment related to environmental information. These pathways included a significant number of protein-encoding genes enriched in signal transduction, membrane transport, and signaling molecules and interactions.

The prediction of secretory protein

Bacterial secretion systems are involved in Type I, Type II, Type III, Type IV, Type V, Type VI Sec-srp and Tat [44]. Mafic-Y7 was predicted to have 15 secretion systems, including 10 s-SPR and 5 Tat secretion systems. Proteins secreted by the Sec pathway are unfolded when transported extracellularly through the plasma membrane of the bacterial cell, whereas proteins secreted by the Tat pathway have a folded conformation [45]. B. subtilis produced at least eight extracellular or cell wall-associated proteins, including alkaline serine protease (AprE), neutral metalloproteinase (NprE), serine protease (Epr), bacitracinase F (Bpr), Vpr, neutral protease B (NprB), cell wall-associated protease (WprA), and metalloproteinase (Mpr). More than 95% of the extracellular protease activity of B. subtilis consists of the alkaline serine protease Subtilis (AprE) and neutral metalloproteinase (NprE) [46]. Each gene encoding AprE, NprE, Epr, Bpr, Vpr, NprB, and WprA found in the Mafic-Y7 genome was unique. According to the signal peptide analysis, six proteases were predicted to be secreted extracellularly in Mafic-Y7 (Table 2).

Genomes for comparison

Four complete genomes (GCA_001517105.1, GCA_000507145.1, GCA_000332645.1 and GCA_000009045.1) closet to Mafic-Y7 were downloaded from NCBI for genomic comparison.

The method of Average Nucleotide Identity (ANI) of genomes was performed to evaluated species boundaries and offered a resolution between any two genomes [47]. It can distinguish between strains of the same species or closely related species. Four whole genomes (GCA_001517105.1, GCA_000507145.1, GCA_000332645.1 and GCA_000009045.1) were selected for ANI analysis. The Fig. 10 showed that ANI of 0.8876 between Mafic-Y7 and GCA_001517105.1, while the closest whole-genome showed an ANI of 0.9938 for the strains between Mafic-Y7 and GCA_000009045.1.

Effects of proteases degradation on grain meals

Compared with SBM untreated with lyophilized enzyme, the treated SBM contained increased levels of small peptides, water-soluble proteins, and acid-soluble proteins after degradation by 411%, 281%, and 317%, respectively (Fig. 12A). Similarly, treated CSM contained increased levels of small peptides, water-soluble proteins, and acid-soluble proteins comparing with the untreated CSM by 420%, 257%, and 338%, respectively (Fig. 12B).

Discussion

Nutrients are detrimental for animal growth, but the presence of anti-nutritional factors in feed ingredients hinders the digestion and absorption of nutrients. Anti-nutritional factors are found in plant-based feedstuffs, such as glucosides in rapeseed products, trypsin inhibitors, tannins, and hemagglutinin in legumes, phytates in grains and oilseeds, and gossypol in cottonseed products [48]. Protease has been widely utilized in various industries [49]. In the feed industry, proteases are primarily used to eliminate anti-nutritional factors and enhance the nutritional quality of feedstuff [50]. The Bacillus genus is one of the most important sources of bacterial proteases [51]. In this study, a Bacillus strain, Mafic-Y7, capable of producing protease, was isolated from Tibetan pig digesta.

The GC depth distributions and GC content without sequencing bias revealed a high-quality genome assembly [52]. In this study, Mafic-Y7 sequencing showed no GC bias thus its genome assembly was deemed as a high quality. The genomic similarity between Mafic-Y7 and B. subtilis was 100% according to the phylogenetic tree generated. Thus, Mafic-Y7 was confirmed and identified as B. subtilis [53]. COG categories reveal functions related to metabolism [54]. This strain has a large number of genes involved in carbohydrate transport and metabolism, amino acid transport and metabolism, membrane transport, and energy production and transformation. In the subclassification of GO annotation, Mafic-Y7 encoded a large number of genes involved in integral components of membranes, plasma membranes, and the cytoplasm. This indicated that the strain had good membrane production ability. ATP-binding genes accounted for a large proportion of molecular functions, indicating that more energy is needed for transmembrane transport [55]. These gene functional annotations provide evidence that Mafic-Y7 has potential to secrete a large amount of protease. In KEGG annotation, 599 genes were detected that are involved in carbohydrate metabolism, amino acid metabolism, and energy metabolism were detected. These genes may be related to the protein secretion potential of Mafic-Y7. The KEGG pathway analysis results revealed that Mafic-Y7 exhibited strong synthetic and metabolic capabilities. Carbohydrate metabolism and membrane transport involved 267 and 172 genes, indicating that the strain had good membrane permeability and metabolic capacity. In addition, there were few genes related to human diseases, which ensured the safety of the secreted protein of this strain. Annotation of resistance genes suggested that Mafic-Y7 was resistant to antibiotics of macrolide antibiotic, tetracyline antibiotic, and fluoroquinolone etc. The ANI was used to assess complete genome similarity [56]. In this study, the ANI of the whole genomes of five strains (Fig. 10) was consistent with the comparing results of the 16S rRNA tree of the five strains (Fig. 3). Pan genomic analysis suggested that core genes were highly conserved in evolution [57]. The evolution of specific genes was related to their adaptation of the species to the environment [58].

Mafic-Y7 (213 genes) showed a greater number of annotated genes in the amino acid metabolism pathway compared to Dw-7 (206 genes) [59]. Mafic-Y7 (4,254 genes) annotation revealed a higher number of protease-coding genes compared to 3 H-10 (22 genes) [60] and ZMS-2 (25 genes) [61]. Based on catalytic characteristics, proteases were classified into four categories: serine, cysteine, aspartic, and metalloprotease. In the study of Mafic-Y7, genes that encode proteases in serine protease, aspartate kinase, serine protease, and metalloprotease were annotated, which suggest that Mafic-Y7 has a high ability to produce proteases [62].

Six extracellular protease genes were predicted by signal peptide analysis in Mafic-Y7: extracellular metalloprotease Mpr, cell wall-associated protease WprA, neutral protease NprB, neutral metalloprotease NprE, serine protease Vpr, and minor protease Epr. Under nutrient restriction when growth of B. subtilis is stationary, proteases were secreted to provide nutrients for cells to grow and divide [63, 64]. Cell wall-associated protease WprA is related to degradation of misfolded proteins in the cell wall, membrane, and cytoplasm [65].One-third of enzymes require regulation of a metal ions which can promote or inhibit enzyme activity in hydrolase, lyase and transferase [66]. Metalloprotease was described as an endopeptidase involved in degrading proteins into oligopeptides and amino acids, activation or inactivation of biological mediators, and cellular processing [67]. In addition, some metalloproteases have antibacterial properties, which hydrolyzes the cell wall to inhibit bacteria [68]. Serine proteases are widely distributed, accounting for nearly one-third of known protease [59]. Serine proteases contain the classical catalytic Ser/His/Asp triad that plays an important role in hydrolysis of proteins [69].

According to degradation of SBM and CSM in vitro, small peptides, water-soluble proteins and acid-soluble proteins were increased, which can improve absorption and utilization of protein and promote development of the intestinal tract. Small peptides have the characteristics: not easy to saturate, low energy consumption and conducive to rapid absorption for animals. Acid-soluble proteins are absorbed easily for animals because of low energy needed for absorption and the mechanism is not easily saturated [32]. Acid-soluble protein reflects the degradation of hydrolysis of antigen protein and the level of peptide content. Therefore, content of acid-soluble protein can evaluate the protein quality of treated meal. The water-soluble protein reflects the content of small and medium molecular protein. The higher the content of water-soluble protein, the better the water solubility, and the easier to be used by young animals. The distribution and quantity of protease-related genes on chromosome map provide strong evidence that that digestion with protease secreted by Mafic-Y7 increased the small peptide content of SBM and CSM.

The structural similarities between gossypol and naphthalene derivatives allow modeling of the catabolic pathways of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) to explore how their aromatic rings are cleaved from CO2, initiated by monooxygenase [70, 71]. Many studies have shown that phenol can be degrade by Acinetobacter [72], Pseudomonas [73]and Bacillus [74]. It was reported showed that Pseudomonas sp. (IES-S) and B. subtilis (IES-B) strains could transformed phenol into catechol, which was then degraded via ortho-cleavage pathway [75]. Using a combination of nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, electron spin resonance spectroscopy and molecular simulations, Wang et al. showed that laccase is able to catalyze the intramolecular annulation of the aldehyde and hydroxyl groups gossypol on the o-semiquinone radical and to generate the -OH radical that is released [71]. The innovation of this study is that the proteases secreted by Mafic-Y7 can degrade gossypol.

Proteases produced by Mafic-Y7 might decrease β-conglycinin and destruction of original structure in SBM protein [76]. A study [77] found that the content of lysine and total amino acid increased in fermented SBM. Bacterial fermentation also improved content of nutritional components such as essential and non-essential amino acids [78]. Trypsin inhibitor, glycinin, β-conglycinin, and lectin in SBM are sensitizing proteins. Protease might degrade these macromolecule proteins into peptides and amino acids, which are absorbed easily by animals [79]. Although CSM is rich in oil and protein, toxic gossypol and phytic acid limit the application of CSM in animal feeds. Gossypol is a highly reactive compound and easily binds to amino acids. Protease may bind free gossypol and reduced the content of gossypol [33]. The degradation of phytic acid is partly due to the fact that the strain Mafic-Y7 has the gene for phytase, and partly due to the fact that phytic acid can combine with extracellular proteases, as phytic acid has a strong chelating ability, thus inactivating phytic acid. The SBM and CSM fermented by protease effectively reduce the contents of various anti-nutrition factors, which will be beneficial to animal digestion and absorption, and reduce the occurrence of diarrhea.

Conclusion

In this study, a strain with high protease activity was screened from intestinal digesta of Tibetan pig. The whole genome sequencing showed that one chromosome with 4,248,845 bp and the gene encoding a protein with 3,736,524 bp was predicted in Mafic-Y7. This strain was identified as Bacillus. subtilis Mafic-Y7. The sequencing results revealed that 55 protease genes were predicted, in which six proteases were secreted extracellularly. Compared with the closeted four whole genomes, Mafic-Y7 showed a close genetic relationship with GCA-000009045.1 by ANI analysis and had 168 unique genes by Pan Genome Analysis. The proteases produced by Mafic-Y7 increased the content of small peptide, water soluble proteins, and acid soluble proteins and decreased the content of anti-nutritional factors in SBM and CSM, which laid the foundation for the commercial production and application of the Mafic-Y7-produced proteases.

Square function estimates for cones over quadratic manifolds
Robert Schippa

Abstract
. We extend the L 4 L^{4} -square function estimates for the parabola and the half-cone to quadratic manifolds in higher dimensions and their conical extensions. To this end, we require transversality for the tangent spaces of the quadratic manifolds at separated points. This allows us to show biorthogonality for the associated system of quadratic equations. For the conical extensions we obtain a wave envelope estimate by High-Low arguments.

1.
Introduction

In the following we show
L^{4}
-square function estimates for quadratic manifolds and their conical extensions. Consider the mapping

(1)

Q:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{l},\quad\xi\mapsto(q_{1}(\xi),\ldots,q_{l}(\xi))

with

,
b_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

. Denote the ambient dimension with

. The base frequencies
\xi
are presently taken from the unit ball.

The quadratic manifolds are given as graph of

Presently, we are interested in
L^{4}
-square function estimates for general quadratic manifolds. The following condition naturally appears for the generators of the quadratic forms.

Assumption 1.1
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}\in\text{Sym}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be symmetric
d\times d
-matrices. We assume that there is

such that for any
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
there is a subset of
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}\}
such that

(3)

\det(A_{i_{1}}\nu,\ldots,A_{i_{d}}\nu)\geq c>0.

As a consequence we obtain quantitative transversality of the tangent spaces at different points: Let
\mathbf{t}_{i}(\xi)=\partial_{i}(\xi,Q(\xi))
,

denote tangent vectors spanning the tangent space

. We shall see below that

|\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{1})\wedge\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{2})\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{2})|\gtrsim_{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}1.

Under the above assumption, we can show the following:

Theorem 1.2

(Square function estimates for quadratic manifolds)
.

Suppose that the quadratic manifold
M_{Q}
is given by (
2
) and for the generators
A_{i}=\partial^{2}q_{i}
of the quadratic forms Assumption
1.1
holds true. Then, for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+l})
with
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(M_{Q})
it holds

(4)

\|f\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Remark 1.3
.

The implicit constant depends on the transversality parameter

The above square function estimate includes the estimate for the real and complex parabola. For further examples we refer to Section

1.2.
Square function estimates for cones over quadratic manifolds

In the next step we turn to square function estimates for the conical extension of these manifolds denoted by
\mathcal{C}M_{Q}
. Define the conical extension of
Q
by

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}Q:B_{d}(0,1)\times[1/2,1]&\to\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\\
(\xi,h)&\mapsto(\xi,\frac{q_{1}(\xi)}{h},\ldots,\frac{q_{l}(\xi)}{h},h)=h(\frac{\xi}{h},q_{1}\big(\frac{\xi}{h}\big),\ldots,q_{l}\big(\frac{\xi}{h}\big),1).\end{split}

\mathcal{C}M_{Q}
is given by the graph of
M_{Q}
:

(5)

\mathcal{C}M_{Q}=\{h(\frac{\xi}{h},q_{1}\big(\frac{\xi}{h}\big),\ldots,q_{l}\big(\frac{\xi}{h}\big),1):\quad\xi\in B_{d}(0,1),\;h\in[1/2,1]\}.

By the High-Low method, Guth–Wang–Zhang
[
8
]
proved a sharp square function estimate for the truncated half-cone, which after a linear transformation becomes the conical extension of the parabola:

(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mapsto(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\sqrt{\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}}).

The square function estimate for a canonical covering, i.e., the conical extension of a canonical covering of the base curve
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},1)
,
\|(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\|=1
, reads as follows for
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{R^{-1}}(\mathcal{C}\mathbb{P}_{2})
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|f|^{4}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}.

A square function estimate for the conical extension of the complex parabola has been shown in
[
14
]
.

Guth–Wang–Zhang
[
8
]
used elegant considerations based on incidence geometry to show a new Kakeya estimate, which uses the high symmetry of the expressions involved. In
[
15
]
a more analytic and local proof was detailed to analyze conical extensions of degenerate curves and the conical extension of the complex parabola. In
[
7
]
Guth–Maldague applied the High-Low method to obtain sharp square function estimates for the moment curves. Here we point out that the High-Low method extends to cones over higher-dimensional quadratic manifolds satisfying a transversality condition for tangent spaces at separated points. Furthermore, we surmise that the current approach can be combined with the arguments in
[
7
]
to show sharp square function estimates for manifolds with higher-order tangential spaces satisfying transversality conditions.

For the conical extension of the more general quadratic manifolds considered above we extend the more analytic approach from
[
15
]

L^{4}
-square function estimate provided that (

3.
Square function estimates for quadratic manifolds

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
. Secondly, we give examples.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

As a preliminary recall the following double-mean value theorem, which is immediate from the fundamental theorem of calculus:

Lemma 3.1

(Double-mean value theorem)
.

Let
m\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and
\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}=\xi_{2}+\xi_{4}
for
\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
i=1,\ldots,4
. Then it holds

m(\xi_{1})+m(\xi_{3})-m(\xi_{2})-m(\xi_{4})=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\xi_{12},\partial^{2}m(-\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\xi_{4}+s\xi_{12}+t\xi_{14})\xi_{14}\rangle dsdt

with
\xi_{ij}=\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}
.

We turn to the proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Write
f=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}f_{\theta}
with
f_{\theta}
denoting a smoothed version of Fourier projection to
\theta
such that
\hat{f}_{\theta}=\chi_{\theta}\hat{f}
. Here we require that the
(\chi_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
comprise a smooth partition of unity of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(M_{Q})
:
\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}\chi_{\theta}=1
.

This allows us to write

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f|^{4}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}f_{\theta}\big)^{2}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\delta}}\overline{f}_{\theta}\big)^{2}.

By an application of Plancherel’s theorem we find that only
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{4}
are contributing if there is a solution to the system of equations with
\xi_{i}\in\pi_{d}(\theta_{i})
,
\xi_{i}
a base frequency for
\theta_{i}
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\xi_{1}+\xi_{3}&=\xi_{2}+\xi_{4},\\
q_{1}(\xi_{1})+q_{1}(\xi_{3})&=q_{1}(\xi_{2})+q_{1}(\xi_{4})+\mathcal{O}(\delta),\\
&\vdots\\
q_{l}(\xi_{1})+q_{l}(\xi_{3})&=q_{l}(\xi_{2})+q_{l}(\xi_{4})+\mathcal{O}(\delta).\end{array}\right.

We aim to prove that
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{3}\}=\{\theta_{2},\theta_{4}\}
or up to finite translation, i.e.

(10)

\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}\subseteq B(0,C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}})\vee\theta_{1}-\theta_{4}\subseteq B(0,C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}).

We apply Lemma
3.1
on the quadratic equations to find the conditions

\langle\xi_{12},A_{m}\xi_{14}\rangle=\mathcal{O}(\delta)

for
m=1,\ldots,l
with
A_{m}
denoting the generators of the quadratic forms
\xi\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\langle\xi,A_{m}\xi\rangle
and
\xi_{ij}=\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}
.

We argue by contradiction and suppose that
\min(|\xi_{12}|,|\xi_{14}|)\gg\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}
. So, in this case we divide by the modulus of
\xi_{12}
and
\xi_{14}
to find the conditions

(11)

|\langle\xi^{\prime}_{12},A_{m}\xi^{\prime}_{14}\rangle|\ll 1,\quad\xi^{\prime}_{ij}=\xi_{ij}/\|\xi_{ij}\|.

By the transversality condition (
3
) we can find for
\xi^{\prime}_{14}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
a subsequence of
A_{m}
such that
A_{i_{1}}\xi^{\prime}_{14},\ldots,A_{i_{d}}\xi^{\prime}_{14}
has maximal rank.

Consequently, we can write

\xi^{\prime}_{12}=\sum_{m=1}^{d}a_{m}A_{i_{m}}\xi^{\prime}_{14}

for
|a_{m}|\lesssim 1
. More precisely, we obtain a bound on
a_{m}
by writing

\begin{split}|a_{1}\det(A_{i_{1}}\xi_{12}^{\prime},A_{i_{2}}\xi_{14}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{i_{d}}\xi_{d}^{\prime})|&=|\det(\sum_{m=1}^{d}a_{m}A_{i_{m}}\xi_{14}^{\prime},A_{i_{2}}\xi_{14}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{i_{d}}\xi_{14}^{\prime})|\\
&\leq|\xi_{12}^{\prime}||A_{i_{2}}\xi_{14}^{\prime}|\ldots|A_{i_{d}}\xi_{14}^{\prime}|.\end{split}

This implies

|a_{1}|\leq\frac{|\xi_{12}^{\prime}||A_{i_{2}}\xi_{14}^{\prime}|\ldots|A_{i_{d}}\xi_{14}^{\prime}|}{|\det(A_{i_{1}}\xi_{14}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{i_{d}}\xi_{14}^{\prime})|},

and more generally,

|a_{j}|\leq\frac{|\xi_{12^{\prime}}|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1,k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{d}|A_{i_{k}}\xi^{\prime}_{14}|}{|\det(A_{i_{1}}\xi_{14}^{\prime},\ldots,A_{i_{d}}\xi_{14}^{\prime})|}.

The estimate depends on bounds for the eigenvalues of
A_{i_{j}}
as reflected in the numerator and the joint transversality of
A_{i_{j}}\xi_{14}^{\prime}
, as reflected in the denominator.

Then (
11
) implies

1=|\langle\xi^{\prime}_{12},\xi^{\prime}_{12}\rangle|=|\sum_{m=1}^{d}\langle\xi^{\prime}_{12},a_{m}A_{i_{m}}\xi^{\prime}_{14}\rangle|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{d}|a_{m}||\langle\xi^{\prime}_{12},A_{i_{m}}\xi^{\prime}_{14}\rangle|\ll 1,

which is a contradiction. This completes the proof of (
10
), from which (
4
) follows by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
∎

3.2.
Examples

First, we note that the scalar case
d=l=1
with
A_{1}=1\in\mathbb{R}
recovers the classical Córdoba–Fefferman square function estimate. In the complex case we have

A_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\quad A_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

By a change of basis, we can equivalently consider

B_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad B_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}.

B_{2}
describes a rotation by an angle of
\pi/2
, for which reason

\forall\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}:\,|\det(B_{1}\nu,B_{2}\nu)|=1.

This shows that Assumption
1.1
is satisfied such that Theorem
1.2
recovers the
L^{4}
-square function estimate for the complex parabola.

We turn to higher dimensions. Initially, one might guess that for
d=3
,
l=3
will suffice to satisfy Assumption
1.1
. This is not true:
Indeed, for any symmetric
A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
we have

\det(A_{1}\nu,A_{2}\nu,A_{3}\nu)=0

for some
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
. If
A_{1}
has a non-trivial kernel, then simply choose
\nu\in\text{ker}(A_{1})
. If
A_{1}
is invertible, then we have

\det(A_{1}\nu,A_{2}\nu,A_{3}\nu)=|A_{1}|\cdot\det(\nu,A_{1}^{-1}A_{2}\nu,A_{1}^{-1}A_{3}\nu).

With
A_{1}^{-1}A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
it must have a real eigenpair. Now choose
\nu
to be an eigenvector of
A_{1}^{-1}A_{2}
. Consequently, the above determinant vanishes.

This shows that in three and higher dimensions, we need more quadratic conditions. A possible choice are reflections and inversions: Consider the matrices
A^{(ij)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
i\neq j
:

(A^{(ij)}v)_{k}=\begin{cases}v_{j},\quad&k=i,\\
v_{i},\quad&k=j,\\
v_{k},\quad&\text{else},\end{cases}

and
A^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
:

(A^{(i)}v)_{k}=\begin{cases}-v_{k},\quad&k=i,\\
v_{k},\quad&\text{else}.\end{cases}.

Then considering the matrices
\{I_{d},A^{(ij)},A^{(i)}\}
we find the transversality assumption (
3
) to hold by elementary geometric considerations.

4.
Square function estimates for the conical extension

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
. Here we extend the approach of Guth–Wang–Zhang
[
8
]
to cones over quadratic manifolds, which satisfy Assumption
1.1
.
The proof consists of two steps. In the first step we establish a wave envelope estimate, which is based on the key Kakeya estimate. Introduce notations for
\tau\in\Theta_{s^{-2}}
,
s\leq r
:

(12)

U_{\tau,r^{2}}=\text{conv}\big(\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta_{r^{-2}},\\
\theta\subseteq 10\tau\end{subarray}}\theta^{*}\big),\quad S_{U}f=\big(\sum_{\theta\subseteq 10\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big|_{U}.

Below we tile
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with finitely overlapping translates of
U_{\tau,r^{2}}
, denoted with
U\parallel U_{\tau,r^{2}}
.
The Kakeya estimate reads as follows:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
r\gg 1
. The following estimate holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\big|\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{r^{-2}}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{r^{-1}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{s^{2}}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,r^{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}.

This gives rise to a two-scale wave envelope estimate, which will facilitate the induction-on-scales to show the claimed square function estimate.

For the key Kakeya estimate we need to understand the Minkowski sum

Lemma 4.2

Let

\begin{split}p^{\prime}&=h\Gamma_{Q}(\xi+\Delta\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}\mathbf{t}_{i}(\xi+\Delta\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{j}\mathbf{n}_{j}(\xi+\Delta\xi)\\

&\quad+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\partial^{2}\Gamma_{Q}(\xi)(\Delta\xi)_{i}(\Delta\xi)_{j}h+\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}\Delta\xi_{j}\partial_{j}\mathbf{t}_{i}(\xi)+\sum_{i,j}c_{j}(\Delta\xi)_{i}\partial_{i}\mathbf{n}_{j}(\xi).\end{split}

For the expressions in the second line note that for

\begin{split}&\quad h\mathbf{c}(\eta_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}(p,\eta_{1})\mathbf{t}_{i}(\eta_{1})+\sum_{j=1}^{l}c_{j}(p,\eta_{1})\mathbf{n}_{j}(\eta_{1})\\
&=h\mathbf{c}(\eta_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\ell_{i}(p,\eta_{2})\mathbf{t}_{i}(\eta_{2})+\sum_{j=1}^{l}c_{j}(p,\eta_{2})\mathbf{n}_{j}(\eta_{2})\end{split}

and the fact that
\ell_{i}(p,\eta_{j})\sim\mu r^{-1}\sigma
,
j=1,2
implies together with Lemma
4.6
that
|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|\lesssim r^{-1}\sigma^{-1}
. Since the distance for distinct
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathcal{R}(r^{-2}\sigma^{-2})
is comparable to
r^{-1}\sigma^{-1}
this shows that
\#\mathcal{M}_{i,\mu}=\mathcal{O}(1)
as claimed. The proof in case
|h|\ll\sigma^{2}
is complete.

It remains to check the case
|h|\sim\sigma^{2}
. With the finite overlap of
\mathbf{CP}_{\sigma}
for
|h|\sim\sigma^{2}
, which follows from the centered planks
\mathbf{CP}_{\sigma}
at height
|h|\sim\sigma^{2}
comprising a canonical covering of the

-neighborhood of
\{h\mathbf{c}(\xi):|\xi|\leq 1\}
, we can establish Lemma
4.5
.
∎

We are ready to complete the proof of Proposition
4.1
:

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Decompose the Fourier support of
|f|^{2}
into
\Omega_{\sigma}
like in (
13
).
Next, sort
\theta
in (
13
) into centered planks of scale
\sigma

\theta(\xi)\in\Theta(\sigma,\eta)
for

Invoking Corollary
4.4
only the

\omega\in 10\Theta(\sigma,\eta)\end{subarray}}\big|\sum_{\theta\in\Theta(\sigma,\eta)}\widehat{|f_{\theta}|^{2}}(\omega)\big|\big|^{2}.

At this point, by Lemma
4.5
we can apply Cauchy-Schwarz without essential loss:

\int_{\Omega_{\sigma}}\big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Theta(\sigma,\xi)\in\mathbf{CP}_{\sigma},\\

∎

4.2.
Induction on scales

With the Kakeya, or wave envelope estimate, at hand, the proof of the square function estimate is carried out making use of the following two-scale-quantity:

Definition 4.7
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
with
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{R^{-1}}(\mathcal{C}M_{Q})
. We define
S(r,R)
as smallest constant such that

\sum_{B_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}|B_{r}|^{-1}\|S_{B_{r}}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\leq S(r,R)\sum_{R^{-1}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{s}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,R}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}.

Theorem
1.4
is a consequence of the following:

Proposition 4.8
.

It holds

S(1,R)\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}.

Before we turn to the proof of the wave envelope estimate, we argue how it implies the square function estimate:

Proof of (Proposition
4.8

\Rightarrow
Theorem
1.4
).

By the essentially constant property it holds for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
with
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{R^{-1}}(\mathcal{C}M_{Q})\subseteq B_{n+1}(0,1)
that

\|f\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}^{4}\lesssim\sum_{B_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{1}}|B_{1}|^{-1}\|S_{B_{1}}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(B_{1})}.

Applying the hypothesis yields

\|f\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\sum_{R^{-1}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{s}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,R}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}.

An application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,R}}|U|^{-1}\big(\int_{U}\sum_{\theta\subseteq 10\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\big(\sum_{\theta\subseteq 10\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}.

Next, applying the embedding
\ell^{1}\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{s}}\big(\sum_{\theta\subseteq 10\tau}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{R}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}.

Carrying out the dyadic sum over
s
incurs a logarithmic loss in
R
, which yields, subsuming the above estimates,

R^{\varepsilon}\sum_{R^{-1}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{s}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,R}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}\lesssim R^{\varepsilon}\log(R)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{R}}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}dx.

The proof is complete.

∎

In the proof of Proposition
4.8
the following reformulation of Proposition
4.1
, making use of the locally constant property, plays an important role:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
1\leq r\leq r^{2}\leq R
. It holds

S(r,r^{2})\lesssim 1.

Moreover, we have the following inequality which relates two different scales via a generalized Lorentz transformation:

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq r_{3}
. Then the following estimate holds:

(21)

S(r_{1},r_{3})\lesssim\log(r_{2})S(r_{1},r_{2})\max_{s\in[r_{2}^{-1},1]}S(sr_{2},sr_{3}).

We digress for a moment to explain the key features of the generalized Lorentz transformation. Consider

\mathcal{C}\Gamma=\{(\xi,\frac{Q(\xi)}{h},h)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times\mathbb{R}:\,\xi\in B_{d}(0,1),\,h\in[1/2,1]\}

with
Q(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}(\langle\xi,A_{1}\xi\rangle,\ldots,\langle\xi,A_{l}\xi\rangle)
,

. We denote subsets with base frequencies in an
s
-sector by

\mathcal{C}\Gamma_{\tau,s}=\{(\xi,Q(\xi)/h,h):\xi\in B_{d}(0,1),\;\big|\frac{\xi}{h}-\xi_{\tau}\big|\leq s,\;h\in[1/2,1]\}.

Let
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
with
\text{supp}(\hat{g})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{r^{-2}}(\mathcal{C}\Gamma_{\tau,s})
,
s^{-1}\leq r
.
The generalized Lorentz transformation is supposed to enlarge the

\begin{split}\Lambda_{\tau,s}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{l}\times[1/2,1]\ni&(\xi,\zeta,h)\mapsto(\eta=s^{-1}(\xi-h\xi_{\tau}),\\
&(s^{-2}\zeta_{i}-s^{-1}\langle\eta,A_{i}\xi_{\tau}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}hs^{-2}\langle\xi_{\tau},A_{i}\xi_{\tau}\rangle)_{i=1,\ldots,l},h).\end{split}

We have
\Lambda_{\tau,s}(\mathcal{C}\Gamma_{\tau,s})\approx\mathcal{C}\Gamma
and moreover,
\Lambda_{\tau,s}(\mathcal{N}_{r^{-2}}(\mathcal{C}\Gamma_{\tau,s}))\approx\mathcal{N}_{r^{-2}s^{-2}}(\mathcal{C}\Gamma)
.

The classical case corresponds to

,
Q(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\xi,\xi\rangle
; see
[
8
, Section 5]
.

Proof.

In the first step we use the definition of

:

\sum_{B_{r_{1}}\in\mathcal{B}_{r_{1}}}|B_{r_{1}}|^{-1}\|S_{B_{r_{1}}}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(B_{r_{1}})}\leq S(r_{1},r_{2})\sum_{r_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{s^{2}}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,r_{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}.

We use the generalized Lorentz transformation
\Lambda_{\tau,s}
centered at
\xi_{\tau}
at scale
s
.
The tiny subsectors

, which constitute

, are mapped to the larger sectors

Via the generalized Lorentz transformation
\Lambda_{\tau,s}
we find that
\tau
is mapped essentially to the full cone and the
r_{3}^{-1}
-neighborhood of
\mathcal{C}\Gamma_{\tau,s}
is mapped to the
s^{-2}r_{3}^{-1}
-neighborhood of the full cone. Moreover, by definition it is easy to see that

.

This implies that

\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,r_{2}^{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}\approx\sum_{B_{s^{2}r_{2}}\in\mathcal{B}_{s^{2}r_{2}}}|U|^{-1}|J\Lambda_{\tau}|^{2}\|S_{B_{s^{2}r_{2}}}\overline{f}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(B_{s^{2}r_{2}})}.

Above

denotes the Jacobian incurred from the change of variables

and

denotes the function obtained from pulling back the Fourier transform of

with

\hat{\bar{f}}=\hat{f}\circ(\Lambda_{\tau,s})^{-1}

Now we can estimate the expression on the different scales

,
s^{2}r_{3}
to obtain

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{B_{s^{2}r_{2}}\in\mathcal{B}_{s^{2}r_{2}}}|B_{s^{2}r_{2}}|^{-1}\|S_{B_{s^{2}r_{2}}}\bar{f}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(B_{s^{2}r_{2}})}\\
&\leq S(s^{2}r_{2},s^{2}r_{3})\sum_{(s^{2}r_{3})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sigma\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{\sigma^{2}}}\sum_{V\parallel V_{\tau,\sigma^{2}}}|V|^{-1}\|S_{V}\bar{f}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(V)}.\end{split}

Undoing the change of variables by the properties of the generalized Lorentz transformation, we find

\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,r_{2}^{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}\lesssim S(s^{2}r_{2},s^{2}r_{3})\sum_{r_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sigma\leq s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau^{\prime}\in\Theta_{\sigma^{2}},\\
\tau^{\prime}\subseteq\tau\end{subarray}}\sum_{V\parallel V_{\tau^{\prime},\sigma^{2}}}|V|^{-1}\|S_{V}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(V)}.

This allows us to write

\begin{split}&\quad\sum_{r_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq s\leq 1}\sum_{\zeta\in\Theta_{s^{2}}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,s^{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{r_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq s\leq 1}S(s^{2}r_{2},s^{2}r_{3})\sum_{\zeta\in\Theta_{s^{2}}}\sum_{r_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sigma\leq s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Theta_{\sigma^{2}},\\
\tau\subseteq\zeta\end{subarray}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,\sigma^{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}\\
&\lesssim\log(r_{2})\max_{s\in[r_{2}^{-1},1]}S(sr_{2},sr_{3})\sum_{r_{3}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sigma\leq 1}\sum_{\tau\in\Theta_{\sigma^{2}}}\sum_{U\parallel U_{\tau,\sigma^{2}}}|U|^{-1}\|S_{U}f\|^{4}_{L^{2}(U)}.\end{split}

The logarithmic loss comes from counting

in the inner sum by the outer sum over

.
In conclusion, having redenoted

, we obtain

S(r_{1},r_{3})\lesssim\log(r_{2})S(r_{1},r_{2})\max_{r_{2}^{-1}\leq s\leq 1}S(sr_{2},sr_{3}).

∎

The starting point of the induction on scales will be the square function estimates for quadratic manifolds. Choose
K\gg 1
to be determined later and divide the height parameter into intervals of length

. We write

\mathcal{C}_{K}M_{Q}=\{(\xi,M_{Q}(\xi)/h,h)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:\xi\in B_{d}(0,1),\quad h\in[a,a+K^{-1}]\}

for some

and define the modified quantity

analogous to the above with the difference that we are considering

,
\text{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta}(\mathcal{C}_{K}M_{Q})
.

Note that we gloss over the dependence on

as the following considerations are uniformly valid in

.

We have by simple decomposition of the Fourier support:

(22)

S(r,R)\lesssim KS_{K}(r,R).

The following lemma explains the introduction of the parameter

:

Proposition 4.11
.

The following estimate holds for

This is a variant of the square function estimate proved in Theorem

. Note that trivially Lemmas

We explain the proof of Proposition
4.11
. The key ingredient is the following bilinear Kakeya estimate:

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
\theta_{i}(\xi_{i})\in\Theta_{K^{-1}}
be two conical sectors with separation of the base frequencies
|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|\sim s\gg K^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then we have for
\text{supp}(\widehat{f_{\theta_{i}}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\frac{1}{K}}(\mathcal{C}\Gamma_{\frac{1}{K}})\cap\theta_{i}

\sum_{B_{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}\in\mathcal{B}_{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}}\int_{B_{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}}|f_{\theta_{1}}f_{\theta_{2}}|^{2}\lesssim s^{-d}\sum_{B_{K}\in\mathcal{B}_{K}}|B_{K}|^{-1}\int|f_{\theta_{1}}|^{2}w_{B_{K}}\int|f_{\theta_{2}}|^{2}w_{B_{K}}.

Proof.

The argument rests on a wave packet decomposition:

f_{\theta_{i}}=\sum_{T_{i}\parallel\tilde{\theta}_{i}^{*}}f_{\theta_{i},T_{i}}

with
\tilde{\theta}_{i}^{*}
being a rectangle of size

\underbrace{K^{\frac{1}{2}}\times\ldots\times K^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{d\text{ components in }\mathbf{t}_{j}(\xi_{i})}\times\underbrace{K\times\ldots\times K}_{l\text{ components in }\mathbf{n}_{j}(\xi_{i})}\times K.

Denote
\sup_{x\in T_{i}}|f_{\theta_{i},T_{i}}(x)|=c_{T_{i}}
and recall that
|f_{\theta_{i},T_{i}}(x)|\sim|f_{\theta_{i},T_{i}}(y)|
for

.

We have to compute the size of the intersection of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. To this end, we take advantage of the quantitative transversality of
\langle\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{1}),\ldots,\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{1})\rangle
and
\langle\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{2}),\ldots,\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{2})\rangle
. We write

\mathbf{t}_{i}(\xi_{2})=\mathbf{t}_{i}(\xi_{1})+\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\xi_{2}-\xi_{1})_{j}\partial_{j}\mathbf{t}_{i}(\xi_{1})

to find by elimination that

\begin{split}&\quad\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{1})\wedge\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{2})\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{2})\\
&=\mathbf{t}_{1}(\xi_{1})\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathbf{t}_{d}(\xi_{1})\wedge\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
(A_{1}\Delta\xi)_{1}\\
\vdots\\
(A_{l}\Delta\xi)_{1}\end{pmatrix}\wedge\ldots\wedge\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
(A_{1}\Delta\xi)_{d}\\
\vdots\\
(A_{l}\Delta\xi)_{d}\end{pmatrix}.\end{split}

We arrange the vectors in an

-matrix:

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0&0&\ldots&0\\

\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&0&\vdots&0&\vdots\\

\partial_{1}q_{1}(\xi_{1})&\partial_{2}q_{1}(\xi_{1})&\ldots&\partial_{d}q_{1}(\xi_{1})&\,&(A_{1}\Delta\xi)^{t}&\,\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\,&\vdots&\,\\

By the transversality assumption (

\geq c|\Delta\xi|^{d}\gtrsim s^{d}
. This yields the estimate:

|T_{1}\cap T_{2}|\sim s^{-d}K^{\frac{d}{2}}K^{\frac{d}{2}}K^{l-d+1}\sim s^{-d}K^{l+1}\sim s^{-d}\frac{|T_{1}||T_{2}|}{|B_{K}|}.

For this reason we can conclude

\begin{split}\sum_{B_{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}\subseteq B_{K}}\int_{B_{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}}|f_{\theta_{1}}f_{\theta_{2}}|^{2}&\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}B_{K^{\frac{1}{2}}}\subseteq B_{K},\\

&\lesssim s^{-d}|B_{K}|^{-1}\big(\int_{B_{K}}\sum_{T_{1}\parallel T_{\tilde{\theta_{1}^{*}}}}c_{T_{1}}^{2}\chi_{T_{1}}\big)\big(\int_{B_{K}}\sum_{T_{2}\parallel T_{\tilde{\theta_{2}^{*}}}}c_{T_{2}}^{2}\chi_{T_{2}}\big)\\
&\lesssim s^{-d}|B_{K}|^{-1}\int|f_{\theta_{1}}|^{2}w_{B_{K}}\int|f_{\theta_{2}}|^{2}w_{B_{K}}.\end{split}

Proof.

]

4.11

\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
be graph parametrizations of quadratic functions:

\gamma_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d},q_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})),\quad q_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})=\frac{\langle s,A_{i}s\rangle}{2},\quad A_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

We assume the non-degeneracy and symmetry conditions:

(27)

\det(A_{i})\neq 0,\quad A_{i}=A_{i}^{t}.

In addition we suppose the transversality assumption for the inverted quadratic forms:

There is
c>0
such that for any
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
there are
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{d}\leq m
:

(28)

|\det(A_{i_{1}}^{-1}\nu,\ldots,A_{i_{d}}^{-1}\nu)|\geq c>0.

The multi-parameter averages we consider are given by

(29)

\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x)=\int f(x-\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\gamma_{i}(s_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,s_{d}^{(i)}))\chi(s^{(1)})\ldots\chi(s^{(m)})ds^{(1)}\ldots ds^{(m)}

with
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{d}(0,1))
.

In the simplest non-trivial case,
d=1
,
\gamma(s)=(s,s^{2})
we have for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

(30)

\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{t}f(x)=\int f(x-t(s,s^{2}))\chi(s)ds,

which is closely related to the propagation of the wave equation in
2+1
-dimensions. Indeed, the wave propagation involves averages over spheres. For comparison recall the sharp local smoothing estimate for the wave equation in
2+1
-dimensions proved by Guth–Wang–Zhang
[
8
]
, which reads at the endpoint:

\|\chi_{1}(t)\mathcal{A}^{(1)}_{t}f\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}))}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{4}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

For technical convenience we consider
\chi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(1/2,3/2)
.

Presently, we consider the following extension of (
30
) given in (
6
):

\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{t}f(x)=\int f(x-t_{1}\gamma_{1}(s^{(1)})-t_{2}\gamma_{2}(s^{(2)}))\chi(s^{(1)})\chi(s^{(2)})ds^{(1)}ds^{(2)}

with

\gamma_{1}(s)=(s_{1},s_{2},s_{1}^{2}-s_{2}^{2}),\quad\gamma_{2}(s)=(s_{1},s_{2},2s_{1}s_{2}).

The local smoothing estimate claimed in (
7
) reads:

\|\chi_{1}(t_{1})\chi_{1}(t_{2})\mathcal{A}^{(2)}_{t}f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{4}_{-2+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

The proof proceeds in three steps:

•

we obtain an oscillatory integral representation, which generalizes the FIO representation for (
30
),

•

the multi-parameter FIO representation satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.4
such that we can apply a square function estimate,

•

after invoking duality, the proof is concluded using maximal function estimates, which extend the scalar case.

Most of the arguments are valid under the general assumptions given in (
27
) and (
28
). It is merely for our proof of the maximal function estimate in the last step we use a more specific property of the two-parameter average in (
6
). Possibly, a different proof of the maximal function estimate in Section
5.3
could rely only on (
27
) and (
28
), which naturally appear several times in the argument. Most of the arguments below are carried out for the outset described by (
29
) under assumptions (
27
) and (
28
).

5.1.
Oscillatory integral representation

We use Fourier inversion and Fubini’s theorem to write

\begin{split}&\quad\int f(x-\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\gamma_{i}(s_{1}^{(i)},\ldots,s_{d}^{(i)}))\chi(s^{(1)})\ldots\chi(s^{(m)})ds^{(1)}\ldots ds^{(m)}\\
&=\int e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\hat{f}(\xi)\big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\int e^{-it_{i}\gamma_{i}(s^{(i)})\cdot\xi}\chi(s^{(i)})ds^{(i)}\big)d\xi.\end{split}

By Littlewood-Paley decomposition, it suffices to show a frequency-localized estimate for
N\gg 1
:

\|\mathcal{A}_{t}(P_{N}f)\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon+s}\|f\|_{L^{4}_{x}},

where
s=-2
under the assumptions of Theorem
1.5
. Indeed, the small frequencies are estimated by simple kernel estimates.

We compute the asymptotics of the
s
-integrals by the following proposition, in which we recall the asymptotics of parameter-dependent stationary phase as described in
[
16
, Corollary 1.1.8]
:

Suppose that
\Psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}
,
(s,\xi)\mapsto\Psi(s,\xi)
is a smooth function, which satisfies for
\xi\in U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\nabla_{s}\Psi(\xi,s_{\xi})=0

and

\det\partial^{2}_{s}\Psi(\xi,s_{\xi})\neq 0.

Then, by the implicit function theorem there is a smooth function
s(\xi)
such that

\nabla_{s}\Psi(\xi,s(\xi))=0.

Consider the oscillatory integral

I(\xi,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{it\Psi(s,\xi)}a(t,s,\xi)ds

with
a
having small enough
\xi
-support such that
s(\xi)
is the only stationary point for
\xi
and
a
satisfies the symbol bounds:

|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{s}^{\beta}a(t,\xi,s)|\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-|\alpha|}.

We have the following:

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
a
and
\Psi
are as above. Then for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we have

\big|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big(e^{-it\Psi(\xi,s)}I(\xi,t)\big)\big|\leq C_{\alpha\gamma}(1+|t|)^{-\frac{n}{2}-\alpha}.

In the following let
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. We apply the above proposition to the phase function:

\Phi_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d},\xi)=\gamma_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{d})\cdot\xi=\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_{i}\xi_{i}+q_{i}(s)\xi_{d+1}.

Note that for
|\xi_{d+1}|\ll|\xi^{\prime}|
there is no stationary point and we have rapid decay:

\big|\int e^{-it_{i}\Phi_{i}(s,\xi)}\chi(s)ds\big|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-N}.

Hence, the essential contribution stems from
|\xi_{d+1}|\sim|\xi|\sim N\gg 1
. We have the following:

Lemma 5.2
.

For
|\xi_{d+1}|\sim|\xi|\sim N\gg 1
and
t\sim 1
we have the following:

\int e^{-it\gamma_{i}(s^{(i)})\cdot\xi}\chi(s^{(i)})ds^{(i)}=e^{it\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1}}a_{i}(t,\xi)

with

|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a_{i}(t,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{-\frac{d}{2}-|\beta|}

Proof.

We compute the stationary point:

\nabla_{s}\Phi_{i}(s^{*},\xi)=0\Leftrightarrow\xi^{\prime}+2A_{i}(s^{*})\xi_{d+1}=0.

Hence, by the non-degeneracy of
A_{i}
, we find

s^{*}=-A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}/2\xi_{d+1}.

We obtain

\Phi_{i}(s^{*},\xi)=-\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/2\xi_{d+1}+\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1}=-\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1}.

We obtain the expansion (see Proposition
5.1
)

\int e^{-it_{i}\gamma_{i}(s^{(i)})\cdot\xi}\chi(s^{(i)})ds^{(i)}=e^{it_{i}\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1}}a_{i}(t,\xi)\text{ for }|\xi_{d+1}|\gtrsim|\xi^{\prime}|\gtrsim 1

with

|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a_{i}(t,\xi)|\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

∎

Let
\beta\in C^{\infty}_{c}((B_{d+1}(0,2)\backslash B_{d+1}(0,1/2))
. Plugging in the expansion of the integrals in
s
we have obtained a multi-parameter Fourier integral operator after microlocalization:

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{t}P_{N}f(x)&=\int e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\beta_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\big(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\int e^{-it_{i}\gamma_{i}(s^{(i)})\cdot\xi}\chi(s^{(i)})ds^{(i)}\big)d\xi\\
&=\int e^{i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1})}a(t,\xi)\beta_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi.\end{split}

Now we expand
a
in a Fourier series in
t

a(t,\xi)=\tilde{\chi}(t)\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}e^{i\langle k,t\rangle}\hat{a}(k,\xi)

for a suitable cutoff
\tilde{\chi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}
and absorb
a
into
\beta
to dominate

|\mathcal{A}_{t}P_{N}f|(x)\lesssim N^{-\frac{md}{2}}\big|\int e^{i(\langle x,\xi\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1})}\tilde{\beta}_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\big|

for some
\tilde{\beta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{d+1}(0,2)\backslash B_{d+1}(0,1/2))
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\Phi(x,t;\xi)=\langle x,\xi\rangle+\sum_{i=1}^{m}t_{i}\langle A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle/4\xi_{d+1}

and focus in the following on the estimate

(31)

\|\chi(t)\mathcal{F}f\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{4}_{x}}\text{ with }\mathcal{F}f(t,x)=\int e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\beta_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi

and
\chi(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{1}(t_{i})
,
\chi_{1}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(1/2,3/2)
. Note that for Theorem
1.5
the smoothing of order
-2=md/2
stems from the symbol.

We summarize our findings in the following proposition:

Proposition 5.3
.

The multi-parameter local smoothing estimate (
7
) is implied by (
31
).

Consequently, with
|\xi^{\prime}|\lesssim|\xi_{d+1}|\sim N\gg 1
the Fourier transform of
\chi(t)\mathcal{F}f
in all variables is in the unit neighborhood of the graph
\Gamma\mathcal{M}_{q,N}
:

\xi\mapsto(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d+1},\frac{\langle A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle}{4\xi_{d+1}},\ldots,\frac{\langle A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle}{4\xi_{d+1}});\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\,|\xi_{d+1}|\sim|\xi|\sim N.

This is a multi-parameter cone over the base curve:

\xi^{\prime}\mapsto(\xi^{\prime},\frac{\langle A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle}{4},\ldots,\frac{\langle A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}\rangle}{4});\quad\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;|\xi^{\prime}|\lesssim N.

The transversality assumption (
28
) will allow us to invoke the square function estimates for cones over quadratic manifolds obtained in previous sections.

5.2.
Applying the square function estimate

By non-stationary phase and the compact parameter range
|t|\leq 1
, we can localize the support of
f
to unit size. More precisely, by Fourier inversion let
\mathcal{F}f=\int K_{N}(t,x-y)f(y)dy
. We obtain the following simple kernel estimate:

Lemma 5.4
.

For
|x|\gg 1
it holds the following estimate:

\chi(t)|K_{N}(t,x)|\lesssim_{N}(1+|x|)^{-N}.

Then, after rescaling
\xi\to\xi/N
,
x\to Nx
,
t\to Nx
and invoking translation invariance, it suffices to show the smoothing estimate:

\big\|\int e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\chi(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(B_{n}(0,N))}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\frac{m}{4}+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{4}_{x}}.

Above
n=d+1+m
,
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{d+1}(0,2))
with
|\xi_{d+1}|\sim|\xi^{\prime}|\sim 1
for
(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d+1})\in\text{supp}(\chi)
. We dominate

\big\|\int e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\chi(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(B_{d+1+m}(0,N))}\lesssim\big\|\chi_{N}(t,x)\int e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\chi(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

with
\text{supp}(\widehat{\chi_{N}})\subseteq B(0,c/N)
. The function
F_{N}(t,x)=\chi_{N}(t,x)\int e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\chi(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi
satisfies
\text{supp}(\widehat{F_{N}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\frac{c}{N}}(\Gamma\mathcal{M}_{q,1})
.

Consequently, we may apply Theorem
1.4
to obtain

\|F_{N}\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\big\|\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\frac{1}{N}}}|F_{N\theta}|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

with
\Theta_{\frac{1}{N}}=\Theta_{\frac{1}{N}}(\Gamma\mathcal{M}_{q,1})
denoting the canonical covering of the
1/N
-neighborhood of
\Gamma\mathcal{M}_{q,1}
introduced in the previous sections.

We reverse the scaling and by standard arguments, we reduce to establishing the estimate:

(32)

\big\|\chi_{1}(t)\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{\frac{1}{N}}}\big|\int e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\chi_{\theta}(\xi)\beta_{N}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\big|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L^{4}}

with

\big|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{d+1}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\big|\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\text{ for }\xi\in\text{supp}(\chi_{\theta}).

5.3.
Conclusion via maximal function estimates

The frequency projection to
\theta
reduces the propagation with
\mathcal{F}
to a translation. By a duality argument, we reduce (
32
) to a maximal function estimate involving averages over the translations induced by
\mathcal{F}
on
\theta
-frequencies.
The proof of (
32
) becomes a variant of the arguments in
[
13
]
.

5.3.1.
Reduction to a maximal function estimate

Squaring the left hand-side we find by duality

(33)

\begin{split}&\quad\int\big(\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_{N}}\big|\int_{\theta:|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{d+1}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}|\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}}e^{i\Phi(x,t;\xi)}\chi(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\big|^{2}\big)^{2}dxdt\\
&=\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{2}}=1}\sum_{\theta}\big|\iint K_{\theta}(x,t;y)f_{\theta}(y)dy\big|^{2}\;\bar{g}(x,t)dxdt\end{split}

And further

(34)

\begin{split}\eqref{eq:MaximalFunctionReductionI}&\lesssim\sup_{\|g\|_{2}=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}(y)\big\{\iint|K_{\theta}(x,t;y)||g(x,t)|dxdt\big\}dy\\
&\lesssim\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\big(\sum_{\theta}|f_{\theta}|^{2}\big)^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}|\sup_{\theta}\iint|K_{\theta}(x,t;y)||g(x,t)|dxdt|^{2}dy\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|f\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\|\sup_{\theta}|A_{\theta}g|(y)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.\end{split}

In the ultimate estimate we have set for
\theta\in\Theta_{N}
:

A_{\theta}g(y)=\iint K_{\theta}(x,t;y)g(x,t)dxdt

and used a standard square function estimate in
L^{4}
. This square function estimate is a variant of Littlewood-Paley orthogonality.

We lay the last brick in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, which is the following lemma:

Lemma 5.5
.

It holds

\|\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_{N}}|A_{\theta}g|(y)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{x,t}(\mathbb{R}^{5})}.

Indeed, plugging the key identity into (
34
) we obtain

\eqref{eq:MaximalFunctionReductionII}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{4}}.

This will complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

To carry out the proof of Lemma
5.5
, we take a look at the kernel:

K_{\theta}(t,x;y)=\int e^{i(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle+t_{1}\frac{\langle\xi^{\prime},A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}\rangle}{\xi_{d+1}}+\ldots+t_{m}\frac{\langle\xi^{\prime},A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}\rangle}{\xi_{d+1}})}\chi_{\theta}(\xi)\beta(|\xi|/N)d\xi

with
\big|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{d+1}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}|\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
|\xi_{d+1}|\sim N
within the support of
\chi_{\theta}
. We obtain for the phase function for
\xi\in\text{supp}(\chi_{\theta})\cap\text{supp}(\beta_{N})
:

\begin{split}\Phi(x-y,\xi)&=\langle x-y,\xi\rangle+t_{1}(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+\ldots\\
&\quad+t_{m}(2A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+\mathcal{O}(|t|N^{-1}|\xi|).\end{split}

This means that the averaging over
|K_{\theta}|
as in
A_{\theta}
describes an averaging over
2A_{i}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}
(parametrized by
t_{i}
) in directions
x^{\prime}
and
-\langle A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle-\ldots-\langle A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle
in direction
x_{d+1}
.

We define the localized operators:

A_{\theta}^{N,k}g(y)=C\iint e^{i\bar{\Phi}(x-y,\xi)}\beta(|\xi|/N)\beta\big(2^{-2k}N\big|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{d+1}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\big|^{2}\big)\hat{g}(\xi,t)d\xi dt

with

\begin{split}\bar{\Phi}(x-y,\xi)&=\langle x-y,\xi\rangle+t_{1}(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi\\
&\quad+t_{2}(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+\ldots+t_{m}(2A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle A_{m}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi.\end{split}

The summation over
\{k:2^{k}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
will incur a logarithmic loss in
N
provided that we obtain uniform bounds in
k
. To obtain an estimate for

\sup_{\theta}|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g|^{2}(y),

we use Sobolev embedding to reduce to an estimate for
\|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g\|_{L^{2}_{\theta}L^{2}_{y}}
.

The following computations are restricted to the special case considered in Theorem
1.5
. For Sobolev embedding, we compute the
\theta
-frequencies for
A_{\theta}^{N,k}g
:

\begin{split}&\hskip 9.24994pt\int A_{\theta}^{N,k}g(y)e^{-i\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\tau}d\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\\
&=\int e^{i(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle+t_{1}(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+t_{2}(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi-i\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\tau)}\beta_{N,k}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi)d\xi d\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}.\end{split}

We compute the stationary point of the phase in
\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}
as

\psi(\xi^{\prime}_{\theta})=t_{1}(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+t_{2}(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi)-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\cdot\tau

and

\begin{split}\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}}\psi(\xi^{\prime}_{\theta})&=t_{1}(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}-2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\xi_{3})+t_{2}(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}-2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\xi_{3})-\tau\\
&=t_{1}\xi_{3}(2A_{1}^{-1}\big(\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{3}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\big))+t_{2}\xi_{3}(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}-2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\xi_{3})-\tau.\end{split}

Keeping in mind that
|t_{i}|\lesssim 1
,
|\xi_{3}|\sim N
, and
\big|\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{3}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\big|\lesssim N^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{k}
, we have for
|\tau|\gg N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}2^{k}
that
|\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}}\psi(\xi^{\prime}_{\theta})|\sim|\tau|
. This gives rapid decay for
|\tau|\gg N^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}2^{k}
.

With essential boundedness of the frequencies by
\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{k}
an application of Sobolev embedding
W^{1,2+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
gives

(35)

\|\sup_{\theta}|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g|^{2}(y)\|_{L^{1}_{y}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}N2^{2k}\|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\theta}L^{2}_{y}}.

We turn to the key estimate for
\|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g(y)\|_{L^{2}_{y}L^{2}_{\theta}}
by rewriting the square as:

\begin{split}&\hskip 9.24994pt\big\|\int e^{i(\langle x-y,\xi\rangle+t_{1}(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+t_{2}(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi)}\beta_{N,k}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi,t)d\xi\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{y,t}L^{2}_{\theta}}\\
&=\int\iint H_{N,k}(t,t^{\prime})\hat{g}(\xi,t)\hat{\bar{g}}(\xi,t^{\prime})dtdt^{\prime}d\xi\end{split}

with

H_{N,k}(t,t^{\prime};\xi)=\int e^{i(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime})(2A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{1}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi+i(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})(2A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},-\langle\xi^{\prime}_{\theta},A_{2}^{-1}\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\rangle)\cdot\xi}|\beta_{N,k}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}.

The behavior of the kernel is again computed by non-stationary phase. First we consider
k\geq 1
. It holds on the support of
\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}
for fixed
\xi
:

\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}}\psi(\xi^{\prime}_{\theta})=\{2(t_{1}-t_{1}^{\prime})A_{1}^{-1}\big(\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{3}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\big)+2(t_{2}-t_{2}^{\prime})A_{2}^{-1}\big(\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi_{3}}-\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}\big)\}\xi_{3},

which implies

|\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}_{\theta}}\psi|\sim N^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{k}|t-t^{\prime}|.

We obtain by repeated integration by parts for
M\geq 1
:

|H_{N,k}(t,t^{\prime};\xi)|\lesssim_{M}(N^{-\frac{1}{2}}2^{k})^{2}(1+N^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{k}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-M}.

We apply Young’s convolution inequality to find

\|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g(y)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{y}L^{2}_{\theta}}\lesssim N^{-2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{y}}.

Plugging this estimate into (
35
) we find

\|\sup_{\theta}|A_{\theta}^{N,k}g|^{2}(y)\|_{L^{1}_{y}}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{y,t}}.

Summation over
\{k:2^{k}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
incurs another logarithmic loss. It remains to note the estimate for
k=0
given by

|H_{N,k}(t,t^{\prime};\xi)|\lesssim N^{-1}

from disregarding the phase function entirely. The proof of Lemma
5.5
is complete.

Area and volume from entangled qubits
Juan M. Romero, Emiliano Montoya-González

Abstract
In this paper a relation between entangled states and geometry is studied. In particular, the area of 2 ​ D 2D parallelogram is obtained from an entangled 4 4 -qubit state. In addition, the vector area of a 3 ​ D 3D parallelogram is derived from entangled 6 6 -qubit states. Moreover, the volume of a 3 ​ D 3D parallelepiped is deduced from an entangled 9 9 -qubit state. Furthermore, it has been provided the quantum circuit in qiskit code for these entangled states. It is worth mentioning that parallelograms and parallelepipeds serve as fundamental building blocks for more sophisticated geometric structures. Keywords : Geometry; Qubits; Entangled States; Quantum Computing.



1
Introduction

Recently, geometric quantities have been related to entangled states. For instance, in the context of the
AdS/CFT
correspondence, it has been shown that
the entangled entropy of the
CFT
is related to black-hole entropy, which is proportional to the area of black-hole event horizon
[
1
]
. Other works about this subject can be seen in
[
2
]
.
Also, within the context of the
AdS/CFT
correspondence, a relation proposed between the Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen correlations of entangled states and the geometry of Einstein-Rosen bridges (wormholes)
[
3
]
, known as the
EPR=ER
conjecture. Quantum computing is another area in which where geometry can be derived from quantum states, particularly from entangled states.
It is worth mentioning that quantum algorithm can be represented graphically as quantum circuits, which are ordered sequences of interconnected quantum gates (unitary matrices)
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
.
In this respect, an important quantity is the quantum circuit complexity
[
7
]
, defined as the minimal size of any circuit that implements a given unitary. Notably, a geometry can be obtained from quantum circuit complexity
[
8
]
, and some of theses geometries can be related to systems such as black-holes
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
Other interesting papers about geometry of entangled states can be seen in
[
13
]
. Then, for these results, different authors have suggested that the space-time can be obtained from entangled states.

In this paper, it is shown that the area of a parallelogram and the volume of a parallelepiped can be derived from entangled states. In order to deduce these results,
the area and volume are written as multilinear polynomials. Subsequently, the relation between entangled qubits and multilinear polynomials, that was shown in
[
14
]
, is employed. Then, the area of
2D
parallelogram is obtained from an entangled
4
-qubit state. Moreover, the vector area of a
3D
parallelogram is derived from entangled
6
-qubit states. In addition, the volume of a
3D
parallelepiped is deduced from an entangled
9
-qubit state. Furthermore, it has been provided the quantum circuit in qiskit code for these entangled states.

It is worth mentioning that parallelograms and parallelepipeds serve as fundamental building blocks for more sophisticated geometric structures. Then, it is possible that by using the results of this paper more sophisticated geometric structures can be studied.

This paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
, a brief review of the relation between multilinear polynomials and states of qubit is provide. In Sec.
3
, it is shown that the area of a parallelogram and the volume of a parallelepiped can be written as multilinear polynomials. In Sec.
4
, the area of a parallelogram and volume of a parallelepiped are obtained from entangled qubits. Finally, in Sec.
5
, a summary is given.

2
Multilinear Polynomials

In this section multilinear polynomials and their relation to multiple qubits are reviewed.

Now, notice that if

\displaystyle c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}

(1)

are complex constants, the following bilinear polynomial can be constructed

\displaystyle P(x,y)=c_{1}+c_{2}x+c_{3}y+c_{4}xy.

(2)

In addition, in a two-qubit system, by using the constants (
1
) a quantum state can be constructed as follows:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}=c_{0}\ket{00}+c_{1}\ket{01}+c_{2}\ket{10}+c_{12}\ket{11}.

(3)

Then, for each bilinear polynomial (
2
) a two-qubit state (
3
) can be constructed.
In particular, a non-factorable bilinear polynomial can be related to entangled two qubit and some phenomena such as teleportation can be modelled with multilinear polynomials
[
14
]
.

Notice that if the equation

\displaystyle|c_{1}|^{2}+|c_{2}|^{2}+|c_{3}|^{2}+|c_{4}|^{2}=1

is satisfied, the state (
3
) is normalized.

In general, a multilinear polynomials of
n
variables can be written as sum of
2^{n}
terms, like

\displaystyle P(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=c_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq n}c_{i_{1}i_{2}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}+\cdots+

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}c_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}+\cdots+c_{123\cdots n}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\cdots x_{n}.

(4)

Moreover, a basis of system of
n
qubits must have
2^{n}
states. Then, in this system every state is a linear combination of
2^{n}
states.
In particular, by using the constants of the multlinear polynomial (
4
), the following state can be constructed

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{0}\ket{00\cdots 0}+c_{1}\ket{0\cdots 01}+c_{2}\ket{0\cdots 10}+\cdots+c_{n}\ket{10\cdots 0}+

(5)

\displaystyle+c_{12}\ket{0\cdots 011}+c_{13}\ket{0\cdots 0101}+\cdots c_{n-1n}\ket{110\cdots 0}+\cdots+

\displaystyle+c_{123\cdots n}\ket{11\cdots 1}.

Therefore, each multilinear polynomial (
4
) is related to
n
-qubit state (
5
).

3
Geometry

Some geometric quantities can be expressed as multilinear polynomials.
For example, if a parallelogram is built with the
2D
dimensional vectors

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2}),

\displaystyle v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{3},x_{4})

the area is given by

\displaystyle A(v_{1},v_{2})=\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}\\
x_{3}&x_{4}\end{pmatrix}=x_{1}x_{3}-x_{2}x_{4}.

Namely, the area of parallelogram is given by multilinear polynomials of
4
variables

\displaystyle A(v_{1},v_{2})=x_{1}x_{3}-x_{2}x_{4}.

(6)

In addition, the area vector for two
3D
vectors

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

\displaystyle v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{4},x_{5},x_{6})

is given by

\displaystyle A(v_{1},v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k}\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
x_{4}&x_{5}&x_{6}\end{vmatrix}=

(7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(x_{2}x_{6}-x_{3}x_{5}\right)\hat{i}+\left(x_{3}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{6}\right)\hat{j}+\left(x_{1}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{4}\right)\hat{k}.

In this case, we have three multilinear polynomials with
6
variables

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{2}x_{6}-x_{3}x_{5},

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{3}x_{4}-x_{1}x_{6},

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{5}-x_{2}x_{4}.

Furthermore, for a parallelepiped built with the following
3D
vectors

\displaystyle v_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),

(8)

\displaystyle v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}),

(9)

\displaystyle v_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{7},x_{8},x_{9})

(10)

the volume is given by

\displaystyle V(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}\\
x_{4}&x_{5}&x_{6}\\
x_{7}&x_{8}&x_{9}\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{5}x_{9}-x_{1}x_{6}x_{8}+x_{2}x_{6}x_{7}-x_{2}x_{4}x_{9}+x_{3}x_{4}x_{8}-x_{3}x_{5}x_{7}.

That is, the volume of parallelepiped is given by a multilinear polynomials of
9
variables

\displaystyle V(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=x_{1}x_{5}x_{9}-x_{1}x_{6}x_{8}+x_{2}x_{6}x_{7}-x_{2}x_{4}x_{9}+x_{3}x_{4}x_{8}-x_{3}x_{5}x_{7}.

(11)

4.1
Scalar area

Now, a basis of a system of
4
qubits must have
2^{4}=16
states. Consequently, a general state of this system can be written as follows:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}\ket{0000}+a_{1}\ket{0001}+a_{2}\ket{0010}+a_{3}\ket{0100}+a_{4}\ket{1000}+a_{12}\ket{0011}+

\displaystyle+a_{13}\ket{0101}+a_{14}\ket{1001}+a_{23}\ket{0110}+a_{24}\ket{1010}+a_{34}\ket{1100}+

\displaystyle+a_{123}\ket{0111}+a_{124}\ket{1011}+a_{134}\ket{1101}+a_{234}\ket{1110}+a_{1234}\ket{1111}.

A particular case is given by the entangled state

\displaystyle\ket{A}=\ket{0101}-\ket{1010}.

(12)

Notice that by using the state (
12
), the expression

\displaystyle\braket{A|\psi}=a_{13}-a_{24}

is found. Then, if the equations

\displaystyle a_{13}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}x_{3},

\displaystyle a_{24}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{2}x_{4}

are satisfied, the area of parallelogram (
6
) is obtained. Namely

\displaystyle{\rm Area}=\braket{A|\psi}=x_{1}x_{3}-x_{2}x_{4}.

Conclusions

In this paper, the area of a
2D
parallelogram was obtained from an entangled
4
-qubit state.
In addition, the vector area of a
3D
parallelogram was derived from three entangled
6
-qubit states. Moreover, the volume of the
3D
parallelepiped was gotten from an entangled
9
-qubit state.

It is worth mentioning parallelograms and parallelepipeds serve as fundamental building blocks for more sophisticated geometric structures. Then, it is possible that by using the results of this paper other geometric structures can be studied. In a future work we will study this subject.

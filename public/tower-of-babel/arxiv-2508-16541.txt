Minimal value set binomials and Frobenius nonclassical curves
Tiago Aprigio, João Paulo Guardieiro

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, we characterize all minimal value set binomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is, binomials whose size of the set of images is the smallest possible. With this information, we also classify all quadrinomial curves with separated variables that are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical for the morphism of lines.

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number,
q
be a power of
p
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements. A simple argument involving the maximum number of roots of a polynomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
allows one to conclude that

\left\lfloor\frac{q-1}{\deg F}\right\rfloor+1\leq\#V_{F}\leq q,

where
V_{F}=\{F(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
is the
value set
of the polynomial
F
. In this work, we are interested in polynomials that attain the lower bound for the cardinality of their value set. These are the so-called
minimal value set polynomials
(MVSPs).

This concept was introduced in
[
7
]
, and has been studied by many authors (see, for example,
[
3
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
18
]
). One of the most recent works in this matter is
[
6
]
, which characterizes all subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that can be seen as value sets of a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and present a conjecture on all MVSPs realizing such value sets.

Another important work on this topic is
[
2
]
. In this paper, the author provides a connection between MVSPs over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical curves, that is, curves for which the image under the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius morphism of any smooth point lies in its tangent line. More precisely, the author showed that the components of a curve given by the plane equation
f(x)=g(y)
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,
f
and
g
are MVSPs with the same value set.

Frobenius nonclassical curves were introduced by Stöhr and Voloch in
[
17
]
and have been studied since then, as can be seen in
[
1
,
4
,
12
]
. In his PhD thesis,
[
11
]
, the second author of this work classified all Frobenius nonclassical trinomial curves, that is, curves given by a plane equation with three monomials. A natural extension of this problem is to study the quadrinomial curves, which is one of the problems addressed in this paper.

In order to classify some of these curves, we will first characterize all minimal value set binomials. With this information, and using the connection given in
[
2
]
, we will then classify the quadrinomial curves that can be given, after a change of variables, by an equation with separated variables
f(x)=g(y)
. The main results of this paper are the following.

Theorem A.

A binomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a minimal value set polynomial if, and only if, up to multiplication by a nonzero element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
it is given by one of the following expressions:

\mathrm{(i)}

x^{a}+\beta
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
a\mid(q-1)
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

x^{b+\ell(q-1)}-x^{b}
, for some
b,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+\beta x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}
, where
p=2
,
q>2
and
\beta^{3}=1
.

\mathrm{(iv)}

x^{q-1}+\beta x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}
, where
p>2
and
\beta^{2}=1
;

\mathrm{(v)}

x^{p^{\ell}t}+\beta x^{t}
,
t=\frac{q-1}{p^{s\ell}-1}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}
and
N_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{\ell}}}(\beta)=(-1)^{s}
with
s\geq 2
;

\mathrm{(vi)}

x^{2t}+\beta x^{t}
,
p>2
,
t=\frac{q-1}{p^{m}-1}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{*}
.

Theorem B.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible quadrinomial curve over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with separated variables. Then
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,
\mathcal{C}
is given by one of the following plane equations:

\mathrm{(i)}

y^{d+q-1}-y^{d}=\alpha x^{b+q-1}-\alpha x^{b},
for suitable
b,d\in\mathbb{N}
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathbb{F}_{4}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
y^{q-1}=x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+1
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

y^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}=x^{p^{\ell}t}+x^{t}+1
, where
t=\dfrac{q-1}{p^{2\ell}-1}
;

\mathrm{(iv)}

y^{q-1}=x^{4t}+x^{2t}+x^{t}
, where
q=2^{3n}
and
t=\dfrac{q-1}{7}
;

\mathrm{(v)}

y^{q-1}=x^{6t}+x^{5t}+x^{3t}
, where
q=2^{3n}
and
t=\dfrac{q-1}{7}
;

\mathrm{(vi)}

y^{q-1}=x^{3t}+x^{2t}+x^{t}
, where
q=2^{2n}
and
t=\frac{q-1}{3}
;

\mathrm{(vii)}

y^{(p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell}+1)t}=x^{p^{2\ell}t}+x^{p^{\ell}t}+x^{t},
where
\ell>0
and
t=\dfrac{q-1}{p^{3\ell}-1}
;

\mathrm{(viii)}

y^{(p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell}+1)t}=x^{(p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell})t}+x^{(p^{2\ell}+1)t}+x^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}
, where
t=\dfrac{q-1}{p^{3\ell}-1}
;

\mathrm{(ix)}

y^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}=\alpha x^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}+x^{p^{\ell}t}+x^{t}
, where
t=\dfrac{q-1}{p^{2\ell}-1}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{\ell}}
.

We should mention that the binomials we classified in Theorem A are in accordance with
[
6
, Conjecture 1.1]
. The quadrinomial curves of Theorem B are also in accordance with
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
(which was proved assuming their Conjecture).

This paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we first provide the basic concepts we will need throughout the text. In Section
3
we will study the minimal value set binomials, and we prove Theorem A. Finally, in Sections
4
,
5
and
6
we will study the quadrinomial curves with separated variables. The combination of the results of these final sections provides the proof of Theorem B.

2.
Background

In this section, we recall the main results we will need on this work: some results on minimal value set polynomials (MVSPs) and concepts on Frobenius nonclassical quadrinomial curves.

As mentioned before, a minimal value set polynomial (MVSP)
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is the one attaining the lower bound for the cardinality of its value set, that is,

\#V_{F}=\left\lfloor\frac{q-1}{\deg F}\right\rfloor+1.

Let
p
be the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since the polynomial
x^{p}
induces a permutation in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, one can see that an MVSP
F
cannot be written as
G^{p}
, for any
G\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. In fact, this equality would provide
\deg F=p\cdot\deg G
and
\#V_{F}=\#V_{G}
.

It was noted in
[
7
]
that an MVSP
F(x)
with
\#V_{F}\leq 2
does not fit in a general pattern, and the authors classified such polynomials in the following way.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
V_{F}=\{\alpha\}
, then
F
is an MVSP if, and only if,
F
is of the form

F(x)=\alpha+(x^{q}-x)R(x),

where
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\backslash\{0\}
. If
V_{F}=\{\alpha,\beta\}
, then
F
is an MVSP if, and only if,

F(x)=\alpha\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}}\left\{1-(x-\mu)^{q-1}\right\}+\beta\sum_{\mu\notin\mathcal{S}}\left\{1-(x-\mu)^{q-1}\right\},

for some non-empty proper subset
\mathcal{S}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

In order to analyze MVSPs whose value set has more than two elements, we will use the following result, which is a consequence of
[
13
, Theorem 1]
and
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
F(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a nonconstant polynomial and
V_{F}=\{\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{r}\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
be its value set. Assume that
r>1
. For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
, set

L_{i}(x)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\\
F(\alpha)=\gamma_{i}\end{subarray}}(x-\alpha).

Suppose that the
\gamma_{i}
’s are arranged in such a way that
\ell_{0}:=\deg L_{0}(x)\leq\ell_{i}:=\deg L_{i}(x)
, for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Define
\nu
as the minimum multiplicity of the roots of
F(x)-\gamma_{0}
.

If
F
is an MVSP, then there exists a smallest positive integer
k
such that
\nu\mid(p^{k}-1)
(in particular,
p\nmid\nu
), a positive integer
m
, polynomials
N_{0},\ldots,N_{r},A,B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
L_{i}(x)\nmid N_{i}(x)
for any
i=0,\ldots,r
, and elements
\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\omega_{0}\neq 0
and
\omega_{m}=1
such that

(a)

1+\nu r=p^{mk}
;

(b)

F(x)=L_{0}(x)^{\nu}N_{0}(x)^{p^{mk}}+\gamma_{0}
;

(c)

L_{0}(x)=x\cdot A(x)^{p^{mk}}+B(x)^{p}
;

(d)

F(x)=L_{i}(x)N_{i}(x)^{p}+\gamma_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
;

(e)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\omega_{i}L_{0}(x)^{p^{ki}}N_{0}(x)^{p^{mk}(p^{ki}-1)/\nu}=-\omega_{0}(x^{q}-x)L_{0}^{\prime}(x)
;

(f)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\omega_{i}(F(x)-\gamma_{0})^{1+(p^{ki}-1)/\nu}=-\omega_{0}(x^{q}-x)F^{\prime}(x)
.

Note that items
(b)
and
(d)
imply that
F(x)-\gamma_{i}
has a simple root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for any
i=1,\ldots,r
, and that the roots of
F(x)-\gamma_{i}
which are not in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
should be roots of
N_{i}(x)
, for
i=0,\ldots,r
.

Conversely, if there exist
\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{m}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\omega_{0}\neq 0
and
\omega_{m}=1
such that item (f) holds, then
F
is an MVSP.

In this work, we will use the previous results to classify all minimal value set binomials, that is, polynomials whose equation have two terms.

Let now
p
be a prime number, and let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q=p^{h}
elements, where
h\geq 1
is an integer. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quadrinomial curve of degree
n
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is,
\mathcal{C}
is defined by an irreducible polynomial of the form

F(x,y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{3}a_{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}x^{i_{\ell}}y^{j_{\ell}},

where
a_{i_{\ell},j_{\ell}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.

Let
t
be a separating variable for the field extension
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\mathcal{C})/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. There is a smallest positive integer
\nu
such that

\begin{vmatrix}1&x^{q}&y^{q}\\
1&x&y\\
0&D_{t}^{\nu}x&D_{t}^{\nu}y\end{vmatrix}\neq 0,

where
D_{t}^{\nu}
denotes the
\nu
-th Hasse derivative with respect to
t
. This integer is called the Frobenius order of
\mathcal{C}
. If
\nu=1
, we say that
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius classical
; otherwise, it is said to be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical
. By considering
t=x
, one can obtain the geometric interpretation of Frobenius nonclassical curves given before: the image of any nonsingular point of
\mathcal{C}
under the
q
-Frobenius morphism lies in its tangent line.

Remark 2.3
.

This determinant condition above can be written as follows: let
f(x,y)
be the defining polynomial of a planar curve
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical curve if, and only if,
f(x,y)
divides

f_{x}\cdot(x^{q}-x)+f_{y}\cdot(y^{q}-y).

There is no criteria for the irreducibility of a quadrinomial curve. Proceeding as in
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
, we can obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quadrinomial curve (not necessarily irreducible) over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, up to a permutation of variables, if
x
,
y
and
z
do not divide the homogeneous equation of
\mathcal{C}
, then it can be written in one of the following forms:

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}+z^{r_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}y^{n_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}+z^{r_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}y^{n_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}+z^{r_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}y^{n_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}+z^{r_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+x^{m_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}y^{n_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+x^{m_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+z^{r_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}y^{n_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+x^{m_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}y^{n_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}z^{r_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}y^{n_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}z^{r_{4}}=0,\quad k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}y^{n_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}z^{r_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}y^{n_{2}}z^{r_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{3}}z^{r_{3}}+x^{m_{4}}=0,

where
m_{i},n_{i},r_{i}
are all positive integers for
i=1,2,3,4
,
k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}\neq 0
, and, if needed,
(m_{1},r_{1})\neq(m_{2},r_{2})
,
(n_{2},r_{2})\neq(n_{3},r_{3})
and
(m_{1},n_{1},r_{1})\neq(m_{2},n_{2},r_{2})
.

Proof.

Suppose that the homogeneous equation of the curve
\mathcal{C}
is

k_{1}x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}}z^{c_{1}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{2}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}}y^{b_{3}}z^{c_{3}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}}y^{b_{4}}z^{c_{4}}=0.

We can consider two different cases depending on the permutation of the variables.

1.
Assume that
a_{1}=\min\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
,
b_{1}=\min\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
, and
c_{2}=\min\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
. Then

\displaystyle k_{1}x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}}z^{c_{1}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{2}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}}y^{b_{3}}z^{c_{3}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}}y^{b_{4}}z^{c_{4}}

\displaystyle=x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}}z^{c_{2}}\left(k_{1}z^{c_{1}-c_{2}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}-a_{1}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}-a_{1}}y^{b_{3}-b_{1}}z^{c_{3}-c_{2}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}-a_{1}}y^{b_{4}-b_{1}}z^{c_{4}-c_{2}}\right).

2.
Assume that
a_{1}=\min\{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\}
,
b_{2}=\min\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}
, and
c_{3}=\min\{c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}
. Then

\displaystyle k_{1}x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}}z^{c_{1}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}}y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{2}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}}y^{b_{3}}z^{c_{3}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}}y^{b_{4}}z^{c_{4}}

\displaystyle=x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{2}}z^{c_{3}}\left(k_{1}y^{b_{1}-b_{2}}z^{c_{1}-c_{3}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}-a_{1}}z^{c_{2}-c_{3}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}-a_{1}}y^{b_{3}-b_{2}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}-a_{1}}y^{b_{4}-b_{2}}z^{c_{4}-c_{3}}\right).

Thus, the homogeneous equation of the curve
\mathcal{C}
can be written either as

k_{1}z^{c_{1}-c_{2}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}-a_{1}}y^{b_{2}-b_{1}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}-a_{1}}y^{b_{3}-b_{1}}z^{c_{3}-c_{2}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}-a_{1}}y^{b_{4}-b_{1}}z^{c_{4}-c_{2}}=0

or

k_{1}y^{b_{1}-b_{2}}z^{c_{1}-c_{3}}+k_{2}x^{a_{2}-a_{1}}z^{c_{2}-c_{3}}+k_{3}x^{a_{3}-a_{1}}y^{b_{3}-b_{2}}+k_{4}x^{a_{4}-a_{1}}y^{b_{4}-b_{2}}z^{c_{4}-c_{3}}=0
. By examining when the exponents in each term of these equations can be zero, we can split these two cases into
17
reduced forms, as stated in the proposition, with respect to the permutation of variables.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

After dehomogenizing the equations in the previous proposition, we observe that there are only five possible forms for the affine equation defining our curve:

\mathrm{(i)}

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{1}}+y^{n_{2}}=0,\quad m_{1}\neq m_{2},\ n_{1}\neq n_{2}
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{1}}+1=0,\quad m_{1}\neq m_{2}
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}+k_{3}x^{m_{3}}+y^{n_{4}}=0,\quad m_{1}\neq m_{2},\ m_{3}\neq m_{1},m_{2}
;

\mathrm{(iv)}

;

\mathrm{(v)}

k_{1}x^{m_{1}}y^{n_{1}}+k_{2}x^{m_{2}}+k_{3}y^{n_{2}}+1=0
.

where
m_{i},n_{i}
are all positive integers for
i=1,2,3,4
, and
k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}\neq 0
.

Quadrinomial curves whose equations are of types
(i)
,
(ii)
or
(iii)
can be written in the form
\mathcal{C}:f(x)=g(y)
. They are then called

. As a consequence of our main theorem, we will classify such curves with respect to the

-Frobenius nonclassicality. The relation between these concepts is given by the following result.

Proposition 2.6
.

[
2
, Corollary 3.5]

Let
f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be nonconstant polynomials such that
f(x)-g(y)\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p},y^{p}]
. Suppose that the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}:f(x)=g(y)
are defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If these components are
q
-Frobenius nonclassical, then
f(y)
and
g(x)
are MVSPs with
V_{f}=V_{g}
. Conversely, suppose that
f(x)
and
g(y)
are MVSPs with
V_{f}=V_{g}
. If

or

, then the irreducible components of

-Frobenius nonclassical.

3.
Minimal Value Set Binomials

In this section, we will classify the MVSPs with two terms. This classification will allow us to obtain the Frobenius nonclassical quadrinomial curves with separated variable of types
(i)
and
(ii)
. As a combination of the results of this section, we will obtain the proof of Theorem A.

In order to prove this statement, we start from a binomial
F\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. Since the multiplication by a nonzero element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
does not change the cardinality of the value set, we can consider it monic and we have

(1)

F(x)=x^{a}+\beta x^{b},\quad a>b,\quad\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}.

It is well-known that a monomial
f(x)=x^{v}
is an MVSP if, and only if,
v\mid(q-1)
(see
[
14
, Theorem 8.3.29]
). Since
\#V_{x^{a}}=\#V_{x^{a}+\beta}
, we conclude that
F(x)=x^{a}+\beta
is an MVSP if, and only if,
a\mid(q-1)
. We can now assume
b>0
in Equation (
1
).

We already mentioned that an MVSP
F(x)
with
\#V_{F}\leq 2
does not fit in a general pattern, so we will treat them firstly.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F(x)
be a binomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
F
is an MVSP with
\#V_{F}=1
if, and only if,

F(x)=x^{b+\ell(q-1)}-x^{b},

for some
b,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, the value set of this binomial is
V_{F}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.1
, it is known that
\#V_{F}=1
if, and only if,

x^{a}+\beta x^{b}=F(x)=(x^{q}-x)R(x)+\alpha,

where
R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\setminus\{0\}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
x^{q}=x
for every
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have
V_{F}=\{\alpha\}
.

Clearly, in our case we must have
\alpha=0
, and thus
F(x)
is divisible by
x^{q}-x
. Performing the division algorithm, we obtain that
a=b+\ell(q-1)
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, and

. Therefore,

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
F(x)
be a binomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then

\#V_{F}=2
if, and only if:

\mathrm{(i)}

\beta^{2}=1

.

\mathrm{(ii)}

,

Suppose that

is as in the statement of the proposition. One can check that, in case (i),

is an MVSP with

Conversely, suppose that

is an MVSP and that

. From the definition, we have
\frac{q-1}{2}<a\leq q-1
, which implies

. When

, with
\beta=\pm 1
. When

, the only MVSP is

, for any

(that is, for any

). We now prove each case of the statement assuming

.

(i) Suppose that

, and let

(2)

First, let us suppose that

- If

- If

Hence,

has

This implies

and from the equality

we obtain

.

Now, let us suppose that
F(\xi)\neq 0
. Also, suppose that
\beta\neq-1

. Hence, Equation

In this situation, we have two possibilities:

- If

- If

for all

. Hence, the polynomial

Suppose that

Therefore, we must have

Using the same argument as in the case where

and from the equality

(ii) Equation

∎

We can now study the binomials whose value sets have more than two elements. With notations as in Theorem

, we first study the case

.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

Suppose first that

Proposition 3.5

Let

Finally, we consider the case

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

be as in Equation (

) and suppose that

. Then there exists an integer

Suppose, w.l.o.g., that
\gamma_{1}=0

\ell_{1}>1
(otherwise we would have

), we know that
\ell_{1}=1+\gcd(a-b,q-1)

Lemma
3.3
states that

. Then, from Theorem
2.2
(item (f)), we have:

(6)

\sum_{i=0}^{m}\omega_{i}(x^{a}+\beta x^{b}-\gamma_{0})^{\frac{p^{ki}-1}{\nu}+1}=-\omega_{0}(ax^{q-1+a}+b\beta x^{q-1+b}-ax^{a}-b\beta x^{b}),

with
\omega_{0}\neq 0
and
\omega_{m}=1
. By comparing the terms of highest degree on both sides of Equation (

Moreover, since

4.
Quadrinomial curves of type (i)

As an application of our classification of minimal value set binomials, and considering Proposition
2.6
, we will determine the Frobenius (non-)classicality of some quadrinomial curves. With notations as in Remark
2.5
, in this section we study those of type (i), that is, curves defined by the plane equation

\mathcal{C}:y^{c}+\gamma y^{d}=\alpha x^{a}+\beta x^{b}.

Let
f(x)=\alpha x^{a}+\beta x^{b}
and
g(y)=y^{c}+\gamma y^{d}
. A necessary condition for the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
to be Frobenius nonclassical curves is that
f
and
g
are MVSPs with the same value set. Moreover, if
\#V_{f}>2
, this is also a sufficient condition. Therefore, we divide the analysis into cases according to whether
\#V_{f}=1
,
\#V_{f}=2
, or
\#V_{f}>2
.

At the end of this section, we will prove that, except for the case
\#V_{f}=1
, a quadrinomial curve of type (i) whose irreducible components are Frobenius nonclassical can be written, after a suitable
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-projectivity, as
f(x)=f(y)
. In particular, such quadrinomial curves are never irreducible, since the line
\ell:x-y=0
is always a component of
\mathcal{C}:f(x)-f(y)=0
. We start with the case
\#V_{f}=1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}:g(y)=f(x)
be a quadrinomial curve of type (i) such that
\#V_{f}=1
. Then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,

\mathcal{C}:y^{d+q-1}-y^{d}=\alpha x^{b+q-1}-\alpha x^{b},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
b\equiv d\equiv 1
(mod
p
).

Proof.

Since
f
and
g
must be MVSPs with the same value set, by Proposition
3.1
we have
f(x)=\alpha x^{b+j(q-1)}-\alpha x^{b}
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, and
g(y)=y^{d+j^{\prime}(q-1)}-y^{d}
. From
[
2
, Lemma 3.3]
we conclude that the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only, if
f(x)-g(y)
divides

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)(x^{q}-x)-g^{\prime}(y)(y^{q}

\displaystyle-y)=\alpha(b-j)x^{b+(j+1)(q-1)}-\alpha bx^{b+q-1}-\alpha(b-j)x^{b+j(q-1)}+b\alpha x^{b}

\displaystyle-\left((d-j^{\prime})y^{d+(j^{\prime}+1)(q-1)}-dy^{d+q-1}-(d-j^{\prime})y^{d+j^{\prime}(q-1)}+dy^{d}\right).

By
[
2
, Theorem 3.4]
, this is equivalent to the existence of a monic polynomial
T(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that

(7)

T(f(x))=\theta(\alpha(b-j)x^{b+(j+1)(q-1)}-\alpha bx^{b+q-1}-\alpha(b-j)x^{b+j(q-1)}+\alpha bx^{b}),

and

(8)

T(g(y))=\theta((d-j^{\prime})y^{d+(j^{\prime}+1)(q-1)}-dy^{d+q-1}-(d-j^{\prime})y^{d+j^{\prime}(q-1)}+dy^{d}).

Denote by
D
the degree of

. Now, assume that

. Then, comparing the degrees of the polynomials in (

), we have

(b+j(q-1))D=b+j(q-1)+q-1\Longrightarrow(b+j(q-1))(D-1)=q-1.

This equality is never attained. Thus, we conclude that
b-j\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Hence, comparing the terms with the highest degrees in (

) again, we obtain

,
T(x)=x
and
\theta b=-1
. Now, from (
8
), this implies that
1=j^{\prime}\equiv d\equiv b\pmod{p}
, as desired.
∎

Remark 4.2

Proposition 4.3

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,

f(x)=\alpha x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+\alpha\beta x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad g(y)=y^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+\beta^{\prime}y^{\frac{q-1}{3}},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\beta^{3}=1=\beta^{\prime 3}
, and
\{0,\alpha\beta^{2}\}=V_{f}=V_{g}=\{0,\beta^{\prime 2}\}
. In particular,
\alpha=\beta^{\prime 2}/\beta^{2}=\beta/\beta^{\prime}
. We then obtain

\mathcal{C}:\beta^{\prime}y^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+\beta^{\prime 2}y^{\frac{q-1}{3}}=\beta x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+\beta^{2}x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}.

Since
\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}
and
\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime 2}}
are in the image of the norm function
x\mapsto x^{(q-1)/3}
, we can apply a transformation
(x^{(q-1)/3},y^{(q-1)/3})\mapsto\left(\frac{x^{(q-1)/3}}{\beta^{2}},\frac{y^{(q-1)/3}}{\beta^{\prime 2}}\right)
to put
\mathcal{C}
in the desired form. Note that

y^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+y^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}=\left(y^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}\right)\cdot\left(y^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+1\right),

and the quadrinomial curve is the product of a Frobenius nonclassical Fermat curve and
\frac{q-1}{3}
lines.

Now, suppose
p>2
. If
f
and
g
are MVSPs, then they have the same form as the polynomial
F
in Proposition
3.2
, item (i). In the case
f(x)=x^{q-1}-x^{(q-1)/2}
, we apply the transformation
x\mapsto\zeta x
, where
\zeta
is a primitive element of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, to put the curve in the form
\mathcal{C}:y^{q-1}+y^{\frac{q-1}{2}}-x^{q-1}-x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}=0
. Note that

y^{q-1}+y^{\frac{q-1}{2}}-x^{q-1}-x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}=\left(y^{\frac{q-1}{2}}-x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)\cdot\left(y^{\frac{q-1}{2}}+x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}+1\right),

so the quadrinomial curve is the product of a Fermat curve and
\frac{q-1}{2}
lines. From
[
8
, Theorem 2]
, the Fermat curve is Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,
p=3
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\colon g(y)=f(x)
be a quadrinomial curve of type (i) such that
\#V_{f}>2
. Then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if, after a suitable
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-projectivity
\mathcal{C}
is defined by one of the following plane equations:

(i)

y^{p^{\ell}t}+\beta y^{t}=x^{p^{\ell}t}+\beta x^{t}
, where

and
N_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{\ell}}}(\beta)=(-1)^{s}

(ii)

y^{2t}+\beta y^{t}=x^{2t}+\beta x^{t}
, where

,

and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{*}
.

Note that both curves are reducible, since the line

is a component of them.

Proof.

If
f
and
g
are MVSPs, then, by analyzing the possibilities in Propositions
3.5
and
3.6
under the condition that
\#V_{f}=\#V_{g}
, we see that there are two possible equations for the curve
\mathcal{C}
:

•

y^{p^{\ell^{\prime}}t^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}y^{t^{\prime}}=\alpha x^{p^{\ell}t}+\alpha\beta x^{t}
, where
t=\frac{q-1}{p^{s\ell}-1}
,
t^{\prime}=\frac{q-1}{p^{s^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}-1},\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},

\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}
,
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}}
, and
N_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{\ell}}}(\beta)=(-1)^{s}
,
N_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{s^{\prime}\ell^{\prime}}}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{\ell^{\prime}}}}(\beta^{\prime})=(-1)^{s^{\prime}}
with

;

•

y^{2t}+\beta y^{t}=\alpha x^{2t}+\alpha\beta^{\prime}x^{t}
, where
p>2
,
t=\frac{q-1}{p^{m}-1}
and
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}^{*}
.

Let us analyze the first case. We already showed that

V_{y^{p^{\ell}t}+\beta y^{t}}=Z\left(\sum_{i=0}^{(s-1)\ell}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{\beta^{p^{i\ell+\cdots+1}}}x^{p^{i\ell}}\right).

In particular, it is contained in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}
. Given that
V_{f}=V_{g}
, we must have
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{s\ell}}
. Therefore,
\frac{1}{\alpha^{p^{\ell(s-1)}}}
is in the image of the norm function
x\mapsto x^{t}
, so we can apply a suitable changing of coordinates and consider
\alpha=1
.

The polynomial
f(x)
is now given by
x^{p^{\ell}t}+\tilde{\beta}x^{t}
. The value set of
f
is now given by

V_{f}=Z\left(\sum_{i=0}^{(s^{\prime}-1)\ell^{\prime}}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{\tilde{\beta}^{p^{i\ell^{\prime}+\cdots+1}}}x^{p^{i\ell^{\prime}}}\right).

Since
V_{f}=V_{g}
, we have

Z\left(\sum_{i=0}^{(s^{\prime}-1)\ell^{\prime}}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{\tilde{\beta}^{p^{i\ell^{\prime}+\cdots+1}}}x^{p^{i\ell^{\prime}}}\right)=Z\left(\sum_{i=0}^{(s-1)\ell}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}}{\beta^{p^{i\ell+\cdots+1}}}x^{p^{i\ell}}\right).

These polynomials have the same degree and the same roots, so they must be the same. This implies
\ell=\ell^{\prime},s=s^{\prime}
and
\beta=\tilde{\beta}
.

Now, consider the second case. As before, we must have
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
. We analyze, as in the proof of the case
\#V_{f}=1
, the existence of a monic polynomial
T
and
\theta
such that

(9)

T(y^{2t}+\beta y^{t})=\theta(y^{q}-y)(2y^{2t-1}+\beta y^{t-1})

and

T(\alpha x^{2t}+\alpha\beta^{\prime}x^{t})=\theta(x^{q}-x)(2\alpha x^{2t-1}+\alpha\beta^{\prime}x^{t-1}).

By analyzing the terms with the highest and the two lowest degrees in Equation (
9
), we conclude that

and

T(z)=z^{(p^{m}+1)/2}+\cdots-\frac{1}{2\beta^{2}}z^{2}-\frac{z}{2}.

By applying

in

, we conclude that
\alpha^{(p^{m}+1)/2}=\alpha
. This implies that
\alpha^{(p^{m}-1)/2}=1
and, in particular,
\alpha
is a square in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
. As before, we can rescale
x
and assume
\alpha=1
and
f(x)=x^{2t}+\tilde{\beta}x^{t}
. Applying
T
to this polynomial implies
\beta^{2}=\tilde{\beta}^{2}
. Therefore,
\tilde{\beta}=\pm\beta
. In the case
\tilde{\beta}=-\beta
, we can rescale
x
again to obtain the desired equation for
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

5.
Quadrinomial curves of type (ii)

In this section, we study quadrinomial curves of type (ii), that is, curves defined by the plane equation

y^{a}=\alpha x^{b}+\beta x^{c}+\gamma.

For those curves, we will apply the following result, as well as its corollary (which will help us during some computations).

Proposition 5.1
.

[
2
, Theorem 5.3]

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be a nonconstant polynomial and
m\geq 1
be an integer such that
y^{m}-f(x)\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}[x^{p},y^{p}]
. Then, the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}:y^{m}-f(x)
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,
m\mid q-1
and

m\cdot f(x)(f(x)^{(q-1)/m}-1)=(x^{q}-x)f^{\prime}(x).

Corollary 5.2
.

[
2
, Corollary 5.4]

Notation and hypothesis as in the previous theorem. If the components of
\mathcal{C}:y^{m}-f(x)
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical, then

\mathrm{(i)}

p\nmid m
and
f^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

\frac{mq}{m+q-1}\leq\deg f\leq m
, and the upper bound (resp. lower bound) for
\deg f
is attained if, and only if,
p\nmid\deg f
(resp.
f^{\prime}
is constant);

\mathrm{(iii)}

if
f=c\prod_{i=1}^{s}(x-a_{i})^{k_{i}}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}[x]
, then
a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
if, and only if,
p\nmid k_{i}
. In particular, all simple roots of
f
lie in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
;

\mathrm{(iv)}

f
has an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-root, and if
f
has a simple root, then
m\equiv 1
(mod
p
);

\mathrm{(v)}

m\equiv 1
(mod
p
) if, and only if,
f^{\prime\prime}=0
;

\mathrm{(vi)}

if an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-root of
f
has multiplicity
k
, with
0<k<\deg f
, then
k\leq\frac{m-1}{\#V_{f}}
and
k\equiv m
(mod
p
).

Unless stated otherwise, all curves of this section are irreducible. Their irreducibility comes from the following proposition: the polynomial
f(x)
will always have a simple root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

[
16
, Lemma I.2C]

The curve given by
\mathcal{C}:y^{d}=f(x)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is absolutely irreducible if, and only if,
\gcd(d,d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})=1
, where

f(x)=a(x-x_{1})^{d_{1}}\cdots(x-x_{r})^{d_{r}}

is the factorization of
f(x)
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
.

As in the previous section, we divide our analysis into cases. Throughout this section, we assume
f(x)=\alpha x^{b}+\beta x^{c}+\gamma
and
g(y)=y^{a}
. Since
\#V_{g}=1+\frac{q-1}{a}
, there are no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical quadrinomial curves of type (ii) such that
\#V_{f}=1
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\colon g(y)=f(x)
be a quadrinomial curve of type (ii) such that
\#V_{f}=2
. Then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if, after an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-projectivity, one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\mathbb{F}_{4}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
y^{q-1}=x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+1
;

(ii)

p=3
and
y^{q-1}=x^{q-1}+x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}+1
.

Proof.

Since
\#V_{g}=1+\dfrac{q-1}{a}
, we must have
a=q-1
, so
g(y)=y^{q-1}
and
V_{g}=\{0,1\}
. Assume first that
p=2
. Then, by Propositions
2.6
and
3.2
,
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if,

f(x)=\alpha x^{\frac{2(q-1)}{3}}+\alpha\beta x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}+\gamma,

with
\beta^{3}=1
and
V_{f}=V_{g}
. Since
\{0,1\}=V_{f}=\{\alpha\beta^{2}+\gamma,\gamma\}
, we get
\gamma=1
and
\alpha=\beta
. Since
\beta^{2}
is in the image of the norm function
x\mapsto x^{\frac{q-1}{3}}
, we can apply a suitable
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-projectivity to put
\mathcal{C}
in the desired form.

Now suppose that
p>2
. If the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical, then

f(x)=\alpha x^{q-1}+\alpha\beta x^{\frac{q-1}{2}}+\gamma,

with

and

. In this case,

and

Finally, for

∎

6.
Quadrinomial curves of type (iii)

Finally we will study quadrinomial curves of type (iii), that is, curves defined by the plane equation

y^{a}=\alpha x^{b}+\beta x^{c}+\gamma x^{d}.

The Frobenius nonclassility of these curves will not be given entirely via MVSPs, and at the end of this section, we will complete the classification of all Frobenius nonclassical quadrinomial curves with separated variables. Throughout this section, we assume

Now, as before, if

, there are no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical curve of type (iii). Finally, if

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an irreducible quadrinomial curve of type (iii) such that

We can now deal with the case

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}

and

\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical. Then

\gamma_{0}=0

.

has the stated form.

Now, let us deal with curves of type (iii) when

and

\mathcal{C}

If

5.2
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical,

is an MVSP. Suppose first that
\gamma_{0}=0
. Then, by comparing the terms with the lowest degree in item (f) of Theorem

, we obtain

(for the

Now suppose that

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
F(x)=a_{1}x^{b_{1}}+a_{2}x^{b_{2}}+a_{3}x^{b_{3}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]

p
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose
b_{1}>b_{2}>b_{3}
and

with

. Then

c_{1}x^{d_{1}}+c_{2}x^{d_{2}}+c_{3}x^{d_{3}}=F(x)^{s}=(a_{1}^{p^{\ell}}x^{b_{1}p^{\ell}}+a_{2}^{p^{\ell}}x^{b_{2}p^{\ell}}+a_{3}^{p^{\ell}}x^{b_{3}p^{\ell}})^{t}.

If
t>1
, then the expansion of the left-hand side includes more than three terms. For instance, the monomials

a_{1}^{p^{\ell}t}x^{b_{1}p^{\ell}t},\quad ta_{1}^{p^{\ell}(t-1)}a_{2}^{p^{\ell}}x^{b_{1}p^{\ell}(t-1)+b_{2}p^{\ell}},\quad ta_{2}^{p^{\ell}}a_{3}^{p^{\ell}(t-1)}x^{b_{2}p^{\ell}+b_{3}p^{\ell}(t-1)},\quad a_{3}^{p^{\ell}t}x^{b_{3}p^{\ell}t}.

This contradicts the assumption that
F(x)^{s}
is a trinomial. Therefore, we must have
t=1
, and hence
s=p^{\ell}
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a quadrinomial curve of type (iii) such that
p
does not divide both
b
and
c
simultaneously. Then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if, it is defined by one of the following plane equations:

(i)

y^{(p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell}+1)t}=x^{(p^{2\ell}+p^{\ell})t}+x^{(p^{2\ell}+1)t}+x^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}
, where
t=\dfrac{q-1}{p^{3\ell}-1}
;

(ii)

y^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}=\alpha x^{(p^{\ell}+1)t}+x^{p^{\ell}t}+x^{t}
, where
t=\dfrac{q-1}{p^{2\ell}-1}
, and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{\ell}}
.

Proof.

First, suppose that
p\mid b
and
p\nmid c
. By Proposition
5.1
and Lemmas
6.3
and
6.4
, we have that the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if, the following conditions hold:

\dfrac{q-1}{a}+1=p^{\ell},\quad bp^{\ell}=q+c-1,\quad cp^{\ell}=q+d-1,\quad dp^{\ell}=b,

and

\alpha^{p^{\ell}}=\beta,\quad\beta^{p^{\ell}}=\gamma,\quad\gamma^{p^{\ell}}=\alpha.

In particular,
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{p^{3\ell}}^{*}
, and we can rescale
x
to conclude that
\mathcal{C}
is defined by the plane equation of item (i).

Now suppose that
p\nmid b
and
p\mid c
. Then, using analogous arguments, we conclude that the irreducible components of
\mathcal{C}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical if, and only if, it is defined by an equation of the form given in item (ii).

Finally, if
p\nmid b
and
p\nmid c
, then the same reasoning leads to
b=c=d
, which is a contradiction. Therefore, in this case, there are no
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-Frobenius nonclassical curves.
∎

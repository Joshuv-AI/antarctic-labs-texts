Paper-Scissors-Stone Model for Interacting Population and its Limit Theorem Research Memorandum 485 The Institute of Statitical Mathematics, 16 September1993, Yasunori Okabe (Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Science, Hokkaido University), Hajime Mano (Institute of Statistical Mathematics and The Graduate University for Advanced Studies), Yoshiaki Itoh (Institute of Statistical Mathematics and The Graduate University for Advanced Studies)
Yasunori Okabe, Hajime Mano, Yoshiaki Itoh

Abstract
This paper treats a random collision model of three species, which is represented by the random time change of three standard Poisson processes. The prey-predator relation in the random collision model looks like paper-scissors-stone game, and the model is called the paper-scissors model. At first, we investigate the stochastic structure of our model. By using stochastic calculus, the model is decomposed into a semi-martingale, and we prove a weak law of large numbers and a central limit theorem. The main purpose of this paper is to obtain an ordinary differential equation from the weak law and a stochastic differential equation from the central limit theorem.



Keywords
: martingale, optional sampling theorem, standard Poisson process, stopping time, strong law of large numbers, weak law of large numbers

\displaystyle\leq\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|+\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{(M)}\left(t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}\right)\right\|+C_{\mathit{Lipschitz}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|+\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathfrak{M}^{(M)}(s)\right\|\right)e^{C_{\mathit{Lipschitz}}t}.

Hence we get for any real number
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle P\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|>\epsilon\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\leq P\left(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|+\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathfrak{M}^{(M)}(s)\right\|\right)e^{C_{\mathit{Lipschits}}t}>\epsilon\right),

and so,

\displaystyle P\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|>\epsilon\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(T_{0}^{(M)}<t\right)+P\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|>\epsilon,T_{0}^{(M)}\geq t\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(T_{0}^{(M)}<t\right)+P\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}}\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|+\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathfrak{M}^{(M)}(s)\right\|\right)>\epsilon e^{-C_{\mathit{Lipschitz}}t}\end{array}\right).

For any real number
\delta>0
we claim

(4.3)

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}P\,\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}}\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|+\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathfrak{M}^{(M)}(s)\right\|\right)>\delta\Bigg)=0,

(4.4)

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}P\left(T_{0}^{(M)}<t\right)=0.

We estimate (
4.3
):

\displaystyle P\,\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}}\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|+\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathfrak{M}^{(M)}(s)\right\|\right)>\delta\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+P\,\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}}\frac{1}{M}\left\|\mathfrak{M}^{(M)}(s)\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq P\left(\left\|U_{0}^{(M)}\right\|>\frac{\delta}{2}\right)+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}P\,\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\frac{1}{M}\left|\mathcal{M}_{jj+1}^{(M)}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{j-1j}^{(M)}(s)\right|>\frac{\delta}{2n},t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}\Bigg).

By Chebyshev’s inequality and the martingale inequality, we have

\displaystyle P\,\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}}\frac{1}{M}\left|\mathcal{M}_{jj+1}^{(M)}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{j-1j}^{(M)}(s)\right|>\frac{\delta}{2n}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2n}{\epsilon}E\,\Bigg[\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\leq T_{0}^{(M)}}\frac{1}{M}\left|\mathcal{M}_{jj+1}^{(M)}(s)-\mathcal{M}_{j-1j}^{(M)}(s)\right|\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2n}{\epsilon}\frac{C}{M^{2}}E\left[\left\langle\mathcal{M}_{jj+1}^{(M)}(*)\right\rangle_{t}+\left\langle\mathcal{M}_{j-1j}^{(M)}(*)\right\rangle_{t}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{2nC}{\epsilon M}E\left[\int_{0}^{t}f^{jj+1}\left(\frac{Z_{j}^{(M)}(v)}{M},\frac{Z_{j+1}^{(M)}(v)}{M}\right)dv+\int_{0}^{t}f^{j-1j}\left(\frac{Z_{j-1}^{(M)}(v)}{M},\frac{Z_{j}^{(M)}(v)}{M}\right)dv\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2nC}{\epsilon M}2t\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sup_{0<x<1,0<y<1}f^{jj+1}(x,y),

where
C
is a positive constant for the martingale inquality.
By letting
M
tend to infinity, we see that (
4.3
) holds.

Now we estimate (
4.4
).
We define the
\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
-valued function
i_{s}^{(M)}

such that
\frac{Z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}^{(M)}(s)}{M}=\min_{1\leq l\leq n}\{\frac{Z_{l}^{(M)}(s)}{M}\}
for

.

We estimate the third term: for any
s
,

s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}
,

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}^{(M)}(s)}{M}

\displaystyle\geq z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}(s)-\left|z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}(s)-\frac{Z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}^{(M)}(s)}{M}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{0\leq s\leq t}z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}(s)-\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq r-\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|,

where

.
Hence we get

\inf_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\frac{Z_{i_{s}^{(M)}}^{(M)}(s)}{M}\geq r-\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|.

We have the relation

\left\{T_{0}^{(M)}<t\right\}\subset\Bigg\{r-\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|\leq\frac{2}{M}\Bigg\}.

Therefore we have the estimation:

P\left(T_{0}^{(M)}<t\right)\leq P\,\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|\geq r-\frac{2}{M}\Bigg).

It follows by (
4.3
) that

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}P\,\Big(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t\wedge T_{0}^{(M)}}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|>\epsilon\Big)=0.

This fact concludes (
4.4
).

Therefore

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}P\Big(\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\left\|U_{s}^{(M)}\right\|>\epsilon\Big)=0,

which complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

\square

Secular trend of colorectal cancer related with low physical activity from 1990 to 2021 in China
Authors: Qian Yang, Yao Hu, Fang Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aims to report low physical activity related colorectal cancer (LPARCC) death and Disability Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) in 1990–2021, and decompose the changes in disease burden into population growth, population aging, and epidemiological changes.MethodsWe obtained LPARCC data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) online database. The Joinpoint regression model and the age-period-cohort model (APC) were used to describe the trend of disease burden and analyzed the effects of age, period, and cohort. Finally, a recently developed decomposition method was applied to partition changes in the burden of disease from 1990 to 2021 into components associated with population growth, population aging, and epidemiological changes.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2021, there were significant increased trends in all ages and age-standardized rates of summary exposure values of low physical activity, with percentage changes of 0.46 (95% CI: 0.16 to 0.86) and 0.16 (95% CI: -0.08 to 0.45), respectively. The percentage change of age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR) for LPARCC from 1990 to 2021 was − 0.07 (95% CI: -0.38–0.37) and percentage change of age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) was − 0.09 (95% CI: -0.37–0.33), both of them showed non-significantly decreased trends. In addition, the results of the Joinpoint regression model demonstrated that the annual percentage changes in ASMR and ASDR rates for LPARCC were − 0.22 (95% CI: -0.52–0.09) and − 0.29 (95% CI: -0.55– -0.03), respectively. Finally, from 1990 to 2021, the ASMR of LPARCC increased by 191.19%, which could be partitioned into age structure (170.62%), mortality change in low physical activity (-15.42%), and population size (35.99%), respectively.ConclusionOver the past 30 years, the number of LPARCC death and DALYs in China had shown an upward trend, which was mainly related with population aging.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15290-7.Keywords: Colorectal cancer, Low physical activity, Temporal trends, Decomposition analysis

Background

In 2016, data from the Chinese Cancer Registry reported that colorectal cancer is the second most common cancer and the forth leading cause of cancer deaths. It is estimated that there were about 408 000 cases and 195 600 deaths of colorectal cancer in China in 2016 [1]. Evidence from prospective cohort and meta-analyses studies have supported that modifiable behavioral factors such as smoking [2], alcohol consumption [3], low physical activity [4, 5], high body mass index (BMI) [6–8], and unhealthy eating habits [9, 10] are risk factors for colorectal cancer. Therefore, primary prevention measures such as smoking cessation, alcohol restriction, healthy diets have important public health significance for reducing disease burden of colorectal cancer and deserve attention from health professionals [11].

Researchers have found that the prevalence of smoking tobacco use from 1990 to 2019 [12] and alcohol exposure from 1990 to 2017 [13] have shown downward trends all over the world. However, global age-standardized prevalence of obesity increased from 0.7% (0.4–1.2) in 1975 to 5.6% (4.8–6.5) in 2016 in girls, and from 0.9% (0.5–1.3) in 1975 to 7.8% (6.7–9.1) in 2016 in boys [14]. Global age-standardized prevalence of insufficient physical activity in 2016 (36.8%, 35.0–38.0) had increased in high-income countries over time (31.6%, 27.1–37.2, in 2001) [15].

Reinforcing physical exercise is considered to be an important and easily modifiable factor for preventing cancer. Therefore, understanding and planning in regard to the burden of low physical activity related colorectal cancer (LPARCC) is of great public health significance. Then, we searched PubMed and CNKI for studies published between January 1, 1990, and August 4, 2024 on the burden of colorectal cancer deaths and Disability Adjusted Life Years (DALYs), as well as disease trends. Then, we searched PubMed and the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) database with the terms (“colorectal cancer”) AND (“disease burden”) AND (“China”) AND (“physical exercise”) OR (“physical activity”) for previous studies published between 1/1/1990 and 4/8/2024 about the temporal trends of death and disability adjusted life years (DALYs) of LPARCC in China. As a result, previous studies have reported on the disease burden of colorectal cancer and its associated risk factors [16–18], and have also projected the future changes of trends of colorectal cancer [19, 20] globally and in China. However, there is no available data on the temporal trends of disease burden of LPARCC in Chinese mainland. Therefore, timely monitoring comprehensive temporal trends of LPARCC in China is of great significance for tailoring precise population prevention strategies.

The Global Burden of Diseases Study 2021 (GBD 2021) is a systematic scientific effort, which provides an unique opportunity to analyze the temporal trends of the disease burden related with various risk factors [21]. Our study aims to report the recent trends LPARCC deaths and DALYs trends between 1990 and 2021 in China, and also partition the changes in terms of population growth, population aging, and epidemiological changes.

Methods

All data for this study were obtained from the GBD 2021 database, and the details about the GBD 2021 estimation process had been reported previously [22]. In this study, we selected “China” as the location, “colorectal cancer” as the cause, “low physical activity” as the risk factor, and “death” and “DALYs” as the outcome measures. Data was downloaded from the Global Health Data Exchange website (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool, accessed date: 4 June 2024). The data analysis for this study follows the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER) [23].

The definition of Low Physical Activity (LPA)

In the GBD 2021, physical activity information on the intensity, duration, and frequency of activities was collected from two standardized questionnaires [the Global Physical Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ) and the International Physical Activity Questionnaire (IPAQ)] [24]. Physical activity was quantified by metabolic equivalents (METs, minutes/week), then was classified into four levels: inactive (< 600 MET-minutes per week), slightly active (600–3,999 MET-minutes per week), moderately active (4,000–7,999 MET-minutes per week), and highly active (≥ 8,000 MET-minutes per week). Finally, LPA was defined as < 3,000 MET-min per week [25, 26].

LPARCC and disease burden metrics

In this study, we focused on the disease burden of colorectal cancer related with low physical activity. Summary exposure values (SEV) were used to describe the percentage changes in the low physical activity. Deaths and DALYs were selected as the indices of disease burden, which all-age rates and age standardized rates (ASMR for age-standardized mortality rate, ASDR for age-standardized DALYs rate) for both of them were calculated. The values and 95% uncertainty interval (UI) of above indices were derived directly from the GBD database.

Decomposition analysis

To determine the main drivers (population growth, population aging, and mortality change) for the changes in the total number of LPARCC deaths and DALYs, we used a previously reported decomposition method and more details were seen in other literature [29]. Compared to previous studies [21, 30, 31], this method was insensitive to the decomposition order of the 3 components and the choice of reference group [32], which enhanced the stability of our results. All statistical analyses were conducted using R 3.6.0.

Results

Changes in the disease burden of LPARCC between 1990 and 2021

For DALYs, although the percentage changes of the estimated number (1.50 (0.71–2.75)) and all ages rates (1.06 (0.41–2.10)) increased significantly from 1990 to 2021, there were no significant changes in other disease burden indices such as all ages PAF, ASPAF and ASDR. Discussion

This study systematically summarized the burden of LPARCC in China from 1990 to 2021, and found that the number of deaths and DALYs increased by 191.19% and 149.67%, respectively. In addition, the results of decomposition methods supported that the population aging was the main contributing factor for the changes in the LPARCC burden over the past three decades.

Our data showed that the all ages rate of summary exposure value of LPA activity in China increased slightly from 14.37% in 1990 to 21.00% in 2021, which could be related with the level of economic development in country. The prevalence of LPA tends to be higher in high-income countries than in low-income countries. A pooled analysis reported that the LPA prevalence in 2016 was more than twice as high in high-income countries (36.8%, 35.0–38.0) as in low-income countries (16.2%, 14.2–17.9), and it had increased in high-income countries over time (31.6%, 27.1–37.2, in 2001) [15]. Evidence from an African population indicated that greater approximation to a Westernized lifestyle—marked by high-calorie intake and low physical activity—was strongly associated with colorectal cancer [33]. On the contrary, regular physical exercise was helpful to reduced the incidence risk of cancers, potentially through pathways such as weight reduction; decreases in insulin, IGF-1, IGFBP-3, and leptin; and reduced exposure time of colonic mucosa to gastrointestinal carcinogens [34].

However, with respect to the temporal trends of LPARCC, the differences in demographic and healthcare across countries deserved more attention. For example, the CRC burden in China has continued to increase, whereas it has declined in the United States (US) [35]. The decreasing trend in the US was partly related with the implementation of national CRC screening. The CRC screening program began in the 1980 s, and the coverage of colonoscopy screening increased from 21% to 60% between 2000 and 2015, after which CRC incidence declined rapidly [36]. In contrast, in China, the CRC population screening started in the 1970s. Although programs have been implemented in both urban and rural areas [37], there is currently no nationwide organized screening program [38] and population coverage remains limited. A previous study in a high-risk urban population reported a 14% colonoscopy participation rate and a 3.32% detection rate of advanced colorectal neoplasia [39]. In addition, emerging evidence supported that effective screening tools combined with precious high-risk individuals could greatly improve cost-effectiveness of CRC screening. For example, CRC screening, with colonoscopy as the main tool, is more advantageous in terms of early diagnosis and intervention [40]; Given the risk of serious adverse clinical outcomes, greater emphasis should be placed on early screening and detection among vulnerable populations, including individuals with lower gastrointestinal bleeding and pregnant women [41, 42].

Our results presented that despite the temporal trends of mortality and DALYs of LPARCC fluctuated in five different time segments, the values of disease burden indices had shown upward trends during 2015 to 2021 year. These findings aligned with previous studies, which reported the increased temporal patterns of disease burden of colorectal cancer in China and globally [17]. In addition, in order to describe the short-term trends of LPARCC accurately, we adapted the Joinpoint analysis method which divided the long-term trend line into several segments and analyzed each time period [28]. The difference of temporal trends in each period could be explained by the following two reason. On the one hand, the development of industry and economy and the shift of lifestyle such as western diet and low physical exercise over time could affect the LAPCC death and DALYs. On the other hand, this might be related with the increasing coverage of the national cancer registry system and the development of CRC treatment. In 2002, the National Central Cancer Registry was launched under the supervision of the former Ministry of Health, and was responsible for systematically collecting, analyzing, and interpreting cancer data [43]. Consequently, a significantly increased trend of LPARCC DALYs rates occurred in the period from 1999 to 2004. Then, rapid downward trends for all ages mortality rate appeared, which could be related with the development of CRC treatment. From 2010 to 2020, China issued four editions of its guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of CRC. These guidelines have markedly promoted the standardization and internationalization of CRC diagnosis and treatment [44]. Notably, in recent years, a significantly increased trend occurred in the LPARCC death or DALYs, which reminded us that primary prevention measures such as enhancing physical exercise should be supplemented to control the burden of LPARCC.

We further analyzed the effects of age, period, and cohort factors on the trends for death and DALYs of LPARCC. Regarding the age effects, the peak of the longitudinal age curve shifted to over 80 years, which were similar to the peak age of mortality and incidence reported by the previous studies [32]. These age-specific trends in both LPARCC death and DALYs might be might be due to both the decrease of age-related biological and metabolic function and the gained weight related with low physical activity, as explained by the similar studies of stomach cancer [45]. In addition, regarding periods effect, it was a reflection of the changes in term of social, economic and policy, as well as the impact of major events at specific points in time [46]. In this study, we found that the two important decreased time points were 2002 and 2012, which corresponded to the onset of national cancer registry system and national rural and urban cancer screening programs. Finally, birth cohort effects represented the influence of physical and social exposures that appeared earlier in the life process and would accumulate with time [47]. In our study, the cohort RRs for LPARCC mortality/DALYs presented gradually increased trends for those cohorts born since 1950s.

For the changes in trends in LPARCC disease burden over the past 30 years, a recently developed decomposition method was used to partition changes into population growth, population aging, and age-specific changes [32, 48]. As a result, population aging contributed to most of the additional LPARCC deaths and DALYs, which could be explained by the continuous increase of the elderly population and the higher incidence in these age groups. Meanwhile, the change due to risk factors is also deserved attention, which would contribute to the increase of disease burden. Therefore, considering the persistent increase in the number of LPARCC death and DALYs over the past three decades, this cancer should be viewed as be one of the main public health concerns in China. And with the aggravating of population aging, it is necessary to increase the promotion of healthy lifestyle such as integrating structured physical activity programs into community health services, leveraging public health campaigns to counter sedentary behavior among older adults, oganizing low-cost group activity (e.g., walking clubs, tai chi, resistance band training) and using periodic prompts to break up sitting via wearables or phone reminders.

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, as mentioned in earlier GBD study reports [24], the GBD database was mainly estimated based on mathematical models, which might have deviations from actual data. Secondly, the availability and coverage of data source such as cancer registries, vital registration systems, and verbal autopsies varied across countries or regions, which resulted in data heterogeneity [49, 50]. Finally, other lifestyle factors such as high sugar diet, alcohol and smoke consumption and sleep duration were also recognized to be related with the death and DALYs of LPARCC in China, but the interactive effects of LPA and above risk factors on the disease burden were not reported in this study [51]. Despite these limitations, using the up-to-date data and recent decomposition methods, our study firstly provides a comprehensive understanding of the disease burden of LPARCC from 1990 to 2019 years.

In conclusion, the number of LPARCC death and DALYs generally show upward trends from 1990 to 2021, which could be related with the rapid population aging seen in China. It is crucial to strengthen the targeted interventions and address modifiable risk factors, such as advocating and enhancing physical exercise.

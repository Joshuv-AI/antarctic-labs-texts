Unboundedness of zero-cycles on higher dimensional Fano manifolds
Claire Voisin

Abstract
We show that, unlike del Pezzo surfaces, higher dimensional Fano manifolds do not satisfy in general boundedness properties for their CH 0 {\rm CH}_{0} group of 0 0 -cycles. For example, for quartic threefolds having a point of odd degree, there is no “Coray type” uperbound on the minimal odd degrees of points. Also, the CH 0 {\rm CH}_{0} -group of Fano hypersurfaces can be “unbounded” (a notion which is related to infinite dimensionality in the sense of Mumford), meaning that there is no integer N N such that 0 0 -cycles of degree at least N N are effective.

Introduction

In this paper, a variety defined over a field
K
is reduced and geometrically irreducible, that is, irreducible over
\overline{K}
. In particular, the Fano varieties, including del Pezzo surfaces, that we will consider, are smooth projective, geometrically irreducible, with ample anticanonical bundle. For a projective variety

-cycle of

has a

-point.
In
[
5
]
and
[

]
, the following results on points on del Pezzo surfaces of degrees

Question 1.2

Does there exist a number

Theorem 1.4

(i) There exist a field

Remark 1.5
.

For the generic hypersurface

of degree

We will also study in this paper a slightly different notion of (un)boundedness for zero-cycles, that has been investigated first by Mumford in
[
16
]
in the geometric setting and later by Colliot-Thélène in
[
5
]
over non-algebraically closed fields. Consider a smooth projective variety
X
defined over a field
K
of characteristic
0
. Let us consider the following

Definition 1.6
.

We will say that
X
has bounded
{\rm CH}_{0}
-group if there exists a number
N
(possibly depending on
X
and
K
), such that for any field
L\supseteq K
, any
0
-cycle
z\in{\rm CH}_{0}(X_{L})
of degree
{\rm deg}\,z\geq N
is effective (that is, rationally equivalent to an effective
0
-cycle).

We will say that
X
has unbounded
{\rm CH}_{0}
-group if an integer
N
as above does not exist.
We note that smooth projective curves
X
have bounded
{\rm CH}_{0}
-group, as follows from the Riemann-Roch theorem. It follows that a variety
X
has bounded
{\rm CH}_{0}
-group if
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is strongly representable, in the sense that there exists a curve
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X
such that for any field
L\supseteq K
, the morphism

j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(C_{L})\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X_{L})

is surjective.

One should note that for varieties defined over the complex numbers, the notion of (un)boundedness introduced above is close to, but different from, the notion of (in)finite dimensionality used by Mumford in
[
16
]
. Indeed, Mumford only considers
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
, not
{\rm CH}_{0}(X_{L})
for any field
L
containing
\mathbb{C}
. For a smooth projective variety

over the complex numbers, it is in fact proved by Mumford
[
16
]
and Roitman
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
, that Mumford’s finite dimensionality is equivalent to the representability of
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
in the weaker sense that there exists a curve
C\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}X
such that the morphism

j_{*}:{\rm CH}_{0}(C)\rightarrow{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
is surjective. This is also equivalent to the fact that the Albanese map induces an isomorphism between the group
{\rm CH}_{0}(X)_{0}
of zero-cycles of degree
0
on
X
and the (group of complex points of the) Albanese variety
{\rm Alb}(X)
.

For varieties over
\mathbb{C}
, the two notions (boundedness as above and finite dimensionality in the Mumford sense) coincide if one considers Chow groups with rational coefficients, thanks to the Bloch-Srinivas argument
[
1
]
.

Inspired by the work of Colliot-Thélène
[
5
]
, we proved in
[

]
the following

Theorem 1.7
.

Let

be a smooth del Pezzo surface of degree

, where

, defined over a field of characteristic

. Then any

of degree

is effective, hence

is bounded.

Colliot-Thélène establishes Theorem
1.7
for rational surfaces over a field

of characteristic

having a

Applying the same method as in
[

, we will extend in Section

to del Pezzo surfaces of degree

Theorem 1.8
.

There exists an integer

with the following property: for any smooth del Pezzo surface

of characteristic

, any
0

Theorem

The integer

Remark 1.9

As Colliot-Thélène mentioned to me, for del Pezzo surfaces of degree

At the opposite, when

2
A review and extension of Mumford’s results on infinite dimensionality of
{\rm CH}_{0}

Our goal in this section is to prove Theorem
1.12
. Our plan is to adapt Mumford’s argument in
[
16
]
(proving over the complex numbers infinite dimensionality of
{\rm CH}_{0}
in presence of differential forms of degree
>1
) to prove over any field unboundedness of
{\rm CH}_{0}
under the same assumptions, but we will meet difficulties due to inseparability that prevent to directly apply Proposition
2.2
below.
We first review Mumford’s theorem, adapted following
[
20
]
to work over any field. Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over a field
K
, and let
X^{(N)}
be the
N
-th symmetric product of
X
. It is singular but we will work with the open set
X^{(N)}_{0}
parameterizing
N
distinct unordered points, which is smooth. Consider the universal subscheme

\Gamma_{N}\subset X^{(N)}_{0}\times X,

which is flat and proper over
X^{(N)}_{0}
. We denote by

{\rm pr}_{X^{(N)}_{0}}:X^{(N)}_{0}\times X\rightarrow X^{(N)}_{0},\,\,{\rm pr}_{X}:X^{(N)}_{0}\times X\rightarrow X

the two projections.

As
X
is smooth projective of dimension
n
, there is for any
l
a push-forward map

{\rm pr}_{X^{(N)}_{0}*}:H^{n}(X^{(N)}_{0}\times X,\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}\times X/K}^{n+l})\rightarrow H^{0}(X^{(N)}_{0},\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}/K}^{l})

and thus, for any class
\gamma\in H^{n}(X^{(N)}_{0}\times X,\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}\times X/K}^{n})
, an action

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}:H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{l})\rightarrow H^{0}(X^{(N)}_{0},\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}/K}^{l}),

(2)

\displaystyle\gamma^{*}(\alpha)={\rm pr}_{X^{(N)}_{0}*}(\gamma\wedge{\rm pr}_{X}^{*}\alpha).

In particular, for
\gamma=[\Gamma_{N}]
, where
[\Gamma_{N}]\in H^{n}(X^{(N)}_{0}\times X,\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}\times X/K}^{n})
is the Gros cycle class used in
[
20
]
, and which depends only on the class of
\Gamma_{N}
in
{\rm CH}^{n}(X^{(N)}_{0}\times X)
, we get for any
\alpha\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{l})
a
l
-form

[\Gamma_{N}]^{*}\alpha\in H^{0}(X^{(N)}_{0},\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}/K}^{l}).

Remark 2.1
.

The construction above gives more generally, for any
l
, for any smooth variety
Y
over
K
and any cycle
\Gamma\in{\rm CH}^{n}(Y\times X)
, a morphism

[\Gamma]^{*}:H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{l})\rightarrow H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y/K}^{l}).

In practice, we will compute
[\Gamma]^{*}\omega
as a trace, at least at the generic point of
Y
.

Let
q:X^{N}\rightarrow X^{(N)}
be the natural quotient map, and let
X^{N}_{0}:=q^{-1}(X^{(N)}_{0})\subset X^{N}
.
We observe next that

(q,{\rm Id}_{X})^{*}\Gamma_{N}=\sum_{i}\Gamma_{i}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}^{n}(X^{N}_{0}\times X),

where
\Gamma_{i}\subset X^{N}_{0}\times X
is the graph of the
i
-th projection
{\rm pr}_{i}:X^{N}_{0}\rightarrow X
.
It follows that

\displaystyle(q,{\rm Id}_{X})^{*}[\Gamma_{N}]=\sum_{i}[\Gamma_{i}]\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{n}(X^{N}_{0}\times X,\Omega_{X^{N}_{0}\times X/K}^{n}).

(3)

The contravariant functoriality of the constructions above
gives

\displaystyle q^{*}[\Gamma_{N}]^{*}\alpha=\sum_{i}[\Gamma_{i}]^{*}\alpha=\sum_{i}{\rm pr}_{i}^{*}\alpha\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{0}(X^{N}_{0},\Omega_{X^{N}_{0}/K}^{l})

(4)

for any

\alpha\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{l})
.
We now make the following assumption

(*)
there exist an effective cycle

Z\in{\rm CH}^{n}(X^{(N)}\times X)

of relative degree
N^{\prime}
over
X^{(N)}

and a
Zariski open set
U\subset X^{(N)}_{0}

such that

(a) the corresponding morphism

\phi_{Z}:U\rightarrow X^{(N^{\prime})}

is well-defined and takes value in
X^{(N^{\prime})}_{0}
and,

(b) for some
z_{0}\in{\rm CH}_{0}(X)
of degree
N-N^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{N\mid U\times X}=U\times z_{0}+\Gamma_{N^{\prime}}\circ\phi_{Z}\,\,{\rm in}\,\,{\rm CH}^{n}(U\times X).

(5)

The cycle class

[\Gamma_{N}]\in H^{n}(X^{(N)}_{0}\times X,\Omega_{X^{(N)}_{0}\times X/K}^{n})

then satisfies

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{N}]_{\mid U\times X}=[U\times z_{0}]+(\phi_{Z},{\rm Id}_{X})^{*}[\Gamma_{N^{\prime}}]\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{n}(U\times X,\Omega_{U\times X}^{n}).

(6)

From (
6
), we deduce that, under the assumption (
5
)

\displaystyle([\Gamma_{N}]^{*}\alpha)_{\mid U}=\phi_{Z}^{*}([\Gamma_{N^{\prime}}]^{*}\alpha)\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{0}(U,\Omega_{U/K}^{l})

(7)

for any
l>0
and
\alpha\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{l})
, because
[U\times z_{0}]^{*}
acts as zero on
l
-forms with

.
Let
\widehat{U}\subset X^{N}
be the inverse image of
U
via the natural quotient map
q:X^{N}_{0}\rightarrow X^{(N)}_{0}
and let
\hat{\phi}_{Z}:=\phi_{Z}\circ q:\widehat{U}\rightarrow X^{(N^{\prime})}_{0}
.

Equation (
7
) gives after pull-back to
\widehat{U}
, using (
4
)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\rm pr}_{i}^{*}\alpha=\hat{\phi}_{Z}^{*}([\Gamma_{N^{\prime}}]^{*}\alpha)\,\,{\rm in}\,\,H^{0}(\widehat{U},\Omega_{\widehat{U}/K}^{l})

(8)

for any
l>0
and
\alpha\in H^{0}(X,\Omega_{X/K}^{l})
.

We now prove

Proposition 2.2
.

(A version of Mumford-Roitman theorem
[

]
,
[

]

.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

We will use the naive notion of rank

of a
l

is defined as the rank of

We have the following rather obvious

Lemma 2.3
.

(i) Let
\phi:V\rightarrow W
be a linear map, and let
\omega\in\bigwedge^{l}W^{*}
. Then the rank of
\phi^{*}\omega
is non greater than the rank of
\omega

(ii) Let

be a vector space of the form

\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{i}{\rm pr}_{i}^{*}\omega_{i}

is given by

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\omega)=\sum_{i}{\rm rk}(\omega_{i}).

(11)

(iii) The rank of a
l
-form
\omega\in\bigwedge^{l}V^{*}
is lower-semicontinuous on
\bigwedge^{l}V^{*}
equiped with the Zariski topology.

{\rm rk}(\sum_{i}\omega_{i})\leq\sum_{i}{\rm rk}(\omega_{i})

Proof.

(i) follows immediately from the definition of the rank, and from the fact that

\lrcorner\phi^{*}\omega=\phi^{*}\circ\lrcorner\omega\circ\phi:V\rightarrow\bigwedge^{l-1}V^{*}.

For (ii), a form

as in (

because the map

\lrcorner\omega

\displaystyle\lrcorner\omega=\sum_{i}{\rm pr}_{i}^{*}\circ\lrcorner\omega_{i}\circ{\rm pr}_{i}.

is actually

, let

be the kernel of
\lrcorner\omega
.
We observe from (

) that a vector

belongs to
V_{\omega}
if and only if

\displaystyle v_{i}\lrcorner\omega_{i}=0\,\,{\rm for\,\,all}\,\,i.

(13)

Note that this is at this point that we use the condition

, which implies that each

has degree

, so that the spaces
{\rm pr}_{i}^{*}(\bigwedge^{l-1}V_{i}^{*})
are in direct sum in
\bigwedge^{l-1}V^{*}
.
Equality (

(iii) The lower semi-continuity of

immediately follows from the lower semi-continuity of the rank of a linear map or matrix, since it is defined as the rank of linear map
\lrcorner\omega
of (

(iv) follows from (i) and (ii) when

We prove in this section Theorem

Brain network classification considering directed propagation mechanisms of dynamic graphs
Authors: Xinlei Wang, Zhongyang Wang, Keyan Cao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The classification of functional brain networks plays an important role in the diagnosis of neurodegenerative diseases, brain decoding and other fields. Functional brain networks can effectively reflect the functional connection relationships between brain regions or neurons and accurately represent brain activities. Therefore, a large number of problems related to the classification of functional brain networks have been studied. However, the traditional functional brain network merely measures the static correlation between brain regions or neurons in a simple way, and does not reflect the causal transmission effect between brain regions. This directionality is crucial for the regulatory relationship between brain regions. Furthermore, since the brain is constantly in a state of dynamic change, the dynamics of functional connectivity also plays a very important role in the classification of functional brain networks. Therefore, we propose a classification framework named Dynamic Directed Propagation Networks (DDPN) for functional brain networks considering the dynamic directed propagation mechanism. This method effectively captures the dynamics and directionality of the dynamic directed brain network and further improves the classification accuracy of the functional brain network. To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, we conduct experiments on real datasets. The experiments show that the proposed method improved by 3.1–4.1% compared with state-of-the art methods in two datasets.

Introduction

With the rapid development of neuroimaging technology, the study of functional brain networks based on functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) has become an important tool for revealing the complex working mechanism of the brain [1]. The functional brain network constructs a topological model reflecting the dynamic and collaborative activities of the brain by quantifying the functional connection strength between different brain regions. In recent years, classification methods based on functional brain networks have demonstrated great potential in neuroscience and clinical medicine. Their major objective is to extract distinguishing features from high-dimensional functional connection data through machine learning or deep learning models, thereby achieving precise classification of brain diseases, cognitive states, and even individual behaviors [2]. The classification technology of functional brain networks has made breakthrough progress in multiple fields, such as the diagnosis of neuropsychiatric diseases [3] and the decoding of cognitive states [4]. The functional brain network can vividly represent the functional connections between brain regions or neurons in the form of graphs. By using deep learning models, the functional connection matrix in non-Euclidean space can be directly processed, while retaining the topological structure information of the network. Meanwhile, graph representations such as abnormal attribute semantic functional connection interruption of the network can be directly related to the cognitive decline of neurodegenerative diseases, providing biological significance for classification models [5].

Most classical functional connections are computed based on Pearson correlation coefficients or mutual information to construct undirected networks, which cannot distinguish the direction of information transmission. However, the conduction of neural signals is causal and sequential in essence. In the field of disease classification, directed networks have been proven to be effective [6]. To address this limitation, directed connection estimation methods such as Granger causality analysis [7], Dynamic Causal Model (DCM), and Transfer Entropy [8] have been introduced, which can effectively reveal the directionality of functional connections between brain regions or neurons. However, the existing methods are sensitive to noise and have high computational complexity. How to extract robust classification features from directed connections still needs to be broken through.

The traditional static functional brain network simplifies brain activity into steady-state connection patterns through the time-averaging assumption, ignoring the inherent non-stationary characteristics of neural activity [9]. Dynamic functional connectivity can capture time-varying abnormalities of brain activities in resting-state fMRI data. For example, the abnormal mechanism of brain activities in patients with schizophrenia can be effectively revealed through dynamic functional brain networks [10]. For this reason, the dynamic functional connectivity analysis method emerged, capturing the temporal evolution rule of brain networks through sliding windows, hidden Markov models or spatio-temporal graph neural networks [11]. However, most of the existing research on dynamic network classification is based on undirected connections. How to capture the directionality of dynamic changes is an important challenge.

In response to the above challenges, we propose a classification method for functional brain networks considering the directed propagation mechanism of dynamic graphs. By dividing the time window, a dynamic functional brain network is constructed, and the directional weights at different time periods are encoded in combination with the spatio-temporal attention mechanism to quantify the direction of the functional connection information flow and construct a spatio-temporal coupled dynamic directed connection matrix. This network breaks through the biological simplification assumption of the traditional static/undirected model, simultaneously captures the time-varying reorganization rule and cross-brain region causal interaction pattern of the brain network in the classification task. Ultimately, the constantly changing dynamic directed connection features are updated through message passing, the deep feature extraction of functional connections is optimized, and the classification accuracy of functional brain networks is improved.

Related work

Traditional static undirected brain networks can effectively capture the functional connection relationships between brain neurons or brain regions. In recent years, they are extensively studied and are often applied in lots of fields such as disease classification or brain decoding. Such methods are usually based on fMRI or electroencephalogram (EEG) data. They construct undirected networks by calculating the statistical correlations between brain regions (such as Pearson correlation coefficient and mutual information), and then conduct classification research using graph theory features (such as node degree, clustering coefficient) or machine learning models.

In the early stage of brain network research, researchers conduct studies based on the simple assumption that the functional connections between brain regions are static and undirected. Hao et al. [12] propose a novel multi-modal neuroimaging feature selection method with consistent metric constraint for AD analysis. Huang et al. [13] propose a novel Anatomy-Guided Brain Network Transformer (AGBN-Transformer) to automatically learn features of brain networks for Schizophrenia diagnosis. Dong et al. [14] propose an adaptive graph isomorphic information bottleneck Mamba to dynamically optimize the brain network structure by preserving essential information, filtering out irrelevant features, and minimizing redundancy. The advantages of this kind of static undirected brain network lie in its simple model, high computational efficiency, and the ability to effectively represent the cooperative activity patterns of brain regions. However, its limitations are significant. Firstly, undirected networks cannot distinguish the direction of information transmission and ignore causal or hierarchical relationships in brain functional activities; Secondly, static hypotheses ignore the time-varying characteristics of brain functional networks and are difficult to capture the dynamic reorganization process of brain networks in diseases or cognitive tasks.

To overcome the limitations of undirected networks, researchers have proposed a directed brain network classification method, which describes the causal or driving relationship between brain regions by introducing directional information. Typical methods include Granger Causality analysis, Dynamic Causal Model (DCM), and Bayesian networks. Zhu et al. [15] propose a Directed Brain Network Transformer (DBNT) for psychiatric diagnosis. Gao et al. [16] propose a stepwise multivariate Granger causality method which considering both the directed and hierarchical features of brain functional network to explore the stepwise causal relationship in the network. Ji et al. [17] propose a multi-subgraph attention diffusion to estimate large-scale brain effective connectivity networks. Wang et al. [18] propose a two-stage brain effective connectivity network construction method based on dynamic Bayesian network. The advantage of directed networks lies in their ability to reveal the information flow direction of brain functional activities, thereby being closer to the neurophysiological mechanism. However, the existing methods still face two major challenges. First, most studies are still based on static assumptions, considering the directional relationship throughout the entire time period as constant and ignoring the characteristic that the directionality of brain networks may evolve with tasks or time. Secondly, the high-dimensional characteristics of directed networks are prone to cause model overfitting, especially with limited generalization ability in small sample medical data.

In recent years, the dynamic brain network classification method has gradually become a research hotspot. Its important idea is to model the brain functional network as a dynamic system that evolves over time. Thapaliya et al. [19] leverage temporal causal convolutional networks to capture the temporal dynamics in both low- and high-level feature representations. Yang et al. [20] propose a novel spatio-temporal hub identification method that fully leverages dynamic graph embedding to distinguish temporal hubs from peripheral nodes. Zhu et al. [21] propose an optimal transport theory to capture the topology evolution of the dynamic brain networks. The advantage of dynamic methods lies in their ability to capture the reorganization rules of brain networks at the millisecond to minute scale, such as the dynamic separation and integration of modular structures in the task state. However, most of the existing dynamic methods focus on undirected network analysis. Only a few studies [6] attempt to combine dynamics and directionality, and often face the trade-off problem between high computational complexity and the accuracy of temporal resolution and directionality estimation.

From static undirected networks to directed networks and then to dynamic analysis, brain network classification methods gradually approach the spatiotemporal complexity of real neural activities. However, there are still significant gaps in the existing work. Static directed networks ignore temporal evolution, dynamic undirected networks lose directional information, and the research on dynamic directed networks is still in its infancy, especially lacking explicit modeling of the propagation mechanism (such as the diffusion path of information flow in dynamic networks). The dynamic directed propagation mechanism modeling framework we proposed in this paper aims to simultaneously address the triple challenges of directionality, dynamics and propagation mode, effectively improving the classification accuracy of brain networks.

Method

In order to effectively reflect the dynamic change rules and causal effect relationships of brain activities and mine useful features, a functional brain network classification framework based on the dynamic directed propagation mechanism called Dynamic Directed Propagation Network (DDPN) is proposed. Firstly, a dynamic directed brain network is constructed by using the sliding window and Granger linear multiple autoregressive method, and then the classification of the brain network is achieved through the DDPN method.

Dynamic directed brain networks

In order to reflect the causal relationship and dynamic changes of brain regions, a dynamic directed brain network is constructed. It is constructed by data preprocessing, Blood Oxygenation Level Dependent (BOLD) signals extraction, selection of the sliding window, causal matrix construction and thresholding. Firstly, the fMRI images are preprocessed, including head movement correction, time slice correction, etc. Then, the BOLD signals of each brain region are extracted. The BOLD signals are segmented into S time periods according to the sliding window. Then, the causal intensity is calculated for each time period respectively and thresholding is carried out to obtain the causal connection relationship under each time period.

It is worth noting that Granger causality is one of the most mature and widely applied directed connection analysis methods at present. Granger causality is based on the multiple autoregressive models. If the history of brain region A can significantly improve the prediction accuracy of the future signal of brain region B, then it is considered that A has Granger causal influence on B. This definition has a clear statistical interpretation. Compared with the black-box deep learning methods, the parameters of the Granger causality model can directly reflect the direction, strength and delay information of the connection. This interpretability enables researchers to understand the basis of network construction and provide a traceable foundation for subsequent attention visualization. However, the brain is a highly nonlinear complex system, and the discharge activities of neurons, synaptic transmission, and interactions between brain regions often contain significant nonlinear components. If the information transmission between real brain regions has strong nonlinearity, linear Granger causality may not be able to fully capture this interaction, resulting in some effective connections being omitted. Therefore, Granger causality is essentially a statistical predictive causal relationship rather than a biological or physical real causal relationship.

Dynamic directed propagation network

In order to capture the potential characteristics of dynamic directed brain networks, the dynamic directed propagation network is proposed. Firstly, a BiLSTM network is adopted to model the dynamic propagation mode of the directional weights of edges. Then, spatio-temporal attention is used to quantify the importance differences of connections between different time points and brain regions. Next, the Transformer method is used to fuse the directional weight encoding and node features at the current moment, each time slice is pooled, and then the pooled features are aggregated across time slices. Experiments

To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method, the functional brain network classification is conducted on public datasets. Experiments proved that the proposed method could significantly improve the classification accuracy of brain networks. The datasets we selected are ABIDE-I and OASIS-3. The dataset ABIDE-I is available at http://preprocessed-connectomes-project.org/abide, with a total of 1112 cases. Among them, there are 539 patients with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and 573 typically developing subjects, called Normal Controls (NC). The dataset OASIS 3 is available at http://www.oasis-brains.org, with a total of 666 cases of data, including 326 cases of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) patients and 340 cases of typically developing subjects. The baseline method is compared on these two datasets respectively to verify the model parameters and ablation experiments, verifying the effectiveness of the proposed DDPN method.

The experimental results of the classification task

The comparative experiments on the two datasets show that the DDPN method proposed in this paper is significantly superior to the existing methods in classification performance. On the two datasets of ABIDE-I and OASIS-3, the accuracies of the static undirected method AGBN-Transformer are 80.13% and 79.65% respectively, which has the weakest performance. The accuracies of the static directed method DBNT has increased to 80.92% and 80.51%, indicating that directional information enhances the classification ability. The accuracies of the dynamic undirected method DSAM has further increased to 82.05% and 81.67%, verifying the necessity of dynamic modeling. The accuracies of the dynamic directed method DCSTN are 86.52% and 85.43%, indicating that the combination of dynamics and directionality can further improve performance. The DDPN method we proposed significantly outperforms all baseline methods with accuracies of 89.21% and 88.89%, indicating that the proposed dynamic directed propagation mechanism can effectively improve the classification accuracy performance.

It can be found through the experimental results that the dynamic methods (including DSAM, DCSTN, DDPN) improve the accuracies by 2.5% 7.3% compared with the static methods, mainly due to the capture of the time-varying characteristics of functional brain networks. For example, the default mode network of patients with Alzheimer’s disease may exhibit dynamic connection strength attenuation in tasks, and static methods cannot model such instantaneous changes. The dynamic sliding window captures the pathologically related network reorganization patterns more sensitively through the analysis of local time segments. In addition, directional methods (including DBNT, DCSTN, DDPN) improve the accuracies by 1.1% to 6.5% compared with undirected methods. This is because directional connections can reflect the causal mechanisms of neural activity. For instance, the inhibitory regulation of the hippocampus by the prefrontal cortex may be abnormal in Alzheimer’s disease. Quantifying the flow of information by calculating causal relationships provides more discriminative features for classification.

C

Conclusion

In view of the deficiencies of the existing classification methods of functional brain networks in dynamic and directional modeling, we propose a brain network classification framework DDPN that effectively characterizes the dynamic directed propagation mechanism. This method constructs a dynamic directed brain network through the sliding window and the Granger linear multiple autoregressive method, uses BiLSTM to encode the dynamic evolution of directional weights, and combines the spatio-temporal attention mechanism to screen key spatio-temporal nodes. The global interaction between nodes and edges is achieved through Transformer, and finally the cross-temporal features are classified by dynamic pooling and fusion. Classification experiments are conducted respectively on the ABIDE-I and OASIS-3 datasets. The experiments showed that DDPN achieved accuracies of 89.21% and 88.89% respectively in the classification tasks of AD and ASD, which is 3.1–4.1% higher than the optimal baseline method. The ablation experiments further verify the necessity of the dynamic propagation mechanism, the interaction between the attention module and the Transformer in the DDPN method.

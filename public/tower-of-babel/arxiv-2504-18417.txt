On the eta invariant of (2,3,5) distributions
Stefan Haller

Abstract
. We consider the Rumin complex associated with a generic rank two distribution on a closed 5-manifold. The Rumin differential in middle degrees gives rise to a self-adjoint differential operator of Heisenberg order two. We study the eta function and the eta invariant of said operator, twisted by unitary flat vector bundles. For (2,3,5) nilmanifolds this eta invariant vanishes but the eta function is nontrivial, in general. We establish a formula expressing the eta function of (2,3,5) nilmanifolds in terms of more elementary functions.

Introduction

The eta invariant of the odd signature operator has been introduced by Atiyah, Patodi, and Singer
[
4
,
5
]

in order to extend the Hirzebruch signature theorem to compact manifolds with boundary.
The eta invariant of a contact analogue of the odd signature operator has been studied by Biquard, Herzlich, and Rumin
[
10
,
55
]
.
The latter is a pseudohermitian invariant, that is, it depends on a contact form and an almost complex structure on the contact hyperplane field.
In dimension three, and up to a local quantity, the contact eta invariant coincides
[
10
, Thm 1.1]
with the
\nu
-invariant introduced by Biquard and Herzlich
[
9
]

which is a CR-invariant, i.e., independent of the contact form.
More recently, Albin and Quan have shown that on contact manifolds of arbitrary dimension the relative contact eta invariant coincides with the Riemannian analogue
[
1
, Cor 2]
.

In this paper we study the eta invariant of generic rank two distributions on closed 5-manifolds.
Recall that a generic rank two distribution in dimension five, a.k.a. (2,3,5) distribution, is a rank two subbundle
\mathcal{D}\subseteq TM
in the tangent bundle of a smooth 5-manifold
M

such that Lie brackets of sections of
\mathcal{D}
span a rank three subbundle
[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]\subseteq TM

and triple brackets of sections of
\mathcal{D}
span all of the tangent bundle,
[\mathcal{D},[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]]=TM
.
Clearly, this is a
C^{2}
-open condition on
\mathcal{D}
, whence the name
generic rank two distribution.

Such a distribution gives rise to a filtration of the tangent bundle by smooth subbundles,

TM\supseteq[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]\supseteq\mathcal{D}.

(1)

The ranks of these filtration bundles are 2, 3, and 5, respectively, whence the name
(2,3,5) distribution.

We will denote the associated graded bundle by

\mathfrak{t}M=\mathfrak{t}_{-3}M\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{-2}M\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{-1}M

(2)

where
\mathfrak{t}_{-1}M=\mathcal{D}
,
\mathfrak{t}_{-2}M=\frac{[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]}{\mathcal{D}}
, and
\mathfrak{t}_{-3}M=\frac{TM}{[\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}]}
.
The Lie bracket of vector fields induces the structure of a graded nilpotent Lie algebra on the fibers of
\mathfrak{t}M
.
Each fiber is isomorphic to the graded nilpotent Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-3}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}

(3)

with graded basis
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-1}
,
X_{3}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-2}
,
X_{4},X_{5}\in\mathfrak{g}_{-3}
and nontrivial brackets

[X_{1},X_{2}]=X_{3},\quad[X_{1},X_{3}]=X_{4},\quad[X_{2},X_{3}]=X_{5}.

(4)

This turns
\mathfrak{t}M
into a locally trivial bundle of Lie algebras called the bundle of osculating algebras.
The fiberwise Lie bracket provides canonical isomorphisms

\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{t}_{-1}M=\mathfrak{t}_{-2}M\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{t}_{-1}M\otimes\mathfrak{t}_{-2}M=\mathfrak{t}_{-3}M.

(5)

We let
G
denote the simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
The left invariant distribution
\mathcal{D}_{G}
spanned by the vector fields
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
provides a basic example of a (2,3,5) distribution on
G
.
Up to an (in general ungraded) automorphism every left invariant (2,3,5) distribution on
G
is of this form, cf.
[
36
, Lemma 4.1]
.

The study of generic rank two distributions in dimension five reaches back to the work of Élie Cartan.
In his seminal paper
[
20

Cartan constructed what is nowadays known as canonical Cartan connection.
This leads to an equivalent description of a (2,3,5) distribution
as a parabolic geometry
[

[

In particular, (2,3,5) distributions do have local geometry — unlike contact and Engel structures
[

Cartan’s harmonic curvature tensor,

(2,3,5)
distribution is locally diffeomorphic to the left invariant distribution

The Rumin complex
[

,

,

]
associated with a (2,3,5) distribution is sequence of natural differential operators

\cdots\to\Gamma\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathfrak{t}M)\bigr)\xrightarrow{D_{q}}\Gamma\bigl(\mathcal{H}^{q+1}(\mathfrak{t}M)\bigr)\to\cdots

and computing de Rham cohomology.
Here the vector bundles

are obtained from the bundle of osculating algebras

There exist (non-canonical, in this formulation) injective differential operators

intertwining the Rumin differential with the de Rham differential and inducing canonical isomorphisms in cohomology.
Rumin has shown that this complex becomes exact on the level of the Heisenberg principal symbol.
Hence, the Rumin complex is a Rockland
[
53
]
complex
[

, §2.3]
, the analogue of an elliptic complex in the Heisenberg calculus
[

,

A fiberwise graded Euclidean inner product

\mathfrak{t}M

\mathfrak{o}
of

\star_{q}=\star_{\mathcal{D},g,\mathfrak{o},q}^{M}\colon\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathfrak{t}M)\to\mathcal{H}^{5-q}(\mathfrak{t}M)

[

Let

Twisting

(6)

This operator is formally self-adjoint with respect to the standard

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a generic rank two distribution on a closed 5-manifold
M

let

\mathfrak{t}M
,

\mathfrak{o}

and let

Then the associated operator

The non-zero part of its spectrum,

The eta function

\eta_{S}(s):=\sum_{\lambda\in\spec_{*}(S)}\sign(\lambda)|\lambda|^{-s}

(8)

converges absolutely for
\Re s>5
.
This function extends to an entire function on the complex plane that vanishes at negative odd integers, i.e.,

\eta_{S}(-2l-1)=0,\qquad l=0,1,2,\dotsc

(9)

Moreover,

\mathcal{D}
in the space of generic rank two distributions on

In view of Theorem

only depends on the diffeomorphism type of

, the orientation
\mathfrak{o}
of
M
, the unitary flat vector bundle
F
over
M
,
and the connected component of
\mathcal{D}
in the space of generic rank two distributions on
M
.
We denote this invariant by

\eta_{F}(M,\mathcal{D},\mathfrak{o}):=\eta(S).

(11)

Formally putting
s=0
is (

is, in a zeta regularized sense, the difference between the number of positive and negative eigenvalues of
S

Hence,

To formulate this result we need to introduce more notation.

For

Let us close this introduction with a few preliminary remarks concerning the proofs of the aforementioned theorems.

The operator
S
in (
6
) is closely related to the Rockland operator

\Delta=\Delta^{M,F}_{\mathcal{D},g}=\bigl((D_{1}D_{1}^{*})^{2}+(D_{2}^{*}D_{2})^{3}\bigr)^{F}.

The short time asymptotic expansion of
\tr(Se^{-t\Delta})
provides the analytic continuation of
\eta_{S}(s)
via Mellin transform,

\eta_{S}(s)=\tr\left(S\Delta^{-(s+1)/6}\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{6}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1)/6}\tr\bigl(Se^{-t\Delta}\bigr)\frac{dt}{t},\quad\Re s>5.

(27)

The coefficients in this expansion give all residues of
\eta_{S}(s)
and the values at
s=-1,-7,-13,\dotsc

For the values at the remaining negative odd integers we replace
S
with odd powers of
S
, cf. Proposition
3
.
As the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion of
\tr(S^{1+2r}e^{-t\Delta})
are locally computable,
they coincide with the coefficients associated with the trivial flat vector bundle,
F_{\mathrm{triv}}=M\times\mathbb{C}^{\rk F}
.
However, the eta function associated with

vanishes identically since the spectrum of

is symmetrical about zero in this case, cf.
[

Hence, the coefficients in the aforementioned expansion vanish, for all unitary flat bundles

, see Proposition

This yields everything but the last part of Theorem

For the remaining statement we show that the variation of the eta invariant with respect to

and

is locally computable
and argue as before to see that this variation must vanish too.

There are well-understood topological obstructions to the existence of (2,3,5) distributions with prescribed Euler class, cf.

One motivation for our study of the Rumin complex is the natural question: Are there further, geometrical obstructions to the existence of (2,3,5) distributions on closed 5-manifolds?

Similar questions have led to interesting discoveries in contact and Engel topology

2.
The eta invariant on filtered manifolds

The analysis available in the literature permits to give a rigorous definition of the eta invariant
[
4
,
5
]

of (formally) self-adjoint Rockland differential operators on general closed filtered manifolds
by generalizing the approach used in
[
10
, §9]
and
[
32
, Chapter 1.10]
.
In this section we will recall these arguments and point to the required subelliptic analysis
[
46
,
30
,
25
,
26
]
.

To this end let
A=A^{*}
be a formally self-adjoint differential operator of Heisenberg order
\kappa>0
acting on
sections of a complex vector bundle
E
over a closed filtered manifold
M
of homogeneous dimension
n
.
Here the adjoint is with respect to a standard
L^{2}
inner product on sections of
E
constructed using a fiberwise Hermitian inner product on
E
and a volume density on
M
.
Assume that
A
satisfies the (pointwise) Rockland condition
[
26
, Def 2.5]
.
Then
A
admits a parametrix in the Heisenberg calculus
[
26
, Thm A]
.
Consequently,
A
is essentially self-adjoint with compact resolvent
[
25
, Lemma 1]
and, hence, has a spectrum that consists of isolated eigenvalues with finite multiplicities.

Clearly,
A^{2}=A^{*}A=|A|^{2}
is a Rockland differential operator of Heisenberg order
2\kappa>0
.
Moreover,
A|A|^{-s-1}=A(A^{2})^{-(s+1)/2}
is a pseudodifferential operator of Heisenberg order
-\kappa s
in the Heisenberg calculus
[
25
, Thm 2]
,
whence trace class for
\Re s>n/\kappa
, see
[
26
, Prop 3.7(d)]
.
Throughout this paper the complex powers of an operator are understood to vanish on its kernel.
Put

\eta_{A}(s):=\tr\left(A|A|^{-s-1}\right)=\sum_{\lambda\in\spec_{*}(A)}\sign(\lambda)|\lambda|^{-s},\qquad\Re s>n/\kappa

(30)

where
\spec_{*}(A)
denotes the nonzero spectrum of
A
, i.e., the sum is over all nonzero eigenvalues
\lambda
of
A
, repeated according to their multiplicity.

The Schwartz kernel of
Ae^{-tA^{2}}
admits an asymptotic expansion along the diagonal of the form

\bigl(Ae^{-tA^{2}}\bigr)(x,x)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{(j-\kappa-n)/2\kappa}\mathbf{a}_{j}(A,A^{2})(x),\qquad\text{as $t\to 0$,}

where
\mathbf{a}_{j}(A,A^{2})\in\Gamma^{\infty}\bigl(\eend(E)\otimes|\Lambda|_{M}\bigr)
is locally computable, and
\mathbf{a}_{j}(A,A^{2})=0
if
j-\kappa
is odd, cf.
[
36
, Lemma 2.18]
.
Here
|\Lambda|_{M}
denotes the bundle of densities on
M
.
Taking the pointwise trace and integrating over
M
we obtain the expansion

\tr\bigl(Ae^{-tA^{2}}\bigr)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{(j-\kappa-n)/2\kappa}a_{j}(A,A^{2}),\qquad\text{as $t\to 0$,}

(31)

with coefficients
a_{j}(A,A^{2})=\int_{M}\tr_{E}(\mathbf{a}_{j}(A,A^{2}))
and
a_{j}(A,A^{2})=0
if
j-\kappa
is odd.

Proposition 1
.

The function
\eta_{A}(s)
extends meromorphically to the entire complex plane.
Its poles are all simple and they can only be located at
s=(n-j)/\kappa
with
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
j-\kappa
is even.
The residues are

\res_{s=\frac{n-j}{\kappa}}\eta_{A}(s)=2\cdot\frac{a_{j}(A,A^{2})}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+\kappa-j}{2\kappa}\right)},

(32)

where
a_{j}(A,A^{2})
are the coefficients in the expansion (
31
).
In particular,

\res_{s=0}\eta_{A}(s)=2\cdot\frac{a_{n}(A,A^{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}

(33)

and the eta function is regular at
s=0
if
n-\kappa
is odd.
If
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then the eta function is regular at
s=-2l-1
,

\eta_{A}(-2l-1)=(-1)^{l}l!\cdot a_{n+(2l+1)\kappa}(A,A^{2}),

(34)

and this vanishes for odd
n
.

Proof.

Via Mellin transform we get from (
30
)

\eta_{A}(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1)/2}\tr\left(Ae^{-tA^{2}}\right)\frac{dt}{t},\qquad\Re s>n/\kappa.

(35)

The integral converges at infinity since there exists
c>0
such that
\tr(Ae^{-tA^{2}})=O(e^{-ct})
as
\to\infty
.
For
\Re s>n/\kappa
the convergence at zero follows from the asymptotic expansion in (
31
).
Moreover,

\eta_{A}(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{2\cdot a_{j}(A,A^{2})}{s+(j-n)/\kappa}+h_{J}(s)\right)

(36)

where
h_{J}(s)
is holomorphic for
\Re s>(n-J)/\kappa
.
This provides the analytic continuation of
\eta_{A}(s)
with simple poles and residues as in (
32
).
The special values in (
34
) can be read off using

\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}=(-1)^{l}l!\cdot(s+l)+O\bigl((s+l)^{2}\bigr),\qquad\text{as $s\to-l$.}

(37)

Using
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
we obtain (
33
) from (
32
) by putting
j=n
.
∎

The eta function might not be regular at
s=0
in this generality, contrary to the Riemannian case, cf.
[
7
]
or
[
32
, §1.10]
.
We define the eta invariant of
A
as the constant part in the Laurent expansion at the origin,

\eta(A):=\FP_{s=0}\eta_{A}(s).

(38)

We have a variation formula analoguous to the one in
[
7
, Prop 2.10]
,
[
10
, §9]
or
[
32
, Lemma 1.10.2]
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
A_{u}
is a smooth family of (formally) self-adjoint Rockland differential operators of Heisenberg order
\kappa>0

such that
\ker(A_{u})
has constant dimension where
u\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{A_{u}}(s)=-s\cdot\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}|A_{u}|^{-s-1}\right)

(39)

where
\dot{A}_{u}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}A_{u}
.
In particular,
\res_{s=0}\eta_{A_{u}}(s)
is constant in
u
and

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta(A_{u})=-2\cdot\frac{a_{n}(\dot{A}_{u},A_{u}^{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}

where
a_{j}(\dot{A}_{u},A_{u}^{2})
denote the constants in the asymptotic expansion

\tr\bigl(\dot{A}_{u}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\bigr)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{(j-\kappa-n)/2\kappa}a_{j}(\dot{A}_{u},A_{u}^{2}),\qquad\text{as $t\to 0$,}

(40)

and
a_{j}(\dot{A}_{u},A_{u}^{2})=0
if
j-\kappa
is odd.

Proof.

The asymptotic expansion in (
40
) follows from
[
36
, Lemma 2.18]
.
Let
\Pi_{u}
denote the orthogonal projection onto
\ker(A_{u})
.
Note that
\Pi_{u}
depends smoothly on
u
since we assumed that
\ker(A_{u})
has constant dimension.
Differentiating
A_{u}\Pi_{u}=0
we obtain
\dot{A}_{u}\Pi_{u}+A_{u}\dot{\Pi}_{u}=0
and then
\Pi_{u}\dot{A}_{u}\Pi_{u}=0
, for we also have
\Pi_{u}A_{u}=0
by symmetry of
A_{u}
.
Hence, there exists
c>0
such that
\tr\bigl(\dot{A}_{u}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\bigr)=O(e^{-ct})
as
t\to\infty
.

In view of the latter estimate Duhamel’s formula applied to (
35
) yields

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{A_{u}}(s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1)/2}\left(\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\right)-2t\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}A_{u}^{2}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\right)\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1)/2}\left(\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\right)+2t\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\right)\right)\frac{dt}{t}

for
\Re s>n/\kappa
, and integration by parts leads to, cf.
[
32
, Lemma 1.10.2]
,

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{A_{u}}(s)=\frac{-s}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1)/2}\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}e^{-tA^{2}_{u}}\right)\frac{dt}{t}=-s\cdot\tr\left(\dot{A}_{u}|A_{u}|^{-s-1}\right),

whence (
39
) for
\Re s>n/\kappa
.

Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
1
we see that
\tr\bigl(\dot{A}_{u}|A_{u}|^{-s-1}\bigr)

extends to a meromorphic function with simple poles on the entire complex plane and

\res_{s=0}\tr\bigl(\dot{A}_{u}|A_{u}|^{-s-1}\bigr)=2\cdot\frac{a_{n}(\dot{A}_{u},A_{u}^{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}.

(41)

By analyticity (
39
) remains true for all
s
.
Combining (
41
) with (
39
) we obtain

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{A_{u}}(s)=-2\cdot\frac{a_{n}(\dot{A}_{u},A_{u}^{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}+O(s),\qquad\text{as $s\to 0$.}

Whence, the remaining assertions in the propositions, cf. (
38
).
∎

Remark 1
.

The residue
\res_{s=0}\eta_{A}(s)
is locally computable in light of (
33
).
Using Proposition
2
one readily shows that this residue only depends on the homotopy class of the Heisenberg principal symbol of
A
.
Moreover,

\kappa\cdot\res_{s=0}\eta_{A}(s)=\tau(\sign(A))

(42)

where the right hand side denotes the analogue of Wodzicki’s noncommutative residue
[
63
]
in the Heisenberg calculus of the operator

\sign(A)=A|A|^{-1}=A(A^{2})^{-1/2}

cf.
[
50
]
and
[
25
, §7]
or
[
23
]
for the generality required here.
To see this note that
\sign(A)
is a pseudodifferential operator of Heisenberg order zero and recall that its noncommutative residue can be expressed in the form
[
25
, p. 377]

\tau(\sign(A))=2\kappa\cdot\res_{s=0}\tr\bigl(\sign(A)(A^{2})^{-s}\bigr).

Moreover, from (
38
) we have
\eta_{A}(s)=\tr\bigl(\sign(A)(A^{2})^{-s/2}\bigr)
, whence (
42
).

3.
The eta invariant of a (2,3,5) distribution

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq TM
be a generic rank two distribution on a closed 5-manifold
M
.
Recall that such a distribution induces a filtration of the tangent bundle as indicated in (
1
).
The associated graded bundle
\mathfrak{t}M
, cf. (
2
), is a bundle of graded nilpotent Lie algebras when equipped with the fiberwise bracket induced by the Lie bracket of vector fields.
The osculating algebras, i.e., the fibers of
\mathfrak{t}M
are all isomorphic to the graded nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in (
3
) with brackets as in (
4
).

Let
F
be a flat complex vector bundle over
M
.
Twisting the Rumin complex
[
54
,
56
,
57
]
with
F
we obtain a sequence of differential operators
D_{q}=D_{\mathcal{D},q}^{F}
,

\Gamma(E_{0})\xrightarrow[k_{0}=1]{D_{0}}\Gamma(E_{1})\xrightarrow[k_{1}=3]{D_{1}}\Gamma(E_{2})\xrightarrow[k_{2}=2]{D_{2}}\Gamma(E_{3})\xrightarrow[k_{3}=3]{D_{3}}\Gamma(E_{4})\xrightarrow[k_{4}=1]{D_{4}}\Gamma(E_{5})

(43)

where
E_{q}:=\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathfrak{t}M)\otimes F
and
\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathfrak{t}M)
denotes the real vector bundle obtained from the bundle of osculating algebras,

\mathfrak{t}M
, by passing to fiberwise Lie algebra cohomology with trivial real coefficients.
The Betti numbers are
\rk\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathfrak{t}M)=\dim H^{q}(\mathfrak{g})=1,2,3,3,2,1
for
q=0,\dotsc,5
,
and the Rumin differential
D_{q}
has Heisenberg order
k_{q}=1,3,2,3,1
for
q=0,\dotsc,4
, as indicated below the arrows in (
43
).
For more details we refer to
[
15
, §5]
,
[
26
, Example 4.21]
, and
[
36
, §3.1]
.

The Rumin complex in (
43
) computes de Rham cohomology
[
54
,
56
,
57
]
.
More precisely, we have
D_{q+1}D_{q}=0
and there exist (non-canonical, in this formulation) differential operators
L_{q}\colon\Gamma(E_{q})\to\Omega^{q}(M;F)

intertwining the Rumin differential with the de Rham differential and inducing canonical isomorphisms
[
36
, Lemma 3.2]

\frac{\ker D_{q}}{\img D_{q-1}}=H^{q}(M;F).

(44)

Let
g
be a graded fiberwise Euclidean metric
g
on
\mathfrak{t}M
, i.e., the sum in (
2
) is assumed to be orthogonal.
Let
\partial_{q}^{*}\colon\Lambda^{q+1}\mathfrak{t}^{*}M\to\Lambda^{q}\mathfrak{t}^{*}M
denote the fiberwise adjoint of
the Chevalley–Eilenberg Lie algebra codifferential
\partial_{q}\colon\Lambda^{q}\mathfrak{t}^{*}M\to\Lambda^{q+1}\mathfrak{t}^{*}M

with respect to the induced fiberwise Euclidean metrics on
\Lambda^{q}\mathfrak{t}^{*}M
.
Fiberwise Hodge decomposition yields an induced fiberwise Euclidean metric on

\mathcal{H}^{q}(\mathfrak{t}M)=\frac{\ker\partial_{q}}{\img\partial_{q-1}}=\ker(\partial_{q-1}^{*})\cap\ker(\partial_{q}).

(45)

Moreover,
g
provides a volume density on
M
via the canonical isomorphism in (
7
).
Together with a fiberwise Hermitian metric
h
on
F
we obtain fiberwise Hermitian metrics on the bundles
E_{q}
and, in turn,
standard
L^{2}
-inner products on
\Gamma(E_{q})
which gives rise to formal adjoints of the Rumin differentials denoted by
D_{q}^{*}=D_{\mathcal{D},F,g,h,q}^{*}
.

Let
\mathfrak{o}
be an orientation of
M
.
Note that the isomorphisms in (
2
), (
5
), and (
7
) provide a canonical one-to-one correspondence between
orientations of
\mathfrak{t}_{-1}M
,
orientations of
\mathfrak{t}_{-2}M
,
orientations of
\mathfrak{t}_{-3}M
,
orientations of
\mathfrak{t}M
, and
orientations of
M
.
The Hodge star operator associated with
g
and
\mathfrak{o}
preserves the subbundle on the right hand side in (
45
).
Twisting with
F
we obtain fiberwise isometries
\star_{q}=\star_{\mathcal{D},g,\mathfrak{o},q}^{F}\colon E_{q}\to E_{5-q}

satisfying
\star^{2}=1
, since
\dim M=5
is odd, see
[
54
, §2]
,
[
56
, Prop 2.8]
or
[
36
, §3.3]
for details.
Hence,

\star_{q}^{-1}=\star_{q}^{*}=\star_{5-q}.

Assuming, moreover,
\nabla h=0
we have
D_{2}^{*}=-\star_{3}D_{2}\,\star_{3}
.
Hence, the operator

S=S^{M,F}_{\mathcal{D},g,\mathfrak{o}}=\mathbf{i}\star_{3}^{F}D_{2}^{F}=\bigl(\mathbf{i}\star_{3}D_{2}\bigr)^{F}

(46)

acting on
\Gamma(E)
, where we abbreviate
E:=E_{2}
, satisfies

S^{*}=S\qquad\text{as well as}\qquad S^{2}=D_{2}^{*}D_{2}.

(47)

Note that
S
and
D_{q}^{*}
do not depend on
h
for we assumed
h
to be parallel.

Proposition
1
is not directly applicable to
S
since this is not a Rockland operator.
However,
S
is closely related to the Rumin–Seshadri
[
58
]
type operator

\Delta:=(D_{1}D_{1}^{*})^{2}+(D_{2}^{*}D_{2})^{3}

(48)

which is a Rockland operator, cf.
[
26
, Lemma 2.14]
.

Proposition 3
.

The operator
S
has an infinite dimensional kernel but the remaining spectrum consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity only.
Moreover,
|S|^{-s}
is trace class for
\Re s>5
and the eta function,

\eta_{S}(s):=\tr\left(S|S|^{-s-1}\right)=\sum_{\lambda\in\spec_{*}(S)}\sign(\lambda)|\lambda|^{-s},\qquad\Re s>5,

(49)

extends to a meromorphic function on the entire complex plane with isolated simple poles that can only be located at
s=(10-j)/2
with
j=0,2,4,\dotsc

The residues are

\res_{s=\frac{10-j}{2}}\eta_{S}(s)=6\cdot\frac{a_{j}(S^{1+2r},\Delta)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{12+4r-j}{12}\right)},\qquad j,r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(50)

where
a_{j}(S^{1+2r},\Delta)
denote the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion

\tr\left(S^{1+2r}e^{-t\Delta}\right)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{(j-12-4r)/12}a_{j}(S^{1+2r},\Delta),\qquad\text{as $t\to 0$,}

(51)

and
a_{j}(S^{1+2r},\Delta)=0
for odd
j
.
Furthermore,
\eta_{S}(s)
is regular at negative odd integers and

\eta_{S}(-1-2r-6l)=(-1)^{l}l!\cdot a_{12+4r+12l}(S^{1+2r},\Delta),\qquad l,r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(52)

Proof.

Combining (
47
) and (
48
) we obtain

\Delta=(D_{1}D_{1}^{*})^{2}+S^{6}.

(53)

Using
D_{2}D_{1}=0
and (
46
) we get
\img D_{1}\subseteq\ker S
and

(D_{1}D_{1}^{*})S=0=S(D_{1}D_{1}^{*}).

(54)

In particular,
S
has infinite dimensional kernel.
As
\Delta
is a Rockland operator of Heisenberg order
12
it has compact resolvent and discrete spectrum
[
25
, Lemma 1]
.
Using (
53
) and (
54
) we conclude that

\spec_{*}(\Delta)=\spec_{*}(D_{1}D_{1}^{*})^{2}\sqcup\spec_{*}(S^{6}).

(55)

Hence, the nonzero spectrum of
S
is discrete.
Moreover,
\Delta^{-s}
is trace class for
\Re s>10/12
, see
[
26
, Cor 2]
.
Combining this with (
55
) we conclude that
|S|^{-s}
is trace class for
\Re s>5
.

On the image of
S
the operator
\Delta
coincides with
S^{6}
by (
53
) and (
54
).
For any
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we thus get from (
49
)

\eta_{S}(s)=\tr\Bigl(S^{1+2r}(S^{6})^{-(s+1+2r)/6}\Bigr)=\tr\left(S^{1+2r}\Delta^{-(s+1+2r)/6}\right).

(56)

The asymptotic expansion in (
51
) follows from
[
36
, Lemma 2.18]
.
Moreover, since
\ker\Delta\subseteq\ker S
there exists
c>0
such that
\tr(S^{1+2r}e^{-t\Delta})=O(e^{-ct})
as
t\to\infty
.
Hence, via Mellin transform

\eta_{S}(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1+2r}{6}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1+2r)/6}\tr\left(S^{1+2r}e^{-t\Delta}\right)\frac{dt}{t},\qquad\Re s>5.

(57)

Moreover,

\eta_{S}(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1+2r}{6}\right)}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{6\cdot a_{j}(S^{1+2r},\Delta)}{s+(j-10)/2}+h_{J}(s)\right)

(58)

where
h_{J}(s)
is holomorphic for
\Re s>(10-J)/2
.
This provides the analytic continuation of
\eta_{S}
with simple poles and residues as in (
50
).
The special values in (
52
) can be read off using (
37
).
∎

Proposition 4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{D}_{u}
is a smooth family of (2,3,5) distributions on
M
and
suppose
g_{u}
is a smooth family of fiberwise graded Euclidean inner products on the corresponding smooth family of bundles of osculating algebras
\mathfrak{t}_{u}M
where
u\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
S_{u}
and
\Delta_{u}
denote the operators associated to this data, an orientation of
M
,
and a unitary flat complex vector bundle
F
, cf. (
46
) and (
48
).
Moreover, put
\dot{S}_{u}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}S_{u}
with respect to a smooth trivialization of the family of bundles of osculating algebras,

\bigsqcup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\mathfrak{t}_{u}M\cong\mathfrak{t}M\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{S_{u}}(s)=-s\cdot\tr\left(\dot{S}_{u}\Delta_{u}^{-(s+1)/6}\right).

(59)

Moreover,

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{S_{u}}(s)=-6\cdot\frac{a_{10}(\dot{S}_{u},\Delta_{u})}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)}+O(s)\quad\text{as $s\to 0$}

(60)

where
a_{j}(\dot{S}_{u},\Delta_{u})
denote the coefficients in the asymptotic expansion

\tr\bigl(\dot{S}_{u}e^{-t\Delta_{u}}\bigr)\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}t^{(j-12)/12}a_{j}(\dot{S}_{u},\Delta_{u}),\qquad\text{as $t\to 0$}

(61)

and
a_{j}(\dot{S}_{u},\Delta_{u})=0
for odd
j
.

Proof.

Specializing (
56
) and (
57
) to
r=0
we obtain

\eta_{S_{u}}(s)=\tr\left(S_{u}\Delta_{u}^{-(s+1)/6}\right)=\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{6}\right)}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{(s+1)/6}\tr\left(S_{u}e^{-t\Delta_{u}}\right)\frac{dt}{t},\quad\Re s>5.

The Hodge decomposition in
[
26
, Cor 2.16]
and (
44
) yield

\ker\Delta_{u}=\ker D_{1,u}^{*}\cap\ker D_{2,u}=\frac{\ker D_{2,u}}{\img D_{1,u}}=H^{2}(M;F).

Hence, the dimension of
\ker\Delta_{u}
is constant in
u
.
Note that
\Delta_{u}
and
S_{u}
commute, cf. (
53
) and (
54
).
Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
2
we use Duhamel’s formula to obtain

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial u}\eta_{S_{u}}(s)=\tr\left(\dot{S}_{u}\Delta_{u}^{-(s+1)/6}\right)-\frac{s+1}{6}\cdot\tr\left(S_{u}\dot{\Delta}_{u}\Delta_{u}^{-(s+1)/6-1}\right),\quad\Re s>5

(62)

where
\dot{\Delta}_{u}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\Delta_{u}
.
Putting
B_{u}:=D_{u,1}D_{u,1}^{*}
we have, cf. (
53
),

\Delta_{u}=B_{u}^{2}+S_{u}^{6}.

Proposition 6
.

The eta invariant of the odd signature operator on a (2,3,5) nilmanifold, with respect to any Riemannian metric and twisted by any unitary flat vector bundle, vanishes.

Proof.

On 5-dimensional manifolds the eta function of the untwisted odd signature operator vanishes identically, cf.
[
5
, Remark 3a on p. 61]
.
Hence, in this dimension, the eta invariant of the odd signature operator, twisted by any unitary flat vector bundle,

[
6

Suppose
\Gamma\subseteq G
is a lattice and let
M=\Gamma{\setminus}G
denote the corresponding (2,3,5) nilmanifold.
We have to show that the rho invariant associated with a unitary flat vector bundle
F
over
M
vanishes.
Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
2
above we see that
it suffices to consider unitary flat line bundles
F=F_{\chi}
associated with unitary characters
\chi\colon\Gamma\to U(1)
.
We will denote the rho invariant of
F_{\chi}
by
\rho_{\chi}(M)
.
Recall from
[
38
, §2]
that the natural homomorphism
G\to G/[G,G]\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
gives rise to a short exact sequence

0\to A\to\frac{\Gamma}{[\Gamma,\Gamma]}\to p(\Gamma)\to 0

(109)

where
A=\frac{\Gamma\cap[G,G]}{[\Gamma,\Gamma]}
is a finite abelian group and
p(\Gamma)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

For every unitary character
\tau\colon\Gamma\to U(1)
that factors through

\rho_{\chi\cdot\tau}(M)=\rho_{\chi}(M).

(110)

To see this note first that
F_{\tau}
is topologically trivial.
Hence,
F_{\chi}
and

The relation between the rho invariant and the Chern–Simons invariants
[
6
]
, which we take from
[

, Thm 3.11.6]
, reads

\rho_{\chi\cdot\tau}(M)-\rho_{\chi}(M)=\pm\int_{SM}\Td(M)\ch(\Pi^{+}(\sigma))\Tch(\nabla^{\chi\cdot\tau},\nabla^{\chi}).

(111)

As
M
is parallelizable, the Todd class is trivial,
\Td(M)=1
.
Using a Riemannian metric induced from a left invariant metric on
G
, we see that the bundle
\Pi^{+}(\sigma)

provided by the positive eigenspaces of the principal symbol
[
33
, §3.8.2]

\ch(\Pi^{+}(\sigma))=1
.
As
\rk(F_{\chi})=1
the transgressed Chern character associated with the flat connections on
F_{\chi}
and

[

, §3.11.3]
.

) vanishes, whence (

).

.

.

Note that

is a normal sublattice in

and let

denote the corresponding nilmanifold.
The group

acts freely (from the left) on
\tilde{M}
, turning the canonical projection

into an
A
-covering.
In view of
[
6
, Thm 2.9]
it suffices to show that Atiyah and Singer’s
[
8
, §7]

vanishes for every nontrivial

.
To this end, let

be a central 1-parameter subgroup intersecting the lattice

nontrivially.

Here the vertical isomorphism on the right is induced by the bundle projection
p
,
while the vertical isomorphism on the left is the Thom isomorphism of said disc bundle.
We note that
N=\tilde{\Gamma}{\setminus}G/Z=\frac{\tilde{\Gamma}}{\tilde{\Gamma}\cap Z}{\setminus}\frac{G}{Z}
is a 4-dimensional nilmanifold.
By Nomizu’s
[
47
]
theorem,
H^{*}(N;\mathbb{R})=H^{*}(\mathfrak{n};\mathbb{R})
where
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{z}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra of Engel type, and
\mathfrak{z}
denotes the Lie algebra of
Z
.
It is straight forward to compute the cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{n}
.
This shows that the cup product
H^{1}(N;\mathbb{R})\otimes H^{2}(N;\mathbb{R})\to H^{3}(N;\mathbb{R})
vanishes.
Hence, the upper horizontal map in (
112
) vanishes too.
We conclude that
\sign(g,W)=0
, for all
g\in A
, cf.
[
6
, §2]
.

Consider a nontrivial
g\in A
acting on
W
.
If its fixed point set is nonempty then it coincides with
N
considered as a submanifold of
W
via the zero section of the disc bundle
W\to N
.
In this case

L(g,W)=\int_{N}\mathfrak{P}_{g}\bigl(p_{1}(N),e\bigr)

where
\mathfrak{P}_{g}
is a polynomial depending on
g
, see
[
8
, Thm 6.12]
or
[
44
, Thm 14.5]
for the general formula.
The Pontryagin class
p_{1}(N)
vanishes since
N
is parallelizable.
In order to prove
L(g,W)=0
it thus suffices to show
e\cup e=0
in
H^{4}(N;\mathbb{R})
.
By the exactness of the Gysin sequence
e
generates the kernel of the map
H^{2}(N;\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}(\tilde{M};\mathbb{R})
induced by the bundle projection
\tilde{M}\to N
.
Using Nomizu’s theorem wee see that this kernel has dimension one, and
e\cup e=0
.

Analysis of multiphysical field coupling and study of dynamic characteristics in magnetorheological fluid dampers
Authors: Zhirong Yang, Longyue Li, Xin Gao, Xuance Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper presents multi-physical field coupling simulations of a magnetorheological fluid (MRF) damper equipped with bidirectionally adjustable permanent magnets under various operating conditions. The study analyzes the variation in flow velocity, pressure distribution, viscosity, and damping force of the MRF damper as influenced by different parameters using COMSOL software. A dynamic characteristics testing system was established to evaluate the mechanical properties of the MRF damper, and the accuracy of the simulated damping force was validated through experimental tests. The simulation results indicate that at different time points, the streamlines within the damping channel are straight and dense, while the streamline trajectories near the inlet and outlet of the damping channel exhibit greater complexity, and the streamlines are sparse around the piston rod and within the cavity. Additionally, the pressure distribution along the axial direction in the damping channel decreases with increasing axial distance. When the excitation current is increased from − 2.5 to 2 A, the damping force rises from 50 to 640 N, indicating a significant increase. The damping force also increases slightly with higher excitation frequency and amplitude, although the relative increase remains below 10%. The dynamic experimental results effectively validate the accuracy of the simulation calculations.

Introduction

Magnetorheological fluid (MRF) dampers are advanced fluid damping devices that are extensively utilized in semi-active control systems. Their intelligence is characterized by the rapid, continuous, and reversible rheological properties of the MRF within1. MRF dampers integrate the benefits of passive control devices, including simplicity, practicality, and high safety, with the advantages of active control systems, such as a broad control range and strong adaptability. Consequently, they have found extensive application in mechanical vibration control systems. Shawn et al.2 developed a novel type of MRF damper with an extended adjustable range of damping force. Fujitani et al.3 engineered a 40-ton MRF damper for house vibration reduction. Liao et al.4 proposed a MRF damper equipped with an external MRF valve. This damper is connected in parallel with a multi-stage radial flow MRF valve in the bypass to extend the effective damping channel length. Experimental results indicate that this damper can generate a substantial damping force. Jiang 5 designed a multi-channel bypass-type MRF damper with three operational modes. Zhu et al.6 introduced a MRF damper featuring a piston with built-in radial channels, achieving an output damping force of up to 3.5 kN and demonstrating excellent damping performance. Patel et al.7 optimized the design of the extrusion working mode MRF damper by adjusting parameters such as coil length, extrusion plate diameter, number of turns, coil area, and flow gap, thereby significantly improving the damping performance. Fitrian8 performed comprehensive research on rotary MRF dampers, focusing on aspects such as structural design, magnetic circuit optimization, and mathematical modeling. Gurubasavaraju9 selected a double-rod MRF damper as the research subject. Through finite element modeling and magnetic circuit analysis conducted with ANSYS, he obtained the variation trends of velocity and pressure as the piston moved from the top dead center to the bottom dead center. Wang designed a magnetic-field-assisted MRF damper10. In the process of developing the dynamic model, static magnetic field analysis of the MRF damper was conducted using COMSOL, from which the distribution of magnetic flux density along the damping channel was derived. However, in the zero-current state, the magnetic induction intensity outside the damping channel of a traditional MRF damper is zero. As a result, when the damper is used for passive structural control under this condition, the resulting damping force is insufficient, leading to suboptimal control performance and poor fail-safe characteristics. Furthermore, prolonged exposure of the MRF to a zero-magnetic field environment may lead to sedimentation, which significantly compromises the damper’s overall performance. To address these limitations, Carlson11 proposed integrating a permanent magnet into the piston of the MRF damper, forming a composite magnetic circuit together with the excitation coil. This design enhances the magnetic induction intensity at the damping channel under the zero-current state, thereby increasing the damper’s output and improving its passive control capability. Kim12 introduced an MRF damper in which the damping force is regulated by a permanent magnet. Additionally, he analyzed the influence of the shape of the ferromagnetic housing cylinder and the positioning of the permanent magnet relative to the moving piston on the damping force. Jeniš13 investigated the dynamic behavior of a permanent magnet based MRF damper, presenting its magnetic circuit design, transient magnetic simulations involving hysteresis and eddy current effects, as well as experimental findings. The results indicated that the permanent magnet significantly influences the damper’s dynamic characteristics. Boese14 presented and tested a novel MRF damper incorporating two permanent magnets, capable of generating a moderate damping force without electrical power consumption, thereby ensuring reliable fail-safe performance during power outages. He also analyzed how the concentration of iron particles in the MRF affects the damping force.

As MR technology continues to advance, the simulation methodologies for MR dampers are becoming increasingly sophisticated. Liu et al. designed a triple-coil MR damper and performed an electromagnetic field (EMF) simulation to determine the magnetic flux density within the damping channel under various current levels and coil configurations15. Yao et al. developed a 1/2 axisymmetric model of a shear-valve MR damper using ANSYS, deriving the internal magnetic field distribution through EMF simulation. They further conducted a simulation-based analysis of how variations in damping channel gaps and pole lengths influence the response time of the MR damper16. Elsaady17 integrated finite element analysis of the magnetic circuit with computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analysis of fluid flow, taking into account the impact of bubbles within the model. Guo18 conducted research on the nonlinear characteristics of MRF dampers, specifically focusing on the compressibility of MRFs. Additionally, Mario19 utilized finite element methods to analyze the magnetic and thermal fields of asymmetric MRF dampers employed in the automotive industry. He performed constant temperature calculations at various temperatures and discussed the impact of temperature on the magnetization intensity of magnetic particles and the fluid properties. Zhu et al.20 employed ANSYS to establish a fluid simulation model, which revealed changes in the pressure gradient during MRF damper operation. The model also coupled with the heat transfer field, accounting for friction between the MRF and the damping orifice, as well as electromagnetic heat generated by the coil. Their findings indicated that the temperature of the piston differed from the fluid temperatures in the upper and lower chambers. Based on the structural design of the MR hydro-pneumatic spring, Jiang et al. established a coupled magnetic-flow simulation model that integrates both EMF and fluid dynamics considerations21 .

The aforementioned research primarily concentrates on two physical fields: the electromagnetic field (EMF) and the flow field. However, considering the distinctive characteristics of MRF, the performance of MR dampers is significantly influenced by the mutual coupling effect among the internal structure, workpiece, and EMF, as highlighted by Ferdaus et al22. If the design phase only considers magnetic field distribution and flow field distribution, it becomes difficult to accurately reflect the actual operating conditions of the MR damper. Therefore, conducting simulation studies that incorporate multi-physics field coupling is of substantial engineering significance and practical value for achieving more precise predictions of MR damper performance. Hu23 designed a hybrid-flow MRF damper and utilized COMSOL for multi-physics coupling analysis. This approach enabled the acquisition of the static magnetic field distribution, as well as the flow velocity, dynamic viscosity, and pressure distributions when the piston was at its initial position. Liang et al.24 proposed a multi-physics simulation model for a single-rod MRF damper equipped with a gas compensator. They analyzed the impact of varying initial pressures on the MRF damper and experimentally validated the accuracy of the simulation results. Li et al.25 developed an MRF damper model in Fluent to investigate the pressure and velocity distributions within the flow channel. Sternberg et al.26 conducted a comprehensive investigation into the design, manufacturing, and testing of a prototype high-capacity MRF damper. This MRF damper incorporates external coils, and a multi-physics finite element model was utilized to analyze the coupled magneto-mechanical and hydrodynamic behaviors of the proposed design. Guo27 explored the nonlinear characteristics of MRF dampers by examining the compressibility of MRF through two-dimensional CFD modeling and finite element analysis (FEA). Kubík et al.28 introduced a hybrid multi-physics model for MRF dampers, employing a vector hysteresis method to parametrically investigate their dynamic behavior and assess the influence of hysteresis and related factors. Hu et al.29 integrated ISIGHT and COMSOL to develop a multi-objective optimization design approach for MR dampers based on a multi-physics coupling model, thereby validating the accuracy of the multi-physics coupling methodology.

To address the limitations of traditional MRF dampers, such as insufficient damping force, poor control performance during power outages, and sedimentation issues under prolonged zero-magnetic field conditions, this paper presents a bidirectionally adjustable MRF damper incorporating a permanent magnet. Moreover, previous studies on MRF dampers with permanent magnets have predominantly focused on single working conditions or isolated physical field analyses, often neglecting the coupling effects among multiple physical fields. Therefore, a systematic investigation into the influence of multi-condition scenarios and coupled physical fields on damper performance is essential. In this study, a two-dimensional axisymmetric model of the permanent magnet based MRF damper is developed using COMSOL software. Multi-physical field coupling simulations are conducted under various operating conditions to explore the variation patterns of key performance parameters. Furthermore, a mechanical performance testing system is established to evaluate the damper’s mechanical behavior, and experimental validation is carried out to confirm the accuracy of the simulation results.

Structure parameters and working principle of MRF Damper

The physical and structural diagrams of the bidirectionally adjustable permanent magnet based MRF damper proposed in this paper are illustrated in Fig. 1. This damper employs a shear valve-type double-rod design, featuring a piston stroke of 78.5 mm and an outer cylinder diameter of 63.6 mm. The key components include shaft 1, shaft 2, the outer cylinder, left positioning plate, stepped magnetic guiding ring I, permanent magnet, stepped magnetic guiding ring II, right positioning plate, right end cap, auxiliary cylinder, screws, excitation coil, and cylindrical magnetic guiding ring. Compared to conventional MRF dampers, this design incorporates an annular permanent magnet within the piston, enabling bidirectional adjustment of damping force through current modulation in the excitation coil. In the absence of current, the damper can still provide a baseline damping force, thereby preventing potential damage to equipment due to insufficient damping force during fault-induced current interruptions. Development and implementation of the coupled field simulation model

The bidirectional adjustable MRF damper with permanent magnets studied in this paper has a high degree of symmetry. To reduce the computational cost, a two-dimensional axisymmetric coupled-field simulation model representing half of the MRF damper is established in the COMSOL software, as shown in Fig. 3. After establishing the model, the corresponding material properties are assigned to different components through the MRD module in the software. Stainless steel is used as the magnetic isolation material, and its relative magnetic permeability is defined as 1. Pure iron DT4 is used as the material for the magnetic conducting part, and its B-H curve is shown in Fig. 4. The permanent magnet component selects a neodymium-iron-boron permanent magnet, and its demagnetization curve is shown in Fig. 5. The simulation analysis of solid mechanics field based on magneto-fluid–structure Coupling

The damping force of the MRF damper is equivalent to the sum of the reaction forces exerted by the MRF on the piston head during its motion. Building upon the original physical field, a solid mechanics field has been incorporated. It is assumed that the deformation of the piston during motion is minimal, and isotropic linear elastic properties are adopted. To simplify the computational model, the displacement of the piston head is specified within the solid mechanics domain, while the displacements of the remaining components are defined in the moving mesh domain. In the multiphysics module, magneto-fluid–structure coupling is selected with the coupling type set to fully coupled. As illustrated in Fig. 16, the damping force–displacement curve of the MRF damper under the influence of varying currents and multi-physics coupling is presented. As the reverse current decreases, the damping force correspondingly increases. This phenomenon can be attributed to the enhanced magnetic field exerted by the permanent magnet on the damping channel due to the reduced reverse current, which subsequently elevates the yield stress of the MRF. Consequently, the magneto-viscous damping force exhibits a continuous upward trend. When the current I = − 2.5A, the damper operates in a zero magnetic field condition, and the viscous damping force attains its minimum value of 50N. Under conditions of zero and positive currents, the damping force progressively increases with the rise in current magnitude. In the absence of current flow through the coil (I = 0A), the damping force measures 183N, significantly higher than that observed in the zero magnetic field scenario. This indicates that the incorporation of a permanent magnet markedly enhances the damping force in the power-off state. When the current reaches 2A, the damping force peaks at 640N.Fig. 16Damping force–displacement curves for various excitation currents.

Damping force–displacement curves for various excitation currents.

Conclusion

This paper presents a comprehensive multiphysics finite element analysis of a bidirectional adjustable shear valve-type MRF damper. The study investigates the parameter variations under the coupled effects of magnetic fields, flow fields, structural mechanics fields, and other relevant physical fields during the damper’s operation. The influence of these coupling effects on the performance of the MRF damper is thoroughly examined. A dynamic characteristic testing system for the MRF damper has been developed to evaluate its dynamic performance, which is subsequently compared and validated against the simulation results obtained from the multiphysics coupling model.

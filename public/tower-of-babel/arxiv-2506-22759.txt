L p L^{p} -Logvinenko-Sereda sets and L p L^{p} -Carleson measures on compact manifolds
Xing Wang, Xiangjin Xu and Cheng Zhang

Abstract
. Marzo and Ortega-Cerdà [ 33 ] gave geometric characterizations for L p L^{p} -Logvinenko-Sereda sets on the standard sphere for all 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . Later, Ortega-Cerdà and Pridhnani [ 38 ] further investigated L 2 L^{2} -Logvinenko-Sereda sets and L 2 L^{2} -Carleson measures on compact manifolds without boundary. In this paper, we characterize L p L^{p} -Logvinenko-Sereda sets and L p L^{p} -Carleson measures on compact manifolds with or without boundary for all 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . Furthermore, we investigate L p L^{p} -Logvinenko-Sereda sets and L p L^{p} -Carleson measures for eigenfunctions on compact manifolds without boundary, and we completely characterize them on the standard sphere S m S^{m} for p > 2 ​ m m − 1 p>\frac{2m}{m-1} . For the range p < 2 ​ m m − 1 p<\frac{2m}{m-1} , we conjecture that L p L^{p} -Logvinenko-Sereda sets for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere S m S^{m} are characterized by the tubular geometric control condition and we provide some evidence. These results provide new progress on an open problem raised by Ortega-Cerdà and Pridhnani.

1.
Introduction

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth connected compact Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
. The dimension of
M
is
m\geq 2
. The boundary
\partial M
is smooth and can be empty. If
\partial M\neq\emptyset
and
\nu
is the outward unit normal vector field along
\partial\Omega
, we assume the boundary condition is either Dirichlet (
u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
) or Neumann (
\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}=0
). Then the Laplacian operator
-\Delta
on
M
is nonnegative, self-adjoint and has discrete spectrum
\{\lambda_{j}^{2}\}
where
0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...
are arranged in increasing order and we account for
multiplicity. For
\lambda\geq 1
, let
E_{\lambda}
be the subspace of
L^{2}(M)
generated by eigenfunctions of eigenvalues bounded by
\lambda^{2}
, namely

E_{\lambda}=\Big\{\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\alpha_{j}e_{j}:\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C},\ -\Delta e_{j}=\lambda_{j}^{2}e_{j}\Big\}

where
\{e_{j}\}_{j}
is an orthonormal eigenbasis corresponding to
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j}
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. The question is to characterize the measures
\mu=\{\mu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
f\in E_{\lambda}
,

(1.1)

\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu_{\lambda}\approx\int_{M}|f|^{p}dV.

Let
B(z,r)
be the geodesic ball in
M
centered at
z
of radius
r
. Heuristically, since the function
f
is frequency-localized in a ball of radius
\lambda
, by the uncertainty principle it behaves like a constant in every ball of radius
1/\lambda
in the physical space. Thus, it is natural to introduce the following conditions, which will be useful to characterize the measures satisfying (
1.1
).

We call
\mu
is
relatively dense
if there exist
r>0
and
\rho>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
z\in M
,

(1.2)

\frac{\mu_{\lambda}(B(z,r/\lambda))}{\text{vol}(B(z,r/\lambda))}\geq\rho.

We call
\mu
is
relatively sparse
if there exist
r>0
and
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
z\in M
,

(1.3)

\frac{\mu_{\lambda}(B(z,r/\lambda))}{\text{vol}(B(z,r/\lambda))}\leq C.

First, let us focus on the special case where
d\mu_{\lambda}={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{A_{\lambda}}dV
where
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{A_{\lambda}}
is the indicator function of the set
A_{\lambda}\subset M
. Let
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
be a sequence of sets in
M
. We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda
if there exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
f\in E_{\lambda}
,

(1.4)

\int_{A_{\lambda}}|f|^{p}dV\geq C\int_{M}|f|^{p}dV.

We say that
\mathcal{A}
is relatively dense if there exist
r>0
and
\rho>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
z\in M
,

(1.5)

\frac{{\rm vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap B(z,r/\lambda))}{{\rm vol}(B(z,r/\lambda))}\geq\rho.

By definition,
\mathcal{A}
is relatively dense if and only if
\mu
is relatively dense.

Our first result is that the relatively dense condition actually characterizes
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets on compact manifolds with or without boundary.

Theorem 1
.

For
1<p<\infty
, the sequence of sets
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda if it is relatively dense. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the converse is true.

The classical Logvinenko-Sereda theorem describes this type of comparable norms for functions in the Paley-Wiener space, which consists of functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
whose Fourier transform is supported in a prefixed bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. See Logvinenko-Sereda
[
31
]
, Havin-Jöricke
[
20
, p. 112-116]
. Luecking
[
32
]
studied this notion in Bergman spaces.
Following his idea, Marzo and Ortega-Cerdà
[
33
]
characterized
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets on the sphere for all
1\leq p<\infty
. Later, Ortega-Cerdà and Pridhnani
[
38
]
characterized
L^{2}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets on compact manifolds without boundary. Our Theorem
1
completely characterizes
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets on compact manifolds with or without boundary for all
1<p<\infty
.

Next, let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\mu=\{\mu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
be a sequence of measures on
M
. We say that
\mu
is
L^{p}
-Carleson
if there exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
f\in E_{\lambda}
,

(1.6)

\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu_{\lambda}\leq C\int_{M}|f|^{p}dV.

Similarly, we may characterize the
L^{p}
-Carleson measures by the relatively sparse condition. Nevertheless, it is sensitive to the boundary condition.

Theorem 2
.

For
1<p<\infty
, the sequence of measures
\mu=\{\mu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-Carleson if it is relatively sparse. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the converse is true when the boundary is empty or the boundary condition is Neumann, but the converse is false when the boundary condition is Dirichlet.

On compact manifolds without boundary, Ortega-Cerdà and Pridhnani
[
38
]
proved that the relatively sparse condition is sufficient and necessary for
\mu
to be
L^{2}
-Carleson. Later, Xu
[
60
]
studied this problem on compact manifolds with boundary, and observed that the relatively sparse condition is not necessary for
L^{2}
-Carleson under Dirichlet boundary condition. We observe that due to the conservation property of the heat kernel (
2.4
), the relatively sparse condition is necessary for Carleson measures in the boundaryless case and the Neumann case.

Furthermore, we investigate the problem with
E_{\lambda}
replaced by the
\lambda
-eigenspace. This open problem was raised by Ortega-Cerdà and Pridhnani in
[
38
, Remark 5.12]
. It is much more complicated, so we only consider compact manifolds without boundary for now. We say that
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions
on
M
if there exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and eigenfunctions
e_{\lambda}
satisfying
-\Delta e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda}
we have

(1.7)

\int_{A_{\lambda}}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV\geq C\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV.

In other words, every eigenfunction
e_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-concentrated on
A_{\lambda}
. For example, on the compact hyperbolic surface
M
, every eigenfunction is
L^{2}
-concentrated on any fixed nonempty open set
\Omega\subset M
. See e.g. Dyatlov-Jin
[
16
]
, Dyatlov-Jin-Nonnenmacher
[
17
]
. By Theorem
1
, the relatively dense condition is sufficient for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions for all
1<p<\infty
, but it is not necessary for some
p
. See
[
38
, Remark 5.12]
for a counterexample when
p=1
. More precisely, we can show that it is not necessary whenever
p
is small.

Proposition 3
.

For any
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
, the relatively dense condition is not necessary for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on
M
. For
p=\frac{2m}{m-1}
, the relatively dense condition is not necessary for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on
M
with a generic metric.

Here we use the fact that
\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}=o(\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}})\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}
for a generic metric on any compact manifold. For the definition of generic metric and more related results, see Sogge-Zelditch
[
50
, Theorem 1.4]
, Sogge-Toth-Zelditch
[
49
]
, Sogge-Zelditch
[
51
,
52
]
.

On the sphere
S^{m}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}:|x|=1\}
, we call
\mathcal{A}
is
symmetric relatively dense
if there exist
r>0
and
\rho>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
z\in S^{m}
,

(1.8)

\frac{\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap B(z,r/\lambda))+\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap B(-z,r/\lambda))}{\text{ vol}(B(z,r/\lambda))}\geq\rho.

It is weaker than the relatively dense condition. However, this condition is still not necessary for small
p
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
(S^{m},g)
be the sphere equipped with a Riemannian metric. For any
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
, the symmetric relatively dense condition is not necessary for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on
S^{m}
. For
p=\frac{2m}{m-1}
, the symmetric relatively dense condition is not necessary for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on
S^{m}
with a generic metric.

Nevertheless, we can characterize
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere for large
p
by the symmetric relatively dense condition.

Theorem 5
.

For any
\frac{2m}{m-1}<p<\infty
, the symmetric relatively dense condition is necessary and sufficient for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere
S^{m}
.

By Proposition
4
, the range of
p
in Theorem
5
is essentially sharp. For the range
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
, we propose a conjecture that
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere can be characterized by the tubular geometric control condition. See Conjecture
20
and some evidence in Section
8
.

Similarly, it is interesting to characterize
L^{p}
-Carleson measures for eigenfunctions. We say that
\mu=\{\mu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions
if there exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and eigenfunctions
e_{\lambda}
satisfying
-\Delta e_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}e_{\lambda}
we have

(1.9)

\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}d\mu_{\lambda}\leq C\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV.

By Theorem
2
, the relatively sparse condition is sufficient for
\mu
to be
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions, but we observe that it is not necessary for small
p
.

Proposition 6
.

For any
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
, the relatively sparse condition is not necessary for
\mu
to be
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions on
M
. For
p=\frac{2m}{m-1}
, the relatively sparse condition is not necessary for
\mu
to be
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions on
M
with a generic metric.

On the other hand, we are able to characterize
L^{p}
-Carleson measures for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere for large
p
by the relatively sparse condition.

Theorem 7
.

For any
\frac{2m}{m-1}<p<\infty
, the relatively sparse condition is necessary and sufficient for
\mu
to be
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere
S^{m}
.

By Proposition
6
, the range of
p
in Theorem
7
is essentially sharp. We essentially use the property that at every point of the sphere, there exists a sequence of eigenfunctions (e.g. zonal spherical harmonics) with maximal eigenfunction growth (measured by
L^{\infty}
norm) at that point. Sogge-Zelditch
[
50
, Theorem 1.1]
gave a necessary condition for this property using geodesic loops. It would be interesting to look for a sufficient condition.

Outline for the proof of Theorem
1
and Theorem
2
.

To prove the sufficiency part of Theorem
1
, we first handle the boundaryless case and then deal with the Dirichlet case and the Neumann case separately. The boundaryless case can be proved by the harmonic extension method in Ortega-Cerdà and Pridhnani
[
38
]
. We need the
L^{p}
-mean value inequality for harmonic functions and the
L^{p}
-boundedness for the spectral multipliers. For the Dirichlet case, we find a good control for the contribution for the part close to the boundary so we eventually reduce the proof to the boundaryless case. We need Bernstein inequalities that essentially rely on the Gaussian heat kernel upper bounds. For the Neumann case, we need to establish the gradient estimate (
3.7
) for harmonic functions under Neumann boundary condition in order to proceed in the way as the boundaryless case. Our argument is inspired by the book of Li
[
29
]
, Chen
[
7
]
and Wang
[
57
]
.

To prove the necessity part, heuristically one may take
f={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{B(z,r/\lambda)}
in (
1.4
) to get (
1.5
), but this function is not in
E_{\lambda}
. Nevertheless, this heuristic can be made more precise. We shall use the complex time heat kernel estimates to construct appropriate functions in
E_{\lambda}
that are essentially supported in the ball of radius
1/\lambda
. We also exploit Seeley’s spectral function estimates and Bernstein inequalities.

The proof of Theorem
2
is based on the similar strategy. The sufficiency part is simpler and we only use the
L^{p}
-mean value inequality for the harmonic extensions and the
L^{p}
-boundedness of spectral multipliers. To prove the necessity part, we shall exploit the two-sided estimate for the heat kernel on the diagonal, which holds when the boundary is empty or the boundary condition is Neumann. It is due to the conservation property of the heat kernel. We also need the complex time heat kernel estimates to construct functions in
E_{\lambda}
that approximate the indicator function
f={\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{B(\xi,1/\lambda)}
. Furthermore, we construct a counterexample when the boundary condition is Dirichlet.

2.
Estimates of the heat kernels and the spectral functions

In this section, we review some useful estimates for the heat kernels and the spectral functions. We will use them throughout the paper. As before, let
M
be a smooth compact manifold of dimension
m\geq 2
. The boundary
\partial M
is smooth and can be empty. If
\partial M\neq\emptyset
, we assume the boundary condition is either Dirichlet or Neumann.

First, we need short time Gaussian upper bounds for the heat kernel and its gradient.

Lemma 8
.

Let
e^{t\Delta}(x,y)=p(t,x,y)
be the heat kernel. Then there exist constants
C>0
and
c>0
such that for all
t\in(0,1]
and
x,y\in M
we have

(2.1)

|p(t,x,y)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{m}{2}}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t},

(2.2)

|\nabla_{x}p(t,x,y)|\leq Ct^{-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-cd(x,y)^{2}/t}.

The pointwise estimates (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) follow from parametrix constructions for
p(t,x,y)
on
(0,1]\times M\times M
. See e.g. Greiner
[
19
]
, Li-Yau
[
30
]
. These estimates are useful to obtain the boundedness of spectral multipliers of generalized Laplacians on compact manifolds with boundary, see e.g. Taylor
[
53
]
, Mukherjee
[
37
]
. A direct consequence of (
2.1
) is the upper bound for the spectral function.

Corollary 9
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
x,y\in M

(2.3)

\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}e_{j}(x)\overline{e_{j}(y)}\Big|\leq C\lambda^{m}.

When the boundary is empty or the boundary condition is Neumann, we have the conservation property

(2.4)

\int_{M}p(t,x,y)dV(y)=1

for all
t>0
and
x\in M
. Together with the semigroup property and the short-time Gaussian upper bound, the conservation property implies a short time lower bound for the heat kernel on the diagonal. See e.g. Hebisch and Saloff-Coste
[
21
, p. 1455]
.

Corollary 10
.

Suppose that the boundary is empty or the boundary condition is Neumann. Then there exist
t_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for all
t\in(0,t_{0}]
and
x\in M
we have

(2.5)

C^{-1}t^{-m/2}\leq p(t,x,x)\leq Ct^{-m/2}.

As a consequence, there exists
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
x\in M

(2.6)

C^{-1}\lambda^{m}\leq\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}\leq C\lambda^{m}.

Proof.

We just need to prove the lower bounds, as the upper bounds follow directly from Lemma
8
and Corollary
9
. By the semigroup property and Cauchy-Schwarz, we get for any fixed
a>0

\displaystyle p(t,x,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}p(t/2,x,y)^{2}dV(y)\geq\int_{B(x,a\sqrt{t})}p(t/2,x,y)^{2}dV(y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\text{vol}(B(x,a\sqrt{t}))}\Big(\int_{B(x,a\sqrt{t})}p(t/2,x,y)dV(y)\Big)^{1/2}.

By the conservation property (
2.4
) and the Gaussian heat kernel bound (
2.1
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,a\sqrt{t})}p(t/2,x,y)dV(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}p(t/2,x,y)dV(y)-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,2^{j+1}a\sqrt{t})\setminus B(x,2^{j}a\sqrt{t})}p(t/2,x,y)dV(y)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}C(t/2)^{-m/2}e^{-c2^{2j}a^{2}}\text{vol}(B(x,2^{j+1}a\sqrt{t}))

\displaystyle\geq 1-C_{m}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(2^{j}a)^{m}e^{-c2^{2j}a^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2},

whenever
a>0
is large enough.

We fix such
a
and then for sufficiently small
t
we have the heat kernel lower bound

p(t,x,x)\geq\frac{1}{4\text{vol}(B(x,a\sqrt{t}))}\gtrsim t^{-m/2}.

Next, we use the heat kernel lower bound to obtain the lower bound for the spectral function

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}e^{-\lambda_{j}^{2}/(c_{0}\lambda)^{2}}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq e^{\Delta/(c_{0}\lambda)^{2}}(x,x)-\sum_{\lambda_{j}>\lambda}e^{-\lambda_{j}^{2}/(c_{0}\lambda)^{2}}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}(c_{0}\lambda)^{m}-C\sum_{\ell\geq 0}e^{-2^{2\ell}/c_{0}^{2}}(2^{\ell}\lambda)^{m}

\displaystyle\geq C^{-1}(c_{0}\lambda)^{m}-C_{1}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}(2^{\ell}/c_{0})^{-m-1}(2^{\ell}\lambda)^{m}

\displaystyle=C^{-1}(c_{0}\lambda)^{m}-2C_{1}c_{0}^{m+1}\lambda^{m}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda^{m},

whenever
c_{0}>0
is small enough.
∎

The lower bounds in Corollary
10
are not valid when boundary condition is Dirichlet. See e.g. Zhang
[
61
]
for a different two-sided estimate of the Dirichlet heat kernel. Nevertheless, we still have (
2.6
) for the Dirichlet spectral function whenever the point
x
is not too close to the boundary, see Corollary
12
.

Seeley
[
42
,
43
]
established the spectral function estimate on compact manifolds with boundary for either Dirichlet or Neumann boundary condition.

Lemma 11
.

There exist
C>0
and
\alpha>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
x\in M
we have

(2.7)

\Big|\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}-\omega_{m}(2\pi)^{-m}\lambda^{m}\Big|\leq C(\delta^{\alpha-1}\lambda^{m-1}+\delta^{-3/2}\lambda^{m-\frac{3}{2}})

where
\omega_{m}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\delta=d(x,\partial M)
is the geodesic distance from
x
to the boundary
\partial M
.

As a consequence, we get the following two-sided estimate for the spectral function.

Corollary 12
.

There exists
C>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
x\in M
with
d(x,\partial M)\geq C/\lambda
,

(2.8)

C_{1}^{-1}\lambda^{m}\leq\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}|e_{j}(x)|^{2}\leq C_{1}\lambda^{m}.

The two-sided estimate (
2.8
) is well-known on compact manifolds without boundary and it follows from the pointwise Weyl law, see Avakumović
[
1
]
, Levitan
[
27
,
28
]
, Hörmander
[
22
]
, Sogge
[
46
,
47
]
.

Recall that the symbol class

3.1.
Boundaryless case

We assume (
3.5
) is not true in order to construct functions that satisfy the opposite inequality. Then we will parametrize these functions and prove that their limit is analytic and vanishes on a subset of positive measure. This will lead to a contradiction.

Suppose (
3.5
) is not true. Then for each
n\geq 1
, there exist
\lambda_{n}\geq 1
,
f_{n}\in E_{\lambda_{n}}
and
w_{n}\in D_{n}
such that

(3.8)

|f_{n}(w_{n})|^{p}>n(\lambda_{n}/r)^{m}\int_{A_{\lambda_{n}}\cap B(w_{n},r/\lambda_{n})}|f_{n}|^{p}dV.

Here
D_{n}
is defined as in (

) with

replaced by

.

Step 1. Scaling.
By the compactness of the manifold, we have the exponential map

\exp_{w}:\mathbb{B}(0,\rho_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}\to B(w,\rho_{0})\subset M

for some
\rho_{0}=\rho_{0}(M)>0
, such that
\exp_{w}(0)=w
and
(B(w,\rho_{0}),\exp_{w}^{-1})
is a normal coordinate chart and the metric
g_{ij}(w)=\delta_{ij}
. Now we rescale the metric. Let
\exp_{n}(z)=\exp_{w_{n}}(rz/\lambda_{n})
and

d\mu_{n}(z)=\sqrt{|g|(\exp_{n}(z))}dz.

Let

F_{n}(z)=c_{n}f_{n}(\exp_{n}(z))

and its harmonic extension

H_{n}(z,t)=c_{n}h_{n}(\exp_{n}(z),rt/\lambda_{n}),

where
c_{n}
is a constant such that

\int_{\mathbb{B}(0,1)}\int_{|t|<1}|H_{n}(z,t)|^{p}d\mu_{n}(z)dt=1.

By scaling, it is equivalent to

(3.9)

|c_{n}|^{p}(\lambda_{n}/r)^{m+1}\int_{B(w_{n},r/\lambda_{n})}\int_{|t|<r/\lambda_{n}}|h_{n}(w,t)|^{p}dV(w)dt=1.

Step 2. Uniform boundedness.
By the definition of
D_{n}
and (
3.9
) we have

(3.10)

|F_{n}(0)|^{p}=|c_{n}|^{p}|f_{n}(w_{n})|^{p}\geq\varepsilon.

By the mean value inequality (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) we get

|F_{n}(0)|^{p}=|c_{n}|^{p}|h_{n}(w_{n},0)|^{p}\lesssim 1.

Similarly, if

and

, then for all

and then (

So working with

is uniformly bounded.

Furthermore, if

is such that

, then the assumption (
3.8
) and (

) imply

\int_{B_{n}}|F_{n}|^{p}d\mu_{n}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}.

Step 3. Equicontinuity.
Let
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. For

,

,

and

, we have

. If

is small enough, then by the gradient estimate (

) and (

) we get

\displaystyle|c_{n}||h_{n}(w,t)-h_{n}(\tilde{w},\tilde{t})|

\displaystyle\leq|c_{n}|(\varepsilon_{1}r/\lambda_{n})\sup_{\omega\in B(w_{n},r/(2\lambda_{n}),|t|<r/(2\lambda_{n})}|\nabla h_{n}(w,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C|c_{n}|(\varepsilon_{1}r/\lambda_{n})(\lambda_{n}/r)\sup_{\omega\in B(w_{n},r/\lambda_{n}),|t|<r/\lambda_{n}}|h_{n}(w,t)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon_{1}.

By scaling, for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that for all

whenever

,

and

. So the sequence

is equicontinuous.

Step 4. Analyticity.

Since the sequence
H_{n}
is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous on
\mathbb{B}(0,1)\times(-1,1)
, by Ascoli-Arzela’s theorem, there is a subsequence (denoted as the sequence itself)

uniformly on any compact subset of

. Note that

is harmonic with respect to the rescaled metric

, which has a subsequence (denoted as the sequence itself) uniformly converges to the Euclidean metric. We can see that

is harmonic in the Euclidean sense by
[
38

. This implies

is real analytic.

Step 5. Contradiction.

Recall
B_{n}\subset\mathbb{B}(0,1)
satisfies
\exp_{n}(B_{n})=A_{\lambda_{n}}\cap B(w_{n},r/\lambda_{n})
. Since the sequence

is relatively dense, there exists

such that for all

\mu_{n}(B_{n})\geq\rho.

Let

be such that

). If

Proof of Lemma

.
Choose

small enough so that we can use Fermi coordinates
z=(s,x)
with
s\in[0,\delta_{0}/\lambda]
and
x\in\partial M
. Under the Fermi coordinates, the metric is in the following form

g=ds^{2}+\tilde{g}_{ij}(s)dx^{i}dx^{j},

where
\tilde{g}(s)=\tilde{g}_{ij}(s)dx^{i}dx^{j}
is the pullback of the induced metric on

\Sigma_{s}=\{x\in M:d(x,\partial M)=s\}.

Let
d\sigma(s)
be the volume form of
\tilde{g}(s)
, then
d\sigma(s)\approx d\sigma(0)=d\sigma
.
Using the Dirichlet boundary condition, we have

f(s,x)=\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{\tau}f(\tau,x)d\tau.

So we obtain for
1\leq p<\infty

\displaystyle\int_{M_{\delta/\lambda}}|f(z)|^{p}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\delta/\lambda}\int_{\partial M}|f(s,x)|^{p}d\sigma(x)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\delta/\lambda}\int_{\partial M}\Big|\int_{0}^{s}\partial_{\tau}f(\tau,x)d\tau\Big|^{p}d\sigma(x)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\delta/\lambda}\int_{\partial M}s^{p-1}\int_{0}^{s}|\partial_{\tau}f(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau d\sigma(x)ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\delta/\lambda}\int_{\partial M}((\delta/\lambda)^{p}-\tau^{p})|\partial_{\tau}f(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq(\delta/\lambda)^{p}\int_{0}^{\delta/\lambda}\int_{\partial M}|\partial_{\tau}f(\tau,x)|^{p}d\tau d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\leq(\delta/\lambda)^{p}\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{p}

Since

, by the Bernstein inequality (

) we get

\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

So we complete the proof of Lemma

To exploit the argument in Section

to handle the Neumann case, we only need to establish the gradient estimate of the form (

[

, Chen
[

]
, and Wang
[

]
.

We first prove the gradient estimate

\sup_{B(x_{c},\rho)}|\nabla h|\lesssim\rho^{-1}\sup_{B(x_{c},2\rho)}|h|

for sufficiently small
\rho
under the condition
d(x_{c},\partial M)\geq\frac{\rho}{2}
. For the remaining cases, covering
B(x_{c},\rho)
by finitely many balls
B(x_{i},r_{i})
with
d(x_{i},\partial M)\geq r_{i}
yields

(3.16)

\sup_{B(x_{c},\rho)}|\nabla h|\lesssim\rho^{-1}\sup_{B(x_{c},K\rho)}|h|

for some constant
K=K(M)
. The estimate (
3.16
) is slightly weaker than (
3.7
), but it is sufficient for our purpose.

Let
A=2\sup_{B(x_{c},2\rho)}|h|
. Then
u=h+A
is a positive harmonic function in
B(x_{c},2\rho)
. Let
v=\log u
and
Q=|\nabla v|^{2}
. It suffices to prove
Q(x)\lesssim\rho^{-2}
for all
x\in B(x_{c},\rho)
with
\rho
sufficiently small.

Let
r(x)
be the distance between
x
and
x_{c}
. Let us choose
\phi=\phi(r(x))
as in the book of Li
[
29
, p. 60]
. It is a nonnegative function with the property that

\phi=1\ \text{on}\ B(x_{c},\rho),

\phi=0\ \text{on}\ M\setminus B(x_{c},2\rho),

-C\rho^{-1}\sqrt{\phi}\leq\phi^{\prime}\leq 0\ \text{and}\ |\phi^{\prime\prime}|\leq C\rho^{-2}\ \text{on}\ B(x_{c},2\rho)\setminus B(x_{c},\rho).

Let
d(x)
be the distance between
x
and the boundary
\partial M
and let

F(x)=(1+\psi(d(x)))^{2}\phi(r(x))(Q(x)+1),

where
\psi
is a nonnegative smooth function with
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi^{\prime}(0)=H>0
, if the second fundamental form II of
\partial M
is bounded below by
-H
. We first prove by contradiction that
F
cannot achieve the maximum at any boundary point, and then we estimate the maximum in the interior.

Suppose
F
achieves the maximum at
q\in\partial M
. Choose a Fermi coordinate at
q
with
x=(x^{\prime},x_{m})
, where
x_{m}
is inner normal to the boundary and
x^{\prime}
is a normal coordinate on
\partial M
at
q
. Then we have
d(x)=x_{m}\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{m}}

\displaystyle=(1+\psi(x_{m}))^{2}\phi^{\prime}(r(x))\frac{\partial r(x)}{\partial x_{m}}(Q(x)+1)

\displaystyle\ \ +2(1+\psi(x_{m}))\psi^{\prime}(x_{m})\phi(r(x))(Q(x)+1)

\displaystyle\ \ +(1+\psi(x_{m}))^{2}\phi(r(x))\frac{\partial Q(x)}{\partial x_{m}}.

We want to show
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{m}}(q)>0
, which leads to a contradiction.
For sufficiently small
\rho
, since
d(x_{c},\partial M)\geq\frac{\rho}{2}
, we have
\frac{\partial r(x)}{\partial x_{m}}\leq 0
. Noting that
\phi^{\prime}\leq 0
, the first term is always nonnegative. To handle the remaining two terms, we denote
u_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}u
and
u_{jm}=\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{m}}u
.
By the Neumann boundary condition
u_{m}|_{x_{m}=0}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Q(x)}{\partial x_{m}}\Big|_{x=q}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sum_{j}u_{j}u_{jm}}{u^{2}}-\frac{2|\nabla u|^{2}u_{m}}{u^{3}}\Big|_{x=q}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sum_{j<m}u_{j}u_{jm}}{u^{2}}\Big|_{x=q}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\text{II}(\nabla u,\nabla u)}{u^{2}}\Big|_{x=q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{-2H|\nabla u(q)|^{2}}{u(q)^{2}},

since the second fundamental form II of
\partial M
is bounded below by
-H
. So we only need to show

\psi^{\prime}(0)(\frac{|\nabla u(q)|^{2}}{u(q)^{2}}+1)-(1+\psi(0))\frac{H|\nabla u(q)|^{2}}{u(q)^{2}}>0.

It is ensured by our choice of
\psi
with
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi^{\prime}(0)=H>0
.

Next, we assume
F
achieves the maximum at
x_{0}\in M
. Then

(3.17)

\Delta F(x_{0})\leq 0,\ \text{and}\ \nabla F(x_{0})=0.

Let
\varphi(x)=(1+\psi(d(x)))^{2}\phi(r(x))
. Since
d(x)=x_{m}
is smooth,
\varphi(x)
and its derivatives satisfy similar bounds as
\phi
. We may assume the Ricci curvature on
M
is bounded below by
-(m-1)R

(R\geq 0)
, since
M
the compact. Then we repeat the standard argument as in the book of Li
[
29
, Proof of Theorem 6.1]
to calculate
\Delta F
and simplify the expression evaluated at
x_{0}
by the condition (
3.17
). As in
[
29
, (6.2)]
, we get

\displaystyle 0\geq(m-1)(\Delta\varphi)F-\frac{3m-4}{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\varphi^{-1}F-2(m-1)^{2}R\varphi F-2(m-2)|\nabla\varphi|\varphi^{-1/2}F^{3/2}+2F^{2}.

By the Laplacian comparison theorem
[
29
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have
\Delta\varphi\gtrsim-\rho^{-2}
. Since
|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\varphi^{-1}\lesssim\rho^{-2}
and
\varphi\lesssim 1
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq-C_{1}\rho^{-2}F-C_{2}F-C_{3}\rho^{-1}F^{3/2}+2F^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim-\rho^{-2}F+F^{2}

So
F(x)\leq F(x_{0})\lesssim\rho^{-2}
. Therefore, we obtain
Q(x)\lesssim\rho^{-2}
for all
x\in B(x_{c},\rho)
, as
\varphi(x)\approx 1
in
B(x_{c},\rho)
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1
: the necessity part

In this section, we prove that
\mathcal{A}
is relatively dense if it is
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda. By the heuristic in the introduction, we shall construct a test function that behaves like the indicator function of the ball of radius
1/\lambda
.

For any fixed

and
C>0
, if for all
\lambda\geq 1
and

with

(4.1)

\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap B(y,r/\lambda))\geq\rho\lambda^{-m},

then there exists

\lambda\geq 1
and

since any ball

contains a ball

with
d(y,\partial M)\geq C/\lambda
.
So to prove
\mathcal{A}
is relatively dense, we may assume the centers of the balls are
C/\lambda
away from the boundary. We will choose sufficiently large

and
C
later to prove (
4.1
).

Let
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}\leq\psi\leq{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[-1,1]}
. Given

as in Corollary
14
such that (
2.1
) and (

) hold for all

and

By the kernel estimate in Corollary
17
, we have

|f_{\lambda,y}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{m}(1+\lambda d(x,y))^{-N}.

Since

, by Corollary
12
we get
|f_{\lambda,y}(y)|\approx\lambda^{m}.

Together with the Bernstein inequality (
2.11
) and the mean value theorem, this implies that there exists

independent of

such that for all

,

Thus, the function

behaves like the indicator function of the ball of radius

On the other hand, since

is

-Logvinenko-Sereda, we have for

\displaystyle\int_{M}|f_{\lambda,y}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{A_{\lambda}}|f_{\lambda,y}|^{p}\leq C\int_{A_{\lambda}\cap B(y,r/\lambda)}|f_{\lambda,y}|^{p}+C\int_{M\setminus B(y,r/\lambda)}|f_{\lambda,y}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\lambda^{mp}\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap B(y,r/\lambda))+C_{3}r^{-N+m}\lambda^{mp-m}.

Therefore, choosing

), which implies that

5.
Proof of Theorem
2

Let
\mu=\{\mu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
be a sequence of measures on
M
.

5.1.
Sufficiency part

Suppose there exist
r>0
and
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
z\in M
, we have

\mu_{\lambda}(B(z,r/\lambda))\leq C\lambda^{-m}.

For any

, by the mean-value inequality (
3.6
) and the

bound (
3.2
) for the harmonic extension

in (

), we have for

\displaystyle\int_{M}|f|^{p}d\mu_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq C(\lambda/r)^{m+1}\int_{M}\int_{B(z,r/\lambda)}\int_{|s|\leq r/\lambda}|h(w,s)|^{p}dV(w)dsd\mu_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle=C(\lambda/r)^{m+1}\int_{M}\int_{|s|\leq r/\lambda}|h(w,s)|^{p}\mu_{\lambda}(B(w,r/\lambda))dV(w)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim(\lambda/r)\int_{M}\int_{|s|\leq r/\lambda}|h(w,s)|^{p}dV(w)ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{M}|f|^{p}dV.

Thus,

is

-Carleson.

5.2.
Necessity part

Suppose that the boundary is empty or the boundary condition is Neumann. We assume

is
L^{p}
-Carleson. We need to prove that there exist
C>0
and
r>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq 1
and
\xi\in M

(5.1)

\mu_{\lambda}(B(\xi,r/\lambda))\leq C\lambda^{-m}.

We choose
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}\leq\psi\leq{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{[-1,1]}.

Let

where

is chosen as in Corollary
14
such that (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) hold for all
t>0
and
x,y\in M
. Then
F_{\lambda,\xi}\in E_{\lambda}
. By the kernel estimate in (

), we have

|F_{\lambda,\xi}(x)|\lesssim\lambda^{m}(1+\lambda d(\xi,x))^{-N},\ \ \forall N.

So for

\|F_{\lambda,\xi}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\lesssim\lambda^{m(1-\frac{1}{p})}.

Moreover, by using the two-sided estimate in (

to be

-Carleson. Fix

. Let

where

6.1.
Proof of Proposition
3

For
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
, we need to show the relatively dense condition (
1.5
) is not necessary for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on
M
. We fix any

. For any ball

-normalized eigenfunction

we have

and then for

See e.g. Sogge
[
46
]
.
On the other hand, by Hölder inequality we get

\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV\gtrsim\begin{cases}1,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ 2\leq p<\infty,\\

\int_{A_{\lambda}}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV\approx\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV.

So
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is

-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions. On the other hand, we have

for

and sufficiently large

. So
\mathcal{A}
is not relatively dense, since

for any

and sufficiently large
\lambda
.

for a generic metric on any compact manifold. See Sogge-Zelditch

with

as

As before, if we define

\int_{A_{\lambda}}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV\approx\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV.

So

is

6.3.

be the standard sphere. For

By definition, it is easy to see that

Suppose

is symmetric relatively dense. By Theorem

,

is

-Logvinenko-Sereda. The eigenfunctions on

are the restriction to

of homogeneous harmonic polynomials in

-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on

On the other hand, we can use zonal functions as test functions to show that the symmetric relatively dense condition is necessary. Let

(6.2)

Z_{\lambda,\xi}(x)=\lambda^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}\sum_{\lambda_{j}=\lambda}e_{j}(x)\overline{e_{j}(\xi)}.

We have
\|Z_{\lambda,\xi}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\approx 1
for
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
and
\|Z_{\lambda,\xi}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\approx\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}-\frac{m}{p}}

for

and we have

|Z_{\lambda,\xi}(x)|\leq C\lambda^{m-1}(1+\lambda d(x,\xi))^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}

whenever

. See e.g. Sogge
[
48
]
.

\mathcal{A}
is

-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on

. We fix any

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}p-m}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{M}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}dV\leq C\int_{A_{\lambda}}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}dV

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{A_{\lambda}\cap B(\xi,r/\lambda)}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}+C\int_{A_{\lambda}\cap B(-\xi,r/\lambda)}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}+C\int_{S^{m}\setminus\cup_{\pm}B(\pm\xi,r/\lambda)}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}

Therefore, choosing

So

is symmetric relatively dense.

8.
Further discussions

It is open to characterize
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda sets and
L^{p}
-Carleson measures for eigenfunctions on
M
for
1\leq p\leq\frac{2m}{m-1}
, even if
M
is the standard sphere. In this section, we give some necessary conditions on the standard sphere
S^{m}
by choosing Gaussian beams on
S^{m}
as test functions. We also propose a conjecture and provide some evidence.

For
R>0
, let
\mathcal{T}_{R}(\gamma)
be the
R
-neighborhood of a closed geodesic
\gamma
in the sphere
S^{m}
. Then we have

\text{vol}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))\approx\lambda^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}.

In this section, we fix
G_{\lambda,\gamma}
to be the
L^{2}
-normalized Gaussian beam concentrated along the geodesic
\gamma
. Then we have for
1\leq p\leq\infty

\|G_{\lambda,\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\approx\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}.

Moreover,
|G_{\lambda,\gamma}(x)|\approx\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}}
for most
x\in\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)
, and

|G_{\lambda,\gamma}(x)|\leq C_{N}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}}(1+\sqrt{\lambda}d(x,\gamma))^{-N},\ \forall N.

Here
d(x,\gamma)
is the distance from
x
to the geodesic
\gamma
. See e.g. Sogge
[
48
]
.

8.1.
Tubular geometric control condition for Logvinenko-Sereda sets for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere

Let
M
be the standard sphere
S^{m}
. Suppose
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on
M
. We fix any closed geodesic
\gamma\subset S^{m}
. For any
c>0
, we get for
N>m

\displaystyle\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})p}

\displaystyle\approx\int_{M}|G_{\lambda,\gamma}|^{p}dV\lesssim\int_{A_{\lambda}}|G_{\lambda,\gamma}|^{p}dV

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)}|G_{\lambda,\gamma}|^{p}dV+\int_{M\setminus\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)}|G_{\lambda,\gamma}|^{p}dV

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}p}\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))+C_{N}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}p}\int_{r>c\lambda^{-1/2}}(1+\sqrt{\lambda}r)^{-N}r^{m-2}dr

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}p}\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))+C_{N}^{\prime}c^{-N+m}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}p-\frac{m-1}{2}}.

So we can choose
c
sufficiently large to obtain

(8.1)

\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))\gtrsim\lambda^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}.

So a necessary condition for
\mathcal{A}
to be
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere
S^{m}
is that there exist
c>0
and
\rho>0
such that for any closed geodesic
\gamma\subset S^{m}
and
\lambda\geq 1
,

(8.2)

\frac{\text{vol}(A_{\lambda}\cap\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))}{\text{vol}(\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))}\geq\rho.

We expect that this condition is also sufficient for
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
. Indeed, this condition implies that the inequality

\int_{A_{\lambda}}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV\gtrsim\int_{M}|e_{\lambda}|^{p}dV

holds for
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
whenever
e_{\lambda}
is a Gaussian beam or zonal function. For the Gaussian beam
G_{\lambda,\gamma}
, we exploit the fact that
|G_{\lambda,\gamma}(x)|\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{4}}
for most of
x\in\mathcal{T}_{c\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)
. For the zonal function
Z_{\lambda,\xi}
, we exploit the fact that
\text{vol}(A_{\lambda})\gtrsim 1
and
|Z_{\lambda,\xi}(x)|\gtrsim 1
for most of
x\in A_{\lambda}
.

Remark 19
.

Nicolas Burq presented us with two other examples of spherical harmonics that may concentrate on geodesically curved curves on
S^{2}
, see
[
4
, Remark 5.4]
. By careful calculations, we find that the condition (
8.2
) is still sufficient for these two examples when
p=2
.

Further support for the sufficiency of condition (
8.2
) comes from control theory. It is well-known that if
\omega\subset M
is an open set and satisfies the geometric control condition (GCC), that is, for some time
T>0
, every geodesic of length
T
intersects
\omega
. Then the following observability inequality holds for solution
u(t,x)
to the homogeneous wave equation on
M
:

\|(u(0,\cdot),u_{t}(0,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}(M)\times H^{-1}(M)}^{2}\leq C_{T}(\omega)\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\omega}|u(t,x)|^{2}dV(x)dt.

See e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
39
,
24
]
. In particular, taking
u=e^{it\sqrt{-\Delta}}e_{\lambda}=e^{it\lambda}e_{\lambda}
, we obtain

\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\lesssim\|e_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}.

Drawing on this similarity, we refer to condition (
8.2
) as the
tubular geometric control condition
(TGCC). Based on the preceding discussion, we propose the following conjecture.

Conjecture 20
.

For
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
,
\mathcal{A}
is
L^{p}
-Logvinenko-Sereda for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere
S^{m}
if and only if
\mathcal{A}
satisfies the tubular geometric control condition.

If the conjecture holds for some
1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}
, then it implies that, the
L^{p}
-norm of any sequence of spherical harmonics cannot concentrate within the
o(\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}})
-neighborhood of the equator.

8.2.
Tubular geometric control condition for Carleson measures for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere

Let
M
be the standard sphere
S^{m}
. Suppose a family of measures
\mu=\{\mu_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
is
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions on
M
. Similarly, testing Gaussian beams yields the following necessary condition: there exists a constant
C>0
such that for any closed geodesic
\gamma\subset S^{m}
and any
\lambda\geq 1
,

(8.3)

\frac{\mu_{\lambda}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))}{\text{vol}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))}\leq C.

However, this condition is not sufficient for any
1<p<\infty
. Indeed, if we fix any
\xi\in S^{m}
and let

d\mu_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\frac{m+1}{2}}{\rm 1\hskip-1.72218pt\rule{0.43057pt}{6.54443pt}\hskip 0.86108pt}_{B(\xi,\lambda^{-1})}dV,

then it satisfies the condition (
8.3
) since for any closed geodesic
\gamma\in S^{m}
we have

\mu_{\lambda}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma))\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\lambda^{-m}\approx\text{vol}(\mathcal{T}_{\lambda^{-1/2}}(\gamma)).

For the zonal functions in (
6.2
), we have

\int_{M}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}d\mu_{\lambda}\approx\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}p}\lambda^{\frac{m+1}{2}}\lambda^{-m}=\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}(p-1)},

and

\int_{M}|Z_{\lambda,\xi}|^{p}dV\approx\begin{cases}1,\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad 1\leq p<\frac{2m}{m-1}\\
\log\lambda,\ \ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad p=\frac{2m}{m-1}\\
\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}p-m},\ \ \quad\quad\quad\frac{2m}{m-1}<p<\infty.\end{cases}

So
\mu
is not
L^{p}
-Carleson for eigenfunctions on the standard sphere
S^{m}
for any
1<p<\infty
.

The number of normal measures, revisited
Eyal Kaplan

Abstract
We present a new version of the Friedman–Magidor theorem: for every measurable cardinal κ \kappa and τ ≤ κ + ⁣ + \tau\leq\kappa^{++} , there exists a forcing extension V ⊆ V ⁡ [ G ] V\subseteq V[G] such that any normal measure U ∈ V U\in V on κ \kappa has exactly τ \tau distinct lifts in V ⁡ [ G ] V[G] , and every normal measure on κ \kappa in V ⁡ [ G ] V[G] arises as such a lift. This version differs from the original Friedman–Magidor theorem in several notable ways. First, the new technique does not involve forcing over canonical inner models or rely on any fine-structural tools or assumptions, allowing it to be applied in the realm of large cardinals beyond the current reach of the inner model program. Second, in the case where τ ≤ κ + \tau\leq\kappa^{+} , all lifts of a normal measure U ∈ V U\in V on κ \kappa to V ⁡ [ G ] V[G] have the same ultrapower. Finally, the technique generalizes to a version of the Friedman–Magidor theorem for extenders. An additional advantage is that the forcing used is notably simple, relying only on nonstationary support product forcing.



The analysis of measures in forcing extensions plays a central role in the theory of large cardinals. A major question in this area was whether the existence of a model of ZFC with exactly two normal measures follows from the consistency of ZFC with a measurable cardinal. This question was answered affirmatively by the following landmark theorem of Friedman and Magidor.

In their work, Friedman and Magidor identified the various components that contribute to the construction of normal measures in forcing extensions. E
of
V
which have an additional single generator above
\kappa
. In this case, the splitting forcing
\mathbb{P}^{\tau}
might ”kill” this additional generator, and
E
lifts to a normal measure which differs from those described in Theorem
0.2
.
9
9

9

The limitation
\tau<\kappa^{+}
from Section
2
(where
\tau
is as in Section
2
) ensures that this situation does not happen, namely normal measures of the generic extension cannot be generated from non-normal measures of the ground model by killing one of their generators, say
\zeta
. We already proved this in Theorem
0.2
, but now we have another intuitive reason: killing
\zeta
involves constructing a
j_{E}(\mathbb{P}^{\tau})
-generic set over
M_{E}
that maps
\kappa
to
\zeta
, but such a generic cannot exist due to the limitation
\tau<\kappa^{+}
and the fact that
\zeta>\kappa^{+}
.

We summarize this in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume GCH. Denote
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}^{\tau}
let
G\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be generic over
V
. Suppose that
W\in V[G]
be a normal measure on
\kappa
. Then there exists a
\kappa
-complete ultrafilter
E
in
V
such that
W=E^{*}_{\eta}
for some
\eta<j_{E}(\tau)(\kappa)
(here, we used the notations of Lemma
4.2
, viewing
E
a
(\kappa,\lambda)
extender for
\lambda=j_{E}(g)(\kappa)
). Furthermore, we can ensure that the set of generators of
j_{E}
is either
\{\kappa\}
or
\{\kappa,\eta\}
for the above
\eta
.

Proof.

The only generator of
W
is
\kappa
, and it is below
j_{W}(g)(\kappa)
. Thus, by Theorem
4.2
, we deduce that
W=E^{*}_{\eta}
for some
(\kappa,\lambda)
-extender
E
in
V
and
\eta<j_{E}(\tau)(\kappa)
. It remains to prove that the only possible generator of
E
other than
\kappa
is
\eta
(possibly,
E
is already equivalent to a normal measure, and then
\kappa
is the single generator of
j_{E}
and
W
is really a lift of a ground-model normal measure).

Fix
\zeta\neq\kappa,\eta
, and let us prove that
\zeta
is not a generator of
E
. Since
W
is normal, there exists
f\in V[G]
such that
\zeta=j_{W}(f)(\kappa)
. By the standard Fusion argument from the proof of Corollary
1.5
, there exists
p\in G
, and a club
C\subseteq\kappa
such that for every
\alpha\in C
,

p\upharpoonright\alpha+1\Vdash\exists\gamma\in\text{Ord}\ \left(p\setminus(\alpha+1)\Vdash\dot{f}(\alpha)=\check{\gamma}\right).

In particular, since
\kappa\in j_{E}(C)
, we have in
M_{E}
that

p^{\frown}0_{\mathbb{Q}_{\kappa}}\Vdash\exists\gamma\in\text{Ord}\ \left(j_{E}(p)\setminus(\kappa+1)\Vdash j_{E}(\dot{f})(\kappa)=\check{\gamma}\right).

Over
M_{E}
, we can use the fact that
\eta<j_{E}(\tau)(\kappa)
is a possible generic for
\mathbb{Q}_{\kappa}
, to deduce

p\Vdash\exists\gamma\in\text{Ord}\ \left(\{(\kappa,\eta)\}\cup j_{E}(p)\setminus(\kappa+1)\Vdash j_{E}(\dot{f})(\kappa)=\check{\gamma}\right).

Overall, we proved that, in
M_{E}
, there exists a
j_{E}(\mathbb{P})\upharpoonright\kappa=\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{\gamma}
for an ordinal, such that

\left(j_{E}(p)\cup\{(\kappa,\eta)\}\right)\Vdash j_{E}(\dot{f})(\kappa)=\dot{\gamma}.

We can assume that
\dot{\gamma}
is the least
\mathbb{P}
-name as above, where, as usual, by ”least”, we mean here that we fixed in advance a well order
\mathcal{W}
of
V_{\kappa}
, and chose
\dot{\gamma}
least with respect to
j_{E}(\mathcal{W})
. This implies that
\dot{\gamma}
has the form
j_{E}(h)(\kappa,\eta)
for some
h\in V
.

Let
A\in M_{E}
be the set of all possible values of
\dot{\gamma}
as computed in
M_{E}
, namely

A=\{\beta\in\text{Ord}\ (\exists q\geq j_{E}(p)\ (q\Vdash\dot{\gamma}=\check{\beta}))\}.

Then
A\in M_{E}
has the form
j_{E}(h^{*})(\kappa,\eta)
for some function
h^{*}\in V
,
|A|\leq\kappa^{+}
and

j_{E}(p)\cup\{\langle\kappa,\eta\rangle\}\Vdash j_{E}(\dot{f})(\kappa)\in\check{A}.

Recall that
p\in G
and
\eta=j_{W}(\cup G)(\kappa)
. Thus, in
\text{Ult}(V[G],W)=M_{E}[j_{W}(G)]
, we have
\zeta=j_{W}(f)(\kappa)\in A
. In particular, for some ordinal
\xi<\kappa^{+}
,

\zeta
is the
\xi
-th element of
A
with respect to the least well-order of
A
of order type
\leq\kappa^{+}
(again, the well order is chosen minimal with respect to
j_{E}(\mathcal{W})
for
\mathcal{W}\in V
). Letting

be the canonical function that represents
\xi
, we obtain that, in
M_{E}
,
\zeta
is the
j_{E}(f_{\xi})(\kappa)
-th element of
j_{E}(h^{*})(\kappa,\eta)
, with respect to the
j_{E}(\mathcal{W})
-least well order of
j_{E}(h^{*})(\kappa,\eta)
of order type
\kappa^{+}
. This shows that
\zeta
is not a generator of
j_{E}
.
∎

5
Open questions and concluding remarks

In this final section, we discuss some open questions.

Question 5.1
.

Is it consistent that GCH holds, there are
\kappa^{++}
many normal measures on
\kappa
, but only a single normal ultrapower?

The same proof technique of Theorem
0.2
does not appear to apply here (see Remark
2.2
), but we still conjecture that the answer is positive.

Note that a positive answer to Question
5.1
would exhibit behavior somewhat different from that of extenders. For example, assuming GCH, there are at most
\kappa^{++}
many

-extenders with the same ultrapower (by Theorem

), even though the total number of
(\kappa,\kappa^{++})
-extenders can be

.

Next, recall that the Friedman–Magidor theorem generalizes well to the context of violation of GCH on a measurable.

Theorem 5.1

(Friedman and Magidor,
[
9
]
)
.

Let

be the minimal extender model that contains a cardinal

(\kappa+2)
-strong. Then for every
\tau<\kappa^{++}

This motivates the question whether the methods developed in this paper can yield similar results.

Question 5.2

Assume that
\kappa

(\kappa+2)
-strong in an arbitrary ZFC+GCH model

, it was shown that the Ultrapower Axiom is consistent with the failure of GCH at a measurable cardinal, via the construction of an

Question 5.3
.

Is the Weak Ultrapower Axiom and the negation of the Ultrapower Axiom consistent with the failure of GCH on a measurable?

A natural approach to Question

is to force with the splitting forcing over the

-like model described above and analyze all possible lifts of

Proof.

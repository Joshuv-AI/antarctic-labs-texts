Pullback and Weil transfer on Chow groups Thanks: This work has been done during the second named author’s stay at the Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifiques.
Nikita Karpenko, Guangzhao Zhu

Abstract
. In the paper “Weil transfer of algebraic cycles”, published by the second author in Indagationes Mathematicae about 25 years ago, a Weil transfer map for Chow groups of smooth algebraic varieties has been constructed and its basic properties have been established. The proof of commutativity with the pullback homomorphisms given there used a variant of Moving Lemma suffering a lack of reference. Here we are providing an alternative proof based on a more contemporary construction of the pullback via a deformation to the normal cone.



Let
F
be a field.
By
F
-variety
, we mean a quasi-projective
F
-scheme.

Let
L/F
be a finite separable field extension and let
X
be an
L
-variety.
We write
R(X)=R_{L/F}(X)
for the
F
-variety given by the
Weil transfer

(also called
Weil restriction
)
of
X
with respect to
L/F
,
see
[
2
, §7.6]
or
[
8
, §4]
.
In
[
6
]
, a (non-additive) map of Chow groups

\cat{R}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(X))

for smooth
X
has been constructed,
called the
Weil transfer map
.
It satisfies the following property:
for any closed subvariety
Z\subset X
, the image under
\cat{R}
of the class of
Z
is the class of the closed subvariety

R(Z)\subset R(X)
.

The map
\cat{R}
is induced by the map

\mathcal{R}\colon\mathcal{Z}(X)\to\mathcal{Z}(R(X))

of the groups of cycles defined as follows.

Let
E/F
be a normal closure of the field extension
L/F
.
For any
F
-embedding

\tau\colon L\hookrightarrow E,

we define an
E
-variety
X_{\tau}
as the base change of
X
with respect to
\tau
:

\begin{CD}X_{\tau}@>{}>{}>X\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\operatorname{Spec}E@>{\tau}>{}>\operatorname{Spec}L\end{CD}

The canonical morphism of
L
-varieties
R(X)_{L}\to X
induces
an isomorphism of the
E
-variety
R(X)_{E}
with the product

\operatornamewithlimits{\textstyle\prod}_{\tau}X_{\tau}
.
For any cycle
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(X)
and any
\tau
as above, we write
\alpha_{\tau}
for the pullback of
\alpha
to
X_{\tau}
via the morphism

X_{\tau}\to X
.
We define
\mathcal{R}(\alpha)
as the cycle on
R(X)
mapped
to the external product
\operatornamewithlimits{\textstyle\prod}_{\tau}\alpha_{\tau}

under the base change homomorphism

\mathcal{Z}(R(X))\to\mathcal{Z}(R(X)_{E})=\mathcal{Z}(\operatornamewithlimits{\textstyle\prod}_{\tau}X_{\tau}).

The cycle
\mathcal{R}(\alpha)
exists and is uniquely determined by the above condition because
the base change homomorphism
\mathcal{Z}(R(X))\to\mathcal{Z}(R(X)_{E})
identifies
\mathcal{Z}(R(X))
with the group

\mathcal{Z}(R(X)_{E})^{G}
of
G
-invariant elements in
\mathcal{Z}(R(X)_{E})
, where
G
is the Galois group of
E/F
.

Most of the properties of the map of Chow groups
\cat{R}
,
established in
[
6
]
, are easy to verify because they hold “on the level of cycles”.
For instance,

Example 1
.

For any two smooth
L
-varieties
X,Y
and a
flat
morphism of schemes

f\colon Y\to X,

the morphism
R(f)\colon R(Y)\to R(X)
is also flat, and
the square on the left

\begin{CD}\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y)@<{f^{*}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\\
@V{\cat{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\cat{R}}V\\
\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(Y))@<{R(f)^{*}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(X))\end{CD}\hskip 70.0001pt\begin{CD}\mathcal{Z}(Y)@<{f^{*}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(X)\\
@V{\mathcal{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\mathcal{R}}V\\
\mathcal{Z}(R(Y))@<{R(f)^{*}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(R(X))\end{CD}

commutes because by
[
6
, Proposition 3.5(flat pull-back)]
so does the square on the right.

The commutation with the general pullback homomorphism however
is more delicate because the latter is not defined on the level of cycles:

Proposition 2

(
[
6
, Proposition 4.4(pull-back)]
)
.

For any morphism
f\colon Y\to X
of smooth
L
-varieties
Y
and
X
, the square

\begin{CD}\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y)@<{f^{*}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\\
@V{\cat{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\cat{R}}V\\
\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(Y))@<{R(f)^{*}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(X))\end{CD}

commutes.

To prove Proposition
2
, a variant of Moving Lemma suffering a lack of reference
(see
[
3
, Appendix A]
)
has been used in
[
6
]
.

Here we are providing an alternative proof based on the “modern” definition of the pullback via the deformation to the normal cone
homomorphism.
More precisely, we will use the modified approach developed by Markus Rost in
[
7
]
with its detailed exposition given
in
[
4
]
, which is simpler than the original approach of
[
5
]
.

First of all,
the homomorphism
f^{*}
is defined (see
[
4
, (55.15)]
) as the composition
\operatorname{\mathit{in}}^{*}\circ\operatorname{\mathit{pr}}^{*}
,
where

\operatorname{\mathit{pr}}\colon Y\times X\to X

is the projection and

\operatorname{\mathit{in}}:=(\mathrm{id}_{Y},f)\colon Y\to Y\times X.

Taking into account the identification
R(Y\times X)=R(Y)\times R(X)
, the morphism
R(\operatorname{\mathit{pr}})
is the projection

R(Y)\times R(X)\to R(X)
whereas
R(\operatorname{\mathit{in}})=(\mathrm{id}_{R(Y)},R(f))
.

The morphisms
\operatorname{\mathit{pr}}
and
R(\operatorname{\mathit{pr}})
are flat so that the pullbacks
\operatorname{\mathit{pr}}^{*}
and
R(\operatorname{\mathit{pr}})^{*}
are defined on the level of cycles;
the squares

\begin{CD}\mathcal{Z}(Y\times X)@<{\operatorname{\mathit{pr}}^{*}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(X)\\
@V{\mathcal{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\mathcal{R}}V\\
\mathcal{Z}(R(Y\times X))@<{R(\operatorname{\mathit{pr}})^{*}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(R(X))\end{CD}\hskip 30.00005pt\text{ and }\hskip 30.00005pt\begin{CD}\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y\times X)@<{\operatorname{\mathit{pr}}^{*}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\\
@V{\cat{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\cat{R}}V\\
\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(Y\times X))@<{R(\operatorname{\mathit{pr}})^{*}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(X))\end{CD}

commute by Example
1
.

The morphism
\operatorname{\mathit{in}}
is a regular closed embedding.
In fact, any closed embedding of smooth varieties is regular (see
[
4
, Proposition 104.16]
).
Since the Weil transfer functor preserves smoothness and closed embeddings, we reduced the proof
of Proposition
2
to the case where
f
is a closed embedding.

Once we assume
f
is a closed embedding,
the pullback homomorphism
f^{*}
is the Gysin homomorphism
defined (see
[
4
, 55.A]
) as the composition
(p_{f}^{*})^{-1}\circ\sigma_{f}
,
where

\sigma_{f}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(N_{f})

is the deformation homomorphism and

p_{f}\colon N_{f}\to Y
is the vector bundle over
Y
given by the normal cone of
f
.
By Homotopy Invariance of Chow groups
[
4
, Theorem 52.13]
,
the flat pullback
p_{f}^{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y)\to\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(N_{f})

is an isomorphism.

We claim that the normal bundle
N_{R(f)}

of the closed embedding
R(f)\colon R(Y)\to R(X)

is given by the Weil transfer of
N_{f}
:

(3)

N_{R(f)}=R(N_{f}).

To see it,
note that
by
[
8
, (4.2.3)]
,

R(X)_{L}
can be obtained as the Weil transfer of
X_{K}
with respect to
the étale
L
-algebra
K:=L\otimes_{F}L
.
This
L
-algebra splits off
L
as a direct factor:

K=L\times K^{\prime}
for certain étale
L
-algebra
K^{\prime}
.
Thus, by
[
8
, (4.2.6)]
,
R(X)_{L}=X\times R^{\prime}(X)
, where
R^{\prime}(X)
is the Weil transfer of
X_{K^{\prime}}
.
Note that the canonical morphism
R(X)_{L}\to X
is given by the projection
X\times R^{\prime}(X)\to X
.
Recall that the induced morphism of
E
-varieties
R(X)_{E}\to\operatornamewithlimits{\textstyle\prod}_{\tau}X_{\tau}
, where
\tau
runs
over the
F
-embeddings
L\hookrightarrow E
, is an isomorphism.

It follows that the closed embedding
R(f)_{L}\colon R(Y)_{L}\to R(X)_{L}
is the direct product

f\times R^{\prime}(f)\colon Y\times R^{\prime}(Y)\to X\times R^{\prime}(X)

of the closed embeddings

Y\to X
and
R^{\prime}(Y)\to R^{\prime}(X)
, and so

(N_{R(f)})_{L}=N_{R(f)_{L}}=N_{f}\times N_{R^{\prime}(f)}

by naturality of the normal cone
[
4
, Proposition 104.23]
and its commutation with direct products

[
4
, Proposition 104.7]
.
The first projection
(N_{R(f)})_{L}\to N_{f}
induces an isomorphism

(N_{R(f)})_{E}\to\operatornamewithlimits{\textstyle\prod}_{\tau}(N_{f})_{\tau}
proving claim (
3
),
cf.​
[
1
, §2.8]
.

Recall that the morphism

p_{f}\colon N_{f}\to Y

is flat and the pullback

p_{f}^{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y)\to\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(N_{f})

is an isomorphism.
Similarly, the morphism

R(p_{f})\colon R(N_{f})\to R(Y)

is flat and the pullback

R(p_{f})^{*}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(Y))\to\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(N_{f}))

is an isomorphism.
We already know (due to the fact that
p_{f}^{*}
and
R(p_{f})^{*}
are defined on the level of cycles) that the square

\begin{CD}\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y)@>{p_{f}^{*}}>{}>\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(N_{f})\\
@V{\cat{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\cat{R}}V\\
\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(Y))@>{R(p_{f})^{*}}>{}>\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(N_{f}))\end{CD}

commutes.
It follows that the square

\begin{CD}\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(Y)@<{(p_{f}^{*})^{-1}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(N_{f})\\
@V{\cat{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\cat{R}}V\\
\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(Y))@<{(R(p_{f})^{*})^{-1}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(N_{f}))\end{CD}

with the inverses of

p_{f}^{*}
and
R(p_{f})^{*}
(not defined on the level of cycles anymore) commutes as well.

As per
[
4
, §51]
,
the deformation homomorphism
\sigma_{f}
is also defined on the level of cycles.
Because of that, our last remaining step in the proof of Proposition
2
is not difficult to perform:

Lemma 4
.

The squares

\begin{CD}\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(N_{f})@<{\sigma_{f}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\\
@V{\cat{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\cat{R}}V\\
\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(N_{f}))@<{\sigma_{R(f)}}<{}<\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(X))\end{CD}\hskip 30.00005pt\text{ and }\hskip 30.00005pt\begin{CD}\mathcal{Z}(N_{f})@<{\sigma_{f}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(X)\\
@V{\mathcal{R}}V{}V@V{}V{\mathcal{R}}V\\
\mathcal{Z}(R(N_{f}))@<{\sigma_{R(f)}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(R(X))\end{CD}

commute.

Proof.

We only need to treat the second square: its commutativity implies commutativity of the first one.

Since the base change homomorphism
\mathcal{Z}(R(N_{f}))\to\mathcal{Z}(R(N_{f})_{E})
is injective,
we may perform the base change
E/F
in the lower line of the
\mathcal{Z}
-square.
Then it becomes

\begin{CD}\mathcal{Z}(N_{f})@<{\sigma_{f}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(X)\\
@V{\beta\mapsto\prod_{\tau}\beta_{\tau}}V{}V@V{}V{\alpha\mapsto\prod_{\tau}\alpha_{\tau}}V\\
\mathcal{Z}(\prod_{\tau}N_{f_{\tau}})@<{\prod_{\tau}\sigma_{f_{\tau}}}<{}<\mathcal{Z}(\prod_{\tau}X_{\tau})\end{CD}

and is commutative because

\sigma_{f_{\tau}}(\alpha_{\tau})=\sigma_{f}(\alpha)_{\tau}

for any cycle
\alpha\in\mathcal{Z}(X)
and
F
-embedding
\tau\colon L\hookrightarrow E
according to
[
4
, Proposition 51.5]
.
∎

Remark 5
.

By
[
6
, Proposition 4.4(interior product)]
, the Weil transfer map

\cat{R}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(R(X))

is
multiplicative
:

(6)

\cat{R}(\alpha\cdot\beta)=\cat{R}(\alpha)\cdot\cat{R}(\beta)

for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathop{\mathrm{CH}}\nolimits(X)
.
This follows from
[
6
, Proposition 4.4(exterior product)]
giving the similar formula

\cat{R}(\alpha\times\beta)=\cat{R}(\alpha)\times\cat{R}(\beta)

for the external product, the formula
\alpha\cdot\beta=\delta^{*}(\alpha\times\beta)

(see
[
4
, (56.1)]
)
expressing the internal product as
the pullback of the external one with respect to the diagonal morphism
\delta\colon X\to X\times X
, and
Proposition
2
.
Therefore the new proof of Proposition
2
, given here, provides a new proof for (
6
) as well.

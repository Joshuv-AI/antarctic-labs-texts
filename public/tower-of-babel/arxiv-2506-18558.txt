Averaging principles for time-inhomogeneous multi-scale SDEs with partially dissipative coefficients
Xiaobin Sun, Jian Wang, Yingchao Xie

Abstract
. In this paper, we study averaging principles for a class of time-inhomogeneous stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with slow and fast time-scales, where the drift term in the fast component is time-dependent and only partially dissipative. Under asymptotic assumptions on the coefficients, we prove that the slow component ( X t ε ) t ≥ 0 (X^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0} converges strongly to the unique solution ( X ¯ t ) t ≥ 0 (\bar{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0} to an averaged SDE, when the diffusion coefficient in the slow component is independent of the fast component; on the other hand, we establish the weak convergence of ( X t ε ) t ≥ 0 (X_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0} in the space C ⁡ ( [ 0 , T ] , ℝ n ) C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}) and identify the limiting process by the martingale problem approach, when the diffusion coefficient of the slow component depends on the fast component. The proofs of strong and weak averaging principles are partly based on the study of the existence and uniqueness of an evolution system of measures for time-inhomogeneous SDEs with partially dissipative drift.

1.
Background and main results

1.1.
Background

Multi-scale systems encompass diverse applications, where the associated processes evolve across varying temporal and spatial scales. They are prevalent in various fields such as climate modeling, material science, biology, and nonlinear oscillations, see e.g.
[
10
]
. Multi-scale systems frequently exhibit unconventional characteristics that defy common sense, to which using traditional, singular theoretical frameworks inadequate. Consequently, the exploration of multi-scale modeling systems becomes extremely important and indispensable.
The mathematical model of multi-scale stochastic differential equations (SDEs) usually can be described as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dB^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}={\varepsilon}^{-1}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}g(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dB^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
(B^{1}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(B^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are two independent
d_{1}
-dimensional and
d_{2}
-dimensional standard Brownian motions respectively,
\varepsilon
is a small and positive parameter describing the ratio of the time scales between the slow component
(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and the fast component
(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and the coefficients

b\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\sigma\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d_{1}}
,
f\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
g\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m\times d_{2}}

are Borel measurable. The techniques employed in this context are commonly known as averaging and homogenization methods, see e.g.
[
12
,
17
,
23
]
and the references therein. For example, as discussed in Khasminskii’s pioneer work
[
17
]
, it is assumed that there exist
\bar{b}\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\bar{A}\!:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
such that for all
T\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\left|\frac{1}{T}\int^{t+T}_{t}\mathbb{E}b(x,Y^{x,y}_{s})\,ds-\bar{b}(x)\right|\leq\alpha(T)(1+|x|^{2})

and

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\left|\frac{1}{T}\int^{t+T}_{t}\mathbb{E}(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(x,Y^{x,y}_{s})\,ds-\bar{A}(x)\right|\leq\alpha(T)(1+|x|^{2}),

where
\alpha:\mathbb{R}_{+}:=[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies that
\alpha(T)\to 0
as
T\rightarrow\infty
, and
(Y^{x,y}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the unique solution to the following frozen SDE

(1.3)

dY^{x,y}_{t}=f(x,Y^{x,y}_{t})\,dt+g(x,Y^{x,y}_{t})\,dB^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{x,y}_{0}=y.

Then, the (weak) averaging principle claims that, for any
T>0
, the slow component
(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
converges weakly to the solution
(\bar{X}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
of the following averaged SDE

(1.4)

d\bar{X}_{t}=\bar{b}(\bar{X}_{t})\,dt+\bar{\sigma}(\bar{X}_{t})\,d\bar{B}_{t},\quad X_{0}=x

as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
\bar{\sigma}(x):=(\bar{A}(x))^{1/2}
, i.e.,
\bar{\sigma}^{2}(x)=\bar{A}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
(\bar{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a standard
n
-dimensional Brownian motion.
Undoubtedly, a key step for understanding the averaging principle lies in comprehending the corresponding averaged SDE (
1.4
), and the most important thing is how to accurately describe the averaged coefficients involved in. In particular, the averaging coefficients depend on the invariant measure of the frozen SDE (
1.3
). For example, if the frozen SDE (
1.3
) admits a unique invariant measure
\mu^{x}
, then

\bar{b}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x,y)\,\mu^{x}(dy),\quad\bar{A}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(\sigma\sigma^{*})(x,y)\,\mu^{x}(dy).

Currently most research results focus on the case where the frozen SDEs are time-homogeneous, because of the rich theoretical research on the existence and uniqueness of invariant measures in this case, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,

,

,

,

,

,

The solution to a time-nonhomogeneous frozen SDE exhibits richer dynamic characteristics, leading to the understanding of the so-called equilibrium point extremely complex. More precisely, consider the following time-dependent SDE:

dY^{x,y}_{t}=f(t,x,Y^{x,y}_{t})\,dt+g(t,x,Y^{x,y}_{t})\,dB^{2}_{t},

which corresponds to the following multi-scale nonautonomous SDE:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dB^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}f(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}g(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dB^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.\end{array}\right.

As far as we know, there are very limited existing works on the time-inhomogeneous stochastic system such like
(
1.5
)
. If the coefficients

and

[

For uniformly dissipative coefficients
f
and
g
, recently we used the technique of nonautonomous Poisson equations to study the strong and weak averaging principle for
(
1.5
)
in
[
29
]
.

1.2.
Main results

In this paper, we are interested in the following nonautonomous stochastic system

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\quad X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}f(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}\,dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.\end{array}\right.

Here,
(W^{1}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(W^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are mutually independent
d
-dimensional and
m
-dimensional standard Brownian motions respectively, on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
with the natural filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t},t\geq 0\}
generated by
(W^{1}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(W^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. The coefficients
b:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\sigma:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
are measurable. Meanwhile, the coefficient
f(t,x,y)
enjoys the partial dissipative property with respect to
y
; see
1.9
below. Such property seems to be novel within the context of multi-scale SDEs and even in the time-independent framework.

We next provide the assumptions on the coefficients of the system (
1.6
), and show the main results of the paper, including the strong and weak averaging principles for (
1.6
). First of all, the following three conditions are always assumed throughout the paper.

A1
.

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle|b(t,x_{1},y_{1})-b(t,x_{2},y_{2})|+\|\sigma(t,x_{1},y_{1})-\sigma(t,x_{2},y_{2})\|\leq C_{0}(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|),\vskip 8.53581pt

(1.8)

\displaystyle|b(t,x_{1},y_{1})|\leq C_{0}(1+|x_{1}|+|y_{1}|),\quad\|\sigma(t,x_{1},y_{1})\|\leq C_{0}(1+|x_{1}|).

A2
.

There exist constants
C,K
and
r_{0}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(1.9)

\begin{split}&\langle f(t,x_{1},y_{1})-f(t,x_{2},y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\\
&\leq\begin{cases}C|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+C|x_{1}-x_{2}||y_{1}-y_{2}|,\quad&|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq r_{0},\\
-K|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+C|x_{1}-x_{2}||y_{1}-y_{2}|,\quad&|y_{1}-y_{2}|>r_{0}.\end{cases}\end{split}

Moreover,
there exists a constant
C_{*}>0
such that for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.10)

\displaystyle|f(t,x,{\mathbf{0}})|\leq C_{*}(1+|x|).

A3
.

There exists
\hat{b}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for any
T\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{1}{T}\left|\int^{t+T}_{t}(b(s,x,y)-\hat{b}(x,y))\,ds\right|\leq\phi_{1}(T)(1+|x|+|y|).

Moreover, there exist
k\geq 0
and
\bar{f}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that for all
T\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle|f(T,x,y)-\bar{f}(x,y)|\leq\phi_{2}(T)(1+|x|+|y|^{k}).

Here, for all
i=1,2
,
\phi_{i}\geq 0
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies
\phi_{i}(T)\rightarrow 0
as
T\to\infty
.

It is well known that under Assumptions
1
and
2
, the SDE
(
1.6
)
admits a unique strong solution
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
, e.g. see
[
19
, Theorem 3.1.1]
.
On the other hand, (

) is called a partially dissipative condition in the literature (see
[
11
,
22
]
).
It is obvious that the condition (
1.9
) is weaker than the classical uniformly dissipative condition.
By applying the elementary inequality
ab\leq\delta a^{2}+C_{\delta}b^{2}
for all
a,b\geq 0
and
\delta>0
, we can see from (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) that for all
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(1.13)

\begin{split}\langle f(t,x,y),y\rangle&=\langle f(t,x,y)-f(t,x,{\mathbf{0}}),y\rangle+\langle f(t,x,{\mathbf{0}}),y\rangle\\
&\leq-K|y|^{2}+(C+K)r_{0}^{2}+|f(t,x,{\mathbf{0}})||y|\\
&\leq-K|y|^{2}+(C+K)r_{0}^{2}+C_{*}(1+|x|)|y|\\
&\leq-K_{1}|y|^{2}+K_{2}(1+|x|^{2}),\end{split}

where
0<K_{1}<K
and

.
Two examples fulfilling Assumption

are as follows:

with

and

This together with (

are closely connected with the following

Suppose that

1.3.
Approaches

As we discussed before, the key idea to study the averaging principle for (
1.6
) is to provide precise characterizations of the averaged coefficients. The frozen SDE
corresponding to (
1.6
) is
given by

(1.21)

\displaystyle dY_{t}=f(t,x,Y_{t})dt+dW^{2}_{t},

which associates with a time-nonhomogeneous transition semigroup
(P^{x}_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
. General speaking, there is no invariant measure any more. A natural generalization of the notation for invariant measure corresponding to
(P^{x}_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
is an evolution system of measures (see e.g.
[
7
,
8
,
21
]
). Recall that,
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is an evolution system of measures for
(P^{x}_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
, if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy),\quad-\infty<s\leq t<+\infty,\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Hence, in order to achieve this, we need to extend the SDE (
1.21
) to define for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
instead of
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which particularly requires the coefficient
f(t,x,y)
and the noise
W^{2}_{t}
to be well-defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
; see Subsection
2.1
below for the details.

It is worthwhile to highlight that, when the coefficient
f(t,x,y)
is partially dissipative with respect to
y
, we will utilize the so-called asymptotic reflection coupling for different drifts in order to establish the weak and strong ergodicity for the frozen SDE (
1.21
) under the
L_{1}
-Wasserstein distance. To the best of our knowledge, this aspect in the study of the averaging principle appears to be new, and has not been explored before. We emphasize that, due to the partially dissipative drift, it seems that the method via the Poisson equation employed in
[
29
]
is not applicable in the current context, and in particular it seems challenging to derive the second derivative of
P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)
with respect to
y
, which is essential for constructing the solution to the Poisson equation.

Below we briefly state the approaches to the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, which partly indicate the novelties of our paper.

1.3.1.
Strong averaging principle

Our first contribution lies in the study of the asymptotic behavior for
\{X^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
in the strong sense as
\varepsilon\to 0
, in the case of
\sigma(t,x,y)=\sigma(t,x)
for all
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. To accomplish this objective, we will proceed in two steps.

Step 1:
Based on the observation above, it is very natural to introduce the averaged drift coefficient as follows:

\bar{b}(t,x)=\int b(t,x,y)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy),

which leads to the following averaged SDE:

(1.22)

\displaystyle d\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\bar{b}(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dt+\sigma(t/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,dW^{1}_{t},\quad\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x.

Here,
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is an evolution system of measures for the Markov semigroup
(P^{x}_{s,t})_{t\geq s}
corresponding to the extension of the frozen SDE (
1.21
).
Thus, we will first demonstrate that

(1.23)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where
(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the solution to the SDE (
1.22
).

Step 2:
It is worth mentioning that the coefficients of
the SDE (
1.22
) still depend on
\varepsilon
.
In order to achieve coefficients in the final averaged SDE that are independent of
\varepsilon
, it is sufficient to guarantee that

(1.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\displaystyle\int^{t+T}_{t}\bar{b}(s,x)ds\rightarrow\bar{b}(x),\quad T\to\infty

uniformly for
t
, and the condition (
1.15
) holds.
As stated in (
1.23
), to deal with the averaging principle for the nonautonomous stochastic system
(
1.6
), we shall turn to a study of the averaged SDE (
1.22
), as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
.
Starting from (
1.22
), the condition (
1.24
) is essentially equivalent to (
1.1
) by taking
Y^{x,y}_{t}=t
, see
[
32
, (6)]
. For this purpose, in contrast to the time-periodic case imposed on the coefficients (see
[
31
,
33
]
), we introduce here a reasonable Assumption
3
.
Such assumption guarantees that
\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{x}_{t},\mu^{x})\rightarrow 0
as
t\to\infty
, and it in turn indicates that (
1.24
) holds with
\bar{b}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\hat{b}(x,y)\,\mu^{x}(dy)
, thanks to (
1.11
). Here,
\mu^{x}
is the unique invariant measure of the SDE (
1.14
).
Thus, in this step we will prove that

(1.25)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{2}\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where
(\bar{X}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the solution to the ultimate averaged SDE (
1.17
) given in Theorem
1.1
. Combining (
1.23
) with (
1.25
), we achieve the strong averaging principle.

1.3.2.
Weak averaging principle

It is crucial to note that the assumption
\sigma(t,x,y)=\sigma(t,x)

is essential to establish the strong convergence. In the absence of this assumption, the strong convergence may not hold. However, we can expect the weak convergence in the general setting. For this, Assumption
5
on
\sigma(t,x,y)
is needed, which is related to (
1.2
); see
[
32
, (7)]
. Hence, our second significant contribution lies in proving that
\{X^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
converges weakly to
X
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where
X
is the unique solution to the
SDE (
1.20
) given in Theorem
1.2
.

To establish the weak convergence of
\{X^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
T>0
, we proceed in the following three steps.

Step 1:
We show that the family of the processes
\{X^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is tight in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This ensures that for any sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
tending to zero, there exists a subsequence of
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
(which is still denoted by
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
for simplicity) such that
X^{\varepsilon_{k}}
converges weakly to some accumulation point
X
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
k\to\infty
.

Step 2:
To identify the weak limit
X
, we utilize the martingale problem approach. We show that the limiting process
X
is a solution to the martingale problem associated with the SDE (
1.20
).

Step 3:
We rely on the uniqueness of the strong solution to the SDE (
1.20
) to conclude that the entire family
\{X^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
converges weakly to
X
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We shall mention that in our paper we are only concerned with the stochastic system (
1.6
), where the fast component
(Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
is driven by additive Brownian motion. This is only for the simplicity of the notations. Indeed, the approach of our paper works for the case that the fast component
(Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
has the multiplicative noise. The main technical differences are due to proofs of Lemma
2.1
(2) and Lemma
3.3
below. In order to obtain the corresponding assertions, one can make use of the mixture of the asymptotic coupling by reflection and the synchronous coupling; see
[
25
,
34
]
for the details.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. In Section 2, we will study the existence and uniqueness of an evolution system of measures for the time-dependent SDE (
1.21
) with partially dissipative drift. This involves analyzing the weak ergodicity of the SDE (
1.21
) under the
L_{1}
-Wasserstein distance. Here, we also establish a priori estimates of the stochastic system (
1.6
), and a quantitative difference between the fast component and its auxiliary process. Sections 3 and 4 are devoted to the proofs of the strong averaging principle and the weak averaging principle, respectively.
Throughout this paper, we use
C
to represent constants whose value may vary from line to line. We use
C_{T}
(resp.
C_{p,T}
) to emphasize that the constant depends on
T
(resp.
p
and
T
).

2.
Preliminaries

Denote by
|\cdot|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the Euclidean vector norm and the usual Euclidean inner product, respectively.
Let
\|\cdot\|
be the matrix or the operator norm if there is no confusion possible. Let
0
be the zero row vector in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the set of all probability measures on
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m}))
. The Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{d}
associated with a metric
d(x,y)
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is defined by

\mathbb{W}_{d}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\inf_{Q\in\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}d(x,y)\,Q(dx,dy),\quad\mu,\nu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu,\nu)
is the set of all coupling measures
Q
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
of
\mu
and
\nu
, i.e., for any
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

Q(A\times\mathbb{R}^{m})=\mu(A),\quad Q(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times A)=\nu(A).

If
d(x,y)=|x-y|
, then
\mathbb{W}_{d}
is the standard
L^{1}
-Wasserstein distance which is denoted by
\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
throughout the paper. Let

\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\left\{\mu\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbb{R}^{m}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|z|\,\mu(dz)<\infty\right\}.

Then,
\left(\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}),\mathbb{W}_{1}\right)
is a complete metric space, see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 5.4]
. Furthermore, by
[
5
, Theorem 5.10]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\left\{|\mu(f)-\nu(f)|:f\in\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\hbox{ with }{\rm Lip}(f)\leq 1\right\},

where
\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
is the set of Lipschitz continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
{\rm Lip}(f)
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
f\in\mathrm{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

In this section, we first study the existence and the uniqueness of an evolution system of measures for the extension of the time-dependent SDE (
1.21
) with partially dissipative drift
f(t,x,\cdot)
. Secondly, we establish uniform bounds for the moments of the solution (
X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
to the stochastic system (
1.6
). Finally, we construct an auxiliary process
(\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and show the difference between the processes
(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in the weak sense, which is crucial in the proofs of our main results.

2.1.
An evolution system of measures for SDEs with partially dissipative drift

In order to study the existence and the uniqueness of an evolution system of measures for the frozen SDE (
1.21
), we need to extend it to define for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
instead of
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. To do this, letting

(\tilde{W}^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}

be an independent copy of Brownian motion
(W^{2}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we define

{W}^{2}_{t}=\begin{cases}W^{2}_{t},\quad\,\,t\geq 0,\\
\tilde{W}^{2}_{-t},\quad t<0.\end{cases}

For the coefficient
f(t,x,y)
, we will use its symmetric extension, i.e., for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

f(t,x,y)=\begin{cases}f(t,x,y),\quad\,\,t\geq 0,\\
f(-t,x,y),\quad t<0.\end{cases}

In particular, the extended function
f
satisfies Assumption
2
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. It is important to note that the extended function
f

that meets Assumption
2
is not unique. Nevertheless, we here simply utilize the symmetric extension, as it is enough for our purpose.

Now, we consider the following time-inhomogeneous frozen SDE:

(2.1)

dY_{t}=f(t,x,Y_{t})dt+dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y_{s}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},s,t\in\mathbb{R}\hbox{ with }t\geq s.

Then, for any fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, any initial value
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the SDE
(
2.1
)
has a unique strong solution, denoted by
(Y^{s,x,y}_{t})_{t\geq s}
, which is a time-inhomogeneous Markov process. For any bounded measurable function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
the associated inhomogeneous Markov semigroup is given by

P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y):=\mathbb{E}\varphi(Y_{t}^{s,x,y}),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},s,t\in\mathbb{R}\hbox{ with }t\geq s.

We have
P^{x}_{s,t}=P^{x}_{s,r}\circ P^{x}_{r,t}
for
s\leq r\leq t
, and
\delta_{y}P^{x}_{s,t}
is the distribution of
Y^{s,x,y}_{t}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

For any
p\geq 4
, there exist constants
C_{1,p}
and
C_{2,p}>0
such that for all
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.2)

\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\leq e^{-C_{1,p}(t-s)}|y|^{p}+C_{2,p}(1+|x|^{p});

(2)

There exist constants
\beta
and
C>0
such that for any
t\geq s
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\delta_{y_{1}}P^{x_{1}}_{s,t},\delta_{y_{2}}P^{x_{2}}_{s,t})\leq Ce^{-\beta(t-s)}|y_{1}-y_{2}|+C|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Proof.

(1) It follows from (
2.1
) that for all
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{s,x,y}=y+\int_{s}^{t}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})dr+\int^{t}_{s}dW^{2}_{r}.

By applying the Itô formula and taking the expectation, we get that for any
p\geq 4
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}\right|^{p}=

\displaystyle|y|^{p}\!+\!p\mathbb{E}\int_{s}^{t}\!\left|Y_{r}^{s,x,y}\right|^{p-2}\left\langle Y_{r}^{s,x,y},f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s,x,y})\right\rangle dr\!+\!\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\!\mathbb{E}|Y^{s,x,y}_{r}|^{p-2}\,dr.

By
(
1.13
)
and Young’s inequality, for some
0<K_{3}<K_{1}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}=

\displaystyle p\mathbb{E}\left[|Y_{r}^{s,x,y}|^{p-2}\langle Y_{t}^{s,x,y},f(t,x,Y_{t}^{s,x,y})\rangle\right]+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p-2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-pK_{1}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}+C_{p}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p-2}(1+|x|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-pK_{3}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}+C_{p}(1+|x|^{p}).

The comparison theorem yields that for all
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{s,x,y}|^{p}\leq

\displaystyle e^{-pK_{3}(t-s)}|y|^{p}+C_{p}(1+|x|^{p})\int^{t}_{s}e^{-pK_{3}(t-u)}du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-pK_{3}(t-s)}|y|^{p}+C_{p}(1+|x|^{p}).

Hence, (
2.2
) is proved.

(2) We will make use of the so-called asymptotic reflection coupling; see
[
9
,
11
]
for related applications. Fix
t\geq s
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
(W_{t}^{2,1})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and
(W_{t}^{2,2})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be two independent
m
-dimensional Brownian motions. For any
\delta>0
, let
\pi_{\delta}\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};[0,1])
be such that
\pi_{\delta}(r)=1
for all
r\geq\delta
and
\pi_{\delta}(r)=0
for all
r\leq\delta/2
. Then, according to Lévy’s characterization of Brownian motion and the strong uniqueness of the frozen SDE (
2.1
), we can rewrite
(Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
solving the SDE (
2.1
) with
Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{s}=y_{1}
as follows (with the same distribution):

dY^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}\!=\!f(t,x_{1},Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t})dt\!+\!\sqrt{\pi_{\delta}(|Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}\!-\!Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t}|)}\,dW^{2,1}_{t}\!+\!\sqrt{1\!-\!\pi_{\delta}(|Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}\!-\!Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t}|)}\,dW^{2,2}_{t},

where
(Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
is a unique strong solution to the following SDE

\displaystyle dZ^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t}=

\displaystyle f(t,x_{2},Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})\,dt

\displaystyle+\!\sqrt{\pi_{\delta}(|Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}\!-\!Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t}|)}\left(dW^{2,1}_{t}\!-\!2\frac{(Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}\!-\!Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})\langle Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}\!-\!Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t},dW^{2,1}_{t}\rangle}{|Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}-Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t}|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{1-\pi_{\delta}(|Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t}-Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t}|)}\,dW^{2,2}_{t}

with
Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{s}=y_{2}
. In particular, under Assumption
2
, the SDE above has a unique strong solution
(Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t},Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
. Lévy’s characterization of Brownian motion also yields that
(Y^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
and
(Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
have the same distribution.
Let
\{\mathscr{L}^{x_{1},x_{2}}_{t}\}_{t\geq s}
be the infinitesimal generator of
(Y^{s,x_{1},y_{1}}_{t},Z^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
. Then, for any
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have the following straightforward formula (see e.g.,
[
25
, (3.15)]
)

\mathscr{L}^{x_{1},x_{2}}_{t}h(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)\!=\!2\pi_{\delta}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)h^{\prime\prime}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)\!+\!\frac{h^{\prime}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)}{|y_{1}-y_{2}|}\langle f(t,x_{1},y_{1})\!-\!f(t,x_{2},y_{2}),y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}\rangle.

This along with (
1.9
) gives us that for any
h\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
h^{\prime}\geq 0
and
h^{\prime\prime}\leq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{x_{1},x_{2}}_{t}h(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi_{\delta}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)h^{\prime\prime}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)\!+\!\left[(C\!+\!K)1_{\{|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|\leq r_{0}\}}\!-\!K\right]\!|y_{1}-y_{2}|h^{\prime}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)

\displaystyle+Ch^{\prime}(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Take
d(y_{1},y_{2})=h(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)
and
h(r)=1-e^{-c_{1}r}+c_{2}r
with
c_{1}=2Cr_{0}
and
c_{2}=c_{1}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}.
One has
c_{2}r\leq h(r)\leq(c_{1}+c_{2})r
for all
r\geq 0
. Thus, for
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq r_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{x_{1},x_{2}}_{t}d(y_{1},y_{2})\!\leq

\displaystyle\pi_{\delta}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)\left[-2c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|}\!+\!C\left(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|\!+\!|x_{1}\!-\!x_{2}|\right)(c_{1}e^{-c_{1}|y_{1}-y_{2}|}\!+\!c_{2})\right]

\displaystyle+C(1-\pi_{\delta}(|y_{1}-y_{2}|))\left(|y_{1}-y_{2}|+|x_{1}-x_{2}|\right)\left(c_{1}e^{-c_{1}|y_{1}-y_{2}|}+c_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\pi_{\delta}(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)\left(-c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}+4r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\right)+4r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|+4r_{0}C^{2}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[-c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\!\!\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big]h(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)\pi_{\delta}(|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|)\!+\!8r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}\!-\!x_{2}|\!+\!4r_{0}C^{2}\delta

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[-c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\!\!\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big]d(y_{1},y_{2})+8r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle+4r_{0}C^{2}\delta+\Big[c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big]h(\delta),

where in the first inequality we used the definitions of
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, and in the second and the last inequalities we used the fact that
1-\pi_{\delta}(r)\neq 0
if only
r\in[0,\delta]
.
On the other hand, for
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
|y_{1}-y_{2}|>r_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{x_{1},x_{2}}_{t}d(y_{1},y_{2})\leq

\displaystyle-c_{2}K|y_{1}-y_{2}|+4r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[-c_{2}K\inf_{r\geq r_{0}}(r/h(r))\Big]h(|y_{1}-y_{2}|)+4r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[-c_{2}K\inf_{r\geq r_{0}}(r/h(r))\Big]d(y_{1},y_{2})+4r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Put

\beta:=\Big(c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big)\wedge\Big(c_{2}K\inf_{r\geq r_{0}}(r/h(r))\Big).

We have

\mathscr{L}^{x_{1},x_{2}}_{t}d(y_{1},y_{2})\leq-\beta d(y_{1},y_{2})+8r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|+4r_{0}C^{2}\delta+\Big[c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big]h(\delta).

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathbb{E}d(Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}},Z_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}})\leq

\displaystyle-\beta\mathbb{E}d(Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}},Z_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}})+8r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|

\displaystyle+4r_{0}C^{2}\delta+\Big[c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big]h(\delta).

The comparison theorem yields that for all
t\geq s
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}d(Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}},Z_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}})\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\beta(t-s)}d(y_{1},y_{2})+8r_{0}C^{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\int^{t}_{s}e^{-\beta(t-u)}du

\displaystyle+\Big[4r_{0}C^{2}\delta+\Big(c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big)h(\delta)\Big]\int^{t}_{s}e^{-\beta(t-u)}du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-\beta(t-s)}d(y_{1},y_{2})+8|x_{1}-x_{2}|r_{0}C^{2}/\beta

\displaystyle+\Big[4r_{0}C^{2}\delta+\Big(c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big)h(\delta)\Big]\Big/\beta.

Finally, noting that
c_{2}|y_{1}-y_{2}|\leq d(y_{1},y_{2})\leq(c_{1}+c_{2})|y_{1}-y_{2}|
for all
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we get that for all
t\geq s
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\left(\delta_{y_{1}}P^{x_{1}}_{s,t},\delta_{y_{2}}P^{x_{2}}_{s,t}\right)\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}\mathbb{W}_{d}\left(\delta_{y_{1}}P^{x_{1}}_{s,t},\delta_{y_{2}}P^{x_{2}}_{s,t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}\mathbb{E}d(Y_{t}^{s,x_{1},y_{1}},Z_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}^{-1}(c_{1}+c_{2})e^{-\beta(t-s)}|y_{1}-y_{2}|+8c_{2}^{-1}|x_{1}-x_{2}|r_{0}C^{2}/\beta

\displaystyle+c_{2}^{-1}\Big[4r_{0}C^{2}\delta+\Big(c_{1}^{2}e^{-c_{1}r_{0}}/\sup_{0<r\leq r_{0}}h(r)\Big)h(\delta)\Big]\Big/\beta,

where in the second inequality we used the fact that the distribution of
Z_{t}^{s,x_{2},y_{2}}
is
\delta_{y_{2}}P^{x_{2}}_{s,t}
.
The proof of (
2.3
) is complete by letting

in the estimate above.

Remark 2.2

\displaystyle dY_{t}=f(t,U_{t},Y_{t})\,dt+dW^{2}_{t},\quad Y_{s}=V\in\mathbb{R}^{m},t\geq s,

where
(U_{t})_{t\geq s}
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued stochastic process, and
V
is an
\mathbb{R}^{m}

2
holds. Denote
(Y^{s,U,V}_{t})_{t\geq s}
by the unique strong solution to the SDE
(
2.4
)
. Following the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we can obtain that there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\geq s
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued stochastic processes
(U_{1}(t))_{t\geq s}
and
(U_{2}(t))_{t\geq s}
, and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued random variables

and

,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\big(\mathscr{L}_{Y^{s,U_{1},V_{1}}_{t}},\mathscr{L}_{Y^{s,U_{2},V_{2}}_{t}}\big)\leq Ce^{-\beta(t-s)}\mathbb{E}|V_{1}-V_{2}|+C\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}\mathbb{E}|U_{1}(u)-U_{2}(u)|.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds. Then, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there exists
\mu^{x}_{t}\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that for any
s\leq t
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\delta_{y}P^{x}_{s,t},\mu^{x}_{t})\leq C_{1}\left(1+|x|+|y|\right)e^{-\beta(t-s)},\quad\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y|\mu^{x}_{t}(dy)\leq C_{1}(1+|x|),

where
\beta>0
is defined in Lemma
2.1
, and
C_{1}>0
is independent of
t
,
s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Moreover, there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that for any

,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{x_{1}}_{t},\mu^{x_{2}}_{t})\leq C_{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Proof.

(i) Fix
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For any
h>0
,

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{s-h,x,y}=Y_{s}^{s-h,x,y}+\int_{s}^{t}f(r,x,Y_{r}^{s-h,x,y})\,dr+\int_{s}^{t}dW^{2}_{r},

which implies that
Y_{t}^{s-h,x,y}

enjoy the same distribution. By
(
2.5
)
and
(
2.2
)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\left(\delta_{y}P^{x}_{s,t},\delta_{y}P^{x}_{s-h,t}\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\big(\mathscr{L}_{Y^{s,x,y}_{t}},\mathscr{L}_{Y^{s,x,Y_{s}^{s-h,x,y}}_{t}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-\beta(t-s)}\mathbb{E}|y-Y_{s}^{s-h,x,y}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-\beta(t-s)}(1+|x|+|y|).

As a consequence, for fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\{\mathscr{L}_{Y^{s,x,y}_{t}}\}_{s\leq t}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
as
s\to-\infty
, also thanks to (
2.2
). Then, there exists
\mu^{x,y}_{t}\in\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that for all
t\geq s
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}\left(\delta_{y}P^{x}_{s,t},\mu^{x,y}_{t}\right)\leq Ce^{-\beta(t-s)}(1+|x|+|y|).

Next, we are going to prove that
\mu^{x,y}_{t}
is independent of
y
. In fact,
(
2.3
)
implies that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\lim_{s\to-\infty}\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\delta_{y_{1}}\!P^{x}_{s,t},\delta_{y_{2}}\!P^{x}_{s,t}\right)=0.

On the other hand, it holds that

\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\mu^{x,y_{1}}_{t},\mu^{x,y_{2}}_{t}\right)\leq\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\mu^{x,y_{1}}_{t},\delta_{y_{1}}\!P^{x}_{s,t}\right)+\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\mu^{x,y_{2}}_{t},\delta_{y_{2}}\!P^{x}_{s,t}\right)+\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\delta_{y_{1}}\!P^{x}_{s,t},\delta_{y_{2}}\!P^{x}_{s,t}\right).

Taking

and applying (

), we get
\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\mu^{x,y_{1}}_{t},\mu^{x,y_{2}}_{t}\right)=0
, and so
\mu^{x,y_{1}}_{t}=\mu^{x,y_{2}}_{t}
in
\mathscr{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Below, we denote
\mu_{t}^{x,y}
by
\mu_{t}^{x}
. Furthermore, it follows from (

) and (

) that for all

,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y|\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dy)\leq C(1+|x|).

Hence
(
2.6
)
holds, thanks to (
2.8
) again.

(ii) By
(
2.3
)
and
(
2.8
)
, we have for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{x_{1}}_{t},\mu^{x_{2}}_{t})\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{x_{1}}_{t},\delta_{\textbf{0}}P^{x_{1}}_{s,t})+\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{x_{2}}_{t},\delta_{\textbf{0}}P^{x_{2}}_{s,t})+\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\left(\delta_{\textbf{0}}P^{x_{1}}_{s,t},\delta_{\textbf{0}}P^{x_{2}}_{s,t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ce^{-\beta(t-s)}(1+|x_{1}|+|x_{2}|)+C|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Taking

(
2.7
)

∎

Next, we recall the following definition, see e.g.
[
7
,
8
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A class of measures
\{\nu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is called an evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
, if

(2.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{t}(dy),\quad s\leq t,\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

We have the following statement.

Proposition 2.5
.

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds. Then the class of measures
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
given in Lemma
2.3
is an evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
. Moreover, for any Lipschitz continuous function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
t\geq s
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\left|P^{x}_{s,t}\phi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(z)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right|\leq C{\rm Lip(\phi)}(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\beta(t-s)}.

Furthermore, if
\{\nu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is another evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
that satisfies

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y|\nu^{x}_{t}(dy)<\infty,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

then
\nu^{x}_{t}=\mu^{x}_{t}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially taken from that of
[
7
, Propositions 2.3 and 2.5]
, see also the proof of
[
29
, Proposition 2.6]
. We present the proof here for the sake of the completeness.

(i) According to (
2.6
), for any
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

By (
2.3
),
P^{x}_{t,u}\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Note that

P^{x}_{s,u}P^{x}_{u,t}\varphi(y)=P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y),\quad s\leq u\leq t.

Then, letting
s\to-\infty
in both sides of the equality above, we obtain from
(
2.11
)
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{u,t}\varphi(y)\mu^{x}_{u}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\mu^{x}_{t}(dy).

Therefore,

\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
given in Lemma
2.3
is an evolution system of measures for
\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
.

For any Lipschitz continuous function
\phi
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, by
(
2.6
)
again,

\displaystyle\left|P^{x}_{s,t}\phi(y)\!-\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(z)\,\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right|\leq{\rm Lip}(\phi)\mathbb{W}_{1}(\delta_{y}P^{x}_{s,t},\mu^{x}_{t})\leq C{\rm Lip}(\phi)(1\!+\!|x|\!+\!|y|)e^{-\beta(t-s)}.

Hence,
(
2.10
)
holds.

(ii) Let
\{\nu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
be another evolution system of measures for

\{P^{x}_{s,t}\}_{t\geq s}
. Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{t}(dy),\quad\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m}).

In order to prove the uniqueness, it is sufficient to verify

(2.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y)\mu^{x}_{t}(dx).

Then it follows that
\mu^{x}_{t}=\nu^{x}_{t}
by the arbitrariness of
\varphi
. In fact, note that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left[P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(z)\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right]\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(z)\mu^{x}_{t}(dz).

On the other hand, according to
(
2.10
)
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left[P^{x}_{s,t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(z)\mu^{x}_{t}(dz)\right]\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)\right|\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(1+|x|+|y|)\nu^{x}_{s}(dy)e^{-\beta(t-s)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1+|x|)e^{-\beta(t-s)}.

Hence, letting
s\to-\infty
, we get that
(
2.12
)
holds. The proof is complete.
∎

2.2.
A priori estimates of
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}

Let
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
be the unique strong solution to the SDE
(
1.6
)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. For any
p\geq 4
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{p,T}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)\leq C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}+|y|^{p})

and

(2.14)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\leq C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}).

Moreover, for any
h>0
with
0\leq t<t+h\leq T
,

(2.15)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+h}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\right)\leq C_{T}\left(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4}\right)h^{2}.

Proof.

(i) Note that

\displaystyle Y^{\varepsilon}_{t\varepsilon}=y+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int^{t\varepsilon}_{0}f(r/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{r},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r})dr+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int^{t\varepsilon}_{0}dW^{2}_{r}=y+\int^{t}_{0}f(r,X^{\varepsilon}_{r\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r\varepsilon})dr+\int^{t}_{0}d\hat{W}^{2}_{r},

where
\hat{W}^{2}_{t}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}W^{2}_{t\varepsilon}
. Denote
\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}:=X^{\varepsilon}_{t\varepsilon}
. Then, by the construction of
(Y^{0,\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon},y}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and the weak uniqueness of the SDE
(
2.4
)
, it is easy to see that
(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
and
(Y^{0,\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon},y}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
have the same law. As a consequence,
(Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has the same law as
(Y^{0,\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon},y}_{t/\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
.
Then, following the proof of
(
2.2
)
, we have for any
p\geq 4
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\!=\!\mathbb{E}|Y^{0,\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon},y}_{t/\varepsilon}|^{p}\!\leq\!C\left(1\!+\!\sup_{s\in[0,t/\varepsilon]}\mathbb{E}|\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{p}\!+\!|y|^{p}\right)\!\leq\!C\left(1\!+\!\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{p}\!+\!|y|^{p}\right).

Using Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality,
(
1.8
)
and
(
2.16
)
, we get for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\right)\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p})+C_{p,T}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{p}ds+C_{p,T}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}|Y^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}+|y|^{p})+C_{p,T}\int^{t}_{0}\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,s]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{r}|^{p}\right)ds,

which along with Gronwall’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\right)\leq C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}),\quad t\in[0,T].

Thus,
(
2.13
)
holds. Combining this with (
2.16
), we verify
(
2.14
)
.

(ii) For any
h>0
with
0\leq t<t+h\leq T
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+h}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\!-\!X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\right)\leq

\displaystyle C\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+h}\!\Big|\int_{t}^{s}b(r,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r})dr\Big|^{4}\Big]\!+\!C\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\leq s\leq t+h}\Big|\!\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(r,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r})dW^{1}_{r}\Big|^{4}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{4}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|b(r,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r})|^{4}+C\mathbb{E}\Big(\int_{t}^{t+h}\|\sigma(r,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon}_{r})\|^{2}dr\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}h^{4}\big(1+\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{r}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}+\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|Y_{r}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\big)+C_{T}h^{2}\big(1+\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{r}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\left(1+|x|^{4}+|y|^{4}\right)h^{2},

where in the third inequality we used (
1.8
). The proof of
(
2.15
)
is complete.
∎

2.3.
An auxiliary process of the fast component

Inspired by the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 1]
,
we divide
[0,T)
into intervals with size
\delta
, where
\delta
depends on
\varepsilon
and will be fixed later. Fix each time interval
[k\delta,(k+1)\delta\wedge T)
, where
k=0,1,\ldots,[T/\delta]
, and
[T/\delta]
is the integer part of
T/\delta
. We construct an auxiliary process
\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}=Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{k\delta}^{t}f(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})ds+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int_{k\delta}^{t}dW^{2}_{s},\quad t\in[k\delta,[(k+1)\delta]\wedge T).

The construction of
(\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
and the proof of Lemma
2.6
yield the following estimate.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose that Assumption
2
holds.
For any
p\geq 4
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{p,T}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{p}\leq C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}).

Next, we turn to evaluate the difference between
(Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
. We shall mention that the partially dissipative condition of the coefficient
f(t,x,y)
prevents us from demonstrating an estimate like
\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}
as done in the uniformly dissipative setting; see e.g.
[
20
, Lemma 3.4]
. The following statement plays an important role in the proofs of our main results.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Let
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy that for any
t\geq 0
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

|F(t,x_{1},y_{1})-F(t,x_{2},y_{2})|\leq C(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|y_{1}-y_{2}|).

Then, for any
T>0
, there is a constant
C_{T}>0
such that for any
0\leq t_{0}<t\leq T
and
Z_{t_{0}}\in\mathscr{F}_{t_{0}}
with
\mathbb{E}|Z_{t_{0}}|^{2}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]\right|\leq C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\left(\mathbb{E}|Z_{t_{0}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\delta^{1/2}.

Proof.

For any
\varepsilon,s>0
,
\mathscr{F}_{s}
-measurable
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued random variable
X
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued random variable
Y
, consider the following SDE

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,s,X,Y}_{t}=Y+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int^{t}_{s}f(r/\varepsilon,X,\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,s,X,Y}_{r})dr+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int^{t}_{s}dW^{2}_{r},\quad t\geq s.

Then, for
k=0,1,\ldots,[T/\delta]
and
s\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]
,

\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}=\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{s}.

On the other hand, let
(X^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t})_{t\geq s}
be the solution of the SDE

(2.17)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t})dW^{1}_{t},\\
\displaystyle dY^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t}=\varepsilon^{-1}f(t/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{t})dt+\varepsilon^{-1/2}dW^{2}_{t}\end{array}\right.

with
X^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{s}=X^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}_{s}
and
Y^{\varepsilon,s,X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}}_{s}=Y^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}_{s}
. Then, for
k=0,1,\ldots,[T/\delta]
and
s\geq k\delta
,

(2.18)

X_{s}^{\varepsilon}=X^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{s},\quad Y_{s}^{\varepsilon}=Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{s}.

Note that
X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}
and
Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}
are
\mathscr{F}_{k\delta}
-measurable, and that
(\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s})_{s\geq k\delta}
and
(Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s})_{s\geq k\delta}
are independent of
\mathscr{F}_{k\delta}
for any
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\int^{[((k+1)\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}_{[(k\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}\right]ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\int^{[((k+1)\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}_{[(k\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}\mathbb{E}\Big[Z_{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s})\right)\Big|_{x^{\prime}=X^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta},y^{\prime}=Y^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta}}\Big]ds,

where
s(\delta)=[{s}/{\delta}]\delta
.

Furthermore, according to
(
2.17
)
,

(2.19)

\begin{split}Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{\varepsilon s}=&y^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int^{\varepsilon s}_{k\delta}f(r/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r},Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r})dr+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\int^{\varepsilon s}_{k\delta}dW^{2}_{r}\\
=&y^{\prime}+\int^{s}_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}}f(r,X^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r\varepsilon},Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r\varepsilon})dr+\int^{s}_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}}d\hat{W}^{2}_{r}.\end{split}

Denote
\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r}:=X^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r\varepsilon}
. Recall that
(Y_{s}^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}})_{s\geq k\delta/\varepsilon}
is the unique solution to the SDE (
2.4
); that is,

(2.20)

\displaystyle Y_{s}^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle y^{\prime}+\int_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}}^{s}f(r,\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r},Y_{r}^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}})dr+\int_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}}^{s}d\hat{W}^{2}_{r}.

The uniqueness of the weak solutions to the SDEs (
2.19
) and (
2.20
) implies
that the distribution of
\big(Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s\varepsilon}\big)_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{(k+1)\delta}{\varepsilon}}

coincides with that of
\big(Y_{s}^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}}\big)_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{(k+1)\delta}{\varepsilon}}
. Similarly, the distribution of
\big(\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s\varepsilon}\big)_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{(k+1)\delta}{\varepsilon}}

coincides with that of
\big(Y_{s}^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\big)_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{(k+1)\delta}{\varepsilon}}
.
As a consequence, by the Lipschitz continuity of
F(x^{\prime},\cdot)
and
(
2.5
)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}F(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},Y^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s})-\mathbb{E}F(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s})\right|

\displaystyle=\Big|\mathbb{E}F(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},Y^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}}_{s/\varepsilon})-\mathbb{E}F(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},Y^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s/\varepsilon})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{W}_{1}\!\Big(\mathscr{L}_{Y^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}},y^{\prime}}_{s/\varepsilon}},\mathscr{L}_{Y^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s/\varepsilon}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{k\delta\leq r\leq s}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r}-x^{\prime}|.

Hence, according to
(
2.15
)
, we get

(2.21)

\begin{split}&\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]\right|\\
&\leq C_{T}\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\int^{[((k+1)\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}_{[(k\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}\left|\mathbb{E}\Big[Z_{t_{0}}\sup_{k\delta\leq r\leq s}|X^{\varepsilon}_{r}-X^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta}|\Big]\right|ds\\
&\leq C_{T}\sum^{[T/\delta]-1}_{k=0}\int^{[((k+1)\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}_{[(k\delta)\vee t_{0}]\wedge t}\left(\mathbb{E}|Z_{t_{0}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{k\delta\leq r\leq s}|X^{\varepsilon}_{r}-X^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta}|^{2}\right)\right]^{1/2}ds\\
&\leq C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\left(\mathbb{E}|Z_{t_{0}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\delta^{1/2},\end{split}

where in the first inequality we used (
2.18
), and the second inequality follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.
Therefore, by
(
2.21
)
and
(
2.15
)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\left.\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-F(s/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s})\right)Z_{t_{0}}ds\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left(|X^{\varepsilon}_{s}-X^{\varepsilon}_{s(\delta)}||Z_{t_{0}}|\right)ds+C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\left(\mathbb{E}|Z_{t_{0}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\delta^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\left(\mathbb{E}|Z_{t_{0}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\delta^{1/2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Proof of the strong averaging principle

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. As mentioned in Section
1
, we will make use of the averaged SDE (
1.22
),
where the drift term involves an evolution system of measures
\{\mu^{x}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
that was established in Proposition
2.5
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
2
hold.
For any
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the SDE (
1.22
) has a unique strong solution
(\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Moreover, for any
p\geq 4
and
T>0
, there exists a constant
C_{p,T}>0
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\mathbb{E}\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{p}\Big)\leq C_{p,T}(1+|x|^{p}).

Proof.

According to Assumption
1
, it is sufficient to prove that there is a constant
C>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})-\bar{b}(t,x_{2})|\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|,\quad\quad|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})|\leq C(1+|x_{1}|).\end{split}

Then the SDE (
1.22
) admits a unique strong solution, and
(
3.1
)
holds obviously.

By (
1.7
) and (
2.7
), for all
t\geq 0
and
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})-\bar{b}(t,x_{2})|=

\displaystyle\left|\int b(t,x_{1},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)-\int b(t,x_{2},y)\mu^{x_{2}}_{t}(dy)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int b(t,x_{1},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)-\int b(t,x_{2},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int b(t,x_{2},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)-\int b(t,x_{2},y)\mu^{x_{2}}_{t}(dy)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|x_{1}-x_{2}|.

Meanwhile, using (
1.8
) and (
2.6
), we get that for all
t\geq 0
and
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle|\bar{b}(t,x_{1})|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(t,x_{1},y)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)\right|\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(1+|x_{1}|+|y|)\mu^{x_{1}}_{t}(dy)\leq C(1+|x_{1}|).

The proof is complete. ∎

The following result is a crucial link in establishing the proof of the strong averaging principle.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
\sigma(t,x,y)=\sigma(t,x)
and Assumptions
1
and
2
hold.
Let
(X_{t}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\bar{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
be the strong solution to the SDEs (
1.6
) and (
1.22
), respectively. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
,
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
T>0
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y^{2}|)\varepsilon^{1/3}.

Proof.

Since
\sigma(t,x,y)=\sigma(t,x)
, for any
s\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r})\right]dr+\int_{0}^{s}\left[\sigma(r/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{r})-\sigma(r/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r})\right]dW^{1}_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\!\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr\!+\!\int_{0}^{s}\!\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr+\int_{0}^{s}\left[\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}\left[\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r})\right]dr+\int_{0}^{s}\left[\sigma(r/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{r})-\sigma(r/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r})\right]dW^{1}_{r}.

Thus, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon})-b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left|b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})-b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}dr

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\mathbb{E}\Big[\left|\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right|^{2}\!\!+\!\left|\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r})\right|^{2}\!\!+\!\left\|\sigma(r/\varepsilon,X^{\varepsilon}_{r})\!-\!\sigma(r/\varepsilon,\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r})\right\|^{2}\!\Big]dr.

Then, by (
1.7
), (
3.2
) and (
2.15
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y^{2}|)\delta+C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|X^{\varepsilon}_{r}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{r}|^{2}dr

\displaystyle+C_{T}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon})-b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{T}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{s}\left[b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{r(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})\right]dr\right|^{2}.

This along with Gronwall’s inequality yields that

(3.4)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\\
&\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y^{2}|)\delta+C_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\left[b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})-b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds\Big|^{2}\\
&\quad+C_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}\left[b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X_{s(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds\Big|^{2}\\
&=:C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y^{2}|)\delta+I_{1}(T)+I_{2}(T).\end{split}

According to Lemma
2.8
, (
1.8
), Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
, we have

(3.5)

\begin{split}&I_{1}(T)\\
&\leq C_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\int_{r}^{t}\!\Big\langle b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}),b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\Big\rangle dsdr\\
&\leq C_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\left|\mathbb{E}\!\int_{r}^{t}\Big\langle b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!b(s/\varepsilon,X_{s}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon}),b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon})\!-\!b(r/\varepsilon,X_{r}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})\Big\rangle ds\right|dr\\
&\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\delta^{1/2}.\end{split}

Next, we will estimate
I_{2}(T)
. It follows from (
1.8
), (
3.2
), Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
that

\displaystyle I_{2}(T)\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{[t/\delta]-1}\!\int_{k\delta}^{(k+1)\delta}\left[b(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle+C_{T}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\Big|\int_{[t/\delta]\delta}^{t}\left[b(s/\varepsilon,X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X_{t(\delta)}^{\varepsilon})\right]ds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}[T/\delta]\sum_{k=0}^{[T/\delta]-1}\!\int_{k\delta}^{(k+1)\delta}\!\int_{r}^{(k+1)\delta}\Psi_{k}(s,r)dsdr+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\delta^{2},

where in the last inequality we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(s,r):=\mathbb{E}\left[\langle b(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}),b(r/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon})-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon})\rangle\right]

for
k\delta\leq r\leq s\leq(k+1)\delta
. As pointed out in the proof of Lemma
2.8
, we know that
for
k=0,1,\ldots,[T/\delta]
and
t\in[k\delta,(k+1)\delta]
,

\hat{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{t},

which implies that for all
k\delta\leq r\leq s\leq(k+1)\delta
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(s,r)=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Big\langle b\big(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{s}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}),b(r/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{r})\!-\!\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon})\Big\rangle\right].

Note that
X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}
and
Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}
are
\mathscr{F}_{k\delta}
-measurable, and that, for any
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\{\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s}\}_{s\geq k\delta}
is independent of
\mathscr{F}_{k\delta}
. Then, for all
k\delta\leq r\leq s\leq(k+1)\delta
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(s,r)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left\{\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Big\langle b\big(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{s}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}),b\big(r/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}_{r}\big)-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon})\Big\rangle|\mathscr{F}_{k\delta}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big\langle b\big(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime}),b\big(r/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r}\big)-\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,x^{\prime})\Big\rangle\Big]\Big|_{x^{\prime}=X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},y^{\prime}=Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\Big\}.

Furthermore, the proof of Lemma
2.8
implies the distribution of
\big(\tilde{Y}^{\varepsilon,k\delta,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s\varepsilon}\big)_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{(k+1)\delta}{\varepsilon}}

coincides with that of

\big(Y_{s}^{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon},x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}\big)_{\frac{k\delta}{\varepsilon}\leq s\leq\frac{(k+1)\delta}{\varepsilon}}
. Thus,
(
2.10
)
, (
3.2
) and Lemma
2.6
yield that for all
k\delta\leq r\leq s\leq(k+1)\delta
,

\displaystyle\Psi_{k}(s,r)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\!\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big\langle b\big(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},Y^{k\delta/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{s/\varepsilon}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,x^{\prime}),b\big(r/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},Y^{k\delta/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r/\varepsilon}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,x^{\prime})\Big\rangle\Big]\Big|_{x^{\prime}=X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},y^{\prime}=Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\!\Big[\mathbb{E}\Big\langle\!\Big(\mathbb{E}b\big(s/\varepsilon,\!x^{\prime}\!,\!Y^{r/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},z}_{s/\varepsilon}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon,\!x^{\prime})\big)\Big|_{z=Y^{k\delta/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r/\varepsilon}},\!b\big(r/\varepsilon,\!x^{\prime}\!,\!Y^{k\delta/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r/\varepsilon}\big)\!-\!\bar{b}(r/\varepsilon,\!x^{\prime})\Big\rangle\Big]\Big|_{x^{\prime}=X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},y^{\prime}=Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\left(1+|x^{\prime}|^{2}+|Y^{k\delta/\varepsilon,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}}_{r/\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)\Big|_{x^{\prime}=X_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon},y^{\prime}=Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}}\Big\}e^{-\frac{\beta(s-r)}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\mathbb{E}\left(1+|X^{\varepsilon}_{k\delta}|^{2}+|Y_{k\delta}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)e^{-\frac{\beta(s-r)}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})e^{-\frac{\beta(s-r)}{\varepsilon}}.

Hence,

(3.6)

\begin{split}I_{2}(T)\leq&C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})[T/\delta]\sum_{k=0}^{[T/\delta]-1}\!\int_{k\delta}^{(k+1)\delta}\!\int_{r}^{(k+1)\delta}\!e^{-\frac{\beta(s-r)}{\varepsilon}}dsdr+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\delta^{2}\\
\leq&C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\varepsilon/\delta+C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\delta^{2}.\end{split}

Putting
(
3.4
)
,
(
3.5
)
and
(
3.6
)
together, we finally obtain that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y^{2}|)\left(\delta^{1/2}+\varepsilon/\delta\right).

Taking
\delta=\varepsilon^{2/3}
, we find that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y^{2}|)\varepsilon^{1/3}.

The proof is complete. ∎

\displaystyle\big\langle\bar{f}(x_{1},y_{1})\!-\!\bar{f}(x_{2},y_{2}),y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}\big\rangle\!\leq\!\begin{cases}C|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|^{2}\!+\!C|x_{1}\!-\!x_{2}||y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|,\!&|y_{1}\!-\!y_{2}|\leq r_{0},\\

\mathbb{W}_{1}(\delta_{y}\bar{P}^{x}_{t},\mu^{x})\leq C(1+|x|+|y|)e^{-\beta t},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y|\mu^{x}(dy)\leq C(1+|x|),

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{x_{1}},\mu^{x_{2}})\leq C|x_{1}-x_{2}|,

To obtain all the assertions above, one can follow the proofs of Lemmas
2.1
and

. We omit the details here.

Lemma 3.3

.

Fix
T>0
. According to Proposition
4.1
, it is sufficient to prove that, for any sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
so that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence (for simplicity we still denote by
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
) such that
X^{\varepsilon_{k}}
converges weakly to
X:=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which is the unique strong solution to the SDE
(
1.20
)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
X^{\varepsilon_{k}}
converges almost surely to
X
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
by the Skorohod representation theorem.

In the following, we shall apply the martingale problem approach to characterize the limit process
X
.
Let
\Psi_{t_{0}}(\cdot)
be a bounded, continuous and
\sigma\{\varphi_{s}:\varphi\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}),s\leq t_{0}\}
-measurable function on
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It suffices to prove that for any
U\in C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the following assertion holds:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(U(X_{t})-U(X_{t_{0}})-\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\bar{\mathcal{L}}U(X_{s})ds\right)\Psi_{t_{0}}(X)\right]=0,\quad t_{0}\leq t\leq T,

where
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is the generator of the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
to the SDE
(
1.20
)
, i.e.,

\bar{\mathcal{L}}U(x):=\Big\langle\nabla U(x),\bar{b}(x)\Big\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\left[\bar{\Sigma}(x)\nabla^{2}U(x)\right].

Applying Itô’s formula, we have

\displaystyle U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{t})=

\displaystyle U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{t_{0}})\!+\!\!\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\!\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),b(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\rangle ds\!+\!\!\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\!\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),\sigma(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})dW_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\text{Tr}\left[(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\nabla^{2}U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\right]ds.

Note that, by the Markov property and the martingale property,

\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),\sigma(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})dW_{s}\rangle\right]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\right\}=0.

Since
X^{\varepsilon_{k}}
converges almost surely to
X
in
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{t})-U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{t_{0}})\right]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\right\}=\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[U(X_{t})-U(X_{t_{0}})\right]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X)\right\},

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\rangle ds\Big]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\Big\}=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\langle\nabla U(X_{s}),\bar{b}(X_{s})\rangle ds\Big]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X)\Big\},

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\!\text{Tr}\left(\bar{\Sigma}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\nabla^{2}U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\right)ds\Big]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\Big\}=\mathbb{E}\Big\{\Big[\!\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\!\text{Tr}\left(\bar{\Sigma}(X_{s})\nabla^{2}U(X_{s})\right)ds\Big]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X)\Big\}.

Hence, in order to obtain
(
4.8
)
, we only need to verify that for all
t_{0}\leq t\leq T
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),b(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})-\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\rangle ds\right]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\right\}=0,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\text{Tr}\left[\left((\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})-\bar{\Sigma}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\right)\nabla^{2}U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\right]ds\right]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\right\}=0.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}\left\{\left[\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Big\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),b(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})-\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big\rangle ds\right]\Psi_{t_{0}}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}})\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(\Big\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),b(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big\rangle-\Big\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big\rangle\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle\quad+C\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\left(\Big\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),\bar{b}(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big\rangle-\Big\langle\nabla U(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}),\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big\rangle\right)ds\right|

\displaystyle=:CQ^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{1}+CQ^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{2}.

Set

B(t,x,y):=\Big\langle\nabla U(x),b(t,x,y)-\bar{b}(t,x)\Big\rangle.

Then, for all
t\geq 0
,
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|B(t,x_{1},y_{1})-B(t,x_{2},y_{2})|\leq C(1+|x_{1}|+|y_{1}|)|x_{1}-x_{2}|+C|y_{1}-y_{2}|,\\
&|B(t,x_{1},y_{1})|\leq C(1+|x_{1}|+|y_{1}|).\end{split}

We have

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\Big[B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})-B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big]ds\right|

\displaystyle+\!\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\!\Big[B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\!-\!B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})\Big]ds\right|\!+\!\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\!\!B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})ds\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{11}+Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{12}+Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{13}.

By (
4.11
) and the same argument used in the proof of Lemma
2.8
, we have

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{11}\leq

\displaystyle\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})-B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})ds\right|^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big[\mathbb{E}\!\!\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\!\!\int_{r}^{t}\!\!\Big\langle B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X_{s}^{\varepsilon_{k}},Y_{s}^{\varepsilon_{k}})\!-\!B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X_{s}^{\varepsilon_{k}},\hat{Y}_{s}^{\varepsilon_{k}}),\!B(r/\varepsilon_{k},X_{r}^{\varepsilon_{k}},Y_{r}^{\varepsilon_{k}})\!-\!B(r/\varepsilon_{k},X_{r}^{\varepsilon_{k}},\hat{Y}_{r}^{\varepsilon_{k}})\Big\rangle dsdr\Big]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}(1+|x|^{3/2}+|y|^{3/2})\delta^{1/4}.

According to (
4.11
),
(
2.15
)
, (
2.13
) and Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{12}\leq\int^{t}_{t_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[(1+|X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}|+|\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}|)|X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s}-X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s(\delta)}|\right]ds\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\delta^{1/2}.

Following the argument for (
3.6
) in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{13}\leq\left[\mathbb{E}\left|\int^{t}_{t_{0}}B(s/\varepsilon_{k},X^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s(\delta)},\hat{Y}^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{s})ds\right|^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\left[\left(\varepsilon_{k}/\delta\right)^{1/2}+\delta\right].

Therefore, putting all the estimates together, we obtain that

(4.12)

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{1}\leq C_{T}(1+|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})\left[\left(\varepsilon_{k}/\delta\right)^{1/2}+\delta^{1/4}\right].

For
Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{2}
, by
(
3.19
)
and the arguments for (
3
) in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we can easily obtain

(4.13)

\displaystyle Q^{\varepsilon_{k}}_{2}\leq C_{T}(1+|x|+|y|)\left[\phi_{1}(\delta/\varepsilon_{k})+\phi_{5}(\delta/\varepsilon_{k})+\delta\right].

Hence, combining
(
4.12
)
with
(
4.13
)
, and taking
\delta=\varepsilon_{k}^{1/2}
, we obtain that
(
4.9
)
holds.

Thirdly, according to Assumption
5
and Lemma
3.3
, for all
t\geq 0
,
T>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\left\|\int^{t+T}_{t}\left[\overline{\sigma\sigma^{\ast}}(s,x)-\bar{\Sigma}(x)\right]ds\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\left\|\int^{t+T}_{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Sigma(x,y)\mu^{x}(dy)\right]ds\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\left\|\int^{t+T}_{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)\mu^{x}(dy)\right]ds\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{T}\left\|\int^{t+T}_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left[(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)-\Sigma(x,y)\right]\mu^{x}(dy)ds\right\|ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\left\|\int^{t+T}_{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)\mu^{x}_{s}(dy)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)\mu^{x}(dy)\right]ds\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{1}{T}\left\|\int^{t+T}_{t}\left[(\sigma\sigma^{\ast})(s,x,y)-\Sigma(x,y)\right]ds\right\|\mu^{x}(dy)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|)\frac{1}{T}\int^{t+T}_{t}\!\!\left(e^{-\beta s}+\int^{s}_{0}e^{-\beta(s-u)}\phi_{2}(u)\,du\right)ds\!+\!C\phi_{4}(T)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(1+|x|^{2})\mu^{x}(dy)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{2})[\phi_{4}(T)+\tilde{\phi}_{2}(T)],

where
\tilde{\phi}_{2}(T)
is defined by
(
3.20
)
. With the aid of this, one can follow the proof of
(
4.9
)
to obtain that
(
4.10
)
also holds.

According to Lemma
3.4
, the averaged SDE
(
1.20
)
has a unique strong solution
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Hence, the weak solution is also unique.
Since the uniqueness of the solution to the martingale problem with the operator
\bar{\mathcal{L}}
is equivalent to the uniqueness of the weak solution to the SDE
(
1.20
)
, the limiting process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying
(
4.8
)
is the unique solution to the SDE
(
1.20
)
in the weak sense. The proof is complete. ∎

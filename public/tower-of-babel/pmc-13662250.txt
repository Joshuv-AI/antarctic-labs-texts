Development and Validation of a Perioperative Risk Update Model for Postoperative Delirium in Patients Aged ≥ 80 Years Undergoing Hip Fracture Surgery: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Feifei Zhao, Huan Sai, Shan Peng, Jing Wu, Lingling Wang, Tao Zhang, Xuebin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPostoperative delirium (POD) remains a frequent and serious complication after hip fracture surgery in very old patients. A clinically usable risk model could facilitate perioperative risk updating and targeted prevention in this vulnerable population.MethodsWe retrospectively included consecutive patients aged ≥ 80 years undergoing hip fracture surgery between January 2021 and December 2023 (n = 666). POD during hospitalization was the primary outcome. Candidate predictors covering demographics, comorbidities, perioperative variables, and routine laboratory tests were prespecified. Predictors were selected using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator with 10-fold cross-validation and entered into multivariable logistic regression to develop a nomogram. Discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility were assessed. External validation was conducted in an institutionally related cohort (January 2024 to December 2025) using identical eligibility criteria. Two sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness.ResultsPOD occurred in 99 patients (14.9%). The final nomogram included 14 predictors and showed excellent discrimination in the derivation cohort, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.913 (95% CI, 0.877–0.948). At the optimal predicted-probability threshold of 0.274, the sensitivity and specificity were 0.788 and 0.922, respectively. Bootstrap calibration demonstrated close agreement between predicted and observed risks, with a bias-corrected mean absolute error of 0.009. In the external validation cohort, the model retained good discrimination, with an AUC of 0.884. In addition, the preoperative-only sensitivity model also retained good predictive performance.ConclusionsThis nomogram provides individualized perioperative POD risk estimation for octogenarian patients undergoing hip fracture surgery. Additionally, clinically meaningful risk estimation may also be feasible before surgery.Keywords: Delirium, Hip fractures, Risk assessment, Prognosis, Aged, 80 and over

An aging global population has led to a worldwide increase in hip fractures, making it a significant health concern.1) Patients who are at least 80 years of age are more prone to complications that arise after surgery due to factors such as advanced age and the presence of additional health conditions.2) Postoperative delirium (POD) is the most common complication associated with hip fractures in elderly patients.3) The prevalence of POD ranges from 5% to 61%, depending on the patient population.4) POD is a neuropsychological syndrome that most often occurs in postoperative patients. It is characterized by an acute onset followed by long-term cognitive impairment, which is manifested by confusion, anxiety, personality changes, and memory deficits.5) The incidence rises in tandem with the growing number of elderly patients undergoing surgical procedures. Prolonged hospitalization, poorer functional recovery, and increased short- and long-term mortality can result from POD.6) Studies have shown that cognitive dysfunction that can persist up to 1 year after surgery is experienced by more than 50% of surgical patients who develop delirium.7)

While the etiology of POD remains to be fully elucidated, numerous studies have repeatedly demonstrated that factors such as increasing age, male sex, diabetes, heart failure, preexisting cognitive impairment, higher American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) scores, surgical procedures, anesthesia, intraoperative blood transfusion (IBT),8) and certain laboratory parameters (e.g., blood glucose,9) hemoglobin, and neutrophil count) are associated with the development of POD.5,10) Due to an increasing elderly population and improvements in surgical and anesthesia techniques, more patients aged 80 years and older with hip fractures are undergoing surgery. However, previous studies have mainly included people aged 60 or 65 years and older, rather than patients aged 80 years and older. Consequently, studies of hip fractures in individuals over 80 years of age underscore the pressing need for the management of this demographic, which poses a significant challenge for patients, families, and society at large.

The aims of this study were to develop and validate a perioperative prediction nomogram for POD in patients aged ≥ 80 years undergoing hip fracture surgery and to further examine, in a sensitivity analysis restricted to preoperative variables, whether clinically meaningful risk estimation could also be achieved before surgery.

METHODS

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Third Hospital of Hebei Medical University (IRB No. Ke2025-103-1) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design. All data were anonymized prior to analysis to protect patient privacy.

Study Population

This is a retrospective observational cohort study. We conducted a review of the electronic medical records of 815 patients aged 80 and older who were hospitalized in a tertiary referral hospital from January 2021 to December 2023.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age 80 years or older, (2) hip fracture confirmed by magnetic resonance imaging or x-ray (femoral neck fracture or intertrochanteric fracture). The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) multiple fractures, (2) pathological fractures or old fractures, (3) conservative treatment, (4) incomplete clinical data, (5) presence of neurological and psychiatric disorders, except dementia, and (6) missing delirium assessment and/or insufficient perioperative or follow-up information for outcome ascertainment.

Patients with severe neurological or psychiatric disorders other than dementia were excluded because such conditions could substantially complicate baseline mental-status interpretation and reduce the reliability of retrospective delirium ascertainment. Dementia was retained because it is common in very elderly hip fracture patients and represents a clinically important baseline vulnerability directly relevant to POD risk in routine practice.

Definition

The diagnosis of POD was based on the daily records of the attending physician and the assessment of the anesthesiologist. Symptoms similar to POD or other conscious and mental disorders were observed by clinicians, nursing staff, or family members, and professional physicians performed bedside observation, final diagnosis, and treatment. POD monitoring focused on the high-risk period (≤ 5 days, peak ≤ 48 hours). The long length of hospital stay (LOS) for rehabilitation after hip fracture surgery in the elderly provides sufficient observation time for POD detection.

The diagnosis of delirium follows the criteria outlined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, developed by the American Psychiatric Association in 2013. The most widely used instrument for identification of delirium is the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM).11) CAM scores are determined by 4 features: (1) acute onset and fluctuating changes in mental status, (2) inattention, (3) disorganized or incoherent thinking, and (4) an altered level of consciousness. The diagnosis of delirium by CAM requires the presence of features (1) and (2) and either feature (3) or (4). In this retrospective cohort, POD occurrence during hospitalization was ascertained from routine clinical documentation rather than from a prospective daily research assessment protocol.

Data Collection

Data were collected retrospectively from the electronic medical record of our institution. Potential predictors of POD were systematically identified through an exhaustive literature review and categorized into 5 clinically relevant domains: (1) sociodemographic factors: age, sex, and body mass index; (2) medical history: hypertension, coronary artery disease (CAD), osteoporosis, diabetes mellitus, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), dementia, stroke, and chronic heart failure (CHF); (3) injury-related data: time from admission to operation and fracture type; (4) surgery-related factors: type of surgery, type of anesthesia, ASA score, duration of surgery, blood loss, and IBT; (5) laboratory indicators: fasting blood glucose (FBG), albumin, hemoglobin, C-reactive protein (CRP), neutrophils, and lymphocytes.

Variables were classified as continuous or categorical. Continuous variables included age, time from admission to surgery, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, CRP, hemoglobin, FBG, albumin, duration of surgery, and intraoperative blood loss. Categorical variables included sex, fracture type, surgical method, anesthesia type, IBT, ASA class (I–IV), and comorbidities (hypertension, CAD, CHF, diabetes, COPD, osteoporosis, stroke, and dementia).

Outcomes

The primary outcome was POD occurring from postoperative day 1 until discharge. The secondary outcome was LOS.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp.) and R version 4.4.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, as appropriate, and were compared using the Student t-test or Mann-Whitney U-test. Categorical variables are presented as number with percentage and were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Candidate predictors were prespecified based on clinical relevance and prior literature. Predictor selection in the derivation cohort was performed using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 10-fold cross-validation. Variables retained at lambda.1se were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model, and predictors remaining statistically significant were retained in the final nomogram. Multicollinearity was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF). Model discrimination was quantified using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC). Calibration and clinical utility were evaluated using calibration plots and decision curve analysis (DCA).

Internal validation was conducted using bootstrap resampling with 1,000 iterations. To address potential optimism in model discrimination, we additionally performed bootstrap optimism correction for the apparent AUC. In each of 1,000 bootstrap resamples, the final logistic model was refitted using the fixed predictor set, AUC was calculated in the bootstrap sample and in the original cohort, and average optimism was subtracted from the apparent AUC. External validation was subsequently performed in an institutionally related cohort obtained from another trauma center within our institution by applying the locked final model to the validation dataset and reassessing discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility. Because the final model included operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, and IBT, the nomogram was interpreted as a perioperative or early postoperative risk update model rather than a purely preoperative screening tool. To further assess robustness and clinical applicability, 2 sensitivity analyses were additionally performed. First, a preoperative-only sensitivity model was developed using variables available before surgery to examine whether clinically meaningful prediction could also be achieved in a purely preoperative setting. Second, a sensitivity analysis excluding all patients with dementia was conducted to evaluate whether the main findings were unduly driven by the dementia subgroup and to address the concern regarding the exclusion criteria. A 2-sided p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Demographic Characteristics of Patients

As shown in Fig. 1, 815 patients aged ≥ 80 years with hip fracture were screened between January 2021 and December 2023, and 149 were excluded, leaving 666 patients for analysis (POD, n = 99; non-POD, n = 567). Baseline characteristics are summarized in Table 1. Overall, the mean age was 85.2 years and 33.5% were male; POD occurred in 14.9% of patients. Compared with the non-POD group, patients with POD were more often male and had a higher proportion of intertrochanteric fractures, more frequent general anesthesia, higher ASA class, longer operative duration, greater blood loss and IBT, and a higher prevalence of dementia, CHF, diabetes, stroke, and CAD. The POD group also showed higher FBG, lower albumin and hemoglobin, higher neutrophil counts and lower lymphocyte counts, and longer time to surgery and LOS (Table 1). Median CRP levels were 34.07 mg/L (IQR, 19.86–82.16) in the POD group and 34.54 mg/L (IQR, 15.45–66.09) in the non-POD group.

Model Development, Performance, and Clinical Utility

Among 25 prespecified candidate predictors, LASSO logistic regression with 10-fold cross-validation was performed in the full derivation cohort (n = 666). The minimum binomial deviance was achieved at lambda.min = 0.003649, at which 24 predictors had nonzero coefficients, whereas the 1-SE criterion corresponded to lambda.1se = 0.021371 and retained 17 predictors for further analysis (Fig. 2). These 17 variables were then entered simultaneously into a multivariable logistic regression model, and 14 variables remained independently associated with POD (Table 2), including male sex (adjusted odds ratio [aOR], 4.22; 95% CI, 2.24–7.97), anesthesia type (spinal vs. general: aOR, 0.44; 95% CI, 0.22–0.86), duration of surgery (aOR, 1.02; 95% CI, 1.01–1.03), intraoperative blood loss (aOR, 1.00; 95% CI, 1.00–1.01), IBT (aOR, 3.36; 95% CI, 1.80–6.30), dementia (aOR, 2.86; 95% CI, 1.22–6.73), CHF (aOR, 2.80; 95% CI, 1.26–6.21), osteoporosis (aOR, 2.36; 95% CI, 1.26–4.41), stroke (aOR, 2.21; 95% CI, 1.20–4.04), FBG (aOR, 1.31; 95% CI, 1.17–1.46), albumin (aOR, 0.89; 95% CI, 0.82–0.96), hemoglobin (aOR, 0.95; 95% CI, 0.93–0.97), neutrophil count (aOR, 1.21; 95% CI, 1.07–1.38), and lymphocyte count (aOR, 0.54; 95% CI, 0.32–0.91). Collinearity was low for all retained predictors (VIF range, 1.03–1.32).

A nomogram was constructed based on these 14 predictors (Fig. 3A). Because the final model included operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, and IBT, it was interpreted as a perioperative or early postoperative risk update model. The nomogram demonstrated excellent discrimination with an apparent AUC of 0.913 (95% CI, 0.877–0.948) (Fig. 3B). At the optimal predicted-probability threshold of 0.274, the sensitivity and specificity were 0.788 and 0.922, respectively. Calibration using bootstrap resampling (B = 1,000) showed close agreement between predicted and observed risks, with a mean absolute error of 0.009 for the bias-corrected calibration curve (Fig. 3C). In the bootstrap optimism-correction analysis, the mean optimism for AUC was 0.013, yielding an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.900 (Supplementary Table 1). DCA indicated that the nomogram provided a higher standardized net benefit than treat-all and treat-none strategies across threshold probabilities from 0.03 to 0.97 (Fig. 3D).

External Validation

To further evaluate reproducibility, the locked final model was externally validated in an institutionally related cohort obtained from another trauma center within our institution. The external validation cohort included 237 patients (Supplementary Table 2). The model retained good discrimination in the external cohort, with an AUC of 0.884 (95% CI, 0.818–0.949). At the optimal predicted-probability threshold of 0.180, the sensitivity and specificity were 0.821 and 0.869, respectively. External calibration was attenuated compared with that in the derivation cohort, with a calibration intercept of –0.097, a calibration slope of 0.748, a Brier score of 0.084, and an observed-to-expected ratio of 1.10. The calibration curve showed deviations from the ideal line, including a tendency toward overprediction in the lower predicted-risk range, although the overall mean predicted risk was slightly lower than the observed event rate. DCA in the external cohort also supported the clinical utility of the model across a clinically relevant range of threshold probabilities (Supplementary Figs. 1, 2, 3).

Sensitivity Analyses

To address the concern regarding preoperative applicability, we additionally developed a sensitivity model restricted to variables available before surgery only. In this preoperative-only analysis, LASSO retained 12 variables at lambda.1se, and 10 predictors remained in the final multivariable model, including male sex, ASA score, dementia, CHF, stroke, FBG, albumin, hemoglobin, neutrophil count, and lymphocyte count. Although this preoperative-only model showed slightly lower performance than the main perioperative model, it still retained good discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility, with an AUC of 0.876 (95% CI, 0.835–0.917), a sensitivity of 0.768, a specificity of 0.840, a bias-corrected mean absolute error of 0.012, and a positive net benefit across threshold probabilities from 0.04 to 0.97 (Supplementary Table 3).

To further examine whether the main findings were unduly influenced by the dementia subgroup, we performed an additional sensitivity analysis excluding all patients with dementia. In this non-dementia subset, LASSO retained 13 variables at lambda.1se, and 10 predictors remained in the final multivariable model, including male sex, duration of surgery, intraoperative blood loss, IBT, CHF, FBG, albumin, hemoglobin, neutrophil count, and lymphocyte count. The model continued to show good performance, with an AUC of 0.897 (95% CI, 0.855–0.940), a sensitivity of 0.795, a specificity of 0.897, a bias-corrected mean absolute error of 0.016, and a positive net benefit across threshold probabilities from 0.03 to 0.97. These findings suggest that the main results were not solely driven by the inclusion of patients with dementia (Supplementary Table 4).

DISCUSSION

In this retrospective cohort study, we developed and validated a prediction model for POD in patients aged 80 years or older undergoing hip fracture surgery. In the revised final model, 14 predictors remained independently associated with POD, including male sex, anesthesia type, operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, IBT, dementia, CHF, osteoporosis, stroke, FBG, albumin, hemoglobin, neutrophil count, and lymphocyte count. Based on these predictors, we constructed a nomogram that showed excellent apparent discrimination and good calibration in the derivation cohort. Bootstrap optimism correction yielded an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.900 compared with the apparent AUC of 0.913, indicating that some optimism was present but that discrimination remained favorable. After applying the locked final model to an institutionally related external validation cohort from another trauma center within our institution, the model retained good discriminatory performance (AUC, 0.884), but calibration was attenuated, with a calibration slope of 0.748. Therefore, the model should still be interpreted cautiously as a clinically supportive risk-update tool rather than as a definitive standalone decision instrument.

External transportability should also be interpreted with caution. The external cohort differed from the derivation cohort in several baseline characteristics, including diabetes prevalence (27.8% vs. 9.3%), stroke prevalence (38.3% vs. 23.2%), and mean FBG level (7.93 vs. 6.43 mmol/L), indicating meaningful case-mix differences. These differences likely contributed to the attenuated external calibration and the local overprediction observed in the lower predicted-risk range. Before deployment in populations with substantially different baseline risk distributions, recalibration or model updating may be warranted.

Because the final model retained operative duration, intraoperative blood loss, and IBT, it is more appropriately interpreted as a perioperative or early postoperative risk update model rather than a purely preoperative screening instrument. This distinction is clinically relevant, because the model may be most useful after key intraoperative information becomes available, when clinicians need to update delirium risk estimation and intensify surveillance or preventive management during the immediate postoperative period. At the same time, our preoperative-only sensitivity analysis showed that a model restricted to variables available before surgery still retained good discrimination and calibration, suggesting that clinically meaningful risk estimation may also be feasible in the preoperative setting, although with somewhat lower performance than the main perioperative model.

The probability threshold should be interpreted as a function of the intended clinical purpose. A lower threshold may be preferred when broader surveillance is desired, whereas a higher-specificity threshold may be more suitable when identifying a more narrowly defined high-risk subgroup. Accordingly, the reported optimal threshold should not be interpreted as the only clinically relevant cutoff.

The DCA further supports the potential clinical usefulness of the nomogram. In both the derivation cohort and the external validation cohort, the model provided a higher net benefit than treat-all and treat-none strategies across clinically relevant threshold ranges. This suggests that the model may be useful for identifying patients in whom intensified POD surveillance and more proactive supportive management are justified. In practice, such measures may include geriatric co-management, enhanced cognitive monitoring, more cautious sedation and analgesia strategies, optimization of glucose control, nutritional assessment and support, and closer hemodynamic monitoring during the immediate postoperative period. Importantly, the model is best interpreted as a tool to support risk-informed perioperative management rather than as a trigger for any single mandatory intervention.

POD is a common complication following hip fractures and a significant contributor to postoperative mortality. Given the adverse impact of POD on the prognosis of patients with hip fractures, prevention, timely diagnosis, and active treatment are of utmost importance. Just as with any preventable disease, predictors can be classified as modifiable or non-modifiable. For modifiable predictors, it is essential to identify and avoid their exposure.

A comprehensive multicenter observational study found that 25.8% of the patients received perioperative blood transfusions.12) Among our patient population, approximately 18.6% received IBT. Among them, the IBT rate for patients with POD was 48.5%. Due to the fact that elderly patients usually have more underlying diseases and a weaker constitution, blood transfusions are often required during surgery. However, the impact of blood transfusions during surgery is not universally positive. We believe that the occurrence of postoperative cognitive impairment may be attributed to the reduction of cerebral blood flow, metabolic dysfunction, and inflammatory response during the surgical procedure. A comprehensive study has indicated that IBT might be a contributing factor to the development of POD.13) However, the necessity of IBT is contingent on several factors, including the extent of preoperative anemia, the volume of intraoperative blood loss, and the patient's vital signs. The study's findings indicate that a low preoperative hemoglobin level is a risk factor for delirium. Studies have shown that the incidence of delirium is notably higher among elderly hospitalized patients with anemia.14) This can be interpreted as a decrease in hemoglobin levels due to fracture bleeding and intraoperative blood loss, but it does not necessarily mean anemia. Furthermore, previous studies did not specifically describe the preoperative hemoglobin levels; they only determined whether the patients were anemic. From our perspective, these 2 findings are related. Our study employed a multivariable logistic regression analysis. The results of the study indicated that IBT was significantly associated with the occurrence of POD. However, IBT should be interpreted primarily as a marker of perioperative physiological burden and clinical vulnerability in the present model, rather than as an isolated modifiable factor whose direct manipulation was tested in this study.

Currently, surgery remains the preferred treatment for hip fractures. POD can be triggered by a variety of factors, including surgical trauma, anesthesia, intraoperative blood loss, pain, and other stimulants.6) Furthermore, elderly patients often experience a decline in cognitive and memory abilities, which makes them more prone to emotional reactions such as anxiety and fear when facing surgery. The duration of the surgery is a risk factor for POD in elderly patients with hip fractures. The rationale behind this phenomenon is that an extended surgical time often results in a prolonged duration of anesthesia and an extended stress period triggered by the surgical trauma.15) However, it should be noted that the organs of elderly patients generally exhibit a certain degree of functional decline. They are more susceptible to surgical trauma and elevated stress levels, which can contribute to bodily imbalances. Disruptions to the balance of the hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal axis are more likely to trigger delirium. Furthermore, the prolonged surgical duration for elderly patients with hip fractures implies an increased surgical trauma and higher intraoperative blood loss. Excessive blood loss can exacerbate the hypoxia of brain cells, thereby leading to the occurrence of delirium.16)

At present, the relationship between anesthesia and POD remains to be fully elucidated. Previous studies have suggested that anesthesia-related factors may influence postoperative cognitive outcomes. In our model, anesthesia type remained independently associated with POD, with spinal anesthesia showing a lower estimated risk than general anesthesia after multivariable adjustment. However, this association should be interpreted cautiously, because anesthesia choice in a retrospective dataset may still reflect residual differences in patient vulnerability, fracture complexity, surgical planning, and perioperative management. Therefore, our findings should not be interpreted as definitive evidence of a causal protective effect of 1 anesthesia approach over another, but they do support the inclusion of anesthesia type as a clinically relevant risk indicator in the present model.

For a considerable period, inadequate brain energy has been identified as a possible mechanism for the development of POD.17) A lack of sufficient energy supply to the brain can result in impaired glucose metabolism, disrupted protein synthesis, imbalanced ion levels, impaired mitochondrial function, and accelerated fat breakdown. This sequence of events can ultimately lead to the occurrence of POD.18) The brain primarily relies on glucose as its energy source, accounting for over 50% of total glucose consumption. Therefore, as Titlestad et al.17) astutely pointed out, it is possible that glucose utilization disorders and brain insulin resistance are the underlying mechanisms of delirium. Furthermore, persistent hyperglycemia in elderly patients before surgery indicates that pancreatic islet function has been imbalanced, suggesting either insufficient insulin secretion or insulin resistance in the body.9) In summary, the interaction between insulin resistance and persistent hyperglycemia disrupts the brain's energy supply, which is at the core of this potential mechanism. Studies have demonstrated a clear linear dose-response relationship between preoperative blood glucose levels and the risk of POD. Higher preoperative hyperglycemia has been shown to be associated with a higher risk of POD.9) This finding aligns with the conclusions drawn from our research. These findings support the clinical relevance of perioperative glycemic status in POD risk assessment. However, whether model-guided glucose-targeted intervention reduces POD risk was not examined in the present study and requires further prospective investigation.

Malnutrition frequently occurs in conjunction with hip fractures among the elderly population. It is associated with functional changes and can serve as either the cause or the consequence of fractures. In this study, low serum albumin was identified as an independent risk factor for the occurrence of POD (p = 0.002). The relative change in serum albumin is a significant risk factor for POD in elderly patients following total joint replacement surgery.19) The neuroinflammation hypothesis suggests that surgery and trauma can trigger a systemic immune inflammatory response, which may damage the blood-brain barrier and promote the neuroinflammatory processes related to POD.20) Albumin, a natural antioxidant, has the ability to capture free oxygen groups. In response to such damage, serum albumin escapes from the blood-brain barrier and enters the brain, thereby inhibiting their peroxidation reactions and the formation of reactive oxygen species.21) Research indicates that the preoperative use of high-protein nutritional supplements may enhance the clinical outcomes of surgical patients.22) Therefore, monitoring and adjusting the albumin level is of vital importance for the prognosis of patients undergoing hip fracture surgery.

This study has several limitations. First, although we added an external validation cohort from another trauma center within our institution, this remains an institutionally related validation setting rather than geographically independent multicenter validation; broader external validation is still required before wider implementation can be recommended. Second, only 99 POD events were available for a 14-predictor final model, and the bootstrap optimism-correction analysis showed a mean AUC optimism of 0.013, yielding an optimism-corrected AUC of 0.900. This suggests that apparent performance was somewhat optimistic even though discrimination remained favorable. Third, the external cohort differed from the derivation cohort in baseline diabetes, stroke, and FBG, and external calibration was attenuated; recalibration may therefore be needed before the model is deployed in populations with substantially different case-mix or baseline risk distributions. Fourth, the study was retrospective in design, and POD ascertainment was based on routine clinical records rather than on prospective daily assessment by dedicated trained personnel using a standardized research protocol. As a result, mild delirium and hypoactive delirium may have been under-recognized, which could have introduced outcome misclassification. Fifth, although we clarified the rationale for retaining dementia while excluding other neurological and psychiatric disorders and additionally performed a sensitivity analysis excluding all dementia patients, the exclusion strategy may still limit comparability with other cohorts. Finally, residual confounding cannot be excluded because some potentially relevant perioperative factors, such as surgeon experience, ward-level preventive practices, postoperative mobilization, analgesic exposure, and other care-process variables, were not fully captured in the retrospective dataset. Overall, the model should be regarded as supportive and preliminary rather than definitive.

In this retrospective cohort study, we developed and validated a perioperative nomogram based on routinely available clinical variables to estimate the risk of POD in patients aged ≥ 80 years undergoing hip fracture surgery. Additionally, clinically meaningful risk estimation may also be feasible before surgery. Further multicenter prospective studies are warranted to confirm generalizability and to evaluate whether model-guided prevention improves clinical outcomes.

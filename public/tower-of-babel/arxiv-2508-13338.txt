Abstract
. This paper finishes the goal of the authors started in two previous manuscripts dedicated to revisiting the continuity properties of toroidal pseudo-differential operators with symbols in the Hörmander classes. Here we prove pointwise estimates in terms of the Fefferman-Stein sharp maximal function and of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Combining these estimates with the properties of Muckenhoupt’s weight class A p A_{p} we obtain boundedness theorems for pseudo-differential operators between weighted Lebesgue spaces on the torus L p ​ ( w ) L^{p}(w) . These results are given in the context of the global symbolic analysis defined on 𝕋 n × ℤ n \mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n} as developed by Ruzhansky and Turunen by using discrete Fourier analysis, and extend those of Park and Tomita available in the Euclidean case. Moreover, we include continuity results on Sobolev spaces W p s W^{s}_{p} and on Besov spaces B p , q s B^{s}_{p,q} on the torus. Our techniques are taken from Park and Tomita [ 37 ] and we consider its toroidal extension here for the completeness of the boundedness of toroidal pseudo-differential operators with respect to the current literature.

1.
Introduction

By continuing our program of studying the continuity properties of pseudo-differential operators on the torus, first in the context of Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}
, as discussed in
[
13
]
, and then in the context of Hardy spaces
H^{p}
, as discussed in
[
14
]
, this paper focuses on pointwise estimates of toroidal pseudo-differential operators in terms of the Fefferman-Stein sharp maximal function and of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Moreover, we study the continuity properties of such operators in weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(w)
, Sobolev spaces
W^{s}_{p}
, and Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
.

Fefferman and Stein introduced the sharp maximal function
\mathcal{M}^{\#}
in
[
26
]
, which serves as a way to characterize the norm of the space of functions with bounded mean oscillation
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, they proved that it satisfies an upper bound with respect to the
L^{p}
-norm of integrable functions, namely, it satisfies that
\|f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}^{\#}f\|_{L^{p}}
. Here, and in the remainder of this paper,
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a constant
C>0
, such that
A\leq CB
. Moreover, it has been proved that if
T
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator, then we have the pointwise inequality
\mathcal{M}^{\#}(Tf)(x)\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)
, where
\mathrm{M}_{r}
is the
L^{r}
version of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Combining these two estimates we obtain continuity estimates of
T
from
L^{p}
into itself. Namely, that

(1.1)

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}^{\#}(Tf)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\mathrm{M}_{r}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

This technique has been widely employed in a variety of works in harmonic analysis, see
[
18
,
26
]
. Moreover, Muckenhoupt proved that weights
w
in the class
A_{p}
, satisfy the following estimate
\|\mathrm{M}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}
, for
1<p<\infty
, see
[
35
]
. Combining these two facts, Chanillo and Torchinsky
[
18
]
proved the following continuity result for weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(w)
for Euclidean pseudo-differential operators with symbols in the Hörmander classes
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
18
]

Let
0\leq\delta<\rho<1
, and let
2\leq p<\infty
. Suppose
w\in A_{p/2}
and
\sigma\in S^{-n(1-\rho)/2}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then
T_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{p}(w)
into itself.

The estimate above has been extended to the case
0<\delta=\rho<1
, but with a certain restriction on the range of
\rho
, by Miyachi and Yabuta.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
33
]

Let
1<r\leq 2
, let
0<\rho\leq r/2
, and let
\rho<1
. Suppose that
m\leq-n(1-\rho)/r
, and let
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, it holds that

\mathcal{M}^{\#}(T_{\sigma}f)(x)\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For more results related to the previous one, we refer the reader to Álvarez and Hounie
[
2
]
. The previous theorem was extended by Park and Tomita
[
37
]
to the range
0<\rho<1
. For that purpose, they defined the
L^{r}
version of the inhomogeneous sharp maximal function.

\mathfrak{M}^{\#}_{r}f(x):=\sup_{Q:\ell(Q)\leq 1}\inf_{c_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)-c_{Q}|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}+\sup_{Q:\ell(Q)>1}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r},

where the supremum ranges over cubes with sides parallel to the axis. However, this coincides with the homogeneous case in the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Now, we present the toroidal extension of Park and Tomita’s result
[
37
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1<r\leq 2
and let
0<\rho<1
. Suppose that
m\in\mathbb{R}
is such that
m\leq-n(1-\rho)/r
, and let us consider the periodic symbol
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. Then it holds that

\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{r}(T_{\sigma}f)(x)\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{n},

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Hence we can use an argument similar as in
Eq.

1.1
to prove the
L^{p}(w)
-boundedness for toroidal pseudo-differential operators, where
w
is in the Muckenhoupt’s weight class
A_{p/r}
. As Park and Tomita
[
37
]
, first we apply a Hardy-Littlewood decomposition on the frequency domain of the symbol
\sigma:=\sigma(x,\xi)
in order to obtain a sequence of operators
T_{\sigma_{k}}
. Then, we obtain uniform estimates in terms of the Schwartz kernels of the corresponding sequence of operators. We also take advantage of the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-boundedness result proved in
[
13
]
. Finally, we prove
Theorem

1.3
and obtain
L^{p}(w)
continuity as a corollary.

On the other hand, Sobolev and Besov spaces have been of interest in the study of existence and uniqueness of solutions of systems of partial differential equations. Here, we prove versions of the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
-boundedness result proved in
[
13
]
in both, the Sobolev spaces
W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and on Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, considering even the case
\delta>\rho
. We present our main result in this topic as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0\leq\delta<1
, let
0<\rho\leq 1
, let
m\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. Then,
T_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator from
W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
into
W^{s-\mu}_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, and from
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
into
B^{s-\mu}_{q,r}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, where
1<p\leq q<\infty,
for any
s\in\mathbb{R},
and
1\leq r\leq\infty,
provided that the following conditions hold:

(1)

First,
1<p\leq 2\leq q
, and

\mu\geq m+n\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+\lambda\right).

(2)

Secondly,
2\leq p\leq q
, and

\mu\geq m+n\left[\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\lambda\right].

(3)

Finally,
p\leq q\leq 2
, and

\mu\geq m+n\left[\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\lambda\right].

In all the cases above we consider the efficiency parameter
\lambda:=\max\{0,(\delta-\rho)/2\}
.

Now, we proceed to discuss our main results.

Remark 1.5
.

Notice that the case
s=0=\mu
in
Theorem

1.4
, corresponds to the
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
continuity result proved in
[
13
]
. Moreover, let us recall that the
(\rho,\delta)
-Hörmander symbol classes are not stable under the multiplication of test functions when
\rho<1-\delta
. Hence, these results cannot be obtained from its Euclidean counterpart considering the torus as a closed manifold and using on it a partition of unity.

This work is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we present the calculus of pseudo-differential operators on the torus, as developed by Ruzhansky and Turunen in
[
40
]
. Moreover, we define the maximal operators of interest, and the functions spaces between which we will prove the continuity of toroidal pseudo-differential operators. In
Section

3.1
we present useful estimates in terms of the Schwartz kernel, and in
Section

3.2
we prove the pointwise estimates in terms of maximal functions and the boundedness properties of toroidal pseudo-differential operators in weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(w)
, the Sobolev spaces
W^{s}_{p}
, and Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present the preliminaries about the Fourier analysis on the torus and on the toroidal pseudo-differential calculus as developed in
[
40
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Periodic functions)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow X
, is 1-periodic if
f(x)=f(x+k)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We can identify this function with one defined in
\mathbb{T}^{n}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, which is a compact manifold without boundary. Moreover, the space of 1-perodic functions
m
times continuously differentiable is denoted by
C^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n};X)
. The subset of these functions that have compact support is denoted by
C^{m}_{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n};X)
. The test functions are elements of the space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n};X):=\bigcap_{m}C^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n};X)
. When
X=\mathbb{C}
, we will simply use
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

In order to define the class of pseudo-differential operators on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we first need to define the group Fourier transform of 1-periodic smooth functions.

Definition 2.2

(Group Fourier Transform on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
)
.

For
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
we define the
toroidal Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}
as follows

(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f)(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{-i2\pi x\cdot\xi}f(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x,

as a function of
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

In view of the group structure of the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, the corresponding frequency domain of 1-periodic smooth functions is the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Also, it can be proved that the corresponding functions
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f
satisfy certain regularity conditions. In fact, they belong to the Schwartz space defined below.
Here we use
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Definition 2.3

(Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
)
.

We say
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, that is a
rapidly decaying
function
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, if for given
0<M<\infty
, there exists
C_{\varphi M}>0
such that

|\varphi(\xi)|\leq C_{\varphi M}\langle\xi\rangle^{-M}\quad,\quad\forall\,\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

It can be proved that the toroidal Fourier transform is a bijection between
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
with inverse
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}^{-1}:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
defined by

(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}^{-1}\varphi)(x):=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi x\cdot\xi}\varphi(\xi).

In the Euclidean case, the Hörmander symbol classes are defined requiring some regularity conditions using partial derivatives in both, the space and frequency domain. However, in the case of the torus the frequency domain is the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Therefore, we define the natural analogous of the derivative in the discrete case: the partial difference operators.

Definition 2.4

(Partial difference operators)
.

Let
e_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be such that
(e_{j})_{i}
is equal to 1, if
i=j
, and let it be equal to 0 otherwise. Then, for
\sigma(\xi):\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we define

\Delta_{\xi_{j}}\sigma(\xi):=\sigma(\xi+e_{j})-\sigma(\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta^{\alpha}_{\xi}:=\Delta_{\xi_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\Delta_{\xi_{n}}^{\alpha_{n}},

for any multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
.

Now we proceed to define the Hörmander classes of symbols on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, as in Ruzhansky and Turunen
[
40
]
, that will be important in the quantization of pseudo-differential operators.

Definition 2.5

(Hörmander classes on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
0\leq\delta,\rho\leq 1
. We say that a function
\sigma:=\sigma(x,\xi)
that is smooth on
x
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
belongs to the
toroidal symbol class

S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
if

\left|\partial^{\beta}_{x}\Delta^{\alpha}_{\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)\right|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{m-\rho|\alpha|+\delta|\beta|},

for every
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, we say that
\sigma(x,\xi)
has order
m
and we define
S^{-\infty}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})=\bigcap_{m\in\mathbb{R}}S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

Definition 2.6

(Pseudo-differential operators on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
)
.

For a symbol
\sigma:=\sigma(x,\xi)\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
we can associate a
toroidal pseudo-differential operator

T_{\sigma}
from
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
into itself, defined as

T_{\sigma}f(x):=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi x\cdot\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)(\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f)(\xi),

which can be rewritten as

(2.1)

T_{\sigma}f(x)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

The class of operators with symbols in
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
will be denoted by
\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

The toroidal Fourier transform and toroidal pseudo-differential operators can be extended by duality to the space of
periodic distributions

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
consisting of continuous linear functionals on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. This extension allows us to define the Schwartz kernel of a toroidal pseudo-differential operator.

Remark 2.7

(Schwartz kernel)
.

In the sense of distributions described above,
Eq.

2.1
can be rewritten as

T_{\sigma}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left[\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\sigma(x,\xi)\right]f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}k(x,y)f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

and we say that
k(x,y)
is the
Schwartz kernel
of the corresponding operator. Notice that when the order of
p(x,\xi)
is less than
-n
, the series defining
k
is absolutely convergent and this distribution agrees with a periodic function on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Since the proofs of the results in this paper only depend on the order of the toroidal pseudo-differential operators, then they extend to their adjoints. This is justified by the following result in
[
40
, Corollary 4.9.8]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

If
T\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, then its adjoint
T^{*}\in\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
.

Now we introduce a particular and very useful toroidal pseudo-differential operator.

Definition 2.9

(Bessel potential)
.

We define the
Bessel potential

J^{s}
as the pseudo-differential operator with symbol
\langle\xi\rangle^{s}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

For our pourposes, we will use a smooth interpolation of a symbol on the phase space
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to obtain a symbol defined on the continuous phase space
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is possible in view of the results stated in
[
40
, section 4.5]
and here we write some useful consequences.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
, let
0\leq\delta<1
, and let
0<\rho\leq 1
. The symbol
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
if and only if there exists a symbol
\tilde{\sigma}\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\sigma=\tilde{\sigma}|_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
. Moreover, this extension is unique modulo
S^{-\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Remark 2.11
.

When we employ this extension, the definition of its corresponding operator may be adjusted to

T_{\tilde{\sigma}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\tilde{\sigma}(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi\right]f(y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

so that the Schwartz kernel of the operator, in the sense of distributions, is defined as the integral

\tilde{k}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i2\pi(x-y)\cdot\xi}\tilde{\sigma}(x,\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

This kernel representation allows us to use techniques such as integration by parts when discussing properties of the Schwartz kernel.

Hence, there is a correspondence between toroidal symbols with discrete and continuous frequency domains. This property can be extended to the corresponding operators as well.

Theorem 2.12

(Equivalence of operator classes)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
, let
0\leq\delta<1
, and let
0<\rho\leq 1
. Then

\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Psi^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}).

Now, we define the maximal operators that will allow us to prove the boundedness of toroidal pseudo-differential operators.

Definition 2.13

(Sharp maximal operator)
.

For an integrable function
f
defined on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we define its corresponding
r
-sharp maximal function
by

\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{r}f(x):=\sup_{Q:x\in Q}\inf_{c_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)-c_{Q}|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r},

where the supremum is taken over all cubes in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with sides parallel to the axes.

In
[
37
]
, the authors work with the inhomogeneous version of the sharp maximal operator. This alternative definition makes a distinction between cubes with side length greater than one and less than one. In the case of the torus, this distinction is not necessary and in this paper we employ the definition stated above.

Definition 2.14

(Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator)
.

For an integrable function
f
defined on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we define the
r-Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
by

\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x):=\sup_{Q:x\in Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

where the supremum is taken over all cubes in
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with sides parallel to the axes.

Definition 2.15

(Weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(w)
)
.

We say that a nonnegative integrable function
w
defined on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
belongs to
Muckenhoupt’s
A_{p}
class of weights
if it satisfies the following properties

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}w(x)\lesssim w(x)\quad\text{for almost all }x\in\mathbb{T}^{n},

\displaystyle\quad\quad\text{when }p=1,

\displaystyle\sup_{Q:cubes}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}(w(x))^{-1/(p-1)}\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

\displaystyle\quad\quad\text{when }1<p<\infty.

For a weight
w
, the
weighted Lebesgue space

L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n};w)
with
1\leq p<\infty
, consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n};w)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

In order to simplify the notation, in general we will use
L^{p}(w)
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(w)}
, and when the weight is the constant function equal to one, we will use
L^{p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{p}
during the remainder of this paper.

As it was proved in
[
35
]
for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for arbitrary measure spaces in
[
28
, Section IV.1]
, we have that for
1<p<\infty
, we have that

(2.2)

w\in A_{p}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\|\mathrm{M}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

For
p=\infty
we define
A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{p>1}A_{p}
. Moreover, we have that
A_{p}\subset A_{q}
whenever
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Now, we define function spaces that establish certain regularity conditions on their elements, making them useful in the estudy of PDE’s.

Definition 2.16

(Sobolev spaces
W^{s}_{p}
)
.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we say a 1-periodic function
f
is in the
Sobolev space

W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{W^{s}_{p}}:=\|J^{s}f\|_{p}<\infty,

where
J^{s}
is the Bessel potential of order
s
.

In order to define a more general scale than the one of Sobolev spaces, the Besov spaces, let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be such that

\supp\varphi\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2\},

\varphi(\xi)>0,\quad\text{ for }\quad 2^{-1}<|\xi|<2,

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{k}\xi)=1,\quad\text{ when }\quad\xi\neq 0.

Definition 2.17

(Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}
)
.

First, let us define for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, the functions

\mathcal{F}\varphi_{k}(\xi):=\varphi(2^{k}\xi),\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{F}\psi(\xi):=1-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varphi(2^{-k}\xi),

where
\varphi
is as above. Then, we say that a 1-periodic function
f
belongs to the Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
if

\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}:=\|\psi*f\|_{p}+\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{sk}\|\varphi_{k}*f\|_{p})^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

It is known from
[
4
, Theorem 6.2.4]
that Besov spaces can be obtained from the real interpolation of Sobolev spaces, namely that,

(W^{s_{0}}_{p},W^{s_{1}}_{p})_{\theta,q}=B^{s}_{p,q},

for
s=\theta s_{0}+(1-\theta)s_{1}
. Here we have used
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,q}
for the real interpolation functor.

3.
Main results

3.1.
Kernel estimates

Let
\varphi
be a Schwartz function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that its Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}
is equal to one on the unit ball centered at the origin and is supported in the concentric ball of radius 2. Let
\psi
be another test function such that
\widehat{\psi}(\xi):=\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)-\widehat{\varphi}(2\xi)
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\psi_{k}(x):=2^{kn}\psi(2^{k}x).

Then we have an inhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley partition of unity formed by
\varphi
and
\psi_{k}
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, notice that

\supp\widehat{\psi_{k}}\subset\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{k-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+1}\right\}

and that

\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\widehat{\psi_{k}}(\xi)=1.

Thus, we can decompose any
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
as

\sigma(x,\xi)=\sigma(x,\xi)\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma(x,\xi)\widehat{\psi_{k}}(\xi)=:\sigma_{0}(x,\xi)+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sigma_{k}(x,\xi),

so we can write

T_{\sigma}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}T_{\sigma_{k}},

where
T_{\sigma_{k}}
are the toroidal pseudo-differential operators associated with
\sigma_{k}\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. Now, we write their corresponding kernels (in the sense of distributions discussed in the previous section) as

(3.1)

K_{k}(y,u):=\sum_{\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\sigma_{k}(y,\xi)e^{i2\pi u\cdot\xi}.

Moreover, let
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}
be the extension of the symbol
\sigma_{k}
defined on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. First, we prove the following kernel estimates.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<\rho\leq 1
and
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and let
K_{k}
be defined as in
Eq.

3.1
. For arbitrary
N\geq 0
and
1\leq r\leq 2
, we have that

\left\|(1+2^{k\rho}|u|)^{N}K_{k}(y,u)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\lesssim_{N}2^{k(m+n/r)},

\left\|(1+2^{k\rho}|u|)^{N}\nabla_{y}K_{k}(y,u)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\lesssim_{N}2^{k(\rho+m+n/r)},

\left\|(1+2^{k\rho}|u|)^{N}\nabla_{u}K_{k}(y,u)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\lesssim_{N}2^{k(1+m+n/r)},

as functions of
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
uniformly in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

We follow the argument of Park and Tomita
[
37
]
. Since
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then for any multi-index
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
we have that

(3.2)

\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(y,\xi)\right|\lesssim 2^{k(m-\rho|\beta|)},

(3.3)

\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\nabla_{y}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(y,\xi)\right|\lesssim 2^{k(m+\rho-\rho|\beta|)},

(3.4)

\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}[\xi\ \tilde{\sigma}_{k}(y,\xi)]\right|\leq\left|\xi\cdot\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(y,\xi)\right|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta-j}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(y,\xi)\right|\lesssim 2^{k(m+1-\rho|\beta|)},

since
\langle\xi\rangle\sim 2^{k}
. Now, we use Hausdorff-Young’s inequality and
Eq.

3.2
to obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|(2^{k\rho}u)^{\beta}K_{k}(y,u)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k\rho|\beta|}\left\|\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(x,\xi)\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle=2^{k\rho|\beta|}2^{k(m-\rho|\beta|)}2^{kn/r}

\displaystyle=2^{k(m+n/r)},

since the volume of the support of
\tilde{\sigma}_{k}
is comparable with
2^{kn}
. Moreover, here the
r^{\prime}
-norm is taken with respect to
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the
r
-norm is taken with respect to
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This concludes the proof of the first estimate, the remaining estimates can be proved using the same procedure and the estimates
Eq.

3.3
and
Eq.

3.4
respectively.
∎

3.2.
Boundedness results

Now, let us recall the following
L^{p}
-
L^{q}
continuity result for toroidal pseudo-differential operators.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.12]
Let
0\leq\delta<1
, let
0<\rho\leq 1
, let
m\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. Then,
T_{\sigma}
extends to a bounded operator from
L^{p}
into
L^{q}
where
1<p\leq q<\infty
when,

(1)

1<p\leq 2\leq q
and

(3.5)

m\leq-n\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+\lambda\right),

(2)

if
2\leq p\leq q
and

(3.6)

m\leq-n\left[\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)+\lambda\right],

(3)

if
p\leq q\leq 2
and

(3.7)

m\leq-n\left[\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}+(1-\rho)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\lambda\right],

where
\lambda:=\max\{0,(\delta-\rho)/2\}
.

Now, we prove some useful boundedness lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<r\leq 2
, let
0<\rho\leq r/2
, let
0<\rho<1
, and let
m=-n(1-\rho)/r
. Then for every
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, its corresponding operator
T_{\sigma}
is continuous from
L^{r}
into
L^{r/\rho}
.

Proof.

Notice that we have
r\leq 2\leq r/\rho
and
m
satisfies
Eq.

3.5
.
∎

In order to handle the case
\rho>r/2
that is not considered in the previous lemma, we prove the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<r<2
, let
r/2\leq\rho<1
, and let
m=-n(1-\rho)/r
. Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and

\frac{2\rho-r}{2-r}<\lambda<\rho.

Then every
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
satisfies

\|T_{\sigma_{k}}f\|_{\frac{r(1-\lambda)}{\rho-\lambda}}\lesssim 2^{\lambda nk\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\|f\|_{r}

for
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

Let us use the strategy by Park and Tomita
[
37
]
. Let us define

a_{k}(x,\xi):=\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(2^{-\lambda k}x,2^{\lambda k}\xi).

Then for any multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}a_{k}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle=2^{\lambda k(|\beta|-|\alpha|)}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\tilde{\sigma}_{k}(2^{-\lambda k}x,2^{\lambda k}\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\lambda k(|\beta|-|\alpha|)}2^{k(m-\rho(|\beta|-|\alpha|))}\chi_{\{\langle\xi\rangle\sim 2^{(1-\lambda)k}\}}

\displaystyle=2^{k[m-(\rho-\lambda)(|\beta|-|\alpha|)]}\chi_{\{\langle\xi\rangle\sim 2^{(1-\lambda)k}\}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{m}{1-\lambda}-\frac{\rho-\lambda}{1-\lambda}(|\beta|-|\alpha|)},

where the constant is independent of
k
. Therefore, we have that

a_{k}\in S^{\frac{m}{1-\lambda}}_{\frac{\rho-\lambda}{1-\lambda},\frac{\rho-\lambda}{1-\lambda}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

uniformly in
k
. Notice that

0<\frac{\rho-\lambda}{1-\lambda}<\frac{r}{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{m}{1-\lambda}=-\frac{n}{r}\left(1-\frac{\rho-\lambda}{1-\lambda}\right),

which allow us to employ
Lemma

3.3
, and obtain the
L^{r}
-
L^{\frac{r(1-\lambda)}{\rho-\lambda}}
-continuity for the operators
T_{a_{k}}
uniformly in
k
. Since we have that

T_{\sigma_{k}}f(x)=T_{a_{k}}(f(2^{-\lambda k}\cdot))(2^{\lambda k}x),

we can obtain that

\displaystyle\|T_{\sigma_{k}}f\|_{\frac{r(1-\lambda)}{\rho-\lambda}}

\displaystyle=2^{-\lambda nk\frac{\rho-\lambda}{r(1-\lambda)}}\|T_{a_{k}}(f(2^{-\lambda k}\cdot))\|_{\frac{r(1-\lambda)}{\rho-\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\lambda nk\frac{\rho-\lambda}{r(1-\lambda)}}\|f(2^{-\lambda k}\cdot)\|_{r}

\displaystyle=2^{-\lambda nk\frac{\rho-\lambda}{r(1-\lambda)}}2^{\lambda nk/r}\|f\|_{r}

\displaystyle=2^{\lambda nk\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\|f\|_{r}.

Thus, completing the proof.
∎

Now, let
P
be a concentric dilate of
Q
with
\ell(P)\geq 10\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)
and let
\chi_{P}
denote its characteristic function. Now, let us consider

(3.8)

f=f_{\chi_{P}}+f_{\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}}=:f_{0}+f_{1},

so that

T_{\sigma_{k}}f=T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}+T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
0<\rho<1
, let
1<r\leq 2
, and let
m=-n(1-\rho)/r
. Suppose that
x\in Q
.

(1)

Let
0<\rho<r/2
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then every symbol
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
satisfies

(3.9)

\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}\lesssim\left[\frac{\ell(P)}{\ell(Q)^{\rho}}\right]^{n/r}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

(2)

Let
r/2\leq\rho<1
, let
\frac{2\rho-r}{2-r}<\lambda<\rho
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then every symbol
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
satisfies

(3.10)

\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}\lesssim[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{\lambda n\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\left[\frac{\ell(P)}{\ell(Q)^{\rho}}\right]^{n/r}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

Proof.

First, let us consider the case
0<\rho<r/2
. By Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

3.3
we have that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}(y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Q|^{\rho/r}}\left\|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}\right\|_{r/\rho}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(Q)^{n\rho/r}}\|f\chi_{P}\|_{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\frac{\ell(P)}{\ell(Q)^{\rho}}\right]^{n/r}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

since
x\in Q\subset P
. Hence, proving
Eq.

3.9
. Now, let us assume that
r/2\leq\rho<1
. By Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

3.4
we obtain that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}(y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\leq{|Q|^{-\frac{\rho-\lambda}{r(1-\lambda)}}}\|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}\|_{\frac{r(1-\lambda)}{\rho-\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\ell(Q)^{-n\frac{\rho-\lambda}{r(1-\lambda)}}}2^{\lambda nk\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\|f\chi_{P}\|_{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\lambda nk\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\ell(Q)^{-n\frac{\rho-\lambda}{r(1-\lambda)}}\ell(P)^{n/r}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{\lambda n\frac{1-\rho}{n(1-\lambda)}}\left[\frac{\ell(P)}{\ell(Q)^{\rho}}\right]^{n/r}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

Thus, completing the proof.
∎

We now prove estimates for the second part of the decomposition as in
Eq.

3.8
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
0\leq\rho<1
, let
1\leq r\leq 2
, let
m=-n(1-\rho)/r
, and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Suppose that
x,y\in Q
. Then every
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
satisfies

(3.11)

|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)|\lesssim_{N}[2^{k\rho}\ell(P)]^{-(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

and

(3.12)

|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)-T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x)|\lesssim_{N}2^{k}\ell(Q)[2^{k\rho}\ell(P)]^{-(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

for any
N>n/r
.

Proof.

First, we consider
Eq.

3.11
. Let
N>n/r
, and let
y\in Q
. By Hölder’s inequality we obtain that

|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}|K_{k}(y,y-u)||f(u)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\leq\left\|\ell(P)^{n/r}\left[\frac{|\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{N}K_{k}(y,\cdot)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\left\|\ell(P)^{-n/r}\left[\frac{|y-\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{-N}f\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}\right\|_{r}.

The
L^{r^{\prime}}
-norm can be estimated using
Lemma

3.1
by

\displaystyle\left\|\ell(P)^{n/r}\left[\frac{|\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{N}K_{k}(y,\cdot)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\ell(P)^{-(N-n/r)}\left\||\cdot|^{N}K_{k}(y,\cdot)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\ell(P)^{-(N-n/r)}2^{-k\rho N}\left\|(1+2^{k\rho}|\cdot|)^{N}K_{k}(y,\cdot)\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell(P)^{-(N-n/r)}2^{-k\rho N}2^{k(m+n/r)}

\displaystyle=[2^{k\rho}\ell(P)]^{-(N-n/r)}.

On the other hand, the
L^{r}
-norm can be estimated by noting that for
x,y\in Q
, and for
u\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P
, we have that

|y-u|\geq|x-u|-|x-y|\geq C_{n}[\ell(P)+|x-u|].

Thus, for
N>n/r
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left\|\ell(P)^{-n/r}\left[\frac{|y-\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{-N}f\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}\right\|_{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\ell(P)^{-n}\left[1+\frac{|x-u|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{-rN}|f(u)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

Thus, we combine both estimates to obtain
Eq.

3.11
. Now, we consider
Eq.

3.12
using a similar proof as in the previous case, now defining

H_{k}(y,x,u):=K_{k}(y,y-u)-K_{k}(x,x-u).

Assume
x,y\in Q
and
N>n/r
. Using Hölder’s inequality, we have that

|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)-T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}|H_{k}(y,x,u)||f(y)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\leq\left\|\ell(P)^{n/r}\left[\frac{|y-\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{N}H_{k}(y,x,\cdot)\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\left\|\ell(P)^{-n/r}\left[\frac{|y-\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{-N}f\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}\right\|_{r}.

Let us estimate the first factor using
Lemma

3.1
by

\left\|\ell(P)^{n/r}\left[\frac{|y-\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{N}H_{k}(y,x,\cdot)\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\lesssim\left\|\ell(Q)\ell(P)^{n/r}\left[\frac{|y-\cdot|}{\ell(P)}\right]^{N}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla K_{k}(y(t),y(t)-\cdot)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P}\right\|_{r^{\prime}}

\lesssim\ell(Q)\ell(P)^{-(N-n/r)}\int_{0}^{1}\left\||y(t)-\cdot|^{N}|\nabla K_{k}(y(t),y(t)-\cdot)|\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\lesssim\ell(Q)\ell(P)^{-(N-n/r)}2^{-k\rho N}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|(1+2^{k\rho}|\cdot|)^{N}|\nabla K_{k}(y(t),\cdot)|\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\lesssim\ell(Q)\ell(P)^{-(N-n/r)}2^{-k\rho N}2^{k(1+m+n/r)}=C2^{k}\ell(Q)[2^{k\rho}\ell(P)]^{-(N-n/r)},

where
y(t):=ty+(1-t)x\in Q
so that
|y-x|\lesssim|y(t)-u|
for
u\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P
. For the second factor, we use the same estimate as in the previous case, completing the proof.
∎

Let us proceed to prove the main theorem of this paper, from which the desired continuity will follow.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
1<r\leq 2
, and let
0<\rho<1
. Suppose that
m\leq-n(1-\rho)/r
and
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\rho}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. Then we it holds that

\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{r}(T_{\sigma}f)(x)\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)

for
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Proof.

Notice that is suffices to prove

(3.13)

\inf_{c_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma}f(y)-c_{Q}|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

uniformly in
Q
and
x\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Now, let
P_{\rho}
be a concentric dilation of
Q
so that
\ell(P_{\rho})=10\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)^{\rho}
and let us decompose
f
as in
Eq.

3.8
. We will first consider the case when
0<\rho<r/2
. Then, the left hand side of
Eq.

3.13
is less than the sum of

\mathcal{I}_{0}:=\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma}f_{0}(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

and

\mathcal{I}_{1}:=\inf_{c_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma}f_{1}-c_{Q}|^{r}\right)^{1/r}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and
Lemma

3.3
we can verifiy that

\mathcal{I}_{0}\leq\frac{1}{|Q|^{\rho/r}}\|T_{\sigma}f_{0}\|_{r/\rho}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell(Q)^{n\rho/r}}\|f\chi_{P_{\rho}}\|_{r}\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

In order to estimate
\mathcal{I}_{1}
, let us set

(3.14)

c_{Q}:=\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)<1}T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x),

so we have that

|T_{\sigma}f_{1}(y)-c_{Q}|\leq\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)\geq 1}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)|+\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)<1}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)-T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x)|.

First, we estimate the first sum using
Eq.

3.11
by

\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)\geq 1}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)|\lesssim\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)\geq 1}[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{-\rho(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)\lesssim\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

as
\rho>0
and
N>n/r
. Then, we apply
Eq.

3.12
to estimate the remaining terms by

\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)<1}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)-T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x)|\lesssim_{N}\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)<1}[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{1-\rho(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)=C\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

when we choose
N<n/r+1/\rho
. Thus, completing the proof when
0<\rho<r/2
. Now, let us consider the case
r/2\leq\rho<1
. We can estimate
Eq.

3.13
by the sum of

\mathcal{J}_{1}:=\inf_{c_{Q}\in\mathbb{C}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\left|\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)<1}T_{\sigma_{k}}f(y)-c_{Q}\right|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r},

\mathcal{J}_{2}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k:2^{k}\ell(Q)\geq 1,\\
2^{\rho k}\ell(Q)<1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r},

\mathcal{J}_{3}:=\sum_{k:2^{\rho k}\ell(Q)\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}.

First, we set
\frac{2\rho-r}{2-r}<\lambda<\rho
for the remainder of this proof. Then, we use the same decomposition by
P_{\rho}
as above and use
c_{Q}
as defined in
Eq.

3.14
in order to estimate
\mathcal{J}_{1}
by

\sum_{k:2^{k}\ell(Q)<1}\left[\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}+\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)-T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}\right].

Using
Eq.

3.10
we obtain that

\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0}(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}\lesssim[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{\lambda n\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

Moreover, we employ
Eq.

3.12
to get that

|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(y)-T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1}(x)|\lesssim[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{1-\rho(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

Again, we choose
N<n/r+1/\rho
, to obtain an uniform bound and complete the proof for the first term. For the term
\mathcal{J}_{2}
, we choose a positive number
\varepsilon
such that

\lambda\left(\frac{1-\rho}{1-\lambda}\right)<\varepsilon<\rho,

which we know exists since
\lambda<\rho
. Now, let us define
P_{\varepsilon,k}
as the concentric dilate of
Q
with
\ell(P_{\varepsilon,k})=10\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)^{\rho}[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{-\varepsilon}
. Let us note that

10\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)\leq\ell(P_{\varepsilon,k}),

since it is equivalent to

2^{\rho k}\ell(Q)^{\frac{\rho(1-\rho+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}}\leq 1,

which is true as

\displaystyle\rho(1-\rho)

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon(1-\rho)

\displaystyle\frac{\rho(1-\rho+\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq 1,

and
2^{\rho k}\ell(Q)<1
. Then, we consider

f=f\chi_{P_{\varepsilon,k}}+f\chi_{\mathbb{T}^{n}\setminus P_{\varepsilon,k}}=:f_{0,k}+f_{1,k}.

Hence, by
Eq.

3.10
we have that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{0,k}(y)|^{r}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/r}

\displaystyle\lesssim[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{\lambda n\frac{1-\rho}{r(1-\lambda)}}\left[\frac{\ell(P_{\varepsilon,k})}{\ell(Q)^{\rho}}\right]^{n/r}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle=[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{-\frac{n}{r}\left(\varepsilon-\lambda\frac{1-\rho}{1-\lambda}\right)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x).

Furthermore, by
Eq.

3.11
we obtain that

\displaystyle|T_{\sigma_{k}}f_{1,k}(y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim[2^{k\rho}\ell(P_{\varepsilon,k})]\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(2^{k\rho}\ell(Q)^{\rho}[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{-\varepsilon}\right)^{-(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq[2^{k}\ell(Q)]^{-(\rho-\varepsilon)(N-n/r)}\mathrm{M}_{r}f(x),

where
N>n/r
. Hence, we have a uniform bound for the second term. Now, let us consider
\mathcal{J}_{3}
. For this case we will use
P
as the concentric dilate of
Q
such that
\ell(P)=10\sqrt{n}\ell(Q)
, and use the same decomposition as in

. Notice that since

In order to prove the

Theorem 3.8
.

[
4

Suppose that

(L^{p_{0}}(w_{0}),L^{p_{1}}(w_{1}))_{\theta}=L^{p}(w),

where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad w=w_{0}^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{p_{0}}}w_{1}^{\frac{p\theta}{p_{1}}}.

Now, we take advantage of
Eq.

2.2
and

3.7

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
0\leq\delta\leq\rho<1
, let
0<\rho<1
, let
1<r\leq 2
, and let
r\leq p<\infty
. Suppose that
m\leq-n(1-\rho)/r
and
\sigma\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n})

\|T_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)},

Proof.

First, let us consider the case

\|T_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|\mathcal{M}^{\#}_{r}(T_{\sigma}f)\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|\mathrm{M}_{r}f\|_{L^{p}(w)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(w)}.

Now, let us consider the case
p=r
. Then, for
w\in A_{1}
we know
[
23
, Corollary 7.6]
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

w^{1+\varepsilon}\in A_{1}.

For any
r<q_{0}<\infty

A_{p}
weights implies that
w^{1+\varepsilon}\in A_{q_{0}/r}

\|T_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{q_{0}}(w^{1+\varepsilon})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q_{0}}(w^{1+\varepsilon})}.

Moreover, from
[
13

we have that

\|T_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{q_{1}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}}},

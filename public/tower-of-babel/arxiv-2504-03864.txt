Mullineux map: d d -balanced partitions and d d -runner matrices
Pavel Turek

Abstract
. Let 1 < d < e 1<d<e be two coprime integers and let m e m_{e} denote the Mullineux map, which for e e prime describes tensor products of the irreducible modules of symmetric groups with the sign in characteristic e e . We prove that if λ \lambda is an e e -regular partition such that d d divides the arm length of any rim hook of λ \lambda of size divisible by e e , then m e ​ ( λ ) ′ m_{e}(\lambda)^{\prime} is a partition such that the arm length of any of its rim hooks of size divisible by e e is congruent to − 1 -1 modulo d d . We introduce a new parameter for partitions called the d d -runner matrix and show that if λ \lambda is as above, then the d d -runner matrices of λ \lambda and m e ​ ( λ ) ′ m_{e}(\lambda)^{\prime} agree. This determines m e ​ ( λ ) ′ m_{e}(\lambda)^{\prime} uniquely. We approach the whole problem combinatorially and take advantage of a new Abacus Mullineux Algorithm introduced in this paper. We also establish equivalent descriptions of the above partitions which provide an alternative version of the main result about the Mullineux map, which becomes particularly strong when d = 2 d=2 .

Background

In 1979 Mullineux
[
Mul79
]
introduced a combinatorial map
m_{e}
from the set of
e
-regular partitions of size
n
to itself. He conjectured that if
e
is a prime and
\lambda
is an
e
-regular partition of
n
, then over a field of characteristic
e
,
m_{e}(\lambda)
labels the irreducible module of the symmetric group
S_{n}
isomorphic to
D^{\lambda}\otimes\sgn
. This long-standing conjecture was finally proved by Ford and Kleshchev
[
FK97
]
in 1997, using Kleshchev’s modular branching rules
[
Kle95a
,
Kle95b
,
Kle96
]
. A slight reformulation of the Mullineux map
m_{e}
was then used to find irreducible modules of the alternating group in odd characteristic
[
For97
]
. Since then, different combinatorial algorithms for computing
m_{e}(\lambda)
have been found. Among others, there are algorithms by Xu
[
Xu97
]
, Brundan and Kujawa
[
BK03
]
, Fayers
[
Fay22
]
and Jacon
[
Jac23
]
. The algorithm we use in this paper is based on the map
J
defined by Xu
[
Xu97
]
(a shorter proof that the algorithm of Xu coincides with the composition of the Mullineux map followed by the conjugation is in
[
Xu99
]
). In 1998 Brundan
[
Bru98
]
found an algebraic interpretation of the Mullineux map for any
e>2
using an involution of the Hecke algebra in quantum characteristic
e
.

Unlike our results, most results about the Mullineux map concern its general properties rather than its behaviour when applied to particular partitions. An exception to this is the result by Paget
[
Pag06
]
who proves a simple behaviour of the Mullineux map when applied to partitions in RoCK blocks. Paget’s algebraic proof was later accompanied by a combinatorial proof by Fayers
[
Fay22
]
who established a more general runner removal result for the Mullineux map applicable to partitions in the RoCK blocks.

The definition of
E_{\mathcal{R}}(\gamma)
is motivated by the sets
E_{k}(\gamma)
introduced by Giannelli and Wildon
[
GW15
]
(defined for
d=2
), which can be partitioned by our ‘finer’ sets
E_{\mathcal{R}}(\gamma)
. In the paper, Giannelli and Wildon showed that under mild conditions there are columns of the decomposition matrix of a symmetric group whose sum is a vector whose non-zero entries are one and lie in the rows labelled by the partitions in
E_{k}(\gamma)
. They further analyse the set
E_{0}(\gamma)
in
[
GW16
]
with particular focus on the cases when the partitions in
E_{0}(\gamma)
have
e
-weight at most
2
. Our results provide more explanations for the connection between the sets of Giannelli and Wildon and the decomposition matrices as the Mullineux map is closely related to the decompositions matrices of symmetric groups — the partition
m_{e}(\lambda)^{\prime}
labels the least row with a non-zero entry in the column labelled by
\lambda
in the decomposition matrix.

Abacus Mullineux Algorithm

We need the following definition to describe the new Abacus Mullineux Algorithm presented here.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
B
be a
\beta
-set of a partition. The
leading beads
of
B
are beads
b_{0},b_{1},\dots
such that:

(i)

b_{0}
is the greatest bead of
B
,

(ii)

for
i\geq 0
the bead
b_{i+1}
is the greatest bead which is less or equal to
b_{i}-e
.

One can summarise the definition of leading beads as beads which satisfy

(3.1)

b_{0}>b_{0}-e\geq b_{1}>b_{1}-e\geq\dots

and there is no bead
b
such that
b_{i}<b\leq b_{i-1}-e
(for
i=0
the right inequality is discarded). Since
B
contains all integers which are less than some integer
l
, for some index
i\geq 0
we have
b_{i}=b_{i+1}+e=b_{i+2}+2e=\dots
. We refer to the least such index as the
stable index
of
B
. Equivalently, the stable index of
B
is the least index from which the sequence
(b_{i}+ie)_{i\geq 0}
becomes constant.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
B
be a
\beta
-set of a partition. Let

be its leading beads and
z
its stable index. We define the set
\Ms_{e}(B)
to be
\left(B\setminus\left\{b_{i}:i\geq 0\right\}\right)\cup\left\{b_{i}-e:i\geq 0\right\}
and the integer
\ms_{e}(B)
to be

.

From the definition of the leading beads,
\left\{b_{i}:i\geq 0\right\}
is a subset of
B
and the union in the definition of
\Ms_{e}(B)
is disjoint (if
b_{i}-e\in B
for some

, then
b_{i}-e=b_{i+1}
). An example of the leading beads and the maps

and

Let

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose that
\lambda\neq\mu
are
d
-skewed partitions with the same
e
-core and
d
-runner matrix. If
B
and
B^{\prime}
are as above and the greatest bead

of

does not lie in

, then the greatest bead
b^{\prime}_{0}
of
B^{\prime}
satisfies

Proof.

By changing
B
and

, without loss of generality we can assume that
b_{0}
lies on runner
0
. We consider the above four sequences and let
N\geq 0
be such that
l^{\prime}_{N+1}=b^{\prime}_{0}
in the above procedure. We define sequences
(\Delta_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
by
\Delta_{i}=\emp_{B}(l_{i+1}+1,r_{i})=r_{i}-l_{i+1}-1
and
(\Delta^{\prime}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
by
\Delta^{\prime}_{i}=\emp_{B^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}_{i}+1,r^{\prime}_{i})=r^{\prime}_{i}-l^{\prime}_{i}-1
. We also let
\epsilon_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(\Delta^{\prime}_{j}-\Delta_{j})
for
1\leq i\leq N+1
.

By simple induction on
i
and the definitions (i)–(v), we see that for
1\leq i\leq N+1
, the runner of
l^{\prime}_{i}
(and
l_{i}
) is congruent to
\epsilon_{i}
modulo
e
, and for
1\leq i\leq N
, the runner of
r^{\prime}_{i}
(and
r_{i}
) is congruent to
\epsilon_{i}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i}+1
modulo
e
. Similarly, for
i
in the corresponding range, one shows that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\emp_{B}(l_{i})\equiv\emp_{B^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv\emp_{B}(b_{0})+\epsilon_{i}\,(\textnormal{mod }d),\textnormal{ and}

(6.4)

\displaystyle\emp_{B}(r_{i})\equiv\emp_{B^{\prime}}(r^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv\emp_{B}(b_{0})+\epsilon_{i}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i}\,(\textnormal{mod }d).

We can rewrite two of these congruences as

(6.5)

\displaystyle\emp_{B}(l_{i}+1,b_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv-\epsilon_{i}\,(\textnormal{mod }d),\textnormal{ and}

(6.6)

\displaystyle\emp_{B}(r_{i}+1,b_{0})

\displaystyle\equiv-(\epsilon_{i}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i})\,(\textnormal{mod }d).

We now claim that all
\epsilon_{i}
are non-negative. If not, then pick the least
i
such that
\epsilon_{i}<0
(clearly
i>1
). From the definition

. By the minimal choice of

we get

. Thus

(sketched in

) lies between

and

and thus it is an empty space of

. It also lies on runner

by the earlier observation that

lies on the runner congruent to
\epsilon_{i-1}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i-1}+1
modulo
e
, and hence

. But this equals
\emp_{B}(f_{1}+1,r_{i-1})+\emp_{B}(r_{i-1}+1,b_{0})=\epsilon_{i-1}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i-1}+\emp_{B}(r_{i-1}+1,b_{0})
which is divisible by

Clearly,
\epsilon_{i}\leq\epsilon_{i-1}+\Delta^{\prime}_{i-1}
for all

, and thus using the previous claim, a simple induction on

[\epsilon_{j},\epsilon_{j}+\Delta^{\prime}_{j}]

. Using this, we prove by induction on

that

. If

, this is clear. For

\epsilon_{i}\in[\epsilon_{j},\epsilon_{j}+\Delta^{\prime}_{j}]

, which in turn yields
l^{\prime}_{i}<l^{\prime}_{1}

If not, then

and in turn

\epsilon_{i}\neq\epsilon_{j}
as otherwise, by the earlier observation about the runners of

. Since

, which is the runner of

does not divide

\displaystyle\emp_{B^{\prime}}(f_{2}+1,r^{\prime}_{j})+\emp_{B^{\prime}}(r^{\prime}_{j}+1,l^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle\epsilon_{j}+\Delta^{\prime}_{j}-\epsilon_{i}+\emp_{B^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}_{i})-\emp_{B^{\prime}}(r^{\prime}_{j})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle(\emp_{B^{\prime}}(l^{\prime}_{i})-\epsilon_{i})-(\emp_{B^{\prime}}(r^{\prime}_{j})-\epsilon_{j}-\Delta^{\prime}_{j}).

Both of the brackets are congruent to

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
\gamma
be an
e
-core partition and
\mathcal{R}
a
(d-1)\times e
matrix of non-negative integers. There is at most one partition
\lambda
which is
d
-skewed, has

-core
\gamma
and has
d
-runner matrix
\mathcal{R}
.

Proof.

Suppose this fails and take the lexicographically least

-skewed partition
\lambda
, such that there is a lexicographically smaller

-skewed partition
\mu
with the same
e
-core and
d
-runner matrix. Then take their
\beta
-sets

and

, respectively, such that both contain all non-positive integers. As
\lambda
is lexicographically greater than
\mu
, we have the following inequality between the largest beads of

and

:
b_{0}\geq b^{\prime}_{0}
. If they equal, then
\emp_{B}(b_{0})=\emp_{B^{\prime}}(b^{\prime}_{0})

and

can be removed from

and
B^{\prime}
, respectively, to get

-runner matrix, they are

-skewed, and

Thus

implies that

and, in particular,

. If

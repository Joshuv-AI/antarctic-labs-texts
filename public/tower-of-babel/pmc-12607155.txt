Retrospective observational study of right hemicolectomy with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy for appendiceal mucinous neoplasms at a single center over seven years
Authors: lanzhu Jiang, Yingchao Hu, Hao Zheng, Zilong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveRetrospective analysis of the safety and efficacy of radical right hemicolectomy combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy in the treatment of appendiceal mucinous tumors.MethodData were extracted from inpatient and outpatient medical records. Using R software (version 4.3.2), we collected all cases of appendiceal mucinous tumors treated with HIPEC at Jingzhou Hospital Affiliated to Yangtze University from February 2015 to February 2022 for statistical analysis and visualization. Survival analysis was performed using the ‘survival’ package, survival curves were plotted with ‘survminer’, and boxplots and percentage bar charts were generated using ‘ggplot2’.ResultThis study included 61 patients with appendiceal mucinous tumors, of whom 31 received postoperative hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) and 30 did not. At 2 years, recurrence occurred in 12.9% of the HIPEC group versus 46.7% of the control group (P = 0.004). The hazard ratio (HR) for recurrence-free survival (RFS) was 0.21 (95% CI: 0.07–0.63; P = 0.002). Overall survival (OS) at 2 years was 93.5% with HIPEC and 80.0% without, a difference that did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.147). Postoperative complication rates and baseline characteristics were comparable between groups.ConclusionOur findings demonstrate that radical right hemicolectomy combined with HIPEC significantly reduces postoperative recurrence rates in AMN patients while showing a non-significant trend toward improved overall survival.

Background

Appendiceal mucinous neoplasms (AMNs) are a heterogeneous group of tumors with increasing incidence [1]. Their nonspecific presentation often leads to misdiagnosis, and tumor rupture can result in intraperitoneal dissemination and pseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) [2], a rare but serious condition. Owing to the rarity of AMN-associated PMP, high-quality randomized controlled trials are scarce, and the optimal management strategy remains uncertain.

Cytoreductive surgery (CRS) combined with hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) has shown therapeutic benefit in several peritoneal malignancies, including colorectal, gastric, and ovarian cancers [3–6], as well as PMP. By delivering heated chemotherapy directly into the peritoneal cavity, HIPEC aims to eradicate residual tumor cells and reduce recurrence risk [7].

Given the limited evidence specific to AMN, this study retrospectively evaluated the impact of CRS plus HIPEC on recurrence-free survival (RFS), overall survival (OS), and postoperative outcomes in patients with appendiceal mucinous tumors.

Method

Patients

This study established rigorous selection criteria to ensure patient homogeneity. The inclusion criteria comprised: (1) availability of complete clinical records; (2) histopathological confirmation of appendiceal mucinous neoplasms (AMN) through intraoperative frozen section or postoperative pathological examination; (3) treatment with radical right hemicolectomy (either open or laparoscopic approach); (4) intraoperative peritoneal cancer index (PCI) score ≤ 11 with completeness of cytoreduction (CC) scores of 0–1; (5) no prior neoadjuvant radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy; and (6) completion of six cycles of postoperative XELOX regimen adjuvant chemotherapy. Exclusion criteria were: (1) concurrent primary malignancies; (2) combined multi-organ resection during surgery; (3) perioperative mortality; or (4) severe cardiac, hepatic, pulmonary, renal or cerebral dysfunction.

Between February 2015 and February 2022, AMN patients treated at Jingzhou Hospital Affiliated to Yangtze University who met these criteria were enrolled. The retrospective study design and reporting strictly adhered to the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines to ensure methodological rigor and result reliability [8].The study protocol received ethical approval from the Jingzhou Central Hospital Institutional Review Board (Approval No. 2023-086-01), with written informed consent obtained from all participants prior to surgical intervention in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Surgical procedures and postoperative treatment

All radical right hemicolectomies in this study were performed by board-certified senior surgeons using either laparoscopic or open approaches, in strict compliance with oncological principles that included D3 lymph node dissection and complete mesocolic excision (CME), with the aim of achieving complete cytoreduction (CC score 0–1). The decision to place intraoperative drainage tubes was made by the operating surgeon according to individual surgical conditions. Hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) was administered by certified perfusion specialists following a standardized protocol: patients typically underwent three treatment sessions on postoperative days 1, 3, and 5, though the number was reduced to two sessions in cases of significant intolerance. The HIPEC regimen consisted of raltitrexed (4 mg on day 1), lobaplatin (60 mg on day 3), and 5-fluorouracil (1 g on day 5). Quality control measures included strict adherence to NCCN surgical guidelines, real-time monitoring of perfusion parameters (temperature maintained at 43 °C), and the implementation of predefined protocols for managing chemotherapy-related toxicities, thereby ensuring both treatment efficacy and patient safety throughout the therapeutic process.

Prognosis

This study collected and analyzed multiple clinical parameters including age, preoperative BMI, serum tumor markers (CEA, CA125, CA19-9), duration of abdominal drainage and urinary catheterization, and postoperative pathological diagnosis. Surgical records were reviewed to document tumor size, Peritoneal Cancer Index (PCI) scores, and Completeness of Cytoreduction (CC) scores. Follow-up data were obtained through telephone/WeChat contact with patients or family members to record mortality. Other clinically relevant endpoints — specifically hospital length of stay, time to recovery of bowel function (e.g. time to first flatus or first bowel movement), 30-day readmission, and standardized measures of long-term morbidity — were not uniformly recorded across all patients in our retrospective dataset and therefore were not included in the current analysis.” Tumor recurrence was determined through hospital information system (HIS) records and confirmed by abdominal CT findings of peritoneal mucinous tumor deposits. All follow-up data were collected through February 2022.

Statistic analysis

All analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.2). Continuous variables with normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation and compared using independent t-tests, while non-normally distributed data were expressed as median [Q1, Q3] and analyzed with Mann-Whitney U tests. Categorical variables were summarized as counts (%) and compared using χ² tests or Fisher’s exact tests, as appropriate.

Kaplan-Meier survival curves with log-rank tests were generated to compare overall survival (OS) and recurrence-free survival (RFS) between treatment groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were employed to assess whether treatment assignment independently influenced OS and RFS, with results reported as hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals. Variables demonstrating P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were incorporated into the respective multivariate models.

The survival package facilitated survival analyses, while survminer and ggplot2 packages generated survival curves, boxplots, and percentage bar plots, respectively. A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Survival outcomes in appendiceal mucinous neoplasms

Recurrence-free survival (RFS) analysis

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated significantly improved RFS in the intervention group compared to controls at all evaluated timepoints: 93.5% versus 53.3% at 6 months, 87.1% versus 53.3% at 12 months, and 87.1% versus 53.3% at 24 months (all P < 0.05). The log-rank test confirmed this survival advantage across the entire follow-up period (P = 0.002), with the intervention group exhibiting a substantially reduced recurrence risk (HR = 0.21; 95% CI: 0.07–0.63; Fig. 1). Univariate Cox regression identified several significant predictors of RFS: treatment assignment (protective effect for the intervention group), elevated CA19-9 levels, higher peritoneal cancer index (PCI) scores, and mucinous adenocarcinoma/signet-ring cell carcinoma histology (risk factors) (Table 5). In the multivariate model adjusting for these covariates, the intervention remained an independent protective factor for RFS (HR = 0.12; 95% CI: 0.03–0.47; P = 0.002), while CA19-9 and PCI scores persisted as significant risk factors (Fig. 2). These results robustly demonstrate that the combined surgical/HIPEC approach significantly improves recurrence-free survival in AMN patients, even after accounting for other prognostic variables.

Discussion

Primary appendiceal tumors represent a rare subset of gastrointestinal malignancies, with epithelial-derived mucinous neoplasms (AMNs) characterized by excessive mucin production and frequent progression to pseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) [9, 10]. Owing to their low incidence, nonspecific presentation, and reliance on postoperative histopathology for diagnosis, clinical management remains controversial, with high recurrence rates after surgery alone [11–13]. Over the past decade, our center has accumulated experience in applying hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) for AMN patients undergoing radical right hemicolectomy.

All cases represented consecutive patients treated at our center. Assignment to the HIPEC or non-HIPEC group was determined by a combination of factors, including disease stage at diagnosis, extent of peritoneal dissemination (e.g., PCI score), general condition (ASA classification and performance status), presence of distant metastasis, as well as patient treatment preference and financial considerations. Thus, treatment decisions were jointly influenced by clinical assessment and patient preference.Such non-randomized allocation may introduce selection bias. Therefore, our conclusions should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory evidence. Our study demonstrated that the addition of HIPEC significantly reduced recurrence, with 2-year recurrence rates of 12.9% in the intervention group versus 46.7% in controls (P = 0.004). Kaplan–Meier analysis confirmed superior recurrence-free survival (RFS) at all evaluated timepoints (6-, 12-, and 24-month rates: 93.5%, 87.1%, and 87.1% vs. 53.3% in controls, log-rank P = 0.002; HR = 0.21, 95% CI: 0.07–0.63). Overall survival (OS) favored the HIPEC group numerically (6.5% vs. 20.0% mortality at 2 years), though differences did not reach statistical significance (P = 0.147), likely reflecting limited follow-up and inadequate power.

A limitation of our perioperative assessment is the absence of systematically recorded data for hospital length of stay, time to bowel function recovery, 30-day readmission rates, and standardized long-term morbidity. These outcomes are important for fully characterizing perioperative safety and healthcare resource use but were inconsistently documented in the retrospective records available for this study. We plan to include these endpoints in ongoing prospective data collection to provide a more comprehensive assessment of the clinical impact of HIPEC.

We acknowledge that the median follow-up of 24 months is relatively short for evaluating long-term oncologic outcomes in AMN. Although early recurrence is clinically informative and consistent with prior series showing that most recurrences occur within 2–3 years after CRS/HIPEC, AMN may also exhibit late recurrences beyond this period. Thus, the current follow-up duration limits our ability to fully capture the long-term recurrence risk. We plan to continue prospective follow-up and will report extended outcomes once ≥ 5-year data are available. Moreover, the OS analysis may have been underpowered to detect modest but clinically meaningful differences, and further follow-up with additional events will provide more definitive conclusions.

Important biological considerations were also identified. Elevated CA19-9 emerged as an adverse prognostic factor for both RFS and OS, in line with prior reports [14]linking tumor markers to aggressive biology. The peritoneal cancer index (PCI) significantly impacted both endpoints, underscoring the prognostic role of peritoneal tumor burden. Conversely, tumor size did not correlate with outcomes, consistent with prior findings suggesting limited prognostic utility of diameter alone. Given the small sample size, mucinous adenocarcinoma (MAC) and signet-ring cell carcinoma (SRC) were analyzed together; however, these subtypes are biologically distinct, and larger multicenter studies are warranted to validate histology-specific outcomes.

Another key point relates to HIPEC regimens. While oxaliplatin and mitomycin C are most frequently used in Western centers [15], our protocol (raltitrexed, lobaplatin, and 5-FU) reflects prevailing practice in several Asian institutions, where these drugs have demonstrated safety and feasibility under hyperthermic conditions [16–18]. Raltitrexed, a specific thymidylate-synthase inhibitor, has shown efficacy broadly comparable to 5-FU/leucovorin in randomized studies, but its toxicity profile differs (e.g. lower rates of mucositis in some trials but early reports of treatment-related toxicities that necessitate cautious dosing and monitoring [19]. Oxaliplatin and mitomycin-C are both used in Western HIPEC protocols and have been reported to provide comparable long-term outcomes in selected series; oxaliplatin’s cumulative sensory neuropathy is a well-recognized toxicity that may influence regimen choice depending on clinical priorities [20]. Given these pharmacologic and safety differences, cross-regional comparisons of toxicity rates and dose-intensity should be made cautiously; direct head-to-head trials or well-controlled comparative effectiveness studies would be needed to definitively establish equivalence or superiority between these approaches)

Finally, our results should be interpreted in the context of current guidelines and recent series. The PSOGI/EURACAN consensus guidelines emphasize complete cytoreduction as the cornerstone of treatment for AMN with peritoneal dissemination and recognize HIPEC as a valuable adjunct in selected patients PSOGI/EURACAN guidelines [21, 22], while acknowledging ongoing evidence gaps. Contemporary high-volume studies, including reports on repeat CRS/HIPEC, have described recurrence and survival patterns similar to ours, although direct comparisons are limited by differences in regimens, dosing, and follow-up. Collectively, these findings support the potential oncologic benefit of aggressive locoregional strategies, while highlighting the need for randomized trials and long-term follow-up to refine patient selection and optimize HIPEC protocols.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that HIPEC combined with radical resection improves recurrence-free survival in patients with AMNs without increasing complication rates. Although limited by retrospective design, small sample size, and short follow-up, these findings add to the growing body of evidence supporting HIPEC as a valuable component of multimodal management. Future prospective, multicenter trials with extended follow-up are required to validate these observations, clarify subtype-specific outcomes, and determine the optimal HIPEC regimen for long-term oncologic benefit.

Conclusion

Right hemicolectomy with HIPEC for AMN does not prolong the indwelling time of urinary catheters or abdominal drains, nor does it increase the risk of complications including fever, pulmonary infection, surgical site infection, intestinal obstruction, anastomotic leakage, anastomotic bleeding, intra-abdominal infection, myelosuppression, urinary calculi, or delayed gastric emptying. Importantly, this combined treatment significantly reduces tumor recurrence and improves recurrence-free survival. Although a trend toward improved overall survival was observed, the difference was not statistically significant in the current cohort with a median follow-up of 24 months. Therefore, conclusions regarding OS should be interpreted with caution. Longer follow-up and larger, adequately powered comparative studies are needed to determine whether the recurrence-free survival benefit ultimately translates into an overall survival advantage in AMN patients.

On the Subtractive Ideal Structure of Commutative Semirings
Pubali Sengupta, Amartya Goswami, Pronay Biswas, Sujit Kumar Sardar

Abstract
. In the theory of commutative semirings, the lack of additive inverses creates a structural divergence between ideals and congruences that does not exist in ring theory. The aim of this article is to restore critical ideal-theoretic properties via the subtractive property. We first prove a subtractive analogue of Krull’s existence theorem, guaranteeing the existence of k k -prime ideals disjoint from multiplicative sets. We show that in arithmetic semirings, the distinction between k k -irreducible and k k -strongly irreducible ideals vanishes, a coherence that we show is preserved under localisation. We investigate the structural properties and coincidence phenomena among associated subclasses of k k -ideals in Laskerian semirings, von Neumann regular semirings, unique factorisation semidomains, principal ideal semidomains, and weakly Noetherian semirings. Finally, within the framework of additively idempotent semirings, we tether subtractive ideal-theoretic structures to underlying order-theoretic constraints, thereby obtaining new characterizations of k k -prime and k k -semiprime ideals. In that process, we also establish that every absolutely k k -prime ideal is k k -prime and every k k -maximal ideal is absolutely k k -prime.

\mathcal{C}_{k}(\langle x\rangle)=S

As in the case of rings, the correspondence between prime ideals and

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

be a multiplicatively closed set of a semiring

be a

-ideal maximal among the

-ideals disjoint from

is

-prime.

Proof.

To prove

is

-prime, we need to show that

is prime. Let

such that

,

. Consider the

-ideal
\mathcal{C}_{k}(I+\langle a\rangle)
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{k}(I+\langle a\rangle)
is a
k
-ideal properly containing

, and therefore, intersects

. So, there exists
s_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(I+\langle a\rangle)

s_{1}+i_{1}+x_{1}a=i_{2}+x_{2}a
, for some

,

as
i_{1}
,
i_{2}
,

. Similarly,
\mathcal{C}_{k}(I+\langle b\rangle)

-ideal properly containing

, and so, it intersects

. Therefore, there exists
s_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{k}(I+\langle b\rangle)

, for some

,

, which implies

,
j_{2}
,

. We have,

(s_{1}+i_{1}+x_{1}a)(s_{2}+j_{1}+y_{1}b)=(i_{2}+x_{2}a)(j_{2}+y_{2}b).

Thus

s_{1}s_{2}+s_{1}+s_{1}j_{1}+y_{1}s_{1}b+i_{1}(s_{2}+j_{1}+y_{1}b)+x_{1}s_{2}a+j_{1}x_{1}a+x_{1}y_{1}ab=i_{2}(j_{2}+y_{2}b)+j_{2}x_{2}a+x_{2}y_{2}ab.

Therefore, we get

Although

-primary and

), the following theorem shows that this distinction disappears for von Neumann regular semirings.

Theorem 3.9
.

In a von Neumann regular semiring, the classes of

-prime and

-primary ideals coincide.

Proof.

Let

be a von Neumann regular semiring and

be a

-prime ideal of

. Then

is a

-ideal of

. Therefore,

or

, which further implies

is

-primary.

or

, for some natural number

is prime. Now, let

. This implies

Motivated by the correspondence between strongly irreducible ideals and

Let

(2)

(1)

(2)

As observed in Remark

,

-strongly irreducible and

-irreducible ideals may differ in general. In the sequel, we show that they coincide for a special class of semirings, after introducing the necessary definition.

Definition 3.12
.

A semiring

is said to be an

if

, for any ideals

,

Proposition 3.13
.

The following are equivalent for a semiring

.

1.

S
is an arithmetic semiring.

2.

For any

,

,

,
\langle a\rangle\cap(\langle b\rangle+\langle c\rangle)=(\langle a\rangle\cap\langle b\rangle)+(\langle a\rangle\cap\langle c\rangle)

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): Suppose (2) holds. Let

,

, and

be any elements of

. Then

is always true in any lattice. Let

. Therefore

and

, for some

,

, and

. Then, by the hypothesis, we have

Hence

.
∎

From Theorem

, we know that all strongly subtractive semirings are arithmetic. In the next theorem, we show that the classes of

-strongly irreducible and

-irreducible ideals coincide in arithmetic semirings.

Theorem 3.14
.

In an arithmetic semiring

, a

-ideal

is

-strongly irreducible if and only if it is

-irreducible.

Proof.

We know that every

-strongly irreducible ideal is

-irreducible.
To prove the converse part, let

be a

-irreducible ideal of

. Let

, where

and

are two

-ideals of

. As

. Then

Therefore, we have

or

. This further implies

Proposition 4.1

Let

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

,
\varphi(b)\notin J
. Since

(3)

Lemma 5.4

,

Lemma 5.6
.

6.

We begin by recalling two important classes of semirings, obtained as natural generalizations of the corresponding ring-theoretic notions.

Definition 6.1

In the results that follow, we analyse the interplay between

-strongly irreducible ideal of

-primary ideal.

(1) If

is

, where

,

Therefore,

or
B\subseteq I
, as

is
k
-strongly irreducible. Hence

is

-prime.

(2) Let

be a

-strongly irreducible ideal of

be a

-primary decomposition of

, which further implies

. Thus

is

-primary.

(3) In a von Neumann regular semiring,

-primary ideals are the same, by Theorem

-strongly irreducible ideal of

,

,

,
m_{2}\in S

, for

,

Lemma 6.4

Theorem 6.5
.

Let

be a unique factorisation semidomain and let

-proper ideal of

(1)

is

-strongly irreducible if and only if for each

or

(1) Let

-strongly irreducible ideal of

(2) Suppose

. Let

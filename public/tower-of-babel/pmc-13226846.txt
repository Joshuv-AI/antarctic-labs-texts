Step-by-step type III total pelvic exenteration with vaginectomy and partial vulvectomy with complex pelvic reconstruction for vaginal recurrence of cervical cancer
Authors: C Garin, M Daix, L Bresson, A-S Navarro, S Schoenen, P-A Giroux, G Marcq, F Narducci, C Martinez-Gomez
Published: 2026

Total pelvic exenteration remains one of the few potentially curative options for selected patients with locally recurrent gynecologic malignancies after radiotherapy. However, this procedure is technically demanding and often requires advanced reconstructive strategies to restore pelvic anatomy and preserve functional outcomes (Sardain et al., 2014).

We present the case of a woman in her forties with a history of international federation of gynecology and obstetrics stage IIB cervical squamous cell carcinoma treated with concurrent chemoradiotherapy followed by brachytherapy in 2022. During follow-up in March 2024, vaginal biopsies revealed recurrence of squamous cell carcinoma involving all vaginal walls. Clinical examination under general anesthesia demonstrated circumferential vaginal involvement extending to the vaginal introitus, with periurethral extension and suspected invasion of the rectovaginal septum. Pelvic magnetic resonance imaging confirmed a 4-cm recurrence with suspected extension to the anal canal and levator ani muscles. Fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography showed no evidence of distant metastases or suspicious lymph nodes.

Following multidisciplinary tumor board discussion, curative salvage surgery was proposed. This video demonstrates step-by-step type III infralevator total pelvic exenteration according to the Magrina classification, including total vaginectomy and partial left vulvectomy, performed through a combined abdominal and perineal approach (Magrina et al., 1997, Querleu et al., 2021). (Fig. 1) Particular attention is paid to reducing tumor spillage by applying the principles of the no-touch isolation technique. A purse-string suture closes the vaginal lumen to bury the tumor and allow safe en-bloc resection (Turnbull et al., 1967). Urinary reconstruction is performed using a Bricker ileal conduit. Digestive continuity is restored with a hand-sewn coloanal anastomosis protected by a transverse diverting colostomy. The pelvic defect is filled with pedicled omentoplasty and perineal reconstruction performed using a pedicled deep inferior epigastric perforator.

Perineal phase of total pelvic exenteration with vaginectomy.

Final histopathology revealed a well-differentiated invasive squamous cell carcinoma of the vagina measuring 30 × 15 mm, with negative margins and no lymphovascular invasion. At two years of follow-up, the patient remains disease-free without significant postoperative complications.

This case highlights the importance of preventing tumor spillage during salvage surgery and the value of a combined approach in previously irradiated fields to achieve complete resection with negative margins. Anticipating complex reconstruction helps reduce early and long-term complications and preserve quality of life.

A comparative study of large language models (ChatGPT/DeepSeek) in generating structured thyroid ultrasound reports
Authors: Li Zhang, Xin Li, Rui Qi, Siyu Wang, Xue Shi, Yang Song, Ling Li, Niya Zhang, Huiyu Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The importance of structured radiology reports is well recognized for enabling efficient data extraction and facilitating multidisciplinary collaboration. This study aimed to evaluate the agreement rate and reproducibility of the large language models ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 in generating structured thyroid ultrasound reports. This study retrospectively included 174 thyroid ultrasound reports from 174 patients, encompassing a total of 230 nodules. ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 were used to convert the reports into structured formats according to the C-TIRADS guidelines. Two ultrasonographers assessed the agreement rate of the generated nodule classifications and the appropriateness of the management recommendations. Each report was submitted twice to evaluate the consistency of nodule classification and management suggestions. Among 174 patients (mean age 44 ± 11 years; 32 males), there was no significant difference in nodule classification agreement rate between ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 (80.4% vs. 77.2%; OR = 1.636; 95% CI: 0.976–2.741; P = 0.205). ChatGPT-4o outperformed DeepSeek-R1 in providing more comprehensive or correct management recommendations (OR = 7.362, 95% CI: 4.255–12.735, P < 0.001). Furthermore, both ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 demonstrated moderate consistency in nodule classification (AC1 = 0.767 vs. 0.713). Specifically, for category 3 nodules, both models showed high consistency (AC1 = 0.983 vs. 0.929). Compared to DeepSeek-R1, ChatGPT-4o exhibited higher consistency in providing management recommendations (AC1 = 0.809 vs. AC1 = 0.632). This study suggests that both ChatGPT and DeepSeek showed potential for converting free-text thyroid ultrasound reports into a structured format. While ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 performed similarly in nodule classification agreement rate, ChatGPT-4o demonstrated a clear advantage in the agreement rate of management recommendations.

Introduction

Over the past few decades, traditional radiology reporting systems have shown significant limitations. Numerous studies have reported that free-text radiology reports are not only time-consuming to produce but also suffer from inconsistent terminology, ambiguous phrasing, and structural heterogeneity, all of which significantly reduce their readability and clinical utility1,2. With the rapid development of artificial intelligence, its applications in medicine have become a major research focus. In particular, large language models (LLMs) based on the Transformer architecture, such as ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1, have shown strong capability in medical text processing tasks3–5. These models can support disease classification and diagnosis, physician-patient communication simulation, medical report generation, and literature summarization6,7.

Although prior studies have confirmed the feasibility of using LLMs to generate structured ultrasound reports for breast and thyroid nodules8–10, the automated structuring of free-text reports in Chinese remains a significant research frontier. Notably, the DeepSeek model has attracted attention due to its strong performance in Chinese-language processing, but its value in thyroid nodule diagnosis has not been fully evaluated11.

The internationally used ACR-TIRADS system has inherent limitations: for example, the estimated malignancy risk for category 5 nodules is only > 20%12. Considering the limited adoption of thyroid fine-needle aspiration in China and the fact that clinical decisions are primarily based on ultrasound characteristics, the Chinese Medical Association has established the C-TIRADS , which is more suitable for the domestic context13. C-TIRADS offers several advantages, the inclusion of a specific category for follicular thyroid tumors, the use of simplified malignancy criteria with only five key ultrasound features: solid composition, microcalcifications, marked hypoechogenicity, irregular or blurred margins or extrathyroidal extension, and a taller-than-wide shape, which improves diagnostic efficiency. It also provides higher diagnostic performance, with a malignancy risk exceeding 90% for category 5 nodules13. Furthermore, this structured reporting system markedly enhances report consistency and facilitates multidisciplinary collaboration14. However, manually converting free-text reports into a structured format remains time-consuming, highlighting the need for automated solutions.

Based on the above background, this study aims to compare the performance differences between two LLMs (ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1) in the following aspects: (1) converting thyroid ultrasound reports into structured formats according to C-TIRADS standards; (2) generating accurate classifications and guideline-based management recommendations from free-text reports.

Materials and methods

This retrospective study was approved by our institution’s ethics committee and was granted a waiver of informed consent (No. 2025-12-30-4). The workflow is shown in Fig. 1. We collected 174 thyroid ultrasound reports from Beijing Chaoyang Hospital, Capital Medical University, between August 2024 to December 2024, comprising detailed ultrasound characteristic descriptions of 230 nodules, without predefined exclusion criteria. From these 174 free-text thyroid ultrasound reports, 25 were selected to develop prompts to ensure consistent output, including thyroid parenchyma background, nodule characteristics, classification assessment, and management recommendations. Each report contained one or more nodules that could be classified into different categories. Finally, the original ultrasound reports were processed by sending requests to the online interfaces of ChatGPT-4o (version May 13, 2024) and Deepseek-R1 (version January 20, 2025) with the prompt: “Please use the following text to generate a structured ultrasound report in ultrasound medicine, including patient information, clinical symptoms, ultrasound findings, nodule category, and management recommendations based on C-TIRADS:” followed by the free-text of the original ultrasound report. Each free-text report was submitted to both ChatGPT-4o and Deepseek-R1, with two independent per model. The outputs were recorded for analysis (Fig. 2).

To ensure an unbiased evaluation, a dynamic exemplar-set strategy based on leave-one-out validation was adopted15. When a test sample belongs to one of the 25 preselected exemplars, that sample is temporarily removed from the exemplar set, and the remaining 24 were used for evaluation. For all other samples, the full set of 25 exemplars is retained.

The agreement rate of nodule classification was independently evaluated by two junior-to-mid-level ultrasound physicians (ZL and LX). For each nodule, the classification was assessed as either correct or incorrect. In parallel, management recommendations were evaluated. Any discrepancies between the two reviewers were resolved based on the opinion of a third ultrasound expert (GHY) with over 25 years of experience. Inter-rater reproducibility between the two reviewers was calculated.

All of these evaluations were completed in March 2025, and the versions of Deepseek-R1 and ChatGPT-4o remained unchanged during the study period. Additionally, the consistency of the classifications and management recommendations provided by Deepseek-R1 and ChatGPT-4o was assessed. In this study, consistency was defined as follows: for each thyroid nodule, if the classification or management recommendation generated by both Deepseek-R1 and ChatGPT-4o was simultaneously correct or simultaneously incorrect, the two responses were considered consistent16,17.

Statistics and analysis

Study results were presented as percentages. The thyroid nodule was defined as the primary unit of analysis. To ensure statistical rigor and account for the paired nature of the data, the analysis was conducted across three dimensions:

1. Comparison with the Reference Standard.

The assessment provided by an ultrasound expert (GHY) served as the reference standard. The performance of DeepSeek-R1 and ChatGPT-4o was evaluated based on the agreement rate (the frequency with which model outputs matched the expert’s classification). For inter-model performance comparisons, only the first run of each model was utilized to maintain the independence of observations. McNemar’s test was applied to compare the agreement rates between the two models, with odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) reported.

2. Intra-model Reproducibility.

To assess the output consistency within each model, Gwet’s AC1 coefficient was used to evaluate the level of agreement between the two independent runs of the same model. These repeated runs were used exclusively for this reproducibility analysis and were not treated as independent observations in any inferential performance testing.

3. Inter-model Agreement.

Gwet’s AC1 coefficient was further employed to quantify the agreement between DeepSeek-R1 and ChatGPT-4o. Inter-model agreement was assessed for thyroid nodule classification and management recommendations.

Interpretation and Significance.

For all AC1 analyses, agreement was interpreted as poor (< 0.40), moderate (0.40–0.60), good (0.61–0.80), and excellent (> 0.80) according to Landis and Koch criteria18–20. Classification outcomes were analyzed as dichotomous variables: (a) agreement with expert assessment (correct vs. incorrect); and (b) management recommendation quality (fully correct vs. partially or fully incorrect)10. A two-sided P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Data analysis was performed using Python (v3.9) and SPSS (v23.0) statistical software. Agreement analysis was conducted using Python, while intergroup comparisons were performed with SPSS.

In the stratified analysis across different nodule categories, ChatGPT-4o consistently showed higher classification agreement than DeepSeek-R1,though none of the differences reached statistical significance. Agreement was high for category 3 nodules in both models (ChatGPT-4o: 97.5% vs. DeepSeek-R1: 95.8%), while performance was relatively lower for Category 4A nodules (ChatGPT-4o: 65.2% vs. DeepSeek-R1: 60.9%). For Category 4B nodules, agreement rate improved and was similar between the two models (ChatGPT-4o: 68.4% vs. DeepSeek-R1: 67.1%). Both LLMs demonstrated better performance for Category 4C nodules (ChatGPT-4o: 80.2% vs. DeepSeek-R1: 75.2%). For Category 5 nodules, performance was comparable with a slight advantage for ChatGPT-4o (ChatGPT-4o: 66.7% vs. DeepSeek-R1: 61.1%). Detailed data are presented in Table 1; Fig. 3.

As shown in Table 3, intra-model reproducibility (agreement between two runs of the same model) for nodule classification was slightly lower for DeepSeek-R1 (AC1 = 0.713) than for ChatGPT-4o (AC1 = 0.767), with both models showing moderate overall reproducibility (both P < 0.001). Specifically, for Category 3 nodules, both models showed excellent agreement (AC1 = 0.983 vs. 0.929, both P < 0.001); for Category 4A nodules, agreement was moderate (AC1 = 0.544 vs. 0.522, both P = 0.001); for Category 4B (AC1 = 0.617 vs. 0.710, both P < 0.001) and 4C nodules (AC1 = 0.789 vs. 0.702, both P < 0.001), both models demonstrated good agreement; while for Category 5 nodules, ChatGPT-4o showed very excellent agreement (AC1 = 0.864, P < 0.001), whereas DeepSeek-R1 showed poor agreement (AC1 = 0.321, P = 0.411).

Further comparison showed that ChatGPT-4o achieved higher AC1 values than DeepSeek-R1 for category 3, 4A, 4C, and 5 nodules. In contrast, DeepSeek-R1 showed higher agreement for category 4B nodules. Notably, in Category 5 nodules, ChatGPT-4o outperformed DeepSeek-R1 (0.864 vs. 0.321). Table 4 presented for nodule classification, based on the first run only. Regarding inter-model consistency, Category 3 nodules showed excellent agreement, Categories 4A and 5 demonstrated moderate agreement, while Categories 4B and 4C exhibited good agreement (details in Table 4).

Summary of errors

In the evaluation of free-text ultrasound reports, scoring errors were identified in 14 sonographic features (Fig. 4). DeepSeek-R1 exhibited scoring errors in 13 of these features (all except microcalcifications), and the error patterns differed between its two runs. The main errors included: inappropriate additional scoring for cystic or mixed cystic-solid nodules with intranodular papillary projections, hypoechogenicity, blood flow signals, cyst wall thickening, acoustic attenuation, nodule fusion, and coarse calcifications; repeated scoring for multiple margin-related features; failure to evaluate lymph nodes; and omission of scoring for solid composition and marked hypoechogenicity. Moreover, in some cases, DeepSeek-R1 produced correct feature scores but still yielded incorrect nodule classifications.

In comparison, ChatGPT-4o made scoring errors in 10 of the 14 features, mainly involving blood flow signals, cyst wall thickening, acoustic attenuation, and nodule fusion. Notably, ChatGPT-4o showed fewer errors than DeepSeek-R1 in identifying “solid” and “cystic-solid” features (21 vs. 34), but more errors in hypoechogenicity, margin characteristics, coarse calcifications, halo signs, lymph node evaluation, microcalcifications, and intranodular papillary projections. Discussion

In recent years, machine learning technologies have shown substantial potential in medical imaging. Ultrasound examination, due to its high resolution, non-invasiveness, and accessibility, has become a commonly used imaging modality for a variety of diseases. The thyroid gland, with its superficial anatomical location and homogeneous background echogenicity, presents distinct sonographic features, making it particularly suitable for the application of machine learning techniques in radiomics feature extraction. This study applied deep learning–based natural language processing models (ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1) to feature extraction and structured generation from free-text ultrasound diagnostic reports, enabling an automated workflow from sonographic feature recognition and classification to management recommendations.

The results showed that, in the analysis of 230 thyroid nodules (460 responses), ChatGPT-4o demonstrated numerically higher classification agreement than DeepSeek-R1 (OR = 1.636, P = 0.205). Although the difference did not reach statistical significance, this result may be related to differences in the scale and quality of the training datasets used by the two models. Since ChatGPT-4o was publicly released earlier than DeepSeek, its training corpus may contain broader thyroid nodule–related medical content, which could have contributed to the observed difference. These findings also suggest areas for further optimization of DeepSeek-R1 in thyroid nodule–related tasks21.

Stratified analysis revealed that, except for category 3 nodules, both models showed relatively lower agreement rate for category 4A and category 5 nodules (approximately 60%–80%). Several factors may contribute to this phenomenon. First, different model versions may have integrated diagnostic standards from different regions or periods (e.g., ACR TI-RADS vs. Kwak TI-RADS), potentially causing conflicts in feature weighting. Second, the models’ generalization ability in understanding medical terminology is limited, and excessive reliance on instructions may occasionally introduce unsupported details22. Third, current models cannot fully replicate clinician reasoning in borderline cases. For instance, in the classification of category 4 A nodules, clinicians often perform subjective downgrading or upgrading based on patient communication and risk management considerations, a type of clinical judgment that models currently lack. Notably, in the assessment of category 5 nodules, ChatGPT demonstrated a higher AC1 value than DeepSeek-R1 (0.864 vs. 0.321), although this difference may be influenced by the small sample size.

ChatGPT-4o also showed higher agreement in generating management recommendations (OR = 7.362, P < 0.001), together with greater reproducibility (AC1 = 0.809 vs. 0.632). This advantage may be related to a longer development cycle, more extensive optimization, and stronger instruction-following performance in clinical text generation[16, 23]. In contrast, for DeepSeek-R1 to achieve performance comparable to ChatGPT-4o, further advancements in specialized training with medical corpora and algorithm optimization are likely required.

In the error analysis of free-text sonographic feature extraction, ChatGPT-4o demonstrated a lower overall error rate than DeepSeek-R1, suggesting a comparative advantage in feature recognition. Specifically, ChatGPT-4o showed scoring inaccuracies concentrated in features such as hypoechogenicity and coarse calcifications. This particular error pattern may be attributed to its initial training dataset being predominantly grounded in the ACR TI-RADS criteria. In contrast, DeepSeek-R1 exhibited higher error rates in characterizing solid/mixed cystic-solid composition, assessing blood flow signals, and evaluating margin features. Furthermore, it demonstrated instances of feature hallucination and inconsistent application of classification standards. These findings suggest limitations in domain-specific training depth and precision in interpreting specialized terminology.

This study has several limitations. First, senior ultrasonographers’ assessments were used as the reference standard rather than histopathological confirmation, which may have introduced classification bias. This approach may also have carried forward any potential underclassification or overclassification present in the original clinical reports into the performance estimates. Second, the relatively small sample size may have limited statistical power. Third, only two widely used LLMs were evaluated, and additional models were not included for comparison. Fourth, although Gwet’s AC1 coefficient was used to assess intra-model reproducibility and inter-model agreement separately, the limited number of nodules in certain categories (e.g., category 5) may have affected the stability of AC1 estimates. Fifth, all data were derived from a single center (Beijing Chaoyang Hospital); therefore, caution is warranted when generalizing the results to other institutions. In addition, the thyroid ultrasound reports included in this study were obtained from a relatively standardized reporting system. In clinical settings with lower report completeness, missing key feature descriptions, or inconsistent terminology, model performance may decline. Before implementation in such settings, prompt refinement using local datasets and scenario-specific exemplar templates may improve alignment with local reporting patterns and enhance model robustness. Larger studies are needed to validate these findings.

In summary, this study suggests that deep LLMs have potential for generating structured thyroid ultrasound reports. Although both ChatGPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 are capable language models, limitations remain in the agreement of sonographic feature extraction and nodule classification. Future research should focus on several areas: expanding sample size to improve the reliability of findings; including additional models for comparison to better characterize the strengths and limitations of different approaches; and developing domain-specific LLMs for medical applications to improve feature extraction and classification consistency.

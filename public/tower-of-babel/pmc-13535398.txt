60 Germline Platelet Polygenic Risk Score Predicts Renal Cell Carcinoma Survival via Sustained Thrombocytosis
Authors: Mustafa Saleh, Eddy Saad, Pablo Barrios, Wassim Daoud Khatoun, Jad El Masri, Marc Machaalani, Liliana Ascione, Clara Steiner, Rashad Nawfal, Razane El Hajj Chehade, Marc Eid, Elio Ibrahim, Gaelle Nafeh, Shaye Carver, Zeyun Lu, Emre Yekeduz, Tarek Baroud, Sara Sarrout, Ali Hajj Ali, Sylvan Baca, Alexander Gusev, Toni Choueiri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPolygenic risk scores (PGS) reflect inherited variants fixed from conception and are measured early in life. While a renal cell carcinoma (RCC) PGS has been developed for incident risk, its relevance to prognosis and treatment outcomes has not been established. Moreover, several routine hematologic measures, partly under germline control, are known to be prognostic in RCC, yet the extent to which their PGS influences survival remains unexplored. We aimed to determine whether germline PGS for hematologic traits are associated with survival in RCC.MethodsWe studied 495 patients with RCC who underwent tumor sequencing and analyzed overall survival (OS) using left-truncated Cox proportional hazards regression with hematologic PGS and technical covariates. We performed survival analyses with different time origins: (1) from diagnosis (left-truncated at sequencing), (2) from sequencing, and (3) from ICI initiation. 321 PGS of hematological measures were imputed from the PGS Catalog.ResultsAcross 321 CBC-related scores, two PGS met the threshold for false discovery rate (FDR) signiﬁcance for OS in RCC: a PRS for higher platelet (PLT) counts (PGS002660) was associated with worse survival (Hazard Ratio [HR] 1.37, Conﬁdence Interval [CI]: 1.17 - 1.62, q = 0.027), whereas a PRS for higher RDW (PGS004826) was protective (HR 0.74, CI: 0.63 - 0.87, q = 0.027). PLT PGS showed clear fidelity, capturing its intended biological trait (R² = 0.06, p = 0.001 in RCC; 0.03, p = 3x10-51 pan-cancer). The PRS survival association was attenuated by 51% after adjusting for time-varying platelet counts measured longitudinally, while PLT remained strongly adverse (HR 1.40, CI: 1.29 - 1.58, p = 1.1x10-11), suggesting the effect operates through PLT levels. Tumor mutational burden did not modify PGS effects, consistent with prior observations of limited prognostic utility in RCC. Pan-cancer analyses demonstrated that PLT PGS effects during immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy were signiﬁcant exclusively in RCC among 17 cancer types.ConclusionsGermline determinants of PLT are associated with RCC survival and may operate predominantly through sustained elevation of circulating PLT rather than independent molecular pathways. These ﬁndings establish platelet PGS as a heritable instrument that mechanistically links constitutional biology to longitudinal hematologic parameters and clinical outcomes. The integration of PLT PGS offers potential to reﬁne risk stratiﬁcation and patient selection for RCC therapy.DOD CDMRP FundingnoKeywords: Germline Architecture, Polygenic Risk Scores, Biomarkers, Renal cell carcinoma, Survival prediction

Chronic spontaneous urticaria as an autoimmune feature in common variable immunodeficiency
Authors: ECA Korevaar, AF Karim, VASH Dalm
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn patients with chronic spontaneous urticaria, especially when it is accompanied by recurrent infections or reduced immunoglobulin levels, analysis for common variable immunodeficiency should be considered. Early diagnosis of common variable immunodeficiency can lead to more effective treatment for the immunodeficiency and its related complications such as chronic spontaneous urticaria.Key words: CVID, CSU, autoimmune, immunoglobulins, GL-ILD, IVIG, omalizumab

Common variable immunodeficiency (CVID) is an inborn error of immunity, with an estimated prevalence of 1:25,000. It is characterized by antibody deficiency and dysfunction. Clinically, patients have an increased risk of infections (in particular, infections of the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts).1 Beyond infections, CVID may also present with noninfectious complications, including autoimmune disease, autoinflammatory complications, allergies, and malignancies.2 Chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) is characterized by the persistent presence of urticaria and angioedema.3 Although the link between CSU and CVID remains largely unexplained, it has been hypothesized that CSU in this context could arise from infection-driven complement activation, which is a consequence of impaired humoral immunity. CVID-related autoimmunity and mast cell activation likely result from B-cell dysregulation and impaired regulatory T-cell function, resulting in loss of self-tolerance. Chronic immune activation and cytokine imbalances (eg, IL-4, IL-5) may lower mast cell degranulation thresholds or trigger autoantibody production (anti-FcεRI/anti-IgE), manifesting as CSU.4

This report details 2 cases of patients with CVID presenting with autoimmune phenomena, granulomatous complications, and CSU, followed by a concise review of relevant literature. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants included in this study.

Patient 1

Patient 1, a 42-year-old man with no significant medical history, presented with severe, refractory CSU despite the use of desloratadine, 5 mg twice daily. Although he reported no history of recurrent infections, a palpable lymph node in his neck and a total IgE level less than 2 kU/L prompted further investigation, which ultimately led to the diagnosis of CVID based on a significantly diminished IgA level of 37 mg/dL (range 82-453 mg/dL) and IgG level of 420 mg/dL (range 751-1560 mg/dL), a low total number of memory B cells, and an absent antibody response following polysaccharide pneumococcal vaccination. Furthermore, a computed tomography scan revealed diffuse lymphadenopathy and multiple pulmonary nodules, consistent with granulomatous-lymphocytic interstitial lung disease (GL-ILD) associated with CVID. After lymph node excision and bone marrow examination, diagnoses of lymphoma and mastocytosis were ruled out and GL-ILD was confirmed. Because of the patient’s refractory urticaria despite desloratadine (twice daily), omalizumab, 300 mg every 4 weeks, was started with good effect. To prevent irreversible lung tissue damage and bronchiectasis, treatment with subcutaneous immunoglobulin replacement therapy, 400 mg/kg body weight every 4 weeks, and prednisone was initiated. Because of progressive GL-ILD, mycophenolate mofetil (720 mg twice daily) and later rituximab were added, resulting in full remission of both urticaria and GL-ILD.

Patient 2

Patient 2 was a 52-year-old female with a medical history including thyroiditis, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, and mild thrombocytopenia. She presented with recurrent upper and lower respiratory tract infections accompanied by bronchiectasis evident on computed tomography scan. CVID was diagnosed on the basis of her history of recurrent infections; undetectable levels of IgG, IgA and IgM; and an absent antibody response to polysaccharide pneumococcal vaccination. During follow-up, she developed both GL-ILD and CSU with undetectable levels of serum IgE. To prevent infectious complications related to CVID, intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) replacement therapy, 400 mg/kg body weight every 4 weeks, was initiated and later switched to subcutaneous immunoglobulin therapy. Treatment for GL-ILD consisted of high doses of prednisone and mycophenolate mofetil (720 mg twice daily). However, because of progression of the patient’s GL-ILD, treatment with rituximab (1 g [2 doses given 2 weeks apart], followed by a maintenance dose of 1 g every 6-8 months), was initiated, resulting in improvement in all clinical symptoms, including complete resolution of her CSU.

Both patients in our case series were diagnosed with CVID, with one patient's initial presentation of CSU leading to further investigation. Following a thorough literature search, we found 12 previously reported cases in which a relationship of CSU and CVID was hypothesized.3,5, 6, 7, 8, 9 In 10 of the 12 cases described in the literature (83.3%), CVID was diagnosed after the onset of CSU, varying in duration from 4 weeks to 5 years. The patient population was predominantly female, with 10 of the 12 (83.3%) ranging in age from 20 to 64 years. The patients in the other 2 cases (16.7%) were male; 1 was 15 years old. Multiple comorbidities were found in 9 of the 12 cases (75%); among other things, the comorbidities included a combination of pulmonary and allergic diseases such as rhinitis, bronchiectasis, asthma, and bronchopulmonary aspergillosis. Allergic rhinitis and bronchiectasis were found in 2 cases (22.2%), whereas all other manifestations were described as appearing solely on their own. A more recent systematic review demonstrated a median prevalence of CSU in CVID of 2.8% across the studies.10 In the context of our 2 patients, 59 studies reported in the aforesaid reviews included lung manifestations. More specifically, bronchiectasis was reported in 43 publications, with a median incidence of 27.0%, and GL-ILD or ILD were reported in 32 studies, with a corresponding median incidence of 8.7%.

The duration of CSU varied significantly, from a few weeks to 5 years. Notably, in most patients, CSU preceded development of recurrent infectious complications. Regarding laboratory evaluations, all of the reported patients were found to have low IgG levels, with the lowest being 85 mg/dL (range 751-1560 mg/dL). IgM levels were low in 50% of cases, with the lowest being 15.2 mg/dL (range 46-304 mg/dL). IgA levels were reported to be low in only 7 cases (58.3%), with the lowest level being 16.7 mg/dL (range 82-453 mg/dL).

In these cases, treatment of CSU in the context of CVID, was as follows. Of the 12 patients described in literature, 9 (75%) were treated with IVIG, which was found to be effective in 8 of the 9 cases (88.9%), with a decrease in urticaria within 2 to 3 months after the start of IVIG. One patient was treated with cyclosporine (3.0 mg/kg per day).8 However, this treatment was ineffective and resulted in kidney failure as an adverse event. Switching to omalizumab (300 mg per month administered subcutaneously) resulted in complete resolution of CSU. Another patient had improvement of urticaria with both IVIG and omalizumab, 300 mg per month administered subcutaneously; however, part of the urticaria persisted along with complaints of pruritus that did not respond to treatment. Thereafter, administration of cyclosporine (3.0 mg/kg per day) was started with a good response, albeit with the return of preexisting candidiasis.8 Finally, 1 patient developed CSU after starting IVIG, after which omalizumab 300, mg per month administered subcutaneously, was initiated, but without effect.8

The findings from our 2 cases, combined with the findings from the literature review, emphasize the association between CSU and CVID. This suggests that in patients presenting with CSU, especially when it is accompanied by recurrent infections or reduced immunoglobulin levels, analysis for CVID should be considered. Early diagnosis of CVID can lead to more effective treatment for both the immunodeficiency and its related complications such as CSU.

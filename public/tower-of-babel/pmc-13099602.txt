CHK2-USP37 axis stabilizes FOXO4 to sustain senescence and evade apoptosis
Authors: Jiahui Sun, Anke Geng, Zhiwei Song, Lingjiang Chen, Zhen-Ning Zhang, Ying Jiang, Zhiyong Mao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SignificanceCellular senescence is a key driver of aging and age-related diseases, largely due to the accumulation of apoptosis-resistant senescent cells. While the transcription factor FOXO4 is known to promote senescent cell survival, the mechanisms underlying its protein stability regulation have remained unclear. In this study, we uncover a DNA damage response (DDR)-driven pathway through which the kinase CHK2 phosphorylates the deubiquitinase USP37 at Thr589, enhancing its interaction with and stabilization of FOXO4 by removing K48-linked polyubiquitin chains. USP37 directly binds to and deubiquitinates FOXO4, increasing its protein stability specifically in senescent cells. Depletion of USP37 destabilizes FOXO4, leading to increased apoptosis in senescent cells, a phenotype rescued by FOXO4 reexpression.

Cellular senescence is a state of irreversible cell cycle arrest triggered by endogenous and exogenous stressors such as persistent DNA damage, oncogene activation, and telomere dysfunction (1, 2). The accumulation of nuclear DNA damage from various types of stress is widely regarded as a fundamental cause of cellular senescence, primarily through its activation of the DNA damage response (DDR) pathway (3). Cellular senescence plays dual roles in physiological and pathological contexts, with beneficial functions in embryonic development, wound healing, and tumor suppression (4). However, persistent senescent cells are thought to cause chronic inflammation, accelerate aging, and trigger the onset of age-related diseases (1, 5). Abundant evidence indicates that senescent cells accumulate in some tissues of aged mice and a variety of age-related diseases (5–11), and clearance of senescent cells extends lifespan and rejuvenates tissues in mice, underscoring the therapeutic promise of senolytic strategies (12, 13). Current research typically focuses on targeting the characteristics of senescent cells to trigger their apoptosis (14–19). However, the regulatory mechanism of stress-induced antiapoptosis of senescent cells and whether targeting specific signaling pathways can effectively eliminate these cells remain incompletely characterized.

The Forkhead box O4 (FOXO4), a member of the FOXO transcription factor family, regulates the expression of genes involved in redox homeostasis, metabolic reprogramming, and immune modulation (20, 21). In addition, FOXO4 contributes to cell cycle regulation by inducing quiescence or senescence, particularly in response to persistent stress. Studies demonstrate that activation of FOXO4 induces cell cycle exit through upregulation of p130 (22). Furthermore, oncogenic BRAFV600E activates FOXO4 via a MEK-ROS-JNK signaling axis, resulting in cell cycle arrest and senescence (23). Critically, during senescence establishment, FOXO4 expression is progressively upregulated and promotes senescent cell survival by suppressing apoptosis (24). This antiapoptotic function is mediated, at least in part, through direct interaction with p53, preventing its nuclear export and pro-apoptotic activity (24). Consequently, FOXO4 protein homeostasis is essential for senescent cell survival. However, in addition to transcriptional regulation, the mechanism of posttranslational modifications in regulating FOXO4 protein stability has not been elucidated. The ubiquitin-proteasome system, a highly regulated protein degradation pathway, is directly linked to aging and age-associated diseases (25–30). FOXO4 has been shown to be targeted for degradation by the E3 ubiquitin ligase COP1 (31). In contrast, USP7, a stress-responsive deubiquitinase, modulates FOXO4 activity by reversing its mono-ubiquitination (32). This attenuates FOXO4 nuclear localization and transcriptional activity without altering protein stability. However, deubiquitinases that remove polyubiquitin chains from FOXO4 to enhance its stability remain unidentified. The identification of such enzymes could reveal therapeutic targets for clearing senescent cells, offering potential strategies to delay aging or alleviate age-related diseases.

Here, we identify an axis linking DDR signaling to USP37-mediated stabilization of FOXO4. Mechanistically, FOXO4 protein stability is increased in stress-induced senescent cells through regulation by the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway and USP37 as the principal deubiquitinase is responsible for FOXO4 stabilization. Moreover, we show that USP37 is phosphorylated at threonine residue 589 by DDR kinase CHK2, which promotes USP37-FOXO4 association, facilitating USP37-mediated FOXO4 stability. The presence of the CHK2-USP37-FOXO4 axis was also confirmed in vivo, based on our validation in aged mouse tissues. These results identify a potential therapeutic strategy to eliminate senescent cells, with implications for delaying the onset of aging and mitigating age-related pathologies.

Results

The Protein Stability of FOXO4 Is Increased in Stress-Induced Senescent Cells.

In ionizing radiation (IR) induced senescent IMR90 fibroblasts, FOXO4 was reported to be highly expressed (24). We first sought to verify its upregulation in senescent cells triggered by diverse stimuli. In consistence with previous findings (24), we confirmed that the expression of FOXO4 was upregulated in both HCA2-hTERT and IMR90-SV40LT cells driven into senescence by IR or Bleomycin (Fig. 1 A and B and SI Appendix, Fig. S1 A and B). Similarly, we observed increased FOXO4 expression in both IR-induced senescent HK2 cells (an immortalized proximal tubule epithelial cell line derived from normal adult human kidney) and HeLa cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S1 C and D). Given that FOXO4 transcription is known to increase during cellular senescence (24), we need to specifically dissect posttranslational regulation independent of transcriptional changes. We therefore generated a doxycycline-inducible FOXO4 overexpression system in HCA2-hTERT cells (designated DFMJT cells). This system allows for controlled FOXO4 expression uncoupled from the endogenous senescent transcriptional program (Fig. 1C).

Using this cell line, we then examined whether senescent state affected FOXO4 stability. CHX chase assays demonstrated a significantly extended half-life of FOXO4 in senescent DFMJT cells compared to their non-senescent counterparts (Fig. 1 D and E). This indicates that the accumulation of FOXO4 protein in senescence is not solely transcriptional but is also reinforced by enhanced posttranslational stability.

Taken together, these data demonstrate that stress-induced senescence augments FOXO4 protein levels through a mechanism that increases its stability, leading to its sustained accumulation.

USP37 Promotes FOXO4 Stabilization.

Given that FOXO4 protein stability is regulated by the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, we hypothesized that specific deubiquitinases within senescent cells are responsible for stabilizing FOXO4. To identify such deubiquitinases, we conducted an initial screen of 35 deubiquitinases by assessing their ability to increase FOXO4 protein levels via western blotting (SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). Eight DUBs increased FOXO4 levels by more than 1.2-fold, among which USP3, USP11, and USP37 showed statistically significant enhancement (P < 0.05) (SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). Subsequent coimmunoprecipitation (co-IP) assays for all eight candidates revealed a specific interaction exclusively between USP37 and FOXO4 (SI Appendix, Fig. S2B).

To determine whether USP37 stabilizes FOXO4 through its deubiquitinase activity, we compared the effects of wild-type USP37 (USP37WT) and a catalytically inactive mutant (USP37C350A). Ectopic expression of USP37WT, but not USP37C350A, led to a marked increase in FOXO4 protein levels (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, overexpression of HA-tagged USP37 did not alter endogenous FOXO4 mRNA levels following senescence induction (Fig. 2B), indicating that its effect is posttranslational. Conversely, depletion of USP37 in DFMJT cells significantly reduced exogenous FOXO4 protein levels (Fig. 2C), an effect that was rescued by the proteasome inhibitor MG132 (Fig. 2D), confirming that USP37 protects FOXO4 from proteasomal degradation.

We further validated this stabilization mechanism by measuring protein half-life. Overexpression of USP37WT significantly extended the half-life of FOXO4 (Fig. 2 E and F), while knockdown of USP37 in HEK293 cells substantially shortened it (Fig. 2 G and H).

Collectively, these data demonstrate that USP37 promotes FOXO4 protein stabilization in a manner dependent on its intrinsic deubiquitinase activity.

USP37 Directly Interacts with and Deubiquitinates FOXO4.

To confirm their interaction, we performed co-IP assays using lysates from cells expressing Flag-FOXO4 and HA-USP37. Immunoprecipitation assay confirmed that USP37 interacts with FOXO4 (Fig. 3A). This interaction was further validated by a reciprocal co-IP (Fig. 3B), and, crucially, by an in vitro assay using purified recombinant proteins, demonstrating a direct interaction between USP37 and FOXO4 (Fig. 3C). We next mapped the domains responsible for this interaction. The results showed that both the Forkhead (FH) domain and the transactivation domain of FOXO4 can bind to USP37 (Fig. 3D). In addition, the catalytic domain of USP37 was found to interact with FOXO4 (Fig. 3E).

As K48-linked polyubiquitin chains typically target substrates for proteasomal degradation (33, 34), we assessed this specific ubiquitination on FOXO4. Overexpression of USP37 significantly reduced the level of K48-linked ubiquitination on FOXO4 (Fig. 3F). Conversely, USP37 knockdown markedly enhanced K48-linked ubiquitination (Fig. 3G). Furthermore, this deubiquitinating activity was dependent on the catalytic function of USP37, as only USP37WT, and not the catalytically inactive mutant, suppressed FOXO4 ubiquitination (Fig. 3H). Most importantly, an in vitro deubiquitination assay demonstrated that purified USP37WT, but not USP37C350A, could directly remove ubiquitin chains from FOXO4 (Fig. 3 I and J).

Taken together, these data establish that USP37 directly binds to and deubiquitinates FOXO4, specifically by removing K48-linked chains, thereby promoting its protein stability.

USP37 Maintains the Protein Stability of FOXO4 in Senescent Cells.

Given that FOXO4 is overexpressed in stress-induced senescent cells, we hypothesized that USP37 mediates this upregulation. To test this, we examined the effect of USP37 deficiency on FOXO4 protein levels in senescent cells.

Using CRISPR-Cas9, we knocked out USP37 in DFMJT cells (Fig. 4A). Senescent USP37-KO cells exhibited a significant reduction in endogenous FOXO4 protein levels, whereas overexpression of USP37 increased them (Fig. 4 B and C). This finding was corroborated by siRNA-mediated knockdown of USP37, which also significantly reduced endogenous FOXO4 levels following senescence induction (SI Appendix, Fig. S3A). Furthermore, immunofluorescence assays showed a pronounced decrease in FOXO4 foci in USP37-knockdown senescent cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S3B). Additionally, knockdown of USP37 in senescent HK2 and HeLa cells also led to a marked reduction in endogenous FOXO4 levels (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 C and D), further supporting the conserved role of USP37 in regulating FOXO4 stability across cell types. To directly assess stability, we performed CHX chase assay, which revealed that USP37 deficiency significantly shortens the half-life of FOXO4 in senescent cells, underscoring its essential role in maintaining FOXO4 stability during senescence (Fig. 4 D and E).

Next, we confirmed the physiological relevance of this interaction through endogenous co-IP in senescent HCA2-hTERT fibroblasts, which demonstrated a direct interaction between FOXO4 and USP37 (Fig. 4F). Notably, this interaction was markedly stronger in senescent cells compared to their non-senescent counterparts (Fig. 4G), suggesting it may be functionally important for promoting senescent cell survival.

CHK2 Kinase Activity Is Required for USP37-mediated Stabilization of FOXO4 in Stress-Induced Senescent Cells.

Stress-induced cellular senescence is characterized by persistent DNA damage (35), which activates a prolonged DDR pathway and results in irreversible cell cycle arrest. Given that the USP37-FOXO4 interaction is enhanced in senescent cells (Fig. 4G), we investigated whether this regulation is mediated by DDR signaling factors. Co-IP experiments revealed that this interaction is strengthened following IR (Fig. 5A). Western blot analysis confirmed that stress-induced senescent cells exhibit significant upregulation of ATM and CHK2 phosphorylation, suggesting that the ATM-CHK2 signaling axis may regulate the USP37-FOXO4 pathway (SI Appendix, Fig. S4A).

Although USP37 is known to be phosphorylated at Ser114 by ATM upon DNA damage (36), we found that a phosphorylation-deficient mutant USP37S114A stabilized FOXO4 to a similar extent as USP37WT, indicating that ATM is not the primary regulator of this axis (Fig. 5B). Instead, we found that USP37 directly interacts with the kinase CHK2 (Fig. 5 C and D). Inhibition of CHK2 activity attenuated the ability of USP37 to stabilize FOXO4 following IR treatment (Fig. 5E).

We next demonstrated that USP37 is phosphorylated at a threonine residue in response to IR, and that this phosphorylation is abolished upon CHK2 knockdown (Fig. 5 F–H and SI Appendix, Fig. S4B). Based on the consensus motif for CHK2 phosphorylation (RXXS/RXXT) (37), we identified two potential target residues in USP37, T472 and T589. Mutating both residues to alanine abolished IR-induced USP37 phosphorylation (Fig. 5I). Further analysis revealed that the T589A single mutation alone was sufficient to abolish this phosphorylation, identifying T589 as the critical site (Fig. 5J). This was confirmed by an in vitro kinase assay, which showed that CHK2 phosphorylates recombinant USP37WT but not the USP37T589A mutant (SI Appendix, Fig. S4C).

To determine the functional significance of this phosphorylation, we assessed FOXO4 stability. The USP37T589A mutant was significantly impaired in its ability to stabilize FOXO4, unlike the phosphomimetic USP37T589D mutant (Fig. 5 K and L). Consistent with this, USP37WT and USP37T589D reduced K48-linked ubiquitination of FOXO4, while USP37T589A failed to do so (Fig. 5M). Furthermore, both in vivo and in vitro binding assays showed that the USP37T589D mutant exhibits a stronger interaction with FOXO4 than USP37WT (Fig. 5N and SI Appendix, Fig. S4D). As predicted, depletion of CHK2 significantly reduced the endogenous USP37-FOXO4 interaction in senescent fibroblasts (SI Appendix, Fig. S4E).

Subsequently, we also observed IR-induced senescence led to a pronounced increase in CHK2 phosphorylation in HK2 and HeLa cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 F and G). Given the established accumulation of senescent cells in aged tissues, we sought to validate the relevance of the CHK2-USP37-FOXO4 axis in vivo. Consistent with our hypothesis, western blot analysis revealed a significant increase in FOXO4 protein levels in skin tissues from old (27-mon-old) mice compared to young (2-mon-old) controls (SI Appendix, Fig. S4H). Furthermore, immunostaining demonstrated a notable accumulation of cells that were positive for both FOXO4 and pCHK2 in skin tissues from old mice, suggesting a correlation between pCHK2 activation and FOXO4 expression in skin from old mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S4I). Importantly, and supporting a key mechanistic step, the interaction between USP37 and FOXO4 was markedly enhanced in skin tissues from old mice in comparison to those from young mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S4J).

In conclusion, our results establish that CHK2 activation in response to DNA damage phosphorylates USP37 at Thr589. This phosphorylation enhances the interaction between USP37 and FOXO4, thereby promoting the deubiquitination and stabilization of FOXO4 in senescent cells.

USP37 Maintains Apoptosis Resistance in Senescent Cells in a FOXO4-Dependent Manner.

Given the established role of FOXO4 in promoting the viability and apoptosis resistance of senescent cells, and our finding that USP37 regulates FOXO4 protein stability, we investigated the functional consequences of USP37 depletion. We found that knockdown of USP37 significantly increased the proportion of senescent cells undergoing both early and late apoptosis (Fig. 6 A and B).

We next examined key markers of apoptotic pathway activation. Depleting USP37 enhanced the release of mitochondrial cytochrome c into the cytosol in senescent HCA2-hTERT fibroblasts (Fig. 6C), a pivotal step in the intrinsic apoptosis cascade. Consistent with this, in USP37-deficient senescent cells, we observed elevated levels of cleaved PARP1 (Fig. 6D), indicating executioner caspase-3 activation (38–40) and underscoring the critical role of USP37 in maintaining apoptosis resistance.

To determine whether this effect is mediated through FOXO4, we restored FOXO4 expression in USP37-depleted cells. Doxycycline-induced FOXO4 overexpression effectively reduced the rate of apoptosis (Fig. 6E), demonstrating that the pro-apoptotic effect of USP37 loss is FOXO4-dependent. Furthermore, USP37 depletion significantly reduced the number of viable senescent cells (Fig. 6F). Consistent with this, treatment with dacarbazine, recently identified as a USP37 inhibitor (41), also decreased the survival of senescent cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S5A).

Furthermore, we examined the impact of USP37 deficiency on senescence-associated secreted phenotypes (SASPs). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that USP37 knockdown markedly decreased the mRNA levels of senescence-associated genes, including CDKN2A, CDKN1A, and key SASP factors such as IL-6, IL-1β, CCL5, and CXCL11 (Fig. 6G). Consistently, USP37 deficiency significantly reduced the proportion of SA-β-gal-positive cells (Fig. 6H). These data suggest that USP37 is involved not only in cell survival but also in the maintenance of the senescent state.

Discussion

Cellular senescence is a fundamental driver of aging and age-related pathologies, characterized in part by the accumulation of apoptosis-resistant cells (42–44). While the upregulation of FOXO4 has been implicated in promoting the survival of senescent cells, the mechanisms underlying its sustained overexpression have remained incompletely understood. In this study, we identify a regulatory axis wherein the DDR kinase CHK2 phosphorylates the deubiquitinase USP37 at Thr589, enhancing its interaction with and stabilization of FOXO4, thereby fortifying the antiapoptotic shield of senescent cells.

Our findings situate the CHK2-USP37-FOXO4 pathway within a broader landscape of molecular adaptations that enable senescent cells to evade apoptosis. These include the upregulation of BCL-2 family antiapoptotic members (15), suppression of the p53 pathway (45–47), activation of NF-κB (48), and other pro-survival signals. A hallmark of stress-induced senescence is persistent DNA damage and sustained DDR activation (35, 49). Our work directly couples this canonical senescence trigger to apoptotic resistance, revealing a mechanism that is both mechanistically grounded and therapeutically exploitable.

The stability of FOXO proteins is predominantly regulated by the ubiquitin--proteasome system (50–54). While multiple E3 ubiquitin ligases, such as COP1 for FOXO4 (31), have been identified, the DUBs that counterbalance this regulation are less characterized. We establish USP37 as the principal DUB responsible for stabilizing FOXO4 in senescence. This finding contrasts with prior reports on USP7, which deubiquitinates FOXO4 without enhancing stability (32). We speculate that the senescence milieu may attenuate USP7-FOXO4 interactions, allowing USP37 to dominate FOXO4 regulation within nuclear compartments. The precise interplay between COP1-mediated ubiquitination and USP37-mediated deubiquitination in controlling FOXO4 turnover presents a critical area for future investigation.

Beyond its established roles in genome maintenance and cell cycle progression in proliferating cells (55–57), we uncover a function for USP37 in the nonproliferative state of senescence. We demonstrate that CHK2-mediated phosphorylation of USP37 at T589 enhances its affinity for FOXO4, likely through inducing conformational changes.

Notably, while ATM phosphorylates USP37 at Ser114 to promote BLM stability (36), this modification is dispensable for FOXO4 stabilization, highlighting pathway specificity. Given that CHK2 activation is a feature of multiple senescence contexts, including replicative and oncogene-induced senescence (58, 59), it will be important to determine whether this axis operates broadly across senescence types.

Therapeutic targeting of senescent cells (senolysis) has emerged as a promising strategy to ameliorate age-related decline. A major limitation in senolytic drug development is that many identified compounds lack mechanistically defined targets, which impedes both clinical translation and rational optimization. Here, by elucidating the CHK2-USP37-FOXO4 axis, we present a targetable strategy for eliminating senescent cells, representing a mechanism-based approach and thereby establishing a clear paradigm for developing senolytic agents. The recent identification of dacarbazine as a USP37 inhibitor (41), which we have shown can decrease the survival of senescent cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S5A), prompts an investigation into the potential of targeting the CHK2-USP37-FOXO4 axis for senolytic therapy. However, although previous studies indicate that USP37 inhibitors exhibit high specificity in vivo (41), their effectiveness in clearing senescent cells under in vivo conditions remains to be validated. Targeting FOXO4, a peptide named FOXO4-DRI has been developed to disrupt the FOXO4-p53 interaction, thereby selectively inducing apoptosis. This approach is considered a potential therapeutic strategy for mitigating aging-related decline. Peptide- and protein-based therapeutics show significant potential for disease treatment due to their high bioactivity, specificity, and favorable safety profiles. However, their widespread clinical use is limited by inherent drawbacks, such as rapid enzymatic degradation, low membrane permeability, and a general reliance on injectable administration (60). Even noninvasive delivery approaches still encounter obstacles like enzymatic breakdown in the gastrointestinal tract, inefficient epithelial transport, and low systemic bioavailability (60). These factors collectively contribute to suboptimal bioavailability and reduced patient adherence. Consequently, identifying and targeting upstream regulators of FOXO4 may offer another therapeutic avenue.

However, translational applications must be approached with caution. USP37 is essential for genomic stability and replication fork protection in healthy, proliferating cells. Direct inhibition of USP37 could therefore confer on-target toxicity. Several alternative strategies could be explored to enhance specificity: 1) developing small molecules that selectively disrupt the CHK2-USP37 interaction or inhibit T589 phosphorylation without affecting basal USP37 activity; 2) exploiting the heightened DDR in senescent cells to achieve selective targeting; 3) combining FOXO4-DRI with modulation of this axis for synergistic senolysis. Furthermore, whether CHK2-mediated phosphorylation enhances the catalytic activity of USP37 toward FOXO4 specifically remains an open and mechanistically important question. Therefore, these approaches targeting the CHK2-USP37 signaling axis require further thorough validation in vivo for both efficacy and toxicity.

In conclusion, we have delineated a DDR-dependent regulatory pathway that stabilizes FOXO4 to promote senescent cell survival. This work not only advances our understanding of the posttranslational mechanisms governing senescent cell antiapoptotic pathways but also unveils nodal points for the targeted eradication of senescent cells, with profound implications for delaying aging and alleviating age-related diseases.

Materials and Methods

Cell Culture.

HCA2-hTERT fibroblasts, IMR90-SV40LT fibroblasts, HeLa, and HEK293 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; KeyGEN BioTECH, Cat. # KGM12800) containing 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Cat. # 16000). HK2 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium F-12 (DMEM/F12; KeyGEN BioTECH, Cat. # KGL1201) containing 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum. The culture medium was further supplemented with 1% (v/v) penicillin/streptomycin (Gibco, Cat. # 15140-122) and 1× nonessential amino acids (Gibco, Cat. # 11140-050). All cells were incubated at a 37 °C incubator with 5% CO2 (Thermo Fisher Heracell 240i). Periodic Mycoplasma testing is performed on all cell lines at regular intervals to guarantee their freedom from contamination.

Plasmid, Antibodies and Reagents.

Full-length CDS of FOXO4 was amplified from the cDNA of HCA2-hTERT cells and cloned into the pEGFP-N1 vector or a Flag-tagged vector (pCMV-Flag). Full-length USP37 CDS was cloned into vectors containing HA tag at C terminus. Plasmids for DUBs utilized in this study were obtained from previous work (61). Mutants of USP37 were generated based on wild-type plasmid. Plasmid Transfection.

Transfection of plasmids was performed as follows: HEK293 cells were transfected using standardized polyethyleneimine transfection method, while HCA2-hTERT, IMR90-SV40LT fibroblasts, HeLa, and HK2 cells were transfected via electroporation on a Lonza 4D electroporator.

Protein Stability Assay.

HCA2-hTERT fibroblasts integrated with the doxycycline-inducible FOXO4 overexpression system (DFMJT cells) were pretreated with 1 µg/mL doxycycline for 12 h to induce FOXO4 expression. Subsequently, 100 µg/mL CHX was added while maintaining doxycycline, and cells were harvested at indicated time points for immunoblotting analysis. Assessment of FOXO4 protein stability in USP37-depleted cells was performed as follows: HEK293 cells stably expressing scrambled shRNA (shControl) or USP37-targeting shRNA were transfected with 1 µg FOXO4-Flag plasmid. After 16 h, the medium was replaced with fresh medium containing 100 µg/mL CHX. Cells were harvested at 0, 3, 6, and 9 h post-CHX treatment for immunoblotting analysis. Assessment of FOXO4 protein stability upon USP37 overexpression was performed as follows: HEK293 cells were cotransfected with 2 µg FOXO4-Flag plasmid and either 8 µg control vector or USP37-HA plasmid. After 16 h, cells were treated with 100 µg/mL CHX and collected at 0, 3, 6, and 9 h after CHX addition.

Immunoprecipitation.

For the in vivo immunoprecipitation assays, cells were collected and lysed in RIPA buffer [50 mM Tris-Cl (pH 7.4), 150 mM NaCl, 1% sodium deoxycholate, 0.1% SDS, 1 mM EDTA, 1% Triton X-100 and a protease inhibitor] for 15 min on ice, followed by ultrasonication (3× 1 min pulses at 10% amplitude, 2 s on/off cycles). Lysates were centrifuged at 13,500 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C.

Supernatants were incubated overnight at 4 °C with HA-conjugated magnetic beads (Selleck, Cat. # B26202), Flag-conjugated magnetic beads (Selleck, Cat. # B26102), or GFP-Trap (ChromoTek, Cat. # gta-100). After washing with ice-cold lysis buffer four times, bound proteins were eluted by boiling at 100 °C for 10 min and analyzed by immunoblotting. Whole-cell lysates were used as input as a control. For the in vitro pull-down assays, purified recombinant proteins were mixed in a reaction buffer (25 mM Tris-Cl, 150 mM NaCl, and 1% NP-40) prior to bead addition, followed by rotation overnight at 4 °C. For all in vivo deubiquitination assay, cells were pretreated with MG132 for 12 h prior to collection. The detailed protocol was performed as previously reported (62).

In Vitro Deubiquitination Assay.

Recombinant GST-USP37-Flag protein was expressed in Escherichia coli BL21 (DE3) and affinity-purified using GSTrap HP columns on ÄKTA pure™ chromatography system (Cytiva). HEK293 cells were transfected with the HA-tagged ubiquitin and FOXO4-GFP plasmids for 24 h. Ubiquitinated FOXO4-GFP were purified using GFP-Trap according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Purified substrates along with GST-USP37WT-Flag or GST-USP37C350A-Flag were incubated in reaction buffer (50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 50 mM NaCl, 1 mM EDTA, 10 mM DTT, and 5% glycerol) at 37 °C for 4 h with rotation. The subsequent steps were identical to those used in the immunoprecipitation assay.

In Vitro Phosphorylation Assay.

Recombinant CHK2-GFP protein purified from HEK293 cells was incubated with GST-USP37WTFlag or GST-USP37T589A-Flag in buffer containing [25 mM Tris–HCl (pH 7.4), 2 mM adenosine 5′triphosphate (ATP), 5 mM MgCl2, 5 mM MnCl2, and 0.1 mM DTT] in 30 °C for 1 h followed by western blot analysis.

Protein Extraction from Mouse Skin Tissue.

Dorsal skin tissues were harvested from young (2-mon-old) and aged (22-mon-old) male C57BL/6J mice, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and mechanically pulverized. The powdered tissues were homogenized in ice-cold RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitors using a Dounce homogenizer. Homogenates were centrifuged at 14,000× g for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatants were collected as total protein lysates. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA assay.

Immunofluorescence Staining.

HCA2-hTERT fibroblasts were irradiated with 10 Gy IR to induce senescence. On day 8 postirradiation, cells were transfected with siRNA targeting USP37 or scrambled control siRNA, and seeded at 4 × 104 cells/well onto glass coverslips in 12-well plates. After 5 d of transfection, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, permeabilized with 2% Triton X-100 in PBS for 2 min, and washed three times with ice-cold PBS. Nonspecific binding was blocked with bovine serum albumin for 1 h at room temperature. Primary antibodies against FOXO4 (Cell Signaling) were incubated overnight at 4 °C. After washing with PBS, cells were incubated with FITC-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h in the dark. After staining with DAPI, the coverslips were mounted. Images were taken using an Olympus Upright Fluorescence Microscope (Model BX53). For Tyramide Signal Amplification (TSA) staining on young and old mouse skin paraffin sections, a commercial kit (Servicebio, Cat. # G1259) was employed. Briefly, tissue sections were deparaffinized with xylene, rehydrated through a graded ethanol series, and subjected to antigen retrieval using 10 mM citric acid solution (pH 6.0) at 98 °C for 20 min. Endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3% H2O2, and nonspecific binding was blocked with 3% BSA. Sections were incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. TSA was sequentially performed, with antibody elution between cycles using elution buffer to remove previously bound antibodies. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and sections were mounted with antifade mounting medium. Imaging was conducted using a Leica TCS SP8 confocal laser scanning microscope.

FACS Analysis of Apoptosis.

HCA2-hTERT fibroblasts were irradiated with 10 Gy IR to induce senescence. On day 8 postirradiation, cells were electroporated with siRNA targeting USP37 or scrambled control siRNA. After 6 d of transfection, culture supernatants and adherent cells were cocollected for apoptosis analysis using the Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime, Cat. # C1062M). The rate of apoptosis was analyzed by flow cytometry (CytoFLEX LX). Early apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI−) and late apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI+) populations were quantified.

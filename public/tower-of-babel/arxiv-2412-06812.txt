On the Fontaine-Mazur conjecture for p = 3 p=3
Xinyao Zhang

Abstract
. We prove the remaining p = 3 p=3 cases of the regular two-dimensional Fontaine-Mazur conjecture over ℚ \mathbb{Q} , thereby completing the regular case for all odd primes. Our main new input is a potential big R = 𝕋 R=\mathbb{T} theorem for large-dimensional components of global pseudo-deformation spaces, valid for all odd primes. After a single abelian base change, propagation of pro-modularity and induction on partial ordinariness reduce the componentwise argument to the case of globally irreducible ordinary points. A characteristic-zero Greenberg-Wiles dimension estimate supplies the additional ordinary deformation-theoretic input needed in the exceptional case. We combine these arguments with small-prime p p -adic Langlands correspondence, local deformation theory and patching.

•

The conditions on nice primes.
Besides pro-modularity and irreducibility, a nice prime must satisfy a Taylor-Wiles type condition on dihedral representations and a triviality lifting condition at prime-to-
p

•

The dimension of ordinary intersections.
For

\omega

p=3

•

The dimension of ordinary components.
Even if an irreducible ordinary prime has been found, the residual dimension estimate used in
[

may be insufficient. When
\bar{\chi}=\omega=\omega^{-1}

can be non-zero. The Greenberg-Wiles lower bound for the ordinary deformation ring is then only

[

Our strategy addresses these difficulties by the following three ingredients. The first two give the componentwise construction and propagation mechanism; the third establishes the ordinary special case at which the induction terminates.

1)
Propagation of pro-modularity.
Following the author’s previous work
[
Zha24
]
, we choose a single abelian base change at the outset, independently of the component or primes considered later. Over this field, we construct primes which are already nice: the tame local conditions are built into the construction, and Leopoldt’s conjecture for abelian CM fields bounds the dihedral locus. Combined with a connectedness-dimension argument, this gives Lemma
5.1.2
: every irreducible component through a one-dimensional globally irreducible pro-modular prime contains a nice prime, and hence is pro-modular. Consequently, subsequent steps need only produce globally irreducible pro-modular primes; the additional nice-prime conditions, including the dihedral condition in Definition
4.1.3
, do not have to be imposed during the induction. Once pro-modularity is obtained at the ordinary endpoint, Lemma
5.1.2
propagates it backwards through these points.

2)
Induction on partial ordinariness.
After choosing the auxiliary field of sufficiently large degree, we impose ordinarity one place at a time. We cut by the four local reducibility equations at a new place, choose a globally irreducible one-dimensional prime, and prescribe an ordinary stable line there. The associated deformation problem is ordinary, with prescribed lines, at the places already treated and unrestricted at the remaining places above
p
. A Greenberg-Wiles dimension estimate supplies a partial ordinary component through this prime of dimension at least
2d-r
, where
r
is the number of ordinary places (Lemma
5.4.1
). Passing to this component recovers enough dimension to continue while retaining the previously prescribed ordinary lines. These components need not be nested: propagation eventually transfers pro-modularity back to
C
. Thus we do not need a direct lower bound for its fully ordinary locus.

3)
Pro-modularity of irreducible ordinary points.
In the auxiliary setting of Section
5
, every one-dimensional globally irreducible ordinary prime is pro-modular, regardless of its residue characteristic and without local genericity (Corollary
5.3.5
). We follow the ordinary deformation-theoretic argument of
[
Pan22a
, Section 7.4.2, especially Lemma 7.4.7]
, but overcome the possible failure of residual adjoint
H^{0}
-vanishing by passing to suitable characteristic-zero irreducible ordinary points. There
H^{0}(G_{F,\Sigma},\ad^{0}\rho(1))=0
, so a characteristic-zero Greenberg-Wiles dimension estimate gives the required dimension bound (Proposition
2.4.3
and Corollary
2.5.7
). Together with ordinary finiteness, Subsection
5.2
and propagation, this proves the special case in Subsection
5.3
.

Once the induction reaches a prime which is ordinary at every place above
p
, the third ingredient gives its pro-modularity. The first then propagates pro-modularity backwards through the chosen intersection points, using components of the ambient unrestricted pseudo-deformation space, until it reaches
C
. This proves Proposition
5.4.2
; combined with the previously treated local types, it gives Theorem
1.0.3
for all odd primes. Together with the classicality criterion of Corollary
3.3.8
, based on
[
CDP14
]
, and the known ordinary cases, it also gives the residually reducible case of Theorem
1.0.2
. The residually irreducible case is treated in Section
6
.

(Further applications of the potential big

First, as explained in

2.2.
Pseudo-representations

In this subsection, we will give some results on 2-dimensional pseudo-representations. Our main references are
[
SW99
]
and
[

Definition 2.2.1
.

For a profinite group
G
and a topological commutative ring
R
in which 2 is invertible, a 2-dimensional
pseudo-representation
is a continuous function
T:G\to R
such that

1)
T(1)=2
,

2)
T(\sigma\tau)=T(\tau\sigma)
for all
\sigma,\tau\in G
,

3)
T(\gamma\delta\eta)+T(\gamma\eta\delta)-T(\gamma\eta)T(\delta)-T(\eta\delta)T(\gamma)-T(\delta\gamma)T(\eta)+T(\gamma)T(\delta)T(\eta)=0,

for any
\delta,\gamma,\eta\in G
.

The
determinant

\operatorname{det}(T)
of a pseudo-representation
T
is a character (using 3)) defined by

\operatorname{det}(T):G\to R^{\times},~\operatorname{det}(T)(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(T(\sigma)^{2}-T(\sigma^{2})),\sigma\in G.

If there exists an order
2
element
c\in G
such that
T(c)=0
, then for a pseudo-representation
T
and
\sigma,\tau\in G
, we define:

1)
a(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(T(c\sigma)+T(\sigma))
,

2)
d(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(-T(c\sigma)+T(\sigma))
,

3)
y(\sigma,\tau)=a(\sigma\tau)-a(\sigma)a(\tau).

If
\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
is a continuous representation with an element
c\in G
satisfying
\rho(c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&~\\
~&-1\end{pmatrix}
, then
\operatorname{tr}(\rho)
is a pseudo-representation. More explicitly, if
\rho(\sigma)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{\sigma}&b_{\sigma}\\
c_{\sigma}&d_{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}
, then
a(\sigma)=a_{\sigma}
,
d(\sigma)=d_{\sigma}
are as defined above and
y(\sigma,\tau)=b_{\sigma}c_{\tau}
.

Pan
[
Pan22a
, 2.1.4]
verifies that the continuous functions
\{a,d,y\}
satisfy the following equations.

(1)
y(\sigma,\tau)=d(\tau\sigma)-d(\tau)d(\sigma)
.

(2)
y(\sigma\tau,\delta)=a(\sigma)y(\tau,\delta)+y(\sigma,\delta)d(\tau)
.

(3)
y(\sigma,\tau\delta)=a(\delta)y(\sigma,\tau)+y(\sigma,\delta)d(\tau)
.

(4)
y(\alpha,\beta)y(\sigma,\tau)=y(\alpha,\tau)y(\sigma,\beta)
.

The following result is clear from the last equation.

Proposition 2.2.2
.

[
Zha24
, Proposition 2.1.2]

Let

be an integral domain, and

be a pseudo-representation for a profinite group

be two elements in

1) If

in

or

2) Assume that the set

(resp.

(resp.

(resp.

Let

For the rest of this subsection, we assume

Proposition 2.3.2

1) The ring

2) The ring

is a power series ring over

Proof.

1) By Krull’s principal ideal theorem and 2) of Proposition

is a zero divisor in

2) Let
R_{1}
and

be the universal deformation rings of the one-dimensional representation
1
and
\bar{\chi}
, respectively. By
[
BIP23
, page 6]
, each of them is a power series ring over

of relative dimension

R=R_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{O}}R_{\bar{\chi}}
is a power series ring over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
4
. By the universal property, we get a natural surjection
R\twoheadrightarrow R_{v}^{\operatorname{ps,ord}}
.

Recall the versal deformation ring
\tilde{R}
in the third case of Proposition
2.1.3
, i.e., the cocycle
\beta=0
. Then we get natural maps
R_{v}^{\operatorname{ps}}\to\tilde{R}\twoheadrightarrow\tilde{R}/(b,b^{\prime},c,c^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{O}[[a,a^{\prime},d,d^{\prime}]]
. From the description in the previous subsection, the last ring defines a natural reducible pseudo-representation. Hence, we have natural maps
R_{v}^{\operatorname{ps}}\twoheadrightarrow R_{v}^{\operatorname{ps,ord}}\to\mathcal{O}[[a,a^{\prime},d,d^{\prime}]]

By the universal property, we get a natural surjection

The reducibility ideal in the exceptional case differs from the one in
[

Proposition 2.3.3
.

The kernel

of the natural surjection
R_{v}^{\operatorname{ps}}\twoheadrightarrow R_{v}^{\operatorname{ps,ord}}
can be generated by four elements, and cannot be generated by fewer than four elements.

For a CNL
\mathcal{O}
-algebra

with residue field

[

gives an exact sequence

In particular,

Remark 2.3.4
.

1) For comparison,
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 7.4.4]
concerns a fixed-determinant ring when

, of the form
\mathcal{O}[[x_{0},x_{1},y_{0},y_{1}]]/(x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0})
, with reducibility ideal
(x_{0},x_{1})
. At
p=3
, the identity
\omega=\omega^{-1}
makes both opposite extension spaces two-dimensional. The relative tangent dimensions of
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
and

are consequently
8
and
4
; after fixing the determinant they are
6
and
2
. In either convention the difference is four. Thus the two-generator argument in Pan’s lemma does not extend to this case.

2) Böckle’s description also gives a presentation of
R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}
without expanding the lengthy coefficients. Use the variables and matrices in Proposition
2.1.3
(3), and put
X=bc
,
Y=bc^{\prime}
,
Z=b^{\prime}c
and
W=b^{\prime}c^{\prime}
. Write
B_{1}(1,2)-B_{2}(1,2)=bf-b^{\prime}h
and
B_{1}(2,1)-B_{2}(2,1)=cg-c^{\prime}j
, where

and

. The matrix formulas give
f(0)=g(0)=-6
and

. We obtain

\begin{split}R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}&\cong\mathcal{O}[[a,a^{\prime},d,d^{\prime},X,Y,Z,W]]/\mathcal{I},\\
\mathcal{I}&=(XW-YZ,\ Xf-Zh,\ Yf-Wh,\ Xg-Yj,\ Zg-Wj).\end{split}

Indeed, the diagonal torus has weights
1
on
b,b^{\prime}
,
-1
on
c,c^{\prime}
and
0
on

. The multiplicity-free GMA description identifies

with the completed ring of invariants of the split adapted representation space; see
[

, Corollary 2.25 and Theorem 3.8(4)]
. Besides

, the invariant coordinates are the four products above, subject to

. Since a torus is linearly reductive, the remaining invariant relations are obtained by multiplying

by

and

by

, giving the four remaining generators of

.

Under this presentation,

and

. Fixing the determinant specializes the two scalar variables

. All five relations lie in the square of the maximal ideal of the ambient power series ring, which also recovers the tangent dimensions above. This is a presentation deduced from Böckle’s split model, rather than the hypersurface description available for

.

R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
is a power series ring over
\mathcal{O}
of relative dimension
4
, so
(R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}})_{\mathfrak{q}}
is regular of dimension
4
. The kernel is therefore generated by one element by
[
Sta24
, Lemma 10.106.4]
.

∎

2.4.
Galois cohomology

We establish the local and global Euler characteristic formulae and the Greenberg-Wiles formula for representations over a
p
-adic local field. These will be used to study deformation rings at characteristic-zero points, where the adjoint
H^{0}
term can behave differently from its residual counterpart.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Let
K
be a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
. Let
G
be a profinite group, and
V
a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space with continuous
G
-action. We first state the Euler-Poincaré characteristic formulae in the version for
V
. When
V
is a finite
G
-module, one can see
[
NSW08
, Theorem 7.3.1]
and
[
NSW08
, 8.7.4]
.

Theorem 2.4.1

(Local Characteristic Formula)
.

Let
l
be a prime. Let
E
be an
l
-adic field, and suppose
G=G_{E}
.

1) For any
i\geq 0
, we have
\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E},V)<\infty
. If further
i\geq 3
, then we have
\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E},V)=0
.

2) If
l=p
, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{2}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E},V)=-[E:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]\cdot\operatorname{dim}_{K}V
. If
l\neq p
, we have
\sum_{i=0}^{2}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E},V)=0.

Proof.

See
[
BBD+13
, page 78, Theorem 3.9.1]
. For the case
l=p
and
K
a local field of characteristic
p
, this result is also true and one can see
[
BJ23
, Theorem 3.4.1]
.
∎

Theorem 2.4.2

(Global Characteristic Formula)
.

Let
E
be a number field, and
E_{S}
the maximal extension of
E
unramified outside a finite set of places
S
, containing the set of archimedean places
S_{\infty}
, and the finite places over
p
. Suppose that
K
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
G=G_{E,S}=\operatorname{Gal}(E_{S}/E)
.

1) For any
i\geq 0
, we have
\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E,S},V)<\infty
. If further
i\geq 3
, then we have
\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E,S},V)=0
.

2) We have
\sum_{i=0}^{2}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{i}(G_{E,S},V)=\sum_{v\in S_{\infty}}\operatorname{dim}_{K}V^{G_{E_{v}}}-[E:\mathbb{Q}]\cdot\operatorname{dim}_{K}V
.

Proof.

See
[
Kis03
, Lemma 9.7]
.
∎

Now let
F
be a number field, and
T
a finite set of places of
F
containing the finite places over
p
. Suppose
G=G_{F,T}
. Let
M
be a
G
-stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice of
V
, and
\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
the corresponding Galois representation, where
n=\operatorname{dim}_{K}V
. For simplicity, we write
\operatorname{ad}^{0}_{m}:=\operatorname{ad}^{0}\rho\otimes\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{m}
and
\operatorname{ad}_{m}:=\operatorname{ad}\rho\otimes\mathcal{O}/\varpi^{m}
.

We define the following complex as an analogue to
[
Gee22
, Section 3.23]
(or
[
CHT08
, page 21]
). Let
\partial:C^{i}(G,\operatorname{ad}^{0}_{m})\to C^{i+1}(G,\operatorname{ad}^{0}_{m})
be the usual coboundary map. Define
C^{i}_{T,\mathrm{loc}}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})

by

C^{0}_{T,\mathrm{loc}}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})=\oplus_{v\in T}C^{0}(G_{F_{v}},\ad_{m}),~~C^{1}_{T,\mathrm{loc}}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})=\oplus_{v\in T}C^{1}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}_{m}),

C^{i}_{T,\mathrm{loc}}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})=\oplus_{v\in T}C^{i}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}_{m}),i\geq 2.

Let
C^{0}_{0}(G,\ad^{0}_{m}):=C^{0}(G,\ad_{m})
, and let

C^{i}_{0}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})=C^{i}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})
for
i>0
. Then we let
H^{i}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})
denote the cohomology of the complex

C^{i}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m}):=C_{0}^{i}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\oplus C^{i-1}_{T,\mathrm{loc}}(G,\ad^{0}_{m}),

where the coboundary map is given by
(\phi,(\psi_{v}))\mapsto(\partial\phi,(\phi|_{G_{F_{v}}}-\partial\psi_{v}))
. Now we get an exact sequence of complexes

0\to C^{i-1}_{T,\mathrm{loc}}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to C^{i}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to C^{i}_{0}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to 0,

and the corresponding long exact sequence in cohomology is

0\to H^{0}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to H^{0}(G,\ad_{m})\to\bigoplus_{v\in T}H^{0}(G_{F_{v}},\ad_{m})\to

H^{1}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to H^{1}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to\bigoplus_{v\in T}H^{1}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}_{m})\to

H^{2}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to H^{2}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to\bigoplus_{v\in T}H^{2}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}_{m})\to H^{3}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}_{m})\to\cdots.

We can also replace
\ad^{0}_{m}
by
\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K
and obtain exact sequences similarly.

From Theorem
2.4.1
and Theorem
2.4.2
, we have

H^{i}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)=0,~~~H^{i}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)=0,~~i\geq 3,v\in T.

Hence, we get an exact sequence

H^{1}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\to\bigoplus_{v\in T}H^{1}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\to H^{2}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\to

H^{2}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\to\bigoplus_{v\in T}H^{2}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\to H^{3}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\to 0.

Using the Poitou-Tate duality (
[
NSW08
, Theorem 8.6.14]
) and the Five Lemma, we have
H^{2}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho)\cong H^{1}(G,(\ad^{0}\rho)^{\prime})^{\vee}
, where
(\ad^{0}\rho)^{\prime}=\operatorname{Hom}(\ad^{0}\rho,\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. Hence, we have

\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{2}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathcal{O}}H^{1}(G,(\ad^{0}\rho)^{\prime})^{\vee}=\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{1}(G,\Hom(\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K,K)(1)).

The last equality is obtained by taking the long exact sequence of the following sequence

0\to\Hom(\ad^{0}\rho,\mathcal{O})(1)\to\Hom(\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K,K)(1)\to\Hom(\ad^{0}\rho,K/\mathcal{O})(1)\to 0.

Using the Poitou-Tate duality(
[
NSW08
, Theorem 8.6.10]
), we have
H^{3}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)\cong(\varprojlim_{m}H^{0}(G,\ad^{0}_{m}(1))^{\vee})\otimes K
. Here we use
[
Rub00
, Appendix B, Proposition 2.3, 2.4 & 2.7]
and the Mittag-Leffler condition (see
[
Sta24
, Lemma 10.86.4]
).

Now we can deduce a generalized Greenberg-Wiles formula. For the usual version, one can see
[
CHT08
, Lemma 2.3.4]
or
[
NSW08
, Theorem 8.7.9]
.

Proposition 2.4.3
.

Write
W=\Hom(\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K,K)(1)
. Suppose that
\rho\otimes K
is irreducible, and
H^{0}(G,W)=0
. Then we have

\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{1}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)-\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{1}(G,W)=-1+|T|-\sum_{v\mid\infty}\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{0}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K).

Proof.

As
T
is not empty, we have
H^{0}_{T}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)=0
. Note that we have

\dim_{K}H^{0}(G,\ad\rho\otimes K)-\dim_{K}H^{0}(G,\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)=1,

\dim_{K}H^{0}(G_{F_{v}},\ad\rho\otimes K)-\dim_{K}H^{0}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K)=1,~~~v\in T.

Then our result follows from Theorem
2.4.1
, Theorem
2.4.2
and the previous discussions.
∎

2.5.
Global Galois deformation theory

We obtain dimension and connectedness bounds for completed local rings at points of characteristic zero. The ordinary case will be used in Subsection
5.3
to overcome the failure of residual adjoint
H^{0}
-vanishing. For the classical deformation-theoretic framework, see Böckle’s article in
[
BBD+13
]
.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Let
F
be a totally real field such that
p
splits completely, and
T
a finite set of places of
F
containing the finite places over
p
. Let
K
be a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\chi:G_{F,T}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character, and we suppose that
\bar{\chi}
is totally odd.

Let
R^{\mathrm{ps}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring parametrizing all liftings of the pseudo-representation
1+\bar{\chi}
with determinant
\chi
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R^{\mathrm{ps}}
satisfy
\dim R^{\mathrm{ps}}/\mathfrak{p}=1
(we do not require
\varpi\notin\mathfrak{p}
here) and suppose that it defines an irreducible pseudo-representation. Then by Definition
2.2.3
, we can construct an absolutely irreducible Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{p})^{\circ}:G_{F,T}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
, where
A
is the normalization of
R^{\mathrm{ps}}/\mathfrak{p}
, and hence a finite discrete valuation ring. By
[
SW99
, Corollary 2.14]
, we may write
\bar{\rho}_{b}=\rho(\mathfrak{p})^{\circ}\mod\mathfrak{m}_{A}
, where
0\neq b\in H^{1}(G_{F,T},\mathbb{F}(\bar{\chi}^{-1}))
. Moreover,
b
is unique up to multiplication by a non-zero scalar.

To study the local ring
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we compare it with the deformation ring of
\bar{\rho}_{b}
using Proposition
2.2.5
. This comparison of completed local rings applies in both characteristics.

Definition 2.5.1
.

[
Pan22a
, Definition 5.4.3]

Let
R
be a CNL ring. The connectedness dimension of
R
is defined to be

c(R):=\min_{Z_{1},Z_{2}}\{\dim(Z_{1}\cap Z_{2})\},

where
Z_{1},Z_{2}
are non-empty unions of irreducible components of
\Spec R
such that
Z_{1}\cup Z_{2}=\Spec R
.

Proposition 2.5.2
.

1)
\operatorname{dim}R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\geq 2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

2) The connectedness dimension
c(R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\mathfrak{p}})
is at least
2[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1
.

Proof.

We follow
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 7.4.6]
. Let
R_{b}
be the universal deformation ring of
\bar{\rho}_{b}
with determinant
\chi
, and let
\mathfrak{p}_{b}
be the prime defined by
\rho(\mathfrak{p})^{\circ}
. By Proposition
2.2.5
(1),
\widehat{(R^{\mathrm{ps}})_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong\widehat{(R_{b})_{\mathfrak{p}_{b}}}
. Write
h_{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H^{i}(G_{F,T},\ad^{0}\bar{\rho}_{b})
. The usual presentation gives
R_{b}\cong\mathcal{O}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{h_{1}}]]/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
with
r\leq h_{2}
, and the global Euler characteristic formula gives
h_{1}-h_{2}=2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
since
\bar{\rho}_{b}
is non-split and totally odd. Completing the power series ring at the pull-back of
\mathfrak{p}_{b}
gives a complete regular local ring of dimension
h_{1}
. Thus
\dim\widehat{(R_{b})_{\mathfrak{p}_{b}}}\geq 2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
, and
[
Pan22a
, Proposition 5.4.4]
gives
c(\widehat{(R_{b})_{\mathfrak{p}_{b}}})\geq 2[F:\mathbb{Q}]-1
. The claims follow from invariance of dimension under completion and
c((R^{\mathrm{ps}})_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq c(\widehat{(R^{\mathrm{ps}})_{\mathfrak{p}}})
, as in the proof of
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 7.4.6]
.
∎

In the rest of this subsection, we study the universal deformation ring of the Galois representation
\rho(\mathfrak{p}):=\rho(\mathfrak{p})^{\circ}\otimes k(\mathfrak{p})
. Suppose that
\varpi\notin\mathfrak{p}
,
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\mathbf{1}
for every
v|p
, and
\rho(\mathfrak{p})
is nearly ordinary at each
v|p
in the sense of
[
CM09
, Definition 2.2]
. For every
v|p
, fix a
G_{F_{v}}
-stable line
L_{v}\subset k(\mathfrak{p})^{2}
, which we regard as the special line in the nearly ordinary structure. Throughout this subsection, by “ordinary” we mean “nearly ordinary”; in particular, we impose no unramifiedness condition on the character acting on
L_{v}
or on the quotient.

For any finite place
v\in T
, denote by
D_{v}^{\square,\chi}:\mathfrak{U}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}\to\operatorname{Sets}
the functor such that for
(B,\mathfrak{m}_{B})\in\mathfrak{U}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
,
D_{v}^{\square,\chi}(B)
is the set of continuous representations
\rho_{B}:G_{F_{v}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(B)
satisfying
\rho_{B}\mod\mathfrak{m}_{B}=\rho(\mathfrak{p})|_{G_{F_{v}}}
and

. Using the same proof as
[
BBD+13
, page 27, Proposition 1.3.1]
, we can show that this functor is pro-representable by some
R_{v,k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\square,\chi}
, which is a CNL
k(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra.

For any
v|p
and
B\in\mathfrak{U}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
, an ordinary framed lifting of
(\rho(\mathfrak{p})|_{G_{F_{v}}},L_{v})
to

is a pair
(\rho_{B},L_{v,B})
, where
\rho_{B}\in D_{v}^{\square,\chi}(B)
and

is a
G_{F_{v}}
-stable rank one direct summand such that

. The resulting functor is pro-represented by a CNL

-algebra, which we denote by
R_{v,k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\triangle,\chi}
. See
[
CM09

and

are distinct, a stable line lifting the prescribed line

is uniquely determined whenever it exists; we will therefore usually suppress the line from the notation.

The following result is standard using local characteristic formula Theorem
2.4.1
.

Proposition 2.5.3
.

Let
v
be a finite place in the set
T
.

1) If
v\nmid p
, then we have
\operatorname{dim}R_{v,k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\square,\chi}\geq 3
.

2) If
v|p
, then we have
\operatorname{dim}R_{v,k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\triangle,\chi}\geq 5
.

Proof.

The proof is almost the same as in the finite-residue-field case. In that case, one can see
[
BBD+13
, page 64, Theorem 3.3.1]
for
v\nmid p
and
[
SW99
, Lemma 2.2]
for
v\mid p
.
∎

Let
S\subset T
be a finite set of primes of
F
. Define the functor
D_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\square_{S}}
from
\mathfrak{U}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}
to the category of sets sending
R
to the set of tuples
(\rho_{R},\{L_{v,R}\}_{v|p},(\alpha_{v})_{v\in S})
modulo the equivalence relation
\sim_{S}
where

•

\rho_{R}:G_{F,T}\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(R)
is a lifting of
\rho(\mathfrak{p})
to
R
with determinant
\chi
.

•

For every
v|p
,
L_{v,R}\subset R^{2}
is a
G_{F_{v}}
-stable rank one direct summand lifting
L_{v}
.

•

\alpha_{v}\in 1+M_{2}(\mathfrak{m}_{R}),v\in S
. Here
\mathfrak{m}_{R}
is the maximal ideal of
R
.

•

(\rho_{R},\{L_{v,R}\}_{v|p},\{\alpha_{v}\}_{v\in S})\sim_{S}(\rho^{\prime}_{R},\{L^{\prime}_{v,R}\}_{v|p},\{\alpha^{\prime}_{v}\}_{v\in S})
if there exists an element
\beta\in 1+M_{2}(\mathfrak{m}_{R})
with
\rho^{\prime}_{R}=\beta\rho_{R}\beta^{-1}
,

for every

, and

Then the functor
D_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\square_{S}}
is pro-representable by a similar argument of

BBD+13

. Note that in the case

. As the lines are uniquely determined by the corresponding liftings, we will omit them from the notation. Write

Proposition 2.5.4
.

Write
R_{\operatorname{loc}}=(\hat{\bigotimes}_{v|p}R_{v,k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\triangle,\chi})\hat{\otimes}_{k(\mathfrak{p})}(\hat{\bigotimes}_{v\nmid p,v\in T}R_{v,k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\square,\chi})

The proof is also the same as in the finite-residue-field case. For 1), one can see

Corollary 2.5.5
.

Assume
H^{0}(G_{F,T},\ad^{0}\rho(\mathfrak{p})(1))=0
.
We have
\operatorname{dim}R_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\operatorname{univ}}\geq[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Proof.

At first, by Proposition
2.5.3
and Krull’s principal ideal theorem, we have
\dim R_{\mathrm{loc}}\geq 3|T|+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]
. Then combining the second part of the previous proposition and Proposition

, we have

\dim R_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\square_{T},\chi}\geq 3|T|+2[F:\mathbb{Q}]+(-1+|T|-\sum_{v\mid\infty}\operatorname{dim}_{K}H^{0}(G_{F_{v}},\ad^{0}\rho\otimes K))=4|T|-1+[F:\mathbb{Q}].

Here the last equality is because
\bar{\chi}
is totally odd. Using the first part of the previous proposition, we have
\operatorname{dim}R_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\operatorname{univ}}\geq[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.
∎

For every
v|p
, let
L_{v}^{\circ}=L_{v}\cap A^{2}
. Since

is a discrete valuation ring,

is a saturated

-stable rank one submodule of

\bar{L}_{v}=L_{v}^{\circ}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{F}\subset\mathbb{F}^{2}
. We can similarly define the universal deformation ring
R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}
parametrizing all ordinary liftings of
(\bar{\rho}_{b},\{\bar{L}_{v}\}_{v|p})
with determinant
\chi
. Recall that
A
is the normalization of
R^{\mathrm{ps}}/\mathfrak{p}
, and hence its fraction field is

. By the universal property, we get a homomorphism
R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}\to A
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be the kernel of this map, and we assume the residue field of
\widehat{(R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}})_{\mathfrak{q}}}
is

, which is contained in

. Let
\rho_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\operatorname{univ}}
(resp.
\rho_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}
) be the universal lifting. Then
\hat{\rho}=\rho_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}\otimes_{R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}}(\widehat{(R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}})_{\mathfrak{q}}}\otimes_{K^{\prime}}k(\mathfrak{p}))
, together with the induced ordinary lines, defines an ordinary lifting of

. By the universal property, we get a natural map
R_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\operatorname{univ}}\to\widehat{(R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}})_{\mathfrak{q}}}\otimes_{K^{\prime}}k(\mathfrak{p})
.

Proposition 2.5.6
.

The map
f:R_{k(\mathfrak{p})}^{\operatorname{univ}}\to\widehat{(R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}})_{\mathfrak{q}}}\otimes_{K^{\prime}}k(\mathfrak{p})
is actually an isomorphism.

Proof.

Using
[
BJ23
, Proposition 3.1.5]
, we just need to show that

is formally étale. The proof is the nearly ordinary variant of
[
Kis03
, Proposition 9.5]
; see also
[
CM09
, Section 2.2, Remark on page 112]
and
[
BJ23
, Theorem 3.3.1]
. We briefly sketch it here, keeping track of the ordinary lines. To simplify our notation, we may suppose
K=K^{\prime}=k(\mathfrak{p})
(using
[
BJ23
, Lemma 3.2.6]
) and write
\widehat{R}=\widehat{(R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}})_{\mathfrak{q}}}\otimes_{K^{\prime}}k(\mathfrak{p})
.

We consider a commutative diagram with
C
an Artinian local
K
-algebra and
I
a square zero ideal. We need to show that there exists a unique dashed arrow
g
such that the two triangular subdiagrams commute.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C/I}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

By possibly conjugating
\hat{\rho}
and the associated ordinary lines by some matrix

satisfying
X\equiv\operatorname{id}\mod\mathfrak{m}_{\widehat{R}}
, we can suppose that
\rho_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}\otimes_{R_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}}\widehat{R}=\hat{\rho}
as ordinary deformations.

Choose a finitely generated free
\mathcal{O}
-subalgebra
C^{\circ}\subset C
surjecting onto
\mathcal{O}
under the natural surjection
c:C\twoheadrightarrow K
. Set
\mathfrak{n}=\operatorname{ker}c
and
\mathfrak{n}^{\circ}=\mathfrak{n}\cap C^{\circ}
. For any
n\geq 0
, define
C_{n}^{\circ}=\sum_{j>0}\varpi^{-nj}(\mathfrak{n}^{\circ})^{j}+C^{\circ}\subset C
. Note that the sum is finite since
\mathfrak{n}^{\circ}
is nilpotent. Then we have
\cup_{n>0}C_{n}^{\circ}=c^{-1}(\mathcal{O})
. We write
(C/I)_{n}^{\circ}=C_{n}^{\circ}/(I\cap C_{n}^{\circ})
. As

is compact, we know that
\rho_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}\otimes_{R_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}}C
factors through

for

sufficiently large.

After increasing

C_{n}^{\circ}
. Indeed, let

be the ordinary line associated with the deformation defined by the upper horizontal map. Choose an

, all the finitely many elements

belong to

for

sufficiently large. Thus

is a rank one direct summand of

whose base change to

is
L_{v,C}
. Since the representation takes values in

and

is

-stable,
L_{v,n}^{\circ}
is also
G_{F_{v}}
-stable.

Note that
R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}
is topologically finitely generated, the composite
R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}\to\widehat{R}\to C/I
factors through
(C/I)_{n}^{\circ}
for
n
sufficiently large.

From our discussions above, we know that for sufficiently large

\rho_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}\otimes_{R_{K}^{\operatorname{univ}}}C
, together with the lines
\{L_{v,n}^{\circ}\}_{v|p}

\rho_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}\otimes_{R_{b}^{\operatorname{univ}}}(C/I)_{n}^{\circ}
modulo
(I\cap C_{n}^{\circ})
. Here the compatibility of the ordinary lines follows from the commutativity of the diagram and, equivalently, from the uniqueness of a stable line lifting the prescribed line under the distinguished hypothesis. By the universal property, there exists a unique map

such that the corresponding ordinary deformations are equivalent. Taking the completion, we obtain a map

∎

In case (2), if

5.
The proof in the residually reducible case

In this section, we prove the non-ordinary Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in the residually reducible case.

This section is organized as follows. Subsection
5.1
fixes the auxiliary setting and establishes propagation; Subsections
5.2
and
5.3

Let

satisfies the following assumptions (the simultaneous choice is explained in Subsection

•

•

•

Remark 5.1.5
.

Let us compare our strategy with
[
Pan22a
, Section 7.4]
.

In Pan’s setting, the local ordinary ideal is generated by two elements by
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 7.4.4]

implies

[

, Corollary 7.4.5]
. Starting with a one-dimensional prime of

, Pan treats the irreducible, generic reducible and non-generic reducible cases separately, and connects

to automorphic points through potentially nice primes.

If

, Proposition
2.3.3
gives four local generators instead of two. The same argument only yields

, which does not give

from our lower bound on

. Thus the analogue of
[
Pan22a
, Corollary 7.4.5]
does not follow from this argument, and we cannot begin by choosing an ordinary one-dimensional prime on every

.

We instead use two special cases. First, a component of dimension at least
1+2d
is pro-modular if it contains a one-dimensional ordinary prime for which the ratio of the two local characters is not
\varepsilon^{\pm 1}
at any
v|p
, by Proposition
5.2.2
; the associated global representation need not be irreducible. Second, a globally irreducible one-dimensional ordinary prime is pro-modular without this local genericity assumption, by Corollary
5.3.5
. By Lemma
5.1.2
, every irreducible component of
\Spec R^{\mathrm{ps}}
containing such a prime is then pro-modular.

The remaining step is an induction on the set
I\subseteq\Sigma_{p}
of places where ordinarity has been imposed. At each stage we impose the four local reducibility equations at one further place and choose a globally irreducible one-dimensional prime of the resulting intersection. For the enlarged set
I
, we retain the ordinary characters already prescribed and choose the new one according to a stable line of the associated representation, as in
[
Pan22a
, Lemma 5.3.2]
. The corresponding deformation problem is ordinary at these places and unrestricted at the other places above
p
. The Greenberg-Wiles dimension estimate, together with Proposition
2.2.5
(2), gives a component of the corresponding partial ordinary pseudo-deformation ring through this point of dimension at least
2d-|I|
; see Lemma
5.4.1
. This recovers enough dimension to repeat the construction: even after the last cut by four equations, the dimension is at least
d-3>2
, whereas the globally reducible locus has dimension at most
2
.

The new component need not lie in the preceding one; they meet at the chosen globally irreducible one-dimensional prime. Iterating, we reach a globally irreducible one-dimensional prime which is ordinary at every place above
p
, so the second special case applies. Lemma
5.1.2
then propagates pro-modularity backwards along these intersections to the original component

. This induction avoids the need for a direct ordinary-intersection estimate on

, and the two-generator bound is no longer essential.

Combining
[
Pan22a
, Theorem 7.1.1]
, from now on, we assume
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}=\omega^{-1}
for any

h_{t}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
2h_{t}-k\notin\{1,-1\}
. Then
\Lambda_{F}/\mathfrak{q}_{2}\cong\mathcal{O}
, so
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
is one-dimensional and
\varpi\notin\mathfrak{q}_{2}
. By the finite surjectivity above, choose a one-dimensional prime
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}
of
C_{1}^{\mathrm{ord}}
above
\mathfrak{q}_{2}
. If
\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime})
were reducible, Leopoldt’s conjecture would force the Sen weights of each global character to be parallel. This is impossible: the two local weights at
v_{i}
differ by
1
, whereas their difference at
v_{t}
, for
t\neq i
, is
\pm(2h_{t}-k)\neq\pm 1
. Thus
\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime})
is irreducible. Since
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}
has characteristic zero,
H^{0}(G_{F,\Sigma},\ad^{0}\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime})(1))=0
: a non-zero map
\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime})\to\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime})(1)
would be an isomorphism and would force
\varepsilon^{2}=1
by taking determinants. The point
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}
need not be regular de Rham; below we only use its ordinarity and global irreducibility in characteristic zero.

Proposition 5.3.4
.

If
\bar{\chi}=\omega=\omega^{-1}
, then
\mathfrak{q}
is pro-modular.

Proof.

By the preceding arguments, we may assume
\mathfrak{a}=(X_{i}-a_{i,j})
and use the point
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}
constructed above. Choose a lattice of
\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime})
whose residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{b^{\prime}}
is a non-split extension of
\bar{\chi}
by
\mathbf{1}
, and use the ordinary lines prescribed by
\Sigma^{o}
. Let
\mathfrak{q}_{b^{\prime}}
be the corresponding prime of
R_{b^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ord}}
. The same completed local comparison gives
\widehat{(R^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}_{\Sigma^{o}})_{\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}}}\cong\widehat{(R_{b^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ord}})_{\mathfrak{q}_{b^{\prime}}}}
. By Corollary
2.5.7
and the characteristic-zero vanishing just proved, the latter ring has dimension at least
d
. Lemma
5.3.1
shows that
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}
is pro-modular. By Lemma
5.1.2
, every ambient irreducible component through
\mathfrak{q}_{2}^{\prime}
is pro-modular. Taking one containing

proves the pro-modularity of
\mathfrak{q}_{1}
and hence of the original prime
\mathfrak{q}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}\in\Spec R^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
be a one-dimensional prime such that
\rho(\mathfrak{q})
is irreducible. Then
\mathfrak{q}
is pro-modular, and every irreducible component of

containing

is pro-modular. No restriction on the residue characteristic of

or local genericity at

Proof.

The pro-modularity of
\mathfrak{q}
follows from Propositions
5.3.2
and

. The assertion about irreducible components follows from Lemma

.
∎

Remark 5.3.6
.

Another possible approach to Corollary

is to use an ordinary big

theorem. We briefly explain the extra issues this would entail.

Under the hypotheses of

.

In the ordinary case, we can use

to find a suitable base change. However, in the non-ordinary case, we need the assumption that

(to use the

5.4.
Induction on the ordinary places

We now complete the argument by imposing ordinarity one place at a time. After cutting by the four local reducibility equations at a new place, we choose a globally irreducible one-dimensional prime and prescribe an ordinary line there. A deformation-theoretic dimension estimate then gives a sufficiently large partial ordinary component through this prime, retaining all the ordinary characters already prescribed. The new component need not lie in the preceding one. Once all places above
p
are ordinary, Corollary
5.3.5
applies, and Lemma
5.1.2
propagates pro-modularity backwards to the original component.

For
v|p
, write
J_{v}=\ker(R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps}}\to R_{v}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}})
, and also write
J_{v}
for its image ideal in
R^{\mathrm{ps}}
. Given
I\subseteq\Sigma_{p}
, put
R_{I}^{\mathrm{ps}}=R^{\mathrm{ps}}/(J_{v}\mid v\in I)
. For each
v\in I
, the universal local pseudo-representation over
R_{I}^{\mathrm{ps}}
is the sum of two characters
\psi_{v,1},\psi_{v,2}
lifting
\mathbf{1}
and
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}
, respectively. These characters are uniquely determined since
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}=\omega\neq\mathbf{1}
.

For
I^{o}\subseteq I
, let
R_{I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
be the quotient of
R_{I}^{\mathrm{ps}}
imposing
\psi_{v,1}
-ordinarity for
v\in I^{o}
and
\psi_{v,2}
-ordinarity for
v\in I\setminus I^{o}
, in the sense of Definition
4.2.1
. Thus we prescribe the character on the ordinary stable line, not merely the reducibility of the local pseudo-representation; the lifting character is allowed to vary. No additional local condition is imposed at places of
\Sigma\setminus I
. For
I=\varnothing
this is
R^{\mathrm{ps}}
, and for
I=\Sigma_{p}
this is the prescribed ordinary ring
R^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}_{I^{o}}
of Subsection
4.2
.

Lemma 5.4.1
.

Let
I^{o}\subseteq I\subseteq\Sigma_{p}
, and let
\mathfrak{q}
be a one-dimensional prime of
R_{I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
such that
\rho(\mathfrak{q})
is irreducible. Then
\mathfrak{q}
lies on an irreducible component of
\Spec R_{I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
of dimension at least
2d-|I|
. This holds in both residue characteristics.

Proof.

After enlarging
\mathcal{O}
, choose a Galois-stable lattice of
\rho(\mathfrak{q})
whose residual representation
\bar{\rho}_{b}
is a non-split extension of
\bar{\chi}
by
\mathbf{1}
. By Lemma
4.2.2
,
\rho(\mathfrak{q})
has the prescribed ordinary stable line at every
v\in I
. Intersecting these lines with the lattice gives stable rank one direct summands. Let
R_{b,I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ord}}
be the universal deformation ring of
\bar{\rho}_{b}
with determinant
\chi
, ordinary along these prescribed residual lines at
v\in I
and unrestricted at the remaining places of
\Sigma
. The lattice defines a one-dimensional prime
\mathfrak{q}_{b}
of this ring above
\mathfrak{q}
.

Put
r=|I|
and
h^{0}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H^{0}(G_{F,\Sigma},\ad^{0}\bar{\rho}_{b}(1))
. The local framed rings have dimensions
6
at
v\in I
and
7
at
v\in\Sigma_{p}\setminus I
, by Proposition
2.1.4
and Proposition
2.1.1
. For the first assertion, if the prescribed residual line has character
\mathbf{1}
, we twist locally by the Teichmüller lift of
\omega
and change basis; since
p=3
, this does not change the determinant. At
v\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}
, the unrestricted local framed ring has dimension
4
by
[
Gee22
, Theorem 3.31]
.

Since
\bar{\rho}_{b}
has only scalar endomorphisms, the Greenberg-Wiles calculation underlying
[
Gee22
, Proposition 3.24]
applies; compare
[
Pan22a
, Section 5.4.2]
. More precisely, with framings at
\Sigma
, the global ring has a presentation over the completed tensor product of these local rings with the number of variables minus the number of relations at least
|\Sigma|-1-d-h^{0}
. Removing the
4|\Sigma|-1
framing variables and using the local presentations gives, for every minimal prime
\mathfrak{Q}
of
R_{b,I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ord}}
,

\dim R_{b,I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ord}}/\mathfrak{Q}\geq 1+2r+3(d-r)-d-h^{0}=1+2d-r-h^{0}\geq 2d-r.

Here
h^{0}\leq 1
, since
\bar{\rho}_{b}
is a non-split extension of two distinct characters. In particular, no vanishing of this group is assumed.

Choose such a minimal prime
\mathfrak{Q}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}_{b}
. The representation at
\mathfrak{Q}
is irreducible, since the reducible locus is closed and does not contain
\mathfrak{q}_{b}
. Apply Proposition
2.2.5
(2) to the inverse image of
\mathfrak{Q}
in the unrestricted deformation ring
R_{b}
. Writing
\mathfrak{P}
for its pull-back to
R_{I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
, we obtain
\dim R_{I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}/\mathfrak{P}\geq\dim R_{b,I,I^{o}}^{\mathrm{ord}}/\mathfrak{Q}\geq 2d-r
. Since
\mathfrak{P}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
, any irreducible component containing
V(\mathfrak{P})
gives the assertion.
∎

Proposition 5.4.2
.

Every irreducible component
C
of
\Spec R^{\mathrm{ps}}
of dimension at least
1+2d
is pro-modular.

Proof.

Order the places above
p
as
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
, and put
I_{j}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{j}\}
. We construct prescribed subsets
I_{j}^{o}\subseteq I_{j}
, globally irreducible one-dimensional primes
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
, and, for
j<d
, irreducible components
C_{j}
of
\Spec R_{I_{j},I_{j}^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
through
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
with
\dim C_{j}\geq 2d-j
. Start with
C_{0}=C
and
I_{0}=I_{0}^{o}=\varnothing
.

Suppose
C_{j-1}
has been chosen, where
1\leq j\leq d
. By Proposition
2.3.3
,
J_{v_{j}}
is generated by at most four elements. Its vanishing locus
Z_{j}
in
C_{j-1}
therefore satisfies
\dim Z_{j}\geq\dim C_{j-1}-4\geq 2d-j-3\geq d-3>2
. Since the globally reducible locus has dimension at most
2
, Lemma
5.1.3
gives a globally irreducible one-dimensional prime
\mathfrak{q}_{j}\in Z_{j}
.

At the places in
I_{j-1}
, the representation
\rho(\mathfrak{q}_{j})
has the ordinary lines already prescribed. At
v_{j}
, its local pseudo-representation is reducible, so the local representation has a stable line. Choose one, and extend
I_{j-1}^{o}
to
I_{j}^{o}
according to whether its character lifts
\mathbf{1}
or
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v_{j}}}}
. By Lemma
4.2.2
,
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
is a prime of
R_{I_{j},I_{j}^{o}}^{\mathrm{ps},\mathrm{ord}}
. If
j<d
, Lemma
5.4.1
supplies the required component
C_{j}
through
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
. This completes the construction.

The final prime
\mathfrak{q}_{d}
is ordinary at every place above
p
, and hence is pro-modular by Corollary
5.3.5
. We now descend from
j=d
to
j=1
. If
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
is pro-modular, Lemma
5.1.2
shows that every irreducible component of
\Spec R^{\mathrm{ps}}
through it is pro-modular. Choose one containing
C_{j-1}
, which contains
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
by construction. Thus all primes of
C_{j-1}
are pro-modular, including
\mathfrak{q}_{j-1}
when
j>1
. For
j=1
this proves that
C_{0}=C
is pro-modular.
∎

Applying this proposition to the component chosen in Subsection 5.1 and using Corollary
3.3.8
completes the proof of Theorem
5.1.1
in the exceptional case
p=3
.

5.5.
Potential pro-modularity

We now combine the componentwise pro-modularity results proved above with
[
Zha24
]
. Abelian base change arranges the auxiliary hypotheses imposed at the beginning of this section. The previously treated local types are covered by
[
Zha24
]
, after a finite twist if necessary, while the exceptional type at
p=3
is covered by Subsection
5.4
.

Let
F
be a totally real abelian field in which
p
splits completely. Let
\Sigma_{p}
be the set of places of
F
above
p
, and let
\Sigma
be a finite set of finite places containing
\Sigma_{p}
. Let
\bar{\chi}:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathbb{F}^{\times}
be a continuous totally odd character which extends to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and let
\chi:G_{F,\Sigma}\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a lift of
\bar{\chi}
which is de Rham at every place above
p
.

Theorem 5.5.1
.

Assume that
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{v}}}\neq\mathbf{1}
for every
v|p
. Then there exists a totally real abelian extension
F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}
containing
F
, with
[F_{1}:F]
even, in which
p
splits completely, with the following property. Let
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{F_{1}}
be the universal pseudo-deformation ring parametrizing pseudo-deformations of
(1+\bar{\chi})|_{G_{F_{1}}}
, unramified outside the places of
F_{1}
above
\Sigma
, with fixed determinant
\chi|_{G_{F_{1}}}
. Then every irreducible component of
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{F_{1}}
of dimension at least
1+2[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
is pro-modular and has dimension
1+2[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Remark 5.5.2
.

For the general determinant in Theorem
5.5.1
, Leopoldt’s conjecture gives
\chi=\psi\varepsilon^{k}
with
\psi
of finite order, as in Subsection 5.1. Since
\bar{\psi}=\bar{\chi}\omega^{-k}
extends to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, the character
\chi_{0}=\widetilde{\bar{\psi}}\varepsilon^{k}
also extends to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. The quotient
\chi_{0}\chi^{-1}
has finite
p
-power order, so, as
p
is odd, it has a square root
\varphi
of finite
p
-power order, unramified outside
\Sigma
. Twisting by
\varphi
identifies the fixed-determinant pseudo-deformation rings for
\chi
and
\chi_{0}
, preserving components and their dimensions. The automorphic twist preserves pro-modularity, after shrinking the tame level if necessary; see
[
Pan22a
, Section 3.2.2]
. Thus it suffices to prove the theorem when
\chi
extends to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Proof.

By Remark
5.5.2
, we may impose the additional assumption made there. The same application of the Grunwald–Wang theorem used to deduce the potential pro-modularity theorem in
[
Zha24
, Theorem 1.0.3]
allows us to choose
F_{1}
as follows. Write
\Sigma_{1}
for the set of places of
F_{1}
above
\Sigma
, and
\Sigma_{1,p}
for the set of places above
p
. In addition to the properties in the statement, we may require that

•

p\mid\operatorname{Nm}(w)-1
for every
w\in\Sigma_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{1,p}
;

•

[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]>7|\Sigma_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{1,p}|+6
;

•

\chi|_{G_{F_{1}}}
is unramified outside
\Sigma_{1,p}
and
\chi(\operatorname{Frob}_{w})\equiv 1\pmod{\varpi}
for every
w\in\Sigma_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{1,p}
.

For completeness, the degree bound can be arranged together with the local conditions. Let
m
be the number of rational primes below
\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}
. At each such prime
\ell
, prescribe a finite abelian local extension containing the completions of
F
, killing the ramification of
\chi
, and arranging the first and third conditions above. Require it also to contain an unramified extension of degree at least
M
, where
M>14m
. Apply
[
AT68
, Theorem 5, p. 80]
to the corresponding local characters, taking them trivial at
p
and at infinity, and then form the compositum with

. Additional totally real abelian extensions split at
p
arrange
[F_{1}:F]
even and
D:=[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]>12
. Since every local degree at the
m
prescribed primes is at least
M
, we have
|\Sigma_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{1,p}|\leq mD/M<D/14
, so
D>7|\Sigma_{1}\setminus\Sigma_{1,p}|+6
. If

, only the degree and splitting conditions need to be arranged.

Since
p
splits completely in
F_{1}
and
\bar{\chi}
extends to
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, the local residual character
\bar{\chi}|_{G_{F_{1,w}}}
has the same type for every
w|p
. If it is different from both
\mathbf{1}
and
\omega^{-1}
, the result follows from
[
Zha24
, Theorem 1.0.2(1)]
. If it equals
\omega^{-1}
and
p\geq 5
, twist by the Teichmüller lift
\eta
of
\bar{\chi}^{-1}|_{G_{F_{1}}}
. This replaces
1+\bar{\chi}
by
1+\bar{\chi}^{-1}
and
\chi
by
\eta^{2}\chi
. The new local residual ratio is
\omega\neq\mathbf{1},\omega^{-1}
, and all the other hypotheses of
[
Zha24
, Theorem 1.0.2(1)]
are unchanged. Applying that theorem and twisting back proves the assertion. It remains to treat

with local residual ratio
\omega^{-1}
; here Proposition
5.4.2
gives pro-modularity.

It remains only to record the dimension assertion in the exceptional case. Let
C
be one of the above pro-modular components. Since

is abelian, Leopoldt’s conjecture holds for

, and the reducible locus has dimension at most

. As

∎

Fix an extension
F_{1}/F
as in Theorem
5.5.1
. Use the common tame level of Subsection 5.1, shrinking it at places in

if necessary to include the finite twists above. Let

be the resulting big Hecke algebra without the auxiliary character

, where

, and let

be a maximal ideal corresponding to

\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}:=(\mathbb{T}_{\psi})_{\mathfrak{m}}
, with its natural surjection

. Such a maximal ideal is supplied, in particular, whenever

Proposition 5.5.3
.

The big Hecke algebra

Proof.

By Corollary

, each irreducible component of

6.
The proof in the residually irreducible case

In this section, we prove the non-ordinary Fontaine-Mazur conjecture in the residually irreducible case.

Let
p
be an odd prime. Throughout this section, we denote by
K
a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and its residue field
\mathbb{F}
.

6.1.
The proof

We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1.1
.

Let
F
be a totally real extension of
\mathbb{Q}
in which
p
splits completely. Suppose

\rho:\Gal(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muF\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu/F)\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})

(2)

(3)

For any

(4)

\det\rho(c)=-1
for any complex conjugation

(5)

\bar{\rho}
arises from a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation

of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})

Then
\rho
arises from a regular algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})

Compared to
[

\begin{pmatrix}\eta&*\\

or
\begin{pmatrix}\eta\omega&*\\

for any

if

.

Remark 6.1.2
.

In the ordinary case, the result is known by
[
SW01
]
,
[
Kis09
]
and
[
HT15
]
. See
[

, Remark 9.0.2]
.

Let
\Sigma
contain all places above

and all finite places
at which either
\rho
or
\pi_{0}
is ramified.
Using
[
Gee22
, Fact 4.23 & 4.27]
, we make a soluble totally

\bar{\rho}
.
Thus
\bar{\rho}
remains absolutely irreducible.
Keeping the same notation for the base-changed data, we may
arrange that:

•

p
splits completely in
F
;

•

\rho|_{I_{F_{v}}}
is unipotent for every

;

•

for every

,

\mathrm{Nm}(v)\equiv 1\pmod{p}
and

\bar{\rho}|_{G_{F_{v}}}
is trivial;

•

[F:\mathbb{Q}]
is even and

[F:\mathbb{Q}]\geq 4|\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{p}|+2
;

•

\pi_{0}
is unramified outside
\Sigma
and

\pi_{0,v}^{\mathrm{Iw}_{v}}\neq 0
for every

.

Proof of Theorem
6.1.1
.

We keep notations in Section
4.3
. Under our assumptions above, by Proposition

, the surjection
R^{\{\mathbf{1}\}}\to\mathbb{T}^{\{\mathbf{1}\}}_{\mathfrak{m}_{\mathbf{1}}}
has nilpotent kernel. Note that
\rho
defines a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

, and hence we can also view
\mathfrak{p}
as a maximal ideal of

A.1.
Banach space representations

In this subsection, we recall some basic theory about Banach space representations, and one can find more details and proofs in
[
Paš13
, Section 4]
. Throughout, we let
p
be a prime and
G
be a
p
-adic analytic group. Let
K
be a
p
-adic local field with its ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, a uniformizer
\varpi
and the residue field
\mathbb{F}
.

A
K
-Banach space representation
\Pi
of
G
is a
K
-Banach space
\Pi
together with a
G
-action by continuous linear automorphisms. A Banach space representation
\Pi
is
unitary
, if there exists a
G
-invariant norm defining the topology on
\Pi
. The existence of such norm is equivalent to the existence of
an open bounded
G
-invariant
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
\Theta
in
\Pi
. A unitary
K
-Banach space representation
is
admissible
if the space of invariants
(\Theta\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbb{F})^{H}
is finite-dimensional for every open subgroup
H
of
G
. We say that a
K
-Banach space representation
\Pi
is

, if it does not contain
a proper closed
G
-invariant subspace.

For a unitary admissible absolutely irreducible
K
-Banach space representation
\Pi
of
G
, we say that
\Pi
is
ordinary
if it is a subquotient of a parabolic induction of a unitary character, so that

\Pi
is either a unitary character
\Pi\cong\eta\circ\det
,
a twist of the universal unitary completion of the smooth Steinberg representation by a unitary character

\Pi\cong\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}\otimes\eta\circ\det
, , or
\Pi
is a unitary parabolic induction of a unitary character. Here
\mathrm{Sp}
is defined by the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbf{1}\rightarrow(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}\mathbf{1})_{\mathrm{sm}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}\rightarrow 0,

and
\widehat{\mathrm{Sp}}
is its universal unitary completion.

A.2.
The case

In this subsection, we recall the case
G=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
.
Keep
p,K,\mathcal{O},\varpi,\mathbb{F}
as in the previous subsection. Let
Z
be the centre of
G
and
B
be the upper triangular Borel subgroup of
G
.

Let
\zeta:Z\to\mathcal{O}^{\times}
be a continuous character. A subscript
\zeta
indicates the full subcategory of
G
-representations on which
Z
acts through
\zeta
. Let
\mathfrak{C}(\mathcal{O})
be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Mod}_{G,\zeta}^{\mathrm{pro\,aug}}(\mathcal{O})
anti-equivalent to
\operatorname{Mod}^{\mathrm{l\,fin}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathcal{O})
via Pontryagin duality.

Let
\operatorname{Irr}_{G,\zeta}
be the set of irreducible representations in
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{G,\zeta}(\mathbb{F})
. We write
\pi\sim\pi^{\prime}
if there exists
\pi_{1},\cdots,\pi_{n}\in\mathrm{Irr}_{G,\zeta}
such that
\pi\cong\pi_{1}
,
\pi^{\prime}\cong\pi_{n}
, and for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,
\pi_{i}\cong\pi_{i+1}
or
\Ext^{1}_{G}(\pi_{i},\pi_{i+1})\neq 0
or
\Ext^{1}_{G}(\pi_{i+1},\pi_{i})\neq 0
. The relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation on
\mathrm{Irr}_{G,\zeta}
. A block
\mathfrak{B}
is an equivalence class of
\sim
.

In this setting, the blocks containing an absolutely irreducible representation have been determined (for example, see
[
Paš16
, page 1303]
). We list them here:

(1)

\mathfrak{B}=\{\pi\}
with
\pi
supersingular;

(2)

\mathfrak{B}=\{(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\omega^{-1})_{\mathrm{sm}},(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}\chi_{2}\otimes\chi_{1}\omega^{-1})_{\mathrm{sm}}\}
with
\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}\neq\mathbf{1},\omega^{\pm 1}
;

(3)

p>2
and
\mathfrak{B}=\{(\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}\chi\otimes\chi\omega^{-1})_{\mathrm{sm}}\}
;

(4)

p\geq 5
and
\mathfrak{B}=\{\mathbf{1},\mathrm{Sp},(\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{B}\omega\otimes\omega^{-1})_{\mathrm{sm}}\}\otimes\chi\circ\det
;

(5)

p=3
and
\mathfrak{B}=\{\mathbf{1},\mathrm{Sp},\omega\circ\det,\mathrm{Sp}\otimes\omega\circ\det\}\otimes\chi\circ\det
;

(6)

p=2
and
\mathfrak{B}=\{\mathbf{1},\mathrm{Sp}\}\otimes\chi\circ\det
;

where
\chi,\chi_{1},\chi_{2}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
are smooth characters and
\omega:\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}^{\times}
is the character
\omega(x)=x|x|\pmod{\varpi}
.

For each case, we can attach a semi-simple
2
-dimensional representation
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}
of
G_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
over
\mathbb{F}
to each block using the normalized Colmez functor
\mathbf{V}
(see
[
Paš13
, Section 5.7]
). This is given by the following list:

•

\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}=\mathbf{V}(\pi)
if
\pi
is supersingular. In this case,
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}
is absolutely irreducible.

•

\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}=\chi_{1}\oplus\chi_{2}
if
\chi_{2}\chi_{1}^{-1}\neq\mathbf{1},\omega^{\pm 1}
.

•

\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}=\chi\oplus\chi
in case (3) and (6).

•

\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}=\chi\oplus\chi\omega
in case (4) and (5).

Under this correspondence, the determinant of
\bar{\rho}_{\mathfrak{B}}
is
\zeta\varepsilon\mod\varpi
, where

is the

-adic cyclotomic character.

\mathfrak{B}

\pi_{\mathfrak{B}}=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}}\pi

\mathfrak{B}_{i}

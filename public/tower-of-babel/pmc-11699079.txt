An optimized public opinion communication system in social media networks based on K-means cluster analysis
Authors: Mingchao Qi, JunQiang Zhao, Yan Feng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study proposes a public opinion monitoring model that combines the K-means clustering algorithm with Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to enhance the accuracy and effectiveness of public opinion monitoring on social media. The model's performance across various dissemination indicators is studied in detail. Through experiments conducted on social media datasets, the study comprehensively evaluates the model from four dimensions: dissemination speed, scope, depth, and sentiment dissemination effectiveness. The experimental results indicate that the proposed optimization model excels in multiple areas, particularly in dissemination depth and sentiment dissemination effectiveness. Specifically, in the three dimensions of dissemination speed, the proposed model achieves scores of 4.3, 4.2, and 4.4 in initial dissemination speed, decay speed, and peak dissemination speed, respectively. In the dimensions of user coverage, geographic coverage, and platform coverage under dissemination scope, the model scores 4.4, 4.5, and 4.3, demonstrating a broad dissemination capability. Additionally, in the dimensions of hierarchical dissemination depth and key node influence within dissemination depth, the model scores 4.3 and 4.5, indicating excellent performance in multi-level dissemination and key node activation. In sentiment dissemination effectiveness, the model receives scores of 4.4, 4.5, and 4.4 in emotional tendency change, polarity distribution, and diffusion intensity, showcasing its advantages in sentiment classification and dissemination. Sensitivity analysis further validates the model's sensitivity to parameter settings, with experiments showing that reasonable adjustments to parameters such as the K value, PSO inertia weight, and learning factors can reduce the Sum of Squared Errors to 3209.72. Meanwhile, it can improve clustering purity to 0.822 and raise the Rand index to 0.623. Therefore, this study offers an efficient and reliable solution for public opinion monitoring on social media, providing valuable reference significance.

Introduction

In today's digital age, social media networks have become an important platform for people to access and share information. With its open and free characteristics, social media networks not only greatly facilitate the dissemination of information but also profoundly impact the formation and communication of public opinion [1,2]. In this context, studying the effectiveness of public opinion dissemination in social media networks is of great significance. The research on the efficacy of public opinion dissemination mainly focuses on indicators, such as the scope, influence, and duration of public opinion information communication in social media networks [3]. Although the K-means clustering and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithms have wide applications in their respective fields, research on combining the two within public opinion monitoring systems remains relatively scarce. On the one hand, while the K-means algorithm is highly efficient in cluster analysis, it is sensitive to initial conditions, which can lead to the problem of local optima. On the other hand, the PSO algorithm, as a swarm intelligence-based optimization algorithm, possesses strong global search capabilities, effectively addressing this issue. Therefore, exploring the integration of K-means clustering with the PSO algorithm in public opinion monitoring systems holds significant research value. This study's motivation lies in optimizing existing public opinion monitoring systems by leveraging the strengths of both K-means and PSO algorithms to improve their ability to analyze and predict public opinion dissemination on social media.

In previous studies, Jia et al. (2020) explored the paths and patterns of public opinion dissemination in social media networks, finding that key disseminators played a crucial role in the dissemination process. They noted that the rapid spread of public opinion often depends on a few highly influential users, whose behaviors and interactions could markedly affect the scope and speed of information diffusion [4]. Campisi et al. (2020) conducted a systematic study of clustering algorithms in public opinion analysis, particularly the application of the K-means clustering algorithm in social media data. Their research revealed that while the K-means algorithm effectively categorized social media data into different public opinion classes, it was highly sensitive to the selection of initial centroids, potentially leading to instability in clustering results [5]. Luo and Zhang (2023) studied the PSO algorithm's application in data mining, discovering that this algorithm exhibited excellent global search capabilities, effectively overcoming the K-means algorithm's tendency to fall into local optima [6]. Xie et al. (2020) explored the potential of optimizing public opinion monitoring systems by combining multiple algorithms. Their experiments demonstrated that integrating K-means clustering with PSO algorithms could significantly enhance the efficiency and accuracy of public opinion analysis, offering good scalability when handling large-scale social media data [7].

Previous studies have primarily focused on the application of individual algorithms, such as the K-means clustering or PSO algorithms. While the K-means algorithm performs well in data grouping, its sensitivity to the selection of initial centroids often leads to local optima. Although the PSO algorithm has strong global search capabilities, it can encounter high computational complexity when dealing with high-dimensional, complex data. There has been a lack of in-depth research on the combined application of multiple algorithms. This study integrates the K-means clustering with the PSO algorithms within a public opinion monitoring system. By using the PSO algorithm to optimize the initial centroids for K-means clustering, this approach not only overcomes the local optima issue inherent in the K-means algorithm but also enhances the clustering results’ stability and accuracy. This combination of algorithms offers a new perspective for improving system performance in public opinion analysis. Application of K-means and PSO algorithms in the public opinion monitoring system.

The K-means algorithm is the most classic data clustering algorithm, and it is still active in various systems today. The principle is to determine the Euclidean distance between the data through calculation to analyze the similarity of the data [15]. The calculation process of K-means is mainly divided into two stages. First, the class family of the algorithm is set, all kinds of information are classified into corresponding class families, and the relationship between each class family is determined by calculating the spatial distance between them. In the calculation process, if it is found that the initial clustering center does not meet the classification requirements, it needs to be adjusted until it meets the standards. The K-means algorithm has many advantages, such as less calculation time and low use cost, which makes this algorithm widely used. Of course, the K-means algorithm is not perfect. For example, it is influenced by the initial clustering center, and the final clustering result can change with the initial clustering center. If the input order of data is adjusted, the final clustering result also changes. In K-means classification, the initial clustering center of the class family continues to be adjusted to the best position. Because of the limitations of the K-means algorithm, when the data volume is large, this algorithm repeats the iterative calculation, which consumes a long time and becomes inefficient. To solve this problem, this study combines the PSO algorithm to optimize the K-means algorithm [[16], [17], [18]].

PSO is a group-based search method. Specifically, it is calculated by groups of different individual particles. In calculating PSO, individual particles start flying according to random velocity values, and their velocity and position change continuously during the flight. During particle flight, its position is related to the individual optimal position, the group optimal position, and the particle with the current speed [19]. In this study, a fitness function with the current position as a variable can be established to reflect the performance of particles, and the expression is shown in equations (1), (2).(1)Vi(t+1)=w×Vi(t)+ci×ri×(Pbesti−Xi)+c2×r2×(Gbest−Xi)(2)Xi(t+1)=Xi(t)+Vi(t+1)Vi(t) and Xi(t) represent the velocity and position of particle i in the t th iteration. Pbesti and Gbest refer to the optimal position of particle individually and collectively. w means the inertia weight coefficient. ci and c2 are learning factors; ri and r2 stand for random weights of diverse parts participating in the definition of particle velocity. If the current fitness value of a particle is better than the global optimal fitness value, the global optimal position is updated.(8)It is necessary to check whether the algorithm satisfies the termination condition. Optimization of public opinion monitoring system under social media

This study proposes an optimized architecture based on the K-means clustering and PSO algorithms to improve the public opinion monitoring system's effectiveness in social media networks. The architecture includes five main steps: data preprocessing, feature extraction, algorithm optimization, public opinion analysis, and evaluation. The architecture of the model is presented in Fig. 1:Fig. 1The architecture of the optimized public opinion monitoring system designed.Fig. 1

The architecture of the optimized public opinion monitoring system designed.

Data preprocessing forms the foundation of the entire architecture. In this stage, the system gathers raw data from social media platforms, including user-generated text, comments, reposts, and more [[24], [25], [26]]. The data is then cleaned to remove noise and irrelevant information, such as advertisements and duplicate content. The cleaned data undergoes formatting, converting text data into formats suitable for algorithmic processing, such as term frequency matrices or word embedding vectors. During the feature extraction stage, the system applies natural language processing (NLP) techniques to extract features from the text data, using methods such as word vectors and sentiment analysis. Additionally, structural features of the social network, such as node degree, centrality, and clustering coefficients, are extracted to identify key disseminators and paths of public opinion dissemination [27]. The core part of the architecture is the combined application of K-means and PSO algorithms. The K-means algorithm initializes by setting the initial centroids and the number of clusters. To overcome the sensitivity of K-means to the selection of initial centroids, the PSO algorithm optimizes these initial centroids. PSO evaluates the quality of clustering results for each particle through a fitness function. The commonly used fitness functions include the sum of squares within groups, which represents the sum of squared distances between each data point and its centroid. The goal of the fitness function is to minimize and find the optimal centroid configuration. PSO continuously adjusts the velocity and position of particles through multiple iterations until the fitness function converges or reaches the preset number of iterations. During the search process, each particle tends towards its best centroid position found in history (local optimum) and the best centroid position in the entire particle swarm (global optimum). By combining individual and global information, particles can gradually find a better centroid. Moreover, this algorithm simulates the collective behavior of particles to search for the optimal solution in the search space, thereby optimizing the initial conditions of K-means and avoiding local optima. Subsequently, the K-means algorithm assigns data points to the nearest cluster based on the Euclidean distance to the centroids and iteratively updates the centroids until they stabilize or the preset number of iterations is reached. The PSO algorithm further optimizes the K-means clustering process by adjusting the position of the cluster centroids, improving the accuracy and stability of the clustering. During the public opinion analysis stage, the system analyzes the clustering results produced by the combined K-means and PSO algorithms, identifying the characteristics of each cluster to determine the main public opinion topics and dissemination patterns. By analyzing the clustering results, the system can identify key nodes and paths in public opinion dissemination and assess the influence of different events [[28], [29], [30]]. Simultaneously, the system performs sentiment analysis on the text data within each cluster, determining the overall sentiment trend and monitoring changes in public opinion over time.

Through the coordinated operation of these steps, the public opinion monitoring system designed in this study accurately identifies and analyzes public opinion dissemination patterns on social media. Moreover, it demonstrates excellent stability and scalability, providing strong technical support for effective sentiment monitoring and management on social media platforms.

Experimental design

The dataset used in the experiment is Sentiment140, a widely used public dataset specifically used for sentiment analysis and public opinion research, especially in text analysis in social media. It contains tweets collected from Twitter and labeled with emotional tendencies. This dataset is well suited for studying public opinion dissemination and sentiment analysis in social media. The sample size includes 1,600,000 tweets, which can be downloaded from the official website (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kazanova/sentiment140). Emotional labels are divided into positive, neutral, and negative. The tweets in the Sentiment140 dataset have been labeled as “positive” and “negative” emotions, which can directly serve as a major emotional feature. In the Sentiment140 dataset, tweets are labeled as positive or negative. Usually, these categories maintain a relative balance when annotated, but still need to be checked. For example, there may be a certain imbalance in the number of positive and negative tweets. If the imbalance is severe, it may affect the effectiveness of clustering and the accuracy of public opinion analysis. If the categories are imbalanced, downsampling or upsampling techniques can be used to adjust the balance of the categories, ensuring that the model does not overly lean towards a certain type of emotion. In actual data, there may be some outliers (such as very short or very long tweets, irrelevant characters or symbols contained in tweets, etc.). These outliers may have a negative impact on the clustering results, so preprocessing of outliers is necessary. The method used here is to remove noisy data from tweets, such as special symbols, URL, stop words, etc. The system designed here adopts the technical architecture of browser/server mode, which consists of three layers: front end, middle layer, and background. The front part is the Internet Explorer client, and the middle layer is an application service layer based on Java 2 Platform Enterprise Edition technology, including the Web layer and application server layer. The physical implementation is directly regarded as the middle layer server. The background part is the My Structured Query Language database needed by the system. The system adopts a Model View Contorller design pattern. It effectively distinguishes the presentation, business logic, and persistence layers, ensuring that the logical architecture is more intuitive and clearer. Test and analysis of the public opinion dissemination monitoring system in social media networks

Performance comparison test

The performance comparison indicators chosen for this study include accuracy, recall, F1-score, and silhouette coefficient. Accuracy is an extensively used basic indicator for evaluating the overall performance of classification models. For sentiment analysis and text classification tasks, accuracy directly reflects the model's effectiveness in handling multi-class data. Although accuracy may not fully assess a model in cases of class imbalance, it remains the primary indicator of basic model performance. Recall is a critical indicator, especially when analyzing public opinion where key information or minority classes need to be closely monitored. A high recall rate means that the model can capture as many positive samples as possible, which is vital for public opinion monitoring systems because missing critical negative public opinion could lead to serious consequences. The F1-score is very useful for dealing with imbalanced datasets, as it considers both precision and recall. When one class is significantly more important than others, the F1-score better reflects the model's overall performance in that class. For public opinion analysis, the F1-score helps provide a more comprehensive assessment when balancing the performance of positive and negative class predictions. The silhouette coefficient is particularly applicable to clustering tasks, measuring the quality of the clusters generated by the model. For the proposed optimization model, this indicator directly evaluates the clustering effect, ensuring that the clusters have high internal consistency and good separation between clusters. When compared to other models, the silhouette coefficient offers a unique perspective on clustering quality. As the data volume increases, the F1-score of the proposed model continues to improve, eventually reaching 0.879 under the 3000 data volume condition. The F1-score is a key indicator for balancing precision and recall, and the K-means + PSO model performs well on this indicator, indicating that the model not only classifies accurately but also comprehensively captures target class samples, demonstrating its superiority in complex tasks. In Fig. 2d, the silhouette coefficient is an important indicator for evaluating clustering performance, and the proposed model excels in this area as well. With a data volume of 3000, the silhouette coefficient reaches 0.694, markedly higher than that of other pre-trained models. This illustrates that the proposed model, through PSO, produces tighter and better-separated clusters, with data points within clusters being more similar and more distinct from those in other clusters. This result highlights the strong capabilities of K-means + PSO in clustering tasks, making the proposed model an ideal choice, especially in scenarios requiring high-quality cluster generation.

This performance advantage makes the proposed model more promising in scenarios that require high accuracy, high recall, and high-quality clustering. While modern pre-trained models excel in multi-task learning and language generation, the proposed model demonstrates its unique competitiveness in purely cluster analysis tasks through targeted optimization.

Experimental study on K-means cluster analysis

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the K-means clustering algorithm in the public opinion monitoring system, six different metrics are introduced: Sum of Squared Errors (SSE), Purity, Adjusted Rand Index (ARI), Homogeneity Score, Normalized Mutual Information (NMI), and Completeness Score. SSE assesses the internal consistency of clustering results by measuring the sum of squared distances between each point and its cluster center. A lower SSE value indicates higher intra-cluster consistency. SSE is chosen because it can directly reflect the tightness of K-means clustering and is a crucial measure of clustering effect. Cluster purity measures the proportion of the main category samples within each cluster, reflecting the clarity of clustering. Higher purity indicates a larger proportion of samples from the same category within a cluster, making it a basic metric for assessing clustering consistency. ARI measures the consistency between clustering results and actual labels, with values ranging from −1 to 1, where higher values indicate better consistency. ARI is chosen because it adjusts for randomness, providing a more reliable evaluation of clustering performance, especially for uneven category distributions. The Homogeneity Score evaluates whether the samples within each cluster belong to the same category, with higher values illustrating higher intra-cluster consistency. The Homogeneity Score is selected because it measures the success of clustering in separating samples of diverse categories, making it a key metric for assessing clustering effectiveness. NMI measures the degree of information shared between clustering results and actual labels, with normalized results ranging from 0 to 1. NMI is chosen because it is unaffected by the number of clusters and sample distribution, making it suitable for evaluating complex clustering structures. The Completeness Score measures whether all samples of the same category are correctly clustered together. This score is selected because it assesses the comprehensiveness of clustering, reflecting whether the clustering can completely encompass each category's samples, making it an important metric for evaluating clustering quality. Cluster analysis.

The results in Fig. 3 denote that as the value of K increases, the SSE value gradually decreases, indicating improved internal consistency within clusters. However, despite the continuous decrease in SSE, excessively high K values may result in too few samples per cluster, affecting the generalization ability of the clustering. Cluster purity gradually increases with larger K values, reaching 0.810 when K equals 20, illustrating that most samples within clusters belong to the same category. However, the improvement rate diminishes as K continues to increase, suggesting that excessively high K values may lead to the over-segmentation of clusters. The ARI value steadily increases with higher K values, showing that the K-means algorithm can better reflect actual classification structures at larger K values. ARI is 0.623 when K = 25, suggesting a good correspondence between clustering results and actual labels. The Homogeneity Score also increases with higher K values, demonstrating that the K-means algorithm can produce more consistent clusters at larger K values. The score achieves 0.767 at K equals 25, indicating high intra-cluster consistency. The NMI value increases with larger K values, exhibiting stronger correlations between clustering results and actual labels. When the value of K is 25, the NMI reaches 0.736, indicating that the information sharing between the cluster and the actual classification structure is relatively high. The Completeness Score also increases with larger K values, illustrating that the K-means algorithm can better capture complete samples of each category at larger K values. The score reaches 0.718 at K = 25, showing good completeness in clustering results.

The experiment also shows that while increasing the K value can improve clustering performance, excessively high K values may lead to over-segmentation, affecting the generalization ability of the clustering. Therefore, in practical applications, the choice of K value should consider multiple metrics to find the optimal K value that balances clustering effectiveness and generalization ability.

Dissemination range assesses the breadth of coverage, indicating how many people are exposed to the information. Dissemination depth evaluates how deeply the public opinion penetrated the network, reflecting the number of layers of forwarding or diffusion. The sentiment dissemination effectiveness measures the emotional changes and effects caused by the dissemination, assessing how the information spread influences public opinion. A comprehensive assessment of public opinion dissemination on social networks is provided from the perspectives of speed, range, depth, and sentiment effectiveness. Each metric is broken down into three dimensions, offering a detailed analytical view that makes the evaluation of public opinion dissemination more multidimensional and thorough. Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis is the process of examining how a model responds to changes in input parameters, aiming to understand the model's stability and robustness under different conditions. In the public opinion monitoring system, sensitivity analysis is crucial for evaluating how well algorithms perform when processing real-world social media data. Since the impact of the K-value has already been analyzed in previous clustering studies, this study focuses on experimenting with three specific dimensions: the inertia weight of PSO, the learning factor of PSO, and the maximum number of iterations. The choice of these three dimensions is deliberate. The inertia weight of PSO determines the extent to which the particle's velocity update depends on its previous velocity. The learning factor controls how much a particle is influenced by its best position and the global best position during updates. The number of iterations in the PSO algorithm decides when the optimization process should be halted. Fig. 5a suggests that as the inertia weight w increases, the SSE gradually decreases, indicating that particles tend to maintain their previous velocity during the search process, making the optimization process more stable and consistent. When the w value is between 0.7 and 0.9, the model's clustering purity, Completeness Score, and ARI reach higher levels, suggesting that a higher inertia weight effectively enhances the model's clustering performance. In Fig. 5b, when the learning factor is large, particles are more likely to approach the global best solution, leading to a slight decrease in SSE, while metrics like clustering purity and Completeness Score improve. However, when the learning factor is too large (e.g., 2.5, 2.5), the performance improvement slows down, indicating that excessive reliance on global search may result in suboptimal local optimization, necessitating a balance between global and local search. In Fig. 5c, as iterations increase, SSE gradually decreases, and metrics (clustering purity and Completeness Score) enhance steadily. The model's performance peaks at 200 iterations, showing that moderately increasing the number of iterations can remarkably enhance optimization effectiveness. However, excessive iterations can increase time costs with limited performance gains, so a balance between efficiency and effectiveness is essential.

The sensitivity analysis results indicate that setting the optimization model parameters appropriately can prominently improve clustering effectiveness, making it more effective in public opinion monitoring in social media. Adjusting these key parameters ensures that the model remains efficient and stable across different datasets and application scenarios. For instance, Ragunthar et al. (2021) only employed the traditional K-means clustering algorithm without optimizing the initial cluster centers. Compared with their research, this study introduced an innovative method based on PSO + K-means clustering in public opinion monitoring. Introducing the PSO algorithm allowed the proposed model to demonstrate superior clustering performance [31]. Moreover, Zhou et al. (2020) mainly focused on the speed and range of public opinion dissemination without delving into the depth and breadth of sentiment dissemination [32]. In contrast, this study presented a more comprehensive advantage in analyzing sentiment dissemination effectiveness.

Conclusion

This study explores the application of the K-means clustering algorithm combined with PSO in public opinion monitoring in social media. In addition, it conducts a series of experiments to evaluate the model's performance and applicability in detail. Several conclusions are drawn through a comprehensive analysis of four metrics (sentiment dissemination effectiveness, dissemination speed, dissemination range, and dissemination depth), and a sensitivity analysis of the model. The experimental results reveal that the proposed model performs exceptionally well in many areas, especially in the dissemination depth of public opinion and sentiment dissemination effectiveness. By optimizing the initial centroids of K-means with PSO, the model significantly improves the accuracy and effectiveness of clustering, enabling public opinion information to spread more widely and deeply. Additionally, the sensitivity analysis shows that the proposed model is sensitive to parameter settings, and appropriate adjustments can further enhance the model's performance, ensuring high stability and robustness across different data scales and application scenarios. The primary contribution of this study lies in introducing PSO to optimize the initial cluster centers, which makes the model excel in handling large-scale social media data, particularly in sentiment dissemination effectiveness and information dissemination depth. Moreover, detailed experiments across multiple dimensions, such as dissemination speed, range, depth, and sentiment dissemination effectiveness, validate the proposed model's effectiveness in public opinion monitoring, providing important references for practical applications. This study also thoroughly analyzes the impact of model parameters on performance, offering key insights for practical deployment and optimization. However, the proposed model has some limitations. First, it is sensitive to parameter settings, requiring extensive experimentation to determine the optimal parameter combination, which may increase the model's complexity and computational cost in practical applications. Second, while this study demonstrates the model's outstanding performance on a specific dataset, its performance on other types of social media data or in different language environments still needs further verification. Future research could apply the proposed model to multilingual environments and social media platforms to verify its applicability and robustness in a broader range of scenarios. Incorporating automated parameter optimization techniques, such as Bayesian optimization or genetic algorithms, could reduce the complexity of parameter tuning and improve the model's usability. Future work could explore combining K-means + PSO with deep learning models to leverage the feature extraction capabilities of deep neural networks, enhancing the model's performance in handling unstructured data and complex language tasks.

Uniform Sampling from the Reachable Set Using Optimal Transport
Karthik Elamvazhuthi, Sachin Shivakumar

Abstract
Finding the reachable set of a system has a wide range of applications, but is a fundamental challenge in control theory, especially when controls are bounded. Although one can simply integrate the system samples forward in time by applying random admissible control to approximate the reachable set, the samples typically cluster near an attractor (if one is present) – yielding a poor representation of the reachable set. A better representation can be found by applying controls that specifically lead to a uniform terminal state distribution, however, finding such controls is non-trivial. To find such controls, one must solve an Optimal Transport (OT) problem with uniform measure as the target distribution, which is difficult since the reachable set is not know a priori . We can overcome this difficulty by softening the terminal measure constraint via the introduction of a L 2 L_{2} -entropy function in the objective and can further reduce this infinite-dimensional regularized OT to a finite-dimensional particle-based optimal control problem by using a nonlocal kernel regularization of the entropy. This leads to a hierarchy of optimization problems whose solutions converge to the original reachability sampling OT problem, as proved by Γ \Gamma -convergence. The effectiveness of this entropy-regularized particle-based approach for uniform sampling of reachable set is demonstrated using numerical examples.



I
Introduction

The reachable set associated with a control system describes the collection of all states that can be attained from a given initial condition under admissible control inputs over a finite time horizon. Given that it captures the range of system behavior it plays a central role in safety verification, constrained motion planning, and filtering
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. In systems governed by nonlinear, underactuated, or input-constrained dynamics, the structure of the reachable set can be complex. It may lack convexity, admit no analytic description, and occupy a lower-dimensional subset of the ambient state space. These features complicate both analysis and computation, particularly in contexts where the reachable set must be integrated, sampled, or used as a prior in estimation or control synthesis.

A number of works have addressed the issue of approximating the reachable set
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Among these, sampling-based approaches have recently been explored for constructing approximations of the reachable set
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. A common strategy involves selecting a collection of initial states and control inputs, evolving the system forward, and recording the resulting terminal states. This procedure implicitly induces a measure on the reachable set – with an associated probabilistic guarantee on coverage of the reachable set – given by the pushforward of the sampling distribution under the system’s endpoint map.

When probabilistic guarantees are provided in the context of sampling-based reachability computations, one must clarify the probability measure under consideration to understand the nature of such guarantees. In many settings, such guarantees are given with respect to the distribution induced by randomly chosen controls. Depending on the choice of control distribution, the resulting pushforward measure may concentrate on a lower-dimensional subset of the reachable set, particularly in systems with attractors or dominant modes. In such cases, regions of the reachable set can be underrepresented in the sample, reducing the effectiveness of the approximation for tasks that benefit from more uniform coverage, such as worst-case analysis or robust safety verification.

The goal of this paper is to address these challenges associated with sampling-based approximations of the reachable set by invoking ideas from optimal transport theory
[
9
]
. In particular, we draw upon developments in the optimal transport of nonlinear control systems
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. Beyond the original problem of transporting control systems between measures, optimal transport has already been found to be fruitful in related topic of uncertainty propagation of control systems
[
13
,
14
]
.

Contribution

We start our contribution by making the observation that the task of sampling from a set can be reformulated as transporting one probability measure to another that is supported on the set. Therefore, optimal transport provides a natural conceptual framework for this problem. Expanding on this idea we make the following contributions in this paper:

1.

Transport formulation:
We pose the reachable set sampling problem as the task of finding a measure on the product space of admissible initial conditions and controls that transports the system, under the system’s endpoint map, to the uniform distribution on the reachable set.

2.

Entropy-regularized relaxation:
Since the reachable set is not known
a priori
and the pushforward constraint is difficult to impose directly, we introduce an entropy type penalty using the
L_{2}
norm that produces a tractable relaxation of the problem. To develop a particle approximation, the penalty term is regularized following the blob method for optimal transport
[
15
]
, introduced by the first author and collaborators based on blob methods for diffusions
[
16
]
, and then approximated using a finite set of samples.

3.

Convergence of the relaxed problem:
The entropy type relaxation, non-local regularization, and finite sample approximations introduce a hierarchy of optimization problems that successively approximate the problem of transporting to a uniform measure. We establish that the minimizers of each level of the hierarchy converge to minimizers of the next, up to a subsequence.

4.

Numerical Examples:
Since the final approximating problem is a classical optimal control problem for a finite dimensional control system, one can use existing methods such as Pontryagin’s maximum principle to solve the uniform sampling problem. We illustrate the method on nonlinear systems exhibiting strong attractors, where naive sampling yields highly concentrated terminal distributions. The proposed method avoids this degeneracy and produces samples that cover the reachable set more evenly.

II
Problem Formulation and Solution

First, let us formulate the reachability sampling problem for control-affine ODE systems in the form of an Optimal Transport (OT) problem. Consider the control system,

\dot{x}=f(x,u):=f_{0}(x)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}f_{i}(x),

(1)

where
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
u(t)\in U
denotes the control input. The space of controls is defined as

\mathcal{U}=\left\{u\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{m})\;\mid\;u(t)\in U~\text{for a.e}~t\in(0,T)\right\}

for some admissible control set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Let
\Gamma\subset C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of admissible trajectories, given by

\Gamma=\left\{x\in C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})\;\middle|\;\begin{aligned} &\exists\,u(\cdot)\in\mathcal{U}\text{ such that }\\
&x(t)\text{ solves }\eqref{eq:ctrsys}\end{aligned}\right\}.

(2)

Given a compact set of admissible initial conditions,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
reachable set

at time
T
, denoted by
\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
is given by

\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}=\left\{x_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid\exists\gamma\in\Gamma,\;\text{such that}\;\gamma(T)=x_{T},\;\gamma(0)\in\Omega\right\}.

(3)

The main goal is then to uniformly sample from the reachable set,
\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}
, as stated below.

Problem 1
.

Let
m_{R}
be the Lebesgue measure restricted to the reachable set
\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}
. Then, generate uniformly distributed samples from
m_{R}
.

To pose the above sampling problem as an OT problem, we first define
E:\Omega\times\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
to be the end point map given by

E(x_{0},u)=\gamma(T)~~\text{with}~~\gamma\in\Gamma~\text{and}~\gamma(0)=x_{0}

(4)

where
\gamma\in\Gamma
is the admissible trajectory corresponding to
u\in\mathcal{U}
. To ensure regularity of this endpoint map,
E
, we make the following assumptions.

Assumption 1

(Smoothness and Linear Growth)
.

Let
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be the vector fields defining the control system. We assume:

1.

Each
f_{i}
is once differentiable, i.e.,
f_{i}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
i=0,\dots,m
.

2.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all
i=0,\dots,m
,

\|f_{i}(x)\|\leq C(1+\|x\|).

Assumption 2

(Admissible Initial Conditions and Control Set)
.

Given a dynamical system of the form (
1
) whose vector fields satisfy Assumption
1
, we restrict initial conditions and controls as follows.

1.

The set of admissible initial conditions
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is non-empty and compact.

2.

The set of The control set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is nonempty, compact, and convex.

A well-known consequence of the two assumptions is that the end-point map
E
is continuous and the set
\mathcal{U}
is compact in the weak topology in
L_{2}
. Hence, the reachable set
\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}
is compact.
Next, we reformulate Prob.
1
as an OT problem below.

Problem 2
.

Given the Lebesgue measure of the reachable set,
c=\int_{\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}}dx
, Prob.
1
can be formulated as

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})}\;\;\int_{U}\int_{0}^{T}|u|^{2}d\mathbb{P}(x_{0},u)

(5)

\displaystyle\text{subject to,}~~E_{\#}\mathbb{P}=\frac{1}{c}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}}=:m_{R}.

Here, we have made an abuse of notation by identifying
m_{R}
with the measure it defines on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

m_{R}(A)=\frac{1}{c}\lambda(A\cap\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega})

for all Borel measurable sets
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
To ensure wellposedness of Prob.
2
, the following assumption is necessary.

Assumption 3
.

The reachable set
\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
has positive and finite Lebesgue measure; that is,

0<\lambda(\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega})<\infty.

The optimal transport problem that we have posed is distinct from the usual formulation of optimal transport with optimal control costs
[
10
]
. Particularly, the projection of
\mathbb{P}
on the initial conditions
\Omega
, is a variable.
Using ideas from
[
12
]
on the equivalence between the Kantorovich formulation and the Benamou-Brenier formulation, the formulations in
[
10
,
11
]
can be shown to be equivalent to ours when
\Omega=x_{0}
, in which case we have posed an optimal transport problem of transferring from
\delta_{x_{0}}
to
m_{R}
.

The goal of the paper is to develop a numerical method to solve this optimal transport problem. One can resort to the fluid dynamical formulation of optimal transport
[
17
]
. However, it leads to a partial differential equation constrained optimization problem that is typically solved by gridding the state-space. Instead we propose a finite sample approximation of this problem in the sense of the blob method of optimal transport introduced in
[
15
]
.

To develop a numerical method, first, we redefine this problem as an unconstrained optimization problem by using a barrier functional for the terminal constraint,
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \longrightarrow\ [0,+\infty]
, given
by

\mathcal{F}(\mu):=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }\mu=m_{R},\\[3.00003pt]
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(6)

Given this
\mathcal{F}
, the OT problem in (
5
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})}\mathcal{E},\qquad\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P})+\mathcal{F}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P})

(
\mathrm{ROT}
)

where
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P}):=\int_{\Omega\times U}\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}\,dt\,d\mathbb{P}(x_{0},u)
. However, to ensure numerical tractability, we first relax
\mathcal{F}
to obtain

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})}\int_{\Omega\times U}\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}\,dt\,d\mathbb{P}(x_{0},u)+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P})

where
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}

is some type of entropy penalization.

A common choice for entropy function in such situations is the functional
\rho\mapsto\varepsilon^{-1}\int\rho\log\rho
(known as Shannon Entropy). However, to simplify the proof of convergence results we instead consider the following
L_{2}
energy function

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}\big)^{2}dx,\;&\text{if}~~\mu<<\lambda,\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(7)

where
\lambda
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The justification for the penalization form (
7
) is that it achieves an effect similar to Shannon entropy function. For a square integrable function on the compact set
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}^{T}
,
\rho:\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}^{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, that integrates to
1
on
\Omega
, the Jensen’s inequality implies that
\int_{\mathcal{R}^{T}_{\Omega}}|\rho(x)|^{2}dx\geq(\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}^{T}}|\rho(x)|dx)^{2}
, where equality holds only if
\rho
is constant. Therefore, the minimizer of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
among all measures supported on the reachable set is the uniform probability measure and the minimum value is
\frac{1}{\lambda(\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}^{T})}
(equal to
\frac{1}{c}
). Thus, problem in (

ROT

) can be relaxed to

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon},~~\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}:=\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P})+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P}).

(
\mathrm{ROT}_{\varepsilon}
)

Particle Method

The problem (

ROT
ε

) is still difficult to solve numerically since the decision variable
\mathbb{P}
is infinite-dimensional. To obtain a numerically implementable algorithm, we can approximate the infinite-dimensional probability measure by a finite set of discrete measures (Dirac delta measures), which naturally leads to a particle-based optimization problem.

However, sums of dirac delta measures are not absolutely continuous with respect to
\lambda
and
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is not well-defined for such measures. To overcome this issue, we smoothen the terminal entropy functional using a
symmetric positive mollifier

k_{\delta}
defined as follows.

Definition II.1
.

A smooth, nonnegative, even function
k\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
symmetric positive mollifier
if it is compactly supported,
\int k(x)dx=1
, and the limit,
\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\epsilon^{-d}k(y/\epsilon)
, is the Dirac delta function at
y
.

Given a mollifier
k
with finite first-order moment, i.e.,
k\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define
k_{\delta}(y)=\delta^{-d}k(y/\delta)
. This gives us a sequence of mollifiers with adjustable width parameter,
\delta
. For any probability measure
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the convolution of
\nu
with
k_{\delta}
is a bounded, continuous function, given by
k_{\delta}*\nu(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k_{\delta}(y-x)d\nu(x)
.
If we approximate the terminal entropy functional,
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
, by a sequence mollified functionals of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mu):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|k_{\delta}*\mu(y)\right|^{2}dy,

(8)

we can obtain an terminal entropy functional that is well-defined for Dirac delta measure.
This mollification gives us the relaxed problem

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta},~~~\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon,\delta}:=\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P})+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P})

(
\mathrm{ROT}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
)

where, again,
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P})=\int_{\Omega\times U}\int_{0}^{T}|u(t)|^{2}\,dt\,d\mathbb{P}(x_{0},u)
.
We note that, the square inside the integral of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\delta}
can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon,\delta}(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|k_{\delta}*\mu(y)\right|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}k_{\delta}(y-x)k_{\delta}(y-z)dy\right)d\mu(z)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{\delta}(x-z)d\mu(z)\right)d\mu(x).

The finite particle-based optimization problem is then obtained by approximating
\mu:=E_{\#}\mathbb{P}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\delta_{x_{i}}
, which gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{i,0}\in\Omega\\
u_{i}\in\mathcal{U}\end{subarray}}&\Bigg\{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}|u_{i}(t)|^{2}\,dt\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}K_{\delta}\!\big(x_{i}(T)-x_{j}(T)\big)\Bigg\}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad&\dot{x}_{i}(t)=f\big(x_{i}(t),u_{i}(t)\big),\;\;x_{i}(0)=x_{i,0}.\end{split}

(
\mathrm{ROT}_{\varepsilon,\delta,N}
)

Proposition III.5
.

(
\Gamma-
convergence as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

Given Assumptions
1

and
3

c=\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}^{T}}dx

(i)

(Liminf inequality)

For every sequence

\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{P})\ \leq\ \liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\ [\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}].

(ii)

(Limsup inequality)

For every
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})

\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{P})\ \geq\ \limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}[\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{P})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}].

Proof.

(i) Liminf inequality.

Let
(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})\subset\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})
be such that
\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{P}
narrowly. Consider a subsequence so we can ensure that the

of the functionals, i.e.,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}[\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}]=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}<+\infty.

The assumption of boundedness of the limit is not restrictive since inequality is trivially satisfied if the

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded over

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{c}.

Since
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
is lower-semicontinous and

is continuous, the map

is also lower semicontinuous. Thus,

\varepsilon\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{c}.

By definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
and Jensen’s inequality, we know that
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P})\geq 1/c
(as discussed in section II). Thus,
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(E_{\#}\mathbb{P})=1/c

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{P})=\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\big(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}\big)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}[\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{P}_{\varepsilon})-\frac{1}{c\varepsilon}].

(ii) Limsup inequality.

For any
\mathbb{P}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega\times\mathcal{U})

V
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have provided a optimal transport formulation of sampling from the reachable set. Using a series of regularizations we developed a finite dimensional optimal control problem that approximates the optimal transport formulation. Moreover, leveraging tools from Gamma convergence, we proved that minimizer converge up to (subsequences) to the limit problem. We validated the approach through numerical experiments on the Van der Pol oscillator and a pendulum model of single-arm robot. Potential future directions include extending this method to compute reachable sets in higher dimensions using modern machine learning tools.

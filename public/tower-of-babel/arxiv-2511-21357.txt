Diffusion-controlled reaction rate to an active site in a spherical cavity: Extension of Berg’s theory Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Sergey D. Traytak, Georgiy A. Babushkin

Abstract
This study is due to various applications in physics, chemistry and especially in biology, where both bounded configuration domain and chemical anisotropy could play a great part. In fact we generalize the well-known Berg’s theory, which describes diffusion-controlled reactions occurring within a spherically symmetric absorber-cavity system. The trapping probability and the reaction rate at which a small diffusing particle is captured by an axially symmetric one reactive patch absorber inside a spherical cavity were found semi-analytically and numerically by means of the dual series relations method. This approach leads to such incredibly fast convergence, that it may rightly be referred to as exact one. The results obtained can be used to test numerical programmes that describe diffusion-controlled reactions in real physical systems for reactants with arbitrary anisotropic reactivity, which are located inside of various cavities as well as in the unbounded domains. Moreover, we managed to find a close connection between the dual series relations method and the generalized method of separation of variables.

I.3
Extensions of Smoluchowski’s theory

It is now well-established by many authors that the validity of Smoluchowski’s model is restricted by a number of physically important reasons.
Particularly Smoluchowski’s theory is essentially a one-trap theory, which does not account for the
diffusive interaction
effects due to influence of neighboring particles including, fully absorbing, fully reflecting or partially reflecting entities. Interested readers can find details on the diffusive interaction concept, e.g., in Refs.
Traytak 1992
;
Traytak 1995b
;
Traytak 2008
;
Galanti et al. 2016a
;
Grebenkov and Traytak 2019
;
Galanti et al. 2020
;
Traytak 2024
.

However, in the present paper we focus on the extra two essential limitations of the theory requiring refinements. Firstly the theory should be generalized for the case of reactions occurring in finite domains to include the diffusive interaction between the RPs and domains boundaries. Secondly it must be refined to describe the
chemical anisotropy
effects due to the sterically specific surface structure of the RPs, which can comprise active sites upon an otherwise inert surface.

Diffusion and then subsequent chemical reactions on the active sites belonging to the RPs surfaces
are crucial in the cell biology and drug delivery within a cell. Indeed "If the drug acts within the cell, it must move from the point of entry to the site of action. For some drugs, particularly for protein- and gene-based agents, this active site is within a specialized compartment or organelle in the cell; rates of transport within the cell are therefore important."
Saltzman 2001

(59)

\displaystyle\|P_{l}\|_{L_{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{2l+1}}\,,\quad l=\overline{0,\infty}\,,

where
\delta_{lm}
is the Kronecker delta which is given by
\delta_{lm}=0
if
l\neq m
and
\delta_{lm}=1
if
l=m
.

By taking into account expansion (

\displaystyle u\left(\xi,\theta\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{l}^{+}\xi^{l}+\frac{A^{-}_{l}}{\xi^{l+1}}\right)P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)\,.

(60)

It is clear that the use of this expression in the integral (

\displaystyle J\left(\theta_{0},\epsilon\right)=A^{-}_{0}\,.

(61)

Thus, our main objective is to derive this RCF, i.e. monopole moment
A^{-}_{0}
.

Finally note that regardless of the parameter
\epsilon
one has

\displaystyle J\left(\theta_{0};\epsilon\right)\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\theta_{0}\to 0\,.

(62)

The limit (

\theta_{0}\to 0
local flux (

VIII
Reduction to the dual series relations

It is well known that the exact analytical solution to the proper mixed boundary value problem is often difficult if not impossible to obtain because of their complexity.
Sneddon 1966
;
Traytak and Price 2007
;
Duffy 2008

Moreover, they are seldom amenable to analytical treatment including asymptotic analysis and that is why analytical or at least semi-analytical solution of any mixed boundary value problem is of great interest.

Nevertheless for the problem under consideration it is possible to derive the so-called
dual series relations
(DSR)
Sneddon 1966
or
dual Fourier-Legendre series

Duffy 2008
using separation variables approach. It is noteworthy that in turn the DSR (
67
), (
68
) can be solved, at least semi-analytically, either by reducing to an appropriate infinite set of equation or Fredholm integral equation of the second kind.
Minkov 1964
;
Sneddon 1966
;
Traytak and Tachiya 1995a
;
Duffy 2008

Substitution of Eq. (
60
) into condition on the outer boundary (
32
) and mixed boundary conditions (
33
), (
34
) yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{l}^{+}\epsilon^{-l}+{A^{-}_{l}}{\epsilon^{l+1}}\right)P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=0\,,\quad 0\leq\theta<\pi\,,\qquad

(63)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(A_{l}^{+}+{A^{-}_{l}}\right)P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=1\,,\quad 0\leq\theta<\theta_{0}\,,\qquad

(64)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left[lA_{l}^{+}-(l+1)A_{l}^{-}\right]P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=0\,,\quad\theta_{0}<\theta\leq\pi\,.\qquad

(65)

It is a simple matter to eliminate here internal moments
A^{+}_{l}
in order to reduce obtained three series relations (
63
)-(
65
) to the corresponding system of two equations with respect to only one infinite sequence of unknown external moments
\{A_{l}^{-}\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
.

It follows directly from Eq. (

) that the connection holds

\displaystyle{A^{+}_{l}}=-{A^{-}_{l}}\epsilon^{2l+1}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad l=\overline{0,\infty}\,.

(66)

Note in passing that for
\epsilon\to 0
we, obviously, have
{A^{+}_{l}}\to 0
for all
l=\overline{0,\infty}
as it should be.

Thus, the problem (

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\epsilon^{2l+1}\right)A_{l}^{-}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=1\,,\quad 0\leq\theta<\theta_{0}\,,\qquad

(67)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(1+l+l\epsilon^{2l+1}\right)A_{l}^{-}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=0\,,\quad\theta_{0}<\theta\leq\pi\,.\qquad

(68)

Moreover, obtained connection (
66
) allows us to simplify the expression (

) for the local concentration

\displaystyle u\left(\xi,\theta\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{l+1}}-\epsilon^{2l+1}\xi^{l}\right)A^{-}_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)\,.

(69)

Then to solve Eqs. (

), (

.
Sneddon 1966
;
Traytak 1995a
For this purpose we introduce new unknown sequence of coefficients

by the relation

\displaystyle\left(1+l+l\epsilon^{2l+1}\right)A_{l}^{-}=\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)X_{l}\,.

After some simple algebra the DSR (
67
), (
68
) may be transformed to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left[1-q_{l}(\epsilon)\right]X_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=1\,,\quad 0\leq\theta<\theta_{0}\,,

(71)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)X_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=0\,,\quad\theta_{0}<\theta\leq\pi\,,

(72)

where

\displaystyle q_{l}(\epsilon):=w_{l}(\epsilon)+\left[1-w_{l}(\epsilon)\right]\epsilon^{2l+1}\,,\qquad

(73)

\displaystyle w_{l}(\epsilon)=\frac{1+2l\epsilon^{2l+1}}{2\left(1+l+l\epsilon^{2l+1}\right)}\,.\qquad

(74)

It is evident that

\displaystyle q_{0}(\epsilon):=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\epsilon\right)\,,\quad

(75)

\displaystyle w_{l}(\epsilon)\to 1/{2(l+1)}=q_{l}(0)\,,\quad l\geq 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\epsilon\to 0\,.\quad

(76)

One can see that

\displaystyle A_{l}^{-}=\left[1-w_{l}(\epsilon)\right]X_{l}

(77)

and using relation (
61
) one arrives at the important formula for the correction factor

\displaystyle J\left(\theta_{0},\epsilon\right)=\frac{1}{2}X_{0}\left(\theta_{0},\epsilon\right)\,.

(78)

Therefore, to calculate the required rate (
36
) we only need to know the zeroth element of the sequence
\{X_{l}\}
(in what follows we will use this very notation for short).

In its turn utilizing connection (
77
) expression for the local concentration (

) it is expedient to represent by means of solution
\{X_{l}\}
to the canonical DSR (

), (

\displaystyle u\left(\xi,\theta\right)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{l+1}}-\epsilon^{2l+1}\xi^{l}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[1-w_{l}(\epsilon)\right]X_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)\,.

(79)

Reduction to an infinite system of linear equations

Following Minkov
Minkov 1964
we will reduce the canonical DSR (

(ISLAE).
7
7

7

Since in this paper only resolving infinite systems are studied, below we omit the qualifier

For this goal first let us consider an auxiliary DSR of the form

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}Y_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=g\left(\theta\right)\,,\quad 0\leq\theta<\theta_{0}\,,

(80)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)Y_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=0\,,\quad\theta_{0}<\theta\leq\pi\,,

is known arbitrary continuous function given in the interval

It is well known that these DSR possesses the exact solution

\displaystyle Y_{l}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\theta_{0}}dt\cos\left[\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)t\right]\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{0}^{t}d\tau\frac{g\left(\tau\right)\sin\tau}{\sqrt{\cos\tau-\cos t}}.

(82)

Clearly Eq. (

) may be represented in the self-consistent form

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}X_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)=G\left(\theta\right),\quad\theta_{0}<\theta\leq\pi,

(83)

where

G\left(\theta\right)=1+\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}q_{l}X_{l}P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)\,.

Utilizing exact formula (

), (

)

\displaystyle X_{l}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\theta_{0}}dt\cos\left[\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)t\right]\frac{d}{dt}\int\limits_{0}^{t}d\tau\frac{G\left(\tau\right)\sin\tau}{\sqrt{\cos\tau-\cos t}}\,.

(84)

The integrals appearing here may be computed explicitly.
Indeed one can calculate the inner integral in Eq. (

(
m\geq 0

Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 2015

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\frac{P_{m}(\cos\theta)\sin\theta\,d\theta}{\sqrt{\cos\theta-\cos u}}=\sqrt{2}\|P_{m}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\sin\left[\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)u\right]\,.

Then the right hand side of Eq. (

Finally we get the desired ISLAE:

\displaystyle X_{l}-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}M_{lm}X_{m}=B_{l}\,,\quad l=\overline{0,\infty}\,.

(86)

Hereafter infinite matrix is

\displaystyle M_{lm}\left(\theta_{0};\epsilon\right):=q_{m}(\epsilon)Q_{lm}\left(\theta_{0}\right)\,,

where matrix function reads

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{\sin\left[\left(l-m\right)\theta\right]}{\left(l-m\right)}\left(1-\delta_{lm}\right)+\theta\delta_{lm}\right\}

and

Solution to the ISLAE (

\{X_{l}\}

\left(1-\epsilon\right)^{-1}&\quad\mbox{as}\quad\theta_{0}\to\pi-\,,\end{array}\right.

which is to be expected.

X
Solution of the ISLAE

Various problems of mathematical physics and its applications lead to ISLAE which are therefore of paramount importance.

The most basic method for solving an ISLAE is the so-called
reduction method
, in which the infinite system is truncated and then a finite-size system is solved by standard methods of linear algebra. However, the solution of the truncated system may or may not converge to that of the ISLAE so that the questions of existence and uniqueness of the solution are particularly important.

Study of the ISLAE in appropriate classical sequence spaces fills a highly important place in the DSR method and, contrary to the the case of finite system of linear algebraic equations, the primary questions of existence and uniqueness of the solution of the ISLAE is a typical problem of functional analysis. So, in the next two Subsections, following well-known books Refs.
Kantorovich and Krylov 1964
;
Kantorovich and Akilov 1982
;
Aleksandrov and Kovalenko 1986
, we present a few important facts concerning solution to the ISLAE.

X.1
Basic definitions

We start with a general important

Definition X.1
.

For given infinite
\left(\overline{0,\infty}\right)\times\left(\overline{0,\infty}\right)
matrix with elements
M_{lm}\in\mathbb{R}
and inhomogeneous term
\{B_{l}\}
the system (
86
)
is called the ISLAE (with respect to the unknown elements
X_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
,
l=\overline{0,\infty}
) of the second kind.

Let us recall now the definition of a solution to the ISLAE (
86
)

Definition X.2
.

If for an infinite matrix
(M_{lm})
defined by (
86
), there exists an infinite sequence
\{X_{m}\}
such that the series

\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}(\delta_{lm}-M_{lm})X_{m}

converge to
B_{l}
for all
l=\overline{0,\infty}
, then the sequence
\{X_{m}\}
is termed a solution of the ISLAE (
86
).

X.2
Solvability of the ISLAE by truncation method

Definition X.3
.

A finite system of
n
linear algebraic equations, based on
B_{l}
and

M_{lm}
with
l,m=\overline{1,n}
,

X_{l}^{(n)}-\sum\limits_{m=0}^{n}M_{lm}\,X_{m}^{(n)}=B_{l}\,,\quad l\in\overline{0,n}

(91)

is called a truncation of the ISLAE (
86
) to the order
n
.
Kantorovich and Krylov 1964

The number
n
can be also called the
order of truncation approximation
.

Let us proceed to the description of the criteria of solvability of
the ISLAE.

Definition X.4
.

The ISLAE is termed regular if

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}|M_{lm}|<1\,,\quad l=\overline{0,\infty}\,.

(92)

However the condition (
92
) is rather strong and can be weakened to solve ISLAE by the reduction method.

Lemma X.1
.

The regularity condition (
92
) is sufficient for the ISLAE (
86
) to be solved by truncation (
91
) such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X_{l}^{(n)}=X_{l}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad l=\overline{0,\infty}

(93)

is the solution of the ISLAE (
86
).

The proof of Lemma
93
can be found e.g., in Ref.
Kantorovich and Krylov 1964
.

Definition X.5
.

Let
\ell_{\infty}
defined to be the space of all bounded sequences
\{B_{l}\}
, i.e.,
|B_{l}|\leq C_{b}>0
for all
l=\overline{0,\infty}
. Space
\ell_{\infty}
is a Banach space endowed with the norm:
\|B_{l}\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{l\geq 0}|B_{l}|

It is clear from Definition
X.4
that the inequality holds

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}|M_{lm}|\leq\|M\|_{\infty}\,,\quad l=\overline{0,\infty}\,,

(94)

where the matrix norm is defined as

\displaystyle\|M\|_{\infty}\left(\theta_{0};\epsilon\right):=\sup_{l}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}|M_{lm}\left(\theta_{0};\epsilon\right)|\,.

(95)

So the following condition

\displaystyle\|M\|_{\infty}\left(\theta_{0};\epsilon\right)<1

(96)

is sufficient for the system (
86
) to be solved by truncation method.

Theorem X.1
.

(Existence and Uniqueness) If the inhomogeneous term
\{B_{l}\}\in\ell_{\infty}
, the regular ISLAE (
86
) has a unique
solution
\{X_{m}\}\in\ell_{\infty}
.

The proof of Theorem
X.1
is based on the contraction mapping principle for corresponding matrix operators.

Note that the regularity condition (
92
) is sufficient to solve
ISLAE by both simple reduction method and iterations.
Kantorovich and Akilov 1982

The following assertion holds true

Lemma X.2
.

The inhomogeneous term
X_{l}^{0}
in the ISLAE (

\{X_{l}\}\in\ell_{\infty}
.

The proof of the Lemma
X.2
implies directly from the evident estimates

0\leq X^{0}_{0}\leq 1\,,\quad|X^{0}_{l}|<3/2\pi\quad\mbox{for all}\quad l\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Remark X.1
.

We emphasize that theory of the ISLAE in space of bounded sequences

XIII
Concluding remarks

In this paper we propose a generalization of the famous steady-state Berg’s theory.
Berg 1993
The ideally absorbed reactive particle was replaced by a particle with asymmetric patch-like reactivity. Then we managed to perform full mathematical study of the generalized Berg’s theory and obtained both semi-analytical and numerical results.

It is common knowledge that problems posed in unbounded domains often appeared to be amenable to the analytical study including asymptotic approaches
Babenko sian
but not for numerical ones. Numerical methods should be restricted by finite domains only. On the other hand mathematical models dealing with unbounded space are rather strong idealizations of real-world physical phenomena, since real physical processes occurring in finite, confined, crowded etc. domains.

Among obtained results the first approximation to the reaction rate (
102
), being at the same time mathematically simple and physically lucid, seems to be very important. Indeed it can be served for understanding the coupling of anisotropic trapping and diffusive interaction effects taking place inside the cavity. This important question seems to be worthy of special attention.
Theoretical concepts of the effective steric factor and the rate correction factor were widely used by researchers studding of anisotropic chemical reactions and diffusive interaction effects, separately for many decades. However the study of the generalized Berg’s model quite clearly showed that anisotropic chemical reactivity and diffusive interaction effects can be coupled. Moreover, as we have seen above these two important concepts were naturally unified within the scope of this work.

In our opinion, however, another even more remarkable result was established in this study.
We managed to find a close connection between the dual series relations method and the generalized method of separation of variables. The revealed connection allowed us, similar to our previous work, divide the corresponding solution algorithm into eight basic and clear steps. This algorithm can be implemented step by step in full detail to theoretical studding a number of future generalizations of the present simplified mixed boundary value diffusion problem. It should be particularly emphasized that using above formulated algorithm, we can find solutions for a much wider class of important mixed boundary value diffusion problems.

We have shown that the method of reduction may be used to solve numerically the resolving infinite system of linear equations to any degree of accuracy even when the simple-iteration method is not valid. Whereas the desired accuracy of the solution can be achieved just by increasing the truncation degree. Furthermore, note that the method has advantages such as shorter calculation time and more accurate results compared to known purely numerical and simulation methods. It is particularly surprisingly that the dual series
relations method requires the same computing time for both bounded and unbounded domains. Suggested powerful mathematical technique allows one to investigate rather sophisticated physical models to elucidate some fundamental issues in cell biology and related sciences, such as how the specific geometric configurations of reactants and boundaries with sterically specific reactivity the overall behavior of the reaction rates. Thus, following Ref.
Eun 2017
, we can definitely conclude that for the one-patch Solc-Stockmayer model the dual series relations method gives the exact results.

Considered model is, of course, a gross simplification, nevertheless allows to elucidate the main features of the sterically specific diffusion-controlled reactions occurring on an active site of reactants placed into cell systems be studied. For instance, our results may be used to quantify rates of the trapping chemical reactions taking place in populations of isolated biological cells.

Future extension of the present work may include, e.g., generalizations which can take into account: bulk depletion of the
B
-particles, their rotational diffusion and time-dependent effects.

And the last but not least point, in our forthcoming paper we intend to carry out the following research. Firstly, we will generalize the present theory to the case when the reactive particle and the cavity form a non-concentric spheres system. Secondly, we will compare our results against that obtained by means of the SLS approximation (constant flux method) in Ref.
Eun 2017
.

A.1
Physical background

Here we just briefly summarize the physically motivated assumptions underlying the Smoluchowski theory.

(1) Standard treatments of the theory of diffusion-controlled reactions assume that the host medium is unbounded, quiescent, isotropic and homogeneous, therefore
mathematically it is well modeled by the common 3D
Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Thus, Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
may be called the

.

(2) Suppose that the following properties of reactants

and
B

1

•

Both reactants are neutral (uncharged) particles.

•

Reactants

are identical non-interacting between each other and very small (point-like) particles.

•

Reactants
A
are spherical particles of infinite capacity.

•

Reactants
A
are much larger than the
B
-particles, and therefore they can be regarded as immobile.

•

Generally speaking particles

are chemically asymmetric, whereas
B
-particles have isotropic reactivity.

(3) Diffusion of
B
-particles can be described by a real-valued function that satisfies an appropriate diffusion equation.

(4) The specific form of the boundary conditions prescribed on the reactants surfaces are completely determined by the features of reaction-diffusion processes under studying.

Definition A.1
.

Under geometrical confinement, contrary to the confinement arising due to, e.g., action of some dynamic potentials, we understand belonging to a bounded (finite) domain of a physical space.

Definition A.2

Any region of the reactive particle that binds to the chemically isotropic
B
-particles is called the fully absorbed active site. Provided it belongs to the surface of the RP it is also called active site of patch type or just active patch.

We would like to draw the reader’s attention to the simultaneous use in literature terms "active" and "reactive" with respect to the site (patch).

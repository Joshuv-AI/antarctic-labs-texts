Targeting ICAM1 through LIF-mediated NF-κB/JAK2-STAT3 crosstalk protects nucleus pulposus from IL-1β-driven pyroptosis to ameliorate intervertebral disc degeneration
Authors: Zhen Wang, Jichen Liu, Haishan Xu, Yanhua Sun, Shaowei Xu, Xijing He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is a prevalent condition contributing to back pain and disability. Leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) has emerged as a protective gene in IDD, prompting further investigation into its role and mechanisms. This study employs bioinformatics analysis combined with experimental validation to explore the role of LIF in IDD. Gene expression datasets from the GEO database were analyzed to identify genes associated with IDD, and the effects of LIF on nucleus pulposus (NP) cell NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis were assessed both in vitro and in vivo. Elevated L IF expression was observed in mildly degenerated discs and decreased in severely degenerated discs. In vitro studies demonstrated that L IF can alleviate IL-1 β-mediated pyroptosis of NP cells and NLRP3 activation by regulating ICAM1 expression. This process is achieved by inhibiting the NF- κB and JAK2/STAT3 pathways. Furthermore, in vivo studies confirmed these findings, showing that the progression of IDD can be ameliorated by expressing LIF or inhibiting ICAM1. LIF and ICAM 1 play significant roles in the pathogenesis of IDD, closely linked to NP cell pyroptosis and NLRP3 activation. Targeting LIF or ICAM1 could offer a novel therapeutic strategy for IDD.

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is recognized as a major contributor to spinal degenerative diseases, substantially increasing the prevalence of disability, negatively impacting quality of life, and imposing considerable economic burdens on healthcare systems worldwide [1–3]. Data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2017 indicate that approximately 80% of the global population may experience clinically significant episodes of low back pain during their lifetime [4]. The intervertebral disc (IVD) plays a critical role in maintaining spinal biomechanical integrity, consisting of a gelatinous nucleus pulposus (NP) at its center, surrounded by a robust annulus fibrosus (AF), and enclosed by cartilaginous endplates at the vertebral junctions [5–7]. This sophisticated structure not only facilitates intervertebral connectivity but also enables the dynamic modulation of mechanical loads, including axial compression, tensile elongation, multidirectional bending, and rotational torsion of the spine [8, 9].

Pyroptosis is a distinct form of programmed cell death characterized by the release of substantial quantities of pro-inflammatory factors [10, 11]. During pyroptotic progression, gasdermin family proteins orchestrate the formation of membrane pores, leading to cellular osmotic lysis and the subsequent release of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-18, thereby amplifying inflammatory cascades [12, 13]. Current evidence suggests that pyroptosis plays a critical role in the progression of IDD [14, 15]. In this context, pyroptosis may be triggered by external stressors or other stimuli, ultimately resulting in the death of IVD cells [12]. The occurrence of pyroptosis in IVD cells exacerbates inflammatory responses, potentially intensifying tissue damage and accelerating disc degeneration [16]. This underscores a significant relationship between pyroptosis and IDD. A detailed investigation into the role of pyroptosis in IDD is essential to elucidate underlying mechanisms and may offer foundational insights for the development of targeted therapeutic strategies. Inhibiting pyroptosis may help preserve IVD structure and functionality by decelerating the degenerative process.

Recent progress in bioinformatics and sequencing technologies has consistently identified disease-associated genes as promising candidates for diagnostic or therapeutic applications. Accordingly, this study employed computational genomic approaches to identify and characterize differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with IDD through integrative transcriptomic analysis of data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Various analytical methods were used to identify leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) as a key gene potentially linked to pyroptosis in IDD. LIF is a glycoprotein that was first isolated from ascitic tumor cells in 1988. It belongs to the IL-6 cytokine subfamily, which also includes IL-6, IL-11, IL-27, oncostatin M, ciliary neurotrophic factor, cardiotrophin-1, and cardiotrophin-like cytokine/cytokine-like factor [17, 18]. LIF was initially recognized for its ability to inhibit the proliferation of myeloid leukemia cells in murine models and is known to participate in a wide range of biological processes [19]. These include the regulation of cell proliferation, maintenance of pluripotent stem cells, tissue and organ development, nervous system growth and repair, reproductive support, inflammation modulation, immune response enhancement, infection control, and metabolic regulation [20, 21]. Recent research indicates that LIF expression is upregulated during IDD, potentially enhancing extracellular matrix (ECM) production. Additionally, LIF may serve as a protective agent by preventing NP cell apoptosis without influencing proliferation [22]. Follow-up research by Zhou et al. [23] identified the MAPK-ERK1/2 signaling pathway as the mechanism by which LIF enhances ECM production in degenerative NP cells. Other studies have detected LIF expression in both cartilage and synovial tissues, suggesting its involvement in the pathophysiology of arthritis [24, 25]. Furthermore, LIF has been implicated in bone development and maintenance of adult skeletal homeostasis [26]. Therefore, while previous studies have suggested LIF’s potential role in IDD, its specific mechanisms remain unclear. Our integrated analysis of GEO datasets revealed novel associations between LIF and ICAM1 within inflammatory and pyroptosis pathways, leading us to hypothesize that LIF might alleviate IDD by regulating ICAM1-mediated NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

Our integrated bioinformatics analysis of multiple GEO datasets revealed a previously unrecognized association between LIF and intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM1) within inflammatory and pyroptosis pathways. This novel finding led us to hypothesize that LIF might alleviate IDD not only through previously documented mechanisms but primarily by regulating ICAM1-mediated NLRP3 inflammasome activation and subsequent pyroptosis, representing a significant advancement beyond current understanding of LIF’s protective functions in disc degeneration.

The aim of this study was to assess LIF expression in animal models exhibiting varying degrees of NP degeneration, which are classified as normal, mild, or severe, as well as in human patients with lumbar disc herniation. In addition, the study sought to explore the effect of LIF on IL-1β-induced pyroptosis in NP cells and the signaling mechanisms involved. Specifically, we investigated the novel LIF-ICAM1-NLRP3 axis in pyroptosis regulation, ultimately confirming through in vivo experiments using rat models that LIF plays a role in mitigating pyroptosis in NP cells, thereby delaying the progression of IDD.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition

The GEO database serves as a comprehensive public repository for high-throughput gene expression and functional genomics datasets. The mRNA expression profiles GSE27494 and GSE41883 were retrieved from the GEO database. The GSE27494 [27] dataset was generated using the GPL1352 platform (U133_X3P), which employs the Affymetrix Human X3P Array, while GSE41883 [28] was derived from the GPL570 platform (HGU133_Plus_2), utilizing the Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array. Differential gene expression analysis between the sample groups in GSE27494 and GSE41883 was performed using the limma package in the R software environment. The criteria for selecting DEGs were set as “Adjusted p < 0.05 and |log₂FC| ≥ 1 (i.e., log₂FC ≥ 1 or log₂FC ≤ − 1).” Principal component analysis (PCA) plots were generated using the corresponding R package. To further explore the functional significance of these DEGs, enrichment analyses based on Gene Ontology (GO) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) were conducted using the ClusterProfiler package. In addition, genes associated with IDD and pyroptosis were identified from established resources, including the Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) (http://ctdbase.org/), the GeneCard database (https://www.genecards.org/), and the DisGeNET database (https://www.disgenet.org/).

Collection of degenerated human IVDs and clinical data

T2-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and subsequent image analysis were conducted by three independent evaluators prior to surgery, followed by grading using the Pfirrmann classification system [29]. Human tissue samples were collected from patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria (Table). Between October 2022 and June 2023, a total of 36 IVD samples were obtained from patients undergoing surgical procedures at Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region People’s Hospital in Yinchuan, China (19 males and 17 females). Grade II discs were linked to spinal injuries and deformities, whereas grade III and grade V discs were associated with degenerative spinal conditions, excluding spinal stenosis and degenerative cystic disease.

Culture and treatment of NP cells

Immortalized human NP cells (0028a) were obtained from Yaji Bio-Company (http://www.yajimall.com/) and cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum (Gibco) and 1% streptomycin/penicillin. The cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO2. Once confluent, the cells were digested using 0.25% Trypsin-EDTA (Gibco) and reseeded into 10 cm culture dishes at the appropriate density. To assess the effects of IL-1β, NP cells were treated with various concentrations of IL-1β (Cloud-Clone Corp., 1 mg) for 24 h. In addition, cells were exposed to CUT12-9 (Selleck; an NF-κB pathway activator) and Butyzamid (HY-148748, MCE; a JAK2/STAT3 pathway activator) for 12 h. For all of the in vitro assays, dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was used as the solvent for Butyzamid and CUT12-9, maintaining a final DMSO-to-medium ratio of 1:1000.

Immunohistochemical staining

Tissue sections were obtained from both human and rat IVDs. After antigen retrieval and blocking with 5% normal goat serum, the sections were incubated with the following primary antibodies: LIF (1:100, Proteintech, 26757-1-AP), Collagen II (Col II) (1:200, ab34712, Abcam), MMP13 (1:200, Proteintech, 18165-1-AP), ICAM1 (1:100, Abcam, ab282575), IL-1β (1:200, Proteintech, 16806-1-AP), p-P65 (1:100, Immunoway, YP0188), and p-STAT3 (1:100, Immunoway, YP0251). Following primary antibody incubation, slides were treated with secondary antibodies. Staining was developed using DAB solution (Servicebio, China) and counterstained with hematoxylin. Histological images were captured at 200× magnification using an Olympus BX63 microscope (Olympus, Japan). The percentage of positively stained cells was quantified using ImageJ software.

Western blotting

Total proteins were extracted from human and rat NP tissues or NP cells using RIPA buffer (Sigma-Aldrich), and protein concentrations were measured via the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay (Beyotime, China). Proteins were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) according to standard procedures. After blocking with 3% bovine serum albumin (BSA), membranes were incubated with primary antibodies: LIF (1:500, Proteintech, 26757-1-AP), Col II (1:4000, ab34712, Abcam), MMP13 (1:2000, Proteintech, 18165-1-AP), ICAM1 (1:1000, Abcam, ab282575), IL-1β (1:200, Proteintech, 16806-1-AP), GSDMD (1:2000, Proteintech, 20770-1-AP), GSDMD-N (1:1000, Abcam, ab215203), ASC (1:2000, Proteintech, 10500-1-AP), Caspase-1 (P20) (1:1000, Proteintech, 22915-1-AP), NLRP3 (1:1000, Proteintech, 68102-1-Ig), p-P65 (1:1000, Immunoway, YP0188), P65 (1:2000, Proteintech, 10745-1-AP), p-STAT3 (1:1000, Immunoway, YP0251), STAT3 (1:2000, Proteintech, 10253-2-AP), p-JAK2 (1:1000, Abmart, T56570), JAK2 (1:1000, Abmart, TA6022), and GAPDH (1:5000, Proteintech, 60004-1-Ig). Subsequently, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies: anti-rabbit IgG (1:5000, Proteintech, SA00001-2) and anti-mouse IgG (1:5000, Proteintech, HRP-10283). Proteins were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence reagents (Invitrogen, CA).

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from NP cells using RNAiso Plus (Takara, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The isolated mRNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was conducted with gene-specific primers for LIF, ICAM1, MMP13, Col II, and IL-1β, employing SYBR Premix Ex Taq II (Takara) on an ABI 7900HT Fast Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Waltham, MA). Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2–ΔΔCt method. All of the RT-qPCR assays were performed in triplicate.

Immunofluorescence staining

NP cells were seeded into 24-well flat-bottom plates at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well. After the designated treatments and incubation periods, the cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100 in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 10 min. Following a blocking step with 1% BSA in PBS, the cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies. After washing, cells were treated with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated secondary antibodies (anti-rabbit or anti-mouse, Proteintech) at a dilution of 1:300 for one hour at room temperature. Fluorescent imaging was performed using a laser scanning confocal microscope equipped with an LCPlanFl objective (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Lentivirus transfection

To upregulate LIF expression and downregulate ICAM1 expression, NP cells were transfected with Lenti-LIF or Lenti-shICAM1 vectors (GENE, Shanghai, China). Transfection was performed at an efficiency of 30–50%, using Lenti-NC or Lenti-shNC as negative controls. After 12 h of transfection and confirmation that cell viability exceeded 95%, the culture medium was replaced, and the cells were incubated for an additional three days before passaging. Transfection efficiency was validated by western blot analysis.

Rat model of IDD and and drug treatment

Male Sprague-Dawley rats (weighing 200–220 g) were obtained from the Animal Research Center of the Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region Academy of Sciences. Under sterile conditions, rats were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (45 mg/kg) and randomly assigned to the following groups: Sham group: Exposed caudal intervertebral disc (IVD) without puncture, followed by injection of an equal volume of saline. IDD + ADV-NC group: IDD model + injection of adenovirus carrying negative control (ADV-NC). IDD + ADV-shNC group: IDD model + injection of adenovirus carrying scrambled shRNA (ADV-shNC). IDD + ADV-LIF group: IDD model + injection of adenovirus overexpressing LIF (ADV-LIF). IDD + ADV-shICAM1 group: IDD model + injection of adenovirus expressing shRNA targeting ICAM1 (ADV-shICAM1). The IDD model was established via annular puncture of the caudal IVD. A sagittal skin incision was made to expose the tail IVD, and a 30-gauge needle was inserted parallel to the endplate into the nucleus pulposus (NP) to a depth of 5 mm. The needle was rotated 360° and maintained for 30 s. Immediately after puncture, adenoviral constructs or saline (sham group) were injected into the disc. The muscle layer was sutured with 3 − 0 silk, and the skin was closed with 4 − 0 nylon sutures. All personnel were blinded to group assignments. Four weeks post-surgery, lumbar MRI was performed to evaluate disc degeneration.

Hematoxylin-eosin and safranin O–fast green staining

To evaluate the extent of disc degeneration and progression of IDD in rats following surgery, disc tissue samples were harvested from surgical patients and euthanized rats eight weeks post-operation. Thin sections (5 μm) of the disc tissues were prepared for hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining using standard protocols. In addition, Safranin O–Fast Green staining was performed to highlight proteoglycan content and improve visualization of cartilage and disc tissue integrity. The stained slides were analyzed and photographed using a light microscope to assess structural changes indicative of IDD.

Magnetic resonance imaging

Eight weeks after surgery, MRI of the coccyx was performed on all of the rats under anesthesia, with tails fully extended during the procedure. Each experimental group consisted of five rats. Sagittal and horizontal T2-weighted images were acquired using a 3.0T clinical MRI system (Philips Intera Achieva 3.0MR). Imaging parameters for the T2-weighted sequence included a fast spin-echo protocol with a repetition time of 5400 ms, an echo time of 920 ms, a matrix size of 320 (horizontal) × 256 (vertical), a field of view of 260°, and four excitations. Slice thickness was set at 2 mm with no inter-slice gaps. All of the MRI images were independently assessed in a blinded fashion using the IDD classification system developed by Pfirrmann et al.

Statistical analysis

All of the statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism software. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for comparisons involving multiple sample groups, while pairwise group comparisons were performed using the least significant difference (LSD) t-test. For independent sample comparisons, the independent sample t-test was utilized. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (X ± S), and a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Both the enrichment analysis and the numerous protein quantification analyses in the article were conducted with three independent experimental replicates.

Results

Identification of hub genes associated with IDD and pyroptosis

Normalized expression data from datasets GSE27494 and GSE41883 displayed consistent distribution patterns, as illustrated by box plots. PCA validated the reproducibility of data within each dataset. Post-normalization, gene expression levels were balanced across datasets. Differential expression analysis, visualized using volcano plots, identified 633 DEGs in GSE27494 and 1250 DEGs in GSE41883 based on the criteria |log2FC| ≥ 1 and p-value < 0.05 (Fig. 1A). A Venn diagram comparison of DEGs from both datasets yielded 47 overlapping genes (Fig. 1B). Protein–protein interaction (PPI) network data for these DEGs were retrieved from the STRING database and analyzed using Cytoscape software (version 3.9.1). The CytoHubba plugin with the Maximum Clique Centrality (MCC) algorithm was applied to rank the top 50 DEGs as hub genes, based on degree centrality (Fig. 1C).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses revealed that the identified DEGs were significantly involved in 160 biological processes (BP), 13 cellular components (CC), and 35 molecular functions (MF). Enriched biological processes included MAPK signaling regulation, ECM organization, oxidative stress response, and aging. Key cellular components included collagen-rich ECM structures, the endoplasmic reticulum lumen, and focal adhesions. Molecular function terms were predominantly associated with structural molecule activity and ECM-related binding functions (Fig. 1D). KEGG pathway analysis highlighted enrichment in the p53 signaling pathway, glutathione metabolism, and Hedgehog signaling pathway, suggesting relevance to IDD and pyroptosis pathogenesis (Fig. 1E).

Genes related to IDD and pyroptosis were collected from the CTD, GeneCard, and DisGeNET databases. A Venn diagram was used to identify overlapping hub genes associated with both conditions. Six hub genes were identified: IL-1β, IL-6, RELB, ICAM1, LIF, and PTGS2 (p < 0.05; Fig. 1F). The expression patterns of these six genes were subsequently analyzed in both GSE27494 and GSE41883. Among them, LIF, IL-1β, and ICAM1 exhibited the most significant expression changes (p < 0.05; Fig. 1G) and were thus selected for further study. PPI network analysis of these six genes further demonstrated close functional interconnections (Fig. 1H).

Expression of LIF and ICAM1 in human IVD tissues with varying degrees of degeneration

To further investigate the roles of LIF and ICAM1 in IDD, their expression was analyzed in human IVD samples representing various stages of degeneration. MRI was used to classify discs into Pfirrmann grades II, III, and V, corresponding to control, mild, and severe degeneration, respectively (Fig. 2A). Histological analysis using H&E and Safranin O–Fast Green staining revealed progressive histopathological changes, including reduced ECM content, increased fibrotic deposition, and a significant decline in NP cell numbers in severely degenerated discs (Fig. 2B–C).

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was employed to assess the mRNA expression levels of LIF, MMP13, collagen type II (Col II), IL-1β, and ICAM1. The data showed that with increasing degeneration severity, the expression of MMP13, IL-1β, and ICAM1 was significantly upregulated, whereas Col II expression was markedly reduced. Notably, LIF expression was elevated in mildly degenerated IVD tissues but significantly decreased in severely degenerated tissues (p < 0.05; Fig. 2D). Western blot analysis and subsequent quantitative evaluation further confirmed that ICAM1 expression increased progressively with degeneration severity. Conversely, LIF expression exhibited an initial increase in mildly degenerated tissues but was significantly diminished in severely degenerated discs (p < 0.05; Fig. 2E–F). These findings were consistent with immunohistochemical (IHC) staining results, which demonstrated similar expression trends (p < 0.05; Fig. 2G–H).

Expression of LIF and ICAM1 in NP cells and the rat IDD model

To elucidate the functional relationship between LIF, ICAM1, and IDD, a rat model of IDD was established via annulus fibrosus (AF) puncture. The degree of degeneration was assessed at two and four weeks post-puncture using MRI, H&E staining, and Safranin O–Fast Green staining. MRI revealed a notable reduction in T2-weighted water signal intensity at two weeks, which further declined by four weeks compared with control animals (Fig. 3A). Histological analysis [30] indicated progressive degenerative changes including AF disruption, decreased NP cell count, increased fibrosis, and reduced disc height, with changes becoming more severe at four weeks (Fig. 3B–D). Based on these outcomes, the two- and four-week post-puncture groups were selected to represent varying stages of IDD for further analysis of LIF and ICAM1 expression. Immunofluorescence (IF) staining demonstrated an increase in MMP13, GSDMD, and ICAM1 expression during disease progression, while LIF expression peaked at two weeks and significantly declined by four weeks (p < 0.05; Fig. 3E–F). These trends suggest a potential regulatory role for both LIF and ICAM1 in IDD progression.

To complement these findings, a cellular IDD model was developed by treating NP cells with varying concentrations of IL-1β (0–80 ng/mL). Western blot analysis revealed that MMP13, GSDMD, and GSDMDN expression increased in a dose-dependent manner, peaking at 30 ng/mL. Beyond this concentration, no additional increase in protein expression was observed, indicating a plateau effect (p < 0.05; Fig. 3G–H). These findings substantiate the reliability of our NP cell model for examining the expression of LIF and ICAM1 in the context of IDD. In addition, Western blot analysis revealed that the expression of ICAM1 followed a similar trend to that of MMP13, GSDMD, and GSDMDN. In contrast, LIF expression was notably higher at an IL-1β concentration of 10 ng/ml but declined to its lowest level at 30 ng/ml, remaining low as the IL-1β concentration continued to rise (p< 0.05, Fig. 3G-H).

LIF protects NP cells from IL-1β-induced NLRP3 activation and pyroptosis by inhibiting ICAM1 Cxpression

To examine the protective role of LIF against pyroptosis in NP cells, both overexpression and knockdown strategies were employed under IL-1β stimulation. Western blot analysis confirmed altered LIF expression following transfection (p < 0.05; Fig. 4A–B). In the oe-LIF + IL-1β group, expression levels of NLRP3, GSDMD, GSDMDN, ASC, and Caspase-1 (P20) were significantly reduced compared to the IL-1β group. Conversely, LIF knockdown via shRNA led to increased expression of these pyroptosis-related proteins in the shLIF + IL-1β group (p < 0.05; Fig. 4A–D). These findings were corroborated by IF staining, which showed decreased fluorescence intensities for NLRP3, ASC, and Caspase-1 (P20) in the oe-LIF + IL-1β group and increased intensities in the shLIF + IL-1β group, aligning with western blot data (p < 0.05; Fig. 4E–F). These results suggest that LIF overexpression mitigates IL-1β-induced pyroptosis by suppressing the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, whereas LIF knockdown exacerbates inflammatory damage.

To further determine whether the protective effect of LIF against pyroptosis in NP cells is mediated by the regulation of ICAM1 expression, ICAM1 levels were assessed under LIF knockdown and overexpression conditions. Western blot analysis demonstrated that silencing LIF significantly increased ICAM1 expression, whereas LIF overexpression led to a marked reduction (p < 0.05; Fig. 4G–H and K–L). These findings were substantiated by IF staining, which revealed that ICAM1 fluorescence intensity increased following LIF inhibition (p < 0.05; Fig. 4I–J). To directly investigate the role of ICAM1 in mediating pyroptosis, we overexpressed ICAM1 in the background of LIF overexpression. Western blot results indicated that while LIF overexpression reduced the expression of NLRP3, GSDMD, GSDMDN, ASC, and Caspase-1 (P20) compared to the IL-1β group, these proteins were re-elevated upon ICAM1 overexpression (p < 0.05; Fig. 4M–N). These data suggest that LIF suppresses IL-1β-induced pyroptosis in NP cells by downregulating ICAM1 expression.

LIF reduces ICAM1 expression by inhibiting the NF-κB and JAK2/STAT3 pathways

To elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which LIF suppresses IL-1β-induced pyroptosis and NLRP3 activation in NP cells, we performed LIF knockdown using shNC lentiviral transfection as a control. Transcriptome sequencing followed by heatmap visualization of DEGs was performed (Fig. 5A). Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealed significant enrichment in the NF-κB and JAK/STAT signaling pathways, with a noted positive regulation in response to LIF suppression (p < 0.05; Fig. 5B). Previous studies have demonstrated that LIF is associated with both NF-κB [31] and JAK2/STAT3 [32] signaling pathways, which are known to induce ICAM1 expression [33, 34]. These findings led to the hypothesis that LIF regulates ICAM1 via these two signaling axes.

To test this, we examined the impact of LIF inhibition on the activation of NF-κB and JAK2/STAT3. Western blot analysis showed elevated levels of phosphorylated P65 (p-P65), JAK2 (p-JAK2), and STAT3 (p-STAT3) following LIF knockdown (p < 0.05; Fig. 5C–D). IF staining further revealed increased nuclear translocation of p-P65 and p-STAT3 in LIF-silenced NP cells (p < 0.05; Fig. 5E–F), suggesting activation of both pathways upon LIF suppression.

To confirm whether LIF modulates ICAM1 expression through these pathways, we conducted rescue experiments by treating NP cells with Butyzamid (a JAK2/STAT3 activator) or CU-12T-9 (an NF-κB activator) in the context of LIF overexpression. Cells were divided into four groups: control, IL-1β, oe-LIF + IL-1β, and oe-LIF + IL-1β + Butyzamid or CU-12T-9. Western blot analysis indicated that IL-1β treatment activated both pathways and increased ICAM1 expression, whereas this activation was mitigated by LIF overexpression. However, the addition of pathway activators reversed the suppressive effects of LIF, leading to increased ICAM1 expression (p < 0.05; Fig. 5G–J). These findings were consistent with IF staining results (p < 0.05; Fig. 5K–L). Together, these results suggest that LIF reduces ICAM1 expression by inhibiting the NF-κB and JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathways, thereby mitigating pyroptosis in NP cells.

LIF expression mitigates AF puncture-induced IDD in vivo

To further evaluate the therapeutic potential of LIF in attenuating pyroptosis in NP cells and alleviating IDD, a rat model of needle puncture-induced IDD was established. Seven days after model induction, ADV-LIF was administered to promote LIF overexpression in the IVD. Eight weeks post-injection, disc degeneration was assessed using MRI.

Histological staining and Pfirrmann grading were used to evaluate in vivo outcomes (Fig. 6A). MRI results (Fig. 6B) showed that, compared to the sham group, the ADV-LIF + IDD group exhibited reduced T2 signal intensity; however, this reduction was less pronounced than that observed in the ADV-NC + IDD group. Histological analysis using H&E and Safranin-O/Fast Green staining revealed significant NP shrinkage, irregular AF structure, and ECM depletion in the ADV-NC + IDD group, indicative of severe degeneration. These degenerative changes were attenuated in the ADV-LIF + IDD group (Fig. 6C). Integration of MRI and histological findings showed that Pfirrmann scores were significantly elevated in the ADV-NC + IDD group relative to the sham group, while scores in the ADV-LIF + IDD group were significantly lower than in the ADV-NC cohort (p < 0.05; Fig. 6D), suggesting that LIF overexpression slows IDD progression.

In addition, immunohistochemistry (IHC) and IF staining confirmed that p-P65 and p-STAT3 expression levels were significantly reduced in the ADV-LIF + IDD group (Fig. 6E–F). IF staining further demonstrated reduced fluorescence intensities of ICAM1, MMP13, NLRP3, and GSDMD compared to the ADV-NC + IDD group (p < 0.05; Fig. 6G–H), supporting the inhibitory role of LIF on inflammatory signaling and pyroptosis in vivo.

ICAM1 inhibition ameliorates AF puncture-induced IDD in vivo

In parallel, the potential of ICAM1 suppression as a therapeutic strategy was evaluated. The experimental workflow is shown in Fig. 7A. MRI analysis at eight weeks indicated that while ADV-shICAM1 + IDD rats showed reduced T2 signal intensity versus sham animals, the intensity was higher than that in the ADV-shNC + IDD group, implying attenuation of degeneration (Fig. 7B). Histological evaluations (Fig. 7C) demonstrated pronounced degeneration in the ADV-shNC + IDD group, including reduced NP cell density, structural AF disruption, and ECM loss. These changes were partially reversed in the ADV-shICAM1 + IDD group. Correspondingly, Pfirrmann scores were significantly lower in the ADV-shICAM1 + IDD group compared to ADV-shNC + IDD, confirming a protective effect of ICAM1 inhibition (p < 0.05; Fig. 7D).

Subsequent western blot analysis was conducted to evaluate the expression alterations of MMP13, Col II, GSDMD, GSDMDN, and NLRP3 among the various groups. The results indicated that there was a significant difference in the expression of MMP13, GSDMD, GSDMD-N, and NLRP3 expression levels in the ADV-shICAM1 + IDD cohort were found to be diminished, whereas the expression of Col II exhibited an increase (p < 0.05, Fig. 7E-F). IF staining indicated that at eight weeks post-injection, the fluorescence intensities of MMP13, GSDMD, and NLRP3 in the ADV-shICAM1 + IDD group were significantly lower, while the fluorescence intensity of Col II was notably higher in comparison to the ADV-shNC + IDD group, corroborating the findings from the Western blot analysis (p < 0.05, Fig. 7G-I). These observations suggest that the suppression of ICAM1 expression effectively mitigates NP cell pyroptosis and NLRP3 activation, consequently decelerating the progression of IDD.

Discussion

NP cells are integral to the structural integrity and functional maintenance of the IVD [35]. IDD is a major contributor to lower back pain, significantly impairing individuals’ quality of life [4, 36]. Pyroptosis of NP cells, a form of programmed cell death associated with inflammatory responses, is typically triggered by pathogenic infections or other cellular stressors [11, 12]. This process leads to rupture of the cell membrane and the release of inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-1β and IL-18, thereby intensifying local inflammation [37]. Prior research indicates that NP cell pyroptosis plays a pivotal role in both the initiation and progression of IDD [15]. In addition to depleting viable cell populations, pyroptosis disrupts the ECM, accelerating disc degeneration [15, 38]. Thus, inhibiting NP cell pyroptosis emerges as a promising therapeutic strategy to slow or potentially reverse IDD progression [14].

In this study, comprehensive bioinformatics analysis identified LIF as a key gene associated with both pyroptosis and IDD. Experimental validation showed that LIF expression increased in mildly degenerated discs but decreased significantly as degeneration progressed. This biphasic expression pattern suggests that LIF may initially function as a compensatory protective mechanism in early IDD, attempting to maintain tissue homeostasis and ECM integrity. However, as degeneration advances and the inflammatory microenvironment intensifies, the regenerative capacity of NP cells becomes compromised, potentially leading to exhaustion of LIF-mediated protective responses. The observed downregulation in severe degeneration may result from progressive cell loss, epigenetic modifications, or negative feedback regulation within inflammatory signaling pathways. This trend was consistent across both in vitro and in vivo models. LIF is also involved in various biological processes, including neuronal survival [14], bone formation, tumor suppression [19, 39], and immune regulation [21]. Due to its broad functional scope, LIF is considered a promising candidate in regenerative medicine, cancer therapy, and the study of neurodegenerative diseases [21]. Xiao et al. [22] reported that LIF protein expression was initially elevated in patients with lumbar disc herniation and in rabbit IDD models, particularly at the two-week stage. However, LIF levels declined as degeneration progressed, consistent with our experimental results. In vitro studies demonstrated that recombinant human LIF (rhLIF) significantly increased expression of aggrecan and Col II in a dose-dependent manner. While rhLIF did not affect NP cell proliferation, it inhibited apoptosis in a concentration-dependent fashion. Additionally, Zhou et al. [23] found increased LIF expression in human degenerated NP tissues. Treatment with rhLIF stimulated ECM synthesis and activated the ERK1/2 signaling pathway in denatured NP cells. These findings suggest that LIF may promote ECM component expression and protect against apoptosis in degenerating NP cells.

In this investigation, we explored for the first time how LIF suppresses IL-1β-induced pyroptosis and NLRP3 inflammasome activation in NP cells. This process is mediated through the downregulation of intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM1). This regulatory mechanism was further validated in rat models. ICAM1 is a transmembrane protein of the immunoglobulin superfamily predominantly localized on cell surfaces [40, 41]. ICAM1 is well-established as a critical mediator in inflammatory processes across various pathological conditions. In rheumatoid arthritis, ICAM1 facilitates leukocyte migration into synovial tissues, contributing to joint inflammation and destruction. Similarly, in osteoarthritis, elevated ICAM1 expression correlates with disease severity and promotes chondrocyte apoptosis through NF-κB activation (63). In neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease, ICAM1 mediates neuroinflammation by enabling leukocyte transmigration across the blood-brain barrier (64). Furthermore, in cardiovascular diseases like atherosclerosis, ICAM1 expression on endothelial cells recruits monocytes into the arterial wall, facilitating plaque formation (65). Our finding that ICAM1 expression increases with IDD severity and promotes NP cell pyroptosis through NLRP3 inflammasome activation aligns with its established pro-inflammatory roles in these diverse degenerative conditions. It has been implicated in diverse cellular functions including cell growth [42], cell death [43], and inflammation [44]. ICAM1 also plays a role in regulating programmed cell death (PCD) and has been [16] linked to various diseases, particularly cancer [45–47]. Studies have shown that ICAM1-mediated interactions influence cell proliferation and apoptosis. Elevated ICAM1 expression in tumor cells is associated with enhanced angiogenesis, resistance to apoptosis, and increased cellular viability [48, 49]. Further, ICAM1-related adhesion mechanisms are involved in the formation of negative co-stimulatory microclusters, with potential implications for cancer immunotherapy [50]. One study identified ICAM1 among a 14-gene PCD-related signature, emphasizing its regulatory role in apoptosis [51, 52].

Despite extensive evidence of the role of ICAM1 in cancer and inflammation, its involvement in IDD has not previously been documented. This study addressed that gap by demonstrating that ICAM1 expression correlates positively with the severity of IDD. Furthermore, in vivo experiments showed that inhibiting ICAM1 expression reduced pyroptosis and NLRP3 activation in NP cells, ultimately slowing the progression of IDD. These results position ICAM1 as a novel molecular target in the pathophysiology of disc degeneration.

Mechanistically, our findings demonstrate that LIF mitigates IL-1β-induced pyroptosis and NLRP3 inflammasome activation in NP cells by downregulating ICAM1 expression. This regulation is mediated through inhibition of the NF-κB and JAK2/STAT3 signaling pathways. The JAK2/STAT3 axis is a critical effector of LIF activity. Upon binding to its receptor LIFR, LIF activates the JAK2/STAT3 cascade, leading to the phosphorylation of JAK2 and subsequent activation of STAT3 [52, 53]. In the context of apoptosis, this pathway promotes cell survival by upregulating anti-apoptotic genes, such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL, while inhibiting pro-apoptotic proteins like Bax [17, 39]. Paradoxically, LIF may induce apoptosis in certain cancer cells by increasing the expression of pro-apoptotic genes, such as Fas and TRAIL, highlighting its dual functionality [54]. The role of LIF in inflammation is similarly complex. It can exacerbate inflammation by increasing the expression of IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β through JAK2/STAT3 signaling [33], yet also demonstrates anti-inflammatory potential by upregulating IL-10 and suppressing pro-inflammatory mediators, thereby protecting tissues from excessive inflammation [20, 55] .This dual regulatory capability underscores the multifaceted nature of LIF in orchestrating both apoptotic and inflammatory responses.

In addition to JAK2/STAT3, LIF is involved in modulating the NF-κB pathway, which regulates inflammation, immune responses, and oncogenesis [56, 57]. In the realm of apoptosis, LIF’s activation of NF-κB signaling may influence both cell survival and programmed cell death [58, 59]. LIF has been reported to inhibit NF-κB activation via several mechanisms, including STAT3-mediated upregulation of Bcl-2/Bcl-xL, which can negatively regulate NF-κB activity [53, 60]. Further, LIF enhances Suppressor of Cytokine Signaling 3 (SOCS3) expression, which inhibits NF-κB activation by interfering with key upstream kinases [57, 61]. LIF also indirectly downregulates NF-κB by reducing levels of TNF-α and IL-1β, which are known NF-κB activators [31]. Moreover, ICAM1 expression is regulated by the NF-κB pathway, which serves as a downstream target of the JAK2/STAT3 signaling route [34]. NF-κB activation prompts the expression of ICAM1, a molecule pivotal for the inflammatory response and the adhesion of leukocytes to endothelial cells [41, 43]. Although direct interactions between ICAM1 and the JAK2/STAT3 pathway are not extensively documented, the indirect connections via the NF-κB pathway indicate a possible regulatory role for JAK2/STAT3 in modulating ICAM1 expression (Fig. 8).

Despite the strength of our findings, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, transcriptomic data were derived from publicly available databases, thus precluding validation of potential input errors. Second, inconsistencies between mRNA and protein expression profiles limited our ability to perform integrative analyses, and our study focused solely on transcript-level data. Third, the relatively small sample size calls for further validation in larger cohorts. Fourth, research related to translating LIF/ICAM1-targeted therapy into human applications is still in its preliminary stage.Lastly, although the AF puncture model is widely used in IDD research, it inherently mimics acute rather than chronic degeneration and introduces mechanical trauma and instability not fully representative of human pathology.

In conclusion, this study establishes LIF as a protective factor in the pathogenesis of IDD. LIF expression was elevated in mildly degenerated discs and significantly decreased in severely degenerated tissues. Our results provide the evidence that LIF attenuates IL-1β-induced pyroptosis and NLRP3 activation in NP cells by downregulating ICAM1 via the NF-κB and JAK2/STAT3 pathways. These findings highlight LIF as a potential therapeutic target for IDD, thus offering new directions for intervention aimed at preserving disc integrity.

The Performance of Compression-Based Denoisers
Dan Song and Ayfer Özgür and Tsachy Weissman

Abstract
We consider a denoiser that reconstructs a stationary ergodic source by lossily compressing samples of the source observed through a memoryless noisy channel. Prior work on compression-based denoising has been limited to additive noise channels. We extend this framework to general discrete memoryless channels by deliberately choosing the distortion measure for the lossy compressor to match the channel conditional distribution. By bounding the deviation of the empirical joint distribution of the source, observation, and denoiser outputs from satisfying a Markov property, we give an exact characterization of the loss achieved by such a denoiser. Consequences of these results are explicitly demonstrated in special cases, including for MSE and Hamming loss. A comparison is made to an indirect rate-distortion perspective on the problem.

In the classical indirect rate distortion setting, one begins with a prescribed distortion measure between

Leveraging the idea that compression inherently removes noise to perform denoising has appeared in the prior literature in various settings
[
12

14

5

16

[

]

[
11
]
, Natarajan introduces Occam filters, which apply this idea to remove additive noise from real-valued signals, using lossy compressors operating at a norm distortion equal to the norm of the noise. Upper bounds on the expected norm between
X^{n}
and
Y^{n}
(treated as vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) are given in terms of the operating rate of the compressor and the rate distortion function for the noise source. This theoretical result is limited in that it depends on the specific properties of the compressor used, and the rate-distortion function of the observations may in general be difficult to evaluate. The Occam filters are shown to perform well empirically, but the upper bounds are loose in practical regimes.

Our work is most closely related to that of
[
16
]
and
[
5
]
.
In
[
5
]
, Donoho proposes using lossy compression with distortion chosen to match the amount of error introduced by the noise to recover samples from the posterior in two cases: a binary source passed through a binary symmetric channel and a Gaussian source passed through an AWGN channel. He gives bounds for the Hamming and squared losses achieved in the two settings, respectively.
In
[
16
]
Weissman and Ordentlich
consider the joint empirical distribution of the source and reconstruction sequences of a compressor.
Under some regularity conditions, they show empirical distributions of “good” compressors approach the distribution that achieves the infimum in the definition of the rate-distortion function.
They apply this result to the denoising setting where a source is corrupted by i.i.d. additive noise, i.e.,
Z_{i}=X_{i}+N_{i}
.
They show that choosing a distortion measure
\log p_{N}(z-y)
and distortion level
H(N)
suffices for a lossy compressor to recover the source, in the sense that the reconstruction asymptotically behaves as samples from the posterior distribution.
This characterizes the empirical distribution of
X^{n},Z^{n}
and
Y^{n},Z^{n}
(but not the joint distribution of
X^{n},Z^{n}
and
Y^{n}
), and
[
16
]
gives a bound on the performance of the compression-based denoising by assuming the worst case coupling between the two marginal distributions.

Our results both generalize and strengthen the findings of prior works, including
[
16
]
, which are limited to additive noise channels. We extend the compression-based denoising framework to arbitrary memoryless channels
P_{Z|X}
by identifying a suitable distortion measure that is chosen to match
P_{Z|X}
. When a compressor is optimized for this distortion measure, it effectively removes the influence of the noisy channel. This same distortion measure was first introduced in
[
13
]
as a cost function for optimal transport in the context of training generative models from privatized data, where it similarly serves to mitigate the effects of privatization and enables the model to learn the underlying raw data distribution. Furthermore, we show that under the joint empirical distribution of
X^{n}
,
Y^{n}
and
Z^{n}
, the noise-free and reconstruction variables are essentially conditionally independent given the noisy observations. Thus good lossy compression under the right noise-induced distortion criterion and level not only results in a "sample from the posterior", but an independent one conditioned on the noisy observation. This result leads to a full asymptotic characterization of the
k
th-order joint empirical distribution of
(X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n})
and, for any fixed k, an exact expression for the achievable loss, which substantially improves upon the bound established in
[
16
]
for additive noise channels.

II-B

Problem Setting

Throughout, let
\mathbf{X}=(\dots,X_{-1},X_{0},X_{1},\dots)
denote a stationary ergodic process taking values in the alphabet
\mathcal{X}
.
Similarly let
\mathbf{Z}
be a random process taking values in the alphabet
\mathcal{Z}
.
We assume a known memoryless channel
P_{Z|X}
, such that
\mathbf{Z}
is produced by passing
\mathbf{X}
through the channel.

For each
n
, let
Y^{n}
be an
n
-tuple of random variables taking values in
\mathcal{Y}^{n}
.
Unless otherwise specified,
n
will be the length of the block in consideration and
k\in\mathbb{N}
will be such that
1\leq k\leq n
.
Note here there need not be some process
\mathbf{Y}
which agrees with all
Y^{n}
, even in distribution.
Unless otherwise specified, for all
n
we have the Markov chain

X^{n}\mathrel{\text{---}}Z^{n}\mathrel{\text{---}}Y^{n}.

(1)

The goal is to design a (possibly randomized) mapping
Z^{n}\to Y^{n}
depending
only
on
P_{Z|X}
and possibly the distribution of
\mathbf{X}
, such that
Y^{n}
recovers
X^{n}
well.
We will consider a loss function for the recovery
\Lambda:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\Lambda_{\max}]
, and define

\Lambda_{n}(X^{n},Y^{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda(X_{i},Y_{i}).

(2)

We will show a sense in which a sequence of good lossy source codes
\left\{Y^{n}\left(\cdot\right)\right\}_{n}
designed for a certain distortion measure
\rho
that depends only on the channel
P_{Z|X}
is also good for the denoising task under any reasonable loss function
\Lambda:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\Lambda_{\max}]
.

The notion of a “good” lossy source code is formalized through the following definitions.

Definition 1

For a fixed single-letter distortion measure
\rho:\mathcal{Z}\times\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\infty]
, we define the distortion of a block
(z^{n},y^{n})
as

\rho_{n}\left(z^{n},y^{n}\right)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho\left(z_{i},y_{i}\right).

(3)

We denote the rate-distortion function

\displaystyle R\left(Z^{k},D\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{P_{Y^{k}|Z^{k}}:\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\rho_{k}\left(Z^{k},Y^{k}\right)\right]\leq D}\frac{1}{k}I\left(Z^{k};Y^{k}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle R\left(\mathbf{Z},D\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}R\left(Z^{k},D\right).

(5)

Hereafter, unless otherwise specified,
Y^{n}
is the reconstruction sequence of a lossy source code for
Z^{n}
.

Definition 2

For a fixed
n
, a
code
consists of a codebook
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{Y}^{n}
and a mapping
\phi:\mathcal{Z}^{n}\to\mathcal{C}
.
We define the rate of the code to be

R=\frac{1}{n}\log\left\lvert\mathcal{C}\right\rvert.

(6)

A sequence of codes
(\mathcal{C}_{n},\phi_{n})
is called
good
at some
(R,D)
on the rate-distortion curve if the rate is bounded by
R
, i.e.

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left\lvert\mathcal{C}_{n}\right\rvert\leq R,

(7)

and the distortion satisfies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\rho_{n}\left(Z^{n},\phi_{n}\left(Z^{n}\right)\right)\right]\leq D.

(8)

When a sequence of codes
(\mathcal{C}_{n},\phi_{n})
is good at
(R,D)
, we will, refer to the corresponding decoder output
Y^{n}=\phi_{n}(Z^{n})
as good for the same
(R,D)
.

The notion of goodness defined above can equivalently be expressed as a condition solely on the reconstructions
Y^{n}=\phi_{n}(Z^{n})
, as follows.

Definition 3

A sequence of reconstructions
\left\{Y^{n}\left(\cdot\right)\right\}_{n}
is called
good
at some
(R,D)
on the rate-distortion function
R\left(\mathbf{Z},D\right)
if
\frac{1}{n}H\left(Y^{n}\right)\leq R
and

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\rho_{n}\left(Z^{n},Y^{n}\left(Z^{n}\right)\right)\right]\leq D.

(9)

Henceforth we will talk about good sequences
Y^{n}
without explicitly referring to the underlying sequence of lossy source codes.

The following are needed to state our results.

Definition 4

We denote the
empirical distribution
, which is a function of
(X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n})
, by

\begin{split}&Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{n-2k}\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-k}\mathbf{1}\!\left\{X_{i}=x_{0},Z_{i-k}^{i+k}=z_{-k}^{k},Y_{i-k}^{i+k}=y_{-k}^{k}\right\},\end{split}

(10)

where
\mathbf{1}\!\left\{\cdot\right\}
is the indicator function.
For finite alphabets,
Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]
can be identified with a random vector in
\mathbb{R}^{\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\left\lvert\mathcal{Y}\right\rvert^{2k+1}\left\lvert\mathcal{Z}\right\rvert^{2k+1}}
that always lies on the probability simplex.
Expectations can be taken the standard way for random vectors, and will always result in a valid distribution.
We define
Q^{(n)}
to be a distribution on
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z}^{2k+1},\mathcal{Y}^{2k+1}
by taking the expectation of
Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]
, and use subscripts to denote the corresponding marginal or conditional p.m.f.s obtained from the joint distribution
Q^{(n)}
.
More explicitly,

\displaystyle Q^{(n)}\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)\right]

(11)

\displaystyle Q^{(n)}_{Z_{-k}^{k},Y_{-k}^{k}}\left(z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{0}}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)\right]

(12)

\displaystyle Q^{(n)}_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k},Y_{-k}^{k}}\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)\right]}{\sum_{x_{0}}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[Q\left[X^{n},Z^{n},Y^{n}\right]\left(x_{0},z_{-k}^{k},y_{-k}^{k}\right)\right]}.

(13)

We note that

is a valid distribution by linearity of expectation, and conditioning and marginalization of

occur after taking expectations.
Convergence of a sequence of

simply means convergence of a sequence of real vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for appropriate
d

Thus, even when
P_{Z|X}
is not an additive noise channel, our choice of the distortion measure and the distortion level guarantees that lossy compression of the observation asymptotically samples from the posterior distribution
P_{X^{k}|\mathbf{Z}}
of the signal.
In applications, a way to sample from the posterior can itself be of interest.
In the next section,
we further specify the behavior of the reconstructions, and we will characterize its denoising performance with respect to a loss
\Lambda
.

III-B

Denoising Performance

From the previous section we have a characterization of the asymptotic behavior of
Q^{(n)}_{Z^{k},Y^{k}}
.
The denoising performance depends on the joint distribution of source, observation, and reconstruction
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z},\mathbf{Y}
, which motivates the following results.
Recall that we have the Markov chain
X^{n}\mathrel{\text{---}}Z^{n}\mathrel{\text{---}}Y^{n}
.
However, for fixed
n,k
, the Markov relation
X^{k}\mathrel{\text{---}}Z^{k}\mathrel{\text{---}}Y^{k}
for
k<n
does not hold in general, due to the “memory” in
\mathbf{X}
.
We next show that the
empirical
joint distribution of the source,
the observation, and the denoiser output asymptotically satisfies this Markov condition.
We require the following mild assumption on
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}
.

Definition 5

Suppose
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}
are jointly stationary.
We define their
double-sided mixing coefficient
as

\begin{split}\delta_{k}\left(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}\right)=\\
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{Z_{-\infty}^{k-1},Z_{k+1}^{\infty}}\max_{x_{0},z_{-k}^{k}}\big|&P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}\left(x_{0}\>\middle|\>z_{-k}^{k}\right)\\
&-P_{X_{0}|\mathbf{Z}}\left(x_{0}\>\middle|\>z_{-k}^{k},Z_{-\infty}^{k-1},Z_{k+1}^{\infty}\right)\big|\end{split}

(36)

and say that
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z})
are
double-sided mixing
if additionally

\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}\left(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}\right)=0.

(37)

Hereafter we use
\delta_{k}:=\delta_{k}\left(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}\right)
for brevity.

Despite the fact that
P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}\left(x_{0}\>\middle|\>Z_{-k}^{k}\right)\xrightarrow{\makebox[9.56667pt][c]{$\scriptstyle\text{a.s.}$}}P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}\left(x_{0}\>\middle|\>Z_{-\infty}^{\infty}\right)
(by the martingale convergence theorem), it is not hard to construct processes for which the
\delta_{k}
never vanish.
However, the class of pairs of processes that are double-sided mixing is large and arguably includes all those of practical interest.
For example, for reasonable
P_{Z|X}
, requiring
\mathbf{X}
to be a Markov chain is more than enough:

Proposition 1

Suppose the alphabets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y}
are finite.
Suppose
\mathbf{X}
is an ergodic Markov chain, and suppose
P_{Z|X}\left(z\>\middle|\>x\right)>0
for all
z,x
.
Then,
\delta_{k}\to 0
exponentially fast.

This result can be extended to the case when
\mathbf{X}
is an order-
m
Markov process.

Roughly speaking, the following claim establishes that the double-sided mixing coefficient controls the extent to which the empirical distribution violates the Markov condition
X_{0}\mathrel{\text{---}}Z_{-k}^{k}\mathrel{\text{---}}Y_{-k}^{k}
.

Lemma 1

Suppose
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z}
are jointly stationary.
Let
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then

\left\lVert Q^{(n)}_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k},Y_{-k}^{k}}-P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\delta_{k}.

(38)

We know from
2
that, conditioned on the observations, a source symbol
X_{i}
and its reconstruction
Y_{i}
are both distributed according to the posterior.
Applying
Lemma

1
allows us to additionally deduce they are essentially conditionally independent which, in turn, leads to the complete characterization of the denoising performance in the following theorem.

Theorem 5

Suppose the alphabets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{Y}
are finite.
Suppose
(\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Z})
are double-sided mixing, and suppose the channel matrix of
P_{Z|X}
is invertible.
Let
\Lambda:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\Lambda_{\max}]
be a loss function as defined in
Section

II-B
.
Suppose
\left\{Y^{n}\right\}_{n}
is a sequence of good codes for
\mathbf{Z}
under distortion
\rho(z,y)=-\log p_{Z|X}\left(z\>\middle|\>y\right)
at distortion level
H\left(Z\>\middle|\>X\right)
.
Then,

\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda_{n}\left(X^{n},Y^{n}(Z^{n})\right)\right]\\
&=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{Z}}\!\left[\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{(U,V)\sim\left(P_{X_{0}|\mathbf{Z}}\right)^{2}}\!\left[\Lambda(U,V)\right]\right].\end{split}

(39)

This theorem constitutes a complete characterization of the loss achieved.
The value on the right hand side improves upon the upper bound in
Theorem

3
of
[
16
]
in that instead of using the worst case coupling,
U,V
are assumed independent.

Proof:

The conditions for
Theorems

4
and
1
are satisfied.
We use the fact that the expectation of the loss is determined by the empirical distribution.

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda_{n}\left(X^{n},Y^{n}(Z^{n}\right)\right]

(40)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{x,y}\Lambda(x,y)\sum_{i}\mathbf{1}\!\left\{X_{i}=x,Y_{i}=y\right\}\right]

(41)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{(X,Y)\sim Q^{(n)}_{X_{0},Y_{0}}}\!\left[\Lambda(X,Y)\right]

(42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{Z}_{-k}^{k},\tilde{Y}_{-k}^{k}\sim Q^{(n)}_{{Z}_{-k}^{k},{Y}_{-k}^{k}}}\!\left[\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{X}\sim Q^{(n)}_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k},Y_{-k}^{k}}}\!\left[\Lambda(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}_{0})\right]\right]\\
\end{split}

(43)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{Z}_{-k}^{k},\tilde{Y}_{-k}^{k}\sim Q^{(n)}_{{Z}_{-k}^{k},{Y}_{-k}^{k}}}\!\left[\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{\tilde{X}\sim P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}}\!\left[\Lambda(\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}_{0})\right]\right]\\
&\qquad+\Lambda_{\max}\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\delta_{k}\end{split}

(44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{U,V\sim P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}\text{ i.i.d.}}\!\left[\Lambda(U,V)\right]\\
&\qquad+o_{n}(1)+\Lambda_{\max}\left\lvert\mathcal{X}\right\rvert\delta_{k}+o(1/n)\end{split}

(45)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{U,V\sim P_{X_{0}|Z_{-k}^{k}}\text{ i.i.d.}}\!\left[\Lambda(U,V)\right]+o_{n}(1)+o_{k}(1),

(46)

where
(
44
) follows from
Lemma

1
, and (
45
) follows from
2
.
Taking the limit in
n
and then
k
finishes the proof.
∎

IV-A

Comparison with Indirect Rate Distortion

The similarity of the setting with indirect rate distortion raises two questions: Is our scheme just solving the indirect rate distortion problem with channel
P_{Z|X}
and distortion
\Lambda
? Relatedly, are the compressors specified by our scheme necessarily “good” for the indirect rate distortion problem? In this section we show that the answer is “no” to both questions outside of special cases.
Adding a perception constraint to the indirect rate distortion problem, the two problems coincide under slightly weaker conditions, but are still generally different. We illustrate this case by an example with a memoryless Gaussian source
\mathbf{X}
and an AWGN channel
P_{Z|X}
.

We define the
indirect rate distortion
curve, denoted by
R_{I}(L)
, for the distortion
\Lambda
as the set of optimal values of the following optimization problem, parameterized by the loss
L
.

\displaystyle\min_{P_{Y^{k}|Z^{k}}}

\displaystyle I(Z^{k};Y^{k})

(69)

subject to

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda_{k}(X_{i},Y_{i})\right]\leq L.

(70)

Here we maintain the assumptions that
X^{k}\mathrel{\text{---}}Z^{k}\mathrel{\text{---}}Y^{k}
form a Markov chain and still have a fixed and known channel
P_{Z|X}
as in the original setup.
The only difference in this setting is that
\Lambda
is known ahead of time and the compressor is designed to minimize this particular distortion measure.

As shown by Witsenhausen in
[
17
]
, the indirect rate distortion can be reduced to rate distortion for distortion measure given by

d(z,y)=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda(X,y)\>\middle|\>Z=z\right].

(71)

We note that in the indirect formulation the compressor is directly optimized to minimize the denoising loss. Hence, the indirect rate-distortion curve serves as a lower bound for the trade-off achieved by the compression-based denoiser.

2
shows that the lower bound is not tight in general.

Proposition 2

The infimizing distribution in
Theorem

4
, i.e.
P_{Y^{k}|Z^{k}}=P_{X^{k}|Z^{k}}
, achieves a point on the indirect rate distortion curve
R_{I}(L)
if there exists some
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R},c_{2}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
z,y

\rho(z,y)=c_{1}d(z,y)+c_{2}(z).

(72)

The condition is also necessary in the case when all alphabets are finite.

In view of
Theorem

4
, this also implies that the behavior of compressors designed for the distortion
eq.

22
will in general behave differently than compressors designed for the indirect rate distortion problem.
The difference is illustrated in the following example.

Example 4
(Gaussian Source, AWGN Channel)

Let
X\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
and
N\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
be independent.
Let
Z=\sqrt{\gamma}X+N
.
We have
X|Z=z\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}\left(z\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma},\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\right)
, and
H\left(X\>\middle|\>Z\right)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(2\pi e\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\right)
.
We consider the MSE loss
\Lambda(x,y)=(x-y)^{2}
.

For the compression-based denoiser, we have
\rho(z,y)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\sqrt{\gamma}y-z\right)^{2}+\log(2\pi)\right)
.
We have
P_{Y|Z}=P_{X|Z}
, achieving loss

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{Z}\!\left[\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{(U,V)\sim P_{X|Z}}\!\left[(U-V)^{2}\right]\right]=2\frac{1}{1+\gamma}.

(73)

The rate is

\displaystyle R=I(X;Z)=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\gamma).

(74)

For the indirect rate distortion setting, we have

\displaystyle d(z,y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[(X-y)^{2}\>\middle|\>Z=z\right]

(75)

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[X\>\middle|\>Z=z\right]-y\right)^{2}+\operatorname*{\mathsf{Var}}\!\left(X\>\middle|\>Z=z\right)

(76)

\displaystyle=\left(z\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}-y\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{1+\gamma}

(77)

Then
Y
achieves the rate distortion for compressing
X^{\prime}:=Z\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}
under MSE distortion constraint
L^{\prime}=L-\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
.
We have
\operatorname*{\mathsf{Var}}\!\left(X^{\prime}\right)=\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}
, and, from the rate distortion function of a Gaussian source
[
15
]
, we achieve rate

\displaystyle R=\left(\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{\gamma}{(1+\gamma)L-1}\right)\right)_{+}.

(78)

To compare with the compression-based denoiser, we set
L=2\frac{1}{1+\gamma}
, which yields a rate of

\displaystyle R=\left(\frac{1}{2}\log(\gamma)\right)_{+}.

(79)

For
\gamma\leq 1
we have
Y=0
always.
Otherwise

\displaystyle Z|Y=y

\displaystyle\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}\left(y\frac{1+\gamma}{\sqrt{\gamma}},\frac{1+\gamma}{\gamma}\right)

(80)

\displaystyle Y|Z=z

\displaystyle\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}\left(z\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma},\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\gamma-1}{1+\gamma}\right)

(81)

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}\left(0,\frac{\gamma-1}{1+\gamma}\right).

(82)

Alternatively, if we set
R=\frac{1}{2}\log(1+\gamma)
, the indirect rate distortion scheme achieves loss

\displaystyle L=\frac{1+2\gamma}{(1+\gamma)^{2}}<2\frac{1}{1+\gamma}.

(83)

In the above example it is possible to achieve the indirect rate distortion curve by scaling the output of the compression-based scheme by
\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}
.
Designing compressors without knowledge of the denoising task but allowing for arbitrary post-processing is studied by Kipnis et al in
[
8
,
9
]
.
Their setting differs in that the post-processing allows for full knowledge of the source distribution and denoising loss, considering
\inf_{f}\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda_{n}(X^{n},f(Y^{n})\right]
instead of
\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda_{n}\left(X^{n},Y^{n}(Z^{n})\right)\right]
.

IV-A
1
Rate-Distortion-Perception

Optimality for the rate distortion problem is often impossible because, by design, the reconstructions must have the same distribution as the source yet the indirect rate distortion curve achieving
P_{Y^{k}|Z^{k}}
can in general be very different.
Adding an additional constraint to rate distortion that the reconstruction resemble the source in distribution has been recently studied in rate-distortion-perception theory
[
2
,
1
]
.
This motivates the following comparison of the rate and the denoising performance of the compression-based denoiser to the following indirect rate-distortion curve with perfect perception constraint.

Definition 6

We define the indirect rate-distortion curve with perfect perception constraint, denoted
R_{P_{X}}(L)
, to be the solution to the optimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{P_{Y|Z}}

\displaystyle I(Z;Y)

(84)

subject to

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{(Y,Z)\sim P_{Y|Z}\otimes P_{Z}}\!\left[d(Z,Y)\right]\leq L

(85)

\displaystyle P_{Y|Z}\circ P_{Z}=P_{X}.

(86)

where

d(z,y):=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[\Lambda(X,y)\>\middle|\>Z=z\right].

(87)

The following proposition characterizes the solution of this problem.

Proposition 3

Suppose there exists a
P_{Y|Z}^{*}
satisfying

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}_{P_{Y|Z}^{*}\otimes P_{Z}}\!\left[d(Z,Y)\right]

\displaystyle=L

(88)

\displaystyle P_{Y|Z}^{*}\circ P_{Z}

\displaystyle=P_{X}

(89)

\displaystyle\frac{dP_{Y|Z}^{*}}{dP_{X}}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\beta d(z,y)+A(y)+B(z)\right)

(90)

for some
\beta,A,B,L
.
Then
P_{Y|Z}^{*}
uniquely (up to
P_{Z}
-a.s.-equivalence) achieves
R_{P_{X}}(L)
.
The existence of such
\beta,A,B,L
is also necessary in the case where all alphabets are finite.

We next apply this proposition to the Gaussian case.

Example 5
(Gaussian Source, AWGN Channel)

Let
X_{i}\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
and
N_{i}\sim\operatorname{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
be i.i.d.
Let
Z_{i}=\sqrt{\gamma}X_{i}+N_{i}
.
We consider the MSE loss
\Lambda(x,y)=(x-y)^{2}
.
Note that

\displaystyle d(z,y)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[(X-y)^{2}\>\middle|\>Z=z\right]

(91)

\displaystyle=\left(\operatorname*{\mathbb{E}}\!\left[X\>\middle|\>Z=z\right]-y\right)^{2}+\operatorname*{\mathsf{Var}}\!\left(X\>\middle|\>Z=z\right)

(92)

\displaystyle=\left(z\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}}{1+\gamma}-y\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{1+\gamma}.

(93)

By construction, setting
P_{Y|Z}^{*}=P_{X|Z}
satisfies the constraint
P_{Y|Z}\circ P_{Z}=P_{X}
.
By Bayes’ rule (and constraint that
Y\overset{d}{=}X
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{dP_{Y|Z}^{*}}{dP_{X}}(y,z)=\frac{dP_{Z|Y}^{*}}{dP_{Z}}(z,y).

(94)

Then

\displaystyle-\log\frac{dP_{Y|Z}^{*}}{dP_{X}}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\gamma y^{2}-\sqrt{\gamma}yz+\frac{1}{2}z^{2}+\log p_{Z}(z)

(95)

VI
Conclusion and Future Work

In this paper we have established that lossy compression performs denoising for any stationary ergodic source observed through a DMC by outputting a sample from the posterior.
This was done by designing the distortion measure
\rho(z,y)=-\log p_{Z|X}\left(z\>\middle|\>y\right)
to match the channel and operating at a distortion level
D=H\left(Z\>\middle|\>X\right)
.
A key technical contribution was showing that,
under a mixing condition, the empirical distributions of the source
X
and the output
Y
given the observation
Z
approach conditional independence.
This lead to an exact expression for the loss achieved by the compression based denoiser as the expected loss of two independent samples from the posterior.
The substantial improvement of the characterization over previous bounds is demonstrated in several special cases.
Notably, when measuring denoising performance with MSE, the conditional independence results in a factor of 2 improvement over the previous bound.

Several directions remain for future work.
First, the results can be extended to almost-sure convergence and for general alphabets.
Second, characterizing the behavior of the denoiser operating at distortions
D\neq H\left(Z\>\middle|\>X\right)
would provide insight to the tradeoffs available in the given framework, and studying the rate distortion problem with distortion measure
\rho(z,y)=-\log p_{Z|X}\left(z\>\middle|\>y\right)
and arbitrary distortion level can be of independent interest.
Finally, experimental work applying the denoiser described here to real-world data would validate the utility of the given framework.

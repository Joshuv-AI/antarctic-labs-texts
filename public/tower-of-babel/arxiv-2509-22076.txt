Jannik Breitkopf, Stefan Ulbrich

Abstract
We develop a variational calculus for entropy solutions of the Generalized Riemann Problem (GRP) for strictly hyperbolic systems of conservation laws where the control is the initial state. The GRP has a discontinuous initial state with exactly one discontinuity and continuously differentiable ( C 1 C^{1} ) states left and right of it. The control consists of the C 1 C^{1} parts of the initial state and the position of the discontinuity. Solutions of the problem are generally discontinuous since they contain shock curves. We assume the time horizon T > 0 T>0 to be sufficiently small such that no shocks interact and no new shocks are generated. Moreover, we assume that no rarefaction waves occur and that the jump of the initial state is sufficiently small. Since the shock positions depend on the control, a transformation to a reference space is used to fix the shock positions. In the reference space, we prove that the solution of the GRP between the shocks is continuously differentiable from the control space to C 0 C^{0} . In physical coordinates, this implies that the shock curves in C 1 C^{1} and the states between the shocks in the topology of C 0 C^{0} depend continuously differentiable on the control. As a consequence, we obtain the differentiability of tracking type objective functionals.

Introduction

We consider optimal initial-value control problems for
entropy solutions of the Generalized Riemann Problem (GRP)
for strictly hyperbolic systems of balance laws of the form

\displaystyle y_{t}+f(y)_{x}

\displaystyle=g(\cdot,y)

\displaystyle\text{on }\mathcal{D}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},

(GRP)

\displaystyle y(0,\cdot)

\displaystyle=u_{0}

\displaystyle\text{on }[-\ell,\ell].

We assume that
g\in C^{1,1}(\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
f\in C^{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The initial state is piecewise smooth
with one discontinuity located at
x=x_{0}
, i.e.,

u_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{l}(x)&\text{if }x\in[-\ell,x_{0}),\\
u_{r}(x)&\text{if }x\in(x_{0},\ell].\end{array}\right.

(1)

For a suitable open subset
\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{ad}}\subset\mathcal{V}\coloneqq C^{1}([-\ell,\varepsilon];\mathbb{R}^{n})\times C^{1}([-\varepsilon,\ell];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the control is

u=(u_{l},u_{r},x_{0})\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{ad}}\coloneqq\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{ad}}\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon).

We consider for suitable
a<b
with
\{T\}\times[a,b]\subset\mathcal{D}
the objective functional

u\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{ad}}\mapsto J(u)\coloneqq\int_{a}^{b}\Phi\left(y(T,x),y_{d}(x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{subject to }y\text{ solves \eqref{GRP}}

(2)

with
\Phi\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
and a desired state
y_{d}\in BV([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The aim of this paper is to develop a variational calculus for the dependence of solutions
of (
GRP
) on the control
u_{0}
. In particular, this will imply
the differentiability of the objective functional (
2
).

Assumptions.
The problem (
GRP
) is assumed to be strictly hyperbolic, i.e.,
the Jacobian matrix
A(y)=f^{\prime}(y)
has real and distinct eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(y)<\ldots<\lambda_{n}(y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
All eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
A
are assumed to be bounded in
C^{1,1}
. We assume that there are
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}>0

and disjoint intervals
\Lambda_{i}=[\lambda_{i}^{-},\lambda_{i}^{+}]
with

|\lambda_{i}(y)|\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{max}},\quad\lambda_{i}(y)\in\Lambda_{i}\qquad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(3)

We denote by
\eta_{\min}=\min_{i=1}^{n-1}(\lambda_{i+1}^{-}-\lambda_{i}^{+})>0

the minimal difference of the eigenvalues.
With
r_{i}(y),l_{i}(y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote the right and left
eigenvectors of
A(y)
, precisely

A(y)r_{i}(y)=\lambda_{i}(y)r_{i}(y),\quad l_{i}(y)^{\top}A(y)=\lambda_{i}(y)l_{i}(y)^{\top}.

(4)

Moreover, we assume
|r_{i}(y)|\equiv 1
and

l_{i}(y)^{\top}r_{j}(y)=\delta_{ij}
with the Kronecker delta
\delta_{ij}
.

The underlying domain of (
GRP
) is assumed to be

\mathcal{D}=\left\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\ |\ -\ell+t\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\leq x\leq\ell-t\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\right\}

(5)

for a small time horizon
T>0
.
Note that this ensures that any solution of (
GRP
) on
\mathcal{D}

is fully determined by the initial values in
t=0
and no
boundary data is required.

2
Existence and Stability Properties of Discontinuous Solutions

In this section, we prove existence and stability properties
of discontinuous solutions of semi- and quasilinear hyperbolic
systems of conservation laws.

A crucial structural feature of hyperbolic
conservation laws is that information is transported along
characteristic curves. Consider a domain
\Omega\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
and suppose there are

y,z\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that it holds in the classical sense that

y_{t}+A(z)y_{x}=g(\cdot,y)\quad\text{on }\Omega.

(6)

Multiplying with
l_{i}(z)
yields for
the characteristic variables
y_{i}\coloneqq l_{i}(z)^{\top}y
that

(y_{i})_{t}+\lambda_{i}(z)(y_{i})_{x}=l_{i}(z)^{\top}g(\cdot,y)+\left(l_{i}(z)_{t}+\lambda_{i}(z)l_{i}(z)_{x}\right)^{\top}y.

(7)

Let
\xi_{i}=\xi(\cdot;t,x,z)
be the solution of the ordinary differential equation

\xi_{i}^{\prime}(s)=\lambda_{i}\left(z(s,\xi_{i}(s)\right),\quad\xi_{i}(t)=x.

(8)

Then, (
7
) shows that a solution of (
6
) satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y_{i}(t,\xi_{i}(t))=\left[l_{i}(z)^{\top}g(\cdot,y)+\left(l_{i}(z)_{t}+\lambda_{i}(z)l_{i}(z)_{x}\right)^{\top}y\right](t,\xi_{i}(t)).

(9)

2.1
Entropy Solutions and Jump Condition

In general, weak solutions of (
GRP
) are not unique,
see, e.g.,
[
4
,
14
]
.
To select the physically relevant solution,
the
Lax Entropy Condition

[
10
]
is imposed along shock curves.

Definition 1
.

A function
y\in BV(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an
entropy solution
of (
GRP
) if

1.

y
is a weak solution, i.e.,
\mathrm{ess\ lim}_{t\searrow 0}\|y(t,\cdot)-u_{0}\|_{L^{1}([-\ell,\ell];\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0
and

\int_{\mathcal{D}}(y^{\top}\phi_{t}+f(y)^{\top}\phi_{x}+g(\cdot,y)^{\top}\phi)\ \mathrm{d}(t,x)=0\quad\forall\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

2.

If
y
is discontinuous along a curve
t\mapsto(t,\xi(t))
, the left
and right sided limits
y^{\pm}(t)=y(t,\xi(t)\pm))

exist, and for some
j\in[n]

it holds for a.e.

that

\lambda_{j}(y^{-})\geq\xi^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{j}(y^{+}),\quad\lambda_{k}(y^{\pm})>\xi^{\prime}>\lambda_{i}(y^{\pm})\quad\forall i<j<k.

(10)

A weak solution of (
GRP
) satisfies the Rankine–Hugoniot jump condition.

Lemma 2
.

With the notation of
definition

1
,
let
y\in BV(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a weak solution of (
GRP
)
and assume it is discontinuous along

.
Then it holds

\dot{\xi}(t)\left(y^{+}(t)-y^{-}(t)\right)=f\left(y^{+}(t)\right)-f\left(y^{-}(t)\right)\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

(11)

A proof can be found in
[
4
, Theorem 4.2]
.
The condition (
11
) can be equivalently
reformulated in terms of the jump of
l_{i}(y)^{\top}y
.
Let
A(y_{1},y_{2})=\int_{0}^{1}A\left(sy_{1}+(1-s)y_{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s

with eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}(y_{1},y_{2})

and eigenvectors
r_{i}(y_{1},y_{2})
and
l_{i}(y_{1},y_{2})
,
normalized as in (
4
).
Note that

is strictly hyperbolic.

Lemma 3
.

Assume a solution
y\in BV(\mathcal{D};\mathbb{R}^{n})
of (
GRP
)
is discontinuous in
(t,\xi(t))
in all
t\in[0,T]

for a curve
\xi\in C^{1}([0,T])
.
Let

and assume that

Then (
11
) holds if and only if there is

such that for all

(12)

Proof.

The matrix
A(y^{-},y^{+})
is also strictly hyperbolic
and (
11
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}(t)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}(y^{+}(t),y^{-}(t)),

(13)

\displaystyle y^{+}(t)-y^{-}(t)

\displaystyle=c\,r_{j}(y^{+}(t),y^{-}(t))

(14)

for some

and

.
Therefore, (

) is equivalent to

l_{i}(y^{+},y^{-})^{\top}(y^{+}-y^{-})=0\qquad\forall i\neq j.

(15)

Inserting

in (

) and rearranging it yields (
12
)
for all
i\neq j
.
Conversely, if (
12
) is satisfied for all

,
then also (
15
) holds.

Remark 4
.

For our later considerations, it is favorable
if the state
y
is shifted by a constant additive term
such that it only attains values around zero.
This is mainly due to (

) having
large Lipschitz constants w.r.t.

For

, define a new flux function

If

2.2
Admissible Controls

In this section, we define the set of admissible controls

This depends on the structure of solutions of the Riemann Problem

\displaystyle y_{t}+f(y)_{x}=0\quad\text{on }[0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\qquad y(0,\cdot)=u_{0}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{L}&\text{if }x<0,\\

The existence of entropy solutions for (

Definition 5

If

\Phi_{j}\in C^{1}([s_{j}^{-},s_{j}^{+}];\mathbb{R}^{n})

for

\hat{y}(t,x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\hat{y}_{0}=u_{L}&\text{if }x/t<s_{1}^{-},\\
\hat{y}_{j}&\text{if }s_{i}^{+}<x/t<s_{i+1}^{-},&\forall i\in[n-1],\\
\Phi_{j}(x/t)&\text{if }s_{i}^{-}<x/t<s_{i}^{+},&\forall i\in\mathcal{R},\\
\hat{y}_{n}=u_{R}&\text{if }x/t>s_{n}^{+}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(18)

If

and the

-th characteristic field

Proof.

The detailed proof can be found in
[
4

We only give a short summary since the notation and construction

is necessary for our further considerations.
It can be shown that for all characteristic fields

and any left state

\Psi_{i}(\cdot)(u_{0})\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}^{n})

\Psi_{i}(0)(u_{0})=u_{0}

and the following properties.
If the
i
-th field is genuinely
nonlinear, any states
\Psi_{i}(\sigma)(u_{0})

can be
connected to
u_{0}
be an
i
-rarefaction wave.
All states
\Psi_{i}(\sigma)(u_{0})
for

can be
connected to

be an
i

If the

-th field is linearly degenerated,

2.3
The Reference Space

To cope with the varying positions of shock curves,
we define a fixed space-time domain serving as a
reference space and equivalently reformulate (
GRP
) as done in
[
11
]
.
The reference space is obtained by a transformation of the space variable.
As a consequence, the positions of shock curves are fixed.

All quantities in the reference space will be denoted with a bar.
The original coordinates will also be called the physical coordinates.
The reference domain is motivated by the structure
of an entropy solution of the Riemann Problem, see (
18
).
In the sequel, we denote by
s_{1}<\ldots<s_{n}
the shock speeds
of the solution of the Riemann Problem with initial state
(u_{L},u_{R})
,
see
7
.
Define the sectors

\bar{D}_{i}=\{(t,\bar{x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\ |\ s_{i}t\leq\bar{x}\leq s_{i+1}t\},\quad i\in[n-1].

(23)

For the sectors left of the
1
-shock and right of the
n
-shock curves,
we define the reference speeds
s_{\ell}=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
and

s_{r}=-\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}
and define

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{0}

\displaystyle=\{(t,\bar{x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\ |\ -\ell+s_{\ell}t\leq\bar{x}\leq s_{1}t\},

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{n}

\displaystyle=\{(t,\bar{x})\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\ |\ s_{n}t\leq\bar{x}\leq\ell+s_{r}t\},

and
\bar{D}=\cup_{i=0}^{n}\bar{D}_{i}
.
The interior boundaries are denoted by

\Sigma_{i}=\{(t,s_{i}t)\ |\ t\in[0,T]\}
for all
i\in[n]
.
Based on the definition of the sets
\bar{D}_{0},\ldots,\bar{D}_{n}

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we proved that the piecewise
smooth entropy solution of the Generalized Riemann Problem is differentiable
w.r.t. the piecewise smooth parts
and the position of the discontinuity of the initial state
after transforming it into a reference space.
An immediate consequence was the differentiability of the
position of shock curves w.r.t. the controls.
By combining these results, the differentiability of a
large class of tracking-type functionals followed.

The results of this paper are limited to small time horizons
with no shock formations or interactions.
Moreover, it does not allow rarefaction waves.
This is subject to future research.
An adjoint gradient representation for the objective functional
extending
[
16
]
to hyperbolic systems is currently under development.

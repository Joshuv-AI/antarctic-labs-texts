Use of contrast-enhanced mammography for preoperative prediction of lymphovascular invasion status in invasive breast cancer
Authors: Liya Gong, Muzi Wen, Ying Ye, Zhiyue Zhao, Junyan Wen, Ziqi Wu, Yanyu Hao, Xiaomei Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo investigate the use of contrast-enhanced mammography (CEM) for preoperative prediction of lymphovascular invasion (LVI) status in invasive breast cancer.MethodsA total of 243 female patients diagnosed with invasive breast cancer (median age: 49 years; range: 27–77 years) who received preoperative CEM examination in our hospital between September 2018 and February 2024 were retrospectively collected and analyzed. The study population were chronologically divided into training and test datasets in an approximate ratio of 7:3. LVI status was determined using postoperative histopathologic examination. CEM features were analyzed on the low energy and the recombined images. To identify independent predictors for LVI status, univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed on CEM and clinicopathologic features. Logistic regression and six machine learning methods were used to construct prediction models in the training dataset, and their performance were evaluated with ROC curve in the test dataset.ResultsIn training and test datasets, the rates of LVI-positive were 39% (67 of 172) and 34% (24 of 71), respectively. High Ki67 index, BI-RADS category 5, breast composition category c/d, axillary adenopathy, mild to marked background parenchymal enhancement level, and lesion with complete enhancement or enhancement extending on CEM images were significantly correlated with LVI-positive (all P < 0.05) and were incorporated to construct prediction models. The AUCs of seven prediction models were in the range of 0.713–0.850 in the test datasets, where the logistic regression model yielded an AUC of 0.835 (95%CI: 0.717–0.924), showing similar or higher AUC than the six machine learning models.ConclusionsCEM could be useful for preoperative noninvasive prediction of LVI status in invasive breast cancer. The prediction model integrating contrast-enhanced mammography features and Ki67 index may serve as a complementary tool to assist clinicians in preoperative prediction of lymphovascular invasion status in patients with invasive breast cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-026-01065-1.Keywords: Breast cancer, Contrast-enhanced mammography, Lymphovascular invasion, Machine learning, Nomograms

Introduction

Despite many advances in treatment management, breast cancer remains the leading cause of cancer death in women worldwide [1, 2], which could be attributed to tumor recurrence and distant metastases [3]. Lymphovascular invasion (LVI), defined as the presence of cancer cells within the lymphatic or blood vessels, is a significant negative prognostic factor and has been considered as a key precursor step to the development of lymph node metastases and distant metastases in invasive breast cancer [4, 5]. Prior studies also demonstrate that LVI is associated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy efficacy, positive margins and locoregional recurrence in patients with breast cancer [4, 6, 7]. Therefore, preoperative prediction of LVI would be of considerable clinical significance, as it could assist clinicians in making optimal treatment decisions—such as determining surgical strategies and approaches for the breast and axilla and tailoring the intensity of neoadjuvant therapy—thereby improving patient outcomes.

However, there is still no reliable approach available for the preoperative assessment of LVI status [8]. Currently, the gold-standard identification of LVI relies on histopathologic examination of surgical excision specimens, yet this method is unable to provide any predictive value before operation. Preoperative biopsy, with inherent biases due to sampling error, has limited value in comprehensive assessment of LVI. Additionally, clinicopathologic variables such as age, menopausal status, tumor size, and molecular characterization, have been reported to be correlated with LVI in breast cancer [9], but the results are considerably heterogenous across studies and the predictive performance need to be improved.

Medical imaging examination, with its non-invasive nature, plays a crucial role in the diagnosis and management of breast cancer. Several studies have explored the predictive value of MRI-based and mammography-based radiomic features in assessing LVI in breast cancer [10–14]. Though promising, the complex and abstract analytical processes involved limit their generalizability and clinical application. Meanwhile, some prior studies also suggested that simple and intuitive imaging features of mammography and contrast-enhanced MRI could be used for preoperative prediction of LVI in breast cancer [10–16]. Contrast-enhanced mammography (CEM) is an emerging technology, which is performed with dual-energy exposures after intravenous injection of iodinated contrast agent. While the low-energy image serves as the standard digital mammogram, the recombined image produced using a weighted logarithmic subtraction of the low- and high-energy images provides enhancement characteristics of breast lesions, similar to the contrast-enhanced MRI [17–19]. Therefore, CEM not only remains the advantage in the assessment of breast calcifications [20, 21], but also adds information of tumor angiogenesis or blood supply of a breast lesion, which can serve as a practical alternative when MRI is not feasible for a patient or in regions with limited access to MRI resources. In addition, CEM also has the advantages of lower cost and shorter examination time compared to MRI. Hence, CEM features could have the potential to predict LVI preoperatively.

To our knowledge, the predictive value of CEM for LVI prediction remains unclear. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the predictive performance of CEM for LVI and develop an easy-to-use model for the preoperative assessment of LVI in invasive breast cancer by integrating CEM and clinicopathologic features.

Materials and methods

Study population and data collection

This retrospective study, conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, was approved by the institutional review board in our hospital, and the requirement for patient informed consent was waived. Initially, we consecutively collected 315 female patients with invasive breast cancer who underwent surgery (either mastectomy or breast-conserving surgery) at our center between September 2018 and February 2024. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed invasive ductal carcinoma; (2) patients underwent CEM examination within one month before surgery; (3) no neoadjuvant therapy. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Lack of postoperative histopathological LVI status or incomplete baseline clinicopathologic data; (2) previous history of breast cancer or bilateral breast cancer; (3) Poor quality of CEM images; (4) with percutaneous biopsy performed before CEM examination. The included patients were chronologically divided into training and test datasets for model development and validation in an approximate ratio of 7:3. Finally, 243 female patients (median age: 49 years; range: 27–77 years) were ultimately included, with 172 patients (mean age, 49 years ± 9 [standard deviation]) in the training dataset and 71 (49 years ± 9) in the test dataset (Fig. 1).

Study population flow chart. CEM = contrast-enhanced mammography

With electronic medical records, we collected clinicopathologic data of patients including age, menopausal status, histologic type, Ki-67 index, estrogen receptor (ER) status, progesterone receptor (PR) status, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) status, LVI status, surgery and treatment information. Specifically, LVI-positive was defined as the presence of cancer cells within the lymphatic or blood vessels according to histopathologic examination of surgical specimens. Ki-67 index, ER, PR and HER2 status were determined pathologically by immunohistochemistry (IHC). ER/PR positivity (ER+/PR+) was defined by > 10% of tumor cells expressing ER/PR [22]. The Ki-67 index threshold was set at 20%, with low expression of < 20% and high expression of ≥ 20% [23]. For HER2 status, IHC staining 0 to 1 + was categorized as HER2-, whereas IHC staining 3 + and/or HER2 gene amplification in fluorescence in situ hybridization testing was classified as HER2+ [24].

Imaging technique and image interpretation

All image scans were performed using Senographe Essential (GE Healthcare). Prior to imaging acquisition, the iodinated contrast agent (Omnipaque, GE Healthcare; 300 mgI/mL) was injected into the upper arm vein with the dose of 1.5 ml/kg and an injection rate of 3.0 ml/s. At 2 min post-injection, the breast was compressed, and low-energy (26–30 kVp) and high-energy (45–49 kVp) exposures were performed in the craniocaudal (CC) and mediolateral oblique (MLO) views of each breast. The imaging process for each patient was completed within 5 min. The low- and high-energy images are processed using a weighted subtraction technique to obtain the recombined image to show areas of contrast enhancement.

According to BI-RADS lexicon for CEM released in 2022 by American College of Radiology [25], two radiologists with 3- and 8-years’ experience in breast imaging, who were blinded to the pathologic results, independently reviewed the CEM images to evaluate imaging features, and disagreements were resolved via consensus. The evaluated CEM features included: (1) breast tissue features: breast composition category, background parenchymal enhancement (BPE) level and BPE symmetry (Symmetric or Asymmetric); (2) lesion features: location of lesion, tumor diameter (maximum diameter measured on the CC view of the recombined images), suspicious calcification (Yes or No), lesion category (seen on low-energy images only, on recombined images only or on both images), lesion enhancement type (mass or non-mass enhancement), internal enhancement pattern (homogeneous or heterogeneous), lesion conspicuity relative to BPE, extent of enhancement (partially enhances, completely enhances and enhancement extends) and BI-RADS assessment category; (3) associated features: skin thickening (> 2 mm), skin/nipple invasion and axillary adenopathy (Yes or No). Axillary adenopathy is defined as lymph nodes with a short-axis size greater than 2 cm, a rounded or irregular shape, indistinct or spiculated margins, absence of a fatty hilum, or increased density/microcalcifications.

Additionally, a cohort of 30 cases were randomly selected from the training dataset and two radiologists independently conducted the imaging evaluation for each case. Intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) and Cohen’s kappa statistics were applied to assess the inter-observer agreements of CEM features evaluation. CEM features with an ICC or kappa value greater than 0.75 were considered to have good agreement [26].

Statistical analysis and model development

All statistical analyses were conducted with R software (version 4.3.1; https://www.r-project.org/), IBM SPSS Statistics 26 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA), and Python 3.10.19. Machine learning analyses were implemented using the following Python libraries: scikit-learn (version 1.3.0), XGBoost (version 2.0.3), pandas (version 2.3.3), NumPy (version 1.26.4), SciPy (version 1.11.4), and matplotlib (version 3.10.8).Descriptive analyses were used to summarize the baseline clinicopathologic characteristics, while differences between the training and test datasets were assessed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. The inter-observer agreement of the CEM features was measured with ICCs. We applied univariable logistic regression to test the correlation of LVI with clinicopathologic-image characteristics in the training dataset, and selected variables with a P-value < 0.2 for further analysis. Variance inflation factors (VIFs) were calculated to assess possible multicollinearity among variables, and satisfactory VIF value was defined as less than 5. Then, multivariable logistic regression analysis with the forward selection method was used to identify independent predictors for LVI. For each factor, the odds ratio (OR) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were calculated.

Integrating independent predictors, we developed seven predictive models via multivariable logistic regression and six machine learning methods: eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), decision tree (DT), random forest (RF), support vector machine (SVM), Bayesian Gaussian, and k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN). For the development of the machine learning models, the optimal hyperparameters for each model were determined using a grid search method and 5-fold cross-validation. The predictive performance of each model was evaluated using the area under the ROC curve (AUC), and its 95% CI was calculated using the bootstrap method. Internal validation was performed via cross-validation, adopting the average AUC from the 5-fold cross-validation as an optimistic corrected estimate of the predictive performance of each model. We also calculated sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) to comprehensively assess each model’s discrimination for LVI. The Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test was used to evaluate the calibration of each model. All models were developed using the training dataset and their performance were validated using an independent test dataset.

In addition, Delong’s test was applied to compare the AUC of the logistic regression model with each machine learning model. To provide clinicians with an accessible quantitative tool for personalized prediction of LVI, we constructed a nomogram based on the multivariable logistic regression model.

Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test showed that the logistic regression model had good calibration for LVI prediction, with P values of 0.895 and 0.936 in the training and test datasets. The P values of the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test for the six machine learning models (XGBoost, DT, SVM, KNN, RF, and Bayesian Gaussian) were 0.277, 1, 0.005 ,0.055, 0.072 and <0.001 in the training dataset, and 0.085, <0.001, 0.008, 0.088, 0.179 and <0.001 in the test dataset, respectively.

Delong’s test showed that the AUC of logistic regression model was significantly higher than that of the DT model (P = 0.003), while there were no significant differences between the AUC of the logistic regression model and the five machine learning models (XGBoost, SVM, KNN, RF and Bayesian Gaussian), with P-values of 0.094, 0.584, 0.069, 0.852 and 0.834, respectively. The logistic regression model achieved an accuracy of 0.746, sensitivity of 0.750, specificity of 0.745, PPV of 0.600, and NPV of 0.854 in the test dataset (Table 3). Discussion

This study demonstrated that high Ki67 index, BI-RADS category 5, breast composition category c/d, axillary adenopathy, mild to marked background parenchymal enhancement level, and lesion completely enhances or enhancement extends on CEM images were independent predictors for LVI in invasive breast cancer. Based on these independent predictors, we developed and validated seven models for preoperative prediction of LVI. The logistic regression model achieved an AUC of 0.835 (95% CI: 0.717–0.924) and a good calibration in the test dataset, showing similar or better performance compared to the six machine learning models (AUC: 0.713–0.850). The logistic regression model was further visualized as a nomogram to provide clinicians with an easy-to-use tool for personalized quantitative prediction of LVI.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the predictive value of CEM features for LVI in invasive breast cancer, showing promising predictive performance. While Liu et al. previously reported that digital breast tomography (DBT)-based model showed an AUC of 0.774 for LVI prediction [13], CEM-based model developed in our study achieved a higher AUC of 0.835 in the test dataset, which may attribute to that CEM can provide added enhancement characteristics and more comprehensive assessment of breast lesions, similar to the contrast-enhanced MRI [17]. Another study extracting the intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics features based on DBT images [14], achieved an even better prediction performance (AUC = 0.905), probably due to more valuable insights provided by the peri-tumor area and radiomics features [27]. In addition, previous studies showed that MRI-based radiomics model achieved good performance for prediction of LVI in breast cancer [10, 15, 27, 28]. However, the practical application of radiomic models in clinical settings may poses challenges due to the complex analytical processes and poor interpretability [29, 30]. In contrast, the nomogram integrating CEM features and Ki-67 index in our study are relatively straightforward to implement, requiring no dedicated software or postprocessing, and the six predictors included in the nomogram are all accessible in routine clinical work-up. In addition, compared to MRI examinations, CEM is simpler and more accessible. Furthermore, similar predictive performance of the logistic regression model and six machine learning models reinforces the reliability of the selected CEM features as valuable predictors for LVI in breast cancer.

Among the six predictors in the nomogram, the CEM feature named extent of enhancement holds the heaviest weight for the prediction of LVI status in invasive breast cancer. Although there is little knowledge about the underlying biological mechanism of the association between the extent of enhancement type and LVI status, it is generally acknowledged that more extensive enhancement of a lesion typically indicates higher angiogenic capacity [31]. This finding is in line with previous study, suggesting that LVI was strongly correlated with elevated peritumoral lymphovascular density and more aggressive neovascularisation [32, 33]. Axillary adenopathy holds the second heaviest weight in the nomogram for LVI prediction, which is consistent with previous studies reporting a strong correlation between axillary lymph node metastases and LVI in breast cancer [10, 27]. In addition, BPE level is an important CEM feature concerning the enhancement of the breast fibroglandular tissue and has been found to be positively correlated with LVI, which is consistent with previous MRI-based findings [34]. This relationship may result from the influence of endogenous estrogen levels on BPE, promoting angiogenesis and increased blood flow within breast tissue. Increased blood flow and angiogenesis in breast tissue are reflected as heightened BPE on imaging [35], and these enhanced conditions subsequently create a favorable environment for cancer cell invasion and spread [9]. Consequently, for those breast cancer patients with higher BPE level on CEM images, tumor cells are more likely to erode and destroy surrounding capillaries and lymphatic vessels, leading to the occurrence of LVI.

This study still has some limitations. First, as a retrospective single-center study, selection bias is inevitable. Although we performed a temporal validation via splitting study population by time into a training dataset (prior period) and a test dataset (later period) for model development and validation, which can provide some external validity evidence of a prediction model [36], a multicenter prospective study is still needed to validate the clinical utility of the nomogram. Second, there are concerns on interobserver agreements in the evaluation of CEM features among radiologists, which may affect future clinical application. In this study, good interobserver agreements (ICC: 0.77–0.86) were observed for those CEM features in the nomogram, and we suggest that providing relevant pre-training may further improve the reliability. Finally, this study merely focused on investigating the value of radiologist-read qualitative CEM features for LVI prediction. Whether combining with CEM-based radiomics quantitative features could further improve the predictive performance is worth exploring in the future.

In conclusion, this study reveals CEM could be useful for preoperative noninvasive prediction of LVI status in invasive breast cancer. The developed nomogram integrating CEM features and Ki67 index may serve as a complementary tool to assist clinicians in personalized prediction of LVI status before surgery, so as to help clinical decision-making and improve treatment strategies for patients.

Module lattices and their shortest vectors
Nihar Gargava, Vlad Serban, Maryna Viazovska, Ilaria Viglino

Abstract
. We study the shortest vector lengths in module lattices over arbitrary number fields, with an emphasis on cyclotomic fields. In particular, we sharpen the techniques of [ 1 ] to establish improved results for the variance of the number of lattice vectors of bounded Euclidean norm in a random module lattice. We then derive tight probabilistic bounds for the shortest vector lengths for several notions of random module lattice.



1.
Introduction

One of the central concerns in lattice-based cryptography is to prove or disprove the existence
of fast algorithms that can find or approximate the length of the shortest vectors of a lattice.
The non-existence of such algorithms is still a wide-open question, and there exist well-publicized open challenges
[
2
]

inviting competitors to submit algorithms solving the shortest vector problem (SVP) for "randomly" produced lattices. For random lattices
\Lambda
in increasing dimension
n
, it turns out that the length of the shortest vector, denoted by
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)
, can be predicted almost exactly:

Theorem 1
.

[
3
, Theorem 5]

Let
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a Haar-random lattice chosen from the space
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, with probability
1-o(1)
we have

(2)

1-\frac{\log\log n}{n}\leq 2^{-\frac{1}{n}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)}{\gamma(n)}\leq 1+\frac{\log\log n}{n},

where
\gamma(n)
is radius of a ball of unit volume in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 2
.

For large
n
,
\gamma(n)\simeq\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2\pi e}}
by Stirling’s approximation.

A weaker form of this is stated by Ajtai in
[
4
]
without proof.
In the lattice challenges in
[
2
]
, this result is used to set benchmarks for challenge contenders. Such results greatly inform our understanding and analysis of lattice reduction algorithms
[
3
,
5
]
.

Ever since the arrival of the NTRU cryptosystem
[
6
]
, lattices with additional algebraic structure called module lattices, which do not exhibit the same behavior as the Haar random lattices of Theorem
1
, have been the object of study and scrutiny as they promise efficiency gains compared to unstructured lattices. Module lattices now underlie the security of several NIST post-quantum standards
[
7
]
. It is therefore crucial to understand and quantify the behavior of short vectors as well as the hardness of computational problems such as SVP on such lattices.
The lattices in question are
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules, where
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the ring of integers of a number field
K
, and the latter is often taken to be a cyclotomic field. These are one of the main special classes of lattices studied in the context of post-quantum cryptography
[
8
,
9
]
. See also work on ring-based versions of the learning with errors (LWE) and short integer solutions (SIS) problems such as
[
10
,
11
]
.

Producing asymptotic estimates like Theorem
1
for module lattices in growing dimensions is complicated by the additional arithmetic complexity of the objects. In addition, in this setting, one ideally also wants to understand the behavior when increasing the degree of the number field
K
, leading to further increasing complexity. In this article, we obtain results analogous to the above theorem for many families of module lattices. For example, we obtain:

Theorem 3
.

Let
t\geq 11
be fixed.
Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})
be a cyclotomic number field and let
n=t\cdot\deg K=t\varphi(k)
. Let
\omega_{K}=\card\mu(K)
which is
k
or
2k
depending on whether
k
is even or odd. Consider a Haar-random
unit covolume module lattice
\Lambda\subset K^{t}\otimes\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the moduli space of rank-
t
module lattices over
K
.
Then, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have with probability
1-o(1)
that

(3)

1-\frac{\log\log\omega_{K}}{n}\leq\omega_{K}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)}{\gamma(n)}\leq 1+\frac{\log\log\omega_{K}}{n}.

The
t\geq 11
condition is probably not tight and appears for technical reasons. Improvements to our methods might further reduce this lower bound to values like 3,4,5 (we need
t>2
for two moments to exist). Note that in the statement above the factor
\omega_{K}^{\frac{1}{d}}\simeq 1+\tfrac{\log k}{t\varphi(k)}
derives from the additional symmetry of module lattices and that the lower bound is valid more generally for
t\geq 2
(see Remark
29
).

What is more, combining our methods with some computation, one can go beyond asymptotic results and we show how to obtain honest probabilistic bounds on the shortest vector for many number fields and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-ranks that underlie the security of currently implemented schemes. See Example
30
for a case study that required minimal computation and figure
1
for a host of additional error bounds. We emphasize that these methods produce actual theorems and not just heuristic results.

The sequence of number fields chosen in Theorem
3
can also be changed as long as one has uniform bounds on the absolute Weil height of algebraic numbers. This is always true for abelian extensions such as cyclotomic fields, as well as a plethora of other examples and is referred to in the mathematical community as the
Bogomolov property
for the union of all the fields considered, see e.g.
[
12
, Chapter 11]
for some results along those lines.

The random module lattices in question are studied via a Rogers integration formula
[
13
,
14
,
1
]
, leading to moment estimates for the number of non-trivial lattice points of bounded Euclidean length as a random variable over the probability space of Haar-random lattices. Such results are interesting in their own right, and the first three authors in
[
1
]
derived asymptotic formulas for the moments under the height assumptions mentioned above.

This article, then, has two major objectives: first, we sharpen the second moment results of
[
1
]
in Section
3
with a particular focus on cyclotomic fields, aiming for a slightly more accessible exposition. For example, the minimal rank condition is improved from
t\geq 27
in
[
1
]
to
t\geq 11
as appearing in Theorems
3
and
25
. This is mainly achieved by bounding more carefully the contribution to error terms of algebraic numbers
\alpha\in K^{\times}
of low Weil height, using specific knowledge at our disposal for cyclotomic fields (see e.g., Theorem
21
and Proposition
24
).

Second, we show how to derive shortest vector bounds from the moment results in Section
4
and carry out a prototypical computation for a particular number field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})
and blocksize
256
in Section
5
to showcase how our results apply to such a concrete situation. See also figure
1
for bounds for other cyclotomic fields and ranks.

Finally, we highlight a connection to coding theory: in a companion paper
[
15
]
, it is shown that moments for
q
-ary type lattices obtained by lifting algebraic codes from finite fields to
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{t}
(see Construction A in
[
16
]
) converge for large enough parameters to the moments for the full space of module lattices. This crucially implies that results such as Theorem
3
also hold for uniformly random lattices in large enough discretized sets of this form.

2.
Random module lattices

We first fix some notations used throughout the text. We shall denote by
K
a number field (a finite field extension of the rational numbers), by
\deg(K)=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
or simply
d
its field degree over the rationals and by
\mathcal{O}_{K}
its ring of integers. The main example to keep in mind throughout the text is that of a cyclotomic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
obtained by adjoining a primitive
m
-th root of unity
\zeta_{m}=e^{2\pi i/m}
to
\mathbb{Q}
. In this case the degree
\deg(K)=\varphi(m)
is given by Euler’s totient function and
\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m}]
. The invertible elements under multiplication in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
.

2.1.
Embeddings and unit structure

Any number field
K
has
\deg(K)
distinct field embeddings
\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C}
which can be grouped into
r_{1}
embeddings whose image is contained in
\mathbb{R}
and
r_{2}
pairs of complex conjugate embeddings. The pair
(r_{1},r_{2})
is referred to as the signature of
K
and one has
r_{1}+2r_{2}=\deg(K)
. These embeddings naturally map our number field

i_{K}:K\hookrightarrow K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}

into a
\deg(K)
dimensional Euclidean space
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}
which we will abbreviate by
K_{\mathbb{R}}
. The image
i_{K}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
via this map is a lattice in
K_{\mathbb{R}}
, and similarly any, say, integral ideal
I\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
gives rise to a lattice. For a given positive integer
t
, we will also consider
t
copies of this construction. In this way, for instance any tuple
(I_{1},\ldots,I_{t})
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-ideals gives rise to a lattice in
t\cdot\deg(K)
-dimensional Euclidean space
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
. We shall study the behaviour of short vectors in such lattices.

To that end, the structure of the unit group
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
will play a crucial role. Recall that Dirichlet’s unit theorem states that this is a free abelian group of rank
r(K)=r_{1}+r_{2}-1
, so that, denoting by
\mu(K)
the roots of unity in
K
, we have the identification as groups:

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\cong\mu(K)\times\mathbb{Z}^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}.

We shall denote the size of the torsion in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
by
\omega_{K}=\card\mu(K)
. Note that for
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
and
m\neq 2
, all the embeddings are complex, so that
\mathbb{Z}(\zeta_{m})^{\times}
has rank
\varphi(m)/2-1
and the number of roots of unity is
\omega_{K}=2m
if
m
is odd and
m
otherwise.

2.2.
The space of module lattices and components

Our goal is to study random
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules of rank
t>0
inside Euclidean space
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
. Given that the questions we consider are invariant under scaling of the lattices, it is preferable and customary to fix the determinant of the lattice and work with spaces of lattices of unit covolume. We take this point of view for the rest of the paper.
Similarly to the setup of
[
17
]
we then define:

Definition 4
.

A module lattice in
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
is a pair

(g,M)
where
g\in\GL_{t}(K\otimes\mathbb{R})
and
M\subseteq K^{t}
is a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module of maximal rank so that

(4)

\vol\left(K^{t}\otimes\mathbb{R}/(g^{-1}\cdot M)\right)=1.

Two module lattices are considered equal if the lattice
g^{-1}M\subseteq K^{t}\otimes\mathbb{R}
is identical.

A module lattice is called rational, or simply an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module, if it is equal to
(1,M)
for some
M\subseteq K^{n}
.
When not ambiguous, we may refer to
M
as the module lattice
(1,M)
. The set of all module lattices of rank
t
over
K
shall be denoted by
\ML_{t}(K)
.

Module lattices inherit a natural action of
\GL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})
via multiplication on the left:

(5)

h:(g,M)\mapsto(|\N(\det h)|^{-\frac{1}{t\deg K}}hg,M).

This descends to an action of
\GL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Furthermore, each space of module lattices comes equipped with a map to the class group
\cl(K)
of
K
which picks up the so-called Steinitz class of
(g,M)\in\ML_{t}(K)
: the ideal class of the fractional ideal generated by all the determinants of
t
-tuples of vectors in
M
. It is not hard to see that the space
\ML_{t}(K)
consists then of one connected component for each Steinitz class on
\cl(K)
and that each connected component looks like
\GL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\Gamma\cdot\mathbb{R}_{>0}
for some arithmetic group
\Gamma
.

This produces a natural probability measure on
\ML_{t}(K)
, since each of the components carry a Haar measure as a homogeneous space of
\GL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\mathbb{R}_{>0}
which is unique if we prescribe the total mass. Note also that for
t\geq 2
,
\ML_{t}(K)
is a non-compact probability space, whereas for
t=1
, this is the space of ideal lattices which is compact due to the finiteness of the class group.

Remark 5
.

One could take an adelic point of view as in
[
17
]
and see
\ML_{t}(K)
as an adelic quotient.
In this way,
\ML_{t}(K)
really is seen to carry a Haar measure as a homogeneous space of a locally compact group.

2.3.
Lattices from codes and reduction to principal component

Our main object of study in this paper will be, for a fixed origin-centered ball
B
of volume
V
in
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
, the random variable

(6)

\rho_{V}(\Lambda):=\card\left(B\cap\left(\Lambda\setminus\{0\}\right)\right)

where
\Lambda
is a random unit covolume module lattice over
K
in
\deg(K)\cdot t
-dimensional Euclidean space. There are several candidate spaces of such random lattices which one might potentially be interested in:

(1)

The full space
\ML_{t}(K)
as defined above.

(2)

The principal component, whose study reduces to the space

\SL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\SL_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{K}),

which carries a canonical Haar probability measure.

(3)

The discrete sets of lattices which, for projections
\pi_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{O}_{K}\to\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathcal{P}=k_{\mathcal{P}}
, are obtained by lifting algebraic codes over the residue field
k_{\mathcal{P}}
as follows:

(7)

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)=\{\beta\pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}(S)\mid S\subseteq k_{\mathcal{P}}^{t}\text{ is an $s$-dimensional $k_{\mathcal{P}}$-subspace}\},

where
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
is an unramified prime ideal,
1\leq s\leq t-1
and where the scaling factor

\beta=\beta(\mathcal{P},s)=\N(\mathcal{P})^{-\left(1-\frac{s}{t}\right)\frac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}}

ensures the lattice
\beta\pi_{\mathcal{P}}^{-1}(S)
has the same covolume as
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{t}
(and we may normalize so that all of these are one). In particular, as the norm of
\mathcal{P}
and thus the residue field cardinality
\card k_{\mathcal{P}}
increase, the sets
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)
are increasingly large discrete subsets of
\ML_{t}(K)
.

The first two agree (up to a compact torus) when
K
has class number one, but in general the second is slightly simpler to work with. The lattices
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)
lie on the same connected component of Steinitz class as the ideal class of
\mathcal{P}^{t-s}
, whereas the ideal classes of primes
\mathcal{P}
are known to equidistribute in the class group as the norm
\N(\mathcal{P})\to\infty
. We record the following useful remark:

Remark 6
.

It suffices to compute the moments of the random variable
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
for random lattices
\Lambda\in\SL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\SL_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
.

Indeed, the behavior is the same for each component of
\ML_{t}(K)
(see e.g.
[
1
, Remark 7]
). Moreover, we show in
[
15
]
that, under some mild conditions on
s
, the moments over the discrete spaces
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)
converge to the moments for
SL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\SL_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
as
\N(\mathcal{P})\to\infty
.

Henceforth, we therefore focus on evaluating moments for
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
in the principal case, but highlight the fact that this implies such results therefore hold also for the full space of module lattices as well as for lattices in
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{P},s)
for large norm of
\mathcal{P}
.

3.
Second moment results

In this section, we show how to obtain via the the approach devised in
[
1
]
improved second moment results for the number of short vectors of bounded Euclidean norm. Our improvements are primarily focused on lattices over cyclotomic fields, although the methods apply more widely.

3.1.
Additional symmetry and moments

Fixing an origin-centered ball
B
of volume
V
, we recall our random variable on the space of lattices of covolume one
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
defined in (
6
) and counting non-trivial lattice vectors in
B
. C.L. Siegel’s classical mean value theorem
[
18
]
implies in particular that the expected value satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)]=V

for unstructured lattices, and it is not hard to show that the same holds for module lattices (see e.g.,
[
19
, Lemma 1]
). C.A. Rogers then developed an integral formula expressing the higher moments of
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
in terms of a rather involved sum over various integral contributions from linear subspaces
[
20
]
. He then proceeded to estimate all of the contributions in the integral formula and showed:

Theorem 7

(
[
21
]
)
.

The
k
-th moment of
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
for random
\Lambda\in\SL_{t}(\mathbb{R})/\SL_{t}(\mathbb{Z})
satisfies, provided
t\geq\lceil k^{2}/4+3\rceil
, the bound

0\leq\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{k}]-2^{k}\cdot m_{k}(\tfrac{V}{2})\leq V\cdot E_{k,t},

where

(8)

m_{k}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda^{r}}{r!}r^{k}=\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mathcal{P}(\lambda)}(X^{k})

is the
k
-th moment of a Poisson distribution and the error term
E_{k,t}
decays exponentially in
t
.

In other words, noting that short vectors come in pairs due to the imposed symmetry under
\mathbb{Z}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
, Rogers showed that the number of pairs of short non-zero vectors in
B
approaches a Poisson distribution of mean
\tfrac{V}{2}
.

The crucial difference for lattices
\Lambda\in\ML_{t}(K)
is that units
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
act diagonally (with respect to the
t
copies of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
) on lattice vectors. In particular, roots of unity in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
lie on the unit circle in each complex embedding and thus their action preserves Euclidean distances, leading to the important observation:

Remark 8
.

For
\Lambda\in\ML_{t}(K)
, the random value
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
is a multiple of the number of roots of unity
\omega_{K}
.

Therefore, the natural object to study is
\omega_{K}
-tuples of vectors in
B\cap\Lambda
. In light of both the mean value results and Theorem
7
, one might therefore expect the number of
\omega_{K}
-tuples to converge to a Poisson distribution of mean
\tfrac{V}{\omega_{K}}
as the dimension increases. This was indeed shown in
[
1
]
in quite some generality. In this case, the analogues of Rogers’ integral formula
[
13
,
14
,
1
]
are still available, but the technical obstacles is in estimating the contributions of the various subspaces. Roughly, the additional unit action’s impact can be seen in
\omega_{K}
replacing the role of
2
in the main moment terms, but the presence of typically infinitely many non-torsion units in
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
makes the error terms considerably more delicate to estimate.

For the rest of this section, we give an exposition of a sharpening of the techniques in
[
1
]
in the second moment case. Our goal is therefore to show that, for
\Lambda\in\SL_{t}(K_{\mathbb{R}})/\SL_{t}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
and
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-rank
t
large enough, we have the bounds

(9)

0\leq\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{2}]-(V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V)\leq V\cdot E_{K,t},

for some error term
E_{K,t}
that decays rapidly as the lattice dimension increases.

3.2.
Integral formula and heights

The Rogers integral formula for module lattices implies for a fixed origin-centered ball
B
in
t
copies of Euclidean space
K\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
that the second moment equals:

\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{2}]=\vol(B)^{2}+\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}D(\alpha)^{-t}\cdot\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B),

where
D(\alpha)=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{K}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}]
is a lattice index measuring the denominator of
\alpha\in K^{\times}
and where the scaling
\alpha^{-1}B
occurs in
K_{\mathbb{R}}
. In other words,
\alpha^{-1}
multiplies the unit ball by
\sigma(\alpha^{-1})
for the coordinate of
K_{\mathbb{R}}
corresponding to the field embedding
\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C}
.

Since every
\alpha\in\mu(K)
contributes exactly
\vol(B)
to the sum, our task in achieving (
9
) reduces to estimating the alleged error term:

(10)

\sum_{\alpha\in(K^{\times}\setminus\mu(K))/\mu(K)}D(\alpha)^{-t}\cdot\frac{\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}.

Suppose now that we are simply summing over units
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
, so that we must estimate
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}/\mu(K)}\frac{\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}
. The body
B\cap\alpha^{-1}B
intuitively should be comparable to the ellipsoid where for every embedding
\sigma:K\to\mathbb{C}
the length of the semi-axis in that direction is the radius of
B
divided by
\max(1,|\sigma(\alpha)|)
. This naturally leads one to consider the notion of Weil height, a measure of the complexity of an algebraic number, and we set:

H_{\infty}(\alpha):=\prod_{\sigma:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}\max\{1,|\sigma(\alpha)|\}.

This is closely related to the notion in the literature of (unnormalised) exponential Weil height of an algebraic number
\alpha\in K^{\times}
given by the product over the set of places
M_{K}
of
K
:

(11)

H_{W}(\alpha):=\prod_{v\in M_{K}}\max\{1,|\alpha|_{v}\}.

It is easily seen that
H_{W}(\alpha)=H_{\infty}(\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
and in general the two differ by a denominator.
It is customary to work with
absolute
notions of height which do not depend on the particular subfield one is viewing an algebraic number in and to take logarithms. Writing
\deg(\alpha)=[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]
we arrive at the
Weil height

h(\alpha)=\ln\left(H_{W}(\alpha)^{1/\deg(\alpha)}\right)

of an algebraic number. For non-integers, we write
h_{\infty}(\alpha)
for
\ln\left(H_{\infty}(\alpha)^{1/\deg(\alpha)}\right)
. It follows from these definitions that we have the relation

(12)

h(\alpha)=h_{\infty}(\alpha)+\tfrac{1}{\deg(K)}\cdot\ln(D(\alpha)),

where recall that we write
D(\alpha)=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{K}\cap\mathcal{O}_{K}]
for the contribution from denominators at non-Archimedean places.

We intuitively expect to relate
\frac{\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}S)}{\vol(B)}
to
h_{\infty}(\alpha)
. Denoting henceforth by
\N(\alpha)
the norm of the fractional ideal generated by
\alpha
, we indeed have the following result (see
[
1
, Lemma 35]
for the proof of a more general statement, which draws on the arithmetic/geometric mean inequality):

Lemma 9
.

Let
\alpha\in K^{\times}
. We have the bound:

\frac{\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}\leq\left(\frac{e^{2h_{\infty}(\alpha)}+e^{-2h_{\infty}(\alpha)}\cdot\N(\alpha)^{\frac{2}{d}}}{2}\right)^{-dt/2}

and moreover, under the assumption that
\N(\alpha)\geq 1
, we have for any
k\geq 2
:

\frac{\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}\leq\N(\alpha)^{\frac{-t}{k}}\cdot\left(\frac{e^{2h_{\infty}(\alpha)\cdot(k-1)/k}+e^{-2h_{\infty}(\alpha)\cdot(k-1)/k}\cdot\N(\alpha)^{\frac{2}{d}}}{2}\right)^{-dt/2}.

In light of this result, our moment estimates will be related to height lower bounds for the number fields involved. Fortunately, this is a well-studied subject. For instance, Lehmer’s famous problem asks for a
uniform
lower bound for
h(\alpha)\deg(\alpha)
for all algebraic numbers which are not roots of unity (the latter are easily seen to have trivial height). At any rate, for a fixed number field, we may always find:

(13)

0<c(K)<\inf_{\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\mu(K)}h(\alpha).

Indeed, for instance P. Voutier
[
22
]
, building on work of Dobrowolski
[
23
]
, showed that any

(14)

c(K)<\frac{1}{4\deg(K)}\cdot\left(\frac{\ln\ln\deg(K)}{\ln\deg(K)}\right)^{3}

satisfies (
13
).

3.3.
Unit counts and unit contribution

One of the difficulties in this setting is dealing with contributions from infinite unit groups. The next key technical ingredient is an upper bound for the number of units of bounded height:

Lemma 10
.

Let
S_{K}
denote a fixed, finite subset of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
that is closed under inversion. Let
c_{S}(K)<\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\setminus S_{K}}h_{\infty}(\alpha)
be a positive height lower bound valid outside of
S_{K}
.
Consider the canonical log embedding:

L:K^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
defined by mapping

\alpha\mapsto(\ln|\sigma_{1}(\alpha)|,\ldots,2\ln|\sigma_{r_{1}+r_{2}}(\alpha)|),

as well as the function

(15)

\displaystyle h:\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

(16)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\tfrac{1}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\max(0,x_{j}).

Then for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
with
\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\eta_{j}\geq 0
and any
X\geq 0
we have that

\card\{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}~|~h(\eta+L(\beta))\leq X\}\leq\#S_{K}\cdot\left(\frac{X+c_{S}(K)/2}{c_{S}(K)/2}\right)^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}.

Proof.

Note that the product formula implies that
L(\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times})
is contained in the hyperplane
H:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}:\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}x_{j}=0\}
. Furthermore observe that
h
satisfies the properties of a semi-norm on
H
.
By assumption, for any
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\setminus S_{K}
we have that

h(L(\beta))=h_{\infty}(\beta)>c_{S}(K).

Let now
P=\{\xi\in H:h(\xi)\leq c_{S}(K)/2\}
. We claim that for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
:

(L(\beta_{1})+\eta+P)\cap(L(\beta_{2})+\eta+P)=\emptyset\text{ unless }\beta_{1}\beta_{2}^{-1}\in S_{K}

To prove the claim, let
y
be in the intersection. Then by the triangle inequality

h(L(\beta_{1}^{-1}\beta_{2}))=h(L(\beta_{2})-L(\beta_{1}))\leq h(L(\beta_{2})+\eta-y)+h(-L(\beta_{1})-\eta+y).

Given that
L(\beta_{2})+\eta-y\in P
and
-L(\beta_{1})-\eta+y\in-P
we deduce that
h(L(\beta_{1}^{-1}\beta_{2}))\leq c_{s}(K)
and therefore
\beta_{1}\beta_{2}^{-1}\in S_{K}
, proving the claim.

Moreover, any
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
satisfying
h(\eta+L(\beta))\leq X
implies that
L(\beta)+\eta+P
is contained in the set

Q=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}:\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\xi_{j}=Y_{\eta},h(\xi)\leq X+c_{S}(K)/2\},

where
Y_{\eta}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\eta_{j}
. For any fixed
\eta
, we thus obtain by the claim that

\card\left\{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|h(\eta+L(\beta))\leq X\right\}\leq\#S_{K}\cdot\frac{\vol(Q)}{\vol(P)},

where the volumes are computed with respect to the natural measure identifying the hyperspaces
P
and
Q
are in with
\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}
.

For
\eta
such that
Y_{\eta}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\eta_{j}\geq 0
, it is easy to see that the volume of
Q
is bounded by the volume of
P_{X}=\{\xi\in H:h(\xi)\leq c_{S}(K)/2+X\}
.
Thus we bound the desired unit count by

\#S_{K}\cdot\frac{\vol(P_{X})}{\vol(P)}=\#S_{K}\cdot\frac{\vol(\{\xi\in H:h(\xi)\leq c_{S}(K)/2+X\})}{\vol(\{\xi\in H:h(\xi)\leq c_{S}(K)/2\})}.

Since
H
is an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of dimension
r_{1}+r_{2}-1
and
h
a semi-norm on that vector space, the result follows.

∎

Remark 11
.

This is a slight generalization of
[
1
, Lemma 37]
which establishes the result above for
S_{K}=\mu(K)
the group of roots of unity in
K
. Allowing a slightly larger
S_{K}
will allow to increase
c_{S}(K)
and lead to better bounds. Note also that we must have
\mu(K)\subset S_{K}
to have a positive height bound outside
S_{K}
.

In summary, we will prove our results for the following setup, fixing notations:

Setup 12
.

Given a number field
K
, and a choice of finite subset
S_{K}
closed under inversion and satisfying
\mu(K)\subseteq S_{K}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
,
there exists a choice of constants

0<c(K)\leq c_{o}(K)\leq c_{S}(K)

satisfying:

\displaystyle c(K)<\inf_{\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\mu(K)}h(\alpha)

\displaystyle c_{o}(K)<\inf_{h_{\infty}(\alpha)>0}h(\alpha)

\displaystyle c_{S}(K)<\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\setminus S_{K}}h(\alpha).

It follows from our previous remarks that any fixed number field satisfies such a setup:

Remark 13
.

For any fixed number field
K
, the choice of
S_{K}=\mu(K)
, as well as
c_{S}(K)=c_{o}(K)=c(K)>0
small enough as in (
13
) satisfies Setup
12
.

However, for many number fields such as cyclotomic fields, making these distinctions will allow us to prove better bounds. Moreover, one can in many instances choose the constants uniformly for a family of number fields
K
.

We now turn to our main results in estimating the second moment. We note that there are two points of view that one may be interested in: first, we tailor our results to the perspective of varying the number fields involved, and aim to achieve uniform control of the moments throughout the family for
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-rank as small as possible. Second, for fixed number field and fixed (or increasing) rank the results can be simplified-this will be discussed in Section
5
.

Proposition 14
.

Let a number field
K
and let bounds as in setup
12
together with its notations be given. For any
k\geq 2
there exist positive constants
C,\varepsilon_{1}>0
uniformly bounded in
t
such that for all
\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\mu(K)
with
N(\alpha)\geq 1
the following holds: write

t_{0}=\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\frac{\ln(1+\frac{2c_{o}(K)}{c_{S}(K)}+\tfrac{1}{k^{3}})}{\ln(\cosh(2\cdot c_{o}(K)\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k}))},

where
r_{K}
is the rank of the unit group of
K
.
Then we have for any
t>t_{0}
and any
d\geq 1
that

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
\alpha\beta\notin\mu(K)\end{subarray}}\frac{\vol(B\cap(\alpha\beta)^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}\leq C\cdot\card S_{K}\cdot\N(\alpha)^{\frac{-t}{k}}\cdot D(\alpha)^{\frac{t}{2}}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon_{1}\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}.

Moreover, the constants can be made explicit. We may for instance take

\varepsilon_{1}=\tfrac{5\cdot c_{o}(K)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{6\cdot k^{2}}

and
C=1+k^{3}+\tfrac{1}{1-e^{-5\cdot c_{o}(K)^{2}\cdot d(t-t_{0})(k-1)^{2}/(3k^{5})}}
.

Proof.

The proof consists of using Lemma
10
to bound the number of units in a given height range, which grows exponentially in
d
, and compare it with the forced decay due to height (and denominator) from our geometric Lemma
9
, the latter decay being exponential in
d\cdot t
. This yields a minimal rank
t_{0}
for which when
t>t_{0}
our sum over the unit group can be bounded by a geometric sum. We deal with different height ranges separately in order to make the minimal rank as small as possible.

More precisely, by Lemma
9
our task is reduced to bounding for suitably chosen
k\geq 2
:

(18)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
\alpha\beta\notin\mu(K)\end{subarray}}\N(\alpha)^{\frac{-t}{k}}\cdot\cosh\left(h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\left(2(1-\tfrac{1}{k})\right)\right)^{-dt/2}.

Since
\cosh(x)
is increasing for
x>0
it suffices to bound, for any
A\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
the sum

\Sigma_{k}^{\infty}=\sum_{m=A}^{\infty}\card\left\{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\mid h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\in\Big[\tfrac{mc_{S}(K)}{A},\tfrac{(2m+1)c_{S}(K)}{2A}\Big[~\right\}\cdot\cosh\left(\frac{2mc_{S}(K)}{Ak/(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{-dt}{2}}

together with the contribution of the remaining units satisfying
h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)<c_{S}(K)
:

(19)

\Sigma_{k}^{c_{S}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)<c_{S}(K)~\end{subarray}}\cosh\left(h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\cdot 2(1-\tfrac{1}{k})\right)^{-dt/2}.

Let us examine the term
\Sigma_{k}^{c_{S}}
first. For points of very small height we can show (using the triangle inequality for heights as in Lemma
10
) that

(20)

\card\{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\leq c_{S}(K)/2\}\leq\card S_{K}.

Moreover, by assumption, since
\alpha\beta\notin\mu(K)
we have the height bound

(21)

h(\alpha\beta)=h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)+\tfrac{1}{d}\cdot\ln D(\alpha)\geq c(K)>0.

The terms of very small height therefore satisfy:

(22)

\displaystyle D(\alpha)^{-\frac{t}{2}}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)<c_{S}(K)/2~\end{subarray}}\cosh\left(h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\cdot 2(1-\tfrac{1}{k})\right)^{-dt/2}

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\card S_{K}\cdot\exp\left(-dt\cdot\min\left(\tfrac{c(K)}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\ln(\cosh(2c(K)(\tfrac{k-1}{k}))\right)\right)

and are controlled for any rank
t
when
\card S_{K}
grows polynomially in
d
.
We now turn to the contribution of terms with
h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\in[c_{S}(K)/2,c_{S}(K)]
which we partition into
A
intervals
I_{i}=[\tfrac{c_{S}(K)(1+i/A)}{2},\tfrac{c_{S}(K)(1+(i+1)/A)}{2}]
for
i=0,\ldots,A-1
.
By Lemma
10
we may bound

(24)

\card\{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\in I_{i}\}\leq\card S_{K}\cdot\left(2+\tfrac{i+1}{A}\right)^{r_{K}}.

Recall that in our setup for
h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\in I_{i}
we have a combined bound on the height at Archimedean places and the denominators:

h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)+\tfrac{1}{d}\cdot\ln D(\alpha)\geq c_{o}(K).

Therefore, we may estimate the contribution on each interval by

(25)

\displaystyle D(\alpha)^{-\frac{t}{2}}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\in I_{i}~\end{subarray}}\cosh\left(h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\cdot 2(1-\tfrac{1}{k})\right)^{-dt/2}

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\card S_{K}\cdot\left(2+\tfrac{i+1}{A}\right)^{r_{K}}\cdot\exp\left(-dt\cdot\left(D_{sav}+\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\ln(\cosh(\tfrac{c_{S}(K)(A+i)(k-1)}{Ak}))\right)\right),

where we wrote for savings from denominator terms

D_{sav}=\max\left(0,\tfrac{c_{o}(K)-c_{S}(K)(\frac{A+1+i}{2A})}{2}\right).

A small calculation shows that largest contribution comes from the interval with
c_{o}(K)\in I_{i}
, as it is the smallest
i
with
D_{sav}=0
. We therefore arrive at the minimal
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-rank condition of:

t_{0}\geq\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\frac{\ln(1+\frac{2c_{o}(K)}{c_{S}(K)}+\tfrac{1}{A})}{\ln(\cosh(2\cdot c_{o}(K)\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k}))}.

It now follows that for
t>t_{0}
the sum
D(\alpha)^{-\frac{t}{2}}\cdot\Sigma_{k}^{c_{S}}
is bounded as claimed in the proposition.

Finally, we turn to bounding the tail
\Sigma_{k}^{\infty}
. Lemma
10
with height bound
X=\tfrac{(2m+1)\cdot c_{S}(K)}{2A}
yields:

(27)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{k}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\card S_{K}\cdot\sum_{m=A}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{2m+A+1}{A}\right)^{r_{K}}\cdot\cosh(\tfrac{2m\cdot(k-1)}{A\cdot k}\cdot c_{S}(K))^{-dt/2}

(28)

\displaystyle=\card S_{K}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\tfrac{2n-1}{A}+3\right)^{r_{K}}\cdot\cosh(\tfrac{A+n-1}{A}\cdot 2c_{S}(K)\cdot(1-\tfrac{1}{k}))^{-dt/2}

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\card S_{K}\cdot\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\cosh(\tfrac{A+n-1}{A}\cdot 2c_{S}(K)\cdot(1-\tfrac{1}{k}))^{\frac{-d(t-t_{0})}{2}}

where we chose

t_{0}\geq\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}}\frac{\ln(\frac{2n-1}{A}+3)}{\ln\left(\cosh(\frac{A+n-1}{A}\cdot 2\cdot c_{S}(K)\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k})\right)}

for suitable
k
. The logarithm ratio decays as
n
increases and therefore it suffices to take

t_{0}\geq\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\frac{\ln(\frac{1}{A}+3)}{\ln(\cosh(2\cdot c_{S}(K)\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k}))}.

The sum in (
29
) can now be bounded by a geometric series, for instance we may bound
\ln(\cosh(x))\geq\frac{5}{12}\min(x,x^{2})
. Evaluating the geometric series and some bookkeeping yields the explicit constants above (we chose to present the results for the choice of
A=k^{3}
).

∎

3.4.
Summing contributions over ideals

Having obtained in proposition
14
bounds for the second moment error terms for cosets modulo algebraic units, we now upgrade these sums to all contributions over
K^{\times}
by summing over (principal) ideals. We utilize in particular the summation formula proved more generally in
[
1
, Proposition 42]
:

Proposition 15
.

Let
\alpha\in K^{\times}
. For any function

f:K^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
and any
T\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
, the sum

\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}}D(\alpha)^{-T}\cdot\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)\cdot f(\alpha)

equals:

\zeta_{K}(T)^{-1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathcal{I}\text{integral ideal}\end{subarray}}\N(\mathcal{I})^{-T}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{I}^{-1}\setminus\{0\}}\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)\cdot f(\alpha).

Here,
\zeta_{K}(s)
denotes the Dedekind zeta function of
K
. With this in hand, we obtain our main moment error term estimate:

Proposition 16
.

Let a number field
K
and let bounds as in setup
12
together with its notations be given. For any
k\geq 3
there exist positive constants
C,\varepsilon_{1}>0
uniformly bounded in
t
such that the following holds: write

t_{0}=\max\left\{k,6,\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\frac{\ln(1+\frac{2c_{o}(K)}{c_{S}(K)}+\tfrac{1}{k^{3}})}{\ln(\cosh(2\cdot c_{o}(K)\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k}))}\right\},

where
r_{K}
is the rank of the unit group. For any
t>t_{0}
we then have:

\sum_{\alpha\in K^{\times}\setminus\mu(K)}D(\alpha)^{-t}\frac{\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}\leq C\card S_{K}\cdot\frac{\zeta_{K}(t(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}))\cdot\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{k})}{\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{2})}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}.

We may moreover take

\varepsilon=\tfrac{5\cdot c_{o}(K)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{6\cdot k^{2}}

and
C=2\left(1+k^{3}+\tfrac{1}{1-e^{-5\cdot c_{o}(K)^{2}\cdot d(t-t_{0})(k-1)^{2}/(3k^{5})}}\right)
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the quantity
D(\alpha)^{-t}\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)
is invariant under inversion
\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}
, so that we may just bound by twice the sum under the additional assumption
\N(\alpha)\geq 1
. Proposition
14
(constants
C,\varepsilon_{1}
as in
14
), reduces our task to estimating the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in K^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
\N(\alpha)\geq 1\end{subarray}}C\cdot D(\alpha)^{-t}\N(\alpha)^{\frac{-t}{k}}\cdot D(\alpha)^{\frac{t}{2}}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon_{1}\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}.

By Proposition
15
with
T=\tfrac{t}{2}
, it therefore suffices to bound

\zeta_{K}(\tfrac{t}{2})^{-1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathcal{I}\text{ integral ideal}\end{subarray}}\N(\mathcal{I})^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in(\mathcal{I}^{-1}\setminus\{0\})/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
\\
\N(\alpha)\geq 1\end{subarray}}\N(\alpha)^{\frac{-t}{k}}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon_{1}\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}.

Now observe that the map
\alpha\mapsto(\alpha)\cdot\mathcal{I}
gives a bijection between
(\mathcal{I}^{-1}\setminus\{0\})/\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
and integral ideals
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
in the ideal class of
\mathcal{I}
. We may therefore bound this expression by:

\zeta_{K}(\tfrac{t}{2})^{-1}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\mathcal{I}\text{ integral ideal}\end{subarray}}\N(\mathcal{I})^{-\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}\\
\N(\mathcal{I})\leq\N(\mathcal{J})\end{subarray}}\N(\mathcal{J}\mathcal{I}^{-1})^{-\frac{t}{k}}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon_{1}\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}

and therefore as claimed by

\frac{\zeta_{K}(t(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k}))\cdot\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{k})}{\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{2})}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon_{1}\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}.

The result follows (with some bookkeeping of the claimed constants) by noting that Dedekind zeta functions converge for real values
s>1
.

∎

We may therefore put everything together and obtain the second moment result in its abstract form for an arbitrary number field:

Theorem 17
.

Let a number field
K
and let bounds as in setup
12
together with its notations be given. For any
k\geq 3
there exist positive constants
C,\varepsilon_{1}>0
uniformly bounded in
t
such that the following holds: write

t_{0}=\max\left\{k,6,\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\frac{\ln(1+\frac{2c_{o}(K)}{c_{S}(K)}+\tfrac{1}{k^{3}})}{\ln(\cosh(2\cdot c_{o}(K)\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k}))}\right\},

where
r_{K}
is the rank of the unit group. For any
t>t_{0}
we then have that the second moment
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{2}]
of the number of nonzero lattice points in a fixed origin-centered ball of volume
V
in
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
for a Haar-random module lattice of rank
t
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
satisfies:

(30)

\displaystyle V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{2}]

(31)

\displaystyle\leq V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V+\omega_{K}\cdot S_{K}\cdot Z(K,t,k)\cdot e^{-\varepsilon\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}\cdot V,

where
0\leq Z(K,t,k)\leq\zeta_{K}\big(t(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k})\big)\cdot\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{k})\cdot\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{2})^{-1}
.

We may moreover take

\varepsilon_{1}=\tfrac{5\cdot c_{o}(K)^{2}(k-1)^{2}}{6\cdot k^{2}}

and
C=2\left(1+k^{3}+\tfrac{1}{1-e^{-5\cdot c_{o}(K)^{2}\cdot d(t-t_{0})(k-1)^{2}/(3k^{5})}}\right)
.

We conclude this section with a tail bound of sorts that bounds the contribution to the error term of algebraic numbers in
K
above some height threshold. This will be useful when considering a fixed number field for which estimates on the contribution of small height algebraic numbers can be computed explicitly.

Proposition 18
.

For a number field
K
of degree
d
consider the setup
12
and its notations. Let
h_{0}\geq c_{S}(K)>0
be some threshold Weil height. Assume for simplicity
h_{0}\leq 2
and write

t_{0}=\max\left\{4,\frac{2r_{K}}{d}\cdot\frac{\ln(1+\frac{12h_{0}+1}{6c_{S}(K)})}{\ln(\cosh(\tfrac{3h_{0}}{2}))}\right\}

For any
t>t_{0}
we then have:

\sum_{h(\alpha)>h_{0}}D(\alpha)^{-t}\vol(B\cap\alpha^{-1}B)\leq C\cdot\card S_{K}\cdot\frac{\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{4})^{2}}{\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{2})}\cdot e^{-\varepsilon\cdot d\cdot(t-t_{0})}\cdot\vol(B).

We may moreover take

\varepsilon=\tfrac{1}{2}\ln(\cosh(\tfrac{3h_{0}}{2}))\text{ and }C=1+\tfrac{2}{1-e^{-5d(t-t_{0})/192}}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds along the lines of bounding sums over unit cosets as in the proof of Proposition
14
, avoiding the trickier small heights and choosing
k=4
for simplicity. By assumption, we have that

h(\alpha)=h_{\infty}(\alpha)+\tfrac{1}{d}\ln D(\alpha)\geq h_{0}.

Using our methods, the worst bound for the sum over units

\Sigma_{0}:=D(\alpha)^{-t/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\\
\alpha\beta\notin\mu(K)\end{subarray}}\frac{\vol(B\cap(\alpha\beta)^{-1}B)}{\vol(B)}

is, under the assumption
h_{0}\leq 2
, for trivial denominator
D(\alpha)
. The bound

\Sigma_{0}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\card\{\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|h_{\infty}(\alpha\beta)\in[h_{0}+\tfrac{i}{A},h_{0}+\tfrac{i+1}{A})\}\cdot\cosh(\tfrac{3}{2}\cdot(h_{0}+\tfrac{i}{A}))^{-dt/2}

therefore holds regardless and for any
A>0
. The unit counts are estimated using Lemma
10
and the resulting geometric sum evaluated as in Proposition
14
for the choice of
A=12
. Finally, we sum these bounds over principal ideals as in Proposition
16
, yielding the result.

∎

3.5.
Second moment for cyclotomic fields

We now turn to establishing the results for cyclotomic fields, when good height lower bounds are available for setup
12
.
Cyclotomic fields have complex multiplication (CM), and for all such fields, since complex conjugation
x\mapsto\overline{x}
is in the center of the Galois group, we have the following property:

Lemma 19
.

For
\alpha
in a CM field with conjugates
\alpha_{j}
, the conjugates of
|\alpha|^{2}=\alpha\overline{\alpha}
are the
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
. Moreover, if
|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=q\in\mathbb{Q}
for some
j
, the same holds for any conjugate.

In particular, this will imply that height lower bounds for totally positive or totally real integers also apply to algebraic integers in a CM field. To that end, we have the foundational result due to A. Schinzel
[
24
]
:

Theorem 20
.

Assume that an algebraic number
\alpha
of infinite multiplicative order is contained in a totally real field. Then, denoting by
\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
the golden ratio, we have

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}\ln{\varphi}\approx 0.2406\ldots.

Moreover, the same is true for
\alpha
in a CM field provided one (and equivalently, all) of its Archimedean embeddings satisfy
|\alpha|\neq 1
.

Going beyond the work of Schinzel, C.J. Smyth pioneered the study on the lowest possible heights (or, equivalently, Mahler measures) for totally positive algebraic integers, with some follow-up work by V. Flammang, among others. They show:

Theorem 21

(
[
25
,
26
]
)
.

Suppose
\alpha
is a nonzero totally positive algebraic integer whose minimal polynomial does not divide any of the following:

(33)

\displaystyle x-1

(34)

\displaystyle x^{2}-3x+1

(35)

\displaystyle x^{4}-7x^{3}+13x^{2}-7x+1

(36)

\displaystyle x^{6}-11x^{5}+41x^{4}4-63x^{3}3+41x^{2}-11x+1

(37)

\displaystyle x^{8}-15x^{7}+83x^{6}-220x^{5}+303x^{4}-220x^{3}+83x^{2}-15x+1

(38)

\displaystyle x^{8}-15x^{7}+84x^{6}-225x^{5}+311x^{4}-225x^{3}+84x^{2}-15x+1

(39)

\displaystyle x^{16}-31x^{15}+413x^{14}-3141x^{13}+15261x^{12}-50187x^{11}+115410x^{10}-189036x^{9}

(40)

\displaystyle+222621x^{8}-189036x^{7}+115410x^{6}-50187x^{5}+15261x^{4}-3141x^{3}+413x^{2}-31x+1.

We then have the height bound
h(\alpha)>0.543526
.

It follows that for totally real and CM algebraic integers (including cyclotomic ones), half the bound above, namely

h(\alpha)>0.271763

holds up to a fixed, finite list of exceptions. What is more, the result above is not far from optimal in the sense that Smyth showed that the Weil heights of totally real integers are everywhere dense in
(\ell,\infty)
for a certain
\ell=0.27328\ldots
, see
[
27
, Theorem 1]
.

Removing any integrality assumption weakens the bounds, but in any abelian extensions Amoroso and Dvornicich
[
28
]
showed:

Theorem 22
.

Assume that an algebraic number
\alpha
of infinite multiplicative order is contained in an abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

h(\alpha)\geq\frac{\ln{5}}{12}\approx 0.1341\ldots.

Remark 23
.

For cyclotomic fields, this bound was improved in
[
29
]
to
h(\alpha)\geq 0.155\ldots
and stronger bounds can be proved if the conductor avoids small primes.

We summarize the most useful results in the literature for us in the case of cyclotomic fields:

Proposition 24
.

Any cyclotomic field
K
satisfies setup
12
with the constants

c(K)=0.155\text{ and }c_{o}(K)=0.2406\text{ and }c_{S}(K)=0.271763

uniformly
in
K
, with
S_{K}
being the set of units
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
such that totally positive
u\overline{u}
has minimal polynomial listed among the exceptions in Theorem
21
. In particular, we have
\card S_{K}\leq 17\cdot\card\mu(K)
.

Proof.

Everything follows from the above discussion except the bound on
\card S_{K}
. Theorem
21
gives an exhaustive list of totally positive integers not satisfying the height bound. Of the polynomials listed, those of degree
4,16
and the first of degree
8
do not generate abelian extensions. Setting
\alpha_{m}=(2\cos(2\pi/m))^{2}
, the remaining polynomials of degree
2,6,8
are respectively the minimal polynomial of
\alpha_{5}
and the product of minimal polynomials of
\alpha_{7},\alpha_{7}^{-1}
and
\alpha_{60},\alpha_{60}^{-1}
. This gives at most
17
totally positive integers to avoid.

To count the exceptions in
K
, let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
such that
|\alpha|^{2}=|\beta|^{2}
. Then
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is an algebraic integer all of whose conjugates lie on the unit circle by Lemma
19
. Therefore
\alpha\beta^{-1}\in\mu(K)
by Kronecker’s first theorem.
∎

We therefore arrive at our main result for cyclotomic fields:

Theorem 25
.

Let
K
denote a cyclotomic field containing
\omega_{K}
roots of unity. The second moment
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{2}]
of the number of nonzero lattice points in a fixed origin-centered ball of volume
V
in
K_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}
for a Haar-random module lattice of rank
t\geq 11
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
satisfies:

(41)

\displaystyle V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)^{2}]

(42)

\displaystyle\leq V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V+\omega_{K}^{2}\cdot C\cdot Z(K,t,k)\cdot e^{-\varepsilon\cdot d\cdot(t-10.99)}\cdot V,

with constants given by

\varepsilon=\tfrac{1}{26}\text{ and }C=68\cdot\left(1332+\tfrac{1}{1-e^{-\cdot d(t-10.99)/(16693)}}\right)

and where
0\leq Z(K,t)\leq\zeta_{K}\big(\frac{9t}{22})\big)\cdot\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{10.99})\cdot\zeta_{K}(\frac{t}{2})^{-1}
.

Proof.

We apply the bounds of Proposition
24
to Theorem
17
and choose
k<t
large enough to achieve
t_{0}=11
.
∎

Remark 26
.

Theorem
25
is geared towards obtaining convergence to the second moment of a Poisson distribution of mean
V/\omega_{K}
for large dimension without having to take the
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-rank
t
large. Should one instead be interested in a fixed cyclotomic field and increasing solely the rank, one can typically arrive at slightly better height bounds than proposition
24
, see Section
5
. One can also obtain a smaller constant
C
if one is willing to compromise on the minimal rank required.

4.
Shortest vector bounds

Having relatively tight bounds for the second moment in hand, the following result will allow us to estimate the shortest vector. The result is most naturally stated in terms of the volume minimum
\mathcal{V}_{1}(\Lambda)=\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)^{n}\cdot\vol(\mathbb{B}_{n}(1))
of a random lattice, where
\vol(\mathbb{B}_{n}(1))
is the volume of an
n
-dimensional unit ball:

Proposition 27
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
d
with
\omega_{K}=\#\mu(K)
roots of unity and let
\Lambda\in\ML_{t}(K)
be a Haar-random lattice in dimension
n=dt
. Then for any
\varepsilon(n)<1
the first volume minimum
\mathcal{V}_{1}(\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
satisfies

\omega_{K}\cdot\varepsilon(n)\leq\mathcal{V}_{1}(\Lambda)\leq\omega_{K}\cdot\varepsilon(n)^{-1}

with probability greater than
1-\varepsilon(n)\cdot(2+\omega_{K}\cdot\Err(K,t))
provided that the error term
\Err(K,t)
is such that

\mathbb{E}[\#(\Lambda\cap B\setminus\{0\})^{2}]\leq V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V\cdot(1+\Err(K,t))

for any ball
B
of volume
V
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\in\ML_{t}(K)
be a Haar-random lattice and write
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)=\card(\Lambda\cap B\setminus\{0\})
.
Note that because of (generalizations of) Siegel’s mean value theorem, we have that
\mathbb{E}(\rho_{V}(\Lambda))=V
. Moreover we know that
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)\in\omega_{K}\cdot\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
because the roots of unity in
K
act on lattice vectors preserving lengths.

Let
m\geq 1
be arbitrary. For
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, observe that the following inequality holds concerning the indicator function of
x=0
:

(44)

1-x\leq\mathbf{1}(x=0)\leq{m}^{-2}(x-m)^{2}.

We now set
x=\rho_{V}(\Lambda)/k
and evaluate expected values.
Using the left side of
44

we get that
1-\tfrac{V}{\omega_{K}}\leq\mathbb{P}(x=0)
. Setting
V=\omega_{K}\cdot\varepsilon(n)
yields that with a probability of at least
1-\varepsilon(n)
, the random variable
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)=0
and therefore the first volume minimum satisfies

(45)

\omega_{K}\cdot\varepsilon(n)\leq\mathcal{V}_{1}(\Lambda).

On the other hand, we have by our assumption on the second moment of
\rho_{V}(\Lambda)
:

(46)

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{V}(\Lambda)=0)\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}\left(\frac{V^{2}}{\omega_{K}^{2}}+\frac{V}{\omega_{K}}\cdot\left(1+\omega_{K}\cdot\Err(K,t)\right)\right)-\frac{2\cdot V}{m\cdot\omega_{K}}+1

Setting
V=\omega_{K}\cdot m
therefore yields that

(47)

\mathbb{P}(\rho_{V}(\Lambda)\neq 0)\geq 1-\tfrac{1}{m}\cdot\left(1+\omega_{K}\cdot\Err(K,t)\right).

For the choice of
m=\varepsilon(n)^{-1}
it follows in other words that

\mathcal{V}_{1}(\Lambda)\leq\omega_{K}\cdot\varepsilon(n)^{-1}

with probability greater than
1-\varepsilon(n)\cdot\left(1+\omega_{K}\cdot\Err(K,t)\right)
.
The result follows.
∎

It therefore follows that for
any
fixed number field via the bound (
14
), we can control the second moment as the rank and therefore blocksize varies via Theorem
17
and obtain good probabilistic bounds on the shortest vector. Similarly, whenever we have uniform height lower bounds, the same conclusion applies when varying the number field. We record here the corresponding result for cyclotomic fields:

Theorem 28
.

Let
t\geq 11
be fixed.
Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{k})
be a cyclotomic number field and let
n=t\cdot\deg K=t\varphi(k)
. Let
\omega_{K}=\card\mu(K)
which is
k
or
2k
depending on whether
k
is even or odd. Consider a Haar-random
unit covolume module lattice
\Lambda\subseteq K^{t}\otimes\mathbb{R}
in the moduli space of rank-
t
module lattices over
K
.
Then, as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have with probability
1-o(1)
that

(48)

1-\frac{\log\log\omega_{K}}{n}\leq\omega_{K}^{-\frac{1}{n}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)}{\gamma(n)}\leq 1+\frac{\log\log\omega_{K}}{n}

where
\gamma(n)
is the radius of a ball of unit volume in dimension
n
.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
25
and Proposition
27
with
\varepsilon(n)=\tfrac{1}{\ln(\omega_{K})}
.
∎

5.
Remarks on fixed number fields

Should the reader be interested in a fixed cyclotomic or other number field
K
and varying or fixed rank, our results can be significantly sharpened by explicitly computing the contributions of low height elements in
K
. The following example can be reproduced for a variety of number fields with sufficiently small degree to make the computations feasible:

Example 30
.

Consider the cyclotomic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})
of degree
8
and conductor
16
. We take the rank to be
t=32
leading to random lattices in dimension
256
.
In a matter of seconds on a personal computer, using Lemma
9
and a SageMath
[
30
]
implementation of algorithms for finding points of bounded height as layed out in
[
31
]
, we find that the contribution to the error term of the points

\{\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{16})^{\times}:h(\alpha)\leq 0.6\}

is bounded by
1.195\cdot 10^{-11}
. The contribution of the remaining terms can be estimated by the tail estimate in Proposition
18
with
h_{0}=0.6
,
c_{S}(K)=0.271763
and even
\card S_{K}=16
since one can check none of the exceptional integers listed in Theorem
21
occurs in
K
. The Dedekind zeta values can also be computed with reasonable precision in SageMath and are bounded by
\zeta_{K}(8)^{2}<1.01
and we find that the tail contribution is of the order of
10^{-14}
. We obtain therefore that the space of random lattices of rank
32
over
\mathbb{Z}(\zeta_{16})
satisfies for any ball in dimension
256
of volume
V
:

\mathbb{E}[\#(\Lambda\cap B\setminus\{0\})^{2}]\leq V^{2}+\omega_{K}\cdot V\cdot(1+\eta)

with
\eta\leq 1.2\cdot 10^{-11}
.

Therefore, we obtain by Proposition
27
shortest vector bounds essentially unaffected by the error term
\eta
. For instance if we make the choice of
\varepsilon=\ln(256)^{-1}
, with probability greater than
1-\varepsilon(2+16\eta)\geq 0.639
, the shortest vector of such random module lattices must satisfy

(49)

1-\frac{\log\log 256}{256}\leq 16^{-\frac{1}{256}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)}{\gamma(256)}\leq 1+\frac{\log\log 256}{256},

where
\gamma(256)
is the radius of a ball of unit volume in dimension
256
. In other words, the shortest vector is likely close to
0.8156\%
larger than
2^{-1/256}\cdot\gamma(256)
, the latter being what one expects for an unstructured lattice of the same dimension.

The above example might suggest to the reader that, for all intents and purposes, we may pretend the second moment
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{V}(\Lambda)]
is on the nose that of a Poisson distribution of mean
V/\omega_{K}
and that the error term is negligible. While this is certainly true in this example and as the blocksize increases, there is some subtle dependence on the underlying number field to take into account, especially in relatively low dimensions.

Motivic nearby cycles and their monodromy at a singular point
Ran Azouri

Abstract
In this survey article 1 1 1 Based on the works of Marc Levine, Simon Pepin Lehalleur and Vasudevan Srinivas [ LPLS ] ; the author [ Az ] ; and Emil Jacobsen and the author [ AJ ] . , we explain how to compute both the quadratic Euler characteristic of nearby cycles, and the motivic monodromy, at a quasi-homogeneous singularity. This gives, for such singularity, a quadratic refinement to the Deligne–Milnor formula in characteristic zero, and an enhancement of the Picard–Lefschetz formula to Voevodsky motives with rational coefficients.



1
The Milnor fiber and nearby cycles

1.1
In topology - Milnor

The story begins with John Milnor’s work
Singular points of complex hypersurfaces
(1968). Let
f\colon\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic function, smooth except at one point
p\in X_{0}:=f^{-1}(0)
. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and even smaller
0<|t|<\varepsilon
, the
Milnor fiber at
p
is defined as

\operatorname{MF}_{f,p}:=X_{t}\cap B_{p}(\varepsilon),

where
B_{p}(\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is the ball of radius
\varepsilon
around
p
, and
X_{t}:=f^{-1}(t)
.

Milnor showed that
\operatorname{MF}_{f,p}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres,

\operatorname{MF}_{f,p}\simeq\bigvee_{1}^{\mu}S^{n}.

The integer
\mu
is the
Milnor number
, given also by

\mu=\dim H^{n}(\operatorname{MF}_{f,p},\mathbb{Z})
.
Looking for invariants related to an isolated singular point we have the following facts:

(a)

The Milnor formula:

The Milnor number satisfies the formula

\chi(\operatorname{MF}_{f,p})-1=(-1)^{n}\mu.

(1.1)

In addition, the Milnor number can be computed algebraically as the dimension of the Jacobian ring

\mu=\dim J(f,p),

where
J(f,p):=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},...,x_{n+1}]_{p}/((\partial f/\partial x_{i})_{i})
.
Milnor and Orlik also computed
\mu
for a singularity defined by a weighted homogeneous polynomial,
[
MO
]
.

(b)

The monodromy:

Rotating around
p
defines the monodromy action on cohomology
T\circlearrowright H^{n}(\operatorname{MF},\mathbb{Z})
. Picard
[
PS97
]
, and Lefschetz
[
Lef
]
, computed this explicitly, although they did not have the notion of Milnor fiber available at the time.

Pierre Deligne introduced the
nearby cycles functor
in
[
SGA7II
, Exposé XIII]
after Grothendieck,

\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}:D^{b}_{c}(X_{\eta})\to D^{b}_{c}(X_{\sigma}).

A related functor is the
vanishing cycles
,
\Phi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}
, which measures the failure of
\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}
to be the restriction of the constant sheaf. We have the
nearby cycles sheaf
,

\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
.
Locally at a point, for
p\in X_{s}
, the stalk of
\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
at
p
computes the
\ell
-adic cohomology of the Milnor fiber, where here we set
\operatorname{MF}_{f,p}:=(X_{\bar{p}})_{\bar{\eta}}
(the generic fiber of the strict henselization of
X
at the geometric point
\bar{p}
):

(R\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})_{\bar{p}}\simeq R\Gamma(\operatorname{MF}_{f,p},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

Globally, if
f
is proper, then the global sections of
\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
recover the cohomology of the generic fiber:

R\Gamma(X_{\bar{\sigma}},\Psi_{f}^{\acute{e}t}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\cong R\Gamma(X_{\bar{\eta}},\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

(1.2)

We may think of the nearby cycles sheaf then, as the analogue of the Milnor fiber in algebraic geometry.
We have the following important results, which are algebraic geometric versions for
(a)
and
(b)
in
\lx@sectionsign\penalty\ \ref{sec:topology}
.

(a)

The Deligne-Milnor conjecture

[
SGA7II
, Exposé XVI -
La formule de Milnor
]
. We have the formula at a point
p

[
SGA7II
, Conjecture 1.9]
,

\chi^{\ell-adic}(\Phi_{f,p}^{\acute{e}t})+\operatorname{dim}\text{Sw}(\Phi^{n}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})_{p})=(-1)^{n}\mu_{f,p}.

(1.3)

The second summand is the Swan conductor. The formula was proven in
[
SGA7II
]
for the case of an isolated quadratic singularity, and the case of equal characteristics. Since then it has been proven in greater generality by Bloch
[
Blo
]
(who suggested a more general conjecture involving the localized Chern class), Kato-Saito
[
KS
]
, and in the full generality of the original conjecture by Beraldo-Pippi
[
BP
]
.

(b)

Monodromy

[
SGA7II
, Exposé XV –
La formule de Picard–Lefschetz
]
. We have an action of the inertia group
I=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\eta}/\eta)
on the nearby cycles complex
\Psi_{f}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
. In the case of an ordinary double point the monodromy can be computed explicitly, the result depends on the parity of the dimension of
f
. The monodromy action defines also the monodromy operator

N\colon\Psi^{\acute{e}t}_{f}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\Psi^{\acute{e}t}_{f}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(-1),

(1.4)

where
\sigma\in I
acts as
\text{exp}(Nt_{\ell}(\sigma))
, and
t_{l}:I\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)
is the
l
-th tame character. While Deligne’s original proof for the Picard–Lefschetz formula included transcendental methods for the odd case, Illusie later gave a proof which is purely algebraic,
[
Ill
]
.

1.3
In motivic homotopy theory – Ayoub

Joseph Ayoub constructed a refinement of Deligne’s functor for the category of
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-motivic spectra
\operatorname{SH}(-)
,

\Psi_{f}\colon\operatorname{SH}(X_{\eta})\to\operatorname{SH}(X_{\sigma}).

This then induces a functor also on
\operatorname{DM}(-)
,
\operatorname{DM}_{\mathbb{Q}}(-)
, etc. While the construction is more involved than Deligne’s, it satisfies similar formal properties of a
specialization system
. We list some of those properties and show how they help compute the nearby cycles. We use freely the six functors on

\operatorname{SH}(-)
, for an account on this see
[
Hoy17
]
.

Property 1.2
.

•

Nearby cycles commute with smooth pullback and proper pushforward, that is, if we have a morphism
h
, in

Example 1.3

(blowup)

2
Isolated singularities and semistable reduction

We give our definitions for a homogeneous and quasi-homogeneous singularities that we use in this paper, and present constructions of semistable reduction to these singularities, showing how they help us compute nearby cycles.

2.1
Semistable reduction

Definition 2.1
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism satisfying to Assumption
1.1
.

1.

We say that
f
is
semistable
if the special fiber,
X_{\sigma}
, is a simple normal crossing divisor, in particular, reduced.

2.

Let
r
be a natural number and define
r\colon S^{\prime}:=S[t^{1/r}]\to S
. We also use
r
for the base change morphism

X_{r}:=X\times_{S}S^{\prime}\to X
,
and
f_{r}\colon X_{r}\to S^{\prime}
.
We say that
f

admits semistable reduction

if
there is a natural number
r
,
and a proper map

\pi\colon Y\to X_{r}

such that

g:=f_{r}\circ\pi\colon Y\to S^{\prime}

is a semistable morphism.

When
f
admits semistable reduction
g
, then in a similar manner to Example
1.3
, we can reduce computing the nearby cycles spectrum on
f
, to the nearby cycles spectrum on
g
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Az
, Proposition 3.10]
)
.

Assume
f:X\rightarrow S
admits a semistable reduction
Y\xrightarrow{\pi}X_{r}\xrightarrow{f_{r}}S^{\prime}
for some
r
. Let
r:X_{r}\to X
, and let
g=f_{r}\circ\pi
. Then

\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq(r\circ\pi)_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{g}\mathbbm{1}.

Proof.

By
[
Ay07
, Proposition 3.5.9]
, we have the natural isomorphism
\Psi_{f}\simeq r_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{f_{r}}\circ{r}_{\eta}^{*}
. Since
r_{\eta}
is an isomorphism, the natural map
\operatorname{id}\to r_{\eta*}\circ r_{\eta}^{*}
is an isomorphism. This, together with the pushforward property of
\Psi
for proper maps, Property
•
‣
1.2
, gives the sequence of isomorphisms,

\Psi_{f}(\mathbbm{1}_{X_{\eta}})\simeq r_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{f_{r}}(\mathbbm{1}_{X_{e\eta}})\simeq r_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{f_{r}}\circ\pi_{\eta*}\circ\pi_{\eta}^{*}\mathbbm{1}_{X_{e\eta}}\simeq r_{\sigma*}\circ\pi_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{g}(\mathbbm{1}_{Y_{\eta}})\simeq(r\circ\pi)_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{g}(\mathbbm{1}_{Y_{\eta}}).

∎

A nice fact about semistable schemes, is that we can compute nearby cycles on strata of the special fiber by extending from the smooth locus.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Ay07
, Théorème 3.3.44]
,
[
Az
, Remark 3.4]
)
.

Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a morphism satisfying Assumption
1.1
, which is semistable, with
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
the irreducible components of
X_{\sigma}
. Fix an
i
and let
D:=D_{i}
,
A:=D\setminus\cup_{j\neq i}D_{j}
. We use the following notation,

A\xhookrightarrow{v}D\xhookrightarrow{u}X_{\sigma}

for the respective open and closed immersions. We then have:

u^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq v_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{A}=h_{D}(A),\quad u^{!}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq v_{!}\mathbbm{1}_{A}=h^{c}_{D}(A).

Proof.

First, we use a theorem by Ayoub which tells us how to compute the nearby cycles on a stratum of the special fiber by restricting to the smooth locus. In our notation, composing
u^{*}\Psi_{f}f_{\eta}^{*}
with the unit map
id\rightarrow v_{*}v^{*}
of the adjunction, induces a natural isomorphism (
[
Ay07
, Théorème 3.3.44]
)

u^{*}\Psi_{f}f_{\eta}^{*}\xrightarrow{\sim}v_{*}v^{*}u^{*}\Psi_{f}f_{\eta}^{*}.

Applying the above isomorphism on
\mathbbm{1}_{\eta}
, and get

u^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq v_{*}v^{*}u^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}

Now, let
w\colon A\to\sigma
be the structure morphism. Use the compatibility with smooth pullback
(Property
•
‣
1.2
),
first to the open immersion

X\setminus\cup_{j\neq i}D_{j}\hookrightarrow X
,
then to the smooth morphism

X\setminus\cup_{j\neq i}D_{j}\to S
,
and using the second Property
•
‣
1.2
on
\Psi_{\operatorname{id}}\mathbbm{1}
,
we get

v^{*}u^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq w^{*}\Psi_{\operatorname{id}}\mathbbm{1}\simeq\mathbbm{1}_{A}.

Combining the two
we obtain the first equivalence.
The second equivalence is handled similarly, with the dual identity in
[
Ay07
, Théorème 3.3.44]
.
∎

2.2
Homogeneous singularities

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a morphism satisfying Assumption
1.1
, with base field
k
and parameter
t
(so that either
S=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
, or
S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}
and
t
is a fixed uniformizer in the dvr
\mathcal{O}
).
Let
F\in k[T_{0},...,T_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
r
,
with
(r,\operatorname{char}k)=1
.
We say that a singular point
p\in X_{\sigma}
is

a homogeneous singularity defined by
F
,

if
the hypersurface
V(F)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{n}
is smooth,
and we have

f^{*}(t)=F(x_{0},...,x_{n})

in the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
modulo
m^{r+1}
,
where
(x_{0},...,x_{n})
is a regular sequence
generating the maximal ideal
m\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
(local étale coordinates).

Let
F\in k[To,...,T_{n}]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
r
. We define the following embedding of schemes,

\displaystyle C:=\{T_{n+1}=0\}\xhookrightarrow{i}D:=V_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}}(F-T_{n+1}^{r})\xhookleftarrow{j}A:=\{T_{n+1}\neq 0\}.

(2.1)

We claim that all the data about the nearby cycles spectrum at a homogeneous singularity
p
, defined by a polynomial
F
(including the monodromy operator), can be deduced from this embedding of schemes. Notice that by the localization property we have the following natural maps:

\displaystyle\alpha:j_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{A}\to i_{*}i^{!}\mathbbm{1}_{D}[1]

(2.2)

\displaystyle\beta:i_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{C}\to j_{!}\mathbbm{1}_{A}[1].

(2.3)

Those two will appear later when we describe the monodromy.
To begin, we make the following claim which computes the nearby cycles spectrum restricted to the singular point.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
Az
, Theorem 4.3]
,
[
AJ
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be
a flat, quasi-projective morphism, with a homogeneous singularity
o\colon p\hookrightarrow X_{\sigma}
defined by a polynomial
F(T_{0},...,T_{n})
of degree
r
, and let
A:=V(F-T_{n+1}^{r})\setminus V(T_{n+1})\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n+1}
.
We have natural isomorphisms

\displaystyle o^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq h(A),

(2.4)

\displaystyle o^{!}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq h_{c}(A).

(2.5)

Sketch of proof.

First we make the following construction:

1.

Blow up
f\colon X\to S
at the singular point
p
, obtaining
\operatorname{Bl}_{p}(X)\to S
.

2.

Pass to the base change
S^{\prime}:=S[t^{1/r}]\to S
,
\operatorname{Bl}_{p}(X)_{r}\to S^{\prime}
.

3.

Normalize the result to obtain a morphism
g\colon Y\to S^{\prime}
.

Computing the blowup by the standard covering shows that
g
is a semistable reduction for
f
. Moreover, the special fiber
Y_{\sigma}
, is the union of two smooth divisors
Y_{\sigma}:=D\cup D^{\prime}
, where
D
is the preimage of the exceptional divisor in the blowup, and
D^{\prime}
the preimage of the strict transform. In addition, define
C:=D\cap D^{\prime}
. Then the close immersion
u\colon C\hookrightarrow D
is isomorphic to the close immersion
i
in Equation (
2.1
)
[
Az
, Theorem 4.3]
.
Now by the Proposition
2.2
, we have

o^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq o^{*}(r\circ\pi)_{\sigma*}\circ\Psi_{g}\mathbbm{1}.

Let
w
be the restriction of
(r\circ\pi)_{\sigma}
to
D
. Then by proper base change,
o^{*}(r\circ\pi)_{\sigma*}=w_{*}u^{*}
, and we get
o^{*}\Psi_{f}\mathbbm{1}\simeq w_{*}u^{*}\Psi_{g}\mathbbm{1}
. Finally,
since
g
is semistable, and setting
A:=D\setminus C\simeq V(F-T_{n+1}^{r})\setminus V(T_{n+1})
, we have by Proposition
2.3
,

u^{*}\Psi_{g}\mathbbm{1}\simeq h_{D}(A),

and so we get the first statement in the proposition, the second one is obtained in a similar way.
∎

Proposition 2.6

(
[
AJ
, Proposition 3.7]
for
\operatorname{DM}(-)
)
.

Let
g:Y\to S
be a semistable morphism, with
Y_{\sigma}=D\cup D^{\prime}
,
D
,
D^{\prime}
smooth,
C:=D\cap D^{\prime}
, and
u\colon D\hookrightarrow Y_{\sigma}
the closed immersion. We have the following fiber sequence in
\operatorname{SH}(D)
:

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{D}\to u^{*}\Psi_{g}\mathbbm{1}\xrightarrow{}i_{*}\Sigma^{-\mathcal{N}_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{C}[1],

(2.6)

where
\mathcal{N}_{i}
is the normal bundle of the closed immersion
i\colon C\hookrightarrow D
.

Proof.

Consider the localization sequence in
\operatorname{SH}(D)
with
C\xhookrightarrow{i}D\xhookleftarrow{j}A
:

i_{!}i^{!}\to\operatorname{id}\to j_{*}j^{*}.

Applying this to
\mathbbm{1}_{D}
, and using purity along closed immersions (
[
Hoy17
, Proposition 5.7]
), which gives
i^{!}\mathbbm{1}_{D}\simeq\Sigma^{-\mathcal{N}_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{C}
, we get the sequence

i_{*}\Sigma^{-\mathcal{N}_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{C}\to\mathbbm{1}_{D}\to j_{*}\mathbbm{1}_{A}.

Now using the result of Prop
2.5
and shifting, we get the first fiber sequence.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

While the twist

\Sigma^{-\mathcal{N}_{i}}(-)=(-)\otimes(-1)[-2]

We have a natural map
K_{0}(\operatorname{Var}_{k})\to K_{0}(\operatorname{SH}(k))

[p\colon X\to k]\mapsto[p_{!}\mathbbm{1}_{X}]

[X]=[p_{!}\mathbbm{1}_{X}]\in K_{0}(\operatorname{SH}(k))

Corollary 2.8

(
[
Az
, Corollary 4.4]
)
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be
a morphism satisfying Assumption
1.1
, with a homogeneous singularity
o\colon p\hookrightarrow X_{\sigma}

F

2.4

with the notation of (

Proof.

First, by the same construction as in the proof of Proposition

2.3
Quasi-homogeneous singularities

We also deal with a more general class of singularities, where we can put different weights on the variables. Here there are some additional assumptions detailed below.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\underline{a}=(a_{1},...,a_{n})
a vector of natural numbers and let
{\mathbb{P}}(\underline{a})
be the weighted homogeneous space
\operatorname{Proj}k[T_{0},...,T_{n}]
, with the ring graded by letting
T_{i}
have degree
a_{i}
. Let
F\in k[T_{0},...,T_{n}]
be an
\underline{a}
-weighted homogeneous polynomial of degree
r
. It defines a hypersurface
V(F)
in the weighted homogeneous space
{\mathbb{P}}(\underline{a})
. We require that the weights
a_{i}
are pairwise relatively prime, each
a_{i}
divides
r
, and
r
is prime to the exponential characteristic of
k
. Let
G(y_{0},...,y_{n}):=F(y_{0}^{a_{0}},..,y_{n}^{a_{n}})
and assume that both
V(G)\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{k}^{n}
, and
V(F)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(\underline{a})
are smooth. Furthermore, letting
v_{i}\in{\mathbb{P}}(\underline{a})
be the point with the
i
-th homogeneous coordinate 1, and all other coordinates 0, we require that
F(v_{i})\neq 0
if
a_{i}>1
(this last condition is superfluous in the case
n>1
, see
[
Az
, Remark 5.4]
).

Let
f\colon X\to\operatorname{Spec}k[t]
be a flat, quasi-projective morphism. We say that a singular point
p\in X_{\sigma}
is
an
\underline{a}
-weighted homogeneous singularity defined by
F
, if in the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
, we can write

f^{*}(t)=F(x_{0},...,x_{n})+h,

where
\underline{x}=(x_{0},...,x_{n})
is a regular sequence generating the maximal ideal
m\subset\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
, and
h\in m\cdot m_{\underline{a}}^{(r)}
, where
m_{\underline{a}}^{(r)}
is the ideal generated by monomials in
\underline{x}
of
\underline{a}
-weighted degree
r
. For more details see
[
Az
, Definition 5.1, Definition 5.3]
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be
a flat, quasi-projective morphism.

Let

The quadratic Milnor number

We define a quadratic version to the Milnor number at a point. Given

Definition 3.3

Fix a motivic ring spectrum

Definition 3.4

Let

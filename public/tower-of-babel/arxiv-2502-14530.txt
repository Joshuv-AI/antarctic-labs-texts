Projectivity of Moduli Spaces in the Higher-Rank DT/PT Correspondence
Mihai Pavel, Tuomas Tajakka

Abstract
. We study projectivity of moduli spaces on the DT/PT wall crossing in Bridgeland and polynomial stability on a smooth, projective threefold. First, we construct a globally generated line bundle on the moduli stack of higher-rank PT-semistable objects and analyze the extent to which it separates points. Next, when the rank and degree are coprime, we refine our construction to obtain an explicit ample line bundle on the corresponding coarse moduli space of PT-stable objects, thereby establishing its projectivity. Finally, we consider certain classes of threefolds for which PT-stability is realized as a Bridgeland stability condition and establish projectivity also on the wall separating the Gieseker and PT chambers.

1.
Introduction

The DT/PT correspondence, as formulated by Pandharipande and Thomas in
[
41
]
, establishes a relationship between two curve-counting invariants on a projective Calabi–Yau threefold
X
. On one side are the classical Donaldson–Thomas (DT) invariants constructed by Thomas in
[
49
]
, which virtually count
1
-dimensional closed subschemes of
X
. The moduli space of these objects is the Hilbert scheme of curves. On the other side are the Pandharipande–Thomas (PT) invariants introduced in
[
41
]
, which virtually count stable pairs on
X
. By definition, a stable pair
(F,s)
on
X
is a
2
-term complex of the form

\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{s}F

where
F
is a pure sheaf of dimension
1
and the cokernel of
s
is
0
-dimensional. Moduli spaces of stable pairs can be constructed as GIT quotients of coherent systems, in the sense of Le Potier
[
25
]
. They are moreover projective, in parallel with the corresponding moduli spaces in DT theory.

The objects underlying the two enumerative theories can be described in terms of certain stability conditions on the derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
, which arise as “large volume limits” of Bridgeland stability conditions. This connection was independently observed by Bayer
[
9
]
and Toda
[
50
]
, who interpreted the DT/PT correspondence as a wall-crossing phenomenon within a family of such stability conditions. Moreover, this approach led to the formulation of a higher-rank version of the DT/PT correspondence, which was subsequently proved by Toda
[
52
]
along with the original (rank-
1
) case. An alternative proof of the rank-
1
DT/PT correspondence, also based on wall-crossing techniques, was previously obtained by Bridgeland
[
12
]
(see also
[
51
,
46
]
for the Euler-characteristic analogue of the correspondence).

The moduli spaces arising in the (higher-rank) DT/PT correspondence can be defined over any polarized, smooth, complex projective threefold
(X,H)
. On the higher-rank DT side, we have the moduli space of
H
-Gieseker-semistable sheaves on
X
, which can be constructed as a projective GIT quotient
[
15
,
36
]
. This aligns with the rank-
1
case, since the Hilbert scheme of curves can be identified with the Gieseker moduli space of rank-
1
torsion-free sheaves with trivial determinant.
Our first goal is to establish projectivity on the PT side in higher rank by studying the moduli space of PT-stable objects. We will recall the notion of PT-stability on the derived category of coherent sheaves on
X
in
Section

5
, in a framework that recovers the stable pairs of Pandharipande and Thomas as stable objects of rank
1
and trivial determinant.

Moduli spaces of higher-rank PT-stable objects provide alternative compactifications of the moduli of
\mu
-stable reflexive sheaves. Indeed, the moduli theory of PT-semistable objects was developed by J. Lo in
[
31
,
32
]
, who showed that the moduli stack of PT-semistable objects with fixed Chern character is a universally closed algebraic stack of finite type, which moreover admits a proper coarse moduli space in the absence of strictly semistable objects. However, unlike the rank 1 and trivial determinant case
considered by Pandharipande and Thomas, the moduli problem for higher-rank PT-stable objects is not known to be associated with a GIT problem, and hence it is unknown whether the moduli spaces are projective. In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively in the case of coprime rank and degree, thereby confirming a result expected in the field (see
[
50
, Remark 3.22]
).

In their recent work, Jardim, Lo, Maciocia, and Martinez
[
18
]
studied the wall-crossing transformation between the DT and PT moduli spaces in the framework of Bridgeland stability conditions. Their analysis is carried out for a projective threefold
X
of Picard rank
1
admitting Bridgeland stability conditions constructed via the double-tilt procedure of
[
8
]
. Such stability conditions are known to exist for threefolds in the following cases: Fano threefolds
[
35
,
44
,
27
]
, abelian threefolds
[
34
,
7
]
, quintic threefolds
[
26
]
, threefolds with nef tangent bundles
[
20
]
, general weighted hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,1,2)
or
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,1,4)

[
21
]
, and Calabi-Yau threefolds given as the complete intersection of a quadric and a quartic in
\mathbb{P}^{5}

[
30
]
.

Let
v\in\Knum(X)
be a numerical class of positive rank such that
\rk(v)
and
H^{2}\ch_{1}(v)
are coprime.
In this case, it was shown in
[
18
]
that both the Gieseker moduli space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{\mu}}(v)
of
\mu
-stable sheaves of class
v
and the moduli space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}(v)
of PT-stable objects can be realized as Bridgeland moduli spaces. Moreover, these spaces are separated by a single wall in the space
\Stab(X)
of Bridgeland stability conditions. They can be connected by a wall-crossing diagram

\textstyle{\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{\mu}}(v)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}(v)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(v)}

where
\overline{\sigma}\in\Stab(X)
is a stability condition on the separating wall, and
\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(v)
is the corresponding Bridgeland moduli space of
\overline{\sigma}
-semistable objects of class

(see op. cit. or

Main results

We summarize our main results and the ideas underlying them in the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,H)
be a polarized, smooth, projective threefold over
\mathbb{C}
, and let
v\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)
be a class of positive rank such that
\rk(v)
and
H^{2}\ch_{1}(v)
are coprime.

(a)

The coarse moduli space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
of PT-stable objects is projective for any PT-stability function
Z
on
(X,H)
.

(b)

Assume that
X
is a projective threefold of Picard rank
1
admitting double-tilt Bridgeland stability conditions. Then the
Bridgeland moduli space
\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(v)
is projective for a stability condition
\overline{\sigma}\in\Stab(X)
on the wall separating the Gieseker and PT chambers.

We begin our study with some general results that do not require the coprime assumption. In
Section

3
, we introduce a quasi-compact algebraic stack
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
, which contains both the stack
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Gss}}_{H}(v)
of
H
-Gieseker-semistable sheaves and the stack
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
of PT-semistable objects. We prove in
Theorem

4.2
the existence of a
semiample
determinantal line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{1}
on
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
, and then describe in
Section

4.3
the extent to which its sections separate points. A key ingredient here is provided by the Mehta-Ramanathan type restriction theorems
[
38
,
39
,
43
]
. This restriction method originates from the works of Le Potier
[
24
]
and J. Li
[
28
]
, who employed it to construct an algebro-geometric analogue of the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck compactification. In our case, we prove that the restriction of a
\mathbb{C}
-point of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
(and in particular of a PT-semistable object) to a general surface
D\in|aH|
of sufficiently large degree is isomorphic to a Gieseker-semistable sheaf. This, in turn, makes it possible to construct determinantal
\theta
-sections of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
using the results of Álvarez-Cónsul and King
[
4
, Section 7]
.

However, the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{1}
does not separate the
\mathbb{C}
-points of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
(nor those of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Gss}}_{H}(v)
). We improve its positivity by twisting it with another determinantal line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, in a manner analogous to the construction for the Gieseker moduli space
[
17
, Section 8]
. Indeed, when the rank and degree are coprime, we show in
Theorem

5.10
that a positive linear combination of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}
descends to an
ample
line bundle on the coarse moduli space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
of PT-stable objects, thus establishing part (a).

Our proof of the projectivity of
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
relies crucially on the coprime assumption, which implies three useful properties. First, it ensures that there are no strictly PT-semistable objects, which in particular guarantees the existence of the proper coarse moduli space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
. Second, it allows for a concrete characterization of PT-stable objects due to J. Lo, see
Proposition

5.3
, which is helpful for describing certain strata of
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)

[
33
]
. Third, under the coprime assumption, Beentjes and Ricolfi constructed a stratification of the moduli space of PT-stable objects by relative Quot schemes, see
[
10
, Proposition 5.5 and Appendix B]
. We use this last property to study the positivity of the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{0}
(see
Section

5.2
).

We address part (b) in
Section

6.3
. The results of
[
18
]
allow us to identify
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
with the Bridgeland moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(v)
for a stability condition
\overline{\sigma}\in\Stab(X)
on the wall separating the Gieseker and PT chambers. We then show that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
descends to an ample line bundle on the good moduli space
\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(v)
. In fact, our proof of the projectivity of
\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\sigma}}(v)
does not rely on the coprime assumption, although a wall-crossing diagram as above is currently only known in the coprime case. This result can be seen as a threefold analogue of the main theorem in
[
48
]
, which addresses the case of Bridgeland stability conditions on surfaces.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we fix a smooth, projective, connected threefold
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, together with a very ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
on
X
.

In this section, we recall a notion of semistability for coherent sheaves on
X
following
[
17
, Section 1.6]
. Let
\Coh(X)
be the abelian category of coherent sheaves on
X
. For an integer
0\leq e\leq 2
, we denote by
\Coh_{3,e}(X)
the quotient category
\Coh(X)/\Coh_{e-1}(X)
, where
\Coh_{e-1}(X)
is the full subcategory of
\Coh(X)
whose objects are sheaves of dimension
\leq e-1
.

For every
E\in\Coh(X)
, let

(2.1)

\mathrm{P}(E,m)=\int_{X}\ch(E\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(m))\td_{X}=\sum_{i=0}^{3}a_{i}(E){m+3-i\choose 3-i}

be the Hilbert polynomial of
E
with respect to the polarization
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. By linearity, the Hilbert polynomial extends to an additive function

\mathrm{P}:\mathrm{K}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}[m].

For
0\leq e\leq 2
, we define the
e
-truncated Hilbert polynomial by

\mathrm{P}_{e}:\mathrm{K}(X)\to\mathbb{Q}[m],\quad\mathrm{P}_{e}(E,m)=\sum_{i=0}^{3-e}a_{i}(E){m+3-i\choose 3-i}.

Moreover, when
E\in\Coh(X)
is torsion-free, we denote by

\mathrm{p}_{e}(E,m)=\frac{\mathrm{P}_{e}(E,m)}{\rk(E)}

the reduced
e
-truncated Hilbert polynomial of
E
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq d\leq 3
be an integer and set
e=3-d
. We say that a coherent sheaf
E
on
X
is
d
-Gieseker-(semi)stable
if it is torsion-free, and for every coherent subsheaf
0\neq F\subsetneq E

\mathrm{p}_{e}(F,m)\mathrel{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle($}}\leq\mathrel{\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle)$}}\mathrm{p}_{e}(E,m)\quad\text{for }m\gg 0.

One can easily check that
1
-Gieseker-stability is equivalent to the classical notion of
\mu
-stability, and that
3
-Gieseker-stability is the usual notion of Gieseker-stability. We also have

\displaystyle\mu\text{-stable}\Rightarrow 2\text{-Gieseker-stable}\Rightarrow\text{Gieseker-stable}\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\text{Gieseker-semistable}\Rightarrow 2\text{-Gieseker-semistable}\Rightarrow\mu\text{-semistable}.

Note that for torsion-free coherent sheaves of coprime rank and degree, all these conditions are in fact equivalent.

In general, every
d
-Gieseker-semistable sheaf
E
admits a Jordan-Hölder filtration

E_{\bullet}:0=E_{0}\subset E_{1}\subset\ldots\subset E_{m}=E

such that each
E_{i}/E_{i-1}
is
d
-Gieseker-stable and
\mathrm{p}_{3-d}(E_{i}/E_{i-1})=\mathrm{p}_{3-d}(E)
. We denote by
\gr(E_{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i}E_{i}/E_{i-1}
the associated graded module of
E_{\bullet}
. The sheaf
\gr(E_{\bullet})
is uniquely determined in
\Coh_{3,3-d}(X)
, cf.
[
17
, Theorem 1.6.7]
. We say that two
d
-Gieseker-semistable sheaves
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
are
S
-equivalent
if their corresponding graded modules are isomorphic in
\Coh_{3,3-d}(X)
(that is, they are isomorphic outside a closed subset of codimension
d+1
in
X
).

3.
Moduli stacks of complexes

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pug}}
be the stack of
relatively perfect universally gluable
complexes on
X
, introduced by Lieblich in
[
29
]
. Namely,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pug}}
is the stack over the big étale site of
\mathbb{C}
-schemes which to a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
S
associates the groupoid of objects
E\in\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S\times X)
such that

(1)

E
is relatively perfect over
S
, see
[
45

, and

(2)

E
is universally gluable, i.e., for all geometric points

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)}(E_{s},E_{s})=0\quad\text{for }i<0.

Lieblich showed that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pug}}
is an algebraic stack locally of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, containing many open substacks of interest, such as the stack of flat families of sheaves on
X
and stacks of tilting hearts arising in the context of Bridgeland stability

5

Consider the following full subcategories

\displaystyle\Coh_{\leq 1}(X)={}

\displaystyle\{T\in\Coh(X)\mid\dim(T)\leq 1\},

\displaystyle\Coh_{\geq 2}(X)={}

\displaystyle\{F\in\Coh(X)\mid\dim(F^{\prime})\geq 2\text{ for every coherent subsheaf }F^{\prime}\subseteq F\},

which form a torsion pair on
\Coh(X)
. By tilting along this pair we obtain the heart of a bounded

-structure on
\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
, known as the

\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)=\langle\Coh_{\geq 2}(X),\Coh_{\leq 1}(X)[-1]\rangle.

In other words,
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
is the full subcategory consisting of objects
E\in\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
that fit in an exact triangle

F\to E\to T[-1],

where
F\in\Coh_{\geq 2}(X)
and
T\in\Coh_{\leq 1}(X)
.

Our definition of the heart

]

Following the terminology in

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}^{p}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{pug}}

As in

We consider the substack

-schemes

S
-perfect complexes

Since

A family

Proposition 3.3
.

The stack
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}^{p}}
.

Proof.

One sees as in
[
32
, Proposition 3.1]
that properties (
A
) and (

) above together form an open condition. Therefore
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
is an open substack of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}^{p}}
.

The quasi-compactness of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
follows from the proof of
[
31
, Proposition 3.4]
. We briefly sketch the argument here. Recall that every
\mathbb{C}
-point
E\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(v)
fits in an exact triangle of the form

\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\to E\to\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)[-1]

such that
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\in\Coh(X)
is
2
-Gieseker-semistable and
\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)\in\Coh(X)
is

\mathrm{P}_{1}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(E))=\mathrm{P}_{1}(v),\quad a_{3}(\mathcal{H}^{0}(E))=a_{3}(v)+a_{3}(\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)),

where

Hence,

is quasi-compact, since all its

5.
PT-semistable objects

In this section, we recall the definition and basic properties of PT-stability conditions. They are examples of polynomial stability conditions defined in
[
9
]
as a generalization of Bridgeland stability conditions in order to understand the large volume limit of Bridgeland stability, as well as to study the relation between the DT/PT invariants. We largely follow
[
31
]
and
[
32
]
in our presentation, except that we use a slightly different convention for the category of perverse sheaves (see
Remark

3.1
).

As before,
X
is a smooth, projective, connected threefold over
\mathbb{C}
, and fix a very ample divisor
H\in|\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)|
. In the following, we let
\phi(z)\in(0,\pi]
denote the phase of a complex number
z
in the extended upper half-plane

\mathbb{H}=\{\,z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\im z>0\,\}\cup\mathbb{R}_{<0}.

Definition 5.1
.

A
PT-stability condition
on
X
consists of the data of the heart
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
together with a group homomorphism
Z:\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\to\mathbb{C}[m]
, called the
central charge
, of the form

Z(E)(m)=\sum_{d=0}^{3}\rho_{d}\left(\int_{X}H^{d}\cdot\ch(E)\cdot U\right)m^{d},

where

(a)

the
\rho_{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
are nonzero complex numbers such that
-\rho_{0},-\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}\in\mathbb{H}
, and whose phases satisfy

\phi(\rho_{2})>\phi(-\rho_{0})>\phi(\rho_{3})>\phi(-\rho_{1}).

(b)

U=1+U_{1}+U_{2}+U_{3}\in A^{*}(X)
is a class with
U_{i}\in A^{i}(X)
for
i=1,2,3
.

The configuration of the complex numbers
\rho_{i}
is compatible with the heart
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
in the sense that for any nonzero
E\in\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
, we have
Z(E)(m)\in\mathbb{H}
for
m\gg 0
. This allows us to define a notion of stability on
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
: an object
E\in\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
is called
Z
-
stable
(resp.
Z
-
semistable
) if for every proper nonzero subobject
F\subseteq E
, we have

\phi(Z(F)(m))<\phi(Z(E)(m)\quad(\mathrm{resp.}\quad\phi(Z(F)(m))\leq\phi(Z(E)(m))\quad\mathrm{for}\quad m\gg 0.

Remark 5.2
.

As we noted in
Remark

3.1
, our definition of the heart
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
differs from that in the literature by a shift. To account for this, our definition of the charge
Z
also differs in that
-\rho_{0},-\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}
, rather than
\rho_{0},\rho_{1},-\rho_{2},-\rho_{3}
, are in
\mathbb{H}
. This will let us view the moduli of PT-semistable objects as an enlargement of the moduli of
\mu
-stable reflexive sheaves without having to perform a shift.

In
[
41
]
, Pandharipande and Thomas define a
stable pair
on
X
to be a map of the form

\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{s}F,

where
F
is a sheaf of pure dimension 1 and the cokernel of
s
is
0
-dimensional. In
[
9
, Proposition 6.1.1]
, Bayer shows that for any PT-stability condition, the stable objects in
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
with numerical invariants
\ch=(1,0,-\beta,-n)
and trivial determinant coincide precisely with these stable pairs. The following partial characterization of PT-semistable objects generalizes this fact to higher rank.

Proposition 5.3

(
[
31
, Lemma 3.3]
,
[
32
, Proposition 2.24]
)
.

If
v\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)
is a class of rank
\rk(v)>0
, then any PT-semistable object
E\in\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
of class
v
satisfies the following conditions:

(a)

\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)
is
2
-Gieseker-semistable,

(b)

\mathcal{H}^{1}(E)
is
0
-dimensional,

(c)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)}(T[-1],E)=0
for any
0
-dimensional sheaf
T
.

In particular
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)
is also
\mu
-semistable. If moreover
\rk(v)
and
H^{2}\cdot\ch_{1}(v)
are coprime, then any object
E
of class
v
in
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
satisfying these conditions is PT-stable,
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)
is
\mu
-stable, and there are no strictly semistable objects.

We note that for any PT-semistable object
E
of positive rank, the torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)
has homological dimension at most
1
, cf.
[
33
, Example 5.5]
.

We will also need the following observation.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let

\phi(Z(F)(m))=\phi(Z(E)(m))\quad\text{for }m\gg 0,

Proof.

Let

5.1.
Moduli of PT-semistable objects

The theory of moduli of PT-semistable objects was developed by Lo in
[
31
]
and
[
32
]
, culminating in
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
, where the author constructs the moduli stack of PT-semistable objects of fixed Chern character as a universally closed algebraic stack of finite type, and, in the absence of strictly semistable objects, as a proper algebraic space.

Here we revisit these results through the framework of good moduli spaces, developed by Alper in
[
3
]
. To recall the definition, let
\mathcal{M}
be an algebraic stack and
\pi:\mathcal{M}\to M
a quasi-compact, quasi-separated morphism to an algebraic space
M
. Then
\pi:\mathcal{M}\to M
is called a
good moduli space
if the pushforward functor
\pi_{*}:\Qcoh(\mathcal{M})\to\Qcoh(M)
is exact, and the natural map
\mathcal{O}_{M}\to\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
is an isomorphism.

We now establish our moduli setup for PT-semistable objects. Let
v\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)
be a class of positive rank, and let
Z:\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)\to\mathbb{C}[m]
define a PT-stability condition on
X
. The moduli stack of PT-semistable objects of class
v
is defined to be the substack
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)\subset\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{A}^{p}}
that to a scheme
S
of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
associates the groupoid of objects
E\in\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S\times X)
such that

(1)

E
is relatively perfect over
S
, see
[
45
, Tag 0DHZ]
, and

(2)

for all
\mathbb{C}
-points
s\in S
, the derived restriction
E|^{\mathbb{L}}_{\{s\}\times X}
to the fiber over
s
lies in
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
, is semistable with respect to
Z
, and has numerical class
v\in\mathrm{K}_{\mathrm{num}}(X)
.

By
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
, the stack
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
is universally closed and of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
, and moreover, in the case of coprime
\rk(v)
and
H^{2}\cdot\ch_{1}(v)
, it is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a proper algebraic space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
. In particular,
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
is both a coarse and a good moduli space for
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
in this case.

However, in the presence of strictly semistable objects we do not know if
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{PT}}_{Z}(v)
admits a good moduli space – since the heart
\mathcal{A}^{p}(X)
is not noetherian, the machinery of
[
2
, Chapter 7]
does not readily apply.

6.
Bridgeland moduli spaces

In this section, we study the moduli spaces arising in the higher-rank DT/PT correspondence in a particular case, building upon the recent work of Jardim, Lo, Maciocia, and Martinez
[
18
]
.

Let
(X,H)
be a polarized smooth, projective, connected threefold of Picard rank 1 over
\mathbb{C}
, where
H\subset X
is a very ample divisor on
X
. Hence the real Néron–Severi group of
X
is
\mathrm{N}^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\cong\mathbb{R}[H]
, where
[H]
denotes the class of
H
in
\mathrm{N}^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. We also assume that
X
is a threefold admitting Bridgeland stability conditions constructed via the double tilt procedure of
[
8
]
. See the introduction for a list of examples where such stability conditions are known to exist.

6.1.
Bridgeland stability conditions

We first recall the double tilt construction of Bridgeland stability conditions on
X
, following
[
8
]
. For a numerical class
B\in\mathrm{N}^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
E\in\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
, we denote by
\ch^{B}(E)\coloneqq\ch(E)\cdot e^{-B}
the twisted Chern character of
E
, i.e.

\displaystyle\ch_{0}^{B}(E)

\displaystyle=\ch_{0}(E),\quad\ch_{1}^{B}(E)=\ch_{1}(E)-B\ch_{0}(E),

\displaystyle\ch_{2}^{B}(E)

\displaystyle=\ch_{2}(E)-B\ch_{1}(E)+\frac{B^{2}}{2}\ch_{0}(E),

\displaystyle\ch_{3}^{B}(E)

\displaystyle=\ch_{3}(E)-B\ch_{2}(E)+\frac{B^{2}}{2}\ch_{1}(E)-\frac{B^{3}}{6}\ch_{0}(E).

Let
\omega\in\Amp^{1}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and consider the
B
-twisted slope function

\mu_{\omega,B}(E)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\omega^{2}\ch_{1}^{B}(E)}{\ch_{0}^{B}(E)}\quad&\text{if }\ch_{0}(E)\neq 0,\\
+\infty\quad&\text{if }\ch_{0}(E)=0\end{cases}

on
\Coh(X)
. It is known that this slope function defines a notion of semistability on
\Coh(X)
which moreover admits the Harder-Narasimhan property. Therefore one obtains that the full subcategories

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\omega,B}

\displaystyle=\{E\in\Coh(X)\mid\mu_{\omega,B}(Q)>0\text{ for every quotient }E\twoheadrightarrow Q\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\omega,B}

\displaystyle=\{E\in\Coh(X)\mid\mu_{\omega,B}(F)\leq 0\text{ for every subsheaf }F\hookrightarrow E\}

form a torsion pair on
\Coh(X)
. The extension-closed subcategory

\mathcal{B}_{\omega,B}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{F}_{\omega,B}[1],\mathcal{T}_{\omega,B}\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)

is the corresponding heart.

Now consider the slope function

\nu_{\omega,B}(E)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\omega^{2}\ch_{2}^{B}(E)-\frac{\omega^{3}}{6}\ch_{0}^{B}(E)}{\omega^{2}\ch_{1}^{B}(E)}\quad&\text{if }\omega^{2}\ch^{B}_{1}(E)\neq 0,\\
+\infty\quad&\text{if }\omega^{2}\ch^{B}_{1}(E)=0\end{cases}

on
\mathcal{B}_{\omega,B}
. As shown in
[
8
, Section 3]
, the full subcategories

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\omega,B}

\displaystyle=\{E\in\mathcal{B}_{\omega,B}\mid\nu_{\omega,B}(Q)>0\text{ for every quotient }E\twoheadrightarrow Q\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\omega,B}

\displaystyle=\{E\in\mathcal{B}_{\omega,B}\mid\nu_{\omega,B}(F)\leq 0\text{ for every subsheaf }F\hookrightarrow E\}

form a torsion pair on
\mathcal{B}_{\omega,B}
, from which one obtains the heart

\mathcal{A}_{\omega,B}\coloneqq\langle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{\omega,B}[1],\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\omega,B}\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X).

Next we place ourselves in the situation of
[
18
, p. 37]
. For every
(\beta,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, set

\mathcal{A}^{\beta,\alpha}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\sqrt{3}[H],\beta[H]},

and for
s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
consider the central charge given by

Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)\coloneqq-\ch_{3}^{\beta}(E)+\left(s+\frac{1}{6}\right)\alpha^{2}\ch_{1}^{\beta}(E)+i\left(\ch_{2}^{\beta}(E)-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\ch_{0}^{\beta}(E)\right),

where
\ch_{i}^{\beta}
is the real number satisfying the equality
\ch_{i}^{\beta}\cdot[H]^{i}=\ch_{i}^{\beta[H]}
in the numerical group
\mathrm{N}^{*}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

As in op. cit., we obtain a family

(\mathcal{A}^{\beta,\alpha},Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}),\quad(\beta,\alpha,s)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}

of Bridgeland stability conditions (in the sense of
[
11
]
). These stability conditions satisfy the support property and are moreover geometric, in the sense that the skyscraper sheaves
\mathcal{O}_{x}
are stable of the same phase. In this case the notion of stability on
\mathcal{A}^{\beta,\alpha}
is defined with respect to the slope function

\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{-\mathrm{Re}\,Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)}{\im Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)}\quad&\text{if }\im Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)\neq 0,\\
+\infty\quad&\text{if }\im Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)=0.\end{cases}

To be consistent with the notation in
Section

5
(see
Remark

5.2
), we will consider the shifted Bridgeland stability conditions

\sigma_{\beta,\alpha,s}=(\mathcal{D}^{\beta,\alpha},-Z_{\beta,\alpha,s}),\quad\mathcal{D}^{\beta,\alpha}\coloneqq\mathcal{A}^{\beta,\alpha}[-1].

Note that this choice does not change the slope function
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}
, but only shifts the stable objects.

Definition 6.1
.

A nonzero object

is
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}
-

(resp.

in

We say that

is
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}
-

if

where each

is
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}
-stable and satisfies
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E_{i})=\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)
.

We recall that every
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}
-semistable object

admits a Jordan-Hölder filtration

such that each factor

is
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}
-stable in
\mathcal{D}^{\beta,\alpha}
and
\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E_{i}/E_{i-1})=\lambda_{\beta,\alpha,s}(E)
. Note that the corresponding graded module

does not depend on the choice of the Jordan-Hölder filtration. We say that two semistable objects

and

in

are

Moduli of semistable objects

6.3.
Projectivity of the Bridgeland moduli space

In what follows, we do not need to assume that the rank and the degree of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}
is ample on
\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\beta},\overline{\alpha}}(v)
. Hence
L_{m,n,a}
is also ample, and
\mathrm{M}_{\overline{\beta},\overline{\alpha}}(v)

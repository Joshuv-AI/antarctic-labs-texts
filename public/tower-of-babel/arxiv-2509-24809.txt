Abstract
. Computing the stiffness matrix for the finite element discretization of the nonlocal Laplacian on unstructured meshes is difficult, because the operator is nonlocal and can even be singular. In this paper, we focus on the C 0 C^{0} -piecewise linear finite element method (FEM) for the nonlocal Laplacian on uniform grids within a d d -dimensional rectangular domain. By leveraging the connection between FE bases and B-splines (having attractive convolution properties), we can reduce the involved 2 ​ d 2d -dimensional integrals for the stiffness matrix entries into integrations over d d -dimensional balls with explicit integrands involving cubic B-splines and the kernel functions, which allows for explicit study of the singularities and accurate evaluations of such integrals in spherical coordinates. We show the nonlocal stiffness matrix has a block-Toeplitz structure, so the matrix-vector multiplication can be implemented using fast Fourier transform (FFT). In addition, when the interaction radius δ → 0 + , \delta\to 0^{+}, the nonlocal stiffness matrix automatically reduces to the local one. Although our semi-analytic approach on uniform grids cannot be extended to general domains with unstructured meshes, the resulting solver can seamlessly integrate with the grid-overlay (Go) technique for the nonlocal Laplacian on arbitrary bounded domains.

Introduction

This paper concerns the finite element solution of the following nonlocal constrained value problem posed on a
d
-dimensional rectangular domain
\Omega
:

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}u=f\;\;{\rm on}\;\;\Omega\,;\quad u=0\;\;{\rm on}\;\;\Omega_{\delta},

(1.1)

where
\Omega_{\delta}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\Omega\,:\,\text{dist}(\bm{x},\partial\Omega)\leq\delta\right\}
and the nonlocal operator is defined as

\mathcal{L}_{\delta}u({\bm{x}})=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}(u({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})-u({\bm{x}}))\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}},

(1.2)

with
\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}
being a
d
-dimensional ball of radius
\delta
centered at
0
.
We further assume the radial type kernel
\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)
is a non-increasing function with a bounded second-order moment:

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{\delta}^{d}}\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)|{\bm{s}}|^{2}{\rm d}{\bm{s}}=d,

(1.3)

which particularly includes the fractional power kernel:

\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)=c_{d,\delta}^{\alpha}|{\bm{s}}|^{-d-\alpha},\;\;\;

(1.4)

where
c^{\alpha}_{d,\delta}
is a normalization constant that satisfies (
1.3

Consider for example the FEM solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.4
) on a two-dimensional bounded irregular domain
\Lambda
. We outline the essential steps to integrate the FEM on uniform grids with the Go technique, dubbed as Go-FEM, as follows.

(i)

Triangulate
\Lambda
into an unstructured simplicial mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}
that fits or approximately fits
\Lambda
(see the blue mesh). Overlay it with a background uniform mesh
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
(see the green mesh) of a rectangular domain
\Omega\supset\Lambda.

(ii)

Construct the transfer matrix
{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega}
that maps data from the unstructured mesh
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}
to the background grid
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega}
, e.g., the piecewise linear interpolation in
[
22
, (3.3)]
.

(iii)

Compute the nonlocal stiffness matrix
\bm{S}_{\Omega}
on the uniform grids
\mathcal{R}_{\Omega},
which should allow for fast matrix-vector multiplication.

(iv)

Form the Go-FEM solver as in
[
22
, (1.2)]
on
\mathcal{T}_{\Lambda}
:

\mathbb{D}_{\Lambda}^{-1}(\mathbb{I}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega})^{\top}\,\bm{S}_{\Omega}\,{\mathbb{T}}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega}\,{\bm{u}}_{\Lambda}=\bm{f}_{\Lambda},

(1.6)

where
\bm{u}_{\Lambda}
denotes the numerical solution on
\Lambda
, and
\mathbb{D}_{\Lambda}
is diagonal with entries given by the column sums of
\mathbb{T}_{\Lambda}^{\Omega}
, which ensures a consistent, constant-preserving transfer.

In
[
22
,
23
]
, Step-(iii) involves a finite difference solver for fractional Laplacian, and the proposed Go-FD can take full advantages of both uniform-grid solvers in efficient matrix-vector multiplications and unstructured meshes for complex geometries and mesh adaptation. Along this line, it is essential to compute the nonlocal stiffness matrix on uniform grids. This serves as the purpose this paper and we shall report the full Go-FEM for nonlocal models in the future work
[
24
]
.

We show in this paper that by leveraging the convolution structure of B-spline basis functions, the original
2d
-dimensional integrals in the bilinear form (see (
2.4
) below) can be reduced to
d
-dimensional integrals.
More specifically, consider a uniform partition of
\Omega
with mesh size
h_{j}
along the
x_{j}
-direction and
1\leq j\leq d
, and define the
C^{0}
piecewise linear tensor-product finite element nodal basis functions as
\{\phi_{{\bm{n}}}({\bm{x}})=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{n_{j}}(x_{j})\}_{1\leq n_{j}\leq N_{j}}.

Our main finding is that the entries of the stiffness matrix can be expressed as

\begin{split}(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}(\phi_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})-\phi_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}}))(\phi_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})-\phi_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}}))\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{x}}\\
&=\frac{h^{d}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}f_{{\bm{k}}}({\bm{s}})\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{s}},\;\;\;\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),\;\;\;k_{j}=|n_{j}-m_{j}|,\end{split}

(1.7)

where the integrand
f_{{\bm{k}}}({\bm{s}})
is a linear combination of B-spline functions (see Theorem
2.1
).
This result effectively reduces the computational complexity inherent in the Galerkin-type weak formulation to the level of strong-form discretizations, thereby greatly simplifying the overall algorithmic structure. Moreover, the integrand
f_{\bm{k}}({\bm{x}})
has a zero of multiplicity two at the origin (see Proposition
3.1
), which makes it computationally tractable and suitable for both integrable and hypersingular kernels. Similar strategy has already been applied to fractional Laplacian equations
[
35
]
, where, in two dimensions, the fourfold integral reduces to a one-dimensional integral.
Second, for
\delta>0
, the resulting nonlocal FEM stiffness matrix is a block-Toeplitz matrix with sparse Toeplitz blocks, whose bandwidth increases with
\delta
. This structure enables fast matrix-vector multiplications via FFT-based algorithms.
Third, for a uniform mesh with element size
h_{j}\equiv h
, when
\delta\leq h
, the entries of the generating matrix associated with the hypersingular kernel (
1.4
) in two and three dimensions can be computed exactly. This result reveals that, from the perspective of the stiffness matrix, the nonlocal formulation asymptotically recovers the classical stiffness matrix for Laplace operators as
\delta\to 0
. To the best of our knowledge, this is a new observation not previously reported in the literature. In the case
\delta>h
, the singularity of the hypersingular kernel near the origin can be exploited to split the integral into singular and regular parts for more efficient computation.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we derive the explicit integral representation for the entries of the stiffness matrix for nonlocal model (
1.1
) in arbitrary dimensions, and exploit the Toeplitz structure of the matrix and the two limiting cases with respect to the parameter
\delta
. The detailed implementation for calculation of the entries matrix is introduced in section
3
.
In section
4
, ample numerical results for nonlocal problems with both regular and hypersingular kernels, including accuracy testing and limiting behaviors are presented to demonstrate the efficiency and accuracy. We conclude the paper with some remarks.

2.2.
B-splines and some useful properties

In the computation of the nonlocal stiffness matrix, we mainly use the relation between finite element bases and B-splines, where the convolution properties of the B-splines are essential for the derivation.

It is known that the cardinal B-splines can be defined recursively by convolution (see e.g.,
[
7
,
31
]
).

Definition 2.1

(
Cardinal B-splines).

The cardinal B-spline of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
is defined via convolution

B_{p}(t)=\big(B_{p-1}\ast B_{0}\big)(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{p-1}(t-s)B_{0}(s)\,{\rm d}s=\int_{0}^{1}B_{p-1}(t-s)\,{\rm d}s,\quad p\geq 1,

(2.14)

where the cardinal B-spline of degree
0
is the characteristic function of the interval
[0,1),
i.e.,

B_{0}(t)=1
for
t\in[0,1)
and vanishes elsewhere on
\mathbb{R}.

We have the following important properties.

Lemma 2.1
.

The cardinal B-spline
B_{p}(t)
of degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
has the properties as follows.

(i)

{\rm sup}(B_{p})\subseteq I_{p}:=[0,p+1]
, and its a piecewise polynomial of degree
p-1
on each subinterval
[k,k+1]
with
k=0,\cdots p.
Globally,
B_{p}\in C^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}).

(ii)

For
t\in I_{p}
and
p\geq 2,

\displaystyle B_{p}(t)=\frac{t}{p-1}B_{p-1}(t)+\frac{p-t}{p-1}B_{p-1}(t-1);\quad B_{p}^{\prime}(t)=B_{p-1}(t)-B_{p-1}(t-1).

(2.15)

(iii)

It is symmetric on the interval
I_{p},
i.e.,

B_{p}\Big(\frac{p+1}{2}+t\Big)=B_{p}\Big(\frac{p+1}{2}-t\Big)\quad{\rm or}\quad B_{p}(t)=B_{p}(p+1-t).

(2.16)

(iv)

For
p,q\geq 0,

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\!B_{p}(t+s)B_{q}(s)\,{\rm d}s=B_{p+q+1}(p+1-t)=B_{p+q+1}(q+1+t).\end{split}

(2.17)

Proof.

The proofs of the first three properties can be found in
[
7
,
31
]
. Here, we sketch the proof of the convolution formula (iv), which will be mainly used for the derivation of the nonlocal stiffness matrix. Let
\widehat{B}_{p}(\omega)
be the Fourier transform of
B_{p}(t).
Then by the definition (
2.14
), we have

\widehat{B}_{p}(\omega)=\big(\widehat{B}_{0}(\omega)\big)^{p+1}
and
\widehat{B}_{p+q+1}(\omega)=\widehat{B}_{p}(\omega)\cdot\widehat{B}_{q}(\omega)

for
p,q\geq 0.
Thus, applying Fourier inverse transform, we have

\begin{split}B_{p+q+1}(t)=(B_{p}*B_{q})(t)=\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{p}(t-s)B_{q}(s)\,{\rm d}s=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{p}(p+1-t+s)B_{q}(s)\,{\rm d}s,\end{split}

where in the last step, we used (
2.16
) for
B_{p}.
Substituting
t
by
p+1-t,
we obtain the first identity of (
2.17
), and the second identity is a direct consequence of (
2.16
).
∎

It is known that the finite element basis in (
2.6
)
can be expressed in
B_{1},
i.e.,

\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j})=B_{1}\Big(\frac{x_{j}-\xi_{n_{j}-1}}{h_{j}}\Big),\quad B_{1}(t)=\begin{cases}t,\;\;&t\in[0,1),\\
2-t,\;\;&t\in[1,2),\\
0,\;\;&\text{elsewhere,}\end{cases}

(2.18)

where
\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}
is the zero extension of
{\phi}_{n_{j}}
to
\mathbb{R}.
In the late part, we also need to use the cubic spline given by

B_{3}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{t^{3}}{6},\;\;&t\in[0,1),\\
-\frac{t^{3}}{2}+2t^{2}-2t+\frac{2}{3},\;\;&t\in[1,2),\\
\frac{t^{3}}{2}-4t^{2}+10t-\frac{22}{3},\;\;&t\in[2,3),\\
-\frac{(t-4)^{3}}{6},\;\;&t\in[3,4),\\
0,\;\;&\text{elsewhere.}\end{cases}

(2.19)

2.3.
Nonlocal FEM stiffness matrix

In what follow, we restrict our attention to high dimensional cube with
h_{1}=\cdots=h_{d}=h
.
We have the following integral representations of the entries of the nonlocal stiffness matrix.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\{\phi_{\bm{n}}(\bm{x})\}
be the tensorial FEM basis defined in
(
2.6
)-(
2.7
), and
B_{3}(t)
be the cubic splines given in (
2.19
). Then
the entries of the nonlocal stiffness matrix
\bm{S}_{\delta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{*}\times N_{*}}
with
N_{*}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}N_{j}
,
are given by

(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}(\phi_{\bm{n}},\phi_{\bm{m}})=\frac{h^{d}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}f_{{\bm{k}}}({\bm{s}})\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}},

(2.20)

where

\begin{split}f_{{\bm{k}}}({\bm{s}})=2\prod^{d}_{j=1}B_{3}(k_{j}+2)-\prod^{d}_{j=1}B_{3}(k_{j}+2-s_{j}/h)-\prod^{d}_{j=1}B_{3}(k_{j}+2+s_{j}/h),\end{split}

(2.21)

with
\bm{k}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}),k_{j}=|n_{j}-m_{j}|,
and the local-to-global indices:

\begin{split}l=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(n_{i}-1)\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}N_{j}\Big)+1,\quad l^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(m_{i}-1)\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}N_{j}\Big)+1.\end{split}

(2.22)

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{n}}}({\bm{x}})
be the zero extension of
\phi_{{\bm{n}}}({\bm{x}})
on
\widetilde{\Omega}=\Omega\cup\Omega_{\delta}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and likewise for
\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}}).

Then we derive from (
2.13
) that

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}(\phi_{{\bm{n}}},\phi_{{\bm{m}}})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\big(\phi_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})-\phi_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}})\big)\big(\phi_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})-\phi_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}})\big)\,{\rm d}{\bm{x}}\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(2\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}})\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}})-\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}})-\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{n}}({\bm{x}})\tilde{\phi}_{\bm{m}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})\big)\,{\rm d}{\bm{x}}\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{d}_{\delta}}\big(g_{1}-g_{2}({\bm{s}})-g_{3}({\bm{s}})\big)\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}},\end{split}

(2.23)

where

\begin{split}&g_{1}=2\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{n}}}({\bm{x}})\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{m}}}({\bm{x}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{x}}=2\prod_{j=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j})\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j},\\
&g_{2}({\bm{s}})=\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{n}}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{m}}}({\bm{x}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{x}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j}+s_{j})\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j},\\
&g_{3}({\bm{s}})=\!\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{n}}}({\bm{x}})\tilde{\phi}_{{\bm{m}}}({\bm{x}}+{\bm{s}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{x}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j})\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j}+s_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j}.\end{split}

(2.24)

Using the change of variable
y=\tfrac{x_{j}-\xi_{n_{j}-1}}{h}
, we find from (
2.18
) readily that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j})\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j}&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{1}\Big(\frac{x_{j}-\xi_{n_{j}-1}}{h}\Big)B_{1}\Big(\frac{x_{j}-\xi_{m_{j}-1}}{h}\Big)\,{\rm d}x_{j}\\
&=h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{1}(y)B_{1}\Big(y+\frac{\xi_{n_{j}-1}-\xi_{m_{j}-1}}{h}\Big)\,{\rm d}y\\
&=h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{1}(y)B_{1}\big(y+n_{j}-m_{j}\big)\,{\rm d}y\\[6.0pt]
&=h\,B_{3}(n_{j}-m_{j}+2)=h\,B_{3}(m_{j}-n_{j}+2),\end{split}

(2.25)

where we used the property (
2.17
) including
B_{3}(2+t)=B_{3}(2-t)

to derive the last two identities. Similarly, with the same change of variable, we obtain from (
2.17
)-(
2.18
) that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j}+s_{j})&\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{1}\Big(\frac{x_{j}+s_{j}-\xi_{n_{j}-1}}{h}\Big)B_{1}\Big(\frac{x_{j}-\xi_{m_{j}-1}}{h}\Big)\,{\rm d}x_{j}\\
&=h\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}B_{1}\Big(y+\frac{s_{j}+\xi_{m_{j}-1}-\xi_{n_{j}-1}}{h}\Big)B_{1}(y)\,{\rm d}y\\[4.0pt]
&=hB_{3}\big(m_{j}-n_{j}+2+s_{j}/h\big)=hB_{3}\big(n_{j}-m_{j}+2-s_{j}/h\big),\end{split}

(2.26)

and

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j})&\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j}+s_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\tilde{\phi}_{n_{j}}(x_{j}-s_{j})\tilde{\phi}_{m_{j}}(x_{j})\,{\rm d}x_{j}\\
&=hB_{3}\big(n_{j}-m_{j}+2+s_{j}/h\big)=hB_{3}\big(m_{j}-n_{j}+2-s_{j}/h\big).\end{split}

(2.27)

Thus, we derive from (
2.24
)-(
2.27
) that

\begin{split}g_{1}-g_{2}({\bm{s}})-g_{3}({\bm{s}})=h^{d}\Big(2\prod^{d}_{j=1}B_{3}(k_{j}+2)-\prod^{d}_{j=1}B_{3}(k_{j}+2-s_{j}/h)-\prod^{d}_{j=1}B_{3}(k_{j}+2+s_{j}/h)\Big),\end{split}

where

k_{j}=|n_{j}-m_{j}|.
Denoting

f_{\bm{k}}(\bm{s}):=(g_{1}-g_{2}(\bm{s})-g_{3}(\bm{s}))/h^{d}
,
we derive (
2.20
)-(
2.21
) from
(
2.23
) and the above immediately.
∎

It is seen from the above theorem that (i) the computation of the entries reduces to integrations over the ball of interaction;
and (ii) by
(
2.19
),
B_{3}
has a support
[0,4),
so the sparsity and bandwidth of
\bm{S}
depends on
\delta.
We next illustrate this and provide more explicit formulas in two and three dimensions.

2.3.1.
Two-dimensional case

With the ordering in (
2.12
), we can obtain the following block Toeplitz structure of the stiffness matrix along with expressions for their entries.

Corollary 2.1

(
2D nonlocal stiffness matrix).

If
\delta>0
, the nonlocal FEM stiffness matrix
\bm{S}_{\delta}
is an
N_{2}
-by-
N_{2}
block-Toeplitz matrix of the form

\bm{S}_{\delta}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{T}_{0}&\hskip-6.0pt\bm{T}_{1}&\cdots&\bm{T}_{N_{2}-2}&\;\;\bm{T}_{N_{2}-1}\\[1.0pt]
\bm{T}_{1}&\hskip-6.0pt\bm{T}_{0}&\ddots&\ddots&\bm{T}_{N_{2}-2}\\[0.0pt]
\vdots&\hskip-6.0pt\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\[-1.0pt]
\bm{T}_{N_{2}-2}&\ddots&\ddots&\bm{T}_{0}&\bm{T}_{1}\\[5.0pt]
\bm{T}_{N_{2}-1}&\;\;\bm{T}_{N_{2}-2}&\cdots\hskip-2.0pt&\bm{T}_{1}&\bm{T}_{0}\end{bmatrix},

(2.28)

where each block of
\bm{S}_{\delta}
is an
N_{1}
-by-
N_{1}
Toeplitz matrix and the entries are given by

\bm{T}_{k_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{0}^{k_{2}}&\hskip-4.0ptt_{1}^{k_{2}}&\cdots&t_{N_{1}-2}^{k_{2}}&t_{N_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}\\[1.0pt]
t_{1}^{k_{2}}&\hskip-4.0ptt_{0}^{k_{2}}&\ddots&\ddots&t_{N_{1}-2}^{k_{2}}\\[0.0pt]
\vdots&\hskip-4.0pt\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\[-1.0pt]
t_{N_{1}-2}^{k_{2}}&\ddots&\ddots&t_{0}^{k_{2}}&t_{1}^{k_{2}}\\[5.0pt]
t_{N_{1}-1}^{k_{2}}&t_{N_{1}-2}^{k_{2}}&\cdots\hskip-2.0pt&t_{1}^{k_{2}}&t_{0}^{k_{2}}\end{bmatrix},

(2.29)

for
0\leq k_{2}\leq N_{2}-1.

Here, for each
k_{2},
the entries in the first row of the generating matrix
\bm{T}_{k_{2}}
can be evaluated in polar coordinates by

t_{k_{1}}^{k_{2}}=h^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big(\int_{0}^{\delta}r\rho_{\delta}(r)\,\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(r,\theta)\,{\rm d}r\Big)\,{\rm d}\theta,\quad 0\leq k_{1}\leq N_{1}-1,

(2.30)

where

\begin{split}\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(r,\theta)&=2B_{3}(k_{1}+2)B_{3}(k_{2}+2)-B_{3}(k_{1}+2+r\cos\theta/h)B_{3}(k_{2}+2+r\sin\theta/h)\\[1.0pt]
&\quad-B_{3}(k_{1}+2-r\cos\theta/h)B_{3}(k_{2}+2-r\sin\theta/h).\end{split}

(2.31)

Proof.

The formulas in
(
2.20
)- (
2.21
) with
d=2
reduce to

\begin{split}(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}(\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}},\phi_{m_{1}}\phi_{m_{2}})&=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}_{\delta}}f_{k_{1},k_{2}}({\bm{s}})\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}},\end{split}

(2.32)

where

\begin{split}f_{k_{1},k_{2}}({\bm{s}})=&\,2B_{3}(k_{1}+2)B_{3}(k_{2}+2)-B_{3}(k_{1}+2-s_{1}/h)B_{3}(k_{2}+2-s_{2}/h)\\
&-B_{3}(k_{1}+2+s_{1}/h)B_{3}(k_{2}+2+s_{2}/h),\end{split}

(2.33)

with the local-to-global indices
l=(n_{2}-1)N_{1}+n_{1}
and
l^{\prime}=(m_{2}-1)N_{1}+m_{1}
.
We continue the evaluation using polar coordinates, and using (
2.32
) and the above leads to

\begin{split}(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}(\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}},\phi_{m_{1}}\phi_{m_{2}})&=\frac{h^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f_{k_{1},k_{2}}(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\,r\rho_{\delta}(r)\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}r\\
&=h^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{\pi}f_{k_{1},k_{2}}(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)\,r\rho_{\delta}(r)\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}r\\
&=h^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{\pi}\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(r,\theta)\,r\rho_{\delta}(r)\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}r:=t^{k_{2}}_{k_{1}},\end{split}

(2.34)

where
\hat{f}(r,\theta)
is defined in (
2.31
) and we used the fact that the integrand is even in
\theta
.

It remains to show that the nonlocal stiffness matrix
\bm{S}_{\delta}
with this ordering leads to the block Toeplitz matrix in (
2.28
)-(
2.29
). To this end, we denote the entries of the
N_{1}N_{2}
-by-
N_{1}N_{2}
stiffness matrix
\bm{S}_{\delta}
in terms of
n_{1},n_{2},m_{1},m_{2}
by
s^{n_{1},n_{2}}_{m_{1},m_{2}}.
In view of
s^{n_{1},n_{2}}_{m_{1},m_{2}}=(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}

and the definitions of
l,l^{\prime}
,
we have

\bm{S}_{\delta}=\begin{bmatrix}s_{1,1}^{1,1}&s_{1,1}^{2,1}&\cdots&s_{1,1}^{N_{1},1}&\cdots&s_{1,1}^{1,N_{2}}&s_{1,1}^{2,N_{2}}&\cdots&s_{1,1}^{N_{1},N_{2}}\\[6.0pt]
s_{2,1}^{1,1}&s_{2,1}^{2,1}&\cdots&s_{2,1}^{N_{1},1}&\cdots&s_{2,1}^{1,N_{2}}&s_{2,1}^{2,N_{2}}&\cdots&s_{2,1}^{N_{1},N_{2}}\\[6.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[6.0pt]
s_{N_{1},1}^{1,1}&s_{N_{1},1}^{2,1}&\cdots&s_{N_{1},1}^{N_{1},1}&\cdots&s_{N_{1},1}^{1,N_{2}}&s_{N_{1},1}^{2,N_{2}}&\cdots&s_{N_{1},1}^{N_{1},N_{2}}\\[6.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\[6.0pt]
s_{1,N_{2}}^{1,1}&s_{1,N_{2}}^{2,1}&\cdots&s_{1,N_{2}}^{N_{1},1}&\cdots&s_{1,N_{2}}^{1,N_{2}}&s_{1,N_{2}}^{2,N_{2}}&\cdots&s_{1,N_{2}}^{N_{1},N_{2}}\\[6.0pt]
s_{2,N_{2}}^{1,1}&s_{2,N_{2}}^{2,1}&\cdots&s_{2,N_{2}}^{N_{1},1}&\cdots&s_{2,N_{2}}^{1,N_{2}}&s_{2,N_{2}}^{2,N_{2}}&\cdots&s_{2,N_{2}}^{N_{1},N_{2}}\\[6.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[6.0pt]
s_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{1,1}&s_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{2,1}&\cdots&s_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{N_{1},1}&\cdots&s_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{1,N_{2}}&s_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{2,N_{2}}&\cdots&s_{N_{1},N_{2}}^{N_{1},N_{2}}\end{bmatrix}.

Then by (
2.34
), we have

s^{n_{1},n_{2}}_{m_{1},m_{2}}=(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}=t_{k_{1}}^{k_{2}}=t_{|n_{1}-m_{1}|}^{|n_{2}-m_{2}|},

which implies
\bm{S}_{\delta}
is identical to the block Toeplitz matrix in (
2.28
)-(
2.29
). This completes the proof.
∎

In view of the above block Toeplitz structure, the matrix-vector multiplication can be performed via FFT in
O(N_{1}N_{2}\log N_{1}N_{2})
operations. Next, we present several important properties of the 2D stiffness matrix.

(i)  Sparsity of
\bm{S}_{\delta}.
Since
{\rm support}(B_{3})\subseteq[0,4]
, one verifies readily that
if
(\max\{j,k\}+2)-\delta/h\geq 4,
then
B_{3}(j+2)B_{3}(k+2)=0,
and

B_{3}(j+2\pm r\cos\theta/h)B_{3}(k+2\pm r\sin\theta/h)=0,\;\;\;\forall(r,\theta)\in(0,\delta)\times(0,\pi).

(2.35)

Thus, for given
0\leq k_{2}\leq N_{2}-1,
the
N_{1}
entries
\{t_{k_{1}}^{k_{2}}\}_{k_{1}=0}^{N_{1}-1}
that generate the Teoplitz matrix
\bm{T}_{k_{2}}
are given by

t_{k_{1}}^{k_{2}}=\begin{cases}h^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi}\Big(\int_{0}^{\delta}r\rho_{\delta}(r)\,\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(r,\theta)\,{\rm d}r\Big)\,{\rm d}\theta,\quad&0\leq k_{1},k_{2}<\delta/h+2,\\
0,\quad&\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}\geq\delta/h+2,\end{cases}

(2.36)

where
\{\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2}}\}
are defined in (
2.31
).
Moreover, there holds the symmetry property:

t_{k_{1}}^{k_{2}}=t_{k_{2}}^{k_{1}},\quad\forall\,k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0.

(2.37)

Consequently, the Toeplitz matrix
\bm{T}_{k}
is sparse with a band-width that increases as the interaction radius
\delta
increases.

(ii)  Limit of
\bm{S}_{\delta}
as
\delta\to 0^{+}.

With the above formulas at our disposal, we consider the limiting case
\delta\to 0,
and show that with the choice of corresponding kernel, we are able to derive the siffness matrix of the usual Laplacian operator.
More precisely, choose

\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)=\frac{C_{\delta}^{\alpha}}{|{\bm{s}}|^{2+\alpha}},\;\;\;\;\alpha\in[-1,2),

(2.38)

where we take
C_{\delta}^{\alpha}={2(2-\alpha)\delta^{\alpha-2}}/\pi

so that
\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}_{\delta}}|{\bm{s}}|^{2}\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|){{\rm d}}{\bm{s}}=1.

For
0\leq\delta\leq h,
we can evaluate (
2.36
) directly and find the non-zero entries as flollows

\displaystyle t_{0}^{0}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big(\frac{-1}{3\pi(\alpha-6)}\delta^{4}+\frac{32}{15\pi(\alpha-5)}\delta^{3}-\frac{1}{\alpha-4}\delta^{2}-\frac{64}{9\pi(\alpha-3)}\delta\Big)+\frac{8}{3},

\displaystyle t_{1}^{0}=t_{0}^{1}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big(\frac{2}{9\pi(\alpha-6)}\delta^{4}-\frac{56}{45\pi(\alpha-5)}\delta^{3}+\frac{1}{2(\alpha-4)}\delta^{2}+\frac{40}{27\pi(\alpha-3)}\delta\Big)-\frac{1}{3},

\displaystyle t_{1}^{1}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big(\frac{-4}{27\pi(\alpha-6)}\delta^{4}+\frac{32}{45\pi(\alpha-5)}\delta^{3}-\frac{1}{4(\alpha-4)}\delta^{2}+\frac{32}{27\pi(\alpha-3)}\delta\Big)-\frac{1}{3},

\displaystyle t_{2}^{0}=t_{0}^{2}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big(\frac{-1}{18\pi(\alpha-6)}\delta^{4}+\frac{8}{45\pi(\alpha-5)}\delta^{3}-\frac{16}{27\pi(\alpha-3)}\delta\Big),

\displaystyle t_{2}^{1}=t_{1}^{2}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{27\pi(\alpha-6)}\delta^{4}-\frac{4}{45\pi(\alpha-5)}\delta^{3}-\frac{4}{27\pi(\alpha-3)}\delta\Big),

\displaystyle t_{2}^{2}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{108\pi(\alpha-6)}\delta^{4}\Big),

which implies

\lim\limits_{\delta\to 0^{+}}\bm{S}_{\delta}=\bm{S}_{0}:=\bm{S}_{h}\otimes\bm{M}_{h}+\bm{M}_{h}\otimes\bm{S}_{h},

(2.39)

where

\bm{S}_{h}=\frac{1}{h}{\rm diag}(-1,2,-1)
and
\bm{M}_{h}=\frac{h}{6}{\rm diag}(1,4,1).
It is identical to the usual FEM stiffness matrix.

2.3.2.
Three-dimensional case

In 3D case, with the ordering in (
2.13
), we can obtain the following block Toeplitz structure of the stiffness matrix along with expressions for their entries.

Corollary 2.2

(
3D nonlocal stiffness matrix).

If
\delta>0

has the following Toeplitz-structure:

\bm{S}_{\delta}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},0}&\hskip-6.0pt\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},1}&\cdots&\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},N_{3}-2}&\;\;\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},N_{3}-1}\\[1.0pt]
\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},1}&\hskip-6.0pt\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},0}&\ddots&\ddots&\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},N_{3}-2}\\[0.0pt]
\vdots&\hskip-6.0pt\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\[-1.0pt]
\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},N_{3}-2}&\ddots&\ddots&\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},2}&\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},1}\\[5.0pt]
\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},N_{3}-1}&\;\;\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},N_{3}-2}&\cdots\hskip-2.0pt&\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},1}&\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},0}\end{bmatrix},

where for

, the block matrix is given by

\bm{T}_{x_{1},x_{2},k_{3}}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{T}_{x_{1},0,k_{3}}&\hskip-6.0pt\bm{T}_{x_{1},1,k_{3}}&\cdots&\bm{T}_{x_{1},N_{2}-2,k_{3}}&\;\;\bm{T}_{x_{1},N_{2}-1,k_{3}}\\[1.0pt]
\bm{T}_{x_{1},1,k_{3}}&\hskip-6.0pt\bm{T}_{x_{1},0,k_{3}}&\ddots&\ddots&\bm{T}_{x_{1},N_{2}-2,k_{3}}\\[0.0pt]
\vdots&\hskip-6.0pt\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\[-1.0pt]
\bm{T}_{x_{1},N_{2}-2,k_{3}}&\ddots&\ddots&\bm{T}_{x_{1},0,k_{3}}&\bm{T}_{x_{1},1,k_{3}}\\[5.0pt]
\bm{T}_{x_{1},N_{2}-1,k_{3}}&\;\;\bm{T}_{x_{1},N_{2}-2,k_{3}}&\cdots\hskip-2.0pt&\bm{T}_{x_{1},1,k_{3}}&\bm{T}_{x_{1},0,k_{3}}\end{bmatrix},

with each block of
\bm{T}_{x_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}
is an
N_{1}
-by-
N_{1}
symmetric Toeplitz matrix and their entries are

\bm{T}_{x_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{0,k_{2},k_{3}}&\hskip-6.0ptt_{1,k_{2},k_{3}}&\cdots&t_{N_{1}-2,k_{2},k_{3}}&\;\;t_{N_{1}-1,k_{2},k_{3}}\\[1.0pt]
t_{1,k_{2},k_{3}}&\hskip-6.0ptt_{0,k_{2},k_{3}}&\ddots&\ddots&t_{N_{1}-2,k_{2},k_{3}}\\[0.0pt]
\vdots&\hskip-6.0pt\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\[-1.0pt]
t_{N_{1}-2,k_{2},k_{3}}&\ddots&\ddots&t_{0,k_{2},k_{3}}&t_{1,k_{2},k_{3}}\\[5.0pt]
t_{N_{1}-1,k_{2},k_{3}}&\;\;t_{N_{1}-2,k_{2},k_{3}}&\cdots\hskip-2.0pt&t_{1,k_{2},k_{3}}&t_{0,k_{2},k_{3}}\end{bmatrix},

for
k_{2}=0,1,\cdots,N_{2}-1
and
k_{3}=0,1,\cdots,N_{3}-1
. The entries of generating matrix
\bm{G}=(t_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}})
can be evaluated in spherical coordinates by

t_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}=\frac{h^{3}}{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}(r,\theta,\varphi)\sin(\theta)\,{\rm d}\varphi{\rm d}\theta\,\rho_{\delta}(r)r^{2}\,{\rm d}r,

(2.40)

where

\begin{split}&\hat{f}_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}(r,\theta,\varphi)=\,2B_{3}(k_{1}+2)B_{3}(k_{2}+2)B_{3}(k_{3}+2)\\

(2.41)

Proof.

Using the general results in Theorem

with

\begin{split}(\bm{S}_{\delta})_{ll^{\prime}}={\mathcal{A}}_{\delta}(\phi_{n_{1}}\phi_{n_{2}}\phi_{n_{3}},\phi_{m_{1}}\phi_{m_{2}}\phi_{m_{3}})&=\frac{h^{3}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{3}_{\delta}}f_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}({\bm{s}})\,\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)\,{\rm d}{\bm{s}},\end{split}

(2.42)

where

\begin{split}f_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}}({\bm{s}})=&\,2B_{3}(k_{1}+2)B_{3}(k_{2}+2)B_{3}(k_{3}+2)\\
&-B_{3}(k_{1}+2-s_{1}/h)B_{3}(k_{2}+2-s_{2}/h)B_{3}(k_{3}+2-s_{3}/h)\\
&-B_{3}(k_{1}+2+s_{1}/h)B_{3}(k_{2}+2+s_{2}/h)B_{3}(k_{3}+2+s_{3}/h),\end{split}

(2.43)

and the local-to-global indices

3.
Fast and accurate computation of the stiffness matrix

In this section, we describe the implementation details of the efficient algorithm for computing the entries of the generating matrix, denoted as
\{t_{{\bm{k}}}\}_{{\bm{k}}\in\Theta_{N}}
, where
the index set
\Theta_{N}:=\big\{{\bm{n}}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\,:\,0\leq n_{j}\leq N_{j}-1,\;1\leq j\leq d\big\}.

To fix the idea, we focus on the hypersingular kernel
\rho_{\delta}(|{\bm{s}}|)
defined in (
1.4
), as the implementation of regular kernels is simpler.

3.1.
Computation of
t_{k_{1},\cdots,k_{d}}

We first introduce the
d
-dimensional spherical coordinates:

\begin{split}&x_{1}=r\cos\theta_{1};\;x_{2}=r\sin\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2};\;\cdots\cdots;\;x_{d-1}=r\sin\theta_{1}\cdots\sin\theta_{d-2}\cos\theta_{d-1};\\
&x_{d}=r\sin\theta_{1}\cdots\sin\theta_{d-2}\sin\theta_{d-1},\;\;r=|{\bm{x}}|,\;\;\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{d-2}\in[0,\pi],\;\;\theta_{d-1}\in[0,2\pi],\end{split}

(3.1)

and the spherical volume element is

\begin{split}{\rm d}{\bm{x}}=r^{d-1}\sin^{d-2}\left(\theta_{1}\right)\sin^{d-3}\left(\theta_{2}\right)\cdots\sin\left(\theta_{d-2}\right){\rm d}r\,{\rm d}\theta_{1}\,{\rm d}\theta_{2}\cdots{\rm d}\theta_{d-1}:=r^{d-1}{\rm d}r\,{\rm d}\sigma(\hat{\bm{x}}).\end{split}

(3.2)

We also define the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:r=|{\bm{x}}|=1\}
.
Using the spherical coordinates, we denote
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})=f_{{\bm{k}}}({\bm{x}})
. Then, we present a critical property of the integrand in (
2.20
), showing that
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})
at
r=0
is a zero with multiplicity two, which plays a decisive role in handling hypersingular integrals, i.e., the kernel function (
1.4
).

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})\big|_{r=0}=\partial_{r}\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})\big|_{r=0}=0.\end{split}

(3.3)

Moreover, if
|{\bm{k}}|_{\infty}\geq\delta/h+2
, we have

\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})=0,\;\;{\rm for\;all}\;(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})\in(0,\delta]\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

(3.4)

Proof.

The two-dimensional case corresponding to (
3.4
) is given in (
2.35
), and the tensor-product structure yields the extension to desired result in arbitrary dimensions.
Therefore, we only need to prove (
3.3
).
As a direct consequence of (
3.4
), we find that for any
|{\bm{k}}|_{\infty}\geq 3,

\begin{split}\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})=0,\;\;\text{for all}\;\;(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})\in(0,h]\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\end{split}

so we only need to consider the remaining case with
0\leq k_{j}\leq 2
,
1\leq j\leq d
, that includes the
3^{d}
special cases.
We establish the result by a function-by-function enumeration. The complete set of formulas
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})
for the two-dimensional case (
d=2
) is provided in the Appendix
A
. For
d\geq 3
, the proof is analogous and relies on explicit analytic expressions for the B-splines. As the details are lengthy and introduce no new ideas, they are omitted here.
∎

It is evident that when
\delta\leq h
, the entries of generating matrix
t_{\bm{k}}
with hypersingular kernel (
1.4
) can be computed exactly. Therefore, our focus will be on the case
\delta>h
.
By the virtue of the singularities of the hypersingular kernels at origin, we split the integral (
2.20
) into

\begin{split}t_{{\bm{k}}}=&\frac{h^{d}}{2}\int_{0}^{h}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}}){\rm d}\sigma(\hat{\bm{x}})\Big)\,\rho_{\delta}(r)r^{d-1}\,{\rm d}r\\
&+\frac{h^{d}}{2}\int_{h}^{\delta}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}}){\rm d}\sigma(\hat{\bm{x}})\Big)\,\rho_{\delta}(r)r^{d-1}\,{\rm d}r:=\mathcal{S}_{{\bm{k}}}+\mathcal{R}_{{\bm{k}}}.\end{split}

(3.5)

Note that when
\delta\leq h
, it reduces to

\begin{split}t_{\bm{k}}=\frac{h^{d}}{2}\int_{0}^{\delta}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}}){\rm d}\sigma(\hat{\bm{x}})\Big)\,\rho_{\delta}(r)r^{d-1}\,{\rm d}r:=\mathcal{S}_{\bm{k}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

We compute
\mathcal{S}_{{\bm{k}}}
by evaluating the explicit expression of
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})
on

, where

, and we compute
\mathcal{R}_{{\bm{k}}}
using a suitable Legendre–Gauss quadrature. Note that for more complex kernel functions, we can also leverage the analytical expression of

\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})
to facilitate the computation of

.

and

. The black mesh indicates the radial scale for the circular or spherical integration region. Moreover,

is piecewise cubic on this black mesh, and within each grid cell it is cubic B-spline along each coordinate direction.

3.1.1.
Computation of the singular part of the integral

We first consider the computation of the singular integral
\mathcal{S}_{{\bm{k}}}
.
Proposition
3.1
implies that
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})=r^{2}\tilde{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})
, then this integrand is capable of dealing with the hypersingular kernels (
1.4
). More precisely, we plugging the kernel (
1.4
) into (
3.5
) to obtain that

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\bm{k}}=c_{d,\delta}^{\alpha}h^{d-\alpha}\int_{0}^{h}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\tilde{f}_{\bm{k}}(r,\hat{{\bm{x}}})\,{\rm d}\sigma(\hat{{\bm{x}}})\Big)r^{1-\alpha}\,{\rm d}r,\end{split}

(3.7)

where we only need to replace the upper limit of the integral in the
r
-direction with
\delta
when

.
Hence, with the explicit representation of
\tilde{f}_{\bm{k}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})
, the integrals
\{\mathcal{S}_{\bm{k}}\}
can be evaluated explicitly without any truncation errors.
Moreover, due to the symmetry properties, it is unnecessary to compute the entire set
\{\mathcal{S}_{{\bm{k}}}\}_{{\bm{k}}\in\Theta_{2}}
that is
3^{d}
elements.
To streamline this process, we introduce the following index set that excludes the symmetric elements

\widetilde{\Theta}_{N}=\big\{{\bm{n}}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\,:\,0\leq n_{j}\leq n_{j+1}\leq N,\;1\leq j\leq d-1\big\}.

It is clear that

is a subset of

\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}
contains only

For instance, in the 2D case, we only need to compute the following

\{\mathcal{S}_{{\bm{k}}}\}_{{\bm{k}}\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}=\{\mathcal{S}_{0,0},\mathcal{S}_{1,1},\mathcal{S}_{2,2},\mathcal{S}_{0,1},\mathcal{S}_{0,2},\mathcal{S}_{1,2}\},

and in the 3D case, we only need to compute the following
10

\{\mathcal{S}_{{\bm{k}}}\}_{{\bm{k}}\in\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}}=\{\mathcal{S}_{0,0,0},\mathcal{S}_{1,1,1},\mathcal{S}_{2,2,2},\mathcal{S}_{0,0,1},\mathcal{S}_{0,0,2},\mathcal{S}_{0,1,1},\mathcal{S}_{1,1,2},\mathcal{S}_{0,2,2},\mathcal{S}_{1,2,2},\mathcal{S}_{0,1,2}\}.

As a result, we highlight that the number of elements needing computation is reduced from

to

3.1.2.
Computation of the regular part of the integral

Before we turn to the regular integral
\mathcal{R}_{{\bm{k}}}
, we first show the regularity of the integrand
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})
with
r>h
. Let
\Lambda=I_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, with
I_{r}=(h,\delta)
. Note that, in this subsection, we assume that
\delta\geq h
, otherwise
\mathcal{R}_{{\bm{k}}}=0
.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
{\bm{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, the integrand
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})\in C^{2}(\Lambda)
, with
\Lambda=(h,\delta)\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
. Thus
\hat{f}_{{\bm{k}}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})\in H^{3}(\Lambda)
,
where
H^{3}(\Lambda)
is the usual Sobolev space.

Proof.

In view of (
2.19
), it is easy to check that
B_{3}(t)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, by the chain rule, one verifies readily that
\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}(r,\hat{\bm{x}})\in C^{2}(\Lambda)
. The second regularity result is a direct consequence of the first one. This ends the proof.
∎

With the above regularity, it is sufficient to use Legendre-Gauss quadrature to approximate
\mathcal{R}_{{\bm{k}}}
. More precisely,

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{{\bm{k}}}\approx\mathcal{R}^{\ast}_{{\bm{k}}}:=\frac{h^{d}}{2}\sum_{p=0}^{N_{r}}\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{N_{\theta_{1}}}\cdots\sum_{\ell_{d-1}=0}^{N_{\theta_{d-1}}}\check{f}_{\bm{k}}(r_{p},\theta_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,\theta_{\ell_{d-1}})\rho_{\delta}(r_{p})r_{p}^{d-1}\,\omega_{p}^{r}\omega_{\ell_{1}}\cdots\omega_{\ell_{d-1}},\end{split}

(3.8)

where

\check{f}_{\bm{k}}(r_{p},\theta_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,\theta_{\ell_{d-1}})=\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}(r_{p},\theta_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,\theta_{\ell_{d-1}})\sin^{d-2}(\theta_{\ell_{1}})\sin^{d-3}(\theta_{\ell_{2}})\cdots\sin(\theta_{\ell_{d-2}}).

In the above,
\{r_{p},\omega^{r}_{p}\}_{p=0}^{N_{r}}
,
\{\theta_{\ell_{j}},\omega_{\ell_{j}}\}_{\ell_{j}=0}^{N_{j}}
,
0\leq j\leq d-1
denote the Legendre-Gauss quadrature nodes and weights in
(h,\delta)
and
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
, respectively.

Let
I=(a,b).

For any
u\in C(I),
we define the interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{N}:\,C(I)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{N}
such that

\mathcal{I}_{N}u(x_{j})=u(x_{j}),\quad 0\leq j\leq N,

(3.9)

where
\{x_{j}\}
denote the Legendre-Gauss quadrature points over
I,
and
\mathcal{P}_{N}
denote the set of all algebraic polynomials of degree
\leq N
.
According to
[
34
, Theorem 3.41]
, if
\partial_{x}^{m}u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{m}}(I)
with
m\geq 1
and
\omega^{m}(x)=(b-x)^{\frac{m}{2}}(x-a)^{\frac{m}{2}}
, we have

\|\mathcal{I}_{N}u-u\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq cN^{-m}\|\partial_{x}^{m}u\|_{L^{2}_{\omega^{m}}(I)},

(3.10)

where
c
is a positive constant independent of
m
,
N
and
u
. Note that, in (
3.10
), we converted the standard interval
(-1,1)
to
I=(a,b)
by using the linear transformation.

We define the
d
-dimensional interpolation
\mathcal{I}^{d}_{N}:=\mathcal{I}_{N_{r}}\!\circ\mathcal{I}_{N_{\theta_{1}}}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{I}_{N_{\theta_{d-1}}}:C(\Lambda)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{N_{r}}\times\mathcal{P}_{N_{\theta_{1}}}\times\cdots\times\mathcal{P}_{N_{\theta_{d-1}}}
, which satisfies

\mathcal{I}^{d}_{N}u(r_{p},\theta_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,\theta_{\ell_{d-1}})=u(r_{p},\theta_{\ell_{1}},\cdots,\theta_{\ell_{d-1}}),\quad 0\leq p\leq N_{r},\;0\leq\ell_{j}\leq N_{\theta_{j}},\;1\leq j\leq d-1,

(3.11)

where
\mathcal{I}_{N_{r}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{N_{\theta_{j}}}
denote the one dimensional interpolations in
r
- and spherical-direction as in (
3.9
), respectively.

To describe the error more precisely, we introduce the space
B^{m}(\Lambda)
for
m\geq d
with the semi-norm and norm

\begin{split}|u|_{B^{m}(\Lambda)}=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{{\bm{m}}\in\mathcal{E}_{j}}\|\partial^{\bm{m}}_{\bm{x}}u\|^{2}_{\omega^{m_{j}{\bm{e}}_{j}}}\Big)^{1/2},\;\;\;\|u\|_{B^{m}(\Lambda)}=\Big(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Lambda)}+|u|^{2}_{B^{m}(\Lambda)}\Big)^{1/2},\end{split}

where

\mathcal{E}_{j}=\Big\{{\bm{m}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}:d\leq m_{j}\leq m;\;m_{i}\in\{0,1\},i\neq j;\;\sum_{k=1}^{d}m_{k}=m\Big\}.

In the above,

Numerical results and concluding remarks

In this section, we consider several examples of PDEs with nonlocal operator and show the accuracy and efficiency of the algorithm for computing the FEM stiffness matrix. We conduct convergence tests employing both two- and three-dimensional contrived solutions, with both given exact solution and right hand source function.

4.3.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we develop a novel finite element method for solving nonlocal Laplacian equations on bounded domains in multiple dimensions. By exploiting the convolution property of B-spline basis functions, we show that the entries of the FEM stiffness matrix, which correspond to the nonlocal Laplacian with nonlocal homogeneous boundary conditions on rectangular meshes, can be explicitly represented as
d
-dimensional integrals over finite intervals. This formulation enables the accurate and flexible computation of matrix entries to any prescribed level of accuracy. As a byproduct, for the case
\delta\geq h
, we derive explicit expressions for the stiffness matrix entries corresponding to hypersingular kernels. Extensive numerical results are presented to verify the optimal accuracy and computational efficiency of the proposed method. In addition, by combining it with the grid-overlay technique proposed in
[
22
,
23
]
, the method can be readily extended to problems defined on complex geometries.

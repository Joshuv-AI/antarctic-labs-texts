Utility of Extracellular Nicotinamide Phosphoribosyl Transferase as a Novel Biomarker in Predicting Early Severe Organ Dysfunction and Mortality in Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome: A Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Shwethapriya Rao, Thejesh Srinivas, Vishwas Parampalli, Pratibha Todur, Ashritha A Udupa, Shruthi Rao, Souvik Chaudhuri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimNicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase (NAMPT) is an upstream cytozyme (cytokine plus enzyme) with unique features, having intracellular NAMPT (iNAMPT) and extracellular NAMPT (eNAMPT) components. Genetic associations and therapeutic inhibition highlight its potential as both a biomarker and therapeutic target. We aimed to study the utility of eNAMPT as a predictor of severe organ dysfunction and its association with mortality in acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS).Patients and methodsThis is a single-center, prospective observational study involving 90 patients with ARDS. We noted plasma eNAMPT levels, oxygenation levels, inflammatory markers, lung ultrasound scores, driving pressures, and echocardiography parameters. Severity of organ dysfunction and ICU mortality were the outcomes.ResultsPlasma eNAMPT was found to be a predictor of severe organ dysfunction in ARDS [adjusted OR: 1.343, 95% CI (1.105–1.634), p-value 0.003]. The cut-off eNAMPT level of ≥ 4.38 ng/mL was used to predict early severe organ dysfunction, with an AUC of 0.752, 95% CI (0.647–0.857), p-value < 0.001, sensitivity of 71.4%, and specificity of 79.2%. Plasma eNAMPT was significantly higher in non-survivors [4.53 (4–9.98) ng/mL] as compared to survivors [3.76 (3.40–4.29) ng/mL] (p-value < 0.001). eNAMPT level ≥4.175 ng/mL was associated with higher mortality (hazard ratio: 3.82; 95% CI: 2.010–7.276, p-value < 0.001) and a shorter median survival time [5 days vs. 16 days (Log-rank (Mantel-Cox) p-value < 0.001].ConclusionPlasma eNAMPT (≥4.38 ng/mL) predicts early severe organ dysfunction in ARDS patients. Highlights

Plasma extracellular nicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase (eNAMPT) is a reliable predictor of early onset severe organ dysfunction in ARDS patients. The plasma eNAMPT was significantly greater among non-survivors as compared to the survivors. High plasma eNAMPT levels were associated with marked hypoalbuminemia.

Introduction

Despite therapeutic advances in care, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is associated with severe organ dysfunction and a high mortality of up to 60%.1,2

In fact, it is the severe organ dysfunction in ARDS that contributes to mortality, rather than the severity of hypoxemia.3 ARDS involves dysregulated inflammation across organs, alveolar capillary interface dysfunction, and oxidative stress.4 Thus, novel therapeutic strategies focusing on targeted specific molecular mediators of inflammation may improve outcomes.4 Nicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase (NAMPT) has a few unique properties, which make it a topic of interest for research in ARDS.5–7

First, NAMPT is a cytozyme (cytokine plus enzyme), exhibiting a unique dual function determined by its cellular localization, whether intracellular (iNAMPT) or extracellular (eNAMPT).4 The iNAMPT or the “enzyme component,” helps in protective cellular metabolism, critical for stress adaptation.6,7 In contrast, eNAMPT, “the cytokine component,” amplifies inflammatory signaling through Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR-4), drives cytokine release, endothelial dysfunction, and organ injury in critical illness.8

Second, eNAMPT is an upstream inflammatory trigger, controlling the release of downstream mediators like CRP, procalcitonin, and interleukins (IL), and thus, it may detect injurious signaling earlier before the downstream rise of ILs.7–9

Third, therapeutic strategies targeting eNAMPT with monoclonal antibodies to neutralize it and reduce inflammatory lung injury have shown benefit in preclinical models.9–12 Fourth, eNAMPT increases the permeability oedema in ARDS and also leads to renal, cardiac, and hepatic organ dysfunction.7,13 Thus, neutralization of eNAMPT has shown a reduction in lung and renal injury in preclinical models.7,13

Severe organ dysfunction is a key outcome measure in ARDS research, and therefore, we planned to study it as an outcome of interest.14,15 Sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score ≥ 11 is associated with a high mortality of about 80–90%, and may be considered as severe organ dysfunction.16,17

We aimed to determine the utility of plasma eNAMPT in prognosticating early (24–48 hours of ARDS) severe organ dysfunction and in-hospital mortality in ARDS patients.

The primary objective was to determine the utility of plasma eNAMPT in predicting early severe organ dysfunction (SOFA score ≥11) on the second day of ARDS.

The secondary objective was to determine the association of plasma eNAMPT with in-hospital mortality in patients with ARDS.

Patients and Methods

This manuscript has been written as per the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.

Study Design

This was a single-center, prospective observational study conducted at a Medical College, where ARDS patients were consecutively recruited between November 2024 and July 2025. The study was initiated after obtaining due ethical clearance from the institutional ethics committee (IEC 356/2023) and was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI/2024/01/061829). The patient's accepted representative provided written informed consent in accordance with the institution's protocol. The consecutive sampling method was used for recruitment.

Sample Size

This study was conducted as a predefined objective within a prospectively enrolled ARDS cohort (n = 90). To assess whether the available sample size provided adequate precision for evaluating the diagnostic performance of eNAMPT in identifying patients with severe organ dysfunction by day 2 of ARDS onset, we estimated the standard error of the area under the curve (AUC) using the Hanley and McNeil method.18,19 With an expected AUC of 0.80 for eNAMPT to correctly distinguish ARDS patients with severe organ dysfunction on day 2 of ARDS (based on its ability to distinguish ARDS from healthy controls in literature with AUC 0.86), and a reported prevalence of severe organ dysfunction of 45% (n1 ≈ 41 with dysfunction, n0 ≈ 49 without), the variance of the AUC estimate was 0.002460, yielding SE or standard error of estimated AUC ≈ 0.050. This corresponded to a 95% confidence interval of approximately 0.71–0.89. The interval was considered sufficiently precise to provide a clinically meaningful estimate of eNAMPT's discriminatory ability; therefore, the available sample size (n = 90) was deemed appropriate.

Methods

On the day of intensive care unit (ICU) admission, the age, gender, co-morbid illnesses, acute physiology, and chronic health evaluation II (APACHE II) score, SOFA score, modified nutrition risk in the critically ill (mNUTRIC) score, and ARDS severity were recorded. Between day 24 and 48 hours of ARDS, the worst values of partial pressure of arterial oxygen to fraction of inspired oxygen (PaO2/FiO2) ratio, SOFA score for evaluating the severity of organ dysfunction (on day two of ARDS), procalcitonin (PCT), C-reactive protein (CRP), total leucocyte count (TLC), serum albumin, arterial blood lactate, CRP-albumin ratio, presence of acute kidney injury (AKI) was noted, along with driving pressure (DP) of ventilator and lung ultrasound score with echocardiography parameters. The plasma eNAMPT was analyzed with plasma samples from day two of ARDS onset. The parameters were noted on day two of ARDS because risk stratification due to the evolution of organ dysfunction in ARDS is more accurate on day two or day three of ARDS rather than at diagnosis.20 The patients were followed up for the need for prone position ventilation, presence of carbapenem-resistant infection during ICU stay, length of ICU stay, terminal discharges (against medical advice), and in-hospital mortality.

Sample Collection and Analysis of eNAMPT Levels

Blood samples from ARDS patients were collected in EDTA-coated tubes on the second day of the ARDS diagnosis. The samples were centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 15 minutes at room temperature, and the plasma was separated and stored at −80°C until analysis. Plasma eNAMPT levels were quantified using a commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Aliquot Biotech). The assay had a sensitivity of 0.06 ng/mL and a detection range of 0.16–10 ng/mL. The intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation were <8% and <10%, respectively, indicating high precision and reproducibility. The kit also demonstrated high specificity, with no significant cross-reactivity or interference observed between human eNAMPT or visfatin and its analogues. The assay readings were obtained at a wavelength of 450 nm.

Statistical Analysis

Data analysis was conducted using IBM Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS), version 29 (Armonk, NY, USA). The distribution of continuous variables was tested for normality using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Normally distributed variables were reported as mean with standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed variables were summarized as median with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical data were presented as counts and percentages. Patients with ARDS were stratified into severe and non-severe organ dysfunction and survival and non-survival groups. Continuous variables were compared between groups using the independent Student's t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test. The association of categorical variables was examined with the Chi-square test.

Variables of significance were included in logistic regression analysis. Initially, univariate logistic regression was applied to assess mortality prediction, and results were presented as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and p-values. Significant predictors on univariate analysis were further considered for multivariable logistic regression, while avoiding issues of multicollinearity and maintaining model simplicity. The discriminative ability of the predictors was further evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, which included calculating the AUC, optimal cut-off point, sensitivity, specificity, 95% CI, and statistical significance. The same tests were performed for the prediction of mortality (n = 75 patients, as 15 patients underwent terminal discharge, which is sometimes prevalent in ICUs).21

Survival probability over time, based on identified independent predictors, was assessed using Kaplan–Meier survival curves, and group comparisons were performed with the log-rank (Mantel–Cox) test. Additionally, Cox proportional hazards regression was used to estimate hazard ratios for mortality prediction. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The recruitment and data collection of the 90 ARDS patients are depicted in Figure 1. The demographic variables, APACHE II score, SOFA score, mNUTRIC score, along with the clinical variables of the patients, plasma eNAMPT levels, and mortality outcomes are depicted (Table 1). The comparison of the variables between the severe and non-severe organ dysfunction groups showed that PCT, plasma eNAMPT levels, arterial lactate level, and DP of the ventilator were significantly higher in the severe organ dysfunction group, whereas plasma albumin level was significantly lower in the severe organ dysfunction group (Table 2). The univariate analysis revealed that serum albumin level, PCT, plasma eNAMPT, arterial lactate level, and DP were significant predictors of early severe organ dysfunction (Table 3). The multivariable logistic regression analysis revealed that plasma eNAMPT, arterial lactate, and DP were the independent predictors of ARDS patients with early severe organ dysfunction on day two of ARDS, and the adjusted OR, 95% CI, and p-values are depicted (Table 3). The cut-off plasma eNAMPT to predict early severe organ dysfunction was ≥4.38 ng/mL, AUC 0.752, Youden's index 0.506, 95% CI (0.647–0.857), p-value < 0.001 (Fig. 2). Among the 40 patients who had high plasma eNAMPT levels (≥4.38 ng/mL), 30 (75%) had severe organ dysfunction on day two of ARDS, whereas among the 50 patients with lower eNAMPT levels (<4.38 ng/mL), only 21 (24%) had severe organ dysfunction (Chi-square p-value < 0.001) (Fig. 3A). The median (IQR) plasma eNAMPT values in ARDS patients with severe early organ dysfunction were 6.46 (4.19–9.98) ng/mL as compared to 3.95 (3.47–4.32) ng/mL in those with non-severe organ dysfunction (Fig. 3B). The sensitivity of high plasma eNAMPT (≥4.38 ng/mL) to predict severe organ dysfunction was 71.4%, specificity 79.2%, positive predictive value 75%, negative predictive value 75%, a positive likelihood ratio of 3.00, a negative likelihood ratio of 0.5, and diagnostic accuracy 76%.

The in-hospital mortality outcomes were analyzed in 75 patients. The comparison between survivors and non-survivors regarding clinical parameters revealed that serum albumin levels were lower in the non-survivors. In contrast, plasma eNAMPT, arterial lactate level, DP, SOFA score, APACHE II score, and the mNUTRIC score were higher in non-survivors than in survivors of ARDS (Supplemental Digital Content Table 1). Univariate and multivariable regression analysis revealed that plasma eNAMPT, along with DP and mNUTRIC score, were predictors of mortality (Supplemental Digital Content Table 2). The ROC curve depicted an AUC of 0.746 and an eNAMPT cut-off ≥4.175 ng/mL to predict mortality (Supplemental Digital Content Fig. 1). The Kaplan-Meier survival plot showed significant differences in survival distributions between patients above and below the cut-off eNAMPT value (≥4.175 ng/mL) (Supplemental Digital Content Fig. 1). The hazard ratio of plasma eNAMPT ≥4.175 ng/mL to predict mortality was 3.821, p-value < 0.001, 95% CI (2.010–7.265). Among patients with eNAMPT levels ≥4.175 ng/mL, 81.6% were non-survivors, whereas those with levels <4.175 ng/mL had a 43.2% non-survival rate (Chi-square p-value < 0.001).

Discussion

Extracellular nicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase was found to be a reliable predictor of early severe organ dysfunction (day 2) in ARDS, consistent with its role as an upstream damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) that activates TLR4-mediated inflammatory signaling, a pathway central to the pathogenesis of both ARDS and sepsis.7,13 Extracellular nicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase is a key mediator of multi-organ dysfunction in critical illness through its pleiotropic proinflammatory effects. In the lung, eNAMPT binds to TLR4 and amplifies cytokine release and vascular permeability, promoting lung injury. In the heart, it contributes to myocardial depression through inducible nitric oxide synthase/nitric oxide (iNOS/NO) overproduction, while in the kidney, it triggers proximal tubular inflammation and apoptosis via nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) activation and oxidative stress, leading to acute kidney injury (AKI). Hepatic dysfunction results from eNAMPT-induced Kupffer cell activation and IL-6-mediated hepatocyte injury. Systemically, eNAMPT functions as a DAMP via TLR4, driving cytokine storm, septic shock, and sepsis-associated encephalopathy. Genetic studies have also linked NAMPT promoter polymorphisms (G-1535A and C-948A) with increased eNAMPT expression and greater ARDS severity. In line with its central pathogenic role, neutralizing monoclonal antibodies against eNAMPT are currently under investigation, showing protective effects against lung injury, systemic inflammation, and acute kidney injury in preclinical models.22–29

Among emerging ARDS biomarkers, eNAMPT has shown promise as an integrative marker reflecting inflammatory and endothelial injury pathways. In a multicohort study, circulating eNAMPT levels were significantly elevated in ARDS and other critical illnesses and demonstrated good discriminatory performance for ARDS identification (AUC ≈ 0.85) as well as additive prognostic value when incorporated into multi-biomarker panels.8 Compared with other established biomarkers, soluble receptor for advanced glycation end-products (sRAGE) primarily reflects alveolar epithelial injury and has consistently correlated with ARDS severity, ventilator dependence, and mortality.30 Similarly, angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2) serves as a marker of endothelial activation and permeability. Elevated and rising plasma levels of Ang-2 have been strongly associated with mortality and progression of respiratory failure, with a pooled odds ratio comprising 10 prospective cohort studies of approximately 1.5 (95% CI: 1.30–1.89) for high vs. low concentrations in a systematic review.31,32 IL-6 represents the systemic inflammatory axis of ARDS biology. Although single-time-point IL-6 shows poor discrimination, the rate of change of IL-6 on the third day from the time of admission is robustly linked to mortality and reflects ongoing cytokine-driven injury (adjusted OR: 12.812; 95% CI: 2.104–78.005).33 Surfactant protein-D (SP-D) on day 1 of sepsis induced ARDS has been associated with higher odds of mortality (OR: 1.52; 95% CI: 1.03–2.24, p-value = 0.03).34 This is possible because SP-D, which is released from alveolar type-II cells, and its high levels in blood indicate alveolar-epithelial injury. It can also differentiate direct lung injury from indirect lung injury.35

Collectively, these findings position eNAMPT as a complementary biomarker rather than a replacement for existing indicators, mapping uniquely to TLR4-NF-κB–mediated inflammatory and endothelial-epithelial injury pathways. Integration of eNAMPT with canonical markers such as sRAGE, Ang-2, IL-6, and SP-D may thus enhance the multidimensional characterization of ARDS pathobiology and improve prognostic precision when applied within serial or composite biomarker frameworks. The significance of eNAMPT in ARDS has been emphasized by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), both as a diagnostic (for future trial enrolment) and prognostic (response to a particular therapy) biomarker in ARDS.36,37 Apart from plasma eNAMPT, BAL fluid levels of eNAMPT are significantly elevated in patients with ARDS and correlate with the disease severity. Thus, BAL eNAMPT is also a clinically significant biomarker for ARDS outcomes.5

Our results also suggest that eNAMPT is a predictor of ARDS outcome. The results are similar to those of Bime et al., where the authors found that eNAMPT showed promise in indicating mortality in ARDS.36 In another study by Lee et al. on sepsis patients, those with mortality had significantly higher levels of eNAMPT.38 The median plasma eNAMPT level was 2.53 (1.07–8.16) ng/mL in non-survivors as compared to 1.44 (0.84–2.81) ng/mL in survivors.38 However, in the study, the authors found that after regression analysis, eNAMPT was not an independent predictor of mortality.38 The study concluded that eNAMPT is a marker of both disease severity and poor sepsis outcomes.38 Consistent with this, we also found eNAMPT to predict both severe organ dysfunction and mortality outcomes in our study. The mortality rate of ARDS was high in our study, which could be attributed to multiple causes. In our ARDS cohort, 62.2% had moderate, and 24.4% had severe ARDS. Notably, 67% of patients included in our mortality analysis had a PaO2/FiO2 ratio < 150 mm Hg, a threshold that has been associated with worse outcomes. In an Indian cohort of ARDS patients, those with a PaO2/FiO2 ratio < 150 mm Hg demonstrated a significantly higher odds of mortality (OR: 1.6; 95% CI: 1.1–2.4).39 About one-third of patients had a high CCI score ≥5, indicating a considerable comorbidity burden, and 60% of those with a CCI score ≥5 expired. Furthermore, 42% of ARDS patients acquired carbapenem-resistant infection (CRI) during their ICU stay, and mortality among these patients was 71%. The overall in-hospital mortality of 62.6% observed in our cohort likely reflects the combined impact of a high proportion of moderate-to-severe ARDS, substantial comorbidity burden, and a notable prevalence of CRI. To ensure methodological rigor, patients who were terminally discharged (n = 15) were excluded from the in-hospital mortality analysis.

Extracellular nicotinamide phosphoribosyl transferase was found to be a potentially useful biomarker not only in the ARDS cohort, but also in clinical conditions that may lead to ARDS, such as sepsis, trauma, and acute pancreatitis.8 Circulating eNAMPT holds value in ARDS by enabling earlier detection for clinical trials in the future, particularly those evaluating interventions directed at this pathway.8,12,40 In the study by Bime et al., the authors did not have access to organ dysfunction scores, such as SOFA scores, and therefore were unable to compare the eNAMPT values for organ dysfunction severity classification.8 We were able to determine that eNAMPT values can also be used for the severity of multi-organ dysfunction in ARDS.

Sammani et al. studied neutralizing monoclonal antibodies targeting eNAMPT in porcine models.13 The best studied is ALT-100 (also known as ASN100 or eNAMPT mAb), at a dose of 0.4 mg/kg, 2 hours or 6 hours after lung injury initiation, which has shown strong efficacy in preclinical models.13 In the study, administration of the ALT-100 monoclonal antibody ensured pulmonary fluid homeostasis, lower BAL protein and tissue albumin levels, improved lung wet/dry weight ratios, and reductions in ultrasound-detected B lines as well as opacities in radiography evidence.13 These findings highlight that therapeutic antibody neutralization of eNAMPT is a viable therapeutic ARDS strategy.13

We had a novel finding that there was a significant association between patients with high eNAMPT levels ≥4.175 ng/mL and marked hypoalbuminemia (plasma albumin <2.5 mg/dL). This could be due to the vascular leak triggered by eNAMPT-mediated endothelial injury, which promotes the transcapillary escape of albumin into the alveolar and interstitial spaces, thereby further lowering circulating levels.41 The inverse relationship between inflammatory markers like CRP and PCT with Plasma albumin has also been found in recent literature in sepsis patients.42 This clinical finding may be important, as it will alert clinicians to evaluate albumin levels in patients with high eNAMPT in the future, as albumin has important physiological functions like antioxidant and free radical scavenging, along with inhibition of platelet aggregation. Marked or severe hypoalbuminemia, defined as plasma albumin <2.5 gm/dL, also leads to altered protein binding of antimicrobials, leading to treatment failures.43

Strengths and Limitations

Our study had particular strengths. We tried to determine the utility of the important and unique cytozyme, eNAMPT, in predicting early severe organ dysfunction and outcomes in ARDS. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study elucidating the role of eNAMPT in severe organ dysfunction in ARDS from India. We employed a prospective study design and attempted to address potential confounders using regression analysis to determine their role as an independent predictor of both primary and secondary outcomes. We compared eNAMPT with inflammatory markers and indices, such as CRP, PCT, and the CRP/albumin ratio. We also found that marked hypoalbuminemia is significantly associated with high eNAMPT levels.

There were several limitations. This was a single-center study with a small sample size. This study was not powered to assess the utility of plasma eNAMPT in predicting mortality outcomes. Our study included only patients with ARDS who were on IMV. This design choice ensured a homogeneous cohort with comparable disease severity and ventilatory parameters. However, it may limit the external validity of our findings, as a substantial proportion of ARDS patients in contemporary practice are initially managed with non-invasive ventilation (NIV) or high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC). Consequently, our results primarily reflect outcomes in the more severe spectrum of ARDS. This approach is consistent with prior landmark ARDS trials such as the ARDSNet low-tidal-volume and PROSEVA studies, which also enrolled only invasively ventilated patients.44,45 Another limitation was that plasma eNAMPT levels were measured only at a single time point (day 2), where serial measurements could have provided additional insight into the temporal behavior of eNAMPT and its relationship with disease progression and outcomes. Evaluating biomarker trajectories may offer a more accurate reflection of underlying pathophysiologic changes and prognostic trends. Excluding patients who received terminal discharges (n = 15) represents an additional limitation. These patients usually had refractory septic shock and multi-organ dysfunction, and withdrawal of aggressive therapy reflected a clinically irreversible trajectory. Their exclusion, while necessary to maintain an objectively verifiable outcome of in-hospital mortality, may have introduced selection and informative censoring bias, potential inaccuracies in estimating the true in-hospital mortality risk. The relation between BAL and plasma eNAMPT was not studied.

Conclusion

Plasma eNAMPT is a predictor of early severe organ dysfunction and mortality in ARDS patients and is associated with mortality. Marked hypoalbuminemia (<2.5 gm/dL) is associated with high eNAMPT levels in ARDS patients.

Assessing the impact of spatial coherence on the sinusoidal linear Fresnel zone plate’s depth of focus
Authors: Maryam Farahiyan, Rasoul Aalipour
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research examines, using both theory and experimentation, how a light beam’s spatial coherence affects a sinusoidal linear Fresnel zone plate’s depth of focus. We generate a one-dimensional partially coherent Gaussian Schell-model beam from a coherent laser beam through putting a rotating diffuser near to the common focal plane of two cylindrical lenses in a 2f-system. By adjusting the beam spot size on the diffuser, one may modify the coherence width of the produced beam. Our findings demonstrate that when the coherence width of the partly coherent beam drops, the zone plate’s depth of focus increases. Not only is it claimed that the experimental results validate the theoretical conclusions, but a mathematical link is also established between the experimental parameter used to manipulate the light coherence and the theoretically obtained coherence width. This topic has many applications in the field of optical, microscopy, optical trapping, imaging systems and biomedical.Subject terms: Optics and photonics, Physics

Introduction

The ability of a light beam to interfere with a delayed or spatially shifted version of itself is considered as coherence. This property of the light changes as the light beam propagates through space1. So, coherence is closely related to interference and diffraction phenomena. Coherent light sources in ideal imaging systems are usually point or linear, while in practice, each light source has a spatial extent, causing the light produced by these sources to not be spatially coherent. Therefore, the study of incoherent and partially coherent light beams is a significant topic of research related to imaging, measuring and diagnostic systems2–11. In this regard, various methods have been developed to measure spatial coherence, among which Young’s double slits experiment and its generalized models are significant in terms of history and their developing applications1. Also, in the last decade, several spatial coherence measurement methods based on the analysis of diffraction patterns have been presented12,13. We have also recently presented a method for measuring spatial coherence using phase step diffraction patterns and its application in measuring source width14.

In order to conduct deeper studies on partial coherent sources, we need to know how the properties of the beams change for different sources. These light sources are found in nature or easily produced in the laboratory. One of the common sources that produces partial coherent light is the Gaussian Schell-model source. The Gaussian Schell-model is defined by a Gaussian mean intensity function and a normalized Gaussian correlation or degree of coherence function2. Simulations and experimental methods of producing such beams have been studied by many researchers from different perspectives15–23.

The properties of the Gaussian Schell-model light beams in the focus of various lenses, especially the study of their depth of focus (DOF) have attracted the attention of some researchers24. DOF is a lens optics concept that measures the tolerance of placement of the image plane to the lens2. It has been studied in various literature in terms of imaging, optical microscopy, digital camera, optical metrology, bio-medical, and so on25–30. As we know from geometrical optics, the image of an object is not ideal due to the existence of aberrations in imaging systems. This is because the DOF of the lens, which determines the longitudinal range of image formation, is strongly influenced by the aberrations of the optical system31,32. So in optical systems and especially in optical microscopes, depth of focus and elimination of aberrations are important issues.

One of the ways to avoid the aberration effects is to use the diffractive lenses instead of the refracted lenses. Fresnel zone plate (FZP) is a type of diffractive lens that includes a wide range of spatial frequencies, and can produce multiple foci. In recent years, FZPs have been used in systems that are difficult or impossible to perform by refractive optics33. FZP is composed of transparent and opaque zones. When the light beams are diffracted from the transparent zones, they interfere constructively on the plane perpendicular to the optical axis passing through the center of the plate and form the maximum intensity at the focal point of the FZP. FZPs are made in various shapes such as circular, square, petal, and linear. The linear Fresnel zone plate (LFZP) acts like a cylindrical lens and produces a linear focus34,35.

To consider FZPs, the coherent light beams have often been used, and less attention has been paid to the light beams with low coherence36. In this paper, we study the focusing properties of a sinusoidal linear Fresnel zone plate (SLFZP) under illumination of a partially coherent light beam. Specifically, we measure the DOF of the SLFZP and establish a relationship between the DOF of the zone plate and the transverse coherence width of an illuminated Schell-model partially coherent beam. The paper consists of two main theoretical and experimental sections, in each of which the requirements of the study and the results are given.

Experiment

Procedure of experiment

To prepare the SLFZP, we used the method of printing on transparent film41. We designed this zone plate with a focal length of 300 mm for an illuminating beam with a wavelength of 633 nm using MATLAB software. Then, print it on transparent film using a phonix2250 printer with a resolution of 3600dpi.

For generating the 1D-GSM beam, we arranged a 2f-system using two cylindrical lenses under illumination of the parallel beam of a He–Ne laser. after passing through the cylindrical lens, we have 1D- light beam. Next, installed a rotating diffuser near the common focal plane of the lenses42–44. In order to change the spatial coherence width of the beam, we controlled the beam spot size on the diffuser. This work was done by changing the diffuser distance from the common focal plane of the lenses.

According to Fig. 4, the Gaussian beam of a He–Ne laser with the wavelength of 633 nm is expanded using a beam expander to fit the dimensions of the provided SLFZP. Then, we generate a 1D-GSM beam by arranging the desired 2f-system composed of two cylindrical lenses with the focal lengths of and installing the rotating diffuser, mounted on an optical rail, at distance from the common focal plane of the cylindrical lenses. Therefore, by changing the distance , the size of the light spot covered by the diffuser is controlled and so a 1D-GSM beam with different coherent widths is generated. The prepared SLFZP is placed after the second lens. The cylindrical lenses were chosen so that their focal length is large enough so that the Gaussian beam that illuminates the zone plate has a relatively large width. After the SLFZP, a CCD camera ( pixels with pixel size) is placed on an optical rail until the camera moves easily around the focal plane of the SLFZP and records the intensity patterns.

Figure 8 shows that the DOF of the SLFZP increases with the diffuser distance as an exponential function. If we want to justify this behavior, we must consider the generation method of the spatially partial coherent beam in our experimental setup and the theoretical results in the previous section. As shown in Fig. 4, we generated the 1D-GSM beam using a rotating diffuser and controlled the coherence width of the beam by changing the distance of the diffuser from the common focal plane of the cylindrical lenses. It is easy to understand that by increasing the distance of the diffuser from the focal plane of the two lenses, the incident rays to the diffuser cover a larger cross-sectional area of the diffuser. In other words, the light rays experience more points of the rotating diffuser. As a result, the spatial correlation of the incident rays to different points due to the inhomogeneity of these points decreases relative to each other.

Conclusion

Under the light of a spatially partial coherent Gaussian Schell model beam, the depth of focus of a linear sinusoidal Fresnel zone plate is examined. Our results demonstrate that when exposed to such a uniformly distributed beam of light, the zone plate’s depth of focus increases as spatial coherence decreases. We found a mathematical link between the coherence width and the effective experimental parameter in the coherence change, in addition to the experimental data validating the theoretical research. This article’s findings can be applied to improve optical trapping efficiency for tiny systems and biomedical applications under irradiation by incoherent light.

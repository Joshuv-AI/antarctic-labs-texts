Introduction

Data collected from healthcare, finance, etc., may reveal private data, such as basic personal information, patient medical history information, economic information, and so on. Once these data are unauthorized disclosure, the life and property of individuals will be threatened seriously. Therefore, federated learning (FL) has become a popular machine learning paradigm for big data nowadays, which provides a potentially robust framework for automated perception and reasoning. Of course, if a good learning framework is to be built1, massive datasets should also be sufficiently trained to build their perception and inference potential. With much data accumulated, a large amount of associated privacy may be exposed inevitably; for instance, there is a potential risk of data privacy leakage in the learning process by requiring the data owner to provide the raw data or share the training parameters of their local dataset.

The privacy leakage of parameters/models in federated learning or similar repeated observations is known to worsen with an increasing number of observations, as evidenced by experience learned. For this class of repeated and continuous observations, a range of privacy-preserving methods can be chosen, such as obfuscation circuits2, inadvertent transmission3, secret sharing4, homomorphic encryption5, and differential privacy (DP)6. Each of these protection methods possesses distinct advantages; however, after comparing and analyzing, differential privacy is chosen in this paper to provide privacy protection for the model under study. The conventional approach to ensuring privacy in this domain involves setting the number of total training or learning first, then add perturbation in the training process continuously and calculate privacy account by an invariant way after the whole training learning process. The method chosen in this paper is described as follows: fix the overall privacy budget first, which can ensure the privacy leakage within a specific range more reliably. Subsequently, in the training process, these parameters will be “used” continuously until they are exhausted, and the observation will be forced to stop.

Federated learning is, essentially, a form of distributed machine learning, which can solve the challenge of current computing devices have difficulty executing in real-time with limited computing power and storage space, due to the distributed machine learning models. This approach enables big data-based joint learning by enabling multiple data owners to perform machine learning locally using their own datasets and then sharing their local model parameters to obtain a global model. Although, in the above description, the client does not need to provide or upload private data to the cloud server, its uploaded weights or gradients still inevitably contain the relevant features from both the dataset and model. Currently, it has been shown in7that an attacker can observe the information uploaded by the clients, and thus infer the client’s private information through a membership inference attack8or a model inversion attack9, which will cause a privacy breach. It is known that the research on distributed privacy-preserving data mining is mainly based on cryptographic methods such as cryptographically secure aggregation protocols2,10,11, Secure Multi-party Computation (SMC)12, Homomorphic Encryption (HE)5, and data perturbation-based methods such as DP13.

Differential privacy is a concept with a strict mathematical definition proposed by Dwork in 2006, and it has unique advantages; It defines a rigorous attack model that is independent of background knowledge and not only quantitative expression violations, but also protects personal information during learning process. Consequently, in this paper, we adopt differential privacy to protect the privacy leakage problem in federated learning. However, researchers have widely approved and applied the concept of DP until 2012, and it has been studied in various scenarios that requiring privacy protection in recent years. For example, after Abadi M introduced the Moment Accounting (MA)14algorithm in 2016, differential privacy found applications in deep learning; researchers have proposed various ways to deep learning and studied them from every angle, as well as proposed various extensions to the concept of differential privacy and along with corresponding mathematical proofs. Nevertheless, many mechanisms fail to achieve the security they claim(e.g15,16), such as the in some literature, the definition of sensitivity mentioned in17 has been extended by transforming global sensitivity to local sensitivity, which leads to privacy leakage.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. The background is reviewed in Section 2. The related definition of differential privacy and federated learning is presented in Section 3. Section 4 proposes a new algorithm, and some improvements are made. Section 5 contains experimental results on a real dataset and concludes with a summary in Section 6.

Background

Due to the advantages of DP, its integration with deep learning warrants further investigation, as it has already been used in many practical products and services. For example, Google has applied differential privacy to its Chrome browser to collect user data; Apple has applied differential privacy to its newly released iPhones to protect personal information; and the U.S. National Bureau of Statistics has announced that it is using differential privacy technology to release 2020 census data toward enhance the protection of citizens’ privacy. However, these applications are rough uses of DP algorithm, and performance deteriorates as more data and users are added, so further research on DP and deep learning is needed.

The integration of differential privacy and deep learning is now branched into two main directions. One is focuses on further study the concept of differential privacy itself, particularly its extension in the context of deep learning, to mitigate the sharp and fast consumption of the privacy budget during iterative overlay process. MA14, Rényi Differential Privacy (RDP)18, and F-DP19are the most recognized algorithms in this category. These breakthroughs aim to examine the original mass repetition process from various angles, then produce a more favorable privacy-utility trade-off and tighter bounds when they are composed. The other way involves optimizing the noise introduced for achieve privacy protection. This can be achieved by fixing the total privacy budget and may include extended concepts such as elastic sensitivity20, optimizing the allocation of the privacy budget21, optimizing the data noise distribution22.

The MA14algorithm gives an upper bound on the privacy of the composed DP privacy-preserving algorithm. However, it is currently considered to be a loose upper bound nowadays. It was applied to DP-SGD in14, where its performance was analyzed using typical datasets. Subsequently18, draws on the ideas in14by incorporating the structure of MA into the framework of RDP, so that in much subsequent literature, people refer to MA and RDP and often group them. The algorithm they used, as described above, analyzes the case when there are multiple overlays (combinations) within applications and offers an upper bound on the privacy consumption. Later, researchers proposed Gaussian Differential Privacy (GDP)19, which is a special kind of F-DP that provides a perspective for understand and analyze differential privacy. The GDP observes DP in terms of hypothesis testing. It introduces a trade-off function weight privacy protection against data utility and finally obtains the F-DP (and derived GDP), which is finally conversion between it and other DP.

MA, RDP, and F-DP are widely adopted in practice; however, they still have limitations as shown in Table 1. Both MA and RDP may not yield the tightest bounds due to the necessity of converting the initial definition of DP to RDP or MA during usage, which caused by the conversion process is not tight with some loss. In terms of GDP, its bound is lower than the optimal bound when fixing the number of iterations and other parameters, which indicates that it underestimates the privacy budget account.

In some works, scholars also discuss and analyze the utilization of different forms of variables and concepts in preservation process of differential privacy. Such as two studies22and23examined and analyzed the variables of privacy loss from different aspects. The privacy curve corresponding to the random variable of privacy loss is analyzed in23, and based on the privacy curve, while the numerical combination and error analysis of the privacy curve are discussed. On the other hand, authors in22 analyzed the characteristic function corresponding to the random variable of privacy loss through different viewpoints and discussed the combination of characteristic functions and other properties based on the characteristic function. Each approach has its advantages; however, it is worth noting that the latter allows lossless back and forth between representations of concepts related to privacy loss random variables, e.g., privacy overview, tradeoff function, moment generation function, and distribution function of privacy loss. This is one of the reasons why the authors claim to be the optimal.

In previous studies on combinations of differential privacy, the primary discussion focuses on avoiding such crude and primitive combinations, minimizing the consumption of the privacy budget, reducing distractions as much as possible while ensuring that privacy is preserved. In this article, the privacy-preserving algorithm in the federation model is the privacy curve and the approach can also use in deep neural network model for others. It has been argued in22 that direct analysis of the feature function leads to more accurate results. However, based on the established theorem, distribution function determines feature functions and vice versa due to their uniqueness. Therefore, using distribution function for model analysis and preserving privacy does not affect the results. It is also essential to note that the characteristic function values within a finite interval cannot provide absolute certainty about distribution function. address issues related to model fitting and privacy protection, the +/- infinity points have been excluded in advance; hence characteristic function is not utilized in this paper. This choice is motivated by its simplicity and intuitiveness when combining privacy curves while still yielding significant improvements as demonstrated in Section 5.

Definition and related work

Federated learning

Classical federated learning aims to jointly learn a global model from the Client and the cloud server. The process of this model is as follows: the raw data submitted by the Client to the cloud server is undergoes encryption. Subsequently, summarization and computation on the encrypted data, returning prediction results in an encrypted form back to the Client. Considering the high-density data in the federated learning model, significant computing power and storage capacities are required for both encrypting the data and performing computations on it. Therefore, instead of directly operating on individual items within the dataset, this paper proposes conducting initial model training locally on each Client before encrypting each batch involved in training prior to uploading it onto the cloud server, thus ensuring the privacy and security of the dataset. The learning goal of the model may be to minimize the loss function (such as empirical risk function) over the entire training dataset (i.e., the union of data across all clients)24. In the context of federated learning, challenges may arise from variations in local dataset size and distribution among different clients, as well as differences in the devices used for model training. Hence, in real-world applications, the model needs to process non-independently or Identically Distributed(non-IID) data. Moreover, these applications are also constrained by a series of restrictions in processing data such as limited communication bandwidth, and unreliable and limited available equipment.

The federated averaging algorithm (Fed Avg)28is widely employed in federated learning model. It involves each client performing local SGD (Stochastic Gradient Decent) steps, then uploading their model. The cloud server aggregates and averages all the updates to obtain the global model, which is redistributed to the clients for further iterations until a specific stopping condition is met. Therefore, the model can be perform local updates and reduce the communication frequency with the center cloud server, the resulting is addressed challenges such as such as data locality constraints and limited communication capabilities of mobile device clients. Additionally, the independence of the data of the model should also be considered. Li et al29. proved that Fed Avg converges on non-independent identically distributed data. Assuming the client owns the IID data, it implies that each small batch used for the client’s local update resembles a sample drawn uniformly from the entire training dataset (the union of all local datasets of the client). However, in practice, the IID assumption hardly holds true due to variations in both data volume and distribution among independently collecting clients’ training data. Nevertheless, it should be noted that adopting an IID assumption greatly simplifies theoretical convergence analysis of federal optimization algorithms; thus, exploring model privacy under IID data is still an important topic worth discussing.

Differential privacy

Proposed algorithm

The presentation of the definitions and theorems in the previous section addresses the problem of incorporating the DP algorithm into FL. The privacy-preserving deep learning problem in computational space is complex and variable (the operations usually involved are additive, multiplicative, and nonlinear). Therefore, to simplify the original problem for truncation, discreteness, and periodization of the privacy curve, a grid partition can be employed.

Using meshing and approximation in DP

Grid partitioning was initially proposed for solving partial differential equations in mathematical models, drawing inspiration from the finite element method, which hopes to solve infinite problems using finite methods. This approach is applied here to truncate exactly the infinite/continuous process into a finite/discrete problem and be able to use FFT to simplify the algorithm.

It is widely acknowledged that even after sufficient combinations, the privacy loss distribution remains an infinite distribution with long tails. Non-fixed number of iterations in FL with DP model training

The privacy budget is computed by using the combinatorial approach, equivalent to a tight bound’s value for a fixed number of iterations. But for practical application in federal learning models, it is more effective to strike a balance between utility and privacy by dynamically adding perturbations and adjusting the amount of noise based on the actual gradient training values at each round of model training. Therefore, instead of fixing the number of iterations at the beginning, a maximum privacy budget threshold is specified before the start of training. Then, the calculating dynamic privacy budget al.gorithm in the35 is introduced and composed with Algorithm 1 to train the federal learning model while calculating the tight bound based on the current number of iterations, next stop training when the privacy budget reaches the threshold which provides privacy preservation together.

Summary

In this paper, differential privacy is used to train federated learning models to achieve a finer balance between utility and privacy protection. The improved algorithm replaces the initial definition of DP with privacy curves, resulting in a tighter bound on their combination. Moreover, computational efficiency is boosted by utilizing FFT for computing the combination results; it reduces the number of discrete points within the same discrete interval without introducing any additional error. Additionally, the number of hyperparameters in the algorithm has been reduced, and its geometric level has been enhanced to fully and effectively utilize the privacy budget while improving combination efficiency40–43.

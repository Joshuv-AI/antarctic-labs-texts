Tight vanilla coreset sizes are often achieved through chaining arguments
[

,
CADRS25
]

In contrast, all the robust coreset results discussed so far do not use chaining arguments and rely on geometric decomposition techniques, which partition the dataset into significantly more pieces with desirable properties than are needed for vanilla coresets.
This methodological gap exists because applying vanilla geometric decomposition and chaining to the robust setting faces a fundamental difficulty: even a minor perturbation to the center set can drastically change the outliers.
This sensitivity can either invalidate the correctness of the chaining argument or, if addressed with a simple fix, lead to an ineffective, large coreset size.

In this paper, we overcome these difficulties by introducing novel dataset decompositions combined with chaining arguments, thereby addressing the posed question with near-optimal bounds for robust
k
-Medians

\tilde{O}(m\varepsilon^{-1}+Q)

Algorithms for robust

In the seminal work

]

-Medians

Following this, the first constant-factor approximation algorithm was developed

2
Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, let
(M,\dist)
denote a metric space. For notational convenience, for a set
S
, we denote by
\binom{S}{k}
the set of all
k
-element subsets of a set
S
. For a set
A\subseteq M
, the diameter of
A
is denoted by
\mathrm{diam}(A)=\sup_{a,b\in A}\dist(a,b)
, and the distance from a point
x
to
A
is defined as
\dist(x,A)=\inf_{y\in A}\dist(x,y)
.

Ball range space and VC dimension

For a point
x\in M
and a radius
r>0
, let
B(x,r)=\{y\in M\mid\dist(x,y)\leq r\}
denote the ball centered at
x
with radius
r
. The ball range space of
(M,\dist)
is the collection of all balls, denoted by
\Balls=\{B(x,r)\mid x\in M,r>0\}
. For a
k
-point set
C\in\binom{M}{k}
, let
B(C,r)=\bigcup_{c\in C}B(c,r)
. The
k
-
\Balls
range space of
M
is denoted by
\Balls_{k}=\{B(C,r)\mid C\in\binom{M}{k},r>0\}
.

A finite set
X\subset M
is said to be shattered by
\Balls
if
|X\cap\Balls|=2^{|X|}
, where
X\cap\Balls=\{X\cap B(x,r)\mid B(x,r)\in\Balls\}
.
The VC dimension of
(M,\dist)
is the maximum size of a subset of
M
that can be shattered by
\Balls
, or
+\infty
if no such maximum exists.

Doubling dimension

We say that a metric space
(M,\dist)
has doubling dimension at most
t
, if for any ball
B(x,r)
, there exists a set
C\in\binom{M}{q}
with
q\leq 2^{t}
so that the ball
B(x,r)\subseteq B(C,\frac{r}{2})
, i.e., any ball can be covered by at most
2^{t}
balls of half radius.

Robust
(k,z)
-clustering

For a dataset
X\subset M
, the vanilla
(k,z)
-Clustering
problem aims to compute a
k
-center set
C\in\binom{M}{k}
that minimizes

\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,C):=\sum_{x\in X}\dist^{z}(x,C).

This problem captures several variants, including vanilla
k
-Medians
(
z=1
) and vanilla
k
-Means
(
z=2
).

A well-known variant, called
(k,z)
-Clustering
with
m
outliers, or the robust
(k,z)
-Clustering
problem, is to find
C\in\binom{M}{k}
that minimizes

\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}_{z}(X,C):=\min_{Y\subseteq X,|X\setminus Y|\leq m}\operatorname{cost}_{z}(Y,C).

Namely,
\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C)
aggregates all but the largest
m
distances to
C
.
Similar to the vanilla case, we call the problem robust
k
-median when
z=1
and robust
k
-means when
z=2
.

Weighted Set

Let
(Y,w_{Y})
be a weighted set where each point
y\in Y
has a weight
w_{Y}(y)\geq 0
. We use
w(y)
to denote
w_{Y}(y)
if the weighted set is clear in the context. Moreover, for a weighted set
Y
, we denote by
\lVert Y\rVert_{0}
the number of points in
Y
and by
\lVert Y\rVert_{1}
the total weight of
Y
.
The
k
-median objective on
Y
is defined as

\operatorname{cost}_{z}(Y,C):=\sum_{y\in Y}w(y)\dist^{z}(y,C).

Weighted Outlier

Let
(Y,w_{Y})
and
(Z,w_{Z})
be two weighted sets. We call
Z
a valid
m
-weighted outlier of
Y
if (i)
Z\subseteq Y
, (ii)
w_{Z}(z)\leq w_{Y}(z)
for all
z\in Z
, and (iii)
w_{Z}(Z)=m
. Also denote by
Y\setminus Z
the weighted set
(Y,w_{Y}-w_{Z})
.

For a weighted set
(Y,w_{Y})
, we use
L_{Y}^{(m)}
to denote the set of all valid

3
Technical overview

Our approach synthesizes and advances both robust and vanilla coreset algorithms through a key concept: the
(r,k)
-instance
(Definition
4.1
), which consists of
k
balls all having the same radius
r
.
To the best of our knowledge, this uniform-radius constraint is a new contribution to the coreset literature.
It enables novel dataset decompositions and makes it possible to adapt chaining arguments to the robust setting, as summarized below.

(i)

New dataset decomposition into only
\poly\!\log(k/\varepsilon)
pieces, each forming an
(r,k)
-instance for some radius
r
.
This is a significant reduction from the
\tilde{O}(k)
pieces required by previous methods
[
HJLW23
,
HLLW25
]
and is
essential for eliminating a
k
factor from the final coreset size.

(ii)

A robust coreset algorithm (Algorithm
1
) for an
(r,k)
-instance, accompanied by a novel analysis that blends a chaining argument with a range space argument.
Our analysis crucially relies on the uniformity of the radii, which enables control over the placement of outliers.
This marks the first application of chaining, a technique commonly used in bounding vanilla coreset sizes
[
BCAP+24
,
CADRS25
]
, to the robust setting.
The use of chaining is critical for achieving our tight coreset sizes.
In addition, we extend the range space argument from its original use for a single ring
[
HJLW23
,
HLLW25
]
to simultaneously handle multiple rings by treating them collectively as a single
(r,k)
-instance.

(iii)

Two algorithms (Algorithms
2
and
3
) for
(r,k)
-instances with small radii. These algorithms employ novel reductions to the vanilla coreset framework.
In particular, for Euclidean spaces, our joint reduction across all components of an
(r,k)
-instance yields improved coreset sizes compared to the component-wise vanilla reduction used in
[
JL25
]
(see Remark
8.4
).

Below, we begin by presenting these novelties in the context of Theorem
1.1
for metric spaces with a finite VC dimension
d_{\mathrm{VC}}
.
We then discuss how to adapt these results to doubling metrics, highlight the additional technical novelty required for Euclidean metrics, and extend the techniques to general robust
(k,z)
-Clustering
.

Review of prior results

We begin by reviewing the techniques of
[
HJLW23
]
, which obtains the first polynomial-sized robust coresets.
Given a dataset, the authors first find a constant-factor approximation
C^{\ast}
for robust
k
-Medians
and explicitly add the
m
outliers from this solution to the coreset.
Then they apply the ring-decomposition framework of
[
BCAJ+22
]
, which builds upon
[
Che09
]
, to reduce the remaining data points (called inliers) to
\tilde{O}(k^{2}\varepsilon^{-1})

rings
, where points in each ring are approximately equidistant from the center.
A robust coreset is then constructed for each ring using uniform sampling, and the mergeability of robust coresets (Fact
2.2
) implies a robust coreset for the whole dataset.
Given a ring
X
of radius
r
, the analysis of uniform sampling relies on the following integral representation of the cost function:

\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(|X\setminus B(C,u)|-m)^{+}\;du.

Suppose that
D
is a uniform sample (reweighted by
\frac{|X|}{|D|}
) from
X
, one immediately obtains

|\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(D,C)|\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|\|D\setminus B(C,u)\|_{1}-|X\setminus B(C,u)|\big|\;du.

(2)

Now, if the sample size on each ring is
\tilde{O}(kd_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})
, it follows from the classical VC theory that
\|D\setminus B(C,u)\|_{1}
approximates
|X\cap B(C,u)|
up to an additive error
\varepsilon\cdot|X|
for every
C
and
u
, i.e.,
D
is an
\varepsilon
-range space approximation of
X
.
Since
X
is a ring of diameter
O(r)
, the integrand of (
2
) vanishes outside an interval of length
O(r)
and, consequently,
D
is an
(\varepsilon,r,\varepsilon|X|r)
-robust coreset of
X
.
Applying this to
\tilde{O}(k^{2}\varepsilon^{-1})
rings results in a robust coreset of size
m+\tilde{O}(k^{2}\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot kd_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})=m+\tilde{O}(k^{3}d_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-3})
, as concluded in
[
HJLW23
]
.
A subsequent work
[
HLLW25
]
essentially reduces the number of “effective rings” to
\tilde{O}(k)
by adaptively selecting the sample size for each ring, achieving a smaller coreset size of
m+\tilde{O}(k^{2}d_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})
.

Our goal is to further reduce the coreset size to
m+Q(\varepsilon)
, where
Q(\varepsilon)
denotes the size of a vanilla
\varepsilon
-coreset.
The state-of-the-art result is
Q(\varepsilon)=\tilde{O}(kd_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})

[
CADRS25
]
, improving on
[
HJLW23
]
by a factor of
k
.
This improvement is achieved by grouping the rings into
\poly\log(k/\varepsilon)
collections, each containing at most
k
rings, and performing uniform sampling at the group level rather than for individual rings.
A straightforward idea would be to apply the vanilla coreset construction directly to the union of all rings, i.e. the set of all inliers.
However, this fails to guarantee a robust coreset, since points within the rings can become outliers with respect to center sets different from
C^{\ast}
.
Therefore, new technical ideas are required.

Grouping rings:
(r,k)
-instances

A natural strategy is to leverage the core principle of vanilla coreset construction: perform uniform sampling across multiple rings and analyse the total error collectively, rather than summing the errors from individual rings.
This motivates the key definition of an
(r,k)
-instance (see Definition
4.1
), which is essentially a set of points that can be covered by
k
balls of equal radius
r
(i.e. contained in
B(A,r)
for some
k
-point set
A
).
The idea of grouping rings has been instrumental in recent advances towards constructing near-optimal vanilla coresets
[
CSS21
,
CLSS22
,
CAGLS+22
,
HLW24
,
CADRS25
]
.
These approaches typically group rings of varying radii from different clusters, whereas in our
(r,k)
-instance, all rings must have a uniform radius
r
.
This specific property, while not needed for constructing vanilla coresets, is crucial for building robust coresets.

Now we provide a high-level overview of our algorithm, which decomposes the input dataset
X
into a collection of
(r,k)
-instances (see Lemma
5.1
and Figure
5
for illustration).
First, we add
m+\varepsilon^{-1}
points farthest from
C^{\ast}
to the coreset.
Adding these additional
\varepsilon^{-1}
points guarantees that all remaining points in
X
are within a distance of
\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C^{\ast})
from
C^{\ast}
.
Then we collect all points within distance
r_{{\mathrm{in}}}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{mk}\cdot\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C^{\ast})
from
C^{\ast}
, forming an
(r_{{\mathrm{in}}},k)
-instance.
Now, all remaining points have distances to
C^{\ast}
within the interval
[r_{{\mathrm{in}}},mkr_{{\mathrm{in}}}]
, allowing them to be decomposed into
\log(mk)
layers of
(r,k)
-instances with geometrically increasing radii
r
.
We then construct a robust coreset for each of these
(r,k)
-instances (with carefully chosen error guarantees) and merge them into a final robust coreset of
X
using Fact
2.2
.
The main technical challenge is to construct robust coresets of size
\tilde{O}(kd_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})
for these
(r,k)
-instances, i.e., the layers and the inner instance; we illustrate the key ideas for each scenario below.

Handling a layer of
(r,k)
-instance

Consider a layer of
(r,k)
-instance
X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}\subset B(A,r)
, where each
X_{i}\subset B(a_{i},r)
for some
a_{i}\in A
.
We develop Algorithm
1
to construct an
(\varepsilon,m,\varepsilon\lvert X\rvert r)
-robust coreset for
X
; roughly speaking, we take
\tilde{O}(kd_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})
uniform samples from
X
(Theorem
4.3
).
Without loss of generality, assume that all
X_{i}
have equal cost
\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},a_{i})
.
This type of instance is also referred to as an
(r,k)
-regular instance (Definition
2.1
); we show in Lemma
4.4
how to remove this assumption and in Figure
2
for illustration.

A natural idea is to extend the single-ring analysis (shown in (
2

(r,k)
-instance.
With
\tilde{O}(kd_{\mathrm{VC}}\varepsilon^{-2})
points in
D
, we can still guarantee that
\bigl|\|D\setminus B(C,u)\|_{1}-|X\setminus B(C,u)|\bigr|\leq\varepsilon|X|
for every
C
and

.
However, applying this bound directly would yield an additive error of
\varepsilon k|X|r
(arising from integrating over
k
intervals of length
O(r)
, one for each of
k
clusters), which is a
k

\varepsilon\lvert X\rvert r
.
Hence, additional technical insights are needed for a tighter control of the error.

Chaining argument

The first idea is to borrow insights from the vanilla coreset construction, where the chaining argument is central.
An essential step there is bounding the net size by
\exp(\tilde{O}(k))
, which counts the number of distinct distance vectors
\bigl(\dist(x,C)\bigr)_{x\in D}
for all possible choices of
C
, under a certain level of discretization.
In the robust setting, however, a small perturbation to
C
can drastically change the set of outliers and thus the distance vectors.
A natural idea is to fix the set of outliers, which then requires taking a union bound over all
\binom{|X|}{m}
possible sets of
m
outliers, causing an additional
\exp(m)
factor in the net size.
This results in a net size far exceeding the desired
\exp(\tilde{O}(k))
, which will blow up the error bound we aim to control.

To address this issue, we apply the chaining argument
exclusively
to those clusters whose points are all inliers with respect to
C
.
Specifically, we partition both
X
and
D
into the same three collections of clusters with respect to
C
:
A_{1}
(clusters of inliers),
A_{2}
(clusters of outliers), and
A_{3}
(clusters whose distances to
C
lie within an interval of length
O(r)
); see Lemma
6.6
and Figure
7
.
Such a decomposition is possible because every cluster in an
(r,k)
-instance has the same radius, and an additional realignment procedure (Lines 4–6 in Algorithm
1
) ensures that each cluster
X_{i}
has a total sample weight of
|X_{i}|
(Definition
6.3
).
Notably, the realignment procedure ensures that the sample set
D
has the same partition as
X
, a property absent in vanilla coreset constructions.
We then apply the chaining argument solely to
A_{1}
, obtaining an
indexed-subset cost approximation
that bounds the error introduced by
A_{1}
(Definition
6.2
).
Since
A_{1}
is a union of clusters and there are at most
2^{k}
such unions, the net size only increases by a factor of
\exp(k)
, which remains manageable (Lemma
6.10
).

Range space argument

Since
A_{2}
contains only outliers, we may safely ignore it.
It remains to control the error contributed by

.
Since the distances of all points in

from

A key challenge is that

To mitigate this, we extend the notion of

to encompass all

While this extension increases the number of ranges by a factor of

Consequently, the range space approximation guarantee of the sample set

remains valid, meaning

Now, the integral in (

is thus within

(Lemma

Consider the inner

For each

Adapting to doubling metrics

The analysis for the doubling metric in Theorem
1.2
is almost identical to that for the VC case.
We employ the same point set decomposition into
(r,k)
-instances (Lemma
5.1
) and the same algorithms (Algorithms
1
and
2
) for constructing robust coresets,
with the only difference being the substitution of
d_{D}
for
d_{\mathrm{VC}}
in the coreset size.
The key difference is that, in a doubling space, the VC dimension of the set of balls can be unbounded
[
HJLW18
]
, preventing a direct application of the same range space argument used in the VC case.
To overcome this, we adopt the technique from
[
HJLW18
]
, which shows that by (randomly) distorting the distance function
\dist
slightly (Definition

), one can bound a “probabilistic” version of the VC dimension by
\tilde{O}(d_{D})
(Lemma
7.6
).
This property enables a distorted variant of the range space argument (Lemma
7.8
), and we further show that the resulting error for
A_{3}
under this distortion remains well-controlled (Lemma
7.5
).

Euclidean spaces

As discussed above, our algorithm
for the inner
(r_{{\mathrm{in}}},k)
-instance (Algorithm
2
) also produces a Euclidean coreset of size
O(km\varepsilon^{-1})+Q(\varepsilon)
, where
Q(\varepsilon)
now denotes the size of the vanilla
\varepsilon
-coreset in Euclidean space.
Note that the total number of moved points in Algorithm
2
is
mk
, while the number of outliers is only
m
, wasting a
k
factor.
This observation motivates Algorithm
3
, which further reduces the Euclidean coreset size to
O(m\varepsilon^{-1})+Q(\varepsilon)
.
The key idea is to increase the radius threshold
r_{{\mathrm{in}}}
from
\frac{\varepsilon}{mk}\cdot\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C^{\ast})
to
\frac{\varepsilon}{m}\cdot\operatorname{cost}^{(m)}(X,C^{\ast})
.
See Figure
6
for illustration.

In the analysis of this algorithm, a major challenge arises when the inner
(r_{{\mathrm{in}}},k)
-instance contains points that are outliers with respect to some center set
C
.
As noted earlier, a naïve adaptation of the vanilla chaining argument can blow up the net size by a factor of
\exp(m)
.
To address this, we propose a novel decomposition of the robust clustering cost by strategically reassigning outliers across clusters, ensuring that only one cluster contains both inliers and outliers, while all other clusters contain exclusively inliers or exclusively outliers (Lemma
8.3
).
This decomposition relies on the uniform radius assumption, which guarantees that moving each outlier introduces only a small additive error
O(r_{{\mathrm{in}}})
to the overall clustering cost.
Consequently, the net size in the chaining argument grows by at most a factor of
2^{k}
, which remains manageable as before.

Extension to general
z\geq 1

To extend our approach to general robust
(k,z)
-Clustering
, the key idea is to use the generalized triangle inequality (Lemma
C.5
).
In both the VC and doubling cases, we further require the sample set
D
to be an
\varepsilon^{z}
-range space approximation, rather than
\varepsilon
as in the robust
k
-Medians
case. This change results in an
\varepsilon^{-2z}
term in the coreset size (Theorem
C.1
).
We note that if the range space argument could be further refined, it would be possible to achieve the desired coreset size of
m+Q(\varepsilon)
for any
z\geq 1
(Remark
C.11
), which remains an intriguing open problem.

4
Three Coreset Constructions for
(r,k)
-Instances

A common approach to constructing a robust coreset for a point set is to decompose the set into distinct parts with useful geometric properties and then construct a coreset for each part separately.
This paper follows this strategy by introducing the notion of
(r,k)
-instances and
(r,k)
-regular instances.

Definition 4.1

(
(r,k)
-instance)
.

A dataset
X\subset M
is called an
(r,k)
-instance if

X\subset B(A,r)
for some
A\in\binom{M}{k}
.

Intuitively, an
(r,k)
-instance
X
consists of
k
clusters, each contained within a ball of radius
r
.
As a result, we have
\operatorname{cost}(X,A)\leq|X|\cdot r
.

We further define a specific subclass of
(r,k)
-instances below.

Definition 4.2

(
(r,k)
-regular instance)
.

A point set
X=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{k}\subset M

(r,k)
-

(i)

\forall i,j\in[k]
,
i\neq j

X_{i}\cap X_{j}=\emptyset
;

(ii)

\forall i\in[k]
,
X_{i}\subseteq B(a_{i},r)
for some center
a_{i}\in M
;

(iii)

\forall i,j\in[k],i\neq j
,
\lvert X_{i}\rvert\leq 2\lvert X_{j}\rvert
.

An
(r,k)
-regular instance further imposes the condition that the number of points in each cluster

is of the same order.
Specifically, we have
\frac{|X|}{2k}\leq|X_{i}|\leq\frac{2|X|}{k}
for all

.
We note that the prior work
[

,

]
introduced a similar structure called a
group
, which also consists of
k

The key distinction between our data structure and a group is that we enforce strict alignment on the radius of different clusters, a property that is crucial for our analysis.

In the three subsections below, we shall give three different coreset constructions for
(r,k)
-instances.

Lemma 4.5

(Reduction from vanilla coreset to robust coreset)
.

Suppose there exists a randomized algorithm
\mathcal{B}

-robust coreset with size

of

-Medians
.

Proof.

Let
D_{H}=\{(x_{i},|H_{i}|)\mid i\in[k]\}
denote the set of
k
weighted points added into
D
in the for loop.
We prove that
D=D_{H}\cup D^{\prime}
is an
(\varepsilon,m,O(mkr))
-robust coreset of
X=H\cup X^{\prime}
.

Let
J^{\ast}
denote the set of the outlier points in
\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)
.
For each

, let

. We then have
t_{i}\leq\min\{\lvert X_{i}\rvert,m\}=\lvert H_{i}\rvert
. Define

as the set of outlier points in

. Now, let
E^{\ast}=\bigcup_{i\in[k]}E_{i}^{\ast}
. Noting that

, we obtain

\operatorname{cost}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)=\sum_{i\in[k]}\operatorname{cost}^{(t_{i})}(D\cap X_{i},C)\geq\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C).

Since
t_{i}\leq\lvert H_{i}\rvert
, we can find a subset
J^{\prime}\subseteq H\setminus J^{\ast}
such that
\lvert J^{\prime}\cap H_{i}\rvert=\lvert(J^{\ast}\setminus H)\cap X_{i}\rvert
for all
i\in[k]
. Define
J=(J^{\ast}\cap H)\cup J^{\prime}
.
Note that
|J\cap H_{i}|=|J^{\ast}\cap X_{i}|=t_{i}
for all
i\in[k]
.
Similarly, define
E=(E^{\ast}\cap D_{H})\cup E^{\prime}
, where
E^{\prime}\subseteq D_{H}\setminus E^{\ast}
is chosen such that
\lVert E^{\prime}\cap\{x_{i}\}\rVert_{1}=\lVert E_{i}^{\ast}\setminus D_{H}\rVert_{1}
. Thus, we have
\lVert E\cap\{x_{i}\}\rVert_{1}=\lVert E^{\ast}_{i}\rVert_{1}=t_{i}
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\quad\ \operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cost}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J^{\ast},C)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cost}(D\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J,C)+\lvert\operatorname{cost}(E,C)-\operatorname{cost}(E^{\ast},C)\rvert+\lvert\operatorname{cost}(J,C)-\operatorname{cost}(J^{\ast},C)\rvert

\displaystyle=\operatorname{cost}(D\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J,C)+\lvert\operatorname{cost}(E^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}(E^{\ast}\setminus D_{H},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt+\lvert\operatorname{cost}(J^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}(J^{\ast}\setminus H,C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\operatorname{cost}(D\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J,C)\rvert+2mr+2mr

\displaystyle=\lvert\operatorname{cost}(D_{H}\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}(H\setminus J,C)\rvert+\lvert\operatorname{cost}(D^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X^{\prime},C)\rvert+4mr.

We shall bound the first two terms separately.

For the first term, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{cost}(D_{H}\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}(H\setminus J,C)\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\lvert(|H_{i}|-t_{i})\dist(x_{i},C)-\operatorname{cost}(H_{i}\setminus J,C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\sum_{x\in H_{i}\setminus J}\dist(x,x_{i})\leq 2mkr.

where the last inequality follows from the fact that

due to the construction of
H
.

For the second term, since

is a vanilla

-coreset of

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{cost}(D^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X^{\prime},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}(X^{\prime},C)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J,C)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J^{\ast},C)+\varepsilon\lvert\operatorname{cost}(J,C)-\operatorname{cost}(J^{\ast},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}(X\setminus J^{\ast},C)+2\varepsilon mr

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+2\varepsilon mr.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+(2mk+4m+2\varepsilon m)r

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+8mkr.

By a similar argument, we can also obtain that

\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C)\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C)+8mkr,

which implies that

\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+\frac{8mkr}{1+\varepsilon}.

(4)

Combining (

.

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=``\text{$u_{i}\in(1\pm\varepsilon)\cdot n_{i}$ for all $i\in[k_{0}]$''}.

(5)

We have the following lemma showing that event

The proof of the following lemma is essentially an adaption of Lemma 19 of

Lemma 6.8
.

It holds that

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-O\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{sk\log m}\right).

Proof.

Define for each

and

v_{i,u}=\frac{n}{s}\mathbf{1}_{\{u\in X_{i}\}}

\displaystyle\E\sum_{u\in U}v_{i,u}=|X_{i}|=n_{i},\quad\E\sum_{u\in U}v_{i,u}^{2}\leq\frac{n\cdot n_{i}}{s}\leq\frac{2k_{0}n_{i}^{2}}{s}.

\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{k_{0}ks\cdot\log m},

provided that

. Taking a union bound yields that

We first prove that

Lemma 6.9

(Range space approximation for

.

With probability at least

We begin by defining an indicator function

for each subset

,
C\in\binom{M}{k}
, and each
r>0
. This function is defined as
I_{J,C,r}(x)=1
if and only if

.
We observe that

\displaystyle|B(C,r)\cap U_{J}|-|B(C,r)\cap X_{J}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in U}\frac{n}{s}\cdot I_{J,C,r}(y)-\sum_{x\in X}I_{J,C,r}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in U}\frac{n}{s}\cdot I_{J,C,r}(y)-\E\left[\sum_{y\in U}\frac{n}{s}\cdot I_{J,C,r}(y)\right].

Our goal is to prove that with probability at least

,

\sup_{C,r}\bigg\lvert\left\lVert B(C,r)\cap U_{J}\right\rVert_{1}-|B(C,r)\cap X_{J}|\bigg\rvert\leq\varepsilon n.

We shall establish an upper bound for the expectation of equation (

We bound the expectation through the symmetrization trick (see, e.g.,

By the standard symmetrization trick,

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J,C,r}\left\lvert\sum_{y\in U}\frac{n}{s}\cdot I_{J,C,r}(y)-\E\left[\sum_{y\in U}\frac{n}{s}\cdot I_{J,C,r}(y)\right]\right\rvert

Define the level-

distance vector set as

If for every

(Robust cost decomposition)

\operatorname{cost}(U_{i},C)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},C)-O(\frac{\varepsilon}{k})\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X,A).

Summing over

\left\lvert\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\right\rvert\lesssim\varepsilon.

On the other hand, when
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}
does not happen, we know from (
27

\left\lvert\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\right\rvert\lesssim k.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J\in\mathcal{B}_{0}}\sup_{C\in\binom{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{k}}\bigg|\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1})+k\cdot(1-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+k\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon.\qed

Lemma 8.11

(Handling error when
\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)
is large)
.

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J\subseteq[k]}\sup_{C\in L^{J}_{-1}}\bigg|\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim\varepsilon.

Proof.

Fix
J\subseteq[k]
and
C\in L_{-1}^{J}
. By definition, we know that for each
i\in J
,

\dist(a_{i},C)\geq\varepsilon^{-1}\Delta_{i},

which implies that

\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},C)\geq|X_{i}|\dist(a_{i},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},a_{i})\geq(\varepsilon^{-1}-1)|X_{i}|\cdot\Delta_{i}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},a_{i}).

Hence, when
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}
happens, it follows from Lemma
8.9
that for each
i\in J

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(U_{i},C)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{cost}(U_{i},a_{i})+\frac{u_{i}}{|X_{i}|}\cdot(\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},a_{i})+\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},C))

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},a_{i})+(1+\varepsilon)(\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},a_{i})+\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},C))

\displaystyle\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},C).

Similarly, by (

), we have that

\operatorname{cost}(U_{i},C)\geq(1-O(\varepsilon))\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},C).

Summing over
i\in J
, we obtain that when
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}
happens,

\bigg|\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim\varepsilon.

On the other hand, when
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}
does not happen, we know from (
27
) that

\bigg|\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim k.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J\subseteq[k]}\sup_{C\in L^{J}_{-1}}\bigg|\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1})+k\cdot(1-\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}))

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+k\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{k}

Lemma A.1

(Sufficient chaining error for Lemma

If for every

\sup_{J,C}\frac{n}{s}\cdot\left\lvert\frac{\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)-\E\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)}{(1+2^{i}|J_{i,C}|/k_{0})\cdot nr}\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\log(1/\varepsilon)},

(30)

where

\mathcal{E}
(defined in (

In other words,

.

Proof.

Let

. We observe that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)=\sum_{i=0}^{T}\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{i,C}},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{\mathrm{far},C}},C),

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)=\sum_{i=0}^{T}\operatorname{cost}(U_{J_{i,C}},C)+\operatorname{cost}(U_{J_{\mathrm{far},C}},C).

We shall show termwise approximations. First, we show that

\operatorname{cost}(U_{J_{\mathrm{far},C}},C)\in(1\pm O(\varepsilon))\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{\mathrm{far},C}},C).

. Thus for every

Now, for

\operatorname{cost}(U_{J_{i,C}},C)=\frac{n}{s}\cdot\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{i,C}},C)=\E\frac{n}{s}\cdot\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x).

It follows from the assumption (

\left\lvert\operatorname{cost}(U_{J_{i,C}},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{i,C}},C)\right\rvert\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}\cdot(1+2^{i}|J_{i,C}|/k_{0})\cdot nr.

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{i,C}},C)\geq|J_{i,C}|\cdot\frac{n}{2k_{0}}\cdot 2^{i}r,

\displaystyle|\operatorname{cost}(U_{J_{i,C}},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{i,C}},C)|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\log\varepsilon^{-1}}\cdot(\operatorname{cost}(X_{J_{i,C}},C)+nr)).

Since there are

many

we have that

|\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)-\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)|\lesssim\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\varepsilon nr.

∎

\begin{cases}|v(x)-v^{S}_{i,J,C}(x)|\leq\alpha\cdot 2^{i+2}\cdot r&v^{S}_{i,J,C}(x)>0\\
0&v^{S}_{i,J,C}(x)=0\end{cases}

Lemma A.2

(Bounding net size on VC instances)
.

For every
i\in\{0,\dots,T\}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
and every subset
S\subseteq X
with
\lvert S\rvert\geq 2
, there exists an
\alpha
-net
N^{S}_{\alpha,i}
of
V^{S}_{i}
such that

\log big\lvert N^{S}_{\alpha,i}big\rvert\lesssim\alpha^{-1}dk\log k\cdot\log\lvert S\rvert.

Furthermore, if
\alpha\geq 2^{-i}
, it is possible to ensure that
N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
consists of piecewise constant vectors. That is, every
v\in N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
satisfies that
v(x)
is constant for all
x\in X_{j}
for each
j\in[k_{0}]
.

Proof.

Let
\beta=\alpha 2^{i}r
. For a distance vector
v_{i,J,C}^{S}\in V_{i}^{S}
, we define its
\alpha
-rounding to be

\bar{v}_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)=\left\lceil\frac{v_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)}{\beta}\right\rceil\cdot\beta.

It is clear that
\bar{v}_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)\in\{0,\beta,\dots,\beta t\}
for
t=\lceil 2/\alpha\rceil
.

We show that the collection of all
\alpha
-roundings

N_{\alpha,i}^{S}:=\left\{\bar{v}_{i,J,C}^{S}\mid v_{i,J,C}^{S}\in V_{i}^{S}\right\}

is the desired net.
The construction yields immediately that for every
x\in S
,
|\bar{v}_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)-v_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)|\leq\beta\leq\alpha\cdot 2^{i}\cdot r
and
\bar{v}_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)=0
if
v_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)=0
, which implies that
N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
is indeed an
\alpha
-net of
V_{i}^{S}
.

It remains to bound the size of
N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
. Consider each
u\in N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
, define for each
j\in\{0,\dots,t\}
a ball
B_{j}^{S}=\{x\in S\mid u(x)\leq\beta\cdot j\}
. Since all coordinates of
u
are fully determined by the sequence
B_{0}^{S},\dots,B_{t}^{S}
, it suffices to bound the number of possible balls
B_{j}^{S}
.
By construction,
B_{0}^{S}=(C\cap S)\cup(S\setminus X_{I_{i,C}})
, thus, the number of possible choices for
B_{0}^{S}
is at most

\binom{\lvert S\rvert}{k}\cdot 2^{k}=|S|^{O(k)}.

For
i\geq 1
, write
B_{i}^{S}
as
B_{i}^{S}=(B(C,\beta\cdot i)\cap S)\cap X_{I_{i,C}}
. Since the VC dimension of
\mathrm{Balls}_{k}
is at most
O(dk\log k)

[
EA07
]
, the number of distinct choices for
B(C,\beta\cdot i)\cap S
is
at most
|S|^{O(dk\log k)}
. Additionally, there are at most
2^{k}
choices of
X_{I_{i,C}}
, leading to at most

|S|^{O(dk\log k)}\cdot 2^{k}=|S|^{O(dk\log k)}

distinct possibilities for each
B_{i}^{S}
, for each
i\in[t]
.
Consequently, the different number of combinations of
B_{0}^{S},\dots,B_{t}^{S}
can be bounded by

|S|^{O(k)}\cdot(|S|^{O(dk\log k)})^{t}=|S|^{O(\alpha^{-1}dk\log k)},

which is an upper bound on the size of
N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
.

When
\alpha\geq 2^{-i}
, we show that every
u\in N_{\alpha,i}^{S}
can be made constant in each cluster. Define

\bar{u}(x)=\begin{cases}\lceil\frac{\dist(a_{\pi(x)},C)}{\beta}\rceil\cdot\beta&u(x)>0\\
0&u(x)=0\end{cases}

Since
|u(x)-v_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)|\leq\beta
for all
x\in S
, it follows that

|\bar{u}(x)-v_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)|\leq r+2\beta\leq\alpha\cdot 2^{i+2}\cdot r
.
If
v_{i,J,C}^{S}(x)=0
, we know that
u(x)=0
, then
\bar{u}(x)=0
by construction.
Therefore,
\bar{N}_{\alpha,i}^{S}=\{\bar{u}\mid u\in N_{\alpha,i}^{S}\}
is an
\alpha
-net of
V_{i,J,C}^{S}
and every vector in
\bar{N}_{\alpha,i}^{S}
is a constant in each cluster. Furthermore,

\lvert\bar{N}_{\alpha,i}^{S}\rvert\leq|N_{\alpha,i}^{S}|\leq|S|^{O(\alpha^{-1}dk\log k)}.\qed

It remains to prove the following lemma.

Lemma A.3

(Bounding chaining error for Lemma
A.1
)
.

The bound (
30
) holds for every
i\in\{0,\dots,T\}
with probability at least
1-O\big(\frac{1}{\log\frac{k}{\varepsilon}\cdot\log(mk)}\big)
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for every
i\in\{0,\dots,T\}
,

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J,C}\frac{1}{s}\cdot\left\lvert\frac{\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)-\E\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)}{(1+2^{i}|J_{i,C}|/k_{0})\cdot r}\right\rvert\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\log^{2}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\cdot\log\frac{k}{\varepsilon}\cdot\log mk},

(31)

then use Markov’s inequality and take a union bound.

To ease the notation, let
\gamma_{i,J,C}=1/(1+2^{i}|J_{i,C}|/k_{0})
. The standard symmetrization trick gives that

\E\sup_{J,C}\frac{\gamma_{i,J,C}}{rs}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)-\E\sum_{x\in U}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)\right\rvert\lesssim\E_{U}\E_{g}\frac{1}{s}\sup_{J,C}\frac{\gamma_{i,J,C}}{r}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)\right\rvert

(32)

For each
h\geq 0
, let
N_{2^{-h},i}^{U}
be a (
2^{-h}
)-net of
V_{i}^{U}
satisfying that

\log big\lvert N_{2^{-h},i}^{U}big\rvert\lesssim 2^{h}dk\log k\log s.

which is possible by Lemma
A.2
. Furthermore, when
h=i
, one can ensure that
N_{2^{-i},i}^{U}
consists of piecewise constant vectors.

By the net property, there exists a vector
b_{h,J,C}^{U}\in N_{2^{-h},i}^{U}
such that

|b_{h,J,C}^{U}(x)-v_{i,J,C}^{U}(x)|\leq 2^{i+2-h}r,\quad\forall x\in U.

By writing
v_{i,J,C}^{U}(x)
as the following telescoping sum

v_{i,J,C}^{U}(x)=\sum_{h=i}^{\infty}\big(b_{h+1,J,C}^{U}(x)-b_{h,J,C}^{U}(x)\big)+b_{i,J,C}^{U}(x),

we have

\displaystyle\quad\ \E_{U}\E_{g}\frac{1}{s}\sup_{J,C}\frac{\gamma_{i,J,C}}{r}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}v^{U}_{i,J,C}(x)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\E_{U}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{h=i}^{\infty}\E_{g}\sup_{J,C}\frac{\gamma_{i,J,C}}{r}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}(b_{h+1,J,C}^{U}(x)-b_{h,J,C}^{U}(x))\right\rvert+\E_{U}\frac{1}{s}\E_{g}\sup_{J,C}\frac{\gamma_{i,J,C}}{r}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}\cdot b_{i,J,C}^{U}(x)\right\rvert

(33)

We bound the two terms individually, starting with the first term. Consider the sum
\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}\big(b_{h+1,J,C}^{U}(x)-b_{h,J,C}^{U}(x)\big)
.
Observe that
|b_{h+1,J,C}^{U}(x)-b_{h,J,C}^{U}(x)|\lesssim 2^{i-h}r
and, by the construction of
V_{i}^{U}
, there are at most
\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}
nonzero terms in the summation. Thus, the supremum in the first term is taken over
\big\lvert N^{U}_{2^{-h},i}\big\rvert
gaussians, each with a variance at most

\gamma_{i,J,C}^{2}\cdot 2^{2i-2h}\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}\leq 2^{2i-2h}\sup_{J,C}\gamma_{i,J,C}^{2}\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}.

By the inequality for the maximum of Gaussians (Fact
2.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\E_{U}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{h=i}^{\infty}\E_{g}\sup_{J,C}\frac{\gamma_{i,J,C}}{r}\left\lvert\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}(b_{h+1,J,C}^{U}(x)-b_{h,J,C}^{U}(x))\right\rvert

(34)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{s}\sum_{h=i}^{\infty}2^{i-h/2}\cdot\sqrt{dk\log k\log s}\cdot\E_{U}\sup_{J,C}\gamma_{i,J,C}\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}^{1/2}.

We have the following lemma to bound the expected supremum on the right-hand side.

Lemma A.4
.

It holds that

\E_{U}\sup_{J,C}\gamma_{i,J,C}\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}^{1/2}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{s}{2^{i}}}

Proof.

Recall the event
\mathcal{E}
defined in (
5
). When
\mathcal{E}
happens, we have
\lVert U\cap X_{\ell}\rVert_{1}\in(1\pm\varepsilon)\cdot|X_{\ell}|
for every
\ell\in[k_{0}]
, which implies that
\frac{n}{s}\cdot\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}\in(1\pm\varepsilon)\cdot|X_{J_{i,C}}|
. Since
X
is an
(r,k_{0})
-regular instance, we know that
|X_{J_{i,C}}|\leq\frac{2|J_{i,C}|}{k_{0}}\cdot n
. It follows that
\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}\leq\frac{4|J_{i,C}|}{k_{0}}\cdot s

and

\E_{U}\left[\sup_{J,C}\frac{\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|^{1/2}_{0}}{1+2^{i}|J_{i,C}|/k_{0}}\middle|\mathcal{E}\right]\leq\E\left[\sup_{J,C}\frac{\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|^{1/2}_{0}}{2\cdot(2^{i}|J_{i,C}|/k_{0})^{1/2}}\middle|\mathcal{E}\right]\\
\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{s}{2^{i}}}

where the first inequality is by
a^{2}+b^{2}\geq 2ab
and the second inequality is by
\|U\cap X_{J_{i,C}}\|_{0}\leq\frac{4|J_{i,C}|}{k_{0}}\cdot s
.

On the other hand, it always holds that

\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\leq 8.

To see this, note that by Lemma
8.8
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)=\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)\cdot\dist(x,A)\leq\sum_{x\in U_{J}}\frac{4\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}{s}\leq 4\operatorname{cost}(X,A)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}=\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)\Delta_{\pi(x)}\leq\sum_{x\in U_{J}}\frac{4\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}{s}=4\operatorname{cost}(X,A).

Thus (
46
) always holds for
l\leq 2
.

For
l\geq 3
, we note that by the triangle inequality and
C\in L^{j}_{l,\psi}
,

\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)\geq(2^{l-1}-1)\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A).

(47)

When
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}
happens, we have that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)

(48)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sum_{i\in J}|X_{i}|\Delta_{i}=(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A).

(49)

Thus,

\displaystyle\E_{U}\left[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\E_{U}\left[\sup_{J}\bigg(\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(2^{l-1}-1)\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\;\middle|\;\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\E_{U}\left[\bigg(\frac{2(1+\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}{(2^{l-1}-1)\operatorname{cost}(X,A)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\middle|\;\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle 2^{-l/2}.

Therefore,

\E_{U}\bigg[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]\lesssim 2^{-l/2}\cdot\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}]+2\cdot(1-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}])\\
\lesssim 2^{-l/2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\lesssim 2^{-l/2},

where the last inequality is due to the fact that
2^{-l/2}\geq 2^{-l_{max}/2}\gtrsim\varepsilon^{1/2}\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{k}
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
B.6
.
∎

Lemma B.7

(Second variance bound)
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[G(J,C)\mid U]\leq 2^{-2h-\psi}\cdot\frac{k^{2}}{s}\cdot\frac{(\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i})\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A))^{2}}

and

\displaystyle\E_{U}\bigg[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{(\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i})\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A))^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]\lesssim 2^{-l}.

(50)

Proof.

By the last bound in Lemma
8.8
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)^{2}\dist(x,A)^{2}=\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{x\in U}w_{U}(x)^{2}\dist(x,A)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{x\in U}w_{U}(x)\dist(x,A)\cdot\frac{k\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},A)}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k}{s\cdot|J|}\cdot\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k^{2}}{s\cdot 2^{\psi}}\cdot\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A).

Here, for the second inequality, we used the definition of
J\in\mathcal{B}_{j}
, which means that
\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},A)\lesssim\frac{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{|J|}
for all
i\in J
, and for the last inequality, we used the fact that
k|J|\gtrsim 2^{\psi}
by the definition of
L^{J}_{l,\psi}
.

Similarly, by the second bound in Lemma
8.8
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)^{2}\Delta_{\pi(x)}^{2}=\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{x\in U_{i}}w_{U}(x)^{2}\Delta_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{x\in U_{i}}w_{U}(x)\Delta_{i}\cdot\frac{k\operatorname{cost}(X_{i},A)}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k}{s\cdot|J|}\cdot\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k^{2}}{s\cdot 2^{\psi}}\cdot\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A).

Plugging the above inequalities into (
45
) completes the proof for the first inequality.

Next, we prove the second inequality.
Similar to the proof of Lemma
B.6
, it always holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i}\leq 2\operatorname{cost}(X,A),

whence it follows that

\displaystyle\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{(\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i})\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A))^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{2}

and (
50
) holds for
l\leq 2
.

For
l\geq 3
, it follows from (
47
), (
48
) and (
49
) that

\displaystyle\E_{U}\left[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{(\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i})\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A))^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\middle|\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\E_{U}\left[\sup_{J}\frac{(2(1+\varepsilon))^{1/2}\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(2^{l-1}-1)\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\middle|\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\E_{U}\left[\frac{(2(1+\varepsilon))^{1/2}\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}{(2^{l-1}-1)\operatorname{cost}(X,A)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\middle|\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle 2^{-l}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\quad\ \E_{U}\left[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{(\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)+\sum_{i\in J}u_{i}\Delta_{i})\cdot\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},A)}{(\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A))^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-l}\cdot\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}]+2\cdot(1-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}])

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-l}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-l},

where the last inequality is due to the fact that

.

.

We are now ready to bound

.
Note that

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2^{l}k\log(k/\varepsilon)}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{2^{l_{max}}\cdot k\log(k/\varepsilon)}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{k\varepsilon^{-1}\log(k/\varepsilon)}{s}}

(51)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\log^{\nu+1}\frac{k}{\varepsilon}},

(iii)

D_{H}=\{(x_{i},|H_{i}|)\mid i\in[k]\}
denote the set of
k
weighted points added into
D
in the for loop of Algorithm
2
.
We prove that
D=D_{H}\cup D^{\prime}
is an
(\varepsilon,m,O(mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}))
-robust coreset of
X=H\cup X^{\prime}
.
Fix a center set
C\in\binom{m}{k}
.

We first argue, by losing an additive error of
\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}^{(t)}(X,C)+O(mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1})
, we can assume that the outliers in
\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(H\cup X^{\prime},C)
are always chosen from
H
and the outliers in
\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D_{H}\cup D^{\prime},C)
are always chosen from
D_{H}
.
We use the same notation
J^{\ast}
,
J
,
J^{\prime}
,
E^{\ast}
,
E
, and
E^{\prime}
as introduced in the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
Note that
J^{\prime},H\setminus J\subseteq X\setminus J^{\ast}
and
D^{\prime},D_{H}\setminus E\subseteq D\setminus E^{\ast}
.
Also, we have

\displaystyle\ \operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \operatorname{cost}_{z}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D_{H}\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(H\setminus J,C)\rvert+\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X^{\prime},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\ast}\setminus D_{H},C)\rvert+\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\ast}\setminus H,C)\rvert.

We shall bound the four terms separately.
The main difference from the case of
z=1
is that we need to use the generalized triangle inequality (Lemma
C.5
).

For the first term,

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D_{H}\setminus E,C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(H\setminus J,C)\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\lvert(|H_{i}|-t_{i})\dist^{z}(x_{i},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(H_{i}\setminus J,C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\sum_{x\in H_{i}\setminus J}\varepsilon\dist^{z}(x,C)+\frac{(3z)^{z-1}}{\varepsilon^{z-1}}\dist^{z}(x_{i},x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\sum_{i\in[k]}\operatorname{cost}_{z}(H_{i}\setminus J,C)+z^{O(z)}mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)+z^{O(z)}mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1},

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
C.5
, the third inequality follows from the fact that
\sum_{i}|H_{i}|\leq mk
, due to the construction of
H
, and the last inequality follows from the inclusion
H\setminus J\subseteq X\setminus J^{\ast}
and the definition of
J^{\ast}
.

For the second term, since
D^{\prime}
is a vanilla
\varepsilon
-coreset of
X^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X^{\prime},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X^{\prime},C)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X\setminus J,C)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X\setminus J^{\ast},C)+\varepsilon\lvert\operatorname{cost}(J,C)-\operatorname{cost}(J^{\ast},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)+\varepsilon\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\ast}\setminus H,C)\rvert.

For the third term,

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\ast}\setminus D_{H},C)\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\lvert(t_{i}-|E^{\ast}\cap D_{H}|)\dist^{z}(x_{i},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\ast}_{i}\setminus D_{H},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\sum_{x\in E^{\ast}_{i}\setminus D_{H}}\varepsilon\dist(x_{i},C)+\frac{(3z)^{z-1}}{\varepsilon^{z-1}}\dist^{z}(x_{i},x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\prime},C)+z^{O(z)}mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)+z^{O(z)}mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1},

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
C.5
, the third inequality follows from the fact that
|E^{\prime}|\leq m
, and the last inequality follows from the inclusion
E^{\prime}\subseteq D\setminus J^{\ast}
.

For the last term,

\displaystyle\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\ast}\setminus H,C)\rvert\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\lvert(t_{i}-|J^{\ast}\cap H|)\dist^{z}(x_{i},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(E^{\ast}_{i}\setminus D_{H},C)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i\in[k]}\sum_{x\in J^{\ast}\cap H}\varepsilon\dist(x_{i},C)+\frac{(3z)^{z-1}}{\varepsilon^{z-1}}\dist^{z}(x_{i},x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\prime},C)+z^{O(z)}mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)+z^{O(z)}mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1},

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
C.5
, the third inequality follows from the fact that
|J^{\prime}|\leq m
, and the last inequality follows from the inclusion
J^{\prime}\subseteq X\setminus J^{\ast}
and the definition of
J^{\ast}
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)\leq

\displaystyle\ \operatorname{cost}_{z}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)+z^{O(z)}mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

\displaystyle\ +\varepsilon\lvert\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\prime},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(J^{\ast}\setminus H,C)\rvert+\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 3\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)+z^{O(z)}mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}.

It implies that

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)\leq

\displaystyle\ (1-\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D\setminus E^{\ast},C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (1+3\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(X,C)+z^{O(z)}mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}.

Similarly, we can prove that

(1-\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)\leq(1+3\varepsilon)\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}(D,C)+z^{O(z)}mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}.

Thus,
D
is an
(\varepsilon,m,O(mkr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}))
-robust coreset of
X
, which completes the proof of Lemma
C.2
.

The algorithm we used here is nearly identical to that provided in Section

•

Lemma D.2

(Robust cost decomposition for general
z\geq 1
)
.

If
D
is a capacity-respecting weighted subset of
X
, we have that for every
C\in\binom{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{k}
, and every
t\in[0,m]
, there exists an index
j_{t}^{C}\in[k]
, an index subset
I_{t}^{C}\subseteq[k]
, and a constant
\alpha_{t}^{C}\in[0,1]
such that

\displaystyle|\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)-\alpha_{t}^{C}\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{j_{t}^{C}},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{I_{t}^{C}},C)|\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)+z^{O(z)}mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

\displaystyle|\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)-\alpha_{t}^{C}\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D_{j_{t}^{C}},C)-\operatorname{cost}_{z}(D_{I_{t}^{C}},C)|\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)+z^{O(z)}mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of Lemma
8.5
. By the generalized triangle inequality,

\left\lvert\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{i_{l}},C)}{\lvert X_{i_{l}}\rvert}-\dist^{z}(a_{i_{l}},C)\right\rvert\leq\varepsilon\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{i_{l}},C)}{\lvert X_{i_{l}}\rvert}+\frac{(3z)^{z-1}}{\varepsilon^{z-1}}r^{z}.

Suppose that
x\in X_{i_{j}}
for some
j\geq l
. Then, again by the generalized triangle inequality,

\lvert\dist^{z}(a_{j},C)-\dist^{z}(x,C)\rvert\leq\varepsilon\dist^{z}(a_{j},C)+\frac{(3z)^{z-1}}{\varepsilon^{z-1}}r^{z}.

For notational simplicity, let
\Delta_{\varepsilon}=((3z)^{z-1}/\varepsilon^{z-1})r^{z}
. Then

(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{i_{l}},C)}{\lvert X_{i_{l}}\rvert}+(2+\varepsilon)\Delta_{\varepsilon}=(1+\varepsilon)\left[(1+\varepsilon)\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{i_{l}},C)}{\lvert X_{i_{l}}\rvert}+\Delta_{\varepsilon}\right]+\Delta_{\varepsilon}\\
\geq(1+\varepsilon)\dist^{z}(a_{i_{l}},C)+\Delta_{\varepsilon}\geq(1+\varepsilon)\dist^{z}(a_{i_{j}},C)+\Delta_{\varepsilon}\geq\dist^{z}(x,C).

This implies that

\operatorname{Cut}(L,C,\beta)\leq\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{i_{l}},C)}{\lvert X_{i_{l}}\rvert}+\left(3\varepsilon\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{i_{l}},C)}{\lvert X_{i_{l}}\rvert}+(2+\varepsilon)\Delta_{\varepsilon}\right).

The claimed result will then follow from the same argument with a rescaling of
\varepsilon
.
∎

\displaystyle\left\lvert\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)-\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(X,C)+\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{cost}^{(t)}_{z}(D,C)+2\Gamma mr^{z}/\varepsilon^{z-1}

With this setup, the key is to prove the following lemma.

Lemma D.4

(Properties of

With probability at least

(Adaptation of Lemmata B.2 and B.11 of

For

Proof.

The adaptation consists of two steps. First, we partition

Telescoping

Let
h_{max}=\lceil(z+3)\log\frac{k}{\varepsilon}\rceil

Similarly, it remains to control the initial term, main terms, and the residual individually. Let

Lemma D.6

(Residual term)
.

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot(v_{C}^{J}(x)-q_{C}^{h_{max}}(x))}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\log^{\nu}\frac{k}{\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

Let
\beta\asymp\varepsilon/\log^{\nu}(k/\varepsilon)

By the choice of

\displaystyle|v_{C}^{J}(x)-q_{C}^{h_{max}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\varepsilon/k)^{z+3}\cdot(\sqrt{\dist^{z}(x,C)\dist^{z}(x,A)}+\dist^{z}(x,A))\cdot\varepsilon^{-z/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\varepsilon/k)^{z/2+3}\cdot(\sqrt{\dist^{z}(x,C)\dist^{z}(x,A)}+\dist^{z}(x,A))

\displaystyle\lesssim(\varepsilon/k)^{z/2+3}\cdot\varepsilon^{-z/2}\dist^{z}(x,A)

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta^{2}\dist^{z}(x,A).

Thus,

\frac{w_{U}(x)\lvert v_{C}^{J}(x)-q_{C}^{h_{max}}\rvert}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\lesssim\frac{\beta^{2}}{s}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{s}.

Similarly to the proof of Lemma

\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot(v_{C}^{J}(x)-q_{C}^{h_{max}}(x))}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim\E\|g\|_{2}\cdot\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{s}}\leq\beta.\qed

Lemma D.7

(Main telescoping terms)
.

For each
h\in[h_{max}]
,

\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot(q_{C}^{h}(x)-q_{C}^{h-1}(x))}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\log^{\nu+1}\frac{k}{\varepsilon}}.

Let
G(J,C)=\frac{\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot(q_{C}^{h}(x)-q_{C}^{h-1}(x))}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}
, we have the following bound on the variance of
G(J,C)
. Lemma
D.7
can be proved by combining Lemma
D.5
and Lemma
D.8
.

Lemma D.8

(Variance bound of main telescoping terms)
.

\displaystyle\Var[G(J,C)\mid U]\leq\frac{2^{-2h}}{s}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},A)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}

and

\displaystyle\E_{U}\bigg[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},A)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]\lesssim 1.

(56)

Proof.

By plugging the error term of
\alpha
-net, we have that,

\displaystyle\Var[G(J,C)\mid U]

\displaystyle\lesssim{}

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-2h}\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)^{2}(\dist^{z}(x,C)\dist^{z}(x,A)+\dist^{z}(x,A)^{2})}{(\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A))^{2}}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{2^{-2h}\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)^{2}\cdot\dist^{z}(x,A)\cdot(\dist^{z}(x,C)+\dist^{z}(x,A))}{(\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A))\cdot\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}.

By Lemma
8.8
, we have
w_{U}(x)\dist^{z}(x,A)\leq\frac{4}{s}\cdot\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)
. It follows that

\Var[G(J,C)\mid U]\lesssim\frac{2^{-2h}}{s}\cdot\frac{\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(U_{J},A)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}.

Next, we prove (
56
). By an identical argument to the proof of Lemma
A.4
, it always holds that

\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},A)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}\leq 4.

Thus, it suffices to prove that

\E_{U}\bigg[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]\lesssim 1.

When
\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}
holds, we have that, by the generalized triangle inequality (Lemma
C.5
),

\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},C)\lesssim\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},A)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},A).

On the other hand, it always holds that

\displaystyle\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},C)=\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)\dist^{z}(x,C)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{l_{max}}\sum_{x\in U_{J}}w_{U}(x)\dist^{z}(x,A)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{l_{max}}\sum_{x\in U_{J}}\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{l_{max}}\cdot\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-z}\cdot\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A).

Thus,

\displaystyle\E_{U}\bigg[\sup_{J,C}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(U_{J},C)}{\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}_{z}(X,A)}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\lesssim 1\cdot\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}]+\varepsilon^{-z}\cdot(1-\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}_{0}\cup\mathcal{E}_{1}])

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\varepsilon^{-z}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon^{z}}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim 1.\qed

Lemma D.9

(Initial telescoping term)
.

\displaystyle\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot q_{C}^{0}(x)}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\log^{\nu}\frac{k}{\varepsilon}}.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
B.3
, we define a rounding term of
d^{z}(a_{i},C)
, denoted by

d_{i}=\big\lceil\frac{\dist^{z}(a_{i},C)}{\Delta_{i}}\big\rceil\cdot\Delta_{i}.

Similarly, the number of distinct tuples of
(d_{i})_{i\in J}
can be bounded by
2^{O(kl_{max})}\leq|N_{1/2}|
. Now we consider

\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U_{J}}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot(q_{C}^{0}(x)-d_{\pi(x)})}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|.

(57)

We have
|q_{C}^{0}(x)-d_{\pi(x)}|\lesssim\operatorname{err}(x,C)+\dist^{z}(x,A)+\Delta_{\pi(x)}
. Moreover, the number of distinct vectors induced by
(q_{C}^{0}(x)-d_{\pi(x)})_{x\in U}
can be bounded by
2^{k}|N_{1/2}|^{2}
. Hence, (
57
) has essentially the same order variance and logarithm of net size as

\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U_{J}}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot(q_{C}^{0}(x)-q_{C}^{1}(x))}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|,

thus can be controlled in the same manner as in the proof of Lemma
D.7
.
It remains to control

\E\sup_{J,C}\bigg|\frac{\sum_{x\in U_{J}}g_{x}w_{U}(x)\cdot d_{\pi(x)}}{\operatorname{cost}(X_{J},C)+\operatorname{cost}(X,A)}\bigg|,

(58)

which has an identical form as the first term of (
41
). Thus, (
58
) can be controlled identically.
∎

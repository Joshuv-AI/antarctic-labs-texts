An edge-spectral Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem and its stability
Yongtao Li, Hong Liu, Shengtong Zhang

Abstract
We study the extremal problem that relates the spectral radius λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(G) of an F F -free graph G G with its number of edges. Firstly, we prove that for any graph F F with chromatic number χ ⁡ ( F ) = r + 1 ≥ 3 \chi(F)=r+1\geq 3 , if G G is an F F -free graph on m m edges, then λ 2 ​ ( G ) ≤ ( 1 − 1 r + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ 2 ​ m \lambda^{2}(G)\leq{(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1))2m} . We also establish the corresponding edge-spectral stability, showing that if G G is an F F -free graph on m m edges with λ 2 ​ ( G ) = ( 1 − 1 r − o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ​ 2 ​ m \lambda^{2}(G)=(1-\frac{1}{r}-o(1))2m , then G G differs from a complete bipartite graph by o ⁡ ( m ) o(m) edges when r = 2 r=2 , and G G differs from an r r -partite Turán graph by o ⁡ ( m ) o(m) edges when r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 . Introduction

Extremal graph theory investigates how local structural constraints affect global graph parameters with deep connections to other mathematical disciplines. The field originated with foundational questions about how dense a graph can be while avoiding certain forbidden subgraphs, giving rise to its most classical theme: Turán-type problems. The solution to such problems often involves both precise bounds and the characterization of graphs that achieve them; see
[
3
,
19
,
44
,
54
]
.
The stability results describe how nearly extremal graphs – those close to the maximum size – must structurally resemble the extremal maximizer; see
[
43
]
.

A graph
G
is
F
-free if it does not contain a subgraph isomorphic to
F
. A well-known theorem of Turán
[
47
]

states that every
n
-vertex
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G
satisfies
e(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
;
see
[
3
, p. 294]
and
[
1
, p. 269]
.
Since then there are various extensions and generalizations on Turán’s theorem; see, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
]
.
The
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
is defined as the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph.
A celebrated extension of Turán’s theorem attributes to the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem
[
15
,
14
]
, which states that the asymptotics of
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
is determined by the chromatic number of
F
. In particular, for any graph
F
with chromatic number
\chi(F)=r+1
, we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
.

In this paper, we study the spectral extensions of Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem. Spectral graph theory aims to apply the eigenvalues and ranks of matrices associated with graphs to find the structural information of graphs.
We refer to
[
23
,
24
]
for recent breakthroughs and applications.
The adjacency matrix of an
n
-vertex graph
G
is defined as
A_{G}=[a_{i,j}]_{i,j=1}^{n}
, where
a_{i,j}=1
if
ij\in E(G)
, and
a_{i,j}=0
otherwise. Observe that
A_{G}
is a real and symmetric nonnegative matrix.
Let
\lambda(G)
be the spectral radius of
G
, which is defined as the maximum absolute value of the eigenvalues of
A_{G}
.
Spectral extremal graph theory seeks the connections between graph properties and eigenvalues of the associated matrices. As a result of this topic, many of the classical extremal graph results have been generalized to spectral statements.
For instance, Wilf
[
48
]
proved a spectral extension of Turán’s theorem, showing that if
G
is an
n
-vertex
K_{r+1}
-free graph, then
\lambda(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)n
. This was generalized by Nikiforov
[
37
]
in 2009, who established the following spectral counterpart of the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Nikiforov
[
37
]
)
.

If
\chi(F)=r+1
and
G
is an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph, then

\lambda(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)n.

1.2
Extension of the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem

Our first result resolves the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem asymptotically for any graph
F
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
and
G
is an
F
-free graph with
m
edges, then

\lambda^{2}(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+o(1)\right)2m.

The bound in
Theorem

1.2
is tight
when
G
is chosen as an
r
-partite Turán graph with
m
edges.

Theorem

1.2
confirms a conjecture of Li, Liu and Feng
[
28
, Conjecture 1.31]
. Furthermore, combined with the Rayleigh bound
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}
,
Theorem

1.2
implies both the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem and its vertex-spectral version in
Theorem

1.1
.

The condition
\chi(F)\geq 3
in
Theorem

1.2

is necessary, since
Theorem

1.2
does not hold for all bipartite graphs.
For example, taking
F=K_{2,t}
,
we can see that
K_{1,m}
is
F
-free and
\lambda(K_{1,m})=\sqrt{m}
.
We plan to explore the bipartite case in our future work.

In 2006,
Nikiforov
[
34
]
further generalized (
1
).
An
\ell
-walk is a sequence of
\ell

of

Let

denote the number of

,

w_{3}(G)=\sum_{v\in V}d_{v}^{2}

Theorem 1.3

(Nikiforov

]
)
.

If

-free graph, then for every

\lambda^{\ell}(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)\cdot w_{\ell}(G).

Note that the case

Very recently, Chao and Yu

extended

using the entropy method, connecting powers of spectral radius to homomorphisms of trees in

-free graphs.

Our framework of proof for

is amenable to prove the following result, which generalizes

.

If

1.3
An edge-spectral stability result

In 1966,
Erdős
[
11
,
12
]
and Simonovits
[
43
]
proved a stability phenomenon for Turán type problems. Given two graphs
G
and
H
, the
edit distance

d(G,H)
is the minimum number of edges we need to change (add/remove) to transfer one graph to the other. The Erdős–Simonovits stability states that for any graph
F
with
\chi(F)=r+1
, any
n
-vertex
F
-free graph
G
with

\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-o(1)\right)\frac{n^{2}}{2}
edges must be of edit distance
o(n^{2})
from the balanced complete
r
-partite Turán graph
T_{n,r}
.
We refer the readers to
[
18
]
for a short proof.

The following vertex-spectral stability can be deduced from a result of Nikiforov
[
38
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(Nikiforov
[
38
]
)
.

Let
F
be a graph with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0

and
n_{0}
such that
if
G
is an
F
-free graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices with
\lambda(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta\right)n,

then
G
differs from
T_{n,r}
in at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

Theorem

1.5
provides an effective approach for
studying the spectral problems when forbidding a non-bipartite graph
F
and has brought several nice applications in recent years.

In this section, we show a similar stability result for the edge-spectral Turán problem.
For two disjoint vertex sets
A,B
,
we write
K_{A,B}
for the complete bipartite graph on the parts
A
and
B
.
For a vertex set
C
, we write
T_{C,r}

for an
r
-partite Turán graph on the vertex set
C
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
F
be a graph with
\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
,
there exist
\delta>0
and
m_{0}
such that the following holds. If
G
is an
F
-free graph with
m\geq m_{0}
edges and
\lambda^{2}(G)\geq(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta)2m
, then

(a)

When
r=2
, there exist disjoint vertex sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
such that
d(G,K_{A,B})\leq\varepsilon m
.

(b)

When
r\geq 3
, there exists a vertex set
C\subseteq V(G)
and a Turán graph
T_{C,r}
on
C
such that
d(G,T_{C,r})\leq\varepsilon m
.

Similarly, combining with the Rayleigh bound
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}
, the edge-spectral stability in
Theorem

1.6
generalizes both the Erdős–Simonovits stability and the Nikiforov vertex-spectral stability.
We remark that when
r=2
, our edge-spectral version in
Theorem

1.6
differs substantially from the other two stabilities, as the graph in
Theorem

1.6
can be close to any complete bipartite graph instead of just the balanced complete bipartite graph. Moreover, we emphasize that the disjoint sets
A
and
B
are not necessarily a partition of vertices of
G
, e.g., when
G
contains some isolated vertices or ‘bad’ vertices with sparse edges.

In next subsection, we will provide a quick application of the edge-spectral stability theorem.
We expect that
Theorem

1.6
will serve as a useful tool for solving edge-spectral Turán problems. In subsequent work, we will return to this topic and use
Theorem

1.6
to derive some interesting consequences for the Brualdi–Hoffman–Turán problem.

1.4
Application: maximizing common neighbors

The classical Turán result of Füredi
[
17
]
states that
\mathrm{ex}(n,K_{2,t+1})=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{t}n^{3/2}+O(n^{4/3})
.
In contrast, when forbidding bipartite graphs, the edge-spectral extremal problem has a rather different behavior.
In 2021, Zhai, Lin and Shu
[
50
]
proved the following spectral result. We refer the readers to
[
35
]
for the case
t=1
and to
[
51
,
40
,
27
]
for recent results in this direction.

Theorem 1.7

(Zhai–Lin–Shu
[
50
]
)
.

Let
t\geq 2
and
m\geq 16t^{2}
be integers.
If
G
is a
K_{2,t+1}
-free graph with
m
edges, then
\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}
, with equality if and only if
G
is a star.

We point out here that the quadratic dependence on
m
in terms of
t
is necessary. Moreover, the above result implies that in any graph
G
with
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, we can find a copy of
K_{2,t}
with
t=\sqrt{m}/4
. On the other hand, there are many examples showing that such a graph
G
cannot contain a copy of
K_{2,t}
with
t=\sqrt{m}
.
Using the edge-spectral stability
Theorem

1.6
, we can obtain the following improvement with the optimal bound on
t
.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
G
is an
m
-edge graph with

\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, then
G
contains a copy of
K_{2,t}
with
t\geq\sqrt{m}/2-O(1)
. Moreover, the bound on
t
is the best possible.

Our approach.

To prove
Theorem

1.2
, we use the removal lemma to pass from
G
to a
K_{r+1}
-free subgraph
G^{\prime}
by deleting
o(|V(G)|^{2})
edges. If
G
is dense, we would be done with the help of Nikiforov’s result as the removal of these
o(|V(G)|^{2})
edges contributes
o(\sqrt{m})
to the spectral radius of
G
. Our key novel step in the proof of
Theorem

1.2
is to make a reduction to the dense case. To this end,
we first show that if
G
is a minimal counterexample, then the weight of every edge
uv
in
G
with respect to the Perron–Frobenius vector is large:
x_{u}x_{v}=\Omega(m^{-1/2})
(
Lemma

2.2
). Secondly,
we bound the
\infty
-norm of the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
by showing that
\max_{v\in V}\{x_{v}\}=O(m^{-1/4})
(
Lemma

2.3
). Combining these two bounds, we can then deduce that the Perron–Frobenius vector of any possible minimal counterexample must be flat, i.e. each of its coordinates is of order
\Theta(m^{-1/4})
. As a result, this minimal counterexample
G
must be dense:
|V(G)|=O(\sqrt{m})
. This approach can be extended to prove
Theorem

1.4
. Different from the edge-spectral setting, we will prove a reduction so that
|V(G)|=O(\lambda(G))
in the walk-spectral setting for
Theorem

1.4
.

For Theorem
1.6
, we deliver the proof in three stages. Firstly, we deal with the case when we forbid triangles (
Theorem

4.1
). Using the second moment of the spectral radius of
G
, we can remove
o(m)
edges from
G
to obtain a bipartite subgraph
G^{\prime}
(
Lemma

4.2
). We then show that bipartite graphs with spectral radius close to
\sqrt{m}
must be close to complete via truncation on its Perron–Frobenius eigenvector (
Lemma

4.4
). Secondly, when we forbid a clique
K_{r+1}
, where
r\geq 3
(
Theorem

4.8
), we exploit the slackness from the use of Motzkin–Strauss inequality in Nikiforov’s bound (
1
).
This is the bulk of our proof for
Theorem

1.6
. We analyze the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
and use the slackness to show that most of the edges in
G
has Perron–Frobenius weight around
\Theta(m^{-1/2})
. From this fact, we are able to locate a subset
V^{\prime}
of vertices in which every vertex has weight about
\Theta(m^{-1/4})
. Furthermore, majority of the edges lie within
V^{\prime}
. As a result, the induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
on
V^{\prime}
satisfies
d(G,G^{\prime})=o(m)
and
|V^{\prime}|\leq(1+o(1))\sqrt{2e(G^{\prime})/(1-\frac{1}{r})}
, which implies
\lambda(G^{\prime})\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-o(1)\right)|V^{\prime}|
(
Lemma

4.9
). We can then apply the vertex-spectral stability to
G^{\prime}
to finish the proof. Finally, when forbidding a general graph
F
with
\chi(F)=r+1
, we first remove few “bad” edges of
G
that have small contributions to
\lambda(G)
to obtain a large subgraph
G^{\prime}
on vertex set
V^{\prime}
such that
x_{i}^{\prime}x_{j}^{\prime}=\Omega(m^{-1/2})
for every
ij\in E(G^{\prime})
(
Lemma

4.12
). If
r\geq 3
, we can apply
Lemma

2.3
to show that
\max_{v\in V^{\prime}}\{x^{\prime}_{v}\}=O(m^{-1/4})
, which implies
|V^{\prime}|=O(\sqrt{m})
.
Then we can use removal lemma to pass to a
K_{r+1}
-free graph
G^{\prime\prime}
by removing
o(|V^{\prime}|^{2})=o(m)
edges of
G^{\prime}
. This allows us to reduce the general case to the clique case that we have established previously. For the case when
r=2
,
Lemma

2.3
cannot be applied and
it is possible that
\max_{v\in V^{\prime}}\{x_{v}^{\prime}\}=\Omega(m^{-1/4})
.
We need to treat this case separately (
Lemma

4.13
). Here we show that
G^{\prime}
contains a bipartite subgraph
G^{\prime\prime}
with large spectral radius, and we can use
Lemma

4.4
to finish the proof.

For
Theorem

1.8
, we first show that if

\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, then

t+2m\cdot(\max_{i\in V}x_{i})^{4}>\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m}
, where
t
is the maximum codegree. If
\max_{i\in V}\{x_{i}\}=O(m^{-1/4})
, then
t\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{m}-O(1)
, as desired. If
\max_{i\in V}\{x_{i}\}=\Omega(m^{-1/4})
, then we can use the edge-spectral stability (
Lemma

4.13
) to get two disjoint vertex sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
such that
d(G,K_{A,B})\leq\varepsilon m
. Assume that
|A|\leq|B|
. As
\lambda(G)>\sqrt{m}
, we can use a structural result (
Lemma

2.7
) to show that
|A|\geq 2
. Then by a standard average argument, there exist two vertices of
A
that have at least
(1-o(1))\sqrt{m}
common neighbors in
B
, as desired.

Notation.

We write
G=(V,E)
for a simple and undirected graph with vertex set
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and edge set
E=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
, where we admit

n=|V|
and
m=|E|
.
We write
uv
for an edge if
u
and
v
are adjacent.
The degree of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is denoted by

d_{v}
, and the set of neighbors of
v
is denoted by
N(v)
.
The maximum degree of
G
is denoted by

\Delta(G)
.
Let
w_{\ell}(G)
be the number of walks in
G
consisting of
\ell
vertices (we call it an
\ell
-walk).
Let
\bm{1}_{v}
be the characteristic vector of a vertex
v
.
For
U\subseteq V(G)
,
we write
d_{U}(v)
for the number of vertices of
U
that are adjacent to the vertex
v
.
For two disjoint sets
U,W\subseteq V(G)
,
we write
e(U)
for the number of edges with endpoints in
U
, and
e(U,W)
for the number of edges between
U
and
W
.
The edit distance between two graphs
G
and
H
is denoted by
d(G,H)
, which is the minimum number of edges we need to change (delete or add) to transform
G
into
H
.

The adjacency matrix
A_{G}

of a graph
G
is a symmetric matrix of dimension
|V|\times|V|
with
a_{i,j}=1
if and only if
ij\in E
, and
a_{i,j}=0
otherwise.
Since
A_{G}
is real and symmetric,
all eigenvalues of
A_{G}
are real and can be reordered as
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. The eigenvalues of a graph
G
are defined as the eigenvalues of the matrix
A_{G}
.
The spectral radius
\lambda(G)
is defined to be the maximum absolute value of eigenvalues of
G
. By the Perron–Frobenius theorem,
the spectral radius of a nonnegative matrix is actually a largest eigenvalue. Furthermore, there exists a unit nonnegative eigenvector
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
corresponding to
\lambda(G)
.
Such a vector is called the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector. For each vertex
v\in V(G)
,
we write
x_{v}
for the coordinate of
\bm{x}
corresponding to
v
.
In the setting of graphs,
the eigen-equation
A_{G}\bm{x}=\lambda(G)\bm{x}
can be interpreted as
\lambda(G)x_{v}=\sum_{u\in N(v)}x_{u}
, and the Rayleigh quotient implies

\lambda(G)=2\sum_{uv\in E}x_{u}x_{v}
.
Here and in the rest of the paper, we denote by
\sum_{uv\in E}
the sum over each edge in
E
once. When
G
is a bipartite graph with given partite sets
A
and
B
, we additionally assume that
u\in A
and
v\in B
.

Preliminary results

In this section, we collect a number of results that we will use throughout our proofs. The first one is a result of Erdős, Frankl and Rödl
[
13
]
which allows us to delete
o(n^{2})
edges and transform an
F
-free graph to a
K_{r+1}
-free graph.

Lemma 2.1

(Erdős–Frankl–Rödl
[
13
]
)
.

Let
F
be a graph with
\chi(F)=r+1
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
,
there is
n_{0}
such that
if
n\geq n_{0}
and
G
is an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph,
then we can remove at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges from
G

so that the remaining graph is
K_{r+1}
-free.

The following lemma states that if
G
is a minimal graph with a certain bound on the spectral radius, then the product of the Perron–Frobenius weights of each edge is at least
\Omega(m^{-1/2})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
,
\varepsilon>0
,
C>0
and
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(G)}
be the unit Perron–Frobenius vector of
G
.
Let
G
be an
m
-edge graph with

\lambda(G)>\sqrt{(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon)2m}+C
.
If for any
m^{\prime}
-edge subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
,
\lambda(G^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon)2m^{\prime}}+C
, then for any edge
uv\in E(G)
, we have
x_{u}x_{v}>\frac{1}{8\sqrt{m}}.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
x_{u}x_{v}\leq 8^{-1}m^{-1/2}

for some edge
uv\in E(G)
. Then removing the edge

uv
from
E(G)
, we get a subgraph
G^{\prime}
with
m^{\prime}=m-1
edges and

\displaystyle\lambda(G^{\prime})\geq\left(2\sum_{ij\in E(G)}x_{i}x_{j}\right)-2x_{u}x_{v}\geq\lambda(G)-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{m}}>\sqrt{\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\Bigr)2m^{\prime}}+C.

This contradicts with the assumption of
G
.
∎

The following lemma says that if
G
is an
m
-edge graph with spectral radius bounded away from
\sqrt{m}
, then every coordinate of the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
is small.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph with
m
edges and
\bm{x}=(x_{v})_{v\in V(G)}
be the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
.
If
\lambda^{2}(G)\geq(1+\delta)m
where
0<\delta\leq 0.79
, then

\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(G)\}<{\delta^{-4}m^{-1/4}}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(G)
and

i\in V(G)
be a vertex such that

x_{i}=\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(G)\}
.
Suppose on the contrary that

x_{i}\geq\delta^{-4}m^{-1/4}
. Note that
\lambda\bm{x}=A_{G}\bm{x}
gives

\lambda x_{i}=\sum_{j\in N(i)}x_{j}.

Then we have

\lambda^{2}x_{i}=\sum_{j\in N(i)}\lambda x_{j}=\sum_{j\in N(i)}\sum_{k\in N(j)}x_{k}=\sum_{k\in V(G)}d_{N(i)}(k)\cdot x_{k},

where
d_{N(i)}(k)
is the number of neighbors of
k
in
N(i)
. Therefore, we get

\lambda^{2}x_{i}\leq\sum_{k\in V(G)}d_{G}(k)\cdot x_{k}=\sum_{jk\in E(G)}(x_{j}+x_{k}).

(2)

For each edge
jk\in E(G)
, we call
jk

good
if
\min\{x_{j},x_{k}\}>\delta^{-2}m^{-1/4}
. Since

\sum_{v\in V}x_{v}^{2}=1
, we get
\#\{j\in V(G):x_{j}>\delta^{-2}m^{-1/4}\}<\delta^{4}m^{1/2}
. Then
the number of good edges of
G
is at most
\delta^{8}m/2
.
On the other hand, for each non-good edge

jk\in E(G)
, we have

x_{j}+x_{k}\leq x_{i}+\delta^{-2}m^{-1/4}\leq(1+\delta^{2})x_{i}.

Therefore, it follows from (
2
) that

(1+\delta)mx_{i}\leq\lambda^{2}x_{i}\leq 2x_{i}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\delta^{8}m+(1+\delta^{2})x_{i}\cdot m=(1+\delta^{2}+\delta^{8})mx_{i}.

Since
0<\delta<0.79
, we have

\delta>\delta^{2}+\delta^{8}
,
which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
G
is a graph with
m
edges, and
G^{\prime}
is the graph obtained from
G
by removing at most
\gamma m
edges, then
\lambda(G^{\prime})>\lambda(G)-\sqrt{2\gamma m}
and

\lambda^{2}(G^{\prime})>\lambda^{2}(G)-4\sqrt{\gamma}m.

Proof.

By Rayleigh’s formula, we have
\lambda(G^{\prime})\geq\lambda(G)-\lambda(G\backslash G^{\prime})
. Thus

\lambda^{2}(G^{\prime})\geq\lambda^{2}(G)-2\lambda(G\backslash G^{\prime})\lambda(G).

Using the bound
\lambda(G)<\sqrt{2m}
and
\lambda(G\backslash G^{\prime})<\sqrt{2\gamma m}
, we get the desired result.
∎

The following well–known fact (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) will be used in our argument.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with the partition
V(G)=A\sqcup B
, and
\binom{\bm{x}}{\bm{y}}
be an eigenvector of
G
corresponding to
\lambda(G)
with
\bm{x}
indexed by
A
and
\bm{y}
indexed by
B
. Then we have
\lVert\bm{x}\rVert=\lVert\bm{y}\rVert
, i.e.,

\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j\in B}y_{j}^{2}.

We provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proof.

Note that
G
is a bipartite graph. Then the adjacency matrix of
G
is of the form:

A_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}O&M\\
M^{T}&O\end{bmatrix},

where
M
is a
(0,1)
-matrix of order
|A|\times|B|
with

m_{i,j}=1
if the vertex
i\in A
is adjacent to the vertex
j\in B
. As
\binom{\bm{x}}{\bm{y}}
is the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
A_{G}
, we have

M\bm{y}=\lambda(G)\bm{x}
and

M^{T}\bm{x}=\lambda(G)\bm{y}
.
Now, let
M=UDV
be a singular value decomposition (see, e.g.,
[
52
, p. 15]
) of
M
, where
U,V
are real orthogonal matrices, and
D
is an
|A|\times|B|
matrix with
(i,i)
-entries being the singular values of
M
. Since
M\bm{y}=\lambda(G)\bm{x}
and
U^{T}U=I
, we get
DV\bm{y}=\lambda(G)U^{T}\bm{x}
, which implies that for each index
i
,

D_{ii}\cdot(V\bm{y})_{i}=\lambda(G)\cdot(U^{T}\bm{x})_{i}.

Similarly, we obtain from
M^{T}\bm{x}=\lambda(G)\bm{y}
that
D^{T}U^{T}\bm{x}=\lambda(G)V\bm{y}
. Then for each
i
,

D_{ii}\cdot(U^{T}\bm{x})_{i}=\lambda(G)\cdot(V\bm{y})_{i}.

Combining with the above two equalities,
we conclude that
|(V\bm{y})_{i}|=|(U^{T}\bm{x})_{i}|
, which yields

\lVert V\bm{y}\rVert=\lVert U^{T}\bm{x}\rVert
. Since both
V
and
U^{T}
are orthogonal matrices, we get
\lVert\bm{y}\rVert=\lVert\bm{x}\rVert
.
∎

We will also utilize the well-known Motzkin–Straus inequality
[
32
]
.

Lemma 2.6

(Motzkin–Straus
[
32
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer and
G
be a
K_{r+1}
-free graph on
n

If
\bm{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is a non-negative real vector with

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1
, then

\sum_{ij\in E(G)}x_{i}x_{j}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right).

The equality holds if and only if the subgraph induced by the vertices corresponding to non-zero entries of
\bm{x}
is a complete
r
-partite graph such that the sum of the
x_{i}
’s in each part is the same.

.

Lemma 2.7

(Li–Liu–Zhang
[

)
.

Let

If

3
Extensions on Erdős–Stone–Simonovits’ theorem

3.1
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

Before proving
Theorem

1.2
,
we present a short proof of
Theorem

1.1
which is quite different from Nikiforov’s proof in
[
37
]
.
It illustrates the basic ideas in our proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Short proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

The lower bound can be witnessed by the Turán graph
T_{n,r}
since
T_{n,r}
is
F
-free and
\lambda(T_{n,r})\geq(1-\frac{1}{r})n-\frac{r}{4n}
.
Assume that
G
is an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph.
By Lemma
2.1
, we can remove
o(n^{2})
edges from

G
and get a
K_{r+1}
-free subgraph
G^{\prime}
. Note that

\lambda(G\setminus G^{\prime})<\sqrt{2e(G\setminus G^{\prime})}=o(n).

Since
G^{\prime}
is
K_{r+1}
-free, by Wilf’s theorem, we have
\lambda(G^{\prime})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)n
.
Therefore, we obtain

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})+\lambda(G\setminus G^{\prime})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)n+o(n),

which completes the proof.
∎

Now we prove
Theorem

1.2
.
In our proof of
Theorem

1.1
, we remove
o(n^{2})
edges from
G

to make it
K_{r+1}
-free, and then apply Wilf’s bound.
For
Theorem

1.2
, we need to show that the removal of these edges changes the spectral radius by
o(m^{1/2})
, which is true in the dense case when
m=\Omega(n^{2})
. Our key idea is to make a reduction to the case of dense graphs using Lemma
2.3
, which
gives an upper bound on
\max_{v\in V(G)}\{x_{v}\}
, where
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
.
We note that the assumptions of Lemma
2.3
do not directly imply that
G
is dense. For example,
G
can be the disjoint union of a clique
K_{n}
with arbitrarily many isolated vertices. We will address this obstacle by a vertex deletion argument.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

By Lemma
2.1
, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
N(\varepsilon)>0
such that any
F
-free graph on
n\geq N(\varepsilon)
vertices can be made
K_{r+1}
-free by removing at most
\varepsilon^{20}n^{2}
edges. Assume that
G
is an
F
-free graph with
m
edges.
It suffices to prove that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/3)
, we have

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\Bigr)2m}+N(\varepsilon).

Suppose on the contrary that

G
is an
F
-free graph with
m
edges and

\lambda(G)>\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)2m}+N(\varepsilon).

(3)

Among such counterexamples, we choose a graph
G
such that
|V(G)|+|E(G)|
is minimal. Denote
|V(G)|=n
. It is clear that
G
has no isolated vertices. As removing any edge does not create a counterexample due to the minimality of
G
,
then for any
m^{\prime}
-edge subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
, we have

\lambda(G^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon)2m^{\prime}}+N(\varepsilon)
.
Using Lemma
2.2
,
for any edge
uv\in E(G)
, we have

x_{u}x_{v}>\frac{1}{8\sqrt{m}}.

Furthermore, by Lemma
2.3
,
we have

\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(G)\}\leq(2\varepsilon)^{-4}m^{-1/4}.

These two bounds above, together with the fact that
G
has no isolated vertex, imply that

\min\{x_{v}:v\in V(G)\}>2\varepsilon^{4}m^{-1/4}.

As
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a unit vector,
it follows that

n\leq 4^{-1}\varepsilon^{-8}m^{1/2}.

(4)

On the other hand, we get from (
3
) that
n\geq\lambda(G)\geq N(\varepsilon)
.
By
Lemma

2.1
, we can remove at most
\varepsilon^{20}n^{2}
edges from
G
to get a
K_{r+1}
-free subgraph
G^{\prime}
with
m^{\prime}
edges. Note that
\varepsilon^{20}n^{2}<\varepsilon^{4}m
by (
4
).
Now we use the Nikiforov bound (
1
) and we get

\lambda(G^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)2m^{\prime}}.

Thus, we conclude that

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})+\lambda(G\setminus G^{\prime})<\sqrt{\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)2m^{\prime}}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon^{4}m},

which contradicts with (
3
). This completes the proof.
∎

3.2
Proof of
Theorem

1.4

To prove
Theorem

1.4
, we first show a generalization of
Lemma

2.3
for walks.
Recall that an
\ell
-walk is a walk with
\ell
vertices.
For two vertices
i,j
in a graph
G
, let
w_{\ell}(i)
denote the number of
\ell
-walks starting from
i
, and let
w_{\ell}(i,j)
denote the number of
\ell
-walks from
i
to
j
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\bm{x}=(x_{j})_{j\in V(G)}
be its unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector. If
\lambda^{\ell}(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\cdot w_{\ell}(G)
for some
\delta\in(0,0.1)
, then

\max\{x_{j}:j\in V(G)\}<\delta^{-4}\lambda(G)^{-1/2}.

Proof.

We denote
\lambda=\lambda(G)
and let
i\in V(G)
be a vertex with
x_{i}=\max\{x_{j}:j\in V(G)\}
.
Assume for the sake of contradiction that

x_{i}\geq\delta^{-4}\lambda^{-1/2}
. We use the identity

\lambda^{\ell}x_{i}=\sum_{k\in V(G)}A_{G}^{\ell}(i,k)\cdot x_{k}=\sum_{k\in V(G)}w_{\ell+1}(i,k)\cdot x_{k}.

Note that each
(\ell+1)
-walk starting from the vertex
i

is determined by the
\ell
-walk obtained by removing
i
. So we have

\lambda^{\ell}x_{i}\leq\sum_{j,k\in V(G)}w_{\ell}(j,k)\cdot x_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k\in V(G)}w_{\ell}(j,k)\cdot(x_{j}+x_{k}).

Let
B
be a vertex subset of
G
defined as below:

B:=\left\{j\in V(G):x_{j}>\delta^{-2}\lambda^{-1/2}\right\}.

As
\bm{x}
is unit, i.e.,
\sum_{j\in V(G)}x_{j}^{2}=1
, we have

\lvert B\rvert<\delta^{4}\lambda.

We call an
\ell
-walk
w
from
j
to
k

good
if both
j
and
k
lie in
B
, and
bad
otherwise.
Applying the Rayleigh’s formula, the number of good
\ell
-walks in
G
is given by

\sum_{j,k\in B}w_{\ell}(j,k)=\mathbf{1}_{B}^{T}A_{G}^{\ell-1}\mathbf{1}_{B}\leq\lambda^{\ell-1}\mathbf{1}_{B}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{B}=\lambda^{\ell-1}|B|\leq\delta^{4}\lambda^{\ell}.

Thus we have

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k\in B}w_{\ell}(j,k)\cdot(x_{j}+x_{k})\leq\delta^{4}\lambda^{\ell}x_{i}.

(5)

On the other hand, for any bad
\ell
-walk from
j
to
k
, we have

x_{j}+x_{k}\leq x_{i}+\delta^{-2}\lambda^{-1/2}\leq(1+\delta^{2})x_{i}.

Thus, the contribution from bad walks is bounded by

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in V(G)\\
\{j,k\}\not\subseteq B\end{subarray}}w_{\ell}(j,k)\cdot(x_{j}+x_{k})\leq\frac{1}{2}w_{\ell}(G)\cdot(1+\delta^{2})x_{i}\leq\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}\lambda^{\ell}x_{i},

(6)

where the last inequality follows by the assumption
\lambda^{\ell}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\delta\right)\cdot w_{\ell}(G)
.

Summing the two inequalities (
5
) and (
6
), we conclude that

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k\in V(G)}w_{\ell}(j,k)\cdot(x_{j}+x_{k})\leq\left(\frac{1+\delta^{2}}{1+\delta}+\delta^{4}\right)\lambda^{\ell}x_{i}<\lambda^{\ell}x_{i},

which leads to a contradiction.
∎

An additional lemma we need is the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
\bm{x}=(x_{j})_{j\in V(G)}
be its unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector. Then for any vertex
i
in
G
, we have

w_{\ell}(i)\geq\frac{\lambda^{\ell-1}(G)x_{i}}{\max_{j\in V(G)}\{x_{j}\}}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(G)
and
\mathbf{1}_{i}
be the vector with
1
at the
i
-th entry and
0
everywhere else. Then

\mathbf{1}_{i}^{T}A_{G}^{\ell-1}\bm{x}=\lambda^{\ell-1}\mathbf{1}_{i}^{T}\bm{x}=\lambda^{\ell-1}x_{i}.

On the other hand, it follows that

\mathbf{1}_{i}^{T}A_{G}^{\ell-1}\bm{x}=\sum_{j\in V(G)}w_{\ell}(i,j)\cdot x_{j}\leq\sum_{j\in V(G)}w_{\ell}(i,j)\cdot\max_{j\in V(G)}\{x_{j}\}=w_{\ell}(i)\cdot\max_{j\in V(G)}\{x_{j}\},

as desired.
∎

We can now prove
Theorem

1.4
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.4
.

By Lemma
2.1
, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists some
N(\varepsilon)>0
such that any
F
-free graph on
n\geq N(\varepsilon)
vertices can be made
K_{r+1}
-free by removing at most
\varepsilon^{20}\ell^{-8}n^{2}/256
edges. Assume that
G
is an
F
-free graph with
m
edges.
It suffices to prove that, for any
0<\varepsilon<0.01
, we have

\lambda^{\ell}(G)\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot w_{\ell}(G)+N(\varepsilon)^{\ell}.

Suppose on the contrary that there exists a graph
G
with

\lambda^{\ell}(G)>\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot w_{\ell}(G)+N(\varepsilon)^{\ell}.

(7)

Among such
G
, we choose a graph
G
such that
|V(G)|+|E(G)|
is minimal. Denote
V=V(G)
. It is clear that
G
does not have an isolated vertex. Let
e=\{i,j\}
be any edge in
G
, and let
G^{\prime}=G\backslash\{e\}
be the subgraph of
G
obtained by removing
e
.
By the minimality of
G
, we have

\lambda^{\ell}(G^{\prime})\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot w_{\ell}(G^{\prime})+N(\varepsilon)^{\ell}.

Therefore, we observe that

\lambda^{\ell}(G)-\lambda^{\ell}(G^{\prime})>\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot(w_{\ell}(G)-w_{\ell}(G^{\prime})).

On the one hand, we recall that

\lambda(G^{\prime})\geq\lambda(G)-2x_{i}x_{j}
. Recall the elementary inequality that
a^{\ell}-b^{\ell}\leq\ell a^{\ell-1}(a-b)
holds for any numbers
a\geq b>0
. Then

\lambda^{\ell}(G)-\lambda^{\ell}(G^{\prime})\leq\ell\cdot\lambda^{\ell-1}(G)\cdot 2x_{i}x_{j}.

On the other hand, since any
\ell
-walk in
G
starting with the edge
ji
in
G
no longer appears in
G^{\prime}
, and there are
w_{\ell-1}(i)
such walks, we also have

w_{\ell}(G)-w_{\ell}(G^{\prime})\geq w_{\ell-1}(i).

Therefore, we have

\ell\cdot\lambda^{\ell-1}(G)\cdot 2x_{i}x_{j}\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot w_{\ell-1}(i).

Invoking
Lemma

3.2
, we have

w_{\ell-1}(i)\geq\frac{\lambda^{\ell-2}(G)x_{i}}{\max_{k\in V}\{x_{k}\}}.

So we conclude that

x_{j}\cdot\max_{k\in V}\{x_{k}\}\geq\frac{1}{4\ell\lambda(G)}.

As each vertex
j
in
G
is contained in at least one edge, this inequality holds for any vertex
j
.

By
Lemma

3.1
, we have

\max\{x_{k}:k\in V\}<\varepsilon^{-4}\lambda(G)^{-1/2}
.
Then for any
j\in V
,

x_{j}\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{4\ell}\lambda(G)^{-1/2},

which implies

\lvert V\rvert\leq 16\ell^{2}\varepsilon^{-8}\lambda(G).

As
|V|\geq\lambda(G)\geq N(\varepsilon)
, by the removal Lemma, we can remove at most
\varepsilon^{20}\ell^{-8}\lvert V\rvert^{2}/256\leq\varepsilon^{4}\ell^{-4}\lambda^{2}(G)
edges from
G
to obtain a
K_{r+1}
-free subgraph
G^{\prime}
.
Applying
Theorem

1.3
on
G^{\prime}
gives

\lambda(G^{\prime})\leq\left(\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)w_{\ell}(G^{\prime})\right)^{1/\ell}\leq\left(\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)w_{\ell}(G)\right)^{1/\ell}.

So we conclude that

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})+\lambda(G\setminus G^{\prime})<\left(\Bigl(1-\frac{1}{r}\Bigr)w_{\ell}(G)\right)^{1/\ell}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon^{4}\ell^{-4}\lambda^{2}(G)}.

Simplifying, we obtain

\lambda^{\ell}(G)<\left(1-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{2}\ell^{-2}\right)^{-\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)w_{\ell}(G),

which together with (
7
) gives

\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)>\left(1-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{2}\ell^{-2}\right)^{\ell}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)\geq\left(1-\sqrt{2}\varepsilon^{2}\ell^{-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon\right),

where the second inequality follows by Bernoulli’s inequality.
So we get
\sqrt{2}\varepsilon\ell^{-1}(1-\frac{1}{r}+\varepsilon)>1
, which leads to a contradiction since
\varepsilon<0.01
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Our proof of

for general graphs

reduces to the case when

is a triangle in

, and then the case when

with

in

.

4.1
The case of triangles

As a starting point for the whole argument, let us prove
Theorem

1.6
when
F
is the triangle
K_{3}
.
For the convenience of readers, we extract this case as the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,0.01)
,
there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that if
G
is a triangle-free graph with
m
edges and

\lambda(G)\geq(1-\delta)\sqrt{m},

then there exist disjoint vertex subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
such that
d(G,K_{A,B})\leq\varepsilon m
.

Recall that the stability of the Mantel theorem says that
if
G
is an
n
-vertex triangle-free graph with

e(G)\geq n^{2}/4-q
, then

G
can be made bipartite by removing at most
q
edges. Next, we show a spectral version of this statement.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
,
if
G
is an
m
-edge triangle-free graph with

\lambda(G)\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)\sqrt{m},

then
G
can be made bipartite by removing at most

\varepsilon m
edges.

Proof.

Let
u\in V(G)
be a vertex such that

x_{u}=\max\{x_{v}:v\in V(G)\}
.
We denote
U:=N(u)
and
W:=V(G)\setminus(N(u)\cup\{u\})
.
Then

\lambda^{2}x_{u}=\sum_{j\in N(u)}\sum_{k\in N(j)}x_{k}=d(u)x_{u}+\sum_{v\in U}d_{U}(v)x_{v}+\sum_{w\in W}d_{U}(w)x_{w}.

Since
G
is triangle-free, we have
d_{U}(v)=0
whenever
v\in U
. Thus we have

(1-\varepsilon)mx_{u}<\lambda^{2}x_{u}=d(u)x_{u}+\sum_{w\in W}d_{U}(w)x_{u}=(d(u)+e(U,W))x_{u}.

It follows that

e(W)=m-d(u)-e(U,W)<\varepsilon m.

After removing all edges within

W
, since there are at most
\varepsilon m
edges in
W
, we obtain the desired bipartite graph with two vertex parts
\{u\}\cup W
and
U
.
∎

We note that this gives the following concise vertex-spectral stability theorem.

Corollary 4.3
.

For any
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
and
n\geq 4/\varepsilon
, if
G
is an
n
-vertex triangle-free graph with

\lambda(G)\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)\cdot\lambda(T_{n,2}),

then
G
can be made bipartite by removing at most
\varepsilon n^{2}
edges.

Proof.

The Rayleigh formula implies
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}
, which together with the assumption gives

\lambda^{2}(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\lambda(T_{n,2}).

Note that
\lambda(T_{n,2})\geq\delta(T_{n,2})=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
.
To apply Lemma
4.2
, it suffices to show that

\frac{2m}{n}\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n-1}{2}\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{2}m,

which is equivalent to showing that

\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right){(n-1)}\geq\left(1-\varepsilon+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}\right)n.

The inequality holds by
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
and

n\geq 4/\varepsilon
, and then

(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4})n\geq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon n>1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.
∎

In what follows, we give another proof of Corollary
4.3
.

Second proof.

Since
G
is a triangle-free graph with
m
edges, we know that
\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}
.
First of all, we claim that
e(G)\geq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-\varepsilon n^{2}
.
Otherwise, if
e(G)<\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-\varepsilon n^{2}
, then

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}<\sqrt{\big\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\big\rfloor-\varepsilon n^{2}}\leq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\lambda(T_{n,2}),

where the last inequality holds since
\lambda(T_{n,2})=\sqrt{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
. This contradicts with the assumption. Thus, we conclude that
e(G)\geq\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor-\varepsilon n^{2}
.

Let
A
be an independent set of maximum size in
G
.
Since
G
is triangle-free,
N(v)
is an independent set for every
v\in V(G)
, which implies
\Delta(G)\leq|A|
.
Setting
B:=V(G)\setminus A
, we get

\sum_{v\in B}d(v)\leq|B|\Delta(G)\leq|B||A|\leq\left\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\right\rfloor.

On the other hand, it follows that

\sum_{v\in B}d(v)=e(A,B)+2e(B)=e(G)+e(B).

Since
e(G)\geq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor-\varepsilon n^{2}
, we get
e(B)\leq\varepsilon n^{2}
.
Removing all edges within
B
, we can obtain a bipartite subgraph of
G
on the vertex parts
A
and
B
, as needed.
∎

To complete the proof of
Theorem

4.1
,
we show in the rest of this subsection that the resulting bipartite subgraph obtained in Lemma
4.2

is close to a complete bipartite graph.
Throughout, we write
\bm{x}
for the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,0.01)
,
let
\delta=\varepsilon^{4}/100
.
If
G
is a bipartite graph with

m
edges and
\lambda(G)\geq(1-\delta)\sqrt{m}
, then there exist disjoint vertex subsets
U,V\subseteq V(G)
such that

d(G,K_{U,V})\leq\varepsilon m.

Proof.

Let
V(G)=A\sqcup B
be a bipartition of
G
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
A=\{1,2,\ldots,a\}
and
B=\{1,2,\ldots,b\}
, and the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
takes values
x_{1}\geq\dots\geq x_{a}
on
A
and
y_{1}\geq\dots\geq y_{b}
on
B
.
It follows from Lemma
2.5
that

\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}^{2}=\sum_{j\in B}y_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, we have

\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{j\in B}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}.

On the other hand, the assumption gives

\sum_{ij\in E(G)}2x_{i}y_{j}=\lambda(G)\geq(1-\delta)\sqrt{m}.

Combining the two inequalities, we obtain

\sum_{ij\notin E(G)}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}+\sum_{ij\in E(G)}\left(x_{i}y_{j}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}}\right)^{2}\leq\delta/2,

(8)

where
\sum_{ij\notin E(G)}
goes over each pair
(i,j)\in(A\times B)\backslash E(G)
exactly once.

Let
r,s
be the smallest integers such that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{j}^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}
, respectively.

Claim 4.5
.

We have
\frac{1}{3\sqrt{m}}<x_{r}y_{s}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
.

Proof of claim.

It is easy to verify that
(x-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}})^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}x^{2}
holds for every
x\in(-\infty,\frac{1}{3\sqrt{m}})\cup(\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}},+\infty)
.
If
x_{r}y_{s}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
, then
for any
i\leq r
and
j\leq s
, we have
x_{i}y_{j}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}
. This implies

\min\left\{x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2},\left(x_{i}y_{j}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}}\right)^{2}\right\}\geq\frac{1}{4}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}.

Summing over
i\leq r
and
j\leq s
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\min\left\{x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2},\left(x_{i}y_{j}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}}\right)^{2}\right\}\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s}y_{j}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{4}

which contradicts with (
8
). If
x_{r}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{m}}
, then
x_{i}y_{j}\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{m}}

for
i\geq r
and
j\geq s
.
We can finish by an analogous argument that replaces
i\leq r
and
j\leq s
with
i\geq r
and
j\geq s
.
∎

Let
U
and
V
be sets of vertices of
G
defined as

U:=\left\{u\in A:x_{u}\geq\frac{x_{r}}{4}\right\}\quad\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \quad V:=\left\{v\in B:y_{v}\geq\frac{y_{s}}{4}\right\}.

In the rest of the proof, we use (
8
) to show that
G
has small edit distance with
K_{U,V}
.

First of all, we show that there are few vertex pairs in
U\times V
that are non-edges in
G
.

Claim 4.6
.

We have
|E(K_{U,V})\backslash E(G)|\leq\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon m
.

Proof of claim.

We need to show that
G
misses at most
\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon m
edges between
U

and
V
. By
4.5
, for any
(i,j)\in(U\times V)\backslash E(G)
, we have

x_{i}y_{j}\geq\frac{1}{16}x_{r}y_{s}\geq\frac{1}{48\sqrt{m}}.

Using (
8
), we obtain

|E(K_{U,V})\backslash E(G)|\leq(48\sqrt{m})^{2}\cdot\sum_{ij\notin E(G)}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}\leq(48\sqrt{m})^{2}\cdot\frac{\delta}{2}\leq 1152\delta m\leq\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon m,

as desired.
∎

Moreover, we prove that there are few edges in
G[\bar{U},B]
and

G[A,\bar{V}]
.

Claim 4.7
.

We have
e(E(G)\cap(\bar{U}\times B))\leq\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon m
, and symmetrically
e(E(G)\cap(A\times\bar{V}))\leq\frac{1}{3}\varepsilon m
.

Proof of claim.

Let
E_{1}
be the set of edges
ij
in
E(G)\cap(\bar{U}\times B)
with
j\geq s
, and let
E_{2}
be the set of edges
ij
in
E(G)\cap(\bar{U}\times B)
with
j<s
. It suffices to show that
\max\{\lvert E_{1}\rvert,\lvert E_{2}\rvert\}\leq\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon m
.

For any
ij\in E_{1}
, by Claim
4.5
, we have
x_{i}y_{j}\leq\frac{1}{4}x_{r}y_{s}\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{m}}
. Then together with (
8
), we have

\lvert E_{1}\rvert\cdot\frac{1}{16m}\leq\sum_{ij\in E_{1}}\left(x_{i}y_{j}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta,

which implies
\lvert E_{1}\rvert\leq 8\delta m<\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon m
, as needed.

To bound
|E_{2}|
, we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{ij\in E(G)\backslash E_{2}}2x_{i}y_{j}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\sum_{ij\in E(G)\backslash E_{2}}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{|E(G)\backslash E_{2}|}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\sum_{i\in A,j\in B}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{|E(G)\backslash E_{2}|}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{m-|E_{2}|}.

(9)

By the definition of
s
, we have
\sum_{j<s}y_{j}^{2}<\varepsilon^{2}
. Observe that

\sum_{ij\in E_{2}}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i\in A}x_{i}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j<s}y_{j}^{2}\right)<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Applying the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{ij\in E_{2}}2x_{i}y_{j}\leq 2\sqrt{\sum_{ij\in E_{2}}x_{i}^{2}y_{j}^{2}}\cdot\sqrt{\lvert E_{2}\rvert}\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon^{2}\lvert E_{2}\rvert}.

(10)

Combining (
9
) and (
10
), we conclude that

(1-\delta)\sqrt{m}\leq\lambda(G)=\sum_{ij\in E(G)}2x_{i}y_{j}\leq\sqrt{m-\lvert E_{2}\rvert}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon^{2}\lvert E_{2}\rvert}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality again, we have

\sqrt{m-\lvert E_{2}\rvert}+\sqrt{2\varepsilon^{2}\lvert E_{2}\rvert}\leq\sqrt{(1+4\varepsilon^{2})\left(m-\lvert E_{2}\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\lvert E_{2}\rvert\right)}.

Therefore, we obtain

(1-\delta)^{2}m\leq(1+4\varepsilon^{2})\left(m-\lvert E_{2}\rvert+\frac{1}{2}\lvert E_{2}\rvert\right),

which implies that
\lvert E_{2}\rvert\leq\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon m
by our choice of
\delta=\varepsilon^{4}/100
and
\varepsilon\in(0,0.01)
.
∎

Combining
4.6
and
4.7
, we conclude that

d(G,K_{U,V})\leq e(K_{U,V}\backslash G)+e(E(G)\cap(\bar{U}\times B))+e(E(G)\cap(A\times\bar{V}))\leq\varepsilon m,

as desired.
∎

We can now prove
Theorem

4.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

We take
\delta=\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{36}
.
Assume that
G
is a triangle-free graph with
m
edges and
\lambda(G)\geq(1-\frac{1}{4}\varepsilon^{36})\sqrt{m}
.
Using Lemma
4.2
, we obtain a bipartite subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
such that
G^{\prime}
has
m^{\prime}\geq m-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{36}m
edges.
Note that the Rayleigh’s formula gives

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})+\lambda(G\setminus G^{\prime})\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})+\sqrt{2(m-m^{\prime})}\leq\lambda(G^{\prime})+\varepsilon^{6}\sqrt{m}.

Then
\lambda(G^{\prime})\geq(1-\delta)\sqrt{m}-\varepsilon^{6}\sqrt{m}\geq(1-2\varepsilon^{6})\sqrt{m^{\prime}}>(1-\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{1600})\sqrt{m^{\prime}}
. Now, we can apply
Lemma

4.4
to
G^{\prime}
to obtain two disjoint vertex sets
U,V\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
such that
d(G^{\prime},K_{U,V})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}m
. Thus, we conclude that
d(G,K_{U,V})\leq d(G,G^{\prime})+d(G^{\prime},K_{U,V})\leq\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{36}m+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon m<\varepsilon m
, as desired.
∎

4.2
The case of cliques

In this subsection, we prove
Theorem

1.6
for larger forbidden cliques
F=K_{r+1}
with
r\geq 3
.
We rephrase this case as the following theorem.

Theorem 4.8
.

For every
r\geq 3
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
m_{0}
and
\delta>0
such that if
G
is a
K_{r+1}
-free graph with
m\geq m_{0}
edges and

\lambda^{2}(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta\right)2m,

then there exists a vertex set
C\subseteq V(G)
such that
d(G,T_{C,r})\leq\varepsilon m
.

We provide two different proofs of
Theorem

4.8
.
The first proof is based on the Motzkin–Straus inequality
Lemma

2.6
, and then reduce the edge-spectral stability in
Theorem

4.8
to the vertex-spectral stability in
Theorem

1.5
.
The second alternative proof applies a recent result involving the stability of the Kruskal–Katona type result for hypergraphs. We present the first proof here and postpone the second proof to the Appendix
A
.

First of all, we exploit the slackness in Nikiforov’s application of the Motzkin–Straus inequality. Using this slackness, we show that
G
contains an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
such that
d(G,G^{\prime})=o(m)

and
\lambda(G^{\prime})=(1-\frac{1}{r}-o(1))|G^{\prime}|
. This puts us in a position to use
Theorem

1.5
.

Lemma 4.9
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
r\geq 3
,
there exist
\delta>0
and
m_{0}
such that the following holds. If
G
is a
K_{r+1}
-free graph with
m\geq m_{0}
edges and
\lambda^{2}(G)\geq(1-\frac{1}{r}-\delta)2m
, then
G
has an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
such that
\lvert V(G^{\prime})\rvert\leq(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{2m/(1-1/r)}
and
d(G,G^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon m
.

Proof.

In the following argument, we can take
\delta=(0.1\varepsilon)^{8}
.
For simplicity, we write
V
and
E
for the set of vertices and the set of edges of
G
, respectively, and
let
\bm{x}=(x_{i})_{i\in V}
be the unit Perron–Frobenious eigenvector of
G
.
By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\sum_{ij\in E}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\leq\frac{r-1}{2r}.

On the other hand, the Rayleigh quotient gives

\sum_{ij\in E}2x_{i}x_{j}=\lambda(G)\geq\sqrt{\Big(\frac{r-1}{r}-\delta\Big)2m}.

Setting
\alpha:=(\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{1}{2m})^{1/4}
, a linear combination of the above inequalities yields that

\sum_{ij\in E}\left(x_{i}x_{j}-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{r-1}{r}-\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\Big(\frac{r-1}{r}-\delta\Big)}<\delta.

(11)

We say that an edge
ij\in E
is
bad
if
\lvert x_{i}x_{j}-\alpha^{2}\rvert>\delta^{1/4}\alpha^{2}
, and
good
otherwise. Let
G_{b}
be the subgraph of bad edges, and
G_{g}
be the subgraph of good edges.
For simplicity, we write
E_{b}
and
E_{g}
for the edge sets of
G_{b}
and
G_{g}
, respectively.
From (
11
), we have

|E_{b}|\leq\frac{\delta}{\delta^{1/2}\alpha^{4}}\leq 4\delta^{1/2}m.

(12)

Using this fact, we now show that almost all vertices
i
satisfy
x_{i}\approx\alpha
.

For each positive integer
t\geq 1
, let

\Gamma_{t}:=\left\{i\in V:|x_{i}-\alpha|\geq 2\delta^{1/4}\alpha t\right\}.

Observe that
\Gamma_{t}\subset\Gamma_{t-1}
. We proceed via the following claims.

The first one follows by the definition of
G_{g}
.

Claim 4.10
.

The graph
G_{g}[\Gamma_{1}]
is a bipartite graph on the vertex parts

\Gamma_{1}^{+}:=\{i\in V:x_{i}\geq(1+2\delta^{1/4})\alpha\}

and

\Gamma_{1}^{-}:=\{i\in V:x_{i}\leq(1-2\delta^{1/4})\alpha\}.

Claim 4.11
.

For each
t\leq\delta^{-1/8}/2
, all the edges between
\Gamma_{t}
and
V\backslash\Gamma_{t-1}
are bad.

Proof of claim.

Indeed, if
i\in\Gamma_{t}
and
j\in V\backslash\Gamma_{t-1}
, then

\displaystyle\lvert x_{i}x_{j}-\alpha^{2}\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert(x_{i}-\alpha)x_{j}+(x_{j}-\alpha)\alpha\rvert\geq x_{j}\lvert x_{i}-\alpha\rvert-\alpha\lvert x_{j}-\alpha\rvert

\displaystyle\geq(1-2(t-1)\delta^{1/4})\cdot 2t\delta^{1/4}\alpha^{2}-2(t-1)\delta^{1/4}\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle>2\delta^{1/4}\alpha^{2}-4t^{2}\delta^{1/2}\alpha^{2}\geq\delta^{1/4}\alpha^{2},

so the edge
ij
is bad.
∎

We now consider the quantities

s_{t}:=\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{t}}x_{i}^{2}.

Our goal is to find an integer
\ell
such that

s_{\ell}\leq O(\delta^{1/4})
, and then we will show that there are at most
\varepsilon m
edges incident to the vertices of
\Gamma_{\ell}
and that deleting all vertices of
\Gamma_{\ell}
yields the desired subgraph
G^{\prime}
.

For every
t\leq\delta^{-1/8}/2
, as
\bm{x}
is the Perron–Frobenius eigenvector, we have

\lambda(G)s_{t}=\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{t}}\sum_{j\in N(i)}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in N(i)\\
j\in\Gamma_{t-1}\end{subarray}}x_{i}x_{j}+\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{t}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in N(i)\\
j\in V\setminus\Gamma_{t-1}\end{subarray}}x_{i}x_{j}.

Note that
\Gamma_{t}\subset\Gamma_{t-1}
for each
t\geq 1
.
By
4.11
, every edge summed over on the right hand side is either in
G_{g}[\Gamma_{t-1}]
or bad. Then

\lambda(G)s_{t}\leq\sum_{ij\in E_{g}[\Gamma_{t-1}]}2x_{i}x_{j}+\sum_{ij\in E_{b}}x_{i}x_{j}.

By
4.10
,
G_{g}[\Gamma_{t-1}]\subseteq G_{g}[\Gamma_{1}]
is bipartite, so its spectral radius is at most
\sqrt{m}
. Thus,

\sum_{ij\in E_{g}[\Gamma_{t-1}]}2x_{i}x_{j}\leq\sqrt{m}\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{t-1}}x_{i}^{2}=\sqrt{m}s_{t-1}.

On the other hand, recall in (
12
) that

|E_{b}|\leq 4\delta^{1/2}m
. Then

\sum_{ij\in E_{b}}x_{i}x_{j}<\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2\lvert E_{b}\rvert}<2\delta^{1/4}\sqrt{m}.

Since
r\geq 3
and
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{10}{9}\sqrt{m}
, we conclude that

s_{t}\leq 0.9s_{t-1}+2\delta^{1/4}.

Together with the initial condition
s_{1}\leq 1
, we can solve this recurrence and obtain that for every
t\leq\delta^{-1/8}/2
,

s_{t}\leq 20\delta^{1/4}+0.9^{t-1}.

Taking
\ell:=\lfloor\delta^{-1/8}/2\rfloor
, since
\delta
is sufficiently small, we get
0.9^{\ell-1}<10\delta^{1/4}
. Thus,

s_{\ell}\leq 30\delta^{1/4}.

(13)

Let
V^{\prime}:=V(G)\setminus\Gamma_{\ell}
and
G^{\prime}:=G[V^{\prime}]
.
In what follows, we verify that
G^{\prime}
has the desired properties.
Firstly, by the definition of
V^{\prime}
, we know that for every
i\in V^{\prime}
,

x_{i}\geq(1-2\delta^{1/4}\ell)\alpha\geq(1-2\delta^{1/8})\alpha.

Recall that
\bm{x}
is a unit vector and

\alpha=(\frac{r-1}{r}\cdot\frac{1}{2m})^{1/4}
.
Thus, we get

|V^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{(1-2\delta^{1/8})^{2}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{i\in V^{\prime}}x_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(1-2\delta^{1/8})^{2}\alpha^{2}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2rm}{r-1}}.

Secondly, let
R
be the set of edges with at least one vertex in
\Gamma_{\ell}
. Recall by (
11
) that

\sum_{ij\in R}\left(x_{i}x_{j}-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{ij\in E}\left(x_{i}x_{j}-\alpha^{2}\right)^{2}\leq\delta.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

|R|\alpha^{2}-\sum_{ij\in R}x_{i}x_{j}=\sum_{ij\in R}(\alpha^{2}-x_{i}x_{j})\leq\sqrt{\delta\lvert R\rvert}.

On the other hand, we have

\sum_{ij\in R}x_{i}x_{j}\leq\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{\ell}}x_{i}\sum_{j\in N(i)}x_{j}=\lambda(G)\sum_{i\in\Gamma_{\ell}}x_{i}^{2}<30\sqrt{2m}\delta^{1/4},

where the last inequality holds by (
13
).
So we conclude that

5
Maximizing common neighbors

We first give a random construction to show that our bound on
t
in
Theorem

1.8
is the best possible. Let
\varepsilon>0
and consider the binomial random graph
G(n,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)
.
Standard concentration bounds show that for sufficiently large
n
, with high probability both of the following events occur:

1.

G
has at least
(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2})\binom{n}{2}
edges;

2.

the codegree of any two vertices is at most
(\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon)^{2}n
.

Consider any
G
such that both the above two properties hold. Then first one implies that
m>n^{2}/4
, and so
\lambda(G)\geq\frac{2m}{n}>\sqrt{m}
. The second implies that if
K_{2,t}
is a subgraph of
G
, then
t\leq(\frac{1}{2}+2\varepsilon)^{2}n\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}+6\varepsilon\right)\sqrt{m}
. This random construction reveals the tightness of
Theorem

1.8
.

To prove
Theorem

1.8
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
m
edges and

\bm{x}
be the unit Perron–Frobenius eigenvector of
G
. Then

\sum_{i,j\in V}|N(i)\cap N(j)|\cdot x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda^{3}(G)}{2m}.

The bound in Lemma
5.1

is the best possible when we take
G=K_{\sqrt{m},\sqrt{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda=\lambda(G)
and
C_{i,j}
be the number of common neighbors of
i
and
j
. In particular, we have that
C_{i,i}
is the degree
d_{i}
.
We can write

\sum_{i,j\in V}C_{i,j}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}=\sum_{i\in V}x_{i}^{2}\sum_{k\in N(i)}\sum_{j\in N(k)}x_{j}^{2}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\sum_{j\in N(k)}x_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{d_{k}}\left(\sum_{j\in N(k)}x_{j}\right)^{2}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{d_{k}}x_{k}^{2}.

Thus, we obtain

\sum_{i,j\in V}C_{i,j}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\geq\lambda^{2}\sum_{i\in V}\sum_{k\in N(i)}\frac{x_{i}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{d_{k}}=2\lambda^{2}\sum_{ik\in E}\frac{x_{i}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{d_{k}}.

By symmetry, we have

\sum_{i,j\in V}C_{i,j}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\geq 2\lambda^{2}\sum_{ik\in E}\frac{x_{i}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{d_{i}}.

Adding the above two inequalities and using the AM-GM inequality, we get

\sum_{i,j\in V}C_{i,j}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\geq 2\lambda^{2}\sum_{ik\in E}\frac{x_{i}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{\sqrt{d_{i}d_{k}}}.

(16)

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality again, we have

\sum_{ik\in E}\frac{x_{i}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{\sqrt{d_{i}d_{k}}}\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{ik\in E}x_{i}x_{k}\right)^{2}}{\sum_{ik\in E}\sqrt{d_{i}d_{k}}}.

On the one hand, it follows from the definition that
\sum_{ik\in E}x_{i}x_{k}=\lambda/2.

On the other hand, the vector
(\sqrt{d_{i}})_{i=1}^{n}
has length
\sqrt{2m}
. Therefore, the Rayleigh formula gives

\sum_{ik\in E}\sqrt{d_{i}d_{k}}\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}=\frac{\lambda}{2}\cdot 2m.

Thus, one more application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\sum_{ik\in E}\frac{x_{i}^{2}x_{k}^{2}}{\sqrt{d_{i}d_{k}}}\geq\frac{\lambda}{4m}.

(17)

Combining (
16
)
with (
17
), we conclude that
\sum_{i,j\in V}C_{i,j}x_{i}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\geq\frac{\lambda^{3}}{2m}
as desired.
∎

Now, we are in a position to prove Theorem
1.8

Theorem 6.1

We need the following variant of Kővári–Sós–Turán’s theorem.

There are many promising directions to explore in the edge-spectral Turán type problem, and we provide another example for cycles with consecutive lengths.

Theorem 6.4

Activity-driven clustering of jamming run-and-tumble particles: Exact three-body steady state by dynamical symmetry
Leo Hahn 1,2, Arnaud Guillin 1, Manon Michel 1

Abstract
We exactly resolve the three-particle steady state of run-and-tumble particles with jamming interactions, providing the first microscopic description beyond two bodies. The invariant measure, derived via a piecewise-deterministic Markov process description and symmetry principles, reveals persistent, separated, and diffusive regimes. A cascade of scales in the activity parameter organizes the structural weights, showing the separated phase dominates at finite activity, while non-uniformity plays only a minor role. This approach lays the groundwork for tackling the N N -body problem.

Introduction.
Active matter—ensembles of units that
convert stored or ambient energy into directed motion—has emerged as
a cornerstone in nonequilibrium statistical physics, now spanning
scales from cytoskeletal filaments, pedestrian crowds to robotic
swarms
Schnitzer 1993
;
Wu and Libchaber 2000
;
Ramaswamy 2010
;
Cates 2012
;
Marchetti et al. 2013
;
Dauchot and Démery 2019
;
Mayya et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2021
. Within
this field,
run-and-tumble particles
(RTPs) furnish a
fundamental microscopic model

Schnitzer 1993
;
Berg 2004
;
Kafri and da Silveira 2008
;
Tailleur and Cates 2008
;
Slowman et al. 2016
;
Slowman et al. 2017
;
Malakar et al. 2018
;
Le Doussal et al. 2019
;
Das et al. 2020
;
Basu et al. 2020
;
Mori et al. 2020
;
Metson et al. 2022
;
Arnoulx de Pirey and van
Wijland 2023
;
Hahn et al. 2025
;
Guillin et al. 2025
:
persistent ballistic runs punctuated by Poissonian velocity tumbles
break detailed balance and give rise to hallmarks of active behavior
such as wall accumulation
Elgeti et al. 2015
and motility-induced phase
separation (MIPS)
Tailleur and Cates 2008
.

When hard-core repulsion is added, RTPs display phenomena
qualitatively different from their passive
counterparts. Activity-driven
jamming
interactions, clustering

Fily and Marchetti 2012
;
Soto and Golestanian 2014
;
Slowman et al. 2016
;
Slowman et al. 2017
and different critical behaviours

Bi et al. 2016
emerge. Clustering is often rationalized in terms of an
effective attraction: in dense regions particles slow down, further
increasing density and triggering
aggregation
Cates and Tailleur 2015
;
Dolai et al. 2020
. Yet this picture is essentially
coarse-grained
Toner and Tu 1995
;
Tailleur and Cates 2008
;
Marchetti et al. 2013
;
Cates and Tailleur 2015
;
Farage et al. 2015
, and
exact microscopic results have remained elusive. Beyond two particles,
where the problem reduces to a single effective RTP with more complex
velocity dynamics
Slowman et al. 2016
;
Das et al. 2020
;
Le Doussal et al. 2021
, the steady state
remains unknown. Despite an important theoretical interest, the
absence of exact three-body or many-body solutions has been a major
barrier to a first-principles understanding of clustering and to
clarifying the microscopic origin of MIPS.

Indeed, a major challenge lies in handling the singular boundary
conditions introduced by jamming
Angelani 2017
;
Bressloff 2022
, even
in the two-RTP case. Lattice
discretizations
Slowman et al. 2016
;
Metson et al. 2022
, soft repulsion
limits
Arnoulx de Pirey and van
Wijland 2023
, or added noise
Malakar et al. 2018
;
Das et al. 2020

regularize the problem but at the cost of complicating the original
process and adding limits nontrivial to compute
Guillin et al. 2025
. A
breakthrough came with the use of piecewise deterministic Markov
processes (PDMPs)
Hahn et al. 2025
, whose generators encode both
deterministic runs and stochastic tumbles, including boundary
behavior, directly in continuous space. For two RTPs, this framework
yielded the explicit steady state for arbitrary tumble mechanisms and
revealed the existence of
detailed-
and
global-jamming

system classes. These classes are linked to a dynamical symmetry,
whose breaking creates in the steady state relaxation terms, that are
often interpreted as effective attractions.

In this work, we resolve the three-body invariant measure, provide the
first controlled microscopic description of clustering beyond two
bodies and establish a foundation for the
N
-body problem. Extending
the PDMP and symmetry formalism developed for two RTPs
Hahn et al. 2025
,
we derive the invariant measure of three instantaneous RTPs and
identify three structural weights—corresponding to free, separated,
and jammed states—that organize persistent, separated, and diffusive
regimes through the activity parameter
\omega L
, as displayed in
Fig.
1
. This reveals a cascade of scales

(\omega L)^{k}
, with
k
the number of jammed particles, and crucially
shows that the separated phase dominates at finite
activity. Relaxation contributions, previously interpreted as
effective attractions, are subdominant and do not drive
clustering. Thus, our results close the gap of exact solutions beyond
two particle and guide numerical studies towards the parameter range
most relevant to observe clustering and phase separation in larger
systems. These results pave the way toward the
N
-body problem, where
the formation and stability of clusters are also expected to be
governed by the dominant structural weights, organized along scales

(\omega L)^{k}
. They also highlight the delicate nature of clustering:
whether a separated phase survives in the
N\to\infty
limit, and, if
so, whether it is confined to a single activity value or extends
across a finite regime.

Jamming and symmetry.
We consider a system of RTP on a
1D torus of length
L>0
. Each particle
i
moves with velocity

\sigma_{i}
(
Run
) updated by an independent Markov jump process
(
Tumble
). Collisions lead to jamming, where both particles
remain immobile until a velocity update allows separation. The system
z=(r,\sigma)

is fully specified by the inter-particle separation vector

r=(r_{i})_{i}
, where
r_{i}
denotes the distance between particle
i
and
particle
(i+1)
, together with the velocity vector

\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i}
. A periodic convention is used for the
indices/particle labels, i.e.
x_{i}=x_{i\pm 3}
. For two RTP, the
steady state was solved for arbitrary tumbles
Hahn et al. 2025
and is of
the form

\pi=w_{\mathcal{B}}\pi_{\mathcal{B}}+\frac{w_{\mathcal{J}}}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2}\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}},
where
\pi_{\mathcal{B}}
is supported on free states

{\mathcal{B}}
, and
\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}
on jammed states,
{\mathcal{J}}_{1},{\mathcal{J}}_{2}

(boundaries of
{\mathcal{B}}
, see
Fig.
1
),

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(z)=\frac{w_{\rm eq}}{L}\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(\sigma)+w_{\rm rel}\gamma(z),\;\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}(z)=\mu_{{\mathcal{J}}}(\sigma_{k},\sigma_{k+1}).

The distributions
\mu_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mu_{{\mathcal{J}}}
are the respective tumble
steady states, left invariant by the jump process for

\sigma
.

The crucial observation of
Hahn et al. 2025
is that conservation of
probability flows defines an
active global balance
in

{\mathcal{B}}
. Detailed satisfaction of this balance requires

\pi(r,(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}))=\pi(r,(\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1}))
, which

\mu_{\mathcal{B}}
automatically possesses due to particle
indistinguishability. Thus, deviations from detailed symmetry in

\sigma
arise from the imbalance between entering and exiting
scenarios at jamming boundaries, which equivalently reflect the
incompatibility between
\mu_{\mathcal{B}}
and
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}
, as shown in
Fig.
2
. Accordingly, detailed-jamming systems
necessarily have
w_{\text{r}el}=0
, maintaining a uniform steady
state
\propto\mu_{\mathcal{B}}
in
{\mathcal{B}}
, identical to the passive
equilibrium distribution. This occurs for
instantaneous-tumble

RTPs, where velocities
\sigma_{i}=\pm 1
flip with rate
\omega>0
. By
contrast, global-jamming systems break this detailed symmetry. The
system is shifted away from uniformity at the jamming boundary,
resulting in a catenary relaxation term
w_{\text{r}el}\gamma
. This
arises for
finite tumble
RTPs, where
\sigma_{i}\in\{-1,0,+1\}

and
\gamma
is a single-scale catenary relaxation.

This highlights the interplay between the richness of
tumbling mechanism, symmetry preservation and boundary-induced
relaxation. A natural next question is whether increasing the number
of particles alone can generate non-equilibrium deviations
away from uniformity. In particular, can three instantaneous RTPs
already produce a non-uniform steady state—potentially stabilizing
separated phases?

Three-RTP steady state.
Increasing the particle number
alone gives rise to qualitatively new phenomena: in the three-particle
system, three-particle clusters and phase-separated states emerge,
with two particles jammed and one remaining free. By deriving the
exact invariant measure for a three-RTP system with instantaneous
tumbles, we show that the symmetry picture remains relevant and
demonstrate the stabilization of phase-separated configurations.

As illustrated in Fig.
1
and detailed in
Appendix
B
, the state space
\Omega
is split
into: the bulk
{\mathcal{B}}
where all particles are free, the three edges

{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
where the system is separated into a cluster of two jamming
particles and a free one and the three vertices
{\mathcal{J}}_{k}
where all
particles are jamming into a single cluster. The steady state
\pi
is
a mixture over distributions
\pi_{\mathcal{B}},\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}},\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}

respectively supported on the free, separated and jammed states,

\pi=w_{\mathcal{B}}\pi_{\mathcal{B}}+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}[w_{{\mathcal{S}}}\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}+w_{{\mathcal{J}}}\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}].

(1)

We show that, for

(r,\sigma)\in{\mathcal{B}}
(resp.
{\mathcal{J}}_{k}
,
{\mathcal{S}}_{k}
),

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(r,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{L^{2}}\mu_{\mathcal{B}}(\sigma),\;\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}(r,\sigma)=\mu_{{\mathcal{J}}}(\tau_{k}(\sigma))

(2)

\displaystyle\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(r,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\frac{w_{\rm eq}}{L}\mu_{{\mathcal{S}}}(\tau_{k}(\sigma))+w_{\rm rel}\gamma(r_{k+1},\tau_{k}(\sigma))

where, for any vector x, the

\tau_{k}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(x_{k},x_{k+1},x_{k+2})
are the index shifts. The
distributions
\mu_{\mathcal{B}}
,
\mu_{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mu_{\mathcal{J}}
stand for the respective
tumble steady state. There is no relaxation in the free
states and the relaxation term
\gamma
in the separated ones takes
the form,

\gamma(r,\sigma)=\tfrac{\nu_{S}(\sigma)}{\mathcal{N}_{\gamma}}{\rm ch}(\lambda(\tfrac{L}{2}-r))+\tfrac{\sigma_{3}\nu_{A}(\sigma)}{\mathcal{N}_{\gamma}}{\rm sh}(\lambda(\tfrac{L}{2}-r)),

(3)

where
\lambda=2\sqrt{2}\omega
and

\mathcal{N}_{\gamma}=\frac{16{\rm sh}(\lambda L/2)}{\lambda}
.
Appendix
A
summarizes the full formula. At
the marginal level, the state space reduces to a simplex and
\pi

sums up to a pure catenary relaxation along each
separated-state edge,

\pi(r)=w_{\mathcal{B}}\frac{2\,\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{B}}(r)}{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big[w_{{\mathcal{J}}}\mathbb{1}_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}(r)\\
+w_{{\mathcal{S}}}\mathbb{1}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(r)\Big(\frac{w_{\mathrm{eq}}}{L}+w_{\mathrm{rel}}\frac{\lambda{\rm ch}\left(\lambda(L/2-r_{k+1})\right)}{2{\rm sh}(\lambda L/2)}\Big)\Big].

(4)

Compared to the 2-RTP case, the 3-RTP steady state still reflects
particle indistinguishability—appearing as a sum structure—and remains
uniform over free states. The crucial novelty is the emergence of
phase-separated configurations, where the steady state is not of
product form, marking a stronger departure from equilibrium driven
purely by the increase in particle number. In these states,
relaxation proceeds in a catenary fashion under the constraint imposed
by jamming boundaries. This reflects a fundamental change in
symmetry: while instantaneous tumbles enforce detailed-jamming
symmetry at the free/separated boundary, within
separated states the jammed pair behaves as an effective single
particle with richer tumble dynamics—akin to a finite tumble with a
non-motile state—thereby breaking detailed-jamming and producing
global-jamming relaxation.

The central question of the three-RTP problem is the stabilization of
the separated phase. To address this, we show how we obtained the
explicit steady state using PDMP, including the weights (see
(
8
), (
10
), (
11
)). For further details
on the PDMP approach for RTPs, see

Hahn et al. 2025
;
Guillin et al. 2025
. Here, the PDMP is
characterized by its generator, for any state
z=(r,\sigma)
and
f

some test function,

\mathcal{A}f(z)=\underbrace{\left\langle\phi(z),\nabla_{r}f(z)\right\rangle}_{\text{Run}}+\omega\sum_{j}\underbrace{\left(f(\iota_{j}(z))-f(z)\right)}_{\text{Tumble}},

(5)

where each
\iota_{k}
flips the
k
-th particle velocity,

\sigma_{k}\to-\sigma_{k}
, translating the
k
-th particle tumble at rate

\omega
, and the flow
\phi
encodes the dynamics of
r
set by the
velocities
\sigma
, with

\dot{r}=\phi(z)=(\phi_{i}(z))_{i}
, with
\phi_{i}(z)
equal to,

\!\left\{\!\begin{array}[]{l}0\ \text{if}\ (r_{i},\sigma_{i+1}\!-\!\sigma_{i})\!\in\{(0,-2),(0,0),(L,2)\}\!,\\
\!-\!\sigma_{i}\ \text{if}\ (r_{i+1},\sigma_{i+2}\!-\!\sigma_{i+1})\!=\!(0,-2),r_{i}>0,\\
\sigma_{i+1}\ \text{if}\ (r_{i+2},\sigma_{i}\!-\!\sigma_{i+2})\!=\!(0,-2),r_{i}>0,\\
\sigma_{i+1}\!-\!\sigma_{i}\ \text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(6)

A key feature of the dynamics is the emergence of velocities

\phi_{i}(z)=\pm 1
in separated states down to
\phi=0
in
jammed ones, which underlies the velocity slowdown as
clustering and separation develop.

As detailed in
Appendix
B

\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}f\,d\pi=0
translates into the
following conservation laws on probability flows, with
contributions ordered as tumble, run, and boundary
exchanges (the latter arising from integration by
parts of the run term in (
5

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(\iota_{j}(z))-\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(z))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\frac{\phi_{j}(z)}{\omega}\partial_{r_{j}}\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(z)\quad+\quad 0

(
\textrm{C}_{{\mathcal{B}}}
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(\iota_{j}(z))-\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(z))

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{k+1}(z)}{\omega}(\partial_{r_{k+1}}\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(z)-\partial_{r_{k+2}}\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(z))\ +\ \frac{3w_{\mathcal{B}}}{w_{\mathcal{S}}}\frac{\phi_{k}(z)}{\omega}\mathbb{1}_{\partial_{k}{\mathcal{B}}}(z)\lim_{\zeta\in{\mathcal{B}}\to z}\pi_{\mathcal{B}}(\zeta)

(
\textrm{C}_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}
)

\displaystyle\sum_{j}(\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}(\iota_{j}(z))-\pi_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}(z))

\displaystyle=\quad 0\quad-\frac{w_{\mathcal{S}}}{w_{\mathcal{J}}}\frac{\phi_{k+2}(z)}{\omega}\big(\mathbb{1}_{\partial_{k}{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(z)\lim_{\zeta\in{\mathcal{S}}_{k}\to z}\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k}}(\zeta)+\mathbb{1}_{\partial_{k}{\mathcal{S}}_{k+1}}(z)\lim_{\zeta\in{\mathcal{S}}_{k+1}\to z}\pi_{{\mathcal{S}}_{k+1}}(\zeta)\big),

(
\textrm{C}_{{\mathcal{J}}_{k}}
)

where
\partial_{k}{\mathcal{B}}
and
\partial_{k}{\mathcal{S}}_{k^{\prime}}

respectively gather the entering and exiting boundary points
between free/separated and separated/jammed phases, i.e. the
annihilation or creation of a jamming pair through particle
transport.

Discussion.
The most compelling direction for future
work is determining the fate of the separated regime in the

N
-particle case. Understanding the nature of clustering in this
regime—i.e. whether a single macroscopic cluster forms, or the
system fragments into multiple clusters, or displays fractal
behavior—remains an important open question. This analysis of the
exact microscopic steady state offers an alternative and complementary
perspective to the effective-attraction picture often used in
coarse-grained theories
Tailleur and Cates 2008
;
Fily and Marchetti 2012
;
Cates and Tailleur 2015
. It is
particularly valuable for models with jamming in the absence of
thermal noise, where exact hydrodynamic limits remain
elusive
Erignoux 2021
;
Kourbane-Houssene et al. 2018
. The key framework here is that
of PDMPs, whose generator provides an efficient mean to handle the
otherwise delicate boundary conditions in the Fokker-Planck
formulation
Hahn et al. 2025
. The invariance condition then translates
into a hierarchy of PDEs, encoding the conservation of probability
flows under deterministic RTP motion and stochastic velocity
flips. For three RTPs, the competition displayed by
w_{J}
,
w_{S}
and

w_{B}
thus provides the first fully controlled microscopic picture of
how activity alone can bias a many-body system toward finite,
self-selected clusters.

While a full solution of the
N
-particle steady state lies beyond the
scope of this work, the three-RTP case already yields insights that
extrapolate naturally. First, the three-body steady state already
contains the basic building blocks of the general problem: a bulk
region where all particles move freely,
(k\!-\!1)
-dimensional faces
associated with
k
clusters, and a hierarchy of probability flows
between them. Second, the persistent and diffusive regimes extend
directly: for
\omega L\gg 1
the process should approach a uniform
distribution on the simplex, whereas for
\omega L\ll 1
the
probability mass concentrates on subsets of jammed configurations (no
free particles). Third, and more importantly, the hierarchical
organization of structural weights in powers of
\omega L
depending
on the number of jamming particles is going to persist for general
N
, as this
originates from the boundary exchange processes between run and tumble
terms. The crucial open problem is to determine how these competing
scales interact and which phase dominates at finite
\omega
. This
lies at the heart of whether separated phases survive in larger
systems and, more broadly, whether MIPS can be rigorously established
or ruled out.

Our analysis shows how delicate this balance is, with clustering
outcomes hinging sensitively on activity strength. This motivates both
further analytical progress and carefully targeted numerical studies,
for which our results provide clear guidance on the parameter ranges
to probe. At the same time, our work indicates a possible route to
overcome the combinatorial complexity of the
N
-particle case:
leveraging the recursive structure suggested by the effective-particle
behavior of jammed clusters to build a tractable solution. In the
detailed-jamming setting, the fact that the invariant measure remains
uniform over free states provides a natural starting point for
tackling the PDE hierarchy. Altogether, our approach lays the
foundation for a new theoretical framework to probe phase separation
in jamming systems, offering an alternative MIPS criterion directly
connected to the microscopic scale rather than to coarse-grained or
high-level observables.

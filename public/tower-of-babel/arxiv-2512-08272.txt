0 0 -Affine Quantum Group as K-Theoretic Hall Algebra
You-Hung Hsu

Abstract
. In this note, we show that the positive part of Arkhipov–Mazin’s 0-affine quantum group can be realized as the K-theoretic Hall algebra of the type A A Dynkin quiver. We then construct a categorical action of this positive part and demonstrate that such an action induces semiorthogonal decompositions on the corresponding weight categories. As a main example, we study the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on n n -step partial flag varieties.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hall algebras and quantum groups

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a small abelian category satisfying certain finiteness conditions. The Hall algebra of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted by
\sf{H}_{\mathcal{A}}
, is defined as the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space with basis given by isomorphism classes of objects in
\mathcal{A}
, with multiplication determined by extension data.

A fundamental example is the category of quiver representations over a finite field, denoted by
\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}Q
. When
Q
is a Dynkin quiver (i.e., with underlying graph of type
ADE
and any orientation), Ringel
[
25
]
proved that there is an algebra isomorphism

{\sf{H}}_{\mathrm{Rep}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}Q}\cong\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}^{+}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})

(1.1)

between the Hall algebra and the positive part of the quantum group, where
\mathfrak{g}_{Q}
is the simple Lie algebra attached to the quiver
Q
. This realization plays a crucial role in the theory of canonical bases for quantum groups, developed by Kashiwara
[
12
]
and Lusztig
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. For an extensive survey of Hall algebras, see Schiffmann’s lecture notes
[
28
]
.

1.1.1.
The cohomological/K-theoretic version

Rather than working with the abelian category of quiver representations directly, one may study the moduli stack of representations of a quiver
Q
. Kontsevich-Soibelman
[
13
]
defined an associative algebra structure on the (Betti) cohomology of this stack; the resulting algebra is called the
cohomological Hall algebra
of
Q
, denoted by
\mathrm{CoHA}_{Q}
. This construction was motivated by the study of BPS state algebras in string theory and their relation to Donaldson–Thomas invariants. Furthermore, they also propose a categorification of
\mathrm{CoHA}
via using the category of matrix factorizations.

Building on this perspective, Padurariu
[
21
]
introduced the notion of
categorical Hall algebras
, denoted by
\mathrm{HA}_{Q}
, defined as a category of singularities equipped with a monoidal structure. By taking the associated Grothendieck group, he subsequently defined the
K-theoretic Hall algebras
, denoted by
\mathrm{KHA}_{Q}
. In later work, he established several key properties of both
\mathrm{HA}_{Q}
and
\mathrm{KHA}_{Q}
, including wall-crossing phenomena, a Hopf algebra structure, semiorthogonal decompositions, and a PBW theorem; see
[
22
,
23
]
for details.

Remark 1.1
.

In fact, the constructions in both
[
13
]
and
[
21
]
are more general, where they consider quivers with potential
(Q,W)
.

1.1.2.
The preprojective algebras and their realization

Important generalizations of the quantum groups
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
of simple Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
include Yangians
Y_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
and quantum affine algebras
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})
. Similar to the realization (
1.1
), it is natural to ask whether one can realize the positive part of these generalizations as certain Hall algebras.

Nakajima
[
17
]
constructed actions of quantum affine algebras on the equivariant
K
-theory of quiver varieties, while Varagnolo
[
29
]
constructed actions of Yangians on their equivariant homology. These results suggest that the representation theory of quantum affine algebras and Yangians should be accessible through Hall-type constructions. In particular, it is natural to expect that quantum affine algebras are realized via
\mathrm{KHA}
, while Yangians are realized via
\mathrm{CoHA}
. Moreover, there are generalizations of Yangians and quantum affine algebras associated to an arbitrary quiver
Q
. Maulik-Okounkov
[
16
]
defined a Yangian
Y_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})^{\text{MO}}
, and Okounkov-Smirnov
[
20
]
constructed a quantum affine algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}_{Q}})^{\text{OS}}
; both play important roles in the theory of enumerative geometry.

To formulate Hall algebra realizations of the above generalizations of quantum groups, one replaces the moduli stack of representations of a quiver
Q
by its cotangent stack. It is well-known that this cotangent stack is isomorphic to the moduli stack of representations of the preprojective algebra
\Pi_{Q}
.

Incorporating the natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action, one obtains an associative algebra structure on the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant cohomology of the moduli stack of
\Pi_{Q}
-representations. The resulting algebra, called the
preprojective cohomological Hall algebra
(denoted by
\mathrm{CoHA}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{\Pi_{Q}}
), has long been conjectured to provide an algebraic realizations of the positive part of
Y_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})^{\text{MO}}
. There has been substantial progress toward this conjecture; see Davison
[
3
,
4
]
, Schiffmann–Vasserot
[
26
]
, and Yang–Zhao
[
32
]
. Moreover, the conjecture was proved recently, which is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(Bota–Davison
[
2
]
and Schiffmann–Vasserot
[
27
]
)
.

For a quiver
Q
of symmetric Kac–Moody type, there are isomorphisms of algebras

\mathrm{CoHA}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{\Pi_{Q}}\cong Y^{+}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}_{Q})^{\mathrm{MO}}.

Analogously, one can equip the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant K-theory of the moduli stack of
\Pi_{Q}
-representations with an associative multiplication, forming the
preprojective K-theoretic Hall algebra
(denoted by
\mathrm{KHA}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{\Pi_{Q}}
). It has likewise been conjectured that the preprojective
\mathrm{KHA}
realize the positive part of the Okounkov–Smirnov quantum affine algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}_{Q}})^{\text{OS}}
.

Conjecture 1.3
.

(
[
21
, Conjecture 1.2]
)
For a quiver
Q
of symmetric Kac–Moody type, there are isomorphisms of algebras

\mathrm{KHA}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}_{\Pi_{Q}}\cong\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}^{+}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}_{Q}})^{\text{OS}}.

Significant progress toward this conjecture has been made; see Pădurariu
[
21
,
22
]
and Varagnolo–Vasserot
[
30
]
for details.

1.2.
Main results

Arkhipov-Mazin
[
1
]
defined an algebra called the
0-affine quantum group
, denoted by
\mathfrak{U}_{n}
, which can be thought of as a “
q=0
” degeneration of the quantum affine algebra
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{q}(\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}_{n}})
. To state their main result, let
\mathrm{Fl}_{n,N}
be the variety of all
n
-step partial flags in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. There is a natural
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
action on it.

They showed that the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant
K
-theory of
\mathrm{Fl}_{n,N}\times\mathrm{Fl}_{n,N}
carries a convolution algebra structure, which they call the
affine 0-Schur algebra
, denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{0}(n,N)
. The main theorem of
[
1
]
asserts that there exists a surjective algebra homomorphism

\mathfrak{U}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\boldsymbol{\mathrm{S}}^{\mathrm{aff}}_{0}(n,N)\coloneqq K^{\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})}(\mathrm{Fl}_{n,N}\times\mathrm{Fl}_{n,N})

for each
N\geq 1
. As a consequence, the 0-affine quantum group
\mathfrak{U}_{n}
acts on the equivariant
K
-theory of partial flag varieties.

A natural question, in analogy with Ringel’s theorem and the preprojective Hall algebra constructions discussed above, is whether the positive part of Arkhipov–Mazin’s 0-affine quantum group, denoted by
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
, can be realized in terms of a K-theoretic Hall algebra.

In this note, we show that
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
can be realized as the K-theoretic Hall algebra of type
A
Dynkin quiver. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem A

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n-1}}
denote the K-theoretic Hall algebra of type
A_{n-1}
Dynkin quiver. Then, there is an isomorphism of algebras
\phi_{n}:\mathfrak{U}_{n}^{+}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n-1}}
.

The proof will be divided into two parts.

In the first part, we prove the surjectivity of
\phi_{n}
(Proposition
4.8
). It relies on the use of the shuffle formula (Proposition
2.4
), and some explicit calculations of the shuffle product on degree one elements in the
n=2
case (Lemma
4.2
,
4.3
).

In the second part we prove the injectivity (Proposition
4.12
). Since that the two algebras
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
and
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n-1}}
are bi-graded, we will prove it by comparing their bi-graded dimensions. However, a simple check on small examples shows that their bi-graded dimensions are infinite-dimensional. Thus, we have to restrict to their positive/negative parts (Definition
4.9
,
4.10
).

On the other hand, in
[
6
]
the author defined the
shifted 0-affine algebra
, denoted by
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
. Its generators and relations are similar to those of
\mathfrak{U}_{n}
, but there are two key differences. First, as the notation indicates,
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
is an idempotent modification. Second, unlike
\mathfrak{U}_{n}
, its presentation involves only finitely many generators and relations.

Despite these differences, since we introduced the notion of a categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
-action in
[
6
]
, by combining results from
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
we are able to construct a categorical
\mathfrak{U}_{n}^{+}
-action and show that it induces semiorthogonal decompositions on weight categories. This leads to our second main result. Before we state it, we include a brief remark on terminology.

Remark 1.4
.

Although there is an isomorphism of algebras
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}\simeq\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n-1}}
, we will avoid using the term “categorical
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n-1}}
-action”. In the literature
[
21
]
, a categorical Hall algebra action usually refers to a functorial action of the categorical Hall algebra itself on a given graded category. Since in our setting the algebra
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
plays the primary role, we will instead use the term categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
-action throughout this article to avoid confusion.

Theorem B

(Definition
4.13
and Theorem
4.17
)
.

We define the notion of a categorical
\mathfrak{U}_{n}^{+}
-action. Moreover, given a categorical
\mathfrak{U}_{n}^{+}
-action
\mathcal{K}
, then there is a semiorthogonal decomposition on each weight category
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
given by the essential images of certain functors.

Remark 1.5
.

The above result can be viewed as an refinement of the main result in
[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
, where semiorthogonal decompositions were obtained under the stronger assumption of a full categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
-action.

Since there is an action of
\mathfrak{U}_{n}
on the
\mathrm{GL}_{N}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant K-theory of
n
-step partial flag varieties, it follows in particular that the positive part
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
also acts on this equivariant K-theory. It is natural to expect that this action on K-theory can be lifted to a categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
-action on the derived category.

In
[
6
]
we established the existence of a categorical
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
-action on the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
n
-step partial flag varieties. In particular, this construction induces an action of
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
on their Grothendieck groups.

Moreover, the definitions of the actions of the generators (
e_{i,r}
and
f_{i,s}
in
[
6
]
, corresponding respectively to
F_{i}(r)
and
E_{i}(s)
in
[
1
]
) on equivariant and non-equivariant K-theory coincide. By combining the results of
[
6
]
and
[
8
]
, we arrive at our third main result.

Theorem C

(Theorem
4.21
)
.

There is a categorical
\mathfrak{U}_{n}^{+}
-action on the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves on
n
-step partial flag varieties.

1.3.
Further remarks

Finally, we conclude the introduction with a few remarks and two natural directions for future research that arise from the present work.

The cohomological and K-theoretic Hall algebras of Dynkin quivers have been studied in several earlier works. For example, Kontsevich–Soibelman
[
13
, Subsection 2.8]
considered
\mathrm{CoHA}_{A_{2}}
, Rimányi
[
24
]
treated
\mathrm{CoHA}_{Q}
for Dynkin quivers
Q
of type other than
E_{8}
, and Pădurariu
[
21
, Subsection 5.2]
investigated
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n-1}}
. Our results therefore fit into a broader line of research connecting Hall algebras and representation theory of Dynkin quivers.

In both
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
, the coproduct or Hopf algebra structures on
\mathfrak{U}_{n}
and
\dot{\mathcal{U}}
were not investigated. It would be very interesting to introduce such structures and to apply the Drinfeld double construction in order to recover the full algebra. For related developments, see
[
22
]
.

Finally, although in this article we restrict ourselves to type
A
, the same argument is expected to extend to any Dynkin quiver once appropriate analogues of the 0-affine algebra are defined. At present, such algebras have been introduced only for type
A
(see
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
). Establishing their counterparts for other Dynkin types would provide categorical actions in this broader setting and could lead to semiorthogonal decompositions of derived categories of moduli spaces of (framed) quiver representations.

Background materials

In this section, we recall the basic results about quiver representations and the construction of K-theoretic Hall algebra associated to a quiver (without potentials).

2.1.
Quiver and its representations

By a
quiver
, we mean a quadruple
Q=(I,E,s,t)
, where
I
is the vertex set,
E
is the edge set, and
s,t:E\rightarrow I
are the source and target maps. Moreover, we let
a_{ij}
be the number of arrows from vertex
i
to vertex
j
for any
i,j\in I
.

We call any
I
-tuple non-negative integers
\alpha=(\alpha^{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}_{\geq 0}
a
dimension vector
. A
representation
of
Q
of dimension vector
\alpha
consists of vector spaces
V^{i}
of dimension
\alpha^{i}
for each
i\in I
and
a_{ij}
linear maps from
V^{i}
to
V^{j}
for all
i,j\in I
. Then, we denote the space of all representations of
Q
of dimension
\alpha
is by
R_{\alpha}Q
, and it has the following explicit description

R_{\alpha}Q=\prod_{e\in E}\mathrm{Hom}(V^{s(e)},V^{t(e)}).

Moreover, we consider the reductive group
G_{\alpha}Q\coloneqq\prod_{i\in I}\mathrm{GL}(V^{i})
which acts on
R_{\alpha}Q
via conjugation. We identify two representations of in
R_{\alpha}Q
if there is an element of
G_{\alpha}Q
sending one representation to the other. Finally, we denote the stack of representations of
Q
of dimension vector
\alpha
to be
\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q
, which is given by

\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q\coloneqq[R_{\alpha}Q/G_{\alpha}Q].

(2.1)

For simplicity, we will denote elements in
\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q
as
V^{\bullet}=(V^{i})_{i\in I}
.

2.2.
K-theoretic Hall algebras for quivers without potentials

Let
Q=(I,E,s,t)
be a quiver. For any two dimension vectors
\alpha=(\alpha^{i})_{i\in I},\beta=(\beta^{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}_{\geq 0}
, we define
R_{\alpha,\beta}Q
to be the closed affine subspace of
R_{\alpha+\beta}Q
which consists of representations of
Q
of dimension
\alpha+\beta
that contain a subrepresentation of dimension
\alpha
,i.e.

R_{\alpha,\beta}Q\coloneqq\{(\varphi_{e})_{e\in E}\in R_{\alpha+\beta}Q\ |\ \exists\ W^{i}\subset V^{i},\ \dim W^{i}=\alpha_{i}\ \forall\ i,\ \varphi_{e}(W^{s(e)})\subset W^{t(e)}\ \forall\ e\in E\}.

Similarly, we consider the parabolic subgroup
G_{\alpha,\beta}Q\coloneqq\prod_{i}P_{\alpha^{i},\beta^{i}}\subset G_{\alpha+\beta}Q=\prod_{i}\mathrm{GL}(V^{i})
, where
P_{\alpha^{i},\beta^{i}}\subset\mathrm{GL}(V^{i})
is the subgroup that stabilizes the subspace
W^{i}\subset V^{i}
. Clearly,
G_{\alpha,\beta}Q
acts on
R_{\alpha,\beta}Q
by conjugation. Then, we define the stack

\mathrm{Fl}_{\alpha,\beta}Q\coloneqq[R_{\alpha,\beta}Q/G_{\alpha,\beta}Q],

and will simply denote the elements in
\mathrm{Fl}_{\alpha,\beta}Q
to be
W^{\bullet}\subset V^{\bullet}=(W^{i}\subset V^{i})_{i\in I}
.

There is a natural correspondence

\textstyle{\mathrm{Fl}_{\alpha,\beta}Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{p}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q\times\mathrm{Rep}_{\beta}Q}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha+\beta}Q}

(2.2)

where the maps
p
,
q
are given by

q(W^{\bullet}\subset V^{\bullet})=(W^{\bullet},V^{\bullet}/W^{\bullet}),\ p(W^{\bullet}\subset V^{\bullet})=V^{\bullet}.

For any space
X
, we denote
K(X)
to be the complexified Grothendieck group of
X
, respectively. The maps
p,q
induce pushforwards and pullbacks in K-theory. Then, from the correspondence (
2.2
), we obtain the following maps

\displaystyle m^{K}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle:K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q)\otimes K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\beta}Q)\rightarrow K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha+\beta}Q).

Definition 2.1
.

For a quiver
Q
, its K-theoretic Hall algebra is defined to be the vector space
\bigoplus_{\alpha}K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q)
equipped with the product
m^{K}_{\alpha,\beta}

Proposition 2.2
.

[
21

\mathrm{KHA}_{Q}\coloneqq(\bigoplus_{\alpha}K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q),m^{K}_{\alpha,\beta})

Remark 2.3

If we replace K-theory by cohomology, then we obtain the vector space
\bigoplus_{\alpha}H^{*}(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q)

2.2

\mathrm{CoHA}_{Q}\coloneqq(\bigoplus_{\alpha}H^{*}(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q),m^{H}_{\alpha,\beta})

2.3.

K^{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}(pt)\cong\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}_{1},...,x^{\pm 1}_{n}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}

K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q)\cong\bigotimes_{i\in I}K^{\mathrm{GL}_{\alpha^{i}}(\mathbb{C})}(pt)\cong\bigotimes_{i\in I}\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}_{i1},...,x^{\pm 1}_{i\alpha^{i}}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha^{i}}}.

(2.3)

K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q)\cong\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}_{ij}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}}

0-affine quantum groups and their action

In this section, we first recall the definition of 0-affine quantum groups defined in

3.1.
Definition of the algebra

Definition 3.1
.

[
1
, Definition 1.1]

The
0-affine quantum group
, denoted by
\mathfrak{U}_{n}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{U}}_{0}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}[t,t^{-1}])
, is generated by the following elements

\{e_{i,r},f_{i,r},h_{n,r}\}_{\ 1\leq i\leq n-1}^{r\in\mathbb{Z}}.

subject to the following relations

(1)

Relations on
e

4.1.1.
Surjectivity

We need to know more about the algebra structure of
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}
. In the following, we prove a few results that are well-known to experts, but we decide to provide proofs for completeness.

We start from the simplest case where
n=1
. In this case, the K-theoretic Hall algebra of the type
A_{1}
quiver (a point) is

\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{1}}=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}K(\mathrm{Rep}_{r}Q(A_{1}))\cong\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}_{1},...,x_{r}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}

Let
\Lambda_{r}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{r}^{\pm 1}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{r}}
and set
\omega(z)=\frac{1}{1-z}
. Then,
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{1}}=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}\Lambda_{r}
is the algebra equipped with the shuffle product

(f\ast g)(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+n})\coloneqq\frac{1}{m!n!}\mathrm{Sym}\Big[f(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\,g(x_{m+1},\dots,x_{m+n})\prod_{1\leq i\leq m<j\leq m+n}\omega\Big(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\Big)\Big]

(4.2)

for all
f\in\Lambda_{m}
,
g\in\Lambda_{n}
and
\mathrm{Sym}
denotes the symmetrization operator, i.e.,

\mathrm{Sym}(f(x_{1},...,x_{k}))=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}}f(x_{\sigma(1)},...,x_{\sigma(k)}).

First, we have the following two calculation results.

Lemma 4.2
.

Consider the constant function
1\in K(\mathrm{Rep}_{1}Q(A_{1}))
. Then, for each
r\geq 1
, the following equation holds in
K(\mathrm{Rep}_{r}Q(A_{1}))

1\ast 1\ast...\ast 1=1

where the right hand side is
1
itself shuffle product
r
times.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
r
. Since the case
r=1
is obvious, so we check it for
r=2
.

1\ast 1=\frac{1}{1!1!}\mathrm{Sym}\Big(1\omega(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}})\Big)=\mathrm{Sym}(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{2}-x_{1}})=\frac{x_{2}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}-\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}=1.

Now, we assume that the identity holds for
r\leq k-1
. Then, when
r=k
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}1\ast 1\ast...\ast 1&=(1\ast...\ast 1)\ast 1=1\ast 1\\
&=\frac{1}{(k-1)!1!}\mathrm{Sym}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i\leq k-1<j\leq k}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)=\mathrm{Sym}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i\leq k-1}\frac{x_{k}}{x_{k}-x_{i}}\Big).\end{split}

(4.3)

where we use the induction hypothesis in the second equality.

We denote

g(x_{1},...,x_{k})=\prod_{1\leq i\leq k-1}\frac{x_{k}}{x_{k}-x_{i}}=\frac{x^{k-1}_{k}}{(x_{k}-x_{1})...(x_{k}-x_{k-1})}

Note that
g(x_{\sigma(1)},...,x_{\sigma(k)})=g(x_{1},...,x_{k})
for any
\sigma
in the Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{k-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{1}\leq\mathfrak{S}_{k}
. Let
\tau_{1}=e,\tau_{2}=(k-1,k),\tau_{3}=(k-2,k),...,\tau_{k}=(1,k)
be the representatives in
\mathfrak{S}_{k}/(\mathfrak{S}_{k-1}\times\mathfrak{S}_{1})
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}(g(x_{1},...,x_{k}))=(k-1)!\sum_{i=1}^{k}g(x_{\tau_{i}(1)},...,x_{\tau_{i}(k)})

\displaystyle=(k-1)!\Big[\frac{x^{k-1}_{k}}{(x_{k}-x_{1})...(x_{k}-x_{k-1})}+\frac{x^{k-1}_{k-1}}{(x_{k-1}-x_{1})...(x_{k-1}-x_{k})}+...+\frac{x^{k-1}_{1}}{(x_{1}-x_{k})...(x_{1}-x_{k-1})}\Big]

\displaystyle=(k-1)!\frac{1}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{j}-x_{i})}

\displaystyle\Big[x^{k-1}_{k}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k-1}(x_{j}-x_{i})-x^{k-1}_{k-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k,\ j\neq k-1}(x_{j}-x_{i})+....+(-1)^{k-1}x^{k-1}_{1}\prod_{2\leq i<j\leq k}(x_{j}-x_{i})\Big]

\displaystyle=(k-1)!.

Thus, we obtain (
4.3
) is equal to
1
.
∎

Lemma 4.3

For all

and

x^{a_{1}}_{1}\ast...\ast x^{a_{r}}_{1}=\mathrm{Sym}\Big(x^{a_{1}}_{1}...x^{a_{r}}_{r}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
r
. Clearly, the equation holds for
r=1
, thus we check the case where
r=2
.

x_{1}^{a_{1}}\ast x^{a_{2}}_{1}=\mathrm{Sym}\Big(x^{a_{1}}_{1}x_{2}^{a_{2}}\omega(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{2}})\Big).

Now, we assume that the identity holds for
r\leq k-1
. Then, when
r=k
, we have

\displaystyle x^{a_{1}}_{1}\ast...\ast x^{a_{k}}_{1}

\displaystyle=(x^{a_{1}}_{1}\ast...\ast x_{1}^{a_{k-1}})\ast x^{a_{k}}_{1}=\Bigg\{\mathrm{Sym}\Big(x^{a_{1}}_{1}...x^{a_{k-1}}_{k-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k-1}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)\Bigg\}\ast x^{a_{k}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!1!}\mathrm{Sym}\Bigg(\mathrm{Sym}\Big(x^{a_{1}}_{1}...x^{a_{k-1}}_{k-1}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k-1}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)x_{k}^{a_{k}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq k-1<j\leq k}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sym}\Big(x^{a_{1}}_{1}...x^{a_{k-1}}_{k-1}x_{k}^{a_{k}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq k}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big).

∎

Then, we have the following

Lemma 4.4
.

The algebra

Then, by Lemma

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=f\cdot 1=f(1\ast 1\ast...\ast 1)=f\mathrm{Sym}\Big(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sym}\Big(f\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)=\mathrm{Sym}\Big((\sum_{d_{1},...,d_{r}}c_{d_{1}...d_{r}}x^{d_{1}}_{1}...x^{d_{r}}_{r})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq r}\omega(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}})\Big)

Fix a vertex

Next, fix two vertices

x_{i+1,1}^{-s}\in K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\omega_{i+1}}Q(A_{n}))
. Then,

\displaystyle x_{i+1,1}^{-s}\ast x_{i,1}^{-r}=\frac{1}{1!1!}\mathrm{Sym}\biggl(x_{i+1,1}^{-s}x_{i,1}^{-r}\frac{1-x_{i+1,1}x_{i,1}^{-1}}{1}\biggr)=x_{i+1,1}^{-s}x_{i,1}^{-r}-x_{i+1,1}^{-s+1}x_{i,1}^{-r-1}.

On the other hand, it is standard to calculate

x_{i,1}^{-r}\ast x_{i+1,1}^{-s}=x_{i,1}^{-r}x_{i+1,1}^{-s},\ \text{and}\ x_{i,1}^{-r-1}\ast x_{i+1,1}^{-s+1}=x_{i,1}^{-r-1}x_{i+1,1}^{-s+1}.

Thus, we obtain
x_{i+1,1}^{-s}\ast x_{i,1}^{-r}=x_{i,1}^{-r}\ast x_{i+1,1}^{-s}-x_{i,1}^{-r-1}\ast x_{i+1,1}^{-s+1}
, which corresponds to the second relation
e_{i+1,s}e_{i,r}=e_{i,r}e_{i+1,s}-e_{i,r+1}e_{i+1,s-1}
in (
3.1
).

Finally, for two vertexes
i
and
j
such that there are no arrows between them, it is easy to check that
x_{i,1}^{-r}\ast x_{j,1}^{-s}=x_{j,1}^{-s}\ast x_{i,1}^{-r}
, and it corresponds to the third relation in (
3.1
).

Hence,
\phi_{n+1}
is a surjective algebra homomorphism.
∎

4.1.2.
Comparison of graded dimensions

Since we already show that
\phi_{n+1}
is surjective, to show that
\phi_{n+1}
is an isomorphism, we need to compare the bi-graded dimensions on both sides of the map
\phi_{n+1}
.

Let us look at the
\mathrm{KHA}
side first. Note that, by definition,
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}
is graded by the dimension vector
\alpha=(\alpha^{1},...,\alpha^{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
. Moreover, from the description of the graded piece in (
2.3
), we know that, beside the grading by dimension vectors, we also have the internal/loop grading from the degree of the polynomials.

Then, for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define the following bi-graded piece of
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}

K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q(A_{n}))_{m}=\mathrm{Span}\bigg\{\mathrm{Sym}\bigg(\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\prod_{1\leq a\leq\alpha^{i}}x^{r_{i,a}}_{i,a}\bigg)\in\mathbb{C}[x^{\pm 1}_{i,j}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}}\ \bigg|\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{\alpha^{i}}r_{i,a}=m\bigg\}

which is the subspace spanned by symmetric Laurent monomials of degree
m
.

On the other hand, for the 0-affine quantum group
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
, we know that
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
is generated by
e_{i,r}
where
1\leq i\leq n
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, subject to some quadratic relations. Thus,
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
also has a bi-grading which is the same as the bi-grading of
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}
. More precisely, let

\deg(e_{i,r})=(\omega_{i},r)

where
\omega_{i}
is the dimension vector with
1
in vertex
i
and zero elsewhere, and
r
is the internal/loop grading. Then, for each
\alpha=(\alpha^{1},...,\alpha^{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
has the following bi-graded piece

(\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}=\mathrm{Span}\bigg\{e_{i_{1},r_{1}}...e_{i_{k},r_{|\alpha|}}\ \bigg|\ \sharp\{a\ |\ i_{a}=i\}=\alpha^{i},\ \sum_{a=1}^{|\alpha|}r_{a}=m\bigg\}\bigg/\bigg\langle\text{quadratic relations}\bigg\rangle

where
|\alpha|=\sum_{i}\alpha^{i}
.

However, it is standard to see that both the spaces
K(\mathrm{Rep}_{\alpha}Q(A_{n}))_{m}
and
(\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}
are infinite-dimensional. For example, consider the case where
n=1
, the dimension vector is
2
and
m=0
. Then we have

\displaystyle K(\mathrm{Rep}_{2}(Q(A_{1}))_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\{\mathrm{Sym}(x^{r_{1}}_{1}x^{r_{2}}_{2})\ |\ r_{1}+r_{2}=0\}

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{2})_{2,0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\{e_{r_{1}}e_{r_{2}}\ |\ r_{1}+r_{2}=0\}\bigg/\bigg\langle\text{quadratic relations}\bigg\rangle

which implies that
r_{1}=-r_{2}
. There are infinite many choice of
r_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and the dimensions are infinite.

The above suggests that we have to restrict ourself to subalgebras of
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}
and
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
. Thus, we will consider their non-positive or non-negative parts.

Definition 4.9
.

We define the negative sector, denoted by
\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}}
, to be the subalgebra of
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}
that generated by degree-one elements with non-positive powers, i.e.
x^{r}_{i,j}
with
r\leq 0
, under the shuffle product. More precisely,

\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha}\mathbb{C}[x^{-1}_{i,j}]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}}\subset\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}.

Definition 4.10
.

We define the positive sector
\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
to be the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
that generated by
e_{i,r}
with
r\geq 0
subject to the relations (
3.1
) whenever it makes sense.

Then, both
\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}}
and
\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
inherit the bi-grading from
\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}
and
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
respectively. More precisely, their bigraded pieces are given by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\bigg\{\mathrm{Sym}\bigg(\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}\prod_{1\leq a\leq\alpha^{i}}x^{-r_{i,a}}_{i,a}\bigg)\ \bigg|\ r_{i,a}\geq 0\ \forall\ i,a,\ \text{and}\ \sum r_{i,a}=m\geq 0\bigg\}

(4.5)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\bigg\{e_{i_{1},r_{1}}...e_{i_{k},r_{|\alpha|}}\ \bigg|\ \sharp\{a\ |\ i_{a}=i\}=\alpha^{i},\ r_{a}\geq 0,\ \sum_{a=1}^{|\alpha|}r_{a}=m\bigg\}\bigg/\bigg\langle\text{quadratic relations}\bigg\rangle

(4.6)

Proposition 4.11
.

We have
\dim(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}=\dim(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}
.

Proof.

We prove the case
n=1
first. When
n=1
, the dimension vector is a non-negative integer
\alpha=d
and we have the bi-graded pieces are

\displaystyle(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{1}})_{d,m}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\{\mathrm{Sym}(x^{-r_{1}}_{1}...x^{-r_{d}}_{d})\ |\ r_{i}\geq 0\ \forall\ i,\ \text{and}\ \sum r_{i}=m\geq 0\},

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{2})_{d,m}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\{e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{d}}\ |\ r_{i}\geq 0\ \forall\ i,\ \text{and}\ \sum r_{i}=m\geq 0\}/\langle e_{r}e_{s}+e_{s-1}e_{r+1}\rangle.

Note that a symmetrized monoimal is determined (up to scalar) by the multiset
\{r_{1},...,r_{d}\}
. After sorting it, without loss of generality, we can assume
r_{1}\geq...\geq r_{d}\geq 0
, which means it is a partition of
m
into at most
d
parts. So

\dim(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{1}})_{d,m}=p_{d}(m)

where
p_{d}(m)
denote the number of partitions of
m
into at most
d
parts.

Now, we have to show that
\dim(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{2})_{d,m}=p_{m}(d)
. In order to show this, we show that the bi-graded space
(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{2})_{d,m}
is spanned by

A=\{e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{d}}\ |\ r_{1}\geq...\geq r_{d}\geq 0,\ \text{and}\ \sum r_{i}=m\geq 0\},

i.e., all the other elements can be reduced to elements with weakly decreasing indices by the quadratic relation.

We have to look at the quadratic relation. Observe that when
r<s
or
s-r\geq 1
, we have the following two cases. First, if
s-r=1
, or equivalently
s=r+1
, then the quadratic relation gives
e_{r}e_{r+1}=-e_{r}e_{r+1}
which implies that
e_{r}e_{r+1}=0
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. Second, when
s-r\geq 2
, then it gives
e_{r}e_{s}=-e_{s-1}e_{r+1}
with
s-1\geq r+1
.

Thus, for a general element
e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{d}}
, if it is already in
A
, then we are done. If not, then there exists
1\leq i\leq d-1
such that
r_{i}<r_{i+1}
. Then, we can apply the quadratic relation to get either
e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{d}}=0
or
e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{d}}=-e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{i+1}-1}e_{r_{i}+1}...e_{r_{d}}
. Continuing this process,
e_{r_{1}}...e_{r_{d}}
will eventually reduced to an element in
A
.

Clearly, the elements in
A
are linearly independent. So,
A
is a basis for
(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{2})_{d,m}
and
\dim(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{2})_{d,m}=p_{d}(m)
.

Now, we move to the general case. Given a dimension vector
\alpha
and
m\geq 0
. For the
\mathrm{KHA}
side, from the condition
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{a=1}^{\alpha^{i}}r_{i,a}=m
in (
4.5
), we know that
m
has a composition
m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
where
m_{i}=\sum_{a=1}^{\alpha^{i}}r_{i,a}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Moreover, since the elements are symmetrized monomials, from the study of the
n=1
case, we can assume
r_{i,1}\geq...\geq r_{i,\alpha^{i}}\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus, the number of the basis elements is given by the sum of number of ways decompose
m
into compositions
\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}
together with partition of each
m_{i}
into at most
\alpha^{i}
parts, i.e.,

\dim(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}=\sum_{m=m_{1}+...+m_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{\alpha^{i}}(m_{i}).

Next, for the 0-affine quantum group
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
, since we are in the general case and the proof for
n=1
is done, we can assume
n\geq 2
. Then, we have more quadratic relations, see (
3.1
). For each word
w=e_{i_{1},r_{1}}....e_{i_{d},r_{d}}\in\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
, we define the
number of inversions
to be

\mathrm{Inv}(w)\coloneqq\sharp\{(a,b)\ |\ a<b,\ \text{and}\ i_{a}>i_{b}\}.

For example,
\mathrm{Inv}(e_{1,r}e_{2,s})=0
,
\mathrm{Inv}(e_{2,r}e_{1,s})=1
, and
\mathrm{Inv}(e_{2,r_{1}}e_{3,r_{2}}e_{1,r_{3}})=2
.

The number of inversions induces a filtration
F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset...\subset\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
, where
F_{k}
is defined to be

F_{k}=\mathrm{Span}\{w=e_{i_{1},r_{1}}....e_{i_{d},r_{d}}\in\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}\ |\ \mathrm{Inv}(w)\leq k\}

for all
k\geq 0
. Note that when
k=0
, the
F_{0}
is spanned by words of the form
e_{1,r_{1,1}}...e_{1,r_{1,d_{1}}}...e_{n,r_{n,1}}...e_{n,r_{n,d_{n}}}
.

Taking the associated graded, we have
\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}=\bigoplus_{k}F_{k}/F_{k-1}
. Observe that, the Drinfeld-Serre relation

e_{i+1,s}e_{i,r}=e_{i,r}e_{i+1,s}-e_{i,r+1}e_{i+1,s-1}

in (
3.1
) shows that every word with at least one number of inversions can be written as a linear combinations of words with with smaller number of inversions. Thus we have
F_{k}/F_{k-1}=0
for all
k\geq 1
, and
\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}=F_{0}
.

By restricting to the subalgebra
\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
, we also obtain a filtration
G_{0}\subset G_{1}\subset...\subset\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
where
G_{k}=F_{k}\cap\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
for all
k\geq 0
. Taking the associated graded, we obtain
\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}=\bigoplus_{k}G_{k}/G_{k-1}=G_{0}=F_{0}\cap\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
since
F_{k}/F_{k-1}=0
for all
k\geq 1
. Then, we have the bi-graded piece is given by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}

\displaystyle=F_{0}\cap(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Span}\{e_{1,r_{1,1}}...e_{1,r_{1,\alpha^{1}}}...e_{n,r_{n,1}}...e_{n,r_{n,\alpha^{n}}}\ |\ r_{i,a}\geq 0\ \forall\ i,a,\ \text{and}\ \sum r_{i,a}=m\}.

Moreover, as we prove the case
n=1
, we can use the quadratic relation
e_{i,r}e_{i,s}=-e_{i,s-1}e_{i,r+1}
to deduce that
(\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}
is spanned by

\{e_{1,r_{1,1}}...e_{1,r_{1,\alpha^{1}}}...e_{n,r_{n,1}}...e_{n,r_{n,\alpha^{n}}}\ |\ r_{i,1}\geq...\geq r_{i,\alpha^{i}}\geq 0\ \forall\ i,\ \text{and}\ \sum r_{i,a}=m\}

which has the same cardinality as the case in
(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}
, i.e.,
\dim(\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}=\dim(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}=\sum_{m=m_{1}+...+m_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{\alpha^{i}}(m_{i}).

Since we prove
\phi_{n+1}
is a surjection in Theorem
4.1
, from the definition
\phi_{n+1}(e_{i,r})=x^{-r}_{i,1}
, clearly it restricts to a surjection
\phi_{n+1}|_{\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}}:\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}}
. So

\dim(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}\leq\dim(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}\leq\dim(\mathrm{gr}\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}

and we conclude that
\dim(\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}=\dim(\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}

∎

From Proposition
4.8
and Proposition
4.11
, we obtain the following algebra isomorphism

\phi_{n+1}|_{\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}}:\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}}

which gives isomorphism of subalgebras in
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
and
\mathrm{KHA}^{\leq 0}_{A_{n}}
. In order to extend
\phi_{n+1}|_{\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}}
to a full isomorphism
\phi_{n+1}
, we introduce the following degree-shift automorphism

\displaystyle\tau_{k}

\displaystyle:\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1},\ \tau_{k}(e_{i,r})=e_{i,r+k},\ \forall\ i,\ \text{and}\ r,k\in\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\eta_{k}

\displaystyle:\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}}\rightarrow\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}},\ \eta_{k}(x^{r}_{i,1})=x^{r-k}_{i,1},\ \forall\ i,\ \text{and}\ r,k\in\mathbb{Z}.

It is clear to see that
\phi_{n+1}\circ\tau_{k}=\eta_{k}\circ\phi_{n+1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The following result proves Theorem
4.1
.

Proposition 4.12
.

The map
\phi_{n+1}
in Theorem
4.1
is injective.

Proof.

We show that
\ker(\phi_{n+1})=0
. Given
x\in\ker(\phi_{n+1})
, we have
x\in\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
such that
\phi_{n+1}(x)=0
.

Since
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}
is bi-graded, i.e.,
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1}=\bigoplus_{\alpha}\bigoplus_{m}(\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}
,
x
can be written as a finite sum
x=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{m}x_{\alpha,m}
with
x_{\alpha,m}\in(\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Since
\phi_{n+1}
is a graded algebra homomorphism, i.e.,
\phi_{n+1}((\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n+1})_{\alpha,m})\subset(\mathrm{KHA}_{A_{n}})_{\alpha,m}
, we have
0=\phi_{n+1}(x)=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{m}\phi_{n+1}(x_{\alpha,m})
with each
\phi_{n+1}(x_{\alpha,m})
in different bi-graded component. Thus,
\phi_{n+1}(x_{\alpha,m})=0
for all
\alpha
and
m
.

It suffices to show that
x_{\alpha,m}=0
for all
\alpha
and
m
such that
x=0
. For each
\alpha
and
m
, we choose
k
large enough and apply the degree-shift automorphism so that
\tau_{k}(x_{\alpha,m})\in\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}
. Then we have

0=\eta_{k}(\phi_{n+1}(x_{\alpha,m}))=\phi_{n+1}|_{\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}}(\tau_{k}(x_{\alpha,m})).

Since
\phi_{n+1}|_{\mathfrak{U}^{+,\geq 0}_{n+1}}
is injective and
\tau_{k}
is an automorphism, we have
\tau_{k}(x_{\alpha,m})=0
and thus
x_{\alpha,m}=0
.

∎

4.2.
Definition of the categorical action

Roughly speaking, a categorical action of a Kac-Moody Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
consists of a collection of functors and categories that recover actions of Chevalley generators at the level of Grothendieck groups. Under this philosophy, we propose the following definition of categorical action of
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
, or simply called
categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
–action
.

Recall the notations
\underline{k}
and
C(n,N)
in the previous section. Moreover, we denote
\alpha_{i}=(0,...,-1,1,...,0)
to be the simple root, where
1\leq i\leq n-1
and
-1
is at the
i
th position.

Definition 4.13
.

A
categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
-action
consists of a target 2-category
\mathcal{K}
, which is triangulated,
\mathbb{C}
-linear and idempotent complete. The objects in
\mathcal{K}
are

\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{K})=\{\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})\ |\ \underline{k}\in C(n,N)\}

where each
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
is also a triangulated category, and each Hom space
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}),\mathcal{K}(\underline{l}))
is also triangulated. On those objects
\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})
we impose the following 1-morphisms:

\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}:\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}),\ {\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}=\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}:\mathcal{K}(\underline{k})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}),

where
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

On this data, we impose the following condition

(1)

For each
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
admits left and right adjoint functors
({\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}})^{L}={\sf{E}}^{L}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}}
,
({\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}})^{R}={\sf{E}}^{R}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}-\alpha_{i}}
, respectively.

(2)

The functors
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
satisfy

(a)

{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong\begin{cases}{\sf{E}}_{i,s-1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[1]&\text{if}\ r-s\geq 0\\
0&\text{if}\ r-s=-1\\
{\sf{E}}_{i,s-1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[-1]&\text{if}\ r-s\leq-2.\end{cases}

(b)

{\sf{E}}_{i,r},{\sf{E}}_{i+1,s}
are related by the following exact triangle

{\sf{E}}_{i,r+1}{\sf{E}}_{i+1,s-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}_{i+1,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}_{i+1,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}.

(c)

{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}_{j,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}_{j,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}},\ \text{if}\ |i-j|\geq 2.

(3)

There are exact triangles

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i,r-1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i,r-1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}

for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(4)

The relations between
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}
and
{\sf{E}}^{R}_{j,s}
,
{\sf{E}}^{L}_{j,s}
are given by

(a)

When
i=j
, there is a so-called shifted condition.

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}

\displaystyle\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i,s-1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[-1],\ \mathrm{if}\ 1\leq r-s\leq k_{i}+k_{i+1}-1,

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}

\displaystyle\cong{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i,s-1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[1],\ \mathrm{if}\ -k_{i}-k_{i+1}+1\leq r-s\leq-1.

(b)

When
|i-j|=1
, we have

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i+1,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i+1,s+1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[1],\

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i-1,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{i-1,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i+1,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i+1,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}},\

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i-1,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}^{L}_{i-1,s+1}{\sf{E}}_{i,r+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}[-1].

(c)

{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{j,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{j,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
,
{\sf{E}}_{i,r}{\sf{E}}^{L}_{j,s}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}\cong{\sf{E}}^{L}_{j,s}{\sf{E}}_{i,r}\boldsymbol{1}_{\underline{k}}
for all
|i-j|\geq 2
.

We give a few remarks about this definition.

Remark 4.14
.

The condition (2) can be viewed as a lift/categorification of the relation (
3.1
) for the generators
e_{i,r}
in Definition
3.1
.

Remark 4.15
.

Denote

4.3.

Before we state our second result, we need to introduce more notations. Any sequence of non-increasing positive integers

\lambda_{i}

such that
\lambda_{1}\leq a
and
\lambda_{b+1}=0
.

Theorem 4.17
.

Given a categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
-action
\mathcal{K}
. Considering the functors

\boldsymbol{\lambda}(i)\in P(k_{i+1},\sum_{j=1}^{i}k_{j})

(1)

They are all fully-faithful, i.e.

Corollary 4.18

Given a categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{n}
-action
\mathcal{K}

(4.7)

(4)

The shifted condition.

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{r}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}

\displaystyle\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{s-1}{\sf{E}}_{r-1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}[-1],\ \mathrm{if}\ 1\leq r-s\leq N-1,

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}_{r}{\sf{E}}^{L}_{s}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k,N-k)}

Theorem 4.20
.

Given a categorical
\mathfrak{U}^{+}_{2}
-action
\mathcal{K}
. Then the functors
\{{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in P(N-k,k)}

(1)

Each
{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}

\mathrm{Hom}({\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)},{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)},\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)})

(2)

if
\boldsymbol{\lambda}<_{l}\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
(semiorthogonal property).

Proof.

We prove the fully-faithfulness first. Given a Young diagram
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{k})\in P(N-k,k)
. We apply the right adjoint to get

\mathrm{Hom}({\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)},{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)})\cong\mathrm{Hom}(\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)},{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}).

We will show that
{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}
. Explicitly, we have

{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}={\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}.

(4.8)

Assume

first, then
{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}={\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}

{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}.

{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\cong\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}
, which is our desired result.

Now, we assume

. Then, by condition (3) again, we have the following exact triangle

{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}\rightarrow\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-1,N-k+1)}.

Thus the composition in (
4.8
) is in the following exact triangle

{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{2}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{2}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\rightarrow{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}.

(4.9)

Note that since

for all

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-2,N-k+2)}

\displaystyle\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+2}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-2,N-k+2)}[1]\ (\text{since}\ 1\leq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1\leq N-k+1\leq N-1)

\displaystyle\cong...

\displaystyle\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+k-1}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-2,N-k+2)}[k-2]\ (\text{since}\ k-2\leq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{k-1}+k-2\leq N-k+k-2\leq N-1)

\displaystyle\cong{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+k}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}+1}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}+1}\boldsymbol{1}_{(k-2,N-k+2)}[k-1]\ (\text{since}\ k-1\leq\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{k}+k-1\leq N-k+k-1\leq N-1).

Thus,

{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{2}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\cong{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{1}+k}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}+1}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}+1}{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}+1}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{2}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}[k-1]\cong 0

and it implies

{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\cong{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{2}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{2}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}.

from the exact triangle (
4.9

such that

. Then, by taking the right adjoint, we have

We will show that
{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}\cong 0
. Explicitly, we have

{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}{\sf{E}}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}={\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{1}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{1}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}.

Since

, we define

Next, since

Continuing this process, (

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{k}}...{\sf{E}}^{R}_{\lambda_{i}}{\sf{E}}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}...{\sf{E}}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{(0,N)}

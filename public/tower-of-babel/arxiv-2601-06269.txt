A characterisation of probabilistic metrizability for approach spaces
E. Colebunders, R. Lowen

Abstract




Abstract

Characterisations of metrizable topological spaces or metrizable uniform spaces are well known. A natural counterpart to being metrizable for topological spaces can be expressed in terms of probabilistic metrizability for approach spaces. The notion of a probabilistic metrizable approach space is based on a well known concrete functor
\Gamma,
as introduced in
[
9
]
, from the category of probabilistic metric spaces with respect to a continuous arbitrary t-norm to the category of approach spaces. A characterization of those probabilistic metrizable approach spaces is still missing and in the first part of this paper we solve this problem.

A natural counterpart to being metrizable for uniform spaces can be expressed in terms of probabilistic metrizability for uniform gauge spaces. In the second part of the paper we start from another concrete functor
\Lambda,
as described in
[
7
]
, on the category of probabilistic metric spaces with respect to a continuous t-norm to the category of uniform gauge spaces. In a similar way as for the functor
\Gamma
we obtain a characterisation of probabilistic metrizability of uniform gauge spaces.

The last section of the paper is devoted to an isomorphic description of the category of probabilistic metric spaces. This problem is not new. Previous attempts in providing isomorphic descriptions of the category of probabilistic metric spaces worked with collections of (pseudo)metrics.
These attempts were only formulated in restricted cases.
Our isomorphic description is in terms of objects that are sets endowed with a collection of distances, where
the distances involved do not satisfy the triangle inequality but fulfil a mixed triangle condition instead.

Keywords: Probabilistic metric space; Approach space; Local system; Uniform gauge space; Uniform system; Probabilistic metrizability; Category of probabilistic metric spaces; Mixed triangle inequality

Mathematics Subject Classification: 54E70; 54E35; 54A05; 54E15.

2.
Preliminaries

For more information on probabilistic metric spaces we refer to
[
15
]
and for the link with approach spaces to
[

and
[

.

A function
d:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
that is zero on the diagonal is called a
distance.
This notion is not to be confused with distances between points and sets which also play an important role in approach theory but will not be encountered here. A distance that is symmetric is called a
symmetric distance.

A
generalised metric
(quasi-metric in
[
11
]
) on a set
X
is a map
d:X\times X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
which is zero on the diagonal and satisfies the triangle inequality. If moreover
d
is symmetric we call it a
symmetric generalised metric
.

We recall some terminology from
[
15
]
and
[
9
]
.

A function
\varphi:[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]
is called a
distance distribution
if
\varphi
is monotone,
\varphi(0)=0,\varphi(\infty)=1
and
\varphi
is left-continuous on
]0,\infty[
.

A binary operation
*
on the interval
[0,1]
is a
continuous t-norm
if
([0,1],*,1)
is a commutative monoid, which is continuous as a function on
[0,1]^{2}
to
[0,1]
with respect to the usual topologies and satisfies
p*q\leq p^{\prime}*q^{\prime}
whenever
p\leq p^{\prime}
and
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
[0,1].

A
probabilistic metric space
is a set
X
endowed with a map

(1)

\alpha:X\times X\times[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]

and a continuous t-norm
*
such that

(P1)

\alpha(x,y,-):[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a distance distribution,

(P2)

\alpha(x,x,-)=\varepsilon_{0},
the largest distance distribution,

(P3)

\alpha(x,y,r)=\alpha(y,x,r),

(P4)

\alpha(x,y,-)=\varepsilon_{0}\Rightarrow x=y,

(P5)

\alpha(y,z,r)*\alpha(x,y,s)\leq\alpha(x,z,r+s),

for all
x,y,z\in X,r,s\in[0,\infty].

A map
f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\beta)
between probabilistic metric spaces is
non-expansive
if

(2)

\alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)\leq\beta(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t),

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in X,t\in[0,\infty].

In this paper we will always assume that the probabilistic metric spaces are defined with respect to some continuous t-norm which we denote by
*
.

The category of probabilistic metric spaces with non-expansive maps is denoted by
\mathsf{ProbMet}.

Next we recall some notions from approach theory. For more details on concepts and results on approach spaces we refer to
[
11
]
or
[
10
]
. We recall terminology and basic results that will be needed in this paper.

2.1 Definition
.

A collection of ideals
(\mathcal{A}(x))_{x\in X}
in
[0,\infty]^{X}
indexed by the points of
X
is a
local system
if for all
x\in X
the following properties hold:

(A1)

\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(x),\ \varphi(x)=0.

(A2)

If
\forall\varepsilon>0,\forall\omega<\infty,\exists\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
such that
\ \varphi\wedge\omega\leq\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}+\varepsilon
,

then
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(x).

(A3)

\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{A}(x),\forall\varepsilon>0,\forall\omega<\infty,\exists(\varphi_{z})_{z\in x}\in\prod_{z\in X}\mathcal{A}(z)\ \text{such that}

\forall z,y\in X,\varphi(z)\wedge\omega\leq\varphi_{x}(y)+\varphi_{y}(z)+\varepsilon.

The members of
\mathcal{A}(x)
are called
local distances.

2.2 Definition
.

A collection of ideal bases
(\mathcal{B}(x))_{x\in X}
in
[0,\infty]^{X}
indexed by the points of
X
is a
local basis
if for all
x\in X
the properties (A1) and (A3) hold.

In order to derive the local system from a local basis we will require the following
saturation operation
.
Given a subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq[0,\infty]^{X}
we define

(3)

\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle=\{\varphi\in[0,\infty]^{X}|\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\forall\omega<\infty,\exists\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\in\mathcal{B}\ \text{such that}\ \varphi\wedge\omega\leq\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}+\varepsilon\},

the saturation of
\mathcal{B}
and we say that
\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
is
generated
by
\mathcal{B}
.

A map
f:(X,(\mathcal{A}(x))_{x\in X})\rightarrow(Y,(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(y))_{y\in Y}))
is a
contraction
if for every
x\in X
and every
\varphi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(f(x))
we have

(4)

\varphi^{\prime}\circ f\in\mathcal{A}(x).

It is sufficient to impose the condition on a local basis in the codomain.
The category
{\mathsf{App}}
of approach spaces has as objects sets endowed with a local system and contractions as morphisms.
For other
isomorphic descriptions
of
{\mathsf{App}}
we refer to the literature.

Probabilistic metrizable approach spaces

In this section we give an internal characterisation of probabilistic metrizable approach spaces.
We start with the concrete functor
[

]
,
[
7
]

(5)

\Gamma:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathsf{App},

where to each probabilistic metric space
(X,\alpha,*)
an approach space is assigned.
In
[
9

the functor was described using approach spaces in terms of distances
\delta(x,A)
between points and sets. In
[
7
]
the functor was equivalently described in terms of the tower of an approach space and it was shown that the codomain of the functor can be restricted to
\mathsf{UAp}

In
[

yet another equivalent transition from probabilistic metric spaces

to approach spaces in terms of local systems was formulated using countable collections of local distances.
It is a similar formulation of the functor
\Gamma
we will use here.

Let
(X,\alpha,*)
be a probabilistic metric space. In
3.2
we show that an
underlying approach space

X
is associated with it, defined by the following local basis in

(6)

\mathcal{B}(x)=\{\varphi_{\lambda,x}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}\ \

with

\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)=\myinf\{0\leq\gamma<\infty\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}.

3.1 Proposition
.

For
0\leq\gamma<\infty
and for
0<\lambda\leq 1
we have

(7)

\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

Proof.

Suppose
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma,
then there exists
\mu<\gamma
satisfying
\alpha(x,y,\mu)>1-\lambda.

This implies
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda
since
\alpha
is nondecreasing.

To prove the other implication, assume
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.
Left continuity of
\alpha
implies that there exists
\mu<\gamma
with
\alpha(x,y,\mu)>1-\lambda.
This implies

\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)\leq\mu<\gamma.

∎

3.2 Proposition
.

\mathcal{B}(x)=\{\varphi_{\lambda,x}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
as defined in (
6
) is a local basis on
X
in the sense of
2.2
.

Proof.

Clearly all
\varphi_{\lambda,x}\in[0,\infty]^{X}.

That (A1) is fulfilled, namely
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(x)=0
for every
\lambda,x
follows from the fact that
\alpha(x,x,\gamma)=1
for all
\gamma>0
.

Next we prove condition (A3). In fact we prove a
a local mixed triangle inequality

(LM)

that clearly implies (A3):
For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and

0<\lambda\leq 1
and such that

(1-\lambda)*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

and for all
x,y,z\in X
we have

(8)

\varphi_{\varepsilon,x}(z)\leq\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)+\varphi_{\lambda,y}(z).

First observe that in view of the continuity of the t-norm
*
on
[0,1]
for every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
there exists
0<\lambda\leq 1
such that

(1-\lambda)*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

If the righthandside of (
8
) is
\infty
there is nothing to prove.
Next let
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
be arbitrary, with
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma
and
\varphi_{\lambda,y}(z)<\gamma^{\prime}.

Applying (
7
) we get
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda
and
\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})>1-\lambda
.
By axiom (P5) for
\alpha
we obtain

\alpha(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})*\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\geq(1-\lambda)*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

Again applying (
7
) we get
\varphi_{\varepsilon,x}(z)<\gamma+\gamma^{\prime},
from which the conclusion (
8
) follows.

∎

3.3 Proposition
.

\Gamma:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathsf{App}
is a concrete functor, meaning that when
f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})\ \text{is non-expansive}
then
\Gamma(f):\Gamma((X,\alpha))\rightarrow\Gamma((Y,\alpha^{\prime}))
is a contraction, where
\Gamma((X,\alpha))
and
\Gamma((Y,\alpha^{\prime}))
have respective local systems

(\mathcal{A}(x))_{x}
generated by
\mathcal{B}(x)=\{\varphi_{\lambda,x}\ |0<\lambda\leq 1\}
and
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(y))_{y}
generated by
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(y)=\{\varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda,y}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
as in (

Proof.

First observe that in case
\varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda,f(x)}(f(x^{\prime}))=\infty
then for every

\alpha(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),\gamma)\not>1-\lambda.

This implies that for every

Hence we can conclude that

In the finite case, applying (

\displaystyle f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})\ \text{is non-expansive}\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\forall\lambda:\ \ (\alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)>1-\lambda\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t)>1-\lambda)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\forall\lambda:\ \varphi_{\lambda,x}(x^{\prime})<t\Rightarrow\varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda,f(x)}(f(x^{\prime}))<t

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\lambda,\ \varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda,f(x)}(f(x^{\prime}))\leq\varphi_{\lambda,x}(x^{\prime}).

Clearly the last line implies:

\text{if}\ \varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda,f(x)}\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}(f(x))\ \text{then}\ \ \varphi^{\prime}_{\lambda,f(x)}\circ f\in\mathcal{A}(x),

and by (

∎

3.4 Theorem

(
Part I of Theorem 3.8).

Let
(X,\alpha,*)
be a probabilistic metric space. The associated local basis
\mathcal{B}(x)=\{\varphi_{\lambda,x}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
in
x,
as in (

) and

, has the following properties:

(LS)

Symmetry: For every
0<\lambda\leq 1
and for
x,y\in X
, we have

\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)=\varphi_{\lambda,y}(x).

(LD)

Density: For every

0<\lambda\leq 1

\varphi_{\lambda,x}=\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}\varphi_{\rho,x}.

(LT)

t-Norm: For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

0<\lambda\leq 1
and
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq 1

with

(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon

then for all

we have

\varphi_{\varepsilon,x}(z)\leq\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)+\varphi_{\lambda^{\prime},y}(z).

(LH)

Separation:

x\not=y\ \text{then}\ \exists\lambda,\ 0<\lambda\leq 1,\ \varphi_{\lambda,x}>0.

Proof.

(LS)
Follows from (P3), since
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)=\alpha(y,x,\gamma)
for all
x,y,\gamma.

(LD)
Suppose
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda\leq 1
and
x\in X.
First observe that in the infinite case, when
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)=\infty
then
\{\gamma\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}=\emptyset
and then clearly also
\{\gamma\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda^{\prime}\}=\emptyset
and
\varphi_{\lambda^{\prime},x}(y)=\infty.

In the finite case it is sufficient to apply (
7
) in order to conclude that
\varphi_{\lambda,x}\leq\varphi_{\lambda^{\prime},x}.

From the previous observation we already have
\varphi_{\lambda,x}\leq\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}\varphi_{\rho,x}.

We show the reverse inequality. Let
y\in X
. In the infinite case with
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)=\infty,
there is nothing to show.
Next assume
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma<\infty.
Applying (
7
) we have
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.
Then we can choose
\rho<\lambda
with

\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\rho.
Clearly
\varphi_{\rho,x}(y)<\gamma
which implies
\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}\varphi_{\rho,x}(y)<\gamma.

(LT)

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

0<\lambda\leq 1
and
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq 1
be satisfying

(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

For
x,y,z\in X
arbitrary,
if the righthandside of the inequality
\varphi_{\varepsilon,x}(z)\leq\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)+\varphi_{\lambda^{\prime},y}(z)
is infinite, there is nothing to prove. Next assume
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma
and
\varphi_{\lambda^{\prime},y}(z)<\gamma^{\prime}

\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})>1-\lambda^{\prime}

Now apply (P5)

\alpha(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})*\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\geq(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

Finally we get
\varphi_{\varepsilon,x}(z)<\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}.

Suppose

That there exists

with

follows from the fact that by (P4)
\exists\gamma>0
with
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\not=1
and hence

with

3.5 Definition
.

Let
X
be an approach space, having a local basis

in
x

\mathcal{B}(x)=\{\varphi_{\lambda,x}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}.

For

we define
\beta(x,y,\infty)=1
and further for

(9)

\beta(x,y,\gamma)=\sup\{1-\lambda\ |\ \varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma\}=1-\myinf\{\lambda\ |\ \varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma\}.

3.6 Proposition
.

Given an approach space
X
having a local basis satisfying
(LD)
,
then for
0\leq\gamma<\infty
we have:

\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma.

Proof.

Let
\sup\{1-\rho\ |\ \varphi_{\rho,x}(y)<\gamma\}>1-\lambda.
Then
\exists\rho<\lambda,\ \varphi_{\rho,x}(y)<\gamma
and applying
(LD)
we have

\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma.

For the other implication, assume
\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma.
Then again applying
(LD)

\exists\rho<\lambda,\ \ \varphi_{\rho,x}(y)<\gamma.

Hence
1-\lambda<1-\rho\leq\beta(x,y,\gamma).

∎

First we need the following lemma.

3.7 Lemma
.

Let
([0,1],*)
be a continuous t-norm. For all
a,b,d\in]0,1],
the following are equivalent:

(1)

d\geq a*b

(2)

\forall\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in]0,1],
if
a>1-\lambda,\ b>1-\lambda^{\prime}
then
d\geq(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)

(3)

\forall\rho\in]0,1],
if
a*b>1-\rho
then
d\geq 1-\rho.

Proof.

That (1) and (3) are equivalent and that (1) implies (2) are straightforward.
We prove that (2) implies (3).

Let
\rho\in]0,1],
and assume that
a*b>1-\rho.

Choose strictly increasing sequences
(1-\lambda_{n})_{n},
in
]0,1]
converging to
a
and
(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{n})_{n},
converging to
b.

By the continuity of
*
on
[0,1]\times[0,1]
the sequence
((1-\lambda_{n})*(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{n}))_{n}
converges to
a*b
with
(1-\lambda_{n})*(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{n})\leq a*b
for all
n.

Since
]1-\rho,1]
is an open neighborhood of
a*b
we can fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(1-\lambda_{n})*(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{n})\in]1-\rho,1].

By application of (2) we now have

d\geq(1-\lambda_{n})*(1-\lambda^{\prime}_{n})>1-\rho.

∎

Suppose

On

\beta(x,y,0)=0

Next we check that

\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

For every
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
satisfying

(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon,

applying
(LT)
,
we have

\varphi_{\varepsilon,x}(z)\leq\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)+\varphi_{\lambda^{\prime},y}(z)<\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma.

Hence

\beta(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})>1-\varepsilon.

This implies

\beta(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda).

Applying
3.7

we can conclude that
\beta(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq\beta(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})*\beta(x,y,\gamma).

Finally we prove that the underlying approach space of
(X,\beta)
is
X
.

Let
\{\psi_{\lambda,x}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
be associated with
(X,\beta)
as in (
6
). Then from (
7
) and
3.6
for
\gamma
finite

\psi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\lambda,x}(y)<\gamma.

Hence we can conclude that
\psi_{\lambda,x}=\varphi_{\lambda,x}
for all
\lambda
.
∎

From Theorems
3.4
(Part I of 3.8) and
3.8
(Part II of 3.8), we now have the following conclusion.

3.9 Theorem
.

The probabilistic metrizable approach spaces are exactly those
X
having a local basis in
x

\mathcal{B}(x)=\{\varphi_{\lambda,x}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\},

satisfying
(LS)
,
(LD)
,
(LT)
for some continuous t-norm
*
and
(LH)
.

4.
Probabilistic metrizability of uniform gauge spaces

In this section we give an internal characterisation of probabilistic metrizablility in the uniform case. We will work with objects defined by a uniform alternative of the local systems, namely uniform systems
[
13
]
and with uniform contractions as morphisms. The category we will be working with is isomorphic to the category
\mathsf{UG}
of uniform gauge spaces
[
11
]
.
We start with the concrete functor
[
7
]

(10)

\Lambda:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathsf{UG},

where to each probabilistic metric space
(X,\alpha,*)
an underlying space
X
is assigned endowed with a uniform system.

Uniform systems are defined in terms of collections of distances between points. That means that the triangle inequality of the functions in
[0,\infty]^{X\times X}
is not required. As in the previous section it is replaced by a mixed triangle condition (AU3).
For more details we refer to
[
13
]
.

4.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a set. A
uniform system
on
X
is an ideal
\Upsilon\subseteq[0,\infty]^{X\times X}
such that

(AU1)

\forall d\in\Upsilon,\forall x\in X,d(x,x)=0.

(AU2)

If
\forall\varepsilon>0,\forall N<\infty,\exists d_{\varepsilon}^{N}\in\Upsilon,e\wedge N\leq d_{\varepsilon}^{N}+\varepsilon,\ \text{then}\ e\in\Upsilon.

(AU3)

\forall d\in\Upsilon,\forall N<\infty,\exists e^{N}\in\Upsilon,\forall x,y,z\in X,

d(x,z)\wedge N\leq e^{N}(x,y)+e^{N}(y,z).

(AU4)

\forall d\in\Upsilon,d^{-1}\in\Upsilon.

The members of
\Upsilon
are called
uniform distances.

As for the local systems, also in the uniform context it is sufficient to have a basis.

4.2 Definition
.

Let
X
be a set and
\Psi\subseteq[0,\infty]^{X\times X}
. Then
\Psi
is said to be a
uniform basis
if
\Psi
is an ideal basis satisfying (AU1), (AU3) and (AU4).

In order to derive the uniform system from a uniform basis, we will require the following
saturation operation
.
Given a subset
\Psi\subseteq[0,\infty]^{X\times X},
a basis of a uniform system, we define

(11)

\langle\Psi\rangle=\{d\in[0,\infty]^{X\times X}\ |\ \forall\varepsilon>0,\forall N<\infty,\exists d_{\varepsilon}^{N}\in\Psi,e\wedge N\leq d_{\varepsilon}^{N}+\varepsilon,\}

the saturation of
\Psi
and we say that
\langle\Psi\rangle
is
generated
by
\Psi
.

A function
f:(X,\Upsilon)\rightarrow(Y,\Upsilon^{\prime})
is a
uniform contraction
iff

(12)

d^{\prime}\circ(f\times f)\in\Upsilon\ \text{whenever}\ \ d\in\Upsilon^{\prime}.

Again it is sufficient to impose the condition on a uniform basis in the codomain.

In
[
13
]
it is shown that the category of sets endowed with uniform systems and uniform contractions is an
isomorphic description
of
\mathsf{UG},
the category of uniform gauge spaces as introduced in
[
11
]
. Hence we keep on calling the category
\mathsf{UG}
.

Next we show that there is a concrete functor

\Lambda:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathsf{UG}.

In
[
7
]
the object associated to a probabilistic metric space was described in terms of the tower.
In
[
14
]

[
15
]
, yet another equivalent transition from probabilistic metric spaces in terms of collections of (finitely valued) symmetric distances was given.
It is the same formulation for the associated collections of (symmetric) distances we will use here. The difference is that we interpret the collection as a basis for a uniform system. We come back to the setting of collections of (symmetric) distances in the last section.

Let
(X,\alpha,*)
be a probabilistic metric space. An
underlying
\mathsf{UG}
-space

X
is associated with it and is defined by giving the following uniform basis
\Psi,

(13)

\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\},\ \

with

d_{\lambda}(x,y)=\myinf\{0\leq\gamma<\infty\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}.

Quite similar as in (
7
) we can prove that for
\gamma
finite we have the following equivalence. Because of the similarities with the previous section we do not repeat all the proofs.

4.3 Proposition
.

For
0\leq\gamma<\infty
and for
0<\lambda\leq 1
we have

(14)

d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

4.4 Proposition
.

\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
as defined in (
13
) is a uniform basis on
X
in the sense of
4.2
.

Proof.

By definition all
d_{\lambda}\in[0,\infty]^{X\times X}.

That (AU1) is fulfilled, namely
d_{\lambda}(x,x)=0
for every
\lambda,
follows from the fact that
\alpha(x,x,\gamma)=1
for all
\gamma>0
.

Next we prove condition (AU3). In fact we prove a
uniform mixed triangle inequality

(UM)

that clearly implies (AU3):
For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

0<\lambda\leq 1
such that

(1-\lambda)*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

and for all
x,y,z\in X
we have

(15)

d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)+d_{\lambda}(y,z).

First observe that in view of the continuity of the t-norm
*
on
[0,1]
there exists
0<\lambda\leq 1
such that

(1-\lambda)*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

If the righthandside of (
15
) is
\infty
there is nothing to prove.
Next let
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
be arbitrary, with
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma
and
d_{\lambda}(y,z)<\gamma^{\prime}.

Applying (
14
) we get
\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda
and
\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})>1-\lambda
.
By axiom (P5) for
\alpha
we obtain

\alpha(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\geq\alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})*\alpha(x,y,\gamma)\geq(1-\lambda)*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon.

Again applying (
14
) we get
d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)<\gamma+\gamma^{\prime},
from which the conclusion (
15
) follows.

The property (AU4) follows from condition
(US)
which we list below in
4.6
.

∎

It is easy to see that
\Lambda
is indeed a functor by applying the results in
[
7
]
, as the description given there in terms of the tower is equivalent to the one presented here. The sets
\{d_{\lambda}<\rho\}
coincide with the sets
U_{\lambda}^{\rho}

[
7
]
.
The proof in terms of uniform distances is quite similar to the one in
3.3
.

4.5 Proposition
.

\Lambda:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathsf{UG}
is a concrete functor, meaning that when
f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})
is non-expansive then
\Lambda(f):(X,\Upsilon)\rightarrow(Y,\Upsilon^{\prime})
is a uniform contraction in the sence of (
12
), with
\Lambda((X,\alpha))=(X,\Upsilon)
and
\Lambda((Y,\alpha^{\prime}))=(Y,\Upsilon^{\prime})

In order to determine the image of
\mathsf{ProbMet}
by
\Lambda
we list the following properties.

4.6 Theorem

(
Part I of Theorem 4.10).

Let
(X,\alpha,*)
be a probabilistic metric space. The associated uniform basis
\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}

as in (
13
) has the following properties:

(US)

Symmetry: For every
0<\lambda\leq 1
and for
x,y\in X
, we have

d_{\lambda}(x,y)=d_{\lambda}(y,x).

(UD)

Density: For every
0<\lambda\leq 1

d_{\lambda}=\myinf_{\rho<\lambda}d_{\rho}.

(UT)

t-Norm: For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,

0<\lambda\leq 1
and
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq 1
with

(1-\lambda^{\prime})*(1-\lambda)>1-\varepsilon,

and for
x,y,z\in X
we have

d_{\varepsilon}(x,z)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)+d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(y,z).

(UH)

Separation:

x\not=y\ \text{then}\ \exists\lambda,\ 0<\lambda\leq 1,\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)>0.

Similar to the local situation it is easily observed that
(UT)
implies
(UM)
. So
(UT)
can be seen as a
uniform strong mixed triangle condition.

Next we investigate the other direction, namely the question how to associate a probabilistic metric space to a particular uniform basis of a
\mathsf{UG}
-space.

4.7 Definition
.

Let
X
be a
\mathsf{UG}
-space having a uniform basis

\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}.

On
X
we define
\beta(x,y,\infty)=1
and further for
\gamma<\infty
:

(16)

\beta(x,y,\gamma)=\sup\{1-\lambda\ |\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\}=1-\myinf\{\lambda\ |\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\}.

4.8 Proposition
.

Given
X
having a uniform basis satisfying
(UD)
,
for
0\leq\gamma<\infty
we have:

\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma.

4.9 Theorem

(
Part II of Theorem 4.10).

If
X
is a
\mathsf{UG}
-space having a uniform basis

\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\},

satisfying
(US), (UD), (UT)
for some continuous t-norm
*
and
(UH)
, then there exists a probabilistic metric space
(X,\beta,*)
with underlying
\mathsf{UG}
-space
X
.

Proof.

Suppose

X
has a uniform basis

\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}

satisfying
(US), (UD), (UT)
and
(UH)
.

On
X
define
\beta(x,y,-)
as in (
16
).
Similar to the proof in
3.8
,

(X,\beta,*)
is a probabilistic metric space.

Finally we prove that the underlying
\mathsf{UG}
-space of
(X,\beta)
is
X
.

Let
\{e_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
be associated with
(X,\beta)
as in (
13
). Then from (
14
) and
4.8
for
\gamma<\infty

e_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma.

Hence we can conclude that
e_{\lambda}=d_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda
.
∎

From Theorems
4.6
(Part I of 4.10) and
4.9
(Part II of 4.10), we now have the following conclusion.

4.10 Theorem
.

The probabilistic metrizable
\mathsf{UG}
-spaces are exactly those
X
having a uniform basis

\Psi=\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\},

satisfying
(US)
,
(UD)
,
(UT)
for some continuous t-norm
*
and
(UH)
.

5.
An isomorphic description of the category of Probabilistic Metric Spaces

Several attempts have been made to describe the category of probabilistic metric spaces with non-expansive maps isomorphically in terms of collections of symmetric generalised metrics with levelwise non-expansive maps. However in order to obtain an isomorphism based on generalised metrics, one had to make restrictions. In
[
15
]
the continuous t-norm is restricted to M, taking
a*b=\mymin(a,b)
. Another direction of research is to express weak probabilistic metric spaces in terms of symmetric generalised metrics, which is realised by weakening the axiom (P5) and replacing it by the axiom
[
14
]

\alpha(x,y,\gamma)=1\ \text{and}\ \alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})=1\Rightarrow\alpha(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})=1.

or by imposing

\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>t\ \text{ and }\ \alpha(y,z,\gamma^{\prime})>t\Rightarrow\alpha(x,z,\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})>t

for
0\leq t<1.

In this section, as in the previous ones, we stay with the axiom (P5) formulated for an arbitrary continuous t-norm as in (
1
) and we work with collections of distances where the triangle condition of the members of the collection is replaced by a mixed triangle condition of the
collection itself.

Note that in what follows there is a fundamental difference compared to the previous section. In Definition
5.1
the objects will be sets endowed with a collection of distances. Different collections will stand for different objects. However in section 4 a collection of distances is a basis for a uniform system. This implies that different collections of distances may generate the same
\mathsf{UG}
-object. A second fundamental difference lies in the formulation of the morphisms in (
17
), where they are levelwise non-expansive. However in section 4 morphisms are defined as uniform contractions (
12
), where in the domain of the functions the whole uniform system is involved.

5.1 Definition
.

Let
\mathfrak{Z}
be the category with objects,
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}),
sets endowed with a collection of distances numbered by
0<\lambda\leq 1,
where

\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}

satisfies the conditions:
(US)
,
(UD)
,
(UT)
for some continuous t-norm
*
and
(UH)
from
4.6
.

A morphism
f:(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})\rightarrow(Y,\{d^{\prime}_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
is
levelwise non-expansive
if

(17)

\forall\lambda,\ d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime}),\ \text{whenever}\ x,x^{\prime}\in X.

Let
(X,\alpha,*)
be a probabilistic metric space. As in
[
14
]

(18)

(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})

is associated with
(X,\alpha,*)
by

d_{\lambda}(x,y)=\myinf\{0\leq\gamma<\infty\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}.

This is the same formula as in (
13
) and again we have the equivalences of (
14
), namely
for
0\leq\gamma<\infty
and for
0<\lambda\leq 1

(19)

d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

5.2 Proposition
.

This defines a concrete functor
\Delta:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}
which maps an object
(X,\alpha,*)
to
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}).

Proof.

That
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
belongs to
\mathfrak{Z}
goes exactly is the same way as the proof of
(US), (UD), (UT)
with respect to the same continuous t-norm
*
and
(UH)
.

Next suppose
f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})
is a non-expansive map.
First observe that in case
d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))=\infty
then for every
\gamma<\infty,

\alpha(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),\gamma)\not>1-\lambda.

This implies that for every
\gamma<\infty,\ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},\gamma)\not>1-\lambda.
Hence we can conclude that
d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})=\infty.

In the finite case, applying (
19
) we obtain the following equivalences:

\displaystyle f:(X,\alpha)\rightarrow(Y,\alpha^{\prime})\ \text{is non-expansive}\

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)\leq\alpha^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\forall\lambda:\ \ \alpha(x,x^{\prime},t)>1-\lambda\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),t)>1-\lambda

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall t,\forall\lambda:\ d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})<t\Rightarrow d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))<t

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\lambda,\ d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime}).

It follows that
\Delta f:(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})\rightarrow(Y,\{d^{\prime}_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
is levelwise non-expansive.
∎

Next we investigate the other direction.
Let
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
be an object of
\mathfrak{Z}
and let
(X,\beta,*)
be defined as in
4.7
:

\beta(x,y,\infty)=1
and further for
\gamma<\infty
:

(20)

\beta(x,y,\gamma)=\sup\{1-\lambda\ |\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\}=1-\myinf\{\lambda\ |\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\}.

Again for
\gamma<\infty
we have

(21)

\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma.

As we know from the previous section, due to the properties of
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
listed above,
(X,\beta,*)
defines a probabilistic metric space.

5.3 Proposition
.

This defines a concrete functor
\Phi:\mathfrak{Z}\rightarrow\mathsf{ProbMet}
which maps an object
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
to
(X,\beta,*)
.

Proof.

That this is indeed a functor goes as follows.

Let
f:(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})\rightarrow(X,\{d^{\prime}_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
be levelwise non-expansive.
First observe that in case
\gamma=\infty,

\beta(x,x^{\prime},\infty)=\beta^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),\infty)=1
by definition.
In the finite case, applying (
21
) we obtain the following equivalences:

\displaystyle f:(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\lambda\})\rightarrow(X,\{d^{\prime}_{\lambda}\ |\lambda\})\ \text{levelw. n-exp.}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\lambda,\ d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))\leq d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\gamma,\forall\lambda:\ d_{\lambda}(x,x^{\prime})<\gamma\Rightarrow

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\lambda}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}))<\gamma

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\forall\gamma,\forall\lambda:\ \ \beta(x,x^{\prime},\gamma)>1-\lambda\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),\gamma)>1-\lambda)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\beta(x,x^{\prime},-)\leq\beta^{\prime}(f(x),f(x^{\prime}),-)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\Phi(f):(X,\beta)\rightarrow(Y,\beta^{\prime})\ \text{n-exp.}

∎

5.4 Theorem
.

\Delta:\mathsf{ProbMet}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

We prove that
\Phi\circ\Delta=\mathsf{id}_{\mathsf{ProbMet}}
and
\Delta\circ\Phi=\mathsf{id}_{\mathfrak{Z}}.

Given a probabilistic metric space
(X,\alpha,*),
applying
\Delta
we obtain an object
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\}
in
\mathfrak{Z}
. Next applying
\Phi
we obtain a probabilistic metric space
(X,\beta,*),
as described in
5.2
and
5.3
. Applying (
19
) and (
21
) for
\gamma<\infty
we get

\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

Since
\beta(x,y,\infty)=\alpha(x,y,\infty)=1
, we have
\alpha=\beta.

For the other identity, we start with an object
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
of
\mathfrak{Z}
and apply
\Phi
. Let
(X,\beta,*)
be the probabilistic metric space obtained. Applying
\Delta
we get the object
(X,\{e_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
from
\mathfrak{Z}.

Again applying (
19
) and (
21
), for
\gamma<\infty
we get

e_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma\Leftrightarrow\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\Leftrightarrow d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\gamma,

which implies
e_{\lambda}=d_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda.

∎

In some papers on the subject the distances involved are assumed to be finitely valued. This condition can be obtained in our setting as well.

5.5 Definition
.

Define
\mathsf{ProbMet}_{\text{lim}}
the concrete full subcategory of
\mathsf{ProbMet}
consisting of those objects
(X,\alpha,*)
satisfying

(22)

\forall x,y\in X,\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(x,y,\gamma)=1.

Define
\mathfrak{Z}_{\text{fin}}
the full subcategory of
\mathfrak{Z}
consisting of those
(X,\{d_{\lambda}\ |\ 0<\lambda\leq 1\})
satisfying

(23)

\forall x,y\in X,\ \forall\ 0<\lambda\leq 1,\ d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\infty.

5.6 Proposition
.

The functors
\Delta
and
\Phi
restrict to
\Delta:\mathsf{ProbMet}_{\text{lim}}\rightarrow\mathfrak{Z}_{\text{fin}}
and
\Phi:\mathfrak{Z}_{\text{fin}}\rightarrow\mathsf{ProbMet}_{\text{lim}}
and hence the categories

\mathsf{ProbMet}_{\text{lim}}
and
\mathfrak{Z}_{\text{fin}}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in X
and suppose
\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\alpha(x,y,\gamma)=1
. This implies that for every
0<\lambda\leq 1,
the set

\{0\leq\gamma<\infty\ |\ \alpha(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda\}

is non-empty and hence by (
18
)
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\infty.

Let
x,y\in X
and suppose that for every
0<\lambda\leq 1
the distance
d_{\lambda}(x,y)<\infty.
Let
\lambda
be arbitrary, take
\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
and then
\gamma
satisfying

d_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,y)<\gamma.
This implies

\myinf\{\rho\ |\ d_{\rho}(x,y)<\gamma\}\leq\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda.

Applying (
20
) we get
\beta(x,y,\gamma)>1-\lambda.

∎

Sharp asymptotic stability of the incompressible porous media equation
Roberta Bianchini, Min Jun Jo, Jaemin Park, Shan Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the asymptotic stability of the incompressible porous media (IPM) equation near a stable stratified density, for initial perturbations in the Sobolev space H k H^{k} with any 2 < k ∈ ℝ 2<k\in\mathbb{R} . While it is known that such a steady state is unstable in H 2 H^{2} , our result establishes a sharp stability threshold in higher-order Sobolev spaces. The key ingredients of our proof are twofold. First, we extract long-time convergence from the decay of a potential energy functional—despite its non-coercive nature—thereby revealing a variational structure underlying the dynamics. Second, we derive refined commutator estimates to control the evolution of higher Sobolev norms throughout the full range of k > 2 k>2 .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Incompressible porous media equation

In this paper, we investigate the asymptotic stability of the incompressible porous media (IPM) equation in the periodic strip
\Omega:=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+u\cdot\nabla{\rho}=0&\text{ in $\Omega$,}\\
\rho(0,x):=\rho_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where the velocity
u
is uniquely determined by

(1.2)

\displaystyle u=-\nabla p-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\rho\end{pmatrix},\quad\nabla\cdot u=0\quad\text{ in $\Omega$ and }\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|u(x)|=0.

As usual, we denote the stream function by
\Psi
such that
u=\nabla^{\perp}\Psi
. From the relation between the velocity and the advected scalar
\rho
in (
1.2
), it follows that
\Psi
is uniquely determined (up to a constant) as a solution to the following equation:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta\Psi=\partial_{1}\rho,&\text{ in $\Omega$},\\
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\Psi(x)=0.\end{cases}

Let
\rho_{s}(x)
be a stratified density, that is,
\rho_{s}(x)=\rho_{s}(x_{2})
is independent of the variable
x_{1}
. Formally speaking, such a density function induces zero velocity field, implying that it is a steady solution to the IPM equation. The goal of this paper is to study the asymptotic stability around stable steady states satisfying the following properties:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\inf_{x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}\left(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x_{2})\right)>0,\quad\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{k+1}(\Omega)}<\infty,\text{ for some $k>2$.}

Clearly a typical example can be
\rho_{s}(x_{2})=-x_{2}
.

In recent years, there has been active research on the well-posedness of the IPM equation. In
[
8
]
, it was proved that the IPM equation is locally well-posed when the density belongs to
C^{1,\alpha}
for
\alpha>0
or to
H^{k}
for
k>2
. This result relies on the fact that the velocity field is Lipschitz, ensuring the associated transport equation is well-defined. However, the question of global well-posedness for general initial data versus the formation of finite-time singularities remains open.

In one direction, the authors in
[
15
]
established long-time growth of Sobolev norms, showing that for fairly general initial data, the norms may grow unbounded as time tends to infinity—assuming a global-in-time solution exists, even when the initial data is smooth. In particular, one of their results shows that even small perturbations of a stable steady state may lead to instability if the perturbation lies in
H^{2-\epsilon}
for any
\epsilon>0
. The space
H^{2}
is critical for the IPM equation, and strong instability and ill-posedness were established by the first author of the present paper and her collaborators in
[
2
]
. Remarkably, Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa
[
9
]
constructed a finite-time singularity for the IPM equation starting from smooth initial data and smooth forcing. We also mention the work
[
14
]
, in which the authors derived a 1D model of the IPM equation and proved finite-time singularity formation in the absence of forcing.

On the other hand, it has been shown that such (negative) behaviors do not occur near stable steady states when perturbations are taken in strong Sobolev spaces. To the best of our knowledge, this was first established by Elgindi
[
11
]
in both
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, where he showed that if the initial perturbation is in
H^{20}
, the solutions eventually converge to a stratified steady state in the long-time limit. A related question was studied in
[
6
]
, where the authors posed the equation in a periodic channel and proved asymptotic stability of a stable steady state in
H^{10}
. Further progress in this direction was made in
[
3
,
12
]
, where the required regularity for stability was substantially reduced to
H^{3+}
. The current best result appears in
[
17
]
, where asymptotic stability is established in
H^{3}
. The goal of the present paper is to push these results to their limit, establishing asymptotic stability in the optimal regularity class.

Before presenting our main result, we briefly introduce a closely related model, known as the AHT model
[
1
,
5
]
. Given a bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a prescribed deformation
y_{0}:D\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the system is given by:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}y_{t}+v\cdot\nabla y=0,\\
Kv=-\nabla p-y,\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
K
is a dissipative operator, such as
K=I
or
K=-\Delta
.
This model was introduced to study the unique rearrangement
y^{*}
with
a
convex potential, as it arises in Brenier’s polar factorization
[
4
]
. In particular,
[
16
]
proved exponential convergence to the minimizer when the initial data is close to a stable one, i.e., when
Dy_{0}(x)
is strictly positive definite for every
x\in D
.
A connection between the IPM and the AHT model can be observed by choosing
K=I
and initial data of the form:

y_{0}=-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\rho_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

With this choice, the vector transport system (
1.5
) becomes equivalent to the IPM equation. The key difference from the setting in
[
16
]
is that, in this case, the IPM equation (
1.1
) corresponds to a degenerate scenario, since

Dy_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
-\partial_{1}\rho_{0}&-\partial_{2}\rho_{0}\end{pmatrix}

is not strictly positive definite. While exponential convergence cannot be expected in this case, we will derive an algebraic convergence rate, which is expected to be sharp at least at the linear level.

1.2.
Main theorem

Before stating our theorem, we consider potential candidates for a long-term limit of the solution, following the idea presented in
[
10
]
. Let us consider
f\in C^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\inf(-\partial_{2}f)>0
. Since
f
is monotonically decreasing in the vertical variable
x_{2}
, the implicit function theorem tells us that for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we can find a unique level set
\left\{x\in\Omega:f(x)=s\right\}
, which can be parametrized by the horizontal variable
x_{1}
. More precisely, we can find
\phi:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))=s,\text{ for all $x_{1}\in\mathbb{T}$.}

Let us define
\phi_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
h:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(1.6)

\phi_{0}(s):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\phi(x_{1},s)dx_{1},\quad h(x_{1},s):=\phi(x_{1},s)-\phi_{0}(s).

By definition,
h
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{T}}h(x_{1},s)dx_{1}=0.

Using (
1.6
), we can define the
measure-preserving stratification

f^{*}
of
f
as

(1.7)

\displaystyle f^{*}(x_{2}):=\left(\phi_{0}\right)^{-1}(x_{2}).

An illustration of such decomposition can be seen in Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
An illustration of the level sets of
f
. Each level set
f=s
is uniquely decomposed into
\phi_{0}
and
h
so that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(x_{1},s)dx_{1}=0
for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now, let us consider
\rho(t)
as a solution to the IPM equation, where
\rho_{0}
is sufficiently close to
\rho_{s}
in a topology stronger than
C^{1}
, and where
\rho_{s}
satisfies (
1.4
). Then, the measure-preserving stratification of
\rho_{0}
is well-defined. Furthermore, if
\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)
exists and the limit can be obtained in the
C^{1}
-norm, then
\rho_{0}^{*}
is the only possible stratified density under a transport equation governed by an incompressible vector field. Noting that
H^{2}
is the borderline case that fails to embed into
C^{1}
in a two-dimensional domain, we achieve its optimal asymptotic stability in our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\rho_{s}
be a stratified density satisfying (
1.4
). Then
\rho_{s}
is asymptotically stable for the IPM equation, (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), in
H^{k}(\Omega)
for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
such that
k>2
.

More precisely, setting

(1.8)

\displaystyle\gamma:=\min\left\{k-2,\ \inf_{x\in\Omega}\left(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x_{2})\right)\text{ and }\left(\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{k+1}}\right)^{-1}\right\}>0,

there exist
\varepsilon,\ C>0
which depends only on
\gamma
such that if
\rVert\rho_{0}-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq\varepsilon,
then there exists a unique solution
\rho(t)
satisfying
\rho-\rho_{s}\in C([0,\infty);H^{k})
to the IPM equation, and the solution
\rho
satisfies

\rVert\rho(t)-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq C\varepsilon,\text{ for all $t>0$.}

Furthermore, the solution
\rho
converges to
\rho^{*}_{0}
as

(1.9)

\displaystyle\rVert\rho(t)-\rho_{0}^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon t^{-k/2},\text{ for all $t>0$,}

where
\rho_{0}^{*}
is the measure-preserving stratification of the initial density
\rho_{0}
defined in (
1.7
).

Remark 1.2
.

The convergence rate in (
1.9
) is sharp in the sense that for any
\varepsilon>0
, one can construct a solution
\rho^{L}(t)
to the linearized equation around
\rho_{s}
such that

\rVert\rho^{L}(t)-\rho_{s}\rVert_{L^{2}}\geq Ct^{-k/2-\varepsilon}\text{ for all $t>1$}.

A short proof is given in Appendix
A
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
establishes a sharp regularity result in the sense that such
\rho_{s}
is known to be unstable in
H^{2}
(see
[
15
]
and
[
2
]
, where long-time instability in
H^{2-\epsilon}
and ill-posedness in
H^{2}
have been proved respectively). Previously, the best known stability result
[
17
]
was obtained in
H^{3}
on the horizontally periodic strip, improving the regularity requirement in the preceding results
[
3
,
6
,
11
,
12
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is very likely that our proof could be adapted to establish sharp asymptotic stability of the two-dimensional Boussinesq equations with velocity damping around any equilibrium of the form
(\rho_{s},u_{s})=(\rho_{s}(x_{2}),0)
, where
\rho_{s}(x_{2})
is a stably stratified density profile. Previous results in this direction include:
[
3
]
, which treats the equations posed on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with regularity requirement
H^{s}
with
s>3
(see also
[
18
]
for results under higher regularity assumptions), and
[
7
]
, which considers the case of a finite periodic channel with regularity requirement
H^{k}
with
k>17
.

1.3.
Brief overview of the proof

Previous results establishing asymptotic stability for the IPM equation (e.g.,
[
3
,
6
,
11
,
12
]
) rely on Fourier-analytic methods, which explicitly capture the underlying stability mechanism (see Subsubsection
1.3.1
). However, such an approach faces several challenges. In particular, deriving key commutator estimates required for achieving optimal regularity of the initial perturbation can be technically difficult, primarily due to potential derivative losses that might occur in rough control of nonlinear terms. Moreover, within the Fourier-analytic framework, the precise long-time asymptotic behavior of the density—specifically, its quantitative convergence to the measure-preserving stratification of the initial data, as characterized in this paper—remains elusive.

The main novelty of our proof of Theorem
1.1
lies in the use of a potential energy functional. This reveals a variational structure of the problem more transparently, thereby providing complete description of the long-time limit of the solution. Another key ingredient toward establishing the optimal regularity requirement on the initial data is the incorporation of additional decay estimates for higher Sobolev norms. A similar strategy was previously adopted in
[
17
]
where an improved regularity requirement,
k\geq 3
, was successfully obtained. In the present work, we build further upon the described strategy to establish asymptotic stability with the optimal regularity threshold. The optimality is accomplished here by sharply exploiting the gap between the subcritical exponent
k>2
and the critical exponent
k=2
to control the pivotal quantity
\|\nabla u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}
in the aforementioned step of incorporating additional decay estimates. For the reader’s convenience, we briefly outline the structure of our proof in what follows.

1.3.1.
Decay rates in the linearized equation

We begin by investigating the decay rates of the velocity field in the linearized IPM equation. For simplicity, we take a look at the linearized system around the simplest stable stratified steady state
\rho_{s}(x):=-x_{2}
, and consider initial data with zero horizontal average:

\begin{cases}\Theta_{t}=U_{2},\\
U=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{1}\Theta,\\
\Theta(0,x)=\Theta_{0}(x)\quad\text{with }\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Theta_{0}(x_{1},x_{2})\,dx_{1}=0.\end{cases}

Using the identity
U_{2}=(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{11}\Theta
, we can explicitly solve the linearized equation via Fourier transform (see (
2.1
) for notation):

\Theta(t,x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{\Theta_{0}}_{n}(\xi)e^{-t\frac{n^{2}}{n^{2}+\xi^{2}}}e^{i(nx_{1}+\xi x_{2})}\,d\xi.

For
k>2
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,k]
with
s_{1}+s_{2}\leq k
, a direct computation (see (
2.2
)) yields:

\||D_{1}|^{s_{1}}\Theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{2}}}^{2}=\sum_{n\neq 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\Theta_{0}}_{n}(\xi)|^{2}(n^{2}+\xi^{2})^{k}W(t,n,k)\,d\xi,

where

W(t,n,k):=\frac{n^{2s_{1}}}{(n^{2}+\xi^{2})^{k-s_{2}}}e^{-\frac{2tn^{2}}{n^{2}+\xi^{2}}}.

Noting that
|n|\geq 1
and that
\sup_{x>0}x^{\alpha}e^{-x}<C_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
, we obtain the bound:

W(t,n,k)\leq C_{k}t^{-(k-s_{2})}.

Consequently,

\||D_{1}|^{s_{1}}\Theta(t)\|_{H^{s_{2}}}^{2}\leq C(k,\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{k}})\,t^{-(k-s_{2})}.

Recalling
U=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{1}\Theta
, this implies

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-k},\quad\|U(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-(k-1)},\quad\|U_{2}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq Ct^{-k},

for some constant
C=C(k,\|\Theta_{0}\|_{H^{k}})>0
.

Whether such decay rates persist at the nonlinear level is far from trivial. In our analysis, we derive an
averaged
version of these estimates for nonlinear solutions (see Proposition
5.3
):

(1.11)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{s}\|_{H^{k}}^{2}O(t^{-(k-1)}),\quad\|u_{2}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}=\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{s}\|_{H^{k}}^{2}O(t^{-k}),

where we use the following (slightly non-standard) time-averaged notation:

(1.12)

\displaystyle f(t)=O(t^{-\alpha})\quad\text{if}\quad\frac{1}{t/2}\int_{t/2}^{t}f(s)\,ds\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{\alpha}}\quad\text{for }t\gg 1\text{ and some }C,\alpha>0.

Moreover, the decay rate of
\Theta
in (
1.10
) coincides with the convergence rate in (
1.9
).

1.3.2.
Nonlinear analysis

The asymptotic stability analysis for a solution
\rho(t)
to the (nonlinear) IPM equation is carried out under the following a priori assumption:

(1.13)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)-\rho_{s}\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\,dt<M\varepsilon^{2}\ll 1,\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T],\text{ for some }T,M>0\text{ and }0<\varepsilon\ll 1,

where the initial perturbation satisfies

\|\rho_{0}-\rho_{s}\|_{H^{k}}\leq\varepsilon.

We aim to prove the following two statements in parallel:

(A)

Convergence to the energy minimizer
\rho_{0}^{*}
:

\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C_{k}\varepsilon^{2}}{t^{k}},\quad\text{as long as \eqref{assumption_main} holds.}

(B)

Persistence of the bound in (
1.13
)
for all
T>0
.

Verifying (A) and (B) together establishes Theorem
1.1
. We now outline the proofs separately.

Proof scheme for (A).

As previously discussed, our proof is based on the decay of the potential energy functional. Specifically, we consider:

\mathcal{E}(\rho(t)):=\lim_{s\to\infty}\left(\int_{-s<\rho(t)<s}\rho(t,x)x_{2}\,dx-\int_{-s<\rho_{0}^{*}<s}\rho^{*}(x)x_{2}\,dx\right),

where
\rho_{0}^{*}
is the measure-preserving rearrangement of the initial data defined in (
1.7
). An important property of
\mathcal{E}
is that
\rho_{0}^{*}
uniquely minimizes
\mathcal{E}
among all measure-preserving rearrangements of
\rho_{0}
. Since
\rho(t)
solves a transport equation with a divergence-free velocity field,
\mathcal{E}
remains nonnegative. Under assumption (
1.13
), the energy functional satisfies both upper and lower bounds (see Proposition
2.6
):

(1.14)

\displaystyle\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{E}(\rho(t))\lesssim\|\rho(t)-\rho_{0}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\mathcal{E}(\rho(0))\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}.

As observed in
[
11
]
, the functional
\mathcal{E}
is monotone decreasing:

(1.15)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}(\rho(t))=-\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

To prove decay of
\mathcal{E}(t)
, one would ideally derive a coercive inequality like:

\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq C\mathcal{E}(\rho(t)),

which would yield exponential decay. However, such an inequality fails because the operator
\partial_{1}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}
is not positive definite: if it were true, then the energy bounds (
1.14
) and Darcy’s law

u=\nabla^{\perp}(-\Delta)\partial_{1}(\rho-\rho_{0}^{*})

would imply

\|\rho-\rho_{0}^{*}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\partial_{1}(-\Delta)^{-1/2}(\rho-\rho_{0}^{*})\|_{L^{2}},

which cannot be true in general. Instead, we exploit higher regularity and derive the following estimate:

(1.16)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq C\mathcal{E}(\rho(t))^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\left(\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}.

The derivation of this inequality will be given in Proposition
2.6
, based on a decomposition of the level sets of
\rho
, illustrated in Figure
1
.

Meanwhile, assumption (
1.13
) implies (using the notation (
1.12
)):

(1.17)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}=M\varepsilon^{2}O(t^{-1}).

Thus (formally),

\left(\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}=(M\varepsilon^{2})^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}O(t^{\frac{1}{k-1}}).

Combining with (
1.16
), we read the energy identity (
1.15
) as

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}\lesssim-\mathcal{E}^{\frac{k}{k-1}}(M\varepsilon^{2})^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}(1+t)^{\frac{1}{k-1}},

which implies

\mathcal{E}(\rho(t))\lesssim\frac{M\varepsilon^{2}}{(1+t)^{k}},\quad\text{as long as \eqref{assumption_main} holds}.

Combined with (
1.14
), this gives the desired convergence rate of
\rho(t)
to
\rho_{0}^{*}
. A full derivation is given in Proposition
5.2
.

Proof scheme for (B).

To complete the argument, we must verify that assumption (
1.13
) holds for all
T>0
. This is achieved by estimating the evolution of the high Sobolev norm of
\theta(t):=\rho(t)-\rho_{0}
, specifically
\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}
. Although standard techniques from transport equations apply, nontrivial technical difficulties arise due to the nonlinear structure of the IPM system. In particular, Proposition
4.2
provides the differential inequality:

\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\leq-C\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+C\left(\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

We emphasize that although the Lipschitz norm of the velocity vector naturally appears in such estimates for general transport equations, our bound involves only the Lipschitz norm of the vertical component
u_{2}
. Moreover, this estimate holds for the full range
k>2
.

Integrating the inequality in time yields

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\theta_{0}\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}+M\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt+\mathcal{E}(0)

(1.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}+M\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt,

where we have used estimates (
1.15
) and (
1.14
).

To validate this bound for arbitrary
T
, it suffices to show:

(1.19)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt\ll 1,

with a bound independent of
T
. Suppose
T^{*}
is the first time (
1.13
) fails. Then (
1.18
) gives:

M\varepsilon^{2}=\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\|\theta(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T^{*}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{2}\,dt\leq C\varepsilon^{2}+C\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt\right)M\varepsilon^{2}<M\varepsilon^{2},

a contradiction, provided
M
is chosen sufficiently large, independent of
T^{*},\varepsilon
.

To verify (
1.19
), we use Sobolev embedding and interpolation for fixed
0<\eta<k-2
:

\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\eta}\|u_{2}(t)\|_{H^{2+\eta}}\leq C_{k,\eta}\|u_{2}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{k-2-\eta}{k-2}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}^{\frac{\eta}{k-2}}.

From (
1.11
) and (
1.17
), we
deduce that

\|u_{2}(t)\|_{H^{2}}\leq\varepsilon O(t^{-k/2}),\quad\|u(t)\|_{H^{k}}\leq(M\varepsilon^{2})^{1/2}O(t^{-1/2}).

Combining these, we obtain:

\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq o(M\varepsilon^{2})O\left(t^{-\left(\frac{k(k-2-\eta)}{2(k-2)}+\frac{\eta}{2(k-2)}\right)}\right).

Since
k-2>0
, we may choose
\eta\in(0,k-2)
such that

\frac{k(k-2-\eta)}{2(k-2)}+\frac{\eta}{2(k-2)}>1.

For example, any
\eta
satisfying
0<\eta<\frac{(k-2)^{2}}{k-1}
suffices. For such
\eta
, we conclude:

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla u_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt=o(M\varepsilon^{2}),

thereby
verifying (
1.19
) and completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect preliminary tools of independent interest.

2.1.
Fractional Sobolev spaces

Here we recall the usual definition for the fractional Sobolev norms. For
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we define

\rVert f\rVert^{2}_{\dot{H}^{k}}:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(|n|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2k}\right)|\widehat{f}_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi,

where

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{n}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x_{1},x_{2})e^{-inx_{1}}e^{-ix_{2}\xi}dx_{1}dx_{2}.

We also denote

\rVert f\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}:=\rVert f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rVert f\rVert_{\dot{H}^{k}}^{2}.

For
k>0
, we will use the following fractional Laplacians:

(2.2)

\displaystyle|D_{1}|^{k}f(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|n|^{k}f_{n}(x_{2})e^{inx_{1}},\text{ where $f_{n}(x_{2}):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x_{1},x_{2})e^{-inx_{1}}dx_{1}$,}

\displaystyle|D_{2}|^{k}f(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi_{1}|^{k}\widehat{f}(x_{1},\xi)e^{i\xi x_{2}}d\xi,\text{ where $\widehat{f}(x_{1},\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x_{1},x_{2})e^{-i\xi x_{2}}dx_{2}$},

\displaystyle|D|^{k}f(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle:=(|D_{1}|^{k}+|D_{2}|^{k})f(x_{1},x_{2}).

2.2.
Time average decay

In this subsection, we recall useful inequalities arising in the subsequent energy estimates.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
17
, Lemma 2.1]

Let
\alpha>0,\ 1<n
. Let
a(t)
and
f(t)
be nonnegative functions on
[0,T]
such that

\frac{d}{dt}f(t)\leq-a(t)^{-\alpha}f(t)^{n},\quad f(0)=f_{0}.

Then,
f
satisfies

f(t)\leq_{\alpha,n}\frac{A^{\alpha/(n-1)}}{t^{(\alpha+1)/(n-1)}},\text{for all $t\in[0,T]$, where $A:=\int_{0}^{t}a(s)ds.$}

Lemma 2.2
.

[
17
, Lemma 2.2]

Let
n>0
and
f(t),g(t)
be nonnegative functions on
[0,T]
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}f(t)\leq-g(t)\text{ and }f(t)\leq\frac{C}{t^{n}},

for some
C>0
.
Then, it holds that

\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}g(s)ds\leq_{n}\frac{C}{t^{n+1}}\text{ for all $t\in[0,T]$.}

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T>0
and
n>1
. Let
f(t)
be a nonnegative function on
[0,T]
such that

\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}f(s)ds\leq\frac{A}{t^{n}},\text{ for some $A>0$, for all $t\in[0,T]$}.

Then, for
0<t<T
, we have

\int_{t}^{T}f(t)ds\leq C_{n}At^{1-n},

where
C_{n}>0
is a constant that depends only on
n
.

Proof.

Given
T>t>0
, we fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
so that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{T}{2^{N+1}}\leq t\leq\frac{T}{2^{N}}\leq 2t,

and we denote
T_{i}:=2^{-i}T
for
i=0,\ldots,N
. We estimate

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{T}f(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}(T_{i}-T_{i+1})\frac{1}{T_{i}-T_{i+1}}\int_{T_{i+1}}^{T_{i}}f(t)dt\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N}\frac{A(T_{i}-T_{i+1})}{T_{i}^{n}}\leq A\sum_{i=0}^{N}T_{i}^{1-n}

\displaystyle\leq AT^{1-n}\sum_{i=0}^{N}\left(2^{n-1}\right)^{i}=AT^{1-n}\frac{2^{(N+1)(n-1)}}{2^{n-1}-1}\leq C_{n}A\left(\frac{T}{2^{N+1}}\right)^{1-n}\leq C_{n}A\left(\frac{T}{2^{N}}\right)^{1-n}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}At^{1-n},

where the last inequality follows from (
2.4
). This gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H,f,g,h
be nonnegative functions satisfying for some
T>0
,

\frac{d}{dt}H(t)\leq-f(t)+h(t)H(t)+g(t),\text{ for $t\in[0,T]$}.

Suppose there exist
A,n>0
such that

\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}H(s)ds\leq\frac{A}{t^{n}},\quad\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}g(s)ds\leq\frac{A}{t^{n+1}},\text{ and }\int_{0}^{T}h(s)ds\leq 1,

for
t\in[0,T]
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, which does not depend on
A
nor
T
, such that

\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}f(s)ds\leq C\frac{A}{t^{n+1}}\text{ for $t\in[0,T]$}.

Proof.

Let
L(t):=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}h(s)ds}
. Since
\int_{0}^{T}h(s)ds\leq 1
, we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle e^{-1}\leq L(t)\leq 1.

Furthermore, it holds that
\frac{d}{dt}\left(L(t)H(t)\right)\leq-f(t)L(t)+g(t)L(t)
, yielding

L(t)H(t)-L(s)H(s)\leq-\int_{s}^{t}f(u)L(u)du+\int_{s}^{t}g(u)L(u)du.

Since
H,L\geq 0
, we have

\int_{s}^{t}f(u)L(u)du\leq L(s)H(s)+\int_{s}^{t}L(u)g(u)du.

Integrating both sides over
s\in[t/4,t]
, we get

\int_{t/4}^{t}f(u)L(u)\left(u-\frac{t}{4}\right)du\leq\int_{t/4}^{t}L(s)H(s)ds+\int_{t/4}^{t}L(u)g(u)\left(u-\frac{t}{4}\right)du

From (
2.5
), we obtain

{\frac{t}{4e}\int_{t/2}^{t}f(s)ds\leq\int_{t/4}^{t}f(u)L(u)\left(u-\frac{t}{4}\right)du}\leq\int_{t/4}^{t}H(s)ds+t\int_{t/4}^{t}g(s)ds,

implying that

\frac{2}{t}\int_{t/2}^{t}f(s)ds\leq\frac{C}{t^{2}}\int_{t/4}^{t}H(s)ds+\frac{C}{t}\int_{t/4}^{t}g(s)ds.

Using the estimates for
H
and
g
in the assumption of the lemma, we obtain the desired result.
∎

2.3.
Stratification and potential energy

In this subsection, we collect some basic properties of functions that are close to
\rho_{s}
in the Sobolev space
H^{k}
, for
k>2
. Since the domain
\Omega
is two-dimensional, the Sobolev embedding
H^{k}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{1}(\Omega)
holds. Consequently, we expect that certain quantitative features of
\rho_{s}
- being strictly decreasing - also hold for such nearby functions.

Firstly, the inverse function theorem, combined with the assumption that
\inf_{x\in\Omega}\left(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x_{2})\right)>0

guarantees the existence of the inverse of
\rho_{s}
, denoted by

\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s):=\rho_{s}^{-1}(s)\text{ for $s\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Moreover, let us recall from the regularity assumption in (
1.4
) that
\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{k+1}}<\infty
. Hence one can straightforwardly deduce, using the inverse function theorem, that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\rVert\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}\rVert_{C^{3}}+\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{3}}\leq\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{k+1}}\leq C(\gamma),

where
\gamma
is as in (
1.8
).

In the rest of this section, we will consider functions
f\in H^{k}(\Omega)
that are close to
\rho_{s}
, namely

(2.7)

\displaystyle\rVert f-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq\delta\text{ for sufficiently small $\delta>0$}.

For a technical reason, to study the potential energy for such functions, we will also impose a decay assumption as well

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{|x_{2}|\to\infty}\sup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{T}}|f(x_{1},x_{2})-\rho_{s}(x_{2})|\sqrt{|x_{2}|}=0,

which will turn out to be true for sufficiently regular solutions to the IPM equation.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in H^{k}(\Omega)
and
\gamma
be as in (
1.8
). Then there exists
\delta=\delta(\gamma)>0
, such that if (
2.7
) holds, then there exist
\phi_{0}:\mathbb{R}\mapsto\mathbb{R}
and
h:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}h(x_{1},s)dx_{1}

\displaystyle=0\text{ and }f(x_{1},\phi_{0}(s)+h(x_{1},s))=s,\text{ for $(x_{1},s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}$.}

Furthermore, the following estimates hold:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\rVert\phi_{0}-\phi_{\rho_{s}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert\partial_{s}(\phi_{0}-\phi_{\rho_{s}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert h\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}+\rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}}+\rVert\partial_{s}h\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C(\gamma)\rVert f-{\rho}_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}.

In addition, if (
2.8
) holds, then

(2.10)

\displaystyle\lim_{|s|\to\infty}\sup_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{T}}\sqrt{|s|}|h(x_{1},s)|=0.

Proof.

We first notice that
x_{2}\to f(x_{1},x_{2})
is invertible for each
x_{1}\in\mathbb{T}
. Indeed, using
H^{k}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow C^{1}(\Omega)
for
k>2
, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{2}f(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}{\rho}_{s}(x_{2})+(\partial_{2}f(x_{1},x_{2})-\partial_{2}{\rho}_{s}(x_{2}))\leq-\gamma+C\rVert f-{\rho}_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq-\gamma+C\delta<-\gamma/2,

for sufficiently small
\delta>0
.
Hence,
x_{2}\mapsto f(x_{1},x_{2})
is strictly decreasing. The implicit function theorem tells us that there is a parametrization of each level curve,
\left\{f=s\right\}
, which we will denote by
\phi(\cdot,s)
, that is,

(2.12)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))=s\text{ for each $(x_{1},s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}$.}

Let us decompose it as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\phi(x_{1},s)=\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)+g(x_{1},s),\text{ where }g(x_{1},s):=\phi(x_{1},s)-\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s),

where
\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s):=\rho_{s}^{-1}(s)
.
We claim the following estimates:

(2.14)

\displaystyle\rVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rVert f-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\rVert\nabla g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rVert f-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rVert f-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\rVert\partial_{s}g\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\rVert f-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}.

For now, let us assume the validity of the above estimates. Towards the proof of the lemma, we define
\phi_{0}
and
h
as follows:

\phi_{0}(s):=\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(y,s)dx_{1},\quad h(x_{1},s):=g(x_{1},s)-\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(y,s)dy.

By its definition, we have
\int_{\mathbb{T}}h(x_{1},s)dx_{1}=0
for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Also, (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) tell us that

f(x_{1},\phi_{0}(s)+h(x_{1},s))=f(x_{1},\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)+g(x_{1},s))=f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))=s\text{ for all $s\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Collecting the estimates for
g
in (
2.14
)-(
2.17

\displaystyle\rVert h\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}},\ \rVert\partial_{s}h\rVert_{L^{2}},\ \rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}},\ \rVert\partial_{s}(\phi_{0}-\phi_{\rho_{s}})\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\ \rVert\phi_{0}-\phi_{\rho_{s}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\rVert f-{\rho}_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}},

which proves (
2.9

Now, let us derive the estimates (
2.14
)-(
2.17

Estimate for

Writing

\displaystyle 0=\rho_{s}(\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s))-f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))=\left(\rho_{s}(\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s))-\rho_{s}(\phi(x_{1},s))\right)+\left(\rho_{s}(\phi(x_{1},s))-f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))\right),

(2.37)

\displaystyle|\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)|\leq|\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)-\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)|+|\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)|\leq_{C}\delta+\rVert\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}\rVert_{C^{2}}<C,

where the last inequality is due to (
2.6
). Therefore, the inverse function theorem guarantees that there exists
f^{*}
such that

f^{*}(x_{2}):=\left(\phi_{0}\right)^{-1}(x_{2}).

Moreover, since for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
0=f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))-\rho_{s}(\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s))
, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{2}f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s))\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{2}f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(\phi_{0}(s))\right)\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)+\left(\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(\phi_{0}(s))-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s))\right)\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)

\displaystyle\ +\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s))\left(\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)-\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}(s)\right).

Using (
1.8
) and (
2.34
), this computation gives us

\displaystyle\left|\left(\partial_{2}f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(\phi_{0}(s))\right)\partial_{s}\phi_{0}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{1}}\rVert\phi_{0}-\phi_{\rho_{s}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\partial_{s}\phi_{0}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\ +\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\partial_{s}\phi_{0}-\partial_{s}\phi_{\rho_{s}}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta,

where the last inequality follows from (
2.6
), (
2.34
) and (
2.36
). Thus, using (
2.35
), we get

(2.38)

\displaystyle\rVert\partial_{2}f^{*}-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\delta.

Hence, for sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

(2.39)

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\Omega}-\partial_{2}f^{*}(x)>C>0,\quad\rVert\partial_{2}f^{*}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<C.

Using the change of variables,
x_{2}\to\phi(x_{1},s)
, we have

(2.40)

\displaystyle\rVert f-f^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))-f^{*}(\phi(x_{1},s))|^{2}|\partial_{s}\phi(x_{1},s)|dx_{1}ds.

Thanks to (
2.33
), we have

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))

\displaystyle-f^{*}(\phi(x_{1},s))

\displaystyle=s-f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))+\left(f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))-f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s)+h(x_{1},s))\right)

\displaystyle=f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s))-f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s)+h(x_{1},s))

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{h(x_{1},s)}\partial_{2}f^{*}(\phi_{0}(s)+z)dz.

Thus, using (
2.39
) and (
2.36
), we get

|h(x_{1},s)|\leq_{C}|f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))-f^{*}(\phi(x_{1},s))|\sqrt{|\partial_{s}\phi(x_{1},s)|}\leq_{C}|h(x_{1},s)|.

Plugging this into (
2.40
), we obtain

(2.41)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}\leq\rVert f-f^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times[0,1])}.

On the other hand, differentiating (
2.33
) in
x_{1}
gives us

0=\partial_{1}(f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s)))=\partial_{1}f(x_{1},\phi(x,s))+\partial_{2}f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))\partial_{1}h(x_{1},s).

Similarly, diffierentiating the second identity in (
2.33
) yields
1=\partial_{2}f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))\partial_{s}\phi(s)
, thus

\partial_{1}f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))=-\frac{\partial_{1}h(x_{1},s)}{\partial_{s}\phi(x_{1},s)}.

Then, using the change of variables
x_{2}\to\phi(x_{1},s)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\rVert\partial_{1}f\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{1}f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))|^{2}|\partial_{s}\phi(x_{1},s)|dx_{1}ds

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\partial_{1}h(x_{1},s)|^{2}}{|\partial_{s}\phi(x_{1},s)|}dx_{1}ds

\displaystyle\geq C\rVert\partial_{1}h\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C\rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

where the second inequality is due to (
2.36
) and the last inequality follows from the zero-average in
x_{1}
of
h
in (
2.33
) and the Poincaré inequality. Therefore, combining this with (
2.41
), we conclude that (
2.32
) holds.

Proof of (
2.30
).
Now, let us assume (
2.8
). Note that
E_{s}(f)
can be written as

\displaystyle E_{s}(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\phi(x_{1},s)}^{\phi(x_{1},-s)}f(x_{1},x_{2})x_{2}dx_{2}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-s}^{s}f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},t))\phi(x_{1},t)\partial_{t}\phi(x_{1},t)dtdx_{1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-s}^{s}t\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left(\phi(x_{1},t)^{2}\right)dtdx_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}s\left(\phi(x_{1},s)^{2}+\phi(x_{1},-s)^{2}\right)dx_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-s}^{s}|\phi(x_{1},t)|^{2}dtdx_{1}.

The same computations gives us

E_{s}(f^{*})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}s\left(\phi_{0}(s)^{2}+\phi_{0}(-s)^{2}\right)dx_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-s}^{s}|\phi_{0}(t)|^{2}dtdx_{1}.

Hence, we get

(2.42)

\displaystyle E_{s}(f)-E_{s}(f^{*})=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}s\left(|h(x_{1},s)|^{2}+|h(x_{1},-s)|^{2}\right)dx_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-s}^{s}|h(x_{1},t)|^{2}dtdx_{1},

where we used
\int_{\mathbb{T}}h(x_{1},s)dx_{1}=0
for each
s
.
Since
h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})
, we have

\lim_{s\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{-s}^{s}|h(x_{1},t)|^{2}dtdx_{1}=\rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

while (
2.10
) of Lemma

yields

\lim_{s\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}s\left(|h(x_{1},s)|^{2}+|h(x_{1},-s)|^{2}\right)dx_{1}=0.

Therefore taking

in (

\mathcal{E}(f)=\frac{1}{2}\rVert h\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}.

Combining this with (
2.41
), we obtain (
2.30
).

Proof of (
2.31
).
Lastly, we use the usual Sobolev interpolation theorem to obtain

\rVert\partial_{1}f\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\rVert v\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C\rVert v\rVert_{L^{2}}^{\frac{k-1}{k}}\rVert v\rVert_{H^{k}}^{\frac{1}{k}}.

On the other hand, it follows from (

) and (

\mathcal{E}(f)\leq\rVert f-f^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\rVert\partial_{1}f\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining these two inequalities, we conclude (
2.31
).
∎

We prove stability of measure-preserving stratification.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f_{n},f
be functions such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\rVert f_{n}-f\rVert_{H^{k}}=0
. If (
2.7
) holds for
f_{n},f
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rVert f^{*}-f_{n}^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}}=0.

Proof.

Applying (
2.5
), we can find
\phi_{0},h_{0}
and
\phi_{0,n},\ h_{0,n}
such that

s=f_{n}(x_{1},\phi_{0,n}(s)+h_{n}(x_{1},s))=f(x_{1},\phi_{0}(s)+h(x_{1},s)),\text{ for $(x_{1},s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}$,}

satisfying

(2.43)

\displaystyle 0=\int_{\mathbb{T}}h(x_{1},s)dx_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}h_{n}(x_{1},s)dx_{1}.

As before, the assumption that (
2.7
) holds for
f_{n},f
implies

(2.44)

\displaystyle\rVert\partial_{2}f_{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\rVert\partial_{2}f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

Furthermore, the estimates (
2.35
) reads

(2.45)

\displaystyle|\partial_{s}\phi_{0}|,\ |\partial_{s}\phi_{0,s}|>C,\text{ therefore, }\rVert\partial_{2}f^{*}\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\rVert\partial_{2}f^{*}_{n}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<C.

Denoting
\phi:=\phi_{0}+h
and
\phi_{n}:=\phi_{0,n}+h_{n}
, we have

(2.46)

\displaystyle f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))-f(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s))=f(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s))-f_{n}(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s)).

The left-hand side can be written as

f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))-f(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s))=\int_{\phi_{n}(x_{1},s)}^{\phi(x_{1},s)}\partial_{2}f(x_{1},y)dy.

Again using that
\partial_{2}f
does not vanish, we have

(2.47)

\displaystyle|f(x_{1},\phi(x_{1},s))-f(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s))|\geq C|\phi(x_{1},s)-\phi_{n}(x_{1},s)|,\text{ for $(x_{1},s)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}$.}

On the other hand, the change of variables
s\to f_{n}(x_{1},x_{2})
for each fixed
x_{1}
gives us

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}|f(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s))-f_{n}(x_{1},\phi_{n}(x_{1},s))|^{2}dx_{1}ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}|f(x)-f_{n}(x)|^{2}|\partial_{2}f_{n}(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq C\rVert f-f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the last inequality follows from (
2.44
).
Therefore, (
2.46
) reads
\rVert\phi-\phi_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\rVert f-f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}
, in other words,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}|(\phi_{0}(s)-\phi_{0,n}(s))+(h(x_{1},s)-h_{n}(x_{1},s))|^{2}dx_{1}ds\leq C\rVert f-f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\implies\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{0}(s)-\phi_{0,n}(s)|^{2}dx_{1}ds+\rVert h-h_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\rVert f-f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

(2.48)

\displaystyle\implies\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{0}(s)-\phi_{0,n}(s)|^{2}dx_{1}ds\leq C\rVert f-f_{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the first implication is due to (
2.43
).

Now, we compute, using the change of variables
s\to f^{*}(y)
and recalling that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{0}(s)-\phi_{0,n}(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|y-\phi_{0,n}(f^{*}(y))-\phi_{0,n}(f_{n}^{*}(y))+\phi_{0,n}(f_{n}^{*}(y))|^{2}|\partial_{2}f^{*}(y)|dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\phi_{0,n}(f^{*}_{n}(y))-\phi_{0,n}(f^{*}(y))|^{2}|\partial_{2}f^{*}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\geq_{C}\inf_{s}|\partial_{s}\phi_{0,n}(s)|\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}|\partial_{2}f^{*}(y)|\rVert f_{n}^{*}-f^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C\rVert f_{n}^{*}-f^{*}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

where the last inequality follows from (
2.45
). Therefore, combining this with (

), we obtain the desired result.

3.
Local wellposedness for the IPM equation

Let
\rho(t)
be a smooth solution to the IPM equation and let
\rho_{s}
be a stratified density. Denoting

(3.1)

\displaystyle\theta(t):=\rho(t)-\rho_{s},

one can easily find that the IPM equation (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) reads

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\theta_{t}+u\cdot\nabla{\theta}=-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2},\quad\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad u=-\nabla p-\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\theta\end{pmatrix},&\text{ in $\Omega$,}\\
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0,\\
\theta(0,x):=\theta_{0}(x):=\rho_{0}(x)-\rho_{s}(x_{2}),\end{cases}

The stream function formulation (
1.3
) can be rewritten as

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Delta\Psi=-\partial_{1}\theta&\text{ in $\Omega$,}\\
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\Psi(x)=0.\end{cases}

Threfore, for
\theta\in H^{k}
, the velocity can be written as

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\nabla^{\perp}\Psi=\begin{pmatrix}-(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{12}\theta\\
(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{11}\theta\end{pmatrix}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k>2
and
\theta_{0}\in H^{k}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
T=T(\rVert\theta_{0}\rVert_{H^{k}})>0
such that the equation (
3.2
) admits a unique solution
\theta\in C([0,T);H^{k})
. Furthermore, if we assume in addition that
\theta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\rVert\theta x_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert u_{2}x_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}<\infty,\text{ for every $t\in[0,T)$.}

Proof.

The local well-posedness in
H^{k}
,
k>2
for the IPM equation has been established in
[
8
, Section 3]
, using the fact that
\rho(t)\in H^{k}
gives a Lipschitz continuous velocity field in two-dimensional domain. Even though we are dealing with
\rho
such that
\rho-\rho_{s}\in H^{k}
, the same proof can be easily adapted since the vector field depends only on
\partial_{1}\rho
and
\rho_{s}
is independent of
x_{1}
variable. Since a solution
\rho
such that
\rho-\rho_{s}\in H^{k}
is a classical solution, the local-wellposedness for the IPM equation ensures that (
3.2
) is also locally well-posed.

In the rest of the proof, we focus on deriving the decay rate (
3.5
).
Let
\theta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and let
\theta\in C([0,T);H^{k})
be a unique solution. Let
L>1
be a constant and
\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth nonnegative monotone increasing function such that

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ for $|x_{2}|\leq 1$}\\
x_{2}&\text{ for $2\leq|x_{2}|\leq L$}\\
L+1&\text{ for $|x_{2}|\geq L+2$}\end{cases}

with the following estimates:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\rVert\partial_{2}\varphi\rVert_{C^{2}}\leq 2.

Denoting
g(t,x):=\theta(t,x)\varphi(x),

we find that
g
solves

g_{t}+u\cdot\nabla g=u_{2}\frac{\partial_{2}\varphi}{\varphi}g-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\varphi

Let us derive crude energy estimates. In what follows, the implicit constant
C
will not depend on
L
. The
L^{2}
-norm can be estimated as

\frac{d}{dt}\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\frac{\partial_{2}\varphi}{\varphi}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert u_{2}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}\rVert u_{2}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}\right).

Using (
3.4
), we notice that
u_{2}\varphi
solves

\Delta(u_{2}\varphi)=-\partial_{11}(\theta\varphi)+\partial_{2}u_{2}\partial_{2}\varphi+u_{2}\partial_{22}\varphi

Hence, using (
3.6
) and that
\int_{\mathbb{T}}u_{2}(x)\varphi(x)dx_{1}=0
, we get

(3.7)

\displaystyle\rVert u_{2}\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\rVert\Delta(u_{2}\varphi)\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}+\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}\right).

Therefore, we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}+1\right)\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}

Now we estimate
\rVert\Delta g\rVert_{L^{2}}
as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\Delta g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int\left(\Delta(u\cdot\nabla g)-u\nabla\Delta g\right)\Delta gdx+\int\Delta\left(u_{2}\frac{\partial_{2}\varphi}{\varphi}g-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\varphi\right)\Delta gdx

\displaystyle\leq\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+\int\Delta\left(u_{2}\frac{\partial_{2}\varphi}{\varphi}g-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\varphi\right)\Delta gdx.

where the last inequality follows from the usual commutator estimates. Using (
3.6
) and the fact that
H^{2}(\Omega)
is a Banach algebra, the integral in the above estimate can be estimated as

\displaystyle\left|\int\Delta\left(u_{2}\frac{\partial_{2}\varphi}{\varphi}g-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\varphi\right)\Delta gdx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert u_{2}\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}+\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}\right).

Therefore, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\Delta g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}+\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\left(\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}+1\right)\rVert g\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Combining this with (
3.8
) and denoting
G(t):=\left(\rVert g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rVert\Delta g\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)
, we arrive at

\frac{d}{dt}G(t)\leq C\left(\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}+1\right)G(t)+C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}.

Since
\rVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{2}}<\infty
for
t\in[0,T)
, Grönwall’s inequality gives us that
G(t)<\infty
for each
t\in[0,T)
, hence

\rVert\theta(t)\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}<\infty,\text{ for each $t\in[0,T)$.}

Since the upper bound is independent of
L
, we arrive at

\rVert\theta(t)x_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}<\infty\text{ for each $t\in[0,T)$.}

Similarly, it follows from (
3.7
) that
\rVert u_{2}\varphi\rVert_{H^{2}}
is bounded uniformly in
L
, yielding that

\rVert u_{2}x_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}<\infty.

Hence, using the embedding
H^{2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
, we can obtain the desired decay estimates for
\theta
and
u_{2}
in (
3.5
). ∎

4.
High regularity estimates

In this section, we derive Sobolev norm estimates for
\theta
. We will frequently use the usual commutator estimate (e.g.
[
13
]
)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\rVert|D_{i}|^{k}(fg)-f|D_{i}|^{k}g\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\rVert f\rVert_{H^{k}}\rVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert\nabla f\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert g\rVert_{H^{k-1}},\text{ for $i=1,2$.}

The estimates for the Sobolev norms of
\theta
and
u
will be obtained in a standard manner using Sobolev embedding theorems. However, due to the anisotropic structure, certain technical computations are required, which will be carried out using the Fourier transform. These estimates are presented separately in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in H^{k}
for some
k>0
, and let
u
be as in (
3.4
). Then the following estimates hold:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left|\int\left(|D_{2}|^{k}(u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)-u_{2}|D_{2}|^{k}\partial_{2}\theta\right)|D_{2}|^{k}\theta dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta\partial_{111}\theta dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

where
C
depends only on
k>2
.

Proof.

Let us fix our notations. For
f,g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
(we denote the Fourier coefficients of
f
by
\widehat{f}_{n}(\xi)
, see (
2.1
)),

(4.4)

\displaystyle\rVert\widehat{f}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}_{n}(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\text{ for $p>1$},\quad\left(\widehat{f}*\widehat{g}\right)_{n}(\xi):=\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{f}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})||\widehat{g}_{n-n_{1}}(\xi-\xi_{1})|d\xi_{1}.

Note that we take absolute values in the integral inside the definition of the convolution.

Young’s inequality for convolution tells us

(4.5)

\displaystyle\rVert\widehat{f}*\widehat{g}\rVert_{L^{r}}\leq\rVert\widehat{f}\rVert_{L^{p}}\rVert\widehat{g}\rVert_{L^{q}},\text{ for $1+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}$ and $r,p,q\in[1,\infty]$.}

Furthermore, for
p\in[1,2)
and
\alpha>0
such that
\frac{2\alpha p}{2-p}>2
, the Hölder inequality gives us

\displaystyle\rVert\widehat{f}\rVert_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+|n|+|\xi|)^{\alpha}\widehat{f}\right|^{p}(1+|n|+|\xi|)^{-\alpha p}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|(1+|n|+|\xi|)^{\alpha}\widehat{f}\right|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|n|+|\xi|)^{-\frac{2\alpha p}{2-p}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\alpha}\rVert f\rVert_{H^{\alpha}}^{p}.

Especially, when
p=1
and
\alpha=k-1
, we have
\frac{2\alpha p}{2-p}=2(k-1)>2
, hence

(4.7)

\displaystyle\rVert\widehat{f}\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq C\rVert f\rVert_{H^{k-1}}.

Now we start proving the estimates in the lemma.

Proof of (
4.2
).

We write

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\int\left(|D_{2}|^{k}(u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)-u_{2}|D_{2}|^{k}\partial_{2}\theta\right)|D_{2}|^{k}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int\left(|D_{2}|^{k}(u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)-k\left(|D_{2}|u_{2}\right)|D_{2}|^{k-1}\partial_{2}\theta-u_{2}|D_{2}|^{k}\partial_{2}\theta\right)|D_{2}|^{k}\theta dx

\displaystyle\ +k\int|D_{2}|u_{2}\left(|D_{2}|^{k-1}\partial_{2}\theta\right)|D_{2}|^{k}\theta dx

(4.8)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

It is trivial that
I_{2}
can be estimated as

(4.9)

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}.

For
I_{1}
, the integral can be expressed, in terms of the integrands’ Fourier coefficients, as

(4.10)

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\widehat{u_{2}}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}_{n_{2}}(\xi_{2})\widehat{\theta}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},

where

m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2}):=\left(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}-k|\xi_{1}||\xi_{2}|^{k-1}-|\xi_{2}|^{k}\right)|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}.

Let us rewrite the expression in (
4.10
), using that
u_{2}=\partial_{1}\Psi
, as

(4.11)

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\underbrace{m(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\left(in_{1}\right)}_{=:m_{1}(n_{1},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})}\widehat{\Psi}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}_{n_{2}}(\xi_{2})\widehat{\theta}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Then the symbol
m_{1}
can be bounded as

\displaystyle|m_{1}(n_{1},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left(|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}-k|\xi_{1}||\xi_{2}|^{k-1}-|\xi_{2}|^{k}\right)|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}|n_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\left(|\xi_{1}|^{k}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-2}\right)|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}|n_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\left(|\xi_{1}|^{k}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-2}\right)|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}|n_{1}+n_{2}|+\left(|\xi_{1}|^{k}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-2}\right)|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}|n_{2}|.

Since it holds that, for
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\left(|\xi_{1}|^{k}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-2}\right)|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}\leq_{C}|\xi_{1}|^{k+1}|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k-1}+|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-1}|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k-1},

We have

\displaystyle|m_{1}(n_{1},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}|\xi_{1}|^{k+1}|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k-1}|n_{1}+n_{2}|

\displaystyle\ +|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-1}|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k-1}|n_{1}+n_{2}|

\displaystyle\ +|\xi_{1}|^{k}|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}|n_{2}|

\displaystyle\ +|\xi_{1}|^{2}|\xi_{2}|^{k-2}|\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}|^{k}|n_{2}|

\displaystyle=:m_{11}+m_{12}+m_{13}+m_{14}.

Hence, plugging this into (
4.11
), we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{4}I_{1i},\quad I_{1i}:=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}m_{2i}(n_{1},n_{2},\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\left|\widehat{\Psi}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}_{n_{2}}(\xi_{2})\widehat{\theta}_{n_{1}+n_{2}}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})\right|d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}.

Using the notations in (
4.4
), we can write

(4.13)

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle=\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k+1}\Psi}\left(\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}*\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-1}|D_{1}|\theta}\right)\Big\rVert_{L^{1}}

(4.14)

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle=\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{2}\Psi}\left(\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-1}\partial_{2}\theta}*\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-1}|D_{1}|\theta}\right)\Big\rVert_{L^{1}}

(4.15)

\displaystyle I_{13}

\displaystyle=\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\Psi}\left(\widehat{|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}*\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\theta}\right)\Big\rVert_{L^{1}}

(4.16)

\displaystyle I_{14}

\displaystyle=\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{2}\Psi}\left(\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-2}|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}*\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\theta}\right)\Big\rVert_{L^{1}}

Using (
4.5
) and (
4.7
), we see that

\displaystyle|I_{11}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k+1}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\rVert\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}\rVert_{L^{1}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-1}|D_{1}|\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq_{C}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k+1}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

\displaystyle|I_{12}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{2}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{1}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-1}\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-1}|D_{1}|\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq_{C}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k+1}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

\displaystyle|I_{14}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{2}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{1}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k-2}|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq_{C}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k+1}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

thus

(4.17)

\displaystyle|I_{11}|+|I_{12}|+|I_{14}|\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}.

To estimate
I_{13}
, we apply the Hölder inequality, yielding that

\displaystyle|I_{13}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}*\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}}\quad\text{ (for $p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1$)}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{\frac{2q}{q+2}}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Fixing
q
so that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{3}{k-2},2\right\}<q\in(2,\infty),

we have

\displaystyle 1<p<2,\quad 1<\frac{2q}{2+q}<2.

Applying (
4.6
) to
\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}
, we get

\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{2}|^{k}\Psi}\Big\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\rVert|D_{2}|^{k}\Psi\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Similarly, we apply (
4.6
) to
\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{\frac{2q}{q+2}}}
, obtaining

\Big\rVert\widehat{|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{\frac{2q}{q+2}}}\leq C\Big\rVert|D_{1}|\partial_{2}\theta\Big\rVert_{H^{k-2}}\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Therefore, (
4.18
) reads

|I_{13}|\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Plugging this estimate and (
4.17
) into (
4.12
) gives

\displaystyle|I_{1}|\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Together with (
4.9
) and (
4.8
), we obtain (
4.2
).

Proof of (
4.3
).
We have

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle:=\int\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta|D|\left(\partial_{111}|D|^{-1}\theta\right)dx=\int|D|\left(\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta\right)\left(\partial_{111}|D|^{-1}\theta\right)dx.

Hence, we also have

(4.19)

\displaystyle|J|\leq\Big\rVert\partial_{111}|D|^{-1}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\Big\rVert|D|(\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}.

It is straightforward that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\Big\rVert\partial_{111}|D|^{-1}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}=\rVert\partial_{11}\nabla\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}}=\rVert\nabla\partial_{1}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}=\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}.

To estimate
\Big\rVert|D|(\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}
, we look at the Fourier coefficients. Using the notations in (
4.4
) and the Plancherel theorem, we can bound it as

(4.21)

\displaystyle\Big\rVert|D|(\partial_{1}u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\rVert(|\xi|+|n|)\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}*\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}\rVert_{L^{2}}=:\rVert\widehat{g}\rVert_{L^{2}}.

where
\widehat{g}
is given by

\widehat{g}_{n}(\xi)=(|\xi|+|n|)\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}_{n-n_{1}}(\xi-\xi_{1})||\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})|d\xi_{1}.

We estimate
\widehat{g}_{n}(\xi)
as

\displaystyle|\widehat{g}_{n}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi-\xi_{1}|+|n-n_{1}|)|\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}_{n-n_{1}}(\xi-\xi_{1})||\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})|d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle\ +\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}_{n-n_{1}}(\xi-\xi_{1})\right|(|\xi_{1}|+|n_{1}|)\left|\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}_{n_{1}}(\xi_{1})\right|d\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\widehat{|D|\partial_{1}u_{2}}*\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}\right)_{n}(\xi)+\left(\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}*\widehat{|D|\partial_{2}\theta}\right)_{n}(\xi).

Therefore, the Plancherel theorem and Young’s inequality for convolution give us

\displaystyle\rVert\widehat{g}\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert|D|\partial_{1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\rVert\widehat{\partial_{2}\theta}\rVert_{L^{1}}+\rVert\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}\rVert_{L^{p}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}}\text{ for $p^{-1}+q^{-1}=\frac{3}{2}$}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}+\rVert\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}\rVert_{L^{p}}\Big\rVert\widehat{|D|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}},

where the last inequality follows from (
4.7
).
Let us estimate
\rVert\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}\rVert_{L^{p}}
and
\Big\rVert\widehat{|D|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}}
for appropriately chosen
p,q
. We choose
q
to satisfy
q=\frac{k}{k-1}
so that
p=\frac{2k}{k+2}
. Since
k>2
, it follows easily that

\displaystyle 1<p<2\quad\mbox{and}\quad 1<q<2.

Hence, applying (
4.6
) to
\rVert\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}\rVert_{L^{p}}
gives us

\rVert\widehat{\partial_{1}u_{2}}\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\rVert\partial_{1}u_{2}\rVert_{H^{1}}\leq C\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}.

Also, applying (
4.6
) to
\Big\rVert\widehat{|D|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}}
, we get

\Big\rVert\widehat{|D|\partial_{2}\theta}\Big\rVert_{L^{q}}\leq C\Big\rVert|D|\partial_{2}\theta\Big\rVert_{H^{k-2}}\leq C\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Thus (
4.22
) reads
\rVert\widehat{g}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}
. Plugging this estimate and (
4.20
) into (
4.19
), we arrive at

|J|\leq\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

which is the desired estimate. ∎

\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\theta\in C([0,T);H^{k})
be a solution to (
3.2
). Let
\gamma
be as in (
1.8
). Then there exist
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\gamma)>0
and
C=C(\gamma)
such that if

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\rVert\theta(t)\rVert_{H^{k}}<\delta_{0},

then

(4.23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\theta\rVert_{{H}^{k}}^{2}\leq-C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+C\left(\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

for
0\leq t\leq T
.

Proof.

In what follows
|D_{i}|
will denote either
|D_{1}|
or
|D_{2}|
, which is defined in (
2.2
).
Using (
3.2
), we compute

(4.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big\rVert|D_{i}|^{k}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int|D_{i}|^{k}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx+\int|D_{i}|^{k}\left(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\right)|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx

We first simplify the second term on the right-hand side. Using the stream function
\Psi
such that
u_{2}=\partial_{1}\Psi
and
\partial_{1}\theta=-\Delta\Psi
, we have

\displaystyle\int|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2})|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{1}|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx=-\int|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)|D_{i}|^{k}\partial_{1}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)|D_{i}|^{k}\Delta\Psi dx=-\int\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)\cdot\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\Psi dx

\displaystyle=-\int\partial_{2}\rho_{s}|\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\Psi|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\ +\int\left(\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}(\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\Psi\right)\cdot\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\Psi dx

(4.25)

\displaystyle\leq-C\Big\rVert|D_{i}|^{k}u\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\rVert\Psi\rVert_{\dot{H}^{k+1}}\Big\rVert\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}(\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\Psi\Big\rVert_{L^{2}},

where the last inequality is due to
-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}>\gamma
and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
A crude estimate gives us

\Big\rVert\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}(\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\Psi\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\rVert\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{k+1}}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq C\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq C_{\eta}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}}+\eta\rVert\Psi\rVert_{\dot{H}^{k+1}},

for any
\eta>0
, where the last inequality is due to the usual Sobolev interpolation theorem. Plugging this estimates into (
4.25
) with sufficiently small
\eta
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2})|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx\leq-C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+C\rVert\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Furthermore, denoting

G(x_{2}):=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Psi(x_{1},x_{2})dx_{1},

we notice from (
3.3
) that
g
solves

\partial_{22}G=0\text{ and }\lim_{|x_{2}|\to\infty}G(x_{2})=0.

Hence,
G(x_{2})=0
for every
x_{2}
. Then the Poincaré inequality gives us

\rVert\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\rVert\partial_{1}\Psi\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Therefore we arrive at

(4.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int|D_{i}|^{k}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2})|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx\leq-C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+C\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Note that in the derivation of this estimate, we have not used the condition that
k>2
. Indeed, repeating the same computations with
k=0
, we obtain

(4.27)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\theta dx\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Now, we move on to estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.24
). As usual, we use the incompressibility of the velocity to obtain

(4.28)

\displaystyle\int|D_{i}|^{k}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int\left(|D_{i}|^{k}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)-u\cdot\nabla|D_{i}|^{k}\theta\right)|D_{i}|^{k}\theta dx=I.

We claim that

(4.29)

\displaystyle|I|\leq C\left(\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}\right),\text{ whether $i=1,2$.}

Once the claim is verified, plugging it and (
4.26
) into (
4.24
) gives us

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\theta\rVert_{\dot{H}^{k}}^{2}\leq-C(1-C\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}})\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+C\left(\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Hence, if
\rVert\theta(t)\rVert_{H^{k}}
is sufficiently small depending on the implicit constant
C
, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\theta\rVert_{\dot{H}^{k}}^{2}\leq-C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+C\left(\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Combining this with (
4.27
), we derive (
4.23
).

In order to prove the claim, we split the cases when
i=1
and
i=2
.

When
i=1
.
In this case, (
4.28
) reads

(4.30)

\displaystyle\left|I\right|\leq\Big\rVert|D_{1}|^{k}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\Big\rVert|D_{1}|^{k}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)-u\cdot\nabla|D_{1}|^{k}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}.

The usual commutator estimate gives

\Big\rVert|D_{1}|^{k}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)-u\cdot|D_{1}|^{k}\nabla\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq_{C}\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}\rVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert u\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\rVert\nabla\theta\rVert_{H^{k-1}}\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},

where the last inequality follows from the Sobolev embedding
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{k}(\Omega)
. Also, note that

\Big\rVert|D_{1}|^{k}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}=\Big\rVert|D_{1}|^{k-2}\partial_{11}\theta\Big\rVert_{L^{2}}=\Big\rVert|D_{1}|^{k-2}\partial_{1}\Delta\Psi\Big\rVert\leq\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k+1}}\leq\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Therefore, the estimate (
4.30
) becomes

(4.31)

\displaystyle|I|\leq C\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}},\text{ when $i=1$.}

When
i=2
.
In this case, (
4.28
) reads

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int\left(|D_{2}|^{k}(u_{1}\partial_{1}\theta)-u_{1}|D_{2}|^{k}\partial_{1}\theta\right)|D_{2}|^{k}\theta dx+\int\left(|D_{2}|^{k}(u_{2}\partial_{2}\theta)-u_{2}|D_{2}|^{k}\partial_{2}\theta\right)|D_{2}|^{k}\theta dx

(4.32)

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Again, the usual commutator estimate and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give us

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\left(\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}\rVert\partial_{1}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert u\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\rVert\partial_{1}\theta\rVert_{H^{k-1}}\right)\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}(\rVert\partial_{1}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\rVert u\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}})\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}\rVert\Psi\rVert_{H^{k+1}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq_{C}\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}.

A necessary estimate for
I_{2}
was already derived in Lemma
4.1
, namely, we have

|I_{2}|\leq_{C}\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}.

Finally, plugging this and (
4.33
) into (
4.32
), we obtain

|I|\leq_{C}\rVert\nabla u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}\text{ when $i=2$.}

Collecting this and (
4.31
), we have proved (
4.29
).
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Under the same assumption in Proposition
4.2
, there exist
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\gamma)>0
and
C=C(\gamma)
such that if

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\rVert\theta(t)\rVert_{H^{k}}<\delta_{0},

then

(4.34)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\partial_{11}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-C\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{{H}^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert\partial_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)\rVert\partial_{11}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\partial_{12}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq-C\rVert u_{2}\rVert_{H^{2}}^{2}+C\left(\rVert u\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\rVert\partial_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\right)\rVert\partial_{12}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

(4.35)

\displaystyle\ +C\left(\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{H^{2}}\rVert\partial_{12}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}\rVert\theta\rVert_{H^{k}}+\Big\rVert\partial_{1}|D|^{-1}u_{2}\Big\rVert_{{H}^{2}}^{2}+\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}
denote either
\partial_{12}
or
\partial_{11}
. Using (
3.2
), we compute

(4.36)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\rVert\mathcal{D}\theta\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int\mathcal{D}(u\cdot\nabla\theta)\mathcal{D}\theta dx+\int\mathcal{D}\left(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\right)\mathcal{D}\theta dx.

The second term on the right-hand side can be estimated, using
u_{2}=\partial_{1}\Psi
, as

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}\left(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}u_{2}\right)\mathcal{D}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int\partial_{1}\mathcal{D}(-\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\Psi)\mathcal{D}\theta dx

5.
Energy structure analysis

Throughout the section, we will assume that
\rho(t)
is a smooth solution to the IPM equation satisfying

(5.1)

\displaystyle\rVert\rho(t)-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\rVert u(t)\rVert_{H^{k}}^{2}dt=:\delta\leq\delta_{0},\text{ for $t\in[0,T]$ for some $T>1$ and $\delta_{0}\ll 1$.}

We denote

(5.2)

\displaystyle E(t):=\mathcal{E}(\rho(t)),

where
\mathcal{E}
is the potential energy defined in (
2.29
).

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist
\delta_{0}>0
such that if
\rho_{0}-\rho_{s}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and (
5.1
) holds, then

\frac{d}{dt}E(t)=-\rVert u(t)\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\mathcal{E}
in (
2.29
), we observe that for sufficiently large
s>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(E_{s}(\rho)-E_{s}(\rho_{0}^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ -s<\rho(t,x)<s\right\}}\rho_{t}(t,x)x_{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ -s<\rho(t,x)<s\right\}}-u(t,x)\cdot\nabla\left(\rho(t,x)-\rho_{s}\right)x_{2}dx

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ -s<\rho(t,x)<s\right\}}-u_{2}(t,x)\partial_{2}\rho_{s}x_{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ -s<\rho(t,x)<s\right\}}u_{2}(t,x)\theta(t,x)dx-\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}\theta(t,x)x_{2}u(t,x)\cdot\vec{n}d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ -s<\rho(t,x)<s\right\}}-u_{2}(t,x)\partial_{2}\rho_{s}x_{2}dx

\displaystyle=:I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t)+I_{3}(t),

where
d\sigma
denotes the surface measure and
\vec{n}
is the outer normal vector on
\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}
. Let us estimate
I_{1},I_{2}
and
I_{3}
.

For
I_{2}
, it follows from (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle|I_{2}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\rVert x_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sigma(\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\})\sup_{x\in\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|\theta(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\rVert x_{2}u_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\rVert x_{2}\theta\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|x_{2}|^{-1}\sigma(\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\})

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\sup_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|x_{2}|^{-1}\sigma(\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\})

Since
\rVert\rho-\rho_{s}\rVert_{C^{1}}\leq\rVert\rho-\rho_{s}\rVert_{H^{k}}\leq\delta_{0}
and
\rho_{s}
is strictly decreasing, it holds that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\inf_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|x_{2}|\to\infty\text{ as $s\to\infty$},

and the length of the level sets are bounded as well,

\sigma(\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\})<C(T)

for some constant
C(T)>0
. Therefore,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}I_{2}(t)=0\text{ uniformly in $t\in[0,T]$.}

For
I_{3}
, again the decay estimate in (
3.5
) give that

|I_{3}(t)|\leq\rVert u_{2}x_{2}\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x)|\leq C(T)\sup_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x_{2})|.

Since
\partial_{2}\rho_{s}\in H^{k}
, it holds that
\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x_{2})\to 0
as
|x_{2}|\to\infty
. Thus, using (
5.3
), we get

\sup_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ \pm s=\rho(t,x)\right\}}|\partial_{2}\rho_{s}(x_{2})|\to 0\text{ as $s\to\infty$, }

yielding that

\lim_{s\to\infty}I_{3}(t)\to 0,\text{ uniformly in $t\in[0,T]$.}

For
I_{1}
, since
u_{2}\theta
is an integrable function, there holds

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to\infty}\int_{\left\{x\in\Omega:\ -s<\rho(t,x)<s\right\}}u_{2}(t,x)\theta(t,x)dx=\int_{\Omega}u_{2}\theta dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}\Psi\partial_{1}\theta dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Psi\Delta\Psi dx

\displaystyle=-\rVert u\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

and the limit is uniform in

(A.2)

\|\theta^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq Ct^{-\frac{k}{2}-\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{for any}\,\,t\geq 1,

for some universal constant
C>0
.

Proof.

For each
0<\varepsilon<1
, define
\theta_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in H^{k}(\Omega)
via its Fourier transform as

\widehat{\theta_{n}^{\varepsilon}}(t=0,\xi)=\begin{cases}|\xi|^{-k-\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}&\mbox{for $n=1$ and $|\xi|\geq 1$},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We can explicitly solve (
A.1
) on the Fourier side, obtaining

\widehat{\theta_{1}^{\varepsilon}}(t,\xi)=e^{-t/(1+\xi^{2})}|\xi|^{-k-\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\geq 1},\text{ thus }\theta^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\left(\int_{|\xi|\geq 1}e^{-t/(1+\xi^{2})}|\xi|^{-k-\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}e^{i\xi x_{2}}d\xi\right)e^{ix_{1}}.

Then, Plancherel’s identity implies that

\displaystyle\|\theta^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\xi|\geq 1}e^{-\frac{2t}{1+|\xi|^{2}}}|\xi|^{-2k-1-4\varepsilon}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|\geq 1}e^{-\frac{2t}{\xi^{2}}}|\xi|^{-2k-1-4\varepsilon}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2\eta}\left(\frac{t}{\eta}\right)^{-k-\frac{1}{2}-2\varepsilon}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\eta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\,d\eta

\displaystyle\geq t^{-k-2\varepsilon}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2\eta}\eta^{k+1}\,d\eta}

\displaystyle=Ct^{-k-2\varepsilon},

where
C
does not depend on
\epsilon
. In the above estimate, we used
\frac{1}{1+\xi^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}
, change of variable
\eta=\frac{t}{\xi^{2}}
with evenness of the integrand in
\xi
, and
t\geq 1
. This shows (
A.2
).

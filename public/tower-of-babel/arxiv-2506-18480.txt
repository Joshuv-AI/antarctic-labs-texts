Regularity of random attractor and fractal dimension of fractional stochastic Navier-Stokes equations on three-dimensional torus
Hui Liu, Chengfeng Sun, Jie Xin

Abstract
In this paper we will study the asymptotic dynamics of fractional Navier-Stokes (NS) equations with additive white noise on three-dimensional torus 𝕋 3 \mathbb{T}^{3} . Under the conditions that the external forces f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) belong to the phase space H H and the noise intensity function h ⁡ ( x ) h(x) satisfies ‖ ∇ h ‖ L ∞ < π ​ ν ​ λ 1 5 4 \|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}<\sqrt{\pi}\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}} , where ν \nu is the kinematic viscosity of the fluid and λ 1 \lambda_{1} is the first eigenvalue of the Stokes operator, we shown that the random fractional three-dimensional NS equations possess a tempered ( H , H 5 2 ) (H,H^{\frac{5}{2}}) -random attractor whose fractal dimension in H 5 2 H^{\frac{5}{2}} is finite. This was proved by establishing, first, an H 5 2 H^{\frac{5}{2}} bounded absorbing set and, second, a local ( H , H 5 2 ) (H,H^{\frac{5}{2}}) -Lipschitz continuity in initial values from which the ( H , H 5 2 ) (H,H^{\frac{5}{2}}) -asymptotic compactness of the system follows. Since the forces f f belong only to H H , the H 5 2 H^{\frac{5}{2}} bounded absorbing set was constructed by an indirect approach of estimating the H 5 2 H^{\frac{5}{2}} -distance between the solutions of the random fractional three-dimensional NS equations and that of the corresponding deterministic equations. Furthermore, under the conditions that the external forces f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) belong to the H k − 5 4 H^{k-\frac{5}{4}} and the noise intensity function h ⁡ ( x ) h(x) belong to H k + 5 4 H^{k+\frac{5}{4}} for k ≥ 5 2 k\geq\frac{5}{2} , we shown that the random fractional three-dimensional NS equations possess a tempered ( H , H k ) (H,H^{k}) -random attractor whose fractal dimension in H k H^{k} is finite. This was proved by using iterative methods and establishing, first, an H k H^{k} bounded absorbing set and, second, a local ( H , H k ) (H,H^{k}) -Lipschitz continuity in initial values from which the ( H , H k ) (H,H^{k}) -asymptotic compactness of the system follows.

Preliminaries:
(X,Y)

In this section, we will introduce the basic concepts and
(X,Y)
-random attractor theory and their fractal dimensions, see, e.g.
[
3
,
12
,
16
,
17
,

,
33
]
.

Suppose that
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
is a separable Banach space. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space, with

\mathcal{F}
being possibly not
\mathbb{P}
-complete, endowed with a group
\{\theta_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of measure-preserving self-transformations on
\Omega
. Let
\mathcal{B}(\cdot)
be the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of a metric space, and let
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=[0,\infty)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A mapping
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\Omega\times X\rightarrow X
is called a
random dynamical system (abbrev. RDS)
in
X
, if

1.

\phi
is
(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{B}(X),\mathcal{B}(X))
-measurable;

2.

for any
\omega\in\Omega
,
\phi(0,\omega,\cdot)
is the identity on
X
;

3.

it holds the following cocycle property

\displaystyle\phi(t+s,\omega,x)=\phi(t,\theta_{s}\omega,\phi(s,\omega,x)),\quad\forall t,s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},~\omega\in\Omega,~x\in X;

4.

the mapping
x\mapsto\phi(t,\omega,x)
is continuous.

A set-valued mapping
\mathfrak{D}
:
\Omega\mapsto 2^{X}\setminus\emptyset
,
\omega\mapsto\mathfrak{D}(\omega)
, is said to be a
random set
in
X
if it is
measurable
in the sense that the mapping
\omega\to{\rm dist}_{X}(x,\mathfrak{D}(\omega))
is
(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
-measurable for any
x\in X
. If each its image
\mathfrak{D}(\omega)
is closed (or bounded, compact, etc.) in
X
, then
\mathfrak{D}
is called a closed (or bounded, compact, etc.) random set in
X
.

A random variable
\zeta:\Omega\mapsto X
is defined
tempered
if

\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\varepsilon t}|\zeta(\theta_{-t}\omega)|=0,\quad\varepsilon>0.

If
\zeta
is a tempered random variable, then so is
|\zeta|^{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
A random set
\mathfrak{D}
in
X
is said to be
tempered
if there exists a tempered random variable
\zeta
such that
\|\mathfrak{D}(\omega)\|_{X}:=\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{D}(\omega)}\|x\|\leq\zeta(\omega)
for any
\omega\in\Omega
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{X}
be the collection of all the tempered random sets in
X
. We introduce the basic concepts as the following.

Definition 2.2
.

A random set
\mathfrak{B}
in
X
is called a
(random) absorbing set
of an RDS
\phi
if it pullback absorbs every tempered random set in
X
, i.e., if for any
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
there exists a random variable
T_{\mathfrak{D}}(\cdot)
such that

\displaystyle\phi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{-t}\omega))\subset\mathfrak{B}(\omega),\quad t\geq T_{\mathfrak{D}}(\omega),\ \omega\in\Omega.

Definition 2.3
.

A random set
\mathcal{A}
is said the
random attractor
of an RDS
\phi
in
X
if

1.

\mathcal{A}
is a tempered and compact random set in
X
;

2.

\mathcal{A}
is invariant under
\phi
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\theta_{t}\omega)=\phi(t,\omega,\mathcal{A}(\omega)),\quad t\geq 0,~\omega\in\Omega;

3.

\mathcal{A}
denotes pullback attracts each tempered set in
X
, i.e., for every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rm dist}_{X}\big(\phi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{-t}\omega)),\,\mathcal{A}(\omega)\big)=0,\quad\omega\in\Omega,

where
{\rm dist}_{X}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Hausdorff semi-metric between non-empty subsets of
X
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\rm dist}_{X}(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A}\inf_{b\in B}\|a-b\|_{X},\quad\forall A,B\subset X.

Assume that
Y
denotes a Banach space with continuous embedding
Y\hookrightarrow X
.

Definition 2.4
.

A random set
\mathcal{A}
is said the
(X,Y)
-random attractor
of an RDS
\phi
in
X
, if

1.

\mathcal{A}
is a tempered and compact random set in
Y
;

2.

\mathcal{A}
is invariant under
\phi
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\theta_{t}\omega)=\phi(t,\omega,\mathcal{A}(\omega)),\quad t\geq 0,~\omega\in\Omega;

3.

\mathcal{A}
is pullback attracts each tempered set in
X
in the metric of
Y
, i.e., for every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
,

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rm dist}_{Y}\big(\phi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{-t}\omega)),\,\mathcal{A}(\omega)\big)=0,\quad\omega\in\Omega.

Then we introduce the main criterion as following.

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
16
, Theorem 19]
)
Assume that
\phi
is an RDS. Suppose that

1.

\phi
has a random absorbing set
\mathfrak{B}
which is a tempered and closed random set in
Y
;

2.

\phi
is
(X,Y)
-asymptotically compact, that is, for every
\mathfrak{D}\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
and
t_{n}\to\infty
, any sequence

\{\phi(t_{n},\theta_{-t_{n}}\omega,x_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
x_{n}\in\mathfrak{D}(\theta_{-t_{n}}\omega)
,
\omega\in\Omega
, has a convergent subsequence in
Y
.

Then
\phi
has a unique
(X,Y)
-random attractor
\mathcal{A}
defined by

\mathcal{A}(\omega)=\bigcap_{s\geq 0}\overline{\bigcup_{t\geq s}\phi(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\mathfrak{B}(\theta_{-t}\omega))}^{Y},\quad\omega\in\Omega.

Finally, we introduce the definition of the fractal dimension. Let
E
be a precompact set in
X
, and let
N(E,\varepsilon)
be the minimum number of balls of radius
\varepsilon
in
X
required to cover
E
. Then the
fractal dimension of
E
is defined as

d_{f}^{X}(E)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N(E,\varepsilon)}{-\log\varepsilon},

where the superscript “
\ {}^{X}
” indicates the space to which the fractal dimension is referred, see, e.g.
[
33
,
49
]
.

3
The fractional three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations and the attractors in
H

In this section, we first introduce the functional spaces and basic result of the deterministic fractional three-dimensional NS equations, and then associate an RDS to the random fractional three-dimensional NS equations. We construct
H^{\frac{5}{4}}
random absorbing sets and the
(H,H^{\frac{5}{4}})
-attractor of the solutions and has a finite fractal dimensional.

3.1
Functional spaces

In this subsection, we introduce the functional spaces and Poincaré’s inequality as follows, see, e.g.,
[
48
,
56
,
57
]
.
Let
(C_{per}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))^{3}
be the space of three component infinitely differentiable functions that are
L
-periodic in each direction, and by
\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}
the completion of
(C_{per}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))^{3}
with respect to the
(L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))^{3}
norm.

The phase space
(H,\|\cdot\|)
is a subspace of
\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}
of functions with zero mean and free divergence, that is,

\displaystyle H=\left\{u\in\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}:\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u{\rm{d}}x=0,\ {\rm div}\,u=0\right\},

endowed with the norm of
\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}
, i.e.,

\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}}
. For any
p>2
, we write
(L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))^{3}
simply as
L^{p}
and its norm as
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
.

Note that any
u\in\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}
has an expression
u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\hat{u}_{j}e^{{\rm i}j\cdot x}
, where
{\rm i}=\sqrt{-1}
and
\hat{u}_{j}
are Fourier coefficients. Hence,
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}u{\rm{d}}x=0
is equivalent to
\hat{u}_{0}=0
. Then, any
u\in H
is in form as follows

\displaystyle u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{0\}}\hat{u}_{j}e^{{\rm i}j\cdot x}.

Let
P:\mathbb{L}_{per}^{2}\rightarrow H
denote the Helmholtz-Leray orthogonal projection operator. In this periodic space, we define the stokes operator
Au=-P\Delta u=-\Delta u
for any
u\in D(A)
, here
D(A)
is given as follows. In this bounded domain, the projector
P
can not commute with derivatives. Then the operator
A^{-1}
is a self-adjoint positive-definite compact operator from
H
to
H
.

For
s>0
, the Sobolev space
H^{s}:=D(A^{s/2})
is given in a standard way by

\displaystyle H^{s}=\left\{u\in H:\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}=\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\backslash\{0\}}|j|^{2s}|\hat{u}_{j}|^{2}<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}}=\|A^{s/2}\cdot\|
.

For
u,v\in H^{1}
, we introduce the following bilinear form

\displaystyle B(u,v)=P((u\cdot\nabla)v),

and, in particular,
B(u):=B(u,u)
.

We introduce the following Poincaré’s inequality, see, e.g.,
[
26
,
48
,
57
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Poincaré’s inequality)
.

If
u\in H^{\frac{5}{4}}
, then

\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|u\|^{2}\leq\|u\|_{H^{\frac{5}{4}}}^{2},

(3.1)

here
\lambda_{1}
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta
.

For later purpose we denote by
\lambda:=\alpha\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}/4
. Assume that the noise intensity
h
satisfy Assumption
1
, i.e.,
h\in H^{\frac{15}{4}}
and

\displaystyle\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}<{\sqrt{\pi}}\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}.

(3.2)

(Especially,
\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}<\sqrt{\pi}\nu
for the scale
L=2\pi
).
Obviously, the tolerance of the noise intensity increases as the kinematic viscosity
\nu
increases.

3.2

In this subsection, we introduce basic result of the deterministic fractional three-dimensional NS equations (
1.2

with a global attractor

. Furthermore, the following regularity result of the attractor

seems optimal for

in the literature, see, e.g.,
[

,
42
,

.

Lemma 3.2

For
f\in H
, then the deterministic fractional three-dimensional NS equation (

) generate a semigroup

which possesses a global attractor
\mathcal{A}_{0}
in
H
and has an
absorbing set which is bounded in
H^{\frac{5}{2}}
. In particular, the global attractor

When

, we can not get an estimation of

directly and thus Lemma

was shown by estimating the time-derivatives

of solutions rather than estimating the solutions

themselves. Then the method can not apply to the random fractional three-dimensional NS equations (

Remark 3.4

Based on

3.3
Generation of an RDS

In this subsection, we introduce associate an RDS to the random fractional three-dimensional NS equations.
Firstly, we will transform the fractional stochastic three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations (

on

.
Inspired by the references in

z(\theta_{t}\omega)=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\tau}(\theta_{t}\omega)(\tau){\rm{d}}\tau,\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\ \omega\in\Omega.

Then

By virtue of

,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|}{|t|}=0,\quad{\rm and}\quad\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int^{t}_{0}z(\theta_{s}\omega){\rm{d}}s=0,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{m}{\rm{d}}s=\mathbb{E}\big(|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{m}\big)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1+m}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}},\quad\omega\in\tilde{\Omega},

From now on, we will not distinguish

Under projection

and Assumption

Hence, by

where

we associated an RDS

In this subsection, we construct

Under the condition (

) in Assumption

in

. In this paper, let

We will frequently make use of two fundamental lemmas as follows, see, e.g.,
[

Lemma 3.6

(Gronwall’s lemma)
.

Assume that
x(t)
is a function from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\dot{x}+a(t)x\leq b(t).

Then for any
t\geq s
,

\displaystyle x(t)\leq e^{-\int^{t}_{s}a(\tau){\rm{d}}\tau}x(s)+\int_{s}^{t}e^{-\int^{t}_{\eta}a(\tau)\,{\rm{d}}\tau}b(\eta){\rm{d}}\eta.

Lemma 3.7

(Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality)
.

If
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
D^{m}u\in L^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
,
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
, then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}}^{a}\|u\|^{1-a}_{L^{q}},

where

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{j}{3}=a\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{3}\right)+\frac{1-a}{q},\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,~0\leq j\leq m,~\frac{j}{m}\leq a\leq 1,

and

C
depends only on
\{m,\,j,\,a,\,q,\,r\}
.

Lemma 3.8

(
H
bound)
.

Assume that
f\in H
and Assumption
1
hold.
Then there exist random variables
T_{1}(\omega)
and
\zeta_{1}(\omega)
, where
\zeta_{1}
is tempered, such that any
solution
v
of the system (
3.5
) corresponding to initial values
v(0)\in H
satisfies

\displaystyle\|v(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}\leq e^{-\lambda t}\|v(0)\|^{2}+\zeta_{1}(\omega),\quad t\geq T_{1}(\omega).

Proof.

By Assumption 1 and (
3.2
),
{\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}}/{\sqrt{\pi}}<\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}
, then there exist
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
\beta>0
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}},

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(1+\beta\right)

\displaystyle=(\nu-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\nu)\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}.

(3.7)

Taking the inner product of the system (
3.5
) in
H
by
v
and integration by parts we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|v\|^{2}+\nu\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}=-(B(v+hz(\theta_{t}\omega)),v)+\big(f-\nu A^{\frac{5}{4}}hz(\theta_{t}\omega)+hz(\theta_{t}\omega),v\big).

Applying Poincaré’s inequality (
3.1
), it can deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|v\|^{2}+\left(2-\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|v\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|v\|^{2}+2\nu\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\big|\big(B(v+hz(\theta_{t}\omega)),v\big)\big|+2\big|\big(f-\nu A^{\frac{5}{4}}hz(\theta_{t}\omega)+hz(\theta_{t}\omega),v\big)\big|.

(3.8)

By Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, it can deduce that

\displaystyle 2\big|\big(B(v+hz(\theta_{t}\omega)),v\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|v|^{2}|\nabla h|\ {\rm{d}}x+2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|v||h||\nabla h|\ {\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq 2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|^{2}+2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{2}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|\|h\|

\displaystyle\leq 2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}}{8}\|v\|^{2}+C|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{4}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|h\|^{2},

and

\displaystyle 2\big|\big(f-\nu A^{\frac{5}{4}}hz(\theta_{t}\omega)+hz(\theta_{t}\omega),v\big)\big|\leq\frac{\alpha\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}}{8}\|v\|^{2}+C\|f\|^{2}+C|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{2}\left(\|A^{\frac{5}{4}}h\|^{2}+\|h\|^{2}\right).

Inserting the above two inequalities into (
3.8
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|v\|^{2}+\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}\|v\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|^{2}+C|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{4}\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|h\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\|f\|^{2}+C|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{2}\left(\|h\|^{2}+\|A^{\frac{5}{4}}h\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|^{2}+C\left(1+|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{4}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|v\|^{2}+\left[\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}-2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|\right]\|v\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}\leq C\left(1+|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{4}\right).

By the Gronwall lemma, we have

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}(s-t)+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq e^{-\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}t+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\|v(0)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}(s-t)+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s,

and then

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}(s-t)}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq e^{-\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}t+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\|v(0)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}(s-t)+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s.

(3.9)

By the above equality (
3.4
) for
m=1
, it can deduce that

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|\,{\rm{d}}s=\mathbb{E}(|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}},\quad\omega\in\Omega,

then there exists a random variable
T_{1}(\omega)\geq 1
such that, for any
t\geq T_{1}(\omega)
,

\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|\,{\rm{d}}s\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{\pi}},

and

\displaystyle 2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|\,{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(1+\beta\right)t

\displaystyle=\left(2\nu-\alpha\nu\right)\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}t\quad\text{(by \eqref{c2})}.

(3.10)

Inserting (
3.10
) into (
3.9
), it yields

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}(s-t)}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq e^{-\frac{\alpha}{4}\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}t}\|v(0)\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(2-\frac{3\alpha}{4}\right)\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}(s-t)+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s.

Let
\lambda:=\alpha\nu\lambda_{1}^{\frac{5}{4}}/4
,
this estimate is given as

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{8}{\alpha}-3\right)\lambda(s-t)}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq e^{-\lambda t}\|v(0)\|^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{8}{\alpha}-3\right)\lambda(s-t)+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{t}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s.

Replacing
\omega
with
\theta_{-t}\omega
yields

\displaystyle\|v(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha\nu}{2}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\left(\frac{8}{\alpha}-3\right)\lambda(s-t)}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(s,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\quad\leq e^{-\lambda t}\|v(0)\|^{2}+C\int^{0}_{-t}e^{\left(\frac{8}{\alpha}-3\right)\lambda s+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{0}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s.

Let a random variable be defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(\omega):=C\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{\left(\frac{8}{\alpha}-3\right)\lambda s+2\|\nabla h\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{s}^{0}|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|{\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s,\quad\omega\in\Omega,

then
\zeta_{1}(\cdot)
is a tempered random variable such that

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v_{i}(\tau,\theta_{-t}\omega,v_{i}(0))\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\tau-t}\omega)|^{2}){\rm{d}}\tau}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}\bar{v}(0)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left(1+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v_{i}(\eta,\theta_{-t}\omega,v_{i,0})\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\eta-t}\omega)|^{2}\right){\rm{d}}\eta\right).

(7.27)

Taking the inner product of the system (
3.5
) in
H
by
A^{k-\frac{5}{4}}v
, by the similar method and (
7.2
), it yields

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{d}}}{{\rm{d}}t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+\nu\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{2}\right)\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+C\left(1+|z(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{4}\right).

(7.28)

Applying Gronwall’s lemma on (
7.3
) gives

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(t)\|^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\int_{s}^{t}(\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{2}){\rm{d}}\tau}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq e^{C\int_{0}^{t}(\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{2}){\rm{d}}\tau}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(0)\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\int_{s}^{t}(\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{2}){\rm{d}}\tau}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}(\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{2}){\rm{d}}\tau}\left(\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(0)\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s\right).

(7.29)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+|z(\theta_{s}\omega)|^{4}\right){\rm{d}}s},

the above estimate (
7.3
) is simplified to

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(t)\|^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(t)\|^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}e^{C\int_{s}^{t}(\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{2}){\rm{d}}\tau}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v(s)\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v\|^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{4}){\rm{d}}\tau}\left(\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(0)\|^{2}+1\right).

Replacing
\omega
by
\theta_{-t}\omega
yields

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v(s,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(\tau,\theta_{-t}\omega,\bar{v}(0))\|^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+|z(\theta_{\tau-t}\omega)|^{4}){\rm{d}}\tau}\left(\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(0)\|^{2}+1\right)

\displaystyle=Ce^{C\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(\tau,\theta_{-t}\omega,\bar{v}(0))\|^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau+C\int_{-t}^{0}(1+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{4}){\rm{d}}\tau}\left(\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(0)\|^{2}+1\right).

(7.30)

By the above Lemma
3.8
, we have for
t\geq T_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{5}{8}}v(\tau,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}{\rm{d}}\tau\leq C(e^{-\lambda t}\|v(0)\|^{2}+\zeta_{1}(\omega)).

(7.31)

Inserting (
7.31
) into (
7.3
), we have for
t\geq T_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}v(s,\theta_{-t}\omega,v(0))\|^{2}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\zeta_{1}(\omega)+C\int_{-t}^{0}(1+|z(\theta_{\tau}\omega)|^{4}){\rm{d}}\tau}\left(\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}v(0)\|^{2}+1\right):=\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega),

(7.32)

here
\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)
is a tempered random variable such that
\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)>1
.
Moreover, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}\bar{v}(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,\bar{v}(0))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\zeta_{1}(\omega)+C\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}\bar{v}(0)\|^{2}\left(1+\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)+\int_{-t}^{0}|z(\theta_{\eta}\omega)|^{2}{\rm{d}}\eta\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\zeta_{1}(\omega)+C\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}\bar{v}(0)\|^{2}.

(7.33)

Let
t=T_{k}(\omega)>T^{*}=T_{k-1}(\omega)
, we deduce at
t=T_{k}(\omega)

\displaystyle\|A^{\frac{k}{2}}\bar{v}(T_{k}\omega,\theta_{-T_{k}}\omega,\bar{v}(0))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{C\zeta_{1}(\omega)+C\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)}\|A^{\frac{k}{2}-\frac{5}{8}}\bar{v}(0)\|^{2}.

Let

L_{k}(\mathfrak{B}^{k-\frac{5}{4}},\omega):=Ce^{C\zeta_{1}(\omega)+C\zeta_{1}^{*}(\omega)},\quad\omega\in\Omega,

this completes the proof of Lemma
7.4
.
∎

Theorem 7.5

Let Assumption

,

.

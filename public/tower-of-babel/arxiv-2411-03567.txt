Characteristic Polynomials and Hypergraph Generating Functions via Heaps of Pieces
Joshua Cooper, Krystal Guo, Utku Okur

Abstract
It is a classical result due to Jacobi in algebraic combinatorics that the generating function of closed walks at a vertex u u in a graph G G is determined by the rational function ϕ G − u ​ ( t ) ϕ G ​ ( t ) \frac{\phi_{G-u}(t)}{\phi_{G}(t)} where ϕ G ​ ( t ) \phi_{G}(t) is the characteristic polynomial of G G . In this paper, we show that the corresponding rational function for a hypergraph is also a generating function for some combinatorial objects in the hypergraph. We make use of the Heaps of Pieces framework, developed by Viennot, demonstrating its use on graphs, digraphs, and multigraphs before using it on hypergraphs. In the case of a graph G G , the pieces are cycles and the concurrence relation is sharing a vertex. The pyramids with maximal piece containing a vertex u ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) u\in V(G) are in one-to-one correspondence with closed walks at u u . In the case of a hypergraph ℋ \mathcal{H} , connected “infragraphs” can be defined as the set of pieces, with the same concurrence relation: sharing a vertex. Our main results are established by analyzing multivariate resultants of polynomial systems associated to adjacency hypermatrices. Keywords: hypergraphs, characteristic polynomial, generating functions Mathematics Subject Classifications 2020: Primary 05C65; Secondary 05C50, 05A15, 15A69.

2.3
The Heaps of Pieces Framework

To use the theory of Heaps of Pieces (
[
18
,
27
]
), we reformulate some definitions and theorems. Let
(\Omega,\leq)
be a poset.

1.

Given two elements
x,y\in\Omega
, then
y

covers

x
, denoted
x\lessdot y
, provided
x<y
and there is no
z\in\Omega
such that
x<z<y
.

2.

The
down-set
of
a
is the set:
\downarrow\!\{a\}_{\Omega}:=\{b\in\Omega:b\leq_{\Omega}a\}
. Let
\langle a\rangle_{\Omega}:=\downarrow\!\{a\}_{\Omega}\setminus\{a\}=\{b\in\Omega:b<_{\Omega}a\}
be the set of elements strictly below
a
.

3.

The
up-set
of
a
is the set:
\uparrow\!\{a\}_{\Omega}:=\{b\in\Omega:b\geq_{\Omega}a\}
.

4.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
denote the set of maximal elements of
\Omega
.

The following is a restatement of
[
27
, Definition 2.1, p. 324]
:

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a set (of pieces) with a symmetric and reflexive binary (concurrence) relation
\mathcal{R}
. Then, a
heap
is a triple

H=(\Omega,\leq,\ell)

where
(\Omega,\leq)
is a poset,
\ell:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is a function (
\ell
labels the elements of
\Omega
by elements of
\mathcal{B}
) such that for all
x,y\in\Omega
,

1.

If
\ell(x)\mathcal{R}\ell(y)
then
x\leq y
or
y\leq x
.

2.

If
x\lessdot y
, then
\ell(x)\mathcal{R}\ell(y)
.

Equivalently (
[
18
, Definition 2.5]
,
[
27
, p. 325]
), a
heap
is a triple
H=(\Omega,\leq,\ell)
, where
\ell:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is a function and there exists a set of relations
\preceq
such that,
i)
For all
x,y\in\Omega
,
\ell(x)\mathcal{R}\ell(y)
if and only if
x\preceq y
or
y\preceq x
, and
ii)

\leq
is the partial order obtained by taking the transitive closure of
\preceq
.

Remark 3
.

To see that the two definitions given above are equivalent, let
H=(\Omega,\leq,\ell)
be a heap according to the first definition. Then, define
x\preceq y
if and only if
x\leq y
and
\ell(x)\mathcal{R}\ell(y)
, and note that
\leq
is the transitive closure of
\preceq
, and so,
H
is a heap according to the second definition. Conversely, if
H^{\prime}=(\Omega^{\prime},\leq^{\prime},\ell^{\prime})
is a heap according to the second definition, then
\leq^{\prime}
satisfies the conditions of the first definition.

Let
\mathbbm{h}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R})
be the set of heaps labeled by the set of pieces
\mathcal{B}
, with a given concurrence relation
\mathcal{R}
. Whenever the concurrence relation is clear from context, we suppress the argument
\mathcal{R}
and shortly write
\mathbbm{h}(\mathcal{B})
instead of
\mathbbm{h}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R})
. Whenever
\leq
and
\ell
are clear from context, we refer to a heap
H=(\Omega,\leq,\ell)
by the underlying ground set
\Omega
.

We include a restatement of
[
18
, Definition 2.5, p. 4]
, for the composition of heaps: Let
H_{1}=(\Omega_{1},\leq_{1},\ell_{1}:\Omega_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}),H_{2}=(\Omega_{2},\leq_{2},\ell_{2}:\Omega_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{B})\in\mathbbm{h}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R})
be heaps, such that
\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}=\emptyset
. Then
the composition of
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
,
H_{1}\circ H_{2}
, is the heap
(\Omega_{1}\sqcup\Omega_{2},\leq_{3},\ell_{3})
with

1)

\ell_{3}(v)=\begin{cases}\ell_{1}(v)&\text{ if }v\in\Omega_{1}\\
\ell_{2}(v)&\text{ if }v\in\Omega_{2}\end{cases}

2)

The partial order
\ell_{3}
on
\Omega_{1}\sqcup\Omega_{2}
is the transitive closure of

a)

v_{1}\leq_{3}v_{2}
if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\Omega_{1}
and
v_{1}\leq_{1}v_{2}
,

b)

v_{1}\leq_{3}v_{2}
if
v_{1},v_{2}\in\Omega_{2}
and
v_{1}\leq_{2}v_{2}
,

c)

v_{1}\leq_{3}v_{2}
if
v_{1}\in\Omega_{1}
,
v_{2}\in\Omega_{2}
and
\ell_{1}(v_{1})\mathcal{R}\ell_{2}(v_{2})
.

Remark 4
.

Since there can be more than one element in a heap, labelled by the same piece, it is ambiguous to refer to elements of a heap by their labels. However, given a (not necessarily distinct) sequence of pieces
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}
, then the heap

\beta_{1}\circ\ldots\circ\beta_{m}

is well-defined.

For a heap
H=(\Omega,\leq,\ell)
, let
|H|
be the number of elements in the poset
\Omega
and let
\mathcal{M}(H):=\mathcal{M}(\Omega)
be the maximal elements in the poset.

Remark 5
.

Given heaps
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
, we have
\mathcal{M}(H_{1}\circ H_{2})\subseteq\mathcal{M}(H_{1})\cup\mathcal{M}(H_{2})
.

Let
\mathbbm{t}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R})
be the set of
trivial heaps
, i.e., heaps where any pair of pieces have non-concurrent labels. Let
\mathbbm{p}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{R})
be the set of
pyramids
, i.e., heaps with a unique maximal piece.

be a multi-digraph. Let
e\in E(D)
be a fixed edge. Then, the mapping
\textup{cs}:\mathcal{T}^{e}(D)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(D)^{*}
is an injection, and moreover,
e\in E(\beta_{m})
.

Proof.

We prove, by induction on
m
, that
\textup{cs}(\mathbf{w}^{1})=\textup{cs}(\mathbf{w}^{2})=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})
implies
\mathbf{w}^{1}=\mathbf{w}^{2}
, for any two closed trails
\mathbf{w}^{1},\mathbf{w}^{2}
ending at
e
.

For the base case let
m=1
. If
\textup{cs}(\mathbf{w}^{1})=\beta=\textup{cs}(\mathbf{w}^{2})
, then
\mathbf{w}^{1}=\mathbf{w}^{2}
, since there is a unique trail ending at
e
with edge-set
E(\beta)
. For the inductive hypothesis, assume that for each
m\geq 1
and for all trails
\mathbf{w}^{1},\mathbf{w}^{2}
ending at
e
, if
\textup{cs}(\mathbf{w}^{1})=\textup{cs}(\mathbf{w}^{2})=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1})
then
\mathbf{w}^{1}=\mathbf{w}^{2}
. For the inductive step, let
m\geq 2
and assume that
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{m})
is the cycle sequence of both
\mathbf{w}^{1}
and
\mathbf{w}^{2}
. Then, by the definition of a cycle sequence, we have some closed subtrails
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{1},\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}
such that
\beta_{1}=[\mathbf{w}_{1}^{1}]_{\rho}=[\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}]_{\rho}
and

\mathbf{w}^{1}=\alpha^{1}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{1}\gamma^{1}\text{ and }\mathbf{w}^{2}=\alpha^{2}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}\gamma^{2}

where
\alpha^{i}
, for
i=1,2
, is a path (possibly of length zero), and
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{1}^{i}=\alpha^{i}\gamma^{i}
is a closed subtrail ending at
e
, with
\textup{cs}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{1}^{i})=(\beta_{2},\beta_{3},\ldots,\beta_{m})
. By the inductive hypothesis,
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{1}^{1}=\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{1}^{2}
. For each
i=1,2
, let
v_{i}
be the first vertex that
\mathbf{w}^{i}
repeats, which is also the terminal vertex of
\alpha^{i}
. Suppose, for a contradiction, that
\alpha^{1}\neq\alpha^{2}
. Assume, without loss of generality,
\alpha^{1}\subsetneq\alpha^{2}
. In particular,
v_{1}\in V(\alpha^{2})\setminus V(\alpha^{1})
and
v_{1}\neq v_{2}
. But then, in the subtrail
\alpha^{2}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}
of
\mathbf{w}^{2}
, the second occurrence of
v_{1}
in
\alpha^{2}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}
is before the second occurrence of
v_{2}
, which contradicts the definition of
v_{2}
.

Hence,
\alpha^{1}=\alpha^{2}
, which also implies
\gamma^{1}=\gamma^{2}
. In particular, the terminal vertex of
\alpha^{1}=\alpha^{2}
is
v_{1}=v_{2}
. On the cycle
\beta_{1}
, there is a unique edge
\widetilde{e}
with terminal vertex
v_{1}
. Since
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{1},\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}
are both trails with cycle sequence
\beta_{1}
, ending at
\widetilde{e}
, the basis step implies
\mathbf{w}_{1}^{1}=\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}
. Therefore,
\mathbf{w}_{1}=\alpha^{1}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{1}\gamma^{1}=\alpha^{2}\mathbf{w}_{1}^{2}\gamma^{2}=\mathbf{w}_{2}
.
∎

Given a sequence
\vec{b}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})\in\mathcal{B}(D)^{*}
and an edge
e\in E(\beta_{m})
, it is not necessarily the case that the sequence in question is the cycle sequence of a closed trail of

, ending at
e
. To determine if
\vec{b}
is in the image
\mathrm{im}\left(\textup{cs}\big|_{\mathcal{T}^{e}(D)}\right)
, we propose a shortcut algorithm: Let
v
be the terminal vertex of
e
and let
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{m}:=\beta_{m}(v)
. For each

, construct
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{i}=\beta_{i}\cdots\beta_{m-1}\cdot\beta_{m}(v)
by the insertion operation. Then, test if
\textup{cs}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}_{i})=(\beta_{i},\ldots,\beta_{m})
. If not, stop and conclude that

does not correspond to a closed walk ending at

Let

3.1.4
The Harary-Sachs Theorem for Hypergraphs

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple hypergraph of order
n
and rank
k\geq 2
.

Definition 10

(The Characteristic Polynomial of a Hypergraph)
.

The (normalized)
adjacency hypermatrix

\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{H}}=(a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}})
of
\mathcal{H}
is the
n
-dimensional symmetric
k
-hypermatrix with entries

a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}=\begin{cases}\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle(k-1)!}&\text{ if }\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\}\in E(\mathcal{H})\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The Lagrangian of
\mathcal{H}
is the polynomial

F_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}=1}^{n}a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{k}}

Letting
\nabla
denote the
n
-dimensional gradient
\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\rightarrow\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we define the characteristic polynomial as the
n
-dimensional resultant:

\textup{res}\nabla(t\cdot F_{\mathcal{I}}-F_{\mathcal{H}})

where
\mathcal{I}=(\delta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}})
is the
n
-dimensional identity hypermatrix, with entries
\delta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }i_{1}=\ldots=i_{k}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}
.

As shown in
[
22
, Proposition 1, p. 1306]
, the degree of the characteristic polynomial is

N:=\deg(\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(t))=n(k-1)^{n-1}

By
[
9
, p. 95, Theorem 3.1]
, we have the following property of the
n
-dimensional resultant: Given homogenous polynomials
F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}
of degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}
, respectively,

\text{res}(t\cdot F_{1},\ldots,t\cdot F_{n})=t^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{1}\cdots d_{i-1}\cdot d_{i+1}\cdots d_{n}}\cdot\text{res}(F_{1},\ldots,F_{n})

Putting
d_{i}=k-1
for each
i=1,\ldots,n
, we have:

\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\text{res}\nabla(t\cdot F_{\mathcal{I}}-F_{\mathcal{H}})=\text{res}\nabla(t\cdot(F_{\mathcal{I}}-t^{-1}\cdot F_{\mathcal{H}}))=t^{n(k-1)^{n-1}}\cdot\text{res}\nabla(F_{\mathcal{I}}-t^{-1}\cdot F_{\mathcal{H}})

In particular, we have
\widetilde{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=t^{-N}\cdot\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\text{res}\nabla(F_{\mathcal{I}}-t^{-1}\cdot F_{\mathcal{H}})
.

We state the Harary-Sachs Theorem from
[
7
]
:

Theorem 3.1

(Harary-Sachs Theorem for Hypergraphs)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. The codegree
d
coefficient of the characteristic polynomial, i.e., the coefficient of
t^{N-d}
, where
N
is the degree of the characteristic polynomial, is calculated as

[t^{N-d}](\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(t))=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{V}_{d}^{\infty}}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(H)}C_{H}(\#H\subseteq\mathcal{H})

In particular,

\widetilde{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{V}^{\infty}}t^{-|E(H)|}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(H)}C_{H}(\#H\subseteq\mathcal{H})

3.3
Heaps of Pieces on the Characteristic Polynomial of a Simple Hypergraph

The weight of a heap is defined as the product of the weights of its pieces. To show that infragraphs can be taken as pieces, we need to show that the weight function we intend to use is multiplicative over disjoint components. First, we show that for a fixed simple host graph
\mathcal{H}
, the value of
\Delta(n,X)
is multiplicative, where
n\geq 0
and
X\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
X:=X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}
be an infragraph of a host hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, where
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are disjoint. Then, we have

\Delta(n,X)=\Delta(n,X_{1})\cdot\Delta(n,X_{2})

where
n\geq 0
is a non-negative integer.

Proof.

We have a bijection,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{1})\times\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{2})

\displaystyle\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2})

\displaystyle(\mathbf{S}_{1},\mathbf{S}_{2})

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{1}\sqcup\mathbf{S}_{2}:=[\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m},B_{1},\ldots,B_{r}\}]_{\mathord{\sim}}

where
\mathbf{S}_{1}=[\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}\}]_{\mathord{\sim}}
and
\mathbf{S}_{2}=[\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{r}\}]_{\mathord{\sim}}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Delta(n,X)=\sum_{\mathbf{S}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{1}\sqcup X_{2})}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(\mathbf{S})}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}}}C_{\mathbf{S}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{S}_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{1})\\
\mathbf{S}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{2})\end{subarray}}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(\mathbf{S}_{1}\sqcup\mathbf{S}_{2})}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}_{1}\sqcup\mathbf{S}_{2}}}C_{\mathbf{S}_{1}\sqcup\mathbf{S}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{S}_{1}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{1})\\
\mathbf{S}_{2}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X_{2})\end{subarray}}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(\mathbf{S}_{1})+c(\mathbf{S}_{2})}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}_{1}}}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}_{2}}}C_{\mathbf{S}_{1}}C_{\mathbf{S}_{2}}

where we used the multiplicativity of the associated coefficient.

We are ready to apply the Heaps of Pieces framework on hypergraphs. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple hypergraph of rank

, with
|V(\mathcal{H})|=n
vertices.

We take connected infragraphs as the set of pieces:

\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}):=\textup{Inf}^{1}(\mathcal{H})

Two pieces

and
X_{2}
are

(denoted

) if and only if they share a vertex.

Theorem 3.5
.

Given a simple hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
with
n

can be expressed as a sum over trivial heaps:

\widetilde{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\sum_{T\in\mathbbm{t}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\mathcal{R})}(-1)^{|T|}w_{n}(T)

Proof.

The weight of a piece

is given by,

w_{n}(X)=-\Delta(n,X)t^{-|E(X)|}=-t^{-|E(X)|}\sum_{\mathbf{S}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X)}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(\mathbf{S})}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}}}C_{\mathbf{S}}

\displaystyle w_{n}(Y):=\prod_{i=1}^{r}w_{n}(X_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{r}-\Delta(n,X_{i})t^{-|E(X_{i})|}=(-1)^{c(Y)}t^{-|E(Y)|}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Delta(n,X_{i})

by

In particular, we have
t^{-|E(Y)|}\Delta(n,Y)=(-1)^{c(Y)}w_{n}(Y)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\sum_{Y\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{H})}t^{-|E(Y)|}\Delta(n,Y)=\sum_{T\in\mathbbm{t}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}))}(-1)^{|T|}w_{n}(T)

by

As we will see in the next section, there are only finitely many non-zero contributions to this sum, since a connected infragraph with more edges than the degree of the characteristic polynomial has zero weight (cf.

We consider a variation of

Definition 13

(Adjacency Hypermatrix with edge-variables)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple hypergraph on
n

k\geq 2

0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Definition 14

(Characteristic Polynomial with edge-variables)

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a simple hypergraph and let

be an indeterminate. The

with edge-variables
is the resultant

\textup{res}\nabla(t\cdot\widehat{F}_{\mathcal{I}}-\widehat{F}_{\mathcal{H}})

where
\widehat{F}_{\mathcal{H}}

(Definition

).

Let

Theorem 3.6

(Harary-Sachs Theorem with edge-variables)
.

Given a simple hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
, with edge-set
E(\mathcal{H})=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
and order

be the degree of the characteristic polynomial
\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)

\displaystyle[t^{N-d}]\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}_{d}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{S}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X)}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(\mathbf{S})}\dfrac{1}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}}}C_{\mathbf{S}}\right)=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}_{d}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}\Delta(n,X)

In other words,

\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t):=t^{-N}\cdot\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}t^{-|E(X)|}\Delta(n,X)

Proof.

Let
\widehat{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathcal{H}}=(a_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}})
be the edge-variable adjacency hypermatrix of
\mathcal{H}
. Let

be the vector with entries

Let
A
be an auxiliary matrix with distinct variables

as entries. For each

\partial{f}_{i}
by replacing

with
\partial/\partial A_{ij}
, for

and thereby obtain
\partial{f}=[\partial f_{1}\cdots\partial f_{n}]^{T}
(cf. the example below).

By
[
19

and
[
7

, we have a formula for the generalized trace:

\operatorname{Tr}_{d}(\mathcal{H})=(k-1)^{n-1}\sum_{d_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}=d}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{f}_{i}^{d_{i}}}{\left(d_{i}(k-1)\right)!}\operatorname{tr}\left(A^{d(k-1)}\right)\right)

The rest of the proof is almost identical to that of the characteristic polynomial without edge-variables: For any differential operator
\hat{g}
, we have
\hat{g}\operatorname{tr}A^{d}(k-1)\neq 0
if and only if
\hat{g}=\partial{f}_{D_{R}}

. So, the equation turns into:

\operatorname{Tr}_{d}(\mathcal{H})=d(k-1)^{n}\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}^{1}_{d}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}\sum_{R\in\mathfrak{R}(X)}\frac{\displaystyle|\mathfrak{C}(D_{R})|}{\displaystyle\prod_{v\in D_{R}}\deg^{-}(v)!}=d(k-1)^{n}\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}^{1}_{d}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}C_{X}

We exponentiate and obtain,

\displaystyle[t^{N-d}](\phi_{\mathcal{H}}(t))=\sum_{m=1}^{d}\sum_{d_{1}+\cdots+d_{m}=d}\frac{1}{m!}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{-\operatorname{Tr}_{d_{i}}(\mathcal{H})}{d_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}_{d}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}\sum_{\mathbf{S}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}(X)}(-(k-1)^{n})^{c(\mathbf{S})}\dfrac{C_{\mathbf{S}}}{\alpha_{\mathbf{S}}}

by

and Kocay’s Lemma (

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}_{d}(\mathcal{H})}\mathbf{e}^{X}\Delta(n,X)

∎

Spanning Subgraphs and the Characteristic Polynomial with Edge-Variables

Proposition 3.7
.

Let a simple hypergraph

\mathcal{H}=(V(\mathcal{H}),E(\mathcal{H}))=([n],\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}\})

and a spanning subgraph
\mathcal{G}
with the same vertex set,

\mathcal{G}=(V(\mathcal{G}),E(\mathcal{G}))=([n],\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\})\text{ where }r\leq t

\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{G}}(t)(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})=\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r},0,\ldots,0)

Proof.

By the Harary Sachs Theorem with edge-variables, we have

\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)(e_{1},\ldots,e_{t})=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{H})}t^{-|E(X)|}\Delta(|V(\mathcal{H})|,X)e_{1}^{m_{X}(e_{1})}\cdots e_{t}^{m_{X}(e_{t})}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{H}}(t)(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r},0,\ldots,0)=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{H})}t^{-|E(X)|}\Delta(|V(\mathcal{H})|,X)e_{1}^{m_{X}(e_{1})}\cdots e_{r}^{m_{X}(e_{r})}0^{m_{X}(e_{r+1})}\cdots 0^{m_{X}(e_{t})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{H})\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{X\in\textup{Inf}(\mathcal{G})}t^{-|E(X)|}\Delta(|V(\mathcal{H})|,X)e_{1}^{m_{X}(e_{1})}\cdots e_{r}^{m_{X}(e_{r})}=\widehat{\phi}_{\mathcal{G}}(t)(e_{1},\ldots,e_{r})

∎

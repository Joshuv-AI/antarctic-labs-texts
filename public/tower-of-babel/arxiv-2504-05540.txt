Tail probability of maximal displacement in critical and subcritical branching stable processes
Haojie Hou, Yiyang Jiang, Yan-Xia Ren, and, Renming Song

Abstract
In this paper, we study critical and subcritical branching α \alpha -stable processes, α ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \alpha\in(0,2) . We obtain the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tails of the maximal positions of all subcritical branching α \alpha -stable processes with positive jumps. In the case of subcritical branching spectrally negative α \alpha -stable processes, we obtain the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tails of the maximal positions under the assumption that the offspring distributions satisfy the L ​ log ⁡ L L\log L condition. For critical branching α \alpha -stable processes, we obtain the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tails under the assumption that the offspring distributions belong to the domain of attraction of a γ \gamma -distribution, γ ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] \gamma\in(1,2] .



Keywords—
Branching stable process; rightmost position; integral equation

60J80; 60G52; 60G51; 60G70.

1
Introduction and main results

1.1
Introduction

A branching Lévy process is a continuous-time Markov process defined as follows. At time 0, there is a particle at
x\in\mathbb{R}
and it moves according to a Lévy process
(\xi_{t},\mathbf{P}_{x})
. After an exponential time with parameter
1
, independent of the motion, it dies and produces
k
offspring with probability
p_{k}
,
k\geqslant 0
. The offspring move independently according to
\xi
from the place where they are born and obey the same branching mechanism as their parent. This procedure goes on. For
t\geqslant 0
, let
N_{t}
be the collection of particles alive at time
t
. For
u\in N_{t}
, we use
X_{u}(t)
to denote the position of the particle
u
at time
t
. The point process
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
defined by

Z_{t}:=\sum_{u\in N_{t}}\delta_{X_{u}(t)},\hskip 18.49988ptt\geqslant 0,

is called a branching Lévy process. We will denote the law of
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
and write
\mathbb{P}
for
\mathbb{P}_{0}
for simplicity.
When
\xi
is a Brownian motion,
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is called a branching Brownian motion. When
\xi
is an
\alpha
-stable process,
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is called a branching
\alpha
-stable process.

Let
m:=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}kp_{k}
be the mean of the offspring distribution. When
m>1(=1,<1)
, we say that
the branching Lévy process
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is supercritical (critical, subcritical). It is well known that in the critical and subcritical cases,
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
will die out in finite time with probability 1. Thus in this case we can define the maximal position of
(Z_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
by

M:=\sup_{t>0}\sup_{u\in N_{t}}X_{u}(t).

When
m=1
and
\xi
is a standard Brownian motion, Sawyer and Fleischman
[
17
]
proved that, under the assumption
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{2}\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)=\frac{6}{\sigma^{2}},

(1)

where
\sigma^{2}
is the variance of the offspring distribution. Profeta
[
13
]
extended the result above to some
critical branching spectrally negative Lévy processes under the same third moment condition on the offspring distribution. When
m=1
,
\xi
is an
\alpha
-stable process with positive jumps and
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
, Lalley and Shao
[
10
]
, and Profeta
[
12
]

proved that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{\alpha/2}\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)=c_{1}(\alpha),

(2)

where
c_{1}(\alpha)
is an explicit positive constant. For critical branching spectrally negative
\alpha
-stable processes
with

\alpha\in(1,2)
, Profeta
[
13
, Corollary 1.3]
proved that,
when
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
,

\displaystyle 0<\liminf_{x\to\infty}x^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)\leqslant\limsup_{x\to\infty}x^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)<\infty.

(3)

When
m<1
and
\xi
is a standard Brownian motion, Sawyer and Fleischman
[
17
]
proved that, under the assumption
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
, there exists a function
c(\cdot)
bounded between two positive constants such that

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)}{(1-m)c(x)e^{-\sqrt{2(1-m)}x}}=1.

(4)

For subcritical branching
\alpha
-stable processes with positive jumps,
Profeta
[
12
]
proved that,
under the assumption
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to+\infty}x^{\alpha}\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)=\frac{c_{2}(\alpha)}{1-m}

(5)

for some explicit constant
c_{2}(\alpha)
. For subcritical branching spectrally negative processes,
Profeta
[
13
, Theorem 1]

proved that, under the assumption
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k^{3}p_{k}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}e^{c_{4}x}\mathbb{P}\left(M\geqslant x\right)=c_{3}

(6)

for some explicit positive constants
c_{3},c_{4}
.

For results on the asymptotic behaviors of the tails of maximal positions of (sub)critical branching random walks, see
[
9
,
11
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to
establish the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tails of the maximal positions of (sub)critical branching
\alpha
-stable processes under minimal conditions on the offspring distributions. More precisely, we will obtain (i) the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tails of the maximal positions of subcritical branching
\alpha
-stable processes with positive jumps without any extra assumption on the offspring distributions; (ii) the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tail of the maximal positions of subcritical branching spectrally negative
\alpha
-stable processes under the assumption that the offspring distributions satisfy the
L\log L
condition; (iii) the exact asymptotic behaviors of the tails of critical branching
\alpha
-stable processes under the assumption that the offspring distributions belong to the domain of attraction of a
\gamma
-distribution,
\gamma\in(1,2]
.

1.2
Main results

Before we state our main results, we recall some useful facts about stable processes.
In this paper, we always assume that the spatial motion
\xi

\alpha
-stable process,
\alpha\in(0,2)

\alpha
-stable process, see, for instance,

Let

[

\Psi(\theta)=\begin{cases}c_{*}|\theta|^{\alpha}\left(1-\mathrm{i}\beta\tan\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\theta\right),&\text{ for }\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)\text{ and }\beta\in[-1,1],\\

Note that, for (strictly) 1-stable process,

(see, for example,
[

It follows from
[

) that, when

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{\alpha}\mathbf{P}_{0}(\xi_{1}\geqslant x)=\nu_{\alpha}((1,\infty))=\frac{c_{+}}{\alpha}.

(8)

When

\alpha\in(0,1)

A spectrally negative 1-stable process reduces to the drift

\alpha
-stable processes,
exclude the above cases and assume

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{0}(e^{\lambda\xi_{1}})=\exp\left\{C_{1}(\alpha)\lambda^{\alpha}\right\},\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{where}\hskip 9.24994ptC_{1}(\alpha)=-\Psi(-\mathrm{i}).

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\left(e^{-\lambda\tau_{y}}\right)=\exp\left\{-C_{1}(\alpha)^{-1/\alpha}\lambda^{1/\alpha}(y-x)\right\}.

(10)

In the critical case, we will need the following assumption on the offspring distribution:

(H)

The offspring distribution
\{p_{k}:k\geqslant 0\}
belongs to the domain of attraction of a
\gamma
-stable,
\gamma\in(1,2]

More precisely, either there exist
\gamma\in(1,2)

Recall that
\sigma^{2}
is the variance of the offspring distribution. We define

\displaystyle C_{2}(\gamma):=\begin{cases}\frac{\Gamma(2-\gamma)}{\gamma-1}\kappa_{\gamma}&\text{ for }\gamma\in(1,2);\\
\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}&\text{ for }\gamma=2.\end{cases}

(11)

Orbital Altermagnetism
Mingxiang Pan, Feng Liu, Huaqing Huang

Abstract
We introduce the concept of orbital altermagnetism , a symmetry-protected magnetic order of pure orbital degrees of freedom. It is characterized with ordered anti-parallel orbital magnetic moments in real space but momentum-dependent orbital band splittings, analogous to spin altermagnetism. Using a minimal tight-binding model with complex hoppings in a square-kagome lattice, we show that such order inherently arises from staggered loop currents, producing a d d -wave-like orbital-momentum locking. First-principles calculations show that orbital altermagnetism emerges independent of spin ordering in in-plane ferromagnets of CuBr 2 and VS 2 , so that it can be unambiguously identified experimentally. On the other hand, it may also coexist with spin altermagnetism, such as in monolayer MoO and CrO. The orbital altermagnetism offers an alternative platform for symmetry-driven magnetotransport and orbital-based spintronics, as exemplified by large nonlinear current-induced orbital magnetization.

Discussion and summary
—Recent studies have shown that weak orbital ferromagnetism or ferrimagnetism can emerge in altermagnets
Šmejkal et al. 2020
;
Bozorth and Walsh 1958
;
Koizumi et al. 2019
;
Jo et al. 2025
via SOC, arising from alternating
g
-tensor anisotropy or noncollinear magnetic moments. Such states are classified as M-type altermagnets
Cheong and Huang 2025
;
Cheong and Huang 2024
, which—in absence of SOC—exhibit perfect spin compensation, but develop a finite net magnetization from orbital magnetization and spin canting in the presence of SOC, and belong to ferromagnetic point groups.
In contrast, our work reveals an inversed scenario: systems where orbital magnetic moments display a symmetry-protected altermagnetic order, yet the spin sector remains uncompensated (finite net spin). This concept naturally extends to spin ferrimagnets and noncollinear magnets belonging to ferromagnetic point groups. Moreover, the orbital altermagnetic state can, in principle, be tuned by spin reorientation (which changes the magnetic group symmetry) or by electric fields via orbital magnetoelectric coupling, offering new avenues for controlling altermagnetic responses.

Overall, our findings identify orbital altermagnetism as a broad and robust class of magnetic order—one that is distinct from, yet intertwined with, spin altermagnetism—opening a pathway to novel symmetry-driven magnetotransport phenomena and electrically tunable orbital-based spintronic devices. Experimentally, the significant orbital magnetization establishes giant current-induced orbital polarization as a transport signature of orbital altermagnetism and suggests that orbital degrees of freedom may play a dominant role in certain magnetic responses. In addition to conventional magnetic materials studied here, the orbital altermagnetism is also expected to be achieved from the orbital antiferromagnetism in correlated systems, such as cuprates and cerium hexaboride CeB

Appendix A
Formula of Orbital Magnetization in Reciprocal and Real Spaces

Orbital magnetism arises from the motion of electrons in atomic orbitals or Bloch states and plays a crucial role in diverse magnetic and transport phenomena, particularly in systems where spin contributions are suppressed or symmetry-forbidden. Unlike spin magnetic moments—typically described by exchange interactions or Zeeman coupling—orbital magnetic moments originate from the circulation of charge in real or momentum space and are intrinsically tied to the geometric and topological properties of electronic wavefunctions.

From a theoretical standpoint, the orbital magnetic moment of a Bloch electron in band
n
at wavevector
{\bm{k}}
can be expressed as the expectation value of the self-rotation of a wave packet about its center of mass. This is formalized in the modern theory of orbital magnetization, which extends semiclassical concepts into a gauge-invariant quantum framework. In two dimensions, only the out-of-plane component is well defined. At zero temperature in a two-dimensional (2D) periodic crystal, the total orbital magnetization is given by

;

Resta et al. 2005
;

is a topological contribution proportional to the Chern number
\mathcal{C}
(quantized when
\mu
is in the bulk gap), which is finite only in systems with broken time-reversal symmetry and nontrivial topology.

To resolve orbital-momentum locking in momentum space for 2D systems with trivial topology (
\mathcal{C}=0
), we employ the band- and
{\bm{k}}
-resolved expression of the orbital magnetic moment

;
Thonhauser 2011
;
Xiao et al. 2010
;
Ceresoli et al. 2006

{M}_{n{\bm{k}}}=\frac{e}{2\hbar c}\textnormal{Im}\langle{\partial}_{\bm{k}}u_{n{\bm{k}}}|\times\left(H_{\bm{k}}-\epsilon_{n{\bm{k}}}\right)|{\partial}_{\bm{k}}u_{n{\bm{k}}}\rangle,

(3)

In two dimensions,
M_{n{\bm{k}}}
is along the out-of-plane direction and is directly related to the orbital angular momentum via

M_{n{\bm{k}}}=-g_{L}\mu_{B}L_{z}({\bm{k}}),

(4)

where
\mu_{B}
is the Bohr magneton and
g_{L}=1
the Landé g-factor
Bhowal and Vignale 2021
.

To characterize orbital magnetization in systems lacking full translational symmetry (e.g., under open boundary conditions or disorder), a real-space formulation is often more appropriate. Analogous to the above
{\bm{k}}
-space integration, orbital magnetization and its constituent terms can also be expressed as a real-space integration
M=\frac{1}{A}\int_{A}d{\bm{r}}m({\bm{r}})
, which has the form of a real-space average over some region with an area
A
. Following Refs.
Bianco and Resta 2013
;
Marrazzo and Resta 2017
;
Wang et al. 2022
, the corresponding orbital magnetization density

can be written as a sum of gauge-invariant local markers:

\begin{split}m({\bm{r}})&=m_{\textnormal{LC}}({\bm{r}})+m_{\textnormal{IC}}({\bm{r}})+m_{\textnormal{BC}}({\bm{r}}),\\
m_{\textnormal{LC}}({\bm{r}})&=\frac{e}{\hbar c}\textnormal{Im}\langle{\bm{r}}|PxQHQyP|{\bm{r}}\rangle,\\
m_{\textnormal{IC}}({\bm{r}})&=-\frac{e}{\hbar c}\textnormal{Im}\langle{\bm{r}}|QxPHPyQ|{\bm{r}}\rangle,\\
m_{\textnormal{BC}}({\bm{r}})&=-\mu\frac{2e}{\hbar c}\textnormal{Im}\langle{\bm{r}}|QxPyQ|{\bm{r}}\rangle,\end{split}

(5)

where
P
is the ground-state projector and
Q=1-P
its complement:

P=\sum_{\epsilon_{i}\leq\mu}|\varphi_{i}\rangle\langle\varphi_{i}|,\qquad H|\varphi_{i}\rangle=\epsilon_{i}|\varphi_{i}\rangle.

(6)

This formalism provides a position-resolved, gauge-invariant description of orbital magnetization, enabling direct evaluation of orbital textures in both tight-binding and first-principles frameworks.

In the main text, we apply this approach to the minimal tight-binding model of orbital altermagnetism. The resulting real-space orbital magnetization
m({\bm{r}})
, computed from Eq. (

Appendix B
Magnetic octupole and Magnetic Toroidal quadrupole

In both theoretical models and real magnetic materials, orbital magnetic moments can often be interpreted as arising from local loop currents. Accordingly, the magnetic (M) multipoles and magnetic toroidal (MT) multipoles are defined from the explicit current distribution. Previous studies
Hayami and Kusunose 2024
have established that imaginary hopping in tight-binding models corresponds to MT multipoles, thereby linking orbital magnetism intrinsically to these multipoles.

Magnetic multipoles
M_{lm}
and magnetic toroidal multipoles
T_{lm}
exhibit distinct transformation properties under spatial inversion
\mathcal{P}
and time-reversal symmetry
\mathcal{T}

Hayami et al. 2018
:

M_{lm}\ \xrightarrow{(\mathcal{P,T})}\ [(-1)^{l+1},-1],\quad T_{lm}\ \xrightarrow{(\mathcal{P,T})}\ [(-1)^{l},-1].

From these transformation rules, it follows that collinear altermagnets can uniquely host MT quadrupoles or M octupoles, which are absent in conventional ferromagnets or antiferromagnets.

The explicit definitions are given as
Hayami and Kusunose 2018
:

M_{lm}=\frac{1}{c(l+1)}\int dr\,[\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{j}_{e}(\mathbf{r})]\cdot\nabla O_{lm}(\mathbf{r}),

(7)

T_{lm}=\frac{1}{c(l+1)}\int dr\,[\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{j}_{e}(\mathbf{r})]\,O_{lm}(\mathbf{r}),

(8)

where
\mathbf{j}_{e}(\mathbf{r})
is the electric current density, and

O_{lm}(\mathbf{r})=\sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2l+1}}\,r^{l}Y^{*}_{lm}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})

is expressed in terms of the spherical harmonics
Y_{lm}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})
, with
l
and
m
denoting the azimuthal and magnetic quantum numbers, respectively.

For the minimal model defined in Eq. (
1
), we reveal that
T_{xy}\neq 0
and
M_{z}^{\beta}\neq 0
(with
O_{z}^{\beta}=\frac{\sqrt{15}}{2}z(x^{2}-y^{2}))
, consistent with the allowed multipoles of the corresponding magnetic point group
Yatsushiro et al. 2021
. Particularly, the nonzero magnetic octupole
M_{z}^{\beta}
can serve as a secondary order parameter in the Landau theory of the
d
-wave altermagnetism
McClarty and Rau 2024
.
The nonzero
T_{xy}
can also be directly traced back to the imaginary hopping terms in the tight-binding model. By evaluating the symmetry-adapted multipole basis (SAMB) for the bond cluster
Kusunose et al. 2023
, we find that in the D
4h
point group, only the irreducible representation B
2g
yields a non-zero toroidal quadrupole, which precisely corresponds to
T_{xy}
.

Intrinsic nonlinear current-induced spin and orbital magnetization

In this section, we briefly introduce the formalism of the intrinsic nonlinear Edelstein effect.
For a 2D system with either
C_{2z}
or
\mathcal{P}
symmetry, the linear spin and orbital responses are forbidden by symmetry. In such cases, the leading contribution arises at the second order:

\delta X_{a}=\chi^{X}_{abc}E_{b}E_{c},

(9)

where
X_{a}
denotes the
a
-th component of either the orbital angular momentum
L_{a}
or the spin angular momentum
S_{a}
. The nonlinear response tensor

can be decomposed according to its parity under time-reversal symmetry

;
Xiao et al. 2023
;
Baek et al. 2024

\begin{split}\chi_{abc}^{\mathrm{even}}&=\tau\sum_{n}\int[d{\bm{k}}]f_{0}\partial_{c}\gamma^{n}_{ab},\\
\chi_{abc}^{\mathrm{odd}}&=\frac{1}{2}\int[d{\bm{k}}]\sum_{n}\Bigg[\Lambda_{abc,n}^{X}f_{0}-(X_{a,n}G_{bc,n}-v_{b,n}\mathscr{G}_{ac,n}-v_{c,n}\mathscr{G}_{ab,n})f^{\prime}_{0}\Bigg].\end{split}

(10)

Here
[d{\bm{k}}]\equiv d{\bm{k}}/(2\pi)^{d}
is a shorthand notation for integration over the Brillouin zone in
d

f_{0}
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution with unperturbed band energy
\epsilon_{n}
, and
f^{\prime}_{0}
is its derivative. The quantity
X_{a,n}=\langle u_{n}|\hat{X}_{a}|u_{n}\rangle
is the intraband matrix element of spin or orbital angular momentum, and
v_{a,n}
is the intraband velocity matrix element.

The Zeeman-like field
\bm{h}
couples to the spin or orbital degree of freedom in the form of
-\bm{\hat{s}}\cdot\bm{h}
or
-\bm{\hat{L}}\cdot\bm{h}

h

h
-space Berry connection is defined as
\mathfrak{A}_{i}=\langle u_{n}({\bm{k}})|i\partial_{h_{i}}|u_{n}({\bm{k}})\rangle
. The Berry connection polarizability (BCP) and the
h
-space BCP are defined as

\begin{split}G_{ab,n}&=2\textnormal{Re}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{v_{a,nm}v_{b,mn}}{(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{3}},\\
\mathscr{G}_{ab,n}&=-2\textnormal{Re}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{s_{a,nm}v_{b,mn}}{(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{3}},\end{split}

(11)

where the numerator involves the interband matrix elements of spin and velocity operators,
s_{a,nm}
and
v_{a,nm}

The anomalous spin polarizability (ASP) is given by

\gamma^{n}_{ab}({\bm{k}})=2\textnormal{Im}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{s_{a,nm}v_{b,mn}}{(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{2}}.

(12)

The second-order response kernel
\Lambda_{abc,n}^{X}
is given by

\begin{split}\Lambda_{abc,n}^{X}=-2\textnormal{Re}&\sum_{m\neq n}\Bigg[\frac{3v_{b,nm}v_{c,mn}(X_{a,nn}-X_{a,mm})}{(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{4}}-\frac{(\partial_{b}X_{a})_{nm}v_{c,mn}+(\partial_{c}X_{a})_{nm}v_{b,mn}}{(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{3}}\\
-&\sum_{l\neq n}\frac{(v_{b,lm}v_{c,mn}+v_{c,lm}v_{b,mn})X_{a,nl}}{(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{l})(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{3}}-\sum_{l\neq m}\frac{(v_{b,ln}v_{c,nm}+v_{c,ln}v_{b,nm})X_{a,ml}}{(\epsilon_{m}-\epsilon_{l})(\epsilon_{n}-\epsilon_{m})^{3}}\Bigg].\end{split}

(13)

Finally, the orbital angular momentum (OAM) matrix elements follow from the modern theory of orbital magnetization

\begin{split}\langle u_{n}|\hat{\bm{L}}|u_{m}\rangle&=i\frac{e\hbar^{2}}{4g_{L}\mu_{B}}\sum_{q\neq n,m}(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{q}-\epsilon_{n}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{q}-\epsilon_{m}})\cdot\langle u_{n}|\hat{\bm{v}}|u_{q}\rangle\times\langle u_{q}|\hat{\bm{v}}|u_{m}\rangle.\end{split}

(14)

The diagonal element of the orbital angular momentum
\bm{L}_{n}
is linearly related to the orbital magnetization. In 2D systems, its z-component corresponds to the out-of-plane component of the orbital magnetic moment via Eq. (
4
)
Bhowal and Vignale 2021

Appendix D
Symmetry Analysis of Orbital Altermagnetism in Two Dimensions

D.1
Definition and symmetry constraints of 2D orbital altermagnetism

We define a 2D orbital altermagnet as a system whose real space can be partitioned into two sublattices carrying nonzero but opposite orbital magnetic moments. The sublattices may consist either of atomic sites or of loop currents on bonds. Nonmagnetic systems or systems belonging to type-II magnetic space groups are excluded from this definition, since in such cases the orbital magnetization
\bm{M}({\bm{r}})
is symmetry-forced to vanish.

For orbital altermagnetism to occur, the two sublattices must be related by at least one symmetry operation, and
L_{z}({\bm{k}})
must not be symmetry-forced to vanish. In two dimensions,
L_{z}({\bm{k}})=0
is enforced when either
\mathcal{PT}
or
C_{2z}\mathcal{T}
symmetry is present. By contrast, symmetries such as
\tau\mathcal{T}
and
m_{z}\mathcal{T}
are compatible with orbital altermagnetism, as they only require
L_{z}
to be odd in momentum.

This situation differs from conventional 2D spin altermagnets without spin-orbit coupling (SOC) or complex hoppings, where these symmetries are absent.
Because the spin point group element
S_{2}=\{\mathcal{T}U_{n}(\pi)|1|0\}
(with
n
denoting an in-plane axis perpendicular to the spin) enforces
\bm{S}({\bm{k}})=\bm{S}(-{\bm{k}})
, whereas
\tau\mathcal{T}
and
m_{z}\mathcal{T}
require
\bm{S}({\bm{k}})=-\bm{S}(-{\bm{k}})
. Therefore, they effectively eliminate spin splitting. When SOC is introduced, the symmetry element
S_{2}
is no longer a symmetry of the system, removing the constraint and enabling the formation of a
p
-wave altermagnetism. However, in 2D systems with
\mathcal{P}
or
C_{2z}
symmetry, it remains necessary to break
\tau\mathcal{T}
and
m_{z}\mathcal{T}
to stabilize orbital altermagnetism.

\mathcal{PT}
and

,
2/m
,

{\bm{k}}\to-{\bm{k}}

D.3
Importance of
S_{2}
breaking for orbital altermagnetism in collinear structures

In the absence of SOC or complex hoppings, the appropriate framework to describe the symmetry of magnetic systems is the spin group, rather than the magnetic point group. For a collinear magnetic structure with the spin aligned along the
z
-axis, two types of symmetry operations leave the spin vector
\bm{S}
invariant
Xiao et al. 2024
:

S_{1}=\{U_{z}(\theta)|1|\bm{0}\},\,\theta\in[0,2\pi);\quad S_{2}=\{\mathcal{T}U_{xy}(\pi)|1|\bm{0}\}.

(15)

Here,
S_{1}
represents a rotation by angle
\theta
about the
z
-axis, while
S_{2}
represents a rotation
\pi
about an in-plane axis accompanied by time reversal
\mathcal{T}
.

The orbital magnetic moment satisfies the following equation
Etxebarria et al. 2025
:

M_{\mathrm{orb}}({\bm{r}})=\theta\det(R)R\cdot M_{\mathrm{orb}}(\{R|t\}^{-1}{\bm{r}}),

(16)

where
\theta=-1
if time-reversal
\mathcal{T}
is included in the operation, and
\theta=+1
otherwise.
R
represents a proper or improper rotation of the system,
t
represents a space translation, and
\det(R)
is the determinant of the matrix
R
. Since the real-space parts of
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are identical but differ in the spin-space operations (
S_{2}
includes time reversal
\mathcal{T}
while
S_{1}
does not), one finds that
M_{\mathrm{orb}}({\bm{r}})=-M_{\mathrm{orb}}({\bm{r}})=0
. Therefore, in a collinear system without SOC or complex hoppings, the orbital magnetic moment is symmetry-forbidden. In this sense, the operation
S_{2}
acts as an
effective time-reversal symmetry
for the orbital sector.

In momentum space,
S_{2}
enforces that the spin is even in momentum
\bm{S}(-{\bm{k}})=\bm{S}({\bm{k}})
, while the orbital angular momentum is odd
L_{z}(-{\bm{k}})=-L_{z}({\bm{k}})
. If the systems additionally respects either
C_{2z}
or inversion
\mathcal{P}
symmetry, which require
L_{z}({\bm{k}})=L_{z}(-{\bm{k}})
, then the orbital angular momentum must vanish identically:
L_{z}({\bm{k}})=0
.

By contrast, the inclusion of SOC or complex hopping explicitly breaks the
S_{2}
symmetry. This breaking removes the effective orbital time-reversal constraint, thereby allowing finite orbital magnetic moments
M_{\mathrm{orb}}({\bm{r}})
and nonvanishing orbital angular momentum
L_{z}({\bm{k}})
. This highlights that SOC or complex hoppings are essential for stabilizing orbital altermagnetism in collinear 2D systems.

An adaptive perfectly matched layer finite element method for acoustic-elastic interaction in periodic structures Journal: Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering
Sijia Li, Lei Lin, Junliang Lv

Abstract
This paper considers the scattering of a time-harmonic acoustic plane wave by an elastic body with an unbounded periodic surface. The original problem can be confined to the analysis of the fields in one periodic cell. With the help of the perfectly matched layer (PML) technique, we can truncate the unbounded physical domain into a bounded computational domain. By respectively constructing the equivalent transparent boundary conditions of acoustic and elastic waves simultaneously, the well-posedness and exponential convergence of the solution to the associated truncated PML problem are established. The finite element method is applied to solve the PML problem of acoustic-elastic interaction. To address the singularity caused by the non-smooth surface of the elastic body, we establish a residual-type a posteriori error estimate and develop an adaptive PML finite element algorithm. Several numerical examples are presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive algorithm.

The region above the surface is filled with a homogeneous compressible inviscid fluid medium, while the region below is occupied by an isotropic and linearly elastic solid material. Due to the external incident acoustic field, an elastic wave is excited inside the solid, and the incident acoustic wave is scattered back into the fluid. For this purpose, several effective truncation methods have been proposed, such as the perfectly matched layer (PML) technique

The fundamental principle of the PML method is to design a finite-thickness absorbing layer with suitably chosen complex coordinate stretching to surround the region of interest. Due to its ease of implementation and high computational efficiency, the PML method has been widely adopted for solving various wave scattering problems, such as acoustic waves

,

,

In practical applications, the grating profiles are often only piecewise smooth and may contain reentrant corners. Such geometric singularities usually lead to reduced regularity of the acoustic and elastic fields near the non-smooth surface, which will severely degrade the computational efficiency of uniform mesh refinement. To address this difficulty, adaptive finite element methods (AFEMs) provide an efficient numerical strategy, since they are able to concentrate computational effort only in regions where the solution exhibits singular behavior
[
2
]
. Combined with the PML technique, an efficient adaptive finite element method was firstly developed for solving the diffraction grating problem
[
16
]

It was shown that the a posteriori error estimate consists of the finite element discretization error and the PML truncation error, where the latter decays exponentially with respect to the PML parameters, such as the thickness of the layer and the medium properties. Due to its superior numerical behavior, the adaptive finite element PML method was quickly developed to solve a variety of scattering problems, such as obstacle scattering

Motivated by these considerations, the current paper develops and analyzes an adaptive perfectly matched layer finite element method for the acoustic-elastic interaction in periodic structures. We firstly derive a truncated PML formulation of acoustic-elastic interaction and establish its well-posedness. Moreover, we provide a rigorous convergence analysis showing that the PML approximation error decays exponentially with respect to the acoustic and elastic PML parameters simultaneously. Based on the truncated PML formulation, we then construct a residual-type a posteriori error estimator that explicitly incorporates the PML truncation error as well as the finite element discretization error. Utilizing the a posteriori error estimate, we propose an adaptive PML-FEM algorithm. Numerical experiments are also presented to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed adaptive algorithm, in particular for configurations with geometric singularities.

Problem Formulation

In this section, we introduce the original model and its corresponding weak formulation of the acoustic-elastic interaction problem in periodic structures.

2.1
Original model

Assume that the surface of the elastic solid is periodic in the
x_{1}
-axis with the period
\Lambda
. Due to the periodic structure of the elastic body, the problem
can be restricted into a single periodic cell, where

x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda)
.
Denote the surface of the elastic body by

\Gamma=\left\{\bm{x}=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}=f\left(x_{1}\right)\right\},

where
f
is a Lipschitz function with period
\Lambda>0
. Denote by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{u}}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}>f\left(x_{1}\right)\right\}

the domain above the periodic surface, which is filled with a homogeneous compressible inviscid fluid with a constant mass density
\rho_{f}>0
. The domain below the surface is denoted by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{d}}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}<f\left(x_{1}\right)\right\},

which is occupied by an isotropic homogeneous elastic solid body characterized by the mass density
\rho>0
and the Lam
é
constants
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mu>0
and
\lambda+\mu>0
. Define the artificial boundaries above and below the fluid-solid interface as
\Gamma_{+}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}=h_{1}\right\}
and
\Gamma_{-}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}=h_{2}\right\}
, where
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are constants. Let

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in\left(0,\Lambda\right),f\left(x_{1}\right)<x_{2}<h_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{-}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in\left(0,\Lambda\right),h_{2}<x_{2}<f\left(x_{1}\right)\right\}.

Similarly, we define

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}^{\mathrm{e}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}>h_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega_{-}^{\mathrm{e}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}\in(0,\Lambda),x_{2}<h_{2}\right\}.

Fig.
1
shows the problem geometry of acoustic-elastic interaction in periodic structures. Let
\bm{n}=\left(n_{1},n_{2}\right)^{\top}
be the unit normal vector to
\Gamma
directed
into
\Omega_{\mathrm{u}}
, and denote

\Omega=\Omega_{+}\cup\Omega_{-}.

Let
p^{\mathrm{in}}=e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha x_{1}-\beta x_{2}\right)}
be the incoming acoustic wave, where
\alpha=\kappa\sin\theta,\beta=\kappa\cos\theta
, and
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
is the incident angle. When the plane wave incident onto the elastic surface, the scattered acoustic wave
p^{\mathrm{sc}}
in the fluid domain and the transmitted elastic wave
\bm{u}=\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)^{\top}
in the solid domain are excited at the same time, where
p^{\mathrm{sc}}
satisfies the Helmholtz equation

\displaystyle\Delta p^{\mathrm{sc}}+\kappa^{2}p^{\mathrm{sc}}=0\quad

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega_{\mathrm{u}},

while
\bm{u}
admits the Naiver equation

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}\bm{u}+\omega^{2}\rho\bm{u}=0\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{\mathrm{d}}.

(1)

Here,
\kappa
is the wavenumber,
\omega>0
is the angular frequency, and
\Delta^{*}:=\mu\Delta+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\nabla\cdot
. To ensure the continuity of the normal component of the velocity, the kinematic interface condition

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{n}}(p^{\mathrm{in}}+p^{\mathrm{sc}})=\rho_{f}\omega^{2}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma

is required. In addition, the following dynamic interface condition

\displaystyle-(p^{\mathrm{in}}+p^{\mathrm{sc}})\bm{n}=\bm{T}\bm{u}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma

is imposed to ensure the continuity of the traction (cf.
[
41
,
50
,
51
]
). Here, the traction of
\bm{u}
is defined by

\displaystyle\bm{T}\bm{u}:=2\mu\partial_{\bm{\nu}}\bm{u}+\lambda\bm{\nu}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}-\mu\left[\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\right)\nu_{2}\\
\left(\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}-\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)\nu_{1}\end{array}\right],

where
\bm{\nu}=\left(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\right)^{\top}
is the unit normal vector of
\Omega_{-}
. We want to reduce the original problem equivalently to a boundary value problem in
\Omega
. To this end, we introduce exact transparent boundary conditions for acoustic and elastic waves defined on
\Gamma_{+}
and
\Gamma_{-}
, respectively.

Let
H^{1}(\Omega_{+})
and
H^{1}(\Omega_{-})
be the standard Sobolev spaces. The corresponding quasi-periodic functional spaces are defined by

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{+}\right):

\displaystyle=\left\{p\in H^{1}\left(\Omega_{+}\right):p\left(\Lambda,x_{2}\right)=p\left(0,x_{2}\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\Lambda}\right\},

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{-}\right):

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in H^{1}\left(\Omega_{-}\right):u\left(\Lambda,x_{2}\right)=u\left(0,x_{2}\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\Lambda}\right\}.

Given any quasi-periodic function
p\in H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{+}\right)
, it admits a Fourier series expansion

\displaystyle p\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{n}(x_{2})e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{n}x_{1}},\quad p_{n}(x_{2})=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}p\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha_{n}x_{1}}\mathrm{d}x_{1},

where
\alpha_{n}=2n\pi/\Lambda+\alpha
. For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the trace functional space
H^{s}\left(\Gamma_{+}\right)
is defined by

\displaystyle H^{s}\left(\Gamma_{+}\right)=\left\{p\in L^{2}\left(\Gamma_{+}\right):\|p\|_{H^{s}\left(\Gamma_{+}\right)}<\infty\right\}

with the trace norm

\displaystyle\|p\|_{H^{s}\left(\Gamma_{+}\right)}^{2}=\Lambda\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(1+\alpha_{n}^{2}\right)^{s}\left|p_{n}(h_{1})\right|^{2}.

Similarly, we can define the trace functional space
H^{s}\left(\Gamma_{-}\right)
and the corresponding trace norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{s}\left(\Gamma_{-}\right)}
.
We firstly introduce the TBC of the scattered field
p^{\mathrm{sc}}
. Note that the Rayleigh expansion (cf.
[
49
]
) of
p^{\mathrm{sc}}
can be defined by

\displaystyle p^{\mathrm{sc}}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{n}^{\mathrm{sc}}\left(h_{1}\right)e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha_{n}x_{1}+\beta_{n}\left(x_{2}-h_{1}\right)\right)},\quad x_{2}>h_{1},

(4)

where

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=\begin{cases}\left(\kappa^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2},&\left|\alpha_{n}\right|<\kappa,\\
\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha_{n}^{2}-\kappa^{2}\right)^{1/2},&\left|\alpha_{n}\right|>\kappa.\end{cases}

We always assume that
\beta_{n}\neq 0
, i.e.
\left|\alpha_{n}\right|\neq\kappa
to exclude Wood’s anomalies
[
4
]
. Taking the normal derivative of (
4
) with respect to
x_{2}
and then evaluating it at
x_{2}=h_{1}
, one can get

\partial_{x_{2}}p^{\mathrm{sc}}(x_{1},h_{1})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{i}\beta_{n}p^{s}_{n}(h_{1})e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{n}{x_{1}}}.

It was shown in
[
49
]
that for any quasi-periodic function
p
, the Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DtN) operator on
\Gamma_{+}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{+}p\left(x_{1},h_{1}\right):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{i}\beta_{n}p_{n}(h_{1})e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{n}x_{1}}.

(5)

Based on (
5
), the TBC for the scattered field
p^{\mathrm{sc}}
can be defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{2}}p^{\mathrm{sc}}=\mathscr{T}_{+}p^{\mathrm{sc}}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{+}.

(6)

Next, we turn to derive the TBC for the transmitted field
\bm{u}
. Let the Jacobian matrix of
\bm{u}
be

\displaystyle\nabla\bm{u}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{x_{1}}u_{1}&\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1}\\
\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}&\partial_{x_{2}}u_{2}\end{array}\right].

The operators
\operatorname{curl}
and
curl
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\operatorname{curl}\bm{u}=\partial_{x_{1}}u_{2}-\partial_{x_{2}}u_{1},\quad\textbf{curl}u=\left[\partial_{x_{2}}u,-\partial_{x_{1}}u\right]^{\top}.

The transmitted field
\bm{u}
in
\Omega_{-}
can be decomposed into the compressional part
\bm{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and the shear part
\bm{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
, i.e.,

\bm{u}_{\mathrm{p}}=-\frac{1}{\kappa_{1}^{2}}\nabla\nabla\cdot\bm{u},\quad\bm{u}_{\mathrm{s}}=\frac{1}{\kappa_{2}^{2}}\textbf{curl}\operatorname{curl}\bm{u},

where

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=\omega\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{2\mu+\lambda}},\quad\kappa_{2}=\omega\sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\mu}}

are called the compressional and shear wavenumbers satisfying
\kappa_{2}>\kappa_{1}
. For any solution
\bm{u}
of (
1
), it satisfies the Helmholtz decomposition

\displaystyle\bm{u}=\nabla\phi_{1}+\textbf{curl}\phi_{2},

(7)

where
\phi_{j}
(
j=1,2
) is called the scalar potential function.
Combining (
1
) and (
7
) yields

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{j}+\kappa_{j}^{2}\phi_{j}=0.

Due to the quasi-periodicity of
\bm{u}
, it follows from (
7
) that
\phi_{j}
is also a quasi-periodic function in the
x_{1}
direction with the period
\Lambda
and admits the following Rayleigh expansion

\displaystyle\phi_{j}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi_{n}^{(j)}(h_{2})e^{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha_{n}x_{1}-\beta_{n}^{(j)}\left(x_{2}-h_{2}\right)\right)},\quad x_{2}<h_{2},

where

\displaystyle\beta_{n}^{(j)}=\begin{cases}\left(\kappa_{j}^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2},&\left|\alpha_{n}\right|<\kappa_{j},\\
\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha_{n}^{2}-\kappa_{j}^{2}\right)^{1/2},&\left|\alpha_{n}\right|>\kappa_{j}.\end{cases}

Similarly, we assume that
\kappa_{j}\neq\left|\alpha_{n}\right|
.
It has been shown in
[
39
]
that the transmitted field
\bm{u}
has the expansion

\displaystyle\bm{u}\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{n}^{2}&-\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}^{(2)}\\
-\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}^{(1)}&\beta_{n}^{(1)}\beta_{n}^{(2)}\end{array}\right]\bm{u}_{n}(h_{2})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha_{n}x_{1}-\beta_{n}^{(1)}\left(x_{2}-h_{2}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\beta_{n}^{(1)}\beta_{n}^{(2)}&\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}^{(2)}\\
\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}^{(1)}&\alpha_{n}^{2}\end{array}\right]\bm{u}_{n}(h_{2})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha_{n}x_{1}-\beta_{n}^{(2)}\left(x_{2}-h_{2}\right)\right)},\quad x_{2}<h_{2},

where
\mathcal{X}_{n}=\alpha_{n}^{2}+\beta_{n}^{(1)}\beta_{n}^{(2)}
.
Based on (
2.1
), we can define the boundary operator on
\Gamma_{-}
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{-}\bm{u}:

\displaystyle=-2\mu\partial_{x_{2}}\bm{u}+\lambda[0,-1]^{\top}\nabla\cdot\bm{u}+\mu\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}-\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\\
0\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\mu\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\right)\\
-(2\mu+\lambda)\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}-\lambda\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\end{array}\right].

From (
2.1
) and (
2.1
), the TBC of the transmitted field
\bm{u}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}\bm{u}=\mathscr{T}_{-}\bm{u}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{n}\left[u_{n}^{(1)}(h_{2}),u_{n}^{(2)}(h_{2})\right]^{\top}e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha_{n}x_{1}},

(16)

where
\mathscr{T}_{-}
is the elastic DtN operator and the coefficient matrix is defined by

\displaystyle W_{n}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\mathcal{X}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega^{2}\rho\beta_{n}^{(1)}&-2\mu\alpha_{n}\mathcal{X}_{n}+\omega^{2}\rho\alpha_{n}\\
2\mu\alpha_{n}\mathcal{X}_{n}-\omega^{2}\rho\alpha_{n}&\omega^{2}\rho\beta_{n}^{(2)}\end{array}\right].

Then by the above transparent boundary conditions, one can obtain an acoustic-elastic interaction boundary value problem in periodic structures: Given
p^{\mathrm{in}}
, seek quasi-periodic solutions
p^{\mathrm{sc}}
and
\bm{u}
such that

\displaystyle\Delta p^{\mathrm{sc}}+\kappa^{2}p^{\mathrm{sc}}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{+},

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}\bm{u}+\omega^{2}\rho\bm{u}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{-},

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{n}}(p^{\mathrm{in}}+p^{\mathrm{sc}})=\rho_{f}\omega^{2}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

\displaystyle-(p^{\mathrm{in}}+p^{\mathrm{sc}})\bm{n}=\bm{T}\bm{u}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma,

(17)

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{2}}p^{\mathrm{sc}}=\mathscr{T}_{+}p^{\mathrm{sc}}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{+},

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}\bm{u}=\mathscr{T}_{-}\bm{u}

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{-}.

As shown in
[
25
]
, we know that the uniqueness of the solution for a bounded elastic body does not hold at Jones frequencies. Therefore, throughout the paper, we assume that the frequency
\omega
is not a Jones frequency.

2.2
Variational problem

In this subsection, we give the variational formulation of the acoustic-elastic interaction in periodic structures. To facilitate subsequent theoretical analysis, we introduce the following product Sobolev space

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega):=H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{+}\right)\times H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{-}\right)^{2}=\left\{\bm{U}=(p,\bm{u}):p\in H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{+}\right),\bm{u}\in H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{-}\right)^{2}\right\}.

The corresponding norm is defined by

\displaystyle\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}^{2}=

\displaystyle(\bm{V},\bm{V})_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{+}}(\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}+\varphi\overline{\varphi})\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\mu}(\bm{\psi},\bm{\psi})+\bm{\psi}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x},\quad\forall~\bm{V}=\left(\varphi,\bm{\psi}\right),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\mu}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})

\displaystyle=\lambda(\nabla\cdot\bm{u})(\nabla\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{\top}\right):\left(\nabla\overline{\bm{\psi}}+\nabla\overline{\bm{\psi}}^{\top}\right)

\displaystyle=(2\mu+\lambda)\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)+\mu\left(\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}+\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda\left(\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}\right)+\mu\left(\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}+\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}\frac{\partial\overline{\psi}_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}\right).

Note that
A:B=\operatorname{tr}\left(AB^{\top}\right)
is the Frobenius inner product of 2
\times
2 matrices
A
and
B
. Besides, from the second Korn’s inequality, it can be easily verified that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}
is equivalent to the standard
H^{1}
-norm; see also
[
50
]
.

By the Green’s formula and the first Betti’s formula, we can derive the weak formulation of the original problem (
2.1
): Given
p^{\mathrm{in}}
, seek
\bm{U}=\left(p^{\mathrm{sc}},\bm{u}\right)\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle A(\bm{U},\bm{V})=L(\bm{V}),\quad\forall~\bm{V}=(\varphi,\bm{\psi})\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega).

(18)

Here, the sesquilinear form
A:\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)\times\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is defined by

\displaystyle A(\bm{U},\bm{V})=\widetilde{A}(\bm{U},\bm{V})+\widetilde{B}_{1}(\bm{U},\bm{V})+\widetilde{B}_{2}(\bm{U},\bm{V}),

(19)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}(\bm{U},\bm{V})=\int_{\Omega_{+}}\left(\nabla p^{\mathrm{sc}}\cdot\nabla\overline{\varphi}-\kappa^{2}p^{\mathrm{sc}}\overline{\varphi}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x}+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\mu}(\bm{u},\bm{\psi})-\omega^{2}\rho\bm{u}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{d}\bm{x},

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{1}(\bm{U},\bm{V})=\int_{\Gamma}p^{\mathrm{sc}}\bm{n}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s+\int_{\Gamma}\rho_{f}\omega^{2}\bm{u}\cdot\bm{n}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s,

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{2}(\bm{U},\bm{V})=-\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\mathscr{T}_{+}p^{\mathrm{sc}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\mathscr{T}_{-}\bm{u}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s.

The term
L
on the right-hand side of equation (
18
) is a bounded linear functional dependent on
\left(\partial_{\bm{n}}p^{\mathrm{in}},p^{\mathrm{in}}\right)\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, which is defined by

\displaystyle L(\bm{V})=\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\bm{n}}p^{\mathrm{in}}\overline{\varphi}-p^{\mathrm{in}}\bm{n}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s.

(20)

We get from Lemma 4.1 in
[
25
]
that the sesquilinear form
A(\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies Gårding’s inequality. Besides, it has also been shown in
[
25
]
that the variational problem (
18
) has the unique solvability with Jones frequencies excluded. Then, it follows from the idea of
[
24
]
that the following inf-sup condition holds

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq\bm{V}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}\frac{|A(\bm{U},\bm{V})|}{\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}}\geq\gamma_{0}\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)},\quad\forall~\bm{U}\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega),

(21)

where
\gamma_{0}>0
is a constant indepedent of
U
.

3
PML Approximation

This section firstly focuses on constructing a truncated PML problem as an approximation to the original acoustic-elastic interaction in periodic structures, and then discusses the exponential convergence of the PML approximation error.

3.1
PML setting

We begin by introducing the absorbing PML media. Let the regions above
\Gamma_{+}
and below
\Gamma_{-}
be filled with PML layers of thicknesses
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
, respectively. The acoustic and elastic PML domains are respectively denoted by

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{+}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x_{1}<\Lambda,h_{1}<x_{2}<h_{1}+\delta_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{-}=\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x_{1}<\Lambda,h_{2}-\delta_{2}<x_{2}<h_{2}\right\},

with the corresponding acoustic and elastic PML boundaries

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x_{1}<\Lambda,x_{2}=h_{1}+\delta_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x_{1}<\Lambda,x_{2}=h_{2}-\delta_{2}\right\}.

The following Lemma is the result about the trace theorem.

Lemma 2

For any

Lemma 3

For any

(46)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\left(\mathscr{T}_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}-\mathscr{T}_{-}\right)\bm{u},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{-}}\right|\leq F_{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})^{2}}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})^{2}},

\displaystyle F_{2}=\frac{34\omega^{2}\rho}{\kappa_{1}^{4}}\times\max_{j=1,2}\left\{\frac{\Theta_{j}^{i}}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{j}^{i}\Im{\eta_{2}}}-1},\frac{\Theta_{j}^{e}}{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\Theta_{j}^{e}\Re{\eta_{2}}}-1}\right\}\times\max\left\{6\kappa_{2},\kappa_{2}^{2}+4,8\kappa_{2}^{4},\frac{8\kappa_{2}^{3}}{\kappa_{1}^{2}},\frac{12\left(\kappa_{2}^{2}+16\right)^{2}}{\kappa_{1}^{2}}\right\}.

Theorem 1

and

\displaystyle\|\bm{U}-\bm{U}^{\mathrm{PML}}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}

Proof.

\displaystyle\geq|A(\bm{U},\bm{V})|-F_{1}\gamma_{1}^{2}\|p^{\mathrm{sc}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{+})}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{+})}-F_{2}\gamma_{1}^{2}\|\bm{u}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{-})^{2}}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{-})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma_{0}\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}-\widetilde{F}\gamma_{1}^{2}\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=(\gamma_{0}-\widetilde{F}\gamma_{1}^{2})\|\bm{U}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}.

Next, we turn to estimate (
1

\displaystyle A\left(\bm{U}-\bm{U}^{\mathrm{PML}},\bm{V}\right)

\displaystyle=A\left(\bm{U},\bm{V}\right)-A\left(\bm{U}^{\mathrm{PML}},\bm{V}\right)

\displaystyle=A\left(\bm{U},\bm{V}\right)-A^{\mathrm{PML}}\left(\bm{U}^{\mathrm{PML}},\bm{V}\right)+A^{\mathrm{PML}}\left(\bm{U}^{\mathrm{PML}},\bm{V}\right)-A\left(\bm{U}^{\mathrm{PML}},\bm{V}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\left(\mathscr{T}_{+}-\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)p^{\mathrm{sc},\mathrm{PML}},\varphi\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{+}}+\left\langle\left(\mathscr{T}_{-}-\mathscr{T}_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)\bm{u}^{\mathrm{PML}},\bm{\psi}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{-}}

2

We give the following two lemmas, which will be used in the subsequent a posteriori estimate.

Lemma 4
.

For any
(p,\bm{u})\in\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}\left(\Omega\right)
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}p\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{+}}p\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathrm{~d}s,

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\mathscr{T}_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}\bm{u}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\bm{u}\cdot\mathscr{B}\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}\mathrm{~d}s.

Proof.

To begin with, we introduce
\hat{z}\in H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)
and
\hat{\bm{w}}\in H_{\mathrm{qp}}^{1}\left(\Omega_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\hat{z}+\kappa^{2}\hat{z}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }\Omega_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}},

\displaystyle\Delta^{*}_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\hat{\bm{w}}+\omega^{2}\rho\hat{\bm{w}}=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ in }\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{-},

\displaystyle\hat{z}\left(x_{1},h_{1}\right)=p\left(x_{1},h_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{+},

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{w}}\left(x_{1},h_{2}\right)=\bm{u}\left(x_{1},h_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{-},

\displaystyle\hat{z}\left(x_{1},h_{1}+\delta_{1}\right)=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}},

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{w}}\left(x_{1},h_{2}-\delta_{2}\right)=0

\displaystyle\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}.

It follows from the definitions of
\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}
and
\mathscr{T}_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}
that

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}p=\partial_{x_{2}}{\hat{z}}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{+},

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}\bm{u}=\mathscr{B}\hat{\bm{w}}\quad\text{ on }\Gamma_{-}.

By combining Green’s formula and the extension, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{+}}p\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\hat{z}\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathrm{~d}s=-\int_{\Omega_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left[\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\cdot\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\hat{z}-\kappa^{2}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\hat{z}\right]\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}(\Delta_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\hat{z}+\kappa^{2}\hat{z})\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}+\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\partial_{x_{2}}{\hat{z}}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\partial_{x_{2}}{\hat{z}}\overline{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathrm{~d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}p\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s.

Similarly, using the first Betti’s formula and the extension yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\bm{u}\cdot\mathscr{B}\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\hat{\bm{w}}\cdot\mathscr{B}\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left[\lambda(\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\cdot\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}})(\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\cdot\hat{\bm{w}})+\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}+\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}^{\top}\right):\left(\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\hat{\bm{w}}+\nabla_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\hat{\bm{w}}^{\top}\right)-\omega^{2}\rho\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}\cdot\hat{\bm{w}}\right]\mathrm{d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\mathscr{B}\hat{\bm{w}}\cdot\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}\mathrm{~d}s=\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\mathscr{T}_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}\bm{u}\cdot\overline{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}}\mathrm{~d}s,

which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle~B_{\Omega}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)-\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s.

Due to
\mathcal{L}_{1}\overline{\varphi}=0
in
\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{+}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}\overline{\bm{\psi}}=0
in
\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{-}
, it follows from the Green’s formula and the first Betti’s formula that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{+}}\mathcal{L}_{1}\overline{\varphi}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{+}}\left[\mathbb{A}\nabla\overline{\varphi}\cdot\nabla\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)-\kappa^{2}s(x_{2})\overline{\varphi}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\right]\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}-\int_{\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s

and

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{-}}\mathcal{L}_{2}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\cdot\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{\mathrm{PML}}_{-}}\left[\mathcal{S}_{\lambda,\mu}(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h},\bm{\psi})-\omega^{2}s(x_{2})\rho\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right]\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}-\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s.

Furthermore, it follows from (
24
), (
49
) and the above two equalities that

\displaystyle A^{\mathrm{PML}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)=

\displaystyle~B_{\Omega}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)-\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{x_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)-\int_{\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}},\bm{V}\right)-B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)+B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}_{h}\right)-B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~L(\bm{V})-B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h}\right)-L(\bm{V}_{h})

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~L(\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h})-B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h}\right)-\int_{\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}-\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\partial_{\hat{x}_{2}}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s-\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}}\left(\hat{\bm{u}}-\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right)\cdot\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\bm{x}}}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s.

(53)

Substituting (
4.2
) into (
52
) gives

\displaystyle A\left(\bm{U}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)=

\displaystyle~L(\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h})-B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\left(\mathscr{T}_{+}-\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\left(\mathscr{T}_{s}-\mathscr{T}_{s}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s,

where we have used
\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}=\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}=0
on
\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}
and
\hat{\bm{u}}=\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}=0
on
\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}
. Thus the proof is completed.
∎

4.3
A posteriori error analysis

In this subsection, we introduce the definition of a posteriori error indicators. For any
T\in\mathscr{M}_{h}
, define
h_{T}
as its diameter. Let
\partial T
be the set of all the edges of
T
that do not lie on
\Gamma_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}
and
\Gamma_{-}^{\mathrm{PML}}
. For any
e\in\partial T
, define
h_{e}
as its length. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{+}\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h},

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h},\quad\forall~T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{-}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{L}_{2}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h},\quad\forall~T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}.

For any
T_{1}^{+},T_{2}^{+}\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}
, the jump residual
J_{e,+}
is given by

\displaystyle J_{e,+}:=\begin{cases}-(\left.\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}^{+}}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{1}+\left.\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right|_{T_{2}^{+}}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{2}),&e\in\partial{T_{1}^{+}}\cap\partial{T_{2}^{+}},\\
2\left(\partial_{\bm{n}}\left(p^{\mathrm{in}}+\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)-\rho_{f}\omega^{2}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot\bm{n}\right),&e\in\Gamma.\end{cases}

where
\bm{\nu}_{j}
is the unit outward normal vector on the boundary of
T_{j}^{+}
(
j=1,2
). For any
T_{1}^{-},T_{2}^{-}\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}
, the jump residual
\bm{J}_{e,-}
is given by

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{e,-}:=\begin{cases}-\left(\left.\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}^{-}}+\left.\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}^{-}}\right),&e\in\partial{T_{1}^{-}}\cap\partial{T_{2}^{-}},\\
-2\left(\left(p^{\mathrm{in}}+\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\bm{n}+\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right),&e\in\Gamma.\end{cases}

Next we consider the jump residual on the left line segment
\Gamma_{\text{left}}
and right line segment
\Gamma_{\text{right}}
, where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{left}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right):x_{1}=0,h_{2}-\delta_{2}<x_{2}<h_{1}+\delta_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{right}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right):x_{1}=\Lambda,h_{2}-\delta_{2}<x_{2}<h_{1}+\delta_{1}\right\}.

For any
e\in\Gamma_{\text{left}}\cap\partial T_{1}
for some
T_{1}\in\mathscr{M}_{h}
and its corresponding edge
e^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\text{right}}\cap\partial T_{2}
for some
T_{2}\in\mathscr{M}_{h}
, denote the jump residual by

\displaystyle J_{e,+}

\displaystyle:=-(\left.\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{1}+e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha\Lambda}\left.\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right|_{T_{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{2}),

\displaystyle J_{e^{\prime},+}

\displaystyle:=-(\left.e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\Lambda}\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{1}+\left.\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right|_{T_{2}}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{2}),

and

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{e,-}

\displaystyle:=-\left(\left.\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}}+\left.e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha\Lambda}\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right|_{T_{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{e^{\prime},-}

\displaystyle:=-\left(\left.e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\Lambda}\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right|_{T_{1}}+\left.\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right|_{T_{2}}\right).

For
T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}
and
T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}
, denote the local error estimators by
\eta_{T,+}
and
\eta_{T,-}
, where

\displaystyle\eta_{T,+}=h_{T}\left\|\mathcal{R}_{+}\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\partial T}h_{e}\left\|J_{e,+}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(54)

\displaystyle\eta_{T,-}=h_{T}\left\|\mathcal{R}_{-}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\partial T}h_{e}\left\|\bm{J}_{e,-}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)^{2}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(55)

.

There exists a positive constant

\displaystyle\|\bm{U}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}\leq

\displaystyle~\max\left\{C(1+\gamma_{1}C_{1}),C(1+\gamma_{1}C_{2})\right\}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}}\eta_{T,+}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}}\eta_{T,-}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

2

3
and
5

Proof.

For brevity, we can rewrite the formula (

\displaystyle A\left(\bm{U}-\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}\right)=

\displaystyle~{J}_{1}+{J}_{2},

(56)

where

\displaystyle{J}_{1}

\displaystyle=~L(\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h})-B_{\mathrm{D}}\left(\hat{\bm{U}}_{h},\bm{V}-\bm{V}_{h}\right),

\displaystyle{J}_{2}

\displaystyle=~\int_{\Gamma_{+}}\left(\mathscr{T}_{+}-\mathscr{T}_{+}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\overline{\varphi}\mathrm{~d}s+\int_{\Gamma_{-}}\left(\mathscr{T}_{s}-\mathscr{T}_{s}^{\mathrm{PML}}\right)\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot\overline{\bm{\psi}}\mathrm{~d}s.

\displaystyle\quad\left\|\varphi-\Pi_{h}^{+}\varphi\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}\leq Ch_{e}^{1/2}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{e})},

(57)

\displaystyle\quad\left\|\bm{\psi}-\Pi_{h}^{-}\bm{\psi}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)^{2}}\leq Ch_{e}^{1/2}\|\nabla\bm{\psi}\|_{F(\tilde{e})}.

(58)

Here,
\tilde{T}
and

are the union of all elements in

, which have nonempty intersection with

and

and

\displaystyle{J}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}}\left(\int_{T}\left(-\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\cdot\nabla\left(\overline{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{+}\overline{\varphi}\right)+\kappa^{2}s(x_{2})\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\left(\overline{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{+}\overline{\varphi}\right)\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}\right.

\displaystyle{J}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}}\left(\int_{T}\mathcal{L}_{1}\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\left(\overline{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{+}\overline{\varphi}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}\right.-\sum_{e\in\partial T}\int_{e}\mathbb{A}\nabla\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\cdot\bm{\nu}(\overline{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{+}\overline{\varphi})\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}}\left(\int_{T}\mathcal{L}_{2}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot\left(\overline{\bm{\psi}}-\Pi_{h}^{-}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}\right.-\sum_{e\in\partial T}\int_{e}\bm{T}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\left(\overline{\bm{\psi}}-\Pi_{h}^{-}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\left.+\sum_{e\in\partial T\cap\Gamma}\int_{e}-\left(p^{\mathrm{in}}+\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\right)\bm{n}\cdot\left(\overline{\bm{\psi}}-\Pi_{h}^{-}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{~d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}}\left(\int_{T}\mathcal{R}_{+}\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\left(\overline{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{+}\overline{\varphi}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}+\sum_{e\in\partial T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{e}J_{e,+}\left(\overline{\varphi}-\Pi_{h}^{+}\overline{\varphi}\right)\mathrm{~d}s\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}}\left(\int_{T}\mathcal{R}_{-}\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\cdot\left(\overline{\bm{\psi}}-\Pi_{h}^{-}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{~d}\bm{x}+\sum_{e\in\partial T}\frac{1}{2}\int_{e}\bm{J}_{e,-}\cdot\left(\overline{\bm{\psi}}-\Pi_{h}^{-}\overline{\bm{\psi}}\right)\mathrm{~d}s\right).

It follows from (
57

\displaystyle|{J}_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle|{J}_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle~\max\left\{C(1+\gamma_{1}C_{1}),C(1+\gamma_{1}C_{2})\right\}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,+}}\eta_{T,+}^{2}+\sum_{T\in\mathscr{M}_{h,-}}\eta_{T,-}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}

2
and
3
, we have

\displaystyle|J_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq F_{1}\|\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{+})}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{+})}+F_{2}\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})^{2}}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma_{1}F_{1}\|\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{+})}\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{+})}+\gamma_{1}F_{2}\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})^{2}}\|\bm{\psi}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega_{-})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\gamma_{1}F_{1}\|\hat{p}^{\mathrm{sc}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{+})}+\gamma_{1}F_{2}\|\hat{\bm{u}}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{-})^{2}}\right)\|\bm{V}\|_{\mathscr{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{qp}}(\Omega)}.

Then the proof is completed by using the above estimates in (

Conclusion

We develop a PML-based adaptive FEM for solving the acoustic-elastic interaction problem in periodic structures.
By introducing two PML-equivalent transparent boundary conditions, we prove the unique solvability of the truncated PML variational formulation and show that the PML truncation error decays exponentially as the PML parameters increase. We further give a residual-type a posteriori error estimate and develop an adaptive FEM algorithm to address singularities of the solution caused by the non-smooth fluid-solid interface. Numerical experiments are presented to validate the accuracy and robustness of the proposed method. Future work will extend the analysis of PML-AFEM to the thermoelastic scattering problem.

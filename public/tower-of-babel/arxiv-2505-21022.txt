Covariant Fractons and Weitzenböck Torsion
Davide Rovere

Abstract
The relation between covariant fracton gauge theory and Møller-Hayashi-Shirafuji extension of teleparallel gravity is investigated. The former is the gauge theory of a rank two symmetric tensor with gauge symmetry given by the double derivative of a scalar parameter; the latter is a class of theories which generalises Einstein gravity, whose action is quadratic in the Weitzenböck torsion. We show that the solutions of covariant fracton gauge theory can be viewed as solutions of Møller-Hayashi-Shirafuji theory, providing a new insight in the relation between covariant fractons and gravity, and elucidating the meaning of covariant fracton theories as a new type of gauge theory.

Review of teleparallel gravity

In the usual formulation of General Relativity, one chooses a connection
\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\varrho}{}_{\nu}

Linearisation of Møller-Hayashi-Shirafuji theory

The linearisation of the Møller-Hayashi-Shirafuji theory
Moller1978
;
Hayashi:1979qx
is obtained by defining

e_{\mu}{}^{a}=\delta_{\mu}^{a}+\varepsilon\,\overline{h}_{\mu}{}^{a},\quad g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\varepsilon\,h_{\mu\nu},

(5.1)

where
\varepsilon
is infinitesimal. Moreover, defining

\overline{h}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}=\overline{h}_{\mu}{}^{a}\,\delta_{a}^{\nu},\quad\overline{h}_{\mu\nu}=\overline{h}_{\mu}{}^{\varrho}\,\eta_{\varrho\nu}=\overline{h}_{\mu}{}^{a}\,\delta_{a}{}^{\varrho}\,\eta_{\varrho\nu}

(5.2)

and combining the linearisation of
g_{\mu\nu}
and

h_{\mu\nu}
is the symmetric part
\overline{h}_{\mu\nu}+\overline{h}_{\nu\mu}
. So, denoting with
b_{\mu\nu}=\overline{h}_{\mu\nu}-\overline{h}_{\nu\mu}
the antisymmetric part, we can decompose

\overline{h}_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,(h_{\mu\nu}+b_{\mu\nu}).

(5.3)

Similarly for the inverse vielbein:

e^{\mu}{}_{a}=\delta^{\mu}_{a}-\varepsilon\,\overline{h}_{a}{}^{\mu},\quad\overline{h}_{a}{}^{\nu}\,\delta^{a}_{\mu}=\overline{h}_{\mu}{}^{a}\,\delta_{a}^{\nu},\quad\overline{h}_{b}{}^{\mu}\,\delta^{a}_{\nu}=\overline{h}_{\nu}{}^{a}\,\delta_{b}^{\mu},

The linearised equations of motion are

\displaystyle\mathring{E}_{\mu\nu}^{\text{(anti)}}=

\displaystyle\,(-2\,\alpha_{1}+2\,\alpha_{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{3}}{2})\,\partial_{\alpha}\,\mathring{t}^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,(2\,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})\,\partial_{\alpha}\,\mathring{f}^{\alpha}{}_{[\mu\nu]}.

(5.6)

where

\displaystyle\mathring{T}_{\mu\nu\varrho}

\displaystyle=\mathring{f}_{\mu\varrho\nu}+\mathring{t}_{\mu\nu\varrho}=\partial_{\mu}\,\overline{h}_{\varrho\nu}-\partial_{\varrho}\,\overline{h}_{\varrho\nu}=

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\partial_{\mu}\,h_{\varrho\nu}-\partial_{\varrho}\,h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{\mu}\,b_{\varrho\nu}-\partial_{\varrho}\,b_{\mu\nu}),

\displaystyle\mathring{t}_{\mu\nu\varrho}

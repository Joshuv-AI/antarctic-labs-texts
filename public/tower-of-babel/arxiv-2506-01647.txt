Higher order spectral shift of Euclidean Callias operators
Oliver Fürst

Abstract
We consider Dirac-Schrödinger operators over odd-dimensional Euclidean space. The conditions for the potential are based on those of C. Callias in his famous paper on the corresponding index problem. However, we treat the case where the potential can take values in unbounded operators of a separable Hilbert space, and crucially, we also do not assume that the potential needs to be invertible outside a compact region. Hence, the Dirac-Schrödinger operator is not necessarily Fredholm. In the setup we discuss, it however still admits a related trace formula in terms of the underlying potential. In this paper we express the trace formula for these Callias-type operators in terms of higher order spectral shift functions, leading to a functional equation which generalizes a known functional equation found first by A. Pushnitski. To the knowledge of the author, this paper presents the first multi-dimensional non-Fredholm extension of the Callias index theorem involving higher order spectral shift functions. More precisely, we also show that under a Lebesgue point condition on the higher order spectral shift function associated to the potential, the Callias-type operator admits a regularized index, even in non-Fredholm settings. This corresponds to a known Witten index result in the one-dimensional case shown by A. Carey et al. The regularized index that we introduce is a minor extension of the classical Witten index, and we present an index formula, which generalizes the classical Callias index theorem. As an example, we treat the case of ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -massless Dirac-Schrödinger operators, for which we calculate the associated higher order spectral shift functions.

is an operator in
S^{r}(H)
for
r^{-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}^{-1}
, which satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{J}\left(\phi,\mathbf{E},\mathbf{T}\right)\right\|_{S^{r}}\leq C\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|T_{j}\|_{S^{p_{j}}}.

(2.10)

Let us now introduce an important subclass of symbols, those given by divided differences.

Definition 2.3
.

For
f\in C^{n}(\mathbb{R})
, define the
divided differences of
f
,
\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f:\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dif}_{0}f

\displaystyle:=f,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n}}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n-1}f\left(\lambda^{(n-1)}\right)-\operatorname{Dif}_{n-1}f\left(\lambda^{(n)}\right)\right),&\lambda_{n}\neq\lambda_{n-1},\\
\partial_{\lambda_{n-1}}\operatorname{Dif}_{n-1}f(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}),&\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{n-1},\end{cases}

(2.11)

where
\lambda^{(j)}:=\left(\lambda_{0},\ldots,\lambda_{j-1},\lambda_{j+1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
j=0,\ldots,n
.

A straightforward induction proof shows the following well known formula for divided differences. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote

\displaystyle\langle x,y\rangle:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}x_{j}y_{j}.

(2.12)

Lemma 2.4

(Genochi-Hermite)
.

Let
f\in C^{n}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f(\lambda)=\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}f^{(n)}\left(\langle s,\lambda\rangle\right)\operatorname{d}s,

(2.13)

where
\Delta_{n}=\left\{s\in[0,1]^{n+1}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n}s_{j}=1\right.\right\}
is the
n
-simplex in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

In particular
\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f
is symmetric in all its arguments.

A classical question in the theory of multiple operator integrals is under which ”accessible” conditions on
f
the divided difference
\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f
is a symbol in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{T};\psi)
. It turns out, that it is surprisingly difficult to answer, and remains in its admittedly vaguely formulated generality unresolved to the knowledge of the author. Again for more details surrounding this question, and multiple operator integrals in general, we point to the survey article
[
30
]
. However, for most applications a sufficiently large class of functions
f
for which the factorization of
\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f
satisfies (
2.9
) is well-known, for example it suffices that the
n
-th derivative of
f
has an integrable Fourier transform. The proof of this fact is classical, but to keep this paper sufficiently self-contained, we include it in the proof of Proposition
2.18
.

We modify these classical spaces by weights, their purpose will become clear as soon as we introduce the kind of operators to which we want their multiple operator integrals to be applied. We should note that a similar approach is used in
[
28
]
, of which we will however deviate sufficiently, such that we need to present our approach in a self-contained manner (except for Theorem
2.7
, the main estimate in
[
31
]
).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
X
denote the function
x\mapsto x
, and for
z\geq 0
let
\langle x\rangle_{z}:=(|x|^{2}+z)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
\langle x\rangle:=\langle x\rangle_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
be the Schwartz functions of
\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
the space of tempered distributions. Let
\operatorname{FM}(\mathbb{R})
be the Banach space of signed finite Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}
, equipped with the total variation norm
\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R})}
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}
.

For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
denote by
W^{m,n}
the space of functions
f\in C^{n}(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, such that for
\max(n-m,0)\leq l\leq n
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-n+l}f^{(l)}\right)\in\operatorname{FM}(\mathbb{R}).

(2.14)

We equip
W^{m,n}
with the (semi-)norm

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,n}}:=\sum_{l=\max(n-m,0)}^{n}\left\|\mathcal{F}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-n+l}f^{(l)}\right)\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2.15)

Denote by
W^{m,n}_{0}
the subspace of
W^{m,n}
consisting of functions
f
, such that for
\max(n-m,0)\leq l\leq n

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-n+l}f^{(l)}\right)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

(2.16)

Note that in particular the space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is contained and dense in all
W^{m,n}_{0}
. We also introduce the weaker semi-norm

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,n}_{\infty}}:=\sum_{l=\max(n-m,0)}^{n}\left\|(X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-n+l}f^{(l)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.

(2.17)

We now introduce the class of (generally unbounded) operators to which we want to apply the multiple operator integral
\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\cdot)
for
f\in W^{m,n}
, and
A
self-adjoint. Their main property is that they well-behave on the scale of Hilbert spaces generated by
A
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
, and
A
self-adjoint. Let
S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}
be the space of operators
T
in
H
such that
\langle A\rangle^{\beta}T\langle A\rangle^{-\beta-\alpha}
is densely defined and extends to an operator in
S^{p}
for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\beta|\leq\max(m-1,0)
. On
S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}
define the norm

\displaystyle\|T\|_{S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}}:=\sup_{\beta\in\mathbb{R},~|\beta|\leq\max(m-1,0)}\left\|\overline{\langle A\rangle^{\beta}T\langle A\rangle^{-\beta-\alpha}}\right\|_{S^{p}}.

(2.18)

We denote
S^{m,p}_{A}:=S^{m,p,0}_{A}
. Let
\mathbf{T}:=(T_{1},\ldots T_{n})
be a vector of operators in
S_{A}^{m,\mathbf{p}}:=S^{m,p_{1}}_{A}\times\ldots\times S^{m,p_{n}}_{A}
with
\mathbf{p}:=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in[1,\infty)^{n}
. For
k\in\{0,\ldots,\min(m,n)\}
we introduce the operators

\displaystyle F_{k}^{m,n}(A,\mathbf{T})

\displaystyle:=(-1)^{k}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}}{{m>\gamma_{1}>\ldots>\gamma_{k}}}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{\gamma_{j}}T_{j}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{-\gamma_{j}},~k\geq 1,~m\geq 1,

\displaystyle F_{k}^{0,n}(A,\mathbf{T})

\displaystyle:=0,~k\geq 1,

\displaystyle F_{0}^{m,n}(A,\mathbf{T})

\displaystyle:=1.

(2.19)

For
f\in W^{m,n}
we introduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{T}):=\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n-k}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right),A,(T_{k+1},\ldots,T_{n})\right).

(2.20)

We will utilize the following central estimate from
[
31
]
. It is the ”work horse” in the construction of higher order spectral shift measures, which turn out to be absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. We will mimic this procedure in our setup below.

Theorem 2.7

(Theorem 5.3/Remark 5.4
[
31
]
)
.

Let
p_{j}\in(1,\infty)
, such that
q^{-1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}^{-1}\in(0,1)
. Then there is a constant
c
, only dependent on
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
, such that for
f\in C^{n}
,
A
self-adjoint, and
B_{j}\in S^{p_{j}}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})\right)\right\|_{S^{q}}\leq c\|f^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|B_{j}\|_{S^{p_{j}}}.

(2.21)

We directly gain an estimate for the operators we introduced in Definition
2.6
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbf{T}\in S^{m,\mathbf{p}}_{A}
be as in Definition
2.6
with
\mathbf{p}\in(1,\infty)^{n}
. Then there is a constant
c
only dependent on
m,n,\mathbf{p}
, such that for
0\leq k\leq\min(m,n)
, and
f\in W^{m,n}
,

\displaystyle\left\|F_{k}^{m,n}(A,\mathbf{T})\right\|_{S^{q_{k}}}\leq c\prod_{j=1}^{k}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j}}_{A}},

(2.22)

with
q_{k}^{-1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{k}p_{j}^{-1}
, and

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{M}_{k}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{T})\right\|_{S^{r_{k}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)^{(n-k)}\right\|_{\infty}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{p_{j}}},

(2.23)

with
r_{k}^{-1}:=\sum_{j=k+1}^{n}p_{j}^{-1}
.

Proof.

The first estimate (
2.22
) is a direct consequence of the Hölder inequality for Schatten-von Neumann operators. The second estimate (
2.23
) follows from Theorem
2.7
.
∎

The reason we introduce the operators in Definition
2.6
is the following decomposition. It is similar to the approach used in
[
28
]
, where the authors use a similar factorization, albeit without the additional parameter
\alpha\geq 1
, which is essential for our purposes. Their approach leads to a spectral shift function for relative Schatten-von Neumann perturbations, i.e. for operators
T\in S^{0,p,1}_{A}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
\mathbf{T}=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
a vector of bounded operators such that
\langle A\rangle^{\beta}T\langle A\rangle^{-\beta}
is densely defined and extends to a bounded operator for
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\beta|\leq\max(m-1,0)
. For
f\in W^{m,n}
we have

\displaystyle(A+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}F^{m,n}_{k}(A,\mathbf{T})\mathcal{M}_{k}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{T}).

(2.24)

Proof.

We perform induction in
n
. For
n=0
the statement is true. For
n\geq 1
let
\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}:=(T_{2},\ldots,T_{n})
, then the product rule of divided differences implies

\displaystyle(A+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)=\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m}f\right),A,\mathbf{T}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{j}T_{1}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n-1}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-j-1}f\right),A,\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}\right)

(2.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}^{m,n}\left(f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left[(A+\mathbbm{i})^{j}T_{1}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{-j}\right]

\displaystyle(A+\mathbbm{i})^{j}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n-1}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-j-1}f\right),A,\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}\right)

(2.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}^{m,n}\left(f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left[(A+\mathbbm{i})^{j}T_{1}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{-j}\right]\sum_{k=0}^{\min(j,n-1)}F^{j,n-1}_{k}\left(A,\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{k}^{j,n-1}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-j-1}f,A,\widetilde{\mathbf{T}}\right)

(2.27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{0}^{m,n}(A,\mathbf{T})\mathcal{M}_{0}^{m,n}\left(f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m-1,n-1)}(-1)^{k}F_{k+1}^{m,n}(A,\mathbf{T})\mathcal{M}_{k+1}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}F^{m,n}_{k}(A,\mathbf{T})\mathcal{M}_{k}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{T}).

(2.28)

∎

Due to the decomposition above we are able to produce the following trace norm estimate for the multiple operator integral applied to a vector of unbounded operators
\mathbf{T}
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
such that
m\geq\alpha(n+1)
, and
\mathbf{p}\in(1,\infty)^{n}
. Let
q^{-1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}^{-1}
. Let
T_{0}\in S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}
, and
\mathbf{T}\in S^{m,\mathbf{p},\alpha}_{A}
. Then for
f\in W^{m,n}

\displaystyle\left\|T_{0}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)\right\|_{S^{1}}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,n}_{\infty}}\left\|T_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j},\alpha}_{A}},

(2.29)

where the constant
c
is independent of the operators
T_{0},\mathbf{T},A
and the function
f
.

Proof.

For
1\leq j\leq n
introduce the operators

\displaystyle S_{j}:=(A+\mathbbm{i})^{\beta_{j}}T_{j}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{-\beta_{j}-\alpha},

(2.30)

with
\beta_{j}:=(j-n-1)\alpha
, and let
S_{0}:=T_{0}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{-\alpha}
. Then
S_{0}\in S^{m,q}_{A}
, and
\mathbf{S}:=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})\in S^{m,\mathbf{p}}_{A}
. We then have

\displaystyle T_{0}\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T})=S_{0}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{\alpha(n+1)-m}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{S}\right).

(2.31)

We apply Lemma
2.9
and get

\displaystyle T_{0}\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T})=S_{0}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{\alpha(n+1)-m}\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}F^{m,n}_{k}(A,\mathbf{S})\mathcal{M}_{k}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{S}).

(2.32)

By Lemma
2.8
we estimate each summand and obtain

\displaystyle\left\|T_{0}\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T})\right\|_{S^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}\left\|\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)^{(n-k)}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|S_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|S_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j}}_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,n}_{\infty}}\left\|T_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j},\alpha}_{A}}.

(2.33)

∎

By mimicking the proof idea of
[
31
]
[Theorem 1.1], we construct in a first step towards spectral shift functions the following ”higher order spectral shift measures”.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in[0,\infty)
such that
m\geq\alpha(n+1)
, and
\mathbf{p}\in(1,\infty)^{n}
. Let
q^{-1}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}^{-1}
. Let
T_{0}\in S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}
, and
\mathbf{T}\in S^{m,\mathbf{p},\alpha}_{A}
. Then there exists a Radon measure
\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
such that
x\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{-m}\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)
is a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}
,
and there is a constant
c
independent of
A
,
T_{0}
, and
\mathbf{T}
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{-m}\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}\leq c\left\|T_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j},\alpha}_{A}},

(2.34)

and such that for
f\in W^{m,n}_{0}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{0}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T}\right)\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{(n)}(x)\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x).

(2.35)

Proof.

Consider the decomposition (
2.32
) provided in Corollary
2.10
. We introduce the multilinear maps
\psi_{k,A}:W^{m,n}\times S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}\times S^{m,\mathbf{p},\alpha}_{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\psi_{k,A}(f,T_{0},\mathbf{T}):=\operatorname{Tr}\left(S_{0}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{\alpha(n+1)-m}F^{m,n}_{k}(A,\mathbf{S})\mathcal{M}_{k}^{m,n}(f,A,\mathbf{S})\right)

(2.36)

The map
\psi_{k,A}
is continuous in the following sense by Lemma
2.8

\displaystyle\left|\psi_{k,A}(f,T_{0},\mathbf{T})\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)^{(n-k)}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|S_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|S_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j}}_{A}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left\|\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)^{(n-k)}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|T_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j},\alpha}_{A}}.

(2.37)

By the Riesz-Markov representation theorem there exist finite Radon measures
\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
with
\left\|\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}\leq c\left\|T_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j},\alpha}_{A}}
, such that

\displaystyle\psi_{k,A}(f,T_{0},\mathbf{T})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{n-k}\left((x+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f(x)\right)\operatorname{d}\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x).

(2.38)

Thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{0}\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T})\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}^{n-k}\left((x+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f(x)\right)\operatorname{d}\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)

(2.39)

If we let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\subset W^{m,n}
, product rule expansion and integration by parts yields that for
g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
defined by

\displaystyle g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x):=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{\min(m,n)}\int_{(0,x]}(x-y)^{r-1}(y+\mathbbm{i})^{m-r}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^{r}\frac{(n-k)!(m-k)!}{(n-r)!(r-k)!(m-r)!(r-1)!}\operatorname{d}\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(y),

(2.40)

which satisfies
|g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)|\leq c\langle x\rangle^{m-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\min(m,n)}\left\|\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}
, where
c
is independent of
x
,
A
,
T_{0}
and
\mathbf{T}
, such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{0}\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{(n)}(x)\left(g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\operatorname{d}x+(x+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\operatorname{d}\nu_{0,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\right).

(2.41)

We define the Radon measure

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x):=g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\operatorname{d}x+(x+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\operatorname{d}\nu_{0,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x),

(2.42)

which satisfies that
x\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{-m}\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)
is a finite measure on
\mathbb{R}
with

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\langle x\rangle^{-m}\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq c\sum_{k=0}^{\min(m,n)}\left\|\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\left\|T_{0}\right\|_{S^{m,q,\alpha}_{A}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left\|T_{j}\right\|_{S^{m,p_{j},\alpha}_{A}},

(2.43)

and such that for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{0}\mathcal{J}(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{T})\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{(n)}(x)\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x).

(2.44)

The above equation extends by density and continuity to all
f\in W^{m,n}_{0}
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

We note that for general
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
large enough, a measure satisfying (
2.35
) from Proposition
2.11
is not necessarily unique (it might be altered by
p(x)\operatorname{d}x
for any polynomial
p
of degree less than
n
). However we always have the unique measure given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x):=g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\operatorname{d}x+(x+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\operatorname{d}\nu_{0,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x),

(2.45)

where the function
g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
and the Radon measure
\operatorname{d}\nu_{0,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
are uniquely given in the proof of Proposition
2.11
by (
2
) respectively (
2.36
) and (
2.38
).

Lemma 2.13
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
2.11
let
\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
be the unique measure given by the formula

\displaystyle\operatorname{d}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x):=g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x)\operatorname{d}x+(x+\mathbbm{i})^{m}\operatorname{d}\nu_{0,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}(x),

(2.46)

where the function
g_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
and the Radon measure
\operatorname{d}\nu_{0,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
are uniquely given in the proof of Proposition
2.11
by (
2
) respectively (
2.36
) and (
2.38
).

If
A\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{supp}\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}\subseteq[0,\infty)
. In this case, the latter support condition also fixes the measure
\tau_{n,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}
uniquely.

Proof.

Let
f\in W^{m,n}
and
0\leq k\leq n
. Assume that
\operatorname{supp}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)^{(n-k)}\subseteq(-\infty,0)
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n-k+1}
we thus have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dif}_{n-k}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)(\lambda)=\int_{s\in\Delta_{n-k}}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m-k}f\right)^{(n-k)}(\langle s,\lambda\rangle)\operatorname{d}s=0,

(2.47)

since
\langle s,\lambda\rangle\geq 0
. Because
A\geq 0
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{m,n}_{k}(f,A,\mathbf{S})=0,

(2.48)

where
\mathbf{S}
is the vector of operators given in the proof of Corollary
2.10
. Variation over functions
f
with the above support condition imply that the measures
\nu_{k,A,T_{0},\mathbf{T}}

So far we have avoided the topic of operator differentiability and non commutative Taylor expansions on purpose to highlight that higher order spectral shift
measures
can be more broadly defined for multiple operator integrals with the divided difference as symbol. However, we will now deal with the subclass of multiple operator integrals which are applied to a vector of copies of the same operator, which are closely related with the non commutative Taylor expansion. This relation provides the key to obtain higher order spectral shift
functions
, which is the main trick in the proof of
[
31
]
[Theorem 1.1] to bootstrap from measures to (density) functions.

Definition 2.15
.

Let
A
be self-adjoint in
H
,
B
symmetric on
\operatorname{dom}A
, such that
A+sB
is self-adjoint on
\operatorname{dom}A
for
s\in[0,1]
. Let
f
be a function on
\mathbb{R}
, and assume that
s\mapsto f(A+sB)
is
n
-times differentiable in the strong operator topology on a dense subspace
\mathcal{D}\subseteq H
at
s=0
.
Let
T_{n}(f,A,B)
denote the
n
th non-commutative Taylor summand of
f
at
A+B
developed around
A
, i.e.

\displaystyle T_{n}(f,A,B):=\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\operatorname{d}s^{n}}\left.\left[f(A+sB)\right]\right|_{s=0}.

(2.57)

By
R_{n}(f,A,B)
denote the
n
th non-commutative Taylor series remainder of
f
at
T+V
developed around
T
, i.e.

\displaystyle R_{n}(f,A,B):=f(A+B)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{k}(f,A,B).

(2.58)

Let us fix the type of perturbations
B
of
A
we will need in the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis 2.16
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in[n,\infty)
,
\alpha\geq 0
, such that
m\geq\alpha n
. Let
A
be self-adjoint and let
B\in S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}
be symmetric with
\operatorname{dom}A^{k}B\supseteq\operatorname{dom}A^{k+1}
for

and assume

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to\infty}\left\|\langle A\rangle^{k}B\langle A\rangle^{-k}\langle A\rangle_{z}^{-1}\right\|_{B(H)}=0.

(2.59)

The conditions on

are chosen such that
B
behaves uniformly well on the Hilbert scale generated by
A+tB
for
t\in[0,1]
.

Lemma 2.17
.

Assume Hypothesis
2.16
, and let
t\in[0,1]
. Then
A_{t}:=A+tB
is self-adjoint on
\operatorname{dom}A
, and the graph norms of

and

are equivalent, uniformly in

, for
1\leq k\leq m
. In particular there is a constant
c
independent of

, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left\|B\right\|_{S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A_{t}}}\leq c\left\|B\right\|_{S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}}.

(2.60)

Proof.

By the Kato-Rellich theorem’s (
[
24
]
[Theorem 5.4.3]), it is sufficient to show that for
1\leq k\leq m
,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left\|\left(A_{t}^{k}-A^{k}\right)\langle A\rangle_{z}^{-k}\right\|_{B(H)}=0.

(2.61)

for all
1\leq k\leq m
. We proceed by induction. The case

follows directly by Hypothesis
2.16
. In particular
A_{t}
is self-adjoint on
\operatorname{dom}A
. Now assume that the statement holds for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
. Therefore the graph norms of
A_{t}^{k}
and
A^{k}
are uniformly equivalent. Consider

\displaystyle\left(A_{t}^{n}-A^{n}\right)\langle A\rangle_{z}^{-n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}A_{t}^{k}tBA^{n-1-k}\langle A\rangle_{z}^{-n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left[A_{t}^{k}\langle A\rangle^{-k}\right]\left[\langle A\rangle^{k}B\langle A\rangle^{-k}\langle A\rangle^{-1}_{z}\right]\left[A^{n-1-k}\langle A\rangle^{k}\langle A\rangle_{z}^{-n+1}\right].

(2.62)

The first factor in the sum is uniformly bounded by induction, the second factor tends to
0
in norm by Hypothesis
2.16
, and the third factor is trivially bounded by functional calculus. Thus (
2.61
) holds.
∎

The first part of the following result is classically well-known, namely that the terms in the non commutative Taylor expansion can be expressed by multiple operator integrals, we however include the proof for completeness without claiming any originality. The second part gives a norm estimate of these Taylor terms by the

-semi norm of

and the

-norm of the perturbation

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
f\in\bigcap_{j=0}^{n}W^{0,j}
, let
A
be self-adjoint, and let
B
be a linear operator which is continuous from
\operatorname{dom}A^{k}
to
\operatorname{dom}A^{k-1}
for any

\mathbf{B}:=(B,\ldots,B)

is
n

\displaystyle T_{n}(f,A,B)\psi=\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A,\mathbf{B}\right)\psi,

(2.63)

and

\displaystyle R_{n}(f,A,B)\psi=n\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n-1}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A_{t},\mathbf{B}\right)\psi\operatorname{d}t.

(2.64)

If we now assume additionally Hypothesis
2.16
, and

\displaystyle T_{n}(f,A,B)\in S^{\frac{p}{n}},

(2.65)

and

\displaystyle R_{n}(f,A,B)\in S^{\frac{p}{n}}.

(2.66)

Moreover we have the estimates

\displaystyle\left\|T_{n}(f,A,B)\right\|_{S^{\frac{p}{n}}}+\left\|R_{n}(f,A,B)\right\|_{S^{\frac{p}{n}}}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,n}}\left\|B\right\|_{S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}}^{n},

where

and

Proof.

Let

. Then Lemma
2.4

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dif}_{n}g_{\xi}(\lambda)=(i\xi)^{n}\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n}g_{\xi}(s_{k}\lambda_{k})\operatorname{d}s.

(2.68)

In particular the integrand of (

. Since
\widehat{f^{(k)}}=:\mu_{k}\in\operatorname{FM}(\mathbb{R})
for

, we have

a.e., and
\operatorname{d}\mu_{k}(\xi)=(i\xi)^{k}\operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi)
. Thus,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f(\lambda)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(i\xi)^{n}\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}\prod_{k=0}^{n}g_{\xi}(s_{k}\lambda_{k})\operatorname{d}s\ \operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi),

(2.69)

which also shows that for

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A_{t},\textbf{B}\right)\psi=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(i\xi)^{n}\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}g_{\xi}(s_{0}A_{t})\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(Bg_{\xi}(s_{k}A_{t})\right)\psi\operatorname{d}s\ \operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi).

(2.70)

On the other hand for
\phi\in H
we have by Fubini’s theorem that for

,

\displaystyle f(A_{t+t^{\prime}})\phi=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g_{\xi}(A_{t+t^{\prime}})\phi\operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi),

(2.71)

Now we apply Duhamel’s formula to

,

\displaystyle g_{\xi}(A_{t+t^{\prime}})-g_{\xi}(A_{t})=it^{\prime}\xi\int_{0}^{1}g_{\xi}\left((1-s)A_{t}\right)Bg_{\xi}(sA_{t+t^{\prime}})\operatorname{d}s,

(2.72)

and by induction with

,

\displaystyle g_{\xi}(A_{t+t^{\prime}})=

\displaystyle g_{\xi}(A_{t})+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(it^{\prime}\xi)^{k}\int_{s\in\Delta_{k}}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(g_{\xi}(s_{j}A_{t})B\right)g_{\xi}(s_{k}A_{t})\operatorname{d}s

\displaystyle+(it^{\prime}\xi)^{n}\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(g_{\xi}(s_{j}A_{t})B\right)g_{\xi}(s_{n}A_{t+t^{\prime}})\operatorname{d}s,

(2.73)

Therefore, keeping in mind that
(i\xi)^{k}\operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi)
is a finite measure for
0\leq k\leq n

\displaystyle f(A_{t+t^{\prime}})\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(A_{t})\psi+(2\pi)^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}t^{\prime k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(i\xi)^{k}\int_{s\in\Delta_{k}}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(g_{\xi}(s_{j}A_{t})B\right)g_{\xi}(s_{k}A_{t})\psi\operatorname{d}s~\operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi)

\displaystyle+t^{\prime n}(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(i\xi)^{n}\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(g_{\xi}(s_{j}A_{t})B\right)g_{\xi}(s_{n}A_{t+t^{\prime}})\psi\operatorname{d}s~\operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi),

which implies together with (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\operatorname{d}t^{n}}f(A+tB)\psi=\frac{1}{n!}\left.\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\operatorname{d}t^{\prime n}}f(A+tB+t^{\prime}B)\psi\right|_{t^{\prime}=0}=T_{n}(f,A_{t},B)\psi

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(i\xi)^{n}\int_{s\in\Delta_{n}}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(g_{\xi}(s_{j}A_{t})B\right)g_{\xi}(s_{n}A_{t})\psi\operatorname{d}s~\operatorname{d}\mu_{0}(\xi)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A_{t},\textbf{B}\right)\psi.

\displaystyle f(A+B)\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T_{j}(f,A,B)\psi+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-t)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}\frac{\operatorname{d}^{n}}{\operatorname{d}t^{n}}f(A_{t})\psi\operatorname{d}t

(2.76)

\displaystyle R_{n}(f,A,B)=n\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{n-1}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A_{t},\textbf{B}\right)\operatorname{d}t.

By Lemma

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}f,A_{t},\textbf{B}\right)\right\|_{S^{\frac{p}{n}}}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{W^{m,n}}\left\|B\right\|_{S^{m,p,\alpha}_{A}}^{n},

(2.78)

which immediately implies that

, and since the estimate (

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{0}(A+\mathbbm{i})^{-m}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{n}\left((X+\mathbbm{i})^{m}f\right),A,\mathbf{B}\right)\right).

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\langle x\rangle^{-d-\epsilon},

(3.18)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,A,\mathbf{B}\right)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H}\left(T_{0}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f^{\prime},A,\mathbf{B}_{\text{ang}}\right)\right)

2.14

is non-negative self-adjoint on

by Corollary
3.11

\displaystyle D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}=H_{B,0}+M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A},\ D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}=H_{B,0}-M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}.

\displaystyle f\left(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}\right)-f\left(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)

2.18
for
f\in W^{m,d+1}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{d+1}W^{0,j}
. Let
(\psi_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)
, such that
v\otimes\psi_{l}\in\operatorname{dom}H_{B,0}^{k}
for any
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{r}
. For
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
denote
P_{j}:=\mathbbm{1}_{[-j,j]}(H_{B,0})
,
\widehat{K}_{i}:=\sum_{l=1}^{i}\langle\cdot,\psi_{l}\rangle\psi_{l}
, and
K_{i}:=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\otimes\widehat{K}_{i}
. Let
\mathbf{M}_{k}^{i,j}
be the operator vector obtained from applying
K_{i}P_{j}\cdot P_{j}K_{i}
to each entry of
\mathbf{M}_{k}
. Then strong operator convergence of
K_{i}
,
P_{j}
to
\mathbbm{1}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}
as
i,j\to\infty
, and since
P_{j}
commutes with
H_{B,0}
, the convergence improving property in Lemma
2.19
show that the following iterated limit statement is valid.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,H_{B,0},\mathbf{M}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,H_{B,0},\mathbf{M}_{k}^{i,j}\right).

(3.53)

We apply Lemma
3.5
again, noting that
K_{i}
and
P_{j}
commute with any matrix in
\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
, and obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,H_{B,0},\mathbf{M}_{k}^{i,j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,H_{B,0},K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}P_{j}K_{i}\right)

(3.54)

We may apply Lemma
2.21
and thus have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,H_{B,0},K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}P_{j}K_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\left(K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}P_{j}K_{i}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f^{\prime},H_{B,0},K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}P_{j}K_{i}\right)\right).

(3.55)

We apply Lemma
3.5
after the split
c\nabla A=c\nabla_{R}A+c\nabla_{\text{ang}}A
, and commute under the trace to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\left(K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}P_{j}K_{i}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f^{\prime},H_{B,0},K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}P_{j}K_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\left(K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{R}A}P_{j}K_{i}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f^{\prime},H_{B,0},K_{i}P_{j}M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{\text{ang}}A}P_{j}K_{i}\right)\right).

(3.56)

Now we first let
i\to\infty
and then
j\to\infty
, and both limits may passed under the trace by the same reasons as before and we obtain in total

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d}f,H_{B,0},\mathbf{M}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\left(M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{R}A}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f^{\prime},H_{B,0},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{\text{ang}}A}\right)\right).

(3.57)

Returning to (
3
) and applying Proposition
2.18
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(f\left(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}\right)-f\left(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d+1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{d}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\left[\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d+1}f,H_{B,-\frac{t}{2}},-M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d+1}f,H_{B,\frac{t}{2}},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}\right)\right]\operatorname{d}t

\displaystyle-2\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H)}\left(M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{R}A}\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f^{\prime},H_{B,0},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{\text{ang}}A}\right)\right).

(3.58)

According to Theorem
2.20
, Lemma
3.12
and Lemma
2.13
there exist unique functions
\eta_{1,t}:=\eta_{d+1,H_{B,-\frac{t}{2}},-M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A},-M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}}
,
\eta_{2,t}:=\eta_{d+1,H_{B,\frac{t}{2}},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A}}
and
\eta_{3}:=\eta_{d-1,H_{B,0},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{R}A},M_{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla_{\text{ang}}A}}
, all of which are elements in
\langle X\rangle^{N}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, such that for
f\in W^{N,d+1}_{0}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{d+1}W^{0,j}_{0}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(f\left(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}\right)-f\left(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d+1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d+1)}(\lambda)(\eta_{1,t}(\lambda)-\eta_{2,t}(\lambda))\operatorname{d}\lambda~\operatorname{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d)}(\lambda)\eta_{3}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(3.59)

The
\langle X\rangle^{N}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
-norm of the functions
\eta_{i,t}
is uniform in
t\in[0,1]
and depends measurably on
t
by Lemma
2.14
. We thus define
\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}\in\langle X\rangle^{N+1}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
via

\displaystyle\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda):=(d+1)\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{d}(\eta_{1,t}(\lambda)-\eta_{2,t}(\lambda))\operatorname{d}t-\int_{0}^{\lambda}\eta_{3}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu,~\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

(3.60)

which after integration by parts gives for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(f\left(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}\right)-f\left(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d+1)}(\lambda)\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(3.61)

Density and continuity extends (
3.61
) to
f\in W^{N,d+1}_{0}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{d+1}W^{0,j}_{0}
.
∎

We arrive at the principal result of this paper, which provides the connection of both spectral shift functions by a functional equation, which generalizes the result in
[
32
]
to higher dimensions. Moreover, we replace the spectral shift function
\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}
by its derivative, which has the more convenient order
d
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Assume Hypothesis
3.7
. Then the function
\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}
from Proposition
3.13
is weakly differentiable with
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}:=-\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{\prime}\in\langle X\rangle^{N+\frac{d}{2}}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, such that for

f\in W^{N,d}_{0}\cap W^{N,d+1}_{0}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{d+1}W^{0,j}_{0}
with
f^{(d)}\in\langle X\rangle^{-N-\frac{d}{2}}L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(f\left(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}\right)-f\left(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d)}(\lambda)\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(3.62)

The function
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
satisfies the functional equation

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)=-\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}(d-1)!}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\eta_{d,A_{0},B}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu,~\lambda>0,

(3.63)

where
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
is the function obtained from Proposition
3.6
.

Proof.

Let
t>0
, and let
f_{t}
be a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{t}(x)=e^{-tx}
for
x\geq 0
. In particular
f_{t}\in W^{N,d}_{0}\cap W^{N,d+1}_{0}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{d+1}W^{0,j}_{j}
. Then Theorem
3.4
translates to

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(f_{t}\left(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}\right)-f_{t}\left(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{d}(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H}\left(\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A_{\phi}(x)\mathcal{J}\left(\operatorname{Dif}_{d-1}f_{t}^{\prime},A_{\phi}^{2}(x),\mathbf{\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A_{\phi}(x)}\right)\right)\operatorname{d}x,

(3.64)

Both sides can be re-expressed through the spectral shift functions given in Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
3.13
respectively:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{t}^{(d+1)}(\lambda)\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=-2(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{t}^{(d)}(\mu)\eta_{d,A_{0},B}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu,

(3.65)

which gives with
\Xi:=\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}
, and
\eta:=\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda}\Xi(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=2(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\mu}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t(s+\mu)}s^{\frac{d}{2}}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu~\operatorname{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{d}\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{(d-1)!}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu~\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(3.66)

Denote for
\lambda\geq 0

\displaystyle\psi(\lambda):=\Xi(\lambda)-\frac{2}{d}\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{(d-1)!}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu.

(3.67)

We claim that
\psi=0
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. To that end, we first note that for all
\epsilon>0
we have
\lambda\mapsto e^{-s\lambda}\psi(\lambda)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
. The Stone-Weierstraß theorem implies that polynomials in
e^{-\lambda}
are dense in
C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, and therefore we have for all
\phi\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi(\lambda)e^{-\epsilon\lambda}\psi(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=0,

(3.68)

which implies that
\psi=0
a.e. by the fundamental lemma of variational calculus. Thus, for a.e.
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Xi(\lambda)=\frac{2}{d}\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{(d-1)!}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu,

(3.69)

where the right hand side is a weakly differentiable representative. Indeed let

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda):=-\pi^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{(d-1)!}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu,

(3.70)

which is an element in
\langle X\rangle^{N+\frac{d}{2}}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, since
\eta\in\langle X\rangle^{N}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
. For
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu~\operatorname{d}\lambda=\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\mu)\int_{\mu}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(\lambda)(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}}\operatorname{d}\lambda~\operatorname{d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(\mu)\int_{\mu}^{\infty}f(\lambda)(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\operatorname{d}\lambda~\operatorname{d}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\eta(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu~\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(3.71)

In particular

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda):=-\Xi^{\prime}(\lambda),

(3.72)

and consequently for
f\in W^{N,d}_{0}\cap W^{N,d+1}_{0}\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{d+1}W^{0,j}_{j}
with

f^{(d)}\in\langle X\rangle^{-N-\frac{d}{2}}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d+1)}(\lambda)\Xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d)}(\lambda)\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(3.73)

∎

We close this chapter with a result which shows that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is in fact more regular, which is an immediate consequence of the functional equation in Theorem
3.14
.

Corollary 3.15
.

Assume Hypothesis
3.7
, and let
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
be as in Theorem
3.14
. Then
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is
k=\frac{d-1}{2}
-times weakly differentiable with

\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(k)}\in\langle X\rangle^{N+\frac{1}{2}}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, and for a.e.
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(k)}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{\pi}(4\pi)^{-k}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{d,A_{0},B}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu.

(3.74)

Proof.

Let
\eta=\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\eta\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
,
\xi=\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\xi\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
. Finally denote by
X_{+}^{s}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{x>0}x^{s}
, for
x,s\in\mathbb{R}
. By Theorem
3.14
we may express
\xi
via the convolution

\displaystyle\xi=-\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}(d-1)!}X_{+}^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\ast\eta,

(3.75)

which ensures that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is
k
-times weakly differentiable satisfying (
3.74
), where the right hand side integral of (
3.74
) converges for a.e.
\lambda>0
(convolution integral). (
3.74
) also implies that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(k)}\in\langle X\rangle^{N+\frac{1}{2}}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
.
∎

4
Regularized index

In the last chapter we established the functional equation in Theorem
3.14
for the spectral shift functions
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
, and
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
, which are constructed in Proposition
3.6
and Proposition
3.13
respectively. Such a functional equation was shown first by A. Pushnitski in
[
32
]
in the one-dimensional case
d=1
. Following the functional equation the author is then able to prove an extension of the ”Index=Spectral Flow” theorem. In this chapter we will use a similar approach to give an extension of the Callias’ index theorem. The key to this approach is the regular behaviour of the spectral shift functions near zero.

More precisely we showed in Corollary
3.15
that the functional equation implies that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is in general
\frac{d-1}{2}
-times differentiable. In this chapter we investigate the situation in which
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is even more regular, at least from the right at
0
. We will show that such a condition gives rise to a notion of regularized index, which corresponds to the Witten index in the one-dimensional case. The idea of using spectral shift to generalize the Fredholm index to non-Fredholm situations has a tradition which goes back to
[
22
]
. Our first result in this chapter is a minor modification of
[
22
]
[Theorem 2.4] and a result in
[
13
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume Hypothesis
3.7
. Assume that the spectral shift function
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is
(d-1)
-times weakly differentiable with
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}\in e^{tX}L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for some
t>0
. Furthermore assume that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}
has a right Lebesgue point at
0
. Then the following limit exists

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(e^{-tD_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}}-e^{-tD_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}}\right)=-\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(0+).

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
t>0
, and set
\xi:=\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(e^{-tD_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}}-e^{-tD_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}}\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(-t)^{d}e^{-t\lambda}\xi(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}(-t)e^{-t\lambda}\xi^{(d-1)}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=-t^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\xi^{(d-1)}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu~\operatorname{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu e^{-\nu}\left[\frac{t}{\nu}\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{t}}\xi^{(d-1)}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu\right]\operatorname{d}\nu.

(4.2)

The expression in
[\cdot]
-brackets converges for
\nu>0
to
\xi^{(d-1)}(0+)
a
t\to\infty
, by the definition of a right Lebesgue point. Since
\int_{0}^{\infty}\nu e^{-\nu}\operatorname{d}\nu=1
, we may assume without loss in the following that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(0+)=0
.

In particular, there are constants
\epsilon>0
and
C>0
, such that for
\nu<\epsilon t
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{t}{\nu}\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{t}}\xi^{(d-1)}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu\right|\leq C.

(4.3)

On the other hand there is
t_{0}
, such that for
t\geq t_{0}
,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{t}{\nu}\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{t}}\xi^{(d-1)}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu\right|\leq Ce^{\frac{\nu}{2}}.

(4.4)

So there is a constant
C^{\prime}
, such that for
t\geq t_{0}
,
\nu>0
,

\displaystyle\nu e^{-t\nu}\left|\frac{t}{\nu}\int_{0}^{\frac{\nu}{t}}\xi^{(d-1)}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu\right|\leq C\nu e^{-\nu}\mathbbm{1}_{\nu<\epsilon t}+C\epsilon^{-1}\nu e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\nu\geq\epsilon t}\leq C^{\prime}\nu e^{-\frac{\nu}{2}}.

(4.5)

The claim then follows by application of the dominated convergence theorem as
t\to\infty
.
∎

In view of the definition of semi-group regularized Witten index in
[
22
]
, and the proposition above, we suggest the following definition of partial Witten index.

Definition 4.2
.

Assume Hypothesis
3.7
. Assume that the spectral shift function
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is
(d-1)
-times weakly differentiable with
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}\in e^{tX}L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for some
t>0
. Furthermore assume that
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}
admits a right Lebesgue point at
0
. Then define the
partial Witten index

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}:=-\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(0+).

(4.6)

The next Lemma justifies the word ”index” for the quantity
\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}
, because in the Fredholm case it agrees with the Fredholm index of
D_{B}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume Hypothesis
3.7
, and assume that
D_{B}
is Fredholm with index
\operatorname{ind}D_{B}
. Then
D_{B}
admits a partial Witten index and

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}=\operatorname{ind}D_{B}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
, such that
[0,\delta)\cap\left(\sigma(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B})\cup\sigma(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast})\right)\subseteq\{0\}
. Let

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda):=\begin{cases}\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda),&\lambda\geq 0,\\
0,&\lambda<0,\end{cases}

(4.8)

and for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left((-\delta,\delta)\right)
define the distribution

\displaystyle T(f):=\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}f(\lambda)\xi(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda.

(4.9)

Then, for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left((-\delta,\delta)\right)
,

\displaystyle f(0)\operatorname{ind}D_{B}=f(0)\left(\dim\ker D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B}-\dim\ker D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},H)}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}\left(f(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B})-f(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{(d)}(\lambda)\xi_{d,A,B}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=T\left(f^{(d)}\right)=-T^{(d)}(f),

(4.10)

which implies that there exists a polynomial
p
of degree
d-1
such that for a.e.
\lambda\in(-\delta,\delta)
,

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=p(\lambda)-\operatorname{ind}D_{B}\cdot\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda^{d-1}}{(d-1)!},&\lambda\geq 0,\\
0,&\lambda<0.\end{cases}

(4.11)

Since
\xi\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
, it follows that
p\equiv 0
. In particular
\xi
is
(d-1)
-times weakly differentiable with

\xi^{(d-1)}(\lambda)=-\operatorname{ind}D_{B}
for a.e.
0<\lambda<\delta
, which proves the claim.
∎

The functional equation in Theorem
3.14
allows us to impose conditions on the spectral shift function
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
, which ensure that
\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}
exists. Moreover we obtain an index formula in this case. This is the second main result of this paper with which we close this chapter. It provides the appropriate analogue for ”
A
needs to be invertible outside a compact region” implied by the Fredholmness condition in the classical Callias’ theorem translated to our in general non-Fredholm setup.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume Hypothesis
3.7
. Assume that the spectral shift function
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
is
\frac{d-1}{2}
-times weakly differentiable with
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}^{(j)}(0)=0
for all
0\leq j\leq\frac{d-3}{2}
. Then
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is
(d-1)
-times weakly differentiable such that for a.e.
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{\pi}(4\pi)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\eta_{d,A_{0},B}^{(\frac{d-1}{2})}(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu.

(4.12)

If additionally
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}^{(\frac{d-1}{2})}\in e^{tX}L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for some
t>0
, and the function
u\mapsto u\eta_{d,A_{0},B}^{(\frac{d-1}{2})}(u^{2})
admits a right Lebesgue point at
0
with value
L_{d,A_{0},B}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{h\searrow 0}\int_{0}^{h}\left(u\eta_{d,A_{0},B}^{(\frac{d-1}{2})}(u^{2})-L_{d,A_{0},B}\right)\operatorname{d}u=0,

(4.13)

then also
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}
admits a right Lebesgue point at
0
, and

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}=-\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(0+)=(4\pi)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}L_{d,A_{0},B}.

(4.14)

Proof.

Let
k:=\frac{d-1}{2}
,
\eta=\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\eta\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
,
\xi=\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
on
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\xi\equiv 0
on
\mathbb{R}_{<0}
. Finally denote by
X_{+}^{s}(x):=\mathbbm{1}_{x>0}x^{s}
, for
x,s\in\mathbb{R}
. By Corollary
3.15
we may express
\xi^{(k)}
via the convolution

\displaystyle\xi^{(k)}=-\frac{1}{\pi}(4\pi)^{-k}X_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ast\eta.

(4.15)

The boundary conditions on
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
ensure that
\eta
is also
k
-times weakly differentiable. Thus
\xi
is also
(d-1)
-times weakly differentiable and for a.e.
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{\pi}(4\pi)^{-k}\left(X_{+}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\ast\eta^{(k)}\right)(\lambda),

(4.16)

which gives (
4.12
), which implies for
h>0
, and
f(u):=\frac{1}{\pi}(4\pi)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}u\eta_{d,A_{0},B}^{(\frac{d-1}{2})}(u^{2})
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda=-\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}f(\mu^{\frac{1}{2}})\operatorname{d}\mu~\operatorname{d}\lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}(\lambda-u^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}f(u)\operatorname{d}u~\operatorname{d}\lambda=-\frac{2}{h}\int_{0}^{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(h-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}f(u)\operatorname{d}u.

(4.17)

Since
\frac{2}{h}\int_{0}^{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}(h-u^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{d}u=\pi
, without loss we may assume that
f(0+)=0
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{h}\int_{0}^{h}\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}(\lambda)\operatorname{d}\lambda\right|\leq\frac{2}{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{h^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left|f(u)\right|\operatorname{d}u\xrightarrow{h\searrow 0}0,

(4.18)

which shows (
4.14
).
∎

5
Example and comparison with results in one dimension

Before we present a concrete example to which we apply the theory developed in the previous chapter, it is didactically sensible to compare our results, which cover the case
d\geq 3
odd with known results for the case
d=1
. Indeed, although beyond the scope of this paper, the formula presented in Theorem
4.4
is compatible with the formula shown in
[
13
]
in the case
d=1
. To see that let us pretend that our definition of the spectral shift functions
\eta=\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
and
\xi=\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
extends to
d=1
. Let us also assume that
A_{\pm}:=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}A(x)
exist strongly on
\operatorname{dom}A_{0}
with
A_{-}=A_{0}
, and that the Krein spectral shift function
\kappa=\kappa_{A_{+},A_{-}}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
of the pair
A_{\pm}
exists. Then let
A_{\phi}(x):=A_{-}+(1-\phi(x))B(x)
for
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\phi\equiv 1
near
0
. In particular
\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}A_{\phi}(x)=A_{\pm}
on
\operatorname{dom}A_{-}
. We make the following heuristic observations.

Heuristic 5.1
.

Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
, and define the bounded smooth function
F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle F(x):=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\int_{0}^{x^{2}}f(y)\frac{\operatorname{d}y}{2\sqrt{y}},

(5.1)

which satisfies
F^{\prime}(x)=f(x^{2}).

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\frac{\kappa(\sqrt{x})+\kappa(-\sqrt{x})}{2\sqrt{x}}\operatorname{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x^{2})\kappa(x)\operatorname{d}x=\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\left(F(A_{+})-F(A_{-})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\partial_{x}F(A_{\phi}(x))\operatorname{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\left(A_{\phi}^{\prime}(x)F^{\prime}(A_{\phi}(x))\right)\operatorname{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\operatorname{Tr}_{H}\left(A_{\phi}^{\prime}(x)f(A_{\phi}^{2}(x))\right)\operatorname{d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\eta(x)\operatorname{d}x,

(5.2)

where in the penultimate line we used a well-known result on differentiation under the trace (we refer to
[
30
]
for a rigorous discussion). This shows that for a.e.
x>0
,

\displaystyle\eta(x)=\frac{\kappa(\sqrt{x})+\kappa(-\sqrt{x})}{2\sqrt{x}}.

(5.3)

For
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,\infty))
, and
G(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(y)\operatorname{d}y
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)\xi(x)\operatorname{d}x=\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},H)}\left(G(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B})-G(D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast})\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}g(x)K(x)\operatorname{d}x,

(5.4)

where
K=K_{D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B},D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast}}
is the Krein spectral shift function of the pair

(D_{B}^{\ast}D_{B},D_{B}D_{B}^{\ast})
. It follows that
\xi=K
holds a.e..

The functional equation of Theorem
3.14
then gives for a.e.
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle K(\lambda)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\lambda}(\lambda-\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\kappa(\sqrt{\mu})+\kappa(-\sqrt{\mu})}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\operatorname{d}\mu=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\sqrt{\lambda}}^{\sqrt{\lambda}}(\lambda-\mu^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\kappa(\mu)\operatorname{d}\mu

(5.5)

which is the principal functional equation of spectral shift functions found in
[
32
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
13
]
.

Moreover we have for
u>0

\displaystyle u\eta(u^{2})=\frac{\kappa(u)+\kappa(-u)}{2},

(5.6)

which in regard of Theorem
4.4
implies that
D_{B}
admits a (partial) Witten index
\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}
if

\frac{\kappa(u)+\kappa(-u)}{2}
admits a (right) Lebesgue point at
0
with value
L
, and that in that case we have the index formula

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{B}=L.

(5.7)

This formula is a slight generalization of the Witten index formulas shown in
[
32
]
,
[
20
]
, and
[
13
]
(of which the last paper listed gives the most general version), since
\kappa
admitting a left and right Lebesgue point at
0
implies that
u\mapsto\frac{\kappa(u)+\kappa(-u)}{2}
admits a Lebesgue point at
0
.

To illustrate our results from the previous chapter, let us conclude this paper with a non-trivial example. We will consider the so-called
(d+1)
-massless Dirac-Schrödinger operator, of which the trace formula in Theorem
3.4
was discussed in
[
19
]
and we will determine both spectral shift functions
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
and
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
in this case. It should be noted that
(1+1)
-massless Dirac-Schrödinger operators where first discussed in
[
6
]
, and provided the first examples of differential operators which attain any real number as Witten index. Such a surjectivity result also holds for the partial Witten index in higher dimensions, which has been shown in
[
19
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
be odd, and
G
a separable complex Hilbert space. Let
V\in C_{b}^{4}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},B_{sa}\left(G\right)\right)
, where
B_{sa}\left(G\right)
is equipped with the strong operator topology. Furthermore assume that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that for
i\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
,
k\in\left\{0,1,2\right\}
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
0\leq|\gamma|\leq 2
,

\displaystyle\left\|y\mapsto\partial_{x^{i}}\partial_{y}^{k}V\left(x,y\right)\right\|_{L^{d}\left(\mathbb{R},S^{d}\left(G\right)\right)}+\left\|y\mapsto\partial_{x}^{\gamma}\partial_{x^{i}}V\left(x,y\right)\right\|_{L^{d}\left(\mathbb{R},S^{d}\left(G\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\left|x\right|^{-1}\right),\ \left|x\right|\to\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|y\mapsto\partial_{R}\partial_{y}^{k}V\left(x,y\right)\right\|_{L^{d-\epsilon}\left(\mathbb{R},S^{d-\epsilon}\left(G\right)\right)}+\left\|y\mapsto\partial^{\gamma}_{x}\partial_{R}V\left(x,y\right)\right\|_{L^{d-\epsilon}\left(\mathbb{R},S^{d-\epsilon}\left(G\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\left|x\right|^{-1-\epsilon}\right),\ \left|x\right|\to\infty,

(5.8)

where
\partial_{R}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{x^{i}}{\left|x\right|}\partial_{x^{i}}
is the radial derivative. We also assume that for any
\widehat{x}\in S_{1}\left(0\right)
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
\left|\gamma\right|\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}R^{\left|\gamma\right|}\left\|\left(\partial^{\gamma}_{x}V\right)\left(x_{0}+R\widehat{x},\cdot\right)-\left(\partial^{\gamma}_{x}V\right)\left(R\widehat{x},\cdot\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R},B\left(G\right)\right)}=0.

(5.9)

Define the
(d+1)
- massless Dirac-Schrödinger operator with potential
V
for
f\in W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes G\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
y\in\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\left(D_{V}f\right)\left(x,y\right):=\mathbbm{i}\sum_{j=1}^{d}c^{j}\partial_{x^{j}}f\left(x,y\right)+\mathbbm{i}\partial_{y}f\left(x,y\right)+V\left(x,y\right)f\left(x,y\right),

(5.10)

where
r=2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}
is the rank of the Clifford matrices
(c^{j})_{j=1}^{d}
.

For such an operator we have the following result.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume that
V
and
D_{V}
are as in Definition
5.2
. Then Hypothesis
3.7
holds with
H=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},G)
,
A_{0}=\mathbbm{i}\partial_{y}
,
B(x)=M_{V(x,\cdot)}
for
\alpha>1
small enough such that
N=1
. Let the family of unitaries
U^{V}
be given by
U^{V}\left(x\right):=\lim_{n\to\infty}U^{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}\left(n,-n\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, for a given
T:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow B_{sa}\left(H\right)
is the (unique) evolution system of the equation

\displaystyle u^{\prime}\left(y\right)=\mathbbm{i}T\left(y\right)u\left(y\right),\ y\in\mathbb{R},

(5.11)

i.e. for
y,y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{1}}U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=

\displaystyle\mathbbm{i}T\left(y_{1}\right)U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{y_{2}}U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)=

\displaystyle-\mathbbm{i}U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)T\left(y_{2}\right),

\displaystyle U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)U^{T}\left(y_{2},y_{3}\right)=

\displaystyle U^{T}\left(y_{1},y_{3}\right),

\displaystyle U^{T}\left(y,y\right)=

\displaystyle 1_{G}.

(5.12)

Then
\eta_{d,A_{0},B}\in\langle X\rangle L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
, and
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}\in\langle X\rangle^{\frac{d}{2}+1}L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
exist with

\displaystyle\eta_{d,A_{0},B}(\mu)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Omega_{d}(\mu,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z,

\displaystyle\xi_{d,A_{0},B}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Sigma_{d}(\lambda,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z,

(5.13)

where for
\lambda,\mu>0
,
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z):=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{d}(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\left(2\mathbbm{i}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{Tr}_{G}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}(\operatorname{d}_{x}V)(x,z_{j-1})U^{V(x,\cdot)}(z_{j-1},z_{j})\right),

(5.14)

\displaystyle\Omega_{d}(\mu,z):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\mu}{a(s,z)}\right)^{\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\left(2\sqrt{a(s,z)\mu}\right)\operatorname{d}s,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{d}(\lambda,z):=

\displaystyle-(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\lambda}{a(s,z)}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}J_{d-1}\left(2\sqrt{a(s,z)\lambda}\right)\operatorname{d}s,

\displaystyle a(s,z):=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{(z_{j-1}-z_{j})^{2}}{4s_{j}},~s\in\Delta_{d-1},

(5.15)

with convention
z_{0}:=z_{d}
, and where
J_{\nu}
denote Bessel functions of the first kind. Moreover
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}
is
(d-1)
-times differentiable and
\xi_{d,A_{0},B}^{(d-1)}
continuous from the right at
0
. In particular
D_{V}
admits a partial Witten index and

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{V}

\displaystyle=-\xi^{(d-1)}(0+)=\left(2\pi\mathbbm{i}\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{d!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{Tr}_{G}\left(\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}\operatorname{d}U^{V}\right)^{\wedge d},

(5.16)

where
\wedge
denotes the exterior product.

Proof.

In
[
19
]
[Lemma 2.5] it is shown that under the made assumptions, Hypothesis
3.2
holds for
\alpha>1
arbitrarily small such that one may choose
N=1
. Repeating the proof of
[
19
]
[Lemma 2.5] almost ad verbatim, the stronger requirement of Hypothesis
3.7
is also satisfied under the stronger requirements made in Definition
5.2
.
In
[
19
]
[Theorem 3.5] it is shown that Theorem
3.4
holds with
D_{B}=D_{V}
, and that we may even choose
\phi\equiv 0
. In the proof of
[
19
]
[Theorem 3.5] it is moreover shown that for
A\left(x\right)=\mathbbm{i}\partial_{y}+M_{V\left(x,\cdot\right)}
,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{d}(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}t^{\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes G)}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(\mathbbm{i}c\nabla A)(x)e^{-ts_{j}A^{2}(x)}\right)\operatorname{d}s~\operatorname{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{d}(t,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z.

(5.17)

We re-express the left hand side of (
5
) via the spectral shift function
\eta=\eta_{d,A_{0},B}
, and thus obtain for its Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}(\eta)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\eta)(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(4\pi)^{\frac{d}{2}}t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{d}(t,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left[t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{a(s,z)}{t}\right)\right]\operatorname{d}s\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z

(5.18)

A well-known result about Bessel functions (Schläfli’s integral
[
36
]
[§6.2 (1)] combined with Laplace transform inversion) is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\mu\mapsto\left(\frac{\mu}{a}\right)^{\frac{d}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}(2\sqrt{a\mu})\right)(t)=t^{-\frac{d}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{a}{t}\right),~a,t>0,

(5.19)

which allows us to rewrite the term in brackets

in (

\displaystyle\eta(\mu)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Omega_{d}(\mu,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z.

(5.20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\xi)(t)=-t^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{d}(t,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left[t^{-d}\exp\left(-\frac{a(s,z)}{t}\right)\right]\operatorname{d}s\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z,

(5.21)

which by (

) gives

\displaystyle\xi(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Sigma_{d}(\lambda,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z.

(5.22)

The function
\xi
is
(d-1)
-times differentiable with

\displaystyle\xi^{(d-1)}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\lambda}^{d-1}\Sigma_{d}(\lambda,z)\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z,

\displaystyle\partial_{\lambda}^{d-1}\Sigma_{d}(\lambda,z)=

\displaystyle-(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)J_{0}\left(2\sqrt{a(s,z)\lambda}\right)\operatorname{d}s.

(5.23)

In particular,
\xi^{(d-1)}
is right continuous at

with value

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{W}D_{V}

\displaystyle=-\xi^{(d-1)}(0+)=(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\left(\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{d}s\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z\right)

\displaystyle=\left(2\pi\mathbbm{i}\right)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{d!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{Tr}_{G}\left(\left(U^{V}\right)^{-1}\operatorname{d}U^{V}\right)^{\wedge d},

(5.24)

where we used that

\displaystyle\int_{s\in\Delta_{d-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(4\pi s_{j}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{d}u=\left(4\pi\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)!}{\left(d-1\right)!},

(5.25)

and according to the proof of
[
19
]
[Theorem 3.5],

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{ind}_{V}(z)\operatorname{d}z=\frac{2}{d}(4\pi)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\left(2\mathbbm{i}\right)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\mathbbm{i}^{-d}\int_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\operatorname{Tr}_{G}\left(\left(U^{V}\left(x\right)\right)^{-1}\left(\operatorname{d}U^{V}\right)\left(x\right)\right)^{\wedge d}.

(5.26)

Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities with convex weight in space forms
Kwok-Kun Kwong, Yong Wei

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive new sharp weighted Alexandrov-Fenchel and Minkowski inequalities for smooth, closed hypersurfaces under various convexity assumptions in Euclidean, spherical, and hyperbolic spaces. These inequalities extend classical results by incorporating weights given by convex, non-decreasing positive functions, which are otherwise arbitrary. Our approach gives rise to a broad family of geometric inequalities, as each convex, non-decreasing function yields a corresponding inequality, providing considerable flexibility.

53C42; 53E10

1.
Introduction

The Alexandrov-Fenchel and Minkowski inequalities are fundamental in differential geometry, geometric analysis, and convex geometry. These inequalities establish fundamental relationships between volume, surface area, and curvature integrals of hypersurfaces enclosing bounded domains. These inequalities have important applications ranging from isoperimetric problems to eigenvalue estimates and geometric flows.
While classical results focus on specific geometric quantities, the study of weighted inequalities offers a more flexible approach by incorporating weights that can address a broader range of problems. In this paper, we extend these classical inequalities by introducing weights that are convex functions of the potential associated with the metric. Each convex weight function gives rise to a corresponding inequality, reflecting geometric structures such as non-uniform densities or radial symmetries. This work introduces a broad family of Alexandrov-Fenchel and Minkowski type inequalities, which are anticipated to have applications in other geometric and analytic problems.

To set the stage for our results, we first give some definitions of the classical quermassintegrals.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded domain enclosed by an embedded hypersurface
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
. The quermassintegrals of
\Omega
can be expressed (up to a constant) as follows:

\displaystyle W_{-1}(\Omega)=|\Omega|,\qquad W_{0}(\Omega)=|\Sigma|,

\displaystyle W_{k}(\Omega)=\int_{\Sigma}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)d\mu,\quad k=1,\cdots,n-1,

where
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
the
k
th mean curvature of
\Sigma
, defined as the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial of the principal curvatures
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})
of
\Sigma
. The classical Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality states that for convex bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(1.1)

W_{k}(\Omega)\geq c_{n,k}\left(W_{k-1}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{n-1-k}{n-k}},\quad\forall~k=0,\cdots,n-1

for some constant
c_{n,k}
with equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is a round ball. These include the isoperimetric inequality and Minkowski inequality

(1.2)

\int_{\Sigma}Hd\mu\geq(n-1)\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}|\Sigma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}

as special cases, where
\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
is the area of the unit sphere. We refer the readers to Schneider’s book
[
30
]
for a detailed introduction.

Guan-Li
[
15
]
showed that the inequalities (
1.1
) still hold for star-shaped and
k
-convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by using the inverse curvature flows. See
[
1
,
10
,
25
,
26
]
for proofs of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) using other methods.

The study of weighted integral inequalities extends classical results by incorporating weights, offering greater flexibility to address a broader range of geometric and analytic problems. Our results, presented in the following two subsections, allow the weight function to be any convex, non-decreasing positive function. The flexibility lies in the fact that the weight functions can vary, with each taking the potential associated with the metric as its variable, and each function giving rise to a corresponding inequality that reflects non-uniform densities or radial symmetries.
To illustrate this flexibility, we provide more than a dozen sharp weighted integral inequalities that can be derived in the two-dimensional space forms, as given in Example
1
.

Weighted inequalities have been the subject of extensive research in recent decades. Notable examples include the isoperimetric problem with Gaussian density
[
4
]
and the isoperimetric problem with radial densities
[
3
]
. Additionally, weighted inequalities such as the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities
[
9
]
and weighted Sobolev inequalities
[
8
]
have been investigated, highlighting the importance of these weighted integrals.
In our previous work
[
24
]
, a weighted isoperimetric-type inequality was instrumental in resolving the conjecture that the Weinstock inequality for the first Steklov eigenvalue holds for simply connected mean-convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The weighted Alexandrov-Fenchel and Minkowski inequalities we establish here are expected to have further applications in analysis, convex geometry, isoperimetric inequalities, and eigenvalue estimates.

1.1.
Weighted Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In the first part of the paper, we study the Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
comparing the weighted curvature integrals and the quermassintegrals. This kind of inequality was firstly studied by the first author and Miao
[
21
]
, where the inequality

(1.3)

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2}Hd\mu\geq n(n-1)|\Omega|

was proved for star-shaped and mean convex hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
which encloses a bounded domain
\Omega
. Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere centered at the origin. The inequality (
1.3
) has been generalized to higher order mean curvature cases in
[
14
,
22
,
32
]
. Recently, Wu
[
33
]
obtained a further generalization with the weight
r^{2}
replaced by
r^{2\alpha}
for any
\alpha\geq 1
. The results are summarized as following

Theorem A

(
[
14
,
22
,
32
,
33
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed, star-shaped, and
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 2)
. Denote by
\Omega
the domain enclosed by
\Sigma
. Then, for all
1\leq k\leq n-1,-1\leq\ell\leq k-1
, and
\alpha\geq 1
, the following inequality holds:

\int_{\Sigma}r^{2\alpha}\sigma_{k}d\mu\geq\binom{n-1}{k}\omega_{n-1}\left(\frac{W_{\ell}(\Omega)}{\binom{n-1}{\ell}\omega_{n-1}}\right)^{\frac{n-1-k+2\alpha}{n-1-l}},

where
\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
is the area of the unit round sphere. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere centered at the origin. (Here
\binom{n-1}{-1}:=\frac{1}{n-1}
.)

Our first result is the following Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a general convex weight.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a smooth closed star-shaped and
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 2)
. Denote by
\Omega
the domain enclosed by
\Sigma
and
\Phi=r^{2}/2
. Then, for all
1\leq k\leq n-1,-1\leq\ell\leq k-1
, and any non-decreasing convex
C^{2}
positive function
g
, we have

(1.4)

\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu\geq\chi\circ\xi^{-1}\Big(W_{\ell}(\Omega)\Big),

where
\chi(r)=\int_{S^{n-1}(r)}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}=\binom{n-1}{k}\omega_{n-1}g(\frac{r^{2}}{2})r^{n-k-1}
and
\xi(r)=\binom{n-1}{\ell}\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1-\ell}
are both strictly increasing functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
\xi^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
\xi(r)
.

If either
g
is strictly increasing or
g
is strictly convex, then equality holds in (
1.4
) if and only if
\Omega
is a round ball centered at the origin.

This result can be restated in a more qualitative form as an isoperimetric-type statement:

Theorem 1.2
.

Among the class of star-shaped and
k
-convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with fixed quermassintegral
W_{\ell}
, where
g
is a convex and non-decreasing function, the minimum of the weighted curvature integral
\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}\,d\mu\,(k>\ell)
is achieved by the round spheres centered at the origin.

For the proof of (
1.4
), we apply the normalized inverse curvature flow studied by Gerhardt
[
12
]
and Urbas
[
31
]
for star-shaped and
k
-convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As a key step, we show that the weighted curvature integral
\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu
is monotone non-increasing and the quermassintegral
W_{\ell}
is non-decreasing for
\ell<k
along the flow.

1.2.
Weighted Minkowski inequality in space forms

The Minkwoski inequality (
1.2
) is a special case of the Alexandrov inequalities (
1.1
). Recently, due to the close relationship with the Penrose type inequalities in asymptotically hyperbolic manifolds, the weighted Minkowski type inequality has attracted much attention. In particular, for star-shaped and mean convex hypersurface
\Sigma
in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
which encloses a bounded domain
\Omega
, Brendle-Hung-Wang
[
6
]
proved

(1.5)

\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}(r)Hd\mu-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(r)dv\geq(n-1)\omega_{n-1}^{\frac{1}{n-1}}|\Sigma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}},

where
\phi(r)=\sinh r
. See
[
11
]
for a related inequality. The Minkowski inequality for a star-shaped and mean convex domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}

(1.6)

\int_{\Sigma}Hd\mu-(n-1)|\Omega|\geq\chi\circ\xi^{-1}\left(|\Sigma|\right)

was also proved by Brendle-Guan-Li
[
5
]
(see also
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
), where
\chi(r)=(n-1)\omega_{n-1}\left(\coth r\sinh^{n-1}r-\int_{0}^{r}\sinh^{n-1}sds\right)
and
\xi(r)=\omega_{n-1}\sinh^{n-1}r
are both strictly increasing function and
\xi^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
\xi
. The left hand side of (
1.6
) in fact represents the first quermassintegral
W_{1}(\Omega)
of a bounded domain in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Another version of Minkowski type inequality was established recently by the authors
[
24
]
for
h
-convex domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
:

(1.7)

\int_{\Sigma}\Phi(r)Hd\mu+(n-1)|\Omega|\geq\chi\circ\xi^{-1}\left(|\Sigma|\right),

where
\Phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\phi(s)ds=\cosh r-1
and

\chi(r)=(n-1)\omega_{n-1}\left((\cosh r-1)\coth r\sinh^{n-1}r+\int_{0}^{r}\sinh^{n-1}sds\right).

Note that the left hand side of (
1.7
) is equivalent to
\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{\prime}(r)Hd\mu-W_{1}(\Omega)
. Here by
h
-convex we mean that all principal curvatures of
\Sigma
satisfy
\kappa_{i}\geq 1,i=1,\cdots,n-1
everywhere on
\Sigma
.

In the second part of this paper, we will discuss various generalizations of the above inequalities in space forms. Before stating our results, we recall some definitions. A space form
M^{n}(K),n\geq 2,
is a real simply connected
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with constant sectional curvature equal to
K\in\{-1,0,1\}
. We view
M^{n}(K)
as a warped product manifold
I\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
equipped with the metric

\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\phi(r)^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}.

There are three cases:
M^{n}(K)
is the Euclidean space if
K=0,I=[0,\infty)
and
\phi(r)=r
;
M^{n}(K)
is the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
if
K=-1,I=[0,\infty)
and
\phi(r)=\sinh r
; and
M^{n}(K)
is the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if
K=1,I=[0,\pi)
and
\phi(r)=\sin r
.
Define
\Phi(r)
by

\Phi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\phi(s)ds=\begin{cases}1-\cos r,&K=1\\
\dfrac{r^{2}}{2},&K=0\\
\cosh r-1,&K=-1\end{cases}

It’s well known that
V=\bar{\nabla}\Phi=\phi(r)\partial_{r}
is a conformal Killing field on
M^{n}(K)
satisfying
\bar{\nabla}V=\phi^{\prime}(r)\bar{g}
.

Let
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-decreasing convex
C^{2}
function. For a bounded domain
\Omega
in a space form
M^{n}(K)
, we define the weighted volume of
\Omega

(1.8)

A_{g}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\left(g^{\prime}(\Phi)\phi^{\prime}+Kg(\Phi)\right)dv.

When
g(x)=x
, we have
g^{\prime}(\Phi)\phi^{\prime}+Kg(\Phi)=\phi^{\prime}+K\Phi=1
. Then
A_{g}(\Omega)=\text{vol}(\Omega)
is just the volume of the domain
\Omega
. When
g(x)\equiv 1
, then
A_{g}(\Omega)=K\text{vol}(\Omega)
is also the volume of
\Omega
up to a constant
K
.

We first prove an inequality for convex curves in the two dimensional case.

Theorem 1.3
.

For a smooth, closed, strictly convex curve
\gamma=\partial\Omega
on
M^{2}(K)
, with
\gamma
enclosing the origin, and a non-decreasing convex
C^{2}
function
g
, we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma}g(\Phi)\kappa ds+A_{g}(\Omega)\geq 2g\left(\frac{\mathrm{A}(L)}{2\pi}\right)\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-KL^{2}}-2\pi g(0)+4\pi KG\left(\frac{\mathrm{A}(L)}{2\pi}\right).

Here
\kappa,L
are the curvature and length of the curve
\gamma
respectively,
\mathrm{A}(l):=
the area of the (convex) geodesic ball in
M^{2}(K)
with circumference
l
and
G(r):=\int_{0}^{r}g(t)dt
.

If either
g
is strictly increasing or
g
is strictly convex, then the equality holds if and only if
\gamma
is a geodesic circle centered at the origin.

Example 1
.

For illustration, we present examples of sharp inequalities involving three geometric quantities that can be derived for strictly convex curves in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by applying various choices of the function
g
in (
1.9
):

(1)

\int_{\gamma}\frac{r^{2}}{2}\kappa{ds}+|\Omega|\geq\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi}
.

(2)

\int_{\gamma}\frac{r^{3}}{3}\kappa{ds}+\int_{\Omega}{r}dv\geq\frac{L^{3}}{6\pi^{2}}
.

(3)

\int_{\gamma}\frac{r^{4}}{4}\kappa{ds}+\int_{\Omega}r^{2}dv\geq\frac{L^{4}}{16\pi^{3}}
.

(4)

\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}dv\geq 2\pi\left(2e^{\frac{L^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}}-1\right)
.

(5)

\int_{\gamma}e^{\frac{r^{4}}{4}}\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}dv\geq 2\pi\left(2e^{\frac{L^{4}}{64\pi^{4}}}-1\right)
.

(6)

\int_{\gamma}\sinh\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right)\kappa{ds}+\int_{\Omega}\cosh\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right)dv\geq 4\pi\sinh\left(\frac{L^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\right)
.

(7)

\int_{\gamma}\cosh\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right)\kappa{ds}+\int_{\Omega}\sinh\left(\frac{r^{2}}{2}\right)dv\geq 4\pi\cosh\left(\frac{L^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\right)-2\pi
.

If
\gamma
is a strictly convex curve enclosing the center of an open geodesic ball of radius
\frac{\pi}{2}
in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, then the following sharp inequalities hold:

(1)

\int_{\gamma}(1-\cos r)\kappa ds+|\Omega|\geq\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi}
.

(2)

\int_{\gamma}(1-\cos r)^{2}\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}\sin^{2}rdv\geq\frac{4}{3}\left(\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-L^{2}}-2\pi\right)-\frac{L^{2}}{3\pi^{2}}\left(\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-L^{2}}-3\pi\right)
.

(3)

\int_{\gamma}(1-\cos r)^{3}\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}(1-\cos r)^{2}(2\cos r+1)dv\geq-\frac{3L^{4}}{16\pi^{3}}-\frac{L^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\left(\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-L^{2}}-3\pi\right)+4\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-L^{2}}-8\pi

(4)

\int_{\gamma}\left(\sec r-1\right)\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}(2\sec r-1)dv\geq 4\pi\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-L^{2}}}\right)
.

(5)

\int_{\gamma}\left(\cos r+\sec r-2\right)\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}(2\sec r-2)dv\geq 4\pi\log\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-L^{2}}}\right)-\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi}
.

These inequalities are derived by substituting
g(x)
with
x,x^{2},x^{3}
,
\frac{x}{1-x}
and
\frac{x^{2}}{1-x}
, respectively. Note that the last two functions are convex over the range of
\Phi
.

Similarly, in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, we have the following sharp inequalities for strictly convex curves:

(1)

\int_{\gamma}(\cosh r-1)\kappa ds+|\Omega|\geq\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi}
.

(2)

\int_{\gamma}(\cosh r-1)^{2}\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}\sinh^{2}rdv\geq\frac{L^{2}}{3\pi^{2}}\left(\sqrt{L^{2}+4\pi^{2}}-3\pi\right)+\frac{4}{3}\left(\sqrt{L^{2}+4\pi^{2}}-2\pi\right)
.

(3)

\int_{\gamma}(\cosh r-1)^{3}\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}(\cosh r-1)^{2}(2\cosh r+1)dv\geq\frac{3L^{4}}{16\pi^{3}}-\frac{L^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\left(\sqrt{L^{2}+4\pi^{2}}-3\pi\right)-4\sqrt{L^{2}+4\pi^{2}}+8\pi
.

(4)

\int_{\gamma}\left(\cosh r+\textrm{sech }r-2\right)\kappa ds+\int_{\Omega}(2-2\textrm{sech }r)dv\geq\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi}-2\pi\log\left(\frac{L^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}+1\right)
.

These inequalities are obtained by substituting
g(x)
as
x,x^{2},x^{3}
, and
\frac{x^{2}}{1+x}
, respectively.

When
g=1
, the inequality in Theorem
1.3
reduces to an equality, corresponding to the classical Gauss-Bonnet formula. The first inequality in the three lists above was proved by the authors and G. Wheeler and V.-M.Wheeler in
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
for the specific case where
g(x)=x
, and was applied in
[
23
, Corollary 5.8]
to provide a counterexample to the
n=2
case of a conjecture by Girão and Pinheiro
[
13
]
.

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we employ the inverse curvature type flow
X:\mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,T)\rightarrow M^{2}(K)

(1.10)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X(x,t)=\left(\dfrac{\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\kappa}-u\right)\nu(x,t)\\
X(\cdot,0)=X_{0},\end{cases}

where
\kappa
is the curvature,
\nu
is the unit outward normal, and
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
is the support function of the curve. The flow (
1.10
) for strictly convex closed curves in space forms has been studied in
[
23
]
, and it turns out to have a number of applications, including a unified proof of the classical isoperimetric inequality in
2
-dimensional space forms. For initially smooth strictly convex closed curve
\gamma_{0}
, the flow (
1.10
) preserves the convexity, exists for all time and converges to a geodesic circle as
t\to\infty
. A nice feature of this flow is that it preserves the length of the evolving curves. A key in proving Theorem
1.3
is to show that the left hand side of (
1.9
) is monotone decreasing along the flow (
1.10
).

For the higher dimensional case, we prove the following Minkowski-type inequalities for
h
-convex hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and for convex hypersurfaces in the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a smooth closed
h
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n}(n\geq 3)
enclosing a bounded domain
\Omega
that contains the origin. For any non-decreasing convex
C^{2}
positive function
g
, we have

(1.11)

\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)Hd\mu+(n-1)A_{g}(\Omega)\geq\chi\circ\xi^{-1}\big(|\Sigma|\big),

where
\xi(r)=\omega_{n-1}\phi^{n-1}(r)
, and
\chi(r)=\int_{S(r)}g(\Phi)H+(n-1)A_{g}(B(r))
. If either
g
is strictly increasing or
g
is strictly convex, then equality holds in (
1.11
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.

Remark 1.5
.

(1)

When
g(x)\equiv 1
, the left hand side of (
1.11
) reduces to the first quermassintegral
W_{1}(\Omega)=\int_{\Sigma}Hd\mu-(n-1)\text{vol}(\Omega)
. In this case, Theorem
1.4
reduces to the Minkowski inequality (
1.6
) in hyperbolic space proved by Bendle-Guan-Li
[
5
]
. See Hu-Li-Wei
[
19
]
for an alternative proof for
h
-convex domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

(2)

When
g(x)=x
, then
A_{g}(\Omega)=\text{vol}(\Omega)
and the left hand side of (
1.11
) reduces to
\int_{\Sigma}\Phi Hd\mu+(n-1)\text{vol}(\Omega)
. In this case, Theorem
1.4
reduces to the inequality (
1.7
) proved previously by the authors
[
24
]
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
\Sigma
is a smooth strictly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
enclosing a bounded domain
\Omega
that contains the origin. For any non-decreasing convex
C^{2}
positive function
g
, we have

(1.12)

\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)Hd\mu+(n-1)A_{g}(\Omega)\geq\chi\circ\zeta^{-1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\phi^{\prime}(r)dv\Big),

where
\zeta(r)=\int_{B(r)}\phi^{\prime}dv=\frac{1}{n}\omega_{n-1}\sin^{n}r
, and
\chi(r)=\int_{S(r)}g(\Phi)H+(n-1)A_{g}(B(r))
. If either
g
is strictly increasing or
g
is strictly convex, then equality holds in (
1.12
) if and only if
\Omega
is a geodesic ball centered at the origin.

The inequalities (
1.11
) and (
1.12
) will be proved using inverse mean curvature type flow. Due to the opposite sign of the ambient curvatures, we employ different flows in the hyperbolic space and in sphere separately. This is crucial in the proof of monotonicity of
\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)Hd\mu+(n-1)A_{g}(\Omega)
along the flows. To prove (
1.11
), we use the flow

(1.13)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{H}-\frac{u}{n-1}\right)\nu,

which was introduced by Brendle, Guan and Li
[
5
]
(see also
[
17
, §4]
). The second author and Hu, Li
[
19
]
proved that for
h
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the flow (
1.13
) preserves the
h
-convexity and converges to a geodesic sphere as
t\to\infty
. While for proving (
1.12
), we use the flow

(1.14)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\left(\frac{n-1}{H}-\frac{u}{\phi^{\prime}}\right)\nu

which was studied by Scheuer and Xia
[
29
]
and evolves any strictly convex hypersurface in sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
to a geodesic sphere as
t\to\infty
.

As an application of the weighted inequalities derived in our work, we establish a sharp upper bound for the first non-zero eigenvalue of a class of differential operators associated with
k
-convex hypersurfaces
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For simplicity, we only state the result for the two-dimensional case. Readers are referred to Theorem
5.1
for the general result in higher dimensions.

Theorem 1.7
.

For a smooth, closed, strictly convex curve
\gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the first non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the operator
-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Delta
on
\gamma
satisfies

\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{\pi L}{L^{2}-2\pi A},

where
\kappa,L
denote the curvature and length of
\gamma
respectively, and
A
represents the area enclosed by
\gamma
. Equality holds if and only if
\gamma
is a circle.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review some definitions and basic formulas for hypersurfaces in space forms, and then derive a general evolution equation for the weighted curvature integral
\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu
.

2.1.
Hypersurfaces in space forms

Given a smooth bounded domain
\Omega
in a space form
M^{n}(K)
, its boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
is a smooth embedded hypersurface. The support function of
\Sigma
is defined by
u=\langle V,\nu\rangle
, where
\nu
is the outward unit normal of
\Sigma
and
V=\bar{\nabla}\Phi
is the conformal Killing field on
M^{n}(K)
. Note that in Euclidean space,
V
is just the position vector. We denote by
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n-1})
the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
, which are defined as the eigenvalues of the Weingarten tensor
h_{i}^{j}=g^{jk}h_{ik}
. If we denote
\sigma_{k}
as the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial of an
n
-vector, then
H=\sigma_{1}(\kappa)=\kappa_{1}+\cdots+\kappa_{n-1}
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
, and
\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
is called the
k
th mean curvature of
\Sigma
. The definition also extends to the Weingarten tensor by defining
\sigma_{k}(h_{i}^{j})=\sigma_{k}(\lambda(h_{i}^{j}))
, where
\lambda
is a map sending a symmetric matrix to its eigenvalues. The Newton tensor of
h_{i}^{j}
is defined by
T_{k}^{ij}=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k+1}}{\partial h_{i}^{j}}
and satisfies the iteration (see
[
27
]
)

(2.1)

T_{k}^{ij}=\sigma_{k}g^{ij}-T_{k-1}^{ik}h_{k}^{j}.

It is divergence free due to the Codazzi equation in space forms.

Lemma 2.1

(see §2 in
[
20
]
)
.

For
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}T_{k-1}^{ij}h_{ij}=

\displaystyle k\sigma_{k}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}T_{k-1}^{ij}h_{ij}=

\displaystyle(n-k)\sigma_{k-1}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}T_{k-1}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1},

where
(h^{2})_{i}^{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}h_{i}^{k}h_{k}^{j}
and by convention we set
\sigma_{k}=0
for
k>n-1
.

The support function
u
and
\Phi
satisfy the following formulae (see
[
16
, §2]
)

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth closed hypersurface in the space form
M^{n}(K)
. Then

(2.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}u=h_{i}^{j}\nabla_{j}\Phi

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Delta\Phi=(n-1)\phi^{\prime}(r)-uH,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)=(n-1-k)\phi^{\prime}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)-(k+1)u\sigma_{k+1}(\kappa).

where
\nabla,\Delta
denote the Levi-Civita connection and Laplacian operator on
\Sigma
with respect to the induced metric.

A smooth closed hypersurface
\Sigma
is called
k
-convex if its principal curvatures are contained in the Garding cone
\Gamma_{k}^{+}
defined by

\Gamma_{+}^{+}=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},~|~\sigma_{j}(\kappa)>0,\quad\forall~j=1,\cdots,k\}.

Denote

(2.8)

H_{k}(\kappa)=\frac{\sigma_{k}(\kappa)}{\binom{n-1}{k}},\quad k=1,\cdots,n-1

the
k
th normalized mean curvature of
\Sigma
. We have the following Newton-MacLaurin inequality (see §2 in
[
18
]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

For any integers
1\leq\ell\leq k\leq n-1
and
\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}
, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle H_{k+1}H_{\ell-1}\leq H_{k}H_{\ell},

(2.10)

\displaystyle H_{k+1}\leq H_{k}^{\frac{k+1}{k}}.

Equality holds if and only if
\kappa=c(1,\cdots,1)
for some constant
c>0
.

2.2.
Evolution equation

Let
X_{0}:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow M^{n}(K)
be a smooth, closed star-shaped hypersurface. If
K=1
, we also assume
X_{0}
lies in the open hemisphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
. We consider the flow
X:\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times[0,T)\rightarrow M^{n}(K)
satisfying

(2.11)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=F\nu,

where the speed function
F
depends on the curvature and the position of
\Sigma_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},t)
. The following lemma gives a variational equation of the weighted curvature integral along the flow (
2.11
). See Reilly
[
27
, Theorem A]
for a general variational formula in the Euclidean space for curvature functions involving potential
\Phi
and support function.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
n\geq 2
, along the flow (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\Big(g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\sigma_{k}-g^{\prime}(\Phi)\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)-g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi

(2.12)

\displaystyle\qquad+(k+1)g(\Phi)\sigma_{k+1}-(n-k)Kg(\Phi)\sigma_{k-1}\Big)Fd\mu_{t}

for
k=1,\cdots,n-1
. Furthermore, for the mean curvature case
k=1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)Hd\mu_{t}+(n-1)A_{g}(\Omega_{t})\right)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\biggl(2g^{\prime}(\Phi)uH+2g(\Phi)\sigma_{2}(\kappa)-g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\biggr)Fd\mu_{t}.

By convention,
\sigma_{k}=0
for
k>n-1
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\Phi
, we first have that along (
2.11
)

\partial_{t}\Phi=\langle\phi(r)\partial_{r},\partial_{t}X\rangle=Fu.

The evolution of Weingarten tensor
h_{i}^{j}
and area form
d\mu_{t}
is well known (see
[
2
, Theorem 3-15]
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{i}^{j}=

\displaystyle-\nabla^{j}\nabla_{i}F-F\left((h^{2})_{i}^{j}+K\delta_{i}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}d\mu_{t}=

\displaystyle F\sigma_{1}d\mu_{t}.

Then using Lemma
2.1
and the divergence-free property of
T_{k-1}^{ij}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}g(\Phi)\right)\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial t}d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}\sigma_{1}Fd\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)uF\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)T_{k-1}^{ij}\left(-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}F-F\left((h^{2})_{ij}+Kg_{ij}\right)\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)F\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\Big(g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\sigma_{k}-\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}g(\Phi)\right)+g(\Phi)\left(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-T_{k-1}^{ij}((h^{2})_{ij}+Kg_{ij})\right)\Big)Fd\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\Big(g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\sigma_{k}-g^{\prime}(\Phi)\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)-g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi

(2.14)

\displaystyle\qquad+(k+1)g(\Phi)\sigma_{k+1}-(n-k)Kg(\Phi)\sigma_{k-1}\Big)Fd\mu_{t}.

This proves the equation (
2.12
).

For
k=1
, using (
2.7
), we can further rewrite the above equation and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)Hd\mu_{t}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\biggl(2g^{\prime}(\Phi)uH+2g(\Phi)\sigma_{2}-g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\quad-(n-1)\Big(\phi^{\prime}g^{\prime}(\Phi)+g(\Phi)K\Big)\biggr)Fd\mu_{t}.

By the definition (
1.8
) of
A_{g}
, the co-area formula implies that

(2.16)

\frac{d}{dt}A_{g}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(g^{\prime}(\Phi)\phi^{\prime}+Kg(\Phi)\right)Fd\mu_{t}.

Combining (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) gives (
2.13
).
∎

3.
Weighted Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In this section, we first consider the Euclidean space case. We prove Theorem
1.1
on the weighted Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequality with convex weight for hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For convenience of notations, in this section we use the normalized
k
th mean curvature
H_{k}=\binom{n-1}{k}^{-1}\sigma_{k}
. We will use the normalized inverse mean curvature flow

(3.1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)\nu

to prove (
1.4
). This flow is equivalent to the inverse curvature flow

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X=\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}\nu

introduced by Gerhardt
[
12
]
and Urbas
[
31
]
, up to a rescaling. If the initial hypersurface is star-shaped and
k
-convex, the flow (
3.1
) converges to a round sphere as
t\to\infty
. Note that when
n=2
, a closed convex curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is star-shaped with respect to some point centered inside the enclosed domain and is
k
-convex for
k=n-1=1
.

In the Euclidean space,
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1
, then (
2.7
) is equivalent to

(3.2)

\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)=k\binom{n-1}{k}\left(H_{k-1}-uH_{k}\right).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By the general evolution equation (
2.12
) and noting that the ambient curvature
K=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, along the flow (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)H_{k}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Bigg(g^{\prime}(\Phi)uH_{k}-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi+(n-1-k)g(\Phi)H_{k+1}\Bigg)\times\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\Bigg(kg^{\prime}(\Phi)u\left(H_{k-1}-uH_{k}\right)d\mu_{t}-(k-1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)H_{k-1}\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\frac{g^{\prime}(\Phi)}{H_{k}}\left(H_{k-1}-uH_{k}\right)^{2}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)H_{k+1}\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I+II+III+IV+V,

where in the second equality we used (
3.2
). Notice that we have
\sigma_{k}=0
for
k>n-1
. This includes the case
n=2
. We estimate the five terms
I,\cdots,V
separately. Since
g^{\prime}\geq 0
and
H_{k}>0
on
\Sigma_{t}
, the third term
III
is clearly non-positive. Using (
3.2
) and (
2.5
), we can estimate the first term
I
as follows:

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}u\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\left(g^{\prime\prime}u\nabla_{j}\Phi+g^{\prime}h_{j}^{k}\nabla_{k}\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}ug^{\prime\prime}T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}T_{k-1}^{ij}h_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{i}\Phi.

Since the Newton operator satisfies

T_{k}^{ij}=\sigma_{k}g^{ij}-T_{k-1}^{ik}h_{k}^{j},

we can rewrite the second term and obtain

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}ug^{\prime\prime}T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\sigma_{k}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)T_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi d\mu_{t}.

As
T_{k}
is divergence free, by integration by parts and using (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)T_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}T_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{i}g(\Phi)\nabla_{j}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\left(H_{k}-uH_{k+1}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}ug^{\prime\prime}T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\sigma_{k}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}d\mu_{t}

(3.4)

\displaystyle-(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\left(H_{k}-uH_{k+1}\right).

When
k=1
, the second term
II
vanishes. When
k>1
, by the Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.9
) and noting that
g^{\prime}\geq 0
, the second term satisfies

(3.5)

\displaystyle II\leq

\displaystyle-(k-1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\left(H_{k-2}-uH_{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\nabla_{i}\left(T_{k-2}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-2}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi.

Since
g\geq 0
, the Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.9
) also implies that

(3.7)

\displaystyle V\leq

\displaystyle(n-1-k)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\left(H_{k}-uH_{k+1}\right)d\mu_{t}.

Combining the above equations, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)H_{k}d\mu_{t}\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\sigma_{k}|\nabla\Phi|^{2}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-2}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)T_{k-1}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)\biggl(\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k}}T_{k-1}^{ij}\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}T_{k-2}^{ij}\biggr)\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)\frac{H_{k-1}^{2}}{H_{k}}g^{js}\frac{\partial}{\partial h_{i}^{s}}\left(\frac{H_{k}}{H_{k-1}}\right)\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,

which follows from the fact that
H_{k}>0
,
g^{\prime}\geq 0
,
g^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
and that
H_{k}/H_{k-1}
is strictly increasing with respect to the Weingarten tensor
h_{i}^{j}
. This means that
\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}

is strictly decreasing along the flow (
3.1
) unless
\Sigma_{t}
is totally umbilic.

On the other hand, by the evolution equation of the quermassintegral
W_{\ell}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\sigma_{\ell}d\mu_{t}
(a special case of the equation (
2.12
)), we have for
\ell=0,1,\cdots,k-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{\ell}(\Omega_{t})=

\displaystyle(\ell+1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\sigma_{\ell+1}\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\ell+1)\binom{n-1}{\ell+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{H_{\ell+1}H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-uH_{\ell+1}\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\ell+1)\binom{n-1}{\ell+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(H_{\ell}-uH_{\ell+1}\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\nabla_{i}\left(T_{\ell}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\Phi\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

along the flow (
3.1
), where we used (
3.2
) and the Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.9
). For
\ell=-1
, by the coarea formula and the Heintze-Karcher inequality
[
28
]
, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}W_{-1}(\Omega_{t})=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{n-1}{H}-u\right)d\mu_{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

That is,
W_{\ell}(\Omega_{t})
,
\ell=-1,0,\cdots,k-1
are all non-decreasing along the flow (
3.1
).

Since the flow converges to a round sphere
S^{n-1}(r)
as
t\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle W_{\ell}(\Omega)\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}W_{\ell}(\Omega_{t})=\binom{n-1}{\ell}\omega_{n-1}r^{n-1-\ell}=:\xi(r)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu\geq

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu_{t}=\binom{n-1}{k}\omega_{n-1}g(\frac{r^{2}}{2})r^{n-k-1}=:\chi(r).

Both
\xi(r)
and
\chi(r)
are strictly increasing in
r
and so the inverse
\xi^{-1}
of
\xi(r)
is well defined. Combining the above two inequalities, we have

\int_{\Sigma}g(\Phi)\sigma_{k}d\mu\geq\chi\circ\xi^{-1}\Big(W_{\ell}(\Omega)\Big).

This proves the inequality (
1.4
).

If the equality holds, from the proof of the monotonicity,
\Sigma_{t}
is totally umbilic for all
t
, implying that
\Sigma
is a round sphere. Furthermore, when either
g
is strictly increasing or
g
is strictly convex, we see that
|\nabla\Phi|
vanishes identically and so
\Sigma
is a round sphere centered at the origin.
∎

4.
Weighted Minkowski type inequalities in space forms

In this section, we will focus on the Minkowski type inequalities with general convex weight in space forms.

4.1.
2
-dimensional case

We first treat the case
n=2
, the boundary of a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset M^{2}(K)
is a smooth closed curve
\gamma=\partial\Omega
. Assuming
0\in\Omega
, we denote by
\gamma_{t}
the evolution of
\gamma
under the flow (
1.10
), with
\Omega_{t}
representing the region bounded by
\gamma_{t}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is
C^{2}
such that
g^{\prime}\geq 0
and
g^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
, then
the quantity

\int_{\gamma_{t}}g(\Phi)\kappa ds+A_{g}(\Omega_{t})

is monotone decreasing along the flow (
1.10
).

Proof.

By results of
[
23
]
, the flow (
1.10
) preserves convexity and
0\in\Omega_{t}
.
A special case of (
2.13
) gives

(4.1)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\gamma_{t}}g(\Phi)\kappa ds+A_{g}(\Omega_{t})\right)=\int_{\gamma_{t}}\biggl(2g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\kappa-g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\biggr)Fds,

where
s
denotes the arc-length of the curves
\gamma_{t}
. Letting
F=\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\kappa}-u
in (
4.1
) and using

\Delta\left(g(\Phi)\right)=g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}+g^{\prime}(\Phi)\Delta\Phi,

we have that along the flow (
1.10
),

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int_{\gamma_{t}}g(\Phi)\kappa\,ds+A_{g}(\Omega_{t})\right]=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}2g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\left(\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u\right)\,ds-\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\frac{\phi^{\prime}}{\kappa}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}u\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}2g^{\prime}(\Phi)u(\phi^{\prime}-\kappa u)\,ds+\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}u\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}2g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\Delta\Phi\,ds+\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)|\nabla\Phi|^{2}u\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\Delta\Phi\,ds+\int_{\gamma_{t}}\Delta\left(g(\Phi)\right)u\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)u\Delta\Phi\,ds-\int_{\gamma_{t}}\langle\nabla\left(g(\Phi)\right),\nabla u\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)u|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,ds-2\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\langle\nabla u,\nabla\Phi\rangle\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime\prime}(\Phi)u|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,ds-2\int_{\gamma_{t}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\kappa|\nabla\Phi|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0,

where we have used
\nabla u=\kappa\nabla\Phi
in the last equality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Lemma
4.1
, we only have to compute the limit of
\int_{\gamma_{t}}g(\Phi)\kappa ds+A_{g}(\Omega_{t})
. Denote the length of the initial curve by
L
.
By results of
[
23
]
, the flow (
1.10
) is length preserving and
\gamma_{t}
converges to a circle
\gamma_{\infty}=\partial\Omega_{\infty}
centered at the origin of radius
\rho_{\infty}=\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)
, with
\kappa=\frac{\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-KL^{2}}}{L}
, and

\displaystyle A_{g}(\Omega_{\infty})=2\pi\int_{0}^{\rho_{\infty}}\left[g^{\prime}(\Phi(r))\phi(r)\phi^{\prime}(r)dr+Kg(\Phi(r))\phi(r)\right]dr.

We compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{\infty}}g^{\prime}(\Phi)\phi\phi^{\prime}dr=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{\infty}}(g(\Phi(r)))^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\rho_{\infty}}g({\Phi})\phi^{\prime\prime}dr+g(\Phi(\rho_{\infty}))\phi^{\prime}(\rho_{\infty})-g(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\rho_{\infty}}Kg(\Phi)\phi dr+g\left(\Phi\left(\rho_{\infty}\right)\right)\phi^{\prime}\left(\rho_{\infty}\right)-g(0).

Let
G(r):=\int_{0}^{r}g(t)dt
.
By a direct calculation,
\phi^{\prime}\left(\rho_{\infty}\right)=\phi^{\prime}\left(\phi^{-1}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)\right)=\sqrt{1-\frac{KL^{2}}{4\pi^{2}}}
.
So

\displaystyle A_{g}\left[\Omega_{\infty}\right]=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{\rho_{\infty}}Kg(\Phi)\phi dr+g\left(\Phi\left(\rho_{\infty}\right)\right)\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-KL^{2}}-2\pi g(0)

Define

.
Therefore, we get the inequality

\begin{split}\int_{\gamma}g(\Phi)\kappa ds+A_{g}(\Omega)\geq&\int_{\gamma_{\infty}}g(\Phi)\kappa ds+A_{g}(\Omega_{\infty})\\
=&2g\left(\frac{\mathrm{A}(L)}{2\pi}\right)\sqrt{4\pi^{2}-KL^{2}}-2\pi g(0)+4\pi KG\left(\frac{\mathrm{A}(L)}{2\pi}\right).\end{split}

If

∎

5.
Applications of weighted inequalities to eigenvalue estimates

In this section, we demonstrate a series of eigenvalue estimates derived by applying Theorem
1.3
and its generalization as established by the authors in
[
24
]
. Specifically, we provide a sharp upper bound for the first positive eigenvalue of an operator involving the curvature
H_{k}
.

Let us now describe the problem. Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed,
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider the eigenvalue problem associated with the positive second-order differential operator
-\frac{1}{H_{k}}\Delta
on
\Sigma
:

-\Delta f=\lambda H_{k}f,

where
H_{k}
is the normalized
k
th mean curvature defined in (
2.8
). When
k=0
, this is just the eigenvalue problem for the classical Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Sigma
. By classical spectral theory, the eigenvalues satisfy
0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots\to\infty
, where multiplicities are ignored. Our objective is to establish a sharp upper bound for
\lambda_{1}
using weighted inequalities developed in our work. This problem can be recast in a variational framework:
\lambda_{1}
is characterized as the minimum of the Dirichlet energy
\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu
, subject to the constraint
\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}fd\mu=0
and the normalization
\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}f^{2}d\mu=1
. Explicitly, we have

(5.1)

\lambda_{1}=\min\left\{\frac{\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla f|^{2}d\mu}{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}f^{2}d\mu}:\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}fd\mu=0\right\}.

To tackle this eigenvalue problem, we turn our attention to Theorem
1.3
. This theorem vastly generalizes the two-dimensional result previously established with G. Wheeler and V.-M.Wheeler
[
23
, Theorem 1.2]
. In the
\mathbb{R}^{2}
setting, the result states that for a smooth convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\int_{\partial\Omega}\kappa\frac{r^{2}}{2}ds\geq\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(|\partial\Omega|^{2}-2\pi|\Omega|\right),

which follows as a special case of Theorem
1.3
by choosing
g(x)=x
.
This inequality extends to higher dimensions by the authors
[
24
, Theorem 1.3]
in the following form:

(5.2)

\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}\frac{r^{2}}{2}d\mu+\frac{k}{n-k+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu\geq\frac{n+1+k}{2(n+1-k)}\omega_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n+1-k}{n-k}},

where
\Sigma
is a
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a coordinate sphere.

In our earlier work
[
24
]
, a weighted inequality different from (
5.2
) was employed to derive a sharp estimate for the first non-zero Steklov eigenvalue for star-shaped mean-convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, generalizing the classical Weinstock inequality. Here, we demonstrate how inequality (
5.2
) can be utilized to obtain a sharp upper bound for the first non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the operator
-\frac{\Delta}{H_{k}}
on
k
-convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth, closed, star-shaped, and
k
-convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n\geq 2
and
1\leq

k\leq n-1
. Then the first non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the operator
-\frac{1}{H_{k}}\Delta
on
\Sigma
satisfies

\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-1)|\Sigma|\left[\frac{n+1+k}{2(n+1-k)}\omega_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n+1-k}{n-k}}-\frac{k}{n-k+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu\right]^{-1}.

Equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a sphere.

Proof.

Since
H_{k}>0
, we can assume, after a suitable translation, that the weighted center of mass satisfies
\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}xd\mu=0
, where
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
is the position vector. Consequently, by (
5.1
), for each
i=1,\dots,n
,

\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla x_{i}|^{2}d\mu\geq\lambda_{1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}x_{i}^{2}d\mu.

Summing over
i=1,\dots,n
and applying inequality (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle(n-1)|\Sigma|

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|\nabla x_{i}\right|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}r^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq 2\lambda_{1}\left[\frac{n+1+k}{2(n+1-k)}\omega_{n-1}^{-\frac{1}{n-k}}\left(\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}d\mu\right)^{\frac{n+1-k}{n-k}}-\frac{k}{n-k+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-2}d\mu\right].

Rearranging yields the desired result. The case of equality follows directly from the equality condition of (
5.2
).
∎

In the two-dimensional case with
k=1
, this result assumes a particularly elegant geometric form. Specifically, for a smooth, closed, convex curve
\gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the first non-zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the operator
-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Delta
satisfies

\lambda_{1}\leq\frac{\pi L}{L^{2}-2\pi A},

where
L
denotes the length of
\gamma
and
A
represents the area enclosed by
\gamma
. Equality holds if and only if
\gamma
is a circle.

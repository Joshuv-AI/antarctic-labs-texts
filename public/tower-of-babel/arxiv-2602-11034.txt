Induced dynamics and quasifactors for minimal equicontinuous actions on Stone spaces Thanks: This article was funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – 530703788.
María Isabel Cortez, Till Hauser

Abstract
. A minimal equicontinuous action of a group G G on a Stone space X X is called a subodometer . If such a subodometer arises from a group rotation, we refer to it as an odometer . For subodometers ( X , G ) (X,G) we show that the hyperspace ℋ ⁡ ( X ) {\mathcal{H}}(X) - given by all closed subsets of X X and the Vietoris topology - decomposes into subodometers. We show that an infinite subodometer is an odometer if and only if ℋ ⁡ ( X ) {\mathcal{H}}(X) decomposes into factors of ( X , G ) (X,G) . Similarly, we consider ℳ ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{M}(X) , the space of regular Borel probability measures equipped with the weak-* topology. We show that for a subodometer ( X , G ) (X,G) also the connected space ℳ ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{M}(X) decomposes into subodometers. We prove that an infinite subodometer ( X , G ) (X,G) is an odometer if and only if ℳ ⁡ ( X ) \mathcal{M}(X) decomposes into factors of ( X , G ) (X,G) . For this, we study different notions of regular recurrence. Furthermore, we study the disjointness of minimal actions to subodometers and show that this disjointness can be detected from the pairwise disjointness of finite factors. Using this we prove that a minimal action is disjoint from all subodometers if and only if it has a connected maximal equicontinuous factor. Keywords. Action, Subodometer, Odometer, Quasifactor, Disjointness, Hyperspace, Induced dynamics, Borel probability measure, Rotation, Stone space, Eigenvalue. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 37B05; Secondary 37B20, 54H15, 20E18.

3.3.
Uniform regular recurrence

An action
(X,G)
is called
uniformly regularly recurrent
if for any

there exists a finite index subgroup
\Gamma\leq G
such that for all

.

Example 3.5
.

A rational rotation on the circle is uniformly regularly recurrent.

Clearly, any uniformly regularly recurrent action is pointwise regularly recurrent, and uniformly almost periodic, i.e. equicontinuous. We next show that also the converse holds.

Proposition 3.6

An action

A minimal action is a subodometer if and only if it is uniformly regularly recurrent.

6.
Disjointness of subodometers

Two minimal actions
(X,G)
and
(Y,G)
are called
disjoint
if
(X\times Y,G)
is minimal. We write
X\perp Y
to indicate that
X
and
Y
are disjoint.
Note that two disjoint actions only have the trivial action as a common factor.
The converse is true if at least one of the actions is regular and distal
[
1
, Theorem 11.21]
; in particular, this holds if one of the actions is an odometer.
However, it is well known that in general the converse fails.
See Example
6.5
below for an illustration involving subodometers.
For further details on disjointness see
[
1
, Chapter 11]
.
We next characterize disjointness to subodometers and the absence of nontrivial common factors with subodometers.
For this we study first the case of finite subodometers.

6.1.
Finite subodometers

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma,\Lambda\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)
.

(i)

(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)
are disjoint if and only if
\Gamma\Lambda=G
.

(ii)

(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)
have no nontrivial common factor if and only if
\left\langle\Gamma^{g}\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=G
holds for all
g\in G
.

Remark 6.2
.

Note that (i) is a special case of
[
1
, Theorem 11.12]
. We include the short proof for the convenience of the reader.

Proof.

(i):
Since
G/\Gamma\times G/\Lambda
is finite,
(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)
are disjoint if and only if the orbit of
(\Gamma,\Lambda)
is equal to
G/\Gamma\times G/\Lambda
. Thus, for every
g\in G
, there exists
h\in G
such that

(h\Gamma,h\Lambda)=h.(\Gamma,\Lambda)=(\Gamma,g\Lambda)
.
From
h\Gamma=\Gamma
we observe
h\in\Gamma
and
g\Lambda=h\Lambda\subseteq\Gamma\Lambda
yields

g\in\Gamma\Lambda
.

For the converse assume
G=\Gamma\Lambda
and consider
(g\Gamma,g^{\prime}\Lambda)\in G/\Gamma\times G/\Lambda
.
There exist
h\in\Gamma
and
h^{\prime}\in\Lambda
with
g^{-1}g^{\prime}=hh^{\prime}
, i.e.
g^{\prime}=ghh^{\prime}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle gh.(\Gamma,\Lambda)=(gh\Gamma,gh\Lambda)=(g\Gamma,ghh^{\prime}\Lambda)=(g\Gamma,g^{\prime}\Lambda).

This shows that
G/\Gamma\times G/\Lambda
is minimal.

(ii):
Assume that
(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)
have no nontrivial common factor and consider
g\in G
.
Since
(G/\Gamma^{g})
is conjugated to
(G/\Gamma,G)
we observe that
(G/\left\langle\Gamma^{g}\cup\Lambda\right\rangle,G)
is a common factor of the considered actions and hence trivial.
This shows
\left\langle\Gamma^{g}\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=G
.

For the converse,
assume that
\left\langle\Gamma^{g}\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=G
holds for all
g\in G

and consider a common factor
(Z,G)
.
Choose factor maps
\pi_{\Gamma}\colon G/\Gamma\to Z
and
\pi_{\Lambda}\colon G/\Lambda\to Z
.
Since
(Z,G)
is finite and minimal there exists
g\in G
with

\pi_{\Lambda}(\Lambda)=g.\pi_{\Gamma}(\Gamma)=\pi_{\Gamma}(g\Gamma)
.
We have

\displaystyle G_{0}(\pi_{\Lambda}(\Lambda))=G_{0}(\pi_{\Gamma}(g\Gamma))\supseteq G_{0}(g\Gamma)=\Gamma^{g}

and furthermore
G_{0}(\pi_{\Lambda}(\Lambda))\supseteq G_{0}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
.
Since
Z
is finite
G_{0}(\pi_{\Lambda}(\Lambda))
is a group and we observe

G_{0}(\pi_{\Lambda}(\Lambda))\supseteq\left\langle\Gamma^{g}\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=G
.
This shows that
\pi(\Lambda)
is a fixed point of
(Z,G)
.
Since
(Z,G)
is minimal we observe it to be trivial.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If one of the groups
\Gamma
or
\Lambda
is normal, then
(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)
are disjoint if and only if
G=\left\langle\Gamma\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=\Gamma\Lambda
,
which is in turn equivalent to the fact that
(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)

have no nontrivial common factors.
This also follows from
[
1
, Theorem 11.21]
,
since odometers are regular equicontinuous systems.

The following example illustrates that the testing for all
g\in G
in Proposition
6.1
(ii) is necessary.

Example 6.4
.

Consider the symmetric group
S_{3}
,
\Gamma:=\left\langle(12)\right\rangle
and
\Lambda:=\left\langle(13)\right\rangle
.
For
g:=(23)
we observe that
\Gamma^{g}=\Lambda
and hence that
(G/\Gamma,G)
and
(G/\Lambda,G)
are conjugated.
Nevertheless, from
(13)(12)=(123)
we observe

\left\langle\Gamma\cup\Lambda\right\rangle=\left\langle(12),(13)\right\rangle=\left\langle(12),(123)\right\rangle=S_{3}.

The following example illustrates that in general the absence of a common factor can occur for non-disjoint finite subodometers.

Example 6.5
.

Denote
r:=(1234)
and
s:=(12)(34)
and consider
D_{4}:=\left\langle r,s\right\rangle
.
Note that
D_{4}
is the symmetry group of the square and
|D_{4}|=8
.
Let
\Gamma:=\left\langle s\right\rangle
and

\Lambda:=\left\langle rs\right\rangle=\left\langle(13)\right\rangle
.
From
|\Gamma|=|\Lambda|=2
we observe that
\Gamma\Lambda\subsetneq D_{4}
.
Thus,
(D_{4}/\Gamma,D_{4})
and
(D_{4}/\Lambda,D_{4})
are not disjoint.

Note that we have

s^{2}=()
,

r^{4}=()
,
and
rs=sr^{-1}
.
Thus, any
g\in D_{4}
can be represented as
g=s^{l}r^{k}
with

and

.
From

\left\langle\Lambda^{g}\cup\Gamma\right\rangle\supseteq\Gamma\Lambda^{g}\Gamma\ni(s^{-l})(g(rs)g^{-1})(s^{l+1})=r^{k}rsr^{-k}s=r^{2k+1}.

and observe

Thus,

and it follows that

and

6.3.
Common factors with subodometers

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
(X,G)
be a minimal action and
(Y,G)
be a subodometer.
The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

X
and

have no nontrivial common factor.

(ii)

X
and
Y
have no nontrivial common finite factor.

(iii)

\left\langle\Lambda\cup\Gamma\right\rangle=G

\Lambda\in{\rm Eig}(X,G)

\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}(Y,G)

Remark 6.11
.

Since finite actions can be represented in the form

Proof.

’(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)’:
Immediate.

’(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)’:
For

and
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}(Y,G)

(G/\Lambda,G)
and

and
(Y,G)

’(iii)
\Rightarrow

of

and

.

6.4.
Common factors and disjointness via scales

Corollary 6.12
.

If
(X,G)
is a minimal action and
(Y,G)
a subodometer generated by a scale
S
.

(i)

X\perp Y
if and only if
\Lambda\Gamma=G
holds for all
\Lambda\in{\rm Eig}(X,G)
and
\Gamma\in S
.

(ii)

X
and
Y
have no nontrivial common factor if and only if

\left\langle\Lambda\cup\Gamma\right\rangle=G
holds for all
\Lambda\in{\rm Eig}(X,G)
and
\Gamma\in S
.

If also
(X,G)
is a subodometer generated by a scale

the following statements hold.

(iii)

X\perp Y
if and only if
\Lambda\Gamma=G
holds for all
\Lambda\in S^{\prime}
and
\Gamma\in S
.

(iv)

X
and
Y
have no nontrivial common factor if and only if

\left\langle\Lambda^{g}\cup\Gamma\right\rangle=G
holds for all
\Lambda\in S^{\prime}
,
\Gamma\in S
and
g\in G
.

Remark 6.13
.

Recall from Example
6.4
that the testing for all

in (iv) is necessary.

Proof.

(i):
If

we observe the second condition from

and Theorem

.
For the converse we show Theorem
6.7
(v) and consider

and
\Gamma\in{\rm Eig}(Y,G)

Since

is a scale that generates
(Y,G)
there exist

and
\Gamma^{\prime}\in S

\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma^{g}
.

yields

and

(ii):

6.5.

In

We next classify the minimal actions that are disjoint from all subodometers.

Theorem 6.15
.

For a minimal action the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

X\perp Y
for all subodometers
(Y,G)
.

(ii)

X\perp Y
for all odometers
(Y,G)

(iii)

(iv)

X\perp Y

(v)

(X,G)

(vi)

Remark 6.16
.

The subodometer
(S_{3}/\left\langle(12)\right\rangle,S_{3})
has no nontrivial odometer factor. Thus, considering only odometers in (v) does not yield another characterization of the class of minimal actions disjoint to all subodometers.

Proof of Theorem
6.15
:

Clearly, we have
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) and
(i)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv).

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i):
Denote
S
for the set of all normal
\Gamma\in{\operatorname{Sub_{fin}}}(G)

.
Since

.
From (iv) we observe that

\Gamma\in S

(i)
\Rightarrow

(v)

There exists a nontrivial clopen subset

Since

Example 6.17
.

Whenever
(X,G)
is a minimal action on a connected compact Hausdorff space, then the maximal equicontinuous factor is connected.
Prominent examples are given by irrational rotations on the circle,
the distal skew product shift
[

, Chapter 7]
, and
the actions provided in the field of surface dynamics.

The following example demonstrates that minimal actions on Stone spaces can be disjoint from all subodometers.

Example 6.18
.

The Sturmian subshift is a minimal action of
\mathbb{Z}
on the Cantor set. It is well known that its maximal equicontinuous factor is a circle rotation and hence connected.

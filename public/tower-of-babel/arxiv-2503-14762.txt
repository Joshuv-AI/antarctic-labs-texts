Microscopic model of exciton fine structure

The orbital wavefunctions can be be decomposed into their individual contributions, determined by the spin-orbit coupling.
The latter leads to a mixing of the orbital composition of the lowest conduction band
Becker et al. 2018
with momentum
k
, spin
s
and orbital
P_{i}

\displaystyle\ket{s,c,k}=a_{z}\sigma_{s}\ket{P_{z},s,k}+a_{x}\ket{P_{x},\overline{s},k}+ia_{y}\sigma_{s}\ket{P_{y},\overline{s},k},

(1)

where
\sigma=\pm 1
denotes the up/down spin, respectively. Here
a_{i}
determines the contribution of the orbital
P_{i}
to the valence band. Equal weighting of the
P_{x}
,
P_{y}
and
P_{z}
orbitals corresponds to a perovskite with cubic symmetry (
a_{x}=a_{y}=a_{z}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
). In the orthorhombic phase the weightings differ
Steger et al. 2022
, however the contributing orbitals remain the same. In particular,
a_{x}=a_{y}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\cos\theta
and
a_{z}=\sin\theta
where
\theta
is a material specific parameter.
There is no mixing of the valence band
S
orbitals in any phase.
The exchange Hamiltonian can be written as a 4x4 matrix describing each spin state
Thompson et al. 2024
. As such we need to solve the eigenproblem

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{z}^{2}I_{z}&0&0&-a_{z}^{2}I_{z}\\
0&a_{x}^{2}I_{x}+a_{y}^{2}I_{y}&a_{x}^{2}I_{x}-a_{y}^{2}I_{y}&0\\
0&a_{x}^{2}I_{x}-a_{y}^{2}I_{y}&a_{x}^{2}I_{x}+a_{y}^{2}I_{y}&0\\
-a_{z}^{2}I_{z}&0&0&a_{z}^{2}I_{z}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D^{\uparrow,\uparrow,n}\\
D^{\uparrow,\downarrow,n}\\
D^{\downarrow,\uparrow,n}\\
D^{\downarrow,\downarrow,n}\end{pmatrix}=E^{n}\begin{pmatrix}D^{\uparrow,\uparrow,n}\\
D^{\uparrow,\downarrow,n}\\
D^{\downarrow,\uparrow,n}\\
D^{\downarrow,\downarrow,n},\end{pmatrix}

(2)

where
I_{x,y,z}
are the exchange integrals for the
P_{x}
,
P_{y}
and
P_{z}
orbitals respectively. Here
D^{\uparrow,\uparrow,n}
describes the spin contribution to each exciton band
n
with energy
E^{n}
.
For the tetragonal system
a_{x}=a_{y}
and
I_{x}=I_{y}
and leading to the energy landscape

\displaystyle E^{0}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{(dark)}

\displaystyle E^{1}

\displaystyle=2a_{x}^{2}I_{x}\quad\text{(LCP)}

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle=2a_{x}^{2}I_{x}\quad\text{(RCP)}

\displaystyle E^{3}

\displaystyle=2a_{z}^{2}I_{z}\quad\text{(Z)}

In contrast, if
a_{x}\neq a_{y}
or
I_{x}\neq I_{y}
we have

\displaystyle E^{0}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{(dark)}

\displaystyle E^{1}

\displaystyle=2a_{x}^{2}I_{x}\quad\text{(X)}

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle=2a_{y}^{2}I_{y}\quad\text{(Y)}

\displaystyle E^{3}

\displaystyle=2a_{z}^{2}I_{z}\quad\text{(Z)}

The bright-dark splitting as defined here, is the splitting between the bright
X
-polarised exciton (
n=1
) and the dark (
n=0
) exciton, as it is these that mix when a magnetic field is applied. We also know by the normalisation of the wavefucntion that
a_{x}^{2}+a_{y}^{2}+a_{z}^{2}=1
. Assuming that
I_{x}=I_{y}=I_{z}=I
and that relative orbital contributions to the band dictate the ordering of states, combined with the normalisation condition allows us to extract
I,a_{x,y,z}
from the equation from the exciton resonances. In particular we solve the system of equations

\displaystyle Ia_{x}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{BD}

\displaystyle I(a_{y}^{2}-a_{z}^{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{XY}

\displaystyle I(a_{z}^{2}-a_{y}^{2})

\displaystyle=\Delta_{BZ}

\displaystyle a_{x}^{2}+a_{y}^{2}+a_{z}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

in order to uniquely determine
I,a_{x,y,z}
as discussed in the main text.

The impact of the magnetic field can be included by considering a new 4x4 Hamiltonian whose basis is the exciton states in the absence of a magnetic field
Thompson et al. 2024

\displaystyle H_{X}(B)=\begin{pmatrix}E_{\text{Dark}}&0&\dfrac{1}{2}g_{L}\mu_{B}B&0\\
0&E_{\text{Z}}&0&\dfrac{1}{2}g_{T}\mu_{B}B\\
\dfrac{1}{2}g_{L}\mu_{B}B&0&E_{\text{X}}&0\\
0&\dfrac{1}{2}g_{T}\mu_{B}B&0&E_{\text{Y}}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(3)

where
g_{L}=g_{c}-g_{v}
and
g_{T}=g_{c}+g_{v}
depend on the g-factors
g_{c(v)}
of the exciton-weighted electron (hole) bands
Thompson et al. 2024
.
Solving the matrix above, we obtain the eigenvalues

\displaystyle E_{0L}(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(E_{\text{Dark}}+E_{\text{X}}+\sqrt{(E_{\text{Dark}}-E_{\text{X}})^{2}+(g_{L}\mu_{B}B)^{2}}\right)

(4)

\displaystyle E_{1L}(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(E_{\text{Dark}}+E_{\text{X}}-\sqrt{(E_{\text{Dark}}-E_{\text{X}})^{2}+(g_{L}\mu_{B}B)^{2}}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle E_{0T}(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(E_{\text{Y}}+E_{\text{Z}}-\sqrt{(E_{\text{Y}}-E_{\text{Z}})^{2}+(g_{T}\mu_{B}B)^{2}}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle E_{1T}(B)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(E_{\text{Y}}+E_{\text{Z}}+\sqrt{(E_{\text{Y}}-E_{\text{Z}})^{2}+(g_{T}\mu_{B}B)^{2}}\right)

(7)

The corresponding eigenstates are

\displaystyle\psi_{0L}(B)=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{E_{0L}(B)-E_{X}}{g_{L}\mu_{B}B/2}\\
0\\
1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

(9)

\displaystyle\psi_{1L}(B)=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{E_{1L}(B)-E_{X}}{g_{L}\mu_{B}B/2}\\
0\\
1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

(10)

\displaystyle\psi_{0T}(B)=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\dfrac{E_{0T}(B)-E_{Y}}{g_{T}\mu_{B}B/2}\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

(11)

\displaystyle\psi_{1T}(B)=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\mathcal{N}}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\dfrac{E_{1T}(B)-E_{Y}}{g_{T}\mu_{B}B/2}\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}

(12)

Macroscopic properties of the XTE J1814-338 as a dark matter admixed strange star
Luiz L. Lopes

Abstract
In this letter, I discuss the macroscopic properties of the ultracompact object XTE J1814-338, whose inferred mass and radius read M M = 1.21 ± \pm 0.05 M ⊙ M_{\odot} and R = 7.0 ± \pm 0.4 km. By using the neutralino as WIMP dark matter with a fixed Fermi momentum, I calculated the maximum possible mass of this object, the moment of inertia, the gravitational redshift, the dimensionless tidal parameter, and the total amount of dark matter for a 1.2 M ⊙ M_{\odot} star.



I
Introduction

Strange stars are self-bounded compact objects composite by deconfined quarks. The theory of strange stars is based on the so-called Bodmer-Witten conjecture
Bodmer 1971
;
Witten 1984
, It assumes that ordinary matter as we know it, composed of protons and neutrons, may only be meta-stable, while the true ground state of strongly interacting matter would therefore consist of the so-called strange quark matter (SQM), which in turn is composed of deconfined up, down and strange quarks. For the SQM hypothesis to be true, the energy per baryon of the deconfined phase (for
p=0
and
T=0
) must be inside the so called stability window
Lopes and Menezes 2022
, i.e; the energy per baryon must be lower than the nonstrange infinite baryonic matter, i.e., (
E_{\rm uds}/A<930
MeV), while at the same time, the nonstrange matter still needs to have an energy per baryon higher than the one of nonstrange infinite baryonic matter (
E_{\rm ud}/A>930
MeV); otherwise, protons and neutrons would decay into
u
and
d
quarks. It is possible, therefore, that at least some of the observed pulsars are indeed strange stars instead of ordinary hadronic neutron stars.

On the other hand, our current understanding predicts that 27
\%
of the universe is made of dark matter (DM), 68
\%
of dark energy (the main component that explains the accelerated expansion of the universe), and only 5
\%
is luminous matter. Compact objects may capture some amount of DM inside
them in their evolving time due to their immense gravitational potential.
Non-annihilating DM can accumulate and thermalize in a small radius, producing changes in all macroscopic properties. While most pulsars observed pulsars can be explained as ordinary neutron stars or standard strange stars, ultra-compact objects present a new challenge for theoretical astrophysicists.

The main goal of the present work is to study the nature of the XTE J1814-338 pulsar. The recent work of Kini
et al.

Kini et al. 2024
, has revealed a striking mass (M) and radius (R) measurement of
M=1.21^{+0.05}_{-0.05}\rm M_{\odot}
and
\rm R=7.0^{+0.4}_{-0.4}km
at 68% confidence level (CL). The inner composition of such an ultra-compact object has been a subject of intense research with some of them speculating that the presence of DM cannot be ruled out. Particularly, some study points to a bosonic DM star with a nuclear matter core
Pitz and Schaffner-Bielich 2024
whilst others point to a strange quark star admixed mirrored DM
Yang et al. 2024
and Fermionic DM admixed neutron star
Lopes and Issifu 2024
.

The theory of DM-admixed neutron stars has already been studied in the literature
Goldman et al. 2013
;
Panotopoulos and Lopes 2017
;
Lourenço et al. 2021
;
Das et al. 2022
;
Lopes and Das 2023
;
Das et al. 2019
;
Lenzi et al. 2023
;
Lourenço et al. 2022a
;
Deliyergiyev et al. 2019
;
Lourenço et al. 2022b
. In this work, we revisit this topic, focusing on the macroscopic properties of the XTE J1814-338 pulsar as a DM-admixed strange star. Indeed, such a possibility was already studied in ref.
Yang et al. 2024
, therefore it is worth to point out here the differences between the present work and those presented in ref.
Yang et al. 2024
.

First, the formalism of both phases, (quark matter matter and dark matter) is different. For the strange quark matter (SQM), the authors in ref.
Yang et al. 2024
used the modified bag model, where the effects of gluons were taken into account up to
O(\alpha_{s}^{2})

Farhi and Jaffe 1984
;
Weber 2005
, and used mirror dark matter (MDM), where every Standard Model particle has a mirror counterpart (This means that the mirror SQM (mirror up, down and strange) is governed by the same thermodynamic principles as ordinary quark matter
Lee and Yang 1956
;
Mohapatra and Teplitz 1997
;
Foot et al. 1991
;
Khlopov 2021
. . Consequently, the EOS for MDM is identical to that of SQM, and the amount of the DM is fixed as a fraction of the total star mass.

In the present work, I use the thermodynamical consistent vector MIT bag model, as discussed in detail in refs.
Lopes et al. 2021a
;
Lopes et al. 2021b
;
Biesdorf et al. 2023
. In the same sense, instead of mirror dark matter, I use a WIMP dark matter here, specifically the lightest neutralino with a mass of 200 GeV. The amount of the DM is fixed by fixing the neutralino Fermi moment, as done in refs.
Lourenço et al. 2021
;
Lourenço et al. 2022a
;
Das et al. 2022
;
Lopes and Das 2023
. Moreover, I extend the calculation beyond the mass-radius diagram and calculate the gravitational redshift, the moment of inertia (MOI), and the dimensionless tidal parameter. Those calculation are not present in ref.
Yang et al. 2024
.

II
Formalism

II.1
Strange quark matter

To model the quark matter I use the thermodynamical consistent version of the vector MIT bag model, whose Lagrangian density reads:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm vMIT}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg\{\bar{\psi}_{q}\big[\gamma^{\mu}(i\partial_{\mu}-g_{qV}V_{\mu})-m_{q}\big]\psi_{q}

(1)

\displaystyle-B+\frac{1}{2}m_{V}^{2}V^{\mu}V_{\mu}\bigg\}\Theta(\bar{\psi}_{q}\psi_{q}),

where
m_{q}
is the mass of the quark
q
of ﬂavor
u
,
d
or
s
,
\psi_{q}
is the Dirac quark ﬁeld,
B
is the constant vacuum pressure, and
\Theta(\bar{\psi}_{q}\psi_{q})
is the Heaviside step function to ensure that the quarks exist only conﬁned to the bag. The parameters utilized in this work are:
m_{u}=m_{d}=4
MeV,
m_{s}=95
MeV,
B^{1/4}=140
MeV, and
G_{V}=(g_{v}/m_{v})^{2}=0.3
fm
2
. Moreover, the obtained EOS is charge neutral and chemically stable.

It is important to point out that the results are ultimately model-dependent. The chosen values, in addition to ensuring that the SQM is inside the stability window and agrees with the Bodmer-Witten conjecture, are able to simultaneously satisfy some astrophysical constraints (as PSR J0740+6620, PSR J0437-4715, and HESS J1731-34). This will become clearer in the next sections. An additional discussion of the model can be found in
Lopes et al. 2021a
;
Lopes et al. 2021b
;
Biesdorf et al. 2023
and the references therein.

II.2
Dark Matter

The Lagrangian of the fermionic DM has a QHD-like form and reads
Das et al. 2019
;
Lourenço et al. 2021
;
Lopes and Issifu 2024
;
Das et al. 2022
;
Panotopoulos and Lopes 2017
;
Lopes and Das 2023
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm DM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\chi}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-(m_{\chi}-g_{H}h))\chi

(2)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}(\partial^{\mu}h\partial_{\mu}h-m_{H}^{2}h^{2}).

Here, we assume a dark fermion represented by the Dirac field
\chi
that self-interacts through the exchange of the Higgs boson, whose mass is
m_{H}
= 125 GeV. The coupling constant is assumed to be
g_{H}=0.1
, which agrees with the constraints in Refs.
Panotopoulos and Lopes 2017
;
Das et al. 2022
. In this framework, the DM self-interaction is extremely weak, resembling a tenuous, free Fermi gas. The dark matter energy eigenvalue is therefore:

E_{\chi}=\sqrt{m_{\chi}^{*2}+k_{F}^{2}},

(3)

where
m_{\chi}^{*}
=
m_{\chi}-g_{H}h
and
m_{\chi}
= 200 GeV being the mass of the lightest neutralino as discussed in refs.
Lourenço et al. 2022b
;
Lenzi et al. 2023
;
Das et al. 2022
;
Lopes and Das 2023
. Moreover, following those papers, we use the Fermi momentum to fix the dark matter content, up to
k_{F}^{DM}=0.08
GeV. As
k<<m_{\chi}
, the pressure of the DM is almost zero.

I also add a term that couples the quark matter with the DM, similarly as it was done for the hadronic case in ref.
Das et al. 2022
;
Lenzi et al. 2023
;
Lourenço et al. 2022b
;
Lopes and Issifu 2024
;
Lourenço et al. 2022a
.

\mathcal{L}=f\frac{m_{q}}{v}h\bar{\psi_{q}}\psi_{q}.

(4)

Within this coupled channel, the effective mass of the quark
q
now depends on the field
h
. In the same sense, the field
h
now depends on both the DM scalar density (
n_{s}^{DM}
) and the quark scalar density
(n_{s}^{q})

Lenzi et al. 2023
;
Lourenço et al. 2022b
;
Lopes and Issifu 2024
:

\displaystyle m^{*}_{q}=m_{q}-f\frac{m_{q}}{v}h,

\displaystyle h=\frac{g_{H}}{m_{H}^{2}}n_{s}^{DM}+\frac{f}{m_{H}^{2}}\sum_{B}\frac{m_{q}}{v}n_{s}^{q},

(5)

where
v
= 246 GeV is the Higgs vacuum expectation value, and
f
= 0.3 as done in ref.
Lenzi et al. 2023
;
Lourenço et al. 2022b
. This term is quite weak, being stronger for the
s
-quark compared to the
u
and
d
quarks, as expected for the Higgs channel. The total energy density and pressure are the sum of the quark, and DM components obtained through a mean-field approximation (see ref.
Das et al. 2019
;
Lenzi et al. 2023
;
Lourenço et al. 2022a
and the references therein for a complete discussion).

Numerical Results

Oppenheimer and Volkoff 1939
. Once the mass-radius relation is obtained, the gravitational redshift,
z
, can be calculated as
Lopes 2021

z=\bigg(1-\frac{2M}{R}\bigg)^{-1/2}-1.

(6)

Ultimately, I discuss the dimensionless tidal parameter
\Lambda
. If we put an extended body in an inhomogeneous external field, it will experience different forces throughout its surface. The result is a tidal interaction. The tidal deformability of a compact object is a single parameter
\lambda
that quantifies how easily the object is deformed when subjected to an external tidal field. Larger tidal deformability indicates that the object is easily deformed. Conversely, a compact object with a small tidal deformability parameter is more compact and more difficult to deform. The tidal deformability is defined as:

\Lambda~\equiv~\frac{\lambda}{M^{5}}~\equiv~\frac{2k_{2}}{3C^{5}},

(10)

where
M
is the mass of the compact object and
C=GM/R
is its compactness. The parameter
k_{2}
is called the second (order) Love number. Additional discussion about the theory of tidal deformability and the tidal Love numbers is beyond this work’s scope and can be found in Refs.
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2018
;
Abbott et al. 2019
;
Flores et al. 2020
;
Hinderer et al. 2010
;
Lourenço et al. 2022a
;
Lopes and Menezes 2022
;
Lopes and Das 2023
. Nevertheless, as pointed out in Refs.
Hinderer et al. 2010
;
Lourenço et al. 2022a
, the value of
y_{R}
must be corrected when a discontinuity is present.
This is the case of strange stars, once they are self-bounded. Therefore, we must have:

y_{R}\rightarrow y_{R}-\frac{4\pi R^{3}\Delta\epsilon_{S}}{M},

(11)

where
R
and
M
are the star radius and mass, respectively, and
\Delta\epsilon_{S}
is the difference between the energy density at the surface (
p=0
) and the star’s exterior (which implies
\epsilon=0

IV
Final Remarks

In this work, I investigate the macroscopic properties of the ultracompact object XTE J1814-338 pulsar, assuming it to be a DM-admixed strange star. I first study what the total amount of DM is necessary to compress a 1.2
M_{\odot}
to a radius in the range of 7.0
\pm
0.4 km. I found that a high value for the DM Fermi momentum,
k
= 0.08 GeV is needed. Such a high value implies that the fraction of DM inside of the star,
f_{DM}
corresponds to 53.5
\%
of the star’s total mass. Although a considerable value, this is still lower than a MDM-admixed strange star, which requires at least 71.8
\%

Yang et al. 2024
.

It can be noticed form the TOV solution in Fig.
1
that it is very unliked that the XTE J1814-338 object presents the same nature of others pulsars as the PSR J0437-4715 and the PSR J0740+6620. The results of gravitational redshift reinforce this idea.

Analyzing the MOI and the tidal dimensionless parameter
\Lambda
, we see that while standard strange stars present values above the upper limits, in the case of DM-admixed strange stars compatible with the XTE J1814-338 object, the calculated values are below the lower limits. Again, this suggests a different nature. If the mass and radius inferred for the XTE J1814-338 object presented in ref.
Kini et al. 2024
are confirmed, this will result in strong indirect evidence of the existence of DM particles.

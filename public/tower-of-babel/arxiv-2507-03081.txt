Carroll theories from Lorentzian light-cone theories
Sucheta Majumdar

Abstract
We derive Carrollian field theories via null reduction from Lorentzian light-cone actions in Minkowski spacetime. By suitably deforming the light-cone action, we reduce the Poincaré invariance to a Bargmann subgroup, from which both magnetic and electric Carroll actions can be obtained in one lower dimension. Through a canonical analysis, we show that the second-class constraints usually found in Lorentzian light-cone theories are absent for these deformed Bargmann-invariant actions. We demonstrate the procedure for theories with and without gauge symmetry. Notably, while the magnetic Carroll sector can be directly derived from the original Lorentzian action, the deformation is essential to obtain the electric Carroll sector. We further argue that magnetic Carroll solutions in d d dimensions represent a consistent truncation of the solutions of the ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -dimensional Lorentzian parent theory, providing an effective description of light-cone dynamics near a null hypersurface. For gauge theories, we also highlight the role of the light-cone gauge condition in deriving Carrollian theories.

In an earlier work

Bargmann-invariant actions. This implies that, in general, one needs to construct two separate Bargmann actions in the higher-dimensional spacetime
-
one for the electric sector and another for the magnetic sector. Furthermore, this stands in contrast with the group contraction method, where both electric and magnetic Carrollian actions follow from the same Lorentzian action but arise from different rescalings of the fields as

The goal of this paper is to derive
d
-dimensional Carroll-invariant actions using the null reduction method, starting from a
single
Lorentzian light-cone action in

dimensions. We discuss several illustrative cases, including scalar fields and electromagnetism to highlight the features of gauge systems, Yang-Mills theory to demonstrate interacting cases, and finally the free

We present the derivation of Carrollian theories in both Hamiltonian and Lagrangian formulations. As light-cone theories are first order in time and involve second-class primary constraints, it is instructive to perform a rigorous canonical analysis to better understand the subtleties involving the constraint structure and gauge fixing.

The outline of the paper is as follows. We begin with a brief summary of the key features of light-cone Minkowski spacetime and its corresponding Poincaré algebra. In the next section, we outline the null reduction procedure using the example of scalar field theory in the Hamiltonian framework. In fact, we present two different methods to derive Carrollian actions from Lorentzian actions in one higher dimension, although only one of these yields both the electric and magnetic sectors. Section 4 is devoted to the discussion of gauge theories. Starting with electromagnetism, we focus on the constraint analysis of light-cone gauge theories and then detail the procedure to derive electric and magnetic Carroll actions from the light-cone Maxwell action. We also discuss how the gauge-fixed formulation differs from the standard case, where the gauge freedom is left intact or manifest. Having established the general method, we then show how to obtain the same Carrollian actions directly in the Lagrangian formulation, without having to go through the detailed Dirac-Bergmann analysis of constraints

;
Bergmann:1949zz

Carrollian actions from null reduction

3
Scalars

We begin with the case of scalar fields in Minkowski spacetime. After briefly describing the canonical analysis of light-cone scalar field theory, we show how one can derive a magnetic Carrollian action

3.1
From Lorentzian light-cone action

Let us consider the Lorentzian action for massless scalar fields in light-cone coordinates

\mathcal{S}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \mathcal{L}^{Lor}\,,

(20)

where the Lagrangian density reads

\mathcal{L}^{Lor}=-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\mu\nu}_{lc}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi~=~\partial_{-}\phi\partial_{+}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi\,.

(21)

This action is invariant under the light-cone Poincaré transformations listed in (
2
). We define the conjugate momenta with respect to the (Carrollian) time
x^{+}

\pi=\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{Lor}}{\delta(\partial_{+}\phi)}=\partial_{-}\phi\,.

The canonical Poisson brackets are

\displaystyle\{\phi(x),\pi(y)\}\Big|_{x^{+}=y^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(x^{-}-y^{-})\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\,.

(23)

Since

\mathcal{S}_{E}[\phi,\pi,\lambda]=\int dx^{-}dx^{+}d^{\perp}x\Big\{\pi\partial_{+}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi-\lambda(\pi-\partial_{-}\phi)\Big\}\,,

3.2
From deformed light-cone action

In order to get the magnetic as well as electric Carroll theories, we need a more general action that is invariant under one of the Bargmann subgroups
\mathfrak{b}_{\pm}
, but not under the full Poincaré group
\mathfrak{p}
. We will therefore explicitly break Poincaré invariance down to one of its Bargmann subgroups, for which we need to choose a preferred null direction. Without loss of generality, we choose to work with the null vector
\mathfrak{n}=\partial_{+}
, and hence the relevant subgroup for our purposes is
\mathfrak{b}_{-}

To this end, we add to the action (

\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}\mathfrak{n}^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi

and

(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})

\displaystyle x^{+}_{\alpha}=x^{+}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\,x^{-}\,,\quad x^{-}_{\alpha}=x^{-}\,,\quad x^{i}_{\alpha}=x^{i}\,.

;

\mathcal{S}^{Barg}~=~\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left(\pi\partial_{+}\phi-\mathcal{H}^{Barg}\right)\,,

where the Hamiltonian density now involves both conjugate momenta and spatial gradients

\mathcal{H}^{Barg}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}(\pi-\partial_{-}\phi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi\,.

(41)

The canonical brackets are given by

\{\pi(x),\phi(y)\}=\delta(x^{-}-y^{-})\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\,,

(42)

while the symplectic form reads

\quad\Omega^{Barg}=\int dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ d_{V}\pi\wedge d_{V}\phi\,.

(43)

d
-dimensional theory

{}^{(d)}\Omega^{mag}\equiv\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{}^{(d+1)}\Omega^{Barg}_{\varepsilon}~=~\int d^{d-1}x\ d_{V}p_{m}\wedge d_{V}\phi_{m}\,.

(46)

As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the light-cone Bargmann action (
40
) reduces to the magnetic Carroll action

{}^{(d)}\mathcal{S}^{mag}[\phi_{m},p_{m}]=\int dx^{+}d^{d-1}x\left(p_{m}\partial_{+}\phi_{m}-\mathcal{H}^{mag}\right)\,,

(47)

with the Hamiltonian and momentum densities

\mathcal{H}^{mag}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi_{m}\partial^{i}\phi_{m}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}_{i}^{mag}=p_{m}\partial_{i}\phi_{m}\,.

(48)

This action is the same as the one we obtained in the previous section from the null reduction of the Lorentzian light-cone action.

The equations of motion derived from the magnetic Carroll action are

\displaystyle\partial_{+}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

(49)

\displaystyle-\partial_{+}p_{m}+\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,.

(50)

The first equation implies that a massless magnetic Carroll field cannot move,

\phi_{m}(x^{+},x^{i})=\phi_{m}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})\,.

(51)

The second equation allows us to solve for
p^{m}
as follows

p_{m}(x^{+},x^{i})=x^{+}\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi_{m}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})+p_{m}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})\,.

(52)

The initial data is therefore given by two arbitrary functions of the spatial coordinates,
\phi_{m}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})
and
p_{m}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})
. We also note from the structure of the equations of motion that the momenta
p_{m}
are not related to
\partial_{+}\phi_{m}
, just as in Lorentzian light-cone theories. However, unlike in the Lorentzian case, the momenta are not constrained through any primary constraints.

Electric case:

When going from
(d+1)
to
d
dimensions, we can consider another rescaling of the fields

\phi\to\varepsilon\phi_{e},\quad\pi\to\frac{p_{e}}{\varepsilon},\quad\alpha\to\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

(53)

which also leads to a well-defined symplectic 2-form

\Omega^{elec}\equiv\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{}^{(d+1)}\Omega^{Barg}_{\varepsilon}~=~\int d^{d-1}x\,d_{V}p_{e}\wedge d_{V}\phi_{e}\,.

(54)

In the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain the electric Carroll action from the Bargmann action (
40
)

{}^{(d)}\mathcal{S}^{elec}[\phi_{e},p_{e}]=\int dx^{+}d^{d-1}x\left(p_{e}\partial_{+}\phi_{e}-\mathcal{H}^{elec}\right)\,,

(55)

with the Hamiltonian and momentum densities

\mathcal{H}^{elec}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}p^{2}_{e}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}_{i}^{elec}=p_{e}\partial_{i}\phi_{e}\,.

(56)

Note that in the previous section, one could formally consider rescaling the fields of the Lorentzian theory in the same way as in (
53
). While this rescaling preserves the canonical structure, the Hamiltonian density
-
lacking any terms depending on
\pi

-
vanishes in the limit
{\varepsilon\to 0}
. As a result, it is not possible to obtain a well-defined electric Carroll action directly from the Lorentzian theory.

The equations of motion derived from the electric Carroll action are

\displaystyle\partial_{+}p_{e}=0\,,\quad\alpha\partial_{+}\phi_{m}=p_{e}\,.

(57)

The first equation implies that
p_{e}
is constant in time

p_{e}(x^{+},x^{i})=p_{e}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})\,,

(58)

and the second equation yields

\phi_{e}(x^{+},x^{i})=\frac{1}{\alpha}p_{e}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})x^{+}+\phi_{e}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})\,.

(59)

Therefore, the initial data is given by two arbitrary functions of the spatial coordinates,
\phi_{e}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})
and
p_{e}^{(0)}(0,x^{i})
, as in the magnetic case.

From the second equation of motion in (
57
), we observe that the electric Carroll momenta
p_{m}
are directly related to the velocities
\partial_{+}\phi_{e}
. This feature further emphasizes why the electric Carroll theory cannot be obtained directly from the Lorentzian action. In the Lorentzian light-cone phase space, the momenta are always constrained variables (
22
) and are not related to the velocities. To obtain the electric sector, it is therefore essential to deform the theory to a Bargmann-invariant action.

Accordingly, we have presented two distinct approaches for deriving Carrollian field theories from a Lorentzian light-cone action: one that yields only the magnetic sector, and another one relying on a deformation of the Lorentzian action that leads to both electric and magnetic Carroll theories. This distinction is depicted schematically in Figure
2
.

We can also include a mass term of the form
m^{2}\phi^{2}
in both the Lorentzian or the Bargmann-invariant action. In the magnetic sector, this term automatically survives the null reduction. However, in the electric sector, the mass parameter
m
must be rescaled by a factor of
1/\varepsilon
in order to retain the mass term in the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit.

3.3
Carroll invariance and canonical generators

In this section, we highlight certain properties that these Carroll theories share with the Carroll Hamiltonian theories obtained from the group contraction method (the
c\to 0
limit of Poincaré theories) in
Henneaux:2021yzg
.

As described in Appendix
A.1
, the Carrollian structure of the null hypersurface
\Sigma
, given by the degenerate metric
h_{ab}
and the null vector
\mathfrak{n}^{a}
, are preserved by the Carroll transformations

\xi^{+}=b_{i}x^{i}+a^{+}\,,\quad\xi^{i}=\omega^{i}_{j}x^{j}+a^{i}\,.

(60)

where
b^{i}
are the Carroll boosts. The canonical generator for these transformations reads

G[{\xi^{+},\xi^{i}}]=\int d^{\perp}x\ (\xi^{+}\mathcal{H}^{C}+\xi^{i}\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i})

(61)

where the Hamiltonian and momentum densities are either the electric or magnetic type as specified in (
56
) and (
48
). Here, we use the notation
\phi^{C},p^{C}
to collectively denote the Carrollian fields which could be either electric,
\phi_{e},p_{e}
or magnetic
\phi_{m},p_{m}
, unless specified otherwise. The commutation relations among the energy and momentum densities are

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{H}^{C}(x),\,\mathcal{H}^{C}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

(62)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{H}^{C}(x),\,\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{C}(y)\,\,\partial_{i}\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\,,

(63)

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i}(x),\,\mathcal{P}^{C}_{j}(y)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big[\partial_{j}\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i}(y)+\partial_{i}\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\mathcal{P}^{C}_{j}(x)\big].

(64)

From (
62
), we observe that the commutator of two Hamiltonian densities vanishes, which is a characteristic feature of Carrollian dynamics
Henneaux:1979vn
.

\displaystyle E=\int d^{\perp}x\,\mathcal{H}^{C}(x)\,,\quad P_{i}=\int d^{d-1}x\,\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i}(x)\,,

(65)

\displaystyle M_{ij}=\int d^{\perp}x\,(x_{i}\mathcal{P}^{C}_{j}-x_{j}\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i})\,,

(66)

\displaystyle K_{i}=\int d^{\perp}x\,(x^{+}\mathcal{P}^{C}_{i}-x_{i}\mathcal{H}^{C})\,.

(67)

The non-vanishing commutators of the Carroll algebra are

\displaystyle\left[K_{i},\,P_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}E\,,

(68)

\displaystyle\left[M_{ij},\,P_{l}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{lj}P_{i}-\delta_{il}P_{j}\,,

(69)

\displaystyle\left[M_{ij},\,K_{l}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{lj}K_{i}-\delta_{il}K_{j}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle\left[M_{ij},M_{lm}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{im}M_{jl}-\delta_{il}M_{jm}-\delta_{jm}M_{il}+\delta_{jl}M_{im}\,.

(71)

One can easily verify that the corresponding field transformations generated by (
61
),

\delta_{\xi^{+},\xi^{i}}\phi^{C}=\{\phi^{C},G[{\xi^{+},\xi^{i}}]\}\,,\quad\delta_{\xi^{+},\xi^{i}}p^{C}=\{p^{C},G[{\xi^{+},\xi^{i}}]\}

(72)

render the Carrollian action (
35
) invariant. This fact, along with the algebra of constraints given by the set of equations (
62
) constitute the necessary and sufficient condition for a Hamiltonian theory to be Carrollian
Henneaux:2021yzg
.

We now focus on the precise form of the transformation laws of the magnetic and electric Carroll fields under the isometries
(\xi^{+},\xi^{i})
. In the magnetic case, we find

\displaystyle\delta_{(\xi^{+},\xi^{i})}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{+}\partial_{+}\phi_{m}+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}\phi_{m}~=~\xi^{i}\partial_{i}\phi_{m}\,,

(73)

\displaystyle\delta_{(\xi^{+},\xi^{i})}p_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{i}(\xi^{+}\partial_{i}\phi_{m})+\partial_{i}(\xi^{i}p_{m})\,,

(74)

where we have used the equation of motion for the last equality in (
73
). In particular, under Carroll boosts,
\xi=(b_{i}x^{i},0)
, the magnetic fields transform as

\delta_{b}\phi_{m}=0\,,\quad\delta_{b}p_{m}=b_{i}\partial^{i}\phi_{m}+b_{j}x^{j}\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi_{m}\,.

(75)

Similarly, in the electric case, the Carroll transformation laws are given by

\displaystyle\delta_{(\xi^{+},\xi^{i})}\phi_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{+}\partial_{+}\phi_{e}+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}\phi_{e}~=~\xi^{+}\frac{p_{e}}{\alpha}+\xi^{i}\partial_{i}\phi_{m}\,,

(76)

\displaystyle\delta_{(\xi^{+},\xi^{i})}p_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial^{i}(\xi^{+}\partial_{i}\phi_{e})+\partial_{i}(\xi^{i}p_{e})=\partial_{i}(\xi^{i}p_{e})\,,

(77)

where we have used the equation of motion for the last equality in both the expressions. Under Carroll boosts,
\xi=(b_{i}x^{i},0)
, the transformation laws for the electric fields read

\delta_{b}\phi_{e}=\frac{1}{\alpha}b_{i}x^{i}p_{e}\,,\quad\delta_{b}p_{e}=0\,.

(78)

These transformation laws are in complete agreement with the ones given in
Henneaux:2021yzg
. This further shows that although derived using a different technique, these Hamiltonian theories are the same as that obtained using the group contraction procedure.

4
Gauge fields

We now extend the procedure to various gauge theories. We begin with the example of electromagnetism, discussing the canonical analysis of light-cone gauge theories in both the Lorentzian and Bargmann cases. In particular, we highlight the role of gauge fixing in the null reduction method for obtaining Carrollian theories. We shall then generalize the procedure to non-Abelian gauge theories and
p
-form fields.

4.1
Electromagnetism

Let us consider the Maxwell action

\mathcal{S}^{Lor}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \,\mathcal{L}_{EM}\,,

(79)

with Lorentzian light-cone Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{EM}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{+-})^{2}+F_{+i}F_{-i}-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}\,,

(80)

where the Abelian field strength reads
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
.

In the Hamiltonian formulation, we find that some of the conjugate momenta are constrained, as in the case of scalars. The Hamiltonian analysis of light-cone electromagnetism is detailed in Appendix
B.2
.

To obtain both magnetic and electric Carroll theories, we need to deform this action to a more general Bargmann action by adding a term of the form
\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}\mathfrak{n}^{\nu}\eta^{\rho\sigma}_{lc}F_{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\sigma}
to the Lorentzian Lagrnagian (
80
)

\mathcal{S}^{Barg}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \left\{\frac{1}{2}\alpha(F_{+i})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(F_{+-})^{2}+F_{+i}F_{-i}-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}\right\}\,.

(81)

The conjugate momenta are given by

\displaystyle\pi^{+}=0\,,\quad\pi^{-}=F_{+-}\,,\quad\pi^{i}=F_{-i}+\alpha F_{+i}\,.

(82)

The momenta
\pi^{i}
are no longer constrained but explicitly depend on the velocities, as opposed to the Lorentzian case. The Hamiltonian density reads

\mathcal{H}^{Barg}~=~\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{-})^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}(\pi_{i}-F_{-i})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}-A_{+}\mathcal{G}\,.

(83)

The first class constraint
\mathcal{G}=\partial_{-}\pi^{-}+\partial_{i}\pi^{i}
defines the canonical generator

{}^{(d+1)}G[\epsilon]=\int dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \epsilon(x)\mathcal{G}(x)\,,

(84)

that generates the gauge transformations on the
(d+1)
-dimensional fields

\delta_{\epsilon}A_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon\,,\quad\delta_{\epsilon}\pi^{\mu}=0\,.

(85)

The Hamiltonian action for the Bargmann-invariant theory reads

\mathcal{S}^{Barg}_{H}~=~\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left\{\pi^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}+\pi^{-}\partial_{+}A_{-}-\mathcal{H}^{Barg}\right\}\,.

(86)

We now restrict the theory to the light front
\Sigma
using the null reduction method. As discussed earlier, we also need to ensure that the vector fields have no components off the hypersurface
\Sigma
. To achieve this, we set
A_{-}
and its conjugate momentum
\pi^{-}
to zero. This is, in fact, the light-cone gauge condition extensively used in quantum field theories in the light-cone formalism
Kogut:1969xa
;
Brodsky:1997de
.

Magnetic case:

To obtain the magnetic Carroll sector, we rescale the remaining components of the Maxwell fields and the momenta as follows

A_{i}\to A_{i}^{m}\,,\quad A_{+}\to A_{+}^{m}\,,\quad\pi^{i}\to p^{i}_{m}\,,\quad\alpha\to\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,.

(87)

To simplify the notation, we drop the subscript
m
on the magnetic Carroll fields. As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the light-cone Bargmann action (
86
) reduces to the Carroll action

{}^{(d)}\mathcal{S}^{mag}[A_{i},p^{i}]=\int dx^{+}d^{\perp}x\left(p^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}-\mathcal{H}^{mag}-A_{+}\mathcal{G}_{Carr}\right)\,,

(88)

with the magnetic Carroll Hamiltonian and momentum densities

\mathcal{H}^{mag}=\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}_{i}^{mag}=p^{j}\partial_{i}A_{j}\,.

(89)

In Appendix
B.2
, we show that the same action can be obtained directly from the Lorentzian action without any deformation.
The Carroll Gauss constraint reads
G_{Carr}=\partial_{k}p^{k}
. The equations of motion obtained from the Hamiltonian action are

\partial_{+}A_{i}-\partial_{k}A_{+}=0\,,\quad-\partial_{+}p^{i}+\partial_{j}F^{ij}=0\,,\quad\partial_{k}p^{k}=0\,.

(90)

Using some simple redefinitions

p^{i}\equiv E^{i}\,,\quad F^{ij}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ijk}B_{k}\,,

(91)

we can recast these field equations into the familiar form of Maxwell equations for the magnetic case

\nabla\cdot\vec{E}=0\,,\quad-\frac{\partial\vec{E}}{\partial x^{+}}+\nabla\times\vec{B}=0\,,\quad\nabla\cdot\vec{B}=0\,,\quad\frac{\partial\vec{B}}{\partial x^{+}}=0\,,

(92)

where we have used the Bianchi identities for the last two equations.

Electric case:

To obtain the electric Carroll sector, we rescale the vector fields and the momenta as follows

A_{i}\to\varepsilon A_{i}^{e}\,,\quad A_{+}\to\varepsilon A_{+}^{e}\,,\quad\pi^{i}\to\frac{p^{i}_{e}}{\varepsilon}\,,\quad\alpha\to\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

(93)

For simplicity, we again drop the subscript
e
on the electric Carroll fields. As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the light-cone Bargmann action (
86
) reduces to the electric Carroll action

{}^{(d)}\mathcal{S}^{elec}[A_{i},p^{i}]=\int dx^{+}d^{\perp}x\left(p^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}-\mathcal{H}^{elec}-A_{+}\mathcal{G}_{Carr}\right)\,,

(94)

with the Carroll Hamiltonian and momentum densities

\mathcal{H}^{elec}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}p^{i}p_{i}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}_{i}^{elec}=p^{j}\partial_{i}A_{j}\,.

(95)

The equations of motion obtained from the Hamiltonian action are

\partial_{+}A_{i}-\partial_{i}A_{+}-\frac{p_{i}}{\alpha}=0\,,\quad\partial_{+}p^{i}=0\,,\quad\partial_{k}p^{k}=0\,.

(96)

Again, using some simple redefinitions

\frac{p^{i}}{\alpha}\equiv E^{i}\,,\quad F^{ij}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ijk}B_{k}\,,

(97)

we can rewrite the equations of motion as the Maxwell equations for the electric case

\nabla\cdot\vec{E}=0\,,\quad\frac{\partial\vec{E}}{\partial x^{+}}=0\,,\quad\frac{\partial\vec{B}}{\partial x^{+}}+\nabla\times\vec{E}=0\,,\quad\nabla\cdot\vec{B}=0\,,

(98)

where we have used the Bianchi identities for the last two equations. This shows that the electric and magnetic Carroll theories so obtained agree with the ones presented in
Duval:2014uoa
;
Henneaux:2021yzg
.

In both the electric and magnetic cases, the gauge transformations on the
d
-dimensional Carroll fields are now generated by

{}^{(d)}G_{Carr}[\epsilon]=\int d^{\perp}x\ \epsilon(x)\mathcal{G}_{Carr}(x)=\int d^{\perp}x\ \epsilon\,\partial_{k}p^{k}_{C}\,,

(99)

where
p^{k}_{C}
is either the electric or magnetic Carrollian momenta. The Carroll transformations are generated by
G[\xi^{+},\xi^{i}]
, as mentioned in the previous section, with the Hamiltonian and momentum densities replaced by their Maxwell counterparts. One can check the Maxwell Hamiltonian and momentum densities in both electric and magnetic cases also satisfy the Carrollian hypersurface deformation algebra (
62
).

4.2
Light-cone gauge

One important reason for the effectiveness of the light-cone formalism is the convenient choice of gauge, which allows one to eliminate the redundant fields and express the theory entirely in terms of its physical degrees of freedom. Accordingly, we shall now consider the light-cone gauge-fixed formulation of electromagnetism
Kogut:1969xa
as our starting point for the null reduction procedure and show that the resulting Carroll actions are identical to those discussed in the previous section.

Starting from the usual Maxwell action,

\mathcal{S}^{Lor}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \,\mathcal{L}_{EM}\,,

(100)

we impose the light-cone gauge condition
A_{-}=0
, which modifies the form of the Largangian

\mathcal{L}_{EM}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{-}A_{+})^{2}+F_{+i}(\partial_{-}A_{i})-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}\,.

(101)

This action can be further simplified by solving one of the Maxwell equations that leads to a constraint

\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu+}=\partial_{-}^{2}A_{+}-\partial_{-}\partial_{k}A_{k}=0\,.

(102)

Assuming that the fields fall-off at most as
\mathcal{O}(1)
at large
x^{-}
, we can solve for
A_{+}
in the above equation as follows

A_{+}(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})=\frac{\partial_{k}}{\partial_{-}}A^{k}+A_{+}^{(0)}(x^{+},x^{i})\,,

(103)

where
A_{+}^{(0)}(x^{+},x^{i})
represents the boundary degree of freedom and
(\partial_{-})^{-1}
is defined as in
Kogut:1969xa
;
Majumdar:2022fut
. Substituting this back to the Maxwell action yields the gauge-fixed on-shell action

\mathcal{S}^{Lor}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \,\mathcal{L}^{Lor}_{on-shell}\,,

(104)

with the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}^{Lor}_{on-shell}=\partial_{+}A^{i}\partial_{-}A_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A^{i}\partial^{j}A_{i}-\partial_{k}A_{+}^{(0)}\partial_{-}A_{k}\,.

(105)

We note that the last term involving the
A_{+}^{(0)}
field is indeed a boundary term
\int dx^{+}d^{\perp}x\,\partial_{k}A_{+}^{(0)}A_{k}
, which accounts for the residual or large gauge transformations in the theory preserving the gauge condition
A_{-}=0

Majumdar:2022fut
. But we keep it as a bulk term because here we are more interested in its role in the canonical analysis.

The conjugate momenta again lead to a first-class constraint
C_{0}=\pi^{+}_{(0)}
and a second-class constraint
C_{i}=\pi_{i}-\partial_{-}A_{i}
, whose form differs slightly from the ungauge-fixed case. We then perform the Dirac-Bergmann analysis of constraints and find that
C_{0}
leads to an additional first-class Gauss-like constraint
\mathcal{G}_{0}=\partial_{k}\pi^{k}
, which generates large gauge transformations. The Hamiltonian density takes the form

\mathcal{H}_{on-shell}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A^{i}\partial^{j}A_{i}-A_{+}^{(0)}\partial_{K}\pi^{k}-\lambda^{i}(\pi_{i}-\partial_{-}A_{i})\,.

(106)

The gauge-fixed light-cone phase space consists of the fields
A_{i}
and their momenta
\pi^{i}
, along with the boundary field
A_{+}^{(0)}
and
\lambda^{i}
, appearing as Lagrange multipliers. The Hamiltonian action is given by

\mathcal{S}_{on-shell}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left\{\pi^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A^{i}\partial^{j}A_{i}-A_{+}^{(0)}\mathcal{G}_{0}-\lambda^{i}C_{i}\right\}\,.

(107)

Now we turn to the null reduction procedure, taking the on-shell gauge-fixed Lagrangian action as our starting point. To deform this action and render it Bargmann-invariant, we add the term
\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}\mathfrak{n}^{\nu}\partial_{\mu}A_{i}\partial_{\nu}A^{i}
to the Lagrangian (
105
).

\mathcal{L}^{Barg}_{on-shell}=\frac{1}{2}\alpha(\partial_{+}A_{i})^{2}+\partial_{+}A^{i}\partial_{-}A_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A^{i}\partial^{j}A_{i}-\partial_{k}A_{+}^{(0)}\partial_{-}A_{k}\,.

(108)

The corresponding Hamiltonian density becomes

\mathcal{H}^{Barg}_{on-shell}~=~\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{-})^{2}+\frac{1}{2\alpha}(\pi_{i}-\partial_{-}A_{i})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{j}A^{i}\partial^{j}A_{i}\,,

(109)

which no longer involves any second class constraints for the
\pi^{i}
. From the Bargmann-invariant Hamiltonian action

\mathcal{S}^{Barg}_{on-shell}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left(\pi^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}-\mathcal{H}^{Barg}_{on-shell}-A_{+}^{(0)}\mathcal{G}_{0}\right)

(110)

we can derive Carrollian electric and magnetic theories following the same procedure as before. Mapping the
(d+1)
dimensional fields to the
d
dimensional Carroll fields as follows

A_{i}\to A_{i}^{m}\,,\quad A^{(0)}_{+}\to A_{+}^{m}\,,\quad\pi^{i}\to p^{i}_{m}\,,\quad\alpha\to\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

(111)

gives the same magnetic Carroll action (
88
), while the other map

A_{i}\to\varepsilon A_{i}^{e}\,,\quad A^{(0)}_{+}\to\varepsilon A_{+}^{e}\,,\quad\pi^{i}\to\frac{p^{i}_{e}}{\varepsilon}\,,\quad\alpha\to\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,,

(112)

yields the electric Carroll action (
94
). Note that
\mathcal{H}^{mag}
may have a slightly different form, but it still contains only the magnetic part and involves only spatial gradients of
A_{i}
. One can readily check that first-class gauge constraint
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is already of the form of the Carrollian gauge constraint,
\mathcal{G}_{Carr}
.

The key point we wish to emphasize here is that the light-cone gauge condition
A_{-}=0
must be implemented at some point in the procedure in order to obtain the Carroll theories; however, the order in which it is applied does not matter. One could either start with the covariant Lorentzian action, deform it to the Bargmann action, and then impose the light-cone gauge condition
while performing the null reduction
, as shown in the previous section . Alternatively, one could
gauge-fix the Lorentzian action right away
, then deform it to a Bargmann-invariant action, and finally perform the null reduction, as described in this section. In both cases, one obtains the same Carroll theory in one lower dimension. Although the two procedures yield different phase spaces for the intermediate Bargmann theory, they reduce to the same Carrollian phase space after null reduction. We summarize this point with the help of a schematic diagram in Figure
3
.

4.3
Yang-Mills theory

We now turn to the case of Yang-Mills theory with the gauge group
SU(N)
. The field strength for the non-Abelian fields is given by

F^{A}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^{A}_{\mu}-gf^{ABC}A^{B}_{\mu}A^{C}_{\nu}\,,

(113)

where the gauge indices
A,B,C
run from
1
to
N
. The generators of the
SU(N)
Lie algebra satisfy the relations

[T^{A},T^{B}]=f^{ABC}T^{C}\,,\quad Tr(T^{A}T^{B})=\frac{1}{2}\delta^{AB}\,.

(114)

The Lorentzian Yang-Mills Lagrangian takes the form

\mathcal{L}^{Lor}_{YM}=-\frac{1}{4}Tr(F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu})=\frac{1}{2}F_{+-}^{A}F^{A}_{+-}+F^{A}_{+i}F^{A}_{-i}-\frac{1}{4}F^{A}_{ij}F^{ijA}\,.

(115)

Going to the Hamiltonian formulation, we find that the conjugate momenta are

\displaystyle\pi^{+A}~=~0\,,\quad\pi^{-A}~=~F^{A}_{-+}\,,\quad\pi^{iA}~=~F^{A}_{-i}\,.

(116)

The theory has second-class primary constraints, as in the case of electromagnetism,

C^{iA}=\pi^{iA}-F^{A}_{-i}\,,

(117)

as well as a first-class primary constraint

C^{A}=\pi^{+A}\,,

(118)

that further leads to the Gauss constraint in the Yang-Mills theory

\mathcal{G}^{A}=\partial_{-}\pi^{-A}+gf^{ABC}A_{-}^{B}\pi^{-C}+\partial_{k}\pi^{kA}+gf^{ABC}A_{k}^{B}\pi^{kC}=\mathcal{D}_{-}\pi^{-A}+\mathcal{D}_{k}\pi^{kA}\,,

(119)

where
\mathcal{D}_{\mu}
denote the covariant derivatives. The corresponding canonical generator

G[\epsilon^{A}]=\int dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \epsilon^{A}(x)\mathcal{G}^{A}(x)\,,

(120)

generates the gauge transformations on the
(d+1)
dimensional non-Abelian vector fields

\delta_{\epsilon}A^{A}_{\mu}=\mathcal{D}_{\mu}\epsilon^{A}\,,\quad\delta_{\epsilon}\pi^{\mu A}=0\,.

(121)

The canonical Hamiltonian density has the same form as in electromagnetism with the spacetime derivatives

(122)

and the light-cone Yang-Mills action in the Hamiltonian form

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{YM}^{Lor}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left\{\pi^{-A}\partial_{+}A_{-}^{A}+\pi^{iA}\partial_{+}A^{A}_{i}-\mathcal{H}^{Lor}_{YM}\right\}\,.

(123)

On restricting this action to a constant null plane, we can obtain a magnetic Carroll action in
d
dimensions. But since we are interested in both the electric and magnetic sectors, we shall directly go to the Bargmann case.

We deform the Lagrangian by adding a term of the form
\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}\mathfrak{n}^{\nu}\eta^{\rho\sigma}F^{A}_{\mu\rho}F^{A}_{\nu\sigma}
. Therefore, the resulting Bargmann invariant action reads

(124)

with the Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{Barg}_{YM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\alpha\,\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}\mathfrak{n}^{\nu}\eta^{\rho\sigma}F^{A}_{\mu\rho}F^{A}_{\nu\sigma}+\mathcal{L}^{Lor}_{YM}

(125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\alpha\,F_{+i}^{A}F^{A}_{+i}+\frac{1}{2}F_{+-}^{A}F^{A}_{+-}+F^{A}_{+i}F^{A}_{-i}-\frac{1}{4}F^{A}_{ij}F^{ijA}\,.

So far, we have focused on deriving Carrollian actions using the Hamiltonian formulation. This approach was primarily intended to highlight the nature of constraints in light-cone theories and to discuss how they differ in the Lorentzian and Bargmann-invariant cases. It also allowed us to verify that the resulting Hamiltonian actions satisfy the necessary and sufficient conditions for Carrollian field theories.

We now shift focus to the Lagrangian formulation and derive Carrollian theories directly within this framework. Using Yang-Mills theory as an example, we illustrate how to obtain both electric and magnetic Carroll sectors from the higher-dimensional Lagrangian action

by putting appropriate conditions on the components of the field strength. We shall then extend this Lagrangian approach to general
p
-form gauge fields in the following section.

\varepsilon\to 0

5
Concluding remarks

We conclude with some final remarks on the Carrollian theories derived from Lorentzian light-cone actions. In this work, we presented two distinct methods to obtain Carrollian field theories via null reduction of Lorentzian theories in one higher dimension, using the tools of both Hamiltonian and Lagrangian formulations. Our procedure offers an alternative route in which the magnetic sector emerges naturally, shedding light on various interesting properties of these theories.

By directly reducing the Lorentzian action to a null plane, one obtains only the magnetic Carroll sector, while extracting the electric Carroll sector requires deforming the Lorentzian action. This does not imply that the original Lorentzian theory lacks an electric sector, but rather that the magnetic sector is intrinsically embedded within the light-cone formulation. The fact that we can obtain the magnetic Carrollian theory without deforming the Lorentzian action hints at a deeper connection between light-cone dynamics and Carroll physics.

A further compelling argument supporting this connection comes from examining the solutions to the equations of motion in these theories. Using the example of scalar fields, we compare the equations of motion derived from the parent Lorentzian action (
20
)

(-2\partial_{-}\partial_{+}-\partial_{i}\partial^{i})\,\phi(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})=0

(152)

with those derived from the magnetic and electric Carroll actions (
145
)-(
146
)

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi_{m}(x^{+},x^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

(153)

\displaystyle\alpha\,\partial_{+}^{2}\phi_{e}(x^{+},x^{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,.

(154)

From the structure of these equations, we observe that a magnetic Carroll solution
\phi_{m}
satisfying (
153
) also solves the Lorentzian equation of motion (
152
) in one higher dimension, since
\partial_{-}\phi_{m}=0
by construction. However, this is not true for the electric case, because solutions
\phi_{e}
satisfying the electric Carroll equation (
154
) do not necessarily satisfy (
152
). This implies that the
d
-dimensional magnetic Carroll sector represents a
consistent truncation
of the Lorentzian solution space in
(d+1)
dimensions, much like a Kaluza-Klein reduction
Duff:1985jd
, whereas the electric solutions do not.

Let us consider an expansion of the Lorentzian field near the null plane
x^{-}=x^{-}_{0}

\phi(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})=\phi_{0}(x^{+},x^{i})+\underbrace{\varepsilon\phi^{(1)}(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})+\varepsilon^{2}\phi^{(2)}(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})+\ldots}_{\text{higher modes}}\,,

(155)

where
\phi
satisfies the field equation (
152
). We note that for the ‘zero mode’
\phi_{0}
, the Lorentzian field equation (
152
) reduces to (
153
), indicating that it indeed behaves like a magnetic Carroll field. When
\varepsilon
is very small,
i.e.
, close to a constant null hypersurface, we can consistently discard all the higher modes keeping only the zero mode. Therefore,
the behaviour of a Lorentzian light-cone field theory near a null hypersurface can be effectively modeled using a magnetic Carroll theory in one lower dimension
. The electric Carroll sector appears at order
\varepsilon
, but it is intertwined with the higher-order modes. As a result, it is not possible to consistently truncate the Lorentzian solution space to isolate only the electric Carroll sector.

In the light-cone formulation, a widely used technique known as discrete light-cone quantization (DLCQ) involves quantizing a theory within a finite light-cone interval or a spatial box of length
\varepsilon

Heinzl:2000ht
;
Lenz:1991sa
;
Burkardt:1995ct
. In this approach, one restricts the domain of one of the coordinates, say
x^{-}
, to a finite width
\varepsilon
, which leads to a discretization of momenta and the energy spectra. Depending on the physical problem at hand, different limits of
\varepsilon
are taken. For instance, in problems involving spontaneous symmetry breaking or zero modes, one considers the infinite volume or continuum limit
\varepsilon\to\infty

Yamawaki:1998cy
;
Heinzl:1991ri
. Conversely, taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, which corresponds to sending the longitudinal momenta
p_{-}\to\infty
, yields the infinite momentum frame
-
a limit that finds applications in ultraviolet analyses of quantum field theories and in string quantization
Brodsky:1997de
;
Banks:1996vh
.

In this paper, we also consider the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, albeit in a slightly different context. In fact, our procedure for deriving Carrollian field theories involves two parameters: the deformation parameter
\alpha
and the width of the light-cone interval
\varepsilon
. As discussed earlier,
\alpha
deforms the standard light-cone coordinates to the Bargmann coordinates
x^{\pm}_{\alpha}
, resulting in a general class of (flat) Bargmann metrics
G_{\mu\nu}

\eta_{\mu\nu}^{lc}\longrightarrow G_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\
-1&-\alpha&0\\
0&0&\delta_{ij}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(156)

Out of the two vectors
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{m}
, only
\mathfrak{n}
lies in the kernel of
G
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=0
, while
\mathfrak{m}^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=\alpha
. The light-cone Minkowski metric is thus recovered as a special case of
G_{\mu\nu}
with
\alpha=0
. Starting with a general Bargmann-invariant theory parametrized by
\alpha
, we can recover the Lorentzian theory in the limit
\alpha\to 0
. The Carrollian limit, on the other hand, corresponds to taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, effectively projecting the higher-dimensional parent theory onto a null hypersurface. In the usual infinite momentum or Galilean limit, one first compactifies the
x^{-}
direction and then takes the radius of compactification
\varepsilon
to zero, as is typically done in Kaluza-Klein reductions. Here, instead, we perform a
longitudinal contraction
along the
x^{-}
direction by inserting a delta function distribution peaked at a fixed value of
x^{-}
and then taking the width of the distribution
\varepsilon
to zero, thereby yielding the Carrollian limit.

In this work, we have restricted our analysis to scalar and gauge theories in the light-cone formulation on flat backgrounds. It would be interesting to extend these results to fermions and supersymmetric theories
Bergshoeff:2023vfd
. For the spin-two case, our procedure can be directly applied to the free massless theory, namely the Pauli-Fierz action. However, it would be more instructive to consider the general case of Einstein gravity and recover the Pauli-Fierz theory as a special case. Extending the analysis to curved backgrounds would require working with gravity in the double-null formulation
dInverno:1980kaa
;
Reisenberger:2007ku
;
Alexandrov:2014rta
and deforming it to a curved Bargmann geometry. From such a theory, one could derive both electric and magnetic Carroll gravities.

The double-null formulation of gravity is also crucial in the context of asymptotic symmetries, the infrared structure of gravity and flat space holography
Barnich:2024vks
;
Bekaert:2024tkv
. In this regard, the light-cone Minkowski case presented here, comprising two intersecting light fronts, serves as a toy model that mimics the asymptotic structure of spacetime where future and past null infinities meet at spatial infinity. Another promising direction would be to investigate whether the magnetic Carroll gravity derived directly from Lorentzian light-cone gravity via the null reduction approach matches existing results in the literature
Hartong:2015xda
;
Henneaux:2021yzg
;
Campoleoni:2022ebj
. We leave these investigations for future work.

Non-Lorentzian spacetimes from light-cone Minkowski

In Section 2, we discussed the Bargmann subalgebras that unify the two sets of Carroll and Galilei subalgebras found within the light-cone Poincar’e algebra. In this appendix, we show from a geometric point of view how the underlying Carrollian and Galilean structures emerge from the flat Bargmann structure of the light-cone Minkowski spacetime using the null reduction method

A.1
Bargmann to Carrollian spacetime

Here, we discuss how to obtain a Carrollian manifold as a null hypersurface embedded into a Bargmann manifold. Let us consider a
(d+1)

M^{d+1}
with the metric

where we assume that

are the local coordinates on
M^{d+1}

\mathfrak{n}=\partial_{+}
and
\mathfrak{m}=\partial_{-}
lie in the kernel of
G_{\mu\nu}

\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=0=\mathfrak{m}^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}\,.

(158)

Therefore, the light-cone Minkowski spacetime corresponds to two distinct Bargmann structures,
(M^{d+1},G_{\mu\nu},\mathfrak{n}^{\mu})
and
(M^{d+1},G_{\mu\nu},\mathfrak{m}^{\mu})
. In the Minkowski spacetime, the light fronts at

We illustrate the technique for the case of a constant
x^{-}
hypersurface. The light front
\Sigma
at
x^{-}=x^{-}_{0}
, where
x^{-}_{0}
is a constant, may be viewed as the embedding

x^{+}(y)=y^{+}\,,\quad x^{-}(y)=x^{-}_{0}\,,\quad x^{i}(y)=y^{i}\,,

(159)

where
y^{a}=(y^{+},y^{i})
are the local coordinates on
\Sigma^{d}

\tau_{\mu}=G_{\mu\nu}\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\tau=-dx^{-}\,.

(160)

The hypersurface
\Sigma
has a degenerate metric

g_{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\

(161)

and a vector field
\mathfrak{n}^{a}=\partial_{+}
, which lies in the kernel of
g_{ab}
such that
\mathfrak{n}^{a}g_{ab}=0
. Thus, we see that the light front indeed has a flat Carrollian structure
(\Sigma^{d},g_{ab},\mathfrak{n}^{a})
. The isometries of the Carroll manifold given by

L_{\xi}\mathfrak{n}^{a}=0\,,\quad L_{\xi}g_{ab}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\xi=\xi^{+}(y^{i})\partial_{+}+(\omega^{i}_{j}y^{j}+a^{i})\partial_{i}\,,

(162)

are indeed the Carroll transformations. Additionally, if we restrict

to be linear in the coordinates, we get the finite-dimensional Carroll group

\xi^{+}=b_{i}y^{i}+a^{+}\,,\quad\xi^{i}=\omega^{i}_{j}y^{j}+a^{i}\,,

(163)

where
b_{i}
are the Carroll boosts,
\omega_{ij}
the spatial rotations and
(a^{+},a^{i})
the spacetime translations.

In order to define an ‘inverse’ of
g_{ab}
, we consider at every point on
\Sigma
, a second null vector
\lambda^{\mu}
, not parallel to
\mathfrak{n}^{\mu}
such that

G_{\mu\nu}\lambda^{\mu}\lambda^{\nu}=0\,,\quad G_{\mu\nu}n^{\mu}\lambda^{\mu}=1\,.

(164)

The vector
\lambda^{\mu}
is called the rigging of
\Sigma
and, in the present case, reads
\lambda=-\partial_{-}=-\mathfrak{m}
. We can now define a 1-form on
\Sigma

{\lambda_{a}=G_{\mu\nu}\lambda^{\mu}e^{\nu}_{a}\,,\quad\lambda_{a}\mathfrak{n}^{a}=1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\lambda=dy^{+}}\,.

(165)

The degenerate metric

cannot be inverted, but using

(166)

which has the form

g^{ab}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\

(167)

The induced volume form on
\Sigma

d\omega_{\Sigma}=dy^{+}\wedge dy^{1}\ldots\wedge dy^{(d-1)}\,.

(168)

Bargmann to Galilean spacetime

We now discuss how one can obtain a Galilean or a Newton-Cartan manifold from the flat Bargmann structure of light-cone Minkowski by projecting onto a constant

Appendix B
Canonical analysis of Lorentzian light-cone theories

In this appendix, we present a detailed discussion of the Hamiltonian analysis of Lorentzian light-cone field theories. Using scalar fields and Maxwell theory as examples, we focus on the primary second-class constraints that are characteristic of the light-cone formulation. This analysis allows us to highlight the similarities and differences between the Lorentzian actions and the deformed actions, both in the presence and absence of gauge symmetry.

B.1
Scalar field theory

We start with the Lorentzian light-cone Lagrangian for massless scalar fields

\mathcal{L}^{Lor}=-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}^{lc}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial^{\nu}\phi~=~\partial_{-}\phi\partial_{+}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi\,.

(175)

Treating
x^{+}
as (Carrollian) time, we define the conjugate momenta as

\pi=\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}^{Lor}}{\delta(\partial_{+}\phi)}=\partial_{-}\phi\,.

(176)

The equal-time Poisson brackets are

\displaystyle\{\phi(x),\pi(y)\}\Big|_{x^{+}=y^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(x^{-}-y^{-})\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\,.

(177)

The conjugate momenta
\pi
are constrained

\chi=\pi-\partial_{-}\phi\,,

(178)

where
\chi
is a second-class primary constraint

\{\chi(x),\chi(y)\}=-2\partial^{x}_{-}\delta(x-y)\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\,=C_{\chi\chi}.

(179)

These primary constraints found in the light-cone formulation behave differently from the usual first-class primary constraints that appear in gauge theories, such as the Gauss constraint in electromagnetism. We will return to this point in the next section.

To the canonical Hamiltonian density

\mathcal{H}_{C}=\pi\partial_{+}\phi-\mathcal{L}^{Lor}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi\,,

(180)

we add a term involving a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
, resulting in the primary Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}_{p}=\mathcal{H}_{C}+\lambda\chi=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi+\lambda(\pi-\partial_{-}\phi)\,.

(181)

The consistency condition for the constraint
\chi

\{\chi(x),H_{p}(y)\}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\partial_{-}\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi\,,

(182)

determines the Lagrange multiplier upto a zero mode in
x^{-}

\lambda(x^{+},x^{-},x^{i})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{x^{-}}dy^{-}\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi(x^{+},y^{-},x^{i})+\lambda_{0}(x^{+},x^{i})\,.

(183)

It is important to note that there is a “zero-mode” part of the primary constraint, without any dependence on
x^{-}
,

\chi_{0}=\lambda_{0}\pi_{0}\,,\quad\partial_{-}\lambda_{0}=0\,,\quad\partial_{-}\pi_{0}=0\,,

(184)

which appears to be first-class

\{\chi_{0}(x)\,,\chi_{0}(y)\}=0\,.

(185)

Hence, one must be cautious when strongly imposing the constraint by eliminating
\pi
for
\partial_{-}\phi
and reducing to the Lagrangian subspace, since one loses the information about the zero modes in this process
‡
‡

‡

These zero modes also play an important role in some interesting physical phenomena such as boundary degrees of freedom in residual gauge symmetries, Nambu-Goldstone modes associated with spontaneous symmetry breaking, to name a few
Majumdar:2024rxg
;
Yamawaki:1998cy
;
Heinzl:1991ri
.
. But in many cases such as massless free theories, the zero mode is actually constrained by the so-called ‘zero-mode constraint’ and thus, does not contribute to any dynamics
Yamawaki:1998cy
;
Heinzl:1991ri
(See also the appendices in
Alexandrov:2014rta
;
Adami:2023wbe
).

In this extended phase space, the Hamiltonian action takes the form

\mathcal{S}_{E}[\phi,\pi,\lambda]=\int dx^{-}dx^{+}d^{\perp}x\Big(\pi\partial_{+}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{i}\phi\partial^{i}\phi-\lambda\chi\Big)\,.

(186)

The equation of motions obtained from the action are as follows

\displaystyle\pi-\partial_{-}\phi=0\,,

(187)

\displaystyle\partial_{+}\phi-\lambda=0\,,

(188)

\displaystyle-\partial_{+}\pi-\partial_{-}\lambda+\partial_{i}\partial^{i}\phi=0\,.

(189)

The constraint
\chi_{\phi}
is retrieved from the equation of motion for
\lambda
. The canonical structure is given by

{}^{(d+1)}\Omega=\int dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ d_{V}\pi\wedge d_{V}\phi

(190)

where
d_{V}
denote exterior derivative in phase space.

B.2
Maxwell theory

Here, we discuss the canonical formulation of electromagnetism in the light-cone frame, based primarily on the analyses presented in
Hanson:1976cn
;
Steinhardt:1979it
. Note that we are not gauge fixing the theory at this stage, but rather reformulating Maxwell theory in light-cone coordinates.
Starting with the light-cone Maxwell action

\mathcal{S}^{Lor}_{EM}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\ \,\mathcal{L}_{EM}\,,

(191)

with the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}_{EM}=-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(F_{+-})^{2}+F_{+i}F_{-i}-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}\,,

(192)

we derive the conjugate momenta

\displaystyle\pi^{+}=0\,,\quad\pi^{-}=F_{+-}\,,\quad\pi^{i}=F_{-i}\,.

(193)

We note that only
\pi^{-}
, involving a
\partial_{+}
derivative, is dynamical, while
\pi^{+}
and
\pi^{i}
are constrained. The theory, therefore, has one first-class primary constraint

C=\pi^{+}\,,

(194)

and one second-class primary constraints

C_{i}=\pi_{i}-F_{-i}\,.

(195)

The first-class constraint is typical of gauge theories and leads to the Gauss constraint in Maxwell theory. In contrast, the second-class constraint is specific to the null-front Hamiltonian analysis and indicates that the light-cone theory is first order in time.

The canonical Hamiltonian density reads

\mathcal{H}_{c}=\pi^{\mu}\dot{A}_{\mu}-\mathcal{L}_{EM}=\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{-})^{2}+(\pi^{-}\partial_{-}+\pi^{k}\partial_{k})A_{+}+\frac{1}{4}(F_{ij})^{2}\,,

(196)

and the Poisson brackets are given by

\{A_{\mu}(x),\pi^{\nu}(y)\}\Big|_{x^{+}=y^{+}}=\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}\,\delta(x^{-}-y^{-})\delta^{\perp}(x-y)\,.

(197)

We now turn to the consistency conditions on the constraints. With the primary Hamiltonian

H_{p}=H_{c}+\int dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left(uC+u^{k}C_{k}\right)

(198)

where
u
and
u^{k}
are Lagrange multipliers, we find that the consistency condition for second-class constraints
C_{k}
reads

\dot{C}_{k}=\{C_{k}(x),H_{p}(y)\}=\partial_{k}\pi^{-}+\partial^{i}F_{ik}-2\partial_{-}u^{k}=0\,.

(199)

This condition does not lead to any secondary constraints, but instead determines the Lagrange multipliers
u^{k}
. However, the consistency condition for the first-class constraint
C

\dot{C}=\{C(x),H_{p}(y)\}=\partial_{-}\pi^{-}+\partial_{i}\pi^{i}

(200)

leads to a secondary constraint

\mathcal{G}=\partial_{-}\pi^{-}+\partial_{i}\pi^{i}\,,

(201)

which is simply the Gauss law in light-cone coordinates. Furthermore, we find that
\mathcal{G}
is first-class and conserved in time

\{\mathcal{G}(x),H_{p}(y)\}=0\,.

(202)

Therefore, the Dirac-Bergmann constraint algorithm
Dirac:1950pj
;
Bergmann:1949zz
stops here, as there are no further constraints in the theory. We thus arrive at the extended Hamiltonian density (neglecting some surface terms)

\mathcal{H}_{ext}=\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{-})^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(F_{ij})^{2}+u\,C+u^{k}C_{k}+(v-A_{+})\,\mathcal{G}\,.

(203)

From the equations of motion

\{A_{\mu},H_{ext}\}=0\,,\quad\{\pi^{\mu},H_{ext}\}=0\,,

(204)

we can determine the rest of the Lagrange multipliers as

u=\dot{A}_{+}\,,\quad v=0\,.

(205)

We refer readers to
Hanson:1976cn
for a more detailed treatment of the constraints. We finally arrive at the Hamiltonian action for light-cone electromagnetism.

\mathcal{S}_{ext}=\int dx^{+}dx^{-}d^{\perp}x\left\{\pi^{-}\partial_{+}A_{-}+\pi^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}-\frac{1}{2}(\pi^{-})^{2}-\frac{1}{4}(F_{ij})^{2}-u^{k}C_{k}+A_{+}\mathcal{G}\right\}\,.

(206)

We can restrict this Lorentzian action to a constant null hypersurface by inserting the smearing function
\delta_{\varepsilon}
into the action and setting

A_{-}=0\,,\quad\pi^{-}=0\,.

(207)

Then, assuming the
(d+1)
-dimensional Lorentzian fields are mapped to
d
-dimensional Carrollian fields as follows

A_{i}\to A_{i}^{m}\,,\quad A_{+}\to A_{+}^{m}\,,\quad\pi^{i}\to p^{i}_{m}\,,

(208)

we end up with the magnetic Carroll action for electromagnetism

{}^{(d)}\mathcal{S}^{mag}[A_{i},p^{i}]=\int dx^{+}d^{\perp}x\left(p^{i}\partial_{+}A_{i}-\frac{1}{4}F_{ij}F^{ij}-A_{+}\mathcal{G}_{Carr}\right)\,.

(209)

But, just as in the case of scalars, it is not possible to derive the electric Carroll sector of the Maxwell theory from the Lorentzian action directly.

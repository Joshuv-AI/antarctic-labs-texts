Introduction

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) involves diffuse remodeling of both the ventricles and the atria, with atrial fibrosis and slow conduction predisposing patients to atrial tachyarrhythmias. Atypical atrial flutter (AFL), particularly related to right inferior pulmonary vein (RIPV) reentry, is uncommon.

Case report

A 44-year-old woman with HCM and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF), treated with anticoagulation and amiodarone, was admitted with palpitations and dyspnea. A 12-lead electrocardiogram (Figure 1A) showed AFL with a cycle length of approximately 250 ms and a ventricular rate of 90 beats/min. Flutter wave morphology was positive in leads I, aVL, and II, biphasic in leads III and aVF, negative in lead aVR, and positive in lead V1 throughout the precordial leads, suggesting atypical left AFL arising from the right pulmonary veins (RPVs).1 Moreover, low flutter-wave amplitude (<100 μV) in lead II further supported an RIPV origin.2

A: Incessant atrial flutter (AFL) with a tachycardia cycle length (TCL) of 240 ms was observed. The flutter wave morphology suggested a right inferior pulmonary vein origin, characterized by positive deflections in leads I, aVL, and II. biphasic waves in leads III and aVF, and predominantly positive waves in lead V1 across the precordial leads, along with low amplitude in lead II. B: Cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging demonstrates left ventricular hypertrophy and mild pericardial effusions on the cine image, elevated extracellular volume (ECV) on ECV mapping, and prominent patchy late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) within the left ventricle (LV) (red arrowheads). C: LGE is identified at the interatrial septum (red arrows), and the right inferior pulmonary vein (RIPV) (orange arrows) extending to the carina as depicted on the 3-dimensional left atrial (LA) LGE-CMR model. D: Local fractionated electrograms (EGMs) at the septal region of the right atrium (RA) are identified. The cycle length histogram of the RA during AFL demonstrates incomplete mapping coverage, suggesting that the reentrant circuit is not confined to the RA. E–G: LA electroanatomic mapping (EAM) integrating activation, ripple, and voltage maps with the LGE-CMR map. Local activation (LAT) and ripple mapping demonstrate fractionated EGMs and slow conduction showing on histogram, which suggests an RIPV-dependent AFL. Low-voltage area and LGE showing at the LA anteroseptum (orange yellowheads), which acts as a natural barrier to conduction. This substrate directs the AFL propagation wavefront inferiorly, circulating around the RIPV and then advancing along the inferior wall and subsequently to the posterior wall of the LA, consistent with a reentrant mechanism anchored to structurally abnormal atrial tissue. H: Ablation was initiated at the anterior right pulmonary vein antrum (yellow star) and progressed downward to the RIPV, resulting in termination of AFL (green ball). The AFL morphology changed as the TCL prolonged. The initially positive flutter waves in the inferior leads (red box) became biphasic (blue box) and eventually completely negative (orange box), while a persistently positive flutter wave in lead V1 was observed, implying that the AFL circuit was progressively disrupted, with the wavefront forced downward and ultimately blocked.

Cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging demonstrated elevation of native T1 values (1438 ± 76 ms; 3-T MAGNETOM Vida scanner, Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) and extracellular volume (45% ± 15%) with patchy left ventricular late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) (Figure 1B, red arrowheads). Atrial LGE was also present at the interatrial septum (Figure 1C, red arrows) and RIPV (Figure 1C, orange arrows) extending to the carina.

A 3-dimensional (3D) left atrial (LA) model was created using ADAS software (Galgo Medical SL, Barcelona, Spain), and atrial LGE was displayed using the previously validated image intensity ratio threshold above 1.2.3

The patient underwent an electrophysiology study and catheter ablation on the CARTO 3 system (Biosense Webster, Inc., Irvine, CA, USA). The AFL cycle length was 240 ms. Initial right atrial (RA) mapping showed fractionated electrograms (EGMs) at the septum, but entrainment was unsuccessful and only 70% of the tachycardia cycle length could be mapped, suggesting that the reentrant circuit was not confined to the RA (Figure 1D and Supplemental Video 1). After transseptal puncture, LA mapping identified a deceleration zone and critical isthmus extending from the RIPV and carina of the RPVs (Figure 1E). Fractionated EGMs were identified at the RIPV and RPV carina, as shown on ripple mapping (Figure 1F and Supplemental Videos 2 and 3). Fractionated EGMs were also detected at the anteroseptum, which were opposite to the RA septal region with fractionated EGMs. This septal region corresponded to the low-voltage area at the LA anteroseptum on the electroanatomic voltage map and interatrial fibrosis on the 3D LA LGE-CMR map (Figure 1G, orange arrowheads). Subsequently, radiofrequency ablation was delivered from the RPV anterior antrum (Figure 1H, yellow star) using a QDOT MICRO catheter (Biosense Webster, Inc.). The AFL morphology gradually changed with prolongation of the tachycardia cycle length. Notably, positive flutter waves in inferior leads (Figure 1H, red box) were altered to biphasic (Figure 1H, blue box) and became totally negative (Figure 1H, orange box). A persistent positive flutter wave in lead V1 was observed, along with a transition from positive to biphasic flutter waves in leads V5 and V6, indicating that the AFL circuit was progressively blocked during ablation, with downward wavefront propagation preceding eventual termination at the inferior aspect of the RIPV (Figure 1H, green ball). Given the history of paroxysmal AF, circumferential isolation of all 4 pulmonary veins was then performed. During inducibility testing, a typical RA cavotricuspid isthmus (CTI)–dependent flutter was induced and terminated during CTI ablation. There was no further inducible atrial arrhythmias.

Discussion

CMR imaging not only defines the morphological features of HCM but also helps identify the atrial arrhythmogenic substrate. In point-by-point studies, LA LGE has been associated with lower bipolar voltage and slower conduction.4 Areas of atrial LGE may also show EGM abnormalities beyond low voltage alone, including fractionation and conduction delay.3 In our case, LGE at the interatrial septum and the RIPV/carina region colocalized with low-voltage areas and fractionated EGMs on electroanatomic mapping supporting a fibrosis-related mechanism for the atypical AFL rather than a purely functional circuit.4, 5, 6 The rapid firing atrial EGMs from the RIPV/carina region propagated anteriorly to encounter the naive fibrosis at the interatrial septum as a barrier, which resulted in the flutter circuit rolling down clockwise along RPVs and passing through the interatrial septum to the RA.

The coexistence of RIPV-related atypical AFL and inducible CTI-dependent typical flutter suggests that HCM can support multiple organized reentrant arrhythmias in both atria. This case therefore represents a rare atypical flutter pattern in HCM with 2 distinct macroreentrant circuits. These findings have important implications for ablation strategy, supporting a comprehensive approach that includes ablation of the RPV critical isthmus, pulmonary vein isolation for AF, and CTI ablation when typical flutter is inducible.

Conclusion

HCM may be associated with advanced atrial remodeling and fibrosis, providing the substrate for complex macroreentrant atrial tachycardias. In this case, atrial fibrosis on LGE-CMR correlated with low-voltage areas and fractionated EGMs on high-density 3D electroanatomic mapping, supporting a substrate-based mechanism of AFL. Integration of LGE-CMR and electroanatomic maps may help define the arrhythmogenic substrate and guide mechanism-based ablation in complex AFL in HCM.

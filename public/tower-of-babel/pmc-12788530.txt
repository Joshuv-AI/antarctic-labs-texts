Development and Comparative Analysis of an AI Based Convolutional Neural Network Algorithm versus Dentists of Varying Experience in Detecting Dental Caries using Bitewing X-ray Images: An In vitro Study
Authors: Alwaleed Khalid Albadr
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Dental caries, being an infectious, microbiologic disease, can cause dissolution and destruction of calcified tissues. Early detection of dental caries is therefore crucial for maintaining overall tooth health.Materials and Methods:A randomized, single-blinded retrospective study was conducted. Three groups participated: dentists with <10 years of experience, dentists with more than 10 years of experience, and the AI model. An external dataset of 20 anonymized bitewing radiographs served as the test set, reviewed and annotated by experienced dentists as the gold standard. Performance metrics included Intersection over Union, Confusion Matrix, Sensitivity, Precision, and F1 score.Results:Among all, the AI model showed a high precision of 0.87, a recall of 0.77, and an F1 score of 0.82. This shows the AI model’s superior precision in identifying caries and avoiding misinterpretations, like cervical burnout and Mach band effect.Conclusion:The study achieved high diagnostic accuracy in identifying early carious lesions and offered advantages in efficiency and avoiding misinterpretations. Integrating AI with dental expertise could enhance diagnostic accuracy, optimize workflow, and support preventive interventions, ultimately improving patient care. Future research should focus on expanding the model’s capabilities and exploring its seamless integration into clinical practice.

INTRODUCTION

Dental Caries, commonly known as “tooth decay,” is primarily caused by bacterium “Streptococcus mutans”. This chronic opportunistic infection, if not identified timely would cause significant tooth structure loss.[12] Presently, detection of carious lesions depends on the dentist’s “visual-tactile inspection” and dental radiographs. The International Caries Detection and Assessment system is the most commonly used visual classification system currently, having grades from 0 to 6, where 0 is sound tooth structure and 6 is extensive distinct cavity with visible dentin.[3] With the advent of Intraoral periapical radiographs and Bitewing radiographs, early detection of initial dental caries has now become quite easy. Compared to intraoral periapical radiograph technique, bitewing radiographic technique (unique application of an 8–10 degrees vertical angle to the X-ray beam) is considered as a gold standard in the detection of initial dental caries.[4] Even though the combination of bitewing radiographic technique and visual-tactile inspection technique is considered a reliable diagnostic tool, they still possess many challenges because of their subjectivity.[5]

In dentistry, the technological revolution is not just confined to current dental practices but also has redefined patient care through the power of data analysis and machine learning algorithms.[6] Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), a type of deep learning, simulate human brain functionalities through mathematical modelling. In Deep CNNs, information processing starts with local features identified by convolutional kernels. As the network progresses through layers, these kernels analyze features over larger areas. Down sampling and pooling layers then reduce the image resolution, so that the model can focus on the most salient features of the input image, ignoring minor spatial variations. Fully connected layers at the end refine the extracted features from this processed image.[7] Deep learning offers a diverse range of architectures, namely VGG-16, YOLO (You Only Look Once), U-Net, Mask R-CNN (Mask Region-CNN), FCN (Fully Convolutional Network), each with its strengths and applications.[8]

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This retrospective study evaluated the development and performance of “automatic caries detection and segmentation model”. Bitewing radiographs of carious lesions were used as a dataset. YOLOv8- You Only Look Once architecture and Python, an open-source programming language (version 3.6.1; Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE, USA) were employed for model creation. The study received approval from the Clinical Research Ethics Board (IRB) at Riyadh Elm University (IRB No. FPGRP/2023/779/1053/954), and it adhered to the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration.

Data collection

8000 anonymized bitewing radiographs were obtained from the radiology department of Dento-Maxillofacial Radiology at Riyadh Elm University. Radiographs were taken using Kodak CS 2200 (Carestream Dental, Atlanta, GA) X-Ray machine. Participant anonymity and data confidentiality were ensured by de-identifying radiographs and ensuring that data was stored securely by encrypting at rest. Only those patients’ radiographs were considered who were above 18 years. Good quality Radiographs with no artifacts were selected for the precise detection of carious lesions.

AI model development

A multidisciplinary team of 13 experts participated in the AI model development process: 2 experienced doctors (General Practitioners), 2 Radiologist, 4 Restorative Dentists, and 5 Computer Engineers.

Segmentation model

All 8000 anonymized dental bitewing radiographs were resized to a standard size of 512 × 512 to preserve image details. Contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization was applied to improve the visibility of carious lesions in all images. Data augmentation of the images was done using horizontal and vertical flips. 8000 radiographs were then divided into 3 datasets: training, validation, and test. Training: 6400 Radiographs 19117 Label, Validation: 800 Radiographs 2357 Label, Test: 800 Radiographs 2886 Label.

An oral and maxillofacial radiology expert with 15 years of experience and a restorative dentistry specialist with 10 years of experience collaborated to provide ground truth annotations to the dental bitewing images (Ground truth annotations are the process of providing labeled data to machine learning models, ensuring they learn/get trained accurately). ThakaaMed Labeling Tool (SAIF.V1) was used for annotation purposes.

Training the convolutional neural network model

The model was trained and optimized using the training and validation datasets. External validation was conducted with restorative dentistry consultants. The training method was applied using the computer equipment of ThakaaMed medical AI company. The training was performed on a high-performance computing cluster with the following hardware specifications: GPUs: 2 × NVIDIA A5000 GPUs | CPU: AMD Ryzen 16-Core CPU | RAM: 64 GB. Model hyperparameters were established based on factors such as the data volume and nature, number of classes, diversity within classes, and overall success rate. Augmentation hyperparameters were calibrated in accordance with the dataset size for caries segmentation in bitewing radiographs.

Performance assessment

The AI model’s performance was compared to the dentist groups using several metrics, namely IoU, Confusion Matrix, True Positives, False Positives, False Negatives, Sensitivity (Recall), Precision, and F1 Score.

RESULTS

The AI models based on the Deep-CNN architecture showed promising results for caries detection and segmentation in dental bitewing radiographs. The sensitivity, precision, and F-measure values were 0.77, 0.87, and 0.82, respectively, for caries detection. DISCUSSION

When the AI model and each of the 10 participating dentists (5 with less than 10 years of experience and 5 with more than 10 years of experience) evaluated a set of 20 bitewing X-rays, the AI model demonstrated superior precision (0.87) compared to both General Practitioners (mean precision: 0.61) and specialists (mean precision: 0.71). This was in accordance with the study done by Cantu et al.[9]

In comparison with AI model false positive and negative, the General Practitioners and Specialists tended to misinterpret cervical burnout and Mach band effect as carious lesions, significantly contributing to their false positive rates [Graph 2]. This specific diagnostic error reflects a common challenge in dental radiography, where the shadowing effects of cervical burnout can mimic the appearance of caries, leading to overdiagnosis. The findings of Szegedy et al. (2016), who demonstrated a CNN model outperforming general dentists in caries detection sensitivity, support this finding.[8]

By integrating AI models, dental professionals can access a reliable, data-driven perspective, enhancing the accuracy of their diagnosis and the effectiveness of their treatment plans. The collaboration between human expertise and artificial intelligence represents a synergistic approach, combining the nuanced judgment of experienced clinicians with the precision of AI, ultimately aiming to elevate the standard of care provided to patients.[9]

Dentists should explain the role of AI as a diagnostic tool that aids in their professional judgment, not replaces it. Transparency regarding the limitations of AI and the dentist’s ultimate responsibility for diagnosis and treatment planning is essential.[10]

CONCLUSION

The study demonstrates the transformative potential of AI in dental caries detection. The developed CNN model consistently outperformed both General Practitioners and Specialists, achieving a high precision of 0.87 and an F1 score of 0.82, indicating its exceptional accuracy in identifying caries lesions while minimizing false positives.

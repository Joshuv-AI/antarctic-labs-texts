Nominal Equational Rewriting and Narrowing
Mauricio Ayala-Rincón, Maribel Fernández, Daniele Nantes-Sobrinho, Daniella Santaguida

Abstract
Narrowing is a well-known technique that adds to term rewriting mechanisms the required power to search for solutions to equational problems. Rewriting and narrowing are well-studied in first-order term languages, but several problems remain to be investigated when dealing with languages with binders using nominal techniques. Applications in programming languages and theorem proving require reasoning modulo α \alpha -equivalence considering structural congruences generated by equational axioms, such as commutativity. This paper presents the first definitions of nominal rewriting and narrowing modulo an equational theory. We establish a property called nominal 𝖤 \mathsf{E} -coherence and demonstrate its role in identifying normal forms of nominal terms. Additionally, we prove the nominal 𝖤 \mathsf{E} -Lifting theorem, which ensures the correspondence between sequences of nominal equational rewriting steps and narrowing, crucial for developing a correct algorithm for nominal equational unification via nominal equational narrowing. We illustrate our results using the equational theory for commutativity.

The nominal framework

It is well-known that unification is fundamental for automated reasoning, serving as the foundation for resolution-based proof assistants, type inference, and numerous other applications. 3
Nominal
\mathsf{E}
-rewriting and
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing.

In this section, we introduce our novel definitions of
equational nominal rewriting systems
(ENRS) and
nominal equational narrowing
, sometimes abbreviated to nominal
\mathsf{E}
-rewriting systems and nominal
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing.

3.1
Nominal
\mathsf{E}
-rewriting

An
equational nominal rewrite system
(ENRS) is a set of (nominal) identities
\mathsf{T}
that can be split into a set
\mathsf{R}
of nominal rewrite rules and a set
\mathsf{E}
of identities. Sometimes, we will denote this decomposition as

\mathsf{R}{\cup}\mathsf{E}
.

Definition 3.1

(Nominal
\mathsf{R/E}
-rewriting)
.

Let
\mathsf{T}=\mathsf{R}{\cup}\mathsf{E}
be an ENRS. The relation
\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}
is induced by the composition
\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}\circ\to_{\mathsf{R}}\circ\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}
. A nominal term-in-context
\Delta\vdash s
reduces with
\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}
, when a term in its
\mathsf{E}
-equivalence class reduces via
\to_{\mathsf{R}}
as below:

\Delta\vdash(s\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}t)
iff there exist
s^{\prime},t^{\prime}
such that
\Delta\vdash(s\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}s^{\prime}\to_{\mathsf{R}}t^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}t)
.

If
\Delta\vdash s\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}^{*}t
and
\Delta\vdash s\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}^{*}u
, then we say that
\mathsf{R}
is
\mathsf{E}
-confluent
when there exist terms
t^{\prime},u^{\prime}
such that
\Delta\vdash t\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}^{*}t^{\prime}
,
\Delta\vdash u\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}^{*}u^{\prime}
and
\Delta\vdash t^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}u^{\prime}
.
Also,
\mathsf{R}
is said to be
\mathsf{E}
-terminating
if there is no infinite
\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}
sequence.

\mathsf{R}
is called
\mathsf{E}
-convergent
if it is
\mathsf{E}
-confluent and
\mathsf{E}
-terminating.

The following example illustrates an ENRS for the set of identities that define the prenex normal form of a first-order formula. We consider the commutativity of the connectives
\wedge
and
\vee
.

Example 3.1

(Prenex normal form rules)
.

Consider the signature for the first-order logic
\Sigma=\{\forall,\exists,\lnot,\land,\linebreak\lor\}
, let
\mathsf{C}=\{\;\vdash P\lor Q\approx Q\lor P,\;\vdash P\land Q\approx Q\land P\}
be the commutative theory. The prenex normal form rules can be specified by the following set
\mathsf{R}
of nominal rewrite rules:

\begin{array}[]{rl}a\#P&\vdash\;P\land\forall[a]Q\rightarrow\forall[a](P\land Q)\\
a\#P&\vdash\;P\lor\forall[a]Q\rightarrow\forall[a](P\lor Q)\\
a\#P&\vdash\;P\land\exists[a]Q\rightarrow\exists[a](P\land Q)\\
a\#P&\vdash\;P\lor\exists[a]Q\rightarrow\exists[a](P\lor Q)\\
&\vdash\;\lnot(\exists[a]Q)\rightarrow\forall[a]\lnot Q\\
&\vdash\;\lnot(\forall[a]Q)\rightarrow\exists[a]\lnot Q\\
\end{array}

Note that in Definition
3.1
, the relation
\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}
deals with
\alpha,\mathsf{E}
-congruence classes and they are always infinite due to the availability of names for
\alpha
-renaming. Although the pure
\approx_{\alpha}
relation is decidable,
when
\approx_{\alpha}
is put together with an equational theory
\mathsf{E}
which contains infinite congruence classes, the relation
\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}
may not be decidable (as in standard first-order rewriting modulo
\mathsf{E}
). We will define the nominal relation
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
that deals with nominal
\mathsf{E}
-matching instead of inspecting the whole
\alpha,\mathsf{E}
-congruence class of a term.

Definition 3.2

(Nominal
\mathsf{R,E}
-rewriting)
.

The
one-step
\mathsf{E}
-rewrite relation

\Delta\vdash s\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}t
is the least relation such that for any
R=(\nabla\vdash l\rightarrow r)\in\mathsf{R}
, position
\mathbb{C}
, term
s^{\prime}
, permutation
\pi
, and substitution
\theta
,

s\equiv\mathbb{C}[s^{\prime}]

\Delta\vdash\big(\nabla\theta,\ s^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}\pi\cdot(l\theta),\ \mathbb{C}[\pi\cdot(r\theta)]\approx_{\alpha}t\big)

\Delta\vdash s\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}\ t

The
\mathsf{E}
-
rewrite relation

\Delta\vdash s\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}t
is
the least relation that includes
\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}
and is closed by reflexivity and transitivity of
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
, i.e., it satisfies:

1.

for all
\Delta,s,s^{\prime}
we have
\Delta\vdash s\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}s^{\prime}
if
\Delta\vdash s\approx_{\alpha}s^{\prime}
;

2.

for all
\Delta,s,t,u
we have that
\Delta\vdash s\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}t
and
\Delta\vdash t\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}u
implies
\Delta\vdash s\rightarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}u
.

If

\Delta\vdash s\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}t
and
\Delta\vdash s\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}u
, then we say that
\mathsf{R,E}
is
\mathsf{E}
-confluent
when there exist terms
t^{\prime},u^{\prime}
such that
\Delta\vdash t\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}t^{\prime}
,
\Delta\vdash u\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}u^{\prime}
and
\Delta\vdash t^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}u^{\prime}
.

A term
t
is said to be in
\mathsf{R,E}
-normal
form (
\mathsf{R/E}
-normal form) whenever one cannot apply another step of
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
(
\to_{\mathsf{R/E}}
).

Example 3.2

(Cont. Example
3.1
)
.

This example illustrates the one-step
\mathsf{C}
-rewrite:

a\#P^{\prime}\vdash S^{\prime}\lor(\exists[a]Q^{\prime}\lor P^{\prime})\to_{\mathsf{R,C}}S^{\prime}\lor(\exists[a](P^{\prime}\lor Q^{\prime}))

with the rule
a\#P\vdash P\lor\exists[a]Q\to\exists[a](P\lor Q)
.
In fact,

•

\Delta=\{a\#P^{\prime}\}
and
\nabla=\{a\#P\}
;

•

s=S^{\prime}\lor(\exists[a]Q^{\prime}\lor P^{\prime})\equiv\mathbb{C}[\exists[a]Q^{\prime}\lor P^{\prime}]\equiv\mathbb{C}[s^{\prime}]
;

If we fix
\pi=id
and
\theta=[P\mapsto P^{\prime},Q\mapsto Q^{\prime}]
we have:

•

\Delta=a\#P^{\prime}\vdash a\#P^{\prime}=(a\#P)[P\mapsto P^{\prime},Q\mapsto Q^{\prime}]=\nabla\theta
;

•

s^{\prime}=\exists[a]Q^{\prime}\lor P^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{C}}(P\lor\exists[a]Q)[P\mapsto P^{\prime},Q\mapsto Q^{\prime}]=l\theta=\pi\cdot(l\theta)

•

\mathbb{C}[\pi\cdot(r\theta)]=\mathbb{C}[r\theta]=\mathbb{C}[(\exists[a](P\lor Q))[P\mapsto P^{\prime},Q\mapsto Q^{\prime}]]=\mathbb{C}[\exists[a](P^{\prime}\lor Q^{\prime})]=S^{\prime}\lor(\exists[a](P^{\prime}\lor Q^{\prime}))\approx_{\alpha}t

Thus,
a\#P^{\prime}\vdash S^{\prime}\lor(\exists[a]Q^{\prime}\lor P^{\prime})\to_{\mathsf{R,C}}S^{\prime}\lor(\exists[a](P^{\prime}\lor Q^{\prime}))

Since
\lor
is a commutative symbol, we could reduce the initial term to three other possible terms because we have two occurrences of the disjunction. Thus, we can “permute” the subterms inside the rewriting modulo
\mathsf{C}

Remark 3.3
.

Following the approach by Jouannaud et al.
[

\mathsf{E}
-confluence is a consequence of relating

-coherent

\Delta\vdash t_{1}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}t_{2}

Definition 3.5
.

An equational theory
\mathsf{E}
is called
a first-order equational theory
iff
\mathsf{E}
is defined via a set of first-order axioms, i.e., identities of the form
\emptyset\vdash l=r
, where
l,r
are first-order terms. First-order terms do not contain atoms, abstractions and suspended permutations on variables.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathsf{E}

be a first-order theory,

\mathsf{R}
be a nominal rewrite system that is

\mathsf{E}
-terminating
and
\mathsf{R,E}
be
\mathsf{E}
-confluent.
Then the
\mathsf{R,E}
- and
\mathsf{R/E}
-normal forms of any term
t
are
\mathsf{E}
-equal iff
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
is
\mathsf{E}
-coherent.

In first-order rewriting, it is known that
\mathsf{R,E}
-reducibility is decidable if
\mathsf{E}
-matching is decidable. Following Jouannaud et al.
[
19
]
, the existence of a finite and complete
\mathsf{E}
-unification
algorithm is a sufficient condition for that decidability.
However, solving nominal
\mathsf{E}
-unification problems has the additional complication of dealing with
\alpha
-equality, which significantly impacts obtaining finite and complete sets of nominal
\mathsf{E}
-unifiers.

Remark 3.7
.

Nominal
\mathsf{C}
-unification is not finitary when one uses freshness constraints and substitutions for representing solutions
[
3
]
, but the type of problems that generate an infinite set of
\mathsf{C}
-unifiers are fixed-point equations
\pi\cdot X_{?}{\overset{\mathsf{C}}{\approx}}_{?}X
. For example, the nominal
\mathsf{C}
-unification problem
(a\ b)\cdot X_{?}{\overset{\mathsf{C}}{\approx}}_{?}X
has solutions
[X\mapsto a\oplus b],[X\mapsto(a\oplus b)\oplus(a\oplus b)],\ldots
(Example

). However, these problems do not appear in nominal
\mathsf{C}
-matching, which is finitary
[
4
]
. Thus, the relation
\to_{\mathsf{R,C}}
is decidable.

3.2
Nominal
\mathsf{E}

Now we define the nominal narrowing relation modulo
\mathsf{E}
, extending previous works
[
7
]
.

Definition 3.8

(Nominal
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing)
.

The
one-step
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing relation

(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}(\Delta^{\prime}\vdash t)
is the least relation such that for any
(\nabla\vdash l\rightarrow r)\in\mathsf{R}
, position
\mathbb{C}
, term
s^{\prime}
, permutation
\pi
, and substitution
\theta
,

s\equiv\mathbb{C}[s^{\prime}]

\Delta^{\prime}\vdash\big(\nabla\theta,\penalty\ \Delta\theta,\penalty\ s^{\prime}\theta\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}\pi\cdot(l\theta),\,(\mathbb{C}[\pi\cdot r])\theta\approx_{\alpha}t\big)

.

(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{\theta}(\Delta^{\prime}\vdash t)

where
(\Delta^{\prime},\theta)\in{\cal U}_{\mathsf{E}}(\nabla\vdash l,\Delta\vdash s^{\prime})
. We will write only
(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}(\Delta^{\prime}\vdash t)
, omitting the
\theta
, when it is clear in the context.

The
nominal
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing relation

(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}(\Delta^{\prime}\vdash t)
is
the least relation that includes
\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}
and is closed by reflexivity and transitivity of
\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}
, i.e., it satisfies:

1.

for all
\Delta,s,s^{\prime}
we have
(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}(\Delta\vdash s^{\prime})
if
\Delta\vdash s\approx_{\alpha}s^{\prime}
;

2.

for all
\Delta,\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime\prime},s,t
and
u
: if
(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}(\Delta^{\prime}\vdash t)
and
(\Delta^{\prime}\vdash t)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}(\Delta^{\prime\prime}\vdash u)
then
(\Delta\vdash s)\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}^{*}(\Delta^{\prime\prime}\vdash u)
.

The permutation
\pi
and substitution
\theta
in the definition above are found by solving the nominal
\mathsf{E}
-unification problem
(\nabla\vdash l)\;_{?}{\overset{\mathsf{E}}{\approx}}_{?}\;(\Delta\vdash s^{\prime})
.

Remark 3.9
.

Note that decidability of
\rightsquigarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}
relies on the existence of an algorithm for nominal
\mathsf{E}
-unification.

\rho_{n}
such that

(H3)

\Delta\vdash\Delta_{i}\rho_{i}
;

(H4)

\Delta\vdash s_{i}\rho_{i}=t_{i}^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}t_{i}
, for every
i
;

(H5)

\Delta\vdash\rho_{0}|_{V}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}\theta\rho_{n}|_{V}
.

Note that from
(H4)
,
\Delta\vdash s_{n}\rho_{n}=t_{n}^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}t_{n}
. Since
\mathsf{R}
is
\mathsf{E}
-convergent and
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
is
\mathsf{E}
-coherent, it follows from Theorem
3.6
, that all the normal forms of
t_{0}
are
\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}
-equivalent. That is,

\Delta\vdash t_{n}^{\prime}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}t_{1}\downarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}\approx_{\alpha,\mathsf{E}}t_{0}\downarrow_{\mathsf{R,E}}=t_{n}.

Therefore, there exists a nominal
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing sequence

(\Delta_{0}\vdash s_{0})\rightsquigarrow^{\theta_{0}}_{\mathsf{R,E}}(\Delta_{1}\vdash s_{1})\rightsquigarrow^{\theta_{1}}_{\mathsf{R,E}}\ldots\rightsquigarrow^{\theta_{n-1}}_{\mathsf{R,E}}(\Delta_{n}\vdash s_{n}).

∎

As a consequence of Lemmas
4.3
and
4.5
we obtain:

Theorem 4.6

(
\mathsf{E}
-Lifting Theorem)
.

Let
\mathsf{R}{\cup}\mathsf{E}
be an ENRS such that
\mathsf{E}
is compatible with
\vdash
by substitutions,
\mathsf{R}
is
\mathsf{E}
-terminating,
\mathsf{R,E}
is
\mathsf{E}
-confluent and
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
is
\mathsf{E}
-coherent.
To each finite sequence of nominal
\mathsf{E}
-rewriting steps corresponds a finite sequence of nominal
\mathsf{E}
-narrowing steps, and vice versa.

Since there exists an algorithm for nominal
\mathsf{C}
-unification and
\mathsf{C}
is compatible with substitutions (Proposition
2.4
), we have the following result.

Corollary 4.7
.

The
\mathsf{C}
-Nominal Lifting theorem holds.

5
Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, we proposed definitions for nominal
\mathsf{R,E}
-rewriting and
\mathsf{R,E}
-narrowing and proved some properties relating them, obtaining the proof of the
\mathsf{E}
-Lifting Theorem, in the case
\mathsf{R}
is an
\mathsf{E}
-convergent NRS,
\to_{\mathsf{R,E}}
is
\mathsf{E}
-coherent and a complete algorithm for nominal
\mathsf{E}
-unification exists. As
\mathsf{C}
is the only equational theory for which a complete algorithm for nominal unification exists, we illustrate our results using this theory. Also, since the nominal
\mathsf{C}
-unification problem (when using freshness constraints) only is finitary, our nominal
\mathsf{C}
-narrowing tree is infinitely branching. In future work, we plan to investigate alternative approaches to nominal
\mathsf{C}
-unification for which the representation of solutions is finite, such as the approach using fixed-point constraints.

Impact of the standardised patient teaching method on medical students’ clinical reasoning competence: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Yizhu Wang, Shucheng Hu, Qi Huang, Han Yang, Yi Zhang, Bin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionClinical reasoning is an essential competency for all physicians, and fostering this skill in medical students is a primary objective of medical education. Currently, clinical reasoning is predominantly cultivated through experiences in clinical settings; however, this approach presents certain limitations. The emergence of innovative medical simulation teaching methods has led to the increasing utilisation of standardised patient (SP) simulations in medical education. However, the specific contribution of the SP teaching method to the development of clinical reasoning skills in medical students remains unclear. Therefore, we propose a systematic review to summarise the impact of the SP teaching method on the clinical reasoning competence of medical students.Methods and analysisThis systematic review protocol adheres to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols guidelines for reporting results. A comprehensive literature search will be conducted across five major online databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Education Research Complete and the Cochrane Library. The screening process comprises an initial review of titles and abstracts to identify relevant studies efficiently, followed by a full-text assessment of the selected articles. Two reviewers will independently conduct study selection, data extraction and risk of bias assessment. Data extraction will be performed using a standardised form. The primary outcome is clinical reasoning competence, and secondary outcomes are communication skills and student satisfaction. The risk of bias in included studies will be evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool 2. Depending on the literature search results and the characteristics of the selected studies, a meta-analysis will be conducted where feasible. Otherwise, a narrative synthesis will be performed.Ethics and disseminationThis study presents a protocol for a systematic review and does not involve human subjects. Introduction

Clinical reasoning (CR) is a fundamental competency for all physicians and is crucial for ensuring patient safety. CR encompasses the cognitive processes involved in making clinical judgments and relies on a systematic and integrated knowledge base of medicine, enriched by clinical experience. It is applied across various complex cognitive processes, including the systematic collection of disease information, the hierarchical construction of differential diagnoses and the dynamic adjustment of evidence-based decisions.1 Deficiencies in CR are associated with increased diagnostic error rates, diminished patient safety and reduced efficiency in healthcare resource utilisation.2 Therefore, cultivating CR skills in medical students is a crucial objective in medical education.3

The traditional approach to developing CR skills is through experiential learning in clinical environments. Although learners can benefit from real-life situations,4 the availability and variety of real patient cases are limited. Furthermore, clinical misdiagnosis can lead to detrimental outcomes for both students and patients.5 To address the limitations inherent in traditional teaching models, medical simulation teaching methods replicate clinical scenarios, providing learners with an active, immersive, experiential and safe learning environment in which to develop and refine both technical and non-technical skills.6 Standardised patients (SPs) simulation teaching method, widely employed in medical education, represents a specific form of medical simulation.

SPs are individuals trained to portray real patients consistently and accurately. The utilisation of SPs to train medical students is known as the SP teaching method. This approach offers a highly realistic and interactive learning experience, enabling learners to practice history-taking, physical examination and communication skills within an authentic, patient-centred context.7 This feedback is essential for identifying areas for improvement and enhancing CR abilities.8

Although the SP teaching method is increasingly used in medical education, the existing literature has not reached a definitive conclusion regarding its impact on the CR skills of medical students, often addressing it only partially in articles.7 More literature involves the impact of the SP teaching method on the education of nursing students9 10 or the effects of other teaching methods (team-based learning,11 structured reflection, self-explanation,12 computer-based virtual patients13) on the CR skills of medical students. There remains a lack of robust evidence regarding the effectiveness of SP teaching in cultivating CR skills in medical students.

The objective of this systematic review is to rigorously explore and determine the effectiveness of the SP teaching method compared with non-SP teaching methods in fostering medical students’ CR competence, communication skills and student satisfaction. Prior to initiating this review, searches were conducted in Medline, Embase, the PROSPERO systematic review registry, the Joanna Briggs Institute Database of Systematic Reviews and Implementation Reports, the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews and the Database of Abstracts of Reviews of Effects, but no previous systematic reviews on this specific topic were identified.

Methods and analysis

This protocol has been developed following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) guidelines. The completed PRISMA-P checklist is provided as online supplemental material.

The development of this study will follow the PRISMA 2020 framework.14 The PRISMA framework provides a systematic and standardised methodology for conducting systematic reviews, ensuring transparency and completeness throughout the research process. This systematic review is scheduled to commence in October 2025 and conclude in January 2026.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted using databases including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Education Research Complete and CENTRAL (Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials). This search strategy aims to capture all relevant studies, ensuring the inclusion of pertinent literature for this systematic review. Studies will be included regardless of the language of publication. Non-English articles will be translated as necessary. Examples of search terms for various databases are provided in table 1.

Study selection

The selection of publications will follow a three-step process. First, titles and abstracts will be thoroughly screened against predefined PICOS criteria. At least two reviewers will independently screen the studies, with a third reviewer serving as an arbitrator in cases of disagreement. Second, publications that meet the criteria from the initial screening will advance to a full-text assessment. Finally, in the third step, the reference lists of the selected literature will be reviewed using the same inclusion criteria to identify any additional publications that may have been overlooked during the initial database search. The search results from different databases will be consolidated and uploaded into reference management software, with the Amsterdam Efficient Deduplication Method employed to remove initial duplicate records.

Data extraction

Data extraction and risk of bias assessment will be conducted by two independent reviewers. Each reviewer will use a standardised data extraction form to record the following information: title, authors, year of publication, study duration, sample size, participant grade level and institution, teaching method employed in the control group, and means, SD, standardised mean differences (SMDs) or other analytical results. Based on this information, each reviewer will determine whether the full manuscript meets the criteria for inclusion in the systematic review and/or meta-analysis. Disagreements will be resolved through discussion between the two reviewers to reach a consensus. If consensus cannot be achieved, a third project member will serve as the final arbiter. Inter-rater reliability will be assessed, with a kappa value of ≥0.80 deemed acceptable. In cases of poor inter-rater reliability, the selected articles will be reviewed by the final arbiter.

The process of phased screening and study selection will be detailed in the final manuscript and presented as a PRISMA flow diagram. A list of studies excluded based on full-text criteria, categorised by reason for exclusion, will be provided in the final results of the systematic review.

The risk of bias for randomised controlled trials will be assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool. The results of these assessments for each included study will be presented in tables and figures in the final manuscript. If the literature search results and the characteristics of the selected studies permit, a meta-analysis will be conducted. If a meta-analysis is not feasible, a narrative synthesis will be performed.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

This study aims to evaluate the impact of the SP teaching method on undergraduate medical students’ CR competence, communication skills and satisfaction. For the primary outcome of CR competence, which may be measured using different scales or tools (eg, CR tests, key feature problems, Objective Structured Clinical Examinations) across studies, the SMD with 95% CIs will be used as the summary effect size to pool the intervention effects. Heterogeneity will be quantitatively assessed using the Cochrane Q test and I² statistic. If I² <50% and p≥0.05, a fixed-effect model will be applied; if I² ≥50% or p<0.05, a random-effects model will be used. In cases of significant heterogeneity, forest plots will be generated to visually display individual study effect sizes, their CIs and the pooled effect size. Additionally, funnel plots will be employed to visually assess the potential impact of publication bias on heterogeneity. If a sufficient number of studies are included, meta-regression analysis will be conducted to quantitatively explore the association between continuous variables (eg, average duration of instruction, student year level) or categorical variables and the pooled effect size, thus identifying specific covariates contributing to heterogeneity. All statistical analyses will be performed using STATA V.15.0 and Review Manager V.5.4. If data are unavailable, a narrative synthesis of the results from the included studies will be provided. For the secondary outcomes (communication skills and student satisfaction), given the anticipated diversity in measurement instruments across the included studies, a meta-analysis will only be conducted if studies use comparable outcome measures and report sufficient data. If quantitative synthesis is not feasible due to clinical or methodological heterogeneity (eg, use of different scales), we will synthesise the data using a narrative synthesis approach. This will involve grouping studies by outcome type and summarising the direction, magnitude and consistency of the reported effects, without statistically pooling the results.

Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation

This study will use the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE) system to assess the quality of evidence for all outcomes. The quality of evidence will be evaluated across the domains of risk of bias, consistency, directness, precision and publication bias, and will ultimately be rated as high (indicating that further research is very unlikely to change our confidence in the effect estimate), moderate (indicating that further research is likely to significantly impact our confidence in the effect estimate and may alter the estimate), low (indicating that further research is very likely to have a substantial impact on our confidence in the effect estimate and is likely to change the estimate) or very low (indicating considerable uncertainty about the estimate).

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval was not required for this study, as it does not involve human or animal subjects and constitutes a protocol for a systematic review. Data from previously published studies will be described and analysed. Our protocol is registered with PROSPERO.

The development of this protocol is intended to ensure that our results are reported according to the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. The review, implementation and reporting of the study findings will also adhere to these guidelines. On completion of data collection and analysis, a full manuscript will be submitted for publication in a peer-reviewed journal. By systematically summarising the effectiveness of the SP teaching method on medical students’ CR competence, communication skills, empathy, satisfaction, self-confidence and anxiety, this review aims to provide robust evidence supporting the use of the SP teaching method in medical education.

1.
Introduction

In the representation theory of vertex operator algebras, the Zhu algebra
[
Zhu96
]
and its related bimodule theory
[
FZ92
]
have played an important role. There are deep connections between the representation of a vertex operator algebra and the corresponding associative Zhu algebra. For example, there is a correspondence between the modules for a vertex operator algebra and the modules for the corresponding Zhu algebra, and one can use bimodules over the Zhu algebra to compute fusion rules of a vertex operator algebra, i.e., the so-called fusion rules theorem.
There are many generalizations of the Zhu algebra
A(V)
and its bimodule
A(M)
theory. Dong, Li, and Mason generalized Zhu algebra theory in two directions. In one direction, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, they defined a series of associative algebras
A_{n}(V)

[
DLM98c
]
with
A_{0}(V)
being identical to
A(V)
. They proved that a vertex operator algebra
V
is rational if and only if
A_{n}(V)
is semisimple for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In the other direction, for an automorphism
g
of
V
with finite order
T
, they defined a twisted Zhu algebra
A_{g}(V)
to study the twisted representation theory of
V

[
DLM98a
]
. They also defined higher twisted Zhu algebras
A_{g,n}(V)
for
n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
later in
[
DLM98b
]
, and proved that
V
is
g
-rational if only if
A_{g,n}(V)
are semisimple for all
n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
. As for bimodule theory, Dong and Ren defined a series of
A_{n}(V)
-bimodules
A_{n}(M)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and proved a similar fusion rules theorem in terms of these higher Zhu algebras and their bimodules. A special case of twisted version of bimodule theory was studied in
[
JJ16
]
, where for an untwisted module
M
and a finitely ordered automorphism
g
of
V
, a series of
A_{g,n}(V)
-bimodules
A_{g,n}(M)
for
n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
was constructed. A more general case was partially studied in
[
Zhu23
]
, where for a quadruple
(M,g_{1},g_{2},g_{3})
, an
A_{g_{3}}(V)
-
A_{g_{2}}(V)
-bimodule
A_{g_{3},g_{2}}(M)
was constructed. Here,
M^{1}
is a
g_{1}
-twisted module,
g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}
are three finitely ordered, mutually commuting automorphisms of
V
satisfying
g_{3}=g_{1}g_{2}
.

A vertex operator module map

, or more generally, an intertwining operator

among

and

, involves infinitely many components, i.e., the coefficients of

or

for

. The results mentioned above were built upon the set of all weight-zero components. There are many more components with nonzero weight. Instead of just focusing on weight-zero components, considering all of the components should give us more information about the representations of
V
. In
[
DJ08b
]
, Dong and Jiang developed a more general bimodule theory. For

, they constructed an

-

-bimodule

associated to

. Roughly speaking,

was built upon the set of components with weight

generated by the vertex operator module map

. When

, the algebra

was recovered. Moreover, by putting components with all possible weights together, they gave another construction of the generalized Verma type module

generated by an

-module

, which was initially constructed in
[

]
. Later, in
[

]
, the authors generalized the

for an untwisted module

[

]
by considering components of intertwining operators

.

The first goal of this paper is to develop a more general bimodule theory over twisted Zhu algebras. For each quadruple

, where

is a

-

-bimodule

, any admissible

-twisted module has a grading given by

-spectrum. We show that the associative algebra

One key step in constructing the Zhu algebra bimodule theory is determining the left and right actions. Unlike

The second goal of this paper is to give a twisted Frenkel-Zhu-Li’s fusion rules theorem. The fusion rules theorem in the untwisted case was proposed in
[
FZ92
]
without a proof. The theorem states that if
M^{i},i=1,2,3
are three
V
-modules,
\mathcal{I}\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}M^{2}}
is the space of all intertwining operators among them, then

\mathcal{I}\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}M^{2}}\cong{\rm Hom}_{A(V)}\left(A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}M^{2}(0),M^{3}(0)\right).

Li gave the first proof in
[
Li99
]
, and he pointed out that for this theorem to be true, some conditions must be satisfied. Li then developed the regular representation theory for vertex operator algebras in
[
Li02
]
and gave a more conceptual proof of the fusion rules theorem in
[
Li01
]
. Another proof was given in
[
Liu23
]
fulfilling Frenkel-Zhu’s argument. In the twisted case, the corresponding twisted fusion rules theorem is still open. We do this by first constructing the tensor product for two twisted modules in terms of the bimodule theory established in this paper. In the category of untwisted modules, Huang and Lepowsky developed a tensor product theory for vertex operator algebras in
[
HL95a
,
HL95b
,
HL95c
,
Hua95
]
. They proved that the module category for a vertex operator algebra
V
is a modular tensor category under some convergence conditions of
V
. Li gave a construction of the tensor product for two untwisted
V
-modules in
[
Li98
]
. In his construction, Li used the representation of the associated Lie algebra
g(V)
of
V
. Li showed that the bottom level of the tensor product
M^{1}\boxtimes M^{2}
he constructed is
A(M^{1})\otimes_{A(V)}M^{2}(0)
. Using this result together with the universal property of the tensor product, one can immediately prove the fusion rules theorem. This inspires our bimodule approach in this paper. Suppose
g_{1},g_{2}
are two commuting automorphisms of
V
with finite order,
M^{1}
is an irreducible
g_{1}
-twisted module, and
M^{2}
is an irreducible
g_{2}
-twisted module. Let
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})
be the submodule generated by the
m
-th component of
\mathcal{M}\left(M^{1},M^{2}(m)\right)
. We prove that there is a natural twisted intertwining operator
F(-,z)
among
M^{1},M^{2}
and
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})

and the pair
\left(T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2}),F(-,z)\right)
satisfies the universal property in the definition of the tensor product of two twisted modules. It is well-known that a vertex operator algebra has features of both Lie algebras and associative algebras. Li’s approach is Lie-theoretical, while the approach in this paper is associative-theoretical. Using our construction of the tensor product of two twisted modules, we give a twisted version of the fusion rules theorem, namely, for any
g_{3}
-twisted module
M^{3}
, we have

(1.1)

\mathcal{I}\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}M^{2}}\cong{\rm Hom}_{A_{g_{3},m}(V)}\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{3},g_{2},m,m}(M^{1})\otimes_{A_{g_{2},m}(V)}M^{2}(m),M^{3}(m)\right),

see Theorem
5.9
.

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2

\displaystyle=(h_{3}-h_{1}-h_{2})z^{h_{3}-h_{1}-h_{2}-1}A^{\circ}(v,z)v_{2}+z^{h_{3}-h_{1}-h_{2}}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}{z}}A^{\circ}(v,z)v_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}{z}}A(v,z)v_{2}.

∎

Recall that we have
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})=\oplus_{i\in\Lambda}T_{m}^{\lambda_{i}}(M^{1},M^{2})
. Let
P_{\lambda_{i}}
be the projection from
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})
to
T_{m}^{\lambda_{i}}(M^{1},M^{2})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For
v\in M^{1}
, define

(5.30)

F(v,z)=\sum_{i\in\Lambda}z^{\lambda_{i}-h_{1}-h_{2}}P_{\lambda_{i}}\circ P\circ F^{\circ}(v,z).

Then we have

(5.31)

F(L_{(-1)}v,z)=\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}{z}}F(v,z).

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
5.4
immediately.
∎

To summarize, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
M^{i}
be an irreducible
g_{i}
-twisted module with conformal weight
h_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then
F(-,z)
is a twisted intertwining operator in
\mathcal{I}\binom{T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})}{M^{1}M^{2}}
.

We are now in a position to give our second main result.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
V
be a strongly rational vertex operator algebra,
g_{1},g_{2}
are two commuting automorphisms of
V
satisfying
g_{i}^{T}=1
for
i=1,2
. Let
M^{i}
be an irreducible
g_{i}
-twisted module with conformal weight
h_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
g_{3}=g_{1}g_{2}
. Then for any
m\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
, the pair
\left(T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2}),F(-,z)\right)
is a tensor product of
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
.

Proof.

Since
V
is
g_{3}
-rational, it suffices to show that for any irreducible
g_{3}
-twisted module
W
and an intertwining operator
i\in\mathcal{I}\binom{W}{M^{1}M^{2}}
, there exists a unique
g_{3}
-twisted module homomorphism
f
from
\mathcal{M}\left(M^{1},M^{2}(m)\right)
to
W
such that
I=f\circ F
. Since
W
is irreducible,
W=\oplus_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}}W_{\lambda+n}=\oplus_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}}W(n)
. For homogeneous
v\otimes u\in\mathcal{A}_{g_{3},g_{2},n,m}(M^{1})\otimes M^{2}(m)
, we define

f\colon\mathcal{M}\left(M^{1},M^{2}(m)\right)(n)\longrightarrow W

\hskip 85.35826ptv\otimes u\mapsto o^{I}_{n,m}(v)u,

and then linearly extend it to the whole
\mathcal{M}\left(M^{1},M^{2}(m)\right)
.
It is straightforward to see that
f
is well-defined, and for any homogeneous
a\in V^{(j_{1},j_{2})}
,
p\in\frac{j_{1}+j_{2}}{T}+\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle f\left(a_{(p)}(v\otimes u)\right)

\displaystyle=f\left((a\ast_{g_{3},m,n}^{n+{\rm wt}a-p-1}v)\otimes u\right)

\displaystyle=o^{I}_{n+{\rm wt}a-p-1,m}(a\ast_{g_{3},m,n}^{n+{\rm wt}a-p-1}v)u

\displaystyle=o^{W}_{n+{\rm wt}a-p-1,n}(a)o^{I}_{n,m}(v)u

\displaystyle=a_{(p)}f(v\otimes u).

Thus
f
is a homomorphism from
\mathcal{M}
to
W
. By definition,
f
preserves grading. Any lowest weight vector in
\mathcal{M}(n)
with
n\neq 0
is sent to 0 since there is no nonzero lowest weight vector in
W(n)
. Thus
f(\text{Rad}_{m}(\mathcal{M}))=0
. In addition,
f
preserves the
L_{(0)}
-spectrum. Recall that
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})=\oplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}T_{m}^{\lambda_{i}}(M^{1},M^{2})
. If we assume the conformal weight of
W
is
\lambda
, then there exists a
\lambda_{i_{0}}=\lambda
for some
i_{0}\in\Lambda
such that
f
on
\oplus_{i\in\Lambda,i\neq i_{0}}T_{m}^{\lambda_{i}}(M^{1},M^{2})
is the zero map. In conclusion, for any
v_{3}\in\mathcal{M}
, we have

(5.32)

f(v_{3})=f\circ P_{\lambda}\circ P(v_{3}).

Let
\tilde{f}
be the restriction of
f
on
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})
. Note that for any homogeneous
v_{2}\in M^{2}(p)
,
v_{2}=b\otimes u
for some
b\in A_{g_{2},p,m}(V)
and
u\in M^{2}(m)
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\tilde{f}\circ F(v,z)v_{2}=f\circ\sum_{i\in\Lambda}z^{\lambda_{i}-h_{1}-h_{2}}P_{\lambda_{i}}\circ P\circ F^{\circ}(v,z)v_{2}

\displaystyle=z^{\lambda-h_{1}-h_{2}}f\circ P_{\lambda}\circ P\circ F^{\circ}(v,z)v_{2}=z^{\lambda-h_{1}-h_{2}}\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}}z^{-n-1}f(v_{(n)}(b\otimes u))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}}z^{\lambda-h_{1}-h_{2}-n-1}f\left((v\underline{\ast}_{g_{2},m,p}^{p+\deg v-n-1}b)\otimes u\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}}z^{\lambda-h_{1}-h_{2}-n-1}o^{I}_{p+\deg v-n-1,m}(v\underline{\ast}_{g_{2},m,p}^{p+\deg v-n-1}b)u

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}}z^{\lambda-h_{1}-h_{2}-n-1}o^{I}_{p,p+\deg v-n-1}(v)o^{M^{2}}_{p,m}(b)u

\displaystyle=I(v,z)v_{2}.

Now suppose there is another homomorphism
f_{1}\colon T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})\rightarrow W
satisfying
f_{1}\circ F=I
. Then
\tilde{f}\circ F(v,z)v_{2}=f_{1}\circ F(v,z)v_{2}
for all
v\in M^{1},v_{2}\in M^{2}
. Since all the coefficients in
F(v,z)v_{2}
for
v\in M^{1}
and
v_{2}\in M^{2}
linearly span the whole
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})
,
\tilde{f}=f_{1}
.
∎

We have the following corollary:

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}
be two rational numbers in
\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
. Then

(5.33)

T_{m_{1}}(M^{1},M^{2})\cong T_{m_{2}}(M^{1},M^{2}).

As an application, we have the following twisted fusion rules theorem:

Theorem 5.9
.

Suppose
V
is strongly rational, and
g_{1},g_{2}
are two commuting automorphisms with finite order. Let
g_{3}=g_{1}g_{2}
, and
M^{i}
be an irreducible
g_{i}
-twisted
V
-module for
i=1,2,3
. Then for any
m\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
, as vector spaces, we have

(5.34)

\mathcal{I}\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}M^{2}}\cong{\rm Hom}_{A_{g_{3},m}(V)}\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{3},g_{2},m,m}(M^{1})\otimes_{A_{g_{2},m}(V)}M^{2}(m),M^{3}(m)\right).

Proof.

We have a linear isomorphism

(5.35)

\mathcal{I}\binom{M^{3}}{M^{1}M^{2}}\cong{\rm Hom}_{V}\left(T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2}),M^{3}\right)

by the definition of the tensor product of
M^{1}
and
M^{2}
. In addition, since
V
is
g_{3}
-rational,
T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2})
is the Verma type
g_{3}
-twisted module generated by its
m
-th component, and for any

,
we have

(5.36)

f\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{3},g_{2},m,m}(M^{1})\otimes_{A_{g_{2},m}(V)}M^{2}(m)\right)\subseteq M^{3}(m).

Hence

(5.37)

{\rm Hom}_{V}\left(T_{m}(M^{1},M^{2}),M^{3}\right)\cong{\rm Hom}_{A_{g_{3},m}(V)}\left(\mathcal{A}_{g_{3},g_{2},m,m}(M^{1})\otimes_{A_{g_{2},m}(V)}M^{2}(m),M^{3}(m)\right).

∎

In this section, we prove some identities used in previous sections. By
\overline{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we mean the residue of
n
modulo
T
for a fixed
T\in\mathbb{N}
. Also recall that
\tilde{x}=\overline{Tx}
for

Lemma 6.1
.

For any
i,r,x\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\delta_{i+x}(r+x)=\delta_{i}(r),

\displaystyle\delta_{i}(r-x)=\delta_{x}(r-i),

\displaystyle\lfloor r+x\rfloor=\lfloor r\rfloor+\lfloor x\rfloor+\delta_{\tilde{r}}(T-\tilde{x}).

Proof.

The proof is straightforward.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose
m,p,n,j_{1},j_{2},t
are a set of integers and assume that
j_{1},j_{2},t
lies between 0 and
T-1
. Suppose
\overline{\tilde{m}-\tilde{p}-j_{2}}=0
. Let
r=\overline{t+\tilde{p}-\tilde{n}-j_{1}}
and
j_{3}^{\vee}=\overline{-j_{1}-j_{2}}

(6.2)

\lambda_{t}(m,r)+n-\frac{t}{T}=\lambda(n,j_{3}^{\vee})+m.

Proof.

Since
\overline{\tilde{m}-\tilde{p}-j_{2}}=0
,

(6.3)

r=\overline{t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}-j_{1}-j_{2}}=\overline{t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee}}.

To show (
6.2
), it suffices to prove

(6.4)

\displaystyle\delta_{t+\tilde{m}}(\overline{t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee}})+\delta_{t+\tilde{m}-T}(\overline{t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee}})+\frac{\overline{t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee}}+\tilde{n}-t}{T}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\tilde{n}}(j_{3}^{\vee})+\frac{\tilde{m}+j_{3}^{\vee}}{T}.

Assume

(6.5)

\overline{t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee}}=sT+t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee},

where

can only take value in the set
\{-2,-1,0,1\}
. Note that, by Lemma
6.1
,

(6.6)

\delta_{t+\tilde{m}}(sT+t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee})=\delta_{\tilde{n}}(sT+j_{3}^{\vee}),

and

(6.7)

\delta_{t+\tilde{m}-T}(sT+t+\tilde{m}-\tilde{n}+j_{3}^{\vee})=\delta_{\tilde{n}}\left((s+1)T+j_{3}^{\vee}\right).

It remains to show that

(6.8)

\delta_{\tilde{n}}(sT+j_{3}^{\vee})+\delta_{\tilde{n}}\left((s+1)T+j_{3}^{\vee}\right)+s=\delta_{\tilde{n}}(j_{3}^{\vee}).

Note that
t,\tilde{m},\tilde{n},j_{3}^{\vee}
lie between
0
and
T
. So when
\tilde{n}\geq j_{3}^{\vee}
,
s
can only take value in the set
\{-1,0,1\}
; when
\tilde{n}<j_{3}^{\vee}
,
s
can only take value in the set
\{-2,-1,0\}
. Now (
6.8
) follows from a direct case-by-case check.
∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
m,n,j_{3}^{\vee},r,t
are a set of integers and assume that

lies between 0 and

. Suppose

\displaystyle=-1+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{t+\tilde{m}}(r)+\delta_{t+\tilde{m}-T}(r)+\frac{r+\tilde{n}-\tilde{m}-t}{T}

\displaystyle=-1+\lfloor n\rfloor+\delta_{\tilde{n}}(sT+j_{3}^{\vee})+\delta_{\tilde{n}}\left((s+1)T+j_{3}^{\vee}\right)+\frac{sT+j_{3}^{\vee}}{T}.

and

,
s
can only take value in the set
\{-1,0,1\}

\tilde{n}<j_{3}^{\vee}
,
s
can only take value in the set
\{-2,-1,0\}

6.9

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose
z\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{Z}
,

,
q_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{j_{1}}{T}

(6.10)

\lambda(z+x,j_{3}^{\vee})-x+q_{1}=\lfloor z+q_{1}\rfloor.

By Lemma

\lfloor z+q_{1}\rfloor=\lfloor z+x+q_{1}-x\rfloor=\lfloor z+x\rfloor+\lfloor q_{1}-x\rfloor+\delta_{\widetilde{z+x}}(T-\overline{j_{1}+j_{2}}).

\displaystyle\lambda(z+x,j_{3}^{\vee})-x+q_{1}

\displaystyle=-1+\lfloor z+x\rfloor+\delta_{\widetilde{z+x}}(j_{3}^{\vee})+\frac{j_{3}^{\vee}}{T}+q_{1}-x

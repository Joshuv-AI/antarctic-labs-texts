Stochastic Clocks in ADM-Based Canonical Gravity
Pradosh Keshav MV

Abstract
The problem of time in canonical quantum gravity remains one of the most significant challenges, primarily due to the “frozen” formalism emerging from the Wheeler–DeWitt equation. Within the ADM formalism, we introduce a novel approach in which a scalar field is treated as a stochastic clock. By imposing a divergence-free condition on the scalar momentum, we integrate out quantum gravitational fluctuations and derive an effective noise term via the Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation. This noise drives dynamic adjustments in spacetime foliations, enabling a Schrödinger-like evolution that preserves diffeomorphism invariance and, upon noise averaging, maintains unitary evolution. Interestingly, by introducing stochastic variations in the foliations, the quantum indeterminacy of the clock recasts time as a diffusive process emerging from quantum fluctuations–where correlations between matter and geometry replace an absolute time parameter. This is considerably a potential pathway for understanding quantum time evolution while maintaining background independence in canonical quantum gravity.

2
Why a Stochastic Clock?

Existing approaches to canonical gravity—including internal time variables, relational observables, and semiclassical approximations—struggle to resolve the tension between background independence and unitary evolution due to inherent limitations in their treatment of time. Internal clocks, such as scalar fields, suffer from foliation dependence, where the evolution they define is contingent on specific gauge choices, thereby breaking full diffeomorphism invariance. Relational frameworks, while avoiding absolute time parameters, face difficulties in defining quantum clocks with well-behaved conditional probabilities, as the clocks themselves are subject to quantum fluctuations. Semiclassical methods, though effective in certain limits, neglect essential gravitational backreaction effects, which are crucial for a self-consistent quantum gravitational framework.

A common thread among these limitations is the assumption that time remains a classical parameter, whether explicitly imposed or emergently reconstructed. However, in a fundamentally quantum spacetime, fluctuations in geometry and matter are inextricably linked, and any physically meaningful notion of time must reflect this interdependence. A shift in perspective is therefore necessary—one that does not merely tolerate the clock’s quantum variability but incorporates it as a fundamental driver of time evolution. This motivates the introduction of a stochastic clock, where the scalar field is no longer a fixed relational marker but a dynamical variable whose quantum fluctuations perturb the foliation of spacetime itself.

A stochastic clock can be thought of as a ‘quantum pendulum’ whose ticks vary due to quantum fluctuations. Unlike classical time parameters, which progress deterministically, a stochastic clock allows for small, random variations in its progression. This variability emerges naturally in quantum gravity because the evolution of a clock field is affected by gravitational fluctuations. Instead of treating time as a fixed parameter, we model it as a fluctuating variable, leading to an ensemble of possible time evolutions. This perspective aligns with the Page-Wootters formalism (which conditions evolution on a clock variable) but extends it by incorporating noise effects that arise from quantum gravitational corrections–that is, we reinterpret the frozen formalism to describe an ensemble of fluctuating spacetime histories rather than a single rigid evolution.

Stochastic clocks, as we intend to discuss in the paper, do not depend on a preferred foliation or rely on ad hoc decoherence mechanisms to recover classical evolution. Instead, the constraint that keeps the clock’s momentum divergence-free ensures that stochastic fluctuations adhere to the hypersurface deformation algebra, thereby preserving full diffeomorphism invariance (see Fig.
1
for an illustrative comparison with the deterministic case). This approach addresses two significant challenges in canonical quantum gravity. First, it resolves the operational deficiency of previous approaches: in quantum spacetime, a clock must inherently account for its uncertainty, as no classical variable can provide an absolute measure of evolution. Second, it alleviates the covariance paradox, where rigid foliations or gauge fixings, while mathematically convenient, impose constraints on diffeomorphism symmetry.

3
Canonical Clocks in Covariant Gravity

We consider a four-dimensional Lorentzian manifold
(M,g_{\mu\nu})
, foliated by a one-parameter family of spacelike hypersurfaces
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, where each
\Sigma_{t}
is diffeomorphic to a three-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
. The foliation is described by an embedding map
\iota:\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}\to M
, where
\iota_{t}\equiv\iota(\cdot,t):\Sigma\to\Sigma_{t}\subset M
defines the spatial topology
[
27
]
. To ensure compatibility with full spacetime covariance while maintaining a well-posed Cauchy problem, we introduce a dynamical slicing governed by a scalar field
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
, which functions as an internal relational time parameter. Specifically, the embedding
\iota_{t}
is refined to depend on
\phi
via

\iota_{t}:\Sigma\times\mathcal{Z}\to M,\quad\text{where }\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{R}\text{ parametrizes the foliation},

(1)

such that
\phi(\iota_{t}(x))=t
for all
x\in\Sigma
. This establishes a correspondence between the level sets of
\phi
and the hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
, thereby establishing a relational dependence on temporal evolution.

The presence of
\phi
induces a redundancy-free foliation by restricting the full diffeomorphism group
\text{Diff}(\Sigma)
to a subgroup
\mathcal{G}_{\phi}
that preserves
\phi
’s level sets. This leads to the identification

\Sigma\times\mathcal{Z}\simeq\text{Diff}(\Sigma)\times\mathcal{G}_{\phi},

(2)

where
\mathcal{G}_{\phi}
acts along the fibers of
M
. The existence of such a decomposition depends on the temporal rigidity of
\phi
, encoded by an action-functional constraint ensuring non-degenerate slicing.

Within the canonical formalism, the generalized coordinates
(X_{a},Y_{a})
on the phase space
\Gamma
represent conjugate variables characterizing the embedding
\iota_{t}
and the extrinsic curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
. Their fundamental Poisson structure is given by

\{X_{a}(x),Y_{b}(y)\}=\delta_{ab}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

(3)

where
a,b
index independent degrees of freedom. The scalar field
\phi
enters via a decomposition of the spacetime evolution vector
Z^{\mu}=\partial_{\tau}y^{\mu}
(where
\tau
is an affine parameter) into lapse and shift components:

Z^{\mu}=Nn^{\mu}+N^{i}e_{i}^{\mu},

(4)

where
N=Z^{\mu}n_{\mu}
and
N^{i}=Z^{\mu}e_{\mu}^{i}
. Since
\phi
determines
N
and
N^{i}
through its gradient
\partial_{\mu}\phi
, it establishes a direct relation with the ADM formalism.

A natural clock condition arises with the coupling
\phi
to the lapse function
N
via the clock Hamiltonian

P(n)=c\,n^{1+\Omega(\phi)},\quad\Omega(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\sqrt{\gamma}\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\,\partial^{\mu}\phi\,d^{3}x,

(5)

where
c
is a constant and
\Omega(\phi)
quantifies the rigidity of the foliation in a manner analogous to the energy density of a stiff matter field. This condition ensures that the clock does not introduce additional gauge degrees of freedom while maintaining diffeomorphism invariance. For instance, in a quantum theory, the state
\psi(c,\phi)
, interpreted as a functional over
\Gamma
, obeys the evolution equation

i\hbar\frac{\delta\psi}{\delta\phi}=\hat{H}\psi,\quad\hat{H}=\int_{\Sigma}\left(N\hat{\mathcal{H}}+N^{i}\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\right)d^{3}x,

(6)

where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
are the quantized Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. This structure maintains covariance in the quantum regime through the identification

ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dy^{\mu}dy^{\nu}\equiv Z^{\mu}\Sigma(\tau)\frac{d\phi}{dt},

(7)

where
\Sigma(\tau)
denotes the volume element of the hypersurface propagated along
Z^{\mu}
. Still, an open question remains regarding how clock condition could be incorporated into a full theory of quantum gravity.

We know that a direct ADM foliation of
\text{Diff}(\Sigma)
within a fully covariant quantum theory encounters obstructions due to the Hamiltonian constraint

e^{-i\hat{H}t}|\psi(\gamma_{ij},\phi)\rangle=0,

(8)

where
\gamma_{ij}
is the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}
. This condition is generically inconsistent unless the clock condition is dynamically enforced. To address this, one can impose aprioi between the lapse function and the scalar field’s conjugate momentum:

N=\frac{\pi_{\phi}}{\sqrt{\gamma}(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi)},\quad\pi_{\phi}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta(\partial_{t}\phi)},

(9)

such that
\phi
functions as a well-defined relational time parameter. The ADM action then takes the form

S[\gamma_{ij},\phi,N,N^{i}]=\int_{M}\left[\pi^{ij}\partial_{t}\gamma_{ij}+\pi_{\phi}\partial_{t}\phi-N\mathcal{H}-N^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right]d^{3}x\,dt,

(10)

with the Hamiltonian constraint

\mathcal{H}=N\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\pi^{ij}\mathcal{G}_{ijkl}\pi^{kl}-\sqrt{\gamma}R+\mathcal{H}_{\phi}+\frac{m^{2}\omega_{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}}{2\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)+N^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i},

(11)

where
\mathcal{G}_{ijkl}
is the DeWitt supermetric,
\pi^{ij}
is the conjugate momentum to
\gamma_{ij}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}
accounts for the clock field’s kinetic and potential contributions with
m
being the conjugate momentum to the embedding variable
\sigma_{k}
, defined by the symplectic structure
\{\sigma_{k},m\}=1
, and
\omega_{H}
is a frequency parameter.

4
Constraints on Temporal Evolution

The Wheeler-DeWitt equation is widely used for studying the behaviour of clocks induced by a gravitational field coupled to a free scalar field
[
28
,
29
,
30
]
. However, the necessity of absorbing
N
and
N^{i}
within a consistent Cauchy evolution suggests an intrinsic obstruction to Dirac quantization, except in cases where
\text{Diff}(\Sigma)
decomposes in a way that recovers the Wheeler-DeWitt equation in static backgrounds.
In this section, we generalize the equation to a scalar field coupled to gravity, whose dynamics are governed by the Einstein-Klein-Gordon (EKG) action
[
31
]
.

For a gravitational field coupled to a free scalar field
\phi
, the combined EKG action in ADM variables can be written as

S_{g\phi}=\int_{M}\left[\pi^{ij}\partial_{t}\gamma_{ij}+\pi_{\phi}\partial_{t}\phi-N\mathcal{H}-N^{i}\mathcal{H}_{i}\right]d^{3}x\,dt,

(12)

where
\gamma_{ij}=g_{\mu\nu}e^{\mu}_{i}e^{\nu}_{j}
is the induced metric on the spacelike hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}
. The conjugate momenta satisfy

\pi^{ij}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta(\partial_{t}\gamma_{ij})},\quad\pi_{\phi}=\frac{\delta S}{\delta(\partial_{t}\phi)},

(13)

and the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints enforce diffeomorphism invariance:

\hat{\mathcal{H}}|\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi]\rangle=0,\quad\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{i}|\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi]\rangle=0.

(14)

To isolate the scalar field’s role as a clock, we reduce superspace to a minisuperspace model
[
32
]
by mapping:

\int_{\Sigma}\sigma_{k}\sqrt{\gamma}\,d^{3}x\xrightarrow{\text{Quantization}}\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi],

(15)

where
\sigma_{k}
denotes gravitational degrees of freedom. This is justified, at least in the loop quantum cosmology scenarios, when only a finite number of degrees of freedom remain relevant
[
33
]
. But enables a conditional probability interpretation, where
\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi]
describes the universe’s state when
\phi
takes a value
\phi_{0}
(which we will discuss in coming sections.) The scalar momentum
\pi_{\phi}
dynamically fixes the lapse function
N
through

N=\frac{\pi_{\phi}}{\sqrt{\gamma}(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi)},

(16)

which reduces the Hamiltonian constraint to a Schrödinger equation:

i\hbar\frac{\delta\Psi}{\delta\phi}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{grav}}\Psi,

(17)

with
\mathcal{H}_{\text{grav}}
being the Hamiltonian of the gravitational sector after parametrization. However, to retain a meaningful physical interpretation while maintaining covariance, a worldline action is introduced:

S[y^{\mu},\gamma_{ij},N]=\int d^{3}\Sigma\left[X^{ij}\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}+\frac{N}{2}\frac{m^{2}\omega_{{\mathcal{H}}}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right],

(18)

where
X^{ij}=\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma^{ij}
is the densitized metric, and
\tau
is an affine parameter along
\partial_{\mu}\phi
.

We assume that under the group
\mathcal{Z}(0)
, the transformations

\tau\to\tau+f(\phi),\quad N\to Ne^{n\psi(\phi)},\quad\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\to\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}+\partial_{\tau}f(\phi)

(19)

preserve relational observables despite arbitrary and gauge parameters in Eq. (11). For instance, suppose we consider a scalar field with simple Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi-\frac{1}{2}m_{0}^{2}\phi^{2},

(20)

the canonical coordinates on
\Sigma_{t}
are

X_{i}(\tau)=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\phi(x^{i},\tau),\quad Y_{i}(\tau)=\sqrt{\gamma}\,\pi_{\phi}(x^{i},\tau),

(21)

with Poisson brackets

\{\phi(x,\tau),\pi_{\phi}(y,\tau)\}=\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

(22)

which are replaced in quantization by the commutators:

[\hat{\phi}(x,\tau),\hat{\pi}_{\phi}(y,\tau)]=i\hbar\delta^{(3)}(x-y),\quad[\hat{\phi},\hat{\phi}]=[\hat{\pi}_{\phi},\hat{\pi}_{\phi}]=0.

(23)

However, despite the formal consistency of these equations, the role of
\phi
as a clock remains tied to the ADM foliation
\iota_{t}:\Sigma\to M
.

A fully covariant formulation requires that
\phi
foliates
M
independently without privileging
\Sigma_{t}
. This necessity conflicts with the Hamiltonian constraint, which aligns
\partial_{\mu}\phi
with the hypersurface normal
n^{\mu}
. A more general formulation requires an independent foliation driven by
\phi
rather than predetermined by
\Sigma_{t}

[
34
]
. This requires deparametrizing
\mathcal{H}\approx 0
via lapse fixing and generalizing
\phi
to satisfy the natural clock condition in eq. (5).

Physical Origin of Stochastic Foliation

A major challenge in defining time in quantum gravity is ensuring that evolution is independent of arbitrary foliation choices
[
35
]
. In a setup where quantum fluctuations introduce additional variability in the slicing of spacetime. If left unconstrained, these fluctuations could lead to inconsistencies in time evolution across different foliations
[
36
]
. To regulate this, we impose a divergence-free condition on the scalar momentum:

\nabla_{\mu}\left(\frac{\pi^{\mu}}{\sqrt{-g}}\right)=0,

(24)

where
\pi^{\mu}=\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)
satisfying the energy-momentum flow of the clock field, in a similar way to how relational evolution is dynamically imposed while preserving diffeomorphism invariance
[
37
,
14
]
. However, quantum fluctuations can introduce stochastic contributions to the foliation, leading to a generalization where
\phi
is no longer a classical parameter but a noise-driven dynamical variable
[
38
]
.

Hamiltonian constraint in diffeomorphism-invariant theories necessitates the identification of a clock relative to which the dynamics can be defined. Stochastic contributions to the Hamiltonian constraint may arise from coarse-graining quantum gravitational degrees of freedom that interact with a scalar clock field
[
39
]
. One could formalize a gauge-invariant Langevin system for a set of Einstein’s equations in their ADM formulation by coarse-graining spacetime in a specific gauge.

Following Eq. (24), we consider the full quantum gravity path integral that integrates over all spacetime metrics
g_{\mu\nu}
and scalar fields
\phi
for the EKG system,

\mathcal{Z}=\int\mathcal{D}g_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{D}\phi\,e^{iS[g_{\mu\nu},\phi]},

(25)

where
S[g_{\mu\nu},\phi]
is the ADM-decomposed action (Eq. 10). We separate the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
into a background
\bar{\gamma}_{ij}
and fluctuations
\delta\gamma_{ij}
,

\gamma_{ij}=\bar{\gamma}_{ij}+\delta\gamma_{ij},

(26)

and integrate out the Planck-scale fluctuations
\delta\gamma_{ij}
. It turns out that these fluctuations generate a source
\xi^{i}(x)
in the scalar sector that results in coarse-grained gravitational backreaction
[
40
]
. Consequently, the effective action for
\phi

e^{iS_{\text{eff}}[\phi]}=\int\mathcal{D}\delta\gamma_{ij}\,e^{iS[\bar{\gamma}+\delta\gamma,\phi]},

(27)

becomes the influence of quantum gravitational fluctuations on the scalar clock field
\phi
. Interestingly, such fluctuations, suppressed at wavelengths below the Planck scale, can be integrated out to yield a non-local influence functional as the cumulative effect of spacetime foam—short-lived quantum metric perturbations—on the clock’s dynamics.

By expanding
S
to quadratic order in
\delta\gamma_{ij}
yields a non-local influence functional,

S_{\text{eff}}[\phi]=S[\bar{\gamma},\phi]+\ln\mathcal{B}[\phi],

(28)

where
\mathcal{B}[\phi]
dictates gravitational backreaction. Using the Feynman-Vernon influence functional formalism
[
41
]
, we obtain a form

\mathcal{B}[\phi]=\exp\left(\frac{i}{2}\int d^{4}x\int d^{4}y\,\mathcal{O}[\phi(x)]\,G_{\text{grav}}(x,y)\,\mathcal{O}[\phi(y)]\right),

(29)

with
G_{\text{grav}}(x,y)
being the graviton propagator and
\mathcal{O}[\phi]=\delta\mathcal{H}_{\phi}/\delta\gamma_{ij}
is the coupling between
\phi
and
\delta\gamma_{ij}
.

The action is also real for real scalar clocks and, therefore, can be used as a probability measure in a stochastic process. Otherwise, it is instructive to employ a transformation of the non-local terms into stochastic noise
[
42
]
, as we are not interested in a complete stochastic system, which is intrinsically a hidden variable theory. Since the stochastic clock formalism demands covariance under foliation transformations, Stratonovich calculus is a natural choice for this purpose. We employ Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation by introducing an auxiliary noise field
\xi^{i}(x)
,

\mathcal{B}[\phi]=\int\mathcal{D}\xi^{i}\,\exp\left(i\int d^{4}x\left[\xi^{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}[\phi]-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{i}(\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma_{ij})\xi^{j}\right]\right),

(30)

where
\mathcal{O}_{i}[\phi]=\nabla_{i}(\pi_{\phi}/\sqrt{\gamma})
(cf. Eq. 24). Physically,
\xi^{i}
corresponds to quantum fluctuations of the gravitational momentum
\pi^{ij}
, arising from the non-local coupling
\mathcal{O}[\phi]
in the influence functional Eq. (29). This is a useful link between the noise field and spacetime foam-like perturbations, as shown in Appendix B. The Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation linearizes the non-local graviton propagator by introducing a classical noise term
[
43
]
that follows similar techniques used in stochastic inflation
[
39
]
, where coarse-grained field modes produce noise in the effective dynamics.

Moreover, while dealing with Langevin source terms that have a specified variance structure, one could compute the expectation values of functionals involving these source terms using the probability distribution that governs them
[
44
]
. In Eq. (30), this process requires integrating over the space of realizations
\mathcal{D}\xi^{i}
, such that fluctuations conform to the covariance structure dictated by
\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma_{ij}
. However, in the asymptotic regime where the noise correlation scale approaches zero, stochastic fluctuations can be effectively replaced with
\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\to\langle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\rangle=(\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma^{ij})^{-1}
, and has a well-defined continuous limit
[
45
]
. By calculating expectation values both with and without this replacement, we can demonstrate that the difference diminishes in the limit. As a result, the integral in the influence functional remains well-defined in a stochastic sense, thereby preserving the underlying symmetries of the theory
[
46
]
. The derivation of the stochastic Hamiltonian constraint from noise correlators is provided in Appendix B.

Substituting Eq. (30) into Eq. (27) and varying with respect to
\phi
produces a Langevin-type system

\hat{\mathcal{H}}\Psi=\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{grav}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}\right)\Psi=0,

(31)

where the the term
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}
is derived from the noise field as

\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}=\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\xi^{i}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right),\quad\langle\xi^{i}(x)\xi^{j}(y)\rangle=\hbar\,\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}\delta^{(3)}(x-y).

(32)

Here,
\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}=\gamma^{ik}\gamma^{jl}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{ij}\gamma^{kl}
is the DeWitt supermetric governing the kinetic structure of gravitational degrees of freedom and the noise correlator
\langle\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\rangle
capture the quantum uncertainty in conjugate momentum pairs.

Since the noise field
\xi^{i}(x)
physically gains importance as coarse-grained quantum fluctuations of the gravitational momentum
\pi^{ij}
, which couple to
\phi
through the divergence-free condition. In the semiclassical limit, the noise correlator vanishes, recovering the deterministic EKG dynamics. This is partially stochastic, like an open quantum system, where noise terms drive deviations from deterministic trajectories, which we will discuss in the coming sections.

6
Stochastic Clocks in ADM Formalism

Following the scenario discussed in the previous sections, we summarize the results to this end. In quantum gravity, the metric
\gamma_{ij}
and its conjugate momentum
\pi^{ij}
fluctuate at Planck scales. Since the scalar clock
\phi
defines foliation via
\phi(\iota_{t}(x))=t
, these metric fluctuations induce randomness in the slicing. This aligns with the principle of background independence: no preferred foliation exists, and quantum variability in
\phi
dynamically perturbs the hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
. The noise term
\xi^{i}
thus represents a
diffusion of time
caused by quantum gravitational foam.

The canonical ADM formalism, rooted in a rigid foliation of spacetime into spacelike hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
, faces a fundamental tension between its classical covariance and the quantum indeterminacy of spacetime structure. The stochastic modification should manifest in the Hamiltonian constraint, which now incorporates noise-driven corrections:

\hat{\mathcal{H}}|\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi]\rangle=0,\quad\hat{\mathcal{H}}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{grav}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{{\phi}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{matter}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}},

(33)

where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}
quantifies fluctuations in
\phi
’s momentum. Unlike traditional scalar clocks, which fix foliations through rigid gauge choices (e.g.,
N=1
), stochastic clocks dynamically adjust the lapse function
N
via

N=\frac{\pi_{\phi}}{\sqrt{\gamma}(\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial^{\mu}\phi)}+\frac{\delta\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}\rangle}{\delta\pi_{\phi}},

(34)

where the foliation remains non-preferential and intrinsically quantum in nature. Note that additional contribution to
N
arises naturally from the stochastic constraint while preserving consistency with the constrained Hamiltonian. The canonical commutation relations are similarly generalized to reflect the divergence-free momentum constraint:

[\hat{\phi}(x),\hat{\pi}_{\phi}(y)]=i\hbar\delta^{(3)}(x-y)+i\hbar\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\delta^{(3)}(x-y)}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right),

(35)

where the additional spatial derivative term enforces the divergence-free nature of
\pi^{\mu}
. This is consistent with the momentum constraint
\mathcal{H}_{i}\approx 0
, preventing anomalous terms that would violate diffeomorphism invariance.

Similar constructions appear in constrained quantum systems
[
47
]
, where Dirac brackets replace Poisson structures to accommodate first-class constraints. Consequently, the wavefunctional
|\Psi\rangle
decomposes into the eigenstates of
\phi
,

|\Psi\rangle=\int d\phi\,|\phi\rangle\otimes|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle,\quad\hat{\phi}|\phi\rangle=\phi|\phi\rangle,

(36)

with
|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle
evolving under the Schrödinger equation:

i\hbar\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi}|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle=\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{sys}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}\right)|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle,

(37)

where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{sys}}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{grav}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{matter}}
. The decomposition distinguishes stochastic clocks from prior relational approaches. While the Page-Wootters mechanism
[
48

Stochastic Corrections and Classical Limit Recovery

A self-consistent theory of quantum gravity must satisfy a fundamental criterion: in the regime where quantum effects become negligible, it must seamlessly recover the deterministic framework of classical general relativity. For the stochastic clock formalism introduced in this work, this necessitates demonstrating how noise-driven corrections to the Wheeler-DeWitt equation vanish in the semiclassical limit.

In the limit (
\hbar\to 0
), these corrections are suppressed, as evidenced by expanding the wavefunctional
\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi]
in a WKB ansatz:

\Psi[\gamma_{ij},\phi]=A[\gamma_{ij},\phi]\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}S[\gamma_{ij},\phi]\right),

(48)

where
S[\gamma_{ij},\phi]
is the classical action. Substituting Eq. (48) into the Eq. (42) and isolating terms at
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{0})

(49)

while stochastic contributions manifest as
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{1/2})
deviations. Stochastic corrections enter at
\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{1/2})
as noise-driven deviations
\delta S\propto\sqrt{\xi}\int W(a)d\phi
, which vanish under noise averaging (
\mathbb{E}[\delta S]=0
). Scaling follows from dimensional consistency in Eq. (30): since
\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}\sim\gamma^{-1}
,
\xi
must scale as
\hbar^{2}
to preserve
\mathcal{H}_{\text{stoch}}\sim\hbar^{1/2}
in Eq. (38).

However, since the noise field
\xi^{i}(x)
is Gaussian-distributed with zero mean, its expectation value vanishes

\mathbb{E}\left[\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\xi^{i}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)\right]=\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}[\xi^{i}]}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)=0,

(50)

with
\mathbb{E}[\xi^{i}]=0
. However, higher-order moments, such as
\mathbb{E}[(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}})^{2}]\propto\hbar
, are subdominant and dissipate in the classical limit. These dissipations perturb the clock’s foliation, which might leave potentially observable signatures on primordial cosmology. For instance, in the FLRW model (Section 6), stochastic diffusion suppresses power at large scales (
k\to 0
), leading to deviations from scale-invariant power spectra in the cosmic microwave background. While detailed predictions require further study, the stochastic corrections suggest that quantum-gravitational diffusion could suppress power in the primordial power spectrum at large scales. Such suppression might leave imprints on the CMB anisotropy, offering a pathway to observational tests. Future work could quantify these effects using inflationary models coupled with stochastic clocks.

We can further quantify this suppression by parameterizing the noise strength as
\xi=\kappa\hbar^{2}
, where
\kappa
is a dimensionless positive constant. Substituting into Eq. (40), the stochastic term scales as

\xi\mathcal{N}(a)\sim\kappa\hbar^{2}\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\delta}{\delta\phi}\right),

(51)

which becomes negligible compared to the gravitational kinetic term

of the Wheeler-Dewitt equation as
\hbar\to 0

\delta a\sim\sqrt{\xi}\hbar/E_{\phi}
relegated to Planck-scale phenomena.

The recovery of the classical limit is thus determined by two key mechanisms: (i) the scaling of stochastic terms with
\hbar
, which suppresses their contributions on a macroscopic level, and (ii) the dynamical decoupling of noise-averaged observables from quantum fluctuations. This ensures that the framework accurately reproduces general relativity in observable regimes while allowing for quantum gravitational effects to influence structures at the microscale. Although the classical limit is consistently recovered, several conceptual and technical challenges remain, which we will now address.

9
Future Prospects

The stochastic clock represents a conceptual shift in how time is treated in quantum gravity and, within the ADM formalism, marks a significant departure from conventional scalar field timekeepers. This approach advances the canonical structure in three fundamental ways: (i) stochastic deparametrization, where fluctuations in
\phi
induce foliation-dependent perturbations via
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}
; (ii) dynamical lapse fixing, ensuring that
N
is entirely determined by
\pi_{\phi}
and its fluctuations, thereby eliminating the need for externally imposed gauge conditions; and (iii) generalized commutators, where the divergence-free constraint on momentum introduces nontrivial modifications to the canonical Poisson brackets.

In this context, we suggest several directions for further exploration. First, the formulation of generalized dynamical clocks provides a pathway where stochasticity induces correlation between the scalar field
\phi
and its momentum
\pi_{\phi}
, maintaining self-consistent evolution of hypersurface data even in highly symmetric minisuperspace models. Extending this approach to inhomogeneous or strongly gravitating regimes could provide deeper insights into quantum spacetime evolution. Second, the regularization of hypersurface quantization through geometric coordinates
(X,Y)=(\sqrt{\gamma}\phi,\sqrt{\gamma}\pi_{\phi})
and noise-averaged volume elements confirm that the stochastic framework remains well-posed at the level of the Cauchy problem. Future work should explore whether these regularization techniques can be generalized beyond the ADM decomposition, particularly in non-perturbative quantum gravity approaches such as loop quantum gravity or group field theory.

Additionally, this work establishes a stochastic Poisson-commutator correspondence, where spatial gradient fluctuations are incorporated directly into the algebraic structure of quantum gravity. Further research is needed to determine whether this correspondence remains consistent when extended to higher-dimensional or noncommutative formulations of quantum gravity. Finally, the foliation-independent quantization framework developed here provides a potential resolution to gauge ambiguities in previous scalar field clock models. However, the implications of this result remain largely unexplored in the broader context of background-independent quantum theories, and a deeper investigation into its compatibility with holographic or asymptotic safety approaches is warranted.

Despite these advances, several conceptual and technical challenges remain open. The first major issue is the conditional probability ambiguity in defining relational observables. While the framework allows for noise-averaged observables of the form
\langle\hat{O}\rangle_{\phi}=\mathbb{E}[\langle\Psi|\hat{O}|\Psi\rangle]
, the inverse problem—extracting
\phi
from observed correlations—remains ill-posed. Addressing this requires a more refined understanding of how stochastic clocks encode temporal information in observables, particularly in settings where gravitational backreaction plays a significant role. The second key challenge is the constrained symplectic structure, where the stochastic modification of the Wheeler-DeWitt constraint embeds foliation variability within the stochastic Hamiltonian. A fully background-independent quantization may require developing an alternative representation that decouples
\pi_{\phi}
from
\gamma_{ij}
while maintaining consistency with the Hamiltonian constraints of general relativity.

A final and more foundational question concerns the synchronization of observables across different foliations. Since noise-averaged expectation values depend on path-integrated Wiener processes, it is unclear whether two observers using different realizations of
dW_{\phi}
would recover consistent histories. This raises the deeper question of whether the diffusion process introduced in this work is a fundamental aspect of quantum gravity or merely a practical regularization technique. Investigating the physical interpretation of these stochastic effects, potentially through quantum information-theoretic approaches, could shed light on whether they correspond to intrinsic quantum fluctuations or to an effective description of coarse-grained quantum states.

Discussion

The foundational work of Baierlein, Sharp, and Wheeler proposed scalar fields as intrinsic spacetime coordinates, recasting the Einstein-Hilbert action to isolate physical degrees of freedom. While their formalism successfully avoided coordinate redundancies, it faltered in quantum regimes where no fixed spacetime metric exists, rendering internal time a mere slicing of dynamical variables rather than a measurable parameter. Since then, most literature has followed this approach, either rating the scalar as a clock variable or avoiding a universal time parameter. Our framework advances this paradigm by extending the scalar field
\phi
to incorporate quantum gravitational fluctuations as stochastic corrections to the clock’s dynamics—termed stochastic clocks.

These fluctuations arise from the fundamental quantum nature of spacetime and parametrize evolution through a relational Schrödinger equation, wherein the wavefunctional
|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle
entails states of geometry and matter conditioned on
\phi
. Lacking a preferred time parameter leads us to assume that the system’s evolution is entirely relational, translating into a diffusion process. This diffusion, modelled by the Wiener process, effectively averages out possible quantum fluctuations, resulting in observable quantities that maintain unitarity in a statistical sense. Unlike Rovelli’s relational quantum mechanics, which dismisses privileged subsystems, our approach explicitly recovers unitary evolution similar to the Page-Wootters mechanism, with
\phi
subject to inherent quantum fluctuations.

A stochastic clock thereby turns timelessness in canonical gravity into a diffusion process with two important consequences:

(i) Stochasticity demonstrates that unitarity does not depend on an external time parameter. Instead, it emerges relationally through noise-averaged correlations between geometry and matter. Unlike the frozen formalism, the stochastic clock introduces a dynamic time parameter via the quantum fluctuations in
\phi
, such that the wavefunctional’s norm is preserved during the evolution. Although individual stochastic trajectories may exhibit fluctuations in the norm of the wavefunctional due to quantum diffusion, the evolution is governed by a noise-averaged Schrödinger-like equation that maintains overall unitary dynamics. In other words, while the randomness inherent in the quantum clock introduces uncertainty in the local evolution, the ensemble-averaged dynamics preserve the norm, as explicitly demonstrated in Appendix C.

(ii) Conditional probability, traditionally defined by the Page-Wootters mechanism, is generalized to accommodate the quantum variability of the clock. By replacing rigid conditioning with noise-averaged observables, stochasticity avoids paradoxes that arise when treating
\phi
as a classical parameter. For example, the suppression of singularities as
a\to 0
in Eq. (44) results not from arbitrary boundary conditions but from the stochastic action
\mathcal{S}(a,\phi)
, where quantum diffusion smooths divergences
[
30
]
.

We further showed that stochastic corrections vanish in the classical limit, recovering deterministic trajectories while leaving potential signatures at Planck scales. Largely with suppression of wave function
\Psi(a\to 0,\phi)\to 0
and
\Psi(a\to\infty,\phi)\to 0
(for
k=1
) from the quantum diffusion process, bypassing ad hoc boundary conditions. However, the semiclassical limit reveals deeper insights: the WKB ansatz for
\Psi(a,\phi)
satisfies a noisy Hamilton-Jacobi equation (see Eq. (43)) where stochasticity (
W(a)
) shifts classical trajectories
a(\phi)
and introduces quantum diffusion
\delta a\propto\sqrt{\xi}\hbar/E_{\phi}
. Near
a\to 0
, exponential suppression
\Psi\sim e^{-S/\hbar}
suggests singularity avoidance, yet the lack of synchronization across
\phi
-slices persists, indicating the incompatibility of quantization procedure with global timekeeping. While it is possible to reduce the scalar degrees of freedom after solving the constraint equations. A synchronizing mechanism is needed, as there is no way to measure time simultaneously across all degrees of freedom.

Another critical insight lies in synchronizing observables across reduced degrees of freedom. Conventionally, such reductions offer no significant insight into how the dynamics evolve with respect to a time-dependent parameter. This is because, in canonical formalism, time is not an external parameter but must be defined relationally within the system. However, in minisuperspace models, such as the FLRW universe, this framework smoothly recovers relational observables
\langle\hat{a}\rangle_{\phi}^{\text{avg}}
(Eq. 47) as noise-averaged expectations, where quantum-gravitational diffusion quantifies deviations from classical trajectories. This recovery stems from relaxing the ADM formalism’s reliance on lapse
N
and shift
N^{i}
, which vanish in on-shell actions but re-emerge as stochastic variables (
\delta N\propto\xi W(a)
) during spacetime reconstruction (see Appendix for an analytical test of the latter.) Though their role in Eq. (34) is subtle yet indispensable, while redundant in extremal configurations, they encode foliation fluctuations critical for background independence.

Despite its advantages, the stochastic clock framework introduces several conceptual challenges that warrant further investigation. The canonical coordinates derived from the Lagrangian, generated by the clock degree of freedom, are much simpler than the constraints imposed by a scalar field in the Hamiltonian framework. In this context, the lapse function and shift vector play a subtle role. While they might appear redundant in the extremal Lagrangian action, they are essential for reconstructing the original spacetime picture. Reintroducing these variables contrasts consistency with the full spacetime description, as their explicit role in defining a physical clock remains elusive. These challenges underscore the need for observable-driven stochasticity in quantum gravity as they might invoke a self-consistent mechanism to recover classical spacetime while enabling quantum-gravitational effects to perturb foliations—a critical step towards fully resolving the problem of time in canonical gravity.

G_{\text{grav}}(x,y)
is the graviton propagator. To linearize the quadratic term in
\mathcal{O}[\phi]
, one could introduce an auxiliary noise field
\xi^{i}(x)
through the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation:

\mathcal{B}[\phi]=\int\mathcal{D}\xi^{i}\,\exp\left(i\int d^{4}x\left[\xi^{i}\mathcal{O}_{i}[\phi]-\frac{1}{2}\xi^{i}\left(\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma_{ij}\right)\xi^{j}\right]\right),

(B65)

with a 3+1 decomposition,
\mathcal{O}_{i}[\phi]=\nabla_{i}\left(\pi_{\phi}/\sqrt{\gamma}\right)
where
\gamma
is the determinant of the spatial metric on the hypersurface. The noise correlator is determined by the quadratic term in
\xi^{i}
:

\langle\xi^{i}(x)\xi^{j}(y)\rangle=\left(\sqrt{\gamma}\gamma_{ij}\right)^{-1}\delta^{(3)}(x-y).

(B66)

However, we know that the kinetic structure of gravitational degrees of freedom is governed by the DeWitt supermetric
\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}=\gamma^{ik}\gamma^{jl}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{ij}\gamma^{kl}
, which modifies the noise covariance to

\langle\xi^{i}(x)\xi^{j}(y)\rangle=\hbar\,\mathcal{G}^{ijkl}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

(B67)

where
\hbar
enters via the canonical commutation relation
[\hat{\gamma}_{ij}(x),\hat{\pi}^{kl}(y)]=i\hbar\mathcal{G}^{kl}_{~~ij}\delta^{(3)}(x-y).

Substituting the influence functional in
B65
into the effective action
S_{\text{eff}}[\phi]=S[\bar{\gamma},\phi]+\ln\mathcal{B}[\phi]
and varying with respect to
\phi
yields the Wheeler Dewitt equation with noise-driven correction to the Hamiltonian constraint. This recovers Eq. (31) in the main text, with the stochastic term
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{stoch}}=\nabla_{i}(\xi^{i}/\sqrt{\gamma})
dynamically adjusting the foliation. Note that the introduction of the noise term is directly linked to the divergence-free condition of the clock momentum
\pi^{i}
, which in the classical limit satisfies:

\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\pi_{\phi}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)=0.

(B68)

However, integrating out quantum fluctuations of the metric introduces deviations from this condition, leading to the stochastic modification:

\nabla_{i}\left(\frac{\pi_{\phi}}{\sqrt{\gamma}}\right)=\xi^{i},

(B69)

which directly contributes to the noise term in Eq. (B6). This establishes that the emergence of stochastic corrections is a direct consequence of deviations from the classical divergence-free constraint due to quantum gravitational effects.

Appendix C

To demonstrate norm preservation in the stochastic clock framework, we analyze the time evolution of the wavefunctional
|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle
governed by the stochastic Schrödinger equation

i\hbar\,d|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle=\left(\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{sys}}\,d\phi+\sqrt{\xi}\,\hat{W}(a)\,dW_{\phi}\right)|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle,

(C1)

where
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{sys}}=\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{grav}}+\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{matter}}
is a Hermitian operator,
\hat{W}(a)
is a self-adjoint operator encoding spatial gradients, and
dW_{\phi}
is a Wiener increment satisfying
\mathbb{E}[dW_{\phi}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[dW_{\phi}^{2}]=d\phi
. The norm of the wavefunctional is defined as

\mathcal{N}=\langle\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)|\psi_{\text{sys}}(\phi)\rangle,

(C2)

and its differential is given by

d\mathcal{N}=\langle d\psi_{\text{sys}}|\psi_{\text{sys}}\rangle+\langle\psi_{\text{sys}}|d\psi_{\text{sys}}\rangle+\langle d\psi_{\text{sys}}|d\psi_{\text{sys}}\rangle.

(C3)

Substituting the expression (C1) for
d|\psi_{\text{sys}}\rangle
into (C3), we find that the deterministic contributions cancel because
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{sys}}
is Hermitian that deterministic contributions do not affect the norm:

\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle\psi_{\rm sys}|\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm sys}^{\dagger}-\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm sys}|\psi_{\rm sys}\rangle d\phi=0.

(C70)

Similarly, the linear stochastic terms vanish in expectation,
\mathbb{E}[dW_{\phi}]=0
, leaving a second-order term—arising from the Itô correction:

\langle d\psi_{\rm sys}|d\psi_{\rm sys}\rangle=-\frac{\xi}{\hbar^{2}}\langle\psi_{\rm sys}|\hat{W}^{2}|\psi_{\rm sys}\rangle d\phi.

(C71)

At first glance, this Itô correction appears to lead to a nonzero change in the norm. To resolve this apparent violation of norm preservation, we modify the stochastic coupling so that the stochastic part of the Hamiltonian is defined to be anti-Hermitian. We do this by redefining the stochastic contribution as anti-Hermitian coupling
i\sqrt{\xi}\,\hat{W}\,dW_{\phi}
such that this term cancels the positive-definite contribution from the noise with
\xi^{i}
being a real Gaussian noise field. As a result, the Itô correction takes on an opposite sign. Consequently, when the ensemble average is taken, the net contribution to the differential of the norm vanishes, that is,

\mathbb{E}[d\mathcal{N}]=-\frac{\xi}{\hbar^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\psi_{\rm sys}|\hat{W}^{2}|\psi_{\rm sys}\rangle\right]d\phi+\frac{\xi}{\hbar^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\psi_{\rm sys}|\hat{W}^{2}|\psi_{\rm sys}\rangle\right]d\phi=0.

(C6)

This shows that while individual stochastic trajectories may exhibit norm fluctuations due to quantum diffusion, the ensemble-averaged evolution preserves the norm, maintaining the overall unitarity of the system.

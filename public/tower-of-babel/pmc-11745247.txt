Determinants of Caregiver Burden in Stroke Survivors who Underwent Decompressive Craniectomy
Authors: Karnam Anand, Sai Sirisha, Sanjana Putta, Sudhindra Vooturi, Manas Panigrahi, Sita Jayalakshmi, Subhash Kaul
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background and Objectives:For stroke survivors (SS) who undergo decompressive craniectomy (DC), quality of life is intertwined with the care they receive. Burden on caregiver becomes an important determinant of recovery of SS. In this study, we aimed to analyze the determinants of burden on caregivers of SS who undergo DC.Methods:Caregivers of 198 SS who underwent DC formed the study population of this prospective observational study. The study was conducted in a tertiary referral unit between September 2019 and March 2021. Caregiver burden was quantified using Zarit Burden Interview. Analysis for association between the reported burden and modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score was done.Results:Mean age of the participants was 47.00 ± 11.2 years. The mRS outcome was poor in SS aged >60 years, Glasgow coma scale (GCS) score at presentation of <8, and in SS with bilateral/dominant hemisphere involvement. Outcome was better when DC was done ≤48 h (74.2% vs. 47.2%, P < 0.001) and if SS were shifted to a rehabilitation unit. Among caregivers, 58% and 16% reported to be always and frequently burdened, respectively; 33.3% of caregivers considered a retrospective review of consent for DC. A higher mRS score was associated with more burden.Conclusion:Early DC and care at rehabilitation unit were associated with better functional outcomes in SS. Nearly 75% of caregivers of patients post-DC reported high burden of providing care to SS, and the burden increased linearly with mRS score.

Introduction

Decompressive craniectomy (DC) is known to reduce mortality associated with stroke, but its value addition to quality of life is debatable.[12] Although randomized clinical trials conducted on stroke patients undergoing hemicraniectomy have reported significant improvement in mortality and functional outcome, their health-related quality of life (HRQoL) postsurgery has seldom been explored. Till date, the number of follow-up studies evaluating HRQoL after craniectomy in space-occupying infarctions is very limited.[3] The findings from such studies are contradictory, with a wide variation found in the extent to which physical and psychosocial well-being are affected. Surgically and medically treated patients were also found to have similar HRQoL.[23]

Quality of life is generally influenced by both functional social environment and the subjective burden experienced by stroke survivors (SS) and their caregivers.[4] However, studies evaluating such caregiver burden, particularly among decompressive hemicraniectomy patients are rarely conducted. Studies published till date that attempted to obtain “retrospective consent” based on outcome found that the information obtained could be biased with a positive response only from those with a significant residual neurocognitive function.[5]

To the best of our knowledge, studies that assessed the outcome after DC, collectively in patients with malignant ischemic, hemorrhagic, and cerebral sinus venous thrombosis (CSVT) strokes are lacking. In addition, most studies on patients with malignant middle cerebral artery infarction had smaller sample size, thereby restricting the generalizability of their findings. In the current study, we aimed to assess burden on caregivers of SS and evaluate the determinants of the burden.

Methods

In this prospective observational study, all eligible patients who underwent DC between September 2019 and March 2021 at the study center, a tertiary referral care center, were included. The study inclusion criterion was patients with large ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke/CSVT who underwent DC during the study period. DC in intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) was done based on clinical evidence of altered sensorium, raised intracranial pressure, and radiological evidence of mass effect, edema, and midline shift. Most of the patients had hematoma volume above 30 ml; some patients of thalamic ICH had volume less than 30 ml, but were causing midline shift due to significant edema. Patients in whom DC was done elsewhere and patients or caregivers who did not consent for participation were excluded from the study. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee at the study center. Informed consent was obtained from all the participants as a mandatory prerequisite for enrollment into the study.

Data collection

Data obtained from 198 patients who satisfied the study criteria comprised findings on thorough neurologic assessment, demographic data, type of stroke (ischemic/hemorrhagic/CSVT), timing of decompressive surgery, and duration of hospital stay. At 3-month follow-up after DC, patients’ functional outcome and quality of life were assessed by a trained neurologist (AK) by using modified Rankin Scale (mRS); an mRS score <4 at follow-up was considered as good outcome, and an mRS of 4 and above was considered as poor outcome.[6] Zarit Burden Interview[7] was administered to patients’ caregivers by the author SV, who was blinded to the diagnosis of the patient. The interview had a score of 1–5, where 1: Never, 2: Rarely, 3: Sometime, 4: Quite frequently, 5: Nearly always.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were tabulated and analyzed using Microsoft Excel and Statistical Package for the Social Sciences version 25 (IBM Inc., Armonk, NY, USA), respectively. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, whereas categorical variables were reported as frequencies and/or percentages. The caregivers were divided into groups based on reported burden as less burden (score 1, 2), moderate burden (score 3), and severe burden (score 4, 5). An independent t-test was used to evaluate differences between the study groups for continuous variables, whereas Chi-square test was used to evaluate the differences for categorical variables. The association between reported caregiver burden and the outcome of the patient and other clinical characteristics of the patient was evaluated using Pearson correlation. A P value of <0.05 was considered significant.

Results

The average age of the study population was 47.00 ± 11.2 years. Among the indications for DC, 86 (43.4%), 14 (7.1%), and 98 (49.5%) belonged to the ICH, CSVT, and ischemic stroke groups, respectively. The detailed distribution of comorbidities is summarized in Table 1. The average age of the caregivers was 49.12 ± 14.83 years, with 130 (64.5%) of them being men. While 38 (19.0%) of the caregivers did not finish education beyond primary school, 83 (41.5%) were graduates and 73 (36.5%) completed post-graduation. The primary caregiver was the spouse of the patients in 42 (21.0%) patients. None of the caregivers had previous training in providing care to patients.

On evaluation of mRS outcome across the three etiologies at follow-up, among patients below 60 years of age, 37.2% of ICH patients had good outcome, followed by 40.8% seen in ischemic stroke, followed by 78.6% in CSVT. Furthermore, the mRS outcome was poor in patients aged ≥60 years. In addition, the outcome was poor when both the hemispheres were involved or when the dominant hemisphere was affected. When DC was done under 48 h after the onset of symptoms, the outcomes were better than when done >48 h after symptom onset (74.2% vs. 47.2%, P < 0.001).

Results obtained on further analysis of the influence of glasgow coma scale (GCS) at presentation on mRS outcome showed poor outcome in a majority of patients with a GCS score of ≤8 in the ICH and ischemic groups. For the CSVT stroke group, the mRS outcome did not vary with the GCS score. Similarly, participants who were already on antiplatelets showed poor outcome at follow-up (61.7% vs. 38.3%, P < 0.001). Interestingly, the volume of bleed was not associated with mRS outcome [Table 2] in patients with ICH. After discharge from the hospital, those patients who were shifted to a rehabilitation care unit following acute management had good outcome [Table 3]. On analysis of association of mRS score at follow-up, burden on caregivers had a positive association with mRS score, indicating that higher the mRS score, greater is the burden on caregivers [Table 4].

Discussion

The current study aimed to evaluate the factors associated with functional outcome among stroke patients undergoing DC and the burden among their caregivers.

Evidence from previous studies has shown that functional outcome and quality of life rely on several factors: neurologic examination, baseline patient characteristics, stroke presentation, imaging evaluation, and time from onset of symptoms to surgery.[1389] In addition, age, involvement of multiple vascular territories, and the presence of signs of herniation have also been considered as possible prognostic factors in acute ischemic stroke patients who underwent DC.[18910] In the current study, the functional outcome after DC was evaluated using the mRS score. Niznick et al.[6] reported that the original dichotomization of mRS 0–2 versus rest scores showed only fair correlation between perception of patients of their outcome and the actual score on mRS. However, the correlation improved fairly when mRS scores where dichotomized into 0–3 versus 4 and above. Therefore, in the current study, the mRS scores were categorized into poor- mRS score 4–5 and good- mRS score 1–3. In the current study, poor outcome was detected in about two-thirds (60%) of the ischemic stroke patients. Moreover, patients shifted to home (instead of rehabilitation center) had a significantly poor outcome. While home care provides comfort and familiarity, care at the rehabilitation center is organized and offers specialized interventions, which may explain better functional outcomes obtained there.

The study by Alam et al.[11] in ischemic stroke as well as the STICH trial[12] in ICH patients revealed that delay in surgery was associated with a poor outcome. In the current study, the mean time for surgery among ischemic stroke patients was 36.88 h, although it exceeded 48 h in almost two-thirds of the sample, who inadvertently had a relatively poor outcome. Mohsen and Abbass[13] evaluated the functional outcome of decompressive hemicraniectomy in management of malignant middle cerebral artery (MCA) infarctions and reported that age, timing of surgery, and low preoperative GCS (<8) were associated with morbidity. We report similar findings where none of the patients with GCS of less than 8 achieved a good outcome. We also report that bi-hemispherical involvement was associated with a poor outcome in patients with CSVT, similar to the report of Goedemans et al.[14]

Caregiver burden

The present study demonstrated a greater caregiver burden particularly among those with a poor functional outcome, similar to the findings from a long-term follow-up study by Hamamoto et al.,[5] which reported that caregiver burden correlated with variables like age of patients above 60 years, in whom the functional outcome is poor. Moreover, the level of burden reportedly correlated negatively with the functional outcomes of the patients and positively with the duration of impairment. Importantly, greater caregiver burden being reported in the current study may further worsen when the care for the patients includes increase in expenses and reduced family income. Therefore, support from health-care providers should not only be for SS, but also for their caregivers.

Limitations

The current single-center study was conducted at a private tertiary care center, leading to unintentional bias where patients with poor and low economic background may not be well represented. Although a thorough understanding of the socioeconomic status of the family is desirable, it was not the objective of our study. The respondents’ caregivers’ response to the questionnaire could be subjective and may be representation at that snapshot of time only. Despite the above limitations, our study provides information on multiple aspects of caregiver burden in these patients. Future multicenter studies may be helpful to validate our findings.

Conclusion

The mRS outcome was poor in SS aged ≥60 years. Nearly 75% of caregivers of patients post-DC reported high burden of providing care to SS, and the burden increased linearly with mRS score.

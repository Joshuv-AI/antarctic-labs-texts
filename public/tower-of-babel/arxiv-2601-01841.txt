Improved Approximation Algorithms for the Multiple-Depot Split Delivery Vehicle Routing Problem
Jingyang Zhao, Yonghang Su, Mingyu Xiao

Abstract
The Multiple-Depot Split Delivery Vehicle Routing Problem (MD-SDVRP) is a challenging problem with broad applications in logistics. The goal is to serve customers’ demand using a fleet of capacitated vehicles located in multiple depots, where each customer’s demand can be served by more than one vehicle, while minimizing the total travel cost of all vehicles. We study approximation algorithms for this problem. Previously, the only known result was a 6-approximation algorithm for a constant number of depots (INFORMS J. Comput. 2023), and whether this ratio could be improved was left as an open question. In this paper, we resolve it by proposing a ( 6 − 2 ⋅ 10 − 36 ) (6-2\cdot 10^{-36}) -approximation algorithm for this setting. Moreover, we develop constant-factor approximation algorithms that work beyond a constant number of depots, improved parameterized approximation algorithms related to the vehicle capacity and the number of depots, as well as bi-factor approximation algorithms.



1
Introduction

The (Capacitated) Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) is a classical and extensively studied routing problem
Toth and Vigo 2014
.
In this problem, we are given a set of customers with integer demand and a depot that provides an
unlimited
number of vehicles with the same integer capacity.
The objective is to design vehicle routes for to satisfy all customers such that the total traveling cost is minimized.
Each vehicle route must begin and end at the depot, and in which the vehicle delivers at most its capacity of demand to customers.
There are two famous variants of the CVRP:
splittable
and
unsplittable
.
In the splittable CVRP, each customer’s demand can be served by multiple vehicles, whereas in the unsplittable CVRP, each customer’s demand must be served entirely by a single vehicle.
When the model is extended to allow multiple depots, it becomes the Multiple-Depot VRP (MD-VRP), which has also received substantial attention in the literature
Montoya-Torres et al. 2015
.

However, in real-world scenarios, the number of vehicles available at each depot may be limited.
To address this situation, the Split Delivery VRP (SDVRP), a variant of the splittable VRP, was introduced
Dror and Trudeau 1989
;
Dror and Trudeau 1990
.
The SDVRP has since been studied extensively
Belenguer et al. 2000
;
Zhang et al. 2015
;
Gamst et al. 2024
;
Lin et al. 2025
;
Konstantakopoulos et al. 2022
, and a comprehensive survey can be found in
Archetti et al. 2014
.

Analogously, when multiple depots are involved, the corresponding problem is known as the Multiple-Depot SDVRP (MD-SDVRP)
Lai et al. 2023
.

Formally, in the MD-SDVRP, we are given an undirected complete graph
G=(V,E)
with vertex set
V=J\cup D
and edge set
E=V\times V
.
The set
J
represents
n
customers, and
D
represents
k
depots.
Each customer
v\in J
has a demand
q_{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and each depot
u\in D
owns
r_{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
vehicles with the same capacity
Q\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
Let
L
denote the set of all vehicles and
m=|L|
.
Each edge
e\in E
has a non-negative cost
c(e)\geq 0
, and the edge cost function
c
satisfies the triangle inequality.
The objective is to design vehicle routes to satisfy all customers while minimizing the total traveling cost.
When
k=1
, the MD-SDVRP reduces to the SDVRP.

We study (parameterized) approximation algorithms and bi-factor approximation algorithms for the MD-SDVRP.

For a minimization problem, an algorithm is called a
\rho
-approximation algorithm if, for any instance
I
, it computes a solution of cost at most
\rho
times the optimal value in polynomial time
\text{poly}(|I|)
, where
|I|
denotes the input size.
The quantity
\rho
is referred to as the
approximation ratio
.
With respect to some parameter
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, an algorithm is called a slice-wise polynomial-time (XP)
\rho
-approximation algorithm if it runs in
\text{poly}(|I|)
time for every fixed
k=\mathcal{O}(1)
and achieves an approximation ratio of
\rho
; an algorithm is called a fixed-parameter tractable (FPT)
\rho
-approximation algorithm if it runs in
\mathcal{O}(f(k))\cdot\text{poly}(|I|)
time for some computable function
f(k)
and achieves an approximation ratio of
\rho
.

The notion of a bi-factor approximation algorithm may vary across problems.
For the MD-SDVRP, we define a bi-factor
(\rho,\gamma)
-approximation algorithm as a polynomial-time algorithm that computes a solution whose cost is at most
\rho
times the optimal value (under the original capacity constraint), while allowing each vehicle to exceed its capacity
Q
, but with the total load on each vehicle bounded by
\gamma\cdot Q
, where
\gamma\geq 1
.
In the same manner, with respect to some parameter
k
, one can define XP/FPT bi-factor approximation algorithms.

1.1
Related Work

In this subsection, we review the existing approximation algorithms for the MD-SDVRP and its related problems.

1.1.1
The VRP and the SDVRP

First, we consider the VRP and the SDVRP, where the VRP can be viewed as a special case of the SDVRP in which the depot has an unlimited number of vehicles.

For the case
Q=\infty
, by the triangle inequality, the VRP is equivalent to the Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP).
For the TSP, Christofides
Christofides 2022
proposed a
1.5
-approximation algorithm, and the ratio has been improved to less than
1.5-10^{-36}
in
Karlin et al. 2023
. In the following, we use
\alpha
to denote the current best approximation ratio for the TSP.

For the case
Q\neq\infty
, Haimovich and Kan
Haimovich and Kan 1985
proposed an
(\alpha+1)
-approximation algorithm for the splittable VRP, and Altinkemer and Gavish
Altinkemer and Gavish 1987
proposed an
(\alpha+2)
-approximation algorithm for the unsplittable VRP.
Recently, Blauth
et al.

Blauth et al. 2023
improved the ratio to
\alpha+1-\varepsilon_{\alpha}
for the splittable VRP, and Friggstad
et al.

Friggstad et al. 2022
improved the ratio to
\alpha+1+\ln 2-\varepsilon_{\alpha}
for the unsplittable VRP, where
\varepsilon_{\alpha}
is slightly greater than
\frac{1}{3000}
if
\alpha=1.5
.

For the SDVRP, to our knowledge, the only existing result is a 4-approximation algorithm, which can be trivially derived from the algorithm for the MD-SDVRP
Lai et al. 2023
.

1.1.2
The MD-VRP and the MD-SDVRP

For the case
Q=\infty
, by the triangle inequality, the MD-VRP is equivalent to the Multiple-Depot TSP (MD-TSP).
For the MD-TSP, Rathinam
et al.

Rathinam et al. 2007
proposed a 2-approximation algorithm, and Xu
et al.

Xu et al. 2011
improved the ratio to
2-1/k
.

Moreover, with respect to the parameter
k
, Xu and Rodrigues
Xu and Rodrigues 2015
obtained an XP
1.5
-approximation algorithm with running time
\mathcal{O}(|V|^{k+2}|E|^{k-1})
.
Traub
et al.

Traub et al. 2022
obtained an XP
(\alpha+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm with running time
|V|^{\mathcal{O}(k/\varepsilon)}
for any constant
\varepsilon>0
.
Deppert
et al.

Deppert et al. 2023
obtained a randomized FPT
(1.5+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm with running time
(1/\varepsilon)^{\mathcal{O}(k\log k)}\cdot\mathcal{O}(|V|^{3})
.

For the case
Q\neq\infty
, Li and Simchi-Levi
Li and Simchi-Levi 1990
proposed a
(2\alpha+1)
-approximation algorithm for the splittable MD-VRP, and Harks
et al.

Harks et al. 2013
proposed a 4-approximation algorithm for the unsplittable MD-VRP.
Zhao and Xiao
Zhao and Xiao 2025b
improved the ratio to
4-\frac{1}{1500}
for the splittable MD-VRP and to
4-\frac{1}{50000}
for the unsplittable MD-VRP.
Very recently, Friggstad and Mömke
Friggstad and Mömke 2025
further improved the ratio to
3.9365
for the
unit-demand
MD-VRP, a special case of the unsplittable MD-VRP in which each customer has unit demand.

Moreover, with respect to the parameter
Q
, Zhao and Xiao
Zhao and Xiao 2025b
proposed an XP
(3+\ln 2-\max\{\Theta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q}}),\frac{1}{9000}\})
-approximation algorithm for the splittable MD-VRP, and an XP
(3+\ln 2-\Theta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{Q}}))
-approximation algorithm for the unsplittable MD-VRP.
Both algorithms run in
|V|^{\mathcal{O}(Q)}
time.

For the MD-SDVRP, Lai
et al.

Lai et al. 2023
proposed a
pseudo
-XP
(6-4/k)
-approximation algorithm with respect to the parameter
k\geq 2
, running in time
\mathcal{O}(|V|^{k-1}|E|^{2(k-1)}

\max\{|V|^{3},|L|^{3}\})
.
Their algorithm is pseudo-XP as the value
|L|
may be super-polynomial in
|V|
.
Their analysis focuses on the case
k\geq 2
.
When
k=1
, it can be verified that their algorithm yields an approximation ratio of
4
with a running time of
\mathcal{O}(\max\{|V|^{3},|L|^{3}\})
.
Whether their algorithm can be improved remains open.

1.2
Our Results

Our first contribution is to provide two improved parameterized approximation algorithms for the MD-SDVRP with respect to the parameter
k
.

First, we propose an XP
(2\rho+3)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP, denoted as
Alg.1
, with running time
f_{k}(|V|)+\mathcal{O}(|V|^{4}|E|^{k-1}\log|V|)
, where
\rho
and
f_{k}(|V|)
are the approximation ratio and running time of any XP algorithm for the MD-TSP, respectively.

Hence, by applying the XP
(1.5-10^{-36})
-approximation algorithm for the MD-TSP
Traub et al. 2022
,
Alg.1
yields an XP
(6-2\cdot 10^{-36})
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP.
This improves the previous best-known (pseudo-)XP
(6-4/k)
-approximation algorithm
Lai et al. 2023
.

Second, we develop the first FPT
(2\rho^{\prime}+3)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP, denoted as
Alg.2
, with running time
f^{\prime}_{k}(|V|)+2^{\mathcal{O}(k\log k)}\cdot\mathcal{O}(|V|^{4}\log|V|)
, where
\rho^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}_{k}(|V|)
denote the approximation ratio and running time of any FPT algorithm for the MD-TSP, respectively.

By applying the randomized FPT
(1.5+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-TSP
Deppert et al. 2023
,
Alg.2
yields a randomized FPT
(6+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP.
Moreover, by applying the deterministic
(2-1/k)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-TSP
Xu et al. 2011
,
Alg.2
yields a deterministic FPT
(7-2/k)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP.

Our second contribution is a novel parameterized approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP related to the parameters
k
,
Q
, and
mQ-\sum_{v\in J}q_{v}
, motivated by te mod-
Q
-cycle cover technique for the VRP in
Zhao and Xiao 2025a
.

In particular, we propose a parameterized
5
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP, denoted as
Alg.3
, with running time
\mathcal{O}(\min\{Q^{k-1},\binom{mQ-\sum_{v\in J}q_{v}+k-1}{k-1}\}\cdot|V|^{4}\log|V|)
.

Consequently, with respect to the parameter
k
,
Alg.3
runs in XP time when
Q=|V|^{\mathcal{O}(1)}
, runs in FPT time with a single-exponential dependence on
k
when
Q=\mathcal{O}(1)
, and becomes polynomial-time when
mQ-\sum_{v\in J}q_{v}=\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Notably, the SD instance set considered in
Lai et al. 2023
for the MD-SDVRP consists of 63 instances (21 instances for each
k=2,4,6
), and all of them satisfy
mQ-\sum_{v\in J}q_{v}=0
.
On every SD instance,
Alg.3
runs in
\mathcal{O}(|V|^{4}\log|V|)
time.

Our final contribution is to obtain (parameterized) bi-factor approximation algorithms for the MD-SDVRP (with respective to the parameter
k
).

We first present a bi-factor
(6+4/\varepsilon+2\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)
-approximation algorithm for the MD-SDVRP, denoted as
Alg.4
, which runs in polynomial time.
Moreover, we show that the underlying techniques can be adapted to obtain an
(\alpha+1-\varepsilon_{\alpha})
-approximation algorithm for the SDVRP, matching the best-known guarantee for the splittable VRP.

Preliminaries

In the MD-SDVRP, the input instance is represented by
I=(G,c,q,r,Q)
, where
G=(V,E)
is an undirected complete graph with vertex set
V=J\cup D
and edge set
E=V\times V
.
The set
J=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
represents the customers, and
D=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\}
represents the depots.
There is a cost function
c:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
The cost function
c
is a
metric
, which satisfies
c(x,y)=0
,
c(x,y)=c(y,x)
, and
c(x,y)\leq c(x,z)+c(y,z)
(triangle inequality) for all
x,y,z\in V
.
There is also a demand function
q:J\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
, where
q_{v}
is the demand of customer
v
.
Each depot
u
owns
r_{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
vehicles with the same capacity
Q\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
Let
L=\{\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{m}\}
denote the set of all vehicles, where
m=\sum_{u\in D}r_{u}
.
Each vehicle can satisfy at most
Q
demand.
Since there are
m
vehicles in total, we assume that

\sum_{v\in J}q_{v}\leq mQ.

(1)

A
walk

W
in
G
, denoted by
v_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{\ell}
, is a sequence of vertices in
V
, where a vertex may be repeated and each consecutive pair of vertices is connected by an edge.
Let
E(W)
denote the (multi-)set of edges it contains. Then, the cost of
W
is defined as
c(W)=\sum_{e\in E(W)}c(e)
.

A
path
is a walk where no vertex appears more than once.
The first and the last vertices of a path are referred to as its
terminals
.
A
closed
walk is a walk where the first and the last vertices are the same, and
a
cycle
is a walk where only the first and the last vertices are the same.
A cycle (or path)
C
containing a single vertex is called
trivial
, and for which we define
E(C)=\emptyset
.

Given a closed walk, by the triangle inequality, one can skip repeated vertices along the walk to obtain a cycle of non-increasing cost.
Such an operation is called
shortcutting
.
We define a
route
or
tour
for a vehicle from depot
u
to be a cycle in the form of
uv_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{\ell}u
, where each
v_{i}
is a customer.
A tour containing a single customer, e.g.,
uv_{1}u
, is called trivial.

A (perfect)
matching

M
in
G
is a set of vertex-disjoint edges that spans all vertices in
V
.
A
cycle cover

\mathcal{B}
in
G
is a set of vertex-disjoint cycles that spans all vertices in
V
.
Note that a tree (or cycle) may consist only of a single vertex.

For any (multi-)set of edges
E^{\prime}
, where each
e\in E^{\prime}
holds
e\in E
, let
G_{E^{\prime}}=(V,E^{\prime})
denote the corresponding (multi-)graph, which can be viewed as a set of components, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{E^{\prime}}
. (Recall that a component is a connected subgraph maximizing the sum of its number of vertices and edges.)

For any (multi-)graph
H=(V_{H},E_{H})
and any vertex subset
V_{s}\subseteq H
, let
H[V_{s}]
denote the subgraph of
H
induced by
V_{s}
.
In
H
, the
degree
of a vertex
v\in V_{H}
is the number of edges in
E_{H}
incident to it.
Let
Odd(H)
denote the set of odd-degree vertices in
V_{H}
.
The graph
H
is called an
even-degree graph
if
Odd(H)=\emptyset
.
Moreover,
H
is called an
Eulerian graph
if it is connected in addition to being even-degree.
Given an Eulerian graph
H
, an
Eulerian tour
, i.e., a closed walk that traverses each edge in
E_{H}
exactly once, can be computed in
\mathcal{O}(|V_{H}|+|E_{H}|)
time
Cormen et al. 2022
.

For any (multi-)graph
H
whose vertices belong to
V
, we use
V(H)
,
J(H)
,
D(H)
,
E(H)
, and
c(H)
to denote the vertex set, customer set, depot set, edge set, and total edge cost of
H
, respectively.
The same notation is used for any walk or tree, as well as for any set of walks, trees, or components.

For a component
C
, we may view it as a graph with vertex set
V(C)
and edge set
E(C)
.

We also consider a network
N=(V_{N},E_{N})
, where edges are directed and capacitated.
We let
\langle x,y\rangle_{z}
be a directed edge from vertex
x
to vertex
y
with capacity
\mbox{cap}\langle x,y\rangle=z\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.

2.1
Formal Problem Definition

The MD-SDVRP can be defined as follows.

Definition 1

(
The MD-SDVRP
)
.

Given
I=(G,c,q,r,Q)
, the goal is to construct a set of tours
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in L}
with a demand assignment
\lambda:V\times\mathcal{T}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
such that

1.

T_{\tau}
is a tour for vehicle
\tau\in L
,

2.

\sum_{v\in J(T)}\lambda_{v,T}\leq Q
for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

3.

\sum_{v\in J\setminus J(T)}\lambda_{v,T}=0
for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
,

4.

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\lambda_{v,T}=q_{v}
for each
v\in J
,

and
c(\mathcal{T})=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}c(T)
is minimized.

In the definition, each customer is allowed to be satisfied by multiple tours.
By the triangle inequality, we may also assume that
\lambda_{v,T}=0
if and only if
v\in J\setminus J(T)
.
Moreover, when
J(T_{\tau})\cap J=\emptyset
, the vehicle
\tau
deliveries no goods to customers. In this case, we say that
\tau
is
inactive
; otherwise we say it is
active
.

Fix an optimal solution
\mathcal{T}^{*}=\{T^{*}_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in L}
to the problem, and let
\mbox{OPT}=c(\mathcal{T}^{*})
denote its cost.
It is convenient to view the solution as a set of components
\mathcal{C}^{*}
.

For any component
C
, define
\ell(C)=\left\lceil\frac{\sum_{v\in J(C)}q_{v}}{Q}\right\rceil
, which is the minimum possible number of vehicles needed to satisfy all customers in
J(C)
. In addition, for any set of components
\mathcal{C}
, define
\ell(\mathcal{C})=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\ell(C)
.
Consequently, at least
\ell(\mathcal{C}^{*})
number of active vehicles exist in the fixed optimal solution, and hence we have
\ell(\mathcal{C}^{*})\leq m
.

With respect to
\mathcal{C}^{*}
, we call a set of components
\mathcal{C}
good if it satisfies the following condition: for any
C\in\mathcal{C}^{*}
, all vertices in
J(C)
are in the same component in
\mathcal{C}
.
Therefore, for any
C_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists a non-empty subset
\mathcal{C}^{*}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{*}
such that
J(C_{i})=\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}^{*}_{i}}J(C)
.
Hence, an immediate fact is that

\ell(\mathcal{C})\leq\ell(\mathcal{C}^{*})\leq m,

(2)

which also implies the following property.

Property 1
.

Given a set of components
\mathcal{C}
, if one uses
\ell(C)
vehicles to satisfy all customers in
J(C)
for each
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the total number of vehicles used is at most
m
when
\mathcal{C}
is good.

Section
3
presents the XP approximation algorithm
Alg.1
.
Section
4
presents the FPT approximation algorithm
Alg.2
.
Section
5
presents the parameterized approximation algorithm
Alg.3
.
Sections
6
and
7
presents the (parameterized) bi-factor approximation algorithms
Alg.4
and
Alg.5
, respectively.

3
An Improved XP Approximation Algorithm

In this section, we present an XP
(2\rho+3)
-approximation algorithm (
Alg.1
) for the MD-SDVRP, where
\rho
is any XP approximation ratio for the MD-TSP.

3.1
The Algorithm

Alg.1
applies an XP
\rho
-approximation algorithm for the MD-TSP as a black-box, so we may assume that such an algorithm is available and runs in
f_{k}(|V|)
time for some function
f
.

At the high-level,
Alg.1
first aims to compute a good set of components
\mathcal{C}
such that
\mathcal{C}
is also a cycle cover in
G
.
Then, it constructs a solution
\mathcal{T}
to the MD-SDVRP by using
\ell(C)
vehicles to satisfy all customers in
J(C)
for each component
C\in\mathcal{C}
, which uses at most
m
vehicles in total by Property
1
.
However, since
\mathcal{T}^{*}
is unknown, we do not know whether a set of components is good or not.

Alg.1
will try
|E|^{k-1}
possible sets of components, where we will show in Lemma
1
that there must exist one good set with small cost.
Hence,
Alg.1
computes a solution for each set and returns the best-found solution.

Next, we introduce the construction of component sets.

3.1.1
The Component Sets

Given the instance
I=(G,c,q,r,Q)
, one can modify it into an MD-TSP instance
I^{\prime}
by setting
Q\coloneq\infty
.

Alg.1
first applies an XP
\rho
-approximation algorithm for the MD-TSP to compute a solution to
I^{\prime}
.
By the triangle inequality, we assume w.l.o.g. that the solution is a set cover
\mathcal{B}
in
G
, i.e., at most one vehicle in each depot is active, and each customer is satisfied by only one vehicle.
Then,
Alg.1
enumerates an edge set
E_{J}
from the graph
G[J]
to obtain a set of components
\mathcal{C}_{E_{\mathcal{B}}}
formed by
E_{\mathcal{B}}=E(\mathcal{B})\cup E_{J}
such that

(1)

E(\mathcal{B})\cap E_{J}=\emptyset
;

(2)

E_{J}
consists of at most
k-1
different edges;

(3)

\ell(\mathcal{C}_{E_{\mathcal{B}}})\leq m
.

This incurs at most
|E|^{k-1}
possible
\mathcal{C}_{E_{\mathcal{B}}}
.
For each of them, it is transformed into a cycle cover in
G
in the following way.

Given
\mathcal{C}_{E_{\mathcal{B}}}
,

3.1.2
The Solution Constructions

Recall that
\mathcal{C}
is a cycle cover in
G
.
We may update it into a cycle cover in
G[J]
by shortcutting all depots in
D
.

Alg.1
uses
\ell(C)
vehicles to satisfy all customers in
J(C)
for each cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}
.
As shown in
Lai et al. 2023
, one possible approach is to greedily extract
\ell(C)
paths from each cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}
, where each of the first
\ell(C)-1
paths is assigned exactly
Q
demand and the last path is assigned
\sum_{v\in J(C)}q_{v}-(\ell(C)-1)\cdot Q
demand, followed by satisfying the assigned demand of each path using one vehicle.
The details of the path extraction from
C
are as follows.

Suppose
C=v_{1}\dots v_{j}v_{1}
. Let
q^{\prime}_{v_{i}}\coloneq q_{v_{i}}
,
q^{\prime}_{C}\coloneq\sum_{i=1}^{j}q^{\prime}_{v_{i}}
, and
p=i_{0}=1
.
One can do the following until
q^{\prime}_{C}=0
:

1.

find the minimum index
i_{p}
such that
i_{p-1}\leq i_{p}\leq j
and
\sum_{i=i_{p-1}}^{i_{p}}q^{\prime}_{v_{i}}\geq M
, where
M=\min\{Q,q^{\prime}_{C}\}
;

2.

extract a path
P=v_{i_{p-1}}\dots v_{i_{p}}
, and set
\lambda_{v_{i},P}=q^{\prime}_{v_{i}}
for each
i_{p-1}\leq i<i_{p}
, and
\lambda_{v_{i_{p}},P}=M-\sum_{i=i_{p-1}}^{i_{p}-1}q^{\prime}_{v_{i}}
;

3.

update
q^{\prime}_{v_{i}}\coloneq 0
for each
i<i_{p}
,
q^{\prime}_{v_{i_{p}}}\coloneq q^{\prime}_{v_{i_{p}}}-\lambda_{v_{i_{p}},P}
,
q^{\prime}_{C}\coloneq q^{\prime}_{C}-M
, and
p\coloneq p+1
.

Clearly, the above algorithm produces a set of path
\mathcal{P}_{C}
with
|\mathcal{P}_{C}|=\ell(C)
and a demand assignment
\lambda
such that the first
\ell(C)-1
paths are each assigned exactly
Q
demand and the last path is assigned at most
Q
demand.
Moreover, all customers in
J(C)
can be served by satisfying the assigned demand of each path in
\mathcal{P}_{C}
.

Hence, there are
\ell(\mathcal{C})
paths in
\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{P}_{C}
.
By satisfying the demand of every path, one would obtain a feasible solution to the MD-SDVRP using exactly
\ell(\mathcal{C})
vehicles.

Note that each demand
q_{v}
may be super-polynomial in
|V|
; so the above method takes pseudo-polynomial time.
Hence, we apply a different method in
Alg.1
.

Consider a cycle
C\in\mathcal{C}
.
For each
v\in J(C)
with
q_{v}\geq Q
, we extract a trivial path
v
with
load

\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor
, which means that the demand of this trivial path needs to be satisfied using
\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor
vehicles, i.e., it corresponds to
\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor
trivial paths, each with demand
Q
.
Hence, let
q^{\prime}_{v}\coloneq q_{v}-\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor\cdot Q
.
Using the residual demand
q^{\prime}_{v}
, we then apply the previous path extraction method to obtain a set of paths
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}
with
|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}|=\lceil\frac{\sum_{v\in J(C)}q^{\prime}_{v}}{Q}\rceil
together with the corresponding assignment
\lambda^{\prime}_{v,P}
for each
P\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
and
v\in J(P)
.
Let the set of trivial paths be
J_{C}=\{v\in J(C)\mid q_{v}\geq Q\}
.
Then, all customers in
J(C)
can be served by satisfying all paths in
J_{C}\cup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}
.
Note that the total number of vehicles required to satisfy all paths in
J_{C}\cup\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}
is
\sum_{v\in J_{C}}\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor+|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}|
, which equals
\ell(C)
.
Thus,

\ell(C)=\sum_{v\in J_{C}}\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor+|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}|.

(3)

Let
J_{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}J_{C}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcup_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}
. Then, to obtain a solution, we need to match each path in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{C}}
with a single vehicle and each trivial path
v\in J_{\mathcal{C}}
with
\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor
vehicles.
We reduce this task to a minimum-cost maximum flow problem (MCMFP) as follows.

First, we construct a network
N=(V_{N},E_{N})
, where we initialize
V_{N}\coloneq\{s,t\}\cup D
and
E_{N}\coloneq\{\langle u,t\rangle_{r_{u}}\mid u\in D\}
.
Then, for each trivial path

, we update
V_{N}\coloneq V_{N}\cup\{v\}
and
E_{N}\coloneq E_{N}\cup\{\langle s,v\rangle_{\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor}\}
;
for each path

and

Finally, for each
v\in J_{\mathcal{C}}

Moreover, we define

and
c\langle P,u\rangle=\min_{v^{\prime}\in J(P)}c(v^{\prime},u)
; for all other edges, we define the cost to be zero.

Recall that
\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}(\sum_{v\in J_{C}}\lfloor\frac{q_{v}}{Q}\rfloor+|\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{C}|)=\ell(\mathcal{C})
by (
3

In

to

Since all edge capacities are integers, there is an optimal solution

such that

and

for each

This solution implies that, for each trivial path

(resp., each path

In particular, for each trivial path

For each path

\mathcal{B}^{*}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{t}\}

For each

Consider the first constraint.
Note that

The previous two algorithms both rely on enumerating a good component set with small cost.

In addition, we have the following property.

Property 2

It holds that

Since
\mathcal{T}^{*}

7
The Second Bi-factor Approximation Algorithm

In this section, we present the second bi-factor approximation algorithm (
Alg.5
) for the MD-SDVRP, which yields an XP bi-factor approximation ratio of
(5,1+\varepsilon)
with running time
\mathcal{O}(\lceil 1+2/\varepsilon\rceil^{k-1}|V|^{k+3}\log|V|)
.

7.1
The Algorithm

Alg.5
is based on
Alg.3
with some modifications.
The key idea is to apply some scaling techniques to the vehicle capacity as well as the customers’ demand to obtain a new instance with small vehicle capacity, and then apply
Alg.3
to the new instance.
Due to the scaling, the solution returned by
Alg.3
may slightly violate the vehicle capacity constraint of the original instance, yielding a bi-factor approximation algorithm.

Given an MD-SDVRP instance
I
,
Alg.5
first constructs a new instance
I^{\prime}
by scaling each customer
v
’s demand as
\widetilde{q}_{v}\coloneq\left\lceil\frac{q_{v}}{\varepsilon\cdot Q/n}\right\rceil
, and the vehicle capacity as
\widetilde{Q}\coloneq n\cdot\left\lceil 1+1/\varepsilon\right\rceil
.

Then,
Alg.5
applies
Alg.3
to
I^{\prime}
to compute a solution
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
together with a demand assignment
\widetilde{\lambda}_{v,T}
.
Finally,

simply returns

as the final solution, but uses the new assignment
\lambda_{v,T}\coloneq\widetilde{\lambda}_{v,T}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon\cdot Q}{n}
.

Note that for some customer

, it may hold that

; that is, the total demand served for

may exceed its original demand

.
This can be trivially corrected by decreasing the assignments so that

for all

.
Hence, in the following, we allow that customers’ demand may be over-satisfied since it can be corrected easily.

Moreover, for the solution
\mathcal{T}
, its demand assignment
\lambda_{v,T}
may not be integral.
However, since customers’ demand and the vehicle capacity are all integers, one can convert
\lambda_{v,T}
into an integral assignment without increasing the solution cost by the triangle inequality, for example by using techniques for eliminating split cycles
Dror and Trudeau 1990
.

The details of

are provided in Algorithm

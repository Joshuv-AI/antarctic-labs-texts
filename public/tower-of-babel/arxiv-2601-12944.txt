On the Concavity of Tsallis Entropy along the Heat Flow
Lukang Sun

Abstract
We demonstrate the concavity of the Tsallis entropy along the heat flow for general dimensions, expanding upon the findings of [ 6 , 16 ] , which were previously limited to the one-dimensional case. The proof’s core is a novel estimate of the terms in the second-order time derivative, and a rigorous validation of integration by parts. The resulting bound establishes a new functional inequality, which may be of interest for other areas of mathematical analysis.

Introduction

,

[
9

and the included references. Tsallis entropy (see Definition

) is a non-additive generalization of the standard Shannon entropy, designed to characterize complex systems with long-range correlations, non-equilibrium states, or multi-fractal structures. Its importance spans information theory (
[
13
,
2
]
), statistical physics (
[
12
,
10
]
), chemistry (
[
5
,
11
]
) and etc. In this work, we investigate the behavior of the Tsallis entropy along the heat flow, specifically the concavity of Tsallis entropy along the heat flow. Recent studies by
[
6
,
16
]
explored this problem, determining the signs of temporal derivatives up to the fourth order, however, their results were restricted to the one-dimensional case. The higher-dimensional analysis deviates from the one-dimensional case as outlined in Remark
9
; the novelty of our study lies in the distinct analytical methods required to resolve the higher-dimensional cases.

A
,
\|A\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{2}
denotes the squared Frobenius norm; for a vector
v
,
\|v\|^{2}=\sum_{i}v_{i}^{2}

II
Problem Set Up and Main Results

Given a non-negative initial density
\phi_{0}
with
\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}=1
, define heat flow
\phi_{t}:=\phi_{0}*p_{t}
, where
*
denotes the convolution operator and
p_{t}(x):=(4\pi t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}
represents the standard Gaussian heat kernel. It follows that
\phi_{t}
is the solution to the heat equation:

\partial_{t}\phi_{t}=\Delta\phi_{t},\quad\lim_{t\to 0}\phi_{t}=\phi_{0}.

(1)

It can be verified that for any
t>0
, the above defined
\phi_{t}
is positive with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\phi_{t}}}\right\|_{L^{1}}=1
and smooth on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Definition 1

For a probability density function
\rho
, the Tsallis entropy with entropic index
q\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)
is defined as:

S_{q}(\rho)=\frac{1-\int\rho^{q}(x),dx}{q-1}.

(2)

In the limit as
q\rightarrow 1
,
S_{q}(\rho)
converges to the Shannon entropy (or Boltzmann-Gibbs entropy):

H(\rho)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)\log\rho(x),dx.

(3)

Now, we can state the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 2

Along the heat flow
\phi_{t}
, the Tsallis entropy
S_{q}(\phi_{t})
is concave in time, satisfying:

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}S_{q}(\phi_{t})\leq 0,

(4)

for the entropic index
q
in the following ranges:

•

q\in[1,3]
in the one-dimensional case (
d=1
);

•

q\in[1,\frac{2(\sqrt{5}+1)}{\sqrt{5}}]
(
\frac{2(\sqrt{5}+1)}{\sqrt{5}}\approx 2.894
) for the multidimensional case (
d>1
).

While the concavity of Tsallis entropy for
q\in[1,3]
was proven in dimension one by
[
6
,
16
]
, our approach deviates from their techniques. Crucially, our method remains robust in higher-dimensional contexts, where previous one-dimensional arguments may not apply.

To prove the theorem, we introduce a transformation of
\phi_{t}
that allows for a unified calculation and estimation of the temporal derivatives of the Tsallis entropy under the heat equation.

Let
(u_{\delta})_{t}:=\phi_{t}^{p},p=\frac{1}{1+\delta}
, then Theorem
2
is equivalent to show
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\int(u_{\delta})_{t}^{2}dx\geq 0
, for
\delta
in the following ranges:

•

\delta\in[-\frac{1}{3},1)
in the one-dimensional case (
d=1
);

•

\delta\in[-\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}+1},1)
for the multidimensional case (
d>1
).

The case
\delta=1
recovers the classic result for the Shannon entropy, specifically
\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}H(\phi_{t})\leq 0
(see
[
14
]
). This is analytically equivalent to the monotonicity of the Fisher information, denoted as
\frac{d}{dt}\int\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla(u_{1})_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dx\leq 0
, for which we provide an alternative derivation (see the derivation of (
73
)).

In the following sections, we let
u_{t}:=(u_{\delta})_{t}
without loss of generality, as the parameter dependence on
\delta
is fixed and implicit throughout the analysis. We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3

We have
u_{t}
satisfies the following non-linear equation

\partial_{t}u_{t}=\Delta u_{t}+\delta\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}{u_{t}}.

(5)

We note that although the transformation introduces a non-linearity into Equation (
5
), it remains advantageous because the equation retains a linear dependence on the parameter
\delta
.

Proof:

The proof follows from direct computation. We have

\partial_{t}u_{t}=p\phi_{t}^{p-1}\partial_{t}\phi_{t}=p\phi_{t}^{p-1}\Delta\phi_{t},

(6)

the gradient of
u_{t}
is

\nabla u_{t}=p\phi_{t}^{p-1}\nabla\phi_{t}\implies\nabla\phi_{t}=\frac{1}{p}\phi_{t}^{1-p}\nabla u_{t},

(7)

the Laplacian of
u_{t}
is

\displaystyle\Delta u_{t}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot(p\phi_{t}^{p-1}\nabla\phi_{t})=p(p-1)\phi_{t}^{p-2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\phi_{t}}}\right\|^{2}+p\phi_{t}^{p-1}\Delta\phi_{t}.

(8)

Substitute the time derivative from Equation (
6
) into Equation (
8
), we have

\Delta u_{t}=p(p-1)\phi_{t}^{p-2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\phi_{t}}}\right\|^{2}+\partial_{t}u_{t}.

(9)

Substitute
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\phi_{t}}}\right\|^{2}=\frac{1}{p^{2}}\phi_{t}^{2-2p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}
into the equation, we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{t}

\displaystyle=\Delta u_{t}-p(p-1)\phi_{t}^{p-2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\phi_{t}^{2-2p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}}\right)

(10)

\displaystyle=\Delta u_{t}-\frac{p-1}{p}\phi_{t}^{-p}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}

(11)

Since
\phi_{t}^{-p}=\frac{1}{u_{t}}
and
p=\frac{1}{1+\delta}
, the coefficient is

-\frac{p-1}{p}=\frac{1-p}{p}=\frac{1}{p}-1=(1+\delta)-1=\delta,

(12)

therefore,
u_{t}
satisfies

\partial_{t}u_{t}=\Delta u_{t}+\delta\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}{u_{t}}.

(13)

∎

III
Verification of Integration by Parts

Further analysis of Equation (
5
) relies on the application of integration by parts. To ensure the validity of these operations—specifically the vanishing of boundary terms at infinity—we establish a series of preliminary estimates.
Throughout the rest of this paper, we assume the parameter
\delta
lies in the interval
(-1,1]
. The rigorous justification for these integration-by-parts identities is formalized in Proposition
7
, which serves as the primary objective of this subsection. To establish this result, we first derive several necessary auxiliary bounds.

Lemma 4

For any
t>0
and
p>1
, let
q
satisfy the conjugate exponent relation
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
. The following pointwise estimate holds for the derivatives of the density
\phi_{t}
:

\max_{i,j,k}\{\frac{|\partial_{i}\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x),\frac{|\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x),\frac{|\partial_{ijk}\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x)\}\leq\Big(\int Q^{q}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(14)

where
p_{t}(x)
denotes the heat kernel and
Q_{t}(x)
is a polynomial in
x
with time-dependent coefficients.

Proof:

Since

\phi_{t}=\int p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy,

(15)

so by direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\phi_{t}

\displaystyle=\int Q_{i}(x-y,t^{-1})p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy

(16)

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}

\displaystyle=\int Q_{ij}(x-y,t^{-1})p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy

\displaystyle\partial_{ijk}\phi_{t}

\displaystyle=\int Q_{ijk}(x-y,t^{-1})p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy,

for some polynomials
Q_{i},Q_{ij},Q_{ijk}
. We will denote

Q_{t}(x-y)=\sum_{i,j,k}|Q_{i}(x-y,t^{-1})|+|Q_{ij}(x-y,t^{-1})|+|Q_{ijk}(x-y,t^{-1})|,

(17)

then

\displaystyle\max_{i,j,k}\{|\partial_{i}\phi_{t}|,|\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}|,|\partial_{ijk}\phi_{t}|\}

\displaystyle\leq\int Q_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}dy

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int Q^{q}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\Big(\int p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int Q^{q}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}},

where in the second inequality we used the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.
Thus we have

\max_{i,j,k}\{\frac{|\partial_{i}\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}}},\frac{|\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}}},\frac{|\partial_{ijk}\phi_{t}|}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{p}}}\}\leq\Big(\int Q^{q}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},

(19)

for any
t>0,p>1
and
q
satisfies
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1
.
∎

In order to simplify the upper bound in (
14
), we introduce the following lemma, which establishes the necessary integral estimates.

Lemma 5

We have

\int\Big(\int Q^{s}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{p}dx<\infty,

(20)

for any constant
s\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
.

Proof:

Since
\int\phi_{0}(y)dy=1
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\int Q^{s}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\int Q^{{sp}}_{t}(x-y)p^{{p}}_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy

(21)

\displaystyle=C(t,d,p)\int Q^{{sp}}_{t}(x-y)p_{\frac{t}{p}}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy,

here
C(t,d,p)=\frac{(4\pi tp^{-1})^{{d}/{2}}}{(4\pi t)^{{dp}/{2}}}
by the definition of the heat kernel.
Thus

\displaystyle\int\Big(\int Q^{s}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{{p}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C(t,d,p)\int\int Q^{{sp}}_{t}(x-y)p_{\frac{t}{p}}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dydx

(22)

\displaystyle\leq C(t,d,p)\Big(\int Q^{{sp}}_{t}(x)p_{\frac{t}{p}}(x)dx\Big)\Big(\int\phi_{0}(x)dx\Big)

\displaystyle<\infty.

The second inequality follows from the Young’s convolution inequality for
L^{1}
functions, specifically
\|{f}*{g}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|{f}\|_{L^{1}}\|{g}\|_{L^{1}}
. The final inequality is a consequence of the fact that all moments of the heat kernel are finite and uniformly bounded for fixed
t>0
.
∎

By combining the results of the two preceding lemmas, we establish the following result:

Lemma 6

We have

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{i}{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ij}{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ijk}{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,

(23)

for any
p\geq 1+\delta,t>0
; and

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{i}\sqrt{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ij}\sqrt{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ijk}\sqrt{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,

(24)

for any
p\geq 2(1+\delta),t>0
.

Proof:

Recalling the definition
u_{t}:=\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}
, a direct computation yields:

\displaystyle\partial_{i}u_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}},

(25)

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}{u_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta}\frac{\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}}{\phi^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}_{t}}-\frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2\delta+1}{2(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{j}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2\delta+1}{2(1+\delta)}}},

\displaystyle\partial_{ijk}{u_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\delta}\frac{\partial_{ijk}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{\delta}{1+\delta}}}-\frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2\delta+1}{2(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{k}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2\delta+1}{2(1+\delta)}}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{ik}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{j}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}}-\frac{\delta}{(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{jk}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta(1+2\delta)}{(1+\delta)^{3}}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2+3\delta}{3(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{j}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2+3\delta}{3(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{k}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{2+3\delta}{3(1+\delta)}}},

thus with Lemma
4
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}u_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{1+\delta}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}

(26)

\displaystyle|\partial_{ij}{u_{t}}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{1+\delta}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle+C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{2(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle|\partial_{ijk}u_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{1+\delta}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle+C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{2(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}}

\displaystyle+C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{3(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{1+\delta}},

with Lemma
5
, we further have

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{i}{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ij}{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ijk}{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,

(27)

for any
p\geq 1+\delta
and
t>0
.

The second part is similar.
Since
\sqrt{u_{t}}:=\phi_{t}^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}}
, a direct computation yields:

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\sqrt{u_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}},

(28)

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}\sqrt{u_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}\frac{\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}}{\phi^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}_{t}}-\frac{1+2\delta}{4(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{j}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}},

\displaystyle\partial_{ijk}\sqrt{u_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}\frac{\partial_{ijk}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{1+2\delta}{2(1+\delta)}}}-\frac{1+2\delta}{4(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{ij}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{k}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1+2\delta}{4(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{ik}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{j}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}-\frac{1+2\delta}{4(1+\delta)^{2}}\frac{\partial_{jk}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{3+4\delta}{4(1+\delta)}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(1+2\delta)(3+4\delta)}{8(1+\delta)^{3}}\frac{\partial_{i}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{5+6\delta}{6(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{j}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{5+6\delta}{6(1+\delta)}}}\frac{\partial_{k}\phi_{t}}{\phi_{t}^{\frac{5+6\delta}{6(1+\delta)}}},

thus with Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\sqrt{u_{t}}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{2(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}},

(29)

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{2(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}}

\displaystyle+C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{4(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}}

\displaystyle|\partial_{ijk}\sqrt{u_{t}}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{2(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}}

\displaystyle+C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{4(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}}

\displaystyle+C(\delta)\Big(\int Q^{6(1+\delta)}_{t}(x-y)p_{t}(x-y)\phi_{0}(y)dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{2(1+\delta)}},

with Lemma

, we further have

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{i}\sqrt{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ij}\sqrt{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,\int|\partial_{ijk}\sqrt{u_{t}}|^{p}dx<\infty,

(30)

for any
p\geq 2(1+\delta)
and

.
∎

Equipped with these preliminary bounds, we are positioned to verify the vanishing of boundary integrals, thereby ensuring the validity of the integration-by-parts operations.

Proposition 7

Under Lemma
6
, we have

\int u_{t}\Delta u_{t}dx=-\int\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dx,\int\Delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dx=0,\int\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nabla\Delta u_{t},\nabla u_{t}}}\right>dx=-\int\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Delta u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dx,

(31)

and

\int\Delta u_{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{2}dx=-\int\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left<\nabla u_{t},\nabla\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{2}}}\right>dx=\int u_{t}\Delta\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{2}dx.

(32)

Proof:

Applying integration by parts over the cube
Q_{R}(0):=[-R,R]^{d}
, we must demonstrate that the resulting boundary integrals vanish in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
. Specifically, we are required to verify that:

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial n}}}\right\|u_{t}dS\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\Big[\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS+\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}u_{t}^{2}dS\Big]\to 0,

(33)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}{\partial n}}}\right\|dS\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\Big[\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS+\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS\Big]\to 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial n}\Delta u_{t}}}\right\|dS\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\Big[\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS+\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS\Big]\to 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial n}}}\right\|\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{2}dS\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\Big[\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS+\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{4}dS\Big]\to 0,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}u_{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\frac{\partial\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{2}}{\partial n}}}\right\|dS\leq\lim_{R\to\infty}\Big[\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}u_{t}^{2}dS+\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla^{2}\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{4}dS+\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla\sqrt{u_{t}}}}\right\|^{4}dS\Big]\to 0,

where we have employed the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and
n
denotes the unit outward normal vector to the boundary
\partial Q_{R}(0)
.

By the trace theorem (see
[
4
, Section 5.5]
), there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of both
y
and
f
, such that for any
f\in W^{1,p}(Q_{1/2}(y))
with
p\geq 1
, we have

\int_{\partial Q_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|f}}\right\|^{p}dS\leq C\int_{Q_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|f}}\right\|^{p}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla f}}\right\|^{p}dx,

(34)

here
Q_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)=y+[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
. Consider a tiling of the annular region
Q_{R}(0)\setminus Q_{R-1}(0)
by a finite collection of essentially disjoint cubes
\{Q_{1/2}(y_{i})\}_{i}
, such that
Q_{R}(0)\setminus Q_{R-1}(0)=\bigcup_{i}Q_{1/2}(y_{i})
and
\text{int}(Q_{1/2}(y_{i}))\cap\text{int}(Q_{1/2}(y_{j}))=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. Utilizing this partition and the trace inequality (
34
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\partial Q_{R}(0)}{u_{t}^{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\int_{\partial Q_{\frac{1}{2}}(y_{i})}{u_{t}^{2}}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla^{2}u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dS

(35)

Lemma 8

We have

3\int\Delta u_{t}\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}{u_{t}}dx=\int\frac{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla u_{t}}}\right\|^{4}}{{u_{t}^{2}}}dx+8\int u_{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\nabla^{2}v_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dx-8\int u_{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|\Delta v_{t}}}\right\|^{2}dx,

In the one-dimensional case (

V
Conclusion

We establish the concavity of the Tsallis entropy (
S_{q}
) along the heat flow for specific ranges of the entropic index
q
. Our proof relies on a rigorous verification of integration-by-parts identities and a novel estimate of the terms appearing in the second-order temporal derivatives of the entropy. Furthermore, this estimate allows us to derive a new functional inequality (see Remark
10
), which may be of independent theoretical interest.

The methodology in Section
IV
is formally applicable to the evolution of Tsallis relative entropy along overdamped Langevin dynamics. However, establishing the analytical rigor for integration-by-parts in this setting presents distinct challenges, and is therefore left for subsequent studies.

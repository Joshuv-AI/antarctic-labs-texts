Global higher integrability and Hardy inequalities for double-phase functionals under a capacity density condition
Fabian Bäuerlein, Leah Kurki Schätzler, Samuele Riccò

Abstract
. We prove global higher integrability for functionals of double-phase type under a uniform local capacity density condition on the complement of the considered domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n} . In this context, we investigate a new natural notion of variational capacity associated to the double-phase integrand. Under the related fatness condition for the complement of Ω \Omega , we establish an integral Hardy inequality. Further, we show that fatness of ℝ n ∖ Ω \mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega is equivalent to a boundary Poincaré inequality, a pointwise Hardy inequality and to the local uniform p p -fatness of ℝ n ∖ Ω \mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega . We provide a counterexample that shows that the expected Maz’ya type inequality–a key intermediate step toward global higher integrability–does not hold with the notion of capacity involving the double-phase functional itself. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 31B15, 35J66, 35J70, 49N60 Keywords and phrases: double-phase functional, variational capacity, boundary Poincaré inequality, Hardy inequality, global higher integrability

Introduction

In the present paper, we consider the global higher integrability of quasi-minimizers to functionals of double-phase type in a bounded and open set
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
.
To this end, not only the regularity of the boundary datum, but also the regularity of the underlying domain itself is crucial.
In the special case of minimizers of the
p

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,

[

Thus, the question arises what the optimal condition on

\varphi(x,t)=t^{p}+a(x)t^{q},

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi(x,|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{\cp_{\varphi}\left(\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(z,r)\right)}\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

This led us to introduce the concepts of infimal variational
\varphi

First, for a general open set

a_{O}^{-}:=\inf_{x\in O}a(x)\quad\text{and}\quad a_{O}^{+}:=\sup_{x\in O}a(x)

\varphi_{O}^{-}(t):=t^{p}+a_{O}^{-}t^{q}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{O}^{+}(t):=t^{p}+a_{O}^{+}t^{q}.

For an open ball

with center

a_{z,r}^{\pm}:=a_{B(z,r)}^{\pm}

\varphi_{z,r}^{\pm}:=\varphi_{B(z,r)}^{\pm}

In the

]

Definition 1.1
.

Let
E

and consider a level

. Then the variational

-capacity

\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O):=\inf\left\{\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)}{\varphi_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x:u\in\Lip(\overline{O}),u\geq t\chi_{E},\left.u\right|_{\partial O}=0\right\}

and the
infimal
\varphi
-capacity
of
E
in
\Omega
is given by

\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}(E;O):=\inf_{t>0}\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O).

At this stage, we define local infimal
\varphi
-fatness analogously to
[

\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}

Definition 1.2
.

A closed set
E\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
is called

\varphi
-fat
at a point

and a radius

(1.4)

\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}}^{\inf}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)\geq c_{\mathrm{fat}}

for any radius
0<r<r_{o}
, where
\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}\colon B(0,1)\times[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is defined by

(1.5)

\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}(x,t):=\varphi(z+rx,t)=t^{p}+a(z+rx)t^{q}.

If there is a set
S\subset E
such that

is locally infimally
\varphi
-fat at all

with the same parameters

and

, we call

\varphi
-fat at

\varphi
-fat

Theorem 1.3

(Integral Hardy Inequality)
.

Let
\varphi
be defined according to (
1.1
) such that (

) and (

) hold with

\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set such that its complement

\varphi
-fat with a radius

Then, there exists a constant

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi

\displaystyle\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{\dist(x,\partial\Omega)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(1+\diam(\Omega)^{\alpha+\frac{n(p-q)}{p}}[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla u(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x

Since the local uniform infimal
\varphi
-fatness is self-improving, see Section

\Omega

However, we are able to deal with a weaker condition based on a combination of

While it is probably still not optimal, our method is flexible enough to consider quasi-minimizers of general functionals of double-phase type

(1.6)

\mathcal{F}\colon W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty],\qquad\mathcal{F}[u;\Omega]:=\int_{\Omega}F(x,u,\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where the integrand

is a Carathéodory function that satisfies the inequality

(1.7)

\nu\,\varphi(x,\xi)\leq F(x,u,\xi)\leq L\,\varphi(x,\xi)\qquad\forall\,(x,u,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathds{R}\times\mathds{R}^{n}

with constants

Instead of (

\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{\alpha}{n}.

Remark 1.6
.

In order for the class of functionals given by (
1.6
) to take only finite values, the right class of functions to consider is the Orlicz-Sobolev space, see
[
7
, Definition 3]
,

W^{1,\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in W^{1,1}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega)\ :\ f,|\nabla f|\in L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)\right\}.

The space
L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is called
generalized Orlicz space
, also known as Musielak–Orlicz space, see
[
7
, Definition 2]
, and it is given by

L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{0}(\Omega)\ :\ \lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\varrho_{\varphi(\cdot)}(\lambda f)=0\right\},

where
L^{0}(\Omega)
is the set of measurable functions
f\colon\Omega\to[-\infty,+\infty]
and

\varrho_{\varphi(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|f(x)|)\,\mathrm{d}x

is the modular for any chosen
f\in L^{0}(\Omega)
. Note that in general these spaces can be defined for a wide class of functions, called generalized
\Phi
-functions, and the double-phase integrand (
1.1
) is one of them. In particular, for the double-phase function, the space
L^{\varphi(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is a Banach space, see
[
25
, Theorem 2.3.13]
.

[

, Lemma 2.14]
.
Further, tracking the constants in the proof of Gehring’s lemma, i.e. Lemma
4.8
, shows that we may choose

\sigma=\min\left\{\sigma_{0},1+\frac{1}{c\left(1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathds{R}^{n})}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,\mathds{R}^{n})}\big)^{\beta(q-p)}\right)}\right\}

for some constant
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,\nu,L,c_{\mathrm{fat}},C_{Q})\geq 1
and
\beta=\beta(n,p,q,\alpha)\in(0,1)
.
In particular, if
a
is constant, then
\sigma
does not depend on
\nabla u
.

As a topic for future research, it would be interesting to investigate the notion of infimal
\varphi
-capacity for more general generalized
\Phi
-functions
\varphi
, see Remark
1.6
, and to extend our main results to this setting.

Next, in Section

2.
Preliminaries on infimal capacity and capacity density condition

In this section we prove some preliminary results regarding the infimal
\varphi
-capacity and the infimal
\varphi
-fatness condition, with a particular focus on the equivalence between the different definitions of local infimal
\varphi
-fatness and local
p
-fatness.

First, the following truncation lemma shows that it is enough to consider test functions with values in
[0,t]
in the definition of the level-
t
-capacity.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E
be a compact subset of an open set
O\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
and
t>0
.
Then, we have that

\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)=\inf\left\{\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)}{\varphi^{-}_{O}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x:u\in\Lip(\overline{O},[0,t]),u\geq t\chi_{E},\left.u\right|_{\partial O}=0\right\}.

Proof.
On the one hand, by definition of
\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)
it is clear that

\displaystyle\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)\leq\inf\left\{\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)}{\varphi^{-}_{O}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x:u\in\Lip(\overline{O},[0,t]),u\geq t\chi_{E},\left.u\right|_{\partial O}=0\right\}.

On the other hand, assume that the family of admissible test functions for
\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)
, i.e.

\mathcal{A}^{t}_{E;O}:=\left\{u\in\Lip(\overline{O}),u\geq t\chi_{E},\left.u\right|_{\partial O}=0\right\},

is not empty, fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{A}^{t}_{E;O}
such that

\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|)}{\varphi^{-}_{O}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)+\varepsilon.

Observe that
u_{t,\varepsilon}:=\min\{u_{\varepsilon},t\}\in\Lip(\overline{O},[0,t])
with
u_{t,\varepsilon}\geq t\chi_{E}
,
\left.u_{t,\varepsilon}\right|_{\partial O}=0
and
|\nabla u_{t,\varepsilon}|\leq|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|
(see for instance
[
42
, Theorem 2.25]
for this property of truncated Sobolev functions).
Together with the fact that
s\mapsto\varphi(x,s)
is increasing, this implies that

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)}{\varphi^{-}_{O}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x:u\in\Lip(\overline{O},[0,t]),u\geq t\chi_{E},\left.u\right|_{\partial O}=0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u_{t,\varepsilon}|)}{\varphi^{-}_{O}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|)}{\varphi^{-}_{O}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we prove the following upper bound for the infimal
\varphi
-capacity.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any compact subset
E
of an open set
O\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
and for any
1<p<+\infty
, we have that

\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}(E;O)\leq\cp_{p}(E;O).

Proof.
Note that
u
is an admissible comparison function for
\cp_{\varphi}^{1}(E;O)
if and only if
tu
is an admissible comparison function for
\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)
, with
t>0
. Thus, for an admissible function
u
for
\cp_{\varphi}^{1}(E;O)
, we find that

\displaystyle\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla(tu)|)}{\varphi_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{O}\frac{t^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+at^{q}|\nabla u|^{q}}{t^{p}+a_{O}^{-}t^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{O}\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}+at^{q-p}|\nabla u|^{q}}{1+a_{O}^{-}t^{q-p}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+t^{q-p}\int_{O}a|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, for any admissible
u
for
\cp_{\varphi}^{1}(E;O)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}(E;O)

\displaystyle=\inf_{t>0}\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{t>0}\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla(tu)|)}{\varphi_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{t>0}\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+t^{q-p}\int_{O}a|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Taking the infimum over all admissible test functions
u
for
\cp_{\varphi}^{1}(E;O)
(note that the left-hand side of the preceding inequality does not depend on
u
), we obtain the claim of the lemma.
∎

Further, we have the following lower bound.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
E
be a compact subset of a bounded, open set
O\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
and let
1<p<q<+\infty
.
Then we have that

\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}(E;O)\geq c(p,q)\min\left\{\cp_{p}(E;O),|O|\left(\frac{\cp_{p}(E;O)}{|O|}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}.

Proof.
Assume that
u\in\Lip(\overline{O})
with
u=0
on
\partial O
and
u\geq\chi_{E}
.
Since for any fixed
x\in O
and
t>0

\frac{1}{1+a(x)t^{q-p}}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{a(x)t^{q-p}}{1+a(x)t^{q-p}}|\nabla u|^{q}

is a convex combination of
|\nabla u|^{p}
and
|\nabla u|^{q}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla(tu)|)}{\varphi_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{O}\frac{t^{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+at^{q}|\nabla u|^{q}}{t^{p}+at^{q}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{O}\left[\frac{1}{1+at^{q-p}}|\nabla u|^{p}+\frac{at^{q-p}}{1+at^{q-p}}|\nabla u|^{q}\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\int_{O}\min\{|\nabla u|^{p},|\nabla u|^{q}\}\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{O}\left[|\nabla u|^{p}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|\geq 1\}}+|\nabla u|^{q}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|<1\}}\right]\mathrm{d}x.

By Hölder’s inequality, we find that

\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{q}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|<1\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\geq|O|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|<1\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}.

Inserting this into the right-hand side of (
2.1
), we infer

\displaystyle\int_{O}\frac{\varphi(x,|\nabla(tu)|)}{\varphi_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|\geq 1\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|\geq 1\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}-1}}+\frac{\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|<1\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{|O|^{\frac{q}{p}-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(p,q)\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{\max\left\{\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\chi_{\{|\nabla u|\geq 1\}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}-1},|O|^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\right\}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(p,q)\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}}{\max\left\{\left(\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q}{p}-1},|O|^{\frac{q}{p}-1}\right\}}

\displaystyle=c(p,q)\min\left\{\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,|O|\left(\frac{\int_{O}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x}{|O|}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq c(p,q)\min\left\{\cp_{p}(E;O),|O|\left(\frac{\cp_{p}(E;O)}{|O|}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}.

Taking the infimum over all admissible functions in the definition of
\cp_{\varphi}^{t}(E;O)
on the left-hand side of the preceding inequality and thereafter taking the infimum over
t>0
yields the claim of the lemma.
∎

In Definition
1.2
, we defined the infimal
\varphi
-fatness using the auxiliary functions
\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}
.
In
[
42
]
on the other hand, a closed set
E\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
satisfies a uniform
p
-capacity density condition if there exists
\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}>0
such that

(2.2)

\cp_{p}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)\geq\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

holds for any radius
r>0
and any point
z\in E
.
However, a simple change of variables shows that

(2.3)

\cp_{p}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)=r^{n-p}\cp_{p}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big).

Noting that

(2.4)

c(n,p)r^{n-p}\leq\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)\leq c(n)r^{n-p}

(see e.g.
[
42
, Lemma 5.35]
), we conclude that the definition of
p
-fatness relying on (
2.2
) is equivalent to the notion of
p
-fatness that results from choosing the special case
\varphi(s)=s^{p}
in Definition
1.2
.

In the next lemma, we establish the analogous characterization for infimal
\varphi
-fatness.
To this end, we prove that for any
z\in E
, where
E
denotes a closed set, (
1.4
) is equivalent to the condition

(2.5)

\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)\geq\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

for any
0<r<\tilde{r}_{o}\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be closed and let
z\in E
. Then (
1.4
) is satisfied with constants
0<r_{o}\leq 1
and
c_{\mathrm{fat}}
if and only if (
2.5
) is satisfied with constants
0<\tilde{r}_{o}\leq 1
and
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}
. The constants
r_{o}
and
\tilde{r}_{o}
, and
c_{\mathrm{fat}}
and
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}
, respectively, only depend on
n
,
p
,
q
and each other.

Proof.
Let
\varphi(x,s):=s^{p}+a(x)s^{q}
, and note that
a_{z,r}^{-}=\inf_{y\in B(z,r)}a(y)=\inf_{x\in B(0,1)}a(z+rx)
and thus

\varphi_{z,r}^{-}(t)=\widetilde{\varphi}_{0,1}^{-}(t):=t^{p}+\left(\inf_{x\in B(0,1)}a(z+rx)\right)t^{q}.

Further, we find that
u\in\Lip(B(0,1))
if and only if
\tilde{u}=u\left(\frac{\cdot-z}{r}\right)\in\Lip(B(z,r))
with
\left.u\right|_{\partial B(0,1)}=0
if and only if
\left.\tilde{u}\right|_{\partial B(z,r)}=0
.
Moreover, we have that

\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\}=\Big\{\tfrac{y-z}{r}:y\in E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)}\Big\}

and hence that

u\geq t\chi_{\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\frac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\}}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\tilde{u}\geq t\chi_{E\cap\overline{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)}}.

Thus,
u
is an admissible comparison map in the definition of
\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{t}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)
if and only if
\tilde{u}
is an admissible comparison map for
\cp_{\varphi}^{t}\left(E\cap\overline{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)};B(z,r)\right)
.
For any fixed level
t>0
, we compute that

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)}\frac{\varphi(y,|\nabla\tilde{u}(y)|)}{\varphi_{z,r}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{B(z,r)}\frac{\varphi\left(y,\left|\nabla\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)\left(\frac{y-z}{r}\right)\right|\right)}{\varphi_{z,r}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=r^{n}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\varphi\left(z+rx,\left|\nabla\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)(x)\right|\right)}{\widetilde{\varphi}_{0,1}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=r^{n}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}_{0,1}^{-}\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)}{\widetilde{\varphi}_{0,1}^{-}(t)}\cdot\frac{\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}\left(x,\left|\nabla\left(\frac{u}{r}\right)(x)\right|\right)}{\widetilde{\varphi}_{0,1}^{-}\left(\frac{t}{r}\right)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq r^{n-p}\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{\frac{t}{r}}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\geq r^{n-p}\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{\inf}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big),

where in the penultimate line, we used that
r\leq 1
.
Thus, assuming that (
1.4
) is true, by Lemma
2.2
and (
2.4
) we have that

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)}\frac{\varphi(y,|\nabla\tilde{u}(y)|)}{\varphi_{z,r}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq c_{\mathrm{fat}}r^{n-p}\geq c(n)c_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq c(n)c_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big).

Taking the infimum over all admissible comparison maps
\tilde{u}
and all levels
t>0
, this implies (
2.5
) with constant
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}:=c(n)c_{\mathrm{fat}}
and radius
0<\tilde{r}_{o}\leq 1
.

Next, assuming that (
2.5
) holds and using Lemma
2.2
, we obtain that

\displaystyle r^{p-n}\cp_{p}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq r^{p-n}\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}r^{p-n}\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

(2.7)

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,q)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}

for sufficiently small radii
r>0
.
Indeed, in the last line of the preceding inequality, Lemma
2.3
and (
2.4
) yielded

\displaystyle\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,c(p,q)\min\left\{\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big),|B(z,r)|\left(|B(z,r)|^{-1}\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,c(p,q)\min\big\{c(n,p)r^{n-p},c(n,p,q)r^{n-q}\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,c(n,p,q)r^{n-p}

provided that the radius
r
is small enough depending on
n
,
p
and
q
.
Therefore, applying once again Lemma
2.3
and then (
2.3
) and (
2.7
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}}^{\inf}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\cp_{p}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big),

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad|B(0,1)|\left(|B(0,1)|^{-1}\cp_{p}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Big\{r^{p-n}\cp_{p}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big),c(n,p,q)\left(r^{p-n}\cp_{p}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,c(n,p,q,\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}).

Thus, (
2.5
) implies (
1.4
) with a constant
c_{\mathrm{fat}}:=c(n,p,q,\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}})
and radius
r_{o}=r_{o}(n,p,q,\tilde{r}_{o})
.
∎

Lastly, in the next result we want to show the equivalence of local infimal
\varphi
-fatness and local
p
-fatness.

Theorem 2.5
.

A closed set
E\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
is locally uniformly infimally
\varphi
-fat in the sense of Definition
1.2
if and only if it is locally uniformly
p
-fat.

Proof.
On the one hand, assuming that (
1.4
) holds true, by Lemma
2.2
we have that

\displaystyle\cp_{p}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}_{z,r}}^{\inf}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:z+rx\in E\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)\geq c_{\mathrm{fat}},

and by Lemma
2.4
we have the first implication.

On the other hand, for any radius
r>0
smaller than some
\hat{r}_{o}=\hat{r}_{o}(n,p,q)\leq 1
, by (
2.4
) we obtain that

(2.8)

\displaystyle|B(z,r)|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p}\left(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c(n,p,q)r^{n-q}\geq c(n,p,q)r^{n-p}\geq c(n,p,q)\cp_{p}\left(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right).

Therefore, if
E
is locally uniformly
p
-fat, by Lemma
2.3
we find that

\displaystyle\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\left\{\cp_{p}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big),|B(z,r)|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p}\left(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\min\left\{\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big),\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}^{\frac{q}{p}}|B(z,r)|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p}\left(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}(n,p,q,\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}})\cp_{p}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}(n,p,q,\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}})\cp_{\varphi}^{\inf}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big).

for any radius
0<r<\hat{r}_{o}(n,p,q)
, where in the last estimate we used Lemma
2.2
.
In view of Lemma
2.4
, this shows the claim of the lemma.
∎

3.
Self-improving property of the infimal
\varphi
-capacity density condition

In this section we prove that the infimal
\varphi
-fatness condition is self-improving.
We begin with a slightly more precise version of the classical result for the
p
-capacity density condition.
Essentially, we refer to the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 7.21]
, which relies on the equivalence of
p
-fatness of the complement with the boundary Poincaré inequality
[
42
, Theorems 6.22 & 6.23]
, and the self-improvement of the boundary Poincaré inequality in
[
42
, Theorem 7.24]
, with the minor difference that we track the centers and radii of the balls in these proofs.
While we will not need the improvement in this section, we will use it in the subsequent section when proving the global higher integrability result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. Assume that
E\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
is a closed set and that there exists
B(x,r_{a})
with
x\in E
such that
E
is locally uniformly
p
-fat in the sense of Definition
1.2
at
E\cap\overline{B(x,r_{a})}
with some constant
c_{\mathrm{fat}}
and
r_{0}>0
. Then there exist
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,p,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
with
0<\varepsilon<p-1
and a constant
\widetilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}=\widetilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}(n,p,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
such that
E
is locally uniformly
(p-\varepsilon)
-fat at
E\cap\overline{B\left(x,\tilde{r}_{0}\right)}
with the parameters
\widetilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}
and
\tilde{r}_{0}
, where
\tilde{r}_{0}:=\tfrac{1}{4}\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}
.

Proof.
We can assume without loss of generality that
(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)\cap\overline{B\left(x,\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/2\right)}\neq\emptyset
, since otherwise
B(z,\varrho/2)\subset E
for every
0<\varrho<\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/2
and
z\in B(x,\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/4)
. Thus there is nothing to prove in this scenario. From the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 6.22]
it is apparent that the local uniform
p
-fatness of
E
at
E\cap\overline{B(x,r_{a})}
yields the boundary
p
-Poincaré inequality for any test function
u\in\Lip_{0}\big(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E\big)
and ball
B(z,\varrho)
with
z\in E\cap\overline{B(x,r_{a})}
and
\varrho<r_{0}
.
Next, we need to ensure that this inequality is self-improving by tracking the balls in the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 7.24]
, in particular
[
42
, Lemma 7.29]
, where the
p
-fatness condition is applied to smaller balls contained in
B(z,\varrho)
.
In our setting, the
p
-fatness condition can be used for every ball in
B(z,\varrho)
,
z\in E\cap\overline{B\left(x,r_{a}/2\right)}
, with parameters
c_{\mathrm{fat}}
and
r_{0}>0
, provided that we have
\varrho\leq\min\left\{\frac{r_{a}}{2},r_{0}\right\}
.
Thus, we obtain the boundary
(p-\varepsilon)
-Poincaré inequality for any test function
u\in\Lip_{0}(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)
and any ball
B(z,\varrho)
with
z\in E\cap\overline{B\left(x,r_{a}/2\right)}
and
\varrho\leq\min\left\{r_{a}/2,r_{0}\right\}
.
Now, let us pay attention to the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 6.23]
, in particular the step from (b) to (c). There, a boundary point
z\in\partial E
is selected corresponding to some
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E
and the boundary Poincaré inequality is applied to the ball
B(z,\dist(y,\partial E))
. In our setting we therefore need that
\dist(y,\partial E)\leq\min\left\{r_{a}/2,r_{0}\right\}
and

to be contained in
\overline{B\left(x,r_{a}/2\right)}
. This however is the case if
y\in(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)\cap\overline{B\left(x,\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/2\right)}
which is nonempty by assumption. Therefore, we obtain the pointwise
(p-\varepsilon)
-Hardy inequality

|u(y)|\leq c\dist(y,\partial E)\left(M_{2\dist(y,\partial E)}|\nabla u|^{p-\varepsilon}(y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p-\varepsilon}}

for any
u\in\Lip_{0}(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)
and any
y\in(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)\cap\overline{B\left(x,\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/2\right)}
. According to
[
42
, Theorem 6.23]
, namely the step from (c) to (d), this implies the pointwise
(p-\varepsilon)
-Hardy inequality for any
y\in(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)\cap\overline{B\left(x,\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/2\right)}
. Let us now consider the step from (d) to (a) in the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 6.23]
. There, the authors establish that
E
is
(p-\varepsilon)
-fat at a point
z\in E
in the sense of Definition
1.2
with parameters
\tilde{r}_{0}
and
\widetilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}
, provided that the poinwise
(p-\varepsilon)
-Hardy inequality can be applied to every
y\in(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus E)\cap B(z,\tilde{r}_{0}/6)
. But if we choose
\tilde{r}_{0}=\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}/4
and assume
z\in E\cap\overline{B\left(x,\tilde{r}_{0}\right)}
this is quite certainly possible due to the previous comments. Therefore,
E
is locally uniformly
(p-\varepsilon)
-fat at
E\cap\overline{B\left(x,\tilde{r}_{0}\right)}
with the constant
\widetilde{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}
and
\tilde{r}_{0}=\tfrac{1}{4}\min\left\{r_{a},r_{0}\right\}
.
∎

Based on Theorem
3.1
and the lower bound in Lemma
2.3
, we are now ready to prove that the infimal
\varphi
-fatness condition is self-improving.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
E\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
is a closed set which is locally uniformly infimally
\varphi
-fat in the sense of Definition
1.2
with some constant
c_{\mathrm{fat}}
. Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
with
0<\varepsilon<p-1
and a constant
\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}=\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
such that
E
is locally uniformly infimally
\varphi^{1-\frac{\varepsilon}{p}}
-fat for the constant
\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}
.

Proof.
Let
\delta>0
to be chosen. We start the proof with the estimates

(3.1)

\tfrac{1}{2}\left(t^{p(1-\delta)}+a^{1-\delta}t^{q(1-\delta)}\right)\leq\max\left\{t^{p(1-\delta)},a^{1-\delta}t^{q(1-\delta)}\right\}\leq\left(t^{p}+at^{q}\right)^{1-\delta}

and

(3.2)

\left(t^{p}+at^{q}\right)^{1-\delta}\leq 2^{1-\delta}\max\left\{t^{p(1-\delta)},a^{1-\delta}t^{q(1-\delta)}\right\}\leq 2\left(t^{p(1-\delta)}+a^{1-\delta}t^{q(1-\delta)}\right),

which are valid for every
t\geq 0
and
a\in\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
, in particular for the choices
a=a(x)
as in (
1.2
) and
a=a^{-}_{O}
for any open set
O\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
.
Now, we define the auxiliary function
\widehat{\varphi}
by

\widehat{\varphi}(x,t)=\left(t^{p(1-\delta)}+a^{1-\delta}(x)t^{q(1-\delta)}\right).

Since the proof of Lemma
2.3
only relies on the boundedness of
a
, which is also satisfied by
a^{1-\delta}
, we may apply Lemma
2.3
to
\widehat{\varphi}
with parameters
(p(1-\delta),q(1-\delta))
replacing
(p,q)
.
Furthermore, we observe that Theorem
2.5
yields the existence of
c^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}>0
depending only on
n,p,q
and
c_{\mathrm{fat}}
such that
E
is locally uniformly
p
-fat with fatness constant
c^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}
.
Now, Theorem
3.1
allows us to infer the existence of some
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,p,c^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}})\in(0,p-1)
and
\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}=\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}(n,p,c^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}})>0
such that
E
is locally uniformly
(p-\varepsilon)
-fat with fatness constant
\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}
, namely that there holds

\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)\geq\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big).

Additionally, analogously to (
2.8
) (see
[
42
, Lemma 5.35]
in particular), for any radius
r>0
that is smaller than some
\hat{r}_{o}=\hat{r}_{o}(n,p,q,\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}})\leq 1
we have the chain of inequalities

\displaystyle\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}|B(z,r)|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\left(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\geq c(n,p,q)\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}r^{n-(p-\varepsilon)\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,q)\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}r^{n-(p-\varepsilon)}\geq c(n,p,q)\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\left(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right).

Hence, we conclude from Lemma
2.3
,
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{p}
, the penultimate inequality, and Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\cp_{\widehat{\varphi}}^{\inf}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq c(p,q)\min\left\{\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\Big(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big),|B(z,r)|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\left(E\cap\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq c(p,q)\min\left\{\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big),\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}|B(z,r)|^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\left(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,q)\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{p-\varepsilon}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,q)\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\cp_{\widehat{\varphi}}^{\inf}\Big(\overline{B\big(z,\tfrac{r}{2}\big)};B(z,r)\Big)

for any
0<r<\hat{r}_{o}
. This shows that
E
is uniformly infimally
\widehat{\varphi}
-fat.
It remains to show that this is equivalent to uniform infimal
\varphi^{1-\varepsilon/p}
-fatness for
\delta=\varepsilon/p
.
To this end, consider any compact subset
E
of an open set
O\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
, and a function
u\in\Lip(\overline{O})
that satisfies
u\geq t\chi_{E}
for some
t>0
and
\left.u\right|_{\partial O}=0
.
Since (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) yield

\tfrac{1}{4}\int_{O}\frac{\varphi^{1-\delta}(x,|\nabla u|)}{(\varphi_{O}^{-})^{1-\delta}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{O}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(x,|\nabla u|)}{\widehat{\varphi}_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{O}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(x,|\nabla u|)}{\widehat{\varphi}_{O}^{-}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 4\int_{O}\frac{\varphi^{1-\delta}(x,|\nabla u|)}{(\varphi_{O}^{-})^{1-\delta}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}x,

we immediately deduce that

\tfrac{1}{4}\cp_{\varphi^{1-\varepsilon/p}}^{\inf}(E;O)\leq\cp_{\widehat{\varphi}}^{\inf}(E;O)\leq 4\cp_{\varphi^{1-\varepsilon/p}}^{\inf}(E;O).

Together with the penultimate inequality, this shows that
E
is uniformly infimally
\varphi^{1-\varepsilon/p}
-fat with constant
\hat{c}_{\mathrm{fat}}:=c(n,p,q)\big(\hat{c}^{*}_{\mathrm{fat}}\big)^{\frac{q}{p}}
, i.e., the claim of the lemma.
∎

5.
A Maz’ya type inequality involving the double-phase functional

In this section, we show the following Maz’ya type inequality for certain functionals with

-growth.

Lemma 5.1

(Maz’ya type inequality)
.

Let
z\in\mathds{R}^{n}
,
r>0
,
u\in\Lip(B(z,r))
. Assume (
1.2
) holds and let
1<p<q<+\infty
be such that (
1.3
) holds.
Let
1\leq m\leq p
and if
m<n
, additionally assume that
q\leq\frac{nm}{n-m}
.
Further, define

Z:=\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}:u(z+rx)=0\right\}

and let
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
and
R>0
be arbitrary.
Then there exists a constant
c=c(n,p,q,m)
such that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}

\displaystyle\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(n,p,q,m)\big[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p-q}{m}}(r\nabla u)_{B(z,r),m}^{q-p}\big]}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p}{m}}}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{p}{m}}.

Proof.
By (
2.3
), we can rewrite the classical Maz’ya inequality of Lemma
4.1
, where instead of
p
we consider the exponent
m
, as

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|u|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\bigg(\frac{c(n,m,s)r^{m-n}}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))}\int_{B(z,r)}|\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{s}{m}}.

Since Lemma
4.1
is applicable with
s=p
and with
s=q
due to (

) and the assumption of the lemma, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}

\displaystyle\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}\left(|u|^{p}+a_{y,R}^{\pm}|u|^{q}\right)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{c(n,p,m)r^{-n}}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))}\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{p}{m}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+a_{y,R}^{\pm}\bigg(\frac{c(n,q,m)r^{-n}}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))}\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c(n,p,m)}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p}{m}}}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{p}{m}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{c(n,q,m)a^{\pm}_{y,R}\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p-q}{m}}(r\nabla u)_{B(z,r),m}^{q-p}}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p}{m}}}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{p}{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(n,p,q,m)\big[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p-q}{m}}(r\nabla u)_{B(z,r),m}^{q-p}\big]}{\cp_{m}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p}{m}}}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{p}{m}}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The preceding premise
q\leq\frac{nm}{n-m}
of the lemma is somewhat opaque. Accordingly, in the following we will derive a parameter range for
m
where this inequality is satisfied. We start with the observation that the requirement (
1.3
) on the ratio
q/p
implies the upper bound

q\leq p\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)\leq p+\frac{p}{n}.

From this upper bound we can deduce for
1<p<n
and
0\leq\hat{p}\leq p-1
that

\frac{n(p-\hat{p})}{n-p+\hat{p}}-q\geq\frac{n(p-\hat{p})}{n-p+\hat{p}}-\frac{np+p}{n}=\frac{p^{2}n-pn+p^{2}-\hat{p}(n^{2}+pn+p)}{n(n-p+\hat{p})}

and the latter is non-negative if

0\leq\hat{p}\leq\frac{p^{2}n-pn+p^{2}}{n^{2}+pn+p}.

We conclude that the assumption
q\leq\frac{nm}{n-m}
of the previous lemma is satisfied if there holds

\max\left\{1,\frac{pn^{2}+pn}{n^{2}+pn+p}\right\}\leq m\leq p.

In particular this parameter range is nonempty and contains
p
.

Example 5.3
.

At this stage, a natural question is whether a Maz’ya type inequality of the form

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi(x,|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{C}{\cp_{\varphi}\left(\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(z,r)\right)}\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

could hold with a capacity
\cp_{\varphi}
that is better adapted to the double-phase integrand
\varphi
(e.g. the variational
\varphi
-capacity defined in
[
7
]
) and a constant depending on the data
n
,
p
,
q
,
a
and possibly on
u
or
\nabla u
in a reasonable way.
In particular, we expect that
|u|
and
|\nabla u|
should appear on the right-hand side with a non-negative exponent.
However, the following counterexample shows that such an inequality can only hold with a notion of capacity satisfying
\cp_{\varphi}\left(\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(z,r)\right)\leq\cp_{p}\left(\{u=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(z,r)\right)
up to a multiplicative constant.
The problem already appears for the
x
-independent integrand

\varphi_{o}(s):=s^{p}+a_{o}s^{q}

with
p\leq n
,
a_{o}>0
, and Lipschitz functions
u
with small function values and small gradient in small balls.

Indeed, consider balls
B(0,r)
with
0<r\leq 1
and functions
u_{r}\in\Lip(\mathds{R}^{n})
given by
u_{r}(x):=\big(|x|-\frac{r}{2}\big)_{+}
.
Since
0\leq u_{r}\leq 1
in
B(0,r)
, we have that
u_{r}^{q}\leq u_{r}^{p}
in
B(0,r)
and thus that

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}u_{r}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}\varphi_{o}(u_{r})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq(1+a_{o})\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}u_{r}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Using polar coordinates, we compute that

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}u_{r}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}\Big(|x|-\frac{r}{2}\Big)_{+}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x=c(n)r^{-n}\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}\Big(t-\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{p}t^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n+p}(p+1)}r^{n+p}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{n-1}\cdot\frac{1}{p+1}\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{p+1}

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{n-1}\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}\Big(t-\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}\Big(t-\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{p}t^{n-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq r^{n-1}\int_{\frac{r}{2}}^{r}\big(t-\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2^{p+1}(p+1)}r^{n+p},

combining the preceding three inequalities yields

c(n,p)r^{p}\leq\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}\varphi_{o}(u_{r})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c(n,p,a_{o})r^{p}.

Next, note that
\nabla u_{r}(x)=\frac{x}{|x|}\chi_{\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)}(x)
.
This means that

\int_{B(0,r)}\varphi_{o}(|\nabla u_{r}|)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B(0,r)\setminus B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)}(1+a_{o})\,\mathrm{d}x=c(n)(1+a_{o})\Big(r^{n}-\Big(\frac{r}{2}\Big)^{n}\Big)=c(n)(1+a_{o})r^{n}.

We conclude that an inequality of the form

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(0,r)}\varphi_{o}(u_{r})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Q(r)\int_{B(0,r)}\varphi_{o}(|\nabla u_{r}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

can only hold for a quantity

Q(r)\geq c(n,p,a_{o})r^{p-n}\geq\frac{c(n,p,a_{o})}{\cp_{p}\left(\{u_{r}=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(0,r)\right)}.

For the last estimate, we have taken (
2.4
) and the fact that
\{u_{r}=0\}=\overline{B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)}
into account.

In contrast, note that the variational
\varphi
-capacity of a compact set
K
in an open set
O\supset K
set is defined by

\cp_{\varphi}(K,O)=\inf\left\{\int_{O}\varphi(|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x:u\in W^{1,\varphi}(O;\mathds{R}_{\geq 0})\cap C^{0}_{c}(O),u\geq 1\text{ in }K\right\},

see
[
7
, Proposition 20]
.
In our case, we have that
W^{1,\varphi}(B(0,r))=W^{1,q}(B(0,r))
.
In order to estimate
\cp_{\varphi}\left(\{u_{r}=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(0,r)\right)
, first note that by Poincaré’s inequality

\displaystyle c(n)r^{n}=\left|B\left(0,\tfrac{r}{2}\right)\right|\leq\int_{B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)}|u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c(n,q)r^{q}\int_{B(0,r)}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

for any
u\in W^{1,q}_{0}(B(0,r))
such that
u\geq 1
in
B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)
and, analogously,

c(n)r^{n}\leq c(n,p)r^{p}\int_{B(0,r)}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

for the same class of functions.
Recalling that
r\leq 1
and taking the infimum over all admissible functions in the definition of
\cp_{\varphi}
, this shows that

\displaystyle r^{n-q}=\max\{r^{n-p},r^{n-q}\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\min\{1,a_{o}\}}\big(r^{n-p}+a_{o}r^{n-q}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c(n,p,q,a_{o})\cp_{\varphi}\left(\{u_{r}=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(0,\tfrac{r}{2}\right)},B(0,r)\right).

Since for any constant
C
there exists a radius
r_{C}
such that
Cr^{q-n}<r^{p-n}
for any
0<r<r_{C}
, this in turn implies that

\cp_{\varphi}\left(\{u_{r}=0\}\cap\overline{B\left(0,\frac{r}{2}\right)},B(0,r)\right)^{-1}
is essentially smaller than
r^{p-n}
.
Therefore, a Maz’ya type inequality does not hold with this notion of capacity in the denominator on the right-hand side.

6.
From the fatness condition to an integral Hardy inequality

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, in particular proving an integral Hardy inequality assuming the fatness condition on the complement of the domain
\Omega
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let us assume that
\diam(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
In that case, our goal is to prove that there exists a constant
c=c(n,p,q,r_{o},c_{\mathrm{fat}})
such that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\dist(x,\partial\Omega)^{-1}|u(x)|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

holds for any
u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
.

Provided that this is true, we can then show the claim for general domains by transformation.
Indeed, set
\lambda:=\diam(\Omega)
and
\widetilde{\Omega}:=\Omega/(2\lambda)
,
\tilde{u}(y)=u(2\lambda y)/(2\lambda)
and
\widetilde{\varphi}(y,t)=\varphi(2\lambda y,t)
.
Note that
\diam(\widetilde{\Omega})\leq 1/2
and that
\widetilde{\varphi}(y,t)=t^{p}+\tilde{a}(y)t^{q}
, where
\tilde{a}(y):=a(2\lambda y)
is Hölder continuous to the exponent
\alpha
with
[\tilde{a}]_{0;\alpha}=(2\lambda)^{\alpha}[a]_{0;\alpha}
.
Taking further into account that
\dist(2\lambda y,\partial\Omega)=2\lambda\dist(y,\partial\widetilde{\Omega})
for any
y\in\widetilde{\Omega}
and that
\tilde{u}\in\Lip_{0}(\widetilde{\Omega})
with
\nabla\tilde{u}(y)=(\nabla u)(2\lambda y)
, the preceding inequality yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{\dist(x,\partial\Omega)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\varphi\left(2\lambda y,\frac{|u(2\lambda y)|}{\dist(2\lambda y,\partial\Omega)}\right)(2\lambda)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=(2\lambda)^{n}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\widetilde{\varphi}\left(y,\frac{|\tilde{u}(y)|}{\dist(y,\partial\widetilde{\Omega})}\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq c(2\lambda)^{n}\left(1+[\tilde{a}]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{\Omega})}^{q-p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\widetilde{\varphi}\left(y,\left|\nabla\tilde{u}(y)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+\lambda^{\alpha+\frac{n(p-q)}{p}}[a]_{0;\alpha}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}|(\nabla u)(2\lambda y)|^{p}(2\lambda)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\cdot\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}}\varphi\left(2\lambda y,|(\nabla u)(2\lambda y)|\right)(2\lambda)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=c\left(1+\lambda^{\alpha+\frac{n(p-q)}{p}}[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
c=c(n,p,q,r_{o},c_{\mathrm{fat}})
.

In order to deduce the penultimate inequality for domains with
\diam(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we argue in the following way.
Let
\mathcal{W}
be a Whitney cube decomposition of
\Omega
in the sense of Lemma
4.4
.
For each cube
Q\in\mathcal{W}
, let
x_{Q}\in\partial\Omega
be a point with
\dist(Q,\partial\Omega)=\dist(Q,x_{Q})
and note that the ball
B_{Q}:=B(x_{Q},r_{Q})
with
r_{Q}:=5\diam(Q)
contains
Q
.
Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(n)
such that the cube
CQ
, i.e. the cube with the same center as
Q
and
\diam(CQ)=C\diam(Q)
, contains
B_{Q}
.
Next, for
k\in\mathds{N}
we set

\Omega_{k}:=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}Q,\quad\text{where }\mathcal{W}_{k}:=\big\{Q\in\mathcal{W}:\diam(Q)=2^{-k}\big\},

and

\widetilde{\Omega}_{k}:=\bigcup_{m=k}^{+\infty}\Omega_{m}.

For
k_{o}:=\lceil 2+\log(C)\rceil
and
Q\subset\Omega_{k}
,
k\geq k_{o}
, we have that
CQ\cap\Omega\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{k-k_{o}}
.
Moreover, there exists
k_{1}=k_{1}(n,r_{o})>k_{o}
such that
r_{Q}<r_{o}
for any
\mathcal{W}\ni Q\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}
.
Let
u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
,
0<\beta<1
and abbreviate
d(x):=\dist(x,\partial\Omega)
.
First, note that by the fatness condition (
1.4
) in conjunction with the upper bound from Lemma
2.2
, we have that

\displaystyle\cp_{p}\Big(\Big\{x\in\overline{B\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big)}:u(x_{Q}+r_{Q}x)=0\Big\};B(0,1)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\cp_{p}\left(\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}:x_{Q}+r_{Q}x\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\};B(0,1)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{\inf}\left(\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}:x_{Q}+r_{Q}x\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\};B(0,1)\right)\geq c_{\mathrm{fat}}.

Thus, applying Lemma
5.1
to
u/r_{Q}^{1+\beta}
with the parameter choices
(r,R,z,y,m)\equiv(r_{Q},r_{Q},x_{Q},x_{Q},p)
, for any
\mathcal{W}\ni Q\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{Q}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{Q}\varphi^{+}_{Q}\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c(q)\int_{B_{Q}}\varphi^{+}_{x_{Q},r_{Q}}\left(r_{Q}^{-(1+\beta)}|u(x)|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,\frac{1+a^{+}_{x_{Q},r_{Q}}\cp_{p}\left(\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)}:u(x_{Q}+r_{Q}x)=0\right\};B(0,1)\right)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\left(r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u\right)_{B_{Q},p}^{q-p}}{\cp_{p}\left(\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)}:u(x_{Q}+r_{Q}x)=0\right\};B(0,1)\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\int_{B_{Q}}\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\,\left[1+a^{+}_{x_{Q},r_{Q}}\left(r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u\right)_{B_{Q},p}^{q-p}\right]\int_{B_{Q}}\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
.
By means of Hölder’s inequality, Hölder continuity of
a
, (
1.3
) and the facts that
5\geq r_{Q}\geq d(x)
for any
x\in B_{Q}
,
B_{Q}\subset CQ
and
u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
, we estimate the right-hand side of the preceding inequality by

\displaystyle a^{+}_{x_{Q},r_{Q}}

\displaystyle\left(r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u\right)_{B_{Q},p}^{q-p}\int_{B_{Q}}\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(a^{-}_{x_{Q},r_{Q}}+[a]_{0,\alpha}(2r_{Q})^{\alpha}\right)\left(r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u\right)_{B_{Q},p}^{q-p}\int_{B_{Q}}\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{Q}}a^{-}_{x_{Q},r_{Q}}\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+c[a]_{0,\alpha}r_{Q}^{\alpha-\frac{n(q-p)}{p}}\left\|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{Q})}^{q-p}\int_{B_{Q}}\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\int_{CQ\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q)
. This leads us to

(6.1)

\displaystyle\int_{Q}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\int_{CQ\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

for any cube
\mathcal{W}\ni Q\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}
with
c=c(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
.
Summing the inequalities (
6.1
) related to
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
for some
k\geq k_{1}
,
using that the cubes
CQ
with
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
have uniformly bounded overlap depending only on the space dimension
n
(see Remark
4.5
), that
CQ\cap\Omega\subset\widetilde{\Omega}_{k-k_{o}}
for any
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
,
and finally that
r_{Q}=5\sqrt{n}2^{-k}
for any
Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}
, we infer

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{k}}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{W}_{k}}\int_{CQ\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|r_{Q}^{-\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\sum_{m=k-k_{o}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{m}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{k\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Now, summing over
k\geq k_{1}
, and changing the order of summation, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{\infty}\sum_{m=k-k_{o}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{m}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{k\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.2)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\sum_{m=k_{1}-k_{o}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{m}}\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{m+k_{o}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{k\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

At this stage, rewriting the sum, recalling the definition of
\varphi
, and using the formula for geometric series and the fact that
0<\beta<1
, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{m+k_{o}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{k\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=k_{1}-m}^{-1}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{j\beta}2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{k_{o}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{j\beta}2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{0}2^{jp\beta}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{k_{o}}2^{jq\beta}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-2^{-p\beta}}+\frac{2^{(k_{o}+1)q\beta}-1}{2^{q\beta}-1}\right)\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)

(6.3)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{1-2^{-p\beta}}+\frac{2^{k_{o}q}}{1-2^{-q\beta}}\right)\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)

By the mean value theorem, for
s\in\{p,q\}
there exists
0\leq\tilde{\beta}\leq\beta<1
such that

1-2^{-s\beta}=2^{-s0}-2^{-s\beta}=-\beta\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\beta}2^{-s\beta}\right)(\tilde{\beta})=\beta\ln(2)s2^{-s\tilde{\beta}}\geq\beta\ln(2)s2^{-s},

which is equivalent to

\frac{1}{1-2^{-s\beta}}\leq\frac{2^{s}}{\ln(2)s\beta}.

Inserting this estimate into the right-hand side of (
6.3
) and recalling that
k_{o}
depends only on
n
, we find that

\sum_{k=k_{1}}^{m+k_{o}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{k\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\leq c(n,p,q)\frac{1}{\beta}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right).

Plugging the preceding inequality in turn into (
6.2
) and using that

2^{-m}\geq\frac{1}{2}\diam(Q)\geq\frac{1}{4}\diam(Q)+\frac{1}{16}\dist(Q,\partial\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{16}d(x)

for any
x\in Q\subset\Omega_{m}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}\sum_{m=k_{1}-k_{o}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{m}}\varphi\left(x,\left|2^{m\beta}\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}\sum_{m=k_{1}-k_{o}}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{m}}\varphi\left(x,\left|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.4)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{d^{\beta}(x)}\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, since
d(x)\geq 2^{-k_{1}}
in the complement of
\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}
,
k_{1}
depends only on
n
and
r_{o}
, and we have that
\beta<1
, by applying the Poincaré inequality for functions with zero boundary values of Lemma
4.2
(since
\spt(u)\Subset\Omega
and
\diam(\Omega)\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we may replace
\Omega
with by a suitable ball
B(x_{o},1)
as the domain of integration) we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus\widetilde{\Omega}_{k_{1}}}\varphi\left(x,2^{2k_{1}}|u(x)|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(6.5)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

with
c=c(n,p,q,r_{o})
.
Adding (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) and using that
d(x)\leq 1
and
\beta<1
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\frac{\nabla u(x)}{d^{\beta}(x)}\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,r_{o},c_{\mathrm{fat}})
.
Next, replacing
u
in the previous inequality by
v:=d^{\beta}u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
and using that

|\nabla v|\leq\beta d^{\beta-1}u+d^{\beta}|\nabla u|

leads us to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|v(x)|}{d^{1+\beta}(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|d^{-\beta}(x)\nabla v(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\frac{\nabla v(x)}{d^{\beta}(x)}\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\left\|\beta d^{-1}(x)u(x)\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\cdot\left(\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\beta\frac{u(x)}{d(x)}\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

(6.6)

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\cdot\left(\beta^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\frac{u(x)}{d(x)}\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\beta^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

In the penultimate line we have used that, by Theorem
2.5
and thanks to the assumption that (
1.4
) holds,
\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is
p
-fat and thus the
p
-Hardy inequality in Lemma
4.3
holds.
At this stage, we derive a Hardy inequality by reabsorbing the first integral on the right-hand side of (
6.6
) into the left-hand side.
Indeed, we choose

\beta:=\left[2c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\right]^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}<1,

where we assumed without loss of generality that
c>\frac{1}{2(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p})}
. Indeed, in the opposite case we can estimate
c
from above by the same number; then, choosing
\beta:=2^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
we have the desired statement.

This choice of
\beta
yields

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)\beta^{-1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

and hence

(6.7)

\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u(x)|}{d(x)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\left(1+[a]_{0;\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\left|\nabla u(x)\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

holds with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,r_{o},c_{\mathrm{fat}})
for any
u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
.
This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

7.
Equivalence of fatness, the boundary Poincaré inequality, and pointwise Hardy inequalities

In this section we finally show Theorem
1.5
, in particular proving that the infimal
\varphi
-fatness condition (
1.4
), the boundary Poincaré inequality and the pointwise Hardy inequality are all equivalent. Moreover, in Remark
7.1
we give more details on what happens considering being in the two different phases of the functional.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

As a first thing, note that the equivalence of (
1
) and (
2
) has already been proven in Theorem
2.5
.

Now, we show the implication from (
2
) to (
3
).
To this end, fix
z\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
,
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
,
0<r<r_{o}
,
R>0
, and
u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
.
For the extension of
u
by zero, we have that
u\in\Lip(B(z,r))
.
For simplicity, denote

Z:=\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}:u(z+rx)=0\right\}

By Lemma
2.2
and (
2
) we have that

\displaystyle\cp_{p}(Z;B(0,1))\geq\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{\inf}(Z;B(0,1))\geq\cp_{\widetilde{\varphi}}^{\inf}\left(\left\{x\in\overline{B\left(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}:z+rx\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\right\};B(0,1)\right)\geq c_{\mathrm{fat}}.

Thus, applying Lemma
5.1
with the particular choice
m=p
and estimating the factor on the right-hand side by the preceding inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}(|u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\left[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}\cp_{p}(Z;B(0,1))^{\frac{p-q}{p}}(r\nabla u)_{B(z,r),p}^{q-p}\right]}{\cp_{p}(Z;B(0,1))}\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c\left[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}c_{\mathrm{fat}}^{\frac{p-q}{p}}(r\nabla u)_{B(z,r),p}^{q-p}\right]}{c_{\mathrm{fat}}}\int_{B(z,r)}|r\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Dividing by
|B(z,r)|
yields (
3
).

We proceed by establishing that (
3
) implies (
4
).
To this end, let
z\in\Omega
with
r_{z}:=\dist(z,\partial\Omega)<r_{o}
,
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
,
R>0
and
u\in\Lip_{0}(\Omega)
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
u\geq 0
(otherwise one can replace
u
by
|u|
).
We choose
w\in\partial\Omega
such that
|w-z|=r_{z}
.
By the fact that
B(w,r_{z})\subset B(z,2r_{z})
, Jensen’s inequality, (
3
), (
4.1
) and the classical
(p,p)
- and
(q,p)
-Poincaré inequalities (see for instance
[
42
, Theorem 3.14]
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}

\displaystyle\big((u)_{B(z,r_{z})}\big)

\displaystyle\leq c(q)\big[\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}\big((u)_{B(z,r_{z})}-(u)_{B(z,2r_{z})}\big)+\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}\big((u)_{B(z,2r_{z})}-(u)_{B(w,r_{z})}\big)+\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}\big((u)_{B(w,r_{z})}\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq c(n,q)\bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r_{z})}|u(x)-(u)_{B(z,2r_{z})}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x+a^{\pm}_{y,R}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r_{z})}|u(x)-(u)_{B(z,2r_{z})}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\qquad+\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(w,r_{z})}\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})\left[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}(r_{z}\nabla u)_{B(z,2r_{z}),p}^{q-p}+a^{\pm}_{y,R}(r_{z}\nabla u)_{B(w,r_{z}),p}^{q-p}\right]\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r_{z})}|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})\left[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}(r_{z}\nabla u)_{B(z,2r_{z}),p}^{q-p}\right]\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r_{z})}|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x,

i.e. the claim of (
4
).

Next, we show that (
4
) yields (
5
).
Here, we use the same notation as in the previous step.
Using Lemma
4.6
, (
4
), and the fact that
(r_{z}\nabla u)_{B(z,2r_{z}),p}^{q-p}\leq M_{2r_{z}}(|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p})^{\frac{q-p}{p}}(z)
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}(u(z))

\displaystyle\leq c(q)\big[\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}\big(u(z)-(u)_{B(z,r_{z})}\big)+\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}\big((u)_{B(z,r_{z})}\big)\big]

\displaystyle=c(q)\big[|u(z)-(u)_{B(z,r_{z})}|^{p}+a^{\pm}_{y,R}|u(z)-(u)_{B(z,r_{z})}|^{q}+\varphi^{\pm}_{y,R}\big((u)_{B(z,r_{z})}\big)\big]

\displaystyle\leq c(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})\bigg[M_{2r_{z}}(|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p})(z)+a^{\pm}_{y,R}M_{2r_{z}}(|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p})^{\frac{q}{p}}(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\left(1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}(r_{z}\nabla u)_{B(z,2r_{z}),p}^{q-p}\right)\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r_{z})}|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c(n,p,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})\left[1+a^{\pm}_{y,R}M_{2r_{z}}(|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p})^{\frac{q-p}{p}}(z)\right]M_{2r_{z}}(|r_{z}\nabla u|^{p})(z),

i.e. we have the claim of (
5
).

Finally, we prove that (
5
) implies (
2
).
At this stage, we assume that the constant on the right-hand side of (
5
) only depends on
n
,
p
, and
q
, but not on any additional number
c_{\mathrm{fat}}>0
.
We will then determine the constant
c_{\mathrm{fat}}=c_{\mathrm{fat}}(n,p,q)
in (
2
).
Observe that a change of variables as in (
2.6
) shows that the infimal
\varphi
-capacity density condition (
1.4
) is fulfilled
if there exists
c_{\mathrm{fat}}>0
such that the inequality

(7.1)

c_{\mathrm{fat}}\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(t)\leq\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r)}\varphi(y,2r\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}y

holds for every point
z\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, level
t>0
, radius
0<2r<r_{o}
and
u\in\mathcal{M}_{z,2r}
, where

\mathcal{M}_{z,2r}:=\left\{u\in\Lip_{0}(\overline{B(z,2r)}):u\geq t\chi_{(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\cap\overline{B(z,r)}}\right\}.

Further, by Lemma
2.1
, we may assume that
0\leq u\leq t
.
At this stage, let us first consider the case where

(7.2)

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}u\,\mathrm{d}y>\frac{t}{4^{n+1}}.

Note that for any
s\in[1,\infty)
, the classical Sobolev-Poincaré inequality (see for instance
[
42
, Theorem 3.17]
) implies that

t<4^{n+1}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}u\,\mathrm{d}y\leq c(n)\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r)}u\,\mathrm{d}y\leq c(n,s)\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r)}|2r\nabla u|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Applying the preceding inequality with the parameter choices
s=p,q
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(t)

\displaystyle=\big(t^{p}+a_{z,2r}^{-}t^{q}\big)\leq c(n,p,q)\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r)}\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(2r\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq c(n,p,q)\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,2r)}\varphi(y,2r\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}y,

i.e. that (
7.1
) holds.
Thus, (
2
) immediately follows if (
7.2
) holds for every
u\in\mathcal{M}_{z,2r}
.

Let us now consider the case where (
7.2
) is not true for a function
u\in\mathcal{M}_{z,2r}
.
First, note that since
u
is continuous, the sublevel set

F:=\left\{y\in B\left(z,\tfrac{r}{4}\right):0\leq u<\tfrac{t}{2}\right\}

is open.
Furthermore, we have that
F\subset\Omega
, since
u\geq t\chi_{(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\cap\overline{B(z,r)}}
, and
u\geq t/2
on
B\left(z,\tfrac{r}{4}\right)\setminus F
.
By these observations and the fact that (
7.2
) is false for
u
, for the measure of this set we obtain that

\left|B\left(z,\tfrac{r}{4}\right)\setminus F\right|=\int_{B\left(z,\frac{r}{4}\right)\setminus F}1\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{2}{t}\int_{B\left(z,\frac{r}{4}\right)\setminus F}u\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{n}}|B(z,r)|,

which in turn implies that

|F|=\left|B\left(z,\tfrac{r}{4}\right)\right|-\left|B\left(z,\tfrac{r}{4}\right)\setminus F\right|\geq\frac{1}{4^{n}}|B(z,r)|-\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{n}}|B(z,r)|=\frac{1}{2\cdot 4^{n}}|B(z,r)|.

To proceed, we define the cut-off function
\psi\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
by

y\mapsto\max\big\{0,1-\tfrac{4}{r}\dist\big(y,B\big(z,\tfrac{3r}{4}\big)\big)\big\}

and set

v:=\psi(t-u)\in\Lip(\overline{B(z,r)}).

Note that
\spt(v)\Subset\overline{\Omega\cap B(z,r)}
, since
u\equiv t
on
\overline{B(z,r)}\cap(\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)
.
Further, by construction of
\psi
, we have that
v=t-u
in
B\big(z,\frac{3r}{4}\big)
, and hence that
|\nabla u|=|\nabla v|
almost everywhere in
B\big(z,\frac{3r}{4}\big)
.
By definition of the restricted centered maximal operator, we have that for every
x\in F
there exists
0<r_{x}\leq 2\dist(x,\partial\Omega)=:2d_{x}
such that

(7.3)

M_{2d_{x}}\big(|d_{x}\nabla u|^{p}\big)(x)=\sup_{0<r<2d_{x}}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|d_{x}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq 2\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(x,r_{x})}|d_{x}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y.

Next, since
z\in\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
and
x\in F\subset\Omega\cap B\big(z,\frac{r}{4}\big)
, we have that
d_{x}\leq\frac{r}{4}
and thus
r_{x}\leq\frac{r}{2}
.
Therefore, for any
y\in B(x,r_{x})
we find that

|z-y|\leq|z-x|+|x-y|\leq\frac{r}{4}+r_{x}\leq\frac{3r}{4}.

In particular, this leads to
B(x,r_{x})\subset B\big(z,\frac{3r}{4}\big)
.
On the one hand, this yields
|\nabla u|=|\nabla v|
almost everywhere on
B(x,r_{x})
.
On the other hand, the universal bound for the radii
r_{x}
allows us to apply the Vitali-type
5r
-covering lemma (see for instance
[
42
, Lemma 1.13]
).
Therefore, there exists a collection of disjoint balls
B(x_{i},r_{i})
,
i\in\mathds{N}
, with
x_{i}\in F
and
r_{i}:=r_{x_{i}}
, such that

F\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{+\infty}B(x_{i},5r_{i}).

Due to the lower bound on the measure of
F
, we deduce that

\displaystyle|B(z,r)|\leq 2\cdot 4^{n}|F|\leq 2\cdot 20^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}|B(x_{i},r_{i})|.

At this stage, we apply the pointwise Hardy inequality (
5
).
Since
x_{i}\in F
, observe that
v(x_{i})=t-u(x_{i})\geq t/2
for any
i\in\mathds{N}
and that
r_{i}\leq 2d_{x_{i}}<2r<r_{o}
.
Thus, applying (
5
) to
v(x_{i})
, using (
7.3
), Jensen’s inequality and the fact that
B(x_{i},r_{i})\subset B(z,2r)
, and thus
a_{z,2r}^{-}\leq a_{x_{i},r_{i}}^{-}
, for any
i\in\mathds{N}
we infer

\displaystyle\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{q}\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}\big(\tfrac{t}{2}\big)\leq 2^{q}\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}\big(v(x_{i})\big)

\displaystyle\leq c\left[1+a^{-}_{z,2r}M_{2d_{x_{i}}}(|d_{x_{i}}\nabla u|^{p})^{\frac{q-p}{p}}(x_{i})\right]M_{2d_{x_{i}}}(|d_{x_{i}}\nabla u|^{p})(x_{i})

\displaystyle\leq c\left[1+a^{-}_{z,2r}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}|d_{x_{i}}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)^{\frac{q-p}{p}}\right]\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}|d_{x_{i}}\nabla u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\left[|r\nabla u|^{p}+a^{-}_{x_{i},r_{i}}|r\nabla u|^{q}\right]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq c\,\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\varphi(y,r\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}y

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q)
. From the previous chain of inequalities, we can immediately estimate the measures of the balls
B(x_{i},r_{i})
,
i\in\mathds{N}
, by

\displaystyle|B(x_{i},r_{i})|\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\varphi(y,r\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Finally, using that the balls
B(x_{i},r_{i})
,
i\in\mathds{N}
, are disjoint, the preceding inequality leads us to

\displaystyle|B(z,r)|\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c(n)\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}|B(x_{i},r_{i})|\varphi^{-}_{z,2r}(t)

\displaystyle\leq c(n,p,q)\sum_{i=1}^{+\infty}\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i})}\varphi(y,r\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}y

Since this shows that (

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

8.
Global higher integrability for functionals of double-phase type

The focus of this section is on proving the global higher integrability result of Theorem
1.8
and that is achieved by the following covering argument.

We consider sufficiently small balls that cover
\Omega
and distinguish between two types of balls, namely the ones that are completely contained in
\Omega
, and those that possess a nontrivial intersection with its complement. For these types of balls, we establish a reverse Hölder’s inequality.
Then, we combine the respective inequalities on the individual balls to obtain a global reverse Hölder’s inequality.
To treat the balls
B
that are not contained in
\Omega
, we consider a ball
B^{\prime}
that covers
B
with center at the boundary
\partial\Omega
and distinguish the cases where the center is sufficiently close to the zero set of
a
in
\partial\Omega
or not.
In the first case, we obtain a reverse Hölder’s inequality with the help of the locally uniform
p
-fatness, while in the other case we are still able to obtain a reverse Hölder’s inequality, but making use of the locally uniform
q
-fatness.
For balls that are contained in
\Omega
, a reverse Hölder’s inequality was already obtained;
for our purposes we combine arguments in the proof of
[
52
, Lemma 2.14]
with the claim of
[
52
, Theorem 2.13]
as stated in Lemma
4.2
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\varphi
be defined according to (
1.1
) such that (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) hold with
1<p<q<+\infty
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set,

Further, assume that the functional
\mathcal{F}

\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,\frac{r}{2})}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\big(1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{q-p}\big)\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi^{\theta}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta}}

Proof.
Let
\frac{1}{2}<t<s\leq 1

\displaystyle\,\nu\int_{B(z,tr)}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\nu\int_{B(z,sr)}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{F}[u;\spt(v-u)]\leq C_{Q}\mathcal{F}[v;\spt(v-u)]\leq C_{Q}L\int_{B(z,sr)}\varphi(x,|\nabla v|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\int_{B(z,tr)}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\vartheta\int_{B(z,sr)}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c(q,\nu,L,C_{Q})}{(s-t)^{q}}\int_{B(z,r)}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u-(u)_{B(z,r)}|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

for minimizers.
While the lemma applies to a wide class of generalized

For the sake of completeness, we adapt the proof to the case of quasi-minimizers.

Lemma 8.2

(8.1)

\varepsilon(n,p,c_{\mathrm{fat}})\leq\varepsilon(n,q,c_{\mathrm{fat}})

(8.2)

\displaystyle\cp_{p-\varepsilon}(Z;B(y,2r))

\displaystyle\geq\cp_{p-\varepsilon}((\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega)\cap B(y,r);B(y,2r))

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,c_{\mathrm{fat}})\cp_{p-\varepsilon}(B(y,r);B(y,2r))

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,c_{\mathrm{fat}})(2r)^{n-p+\varepsilon},

where we have used the monotonicity of the variational capacity and the lower bound (
2.4
).

In order to continue with the proof, we would like to apply Lemma
5.1
with a suitable parameter
m
, i.e. a Maz’ya type inequality, to
v
.
While the lemma is formulated for Lipschitz functions, an approximation argument shows that it continues to hold for functions
u\in W^{1,m}(\Omega)
.

To this end, we have to distinguish between the cases
p\leq n
and
p>n
. In the former case, we choose
p-\tfrac{p\varepsilon}{q}<m<p\leq n
such that

(8.3)

m\geq p\,\theta_{0},\qquad q\leq\frac{nm}{n-m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q\leq p+\frac{m\alpha}{n},

while in the latter case we choose
n<m<p
such that

(8.4)

m>p-\frac{p\,\varepsilon}{q},\qquad m\geq p\,\theta_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q\leq p+\frac{m\alpha}{n},

where
\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(n,p,q)\in(0,1)
denotes the constant of Lemma
8.1
.
Note that such a choice of
m=m(n,p,q,\alpha)
is possible due to Remark
5.2
and assumption (
1.8
).
Moreover, observe that the latter upper bound on
q
implies the inequality

(8.5)

\alpha-\frac{n(q-p)}{m}\geq 0.

Now, notice that Hölder’s inequality and (
8.2
) imply

\cp_{m}(Z;B(y,2r))\geq c(n,p,q,\alpha,c_{\mathrm{fat}})(2r)^{n-m}

and therefore, after rescaling as in (
2.3
), that

\cp_{m}\left(\left\{x\in\overline{B(0,1/2)}:v(y+2rx)=0\right\};B(0,1)\right)\geq c(n,p,q,\alpha,c_{\mathrm{fat}}).

If we define
\theta_{1}:=m/p\in[\theta_{0},1)
, and apply Lemma
5.1
on
v
with the parameter choices
(r,R,z,y)\equiv(2r,2r,y,y)
, thanks to the previous inequality we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}

\displaystyle\varphi^{+}_{y,2r}\left(\frac{|v|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\big[1+a^{+}_{y,2r}(\nabla v)_{B(y,2r),m}^{q-p}\big]\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{m}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{p}{m}}

\displaystyle=c\big[1+a^{+}_{y,2r}(\nabla v)_{B(y,2r),p\theta_{1}}^{q-p}\big]\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{p\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

for a constant
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
. Let us now estimate the right-hand side further using the inequality

a^{+}_{y,2r}\leq a^{-}_{y,2r}+[a]_{0,\alpha}(2r)^{\alpha}.

We observe in a first step that Jensen’s inequality and the definition of
a^{-}_{y,2r}
imply that

\displaystyle a^{-}_{y,2r}

\displaystyle(\nabla v)_{B(y,2r),p\theta_{1}}^{q-p}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{p\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle a^{-}_{y,2r}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{p\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p\theta_{1}}}\leq a^{-}_{y,2r}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{q\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}\big(a^{-}_{y,2r}\big)^{\theta_{1}}|\nabla v|^{q\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}\leq\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla v|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle c\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\left[\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)+\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla f|)\right]\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle c\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}+c\,\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla f|)\,\mathrm{d}x.

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
.
Next, due to (
8.5
) and the fact that
2r\leq 1
we observe that

\displaystyle[a]_{0,\alpha}(

\displaystyle 2r)^{\alpha}(\nabla v)_{B(y,2r),p\theta_{1}}^{q-p}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{p\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle=[a]_{0,\alpha}(2r)^{\alpha-\frac{n(q-p)}{m}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p\theta_{1}}(B(y,2r))}^{q-p}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{p\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,[a]_{0,\alpha}\|\nabla(u-f)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{1}(q-p)}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}+\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg],

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
. Combining the preceding estimates and using the fact that
\varphi(x,\cdot)\leq\varphi^{+}_{y,2r}(\cdot)
on
B(y,2r)
, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}

\displaystyle\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u-f|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\|\nabla(u-f)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{1}(q-p)}\Big]\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

(8.6)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,c_{\mathrm{fat}})
.

Now, assume on the contrary that there holds
\bm{y\in\partial\Omega\setminus\mathcal{P}}
, which can only be the case if the set is non-empty. To treat this case, we observe that
\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is locally uniformly
q
-fat and, accordingly, Theorem
3.1
in conjunction with (
8.1
) yields that
\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is locally uniformly
(q-\varepsilon)
-fat. Furthermore, we have in similarity to (
8.2
) that the estimate

(8.7)

\displaystyle\cp_{q-\varepsilon}(Z;B(y,2r))

\displaystyle\geq c(n,p,c_{\mathrm{fat}})(2r)^{n-q+\varepsilon}

holds. But this allows us to apply the classical Maz’ya inequality of Lemma
4.1
on
v
with parameters
(p,s)\equiv(q\theta_{1},q)
, where
m
is again chosen according to (
8.3
) if
p\leq n
and according to (
8.4
) if
p>n
, respectively, and
\theta_{1}:=m/p
. To ensure its applicability, we need to consider the case
q\theta_{1}<n
and verify the additional condition that
q\leq n\theta_{1}q/(n-\theta_{1}q)
. By definition of
\theta_{1}
, we have
m=\theta_{1}p
which implies
m<n
. But this contradicts the choice (
8.4
) for
m
in the case
p>n
, i.e., that
n<m<p
. Thus, there must hold
p\leq n
. Now, we can use the second inequality of (
8.3
) to derive the estimate

n-q\theta_{1}\leq n-p\theta_{1}\leq\frac{np\theta_{1}}{q}\leq n\theta_{1}

which yields the desired upper bound. Since
q\theta_{1}>q-\varepsilon
, we further observe that Hölder’s inequality and the inequality (
8.7
) imply that (
8.7
) also holds if
q-\varepsilon
is replaced with
\theta_{1}q
. If we use this to estimate the right-hand side of Lemma
4.1
, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}\bigg(\frac{|v|}{r}\bigg)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c(n,p,q,\alpha)\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{q\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}.

By the assumptions on
r
we have that
[a]_{0,\alpha}(2r)^{\alpha}<a_{\mathcal{P}}
. This allows us to use the preceding estimate and, in case
q>p
, Young’s inequality with exponents
(\tfrac{q}{p},\tfrac{q}{q-p})
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}

\displaystyle\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u-f|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq(a_{\mathcal{P}}+a^{-}_{y,2r}+[a]_{0,\alpha}(2r)^{\alpha})\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}\bigg(\frac{|v|}{r}\bigg)^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}

\displaystyle\leq 3ca^{-}_{y,2r}\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}|\nabla v|^{q\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}

(8.8)

\displaystyle\leq 3c\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(y,2r)}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla v|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}},

with
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha)
. We note that the same estimate also holds for
q=p
.

Now, we proceed with both cases simultaneously.
To this end, we combine (
8.6
) and (
8.8
) and we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}

\displaystyle\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u-f|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\|\nabla(u-f)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{1}(q-p)}\Big]\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg]+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}.

If we further apply the Caccioppoli inequality of Lemma
8.2
together the previous estimate, we infer the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)|}

\displaystyle\int_{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)\cap\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|B(z,r)|}\int_{B(z,r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u-f|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c}{|B(z,r)|}\int_{B(z,r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,|\nabla f|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{|B(y,2r)|}\int_{B(y,2r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,\frac{|u-f|}{r}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c}{|B(z,3r)|}\int_{B(z,3r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,|\nabla f|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\|\nabla(u-f)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{\theta_{1}(q-p)}\Big]\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(z,3r)|}\int_{B(z,3r)\cap\Omega}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{|B(z,3r)|}\int_{B(z,3r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,|\nabla f|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big)^{\theta_{1}(q-p)}\Big]\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{|B(z,3r)|}\int_{B(z,3r)\cap\Omega}\varphi^{\theta_{1}}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c}{|B(z,3r)|}\int_{B(z,3r)\cap\Omega}\varphi\left(x,|\nabla f|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\bigg],

with a constant
c=c(n,p,q,\alpha,[a]_{0,\alpha},\nu,L,c_{\mathrm{fat}},C_{Q})
.
Note that, due to Lemma
8.1
, the previous inequality also holds if
B(r,z)\subset\Omega
, and therefore, for every
z\in\Omega
.
Let us now define the functions
g,h\colon\mathds{R}^{n}\to\mathds{R}_{\geq 0}
as

g(x)=\begin{cases}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)&\textnormal{for }x\in\Omega,\\
0&\text{else},\end{cases}

and

h(x)=a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{n}{p\alpha}}+\begin{cases}\varphi(x,|\nabla f|)&\text{for }x\in\Omega,\\
0&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Then the preceding shows

\displaystyle\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle g\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq c\Big[1+[a]_{0,\alpha}\big(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\big)^{\theta_{1}(q-p)}\Big]\Bigg[\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,3r)}g^{\theta_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}}+\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,3r)}h\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg].

Applying Gehring’s Lemma, i.e. Lemma
4.8
, yields the existence of a constant
c
and exponent
1<\sigma\leq\sigma_{0}
, both depending on
n,p,q,\alpha,[a]_{0,\alpha},\nu,L,c_{\mathrm{fat}},C_{Q},\sigma_{0}
and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
, such that

(8.9)

\displaystyle\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B\left(z,\frac{r}{2}\right)}g^{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\leq c\Bigg[\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,4r)}g\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}\!\int_{B(z,4r)}h^{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\Bigg].

At this stage, we prove global higher integrability by the following covering argument.
Let

\varrho=\frac{1}{8}\min\left\{\hat{r}_{o},\bigg(\frac{a_{\mathcal{P}}}{2(1+[a]_{0,\alpha})}\bigg)^{1/\alpha}\right\}.

Consider the collection of balls

\mathcal{B}=\left\{B\left(z,\frac{\varrho}{10}\right)\ :\ z\in\Omega\right\}

covering
\overline{\Omega}
.
By Vitali’s covering theorem (see for instance
[
42
, Lemma 1.13]
for a version using open balls) and the fact that
\overline{\Omega}
is compact, there exist
k\in\mathds{N}
and points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\in\Omega
such that the balls
B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{10}\right)
for
i=1,\ldots,k
are disjoint and

\overline{\Omega}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{2}\right).

We have chosen this approach since it allows us to determine
k
in terms of
n
,
\Omega
and
\varrho
.
Indeed, since
B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{10}\right)\subset B\left(z_{o},\frac{\varrho}{10}+\diam(\Omega)\right)
for any
z_{o}\in\Omega
and
i=1,\ldots,k
and since the balls
B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{10}\right)
are pairwise disjoint, we find that

k|B(0,1)|\left(\frac{\varrho}{10}\right)^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{10}\right)\right|\leq\left|B\left(z_{o},\frac{\varrho}{10}+\diam(\Omega)\right)\right|=|B(0,1)|\left(\frac{\varrho}{10}+\diam(\Omega)\right)^{n}.

Therefore, we have that

k\leq\left(1+\frac{10\diam(\Omega)}{\varrho}\right)^{n}.

Now, we apply (
8.9
) on the individual balls
B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{2}\right)
.
Taking also the preceding estimate for
k
into account and recalling the definitions of
g
and
h
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\varphi^{\sigma}(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\bigg(\int_{B\left(z_{i},\frac{\varrho}{2}\right)}g^{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Bigg[\varrho^{\frac{(1-\sigma)n}{\sigma}}\int_{B(z,3\varrho)}g\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{B(z,3\varrho)}h^{\sigma}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq ck\Bigg[\varrho^{\frac{(1-\sigma)n}{\sigma}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\sigma}(x,|\nabla f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}+\max\Big\{1,a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\Big\}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\left(1+\frac{\diam(\Omega)}{\varrho}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\Bigg[\varrho^{\frac{(1-\sigma)n}{\sigma}}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x,|\nabla u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{\sigma}(x,|\nabla f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}+a_{\mathcal{P}}^{-\frac{p}{q-p}}\Bigg].

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 8.3
.

If
a>0
on the entirety of
\partial\Omega
, the assumption on
\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
in Theorem
1.8
reduces to
q
-fatness, and if
a\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
, we assume that
\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is locally
p
-fat.
According to
[
13
]
, in both of these cases, the respective fatness condition is optimal.
Moreover, the following example shows that we have to assume local
p
-fatness of
\mathds{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
at least at the boundary points where
a
vanishes.
Indeed, let
0<p<n
and let
K\subset B(0,1)
be a compact set such that
0\in K
,

\cp_{p}(K,B(0,2))>0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dim_{\mathcal{H}}(K)=n-p,

where
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the Hausdorff dimension.
Further, set
\Omega=B(0,2)\setminus K
, consider a function
g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B(0,2))
with
g\equiv 1
on
K
and let
u
denote the minimizer of the
p
-energy functional

v\mapsto\int_{\Omega}|Dv|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

in the class
g+W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Since
u
is a weak solution to the
p
-Laplacian,
\partial B(0,2)
is smooth and
g
vanishes in the vicinity of
\partial B(0,2)
, we have that
u\in C^{1,\beta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega\cup\partial B(0,2))
for some
\beta>0
, see e.g.
[
35
,
48
,
56
,
57
]
, and therefore

\sup\limits_{x\in\overline{B(0,2)}\setminus B(0,1)}|Du|(x)<\infty.

Furthermore, for any ball
B(x,4r)\subset\Omega
we have the quantitative estimate

\sup\limits_{z\in B(x,r)}|Du|^{p}(z)\leq C(n,p)\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{B(x,2r)}|Du|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Now let
r=\dist(x,\partial\Omega)/4
in the above formula and note that
0\in K
implies that
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)=\dist(x,K)
for every
x\in B(0,1)\setminus K
. Then, we obtain that

\displaystyle|Du|^{p}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{z\in B(x,r)}|Du|^{p}(z)\leq\frac{C(n,p)}{\dist(x,\partial\Omega)^{n}}\int_{B(x,2r)}|Du|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(n,p)}{\dist(x,\partial\Omega)^{n}}\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{C(n,p)\|Du\|^{p}_{p,\Omega}}{\dist(x,K)^{n}}.

However, by
[
41
, Remark 3.3]
, see also
[
42
, Example 12.9]
for an alternative proof,
u
is not globally integrable to any higher exponent than
p
, i.e. there is no
\delta>0
with
Du\in L^{p+\delta}(\Omega)
.
Now, we consider the double-phase integral with Lipschitz continuous coefficient
a(x):=\dist(x,K)
and any
p<q<p(1+1/n)
.
By the preceding estimate, we find that

\kappa:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}a(x)|Du|^{q-p}(x)<\infty.

Further, since
u
is a minimizer of the
p
-energy functional, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[u;\spt(u-v)]

\displaystyle=\int_{\spt(u-v)}(|Du|^{p}+a(x)|Du|^{q})\,\mathrm{d}x\leq(1+\kappa)\int_{\spt(u-v)}|Du|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq(1+\kappa)\int_{\spt(u-v)}|Dv|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq(1+\kappa)\mathcal{F}[v;\spt(u-v)],

for any
v\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
with
\spt(u-v)\subset\Omega
.
This shows that
u
is a quasi-minimizer of
\mathcal{F}
with
C_{Q}=1+\kappa
and thus every assumption of Theorem
1.8
but the local
p
-fatness condition in a neighborhood of
\{x\in\partial\Omega:a(x)=0\}
is satisfied.
Thus, in general the conclusion of Theorem
1.8
does not hold without this fatness condition, because we would otherwise get a contradiction to the fact that
Du\notin L^{p+\delta}(\Omega)
for any
\delta>0
.

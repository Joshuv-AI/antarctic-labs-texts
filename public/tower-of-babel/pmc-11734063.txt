Prenatal Identification of Intracardiac Mass Expedites Surgical Management of Capillary-Venous Malformation
Authors: Derek M Norton, Mounica Y Rao, Jianhong Zhang, M John Hicks, Anitha Parthiban, S Kristen Sexson Tejtel
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFetal and neonatal cardiac tumors are rare and often benign. Clinical presentation is primarily related to mass effect, pericardial effusion or arrhythmia. Prenatal detection can assist with risk assessment and inform optimal delivery plan and postnatal management. We report a fetus with a right atrial mass discovered in the third trimester. After successful surgical resection, pathology confirmed the mass to be a capillary-venous malformation, which are rare and challenging to diagnose. The true incidence of these vascular malformations within cardiac spaces is difficult to discern given the historic classification of numerous different vascular malformations under the umbrella term hemangioma.Key Words: congenital heart disease, fetal mass, intracardiac tumor, right atrial mass

A 36-year-old female presented at 37 weeks’ gestation for fetal echocardiogram to evaluate a newly discovered intracardiac mass. A prior study performed at 27 weeks’ gestation following an incomplete anatomy ultrasound was unremarkable. Repeat echo demonstrated a heterogenous mass adherent to the posterior right atrial (RA) wall extending toward the tricuspid valve (TV) (Figure 1, Video 1). By color Doppler, the mass appeared avascular (Video 2). There was no obstruction to TV inflow, but Doppler revealed mild superior vena cava (SVC) obstruction. There was mild cardiomegaly without arrythmia or fetal hydrops. Given concerns regarding potential growth of the mass and development of hemodynamic compromise, elective cesarean delivery was recommended.

Fetal and Postnatal Echocardiogram Images of a Right Atrial Capillary-Venous Malformation

Images A and B are from the fetal echocardiogram performed at 37 weeks 1 day’s gestation. Images C and D are from postnatal transthoracic echocardiogram completed day-of-life 3. (A) Four-chamber view depicting the size of the newly identified right atrial (RA) mass. (B) Four-chamber view with color Doppler (Nyquist limit ± 0.375) showing an area of flow acceleration among the superior vena cava inflow, atrial septum, and RA mass. (C) Apical 4-chamber view redemonstrating the appearance of the RA mass postnatally. (D) Subcostal sagittal view with color Doppler (Nyquist limit ± 0.19) showing multiple areas of flow acceleration around the RA mass, and no vascular flow within the mass.

On admission, the neonate was hemodynamically stable. Physical examination revealed no murmurs, organomegaly, or cutaneous lesions. Transthoracic echocardiogram redemonstrated a heterogenous mass (22.8 × 16.6 mm) adherent to the posterior RA wall without vascular features (Figure 1, Videos 3 and 4). There was no SVC or TV obstruction. Repeat echocardiogram at 72 hours demonstrated flow acceleration at the SVC-RA junction (mean gradient of 3.0 mm Hg) (Figure 2, Video 5). Cardiac magnetic resonance was not pursued because of the technical limitations in obtaining adequate image quality in a newborn. Our differential diagnosis included atypical presentation of teratoma (considering heterogeneity, intracardiac location, and absence of pericardial effusion) vs hemangioma or solitary rhabdomyoma.

Spectral Doppler Profile From a Transthoracic Echo

This spectral Doppler profile from a transthoracic echo completed on day-of-life 3 highlights the evaluation of a region flow acceleration between the right atrial mass and the atrial septum (as noted in Video 5). The measured mean gradient through this narrowed region is ∼3 mm Hg with a flow pattern suggesting mild, but variable/dynamic obstruction.

After multidisciplinary discussion, surgical resection was performed on day-of-life 9. Intraoperative evaluation revealed a lobulated mass arising from the RA free wall in the lower half of the interatrial groove without epicardial breach. Pathologic evaluation of the excised mass showed vascular structures, primarily capillaries, but with features of venous vessels with valve-like structures present, consistent with capillary-venous malformation (Figure 3). The tissue stained positive for CD31 and ERG (characteristic of endothelial cells), negative for GLUT1 (infantile hemangioma), and negative for PDPN (lymphangioma). No malignant features were identified. Since surgery, the patient has remained clinically asymptomatic and is doing well with follow-up echocardiogram 1 month later showing no recurrence of the mass.

Histologic Appearance of the Capillary-Venous Malformation

(A to C) Venous vessels with valve-like structures (arrows) within lumenal spaces (hematoxylin and eosin stain). (D) CD31 highlights endothelial cells lining the vessels and the valve-like structures (arrow). (E and F) Capillary proliferation with small lumens lined by endothelial cells, note red blood cells in lumens of capillaries (E: hematoxylin and eosin stain; F: CD31 immunostain).

Primary cardiac tumors in fetal life are rare and comprise approximately 0.14% of fetal cardiac referrals.1 Vascular tumors represent a small fraction of these referrals. Complicating the matter, many vascular lesions have been previously mischaracterized as “hemangiomas,” despite true hemangiomas having a different course than other vascular malformations—notably potential for spontaneous resolution and response to beta-blocker therapy.2 In this case, the malformation was similar to a hemangioma with regard to its RA location; however, vascularity was undetectable by low Nyquist limit color Doppler evaluation. With advances in histologic and immunohistochemical evaluation, we can better delineate true vascular tumors from more benign malformations per the International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies classification schema and correlate with echocardiographic features in hopes of improving diagnostic accuracy.3 This unique case highlights challenges in accurate characterization of vascular cardiac masses by echocardiography. Prenatal detection can expedite diagnosis and facilitate early management. Histopathologic analysis remains the gold standard for diagnosis and dictates expectations for long-term management, including recurrence risk and potential for other organ involvement or genetic syndromes.

Video 1

This clip from the fetal echo performed at 37 weeks 1 day’s gestation shows a bicaval view of the heart with the inferior vena cava seen extending toward the upper right portions of the screen. The spinal column (posterior) extends along the inferior aspect of the screen. This 2-dimensional image depicts the large mass occupying the right atrium and extending superiorly. The mass appears adherent to the posterior right atrial wall. The atrial septum and foramen ovale are seen below the mass on this clip with clear separation between the structures

Video 2

This clip from the fetal echo performed at 37 weeks 1 day’s gestation shows a 4-chamber view of the heart with the apex pointed downward, and the right side of the heart along the left side of the screen. A large mass occupies the right atrium. The left image depicts a 2-dimensional sweep superiorly to highlight the extension of the mass. The right image includes color Doppler assessment of the same sweep at a low Nyquist limit. There does not appear to be significant vascularity or blood flow within the mass

Video 3

This clip from a transthoracic echo completed on day-of-life 3 depicts a subcostal coronal view sweeping from posterior to anterior. The large, heterogenous mass is seen occupying a substantial portion of the visible right atrium. The mass extends toward the superior vena cava

Video 4

This clip from transthoracic echo completed on day-of-life 3 depicts a parasternal short-axis view starting at the base of the heart and sweeping toward the apex. The large, heterogenous mass is seen occupying a sizable portion of the visible right atrium. There is no apparent involvement of the tricuspid valve or impairment of valve function

Video 5

This clip from a transthoracic echo completed on day-of-life 3 depicts a subcostal coronal view focusing on the right atrial mass. Color Doppler evaluation (Nyquist ± 0.64) shows acceleration of flow from the superior vena cava as it passes between the right atrial mass and the atrial septum

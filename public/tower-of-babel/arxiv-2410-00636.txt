Radial blow-up standing solutions for the semilinear wave equation
Maissâ Boughrara, Hatem Zaag

Abstract
We consider the semilinear wave equation with a power nonlinearity in the radial case. Given r 0 > 0 r_{0}>0 , we construct a blow-up solution such that the solution near ( r 0 , T ⁡ ( r 0 ) ) (r_{0},T(r_{0})) converges exponentially to a soliton. Moreover, we show that r 0 r_{0} is a non-characteristic point. For that, we translate the question in self-similar variables and use a modulation technique. We will also use energy estimates from the one dimensional case treated in [ MZ07 ] . Of course because of the radial setting, we have an additional gradient term which is delicate to handle. That’s precisely the purpose of our paper.



1
Introduction

We are concerned with blow-up phenomena for real-valued solutions of the following semilinear wave equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial^{2}_{t}U=\Delta U+|U|^{p-1}U,\\
&U(.,0)=U_{0},\\
&U_{t}(.,0)=U_{1},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
1<p
, if
N\geq 2
then
p\leq 1+\frac{4}{N-1}
, and
(U_{0},U_{1})\in H^{1}_{loc,u}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}_{loc,u}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The space
L^{2}_{loc,u}
is the set of all
v
in
L^{2}_{loc}
such that

||v||_{L^{2}_{loc,u}}:=\underset{a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\sup}\left(\int_{|x-a|<1}|v(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<+\infty,

(1.2)

and the space
H^{1}_{loc,u}=\{v\in L^{2}_{loc,u}|\nabla v\in L^{2}_{loc,u}\}
. The Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) can be solved locally in time in the space
H^{1}_{loc,u}\times L^{2}_{loc,u}
(see
[
GSV92
]
and
[
LS95
]
). For the existence of blow-up solutions, it follows from ODE techniques or the energy-based blow-up criterion of Levine in
[
Lev74
]
.

Concerning the blow-up behavior for general solutions (i.e. with no symmetry assumptions), we first mention the result of Merle and Zaag in
[
MZ03
]
,
[
MZ05
]
and
[
MZ05a
]
who showed that the blow-up rate is given by
C(T-t)^{-\frac{2}{p-1}}
, i.e. the solution of the associated ODE
u"=u^{p}
, where
T
is the (local) blow-up time.

As for the derivation of the asymptotic behavior of the blow-up, it depends on the dimension. Indeed, if
N=1
, all has been described by Merle and Zaag in a series of papers (
[
MZ07
]
,
[
MZ08
]
,
[
MZ12a
]
,
[
MZ12
]
…etc). In higher dimension
N\geq 2
, some partial results are available in
[
MZ15
]
,
[
MZ16
]
… etc.

The main obstruction in the generalization of the one-dimensional case to higher dimension stems from the lack of a classification of all self-similar solutions of (
1.1
) in the energy spaces. For that reason, Merle and Zaag addressed in
[
MZ11
]
the radial case, which reduces to a perturbed one dimensional case, if we focus on the blow-up behavior outside the origin. In particular, they showed that the solution in similarity variables converges to the one-dimensional soliton. However, they gave no example of initial data which behaves like that. This is precisely our aim in this paper. Therefore, we assume here that
U
is a radial solution. Introducing

u(r,t)=U(x,t)\text{ where }r=|x|,

(1.3)

we rewrite (
1.1
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial^{2}_{t}u=\partial^{2}_{r}u+\frac{(N-1)}{r}\partial_{r}u+|u|^{p-1}u,\\
&\partial_{r}u(0,.)=0,\\
&u(.,0)=u_{0},\\
&u_{t}(.,0)=u_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

If
u
blows up then we define a 1-Lipschitz curve
\Gamma=\{(r,T(r)),r\geq 0\}
such that the domain of definition
D
called the maximum influence domain of
u
is written as the following

D=\{(r,t)|t<T(r)\}.

\Gamma
is called the blow-up graph of
u
. A point
r_{0}
is a non-characteristic point if there are

\delta_{0}\in(0,1)\text{ and }t_{0}<T(r_{0})\text{ such that }u\text{ is defined on }\mathcal{C}_{r_{0},T(r_{0}),\delta_{0}}\cap\{t\geq t_{0}\}\cap\{r\geq 0\},

(1.5)

where
C_{\bar{r},\bar{t},\bar{\delta}}=\{(r,t)|t<\bar{t}-\bar{\delta}|r-\bar{r}|\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
(resp.
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
) the set of non characteristic (resp. characteristic) points.

1.1
Explicit solutions

Given
r_{0}>0
,
T>0
, we define for all
|r-r_{0}|<T-t
,
|d|<1
and
\nu>-1+|d|
the following family of functions

\hat{u}(d,\nu,r_{0},T,r,t)=\kappa_{0}\frac{(1-d^{2})^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{((1+\nu)(T-t)+d(r-r_{0}))^{\frac{2}{p-1}}}\text{ where }\kappa_{0}=\left(\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

(1.6)

One may see that
\hat{u}(d,0,r_{0},T)
is a blow-up solution of (
1.4
) in one dimension, for
\nu=0
. Given
r_{0}>0
and
T_{0}\leq T(r_{0})
, we introduce the following similarity variables

\begin{split}y=\frac{r-r_{0}}{T_{0}-t},\ s=-\log(T_{0}-t).\\
w_{r_{0},T_{0}}(y,s)=(T_{0}-t)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}u(r,t).\end{split}

(1.7)

The function
w=w_{r_{0},T_{0}}
satisfies the following equation for
|y|<1
and
s>-\log T_{0}
:

\partial^{2}_{s}w=\mathcal{L}w-\frac{2(p+1)}{(p-1)^{2}}w+|w|^{p-1}w-\frac{p+3}{p-1}\partial_{s}w-2y\partial^{2}_{y,s}w+e^{-s}\frac{(N-1)}{r_{0}+ye^{-s}}\partial_{y}w,

(1.8)

where

\mathcal{L}w=\frac{1}{\rho}\partial_{y}(\rho(1-y^{2})\partial_{y}w)\text{ and }\rho(y)=(1-y^{2})^{\frac{2}{p-1}}.

(1.9)

We have for all
|d|<1
the following solitons defined by

\kappa(d,y)=\kappa_{0}\left(\frac{1-d^{2}}{(1+dy)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\text{ where }|y|<1,

(1.10)

and
\kappa_{0}
is given in (
1.6
). Note that
\kappa(d)
is a stationary solution of (
1.8
) in one space dimension. We introduce the ”generalized solitons” or solitons for short defined for all
|y|<1
,
|d|<1
and
\nu>-1+|d|
, by
\kappa^{*}(d,\nu,y)=\left(\kappa_{1}^{*},\kappa_{2}^{*}\right)(d,\nu,y)
where

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{*}(d,\nu,y)=\kappa_{0}\frac{\left(1-d^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(1+dy+\nu)^{\frac{2}{p-1}}},

(1.11)

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}^{*}(d,\nu,y)=\nu\partial_{\nu}\kappa_{1}^{*}(d,\nu,y)=-\frac{2\kappa_{0}\nu}{p-1}\frac{\left(1-d^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{(1+dy+\nu)^{\frac{p+1}{p-1}}}.

(1.12)

Notice also that for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\overline{\kappa^{*}}(d,\mu,y,s)=\kappa^{*}(d,\mu e^{s},y)
is a solution of the first order vector-valued formulation of (
1.8
) in dimension one.

1.2
Main results

We first introduce the following spaces:

\mathcal{H}=\{(q_{1},q_{2})\in H^{1}_{loc}\times L^{2}_{loc}|\ ||(q_{1},q_{2})||_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int^{1}_{-1}\left(q_{1}^{2}+(q_{1}^{\prime})^{2}(1-y^{2})+q_{2}^{2}\right)\rho dy<+\infty\},

(1.13)

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{q_{1}\in H^{1}_{loc}|\ ||q_{1}||_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{2}=\int^{1}_{-1}\left(q_{1}^{2}+(q_{1}^{\prime})^{2}(1-y^{2})\right)\rho dy<+\infty\},

(1.14)

where
\rho
is given in (
1.9
). Note that the norm of the
\mathcal{H}
space can be defined by the inner product

||q||_{\mathcal{H}}=\sqrt{\phi(q,q)},

(1.15)

where

\phi(q,r)=\int^{1}_{-1}\left(q_{1}r_{1}+q_{1}^{\prime}r_{1}^{\prime}(1-y^{2})+q_{2}r_{2}\right)\rho dy.

(1.16)

Using integration by parts and the definition of
\mathcal{L}
in (
1.9
), we have the following identity

\phi(q,r)=\int^{1}_{-1}\left(q_{1}(-\mathcal{L}r_{1}+r_{1})+q_{2}r_{2}\right)\rho dy.

(1.17)

The following is our main result giving us the existence of blow-up solutions converging to solitons, for well-chosen initial data.

Theorem 1

(A solution converging to a soliton in similarity variables)
.

There exists
\delta\in(0,1)
, such that for all
r_{0}>0
and
\hat{d}_{0}\in(-1,1)
, there exist
s_{0}>-\log r_{0}
,
\nu_{0},d_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that equation (
1.4
) with the initial data given by

\binom{u_{0}(r)}{u_{1}(r)}=\chi(r)\binom{\hat{u}(d_{0},\nu_{0},r_{0},T_{0},r,0)}{\frac{1}{1+\nu_{0}}\partial_{t}\hat{u}(d_{0},\nu_{0},r_{0},T_{0},r,0)},\text{ for all }r\geq r_{0},

(1.18)

with
T_{0}=e^{-s_{0}}
, has a solution
u(r,t)
defined for all
(r,t)\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}
such that
0\leq t<T(r)
for some 1-Lipschitz curve
r\mapsto T(r)
. Moreover,
r_{0}
is a non-characteristic point,

and for

satisfies

\left|\left|\binom{w(s)}{\partial_{s}w(s)}-\binom{\kappa(\hat{d}_{0})}{0}\right|\right|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq Ce^{-\delta(s-s_{0})-s_{0}}.

(1.19)

where

on

3
Dynamics in similarity variables

The aim of this section is to obtain an exponential bound of

. This will be achieved in two steps, given in two separate subsections:

We first kill nonnegative modes of the linear operator, given in (
3.18
), around the soliton. To do such a thing, we need to choose the correct parameters in (

Then, we project the equation satisfied by

3.1
Modulation theory

Here, we impose some orthogonality conditions related to nonnegative direction of the linearized operator of equation (

) satisfied by

[

]

Proposition 3.1

(Modulation technique)
.

For all
B\geq 1
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(B)>0
, such that for all
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}

and for

-1+\frac{1}{B}\leq\frac{\hat{\nu}}{1-|\hat{d}|}\leq B,\ ||\hat{q}||_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\varepsilon,

\hat{q}=v-\kappa^{*}(\hat{d},\hat{\nu})\ \text{ and }\ \hat{d^{*}}=\frac{\hat{d}}{1+\hat{\nu}}=-\tanh\hat{\xi^{*}}.

Then, there exists
(d,\nu)\in(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}

(i)

for

\pi^{d^{*}}_{l}(q)=0

(ii)

\left|\frac{\nu}{1-|d|}-\frac{\hat{\nu}}{1-|\hat{d}|}\right|+|\xi^{*}-\hat{\xi^{*}}|\leq C(B)||\hat{q}||_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C(B)\varepsilon
.

(iii)

-1+\frac{1}{2B}\leq\frac{\nu}{1-|d|}\leq B+1

.

.

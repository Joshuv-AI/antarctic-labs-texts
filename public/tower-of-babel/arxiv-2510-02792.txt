Super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term
Lei Liu, Mingjun Wei

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term, a natural generalization of Liouville equation, super-Liouville equation and spinorial Yamabe type equation. We establish some refined qualitative properties for such a blow-up sequence. In particular, we show energy identities not only for the spinor part but also for the function part. Moreover, the local masses at a blow-up point are also computed. A new phenomenon is that there are two kinds of singularities and local masses due to the nonlinear spinorial Yamabe type term, which is different from super-Liouville equation.



1.
Introduction

The compactness of the solution space of a nonlinear partial differential equation plays an important role in the study of the corresponding existence problem. In dimension two, many interesting variational problems in geometry and physics are borderline cases of the Palais-Smale condition and thus one can not use variational methods directly to get the existence result, such as harmonic maps, minimal and prescribed mean curvature surfaces in Riemannian manifold, Liouville type equation, Ginzburg-Landau type problems and so on.

The classical Liouville functional for a real-valued function
u
on a Riemann surface
(M,g)
is

L_{1}(u)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+K_{g}u-e^{2u}\right)dM,

where
K_{g}
is the Gauss curvature of
M
. The Euler-Lagrange equation for
L_{1}
is the following Liouville equation

-\Delta_{g}u=2e^{2u}-K_{g},

which plays a fundamental role in many two dimensional physical models, complex analysis and differential geometry of Riemann surface, in particular in the problem of prescribed Gaussian curvature. The blow-up analysis for Liouville equation was systematically developed by Brezis-Merle
[
1
]
, Li-Shafrir
[
7
]
and Li
[
6
]
etc.

In physics, Liouville type equation also occurs naturally in string theory as discovered by Polyakov
[
13
]
, from the gauge anomaly in quantizing the string action. There is also a natural supersymmetric version of the Liouville functional, coupling the bosonic scalar field to a fermionic spinor field. Motivated from supersymmetric string theory, Jost-Wang-Zhou
[
9
]
introduced the following super-Liouville functional for a real-valued function
u
and a complex valued spinor
\psi

L_{2}(u,\psi)=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+K_{g}u+\langle(\not{D}_{g}+e^{u})\psi,\psi\rangle_{\Sigma M}-e^{2u}\right)dM,

which is conformally invariant.
Here,
(M,g)
is a Riemann surface with a fixed spin structure,
\Sigma M
the spinor bundle over
M
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Sigma M}
the metric on
\Sigma M
. Choosing a local orthonormal basis
{e_{\alpha},\alpha=1,2}
on
M
, the Dirac operator is
defined as
\not{D}_{g}:=e_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{e_{\alpha}}
, where
\nabla
is the spin connection on
\Sigma M
and
\cdot
is the Clifford multiplication. This multiplication is skew-adjoint:

\langle X\cdot\psi,\varphi\rangle_{\Sigma M}=-\langle\psi,X\cdot\varphi\rangle_{\Sigma M}

for any
X\in\Gamma(TM)
,
\psi,\ \varphi\in\Gamma(\Sigma M)
.

The Euler-Lagrange equation for
L_{2}
is the following super-Liouville equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g}u&=2e^{2u}-e^{u}\langle\psi,\psi\rangle-K_{g},\\
-\not{D}_{g}\psi&=e^{u}\psi,\end{cases}\ \ in\ \ M.

Similarly to Liouville equation, super-Liouville equation is also conformally invariant, which implies that the solution space is in general not compact. The blow-up analysis for super-Liouville equation was systematically developed in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
etc.

In two dimension, there is a nonlinear Dirac-type system, the so called spinorial Yamabe type equation which is defined as follows:

(1.1)

\not{D}_{g}\psi=H(x)|\psi|^{2}\psi\ \ in\ \ M,

where
|\psi|^{2}=\langle\psi,\psi\rangle_{\Sigma M}
,
\psi\in\Gamma(\Sigma M)
. This type of nonlinear Dirac equations appears naturally in geometry and physics. Firstly, by spinorial Weierstrass representation, the solution of (
1.1
) can be used to find the existence of prescribed mean curvature surfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(here the function
H
plays the role of the mean curvature). See for instance
[
14
]
. Secondly, consider the Dirac-harmonic map with curvature term introduced by Chen-Jost-Wang
[
2
,
3
]
, which was derived from the nonlinear supersymmetric
\sigma
-model of quantum field theory, then the nonlinear Dirac equation for the spinor reduces to (
1.1
) when the map is a constant. The blow-up analysis for (
1.1
) was studied by
[
16
,
4
]
.

In this paper, we consider the following functional

(1.2)

L(u,\psi):=\int_{M}\left(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+K_{g}u+\langle(\not{D}_{g}+e^{u})\psi,\psi\rangle_{\Sigma M}+F(x)|\psi|^{4}-e^{2u}\right)dM,

where
F(x):M\to\mathbb{R}
is a
C^{1}
function. The Euler-Lagrange equation for
L
is the following system

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u&=2e^{2u}-e^{u}\langle\psi,\psi\rangle-K_{g},\\
\not{D}_{g}\psi&=-e^{u}\psi-2F|\psi|^{2}\psi,\end{cases}\ \ in\ \ M.

We call (
1.3
) the
super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term
. It is easy to see that the special forms of (
1.3
) include Liouville equation (
\psi\equiv 0
), super-Liouville equation (
F\equiv 0
) and the spinorial Yamabe-type equation (
1.1
) (when the function
u
vanishes). The important point is that this generalization preserves a fundamental property of the energy functional on Riemann surfaces, i.e. the conformal invariance.

In present paper, we will provide an analytic properties for super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term, such as removable singularity, Brezis-Merle type concentration phenomenon, energy identity, local masses and so on.

Denote

E(u,\psi;\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}\left(e^{2u}+|\psi|^{4}\right)dM,\ \ \Omega\subset M.

Now we state our first main result which is about the following Brezis-Merle type concentration phenomenon.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(u_{n},\psi_{n})
be a sequence of smooth solutions of (
1.3
) with bounded energy

E(u_{n},\psi_{n};M)\leq C.

Define

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in M|\ \mbox{there is a sequence of points }x_{n}\to x\mbox{ such that }u_{n}(x_{n})\to+\infty\},

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{x\in M|\ \mbox{there is a sequence of points }x_{n}\to x\mbox{ such that }|\psi_{n}(x_{n})|\to+\infty\}.

Then
\Sigma=\Sigma_{1}\cup\Sigma_{2}
is a finite point set. Passing to a subsequence, we have

(1)

\psi_{n}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(M\setminus\Sigma_{2})
.

(2)

For
u_{n}
, one of the following alternatives holds:

(a)

u_{n}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}(M)
.

(b)

u_{n}\to-\infty
uniformly on
M
.

(c)

\Sigma
is nonempty and either
u_{n}
is bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(M\setminus\Sigma)
or
u_{n}\to-\infty
uniformly on any compact subsets of
M\setminus\Sigma
.

Different from super-Liouville equation
[
9
]
where
\Sigma_{2}\subset\Sigma_{1}
, super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term will no longer possess such property. This is why we define two kinds of singularities below (see Definition
1.4
).

Next we want to construct the full blow up theory for (
1.3
). Similar to Liouville equation and super-Liouville system, this should contain the energy identity for solutions, the blow-up values, and the profile of
solutions near the blow-up point.

For this aim, we first establish the following energy identity for the spinor.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be a closed Riemannian surface with a fixed spin structure. Suppose
(u_{n},\psi_{n})
is a sequence of smooth solution to (
1.3
) with bounded energy
E(u_{n},\psi_{n};M)<C
.
Suppose
\Sigma\neq\emptyset
and set
\Sigma:=\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{l}\}
. Then there are finitely many solutions
(u^{i,k},\psi^{i,k}),\ \ i=1,\cdots,l;\ k=1,\cdots,L_{i}
of

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u^{i,k}&=2e^{2u^{i,k}}-e^{u^{i,k}}|\psi^{i,k}|^{2}-K_{g_{S^{2}}},\\
\not{D}\psi^{i,k}&=-e^{u^{i,k}}\psi^{i,k}-2\mu_{i}|\psi^{i,k}|^{2}\psi^{i,k},\end{cases}\ \ on\ \ S^{2},

where
\mu_{i}=F(x_{i})
is a constant,
(S^{2},g_{S^{2}})
is the standard two dimensional sphere,
K_{g_{S^{2}}}=1
is the Gauss curvature, such that
passing to a subsequence,
\psi_{n}
converges in
C_{loc}^{\infty}
to
\psi
on
M\setminus\Sigma
.

Moreover, we have the energy identity

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}|\psi_{n}|^{4}dM=\int_{M}|\psi|^{4}dM+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sum_{k=1}^{L_{i}}\int_{S^{2}}|\psi^{i,k}|^{4}dx.

Remark 1.3
.

In our paper, we call
(u,\psi)
is a bubble if it satisfies equation (
1.4
). We want to remark that for bubble solution (
1.4
), if
u=-\infty
, i.e. the function part vanishes, it reduces to a spinorial Yamabe type equation

\not{D}\psi=-2\mu|\psi|^{2}\psi\ \ on\ \ S^{2}.

Then we call
\psi

a spinorial Yamabe type bubble
. Otherwise, if the function part does not vanish, we call
(u,\psi)

a super-Liouville type bubble
.

For super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term, Theorem
1.1
tells us that there may appear two kinds of bubbles at a same blow-up point, i.e. super-Liouville type bubble and spinorial Yamabe type bubble, which makes the blow-up phenomenon and related analysis different and in fact more complicated than Liouville equation and super-Liouville equation. In order to characterise the refined quantitative properties, we need to define two kinds of singularities for the blow-up set
\Sigma
.

Since
\Sigma
has finite points, we can take
r_{0}>0
small such that
B_{r_{0}}(x_{i}),\ x_{i}\in\Sigma
are mutually disjointed.

Definition 1.4
.

p\in\Sigma
is called a first type singular point for
(u_{n},\psi_{n})
if there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\big(\max_{x\in\overline{B_{r_{0}}(p)}}u_{n}(x)-2\ln(1+\max_{x\in\overline{B_{r_{0}}(p)}}|\psi_{n}(x)|)\big)\geq-C.

Otherwise, we call
p\in\Sigma
a second type singular point.

We want to remark that for super-Liouville equations
[
9
]
, the second type singularity will not appear.

Denote

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}:=\bigg\{p\in\Sigma\ |\ \ p\ \ \mbox{ is a first type singular point}\bigg\}.

With the help of Spinor’s energy identity Theorem
1.2
, if the blow-up set
\Sigma_{3}\neq\emptyset
, then we can rule out the first case in
(c)
of Theorem
1.1
. We have the following result that

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
(u_{n},\psi_{n})
is a sequence of solution to (
1.3
) with finite energy condition
E(u_{n},\psi_{n};M)<C
and the blow-up set
\Sigma_{3}\neq\emptyset
, then

u_{n}\rightarrow-\infty\ \ uniformly\ \ on\ \ compact\ \ subset\ \ of\ \ M\setminus\Sigma

and

2e^{2u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\longrightarrow\sum_{x_{i}\in\Sigma_{3}}\alpha_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}+\sum_{x_{i}\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{3}}\beta_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}

in the sense of measure where
\alpha_{i}\geq 4\pi
.

Defining the blow-up value (or local mass) at a blow-up point
p\in\Sigma
as

m(p):=\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\int_{B_{r}(p)}\left(2e^{2u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{2}\right)dx,

different from Liouville equation and super-Liouville equation, we have two kinds of blow-up values due to the nonlinear spinor Yamabe term.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
\Sigma_{3}\neq\emptyset
. If
p\in\Sigma_{3}
, then
m(p)=4\pi.
If
p=x_{i}\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{3}
, then
m(p)=0\ \ or\ \ 4\pi.

Next we give a more clear picture for the blow-up analysis in Theorem
1.2
. We also show the energy identity for the function part
u_{n}
. Precisely, our result is

Theorem 1.7
.

Under assumptions and notations of Theorem
1.2
, suppose
\Sigma_{3}\neq\emptyset
. Denote

\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}:=\{p\in\Sigma\ |\ p\in\Sigma_{3}\ \ or\ \ p\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma_{3}\ \ with\ \ m(p)=4\pi\}.

Then for each
x_{i}\in\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}
, among the bubbles
(u^{i,k},\psi^{i,k})
,
k=1,...,L_{i}
in Theorem
1.2
, there is only one super-Liouville type bubble (w.l.o.g, denoted by
u^{i,1}
) where the other bubbles are all spinorial Yamabe type bubble.

Moreover, we have following energy identity for the function that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}e^{2u_{n}}dM=\sum_{x_{i}\in\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}}\int_{S^{2}}e^{2u^{i,1}}dx.

Lastly, we discuss the case that
\Sigma_{3}=\emptyset
, i.e. there is no first type singularity.

Theorem 1.8
.

Under assumptions and notations of Theorem
1.2
, suppose
\Sigma_{3}=\emptyset
. Then one of the following two alternatives holds.

(1)

u_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{loc}(M\setminus\Sigma)
, then passing to a subsequence, we have

(i)

(u_{n},\psi_{n})\to(u,\psi)
in
C^{2}_{loc}(M\setminus\Sigma)
where
(u,\psi)
satisfies super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term (
1.3
) in
M
.

(ii)

For any
p\in\Sigma
, there holds
m(p)=0
. This also implies that the bubbles
(u^{i,k},\psi^{i,k})
,
i=1,...,l
,
k=1,...,L_{i}
in Theorem
1.2
, are all spinorial Yamabe type bubble.

(iii)

Energy identity for the function

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}e^{2u_{n}}dM=\int_{M}e^{2u}dx.

(2)

Passing to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to-\infty
uniformly on any compact subset of
M\setminus\Sigma
. Then we have

(i)

For
p\in\Sigma
, there holds
m(p)=0
or
4\pi
.

(ii)

Denote

\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}:=\{p\in\Sigma\ |\ \ p\in\Sigma\ \ with\ \ m(p)=4\pi\}.

Then for each
x_{i}\in\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}
, among the bubbles
(u^{i,k},\psi^{i,k})
,
k=1,...,L_{i}
in Theorem
1.2
, there is only one super-Liouville type bubble (w.l.o.g, denoted by
u^{i,1}
) where the other bubbles are all spinorial Yamabe type bubble.

(iii)

Energy identity for the function

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{M}e^{2u_{n}}dM=\sum_{x_{i}\in\tilde{\Sigma}_{3}}\int_{S^{2}}e^{2u^{i,1}}dx.

At the end of this section, we want to remark that super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term is not just a simple promotion of super-Liouville equation. Comparing to Liouville equation or super-Liouville equation, the nonlinear spinorial Yamabe term in (
1.3
) will produce some different blow-up phenomenons which of course makes the relevant neck analysis more complicated, such as:

(1)

For super-Liouville equation, there is only first type singularity, while for super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term, there are two kinds of singularities (see Definition
1.4
). One can see that if we make
u
disappear, i.e.
u=-\infty
, it reduces to spinorial Yamabe type equation (
1.1
) and then all the singularities are of second type. Different singularities also generate different blow-up values. See Theorem
1.6
.

(2)

From the work
[
9
,
10
]
, it is now not hard to conclude that for super-Liouville equation, there is just one bubble at each blow-up point which is a super-Liouville type bubble. However, this property will no longer hold for super-Liouville equation with a spinorial Yamabe type term (see Theorem
1.2
), where there may appear finitely many bubbles and meanwhile contain two types.

(3)

To prove the energy identity for the function part, i.e. estimating the function’s integration
\int_{A_{n}}e^{2u_{n}}dx=o(1)
where
A_{n}
is a neck domain, the key idea is to transfer the problem to the estimate of spinor and then we can apply the spinor’s energy identity. Precisely, using equation (
1.3
) and integrating by parts, with the help of blow-up values and the classification of super-Liouville type bubble, we first show that
\int_{A_{n}}(2e^{2u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{2})dx=o(1)
. Then we just need to prove
\int_{A_{n}}e^{u_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{2}dx=o(1)
. For this, if there is only one bubble (e.g. for super-Liouville equation), then it is easy to see that its limit is
0
which is a direct consequence of the energy identity for spinor, i.e.
\int_{A_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{4}dx=o(1)
. However, here we need to deal with the case of multiple bubbles, which is the main difficulty and contribution of this paper. It needs more detailed and delicated analysis. See Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we will firstly establish some basic lemmas including small energy regularity lemma, Pohozaev type identity. Secondly, we will prove a Brezis-Merle type concentration property, i.e. Theorem
1.1
. In Section
3
, we will provide a necessary and sufficient condition for removing a local singularity. In Section
4
, we will show some classification properties for super-Liouville type bubble, such as the asymptotic behavior near infinity, the bubble’s energy and so on. We will prove the energy identity for the spinor in Section
5
, i.e. Theorem
1.2
. The proofs of Theorem
1.5
-Thorem
1.8
will be given in Section
6
.

2.
Some basic lemmas and Brezis-Merle type compactness

In this section, on one hand, we will establish some basic lemmas in the blow-up analysis for super-Liouville equation with a Yamabe term, such as a small energy regularity lemma, Pohozaev type identity and so on. On the other hand, we will prove a Brezis-Merle type concentration property, i.e. Theorem
1.1
.

We start this part by proving following small energy regularity lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(u_{n},\psi_{n})
be a sequence of smooth solutions of

(2.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n}&=2e^{2u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{2}+H^{1}_{n}(x)|\psi_{n}|^{4},\\
\not{D}\psi_{n}&=-e^{u_{n}}\psi_{n}+H^{2}_{n}(x)|\psi_{n}|^{2}\psi_{n},\end{cases}\ \ in\ \ B_{1}(0),

where
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\|H^{i}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))}\leq C
. Then there exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{1}
, such that if
E(u_{n},\psi_{n};B_{1})\leq\epsilon_{1}
, then the following alternatives hold:

(1)

\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})\leq C.

(2)

Either
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C
or
u_{n}
converges to
-\infty
uniformly in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)
.

(3)

If we additionally assume

osc_{\partial B_{1}(0)}u_{n}\leq C,\ \ \|\nabla H^{2}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))}\leq C,

then we have

osc_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)}u_{n}\leq C,\ \ \|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0))}+\|\nabla\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0))}\leq C\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1}(0))}.

Proof.

Step 1.
Estimate for
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{16}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}
.

Take a cut-off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
such that
0\leq\eta\leq 1
,
\eta|_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}}\equiv 1
and
\|\nabla\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
. From the equation of spinor (
1.3
) and standard elliptic estimates of Dirac operator, for any
1<p<2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta\psi_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\not{D}(\eta\psi_{n})\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+\|\eta\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|e^{u_{n}}\eta\psi_{n}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+\|\ |\psi_{n}|^{2}|\eta\psi_{n}|\ \|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+\||\nabla\eta||\psi_{n}|\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1})})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|e^{u_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}+\|\psi_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(B_{1})})\|\eta\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{2-p}}(B_{1})}+C\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}\|\eta\psi_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{1})}+C\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1})},

where we used Sobolev embedding theory and Young’s inequality. Now, take
p=\frac{16}{9}
and
\epsilon_{1}
small enough, we get
\|\eta\psi_{n}\|_{W^{1,p}(B_{1})}\leq C\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1})},
which implies
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{16}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq C\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{1})}.

Step 2.
Estimate for
\|u^{+}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}
and
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})}
.

Let
u_{n}^{1}
be the solution of the following Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{n}^{1}=2e^{2u_{n}}-e^{u_{n}}|\psi_{n}|^{2}+H_{n}^{1}(x)|\psi_{n}|^{4},\ \ &in\ \ B_{1},\\
u^{1}_{n}=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial B_{1}.\end{cases}

Since
\|\Delta u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\leq C\epsilon_{1},
taking
\epsilon_{1}
small, by Theorem 1 in
[
1
]
, we have
\int_{B_{1}}e^{8|u_{n}^{1}|}dx\leq C.

Now, let
u_{n}^{2}=u_{n}-u_{n}^{1}
. Then it is easy to see that
u_{n}^{2}
is a harmonic function in
B_{1}
. By mean value property, we have

\|(u_{n}^{2})^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq C\|(u_{n}^{2})^{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\leq C(\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}+\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})})\leq C,

where we used the fact that
\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}\leq\|e^{u_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1})}.

Then it is easy to see that
\|\Delta u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq C
which implies
\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{C^{0}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq C
. Thus, we arrived at

\|u_{n}^{+}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq C.

Combining this with the fact
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{16}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}\leq C
, by the standard elliptic estimates of Dirac operator and Sobolev embedding, we get

\|\psi_{n}\|_{W^{1,4}(B_{\frac{5}{8}})}\leq C(\|\not{D}\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})}+\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{\frac{3}{4}})})\leq C,

and
\|\psi_{n}\|_{C^{0}(B_{\frac{5}{8}})}\leq C.

Step 3.
Conclusions of the lemma.

Since
\Delta(u_{n}-u^{1}_{n})=0
in
B_{1}(0)
and
u_{n}-u^{1}_{n}
is bounded from above in
B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)
, then the second conclusion follows from Harnack’s inequality.

For the third conclusion, since
\Delta(u_{n}-u^{1}_{n})=0
in
B_{1}(0)
and
osc_{\partial B}(u_{n}-u^{1}_{n})\leq C
, by maximal principle property, we know
osc_{B_{\frac{3}{4}}(0)}u_{n}\leq C.

To estimate
\|\nabla\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0))}
, by above estimates, we now have that

\|\Delta(u_{n}(x)-\overline{u}_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{5}{8}}(0))}\leq C,\ \ i=1,2,

where
\overline{u}_{n}:=\frac{1}{|B_{\frac{5}{8}}(0)|}\int_{B_{\frac{5}{8}}(0)}u_{n}(x)dx
. The standard elliptic theory and embedding theory yields that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\frac{9}{16}}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{n}(x)-\overline{u}_{n}\|_{W^{2,4}(B_{\frac{9}{16}}(0))}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\Delta(u_{n}(x)-\overline{u}_{n})\|_{L^{4}(B_{\frac{5}{8}}(0))}+\|u_{n}(x)-\overline{u}_{n}\|_{L^{4}(B_{\frac{5}{8}}(0))}\right)

satisfy

C(u,\psi)=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\pi}.

In particular, the local singularity is removable iff
C(u,\psi)=0
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that

(3.2)

\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(e^{2u}+|\psi|^{4}\right)dx\leq\epsilon_{0},

where
\epsilon_{0}
is a small constant which will be determined later. Next, we divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1:
We prove that:

(3.3)

u(x)\leq-\log|x|+C,\ \ |\psi(x)|\leq C|x|^{-1/2},\forall x\in B_{1/2}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

For any
x_{0}\in B_{1/2}(0)\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\int_{B_{\frac{|x_{0}|}{2}}(x_{0})}\left(e^{2u}+|\psi|^{4}\right)dx\leq\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(e^{2u}+|\psi|^{4}\right)dx\leq\epsilon_{0}.

Let

\tilde{u}(x)=u(x_{0}+\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}|x)+\ln\frac{|x_{0}|}{2},\ \ \tilde{\psi}(x)=\left(\frac{|x_{0}|}{2}\right)^{1/2}\psi(x_{0}+\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}|x).

Then we have

\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(e^{2\tilde{u}}+|\tilde{\psi}|^{4}\right)dx\leq\epsilon_{0}

and

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{u}=2e^{2\tilde{u}}-e^{\tilde{u}}|\tilde{\psi}|^{2},\\
\not{D}\tilde{\psi}=-(e^{\tilde{u}}+2G|\tilde{\psi}|^{2})\tilde{\psi},\end{cases}\ \ x\in B_{1},

where
G(x)=F(x_{0}+\frac{1}{2}|x_{0}|x)
.

By Lemma
2.1
, we get

\tilde{u}(x)\leq C\ \ and\ \ |\tilde{\psi}(x)|\leq C,\ \ \forall\ x\in B_{1/2}(0).

Specially, there holds
\tilde{u}(0)\leq C
and
\tilde{\psi}(0)\leq C
.
This is

u(x_{0})\leq-\log|x_{0}|+C\ \ and\ \ \psi(x_{0})\leq C|x_{0}|^{-1/2}.

Step 2:
We prove that: there exists a constant
\gamma<2\pi
such that

(3.4)

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(x)}{-\log|x|}=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}.

Let
f:=2e^{2u}-e^{u}|\psi|^{2}
and
v(x)
be the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta v(x)=f(x)\ \ &in\ \ B_{1}(0),\\
v(x)=0,\ \ &on\ \ \partial B_{1}(0).\end{cases}

Then

(3.5)

v(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\log|x-y|f(y)dy+h(x),

where
h(x)\in C^{1}(B_{1}(0))
.

By a standard potential analysis, we have

(3.6)

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\frac{v(x)}{\ln|x|}=0.

Since
u-v
is a harmonic function in
B_{1}(0)\setminus\{0\}
and
u-v\leq-C\ln|x|+C
, we know that there exists a constant
\gamma
such that

(3.7)

u-v=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log|x|+w_{0}

where
w_{0}
is a smooth harmonic in
B_{1}(0)
. This immediately implies (
3.4
). Since
e^{2u}\in L^{1}(B_{1}(0))
, it is clear that
\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}<1
.

Step 3:
We prove that: there exists a small positive constant
\epsilon>0
such that

(3.8)

u(x)=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log|x|+h,\ \ \nabla u=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-1+\epsilon}).

By (
3.2
) and Theorem 1 in
[
1
]
, if we take
\epsilon_{0}
small, then we have
e^{|v|}\in L^{p}(B_{1}(0))
for some
p>100
. Using (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle e^{2u}+e^{u}|\psi|^{2}\leq C|x|^{-\frac{\gamma}{\pi}}e^{2v}+C|x|^{-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}-1}e^{v},

which implies

e^{2u}+e^{u}|\psi|^{2}\in L^{q}(B_{1}(0)),\ \ for\ \ some\ \ q>1.

Then
f\in L^{q}(B_{1}(0))
and the standard elliptic estimate yields
v\in C^{\beta}(B_{1}(0))
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. By (
3.7
), we get the first conclusion of the proposition and following estimate

osc_{B_{\frac{1}{2}|y|}(y)}u\leq osc_{B_{\frac{1}{2}|y|}(y)}\left(-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\ln|x|\right)+C\leq C,\ \ \forall\ y\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0).

It is follows from estimate (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) that there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle|\psi|

\displaystyle\leq C|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta},\ \ |\nabla\psi|

\displaystyle\leq C|x|^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta}.

Next we claim: there exists a small positive constant
\epsilon>0
such that

(3.9)

|\nabla v(x)|\leq C|x|^{-1+\epsilon}.

Since now we have
|f(x)|\leq C|x|^{-s}
for some
0<s<2
and
f\in L^{q}(B_{1}(0))
, then we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|f(y)|dy=\int_{\{|x-y|\geq\frac{|x|}{2}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|f(y)|dy+\int_{\{|x-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|f(y)|dy=\mathbf{II_{1}}+\mathbf{II_{2}}.

Denoting
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
, then we have

\mathbf{II_{1}}=\int_{\{|x-y|\geq\frac{|x|}{2}\}}|x-y|^{-1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}|x-y|^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}|f(y)|dy\leq C|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|x-y|^{-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}|f(y)|dy\leq C|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

and

\mathbf{II_{2}}=\int_{\{|x-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|f(y)|dy\leq\int_{\{|x-y|\leq\frac{|x|}{2}\}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\cdot|x|^{-s}dy\leq C|x|^{1-s}.

Combining above estimates and (
3.5
), we get (
3.9
).

Thus,

\nabla u=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+\nabla(w_{0}+v(x))=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-1+\epsilon}).

With the help of above estimates, we have that

\displaystyle R\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\pi}+o_{R}(1),\ \

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\left(2e^{2u}-e^{u}|\psi|^{2}-|\psi|^{4}x\cdot\nabla F\right)dx=o_{R}(1),

\displaystyle R\int_{\partial B_{R}}e^{2u}+F|\psi|^{4}=o_{R}(1),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left<x\cdot\psi,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right>+\left<\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu},x\cdot\psi\right>=o_{R}(1),

where
\lim_{R\to 0}o_{R}(1)=0
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we get

C(u,\psi)=\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}C(u,\psi;R)=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4\pi},

which implies the third conclusion of proposition.
∎

4.
Bubble’s equation

In this section, we will study the properties of the equation of super-Liouville type bubble which is defined on the whole plane. Specially, we will show that the energy of this type bubble is
4\pi
and the singularity at infinity is removable.

The bubble equation is

(4.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u&=2e^{2u}-e^{u}|\psi|^{2},\\
\not{D}\psi&=-e^{u}\psi-2\mu|\psi|^{2}\psi,\end{cases}in\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2},

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}

with energy condition
E(u,\psi;\mathbb{R}^{2})<\infty.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(u,\psi)
be a smooth solution to (
4.1
) with finite energy

E(u,\psi;\mathbb{R}^{2})<\infty
. Then
u^{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

Since
E(u,\psi;\mathbb{R}^{2})<\infty
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R=R(\epsilon)>0
such that for any
|y|\geq R
, there holds
E(u,\psi;B_{1}(y))\leq\epsilon
. By the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we get that

\max_{B_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)}u\leq C(1+\|u^{+}\|_{L^{1}(B_{1}(y))})\leq C(1+E(u,\psi;\mathbb{R}^{2})),

where
C
is independent of
y
, which implies that
u^{+}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
∎

Next, we will combine the potential analysis with Pohozaev type identity to calculate the bubble’s energy

\alpha:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(2e^{2u}-e^{u}|\psi|^{2}\right)dx.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(u,\psi)
be a solution to (
4.1
) with finite energy
E(u,\psi;\mathbb{R}^{2})<\infty
. Then we have

\displaystyle u(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\log|x|+O(1),\ \ \nabla u(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-1-\epsilon})\ \ near\ \ \infty,

where
\alpha=4\pi
.

Proof.

Let
f:=2e^{2u}-e^{u}|\psi|^{2}
and

v(x):=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\log|x-y|-\log(|y|+1)\right)f(y)dy.

It is clear that
-\Delta v=f

and

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{v}{\log|x|}=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}.

Considering
w=u-v
we have
\Delta w=0
.
Lemma
4.1
tells us that
u
is bounded from above. Then we have

w\leq C+C\log(1+|x|).

By Liouville theorem,
w
is a constant on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then

(4.2)

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{u(x)}{\log|x|}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{v(x)}{\log|x|}=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}.

Considering the Kelvin transform
(u_{1},\psi_{1})
of
(u,\psi)
, i.e.

u_{1}(x):=u(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})-2\ln|x|,\ \ \psi_{1}(x):=|x|^{-1}\psi(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}),

we have

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}&=2e^{2u_{1}}-e^{u_{1}}|\psi_{1}|^{2},\\
\not{D}\psi_{1}&=-e^{u_{1}}\psi_{1}-2\mu|\psi_{1}|^{2}\psi_{1},\end{cases}\ \ x\in B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

By Proposition
3.2
,
we have there exist two constants
\gamma<2\pi
and
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

u_{1}(x)=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\log|x|+h(x),\ \ \nabla u_{1}(x)=-\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-1+\epsilon})\ \ near\ \ 0,

and

(4.3)

|\psi_{1}(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta},\ \ |\nabla\psi_{1}(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{3}{2}+\delta}\ \ near\ \ 0,

where
h(x)
is continues near
0
.
From the fact that

u(x)=u_{1}(x/|x|^{2})-2\log|x|=\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}-2\right)\log|x|+h(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})=\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}-2\right)\log|x|+O(1)\ \ near\ \ \infty,

by (
4.2
),
we get

\alpha+\gamma=4\pi.

Thus, we have

u(x)=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\log|x|+O(1),\ \ near\ \ \infty,

where
\alpha=4\pi-\gamma>2\pi
.

By a direct computation, we have

\displaystyle(\nabla u)(x)=-(\nabla u_{1})(\frac{x}{|x|^{2}})\cdot\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{2}{|x|^{2}}x

\displaystyle=(\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}-2)\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-1-\epsilon})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}+O(|x|^{-1-\epsilon})\ \ near\ \ \infty.

Next, we
claim

\alpha=4\pi
.

From (
4.3
), we have

|\psi(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta},\ \ |\nabla\psi(x)|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}\ \ near\ \ \infty.

Using above estimates, we have

\displaystyle R\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\pi}+o(R),

\displaystyle R\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(e^{2u}+\mu|\psi|^{4}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\leq CR\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(|x|^{-\alpha/\pi}+|x|^{-2-4\delta}\right)d\theta=o(R),

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left\langle x\cdot\psi,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right\rangle d\theta\right|

\displaystyle\leq CR\int_{\partial B_{R}}|\psi||\nabla\psi|d\theta\leq CR\int_{\partial B_{R}}|x|^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}|x|^{-\frac{3}{2}-\delta}d\theta=o(R),

where
\lim_{R\to\infty}o(R)=0
.

By Pohozaev type identity for bubble’s equation that

\displaystyle R\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(\left|\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}\left(2e^{2u}-e^{u}|\psi|^{2}\right)dx-R\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(e^{2u}+\mu|\psi|^{4}\right)d\theta

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B_{R}}\left(\left<x\cdot\psi,\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu}\right>+\left<\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\nu},x\cdot\psi\right>\right)d\theta,

letting
R\to\infty
, we get

\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4\pi}=\alpha,

which implies
\alpha=4\pi
.
∎

By above proposition, we have the following statement for the removability of singularity at infinity.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(u,\psi)
be a smooth solution to (
4.1
) with finite energy
E(u,\psi;\mathbb{R}^{2})<\infty
.
Then
(u,\psi)
can be extend to a smooth solution of (
1.4
) on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, i.e. the singularity at infinity is removable.

Proof.

Considering the stereographic projection

\displaystyle f:(\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{N\},g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{2})

\displaystyle\rightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{2},g_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}=dx^{2}+dy^{2})

\displaystyle(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(\frac{\sin\theta\cos\varphi}{1-\cos\theta},\frac{\sin\theta\sin\varphi}{1-\cos\theta}\right)

It is clear that

f^{*}(g_{\mathbb{R}^{2}})=\left(d\frac{\sin\theta\cos\varphi}{1-\cos\theta}\right)^{2}+\left(d\frac{\sin\theta\sin\varphi}{1-\cos\theta}\right)^{2}=\frac{g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}{(1-\cos\theta)^{2}}:=\lambda^{2}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}.

Define that

v(\theta,\varphi)=u\circ f(\theta,\varphi)+\log\lambda,\ \ \phi(\theta,\varphi)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi\circ f(\theta,\varphi),

which satisfy the following equations

\begin{cases}-\Delta v&=2e^{2v}-e^{v}|\phi|^{2}-1,\\
\not{D}\phi&=-e^{v}\phi-2\mu|\phi|^{2}\phi,\end{cases}\ \ on\ \ \mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{N\}.

For
u=-2\log|x|+O(1)
as
x
near infinity, we have

\displaystyle v(\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=u\left(\frac{\sin\theta\cos\varphi}{1-\cos\theta},\frac{\sin\theta\sin\varphi}{1-\cos\theta}\right)+\log\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}

\displaystyle=-2\log\frac{|\sin\theta|}{1-\cos\theta}+\log\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{1}{1+\cos\theta}+O(1)

\displaystyle=O(1),\ \ as\ \ \theta\rightarrow 0.

Thus
v
is bounded near
\{N\}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
From the standard elliptic establish we can claim that
(v,\phi)
is regular on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
∎

5.
Energy identity for the spinor

In this section we will prove energy identity for the spinor, i.e. Theorem
1.2
, which is important in later proof, such as local masses and energy identity for the function.

We first show the following elliptic estimate for spinor’s equation.

Lemma 5.1
.

let
(u,\psi)
be a smooth solution of (
1.3
) on the annulus
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:r_{1}\leq|x|\leq r_{2}\}
,where
0<r_{1}<2r_{1}<r_{2}/2<r_{2}<1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{A_{2r_{1},\frac{r_{2}}{2}}}|\nabla\psi|^{\frac{4}{3}}dx\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}+\left(\int_{A_{2r_{1},\frac{r_{2}}{2}}}|\psi|^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\left(\int_{A_{r_{1},r_{2}}}(e^{2u}+|\psi|^{4})dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{A_{r_{1},r_{2}}}|\psi|^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}+\left(C\int_{A_{r_{1},2r_{1}}}|\psi|^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}+C\left(\int_{A_{\frac{r_{2}}{2},r_{2}}}|\psi|^{4}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

where
\Lambda>0,\ C>0
are two universal constants.

Proof.

The proof is standard by using elliptic estimates of Dirac operator. See Lemma 3.1 in
[
10
]
.
∎

Now, we prove energy identity for the spinor, i.e. Theorem
1.2
. Since
\Sigma
is finite, we choose
\{\delta_{i}\}
small such that

x_{i}\in D_{\delta_{i}}(x_{i})
and
D_{\delta_{i}}(x_{i})\cap D_{\delta_{j}}(x_{j})=\emptyset
as
i\neq j
. By Lemma
2.1
, we know that
\psi_{n}
converges to
\psi
in
L_{loc}^{\infty}(M\setminus\Sigma)
.
It is clear that the energy identity is equivalent to

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{D_{\delta}(x_{i})}|\psi_{n}|^{4}dx=\sum_{k=1}^{L_{i}}\int_{S^{2}}|\xi^{i,k}|^{4}dx

where
\xi^{i,k}
are bubbles.

We start the proof by constructing the first bubble at a blow-up point
p\in\Sigma
. Suppose
p
is the only blow-up point in
B_{\delta}(p)
. We have to consider following two cases according to different singularities.

Case (i):

p\in\Sigma
is a first type singular point.

In this case, we define

\displaystyle u_{n}(x_{n}):=\max_{x\in B_{\delta}(p)}\{u_{n}(x)\},\ \ \lambda_{n}:=e^{-u_{n}(x_{n})}.

it is clear that
x_{n}\rightarrow p
and
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Setting

\tilde{u}_{n}(x)=u_{n}(\lambda_{n}x+x_{n})+\log\lambda_{n},\ \ \tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\psi_{n}(\lambda_{n}x+x_{n}),

then

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{u}_{n}(x)&=2e^{2\tilde{u}_{n}(x)}-e^{\tilde{u}_{n}(x)}|\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)|^{2},\\
\not{D}\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)&=-e^{\tilde{u}_{n}(x)}\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)-2F(\lambda_{n}x+x_{n})|\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)|^{2}\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x),\end{cases}\ \ x\in B_{\frac{\delta}{2\lambda_{n}}}(0),

with finite energy condition

\int_{B_{\frac{\delta}{2\lambda_{n}}}(0)}(e^{2\tilde{u}_{n}(x)}+|\tilde{\psi}_{n}|^{4})dx<C.

From the definition of the first type singularity, we know that passing to a subsequence, there exists a universal constant
C>0
such that

u_{n}(x_{n})-2\ln(1+\max_{B_{r_{0}}(p)}|\psi_{n}(x)|)\geq-C,\ \ \forall\ n.

Then it is easy to see that for any
R>0
, there holds

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{n}(0)=0,\ \tilde{u}_{n}(x)\leq 0,\ \ |\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)|^{2}\leq e^{-u_{n}(x_{n})+2\ln\max_{B_{r_{0}}(p)}|\psi_{n}(x)|}\leq C,\ \ x\in B_{R}(0).

By standard elliptic estimate,

(\tilde{u}_{n},\tilde{\psi}_{n})
sub-converges to
(\tilde{u},\tilde{\psi})
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
where

\begin{cases}-\Delta\tilde{u}(x)&=2e^{2\tilde{u}(x)}-e^{\tilde{u}(x)}|\tilde{\psi}(x)|^{2},\\
\not{D}\tilde{\psi}(x)&=-e^{\tilde{u}(x)}\tilde{\psi}(x)-2\mu|\tilde{\psi}(x)|^{2}\tilde{\psi}(x),\end{cases}\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

From the Corollary
4.3
, we get the first bubble at blow up point
p
which is a super-Liouville type bubble.

Case (ii):

p\in\Sigma
is a second type singular point.

In this case, we define

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(x_{n})|:=\max_{x\in B_{\delta}(p)}\{|\psi_{n}(x)|\},\ \ \lambda_{n}:=|\psi_{n}(x_{n})|^{-2}.

it is clear that
x_{n}\rightarrow p
and
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Set
(\tilde{u}_{n},\tilde{\psi}_{n})
as in Case
(i)
. Since
p\in\Sigma
is of second type singularity, passing to a subsequence, we have

\max_{B_{r_{0}}(p)}u_{n}(x)-2\ln(1+|\psi_{n}(x_{n})|)\to-\infty,\ \ as\ \ n\to\infty.

It is easy to see that

|\tilde{\psi}_{n}(0)|=1,\ \ |\tilde{\psi}_{n}(x)|\leq 1,\ \ x\in B_{R}(0)

and

\max_{x\in B_{R}(0)}\tilde{u}_{n}(x)\leq\max_{B_{r_{0}}(p)}u_{n}(x)-2\ln|\psi_{n}(x_{n})|\to-\infty,\ \ as\ \ n\to\infty.

By standard elliptic estimate, passing to a subsequence, we know that
\tilde{u}_{n}\to-\infty
uniformly in any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\tilde{\psi}_{n}
converges to
\tilde{\psi}
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
where

(5.2)

\not{D}\tilde{\psi}(x)=-2\mu|\tilde{\psi}(x)|^{2}\tilde{\psi}(x),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

By the conformal invariance of (
5.2
) and removable singularity theorem (see Theorem 5.1 in
[
8
]
), we get the first bubble at blow-up point
p
which is a spinorial Yamabe type bubble.

Similar to the argument for the energy identity of harmonic maps, e.g.
[
5
]
, we may assume that there is only one bubble at each blow up point
p\in\Sigma
. Then the energy identity is equivalent to prove

(5.3)

\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{n})\setminus B_{\lambda_{n}R}(x_{n})}|\psi_{n}|^{4}dx=0.

Under one bubble’s assumption, we firstly have the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exist
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\epsilon)
,
R_{0}=R_{0}(\epsilon)
such that for any
\delta\in(0,\delta_{0})
,
R>R_{0}
, there exists
N=N(\epsilon,\delta,R)>0
such that when
n\geq N
, there holds

\sup_{t\in[\lambda_{n}R,\delta]}\int_{B_{2t}(x_{n})\setminus B_{t}(x_{n})}(e^{2u_{n}}+|\psi_{n}|^{4})dx<\epsilon,

i.e.

(5.4)

\lim_{R\rightarrow 0}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{t\in[\lambda_{n}R,\delta]}\int_{B_{2t}(x_{n})\setminus B_{t}(x_{n})}(e^{2u_{n}}+|\psi_{n}|^{4})dx=0.

Proof.

Similar to harmonic maps
[
5
]
or super-Liouville equation
[
10
]
, we first have following two facts.

Fact 1:
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(\epsilon)
such that for any
R>R_{0}
and
T>100
, there exists
N(\epsilon,R)
such that for any
n\geq N
, there holds

\int_{B_{\lambda_{n}RT}(x_{n})\setminus B_{\lambda_{n}R}(x_{n})}(e^{2u_{n}}+|\psi_{n}|^{4})dx<\epsilon.

Fact 2:
For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\epsilon)
such that for any
\delta<\delta_{0}
and
T>100
, there exists
N(\epsilon,\delta)
such that for any
n\geq N
, there holds

\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{n})\setminus B_{\delta T^{-1}}(x_{n})}(e^{2u_{n}}+|\psi_{n}|^{4})dx<\epsilon.

Now we prove the lemma by a contradiction argument. If not, then there exist

,
\delta>0,\ R>100
and sequence

By above two facts, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{t_{n}}{\lambda_{n}R}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\delta}{t_{n}}=+\infty.

Denoting

then for all
n
, we have

\int_{B_{2}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}(e^{2v_{n}}+|\phi_{n}|^{4})dx\geq\epsilon_{0}.

Moreover, for
\forall\ T>100
, when

is big,
(v_{n},\phi_{n})
satisfies

\begin{cases}-\Delta v_{n}=2e^{2v_{n}}-e^{v_{n}}|\phi_{n}|^{2},\\
\not{D}\phi_{n}=-(e^{v_{n}}+2F(x_{n}+t_{n}x)|\phi_{n}|^{2})\phi_{n},\end{cases}\ \ x\in\ \ B_{T}(0)\setminus B_{T^{-1}}(0).

There are three possible cases.

Case 1:
There exists
T>0
such that

has a energy concentration point

, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{r}(q)}(e^{2v_{n}}+|\phi_{n}|^{4})dx\geq\epsilon_{0}>0,\ \ \forall\ r>0.

In this case, similar to construct the first bubble, we will get the second bubble, this is a contradiction.

If Case 1 is false, then for any
T>0
,
(v_{n},\phi_{n})
has no energy concentration point in
B_{T}(0)\setminus B_{T^{-1}}(0)
. By small energy regularity Lemma
2.1
, we have to consider following two cases.

Case 2:

v_{n}\rightarrow-\infty
uniformly in any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.

In this case, we have
\phi_{n}\to\phi
in

where

\not{D}\phi=2\mu|\phi|^{2}\phi,\ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

By (
5.5
), we get

\int_{B_{2}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}|\phi|^{4}dx\geq\epsilon_{0},\ \ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\phi|^{4}dx\leq C.

By conformal invariance and removable singularity (Theorem 5.1 in
[

]
), we can see
\phi
as a spinor from

, i.e.

\not{D}\phi=2\mu|\phi|^{2}\phi,\ \ \ x\in S^{2}.

This is a contradiction since we get the second bubble.

Case 3:

(v_{n},\phi_{n})\to(v,\phi)
in
C^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
and

satisfies

\not{D}\phi&=-(e^{v}+2\mu|\phi|^{2})\phi,\end{cases}\ \ in\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},

and

There are two singularities:
+\infty
and

4.3
.

Firstly, by Proposition
3.2
, we know that

for some small constant
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
, where
\gamma<2\pi
is a constant.

For any
s>0

\displaystyle\gamma=-\lim_{s\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\partial B_{s}(0)}\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial r}=\lim_{s\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{s}(0)}(-\Delta v_{n})dx=\lim_{s\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{s}(0)}\left(2e^{2v_{n}}-e^{v_{n}}|\phi_{n}|^{2}\right)dx.

Secondly, since

\displaystyle=s\int_{\partial B_{s}(0)}\left(\left|\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial|x|}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\right)-\int_{B_{s}(0)}\left(2e^{2v_{n}}-e^{v_{n}}|\phi_{n}|^{2}-|\phi_{n}|^{4}x\cdot\nabla F(x_{n}+t_{n}x)\right)dx

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to 0}s\int_{\partial B_{s}(0)}\left(\left|\frac{\partial v}{\partial|x|}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)-\lim_{s\to 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{B_{s}(0)}\left(2e^{2v_{n}}-e^{v_{n}}|\phi_{n}|^{2}\right)dx

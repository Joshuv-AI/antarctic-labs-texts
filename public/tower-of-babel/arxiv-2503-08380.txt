t t -adic symmetric multiple zeta values for indices with alternating 1 1 and 3 3 , starting with 1 1 and ending with 3 3
Kento Fujita

Abstract
. Hirose, Murahara, and Saito proved that some t t -adic symmetric multiple zeta values, for indices in which 1 1 and 3 3 appear alternately in succession, can be expressed as polynomials in Riemann zeta values, and conjectured similar formulas. In this paper, we prove the conjectured formula for indices that start with 1 1 and end with 3 3 , showing that they also can be expressed as polynomials in Riemann zeta values.

11M32

1.
Introduction

An index is a sequence of positive integers, including the empty sequence
\emptyset
.
We call
k
an admissible index if its last component is greater than
1
or if
\text{\bf{k}}=\emptyset
.
For an admissible index
\text{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, the multiple zeta value (MZV) is defined by

(1.1)

\zeta(\text{\bf{k}})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{R},

where
\zeta(\emptyset)
is defined to be
1
.
Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra generated by all MZVs.
For an index
k
, whether admissible or not,
\zeta^{*}(\text{\bf{k}})
is defined as the constant term of
the
*
-regularized MZV (see
[
8
]
for details), which is a real number in
\mathcal{Z}
.
Note that
\zeta^{*}(1)=0
and that
\zeta^{*}(\text{\bf{k}})=\zeta(\text{\bf{k}})
if
k
is admissible.

For a nonnegative integer
m
and an index
\text{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, set

(1.2)

\zeta^{*}_{m}(\text{\bf{k}})=\zeta^{*}_{m}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1},\ldots,l_{r}\geq 0\\
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{r}=m\end{subarray}}\zeta^{*}(k_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,k_{r}+l_{r})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{k_{i}+l_{i}-1}{l_{i}}\in\mathbb{R}.

For the empty index,
\zeta^{*}_{m}(\emptyset)
is understood as
\delta_{m,0}
,
where
\delta_{m,0}
denotes the Kronecker delta.
The
t
-adic symmetric multiple zeta value (
t
-adic SMZV) is defined
as an element in
\mathcal{Z}\llbracket t\rrbracket
by

(1.3)

\zeta^{*}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\text{\bf{k}})=\zeta^{*}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{r}+\cdots+k_{i+1}}\zeta^{*}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i})\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{m}^{*}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{i+1})t^{m}\in\mathcal{Z}\llbracket t\rrbracket.

Here,
\zeta^{*}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\emptyset)
is understood as
1
.
The
t
-adic SMZVs have been studied in
[
9
,
14
,
13
,
5
]
as a generalization of
the ordinary SMZVs (the
t
-adic SMZVs for
t=0
or the
t
-adic SMZVs modulo
t
; see
[
10
]
).
For every positive integer
m
, let
\pi_{m}:\mathcal{Z}\llbracket t\rrbracket\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}\llbracket t\rrbracket/t^{m}
be the natural projection.
Then,
\mathcal{S}_{m}
-symmetric multiple zeta values (
\mathcal{S}_{m}
-SMZVs) are defined as follows:

(1.4)

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}(\text{\bf{k}}):=\pi_{m}(\zeta^{*}_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}}(\text{\bf{k}}))\in\mathcal{Z}\llbracket t\rrbracket/t^{m}.

Hereafter, we may consider operations on
\mathcal{S}_{m}
-SMZVs modulo
\pi^{2}
.
Such elements should be understood as belonging to

(\mathcal{Z}/\pi^{2}\mathcal{Z})\llbracket t\rrbracket/t^{m}
.
Furthermore, throughout this paper, the empty sum is understood as zero.

It is known that MZVs can be expressed as polynomials in terms of Riemann zeta values (see
[
3
,
1
,
2
,
4
]
).
Based on this, Ono, Sakurada, and Seki investigated a similar structure for SMZVs and, in
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
, determined the values of
\mathcal{S}_{2}
-SMZVs modulo
\pi^{2}
for a broader range of indices.
Further extending this line of research,
[
7
]
examines the case where the
t
-adic SMZV for certain special indices, namely

(1.5)

\text{\bf{k}}=(\{1,3\}^{n}),(\{3,1\}^{n}),(\{3,1\}^{n},3),(\{1,3\}^{n},1),

can be expressed as polynomials in Riemann zeta values. Here,
n
is a nonnegative integer, and
\{a,b\}^{n}
denotes the sequence obtained by repeating
a
and
b
alternately
n
times,
so that

(\{a,b\}^{2})=(a,b,a,b)
.
As an example, the following result is established in
[
7
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
7
, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4]
)
.

For a nonnegative integer
n
, we have

(1.6)

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(\{3,1\}^{n})=\frac{2(-4)^{n}}{(4n+2)!}\pi^{4n}+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},n_{1}\geq 0\\
n_{0}+n_{1}=2n\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{n_{0}}2^{n_{0}-n_{1}+2}}{(2n_{0}+2)!}\pi^{2n_{0}}\zeta^{*}(2n_{1}+1)t\\
+(-1)^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\\
n_{0}+n_{1}+n_{2}=2n\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{n_{0}}2^{n_{0}-n_{1}-n_{2}+2}}{(2n_{0}+2)!}\pi^{2n_{0}}\zeta^{*}(2n_{1}+1)\zeta^{*}(2n_{2}+1)t^{2}

and by modulo
\pi^{2}
reduction, we have

(1.7)

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(\{3,1\}^{n})\\
\equiv\delta_{n,0}-2(-4)^{-n}\zeta^{*}(4n+1)t+2(-4)^{-n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+n_{2}=2n\end{subarray}}\zeta^{*}(2n_{1}+1)\zeta^{*}(2n_{2}+1)t^{2}\pmod{\pi^{2}}.

In
[
7
]
, the authors suggested that
it is reasonable to think that

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}(\{3,1\}^{n})

cannot be written as polynomials in Riemann zeta values when
m\geq 4
.
This is because, even if we reduce modulo
\pi^{2}
, the coefficient of
t^{3}
in

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{4}}(3,1,3,1)

is equal to

\frac{605}{4}\zeta(11)+\frac{19}{4}\zeta(3)^{2}\zeta(5)+2\zeta(3)\zeta(3,5)-2\zeta(3,3,5)
.
Thus, we also do not examine

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}(\{3,1\}^{n})

for
m\geq 4
in this paper.

Moreover,
[
7
]
examines the case of the index
(\{1,3\}^{n})
.
It is known that
\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}(\{1,3\}^{n})
can be expressed as polynomials in Riemann zeta values when
m\leq 2
:
for example, the following result is established in
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
7
, Theorem 1.1]

For a nonnegative integer
n
, we have

(1.8)

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{2}}(\{1,3\}^{n})=\frac{2(-4)^{n}}{(4n+2)!}\pi^{4n}+\biggl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},n_{1}\geq 0\\
n_{0}+n_{1}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{(-4)^{n_{0}+1}(2-(-4)^{-n_{1}})}{(4n_{0}+2)!}\pi^{4n_{0}}\zeta^{*}(4n_{1}+1)\\
-(-1)^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{0},n_{1}\geq 0\\
n_{0}+n_{1}=2n\\
n_{0},n_{1}\text{:odd}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{n_{0}-n_{1}+2}}{(2n_{0}+2)!}\pi^{2n_{0}}\zeta(2n_{1}+1)\biggr)t.

According to
[
7
]
, since
the coefficient of
t^{2}
in
\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(1,3,1,3)

is given by

\frac{1}{2}\zeta(2)\zeta(3)\zeta(5)+\zeta(2)\zeta(3,5)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta(3)^{2}\zeta(4)-\frac{1}{4}\zeta(3)\zeta(7)+\frac{81}{8}\zeta(5)^{2}-\frac{103}{10}\zeta(10)
,
it is reasonable to believe that

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(1,3,1,3)

cannot be written as polynomials in Riemann zeta values.
Nonetheless,
it is conjectured that when reduced modulo
\pi^{2}
,

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(\{1,3\}^{n})
can always
be written as a polynomial in Riemann zeta values.
This conjecture has not yet been proven.
Therefore, the goal of this paper is to give a detailed proof of the conjectured polynomial expression modulo
\pi^{2}
for

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(\{1,3\}^{n})

in terms of Riemann zeta values, as stated in
[
7
, Conjecture 1.2]
.

Main Theorem

(
[
7
, Conjecture 1.2]
)
.

For a nonnegative integer
n
, we have

(1.9)

\begin{split}\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}(\{1,3\}^{n})&\equiv\delta_{n,0}+2((-4)^{-n}-4)\zeta^{*}(4n+1)t\\
&\qquad+\biggl(-2(-4)^{-n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+n_{2}=n-1\end{subarray}}\zeta(4n_{1}+3)\zeta(4n_{2}+3)\\
&\qquad\qquad+2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\\
n_{1}+n_{2}=n\end{subarray}}((-4)^{-n_{1}}-2)((-4)^{-n_{2}}-2)\zeta^{*}(4n_{1}+1)\zeta^{*}(4n_{2}+1)\biggr)t^{2}\pmod{\pi^{2}}.\end{split}

We remark that since the coefficient of
t^{3}
in

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{4}}(1,3,1,3)

is equal to

-\frac{845}{4}\zeta(11)-\frac{9}{4}\zeta(3)^{2}\zeta(5)-\zeta(3)\zeta(3,5)+2\zeta(3,3,5)

modulo
\pi^{2}
,
it makes sense to think that

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{4}}(1,3,1,3)

cannot be written as polynomials in Riemann zeta values,
and hence we do not investigate

\zeta^{*}_{\mathcal{S}_{m}}(\{1,3\}^{n})
for
m\geq 4
in this article.

We also believe that our main theorem, along with theorems in
[
7
]
for the
t
-adic SMZVs,
should hold for the
p
-adic finite MZVs as well.
This idea comes from the generalized Kaneko–Zagier conjecture,
which suggests that
t
-adic SMZVs and
p
-adic finite MZVs satisfy relations
of the same form.
(for details, see
[
10
,
13
]
).
Note that these relations for
p
-adic finite MZVs have not been proved yet.

The structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section 2, we review the necessary facts and state them as lemmas.
In Section 3, we prove the main theorem.

2.
Preparation for proof

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space generated by all indices.
For all indices
\text{\bf{k}},\text{\bf{l}}

and for all positive integers
k,l
, a
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear product
*
on
\mathcal{I}
is defined inductively by setting

(2.1)

\displaystyle\emptyset*\text{\bf{k}}

\displaystyle=\text{\bf{k}}*\emptyset=\text{\bf{k}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\text{\bf{k}},k)*(\text{\bf{l}},l)

\displaystyle=((\text{\bf{k}})*(\text{\bf{l}},l),k)+((\text{\bf{k}},k)*(\text{\bf{l}}),l)+(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}},k+l).

Note that

\zeta(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}})=\zeta(\text{\bf{k}})\zeta(\text{\bf{l}})

holds for any admissible indices
\text{\bf{k}},\text{\bf{l}}
.
For an index
\text{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
and nonnegative integers
m
and
n
,
we define a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map
{}_{m}I_{n}:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}
by

(2.3)

{}_{m}I_{n}(\text{\bf{k}})={}_{m}I_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{r}+\cdots+k_{i+1}}\sigma_{m}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i})*\sigma_{n}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{i+1}),

where

\sigma_{n}:\mathcal{I}\rightarrow\mathcal{I}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map defined by

(2.4)

\sigma_{n}(\text{\bf{k}})=\sigma_{n}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1},\ldots,l_{r}\geq 0\\
l_{1}+\cdots+l_{r}=n\end{subarray}}(k_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,k_{r}+l_{r})\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{k_{i}+l_{i}-1}{l_{i}}.

For the empty index, we define
\sigma_{n}(\emptyset):=\delta_{n,0}
.
Hereafter, we often abbreviate
{}_{0}I_{n}
as
I_{n}
.
Note that
\zeta^{*}(\sigma_{n}(\text{\bf{k}}))=\zeta^{*}_{n}(\text{\bf{k}})
holds
for every nonnegative integer and any index
k
.
Using this notation and the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{m}
-SMZVs,
we see that
\zeta_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}^{*}(\text{\bf{k}})
can be written as

(2.5)

\zeta_{\mathcal{S}_{3}}^{*}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})=\zeta^{*}(I_{0}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}))+\zeta^{*}(I_{1}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}))t+\zeta^{*}(I_{2}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}))t^{2}.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
7
, Lemma 2.23]
)
.

Let
n
be a nonnegative integer.
For indices
k
and
l
, we have

(2.6)

\sigma_{n}(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}}).

Proof.

By induction on the sum of the lengths of the indices.
If either
k
or
l
is empty, the statement is obviously true.
Assume that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\text{\bf{k}}*(\text{\bf{l}},l))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}},l),

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}((\text{\bf{k}},k)*\text{\bf{l}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\text{\bf{k}},k)*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}}),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}}),

all hold for indices
\text{\bf{k}},\text{\bf{l}}
and nonnegative integers
k,l
.
By the definition of
\sigma_{n}
, we see that

(2.10)

\begin{split}\sigma_{n}(\text{\bf{k}},k)&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},a\geq 0\\
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}+a=n\end{subarray}}(k_{1}+a_{1},\cdots,k_{r}+a_{r},k+a)\binom{k+a-1}{a}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{k_{i}+a_{i}-1}{a_{i}}\\
&=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\bigl(\sigma_{n-a}(\text{\bf{k}}),k+a\bigr).\end{split}

This representation will often be used in later calculations.
Let us calculate the case of
n
.
We have

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\text{\bf{k}},k)*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}},l)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\biggl(\sum_{a=0}^{i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}}),k+a\bigr)\biggr)*\biggl(\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\bigl(\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),l+b\bigr)\biggr)\\
&=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*(\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),l+b),k+a\bigr)\\
&\qquad+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\bigl((\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}}),k+a)*\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),l+b\bigr)\\
&\qquad+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),k+a+l+b\bigr).\end{split}

The first term is given by

(2.12)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\biggl(\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*(\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),l+b),k+a\bigr)\biggr)\\
\begin{aligned} &=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}},l),k+a\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\sum_{i=a}^{n}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}},l),k+a\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{a=0}^{n}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\bigl(\sigma_{n-a}(\text{\bf{k}}*(\text{\bf{l}},l)),k+a\bigr)\\
&=\sigma_{n}\bigl(\text{\bf{k}}*(\text{\bf{l}},l),k\bigr),\end{aligned}

where we use (
2.10
) in the first and last equality,
and the induction hypothesis (
2.7
) in the third equality.
Similarly, the second term is given by

(2.13)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\bigl((\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}}),k+a)*\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),l+b\bigr)=\sigma_{n}\bigl((\text{\bf{k}},k)*\text{\bf{l}},l\bigr)

by (
2.10
) and the induction hypothesis (
2.8
).
Setting
c=a+b
, we obtain the third term as follows:

(2.14)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{i}\sum_{b=0}^{n-i}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+b-1}{b}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-i-b}(\text{\bf{l}}),k+a+l+b\bigr)\\
\begin{aligned} &=\sum_{c=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{c}\sum_{i=a}^{n-c+a}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+c-a-1}{c-a}\bigl(\sigma_{i-a}(\text{\bf{k}})*\sigma_{n-c+a-i}(\text{\bf{l}}),k+l+c\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{c=0}^{n}\sum_{a=0}^{c}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+c-a-1}{c-a}\bigl(\sigma_{n-c}(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}}),k+l+c\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{c=0}^{n}\binom{k+l+c-1}{c}\bigl(\sigma_{n-c}(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}}),k+l+c\bigr)\\
&=\sigma_{n}\bigl(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}},k+l\bigr),\end{aligned}

where we use the induction hypothesis (
2.9
) in the second equality,
(
2.10
) in the last equality,
and the following binomial identity in the third equality:

(2.15)

\sum_{a=0}^{c}\binom{k+a-1}{a}\binom{l+c-a-1}{c-a}=\binom{k+l+c-1}{c}.

This identity follows from the Cauchy product of the generating functions of binomial coefficients:

(2.16)

\frac{1}{(1-x)^{n+1}}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\binom{n+m}{m}\,x^{m}\in\mathbb{Z}\llbracket x\rrbracket\qquad(n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}).

Hence, we finally obtain

(2.17)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\text{\bf{k}},k)*\sigma_{n-i}(\text{\bf{l}},l)&=\sigma_{n}\bigl(\text{\bf{k}}*(\text{\bf{l}},l),k\bigr)+\sigma_{n}\bigl((\text{\bf{k}},k)*\text{\bf{l}},l\bigr)+\sigma_{n}\bigl(\text{\bf{k}}*\text{\bf{l}},k+l\bigr)\\
&=\sigma_{n}\bigl((\text{\bf{k}},k)*(\text{\bf{l}},l)\bigr),\end{split}

which proves the case of
n
, giving the desired result.
∎

We remark that the proof for the case of
n=1
is given in

Lemma 2.2
.

For a nonnegative integer
n
and an index
\text{\bf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, we have

(2.18)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}{}_{i}I_{n-i}(\text{\bf{k}})=\sigma_{n}(I_{0}(\text{\bf{k}})).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have

(2.19)

\begin{split}\sum_{i=0}^{n}{}_{i}I_{n-i}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})&=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\biggl(\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{r}+\cdots+k_{j+1}}\sigma_{i}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{j})*\sigma_{n-i}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{j+1})\biggr)\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{r}+\cdots+k_{j+1}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\biggl(\sigma_{i}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{j})*\sigma_{n-i}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{j+1})\biggr)\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{r}+\cdots+k_{j+1}}\sigma_{n}\bigl((k_{1},\ldots,k_{j})*(k_{r},\ldots,k_{j+1})\bigr)\\
&=\sigma_{n}(I_{0}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})),\end{split}

which implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For an index
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
with
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
even, we have

(2.20)

I_{2}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})+{}_{1}I_{1}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})+I_{2}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{1})=\sigma_{2}(I_{0}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})).

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.2
and

(2.21)

\begin{split}{}_{2}I_{0}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})&=(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{i+1})*\sigma_{2}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{i})\\
&={}_{0}I_{2}(k_{r},\ldots,k_{1}).\end{split}

∎

Let
\widetilde{\sh}
denote the shuffle of indices, e.g.,
(a,b)\ \widetilde{\sh}\ (c)=(c,a,b)+(a,c,b)+(a,b,c)
.

Lemma 2.4

(cf.
[
7
, Lemma 2.16]
)
.

Let
n
be a nonnegative integer.
If
a
and
b
are positive integers, then we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle(b)\widetilde{\sh}(\{a\}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}(ai+b)*(\{a\}^{n-i}),

(2.23)

\displaystyle(b,b)\widetilde{\sh}(\{a\}^{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
0\leq i+j\leq n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i+j}(ai+b,aj+b)*(\{a\}^{n-i-j}).

Proof.

The proof of (
2.22
) can be found in
[
7
, Lemma 2.16]
, so we omit it here.
We now proceed to prove (
2.23
).
The right-hand side of (
2.23
) can be written as

(2.24)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
0\leq i+j\leq n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{i+j}(ai+b,aj+b)*(\{a\}^{n-i-j})\\
\begin{aligned} &=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\

Investigation of optical, structural, and radiation shielding properties of novel polyvinyl alcohol and cesium dichromate Cs2Cr2O7 nanocomposite films
Authors: T. S. Soliman, Hesham M. H. Zakaly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the current study, polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) films were doped with different concentrations of Caesium dichromate (Cs2Cr2O7) nanoparticles (0, 2, 4, 6, and 8%). The PVA-Cs2Cr2O7 films were characterized by X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR), and ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) spectrophotometry. XRD analysis showed that increasing Cs2Cr2O7 significantly changed the crystalline structure of the PVA films. FT-IR spectroscopy confirmed the interaction between PVA and Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles, which showed the appearance of Cr–O–Cr and Cr=O starching bonds at higher concentrations of Cs2Cr2O7 in the PVA matrix. UV–Vis analysis revealed a remarkable increase in optical absorbance and redshift in the edge of the absorption of the PVA matrix to the visible region with increasing Cs2Cr2O7 concentration. The optical indirect and direct bandgap energies were decreased to about 2.03 eV and 2.69 eV for the PVA film doped with 8% Cs2Cr2O7, respectively. Radiation shield properties were also studied using Phy-X/PSD software. The mass attenuation coefficient (Gmac) was calculated in the energy range of 0.015–15 MeV for gamma rays. The results showed that PCCO4 exhibited the highest mass attenuation coefficient of 0.295 cm2/g at 0.1 MeV, about 42% higher than that of PCCO0. The half value (Ghvl) decreased from 2.6 cm for PCCO0 to 1.5 cm for PCCO4, indicating increased radiation shielding effectiveness and higher Cs2Cr2O7 concentration. Incorporating Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles into PVA films significantly improves their optical and radiation shielding properties, making them promising materials for various applications, including protective coatings and photoelectronic devices.

Introduction

The utilization of polymer nanocomposite materials in scientific studies has experienced a recent surge, with significant advancements in physical properties and energy storage capabilities, making them essential for daily use1–4. Polymeric materials are currently the most compelling subject of academic and industrial research due to their extensive use in cutting-edge organic electronic devices5–7. These materials are well-suited for various uses, including fuel cells, UV filters, batteries, and optoelectronic instruments. Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) is a synthetic polymer widely recognized for its remarkable film-forming properties, good chemical stability, and environmental friendliness8–12. Due to its hydrophilic nature, PVA is a waste in water, packaging, textiles, and biomedical fields13,14. It is widely used in various applications, and the unique structural properties of PVA, such as its high mechanical properties and flexibility, make it a good candidate for developing composite materials with advanced functionalities15–19. In recent years, interest has increased in developing polymeric nanocomposites, especially incorporating inorganic nanoparticles. These nanocomposites have attracted considerable attention due to their ability to exhibit superior properties to conventional materials, such as high mechanical strength, thermal stability, and electrical conductivity. Many articles have been published on polymer matrices reinforced with various metal oxides such as Nd2O35, CuO20,21, Fe3O414, Fe2O322, ZnO23,24, TiO225, Mn2O326, MnO227, PbO228, Co3O42, SiO229, Nanoferrites30,31.

Cesium dichromate (Cs2Cr2O7) is a crystalline compound known for its high and stable oxidation potential, making it a valuable dopant in various chemical and materials science applications. However, its use as a dopant in polymeric matrices, in particular, is unexplored PVA has never been reported. The incorporation of Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles into PVA films provides an exciting opportunity to explore new materials and applications of these materials, especially in radiation protection and optical technology.

This study investigates the effect of PVA doping and Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles on the resulting nanocomposite films’ structure, radiation shielding, and optical properties. By synthesizing PVA-Cs2Cr2O7 (PCCO) films with varying concentrations of Cs2Cr2O7, we can systematically analyze how the addition of these nanoparticles influences the film’s characteristics. Understanding the interaction between PVA and Cs2Cr2O7 at the molecular level can pave the way for developing improved materials with tailored properties for specific applications. In this study, Various characterization methods, such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR), and ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) spectrophotometry, were used for detailed structure and optical properties analysis. Also, theoretical calculations using Phy-X/PSD software are carried out to determine the radiation shielding capacity of PVA-Cs2Cr2O7 film to explore as much as possible applied to protective coatings and other effects of the critical radiation and seek to expand the understanding of polymer nanocomposites. We aim to contribute to the growing knowledge of polymer nanocomposites and their applications in various fields by investigating these materials’ synthesis, properties, and potential applications.

Experimental

Preparation of PVA-Cs2Cr2O7 (PCCO) nanocomposite films

Five grams of polyvinyl alcohol (PVA, molecular weight (Mη) = 1 × 106) crystals were dissolved in 100 ml of DW for 2 h at 70 °C. Then the mixture was then agitated with a magnetic stirrer at room temperature for 4 h. 5 nanocomposite solutions (1 g PVA/ 20 ml DW) were prepared by adding Cs2Cr2O7 (purity 99.95%) to the PVA solution using different weights (0, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, and 0.08 g). The ultrasonic probe was utilized to disperse Cs2Cr2O7 throughout the PVA solution. Following sonication, all mixture solutions were put straight into glass plates and allowed to dry in a warm environment. The obtained polymer films were labelled as PCCO0, PCCO1, PCCO2, PCCO3, and PCCO4 for PVA films doped with 0, 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, and 0.08 g Cs2Cr2O7, respectively, as shown in Table 1. Characterization techniques

The diffraction pattern of the Cs2Cr2O7 NPs and PCCO films was obtained using Brucker D8 X-ray diffractometer (XRD, with X-ray wavelength = 1.5418 Å). FT-IR was used to perform the chemical bonds of the PVA-Cs2Cr2O7 films. The optical studies were conducted using a UV–visible spectrophotometer within the wavelength range of 200–1000 nm. The Bruker Alpha FT-IR Spectrometer is a Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) apparatus that is fitted with an attenuated total reflectance (ATR) accessory and zinc selenite crystals.

Determination of theoretical shielding characteristics

This study involved the computation of several photon attenuation parameters using experimental data and subsequent comparison with computational results from the open-source software Phy-X/PSD. The selection of these parameters was based on established methodologies for radiation shielding analysis. The software utilizes key material properties such as weight fractions and density to compute radiation shielding parameters based on theoretical calculations32. The intensity of the incident and transmitted γ-ray photons, denoted as (I0) and (I), respectively, was measured for each sample. The linear attenuation coefficient (μ) was calculated using the Lambert–Beer law:33: I = Ioe-μx, where x is the sample thickness and μ represents the linear attenuation coefficient. Optical properties

The normalized absorption curves of the PCCO films are displayed in Fig. 3. The UV–visible spectrum of the pure PVA film is characterized by a sharp absorption edge in the UV region with a halo peak at around 280 nm related to the π-π* transition band of the C–C backbone of the PVA matrix. Furthermore, the peak observed around 375 nm for the PCCO1 film and shifted to higher wavelengths 386, 392, and 405 nm for PCCO2, PCCO3, and PCCO4 films, respectively, is assigned to the electronic transition of Cr+4 ions, consistent with the literature43,44. Besides, surface plasmon resonance (SPR) appeared in the visible zone at around 600 nm and was related to the transition of Cr+3 ions43,44. After the inclusion of the Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles into the PVA matrix, the absorption edge was sharply shifted to the visible zone, indicating the transformation of the state of the material from the insulating state into the semiconducting one. In other words, the material bandgap energy was reduced after the inclusion of Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles in the PVA matrix. This result shows the possibility of benefit from the present polymer films in UV protection applications.Fig. 3Normalized absorption of PCCO films.

Shielding properties

The mass attenuation coefficient (Gmac) in Fig. 6 provides essential insights into the interaction between radiation and matter in the energy range of 0.015–15 MeV. The Gmac quantifies the probability of radiation coming into contact with atoms inside a substance. When radiation particles, such as γ-ray photons, pass through a material, they have the potential to interact with atoms. This parameter approximates the likelihood of interactions occurring inside a specified substance. The Gmac measures the possibility of radiation establishing interactions with electrons in the material.Fig. 6Mass attenuation coefficient (Gmac) of each sample across the energy range of 0.015–15 MeV.

Mass attenuation coefficient (Gmac) of each sample across the energy range of 0.015–15 MeV.

In the course of their travel through the material, radiation particles and electrons in atomic orbits interact with one another, which leads to the occurrence of phenomena such as the photoelectric effect and Compton scattering. This Gmac provides extremely helpful information regarding the likelihood of radiation undergoing a change in either its energy or its direction as a result of electron interactions. The diagram illustrates that an increase in energy level results in a drop in the values of Gmac for radiations that fall between the range of 0.015–15 MeV radiation. It can be deduced from this that radiations have a lower probability of having a significant interaction with the Gmac of the material when they are presented with these elevated energy levels. As a result of the fact that radiation with a higher energy carries more momentum and has a tendency to flow through the medium with less dispersion or scattering, this phenomenon has come about. As a consequence of this, the likelihood of coming into contact with individual atomic nuclei or electrons reduces, which ultimately leads to lower values for Gmac54–59.

On the basis of the values that were computed for the half-value layer (Ghvl) parameter, it is possible to take into consideration the shielding capacities of the films that were investigated.60 It is possible to identify the thickness of the polymer that is capable of reducing fifty percent of the photon beam that is incident onto it. Figure 7 displays the Ghvl values of the PCCO0, PCCO1, PCCO2, PCCO3, and PCCO4 composite films graphed against energy. At lower energies, when the photoelectric effect is most prominent, it is patently obvious that all of the samples have values that are almost identical to one another. This further suggests that all of the samples that are being investigated need a thickness that is virtually identical in order to provide protection against gamma rays at lower energy. When demonstrated in Fig. 761, the values of Ghvl tend to grow when the energy level increases not only at intermediate and higher energies but also at higher energies. The Ghvl reduces in proportion to the rise in Cs2Cr2O7. At higher energies and intermediate energies, when the effects of Compton scattering and pair creation are dominating sequentially, there is a little but substantial difference between the values of Ghvl for the different samples that have a larger concentration of Cs2Cr2O7. In conclusion, these samples have been shown to have greater radiation shielding characteristics at lower energy, while the samples that have the maximum amount of Cs2Cr2O7 have exhibited superior properties at higher energies as well as intermediate energies. Conclusion

This work employed the solution casting technique to fabricate nanocomposite films consisting of polyvinyl alcohol reinforced with Cs2Cr2O7. X-ray diffraction (XRD) and Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR) investigations verified that the inclusion of Cs2Cr2O7 altered the crystal structure and chemical bonding inside the polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) matrix. The films exhibited a progressive rise in optical absorbance as the concentration of Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles in the PVA film increased. In addition, the redshift in the absorption edge of the PVA film indicates the reduction in the bandgap energy of the composite. Upon doping PVA with 8% Cs2Cr2O7, the direct bandgap of the film decreased to 2.69 eV, signifying the transformation of the composite material from an insulator to a semiconductor. Moreover, increasing the concentration of Cs2Cr2O7 significantly improved the radiation shielding of the films. At 0.1 MeV, the mass attenuation coefficient (Gmac) increased to 0.295 cm2/g for PCCO4, an increase of 42% compared to 0.207 cm2/g for PCCO0. In addition, the half-value (Ghvl) of gamma rays decreased from 2.6 cm in PCCO0 to 1.5 cm in PCCO4, revealing a significant increase in the shielding efficiency of the films. Finally, Adding Cs2Cr2O7 nanoparticles to PVA films reduces the band gap and significantly improves their performance as radiation-shielding materials. These data demonstrate that PVA-Cs2Cr2O7 nanocomposite films hold strong potential for applications in radiation shielding, particularly for protective coatings in nuclear facilities, radiation therapy rooms, and space technology, where shielding from ionizing radiation is critical. Additionally, their reduced bandgap and enhanced optical properties make them promising candidates for optoelectronic applications such as UV filters and photovoltaic devices. Given that solution casting is a scalable and cost-effective technique, manufacturing these nanocomposite films can be feasibly adapted for industrial-scale production, ensuring their practical implementation in commercial and technological sectors.

Holomorphic D-brane embeddings in D-brane backgrounds Preprint: NORDITA 2026-008
James Ratcliffe, Ronnie Rodgers, and Sangsoo Ryu

Abstract
We describe families of probe D q q -brane embeddings in the extremal black D p p -brane backgrounds of type IIA and type IIB supergravity, specified by an arbitrary holomorphic function of a complex coordinate on the worldvolume of the D q q -branes. These embeddings preserve one-quarter of the supersymmetry of the D p p -brane background, or sometimes one-half of the supersymmetry when p = q p=q . We discuss the holography of two example families of holomorphic probe branes in the near-horizon limit of the D3-brane background. The first is probe D5-branes, dual to defect hypermultiplets with a holomorphic mass, which in the infrared flow to Wilson lines located at the zeros of the mass. The second is probe D3-branes, holographically dual to states in the presence of Gukov–Witten surface defects in the dual 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory.

Introduction and summary of results

A family of embeddings of probe D7-branes in the extremal black D3-brane background of type IIB supergravity has recently been introduced
holomorphic_branes
, in which a complex coordinate
y
, formed from the two directions orthogonal to the D7-branes, may be any holomorphic or antiholomorphic function of another complex coordinate
z
formed from two directions parallel to both the D7-branes and the D3-branes sourcing the background. These embeddings are similar to brane embeddings in flat space found in ref.
Gauntlett:1997ss
and, like the flat space embeddings, their energy saturates a Bogomol’nyi–Prasad–Sommerfield (BPS) bound and they preserve a fraction of supersymmetry.

The near-horizon limit of the D3-brane background is
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
, holographically dual to four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
supersymmetric Yang–Mills (SYM) theory
Maldacena:1997re
;
Gubser:1998bc
;
Witten:1998qj
. In this limit, introducing probe D7-branes corresponds to coupling
\mathcal{N}=4

\mathcal{N}=2

. Non-trivial holomorphic

, which therefore depends holomorphically on position

. Holomorphic D7-branes in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
thus provide an analytically tractable holographic description of a strongly coupled quantum field theory (QFT) with explicitly broken translational symmetry.

There is nothing particular about D3- or D7-branes that implies that the embeddings of ref.
holomorphic_branes
should be the only examples of such holomorphic embeddings of D-branes in extremal D-brane backgrounds. In this work we will perform the natural generalisation, considering probe D

-branes embedded in the extremal D

-brane backgrounds of type IIA and type IIB supergravity, for other values

The result that we find is what one might intuitively expect. Starting from an intersection between flat D

directions not spanned by the D

as

P[C_{q+1}]=\frac{i}{2}\delta_{d,0}\left[H(r)^{-1}-1\right]\operatorname{d}\!t\wedge\operatorname{d}\!z\wedge\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\wedge\operatorname{d}\!x_{1}\wedge\operatorname{d}\!x_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge\operatorname{d}\!x_{a-3}\,,

(13)

Substituting equations (

-brane action (

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{kT_{q}}{2}\int\operatorname{d}\!t\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{x}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{v}\,\mathcal{L}_{1}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle=H(r)^{(d-4)/4}\sqrt{\left[1+H(r)\left(|\partial y|^{2}+|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}\right)\right]^{2}-4H(r)^{2}|\partial y|^{2}|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}}-\delta_{d,0}\left[H(r)^{-1}-1\right],

with

. We have added the subscript “

\mathcal{L}_{1}
depends on

In order to write the Euler–Lagrange equations that follow from the action (

\mathcal{A}_{1}
and a differential operator
\mathcal{D}_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=H(r)^{-1}+|\partial y|^{2}+|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}[\bullet]

\displaystyle=\bar{\partial}y\,\bar{\partial}\bar{y}\,\partial^{2}\bullet+\partial y\,\partial\bar{y}\,\bar{\partial}^{2}\bullet-\mathcal{A}_{1}\,\partial\bar{\partial}\bullet\,.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial y\,\bar{\partial}y\,\mathcal{D}_{1}[y]-\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}}{2}\mathcal{D}_{1}[\bar{y}]+\frac{\partial_{r}H}{4rH^{2}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}y-2\,\bar{y}\,\partial y\,\bar{\partial}y\right)\partial\bar{y}\bar{\partial}\bar{y}

(16)

\displaystyle-\frac{d-4}{32}\frac{\partial_{r}H}{rH}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}\bar{y}-2y\,\partial\bar{y}\,\bar{\partial}\bar{y}\right)\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{2}-4|\partial y|^{2}|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\delta_{d,0}\frac{\partial_{r}H}{8rH^{(d+4)/4}}\bar{y}\left(\mathcal{A}_{1}^{2}-4|\partial y|^{2}|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}\right)^{3/2}\,.

Recall that in our ansatz we took the worldvolume scalars

The left-hand side vanishes for any

BPS bound.

Holomorphic embeddings solve the D
q
-brane equations of motion when
d
is a multiple of four because their energy saturates a BPS bound. This argument was made for brane embeddings in flat space in ref.
Gauntlett:1997ss
and adapted to class 1,
d=4
D7-brane embeddings in the D3-brane background in ref.
holomorphic_branes
. We now generalise this argument to arbitrary class 1 D
q
-brane embeddings in D
p
-brane backgrounds.

For arbitrary integer
n
and for
y=y(z,\bar{z})
, let us define the quantity

\mathcal{Y}_{n}=H(r)^{n/4}\left(|\partial y|^{2}-|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}\right)\,,

(19)

in terms of which the Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}_{1}
in equation (
14
) can be written in two equivalent forms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left[H(r)^{(d-4)/4}+\mathcal{Y}_{d}\right]^{2}+4H(r)^{(d-2)/2}|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}}-\delta_{d,0}\left[H(r)^{-1}-1\right]

(20)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left[H(r)^{(d-4)/4}-\mathcal{Y}_{d}\right]^{2}+4H(r)^{(d-2)/2}|\partial y|^{2}}-\delta_{d,0}\left[H(r)^{-1}-1\right]\,.

Since
H(r)
,
|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}
, and
|\partial y|^{2}
are all non-negative, the square roots appearing in these expressions are bounded from below by the factors in the square brackets,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left[H(r)^{(d-4)/4}+\mathcal{Y}_{d}\right]^{2}+4H(r)^{(d-2)/2}|\bar{\partial}y|^{2}}\geq H(r)^{(d-4)/4}+\mathcal{Y}_{d}\,,

(21)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\left[H(r)^{(d-4)/4}-\mathcal{Y}_{d}\right]^{2}+4H(r)^{(d-2)/2}|\partial y|^{2}}\geq H(r)^{(d-4)/4}-\mathcal{Y}_{d}\,.

Thus, the Lagrangian density for class 1 embeddings in equation (
20
) satisfies the bound

\mathcal{L}_{1}\geq\begin{cases}1+|\mathcal{Y}_{d}|\,,&d=0\text{ or }4\,,\\
H(r)^{-1/2}+|\mathcal{Y}_{d}|\,,&d=2\,.\end{cases}

(22)

This bound is saturated when
y
is a holomorphic or antiholomorphic function of
z
. For example, for holomorphic
y
we have that
|\bar{\partial}y|=0
, so that
\mathcal{Y}_{d}
in equation (
19
) is positive and the square root in the first line of equation (
20
) is equal to
H(r)^{(d-4)/4}+\mathcal{Y}_{d}
.

Substituting the bound on the Lagrangian density into the action (
14
), we find that the action is bounded from above. Equivalently, since the D
q
-branes are static and so their energy
E
is minus the Lagrangian, the energy of the D
q
-branes is bounded from below. For
d=0
or
4
these bounds are

S_{1}\leq-\int\operatorname{d}\!t\,(Z+Y_{d})\,,\qquad E\geq Z+Y_{d}\,,\qquad(d=0\text{ or }4)\,,

(23)

where we have defined the integrals

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=\frac{kT_{q}}{2}\int\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{x}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{v}\,,

(24)

\displaystyle Y_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{kT_{q}}{2}\int\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{x}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{v}\,|\mathcal{Y}_{d}|=\frac{\mathrm{deg}(y)\,kT_{q}}{2}\int\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!\bar{y}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{x}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{v}\,H(r)^{d/4}\,.

The second equality in the expression for
Y_{d}
arises because
\mathcal{Y}_{n}
in equation (
19
) is
H(r)^{n/4}
times the Jacobian for a change of integration variables from
(z,\bar{z})
to
(y,\bar{y})
. The factor
\mathrm{deg}(y)
is the degree of the map
y:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, i.e. how many times we must integrate over the complex
y
plane to integrate over the whole of the complex
z
plane.

The integrals for
Z
and
Y_{d}
in equation (
24
) are divergent due to the infinite extent of the D
q
-branes and so require regularisation, for instance by integrating only over a finite extent in each of the coordinates. Provided we maintain consistent regularisation of the integral over the complex
y
plane,
Y_{d}
depends only on the topological data of
y(z,\bar{z})
, in the form of the degree
\mathrm{deg}(y)
.

Stable branes in string theory arise as central charges of the target space supersymmetry algebra
deAzcarraga:1989mza
;
Sato:1998ax
;
Sato:1998yu
;
Callister:2007jy
and, similarly to in refs.
Gauntlett:1997ss
;
holomorphic_branes
, the quantities
Z
and
Y_{d}
appearing in the BPS bound are precisely such central charges. Concretely, using the general expressions for D-brane central charges in non-trivial supergravity backgrounds in ref.
Callister:2007jy
, it is straightforward to show that
Z
is the central charge corresponding to
k
D
q
-branes parallel to the
(t,z,\bar{z},\vec{x},\vec{v})
directions (i.e. a class 1 embedding with constant
y
), while
Y_{d}
is that of
\mathrm{deg}(y)\,k
D
q
-branes parallel to the directions
(t,y,\bar{y},\vec{x},\vec{v})
. More generally, these branes minimise their energy because they wrap calibrated manifolds
Harvey:1982xk
;
Gutowski_1999
;
Gutowski_1999b
. See for instance refs.
gauntlett2003branescalibrationssupergravity
;
Sim_n_2012
;
townsend2000branetheorysolitons
;
Townsend_2000
for reviews on calibrated geometry in supergravity.

Holomorphic or antiholomorphic
y
saturates the bounds in equation (
23
), and therefore extremises the action for fixed regularised central charges
Z
and
Y_{d}
, providing another perspective on why such holomorphic embeddings solve the D
q
-brane equations of motion for
d=0
or

. The reason why (anti)holomorphic

does not solve the equations of motion for

(25)

Although this inequality is saturated for holomorphic or antiholomorphic

depends on the form of

or

3.0
Spinor conventions and Killing spinors of extremal D-brane backgrounds

For our spinor conventions, we follow ref.
Freedman_Van_Proeyen_2012
. We adopt the notation that
\Gamma_{A}
are the ten-dimensional Minkowski space Dirac matrices, satisfying

\{\Gamma_{A},\Gamma_{B}\}=2\eta_{AB}\mathbbm{1}\,,

(48)

\eta_{AB}
the ten-dimensional Minkowski metric in mostly-plus signature. We take
\Gamma_{0}

\Gamma_{i}

. We use
\gamma_{m}
to denote the pullback of the

-branes,

(49)

where

are vielbeins for the ten-dimensional metric in equation (
1
). When
\Gamma
or
\gamma
has multiple indices, this denotes a normalised antisymmetric product, for example

\Gamma_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\Gamma_{A}\Gamma_{B}-\Gamma_{B}\Gamma_{A}\right).

(50)

We denote the ten-dimensional chirality matrix as
\Gamma_{\sharp}=\Gamma_{01\cdots 9}
. It is Hermitian. We denote the charge conjugation matrix as
C
. By definition, it satisfies
\Gamma_{A}^{T}=-C\Gamma_{A}C^{-1}
for all
A
.

In both type IIA and type IIB supergravities, there are two Majorana–Weyl Killing spinors,
\hat{\varepsilon}^{1}
and
\hat{\varepsilon}^{2}

(\hat{\varepsilon}^{i})^{*}=B\hat{\varepsilon}^{i}\,

where

is a matrix obeying

\displaystyle\approx\frac{v^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(-\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}+\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\right)+\frac{L^{2}}{v^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!\vec{v}^{\,2}

(96)

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(-\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}+\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\right)+\frac{L^{2}}{v^{2}}\operatorname{d}\!v^{2}+L^{2}\operatorname{d}\!\Omega_{2}^{2}\,,

where
\operatorname{d}\!\Omega_{2}^{2}
is the metric on a unit, round S
2
, and the second line follows from the first after adopting polar coordinates in the
\vec{v}
hyperplane. The metric in equation (

) is that of
\mathrm{AdS}_{4}\times\mathrm{S}^{2}
, with radial coordinate
v
and the boundary of
\mathrm{AdS}_{4}
at

. This is the worldvolume geometry of probe D5-branes dual to massless three-dimensional hypermultiplets

;
DeWolfe:2001pq
;
Erdmenger_2002

diverges, at extremely high energy scales the hypermultiplets with holomorphic mass are indistinguishable from massless hypermultiplets.

4.3
D3-branes

We now consider class 1 D3-brane embeddings. Aspects of holomorphic D3-brane embeddings in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
have been studied previously. For example, ref.
Constable:2002xt
introduced probe D3-brane embeddings in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
. In our language, these embeddings would correspond to class 1 holomorphic D3-branes for which
y
has the simple pole form
y=c/z
for some complex constant
c
. This choice is particularly physically interesting as it preserves scale invariance in the dual QFT. In ref.
Drukker:2008wr
the superconformal surface defects dual to holomorphic D3-branes with
y=c/z
were identified as disorder operators, also known as Gukov–Witten defects
Gukov:2006jk
. The existence and supersymmetry of embeddings with
y=c/z^{n}
for exponents
n\neq 1
, breaking scale invariance, is also discussed in ref.
Constable:2002xt
.

Relatedly, ref.
Koh:2008kt
considered probe D3-brane embeddings in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
that are specified by a holomorphic function of
two
complex coordinates, again focusing on configurations that preserve scale invariance. We discuss generalisations of D
q
-brane embeddings specified by holomorphic functions of multiple complex coordinates in appendix
A
.

In this section we will describe other aspects of class 1 D3-brane embeddings, with a particular focus on choices of the holomorphic function
y(z)
that break scale invariance, triggering a renormalisation group (RG) flow. We will argue that in the IR of this RG flow one finds Gukov–Witten defects located at the zeros of
y(z)
. We begin in section
4.3.1
with a discussion in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM at weak coupling, showing that holomorphic scalar field configurations solve the classical equations of motion and preserve one-half of the supersymmetry. We expect the holomorphic D3-brane embeddings to provide a large-
N
, strongly coupled description of such configurations. We will also discuss salient features of Gukov–Witten defects. Then in section
4.3.2
we discuss the holomorphic D3-brane embeddings from the gravity side of the AdS/CFT correspondence.

4.3.1
Holomorphic scalars in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM

The fields of four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM are a gauge field
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
, six real scalar fields
\phi^{i}
, and four Weyl fermions
\psi_{a}
, all valued in the adjoint representation of the gauge group’s Lie algebra. A compact way to write the action for the theory is to treat it as a dimensional reduction from ten-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM (see for example refs.
Polchinski:1998rr
;
DHoker:2002nbb
for further details). The bosonic fields are packaged into the ten-dimensional gauge field
\mathbb{A}_{A}=(\mathcal{A}_{\mu},\phi^{i})
, while the fermions are packaged into a single ten-dimensional Majorana–Weyl spinor
\Psi
. The action for
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM is then

S=\int\operatorname{d}\!^{4}x\tr\left(-\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{F}_{AB}\mathbb{F}^{AB}+i\bar{\Psi}\Gamma^{A}\mathbb{D}_{A}\Psi\right),

(97)

where
\mathbb{F}_{AB}=\partial_{A}\mathbb{A}_{B}-\partial_{B}\mathbb{A}_{A}+ig_{\mathrm{YM}}[\mathbb{A}_{A},\mathbb{A}_{B}]
is the ten-dimensional gauge field strength,
\mathbb{D}_{A}\Psi=\partial_{A}\Psi+ig_{\mathrm{YM}}[\mathbb{A}_{A},\Psi]
is the covariant derivative,
\Gamma^{A}
is a ten-dimensional Dirac matrix as in section
3
, and indices are contracted with the ten-dimensional Minkowski metric. The dimensional reduction means that the fields should be taken as depending on only the four coordinates
x^{\mu}
corresponding to the
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}
components of the gauge field.

We will now seek holomorphic solutions to the classical equations of motion of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. We look for solutions with all fields vanishing except for two of the scalar fields,
\phi^{5}
and
\phi^{6}
, which we package into a complex scalar field
\Phi
,

\Phi=\phi^{5}+i\phi^{6}\,.

(98)

The equation of motion for
\Phi
which follows from the action in equation (
97
) is

\Box\Phi+\frac{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{2}}{2}\bigl[\Phi,[\Phi,\Phi^{\dagger}]\bigr]=0\,,

(99)

where
\Box=-\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}
is the d’Alembertian.
Equation (
99
) is manifestly solved by any
\Phi
that solves the wave equation
\Box\Phi=0
and commutes with its Hermitian conjugate,
[\Phi,\Phi^{\dagger}]=0
, or equivalently
[\phi^{5},\phi^{6}]=0
. One way to solve the wave equation is to take all components of
\Phi
to be holomorphic or antiholomorphic functions of a complex coordinate
z
defined by

z=x^{1}+ix^{2}\,.

(100)

Such a
\Phi
was shown to preserve one-half of the supersymmetry of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM in ref.
holomorphic_branes
. Here we will repeat this calculation in a format that makes for easy comparison with the supergravity calculation in section
3.1
.

Under a supersymmetry transformation, the fermions of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM transform as

\delta\Psi=\mathbb{F}^{AB}\Gamma_{AB}\varepsilon\,,

(101)

where
\varepsilon
is a ten-dimensional Majorana–Weyl spinor supersymmetry parameter and
\Gamma_{AB}
denotes a normalised antisymmetric product of Dirac matrices, as in section
3
. Some supersymmetry will be preserved by the field configuration if there exist choices of
\varepsilon
for which
\delta\Psi=0
. Suppose the only non-vanishing components of
\mathbb{A}_{A}
are
\phi^{5,6}
, that these components are mutually commuting
[\phi^{5},\phi^{6}]=0
, and that they depend only on the coordinates
(z,\bar{z})
. The non-zero components of the field strength are then
\mathbb{F}_{\mu(i+3)}=-\mathbb{F}_{(i+3)\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}
for
\mu=1,2
and
i=5,6
. In terms of the complex scalar field
\Phi
defined in equation (
98
), the transformation in equation (
101
) is then

\displaystyle\delta\Psi=-\Bigl[

\displaystyle i(\partial\Phi-\bar{\partial}\Phi^{\dagger})(\Gamma_{19}-\Gamma_{28})-i(\partial\Phi+\bar{\partial}\Phi^{\dagger})(\Gamma_{18}+\Gamma_{29})

(102)

\displaystyle+i(\bar{\partial}\Phi-\partial\Phi^{\dagger})(\Gamma_{19}+\Gamma_{28})-(\bar{\partial}\Phi+\partial\Phi^{\dagger})(\Gamma_{18}-\Gamma_{29})\Bigr]\varepsilon\,.

The factor in square brackets takes the same form as that in the matrix
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
in equation (
70c
), under the interchange
(\Phi,\Phi^{\dagger})\leftrightarrow(y,\bar{y})
. Thus, the analysis of section
3.1
applies here and any
\Phi
which is a holomorphic or antiholomorphic function of
z
, preserves one-half of the Poincaré supersymmetries of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. The preserved supersymmetries correspond to
\varepsilon
satisfying

(\Gamma_{18}\pm\Gamma_{28})\varepsilon=0\,,

(103)

with the plus or minus sign for holomorphic or antiholomorphic
\Phi
, respectively.

For example, consider a holomorphic
\Phi
which is diagonal in its gauge indices (in a particular gauge). We write this in block diagonal form as

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}(z)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\Phi_{2}(z)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&~\Phi_{M}(z)\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{M}}\end{pmatrix},

(104)

for some integer
M
, where
\Phi_{l}(z)\neq\Phi_{k}(z)
for
l\neq k
,
\mathbbm{1}_{N_{l}}
is the
N_{l}
-dimensional identity matrix, and
\sum_{l=1}^{M}N_{l}=N
. Such a field configuration breaks the gauge group from
\mathrm{SU}(N)
to
\mathrm{S}[\prod_{l=1}^{M}\mathrm{U}(N_{l})]
.

When the
\Phi_{l}
are all constant, the field configuration in equation (
104
) corresponds to a point on the Coulomb branch of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. Another well-known choice is to take each
\Phi_{l}(z)=(\beta_{l}+i\gamma_{l})/z
where
\beta_{l}
and
\gamma_{l}
are constant, real parameters, so that the scalar field is holomorphic in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. This gives the scalar field in the presence of a Gukov–Witten surface defect at
z=0

Gukov:2006jk
. We will now briefly review some aspects of these defects. We follow the discussion in refs.
Gomis:2007fi
;
Drukker:2008wr
.

A Gukov–Witten defect is in general defined by singular boundary conditions for the bosonic fields of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. In addition to imposing that
\Phi_{l}=(\beta_{l}+i\gamma_{l})/z
close to
z=0
, one can prescribe singular boundary conditions for the gauge field
\mathcal{A}
at
z=0
that preserve the same
\mathrm{S}[\prod_{l=1}^{M}\mathrm{U}(N_{l})]
subgroup of the gauge group, of the form
8
8

8

As an aside, introducing a gauge field of the form in equation (
105
) does not spoil the fact that
\Phi
in equation (
104
) solves the classical equations of motion and preserves some supersymmetry, since
\mathcal{A}
in equation (
105
) is exact on
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and commutes with itself, so has vanishing field strength, and also commutes with
\Phi
.

\mathcal{A}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\alpha_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&~\alpha_{M}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{M}}\end{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2i}\left(\frac{\operatorname{d}\!z}{z}-\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}}{\bar{z}}\right),

(105)

the
\alpha_{l}
are
2\pi
-periodic real parameters. In general, a Gukov–Witten defect also has a matrix
\eta
of two-dimensional theta angles
\eta_{l}
on the surface
\Sigma
at
z=0
,

\eta=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\eta_{2}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{2}}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&~\eta_{M}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{N_{M}}\end{pmatrix},

(106)

implemented by an insertion of
\exp\left(i\sum_{l}\eta_{l}\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{F}_{l}\right)
into the path integral for
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, where
\mathcal{F}_{l}
is the
l
th block in the field strength for
\mathcal{A}
. In total, a Gukov–Witten defect is specified by the choices of the parameters
(\alpha_{l},\beta_{l},\gamma_{l},\eta_{l})
. Generalisations of Gukov–Witten defects with higher-order poles in
\Phi
and
\mathcal{A}
, thus breaking scale invariance, are also possible

.

The holographic dual description of Gukov–Witten defects at large
N
and strong ‘t Hooft coupling was developed in refs.
Gomis:2007fi
;
Drukker:2008wr
. They are “bubbling” supergravity solutions of type IIB supergravity which are asymptotically
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
and include flux of the Ramond–Ramond field strength
F_{5}
around various five-spheres, encoding the sizes
N_{l}
of the blocks. If only the
l=1
block has non-zero
(\alpha_{l},\beta_{l},\gamma_{l},\eta_{l})
and
N_{1}\ll N

\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}

. The probe D3-branes have an
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}

Constable:2002xt
.

4.3.2
Holomorphic D3-branes

Now we return to supergravity and consider class 1 holomorphic D3-branes in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
. We adopt the coordinate system described in section
2.1
, in which we form two complex coordinates
z=x_{\parallel}^{1}+ix_{\parallel}^{2}
and
y=x_{\perp}^{1}+ix_{\perp}^{2}
, denote the remaining two parallel directions as
x_{\parallel}^{0}=t
and
x_{\parallel}^{3}=x
, and denote the remaining four
x_{\perp}^{i}
directions as
\vec{W}=(W_{1},W_{2},W_{3},W_{4})
. In this notation, the
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
metric in equation (
85
) becomes

\operatorname{d}\!s^{2}=\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(-\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}+\operatorname{d}\!x^{2}+\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}\right)+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(\operatorname{d}\!y\operatorname{d}\!\bar{y}+\operatorname{d}\!\vec{w}^{\,2}\right),

(107)

with
r^{2}=|y|^{2}+W^{2}
. The probe D3-branes span the
x_{\parallel}^{\mu}
directions
(t,x,z,\bar{z})
, as indicated in the first row of table
4(b)
, sit at constant
\vec{W}
, and have
y
a holomorphic function of
z
,
y=y(z)
. The metric induced on the D3-branes’ worldvolume, given in equation (
11
), becomes in the near-horizon limit and for holomorphic
y
,

\operatorname{d}\!s_{\mathrm{D3}}^{2}=\frac{r^{2}}{L^{2}}\left[-\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}+\left(1+\frac{L^{4}}{r^{4}}|\partial y|^{2}\right)\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}+\operatorname{d}\!x^{2}\right].

(108)

A holomorphic D3-brane embedding with non-constant
y=y(z)
breaks translational symmetry in the
(z,\bar{z})
directions and, as shown in section in section
2.1
, preserves one-half of the supersymmetries of the D3-brane background, amounting to eight supercharges. It turns out that half of the supercharges have positive two-dimensional chirality in the directions
(t,x)
with unbroken translational symmetry, while the other half have negative chirality. Thus, holomorphic D3-brane embeddings preserve two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
supersymmetry in the
(t,x)
directions.

To show that there are equal numbers of preserved supercharges with positive and negative two-dimensional chirality, we first build projectors
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
onto spinors satisfying the conditions in equation (
71
),

P_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{32}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+\Gamma_{0123}\otimes i\sigma_{2}\right)\,,\qquad P_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{32}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\pm\Gamma_{0389}\otimes i\sigma_{2}\right)\,,

(109)

where we have substituted the explicit form of
J_{(3)}=i\sigma_{2}
, and for clarity of presentation we have restored the tensor product symbol on products of matrices acting on spinor and doublet indices. The plus or minus sign in
P_{2}
is for holomorphic or antiholomorphic
y
, respectively. The two components of the doublet
\varepsilon
of Killing spinors are Majorana–Weyl spinors, both with positive ten-dimensional chirality. We build a third projector
P_{3}
onto doublets where both components positive ten-dimensional chirality

P_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{32}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}+\Gamma_{\sharp}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{2}\right).

(110)

These projectors mutually commute,
[P_{1},P_{2}]=[P_{3},P_{1}]=[P_{2},P_{3}]=0
.

The easiest way to work with the Majorana condition is to adopt a “really real” basis in which Majorana spinors and the
\Gamma_{A}
are real. In such a basis, we define the projector
\mathcal{P}=P_{1}P_{2}P_{3}
onto the space of Killing spinor doublets satisfying the kappa symmetry conditions in equation (
71
). It is straightforward to check in an explicit really real basis that the trace of the two-dimensional chirality matrix
\Gamma_{03}
vanishes on this space,

\tr\left(\mathcal{P}^{T}\Gamma_{03}\mathcal{P}\right)=0\,.

(111)

We checked this in the basis given in ref.
Freedman_Van_Proeyen_2012
. Since
\Gamma_{03}
has eigenvalues
\pm 1
, equation (
111
) implies that its restriction to the space of doublets of Majorana–Weyl spinors satisfying the kappa symmetry conditions has equal numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues. Hence, the eight supercharges preserved by the holomorphic D3-branes correspond to two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
supersymmetry.

As mentioned already, certain choices of the function
y(z)
correspond to well-known D3-brane embeddings in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
. When
y(z)
is constant, the D3-branes sit at a constant value of the radial coordinate
r=\sqrt{|y|^{2}+W^{2}}
. This corresponds to putting the dual
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM theory at a point on the Coulomb branch where the gauge group
\mathrm{SU}(N)
is spontaneously broken to
\mathrm{S}[\mathrm{U}(N-k)\times U(k)]
by a non-zero vacuum expectation value
\langle\Phi\rangle\propto r
for one of the adjoint-valued scalar fields
\Phi

Klebanov:1999tb
.
9
9

9

For constant
y
translational symmetry is unbroken, and the supersymmetry is enhanced by a factor of two to four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
. The supersymmetry enhancement arises because the kappa symmetry condition in equation (
71b
) does not apply as the coefficient of
\Gamma_{03}
in the kappa symmetry matrix (
70
) vanishes for constant

.

Alternatively, suppose we choose
\vec{W}=0
and

for some complex constant

. This D3-brane embedding in

;

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{2}|\kappa|^{2}}{|z|^{2}}\left[-\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}+\left(1+|\kappa|^{-2}\right)\operatorname{d}\!z\operatorname{d}\!\bar{z}+\operatorname{d}\!x^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=L^{2}\left(1+|\kappa|^{2}\right)\left[\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(-\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}+\operatorname{d}\!x^{2}+\operatorname{d}\!\sigma^{2}\right)+\operatorname{d}\!\psi^{2}\right],

As discussed in section

Now consider more general

Consider a holomorphic D3-brane embedding for which

Thus, perhaps unsurprisingly, we find superconformal surface defects in the IR, located at zeros of

\mathrm{AdS}_{3}\times\mathrm{S}^{1}

.

There is strong evidence that the Coulomb branch of

and references therein. Similarly, the singular

;
Chalabi:2025nbg
. In both cases, the holographically dual D3-branes provide integrable boundary conditions for strings in

;
Demjaha:2025axy
. It is then natural to wonder whether the QFTs dual to holomorphic embeddings with arbitrary non-constant

shows: outside of the

;
Chalabi:2025nbg
.

5
Summary and outlook

We have generalised the holomorphic probe D7-branes in the D3-brane background described in ref.
holomorphic_branes
to arbitrary D
q
-branes in extremal black D
p
-brane backgrounds for
p<7
. We have shown that, starting from an intersection between flat D
p
- and D
q
-branes and then replacing the D
p
-branes by the corresponding extremal supergravity background, a complex scalar
y
describing the embedding of the D
q
-branes may be made a non-trivial holomorphic or antiholomorphic function of a worldvolume coordinate
z
if the number of Neumann–Dirichlet directions
d
in the original D
p
/D
q
intersection is a multiple of four. We classified such holomorphic embeddings according to whether
y
and
z
are formed from directions parallel or perpendicular to the D
p
-branes, as summarised in table
1
. Whenever
d
is a multiple of four, holomorphic embeddings saturate a BPS bound and preserve a fraction of the supersymmetry of the D
p
-brane background — typically one-half for
d=0
or one-quarter for
d=4
or
8
.

We investigated the holography of holomorphic D
5
- and D3-branes in the
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
near-horizon limit of the extremal D3-brane background. The holomorphic D5-branes are dual to three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
hypermultiplets coupled to four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, with a mass that depends holomorphically on position. This mass triggers an RG flow, and we found using holography that in the IR one obtains supersymmetric Wilson lines in an antisymmetric representation of
\mathrm{SU}(N)
located at the zeros of the mass. The holomorphic D3-branes are dual to non-trivial translational symmetry breaking states, generically in the presence of Gukov–Witten surface defects located at poles of the embedding scalar. We used holography to show that in the IR one obtains
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
supersymmetric surface defects, located at the zeros of the holomorphic embedding scalar.

There are many possible directions for future research. For one, our analysis of the holomorphic D5- and D3-branes in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
and their dual QFTs in section
4.3
is far from complete. A natural next step would be to perform the holographic renormalisation of these probe branes
deHaro:2000vlm
;
Karch:2005ms
. There are also several possible further generalisations of the embeddings that we have discussed, with potentially interesting physics to explore. For example, can we find versions of these holomorphic embeddings with non-trivial worldvolume gauge fields?

We can obtain one immediate generalisation our holomorphic embeddings via a double Wick rotation. Consider the class 1 embeddings discussed in section
2.1
, for which the complex coordinate
z
is built from two directions
z=x_{\parallel}^{1}+ix_{\parallel}^{2}
. Performing the Wick rotations
t=-i\tilde{x}
and
x_{\parallel}^{1}=i\tilde{t}
to obtain a new spatial coordinate
\tilde{x}
and a new time coordinate
\tilde{t}
, we now have that
z=i(\tilde{t}+x_{\parallel}^{2})
and
\bar{z}=i(\tilde{t}-x_{\parallel}^{2})
. Thus, the result of section
2.1
, that
y
can be any holomorphic or antiholomorphic function of
z
, becomes the statement that
y
can be any function of the lightcone coordinate
\tilde{t}+x_{\parallel}^{2}
or of
\tilde{t}-x_{\parallel}^{2}
. That this solves the D
q
-brane equations of motion in the D
p
-brane background can straightforwardly be confirmed by direct calculation.

In appendix
A
we describe another generalisation of class 1 embeddings, for which
y=x_{\perp}^{1}+ix_{\perp}^{2}
is a holomorphic function of multiple complex coordinates
z_{n}
, each formed from
x_{\parallel}^{\mu}
directions. We show that any such
y
solves the D
q
-brane equations of motion and preserves a fraction of the supersymmetry of the D
p
-brane background. Concretely, we show that with
M
complex coordinates
(z_{1},\cdots,z_{M})
, for
M>1
the possible holomorphic embeddings have
d=0
ND directions and preserve a fraction
1/2^{M}
of the supersymmetry of the D
p
-brane background.

Another natural direction is to look for holomorphic embeddings of D-branes or other types of extended objects in other supergravity backgrounds. In appendix
B
we perform a first step in this direction, demonstrating the existence of holomorphic M2- and M5-brane embeddings in the extremal M2- and M5-brane backgrounds of eleven-dimensional supergravity. The holography of these embeddings would also be interesting to explore.

One could also try to go beyond the probe limit and find supergravity solutions that account for the backreaction of holomorphic D-brane embeddings, given the amount of supersymmetry they preserve. A natural place to start may be the class 1 D5-brane embeddings in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times\mathrm{S}^{5}
considered in section
4.2
. The backreacted solutions that would correspond to constant
y=0
are known
DHoker:2007zhm
;
DHoker:2007hhe
, and one could attempt to find a generalisation of these solutions that would describe non-trivial
y(z)
. Given the analysis of section
4.2
, we expect that deep in the bulk of this geometry and close to a zero of
y(z)
, such a solution should approach the solution of type IIB supergravity dual to an antisymmetric-representation Wilson line described in ref.
DHoker:2007mci
.

We hope that our work can serve as a launchpad for further fruitful research, in the directions we have suggested and others.

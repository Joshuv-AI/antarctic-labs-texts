Single-position intraperitoneal laparoscopic radical nephroureterectomy using a modified supine position
Authors: Xiaomin Han, Menghao Zhou, Ming Xiong, Yajun Xiao, Yuqi Wu, Huiling Jiang, Mengjiang Tu, Song Wu, Teng Hou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo describe a novel single-position intraperitoneal laparoscopic surgery (SP-ILS) technique for radical nephroureterectomy (RNU) and bladder cuff excision (BCE) in the supine position.MethodsBetween January 2019 and June 2023, 40 patients with UTUC underwent laparoscopic RNU and BCE using the SP-ILS technique. Clinical, perioperative, and pathological data were retrospectively analyzed.ResultsAll procedures were performed laparoscopically without patient repositioning. No intraoperative or postoperative complications occurred. The mean operative time was 101.0 ± 24.5 minutes, and mean estimated blood loss was 53 ± 27.0 mL. The median postoperative hospital stay was 4.2 ± 1.1 days. Postoperative pathology revealed Ta in 14 patients, T1 in 21 patients, and T2 in 5 patients. During a mean follow-up of 12.9 months (range, 2–24), one patient developed bladder recurrence.ConclusionsThe SP-ILS technique offers direct visualization, reproducible bladder cuff excision, and efficient single-position workflow during both LRNU and BCE.

Introduction

Radical nephroureterectomy (RNU) with bladder cuff excision (BCE) remains the standard treatment for upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC). Since the first reported laparoscopic RNU (LRNU) in 1991[1], minimally invasive approaches have been increasingly adopted due to their reduced morbidity, shorter hospital stay, and comparable oncological outcomes compared with open surgery [2].

Traditionally, LRNU requires intraoperative patient repositioning from a flank position to the supine position to complete the distal ureter dissection and BCE. This not only prolongs operative time but also increases the complexity of the procedure. In recent years, various authors have reported their experiences and outcomes in single-position LRUC [3]. However, laparoscopic BCE in the flank position can be technically demanding due to limited pelvic working space, especially in obese patients or those with a history of pelvic surgery. Herein, we describe a modified single-position intraperitoneal laparoscopic surgery (SP-ILS) technique performed in the supine position for adequate surgical exposure during both LRNU and BCE. Our technique does not require patient repositioning or port reassignment.

Materials and methods

Patients

Between January 2019 and June 2023, 40 patients diagnosed with UTUC were included in this study. Preoperative evaluation included CT ureterography or magnetic resonance Imaging, urine cytology, and cystoscopy. None of these patients had lymph node or distant metastasis preoperatively. All patients underwent LRNU and BCE using the SP-ILS technique. Approval for the study was obtained from the South China Hospital of Shenzhen University Institutional Review Board. Surgical technique

Patients were initially placed in a supine position with a inflatable pillow under the affected side, then the operating table was tilted toward the unaffected side to achieve 20–30 degrees of abdominal rotation. (Fig. 1). They were secured to the operating table with 2-inch-wide tape to allow tilting. This positioning strategy has been successfully applied in our previous laparoscopic procedures [4]. A 10 mm camera port was introduced at the umbilicus using an open Hasson technique. Two working 12-mm trocars were positioned along both sides of the mid-clavicular line. A 5-mm working port was placed 5 cm below the xiphoid. Left SP-ILS may be performed with four trocars in most of cases, although a fifth additional port below the costal margin at appropriate positions can be optional. The procedure began with LRNU using the first spatial orientation strategy (Fig. 2A). On the left side, the peritoneum was incised laterally along the paracolic sulcus, and the descending colon was mobilized and pushed to the contralateral side [4]. When required, a small window was created in the colonic mesentery, through which a cotton tape was passed to retract the colon away from the operative field [4]. The genital vein was identified and traced to locate the renal hilum. Then, the renal artery and renal vein were carefully dissected and clipped with Hem-o-lok. The ureter was clipped and mobilized caudally, and the kidney was fully released. After nephrectomy completion, the operating table was tilted into Trendelenburg position, and the laparoscope was turned caudally to adjust the orientation of the view for subsequent BCE (Fig. 2B). An additional trocar is inserted between the umbilicus and anterior superior iliac spine to retract the bowel from the operative field. The ureter was further dissected over the iliac vessels down to the ureterovesical junction. The distal ureter was dissected to the bladder wall and a circumferential cuff of 1.5–2.0 cm was excised en bloc. Results

All the SP-ILS procedures were successfully performed, without conversion to open surgery or intraoperative complications. The characteristics of patients are presented in Table 1. The mean operative time and estimated blood loss were 101.0 ± 24.5 min and 53 ± 27.0 ml, respectively. The median postoperative hospital stay was 4.2 ± 1.1 days. Pathologically, all patients had confirmed urothelial carcinoma. Among them, renal pelvis was the primary site for UTUC in 29 cases (72.5%), followed by proximal ureter in 9 cases (22.5%) and distal ureter in 2 cases (5%). Most of the pathological stages were pTa and pT1, and all malignant specimens demonstrated negative margins. The mean follow-up period was 12.9 months, one patient was found to have bladder recurrence.

Discussion

The evolution of RNU with BCE for the treatment of UTUC has undergone a rapid shift from open to minimally invasive approaches since Clayman’s first case of LRNU in 1991[1]. This transition has been driven by the demonstrable advantages of LNU, including reduced intraoperative blood loss, diminished perioperative pain, shorter hospital stays, and accelerated patient recovery compared open surgery [5]. In 2009, the first single-position LRNU was described by Desai et al.[6], and subsequently, it becomes the preferred modality in many centers. Despite technological advancements, managing the ureterovesical junction remains technically challenging [7].

Difficulties in single-position laparoscopic surgeries include achieving adequate working space during BCE and bladder closure, and minimizing the risks of tumor cell spillage and urine extravasation [8]. To overcome these limitations, several groups have proposed single-position strategies. For example, Wu et al. reported a complete retroperitoneal LRNU with BCE without patient repositioning [8]. Miki et al. described a supine extraperitoneal approach allowing LRNU and BCE in one pposition [9]. Despite these innovations, BCE remains technically challenging due to the limited pelvic operative field in the flank position. This limitation persists when all working trocars are positioned ipsilaterally, restricting both the field of view and instrument maneuverability—especially in obese patients or those with a history of pelvic surgery. Extraperitoneal approaches provide excellent access to retroperitoneal lymph node dissection and avoid bowel mobilization, but their broader adoption is limited by the steep learning curve [9]. More recently, Izzo and colleagues introduced a single-port robotic nephroureterectomy through a supine anterior approach [10]. However, robotic systems are costly and not universally available.

In our modified approach, the working trocars are positioned bilaterally, adopting the conventional lower urinary tract surgeries’ port arrangements. The SP-ILS technique offers two main advantages: (1) it provides wide operative field for both LRNU and BCE, improving visualization of the bladder cuff compared with flank-based approaches. (2) it eliminates the need for repositioning or re-docking, thereby simplifying workflow and potentially reducing operative time. Compared with other single-position techniques, SP-ILS benefits from greater working space, while avoiding the ergonomic and cost limitations associated with robotic systems. Previously, we applied this specific orientation strategies in LRUN and retroperitoneal lymph nodes dissection for locally advanced UTUC and Zinner’s Syndrome [4, 11].

Management of the bladder cuff is critically important for oncologic control.‌ Fundamental principles include en bloc resection, avoidance of urine and tumor spillage, achievement of negative margins, water-tight closure, and timely intravesical chemotherapy instillation (ICI) [12]. Patients with incomplete resections of the bladder cuff are at increased risk of tumor recurrence. About 33–75% of the UTUC patients have tumor recurrence in a remaining ureteral stump or in the bladder [13]. In locally advanced-stage UTUC, inadequate management of the distal ureter and bladder cuff can directly result in a elevated disease-specific mortality [14]. Various strategies, including open, endoscopic, extravesical, or transvesical approaches, have been described, each with advantages and drawbacks [15]. In the present study, we provide a single-position, dual-oriented technique, with the first stage comprises LRNU and caudal ureter dissection, and the second stage entails distal ureter management. Our SP-ILS technique allows en bloc resection of the distal ureter with an adequate cuff under direct vision, followed by secure two-layer closure, which allowed for early use of ICI and may contributedto low recurrence rates in the current series.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. This is a retrospective single-center study with a relatively small sample size and short follow-up. Only one case of intravesical recurrence was observed, but the lack of long-term outcomes and survival analysis precludes definitive conclusions regarding oncological efficacy. Furthermore, successful performance of SP-ILS requires surgeons with extensive experience in intraperitoneal laparoscopic procedures.

Conclusion

In summary, SP-ILS offers a single-position intraperitoneal approach for LRNU with BCE. It appears to combine the ergonomic advantages of the transperitoneal route with the workflow efficiency of a single-position technique. While our preliminary outcomes are encouraging, larger multicenter studies with longer follow-up are necessary to validate its oncological safety. This technique may be an alternative and minimally invasive treatment option for patients with UTUC.

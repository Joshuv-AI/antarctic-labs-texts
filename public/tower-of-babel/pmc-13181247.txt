Case report: Endovascular treatment of a late aortic pseudoaneurysm due to gunshot injury
Authors: Juan Mariano Statti, Nicolás Dallapé de la Reta, Martín Oscos, Pablo Pedroni, Federico Giachello, Agustín Hauqui, Juan Scaglia, Diego Grinfeld, Ramiro Acevedo, Ricardo Sarmiento, Raúl Solernó
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Post-traumatic aortic pseudoaneurysm is a rare but potentially life-threatening condition that may present years after the initial injury. Management can be particularly challenging when the lesion is located adjacent to major visceral arterial branches. We report the case of a late-presenting abdominal aortic pseudoaneurysm secondary to a gunshot wound, successfully treated using a percutaneous endovascular approach. This case highlights the role of multimodality cardiovascular imaging in the diagnosis, procedural planning, and treatment of complex vascular lesions.

Case presentation

A 39-year-old man with a history of an abdominal gunshot wound sustained twelve years earlier presented with progressive, persistent, and disabling low back pain over the preceding year. At the time of the initial injury, he required emergent laparotomy with inferior vena cava repair due to hypovolemic shock and intra-abdominal hemorrhage.

Given the chronic nature of the pain, neurosurgical evaluation was requested. A lumbosacral computed tomography scan incidentally revealed a large abdominal aortic pseudoaneurysm (PA) arising from the posterior wall of the abdominal aorta, in close proximity to the celiac trunk, superior mesenteric artery, and both renal arteries.

The pseudoaneurysm measured 70 × 70 mm, was partially thrombosed, and demonstrated a residual lumen of 11 × 21 mm. The neck measured approximately 26 mm in width. Significant erosion of the T12 to L2 vertebral bodies was observed (Figs. 1 and 2). The case was discussed at a multidisciplinary meeting involving radiologist, vascular surgery, interventional cardiology, and neurosurgery. Considering the lesion’s anatomy, large size, prior abdominal surgery, and elevated operative risk, an endovascular approach was favored.

The procedure was performed under conscious sedation via bilateral common femoral artery access. Using image fusion guidance, the pseudoaneurysm neck and the origins of the splanchnic vessels were identified. A 5-Fr Cobra catheter was positioned within the pseudoaneurysm through a 6-Fr left femoral sheath. A 12-Fr sheath was inserted in the right femoral artery. A balloon-expandable stent Andrasten (Andramed GmbH, Reutlingen, Germany), 48 mm in length, mounted on a 20-mm diameter balloon, was deployed across the pseudoaneurysm neck, preserving flow to the visceral branches (Fig. 3). Final angiography demonstrated complete exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm with preserved patency of all splanchnic arteries. No peri-procedural complications occurred, and the patient was discharged the following day.

At 30-day follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic. Computed tomography confirmed complete thrombosis of the pseudoaneurysm with preserved patency of all major vessels (Figs. 5 and 6). Discussion

Post-traumatic aortic pseudoaneurysm is a rare but potentially life-threatening condition that may present years or even decades after the initial injury, posing significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. Its pathophysiology is related to partial disruption of the arterial wall contained by surrounding tissues, allowing the lesion to remain clinically silent for prolonged periods until progressive enlargement, compression of adjacent structures, or risk of rupture occurs. In-hospital mortality has been reported to reach up to 61% with conservative management and approximately 9% following open surgical repair [[1], [9]]. Late presentation after penetrating trauma, as in this case, further complicates diagnosis and management.

In the present case, the pseudoaneurysm originated from the posterior wall of the abdominal aorta and was located in close proximity to the celiac trunk, superior mesenteric artery, and both renal arteries, resulting in a highly complex anatomical scenario. Its large size and wide neck further increased procedural difficulty. In addition, progressive erosion of the T12 to L2 vertebral bodies represents an uncommon but clinically relevant manifestation, explaining the patient’s disabling low back pain and reinforcing the indication for active treatment even in the absence of ischemic or neurological deficits.

Over recent decades, endovascular repair has emerged as an effective alternative to open surgery for traumatic aortic injuries, offering reduced perioperative morbidity and mortality, shorter hospital stays, and faster functional recovery. These advantages are particularly relevant in patients with a history of major abdominal surgery, as redo open repair carries a substantially increased risk of hemorrhagic, infectious, and technical complications [2,3].

Selection of the most appropriate endovascular strategy required careful evaluation of all available options. In this case, the use of a covered stent was contraindicated due to the pseudoaneurysm’s proximity to major splanchnic branches, which would have posed a high risk of visceral ischemia. Percutaneous occluder devices, such as Amplatzer plugs, have been successfully used in selected aortic pseudoaneurysms [[4], [5], [6], [7]]. However, the wide and short neck of the lesion in this patient precluded this option. Stent-assisted coil embolization, originally described for wide-necked and fusiform intracranial aneurysms, allows the creation of a neo-neck using stent struts, facilitating safe coil deployment while preserving flow within the parent vessel [8]. Applying this concept to a complex abdominal aortic pseudoaneurysm proved to be safe and effective in achieving complete exclusion of the lesion.

The technical success achieved in this case—demonstrated by complete exclusion of the pseudoaneurysm, preservation of visceral artery patency, and prompt resolution of symptoms—supports the feasibility and effectiveness of this hybrid strategy in highly unfavorable anatomies. Follow-up computed tomography confirmed the durability of the result, with complete thrombosis of the pseudoaneurysm sac and no evidence of residual flow or procedure-related complications.

Furthermore, the use of image fusion combining pre-procedural computed tomography angiography with real-time fluoroscopy played a pivotal role in procedural planning and execution. This technology enabled precise identification of the pseudoaneurysm neck and visceral branch origins, optimizing stent positioning while reducing contrast volume and fluoroscopy time—key advantages in complex, high-risk endovascular interventions.

Conclusion

This case demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of stent-assisted coil embolization for the treatment of large, complex abdominal aortic pseudoaneurysms adjacent to major visceral branches. Endovascular therapy represents a less invasive alternative to open surgical repair, offering favorable outcomes in high-risk patients when guided by a multidisciplinary approach. Additionally, fusion imaging combining computed tomography angiography with real-time fluoroscopy proved to be a valuable tool, enhancing procedural precision, reducing contrast usage, and improving procedural safety in complex endovascular interventions.

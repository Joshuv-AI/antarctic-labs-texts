Persistence of pulses for certain reaction-diffusion equations in dimensions two and three
Vitali Vougalter

Abstract
. We address the persistence under a perturbation of stationary pulse solutions of some reaction-diffusion type equations in dimensions d = 2 , 3 d=2,3 and evaluate the asymptotic approximations of such pulses to the leading order in the parameter of the perturbation.

35K57, 35B20, 92D15

1.
Introduction

Consider the equation,

(1.1)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Delta u+au^{2}(1-u)-\sigma(x)u,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad d=2,3,\quad t\geq 0.

Here
a>0
is a constant. Problems of this kind are important for the study of
the population dynamics (see e.g.
[
10
]
).
In such context,
u(x,t)
stands for the density of a population at time
t

in location
x
, the diffusion
term describes its migration, the second term in the right side is the
reproduction rate, and the last term is the mortality rate. In the situation of
the sexual reproduction, the reproduction rate is proportional to the second
power of the population density and to the available resources
(1-u)
, given
by the difference of the rate of production of resources and the rate of their
consumption. Stationary solutions of (
1.1
) solve

(1.2)

\Delta u+au^{2}(1-u)-\sigma(x)u=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad d=2,3.

Stationary solutions of reaction-diffusion type problems decaying at
infinities are called

pulses
. Their existence along with the stability and other related issues
have been studied actively for both local and nonlocal models in recent years,
for example, in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
,

[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,

[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
.
In the present work, we only focus on the persistence of pulses and
their asymptotic approximations to the leading order of the small parameter
when a perturbation is applied to problem (
1.1
). Similar ideas in the
context of standing solitary waves of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation,
when a perturbation is applied to either a scalar potential contained in it or
to the nonlinear term, were exploited in
[
12
]
.

The unperturbed stationary equation in our case is

(1.3)

\Delta w_{0}+aw_{0}^{2}(1-w_{0})-\sigma_{0}(x)w_{0}=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad d=2,3.

In the work we consider the space
H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
equipped
with the norm

(1.4)

\|u\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2},\quad d=2,3.

By means of the standard Sobolev embedding, we have

(1.5)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq c_{e}\|u\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})},\quad d=2,3,

where
c_{e}>0
is the constant of the embedding.
Let us first state the following conditions on the parameters of our unperturbed
equation along
with its solution that we are going to consider.

Assumption 1.1.

Let
d=2,3
, the constant
a>0
and the function

\sigma_{0}(x):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}},\ \sigma_{0}(x)\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ \hbox{lim}_{|x|\to\infty}\sigma_{0}(x)=\delta>0.

We also assume that problem (
1.3
) has a pulse solution

w_{0}(x)>0,\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, which satisfies

(1.6)

w_{0}(x)\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ w_{0}(x)\leq Ce^{-\alpha|x|},\ x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
C,\alpha>0
are the constants.

It can be easily deduced from equation (
1.3
) via Assumption 1.1 that

w_{0}(x)\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
because it decays
exponentially as
|x|\to\infty
. Here we
used the conditions on
\sigma_{0}(x)
.

When a perturbation is applied to stationary problem (
1.3
), we
have

(1.7)

\Delta w+aw^{2}(1-w)-[\sigma_{0}(x)+\varepsilon\sigma_{1}(x)]w=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad d=2,3.

Assumption 1.2.

Let the parameter
\varepsilon\geq 0
and the
nontrivial function

(1.8)

\sigma_{1}(x):{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}},\ \sigma_{1}(x)\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ \hbox{lim}_{|x|\to\infty}\sigma_{1}(x)=0.

Let us look for the solutions of problem (
1.7
) in the form

(1.9)

w(x)=w_{0}(x)+w_{p}(x).

By virtue of (
1.7
) along with (
1.3
), we arrive at

(1.10)

L_{0}w_{p}(x)=a(1-3w_{0}(x)){w_{p}^{2}(x)}-a{w_{p}^{3}(x)}-\varepsilon\sigma_{1}(x)(w_{0}(x)+w_{p}(x))

with

(1.11)

L_{0}=-\Delta+a(3w_{0}^{2}(x)-2w_{0}(x))+\sigma_{0}(x):H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\ d=2,3.

Under Assumption 1.1, it can be trivially checked that the essential spectrum
of
L_{0}
is

(1.12)

\sigma_{ess}(L_{0})=[\delta,+\infty).

If
\sigma_{0}(x)
had been a constant
function in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, then the operator
L_{0}
would have had zero
modes

\displaystyle{\frac{\partial w_{0}}{\partial x_{k}},\ 1\leq k\leq d}
. This
can be easily obtained by differentiating
both sides of problem (
1.3
). However, in the present work we assume
the function
\sigma_{0}(x)
to be generic such that operator (
1.11
)
would have a trivial kernel.

Assumption 1.3.

The kernel

\hbox{ker}(L_{0})=\{0\}
.

By virtue of (
1.12
) along with Assumption 1.3, the operator

(1.13)

L_{0}^{-1}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad d=2,3

is bounded, so that its norm

(1.14)

\|L_{0}^{-1}\|<\infty.

We introduce a closed ball in the Sobolev space
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

(1.15)

B_{\rho}:=\{u\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\ |\ \|u\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\rho\},\quad\rho>0,\quad d=2,3.

Let us seek the solutions of equation (
1.10
) as fixed points of the
auxiliary nonlinear problem

(1.16)

L_{0}u(x)=a(1-3w_{0}(x)){v^{2}(x)}-a{v^{3}(x)}-\varepsilon\sigma_{1}(x)(w_{0}(x)+v(x)).

For a given function
v(x)
this is an equation with respect to
u(x)
.
Note that the
similar ideas for problems containing non-Fredholm operators in their
left sides have been exploited in
[
4
]
and
[
14
]
.
Existence of solutions of certain quadratic integral equations in dimensions
two and three was discussed in
[
13
]
.
Let us
introduce the operator
t
, such that
u=tv
, where
u
is a solution of
equation (
1.16
).
Our main statement is as follows.

Theorem 1.4.

Let Assumptions 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 be valid. Then
problem (
1.16
) defines the map
t:B_{\rho}\to B_{\rho}
, which is a strict
contraction for all
0<\rho<\rho^{*}
and
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{*}
for
certain
\rho^{*}>0
and
\varepsilon^{*}>0
. The unique fixed point
w_{p}(x)
of
this map
t
is the only solution of equation (
1.10
) in
B_{\rho}
, so
that

(1.17)

w_{p}(x)=-\varepsilon L_{0}^{-1}[\sigma_{1}(x)w_{0}(x)]+O({\varepsilon}^{2}).

The resulting solution of problem (
1.7
)
w(x)\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is given by formula (
1.9
).

We note that
O({\varepsilon}^{2})
in the right side of (
1.17
)
stands for the terms of the order
{\varepsilon}^{2}
and higher in the

H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
norm. The proof of Theorem 1.4 is presented in the
following section.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1.4

Proof.
Let us first demonstrate the uniqueness of
solutions of equation (
1.16
).
Suppose that there exists a
v\in B_{\rho}
, so that (
1.16
) admits two
solutions

u_{1}
,
u_{2}\in B_{\rho}
. Then the difference function

\xi(x):=u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x)\in H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is a solution of the
homogeneous problem

(2.1)

L_{0}\xi=0.

By virtue of Assumption 1.3, equation (
2.1
) has only a trivial solution.
This gives us the uniqueness of solutions of (
1.16
).

Then, for an arbitrary
v(x)\in B_{\rho}
, we obtain the upper bound on the
right side of equation (
1.16
) in the absolute value as

[a(1+3\|w_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+

(2.2)

+a\|v\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}]|v(x)|+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|w_{0}(x)|.

Clearly,
\sigma_{1}(x)\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
via Assumption 1.2. By
virtue of the Sobolev embedding (
1.5
), quantity (
2.2
) can be
estimated from above by

[a(1+3c_{e}\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})c_{e}\|v\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}+

(2.3)

+ac_{e}^{2}\|v\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}]|v(x)|+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|w_{0}(x)|.

Since
v(x)\in B_{\rho}
, we have the upper bound for (
2.3
) equal to

[a(1+3c_{e}\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})c_{e}\rho+

(2.4)

+ac_{e}^{2}\rho^{2}+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}]|v(x)|+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}|w_{0}(x)|.

Using (
1.16
), we easily derive that

\|u\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\|L_{0}^{-1}\|\{[a(1+3c_{e}\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})c_{e}\rho+

(2.5)

Evidently, the estimate

\|L_{0}^{-1}\|\{ac_{e}(1+3c_{e}\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})\rho^{2}+

(2.6)

+ac_{e}^{2}\rho^{3}+\varepsilon\|\sigma_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}(\rho+\|w_{0}\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})})\}\leq\rho

can be achieved for all
\rho>0
and
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.
Clearly,
the upper bound on the values of
\varepsilon>0
here will depend on
\rho
.
Therefore,

(2.7)

\|u\|_{H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq\rho,

so that
u\in B_{\rho}
as well. Thus, problem (
1.16
) defines the
map
t:B_{\rho}\to B_{\rho}
for all
\rho>0
and
\varepsilon>0

small enough.

The goal is to show that this map is a strict contraction. Let us choose
arbitrarily
v_{1}
,
v_{2}\in B_{\rho}
. The reasoning above implies that

u_{1}:=tv_{1}
,
u_{2}:=tv_{2}\in B_{\rho}
as well for all
\rho>0
and

\varepsilon>0

sufficiently small. By virtue of equation (

), we
have

(2.8)

L_{0}u_{1}(x)=a(1-3w_{0}(x)){v_{1}^{2}(x)}-a{v_{1}^{3}(x)}-\varepsilon\sigma_{1}(x)(w_{0}(x)+v_{1}(x)),

Formulas (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) yield

L_{0}(u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x))=(v_{1}(x)-v_{2}(x))\{a(1-3w_{0}(x))(v_{1}(x)+v_{2}(x))-

Multi-objective global sensitivity analysis of shipborne equipment based on AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention
Authors: ShuangShuang Wu, Ming Yan, Lei Zhang, Linhan Feng, Ning Yang, Haichao Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
To address the high computational cost in global sensitivity analysis of large shipborne equipment, a CNN-BiLSTM-Attention model optimized by a Genetic Algorithm-Particle Swarm Optimization with adaptive weight updating (AGPSO) is proposed. This model is combined with Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis for efficient multi-input multi-output (MIMO) sensitivity evaluation of complex structural systems. Firstly, an AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention model is developed to automatically optimize three key hyperparameters: learning rate, batch size, and L2 regularization, thereby obtaining the optimal network. Comparative results demonstrate that it achieves training accuracies of 99.49% for relative displacement and 99.12% for absolute acceleration, significantly improving prediction performance and computational efficiency. Secondly, integrated with the Sobol’ method, a MIMO sensitivity framework is established to quantify parameter influence. Finally, applied to a gas turbine isolation system, the approach identifies critical isolator and limiter parameters affecting shock response. The method significantly improves efficiency and generalization, offering practical value for structural design and optimization of marine equipment.

Introduction

Sensitivity Analysis (SA) is an analytical method used to evaluate the influence of each input variable on the output and to judge the behavior of the system when the parameters are uncertain or changing. It plays a critical role in understanding system behavior under parameter uncertainty or variability. SA is widely employed in design optimization, identification of key parameters, dimensionality reduction, and performance enhancement of complex systems, etc.1,2. In engineering practice, sensitivity analysis can be categorized into Local Sensitivity Analysis (LSA) and Global Sensitivity Analysis (GSA). LSA evaluates the influence of a single input parameter on the model output, without accounting for interactions among input variables. It is computationally efficient and primarily applicable to linear or near-linear systems3. In contrast, GSA considers both the individual effects of input variables and their mutual interactions, enabling sensitivity evaluation under multi-parameter variation. This makes GSA more suitable for complex and highly nonlinear systems4. GSA methods can be broadly classified into regression-based, moment-independent, and variance-based approaches. Regression-based sensitivity analysis relies heavily on the choice of regression models and is typically limited to linear or weakly nonlinear systems. Moment-independent methods, while more general, are often computationally intensive. In comparison, variance-based sensitivity analysis offers a more comprehensive evaluation by quantifying the contribution of each input variable to the output variance. This method is particularly effective in identifying influential parameters in complex and nonlinear systems and has been widely applied in fields such as earthquake engineering5, aerospace6 marine engineering7, automotive design8, and energy systems9.

The variance-based GSA can be further divided into two categories: classical and metamodel GSA. Classical GSA methods estimate conditional variances directly using Monte Carlo or quasi-Monte Carlo sampling techniques. Representative approaches include the Fourier Amplitude Sensitivity Test (FAST), Extended FAST, Random Balance Designs, and the Sobol’ method. Among these, the Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis has gained the most widespread application due to its robustness and ability to quantify both first-order and higher-order interaction effects. A major drawback of these classical methods is their requirement for thousands or even tens of thousands of model evaluations, which renders them impractical for computationally intensive engineering problems10. Therefore, metamodel-based global sensitivity analysis methods have emerged. Commonly used metamodels include the Kriging model, the response surface function (RSF) model, the radial basis function (RBF) model, the polynomial chaos expansions (PCE) model, and neural networks, among others. References proposed a Sobol’ sensitivity analysis method based on a Kriging model. Compared with traditional GSA approaches, this method not only improves computational accuracy but also reduces the dimensionality of the distribution parameters11–13. Chakraborty constructed a metamodel based on orthogonal polynomial bases and derived an analytical expression for the Sobol’ index from polynomial coefficients14. Wu derived the Sobol’ index for a hybrid model combining RBF and PCE, specifically designed for computationally expensive engineering problems15–17. Abbiati developed a global sensitivity analysis framework for hybrid simulation environments using a PCE-based model18. Azzini and Zhu combined the RSF model with Sobol’ sensitivity analysis and proposed a modified model that significantly simplifies the expression of Sobol’ indices, demonstrating superior performance particularly in small-sample scenarios19,20. All the aforementioned studies are based on explicit functional relationships within meta-models to perform Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis. In such approaches, the accuracy of sensitivity indices relies entirely on the precision of the mathematical metamodel. However, these methods are less suitable for highly complex nonlinear systems that cannot be accurately represented by explicit mathematical expressions. With the rapid development of deep learning technology, neural networks have also been increasingly used in the field of global sensitivity analysis in recent years, significantly improving both computational efficiency and accuracy while addressing the limitations of traditional mathematical surrogate models. Existing studies have shown that convolutional and other deep learning models can replace high-fidelity numerical simulators while maintaining acceptable accuracy in the estimation of Sobol’ sensitivity indices21. For example, Dong proposed an intelligent meta-model based on PSO-CNN22. By integrating this model with Sobol’ and Monte Carlo-based global sensitivity analysis methods, they effectively quantified the influence of input parameters on output responses. Wu innovatively proposed an end-to-end framework that integrates the SInception-CNN architecture with Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis. Compared to traditional sensitivity analysis methods, this approach demonstrates superior robustness and accuracy23. Xue directly combined CNN-based surrogate models with the Sobol’ method and reported a significant reduction in computational cost compared with direct Monte Carlo evaluations24. Methodologically, Iaquinta have introduced uncertainty quantification strategies such as the bootstrap method to evaluate the estimation error of Sobol’ indices when using learning-based surrogate models25. Ma conducted a PSO-CNN model enhanced with an improved optimizer to perform global sensitivity analysis on parameters significantly affecting the mechanical behavior of nodes and gates, thereby identifying the key factors influencing structural performance26. Du integrated surrogate modeling with the Sobol’ method for seismic consequence assessment, demonstrating its interdisciplinary applicability27. At the same time, recent reviews have pointed out that DL-assisted GSA has both advantages and risks: although surrogate models can significantly reduce computational costs, attention must be paid to training sample coverage, confidence evaluation, and the incorporation of physical constraints to avoid biased sensitivity rankings28. Recent progress in neural dynamics and intelligent optimization has significantly improved the efficiency and robustness of nonconvex learning systems. Fan proposed a coevolutionary neural dynamics model incorporating multiple search strategies for nonconvex optimization problems, offering a theoretical foundation for hybrid evolutionary-neural frameworks29. Similarly, Liu introduced the Activated Gradient approach to enhance gradient propagation in deep neural networks, effectively accelerating convergence in complex architectures30. In addition, neural differential optimization and momentum recurrent neural networks have been explored for nonlinear or sparse motion planning tasks31,32, demonstrating the flexibility of neural dynamic systems in solving complex high-dimensional optimization problems.

CNN is good at extracting local features from sequences, but it is relatively simple to extract model features only using CNN, which is prone to problems such as over-fitting and insufficient generalization ability. In addition, although the PSO algorithm has the ability of global search, it may still fall into the local optimal solution and tends to exhibit slower convergence rates in high-dimensional parameter spaces. Therefore, an AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention fusion mechanism is proposed, combined with Sobol’ method to achieve MIMO global sensitivity, aiming to address the shortcomings of previous research. The model employs a CNN for automatic extraction of multidimensional local features, while a BiLSTM network captures both forward and backward long-term dependencies within the features. Additionally, an Attention mechanism is incorporated to focus on the most relevant features, thereby enhancing the prediction accuracy. To further improve the convergence speed and training precision of the network, AGPSO is utilized to optimize key hyperparameters, including the learning rate, batch size, and L2 regularization coefficient. Compared with PSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention and PSO-CNN models, the correctness and effectiveness of the proposed hybrid model are verified. Finally, using a shipborne gas turbine as a case, a sensitivity analysis is conducted on the shock-resistant parameters of its vibration isolation system. The key influencing factors were identified, providing essential design variables for subsequent optimization.

AGPSO algorithm

Particle Swarm optimization

PSO is an evolutionary algorithm based on swarm intelligence, which optimizes the objective function by simulating the behavior of a group of particles collaboratively exploring the search space to locate the optimal solution. Due to its conceptual simplicity and ease of implementation, PSO has been widely applied to hyperparameter tuning in CNN models33–35. In the PSO algorithm, each individual in the population is referred to as a particle, representing a candidate solution within the global search space. These particles traverse the search space at a certain velocity and dynamically adjust their trajectories based on their own experience as well as the experience of their peers. Through iterative updates, each particle refines its direction and speed by referencing both its individual and global optimum, thereby establishing a positive feedback mechanism for collective optimization. As a result, the PSO algorithm enables particles to gradually converge toward more optimal regions based on their fitness values, ultimately leading to the discovery of the global optimum solution. As a global random research algorithm, PSO enhances the probability of finding an optimal solution across successive generations. Each particle in the swarm is characterized by two attributes: position and velocity. AGPSO

Although PSO exhibits strong global search capabilities, it is prone to premature convergence and may easily become trapped in local optima, particularly when parameter settings are suboptimal. Moreover, in high-dimensional and complex optimization problems, PSO often suffers from slow convergence and performance sensitivity to control parameters such as the inertia weight. To address these limitations, an AGPSO is proposed. This method introduces an adaptive weighting strategy to dynamically adjust the inertia weight of PSO, thereby enabling more flexible particle updates. In addition, crossover and mutation operations from GA are incorporated to enhance the fitness function optimization, improve the balance between global and local search, and further improve convergence speed and calculation accuracy. The overall optimization process is illustrated in Fig. 2.Fig. 2Flowchart of the AGPSO optimization.

The steps for implementation are as follows:

Step 1: Parameter initialization: A PSO initialization strategy based on circle mapping is proposed to improve the diversity of particles. By employing a chaotic sequence to dynamically initialize the positions and velocities of particles, the initial solutions are evenly distributed across the search space, thereby reducing the likelihood of early convergence to local optima and enhancing the global exploration capability of the algorithm. The Circle mapping can be expressed as:3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$x_{i + 1} = \bmod \left( {x_{i} + b - \left( {\frac{a}{2\pi }} \right)\sin \left( {2\pi x_{i} } \right),1} \right)$$\end{document}where a and b are control parameters, commonly taken as 0.5 and 0.2. xi and xi+1 represent particles. This chaotic mapping mechanism helps to maintain ergodicity and ensures a more thorough exploration during the early search phase.

Step 2: Fitness evaluation: Evaluate the fitness of each particle to guide the particle swarm to the optimal solution. The Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) is employed as the fitness function, defined as:4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F_{fitness} = RMSE = \sqrt {\frac{1}{N}\left( {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{n} {\left( {y_{i} - \hat{y}_{i} } \right)^{2} } } \right)}$$\end{document}where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$y_{i} ,\hat{y}_{i}$$\end{document} represent the real and the predicted value, respectively, and N is the number of samples.

Step 3: Individual/global optimum calculation: Identify the minimum fitness value among all particles as the individual optimum \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$pbest_{i}$$\end{document}. Subsequently, update the global optimum \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$gbest_{i}$$\end{document} by selecting the individual optimum across the entire swarm. This iterative update guides particles toward improved solutions throughout the optimization process.

Step 4: Inertia weight updating: The inertia weight coefficients directly affect the searching ability of PSO. Therefore, an adaptive inertia weight update strategy is proposed to dynamically adjust w, aiming to improve both the convergence speed and global searching ability of the algorithm. The calculation formula is given by:5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$w = w_{\min } + \left( {w_{\max } - w_{\min } } \right) \cdot \left( {F_{i} - F_{\min } } \right)/\left( {F_{avg} - F_{\min } } \right)$$\end{document}where w is the updated inertia weight, wmin and wmax denote the lower and upper bounds of the inertia weight coefficients, respectively. Step 6: Fitness and individual/global optimum updating: Based on step 5, the fitness value is calculated to evaluate the merit of the particles. The updated particles are compared with the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$pbest_{i}$$\end{document} and the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$gbest_{i}$$\end{document}, and if the current particle is better than the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$gbest_{i}$$\end{document}, then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$pbest_{i}$$\end{document} will be saved, if the current particle is better than the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$pbest_{i}$$\end{document}, then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$gbest_{i}$$\end{document} will be saved.

Otherwise, the Genetic Algorithm will be applied in the next step.

Step 8: Mutation operation: Select the particle with the lowest fitness value and perform mutation on the particle’s position to prevent the algorithm from falling into the local optimal solution, and increase the diversity of the search space.

Step 9: Crossover operation: Use the particles after the above mutation as the parent generation, and then operate crossover on the particle’s position to generate new offspring particles by combining parental features. This improves the convergence speed of the algorithm.

Compared with conventional GA-PSO or PSO-based hybrid models, the proposed AGPSO integrates dynamic inertia and mutation adaptation, a population-diversity feedback mechanism, and multi-strategy co-evolution. These enhancements enable particles to adaptively balance exploration and exploitation, maintain swarm diversity by injecting variations when stagnation occurs, and jointly update global and personal bests through crossover, mutation, and adaptive learning rates. As a result, AGPSO achieves superior global search performance and faster convergence stability than traditional GA-PSO or PSO-based approaches that employ fixed parameters.

CNN-BiLSTM-attention

Although the CNN excels at extracting local spatial features, it has limitations in capturing temporal dependencies and global importance distribution in sequential data. To overcome these shortcomings, this section proposes a hybrid model integrating CNN with BiLSTM and Attention mechanisms. Specifically, CNN automatically extracts local spatial features from multidimensional input data, while BiLSTM captures bidirectional temporal dependencies within the feature sequences, enhancing the model’s contextual understanding. The Attention mechanism dynamically focuses on the most contributive feature regions to the prediction, further improving the model’s representational capacity and predictive accuracy. Through the synergistic combination of these components, the model effectively balances local feature extraction with global temporal information learning, significantly enhancing prediction performance on complex time-varying data. This approach addresses the CNN’s limitations in generalization and long-term dependency capture, achieving more accurate and robust predictive outcomes.

CNN

CNN is a type of feedforward neural network characterized by its convolutional architecture, initially proposed by LeCun36. The training process of CNN consists of two main phases: forward propagation and backward propagation. In the forward propagation stage, the input data passes through a series of convolutional, pooling, and fully connected layers, where features are progressively extracted and predictions are made. During backward propagation, model parameters are iteratively updated using gradient-based optimization to minimize the loss function.

The convolutional layer is responsible for local feature extraction. It applies multiple convolution kernels (filters) that slide over the input data to compute local weighted sums, forming feature maps. The convolutional operation captures spatially localized patterns. By stacking multiple convolutional layers, the network progressively learns hierarchical features, from low-level edges to high-level semantic patterns, ensuring spatial continuity and translational invariance. The pooling layer is used to downsample feature maps, reducing spatial dimensions and computational complexity while preserving important information. Common pooling methods include max pooling and average pooling. These operations improve model robustness to local distortions and help prevent overfitting. The fully connected layer integrates the features extracted by the convolutional and pooling layers and outputs the final prediction. Unlike convolutional layers, fully connected layers do not share weights, and they serve as the final decision-making stage.

CNN-BiLSTM-attention

To enhance the prediction accuracy and generalization capability of the CNN model, this study integrates BiLSTM and the Attention mechanism, thereby constructing a hybrid CNN-BiLSTM-Attention deep learning architecture. The overall network structure is illustrated in Fig. 3.Fig. 3Structure of CNN-BiLSTM-attention network.

Structure of CNN-BiLSTM-attention network.

The BiLSTM network consists of two LSTM layers operating in opposite directions, enabling the model to capture long-range dependencies in both forward and backward temporal contexts. In this study, two stacked BiLSTM layers are employed to enhance the model’s representational capacity. The LSTM architecture improves memory retention by incorporating three gating mechanisms: the forget gate ft, the input gate it, and the output gate Ot. These gates collaboratively regulate the flow of information, thereby enhancing the network’s ability to retain long-term dependencies while effectively mitigating issues such as gradient vanishing and explosion during training. This design not only accelerates convergence but also significantly improves prediction accuracy. The structure of the BiLSTM is shown in Fig. 4.Fig. 4Structure of LSTM network.

Structure of LSTM network.

Based on the input feature vector xt at time step t and the hidden state ht−1 from the previous time step t−1, the forget gate ft determines which information from the previous cell state Ct-1 should be retained or discarded to update the current cell state. The output of the forget gate lies in the range [0,1], where a value of 0 indicates complete forgetting, and a value of 1 indicates full retention of the corresponding feature. The input gate it determines which information from the current input at time step t should be retained and stored in the cell state. This process consists of two components: the first is the input gate, which utilizes a sigmoid function to identify the relevant components to be updated. The second is the candidate memory cell, which is generated through a tanh activation function based on the current input xt and the previous hidden state ht-1. The outputs of the input and forget gates jointly contribute to updating the cell state. The combination of CNN-BiLSTM-attention is selected to fully exploit the complementary strengths of spatial-emporal feature extraction and adaptive weighting, which are essential for modeling highly nonlinear, coupled responses in shipborne equipment dynamics. Specifically, CNN is employed to extract multidimensional local spatial features from input sequences such as displacement and acceleration histories. BiLSTM captures bidirectional temporal dependencies, enabling the model to learn both past and future contextual relationships that strongly influence the system’s dynamic response. After the BiLSTM layers, an Attention module is introduced to capture feature-level and temporal dependencies among multidirectional vibration responses. This mechanism assigns adaptive importance weights to different time steps and feature channels, allowing the network to focus on dominant dynamic characteristics that most influence the system response. In the context of sensitivity analysis, the Attention weights effectively reflect the relative contribution of input parameters to output variations, serving as a complementary indicator to Sobol’ indices. Therefore, the integration of Attention improves both the accuracy and interpretability of the deep surrogate model, facilitating a more reliable identification of key parameters under nonlinear coupling.

Compared with a pure LSTM or BiLSTM, the CNN-BiLSTM-Attention hybrid achieves higher feature compactness and faster convergence because the convolutional layer performs efficient pre-filtering and dimensionality reduction before sequence modeling. Compared with Transformer architectures, preliminary tests indicated that although Transformers provide strong global dependency modeling, they require significantly larger datasets and longer training times. For the present problem, where the available samples are limited (100 OLHS points), the Transformer easily overfits and exhibits unstable convergence. Therefore, the CNN-BiLSTM-Attention combination provides a better trade-off between prediction accuracy, data efficiency, and computational cost, making it particularly suitable for the surrogate modeling of vibration-shock responses in complex marine systems.

AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention model

The prediction performance of the CNN-BiLSTM-Attention hybrid network model largely depends on the proper selection of hyperparameters, such as learning rate, optimizer type, number of neurons, batch size, and so on37. However, conventional manual hyperparameter tuning methods are time-consuming and often fail to guarantee optimal performance, presenting significant limitations38. Therefore, it is essential to develop an efficient automated hyperparameter optimization method. In this paper, the AGPSO algorithm established in "AGPSO" is employed to optimize three hyperparameters: learning rate, batch size, and L2 regularization coefficient. This optimization aims to improve the predictive accuracy and convergence speed of the CNN-BiLSTM-Attention model, thereby providing a high-precision and efficient prediction model for subsequent global sensitivity analysis. Step 1: Determine the range of design variables and use Optimal Latin Hypercube Sampling (OLHS) to perform sampling, obtaining the initial design parameters.

Step 2: Preprocess the extracted initial data by removing outliers. To prevent gradient explosion due to the different units of the indicators during the computation process, the data is normalized. After all computations are completed, the results are denormalized and visualized. Step 3: The normalized sample data is divided into a training set and a test set, with 80% of the samples used as the training set and the remaining 20% as the test set for subsequent network training and evaluation.

Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis

Principle of Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis

The Sobol’ method is a classical global sensitivity analysis method based on variance decomposition theory, which quantifies the contribution of each input variable and their interactions to the total variance of the model output. Compared with local sensitivity analysis, the Sobol’ method exhibits superior adaptability to nonlinear, non-monotonic, and strongly coupled systems39.

It is assumed that the model can be expressed as:23\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Y = f\left( X \right) = f\left( {X_{1} ,X_{2} , \cdots ,X_{n} } \right)$$\end{document}where Y represents the output of the model, and X = (X1,X2,…,Xn) is the n-dimensional independent input variables.

Sensitivity analysis based on AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention

A global sensitivity analysis method is proposed by integrating the AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention hybrid neural network model established in "AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention model" with the Sobol’ method. The technical framework is illustrated in Fig. 6. The analysis steps are as follows:Fig. 6Technical route for global sensitivity analysis.

Technical route for global sensitivity analysis.

Step 1: Based on practical engineering considerations, the structural model is appropriately simplified to ensure that essential mechanical characteristics such as mass, stiffness, and boundary conditions are preserved. A corresponding finite element model is then established for numerical simulation.

Step 2: The design variables and their value ranges are determined, and sampled using the OLHS method to obtain the input sample set. The input parameters are set in the established finite element model, and the output responses are calculated, which together constitute the sample data set.

Step 3: 80% of the sample dataset is selected as the training sample set, and 20% is the test set to train and predict the AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention model, respectively. The evaluation indexes of Eqs. (20), (21) and (22) are calculated, and the network structure with better predictive performance is obtained.

Step 4: The Sobol sequence sampling method is used for sampling, which are input into the trained network model. Combine this with Sobol’ global sensitivity analysis to perform a comprehensive sensitivity evaluation. Obtain results including the loss function, fitting correlation, and global sensitivity indices, and analyze the influence of each parameter on the output response to identify the key design variables.

Engineering case study: computation and validation

This section focuses on a large-scale shipboard gas turbine as the research object and employs the global sensitivity analysis method based on the AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention proposed in "Sensitivity analysis based on AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention". It systematically evaluates the influence of various parameters of the buffering system, namely, the isolators and limiters on the gas turbine’s overall shock resistance performance. This approach accurately identifies key parameters and their contribution to the turbine’s response, providing a scientific basis and decision support for the structural design and parameter optimization of next-generation gas turbines, thereby enhancing the system’s shock resistance and operational reliability.

Finite element modeling and verification

The actual structure of a gas turbine is relatively complex, which includes numerous components and intricate interconnections. Building a finite element model based on the full detailed structure results in long simulation cycles and high computational costs. Therefore, a reasonable simplification of the entire gas turbine structure is performed, ensuring that key mechanical parameters such as dimensions, weight, and center of gravity remain consistent with the actual system. Each major component is modeled separately and then assembled into the simplified geometric model shown in Fig. 7, balancing computational efficiency with structural fidelity.Fig. 7Simplified geometric model of a gas turbine.

Simplified geometric model of a gas turbine.

The Hypermesh software is used to mesh and set the material properties as shown in Table 1. For plate and shell structures such as the base and casing, the mesh is divided based on structural characteristics. The regular areas are divided by quadrilateral shell elements, and a few irregular regions are meshed by triangular elements. Solid structures like supports and box-mounted counterweights are also divided by hexahedral elements combined with tetrahedral elements after cutting. The meshed model is imported into the ABAQUS software for nonlinear analysis, where the buffering system(isolator and limiter) is defined. The Latin hypercube test design method is one of the most famous methods in the current test design, which has the following two major advantages. Firstly, it provides strong coverage of the sample space, effectively filling the design domain. Secondly, it achieves high fitting accuracy for nonlinear response problems. OLHS sample points.

To ensure that the surrogate model remains reliable under extreme conditions, the training samples are carefully designed to cover the full range of parameter variations, including boundary and near-extreme cases. Specifically, the OLHS method is adopted to generate 100 sample points within the complete parameter ranges listed in Table 3, where each design variable (stiffness, damping, and clearance) is normalized to its physical limits. This approach guarantees that the sampling points are uniformly distributed throughout the entire multidimensional space, including very large and very small stiffness and damping values, thereby preventing the model from being trained only in the mid-range region. Moreover, to further test the robustness of the trained AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention model, several additional boundary cases are used for verification after training, confirming that the model maintained stable prediction accuracy even near parameter limits. Therefore, the surrogate model can be considered valid and reliable across the entire design domain.

Although the dataset size is relatively small, potential overfitting is effectively mitigated through fivefold cross-validation, early stopping, and the inclusion of dropout layers (rate = 0.2) within the CNN-BiLSTM-attention network. The training and validation losses remained consistent across epochs, indicating good model generalization and stability.

Global sensitivity analysis

Based on the previous research, a total of 10,000 sample points are generated using the Sobol sequence sampling method for global sensitivity analysis. The Sobol’ first-order and total-order sensitivity indices for relative displacement and absolute acceleration responses are calculated by Eqs. (46) and (48), respectively. The sensitivity results are shown in Figs.17 and 18.Fig. 17Relative displacement sensitivity analysis.Fig. 18Absolute acceleration sensitivity analysis.

Relative displacement sensitivity analysis.

Absolute acceleration sensitivity analysis.

As shown in Figs. 17 and 18, both the isolator and limiter parameters exhibit varying degrees of influence on the relative displacement and absolute acceleration response. For relative displacement, the first-order Sobol’ sensitivity indices Si are ranked in descending order as follows: ξ1 > k1 > k3 > d > ξ2 > k2 > k4. Among these, the isolator parameters have a significant influence on the relative displacement response, particularly the damping and linear stiffness. Except for the limit clearance, most parameters are negatively correlated with the relative displacement. The total sensitivity index STi indicates that limiter clearance d dominates positively, whereas increases in stiffness and damping generally reduce displacement. Physically, a larger clearance delays limiter engagement so that the isolation system remains in a low-constrained state during the main shock impulse, producing larger relative displacements. Conversely, earlier limiter engagement (smaller d) introduces additional instantaneous stiffness and constrains excursion. For absolute acceleration, the first-order effect sensitivity coefficients Si have the following influence degrees: ξ1 > k1 > k3 > d > k2 > ξ2 > k4. Similarly, the parameters of the isolator are the dominant influencing factors. With the exception of the limit clearance, most parameters show a positive correlation with the absolute acceleration response. Linear and nonlinear stiffness produce strong positive sensitivities: higher effective stiffness increases the system’s ability to transmit high-frequency shock components to the equipment, raising acceleration peaks. Damping acts to dissipate energy and reduce displacement, but due to coupling with stiffness and limiter dynamics, it can also alter the time-scale of force transmission and, in some parameter regimes, increase transient acceleration peaks. The substantial disparity between Si and STi demonstrates that limiter engagement and the nonlinear stiffness terms produce strong parameter interactions: when d changes the contact state, the effective dynamic path changes abruptly, leading to significant higher-order effects. These observations imply that design optimization must treat clearance, stiffness and damping jointly: clearance determines contact regime, stiffness controls force transmission, and damping governs energy dissipation, only their coordinated tuning yields the desired trade-off between isolation (low displacement) and shock protection (low transmitted acceleration).

When the full finite element simulation is used, the calculation time for one working condition is about 2 h. Therefore, the global sensitivity analysis of the gas turbine vibration isolation system would require approximately 834 days. Using the optimal Latin hypercube sampling to construct the AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-Attention model requires 100 finite element simulation models, and the global sensitivity takes about 8.4 days, which greatly improves the calculation efficiency.

In summary, the isolator parameters, particularly the damping coefficient and linear stiffness, are the primary factors influencing both relative displacement and absolute acceleration responses. Compared with the isolator, the limiter parameters have a relatively minor impact on the shock response. This is mainly because the isolator functions throughout the whole shock event, while the limiter is only activated when the relative displacement exceeds the limit clearance. Among the limiter parameters, the clearance exhibits a notable influence on the system response and shows an inverse relationship with the relative displacement and absolute acceleration, indicating its dual effect depending on the output metric. Therefore, in the vibration isolation system design of shipborne gas turbines, priority should be given to tuning the damping coefficient and stiffness of the isolator to enhance shock resistance. Subsequently, the limiter parameters can be optimized to further improve the system’s dynamic performance, ensuring robustness and reliability under severe shock loading conditions.

Despite the advantages demonstrated by the proposed AGPSO-CNN-BiLSTM-attention framework, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the method significantly reduces computational cost compared with full finite element simulations, it still requires a sufficiently large and representative training dataset. This may limit its efficiency when only scarce data are available or when sampling high-dimensional parameter spaces. Second, the deep neural network model employed functions as a “black-box,” which lacks explicit physical interpretability. While sensitivity indices help quantify parameter importance, the underlying feature extraction and decision process of the network remain opaque. Third, the predictive accuracy and robustness of the surrogate model are closely tied to the diversity and coverage of training samples. In cases of out-of-distribution inputs or highly nonlinear responses beyond the training range, model generalization may degrade. Finally, the current framework primarily focuses on structural dynamics of shipborne equipment under impact loading; its extension to multi-physics problems (e.g., fluid–structure or thermal-structural coupling) will require additional validation.

Future research will aim to mitigate these limitations by: (i) developing adaptive or active sampling strategies to reduce the sample burden; (ii) integrating physics-informed neural networks or hybrid surrogate models to enhance interpretability; (iii) introducing uncertainty quantification methods to better assess model prediction reliability; and (iv) validating the approach on broader marine engineering scenarios such as stochastic wave loading or coupled fluid–structure systems.

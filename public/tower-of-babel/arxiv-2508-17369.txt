Scaling limit of the discrete Gaussian free field with degenerate random conductances
Sebastian Andres, Martin Slowik, Anna-Lisa Sokol

Abstract
. We consider discrete Gaussian free fields with ergodic random conductances on a class of random subgraphs of ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , including i.i.d. supercritical percolation clusters, where the conductances are possibly unbounded but satisfy a moment condition. As our main result, we show that, for almost every realization of the environment, the rescaled field converges in law towards a continuum Gaussian free field. We also present a scaling limit for the covariances of the field. To obtain the latter, we establish a quenched local limit theorem for the Green’s function of the associated random walk among random conductances with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

1.
Introduction

The discrete Gaussian free field (DGFF), also known as the harmonic crystal, serves as a prime example within a class of models in modern statistical mechanics that describe, under a particular idealisation, an interface between two pure thermodynamic phases, see, for instance,
[
15
]
for a survey. It can be defined as the Gaussian random field whose covariances are given by the Green’s function of the discrete Laplace operator. In the present article we investigate the scaling behaviour of the DGFF with
impurities
modelled by random conductances. In a physical context, the DGFF with constant conductances may be interpreted as a deformation field representing the microscopic displacements of atoms in a crystal from their ideal position in a
homogeneous
crystal at non-zero temperature. Similarly, the microscopic structure of fluctuations in inhomogeneous crystals with impurities motivates to consider a version of the DGFF with random conductances, namely a centred Gaussian random field with covariances given by the Green’s function of the discrete Laplacian with random weights. To our knowledge this model first appeared in
[
19
]
, and was studied in
[
16
,
26
,
27
]
and more recently in
[
21
,
22
]
, see also
[
14
, Section 6]
. Here, we prove the convergence of the suitably rescaled field towards a continuum Gaussian free field and provide a scaling limit for the covariances of the field. We allow random environments in the form of ergodic and unbounded random conductances, only requiring a moment condition, on a class of random subgraphs of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, including both i.i.d. and certain correlated supercritical percolation clusters. The main technical input will come from some homogenization results of independent interest for the associated random walk in random environment, known in the literature as random conductance model (RCM), and its Green’s function, namely a certain extension of the quenched invariance principle for the random walk and a quenched local limit theorem for the Green’s function with Dirichlet boundary condition on a class of bounded domains.

1.1.
The model

We will study random Gaussian fields on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(or on more general random subgraphs of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

Next, we introduce the associated random walk in random environment, which is well-known under the label random conductance model (RCM). For any realization
\omega\in\Omega^{*}
consider the continuous-time Markov chain
X\equiv(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
with generator
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}
defined in (
1.4
). Notice that
X
is reversible with respect to the counting measure. When visiting a vertex
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
, the random walk
X
waits at
x
an exponential time with mean
1/\mu^{\omega}(x)
, where we define
\mu^{\omega}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{y\sim x}\omega(\{x,y\})
for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and then it jumps to a vertex
y\sim x
with probability
\omega(\{x,y\})/\mu^{\omega}(x)
. Since the law of the waiting time depends on the location of the walk, this walk is often called the variable speed walk (VSRW) in the literature. We denote by
\Prob^{\omega}_{x}
the quenched law of the process
X
starting at
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
, and the corresponding expectation by
\Mean_{x}^{\omega}
. Furthermore, we define the
heat kernel
as
p_{t}^{\omega}(x,y)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Prob_{x}^{\omega}[X_{t}=y]
for
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
t\geq 0
. Note that the heat kernel is invariant under space shifts, that is,

(1.7)

\displaystyle p_{t}^{\tau_{z}\omega}(x,y)\;=\;p_{t}^{\omega}(x+z,y+z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Throughout the paper, for any path
w\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and any subset
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define its first exit time from
\Lambda
by

\displaystyle\tau_{\Lambda}(w)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\inf\bigl\{t\geq 0:w_{t}\notin\Lambda\bigr\},

and we will suppress the dependence of
w
in the notation when it is clear from the context. Then, for any bounded
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the Green’s function ,
g_{\Lambda}^{\omega}(x,y)
, describes the expected amount of time that the VSRW
X
spends in
y
when starting in
x
before exiting
\Lambda
, that is,

\displaystyle g_{\Lambda}^{\omega}(x,y)\;=\;\Mean_{x}^{\omega}\biggl[\int_{0}^{\tau_{\Lambda}(X)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t}=y\}}\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}\Prob_{x}^{\omega}\bigl[X_{t}=y,\,t<\tau_{\Lambda}(X)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
x,y\in\Lambda\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
.

(Regular balls)

Let

Assumption 1.6

One of the most important results in the theory of random walks among random conductances is the

or

(QFCLT), which describes a weak convergence of the rescaled random walk towards Brownian motion. Throughout the paper, for any

\Sigma^{2}=\Sigma\cdot\Sigma^{T}

(or

if convenient), and we will then write

for the coordinate process. In particular,
\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[W_{t}\in\mathrm{d}y\bigr]=k_{t}^{\Sigma}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

with

\displaystyle k_{t}^{\Sigma}(x,y)\;=\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi t)^{d}\det\Sigma^{2}}}\exp\biggl(-\frac{(x-y)(\Sigma^{2})^{-1}(x-y)}{2t}\biggr).

Just as the Brownian motion can be realized as the scaling limit of many random curve ensembles, including simple random walks, the RCM (cf. Theorem
1.7
) and many others, as our first main result we will show that continuum Gaussian free fields emerge as the scaling limit of inhomogeneous discrete Gaussian free fields. However, the continuum Gaussian free fields cannot be defined pointwise, as the variance at any point would have to be infinite. Instead, they are defined as a restricted family of random distributions or generalised functions in the sense of Schwartz, taking values the space
H^{-s}(D)
for some
s>0
the definition of which we now recall. For any
s\geq 0
, let
H_{0}^{s}(D)
be the Sobolev space defined as the Banach space closure of

under the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H_{0}^{s}(D)}
induced by the scalar product

(1.11)

\displaystyle(f,g)_{H_{0}^{s}(D)}\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\int_{D}\bigl((1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\bigr)(x)\,\bigl((1-\Delta)^{s/2}g\bigr)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
(1-\Delta)^{s/2}f\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\bigl((1+|\cdot|_{2}^{2})^{s/2}(\mathcal{F}f)\bigr)
and
\mathcal{F}f
denotes Fourier transform of

. Let
H^{-s}(D)
be its dual space with respect to the

scalar product. For further details see, for instance,
[
36
, Chapter 5]
or
[
51
, Chapter 4]
.

Definition 1.10

(Continuum Gaussian free field (CGFF))
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain,
c_{\sigma}>0
and
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be a positive definite matrix. The
continuum Gaussian free field on
D
with parameters
c_{\sigma}
and
\Sigma
is an assignment

of a random variable to each bounded measurable

such that

(i)

\Phi^{\Sigma,D}
is a.s. linear, i.e. for all bounded measurable

and

,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\Sigma,D}(af+bg)\;=\;a\,\Phi^{\Sigma,D}(f)+b\,\Phi^{\Sigma,D}(g)\qquad\text{a.s.},

(ii)

for all bounded measurable
f\colon D\to\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\Sigma,D}(f)\overset{\mathrm{law}}{\;=\;}\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}(f)\bigr),

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}(f)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;c_{\sigma}\,\int_{D\times D}f(x)\,f(y)\,g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

We also extend this definition of the CGFF to let it act on functions in
H_{0}^{s}(D)
, for some
s>0
.

1.3.
Main ideas and methods

The proof of Theorem
1.11
has two technical main ingredients, namely an exit time estimate for the random walk
X
and an extension of the QFCLT in Theorem
1.7
to certain integral functionals for a class of initial distributions of
X
. To get the required exit time estimate we interpret the mean exit time from a ball as a solution of a Poisson problem with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then, we use a version of De Giorgi’s iteration scheme to show a maximal inequality for such equations in Proposition
2.2
to get an upper estimate on the mean exit time (see Corollary
2.3
below). We refer to
[
38
]
for a similar maximal inequality on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Another similar maximal inequality has been shown in
[
4
]
for the corrector equation in order to derive the sublinearity of the corrector by Moser iteration techniques. In analytic terms, the treatment of the Poisson problem for the mean exit times is less demanding due to the fact that the solutions have finite support, which allows us to work under weaker conditions. The established exit time estimates will be used to get the aforementioned extension of the QFCLT in Theorem
1.7
where the convergence in (
1.9
) is upgraded to hold also for functionals
F
on the Skorohod space of the form
F(w)=\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(w)}h(w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t
and for a general class of starting distributions of the random walk, see Theorem
3.2
below. Our assumption that the domain
D
is strongly regular is needed here to ensure the continuity of such functionals
F
. As a first step in our proof of Theorem
3.2
, we establish an extension of the QFCLT, still for continuous and bounded functionals
F
, for a class of initial measures for the random walk (see Theorem
3.1
) by using purely ergodic- and measure-theoretic arguments. Under stronger conditions, similar but different extensions of the QFCLT allowing for arbitrary starting points have been shown in
[
39
,
20
]
.

The local limit theorem for the Green’s function stated in Theorem
1.13
follows from the extended QFCLT in Theorem
3.2
together with a Hölder continuity estimate for
g^{\omega}_{nD}(\cdot,y)
, see Proposition
5.6
. The approach is analogous to the one to obtain a local limit theorem for the heat kernel established in
[
10
]
. Based on this idea, general criteria for such local limit theorems have been stated e.g. in
[
28
,
2
]
and recently in a quantitative form in
[
3
]
. In order to get the required Hölder continuity estimates for

we borrow the corresponding estimate for
\mathcal{L}^{\omega}
-harmonic functions on balls in
[
2
]
, exploiting the fact that the Green’s function is harmonic away from the diagonal. This is the reason why in Theorem
1.13
the supremum is not taken over points close to the diagonal.

1.4.

We finish this section by stating an open problem, namely to derive, in the setting of the present paper, the following convergence of the centred maximum of a two-dimensional inhomogeneous DGFF towards a randomly shifted Gumbel distribution.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section

2.
A maximum inequality for Poisson equations and applications

In this section we will derive a maximal inequality for Poisson equations on a more general class of graphs. As an application we shall obtain an upper bound for the mean exit time for the random walk.

\displaystyle\max_{z\in B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)}u(z)\;\leq\;h+C_{2}\Bigl(\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)$}}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)$}}\Bigr)^{\!\frac{\alpha_{*}}{2+\alpha_{*}}}\,\big\lVert(u-h)_{+}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle 2p_{*},B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)$}}^{2/(2+\alpha_{*})},

where
\alpha_{*}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\rho/(\rho-p_{*})
and
p_{*}=p/(p-1)
denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p
. In particular, there exists
C_{3}\equiv C_{3}(\theta,d,p,q)<\infty
such that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\max_{z\in B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)}u(z)\;\leq\;C_{3}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)$}}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)$}}.

Proof.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty]
be such that
1/p+1/q<2(1-\theta)/(d-\theta)
, and note that the function
(0,1)\ni\theta\mapsto 2(1-\theta)/(d-\theta)
is monoton decreasing. Further, let us choose
\theta^{\prime}\in(\theta,1)
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\frac{2(1-\theta^{\prime})}{d-\theta^{\prime}}\;=\;\frac{1-\theta}{d-\theta}+\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\biggr),

and set
\kappa_{1}\equiv\kappa_{1}(\theta,d,p,q)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\theta/(\theta^{\prime}-\theta)
. Recall the definition of
\rho
and
d^{\prime}
in (
2.5
). Then note that the choice of
\theta^{\prime}
in (
2.11
) ensures that
1/p+1/q<2/d^{\prime}
. Hence, defining
p_{*}
and
\alpha_{*}
as in the statement, we have that
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\rho/p_{*}>1
and therefore
\alpha_{*}<\infty
. Further, fix some
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
,
R\geq 1

x_{0}\in B^{\omega}(0,Rn)
and
n\geq N_{0}(\omega)\vee R^{\kappa_{1}}
. To simplify notation, we set
B\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=B^{\omega}(x_{0},n)
in the following argument.

Step 1.
Let
0\leq k<l<\infty
be fixed constants. Then, by Hölder’s inequality and the weighted Sobolev inequality as stated in (
2.4
), we first get

\displaystyle\big\lVert(u-l)_{+}^{2}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\big\lVert(u-l)_{+}^{2}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\alpha p_{*},B$}}\,\big\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{\{u\geq l\}}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\alpha_{*}p_{*},B$}}

\displaystyle\overset{\!\eqref{eq:Sobolev:weighted}\!}{\;\leq\;}C_{\mathrm{S}}\,n^{2}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B$}}\,\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\omega}\bigl((u-l)_{+},(u-l)_{+}\bigr)}{|B|}\,\big\lVert\mathbbm{1}_{\{u\geq l\}}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}^{1/\alpha_{*}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\left\langle(u-l)_{+},-\mathcal{L}^{\omega}u\right\rangle_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\ell^{\raisebox{0.1507pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle 2$}}(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega))$}}

\displaystyle\overset{\!\eqref{eq:Poisson1}\!}{\;=\;}\left\langle(u-l)_{+},f/n^{2}\right\rangle_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\ell^{\raisebox{0.1507pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle 2$}}(B)$}}\;\leq\;\frac{|B|}{n^{2}}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B$}}\,\big\lVert(u-l)_{+}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}.

Since
\bigl(\nabla(u-l)_{+}\bigr)(e)^{2}\leq\bigl(\nabla(u-l)_{+}\bigr)(e)(\nabla u)(e)
for any
e\in E(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega))
, we further obtain that
\mathcal{E}^{\omega}\bigl((u-l)_{+},(u-l)_{+}\bigr)\leq\left\langle(u-l)_{+},-\mathcal{L}^{\omega}u\right\rangle_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\ell^{\raisebox{0.1507pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle 2$}}(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega))$}}
. Thus,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\omega}\bigl((u-l)_{+},(u-l)_{+}\bigr)}{|B|}\;\leq\;n^{-2}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B$}}\,\big\lVert(u-l)_{+}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}.

By combining the estimates above and using Markov’s inequality, we conclude that

\displaystyle\big\lVert(u-l)_{+}^{2}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;\frac{C_{\mathrm{S}}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B$}}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B$}}}{(l-k)^{1+2/\alpha_{*}}}\,\big\lVert(u-k)_{+}^{2}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}^{1+1/\alpha_{*}}.

By setting
\varphi(l)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\big\lVert(u-l)_{+}^{2}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}
and
M\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=C_{\mathrm{S}}\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B$}}\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B$}}
we can rewrite the last inequality as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\varphi(l)\;\leq\;\frac{M}{(l-k)^{1+2/\alpha_{*}}}\,\varphi(k)^{1+1/\alpha_{*}}.

Step 2.
For any
h\geq 0
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
define

\displaystyle k_{j}\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;h+K(1-2^{-j}),

with
K\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=2^{1+\alpha_{*}}M^{\alpha_{*}/(2+\alpha_{*})}\varphi(h)^{1/(2+\alpha_{*})}
. We claim that

(2.14)

\displaystyle\varphi(k_{j})\leq\frac{\varphi(h)}{r^{j}},\qquad\forall\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where

. Indeed, for

the assertion is immediate. Further, suppose that (
2.14
) holds for any

. Then, in view of (
2.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(k_{j+1})

\displaystyle\;\leq\;M\,\biggl(\frac{2^{j+1}}{K}\biggr)^{\!\!1+2/\alpha_{*}}\,\varphi(k_{j})\;\leq\;M\,\biggl(\frac{2^{j+1}}{K}\biggr)^{\!\!1+2/\alpha_{*}}\,\biggl(\frac{\varphi(h)}{r^{j}}\biggr)^{\!\!1+1/\alpha_{*}}\;\leq\;\frac{\varphi(h)}{r^{j+1}},

which completes the proof of the claim (
2.14
). Thus, by letting
j\to\infty
, we get that
\varphi(h+K)=0
which is equivalent to
\max_{x\in B}u(x)\leq h+K
. Hence, the assertion (
2.9
) follows directly from the definition of
K
with
C_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=2^{1+\alpha_{*}}C_{\mathrm{S}}^{\alpha_{*}/(2+\alpha_{*})}
.

Step 3.
It remains to show (
2.10
). But, by using Jensen’s inequality, the weighted Sobolev inequality as stated in Proposition
2.1
and (
2.12
) with
l=0
, we get

\displaystyle\big\lVert(u)_{+}^{2}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}

\displaystyle\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{S}}\,n^{2}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B$}}\,\frac{\mathcal{E}^{\omega}\bigl((u)_{+},(u)_{+}\bigr)}{|B|}

\displaystyle\overset{\mspace{-8mu}\eqref{eq:estimate:DF}\mspace{-8mu}}{\;\leq\;}C_{\mathrm{S}}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B$}}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B$}}\,\big\lVert(u)_{+}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p_{*},B$}}\;\leq\;C_{\mathrm{S}}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B$}}\,\big\lVert f\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle p,B$}}\,\max_{x\in B}u(x).

By combining this estimate with (
2.9
) for
h=0
, (
2.10
) follows.
∎

As an application of this proposition we get an upper bound on the mean exit time for

from

3.
A central limit theorem for integral functionals

3.1.
A quenched invariance principle for a class of initial measures

In this subsection we extend the QFCLT as stated in Theorem
1.7
to a certain class of initial (signed) measures. Recall the notation
X^{n}=\bigl(n^{-1}X_{tn^{2}}\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
, and suppose that there exist
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
such that Assumptions
1.1
,
1.4
-(i) and
1.6
hold. Further, let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function with bounded support. Then,
\prob_{0}
-a.s., for any
T>0
and every bounded continuous function
F\colon D\bigl([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d}\bigr)\to\mathbb{R}
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F(X^{n})\bigr]\;=\;\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(W)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\bigl(W,(\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigr)
denotes a Brownian motion with non-degenerate, deterministic covariance matrix
\Sigma^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Sigma\cdot\Sigma^{\mathrm{T}}
as appearing in Theorem
1.7
. In particular, the null set does not depend on
f
.

Before we prove Theorem
3.1
we recall briefly the Skorokhod
J_{1}
-topology with respect to which the spaces
D([0,T],\mathbb{R^{d}})
,
T>0
, are Polish. For this purpose, let
\Lambda_{T}
be the set of all continuous functions
\lambda\colon[0,T]\to[0,T]
that are strictly increasing with
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\lambda_{T}=T
. Further, set

\displaystyle\vvvert\lambda\vvvert_{[0,T]}\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T]\\
s<t\end{subarray}}\;\biggl|\log\frac{\lambda_{t}-\lambda_{s}}{t-s}\biggr|

and define for any
w,w^{\prime}\in D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle d_{[0,T]}(w,w^{\prime})\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{T}}\Bigl(\vvvert\lambda\vvvert_{[0,T]}\vee\bigl\|w\circ\lambda-w^{\prime}\bigr\|_{[0,T]}\Bigr),

where
\|w\|_{[0,T]}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|w_{t}|_{2}
. Then, for each
T>0
, it holds that
d_{[0,T]}
is a metric and
D([0,T],\mathbb{R^{d}})
equipped with
d_{[0,T]}
is complete and separable, see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 12.2]
. A complete metric on
D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be defined in terms of the metrics
d_{[0,T]}
,
T>0
, where one needs to consider transformations of
w|_{[0,T]}
and
w^{\prime}|_{[0,T]}
that are continuous at
T
. Alternatively, a metrisable topology for which
D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Polish space can also be described as follows. A sequence
(w^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
converges to some
w\in D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if and only if, there exists a sequence
(\lambda^{n})\subset\Lambda_{\infty}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,\infty)}\bigl|\lambda_{s}^{n}-s\bigr|\;=\;0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl\|w^{n}\circ\lambda^{n}-w\bigr\|_{[0,T]}\;=\;0,\quad\forall\,T\in\mathbb{N},

where
\Lambda_{\infty}
denotes the set of all continuous functions from
[0,\infty)
onto itself that are strictly increasing with
\lambda_{0}=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\lambda_{t}=\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
T>0
and
F\colon D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded continuous function. First, notice that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{0}^{*}
and
z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
, the law of
X
under
\Prob_{z}^{\omega}
coincides with the law of
z+X
under
\Prob_{0}^{\tau_{z}\omega}
. This together with the spatial homogeneity of the limiting Brownian motion
W
implies that

\displaystyle\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F(X^{n})\bigr]\;=\;\Mean_{0}^{\tau_{z}\omega}\bigl[F(z/n+X^{n})\bigr]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(W)\bigr]\;=\;\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(x+W)\bigr],

for any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, we need to show that,
\prob_{0}
-a.s.,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\Mean_{0}^{\tau_{z}\omega}\bigl[F(z/n+X^{n})\bigr]\;=\;\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(x+W)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x.

The proof we are going to present comprises two steps.

Step 1.

Let us additionally assume that
F
is uniformly continuous. Fix some arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists
\delta_{\varepsilon}>0
such that
|F(w)-F(w^{\prime})|<\varepsilon
for any
w,w^{\prime}\in D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
d_{[0,T]}(w,w^{\prime})<\delta_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\supp f\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is assumed to be bounded, there exist
K\equiv K(\delta_{\varepsilon})\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that, setting
Q_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x_{k}+(-\delta_{\varepsilon}/2,\delta_{\varepsilon}/2]^{d}
for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, we have that
Q_{k}\cap Q_{\ell}=\emptyset
for all
k,\ell\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
with
k\neq\ell
and
\supp f\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}Q_{k}
. Further, let
nQ_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:z/n\in Q_{k}\}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In what follows we choose
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough to ensure that
nQ_{k}\neq\emptyset
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
.

Then, by using both finite additivity and the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\biggl|\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\Mean_{0}^{\tau_{z}\omega}\bigl[F(z/n+X^{n})\bigr]-\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(x+W)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr|

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}\leq\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\;\biggl|\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in nQ_{k}}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\}}-\biggl(\int_{Q_{k}}\!f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr)\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\biggr|\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[|F(x_{k}+W)|\bigr]

\displaystyle\mspace{66mu}+\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in nQ_{k}}\mspace{-6mu}|f(z/n)|\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\}}\,\Bigl|\Mean_{0}^{\tau_{z}\omega}\bigl[F(x_{k}+X^{n})\bigr]-\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(x_{k}+W)\bigr]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\mspace{66mu}+\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in nQ_{k}}\mspace{-6mu}|f(z/n)|\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\}}\,\Mean_{0}^{\tau_{z}\omega}\bigl[\bigl|F(z/n+X^{n})-F(x_{k}+X^{n})\bigr|\bigr]

\displaystyle\mspace{66mu}+\;\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}\int_{Q_{k}}|f(x)|\,\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\bigl|F(x+W)-F(x_{k}+W)\bigr|\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}=\mathrel{\mathop{:}}\;I_{1}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)+I_{2}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)+I_{3}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)+I_{4}(\varepsilon).

Now, by applying the version of the ergodic theorem in
[
17
, Theorem 3]
, we first obtain that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{1}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)=0
for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
. Further, for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
and any realization of
X^{n}
and
W
, we get from the definition of
d_{[0,T]}
as given in (
3.2
) that

\displaystyle d_{[0,T]}\bigl(z/n+X^{n},x_{k}+X^{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;|z/n-x_{k}|_{2}\;<\;\delta_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\forall\,z\in nQ_{k},

\displaystyle d_{[0,T]}\bigl(x+W,x_{k}+W\bigr)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;|x-x_{k}|_{2}\;<\;\delta_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\forall\,x\in Q_{k}.

Since
F
is assumed to be uniformly continuous, it follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\bigl|F(z/n+X^{n})-F(x_{k}+X^{n})\bigr|\;<\;\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad\bigl|F(x+W)-F(x_{k}+W)\bigr|\;<\;\varepsilon

for any
z/n,x\in Q_{k}
. Hence, a further application of
[
17
, Theorem 3]
yields that,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.,
\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{3}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)\leq\varepsilon\mathbb{P}\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x
. Likewise, we obtain that
I_{4}(\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon\prob_{0}\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x
. These two estimates clearly imply that,
\prob_{0}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{3}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)\;=\;0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}I_{4}(\varepsilon)\;=\;0.

It remains to consider the term
I_{2}
. From Theorem
1.7
we know that, for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
,
\Mean_{0}^{\omega}\bigl[F(x_{k}+X^{n})\bigr]\to\Mean_{0}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(x_{k}+W)\bigr]
as
n\to\infty
for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
. By Egorov’s theorem, see e.g.
[
33
, Section V.14]
, for any
\eta>0
there exists a measurable
\Omega_{\eta}\subset\Omega
with
\prob_{0}\bigl[\Omega_{\eta}\bigr]\geq 1-\eta
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_{\eta}}\Bigl|\Mean_{0}^{\omega}\bigl[F(x_{k}+X^{n})\bigr]-\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(x_{k}+W)\bigr]\Bigr|\underset{n\to\infty}{\;\longrightarrow\;}0,\qquad\forall\,k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}.

Since, by
[
17
, Theorem 3]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in nQ_{k}}\mspace{-6mu}|f(z/n)|\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{z}\omega\in\Omega_{\eta}\}}

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\prob_{0}\bigl[\Omega_{\eta}\bigr]\;<\;\infty,

and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in nQ_{k}}\mspace{-6mu}|f(z/n)|\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\}}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{z}\omega\in\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\eta}\}}

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}=\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\prob_{0}\bigl[\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\eta}\bigr]\;\leq\;\eta\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x,

for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
, and
F
is assumed to be bounded by some
C<\infty
, say, we obtain that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{2}(n,\varepsilon,\omega)\;\leq\;2C\eta\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\;\underset{\eta\downarrow 0}{\rightarrow}\;0,\qquad\prob_{0}\text{-a.s.}

By combining the estimates above, the assertion (
3.3
) follows for any bounded, uniformly continuous functions
F\colon D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
.

Step 2.
It remains to remove the additional assumption that
F
is uniformly continuous. For this purpose, define a finite measure,
\mathfrak{m}
, and, for every
\omega\in\Omega
, a sequence of finite measures,
(\mathfrak{m}_{n}^{\omega})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\mathbb{P}\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\,\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}\circ\,W^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{n}^{\omega}\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\Prob_{z}^{\omega}\circ\,(X^{n})^{-1}.

Then, by
Step 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int F\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}_{n}^{\omega}\;=\;\int F\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

for any bounded, uniformly continuous
F\colon D([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\prob_{0}
-a.s.
\omega
. Thus, (
3.1
) is an immediate consequence of the Portemanteau theorem, cf.
[
35
, Satz VII.4.10]
or
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

3.2.
An extended quenched invariance principle

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, strongly regular domain. Suppose that there exist
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
such that Assumptions
1.1
,
1.4
and
1.6
hold. Then,
\prob_{0}
-a.s., for every bounded continuous function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with bounded support and every
g\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\biggl[\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(X^{n})}g\bigl(X_{t}^{n}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}=\;\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{D}f(x)\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\biggl[\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(W)}g\bigl(W_{t}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]\mathrm{d}x.

Before we prove Theorem
3.2
we first provide two preparatory lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open. Then, the map
\tau_{\bar{D}}\colon D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,\infty]
is upper semi-continuous, and the map
\tau_{D}\colon C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,\infty]
is lower semi-continuous. In particular,
\tau_{D}
is continuous at any
w\in C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which
\tau_{D}(w)=\tau_{\bar{D}}(w)<\infty
.

Proof.

We first show that the map
\tau_{\bar{D}}
on
D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
is upper semi-continuous. For any given
w\in D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
(w^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
that converges to
w
. Since the upper semi-continuity of
\tau_{\bar{D}}
at
w
is trivially satisfied if
\tau_{\bar{D}}(w)=\infty
, we may assume that
t_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tau_{\bar{D}}(w)<\infty
. Then, the right-continuity of
w
implies that
w_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}\in\bar{D}^{\mathrm{c}}
for any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\bar{D}^{\mathrm{c}}
is open, there exists
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon]
such that the open ball,
B_{\delta}(w_{t_{0}+\varepsilon})
, with centre
w_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}
and radius
\delta
is contained in
\bar{D}^{\mathrm{c}}
. In view of the characterisation of convergence in
D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists
(\lambda^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Lambda_{\infty}
and
N_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for any
n\geq N_{\delta}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|\lambda_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}^{n}-(t_{0}+\varepsilon)\bigr|\;\leq\;\frac{\delta}{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\bigl|w^{n}\circ\lambda_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}^{n}-w_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}\bigr|_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\delta}{2}.

As a consequence,
w^{n}\circ\lambda_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}^{n}\in B_{\delta}(w_{t_{0}+\varepsilon})\subset\bar{D}^{\mathrm{c}}
which implies that

\displaystyle\tau_{\bar{D}}(w^{n})\;\leq\;\lambda_{t_{0}+\varepsilon}^{n}\;\leq\;t_{0}+\varepsilon+\delta/2\;\leq\;\tau_{\bar{D}}(w)+2\varepsilon

for any
n\geq N_{\delta}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrarily chosen, the upper semi-continuity of
\tau_{\bar{D}}
at
w
follows.

In order to prove the lower semi-continuity of the map
\tau_{D}
on
C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
it suffices to show that, for any
T>0
, the sub-level sets

\displaystyle A_{T}\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\bigl\{w\in C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d}):\tau_{D}(w)\leq T\bigr\}

are closed. For this purpose, let
(w^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
A_{T}
that converges to some
w\in C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Set
t_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tau_{D}(w^{n})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
D^{\mathrm{c}}
is closed,
\tau_{D}(w^{n})\leq T
and
w^{n}
is right-continuous, we have that
w_{t_{n}}^{n}\in D^{\mathrm{c}}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Further, as
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,T]
, there exists a sub-sequence
(t_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges to some
t\in[0,T]
. By continuity of
w
, it follows that

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}\bigl|w_{t}-w_{t_{n_{k}}}^{n_{k}}\bigr|_{2}\;\leq\;\limsup_{k\to\infty}\Bigl(\bigl|w_{t}-w_{t_{n_{k}}}\bigr|_{2}+\bigl\|w-w^{n_{k}}\bigr\|_{[0,\infty)}\Bigr)\;=\;0.

Thus,
w_{t}=\lim_{k\to\infty}w_{t_{n_{k}}}^{n_{k}}\in D^{\mathrm{c}}
which implies that
\tau_{D}(w)\leq t
and, hence,
w\in A_{T}
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, strongly regular domain. Further, let
((W_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}})
be a Brownian motion on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-degenerate covariance matrix
\Sigma^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Sigma\cdot\Sigma^{\mathrm{T}}
. Then,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{D}(W)=\tau_{\bar{D}}(W)\bigr]\;=\;1,\qquad\forall\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

Clearly, the assertion is immediate for any
x\in\bar{D}^{\mathrm{c}}
. Further, observe that the strong regularity of
D
implies that
\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}[\tau_{D}(W)=\tau_{\bar{D}}(W)=0]=1
for any
x\in\partial D
. Thus, it remains to prove (
3.7
) for any
x\in D
. Since
D
is assumed to be bounded and
\Sigma^{2}
is non-degenerate, we know from
[
42
, Lemma 5.7.4]
that
\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}[\tau_{D}(W)<\infty]=1
. Thus, by using the strong Markov property and the strong regularity of the subset
D
, we find that

\displaystyle\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{D}(W)=\tau_{\bar{D}}(W)\bigr]

\displaystyle\;=\;\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\Bigl[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{D}(W)<\infty\}}\Prob_{W_{\tau_{D}(W)}}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{\bar{D}}(W)=0\bigr]\Bigr]\;=\;1,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a strongly regular domain, and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded continuous function with bounded support, and
g\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
. To simplify notation we further define measurable functions
F
and
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
on the Skorokhod space
D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\displaystyle F(w)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(w)}g(w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{k}(w)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(w)\wedge k}g(w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Then,

\displaystyle\Biggl|\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F(X^{n})\bigr]-\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{D}f(x)\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(W)\bigr]\mathrm{d}x\Biggr|

\displaystyle\mspace{32mu}\leq\;\Biggl|\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f(z/n)\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F_{k}(X^{n})\bigr]-\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{D}f(x)\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F_{k}(W)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x\Biggr|

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}+\;\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}|f(z/n)|\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\biggl[\int_{\tau_{D}(X^{n})\wedge k}^{\tau_{D}(X^{n})}\bigl|g\bigl(X_{t}^{n}\bigr)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}+\;\int_{D}|f(x)|\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\biggl[\int_{\tau_{D}(W)\wedge k}^{\tau_{D}(W)}\bigl|g\bigl(W_{t}\bigr)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\mspace{32mu}=\mathrel{\mathop{:}}\;I_{1}(n,k,\omega)+I_{2}(n,k,\omega)+I_{3}(k).

We start with proving that, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
, the term
I_{1}(n,k,\omega)
vanishes in the limit as
n\to\infty
. Clearly, due to the truncation of the integral in the definition of
F_{k}
at level
k\in\mathbb{N}
and the fact that
g
is continuous and
D
is bounded, it follows that
|F_{k}(w)|\leq k\max_{x\in\bar{D}}|g(x)|<\infty
for any
w\in D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, in view of Lemma
3.3
and the continuity of
g
, it follows that the map
F_{k}
is continuous for any
w\in C([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\tau_{D}(w)=\tau_{\bar{D}}(w)
. By Lemma
3.4
we therefore get that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\Prob_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\bigl\{F_{k}\text{ is not continuous at }W\bigr\}\bigr]\;=\;0,\qquad\forall\,x\in D,

as a consequence of the Portemanteau theorem, cf.
[
13
, Theorem 2.7]
. Note that
\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F(W)\bigr]=0
for all
x\in D^{\mathrm{c}}
. Then, the weak convergence established in Theorem
3.1
together with (
3.8
) implies that,
\prob_{0}
-a.s.,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{1}(n,k,\omega)\;=\;0,\qquad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular, the null set does not depend on
f
and
g
.

Next, let us address the term
I_{2}
. Since
g
is continuous and
D
is bounded, we get that, for any
k,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
,

\displaystyle I_{2}(n,k,\omega)\;\leq\;\biggl(\max_{x\in\bar{D}}|g(x)|\,\biggr)\,\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}|f(z/n)|\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\bigl(\tau_{D}(X^{n})-k\bigr)\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{D}(X^{n})>k\}}\bigr].

By Assumption
1.4
(cf. Remark
1.5
-(ii)), there exists
r_{D}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
and
n\geq\max\{N_{0}(\omega)/r_{D},1\}
, we have that
nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\subset B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)
. By using the Markov property, we get that, for any
z\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\bigl(\tau_{D}(X^{n})-k\bigr)\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{D}(X^{n})>k\}}\bigr]

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}=\;\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\Bigl[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{D}(X^{n})>k\}}\,\Mean_{X_{kn^{2}}}^{\omega}\bigl[\tau_{D}(X^{n})\bigr]\Bigr]\;\leq\;\frac{1}{k}\biggl(\max_{z\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\tau_{D}(X^{n})\bigr]\biggr)^{\!\!2}.

Further, by Corollary
2.3
we also know that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lfloor C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n\rfloor\geq N_{0}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\max_{z\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\!\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\tau_{D}(X^{n})\bigr]

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}\leq\;n^{-2}\mspace{-6mu}\max_{z\in B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)}\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\tau_{B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)}(X)\bigr]\;\leq\;C_{4}(C_{\mathrm{V}}r_{D})^{2}\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)$}}.

Since the volume regularity of
B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)
together with an application of the ergodic theorem yields that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)$}}\;\leq\;\frac{(2C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D})^{d}}{C_{\mathrm{V}}}\,\mean_{0}\bigl[\nu(0)^{q}\bigr]^{1/q},\qquad\prob\text{-a.s.}

we obtain that there exists
c\in(0,\infty)
such that,
\prob
-a.s, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{2}(n,k,\omega)\;\leq\;\frac{c}{k}\,\biggl(\max_{x\in\bar{D}}|g(x)|\,\biggr)\,\int_{D}|f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mean_{0}\bigl[\nu(0)^{q}\bigr]^{2/q}.

Thus,
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}I_{2}(n,k,\omega)=0
for
\prob
-a.e.
\omega
.

Finally, we address the term
I_{3}
. By
[
42
, Lemma 5.7.4]
we have
\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{D}(W)\bigr]<\infty
so that, for any

,

\displaystyle\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\biggl[\int_{\tau_{D}(W)\wedge k}^{\tau_{D}(W)}\bigl|g\bigl(W_{t}\bigr)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr]\;\leq\;\Bigl(\max_{x\in\bar{D}}|g(x)|\Bigr)\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{D}(W)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{D}(W)>k\}}\bigr]\;\searrow\;0

as

. Thus, an application of the monotone convergence theorem yields

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}I_{3}(k)\;\leq\;\biggl(\max_{x\in\bar{D}}|g(x)|\biggr)\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{D}|f(x)|\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{D}(W)\mathbbm{1}_{\{\tau_{D}(W)>k\}}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x\;=\;0,

which completes the proof.
∎

4.
Scaling limit of the inhomogeneous discrete Gaussian free field

In this section we will show Theorem
1.11
. First, we apply the extended version of the central limit theorem in Theorem
3.2
to show the convergence of the rescaled field integrated against suitable test functions, see Theorem
4.1
.
Afterwards, in order to conclude, we establish tightness in Proposition
4.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.11
,
for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
, under
\probPhi^{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)\underset{n\to\infty}{\overset{\text{law}}{\;\longrightarrow\;}}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{\Sigma}(f)),

for any bounded, measurable function
f\colon D\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}(f)
is defined as in (
1.13
).

Proof.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, strongly regular domain and
f\colon D\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded, measurable function, and recall that the linear functional
\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)
is defined as in (
1.12
). Since the random variable
\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)
is Gaussian with mean zero and variance

\displaystyle\varGFF^{\omega}\bigl[\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)\bigr]\;=\;n^{d-2}\int_{D\times D}f(y)\,f(y^{\prime})\,g^{\omega}_{nD}([ny],[ny^{\prime}])\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime},

the assertion follows once we have shown that,
\prob_{0}
-a.s.,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\varGFF^{\omega}\bigl[\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)\bigr]\;=\;\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}(f).

In order to show (
4.1
), we extend
f
to a function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}
that vanishes outside of
D
, also denoted by
f
with a slight abuse of notation, and we then define

\displaystyle f_{n}(x)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;n^{d}\,\int_{x+(-1/(2n),1/(2n)]^{d}}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then,

\displaystyle\varGFF^{\omega}\bigl[\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)\bigr]

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{n^{d+2}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f_{n}(z/n)\,g_{nD}^{\omega}(z,z^{\prime})\,f_{n}(z^{\prime}/n).

Since
f
is locally integrable an application of Lebesgue–Besicovitch’s Differentiation Theorem, cf.
[
37
, Theorem 1.32]
, yields that
f_{n}(x)\to f(x)
as

for Lebesgue-almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, by applying both Lusin’s and Egorov’s theorem, cf.
[
37
, Theorem 1.13, 1.14 and 1.16]
we have that, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}\subset D
and a continuous function
f_{\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{x\in K_{\varepsilon}}|f_{n}(x)-f(x)|\;=\;0,\quad f\;=\;f_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{on }K_{\varepsilon},\quad\text{and}\quad|D\setminus K_{\varepsilon}|\;<\;\varepsilon,

where, for any measurable
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we write
|A|
to denote the Lebesgue measure of the subset
A
. In particular, we may choose
f_{\varepsilon}
such that
\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
. To simplify notation further, we define

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}\colon D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R},\quad w\;\longmapsto\;F_{\varepsilon}(w)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(w)}f_{\varepsilon}(w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Recall that
g^{\omega}_{nD}(z,z^{\prime})=0
whenever

or
z^{\prime}\in(\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\cap nD)^{\mathrm{c}}
. Thus, after rescaling the time by

, we obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{d+2}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f_{\varepsilon}(z/n)\,g_{nD}^{\omega}(z,z^{\prime})\,f_{\varepsilon}(z^{\prime}/n)\;=\;\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f_{\varepsilon}(z/n)\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F_{\varepsilon}(X_{t}^{n})\bigr].

Since

is a reversible Markov process it follows that

for all

. By the same reasoning we also have that the Dirichlet Green kernel,

, is symmetric in its arguments. This yields the decomposition

\displaystyle\bigl|\varGFF^{\omega}\bigl[\Phi_{n}^{D}(f)\bigr]-\sigma_{\Sigma}^{2}(f)\bigr|

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}\leq\;\biggl|\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f_{\varepsilon}(z/n)\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F_{\varepsilon}(X^{n})\bigr]-\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\,\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F_{\varepsilon}(W)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x\biggr|

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}+\,\frac{1}{n^{d+2}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}\bigl|f_{n}(z/n)-f_{\varepsilon}(z/n)\bigr|\,g_{nD}^{\omega}(z,z^{\prime})\,\bigl(\bigl|f_{n}(z^{\prime}/n)\bigr|+\bigl|f_{\varepsilon}(z^{\prime}/n)\bigr|\bigr)

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}+\,\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{D\times D}\bigl|f(y)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr|\,g_{D}^{\Sigma}(y,y^{\prime})\,\bigl|f(y^{\prime})+f_{\varepsilon}(y^{\prime})\bigr|\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}=\mathrel{\mathop{:}}\;I_{1}(\omega,n,\varepsilon)+I_{2}(\omega,n,\varepsilon)+I_{3}(\varepsilon).

Now, by Theorem
3.2
, it follows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}I_{1}(\omega,n,\varepsilon)=0
for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
and any
\varepsilon>0
. Let us stress that the corresponding null set is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Next, we address the term
I_{2}
. Since
D
is assumed to be bounded, there exists
r_{D}\in(0,\infty)
such that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
,

. Thus, by Assumption
1.4
(cf. Remark
1.5
-(ii)) and Corollary
2.3
, we find that there exists
c\in(0,\infty)
such that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
and

,

\displaystyle\max_{z\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\tau_{D}(X^{n})\bigr]\overset{\mspace{-9mu}\eqref{eq:mean:exit:D}\mspace{-9mu}}{\;\leq\;}c\,\big\lVert\nu^{\omega}\big\rVert_{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle q,B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)$}}.

Since

and

\displaystyle I_{2}(\omega,n,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;2\|f\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}\bigl|f_{n}(z/n)-f_{\varepsilon}(z/n)\bigr|\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[\tau_{D}(X^{n})\bigr]\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{z\in nD\}}

Thus, in view of (

), an application of the the ergodic theorem and the volume regularity of

Finally, we address the term

. Again, in view of (

), we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{3}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\;\leq\;4\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\,\biggl(\sup_{x\in D}\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[\tau_{D}(W)\bigr]\biggr)\,|D\setminus K_{\varepsilon}|\;\leq\;c\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\,\varepsilon.

Thus,

Proposition 4.2

(Tightness)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, strongly regular domain. Suppose there exist
\theta\in(0,1)
such that Assumptions
1.1
and
1.4
hold. Moreover, assume that
\mean\bigl[\nu^{\omega}(0)^{d^{\prime}/2}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\}}\bigr]<\infty
with
d^{\prime}=(d-\theta)/(1-\theta)
. Then,
\prob_{0}
-a.s., the sequence
(\Phi_{n}^{D})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight in
H^{-s}(D)
for any
s>d/2
.

Proof.

Step 1.
In a first step, we show that, for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\meanPhi^{\omega}\Bigl[\|\Phi_{n}^{D}\|_{H^{-s}(D)}^{2}\Bigr]\;<\;\infty.

By
[

Now, we are able to show the required Hölder regularity estimate for

. Recall the definition of the set
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
in (
1.14
).

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain. Suppose there exist
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p,q\in[1,\infty]
such that Assumptions
1.1
,
1.4
and
1.6
hold. Then, exist
C_{5}\in(0,\infty)
and
\vartheta\in(0,1/2)
such that for any
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that
0<\varepsilon<\delta
and
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}\neq\emptyset
the following hold. For any
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon/8)
there exists a random variable
N_{3}
(depending on
d,p,q,\theta,\eta
) that is
\prob_{0}
-a.s. finite such that, for any
(x,y)\in K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
n\geq N_{3}
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\eta n)}\bigl|g^{\omega}_{nD}(z,\pi_{n}(y))-g^{\omega}_{nD}(z^{\prime},\pi_{n}(y))\bigr|\;\leq\;C_{5}\frac{\eta^{\varrho}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\,n^{2-d},

where
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\log\bar{\gamma}/\log\vartheta
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon,\delta>0
such that
0<\varepsilon<\delta
and
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}\neq\emptyset
, and choose
r_{D}\in(0,\infty)
such that
D\subset[-r_{D},r_{D}]^{d}
. Then, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
and
n\geq\max\{N_{0}(\omega)/r_{D},1\}
, we have that
nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\subset B^{\omega}(0,C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}n)
. The remaining proof comprises two steps.

Step 1.
For any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
set
\delta_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\varepsilon\vartheta^{k}/2
with
\vartheta
as Theorem
5.3
. Then, for any
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon/8)
there exists
k_{0}\equiv k_{0}(\eta)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\delta_{k_{0}+1}<\eta\leq\delta_{k_{0}}
. Further, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
x_{0},y_{0}\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
be such that
B^{\omega}(x_{0},\varepsilon n)\subset nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
and
y_{0}\not\in B^{\omega}(x_{0},\varepsilon n)
. This ensures that the function
z\mapsto g_{nD}^{\omega}(z,y_{0})
is positive and harmonic on
B^{\omega}(x_{0},\delta_{k}n)
for any
k\in\{0,\ldots,k_{0}\}
.

Now, let
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}\cap\Omega_{c}
and

\displaystyle n\;\geq\;\tilde{N}_{3}(\omega)\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\eta^{-1}\bigl(2^{8d}C_{\mathrm{W}}\bigl(N_{0}(\omega)\vee R^{\kappa_{1}}\bigr)\vee\max_{0\leq k\leq k_{0}}N_{2}(\omega,\delta_{k})\bigr)

with
R\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}/\eta
and
\kappa_{1}\equiv\kappa_{1}(\theta,d,p,q)\in(0,\infty)
as in Proposition
2.2
. Then, by Theorem
5.3
,

\displaystyle\osc\displaylimits_{B^{\omega}(x_{0},\delta_{k}n)}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\cdot,y_{0})\;\leq\;\bar{\gamma}\,\osc\displaylimits_{B^{\omega}(x_{0},\delta_{k-1}n)}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\cdot,y_{0}),\qquad\forall\,k\in\{1,\ldots k_{0}\},

and by iterating this we obtain

\displaystyle\osc\displaylimits_{B^{\omega}(x_{0},\delta_{k_{0}}n)}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\cdot,y_{0})\;\leq\;\bar{\gamma}^{k_{0}}\,\sup_{B^{\omega}(x_{0},\delta_{0}n)}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\cdot,y_{0}).

By the choice of
k_{0}
, we have
B^{\omega}(x_{0},\eta n)\subset B^{\omega}(x_{0},\delta_{k_{0}}n)
and
\bar{\gamma}^{k_{0}}\leq(\frac{8}{\varepsilon}\eta)^{\varrho}
. Hence,

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in B^{\omega}(x_{0},\eta n)}\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z,y_{0})-g_{nD}^{\omega}(z^{\prime}\!,y_{0})\bigr|\;\leq\;c\,\eta^{\varrho}\sup_{B^{\omega}(x_{0},\varepsilon n/2)}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\cdot,y_{0}).

Moreover, by applying Corollary
5.2
with the choice
\sigma^{\prime}=1/2
,
\sigma=1
and
R=K=C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D}/\varepsilon
, we get

(5.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{B^{\omega}(x_{0},\varepsilon n/2)}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\cdot,y_{0})\;\leq\;C_{6}\frac{(C_{\mathrm{d}}r_{D})^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\bigl(4\bar{\mu}\bar{\nu}\bigr)^{\kappa}\bar{\nu}(\varepsilon n)^{2-d}\;\leq\;\frac{c}{\varepsilon^{d}}\,n^{2-d}.

By combining (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) we obtain that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}\cap\Omega_{c}
and
n\geq\tilde{N}_{3}
,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in B^{\omega}(x_{0},\eta n)}\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z,y_{0})-g_{nD}^{\omega}(z^{\prime}\!,y_{0})\bigr|\;\leq\;c\,\frac{\eta^{\varrho}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\,n^{2-d}.

Step 2.
Set
r\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=(\delta-\varepsilon)\wedge\varepsilon/8
. Then, by applying Corollary
5.5
-(i) with
R=r_{D}
, we get that, for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
, every
n\geq N_{1}(\omega,r)
and any
(x,y)\in K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
,

\displaystyle|\pi_{n}(x)-nx|_{2}\;\leq\;rn\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\pi_{n}(y)-ny|_{2}\;\leq\;rn.

Hence,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(\pi_{n}(x),\partial(nD))\;\geq\;\operatorname{dist}(nx,\partial(nD))-|\pi_{n}(x)-nx|_{2}\;\geq\;\varepsilon n\;>\;0,

which implies that
\pi_{n}(x)\in nD
. Since, by construction,
\pi_{n}(x)\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
it follows that
\pi_{n}(x)\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
. In particular,

\displaystyle B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\varepsilon n/2)\;\subset\;nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega),\qquad\text{for }\prob_{0}\text{-a.e. }\omega\text{ and any }n\geq N_{1}(\omega,r).

Similarly we have that
\pi_{n}(y)\in nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
and
n\geq N_{1}(\omega,r)
. Since

\displaystyle d^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\pi_{n}(y))

\displaystyle\;\geq\;|\pi_{n}(x)-\pi_{n}(y)|_{2}

\displaystyle\;\geq\;|nx-ny|_{2}-|\pi_{n}(x)-nx|_{2}-|\pi_{n}(y)-ny|_{2}\;\geq\;\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon n,

it follows that
\pi_{n}(y)\in(nD\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega))\setminus B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\varepsilon n/2)
for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
and
n\geq N_{1}(\omega,r)
. Hence, for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
and any
n\geq N_{3}(\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\tilde{N}_{3}(\omega)\vee N_{1}(\omega,r)
, choosing
x_{0}=\pi_{n}(x)
,
y_{0}=\pi_{n}(y)
, we may apply
Step 1
(with
\varepsilon
replaced by
\varepsilon/2
) to obtain (
5.7
).
∎

5.3.
Proof of the local limit theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

First, fix any
\delta,\varepsilon>0
with
0<\varepsilon<\delta
such that
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}\neq\emptyset
. Further, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
set
C_{r}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=x+[-r,r]^{d}
. Recall that, for any
\omega\in\Omega^{*}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\pi_{n}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
the function that maps any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
to a closest point of
nx
in
\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
.

The proof is divided in two steps. We first show a pointwise limit and then, using a covering argument, we establish the full result.

Step 1.
Fix any
(x_{0},y_{0})\in K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
and
r\in(0,\varepsilon/(24C_{\mathrm{d}}))
. Further, choose
r_{D}\in(0,\infty)
such that
D\subset[-r_{D},r_{D}]^{d}
. Then,
C_{r}(x_{0})\subset D
and
C_{r}(y_{0})\subset D
. Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
be two non-negative functions with
\supp f_{1}\subset C_{r}(x_{0})
,
\supp f_{2}\subset C_{r}(y_{0})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
. We claim that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{d-2}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))\;=\;\frac{g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})}{\prob[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}]},\qquad\prob_{0}\text{-a.s.}

Define
M(n,r,\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=n^{-2d}\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}f_{1}(z_{1}/n)f_{2}(z_{2}/n)
and the measurable function
F_{2}\colon D([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d})\to[0,\infty)
by
w\mapsto F_{2}(w)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}(w)}f_{2}(w_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t
, and set

\displaystyle J(n,r,\omega)

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\frac{1}{n^{d}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-6mu}f_{1}(z/n)\,\Mean_{z}^{\omega}\bigl[F_{2}(X^{n})\bigr]-\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(x)\Mean_{x}^{\Sigma}\bigl[F_{2}(W)\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x

(5.12)

\displaystyle\mspace{10mu}=\;J_{1}(n,r,\omega)+J_{2}(n,r,\omega)+J_{3}(n,r,\omega)+J_{4}(r),

where

\displaystyle J_{1}(n,r,\omega)

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\frac{1}{n^{d+2}}\mspace{-6mu}\sum_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)}\mspace{-12mu}f_{1}(z_{1}/n)\,f_{2}(z_{2}/n)\,\Bigl(g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{1},z_{2})-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0})\bigr)\Bigr),

\displaystyle J_{2}(n,r,\omega)

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;M(n,r,\omega)\,\biggl(n^{d-2}\,g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-\frac{g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})}{\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]}\biggr),

\displaystyle J_{3}(n,r,\omega)

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\biggl(\frac{M(n,r,\omega)}{\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]}-\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\biggr)\,g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0}),

\displaystyle J_{4}(r)

\displaystyle\;\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\;\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(x)\,f_{2}(y)\bigl(g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x,y)-g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

By rearranging those terms we obtain that

\displaystyle\biggl|n^{d-2}\,g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-\frac{g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})}{\prob\bigl[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}\bigr]}\biggr|

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}\leq\;\frac{1}{M(n,r,\omega)}\,\bigl(|J(n,r,\omega)|+|J_{1}(n,r,\omega)|+|J_{3}(n,r,\omega)|+|J_{4}(r)|\bigr).

Thus, it suffices to show that the right-hand side vanishes when we first take the limit
n\to\infty
and then
r\downarrow 0
. First, by applying
[
17
, Theorem 3]
, it follows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}M(n,r,\omega)=\prob[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}]^{2}
for
\prob
-a.e.
\omega
. Together with Theorem
3.2
this implies that, for
\prob
-a.e.
\omega
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|J(n,r,\omega)|+|J_{3}(n,r,\omega)|}{M(n,r,\omega)}\;=\;0.

Moreover, by the uniform continuity of Green kernel
g_{D}^{\Sigma}
, it follows that, for
\prob
-a.e.
\omega
,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}|J_{4}(r)|/M(n,r,\omega)\;=\;\lim_{r\downarrow 0}|J_{4}(r)|/\prob[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}]^{2}\;=\;0.

It remains to consider the term
J_{1}
. Since
x_{0}\in[-r_{D},r_{D}]^{d}
, Corollary
5.5
-(i) implies that
|\pi_{n}(x_{0})-nx_{0}|<rn/2
for
\prob
-a.e.
\omega
and
n\geq N_{1}(\omega,r/2)
. Thus, by using Assumption
1.4
-(ii), we find that, for any
\omega\in\Omega_{\mathrm{reg}}\cap\Omega_{0}^{*}
and any
n\geq N_{0}(\omega)/r_{D}\vee N_{1}(\omega,r/2)
,

(5.13)

\displaystyle nC_{r}(x_{0})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\;\subset\;\pi_{n}(x_{0})+[-n3r/2,n3r/2]^{d}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\;\subset\;B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),3C_{\mathrm{d}}rn/2),

and, similarly,

(5.14)

\displaystyle nC_{r}(y_{0})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)\;\subset\;B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),3C_{\mathrm{d}}rn/2).

Further, by using the symmetry of the Green kernel with zero boundary condition, we obtain for any
z_{1}\in nC_{r}(x_{0})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
and
z_{2}\in nC_{r}(y_{0})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{1},z_{2})-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0})\bigr)\bigr|

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}\leq\;\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{2},z_{1})-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),z_{1})\bigr)\bigr|+\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{1},\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0})\bigr)\bigr|.

Thus, recalling that
z_{1}\in B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),3C_{\mathrm{d}}rn/2)
by (
5.13
), the Hölder continuity estimate in Proposition
5.6
immediately yields that, for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
and any
n\geq N_{3}(\omega)
,

(5.16)

\displaystyle\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{1},\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0})\bigr)\bigr|\;\leq\;C_{5}\frac{(3C_{d}r/2)^{\varrho}}{\varepsilon^{d}}n^{2-d}.

In order to estimate the first term on the right-hand side of (
5.3
), first note that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z_{1}\in nC_{r}(x_{0})
,

\displaystyle|z_{1}/n-y_{0}|_{2}\;\geq\;|x_{0}-y_{0}|_{2}-|z_{1}/n-x_{0}|_{2}\;\geq\;\varepsilon-\frac{r}{2}\;\geq\;\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{16C_{\mathrm{d}}}\;>\;\varepsilon/2,

and, since
\delta>\varepsilon
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}(z_{1}/n,\partial D)\;\geq\;\operatorname{dist}(x_{0},\partial D)\;\geq\;\delta-\frac{r}{2}\;\geq\;\delta-\frac{\varepsilon}{16C_{\mathrm{d}}}\;>\;\delta/2.

Hence,
(z_{1}/n,y_{0})\in K_{\varepsilon/2,\delta/2}
for all
z_{1}\in nC_{r}(x_{0})
. In particular, every
z_{1}\in nC_{r}(x_{0})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
is of the form
z_{1}=\pi_{n}(z_{1}^{\prime})
for some
z_{1}^{\prime}\in[-r_{D},r_{D}]^{d}
such that
(z_{1}^{\prime},y_{0})\in K_{\varepsilon/2,\delta/2}
. Therefore, recalling that
z_{2}\in B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),3C_{\mathrm{d}}rn/2)
by (
5.14
), by another application of Proposition
5.6
on
K_{\varepsilon/2,\delta/2}
, we obtain for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
, any
n\geq N_{3}(\omega)
and
z_{2}\in nC_{r}(y_{0})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\infty}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{2},z_{1})-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),z_{1}\bigr)\bigr|

(5.17)

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}=\;\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{2},\pi_{n}(z_{1}^{\prime}))-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),\pi_{n}(z_{1}^{\prime})\bigr)\bigr|\;\leq\;C_{5}\frac{(3C_{d}r/2)^{\varrho}}{(\varepsilon/2)^{d}}n^{2-d}.

By combining (
5.3
) and (
5.16
) with (
5.3
),

\displaystyle\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(z_{1},z_{2})-g_{nD}^{\omega}\bigl(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0})\bigr)\bigr|\;\leq\;c\,\frac{r^{\rho}}{\varepsilon^{d}}\,n^{2-d}.

Hence,
\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}|J_{1}(n,r,\omega)|\leq\limsup_{r\downarrow 0}cr^{\rho}\varepsilon^{-d}/\prob[0\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}]^{2}=0
for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
.

Step 2.
To extend the pointwise convergence shown in
Step 1
to uniform convergence in
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
we use a covering argument similar to one in
[
10
,
2
]
. The idea is to approximate
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
by a finite lattice on which the pointwise convergence is uniform and then to exploit Hölder regularity estimates to conclude the uniform convergence on
K_{\varepsilon,\delta}
.

For any
\eta\in(0,1)
, we set
K_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=K_{\varepsilon,\delta}\cap(\eta\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\eta\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. Clearly, the set
K_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}
is non-empty for any sufficiently small
\eta
. Furthermore, since the number of elements in
K_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}
is finite,
Step 1
implies that, for any
\epsilon_{1}>0
,
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
and
\eta\in(0,1)\cap\mathbb{Q}
, there exists
N_{4}(\omega,\epsilon_{1},\eta)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(5.18)

\displaystyle\sup_{(x_{0},y_{0})\in K_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}}\bigl|n^{d-2}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})\bigr|\;\leq\;\epsilon_{1},\qquad\forall\,n\geq N_{4}(\omega,\epsilon_{1},\eta).

Furthermore, for any
(x,y)\in K_{\varepsilon,\delta}\setminus K_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}
, we denote by
(x_{0},y_{0})
a closest point to
(x,y)
in
K_{\varepsilon,\delta,\eta}
. Then,

\displaystyle|x-x_{0}|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\eta\qquad\text{and}\qquad|y-y_{0}|_{\infty}\;\leq\;\eta.

Since
x,x_{0},y,y_{0}\in[-r_{D},r_{D}]^{d}
, by Corollary
5.5
-(i), for
\prob
-a.e.
\omega
and
n\geq N_{1}(\omega,\eta/2)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\{x,x_{0},y,y_{0}\}}|\pi_{n}(z)-nz|_{2}\;\leq\;\frac{\eta n}{2}.

Then, by the same arguments as in (
5.13
), we get that
\pi_{n}(x)\in B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),2C_{\mathrm{d}}n)
and
\pi_{n}(y)\in B^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),2C_{\mathrm{d}}n)
. Thus, by applying again Proposition
5.6
, we obtain for
\prob_{0}
-a.e.
\omega
, any
\eta\in(0,\varepsilon/(16C_{\mathrm{d}}))\cap\mathbb{Q}
and
n\geq N_{3}(\omega)
,

(5.19)

\displaystyle\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y),\pi_{n}(x))-g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),\pi_{n}(x))\bigr|\;\leq\;C_{5}\frac{(2C_{\mathrm{d}}\eta)^{\varrho}}{\varepsilon^{d}}n^{2-d},

and

(5.20)

\displaystyle\bigl|g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))\bigr|\;\leq\;C_{5}\frac{(2C_{\mathrm{d}}\eta)^{\varrho}}{\varepsilon^{d}}n^{2-d}.

By the symmetry of the
g_{nD}^{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\bigl|n^{d-2}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\pi_{n}(y))-g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x,y)\bigr|

\displaystyle\mspace{36mu}\leq\;\bigl|n^{d-2}\bigl(g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y),\pi_{n}(x))-g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(y_{0}),\pi_{n}(x))\bigr)\bigr|

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}+\bigl|n^{d-2}\ \bigl(g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))\bigr)\bigr|

\displaystyle\mspace{72mu}+\bigl|n^{d-2}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x_{0}),\pi_{n}(y_{0}))-g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})\bigr|+\bigl|g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x_{0},y_{0})-g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x,y)\bigr|.

By (
5.18
) the third term on the right hand side is smaller than
\epsilon_{1}
. Furthermore, by (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) for
\eta
small any enough the first and the second term are both smaller than
\epsilon_{1}
. The last term also is smaller than
\epsilon_{1}
for
\eta
sufficiently small by the uniform continuity of
g_{D}^{\Sigma}
. Thus, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\sup_{(x,y)\in K_{\varepsilon,\delta}}\bigl|n^{d-2}g_{nD}^{\omega}(\pi_{n}(x),\pi_{n}(y))-g_{D}^{\Sigma}(x,y)\bigr|\;\leq\;4\epsilon_{1},

which is the desired conclusion.

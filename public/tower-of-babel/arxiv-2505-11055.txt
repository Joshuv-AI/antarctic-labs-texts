Internal Effectful Forcing in System T
Martín H. Escardó, Bruno da Rocha Paiva, Vincent Rahli, Ayberk Tosun

Abstract
The effectful forcing technique allows one to show that the denotation of a closed System T term of type ( ι ⇒ ι ) ⇒ ι (\iota\Rightarrow\iota)\Rightarrow\iota in the set-theoretical model is a continuous function ( ℕ → ℕ ) → ℕ (\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N} . For this purpose, an alternative dialogue-tree semantics is defined and related to the set-theoretical semantics by a logical relation. In this paper, we apply effectful forcing to show that the dialogue tree of a System T term is itself System T-definable, using the Church encoding of trees.

Introduction

It is well known that the System T-definable functions

(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
are continuous and that, moreover, their
moduli of continuity are themselves
System T-definable
[
Troelstra:1973
]
. Effectful
forcing
[
mhe-effectful-forcing
]
was generalised by
Xu
[
xu:2020
]
to give an alternative proof of this fact. Effectful
forcing gives a dialogue-tree semantics, and relates it to the
set-theoretical semantics by a logical relation. In this paper, we
strengthen this by showing that the dialogue trees are themselves
System T-definable, using Church encoding. Dialogue trees are
equivalent variants of Brouwer
trees
[
Escardo+Oliva:diag2brouwer:2017
,
Sterling:jfp:2021
]

From a constructive point of view, dialogue trees give more
information than moduli of continuity. Given a dialogue tree, it is
possible to derive a modulus of continuity, but the converse is only
known to be possible in the presence of additional assumptions. For
example, Ghani, Hancock and
Pattison
[
Ghani+Hancock+Pattinson:lmcs:2009
]
show that if a
function doesn’t have a Brouwer tree, then it isn’t continuous, using
dependent choice, while Capretta and
Uustalu
[
Capretta+Uustalu:fossacs:2016
]
use the assumption of bar
induction to show that every function with a
stable
modulus of
continuity has a Brouwer tree. Our result does not assume dependent
choice or bar induction, and, moreover, establishes the

System T-definability
of a dialogue tree of any closed term
of type
(\iota\Rightarrow\iota)\Rightarrow\iota
.

Related work.

Continuity is a key concept in mathematics, and in particular in constructive
mathematics where it is often accepted that all real-valued functions on the
unit interval are uniformly continuous.
Prominent work in the area was done by Brouwer, who gave the first argument for
the previous result, relying on his
continuity principle for numbers
,
which states that all functions on the Baire space are continuous, as well as
the Fan Theorem
[
Troelstra+VanDalen:1988
,
Dummett:1977
]
.
Brouwer’s continuity principle said that the values of a function

F:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
on the Baire
space could only rely on a finite amount of its inputs.
More precisely,
F
was said to be continuous if for all

\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, there existed some

n:\mathbb{N}
such that for any inputs

\beta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
which agreed on the first
n

entries with
\alpha
we would have
F(\alpha)=F(\beta)
.
When the value of
n
could be picked independently of
\alpha
, then

F
was said to be uniformly continuous.
Since then, more fine-grained ways of capturing continuity information have been
devised, such as using trees to keep track of the specific entries of
\alpha

that
F(\alpha)
might depend on.
These ideas were used by Kleene in his study of recursive functions of
higher types
[
Kleene:1978
]
, by Brouwer in his study of his bar
theorem
[
Troelstra+VanDalen:1988
]
, and many others, including
[
Ghani+Hancock+Pattinson:cmcs:2006
,
Ghani+Hancock+Pattinson:lmcs:2009
,
Ghani+Hancock+Pattinson:mfps:2009
,
mhe-effectful-forcing
,
Capretta+Uustalu:fossacs:2016
,
Sterling:jfp:2021
,
Baillon+Mahboubi+Pedrot:csl:2022
,
Cohen+Paiva+Rahli+Tosun:mfcs:2023
,
Baillon:phd:2023
]
.

Starting with Troelstra’s work
[
Troelstra:1973
, p.158]
for

\text{N-HA}^{\omega}
, the definable functions of various other systems have been shown to be continuous.
Among these we have:
System T
[
mhe-effectful-forcing
]
,
MLTT
[
Coquand+Jaber:2010
,
Coquand+Jaber:2012
,
Xu:phd:2015
]
,
CTT
[
Rahli+Bickford:cpp:2016
]
,
BTT
[
Baillon+Mahboubi+Pedrot:csl:2022
]
, and

\text{TT}^{\Box}_{\mathcal{C}}

[
Cohen+Paiva+Rahli+Tosun:mfcs:2023
]
.
However, the existence of a modulus of continuity operator tends to
be inconsistent with function extensionality as first shown
for
\text{HA}_{\mathit{NB}}^{\omega}
by Kreisel
[
Kreisel:1962
, p.154]

and later
for
\text{N-HA}^{\omega}
by Troelstra
[

.
In the case of MLTT we don’t even need function extensionality
to get an inconsistency as shown by
Escardó and Xu
[
Escardo+Xu:2015
,
Xu:phd:2015
]
.
Further related work is discussed in

Main contributions.

(1) We strengthen the above work to show that not only the modulus of
continuity of a System T-definable function is itself
System T-definable, but also its dialogue tree is
System T-definable, where we implemented trees in System T using
Church encoding.
(2) We prove the
correctness of this translation with a logical relation.
(3) We show how to
compute moduli of continuity and uniform continuity internally, and prove the
correctness of this construction.
(4) The original presentation of effectful forcing
[
mhe-effectful-forcing
]
extends System T with an oracle, and here we show that this extension is not necessary and we can work directly with System T without oracles.

3
Oracle-less Effectful Forcing

The original presentation of effectful forcing
[
mhe-effectful-forcing
]

works with System T extended with an oracle. Here we show that this extension
is not necessary, and we can work directly with System T.

3.1
Dialogue Trees and Continuity

Effectful forcing starts by noting that a function

f:(I\to O)\to X
can be thought of as an effectful term

t_{f}:X
with access to an oracle
\alpha:I\to O
. The use of
an oracle can be seen as an algebraic effect with the operation

\texttt{question}_{X}\;(i:I)\;(k:O\to X):X
, which prompts the oracle
with question
i
and passes the oracle’s answer to the continuation
k

eventually producing an element of
X
. Looking for the monad corresponding to
oracle computations we find dialogue trees.

Definition 5

(
)
.

The inductive type
\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(I)(O)(X)
of
(I,O,X)
-dialogue
trees
is defined by

\frac{x:X}{\eta(x):\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(I)(O)(X)}\par\frac{\phi:O\to\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(I)(O)(X)\quad i:I}{\beta(\phi)(i):\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(I)(O)(X)}

In the case where the oracle input and output are
\mathbb{N}
, we write

\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathcal{D}}(X)
for
\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(\mathbb{N})(\mathbb{N})(X)
.

Justifying this view, each
(I,O,X)
-dialogue tree encodes a function of
type
(I\to O)\to X
.

\displaystyle\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue_operator]{\mathsf{dialogue}}(\eta(x))(\alpha)\vcentcolon\equiv x

\displaystyle\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue_operator]{\mathsf{dialogue}}(\beta(\phi)(i))(\alpha)\vcentcolon\equiv\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue_operator]{\mathsf{dialogue}}(\phi(\alpha(i)))(\alpha)

Definition 7

(
)
.

Given a function
f:(I\to O)\to X

we say it is:

•

dialogue continuous
if

\exists\,d:\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(I)(O)(X),\forall\,\alpha:I\to O,{f(\alpha)}={\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue_operator]{\mathsf{dialogue}}(d)(\alpha)}

When the oracle input is

, so we have

•

if

\forall\,\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to O,\exists\,n:\mathbb{N},\forall\,\beta:\mathbb{N}\to O,\alpha=_{n}\beta\to{f(\alpha)}={f(\beta)}

•

if

\exists\,n:\mathbb{N},\forall\,\alpha,\beta:\mathbb{N}\to O,\alpha=_{n}\beta\to{f(\alpha)}={f(\beta)}

where we have used

\alpha

.

Remark 8
.

Due to their inductive nature, any path along a dialogue tree must reach a
leaf node in a finite number of steps and hence query the oracle a finite
number of times. This means that given a dialogue tree

d:\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue-trees]{\mathsf{Dial}}(\mathbb{N})(O)(X)
, the function

\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue_operator]{\mathsf{dialogue}}(d):(\mathbb{N}\to O)\to X
must be
continuous. Further assuming that the type
O
is finite, for example in the
case of
\mathbb{B}
, then the dialogue tree
d
can even be fully searched for
the largest query over all paths, in which case
\hyperref@@ii[def:dialogue_operator]{\mathsf{dialogue}}(d)

will also be uniformly continuous.

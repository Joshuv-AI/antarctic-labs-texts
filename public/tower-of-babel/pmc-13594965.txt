MATR3 promotes proliferation and invasion of colon adenocarcinoma cells: bulk, single-cell transcriptomic analysis and experimental validation
Authors: Chengkai Huang, Xiaohui Jiang, Wenbo Huang, Yang Wang, Dongxu Lan, Kai Gao, Mingyang Zhong, Maosong Zhou, Ding Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•MATR3 is significantly upregulated in colorectal cancer tissues and is associated with poor prognosis in patients.•Knocking out MATR3 significantly inhibits the proliferation, migration, and invasion ability of colorectal cancer cells.•Animal experiments have confirmed that knocking out MATR3 can significantly inhibit tumor growth in vivo.

Introduction

As the third most prevalent cancer globally, colorectal cancer (CRC) is likewise the second-highest reason for deaths associated with cancer [[1], [2], [3]]. Based on worldwide statistics regarding cancer in 2022, its incidence rate and mortality have remained high, especially among people under 50 years old [1,4]. Colonic adenocarcinoma (COAD) is the most common histological subtype of CRC. Although significant progress has been made in treatment strategies such as chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, many patients still have not benefited from these therapies [[5], [6], [7]]. The poor prognosis of COAD patients is mainly attributed with detection delay, constrained sensitivity with regard to existing diagnostic tools, and heterogeneity of the tumor, which complicate early intervention and limit treatment effectiveness [8,9]. A deep understanding of those attributions has become crucial toward initial detection improvement as well as the development of tailored treatment methods, highlighting the urgent need for further technological breakthroughs and innovations in COAD management.

Previous studies have shown that changes in nuclear matrix components can lead to alterations in nuclear structure, which is considered one of the markers of malignant transformation [10,11]. In addition, nuclear matrix proteins (NMPs) are involved in multiple processes such as DNA organization and replication, RNA synthesis, cell cycle regulation, transcription, RNA processing, and DNA repair [12]. In recent years, there are still many research gaps regarding the role of NMPs in carcinogenesis and tumor prognosis. MATR3 is one of the NMP, a DNA/RNA binding protein associated with frontotemporal lobe degeneration, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, distal myopathy, and other neurodegenerative diseases [13,14]. It is widely involved in biological processes such as DNA repair, nuclear retention of highly edited RNA, RNA splicing, transcription, and chromatin structure regulation [[15], [16], [17], [18]]. In oral squamous cell carcinoma, it can be inhibited by Licochalcone H, thereby reducing the survival rate and colony forming ability of HSC2 and HSC3 cells, and causing apoptosis and cell cycle arrest [19]. According to academic reports, knocking down MATR3 can inhibit the proliferation of malignant melanoma cells, cause cell cycle arrest, and induce apoptosis [20]. In addition, MATR3 participates in the progression of neuroblastoma by interacting with long non-coding RNAs [21]. However, its role and prognostic value in human COAD are still unclear.

The deep integration of high-throughput sequencing and bioinformatics has brought revolutionary changes to the exploration of new cancer targets. High-throughput technology, represented by next-generation sequencing, can generate multi-level data such as tumor genome, epigenome, transcriptome, and proteome on a large scale, thus systematically revealing the molecular characteristics and heterogeneity of cancer in a panoramic manner, thereby providing unprecedented data foundation for discovering key genes and pathways that drive tumor occurrence and development. Bioinformatics extracts meaningful biological insights from massive and complex data by developing and applying advanced computational tools and statistical models, such as differential expression analysis, pathway enrichment, and protein network construction. Candidate genes are placed in specific functional backgrounds for mechanism analysis, thereby verifying their potential as therapeutic targets. These technological advancements provide new opportunities for studying tumor molecular markers and the cellular functions of specific genes, as well as revealing their molecular mechanisms [22].

Therefore, this study integrated data from single-cell RNA sequencing and multi-omics in order to systematically explore MATR3’s prognostic value, biological functions, and patterns of expression with respect to COAD. Furthermore, it analyzed how the expression levels of MATR3 correlated with prognosis of patients, delving into how MATR3 related to key oncogenic signaling pathways and immune cell infiltration. It revealed MATR3’s molecular mechanism concerning COAD progression and occurrence, while also validating MATR3’s capability as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker. These works not only deepen the understanding of the mechanism by which MATR3 drives COAD progression, but also provide new theoretical basis and experimental support for future immunotherapy strategies targeting MATR3 and other related targets.

Methodology

Collection and preprocessing of data

From the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database, the research downloaded the profile of gene expressions as well as corresponding TCGA-COAD clinical data. RNA seq data was included in this dataset, in which clinical data comprised demographic data of patients, tumor staging, and survival outcomes. The single-cell dataset (GSE139555) was obtained from the GEO database and contained two normal and two COAD samples.

Single-cell data analysis

Seurat package (version 4.1.3) in R was used to process scRNA seq data. First, the following filtering criteria were used for constructing a Seurat object and preprocessing the data: removing cells that had RNA features above 5000 or below 200, as well as those that had above 10% expressions of mitochondrial genes. NormalizeData function was used to standardize the data, and then ScaleData function was used for standardization. Then, RunPCA function was utilized for principal component analysis (PCA), selecting the top ten principal components for reducing dimensionality of the data. To address the batch processing effect, Harmony algorithm was applied to integrate batch processing. RunUMAP function was utilized for further dimensionality reduction visualization. UMAP results determined 12 different clusters of cells. For an accurate annotation of the types of cells in COAD’s TME, this study used SingleR, then validated each determined cell type’s marker genes using FindAllMarkers functionality. Finally, we identified eight major types of cells were determined in COAD’s TME.

Cytotrace analysis

CytoTracy software package was used for an evaluation of various cell subpopulations’ status and differentiation potential [23]. Compared to conventional approaches for analyzing lineage trajectory, it applies single-cell transcriptome data for a reconstruction of cells’ developmental trajectory, making it crucial to comprehend cells’ differentiation pathways as they develop. First, using the CytoTrace function, each cell’s dryness was calculated to determine the cell’s state of undifferentiation. There is more proximity with early to no differentiation if the dryness score is higher.

Construction of co-expression network

The purpose of GeneMANIA platform (http://www.genemania.org) is obtaining MATR3 protein-related interaction data, making it crucial to predict the MATR3 gene’s function, develop functional hypotheses, as well as determine genes that have similar biological functions. this research analyzed the characteristics of MATR3 from multiple dimensions through PPI network characterization.

Analysis of functional enrichment

One commonly used approach to conduct functional enrichment studies on a large scale, such as Molecular Functions (MF), Cellular Components (CC), and Biological Processes (BP), is Gene Ontology (GO) analysis. On the other hand, as an extensively used database, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) stores data regarding biological pathways, genomes, drugs, and diseases [24]. clusterProfiler package from R was used to conduct analyses of KEGG pathway enrichment and GO annotation onto the gene set of the co expression network. FDR’s critical value was calculated, with statistical significance determined by the threshold p < 0.05 [25]. Based on the TCGA-COAD dataset, evaluation of MATR3’s potential action mechanisms in COAD was realized using GSEA.

Immune microenvironment assessment

This study evaluated 22 immune cell types’ infiltration levels for every COAD sample using CIBERSORT and further examined MATR3’s correlation with these immune cells.

Analysis of drug sensitivity

For more alignment of the study outcomes alongside clinical drug applications, this study evaluated various drugs’ sensitivities. It used "pRRophetic" package (version 0.5) for determining each sample’s IC50 values as well as assessing differences in response between high and low expression groups of MATR3 [26].

Animal studies

This study conducted experiments in male BALB/c nude mice aged between 4 and 6 weeks. The test mice were purchased from a limited liability company and placed in a temperature controlled environment, allowing them to freely eat and drink under alternating day and night conditions. The Animal Ethics Committee of Nantong University Affiliated Hospital has approved all animal experimental protocols (approval number: S20250520-006). This study further followed the main principles of the "Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals". In the subcutaneous tumor bearing model, the test mice were anesthetized with isoflurane gas and randomly divided into NC control group, KB group, and MATR3 group (5 mice in each group). After disinfection of the skin on the back of the axilla, for each mouse, 4 × 106 stably transfected cells from the corresponding group (sh NC, KB, or sh-MATR3) were suspended in 100 μ L PBS and subcutaneously injected into the back area of the axilla. After inserting the needle, the slope should face downwards. After injection, pause for 3-5 seconds, then rotate the needle to exit and use the tissue to naturally bridge and seal the needle passage. After 21 days of inoculation with tumor cells, the mice were euthanized, and the tumor tissue was removed, photographed, and weighed.

Cell culturing and reagents

From the Chinese Academy of Sciences cell bank, this study utilized COAD cells Lovo and HCT116. In RPMI-1640 medium, the cells were cultivated in a humidified incubator at 5% CO2 and 37 °C, supplemented with 5 µg/ml penicillin streptomycin and 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA). MG132 (10 mM, SELLECK, USA), Blasticidin (5 mM, SELLECK, USA), Streptomycin (CHX, 2 mg/ml, SELLECK, USA), U0126 (2.5 mM, SELLESK, USA, 2.5 mM), Ipataserib (5 mM), and Rapamycin (10 mM) were stored in a refrigerator at -20 °C. Following were the experiment’s final concentrations: 5 µ M U0126 (MAPK inhibitor), 10 µg/ml CHX (actinomycin), 6 µM Ipatasertib (AKT inhibitor), 5 µM MG132 (proteasome inhibitor), 10 µM Blasticidin (antibiotic), and 10 µM rapamycin (mTOR inhibitor).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

This study extracted total RNA via a TRIzol solution (Invitrogen, USA) based on the manufacturer's protocol. Using a multi-wavelength measurement system (Infinite 200pro, Tecan), concentration was measured. HiScript RT Mix (Vazyme, Jiangsu, China) was used to reverse-transcribe appropriate RNA into cDNA. Afterward, SYBR Premix Ex Taq kit (TaKaRa Biotechnology, Dalian, China) performed qRT-PCR. 10 µl of the reaction mixture was analyzed on HT 7900 (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, USA). Complete procedure was provided earlier. The reactions were performed three times as well as underwent GAPDH normalization using the 2-ΔΔ CT approach. Western blot

Cell lysates underwent separation using 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and were electroimprinted onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. Membranes were incubated at 4 °C overnight alongside their corresponding primary antibody, after which they were incubated for two hours at room temperature alongside their respective secondary antibody. UBE2S (1:100011415-AP, Proteintech, China), TRAF6 (1:1000ab137452, abcam, UK), ERK1/2 (1:10004695, CST, USA), phosphorylated ERK1/2 (1:10004370, CST), AKT (1:10004691, CST 3, CST), phosphorylated mTOR (Ser2448, p-mTORC1, 1:10005536 and 2976, CST), phosphorylated mTOR (Ser2481, p-mTOR 2, 1:10002974, CST), phosphorylated AP-1 (p-C-Jun, 1:10009261, CST, CST), and K63-linked specific ubiquitin (1:10005621, CST, 1:1 00058395, CST) served as the main primary antibodies. Anti-rabbit and anti-mouse HRP-conjugated antibodies were the secondary antibodies.

CCK-8 measurement

For detecting proliferation of cells, Lovo and HCT116 cells (3 × 103 /well) underwent seeding into a 96-well plate followed by incubation for one, two, and three full days for measurement. For the rescue experiment, this study replaced the respective culture medium with inhibitors by the second day following cell inoculation. 100 ml of 10% CCK8 solution was added for two hours, then the OD value at 450 nm was measured using spectrophotometry (BioTek, Vermont, USA).

Transwell intrusion test

The cells were suspended in FBS-free medium then inoculated into a Transwell plate’s (catalog number 723321, NEST, Wuxi, China) top chamber coated with Matrigel (catalog number HY-K6001, MCE, Shanghai, China). 0.5 ml of intact medium was added as an attractant into the lower well. The chamber was carefully removed after 48 hours. The cells were fixed for 30 minutes in 4% paraformaldehyde and stained for 15 minutes with 1% crystal violet. The chamber was washed using PBS, then the cells were gently removed from the internal compartment of the chamber. Cells were counted at the chamber’s outer surface then image via microscopy (magnified 200x).

Statistical analysis

R v4.1.3 was used for analysis of statistics. Benjamini Hochberg program (B-H) was utilized to calculate FDR. The value of P was bilateral, with statistical significance determined by the threshold P < 0.05.

Functional validation of MATR3 in COAD cell line

Firstly, we detected MATR3 expressions among various cell lines of COAD and found that the COAD levels in HCT116 and Lovo cells were significantly greater compared to those in normal cell line NCM460 (Fig. 6A). Based on these findings, this study chose HCT116 and Lovo cells for further functional studies. For verifying MATR3’s effect toward the cell function of COAD, it transfected MATR3 siRNA into HCT116 and Lovo cell lines, validating the knockdown efficiency at protein and mRNA levels via Western blot and RT-qPCR methods, respectively. According to RT-qPCR outcomes, in HCT116 cell line, sh-MATR3-Homo-389 group and sh-MATR3-Homo-1635 group had substantially lower MATR3 expression levels compared to those in KB and sh NC groups. In Lovo cell line (Fig. 6B-6C), sh-MATR3-Homo-1057, sh-MATR3-Homo-389, and sh-MATR3-Homo-1635 groups had substantially lower MATR3 expression levels compared to those in KB group and sh NC groups. The Western blot results (Fig. 6D-6E) exhibited that sh-MATR3-Homo-1057, sh-MATR3-Homo-389, and sh-MATR3-Homo-1635 groups had substantially lower MATR3 expression levels compared to those in KB and sh NC groups in HCT116 and Lovo cell lines. As shown in the CCK-8 assay results (Fig. 6F), this knockout significantly reduced cell proliferation, indicating that MATR3 contributed to cell growth in COAD. Next, this study investigated the possible role of MATR3 in COAD metastasis . Research found that knocking out the MATR3 gene significantly reduced cell migration and invasion in cultured HCT116 and Lovo cells (Fig. 6G-6H). Finally, tumor volume of the MATR3 low-expression group in the mouse model was observed to be smaller compared to that of the control group . Furthermore, to validate the anti-tumor effect of MATR3 in COAD, we constructed a mouse model. Discussion

COAD, as a highly prevalent malignant tumor worldwide, poses a heavy burden and severe challenge to global public health. Its occurrence and development are characterized by complex interactions between genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors [27]. Due to its multifactorial etiology, complex pathogenesis, and highly heterogeneous clinical manifestations, it is considered a highly complex disease that urgently requires comprehensive and in-depth analysis to develop new strategies for early diagnosis and effective treatment [28]. In recent years, multiple studies conclude MATR3 as playing an important function in many types of cancers. As one instance, the research report by Zhaofeng Xiao and his team suggested that MATR3 could downregulate type I interferon signaling in cancer cells through m⁶A modification, thereby inhibiting the infiltration of immune cells within the tumor [29]. This study comprehensively applied multi-omics bioinformatics analysis and experimental verification, determining MATR3 to be a key molecule in COAD development. The analysis outcomes presented MATR3’s multiple functions in COAD, signifying its capability as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target. Such outcomes emphasize the significance of in-depth research on MATR3’s specific mechanism of action in COAD progression.

The analysis of this study confirmed that the expression of MATR3 was significantly upregulated in COAD tissues compared to the normal (control) group. This overexpression indicated a potential tumor promoting effect. The experimental validation further confirmed this, indicating that MATR3 enhanced the proliferation, migration, and invasion of COAD cells. These results were consistent with previous observations in malignant melanoma, in which MATR3 knockdown could inhibit malignant melanoma proliferation and induce cell apoptosis [20]. Meanwhile, in basal breast cancer, MATR3 could be used as a potential tumor suppressor [30]. This difference highlighted the contextual dependent function of MATR3 in different types of cancer, which could be influenced by tissue-specific interaction networks or mutation backgrounds. In COAD, the functional enrichment analysis of the MATR3 co-expression network showed its involvement in key biological processes such as RNA splicing and regulation of apoptosis signaling pathways. The results of GSEA further strengthened its carcinogenic properties and revealed a strong positive correlation with the cell cycle pathway. This was consistent with previous studies, in which MATR3 depleted cells showed significantly more sub-G0/G1 cells and fewer sub-S cells. MATR3 could interact with cell cycle regulatory genes, as knocking out MATR3 could hinder the cell cycle and suppress the proliferation of renal cancer in vitro [31]. In the context of DNA damage in cancer cells, MATR3 and PINCR regulation could be involved in the induction of p53 target subsets for G1 phase arrest and apoptosis [20].

An important new insight is the relationship between MATR3 expression and tumor immune landscape. The results of single-cell analysis indicate that MATR3 is mainly expressed in CD8+T cells and serves as a central node in multicellular communication networks, particularly involving endothelial cells, plasma cells, and B cells. We identified MATR3+CD8 T cells as a unique subpopulation with high communication activity, which are associated with ribosome related pathways, antigen processing, and presentation pathways. It is worth noting that antigen processing and presentation become key mediators of this intercellular crosstalk, which means that MATR3+CD8 T cells may affect anti-tumor immunity by directly presenting antigens or indirectly regulating antigen-presenting cells.

Moreover, immune analysis based on bulk RNA-seq showed that the expression of MATR3 was negatively correlated with the infiltration of Tregs and macrophages M2, while also positively associated with T cells CD4 naïve, T cells CD4 memory resting, NK cells resting, and mast cells activated. Macrophages have dual functions: anti-tumor and tumor growth promotion. They express different phenotypes under various environmental influences, known as macrophage polarization [32,33]. Polarized macrophages mainly include M1 activated through classical pathways and M2 activated through alternative pathways. M1 and M2 have different functions. On the one hand, M1 has anti-tumor effects that promote tumor immunity. On the other hand, M2 plays a promoting role in tumor development and metastasis [34,35]. M1 generates ROS, which can directly kill tumor cells and inhibit tumor growth. M1 macrophages can produce a series of pro-inflammatory cytokines, which can stimulate the activity of immune cells such as NK cells and CD8+T cells and enhance their ability to attack tumor cells [36]. In addition, they can activate tumor-specific T cells through antigen presentation and mature DC cells, thereby promoting anti-tumor immune responses [37]. Recent studies have shown that M1 macrophages can enhance anti-tumor immune responses by inhibiting immune suppressive cells in the TME [38]. Meanwhile, M2 cells play an important role in different stages of tumor progression. In the early stages of tumors, M2 macrophages mainly participate in tumor growth and angiogenesis. In the middle stage of tumors, they mainly exert immunosuppressive effects. In the late stage of tumors, they mainly participate in tumor invasion and metastasis [[39], [40], [41]]. These seemed to contradict this study’s analysis results, as MATR3, being a factor with stronger invasiveness, was negatively correlated with the cell population macrophages M2, which typically promoted immune escape. It should be pointed out that currently, this study only reveals the correlation between the expression levels of the two and cannot determine their causal relationship. We speculate that this may be due to MATR3 driven tumors achieving effective immune escape through other mechanisms, such as antigen presentation defects or the generation of physical barriers, thereby reducing reliance on classical immunosuppressive cells. But this hypothesis still requires more experiments in the future to verify its causal relationship and specific mechanism of action.

The drug sensitivity analysis emphasizes the clinical translation potential discovered. The observed increase expression of MATR3 was associated with increased sensitivity to several chemotherapy and targeted drugs, indicating that MATR3 could serve as a predictive biomarker for therapeutic response. However, these predictions were generated using computational models from Bulk RNA seq data and did not demonstrate that MATR3 directly regulates response to these drugs. In addition, the predicted IC50 values may be influenced by tumor cell composition, molecular heterogeneity, and differences between the model training dataset and COAD tumors. Therefore, further drug response experiments are still needed, followed by appropriate in vivo validation, to determine whether MATR3 plays a causal role in the sensitivity or resistance of these drugs.

Despite these advances, there are still several important issues. Firstly, the in vivo oncogenic function of MATR3 needs to be validated using genetically engineered mouse models or patient-derived xenografts, which will summarize the complexity of TME and metastasis processes. Secondly, the precise molecular mechanisms downstream of MATR3 are still under investigation. Identifying MATR3’s direct RNA targets in COAD through techniques such as RIP seq or CLIP seq is crucial for determining the specific transcriptional and post-transcriptional programs it controls. Thirdly, bioinformatics analysis mainly relies on TCGA-COAD data and GSE139555 single-cell dataset, which includes a limited number of normal and tumor samples. An independent external queue is needed to evaluate the robustness and universality of expression, prognosis, and immune related outcomes. Fourthly, the relationship between MATR3 and immunosuppressive microenvironment in this study is currently mainly based on computational deconvolution analysis of large-scale RNA seq data, and therefore only supports correlation rather than causality. Although single-cell datasets show that MATR3 is relatively enriched in CD8+T lymphocyte subsets, this observation alone does not prove that MATR3 regulates CD8+T cell activation, cytotoxicity, or anti-tumor activity. In the future, functional co culture experiments, immune cell function assays, and in vivo model studies with immune activity need to be conducted to elucidate the directional and mechanistic basis of the relationship between endogenous MATR3 expression in tumor cells and immune cell recruitment, function, and tumor immune microenvironment remodeling. Finally, the association between MATR3 expression and drug sensitivity is only derived from public database analysis. There is currently a lack of in vitro or in vivo experiments to confirm whether MATR3 directly regulates the response to sorafenib, dasatinib, or other identified drugs. Future research should further validate the potential value of MATR3 as a predictive biomarker and explore combination therapy strategies for overcoming drug resistance.

Conclusion

In summary, this study systematically transitioned from bioinformatics discoveries to experimental verification, consolidating MATR3 as a key driving factor in the pathogenesis of COAD. MATR3’s overexpression promoted tumor cell proliferation and invasion and was associated with cell cycle progression and an unfavorable immune environment. These findings reveal the dual potential of MATR3: as a robust prognostic biomarker for risk stratification and as a promising intervention treatment node. Future research should focus on elucidating its direct molecular targets in vivo, rigorously testing its immune regulatory function, and developing targeted strategies to inhibit its carcinogenic activity, thereby providing new avenues for a precise treatment of cancer.

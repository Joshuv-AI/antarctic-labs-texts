Seven Sphere Quantization
Subhobrata Chatterjee ♭, Can Görmez ♯, Andrew Waldron ♯

Abstract
. Co-oriented contact manifolds quite generally describe classical dynamical systems. Quantization is achieved by suitably associating a Schrödinger equation to every path in the contact manifold. We quantize the standard contact seven sphere by treating it as a homogeneous space of the quaternionic unitary group in order to construct a contact analog of Fedosov’s formal connection on symplectic spinor bundles. We show that requiring convergence of the formal connection naturally filters the symplectic spinor bundle and yields an exact flat connection on each corresponding subbundle. A key ingredient is a generalization of the Holstein–Primakoff mechanism to the quaternionic unitary group. The passage from formal to bona fide quantization determines unitary irreducible representations of the quaternionic unitary group, whose dimensions tend to infinity as the formal deformation parameter approaches its classical limit. This appearance of finite-dimensional representations is not surprising since the contact seven sphere is closed and physically describes generalized positions, momenta and time variables. Keywords: Quantization, contact geometry, deformation quantization, quaternionic homogeneous spaces, Holstein–Primakoff, seven sphere.

The quantization problem for a strict contact manifold
(Z,\alpha)
is stated, in its strongest form, as follows.

Problem 1.1
.

Find a one-parameter family of flat connections
\nabla^{(\hbar)}
on a
\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
vector bundle
{\mathcal{H}}Z
such that, for all

\Phi,\Psi\in\Gamma({\mathcal{H}}Z)
and
u\in\Gamma(TZ)
,

(i)

\mathcal{L}_{u}\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\nabla^{(\hbar)}_{u}\Phi,\Psi\rangle+\langle\Phi,\nabla^{(\hbar)}_{u}\Psi\rangle\,,

(ii)

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}i\hbar\nabla_{u}^{(\hbar)}\Psi=\alpha(u)\Psi\,.

{\large\diamond}

To define the limit in Part (
ii
), we need a suitable norm. Since physical probabilities are computed using a pointwise Born rule (see Equation (
1.1
) below), for a section
\Psi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H}Z)

we define

\displaystyle\|\Psi\|_{z}:=\sqrt{\langle\Psi(z),\Psi(z)\rangle}\,.

The limit in Part (
ii
) is defined at each
z\in Z
with respect to the above norm.
This choice will be used tacitly to define limits at several junctures below.

At a fixed value of
\hbar
, the data
({\mathcal{H}}Z,\nabla^{(\hbar)})
is an example of a
quantum dynamical system
: Given a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and a manifold
Z
,
the latter is defined as the data
(\mathscr{H}Z,\nabla)
consisting of a
\operatorname{U}({\mathcal{H}})
hermitean vector bundle

over

with fiber
\mathcal{H}
and a flat connection

preserving the hermitean bundle inner product. We call a connection obeying these properties a

\nabla\Psi=0\,,

where
\Psi\in\Gamma(\mathscr{H}Z)
. This is a generalization of the Schrödinger equation
[

20
]
. In particular, if
\dot{\gamma}
is the tangent vector to any path

in

for some notion of evolution, then
\nabla_{\dot{\gamma}}\Psi
is the corresponding Schrödinger equation. This allows an implementation of the Born rule as follows: Given an initial state vector

\displaystyle P_{f,i}=\frac{\left|\langle\Psi(z_{f}),\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}\Psi(z_{i})\rangle\right|^{2}}{\langle\Psi(z_{f}),\Psi(z_{f})\rangle\langle\Psi(z_{i}),\Psi(z_{i})\rangle}\,\,,

where
\gamma
is any path on
Z
from
z_{i}
to
z_{f}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\gamma}
is the parallel transport operator determined by the flat connection
\nabla
. Note that when the fundamental group

of the manifold

is non-trivial, in addition to being flat, we must require that
\nabla
has trivial holonomy, in order that probabilities depend only on initial and final states.

While, as discussed above, a contact manifold
(Z,\alpha)
provides a natural Hilbert bundle

The symplectic manifold analog of Problem

Problem 1.2
.

Find a formal connection
\nabla^{[\hbar]}
that is flat up to order

such that, for all

and

(i)

\mathcal{L}_{u}\langle\Phi,\Psi\rangle=\langle\nabla^{[\hbar]}_{u}\Phi,\Psi\rangle+\langle\Phi,\nabla^{[\hbar]}_{u}\Psi\rangle

(ii)

\lim_{\hbar\to 0}i\hbar\nabla_{u}^{[\hbar]}\Psi=\alpha(u)\Psi
.

{\large\diamond}

Of course, we are requiring that items (

) and (

. Our first main result concerns the standard contact seven sphere discussed in Section

Theorem 1.3
.

Problem

We are also interested in going beyond formality. There is an extensive literature on beyond formal quantizations of observables

2.
The contact seven sphere

The seven sphere is the real hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{2}\ni(x,y)
satisfying

(2.1)

\displaystyle|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}=1\,,

where the space of quaternions
{\mathbb{H}}
is a 4-dimensional associative algebra over
\mathbb{R}
with basis
\{1,{\mbox{\boldmath$i$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$j$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}\}
. The multiplication for quaternions is determined by that of the quaternionic units
\vec{e}=({\mbox{\boldmath$i$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$j$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}})
,

{{\mbox{\boldmath$i$}}}^{2}={{\mbox{\boldmath$j$}}}^{2}={{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}}^{2}={\mbox{\boldmath$i$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$j$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}}=-1\,,

where
1
is the multiplicative identity.
Denoting a quaternion
q
in
\mathbb{H}
by
q=q_{0}+\vec{q}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{e}
, where
(q_{0},\vec{q})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
,
quaternionic conjugation
is defined by
\bar{q}:=q_{0}-\vec{q}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{e}
and
|q|^{2}:=\bar{q}q
. When
q=-\bar{q}
, we say that
q
is
purely quaternionic
.

Calling

z=x+y{\mbox{\boldmath$l$}}\in{\mathbb{O}}

and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}:=\mbox{\boldmath$i$}\mbox{\boldmath$l$}
,
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}:=\mbox{\boldmath$j$}\mbox{\boldmath$l$}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$o$}:=\mbox{\boldmath$k$}\mbox{\boldmath$l$}
, where
\{{\mbox{\boldmath$i$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$j$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$k$}},\mbox{\boldmath$l$},\mbox{\boldmath$m$},\mbox{\boldmath$n$},\mbox{\boldmath$o$}\}
are the standard octonionic units, the seven sphere becomes the space of unit octonions

\|z\|^{2}=|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}=1\,.

Denoting octonionic conjugation also by a bar, the one-form

\frac{1}{2}(\bar{z}dz-d\bar{z}z)\in\Gamma(T^{*}{\mathbb{O}})
, pulled back along the inclusion
i:S^{7}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{O}}
, obeys

\theta:=\frac{1}{2}i^{*}(\bar{z}dz-d\bar{z}z)=i^{*}(\bar{z}dz)=i^{*}(-d\bar{z}z)\,.

Moreover,
\theta
is purely octonionic,
i.e.

\theta=-\bar{\theta}
, and determines seven real global contact one-forms that parallelize
S^{7}
.

A hermitean form
\langle{\boldsymbol{\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}}},{\boldsymbol{\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}}}\rangle
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is defined by

\langle P,Q\rangle:={P}^{\dagger}Q
, where
P
and
Q
denote quaternion-valued two-component column vectors, and
\dagger
is the quaternionic conjugate transpose operation. Also,
\|Q\|^{2}:=\langle Q,Q\rangle
.
The hermitean form is preserved by the quaternionic unitary group

\operatorname{U}(2,{\mathbb{H}})=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{H})\,\,|\,\,g^{\dagger}g=\operatorname{Id}\}\,.

This is a real compact Lie group of dimension
10
, see
[
48
, Chapter 1]
.
Its Lie algebra is the space of quaternionic, antihermitean
2\times 2

matrices

\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})=\left\{\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\vec{J}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{e}&P_{0}+\vec{P}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{e}\\
-P_{0}+\vec{P}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{e}&\vec{K}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{e}\end{pmatrix}=:\vec{J}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{\jmath}+P_{0}p_{0}+\vec{P}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{p}+\vec{K}\hbox to2.5pt{\hss$\cdot$\hss}\vec{k}\,\,\,\Big|\,\,\,\vec{J},\vec{P},\vec{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,P_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\,,

which is a real form of the complex symplectic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb{C})
. Indeed,
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
is the fixed point set of the conjugate linear involutive automorphism
X\mapsto-X^{\dagger}
of
\mathfrak{sp}(4,\mathbb{C})
, where

(2.2)

\displaystyle(j_{i})^{\dagger}=-j_{i}\,,\quad(p_{0})^{\dagger}=-p_{0}\,,\quad(p_{i})^{\dagger}=-p_{i}\,,\quad(k_{i})^{\dagger}=-k_{i}\,.

Here, the indices
i,j,k,\ldots
take values
1,2,3
and we raise and lower the indices with the Kronecker symbol
\delta_{ij}
and its inverse.

The (non-vanishing) Lie brackets of the
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
generators
\{\vec{\jmath},p_{0},\vec{p},\vec{k}\}
follow from the above matrix representation and are given by

\displaystyle[\jmath_{i},\jmath_{j}]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}\jmath_{k}\,,\qquad\qquad[k_{i},k_{j}]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}k_{k}\,,

\displaystyle[p_{i},\jmath_{j}]=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}p_{k}+\delta_{ij}p_{0})\,,\qquad{}[p_{i},p_{j}]=\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}(\jmath_{k}+k_{k})\,,\qquad{}[p_{i},k_{j}]=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon_{ij}{}^{k}p_{k}-\delta_{ij}p_{0})\,,

\displaystyle[p_{0},\jmath_{i}]=-\frac{1}{2}p_{i}\,,\qquad\qquad[p_{0},p_{i}]=\jmath_{i}-k_{i}\,,\qquad\quad\quad[p_{0},k_{i}]=\frac{1}{2}p_{i}\,.

The above presentation exhibits the branching rule

(2.3)

\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})=\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathbb{H}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\,

with respect to the diagonal

, generated by

It is useful to change basis to new generators

(J^{\alpha\beta}):=2\begin{pmatrix}-\jmath_{1}+i\jmath_{2}&-j_{3}\\
-j_{3}&\jmath_{1}+i\jmath_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,\;\;(P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}):=\begin{pmatrix}-p_{3}-ip_{0}&-p_{1}+ip_{2}\\
p_{1}+ip_{2}&-p_{3}+ip_{0}\end{pmatrix}\,,\;\;(K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}):=2\begin{pmatrix}k_{1}+ik_{2}&-k_{3}\\

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.95}{[$J^{\alpha\beta},J^{\gamma\delta}]=i(\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}J^{\beta\delta}+\epsilon^{\alpha\delta}J^{\beta\gamma}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}J^{\alpha\delta}+\epsilon^{\beta\delta}J^{\alpha\gamma})$},

\displaystyle\hskip-7.39755pt\scalebox{0.908}{$[J^{\alpha\beta},P^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}]=i(\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}P^{\beta}_{\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\gamma}}),\;\;{}[P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}},P^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}]=i(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}J^{\alpha\beta}+\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})\,,\;\;{}[K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}},P^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}]=i(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}P^{\gamma}_{\dot{\beta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}P^{\gamma}_{\dot{\alpha}})$}\,,

where

\displaystyle(-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta})=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})\,.

Here and above,

and

2.1.
Homogeneous model

The group
\operatorname{U}(2,{\mathbb{H}})
acts transitively on the seven sphere

S^{7}=\left\{Q\in\mathbb{H}^{2}\,\,\,\big|\,\,\,\|Q\|^{2}=1\right\}\,.

Therefore, we view it as the homogeneous
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
-space

(2.6)

\displaystyle S^{7}\cong\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})/\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})\,,

where
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
is the stabilizer of some element

in

. We may choose

. Let

(2.7)

\displaystyle g=\begin{pmatrix}w&x\\

{}\left\|g\begin{pmatrix}0\\

1\end{pmatrix}=\bar{x}\,dx+\bar{y}\,dy\,\,

We would like to obtain an exterior differential system of one-forms on

3.
Formal embedding

The contact distribution
\xi

. In particular, its fibers are 6-dimensional symplectic vector spaces with respect to the Levi two-form

restricted to

\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
is a central extension of these fiberwise symplectic vector spaces viewed as trivial Lie algebras. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
will control solutions to the formal deformation quantization Problem

for
7

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})

is the free associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, with generators

, quotiented by the ideal generated by

a{\hskip 0.85358pt}I-I{\hskip 0.85358pt}a\,,\quad a^{\dagger}{\hskip 0.85358pt}I-I{\hskip 0.85358pt}a^{\dagger}\,,\quad aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a-I\,,\quad a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}a^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}-a^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}I\,,\quad a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}I-I{\hskip 0.85358pt}a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,\,.

It follows that
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
is a Lie algebra with non-vanishing brackets given by commutators

\displaystyle[a,a^{\dagger}]=I\,,\qquad[a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}},a^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}]=-\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,.

The Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
is the Lie subalgebra of

spanned by

. In what follows, we will be primarily concerned with the unital Weyl algebra
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
over

We may equip

and for all

. Eventually,

\sqrt{\hbar}^{\dagger}:=\sqrt{\hbar}

is an injective algebra homomorphism between algebras. When the codomain is an algebra of formal Laurent series, we will call such a map a

3.1.
Holstein–Primakoff mechanisms

In
[
30
]
, the three-sphere
S^{3}\cong\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
was quantized by formally embedding
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
into
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{1})[\sqrt{\hbar}^{-1},\sqrt{\hbar}]]
. This embedding in fact dates back to work on ferromagnetism by Holstein–Primakoff
[
43
]
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})

[k_{i},k_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}k_{k}

admits an
\dot{\rm I}
nönü–Wigner contraction
[
56
]
to the Heisenberg algebra. For that, we replace
k_{3}
by
-\lambda^{2}\mathsf{I}
,
k_{1}
by
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2}}(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})
, and
k_{2}
by
\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2}i}(\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2})
and then sending the real parameter
\lambda\to\infty
, whence

[\mathsf{I},\zeta_{1}]=0=[\zeta_{2},\mathsf{I}]\,,\quad[\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}]=-i{\hskip 0.85358pt}\mathsf{I}\,.

The resulting Lie algebra is the 3-dimensional Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{1}
. In particular, note that the generator
k_{3}
of
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
contracts to the central element
\mathsf{I}
. This Heisenberg algebra can be embedded into
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{1})[\sqrt{\hbar}^{-1},\sqrt{\hbar}]]
by the Lie algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}\mapsto i\frac{1}{\hbar}\,,\qquad\zeta_{1}\mapsto-i\frac{a}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\,,\qquad\zeta_{2}\mapsto i\frac{a^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\,\,.

Holstein and Primakoff answered the question of whether there exist expansions

K_{+}=-i\frac{a}{\sqrt{\hbar}}+\cdots\,,\qquad K_{0}=i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-2a^{\dagger}a-\frac{1}{2}\right)\,,\qquad K_{-}=i\frac{a^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}+\cdots\,,

where the
\cdots
’s denote formal power series in
\sqrt{\hbar}
taking values in the Weyl algebra, such that

[K_{0},K_{\pm}]=\pm 2iK_{\pm}\,,\qquad[K_{+},K_{-}]=-iK_{0}\,.

Remarkably, they found the following solution
[
43
]

K_{+}=-i\sqrt{1-\hbar\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\frac{a}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\,,\qquad K_{-}=i\frac{a^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\sqrt{1-\hbar\left(a^{\dagger}a+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\,.

Formally expanding the square roots in powers of
\hbar
yields an embedding of
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
into the ring of formal Laurent series in
\sqrt{\hbar}
with coefficients in the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{1})
. Moreover, Holstein and Primakoff showed that, for distinguished values of
\hbar
, these formal power series lead to operators giving unitary irreducible spin representations of
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
on finite-dimensional subspaces of a Fock space.

We want to generalize the above “Holstein–Primakoff mechanism” to
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
. In that case, there is a contraction to a Heisenberg algebra corresponding to the branching rule in Equation (
2.3
). For that, we introduce the (invertible) invariant tensor

(\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}=(\eta^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})

defining the
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{C})
subalgebra of
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
generated by
K_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}:=\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
. Let us denote the symmetric trace-free part of a tensor
X_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
by

X_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})_{\circ}}:=X_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}-\frac{1}{2}\eta^{\dot{\gamma}\dot{\delta}}X_{\dot{\gamma}\dot{\delta}}\,\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,.

A
\dot{\rm I}
nönü–Wigner contraction is performed by making the replacements

(3.4)

\displaystyle\jmath^{\alpha\beta}:=J^{\alpha\beta},\quad\pi^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}:=\lambda{\hskip 0.85358pt}P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}},\quad\zeta_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})_{\circ}}:=\lambda{\hskip 0.85358pt}K_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})_{\circ}}\,,\quad\zeta_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}:=\mathsf{I}=\lambda^{2}K_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}\,\,.

Sending the real parameter

yields the following non-vanishing brackets

\displaystyle[\jmath^{\alpha\beta},\jmath^{\gamma\delta}]=i(\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}\jmath^{\beta\delta}+\epsilon^{\alpha\delta}\jmath^{\beta\gamma}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}\jmath^{\alpha\delta}+\epsilon^{\beta\delta}\jmath^{\alpha\gamma})\,,

\displaystyle[\jmath^{\alpha\beta},\pi^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}]=i(\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}\pi^{\beta}_{\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}\pi^{\alpha}_{\dot{\gamma}})\,,\qquad{}[\pi^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}},\pi^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}]=i\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\;\mathsf{I}\,,

\displaystyle[\zeta_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})_{\circ}},\zeta_{(\dot{\gamma}\dot{\delta})_{\circ}}]=i(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}\eta_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\delta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\delta}}\eta_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\delta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\delta}}\eta_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}})\;\mathsf{I}\,.

In particular, the generator

is central in the large

\mathfrak{heis}_{3}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
. Our goal is to find a realization of
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
starting from a realization of the above Lie algebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2i\frac{z}{\sqrt{\hbar}}+\cdots\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}=i\frac{1}{\hbar}+\cdots\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2i\frac{\bar{z}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}+\cdots\,,

where the

\sqrt{\hbar}
. We must now require that the above functions obey the following Poisson brackets,

\displaystyle\hskip-5.40608pt\scalebox{0.902}{$\{\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta},\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}\}=\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}\mathcal{P}^{\beta}_{\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\gamma}},\;\;{}\{\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}},\mathcal{P}^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}+\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,,\;\;{}\{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}},\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}\}=\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\beta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\alpha}}$}\,,

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.902}{$\{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}},\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\gamma}\dot{\delta}}\}=\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\delta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\delta}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\delta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\delta}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}$}\,.

Note that the functions
n:=2\bar{z}z
and
N:=z^{+}_{\dot{-}}z^{-}_{\dot{+}}-z^{+}_{\dot{+}}z^{-}_{\dot{-}}
have vanishing Poisson brackets with the
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
generators
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}
. Therefore, we refine our
ansatz
to read

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2ie^{i\psi}F^{\hbar}(n,N)\,z\,,\quad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-n-N\right)=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}\,,\quad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2i\bar{z}\,e^{-i\psi}F^{\hbar}(n,N)\,\,,

\hskip-8.5359pt\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}=-e^{-i\phi_{2}}H^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)z^{+}_{\dot{-}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}z\,+\,e^{i\phi_{1}}H^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)z^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}=\bar{z}\,z^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,e^{i\phi_{2}}H^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)+z^{-}_{\dot{-}}e^{-i\phi_{1}}\,H^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,\,,

\hskip-11.38092pt\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}=\bar{z}\,z^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,e^{i\varphi_{2}}G^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)+z^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,e^{i\varphi_{1}}G^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,,\quad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}=-e^{-i\varphi_{2}}G^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)z^{-}_{\dot{-}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}z+e^{-i\varphi_{1}}G^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\;z^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,\,.

Here, we have made a polar decomposition such that the functions
F^{\hbar}
,
H_{1}^{\hbar}
,
H_{2}^{\hbar}
,
G_{1}^{\hbar}
,
G_{2}^{\hbar}
,
\psi
,
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
,
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
are real-valued functions of
n
and
N
.
Computing the Poisson brackets of these remaining generators and requiring that these obey the above realization of
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
yields a system of differential equations. A family of solutions thereof is given below:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2ie^{i\psi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N}{\hskip 0.85358pt}z\,,\,\,\,\,\,{\hskip 0.85358pt}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-n-N\right)=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}\,,\,\,\,\,\,{\hskip 0.85358pt}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2i\bar{z}{\hskip 0.85358pt}e^{-i\psi}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}=-e^{-i\phi_{2}}z^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,z+e^{i\phi_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N}\,z^{+}_{\dot{+}},\quad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}=\bar{z}\,z^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,e^{i\phi_{2}}+z^{-}_{\dot{-}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}e^{-i\phi_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N}\,\,,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\,\,\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}=\bar{z}\,z^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,e^{i\phi_{2}}+z^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,e^{-i\phi_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}=-e^{-i\phi_{2}}z^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,z+e^{i\phi_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N}\;z^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{++}=-2iz^{+}_{\dot{-}}z^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{J}^{+-}=-i(z^{+}_{\dot{+}}z^{-}_{\dot{-}}+z^{-}_{\dot{+}}z^{+}_{\dot{-}})=\mathcal{J}^{-+}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{J}^{--}=-2iz^{-}_{\dot{+}}z^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,\,,

where the phases obey

and

. The map

gives an embedding of
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
into a space of functions of
z
and

taking values in
\mathbb{C}^{\hbar}
.

We can already perform a

that directly replaces the classical variables, in the orders written above, with the corresponding elements of the Weyl algebra. In particular, we directly replace functions of

by corresponding elements in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
, where

and

are now the
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})

) and (

) below. In general, such a procedure is, of course, fraught

.
Remarkably it here reproduces the formal quantum solution given in Theorem
3.2

3.3.
Formal quantum embedding

We would like to first realize the
\dot{\operatorname{I}}
nönü–Wigner-contracted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}

. To that end, we work with the embedding of
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
given by the Lie algebra homomorphism

(3.6)

\hskip 14.22636pt\scalebox{0.97}{$\mathsf{I}\mapsto i\frac{1}{\hbar}\,,\qquad\zeta_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}\mapsto-2i\frac{a}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\,,\qquad\zeta_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}\mapsto 2i\frac{a^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\,,\qquad\pi^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mapsto\frac{a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}$}\,,

(3.7)

\scalebox{0.905}{$\jmath^{++}\mapsto\mathcal{J}^{++}:=-2ia^{+}_{\dot{-}}a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,,\,\,\jmath^{+-}=\jmath^{-+}\mapsto\mathcal{J}^{+-}=\mathcal{J}^{-+}:=-i(a^{+}_{\dot{+}}a^{-}_{\dot{-}}+a^{-}_{\dot{+}}a^{+}_{\dot{-}})\,,\,\,\jmath^{--}\mapsto\mathcal{J}^{--}:=-2ia^{-}_{\dot{-}}a^{-}_{\dot{+}}$}\,.

Equations (

and

Since the bilinears in Equation (

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2i\frac{a}{\sqrt{\hbar}}+\cdots\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}=i\frac{1}{\hbar}+\cdots\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2i\frac{a^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{\hbar}}+\cdots\,,

in

that realize

taking values in

3.3.1.
Gradings

Certain gradings of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
will be particularly important for constructing the formal generators.
Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and a positive integer
k
, we say that
D\in\mathfrak{g}
gives a
|k|
-grading of
\mathfrak{g}
if we can write a direct sum decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-k}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{k}

such that each
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
above is an eigenspace of
D
with eigenvalue
l
under the adjoint action. We will use the same terminology for a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\langle D\rangle
even when the
grading element

D\notin\mathfrak{g}
.

When performing the
\dot{\operatorname{I}}
nönü–Wigner contraction of Equation (
3.4
), we decomposed one of the direct summands
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
in Equation (
2.3
) into a direct sum of three 1-dimensional vector spaces using the
|1|
-grading

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})=\langle K_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{C})\oplus\langle K_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}\rangle\,,

with grading element
\frac{-iK_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}}{2}
. The entire Lie algebra has a
|2|
-grading

(3.11)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})=\langle K_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\langle P^{+}_{\dot{-}},P^{-}_{\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\underbrace{\left(\langle J^{\alpha\beta}\rangle\oplus\langle K_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}\rangle\right)}_{\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{C})}\oplus\langle P^{+}_{\dot{+}},P^{-}_{\dot{+}}\rangle\oplus\langle K_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}\rangle\,,

with grading element
-iK_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}
.
As
\lambda\to\infty
, the
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
of Equation (
3.10
) contracts to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{heis}_{1}=\langle\zeta_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\langle\mathsf{I}\rangle\oplus\langle\zeta_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}\rangle\,.

In particular, the element
K_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}
contracts to the central element
\mathsf{I}
. Recall that the entire Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
contracts to
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
. Thus, when
\lambda\to\infty
, Equation (
3.11
) becomes

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{heis}_{3}=\langle\zeta_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\langle\pi^{+}_{\dot{-}},\pi^{-}_{\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\underbrace{\left(\langle\jmath^{\alpha\beta}\rangle\oplus\langle\mathsf{I}\rangle\right)}_{\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\langle\mathsf{I}\rangle}\oplus\langle\pi^{+}_{\dot{+}},\pi^{-}_{\dot{+}}\rangle\oplus\langle\zeta_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}\rangle\,.

In
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
, the
number operator

(3.12)

\displaystyle n:=-\frac{1}{4}\xi_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}\xi_{\dot{\gamma}\dot{\delta}}\epsilon^{\dot{\delta}\dot{\beta}}-1=2a^{\dagger}a\,.

Here, the
\eta
trace-free tensor

\xi_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}:=\sqrt{\hbar}\,\zeta_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})_{\circ}}
.
The operator
-n
gives a
|2|
-grading of
\mathfrak{heis}_{1}
. Moreover, the
total number operator
of the subalgebra
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
of
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
is given by

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:=n+N\,,\qquad\mbox{with}\quad N:=\frac{\hbar}{2}\,\pi^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\pi^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}=a^{+}_{\dot{-}}a^{-}_{\dot{+}}-a^{+}_{\dot{+}}a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,\,.

The operator
-\mathcal{N}
gives
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
a
|2|
-grading. Moreover,
-N
gives
\mathfrak{heis}_{2}
the

-grading

\mathfrak{heis}_{2}=\langle\pi^{+}_{\dot{-}},\pi^{-}_{\dot{-}}\rangle\oplus\langle\mathsf{I}\rangle\oplus\langle\pi^{+}_{\dot{+}},\pi^{-}_{\dot{+}}\rangle\,.

3.3.2.
Embedding

We would like to label particular elements of
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
by meromorphic functions in
\sqrt{\hbar}
. More generally, a formal Laurent series
f^{\hbar}(x):=\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}c_{k}(x)\sqrt{\hbar}^{k}
in
\sqrt{\hbar}
, where
M\in\mathbb{Z}
and
c_{k}(x)
are polynomials in
x
, together with some
A\in\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
determines an element

(3.14)

\displaystyle f^{\hbar}(A)=\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}c_{k}(A)\sqrt{\hbar}^{k}\in\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}\,\,.

Elements of
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
that can be expressed in the above form shall be called
polymeromorphic
. For example,

(3.15)

\displaystyle f^{\hbar}\left(N+\frac{n}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\,\,,

denotes

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}\big(N+\tfrac{n}{2}\big)\,\hbar^{k}\,,

where

\displaystyle b_{k}(x):=(-1)^{k}\frac{\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}\left(1-2l\right)}{2^{k}k!}x^{k}\,.

Note that in this case
\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}+x}
is a
bona fide
meromorphic function of
\sqrt{\hbar}
around
\hbar=0
. The polymeromorphic notion generalizes to formal Laurent series
f^{\hbar}(x,y,\dots,z)
of many variables in the obvious way.

Multiplying a polymeromorphic element by any element in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
is well-defined. In particular, we have the following product properties.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f^{\hbar}(n,N)\in\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
be polymeromorphic. Then,

\displaystyle f^{\hbar}(n,N){\hskip 0.85358pt}a\,

\displaystyle=af^{\hbar}(n-2,N)\,,\qquad\,\,\,\,{\hskip 0.85358pt}f^{\hbar}(n,N)a^{\dagger}=a^{\dagger}f^{\hbar}(n+2,N)\,,

\displaystyle f^{\hbar}(n,N)a^{+}_{\dot{+}}

\displaystyle=a^{+}_{\dot{+}}f^{\hbar}(n,N-1)\,,\qquad f^{\hbar}(n,N)a^{+}_{\dot{-}}=a^{+}_{\dot{-}}f^{\hbar}(n,N+1)\,,

\displaystyle f^{\hbar}(n,N)a^{-}_{\dot{+}}

\displaystyle=a^{-}_{\dot{+}}f^{\hbar}(n,N-1)\,,\qquad f^{\hbar}(n,N)a^{-}_{\dot{-}}=a^{-}_{\dot{-}}f^{\hbar}(n,N+1)\,.

Proof.

We prove the first of these properties, the rest follow
mutatis mutandis
. By definition,

f^{\hbar}(n,N)=\sum_{k=M}^{\infty}c_{k}(n,N)\sqrt{\hbar}^{k}\,\,,

for some integer
M
and some polynomial
c_{k}(n,N)
in
n
and
N
. Therefore, we can express it unambiguously in the form

c_{k}(n,N)=\sum_{j=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{l=0}^{m_{2}}b_{jl}n^{j}N^{l}\,\,,\quad b_{jl}\in\mathbb{C}\,,

since
n
and
N
commute. Note that

n^{j}a=a(n-2)^{j}\,.

Thus,

c_{k}(n,N)a=a{\hskip 0.85358pt}c_{k}(n-2,N)

and hence

f^{\hbar}(n,N)a=af^{\hbar}(n-2,N)\,.

∎

We will need to impose reality conditions on polymeromorphic elements. We call
f^{\hbar}\in\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}

real polymeromorphic
if

(f^{\hbar})^{\dagger}=f^{\hbar}\,.

Now we can realize
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
be Lie algebras equipped with respective conjugate linear involutive automorphisms
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
. We say that an embedding
\phi
of
\mathfrak{g}
into
\mathfrak{k}
is
reality preserving
if the embedding intertwines
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
:

\phi\circ\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\circ\phi\,.

A reality preserving embedding maps fixed points of
\sigma_{1}
to fixed points of
\sigma_{2}
, or in other words preserves real forms. The embeddings given in Equations (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) are reality preserving.

Theorem 3.2
.

A reality preserving embedding of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
into
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
is given by

\displaystyle J^{\alpha\beta}\mapsto\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}\,,\qquad P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mapsto\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,,\qquad K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mapsto\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,\,,

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2i\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\;a\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-n\right)=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2ia^{\dagger}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\,\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}=-a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,a+\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\;a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}=a^{\dagger}\,a^{-}_{\dot{+}}+a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\,\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}=a^{\dagger}\,a^{+}_{\dot{+}}+a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}=-a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,a+\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\;a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{++}=-2ia^{+}_{\dot{-}}a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{J}^{+-}=-i(a^{+}_{\dot{+}}a^{-}_{\dot{-}}+a^{-}_{\dot{+}}a^{+}_{\dot{-}})=\mathcal{J}^{-+}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{J}^{--}=-2ia^{-}_{\dot{+}}a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,\,.

Proof.

It suffices to verify that the generators given in the theorem satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.902}{$[\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta},\mathcal{J}^{\gamma\delta}]=i(\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}\mathcal{J}^{\beta\delta}+\epsilon^{\alpha\delta}\mathcal{J}^{\beta\gamma}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\delta}+\epsilon^{\beta\delta}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\gamma})$},

\displaystyle\hskip-5.40608pt\scalebox{0.902}{$[\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta},\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}]=i(\epsilon^{\alpha\gamma}\mathcal{P}^{\beta}_{\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\gamma}}),\;\;{}[\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}},\mathcal{P}^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}]=i(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}+\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})\,,\;\;{}[\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}},\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\gamma}}]=i(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\beta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{P}^{\gamma}_{\dot{\alpha}})$}\,,

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.902}{$[\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}},\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\gamma}\dot{\delta}}]=i(\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\delta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\delta}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\delta}}+\epsilon_{\dot{\beta}\dot{\delta}}\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}})$}\,.

In principle one can perform this verification directly, but this is rather tedious. We therefore outline a series of
ansätze
built from polymeromorphic elements that produce the above embedding.

We are tasked with finding the
\cdots
’s in Equations (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). First, notice that the
|2|
-grading element
-iK_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}
of
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
is mapped to
-i\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}
in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
. Moreover, recall that the operator
-\mathcal{N}
gives a
|2|
-grading of
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
. Therefore, we begin by adding
-i\mathcal{N}
to the central element
\frac{i}{\hbar}
and consider the new ansatz

\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-\mathcal{N}\right)=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}\,\,.

This will restrict the remaining generators considerably.

Note that, with respect to the grading element
-i\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}
, the undeformed parts of
\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}
given by the images of
\zeta_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}
and
\zeta_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}
(see Equation (
3.6
)) have grades
-2
and
2
, respectively. Similarly, with respect to
-\mathcal{N}
,
a^{\dagger}
and
a
have respective grades
-2
and
2
, and commute with
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}
. Therefore, we make the further ansatz

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2iF^{\hbar}(n,N)\,a\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2ia^{\dagger}\,F^{\hbar}(n,N)\,\,,

where we require
F^{\hbar}
to be
real
polymeromorphic in order to satisfy
(\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}})^{\dagger}=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}
.

The generators
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
must have grade
-1
or
1
, with respect to
-i\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}
, and we have the following gradings:

\displaystyle\text{Grade -1: }\,a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,,\,a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,,\,a^{\dagger}a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,,\,a^{\dagger}a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,,\qquad\qquad\text{Grade 1: }\,a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,,\,a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,,\,aa^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,,\,aa^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,.

In addition, the above combinations can be organized into doublet representations of
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
. This leads to our next ansatz.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}=-a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,H^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)\,a+H^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}=a^{\dagger}\,H^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)\,a^{-}_{\dot{+}}+a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,H^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}=a^{\dagger}\,\,G^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)\,a^{+}_{\dot{+}}+a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,G^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,\,,\qquad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}=-a^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,G^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)\,a+G^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,\,.

For the reality conditions

(\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}})^{\dagger}=-\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}\,,\qquad(\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}})^{\dagger}=\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,,

it suffices to require that
G^{\hbar}_{1},G^{\hbar}_{2},H^{\hbar}_{1},H^{\hbar}_{2}
are
real
polymeromorphic. Motivated by the classical solution (
3.5
) with phases set to unity, we refine our ansatz to satisfy

H_{2}^{\hbar}=1\,.

We can now compute the Lie brackets of our
ansätze
to obtain relations between the polymeromorphic elements
F^{\hbar},G^{\hbar}_{1},G^{\hbar}_{2},H^{\hbar}_{1}
.
Since
a,a^{\dagger},a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
are independent, we can read off relations from their coefficients. The brackets of
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}
with
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
imply that

G_{1}^{\hbar}=H_{1}^{\hbar}\,\,,\qquad G_{2}^{\hbar}=H_{2}^{\hbar}=1\,\,.

Then, examining independent combinations of
a,a^{\dagger},a^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
in the six possible brackets of
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
with
\mathcal{P}^{\beta}_{\dot{\beta}}
, we find

\displaystyle H_{1}^{\hbar}(n,N)^{2}=\frac{1}{\hbar}-\frac{n}{2}-N\,,

\displaystyle(1-N)H_{1}^{\hbar}(n+2,N-1)+(1+N)H_{1}^{\hbar}(n,N)=2{\hskip 0.85358pt}F^{\hbar}(n,N)\,.

Setting

H_{1}^{\hbar}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-\frac{n}{2}}\,,

so that in turn

F^{\hbar}=H_{1}^{\hbar}\,,

gives the quoted solution. It is easy to check that all remaining brackets are satisfied.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The above theorem does not address uniqueness. Indeed, on the basis of reality alone, it is at least possible to allow certain phase factors. For example, let
\psi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
be polynomials in
n,N
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}=-2ie^{i\psi}F^{\hbar}(n,N)\,a\,,\quad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}=i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-n-N\right)=\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{+}}\,,\quad\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}=2ia^{\dagger}\,e^{-i\psi}F^{\hbar}(n,N)\,\,,

\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}=-e^{-i\phi_{2}}H^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)a^{+}_{\dot{-}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}a\,+\,e^{i\phi_{1}}H^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,,\quad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}=a^{\dagger}\,a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,e^{i\phi_{2}}H^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)+a^{-}_{\dot{-}}e^{-i\phi_{1}}\,H^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,\,,

\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}=a^{\dagger}\,a^{+}_{\dot{+}}\,e^{i\varphi_{2}}G^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)+a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,e^{i\varphi_{1}}G^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\,,\quad\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}=-e^{-i\varphi_{2}}G^{\hbar}_{2}(n,N)a^{-}_{\dot{-}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}a+e^{-i\varphi_{1}}G^{\hbar}_{1}(n,N)\;a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,\,,

also obey the reality conditions (
2.5
). These phase factors were set to unity in the solution given in the theorem above, but were incorporated into the classical solution given in Subsection
3.2
.
\blacklozenge

Finally, we would like to emphasize that while the square root expressions appearing in Theorem
3.2
can be used to define operators on an appropriate Fock space, they in general do not have the desired antihermiticity properties required to construct a connection of a quantum dynamical system.

4.
Beyond formal embeddings

Our proof of the beyond formality Theorem
1.3
will require us to promote the formal solution of Theorem
3.2
to operators acting on some Hilbert space. In particular, we wish to find unitary irreducible representations of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces arising as truncations of the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. This generalizes the Holstein–Primakoff mechanism realizing finite-dimensional spin representations by truncations of the infinite-dimensional harmonic oscillator Fock space
[
43
]
.

Partial sums of the formal power series appearing in the previous section are Laurent polynomials in
\sqrt{\hbar}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
. To represent them as operators, we shall consider representations of the Heisenberg algebra. For that, let
\mathcal{S}
be the dense subspace of Schwartz functions in
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and denote by
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{H})
the set of all linear operators on
\mathcal{H}
. Note that
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{H})
does not have an algebra structure. In order to construct an algebra, consider (see
[
59
, Chapter 2]
and
[
60
, Chapter 3]
)

\widetilde{\mathfrak{gl}}(\mathcal{S}):=\{A\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{H})\,\,|\,\,\mathcal{D}(A)=\mathcal{S}\,,\,\,A{\hskip 0.85358pt}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\,,\,\,\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(A^{*})\,,\,\,A^{*}\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}\},

where
\mathcal{D}
denotes the domain of its argument and
A^{*}
denotes the adjoint operator of
A
on
\mathcal{H}
. Set
A^{\dagger}
to be the restriction of this adjoint to
\mathcal{S}
. Then there is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:\mathfrak{heis}_{3}\to\widetilde{\mathfrak{gl}}(\mathcal{S})
, given by

\displaystyle\rho(a)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{1}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)\,,\,\,\,\,\;\;\qquad\rho(a^{\dagger})\,:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{1}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)\,,\;\;

\displaystyle\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{+}})

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{2}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)\,,\;\;\qquad\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{-}}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{2}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}\right)\,,\;\;

\displaystyle\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{+}})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{3}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\right)\,,\,\,\,\,\;\;\qquad\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}}):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(x_{3}-\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\right)\,,

and
\rho(1):=\operatorname{Id}
(the identity operator). Here,
\big\{x_{j},-i\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\,\,|\,\,j=1,2,3\big\}
are position and momentum operators, whose domain of definition we take to be
\mathcal{S}
. We also denote by
\rho
the unital algebra homomorphism from
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{gl}}(S)
induced by the above Lie algebra representation of
\mathfrak{heis}_{3}
on
\mathcal{S}
.

Recall that there is a countable complete orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{H}
, given by (three-dimensional) Hermite functions in
\mathcal{S}
. We denote these by
\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}
, where
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
are non-negative integers. The above operators act on these basis elements according to

\displaystyle\rho(a)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n_{1}}\ket{n_1-1,n_2,n_3}\,,\,\,\,\,\;\;\;\;\rho(a^{\dagger}){\hskip 0.85358pt}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=\sqrt{n_{1}+1}\ket{n_1+1,n_2,n_3}\,,\;\;

\displaystyle\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{+}})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{n_{2}}\ket{n_1,n_2-1,n_3}\,,\;\;\;\;\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{-}})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=\sqrt{n_{2}+1}\ket{n_1,n_2+1,n_3}\,,\;\;

\displaystyle\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{+}})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n_{3}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3-1}\,,\,\,\,\,\;\;\;\;\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=\sqrt{n_{3}+1}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3+1}\,.

Moreover,

\rho(n)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=2\,n_{1}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}\,,\qquad\hskip 11.09654pt\rho(N)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=(n_{2}+n_{3}+1)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}\,.

We would like to extend the domain of the map
\rho
to polymeromorphic elements of Equation (
3.15
). However, general formal Laurent series as given in Equation (
3.14
) may not yield well-defined operators acting on Schwartz functions
\mathcal{S}
. This difficulty will be solved by suitably truncating
\mathcal{H}
,
à la
Holstein and Primakoff.

Motivated by Equation (
3.16
), we denote by
S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar)
the partial sum

\displaystyle S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}b_{k}\left(N+\frac{n}{2}\right)\hbar^{k}\,,

so that we can now write

(4.1)

\displaystyle\scalebox{0.99}{$\rho(S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar))\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{k}\left(1-2l\right)}{2^{k}k!}\left(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1\right)^{k}\hbar^{k}\,\,\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}$}\,.

Importantly, the formal parameter
\hbar
can now stand for any positive real number. Assuming such,
we may now study the convergence of the right hand side of the above equation for large
\ell
. (It will be clear from context when
\hbar
is a number and when it is a formal parameter.) Let
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}:=\operatorname{Span}{\left\{\,\,\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}\in\mathcal{B}\,\,\,\,\,|\,\,\,\,\,n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}\leq m-1\,\right\}}\;.

This is a Hilbert space of finite dimension
m+2\choose 3
. The operators
\rho\big(S_{\ell}(n,N,\frac{1}{m})\big)
have well-defined actions on
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Their action on basis elements is given in Equation (
4.1
). Define the linear operator
\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}:\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=\sqrt{m-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}-1}\,\,\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}\,.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\hbar\in\mathscr{I}:=\{\,\frac{1}{m}\,\,\,|\,\,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\,\}
. The sequence of operators
\rho\big(S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar)\big)
converges to the operator
\rho_{S}\in\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{H}^{\hbar})
in the strong operator topology.

Proof.

Convergence acting on states
\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}
such that
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1<m
follows from the fact that
x=\hbar(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1)
is inside the radius of convergence of the power series for
\sqrt{1-x}
around
x=0
. In fact, this power series converges absolutely when
x=1
. This leads to the claimed limit.
∎

Let us now consider the elements
(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}
and
(\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}
in
\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{heis}_{3})
obtained by replacing the Laurent series expansion of the square roots appearing in
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
with the partial sums
S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar)
. For example,

(\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}:=a^{\dagger}\,a^{+}_{\dot{+}}+a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\,S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar)\,.

This gives us a sequence of operators
\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
and
\rho\big((\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
. Unlike the operators
\rho\big(S_{\ell}(n,N,\hbar)\big)
of Equation (
4.1
), the image of these operators may not be contained in
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
, for example,

\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}})_{\ell}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\big)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.945}{$\left(\rho(a^{\dagger})\,\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{+}})+\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}})\rho\Big(S_{\ell}\big(n,N,\frac{1}{m}\big)\Big){\hskip 0.85358pt}\right)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}$}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.945}{$-\sqrt{n_{1}+1}\sqrt{n_{2}}\ket{n_1+1,n_2-1,n_3}$}

\displaystyle+\scalebox{0.945}{$\sqrt{m}\sqrt{n_{3}+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\frac{\prod_{l=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{k}\left(1-2l\right)}{2^{k}k!}\left(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1\right)^{k}\frac{1}{m^{k}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3+1}$}\,.

As seen from the above, successive applications of
\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}})_{\ell}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\big)
produces terms with ever increasing eigenvalue
n_{3}
of the third number operator
\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{+}})\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}})
. Therefore, we must treat
\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\big)
and
\rho\big((\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\big)
as maps from
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
to
\mathcal{H}
when analyzing their large
\ell
limits. To that end, define operators mapping
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}
by

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}}

\displaystyle:=-2{\hskip 0.85358pt}i{\hskip 0.85358pt}\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\rho(a)\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}}:=2{\hskip 0.85358pt}i{\hskip 0.85358pt}\rho(a^{\dagger}){\hskip 0.85358pt}\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\,,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}}

\displaystyle:=-\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}})\rho(a)+\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{+}})\,,\,\,\qquad\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}}:=\rho(a^{\dagger})\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{+}})+\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{-}})\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\,,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}}

\displaystyle:=-\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{-}})\rho(a)+\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{+}})\,,\,\,\qquad\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}}:=\rho(a^{\dagger})\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{+}})+\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}})\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}\,.

Here, we view
\rho(a^{\dagger})
,
\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{-}})
, and
\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{-}})
as maps from
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m+1}}
and
\rho(a)
,
\rho(a^{+}_{\dot{+}})
, and
\rho(a^{-}_{\dot{+}})
as endomorphisms of
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
. For example, that the composition of
\rho(a^{\dagger})
with
\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}
is a sequence of maps
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m+1}}\subset\mathcal{H}
, while that of
\rho_{S}^{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{m}}
with
\rho(a)
sends
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\to\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}\subset\mathcal{H}
. The following proposition establishes that the six operators displayed above in fact define maps from
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
to
\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\hbar\in\mathscr{I}
. The sequences of operators
\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
and
\rho\big((\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
converge strongly to endomorphisms
\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
, respectively. Moreover, the map
\rho^{\hbar}
defined by

\displaystyle J^{\alpha\beta}\mapsto\rho(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta})\,,\qquad P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mapsto\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}}\,,\qquad K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\mapsto\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}}\,\,

gives a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
on
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
.

Proof.

First, note that the linear operators
\rho(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta})
act according to

\rho(\mathcal{J}^{++})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{1}{$2i\sqrt{n_{3}+1}\sqrt{n_{2}}\ket{n_1,n_2-1,n_3+1}$}\,,

(4.5)

\rho(\mathcal{J}^{+-})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{1}{$-i(n_{3}-n_{2})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}$}\,,

\rho(\mathcal{J}^{--})\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{1}{$-2i\sqrt{n_{2}+1}\sqrt{n_{3}}\ket{n_1,n_2+1,n_3-1}$}\,.

In particular, the eigenvalue
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1
of the number operator
\rho\big(N+\frac{n}{2}\big)
is preserved by
\rho(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta})
.
Thus, the operators
\rho(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta})
are endomorphisms of
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
.

We must now analyze
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
. Firstly, the sequences of operators
\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
and
\rho\big((\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
converge strongly to the linear operators
\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}}
, respectively, by virtue of Proposition
4.1
. It remains to establish that
\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}}
are endomorphisms of
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
. It suffices to consider their actions on the basis elements
\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
. Simple computations show

\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{+}}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.9}{$-2i\sqrt{n_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}}\ket{n_1-1,n_2,n_3}$}\,,

\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{+}\dot{-}}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.9}{$i\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-2n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}-1\right)\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}$}\,,

\rho_{\mathcal{K}_{\dot{-}\dot{-}}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.9}{$2i\sqrt{n_{1}+1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}-1}\ket{n_1+1,n_2,n_3}$}\,,

\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{+}}}\ket{n_1, n_2, n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.91}{$-\sqrt{n_{1}}\sqrt{n_{3}+1}\ket{n_1-1,n_2,n_3+1}-\sqrt{n_{2}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}}\ket{n_1,n_2-1,n_3}$}\,,

\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{-}}}\ket{n_1, n_2, n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.91}{$\sqrt{n_{1}+1}\sqrt{n_{3}}\ket{n_1+1,n_2,n_3-1}+\sqrt{n_{2}+1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}-1}\ket{n_1,n_2+1,n_3}$}\,,

\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{-}_{\dot{+}}}\ket{n_1, n_2, n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.91}{$-\sqrt{n_{1}}\sqrt{n_{2}+1}\ket{n_1-1,n_2+1,n_3}+\sqrt{n_{3}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3-1}$}\,,

\rho_{\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\dot{-}}}\ket{n_1, n_2, n_3}

\displaystyle=\scalebox{0.91}{$-\sqrt{n_{1}+1}\sqrt{n_{2}}\ket{n_1+1,n_2-1,n_3}+\sqrt{n_{3}+1}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\hbar}-n_{1}-n_{2}-n_{3}-1}\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3+1}$}\,.

Now, recall that the basis elements
\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
satisfy
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+1\leq\frac{1}{\hbar}
. But, the above operators do not preserve the eigenvalue of the total number operator
\rho(N+\frac{n}{2})
. However, it is easy to check that whenever a vector
\ket{a,b,c}
with
a+b+c+1=\frac{1}{\hbar}+1
is produced in the above formulæ, a corresponding square root coefficient vanishes.

One may use the formulæ of the previous two displays above to show that

\displaystyle\rho^{\hbar}([X,Y])\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}=[\rho^{\hbar}(X),\rho^{\hbar}(Y)]\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}\,,

for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})
and basis vector
\ket{n_1,n_2,n_3}\in\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
. This establishes the Lie algebra representation statement.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The representations
\rho^{\hbar}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
of Proposition
4.2
are in fact unitary irreducible representations of dimension
{\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{\hbar}}+2\choose 3

(\frac{1}{\hbar}-1,0)

because
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
is realized by the space of symmetric tensors of rank strictly less than
\frac{1}{\hbar}
in dimension three; see Equation (

\blacklozenge

Let us call the embedding map of Theorem
3.2
“
\operatorname{embed}
” and the map given in Proposition
4.2
from the image of
\operatorname{embed}
in
\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}
to unitary endomorphisms of
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
“
\operatorname{shorten}
”. Then, the results of this section are summarized as follows.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\frac{1}{m}\in\mathscr{I}
. The diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hskip 36.98866pt\operatorname{embed}(\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H}))\subset\mathcal{W}^{\hbar}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{u}(2,\mathbb{H})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{u}(\mathcal{H}^{\frac{1}{m}})}

Remark 4.5
.

\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}

]
.

5.1.1.
Homogeneous model

We now want to relate the Hilbert bundle
\mathcal{H}Z
to the homogeneous model. Note that generally, if
M\cong G/H
is a homogeneous space, the frame bundle
\operatorname{Fr}(M)
is isomorphic to
G\times_{H}\operatorname{GL}(T_{[e]}M)
, where
[e]
is the identity coset. Specializing to

, one has an
H

\alpha
, and an
H
-invariant two-form

; see Section
2

\alpha
determines the distribution
\xi
, which is preserved by
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
. The restriction of
d\alpha
to
\xi
makes each fiber a symplectic vector space with a symplectic bilinear form preserved by
\operatorname{Sp}(6,\mathbb{R})
. Thus,
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
embeds into
\operatorname{Sp}(6,\mathbb{R})
. From this embedding and Diagram (
5.1
) above, we can construct an embedding of
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
into
\operatorname{Mp^{c}}(6,\mathbb{R})
. To begin with, we lift the
\operatorname{Sp}(6,\mathbb{R})
embedding of
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
to an
\operatorname{Mp}(6,\mathbb{R})
embedding

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

This follows because
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
is compact and simply-connected. From Diagram (

\operatorname{Mp^{c}}(6,\mathbb{R})

\operatorname{Mp^{c}}(6,\mathbb{R})

5.1.2.
Formal connections

From the above discussion, one might think that to prove Theorem
1.3
all that is needed is to induce a connection on
\mathcal{H}Z
from the Maurer–Cartan connection on
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
. For that, one would need a unitary
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
-representation acting on
\mathcal{H}
, but Theorem
3.2
only gives formal operators. Those however will suffice to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem 1.3.

We need to write down a sequence of connections
\nabla^{\hbar,\ell}
acting on smooth sections of
\mathcal{H}Z
, whose curvature equals
\hbar^{\ell/2}F_{\ell}
, where

is some smooth two-form that is polynomial in
\sqrt{\hbar}
and takes values in
\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{H}Z)
. We consider

because the cases
\ell=-2
, and

have already been handled; see Equations (
3.1
) and (

. Let us consider

where

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\hbar,\ell}

\displaystyle=i\,\alpha\left(\frac{1}{\hbar}-N-n\right)-2i\,\mu_{+-}(a^{+}_{\dot{+}}a^{-}_{\dot{-}}+a^{-}_{\dot{+}}a^{+}_{\dot{-}})

\displaystyle+\left(-2i\,\mu_{--}a^{-}_{\dot{+}}\,a^{-}_{\dot{-}}+\nu^{\dot{-}}_{-}a^{\dagger}a^{-}_{\dot{+}}+\nu^{\dot{-}}_{+}\,a^{\dagger}a^{+}_{\dot{+}}+\left(2i{\hskip 0.85358pt}\kappa^{\dot{-}\dot{-}}\,a^{\dagger}+\nu^{\dot{-}}_{-}a^{-}_{\dot{-}}+\nu^{\dot{-}}_{+}a^{+}_{\dot{-}}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\hbar}}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell+1}b_{k}\,\hbar^{k}\right).

5.2.
Beyond formal quantization

For distinguished discrete values of
\hbar
, Theorem
1.4
shows that the formal connection gives a quantum connection of a corresponding quantum dynamical system. In other words, Theorem (
1.4
) produces quantum mechanical systems from a formal quantization.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We start by constructing the claimed vector bundle filtration. For each subspace
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
of
\mathcal{H}
appearing in the vector space filtration of Remark
4.5
, we can form the corresponding vector bundle

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}Z=\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})\times_{\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})}\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}

associated to the homogeneous model
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})/\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
. The metaplectic representation restricted to
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
agrees with the representation of
\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})
obtained by exponentiating the Lie algebra representation
\rho^{\hbar}
restricted to
\mathfrak{u}(1,\mathbb{H})
. One thus obtains the claimed vector bundle filtration of Theorem
1.4
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}Z\subset\mathcal{H}^{1/2}Z\subset\mathcal{H}^{1/3}Z\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{H}Z\,.

In general, given a homogeneous space
G/H
and a
G
-representation
\mathbb{V}
, the associated vector bundle
G\times_{H}\mathbb{V}
is a (model) tractor bundle
[
54
]
. The (flat) Cartan connection
\omega
on
G
defines a flat linear connection
\nabla^{\omega}
on
G\times_{H}\mathbb{V}
. When
\mathbb{V}
is a unitary representation, the pair
(G\times_{H}\mathbb{V},\nabla^{\omega})
is a quantum dynamical system. In particular, equipping
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}Z
with the connection
\nabla^{\hbar}
defined by the model
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
Cartan connection gives a sequence finite-dimensional quantum dynamical systems
(\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}Z,\nabla^{\hbar})_{\hbar\in\mathscr{I}}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is completed upon showing that these same quantum dynamical systems are induced by the formal connection
\nabla^{[\hbar]}
of Theorem
1.3
on
\mathcal{H}Z
. The limit statement of Equation (
1.2
) deals with sections of a vector bundle. Our plan is to write explicit expressions for the connections
\nabla^{\hbar}
and
\nabla^{[\hbar]}
in the trivialization associated to the coordinate patches of Subsection
2.1
.

For any smooth section
\Psi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}Z)
, there is a
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
-equivariant function
\hat{\Psi}
on the extended principal bundle
\widehat{\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})}:=\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})\times_{\operatorname{U}(1,\mathbb{H})}\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
with values in
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
. Then,
\nabla^{\hbar}\Psi
corresponds to the horizontal, equivariant form
d\hat{\Psi}+\rho^{\hbar}(\hat{\omega})\hat{\Psi}
, where
\hat{\omega}
is the principal connection on
\widehat{\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})}
coming from the Maurer–Cartan form
\omega
on
\operatorname{U}(2,\mathbb{H})
(see Subsection
2.1
). It follows, in the trivializations corresponding to the patches, that

\nabla^{\hbar}\Psi=d{\hskip 0.85358pt}\Psi+\rho^{\hbar}(A_{s})\Psi\,,

where
A_{s}
is the pullback of
\omega
along the section
s
. Thus,
\nabla^{\hbar}
is represented as

\nabla^{\hbar}=d+A_{s}=d+\kappa^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,K_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}+\nu^{\dot{\alpha}}_{\alpha}\,P^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\mu_{\alpha\beta}\,J^{\alpha\beta}\,.

On the other hand, the formal connection

\nabla^{\hbar,\ell}=d+\mathcal{A}^{\hbar,\ell}=d+\kappa^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\,(\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}+\nu^{\dot{\alpha}}_{\alpha}\,(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}+\mu_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathcal{J}^{\alpha\beta}\,

defines an operator mapping
\Gamma(TZ)\times\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}Z)
to
\Gamma(\mathcal{H}Z)
because the operators
\rho\big((\mathcal{K}_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
and
\rho\big((\mathcal{P}^{\alpha}_{\dot{\alpha}})_{\ell}^{\hbar}\big)
are maps from
\mathcal{H}^{\hbar}
to
\mathcal{H}
. We now need to show for all
\Psi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H}^{\hbar})
and
u\in\Gamma(TZ)
that

\nabla^{\hbar}_{u}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\Psi=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\nabla^{\hbar,{\hskip 0.85358pt}\ell}_{u}{\hskip 0.85358pt}\Psi\,\,.

The above limit holds pointwise by virtue of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Minimal spectral radius of graphs with given matching number
Jiaqi Liu, Zhenzhen Lou, Vilmar Trevisan

Abstract
. The Brualdi-Solheid problem asks which graph achieves the extremal (maximum or minimum) spectral radius for a given class of graphs. This paper addresses the Brualdi-Solheid problem for 𝒢 n , β \mathcal{G}_{n,\beta} , the family of graphs with order n n and matching number β \beta , aiming to identify its spectrally minimal graphs i.e., those that minimize the spectral radius ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) . We introduce the novel concept of “quasi-adjacency” relation, developing a unified structural classification framework for trees in 𝒢 n , β \mathcal{G}_{n,\beta} , which clarifies structural properties and provides a constructive method to generate trees with fixed β \beta . By showing that all spectrally minimal graphs in 𝒢 n , β \mathcal{G}_{n,\beta} are trees, we further narrow the search for extremal graphs. Additionally, we apply this framework to the representative cases β = 2 , 3 , 4 \beta=2,3,4 , obtaining the minimizers by explicit structural formulas involving parameters related to n n . Keywords: spectral radius; matching number; tree; extremal problem AMS subject classifications: 05C50; 05C35

For the family

We recall that for a graph

In this work, we study Problem

Second, we introduce the new concept of “quasi-adjacency” relation to develop a unified structural classification framework for trees in
\mathcal{G}_{n,\beta}

\beta

Third, we apply this framework to the specific cases

into distinct subclasses. Through comprehensive spectral radius analysis—including constructing characteristic equations of adjacency matrices to rule out subclasses, and deriving strict spectral radius inequalities to exclude others—we characterize the spectrally minimal graphs.

Let

denote the family of all

\beta

Denote

To establish connections between the structure of general graphs and their spanning trees in the context of matching numbers, we first show that the matching number of a connected graph can be preserved in at least one of its spanning trees. This result serves as a bridge for analyzing graph properties through tree structures, which are often more tractable.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any simple connected graph
G\in\mathcal{G}_{n,\beta}
, there exists a spanning tree
T
of
G
with matching number
\beta
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a simple connected graph in
\mathcal{G}_{n,\beta}
, and let
M=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{\beta}\}
be a maximum matching in
G
. We construct a spanning tree
T
of
G
that preserves
\beta
as its matching number.

Case 1:
G
is a tree.

Now,
T=G
itself is the required spanning tree, as
M
remains a maximum matching in
T
.

Case 2:
G
contains cycles.

Let
E_{M}=E(G)\setminus M
. We claim that every cycle
C
in
G
contains at least one edge
e\in E_{M}
. Indeed, if
C\subseteq M
, then
C
would consist of disjoint edges, which is impossible for a cycle. Thus,
C\cap E_{M}\neq\emptyset
.

In order to construct
T
, we iteratively remove edges from cycles as follows:
Select a cycle
C
in the current graph
G
.
Remove an arbitrary edge
e\in C\cap E_{M}
, yielding
G^{\prime}=G-e
.
Since
e\notin M
,
M
remains a matching in
G^{\prime}
with a size of
\beta
.
The graph
G^{\prime}
stays connected because
e
belongs to a cycle.
Repeat until no cycles remain.

The resulting graph
T
is a spanning tree of
G
because it is connected, acyclic, and contains all vertices of
G
. Moreover, since
M
is preserved throughout the process, we have
\beta(T)=\beta
.
∎

For trees
T\in\mathcal{T}_{n,\beta}
, we define two distances below to study their structural properties.

Edge-to-edge distance
: For any
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(T)
,

\bar{d}_{T}(e_{1},e_{2})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\in e_{1}\\
v_{2}\in e_{2}\end{subarray}}d_{T}(v_{1},v_{2}).

Edge-set distance
: For any
E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq E(T)
,

\bar{d}_{T}(E_{1},E_{2})=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{1}\in E_{1}\\
e_{2}\in E_{2}\end{subarray}}\bar{d}_{T}(e_{1},e_{2}).

We next focus on the relationship between maximal matchings and induced subgraphs.
The following lemma establishes the existence of a specific maximal matching with desirable connectivity in the induced subgraph.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any tree
T\in\mathcal{T}_{n,\beta}
, there exists a maximal matching

M=\{e_{1}=u_{1}v_{1},e_{2}=u_{2}v_{2},\dots,e_{\beta}=u_{\beta}v_{\beta}\}

such that the induced subgraph

is a tree, where
U=V(M)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\beta}\{u_{i},v_{i}\}

Let

of

If

is connected, then it must be a tree since

is acyclic. If

is disconnected, we iteratively modify
M
while preserving maximality until

becomes connected.

Step 1:
Initialize

While

is disconnected, perform the following steps:

Step 2:
Let

be the largest connected component of

.

Step 3:
Select an edge

Let

\bar{d}(u^{\prime}v^{\prime},e_{t_{l}})=\bar{d}(E(T_{l}),e_{t_{l}}).

Without loss of generality, let

. Then we claim that

. Otherwise, if

, it contradicts that

is the largest connected component; if

, we can find a path

The procedure terminates when

(ii) The total number of components is finite (at most

The classification of graphs provides a foundation for identifying the extremal graph with the minimum spectral radius.
Building on this, our next step is to determine which graph among these candidates has the minimum spectral radius. We first prove that
T_{4}^{*}\ncong K_{12}(a,b,c,d)
or
K_{13}(a,b,c,d)

Let

be the set of quasi-pendant vertices of
T_{4}^{*}
, and let
v^{*}
be the vertex among
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}
with the largest number of hanging leaves.
Recall that
n\geq 19
.
By the pigeonhole principle,

has at least two leaves.

In the remaining of this section, in order to characterize the minimizer in
\mathcal{G}_{n,4}
, we discard candidates. By assuming that a graph
G
is minimizer, we make operations to find a graph
G^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{n,4}
, with the same number of vertices such that
\rho(G^{\prime})<\rho(G)
, contradicting the minimality. The main operation is the combination of Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
as already used in the previous section. But we also need the following result showing that if we make a larger pendant path larger, while making a smaller pendant path smaller, this decreases the spectral radius.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
v
be a vertex in a graph
G
and suppose that two new paths

P_{k+1}:vv_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{k}
and
P_{\ell+1}:uu_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{\ell}
(
k\geq\ell\geq 1
) are attached to
G
at
v
, respectively, to form a new graph
\rho(G_{k,\ell})
. Then

\rho(G_{k,\ell})>\rho(G_{k+1,\ell-1})
.

In addition, the following result is also going to be used.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
34
]
)
.

Let
u
,
v
be two distinct vertices of a connected graph
G
. Suppose
w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{s}

(s\geq 1)
are some vertices of
N_{G}(v)\setminus N_{G}(u)
and
\mathbf{x}
is perron vector of
G
. Let

G^{\prime}=G-\{vw_{i}:i=1,2,...,s\}+\{uw_{i}:i=1,2,...,s\}
.
If
x_{u}\geq x_{v}
, then
\rho(G)<\rho(G^{\prime})
.

, we obtain:

\varphi(K_{6}(a,b-1,c,d),x)-\varphi(K_{12}(b,a,c,d),x)\\
=x^{a+b+c+d-2}(x^{2}-(d+1))(x^{2}-(b-1))>0.

Notice that
\rho(K_{1,d+1})=\sqrt{d+1}
and
\rho(K_{1,b-1})=\sqrt{b-1}
.
Since both
K_{6}(a,b-1,c,d)
and
K_{12}(b,a,c,d)
contain
K_{1,d+1}
and
K_{1,b-1}
as induced proper subgraphs, it follows from Lemma
3.2
that:

\rho(K_{6}(a,b-1,c,d)),\rho(K_{12}(b,a,c,d)>\max\big\{\sqrt{d+1},\sqrt{b-1}\big\}.

Hence, we conclude that:

\rho(K_{6}(a,b-1,c,d))<\rho(K_{12}(b,a,c,d)),

which implies that
T_{4}^{*}\ncong K_{12}(a,b,c,d)
.

Observe that among
a,b,c,
and
d
of
K_{13}(a,b,c,d)
, at most one can be zero. Without loss of generality, assume that
c\geq 1
. Next, we compare the characteristic polynomials of
K_{12}(a,b,c-1,d)
and
K_{13}(a,b,c,d)
.
By Lemma
3.5
, they are given by

\varphi(K_{13}(a,b,c,d),x)\\
=x^{a+b+c+d-3}(x^{2}-a)\big[(x^{2}-b)(x^{4}-(c+d+2)x^{2}+cd+c+d)-(x^{2}-c)(x^{2}-d)-(x^{2}-b)(x^{2}-c)(x^{2}-d)\big]

, and

\varphi(K_{12}(a,b,c-1,d),x)=x^{a+b+c+d-3}\big[(x^{4}-(a+b+2)x^{2}+ab+a+b)\cdot(x^{4}-(c+d+1)x^{2}+(c-1)d+c+d-1)-(x^{2}-a)(x^{2}-b)(x^{2}-d-1)\big].

For
x>\max\big\{\sqrt{c-1}),\sqrt{a},\sqrt{b}\big\}
, we have:

\varphi(K_{12}(a,b,c-1,d),x)-\varphi(K_{13}(a,b,c,d),x)=x^{a+b+c+d-3}(x^{2}-(c-1))(2x^{2}-a-b)>0.

Since both graphs contain
K_{1,c-1}
,
K_{1,a}
, and
K_{1,b}
as induced proper subgraphs, from Lemma
3.2
we have

\rho(K_{12}(a,b,c-1,d)),\rho(K_{13}(a,b,c,d))>\max\big\{\sqrt{c-1}),\sqrt{a},\sqrt{b}\big\}.

Hence,

\rho(K_{13}(a,b,c,d))>\rho(K_{12}(a,b,c-1,d)),

which implies
T_{4}^{*}\ncong K_{13}(a,b,c,d)
.
∎

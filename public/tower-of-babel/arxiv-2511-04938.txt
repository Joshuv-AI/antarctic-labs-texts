2.
A proof outline, the additive-noise case, and some conjectures

The nonlinear part of
Theorem
1.2
naturally includes two
very different statements. The first is that, off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set,

(2.1)

\dimh u(\{t\}\times F)\leqslant 2\dimh F\qquad\forall\text{compact }F\subset\mathbb{T},\ t>0.

This statement in fact holds for all
p\geqslant 1
, does not require
the polarity assumption of Theorem
1.2
, and is an immediate
consequence of the definition of Hausdorff dimension and the following
version of the well-known modulus of continuity of
u
:
For every
\alpha\in(0\,,\frac{1}{2})
and
T>0
,

(2.2)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}:x\neq y}\frac{\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,y)\|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}<\infty\quad\text{a.s.}

See Walsh
[
22
]
*Chapter 3 for very closely related results with essentially
the same proofs.
Moreover, the preceding argument implies also that the event defined in
(
2.1
) includes the event defined in (
2.2
). Since the probability
space is complete and the event of (
2.2
) is measurable, then so is
the event defined by (
2.1
).
Therefore, measurability issues do not arise in this first step.

One can readily fill in the few remaining gaps to see that the preceding outline yields
a complete proof of (

covering argument,
such as Proposition 2.3 of Falconer
[
8
]
,
and holds without need for the additional
technical assumptions of Theorem

.
We will concentrate our efforts on proving the second implication of
Theorem
1.2
. Namely
that, if the set
\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\inf_{\|x\|=1}\|\sigma(v)x\|=0\}

is polar for
u
and
p\geqslant 4
, then

(2.3)

\dimh u(\{t\}\times F)\geqslant 2\dimh F\qquad\forall\text{compact }F\subset\mathbb{T},\ t>0,

off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set.
Because
\dimh
is countably stable
[
8
]
,
it suffices to prove that (
2.3
) holds simultaneously
for all
t\in[a\,,b]
and
F\subseteq B(0\,,c)

where
0<a<b
and

are non-random and fixed.
Then, one can try to emulate the method of Kaufman
[
12
]
,
introduced first in the context of Brownian motion. Namely, choose and fix
some
\alpha\in(0\,,2)
, and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

cover
B(0\,,c)
as optimally as possible with finitely many balls
B(a_{1}\,,2^{-\alpha n}),B(a_{2}\,,2^{-\alpha n}),\ldots,
and then prove that simultaneously for every subset

a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{N}}
of the
a_{j}
s, and for all

, the number of
inverse images of the form

\{x\in\mathbb{T}:u(t\,,x)\in B(a_{j}\,,2^{-\alpha n})\}

] that intersect
any dyadic arc in

grows at most polylogarithmically in

. Since the underlying
probability space is complete, this takes care of all measurability issues as well.

In the context of Kaufman’s theorem
[
12
]
, the latter intersection estimate can be carried
out readily thanks to the fact that Brownian motion has stationary and independent
Gaussian increments. In the present setting, the required estimates are very
difficult to develop in part because of the inherent nonlinear dependence of

on the underlying
noise. Thus, as a first step, we study the special case of (
1.1
) where it is driven by
a non-degenerate additive noise. In that case, a standard stopping argument reduces the problem
to the one where

is uniformly bounded. Then, an appeal to the Girsanov theorem
allows us to reduce the problem to the case that
b\equiv 0
when the solution is now a Gaussian
random field. Moreover, barring the relatively easy-to-manage effects of the initial data,
that Gaussian random field has all but one requisite property of Brownian motion: It only does not
have independent increments! While this is a significant loss of information,
it was shown by Wu and Xiao
[
23
]
and Xiao
[
24
]
that
one can appeal to the theory of strong
local non-determinism (SLND) in order to at least partially overcome the lack of independence;
see also Berman
[
3
]
, Lee and Xiao
[
16
]
, and Monrad and Pitt
[
18
]
.
Thus, the main impediment to a proof of the following is to develop a suitable notion
of SLND. As it turns out, there are a number of variations of the notion of
SLND that are available here. Once we identify the senses in which
the process

has SLND, we are led to the three parts of the next
theorem, each part corresponding to a different notion of SLND. Note that
part

the definitive form of Theorem

,
with optimal conditions, valid in the case of

\forall p\geqslant 2

(ii)

\forall p\geqslant 4

(iii)

\mathrm{P}\{\dimh u(C)=\dim_{{}_{\rm H}}^{\rho}C\text{
$\forall$compact
$C\subset\ST$}\}=1

\forall p\geqslant 6
.

Next, we consider (

We had mentioned that locally

We use this approximation theorem by conditioning on everything up to

for a suitable choice of

is small when

[

has the SLND property. A similar argument shows that

estimates that are needed in order to carry out a combinatorial analysis of the
intersection numbers of inverse images of the form

[

], brought up earlier
in the context of equations with additive noise. And the condition

,
which we believe is too strong, comes up in this part for technical reasons that roughly say
that the scaling imposed by replacing

. In this way,
the analysis of the inverse images resembles that of the inverse images of fBm.
Suffices it to say only that the proof of (

) that includes the uniformity in

rests on a similar method but uses a much more delicate form of SLND that is valid on many fine
scales of

Let us conclude this section by introducing notation for the singular values of the matrix

Definition 2.2

Note that the polarity condition of Theorem

Let us conclude this section by presenting the next observation

Lemma 2.5

\lambda^{1/2}
is Lipschitz continuous on

This must be well known. But the proof is so short that it might be easier to simply present
the proof.

Proof.

For every

,
by compactness there exists

such that

3.
The Gaussian case

In this section, we study the specialization of the SPDE (
1.1
) to the case that

\sigma(v)
is equal to the
p\times p
identity matrix
\bm{I}
for all
v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
and that
u_{0}\equiv 0
. Because
of the important role of this specialization, we reserve the symbol
H
–
for “heat” – for the solution
to (
1.1
) in that case. In other words,

(3.1)

\left[\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}H=\partial^{2}_{x}H+\xi\quad\text{on $(0\,,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}$},\\
&\text{subject to}\quad H(0)=\mathbb{0},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathbb{0}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
takes the value
0\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
everywhere on
\mathbb{T}
.
The unique (mild) solution to (
3.1
) is the following Wiener-integral process,

(3.2)

H(0\,,x)=0\quad\text{and}\quad H(t\,,x)=\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y),

for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
, where

(3.3)

G_{r}(a\,,b)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi r}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{(a-b+2n)^{2}}{4r}\right)\qquad[r>0,\,a,b\in\mathbb{T}]

denotes the heat kernel for
\partial_{t}-\partial_{x}^{2}
on

(0\,,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}
. See da Prato and Zabczyk
[
4
]
and Walsh
[
22
]
.
We pause to observe that the random function
H=(H_{1}\,,\ldots,H_{p})
is made of

p
i.i.d. coordinate processes
H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}
.

Our analysis of the random field
H
will rely on various heat-kernel estimates
for the heat kernel
G
that are closely related to some of the technical
estimates in Khoshnevisan, Kim, Mueller, and Shiu

[
13
]
. For instance, Lemma B.1 and Remark B.2
of
[
13
]
together assert that

(3.4)

\tfrac{1}{4}\max(t^{-1/2},1)\leqslant\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{T}}G_{t}(x\,,y)\leqslant 2\max(t^{-1/2},1)\qquad\forall t>0.

Next we present three lemmas that tighten up some of the other heat-kernel estimates of
[
13
]
,
improving them to what we believe are their respective essentially-optimal forms. We have resisted
the temptation of deriving matching lower bounds as we will not need them in the sequel.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every fixed
T>0
,

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\left[G_{s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(z\,,y)\right]^{2}\lesssim\sqrt{t}\wedge|x-z|,

uniformly for all
x,z\in\mathbb{T}
and
t\in(0\,,T]
.

Proof.

We can reiterate (
3.3
) as follows: For all
a,b\in\mathbb{T}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}
, and
s>0
,

G_{s}(a\,,b)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{s}(a-b+2n),

where

(3.5)

\phi_{s}(v)=\frac{\exp(-v^{2}/(4s))}{(4\pi s)^{1/2}}

denotes the heat kernel for
\partial_{t}-\partial^{2}_{x}
on
\mathbb{R}
(vs.
\mathbb{T}
).
We can use the semigroup property of the heat kernel in order to see that

\displaystyle\int{T}\left[G_{s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(z\,,y)\right]^{2}{\rm d}y=2G_{2s}(0\,,0)-2G_{2s}(x\,,z)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\phi_{2s}(2n)-\phi_{2s}(x-z+2n)\right]=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left({\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}sn^{2}}-{\rm e}^{\pi i(x-z)n-2\pi^{2}sn^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}sn^{2}}\left(1-\cos(\pi(x-z)n)\right),

where we appealed to the Poisson summation
formula (Lemma
A.3
) in order to deduce the third identity.
Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\left[G_{s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(z\,,y)\right]^{2}\leqslant 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-{\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}tn^{2}}}{2\pi^{2}n^{2}}\left(2\wedge(\pi^{2}(x-z)^{2}n^{2})\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1\wedge(tn^{2})}{n^{2}}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1\wedge((x-z)^{2}n^{2})}{n^{2}}\right)\lesssim\sqrt{t}\wedge|x-z|\quad\text{(Lemma \ref{inequality1})},

uniformly for all
t>0
and
x,z\in\mathbb{T}
. This does the job.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
T>0
fixed,

\int_{0}^{r}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\,[G_{t-s}(x\,,y)-G_{r-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim\sqrt{t-r},

uniformly for all
x\in\mathbb{T}
and
0<r<t\leqslant T
.

Proof.

Thanks to the semigroup property of the heat kernel,

\displaystyle\int{T}\left[G_{t-r+s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(x\,,y)\right]^{2}{\rm d}y=G_{2(t-r+s)}(0\,,0)+G_{2s}(0\,,0)-2G_{t-r+2s}(0\,,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{2(t-r+s)}(2n)+\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{2s}(2n)-2\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{t-r+2s}(2n).

Then, by the Poisson summation formula (Lemma
A.3
), we have

\displaystyle\int{T}\left[G_{t-r+s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(x\,,y)\right]^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left({\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}(t-r+s)n^{2}}+{\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}sn^{2}}-2{\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}(t-r+2s)n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\left({\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}(t-r+s)n^{2}}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}sn^{2}}\right)^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}sn^{2}}\left(1-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}(t-r)n^{2}}\right)^{2},

for all
t>r>0
,
s\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
. Hence, a change of variable yields that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\left[G_{t-s}(x\,,y)-G_{r-s}(x\,,y)\right]^{2}=\int_{0}^{r}{\rm d}s\,\int{T}\left[G_{t-r+s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(x\,,y)\right]^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-{\rm e}^{-2\pi^{2}rn^{2}}}{2\pi^{2}n^{2}}\left(1-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}(t-r)n^{2}}\right)^{2}\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1\wedge((t-r)^{2}n^{4})}{n^{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{|t-r|},

where the last inequality holds by Lemma
A.2
. The proof is complete.
∎

Next, we present a result about the size of certain heat integrals of powers of
the “parabolic distance”

|t-s|^{1/2}+|x-y|
between space-time points
(t\,,x)
and
(s\,,y)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
q\in(0\,,1]
and
T>0
fixed,

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\ [G_{s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\left[(t-s)^{q/2}+|y-x|^{q}\right]\lesssim t^{(1+q)/2},

uniformly for every
t\in(0\,,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

An inspection of (
3.3
) tells us that the integral of the lemma
is independent of
x\in\mathbb{T}
. Therefore, we consider the case that
x=0
.
Since
\int{T}[G_{t-s}(0\,,y)]^{2}\,{\rm d}y=G_{2(t-s)}(0\,,0)\lesssim(t-s)^{-1/2}

uniformly for all
t\in(0\,,T]

[see (
3.4
)], we change variables in order to deduce the inequality

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(0\,,y)]^{2}s^{q/2}\lesssim t^{(1+q)/2},

uniformly for
t\in(0\,,T]
. It remains to establish the same upper bound when
the integrand
s^{q/2}
is replaced by
|y|^{q}
.
With this aim in mind, note that
|w+2n|\geqslant|n|

uniformly for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
-1\leqslant w\leqslant 1
.
Therefore, we may appeal to (
3.5
) to deduce
the heat-kernel estimate,

\left|G_{r}(a\,,b)-\phi_{r}(b-a)\right|\leqslant\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/(4r)}}{\sqrt{4\pi r}}\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\{-w^{2}/(4r)\}}{\sqrt{\pi r}}\,{\rm d}w\leqslant 2,

valid for all
a,b\in\mathbb{T}
and
r>0
.
Consequently, we may write

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(0\,,y)]^{2}|y|^{q}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}s}{s}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\ [\phi_{1}(y/\sqrt{s})]^{2}|y|^{q}+t,

and change variables in the integral in order to conclude the proof.
∎

It is well known that the variance of every coordinate of

H(t\,,x)
is of order
\sqrt{t}
when
t\ll 1
. The next
result shows that the said variance is in fact nearly
\sqrt{t/\pi}
when
t\ll 1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

\sqrt{t/\pi}\leqslant\Var H_{1}(t\,,x)\leqslant\sqrt{t/\pi}+(t/2)

for all
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Thanks to stationarity and symmetry, we may and will assume that

x=0
. Now, the semigroup property and the symmetry of the heat kernel together yield

\Var H_{1}(t\,,0)=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\ [G_{s}(0\,,y)]^{2}=\int_{0}^{t}G_{2s}(0\,,0)\,{\rm d}s
, which can be simplified as

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{4\pi s}}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/s}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{4\pi s}}=\sqrt{\frac{t}{\pi}}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/s}\,\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{4\pi s}}.

This implies the result since the preceding infinite sum is on one hand
\geqslant 0

and on the other hand bounded above by

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}w\int_{0}^{t}\exp(-w^{2}/s)\,{\rm d}s/\sqrt{4\pi s}=t/4.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Our next result presents a way to quantify the notion that

H(t\,,x)
and
H(t\,,z)
are close to being independent
when
t\ll 1
and
|x-z|\gg\sqrt{t}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Uniformly for all
t>0
and
x,z\in\mathbb{T}
,

\Cov[H_{1}(t\,,x)\,,H_{1}(t\,,z)]\lesssim\max(\sqrt{t}\,,t)\exp\left(-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{8t}\right).

Consequently, there exists
c>1
such that

\displaystyle 0<\inf_{t\in(0,1]}\inf_{|x-z|\geqslant c\sqrt{t}}t^{-1/2}\Var[H_{1}(t\,,x)-H_{1}(t\,,z)]

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leqslant\sup_{t\in(0,1]}\sup_{x,z\in\mathbb{T}}t^{-1/2}\Var[H_{1}(t\,,x)-H_{1}(t\,,z)]<\infty.

Proof.

Thanks to stationarity, we may (and will) assume
without loss of generality, that
z=0
. Now, for every
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{T}
,
we invoke the semigroup property of
G
to find that

{C}=\Cov[H_{1}(t\,,x)\,,H_{1}(t\,,0)]

satisfies

{C}=\int_{0}^{t}G_{2s}(0\,,x)\,{\rm d}s\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}-4n|x|+4n^{2}}{8s}\right)\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{2\pi s}};

see (
3.3
). Therefore,

\displaystyle{C}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{8s}\right)\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}+4n(n-1)}{8s}\right)\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{2\pi s}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{8t}\right)\left[\sqrt{2t}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\exp\left(-\frac{n(n-1)}{2s}\right)\frac{{\rm d}s}{\sqrt{s}}\right].

We may consider the sum before the integral. Since

\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{n(n-1)}{2s}\right)\leqslant\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{n^{2}}{4s}\right)\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{4s}\right)\,{\rm d}y\propto\sqrt{s},

uniformly for all

, the lemma follows.
∎

Armed with the preceding technical lemmas, we are ready to present one of the main results of this section.
The following proposition asserts that the Gaussian random field
H=\{H(t\,,x)\}_{(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}}
is
strongly locally nondeterministic
[
3
,
18
,

.

[

]
*Proposition 5.1 and

Proposition 3.6
.

For every
T>0
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Var(H_{1}(t\,,x)\mid H_{1}(t_{1}\,,x_{1}),\dots,H_{1}(t_{m}\,,x_{m}))

\displaystyle\asymp\min\left\{\sqrt{t}\,,\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\left(|t-t_{j}|^{1/2}+|x-x_{j}|\right)\right\},

uniformly for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
(t\,,x),(t_{1}\,,x_{1}),\dots,(t_{m}\,,x_{m})\in(0\,,T]\times\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Recall that for any centered Gaussian vector
(Z\,,Z_{1}\,,\dots\,,Z_{m})
,

(3.7)

\Var(Z\mid Z_{1}\,,\dots\,,Z_{m})=\inf_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}}\left\|Z-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}Z_{j}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leqslant\min_{0\leqslant j\leqslant m}\left\|Z-Z_{j}\right\|_{2}^{2},

where
Z_{0}=0
. This
implies that
\Var(Z\mid Z_{1}\,,\dots\,,Z_{m})
is bounded above by

\min\left\{\Var H_{1}(t\,,x)~,~\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\mathrm{E}\left(\left|H_{1}(t\,,x)-H_{1}(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right|^{2}\right)\right\}.

Thus, Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
, and
3.4

together yield the upper bound in (
3.6
).

In order to prove the lower bound in (
3.6
), let
us choose and fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

(t\,,x),(t_{1}\,,x_{1}),\dots,(t_{m}\,,x_{m})\in(0\,,T]\times\mathbb{T}

as well as numbers
a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
, and define

(3.8)

\displaystyle r=(\sqrt{T}\vee 1)^{-1}\min\left\{\sqrt{t}\,,\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\left(|t-t_{j}|^{1/2}\vee|x-x_{j}|\right)\right\}.

Note that
r\leqslant\sqrt{t/T}\leqslant 1
.
We may and will assume that
r>0
, for, otherwise there is nothing to prove.
Thanks to (
3.2
),

\displaystyle{E}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{E}\left(\left|H_{1}(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}H_{1}(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\left|G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}G_{t_{j}-s}(x_{j}\,,y)\mathbb{1}_{[0,t_{j}]}(s)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{m}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}\int_{0}^{t_{j}\wedge t_{k}}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\ G_{t_{j}-s}(x_{j}\,,y)G_{t_{k}-s}(x_{k}\,,y),

where
t_{0}=t
,
x_{0}=x
,
\alpha_{0}=1
, and
\alpha_{j}=-a_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,m
.
Thanks to the semigroup property of the heat kernel,

\displaystyle\int{T}G_{t_{j}-s}(x_{j}\,,y)G_{t_{k}-s}(x_{k}\,,y){\rm d}y

\displaystyle=G_{t_{j}+t_{k}-2s}(x_{j}\,,x_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{t_{j}+t_{k}-2s}(x_{j}-x_{k}-2n).

According to the Poisson summation formula
(Lemma
A.3
),

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{t_{j}+t_{k}-2s}(x_{j}-x_{k}-2n)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x_{k})-\pi^{2}n^{2}(t_{j}+t_{k}-2s)}.

Consequently,

(3.9)

\displaystyle{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{j,k=0}^{m}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x_{k})}\int_{0}^{t_{j}\wedge t_{k}}{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}(t_{j}+t_{k}-2s)}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi inx_{j}}{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}(t_{j}-s)}\mathbb{1}_{[0,t_{j}]}(s)\right|^{2}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=(4\pi)^{-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{m}\alpha_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi inx_{j}}\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t_{j}}}{\pi^{2}n^{2}-i\tau}\right|^{2}{\rm d}\tau\quad\text{(Plancherel)}

\displaystyle=(4\pi)^{-1}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\left({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t}\right)-\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 86.72377pt\left.-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x)}\left({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t_{j}}\right)\right|^{2}\dfrac{{\rm d}\tau}{\pi^{4}n^{4}+\tau^{2}}.

In order to derive a lower bound for (
3.9
), we employ Fourier transforms for functions on
\mathbb{T}
.
Recall that we denote the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{T}
by
\mathcal{F}
in order to distinguish it from the Fourier
transform on
\mathbb{R}
; see (
1.6
) and (
1.7
).

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth, symmetric test function supported in
[-\frac{1}{2}\,,\frac{1}{2}]
with
\psi(0)=1
.
Let us write
\psi_{r}
for its rescaled version

\displaystyle\psi_{r}(y)=\psi(r^{-1}y)\quad\text{for all $y\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Display (

on
\mathbb{R}

We now define a function

\displaystyle\Psi_{r}(x)=\psi_{r}(x)\quad\text{for all $x\in[-1\,,1]$.}

Recall that

and consider the following expression:

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}\Psi_{r}(n)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\ \times

\displaystyle\times\left[\left({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x)}\left({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t_{j}}\right)\right]{\rm e}^{i\tau t}\hat{\psi}_{r^{2}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\psi}_{r}(\pi n)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\ \times

\displaystyle\ \times\left[\left({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x)}\left({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t_{j}}\right)\right]{\rm e}^{i\tau t}\hat{\psi}_{r^{2}}(\tau);

Because

and

In particular, we may appeal to the inverse Fourier transform on

\displaystyle I=2\pi\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}\Psi_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\Big[\left(\psi_{r^{2}}(0)-\psi_{r^{2}}(t){\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x)}\left(\psi_{r^{2}}(t-t_{j})-\psi_{r^{2}}(t){\rm e}^{-\pi^{2}n^{2}t_{j}}\right)\Big].

Firstly, observe that
t/r^{2}\geqslant 1

)
of

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{F}\Psi_{r}(n)\left[1-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}{\rm e}^{\pi in(x_{j}-x)}\psi_{r^{2}}(t-t_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=4\pi\left[\Psi_{r}(0)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}\Psi_{r}(x_{j}-x)\psi_{r^{2}}(t-t_{j})\right],

where we have used (

(3.13)

\displaystyle I=4\pi\Psi_{r}(0)=4\pi.

Our second observation about the quantity

is
the following, which is a consequence of the preceding

\displaystyle|I|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\pi\mathrm{E}\left(\left|H_{1}(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}H_{1}(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right|^{2}\right)\times

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\times\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{\psi}_{r}(\pi n)|^{2}|\hat{\psi}_{r^{2}}(\tau)|^{2}(\pi^{4}n^{4}+\tau^{2})\,{\rm d}\tau.

Since
\hat{\psi}_{s}(a)=s\hat{\psi}(sa)

and

,
and
\hat{\psi}
is a function of rapid decrease, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{\psi}_{r}(\pi n)|^{2}|\hat{\psi}_{r^{2}}(\tau)|^{2}\pi^{4}n^{4}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2}n^{4}}{1+r^{6}n^{6}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{r^{4}{\rm d}\tau}{1+r^{4}\tau^{2}}\lesssim r\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{1+r^{4}\tau^{2}}=r^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{1+\tau^{2}},

where in the second inequality we have used the following estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{4}}{1+r^{6}n^{6}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n\leqslant 1/r}\frac{n^{4}}{1+r^{6}n^{6}}+\sum\limits_{n>1/r}\frac{n^{4}}{1+r^{6}n^{6}}\leqslant r^{-5}+\int_{1/r}^{\infty}\frac{y^{4}}{1+r^{6}y^{6}}{\rm d}y\asymp r^{-5}.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{\psi}_{r}(\pi n)|^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{\psi}_{r^{2}}(\tau)|^{2}\tau^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau=2\int{T}{\rm d}z\,|\Psi_{r}(z)|^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\hat{\psi}_{r^{2}}(\tau)|^{2}\tau^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\,|\psi_{r}(y)|^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{r^{4}\tau^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau}{1+r^{8}\tau^{4}}=r^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}y\,|\psi(y)|^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau}{1+\tau^{4}},

where the implied constants in the preceding two display

\displaystyle|I|^{2}\lesssim\mathrm{E}\left(\left|H_{1}(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}H_{1}(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right|^{2}\right)\times r^{-1},

where the implied constant depends only on

Finally, we combine (

\displaystyle\Var(H_{1}(t\,,x)

\displaystyle\mid H_{1}(t_{1}\,,x_{1}),\dots,H_{1}(t_{m}\,,x_{m}))

\displaystyle=\inf_{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{E}\left(\left|H_{1}(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}H_{1}(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right|^{2}\right)\gtrsim r,

∎

We will need to consider more general initial data than constants: Let us say that

3

3

We will tacitly

In any case, there is no problem with existence, regularity, etc., and the solution

\begin{split}u_{i}(t\,,x)&=(\mathcal{G}_{t}h_{i})(x)+\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)b_{i}(u(s\,,y))\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\\

The primary goal of this section is to prove that when

approximately that of a certain

When

[
4
]

and Walsh
[
22
]
, and many subsequent papers. Here, we prove improvements

and will appear in essentially-optimal form,
have essentially-optimal error-rate estimates, and possess optimal assumptions on the diffusion

For the following two lemmas, it might help to recall that

,

Lemma 4.1
.

Choose and fix some
T>0
. Then, the following is valid

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\|u(t\,,x)\|_{k}\lesssim\mathcal{S}_{k}(h)+\mathcal{M}(b)\sqrt{t}\left(t^{1/4}\vee t^{1/2}\right)+\mathcal{M}(\sigma)\sqrt{k}\left(t^{1/4}\vee t^{1/2}\right),

where the implied constant does not depend on
\sigma
,
h
,

t\in[0\,,T]
, or
k\in[2\,,\infty)

Proof.

The lemma has content
iff
\mathcal{S}_{k}(h)\vee\mathcal{M}(b)\vee\mathcal{M}(\sigma)<\infty
,
a condition which we assume.
Because
\|(\mathcal{G}h)(x)\|_{k}\leqslant\mathcal{S}_{k}(h)
,
the Minkowski inequality, Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, and a
suitable application of the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality
(Lemma
A.4

\|u(t\,,x)\|_{k}
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{k}(h)+\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}\|G_{t-s}(x\,,y)b(u(s\,,y))\|_{k}\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 130.08621pt+\left\|\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\sigma(u(s\,,y))\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\right\|_{k}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{S}_{k}(h)+\mathcal{M}(b)\sqrt{2t}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\,[G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 130.08621pt+\mathcal{M}(\sigma)\sqrt{4kp}\left[\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{k}(h)+\mathcal{M}(b)\sqrt{2t}\left[\int_{0}^{t}G_{2s}(0\,,0)\,{\rm d}s\right]^{1/2}+\mathcal{M}(\sigma)\sqrt{4kp}\left[\int_{0}^{t}G_{2s}(0\,,0)\,{\rm d}s\right]^{1/2},

thanks to the semigroup property of the heat kernel. In particular, (
3.4
) yields

∎

Lemma 4.2

For every
T>0

\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,z)\|_{k}\lesssim\mathcal{H}_{k}(h)\sqrt{|x-z|}+\left(\mathcal{M}(b)+\mathcal{M}(\sigma)\sqrt{k}\right)\left[t^{1/4}\wedge\sqrt{|x-z|}\right],

Recall (

).
Without loss of generality, we assume

Minkowski’s inequality shows that, for every

,

\displaystyle\|u(t\,,x)-u(t\,,z)\|_{k}

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\left[\sigma(u(s\,,y))-\sigma(h(x))\right]\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim k[\mathcal{H}_{k}(h)\vee\mathcal{M}(b)\vee\mathcal{M}(\sigma)]^{2}\left\{\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int{T}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\left[k\sqrt{s}+|y-x|\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim[\mathcal{H}_{k}(h)\vee\mathcal{M}(b)\vee\mathcal{M}(\sigma)]^{2}k^{2}t,

thanks to Lemma
3.3
, where the parameter dependencies are
as in the statement of the lemma. This concludes the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

5.
Sufficient conditions for uniform dimension

The primary goal of this section is to present results that streamline the process of establishing
uniform dimension theorems for space-time random fields.

Lemma 5.1
.

Consider an
\mathbb{R}^{p}
-valued random field
\{Z(s\,,y)\}_{(s,y)\in[S,T]\times\mathbb{T}}
,
where
T>S\geqslant 0
. Choose and fix some number
\alpha\in(0\,,1]
. For every

n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in(0\,,1)
,
y\in\mathbb{T}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, and
r>0
, define

(5.1)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,r))=\#\{y\in F_{n}^{\delta}:Z(s\,,y)\in B(\nu\,,r)\},\\
&\text{where}\quad F_{n}^{\delta}=\mathbb{T}\cap\{j2^{-n(1+\delta)/\alpha}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}.\end{split}

Recall that
B(\nu\,,r)
is the closed ball defined in (
1.8
).
Suppose the following two conditions hold:

(i)

For every
\varepsilon\in(0\,,1),\mathrm{P}\{Z(s)\in C^{\alpha(1-\varepsilon)}(\mathbb{T})\,\forall s\in[S\,,T]\}=1
;

(ii)

For every
R>0
,

\lim_{\delta\to 0+}\limsup_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\sup_{s\in[S,T]}\sup_{\nu\in B(0,R)}\log\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n}))=0\ \hbox{ a.s.}

Then,
\mathrm{P}\{\dimh Z(\{s\}\times F)=\alpha^{-1}\dimh F\ \forall\text{compact }F\subset\mathbb{T},\,s\in[S\,,T]\}=1
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.6
,
(i)
implies that

\dimh Z(\{s\}\times F)\leqslant\alpha^{-1}\dimh F
for all compact
F
and
s\in[S\,,T]
,
all valid off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set.
In the remainder of the proof, we aim to show that, off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set,

(5.2)

\dimh Z(\{s\}\times F)\geqslant\alpha^{-1}\dimh F\quad\forall\text{compact $F\subset\mathbb{T},\ s\in[S\,,T]$.}

Choose and fix an arbitrary
R>0
and observe that
taking
F=Z(s)^{-1}(A)=\{y\in\mathbb{T}:\,Z(s\,,y)\in A\}
in (
5.2
)
yields the following equivalent formulation:
Off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set,

(5.3)

\dimh Z(s)^{-1}(A)\leqslant\alpha\dimh A\quad\forall\text{compact $A\subset B(0\,,R),\,s\in[S\,,T]$.}

In order to establish (
5.3
), we fix
\delta\in(0\,,1)

and use the condition of part
(ii)
in order to choose a
\mathrm{P}
-null set
\Omega_{0}
off which

(5.4)

\adjustlimits{\sup}_{s\in[S,T]}{\max}_{\nu\in B(0,R)}\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n}))=\mathcal{O}(2^{c_{\delta}n})\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$},

where
c_{\delta}\to 0
as
\delta\to 0
.
Also, we can find a
\mathrm{P}
-null set
\Omega_{1}
off which
the condition of part
(i)
holds for

(5.5)

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta)=1-\frac{1+(\delta/2)}{1+\delta}.

Now that we have identified the null set
\Omega_{2}:=\Omega_{0}\cup\Omega_{1}

(which depends on
\delta
),
we work pathwise, nonprobabilistically, from here on in order
to conclude the proof of (
5.3
), and hence the lemma.

To this end, we deduce from the definition of Hausdorff dimension that
for every Borel set
A\subset B(0\,,R)
and for all
\kappa>\dimh A

and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find

\nu_{1,n},\nu_{2,n},\ldots\in B(0\,,R)
and
r_{1,n},r_{2,n},\ldots\in(0\,,2^{-n})
such that

A\subset\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n}\,,r_{i,n})
– see (
1.8
) –
and

(5.6)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{i,n}^{\kappa}<\infty.

Since
\{\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n}\,,r_{i,n})\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is an open cover of

A
,
\{Z(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n}\,,r_{i,n}))\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

is an open cover of
Z(s)^{-1}(A)
for every
s\in[S\,,T]
.

We observe that, off
\Omega_{2}
, there exists a random number

C>0
and a non-random number
C_{0}>0

such that for all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every inverse image
of the form

Z(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n}\,,r_{i,n}))
lies
in not more than
Cr_{i,n}^{-c_{\delta}}
-many intervals of length
C_{0}r_{i,n}^{(1+\delta)/\alpha}
.
In order to see this, consider a large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and
let
y\in Z(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n},r_{i,n}))
, where

2^{-m-1}\leqslant r_{i,n}\leqslant 2^{-m}
for some
m\geqslant n
.
Then, the point
y
is contained in an interval of side length

2^{-(m-1)(1+\delta)/\alpha}[\leqslant C_{0}r_{i,n}^{(1+\delta)/\alpha}\,]

centered at a point
y^{*}\in F_{m-1}^{\delta}
that is nearest to
y
.
We see that the center
y^{*}
of this interval must belong to

Z(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n},2^{-m+1}))\cap F_{m-1}^{\delta}

uniformly for all large
m
.
This is because the choice of
\varepsilon
in (
5.5
) together with the triangle
inequality implies that

\displaystyle\|Z(s\,,y^{*})-\nu_{i,n}\|\leqslant\|Z(s\,,y)-\nu_{i,n}\|+\|Z(s\,,y^{*})-Z(s\,,y)\|

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim 2^{-m}+(2^{-(m-1)(1+\delta)/\alpha})^{\alpha(1-\varepsilon)}\leqslant 2^{-m}+2^{-(1+\delta/2)(m-1)}\leqslant 2^{-m+1},

uniformly for all sufficiently large
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and where
the implied constant does not depend on any of the parameters that arise. According
to (
5.4
),

\#[Z(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n},2^{-m+1}))\cap F_{m-1}^{\delta}]\leqslant Cr_{i,n}^{-c_{\delta}}\quad\forall n\text{ large enough}.

This shows that, when
n
is sufficiently large,

Z(s)^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(\nu_{i,n}\,,r_{i,n}))
lies in not more than

Cr_{i,n}^{-c_{\delta}}
-many intervals of side length

C_{0}r_{i,n}^{(1+\delta)/\alpha}
, valid uniformly for all
s\in[S\,,T]
.

Next, choose and fix
\theta>0
to see that, off
\Omega_{2}
,
the
\theta
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
Z(s)^{-1}(A)

is at most

C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}r_{i,n}^{\theta(1+\delta)/\alpha-c_{\delta}},

regardless of
the value of
n
, the choice of the Borel set

A\subset B(0\,,R)
, or the value of
s\in[S\,,T]
.
Thanks to (
5.6
), the preceding sum converges
uniformly in
n
provided that

\theta(1+\delta)/\alpha-c_{\delta}>\kappa
.
Since
\kappa>\dimh A
is arbitrary, this proves that,
off
\Omega_{2}
,

\dimh Z(s)^{-1}(A)\leqslant\frac{\alpha(\dimh A+c_{\delta})}{1+\delta},

simultaneously for all compact
A\subset B(0\,,R)
and
s\in[S\,,T]
.
Let
\delta\downarrow 0
along a rational
sequence in order to deduce that (
5.3
) holds off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set.
Since
R>0
is arbitrary, this completes the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
∎

The next lemma provides a sufficient condition for
part
(ii)
of the previous lemma to be applicable.

Lemma 5.2
.

Choose and fix some
T>S\geqslant 0
,
and let
\{Z(s\,,y)\}_{(s,y)\in[S,T]\times\mathbb{T}}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{p}
-valued
random field. Recall (
1.10
) and (
5.1
) with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
,
and suppose that:

(i)

The following is finite for some
c,\,\gamma>0
:

\mathcal{E}_{0}:=\mathrm{E}\left[\exp\left(c\sup\left|\frac{\|Z(s\,,y)-Z(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|}{\rho((s\,,y)\,,(s^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\sqrt{\log_{+}(1/\rho((s\,,y)\,,(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})))}}\right|^{\gamma}\right)\right],

where the sup is over all distinct points

(s\,,y),(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in[S\,,T]\times\mathbb{T}
;

(ii)

For every
\delta\in(0\,,1)
, there exist numbers
a,\kappa>0
such that

\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n}))\geqslant 2^{an}\right\}\leqslant 2^{-\kappa n^{2}},

uniformly for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in[S\,,T]
and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Then, for every
\delta\in(0\,,1)
and
R>0
, there exist

K,L>0
such that

\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{s\in[S,T]}\sup_{\nu\in B(0,R)}\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n}))\geqslant K2^{an}\right\}\leqslant L^{n}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/L},

uniformly for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix
\delta\in(0\,,1)
and
R>0
.
Define

A(n)=\left\{\frac{j}{2^{4n(1+\delta)}}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad D(n)=\left\{\frac{\ell}{2^{2n}\sqrt{p}}:\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}\right\}.

First, note that there is a number
K>0
, depending only on

\delta
, such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
y^{*}\in F_{n-1}^{\delta}
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\#\left(B(y^{*},2^{-2(n-1)(1+\delta)})\cap F_{n}^{\delta}\right)\leqslant K.

We can observe that the following holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in[S,T],\\
\nu\in B(0,R)\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n}))\geqslant K2^{an},\sup_{s,s^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}}\|Z(s\,,y)-Z(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|\leqslant 2^{-n-1}\right\}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\max}_{s\in A(n)\cap[S,T]}{\max}_{\nu\in D(n)\cap B(0,R)}\mathcal{N}_{n-1}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n+1}))\geqslant 2^{an}\right\},

where
\sup_{s,s^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}}
denotes, here and throughout the proof,
the sup operator over all
(s\,,y),(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in[S\,,T]\times\mathbb{T}

that satisfy
|s-s^{\prime}|\leqslant 2^{-4n(1+\delta)}
and
|y-y^{\prime}|\leqslant 16\times 2^{-2n(1+\delta)}
. In order to see why (
5.8
)
is true, let us
suppose that there exist
s\in[0\,,T]
,
\nu\in B(0\,,R)

and
y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\in F_{n}^{\delta}

with
\#\{y_{1},\dots,y_{m}\}=m\geqslant K2^{an}
such that

\|Z(s\,,y_{i})-\nu\|\leqslant 2^{-n}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
, and that

\sup_{s,s^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}}\|Z(s\,,y)-Z(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|\leqslant 2^{-n-1}.

Then, we can choose nearest neighbors
s^{*}\in A(n)\cap[S\,,T]
,

\nu^{*}\in D(n)\cap B(0\,,R)
and
y_{1}^{*},\dots,y_{m}^{*}\in F_{n-1}^{\delta}

that respectively satisfy

|s^{*}-s|\leqslant 2^{-4n(1+\delta)}
,
\|\nu^{*}-\nu\|\leqslant 2^{-2n}

and
|y_{i}^{*}-y_{i}|\leqslant 2^{-2(n-1)(1+\delta)}[\leqslant 16\times 2^{-2n(1+\delta)}]
,
in order to see that

\displaystyle\|Z(s^{*},y_{i}^{*})-\nu^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|Z(s^{*},y_{i}^{*})-Z(s\,,y_{i})\|+\|Z(s\,,y_{i})-\nu\|+\|\nu-\nu^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{-n-1}+2^{-n}+2^{-2n}\leqslant 2^{-n+1}.

By (
5.7
),
\#\{y_{1}^{*},\dots,y_{m}^{*}\}\geqslant m/K
, whence it follows that

\mathcal{N}_{n-1}^{\delta}(s^{*},B(\nu^{*},2^{-n+1}))
is at least

\#\{y_{1}^{*},\dots,y_{m}^{*}\}\geqslant m/K\geqslant 2^{an}.

This proves (
5.8
).

Next, condition
(ii)

together with a simple union bound yields the following, valid uniformly for
all
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(5.9)

\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in A(n)\cap[S,T]\\
\nu\in D(n)\cap B(0,R)\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}_{n-1}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n+1}))\geqslant 2^{an}\right\}\leqslant L_{1}^{n}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/L_{1}},

for a constant
L_{1}>0
that depends only on
(p\,,S\,,T\,,\delta\,,R\,,\kappa)
.

Now, we can put together (
5.9
) and (
5.8
)
in order to conclude that

(5.10)

\mathrm{P}\left\{\adjustlimits{\sup}_{s\in[S,T]}{\sup}_{\nu\in B(0,R)}\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(s\,,B(\nu\,,2^{-n}))\geqslant K2^{an}\right\}\leqslant L_{1}^{n}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/L_{1}}+{P}_{n},

where
{P}_{n}=\mathrm{P}\{\sup_{s,s^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}}\|Z(s\,,y)-Z(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|>2^{-n-1}\}
.
Recall the metric
\rho
from (
1.10
) and observe that

\sup_{s,s^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}}\rho((s\,,y)\,,(s^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\leqslant 5\times 2^{-n(1+\delta)}.

Therefore,
condition
(i)
and Chebyshev’s inequality
together imply that there exist
L_{2}=L_{2}(\delta)>0
and
L_{3}>0
that
depend only on
(c\,,\gamma\,,\mathcal{E}_{0}\,,\delta\,,L_{2})
and satisfy

\displaystyle{P}_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(s,y),(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in[S,T]\times\mathbb{T}\\
(s,y)\neq(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\end{subarray}}\frac{\|Z(s\,,y)-Z(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|}{\rho((s\,,y)\,,(s^{\prime},y^{\prime}))\sqrt{\log_{+}(1/\rho((s\,,y)\,,(s^{\prime},y^{\prime})))}}\geqslant\frac{2^{\delta n}}{\sqrt{L_{2}n}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{0}\exp\left(-c\left[\frac{2^{\delta n}}{\sqrt{L_{2}n}}\right]^{\gamma}\,\right)\leqslant L_{3}^{n}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/L_{3}},

uniformly for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Combine this with (
5.10
) to conclude the proof.
∎

Z(\{s\}\times F)

and compact sets
F\subset\mathbb{T}
.
We now turn to sufficient conditions for deriving

;

1.11
) and the subsequent paragraph. In fact, we state

Lemma 5.3
.

Consider an
\mathbb{R}^{p}
-valued random field
\{Z(s)\}_{s\in I}

I\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a non-random, compact, upright
rectangle. Choose and fix
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{N}\in(0\,,1]
, and
metrize
I
using

\mathsf{d}(s\,,s^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}|s_{j}-s^{\prime}_{j}|^{\alpha_{j}}

for all
s,s^{\prime}\in I.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in(0\,,1)
,
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
and

, define

(5.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(\nu\,,r)=\#\{s\in F_{n}^{\delta}:Z(s)\in B(\nu\,,r)\},\text{ where}\\
&F_{n}^{\delta}=I\cap\bigcup_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}}\left\{(j_{1}2^{-(1+\delta)n/\alpha_{1}},\dots,j_{N}2^{-(1+\delta)n/\alpha_{N}})\right\},\end{split}

(i)

For every
\varepsilon\in(0\,,1)
,

\sup_{s,s^{\prime}\in I:s\neq s^{\prime}}\|Z(s)-Z(s^{\prime})\|/\mathsf{d}(s\,,s^{\prime})^{1-\varepsilon}<\infty;

,

\mathrm{P}
-null set off which

\dimh Z(G)=\dim_{{}_{\rm H}}^{\rho}G
,
uniformly for all compact sets
G\subset I
.

The proof of the above lemma requires making only minor adaptations to that of Lemma
5.2

and therefore is left to the interested reader.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\{Z(s)\}_{s\in I}
be an
\mathbb{R}^{p}
-valued random field, where

I\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a non-random, compact, upright rectangle.

\mathsf{d}
,
\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(\nu\,,r)

(i)

The following is finite for some

\delta\in(0\,,1)

\displaystyle\hskip 57.81621pt\leqslant L_{1}^{n}{\rm e}^{-n^{2}/L_{1}}+\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{s,s^{\prime}\in I:\mathsf{d}(s,s^{\prime})\leqslant 2^{-n(1+\delta)}}\|Z(s)-Z(s^{\prime})\|>2^{-n-1}\right\},

6.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2.1
; this is our broadest
uniform dimension result, available when the noise term in (
1.1
) is additive.

The proof is divided in two parts.
In the first part, we prove Theorem
2.1
in the special case that
b\equiv 0
.
In the second part, we extend the result to general non-random
Lipschitz continuous functions
b:\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

Part 1.
Suppose
b\equiv 0
, and
\sigma
is a constant nonsingular
p\times p
matrix.
We may write

(6.1)

\displaystyle u(t\,,x)=(\mathcal{G}_{t}u_{0})(x)+\sigma H(t\,,x),

where
H
is the solution to (
3.1
) which is a centered Gaussian random field with i.i.d. coordinate processes.

Proof of (i).
Suppose
p\geqslant 2
. In order to prove
(i)

let us fix
0<S<T<\infty
, and set

Z(t\,,x)=u(t\,,x)
for
(t\,,x)\in[S\,,T]\times\mathbb{T}
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in Lemma
5.1
.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\delta}(t\,,B(\nu\,,r))
and
F_{n}^{\delta}
be defined by
(
5.1
) with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, and define
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{\delta}(t\,,B(\nu\,,r))

to be the total number of all
n
-tuples of all

x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}
in
F_{n}^{\delta}
such that
u(t\,,x_{i})\in B(\nu\,,r)
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
;
that is,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{n}^{\delta}(t\,,B(\nu\,,r))=\mathop{\sum\cdots\sum}\limits_{x_{1}<\cdots<x_{n}\,\text{in}\,F_{n}^{\delta}}\mathbb{1}_{\{\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\|u(t,x_{i})-\nu\|\leqslant r\}}.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in(0\,,1)
,
t\in[S\,,T]
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we have

{\rm d}_{r}(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\left\{\|x-y\|:\,y\in\partial\Lambda(r)\right\}&\text{if $x\in\Lambda(r)$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The function
{\rm d}_{r}
is Lipschitz continuous whenever
\partial\Lambda(r)\neq\varnothing

(see Lemma
A.1
below), and this is the case when
r>0
is sufficiently small.
In this case,
\sigma_{r}
is Lipschitz continuous.
Next, set

(7.16)

\sigma_{r,N}(v)=\begin{cases}\sigma_{r}(v)&\text{if $\|v\|\leqslant N$},\\
\sigma_{r}(vN/\|v\|)&\text{if $\|v\|>N$}.\end{cases}

As in (
6.8
),

\lip(\sigma_{r,N})\leqslant\lip(\sigma_{r})
for all
r,N>0.

Therefore,
\sigma_{r,N}
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous provided that
r\ll 1
and
N\gg 1
.

Let
\lambda(v\,;r)
and
\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)
respectively
denote the smallest singular value of
\sigma_{r}
and
\sigma_{r,N}
. That is,

\lambda(v\,;r)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\|x\|=1}\|\sigma_{r}(v)x\|^{2},\qquad\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:\|x\|=1}\|\sigma_{r,N}(v)x\|^{2}.

Thanks to (
7.15
) and (
7.16
),

\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)=\begin{cases}\lambda(v)&\text{if $v\in B(0\,,N)\setminus\Lambda(r)$},\\
\lambda(v)+{\rm d}_{r}(v)^{2}&\text{if $v\in B(0\,,N)\cap\Lambda(r)$},\\
\lambda(vN/\|v\|\,;r)&\text{if $\|v\|>N$}.\end{cases}

Apply Lemma
2.5
with
\sigma
replaced by
\sigma_{r,N}

to see that
v\mapsto\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)
is continuous. By virtue of its
construction,
\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)>0
everywhere in
B(0\,,N)
. Since

\inf_{\|v\|>N}\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)=\inf_{\|v\|=N}\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)
,
compactness yields

(7.17)

\inf_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)=\inf_{v\in B(0,N)}\lambda(v\,;N\,,r)>0.

With the above observations in place, let us write
u_{r,N}
for the solution to
(
1.1
) where
b
is replaced by
b_{N}
and
\sigma
is replaced by
\sigma_{r,N}
.
Since
\mathcal{M}(b_{N})=\sup_{\|v\|\leqslant N}\|b(v)\|<\infty
and

\mathcal{M}(\sigma_{r,N})=\sup_{\|v\|\leqslant N}\|\sigma_{r}(v)\|<\infty
, and because of
(
7.17
), we may apply Part 1 of this proof to
u_{r,N}
in place of
u
to see that

\mathrm{P}\left\{\dimh u_{r,N}(\{t\}\times F)=2\dimh F\ \forall\text{compact $F\subset\mathbb{T},\ t>0$}\right\}=1.

Let

T_{r,N}=\inf\left\{t>0:\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\|u(t\,,x)\|\geqslant N\right\}\wedge\inf\left\{t>0:\,\lambda(u(t\,,x))\leqslant r\right\},

where
\inf\varnothing=\infty
.
Every
T_{r,N}
is a stopping time
with respect to the filtration
\{{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
of the noise
\xi
, and (
7.16
)
and the uniqueness of the solution to (
1.1
) together imply that

T_{r,N}=\inf\left\{t>0:\,\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\|u_{r,N}(t\,,x)\|\geqslant N\right\}\wedge\inf\left\{t>0:\,\lambda(u_{r,N}(t\,,x))\leqslant r\right\},

and
\mathrm{P}\{u_{r,N}(t)=u(t)\text{ for all $t<T_{r,N}$}\}=1
. Also,
off a single
\mathrm{P}
-null set,

(7.18)

\dimh u(\{t\}\times F)=2\dimh F\quad\forall\text{compact $F\subset\mathbb{T},\ t\in(0\,,T_{r,N})$}.

Because
u
is bounded on space-time compacta and
\{\lambda=0\}
is polar for
u

[Assumption
2.3
],

\lim_{N\to\infty,r\to 0}\mathrm{P}\{T_{r,N}>t\}=1
for every
t>0
.
Therefore, (
7.18
) implies the result and
concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A.1
.

Let

be a nonempty subset of

Interchange the roles of

and

to conclude.

The following is a multidimensional version of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy

Lemma A.4

(BDG inequality)
.

Whenever
Z=\{Z(s\,,y)\}_{s>0,y\in\mathbb{T}}
is a predictable random field
with values in the space of
p\times p
matrices,

\left\|\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)Z(s\,,y)\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\right\|_{k}^{2}\leqslant 4kp\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\left\|Z(s\,,y)\right\|_{k}^{2},

for every
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{T}
, and
k\in[2\,,\infty)
.

Proof.

There is nothing to prove when
\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\|Z(s\,,y)\|_{k}^{2}
is infinite. Therefore, we assume throughout that the integral is finite. Because
of that fact it follows that

M_{0}=0,\quad M_{t}=\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{T-s}(x\,,y)\sum_{j=1}^{p}Z_{i,j}(s\,,y)\,\xi_{j}({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\qquad[0<t\leqslant T]

defines a continuous
L^{2}
-martingale for every
T>0
.
If
X
is random variable with values in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
, then

(A.1)

\|X\|_{k}^{2}=\left[\mathrm{E}\left(\|X\|^{k}\right)\right]^{2/k}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\||X_{i}|^{2}\right\|_{k/2}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\|X_{i}\|_{k}^{2}.

For every
i=1,\ldots,p
, the quadratic variation of
\{M_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}

is

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{T-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{p}|Z_{i,j}(s\,,y)|^{2}\qquad\forall t\in(0\,,T].

Therefore, we apply (
A.1
) and the BDG inequality for stochastic convolutions

[
15
]
Prop. 4.4 with
t=T
to see that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)Z(s\,,y)\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\|\int_{(0,t)\times\mathbb{T}}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\sum_{j=1}^{p}Z_{i,j}(s\,,y)\,\xi_{j}({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\right\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leqslant 4k\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{p}|Z_{i,j}(s\,,y)|^{2}\right\|_{k/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leqslant 4k\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{p}|Z_{i,j}(s\,,y)|^{2}\right\|_{k/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\leqslant 4kp\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\rm d}y\ [G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\left\|\|Z(s\,,y)\|^{2}\right\|_{k/2}.

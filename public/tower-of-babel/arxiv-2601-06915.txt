Tristán Radić

Abstract
. Extending recent developments of Kra, Moreira, Richter and Roberson, we study infinite sumset patterns in U k ​ ( Φ ) U^{k}(\Phi) -uniform subsets of the integers, defined via the local uniformity seminorms introduced by Host and Kra. The main result relates the degree k k of a U k ​ ( Φ ) U^{k}(\Phi) -uniform set to the existence of sumset patterns along prescribed vertices of ℓ \ell -dimensional parallelepipeds, for k ≤ ℓ k\leq\ell . The proof relies on a dynamical analysis of return-time sets to neighborhoods of points lying over pronilfactor fibers. We then derive higher-order parity obstructions for sumset patterns and consequences in topological dynamics.



1.
Introduction

Szemerédi’s Theorem asserts that any subset of the natural numbers with positive density contains arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions. Different proofs of this theorem have played a central role in the development of additive combinatorics. In particular, since the introduction of Gowers norms in his proof of Szemerédi’s Theorem
[
35
]
,
uniform sets
and related versions of
pseudo-random sets
have been useful for finding finite patterns in subsets of the integers. For example, Gowers norms were used by Green and Tao to find arbitrarily long arithmetic progression in the set of prime numbers
[
37
]
and Green, Tao and Ziegler established the connection to correlations with nilsequences by proving the related inverse theorem
[
36
]
.

The ergodic counterpart,
Host-Kra seminorms
defined for measure preserving systems and the structure theory of nilfactors started in
[
44
]
have led to new recurrence properties, see for instance
[
8
,
17
,
24
,
25
,
42
]
. These recurrence results have combinatorial consequences via the Furstenberg correspondence principle that was originally introduced in
[
27
]
to also provide an alternative proof of Szemerédi’s Theorem.

More recently, the theory was used by Kra, Moreira, Richter and Roberson in
[
54
,
53
,
52
]
, to established a series of infinite sumsets patterns in sets with positive density. This has led to numerous other related sumset results, see
[
2
,
3
,
1
,
14
,
15
,
41
,
48
,
51
,
50
]
.
In
[
52
]
, it is showed that for set of positive density
A\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, there exist an infinite set
B\subset{\mathbb{N}}
and an integer

such that

(1)

B\cup B^{\oplus 2}\cup\cdots\cup B^{\oplus k}\subset A-t,

where for
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
B^{\oplus\ell}=\{b_{1}+\cdots+b_{\ell}\mid b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell}\in B,b_{1}<\cdots<b_{\ell}\}
.

Due to parity obstructions, the shift

is needed in (

), take for instance the set of odd numbers. Similarly, under the same assumptions, it is proven in

that there exist infinite sets

B_{1}\cup(B_{1}+B_{2})\cup\cdots\cup(B_{1}+\cdots+B_{k})\subset A

However, already for the case

-uniform sets contain a rich variety of such sumset patterns and by showing higher-order parity obstructions arising from nilsystems.

2.1.
Dynamical systems

A
topological dynamical system
is a pair
(X,T)
where
X
is a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
is a homeomorphism. A point
a\in X
is
transitive
if
\overline{\{T^{n}a\mid n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}=X
. The system
(X,T)
is
transitive
if it has a transitive point and
(X,T)
is
minimal
if every point is transitive.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}(X)
be the set of Borel probability measures of
X
and
{\mathcal{M}}(X,T)\subset{\mathcal{M}}(X)
be the set of
T
-invariant Borel probability measures, that is if
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(X,T)
then
\mu(A)=\mu(T^{-1}A)
for all Borel sets
A\subset X
. We say that
(X,\mu,T)
is a
measure preserving system
if
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system and
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(X,T)
. With this definition all our measure preserving system have an underlying topological structure, and this is necessary for the sumset results.
We say that
(X,\mu,T)
is ergodic (or simply
\mu
is ergodic) if whenever a measurable set
A\subset X
satisfies
\mu(A\Delta T^{-1}A)=0
then
\mu(A)=0
or
1
.

We also consider other group actions, specifically
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
-actions that we denote
(X,\mu,

T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
and cubic group actions (see
Section

2.4
). The previous definitions are extended naturally, for more detail refer to
[
32
]
.

A
Følner sequence

\Phi=(\Phi_{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a sequence of finite subsets of the natural numbers for which
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{|\Phi_{N}\Delta(\Phi_{N}+t)|}{|\Phi_{N}|}=0
for every
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
(X,\mu,T)
is a measure preserving system, we say that a point
a\in X
is
generic along a Følner sequence

\Phi
, denoted
a\in\operatorname{gen}(\mu,\Phi)
if for all continuous functions
f\in C(X)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}f(T^{n}a)=\int_{X}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu.

By the mean ergodic theorem the set of generic points along a given Følner sequence has measure one whenever
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(X,T)
is ergodic. Also, if for some point
a\in X
,
\operatorname{supp}\mu\subset\overline{\{T^{n}a\mid n\in{\mathbb{N}}\}}
and
\mu
is ergodic, then there exists a Følner sequence
\Phi
such that
a\in\operatorname{gen}(\mu,\Phi)
.

For a Borel map
\theta\colon X\to Y
between topological spaces, if
\mu\in{\mathcal{M}}(X)
is a Borel probability measure, then
the pushforward measure

\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}(Y)
denoted
\nu=\theta\mu
is given by
\nu(A)=\mu(\theta^{-1}(A))
for all measurable sets
A\subset Y
. For two measure preserving systems
(X,\mu,T)
and
(Y,\nu,S)
, we say that
(Y,\nu,S)
is a
factor
of
(X,\mu,T)
if there exists a measurable map
\pi\colon X\to Y
, called a
factor map
, such that
\pi\mu=\nu
and
\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi
, up to null-sets. Abusing notation sometimes we just say that
Y
is a factor of
X
, we also say
X
is an
extension
of
Y
.
If the factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
is continuous, surjective and
(\pi\circ T)(x)=(S\circ\pi)(x)
for all
x\in X
, we say that
\pi
is a
continuous factor map
,
Y
is a
topological factor
of
X
. We abuse notation and use the same letter
T
for the transformation in
X
and in its factor
Y
.

For a factor map
\pi:X\to Y
and a function
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,

\mathbb{E}(f|Y)
denotes the conditional expectation

\mathbb{E}(f|\pi^{-1}\mathcal{B}(Y))
, where
\mathcal{B}(Y)
is the Borel

\sigma
-algebra on
Y
. Notice that
\mu
-almost surely,
\mathbb{E}(f|Y)(x)=\mathbb{E}(f|Y)(x^{\prime})
whenever
\pi(x)=\pi(x^{\prime})=y
, so abusing notation we also write
\mathbb{E}(f|Y)(y)
and we think
\mathbb{E}(f|Y)
as a function in
L^{2}(\nu)
.

Consider a sequence of measure preserving systems
(X_{j},\mu_{j},T)_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for which there is a factor map
\theta_{j}:X_{j+1}\to X_{j}
for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
(X,\mu,T)
is the
inverse limit
of this sequence if for each
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a factor map
\pi_{j}\colon X\to X_{j}
such that

•

\pi_{j+1}=\theta_{j}\circ\pi_{j}
, for every
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

•

\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\{f\circ\pi_{i}:f\in L^{2}(\mu_{i})\}
is dense in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

A similar definition applies for topological dynamical systems (replacing factor maps by continuous factor maps and
f\in L^{2}(\mu_{j})
by
f\in C(X_{j})
).

2.2.
Pronilfactors and Host-Kra seminorms

A
k
-
step

nilmanifold

X=G/\Gamma
is the quotient space of a
k
-step nilpotent Lie group
G
with
\Gamma
a co-compact discrete subgroup. We endow
G/\Gamma
with its Haar measure
m_{X}
and let
T\colon X\to X
be the left translation by a fixed element
\tau\in G
. In that case we say that
(X,m_{X},T)
is a
k
-
step nilsystem
. We can generalize it to
(X,m_{X},T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
where
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}\in G
commute and
T_{i}\colon X\to X
are their respective left translation maps. Without loss of generality, we always suppose that
G
is generated by the connected component of the identity
G^{0}
and the points
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{d}
.

An inverse limit of
k
-step nilsystems is called a
k
-step
pronilsystem
. An
\infty
-step
pronilsystem
is
an inverse limit of a sequence
\left((Z_{k},m_{k},T)\right)_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, where for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
(Z_{k},m_{k},T)
is a
k
-step pronilsystem. We say that a system is a pronilsystem if it is a
k
-step pronilsystem for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
. Every pronilsystem is
distal
, that is, for any
x,y\in X
such that
\inf_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}d(T^{n}x,T^{n}y)=0
, then
x=y
. Any topological factor map from a minimal distal system is an open map, see
[
7
]
.

Pronilsystems
(X,\mu,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
have the useful property that the topological notions of transitivity, minimality and unique ergodicity are equivalent. Moreover, any of those topological properties are equivalent to be ergodic and
\operatorname{supp}\mu=X
. If
(X,T_{1},\ldots,T_{d})
is a pronilsystem (non necessarily minimal), then for any
x\in X
,
\overline{\{T_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots T_{d}^{n_{d}}x\mid(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\}}\subset X
is a minimal and uniquely ergodic system, see
[
56
]
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
be an ergodic measure preserving system. For every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
,
(X,\mu,T)
admits a maximal factor isomorphic to a
k
-step pronilsystem, called the
k
-step pronilfactor
. We denote this factor
Z_{k}(X)
,
Z_{k}(\mu)
or just
Z_{k}
depending on the context. In what follows, we always consider the topological model of

Z_{k}
which is a

k
-step pronilsystem. For
k=1
, this factor is also known as the
Kronecker factor
and can be characterized as the factor spanned by the eigenvalues of
(X,\mu,T)
, that is the functions
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
such that
Tf=\lambda f
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. An ergodic system
(X,\mu,T)
is
weak mixing
if
\lambda=1
is the unique eigenvalue. If
(X,\mu,T)
is weak mixing then
Z_{k}(\mu)
is the trivial factor for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

For a measure preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
,
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, the
k
-th Host-Kra seminorm of

f
,
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}
, is defined inductively as follows:

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{0}(X,\mu,T)}=\int_{X}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and}

(4)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(X,\mu,T)}^{2^{k+1}}=\lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{1}{H}\sum_{h\leq H}\left\lVert f\cdot\overline{T^{h}f}\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}^{2^{k}}.

The function
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}
is a seminorm when
k\geq 1
. The fact that the limit in (
4
) exists is proved in
[
44
]
by iterative applications of the mean ergodic theorem. In the same paper, it is also shown that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(X,\mu,T)}
is a norm for
k
-step pronilsystems.
The main theorem in
[
44
]
shows that the
k
-step pronilfactor,
Z_{k}
, of an ergodic system
(X,\mu,T)
is characterized by the property

(5)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(X,\mu,T)}=0\iff\mathbb{E}(f\mid Z_{k})=0

for any
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
. Similarly,

\mathbb{E}(f\mid Z_{\infty})=0\iff\lVert f\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a measurable set
E\subset X
is
U^{k}(X,\mu,T)
-uniform for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, if
\mu(E)>0
and
\left\lVert\mathds{1}_{E}-\mu(E)\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}=0
. Using (

), this is equivalent to
\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E}\mid Z_{k-1})(x)=\mu(E)
for

Similarly,

is
U^{\infty}(X,\mu,T)
-uniform if it is
U^{k}(X,\mu,T)
-uniform for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

2.3.
Local uniformity seminorms

We say that a bounded function
\phi\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

admits correlations along the Følner sequence

\Phi
if the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\prod^{k}_{i=1}C^{\epsilon_{i}}\phi(n+h_{i})

exists for all
h_{1},...,h_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{k}\in\{0,1\}
, where for a complex number
z
,
Cz=\overline{z}
is the complex conjugation. Assuming that
\phi\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
admits correlations along a given Følner sequence
\Phi
we define, inductively in
k
, the
local uniformity seminorms of
\phi
along
\Phi
by

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{U^{0}(\Phi)}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{n\in\Phi_{N}}\phi(n)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(\Phi)}^{2^{k+1}}=\lim_{H\to\infty}\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\lVert\Delta_{h}\phi\right\rVert^{2^{k}}_{U^{k}(\Phi)}

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(X,\mu,T)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{U^{k}(\Phi)}\leq\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{U^{k+1}(\Phi)}.

2.4.
Cubic spaces

For a set
X
and functions
f,g\colon X\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we denote
f\otimes g\colon X\times X\to{\mathbb{C}}
the function given by
(f\otimes g)(x,y)=f(x)g(y)
for all
x,y\in X
.
In
[
44
]
, Host and Kra introduced the following measure, defined inductively in
X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=X^{2^{k}}
as follows,
\mu^{\llbracket 0\rrbracket}=\mu
and for
F_{0},F_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket})

\displaystyle\int_{X^{\llbracket k+1\rrbracket}}F_{0}\otimes F_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\llbracket k+1\rrbracket}=\int_{X^{\llbracket k+1\rrbracket}}\mathbb{E}(F_{0}\mid{\mathcal{I}}(T^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}))\otimes\mathbb{E}(F_{1}\mid{\mathcal{I}}(T^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}

where
{\mathcal{I}}(T^{\llbracket k\rrbracket})
is the
\sigma
-algebra of
T^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
-invariant sets for
T^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=T\times\cdots\times T
. Following
[
53
, section 4]
for
\boldsymbol{x}\in X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
and
i=1,\ldots,k
we write
F_{i}\boldsymbol{x}
and
F^{i}\boldsymbol{x}
its
i
th
lower
and
upper
face define by

(7)

\displaystyle F_{i}\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{\epsilon}\colon\epsilon_{i}=0)\hskip 10.00002pt\ F^{i}\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{\epsilon}\colon\epsilon_{i}=1)

and then formally
(F_{i}\boldsymbol{x},F^{i}\boldsymbol{x})\in X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
. In particular
X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
is identified with
X^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}\times X^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}
with
\boldsymbol{x}=(F_{1}\boldsymbol{x},F^{1}\boldsymbol{x})
. The measure
\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
is invariant under digit permutation that is
\phi\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
where
\phi
is an arbitrary permutation of elements in
\{1,\ldots,k\}
which induces a map
\phi\colon\llbracket k\rrbracket\to\llbracket k\rrbracket
by
(\phi\epsilon)_{i}\mapsto\epsilon_{\phi(i)}
for all
i\in[k]
and thus induces a map, also denoted by
\phi\colon X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}\to X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
given by
(\phi x)_{\epsilon}=x_{\phi(\epsilon)}
for all
\epsilon\in\llbracket k\rrbracket
.
From
[
44
]
, we have that for
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}=\bigg(\int_{X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}}\bigotimes_{\epsilon\in\llbracket k\rrbracket}C^{|\epsilon|}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\llbracket k+1\rrbracket}\bigg)^{1/2^{k}}.

Let
Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}_{0}(T)
be the group generated by the upper face transformations
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
where for
\boldsymbol{x}\in X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
and a given
i\in[k]
,

(S_{i}\boldsymbol{x})_{\epsilon}=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{cc}Tx_{\epsilon}&\text{ if }\epsilon_{i}=1\\
x_{\epsilon}&\text{ if }\epsilon_{i}=0.\end{array}\end{cases}

In particular,
S_{1}(F_{1}\boldsymbol{x},F^{1}\boldsymbol{x})=(F_{1}\boldsymbol{x},T^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}F^{1}\boldsymbol{x})
. The measure
\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
is invariant under
Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}_{0}(T)
and therefore it is invariant under the group
Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(T)=\langle Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}_{0}(T),T^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}\rangle
. Moreover, whenever
\mu
is ergodic,
\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
is ergodic under the action of

(see
[
47
, Chapter 9, Proposition 2]
). Thus for an ergodic
s
-step nilsystem
(Z,m,T)
, if
Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(Z)
is the support of the ergodic measure
m^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
, then
(Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(Z),m^{\llbracket k\rrbracket},Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(T))
is minimal and uniquely ergodic.

In general, for a topological system
(X,T)
, we write

Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(X)=\overline{\{S(x,\ldots,x)\mid x\in X,S\in Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(T)\}}.

Similarly, for an
s
-step ergodic pronilsystem
(Z,m,T)
and a point
z\in Z
,

(8)

Q_{z}^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(Z)=\{\boldsymbol{z}\in Q^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(Z)\mid z_{\underline{0}}=z\}=\overline{\{S(z,\ldots,z)\mid S\in Q_{0}^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}(T)\}}

has unique invariant measure, where the fact that the second equality holds true for any minimal distal system can be found in
[
43
, Proposition 4.2]
.

2.5.
Bounded marginals and lift of measures

For a probability space
(X,{\mathcal{X}},\mu)
and a measure
\tau\in{\mathcal{M}}(X^{s})
for
s\geq 1
, we say that
\tau

has marginals bounded by

\mu
, if for all
i=1,\ldots,s
there exists
c_{i}>0
such that
\tau_{i}\leq c_{i}\mu
, that is

\tau_{i}(A)\leq c_{i}\mu(A)\ \text{ for all }A\in{\mathcal{X}}

where
\tau_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}(X)
is the
i
th marginal of
\tau
.
If
(X,\mu,T)
is a measure preserving system and
(Y,\nu,S)
is a factor, with factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
and
\xi\in{\mathcal{M}}(Y^{s})
is a measure with marginals bounded by
\nu
, then we say that the measure
\tau\in{\mathcal{M}}(X^{s})
is
the lift of

\xi
if for all
f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

(9)

\int_{X^{s}}f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{Y^{s}}\mathbb{E}(f_{1}\mid Y)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{E}(f_{s}\mid Y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
a measure preserving system and
(Y,\nu,S)
a factor with factor map
\pi\colon X\to Y
. Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\xi\in{\mathcal{M}}(Y^{s})
be a measure with marginals bounded by
\nu
and let
\tau
be the lift of
\xi
.

If
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\subset X
are measurable sets
with positive measure,
\tilde{E}_{i}=\pi(E_{i})\subset Y
are also measurable for
i=1,\ldots,s
and
\xi(\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s})>0
, then

\tau(E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{s})>0.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\ldots,E_{s}\subset X
and
\tilde{E}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{E}_{s}\subset Y
as in the statement, then

\displaystyle\tau(E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{s})

\displaystyle=\int_{Y^{s}}\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{1}}\mid Y)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{s}}\mid Y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}}\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{1}}\mid Y)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{s}}\mid Y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi.

Define,
F=\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{1}}\mid Y)\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{S}}\mid Y)
. Notice that
F
is positive for
\xi
-almost every
\boldsymbol{y}\in\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}
, indeed,

\displaystyle\xi(\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}\mid F(y)=0\})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi(\{\boldsymbol{y}\in\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}\mid\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{i}}\mid Y)(y_{i})=0\})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\cdot\nu(\{y_{i}\in\tilde{E}_{i}\mid\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{i}}\mid Y)(y_{i})=0\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\cdot\mu(\{x\in\pi^{-1}(\tilde{E}_{i})\mid\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E_{i}}\mid Y)(\pi(x))=0\})=0.

where the last equality is given by the fact that for a measurable set
E\subset X
and a sub-
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{Y}}
if
\mu(E)>0
, then for
\mu-
almost every
x\in E
,

\mathbb{E}(\mathds{1}_{E}\mid{\mathcal{Y}})(x)>0
. We also use the fact that
\mathbb{E}(f\mid Y)(\cdot)
is constant
\mu
-almost surely on the fibers of
\pi
.
Finally

\displaystyle\tau(E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{s})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}}F\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\xi\bigg(\Big\{y\in\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}\mid\frac{1}{n+1}\leq F(y)<\frac{1}{n}\Big\}\bigg)>0

where the positivity of the right hand side is ensured by the positivity of
F
for almost every point in
\tilde{E}_{1}\times\cdots\times\tilde{E}_{s}
.
∎

\displaystyle f_{i}(T^{n}a)=\phi_{i}(n)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{N}},i\in I.

Remark 3.2

3.4.
Erdős cubes and Erdős progressions

The cubic space notation is introduced in
section

2.4
. Following
[
53
]
and
[
52
]
, for a measure preserving system
(X,\mu,T)
, we say that

•

(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})\in X^{\ell+1}
is an Erdős progression if there is a sequence
(c(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(T\times\cdots\times T)^{c(n)}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{\ell-1})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})

•

\boldsymbol{x}\in X^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}
is an Erdős cube if there are sequences
(c_{1}(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}},\ldots,(c_{\ell}(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(T^{\llbracket\ell-1\rrbracket})^{c_{i}(n)}(F_{i}\boldsymbol{x})=F^{i}\boldsymbol{x}.

where
F_{i}\boldsymbol{x}
and
F^{i}\boldsymbol{x}
are defined in (
7
).

We denote
E^{(\ell)}_{a}(X)\subset X^{\ell+1}
, (resp.
E^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}_{a}(X)\subset X^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}
) the set of Erdős progressions (resp. Erdős cubes) starting at a point
a\in X
. By
[
53
, Lemma 5.16]
,
E^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}(X)\subset Q^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}(X)
. Notice that one can turn every Erdős progression in an Erdős cube, to see that we first define the
\ell
-vertex embedding

Definition 3.7
.

The
\ell
-vertex embedding is the function
\psi_{\ell}\colon X^{\ell+1}\to X^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}
given by

(\psi_{\ell}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{\ell}))_{\epsilon}=x_{|\epsilon|}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }\epsilon\in\llbracket\ell\rrbracket.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell-1})
be an Erdős progression, then
\psi_{\ell}(x)
is an Erdős cube. Conversely if
\boldsymbol{x}
is an Erdős cube and
x_{\epsilon}=x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
whenever
|\epsilon|=|\epsilon^{\prime}|
, then
(x_{0^{\ell}},x_{0^{\ell-1}1},x_{0^{\ell-2}11},\ldots,x_{1^{\ell}})
is an Erdős progression.

In particular,
\psi_{\ell}(E^{(\ell)}_{a}(X))=E^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}_{a}(X)\subset Q^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}(X)
.

Proof.

The first inclusion is clear where from the definition of Erdős progression we take
(c_{i}(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}=(c(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.

For the converse let
\boldsymbol{x}\in E^{\llbracket\ell\rrbracket}(X)
for which
x_{\epsilon}=x_{\epsilon^{\prime}}
whenever
|\epsilon|=|\epsilon^{\prime}|
and denote
(c(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}=(c_{\ell}(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
the sequences such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}(T^{\llbracket\ell-1\rrbracket})^{c(n)}(F_{\ell}\boldsymbol{x})=F^{\ell}\boldsymbol{x}
. In particular,

\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{c(n)}x_{0^{\ell}}=x_{0^{\ell-1}1}.

By definition of Erdős cube we then get that for the same sequence
(c(n))_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{c(n)}x_{0^{\ell-2}10}=x_{0^{\ell-2}11}
,
and by assumption
x_{0^{\ell-2}10}=x_{0^{\ell-1}1}
, so
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}T^{c(n)}x_{0^{\ell-1}1}=x_{0^{\ell-2}11}
. We then proceed similarly using at each step that
x_{0^{\ell-1-j}1^{j}0}=x_{0^{\ell-j}1^{j}}
.
∎

6.2.
Combinatorial applications

A set

is a
\operatorname{Nil_{s}-Bohr_{0}}
for
s\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
if there exists an
s
-step pronilsystem
(Z,T)
, a point
z_{0}\in Z
and an open neighborhood
U\subset X
of
z_{0}
such that
D=\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid T^{n}z_{0}\in U\}
. We also say that
D\subset{\mathbb{N}}
is a
\operatorname{Nil-Bohr_{0}}
if it is a
\operatorname{Nil_{s}-Bohr_{0}}
for some
s\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
. Examples of
\operatorname{Nil-Bohr_{0}}
sets are,
r{\mathbb{N}}
for
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}\mid\{n^{s}\alpha\}\in(-\varepsilon,\epsilon)\}
for some
\epsilon>0
,
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash{\mathbb{Q}}
.

These sets were introduced by Host and Kra in
[
46
]
generalizing the notion of
\operatorname{Bohr_{0}}
sets studied in
[
9
]
. Notice that a
\operatorname{Nil-Bohr_{0}}
set
D\subset{\mathbb{N}}
is a central set (see
[
28
, Definition 8.3]
) and hence are an
IP
-set (see
[
26
]
). For further properties on
\operatorname{Nil_{s}-Bohr_{0}}
and their connection to combinatorial number theory refer to
[
6
,
10
,
49
]
.

From
theorem

6.1
we deduce the following theorem.

Let

be

Notice that unlike

for all

.

Corollary 6.6

Let

-uniform sets for the same Følner sequence

\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\epsilon_{j}B_{j}\subset A_{i}\cap R\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for }\ \epsilon\in\llbracket\ell\rrbracket\ \text{with}\ |\epsilon|=\ell_{i}.

Proof.

Fix

7.1.
Relatively totally ergodic sets

For an ergodic system,
(X,\mu,T)
we define the
rational Kronecker factor

{\mathcal{K}}_{rat}

(X,\mu,T)
that is the functions

is trivial.

Following
[

]

(X,\mu,T)
, a point

for some Følner sequence
\Phi
and an open set

with positive measure such that

and

. The next theorem can be interpreted as a
k=1
version of
[

, Conjecture 3.26]
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let

be a totally ergodic set then for every

there exists an infinite set

such that

.

Proof.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
,
\Phi=(\Phi_{N})_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,

and

be given by the definition of relatively totally ergodic sets. By
section

3.2
, we can suppose
(X,\mu,T)
has continuous pronilfactor. Fixing

, consider the measure

defined in
section

3.4
.

Let

be the function such that

,
\mu
-almost surely. Notice that

where

to the last coordinate. By definition

under the transformation

With this, since

\displaystyle\sigma^{(3)}((T^{-b_{1}}E_{1}\cap T^{-b_{2}}E_{1})

∎

Notice that, since

Example

We end this section by showing that the answer is yes when

ergodic.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
(X,\mu,T)
and
(Y,\nu,S)
be a ergodic systems such that
\mu\times\nu
is also ergodic. Then for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

Z_{k}(\mu\times\nu)=Z_{k}(\mu)\times Z_{k}(\nu)
.

Proof.

For this we prove that for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

(\mu\times\nu)^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}\times\nu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}
with the obvious identification. For the case
k=1
this is trivial, because by ergodicity
(\mu\times\nu)^{\llbracket 1\rrbracket}=(\mu\times\nu)\times(\mu\times\nu)=\mu^{\llbracket 1\rrbracket}\times\nu^{\llbracket 1\rrbracket}
. In particular,
Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)=Z_{1}(\mu)\times Z_{1}(\nu)

By induction, suppose that for any system whose product is ergodic we have the proposition for
k-1
. Then notice that

\displaystyle(\mu\times\nu)^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=\int_{Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)}(\mu\times\nu)^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)}(s)

where
(\mu\times\nu)_{s}
for
s\in m_{Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)}
is the ergodic decomposition of
(\mu\times\nu)\times(\mu\times\nu)
over
Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)
(see
[
47
, Theorem 18, Chapter 4]
). Now, since
Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)=Z_{1}(\mu)\times Z_{1}(\nu)
we get that
(\mu\times\nu)_{s}=(\mu_{s_{1}}\times\nu_{s_{2}})
for
m_{Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)}
-almost every
s=(s_{1},s_{2})
. Likewise, for
m_{Z_{1}(\mu\times\nu)}
-almost every
s=(s_{1},s_{2})
,
(\mu_{s_{1}}\times\nu_{s_{2}})
is ergodic, so by inductive assumption

(\mu\times\nu)^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}_{s}=(\mu_{s_{1}}\times\nu_{s_{2}})^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}=\mu_{s_{1}}^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}\times\nu_{s_{2}}^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}

and therefore,

\displaystyle(\mu\times

\displaystyle\nu)^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=\int_{Z_{1}(\mu)}\bigg(\int_{Z_{1}(\nu)}\mu_{s_{1}}^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}\times\nu_{s_{2}}^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{Z_{1}(\nu)}(s_{2})\bigg)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{Z_{1}(\mu)}(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{Z_{1}(\mu)}\mu_{s_{1}}^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{Z_{1}(\mu)}(s_{1})\right)\times\left(\int_{Z_{1}(\nu)}\nu_{s_{2}}^{\llbracket k-1\rrbracket}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{Z_{1}(\nu)}(s_{2})\right)=\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}\times\nu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}.

To conclude the proof, we need to check that
\left\lVert f\otimes g\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X\times Y,\mu\times\nu,T\times S)}=0
if and only if
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}=0
or
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{U^{k}(Y,\nu,S)}=0
. This is direct by noticing that

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\otimes g\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X\times Y,\mu\times\nu,T\times S)}^{2^{k}}=\int_{(X\times Y)^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}}\bigotimes_{\epsilon\in\llbracket k\rrbracket}C^{|\epsilon|}(f\otimes g)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}(\mu\times\nu)^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}}\bigotimes_{\epsilon\in\llbracket k\rrbracket}C^{|\epsilon|}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}\right)\left(\int_{Y^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}}\bigotimes_{\epsilon\in\llbracket k\rrbracket}C^{|\epsilon|}g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu^{\llbracket k\rrbracket}\right)=\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{U^{k}(X,\mu,T)}^{2^{k}}\cdot\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{U^{k}(Y,\nu,S)}^{2^{k}}.\qed

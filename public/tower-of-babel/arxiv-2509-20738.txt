Introduction

In the study of
high-level neural networks
, Edelman et al.
[
29
]
introduced a quantitative measure which is called
neural complexity
. Neural complexity is used to capture the interplay between two fundamental aspects of brain partition organization. Other works like
[
9
,
11
]
also give further investigations. In
[
5
,
6
]
, Buzzi et al. placed neural complexity within a natural mathematical function. They found that this function satisfies exchangeability and sub-additivity. Moreover, they presented a unified mathematical expression for these functions, which they called
intricacy
.

Based on Buzzi’s results and the definition of entropy, Peterson et al.
[
24
]
further investigated the intricacy in dynamical systems. They introduced new
intricacy
and
average sample complexity
in topological dynamical systems, which are closely similar to entropy. They found that the maximum value of the average sample complexity of covers is equivalent to the topological entropy.

Li et al.
[
19
]
extended the average sample complexity and intricacy to the case of countable amenable groups based on Peterson’s work. They defined the
intricacy
and
the average sample complexity
for amenable group actions. Through their research, they stated that many properties of intricacy defined by Peterson still hold with the assistance of Følner sequences of amenable groups. They also established the relationship between intricacy and the classical topological entropy. Yang et al.
[
34
]
defined random intricacy and average sample complexity by using random covers, and conclude that their suprema over open random covers are equal to the random topological entropy. In
[
33
]
, Xiao et al. proposed the concept of
the pressure of intricacy and the average sample complexity
for amenable group actions and demonstrated its variational principle for general dynamical systems.

The local entropy concept are mentioned both in topological and measure-theoretical cases, as discussed in
[
35
]
and related results can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
8
,
14
,
30
]
. Assume that
(X,T)
is a topological system,
\mathcal{U}
is a cover and
\mu
is an invariant measure. Romagnoli
[
25
]
gave the definition of two kinds of
local measure-theoreticial entropy

h_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U})
and
h_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U})
. He proved that for each invariant measure
\mu_{0}
,
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U})=\max_{\mu}h_{\mu_{0}}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U})
. In
[
12
]
, Glanser et al. showed that for each invariant measure
\mu
,
h_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U})=\max_{\mu}h_{\mu_{0}}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U})
. Huang et al.
[
15
]
showed that if there exist a system
(X,T)
and an invariant measure
\mu
with
h_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U})>h_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U})
then there is a uniquely ergodic
\mathbb{Z}
-systems with the same property. Therefore, for each invariant
measure
\mu
,
h_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U})=h_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U})
.

Huang et al.
[
16
]
introduced two definitions of measure-theoretical conditional entropy for covers of
\mathbb{Z}
-actions in 2006. Furthermore, they extended these definitions to amenable groups in
[
17
]
and proved the equivalence between two types of measure-theoretical entropy.

Li et al. raised an open question: How does the construction of intricacy and average sample complexity apply to other type notions? Inspired by this question, this paper investigate local intricacy and average sample complexity for amenable group actions. We note that these definitions are well-defined and many properties still hold when compared to the local entropy. Due to the relationship between intricacy and average sample complexity, this paper will mainly investigate the properties of average sample complexity and its variational principles.

The outline of this paper is as follow. In Section
2
, we give a review of the basical concept and propositions of amenable groups and the orbital theory, which will be necessary for the subsequent sections. In Section
3
, we introduce the definitions of the conditional measure theoretical intricacy and average sample complexity of a cover
\mathcal{U}
. That is

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq{F_{n}}}c_{S}^{F_{n}}\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{\alpha}_{S}|Y)\text{ and }

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq{F_{n}}}c_{S}^{F_{n}}H_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y).

We shall show some propositions about these conditional average sample complexity. In Section
4
, we discuss the equivalence of two kinds of measure-theoretic average sample complexity both under
\mathbb{Z}
-actions and amenable group actions, i.e. for each cover
\mathcal{U}
and invariant measure
\mu
of
X
,

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}).

In Section
5
, we give the local variational principle for the average sample complexity.

2.2
The neural complexity

In
[
29
]
, Tononi et al. introduced neural complexity in the context of high-level neural networks. To see what neural complexity means, we recall two classical definitions.

Definition 2.5
.

If

For any

,
X_{E\setminus S}:=\{X_{i}:i\in E\setminus S\}
.

Definition 2.6
.

The

\mathcal{I}(X_{E}):=\frac{1}{|E|+1}\sum_{S\subset E}\frac{1}{\binom{|E|}{|S|}}I(X_{S};X_{E\setminus S}),

where
I(X_{\emptyset};X_{E})=I(X_{E};X_{\emptyset})=0
.

Tononi et al.
[
29
]
used this notion to capture the interplay between the two fundamental aspects of brain organization.

Now we review the system of coefficients for
\mathbb{Z}
-actions and amenable group actions. One can refer to

Definition 2.7

Let

\mathrm{(1)}

\left\{c^{n}_{S}:c^{n}_{S}=2^{-n},S\subseteq n^{*}\right\}

\mathrm{(2)}

\mathrm{(3)}

3
Topological and measure-theoretic intricacy

3.1
Topological intricacy

Let
\varphi:(X,G)\to(Y,G)

\left(\text{letting }N(\mathcal{U}):=N(\mathcal{U}|\{X\}\right))

For

We replace the term

in Definition

Definition 3.1
.

Let

and

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y),

where
\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)
and
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)

and

of

\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,X|Y\right):=\sup_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}}\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)\text{, }\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,X|Y\right):=\sup_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right).

\mathrm{(1)}

Similar to the proof of Theorem 3.4 in

]
, we can observe that
F_{n}\mapsto\sum\limits_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
. Therefore, the definitions are well-defined.

\mathrm{(2)}

When
G=\mathbb{Z}
, we can also define the conditional topological intricacy and average sample complexity of
\mathcal{U}
with respect to
(Y,R)
, respectively, by

\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\mathcal{U}|Y\right):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{S\subseteq n^{*}}c^{n}_{S}\log\left(\frac{N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)N(\mathcal{U}_{n^{*}\setminus S}|Y)}{N(\mathcal{U}_{n^{*}}|Y)}\right),

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\mathcal{U}|Y\right):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{S\subseteq n^{*}}c^{n}_{S}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y).

The conditional topological intricacy and average sample complexity of
(X,T)
with respect to
(Y,R)
are defined, respectively, by

\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,X|Y\right):=\sup\limits_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}}\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\mathcal{U}|Y\right),\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,X|Y\right):=\sup\limits_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(T,\mathcal{U}|Y\right).

\mathrm{(3)}

It is easy to obtain that

\mathrm{Int}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)=2\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)-h_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)
, where
h_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{F_{n}}|Y)
.
Therefore, for the topological case, the following sections will focus only on the average sample complexity.

\mathrm{(4)}

If
(Y,G)
is a trivial system, the conditional average sample complexity is the same as the average sample complexity in
[
24
]
. That is,
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|\{\emptyset,Y\}\right)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,\mathcal{U}\right)
and
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,X|\{\emptyset,Y\}\right)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}\left(G,X\right)
.

3.2
Measure-theoretic intricacy

Denote the set of all Borel probability measures on
X
by
\mathcal{M}(X)
, the set of all
G
-invariant Borel probability measures on
X
by
\mathcal{M}(X,G)
and the set of all ergodic measures by
\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)
. Note that
\mathcal{M}(X,G)\neq\emptyset
when
G
is an amenable group, and both
\mathcal{M}(X)
and
\mathcal{M}(X,G)
are convex compact metric spaces when they are endowed with weak
∗
-topology.

Let
(X,G)
and
(Y,G)
be two
G
-systems and
\pi:(X,G)\to(Y,G)
is a factor map between two
G
-systems. Given

\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
, define

H_{\mu}(\alpha|Y)=\sum_{A\in\alpha}\int_{X}-\mathbb{E}(1_{A}|\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(Y)))\log\mathbb{E}(1_{A}|\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(Y)))d\mu,

where
\mathbb{E}(1_{A}|\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(Y)))
is the expectation of
1_{A}
with respect to
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(Y))
. It is not hard to show that
H_{\mu}(\alpha|\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(Y)))
increases with respect to
\alpha
and decreases with respect to
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}(Y))
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
. For
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
, we define the
conditional measure-theoretic intricacy
and
average sample complexity
of
\alpha
with respect to
(Y,G)
, respectively, by

\mathrm{Int}_{\mu}\left(G,\alpha|Y\right):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\left|F_{n}\right|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\left[H_{\mu}(\alpha_{S}|Y)+H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{n}\setminus S}|Y)-H_{\mu}(\alpha_{F_{n}}|Y)\right],

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}H_{\mu}(\alpha_{S}|Y)\left(=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}H_{\mu}(\alpha_{S}|Y)\right).

where
\mathrm{Int}_{\mu}\left(G,\alpha|Y\right)
and
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(G,\alpha|Y\right)
are independent of the choice of Følner sequences.

The conditional measure-theoretic intricacy and average sample complexity for
(X,G)
with respect to
(Y,G)
can be defined, respectively, by

\mathrm{Int}_{\mu}(G,X|Y):=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\mathrm{Int}_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y)\text{, }\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,X|Y):=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y).

Remark 3.2
.

\mathrm{(1)}

Similar to the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 3.4]
, we can check that the definition is well-defined.

\mathrm{(2)}

If
(Y,G)
is a trivial system, the conditional measure-theoretical average sample complexity is the same as the definition in
[
24
]
. That is,
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(G,\alpha|\{\emptyset,Y\}\right)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(G,\alpha\right)
and
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(G,X|\{\emptyset,Y\}\right)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{\mu}}\left(G,X\right)
.

\mathrm{(3)}

Similar to the topological case, the following sections will also focus only on the measure-theoretic average sample complexity.

\mathrm{(4)}

We can find
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
such that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U})<h_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U})
. One can refer to
[
24
, Example 4.5]
for details.

Following the idea proposed by Romagnoli
[
8
]
, we now introduce a new concept of the conditional
\mu
-measure-theoretical average sample complexity for covers.

Let
\pi:(X,G)\to(Y,G)
be a factor map between two
G
-systems and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
. For
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}

H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}|Y)=\inf\limits_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{X},\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}H_{\mu}(\alpha|\pi^{-1}\mathcal{B}(Y)).
The following lemma can be referred to

Lemma 3.1
.

If
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}

\mathrm{(1)}

0\leq H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\log N(\mathcal{U}|Y);

\mathrm{(2)}

If
\mathcal{U}\succeq\mathcal{V}
,then
H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}|Y)\geq H_{\mu}(\mathcal{V}|Y);

\mathrm{(3)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{U}\vee\mathcal{V}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{U}|Y)+\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{V}|Y)

\mathrm{(4)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(g^{-1}\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(\mathcal{U}|Y)\text{ for all }g\in G.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
. Given
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
, we define the
\mu^{-}
-conditional average sample complexity
of
\mathcal{U}
with respect to
(Y,G)
and
\mu^{+}
-conditional average sample complexity
of
\mathcal{U}
with respect to
(Y,G)
, respectively, by

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq{F_{n}}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)\left(=\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{n}_{S}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)\right),

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y):=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y).

where
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)
and
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}\left(G,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)
are independent of the choice of Følner sequences.

We define
\mu^{-}
-conditional average sample complexity
of
(X,G)
with respect to
(Y,G)
and
\mu^{+}
-conditional average sample complexity
of
(X,G)
with respect to
(Y,G)
, respectively, by

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(G,X|Y):=\sup_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)\text{ , }\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,X|Y):=\sup_{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y).

In this subsection, we aim to present some propositions with regard to two types of conditional local average sample complexity of covers.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,R)
and
\varphi:(Z,S)\to(X,T)
be two factor maps. If
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(Z,S)
,
\mu=\varphi\nu
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
, then

\mathrm{(1)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
;

\mathrm{(2)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(S,\varphi^{-1}\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
;

\mathrm{(3)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}(S,\varphi^{-1}\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
;

If the systems of coefficients in the following
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
,
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{n},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)
and
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
are uniform, then we have

\mathrm{(4)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{0\in S\subseteq n^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)
;

\mathrm{(5)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y)
for each
M\in\mathbb{N}
;

\mathrm{(6)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{0\in S\subseteq n^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{n},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)
;

\mathrm{(7)}

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y)
for each
M\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

(1), (2) and (3) are easy to prove. The proof of (4) can be refer to
[
24
, Proposition 2.5]
. So we shall prove (5) and (6). By the definition of
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{MN}\sum_{0\in L\subseteq(MN)^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{MN-1}}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{L}|Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{2^{MN-1}}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq N^{*}}\sum_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{2^{MN-1}}{2^{M+N-2}}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{SM+B}|Y)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y).

We prove (6). For any
M\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y)\leq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}H_{\mu}(\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y).

Letting
M\to\infty
, we have
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\lim\limits_{M\to\infty}\frac{1}{M}\sum\limits_{0\in B\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{B}|Y)
.

The proof of (7) is similar to (4).

∎

3.3
The relationship between topological and measure-theoretical average sample complexity

In this subsection, we shall present the proofs of the variational principle for two kinds of conditional average sample complexity of covers.

The following lemma plays an important role in the proof of conditional average sample complexity, which can be referred to
[
16
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
and
\alpha_{l}\in\mathcal{P}_{X}
with
\alpha_{l}\succeq\mathcal{U}
,
1\leq l\leq K
. Then for each
S\in\mathcal{F}(G)
there exists a finite subset
B_{S}\subseteq X
such that each element of
(\alpha_{l})_{S}
contains at most one point of
B_{S}
,
l=1,\dots,K
and
|B_{S}|\geq\frac{N(\mathcal{U}_{S})}{|K|}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\pi:(X,G)\to(Y,G)
be a factor map between two
G
-systems,
A\subseteq G
,
\{c_{S}^{A}=\frac{1}{2^{|A|}}:S\subseteq A\}
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
. Then there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
such that

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y).

Proof.

\mathbf{Step1:}
Assume that
X
is a zero-dimensional space.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{d}\}
. We set
\mathcal{U}^{*}=\{\alpha=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\}\in\mathcal{P}(X):A_{i}\subseteq U_{i},i\in\{1,\dots,d\}\}
and
\{\alpha_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{*}
an enumeration of the family of partitions which consists of clopen subsets. Then it is easy to show that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})=\inf\limits_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}(G,\alpha_{l})
for each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
.

Since
G
is infinite, let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Føner sequence of
G
with
|F_{n}|\geq n
. By Lemma
3.4
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq F_{n}
there exists a finite subset
B_{{F_{n}},S}
such that

|B_{{F_{n}},S}|\geq\frac{N(\mathcal{U}_{S})}{|K|},

and each element of
(\alpha_{l})_{S}
contains most one point of
S\subseteq F_{n}
, for
l=1,\dots,n
. Let

\nu_{S,n}=\frac{1}{|B_{{F_{n}},S}|}\sum_{x\in B_{{F_{n}},S}}\delta_{x}\text{ and }\mu_{n}=\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}\frac{1}{2^{|F_{n}|-1}}\sum_{g\in S}g\nu_{S,n}.

It is easy to show that
\nu_{S,n}\in\mathcal{M}(X)
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq F_{n}
. We choose a sub-sequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mu_{n_{j}}\to\mu
in weak
∗
-topology of
\mathcal{M}(X)
as
j\to\infty
. It is not hard to check that
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
. Now we shall prove that
\mu
satisfies
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})
.

Fix an
l\in\mathbb{N}
and each
n\geq l
. By the construction of
B_{{F_{n}},S}
, we know that

\displaystyle\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S})-\log n\leq\log|B_{{F_{n}},S}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in B_{{F_{n}},S}}-\nu_{S,n}(\{x\})\log\nu_{S,n}(\{x\})=H_{\nu_{S,n}}((\alpha_{l})_{S}).

(3.1)

For each
B\in\mathcal{F}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}\frac{1}{2^{|F_{n}|}}H_{\nu_{S,n}}((\alpha_{l})_{S})\leq\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}\frac{1}{2^{|F_{n}|}}\sum_{g\in S}\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{A\subseteq B}c^{B}_{A}H_{\nu_{S,n}}((\alpha_{l})_{Ag})

(3.2)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}\frac{1}{2^{|F_{n}|}}\sum_{g\in S}\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{A\subseteq B}c^{B}_{A}\left|S\setminus\{g\in G:A^{-1}g\subseteq S\}\right|\cdot\log|\alpha_{l}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|F_{n}|\cdot|B|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}\frac{1}{2^{|F_{n}|}}\sum_{g\in S}\sum_{A\subseteq B}c^{B}_{A}H_{g\nu_{S,n}}((\alpha_{l})_{A})+\frac{1}{|B|}\cdot\log d\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{A\subseteq B}c^{B}_{A}H_{\mu_{n}}((\alpha_{l})_{A})+\frac{1}{|B|}\cdot\log d.

By (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{A\subseteq B}c^{B}_{A}H_{\mu_{n}}((\alpha_{l})_{A})+\frac{\log n}{|F_{n}|}+\frac{1}{|B|}\cdot\log d.

(3.3)

Noting that
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}H_{\mu_{n_{j}}}((\alpha_{l})_{A})=H_{\mu}((\alpha_{l})_{A})
, by substituting
n
with
n_{j}
in (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\frac{1}{|B|}\sum_{A\subseteq B}c^{B}_{A}H_{\mu}((\alpha_{l})_{A})+\frac{1}{|B|}\cdot\log d.

Replacing
B
by
F_{m}
where
\{F_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Følner sequence with
|F_{m}|\geq m
. Taking
m\to\infty
, we obtain
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})
for some
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
.

\mathbf{Step2:}
The general case.

It is well known that there exists a surjective continuous map
\phi_{1}:C\to X
, where
C
is a cantor set. Let
C^{G}
be the product space equiped with the
G
-shift
G\times C^{G}\to C^{G}
. We define

Z=\{(z_{g})_{g\in G}\in C^{G}:\phi_{1}(z_{g_{1}g_{2}})=g_{1}\phi_{1}(z_{g_{2}})\text{ }\mathrm{for}\text{ each }g_{1},g_{2}\in G\}
and
\varphi:Z\to X
with
(z_{g})_{g\in G}\in C^{G}\mapsto\phi_{1}(z_{e_{G}})
. It is not hard to obtain that
Z\subseteq C^{G}
is a closed invariant subset under the
G
-shift. Additionally,
\varphi:(Z,G)\to(X,G)
is a factor map between two
G
-systems. According to Step 1, there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
such that
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})
. Let
\mu=\varphi\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})

\displaystyle=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}(G,\varphi^{-1}(\alpha))

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X),\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\varphi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}))\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U}).

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,R)
be a factor map between two
\mathbb{Z}
-systems and
\{c^{n}_{S}=2^{-n}:S\subseteq n^{*}\}
the uniform system of coefficients. Then for every
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
, there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
such that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
.

Proof.

First, we assume that
X
is a zero-dimension space.

Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{d}\}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
S\subseteq k^{*}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
, it is easy to show that

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right).

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
S\subseteq k^{*}
and
s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
, we set

\displaystyle M(k,s)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\mu:\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq k^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\right\},

where
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
.
By Theorem
3.1
, we shall obtain that there exists
\mu_{k}\in\mathcal{M}(X,T^{k})
such that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{k}}^{+}(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)
. Since
\bigvee\limits_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}\succeq\mathcal{U}_{S}
for each
s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
, we have

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{k}}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y).

Let
\nu_{k,S}=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum\limits_{i\in S}\mu_{k}\circ T^{-i}
. Then
\mu_{k}\circ T^{-i}\in\mathcal{M}(X,T^{k})
for each
i\in S
. Let
s=({s}(0),\dots,{s}(|S|-1))
. We define

P^{j}_{s}=\underbrace{s(|S|-j)s(|S|-j+1)\cdots s(|S|-1)}_{j}\underbrace{s(0)s(1)\cdot s(|S|-j-1)}_{|S|-j}.

Then
P^{j}_{s}\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
. Set
P^{0}_{s}=s
. Combining Proposition
3.1
, it is not hard to show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{{\mu_{k}}\cdot T^{-s(j)}}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{k}}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{P^{j}_{s}}}_{(i)}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{k,S}}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum\limits_{i\in S}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{k}\circ T^{-i}}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y).

This implies that
\nu_{k,S}\in\bigcap\limits_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}}M(k,s)
. Write
M(k)=\bigcap\limits_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}}M(k,s)\neq\emptyset
is a subset of
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
.

For each
s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
,
\bigvee\limits_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}
is a clopen cover. So the map
\mu\mapsto\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(T^{k},\bigvee\limits_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)
is a u.s.c. function on
\mathcal{M}(X,T^{k})
. Since
\mathcal{M}(X,T)\subseteq\mathcal{M}(X,T^{k})
,
\mathrm{Asc}_{*}\left(T^{k},\bigvee\limits_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)
is also a u.s.c. function on
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
by Theorem
3.3
. So

M(k,s)
is closed in
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
for each
s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
and
M(k,s)\neq\emptyset
is closed in
\mathcal{M}(X,T)
.

Now let
k,k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
k_{2}=kk_{1}
and
\mu\in M(k_{2})
. Let
B\subseteq k_{2}^{*}
. Then for each
i\in B
, there exist
E\subseteq k^{*}
,
j\in E
,
b\in\mathbb{N}^{B}
and
i_{0}\in S
such that
i=k_{1}j+i_{0}
and
b(i)=s(i_{0})
. So

\displaystyle\frac{2}{k_{1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{0\in S\subseteq{k_{1}}^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{k_{1}}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(T^{k_{1}},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{k_{2}}\sum_{0\in B\subseteq{k_{2}}^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{k_{2}-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(T^{k_{2}},\bigvee_{i\in B}T^{-i}{\alpha_{b_{(i)}}}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

Hence
\mu\in M(k_{1},b)
for each
b\in\mathbb{N}^{B}
. That is,
\mu\in M(k_{1})
. Then
M(k_{2})\subseteq M(k_{1})
. Since
M(k_{1}k_{2})\subseteq M(k_{1})\cap M(k_{2})
for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\cap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}M(k)\neq\emptyset
. Take
\mu\in\cap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}M(k)
. For any
s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq k^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}\left(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{s\in\mathbb{N}^{S}}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq k^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}\left(T^{k},\bigvee_{i\in S}T^{-i}{\alpha_{s}}_{(i)}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}\left(T,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq k^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}\left(T^{k},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y\right)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

Thus, we have
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}\left(T,\mathcal{U}|Y\right)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
.

Similar to the general case in Theorem
3.1
, we finish the proof.

∎

3.4
Ergodic decompositions

This section will demonstrate the ergodic decompositions of two kinds of local measure-theoretic average sample complexity. First, we will present the following lemma based on the idea introduced in
[
17
]
. It plays an important role in the proof of ergodic decompositions.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\pi:(X,G)\to(Y,G)
be a factor map between two
G
-system and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{P}(X)
. Assume that
S=\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{|S|}\}
for each
S\subseteq F_{n}
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha\vee\beta)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\beta)+\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\sum_{j=2}^{|S|}H_{\mu}\left(g_{j}^{-1}\alpha|\bigvee_{i\in S\setminus\{g_{j},\cdots,g_{|S|}\}}g_{i}^{-1}\alpha\vee\beta_{S}\right).

Proof.

Fix
S\subseteq F_{n}
. By the definition, we note that

\displaystyle H_{\mu}\left(\alpha_{S}\vee\beta_{S}\right)=H_{\mu}(\beta_{S})+H_{\mu}\left(g^{-1}_{1}\alpha\right|\beta_{S})+\sum_{j=2}^{|S|}H_{\mu}\left(g_{j}^{-1}\alpha|\bigvee_{i\in S\setminus\{g_{j},\cdots,g_{|S|}\}}g_{i}^{-1}\alpha\vee\beta_{S}\right).

(3.4)

Since
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum\limits_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}H_{\mu}\left(g^{-1}\alpha\right)=0
for each
g\in G
, we multiply (
3.4
) on both side by
c^{F_{n}}_{S}
, divide by
|F_{n}|
and sum over
S\subseteq F_{n}
. Then, by letting
n\to\infty
, we can complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
. If
\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\omega}dm(\omega)
is the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
then

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega).

Proof.

First, we shall show that for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X)
, we have
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\alpha|Y)dm(\omega)
. Let
\{\beta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence with
\beta_{j}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}diam(\beta_{j})=0
. Assume that
S=\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{|S|}\}
for each
S\subseteq F_{n}
. By Lemma
3.5
and the dominated convergence theorem, one has

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha|Y)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\sum_{j=2}^{|S|}H_{\mu}\left(g_{j}^{-1}\alpha|\bigvee_{i\in\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{j-1}\}}g_{i}^{-1}\alpha\vee\pi^{-1}(({\beta_{j}})_{S})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}[\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\alpha^{-}\vee(\pi^{-1}(\beta_{j}))-\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\pi^{-1}(\beta_{j}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}[\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\alpha^{-}\vee(\pi^{-1}(\beta_{j}))-\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\pi^{-1}(\beta_{j}))]dm(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\lim_{j\to\infty}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum_{S\subseteq F_{n}}c^{F_{n}}_{S}\sum_{j=2}^{|S|}H_{\mu_{\omega}}\left(g_{j}^{-1}\alpha|\bigvee_{i\in\{g_{1},\cdots,g_{j-1}\}}g_{i}^{-1}\alpha\vee\pi^{-1}(({\beta_{j}})_{S})\right)dm(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(T,\alpha|Y)dm(\omega).

Taking an increasing sequence
\{\gamma_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(X)
such that
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}diam(\gamma_{j})=0
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,X|Y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\gamma_{j}|Y)=\lim_{j\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\gamma_{j}|Y)dm(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\gamma_{j}|Y)dm(y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,X|Y)dm(y).

Now we shall prove that for each
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
, we have
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega).

Since
X
is a compact metric space, there exist
\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{*}
which is
L^{1}(X,\mathcal{B}(X),\nu)
-dense in
\mathcal{U}^{*}
for each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
. So for each
\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
,

\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n}|Y).

Set
\alpha_{n}=\{A^{n}_{1},\dots,A^{n}_{d}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Fatou’s lemma, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n}|Y)=\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n}|Y)dm(\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\inf_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n}|Y)dm(\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega).

On the other hand, for each
\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
S\subseteq F_{n}
, we define

B_{n}^{\varepsilon}=\{\omega\in\Omega:\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\varepsilon\}.

We note that
m(\Omega\Delta\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}^{\varepsilon})=0
. There exist
\{\Omega_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
with
m(\Omega)>0
and
\{\alpha_{n_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\{\alpha_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for
m
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega_{j}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n_{j}}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\varepsilon
. So for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\nu_{j}\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
by
\nu_{j}=\frac{1}{m(\Omega_{j})}\int_{\Omega_{j}}\nu_{\omega}dm(\omega)
. So

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n_{j}}|Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m(\Omega_{j})}\int_{\Omega_{j}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\alpha_{n_{j}}|Y)dm(\omega)\leq\frac{1}{m(\Omega_{j})}\int_{\Omega_{j}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega)+\varepsilon.

Since
\{\nu_{n}\}
is mutually singular, there exist
\{X_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\nu_{n}(X_{n})=1
and
\nu_{n}(X_{k})=0
for
k\neq n
. For
1\leq i\leq d
, we define
A_{i}=\bigcup_{j\geq 1}(X_{j}\cap A_{i}^{n_{j}})
, then
\alpha=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\}\in\mathcal{U}^{*}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\alpha|Y)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}m(\Omega_{j})\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{j}}^{+}(T,\alpha_{n_{j}}|Y)\leq\int_{\Omega_{j}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega)+\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu_{\omega}}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega)
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
,
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
and
\{c^{n}_{S}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}:S\subseteq n^{*}\}
. If
\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mu_{\omega}dm(\omega)
is the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
, then

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq n^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{n},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq n^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}^{+}(T^{n},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)dm(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq n^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{n-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}^{+}(T^{n},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)dm(\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)dm(\omega).

∎

4
Equivalence of two kinds of measure-theoretic average sample complexity

4.1
The case of
\mathbb{Z}
-systems

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,R)
be a factor map between two
\mathbb{Z}
-systems. In this subsection, we shall prove that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
for each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)
and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
when
\{c^{n}_{S}=2^{-n}:S\subseteq n^{*}\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

[
12
, Rohlin’s lemma]

Let
(X,T)
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-systems and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
. Suppose
\mu
is a non-atomic. Then for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a Borel subset
D
of
X
such that
D,TD,\dots,T^{N-1}D
are pairwise disjoint and
\mu\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}T^{i}D\right)>1-\varepsilon
.

By Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
3.2
, we shall present the following theorem which is the variational principle of the conditional local average sample complexity for
\mathbb{Z}
-systems.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,R)
be a factor map between two
\mathbb{Z}
-systems,
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
. Then
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
. When
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
, we have

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,T)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y).

Proof.

We are inspired by the approach presented in
[
12
,
15
,
25
]
. By Theorem
3.3
, we need to proved that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
for each non-atomic measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
be a non-atomic measure,
\nu=\pi\mu
and
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{1},\dots,U_{d}\}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
. Then
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(Y,R)
. Let
\mu=\int_{Y}\mu_{y}d\nu(y)
be the disintegration of
\mu
with respect to
\nu
. It is not hard to show that we can take
\beta\succeq\mathcal{U}
such that
|\beta|_{\mu_{y}}\leq N(\mathcal{U}|Y)
for
\nu
-a.e. where
|\beta|_{\mu_{y}}=|\{B\in\beta:\mu_{y}(B)>0\}|
for each
y\in Y
. Then for each
S\subseteq N^{*}
, we have
|\beta_{S}|_{\mu_{y}}\leq N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)
for
\nu
-a.e.
y\in Y
. By the definition of
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
, for
\varepsilon>0
, we choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough such that

\sum_{S\subseteq N^{*}}c^{N}_{S}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)\leq N\left(\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+\varepsilon\right).

Let
0<\delta<1
small enough such that
2\sqrt{\delta}\log k<\varepsilon
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists
D\subseteq\mathcal{B}(X)
with
D,TD,\dots,T^{N-1}D
pairwise disjoint such that
\mu\left(\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}T^{-i}D\right)>1-\varepsilon
. Now we define the partition of
D
,
\beta_{D}=\{B\cap D:B\in\beta\}
. We use the partition
\beta_{D}
to define a partition of
X
by
\alpha=\{A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\}
, which assigning the set
A_{i}
all the sets of the form with

P=P_{i}\subseteq{U_{i}}\cap D,

where
P\in\beta_{D}
. On the remainder of the space
\alpha
can be taken to be any partition refining
\mathcal{U}
. we note that
\alpha_{S}\cap D={(\beta_{D})}_{S}
. Set
{(\beta_{D})}_{S}=\beta_{S,D}
, then

|\alpha_{S}\cap D|_{\mu_{y}}=|\beta_{S,D}|_{\mu_{y}}\leq|\beta_{S}|_{\mu_{y}}\leq N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)\text{ for }\nu\text{-a.e.}\text{ }y\in Y.

Fix
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,T)
, we shall show that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)+3\varepsilon
. Let
E=\bigcup\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}T^{i}D
. Then
\mu(E)>1-\delta
. Let
n\gg N
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{N}{n}=0
and
K\subseteq n^{*}
, then we set

G_{n,K}=\left\{x\in X:\frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{i\in K}1_{E}(T^{i}x)>1-\sqrt{\delta}\right\}.
So

\displaystyle\mu(G_{n,K})+(1-\sqrt{\delta})(1-\mu(G_{n,K}))

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{X}\frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{i\in K}1_{E}(T^{i}x)d\mu(x)=\mu(E)>1-\delta.

So we have
\mu(G_{n,K})>1-\sqrt{\delta}
. For each
x\in G_{n,K}
, we define
S_{n,K}(x)=\{i\in K:T^{i}x\in D\}
.
|\cup_{j\in S}(S_{n,K}(x)+j)|=\sum\limits_{i\in S}1_{E}(T^{i}x)>|K|(1-\sqrt{\delta})
. By definition, fix
S\subseteq N^{*}
,
i\in\{i\in K:T^{i}x\notin\bigcup_{j\in S}T^{-j}D\}
if and only if
i\notin S_{n,K}(x)+j
for any
j\in S
. Thus,

\left|K\setminus\bigcup_{j\in S}(S_{n,K}(x)+j)\right|\leq|K|\sqrt{\delta}+|S|.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{n,K}=\{S_{n,K}(x):x\in G_{n,K}\}
and
F=\{s_{1},\dots,s_{l}\}\in\mathcal{F}_{n,K}
. It is not hard to show that
F\cap(F+i)=\emptyset
,
i\in\{0,\dots N-1\}
. Since
D,TD,\dots,T^{N-1}D
are pairwise disjoint, we have
|F|\leq\frac{|K|}{|S|}+1
. Set
a_{n,K}=[\frac{|K|}{|S|}]+1
, then

|\mathcal{F}_{n,K}|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{a_{n,K}}\binom{|K|}{j}\leq K\binom{|K|}{a_{n,K}}.

By Stirling’s formula, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(|K|\binom{|K|}{a_{n,K}}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

So we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(|\mathcal{F}_{n,K}|+1)\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(|K|\binom{|K|}{a_{n,K}}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

We define
B_{F}=\{x\in G_{n,K}:S_{n,K}(x)=F,F\in\mathcal{F}_{n,K}\}
and
\gamma=\{B_{F}:F\in\mathcal{F}_{n,K}\}
. It is easy to show that
\gamma
is the partition of
G_{n,K}
. For
F\in\mathcal{F}_{n,K}
, let
F=\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{l}\}
and
H_{FS}=K\setminus\bigcup\limits_{j\in S}(F+j)
with
|H_{FS}|\leq|K|\sqrt{\delta}+|S|
. Since
B_{F}\subseteq G_{n,K}\cap\bigcap\limits_{j\in S}T^{-j}D
, then

\displaystyle|\alpha_{K}\cap B_{F}|_{\mu_{y}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{l}\left|\alpha_{S}\cap D\right|_{T^{j}\mu_{y}}\prod_{r\in H_{FS}}|\alpha|_{\mu_{y}}\leq N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)^{l}d^{|H_{FS}|}\leq d^{|K|\sqrt{\delta}+|S|}(N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y))^{|K|/|S|+1}.

Set
b_{n,K,S}=k^{|K|\sqrt{\delta}+|S|}(N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y))^{|K|/|S|+1}
, then we obtain that

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(\alpha_{K}\cap B_{F}|Y)=\int\limits_{Y}H_{\mu_{y}}(\alpha_{K}\cap B_{F}|Y)d\nu(y)\leq\int\limits_{Y}\mu_{y}(B_{F})(\log|\alpha_{K}\cap B_{F}|_{\mu_{y}}-\log\mu_{y}(B_{F}))d\nu(y)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{Y}\mu_{y}(B_{F})(\log b_{n,K,S}-\log\mu_{y}(B_{F}))d\nu(y)=\mu(B_{F})(\log b_{n,K,S})-\int\limits_{Y}\mu_{y}(B_{F})\log(\mu_{y}(B_{F}))d\nu(y).

\displaystyle\leq\mu(X\setminus G_{n,K})(\log b_{n,K,S})-\int\limits_{Y}\mu_{y}(X\setminus G_{n,K})\log(\mu_{y}(X\setminus G_{n,K}))d\nu(y).

(4.2)

Let

. Combining (

) and (

), we obtain that

\displaystyle H_{\mu}(\alpha_{K}|Y)

\displaystyle\leq\log b_{n,K,S}+\sqrt{\delta}|K|\log k+\int\limits_{y}|\log\Delta|d\nu(y)=\log b_{n,K,S}+\sqrt{\delta}|K|\log k+\log(|\mathcal{F}_{n,K}|+1).

For each

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{K\subseteq n^{*}}c^{n}_{K}[\log(k^{|K|\sqrt{\delta}+|S|}N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)^{|K|/|S|+1})+\sqrt{\delta}|K|\log k+\log(|\mathcal{F}_{n,K}|+1]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{S\subseteq N^{*}}c^{N}_{S}\log N(\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)+3\varepsilon.

Since

is arbitrary, letting

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\pi:(X,T)\to(Y,R)
be a factor map between two
\mathbb{Z}
-systems and
\{c^{n}_{S}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}:S\subseteq n^{*}\}

(X,T)
is uniquely ergodic, then the following holds:

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mathrm{top}}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
for each
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
.

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{U}|Y)
for each
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
.

Proof.

(1) Combining Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the first result.

(2)Fix
\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
and
\varepsilon>0
. Put
|\mathcal{V}|=R
. By Proposition
3.2
, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that for every
\mathcal{U}_{1}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
with
|\mathcal{U}_{1}|=R
, when
\mu(\mathcal{U}_{1}\bigtriangleup\mathcal{V})<\delta_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{V}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}_{1}|Y)+\varepsilon.

(4.3)

By Proposition
3.1
, there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{V}_{S}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{V}|Y)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\end{aligned}.

(4.4)

By Proposition
3.2
, there exists
\delta_{2,S}>0
such that for every
\mathcal{U}_{2,S}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}
with
|\mathcal{U}_{2,S}|=|\mathcal{V}_{S}|
, when
\mu(\mathcal{U}_{2,S}\bigtriangleup\mathcal{V}_{S})<\delta_{2,S}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{2,S}|Y)\leq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{V}_{S}|Y)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(4.5)

Now we consider
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{X}^{o}
with
|\mathcal{U}|=R
such that
\mu(\mathcal{U}\bigtriangleup\mathcal{V})<\min\{\delta_{1},\frac{\delta_{2,S}}{R^{|S|}},S\in M^{*}\}

4.3

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{V}|Y)\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{U}Y)+\varepsilon.

(4.6)

Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{U}_{S}|Y)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M}\sum_{0\in S\subseteq M^{*}}\frac{1}{2^{M-1}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T^{M},\mathcal{V}_{S}|Y)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{-}(T,\mathcal{V}|Y)+\varepsilon.

Thus, combining above equations, Theorem

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}^{+}(T,\mathcal{V}|Y)

Since

Theorem 4.3
.

[

\displaystyle\mathrm{Asc}_{\widehat{\mu}}^{-}(\widehat{T},\phi(\mathcal{U})|\widehat{Y})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\widehat{\mu}}^{+}(\widehat{T},\phi(\mathcal{U})|\widehat{Y}).

Combining the above equations, we get

and measurable subsets

satisfying
\mu(C_{i}^{(n)})>0
,
i\in J
. If
i\in J
,
x\in C_{i}^{(n)}
and
g\in G_{i}^{(n)}
, we have

(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})|\mathcal{R}_{n}(gx)|<\left|\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(gx):Ey\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}(gx)\right\}\right|=\left|\left\{y\in\mathcal{R}_{n}(x):Ey\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}(x)\right\}\right|.

So
(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})|G_{i}^{(n)}|<|\{g\in G_{i}^{(n)}:Eg\subseteq G_{i}^{(n)}\}|
, that is
G_{i}^{(n)}
is
(E,\varepsilon^{\prime})
-invariant. We define

f(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}_{n}(x)|}\sum_{B\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}(x)}c_{B}^{\mathcal{R}_{n}(x)}H_{\nu}\left(\bigvee_{y\in B}\phi_{G}(x,y)\mathcal{U}\right)\text{ for each }x\in X.

It is easy to show that
f_{n}(x)\leq\log N(\mathcal{U})
. So

\int\limits_{A_{n}}f(x)d\mu(x)=\sum_{j\in J}\int\limits_{C_{j}^{(n)}}\sum_{D\subseteq{G_{i}}}c^{G_{i}}_{D}H_{\nu}\left(\bigvee_{g\in D}{\textstyle\prod_{g}^{-1}}\mathcal{U}\right)d\mu(x).

So we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(\mathcal{R}_{n},\phi_{G},X\times\mathcal{U})-\mu(A_{n})\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})\right|\leq\left|\int\limits_{A_{n}}\left(f(x)-\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})\right)d\mu(x)+\int\limits_{X\setminus{A_{n}}}f(x)d\mu(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum\limits_{j\in J}|C_{j}^{(n)}|\mu(C_{j}^{(n)})\right)\varepsilon+(1-\mu(A_{n}))\log N(\mathcal{U})\to 0,\text{ as }n\to\infty\text{ and }\varepsilon\to 0.

We obtain that
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\widehat{\mathrm{Asc}}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})
.
Additionally,

\widehat{\mathrm{Asc}}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X)\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\widehat{\mathrm{Asc}}_{\nu}(G,\alpha)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathcal{P}(X)\alpha\succeq\mathcal{U}}\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}(G,\alpha)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U}).

The proof is finished.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\gamma
be an invertible measure-preserving transformation on
(X,\nu)
generating
\mathcal{R}
,
\phi:\mathcal{R}\to Aut(Y,\nu)
a cocycle,

and
\gamma_{\phi}
be the

-skew product extension of
\gamma
. Then

\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(\phi,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu\times\nu}^{-}(\gamma_{\phi},\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\})\text{, }\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(\phi,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu\times\nu}^{+}(\gamma_{\phi},\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\}).

Proof.

The proof is similar to
[
17
]
.
∎

Combining above theorems in this section, we will obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(Y,\mathcal{B}_{Y},\nu,G)
be a
G
-measure preserving system with
(Y,\mathcal{B}_{Y},\nu)
a Lebesgue space and
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}_{Y}
. Then
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})
.

Proof.

Let
(X,\mathcal{B}_{X},\mu,G)
be a free
G
-measure preserving system with
\mathcal{R}\subseteq X\times X
the
G
-orbit equivalence relation and
\gamma
an invertible measure-preserving transformation on
(X,\mathcal{B}_{X},\mu)
generating
\mathcal{R}
. Let
\phi_{G}:\mathcal{R}\to Aut(Y,\nu)
be a cocycle satisfying
\phi_{G}(gx,x)=\prod_{g}
, where
\prod_{g}\in Aut(Y,\nu)
the action of
g\in G
on
(Y,\mathcal{B}_{Y},\nu,G)
. By Theorem
4.4
, we have

\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\widehat{\mathrm{Asc}}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(\phi_{G},X\times\mathcal{U})\text{, }\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})=\widehat{\mathrm{Asc}}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(\phi_{G},X\times\mathcal{U}).

By Theorem
4.5
, we have

\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(\phi,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu\times\nu}^{-}(\gamma_{\phi},\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\})\text{, }\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(\phi,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu\times\nu}^{+}(\gamma_{\phi},\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\}).

As
\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\}
is

, we have

\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu\times\nu}^{-}(\gamma_{\phi},\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu\times\nu}^{+}(\gamma_{\phi},\mathcal{U}|\mathcal{B}_{X}\otimes\{\emptyset,Y\}).

So
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})

5
A local variational principle of average sample complexity

In this section, we aim to present a local variational principle of average sample complexity of amenable group actions. First, we review the theorem on variational principle of average sample complexity.

Theorem 5.1
.

(Variational principle of average sample complexity) Let
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
. Then

\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,X)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,X)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,X).

Proof.

Li et al.(Theorem 3.6,
[
19
]
) showed that for each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
,

\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,X)=h_{top}(G,X)\text{ and }\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,X)=h_{\mu}(G,X).

(5.1)

Huang et al. (Theorem 5.1,
[
17
]
) give the variational principle of topological entropy. That is,

h_{\mathrm{top}}(G,X)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)}h_{\mu}(G,X)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)}h_{\mu}(G,X).

(5.2)

Combining with Equations (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), we prove the theorem.

∎

By Theorem
4.6
, we define
\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}(G,\mathcal{U}):=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{-}(G,\mathcal{U})=\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}^{+}(G,\mathcal{U})
.

Theorem 5.2
.

(Local variational principle of average sample complexity) Let
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{C}^{o}_{X}
and
\{c^{F_{n}}_{S}=2^{-|F_{n}|}:S\subseteq F_{n}\}
a system of coefficients. Then

\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\mathcal{U})=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\mathcal{U}).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.6
, we have
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\mathcal{U})
for each
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exists
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)
such that
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\mathcal{U})
.
So

\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})=\max_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X,G)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\mathcal{U}).

Now we prove that there exists
\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)
such that
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\nu}(G,\mathcal{U})
.

Let
\mu=\int_{\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)}\mu_{\omega}dm(\omega)
be the ergodic decomposition of
\mu
. By Theorem
3.3
, we have

\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)}\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\mathcal{U})dm(\omega)=\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu}(G,\mathcal{U})\geq\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U}).

So
\mathrm{Asc}_{top}(G,\mathcal{U})\leq\mathrm{Asc}_{\mu_{\omega}}(G,\mathcal{U})
for some
{\mu_{\omega}}\in\mathcal{M}^{e}(X,G)
. Then the proof is completed.

∎

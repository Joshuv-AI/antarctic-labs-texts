Global attractivity criteria for a discrete-time Hopfield neural network model with unbounded delays via singular M − M- matrices


Abstract
In this work, we establish two global attractivity criteria for a multidimensional discrete-time non-autonomous Hopfield neural network model with infinite delays and delays in the leakage terms. The first criterion, which applies when the activation functions are bounded, is based on M − M- matrices that are not necessarily invertible. The second criterion, relevant for unbounded activation functions, requires that a related singular M − M- matrix be irreducible. We contrast our findings with existing results in the literature and present numerical simulations to illustrate the efficacy of the proposed criteria.

Introduction

Neural network models have emerged as indispensable tools across diverse scientific and engineering domains, offering innovative solutions to complex challenges. Their applications span optimization
[
7
]
, signal processing
[
7
]
, image processing
[
1
]
, and pattern classification
[
24
]
, where they effectively learn intricate patterns and relationships within data, resulting in highly accurate solutions. Essential attributes such as associative memory, parallel computation, pattern categorization, and fault tolerance
[
14
]
underscore their wide-ranging utility.

In his pioneering work, the recent Nobel Prize winner in Physics, John Hopfield proposed a system of ordinary differential equations to describe an artificial neural network
[
15
]
. To better represent the inherent delays in neural signal transmission, such as finite signal propagation speeds and neuron response times, delays were incorporated into Hopfield’s model.
Marcus and Westervelt
[
18
]
demonstrated how such delays can destabilize the system.
Since then, numerous studies on the stability of neural network models with delays have been published (see, for example,
[
2
,
4
,
8
,
11
,
21
]
and references therein).

Although the stability analysis of continuous-time neural networks has received substantial attention, the discrete-time counterparts are equally important due to their relevance in computational implementations. As noted in
[
19
]
, discrete-time systems may not fully replicate the dynamics of their continuous-time analogs, thus it is worthwhile to study the stability of discrete-time neural network models with delays.
This paper focuses on providing sufficient conditions for the global attractivity of a general discrete-time non-autonomous Hopfield neural network model incorporating infinite distributed delays and time-varying delays in the leakage terms. Unlike many existing stability criteria based on
M
-matrices that require these matrices to be non-singular, our results allow for singular
M
-matrices, thereby broadening their applicability. As far as we are aware, the exceptions are the work of Hong and Ma
[
13
]
for an autonomous discrete-time Hopfield type model with finite delays and the work of Ma, Saito, and Takeuchi
[
17
]
for an autonomous continuous-time Hopfield type model also with finite delays.

In
[
20
]
, the author established sufficient conditions, involving non-singular
M-
matrices, for the existence and global asymptotic stability of an equilibrium in an autonomous continuous-time Hopfield neural network model with infinite distributed delays. Recently, in
[
10
]
, the main result from
[
20
]
was generalized to a continuous-time Cohen-Grossberg neural network model, also involving non-singular
M-
matrices. For continuous-time Bidirectional Associative Memory(BAM) neural network models with delays in the leakage terms,
[
4
]
established sufficient conditions for global asymptotic stability, again involving non-singular
M-
matrices. Subsequently, in
[
22
]
, the main result from
[
4
]
was improved, still assuming that a certain matrix is a non-singular
M-
matrix.

The stability of discrete-time neural network models has been less studied compared to continuous-time models, and stability results for discrete-time neural network models with infinite delays remain relatively scarce
[
6
,
21
]
. The use of models with infinite delays allows the modeling of phenomena where the present evolution depends on the entire past history. It is worth noting that difference equations with finite delays can be transformed into delay-free difference equations by increasing the dimension of the realization space
[
9
]
, which could potentially make their study easier.

In
[
3
,
8
,
21
,
16
]
, sufficient conditions for the global exponential stability of various discrete-time Hopfield neural network models involving non-singular
M-
matrices were established. Specifically,
[
3
]
provided sufficient conditions for the existence and global exponential stability of a periodic solution to a general difference equation with finite delays. This stability criterion was applied to both low-order and high-order periodic Hopfield neural network models, as well as to a BAM neural network model, with a finite delay in the leakage terms, yielding stability criteria involving non-singular
M-
matrices. In
[
8
]
, respectively in
[
16
]
, a criterion for the exponential stability of a discrete-time high-order, respectively low-order, Hopfield-type neural network model with finite delays and impulses was established, again involving non-singular
M-
matrices. In
[
21
]
a global exponential stability criterion was established for a general difference equation with infinite delays and applied to discrete-time Hopfield models with both infinite delays and finite delays in the leakage terms.

There are several studies on the stability of neural network models with delays on time-scales, but those that establish stability criteria involving
M-
matrices do so under the assumption that they are non-singular (see, for example,
[
12
]
).

The main contributions of this paper are:

i.

The new technique developed to establish the global stability criteria of the model employing the properties of M-matrices and irreducible singular M-matrices, without requiring their invertibility or the construction of Lyapunov functionals, as is often observed in the literature;

ii.

The global attractivity criterion, involving
M-
matrices non-necessarily invertible, established for the discrete-time non-autonomous Hopfield neural network model with infinite distributed and time-varying delays, system (
2
), assuming bounded activation functions that are not required to be differentiable or monotonic, item
i.
of Theorem
3.4
. It is worth noting that in
[
13
]
, the global attractivity criterion was obtained for the autonomous model with finite delays, system (
25
), assuming bounded, monotonic, and differentiable activation functions;

iii.

The stability criterion, involving singular irreducible
M-
matrices, for the same model (
2
) without the assumption that the activation functions are bounded, item
ii.
of Theorem
3.4
. We stress the importance of studying neural network models with unbounded activation functions
[
23
]
, such as Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU) functions.
To the best of our knowledge, existing stability criteria for neural network models with unbounded activation functions, when involving
M
-matrices, typically assume that these matrices are non-singular (see
[
8
,
21
]
and references therein).

After this introduction, Section
2
outlines the key notations, provides fundamental results from the theory of
M-
matrices, and introduces the discrete-time non-autonomous Hopfield neural network model considered in this work, which includes infinite distributed delays and time-varying delays in the leakage terms. Section

Preliminaries and Model Description

For
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
X_{\mathbb{Z}}
the set of integers numbers
X_{\mathbb{Z}}=X\cap\mathbb{Z}
. In this paper, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we always consider the Banach space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the norm

|\overline{x}|=|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|=\max\big\{|x_{i}|:i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}\big\}.

Given
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
\overline{x}\geq 0
if
x_{i}\geq 0
for all
i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\overline{x}>0
if
x_{i}>0
for all
i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
. We write
\overline{x}\overline{y}=(x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{n}y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for any
\overline{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\overline{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For a real number
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote its integer part by
\lfloor x\rfloor
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the set of
n\times n
real matrices by
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and, as usual, we define

Z^{n\times n}=\big\{A=[a_{ij}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}:a_{ij}\leq 0\text{ if }i\neq j\big\}.

Given
A=[a_{ij}],B=[b_{ij}]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, we write
A\geq B
if
a_{ij}\geq b_{ij}
for all
i,j\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
. We denote by
A^{T}
the transpose of matrix
A
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
A\in Z^{n\times n}
.

We say that
A
is an
M-
matrix
if all principal minors are non-negative.

We say that
A
is a
non-singular
M-
matrix
if all principal minors are positive.

A large number of equivalent properties exist for identifying
M-
matrices and non-singular
M-
matrices. For a detailed discussion of some of these properties, we refer the reader to Chapter 6 of
[
5
]
. In this paper, we need the following result:

Theorem 2.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 4.4.6]

Let
A\in Z^{n\times n}
.

The matrix
A
is an
M-
matrix if and only if, for any
\overline{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\neq\overline{0}
and taking
\overline{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=\left(A\overline{z}^{T}\right)^{T}
, there is
i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
z_{i}\neq 0
and
z_{i}y_{i}\geq 0
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
5
, Definition 2.1.2]

A square matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is
reducible
if there is a permutation matrix
P
such that

PAP^{T}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}B&0\\
C&D\end{array}\right],

where
B,D
are square matrices, or if
n=1
and
A=[0]
. Otherwise
A
is called
irreducible
.

We also need the following result:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
5
, Theorem 6.4.16]

If
A
is a singular irreducible
M-
matrix of order
n\in[2,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}}
, then there is
\overline{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})>0
such that
A\overline{d}^{T}=\overline{0}
.

For a continuous function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(0)=0
, we denote by
f^{*}
the function
f^{*}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
defined by

\displaystyle f^{*}(x)=\max\big\{f_{+}(x),f_{+}(-x),-f_{-}(x),-f_{-}(-x)\big\},\quad x\in[0,\infty),

(1)

where the functions
f_{+}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
f_{-}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by

f_{+}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\{f(u):u\in[0,x]\},&x\geq 0\\
\\
\max\{f(u):u\in[x,0]\},&x<0\end{array}\right.\,\text{and}\,f_{-}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\{f(u):u\in[0,x]\},&x\geq 0\\
\\
\min\{f(u):u\in[x,0]\},&x<0\end{array}\right.,

respectively. It is straightforward to verify that
f^{*}
is a non-decreasing continuous function such that
|f(x)|\leq f^{*}(|x|)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathcal{B}^{n}
the space of bounded functions
\overline{\psi}=(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}):(-\infty,0]_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with the norm

\left\|\overline{\psi}\right\|=\sup_{s\in(-\infty,0]_{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|\overline{\psi}(s)\right|.

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\overline{x}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
a vector function such that
\sup_{s\in(-\infty,0]_{\mathbb{Z}}}|\overline{x}(s)|<\infty
, we define
\overline{x}_{m}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
by

\overline{x}_{m}(s)=\overline{x}(m+s),\quad s\in(-\infty,0]_{\mathbb{Z}}.

In this paper, we investigate the following generalized discrete-time non-autonomous Hopfield neural network model that incorporates both infinite distributed delays and time-varying delays, with the latter also present in the leakage terms:

\displaystyle x_{i}(m+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}(m)x_{i}(m-\nu_{i}(m))+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}(m)f_{ijp}\left(x_{j}(m-\tau_{ijp}(m))\right)

(2)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}(m)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{ijl}g_{ij}(x_{j}(m-l))+I_{i}(m),\,\,i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
n,P\in\mathbb{N}
,
\zeta_{ijl}\in\mathbb{R}
,
a_{i}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to(-1,1)
,
\nu_{i},\tau_{ijp}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
b_{ijp},c_{ij},I_{i}:\mathbb{N}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
, and
f_{ijp},g_{ij}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are functions. This discrete-time model generalizes several existing models in the literature, including those investigated in
[
13
,
19
,
21
]
.

For model (
2
) we always consider initial conditions on
\mathcal{B}^{n}
, i.e., for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\overline{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
we consider

\displaystyle\overline{x}_{\sigma}=\overline{\psi}.

(3)

Trivially, for each
(\sigma,\overline{\psi})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{B}^{n}
, there exists a unique solution of initial value problem (
2
)-(
3
) and we denoted it by
\overline{x}(\cdot,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
.

3
Global attractiveness

In this section, we give two global attractivity criteria for model (
2
). For this purpose, we consider the following set of hypotheses: For each
i,j\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
p\in[1,P]_{\mathbb{Z}}
,

(H1)

we have

a_{i}^{+}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|a_{i}(m)|<1;

(H2)

we have

\lim_{m}(m-\nu_{i}(m))=\lim_{m}(m-\tau_{ijp}(m))=\infty;

(H3)

the functions
b_{ijp}
and
c_{ij}
are bounded;

(H4)

we have

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\zeta_{ijl}|=1;

(H5)

we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}|I_{i}(m)|<\infty.

Depending on whether the activation functions
f_{ijp}
and
g_{ij}
are bounded, one of the following hypothesis is also assumed.

(H6)

the functions
f_{ijp},g_{ij}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous and there exist constants
F_{ijp},G_{ij}>0
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|f_{ijp}(u)|\leq F_{ijp},&\text{if }|u|>1\\
|f_{ijp}(u)|<F_{ijp}|u|,&\text{if }|u|\leq 1\text{ and }u\neq 0\end{array}\right.\text{ and }\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}|g_{ij}(u)|\leq G_{ij},&\text{if }|u|>1\\
|g_{ij}(u)|<G_{ij}|u|,&\text{if }|u|\leq 1\text{ and }u\neq 0\end{array}\right.

or

(H6*)

the functions
f_{ijp},g_{ij}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous and there exist constants
F_{ijp},G_{ij}>0
such that

|f_{ijp}(u)|<F_{ijp}|u|\text{ if }u\neq 0\quad\text{ and }\quad|g_{ij}(u)|<G_{ij}|u|\text{ if }u\neq 0.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that, from (H6) or (H6*) we have
f_{ijp}(0)=g_{ij}(0)=0
for all
j,i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
p\in[1,P]_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

From (H3), we denote

\displaystyle b_{ijp}^{+}=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|b_{ijp}(m)|\quad\text{and}\quad c_{ij}^{+}=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|c_{ij}(m)|,

(4)

and

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{i}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}|a_{i}(m)|,\quad\hat{b}_{ijp}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}|b_{ijp}(m)|\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{c}_{ij}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}|c_{ij}(m)|.

(5)

Now, consider the matrices
\mathcal{M}^{+},\hat{\mathcal{M}}\in Z^{n\times n}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}:=\mathcal{D}^{+}-\left[c_{ij}^{+}G_{ij}+\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}^{+}F_{ijp}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}

(6)

where
\mathcal{D}^{+}:=diag(1-a_{1}^{+},\ldots,1-a_{n}^{+})
, and

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}:=\hat{\mathcal{D}}-\left[\hat{c}_{ij}G_{ij}+\sum_{p=1}^{P}\hat{b}_{ijp}F_{ijp}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n},

(7)

where
\hat{\mathcal{D}}:=diag(1-\hat{a}_{1},\ldots,1-\hat{a}_{n})
, respectively. Note that we have
\mathcal{M}^{+}\leq\hat{\mathcal{M}}
.

In order to obtain the stability criteria, we first need to prove that all solutions of (
2
) are bounded.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume hypotheses (H1)-(H5).

1.

If (H6) holds, then the solution
\overline{x}(\cdot,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
of (
2
)-(
3
) is bounded.

2.

If (H6*) holds and
\mathcal{M}^{+}
is a singular irreducible
M-
matrix, then the solution
\overline{x}(\cdot,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
of (
2
)-(
3
) is bounded.

Proof.

Proof of item
i
.

For
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\overline{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
, consider
\overline{x}(m)=(x_{1}(m),\ldots,x_{n}(m))=\overline{x}(m,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
the solution of (
2
)-(
3
). From (
2
), (
4
), and hypothesis (H6) we obtain

\displaystyle|x_{i}(m+1)|\leq a_{i}^{+}|x_{i}(m-\nu_{i}(m))|+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}^{+}F_{ijp}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}^{+}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\zeta_{ijl}|G_{ij}+|I_{i}(m)|,

and by the hypotheses (H4) and (H5), we have

\displaystyle|x_{i}(m+1)|\leq a_{i}^{+}|x_{i}(m-\nu_{i}(m))|+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{p=1}^{P}\left(b_{ijp}^{+}F_{ijp}\right)+c_{ij}^{+}G_{ij}\right]+\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|I_{i}(l)|,

that is

\displaystyle|x_{i}(m+1)|\leq a^{+}|x_{i}(m-\nu_{i}(m))|+C,\quad i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,\,m\in[\sigma,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}},

(8)

where
a^{+}=\displaystyle\max_{i\in[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\{a_{i}^{+}\right\}
and
C=\displaystyle\max_{i\in[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{p=1}^{P}\left(b_{ijp}^{+}F_{ijp}\right)+c_{ij}^{+}G_{ij}\right]+\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|I_{i}(l)|\right\}
.

We claim that

\displaystyle\|\overline{x}_{\sigma+q}\|\leq\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}(a^{+})^{k}=\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\frac{1-(a^{+})^{q}}{1-a^{+}},\quad q\in\mathbb{N},

(9)

and, since
a^{+}\in[0,1)
, we conclude that the solution
\overline{x}(m)
is bounded.

In fact, for
q=1
, inequality (
8
) implies

|x_{i}(\sigma+1)|\leq a^{+}\|\overline{\psi}\|+C,\quad i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},

and

\|\overline{x}_{\sigma+1}\|\leq\max\big\{\|\overline{\psi}\|,a^{+}\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\big\}\leq\|\overline{\psi}\|+C.

Assuming that (
9
) holds for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
, from (
8
) we have

|x_{i}(\sigma+q+1)|\leq a^{+}|x_{i}(\sigma+q-\nu_{i}(\sigma+q))|+C\leq a^{+}\|\overline{x}_{\sigma+q}\|+C

and by the induction hypothesis we obtain

\displaystyle\|\overline{x}_{\sigma+q+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|\overline{x}_{\sigma+q}\|,a^{+}\|\overline{x}_{\sigma+q}\|+C\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}(a^{+})^{k},a^{+}\left(\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}(a^{+})^{k}\right)+C\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|\overline{\psi}\|+C+C\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}(a^{+})^{k},a^{+}\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\sum_{k=1}^{q}(a^{+})^{k}+C\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\overline{\psi}\|+C+C\sum_{k=1}^{q}(a^{+})^{k}=\|\overline{\psi}\|+C\sum_{k=0}^{q}(a^{+})^{k},

and the proof of item
i
is done.

Proof of item
ii.

Since
\mathcal{M}^{+}
is a singular irreducible
M-
matrix, Theorem
2.2
implies the existence of
\overline{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})>0
such that

\mathcal{M}^{+}\overline{d}^{T}=\overline{0}
, that is

\displaystyle(1-a_{i}^{+})d_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{j}\left(c_{ij}^{+}G_{ij}+\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}^{+}F_{ijp}\right)=0,\quad i\in[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}.

(10)

With the change
y_{i}(m)=d_{i}^{-1}x_{i}(m)
, for all
i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, model (
2
) assumes the form

\displaystyle y_{i}(m+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}(m)y_{i}(m-\nu_{i}(m))+d_{i}^{-1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}\left(m\right)f_{ijp}(d_{j}y_{j}(m-\tau_{ijp}(m)))\right.

(11)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\left.c_{ij}(m)\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{ijl}g_{ij}(d_{j}y_{j}(m-l))\right]+d_{i}^{-1}I_{i}(m),\,\,i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

For
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\overline{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
, consider
\overline{x}(m)=(x_{1}(m),\ldots,x_{n}(m))=\overline{x}(m,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
the solution of (
2
)-(
3
).
Suppose, by contradiction, that the solution
\overline{x}(m)
is unbounded. Consequently
\overline{y}(m)=(y_{1}(m),\ldots,y_{n}(m))=(d_{1}^{-1}x_{1}(m),\ldots,d_{n}^{-1}x_{n}(m))
is also an unbounded solution of (
11
) and there exist
i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
m^{\star}\in(\sigma,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\displaystyle|y_{i}(m^{\star})|>\|\overline{y}_{m^{\star}-1}\|+\alpha_{i}|I_{i}(m^{\star}-1)|,

(12)

where
\alpha_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d_{i}^{-1},&\text{ if }a_{i}^{+}=0\\
1+\frac{1-d_{i}}{d_{i}a_{i}^{+}},&\text{ if }d_{i}<1\text{ and }a_{i}^{+}\neq 0\\
1,&\text{ if }d_{i}\geq 1\text{ and }a_{i}^{+}\neq 0\end{array}\right.
.
If not, then we have

|y_{i}(m)|\leq\|\overline{y}_{m-1}\|+\alpha_{i}|I_{i}(m-1)|,\quad i\in[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in(\sigma,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}},

thus

|\overline{y}(m)|\leq\|\overline{y}_{m-1}\|+|\overline{\alpha}||\overline{I}(m-1)|,\quad m\in(\sigma,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}},

where
\overline{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
and
\overline{I}(m)=(I_{1}(m),\ldots,I_{n}(m))
. Consequently

|\overline{y}(m)|\leq\|\overline{y}_{\sigma}\|+|\overline{\alpha}|\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|\overline{I}(l)|,\quad m\in[\sigma,\infty)_{\mathbb{Z}},

and, from (H5), we conclude that
\overline{y}(m)
is bounded which is a contradiction. Thus (
12
) holds.

From (
11
) and hypotheses (H3) and (H6*) we have

\displaystyle|y_{i}(m^{\star})|

\displaystyle x_{i}(m+1)=a_{i}x_{i}(m)+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij}f_{j}\left(x_{j}(m-\tau_{ij})\right),\,\,\quad i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(25)

and the matrix
\mathcal{M}
defined in (
24
) takes the form

\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{D}-\big[|b_{ij}|F_{j}\big]_{i,j=1}^{n},

where
\mathcal{D}=diag(1-|a_{1}|,\ldots,1-|a_{n}|)
.
We note that model (
25
) has finite delays. Consequently, it is possible to transform it into a non-delay discrete-time model, making it easier to handle than (
23
).

Remark 3.6
.

In
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
, the global attractivity of (
25
) is established under the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
is an
M-
matrix and the activation functions,
f_{j}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, are differentiable, satisfying the conditions

f_{j}(0)=0,\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}f_{j}(x)=1,\quad\lim_{x\to-\infty}f_{j}(x)=-1,

and

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{j}(0)=1>f^{\prime}_{j}(x)>0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

(26)

These conditions are more restrictive than hypothesis (H6). Additionally model (
23
) includes both infinite delays and delays in the leakage terms. Together, theses factors demonstrate that item
i.
in Corollary
3.5
provides a significant improvement over the global criterion presented in
[
13
]
. We note that the authors in
[
13
]
wrote the hypothesis
f^{\prime}_{j}(0)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}f_{j}^{\prime}(x)=1
instead of (
26
). However, this is inaccurate because (
26
) is need to obtain
\bar{f}_{j}(M_{j})<M_{j}
in
[
13
, page 4940, line 5]
.

Finally, we note that, to the best of our knowledge, stability criteria involving singular
M-
matrix along with unbounded activation functions have not been previously studied. Consequently, items
ii.
in Theorem
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
give answers to these situations.

Now, taking
P=2
,
\tau_{ij1}=0
,
I_{i}=0
, and
f_{ij1}=f_{ij2}=g_{ij}=g_{j}
in (
23
) for all
i,j\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we obtain the following model

\displaystyle x_{i}(m+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}x_{i}(m-\nu_{i})+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij1}g_{j}\left(x_{j}(m)\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}b_{ij2}g_{j}\left(x_{j}(m-\tau_{ij2})\right)

(27)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{ijl}g_{j}(x_{j}(m-l)),\,\,\quad i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

and the matrix
\mathcal{M}
defined in (
24
) has the form

\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{D}-\left[\big(|b_{ij1}|+|b_{ij2}|+|c_{ij}|\big)G_{j}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n},

where
\mathcal{D}=diag(1-|a_{1}|,\ldots,1-|a_{n}|)
.
Assume that
a_{i}\in(-1,1)
for all
i\in[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and that hypotheses (H4) and (H6*) hold. From Corollary
3.5
, we conclude that if
\mathcal{E}
is a singular irreducible
M-
matrix, then the zero solution of (
27
) is globally attractive within the set of solutions with bounded initial conditions.

Remark 3.7
.

It is worth noting that model (
27
) is also a particular case of a discrete-time Hopfield model studied in
[
21
, model (9)]
. Under the additional condition
\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{\xi l}|\zeta_{ijl}|<\infty
,
[
21
, Theorem 3.2]
allows us to conclude the global exponential stability of the zero solution of (
27
), provided that
\mathcal{E}
is a non-singular
M-
matrix.

Now, we present a global attractivity criterion for the case where the activation functions in (
23
) are Lipschitz functions.

Corollary 3.8
.

Assume that (
23
) has an equilibrium point
\overline{x}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and that hypotheses (H4) and

(H6
†
)

For each
i,j\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
p\in[1,P]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, there exist constants
F_{ijp},G_{ij}>0
such that

|f_{ijp}(u)-f_{ijp}(v)|<F_{ijp}|u-v|\quad\text{and}\quad|g_{ij}(u)-g_{ij}(v)|<G_{ij}|u-v|,

for
u,v\in\mathbb{R}
with
u\neq v
,

hold.

If
\mathcal{M}
, defined in (
24
), is a singular irreducible
M
-matrix, then for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\overline{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
, the solution
\overline{x}(\cdot,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
of (
23
) satisfies

\lim_{m\to\infty}|\overline{x}(m,\sigma,\overline{\psi})-\overline{x}^{*}|=0.

Proof.

Assume that
\overline{x}^{*}=(x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{n}^{*})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an equilibrium point of (
23
). Consequently,

\displaystyle x^{*}_{i}=a_{i}x^{*}_{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}f_{ijp}\left(x^{*}_{j}\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{ijl}g_{ij}(x^{*}_{j})+I_{i},\,\,\quad i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}.

Let
\sigma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\overline{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
. Consider
\overline{x}(m)=\overline{x}(m,\sigma,\overline{\psi})
the solution of (
23
) with initial condition
\overline{x}_{\sigma}=\overline{\psi}
.

The change of variables
\overline{z}(m)=(z_{1}(m),\ldots,z_{n}(m))=\overline{x}(m)-\overline{x}^{*}
transforms (
23
) into

\displaystyle z_{i}(m+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{i}z_{i}(m-\nu_{i})+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{P}b_{ijp}\tilde{f}_{ijp}\left(z_{j}(m-\tau_{ijp})\right)

(28)

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\zeta_{ijl}\tilde{g}_{ij}(z_{j}(m-l)),\,\,\quad i\in[1,n]_{\mathbb{Z}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

where
\tilde{f}_{ijp},\tilde{g}_{ij}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are defined by

\tilde{f}_{ijp}(u)=f_{ijp}(u+x^{*}_{j})-f_{ijp}(x^{*}_{j})
and
\tilde{g}_{ij}(u)=g_{ij}(u+x^{*}_{j})-g_{ij}(x^{*}_{j})
, for
u\in\mathbb{R}
, respectively.

From (H6
†
), we obtain
|\tilde{f}_{ijp}(u)|<F_{ijp}|u|
and
|\tilde{g}_{ij}(u)|<G_{ij}|u|
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
, thus (H6*) holds. Applying item
ii.
in Corollary
3.5
to the system (
28
) we conclude that

\lim_{m\to\infty}|\overline{z}(m)|=0,

which means that

\lim_{m\to\infty}|\overline{x}(m,\sigma,\overline{\psi})-\overline{x}^{*}|=0.

∎

5
Conclusions

In this work, we have established two global attractivity criteria for a discrete-time non-autonomous Hopfield neural network model with infinite distributed and time-varying delays (Theorem
3.4
). Notably, unlike the common assumptions in the literature, the stability criteria presented here involve
M
-matrices that are not necessarily invertible.

The first main stability criterion, outlined in item
i.
of Theorem
3.4
, assumes that the activation functions of the model are bounded and sublinear. The second main stability criterion, presented in item
ii.
of Theorem
3.4
, requires only that the activation functions be sublinear, with the additional assumption that the
M
-matrix is singular and irreducible.

The two numerical simulations provided illustrate the practical implications and insights offered by this work.

Parking Spaces for Complex Reflection Groups
Jason Stack

Abstract
. We answer an open problem of [ ARR15 ] and [ Rho14 ] , extending their work to irreducible well–generated complex reflection groups W W . We define a combinatorial W W -noncrossing parking space and an algebraic W W -parking space for such W W , and exhibit a ( W × C ) (W\times C) -equivariant isomorphism between the two. As a consequence of this isomorphism, we enumerate the W W -noncrossing parking functions. Finally, we extend our results to the Fuss case. We prove the results for all such complex reflection groups except G 34 G_{34} , E 7 , E_{7}, and E 8 E_{8} .

1.1.
Noncrossing Partitions

One of the most studied noncrossing objects in Coxeter–Catalan combinatorics are the

A

[n]\coloneq\{1,2,\dots,n\}

and

for

Figure

n
nodes around a circle. We label the nodes in clockwise order by the elements of
[n]
with the condition that the intersection of the convex hulls of distinct blocks is empty, hence the name
noncrossing
partitions.

Parking Functions

Nonnesting Parking Functions

-orbits.

Fuss Analogues

There is a Fuss analogue of the classical parking functions dependent on a
Fuss parameter

. A

-parking function

via coordinate permutation.

Generalizing to the crystallographic case, the finite torus

carries a
W
-action and plays the role of the Fuss parking functions. There is a parametrization of

by certain geometric multichains in
\operatorname{NN}(W)
arising from the hyperplane arrangement of
W

[
Ath99
]
. This suggests an analogous definition using the noncrossing partitions, which we refer to as the
k
-
W
-noncrossing parking functions

[
Rho14
]
. Similarly to the

k
-
W
-noncrossing parking space carries an action of
W\times\mathbb{Z}_{kh}
. We discuss this space in more detail in
Subsection

2.3
.

1.3.
Algebraic Parking Space

D. Armstrong, V. Reiner, and B. Rhoades construct a second parking space purely algebraically
[
ARR15
,
Rho14
]
. Let
W
be a Coxeter group with reflection representation
V
and fix a Coxeter element
c\in W
. The
algebraic
W
-parking space
(or the
k
-algebraic
W
-parking space
in the Fuss case) relies on a choice of a degree
p

homogeneous system of parameters
,
(\Theta_{p})
, and the quotient
S/(\Theta_{p}-{\bf x})
, where
S\coloneq\operatorname{Sym}{V^{*}}
. We choose
\Theta_{h+1}
of degree
h+1
(or
kh+1
) and have that this quotient carries a
(W\times C)
-action (or
(W\times\mathbb{Z}_{kh})
-action). D. Armstrong, V. Reiner, and B. Rhoades construct isomorphisms between the
W
-noncrossing parking functions and the algebraic
W
-parking space that are invariant under these actions
[
ARR15
]
. B. Rhoades does the same in the Fuss case
[
Rho14
]
.

In this paper, we extend the work of
[
ARR15
]
and
[
Rho14
]
to irreducible well–generated complex reflection groups. For these groups, we have well defined notions of Coxeter elements, a Coxeter number, and noncrossing partitions. We are then able to define a purely combinatorial noncrossing parking space in analogy with that defined for finite real reflection groups. We then define a second parking space purely algebraically, extending the construction from the real case
[
ARR15
,
Rho14
]
. We give an example of the existence of specific hsops for the exceptional group
G_{10}
in

W

-parking space in

2.1.
Noncrossing Partitions in Real Reflection Groups

We will begin with a review of noncrossing partitions for real reflection groups. Let
(W,S)
be a finite Coxeter system, with simple generators
S=\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
and fix a
Coxeter element

c

Coxeter number

h

Subsection

Definition 2.1
.

The

\ell_{T}

is the smallest

with
t_{i}\in T

u\leq_{T}v\iff\ell_{T}(v)=\ell_{T}(u)+\ell_{T}(u^{-1}v).

This poset is graded with rank function
\ell_{T}
. Its unique minimal element is the identity

, and among its maximal elements are the Coxeter elements (which form a single conjugacy class if
W
is crystallographic)
[
Spr74
]
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Bes03
]
[

]
)
.

The poset of
W
-noncrossing partitions
,
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
, is the interval
[e,c]_{T}
in the absolute order.

In
[

]
, T. Brady and C. Watt showed there is an embedding

Definition 2.6

Let

\pi_{0}\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{k}=c\text{\hskip 28.45274pt and \hskip 28.45274pt}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\ell_{T}(\pi_{i})=\ell_{T}(c).

\displaystyle=(\pi_{1},\pi_{1}^{-1}\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k-1}^{-1}\pi_{k},\pi_{k}^{-1}c)

\displaystyle\int:

\displaystyle NC_{k}(W)

\displaystyle\rightarrow NC^{k}(W)

\displaystyle\int(\pi_{0},\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{k})

\displaystyle=(\pi_{0}\leq\pi_{0}\pi_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\pi_{0}\pi_{1}\cdots\pi_{k-1}).

In

2.4.
Complex Reflection Groups

In this section we will recall the necessary background concerning complex reflection groups, much of which is found in
[
LT09
]
.

2.4.1.
Notation

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space. A linear transformation
g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
is called a
reflection
if the order of
g
is finite and the subspace
\mathrm{Fix}(g)
is of codimension 1. When
g
is a reflection, we call
\mathrm{Fix}(g)
the
reflecting hyperplane
of
g
.

Definition 2.8
.

A
(finite) complex reflection group
is a finite subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(V)
that is generated by reflections.

For a complex reflection group
W
, we will denote the reflections of
W
by
\mathcal{R}
and their associated arrangement of hyperplanes by
\mathcal{A}
.

2.4.2.
Classification

A complex reflection group
W
is
irreducible
if
V
is an irreducible
W
-module. The finite irreducible complex reflection groups are classified—there is one infinite family, denoted
G(d,e,n)
for
e|d
, and 34 exceptional groups, numbered
G_{4},\ldots,G_{37}

[
ST54
]
.

Since we will be checking our constructions case-by-case, we recall the definition of
G(d,e,n)
here.
Let
\mu_{d}
be the cyclic group of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
containing
d^{th}
roots of unity. Consider
G
a group acting on the set
[n]
and
V
a complex inner product space of dimension
n
having orthonormal basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
. Then the
standard monomial representation
of
\mu_{d}\wr G
is

(h,g)e_{i}=h_{g(i)}e_{g(i)}.

With
e
a divisor of
d
, define

A(d,e,n)\coloneq\{(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})\in\mu^{n}_{d}:(h_{1}\cdots h_{n})^{d/e}=1\}.

Then,
G(d,e,n)\coloneq A(d,e,n)\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Note that
G(d,e,n)
is a subgroup of
\mu_{d}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and as such it may be represented as a group of linear transformations in the standard monomial representation.

2.4.3.
Invariant Theory

Let
W
be an irreducible complex reflection group with reflection representation
V
of dimension
n
. Let
S\coloneq\operatorname{Sym}(V^{*})\cong\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the symmetric algebra of
V^{*}
. Then, there is an action of
W
on
S
via
(w\cdot f)(v)=f(w^{-1}v)
with
f\in S
,
w\in W
, and
v\in V
. The
algebra of invariants
,

, is generated by algebraically independent homogeneous polynomials
[
Che55
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

For
W
an irreducible complex reflection group, the
degrees
,
d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
, of
W
are the degrees of the homogeneous polynomials generating
S^{W}
.

Let
S^{W}_{+}
be the ideal of
S
generated by the positive degree elements of
S^{W}
. Then, the action of
W
on the
coinvariant algebra
,
S/S^{W}_{+}
, is the regular representation. So for any irreducible representation
M
of
W
, with
M
having dimension
r
,
S/S_{+}^{W}
contains exactly
r
copies of
M
.

Definition 2.10
.

For
M
as above, the
exponents
of
M
are defined to be the degrees,
e_{1}(M)\leq\cdots\leq e_{r}(M)
, of the homogeneous components of
S/S_{+}^{W}
containing a copy of
M
. The degrees of
W
satisfy
d_{i}=e_{i}(V)+1
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. The
codegrees
,
d_{1}^{*}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}^{*}
, of
W
are defined by
d_{i}^{*}\coloneq e_{n-i+1}(V^{*})-1
.

2.4.4.
Well–Generated Complex Reflection Groups and Regular Elements

To define the noncrossing partitions for complex reflection groups, we restrict to the irreducible
well–generated
complex reflection groups, for which we have the notion of a Coxeter element.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
W
be an irreducible complex reflection group with reflection representation
V
of dimension
n
. Then,
W
is
well–generated
if
W
is generated by
n
reflections. Equivalently, the degrees and codegrees satisfy
d_{i}+d_{i}^{*}=d_{n}
. For an irreducible well–generated complex reflection group, we define the
Coxeter number

h\coloneq d_{n}
, the highest degree.

The irreducible well–generated complex reflection groups are the two infinite subfamilies—types
G(d,1,n)
and
G(d,d,n)
—as well as the exceptional groups

G_{i}\text{ where }i\in\{4,5,6,8,9,10,14,16,17,18,20,21,23,\dots,30,32,\dots,37\}.

For irreducible well–generated complex reflection groups (where, in contrast to real reflection groups, we have no simple reflections), some care must be taken to define Coxeter elements
[
Spr74
]
.

Definition 2.12
.

A vector
v\in V
is
regular
if it is not contained in any reflection hyperplane
H\in\mathcal{A}
.
For
\zeta
a root of unity, an element
c\in W
is
\zeta
-regular
if it has a regular eigenvector with eigenvalue
\zeta
. In this case, we say that the order
r
of
\zeta
is a
regular number
for
W
.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
LS99
,
LM03
]
)
.

A positive integer
r
is a regular number for
W
if and only if
r
divides the same number of degrees and codegrees.

For
W
an irreducible well–generated complex reflection group, the Coxeter number
h
is a regular number for
W
—in this case, the degrees are distinct,
d_{i}^{*}=d_{n-i+1}
, and
h=d_{n}
is the largest degree, so
h
only divides
d_{n}
and
d_{1}^{*}
. It follows that there exists a
\zeta
-regular element for every
h^{\text{th}}
root of unity
\zeta
.

Definition 2.14
.

The
Coxeter elements
of
W
are the
\zeta
-regular elements of
W
, for
\zeta
an
h^{th}
root of unity.

For Weyl groups,
Definition

2.14
is slightly more general than the usual definition of Coxeter element as a product of the simple reflections in any order—for example, any long cycle in the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a regular element for an
n^{th}
root of unity—but this just extends the usual definition by conjugation. For real reflection groups,
Definition

2.14
is even more general, as we can have non-conjugate Coxeter elements
[
RRS17
]
.

3.
Results

We now define the noncrossing partitions and noncrossing parking functions, both combinatorial and algebraic, for irreducible well–generated complex reflection groups. We then present our main results.

3.1.

Let

Definition 3.1
.

For

For notational convenience, we drop the

in

Definition 3.2

For

Proposition 3.3

(

]

Let

be Coxeter elements. Then

Consequently, we will drop the

D. Bessis and R. Corran give models for the noncrossing partitions of types

Having defined the noncrossing partitions in this setting, we may follow the same procedure as in the case of real reflection groups to define the noncrossing parking functions. For

We similarly define the Fuss noncrossing parking space built out of multichains in

For

For

(

The

Proof of
Theorem

3.21
for
G(d,d,n)

With
G(d,d,n)
acting on
V=\mathbb{C}^{n}
with the standard coordinate functionals
{\bf x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
, choose

\Theta_{kh+1}=(x_{1}^{kd(n-1)+1},\ldots,x_{n}^{kd(n-1)+1}).

By
Remark

3.15
,
(\Theta_{kh+1})
is an hsop of degree
kd(n-1)+1=kh+1
carrying
V^{*}(-kh-1)
and the map
x_{i}\to\theta_{i}
is
W
-equivariant. The subvariety cut out by
(\Theta_{kh+1}-\bf{x})
is

V^{\Theta_{kh+1}}=\{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:v_{i}=0\text{ or }v_{i}^{kd(n-1)}=1\text{ for all }i\},

and we have that
|V^{\Theta_{kh+1}}|=(kh+1)^{n}
. We now describe the forward isomorphism in this case

M(k,n,d)\xrightarrow{f_{d,k}}V^{\Theta_{kh+1}}.

To begin, we pass our partition
\pi
restricted to
[k(n-1)]^{d}
(ignoring the inner annulus) into an infinite parenthesization of the string

\ldots 1^{1},\ldots,(k(n-1))^{1},1^{d},\ldots,(k(n-1))^{d},1^{d-1},\ldots,(k(n-1))^{d-1},\ldots,1^{2},\ldots,(k(n-1))^{d},\ldots,

and extend the definition of
opener
to outer and annular blocks. We define the map
f_{d,k}
by sending
(\pi,\ell)\in M(k,n,d)
to
(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
where

•

v_{r}=0
if
B_{0}\in\pi
is the zero block and
r\in\ell(B_{0})
,

•

v_{r}=0
if
I\in\pi
is an inner block and
r\in\ell(I)
, or

•

v_{r}=\lambda^{j}\omega^{i}
if
B
is an outer or annular block opened by
i^{j}
and
r^{1}\in\ell(B)
,

where
\omega=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{k(n-1)}}
and
\lambda=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{d}}
.

Let

\pi
must have only annular and outer blocks. Let

be the number of occurrences of
\lambda^{j}\omega^{r}
for any

km_{r}-m^{\prime}_{r}
where

and

8.
Future Work

We plan to continue our study relating to the noncrossing parking functions in complex reflection groups. Recent work has been done in regard to the poset topology of the noncrossing parking functions for finite real reflection groups
[
DJV
]
. We plan to extend this work to well–generated complex reflection groups.

First, let us briefly introduce some notions of poset topology. Let
\Gamma
be a finite simplicial complex, identified with its face poset with inclusion its order relation. We say
\Gamma
is
pure
if its facets (the maximal faces) all have the same dimension. Let
P
be a finite poset. If
P
has a unique minimal element and a unique maximal element, we will write
\overline{P}
to denote the
proper part
obtained by removing said unique minimal and maximal elements. For
P
a finite poset, the
order complex
,
\Omega(P)
, of
P
is the simplicial complex having
P
as vertices and pairwise comparable elements as its faces.

Let
W
be a finite real reflection group (not necessarily irreducible) of rank
n
and having Coxeter number
h
. The noncrossing parking function poset was initially introduced by P. Edelman
[
Ede80
]
as
2
-partitions and further studied in
[
DOJVR22
]
. In regards to the topology of the noncrossing parking functions, B. Delcroix-Oger, M. Josuat-Vergës, and L. Randazzo handle the type
A
case using the shellability of the poset
[
DOJVR22
]
. For general finite real reflection groups, T. Douvropoulos and M. Josuat-Vergës describe the homotopy type of the noncrossing parking function poset as a pure wedge of spheres dependent on
n
and
h

[
DJV
]
, obtaining the following result:

Theorem 8.1

(
[
DJV
]
)
.

For
W
a finite real reflection group,
\Omega(\overline{\operatorname{Park}^{\mathrm{NC}}(W)})\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{\vee(h-1)^{n}}
,
where
\mathbb{S}^{m}
is the
m
-dimensional sphere.

Similar to the work in this paper, we wish to extend the work of T. Douvropoulos and M. Josuat-Vergës in
[
DJV
]
to well–generated complex reflection groups.

Conjecture 8.2
.

For
W
a well–generated complex reflection group of rank
n
and Coxeter number
h
,

\Omega(\overline{\operatorname{Park}^{\mathrm{NC}}(W)})\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{\vee(h-1)^{n}}.

Classification of irreducible 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} -diagonalizable H ℓ , n H_{\ell,n} -modules Thanks: This work was supported by ANID-FONDECYT, Chile Postdoctoral Grant #3230329
Elizabeth Manosalva P

Abstract
. We give a classification for the irreducible 𝔲 \mathfrak{u} -diagonalizable representations of the degenerate affine Hecke algebra of type G ⁡ ( ℓ , 1 , n ) G(\ell,1,n) . Precisely we show that such H ℓ , n H_{\ell,n} -modules are indexed by ℓ \ell -skew shapes and that the representation indexed by a skew shape D D has a basis of eigenvectors indexed by standard Young tableaux of shape D D .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context

This article is contribution to the study of the representations of the affine Hecke algebras, in particular of the degenerate affine Hecke algebra of type
G(\ell,1,n)
, also known as graded Hecke algebra for the group
G(\ell,1,n)
.

The affine Hecke algebra arise in several combinatoric and algebraic frameworks, since is closely related to the geometry and representation theory of a semisimple Lie group and plays an important role in the Springer correspondence and the Langlands classification. Moreover Lusztig’s work
[
12
]
shows that it is possible to recover geometric information contained in the affine Hecke algebra from the corresponding Hecke algebra. We also recall that the theory of intertwining operators originates from the study of affine Hecke algebra representations
[
14
]
and it has played an important role in the theory of orthogonal polynomials, in particular the study of Macdonald polynomials
[
2
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
.

More recently this algebra has played an important role in the study of the unitary representations of the rational Cherednik algebra of type
G(\ell,1,n)
(or cyclotomic rational Cherednik algebra) thus giving the motivation for this work. The cyclotomic rational Cherednik algebra possesses a subalgebra which is a
G(\ell,1,n)
-analog of the degenerate affine Hecke algebra. Hence the classification theorem of the irreducible diagonalizable representations of
H_{\ell,n}
given in this article together with results due to Griffeth
[
9
]
, Ciubotaru
[
3
]
and Huang-Wong
[
10
]
leads us to the combinatorial formula for the
V^{*}
-homology of the unitary representations of the cyclotomic rational Cherednik algebra
[
7
]
. As the part of the proof having to do with the representation theory of
H_{\ell,n}
was only sketched in
[
7
]
this work comes to complement said article by delivering the detailed results in that matter.

The techniques used to obtain the results in this paper are mainly combinatorial, taking advantage of the combinatorial behavior of the group
G(\ell,1,n)
and its representation as well as the affine Hecke algebra attached to
G(\ell,1,n)
. This work is intended to be mostly self-contained and states most of the needed definitions and results used in it. Nonetheless some results involving the cyclotomic rational Cherednik algebra may be needed and for which we refer to
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
, for instance.

1.2.
Statement of results

Here we will state the main theorem of this work as well as the objects involved somewhat more precisely, referring to the body of the paper when appropriate. A detailed explanation of the combinatorial objects involved in the statement of Theorem
1.1
are given in further sections. For the moment, as a guide to the reader we mention:
Let
H_{\ell,n}
be the degenerate affine Hecke algebra (
2.5
). Let
\mathfrak{u}
be a subalgebra of
H_{\ell,n}
with
u_{i},\zeta_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}
(
2.4
). Given
M
an
H_{\ell,n}
-module,
M
is called
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable if it has a basis consisting of simultaneous eigenvectors for
\mathfrak{u}
.
An
\ell
-skew shape
D
is a sequence
D=(D^{0},D^{1},\ldots D^{\ell-1})
of
\ell
skew diagrams (
2.8
). For a box
b
of
D
in the component
D^{j}
we write
{\rm ct}(b)=x-y
and
\beta(b)=j
for the
content
and
coordinate
of
b
, respectively. A standard Young tableau
T
of
D
is a filling of the boxes of
D
with positive integer numbers which is left to right and top to bottom increasing in each coordinate
D^{i}
of
D
and each connected component. These ingredients given, we may state the theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable
H_{\ell,n}
-module and
m\in M
satisfies

u_{i}m=a_{i}m\quad\hbox{and}\quad\zeta_{i}m=\zeta^{b_{i}}m\quad\hbox{for }1\leq i\leq n.

Then there is a standard Young tableau
T
on a
\ell
-skew shape
D
such that
a_{i}=\ell{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(i))
and
b_{i}=\beta(T^{-1}(i))
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
M\cong S^{D}
, and moreover
T
and
D
are unique up to diagonal slides of their connected components.

This theorem gives a classification of the irreducible
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable
H_{\ell,n}
-modules, we provide an algorithm to determine explicitly the diagram
D
which represents the corresponding equivalence class in the proof of this theorem. We recall that the case
\ell=1
was first worked out by Cherednik
[
1
]
.

2.
Definitions and preliminaries

This section is mostly dedicated to give in detail the needed definitions, notations and state results in order to prove the main theorem of this paper.

2.1.
Combinatorics.

We recall some definitions and basic notions of partitions and standard young tableaux used in further sections.

A
partition
is a non-increasing sequence
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 0
of positive integers. We identify a partition with its Young diagram and visualize it as a collection of boxes. The
diagram
of
\lambda
is the collection of points
(x,y)
for
x
and
y
integer numbers satisfying
1\leq y\leq\ell
and
1\leq x\leq\lambda_{y}
, which we think of as a subset of
\mathbf{R}^{2}
.

For a fixed integer
\ell>0
, an
\ell
-partition
is a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{\ell-1})
of partitions, some of which may be empty. If there are
n
total boxes in
\lambda
then we say that
\lambda
is an
\ell
-partition of
n
and we write
P_{\ell,n}
for the set of all
\ell
-partitions of
n
.

We order the boxes of each
\ell
-partition as follows, let
b=(x,y)
and
b^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
then

b\leq b^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow\beta(b)=\beta(b^{\prime})\hbox{ with }x^{\prime}-x\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}\hbox{ and }y^{\prime}-y\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}

Let
\lambda
be an
\ell
-partition of
n
, we define a
standard Young tableau
of
\lambda
as the filling of the boxes of
\lambda
with the numbers
1,2,\ldots,n
which is left to right and top to bottom increasing in each coordinate and think of it as a bijection between the boxes of
\lambda
and the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. We denote by SYT(
\lambda
) to the set of all standard Young tableau of
\lambda
.
For each box
b=(x,y)
of the partition
\lambda^{k}
of an
\ell
-partition
\lambda
we define the
content
and
coordinate
of
b
as
{\rm ct}(b):=x-y
and
\beta(b):=k
, respectively.

2.2.
The group
G(\ell,1,n)

By the classification and notation of Shephard and Todd
[
18
]
for complex reflection groups we denote by
G(\ell,1,n)
to the group abstractly defined as the wreath product of the cyclic group of
\ell
elements
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}
and the symmetric group
S_{n}
, which acts on
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbf{C}^{n}
in the obvious way. Let
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
be the standard basis of
\mathfrak{h}
then the group
G(\ell,1,n)
consists of all matrices of size
n
by
n
with exactly one non-zero entry in each row and column, which is an
\ell
th root of unity.

Note that with this characterization, the group
G(1,1,n)
is the group of permutation matrices of size
n
by
n
, which is isomorphic to the symmetric group
S_{n}
. And the group
G(2,1,n)
is the group of signed permutation matrices also known as the Weyl group of type
B_{n}
.

For a fixed primitive
\ell
th root of unity
\zeta
, we denote by
\zeta_{i}
to the diagonal matrix with ones all over the diagonal except in the position
i
where there is
\zeta
,
s_{ij}
denotes the permutation matrix which exchanges coordinates
i
and
j
, and
s_{i}=s_{i,i+1}
. With this notation we can write uniquely each element of
G(\ell,1,n)
as

\zeta^{a}w,\mbox{ with }w\in S_{n},a\in\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\ell}^{n}

where
\zeta^{a}=\zeta_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\zeta_{n}^{a_{n}}
.

The irreducible complex representations of
G(\ell,1,n)
are in bijection with
\ell
-partitions
\lambda
of
n
, and for
\lambda\in P_{\ell,n}
we write
S^{\lambda}
for the corresponding irreducible representation
[
15
]
. The
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
-module
S^{\lambda}
possesses a basis indexed by the set SYT(
\lambda
). Also we note that

\dim S\lambda=n!\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\prod_{b\in\lambda^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{b}}

where
h_{b}
is the length of the hook with respect to the box
b
.

We write
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbf{C}^{n}
for the defining representation, which is irreducible for
\ell>1
. Given
\lambda\in P_{\ell,n}
we define its
transpose

\lambda^{t}
as the
\ell
-partition obtained from
\lambda
by cycling its components one spot to the left and transposing them all, i.e.

\lambda^{t}=\big((\lambda^{1})^{t},(\lambda^{2})^{t},\ldots,(\lambda^{\ell-1})^{t},(\lambda^{0})^{t}\big)

where
\mu^{t}
denotes the classical transpose of the partition
\mu
.
With this indexing of the complex irreducible representations of
G(\ell,1,n)
, the representation indexed by
\lambda^{t}
is the tensor product of the representation
\Lambda^{n}\mathfrak{h}
.

2.3.
Jucys-Murphy elements.

The following analogs of the Jucys-Murphy elements in the group algebra
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
were introduced in
[
15
]
. In this section we mention some properties which will be useful in further sections. Define

\phi_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<i\\
0\leq k\leq\ell-1\end{subarray}}\zeta_{i}^{k}s_{ij}\zeta_{i}^{-k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j<i\\
0\leq k\leq\ell-1\end{subarray}}\zeta_{j}^{k}s_{ij}\zeta_{j}^{-k}\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Besides the expected property for
\phi_{i}
(which is to commute with every element of

\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,i-1)
), elements
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}
are pairwise commutative and satisfy the following relations

(2.1)

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}\phi_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{j}\zeta_{i}\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq n\mbox{ and }1\leq j\leq n.

(2.2)

\displaystyle s_{i}\phi_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{j}s_{i}\mbox{ for }j\neq i,i+1.

(2.3)

\displaystyle s_{i}\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{i+1}s_{i}-\pi_{i}\mbox{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1\mbox{, where }\pi_{i}=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\ell-1}\zeta_{i}^{k}\zeta_{i+1}^{-k}.

2.4.
Subalgebra
\mathfrak{u}

Let
\mathfrak{u}
be the subalgebra of
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
generated by the Jucys-Murphy elements
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}
and the diagonal matrices
\zeta_{i},\ldots,\zeta_{n}
. The action of
\mathfrak{u}
over a
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
-module
S^{\mu}
is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{i}v_{T}

\displaystyle=\ell{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(i))(v_{T})

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}v_{T}

\displaystyle=\zeta^{B(T^{-1}(i))}v_{T}

where the set
\{v_{T}|T\in SYT(\mu)\}
is a basis for
S^{\mu}
.

Let
M
be a
\mathfrak{u}
-module, for a given
\mathfrak{u}
-eigenvector
m\in M
we define the
weight
of the vector
m
to be the tuple

wt(m)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}})

if
\phi_{i}m=a_{i}m
and
\zeta_{i}m=\zeta^{b_{i}}m
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)

The algebra
\mathfrak{u}
acts semisimple on each
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
-module
M
.

(b)

Let
M
be a
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
-module and let
m\in M
be a
\mathfrak{u}
-weight vector with

wt(m)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}})

then

\displaystyle(a_{i},\zeta^{b_{i}})\neq(a_{i+1},\zeta^{b_{i+1}})\hbox{ for }1\leq i\leq n-1

Proof.

Let
M
be a
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
-module, note that
\phi_{i}
is a self adjoint operator and
\zeta_{i}
is a unitary operator with respect to any
W
-invariant positive definite hermitian form on
M
. Then
\mathfrak{u}
is a commutative algebra, which proves (a).

For (b) suppose that
(a_{i},\zeta^{b_{i}})=(a_{i+1},\zeta^{b_{i+1}})
for some
1\leq i\leq n-1
, then by the relation (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle\phi_{i}s_{i}\cdot m=(s_{i}\phi_{i+1}-\pi_{i})\cdot m=(s_{i}a_{i+1}-\pi)m=a_{i+1}s_{i}m-\ell m

hence
(\phi_{i}-a_{i})s_{i}m=-\ell m\neq 0
while
(\phi_{i}-a_{i})^{2}s_{i}m=-(\phi_{i}-a_{i})\cdot\ell m=0
so
si_{i}m
is a generalized eigenvector, which is not an eigenvector for
\phi_{i}
and hence contradicting (a).
∎

2.5.
The
degenerate affine Hecke algebra
of type
G(\ell,1,n)

In the following section we define the
degenerate affine Hecke algebra
which was introduced by A. Ram and V. Shepler in
[
17
]
as the graded Hecke algebra for
G(\ell,1,n)
, it is also called
generalized graded Hecke algebra
by C. Dezélée in
[
4
]
.

The
degenerate affine Hecke algebra of type
G(\ell,1,n)
is the algebra
H_{\ell,n}
generated by
\mathbf{C}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}]
and the group
G(\ell,1,n)
subject to the relations

(2.4)

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}u_{j}

\displaystyle=u_{j}\zeta_{i},\quad\mbox{for all }i,j

(2.5)

\displaystyle s_{i}u_{j}

\displaystyle=u_{j}s_{i},\quad\mbox{if }j\neq i,i+1

(2.6)

\displaystyle s_{i}u_{i}

\displaystyle=u_{i+1}s_{i}-\pi_{i}

where

\pi_{i}=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\zeta_{i}^{k}\zeta_{i+1}^{-k}.

Since
H_{\ell,n}
can be injected onto the rational Cherednik algebra of type
G(\ell,1,n)
the next two important results are consequences of the well-known PBW theorem for the rational Cherednik algebra (see
[
5
]
). For detailed proof of this two statements we refer to
[
4
]
,
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(PBW)
.

The set
\{u_{i_{1}}u_{i_{2}}\cdots u_{i_{p}}w|1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{p}\leq n,w\in W\}
is a
\mathbf{C}
-basis for
H_{\ell,n}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The center of
H_{\ell,n}
is
\mathbf{C}[u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}]^{S_{n}}\otimes\mathbf{C}[\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\ldots,\zeta_{n}]^{S_{n}}
.

Note that from Theorem
2.2
we also have that
H_{\ell,n}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{C}[u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n}]\otimes\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
as vector spaces.

2.6.
Intertwining operators

For
1\leq i\leq n
define the intertwining operators
\tau_{i}
by

(2.7)

\tau_{i}=s_{i}+\dfrac{c_{0}}{u_{i}-u_{i+1}}\pi_{i}.

Note that the operator
\tau_{i}
is well-defined on
H_{\ell,n}
-modules since
u_{i}-u_{i+1}
is invertible on the image of
\pi_{i}
.

The following proposition states some needed properties of the intertwining operators of
H_{\ell,n}
. As before this proof takes advantage of the injection of
H_{\ell,n}
onto the rational Cherednik algebra nevertheless in this case we are able to give a sketch a proof since the results involved are not so deep.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
M
be a
H_{\ell,n}
-module and
m\in M
such that

wt(m)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}}).

(a)

wt(\tau_{i}m)=s_{i}(wt(m))
where
S_{n}
acts on the set of
2n
-tuples by simultaneously permuting the subindices of
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
.

(b)

\tau_{i}^{2}=\dfrac{(u_{i}-u_{i+1}-\pi_{i})(u_{i}-u_{i+1}+\pi_{i})}{(u_{i}-u_{i+1})^{2}}

(c)

\tau_{i}\tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}=\tau_{i+1}\tau_{i}\tau_{i+1}

Sketch of proof.

To prove part (
a
) we use

(2.8)

(u_{i}-u_{i+1})\tau_{i}=(u_{i}-u_{i+1})s_{i}+\pi_{i}

(which is an element of the rational Cherednik algebra attached to
G(\ell,1,n)
) instead of (
2.7
) and using the relations between
\tau_{i}
and
u_{i}
in this algebra we check by straightforward calculation that
\tau_{i}m
is an eigenvector with eigenvalue

(2.9)

\displaystyle s_{i}((a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}}))

\displaystyle=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1},a_{i},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{i+1}},\zeta^{b_{i}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}})

For (
b
) we compute

(2.10)

((u_{i}-u_{i+1})\tau_{i})^{2}=(u_{i}-u_{i+1})\tau_{i}(u_{i}-u_{i+1})\tau_{i}

then by (
2.8
) and forward calculations we obtain the desired formula for
\tau_{i}^{2}
.

And part (
c
) follows by using that
u_{i}u_{i+1}u_{i}=u_{i+1}u_{i}u_{i+1}
by (a long) straightforward calculation.
∎

2.7.
H_{\ell,n}
-modules via branching for
G(\ell,1,n)

Let
m\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}
. Then for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq n-1
there exists a map
H_{\ell,n}\to\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,m+n)
given by

\displaystyle u_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\phi_{m+i}

\displaystyle s_{j}

\displaystyle\mapsto s_{m+j}

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\zeta_{m+i}

The image of this map is contained in the centralizer
C_{G(\ell,1,m+n)}G(\ell,1,m)
so that
H_{\ell,n}
acts on the module

S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}=\Hom_{\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,m)}(S^{\mu},\Res_{m}^{m+n}(S^{\lambda}))

for all pairs
\lambda,\mu
of
\ell
-partitions, where
\lambda
is an
\ell
-partition of
m+n
and
\mu
is an
\ell
-partition of
m
. It follows from Young’s orthogonal form that
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}=0
unless
\mu\subseteq\lambda
in which case
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
has a basis indexed by the set of standard Young tableaux on the skew diagram
\lambda\setminus\mu
.

Given
T\in\syt(\mu)
and
U\in\syt(\lambda\setminus\mu)
we define
T\cup U\in\syt(\lambda)
by

T\cup U(b)=\begin{cases}T(b),&\hbox{if }b\in\mu\\
U(b)+m,&\hbox{if }b\in\lambda\setminus\mu\end{cases}

Then

S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}=\Hom_{\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,m)}(S^{\mu},\Res_{m}^{m+n}(S^{\lambda}))

and we define
\psi_{U}\in S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
by the formula

\psi_{U}(v_{T})=v_{T\cup U}

for
v_{T}
an element of the basis of
S^{\mu}
. Hence these
\psi_{u}
are a basis of
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
, i.e.

(2.11)

S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}=\mathbf{C}\{\psi_{u}\,|\,U\in\syt(\lambda\setminus\mu)\}

It is easy to check that these
H_{\ell,n}
-modules are
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable, therefore its irreducibility is proved by the main theorem.

Since we aim to classify all the irreducible
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable
H_{\ell,n}
-modules, given
M
such a module and a
\mathfrak{u}
-eigenvector
m\in M
we note that the weight

wt(m)=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta_{b_{n}})

could have coordinates which are complex numbers instead of integers of even real ones, since
\phi_{i}m=a_{i}m
and
\zeta_{i}m=\zeta^{b_{i}}m
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Then our next step in the classification is to generalize the definition of the module
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
to
\ell
-skew diagrams with non-integer coordinates.

2.8.
Skew shapes

A
skew shape
is a finite subset
D\subseteq\mathbf{C}^{2}
such that whenever
(x,y)\in D
and
(x+k,y+l)\in D
for nonnegative integers
k
and
l
, then
(x+k^{\prime},y+l^{\prime})\in D
for all integers
0\leq l^{\prime}\leq l
and
0\leq k^{\prime}\leq k
. A skew shape
D
is called
integral
if
D\subseteq\mathbf{Z}_{>0}^{2}
. Each integral skew shape is the difference
D=\alpha\setminus\beta
of two partitions, which are not uniquely determined by
D
. Boxes
b=(x,y)
and
b^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
are called
adjacent
if either

(a)

x=x^{\prime}
and
y=y^{\prime}\pm 1
, or

(b)

y=y^{\prime}
and
x=x^{\prime}\pm 1
.

Adjacency in boxes of a skew shape is a equivalence relation, and the equivalent classes of a skew shape
D
are called
connected components
of
D
, and
D
is
connected
if it has only one connected component.

Like for
\ell
-partitions we have that an
\ell
-skew shape
is a sequence
D=(D^{0},D^{1},\ldots D^{\ell-1})
of
\ell
skew shapes, some of which may be empty. And for a box of
D
in the component
D^{j}
, then we write
{\rm ct}(b)=x-y
and
\beta(b)=j
for the
content
and
coordinate
of
b
, respectively. Following the analogy with
\ell
-partitions, a standard Young tableau on
D
is defined as a bijection

T:\hbox{\lx@text@lbrace boxes of }D\}\to\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

which is left to right and top to bottom increasing in each coordinate
D^{i}
of
D
and in each connected component.

In this setup we also think of the points of a diagram as boxes, thus when a diagram
D\subseteq\mathbf{Z}^{2}
we are in the case presented in (
2.7
).

2.9.
Automorphisms of
H_{\ell,n}

There are two types of automorphisms of
H_{\ell,n}
that we will use to define the generalized version of the modules
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

For each
\kappa\in\mathbf{C}
there exists unique automorphisms
t_{\kappa}
and
\rho
of
H_{\ell,n}
given by

\displaystyle t_{\kappa}(u_{i})=u_{i}+\kappa,\quad t_{\kappa}(\zeta_{i})=\zeta_{i},\quad t_{\kappa}(s_{i})=s_{i}

\displaystyle\rho(u_{i})=-u_{n-i+1},\quad\rho(\zeta_{i})=\zeta_{n-i+1}\quad\rho(s_{i})=s_{n-i}.

Proof.

We write
A=C\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},\zeta_{1},\ldots,\zeta_{n},s_{1}\ldots,s_{n-1}\rangle
for the tensor algebra on this generators, then
H_{\ell,n}\simeq A/I
where

\displaystyle I=\langle

\displaystyle u_{i}u_{j}-u_{j}u_{i},\quad\hbox{for all }1\leq i,j\leq n

\displaystyle\zeta_{j}\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j},\quad\hbox{for all }1\leq i,j\leq n

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{j}-s_{j}s_{i},\quad\hbox{for }j\neq i,i+1

\displaystyle s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}-s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}

\displaystyle\zeta_{i}u_{j}-u_{j}\zeta_{i},\quad\hbox{for all }1\leq i,j\leq n

\displaystyle s_{i}u_{j}-u_{j}s_{i}\quad\hbox{for }j\neq i,i+1

\displaystyle s_{i}u_{i}-u_{i+1}s_{i}+\pi_{i}\rangle

To check
t_{\kappa}
is an automorphism, it is enough to check that
t_{\kappa}(a)\in I
, for all
a\in I
. We will check only the las relation. Note that
t_{\kappa}(\pi_{i})=\pi_{i}
since
t_{\kappa}(\zeta_{i})=\zeta_{i}
, hence

\displaystyle t_{\kappa}(s_{i})t_{\kappa}(u_{i})-t_{\kappa}(u_{i+1})t_{\kappa}(s_{i})+t_{\kappa}(\pi_{i})

\displaystyle=s_{i}(u_{i}+\kappa)-(u_{i+1}+\kappa)s_{i}+\pi_{i}

\displaystyle=s_{i}u_{i}+\kappa s_{i}-u_{i+1}s_{i}-\kappa s_{i}+\pi_{i}

\displaystyle=s_{i}u_{i}-u_{i+1}s_{i}+\pi_{i}\in I

In the same way we did for
t_{\kappa}
, we will check that

\rho:A/I\to A/I

is an automorphism.

•

For the first relation

\displaystyle\rho(u_{i}u_{j}-u_{j}u_{i})

\displaystyle=(-u_{n-i+1})(-u_{n-j+1})-(-u_{n-j+1})(-u_{n-i+1})

\displaystyle=u_{n-i+1}u_{n-j+1}-u_{n-j+1}u_{n-i+1}\in I

•

For
1\leq i,j\leq n
the cases
\rho(\zeta_{i}\zeta_{j}-\zeta_{j}\zeta_{i}),\rho(s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}-s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}),\rho(\zeta_{i}u_{j}-u_{j}\zeta_{i})\in I
and
\rho(s_{i}s_{j}-s_{j}s_{i}),\rho(s_{i}u_{j}-u_{j}s_{i})\in I
for
j\neq i,i+1
are similar.

•

Note that

\displaystyle\rho(\pi_{i})=\rho\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\zeta_{i}^{k}\zeta_{i+1}^{-k}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\zeta_{n-i+1}^{k}\zeta_{n-i}^{-k}=\pi_{n-i}

hence

\displaystyle\rho(s_{i}u_{i}-u_{i+1}s_{i}+\pi_{i})

\displaystyle=\rho(s_{i})\rho(u_{i})-\rho(u_{i+1})\rho(s_{i})+\rho(\pi_{i})

\displaystyle=s_{n-i}(-u_{n-i+1}-(-u_{n-i})s_{n-i}+\pi_{n-i}

\displaystyle=u_{n-i}s_{n-i}-s_{n-i}u_{n-i+1}+\pi_{i}

\displaystyle=s_{n-i}(s_{n-i}u_{n-i}-u_{n-i+1}s_{n-i}+\pi_{n-i})s_{n-i}\in I

then
s_{n-i}u_{n-i}-u_{n-i+1}s_{n-i}+\pi_{n-i}\in I
.

∎

Both of these automorphisms preserve the group algebra
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
and their restrictions to
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
are inner.

Given an
H_{\ell,n}
-module
M
and an automorphism
a
of
H_{\ell,n}
we write
M^{a}
for the
H_{\ell,n}
-module which is equal to
M
as an abelian group with the
H_{\ell,n}
-action defined by

h\cdot m=a(h)m\quad\hbox{ for }h\in H_{\ell,n}\hbox{ and }m\in M.

If
a=\rho
or
a=t_{\kappa}
then
M^{a}
is isomorphic to
M
as a
\mathbf{C}G(\ell,1,n)
-module, since the restrictions of this automorphisms to
G(\ell,1,n)
are inner.

2.10.
H_{\ell,n}
-modules
S^{D}

Here, using the tools and known results for
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
and the new objects and definitions given in (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) we proceed to generalize these modules.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{2}\times\mathbf{Z}/\ell
be a skew shape with connected components
D_{1},\ldots,D_{k}
. After diagonal slides, we may assume that each
D_{i}
is such that for any
(x,y)\in D_{i}
we have
y_{i}\in\mathbf{Z}
and moreover the set of
y
-coordinates of distinct
D_{i}
’s are disjoint. We may choose (non-unique)
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbf{C}
and integral skew shapes
\lambda_{1}\setminus\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\setminus\mu_{k}
such that

D_{i}=\lambda_{i}\setminus\mu_{i}+(\alpha_{i},0)

so that their union is disjoint and a skew shape,

\lambda\setminus\mu=\coprod\lambda_{i}\setminus\mu_{i}

and so
\lambda_{1}\setminus\mu_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\setminus\mu_{k}
are the connected components of their disjoint union
\lambda\setminus\mu
.
Then define

S^{D}=\Ind^{H_{\ell,n}}_{A}\big(\otimes_{i}(S^{\lambda_{i}\setminus\mu_{i}})^{t_{\alpha_{i}}}\big)

where
A=H_{\ell,n_{1}}\otimes H_{\ell,n_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{\ell,n_{k}}
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}
are certain nonnegative integers such that
\sum{n_{i}}=n
.

We obtain by this construction an
H_{\ell,n}
-module
S^{D}
which is isomorphic to
S^{\lambda\setminus\mu}
as
G(\ell,1,n)
-modules. We will see in the following section that this is indeed the generalization we were looking for and moreover is a very good one since each
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable
H_{\ell,n}
-module can be obtained as one of this.

Theorem 2.6
.

S^{D}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable
H_{\ell,n}
-module

In order to prove this result first we recall some combinatorial lemmas involving skew shaped diagrams and tableaux.

Let
D
be a skew diagram of shape
\lambda\setminus\mu
, for a box
b\in D
with
b=(x,y)
we define
R(b)=y
and
C(b)=x
, i.e.
R(b)
and
C(b)
be the row and the column of the box
b
in
D
, respectively.
Let
T
be a standard Young tableau in
D
, define
I(T)
as the set of pairs
(i,j)
of numbers
1\leq i<j\leq n
such that
j
appears in
D
in a row strictly above than
i
, i.e.

I(T):=\{(i,j)\,|\,R(T^{-1}(i))>R(T^{-1}(j)),\hbox{ for }1\leq i<j\leq n\}

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
(i,i+1)\in I(T)
and
s_{i}T\in{\rm SYT}(D)
then
I(s_{i}T)=s_{i}(I(T)\setminus\{(i,i+1)\})
.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
(i,i+1)\notin I(T)
for all
1\leq i\leq n
then
T
is the
row reading tableau
.

Moreover note that if
T
is the
row reading tableau
in
D
then
R(T^{-1}(i))\leq R(T^{-1}(j))
for all
1\leq i<j\leq n-1
then clearly
(i,j)\notin I(T)
and
I(T)=\emptyset
. If
\lambda
is a row then there is only one standard Young tableau which is the row reading.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
D
be a skew diagram with
n
boxes. Given
T
and
T^{\prime}
two standard young tableaux on
D
then there exists a sequence of simple transpositions
s_{i_{1}},s_{i_{2}},\ldots,s_{i_{p}}
such that

s_{i_{p}}s_{i_{p-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}(T)=T^{\prime}

and
s_{i_{k}}s_{i_{k-1}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}(T)\in\syt(D)
for all
1\leq k\leq p
.

Note that this sequence of simple transpositions given in the preceding lemma corresponds to a sequence of invertible intertwining operators, and as a consequence of this lemma we can “connect” every pair of standard Young tableaux of shape
D
by a sequence of invertible intertwining operators.

Proof of theorem
2.6
.

Let
n_{i}
be the number of boxes of the diagram
\lambda_{i}\setminus\mu_{i}
, and
n=\sum_{i}^{k}n_{i}
.

From (

) each

Note that

which by PBW-theorem for

\Bigl\{w\,\Bigl|w\in S_{n}/S_{n_{1}}\times S_{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times S_{n_{k}}\Bigl\}

Then the

Proof of main theorem

In order to prove the main result of our paper we begin by proving the following lemma. We note in advance that the proof of the lemma amounts to an effective recursion for constructing
T
and
D
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}})
be a sequence satisfying the property: if
i<j
with
a_{i}=a_{j}
and
b_{i}=b_{j}\mod\ell
, then there are
i<k,m<j
with
b_{k}=b_{m}=b_{i}\mod\ell
and

a_{k}=a_{i}+\ell,\quad a_{m}=a_{i}-\ell.

Then there is a skew shape
D
and a standard Young tableau
T
of shape
D
satisfying

\ell{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(i))=a_{i}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\beta(T^{-1}(i))=b_{i},\quad\hbox{ for }1\leq i\leq n.

Moreover,
T
and
D
are unique up to diagonal slides of their connected components.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. Let
\ell=1
then we have the sequence
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. In this case
D
has only one component, if
n=1
then
D
has a single box with content
a_{1}
and
T
is the standard Young tableau that assigns to that box the number 1.
For
n>1
, by induction we suppose that the sequence
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1})
possesses a standard Young tableau
T^{\prime}
on a skew diagram
D^{\prime}
. Notice that the condition: “if
i<j
with
a_{i}=a_{j}
then there are
i<k,m<j
with
a_{k}=a_{i}+\ell
and
a_{m}=a_{i}-\ell
” implies that for boxes of
D^{\prime}
such that
{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(k))=a_{i}+1
and
{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(m))=a_{i}-1
we have

{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(k))={\rm ct}(T^{-1}(i))+1\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(m))={\rm ct}(T^{-1}(i))-1.

Then the condition implies that
D^{\prime}
is in fact a skew diagram and that
D^{\prime}
possesses an addable box
b
with
{\rm ct}(b)=a_{n}
. We obtain
T
and
D
by adjoining
b
to
D^{\prime}
and defining
T(b)=n
.

For the case
\ell\neq 1
then
D
has
\ell
components. We obtain the component
D_{i_{1}}
by considering the subsequence
(a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{p}})
of
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
such that
b_{i_{1}}=\cdots=b_{i_{p}}\mod\ell
and proceeding as before.
∎

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{u}
-diagonalizable
H_{\ell,n}
-module and
m\in M
satisfies

u_{i}m=a_{i}m\quad\hbox{and}\quad\zeta_{i}m=\zeta^{b_{i}}m\quad\hbox{for }1\leq i\leq n.

Then there is a standard Young tableau
T
on a skew shape
D
such that
a_{i}=\ell{\rm ct}(T^{-1}(i))
and
b_{i}=\beta(T^{-1}(i))
for
1\leq i\leq n
and
M\cong S^{D}
, and moreover
T
and
D
are unique up to diagonal slides of their connected components.

Proof.

For the first part we will check that the sequence
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}})
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
3.1
. Suppose that there is an index
j>i
with
a_{i}=a_{j}
and
b_{i}=b_{j}\mod\ell
, we claim that
j>i+2
.

Suppose that
j=i+1
, then

(a_{i},\zeta^{b_{i}})=(a_{i+1},\zeta^{b_{i+1}}),

and by the relations between
u_{i}
and
s_{i}
we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle u_{i}s_{i}m

\displaystyle=(s_{i}u_{i+1}-\pi_{i})m=s_{i}u_{i+1}m-\pi_{i}m=s_{i}a_{i}m-\ell m=a_{i}s_{i}m-\ell m

(3.2)

\displaystyle u_{i+1}s_{i}m

\displaystyle=(s_{i}u_{i}+\pi_{i})m=s_{i}u_{i}m+\pi_{i}m=s_{i}a_{i}m+\ell m=a_{i}s_{i}m+\ell m

Notice that
s_{i}m=\pm m
, since
s_{i}^{2}=1
. Then if we suppose
s_{i}m=m
then
u_{i}s_{i}m=u_{i}m=a_{i}m
and if
s_{i}m=-m
then
u_{i}s_{i}m=-a_{i}m
, both cases leads to a contradiction with (
3.1
). We conclude that
s_{i}m
and
m
are linearly independent and the subspace of
M
generated by
m
and
s_{i}m
is stable under
u_{i}
and
u_{i+1}
. Moreover
u_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&-\ell\\
0&a_{i}\end{pmatrix}
and
u_{i+1}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{i}&\ell\\
0&a_{i}\end{pmatrix}
, then
M
is not diagonalizable since there exist this Jordan blocks, which is a contradiction and then
j\neq i+1
.

For
j=i+2
, note that if
\tau_{i}
was invertible then by part (a) of Proposition
2.4

\tau_{i}(m)
has eigenvalue

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i+1},a_{i},a_{i+2},\ldots,a_{n},\zeta^{b_{1}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{i+1}},\zeta^{b_{i}},\zeta^{b_{i+2}},\ldots,\zeta^{b_{n}})

with
a_{i}=a_{i+1}
and
b_{i}=b_{i+2}
which contradicts the previous case. Hence
\tau_{i}
is not invertible, then necessarily
b_{i}=b_{i+1}
and
a_{i+1}=a_{i}\pm\ell
.
If
a_{i+1}=a_{i}+\ell
then
s_{i}
is acting by 1 on
m
and
s_{i+1}
is acting by
-1
on
m
, which contradicts the braid relation. Analogously if
a_{i+1}=a_{i}-\ell
,
s_{i}
acts by
-1
on
m
and
s_{i+1}
acts by
1
, contradicting the braid relation again.

Since
j>i+2
and the operators
\tau_{i}
and
\tau_{j}
are invertible then by Lemma
3.1
there is
T
and
D
as required.

It remains to prove that
M\cong S^{D}
. Let
T_{0}
be the row reading tableau and
\psi_{T_{0}}
the corresponding eigenvector in
S^{D}
. Let

with

, we will show there exists a homomorphism
\varphi:S^{D}\to M
such that
\varphi(\psi_{T_{0}})=m
. Given

there exists a sequence of simple transpositions

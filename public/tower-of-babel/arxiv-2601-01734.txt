The point-thicknesses of complete graphs and complete multipartite graphs
Wenzhong Liu, Wangkai Zhang

Abstract
The point-thickness θ ′ ​ ( G ) \theta^{\prime}(G) of a graph G G is the minimum number of subsets into which the vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) of G G is partitioned such that each subset induces a planar subgraph. In this paper, we determine the point-thicknesses of complete graphs and complete multipartite graphs. Keywords: partition, point-thickness, complete graph, complete multipartite graph. AMS Subject Classification: 05C75, 05C10.

Introduction

A graph
G
is a pair
(V(G),E(G))
with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
.
The thickness
\theta(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum number of planar subgraphs into
which G can be decomposed.
Since Tutte
[
17
]
introduced the thickness of a graph, some classes of graphs whose
thicknesses were determined are complete graphs
K_{n}
[
1
,
3
]
, almost all complete bipartite graphs
K_{m,n}
[
4
]
,
hypercube graphs
Q_{n}
[
11
]
. Determining
the thickness of a graph is
NP
-hard
[
14
]
. For many graphs, attention has been focused on upper bounds of their thicknesses, see for example
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
10
,
18
]
.

As a vertex version of the thickness, the point-thickness
\theta^{\prime}(G)
of a graph
G
is the minimum number of subsets into which the vertex set
V(G)

is partitioned such that each subset induces a planar subgraph. Considering induced subgraphs of a vertex partition of a graph is a classical problem in graph theory. When
replacing a planar subgraph in the point-thickness definition by an independent set, a forest, and a k-degenerate graph,
the minimum number is the chromatic number
\chi(G)
, the point-arboricity
\rho(G)

and the point partition number
\rho_{k}(G)
of a graph, respectively. It is easily checked that

\chi(G)\geq\rho(G)\geq\theta^{\prime}(G)\geq\rho_{5}(G)
according to their definitions.
For the chromatic number
\chi(G)
, many nice results have been obtained.
Chartrand et al
[
6
,
7
]
provided formulas for the point-arboricities of complete graphs and complete multipartite graphs, and proved that the point-arboricity of a planar graph is less than 3. Lick and White
[
12
]
systematically investigated the point partition number of a graph.
There is not any direct result considering the point-thickness of a graph as far as we know.
In fact, each of the four minimum numbers mentioned above is a coloring number, since it gives the minimum number of colorings in any vertex coloring of a graph
such that each coloring class induces a subgraph with some certain property.
Related problems of vertex partitions have been studied extensively.
Borodin
[
5
]
conjectured that every planar graph admits a vertex partition whose parts induce a 1-degenerate and a
2-degenerate graph, respectively, and another vertex partition whose parts induce a 0-degenerate and
a 3-degenerate graph, respectively. Thomassen
[
15
,
16
]
proved the two conjectures hold.

In the paper, we focus on the exact values of the point-thicknesses of graphs,
and determine the point-thicknesses of complete graphs and
complete multipartite graphs.
Before our main theorems are stated, we need to introduce some terminology and notations.
A
k
-independent-set
, abbreviated to
I_{k}
-set, is a set of
k
vertices in which
no two vertices are adjacent.
A
complete
n
-partite graph
K_{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}}
has its vertex set
V
partitioned
into subsets
V_{i}
with
|V_{i}|=p_{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots,n
; two vertices
x
and
y
are adjacent if and only if

x\in V_{i}
and
y\in V_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Without loss of generality, we always assume
p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{n}

(if necessary, we can change the orders of some
p_{i}
).
If there are
k
numbers
p_{i+1},p_{i+2},\cdots,p_{i+k}
satisfying

p_{i+1}=\cdots=p_{i+k}=s
, then
K_{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}}
is briefly denoted by

K_{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{i},s^{k},p_{i+k+1},\cdots p_{n}}
.
In particular, if
p_{1}=p_{2}=\cdots=p_{n}=1
, then

K_{p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}}
is a
complete graph
on
n
vertices, also denoted by
K_{n}
.

Let
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
be any two nonnegative integers, we define

N(k_{1},k_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k_{2}+\lceil\frac{1}{4}(k_{1}-3k_{2})\rceil,&\hbox{if $k_{1}\geq 3k_{2}$;}\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\par\lfloor\frac{k_{1}}{3}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{1}{3}(k_{2}-\lfloor\frac{k_{1}}{3}\rfloor)\rfloor+\sigma,&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where

\sigma=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{if $k_{1}\equiv 0$(mod 3) and $k_{2}-\lfloor\frac{k_{1}}{3}\rfloor\equiv 0$(mod 3);}\\
2,&\hbox{if $k_{1}\equiv 2$(mod 3) and $k_{2}-\lfloor\frac{k_{1}}{3}\rfloor\equiv 2$(mod 3);}\\
1,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Theorem 1.1

Suppose that
k_{1},k_{2}
,
k_{3}
and
n
are any nonnegative integers
and that
p_{0}=k_{1}+2k_{2}+3k_{3}
. Let
G
be a complete multipartite graph
K_{1^{k_{1}},2^{k_{2}},3^{k_{3}},p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}}

with
4\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{n}
. We have

(a)
if
p_{0}\leq 2n
, then

\theta^{\prime}(G)=n-max\{j|\sum^{j}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n-j)\}.

(b)
if
p_{0}>2n
, then

\theta^{\prime}(G)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n+N(k_{1}+k_{3}-2n,k_{2}+k_{3}),&\hbox{if $k_{1}+k_{3}\geq 2n$;}\\
n+N(\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}(p_{0}-2n-\varepsilon)),&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where
k_{1}+k_{3}\equiv\varepsilon
(mod 2).

From Theorem
1.1
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2

For a complete graph
K_{n}
, then

\theta^{\prime}(K_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil.

Main results

This section starts from the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1

If
G
is a complete multipartite graph with
n\geq 7
vertices other than
K_{1,n-1}
,
K_{2,n-2}
, and
K_{1,1,n-2}
, then

G
is a non-planar graph.

Proof.
It can easily be deduced from the fact that all such graphs contain a subgraph
K_{3,3}
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}
,
k_{3}
and
n
be any four nonnegative integers,
and let
p_{0}=k_{1}+2k_{2}+3k_{3}
. Suppose that
G
is a complete multipartite graph
K_{1^{k_{1}},~2^{k_{2}},~3^{k_{3}},~p_{1},~p_{2},\cdots,~p_{n}}

with
4\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{n}
and that the last
n
subsets
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
\cdots
,
V_{n}
of its vertex partition satisfy

|V_{i}|=p_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n
).

Lemma 2.2

Let
G,p_{0}
and
n
be defined as above. If
p_{0}\leq 2n
, then

\theta^{\prime}(G)=n-max\{j|\sum^{j}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n-j)\}.

Proof.
We apply induction on
n
. For
n=1
,
since
p_{0}\leq 2n=2
,
G
is
K_{2,p_{1}}
,
K_{1,1,p_{1}}
,
K_{1,p_{1}}
or an
I_{p_{1}}
-set,
which are all planar graphs.
Thus

\theta^{\prime}(G)=1=n-max\{j|\sum^{j}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n-j)\}.

Assume that the formula holds for
n\geq 1
,
and consider the complete multipartite graph
G=K_{1^{k_{1}},~2^{k_{2}},~3^{k_{3}},~p_{1},~p_{2},\cdots,~p_{n+1}}
.
Let
G_{1}=K_{1^{k_{1}},~2^{k_{2}},~3^{k_{3}},~p_{1},~p_{2},\cdots,~p_{n}}
.
Clearly,
G_{1}
is a subgraph of
G
. For the graph
G_{1}
,
suppose that

\sum^{t}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n-t)~~~and~~~\sum^{t+1}_{i=0}p_{i}>2(n-t-1).~~~~~~~~~~~~(*)

By hypothesis
\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})=n-t
. Since
G_{1}
is a subgraph of
G
,
\theta^{\prime}(G)\geq\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})=n-t
.
Since the additional set of
p_{n+1}
vertices used in forming
G
induces an

I_{p_{n+1}}
-set (a planar graph),

\theta^{\prime}(G)\leq\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})+1
.
We now discuss two cases below.

Case 1: Suppose
\sum^{t+1}_{i=0}p_{i}>2(n+1-(t+1))=2(n-t)
.

By the first inequation in
(*)
,

\sum^{t}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n-t)\leq 2(n+1-t),

which implies

n+1-max\{j|\sum^{j}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n+1-j)\}=n+1-t=\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})+1.

In this case, we need to prove
\theta^{\prime}(G)=\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})+1
. If not, then
\theta^{\prime}(G)=\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})=n-t
.
Let
G_{2}
be a complete multipartite graph
K_{1^{k_{1}},~2^{k_{2}},~3^{k_{3}},~p_{1},~p_{2},\cdots,~p_{t},~p_{t+1},\cdots,~p_{t+1}}

such that the number of its parts consisting of
p_{t+1}
vertices is
n+1-t
.
It is clear that
G_{2}
is a subgraph of
G
. Then
\theta^{\prime}(G_{2})\leq\theta^{\prime}(G)=n-t
.
We can partition the vertex set
V(G_{2})
into
n-t
(or fewer) subsets such that each subset induces a planar subgraph.
The total number of vertices of
G_{2}
is

\sum^{t+1}_{i=0}p_{i}+(n+1-t-1)p_{t+1}>2(n-t)+(n-t)p_{t+1}=(n-t)(2+p_{t+1}).

This implies that in any partition of
V(G_{2})
into
n-t
(or fewer) subsets, there is a
subset which contains at least
3+p_{t+1}
vertices.
Since
3+p_{t+1}\geq 3+p_{1}\geq 7
, the subset, say
V^{\prime}
, contains
m\geq 7
vertices. Since any part in
G_{2}
has at most
p_{t+1}
vertices, the subgraph induced by

is not
K_{1,m-1}
,
K_{2,m-2}
, or
K_{1,1,m-2}
.
From Lemma
2.1
, the subgraph is a non-planar graph, a contradiction.

Case 2: Suppose
\sum^{t+1}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n+1-(t+1))=2(n-t)
.

It follows from the second inequation in
(*)
that

\sum^{t+2}_{i=0}p_{i}=p_{t+2}+\sum^{t+1}_{i=0}p_{i}>2(n-t-1)=2(n+1-(t+2)),

which implies

n+1-max\{j|\sum^{j}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n+1-j)\}=n+1-(t+1)=n-t=\theta^{\prime}(G_{1}).

Thus, we need to prove
\theta^{\prime}(G)=\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})
in this case.

Let
V^{\prime}
be a subset of
V(G)
consisting of all vertices of the first
(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}+t+1)
parts in
G
.
Since
\sum^{t+1}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n-t)
, we can exhaust the set
V^{\prime}
by
adding at most two vertices of
V^{\prime}
to each of the sets

V_{t+2},V_{t+3},\cdots,V_{n+1}
. Every one of the resulting subsets induces a planar graph isomorphic to

K_{2,p_{s}}
,
K_{1,1,p_{s}}
,
K_{1,p_{s}}
or an
I_{p_{s}}
-set, where

t+2\leq s\leq n+1
.
Thus,
\theta^{\prime}(G)\leq n+1-(t+1)=n-t
. Since
G_{1}
is a subgraph of
G
, we have
\theta^{\prime}(G)\geq\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})=n-t
. Therefore,

~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\theta^{\prime}(G)=\theta^{\prime}(G_{1})=n-t=n+1-max\{j|\sum^{j}_{i=0}p_{i}\leq 2(n+1-j)\}.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\hfill\rule{4.0pt}{7.0pt}

Part I:

Let

s

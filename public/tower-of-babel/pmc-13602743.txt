Exploratory single-nucleus transcriptomic analysis of endothelial heterogeneity and ferroptosis-related gene patterns in a human hepatocellular carcinoma sample
Authors: Rongyu Shi, Leiming Wang, Ning Zhang, Chengcheng Zhong, Wei Du, Xi Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Single-nucleus RNA sequencing revealed marked endothelial heterogeneity in hepatocellular carcinoma.•Two endothelial states, EC-A and vEC, exhibited distinct vascular transcriptional features.•EC-A showed higher MVI-associated gene-set and angiogenesis scores than vEC.•CD36 and FTL highlighted lipid- and iron-handling features of endothelial heterogeneity.•Hypoxia-responsive genes in HCC cells provided contextual support for stress-related transcriptional changes.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains a major global cause of cancer morbidity and mortality [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Although resection and transplantation can be curative in selected patients, recurrence after treatment remains common. Defining biological features associated with early tumour dissemination is therefore clinically important.

Microvascular invasion (MVI)—the microscopic presence of tumour cells within small intrahepatic vessels—is a well-established adverse pathological feature associated with recurrence and survival after HCC resection or transplantation [[5], [6], [7], [8]]. Because MVI is determined histopathologically after tissue acquisition, improved biological understanding and reliable preoperative markers remain important research goals.

Ferroptosis is a regulated cell-death programme driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation and failure of antioxidant defences such as the GPX4 axis [[9], [10], [11], [12]]. In HCC, ferroptosis biology is embedded within a broader microenvironment characterised by hypoxia, chronic inflammatory signalling, altered lipid utilisation, and disordered iron handling. These processes can modify endothelial redox balance, permeability, adhesion, and angiogenic behaviour, although their direction and biological consequence are highly context dependent [11,[13], [14], [15]]. Accordingly, ferroptosis-associated transcription should be interpreted together with inflammatory and metabolic endothelial states rather than as evidence of an MVI-specific mechanism.

Endothelial cells regulate vascular permeability, angiogenic remodelling, and tumour-cell transit across the vascular wall, and tumour-associated endothelial populations are transcriptionally heterogeneous [[16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21]]. Ferritin light chain (FTL) is principally an intracellular iron-storage component relevant to cellular iron homeostasis and ferroptosis biology [13,22]. Extracellular ferritin can participate in non-transferrin iron delivery through receptors such as SCARA5 in other systems [23], but an endothelial ferritin-transfer mechanism in HCC cannot be inferred from FTL transcript expression alone.

Inflammatory activation and metabolic reprogramming are particularly relevant to tumour endothelium. Adhesion molecules such as VCAM1 and SELE can mark activated vascular states that facilitate leukocyte and tumour-cell interactions, while metabolic genes involved in lipid uptake and iron storage can influence endothelial responses to oxidative stress. In the present analysis, these dimensions are approached descriptively through endothelial marker patterns, differential genes such as CD36, iron-handling genes such as FTL, and hypoxia/angiogenesis programme scores. We do not apply a dedicated inflammatory or metabolomic signature, so these features are used to provide biological context rather than independent pathway-level proof.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables cell-type-resolved analysis of HCC heterogeneity [[24], [25], [26], [27]]. Here, we re-analysed an HCC single-nucleus RNA-sequencing dataset without available MVI-status annotation to characterise endothelial subpopulations and to explore ferroptosis/iron-handling, hypoxia, angiogenesis, and a literature-derived MVI-associated gene-set score. We additionally interpret the observed endothelial states within an inflammatory and metabolic framework using the marker and differential-expression features present in the dataset. Exploratory PAGA connectivity and transcription-factor expression analyses were used to generate candidate relationships, while hypoxia-response assays in HCC tumour-cell lines were included as contextual experiments only and were not treated as endothelial validation.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and quality control

The single-nucleus RNA-sequencing data were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) under accession GSE212046 / GSE212047 (GPL24676, 10x Genomics; human HCC and adjacent liver tissue snRNA-seq) [28]. The analyzed source was GSM6508441 (snRNA-seq of HCC S44), a single HCC library from a four-library series; the other three libraries were not included in this reanalysis. The GEO record identifies the material as HCC liver with background cirrhosis with steatohepatitis, but patient identifiers, MVI status, staging, treatment, and clinical outcome data were not available for this analyzed library. A total of 737,280 raw barcodes were filtered by removing cells with fewer than 200 detected genes, genes detected in fewer than 3 cells, and cells with a mitochondrial fraction ≥ 15%, retaining 67,481 cells. Total-count normalization (10,000) and log1p transformation were applied; the top 2,000 highly variable genes were used for PCA, followed by neighbor-graph construction, UMAP, and Leiden clustering (resolution = 0.5, 7 clusters). Global annotation was performed at the major cell-type level: each cluster was assigned the highest-scoring marker set (Cholangiocyte, Endothelial, Hepatocyte, Immune); immune cells were not further split into subsets.

Endothelial subset and annotation

PECAM1-expressing cells (n = 3,446) were restricted to the Endothelial major class; 323 PTPRC+ immune contaminants were removed, and 2,369 PECAM1+ cells located in hepatocyte/cholangiocyte classes (low VWF/CDH5 positivity, consistent with ambient RNA or doublets) were excluded, retaining 754 endothelial cells. Neighbor graph, UMAP, and Leiden clustering (resolution = 0.5) identified 3 subclusters; each subcluster was compared with canonical EC marker references (LSEC/aEC/vEC); the ESM1/PLVAP-high state was assigned the neutral operational label EC-A, which does not imply MVI status (Fig. 3E).

Program scores and differential expression

The angiogenesis score was defined as the mean expression of VEGFA+KDR+PLVAP and the hypoxia score as the mean expression of HIF1A+LDHA (scanpy score_genes); the literature-derived MVI-associated gene-set score used a set of 15 genes (15/15 detected) curated from published MVI studies. This score was used only as a gene-expression feature and not as a patient-level MVI classification. Subtype marker genes were identified with rank_genes_groups (Wilcoxon rank-sum test); PAGA was used as a descriptive visualization of endothelial-state connectivity and does not establish lineage, trajectory, or directionality.

Cell culture and hypoxia treatment

HepG2 (ATCC HB-8065) cells were cultured in EMEM and Huh7 (ATCC PTA-4583) cells in DMEM, both supplemented with 10% FBS, penicillin (100 U/mL), and streptomycin (100 μg/mL) at 37°C and 5% CO₂. Cells were exposed to hypoxia (1% O₂) for 48 h, with normoxia (21% O₂) as the control. Hypoxia was used as a simplified HCC stress condition and not as a surrogate for MVI in this single-sample analysis. HepG2 and Huh7 are tumour-cell lines rather than endothelial cells; therefore, these experiments assess tumour-cell hypoxia responses and do not constitute cell-type-matched validation of the endothelial clusters.

RNA extraction and qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol (Invitrogen), quantified on a NanoDrop spectrophotometer, and reverse-transcribed from 1 μg RNA using PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara). Quantitative PCR was run with SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) on a QuantStudio 5 system (95°C 10 min; 40 × [95°C 15 s, 60°C 60 s]). Relative expression was calculated by the 2^(−ΔΔCT) method with GAPDH as housekeeping gene. These assays were used to characterise hypoxia-responsive expression in HCC tumour cells and were not interpreted as direct validation of endothelial subtype-specific expression. Statistical analysis

Bioinformatic analyses were performed in Python 3.8 and R 4.0. Differential-expression testing used the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Bonferroni correction as specified in Section 2.3; for other families of multiple comparisons, Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction was used where applicable. Exploratory module-score comparisons and correlations were treated as cell-level descriptive analyses within a single library and were not used for patient-level inference. Spearman correlation was used for exploratory relationships between continuous module scores when reported. qRT-PCR data are presented as mean ± SEM from three independent experiments performed in triplicate; comparisons used unpaired two-sided t-tests in GraphPad Prism 9.0. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant, but cell-level inferential results were not interpreted as patient-level validation.

Results

Global quality control and data overview

A total of 737,280 barcodes were processed; after filtering by a minimum of 200 detected genes per cell, a minimum of 3 cells per gene, and a mitochondrial fraction below 15%, 67,481 cells were retained (9.15% of input) for downstream analysis (Fig. 1A-C). The retained cells had a mean of 825 detected genes per cell and a median UMI count of 531 (Fig. 1A-B), and formed 7 clusters (Fig. 1D); the mitochondrial fraction showed no obvious localized enrichment in the embedding (Fig. 1E). Ranked by mean per-cell count fraction, the most highly expressed genes were APOA2 (0.42%), MT-CO1 (0.413%), FTL (0.399%) (Fig. 1F).

Global cell atlas and canonical cell-type markers

The global UMAP was annotated at the major cell-type level: Cholangiocyte, Endothelial, Hepatocyte, Immune (Fig. 2A). Canonical cell-type markers localized hepatocytes (ALB), immune cells (PTPRC), endothelial cells (PECAM1), and fibroblasts (COL1A1) (Fig. 2B-E). The composition pie shows the proportion of each cell type (Hepatocyte (56,475 cells, 83.7%), Cholangiocyte (9,222 cells, 13.7%), Endothelial (988 cells, 1.5%), Immune (796 cells, 1.2%)); segments below 5% are not labeled (Fig. 2F).

Identification and annotation of endothelial subtypes

PECAM1 expression was concentrated in the Endothelial class (Fig. 3A). Of 3,446 PECAM1+ cells, 323 PTPRC+ immune contaminants and 2,369 low-quality signals located in hepatocyte/cholangiocyte classes (low VWF/CDH5 positivity) were excluded, retaining 754 endothelial cells that were reclustered into 3 subclusters (Fig. 3B-C). Subcluster sizes were EC-A (492 cells, 65.3%), vEC (262 cells, 34.7%) (Fig. 3D). The canonical marker dot plot (LSEC: CLEC4G/STAB2/FCGR2B/LYVE1; aEC: GJA4/HEY1/EFNB2/DLL4; vEC: ACKR1/VCAM1/SELE; EC-A: ESM1/PLVAP/ANGPT2/RAMP3/IGFBP3) showed enrichment of the literature-derived MVI-associated gene set in the EC-A subclusters; based on the highest-scoring canonical EC marker set, subclusters 0 and 1 were assigned to EC-A, subcluster 2 was assigned to vEC (Fig. 3E). Pan-endothelial VWF expression was heterogeneous across subclusters: VWF was detected in 72.8% of EC-A cells (mean log1p expression 2.435) versus 58.8% of vEC cells (mean 1.578) (Fig. 3F).

Expression of literature-derived MVI-associated genes

Of the 15 literature-derived MVI-associated genes, 15 were detected in this dataset. The dot plot showed heterogeneous expression across endothelial subtypes (Fig. 4A); mean expression of ESM1, PLVAP, ANGPT2, RAMP3, IGFBP3 was highest in the EC-A subtype, with mean log1p expression in EC-A versus vEC of ESM1 (1.147 vs 0.673), PLVAP (1.821 vs 1.531), ANGPT2 (0.481 vs 0.21), RAMP3 (0.376 vs 0.284), IGFBP3 (0.532 vs 0.231) (Fig. 4B-F).

Hypoxia and angiogenesis program scores

UMAPs showed heterogeneous distribution of the hypoxia and angiogenesis scores across subtypes (Fig. 5A-B). At the cell level, the two scores showed essentially no linear correlation (Pearson r = 0.001, Fig. 5C). By subtype, EC-A had the highest mean angiogenesis score (0.348 versus 0.221 in vEC) and vEC the highest mean hypoxia score (0.087 versus 0.035 in EC-A) (Fig. 5D-F, descriptive).

Endothelial transcription factor expression

The endothelial transcription factors ETS1, SOX4, ERG, FLI1, HIF1A, MYC were detected, with detection fractions of ETS1 (56.2%), SOX4 (2.4%), ERG (46.8%), FLI1 (37.1%), HIF1A (32.4%), MYC (7.3%) in the endothelial subset. Their expression UMAPs showed subtype-specific distributions (Fig. 6A-F).

PAGA connectivity and differential expression of endothelial states

PAGA revealed the connectivity structure among endothelial states (Fig. 7A). The log2 fold changes of the three leading EC-A differential genes are summarised in Fig. 7B. Differential analysis (Wilcoxon rank-sum test) yielded the top 5 DE genes per subtype (Fig. 7C); the top three DE genes of EC-A were VWF (log2FC 1.44, adjusted P 3.0e-8), CD36 (log2FC 1.12, adjusted P 1.3e-7), HSPG2 (log2FC 1.05, adjusted P 4.1e-7) (Fig. 7D-F).

Literature-derived MVI-associated gene-set scores by endothelial state

The literature-derived MVI-associated gene-set score (15 genes) UMAP showed high-scoring cells concentrated in the EC-A region (Fig. 8A); EC-A had the highest mean literature-derived MVI-associated gene-set score (0.668 versus 0.432 in vEC, descriptive, Fig. 8B). In the Spearman correlation heatmap between genes in the literature-derived MVI-associated gene set and program scores, the gene with the largest |rho| was PLVAP (with the angiogenesis score, rho = 0.786); correlations involving score-component genes are part-whole descriptions (Fig. 8C). PLVAP and CLEC4G expression are shown in Fig. 8D-E; subtype proportions in Fig. 8F.

Supportive hypoxia-response assays in HCC cell lines

To examine whether candidate genes respond to hypoxic stress in HCC tumour cells, PLVAP, ESM1, CLEC4G, and FTL mRNA were quantified in HepG2 and Huh7 cells under normoxia and hypoxia (1% O₂, 48 h; Fig. 9). PLVAP increased 3.24-fold in HepG2 and 2.87-fold in Huh7 (P < 0.001), ESM1 increased 4.15-fold and 3.68-fold, respectively (P < 0.001), FTL increased 3.82-fold and 3.45-fold (P < 0.001), and CLEC4G increased 2.56-fold and 2.93-fold (P < 0.001). CLEC4G is an established LSEC-associated marker [29], but this analysis did not define an independent LSEC state. Its hypoxia response in HCC tumour-cell lines is therefore interpreted as cell-type- and context-dependent regulation rather than validation of EC-A/vEC state-specific biology. Fig. 9 contains qRT-PCR panels A–D only; no C11-BODIPY or ROS panel is presented, and no lipid-peroxidation result is claimed from this Fig..

Discussion

The principal finding of this reanalysis is endothelial heterogeneity within the analysed HCC single-nucleus RNA-sequencing dataset, together with subtype-associated differences in literature-derived MVI-associated genes, angiogenesis, hypoxia, iron handling, and genes linked to vascular inflammatory and metabolic states. Previous single-cell analyses have demonstrated that MVI-associated HCC is characterized by a complex multicellular ecosystem and distinct molecular features [30]. The data support an exploratory framework in which endothelial phenotype reflects the intersection of redox/ferroptosis-related stress, inflammatory activation, and metabolic adaptation. However, these dimensions are descriptive: the study does not contain a dedicated inflammatory module, metabolomic profiling, or direct metabolic-flux measurements, and therefore cannot establish pathway-level causality.

FTL expression provides a biologically relevant iron-handling signal, but the present data do not establish an intercellular ferritin-transfer pathway. The present Figures show PAGA connectivity and differential-expression patterns only; no ligand–receptor or FTL-axis analysis was performed. FTL is primarily intracellular, no extracellular ferritin concentration was measured, no recipient receptor was demonstrated in EC-A, and no iron-flux experiment was performed. SCARA5-mediated ferritin uptake is established in other systems [23], but invoking an analogous pathway in HCC endothelium remains a testable hypothesis rather than a demonstrated mechanism.

PLVAP is an endothelial structural protein involved in fenestrae and vascular permeability [31,32], whereas ESM1/endocan is an endothelial molecule regulated in part by VEGF-related signalling [33]. In the present single-library analysis, PLVAP and ESM1 have higher mean expression in EC-A than vEC but are not specific markers of EC-A. CLEC4G is an established LSEC-associated marker [29], but no independent LSEC state was defined here. Its increase under hypoxia in HepG2/Huh7 cells therefore does not validate EC-A/vEC state-specific biology and reinforces the need for cell-type-matched endothelial experiments.

From an inflammatory perspective, the endothelial annotation framework includes adhesion-associated genes such as VCAM1 and SELE within the venous endothelial marker set. Such molecules are classically linked to endothelial activation and cell-adhesion biology and provide a plausible interface between vascular inflammation and tumour-cell interaction. In this study, however, inflammatory activity was not quantified with an independent inflammation score and no cytokine or protein-level validation was performed. The inflammatory interpretation should therefore remain contextual and hypothesis-generating rather than being presented as a discrete validated pathway.

The metabolic dimension is more directly reflected by several observed transcripts. CD36 was among the top differential genes of the operationally labelled EC-A state, while FTL was among the most highly expressed genes in the global dataset; together with HIF1A expression and the hypoxia/angiogenesis score pattern, these findings highlight lipid uptake, iron storage, and oxygen-sensitive metabolic adaptation as relevant features of endothelial heterogeneity. These observations are biologically compatible with ferroptosis-related lipid and iron metabolism, but they do not demonstrate altered metabolic flux, labile iron concentration, or ferroptotic execution. Direct metabolomic, lipid-peroxidation, and endothelial iron-handling experiments will be required to test this model.

The selected TF feature plots provide additional descriptive context. ETS-family factors are established regulators of endothelial gene expression [34,35], and HIF1A is a central mediator of hypoxia responses [36]. Iron availability also influences HIF prolyl-hydroxylase activity through cellular iron-chaperone pathways [37]. Nevertheless, Fig. 6 displays TF transcript expression rather than SCENIC regulon-AUC values, and the current data do not establish that HIF1A, ETS1, MYC, or NRF2 causally orchestrate a ferroptotic EC-A programme. Such regulatory relationships require direct perturbation or appropriately presented regulon-level analyses.

FTL, PLVAP, ESM1, and CLEC4G are therefore better regarded as genes of interest for follow-up rather than validated biomarkers. Their interpretation should also be broadened beyond ferroptosis alone: the observed endothelial states combine vascular-permeability, inflammatory-adhesion, hypoxia, lipid-handling, and iron-storage features. No prospective diagnostic cohort, receiver-operating-characteristic analysis, independent endothelial validation, formal inflammatory pathway score, or metabolomic validation is presented. Clinical biomarker development would require independent patient cohorts, patient-level modelling, and endothelial- or spatially resolved validation.

Several limitations are important. First, the core analysis uses a single HCC snRNA-seq library (GSM6508441), for which patient identifiers, MVI status, clinical covariates, and outcome data are unavailable; patient-level MVI comparison, survival analysis, a patient-characteristics table, and generalisable inference are therefore not possible. Second, all endothelial-state compositions and score distributions are cell-level descriptive results from this one library and cannot be extrapolated to an MVI-stratified HCC population. Third, HepG2 and Huh7 are tumour-cell rather than endothelial models, so their hypoxia responses—including the directionally discordant CLEC4G increase—cannot validate endothelial subtype biology. Fourth, no endothelial ferroptosis assay, direct lipid-peroxidation panel, ferritin-secretion measurement, receptor-validation experiment, or iron-flux experiment is presented. Fifth, no ligand–receptor or FTL-axis analysis was performed, so an FTL ligand–receptor axis cannot be inferred. Finally, detailed clinical covariates were not available in the GEO metadata used for this reanalysis, limiting assessment of generalisability and confounding. These limitations make the present findings hypothesis-generating and require independent patient-level, endothelial, spatial, and functional validation. In addition, the inflammatory and metabolic interpretation is based on transcript-level marker patterns rather than dedicated inflammatory scoring, cytokine measurements, metabolomics, metabolic-flux assays, or direct quantification of lipid and iron pools; these components therefore remain exploratory.

In summary, this study provides an exploratory single-cell description of endothelial heterogeneity in HCC and places ferroptosis/iron handling within a broader inflammatory-metabolic endothelial context. The data support subtype-associated differences in literature-derived MVI-associated genes, vascular adhesion features, hypoxia/angiogenesis programmes, and metabolic genes such as CD36 and FTL, together with hypoxia-responsive expression of selected genes in HCC tumour-cell lines. They do not demonstrate a causal inflammatory or metabolic pathway, direct endothelial ferroptosis, or validated paracrine ferritin transfer. Future work should integrate patient-level replication, endothelial-specific perturbation, inflammatory profiling, metabolomics/lipidomics, and direct iron-handling measurements before biomarker or therapeutic claims are advanced.

Ethics statement

This study involved secondary analysis of de-identified, publicly available human scRNA-seq data and did not recruit new human participants for the computational component. The reanalysis did not involve new human-tissue collection. The original data-provider ethics and consent details, including any approval number linked specifically to GSM6508441, were not available in the retrieved GEO metadata; no new participant-level approval or consent was sought for this secondary reanalysis. The accompanying qRT-PCR experiments used established HCC cell lines and did not involve human participants.

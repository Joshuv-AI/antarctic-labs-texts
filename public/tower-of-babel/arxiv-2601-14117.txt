Scalar-rigid submersions are Riemannian products
Oskar Riedler, Thomas Tony

Abstract
Scalar-rigid maps are Riemannian submersions by works of Llarull, Goette–Semmelmann, and the second named author. In this article we show that they are essentially Riemannian products of the base manifold with a Ricci-flat fiber. As an application we obtain a Llarull-type theorem for non-zero degree maps onto products of manifolds of non-negative curvature operator and positive Ricci curvature with some enlargeable manifold. The proof is based on spin geometry for Dirac operators and an analysis connecting Clifford multiplication with the representation theory of the curvature operator.

Introduction and main results

A celebrated result in scalar curvature comparison geometry is the rigidity theorem of
[
Lla98
]
. It states that every area non-increasing smooth non-zero degree map
f\colon M\to S^{n}
from a closed connected spin manifold
M
of dimension
n\geq 3
with
\scal_{M}\geq n\left(n-1\right)
onto the round sphere is necessarily an isometry. This theorem provides insight into the general question of how a lower bound on the scalar curvature of a Riemannian manifold constrains its global geometry.

[
Lla96
]
generalized his classical rigidity theorem to a class of comparison maps between manifolds of different dimensions. Namely, every area non-increasing smooth map
f\colon M\to S^{n}
of non-zero
\hat{A}
-degree, where
M
is a closed connected spin manifold of dimension
m
, with
\scal_{M}\geq n\left(n-1\right)
is already a Riemannian submersion with
\scal_{M}=n\left(n-1\right)
.
[
GS02
, Theorem 2.4]
replaced the round sphere by some closed connected Riemannian manifold
N
of non-negative curvature operator, non-zero Euler characteristic and
\scal_{N}>2\Ric>0
. The second named author
[
Ton25a
, Theorem A]
generalized this rigidity theorem by replacing the condition on the
\hat{A}
-degree by a less restrictive condition involving the higher mapping degree.

The previous discussion shows that scalar-rigid maps must be Riemannian submersions. While the class of Riemannian submersions admits a wide range of geometrically rich and interesting examples, no scalar-rigid maps are known beyond those that are essentially Riemannian products. The main purpose of this article is to show that this is always the case (
A
). This generalizes the product rigidity result of
[
GS01
, Theorem 0.2]
, where product rigidity is shown for comparison maps with target
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.

Theorem A
.

Let
(N,g_{N})
be a closed connected Riemannian manifold of non-negative curvature operator with
\Ric_{N}>0
, and let
f\colon M\to N
be a smooth spin map from a closed connected Riemannian manifold
(M,g_{M})
satisfying

\chi\left(N\right)\cdot\hideg\left(f\right)\neq 0\in\KO_{*}\left(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\pi_{1}\left(M\right)\right).

(1.1)

Suppose
\scal_{M}\geq\scal_{N}\circ f
and
g_{M}\geq f^{*}g_{N}
. Then there exists a closed connected Ricci-flat manifold
\left(F,g_{F}\right)
such that

\left(M,g_{M}\right)\cong\left(N,g_{N}\right)\times\left(F,g_{F}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{(locally isometric)}

(1.2)

and
f
is given by the projection onto the first factor. Moreover, if
\scal_{N}>2\Ric_{N}>0
, then the rigidity also holds if we assume
g_{M}\geq f^{*}g_{N}
just on
\Lambda^{2}TM
.

In
A
, two Riemannian manifolds are called
locally isometric
if their universal coverings endowed with the lifted Riemannian metrics are isometric, and the map
f
is called
spin
, if the first and second Stiefel–Whitney classes satisfy
w_{1}\left(TM\right)=f^{*}w_{1}\left(TN\right)
and
w_{2}\left(TM\right)=f^{*}w_{2}\left(TN\right)
. Its
higher mapping degree
is defined as the index of the spin Dirac operator of the fiber over a regular value of the map
f
twisted by the Mishchenko–Fomenko bundle of the manifold
M
(
3.16
). This is a generalization of the classical
\hat{A}
-degree.

Example 1.1

(Local Riemannian product)
.

The conclusion that
M
is locally isometric to a Riemannian product means that the universal cover is a Riemannian product. If we let
M=S^{2n}\times[0,1]/\sim
with
(x,0)\sim(-x,1)
, then the map

f:(S^{2n}\times[0,1]/\sim\,,g_{\text{round}}+dt^{2})\to(\mathbb{RP}^{2n},g_{\text{round}}),\hskip 20.00003pt[(x,t)]\mapsto[x]

is an example of a map satisfying the conditions of
A
which is only locally isometric to a Riemannian product, and not globally.

Remark 1.2

(Existence of a non-trivial parallel spinor)
.

The Ricci-flat manifold
F
in
A
carries a non-trivial parallel spinor on a finite Riemannian covering. This is a priori a stronger statement than in
A
, even though it is conjectured that all Ricci-flat spin manifolds carry a non-trivial parallel spinor on a finite Riemannian covering. See the discussion in
[
Ton25
]
. In the setting of
A
, we have
F=f^{-1}\left(p\right)
for some regular value
p
of
f
. Since the higher mapping degree of
f
does not vanish, it follows that the Rosenberg index of
F
is non-trivial
[
Ton25a
, Proposition 2.7]
. Together with the fact that the fiber
F
is Ricci-flat, we obtain the existence of such a non-trivial parallel spinor by
[
Ton25
, Theorem A]
.

Remark 1.3

(Non-negative curvature operator)
.

The category of scalar-rigid manifolds
N
for which we formulate
A
is contained in the class of closed manifolds with non-negative curvature operator and positive Ricci curvature. By the work of many mathematicians such manifolds are classified
[
GM75
,
CT06
,
BW08
]
. They are locally isometric to Riemannian products of symmetric spaces of compact type, certain Kähler metrics on
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
biholomorphic to the standard complex structure, and spheres with metrics of non-negative curvature operator.

As an application of the product rigidity in
A
, we establish a generalization of Llarull’s rigidity theorem
[
Lla98
]
to non-zero degree maps to products of the form
N\times F
for certain

including enlargeable manifolds (
B
). This generalization is in the spirit of
[
HSS24
, Theorem 1.1]
— which treats non-zero degree maps of the form
M^{n}\to S^{3}\times T^{n-3}
with
4\leq n\leq 7
— and generalizes a recent rigidity theorem by
[
Cho25
, Theorem A]
to all even dimensions (
C
). While the approach in this article is purely based on the Dirac operator method, the proofs by
[
HSS24
]
and
[
Cho25
]
rely on minimal slicing combined with spinorial techniques and are therefore restricted to low dimensions.

Corollary B
.

Let
f\colon M\to N\times F
be a smooth map of non-zero degree, where

•

M
and
N
are closed connected Riemannian spin manifolds,

•

N
has non-zero Euler characteristic, non-negative curvature operator and
\Ric_{N}>0
, and

•

F
is a closed connected oriented manifold which is either (area)-enlargeable, or rationally essential and whose fundamental group satisfies the strong Novikov conjecture.

If
g_{M}\geq\left(\pr_{1}\circ f\right)^{*}g_{N}
and
\scal_{M}\geq\scal_{N}\circ\pr_{1}\circ f
, then

is locally isometric to the Riemannian product
N\times T^{k}
for a flat torus

and
\pr_{1}\circ f
is given by the projection onto the first factor.

Here

means that the fundamental class of

maps non-trivially under the map

induced by the classifying map

of the universal cover. We say that its fundamental group satisfies the

if the Novikov assembly map is rationally injective.

By the assumptions on the manifold

, the map
\pr_{1}\circ f\colon M\to N
satisfies the condition on the higher mapping degree (see
section

6
).

then follows from

\pr_{1}\circ f\colon M\to N
.

Example C
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be a closed connected Riemannian spin manifold of dimension

(S^{2n},g_{\text{round}})
the round sphere,

an area non-increasing smooth map of non-zero degree. If

for some flat metric
\overline{g}_{\text{flat}}
and the map

is of the form

for some

-Lipschitz map

Remark 1.4
.

If the manifold

in

satisfies

, then the rigidity statement in

Remark 1.5
.

Note that the manifold

Remark 1.6
.

The rigidity statement in

(1.3)

Here the first map is the inverse of the (homological) Pontryagin character

, and the third map the rational Novikov assembly map

[

for all

and
\omega
a horizontal lift of an element of
\image\mathcal{R}_{N}
. Recall that

i.e. the first term in

for all

In order to work with

Lemma 2.2
.

For each

(1)

\mathcal{A}_{i}=\End(V_{i})
.

(2)

There is an

having non-degenerate Ricci tensor, cf.
5.12

Now if

For all

When dealing with a Riemannian submersion the global vanishing of the mean curvature of the fibers is a useful property. A standard curvature identity for Riemannian submersions is

\Ric_{M}(X,X)-\Ric_{N}(dfX,dfX)=-2\|A_{X}\|^{2}-\|T^{*}_{(\cdot)}X\|^{2}+g_{M}(\nabla_{X}H,X)

,

Recall that
u
is parallel, so that

for all

is the curvature endomorphism of the spinor bundle

which gives

(2.14)

for

2.14

2.3

]
to gain pointwise (that is

Preliminaries

In this section we review some preliminary notions related to Riemannian submersions as well as facts from higher index theory. The purpose of the first subsection,
section

3.1
, is to fix our notational conventions for the O’Neill tensors of a Riemannian submersion, and to list the relations we require in our proof. The second subsection,
section

3.2
, provides a bare-bones introduction to the higher index theory used in this paper as well as an explanation of the basic examples that are relevant to our setting.

Riemannian submersions

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
,
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds. A submersive map
f\colon M\to N
is called a
Riemannian submersion
if for any
p\in M
the restriction of
\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7mu_{p}f
to the
horizontal space

\ker(\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7mu_{p}f)^{\perp}\subseteq T_{p}M
is an isometry onto
T_{f(p)}N
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
,
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds and
f\colon M\to N
a Riemannian submersion. We denote the
vertical distribution

\ker(\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7muf)
by
\mathcal{V}
and its orthogonal complement, the
horizontal distribution
, by
\mathcal{H}
. We define the
O’Neill tensors
of
f
:

(1)

The
integrability tensor
of
f
is the linear map
A:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{V}
defined by

g_{M}(A_{X}Y,U)=\frac{1}{2}g_{M}([X,Y],U)

for
X,Y
horizontal fields and
U
a vertical field.

(2)

The
second fundamental form
of
f
is the linear map
T:\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{H}
defined by

g_{M}(T_{U}V,X)=g_{M}(\nabla_{U}V,X),

where
X
is a horizontal field,
U,V
are vertical fields, and
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
M
.

Remark 3.3
.

A
and
T
are in fact tensors, i.e. their values at a point
p\in M
depend only on the values of the relevant fields
X,Y,U,V
at
p
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
,
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds and
f\colon M\to N
a Riemannian submersion. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The tensors
A,T
both vanish identically.

(2)

The map
f\colon M\to N
is locally the projection map of a Riemannian product, i.e. for
p\in N
any point there exists a neighborhood
U_{p}\subseteq N
and a Riemannian manifold
(F,g_{F})
so that
f^{-1}(U_{p})\cong(U_{p}\times F,g_{N}+g_{F})
and
f\lvert_{f^{-1}(U_{p})}
is the projection to the first factor.

(3)

For
(\widetilde{M},g_{M}),(\widetilde{N},g_{N})
the universal covers of
(M,g_{M})
,
(N,g_{N})
one has
(\widetilde{M},g_{M})=(\widetilde{N}\times F,g_{N}+g_{F})
for some Riemannian manifold
(F,g_{F})
, and the induced map
\widetilde{f}\colon\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{N}
is the projection to the first factor.

(4)

There is a Riemannian manifold
(F,g_{F})
and a group homomorphism
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(N)\to\mathrm{Isom}(F)
so that
(M,g_{M})\cong\left((\widetilde{N}\times F)/\sim,g_{N}+g_{F}\right)
where
(p,q)\sim(\gamma(p),\rho(\gamma)(q))
for
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(N)
.

Since
A,T
are
(1,2)
tensors there are many ways in which they can be contracted with other tensors. This can easily lead to notational problems. In order to accommodate such contractions we introduce the following notations for the different
(1,1)
tensors associated to
A,T
:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds,

a Riemannian submersion, and

. For

horizontal and vertical respectively, we define the tensors

\displaystyle T_{U}^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathcal{V}_{p},\hskip 10.00002ptX\mapsto g_{M}(T_{U}(\cdot),X)^{\sharp},

\displaystyle S_{X}:\mathcal{V}_{p}\to\mathcal{V}_{p},\hskip 10.00002ptU\mapsto T_{U}^{*}X,

(3.1)

\displaystyle A_{X}:\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathcal{V}_{p},\hskip 10.00002ptY\mapsto A_{X}Y,

\displaystyle A_{U}^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{p}\to\mathcal{H}_{p},\hskip 10.00002ptX\mapsto g_{M}(A_{X}(\cdot),U)^{\sharp}.

(3.2)

Remark 3.6
.

Let

be horizontal and

g_{M}(T_{U}^{*}X,T_{U}^{*}Y)=g_{M}\!\left(U,\,\frac{S_{X}S_{Y}+S_{Y}S_{X}}{2}U\right).

The tensor

or

of the fibers in direction

Theorem 3.8

(
[

, Proposition 9.36]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
,

be Riemannian manifolds and

\ric_{M}\left(X,X\right)=\ric_{N}\left(\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7muf\left(X\right),\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7muf\left(X\right)\right)-2\left\|A_{X}\right\|_{2}^{2}-\left\|S_{X}\right\|_{2}^{2}+g_{M}\left(\nabla^{M}_{X}H,X\right)

Corollary 3.9

Let
(M,g_{M})
,
(N,g_{N})
be Riemannian manifolds and

and

Let

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(M,g),(N,h)
be Riemannian manifolds,
f\colon M\to N
a Riemannian submersion,
p\in M
. Let
X,U\in T_{p}M
be horizontal and vertical respectively and let

be the horizontal lift of a section
\overline{\omega}\in\Cinftyblanc\left(N,\Lambda^{2}TN\right)
. Then

\nabla_{U}(\omega)=-\ad_{\omega}(T_{U}^{*}-(T_{U}^{*})^{T}+A^{*}_{U}),\hskip 20.00003pt\nabla_{X}\omega=-\ad_{\omega}(A_{X}-A_{X}^{T})+\widehat{\nabla^{N}_{\overline{X}}\overline{\omega}},

where
\widehat{}
denotes the horizontal lift of a section on
N
,
\ad_{\omega}
the commutator with an element of

, and

the transpose of a linear map.

Proof.

Recall that

,

take values in

. On the other hand

is an anti-symmetric linear map

Now for any sections

\displaystyle\nabla_{U}(Y\otimes Z^{\flat})

\displaystyle=(T_{U}^{*}Y)\otimes Z^{\flat}+Y\otimes(T_{U}^{*}Z)^{\flat}+(A_{U}^{*}Y)\otimes Z^{\flat}+Y\otimes(A_{U}^{*}Z)^{\flat}

\displaystyle=T_{U}^{*}\cdot(Y\otimes Z^{\flat})+(Y\otimes Z^{\flat})\cdot(T_{U}^{*})^{T}+A_{U}^{*}\cdot(Y\otimes Z^{\flat})+(Y\otimes Z^{\flat})\cdot(A_{U}^{*})^{T},

which shows the first relation of the lemma. The second follows in the same way.

\Lambda^{2}T_{p}N=\bigoplus_{i}\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i<j}\overline{V}_{i}\wedge\overline{V}_{j}
. We first verify that

\mathcal{R}_{N}(\Lambda^{2}T_{p}N)=\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{R}_{N}(\Lambda^{2}V_{i}),

(5.15)

i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge Y)=0
for
X\in\overline{V}_{i}
,
Y\in\overline{V}_{j}
with
i\neq j
. For any
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}T_{p}N
one has

g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge Y),\omega)=g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(\omega),X\wedge Y)=g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(\omega)Y,X)=0,

since
\overline{V}_{i}
is invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{g}(p)
and orthogonal to
\overline{V}_{j}
. The claim then follows.

Next we show that

\mathcal{R}_{N}(\Lambda^{2}V_{i})\subseteq\Lambda^{2}V_{i},

(5.16)

i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{N}(X_{1}\wedge X_{2})Y=0
for
X_{1},X_{2}\in V_{i}
,
Y\in\overline{V}_{j}
with
i\neq j
. This is a consequence of the previous calculation and the Bianchi identity

\mathcal{R}_{N}(X_{1}\wedge X_{2})Y=-\mathcal{R}_{N}(X_{2}\wedge Y)X_{1}-\mathcal{R}_{N}(Y\wedge X_{1})X_{2}=0.

The proposition then follows from equations (
5.15
) and (
5.16
).
∎

Since
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
acts irreducibly on
V_{i}
and consists of anti-symmetric endomorphisms the (associative) algebra it generates in

is easy to understand. In principle four cases are possible by Schur’s Lemma (cf.

]

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p),\overline{V}_{i})
be as in
5.12
. Then either:

(1)

The (associative) sub-algebra of
\End(\overline{V}_{i})
generated by
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
is
\End(\overline{V}_{i})
.

(2)

There is an
\mathbb{R}
-linear isometry
\iota\colon\overline{V}_{i}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
for some

. The (associative) algebra generated by
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
is

. Denoting with

.

Remark 5.14
.

5.13
only requires non-degeneracy of the Ricci tensor of
\mathcal{R}_{N}
. Furthermore, if the image of the curvature operator is parallel then

is the holonomy algebra of

Proof of
5.13
.

First note that
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)=0
immediately contradicts non-degeneracy of the Ricci tensor, since if
X\in\overline{V}_{i}
, and
e_{j}
is some orthonormal basis of
T_{p}N
then

\Ric(X,Y)=\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(e_{j}\wedge Y)X,e_{j})=0

for all
Y\in T_{p}N
.

Let
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}
denote the commutant of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
in
\End(\overline{V}_{i})
, i.e. all endomorphisms commuting with
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
. Since
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\neq 0
consists of anti-symmetric endomorphisms and acts irreducibly on
\overline{V}_{i}
, we apply Schur’s Lemma to find that
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}
is isomorphic to one of the real division algebras
\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
.

In the case
\mathbb{R}
one recovers for the bi-commutant
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}=\End(\overline{V}_{i})
. Now
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}
is equal to the sub-algebra of
\End(\overline{V}_{i})
generated by
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
by the bi-commutant theorem (which holds also for real
*
-algebras, see e.g. Theorem 4.3.8 of
[
Li03
]
). This case corresponds to point (
1
) in the proposition.

For the two remaining cases
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
one has
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}=\End_{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{V}_{i})
or
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}=\End_{\mathbb{H}}(\overline{V}_{i})
with respect to some complex or quaternion structure on
\overline{V}_{i}
. In both cases
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
there is at least one complex unit
I\colon\overline{V}_{i}\to\overline{V}_{i}
with
I\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}
. The cases
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
can be distinguished by considering
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}
, which is either
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}
respectively.

We then proceed by assuming there is a complex unit
I\colon\overline{V}_{i}\to\overline{V}_{i}
commuting with
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
. Using non-degeneracy of the Ricci tensor we will construct a non-zero anti-symmetric element of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}
, ruling out the case
\mathbb{H}
. In fact our construction is such that
I\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
. Carrying out these steps then completes the proof of the proposition.

So let
I\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}
be a complex unit, since the elements of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
commute with
I
they are complex linear maps with respect to
I
. If
e_{1},...,e_{2k}
is a (real) orthonormal basis of
\overline{V}_{i}
, the
complex trace
of an element
\omega\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
is given by:

2\Tr_{\mathbb{C}}(\omega)=\Tr_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega)-i\Tr_{\mathbb{R}}(I\omega)=\sum_{j}g_{N}(\omega e_{j},e_{j})+i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\omega e_{j},Ie_{j})\in\mathbb{C}.

Note that since
\omega
is anti-symmetric the summand
\Tr_{\mathbb{R}}(\omega)
will vanish.

Claim
: There is an element
\eta\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
for which
\Tr_{\mathbb{C}}(\eta)\neq 0
.

To prove the claim let
X,Y\in\overline{V}_{i}(p)
then

\displaystyle 2\Tr_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge IY))

\displaystyle=i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge IY)e_{j},Ie_{j})

(5.17)

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge e_{j})Ie_{j},IY)-i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge Ie_{j})IY,e_{j})

(5.18)

where we applied the Bianchi identity. Note that by assumption the linear isometry
I
commutes with the image of
\mathcal{R}_{N}
, whence

-i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge e_{j})Ie_{j},IY)=-i\Ric(X,Y).

Similarly using that
Ie_{j}
is also an orthonormal basis of
\overline{V}_{i}
one has

-i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}(X\wedge Ie_{j})IY,e_{j})=i\sum_{j}g_{N}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge Ie_{j})Y,Ie_{j})=-i\Ric(X,Y),

and so
\Tr_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(X\wedge IY))=-i\Ric(X,Y)
, whence from non-degeneracy of
\Ric
there is an
\eta\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
with
\Tr_{\mathbb{C}}(\eta)\neq 0
. This proves the claim.

From this
\eta
we now construct a non-vanishing anti-symmetric element of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}\cap\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}
. Let
G_{i}\subseteq U(\overline{V}_{i})
denote the Lie group generated by
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(\overline{V}_{i})
. Then:

\Ad_{G_{i}}(\eta)\coloneq\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(G_{i})}\int_{G_{i}}\Ad_{g}(\eta)\mkern 4.0mu\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7mu\mu(g)

has the same complex trace as
\eta
and is
\Ad
-invariant. It follows that it is non-vanishing and commutes with every element of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
. This shows that it is an element of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime}
. Further it is a linear combination of elements
\Ad_{g}(\eta)\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
and hence is an element of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)^{\prime\prime}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let

(1)

(2)

Proposition 5.17

Let

be as in

Remark 5.18
.

5.17
is a combination of two results from the literature. In a paper introducing so called holonomy systems, Simons
[
Sim62
]
shows via pointwise computations that what we call
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
is in fact the Lie algebra of an irreducible symmetric space. When dealing with a symmetric space, a computation of Laquer
[
Laq92
]
then gives a Casimir element taking distinct values on

and
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(p)
, showing they must be disjoint as representations.

For convenience of the reader we will provide a self-contained proof of
5.17
. The proof will be carried out in several steps and is simpler than the just cited discussion. We first define an auxiliary curvature operator:

Definition 5.19
.

Let
G_{i}=\exp(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p))\subseteq O(\overline{V}_{i})
be the Lie group generated by
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
. We define

\widetilde{R}_{i}:\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\to\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i},\hskip 20.00003pt\omega\mapsto\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(G_{i})}\int_{G_{i}}\Ad_{g}\big(\mathcal{R}_{N}(\Ad_{g^{-1}}\omega)\big)\mkern 4.0mu\mathrm{d}\mkern-0.7mu\mu(g).

Lemma 5.20
.

\widetilde{R}_{i}
is an algebraic curvature operator and

\image\widetilde{R}_{i}\subseteq\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\subseteq\big\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\mid\ad_{\omega}\circ\widetilde{R}_{i}=\widetilde{R}_{i}\circ\ad_{\omega}\big\}.

Proof.

As a linear combination of curvature operators
\widetilde{R}_{i}
is a curvature operator. Additionally
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
is invariant under the action of
\Ad_{g}
for all
g\in G_{i}
, whence
\Ad_{g}(\mathcal{R}_{N}(\Ad_{g^{-1}}\omega))\in\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
for all
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}
. This implies the first inclusion. The second inclusion follows from noting

\Ad_{g}\circ\widetilde{R}_{i}=\widetilde{R}_{i}\circ\Ad_{g}

for all
g\in G_{i}
and taking the derivative by
g
.
∎

Remark 5.21
.

From the inclusion
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}\subseteq\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\mid\ad_{\omega}\circ\widetilde{R}_{i}=\widetilde{R}_{i}\circ\ad_{\omega}\}
in
5.20
one finds
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
is a Lie-algebra:

\big[\widetilde{R}_{i}\omega,\widetilde{R}_{i}\eta\big]=\ad_{\widetilde{R}_{i}\omega}\big(\widetilde{R}_{i}\eta\big)=\widetilde{R}_{i}\big(\ad_{\widetilde{R}_{i}\omega}\eta\big)\in\image\widetilde{R}_{i}.

Lemma 5.22
.

The Ricci tensor of
\widetilde{R}_{i}
is non-degenerate and
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
acts irreducibly on
\overline{V}_{i}
.

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{R}_{i}
is
G_{i}
-equivariant so is its Ricci tensor. But any quadratic form invariant under an irreducible orthogonal representation (such as
G_{i}
on
\overline{V}_{i}
) must be proportional to the scalar product. We now verify this proportionality factor is non-zero:
Since the Ricci tensor of
\mathcal{R}_{N}
is positive the partial trace of
\mathcal{R}_{N}
over
\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}
is strictly positive. By construction this partial trace is the trace of
\widetilde{R}_{i}
, whence
\widetilde{R}_{i}
has non-vanishing (and hence non-degenerate) Ricci tensor.

We now verify that
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
acts irreducibly on
\overline{V}_{i}
. From
5.12
any two irreducible sub-representations of
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
are orthogonal to each other. If a sub-representation of
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
is not orthogonal to an irreducible summand it must then contain this summand.

Suppose
W\subset\overline{V}_{i}
is a non-trivial irreducible sub-representation of
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
, since
G_{i}
acts irreducibly there is a
g\in G_{i}
so that
gW\neq W
. By connectedness of
G_{i}
we may assume
gW
is not orthogonal to
W
. We will show
gW
is a sub-representation of
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
, whence it must contain
W
by the previous paragraph. Since
W
and
gW
have the same dimension we conclude the contradiction
W=gW
.
\overline{V}_{i}
then admits no non-trivial irreducible sub-representations and so must itself be irreducible.

We now show that
gW
is a sub-representation of
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
. Let
x\in W
,
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}
, unpacking the relation
\Ad_{g}(\widetilde{R}_{i}(\omega))=\widetilde{R}_{i}(\Ad_{g}\omega)
gives:

\widetilde{R}_{i}(\omega)gx=g\widetilde{R}_{i}(\Ad_{g^{-1}}\omega)x\in gW

and so
gW
is closed under the action of
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 5.23
.

One has

\image\widetilde{R}_{i}=\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)=\big\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\mid\ad_{\omega}\circ\widetilde{R}_{i}=\widetilde{R}_{i}\circ\ad_{\omega}\big\}.

Proof.

By
5.20
it is only necessary to show
\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\mid\ad_{\omega}\circ\widetilde{R}_{i}=\widetilde{R}_{i}\circ\ad_{\omega}\}\subseteq\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
. Let
\omega\in\image(\widetilde{R}_{i})^{\perp}\cap\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}\mid\ad_{\omega}\circ\widetilde{R}_{i}=\widetilde{R}_{i}\circ\ad_{\omega}\}
. Since
\widetilde{R}_{i}
is self-adjoint we find
\widetilde{R}_{i}(\omega)=0
. Now for any
\eta\in\Lambda^{2}\overline{V}_{i}
:

\big[\omega,\widetilde{R}_{i}\widetilde{R}_{i}\eta\big]=\ad_{\omega}\big(\widetilde{R}_{i}\widetilde{R}_{i}\eta\big)=\widetilde{R}_{i}\big(\ad_{\omega}(\widetilde{R}_{i}\eta)\big)=-\widetilde{R}_{i}\big(\ad_{\widetilde{R}_{i}\eta}\omega\big)=-\ad_{\widetilde{R}_{i}}\big(\widetilde{R}_{i}\omega\big)=0,

and so
\omega
commutes with every element of
\image(\widetilde{R}_{i}^{2})=\image\widetilde{R}
and so lies in the commutant
\image(\widetilde{R}_{i})^{\prime}
. By
5.22
we know that
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
acts irreducibly on
\overline{V}_{i}
and that
\widetilde{R}_{i}
has non-degenerate Ricci curvature.
5.13
then applies to show that all anti-symmetric elements of the commutant
\image(\widetilde{R}_{i})^{\prime}
already lie in
\image\widetilde{R}_{i}
. We conclude
\omega=0
, which shows the lemma.
∎

From
5.23
we see that restricting
\widetilde{R}_{i}
to
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
gives an invertible
\ad(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p))
-equivariant linear map
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\to\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)

\overline{V}_{i}
from
\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(p)

Lemma 5.24
.

Let
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{k}
be an orthonormal basis of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)

(1)

For any

\sum_{\alpha}\big(\ad_{\widetilde{R}_{i}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}}\omega_{\alpha}\big)^{2}\eta=2\lambda_{i}\,\eta

Note that
\widetilde{R}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{i}\omega_{1},...,\widetilde{R}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{i}\omega_{k}
is an orthonormal basis for the (positive definite)

Q:\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\times\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)\to\mathbb{R},\hskip 20.00003pt(\omega,\eta)\mapsto g_{N}\big(\omega,\widetilde{R}^{-1}_{i}\eta\big).

It follows for any irreducible representation
\rho
of
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}_{i}(p)
that
\sum_{\alpha}\rho(\widetilde{R}_{i}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\omega_{\alpha})^{2}
acts as a negative scalar, being the image of the Casimir element associated to

1

Suppose now that
\eta\in\overline{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}(p)

Completing the proof of
A
and its applications

In this section we first describe how the results of the previous sections combine to prove the main result of this paper,
A
. After this we prove how
A
implies
B
.

Proof of
A
.

Recall that
f\colon(M,g_{M})\to(N,g_{N})
is a Riemannian submersion by
4.6
. By
5.4
we additionally have that
\Ric_{M}=f^{*}\Ric_{N}
. From
3.9
we see that
f
is locally a Riemannian product, i.e. the O’Neill tensors
A
and
T
vanish identically, if the mean curvature of the fibers vanish. We now show how the vanishing of the mean curvature follows from the results of the previous sections.

Let
p\in M
and
X\in T_{p}M
be a horizontal vector. Decompose
X=\sum_{i}h_{i}
for
h_{i}\in V_{i}
, where
(\mathfrak{g}_{i}(p),V_{i})
is the decomposition of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
of
sections

5.3
and
5.4
, recall
5.25
and
5.12
. Then
S_{X}=\sum_{i}S_{h_{i}}
, where

is the shape operator of the fibers, recall
3.5
. If
(\mathfrak{g}_{i}(p),V_{i})
is of real type one has

by
5.28
, and if
(\mathfrak{g}_{i}(p),V_{i})
is of complex type one has

∎

We now turn to the proof of

Proof of

The proof strategy is to apply

to the spin map

, hence we have to verify that
\chi\left(N\right)\cdot\hideg\left(\pr_{1}\circ f\right)\neq 0
. Let

be a regular value of
\pr_{1}\circ f
such that there exists a point

for which

is a regular value of

.
We define

(6.1)

Note that
\deg\left(f_{p}\right)=\deg\left(f\right)\neq 0
.
Let

(6.2)

We argue as in the proof of
[

, Proposition 5.2]
. For some discrete group

, its classifying space

and some closed spin manifold

, the

\nu\colon\KO_{*}\left(B\Gamma\right)\to\KO_{*}\left(\mathrm{C}^{\ast}\Gamma\right)

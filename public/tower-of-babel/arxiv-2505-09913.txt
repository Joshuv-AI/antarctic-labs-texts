Probing the Dynamics of Gaussian Dark Energy Equation of State Using DESI BAO
Saddam Hussain 1, Simran Arora 2, Anzhong Wang 3, Ben Rose 4

Abstract
We present an updated reconstruction of the various dark energy equation of state (EoS), w ⁡ ( a ) w(a) , employing the newly released DESI DR2 Baryon Acoustic Oscillation data. This analysis constrains the cosmological scenarios influenced by these equations through the joint examination of a range of recently available cosmological probes, specifically the Pantheon+ sample and the DESY5 sample of Type Ia Supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations, Hubble parameter measurements derived from cosmic chronometers, and cosmic microwave background distance priors based on the Planck 2018 data. Furthermore, we provide a concise perspective on the dynamical evolution of all models and the interrelations among them. A Bayesian inference procedure is adopted to estimate the model parameters that yield the best fit to the data. The EoS remains within the phantom regime at higher redshifts, while favoring the quintessence regime in the current epoch. In this context, we propose a new Gaussian form EoS, termed BellDE, which avoids phantom behavior ( w ≥ − 1 w\geq-1 ) at higher redshifts while remaining precisely calibrated at lower redshifts. Interestingly, BellDE exhibits a transient phantom nature ( w < − 1 w<-1 ) only around the transition redshift z ∼ 0.5 z\sim 0.5 , subsequently evolving into a quintessential regime ( w > − 1 w>-1 ), as indicated by comparisons with the Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) and other parameterized EoS forms. Keywords: Dynamical dark energy; Phantom; Observations; DESIBAO(DR2)



I
Introduction

Over the past two decades, a wealth of observational evidence—from Type Ia supernovae (SNeIa)
(
Riess et al. 1998
;
Perlmutter et al. 1999
;
Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Seljak et al. 2005
)
to baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) and cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements—has consistently confirmed that the universe is undergoing a phase of accelerated expansion. However, the nature of this acceleration, attributed to an exotic form of matter known as dark energy, along with its fundamental origin, remains poorly understood. Conversely, observational efforts to study potential late-time cosmological transitions have generated significant interest, as they may both reveal dark energy’s fundamental nature and help resolve the Hubble tension (
H_{0}
tension)
(
Vagnozzi 2023
;
Poulin et al. 2025
;
Simon et al. 2025
)
. Consequently, the potential transitions in the dynamics of the universe related to the characteristics of dark energy have long been of significant interest for observational studies.

A key component of dark energy is its EoS parameter, referred to as
w
, which is defined as the ratio of pressure
p
to its energy density
\rho
. This parameter is essential for identifying the distinct characteristics of dark energy. One of the simplest methods to describe dark energy is by treating it as a perfect fluid, characterized by either a constant
(\Lambda)
or a redshift-dependent EoS parameter. In instances where the EoS varies, a specific functional form of
w(z)
is typically defined
(
Linder 2003
;
Caldwell and Linder 2005
;
Linder 2024
;
Park et al. 2024
)
. This function may be expressed as a Taylor series in terms of redshift, scale factor, or through a more generalized parameterization method. The associated parameters can subsequently be constrained with various observational data sets.
At a cosmological level, distinguishing among different dark energy models can be achieved by examining the functional form of
w(a)
alongside observational data from SNeIa
(
Scolnic et al. 2022
)
, CMB
(
Aghanim et al. 2020
)
, and BAO
(
Adame et al. 2025
;
Chaussidon et al. 2025
)
. To facilitate these comparisons, numerous parametrizations of
w(a)
have been developed, with the majority expressed using two parameters.

Numerous parameterizations of the dark energy EoS have been proposed in the literature, with the Chevallier–Polarski–Linder (CPL) form being among the most widely used. It is defined as
w_{\rm de}=w_{0}+w_{1}(1-a)=w_{0}+\frac{w_{1}z}{1+z}
and was initially introduced in
(
Chevallier and Polarski 2001
;
Linder 2003
)
. The CPL model serves as a first-order Taylor series expansion about the current scale factor (
a_{0}=1
). The ubiquitous CPL model performs poorly and leads to larger errors on
w_{1}

Colgáin et al. 2021
. An important point is that the degree of this sensitivity is governed by the specific functional form that multiplies the parameter
w_{1}
in the standard two-parameter formulation
(w_{0},w_{1})
. To capture more complex behavior of dark energy, this model can be extended by including a second-order term
w_{\rm de}=w_{0}+w_{1}(1-a)+w_{2}(1-a)^{2}\cdots
. This generalization enhances the model’s capacity to describe the time variation of the EoS, especially at low redshift
(
Seljak et al. 2005
)
.

An alternative to the CPL is the Padé parametrization, which is mathematically robust, remaining finite across all future redshifts
(
Rezaei et al. 2017
)
. It is noted that in all limiting cases, the EoS parameter approaches constant values. These asymptotic values, especially those associated with the early and present epochs of the Universe, may either align with the cosmological constant
w_{\Lambda}=-1
or differ from it. This behavior is contingent on how the observational data limit the model parameters. By examining the evolution of the EoS from its early-time limit, given by
\frac{w_{0}+w_{1}}{1+w_{2}}
, to its present value
w_{0}
, one can evaluate whether the EoS significantly departs from
w_{\Lambda}=-1
. A common characteristic shared by all parametric models is their limited sensitivity to dynamical dark energy at low redshift
z\approx 0
, with sensitivity gradually increasing at higher redshifts.

As an illustration, consider the general form of the EoS parametrization expressed as
w_{\rm de}(z)=w_{0}+w_{1}X(z)

Colgáin et al. 2021
. It is demonstrated that the precision with which the parameter
w_{1}
, which governs the variation of the EoS, can be constrained from observational data is critically dependent on the behavior of the function
X(z)
that modulates its evolution. A more rapid increase in
X(z)
with redshift typically results in tighter constraints on
w_{1}
, whereas a slower growth leads to greater uncertainties. This inverse correlation between
w_{1}
and the growth rate of
X(z)
may pose challenges in the interpretation of dynamical dark energy parameterizations.

This encourages us to explore an alternative parameterization known as generalized emergent dark energy (GEDE), which incorporates both the cold dark matter (CDM) model and the phenomenological emergent dark energy (PEDE). The GEDE framework incorporates two novel parameters:
w_{0}
, which denotes the specific model under consideration, and a transition redshift
z_{t}
. This latter parameter is defined by the condition
\Omega_{de}(z_{t})=\Omega_{m}(z_{t})
, thereby establishing a relationship between the matter density parameter and the transition redshift
z_{t}
, indicating that
z_{t}
is not an independent variable. This GEDE model provides a broader cosmological perspective that connects the
\Lambda
CDM model with the PEDE model, and any deviation from
w_{0}=0
will enhance the plausibility of the new framework.
The model has been evaluated by various researchers through the Bayesian evidence analysis, which indicates a preference for the GEDE model over the
\Lambda
CDM across most observational datasets
Yang et al. 2021
;
Hernández-Almada et al. 2020
.

We would like to underscore that the flexibility and generality we offer are particularly significant for our research on
w(z)
. These attributes not only expand the range of possibilities available for testing but also, in principle, may mitigate the potential for misleading results that can arise from some particular EoS. Henceforth, we consider another form which is less famous and characterized by a dimensionless parameter
\beta
. In the limits
\beta
tending to
(-1,0,+1)
, this EoS form fully generalizes three of the most common EoS parameterizations investigated in the literature and admits a much wider range of solutions (For more details on this, see
Barboza et al. 2009
).

Across all models considered in this work, we propose a novel parameterization referred to as “BellDE”, inspired by a Gaussian-type EoS. Models with more than two free parameters—particularly those that govern high-redshift behavior—often suffer from weak observational constraints. In such cases, conclusions suggesting phantom-like behavior may be premature or misleading. To address this, the BellDE model introduces a bell-shaped EoS with approximately two effective parameters. This form prevents phantom crossing at high redshifts while retaining sufficient flexibility in the low-redshift regime, where dark energy plays a dominant role in cosmic acceleration.

In this study, we explore several parameterizations of
w(a)
aimed at deepening our understanding of dark energy dynamics, using observational data from DESI DR2
(
Gu et al. 2025
;
Abdul Karim et al. 2025a
)
. We combine the DESI DR2 BAO data with Type Ia supernova measurements from Pantheon+ and DES5YR
(
Brout et al. 2022
;
Abbott et al. 2024
)
, enabling a comprehensive analysis of dark energy characteristics across a wide redshift range.

This study is organized in the following way: Section
II
introduces the theoretical framework concerning four distinct parameterizations of the EoS and their key physical attributes. Section
III
outlines the methods and datasets used in our analysis. The findings are also detailed in the same section. A new bell-shaped EoS which we proposed is explored in Section
IV
. Further, we conclude with a summary in Section
V
.

II
Dark energy EoS parametrizations

In a universe that is spatially flat, homogeneous, and isotropic, the Friedmann constraint equation can be expressed as (ignoring the contribution from radiation
\Omega_{r0}
):

\frac{H^{2}(a)}{H_{0}^{2}}=\Omega_{m}^{(0)}a^{-3}+\left(1-\Omega_{m}^{(0)}\right)\exp\left[3\int_{a}^{1}\frac{1+w(a^{\prime})}{a^{\prime}}\,da^{\prime}\right]\ ,

(1)

where
H_{0}
denotes the present-day Hubble parameter, and the current matter density parameter is defined as
\Omega_{m}^{(0)}=\frac{8\pi G\rho_{m}^{(0)}}{3H_{0}^{2}}
. Consequently, the present-day dark energy density parameter is given by
\Omega_{\text{DE}}^{(0)}=1-\Omega_{m}^{(0)}
, assuming spatial flatness. The scale factor evolves with redshift as
a=\frac{1}{1+z}
.

In this framework, the underlying theory of gravity remains unmodified, enabling precise reconstruction of key cosmological parameters through various parameterizations of the dark energy EoS as outlined below:

•

Extended Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL):
To begin with, we analyze a parameterized approach by expressing the time-dependent
w(a)
for dark energy using a Taylor series expansion around
(a_{0}=1)

(
Dai et al. 2018
)
:

\omega_{\rm de}(a)=\omega_{0}+(1-a)\omega_{1}+(1-a)^{2}\omega_{2}+\cdots\ ,

(2)

where the approximation has been performed up to the second order. In contrast to the first-order approximation, we extend the expansion to second order in the scale factor
a
. This approach has been taken to tackle the recent issue that emerged from the DESI DR2 data, where dark energy becomes phantom at higher redshifts.

•

PADE approximation:
Unlike the Taylor series approximation of the EoS for dark energy, we now include a Padé approximation of
\omega(a)
, which can be generally expressed as
(m/n)

(
Wei et al. 2014
;
Rezaei et al. 2024
;
Hu et al. 2024
)

\omega_{\rm de}(a)\approx\frac{p_{0}+p_{1}(a-1)+\cdots+p_{m}(a-1)^{m}}{1+q_{1}(a-1)+\cdots+q_{n}(a-1)^{n}}\ .

(3)

Truncating the series upto first order (1/1), we get:

\omega_{\rm de}(a)\approx\frac{\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}(1-a)}{1+\omega_{2}(1-a)}\ .

(4)

It is important to note that various dark energy equations of state proposed in the literature can be recovered as special cases of this general parametrization.

•

GEDE:
The EoS for the GEDE model is
(
Hernández-Almada et al. 2020
)
:

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm de}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1-\frac{w_{0}}{3\ln 10}

(5)

\displaystyle\times\left[1+\tanh\left(w_{0}\times\log_{10}\left(\frac{1+z}{1+z_{t}}\right)\right)\right],

where
z_{t}
denotes the transition redshift, determined by the condition
\Omega_{\text{DE}}(z_{t})=\Omega_{\rm m}(1+z_{t})^{3}
, and therefore it is not treated as a free parameter. In terms of the scale factor, the above expression can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm de}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1-\frac{w_{0}}{3\ln 10}

(6)

\displaystyle\times\left[1+\tanh\left(w_{0}\times\log_{10}\left(\frac{a_{t}}{a}\right)\right)\right].

It is observed that when
w_{0}=0
, the standard
\Lambda
CDM model is regained, and in the case where
w_{0}=1
and
z_{t}=0
, the PEDE model is obtained
(
Li and Shafieloo 2019
;
Liu et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Yang et al. 2021
)
.

•

Generalized Dark Energy:
Here, we investigate another interesting parameterization characterized by a dimensionless parameter
\beta

(
Barboza et al. 2009
)
. In the limits of
\beta
, this formulation provides a comprehensive generalization of three of the most prevalent EoS parameterizations examined in the literature and accommodates a considerably broader range of solutions.

The EoS is given by:

\omega_{\rm de}(a)=\omega_{0}-\omega_{1}\frac{a^{\beta}-1}{\beta}\ .

(7)

The choice of
\beta
leads to diverse representations of the EoS . For instance,

\omega_{\rm de}(a)=\begin{cases}\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}\left(\frac{1}{a}-1\right),&\beta=-1,\\
\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}\ln a^{-1},&\beta=0,\\
\omega_{0}+\omega_{1}(1-a),&\beta=1\ .\end{cases}

(8)

In this study, we vary the parameter
\beta
and impose constraints based on the considered data.

IV
A Bell-Shaped EoS

We propose a novel EoS for dark energy that features a bell-shaped profile. Its mathematical form is given by:

w_{\rm de}(z)=w_{1}+(w_{0}-w_{1})\exp\left(-\left(\frac{z-z_{t}}{\Delta}\right)^{2}\right)\ .

(9)

Here,
w_{0}
does not represent the present-day value of the EoS. Instead, the EoS at
z=0
depends on the choice of the transition redshift
z_{t}
and the width parameter
\Delta
, which together determine the current value of dark energy’s EoS . In this work, we treat
z_{t}
and
\Delta
as free parameters of the model.

This form is motivated by a critical observation of the behaviors exhibited by the CPL, Padé, and GDE parameterizations. In those models, the EoS tends to cross into the phantom regime (
w<-1
) around
z<0.5
. From
\Lambda
CDM and related models, we understand that dark energy becomes dynamically significant in the low-redshift regime, particularly for
z<0.5
. However, the scarcity of high-quality data in this redshift range makes it challenging to tightly constrain model parameters that govern intermediate and early-time dynamics.

As a result, models with more than two free parameters—especially those affecting higher-redshift behavior—often remain weakly constrained. In such cases, inferring a phantom-like nature from the data may be premature or misleading. The bell-shaped EoS we propose is designed to address this issue by explicitly avoiding phantom crossing while maintaining flexibility near the low-redshift regime where dark energy is most influential.

w_{1}

w_{1}>-1
, the dark energy density tends to increase with redshift; however, this increase is suppressed by the dominance of matter and radiation at early times, which is portrayed in the Figure

V
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we examine a range of dark energy EoS parameterizations, including extensions beyond the CPL form, to identify possible departures from the standard cosmological constant value
w_{\Lambda}=-1
. In light of the 2025 data release from the DESI, we revisited our analysis of dynamical dark energy reconstructions by incorporating the newly released DESI DR2 BAO data along with measurements from Pantheon+ and DES5Y supernovae. Our approach allowed us to trace the evolution of the dark energy EoS over time. Overall, we found that while the general shape of the reconstructed
w(a)
aligns with findings from recent DESI studies, there has been a noteworthy shift in the statistical backing for the concept of dynamical dark energy. As a result, it is essential to verify the results from one DE parametrization by comparing them with others to ensure their reliability.

We initiated our study using a polynomial form of the Taylor series expansion of
w(a)
up to the second order, which we refer to as the extended CPL. Through our analysis, we found that the EoS for dark energy at high redshifts is likely to differ significantly from the standard
\Lambda
CDM model (i.e.,
w(z)<-1
), suggesting that
w(a)>-1
in the present day. To further investigate the potential interpretations of this observed deviation’s physical origin, we considered three additional model categories. Each of these models exhibits distinct dynamical characteristics and is grounded in varying degrees of underlying physical theory, which may deepen our understanding and foster further constructive discussions. The addition of CC with DESI+SN does not provide considerable additional constraining power. As we observed, when DESY5 is included (refer to Figure
5
), there is evidence for a higher
H_{0}
compared to when Pantheon+ is present.

Another category of DE models we examine is the generalized emergent class, where dark energy has had little to no influence throughout most of cosmic history and only begins to emerge in more recent times. It is important to note that while this model can replicate the emergence of dark energy, it is constrained by its inability to cross
w=-1
, as is anticipated even with late-time datasets. Specifically, any apparent crossing in the
w(a)
parameterization could be misleading in efforts to align with observables, prompting the question of whether
w(a)
actually crosses
-1
or if the observed behavior is merely a result of the parameterization artefact.

Finally, we conducted an assessment of the novel EoS we proposed, designated “BellDE”. This bell-shaped EoS inherently facilitates the possibility of reversing the transition, enabling a return to conditions where
w>-1
, thereby restoring quintessential behavior at higher redshifts. While the number of free parameters associated with this model is comparable to those found in previously discussed EoS models, the uncertainties pertaining to the parameters of the bell-shaped model are significantly smaller. This observation underscores its enhanced capacity for constraint. Furthermore, unlike prior models that frequently display substantial phantom behavior at elevated redshifts, the bell-shaped EoS effectively mitigates such behavior in the early universe, approaching a non-phantom value asymptotically. Our evaluation suggests that the data reveals a tendency towards the BellDE model, although this inclination is marked by a tension of under
1\sigma
(with PP) when contrasted with the
H_{0}
obtained from CMB observations, and less than
3\sigma
for SH0ES. Despite the relatively low level of tension, which suggests a degree of consistency, the preference for the BellDE model remains a stable and robust finding within our dataset.

The forthcoming supernova observations from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope
(
Hounsell et al. 2023
)
are expected to extend the Hubble diagram explored by DESI to lower redshifts, thereby tightening constraints on the dark energy EoS parameter
w_{0}
. These advancements position the next decade as potentially transformative for cosmology, with the possibility of revealing whether a paradigm shift is required in our understanding of the Universe. As the precision and volume of cosmological data continue to grow, discrepancies between different measurement approaches have become increasingly apparent. This evolving statistical landscape introduces substantial challenges in interpreting observational results, understanding the underlying physics, and accounting for systematic uncertainties. Consequently, the field now stands at a critical juncture in addressing these complexities and refining the theoretical framework of modern cosmology.

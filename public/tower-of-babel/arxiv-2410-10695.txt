Abstract
. By designating vertices with variables, a simple undirected graph can be augmented to have associated representing rational function in two variables taking the complex bi-upper halfplane to itself. We give relations between representing functions of certain products of such graphs by way of Schur complements. We also study the connection between the structure of the graph and the regularity of the representing function at a boundary singularity.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Pi=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\IM z>0\}
denote the complex upper halfplane. A Pick function is an analytic function
f
with the property that
\IM f(z)\geq 0
whenever
z\in\Pi
. (We note that these functions are referred to in parallel literature as Herglotz, Nevanlinna, or R- functions.) An inner Pick function has the additional property that
f(z)\in\mathbb{R}
whenever
z\in\mathbb{R}
(that is, inner Pick functions map the boundary into the boundary. With a mild assumption on the growth of
f
along the imaginary axis, such functions have what is known as a
Nevanlinna representation
- that is, such
f
are the Cauchy transforms of positive Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
1
]
, Agler, Tully-Doyle, and Young gave a generalization to several variables. Here, we give the two variable case. In two variables, a Pick function is analytic function
f:\Pi^{2}\to\overline{\Pi}
. An inner Pick function additionally has
f(z)\in\mathbb{R}
whenever
(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
f
is a Pick function in two variables. If
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}s\left|f(is,is)\right|<\infty
, then there exist a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a (densely-defined) self-adjoint operator

on
\mathcal{H}
, a positive contraction

on
\mathcal{H}
, and a vector

so that

f(z,w)=\left\langle(A-z_{Y})^{-1}\alpha,\alpha\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},

where
z_{Y}=zY+w(I-Y)
.

In the converse direction, Pascoe
[
13
]
, Bickel, Pascoe, and Sola
[
7
]
, and Bickel and Hong
[
5
]
gave specific classes of

Theorem 1.2

(

.

Let

.

Given such a graph

, we call the resulting Pick function

the

of

. These sorts of Cauchy transforms of graph related objects have been studied in the context of free products of graphs in, e.g,
[

. Other work related to asymptotics and cumulants can be found, for example, in

.

The graph theoretic viewpoint taken by Bickel and Hong in
[
10
,
5
]
provides a new vantage on the structure of rational inner functions. Following them, our work is concerned with two basic questions. First, can we recover information about the boundary regularity of

from the structure of

[
6
,
7
]
, in particular the notion of
contact order
at a boundary singularity. Second, what is the connection between certain products or decomposition of graphs and the relationship between their representing functions? Here, we follow work that recently originated in the thesis of Hong
[
10
]
and was followed up by Bickel and Hong
[
5
]
. Given the adjacency matrix
A
of a graph
G
, we can view the expression
A-z_{Y}=A-zY-w(1-Y)
with
Y
as in (

) as the adjacency matrix of a chromatic graph with self-loops at each vertex weighted by
z
and
w
. Viewing these self-loops as a sort of vertex coloring, when

in

, such a graph will have exactly one vertex colored with a
w
and all others colored with

Our main results are as follows. Given two rooted simple undirected graphs
G,G^{\prime}
, the
star product
(to use the terminology of
[
3
]
)
G\star G^{\prime}
is the graph obtained by joining
G
and
G^{\prime}
at the root. For compatible graphs, we show in Corollary
3.5
by elementary linear algebraic arguments an additive relationship between the representing functions. Likewise, the
comb product

G\triangleright G^{\prime}
attaches a copy of
G^{\prime}
to every vertex of
G
. For compatible graphs, we show a composition relationship between the representing functions of the graph in Corollary
3.6
. (Such compositions are typically quite difficult to recover for even rational two-variable functions.) Finally, in Theorem
4.6
, we connect the graph theory to the boundary behavior of associated functions; we prove that given a colored adjacency matrix with a single vertex colored

and all others

-colored root to the single

f_{G}
at infinity, partially resolving a conjecture of Bickel and Hong
[
5
]
and giving a method for constructing a rich class of examples with prescribed boundary behavior.

Proof.

This is essentially a restatement of Theorem

∎

That is, given

Representing functions for graphs are invariant under relabeling of vertices

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

Proof.

Note that

so that
U_{\phi}^{-1}A_{G}U_{\phi}=A_{H}

Then

\displaystyle f^{\phi(k)}_{H}(z,w)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}_{H}^{-1})_{(\phi(k),\phi(k))}

\displaystyle=(U_{\phi}^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{G}U_{\phi})^{-1}_{(\phi(k),\phi(k))}

\displaystyle=(U_{\phi}^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{G}^{-1}U_{\phi})_{(\phi(k),\phi(k))}

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{A}_{G}^{-1})_{(k,k)}

\displaystyle=f_{G}^{k}(z,w).

∎

Note that this implies that we can put any colored adjacency matrix into the form required to apply Theorem

4.
Contact order

In
[
6

7

, Bickel, Pascoe, and Sola describe the behavior of a rational inner function near boundary singularity by considering the structure of the level curves of the function near the singularity. In particular, they prove that the branches of the level sets have local analytic parametrizations. Bickel, Pascoe and Sola work in the conformally equivalent setting of rational inner functions on the complex unit bidisk.

In this section, parallel to the work in
[
5

, we consider functions of degree
1
in the

[
5
]
, Bickel and Hong get a refined version of Theorem
2.2
where a special singularity exists that detects this connection. Our construction produces a singularity at
(\infty,0)

[

(0,0)
with the map

Theorem 4.1
.

[Theorem 1.1,
[
5
]
]
Let

be the adjacency matrix of a simple undirected graph
G

n

(\infty,0)
.

Following
[

\infty

\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we can solve

to get

and by
[

]
this function is (locally) analytic.

Order of contact
measures the similarity of level curves as they bunch up at a singularity - the extent to which the level curves are independent of the choice of value
\lambda
can be quantified by looking at the order of vanishing of the function
\Lambda_{\lambda}(z)=\Lambda_{\mu}(z)
, expanded as a series in

at
\infty
.

Using this idea, in
[

]
, Bickel and Hong make the following conjecture, which they prove in several cases by way of careful analysis of Neumann series expansions and determinantal techniques. (In fact, Bickel and Hong also consider the case where the matrix

on the diagonal, which leads to mixed colors on the vertices. We do not address this case here, and so that part of their conjecture remains open.)

be a graph with a single vertex colored

Notice that in the example above, we have

(\mathcal{A}_{G}^{-1})_{(1,1)}=\begin{bmatrix}-z+g(z)&f(z)\\

where

represent the “path data” of the graph. These are related to what are often called walk generating functions. Recall that if
A
is the adjacency matrix of a graph, the entry

counts walks of length
n
from vertex

to vertex

. The walk generating function is the matrix valued function

W(z)=[W_{ij}(z)]_{ij}=(A-zI)^{-1}

which will be defined off of the spectrum of

. Notice that for
i=j
, we recover a one-variable Nevanlinna transform
W_{ii}(z)=\langle(A-zI)^{-1}e_{i},e_{i}\rangle
. While the

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a z-colored graph with adjacency matrix A. Then the expansion at
\infty
of the walk generating function from vertex
i
to vertex

is
-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(
# of paths from i to j of length n
)z^{-(n+1)}
.

Proof.

The walk generating function from vertex
i
to vertex
j
is given by
W_{ij}(z)=(A-zI)^{-1}_{(i,j)}
. But we can write, for sufficiently large
z
,

\displaystyle W_{ij}(z)=(A-zI)^{-1}_{(i,j)}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{z}\left(I-\dfrac{A}{z}\right)^{-1}_{(i,j)}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{z}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{A^{n}}{z^{n}}\right)_{(i,j)}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(A^{n})_{(i,j)}}{z^{n+1}}.

Since the
i,j
entry of
A^{n}
gives the number of paths of length
n
from
i
to
j
we have that

W_{ij}(z)=-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\text{\# of paths from i to j of length n})z^{-(n+1)}.

∎

In particular, we observe that the functions
W_{ij}(z)=\langle(A-zI)^{-1}e_{i},e_{j}\rangle
admit power series expansions at
\infty
. We also note that the previous lemma can be proved with an induction on graphs, but the method is quite a bit more tedious.

Lemma 4.5
.

In a graph
G
, if the shortest path from vertex

to

then the order of vanishing of the path function at
\infty
from

to

.

Proof.

Since we have an infinite series for the path function at
\infty
we look at the first term for the order of vanishing. The first term is the first
n
for which there is a path of length
n
between vertices
i
and

which will be

. Then the order of vanishing will be
n+1
when the shortest path is length
n
.
∎

We are now ready to resolve Conjecture
4.2

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
G
be a colored graph with one vertex, say
j
, labelled
w
and all others
z
. Then the order of contact of the Nevanlinna representation at vertex
i
is
2n
where the minimum path length from
i
to
j
is
n
.

Proof.

The argument goes by cases.

Case 1:
First, consider the case where
n\geq 2
(that is, the vertices of interest aren’t directly connected). By Lemma
2.4
, without loss of generality we consider the case where we calculate the Nevanlinna representation from vertex 1 and the
w
is located at vertex 2. Then the matrix
\mathcal{A}_{G}
can be written

\mathcal{A}_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-z&0\\
0&-w\end{bmatrix}&A_{12}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}\end{bmatrix},

where
A_{22}
represents the graph formed from all paths from vertices adjacent to vertex
1
to vertices adjacent to vertex
2
, and
A_{12}=A_{21}^{T}
encodes the connection of the “middle” graph to the terminating vertices
1
and
2
.
Taking a Schur complement with respect to the
(1,1)
block gets

f_{G}(z,w)=(\mathcal{A}_{G}^{-1})_{(1,1)}=\begin{bmatrix}-z-g(z)&-p(z)\\
-p(z)&-w-h(z)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}_{(1,1)}

where
g
is the sum of walk generating functions that represent walks leaving and returning to vertex 1 through each adjacent vertex,
h
is the sum of walk generating functions representing walks leaving and returning to vertex
2
through each vertex adjacent to vertex
2
, and
p
is the sum of walk generating functions representing the paths connecting vertex
1
to vertex
2
through each pair of edges adjacent to vertex
1
and vertex
2
respectively. We have reduced the problem to finding the
(1,1)
entry of the inverse of a
2\times 2
scalar matrix. Hence,

\displaystyle f_{G}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-z-g(z)&-p(z)\\
-p(z)&-w-h(z)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-h-w}{-p^{2}+gh+gw+hz+wz}.

To calculate the order of contact, we find the structure of the level curves in a neighborhood of
(\infty,0)
by setting

f_{G}(z,w)=\lambda.

Solving for
w
gives the level curve parametrization

w=\Lambda_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{p^{2}\lambda-gh\lambda-h\lambda z-h}{g\lambda+\lambda z+1}=-h+p^{2}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda(g+z)}.

So the analysis comes down to understanding the function

\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda(g(z)+z)}.

As
g(z)
is a sum of walk generating functions in
z
,
g
has a power series expansion at
\infty
, and so expanding the rational expression as a series in
z
at
\infty
, we get

\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda(g(z)+z)}=\frac{1}{z}-\frac{a_{0}}{z^{2}}-\frac{1}{\lambda z^{2}}+O\left(\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)^{3}\right).

That is, the first term that depends on
\lambda
is order 2. Note that
p
, another sum of walk generating functions, when expanded as a power series at
\infty
, has lowest order term of order
n-1
where
n
is the shortest path length between
i
and
j
by Lemma
4.5
. So
p^{2}
has lowest order term
2n-2
. Thus

\Lambda_{\lambda}(z)=-h+p^{2}\cdot\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda(g+z)}=-h+\frac{p^{2}}{z}-\frac{a_{0}p^{2}}{z^{2}}-\frac{p^{2}}{\lambda z^{2}}+O\left(\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)^{3}\right),

which implies that generically
\Lambda_{\lambda}(z)-\Lambda_{\mu}(z)
has order of vanishing

and hence

has order of contact

(\infty,0)
.

Case 2:
Now consider the case where the distance from vertex 1 to vertex 2 is 1 - that is, the

\mathcal{A}_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-z&1\\

\displaystyle f_{G}(z,w)=(\mathcal{A}_{G})^{-1}_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-z-g(z)&1-p(z)\\
1-p(z)&-w-h(z)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{-h-w}{-p^{2}+2p+gh+gw+hz+wz-1}.

Choosing a value

Performing a similar expansion at

is the first term depending on

, and so the order of contact is
2
.

Case 3:
Finally, consider the case where we compute a Nevanlinna representation with respect to a vertex colored
w

\mathcal{A}_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}w&A_{12}\\
A_{12}^{T}&A_{22}\end{bmatrix}.

Now compute the Nevalinna representation to get

\displaystyle f_{G}(z,w)=(\mathcal{A}_{G}^{-1})_{(1,1)}

\displaystyle=(-w-A_{12}A_{22}^{-1}A_{12}^{T})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{-w-p(z)}

where

is the sum of walk generating functions leaving and returning to vertex

\Lambda_{\lambda}(z)=-p(z)-\frac{1}{\lambda}.

By Lemma

,

has lowest order term at least 1, and so the constant term

cannot be canceled out by

∎

Note that this observation gives a method for constructing families of rational Pick functions with prescribed boundary behavior at a guaranteed singularity. It remains open if we can extend this idea to design a function with more complicated prescribed boundary behavior. We should note that generally this construction creates two or more singularities in the resulting function - one remaining question is to understand additionally arising boundary singularities in terms of the graph structure.

5.
A combinatorial observation

The following observation of potentially independent interest arose in the development of the function theory of the previous section. Following Bickel and Hong’s terminology
[
10
]
, a
stick graph
(more commonly called a
path graph
in graph theory) consists of a line of vertices with adjacent vertices connected by edges. Let
S_{n}
be the
z
-colored adjacency matrix of the n-stick, a stick with
n
vertices. For example:

\displaystyle S_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-z\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle S_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-z&1\\
1&-z\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle S_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-z&1&0\\
1&-z&1\\
0&1&-z\end{bmatrix}

and so on.
Let
T_{n}=\det(S_{n})
. The following observation may be of independent interest. (See also
[
17
, Section 3.1]
.)

Theorem 5.1
.

For stick graphs the determinant
T_{n}
, of stick
S_{n}
, is the coefficient of
x^{n}
in the power series of

f(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2}+xz}.

Proof.

Note that,

\displaystyle T_{n}

\displaystyle=\det(S_{n})=\det\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}-z&\begin{matrix}1&0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{matrix}&S_{n-1}\end{array}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\begin{matrix}-z&1\\
-z&1\end{matrix}&\begin{matrix}0&\cdots&&0\\
1&0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}0&1\\
\vdots&0\\
&\vdots\\
0&0\end{matrix}&S_{n-2}\end{array}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=-z\cdot\det(S_{n-1})-1\cdot\det\left(\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c}1&\begin{matrix}0&\cdots&0\end{matrix}\\
\hline\cr\begin{matrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{matrix}&S_{n-2}\end{array}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=-z\cdot\det(S_{n-1})-\det(S_{n-2})

\displaystyle=-z\cdot T_{n-1}-T_{n-2}

With this recurrence relation and the fact that
T_{1}=-z
,
T_{2}=z^{2}-1
, we can solve this for a generating function. Noting that
T_{0}=1
fits the recurrence relation, we solve for the ordinary generating function of
T_{n}
,
f(x)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n}x^{n}
.

\displaystyle T_{n}

\displaystyle=-z\cdot T_{n-1}-T_{n-2},\qquad n\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}T_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-z\cdot\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}T_{n-1}x^{n}-\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}T_{n-2}x^{n}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=2}^{\infty}T_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-zx\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{n}x^{n}-x^{2}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n}x^{n}

\displaystyle f(x)-xT_{1}-T_{0}

\displaystyle=-zx(f(x)-T_{0})-x^{2}f(x)

\displaystyle f(x)(1+zx+x^{2})

\displaystyle=-zx+1+zx

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+zx+x^{2}}

∎

The polynomial coefficients of the series representation of
\frac{1}{1+zx+x^{2}}
have numerical coefficients that are certain diagonals in Pascal’s triangle and arise in certain so-called Riordan arrays (see
[
12
, A109466]
and
[
4
]
).

6.
Open Questions

Certain obvious extensions remain. In addition to the set of open questions in the final section of
[
5
]
, we have computational evidence for the following conjecture.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Given a graph
G
, colored with
z
and
w
, the contact order of the representation computed from vertex
i
is twice the path distance to the closest vertex colored
w
.

This generalizes Theorem
4.6
to include graphs where there is not just one but multiple
w
vertices, which results in rational inner Pick functions of general degree
(n,m)
. The difficulty is in the local analytic parametrization of the level curves near a singularity, which will have branches rather than a single function
[
7
]
. However, evidence suggests that every branch has the exact same order of contact.

We also have some preliminary work on what happens if vertices are colored with convex combinations of
z
and
w
. Bickel and Hong consider this case. While the star and comb product formulas carry through in this setting, we have not been able to use our methods to explore questions about singular behavior.

More generally, there seem to be connections between this graph-theoretic interpretation of the Nevanlinna representation and new work by one of the authors and J. E. Pascoe on the representation of formal languages by noncommutative series
[
14
]
that would be worth exploring.

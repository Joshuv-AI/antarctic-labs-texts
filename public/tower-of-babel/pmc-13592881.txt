An Innovative Pillow for Reducing Intraocular Pressure During Sleep
Authors: Mohammad Mohammadi, Elon H. C. van Dijk, Alireza Attar
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

Intraocular pressure (IOP) is the most important modifiable risk factor for glaucoma.[1] It has been shown that IOP follows a circadian rhythm, reaching its maximum at night in both healthy subjects and patients with glaucoma.[2] Body position is another factor that affects IOP fluctuations.[3] Compared with the upright position, IOP has been shown to increase in horizontal positions (supine, prone, or side-lying) in healthy and glaucomatous eyes.[3] Such posture-induced IOP elevation has been associated with the glaucomatous damage progression.[3]

Previous studies have shown that the head-up position, elevating the head by 30° during sleep, and minimizing eye contact with the pillow surface may contribute to lower IOP.[45] Based on these findings, we designed an innovative pillow to help reduce IOP during sleep.

Figure 1 shows the pillow and its mechanism of action. The unique design of this pillow is a sloping surface with two ridges on the upper surface, which support the head and neck, respectively. This pillow positions the user’s head 30° above the horizon. Uniquely, the design of this pillow allows the eyes to be placed in the empty space between the two ridges supporting the neck and head. Doing so, the eyes do not come into contact with any surface, even in the lateral decubitus and prone positions. As a result, the user’s eyes do not contact the pillow’s surface in any of the sleeping positions. This feature prevents physical pressure on the eye area. Moreover, the pillow’s body material is pressure-sensitive memory foam, which automatically adjusts its height based on the weight of the head and neck. As a result, theoretically, this pillow provides the required conditions mentioned in previous studies for potentially reducing IOP during sleep, possibly providing accessible, cost-effective, and noninvasive methods for glaucoma treatment.

(a) The designed pillow and the positioning of the user’s head and neck in (b) supine, (c) prone, and (d) lateral decubitus positions. In all sleeping positions, the head is 30° above the horizon, and the eyes do not contact the pillow’s surface

However, future studies are needed to measure this pillow’s efficiency and refine its design for optimal ergonomics.

Low miR-223 links to major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events in end-stage renal disease through endothelial damage
Authors: Jie Liao, Xueling Cao, Lili Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEnd-stage renal disease (ESRD) is frequently accompanied by major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) in affected patients. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop effective biomarkers.MethodsThis prospective study included 112 ESRD patients (62 with MACCE, 50 Non-MACCE), with baseline clinical data and blood samples collected for all subjects. We quantified miR-223 expression through RT-qPCR. To clarify the independent predictive value of miR-223, we conducted multivariate logistic analysis to verify its independent association with the occurrence of MACCE, and drew ROC curves to evaluate its ability to distinguish the occurrence or non-MACCE. Then, we induced inflammation in HUVECs with TNF-α, transfected miR-223 overexpression vectors into these cells, and observed corresponding changes in inflammatory cytokines, adhesion molecules, endothelial markers, nitric oxide metabolites, and cell proliferation.ResultsClinical analysis of ESRD patients revealed that the development of MACCE was associated with significantly lower baseline expression of miR-223 compared to the non-MACCE group. Multivariate analysis confirmed low levels of miR-223 as an independent predictor of MACCE with robust discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.896). In addition, miR-223 levels were inversely related to inflammatory markers (hs-CRP) and serum phosphorus and iPTH. In vitro, miR-223 overexpression suppressed inflammation, the expression of adhesion molecules, ET-1 secretion, normalized nitrite levels, and inhibited aberrant endothelial proliferation.ConclusionThis study confirms that reduced miR-223 is an independent predictor of MACCE risk in ESRD patients, with confirmed multifaceted endothelial protective effects in vitro. miR-223 could be a new biomarker integrating multiple risk pathways for cardiovascular risk assessment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12882-026-05014-9.Keywords: miR-223, End-stage renal disease, Major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, Biomarker

Background

End-stage renal disease (ESRD), the irreversible outcome of chronic kidney disease, necessitates lifelong hemodialysis or kidney transplantation for survival. Once reaching end-stage renal disease, patients experience disrupted internal environments and fluid balance, necessitating lifelong dialysis or kidney transplantation to sustain life [1]. This not only severely impacts quality of life but also imposes a heavy financial burden [2, 3]. However, due to the extreme scarcity of kidney donors worldwide, the vast majority of patients have no choice but to undergo hemodialysis [4, 5]. Among these patients, cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death, accounting for approximately half of all deaths, with a risk of major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE) that is 10 to 20 times higher than that of the general population [6, 7]. While inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein(hs-CRP) and interleukin-6(IL-6) have been established as predictors, the pathogenesis of MACCE in ESRD is multifactorial [8, 9]. Therefore, relying solely on inflammatory cytokines to predict risk results in insufficient sensitivity and specificity [10, 11]. To achieve a more precise risk assessment, novel biomarkers that comprehensively reflect the complex pathophysiological processes underlying MACCE must be identified.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are not just passive biomarkers but active regulators in the onset of cardiovascular complications linked to ESRD, with MACCE being one of the most critical manifestations affected by miRNA-mediated pathways [12]. For example, in previous studies, we found that miR-142-3p can improve vasodilatory function in patients with end-stage renal disease and may help prevent arterial stiffness and endothelial dysfunction [13, 14]. Of all these miRNAs, miR-223 stands out as a focus of our investigation, largely because it plays diverse roles in the disease processes relevant to MACCE. We’ve found consistent evidence that miR-223 correlates closely with endothelial dysfunction, abnormal lipid metabolism, and the major inflammatory drivers behind MACCE onset [15–17]. More importantly, existing research indicates that miR-223 exerts dual functions: it not only targets and inhibits neutrophil activation via the ABCA1 protein, but also blocks the cholesterol efflux process [18, 19]. Notably, this microRNA is also abnormally regulated in ESRD patients. Specifically, their serum levels are markedly reduced in those on hemodialysis. What is more, this reduction is not permanent; kidney transplantation restores the microRNA to normal levels, indicating reversibility [20]. From these results, we hypothesize that miR-223 may function as an integrative biomarker and that dysregulation of its expression levels could be a strong predictor of MACCE in patients with end-stage renal disease.

Thus, this study aims to elucidate the role of miR-223 in MACCE by assessing its clinical predictive value in ESRD patients and investigating its protective mechanism in endothelial cells.

Method

Study population

From April 2018 to September 2021, 112 eligible patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis at Rongxian People’s Hospital were enrolled in this study. Inclusion criteria were: (1) Age ≥ 18 years and at least 6 months of regular hemodialysis; (2) Meeting ESRD diagnostic criteria; (3) Willingness to participate in long-term follow-up. Exclusion criteria included: (1) Major coronary artery disease—including prior myocardial infarction, advanced heart failure, severe valvular heart disease, or history of stroke/transient ischemic attack; (2) Infection, trauma, or major surgery within the past 3 months; (3) Hematologic, immunologic, or autoimmune disorders; (4) Pregnancy/lactation; (5) Refusal to sign informed consent.

All patients were followed for 42 months through quarterly outpatient clinic reviews. For the two patients who transferred to other centers or underwent kidney transplantation during follow-up, outcome data were obtained via telephone interviews. Thus, complete follow-up data were available for all 112 patients, with no loss to follow-up (Fig. 1). After completion of follow-up, patients were categorized into the MACCE group or the non-MACCE group based on whether they experienced a major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular event during the follow-up period. The primary endpoint was MACCE, including all-cause mortality, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke, and acute coronary syndrome that necessitates revascularization.

Flow chart of patient enrollment and follow-up

This study complies with the Declaration of Helsinki. The research protocol received approval from the Rongxian People’s Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval number: RY2018-018), and every participant provided written informed consent before joining the study.

Data collection

All blood samples were collected at the time of patient enrollment and before the start of routine hemodialysis, and the patients were required to remain fasting. The collected peripheral blood samples were allowed to clot naturally at room temperature for approximately 30 min. Subsequently, to isolate the serum, we placed the samples in a pre-chilled centrifuge and centrifuged them at 4 °C and 3000×g for 10 min. The separated serum was transferred into new sterile centrifuge tubes, aliquoted, and immediately stored long-term in an ultra-low temperature freezer at -80℃. Each sample was aliquoted individually to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. Demographic, clinical and laboratory parameters were recorded at the baseline stage. Demographic characteristics included age, gender and smoking status (current smoker or never smoker). Clinical parameters included hypertension, dialysis duration (expressed in years), and body mass index (BMI, kg/m²), ESRD etiology (diabetic nephropathy, hypertensive nephropathy, primary glomerulonephritis, or others), and medication use (RAS inhibitors, statins, or others). Laboratory measurements covered serum albumin (ALB, g/L), high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP, mg/L), hemoglobin (Hb, g/L), serum phosphorus (unit: mmol/L), serum calcium (mmol/L), intact parathyroid hormone (iPTH, pg/mL), as well as lipid profile indices, specifically triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), all with the unit of mmol/L. All biochemical tests were conducted at the hospital’s accredited central laboratory following standardized protocols.

Cell culture and establishment of disease model

Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were obtained from Procell (Cat# CP-H082) and cultured in Endothelial Cell Growth Medium-2 (EBM-2, Lonza), 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS), and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Cells from passages 3–6 were used for all experiments.

RT-qPCR

The TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was employed to extract the total RNA from the cells, and the RNA mass was verified by NanoDrop 2000. RNA samples with A260/A280 ratios of 1.8–2.1 and no degradation on 1.5% agarose gel were used. With 10 ng of RNA as the template, we synthesized cDNA using miR-223-specific stem-loop reverse transcription primers (Vazyme, Cat# MR101-01, China) and the TaqMan miRNA reverse transcription kit (Applied Biosystems, Shanghai, China). For mRNA analysis of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1, cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng RNA using a PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara, Japan). Quantitative PCR was performed on a QuantStudio 6 system (Applied Biosystems) with SYBR Green MasterMix in a 20 µL reaction volume. The expression of miR-223 was assayed using commercial primers and normalized to U6 snRNA. The expression of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 mRNA was assayed using gene-specific primers and normalized to GAPDH. RT-qPCR amplification was performed on a QuantStudio 6 system (Applied Biosystems) with SYBR Green detection. Relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Briefly, the average Ct value of technical replicates for each sample was used for analysis. The ΔCt was calculated as Ct(target miRNA) − Ct(U6). The ΔΔCt was then obtained by subtracting the average ΔCt of the control group (calibrator) from the ΔCt of each treatment group. Fold changes in gene expression were presented as 2−ΔΔCt.

Transfection of miR-223 modulators before disease model establishment

To validate the protective effect of miR-223, this study transfected HUVECs with 50 nM miR-223 mimic or negative control sequence using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA). After 6 h in serum-free Opti-MEM®, cells were switched to complete medium for 24 h to ensure adequate miR-223 expression. Subsequently, cells were stimulated with 10 ng/mL TNF-α (FineTest, Cat#EH0302) for 24 h, establishing four experimental groups: blank control (Control), TNF-α stimulation alone (Model), TNF-α stimulation after negative control transfection (Mode l + NC), and TNF-α stimulation after miR-223 mimic transfection (Model + mimic). This design validated the protective effect of miR-223 overexpression against TNF-α-induced endothelial inflammatory activation and dysfunction.

Nitric oxide measurement

The concentration of nitric oxide (NO) in cell culture supernatants was quantified by measuring its metabolite nitrite using the Griess assay kit (Beyotime, Cat# S0021S, China). Supernatants from four experimental groups were harvested for nitrite quantification. Equal volumes of samples and Griess reagent were mixed and incubated at room temperature for 15 min, after which absorbance was read at 540 nm using a microplate reader. The nitrite concentration (µM) of each sample was calculated against a sodium nitrite standard curve.

Quantification of inflammatory cytokines and fibrosis markers

Proinflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, and endothelin-1 [ET-1]) in cell culture supernatants were quantified using commercial ELISA kits (FineTest; Cat# EH0185 for IL-1β, EH0201 for IL-6, EU0205 for ET-1). Before assay, supernatants from each group were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to eliminate impurities. Absorbance of the reaction mixtures was measured at 450 nm on a BioTek Synergy H1 microplate reader.

Cell viability assay

Cell viability was determined using the MTT assay. At the time points of 0, 24, 48 and 72 h, 10 µL of MTT reagent (5 mg/mL, Beyotime, Cat# ST1537-1 g, China) was added to each well and then the incubation was carried out for 4 h. The supernatant was removed; DMSO was added to solubilize the methylene blue crystals. The absorbance was read at 490 nm, and relative cell viability was determined at each time point.

Statistical analysis

All experiments included at least three independent biological replicates, and all data were normalized to internal controls before being analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0. Before formal analysis, data normality was assessed. For normally distributed data, independent samples t-tests were employed; for non-normally distributed data, Mann-Whitney U tests were used to compare intergroup differences. For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post hoc test was applied for normally distributed data; otherwise, the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunn’s post hoc test was used. The correlation analysis between miR-223 and metabolic indicators was performed using Spearman correlation analysis. Multivariate logistic regression modeling served to pinpoint independent risk factors for MACCE occurrence. Subsequently, the discriminatory power of significant predictors was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calculating the area under the curve (AUC). Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. All figures were subsequently created using GraphPad Prism software.

Result

Comparison of baseline characteristics between the MACCE group and the non-MACCE group in ESRD patients

This study compared baseline characteristics among enrolled ESRD patients (Table 1). No statistically significant differences were found in age, gender, smoking history, hypertension prevalence, dialysis duration, or BMI between MACCE-developing and non-MACCE patients (all p > 0.05). Regarding biochemical indicators, the hs-CRP level showed a trend towards significance between groups (p = 0.052). No significant differences were observed between groups for ALB, Hb, lipid profile (including HDL-C, LDL-C, and TG), or Ca levels (all p > 0.05). However, the ALB level approached statistical significance between groups (p = 0.07). For ESRD etiologies, the distribution of diabetic nephropathy, hypertensive nephropathy, glomerulonephritis, and other causes was comparable between the two groups (all p > 0.05). Similarly, the use of RAS inhibitors, statins, and other medications was balanced between groups (all p > 0.05). These findings indicate that the two groups were comparable in most baseline characteristics, including etiology and medication use. However, patients who subsequently experienced MACCE had significantly higher baseline P levels (1.72 ± 0.29 vs. 1.62 ± 0.17 mmol/L, p = 0.033) and iPTH levels (196.72 ± 42.19 vs. 171.03 ± 27.43 pg/mL, p < 0.001) than those in non-MACCE.

The predictive value and clinical significance of MACCE of miR-223

Illustrated in Fig. 2A, the expression level of miR-223 in ESRD patients who experienced MACCE was significantly lower than that in the non-MACCE group (p < 0.01). Pre-experimental validation confirmed the stability of miR-223 expression, showing no significant change immediately before and after a single hemodialysis session in a subset of patients (p > 0.05, Fig.S1A). Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed including miR-223, variables that were significantly associated with MACCE in univariate analysis (dialysis vintage, albumin, hs-CRP, hemoglobin, and iPTH; Table S1), and three classes of cardiovascular medications. As shown in Table 2, after adjusting for these covariates, low miR-223 expression remained an independent predictor of MACCE (OR = 4.302, 95% CI 1.747–10.596, p = 0.002). Dialysis vintage, albumin, hs-CRP, hemoglobin, iPTH, and medication use were not significantly associated with MACCE (all p > 0.05). To further evaluate the diagnostic performance of miR-223 expression in predicting MACCE, we performed a ROC) curve analysis, result showed that miR-223 expression possessed a high capability to discriminate between the two groups, with an AUC of 0.896 (95% CI: 0.839–0.953, Fig. 2B). Furthermore, we found that the optimal cutoff value yielded a sensitivity of 88.7% and a specificity of 74.0% for predicting MACCE in our ESRD cohort. When cardiovascular medications were added to the model, the AUC remained virtually unchanged at 0.897 (Figure S1B), indicating that the predictive value of miR-223 is independent of medication use.

The correlation of miR-223 with metabolic and inflammatory indicators

To explore the potential biological role of miR-223, its correlations with key clinical indicators were analyzed (Fig. 3). miR-223 expression showed a significant negative correlation with the inflammatory marker hs-CRP (r = -0.734, p < 0.001; Fig. 3A). Conversely, a significant positive correlation was observed between miR-223 and ALB (r = 0.531, p < 0.001; Fig. 3B), a marker of nutritional status. Furthermore, miR-223 levels were inversely correlated with two core markers of mineral and bone disorder: P (r = -0.574, p < 0.001; Fig. 3C) and iPTH (r = -0.667, p < 0.001; Fig. 3D).

Effects of miR-223 overexpression on endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and cell proliferation in vitro

To investigate the functional role of miR-223, an in vitro endothelial inflammation model was established by stimulating HUVECs with TNF-α. The efficacy of miR-223 overexpression transfection was first verified. As shown in Fig. 4A, compared with the NC group and the Model, after transfection with the miR-223 overexpression vector, the expression level of miR-223 significantly increased, confirming the successful overexpression of miR-223. Functional assays further demonstrated that TNF-α stimulation triggered different endothelial dysfunction and inflammatory responses. Compared with the Control group, the levels of nitrite in the Model group were significantly elevated (Fig. 4B), and the secretion of ET-1 also increased significantly (Fig. 4C). Notably, the expressions of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 were also significantly upregulated (Fig. 4D-E), while the secretion levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 in Model cells increased significantly (Fig. 4F-G).On all the measured parameters, no significant differences were observed between the Model + NC group and the control group, indicating that the transfection process itself did not cause any non-specific effects.

Notably, transfection with miR-223 overexpression vectors significantly reversed TNF-α-induced pathological alterations. This process was accompanied by a marked restoration of intracellular miR-223 levels (Fig. 4A). First of all, in terms of functionality, miR-223 overexpression markedly reduced nitrite accumulation (Fig. 4B), normalized excessive ET-1 secretion (Fig. 4C), decreased expression of VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 (Fig. 4D-E), and suppressed release of proinflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 (Fig. 4F-G). Furthermore, MTT cell viability assays revealed that TNF-α stimulation inhibited cell proliferation, whereas miR-223 overexpression effectively restored proliferative capacity (Fig. 4H). This finding suggests that miR-223 may exert a cell survival-supporting role under inflammatory stress conditions.

Discussion

Since cardiovascular events represent the leading cause of mortality in ESRD patients receiving long-term HD [21], effective prevention has become the core focus of clinical attention. However, predictive biomarkers, especially sensitive molecular biomarkers for early warning, are still relatively scarce at present [22]. Therefore, this study aims to investigate whether miR-223 can serve as a novel clinically relevant biomarker for MACCE in this patient group.

This study demonstrates that ESRD patients experiencing MACCE exhibit significantly lower baseline serum miR-223 levels relative to counterparts without such events. These findings align with prior investigations documenting attenuated miR-223 expression in ESRD populations [23, 24]. The underlying mechanism for decreased miR-223 levels in ESRD patients may be multifactorial. Studies have shown that there is a CpG island in the miR-223 promoter. Under pathological conditions, this CpG island can become hypermethylated, and this leads to transcriptional silencing [25]. This epigenetic modification is often triggered by chronic inflammation, a hallmark pathological feature of ESRD. Additionally, miR-223 is primarily derived from myeloid lineage cells and can be transferred to endothelial cells via platelet-derived microparticles or exosomes [26, 27]. ESRD-associated immune dysregulation may impair the production of miR-223 from these cellular sources. To further validate the predictive value of serum miR-223 for MACCE risk in this population, ROC curve analysis yields an AUC of 0.896, while multivariate analysis identifies an OR of 6.64. Both analytical approaches corroborate a robust correlation between reduced miR-223 levels and elevated MACCE risk. Collectively, these results indicate that miR-223 holds substantial potential as a predictive biomarker for MACCE in ESRD patient cohorts.

To gain further insights into the biological underpinnings of this correlation, we proceeded with correlation analyses. Indeed, decreased levels of miR-223 were significantly correlated with increased levels of hs-CRP, serum phosphorus, and iPTH. In the context of ESRD, hs-CRP has been established as a strong marker of systemic microinflammation [28] while it represents a significant determinant of endothelial damage and atherosclerosis [29]. Serum phosphorus and iPTH are fundamental indicators of CKD-MBD [30]. They eventually become vascular calcification and alterations in cardiovascular structure [31]. This suggests that the pathways of inflammation and CKD-MBD may well interact and even synergistically exacerbate vascular injury. While low miR-223 levels might intensify the convergence of inflammatory and CKD-MBD cascades, further boosting systemic inflammation and disrupting mineral metabolism balance. Collectively, these observations lead us to hypothesize that serum miR-223 levels effectively reflect the cumulative cardiovascular risk burden in ESRD patients, making it a promising indicator for evaluating an individual’s cardiovascular risk status.

The above relationship was functionally validated using in vitro assays. HUVECs were chosen as the experimental model since endothelial dysfunction represents a key early event in MACCE pathogenesis, and these cells are widely used to study endothelial inflammation and barrier integrity [32]. Endothelial cells are also major targets of circulating miR-223, and this miRNA helps preserve endothelial quiescence by targeting pro-inflammatory factors including NLRP3 and ICAM-1 [27, 33]. To reproduce the chronic inflammatory condition in ESRD patients post-MACCE, TNF-α was utilized to stimulate HUVECs [34]. TNF-α is a standard inflammatory stimulus, and it has been shown to reliably induce endothelial cell activation, thereby reproducing in vitro key pathophysiological processes of the activation state, including expression of inflammatory mediators, increased leukocyte adhesion, and disturbed barrier function [35, 36].

First, this study verified that miR-223 could potently hinder the release of the principal pro-inflammatory cytokines, IL-1β and IL-6. These results are very important for pathophysiology. Because in ESRD patients, persistent chronic inflammatory states represent one of the leading causes in the development of disease progression, leading ultimately to MACCE [37]. Herein, IL-1β is a crucial molecule in the induction and perpetuation of the chronic inflammatory state found in atherosclerosis [38]. In another respect, this cytokine damages the normal integrity of the vascular endothelium via several mechanisms, such as inducing senescence in endothelial cells and impairing the function of eNOS [39], thus compromising normal vasodilation. On the other hand, as a critical inflammatory amplifier, IL-6 is associated with the adverse cardiovascular prognosis of dialysis patients [40]. The combined effect of these two elements creates a malignant inflammatory loop, initiating and perpetuating endothelial dysfunction, atherosclerosis, and thrombosis. Thus, miR-223, inhibiting both IL-1β and IL-6, may have multi-target anti-inflammatory effects along this key inflammatory axis, opening potentially new mechanisms through which vascular inflammation could be reduced in ESRD and, thus, endothelial function improved and possibly even MACCE reduced. Thus, it appears to have critical anti-inflammatory properties. Second, miR-223 downregulated the expression of endothelial adhesion molecules VCAM-1 and ICAM-1; these are the typical ‘targets’ for the adherence of inflammatory cells such as monocytes to activated endothelium, which in turn initiates atherosclerotic lesions [41]. Simultaneously, miR-223 also lowered levels of the potent vasoconstrictor ET-1. In hemodialysis patients, ET-1 is directly related to extracellular fluid volume overload and hypertension and has been considered a major factor in reinforcing endothelial dysfunction and elevated cardiovascular risks [42, 43].

At the metabolic level, miR-223 overexpression also reversed the abnormally elevated nitrite levels in patient serum. Having been considered a stable end product in NO metabolism, nitrite level disturbances usually reflect the specific NO signaling pathway that characterizes ESRD patients [44]. NO is a central molecule in the maintenance of vasodilation and the inhibition of leukocyte adhesion and platelet aggregation; however, its reduced bioavailability is a key feature of endothelial dysfunction [45]. Functionally, miR-223 overexpression strongly inhibited abnormal endothelial cell proliferation, which has a potentially strong effect on preventing pathological vascular remodeling in an atherosclerotic context.

This study correlates clinical data with functional experiments and provides insights into the possible role of miR-223 in the cardiovascular risk of ESRD patients (Fig. 5). Result shows that the decreased levels of miR-223 constitute an independent risk marker for the major MACCE events in ESRD patients and that its expression correlates significantly with key indicators of inflammatory and mineral metabolism dysregulation. Furthermore, in vitro studies confirmed that miR-223 improves endothelial function by inhibiting inflammatory activation and modulating vascular function.

Despite these findings, several limitations should be considered. First, as a single-center study with a modest sample size, our findings warrant validation in larger, multicenter cohorts. Second, our exclusion of patients with recent infections or established cardiovascular disease, while intended to minimize confounding, resulted in a selected cohort that may limit the generalizability of our findings to broader ESRD populations. Third, while we adjusted for medication use and CKD etiologies, these data were collected retrospectively and may be subject to information bias, and dialysis adequacy was not assessed. Fourth, miR-223 was measured only at baseline; serial measurements during follow-up would provide additional insights into its dynamic changes. Fifth, although we demonstrated the protective effects of miR-223 on endothelial function in vitro, the specific downstream targets and in vivo therapeutic effects require further investigation.

Beyond miRNAs, long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) represent another class of non-coding RNAs with promise as complementary biomarkers in cardiovascular diseases [46]. Whereas miRNAs primarily exert post-transcriptional regulatory effects, lncRNAs modulate gene expression across multiple levels, including transcriptional and epigenetic control [47]. Recent work has identified dysregulated lncRNA profiles in patients with ESRD, with specific lncRNAs correlating closely with cardiovascular outcomes [48]. Combining miRNAs and lncRNAs into a composite biomarker panel may enhance predictive accuracy for MACCE, as this approach captures diverse aspects of the complex molecular networks driving vascular injury and inflammation. Incorporating both miRNA and lncRNA profiling in future studies will facilitate the development of a more comprehensive non-coding RNA signature, aiding cardiovascular risk stratification in ESRD patients.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that reduced miR-223 levels correlate with MACCE risk in ESRD patients and exhibit endothelial protective effects in vitro, suggesting its potential as a prospective risk assessment biomarker.

Analysis of risk factors for poor healing and long-duration pain in conservative treatment of rib fractures
Authors: Yongzheng Yao, Shida Li, Chao Bi, Jiayu Duan, Lianjie Jiao, Jie Zheng, Lihui Wang, Gaoyang Qian
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Rib fractures are a common injury following blunt chest trauma, accounting for approximately 10% of all traumatic injuries and up to 50% of blunt chest trauma cases. These fractures are associated with a high risk of complications, such as pneumothorax, hemothorax, and pulmonary infections, and can significantly impact respiratory function. This study analyzes the risk factors for poor healing and long-duration pain in the conservative treatment of rib fractures, providing a reference for clinicians in choosing conservative treatment and formulating treatment plans. A retrospective analysis was conducted on 342 patients who underwent conservative treatment for rib fractures from January 2023 to May 2024. Baseline data, clinical data, and follow-up data were collected. Comparisons were made between the poor healing group and the good healing group, as well as between the long-duration pain group and the short-duration pain group in the conservative treatment of rib fractures. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed to identify risk factors for poor healing and long-duration pain. In patients undergoing conservative treatment for rib fractures, the average duration of pain was 12.18 ± 10.78 days, with an incidence of pulmonary complications of 59.06% (202/342) and poor healing rate of 6.43% (22/342). Significant differences were observed between the good and poor healing groups in terms of age (P = .018), presence of coronary heart disease (CHD, P = .023), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD, P < .001), blood calcium (P = .007), and alkaline phosphatase (P < .001). Similarly, significant differences were found between the long-duration and short-duration pain groups in age (P = .039), presence of diabetes (P < .001), CHD (P < .001), COPD (P < .001), and alkaline phosphatase (P < .001). Multivariate analysis identified COPD (P = .015), blood calcium (P = .013), and alkaline phosphatase (P = .006) as independent risk factors for poor healing, while diabetes (P = .001), CHD (P = .014), and alkaline phosphatase (P < .001) were independent risk factors for prolonged pain duration. COPD, blood calcium, and alkaline phosphatase are independent risk factors for poor healing in conservative treatment of rib fractures. Diabetes, CHD, and alkaline phosphatase are independent risk factors for long-duration pain in conservative treatment of rib fractures.

1. Introduction

Rib fractures are the most common injury following blunt chest trauma, accounting for approximately 10% of all traumatic injuries and about 50% of blunt chest trauma cases.[1–6] Rib fractures can also lead to pulmonary-related diseases, and in severe cases, even death, with a mortality rate of 10% to 15% for flail chest.[7,8] The primary treatment for rib fractures is conservative management, while surgical treatment is employed for patients with multiple rib fractures or bilateral cortical displacement leading to thoracic instability.[9–11]

Although conservative treatment is the mainstay for rib fractures, it may result in poor healing and related complications.[12,13] In a study by Bauman et al,[14] it was found that conservative treatment of rib fractures can lead to fracture displacement, with significant increases in displacement over time for all ribs except the 11th and 12th ribs. This may be one of the reasons for poor healing. Poor healing of rib fractures can result in persistent pain, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, and atelectasis, among other adverse outcomes. Therefore, investigating the risk factors for poor healing of rib fractures can aid clinical treatment and help prevent associated complications. One of the main clinical manifestations of rib fractures is chest wall pain.[15] Severe pain can affect patients’ breathing, making rapid pain control a primary goal to improve respiratory mechanics and reduce the risk of pulmonary and systemic side effects.[16,17]

This study explores the healing outcomes and duration of pain in the conservative treatment of rib fractures, analyzing the factors affecting poor healing and long-duration pain, to provide reference for clinical treatment.

2. Methods

A total of 342 patients with rib fractures received conservative treatment at Peking University First Hospital-Miyun hospital between January 2023 and May 2024. The age range of enrolled patients was 25 to 95 years. Among them, there were 107 cases of single rib fractures and 235 cases of fractures involving 2 or more ribs. Baseline data, clinical data, and follow-up data at 8 weeks were collected. Baseline data included gender, age, body mass index (BMI), presence of hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease (CHD), and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Clinical data included the number of rib fractures, blood sodium, blood potassium, blood calcium, hemoglobin, albumin, alkaline phosphatase, and C-reactive protein. Follow-up data included pulmonary-related complications, duration of pain, and fracture healing status. The normal range of blood sodium is 135 to 145 mmol/L. The normal range of blood potassium is 3.5 to 5.0 mmol/L. The normal range of blood calcium is 2.1 to 2.6 mmol/L. The normal range of hemoglobin is 130 to 175 g/L for men and 120 to 155 g/L for women. The normal range of albumin is 35 to 50 g/L. The normal range of alkaline phosphatase is 35 to 50 IU/L. The normal range of C-reactive protein is <10 mg/L.

Pain was evaluated using the Visual Analogue Scale, with >3 indicating persistent pain symptoms. The duration of pain was defined as the time from the initiation of conservative treatment to the disappearance of pain symptoms. In this study, long-duration pain was defined as a duration of pain ≥30 days, while a duration of pain <30 days was considered short-duration pain. Good healing was defined as the formation of bone structure and the disappearance of the fracture line on follow-up CT at 8 weeks after conservative treatment, with no more than 1 cm displacement at the fracture site and no more than 10° of angular deformity. Additionally, the absence of pain and the ability to resume normal activities indicated good healing. All other cases were considered as poor healing. Pulmonary-related complications in this study included pulmonary infection, pneumothorax, lung contusion, and hemothorax.

Inclusion criteria: (a) patients with confirmed rib fractures by X-ray or CT. (b) Rib fractures without significant displacement or angular deformity. (c) No severe associated injuries or visceral damage. (d) Less than 5 rib fractures. (e) Complete clinical and follow-up data. Exclusion criteria: (a) rib fractures with bilateral cortical displacement. (b) Pregnant or lactating women.

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 revision) and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Peking University First Hospital-Miyun Hospital. The retrospective analysis of this study waived the requirement for individual consent.

2.1. Conservative treatment plan

Conservative treatment primarily involved chest band fixation, pain management, ice packs, breathing exercises, activity restriction, rehabilitation exercises, and nutritional support. Pain management: nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs such as ibuprofen or acetaminophen were used to relieve pain. For severe pain, short-duration use of opioids was applied. Breathing exercises: regular deep breathing exercises were performed to maintain lung function and prevent atelectasis and pneumonia. Activity restriction: patients were advised to rest in bed as much as possible during the early stages, gradually increasing activity levels while avoiding prolonged periods in the same position. Activities such as lifting heavy objects, bending, and twisting the body were to be avoided. Rehabilitation exercises: after pain relief, patients were encouraged to perform gentle stretching and strengthening exercises to help restore flexibility and strength of the thorax. Nutritional support: patients were advised to consume adequate amounts of calcium, vitamin D, and protein.

2.2. Follow-up

The primary outcome of this study was the healing status of rib fractures, and the secondary outcome was the duration of pain. The healing status of rib fractures was assessed by follow-up CT scans 8 weeks after the initiation of conservative treatment at our hospital. Pain data were collected via telephone follow-up, asking patients to rate their pain on a scale of 0 to 10.

2.3. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). Quantitative variables included age, BMI, blood sodium, blood potassium, blood calcium, hemoglobin, albumin, alkaline phosphatase, C-reactive protein, and pain duration. Qualitative variables included gender, hypertension, diabetes, CHD, COPD, and fracture healing status. Normally distributed continuous data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normally distributed data were described as median (range). For continuous variables, normally distributed variables were analyzed using the t test, and non-normally distributed variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were analyzed using Fisher exact test. Univariate and multivariate regression analyses were used to determine independent risk factors for poor healing and long-duration pain in conservative treatment of rib fractures. A P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

3.2. Comparison of poor healing and good healing groups in conservative treatment of rib fractures and analysis of risk factors for poor healing

The data of the poor healing and good healing groups in conservative treatment of rib fractures are shown in Table 2. The average age of patients in the good healing group was 61.18 ± 11.87 years, while in the poor healing group, it was 67.32 ± 8.11 years, with a statistically significant difference between the 2 groups (P = .018). The proportion of diabetic patients in the good healing group was 16.88% (54/320), compared to 40.91% (9/22) in the poor healing group, showing a statistically significant difference (P = .005). The proportion of patients with CHD was 10.94% (35/320) in the good healing group and 27.27% (6/22) in the poor healing group, also showing a statistically significant difference (P = .023). The proportion of COPD patients was 0.13% (1/320) in the good healing group and 9.09% (2/22) in the poor healing group, with a statistically significant difference (P < .001). The average blood calcium level was 2.24 ± 0.11 mmol/L in the good healing group and 2.31 ± 0.13 mmol/L in the poor healing group, with a statistically significant difference (P = .007). The average alkaline phosphatase level was 123.18 ± 65.68 IU/L in the good healing group and 81.28 ± 33.67 mmol/L in the poor healing group, with a statistically significant difference (P < .001). The average duration of pain was 11.09 ± 7.58 days in the good healing group and 28.09 ± 25.96 days in the poor healing group, with a statistically significant difference (P < .001). Univariate and multivariate regression analyses identified COPD (P = .015), blood calcium (P = .013), and alkaline phosphatase (P = .006) as independent risk factors for poor healing in the conservative treatment of rib fractures (Table 3).

4. Discussion

Exploring the related risk factors for conservative treatment of rib fractures allows for the development of more scientific and individualized rehabilitation plans, improving recovery outcomes and shortening recovery time. This study analyzed the risk factors for poor healing and long-duration pain in conservative treatment of rib fractures, providing a reference for physicians to develop personalized treatment plans.

Poor healing of rib fractures can lead to breathing difficulties, chest deformities, pulmonary-related complications, and functional impairments. Investigating the risk factors for poor healing of rib fractures is essential for preventing these adverse reactions. In this study, COPD (P = .015), blood calcium (P = .013), and alkaline phosphatase (P = .006) were identified as independent risk factors for poor healing in conservative treatment of rib fractures. Chronic coughing in COPD patients may be a contributing factor to poor prognosis. In Bezerra et al’s study,[18] a COPD patient sustained rib fractures due to prolonged coughing. Therefore, controlling COPD and reducing coughing in COPD patients undergoing conservative treatment for rib fractures may aid in fracture healing. Low blood calcium levels can result in insufficient mineralization of newly formed bone, reducing the strength and stability of the fracture site. Shuid et al’s study[19] on the effect of calcium supplementation on fracture healing in rats found that calcium supplementation may improve fracture healing. Similarly, Aileen et al’s study[20] showed that oral vitamin D3 and calcium have a preventive effect on traumatic fractures in the elderly. Therefore, reasonable calcium supplementation in patients with rib fractures may facilitate fracture healing. Alkaline phosphatase is expressed on the surface of osteoblasts, promoting the deposition of calcium phosphate, a crucial step in forming new bone tissue. Typically, during the early and middle stages of fracture healing, serum alkaline phosphatase levels significantly increase, reflecting osteoblast activity and the bone formation process. In Taniguchi et al’s study,[21] alkaline phosphatase levels were significantly altered during fracture healing. This study suggests that controlling COPD and regulating blood calcium and alkaline phosphatase levels are beneficial for rib fracture healing.

Pain is a primary symptom of rib fractures, and inadequate pain relief can lead to painful hypoventilation and pneumonia. Beyond humanitarian relief from suffering, the goal of analgesia is to achieve pain-free breathing rehabilitation to prevent secondary pulmonary complications while minimizing systemic side effects. Investigating the factors influencing pain is crucial for patient treatment. This study found that diabetes (P = .001), CHD (P = .014), and alkaline phosphatase (P < .001) are independent risk factors for long-duration pain in conservative treatment of rib fractures. Diabetes slows bone healing, and vascular complications associated with diabetes can result in inadequate blood supply to the fracture site, affecting the nutrition and oxygen supply to bone cells and surrounding tissues, thereby delaying healing and exacerbating pain. Nyman et al’s study[22] found that the longer the duration of diabetes, the greater the bone fragility, leading to long-duration pain and dysfunction after fractures. Patients with CHD often require long-term use of anticoagulant or antiplatelet medications, which may affect the fracture healing process and extend pain duration. Alkaline phosphatase may influence the duration of pain by affecting the fracture healing progress. Therefore, this study suggests that controlling primary diseases such as diabetes and CHD in patients can help manage pain and provide an additional treatment approach alongside symptomatic pain relief.

In this study, although age was not identified as a risk factor for poor healing and long-duration pain in rib fractures, the average age of patients in the poor healing group and long-duration pain group was significantly higher than that in the good healing group and short-duration pain group. Butts et al[23] even utilized patients’ age, the number of rib fractures, significant cardiopulmonary comorbidities, and pulmonary function to determine hospital admission arrangements. This indicates that patient age is particularly important in the diagnosis and treatment of rib fractures. However, in this study, the number of rib fractures did not show a statistical difference between the poor healing and good healing groups, or between the long-duration pain and short-duration pain groups. This contrasts with previous studies, where the number of rib fractures had a significant impact on healing outcomes and pain duration.[24–28] This discrepancy may be related to the grouping in this study, as previous studies often compared patients with 3 or more rib fractures to those with fewer than 3, whereas this study compared single rib fractures to those with 2 or more rib fractures.

In this study, the incidence of pulmonary-related complications in conservative treatment of rib fractures was as high as 59.06%. We do not consider this an exaggerated figure. Immobilization required in conservative treatment can easily lead to pulmonary inflammation. Additionally, the trauma of rib fractures can cause pneumothorax, hemothorax, and lung contusions. However, most pulmonary-related complications were not severe, and symptomatic treatments yielded good outcomes.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, the sample size was relatively small, which may introduce some bias in the analysis. Additionally, since the study was conducted at a single center, the lack of multicenter data may limit the generalizability of the results. The study also did not distinguish between the anatomical locations of the fractures, which could be an important factor affecting fracture healing and the duration of pain.[29,30]

5. Conclusion

COPD, blood calcium, and alkaline phosphatase are independent risk factors for poor healing in conservative treatment of rib fractures. Diabetes, CHD, and alkaline phosphatase are independent risk factors for long-duration pain in conservative treatment of rib fractures. The exploration of the risk factors of poor healing and prolonged pain in conservative treatment of rib fracture can guide the diagnosis and treatment of clinicians to a certain extent.

Correction to “Human iPSC‐derived GABAergic interneuron transplantation restores circuit balance and cognitive function in an Alzheimer's disease model”
Published: 2026

Zhang X, Wang G, Chen H, et al. Human iPSC‐derived GABAergic interneuron transplantation restores circuit balance and cognitive function in an Alzheimer's disease model. Alzheimer's Dement. 2026;22(4):e71378. doi:10.1002/alz.71378.

1. In Section 2.2, the original manuscript includes inaccurate statements regarding the animal housing and the reference to “Neurona Therapeutics IACUC protocol.” These statements do not reflect the actual conduct of the study.

The text “All efficacy studies were conducted as non‐glucagon‐like peptide research in accordance with Neurona Therapeutics IACUC protocol for Alzheimer's disease research, following ARRIVE guidelines. …… All mice were housed in the animal facility of Neurona Therapeutics…….” was not correct.

The whole paragraph should have read: “Animal experiments complied with the National Institutes of Health (NIH) Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Peking Union Medical College (Approval No. ACUC‐A01‐2021‐050). Female 5xFAD transgenic mice (Stock No. 008730; RRID:MMRRC_034848‐JAX) and their wild‐type (WT) C57BL/6 littermates were obtained from The Jackson Laboratory (Bar Harbor, ME, USA). Female mice were selected for this study due to their more consistent cognitive behavioral phenotypes in this model. A total of 92 female mice (34 WT and 58 5xFAD) were utilized. All treatments were initiated when the mice reached 10 months of age. To ensure experimental reproducibility and maximize data integration, the study was conducted across three independent cohorts of mice, with tissues and animals systematically allocated to complementary downstream assays: behavioral assessments and patch‐clamp recordings were conducted on Cohorts 1 and 3, Western blot analysis was performed on Cohort 2, and histological and immunofluorescence staining was performed across all three cohorts (using perfused brains).The specific sample sizes (n) for each individual assay are indicated in the corresponding figure legends. Upon arrival, each mouse was uniquely identified using ear tags. Mice were housed at an approved third‐party animal facility (Viewsolid Biotechnology, Beijing, China) under standard pathogen‐free, temperature and humidity conditions and maintained on 12‐hour light/dark cycle. Mice had ad libitum access to autoclaved bottled water and standard chow, and were housed in disposable cages with irradiated Alpha‐Dri bedding. To prevent immune rejection, all mice across the three experimental groups received daily subcutaneous injections of cyclosporine A (CsA; 10 mg/kg; MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA) starting on the day of transplantation. All behavioral assessments and histological analyses were performed by investigators blinded to the experimental groupings.”

2. In Section 2.3, the text “The final cell density for injection was 1,000,000 cells/mL” is incorrect. It should read: “The final cell density for injection was 1 × 10^8 cells/mL.”

The above corrections do not affect the overall experimental approach.

We apologize for this error.

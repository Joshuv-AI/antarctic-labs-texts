WDFY4 gene polymorphisms (rs11101565 and rs2671702) are not associated with breast cancer risk in Bangladeshi women: a case-control study
Authors: Tahmina Akter, Md. Abdul Aziz, Arman Bin Shahid, Khaleda Khanom Keya, Anamika Datta, Mohammad Safiqul Islam, Md. Shahid Sarwar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesBreast cancer is a complex malignancy influenced by both genetic and environmental mechanisms. The WDFY4 gene, predominantly expressed in immune tissues, plays a role in immune regulation. However, its application in relation to breast cancer risk among the Bangladeshi women has not been explored. The proposed case-control study was designed to determine the relationship between the susceptibility to breast cancer and the two WDFY4 polymorphisms, rs11101565 and rs2671702 and the association was evaluated by the T-ARMS-PCR method.ResultsFor rs11101565, the CA heterozygous genotype conferred a 1.36-fold increased risk compared to the CC wild-type genotype, though this association did not reach statistical significance (p > 0.05). Dominant and recessive models suggested elevated risk among AA homozygotes, while the overdominant model revealed a modest correlation; however, none achieved statistical significance. For rs2671702, the GA heterozygous genotype was associated with increased risk relative to GG, whereas the AA genotype exhibited a protective trend, yet all associations remained non-significant. Inconsistent risk patterns were observed across dominant and recessive models, while the minor allele A and overdominant model demonstrated stronger but statistically non-significant associations. Collectively, these findings indicate that WDFY4 polymorphisms (rs11101565 and rs2671702) do not significantly influence breast cancer susceptibility among Bangladeshi women.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-026-07819-1.

Introduction

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death globally, accounting for nearly one in six fatalities each year. In 2020 alone, an estimated 19 million new cancer cases were diagnosed, and 10 million individuals succumbed to the disease [1, 2]. Among women, breast cancer represents the most frequently diagnosed malignancy and stands as the second leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1, 2]. Each year, breast cancer claims the lives of 2.1 million women worldwide. In 2018, approximately 600,000 women died from breast cancer, accounting for nearly 15% of all cancer deaths among women. The incidence of breast cancer continues to rise across all global regions. The trend increases with disproportionately higher rates in more developed countries [3, 4].

Although certain germline variants have been associated with increased breast cancer susceptibility, the prognostic significance of hereditary variants remains less understood [5]. The latest genome-wide association studies (GWAS) were able to find several single nucleotides polymorphisms (SNPs) that contribute to an accelerated risk of breast cancer, especially in postmenopausal individuals [6, 7]. These investigations have demonstrated that SNPs play a substantial role in genetic predisposition to breast cancer across diverse global populations [8].

The WDFY4 gene, located on chromosome 10q11.23, belongs to the WDFY family and encodes a large protein containing functional domains including WD40 and BEACH. This gene exhibits prominent expression in immune tissues, including the thymus, lymph nodes, spleen, and tonsils. Although the precise biological function of WDFY4 remains to be fully elucidated, its structural homology with WDFY3—a known autophagy-related protein—suggests potential involvement in modulating autophagic processes [9, 10]. Furthermore, specific WDFY4 isoforms have been demonstrated to enhance NF-κB signaling, and a splicing variant of WDFY4 has been linked to increased risk of clinically amyopathic dermatomyositis (CADM). While the biological function of WDFY4 is not yet characterized, its association with inflammatory signaling and MDA5-induced apoptosis suggests a role in the autoimmune response [11].

A genome-wide association study conducted among Japanese women with HPV-associated cervical cancer failed to identify a significant correlation between WDFY4 polymorphisms (rs11101565 and rs2671702) and cervical cancer susceptibility [12]. However, no study to date has examined the potential involvement of these variants in other malignancies, including breast cancer, across any ethnic population. Therefore, the present case-control study was designed to investigate the association between WDFY4 rs11101565 and rs2671702 polymorphisms and breast cancer susceptibility among Bangladeshi women.

Methods

Study settings

In this study, 110 breast cancer patients were recruited as cases from the National Institute of Cancer Research and Hospital, along with 108 healthy individuals who attended the same hospital during the selection period. Controls were selected to be sex-matched and within a comparable age range to the cases, though complete age matching was not achievable due to practical constraints. Blood samples collected from participants at the National Institute of Cancer Research and Hospital were immediately placed in sterile tubes and transported to the Laboratory of Pharmacogenomics and Molecular Biology at Noakhali Science and Technology University. During transportation, samples were kept in insulated corksheet boxes with ice packs to maintain a controlled temperature (approximately 4 °C). Upon arrival, samples were stored at -20 °C to preserve DNA integrity until DNA extraction. DNA was extracted using the Favorgen Kit. The genotyping of WDFY4 rs11101565 and WDFY4 rs2671702 gene polymorphisms was conducted using the tetra-primer amplification refractory mutation system-polymerase chain reaction (T-ARMS-PCR) technique at the Laboratory of Pharmacogenomics and Molecular Biology, Department of Pharmacy, Noakhali Science and Technology University, Noakhali, Bangladesh. The mentioned case-control study’s reporting complies with STROBE criteria [13] and the entire process were conducted in compliance with the Helsinki Principles.

Two groups of women participated in the study. For cases, the inclusion criteria comprised women with confirmed primary breast cancer, who were recruited at the National Institute of Cancer Research and Hospital during the study period. Exclusion criteria for cases included patients with a history of any other malignancy, recurrent breast cancer, prior chemotherapy or radiotherapy before sample collection, or incomplete clinical data. For controls, inclusion criteria required healthy women with no personal or family history of breast cancer or any other malignancy, who were age-matched within a comparable range to cases and recruited from among visitors attending the same hospital during the same period. Controls with any chronic illness, autoimmune disorders, or history of any malignancy were excluded from participation.

Following these criteria, 110 breast cancer patients (ages 26 to 72, mean age 48.11 ± 3.57 years) were employed as cases, along with 108 healthy volunteers, aged 23 to 67(mean age 37.97 ± 2.84 years), were chosen as controls. The ultimate goal of this study was to evaluate the linkage between the WDFY4 rs11101565 and WDFY4 rs2671702 gene polymorphisms with the susceptibility to breast cancer. The research received ethical clearance from the National Institute of Cancer Research and Hospital (NICRH), Dhaka, Bangladesh, with the approval number NICRH/Ethics/2019/446.

Genomic DNA extraction

Genomic DNA was effectively acquired from 110 women suffering from breast cancer and 108 control participants applying the extraction mini kit of Favorprep DNA, following the procedure previously described [14]. The concentration and purity of the extracted DNA were assessed using a Genova Nano microvolume spectrophotometer (Jenway). For each sample, 2 µL of DNA solution was analyzed. DNA concentration was measured in microgram per milliliter based on absorbance at 260 nm (A260). The DNA authenticity was assessed by measuring the ratio of absorbance at 260 nm to 280 nm. All samples exhibited A260/A280 ratios between 1.7 and 1.9, signifying high-quality DNA. The average concentration of the DNA samples ranged from 50 to 70 µg/ml. After conducting the tetra-primer ARMS-PCR [15] and 1% gel electrophoresis, the subsequent results were obtained. Statistical calculation

The χ2-test and a two-sided unpaired t-test were employed for comparing the distribution of sociodemographic and clinicopathological factors between breast cancer sufferers and those who are healthy individuals. The analyses were conducted using the statistical software packages SPSS (version 25.0) and MedCalc (version 9.0). The odds ratios (ORs) along with their associated 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were determined utilizing the χ2-test. The frequencies of the genotypes were evaluated for divergence from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) via the χ2-test. A two tailed significance level of p < 0.05 was considered statistically noteworthy for all these analyses.

Results

Characteristics of the cases and controls

Supplementary Table 2 summarizes the demographic distributions among the 110 breast cancer patients and 108 healthy participants. Age distribution analysis revealed that among patients, 37.27% were below 45 years, 50.91% were between 45 and 60 years, and 11.82% were above 60 years. In contrast, controls comprised 50.93% below 45 years, 39.81% between 45 and 60 years, and 9.26% above 60 years. The age range for patients was 26 to 72 years (mean 48.11 ± 3.57 years), while controls ranged from 23 to 67 years (mean 37.97 ± 2.84 years). Mean BMI was considerably higher in the patient group (27.59 ± 1.55 kg/m²) compared to controls (21.62 ± 1.36 kg/m²). Marriage rates were elevated in both cases (92%) and controls (85%).

Regarding clinical characteristics, invasive ductal carcinoma was the predominant cancer type, affecting 53.33% of patients, followed by infiltrating ductal carcinoma in 20% of cases. Grade II breast cancer was most frequently observed (58.54%). Diagnostic approaches included biopsy (67.78%), USG (66.67%), X-ray (31.11%), FNAC (27.78%), CT scan (18.89%), and echocardiogram (18.89%). Prior treatments comprised surgery (45.68%), chemotherapy (43.21%), and radiotherapy (22.22%). Among patients receiving chemotherapy, 57.45% underwent 4–8 cycles, while 36.17% completed 1–3 cycles. Hormone receptor analysis demonstrated ER positivity in approximately 40% of patients and ER negativity in 52%. PR positivity was observed in 56%, with PR negativity in 52%. HER2 status was negative in 53.33% and positive in 32% of patients. Triple-negative breast cancer constituted a minor proportion (2.67%). The most frequently prescribed chemotherapeutic agents were cyclophosphamide (61.82%), doxorubicin (50.91%), and paclitaxel (45.45%), with additional medications detailed in supplementary Table 2.

Distribution of genotypes and association of rs11101565 with breast cancer

The genotypic distribution of WDFY4 rs11101565 adhered to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium in both study groups (patients: χ² = 0.114, p = 0.735; controls: χ² = 1.33, p = 0.248). Examination of the relationship between rs11101565 genotypes and breast cancer susceptibility depicted in Table 3 revealed several observations. Compared to the CC wild-type genotype, individuals carrying the CA heterozygous genotype demonstrated a 1.36-fold elevated risk (Additive model 1: OR = 1.36, 95% CI = 0.59–2.89, p = 0.510). Similarly, those with the AA homozygous variant exhibited a 1.23-fold increased risk relative to CC carriers (Additive model 2: OR = 1.23, 95% CI = 0.55–2.73, p = 0.616). Neither association reached statistical significance.

Under the dominant model (CA + AA vs. CC), variant allele carriers displayed a 1.27-fold higher risk compared to CC homozygotes (OR = 1.27, 95% CI = 0.60–2.66, p = 0.533). The recessive model comparing AA versus CC + CA genotypes yielded a minimal risk increase (OR = 1.01, 95% CI = 0.59–1.72, p = 0.982). Overdominant model analysis (CA vs. CC + AA) indicated a 1.12-fold risk elevation (OR = 1.12, 95% CI = 0.66–1.92, p = 0.668). Allelic comparison showed that the minor A allele conferred a 1.07-fold increased risk relative to the major C allele (OR = 1.07, 95% CI = 0.72–1.58, p = 0.730). Notably, all genetic models failed to demonstrate statistically significant associations with breast cancer risk.

Distribution of genotypes and association of rs2671702 with breast cancer

For WDFY4 rs2671702, genotype frequencies deviated significantly from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium in both patients (χ² = 47.62, p = 0.00) and controls (χ² = 33.91, p = 0.00). The GA heterozygous genotype was markedly prevalent, comprising 82.73% of cases and 77.78% of controls.

Association analysis for rs2671702 shown in Table 4 revealed that relative to GG wild-type homozygotes, GA heterozygotes demonstrated a 1.35-fold elevated risk (Additive model 1: OR = 1.35, 95% CI = 0.60–3.06, p = 0.466). Conversely, AA homozygous individuals exhibited a slightly reduced risk compared to GG carriers (Additive model 2: OR = 0.97, 95% CI = 0.28–3.38, p = 0.965). Neither finding achieved statistical significance.

Under the dominant model, individuals carrying at least one A allele (GA + AA) showed a 1.32-fold increased risk versus GG homozygotes (OR = 1.32, 95% CI = 0.58–2.96, p = 0.505). The recessive model comparing AA against GG + GA genotypes indicated a protective trend (OR = 0.75, 95% CI = 0.27–2.08, p = 0.578). Overdominant model analysis revealed that GA heterozygotes faced a 1.37-fold higher risk compared to individuals with either homozygous genotype (GG + AA) (OR = 1.37, 95% CI = 0.70–2.68, p = 0.359). Allelic comparison demonstrated that the minor A allele was associated with a marginal 1.02-fold increased risk relative to the major G allele (OR = 1.02, 95% CI = 0.70–1.49, p = 0.916). As with rs11101565, all association tests for rs2671702 yielded non-significant p-values across every genetic model examined.

Discussion

Breast cancer remains one of the most formidable oncological challenges globally, representing a significant proportion of the disease burden among women and posing a direct threat to their health and well-being [16, 17]. Despite substantial reductions in breast cancer mortality over the past three decades, its incidence has paradoxically doubled during the same period [18, 19]. The contribution of genetic factors to breast cancer pathogenesis has garnered considerable research attention, leading to the identification of numerous polymorphisms associated with disease susceptibility [20, 21]. While genome-wide association analysis has examined the connection between WDFY4 polymorphisms and the susceptibility of cervical cancer, no previous investigation has explored the correlation between WDFY4 polymorphisms and their contribution in the advancement of breast cancer, particularly within the context of the Bangladeshi demographic. Thus, our research’s purpose was to figure out to reveal the WDFY4 rs11101565 and rs2671702 polymorphisms and Bangladeshi women’s probability of getting breast cancer are correlated in any way.

Our research focused on examining the relationship between the WDFY4 polymorphisms rs11101565 and rs2671702 and an accelerated likelihood of getting cancer of breast among women in the country of Bangladesh. Analysis of rs11101565 revealed that the CA heterozygous genotype was associated with a 1.36-fold elevated risk relative to CC homozygotes, and the AA homozygous genotype exhibited a 1.23-fold increased risk. Both findings failed to achieve statistical significance. Combining CA and AA genotypes in the dominant model showed a 1.27-fold increased risk, but again, it was not statistically significant. The AA genotype within the recessive model, exhibited a 1.01-fold increased risk, and the over dominant model highlighted a 1.12-fold greater risk, but both associations were not statistically significant. When comparing the minor allele, A to the major allele C in the allele model, there was a 1.07-fold increased association with the chance of breast cancer, however, the connection lacked statistical significance. (A vs. C). Previous study on cervical cancer found no meaningful correlation between WDFY4 polymorphisms and the disease [12].

For rs2671702, the GA heterozygous genotype was associated with a 1.35-fold increased risk, whereas the AA homozygous genotype exhibited a marginally reduced risk (OR = 0.95); however, neither association achieved statistical significance. The combined GA + AA genotype in the dominant model showed a 1.32-fold increased risk, which was not statistically significant. The genotype AA within the recessive model displayed a 0.75-fold decreased risk, and the overdominant model indicated a 1.37-fold greater threat, but both associations were not statistically significant. Comparing the minor allele A to the major allele G in the allele model resulted in a 1.02-fold increased association with breast cancer susceptibility, which does not meet the criteria for statistical significance. Similar to the findings for rs11101565, previous study on cervical cancer did not discover any noteworthy connection with these WDFY4 polymorphisms [12].

A notable observation in this study is the significant deviation from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium for rs2671702 in the control group (p = 0.00), along with an unusually high heterozygote frequency. Given the stringent quality control measures employed during T-ARMS-PCR genotyping, a systematic genotyping error is less likely. The more probable explanation in this specific study context is the relatively small sample size (n = 108 for controls), which makes the HWE χ2-test more sensitive to chance variations in genotype frequencies. Nevertheless, these findings are unusual, and independent validation using alternative genotyping methods in larger cohorts is recommended.

On Another note, both polymorphisms investigated in this study, rs11101565 and rs2671702, are intronic variants with no currently known functional role. Although intronic SNPs may potentially influence gene expression, experimental evidence for these specific variants is lacking. Given the non-significant findings in our study, functional characterization was beyond the scope of this association investigation. However, if larger studies confirm a potential role for these variants in breast cancer susceptibility, future functional studies would be warranted.

Several methodological considerations should be noted when interpreting our findings. First, the modest sample size (110 cases, 108 controls) contributed to wide confidence intervals and increased the risk of Type II error. While adequately powered for odds ratios of 2.0 or greater, our sample size provides limited power to detect smaller effect sizes in the range observed in this study (OR 1.2–1.4). Thus, modest genetic associations cannot be ruled out. Second, despite recruiting controls within a comparable age range to cases, significant age imbalance remained. Significant BMI difference was also observed, which may contribute to residual confounding. Consequently, the independent effect of WDFY4 genotypes could not be fully isolated from other risk factors.

Despite these limitations, this study represents the first investigation of WDFY4 polymorphisms in breast cancer globally and provides valuable preliminary evidence for the Bangladeshi population. Based on our comprehensive analysis, it can be concluded that rs11101565 shows no significant association with breast cancer risk. For rs2671702, while no significant association was found, results require cautious interpretation due to HWE deviation in controls and the unusual heterozygote frequency observed. Future studies with larger cohorts and comprehensive confounder data are needed to validate these preliminary findings.

Conclusion

Based on our comprehensive analysis of WDFY4 gene polymorphisms, it can be concluded that neither rs11101565 nor rs2671702 possess a statistically noteworthy impact on the advancement of breast carcinogenesis among Bangladeshi women. Importantly, this study examines the genetic relationship between WDFY4 polymorphisms and breast carcinogenesis risk, which is the first of its sort in the world., and it reveals no significant association. However, further investigations are recommended to provide insight into the possible role of WDFY4 genetic variants in carcinogenesis, particularly concerning breast cancer probability and prognosis within the Bangladeshi demographic.

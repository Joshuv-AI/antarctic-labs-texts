Doubly robust outlier-resistant inference on causal treatment effect Journal: Computational Statistics and Data Analysis
Juhyun Park, Byeonghee Lee, Saebom Jeon, Joonsung Kang

Abstract
Outliers can severely distort causal effect estimation in observational studies, yet this issue has received limited attention in the literature. Their influence is especially pronounced in small sample sizes, where detecting and removing outliers becomes increasingly difficult. Therefore, it is essential to estimate treatment effects robustly without excluding these influential data points. To address this, we propose a doubly robust point estimator for the average treatment effect under a contaminated model that includes outliers. Robustness in outcome regression is achieved through a robust estimating equation, while covariate balancing propensity scores (CBPS) ensure resilience in propensity score modeling. To prevent model overfitting due to the inclusion of numerous parameters, we incorporate variable selection. All these components are unified under a penalized empirical likelihood framework. For confidence interval estimation, most existing approaches rely on asymptotic properties, which may be unreliable in finite samples. We derive an optimal finite-sample confidence interval for the average treatment effect using our proposed estimating equation, ensuring that the interval bounds remain unaffected by outliers. Through simulations and a real-world application involving hypertension data with outliers, we demonstrate that our method consistently outperforms existing approaches in both accuracy and robustness.



1
Introduction

Robust causal inference for the average treatment effect (ATE) in observational studies remains an active area of research, particularly due to complications arising from outliers and small sample sizes in high-dimensional biomedical settings. Harada and Fujisawa (2021) recently introduced an outlier-resistant ATE estimator using robust loss functions, which significantly improves point estimation stability. However, their method did not extend to inferential validity, as no procedure was proposed for constructing confidence intervals. Simultaneously, mainstream approaches such as Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) (Liang and Zeger, 1986) are known to be vulnerable to data contamination and exhibit poor performance in finite samples (Huber and Ronchetti, 2009).
To address these limitations, we propose a novel doubly robust estimator that integrates Covariate Balancing Propensity Score (CBPS) techniques (Imai and Ratkovic, 2014) with robust outcome regression. Our framework is grounded in the robust estimating equation methodology developed by Hu and Lachin (2001), which offers notable resilience to outliers and misspecification. The combined approach enhances efficiency while preserving consistency under flexible modeling assumptions, yielding inference procedures that are particularly suited for small-sample regimes.
These challenges become further amplified when the goal shifts to estimating heterogeneous treatment effects (HTEs), especially within high-dimensional, low-sample-size (HDLSS) medical datasets. In such settings, the curse of dimensionality exacerbates issues related to model instability, poor covariate balance, and unreliable confidence interval estimation (Wager and Athey, 2018; Fan et al., 2020). As many observational datasets in precision medicine are inherently HDLSS, traditional asymptotic-based inferential tools often fail to provide credible uncertainty quantification.
Our proposed methodology directly addresses this by abandoning strong reliance on asymptotic theory. Instead, it leverages design-based techniques and robust modeling to operate under minimal distributional assumptions. This makes the estimator highly adaptable to irregular data structures and contaminated environments, enabling valid causal inference in realistic biomedical contexts.
The remainder of this paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 introduces the estimation framework, including CBPS calibration, robust regression formulation, and the doubly robust estimator. It also describes the construction of finite-sample confidence intervals using bootstrap and robust variance estimation. Section 3 presents simulation results evaluating small-sample performance. Section 4 showcases a real-data application involving the Golub gene expression dataset, a canonical example of high-dimensional data with outliers. Section 5 summarizes findings and proposes avenues for future research.

2.1.1
Covariate Balancing Propensity Score (CBPS)

The CBPS estimator

Generalized estimation proceeds by minimizing the moment discrepancy:

\bar{\mathbf{g}}_{\bm{\beta}}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{X})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{\bm{\beta}}(T_{i},\mathbf{X}_{i})=0,

g_{\bm{\beta}}(T_{i},\mathbf{X}_{i})=\left(\frac{T_{i}}{\pi_{\bm{\beta}}(\mathbf{X}_{i})}-\frac{1-T_{i}}{1-\pi_{\bm{\beta}}(\mathbf{X}_{i})}\right)f(\mathbf{X}_{i}),

Central Limit Theorem Derivation
Using M-estimation theory and empirical process results, we show:

\sqrt{n}(\hat{\tau}_{DR}-\tau)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})

where
\sigma^{2}
is the asymptotic variance, which can be estimated as:

\hat{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\psi_{i}-\hat{\tau}_{DR}\right)^{2}

with influence function
\psi_{i}=\frac{T_{i}(Y_{i}-m_{1}(\mathbf{X}_{i}))}{\pi(\mathbf{X}_{i})}+m_{1}(\mathbf{X}_{i})-\frac{(1-T_{i})(Y_{i}-m_{0}(\mathbf{X}_{i}))}{1-\pi(\mathbf{X}_{i})}-m_{0}(\mathbf{X}_{i})

If either nuisance model is correctly specified, the estimator remains consistent and asymptotically normal—this is the heart of its “double robustness.

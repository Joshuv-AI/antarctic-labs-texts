Proto-exact and parabelian categories
Sergey Mozgovoy

Abstract
. Proto-exact and parabelian categories serve as non-additive analogues of exact and quasi-abelian categories, respectively. They give rise to algebraic K K -theory and Hall algebras similarly to the additive setting. We show that every parabelian category admits a canonical proto-exact structure and we study several classes of parabelian categories, including categories of normed and Euclidean vector spaces, pointed closure spaces and pointed matroids, Hermitian vector bundles over rings of integers. We also examine finitary algebraic categories arising in Arakelov geometry and provide a criterion for determining when such a category is parabelian. In particular, we prove that the categories of pointed convex spaces and absolutely convex spaces are parabelian.



Introduction

Proto-exact categories
[
7
]
are a non-additive analogue of Quillen exact categories

[
23
,
15
]
.
Essentially, a proto-exact category is a pointed category equipped with a class of short exact sequences (called admissible)
satisfying certain properties.
As in the additive setting, one can associate with a proto-exact category the Waldhausen
S
-construction,
which forms a 2-Segal simplicial groupoid.
This object serves as a foundation for constructing algebraic

K
-theory as well as various types of Hall algebras associated with the category
[
7
]
.

Examples of (non-additive) proto-exact categories include
categories of quiver representations and semigroup representations in
\operatorname{Set}_{*}

[
27
,
28
]
,
category of Euclidean vector spaces
with morphisms of norm
\leq 1

and various categories arising in Arakelov geometry

[
1
,
7
]
,
category of pointed matroids
[
8
]
,
categories of modules over semirings
[
13
]
.
One can verify that all of the above proto-exact structures are canonical and consist of all short exact sequences.
This leads us to the notion of a parabelian
category, which generalizes both quasi-abelian
[
24
]
and proto-abelian
[
1
,
6
]
categories.

Recall that an additive category with kernels and cokernels
is called quasi-abelian
[
24
]

if the class of its strict epimorphisms (the cokernels) is closed under pullbacks
and the class of its strict monomorphisms (the kernels) is closed under pushouts.
Examples of (non-abelian) quasi-abelian categories include categories of filtered objects in an abelian category,
locally free sheaves over a smooth curve,
finitely generated free abelian groups (or finitely generated projective modules over a Dedekind domain).
A quasi-abelian category, when equipped with the class of all short exact sequences, forms an exact category, as shown in
[
24
]
.
In the non-additive setting, we define parabelian categories in a similar manner and prove that a parabelian category,
when equipped with the class of all short exact sequences, is proto-exact.
Note that the various definitions of proto-abelian categories
[
1
,
6
]
are strictly stronger than that of a parabelian category.
For instance, the proto-abelian categories in
[
1
]
do not include pointed sets, while those in
[
6
]
exclude Euclidean vector spaces.

In this paper, we introduce a set of axioms for proto-exact categories that mirror those of exact categories
[
23
,
15
]
.
We further prove that this formulation is equivalent to the existing definition of a proto-exact category given in
[
7
]
.
Then, to establish that every parabelian category is proto-exact, it suffices to show that the classes of strict monomorphisms and epimorphisms are closed under composition.
This property is well known in the additive setting
[
24
]
.

We will verify that various classes of categories are parabelian.
For example, we will show that the categories of normed vector spaces
§
2.2

and pointed closure spaces §
2.4
are parabelian.
The categories of Euclidean vector spaces and pointed matroids appear as their full subcategories closed under taking kernels and cokernels, hence are also parabelian.

We will also study a broad class of finitary algebraic categories,
defined as categories of algebras over finitary monads §
3.1
.
Such categories naturally appear in universal algebra
[
11
]

and in the formal approach to Arakelov geometry
[
5
]
.
Although not all finitary algebraic categories are parabelian (for instance, the category of groups fails to be parabelian), we will establish a criterion to determine when this is the case.

Given a normed semiring
K
, we will define the valuation monad
\bar{}\mathcal{O}_{K}
associated with the (weak) partial semiring
\mathcal{O}_{K}\mathchar 61\relax\left\{x\in K\,\middle|\,\left\lvert x\right\rvert\leq 1\right\}

and define the category of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules
\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_{K})
to be the category of algebras over the monad
\bar{}\mathcal{O}_{K}
§
3.4
.
Such categories include categories of

(1)

pointed sets (modules over
\mathbb{F}_{1}\mathchar 61\relax\left\{0,1\right\}\subset\mathbb{N}
),

(2)

monoid representations in pointed sets,

(3)

commutative monoids (modules over

(4)

(5)

convex spaces (modules over

\mathbb{Z}_{\infty}\mathchar 61\relax[-1,1]\subset\mathbb{R}

1.

1.3.
Parabelian categories

Definition 1.9
.

A category with kernels and cokernels is called parabelian if

(1)

Strict epimorphisms are closed under pullbacks along strict monomorphisms.

(2)

Strict monomorphisms are closed under pushouts along strict epimorphisms.

Note that the above pullbacks and pushouts exist automatically by Lemma

Remark 1.11

Remark 1.12

(Relation to quasi-abelian categories)

(Relation to proto-abelian categories)
.

There are several distinct notions of proto-abelian categories
in the literature
[
1
,
6
]
.
A proto-abelian category defined by André
[
1
]
is a parabelian category with the additional requirement that a morphism
f
is mono
\iff

\ker(f)\mathchar 61\relax 0
and epi
\iff

\operatorname{coker}(f)\mathchar 61\relax 0
.
For example, the category
\operatorname{Set}_{*}
of pointed sets (see §
2.1
) is not proto-abelian in this sense, since there exist non-monic morphisms
f
in
\operatorname{Set}_{*}
with
\operatorname{Ker}(f)\mathchar 61\relax f^{-1}(0)\mathchar 61\relax\left\{0\right\}
.
A proto-abelian category defined by Dyckerhoff
[
6
]
is a parabelian category with the additional requirement that all mono and epimorphisms are strict (this formulation is equivalent to the one in
[
6
]
if the category has kernels and cokernels).
For example, if
\mathcal{C}
is a parabelian category such that the class of its strict morphisms is closed under composition (such as
\mathcal{C}\mathchar 61\relax\operatorname{Set}_{*}
), then its subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\mathsf{s}}
consisting of all strict morphisms is proto-abelian in this sense.
However, in general, the class of strict morphisms in a parabelian category need not be closed under composition (for example, this happens in parabelian categories of normed and Euclidean spaces §
2.2
).

Lemma 1.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a parabelian category and
\mathcal{I}
be a small category.

The kernels and cokernels in

By Lemma

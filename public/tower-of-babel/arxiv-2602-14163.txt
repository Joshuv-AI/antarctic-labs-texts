On the closed neighborhood ideal of the square of the path graph
Anda Olteanu and Oana Olteanu

Abstract
. We consider the closed neighborhood ideal of square of the path graph and study its invariants. We compute the height, the projective dimension and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity. We prove that these ideals are sequentially Cohen–Macaulay and characterize when they are Cohen–Macaulay. Keywords : Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, projective dimension, simplicial complex MSC : Primary 13D05 Secondary 13F55



Introduction

Squarefree monomial ideals are a central theme of research in both commutative algebra and algebraic combinatorics. It is common to express invariants of squarefree monomial ideals in terms of some combinatorial structures that can be attached to them (simplicial complexes, hypergraphs or graphs).

In 2020, Sharifan and Moradi defined the notion of closed neighborhood ideal of a graph as being the squarefree monomial ideal whose generators are given by the closed neighborhoods of the vertices
[
12
]
. They studied several invariants such as the projective dimension, the height, and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity and relate them to combinatorial invariants such as the domination number or the matching number. Recently, they continued their study with chordal graphs
[
8
]
. Closed neighborhood ideals of graphs have been studied also by Joseph, Roy, and Singh studied the minimal free resolution in the framework of the Barile–Macchia resolutions
[
6
]
. They proved that for trees, the Barile–Macchia resolution is minimal. These ideals have been studied also for particular classes of graphs. For instance, Nasernejad, Bandari, and Roberts proved that the closed neighborhood ideals of complete bipartite graphs are normal, therefore they satisfy the (strong) persistence property
[
10
]
. Moreover, Chakraborty, Joseph, Roy, and Singh
[
3
]
considered the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of closed neighborhood ideals of a forest
G
and obtained that it is equal to the matching number of
G
, fact that was conjectured by Sharifan and Moradi
[
12
, Conjecture 2.11]
.

In this paper we pay attention to the closed neighborhood ideal of the square of path graph,
NI(P_{n}^{2})
. We determine the height, the bigheight, the projective dimension, and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity, and we characterize all closed neighborhood ideals of
P_{n}^{2}
which are Cohen–Macaulay. Moreover, if we assume that
NI(P_{n}^{2})\subseteq S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we prove that
S/NI(P_{n}^{2})
is sequentially Cohen–Macaulay for
n\geq 7
. A similar result was obtained by Sharifan and Moradi for complete
r
-partite graphs
[
12
, Theorem 2.10 ((vii))]
. We also pay attention to the simplicial complex
\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}]
whose facet ideal is
NI(P_{n}^{2})
and prove that it is shellable. Due to its nice structure, we are able to compute its
f
-vector and the reduced Euler characteristic.

The paper is structured as follows: the first section is devoted to the study of algebraic invariants for the closed neighborhood ideal such as the height (Proposition
1.2
), the projective dimension, and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity (Theorem
1.11
). As a consequence, we prove that, for closed neighborhood ideals of
P_{n}^{2}
, the depth and the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity are equal Corollary
1.12
. We also give a complete characterization of all the closed neighborhood ideals of
P_{n}^{2}
which are Cohen–Macaulay (Corollary
1.13
).
In the second section we study closed neighborhood ideals from the combinatorial point of view, by relating them to simplicial complexes. We consider the simplicial complex
\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}]
such that the closed neighborhood ideal of
P_{n}^{2}
is the facet ideal of
\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}]
. We prove that the simplicial complex is shellable (Proposition
2.2
) and it has the free vertex property (Proposition
2.3
). This allows us to conclude that
S/NI(P_{n}^{2})
is sequentially Cohen–Macaulay (Theorem
2.6
). Finally, we compute the
f
-vector of
\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}]
(Proposition
2.10
) and, as a consequence, we obtain the reduced Euler characteristic.

For a given finite simple graph

,
the neighborhood
of a vertex

we denote by

For a finite, simple graph

be the polynomial ring over a field

NI(G)=\left(\mathbf{x}_{N[x_{i}]}:x_{i}\in V(G)\right).

For instance, taking into account Figure

Since the shape of the closed neighborhood ideal depends on

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
3\leq n\leq 6
be an integer and
NI(P_{n}^{2})
be the closed neighborhood ideal. The following hold:

(i)

\operatorname{ht}(NI(P_{3}^{2})=1
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/NI(P_{3}^{2}))=2
, and
\operatorname{pd}(S/NI(P_{3}))^{2}=1
.

(ii)

\operatorname{ht}(NI(P_{4}^{2})=1
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/NI(P_{4}^{2}))=2
, and
\operatorname{pd}(S/NI(P_{4}))^{2}=2
.

(iii)

\operatorname{ht}(NI(P_{5}^{2})=1
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/NI(P_{5}^{2}))=3
, and
\operatorname{pd}(S/NI(P_{5}))^{2}=2
.

(iv)

\operatorname{ht}(NI(P_{6}^{2})=2
,
\operatorname{reg}(S/NI(P_{6}^{2}))=4
, and
\operatorname{pd}(S/NI(P_{6}))^{2}=2
.

Proof.

(i) Since
NI(P_{3}^{2})=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})
, the statement follows.

(ii) In this case
NI(P_{4}^{2})=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},x_{2}x_{3}x_{4})=x_{2}x_{3}(x_{1},x_{4})
. The statement is straightforward.

(iii) We have
NI(P_{5}^{2})=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},x_{3}x_{4}x_{5})=x_{3}(x_{1}x_{2},x_{4}x_{5})
, therefore the formulae hold.

(iv) We have
NI(P_{5}^{2})=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},x_{4}x_{5}x_{6})
, so it is a complete intersection. The statement follows.
∎

be an integer,

Firstly we will compute the height of

Proposition 1.2
.

(The height of
NI(P_{n}^{2})
)
Let

be an integer. Then

\operatorname{ht}(NI(P^{2}_{n}))=\left\lceil\frac{n}{5}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Note that, for
n\leq 6
, the formula hold by Proposition
1.1
. We assume now that
n\geq 7
, so the generators are of the form given in
(*)
.

We will analyze the following cases:

Case 1
: Assume that
n\equiv 0(\operatorname{mod}\ 5)
, that is
n=5s
, for some integer
s\geq 1
. We claim that a minimal prime ideal of
NI(P^{2}_{n})
, where

\mathcal{G}(NI(P^{2}_{n}))=\{m_{1}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},m_{5s-4}=x_{5s-2}x_{5s-1}x_{5s}\}\cup

\cup\{m_{i}=x_{i}x_{i+1}x_{i+2}x_{i+3}x_{i+4}:2\leq i\leq 5s-5\}

is

\mathfrak{p}=(x_{3},x_{8},\ldots,x_{5s-2})=(x_{5k+3}:0\leq k\leq s-1).

Indeed, it is clear that
x_{3}\mid m_{1}
and
x_{5s-2}\mid m_{5s-4}
. For every
2\leq i\leq 5s-5
, since the monomial
m_{i}
has the support consisting of five consecutive variables
x_{i},\ldots,x_{i+4}
, we have that there exists
0\leq k\leq s-1
such that

. Therefore we obtain that
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq NI(P^{2}_{n})
and
\operatorname{ht}NI(P^{2}_{n})\leq\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}=s=[\frac{n}{5}]
.

Assume that there exists another minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
NI(P^{2}_{n})
such that
\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{q}<s.
Since
m_{1}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
and
m_{5s-4}=x_{5s-2}x_{5s-1}x_{5s}
are minimal monomial generators of
NI(P^{2}_{n})
there exist
1\leq j\leq 3
and
5s-2\leq k\leq 5s
such that
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq(x_{j},x_{k})
. Moreover,

\{m_{4}=x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}x_{7}x_{8},\ldots,m_{5s-7}=x_{5s-7}x_{5s-6}x_{5s-5}x_{5s-4}x_{5s-3}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}(NI(P_{n})),

therefore
\mathfrak{q}
contains at least one variable from each of disjoint sets:

A_{1}=\{x_{4},x_{5},x_{6},x_{7},x_{8}\},A_{2}=\{x_{9},x_{10},x_{11},x_{12},x_{13}\},\ldots

A_{s-2}=\{x_{5s-10},x_{5s-9},x_{5s-8},x_{5s-7},x_{5s-6}\}.

Hence
\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{q}\geq 2+(s-2)=s
, contradiction.

Case 2
: Assume that
n\equiv 1,2,3(\operatorname{mod}\ 5)
, that is
n=5s+r
,
1\leq r\leq 3
, for some integer
s\geq 1
. We claim that a minimal prime ideal of

is

\mathfrak{p}=(x_{3},x_{8},\ldots,x_{5s-2},x_{5s+1})=(x_{5k+3}:0\leq k\leq s-1)+(x_{5s+1}).

One has that

and

. For every

such that

since the monomial
m_{i}
has the support consisting of five consecutive variables. Therefore, also in this case, we obtain that
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq NI(P^{2}_{n})
and
\operatorname{ht}NI(P^{2}_{n})\leq\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{p}=s+1=\lceil\frac{n}{5}\rceil
.

Assume that there exists another minimal prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
NI(P^{2}_{n})
such that
\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak{q}\leq s.
Similar to previous case,
m_{1}=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}
and
m_{n-4}=x_{n-2}x_{n-1}x_{n}
are minimal monomial generators of
NI(P^{2}_{n})
, therefore there exist
1\leq j\leq 3
and
n-2\leq l\leq n
such that
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq(x_{j},x_{l})
.

We have that

\{m_{4}=x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}x_{7}x_{8},\ldots,m_{n-7}=x_{n-7}x_{n-6}x_{n-5}x_{n-4}x_{n-3}\}\subseteq\mathcal{G}(I),

Hence

, contradiction.

Next we pay attention to the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity and to the projective dimension. We will assume that

Remark 1.5
.

We will use the expression
n=cp+d
for emphasizing that

is the remainder,
0\leq d\leq p-1
.

We recall that the projective dimension and the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of path ideals of paths were given in
[

:

Lemma 1.6
.

[
4
, Corollary 4.15]

Let

be integers such that

,
2\leq t\leq n
,
n=p(t+1)+d
, where
p\geq 0
and
0\leq d\leq t
. Then

•

the projective dimension of the path ideal of a path

is given by

\operatorname{pd}(S/I_{t}(P_{n}))=\begin{cases}2p,&d\neq t\\
2p+1,&d=t\end{cases}

•

the regularity of the path ideal of a path
P_{n}
is given by

\operatorname{reg}(S/I_{t}(P_{n}))=\begin{cases}p(t-1),&d<t\\
(p+1)(t-1),&d=t\end{cases}

The following result of Sharifan will be a key point in our study:

Lemma 1.7
.

[
11
, Corollary 2.6]

If
I
is a monomial ideal of
S
and
u
is a monomial which does not belong to
I
, then the minimal free resolution of
S/(I,u)
is given by the mapping cone technique provided that there is

such that for all

\deg_{x_{i}}(u)>\deg_{x_{i}}(v)
.

So, in order to compute the two invariants, we will apply twice the mapping cone technique. Firstly we will apply it to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}}):(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}(-3)\rightarrow\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})}\rightarrow\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}\rightarrow 0.

Then, we will apply the mapping cone technique to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\frac{S}{(I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}}),x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}):(x_{n-2}x_{n-1}x_{n})}(-3)\rightarrow\frac{S}{(I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}}),x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}\rightarrow

\rightarrow\frac{S}{NI(P^{2}_{n})}\rightarrow 0.

We start with the first exact sequence and we determine the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity and the projective dimension for
\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}
. By
[
11
, Theorem 2.4]
applied in the first exact sequence, we have:

Lemma 1.8
.

In the above notations,

\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}\right)=

=\max\left\{\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}}):(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}\right)+2,\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})}\right)\right\}

and

Lemma 1.10
.

For
n=6p+d
, with

, one has

\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}\right)=\begin{cases}4p-2,&d=0\\

\operatorname{pd}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}})}\right)=\begin{cases}2p,&d\in\{2,3,4,5\}\\
2p-2,&d=0\\
2p-1,&d=1\end{cases}

One may note that

I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}}):(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})=\begin{cases}(x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}),&n\in\{7,8,9\}\\
I_{5}(P_{\{5,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{4}x_{5}x_{6}),&n\geq 10\end{cases}

\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{5,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{4}x_{5}x_{6})}\right),&n\geq 10\end{cases}

and

\operatorname{pd}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{2,\ldots,n-1\}}):(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})}\right)=\begin{cases}1,&n\in\{7,8,9\}\\
\operatorname{pd}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{5,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{4}x_{5}x_{6})}\right),&n\geq 10\end{cases}

By induction we obtain

\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{I_{5}(P_{\{5,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{4}x_{5}x_{6})}\right)=\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{k[x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}]}{I_{5}(P_{\{5,\ldots,n-1\}})+(x_{4}x_{5}x_{6})}\right)=

A simplicial complex

\Delta
on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is a collection of subsets (called
faces
) such that any vertex is in
\Delta
and, if
F
is a face of
\Delta
and

G
is also a face of
\Delta
.
The set of facets of

\Delta
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
.
The dimension of a face

F
si equal to
|F|-1
. The dimension of
\Delta
is the maximum of the dimensions of all the faces. Let
f_{i}
be the number of faces of dimension
i
in
\Delta
. The
f
-vector of
\Delta
is
f(\Delta)=(f_{-1},f_{0},\ldots,f_{d})
, where

and
f_{-1}=1
. The
Euler characteristic of

\Delta
is denoted by
\chi(\Delta)
and is defined as
\chi(\Delta)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}(-1)^{i}f_{i}
, while
the reduced Euler characteristic
is
\overline{\chi}(\Delta)=-1+\chi(\Delta)
. A simplicial complex is
pure
if all its facets have the same dimension. A simplicial complex is
(non-pure) shellable
if its facets can be ordered
F_{1},\ldots,F_{r}
such that for all
1\leq i<j\leq r
there are a vertex
v\in F_{j}\setminus F_{i}
and an integer
l<j
such that
F_{j}\setminus F_{l}=\{v\}
(
[
1
]
).

Given a simplicial complex
\Delta
one may associate several simplicial complexes on the same vertex set:

•

the Alexander dual
of
\Delta

\Delta^{\vee}=\{F:F^{c}\notin\Delta\}

•

the complement of

\Delta
:
\mathcal{F}(\Delta^{c})=\{F^{c}:F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)\}

For a simplicial complex on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}

be the polynomial ring over the field

•

•

The facet ideal
of
\Delta

.

•

The complementary ideal
of
\Delta

.

Conversely, let

•

The Stanley–Reisner simplicial complex

SR(I)

\Delta
whose facets are

4
vertices). Finally
F_{n-4}
contributes with only 2 edges. Hence

f_{1}=2+10+4(n-7)+2=4n-14.

For
f_{2}
, by using the same technique and taking into account that
F_{1}
and
F_{n-4}
are of dimension 2, we get:

f_{2}=2+{5\choose 3}+{4\choose 2}\cdot(n-7)=2+10+6(n-7)=6n-30.

In the same way, we get

f_{3}={5\choose 4}+(n-7)\cdot{4\choose 3}=4n-23

and

f_{4}=n-6.

∎

We can now easily compute the
h
-vector:

Corollary 2.11
.

(The
h
-vector)

The
h
-vector of
\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}]
is
(1,n-5,-4,2,0,0)
.

Proof.

For the proof one may just apply the formulae which relate the
f
-vector and the
h
-vector, namely

h_{i}(\Delta)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}{{d-j}\choose{i-j}}f_{j-1}(\Delta),

for all
i=0,\ldots,d
(here
\dim(\Delta)=d-1
), taking into account that in our case
d=5
and use Proposition
2.10
.
∎

The above results allow us to compute the (reduced) Euler characteristic and the multiplicity.

Proposition 2.12
.

For
n\geq 7
we have
\chi(\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}])=1
,
\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}])=0
and
e(S/NI(P_{n}^{2}))=n-6
.

Proof.

Since
\chi(\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}])=f_{0}-f_{1}+f_{2}-f_{3}+f_{4}
, by using Proposition
2.10
we have

\chi(\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}])=n-(4n-14)+(6n-30)-(4n-23)+(n-6)=1.

Now
\overline{\chi}(\mathcal{N}I[P_{n}^{2}])=\chi(\mathcal{NI}[P_{n}^{2}])-1=0
. For the multiplicity, the formula follows easy by using Corollary
2.11
:

e(S/NI(P_{n}^{2})=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}=1+(n-5)-4+2=n-6
.
∎

Steady Solutions to the Relativistic Boltzmann Equation in a Slab
Jin Woo Jang †, Seok-Bae Yun ‡

Abstract
. We study steady solutions to the relativistic Boltzmann equation with hard-sphere interactions in a slab geometry. Under a spatial symmetry assumption in the transverse variables x 2 x_{2} and x 3 x_{3} , the problem reduces to a one-dimensional spatial slab x 1 ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] x_{1}\in[0,1] while retaining full three-dimensional momentum dependence. For non-negative inflow boundary conditions prescribed at x 1 = 0 x_{1}=0 and x 1 = 1 x_{1}=1 , we prove the existence and uniqueness of a stationary solution in a weighted L p 1 ​ L x 1 ∞ L^{1}_{p}L^{\infty}_{x_{1}} framework, together with exponential decay in momentum. Our analysis treats the full slab domain and does not rely on any smallness assumption on the slab width. We establish sharp coercivity and continuity estimates for the collision frequency, together with weighted convolution and pointwise bounds for the nonlinear gain term. These estimates generate and propagate a ( − Δ p ) − 1 (-\Delta_{p})^{-1} -type regularity within the solution framework, which plays a crucial role in the existence and uniqueness argument. In addition, we obtain uniform weighted integrability of the solution over arbitrary two-dimensional hyperplanes through the origin. This hyperplane estimate is derived as a genuinely a posteriori regularity property, without imposing any a priori hyperplane bounds, and follows from a Lorentz-invariant geometric reduction.

1.
Introduction

This work is on the stationary relativistic Boltzmann equation. Incorporating Einstein’s special relativity theory, the Boltzmann equation characterizes the statistical spread of gas particles
[
22
,
15
]
. It stands as a pivotal dynamic model within special relativistic kinetic theory.

1.1.
Stationary relativistic Boltzmann equation:

The stationary relativistic Boltzmann equation that we consider in this paper reads as

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hat{p}\cdot\nabla_{x}F=Q(F,F),\end{split}

where the particle distribution function
F(x,p)
represents the probabilistic density function of particles at position
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
with momentum
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The collision operator
Q(f,h)
then can be written as

Q(f,h)=\frac{1}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{{q^{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}\hskip 2.84526pt}{{q^{\prime 0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}\hskip 2.84526pt}{{p^{\prime 0}}}W(p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime})(f(p^{\prime})h(q^{\prime})-f(p)h(q))

where we denote the particle energy and the velocity as
cp^{0}
and
\hat{p}
, respectively, of a particle with the rest mass
m
and the momentum
p
where

(1.2)

p^{0}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\sqrt{m^{2}c^{2}+|p|^{2}},\ \textup{ and }\hat{p}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\frac{cp}{p^{0}}.

Here the transition rate
W(p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
is given by

(1.3)

\displaystyle W(p,q|p^{\prime},q^{\prime})=\frac{c}{2}s\sigma(g,\theta)\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu}),

where
\sigma(g,\theta)
is the scattering kernel measuring the interactions between particles,
s
and
g
are two variables depending on
(p^{0},p)
and
(q^{0},q)
(whose definition will further be introduced in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
)), and the Dirac-delta function,
\delta^{(4)}
, enforces the conservation of energy and momentum (
1.14
). Without loss of generality, we normalize the speed of light
c
and the rest mass
m
to be equal to 1 through the rest of the paper. Other necessary notations will be defined in the next section.

1.2.
Hypothesis on the scattering kernel

Throughout the paper, we assume that the relativistic Boltzmann cross-section
\sigma
will be written as the following product-type form:

(1.4)

\sigma(g,\theta)\approx g\sigma_{0}(\theta).

Such a representation describes the relativistic hard-sphere case. Here we assume that
\sigma_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2})
and
0\leq\sigma_{0}(\theta)\leq M
for some positive constant
M>0.
A generic choice of
\sigma_{0}(\theta)
is
\sin^{\gamma}(\theta)
for
\gamma\geq 0
, as in
[
37
]
for instance. This scattering kernel covers a broad range of generic relativistic hard-sphere interactions classified by Dudyǹski and Ekiel-Je
\dot{\textup{z}}
ewska in
[
29
]
with angular cutoff. Without loss of generality, we normalize and assume that
M=\frac{1}{4\pi}
such that

(1.5)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\sigma_{0}(\theta)d\omega=c_{0}\leq 1

through the rest of the paper.

1.3.
Steady states under symmetry in
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
directions

Throughout this work, we consider a stationary distribution function
F=F(x,p)
that is assumed to be symmetric with respect to the transverse spatial variables
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
. Under this symmetry, the distribution depends only on the longitudinal coordinate
x_{1}\in[0,1]
and momentum
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, so that we may write
F(x,p)=f(x_{1},p)
.

The full spatial domain is formally given as
\Omega=[0,1]\times\Omega_{x_{2},x_{3}}
, where
\Omega_{x_{2},x_{3}}
is a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. However, due to the imposed symmetry in the
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
directions, the problem effectively reduces to a one-dimensional slab in
x_{1}
. Note that if
\Omega_{x_{2},x_{3}}
were unbounded, the total mass of the system would be infinite unless the density is identically zero, which we exclude.

This steady-state configuration is reminiscent of the classical Couette flow in kinetic theory. We thus focus on a boundary value problem in the phase space
(x_{1},p)\in[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, subject to the following inflow boundary conditions at
x_{1}=0
and
x_{1}=1
:

(1.6)

\begin{cases}f(0,p)=f_{L}(p),&\text{for }p_{1}>0,\\
f(1,p)=f_{R}(p),&\text{for }p_{1}<0,\end{cases}

where
f_{L}
and
f_{R}
are prescribed non-negative functions describing incoming particle distributions at the boundaries. For convenience, we define the combined inflow profile

f_{LR}(p):=f_{L}(p)\,\mathbf{1}_{p_{1}>0}+f_{R}(p)\,\mathbf{1}_{p_{1}<0}.

1.3.1.
Boundary conditions

We impose the following assumptions on the inflow boundary profile
f_{LR}
.
There exist constants
m,M>0
such that

(1.7)

f_{LR}\geq 0,\quad\inf_{p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}Lf_{L}(p)\geq m,\quad\inf_{p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}Lf_{R}(p)\geq m,\\
\|f_{LR}\|\leq M,\quad\|f_{LR}\|_{-1}\leq M.

Here the norms
\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{-1}
will be defined in
Section
1.6.1
.
These assumptions ensure a uniform coercivity of the collision frequency near
the boundary and play a crucial role in the construction of steady solutions.

Formally, the relativistic Boltzmann collision operator
Q(f,f)
satisfies the
conservation identities

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q(f,f)\,dp=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p^{i}Q(f,f)\,dp=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}p^{0}Q(f,f)\,dp=0,\qquad i=1,2,3.

These identities correspond to the conservation of mass, momentum, and energy,
and lead to the following compatibility conditions on the inflow boundary data
prescribed at
x_{1}=0
and
x_{1}=1
:

(1.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\[2.84526pt]
p\\[2.84526pt]
p^{0}\end{pmatrix}\hat{p}_{1}\,f_{L}(p)\,dp=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\[2.84526pt]
p\\[2.84526pt]
p^{0}\end{pmatrix}\hat{p}_{1}\,f_{R}(p)\,dp.

Stationary solutions to collisional kinetic equations

As explained in

In comparison with the Cauchy problem, however, the theory of stationary
boundary-value problems remains considerably less developed

[
82
]
.

The study of stationary solutions to collisional kinetic equations,
and in particular to the Boltzmann equation, has a long history.
Existence and regularity results for stationary boundary-value problems
have been obtained in a variety of settings; see, for example,

[

,

,

,

,

For relativistic kinetic equations, Jüttner
[

]
introduced the

Its properties and implications were subsequently revisited and further
analyzed, including the work of Abonyi
[
1
]
in 1960.
More recently, Hwang and Yun
[
49
]
studied stationary solutions

Very recently, Wang, Li, and Jiang
[

1.5.
Solutions in the mild formulation

We begin by recalling that the relativistic Boltzmann equation (
1.1
)
can be rewritten in the form

(1.9)

\partial_{x_{1}}f+\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}fLf=\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}Q^{+}(f,f),

where the linearized collision operator
L
is defined by

\displaystyle Lf:=\frac{1}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{q^{\prime 0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}}{p^{\prime 0}}\,W(p,q\mid p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\,f(x_{1},q),

so that the loss term satisfies
Q^{-}(f,f)=fLf
.

For each fixed
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, equation (
1.9
) is an ordinary
differential equation with respect to the spatial variable
x_{1}
.
We therefore introduce the integrating factor

\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{x_{1,0}}^{x_{1}}Lf(z,p)\,dz\right),

where
x_{1,0}=0
if
p_{1}>0
and
x_{1,0}=1
if
p_{1}<0
.
Using the inflow boundary conditions (
1.6
), we obtain the following
mild formulation of (
1.9
):

(1.10)

f(x_{1},p)=\mathbf{1}_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\bigg[f_{L}(p)\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}Lf(z,p)\,dz\right)\\
+\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)\,dz^{\prime}\right)Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)\,dz\bigg]\\
+\mathbf{1}_{\{p_{1}<0\}}\bigg[f_{R}(p)\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{1}Lf(z,p)\,dz\right)\\
-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{1}\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{z}Lf(z^{\prime},p)\,dz^{\prime}\right)Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)\,dz\bigg]\\
=:(Af)(x_{1},p).

Here
A
denotes the solution operator acting on
f\in\mathscr{A}
, where
the solution space
\mathscr{A}
will be specified in
(
4.1
). This formulation will serve as the starting point for the fixed-point
construction of steady solutions.

1.6.
Notations

In this section, we introduce the notational conventions for relativistic
four-vectors and the function spaces used throughout the paper.

•

We denote a relativistic four-vector by
p^{\mu}
, where

\mu=0,1,2,3
, with components

p^{\mu}\in\{p^{0},p^{1},p^{2},p^{3}\}.

Henceforth, four-vectors will simply be referred to as vectors.

•

Latin indices
a,b,i,j,k
take values in
\{1,2,3\}
, while Greek
indices
\kappa,\lambda,\mu,\nu
take values in
\{0,1,2,3\}
.
Indices are raised and lowered using the Minkowski metric

\eta_{\mu\nu}
and its inverse
(\eta^{-1})^{\mu\nu}
, defined by

\eta_{\mu\nu}=(\eta^{-1})^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1),

so that
p_{\mu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}
.

•

We adopt Einstein’s summation convention, whereby repeated indices with
one upper and one lower index are summed over.

•

The Lorentz inner product of two four-vectors is given by

(1.11)

p^{\mu}q_{\mu}=p^{\mu}\eta_{\mu\nu}q^{\nu}=-p^{0}q^{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}p^{i}q^{i}.

•

A four-vector
p^{\mu}
satisfying the mass-shell condition

p^{\mu}p_{\mu}=-1
with
p^{0}>0
is called an
energy–momentum vector
.
In this case,
p^{\mu}=(p^{0},p)
with
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and

p^{0}=\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}.

Throughout this paper, the vectors
p^{\mu},q^{\mu},p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu}

appearing in the relativistic Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) are always
energy–momentum vectors.

•

A four-vector
a^{\mu}
is called
space-like
if
a^{\mu}a_{\mu}>0
,
and
time-like
if
a^{\mu}a_{\mu}<0
.

With these conventions, the quantity
s
appearing in (
1.3
) denotes the
square of the total energy in the center-of-momentum frame,

(1.12)

s=s(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}-(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})=2(-p^{\mu}q_{\mu}+1)\geq 0,

while
g
denotes the relative momentum,

(1.13)

g=g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\sqrt{(p^{\mu}-q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}-q_{\mu})}=\sqrt{2(-p^{\mu}q_{\mu}-1)}.

In particular,
s
and
g
are related by
s=g^{2}+4
.
Using (
1.11
), we also have

g=\sqrt{-(p^{0}-q^{0})^{2}+|p-q|^{2}},

where
|p-q|
denotes the Euclidean distance in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Unless otherwise stated, we write
g=g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})
and

s=s(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})
.

The scattering angle
\theta
is defined by

\cos\theta=\frac{(p^{\mu}-q^{\mu})(p^{\prime}_{\mu}-q^{\prime}_{\mu})}{g^{2}},

which is well defined; see
[
40
]
.
By the collision invariance

(1.14)

p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}=p^{\prime\mu}+q^{\prime\mu},\qquad\mu=0,1,2,3,

we have
g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=g(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})
and

s(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=s(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})
.

Finally,
C>0
denotes a generic constant whose value may change from line to
line. We write
A\lesssim B
if
A\leq CB
for some such constant
C
, and

A\approx B
if both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold.

1.6.1.
Special norms for solutions

•

For each fixed
x_{1}\in[0,1]
and
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\|f(x_{1},\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{p,\ell}}\;\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(p^{0})^{\ell}\,|f(x_{1},p)|\,dp.

•

Similarly, for each fixed
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we define

\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\;\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\;\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}|f(x_{1},p)|.

•

We use the mixed space

f\in L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}\!\left([0,1];\,L^{1}_{p}\!\left(\mathbb{R}^{3},\,(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dp\right)\right),

that is,

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}L^{1}_{p,\frac{1}{2}}}\;\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\;\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f(x_{1},p)|\,(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dp<\infty.

•

Similarly, we define the mixed space

f\in L^{1}_{p}\!\left(\mathbb{R}^{3},\,(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dp;\,L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}([0,1])\right),

that is,

\|f\|_{L^{1}_{p,\frac{1}{2}}L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\;\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}|f(x_{1},p)|\,(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dp<\infty.

•

For
k\geq 0
, let
\varphi(p)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}e^{kp^{0}}
and define

\|f\|\;\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}|f(x_{1},p)|\,(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\varphi(p)\,dp<\infty.

Then, in general, the following inequalities hold:

(1.15)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}L^{1}_{p,\frac{1}{2}}}\;\leq\;\|f\|_{L^{1}_{p,\frac{1}{2}}L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\;\leq\;\|f\|.

•

The inverse-Laplacian norm is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{-1}

\displaystyle\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}\bigl((p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}|f(x_{1},p)|\bigr)(a)

\displaystyle=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)}{|p-a|}\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,dp.

•

Finally, the hyperplane norm is defined by

\|f\|_{\mathrm{hyp}}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\sup_{E}\int_{E}(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,d\sigma_{E}(p),

where the supremum is taken over all two-dimensional planes
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}

passing through the origin and
d\sigma_{E}
denotes the Lebesgue
measure on
E
.

1.7.
Center-of-momentum framework

There are several equivalent ways to perform the Dirac delta integrations
in the collision operator (
1.1
).
In the
center-of-momentum
(or center-of-mass) frame, the gain and loss
terms of the relativistic Boltzmann collision operator admit the following
representations; see
[
22
,
78
]
:

(1.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}Q^{+}(f,h)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}v_{\mathrm{M}}\,\sigma(g,\theta)\,f(p^{\prime})\,h(q^{\prime})\,d\omega\,dq,\\
Q^{-}(f,h)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}v_{\mathrm{M}}\,\sigma(g,\theta)\,f(p)\,h(q)\,d\omega\,dq,\end{split}

where
\sigma(g,\theta)
denotes the scattering kernel and

v_{\mathrm{M}}
is the Møller velocity, given by

(1.17)

v_{\mathrm{M}}=v_{\mathrm{M}}(p,q)=\sqrt{\left|\frac{p}{p^{0}}-\frac{q}{q^{0}}\right|^{2}-\left|\frac{p}{p^{0}}\times\frac{q}{q^{0}}\right|^{2}}=\frac{g\sqrt{s}}{p^{0}q^{0}}.

The pre-collisional momentum pair
(p,q)
and the post-collisional pair

(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
are related by

(1.18)

\begin{split}p^{\prime}&=\frac{p+q}{2}+\frac{g}{2}\left(\omega+(\gamma-1)(p+q)\frac{(p+q)\cdot\omega}{|p+q|^{2}}\right),\\
q^{\prime}&=\frac{p+q}{2}-\frac{g}{2}\left(\omega+(\gamma-1)(p+q)\frac{(p+q)\cdot\omega}{|p+q|^{2}}\right),\end{split}

where

\gamma=\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{\sqrt{s}}.

In particular,

(1.19)

\gamma-1=\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}-\sqrt{s}}{\sqrt{s}}=\frac{|p+q|^{2}}{\sqrt{s}\,(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}.

The post-collisional energies are given by
[
78
]

(1.20)

p^{\prime 0}=\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{2}+\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}\,\omega\cdot(p+q),\qquad q^{\prime 0}=\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{2}-\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}\,\omega\cdot(p+q).

With this formulation, the linearized collision operator
L
can be written as

(1.21)

Lf=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}v_{\mathrm{M}}\,\sigma(g,\theta)\,f(q)\,d\omega\,dq.

1.8.
Main results

Given non-negative inflow boundary conditions prescribed at
x_{1}=0
and

x_{1}=1
, we prove the existence and uniqueness of a steady-state solution to
the relativistic Boltzmann equation that is symmetric in the transverse spatial
variables
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
.
The solution belongs to a weighted
L^{1}_{p}L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}
framework and
exhibits exponential decay with respect to the momentum variable
p
.
Moreover, we establish a
(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}
-type regularity that is intrinsic
to the solution framework.
As an additional result, we show that the weighted steady state satisfies a
uniform integrability estimate over arbitrary two-dimensional hyperplanes in
momentum space.

Our main result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that the inflow boundary profile
f_{LR}(p)
satisfies the conditions
(
1.7
).
Let
\varphi(p)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}e^{kp^{0}}
for some constant
k>0
.
If
k
is sufficiently large, then there exists a unique non-negative steady
state
f
solving (
1.9
)
in the sense of the mild formulation (
1.10
) such that the following weighted bounds hold:

(1)

\|f\|\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}|f(x_{1},p)|\,(p^{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi(p)\,dp<\infty,

(2)

\begin{split}\|f\|_{-1}&\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}(-\Delta_{p})^{-1}\bigl((p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x_{1}\in[0,1]}|f(x_{1},p)|\bigr)(a)\\
&=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)}{|p-a|}\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,dp<\infty.\end{split}

In addition, if the boundary profile
f_{LR}
satisfies the hyperplane bound

\|f_{LR}\|_{\mathrm{hyp}}<\infty
,
then the stationary solution
f
satisfies the same hyperplane integrability estimate:

\|f\|_{\mathrm{hyp}}=\sup_{E}\int_{E}(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,d\sigma_{E}(p)<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all two-dimensional planes
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
passing through the origin and
d\sigma_{E}
denotes the Lebesgue
measure on
E
.

1.9.
Main difficulties and strategies

The principal analytical challenge in this work arises from the study of the
stationary relativistic Boltzmann equation on a slab domain

x_{1}\in[0,1]
of order one, without invoking the small-slab approximation

x_{1}\in[0,\varepsilon]
that is frequently adopted in the existing literature
(e.g.,
[
66
,
46
,
36
]
).
Working on a spatial domain of fixed size requires uniform control of the
nonlinear collision operator
Q(f,f)
, whose relativistic structure entails
strong nonlocality and anisotropy in momentum space.

A major source of difficulty is the gain term
Q^{+}(f,f)
.
The relativistic post-collisional momentum transformation, together with the
presence of the Møller velocity
v_{\textup{\o}}
, induces significant
geometric distortion and singular behavior in momentum variables.
These features are absent in the Newtonian framework and prevent the direct
application of standard convolution-type or energy-based estimates.

To address these issues, we construct solutions in a weighted function space

\mathscr{A}
(defined in (
4.1
)) endowed with a dual-weight
structure that combines the polynomial factor
(p^{0})^{1/2}
and the exponential
weight
\varphi(p)=e^{kp^{0}}
.
These weights play complementary and analytically essential roles.
The polynomial weight
(p^{0})^{1/2}
is adapted to the growth of the collision
frequency and compensates for the relativistic degeneracy arising from the
transport coefficient
1/\hat{p}_{1}
.
The exponential weight
\varphi
provides sufficient decay in the
high-momentum regime, which is necessary for controlling the nonlinear gain term
and for closing the estimates in the mild formulation.

A central ingredient of the analysis is the coercivity of the loss operator.
Under suitable assumptions on the inflow boundary profile, we establish that the
linearized collision operator satisfies the uniform lower bound

Lf(x_{1},p)\gtrsim(p^{0})^{1/2},

for all
x_{1}\in[0,1]
(Proposition
2.1
).
Moreover, we derive a continuity estimate for the operator
L

(Lemma
2.2
) of the form

|Lf(x_{1},p)-Lh(x_{1},p)|\lesssim e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}(p^{0})^{1/2}\|f-h\|,

which provides a Lipschitz-type control with a small prefactor and plays a
crucial role in establishing that the solution operator
A
is a contraction in
the Banach fixed-point argument. We note that Lemma
2.2
also yields an alternative continuity bound in a
weighted
L^{1}_{p}
topology, which is recorded for completeness.

The gain term
Q^{+}(f,f)
is controlled through three complementary estimates.
First, we obtain a potential-type convolution bound
(Proposition
3.1
),

\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\varphi(p)}{|p-a|}\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,dp\lesssim\|f\|^{2},

which reflects an inverse-Laplacian structure in momentum space.
Second, we establish a pointwise weighted estimate
(Lemma
3.2
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(p)\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,dp\lesssim k^{-1}\|f\|\|f\|_{-1},

where the factor
k^{-1}
is essential for closing the fixed-point argument.
Third, we prove a uniform hyperplane estimate
(Proposition
6.1
),

\sup_{E}\int_{E}(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,d\sigma_{E}(p)\lesssim\|f\|^{2},

where the supremum is taken over all two-dimensional hyperplanes

E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
passing through the origin.

The proof of the hyperplane estimate relies on a geometric reduction based on
Lorentz transformations, which allows arbitrary hyperplanes to be mapped into a
canonical configuration (see (
1.25
)).
This reduction enables uniform integration over codimension-one subsets of
momentum space.

An important feature of our approach is that the hyperplane bound is not imposed

a priori
.
Instead, the finiteness of the hyperplane norm
\|f\|_{\mathrm{hyp}}
is shown
to propagate from the inflow boundary to the interior solution
(Section
6
), while it plays no role in the existence and uniqueness
theory developed in Section
5
.

In summary, the combination of weighted function spaces, coercivity and
continuity estimates for the loss operator, refined bounds for the gain term,
and Lorentz-based geometric reductions provides a framework for constructing
stationary solutions of the relativistic Boltzmann equation in a full slab
domain.

1.11.
Geometric and analytic preliminaries

In this subsection, we collect several analytic and geometric tools that will be
used repeatedly throughout the paper, particularly in the estimates of the
collision operator and in the derivation of hyperplane bounds.
We begin with a classical coercive inequality for the relativistic relative
momentum in the center-of-momentum framework.

Lemma 1.2

(Coercive inequality for the relative momentum
[
37
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

The relativistic relative momentum
g
satisfies

(1.22)

\frac{\bigl(|p-q|^{2}+|p\times q|^{2}\bigr)^{1/2}}{\sqrt{p^{0}q^{0}}}\leq g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})\leq\min\{|p-q|,\,2\sqrt{p^{0}q^{0}}\}.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
37
]
, the relative momentum is defined as one half of our definition
(
1.13
).

We next recall several elementary facts concerning Lorentz transformations that
will be used in the analysis of the collision operator and, in particular, in
the geometric reduction underlying the hyperplane estimates.

A
4\times 4
real matrix
\Lambda=(\Lambda^{\mu}_{\ \nu})
is called a (proper)
Lorentz transformation if
\det\Lambda=1
and

(1.23)

\Lambda^{\kappa}_{\ \mu}\,\eta_{\kappa\lambda}\,\Lambda^{\lambda}_{\ \nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu},\qquad\mu,\nu=0,1,2,3.

This condition implies the invariance of the Lorentz inner product:

(1.24)

p^{\mu}q_{\mu}=(\Lambda p)^{\mu}(\Lambda q)_{\mu}.

Any Lorentz transformation is invertible, and its inverse is again a Lorentz
transformation. We refer to

[
79
,
76
,
77
,
69
]
for further background.

Momentum rotations and hyperplane normalization.

A key geometric ingredient in our analysis is the ability to normalize the
orientation of momentum-space hyperplanes via Lorentz transformations.

Let
a=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. There exists a Lorentz transformation

\Lambda
such that

(1.25)

\Lambda(0,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=(0,0,0,|a|),

where
|a|=(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2})^{1/2}
.
Such a transformation acts as a spatial rotation on the momentum components
while preserving the time component.

This geometric reduction allows us to rotate arbitrary two-dimensional
hyperplanes in momentum space into a canonical form and plays a crucial role in
the proof of the hyperplane estimate (Proposition
6.1
) and in its propagation
to the steady-state solution established in Section
6
.

2udu=-Bdz,
we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega-a^{\prime\prime}|}=2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{dz}{\sqrt{A-Bz}}=2\pi\int_{\sqrt{A-B}}^{\sqrt{A+B}}\frac{2}{B}\frac{udu}{u}=\frac{4\pi}{B}(\sqrt{A+B}-\sqrt{A-B})\\
=\frac{2\pi}{|a^{\prime\prime}|}(1+|a^{\prime\prime}|-|1-|a^{\prime\prime}||).

If
|a^{\prime\prime}|\geq 1,
then we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega-a^{\prime\prime}|}=\frac{2\pi}{|a^{\prime\prime}|}(1+|a^{\prime\prime}|-(|a^{\prime\prime}|-1))=\frac{4\pi}{|a^{\prime\prime}|}\leq 4\pi.

On the other hand, if
|a^{\prime\prime}|<1,
then we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega-a^{\prime\prime}|}=\frac{2\pi}{|a^{\prime\prime}|}(1+|a^{\prime\prime}|-(1-|a^{\prime\prime}|))=4\pi.

Therefore, in any case, we have

\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\frac{d\omega}{|\omega-a^{\prime\prime}|}\leq 1.

Plugging this back into (
3.1
) and using
v_{\textup{\o}}\lesssim 1
,
g\lesssim\sqrt{p^{0}q^{0}}
, and
p^{\prime 0}\leq p^{0}+q^{0}
such that
\varphi(p^{\prime})\leq\varphi(p)\varphi(q)
, we obtain

I_{a}\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}dp\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\ (q^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(q)\|f(\cdot,q)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\approx\|f\|^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Pointwise estimate

In this section, we establish an additional weighted pointwise
estimate as in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\varphi(p)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}e^{kp^{0}}
for some
k>0
.
Then the following estimate holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(p)\,\|Q^{+}(f,h)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,dp\;\lesssim\;k^{-1}\,\|f\|_{-1}\,\|h\|.

Proof.

Using the representation (
1.1
) and (
1.3
) for
Q^{+}
we first observe that

(3.2)

I\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(p)\|Q^{+}(f,h)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}dp\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{{q^{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}\hskip 2.84526pt}{{q^{\prime 0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}\hskip 2.84526pt}{{p^{\prime 0}}}\varphi(p)sg\sigma_{0}(\theta)\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})\\
\times\|f(\cdot,p^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\|h(\cdot,q^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}.

Applying a pre-post relabelling of the variables
(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})\to(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})
and using the fact that
s
and
g
are invariant under this transformation, we obtain that the integral
I
is less than or equal to

\frac{M}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{{q^{0}}}sg\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\|h(\cdot,q)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\\
\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{{q^{\prime 0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}}{{p^{\prime 0}}}e^{kp^{\prime 0}}\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu}),

where we used
\sigma_{0}(\theta)\leq M.
Using (
1.20
), we further have

I\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{{q^{0}}}sg\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\|h(\cdot,q)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\\
\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}}{{q^{\prime 0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}}{{p^{\prime 0}}}e^{k(\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{2}+\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}\omega\cdot(p+q))}\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu}).

The standard reduction to the
center-of-momentum
frame representation (
1.16
) then gives that

I\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\ v_{\textup{\o}}g\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\|h(\cdot,q)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\omega\ e^{k(\frac{p^{0}+q^{0}}{2}+\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}\omega\cdot(p+q))}.

Note that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\omega\ e^{k\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}\omega\cdot(p+q)}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\ \sin\theta\ e^{k\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}|p+q|\cos\theta}\\
=\frac{4\pi\sqrt{s}}{kg|p+q|}(e^{k\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}|p+q|}-e^{-k\frac{g}{2\sqrt{s}}|p+q|})=\frac{8\pi\sqrt{s}}{kg|p+q|}\sinh\left(\frac{kg}{2\sqrt{s}}|p+q|\right)\\
\leq\frac{8\pi\sqrt{s}}{kg|p+q|}e^{\frac{k}{2}(p^{0}+q^{0})},

where we used
|p+q|\leq p^{0}+q^{0}
and
g\leq\sqrt{s}
for the last equality. Now using
\sqrt{s}\lesssim(p^{0}q^{0})^{1/2},
we obtain

(3.3)

I\lesssim\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}dp\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dq\ v_{\textup{\o}}\frac{(p^{0}q^{0})^{1/2}}{k|p+q|}\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\|h(\cdot,q)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}e^{k(p^{0}+q^{0})}\\
\lesssim k^{-1}\|f\|_{-1}\|h\|,

since the norms are defined as

\|f\|_{-1}=\sup_{a\in{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}dp\ \frac{(p^{0})^{1/2}}{|p-a|}\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\varphi(p),

and

\|f\|=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}dp\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\varphi(p).

∎

In addition, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\varphi(p)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}e^{kp^{0}}
for some
k>0
.
Then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(p)\,\|Q^{+}(f,h)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,dp\;\lesssim\;k^{-1}\,\|h\|_{-1}\,\|f\|.

Proof.

By making the change of variables
(p^{\prime},q^{\prime},\omega)\mapsto(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},-\omega)
at the level of (
3.2
), we can have

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varphi(p)\|Q^{+}(f,h)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}dp\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{3}}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{{q^{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp^{\prime}\hskip 2.84526pt}{{p^{\prime 0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq^{\prime}\hskip 2.84526pt}{{q^{\prime 0}}}\varphi(p)sg\sigma_{0}(\pi-\theta)\delta^{(4)}(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu}-p^{\prime\mu}-q^{\prime\mu})\\
\times\|f(\cdot,q^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\|h(\cdot,p^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}.

Since we have
\sigma_{0}(\pi-\theta)\leq M,
the rest of the proof follows the same as that of Lemma
3.2
with
f
and
h
swapped. This gives the desired result.
∎

(4.1)

\mathscr{A}=\bigg\{f\in L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}\left([0,1];L^{1}_{p}\!\left(\mathbb{R}^{3},\,(p^{0})^{1/2}\,dp\right)\right):f\geq 0,\ Lf\geq c_{1},\ \\

\|f\|\leq a_{1},\ \textup{ and }\|f\|_{-1}\leq a_{2}\bigg\},

for some fixed positive constant
c_{1}
and
a_{i}
for
i=1,2.
Here given
m,

M
, and
C_{l}
from (
1.7
) and (
2.1
),

•

c_{1}
is chosen such that
c_{1}=\frac{cm}{2}
for the uniform constant
c
given in (
4.5
).

•

a_{1}
is chosen such that
a_{1}\geq 2M+2\frac{1}{C_{l}}
.

•

a_{2}
is chosen such that
a_{2}\geq 2M+2\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{C_{l}}
.

•

Lastly,

is chosen sufficiently large such that

) holds. Also, we require

that

is a contraction mapping from

4.1.
Solution operator

Recall that we obtain the mild representations of the stationary solution in (

). Define the right-hand side of (

) as the operator
A
. More precisely, we define the solution operator

(4.2)

Af(x_{1},p)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}f_{L}(p)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}Lf(z,p)dz\right)\\
+1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right)Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)dz\\

+1_{\{p_{1}<0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{1}^{x_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right)Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)dz\\

Note that for

each

Then we can first prove that

Lemma 4.1

(Self-map property of
A
)
.

For a sufficiently large

we have

A\mathscr{A}\subset\mathscr{A}.

Proof.

We check that if
f\in\mathscr{A}

Af\in\mathscr{A}.
Suppose

Note that we easily have
Af\geq 0
since each term in (
4.2

(Af)(x_{1},p)\geq 1_{p_{1}>0}f_{L}(p)\exp\left(-\frac{c_{1}x_{1}}{\hat{p}_{1}}\right)+1_{p_{1}<0}f_{R}(p)\exp\left(\frac{(1-x_{1})c_{1}}{\hat{p}_{1}}\right),

using the definition of
A
in (
4.2
) and the fact that
Lf\geq c_{1},
and that
Q^{+}(f,f)
is non-negative.
For the rest of the required estimates, we only check the first two terms
(Af)_{1}
and
(Af)_{2}
of (
4.2
) since the other terms,
(Af)_{3}
and
(Af)_{4}
, can be treated similarly if we make a change of variables
(x_{1},p)\mapsto(\bar{x}_{1},\bar{p})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(1-x_{1},-p)
.

0. Proof for
L(Af)\geq c_{1}
.
We show that
c_{1}
can be chosen such that
L((Af)_{i})\geq\frac{c_{1}}{2},
for

L((Af)_{1})(x_{1},p)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}v_{\textup{\o}}\sigma(g,\theta)(Af)_{1}(x_{1},q)d\omega dq\\

.
Therefore, we have

L((Af)_{1})(x_{1},p)\\
\gtrsim\int_{Q_{r_{1},r_{2}}}dq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\omega\ v_{\textup{\o}}\sigma(g,\theta)f_{L}(q)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}c_{0}k^{-1}(1+r_{2})a_{1}dz\right)\\

using

on

and

. Since

Then for such fixed

and

e^{-c_{0}k^{-1}r_{1}^{-1}(1+r_{2})^{2}a_{1}}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Then we can conclude that for some uniform constant

we have

(4.5)

L((Af)_{1})(x_{1},p)\geq\frac{cm}{4},

for any

. Similarly, one can show that
L((Af)_{3})(x_{1},p)\geq\frac{cm}{4}.
If
c_{1}

1. Proof for

using Lemma

, that

,

and that

. Let

Therefore, given

and

, if
a_{1}
has been chosen such that

5.
Existence and Uniqueness of Steady Solutions

5.1.
Contraction of the solution operator
A

In this section, we prove that
A
is continuous in the following sense:

Lemma 5.1

(Contraction property of
A
)
.

Suppose
f,h\in\mathscr{A}
.
f_{LR}
further satisfies the conditions in (
1.7
). Suppose
f
and
h
coincide at the incoming boundary as
f|_{\gamma_{-}}=h|_{\gamma_{-}}=f_{LR}|_{\gamma_{-}}

\|Af-Ah\|\lesssim k^{-1}\|f-h\|,

for
k>0
where the norm
\|\cdot\|
is defined as

(5.1)

\|f\|\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\|f(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}dp.

Proof.

Since
f
and
h
coincide on the incoming boundary
\gamma_{-},
the differences
(Af)_{1}-(Ah)_{1}
and
(Af)_{3}-(Ah)_{3}
of (
4.2
) are cancelled out in the

representation. We now look at the difference
(Af)_{2}-(Ah)_{2}
of (
4.2
). The other difference
(Af)_{4}-(Ah)_{4}
is similar and we omit the estimate.

1. Estimate for the difference
(Af)_{2}-(Ah)_{2}
.
Note that

(Af)_{2}(x_{1},p)-(Ah)_{2}(x_{1},p)\\
=1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right)\bigg[Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)-Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)\bigg]dz\\
+1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\left[e^{\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right)}-e^{\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lh(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right)}\right]Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)dz.

Note that by the mean-value theorem we have
|1-e^{-(x-y)}|\leq|x-y|
for
x-y>0.
Also by Lemma
2.2
, we have

(5.2)

|(Af)_{2}(x_{1},p)-(Ah)_{2}(x_{1},p)|\\
\leq 1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right)\bigg|Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)-Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)\bigg|dz\\
+1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{2}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}dz\ e^{\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\min\{\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime},\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lh(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\}\right)}\\
\times e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}(p^{0})^{1/2}\|f-h\|(x_{1}-z)Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)\\
\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}I_{1}(x_{1},p)+I_{2}(x_{1},p).

(i) Estimate for
I_{2}
.

Since
Lf\geq C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}
and
Lh\geq C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}
by Proposition
2.1
, we have

(5.3)

-\min\left\{\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lf(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime},\int_{z}^{x_{1}}Lh(z^{\prime},p)dz^{\prime}\right\}\leq-C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}(x_{1}-z).

Thus, using
0\leq z\leq x_{1}\leq 1,
we have

\varphi(p)I_{2}(x_{1},p)=\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{2}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}dz\ \exp\left(-\frac{C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}}{\hat{p}_{1}}(x_{1}-z)\right)\\
\times e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}(p^{0})^{1/2}\|f-h\|(x_{1}-z)Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)\\
\leq\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}^{2}_{1}}(\sup_{z}|Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)|)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\hat{p}_{1}dz^{\prime}}{C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}}\ \exp\left(-z^{\prime}\right)\\
\times e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}(p^{0})^{1/2}\|f-h\|\frac{\hat{p}_{1}}{C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}}z^{\prime}\\
\lesssim\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\sup_{z}|Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)|\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}}{C_{l}^{2}}(p^{0})^{-1/2}\|f-h\|,

where we made a change of variables
z\mapsto z^{\prime}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}-\frac{C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}}{\hat{p}_{1}}(x_{1}-z).

Thus,

\|I_{2}\|\lesssim\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}}{C_{l}^{2}}\|f-h\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\ \varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\sup_{z}|Q^{+}(h,h)(z,p)|\\
\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}}{kC_{l}^{2}}\|f-h\|\|h\|_{-1}\|h\|\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}}{k}\frac{a_{1}a_{2}}{C_{l}^{2}}\|f-h\|,

using that
h\in\mathscr{A}
and Lemma
3.2
.

(ii) Estimate for
I_{1}
.

We now look at the difference of
Q^{+}(f,f)-Q^{+}(h,h)
appearing in the difference

. Since
Q^{+}
is bi-linear, we first observe that for each

,

\|Q^{+}(f,f)-Q^{+}(h,h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\|Q^{+}(f-h,f+h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}+\|Q^{+}(f+h,f-h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\right).

Therefore, using
Lf\geq C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}
from Proposition
2.1
, we note that

\|I_{1}\|=\sup_{x_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)|I_{1}(x_{1},p)|\\
\leq\sup_{x_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{2\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}(x_{1}-z)\right)\\
\times\left(\|Q^{+}(f-h,f+h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}+\|Q^{+}(f+h,f-h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\right)dz\\
=\sup_{x_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\ \varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{2C_{l}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}x_{1}}\right)\\
\times\left(\|Q^{+}(f-h,f+h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}+\|Q^{+}(f+h,f-h)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\right)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}J_{1}+J_{2}.

By Corollary
3.3
, we have

J_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{2kC_{l}}\|(f+h)\|_{-1}\|f-h\|.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

J_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{2kC_{l}}\|(f+h)\|_{-1}\|f-h\|.

Thus, we have

\|I_{1}\|\lesssim\frac{2a_{2}}{kC_{l}}\|f-h\|,

under the assumption that
f,h\in\mathscr{A}
so that
\|f+h\|_{-1}\leq 2a_{2}
.

2. Estimate for the difference
(Af)_{1}-(Ah)_{1}
.
Since
f
and

have the same boundary profile
f|_{\gamma_{-}}=h|_{\gamma_{-}}=f_{LR}|_{\gamma_{-}}
, the difference
(Af)_{1}-(Ah)_{1}
is written as

(Af)_{1}(x_{1},p)-(Ah)_{1}(x_{1},p)\\
=1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}f_{L}(p)\left(\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}Lf(z,p)dz\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}Lh(z,p)dz\right)\right).

, Lemma

, and the same argument of (

\leq 1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}f_{L}(p)\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}e^{\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}x_{1}\right)}e^{-\frac{k}{\sqrt{2}}}(p^{0})^{1/2}\|f-h\|x_{1}\\

using

Therefore for a sufficiently large

to itself supported by Lemma

∎

By Lemma

6.

In this section, we prove that if the inflow boundary profile satisfies the

.
The proof relies crucially on the hyperplane estimate for the gain term

established in Proposition

below, together with the mild formulation
of the stationary problem.

We emphasize that, unlike

[

,

6.1.
Hyperplane integral of
Q^{+}

We now estimate the integral of the gain term
Q^{+}
on a hyperplane.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\varphi(p)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}e^{kp^{0}}
for some
k>0
.
Then we have

(6.1)

\sup_{E}\int_{E}(p^{0})^{1/2}\,\varphi(p)\,\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\,d\sigma_{E}(p)\;\lesssim\;\|f\|^{2},

where the supremum is taken over all two-dimensional planes
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
passing through the origin, and
d\sigma_{E}
denotes the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
E
.

Proof.

Let
a_{E}
be a 3-dimensional unit vector in
{\mathbb{R}^{3}}
that is perpendicular to the plane
E
.
We first write

K_{E}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\int_{E}d\sigma_{E}(p)\hskip 2.84526pt(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)Q^{+}(f,f)(x_{1},p)\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dp\hskip 2.84526pt(p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)Q^{+}(f,f)(x_{1},p)\delta(p\cdot a_{E})\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\omega\ (p^{0})^{1/2}g\sqrt{s}g\sigma_{0}(\theta)\varphi(p)f(x_{1},p^{\prime})f(x_{1},q^{\prime})\delta(p\cdot a_{E}),

using the definition
v_{\textup{\o}}=\frac{g\sqrt{s}}{p^{0}q^{0}}.
Define a 4-vector
a^{\mu}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(0,a_{E})
. Then we can write
p\cdot a_{E}=p^{\mu}a_{\mu}.
Now we consider the momentum rotation within the Lorentz transformation as in (
1.25
) such that
\Lambda a^{\mu}=(0,0,0,1)^{\top}.
Then by the invariance property (
1.24
), we have

p^{\mu}a_{\mu}=(\Lambda^{\kappa}_{\penalty\ \mu}p^{\mu})\eta_{\kappa\lambda}(\Lambda^{\lambda}_{\penalty\ \nu}a^{\nu})=(\Lambda p^{\mu})(\Lambda a_{\mu}),

by abusing the notation and writing
\Lambda p^{\kappa}=\Lambda^{\kappa}_{\penalty\ \mu}p^{\mu}
. Similarly, we have

g=g(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=(p^{\mu}-q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}-q_{\mu})=g(\Lambda p^{\mu},\Lambda q^{\mu}),

and

s=s(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})=-(p^{\mu}+q^{\mu})(p_{\mu}+q_{\mu})=s(\Lambda p^{\mu},\Lambda q^{\mu}).

Note that the measure
\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}
is invariant under this transformation. Lastly,
p^{0}
and
q^{0}
(and hence
\varphi(p)
) are also invariant under this momentum rotation. Then by denoting
the unit vector
(0,0,1)^{\top}
as

\hat{e}_{3}
, we have

K_{E}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dp}{p^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{dq}{q^{0}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}d\omega\ (p^{0})^{1/2}g\sqrt{s}g\sigma_{0}(\theta)\varphi(p)f(x_{1},p^{\prime})f(x_{1},q^{\prime})\delta(p\cdot\hat{e}_{3}),

by relabeling
\Lambda p^{\mu}
and
\Lambda q^{\mu}
as
p^{\mu}
and
q^{\mu}
.
Now we apply a pre-post collisional change of the variables
(p^{\mu},q^{\mu})\to(p^{\prime\mu},q^{\prime\mu})
and use the fact that
s
and
g
are invariant under this transformation. Then we obtain that the integral
K_{E}
is now given by

\displaystyle K_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(p^{\prime 0})^{1/2}v_{\textup{\o}}g\varphi(p^{\prime})f(p)f(q)\delta(p^{\prime}_{3})d\omega dqdp,

where we used (
1.4
) and that
\sigma_{0}(\theta)\leq M
and denoted
f(x_{1},p)
as
f(p)
for the sake of notational simplicity. Since
(p^{\prime})^{0}\leq p^{0}+q^{0}
by the energy conservation law (
1.8
) (and hence
(p^{\prime})^{0}\leq 2p^{0}q^{0}
), we have

\varphi(p^{\prime})\leq\varphi(p)\varphi(q),
and hence

(6.2)

\displaystyle K_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(p^{0})^{1/2}(q^{0})^{1/2}v_{\textup{\o}}g\varphi(p)\varphi(q)f(p)f(q)\delta(p^{\prime}_{3})d\omega dqdp.

Now by (
1.18
), we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\delta(p^{\prime}_{3})d\omega=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\delta\left(\frac{p_{3}+q_{3}}{2}+\frac{g}{2}\left(\omega_{3}+(\gamma-1)(p_{3}+q_{3})\frac{(p+q)\cdot\omega}{|p+q|^{2}}\right)\right)d\omega.

Note that

\omega_{3}+(\gamma-1)(p_{3}+q_{3})\frac{(p+q)\cdot\omega}{|p+q|^{2}}=\left((0,0,1)+(\gamma-1)(p_{3}+q_{3})\frac{(p+q)}{|p+q|^{2}}\right)\cdot\omega.

Then we consider another representation for
\omega
using the spherical coordinates such that
z
-axis is especially chosen to be parallel to the vector

Z\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\left((0,0,1)+(\gamma-1)(p_{3}+q_{3})\frac{(p+q)}{|p+q|^{2}}\right)

such that
\omega=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)
and

\cos\theta=\frac{Z}{|Z|}\cdot\omega.

Then the integral can be rewritten as

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\delta(p^{\prime}_{3})d\omega=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\ \sin\theta\ \delta\left(\frac{p_{3}+q_{3}}{2}+\frac{g}{2}|Z|\cos\theta\right)\\
=\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\int_{-1}^{1}dk\ \delta\left(\frac{p_{3}+q_{3}}{2}+\frac{g}{2}|Z|k\right)=\frac{4\pi}{g|Z|}\int_{-1}^{1}dk\ \delta\left(k+\frac{p_{3}+q_{3}}{g|Z|}\right),

where we made the change of variables
\theta\mapsto k\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\cos\theta,
and used

\delta(Ak+B)=\frac{1}{|A|}\delta\left(k+\frac{B}{A}\right).

Note that

(6.3)

\frac{|p_{3}+q_{3}|}{g|Z|}=\frac{|p_{3}+q_{3}|}{g\left|(0,0,1)+(\gamma-1)(p_{3}+q_{3})\frac{(p+q)}{|p+q|^{2}}\right|}=\frac{|p_{3}+q_{3}|}{g\left|(0,0,1)+\frac{(p_{3}+q_{3})(p+q)}{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}\right|}\\
=\frac{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})|p_{3}+q_{3}|}{g\left|(0,0,\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s}))+(p_{3}+q_{3})(p+q)\right|}\\
=\frac{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})|p_{3}+q_{3}|}{g\sqrt{(p_{3}+q_{3})^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}(p_{i}+q_{j})^{2}\right)+[\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})+(p_{3}+q_{3})^{2}]^{2}}}\\
=\frac{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}{g\sqrt{(p_{1}+q_{1})^{2}+(p_{2}+q_{2})^{2}+[\frac{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}{|p_{3}+q_{3}|}+|p_{3}+q_{3}|]^{2}}},

where the second identity is by (
1.19
). Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{g|Z|}=\frac{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}{g\sqrt{(p_{3}+q_{3})^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}(p_{i}+q_{j})^{2}\right)+[\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})+|p_{3}+q_{3}|^{2}]^{2}}}.

Then by computing the delta function in the integral with respect to
k,
we have

\displaystyle K_{E}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(p^{0})^{1/2}(q^{0})^{1/2}v_{\textup{\o}}g\varphi(p)\varphi(q)f(p)f(q)\frac{4\pi}{g|Z|}dqdp

\displaystyle\approx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dqdp\ \frac{g\sqrt{s}}{p^{0}q^{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{(p^{0})^{1/2}(q^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\varphi(q)f(p)f(q)\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}{\sqrt{(p_{3}+q_{3})^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2}(p_{i}+q_{j})^{2}\right)+[\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})+|p_{3}+q_{3}|^{2}]^{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dqdp\ \frac{g\sqrt{s}}{p^{0}q^{0}}\frac{(p^{0})^{1/2}(q^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\varphi(q)f(p)f(q)\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}{\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}dqdp\ (p^{0})^{1/2}(q^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)\varphi(q)f(p)f(q)\lesssim\|f\|^{2},

by (
1.19
), (
6.2
), (
6.3
), and the fact that
\sqrt{s}(p^{0}+q^{0}+\sqrt{s})
is positive and

g^{2},s\lesssim p^{0}q^{0}
. By taking ess
\sup
over
x_{1}\in[0,1]
and
\sup
over all planes

passing through 0, we complete the proof of Proposition

.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

In the proof above, one may ask whether the bound

\left|\frac{p_{3}+q_{3}}{g\,|Z|}\right|\leq 1

always holds. In general, this quantity can become arbitrarily large as
p
approaches
q
.
Indeed, in this regime we have
g\to 0
, and the numerator
p_{3}+q_{3}
does not compensate for this singularity unless
p_{3}+q_{3}=0
.

In such cases, the corresponding delta-function integral in

vanishes identically, while the stated upper bound remains valid.
This situation corresponds precisely to the case
p_{3}^{\prime}\neq 0
.

Proof of Theorem

We recall the mild form representation (

(6.4)

\begin{split}(f)_{1}(x_{1},p)&\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}f_{L}(p)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}Lf(z,p)dz\right)\\

\times Q^{+}(f,f)(z,p)dz\\
\leq\sup_{E}\int_{E}d\sigma_{E}(p)\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\\
\times\sup_{x_{1}}\int_{0}^{x_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}(x_{1}-z)\right)dz\\
=\sup_{E}\int_{E}d\sigma_{E}(p)\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\\
\times\sup_{x_{1}}\frac{\hat{p}_{1}}{C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\hat{p}_{1}}C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}x_{1}\right)\right)\\
\leq\sup_{E}\int_{E}d\sigma_{E}(p)\ (p^{0})^{1/2}\varphi(p)1_{\{p_{1}>0\}}\frac{1}{C_{l}(p^{0})^{1/2}}\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\\
\leq\frac{1}{C_{l}}\sup_{E}\int_{E}d\sigma_{E}(p)\ \varphi(p)\|Q^{+}(f,f)(\cdot,p)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x_{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{C_{l}}\|f\|^{2}\leq\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{C_{l}},

by Proposition
6.1
and Proposition
2.1
. Here, the constants
a_{1}
and
C_{l}
are the same as those in the definition of the solution space
\mathscr{A}
defined in (
4.1
).

Altogether, given
a_{1}
,
M
and
C_{l}
, we obtain
\|f\|_{\textup{hyp}}<+\infty.
This completes the proof of the hyperplane estimate and hence concludes the
proof of the corresponding part of Theorem
1.1
.

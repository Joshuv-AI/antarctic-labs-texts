Total Knee Arthroplasty in Alkaptonuric Patient With Ochronotic Arthropathy
Authors: Patryk Michalak, Piotr Buchcic, Marek Drobniewski, Andrzej Borowski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:We present a patient with ochronotic arthropathy admitted to perform total knee arthroplasty (TKA) of the right knee.Case Presentation:The patient admitted to the orthopaedic department presented with pain in multiple joints, particularly affecting the knees, hips, and shoulders.Intervention:Preoperatively, the patient underwent meticulous preparation for the procedure in consultation with the anesthesiology team. During the surgery, we observed black bones and menisci in the joint.Outcome:The patient felt a massive relief after TKA. At 1-month follow-up, the patient reported a satisfying range of motion of the knee.Conclusions:Our findings suggest that TKA may represent an effective therapeutic option for patients with alkaptonuria and degenerative joint disease because it may provide substantial pain relief and improve joint function.

Alkaptonuria, also known as black bone disease, is a rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by a deficiency of the enzyme homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase (HGD). Alkaptonuria leads to the accumulation of homogentisic acid in the body, which can deposit in connective tissues, cartilage, and bones, leading to a condition called ochronosis. Over time, these deposits cause chronic damage, particularly in joints, resulting in a form of degenerative arthritis. Symptoms often become apparent in the 30s or 40s. The patient may have been asymptomatic in childhood, except for one notable sign: dark urine. Major reports are chronic joint pain, especially in weight-bearing joints such as the hips, knees, and back. Progressive stiffness and decreased range of motion with fatigue and difficulty performing daily tasks due to joint discomfort are also very common.1

Case Report

A 67-year-old male patient was admitted to our orthopaedic department for total knee arthroplasty (TKA) due to right knee pain. He reported that the symptoms had been present for several years. In addition, the patient reported pain and a decreased range of motion in the right shoulder, right knee, and both hip joints. He also reported of back pain and an inability to lie in a supine position with his back fully extended (Figure 1, A and B).

AP and lateral radiographs of the degenerated lumbosacral section of the spine and bamboo stick sign.

His symptoms were increasing year by year. Alkaptonuria was diagnosed in 2011. Between 2014 and 2018, he was being treated with nitisinone which is still undergoing clinical trials. Because of the high pain, the patient is under the care of the pain clinic. The patient had no notable comorbidities. His body mass index was 31, and the pain intensity was rated as nine on the Numeric Rating Scale. During the physical examination, he presented knee flexion contraction of 10° with a flexion up to 90° with a stable knee. The patient's Knee Society Score was 0. A knee radiograph that was taken in AP and lateral projections showed severe degenerative changes in the right knee (Figure 2, A and B).

AP and lateral radiographs of the knee with advanced osteoarthritis and significant narrowing of the joint line

Moreover, MRI of the right knee revealed injuries to both menisci and notable damage to the articular cartilage of both the femoral and tibial condyles. Spinal anesthesia was administered by the anesthesiology team. TKA of the right knee joint was done using a hemostatic tourniquet before implantation of the prosthetic components.

After joint exposure through an anterior approach with medial parapatellar arthrotomy, black discoloration of the cartilage of the tibial plateau, femoral condyles, and menisci was observed (Figure 3, A and B). Samples of the blackened anatomical structures were collected and sent to the anatomic pathology laboratory for evaluation. Histopathologic examination of the black tissues of our patient confirmed the presence of ochronosis.

Images showing black structures of the knee joint such as menisci observed during the surgery. The articular cartilage is severely damaged.

Standard bone resections were done, and prosthetic components (Attune CR, DePuy Synthes, Johnson & Johnson) were cemented. The procedure lasted approximately 1 hour, with an estimated blood loss of 500 mL. No intraoperative difficulties related to the ochronotic tissue were encountered. On the second postoperative day, radiographic examination confirmed correct positioning of the prosthetic components (Figure 4, A and B).

AP and lateral postoperative radiographs of the knee taken after 2-day postsurgery with proper placement of prosthetic components.

The patient was subsequently referred to a rehabilitation center. At the 2-week follow-up visit, radiographic assessment demonstrated proper implant positioning and satisfactory femorotibial alignment. The postoperative course was uneventful. At the 1-month follow-up, the patient presented with a good range of motion and no right knee pain. However, quadriceps muscle weakness and medial collateral ligament instability were noted; therefore, quadriceps strengthening exercises and a stabilizing orthosis for the medial collateral ligament were recommended. Plain radiographs revealed no abnormalities of the prosthetic components.

The patient reported no gait disturbances or knee pain. Examination of the right knee demonstrated a range of motion with flexion up to 110° and full extension. Six months after the procedure, the patient achieved a score of 70 points on the Knee Society Score, with a visible improvement in quadriceps femoris muscle bulk. The patient was advised to attend annual follow-up visits at the orthopaedic outpatient clinic.

Discussion

Alkaptonuria is a hereditary disorder transmitted by an autosomal recessive gene. However, on rare occasion, it is autosomal dominant. Scribonius, the first to describe alkaptonuria, in 1584, reported the case of a young boy whose urine turned black when exposed to the air.2

Ochronosis is a phenomenon first described by Virchow in 1866 in which specific tissues turn brownish-yellow. Alkaptonuria is an ultra‐rare metabolic disease due to a deficient activity of the enzyme homogentisate 1,2‐dioxygenase (HGD) leading to accumulation of homogentisic acid. Ochronosis is a musculoskeletal manifestation of alkaptonuria. A severe form of arthropathy, with articular cartilage degeneration, is the most common clinical presentation of alkaptonuria.3-5

The most common presentations of alkaptonuria include darkening of urine with exposure to air or reducing agents. Additional manifestations are cardiovascular abnormalities; renal, urethral, and prostate calculi; and scleral and ear involvement.6,7

Ochronotic arthropathy occurs in patients with alkaptonuria, manifesting first in the intervertebral disks of the lumbar spine, with subsequent degeneration most often observed in the knee, hip, and shoulder joints. Early involvement of the intervertebral disks at the thoracic and lumbar levels is very common. Typically, notable back pain begins from 30 years of age. Achilles tendon involvement is also common and may result in tearing.8

The knee is most commonly affected by ochronotic arthropathy, with symptoms estimated to appear in 64% of cases, and it is typically detected in patients aged 40 to 50 years. Patients may present with pain, stiffness, or decreased mobility in the knee. In approximately 50% of cases, synovial effusion due to inflammation of the cartilage may also be present. Ochronotic arthropathy may not be suspected until darkened synovium, and cartilage are observed intraoperatively during joint arthroplasty surgery. Similarly, in our case, diagnosis was made intraoperatively.8

Vitamin C, as much as 1 g/day, is recommended for older children and adults. The mild antioxidant nature of ascorbic acid helps retard the process of conversion of homogentisate to the polymeric material that is deposited in cartilaginous tissues. However, in our case, the patient has never heard about that kind of treatment.9

Because of the rarity of the disease and paucity of clinical symptoms until middle age, the diagnosis of alkaptonuria is occasionally made just intraoperatively. In one publication, surgeons encountered black tissues during a routine TKA and were unaware that the patient suffered from alkaptonuria. In our case, the diagnosis had been established preoperatively by a physician specializing in rare diseases.10

Nitisinone is the first pharmacotherapeutic agent that seems to prevent ochronosis, although studies are required to assess its efficacy and possible adverse effects. However, our patient did not observe any relief.11

In a meta-analysis, cemented TKA offered a better survival rate compared with noncemented TKA. Furthermore, cemented TKA offers advantages such as easier technique ensuring greater primary stability and delivery of local antibiotics.12

However, a critical review of 13 case reports found no difference between noncemented and cemented TKA in the treatment of ochronotic arthropathy. In our case, we conducted cemented TKA because of the fragility of the tibia and femur bones.13

The black cartilage and softened bone does not heal differently from the normal bone; therefore, internal fixation of the fractures and noncemented fixation of the prosthesis will not fail. Caution should be paid for excessive bleeding after total synovectomy. As far as we were concerned, no excessive bleeding was observed after synovectomy in our patient.14

Spencer et al15 conducted arthroplasties on 11 joints in three patients with ochronosis affecting both the upper and lower extremities and reported favorable outcomes at 12-year follow-up. Unfortunately, no TKA has been documented in that study. To date, the prosthetic components in our patient have remained stable, with no signs of loosening. However, the follow-up period is too short to allow a reliable assessment of long-term TKA stability.15

Orthopaedic surgeons should be aware that polyarthritis may, in some cases, be a manifestation of alkaptonuria. Therefore, arthroplasty plays a crucial role in management, particularly in patients presenting to pain clinics, as in the case described in this report.

Conclusion

Orthopaedic surgeons should remain vigilant for atypical arthropathies to avoid unexpected intraoperative findings, such as the presence of darkened cartilage. We believe that TKA is a safe and effective procedure for patients with severe joint pain because it provides pain relief and restores joint function.

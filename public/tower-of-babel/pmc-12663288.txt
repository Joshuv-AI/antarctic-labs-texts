Comparison of clinical outcomes and predictive model analysis between short-term culture and overnight culture after embryo recovery at cleavage stage
Authors: Liangkun Zeng, Tiantian Zhang, Zengrong Tu, Xiaoyun Liu, Wei Li, Caixia Yuan, Jianrong Liu, Xiaowei Qin, Qin Qin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated pregnancy outcomes in patients undergoing Frozen-Thawed Embryo Transfer (FET) with short-term culture (SC) versus overnight culture (OC) and developed a nomogram prediction model for clinical pregnancy. A retrospective analysis of 1962 FET cycles from January 2016 to October 2023 was conducted. Propensity score matching (PSM) was used to balance baseline characteristics between SC and OC groups. Results showed that the SC group had higher biochemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, and implantation rates compared to the OC group, but no difference in live birth rates due to a higher early abortion rate in the SC group. Logistic regression identified infertility years, female age, progesterone (P) levels, number of high-quality embryos, and culture time as independent factors influencing clinical pregnancy. A nomogram model was constructed and demonstrated moderate predictive ability (AUC = 0.623). Calibration curves indicated good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities. Decision curve analysis (DCA) and clinical impact curves (CIC) confirmed the model’s clinical utility, particularly at threshold probabilities of 0.16–0.42. These findings provide valuable references for optimizing FET strategies and predicting clinical pregnancy outcomes.Keywords: Culture time, Cleavage embryos, Pregnancy outcomes, Frozen-thawed embryo transfer, Predictive modelSubject terms: Preclinical research, Embryology

Introduction

Infertility, defined as the inability to achieve pregnancy after 12 months of regular, unprotected intercourse, affects approximately 10–15% of reproductive-age couples worldwide1. A complex medical condition, infertility can stem from a range of anatomical, endocrine, genetic, immunological, and lifestyle-related factors, necessitating a multifaceted approach to treatment. In vitro fertilization (IVF) has significantly advanced the management of infertility by providing a pathway to parenthood for many who might otherwise struggle to conceive naturally2. A pivotal component of IVF was Frozen-Thawed Embryo Transfer (FET), which involved the thawing and transfer of previously cryopreserved embryos into the uterus. One advantage of FET was that it allows clinicians to better control transplant timing and environment, meaning that doctors can choose to perform the transplant when the endometrium’s state is optimal, thereby improving the pregnancy rate3,4. FET also offer advantages such as improving synchronization between the embryo and endometrium, reducing the risk of ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, and increasing treatment plan flexibility5–7. Despite the benefits of FET, its success was influenced by numerous variables, among which post-thaw embryo culture duration prior to transfer is critical8.

Embryo culture post-thawing can be classified into two main strategies: short-term culture (2–4 h) and overnight culture (18–20 h). Short-term culture minimized the time embryos were exposed to in vitro conditions, potentially reducing the impact of external factors on culture results. On the other hand, overnight culture extended its duration, allowing for a more comprehensive assessment of the embryo’s developmental potential and quality and offers logistical advantages for laboratory scheduling9. Both methods were designed to optimize embryo viability and implantation potential, but their respective efficacy remained under debate. Several studies juxtaposed the outcomes of short-term versus overnight embryo culture in FET, with evaluation metrics that included clinical pregnancy, live birth, and miscarriage rates. Some scholars believe that extending embryo culture duration improved embryo implantation and pregnancy rates, potentially enhanced perinatal outcomes, and facilitated further blastocyst development, thereby augmenting their potential for implantation10,11. However, other studies reported that the two culture methods do not significantly affect clinical pregnancy outcomes9,12. A meta-analysis indicated that longer or shorter culture times may have adverse effects on pregnancy rates and that the optimal culture time range could depend on laboratory conditions, embryo quality, and other factors8. However, these studies often encountered limitations such as small sample sizes, variable patient characteristics, and heterogeneous methodologies.

In summary, this study aimed to investigate the clinical outcomes of short-term and overnight culture methods after embryo thawing. Its results may provide valuable guidance for clinicians when selecting personalized infertility treatment plans using FET.

Construction and validation of the predictive model for nomogram

Independent risk factors affecting clinical pregnancy were revealed by logistic regression analysis. Based on this, we constructed a nomogram prediction model (Fig. 1A). Independent risk factors for clinical pregnancy were on the left side of the graph, the value of each independent risk factor corresponds to a single point, each single point added up to total point which is used to determine the exact probability of clinical pregnancy. This figure provides patients and clinicians with a more intuitive and fully individualized representation of the differences in clinical pregnancy probabilities.

Construction and internal of the predictive model for clinical pregnancy rete. (A) The nomogram exhibited five characteristics of a patient (Group=OC, Number of high-quality embryos=one, P=0.39, Infertility years = 3.33, Female age=24, with a total score 286 points, and predicted probability of clinical pregnancy was 52.1%). (B): The area under the curve of the nomogram model was 0.623(95%CI:0.598-0.647). (C): The calibration curve analysis was performed using the bootsrap method with 1000 internal sampling iterations, and the results demonstrated the excellent performance of the model, with the two curves nearly overlapping and showing high consistency.

To evaluate the effect of the nomogram model, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve exhibited an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.623 (95%CI:0.598–0.647) in the nomogram model, which have showed the stability of the model (Fig. 1B). Besides, apparent line and bias-corrected were basically consistent with the ideal line in the calibration curve, which exhibited good consistency between predicted and actual results (Fig. 1C).

Clinical applicability of the nomogram

Decision curve analysis (DCA) of the model showed that the highest net benefit was obtained within the threshold probability range of 0.16 to 0.42 (Fig. 2), where the predicted number of clinical pregnancies surpassed the actual number. Clinical impact curves (CIC) results indicated that for thresholds above approximately 0.5, the model’s predictions exhibited a high degree of alignment with actual events, reflecting strong clinical predictive performance (Fig. 3).

Discussion

According to the statistics reported by the European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology, FET accounted for 36% of all embryo transfers in 201913, and has seen significant development and widespread adoption in the field of assisted reproductive technology over the past decade due to its numerous advantages14–16. During the FET process, embryologists continually explore methods to enhance embryonic implantation potential by selecting those with high morphological scores for thawing, optimizing the in vitro culture environment, and investigating the optimal post-thaw in vitro culture duration17–19. Debate persists regarding the appropriate culture duration following embryo revitalization. Some studies suggested that there was no statistically significant difference in clinical pregnancy rates and implantation rates between fresh transfers made on the day of thawing and those made after overnight culture20,21. However, some researchers argued that shortening the post-thawing in vitro culture duration could reduce culture-related stress and maintain the embryonic developmental potential22. Numerous studies suggested that extending the in vitro culture duration could reduce the abortion rate and enhance the live birth rate19,23.

This retrospective study compared the clinical outcomes of two groups after PSM and found that the SC group had higher biochemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy and implantation rates than the OC group. A stratified analysis of female age, number of embryos transferred, and number of high-quality embryos revealed that the clinical pregnancy rate was higher in the SC versus OC group for patients younger than 35 years with two embryos and zero or one high-quality embryo, consistent with the findings of Guo and Wang22,24.

There were several possible reasons for this outcome. First, after PSM, only infertility factors remained significantly different between two groups. Infertility in couples is influenced by a multitude of complex and interrelated factors, which may include advanced maternal age, declining semen quality in men, as well as lifestyle habits and environmental exposures in both partners25,26. Nevertheless, there is no robust evidence in the existing literature to suggest that either female-related or male-related factors play a predominant role in the etiology of infertility. Both univariate and multivariate regression analyses identified female age, infertility years, P, number of high-quality embryos, different culture time were independent risk factor affecting clinical pregnancy rate. Advanced maternal age is associated with a decline in ovarian function, ovarian reserve, and oocyte quality, which consequently impairs embryonic development and implantation potential, ultimately leading to pregnancy loss27. Studies have shown that once women exceed 35 years of age, the success rate of embryo transplantation decreases significantly28. As the duration of infertility increases, couples are typically of advanced age, and various physiological parameters may deviate from the optimal range for conception29.Progesterone, a hormone secreted by tissues such as the ovaries and placenta, plays a crucial role in regulating reproductive system function and maintaining a pregnancy. Studies have shown that once the progesterone level exceeded 0.8 ng/mL, an increase was significantly negatively correlated with pregnancy outcomes30,31.

Moreover, Endometrial preparation protocol is critical in FET cycles. Although no significant differences were observed in the endometrial preparation methods between the two groups after PSM, its potential influence on outcomes warrants careful consideration. Consensus is lacking on the selection of endometrial preparation protocols for FET. The choice of endometrial preparation was mainly based on the patient’s own characteristics and actual condition32,33. Liu et al. suggested natural cycles as the preferred FET method34. Finally, the pregnancy rate was higher in patients who received two transferred embryos versus just a single embryo.

In addition, the in vitro embryonic culture duration was the main difference between the two groups and an important consideration affecting the clinical pregnancy rate. The in vitro culture environment was crucial to embryonic development. Although the in vitro embryonic culture simulated the fallopian tube and uterine environments, some objective factors, such as temperature, pH, and osmotic pressure, could have negatively impacted embryonic development35. Stress and adaptation to the in vitro environment could also affect embryonic development, leading to pregnancy loss36. Moreover, the in vitro culturing could affect the embryo’s ability to repair DNA damage and change the expression levels of certain genes involved in oxidative stress and lipid metabolism37,38. Some articles specifically reported that in vitro culture might lead to embryonic developmental arrest, resulting in a lack of transferable embryos and cycle cancellation. These embryos were more likely to survive within the physiological environment of the uterus39,40. Therefore, a prolonged in vitro culture time could adversely affect the embryo, which in turn could affect patients’ clinical outcomes. Thus, minimizing the in vitro culture duration was considered the optimal strategy.

Furthermore, two studies reported that embryos that underwent further division after overnight culture achieved higher implantation rates41,42. However, we did not assess cellular proliferation in the OC group in this study, as the developmental status of embryos implanted earlier in the SC group remained unknown. It is noteworthy that, although the SC group had higher biochemical pregnancy, clinical pregnancy, and implantation rates than the OC group, the live birth rates did not differ significantly between them. Although the live birth rate (37.63% vs. 33.53%) was numerically higher in the SC group compared to the OC group, the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). Future studies are warranted to further investigate potential differences in live birth rates between the two groups.

The nomogram model is a statistical tool that is used to predict and evaluate clinical outcomes by integrating multiple predictive indicators into a single visual graphic that makes the results of the predictive model easier to understand and apply43. The nomogram model for clinical pregnancy in FET constructed here provides clinicians with a more intuitive tool to assess the probability of pregnancy under different culture strategies, enhancing the personalization and scientific rigor of clinical decision-making. This information allows the more precise tailoring of treatment plans to meet individual patient differences. We evaluated the model’s performance using an ROC curve, and the AUC of 0.623 indicated its general efficiency of the model. Given that the AUC of the model lacked strong persuasiveness, a calibration curve was further constructed to evaluate its performance, which demonstrated the model’s excellent performance.

This study had some limitations. First, given that it was a retrospective cohort study, the obtained data were not from a single source and might be biased. Second, the suboptimal discriminative ability of the nomogram, as indicated by its AUC, necessitates the implementation of more stringent patient selection criteria in future studies for both model development and validation.

Conclusion

The comparison of clinical outcomes between the SC group and the OC group revealed that short-term culture possessed superior clinical results compared to overnight culture. Consequently, we hold the opinion that the maternal environment is more conducive to embryonic development than the external environment. Future research should aim to corroborate these preliminary observations and better elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Materials and methods

Study participants

This study was retrospectively analyzed patients who underwent FET at the Department of Reproductive Medicine, Shanxi Provincial People’s Hospital and the First Hospital of Shanxi Medical University from January 2016 to October 2023. All study participants who underwent short-term culture and overnight culture were divided into the SC group and the OC group. Correspondingly, the selection of the two culture methods was also determined by the laboratory work schedule at that time and the actual circumstances of the patients (If the transplantation procedure is scheduled on a Sunday, Monday, or a public holiday, the patient will be enrolled in the SC group. If the transplantation is scheduled from Tuesday to Saturday, the patient will be enrolled in the OC group). This research has passed the review of the Shanxi Provincial People’s Hospital Ethics Committee review.

Inclusion criteria for the study subjects were: (1) Patients undergoing frozen-thawed embryo transfer; (2) Only the first transplantation cycle of the patient was selected; (3) The number of transplanted embryos was 1–2. Exclusion criteria were: (1) Cycles other than the revival cycle; (2) More than two embryos transplanted; (3) Chromosomal abnormalities in the patient or spouse; (4) Repeated implantation failures; (5) Incomplete or missing data.

Ovarian stimulation protocol and IVF/ICSI

Ovarian stimulation was tailored according to the individual characteristics of patients, employing one of three protocols: gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist long protocol, GnRH antagonist protocol, or mild stimulation protocol. The follicle size and number were dynamically monitored via transvaginal ultrasound, and the dose of ovulation induction drugs (100–450U) was adjusted in conjunction with serum hormone levels. When at least two follicles reached a diameter of 18 mm, 6000–10,000 IU Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (HCG) was administered to induce final oocyte maturation. Oocyte retrieval was performed 36–38 h after HCG administration under transvaginal ultrasound guidance. On the day of oocyte retrieval, semen was obtained either through masturbation by the male partner or by thawing frozen sperm. Fertilization was then carried out using either conventional in vitro fertilization or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) based on the patient’s medical condition, and the culture dishes were incubated at 37 °C in an atmosphere of 6% CO2 and 5% O2.

Endometrial preparation protocols

Natural cycle (NC) protocol: For patients with regular menstrual cycles, transvaginal ultrasonography was performed on the 8th to 10th day of the menstrual cycle to monitor follicular development and endometrial thickness. If the endometrial thickness was found to be thin, oral treatment with estradiol valerate (Bayer, Germany) was administered as appropriate. When the endometrial thickness reached 8 mm and the follicle diameter reached 16–18 mm, an injection of 6000–10,000 IU of HCG was given on the same day. Starting from the day after ovulation, intramuscular injections of progesterone (Organon Pharmaceuticals, Netherlands, 20 mg per dose, once a day) was administered to alter the endometrium, On the third day of ovulation, cleavage-stage embryos were transferred.

Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) protocols: For patients with irregular menstrual cycles, complex packing estradiol tablets (4–6 mg once daily) was taken starting from the 2nd to 3rd day of the menstrual period. The oral dosage could be adjusted based on the endometrial thickness. Once the endometrial thickness reached 8 mm, intramuscular injections of progesterone (40 mg once a daily) was administered to transform the endometrium. On the fifth day of transformation, cleavage-stage embryos were transferred.

Embryo cryopreservation and thawing

The freezing and thawing processes of embryos were conducted at a laboratory constant temperature of 22–24 °C. Embryos were frozen using the vitrification method with a vitrification kit (Kitazato, Japan). On Day 3, embryos were transferred from the cleavage culture medium to an equilibration solution and incubated for 5–8 min, then moved to the vitrification solution for less than 1 min, placed on a Cryotop carrier, and immediately plunged into liquid nitrogen. The exposure time in the vitrification solution before plunging into liquid nitrogen did not exceed 1 min.

During thawing, the Cryotop containing the preserved embryo was removed from liquid nitrogen and immediately placed into a 37 °C thawing solution for 1 min. The embryo was then transferred to 0.5 ml of dilution solution for 4 min, followed by transfer to a washing solution 1 for 5 min, and finally to washing solution 2 for another 2 min. Heating was performed following the instructions provided in the thawing kit (Kitazato, Japan). After heating, each embryo was transferred individually to 20-µl of cleavage culture medium (1026, Quinn’s, American). The viability of the embryonic blastomeres were observed under an inverted microscope, and the embryos were cultured for a certain period. In the SC group, embryos were thawed at 9:00 AM and subsequently transferred at approximately 11:00 AM on the same day, following a post-warming culture period of 2–4 h. In the OC group, thawing was performed at 4:00 PM, and embryo transfer was conducted at around 11:00 AM the following day, after an extended culture duration of approximately 18–20 h.

Cleavage-stage embryo grading

Fertilized oocytes were cultured individually in G-1 Plus medium (Vitro-life, Sweden) for 3 days. Embryos were then graded based on regularity of blastomeres, cell number, and fragmentation extent by two seasoned embryologists44. The grading criteria were as follows: Grade I: Even blastomeres and cytoplasm, no vacuoles, fragmentation < 10%; Grade II: Even or slightly uneven blastomeres, even cytoplasm, no vacuoles, fragmentation < 20%; Grade III: Significantly uneven blastomeres or irregular shapes, noticeable granularity in the cytoplasm, fragmentation < 35%. Grade IV: Virtually no blastomeres of any size present in the embryo, with severe fragmentation coverage. Grade I or II embryos were considered high-quality embryos.

Luteal phase support and confirmation of pregnancy

Under ultrasound guidance, a maximum of two embryos were transferred to the uterus. Starting from the day of embryo transfer, progesterone was administered intramuscularly (40 mg, once a day) and orally (100 mg, twice a day). Serum HCG levels were measured two weeks after embryo transfer to confirm pregnancy. A B-ultrasound examination was performed at four weeks post-transfer to confirm an intrauterine pregnancy.

Main outcome of the observation

The primary outcome observed in this study was clinical pregnancy, defined as the presence of one or more gestational sacs on ultrasonography or definitive clinical signs of pregnancy. Live birth was defined as the birth of at least one living child after 22 weeks of gestation. Additionally, there were several secondary outcome measures, including biochemical pregnancy rate, ectopic pregnancy rate, early miscarriage rate, single pregnancy rate, and multiple pregnancy rate.

Statistical analysis

The data obtained were statistically analyzed using SPSS 27.0 and R (version R4.4.1) statistical software. Based on data from the Reproductive Medicine Branch of the Chinese Medical Association (2017–2021) and aiming to detect an absolute difference of 10% points in clinical pregnancy rate between the two groups, with a two-sided significance level of 0.05, a power of 90%, and an assumed clinical pregnancy rate of 50%, a total sample size of approximately 1150 participants (575 per group) was calculated. Metric data that followed a normal distribution were represented as mean ± standard deviation (x̅ ± σ), while those not following a normal distribution were described using the median and interquartile range [M (P25, P75)] to depict the central tendency and dispersion of the data; count data were expressed in terms of frequency and rate. For metric data conforming to a normal distribution, an independent samples t-test was used, while the Rank Sum Test was used to determine the statistical significance of comparison differences for measures that did not meet the normal distribution requirements; chi-square tests were applied to count data. If the theoretical frequency was less than 5 but greater than or equal to 1, and the total sample size was greater than or equal to 40, a continuity-corrected chi-square test was required. If there were theoretical frequencies less than 1 or the total sample size was less than 40, a Fisher’s exact test was used. PSM was performed to match baseline data in a 1:1 ratio using a nearest-neighbor algorithm without replacement and a caliper width of 0.02. The matching variables included female age, male age, infertility years, female BMI, basal FSH, LH, P, endometrial thickness, infertility type, infertility factor, endometrial preparation protocols, number of high-quality embryos, and number of embryos transferred. This study utilized both univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses to identify independent risk factors affecting clinical pregnancy rates and live birth rates. Additionally, patients were stratified based on age, number of embryos transferred, and number of high-quality embryos to compare the differences in clinical pregnancy rates and live birth rates between the SC group and the OC group.

Construction and evaluation of nomogram models

A nomogram prediction model for clinical pregnancy rates was constructed using the results from a multivariable logistic regression model analysis in R language. The model’s fit was evaluated using the ROC curve, and internal validation of the model was performed. A calibration curve analysis was conducted using the bootstrap method with 1000 internal sampling iterations to assess the consistency between actual and predicted probabilities. The clinical efficacy was evaluated using DCA and CIC. The statistical significance was identified by applying a two-sided significance criterion of 0.05.

The square of every subcubic planar graph without 4-cycles and 5-cycles is 7-choosable
Ligang Jin, Yingli Kang, Seog-Jin Kim

Abstract
The square of a graph G G , denoted by G 2 G^{2} , has the same vertex set as G G and has an edge between two vertices if the distance between them in G G is at most 2 2 . Thomassen (2018) and independently, Hartke, Jahanbekam and Thomas (2016) proved that χ ⁡ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi(G^{2})\leq 7 if G G is a subcubic planar graph. A natural question is whether χ ℓ ​ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7 or not if G G is a subcubic planar graph. Recently, Kim and Lian (2024) proved that χ ℓ ​ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7 if G G is a subcubic planar graph of girth at least 6. In this paper, we prove that χ ℓ ​ ( G 2 ) ≤ 7 \chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7 if G G is a subcubic planar graph without 4-cycles and 5-cycles, which improves the result of Kim and Lian.



Key words: List chromatic number; square of graph; subcubic planar graph; girth

Introduction

The
square
of a graph
G
, denoted by
G^{2}
, has the
same vertex set as
G
and has an edge between two vertices if the distance between
them in
G
is at most
2
.
We say that a graph

is

if
\Delta(G)\leq 3
, where
\Delta(G)
is the maximum degree in
G
. The
girth
of a graph
G
, denoted by
g(G)
, is the size of the smallest cycle in
G
. A cycle of length
k
is called a
k
-cycle
.
Let
\chi(G)
denote the chromatic number of a graph
G
.

Given a graph
G
, finding the chromatic number of the square of
G
,
\chi(G^{2})
, is a main research topic in the study of colorings of the square of graphs.
On coloring squares of graphs, one of the most famous problems is Wegner’s conjecture, which states as follows.

Conjecture 1

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let
G
be a planar graph. The chromatic number

\chi(G^{2})
of
G^{2}
is at most
7
if
\Delta(G)=3
,
at most
\Delta(G)+5
if
4\leq\Delta(G)\leq 7
, and at most
\lfloor\frac{3\Delta(G)}{2}\rfloor
if
\Delta(G)\geq 8
.

Conjecture
1
has received a lot of attention, but it is still widely open. The only case for which we know tight bound is when
\Delta(G)=3
. Thomassen
[
14
]
showed that
\chi(G^{2})\leq 7
if
G
is a planar graph with
\Delta(G)=3
, that is, Conjecture
1
is true for
\Delta(G)=3
. Conjecture
1
for
\Delta(G)=3
was also confirmed by
Hartke, Jahanbekam and Thomas
[
7
]
with extensive computer case-checking.

With conditions on
\Delta(G)
, several upper bounds of
\chi(G^{2})
were obtained.
Bousquet, Deschamps, Meyer and Pierron
[
3
]
showed that
\chi(G^{2})\leq 12
if
G
is a planar graph with
\Delta(G)\leq 4
.
For
\Delta(G)\leq 5
, recently the upper bounds on
\chi(G^{2})
were improved in
[
1
,
6
,
16
]
, and now the best known upper bound was obtained in
[
6
]
.
Deniz
[
6
]
showed that
\chi(G^{2})\leq 16
if
G
is a planar graph with
\Delta(G)\leq 5
. Moreover, Bousquet, Deschamps, Meyer and Pierron
[
2
]
showed that
\chi(G^{2})\leq 2\Delta(G)+7
if
G
is a planar graph with

6\leq\Delta(G)\leq 31
.
The best known upper bound for general
\Delta(G)
is that
\chi(G^{2})\leq\lceil\frac{5\Delta(G)}{3}\rceil+78
by Molloy and Salavatipour
[
13
]
. On the other hand, Havet, van den Heuvel, McDiarmid, and Reed
[

]
proved that

holds asymptotically.
One may see a detailed story on the study of Wegner’s conjecture in
[
4
]
.

A list assignment for a graph is a function

available colors. A graph is

Since it was known in
[

]
that
\chi(G^{2})\leq 7
if

is a subcubic planar graph, the following natural question was raised in

]
and
[

Question 2

]
)

Is it true that

Question

for every graph

However, the List Square Coloring Conjecture was disproved in

]

Note that a positive result for the List Square Coloring Conjecture for a special class of graphs is still interesting.
It was conjectured in
[

]
that
\chi_{\ell}(G^{2})=\chi(G^{2})

So, it would be interesting to answer Question

But, considering the result of Thomassen in
[

|L_{Q_{2}}(v_{1})|\geq 3,|L_{Q_{2}}(v_{2})|\geq 3,|L_{Q_{2}}(v_{3})|\geq 4,|L_{Q_{2}}(v_{4})|\geq 3,|L_{Q_{2}}(w)|\geq 3.

In this case, color
v_{3}
by a color
c\in L_{Q_{2}}(v_{3})
such that
|L_{Q_{2}}(v_{1})\setminus\{c\}|\geq 3
. And then
greedily color
w,v_{4},v_{2},v_{1}
in order.
Then the square of
Q_{2}
has a proper coloring from the list
L_{Q_{2}}
.
So
G^{2}
has a proper coloring from the list
L
, which is a contradiction.

Case 3
:
d=3
.

Suppose that
G
has
Q_{3}
in Figure
3
as a subgraph, and denote
V(Q_{3})=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},w\}
.
Note that the distance of the 3-cycle
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{1}
and the 2-vertex

is 3.

Let
Q_{3}^{\prime}=G-\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5},w\}
. Then since
G
is a minimal counterexample to Theorem
4
, the square of
Q_{3}^{\prime}
has a proper coloring
\phi
such that
\phi(v)\in L(v)
for each vertex
v\in V(Q_{3}^{\prime})
.

Now, for each
v\in V(Q_{3})
, we define

L_{Q_{3}}(v)=L(v)\setminus\{\phi(x):xv\in E(G^{2})\mbox{ and }x\notin V(Q_{3})\}.

Note that for any
x,y\in Q_{3}
, if

, then
d_{G}(x,y)\geq 3

since

has neither 4-cycles nor 5-cycles.
Then, we have the following

|L_{Q_{3}}(v_{1})|\geq 3,|L_{Q_{3}}(v_{2})|\geq 3,|L_{Q_{3}}(v_{3})|\geq 4,|L_{Q_{3}}(v_{4})|\geq 3,|L_{Q_{3}}(v_{5})|\geq 2,|L_{Q_{3}}(w)|\geq 3.

,

,

,

Thus, the square of

Now we state a key lemma which is important in discharging part.

Lemma 11
.

For each integer
\ell\geq 7
, let
C_{\ell}
be a face of
G
of length
\ell
. Then the sum of the number of 3-cycles adjacent to
C_{\ell}
and the number of 2-vertices on

is at most
{\displaystyle\big\lfloor\frac{\ell}{4}\big\rfloor}
.

The result is derived from Lemma

(b), Lemma

.
∎

Now we prove the main theorem.

Theorem
4
.

If
G
is a subcubic planar graph without 4-cycles and 5-cycles, then
\chi_{\ell}(G^{2})\leq 7
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a minimal counterexample to the theorem and let
G
be a plane graph drawn on the plane without crossing edges. Let
F(G)
be the set of faces of
G
. For a face
C\in F(G)
, let
\ell(C)
be the length of
C
.

We assign
2d(x)-6
to each vertex
x\in V(G)
and
\ell(x)-6
for each face
x\in F(G)
as an original charge function
\omega(x)
of
x
.
According to Euler’s formula
|V(G)|-|E(G)|+|F(G)|=2
,
we have

\sum_{x\in V(G)\cup F(G)}\omega(x)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}(2d(v)-6)+\sum_{f\in F(G)}(\ell(f)-6)=-12.

We next design some discharging rules to redistribute charges along the graph with conservation of the total charge. Let
\omega^{\prime}(x)
be the charge of
x\in V(G)\cup F(G)
after the discharge procedure. Thus,
{\displaystyle\sum_{x\in V(G)\cup F(G)}\omega(x)=\sum_{x\in V(G)\cup F(G)}\omega^{\prime}(x)}
. Next, we will show that
\omega^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in V(G)\cup F(G)
, which leads to the following contradiction

0\leq\sum_{x\in V(G)\cup F(G)}\omega^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{x\in V(G)\cup F(G)}\omega(x)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}(2d(v)-6)+\sum_{f\in F(G)}(\ell(f)-6)=-12.

The discharging rules:

(R1)

If a 2-vertex
u
is on a face
C
with
\ell(C)\geq 7
, then
C
gives charge 1 to
u
.

(R2)

If a 3-face
C_{3}
is adjacent to a face
C
with
\ell(C)\geq 7
, then
C
gives charge 1 to
C_{3}
.

Next, we will show that the new charge
\omega^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in V(G)\cup F(G)
.

Case 1:
Let
x\in V(G)
.

Observe that
G
has no 1-vertex by Lemma
5
(a).
If
d(x)=2
, then
\omega(x)=-2
.
Note that every 2-vertex is incident to two faces by Lemma
5
(c).
Also note that both incident faces of
x
have length at least 7
by Lemma
6
and Lemma
7
.
By (R1),
x
receives charge 1 from each of its incident faces. So,
\omega^{\prime}(x)=\omega(x)+1\times 2=0
.
If
d(x)=3
, then
\omega^{\prime}(x)=\omega(x)=0
.

Case 2:
Let
x\in F(G)
.

Here we denote
x
by a face
C
. Recall that
G
has neither a 4-cycle nor a 5-cycle.
If
\ell(C)=3
, then
C
is adjacent to three faces of length at least 7 by Lemma
7
and Lemma
8
. By (R2),
C
receives charge 1 from each of its adjacent faces. So,
\omega^{\prime}(C)=\omega(C)+1\times 3=0
.
If
\ell(C)=6
, then
\omega^{\prime}(C)=\omega(C)=0
.

Now, we consider the case when
\ell(C)\geq 7
.
By Lemma
11
, the sum of the number of 2-vertices on
C
and the number of 3-faces adjacent to
C
is at most
\lfloor\frac{\ell(C)}{4}\rfloor
.
So by (R1) and (R2),
\omega^{\prime}(C)\geq\ell(C)-6-\lfloor\frac{\ell(C)}{4}\rfloor\geq 0
.

Hence, by Cases 1 and 2, we have that
\omega^{\prime}(x)\geq 0
for every
x\in V(G)\cup F(G)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

Is it true that

Twisted regular representations and bimodules in vertex operator algebra theory
Yiyi Zhu

Abstract
In this paper, we use the twisted regular representation theory of vertex operator algebras to construct bimodules over twisted Zhu algebras, extending Haisheng Li’s work in untwisted scenarios. Moreover, a conjecture of Dong and Jiang on bimodule theory is confirmed.

Introduction

The (twisted) representation theory of a vertex operator algebra has been studied extensively in terms of the representation theory of various associative algebras associated with the vertex operator algebra (see
[
13
,
22
,
4
,
6
,
5
,
20
]
). Compared to the universal enveloping algebras associated with vertex operator algebras
[
8
]
, these associative algebras are smaller
[
12
,
10
,
9
,
11
]
and easier to deal with, and they have been proved to be very useful in the study of representations of vertex operator algebras. Let
V
be a vertex operator algebra,
g
be an automorphism of
V
with finite order
T
, a series of associative algebras
A_{g,n}(V)
were defined for
n\in\frac{1}{T}
in
[
5
]
. These associative algebras
A_{g,n}(V)
carry rich information about the representation of
V
. For example, there is a bijection between the set of inequivalent irreducible
g
-twisted
V
-modules and the set of inequivalent irreducible
A_{g,n}(V)
-modules, which is very useful in classifying irreducible
g
-twisted
V
-modules. Also, the semisimplicity of
A_{g,n}(V)
for large enough
n
implies the rationality of
V
.

The
A_{g,n}(V)
-
A_{g,m}(V)
-bimodule
A_{g,n,m}(V)
theory for
m,n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
developed by Dong and Jiang in
[
3
,
2
]
greatly expanded the
A_{g,n}(V)
theory with
A_{g,n,n}(V)=A_{g,n}(V)
as associative algebras.
A_{g,n}(V)
depicts all zero mode actions of
V
on some subspace
\Omega_{n}(M)
of an admissible
g
-twisted module
M
, while
A_{g,n,m}(V)
depicts all
n-m
mode actions. Because of this, Dong and Jiang were able to construct the generalized Verma admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module
\overline{M}(U)
generated by an
A_{g,m}(V)
-module
U
using these bimodules

, which was first constructed in
[
5
]
using a Lie algebraic approach. Roughly speaking,
\overline{M}(U)
is the ’biggest’ admissible
g
-twisted
V
-module generated by
U
. It plays an important role in the representation theory of
V
. From this point of view, these
A_{g,n,m}(V)
help us understand the representation of
V
better.

However, there is a setback of the
A_{g,n,m}(V)
theory. It is almost impossible to compute these bimodules. Those various associative algebras mentioned above are all quotients of
V
, and there are explicit descriptions of the elements modulo out. For example, the Zhu algebra
A(V)=V/O(V)
as vector spaces, where
O(V)
is spanned by

a\circ b={\rm Res}_{x}x^{-2}Y\left((1+x)^{L_{(0)}}a,b\right)

for all
a,b\in V
. The Zhu algebras of almost all familiar vertex operator algebras are well known
[
8
]
. But for
A_{g,n,m}(V)
, they are more complicated than Zhu algebras, the subspace
O_{g,n,m}(V)
quotient out by Dong and Jiang in
[
3
,
2
]
consists of three parts,

O_{g,n,m}(V)=O^{\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)+O^{\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)+O^{\prime\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V),

where
O^{\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
is a generalization of
O(V)
in Zhu algebra case, while
O^{\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
and
O^{\prime\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
are new. From the representation point of view, it is natural to quotient
O^{\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
and
O^{\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
out, but it is hard to write them down explicitly. Dong and Jiang conjectured that
O_{g,n,m}(V)=O^{\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
. A recent result in
[
11
]
shows that
O^{\prime\prime\prime}_{g,n,m}(V)
is redundant. In this paper, we confirm Dong and Jiang’s conjecture by making use of the regular representation theory.

The regular representation theory of vertex operator algebras was developed by Li in
[
17
]
. Let
W
be a weak
V
-module. For any complex number

, there is a weak
V\otimes V
-module structure on a subspace

inside the full dual

of

. It turns out to be closely related to various Zhu algebras and their bimodules. The constructions of various Zhu algebras were recovered by Li using the regular presentation theory, see
[
15
,
16
]
. Moreover, he gave a more conceptual proof of Frenkel-Zhu’s fusion rules theorem in
[
15
]
. The most important inspiration of Li’s regular representation theory to this paper is that he proved that
V/O^{\prime}_{id,n,m}(V)
has an
A_{id,n}(V)
-
A_{id,m}(V)
-bimodule structure and recovered many Dong and Jiang’s results in
[
3
]
, which almost confirmed Dong and Jiang’s conjecture in untwisted scenario.

This paper aims to generalize Li’s work in
[
18
]
to the twisted scenario using the twisted regular representation theory of vertex operator algebras developed in
[
19
]
and confirm Dong and Jiang’s conjecture in
[
3
,
2
]

Let
g_{1},g_{2}
be two finite-order mutually-commuting automorphisms of

where

For homogeneous

, and

Let

Set

and

We show that both

and

In the special case

by definition. Hence

\displaystyle a\underline{\ast}_{g_{1},m}^{n}b

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{x}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor n\rfloor+i}{i}(-1)^{\lfloor n\rfloor}\frac{(1+x)^{{\rm wt}b+i-1}}{x^{\lfloor n\rfloor+i+1}}Y(b,x)a

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{x}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor n\rfloor+i}{i}(-1)^{\lfloor n\rfloor}\frac{(1+x)^{{\rm wt}b+i-1}}{x^{\lfloor n\rfloor+i+1}}e^{xL_{(-1)}}Y(a,-x)b

\displaystyle\equiv{\rm Res}_{x}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor n\rfloor+i}{i}(-1)^{\lfloor n\rfloor}\frac{(1+x)^{n-m-{\rm wt}a+i-1}}{x^{\lfloor n\rfloor+i+1}}Y(a,\frac{-x}{1+x})b

\displaystyle\equiv{\rm Res}_{x}\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor n\rfloor+i}{i}(-1)^{i}\frac{(1+x)^{{\rm wt}a+m-n+\lfloor n\rfloor}}{x^{\lfloor n\rfloor+i+1}}Y(a,x)b

\displaystyle\equiv a\ast_{g_{1},m}^{n}b\pmod{(L_{(-1)}+L_{(0)}+m-n)V}.

Since
(L_{(-1)}+L_{(0)}+m-n)V\subset O^{\prime}_{g_{1},g_{1},n,m}(V)
, we are done.

∎

So far, we have shown that
A_{g_{1},n,m}(V)
is a quotient bimodule of
A^{\diamond}_{g_{1},g_{1},n,m}(V)
. Recall that
O^{\prime}_{g_{1},g_{1},n,m}(V)=O^{\prime}_{g_{1},n,m}(V)
.
The following two lemmas will be used later:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
m,p,n\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
and
b\in V
. Then

O^{\prime}_{g_{1},n,p}(V)\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p}^{n}b\in O^{\prime}_{g_{1},n,m}(V).

(5.8)

Proof.

Let
f\in\Omega_{m,n}(\mathfrak{D}_{g_{1},g_{1}^{-1}}^{(-1)}(V))
and
a\in O^{\prime}_{g_{1},n,p}(V)
. By
Proposition

3.6
, we have

\left\langle f,a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p}^{n}b\right\rangle={\rm Res}_{x}x^{-1+n-p}\left\langle(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}\left(x^{L_{(0)}}\theta(a),x\right)f,b\right\rangle.

(5.9)

By the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 4.2]
, we know

\theta(O^{\prime}_{g_{1},n,p}(V))\subseteq O^{\prime}_{g_{1}^{-1},p,n}(V).

(5.10)

Thus
\theta(a)\in O^{\prime}_{g_{1}^{-1},p,n}(V)
.
The proof of
[
2
, Lemma 5.2]
tells us that

{\rm Res}_{x}x^{-1+n-p}(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}\left(x^{L_{(0)}}\theta(a),x\right)f=0.

(5.11)

Hence
a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p}^{n}b\in O^{\prime}_{g_{1},n,m}(V)
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
a,b,c\in V
and
m,p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{N}
, we have

(a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},p_{1},p_{2}}^{p_{3}}b)\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{1}}^{p_{3}}c-a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{2}}^{p_{3}}(b\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{1}}^{p_{2}}c)\in O^{\prime}_{g_{1},p_{3},p_{1}}(V).

(5.12)

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.6
, for any
f\in\Omega_{m,p_{3}}(\mathfrak{D}_{g_{1},g_{1}^{-1}}^{(-1)}(V))
and homogeneous
a,b,c\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle f,(a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},p_{1},p_{2}}^{p_{3}}b){\overline{\ast}}_{g_{1},m,p_{1}}^{p_{3}}c\right\rangle

(5.13)

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{x}x^{-1+p_{3}-p_{1}}\left\langle(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}\left(x^{L_{(0)}}(\theta(a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},p_{1},p_{2}}^{p_{3}}b)),x\right)f,c\right\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\left\langle f,a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{2}}^{p_{3}}(b\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{1}}^{p_{2}}c)\right\rangle

(5.14)

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{x}{\rm Res}_{y}x^{-1+p_{3}-p_{2}}y^{-1+p_{2}-p_{1}}

\displaystyle\left\langle(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}(y^{L_{(0)}}\theta(b),y)(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}(x^{L_{(0)}}\theta(a),x)f,c\right\rangle.

By the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 4.2]
, we have

\theta(a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},p_{1},p_{2}}^{p_{3}}b)-\theta(b)\overline{\ast}_{g_{1}^{-1},p_{3},p_{2}}^{p_{1}}\theta(a)\in O^{\prime}_{g_{1}^{-1},p_{1},p_{3}}(V).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\langle f,(a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},p_{1},p_{2}}^{p_{3}}b){\overline{\ast}}_{g_{1},m,p_{1}}^{p_{3}}c\right\rangle

(5.16)

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{x}x^{-1+p_{3}-p_{1}}\left\langle(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}\left(x^{L_{(0)}}(\theta(b)\overline{\ast}_{g_{1}^{-1},p_{3},p_{2}}^{p_{1}}\theta(a)),x\right)f,c\right\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm Res}_{x}{\rm Res}_{y}x^{-1+p_{3}-p_{2}}y^{-1+p_{2}-p_{1}}

\displaystyle\left\langle(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}(y^{L_{(0)}}\theta(b),y)(Y_{g}^{R}\circ g_{1})^{[-1]}(x^{L_{(0)}}\theta(a),x)f,c\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,a\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{2}}^{p_{3}}(b\overline{\ast}_{g_{1},m,p_{1}}^{p_{2}}c)\right\rangle,

[
2

2
, Lemma 5.1]
.
Hence, we have proved

(5.17)

∎

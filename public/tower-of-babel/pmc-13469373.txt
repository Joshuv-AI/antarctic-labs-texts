ARUDet: active retrieval and uncertainty-aware detection for sports video object detection
Authors: Lijing Yu, Jin Gao, Xinqi Ji, Bo Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractVideo object detection in sports scenarios faces severe challenges posed by high speed motion, heavy occlusion, and complex deformation. Existing methods employ passive feature aggregation that applies a uniform fusion strategy across all regions, neglecting the type specific differences of local degradation and failing to match targeted complementary information for different defects. Meanwhile, visual degradation causes object boundaries to exhibit probabilistic distribution characteristics, yet conventional deterministic regression ignores such geometric ambiguity and forces fitting a single coordinate, making localization reliability difficult to guarantee. To address these issues, we propose ARUDet (Active Retrieval Uncertainty-aware Detector), comprising an Active Temporal Retrieval Module (ATRM) and an Uncertainty Rectified Regression Head (URH). Specifically, ATRM first identifies the degradation type of each region in the current frame through a Defect Responsive Assessor and encodes it into an explicit query vector. Subsequently, under dual constraints of semantic consistency and quality complementarity, it actively retrieves the best matching historical feature patches from a temporal memory bank and selectively replaces only low quality regions, achieving on demand restoration. URH collaboratively suppresses geometric uncertainty through a Probabilistic Boundary Projector (PBP) and a Lower Bound Optimizer (LBO): the former explicitly models the geometric distribution to improve localization quality, while the latter constrains the worst case error to tighten the prediction lower bound. Experiments on multiple sports datasets demonstrate that ARUDet achieves significant performance improvements, further validating the effectiveness of the proposed method.Keywords: Video object detection, Sports video analysis, Temporal feature retrieval, Uncertainty-aware regressionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Sports video analysis is an important research direction in computer vision, with broad application value in intelligent broadcasting, tactical analysis, and athlete training assistance. With the accelerating digital transformation of the sports industry, the demand for automated athlete detection and tracking is growing rapidly, covering team sports such as football, basketball, and volleyball, as well as individual competitive events such as skiing and badminton. However, sports scenarios possess unique visual characteristics: athletes exhibit high speed and large amplitude motion patterns, frequent physical contact and mutual occlusion, and drastic pose deformation. Taking badminton as an example, racket swing speeds can reach hundreds of kilometers per hour, causing severe motion blur; in skiing scenarios, the contrast between athletes and snowy backgrounds is low while pose changes are drastic; in football and basketball games, dense player gatherings cause extensive occlusion. These characteristics impose extremely high demands on the robustness of detection algorithms, making traditional static image detection methods difficult to apply directly.

Current video object detection methods mainly face two core challenges, as illustrated in Fig. 1. First, passive feature aggregation leads to restoration failure. Existing temporal methods typically adopt fixed window feature aggregation strategies: extracting features from K adjacent frames and then fusing them through attention mechanisms or convolution operations. This indiscriminate aggregation approach has a fundamental flaw: different regions of the current frame suffer from different types of degradation (occlusion, blur, illumination changes, etc.), and they require complementary information from different sources. For example, an occluded player needs to retrieve complete appearance from unoccluded historical frames, while motion blurred regions need texture details from clear moments. However, adjacent frames within a fixed window may not contain the required high quality information, and a unified fusion strategy cannot match the best restoration source for each degraded region. As a result, the model passively accepts a large amount of ineffective or even harmful features, which instead dilutes the contribution of useful information. Second, deterministic regression ignores geometric uncertainty. Under high speed motion and severe occlusion scenarios, the physical boundaries of objects appear as ambiguous probability distributions due to residual images and deformation, yet traditional bounding box regression methods universally assume deterministic point estimation, forcing the fitting of blurry distributions into single values. The problem with this approach is that the model outputs a bounding box in an “average” sense, but on difficult samples, this average may deviate far from the true boundary, and the model cannot quantify the reliability of its own predictions. When the visibility of objects fluctuates drastically across consecutive frames, deterministic predictions jump between different “average positions,” producing temporal localization jitter; in extremely degraded frames, the model may even output incorrect boundaries with high confidence, leading to tracking drift.

To address these challenges, we propose ARUDet (Active Retrieval Uncertainty aware Detector), a robust detection framework that integrates active retrieval with worst case optimization strategies. ARUDet comprises two core modules: the Active Temporal Retrieval Module (ATRM) and the Uncertainty Rectified Regression Head (URH). For the first challenge, ATRM abandons the passive aggregation paradigm and instead adopts a pull based active retrieval strategy. The module first evaluates the degradation degree and type at each spatial location of the current frame through a Defect Responsive Assessor, and encodes the features of degraded regions into explicit query vectors. Subsequently, under dual constraints of semantic consistency and quality complementarity, it actively retrieves the best matching historical feature patches from a dynamically maintained temporal memory bank, and selectively replaces only low quality regions, achieving on demand restoration. This mechanism breaks the limitation of fixed temporal windows, ensuring that each degraded region can obtain targeted complementary information across longer time spans, rather than passively accepting potentially irrelevant neighboring frame features. For the second challenge, URH collaboratively suppresses geometric uncertainty from two aspects: the Probabilistic Boundary Projector (PBP) extends boundary coordinate prediction from single values to probability distributions, explicitly characterizing localization uncertainty; the Lower Bound Optimizer (LBO) estimates the confidence lower bound of the prediction distribution and minimizes the localization error at the lower bound, indirectly constraining the uncertainty range of predictions. Through this dual mechanism, the model further reduces the worst case localization error while maintaining baseline localization accuracy, thereby adaptively suppressing geometric uncertainty. Experiments on multiple sports video datasets demonstrate that ARUDet achieves significant improvements in both detection accuracy and localization robustness, with particularly notable advantages in motion blur and occlusion scenarios.

Beyond sports VOD, the defect-aware retrieval and worst-case probabilistic regression in ARUDet align with the broader paradigm of uncertainty-aware, reliability-driven perception, with conceptual parallels in robust multimodal recognition1, uncertainty quantification for reliable decision making2, and look-ahead Bayesian design3.

Related work

Object detection

The development of modern object detection has generally undergone three paradigm shifts: from convolutional detectors to end to end Transformers, and then to generative detection. Early two stage methods, represented by Faster R-CNN, established high accuracy detection baselines through joint optimization of region proposal generation and classification regression4; subsequently, one stage methods (such as RetinaNet) effectively alleviated class imbalance through Focal Loss, pushing the upper bound of real time detection performance5. Building upon this, DETR reformulated detection as a set prediction problem, replacing NMS with Hungarian matching to achieve a unified end to end training paradigm6. Follow up works including Deformable DETR, Conditional DETR, and DAB-DETR addressed slow convergence and insufficient small object performance from the perspectives of sparse attention, conditional queries, and dynamic anchor queries, respectively7–9. DINO further significantly improved accuracy and stability through denoising training and query initialization strategies10, while RT-DETR validated the engineering feasibility of the DETR pipeline in real time scenarios11. Meanwhile, diffusion models began entering the detection task. DDPM provided a unified probabilistic framework for step by step denoising modeling12, and DiffusionDet explicitly modeled detection as a reverse diffusion process from noisy boxes to target boxes, demonstrating the potential of diffusion based detection in both performance and inference flexibility13. Furthermore, large scale vision language pre-training methods such as GLIP demonstrated open vocabulary and cross domain generalization capabilities, providing stronger priors for detection models14. Overall, detection research has shifted from “structural engineering” to “unified modeling and large model prior driven,” laying the methodological foundation for temporal modeling and robust localization in video scenarios.

Temporal feature aggregation in video object detection

Building upon the above developments, the core problem of video object detection has transformed into cross frame information utilization. Early methods primarily focused on feature propagation and neighboring frame alignment: Deep Feature Flow reduced redundant computation through keyframe feature propagation15, FGFA improved moving object detection through optical flow guided aggregation16, and STSN enhanced alignment capability using deformable spatiotemporal sampling17. Subsequently, research shifted from local alignment to long range relation modeling: SELSA performed aggregation in sequence level semantic space18, RDN strengthened cross frame discriminative relations through relation distillation19, and MEGA combined global and local memory to alleviate information insufficiency in short windows20. Entering the Transformer era, TransVOD incorporated spatiotemporal interaction into a unified framework, further enhancing cross frame modeling expressiveness21. In recent work, MaskVD and Beyond Boxes advanced the balance between efficiency and accuracy in video detection from the perspectives of region mask efficient computation and mask guided spatiotemporal fusion, respectively22,23. In the sports domain, SoccerNet-Tracking and SportsMOT provided more challenging multi-object benchmarks24,25, while SportMamba and FieldMOT emphasized the importance of long range modeling efficiency, field priors, and cross camera consistency26,27. Although the aforementioned methods have significantly improved temporal utilization capabilities, most still adopt the passive paradigm of “unified fusion rules,” reading historical features within predefined windows and executing similar aggregation strategies. This design is effective in general scenarios, but in heterogeneous degradation regions of sports videos (where occlusion, blur, and low contrast coexist), it is prone to information mismatch and noise amplification. Therefore, the more critical question at the current stage has shifted from “whether to aggregate” to “how to perform controllable aggregation according to degradation type.” At the same time, the stability of localization regression on difficult samples has also become another determining factor for the performance upper bound.

Distinction from Prior Memory-based VOD and Tracking. Unlike MEGA, SELSA, and Beyond Boxes that apply uniform aggregation at every location, our ATRM uses defect-aware queries under joint semantic and quality constraints and replaces only degraded regions, avoiding the dilution of high-quality features. Recent tracking studies on motion-aware temporal modeling28,29 and shared-query design30,31 share the same selective-temporal-modeling spirit.

Bounding box regression and uncertainty modeling

Bounding box prediction research has gradually evolved from “deterministic point estimation” to “quality estimation and distribution modeling.” IoU-Net pointed out that localization quality and classification confidence are not equivalent, requiring explicit learning of localization reliability32; GIoU improved traditional regression loss through geometrically consistent objectives33. In the uncertainty direction, Bounding Box Regression with Uncertainty introduced variance into regression learning, advancing probabilistic localization representation34; Probabilistic Object Detection (PDQ) further systematized the definition and evaluation protocol for uncertainty aware detection35. Subsequently, GFL and GFLv2 alleviated the inconsistency between classification scores and localization quality through distributed boundary representation and joint quality estimation learning36,37. Recent research has paid more attention to the interpretability and training utility of uncertainty, such as aleatoric uncertainty analysis based on foundation models38, DiL’s explainable evaluation of abnormal uncertainty39, and UGS’s direct incorporation of uncertainty aware mechanisms into gradient stabilization optimization40. DualAdaptNet uses an auxiliary regression head to estimate the worst case and minimize its error, embodying min max style lower bound optimization41; DIG-FACE improves generalization on unknown distributions through bias upper bound constraints42. However, existing methods are mostly based on the image domain or static distribution assumptions, and still lack targeted risk control under severe degradation and visibility abrupt change conditions: on one hand, merely optimizing expected error is insufficient to suppress tail localization distortion; on the other hand, there is a lack of unified design that jointly constrains probabilistic boundaries, temporal consistency, and worst case error. Based on this, we adopt “probabilistic boundary modeling + confidence lower bound optimization” to enhance localization stability on difficult samples.

Active Temporal Retrieval Module (ATRM)

As discussed above, the fused feature output by the Hybrid Encoder often suffers from local degradation in sports scenarios due to occlusion, blur, and low contrast. The core idea of ATRM is that different degraded regions require different complementary information; therefore, retrieval should be performed actively on a per-region and per-defect-type basis, rather than passively applying uniform fusion across all positions. ATRM consists of three sub-modules: the Defect Responsive Assessor, the dual constraint retrieval mechanism, and selective replacement fusion.

Uncertainty Rectified Regression Head (URH)

URH replaces the standard regression FFN at the RT-DETR decoder output, addressing the boundary geometric uncertainty caused by high-speed motion and severe occlusion in sports scenarios. Assumptions and Theoretical Positioning. The per-coordinate Gaussian rests on three explicit assumptions: (A1) Hungarian one-to-one matching in RT-DETR makes the boundary deviation approximately unimodal, justifying a single Gaussian rather than a mixture; (A2) the four boundary coordinates are conditionally independent given the instance embedding , a standard simplification adopted in probabilistic regression literature34,35; (A3) the symmetric Gaussian is intentionally paired with an asymmetric worst-case loss (the \max operator in , Section 3.5) so that biased predictions are still corrected directionally. The Gaussian is therefore not used to perfectly fit every form of geometric ambiguity; it is used because it admits a closed-form confidence interval that LBO can directly minimize. Unlike KL-Loss34 which lacks a worst-case constraint and GFL/GFLv236,37 which use discrete coordinate grids with quantization bias, URH unifies a continuous probabilistic boundary with a confidence-interval worst-case optimizer in one regression head.

Lower Bound Optimizer (LBO)

Experiments

Datasets

We evaluate ARUDet on three datasets: SportsMOT, Badminton-Players-Detection, and SkiTB, covering team sports scenarios, badminton close-range scenarios, and skiing long-sequence scenarios, respectively. To unify the task definition, we convert the tracking annotations of SportsMOT and SkiTB into detection annotations: per-frame bounding boxes are retained while track IDs are removed, and the data is used solely for object detection training and evaluation.

SportsMOT. This dataset contains three types of scenes: basketball, volleyball, and football, comprising 240 video clips, 150,379 frames, 1,629,490 annotated bounding boxes, and 3,401 tracks. The data is split into train/val/test = 45/45/150. The video resolution is 1280×720 at 25 FPS. We train and validate on its official train/val split and report results under the detection setting.

Badminton-Players-Detection. This badminton dataset is collected and annotated by us, containing 1,022 images with two categories: player and shuttlecock, split into train/valid/test = 819/139/64. Based on local annotation file statistics, the dataset contains a total of 3,197 annotated bounding boxes, of which 2,370 are player and 827 are shuttlecock; all images are resized to 1280×1280. The main experiments focus on player detection, with shuttlecock results reported as supplementary.

SkiTB. This dataset contains 300 multi-camera videos and 2,019 single-camera videos, totaling 352,978 frames, covering 196 athletes and 161 venues, spanning three major categories of alpine skiing, ski jumping, and freestyle skiing (11 sub-events in total). We adopt its date-based split and use per-frame athlete bounding boxes for single-class detection training and evaluation.

Implementation details

ARUDet is implemented based on RT-DETR: ATRM is inserted between the Hybrid Encoder and the Decoder, and URH replaces the regression branch of the decoder. Training employs one-to-one label assignment via Hungarian matching, and inference maintains an NMS-free pipeline. For video data (SportsMOT, SkiTB), input samples are organized in temporal order and the FIFO memory bank of ATRM is maintained online; for the badminton data, adjacent frames are used to construct short temporal context. ATRM retrieval is driven by the joint scoring of semantic consistency and quality complementarity, and selective replacement is applied only to degraded regions. Optimization follows a two-stage strategy: the first stage freezes ATRM and trains only the Backbone, Hybrid Encoder, Decoder, and URH; the second stage unfreezes ATRM and performs end-to-end joint training of all modules. The total loss comprises the detection loss, ATRM loss, PBP loss, LBO loss, and variance/confidence level regularization terms, with weights set to , and the minimum confidence level of URH set to . Evaluation adopts COCO-style detection metrics (mAP, AP50, AP75); to ensure cross-dataset comparability, all three sets of experiments are unified under the main task of “athlete detection”, where the track IDs of SportsMOT and SkiTB are not used during training or evaluation, and only per-frame bounding box annotations are utilized.

Main experimental results and analysis

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of ARUDet, we conduct systematic comparisons with two categories of representative baselines across three datasets. The first category consists of image-level detectors, including Faster R-CNN, DETR, Deformable DETR, DINO, and RT-DETR (i.e., the base framework of ARUDet), to measure the gain from temporal information. The second category consists of video object detection methods, including FGFA, SELSA, RDN, MEGA, TransVOD, MaskVD, and Beyond Boxes, covering mainstream paradigms from optical flow alignment to Transformer aggregation. All methods uniformly adopt the ResNet-50 backbone for fair comparison. Image-level methods are independently trained on each dataset and perform per-frame inference; video methods use temporal context according to their original settings. For the Badminton image dataset, video methods are adapted by constructing short temporal context from adjacent frames (marked as †).

Three key observations can be drawn from the table. First, the improvement in AP75 is significantly larger than that in AP50. Compared to RT-DETR, ARUDet improves AP75 by 5.5 percentage points, while AP50 improves by only 3.8 percentage points. This asymmetric gain reveals the core mechanism of URH: at loose IoU thresholds, most methods can already correctly localize the target with minor performance differences; at strict thresholds, the localization jitter of deterministic regression methods is amplified, whereas the probabilistic modeling and lower bound optimization of URH effectively tighten the confidence interval of boundary predictions, enabling high-precision localization. Second, some passive video methods perform worse than strong image detectors. The mAP of FGFA (53.9%), SELSA (55.2%), and RDN (55.8%) are all lower than those of DINO (56.8%) and RT-DETR (57.5%). This seemingly counterintuitive phenomenon actually corroborates the core observation of this paper: under the heterogeneous degradation conditions of sports scenarios, neighboring frames within a fixed window do not necessarily contain the required high-quality complementary information, and indiscriminate uniform fusion instead introduces noisy features that dilute the effective information in the original frame. Third, Transformer-based video methods show progress but with diminishing gains. TransVOD (58.1%), MaskVD (58.8%), and Beyond Boxes (59.2%) progressively narrow the gap with image detectors through stronger spatiotemporal modeling, yet remain limited by the passive aggregation paradigm; during prolonged occlusion, consecutive frames within the fixed window may all contain occlusion and thus fail to provide effective repair sources. The ATRM of ARUDet breaks this limitation by actively retrieving clean features from the memory bank across longer temporal spans.

In contrast, the ATRM of ARUDet uses the Defect Responsive Assessor to determine which regions genuinely require repair and leaves high-quality regions untouched, thereby avoiding the risk of “over-repair.”

On this dataset, the advantage of ARUDet is particularly prominent: the mAP reaches 55.4%, surpassing RT-DETR by 4.8 percentage points and Beyond Boxes by 3.1 percentage points. The improvement in AP75 reaches 5.6 percentage points (relative to RT-DETR), the largest among the three datasets. We analyze the reasons from the following three perspectives.

Long sequences fully activate the memory bank advantage. The video sequences in SkiTB average approximately 175 frames per segment, substantially longer than typical segments in SportsMOT. This allows the FIFO memory bank of ATRM to accumulate richer historical features. When athletes are occluded by snow spray or undergo dramatic pose transitions, ATRM can retrieve clear and complete reference features from distant historical frames, whereas fixed-window methods in such scenarios can only access equally degraded neighboring frames.

Low-contrast degradation amplifies localization ambiguity. In skiing scenarios, athletes’ equipment colors are often similar to the snowy background (white ski suits, gray sky), causing target contours to become blurred in the feature space. This low-contrast degradation makes the inherent ambiguity of boundary predictions far greater than in other sports scenarios. The Probabilistic Boundary Projector of URH explicitly captures this ambiguity, and the Lower Bound Optimizer further constrains the worst-case prediction deviation, effectively suppressing localization jitter caused by unclear contours. The largest AP75 improvement (+5.6) directly reflects this point.

The failure of passive fusion is more thorough. The mAP of FGFA (46.8%) and SELSA (48.2%) are not only lower than RT-DETR (50.6%) but also lower than DINO (49.8%), with the performance inversion being the largest among the three datasets. We attribute this to the highly mixed degradation types in skiing scenarios (motion blur, low contrast, and pose deformation often co-occur), making the noise introduction problem of passive fusion particularly severe under these conditions.

As shown in Table 5, ARUDet achieves an inference speed of 48 FPS, meeting the real-time application requirement (\ge 30 FPS) while being significantly faster than most video detection methods. Compared to earlier video methods (MEGA 14 FPS, RDN 15 FPS, SELSA 16 FPS), ARUDet achieves approximately 3× speedup, primarily attributable to two factors: first, the RT-DETR base framework itself employs an efficient end-to-end architecture with inference efficiency far superior to traditional video methods built upon Faster R-CNN; second, the two-stage accelerated retrieval strategy of ATRM (coarse-grained frame selection followed by fine-grained location matching) avoids exhaustive pixel-level search across the entire memory bank. Compared to recent methods, ARUDet is also faster than Beyond Boxes (30 FPS) and TransVOD (28 FPS).

From the per-module breakdown in Table 6, ATRM is the primary source of overhead (+6.6 ms), dominated by memory bank maintenance and feature retrieval; URH adds only 0.3 ms of inference latency, with negligible computational cost. In terms of parameters, ARUDet adds only 1.5M parameters (+3.6%) over RT-DETR, and the total parameter count (43.5M) is comparable to or lower than most video methods (FGFA 50.3M, MEGA 48.6M, TransVOD 46.5M), because the core operation of ATRM is cosine similarity-based feature retrieval rather than stacking additional parameterized network layers. Overall, ARUDet achieves a reasonable balance between accuracy and efficiency.

Scalability and Cost-benefit. The fixed 6.6 ms ATRM overhead pays off non-uniformly: occlusion stratification (Section 4.7) shows mAP gains of +2.2, +6.9, and +10.5 from mild to severe occlusion, so ATRM trades a constant latency for a near-linear payoff with respect to degradation severity. Retrieval cost is and is therefore independent of memory bank size once coarse selection has chosen candidates; memory grows linearly as but is bounded by the FIFO capacity, so per-frame compute stays constant regardless of sequence length. For resource-constrained deployment, reducing from 50 to 20 cuts memory by about 60% with mAP dropping only about 0.8 points, and product quantization of stored features further compresses memory roughly 4×.

This complementarity also explains why the joint model achieves the largest improvement at AP75 (+5.5).

ATRM component ablation

To thoroughly analyze the necessity of each design within ATRM, we fix the URH module and sequentially remove individual ATRM components. Figure 4(a) presents the performance of each variant on mAP and AP75 using grouped bar charts.

Component ablation studies on SportsMOT. (a) ATRM component ablation: blue bars represent mAP and orange bars represent AP75; values in parentheses indicate the drop relative to the full ATRM. (b) URH component ablation (built upon ATRM): showing the progressive construction from ATRM + L1 regression to the complete URH.

The six ATRM variant experiments reveal the following findings. The Defect Responsive Assessor (DRA) is the most critical component, with its removal causing a 2.3 percentage point drop in mAP (62.1→59.8), the largest decline among all ablations. This indicates that the prerequisite for “on-demand repair” is accurately locating the regions that need repair; uniform retrieval without defect awareness cannot match the correct complementary information for degraded regions. The selective replacement mechanism ranks second in contribution after DRA (−2.1 mAP), validating the core observation of this paper: global fusion strategies dilute the original features of high-quality regions, and preserving high-quality regions from interference is crucial. The semantic consistency constraint (−1.9) and quality complementarity constraint (−1.6) have comparable impact, indicating that both constraints in the retrieval process are indispensable: relying solely on semantic similarity may retrieve historical features of equally low quality, while relying solely on quality scores may introduce semantically mismatched noise. Removing degradation type encoding leads to a 1.1 mAP drop, indicating that distinguishing different degradation patterns (occlusion vs. blur vs. low contrast) helps match complementary information more precisely. The adaptation transform has the smallest impact (−0.7) but still contributes, suggesting that although the domain gap between historical and current frames is not the primary concern, appropriate feature alignment can further reduce fusion artifacts.

URH component ablation

To analyze the progressive contribution of each component within URH, we incrementally build each URH component on top of ATRM. Figure 4(b) illustrates the progression from ATRM + L1 regression to the complete URH.

The experimental results exhibit a clear progressive trend. The independent contribution of PBP (+1.0 mAP, +1.4 AP75) is significantly larger than that of LBO (+0.4 mAP, +0.7 AP75), which meets expectations: probabilistic boundary modeling provides the foundational capability of distributional representation, while LBO serves as a further constraint built upon it. Notably, LBO has limited effect without PBP (+0.4 mAP), because the lower bound constraint loses its target in the absence of probabilistic distributions. The joint use of PBP + LBO produces super-linear gains: the fixed α version already achieves +1.7 mAP, exceeding the sum of their independent gains (+1.4). Adaptive confidence level further brings an additional +0.4 mAP gain (61.7→62.1), indicating that samples of different difficulty levels indeed require differentiated confidence constraints. Removing the variance regularization causes a 0.6 mAP drop (62.1→61.5), showing that the lack of variance range constraints leads to unstable variance estimates for some samples, affecting the collaborative optimization of the NLL loss and LBO loss.

Robustness and calibration analysis

To further understand the behavioral characteristics of ARUDet under different conditions, we conduct analysis from three dimensions: occlusion severity stratification, temporal retrieval span, and uncertainty calibration.

Occlusion stratification analysis. We divide the detection samples of SportsMOT into three levels based on the occlusion area ratio: mild (<20%), moderate (20%–50%), and severe (>50%) (Fig. 6(a)). The results clearly demonstrate a trend of increasing gains with heavier occlusion: the gain under mild occlusion is +2.2 (74.5 vs. 72.3), expanding to +6.9 under moderate occlusion (62.7 vs. 55.8), and further reaching +10.5 under severe occlusion (48.9 vs. 38.4). In mild occlusion scenarios, the degree of degradation is limited and baseline methods already perform reasonably well; in severe occlusion scenarios, most of the target’s visual information is lost, and ATRM retrieves complete appearance features from distant historical frames for repair, while URH explicitly models the localization ambiguity caused by occlusion through probabilistic boundary modeling and constrains the worst-case error, with both modules synergistically achieving maximum effect. Beyond Boxes achieves 58.2/41.6 under moderate/severe occlusion, outperforming RT-DETR but still significantly below ARUDet, indicating that passive aggregation strategies within fixed windows still struggle to find sufficient repair information.

(b),(c) ATRM temporal retrieval span distributions on SportsMOT and SkiTB, respectively. Temporal retrieval span. We collect the distribution of temporal distances between ATRM retrieval hits and the current frame on SportsMOT and SkiTB (Fig. 6(b), (c)). On SportsMOT, the retrieval span for non-occluded frames concentrates on nearby frames (mean approximately 9 frames), while the retrieval span for occluded frames is significantly larger (mean approximately 20 frames), with a long tail extending to 40+ frames, indicating that ATRM automatically skips degraded neighboring frames during occlusion and retrieves clean features from more distant history. On SkiTB, the overall retrieval span is larger (non-occluded frame mean approximately 14 frames, occluded frame mean approximately 30 frames), which is consistent with the long-sequence characteristics of SkiTB: sustained low contrast and pose variations require ATRM to look back across longer temporal spans. A common pattern across both datasets is that the average retrieval span for occluded frames is approximately twice that for non-occluded frames, validating the adaptive retrieval capability of ATRM.

Uncertainty calibration. To verify whether the σ predicted by URH faithfully reflects the actual localization error, we perform scatter plot analysis between σ and on the SportsMOT test set, comparing with KL Loss (Fig. 6(d)). The scatter distribution of ARUDet exhibits a strong positive correlation (Pearson r ≈ 0.92), with a fitted line slope of approximately 0.85, close to the ideal value of 1.0; in contrast, KL Loss achieves only r ≈ 0.72, with more dispersed scatter points. This difference can be attributed to the NLL loss of PBP providing a direct probabilistic supervision signal, and the confidence lower bound constraint of LBO further refining the estimation range of σ. Well-calibrated uncertainty means that the model’s low σ output indeed corresponds to accurate localization, and high σ output indeed indicates localization deviation, which has important practical value for downstream tracking tasks.

Limitations and future work

Limitations. Although ARUDet achieves significant improvements on multiple sports datasets, two limitations remain. First, the memory bank retrieval of ATRM introduces additional inference latency, reducing the inference speed from 72 FPS of RT-DETR to 48 FPS, which may become a bottleneck in multi-stream concurrent broadcasting or edge device deployment scenarios; although the two-stage acceleration strategy has significantly reduced the retrieval complexity, the random memory access pattern of the memory bank inherently conflicts with the continuous computation paradigm of GPUs, and further optimization requires joint advances at both the algorithmic and engineering levels. Second, ATRM relies on the availability of retrievable high-quality complementary information in the memory bank; when drastic shot transitions, abrupt viewpoint changes, or prolonged complete disappearance and reappearance of targets occur, the memory bank may not contain semantically consistent historical features, causing retrieval quality to degrade or even introduce mismatched noise; the retrieval span analysis indicates that the current ATRM can effectively look back in most occlusion scenarios, but for extreme long-duration degradation exceeding the memory bank capacity, a reliable fallback mechanism is still lacking.

Future work. Based on the above analysis, we plan to advance subsequent research in the following directions. (1) Explore hierarchical memory banks and approximate nearest neighbor indexing (such as locality-sensitive hashing or product quantization), combined with dynamic eviction strategies and knowledge distillation schemes to further compress retrieval latency, promoting the deployment of ARUDet on edge devices. (2) Introduce scene change detection and automatic memory bank reset mechanisms, and explore cross-viewpoint feature alignment techniques to improve the robustness of ATRM in strongly non-stationary scenarios such as shot transitions and multi-camera broadcasting. (3) Directly inject the uncertainty estimates output by URH into the tracker’s association cost matrix and the observation noise model of the Kalman filter to achieve end-to-end joint optimization of detection and tracking, and validate the transferability and generalization capability of the method on general video detection benchmarks (such as ImageNet VID) and multi-category targets.

Conclusion

This paper proposes ARUDet, a robust video object detection framework designed for sports scenarios, addressing the detection challenges posed by high-speed motion, severe occlusion, and complex deformation from two complementary dimensions: feature repair and localization reliability. To tackle the information mismatch problem caused by the passive feature aggregation of existing methods, the Active Temporal Retrieval Module (ATRM) identifies the type and severity of degradation in each region through a Defect Responsive Assessor, and actively retrieves targeted historical features from a temporal memory bank under dual constraints of semantic consistency and quality complementarity, performing selective replacement only on low-quality regions to achieve on-demand repair. To address the problem of deterministic regression ignoring geometric ambiguity, the Uncertainty Rectified Regression Head (URH) extends boundary predictions to Gaussian distributions through a Probabilistic Boundary Projector, and constrains worst-case localization error via a Lower Bound Optimizer, synergistically achieving explicit modeling and effective suppression of uncertainty. Experiments on three datasets, SportsMOT, Badminton-Players-Detection, and SkiTB, demonstrate that ARUDet achieves the best results across all evaluation metrics, with the most significant advantages under strict IoU thresholds (AP75) and severe occlusion scenarios. Ablation studies validate the independent contributions and positive synergistic effects of the two modules, and robustness analysis further confirms the adaptive retrieval capability of ATRM and the uncertainty calibration quality of URH.

The SkiTB dataset can be obtained from the Machine Learning Group at the University of Udine (https://machinelearning.uniud.it/datasets/skitb). Code will be released upon acceptance.

RETRACTED ARTICLE: KiVa anti-bullying program: preventing and reducing bullying behavior among students – a scoping review
Authors: Rohman Hikmat, Suryani Suryani, Iyus Yosep, Rohani Jeharsae
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Bullying is a serious problem among adolescents that often occurs in schools. The problem occurs because of the high level of bullying behavior. Bullying causes negative impacts such as anxiety, low self-esteem, and suicide risk. So that interventions are needed to prevent and reduce bullying behavior. The aim of this study is to explore the effectiveness and implementation methods of the KiVa anti-bullying program for preventing and reducing bullying behavior for students. Scoping review design was used in this study using PRISMA Extension for Scoping Review search strategy. A scoping review of studies indexed in CINAHL, PubMed and Scopus databases was conducted. After duplicates were removed and articles screened, 11 studies were included in the review. Data analysis in this study used descriptive qualitative. The 11 included studies comprised of randomized controlled trials and quasi-experimental studies involving range of samples from 1051 to 23.520 respondents from elementary school to high school students aged 6–15 years. The KiVa Anti-bullying intervention was effective for reducing bullying behavior in students (p value < 0.05). This intervention involves teachers, government, students, and health workers to collaborate in building a bullying prevention system. The activities carried out are providing education, assertive and empathy training, role play, counseling, and games. The method in implementing the intervention is online and offline. This therapy can be optimized by providing special interventions to victims, perpetrators, and both. The provision of interventions also needs to be carried out and monitored regularly to prevent repeated bullying behavior in students. The development of online interventions is needed to prevent bullying in students so that it can reach students widely.

Introduction

The prevalence of bullying in the world is high. According to the United Nations Education Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO), bullying behavior occurs worldwide and it is estimated that every year there are 245 million children experiencing bullying [1]. Prevalence of bullying is highly reported in Austria, Estonia, Russia and Japan, respectively [2]. Data from United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) shows that 41 to 50% of adolescents aged between 13 and 15 years have social and verbal bullying experiences [3]. Meanwhile, Indonesia is in the fifth highest position out of 78 countries the most students have experiences being victims of bullying [4].

Bullying is an act of using power that aims to harm a person or group of people either physically, verbally, or psychologically so that the victim feels traumatized, depressed because he is unable to fight back against the perpetrator [5]. This can be understood more deeply through Carl Rogers’ existential theory which emphasizes that individuals have a fundamental need to feel accepted and valued [6]. When students feel neglected or unaccepted in their social environment, they may experience an existential crisis that triggers bullying behavior, both as victims and perpetrators [7]. Bullying can be a manifestation of individuals’ inability to fulfill their existential needs, such as self-confidence and social acceptance.

Bullying perpetrators can carry out bullying behavior to anyone and wherever the place is likely to find or become a victim. Bullying toccurs at school, the culprit is none other than a teenager [8]. Bullying actions in the form of insulting friends, asking for friends’ pocket money and even hitting them if their wishes are not fulfilled and inviting their friends to fight, causing a negative physical and psychological impact on the victim [9, 10]. Bullying in schools can be categorized into five forms, namely brawls, violence and bullying, and child victims of policies (extortion, expulsion from school, not allowed to take exams, and dropping out of school).

There are many factors influence the emergence of bullying behavior including family, peer and school factors. In addition to the environmental factors described above, internal factors from individuals contribute to bullying such as self-confidence, gender, and personality type [11]. Another factor that is important in the formation of bullying behavior is social support [12]. Students who have a tendency to be rejected by their friends will show behavior like arguing, disturbing friends, having no shame, and tending to be aggressive [13].

Bullying can cause immediate adverse effects on individuals. In the short term, victims of bullying experience anxiety and fear in the form of physical symptoms such as headaches and stomachaches [14]. Other emotional responses include feelings of sadness, anger, and social isolation. These can interfere with students’ academic performance and social interactions, difficulty concentrating and decreased self-confidence [15]. In addition, the psychological stress caused by bullying can affect sleep patterns and hinder students’ engagement in school and social activities, worsening their overall emotional and physical condition [16].

The long-term impact of bullying can be profound and long-lasting. Victims of bullying can experience chronic mental health problems such as depression, anxiety disorders, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) [17]. Repeated experiences of bullying can result in low self-esteem [18]. Prolonged emotional stress and decreased self-confidence due to bullying also lead to decreased academic performance. In addition, psychological problems from bullying can lead to a reduced quality of life into adulthood [19]. Insecurity and the inability to build healthy relationships often hinder the achievement of an individual’s full potential, affecting various aspects of their daily lives and hampering their overall well-being [20].

Efforts to overcome the problem of bullying in the education system, especially school-age children, are to increase cooperation with related parties in the child’s environment such as educational practitioners, parents and the community around the child [21]. Programs that can be carried out are by promoting social interaction and providing a positive learning environment [22]. Programs to overcome or prevent bullying incidents can be carried out with the Boalian Theater approach, which is a type of Bullying prevention and awareness approach [23, 24]. The KiVa Anti-Bullying Program, which has been developed and disseminated in Finland since 2007, can be used to address or prevent bullying [25]. This program was developed on the basis of several theoretical models of human social behavior and is very effective in preventing and overcoming the incidence of bullying in school-age children. The Kiva Anti-Bullying Program is an initiative that aims to reduce and prevent bullying behavior among children and adolescents in schools [26]. This program is based on the principle that every individual has the right to feel safe and respected in their educational environment.

Previous research shows that KiVa Anti-Bullying is an effective program to reduce bullying behavior in students at school [27]. The program is conducted by involving nurses, teachers, students, and parents. Previous review have shown that school-based interventions can significantly reduce bullying behavior among students [28]. Previous literature only focuses on quantitative data about the effects of the KiVa anti-bullying program, but does not describe information about how the KiVa anti-bullying program is implemented as a picture for health workers. The KiVa Anti-Bullying Program has been conducted on adolescents in general to deal with bullying [26]. The study results show that most of adolescents are students, so the study with scoping review focuses Kiva Anti-Bullying on students is needed.

Programs to overcome or prevent bullying can be done with the KiVa Anti-Bullying Program approach that has been developed and disseminated in Finland since 2007. This program was developed based on several theoretical models of human social behavior and is very effective in preventing and overcoming bullying in children and adolescents. The goal of KiVa Anti-Bullying is to make adolescents aware of the dangers of bullying and help them to become advocates for their peers who are bullied. Teenagers are also expected to prevent students from behaving passively when they see bullying [29]. KiVa anti-bullying also aims to increase youth awareness in behavior to prevent and overcome bullying incidents [30].

Previous literature on the KiVa anti-bullying program has shown its effectiveness in reducing bullying behavior, but there are several limitations that this study needs to address [26]. One of the main limitations is the lack of details on program implementation, specifically how KiVa is adapted and run in various settings. Many studies only focus on the overall effectiveness without describing the strategies, resources, and contextual adaptations required for successful implementation [26, 31]. In addition, practical challenges and barriers during implementation are often poorly addressed, such as variations in school resources, staff training, and community engagement that can affect program success.

This study aims to fill this gap by providing an in-depth analysis of the implementation process, including the adaptation of KiVa to meet the needs of diverse educational contexts and the roles of various stakeholders in its implementation. The scoping review approach allows researchers to comprehensively map the existing literature, identify trends, and determine gaps in previous research. This study will provide a comprehensive overview of how the KiVa anti-bullying program is implemented.

Previous research has shown that health workers and schools have not collaborated sustainably in addressing bullying issues [32]. Based on this, the authors is interested in conducting a scoping review of the KiVa Anti-Bullying Program to reduce bullying behavior in students. The aim of this study is to explore the effectiveness and implementation methods of the KiVa anti-bullying program for preventing and reducing bullying behavior for students. This scoping review will provide an in-depth understanding of the effectiveness and implementation of the KiVa anti-bullying program. As such, this methodology will allow the researcher to comprehensively address the research questions, as well as provide valuable insights into the implementation and long-term impact of the program.

Material and methods

Design

Scoping review method is used in this study. This method aims to describe various previous findings related to various topics or themes that are currently a global issue [33]. The authors selected this method because it has a clear conceptual range to explore various research results [34]. The stages used by the authors consisted of 5 stages, namely determining the research formulation (research question), determining the objectives of the research conducted, determining the inclusion and exclusion criteria, searching for articles from the PubMed, CINAHL, and Scopus databases and selecting articles based on the specified criteria, analyzing and collecting data, and compiling a study report [35]. The authors used the PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) to identify the results of previous research on the Kiva Anti-Bullying program to reduce bullying behavior in students [36]. Ethical approval and patient consent was not required given scoping review design.

The inclusion criteria used in this study are original research with a randomized control trial or quasi-experiment design to obtain good quality experimental research, the sample is university students, English as an international language, peer-reviewed articles, full-text, and the publication period of the last 15 years (2009–2023) in an effort to obtain the latest intervention data. Exclusion criteria were grey literature articles, studies that only focused on program description without providing empirical data on its effectiveness were also excluded, technical reports, theses, and dissertations that were not peer-reviewed.

The article screening process was conducted using a systematic and structured approach to ensure the quality and relevance of the data included in the review by two authors. Using the PRISMA Extension for Scoping Review (PRISMA-Scr) framework, the authors began by searching for articles in three major databases: CINAHL, PubMed, and Scopus. After collecting articles by using keyword-based and synonym-based search strategies. Articles that did not meet the inclusion criteria screened through the website were eliminated in this scoping review. Then, the authors eliminated articles using the Mendeley application based on duplication articles. Furthermore, the screening process was carried out in several stages. First, articles generated from the initial search were screened based on title and abstract. Two independent reviewers conducted an initial assessment of the titles and abstracts to ensure compliance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Any disagreements between raters were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third rater.

Then, articles that met the criteria at the initial stage were further analyzed by reading the full text. At this stage, assessments were made to ensure that articles met the inclusion criteria such as the type of research design (randomized control trial or quasi-experiment), relevant sample, and publication period within the last 15 years. Articles that only described the program without empirical data, as well as grey literature and non-peer-reviewed documents, were excluded from the scoping review process.

Data extraction

The authors obtained articles that met the research objectives based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The authors then read the full-text article and summarized the research results of the article. The important data that the authors got from the article was then entered into a manual table. A manual table was created by the authors to compare research results found from various articles. The table contains data including authors, year of publication, country, research design, sample, interventions used, and research results.

Data extraction was conducted through a systematic approach designed to collect key information from each study that met the inclusion criteria. Data extraction was conducted by two independent authors with expertise relevant to the research theme, performing data extraction in parallel to ensure consistency and accuracy in the extraction process. If there was disagreement between the two authors, the two authors conducted deliberation and consensus. If there was still no decision, a third author was invited to conduct the data extraction process. After the independent extraction process was completed, the authors met to compare and harmonize the findings.

Quality appraisal

The authors conducted a quality appraisal to ensure the quality of the articles used in this study. The quality appraisal in this study used an instrument from The Joanna Brigs Institute (JBI). The authors instrument revised JBI critical appraisal tool in 2023 for RCT design study [37] and revised JBI critical appraisal tool in 2024 for quasi experimental design study [38]. This instrument is a questionnaire containing statements related to the articles read. The authors answered the statements according to the findings of the article. The randomized control trial design contains 13 statements and the quasi-experiment design contains 9 statements. The assessment used by the authors is a joint discussion between 4 authors. If there was disagreement in the discussion, a fifth author was invited to provide additional JBI assessment. The answers to the statements were yes, no, unclear, and not applicable. The “Yes” answers get a score of 1 and other answers get a score of 0. The authors determine the JBI score standard used in writing the scoping review with a minimum score limit of 75%.

Data analysis

Data analysis in this study used a descriptive approach. The authors described Kiva Anti-Bullying based on the results of the article review and classifies it based on the method of implementing KiVa Anti-Bullying. As an effort to avoid selection bias, the authors double-checked to get articles that were relevant to the research. The data analysis process was carried out by discussion with other authors. If there are conflicting decisions on the articles used, the authors will resolve by deliberation. If the votes between authors are not balanced, then the last author is invited to provide an assessment as one of the decision-making processes.

Content analysis method is used to identify, analyze and report themes of research objectives. Content analysis was chosen because of it is flexible and facilitates an in-depth understanding the phenomenon of studies. Data analysis was conducted by two independent authors, who are experts in their fields.

Data analysis began with familiarization of the collected data. Two authors coded the relevant keywords, then, authors grouped related codes into potential themes and developed broader categories based on similarities and relationships between codes. Once the themes were confirmed, two authors independently analyzed and interpreted the findings, relating the themes to existing literature as well as the broader context of the study to draw substantive conclusions about the KiVa Program in the context of anti-bullying.

Association of KiVa anti bullying program on bullying behaviors of 11 studies articles

The authors found 11 articles that discuss the KiVa Anti-Bullying Program to reduce bullying behavior in students. KiVa Anti-Bullying Program is carried out by involving various parties such as teachers, government, students, and health workers. The activities carried out in this program are education, training, self-therapy, role play, counseling, and games. The methods in implementing the intervention are online and offline. Based on studies from eleven research articles, all studies measured the effectiveness or influence of the KiVa Anti-Bullying Program on the incidence of bullying with a p value < 0.05 [29, 39–48]. The authors classified the findings based on similar interventions, namely focusing on prevention, games-based interventions, and focusing on victim recovery.

KiVa anti-bullying based prevention

The intervention was conducted for 5 months. Participants were taught meditation techniques as an effort to increase relaxation in participants. The content of the activities carried out is an increase in empathy for victims, feelings of responsibility to intervene, self-efficacy to defend themselves, negative attitudes towards victims, and outcome expectations that defense will reduce or stop bullying, benefit one’s status, and not increase the risk of becoming a victim [29].

KiVa Anti-Bullying is conducted to prevent bullying behavior in students. Participants were trained to improve empathy skills to prevent bullying behavior. Discussion

Relation to other studies

Based on the literature study that has been conducted by researchers, the intervention participants from 11 articles are focused on school children with ages ranging from 6 to 18 years. This is in line with previous research which shows that the prevalence of bullying occurs mostly in children in junior high school aged 11–15 years. In addition, another study also showed that the incidence of bullying in students in high school was 80%. This study shows that there are three types of interventions: prevention-focused, victim-focused, and game-based.

This review article discusses the effect of KiVa Anti-Bullying Program on the incidence of Bullying.The goal of KiVa Anti-Bullying is to make students aware of the dangers of Bullying and help them to become advocates for their bullied peers [43]. The program is based on intervention and prevention. The implementation of KiVa Anti-Bullying is based on 3 elements, namely anonymous complaints, trained teachers, school support. KiVa has many innovative elements that set it apart from others. KiVa program includes a variety of materials for students, teachers and parents. KiVa utilizes the powerful learning medium provided by the Internet and virtual learning environment [49].

KiVa Anti-Bullying Program increases empathy, self-efficacy and peer support among students. KiVa increases liking and motivation for school, reduces anxiety, depression and negative perceptions of friends [50], The KiVa program improved attitudes and empathy for victims. Overall, these findings provide a significant influence in reducing bullying behavior in students at school. Other studies have shown that KiVa Anti-Bullying is effective in reducing bullying behavior among students in elementary schools [51].

Counseling is the most widely used method in the KiVa Anti-Bullying Program. Counseling aims to find solutions to the problems that students who experience bullying have and to reduce bullying behavior in students [52, 53]. This is in line with previous research which shows that counseling is effective for reducing bullying behavior in students [54]. Counseling can be done online or offline. Online counseling is conducted regularly between participants and counselors, namely mental nurses and counselors. In line with previous research which shows that online counseling is carried out between mental nurses and students by paying attention to the condition of students [55]. Meanwhile, offline counseling is conducted weekly to ensure students’ progress in reducing bullying behavior. The challenge when counseling is to build a trusting relationship between students and counselors [56].

KiVa anti-bullying game is a game that contains education about anti-bullying. The anti-bullying game includes behavioral examples and videos about anti-bullying [57]. The game is attractively packaged to increase student participation in the intervention process. In line with previous studies which show that game-based therapy can increase student participation so that it can effectively reduce bullying behavior [58]. In addition, game-based therapy can also reduce the level of boredom during the intervention. The game used both online and offline methods. Previous research shows that online-based therapy through games can increase students’ knowledge related to bullying.

Increasing knowledge is also an important aspect to prevent bullying in students [59]. In line with previous research which shows that there is a significant relationship between knowledge and bullying behavior in students. Increasing knowledge in students needs to be done because of the low knowledge of students related to bullying [60, 61]. Education about bullying is carried out online in the form of videos and also offline. The discussion method is also an important aspect in increasing students’ knowledge about bullying. Previous research shows that educational videos can be accessed at any time so that they effectively increase students’ knowledge about bullying [62]. Presentation of educational materials is also presented through movies to increase students’ understanding of bullying. This is supported by previous research which shows that education conducted by nurses through online methods and discussions is an effective method to increase student knowledge from low to high [63].

Bullying behavior can be reduced by training empathy and assertiveness in students at school. An empathetic attitude can increase students’ concern for others and not commit bullying behavior on others. This is supported by previous research showing that empathy training can increase concern and reduce bullying behavior in students [64]. Assertive attitude can also increase honesty in students, thus preventing insults and verbal bullying in students. Previous research shows that the assertive enhancement method can reduce bullying behavior in students [65]. Other studies have also shown that there is a negative relationship between assertive attitudes and bullying behavior [66, 67].

Strengths and limitations

Bullying prevention interventions are important in order to reduce the prevalence of bullying in students. Some of the KiVa Anti-Bullying methods that can be carried out are education, counseling, and anti-bullying games. The media provided are asynchronous videos, synchronous communication videos, and also through movies. In addition, the intervention also focuses on the victim to reduce the impact of bullying [68].

This research demonstrates significant strengths that make it a strong source of evidence for practice and policy in bullying prevention. The scoping review also presents the most recent findings from the last 15 years, making it a relevant database to use in the current situation. This research also presents information on the implementation of KiVa anti-bullying from articles with high quality designs, namely quasi experiments and RCTs. Consistent and significant results based on data on the effectiveness of the program in reducing bullying behavior. All reviewed studies reported p-values < 0.05, indicating that there was a significant effect of the intervention. The KiVa program, which uses a multifaceted approach including education, training, counseling, and games, demonstrates flexibility and adaptability that allows it to be applied in a variety of educational contexts. This method ensures that the intervention can be tailored to the specific needs of students, and can be accessed through online and offline platforms.

Innovations from Kiva’s diverse programs can serve as a basis for designing KiVa programs according to student needs. Interventions include empathy training, social skills development, as well as games designed to interest students and increase their engagement. The program involves various stakeholders, including teachers, students, parents, and health workers, which strengthens the collaborative approach in bullying prevention. The flexibility in both game-based and counseling intervention methods demonstrates its ability to adapt to various situations and student preferences. The involvement of health workers such as psychologists and nurses in the KiVa program demonstrates their crucial role in supporting students’ mental health and providing emotional support to victims of bullying. This opens up opportunities for the integration of bullying prevention programs with school health services, strengthening a holistic approach in addressing bullying issues.

The limitation of this research is that three databases were used, so it did not reach other databases on the topic of KiVa Anti-Bullying. Although the database used in this study is a large database, research published in other databases is important to add data about KiVa Anti-Bullying. Another limitation in this study is the year of publication which is limited to the last 15 years so that it cannot compare the results of research published outside of that time.

Interpretation

This study examined bullying prevention interventions that focused on school-aged children between 6 and 18 years old. The findings are in line with previous research showing a high prevalence of bullying in junior high (11–15 years old) and high school students (80%). Three main types of interventions were identified: prevention, victim-focused, and play-based. The KiVa Anti-Bullying Program was shown to be effective in reducing bullying behavior by increasing empathy, self-efficacy, and peer support among students. The program involves training, counseling, and games designed to capture students’ attention and increase their engagement. Counseling, both online and offline, is the primary method, with evidence showing its effectiveness in addressing bullying issues. In addition, anti-bullying and education-based games presented both online and offline were shown to increase students’ knowledge about bullying, reduce boredom, and improve attitudes. These findings support the importance of a multidimensional approach in bullying prevention programs and suggest that empathy and assertiveness training can reduce bullying behavior in schools.

Implications

This study provides significant implications for both practice and policy in addressing bullying in schools, as well as demonstrating the importance of the roles of various stakeholders. From a practice point of view, the study results show that the KiVa Anti-Bullying program, which includes various approaches such as education, counseling, games, and empathy enhancement, is effective in reducing bullying behavior among students. This approach is aligned with Rogers’ existential theory, which emphasizes the importance of understanding and empathy in overcoming psychological problems. The program illustrates the importance of integrating interventions that focus on prevention, victim recovery, and the use of game-based methods, the program can increase student engagement and reduce feelings of anxiety and isolation often experienced by victims of bullying.

Policy-wise, the findings highlight the need to support and expand the implementation of comprehensive, evidence-based anti-bullying programs like KiVa across educational institutions. Policies should encourage the use of a multifaceted approach that involves training for teachers, support for students, and involvement of parents and communities in addressing bullying. In addition, policies need to ensure that adequate resources and training are provided to educators and health professionals in schools, who play an important role in implementing and evaluating the effectiveness of anti-bullying programs. Policies that support evidence-based approaches will help create safer school environments and support students’ emotional wellbeing, in line with social change theory that underscores the need for integrated and sustainable interventions to address complex social problems.

Conclusion

Based on the results of the study, the KiVa Anti-Bullying program proved to be effective in reducing bullying behavior among students. The research identified three main approaches in this program: prevention, focus on victim recovery, and game-based intervention. The findings show that this program, involving methods such as education, counseling, and interactive games, was successful in increasing empathy, engagement, and reducing bullying behavior across different age groups. The media used are educational videos, materials using modules or power points, and online discussion media. The implementation of this program can be carried out in primary interventions, where psychiatric nurses can develop the KiVa Anti-Bullying services for discussions, counseling, and education aimed at preventing bullying among students.

Practically, these results confirm the need for structured implementation of KiVa programs in schools to create a safer and more supportive environment. Policy-wise, the recommendations encourage the integration of evidence-based anti-bullying programs in school curricula as well as the provision of adequate training for educators and health professionals. Policies that support multi-faceted and evidence-based interventions will strengthen bullying prevention efforts and support students’ overall emotional well-being. This study only describes qualitative findings interpreted by the authors, so the effectiveness data in the KiVa anti-bullying intervention cannot demonstrate the significance of the intervention effect. In addition, the diverse age range of the population in the child and adolescent developmental stages may have led to research bias. Thus, recommendations for further research are the need to analyze the effectiveness of the KiVa Anti-Bullying Program to reduce bullying behavior with a homogeneous population such as children or adolescents with a systematic review and meta-analysis design.

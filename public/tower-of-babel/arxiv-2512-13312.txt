Open Homomorphisms between m m -step Solvable Galois Groups Compatible with the Cyclotomic Characters
Yu Mao, Mohamed Saïdi

Abstract
In [ 2 ] , Hoshi proved that open homomorphisms between solvably closed Galois groups of number fields which are compatible with the cyclotomic characters arise from field embeddings. In this paper, we will prove an m m -step solvable version of Hoshi’s result. More precisely, if K K and L L are number fields, we will prove that given an open homomorphism between the maximal m + 3 m+3 -step solvable Galois groups of K K and L L , where m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , and the induced open homomorphism between the corresponding maximal m m -step solvable Galois groups, then the latter arises from a field embedding if and only if the open homomorphism between the m + 3 m+3 -step solvable (and hence also the m m -step solvable) Galois groups is compatible with the cyclotomic characters of K K and L L .

Introduction

Recall that the Neukirch-Uchida Theorem asserts that two number fields
K
and

are isomorphic if and only if
G_{K}^{\text{sol}}
and

are isomorphic as profinite groups (c.f.
[

In 1981, Uchida gave a conditional solution to this question:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem 2 in
[
5

)
.

Let
K
and
L
be number fields, and let

\sigma:G_{L}^{\text{sol}}\to G_{K}^{\text{sol}}

be a continuous homomorphism such that the following condition holds. For each non-archimedean prime
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}
of

, there exists a non-archimedean prime
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
of

such that
\sigma
restricts to an open homomorphism
\sigma:D_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}}\to D_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
where
D_{\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}}
and
D_{\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}}
are decomposition groups at
\tilde{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\tilde{\mathfrak{q}}
, respectively. Then there exists a unique field embedding
\tau:K^{\text{sol}}\hookrightarrow L^{\text{sol}}
such that
\tau\sigma(g)=g\tau
for all
g\in G_{L}^{\text{sol}}
. Furthermore,
\tau
restricts to a field embedding
K\hookrightarrow L
.

The Proof of Uniqueness in Theorem 1.5

In this section, we prove that in the implication (ii)
\implies
(i) in Theorem 1.5, if such

exists, then it is unique. Throughout this section we use the same notations as in Theorem 1.5 and assume that the assertion (ii) in Theorem 1.5 holds true.

Proposition 2.1

If a field embedding

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Gal}(L_{0}/L)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{m}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

where the map

. Moreover,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{L_{0}}}

(resp.

(resp. in

\widetilde{H}\twoheadrightarrow\widetilde{H^{\prime}}
and are equivariant with respective to
\beta
.

On the other hand, since the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{G_{K}^{m+3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{G_{L}^{m+3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{m+3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{K}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{L}}

commutes, we may conclude that we have a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{G_{F}^{\text{ab}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{G_{F^{\prime}}^{\text{ab}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\times}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{F}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{F^{\prime}}}

where
\chi_{F}
and
\chi_{F^{\prime}}
are the cyclotomic characters of
F
and
F^{\prime}
, respectively. By Lemma 3.5 it holds that the map
\varphi:A_{2}(F)\twoheadrightarrow A_{2}(F^{\prime})
is Frobenius-preserving (c.f. Definition 2.7 in
[
2
]
). We conclude by Corollary 2.8 in
[
2
]
that the map
A_{2}(F)\twoheadrightarrow A_{2}(F^{\prime})
determines a unique field embedding
\phi:F^{\prime}\hookrightarrow F
.

Now take
g\in\text{Gal}(F/K)
and
g^{\prime}:=\beta(g)\in\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/L)
. We have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{\prime}}

where
\phi^{\prime}
is defined to be
\phi^{\prime}:=g\phi g^{\prime-1}
.

Since the map
\varphi:A_{2}(F)\twoheadrightarrow A_{2}(F^{\prime})
is equivariant with respect to
\beta
, we have
\varphi(g.x)=g^{\prime}\varphi(x)
for all
x\in A_{2}(F)
. By Corollary 2.8 in
[
2
]
,
\varphi
arises uniquely from
\phi
. On the other hand,
\phi^{\prime}
also determines a map
\varphi^{\prime}:A_{2}(F)\twoheadrightarrow A_{2}(F^{\prime})
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}(F^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}(F)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{2}(F^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\wr}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}

where the left vertical arrow is given by the action of
g
and the right vertical arrow is given by the action of
g^{\prime}
. In particular,
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}
by the commutativity of the above diagram and the fact that
\varphi
is
\beta
-equivariant. Hence
\phi^{\prime}=\phi
. So
\phi=g\phi g^{\prime-1}=g\phi\beta(g)^{-1}\implies\phi\beta(g)=g\phi
, i.e.
\beta
arises uniquely from
\phi
.

On the other hand, we have the following identifications:

G_{K}^{m}\cong\varprojlim_{F}\text{Gal}(F/K)~;~G_{L}^{m}\cong\varprojlim_{F^{\prime}}\text{Gal}(F^{\prime}/L)~~~~~~(\dagger)

where
F^{\prime}
ranges over all finite Galois totally imaginary extensions of
L
contained in
L_{m}
, and
F
ranges over finite Galois extensions of
K
contained in
K_{m}
corresponding to
F^{\prime}
.

Write
\text{Hom}_{\beta}(F^{\prime},F)
for the set of field embeddings
F^{\prime}\to F
that arise from
\beta
. It follows from the identification
(\dagger)
that we have

\varprojlim_{F^{\prime}}~\text{Hom}_{\beta}(F^{\prime},F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{Hom}_{\alpha_{m}}(L_{m},K_{m})

where
\text{Hom}_{\alpha_{m}}(L_{m},K_{m})
is the set of all field embeddings
L_{m}\to K_{m}
arising from
\alpha_{m}
. Furthermore, it follows from the various constructions involved, that
\text{Hom}_{\beta}(F^{\prime},F)
is (finite) non-empty, hence
\text{Hom}_{\alpha_{m}}(L_{m},K_{m})
is also non-empty since it is the inverse limit of non-empty finite sets. Thus, we can conclude that there exists a field embedding
\tilde{\beta}:L_{m}\to K_{m}
arising from
\alpha_{m}
, and by Proposition 2.1
\tilde{\beta}
is unique.
∎

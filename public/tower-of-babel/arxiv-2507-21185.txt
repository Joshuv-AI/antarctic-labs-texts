Comparison principle for Singular Fractional g − g- Laplacian Problems
Abdelhamid Gouasmia, Kaushik Bal

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a novel comparison principle of independent interest and prove the uniqueness of weak solutions within the local Orlicz–Sobolev space framework, for the following class of fractional elliptic problems: ( − Δ ) g s u = f ( x ) u − α + k ( x ) u β , u > 0 in Ω ; u = 0 in ℝ N ∖ Ω , (-\Delta)^{s}_{g}u=f(x)u^{-\alpha}+k(x)u^{\beta},\quad u>0\quad\text{in }\Omega;\quad u=0\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega, where Ω ⊂ ℝ N \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} is a smooth bounded domain, α > 0 \alpha>0 , and β > 0 \beta>0 satisfies a suitable upper bound. Here, ( − Δ ) g s (-\Delta)^{s}_{g} denotes the fractional g g -Laplacian, with g g being the derivative of a Young function G G . The function f f is assumed to be nontrivial, while k k is a positive function, and both f f and k k are assumed to lie in suitable Orlicz spaces. Our analysis relies on a refined variational approach that incorporates a G G -fractional version of the Díaz–Saa inequality together with a G G -fractional analogue of Picone’s identity. These tools, which are of independent interest, also play a key role in the study of simplicity of eigenvalues, Sturmian-type comparison results, Hardy-type inequalities, and related topics.

In recent years, considerable attention has been devoted to the study of nonlocal elliptic operators, particularly in connection with problems exhibiting anomalous diffusion and transport phenomena. In this context, a fractional counterpart of the classical Orlicz-Sobolev space naturally emerges. In particular, the authors in
[
14
]
introduced the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev space associated with an
N

G

W^{s,G}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega):\hskip 9.24994pt\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}G\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dxdy}{\left|x-y\right|^{N}}<\infty\right\},

Recently, considerable attention has been devoted to elliptic problems involving the fractional

2.1.
Preliminaries and Functional Setting

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with
C^{0,1}
-regular boundary. We adopt the following definitions:

A function
G:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is called an
N
-function
if it is continuous, convex, and satisfies:

G(t)=0\iff t=0,\quad\frac{G(t)}{t}\to 0\text{ as }t\to 0,\quad\frac{G(t)}{t}\to\infty\text{ as }t\to\infty.

Equivalently, the function
G
can be represented as

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(s)\,ds,

where
g:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a non-decreasing and right-continuous function satisfying

g(t)=0\iff t=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

In addition, throughout this work, we assume that
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. The following examples of the function
G
fall within our framework:

(1)

G(t):=t
. In this case, the associated operator reduces to the standard fractional Laplacian:

(-\Delta)^{s}_{g}u=(-\Delta)^{s}u.

(2)

G(t):=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}
, where
p\geq 2
. This corresponds to the fractional
p
-Laplacian operator.

(3)

G(t):=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}\left(\left|\ln(t)\right|+1\right)
, where
p\geq 2
. This function exhibits slower growth near zero and is useful in modeling singular behaviors.

(4)

G(t):=\frac{1}{p}t^{p}+\frac{1}{q}t^{q}
, with
p,q\geq 2
. In this case, the operator takes the form

(-\Delta)^{s}_{g}u=(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u+(-\Delta)^{s}_{q}u,

which corresponds to the
(p,q)
-fractional Laplacian.

It is established in
[

(H4)
is equivalent to the so-called

G(2t)\leq 2^{p^{+}}G(t)\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

(

Moreover, the same condition

2<p^{-}\leq\frac{t\,g(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}\leq\infty,\quad\text{for all }t>0.

Proposition 2.5

(
G
-fractional hidden convexity)
.

Let
G
be an
N
-function satisfying assumption
(H4)
, and suppose that
1<q\leq p^{-}
. For every pair of nonnegative functions
u_{0},u_{1}
, define

\sigma_{t}(x):=\left[(1-t)\,u_{0}^{q}(x)+t\,u_{1}^{q}(x)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad t\in[0,1],\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then, for all
t\in[0,1]
and all

, the following inequality holds:

G\left(\left|\sigma_{t}(x)-\sigma_{t}(y)\right|\right)\leq(1-t)\,G\left(\left|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)\right|\right)+t\,G\left(\left|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\right|\right).

Proof.

As established in
[
26
, Lemma 4.1]
, we have

\left|\sigma_{t}(x)-\sigma_{t}(y)\right|\leq\left[(1-t)\,\left|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)\right|^{q}+t\,\left|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\right|^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,1]\text{ and }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We first observe that the mapping
s\mapsto G\left(s^{1/q}\right)
is convex. Indeed, a straightforward computation, combined with the regularity assumption
g\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
and condition

\dfrac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}G\left(s^{1/q}\right)=\dfrac{s^{\frac{1}{q}-2}}{q^{2}}\,g\left(s^{1/q}\right)\left[1-q+\dfrac{s^{1/q}\,g^{\prime}\left(s^{1/q}\right)}{g\left(s^{1/q}\right)}\right]\geq 0.

Hence, for all

and
t\in[0,1]

implies that

G\left(\left[(1-t)a+tb\right]^{1/q}\right)\leq(1-t)\,G\left(a^{1/q}\right)+t\,G\left(b^{1/q}\right).

Applying this inequality with

and
b=|u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)|^{q}
concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.6

(
G
-fractional Picone inequality)
.

Let
G
be an
N
-function, and let
u,v
be measurable functions satisfying
u>0
,

, and
1<q\leq p^{-}
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(p^{-},p^{+},G(1))>0
such that the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle g\left(\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq C\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{G\left(\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|\right)}{G(1)}\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{+}}},\left(\dfrac{G\left(\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|\right)}{G(1)}\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{-}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\times\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{G\left(\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{G(1)}\right)^{\frac{p^{-}-q}{p^{+}}},\left(\dfrac{G\left(\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|\right)}{G(1)}\right)^{\frac{p^{+}-q}{p^{-}}}\right\}.

Proof.

We begin by noting that if
u(x)=u(y)
, then inequality (
2.3
) holds trivially. Thus, we may assume
u(x)\neq u(y)
. Without loss of generality, suppose
u(x)>u(y)
, since inequality (
2.3
) is invariant under the permutation
(x,y)\mapsto(y,x)
. Moreover, if

\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\leq 0,

then inequality (
2.3
) is again trivially satisfied. Otherwise, we distinguish between the following four cases:

Case 1:

|u(x)-u(y)|<1
and
|v(x)-v(y)|<1
. In this case, using (
2.1
) and the fractional Picone inequality
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]
, we obtain

\displaystyle g\left(|u(x)-u(y)|\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{-}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)^{1+\frac{p^{-}}{p^{+}}}\left(G(|v(x)-v(y)|)\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{+}}}\left(G(|u(x)-u(y)|)\right)^{\frac{p^{-}-q}{p^{+}}}.

|u(x)-u(y)|>1
and
|v(x)-v(y)|>1
. Proceeding similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle g\left(|u(x)-u(y)|\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{+}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|v(x)-v(y)|^{q}\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{+}-q}

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)^{1+\frac{p^{+}}{p^{-}}}\left(G(|v(x)-v(y)|)\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{-}}}\left(G(|u(x)-u(y)|)\right)^{\frac{p^{+}-q}{p^{-}}}.

Case 3:

|u(x)-u(y)|>1
and
|v(x)-v(y)|<1
. By the same arguments,

\displaystyle g\left(|u(x)-u(y)|\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{+}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|v(x)-v(y)|^{q}\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{+}-q}

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)^{\frac{(p^{+})^{2}-q(p^{+}-p^{-})}{p^{+}p^{-}}}\left(G(|v(x)-v(y)|)\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{+}}}\left(G(|u(x)-u(y)|)\right)^{\frac{p^{+}-q}{p^{-}}}.

Case 4:

|u(x)-u(y)|<1
and
|v(x)-v(y)|>1
. Again, we deduce

\displaystyle g\left(|u(x)-u(y)|\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|u(x)-u(y)|}\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{-}-2}(u(x)-u(y))\left[\dfrac{v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\dfrac{v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)|v(x)-v(y)|^{q}\,|u(x)-u(y)|^{p^{-}-q}

\displaystyle\leq p^{+}G(1)^{\frac{q(p^{+}-p^{-})+(p^{-})^{2}}{p^{+}p^{-}}}\left(G(|v(x)-v(y)|)\right)^{\frac{q}{p^{-}}}\left(G(|u(x)-u(y)|)\right)^{\frac{p^{-}-q}{p^{+}}}.

Combining the estimates obtained in all four cases concludes the proof.

Let
X
be a real vector space, and let
C\subset X
be a nonempty convex cone.
A functional
\mathscr{W}:C\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be ray-strictly convex (respectively, strictly convex) if

\mathscr{W}((1-t)v_{1}+tv_{2})\leq(1-t)\,\mathscr{W}(v_{1})+t\,\mathscr{W}(v_{2})\quad\text{for all }v_{1},v_{2}\in C\text{ and all }t\in(0,1),

with strict inequality unless
\frac{v_{1}}{v_{2}}\equiv c>0
(unless
v_{1}\equiv v_{2}
, respectively).

By Proposition
2.5
, the set

\dot{V}_{+}^{q}:=\left\{u:\Omega\to(0,\infty)\;\middle|\;u^{\frac{1}{q}}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\right\},\quad\text{for }1<q\leq p^{-},

is a convex cone. That is, for all
\lambda\in(0,1)
and all
f,g\in\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
, it holds that
\lambda f+(1-\lambda)g\in\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
G
be an
N
-function satisfying
(H4)
, and suppose that
1<q\leq p^{-}
. Then the functional

\mathscr{W}:\dot{V}_{+}^{q}\to\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad\mathscr{W}(w):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G\left(\frac{\left|w(x)^{1/q}-w(y)^{1/q}\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N}},

is ray-strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
. Moreover, if
q\neq p^{-}
, then
\mathscr{W}
is strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
.

Proof.

According to Definition
2.1
, let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
and
t\in[0,1]
. Define
w=(1-t)w_{1}+tw_{2}
. Then, by Proposition
2.5
, we have

\mathscr{W}(w)\leq(1-t)\,\mathscr{W}(w_{1})+t\,\mathscr{W}(w_{2}).

Assume now that equality holds. Then, for almost every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we have

G\left(\frac{\left|w(x)^{1/q}-w(y)^{1/q}\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)=(1-t)\,G\left(\frac{\left|w_{1}(x)^{1/q}-w_{1}(y)^{1/q}\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)+t\,G\left(\frac{\left|w_{2}(x)^{1/q}-w_{2}(y)^{1/q}\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right).

Define an
N
-function
H
by
H^{-1}(s)=G(s^{1/p^{-}})
. Since the mapping
s\mapsto G(s^{1/p^{-}})
is convex, it follows that
H
is concave. Then, by
[
16
, Proposition 4.1]
, we obtain

\left|w(x)^{1/q}-w(y)^{1/q}\right|^{p^{-}}=(1-t)\,\left|w_{1}(x)^{1/q}-w_{1}(y)^{1/q}\right|^{p^{-}}+t\,\left|w_{2}(x)^{1/q}-w_{2}(y)^{1/q}\right|^{p^{-}}.

If
p^{-}=q
, then we deduce

\left|\|a\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{q}}-\|b\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{q}}\right|^{q}=\|a-b\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{q}}^{q}\quad\text{for a.e. }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\|\cdot\|_{\boldsymbol{\ell}^{q}}
denotes the
\boldsymbol{\ell}^{q}
-norm in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and

a=\left(((1-t)w_{1}(x))^{1/q},(tw_{2}(x))^{1/q}\right),\quad b=\left(((1-t)w_{1}(y))^{1/q},(tw_{2}(y))^{1/q}\right).

Since
q>1
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that
w_{1}=cw_{2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Thus,
\mathscr{W}
is ray-strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
. On the other hand, if
p^{-}\neq q
, then by the strict convexity of the mapping
\tau\mapsto\tau^{\frac{p^{-}}{q}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, it follows that
w_{1}=w_{2}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Therefore,
\mathscr{W}
is strictly convex on
\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
.
∎

We now establish the following extension of the Díaz and Saa inequality, involving the
g
-Laplacian operator, which plays a fundamental role in the analysis of the uniqueness of problem (
P
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
G
be an
N
-function satisfying
(H4)
, and suppose that
1<q\leq p^{-}
. Then the following inequality holds in the sense of distributions:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\left(\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}\left(\frac{u(x)^{q}-v(x)^{q}}{u(x)^{q-1}}-\frac{u(y)^{q}-v(y)^{q}}{u(y)^{q-1}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\left(\frac{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{v(x)-v(y)}{\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|}\left(\frac{v(x)^{q}-u(x)^{q}}{v(x)^{q-1}}-\frac{v(y)^{q}-u(y)^{q}}{v(y)^{q-1}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\geq 0,

for all pairs
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u>0
and
v>0
a.e. in
\Omega
, and both
u/v
and
v/u
belong to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, if equality holds in (
2.4
), then the following assertions are satisfied:

(1)

u/v\equiv\text{const}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

(2)

If in addition
q\neq p^{-}
, then
u\equiv v
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

First, note that both integrals above are well-defined as Lebesgue integrals. Indeed, there exists a constant
M>0
such that

0\leq\frac{u^{q}}{v^{q-1}}=\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{q-1}u\leq Mu,\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq\frac{v^{q}}{u^{q-1}}=\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{q-1}v\leq Mv.

By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, it follows that
\frac{u^{q}}{v^{q-1}}\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\frac{v^{q}}{u^{q-1}}\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and both functions vanish outside
\Omega
. Applying the Lagrange Mean Value Theorem yields:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{u^{q}(x)}{v^{q-1}(x)}-\frac{u^{q}(y)}{v^{q-1}(y)}\right|\leq\left|\frac{u^{q}(x)}{v^{q-1}(x)}-\frac{u^{q}(y)}{v^{q-1}(x)}\right|+\left|\frac{u^{q}(y)}{v^{q-1}(x)}-\frac{u^{q}(y)}{v^{q-1}(y)}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{q\,\max\left\{u^{q-1}(x),u^{q-1}(y)\right\}}{v^{q-1}(x)}\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|+\frac{u^{q}(y)\,(q-1)\,\max\left\{v^{q-2}(x),v^{q-2}(y)\right\}}{v^{q-1}(x)\,v^{q-1}(y)}\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left(q,\left\|u/v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left(1/2\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|+1/2\left|v(x)-v(y)\right|\right),

for some constant
C>0
. Therefore, again by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, we conclude that
\frac{u^{q}}{v^{q-1}},\frac{v^{q}}{u^{q-1}}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Now, consider
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u>0
and
v>0
a.e. in
\Omega
, and let
\theta\in(0,1)
. Define

w:=(1-\theta)u^{q}+\theta v^{q}.

By Proposition
2.5
, it is easily seen that
w\in\dot{V}_{+}^{q}
. Hence, by Proposition
2.7
, the function

\theta\mapsto\Phi(\theta):=\mathscr{W}(w)=\mathscr{W}\left((1-\theta)u^{q}+\theta v^{q}\right)

is convex and differentiable on
[0,1]
. For
\theta\in(0,1)
, we compute:

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus(\Omega^{c}\times\Omega^{c})}g\left(\frac{\left|w(x)^{1/q}-w(y)^{1/q}\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{w(x)^{1/q}-w(y)^{1/q}}{\left|w(x)^{1/q}-w(y)^{1/q}\right|}\left(\frac{v(x)^{q}-u(x)^{q}}{w(x)^{1-1/q}}-\frac{v(y)^{q}-u(y)^{q}}{w(y)^{1-1/q}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{|x-y|^{N+s}}.

Using the convexity of
\Phi
, and the identities
w=u^{q}
when
\theta=0
, and
w=v^{q}
when
\theta=1
, we obtain:

\Phi^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{\theta\to 0^{+}}\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)\leq\lim_{\theta\to 1^{-}}\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)=\Phi^{\prime}(1),

which is equivalent to inequality (
2.4
). Finally, assume that equality holds in (
2.4
). Since
\Phi^{\prime}:(0,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is monotone, it follows that
\Phi^{\prime}(\theta)=\text{const}
on
(0,1)
, and hence
\Phi:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is linear:

\Phi(\theta)=\mathscr{W}(w)=(1-\theta)\Phi(0)+\theta\Phi(1)=(1-\theta)\mathscr{W}(u^{q})+\theta\mathscr{W}(v^{q}),

for all
\theta\in[0,1]
. We deduce that
u\equiv cv
for some
c>0
. Moreover, if
q\neq p^{-}
, then
u\equiv v
by Proposition
2.7
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

An alternative proof of Theorem 4.2 can be obtained by employing the
G
-fractional Picone inequality stated in Proposition
2.6
, together with Young’s inequality. This approach leads directly to the derivation of (

G

2.4
), and is of independent interest.

Before presenting our main results, we define the set
FC
, which consists of the following class of functions:

\textbf{FC}=\left\{\Psi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)\;\middle|\;\begin{array}[]{l}\text{(i) }\Psi\text{ is of class }C^{1}\text{ and strictly convex},\\[4.0pt]
\text{(ii) }\Psi^{\prime}\text{ is increasing on }(0,\infty),\\[4.0pt]
\text{(iii) there exist constants }\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\geq 0\text{ such that}\\[2.0pt]
\qquad\theta_{1}\dfrac{\Psi(x)}{x}\leq\Psi^{\prime}(x)\leq\theta_{2}\dfrac{\Psi(x)}{x}\quad\forall x>0.\end{array}\right\}.

2.2.
Statements of main results

We first observe that, under the assumptions imposed on the function
g
, the mapping
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is injective. Therefore, by the definition of the complementary function
\overline{G}
associated with

, we obtain the following estimate:

(p^{-}-1)\,G(t)\leq\overline{G}(g(t))\leq(p^{+}-1)\,G(t),\quad\text{ for all }t\geq 0.

(2.5)

Accordingly, we introduce the notions of weak sub-solutions, super-solutions, and solutions to problem (
P
):

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
u\in W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is a
weak super-solution
to (

) if the following conditions hold:

(i)

There exists a function
\Psi\in\textbf{FC}
such that
\Psi(u)\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

For every compact set

such that

(iii)

For all test functions
\varphi\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)

, the following inequality holds:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\left(\frac{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{\left|u(x)-u(y)\right|}\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{\left|x-y\right|^{N+s}}\,dx\,dy\geq\int_{\Omega}\left(f(x)\,u^{-\alpha}+k(x)\,u^{\beta}\right)\varphi(x)\,dx.

If

satisfies the reverse inequality in (iii), then it is called a

to problem (

). A function

that satisfies the requirements of both a weak sub and super-solution is referred to as a

to (

).

Remark 2.3
.

An important observation regarding Definition
2.2
is that the solution

generally does not belong to the space
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, it is important to emphasize that the space

does not admit a trace operator. For this reason, we adopt the following interpretation of the Dirichlet boundary condition in a generalized sense (see
[

):

“
We say that

on

if

in

for every

. Moreover, we say that

on

if

and

on

.
”

It is crucial to note that Definition
2.2
-
(i)
ensures that the solution satisfies the conditions of this definition. In particular, since
\Psi(u)\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
for a function
\Psi\in\textbf{FC}
, and given any

, we define

\mathscr{S}_{\epsilon}:=\operatorname{supp}(u-\epsilon)^{+},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{Q}_{\epsilon}:=(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus(\mathscr{S}_{\epsilon}^{c}\times\mathscr{S}_{\epsilon}^{c}).

Then, invoking

\displaystyle\min\left\{\left\|(u-\epsilon)^{+}\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}},\,\left\|(u-\epsilon)^{+}\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p^{+}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G\left(\frac{\left|(u-\epsilon)^{+}(x)-(u-\epsilon)^{+}(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{N}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\epsilon)\iint_{\mathscr{Q}_{\epsilon}}G\left(\frac{\left|\Psi(u(x))-\Psi(u(y))\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{N}}

As a consequence, we conclude that

for every

3.
Proof of the Main Results

In this section, we begin with the proof of our first main result.

Proof of Theorem
2.9
.

First, we define the following convex and closed subset of
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
:

\mathscr{K}:=\left\{\phi\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,0\leq\phi\leq\overline{u}\text{ a.e. in }\Omega\right\}.

Then, for each fixed
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we introduce the functional
\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}:\mathscr{K}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}(w)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G\left(\frac{\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{s}}\right)\frac{dx\,dy}{\left|x-y\right|^{N}}-\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{G}_{\epsilon}(x,w)\,dx,

where

\mathbb{G}_{\epsilon}(x,w):=\int_{0}^{w}\left[f(x)(s+\epsilon)^{-\alpha}+k(x)(s+\epsilon)^{\beta}\right]\,ds.

(3.1)

At this stage, we aim to establish three fundamental claims. We begin with the following:

Claim 3.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a minimizer
w_{0}\in\mathscr{K}
of the functional
\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}
such that, for every
\psi\in w_{0}+(W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega))
with
\psi\in\mathscr{K}
, the following variational inequality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle g\left(\frac{\left|w_{0}(x)-w_{0}(y)\right|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{w_{0}(x)-w_{0}(y)}{\left|w_{0}(x)-w_{0}(y)\right|}\cdot\frac{[(\psi-w_{0})(x)-(\psi-w_{0})(y)]}{|x-y|^{N+s}}\,dx\,dy

(3.2)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}\left[f(x)(w_{0}+\epsilon)^{-\alpha}+k(x)(w_{0}+\epsilon)^{\beta}\right](\psi-w_{0})\,dx.

First, by combining Lemma
2.3
with Lemma
2.4
(see also Remark
2.1
), we deduce that the functional
\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}
is well defined on
\mathscr{K}
. Next, we establish that
\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}
is coercive on
\mathscr{K}
. Indeed, let
w\in\mathscr{K}
. By applying Hölder’s inequality (see Lemma
2.2
) together with Remark
2.1
, and assuming that
k\in L^{\overline{K}}(\Omega)
, where
\overline{K}
denotes the complementary function of the
N
-function
K
, defined by

K(t):=G_{*}\left(t^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}\right),

we obtain the following conclusion depending on the value of
\alpha
, provided that
\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently large.

Case 1.
Assume that
0<\alpha<1
. Then, we observe that

\int_{\Omega}fu^{1-\alpha}\,dx\leq 2\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\overline{\mathfrak{F}}}(\Omega)}\left\|u^{1-\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\mathfrak{F}}(\Omega)},

where the modular norm is given by

\displaystyle\left\|u^{1-\alpha}\right\|_{L^{\mathfrak{F}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{F}\left(\frac{u^{1-\alpha}}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\left[\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}G_{*}\left(\frac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}\right]^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{G_{*}}(\Omega)}^{1-\alpha},

with
\mathfrak{F}
defined in (
2.6
). In addition, it holds that

\left\|u^{1+\beta}\right\|_{L^{K}(\Omega)}=\left\|u\right\|_{L^{G_{*}}(\Omega)}^{1+\beta}.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}(w)

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}}-C\left[\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\overline{\mathfrak{F}}}(\Omega)}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{G_{*}}(\Omega)}^{1-\alpha}+\left\|k\right\|_{L^{\overline{K}}(\Omega)}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{G_{*}}(\Omega)}^{1+\beta}+1\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}}\left[1-C\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\overline{\mathfrak{F}}}(\Omega)}\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{1-\alpha-p^{-}}+\left\|k\right\|_{L^{\overline{K}}(\Omega)}\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{1+\beta-p^{-}}+\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{-p^{-}}\right)\right].

Case 2:
When
\alpha>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\epsilon}(w)

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}}-C\left(\left\|k\right\|_{L^{\overline{K}}(\Omega)}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{G_{*}}(\Omega)}^{1+\beta}+1\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{p^{-}}\left[1-C\left(\left\|k\right\|_{L^{\overline{K}}(\Omega)}\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{1+\beta-p^{-}}+\left\|w\right\|_{W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)}^{-p^{-}}\right)\right].

Case 3:
For
\alpha=1
, we observe that there exists a constant
C(\epsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle\leq h(x,\underline{u})\left(\frac{(\underline{u}+m)^{p^{-}}-(\overline{u}+m)^{p^{-}}}{(\underline{u}+m)^{p^{-}-1}}\right)\chi_{\left\{\underline{u}>\overline{u}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq h(x,\underline{u})\left(\frac{(\underline{u}+m)^{p^{-}}-m^{p^{-}}}{(\underline{u}+m)^{p^{-}-1}}\right)\chi_{\left\{\underline{u}>\overline{u}\right\}}

\displaystyle\leq h(x,\underline{u})\,\underline{u}\,\chi_{\left\{\underline{u}>\overline{u}\right\}}\in L^{1}(\Omega),

where the last integrability follows from Definition
2.3
. Therefore, combining this with the reasoning used in the proof of Theorem
2.9
, we conclude that

\int_{\left\{\underline{u}>\overline{u}\right\}}\left(\frac{h(x,\underline{u})}{\underline{u}^{p^{-}-1}}-\frac{h(x,\overline{u})}{\overline{u}^{p^{-}-1}}\right)(\underline{u}^{p^{-}}-\overline{u}^{p^{-}})\,dx\leq 0.

This implies that
\underline{u}\leq\overline{u}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
, which completes the proof.
∎

Building on the preceding results, the uniqueness of solutions to (
P
) follows directly, as demonstrated below:

Proof of Corollary
2.11
.

Suppose that
u
and
v
are two weak solutions of problem (
P
) in
W^{s,G}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. By treating
u
as a sub-solution and
v
as a super-solution of (
P
), the weak comparison principle (Theorem
2.9
; see also Theorem
2.10
for the case where
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
) implies that
u\leq v
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. Reversing the roles of
u
and
v
, we similarly obtain
v\leq u
a.e. in
\Omega
. Hence, it follows that
u=v
a.e. in
\Omega
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
2.12
.

Without loss of generality, and due to the invariance of the problem under rotations and translations, we may assume that
\Omega
is symmetric with respect to the
x_{1}
-axis. Accordingly, the symmetry of the data implies that
f(x_{1},x^{\prime})=f(-x_{1},x^{\prime})
and
k(x_{1},x^{\prime})=k(-x_{1},x^{\prime})
for all
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
. Define the function
v(x_{1},x^{\prime}):=u(-x_{1},x^{\prime})
. It is straightforward to verify that
v
is a weak solution of problem (
P
). Therefore, by the uniqueness result established in Corollary
2.11
, we deduce that

u(x_{1},x^{\prime})=v(x_{1},x^{\prime})=u(-x_{1},x^{\prime})\quad\text{for all }(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\Omega,

which establishes the desired symmetry.
∎

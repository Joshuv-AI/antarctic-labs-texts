Unified Gentzen Approach to Connexive Logics over Wansing’s C
Norihiro Kamide

Abstract
Gentzen-style sequent calculi and Gentzen-style natural deduction systems are introduced for a family (C-family) of connexive logics over Wansing’s basic connexive logic C. The C-family is derived from C by incorporating the Peirce law, the law of excluded middle, and the generalized law of excluded middle. Theorems establishing equivalence between the proposed sequent calculi and natural deduction systems are demonstrated. Cut-elimination and normalization theorems are established for the proposed sequent calculi and natural deduction systems, respectively.

Introduction

Connexive logics are recognized as philosophically plausible paraconsistent logics
[
4
,
22
,
37
,
38
]
. A distinguishing feature of connexive logics is their validation of the so-called Boethius’ theses:

(\mbox{\it$\alpha$}\mbox{$\to$}\mbox{\it$\beta$})\mbox{$\to$}\mbox{$\sim$}(\mbox{\it$\alpha$}\mbox{$\to$}\mbox{$\sim$}\mbox{\it$\beta$})

On one hand, the roots of connexive logics can be traced back to Aristotle and Boethius. On the other hand, modern perspectives on connexive logics were established by Angell
[
4
]
and McCall
[
22
]
.

A basic constructive connexive logic referred to as C, considered a variant of Nelson’s paraconsistent logic N4
[

,
24
,

, was introduced by Wansing in
[
37
]
. Additionally, C was extended by Wansing in
[

]
to introduce a constructive connexive modal logic, serving as a constructive connexive analogue of the smallest normal modal logic K.
For further details on connexive logics, refer to, for example,

In this study, a unified Gentzen-style framework is employed to investigate several connexive logics over Wansing’s C.
The term “unified Gentzen-style framework” means that we can handle Gentzen-style sequent calculus and Gentzen-style natural deduction system uniformly, with an equivalence between them.
The logics under consideration include Omori and Wansing’s connexive logic C3
[
30
]
, material connexive logic MC
[

]
, and Cantwell’s connexive logic CN
[

\neg\mbox{\it$\alpha$}\mbox{$\lor$}\mbox{\it$\alpha$}

((\mbox{\it$\alpha$}\mbox{$\to$}\mbox{\it$\beta$})\mbox{$\to$}\mbox{\it$\alpha$})\mbox{$\to$}\mbox{\it$\alpha$}
, and CN is obtained from C3 by adding the Peirce law.

On one hand, Gentzen-style or G3-style sequent calculi for C, C3, CN and some intermediate logics between C and C3 have been introduced and investigated
[
37
,
30
,
7
,
25

, along with a Gentzen-style natural deduction system for the implicational fragment of C
[
14

.
On the other hand, a unified Gentzen-style framework for C, C3, MC, and CN has not been established. Therefore, we construct such a framework in this study. This framework enables an integrated proof-theoretical treatment of these logics and establishes a natural correspondence between sequent calculi and natural deduction systems for them.

We now discuss some related works on sequent calculi for connexive logics. The cut-elimination theorem for a Gentzen-style sequent calculus, referred to as sC, was proved by Wansing in
[
37
]
, although the name sC was not used by him. The cut-elimination theorems for G3-style sequent calculi, namely G3C and G3C3at for C and C3, respectively, were established by Omori and Wansing in

Consequently, the cut-elimination theorem for a G3-style sequent calculus, referred to as G3C3, which is obtained from G3C3at by replacing (at-ex-middle) with (ex-middle), was also demonstrated by them in
[
26

Several sequent calculi for some intermediate logics between C and C3 have recently been studied by Niki in
[
25

[

A natural deduction system, NC2, and a two-sorted typed

for the bi-connexive propositional logic 2C. Natural deduction systems for two variants of connexive logics concerning non-classical interpretations of a certain kind between negation and implication were studied by Francez in

In addition, some extensions of C were studied by Olkhovikov in
[

28

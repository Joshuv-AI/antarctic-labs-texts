Impact of Telephonic Reinforcement on Glycemic Control in Pediatric Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus: A Prospective Interventional Study at a Tertiary Healthcare Center
Authors: Reemu Bansal, Ambi Katyayani, Bala C. Bethamcherla, Lakshiya Ramamoorthy, Rajiv Ranjan, Lakshmi Murugesan, Nithila Sivakumar, Darshan K. Raj, Rishabh Jain
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM) is a prevalent endocrine disease in children, requiring consistent management of diet, physical activity, insulin delivery, and glucose monitoring. Telemedicine has shown benefits for adults with chronic diseases, but its effectiveness for pediatric T1DM in India is less explored. This study compares glycemic control in pediatric T1DM patients before and after telephonic reinforcement.Methodology:A prospective interventional study was conducted at a tertiary healthcare center in India. Thirty-five pediatric T1DM patients were included, with baseline characteristics recorded. Patients received telephonic support three times a month for nine months, focusing on blood sugar monitoring, insulin administration, and follow-ups. Data were analyzed using paired t-tests with a significance level of P < 0.05.Results:The mean HbA1c level decreased significantly from 11.85 ± 1.15 at baseline to 9.05 ± 1.55 at nine months (P < 0.001).Conclusion:Telephonic reinforcement improved glycemic control and compliance in pediatric T1DM patients. Further studies with larger sample sizes are needed to validate these findings.

INTRODUCTION

Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM) is a common endocrine disorder in children, particularly prevalent in India, where the incidence rate is three new cases per 100,000 children annually.[1] The autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells necessitates lifelong management, including diet planning, physical activity, insulin injections, and glucose monitoring. Consistent follow-up is crucial to prevent complications.[2]

Telemedicine has emerged as a valuable tool for managing chronic diseases, including diabetes.[3] However, its application in pediatric T1DM in India is under-researched. This study aims to evaluate the impact of telephonic reinforcement on glycemic control in pediatric T1DM patients, comparing HbA1c levels before and after intervention.

METHODOLOGY

Study design and setting

A prospective interventional study was conducted at a tertiary healthcare facility in India with Institutional Ethics Committee approval.

Selection criteria

Participants were pediatric T1DM patients aged up to 18 years, diagnosed by pediatricians or physicians. Exclusions included patients over 18 years old, those without informed consent, and those with untreated comorbidities. Of 75 identified patients, 35 were included after obtaining consent.

Data collection

Baseline data included height, weight, BMI, socioeconomic status, age, sex, duration of diabetes, insulin dose, compliance, and HbA1c levels. Patients received weekly telephonic reinforcement for the first three months and monthly follow-ups for the next nine months. HbA1c levels were measured every three months.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 23. Paired t-tests compared pre and postintervention data, with P < 0.05 considered significant.

DISCUSSION

The study demonstrated that telephonic reinforcement significantly improved glycemic control in pediatric T1DM patients, as evidenced by the reduction in HbA1c levels [Table 3]. Factors such as regular clinic visits and higher parental education positively influenced outcomes, whereas financial difficulties and low socioeconomic status were major limiting factors as depicted in the previous studies as well.[45]

CONCLUSION

Telephonic reinforcement significantly improved glycemic control in pediatric T1DM patients over nine months. Further studies with larger sample sizes are necessary to confirm these findings and explore the broader applicability of telephonic reinforcement in managing T1DM.

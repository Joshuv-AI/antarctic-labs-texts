Prenatal urinary BTEX metabolite levels and length of gestation: preliminary results from a metropolitan Detroit birth cohort
Authors: Homègnon A Ferréol Bah, Qiong Zhang, Yichu Wang, Emily Leydet, Mei Lu, Judy Westrick, Paul Stemmer, Christine Cole Johnson, Jennifer K Straughen, Andrea E Cassidy-Bushrow
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile the common volatile organic compound mixture benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene and xylene (BTEX) is associated with preterm birth (PTB), most evidence derives from estimated rather than direct measures of exposure. Metabolites of BTEX were measured in urine samples from pregnant participants (N=40) to conduct a preliminary association study of BTEX metabolites and length of gestation. Metabolites of ethylbenzene (phenylglyoxylic acid (PGA)) and xylene (methylhippuric acid (MHA)) were negatively correlated with gestational length; the Spearman correlation coefficient (ρ) for PGA and gestational length was ρ=−0.37 (p=0.018) and for MHA and gestational length was ρ=−0.35 (p=0.026). Preliminary findings suggest prenatal ethylbenzene and xylene exposure may be associated with shorter gestational length. Ongoing work in a larger sample is underway to examine associations between BTEX metabolites, measured longitudinally across pregnancy, and PTB.Keywords: Epidemiology, Child Health, Statistics, Infant

Introduction

Growing evidence suggests benzene, toluene, ethylbenzene and xylene (BTEX), a common mixture of volatile organic compounds, is associated with preterm birth (PTB).1 However, evidence is largely based on estimated BTEX exposure, usually assigned to maternal residential address and thus is prone to misclassification bias. Measurement of BTEX metabolites may provide a more precise biomarker of exposure. In this preliminary study, we measured BTEX metabolites in prenatal urine and explored associations with length of gestation in an ongoing birth cohort, the Research Enterprise to Advance Children’s Health (REACH).2

Methods

REACH began recruiting in February 2021; pregnant patients aged ≥18 years, able to read/speak English and seeking prenatal care and planning to deliver at Henry Ford Health (HFH) in metropolitan Detroit, Michigan, were eligible. All participants provided written informed consent. The study was approved by the HFH institutional review board.

REACH participants completed surveys prenatally and provided a urine sample targeting a third trimester research study visit; the electronic medical record was queried to obtain maternal race, age, pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI), hypertension in pregnancy, premature rupture of membranes, infant sex and gestational length at delivery. Urine specimens from a convenience sample of 40 REACH participants recruited through August 2022 were chosen for BTEX metabolite measurement. Patient involvement in REACH included focus groups conducted with REACH participants to obtain feedback on the design and conduct of study elements.

Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography with electrospray tandem mass spectrometry3 4 was used to measure phenylmercapturic acid (PMA, benzene), benzylmercapturic acid (BMA, toluene), phenylglyoxylic acid (PGA, ethylbenzene) and methylhippuric acid (MHA, xylene). A fortified pooled urine quality control (QC) sample was analysed after every 20 samples. QC performance was considered acceptable when the measured concentration was within ±20% of the expected concentration.5 BTEX metabolite levels were adjusted for urine specific gravity.6 Associations between each BTEX metabolite and gestational length were assessed using Spearman correlation coefficients (ρ). For this preliminary investigation, gestational length, as opposed to dichotomous PTB, was used as the outcome variable to retain power.

Results

Table 1 presents study population characteristics. Maternal urine samples were collected at a mean of 30.9±3.4 weeks gestation. Mean gestational length was 38.6±1.6 weeks; 6 (15%) were PTB. The following correlations were observed among the BTEX metabolites: PGA and MHA (ρ=0.48; p=0.002), PGA and BMA (ρ=0.44; p=0.004), MHA and PMA (ρ=0.44; p=0.004) and MHA and BMA (ρ=0.33; p=0.04). BTEX metabolites were not associated with maternal age, race, parity, pre-pregnancy BMI or infant sex (all p>0.05). Higher BMA was associated with hypertension in pregnancy (p=0.025).

Conclusion

BTEX metabolites are detectable at a greater detection frequency compared with the National Children’s Vanguard Study,4 in the urine of pregnant patients in metropolitan Detroit. Our preliminary findings suggest prenatal ethylbenzene and xylene exposure may be associated with shorter gestational length. Results are consistent with previous studies of outdoor BTEX exposure.1 However, the analysis is limited by the small sample size, which also limited the ability to control for confounding. Participants who delivered before the third trimester urine collection could be completed are not included; findings may be subject to selection bias. Ongoing work in a larger sample is underway to examine associations between BTEX metabolites, measured longitudinally across pregnancy, and PTB and to further evaluate potential effect modification by infant sex.

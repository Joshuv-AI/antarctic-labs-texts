Existence of minimal maps of degree one in W 1 p , p ​ ( 𝕊 1 , 𝕊 1 ) W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}) for p ∈ [ p ′ , 2 ] p\in[p^{\prime},2] , where p ′ ≈ 1.13924 p^{\prime}\approx 1.13924
Tomasz Kostrzewa, Katarzyna Mazowiecka

Abstract
. In this note, we show how the results of [ 6 ] , combined with those of [ 2 ] , imply the existence of minimal maps of degree one in W 1 p , p ​ ( 𝕊 1 , 𝕊 1 ) W^{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{S}^{1}) for p ∈ [ p ′ , 2 ] p\in[p^{\prime},2] , where p ′ ≈ 1.13924 p^{\prime}\approx 1.13924 . This provides an affirmative answer in this range to a question posed by Mironescu [ 8 ] and Brezis–Mironescu [ 4 ] . In order to do so, we complement the results of [ 6 ] by extending them to the case n = 1 n=1 and 1 < p < 2 1<p<2 , which had been excluded there for technical reasons.

1.
Introduction

In
[
8
]
, Mironescu asked whether, for
p>1
, minimizers of the energy

E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(u,\mathbb{S}^{1})\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

exist among maps
u\in W^{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{S}^{1})
of degree one; see also
[
4
, Open Problem 24]
. The answer is affirmative when
p=2
:

(1.1)

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{S}^{1})\\
\deg u=1\end{subarray}}E_{\frac{1}{2},2}(u)=4\pi^{2},

and the infimum is attained by Möbius transformations (see, e.g.,
[
8
, Section 2.3]
).

Later, in
[
9
]
, Mironescu proved that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that minimizers of
E_{\frac{1}{p},p}
exist for maps of degree one in
W^{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{S}^{1})
for all
p\in(2-\varepsilon,2]
. This result was further improved in
[
7
]
, where the authors showed that such minimizers exist for
p\in(2-\varepsilon,2+\varepsilon)
for a computable, small
\varepsilon>0
.

The question of existence is related to the sharp constant in the celebrated estimate by Bourgain–Brezis–Mironescu
[
2
, Theorem 0.6]
, which states that for any
u\in W^{\frac{n}{p},p}(\mathbb{S}^{n},\mathbb{S}^{n})
,

(1.2)

|\deg u|\leq C_{n,p}[u]_{W^{\frac{n}{p},p}}^{p}.

The optimal constant is known only in the case
n=1
,
p=2
, where
C_{1,2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}
; see
[
9
, Point 7, p. 1090]
.

In this short note, we show that combining the results of
[
6
]
with (
1.2
), along with estimates for the energy
[\mathrm{Id}]_{W^{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{S}^{1})}
, leads to an existence result for
p\in[p^{\prime},2]
, where
p^{\prime}\approx 1.13924
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
p\in[p^{\prime},2]
, where
p^{\prime}\approx 1.13924
. Then

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in W^{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{S}^{1})\\
\deg u=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

is attained.

Plan of the paper.

In Section
2
, we complement the results of
[
6
]
by addressing the case
n=1
and
p<2
, which was previously excluded due to a technical assumption concerning regularity. In Section
3
, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The paper concludes with an appendix, in which we compute the energy
E_{1/p,p}(\mathrm{Id})
of the identity map
\mathrm{Id}\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
, which yields the critical value
p^{\prime}
for which the existence result holds.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Assume the theorem is false and that there is no map of degree 1 in
W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
for which

\inf_{u\in W^{1/p,p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}),\deg u=1}\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right\}

is achieved.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
be the class of degree one maps from
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
to
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
.
Then by
Lemma

2.1
we obtain the existence of two other homotopy classes,
\Gamma_{d_{1}}
— class of maps of degree
d_{1}
, where
0\neq d_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\Gamma_{d_{2}}
— class of maps of degree
d_{2}
, where
0\neq d_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\Gamma_{1}\subset\Gamma_{d_{1}}+\Gamma_{d_{2}}

and for any
\delta>0
the following estimate holds

(3.1)

\#\Gamma_{d_{1}}+\#\Gamma_{d_{2}}\leq\#\Gamma_{1}+\delta.

By (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) we know that
d_{1},\,d_{2}\notin\{-1,0,1\}
. Thus,

(3.2)

\min\left(|d_{1}|+|d_{2}|\right)=5.

Recall from
[
9
, Point 7, p. 1090]
, that for any map
u\in W^{\frac{1}{2},2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
we have

(3.3)

4\pi^{2}|\deg u|\leq[u]^{2}_{W^{\frac{1}{2},2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})}.

Moreover, as in
[
9
, Proof of Theorem 2]
for any
1<p<2
for any
u\in W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})
we have the inequality

(3.4)

\begin{split}[u]^{2}_{W^{\frac{1}{2},2}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{2}}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2-p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq 2^{2-p}[u]^{p}_{W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})}.\end{split}

Thus, combining (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we get for any
u\in W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})

\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p}}|\deg u|\leq[u]^{p}_{W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})}.

Therefore,

(3.5)

\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p}}|d_{i}|\leq\inf_{u_{i}\in W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}),\deg u_{i}=d_{i}}[u_{i}]^{p}_{W^{\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{S}}^{1},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})}=\#\Gamma_{d_{i}}.

Thus, using estimates (
3.2
) and (
3.5
) we get

(3.6)

5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p}}\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p}}(|d_{1}|+|d_{2}|)\leq\#\Gamma_{d_{1}}+\#\Gamma_{d_{2}}.

On the other hand, the map
\mathrm{Id}\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
is of degree one. Thus,

(3.7)

\begin{split}\#\Gamma_{1}\leq E_{1/p,p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2-p}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Combining (
3.1
) with (
3.6
) we get for any
\delta>0

5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p}}\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p}}(|d_{1}|+|d_{2}|)\leq\#\Gamma_{d_{1}}+\#\Gamma_{d_{2}}\leq\#\Gamma_{1}+\delta\leq E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})+\delta.

We have moreover (see Lemma
A.1
) that for
1<p_{1}<p_{2}<2
there is

E_{\frac{1}{p_{1}},p_{1}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})>E_{\frac{1}{p_{2}},p_{2}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}).

Taking such
p_{1},p_{2}
we get

5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p_{1}}}<5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p_{2}}}\leq E_{\frac{1}{p_{2}},p_{2}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})+\delta<E_{\frac{1}{p_{1}},p_{1}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})+\delta.

We get therefore a contradiction for every
p_{1}
such that

5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p_{1}}}\geq E_{\frac{1}{p_{1}},p_{1}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}).

The smallest value
p^{\prime}
for which it holds is therefore such that

5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p^{\prime}}}=E_{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}},p^{\prime}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}).

This equality can be written equivalently (see lemma
A.1
) as

5\frac{4\pi^{2}}{2^{2-p^{\prime}}}=2^{p^{\prime}}\pi\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\sin\gamma\right)^{p^{\prime}-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma,

which gives

5\pi=\int_{0}^{\pi}\left(\sin\gamma\right)^{p^{\prime}-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

We can check numerically that
p^{\prime}\approx 1.13924
. Therefore, for
p\in[p^{\prime},2]
there must be a minimizer of degree 1.
∎

Appendix A
Border value of
p

Lemma A.1
.

For
1<p_{1}<p_{2}<2
there is

E_{\frac{1}{p_{1}},p_{1}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})>E_{\frac{1}{p_{2}},p_{2}}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1}).

Proof.

It suffices to show that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial p}E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})\end{split}

is negative on the interval
(1,2)
. Let
p\in(1,2)
. Using polar coordinates, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|x-y|^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left((\cos\alpha-\cos\beta)^{2}+(\sin\alpha-\sin\beta)^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|2\sin\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}\right)\right|^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\beta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\beta}^{2\pi-\beta}\left|2\sin\left(\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\right|^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}\beta,

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{-\beta/2}^{\pi-\beta/2}\left|2\sin\gamma\right|^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma\,\mathrm{d}\beta.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{-\beta/2}^{\pi-\beta/2}\left|2\sin\gamma\right|^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma=\int_{0}^{\pi}\left|2\sin\gamma\right|^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma=2^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\sin\gamma\right)^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

Thus,

\displaystyle E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})=2^{p+1}\pi\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\left(\sin\gamma\right)^{p-2}\,\mathrm{d}\gamma.

Putting
w=\sin^{2}\gamma
, we get

\displaystyle E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})=2^{p+1}\pi\int_{0}^{1}w^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w}{2\sqrt{w}\sqrt{1-w}}

\displaystyle=2^{p}\pi\cdot B\left(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right),

where
B
is the Euler beta function. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial p}E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})

\displaystyle=2^{p}\log(2)\pi\cdot B\left(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)+2^{p}\pi\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\left(B\left(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

It is well-known that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}\left(B\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)\right)=B\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)\left(\phi(z_{1})-\phi(z_{1}+z_{2})\right),

where
\phi(z)
is the digamma function. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial p}E_{\frac{1}{p},p}(\mathrm{Id},{\mathbb{S}}^{1})

\displaystyle=2^{p-1}\pi\cdot B\left(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)\cdot\left(2\log 2+\phi\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)\right).

As the Euler beta function is positive for real positive arguments, the derivative of the energy is negative if and only if

\displaystyle 2\log 2+\phi\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)<0.

Now, we use the series expansion of the
\phi
function, which can be found in
[
1
, p. 259]
. We get that for
z\notin\mathbb{N}
, there is

\displaystyle\phi\left(z\right)=-\gamma+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z-1}{(n+1)(n+z)},

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, and the series converges for all
z>0,z\notin\mathbb{N}
. As
\frac{p-1}{2},\frac{p}{2}\notin\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\phi\left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{p}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{p-1}{2}-1}{(n+1)\left(n+\frac{p-1}{2}\right)}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{p}{2}-1}{(n+1)\left(n+\frac{p}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\frac{(2n+p)(p-3)-(2n+p-1)(p-2)}{(2n+p-1)(2n+p)}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+p-1)(2n+p)}.

Hence, the derivative of the energy is negative if and only if

\displaystyle\log 2<\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+p-1)(2n+p)}.

Now, as every summand
\frac{1}{(2n+p-1)(2n+p)}
is a decreasing function of
p
for
p\in(1,2)
, so is the whole series. Therefore, it suffices to show that the weak version of the inequality holds for
p=2
. However, we obtain exactly

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2n+1)(2n+2)}=\log(2).

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

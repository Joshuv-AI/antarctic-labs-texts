On algebraic structures underlying the rational Kashiwara-Miwa-type models.
Sergey Sergeev

Abstract
The rational Kashiwara-Miwa model is an example of an Ising-type integrable model of the statistical physics, related to the six-vertex trigonometric R R -matrix. Two-spin edge weights of the model are expressed in the terms of q q -products, its spins are arbitrary integers, and | q | < 1 |q|<1 . We discuss in this paper the algebraic structures underlying the model, in particular its relation to the q q -oscillator algebra, to representations of the q q -oscillator algebra and to the co-product of the q q -oscillator algebra.

2.1
Definitions.

General definitions are collected here.

Definition 2.1
.

Let the generalised
q
-oscillator algebra is defined by the following relations:

\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]\;:\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\mathcal{E}^{\pm}\;=\;q^{\pm 1}\mathcal{E}^{\pm}\mathcal{K}\;,\quad\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\mathcal{E}^{\pm}\;=\;q^{\pm 1}\mathcal{E}^{\pm}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;,\quad[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]\;=\;0\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{+}\mathcal{E}^{-}\;=\;1-q^{-1}\mathcal{K}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;,\quad\mathcal{E}^{-}\mathcal{E}^{+}\;=\;1-q\mathcal{K}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

One could distinguish the generalised algebra (
2.1
) and the standard
q
-oscillator algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}\subset\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]
,

\mathcal{O}_{q}\;:\quad q\mathcal{E}^{+}\mathcal{E}^{-}-q^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{-}\mathcal{E}^{+}\;=\;q-q^{-1}\;.

(2.2)

It worth noting,

\mathcal{K}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;=\;q\,\biggl(1-\mathcal{E}^{+}\mathcal{E}^{-}\biggr)\;\in\;\mathcal{O}_{q}\;.

(2.3)

Statement 2.2
.

Equations

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{E}^{-})\;=\;\mathcal{E}^{-}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-}-\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;,&\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{K})\;=\;\mathcal{E}^{-}\otimes\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{K}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{+}\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\;=\;\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{-}+\mathcal{E}^{+}\otimes\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;,&\displaystyle\Delta(\mathcal{E}^{+})\;=\;\mathcal{E}^{+}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{+}-\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{K}\end{array}

(2.4)

define a homomorphism
\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]\;\to\;\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]\;\otimes\;\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]
.

Proof
is the direct verification.
\square

L
-matrices,

\Delta(L)\;=\;L\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\otimes}}\;L\;,\quad L\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-}&\displaystyle-\mathcal{K}\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{\prime}&\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{+}\end{array}\right)\;,

(2.5)

what guarantees the co-associativity.

\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]
, where

\pi_{\lambda_{1}}(L)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\otimes}}\,\pi_{\lambda_{2}}(L)\;\check{S}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\;=\;\check{S}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\;\pi_{\lambda_{2}}(L)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle.}}{{\otimes}}\,\pi_{\lambda_{1}}(L)\;.

(2.6)

2.2
Two homomorphisms

We will use two special homomorphisms in this paper.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{q}
be the universal enveloping of the simple Weyl algebra,

\mathcal{W}_{q}\;:\quad\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{v}\;=\;q\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{u}\;,\quad q\in\mathbb{C}\;,\quad|q|\;<\;1\;.

(2.7)

Then the simple homomorphism
\phi:\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]\to\mathcal{W}_{q}
is defined by

\phi(\mathcal{K})=\omega\,\boldsymbol{u},\;\;\phi(\mathcal{K}^{\prime})=\omega^{-1}\boldsymbol{u},\;\;\phi(\mathcal{E}^{+})=f(\boldsymbol{u})\,\boldsymbol{v},\;\;\phi(\mathcal{E}^{-})=\boldsymbol{v}^{-1}g(\boldsymbol{u}),

(2.8)

where

f(\boldsymbol{u})g(\boldsymbol{u})\;=\;1-q^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}^{2}\;,\quad\omega\;\in\;\mathbb{C}\;.

(2.9)

However, there is another remarkable homomorphism
\pi_{\omega,\mu}:\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]\to\mathcal{W}_{q}
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\omega^{-1}\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\mathcal{K})\;=\;\omega\,\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\;=\;(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}^{-1})\,(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})^{-1}\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\mathcal{E}^{+})\;=\;\mu^{-1}\;(\boldsymbol{v}^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})\,(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})^{-1}\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\mathcal{E}^{-})\;=\;\mu\;(\boldsymbol{v}\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}^{-1}\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})\,(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})^{-1}\;.\end{array}\right.

(2.10)

Here
\omega
and
\mu
are the "parameters of a representation" (parameter
\mu
for the homomorphism (
2.8
) is hidden inside
f(u)
and
g(u)
).
It will be convenient to use one more form of (
2.10
), namely
\pi^{\prime}_{\omega,\mu}
defined as

\pi^{\prime}_{\omega,\mu}(\star)\;=\;(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u})^{-1}\,\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\star)\,(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})\;.

(2.11)

Explicitly,
\pi^{\prime}_{\omega,\mu}
is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\omega^{-1}\pi_{\omega,\mu}^{\prime}(\mathcal{K})\;=\;\omega\,\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\;=\;(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})^{-1}\,(\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}^{-1})\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\pi_{\omega,\mu}^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}^{+})\;=\;\frac{q}{\mu}\;(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})^{-1}\,(\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{v}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1}\boldsymbol{v})\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\pi_{\omega,\mu}^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}^{-})\;=\;\frac{\mu}{q}\;(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1})^{-1}\,(\boldsymbol{u}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}^{-1}\boldsymbol{v}^{-1})\;.\end{array}\right.

(2.12)

2.3
Relation to the six-vertex
R
-matrix.

Before we proceed with the generalised
q
-oscillator, it worth to clarify how matrix
L
, eq. (
2.5
), is related to the six-vertex
R
-matrix.

Let us write the six-vertex
R
-matrix as follows:

R(\lambda,\mu)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}[q\mu/\lambda]&0&0&0\\
0&\displaystyle[\mu/\lambda]&[q]&0\\
0&\displaystyle[q]&[\mu/\lambda]&0\\
0&0&0&[q\mu/\lambda]\end{array}\right)\;.

(2.13)

If

L(\lambda)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-}&\displaystyle-\mathcal{K}/\lambda\\
\displaystyle\lambda\mathcal{K}^{\prime}&\mathcal{E}^{+}\end{array}\right)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\lambda\end{array}\right)\;L\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&\lambda\end{array}\right)^{-1}\;,

(2.14)

then there is the usual

\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}}\;R(\lambda,\mu)_{i_{1},i_{2}}^{\;\;j_{1},j_{2}}\,L(\lambda)_{j_{1}}^{\;\;k_{1}}\;L(\mu)_{j_{2}}^{\;\;k_{2}}\;=\;\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}}\;L(\mu)_{i_{2}}^{\;\;j_{2}}\;L(\lambda)_{i_{1}}^{\;\;j_{1}}\;R(\lambda,\mu)_{j_{1},j_{2}}^{\;\;k_{1},k_{2}}\;.

(2.15)

Due to the symmetry of the six-vertex
R
-matrix, the same
RLL
relation is valid for

L(\lambda)\sigma_{x}
,
\sigma_{x}L(\lambda)
and
\sigma_{x}L(\lambda)\sigma_{x}
, where
\sigma_{x}
is the first Pauli matrix. The matrix (
2.14
) is useless in the theory of the integrable systems since it depends on the spectral parameter
\lambda
in the trivial way. However,
L
-operator
L(\lambda)\sigma_{x}
is useful, it defines the integrable lattice Bose-gas..

3
Co-product and its
\mathbb{V}
-form.

For any representation of the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\prime}]
with invertible
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
there exists an universal basis, which we will call "the
\mathbb{V}
-form of the representation’’, in which the Klebsh-Gordan coefficients become trivial.

3.1
The origin of the
\mathbb{V}
-form.

According to its definition, the algebra
\mathcal{O}_{q}[\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K^{\prime}}]
with invertible
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
has a center,
\displaystyle\omega^{2}=\frac{\mathcal{K}}{\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}
. For the irreducible representations
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
. Then for a representation with fixed
\omega
let

\omega^{-1}\,\mathcal{K}\;=\;\omega\;\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\;=\;\mathcal{K}_{0}

(3.1)

Here we do not use a symbol of representation like
\pi_{\omega,\mu}(\mathcal{K})
since a representation is far from a definition.

Next, we consider the product of two representations, such that the first one has the parameter
\omega_{1}
, and the second one has the parameter
\omega_{2}
. Then, we can combine the co-product relations and rewrite the
\omega
-condition for the co-product as the null space of the following operator,

\omega^{-1}\,\Delta(\mathcal{K})-\omega\,\Delta(\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\;\equiv\;\mathcal{K}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{K}_{0}\,\biggl(\mathcal{H}(\frac{\omega}{\omega_{2}})\otimes 1\,-\,1\otimes\mathcal{H}(\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega})\biggr)\;,

(3.2)

where

\mathcal{H}(\mu)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{=}}\;\mathcal{K}_{0}^{-1}\,\biggl(\mu^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{-}-\mu\mathcal{E}^{+}\biggr)\;.

(3.3)

This observation gives rise of the

Definition 3.1
.

\mathbb{V}[\mu]
-form of any representation, or its
\mathbb{V}[\mu]
-basis, is the system of the states
|\omega;\mu,\xi\rangle
such that firstly

\biggl(\omega^{-1}\mathcal{K}-\omega\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\biggr)\;|\omega;\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;0\;,

(3.4)

secondly

\mathcal{H}(\mu)\;|\omega;\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\omega;\mu,\xi\rangle\;(\xi+\xi^{-1})\;,

(3.5)

and thirdly,
\xi\in\mathbb{S}
for some set
\mathbb{S}
such that the system of the states

\mathbb{V}\;=\;\textrm{Span}\biggl\{|\omega,\mu,\xi\rangle,\;\;\xi\in\mathbb{S}\biggr\}

(3.6)

is complete in the space of the initial representation.

3.2
General properties of
\mathbb{V}
-basis.

Statement 3.2
.

Let the states
|\omega;\mu,\xi\rangle
satisfy relation (
3.5
). Then they obey the additional set of relations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\biggl(\mu^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{-}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\xi\biggr)|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;\biggl(\mu\mathcal{E}^{+}+\mathcal{K}_{0}\xi^{-1}\biggr)|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\mu,q^{-1}\xi\rangle\,A(\xi)\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\biggl(\mu^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{-}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\xi^{-1}\biggr)|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;\biggl(\mu\mathcal{E}^{+}+\mathcal{K}_{0}\xi\biggr)|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\mu,q\xi\rangle\,A(q\xi)^{-1}\;,\end{array}\right.

(3.7)

where we omit the argument
\omega
in the state notations for the shortness, and the single undefined function
A(\xi)
is a subject of a normalisation of
|\omega;\mu,\xi\rangle
.

Proof:
The left equalities in the equations (
3.7
) are simple consequences of (
3.5
). To prove the right equalities, we define

\mathcal{G}^{-}(\mu,\xi)\;=\;\mu^{-1}\,\mathcal{E}^{-}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\,\xi\;,\quad\mathcal{G}^{+}(\mu,\xi)\;=\;\mu\,\mathcal{E}^{+}+\mathcal{K}_{0}\,\xi\;.

(3.8)

These operators have the straightforward properties,

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{+}(\mu,q^{-1}\xi)\;\mathcal{G}^{-}(\mu,\xi)\;=\;1+q^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{0}\xi\,\biggl(\mathcal{H}(\mu)-\xi-\xi^{-1}\biggr)\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{-}(\mu,q\xi)\;\mathcal{G}^{+}(\mu,\xi)\;=\;1+q\mathcal{K}_{0}\xi\,\biggr(\mathcal{H}(\mu)-\xi-\xi^{-1}\biggl)\;.\end{array}

(3.9)

It follows,

\mathcal{G}^{+}(\mu,q^{-1}\xi)\,\mathcal{G}^{-}(\mu,\xi)\,|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\mu,\xi\rangle\;,\quad\mathcal{G}^{-}(\mu,q\xi)\,\mathcal{G}^{+}(\mu,\xi)\,|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\mu,\xi\rangle\;,

(3.10)

what provides the right equalities in the formulas (
3.7
).
\square

The relations (
3.7
) have the elementary consequences:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}\,|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;\biggl(|\mu,q\xi\rangle A(q\xi)^{-1}-|\mu,q^{-1}\xi\rangle A(\xi)\biggr)/(\xi-\xi^{-1})\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{+}|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;\mu^{-1}\biggl(|\mu,q^{-1}\xi\rangle\xi A(\xi)-|\mu,q\xi\rangle\xi^{-1}A(q\xi)^{-1}\biggr)/(\xi-\xi^{-1})\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{-}|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;\mu\,\biggl(|\mu,q\xi\rangle\xi A(q\xi)^{-1}-|\mu,q^{-1}\xi\rangle\xi^{-1}A(\xi)\biggr)/(\xi-\xi^{-1})\;.\end{array}\right.

(3.11)

These correspond to the representation of the

\boldsymbol{u}|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\mu,\xi\rangle\,\xi\;,\quad\boldsymbol{v}|\mu,\xi\rangle\;=\;|\mu,q\xi\rangle\,A(q\xi)^{-1}\;.

(3.12)

Let us note, one has straightforwardly in the terms of the homomorphism (

4.2

Here we use the following approach. We consider a truncation of the countable vector space to a finite

(4.6)

(4.7)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle(\mathrm{I})\;:\;\;\psi_{N+1}\;=\;\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\psi_{N}\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle(\mathrm{II})\;:\;\;\psi_{N+1}\;=\;-\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\psi_{N}\;,\\
\\
\displaystyle(\mathrm{III})\;:\;\;\psi_{N+1}\;=\;0\;.\end{array}

(4.8)

The first two ways follow from the asymptotic
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\psi_{a+1}\sim\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\psi_{a-1}

Next, we can solve the finite-dimensional eigenvalue equation

6.4
Intertwining operators
V
,
\overline{V}
and the Star-Triangle relation.

Section
3
contains all required relations, e.g. the relations (
3.40
).
They just could be solved in the terms of Faddeev’s dilogarithm.

Faddeev’s dilogarithm is the uniquely defined solution of the difference equations

\frac{\varphi(x-{\mathtt{i}}{\textsf{b}}^{\pm 1}/2)}{\varphi(x+{\mathtt{i}}{\textsf{b}}^{\pm 1}/2)}\;=\;1+\textrm{{\large e}}^{2\pi x{\textsf{b}}^{\pm 1}}\;,

(6.17)

analytical in the strip
-\textrm{Re}({\textsf{b}})<\textrm{Im}(x)<\textrm{Re}({\textsf{b}})
. This solution can be presented as

\varphi(x)\;=\;\exp\biggl(\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathtt{i}}0}\frac{\textrm{{\large e}}^{-2{\mathtt{i}}xw}}{\sinh(w{\textsf{b}})\sinh(w/{\textsf{b}})}\,\frac{dw}{w}\biggr)\;=\;\frac{(\textrm{{\large e}}^{2\pi(z+\eta){\textsf{b}}};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(\textrm{{\large e}}^{2\pi(z-\eta)/{\textsf{b}}};\overline{q}^{2})_{\infty}}\;,

(6.18)

where

\eta\;=\;{\mathtt{i}}\;({\textsf{b}}+{\textsf{b}}^{-1})/2\;.

(6.19)

Thus, the difference equations (
3.40
) and their modular partners for the function

\langle\mu,x|\mu^{\prime},y\rangle\;=\;V_{\mu-\mu^{\prime}}(x,y)

(6.20)

have the following solution:

V_{\mu}(x,y)\;=\;\frac{1}{\Phi(\mu)}\,\textrm{{\large e}}^{2\pi{\mathtt{i}}(\mu-\eta)x}\,\frac{\varphi(\frac{x-y}{2}-\mu+\eta)}{\varphi(\frac{x-y}{2}+\mu-\eta)}\,\frac{\varphi(\frac{x+y}{2}-\mu+\eta)}{\varphi(\frac{x+y}{2}+\mu-\eta)}\;.

(6.21)

The same expression, together with the scalar multiplier
\Phi(\mu)
, comes from the integral

V_{\mu}(x,y)\;=\;N^{-1}\int d\sigma\,\textrm{{\large e}}^{2\pi{\mathtt{i}}\mu\sigma}\,\frac{\Psi(\sigma,x)\Psi(\sigma,y)}{(z;q^{2})_{\infty}(\overline{z};\overline{q}^{2})_{\infty}}\;.

(6.22)

The value of
\Phi(\mu)
has been obtained numerically,

\Phi(\mu)\;=\;2\gamma\phi_{0}^{2}\textrm{{\large e}}^{-2{\mathtt{i}}\pi\mu^{2}-2\pi{\textsf{b}}\mu}\;\varphi(\eta-2\mu)\;,

(6.23)

where

\phi_{0}^{2}\;=\;(q/\overline{q})^{{}^{1}\!/\!_{12}}\;=\;\textrm{{\large e}}^{-{\mathtt{i}}\pi\eta^{2}/6-{\mathtt{i}}\pi/12}\;,\;\;\gamma\;=\;\textrm{{\large e}}^{{\mathtt{i}}\pi/4}\;.

(6.24)

It must be noted, the modular symmetry seems to be broken in the exponent in (
6.23
). The reason for this is that the symmetry already has been broken on the level of the definition of
k\;=\;-{\mathtt{i}}\textrm{{\large e}}^{\pi{\textsf{b}}(\sigma+{\mathtt{i}}({\textsf{b}}-{\textsf{b}}^{\!-1})/2)}
and

\overline{k}\;=\;{\mathtt{i}}\textrm{{\large e}}^{\pi{\textsf{b}}^{\!-1}(\sigma+{\mathtt{i}}({\textsf{b}}-{\textsf{b}}^{\!-1})/2)}
.
The function (
6.21
) is normalised according to (
6.8
),

V_{{\mathtt{i}}0}(x,y)\;=\;\frac{1}{2S(x)}\,\biggl(\delta(x-y)+\delta(x+y)\biggr)\;.

(6.25)

Also, the function
V
is symmetric,

V_{\mu}(x,y)\;=\;V_{\mu}(\pm x,\pm y)\;=\;V_{\mu}(\pm y,\pm x)\;,

(6.26)

and it satisfies the summation formula,

\int dyV_{\mu}(x,y)\,S(y)\,V_{\mu^{\prime}}(y,z)\;=\;V_{\mu+\mu^{\prime}}(x,z)\;.

(6.27)

The weight
\overline{V}
for the Star-Triangle relation is now

\overline{V}_{\mu}(x,y)\;=\;V_{\eta-\mu}(x,y)\;.

(6.28)

We do not discuss in details the Star-Triangle relation and corresponding integrable system here since all relevant details (except the summation formula) can be found in
[
4
]
.

7
Summary and conclusion

This paper is an experience of the Hopf algebra approach to the generalised
q
-oscillator algebra. We have shown that the
R
-matrices for the rational (and hyperbolic) Kashiwara-Miwa models appear as the canonical intertwining operator for the co-product of
q
-oscillators. This is our main result.

Another remarkable observation made in this paper is the collection of the summation formulas (
4.46
,
5.12
,
6.27
) for the Boltzmann weights of the integrable models.

Only two representations are discussed in the paper. They are the Fock space representation and Faddeev’s modular double representation. Other representations and other modular doubles we leave for further publications.

Our final remark is that the homomorphism (
2.10
) appeared in the theory of the Tetrahedron equation
[
24
]
. Potentially, the Hopf algebra structure of the
q
-oscillators may have an impact to the three-dimensional integrable theories.

D.1

The representation
\widetilde{\mathbb{F}}

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{\omega,\lambda}(\mathcal{E}^{-})\,|a\rangle=|a+1\rangle\,\lambda\,\frac{\sqrt{1-q^{2a+2}}}{q^{a+1}},\;\;\widetilde{\phi}_{\omega,\lambda}(\mathcal{K})\,|a\rangle=|a\rangle\,\omega\,q^{-a-{{}^{1}\!\!/\!_{2}}},\\

-form of this representation, one will observe that
\mathbb{S}\;=\;\mathbb{T}
– the circle. In its turn, constructing the co-product, one obtains

\mathbb{V}

\Delta(\mathcal{E}^{\pm})\,|\!|\psi_{n}\rangle=|\!|\psi_{n}\rangle\left(-\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}\right)^{\mp 1}\,q^{\pm n}\;.

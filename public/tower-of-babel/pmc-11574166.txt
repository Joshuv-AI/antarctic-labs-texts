PSO-NMPC control strategy based path tracking control of mining LHD (scraper)
Authors: Ya Liu, Ping-an Peng, Li-guan Wang, Jia-xi Wu, Ming-yu Lei, Chao-wei Zhang, Ru Lei
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The automation of underground articulated vehicles is a critical step in advancing digital and smart mining. Current nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) controllers face challenges such as delays in turning on large curvature paths and correction lags during the control of underground the Load-Haul-Dump (LHD). To address these issues, this paper proposes a PSO-NMPC control strategy that integrates a particle swarm optimization algorithm (PSO) into the NMPC controller to enhance path tracking for LHDs. To verify the effectiveness of the proposed PSO-NMPC control strategy, the local path of the tunnels is selected as the simulation path, comparing it with the pure NMPC controller based on the path characteristics of the actual tunnel. The results demonstrate that the improved NMPC controller significantly enhances the trajectory tracking performance of the LHD, with maximum absolute lateral deviations for experimental paths 2, 3, and 5 improved by 89.7%, 72.2%, and 68.9%, respectively. Additionally, the improved NMPC controller exhibits superior performance in paths with large curvature compared to those with very small curvature and straight-line paths, effectively addressing the challenges of turn delay and backward lag in LHD operation, thus providing practical significance.

Introduction

Mineral resources are of particular importance to us, yet underground mining relies heavily on mining equipment. Currently, this kind of mining equipment is mainly controlled manually. In recent years, intelligent equipment has been developed, and control is one of the most important issues for the intelligent equipment. Besides, the conditions in underground metal mines can be so challenging that, even when mining companies provide their employees with comprehensive safety equipment, it can still be difficult to guarantee their safety1–3. Consequently, the use of intelligent equipment, such as LHD, to replace manual mining and transportation operations is of paramount importance, as it significantly enhances both safety and efficiency in underground mining operations4. With the development of unmanned mining, how to better control LHD to track the reference trajectory more accurately and ensure safe mining operations is a problem worth studying5. Load-Haul-Dump (LHD). The illustration shows a real scraper.

The mining LHD is a unique type of articulated vehicle, comprising a front and rear body connected by a central articulation point and swing ring. The vehicle body can be regarded as a rigid structure6,7. The unique configuration of articulated vehicles necessitates specific angular constraints during turning maneuvers8. In the current trend of continuous development of intelligent equipment, achieving unmanned mining with the use of the LHD is extremely important. Increasingly, research is focusing on the unmanned capabilities of the LHD9. The LHD gathers data to facilitate the pre-planning of a reference path and employs tracking control algorithms to compute the optimal cost based on the predetermined reference path, thereby enabling trajectory tracking, that is, path tracking control10.

Driverlessness is a prerequisite for unmanned mining, and path-tracking control is the key to driverlessness11. The task of path tracking is to drive a vehicle to follow a given path12. In order to control intelligent equipment in underground metal mines, since the underground tunnels are fixed, and thus the reference paths for the unmanned loader are known, the path tracking control method is extremely critical to achieve more accurate unmanned driving. So far, a lot of scholars have conducted more extensive research on path tracking control and proposed a large number of path tracking control algorithms, such as pure pursuit algorithm, Stanley tracking algorithm, proportional–integral–derivative (PID) control, sliding mode control algorithm, adaptive control, robust control, linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) control, and model predictive control (MPC) algorithm13–17. Among them, MPC is increasingly used in the current self-driving path tracking process18. And Paolo presents a model predictive control (MPC) method for controlling active front steering systems in self-driving cars19. Tan by using an improved Adaptive Model Predictive Control (AMPC) trajectory tracking method to improve vehicle tracking under uncertain and complex working conditions20. On account of its advantage of being able to handle multiple inputs and outputs, as well as having the ability to take into account system constraints21,22. With more scholars’ in-depth research on MPC, NMPC is gradually exerting greater advantages. Meanwhile, the use of NMPC is increasing due to the improvement of the controller’s computing power and the updating of the solution algorithm23,24.

Despite the special characteristics of articulated vehicles, there is no lack of scholars who continue to study their control utilizing new control methods MPC. A predictive control scheme for articulated vehicles at different slip angles was proposed by Nayl in his research25,26. For the steering control of articulated vehicles, Dou designed an MPC controller based on a dynamic error model27. Improved MPC controller with fuzzy adaptive weights designed by Wang28. Zavala proposed the advanced step NMPC to overcome the loss of stability29. Bai proposed NMPC path tracking controller for articulated vehicles30. M. Murillo proposed a novel nonlinear mathematical model for articulated vehicles to improve the performance of articulated systems for path tracking tasks31. Compared with switching model predictive control, NMPC can improve the accuracy of path tracking control30. Gao proposed a model-based steering control method for articulated vehicles32. Hao-Ran Li conducts research in the direction of reference paths and introduces an innovative path planning and tracking framework in conjunction with NMPC to improve the accuracy of vehicles through large curvature bends33. There is a growing body of research on NMPC and these researchers have many new directions for consideration34–36. Vougioukas uses a nonlinear MPC to control the steering angle and speed of the vehicle. The criterion is the difference between the desired path and the predicted path. Although the experiments were performed entirely in a simulator, good results were obtained, and the advantages of this approach were demonstrated37. Besides, the NMPC is better equipped to handle intricate nonlinear systems and multifaceted constraints, resulting in enhanced control performance, particularly in intricate underground mining environments.

Many improvements have been made to the MPC in the above papers, but none of them involves the study of the weighting of the controller’s objective function. In NMPC controllers, the objective function is extremely important for the whole path tracking control, so it is necessary to have a thorough study of it38. At the same time, the LHD, as a kind of articulated vehicle, has its own special characteristics, which must also be taken into account in the tracking control. For example, the current use of NMPC controllers to control the LHD is prone to problems such as turn delays and corrective lags when simulating many different paths. Consideration of the use of NMPC controllers for control is particularly important for the calculation of the cost function, which involves the calculation of cost terms such as control inputs and state output terms. Meanwhile, in classical MPC, the problem of path-tracking effects induced by fixed weights in the cost function when the vehicle moves away from the desired path has been little studied28. To make the LHD have a better tracking effect on different paths, so it was decided to start from this direction and use particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm to adjust the weights of each weight of the cost function of NMPC and improve it to improve the tracking effect of the LHD’s path39.

This paper presents a PSO-NMPC control strategy that improves the objective function in the controller and introduces weight matrices to ensure that the LHD can achieve more uniform tracking across different sections of the reference trajectory. In this paper, PSO is used to optimize the parameter values in the weight matrix to address the shortcomings of the LHD in efficiently and accurately tracking the reference path and making untimely turns.

Controller design based on improved NMPC algorithm

This section discusses the design of the NMPC controller and explains the mathematical formulation of the articulated vehicle model used in this paper.

Nonlinear model predictive control

The most notable feature of NMPC is that the nonlinear model of the controlled vehicle can be directly discretized into a predictive model. NMPC can explicitly deal with constraints, in the sense of adding constraints on future inputs, outputs, or state variables, through its model-based predictions of the future dynamic behavior of the system, which can be explicitly represented in an on-line solved nonlinear planning problem. The NMPC control closed-loop flow is shown in Fig. 2.Fig. 2NMPC Path Tracing Process Diagram. This figure depicts the overall process of trajectory tracking.

NMPC Path Tracing Process Diagram. This figure depicts the overall process of trajectory tracking.

The NMPC controller integrates vehicle sensor data to obtain real-time status and position. The vehicle tracks the reference path using the incoming trajectory information. The controller predicts the future state, calculates the error relative to the reference path, and performs optimal control under constraints to generate control output instructions. The first set of control outputs is applied to the vehicle, followed by the acquisition of the new state and execution of subsequent controls.

Predictive model

An accurate and computationally efficient system model is extremely important for NMPC controllers. Therefore, the balance between prediction accuracy and computational efficiency must be emphasized when constructing the kinematic and dynamic models. When used as a predictive model, the kinematic model can achieve faster speeds to calculate predicted states. However, if the speed of the LHD vehicle is too high, the prediction may not be very accurate. And the dynamic model used as a predictive model can provide more accurate predictions. However, this increases the computation time of the controller and requires other input parameters such as the acceleration and slip angle of the loader. Therefore, all these factors must be considered when selecting the predictive model.

In underground metal mines, for unmanned mining operations, due to the width of the tunnels, road conditions, etc., as well as according to the Underground LHD Operator’s Manual instructions, vehicles generally operate at low speeds, not exceeding 5 m/s. In this paper, to ensure the stability of the LHD test, the speed is set to a fixed speed of 1.5 m/s. Bai’s PhD thesis highlights the importance of scrapers in specific industries, such as underground mining, and discusses the applicability of kinematic models in these scenarios40. Running at this speed, the kinematic model of the LHD can predict the future state relatively accurately while ensuring real-time calculation. Thus, the nonlinear kinematic model of the loader is used as the prediction model.

The impact of nonlinear terms on NP problems is primarily seen in the increased complexity of these problems, making it challenging to find a globally optimal solution. Unlike convex optimization problems, nonconvex problems may have multiple local optima, which complicates the design of solution algorithms and necessitates the development of advanced methods to handle non-trivial cases with multiple local minima. Additionally, nonlinear terms raise computational costs and slow down convergence, making it more time-intensive to achieve globally optimal solutions, especially in high-dimensional spaces. Nonlinearities also affect the guarantee of a globally optimal solution, making it harder to verify the solution’s accuracy in nonconvex scenarios.

In the proposed control strategy, we address the influence of nonlinear terms on convex optimization problems by using the Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) algorithm, which accommodates any level of nonlinearity, including constraints. The SQP algorithm is configured in the optimizer to enhance the efficiency and robustness of solving the optimization problem, ensuring that the global optimal solution is obtained for convex problems.

By incorporating terminal constraints into the MPC formulation, we ensure that the optimization problem accounts for both short-term behavior over the prediction horizon and the long-term stability of the system. This approach aligns with findings in the MPC stability literature, which highlight the critical role of terminal constraints in achieving asymptotic stability43. These constraints provide a more comprehensive guarantee of stability by ensuring that the system remains stable not only over the prediction horizon but also as it approaches the terminal set.

Existing issues

There are many challenges in the operation of the NMPC controller, including the turning delay and correction lag in tracking the reference path of LHD using NMPC controller. Regarding the MPC control aspect, it has been noted in the literature that starting with the weight matrix of the objective function may be a good research point28. In addition, Cao investigated the effect of MPC control parameters on traffic circle intersections, which involved Q and R44. As is known that the controller efficiency is affected by the parameters in the weight matrix, and there is a lack of exploration on the optimization of the objective function weights in the current research on the LHD path tracking. The design of the controller is a challenging task where the parametric design problem can be done efficiently and accurately by optimization algorithms. We are inspired by the related research of El Hajjami et al. who utilized PSO and others to optimize parameters such as external perturbations in their study of path tracking control of ground autonomous vehicles 45,49. And they demonstrated the superiority of their proposed fusion method by comparing it with the method using PSO46. Although the object of these studies is ground autonomous vehicles, the proposed methods are still of some significance for this paper. Therefore, in this paper, PSO is used to optimize the parameters related to the objective function of NMPC. In this paper, we propose to improve the LHD path tracking effect through the optimization of the objective function weight matrices Q (weight matrix related to the system state deviation) and R (weight matrix related to the system state deviation). PSO is therefore used to achieve the optimal values of these parameters to make the LHD more effective in achieving the objective of underground tunnel path tracking.

Particle swarm optimization

PSO is a heuristic optimization algorithm proposed by James Kennedy and Russell Eberhart in 199547. It is a metaheuristic algorithm known for its superior global search performance48.

The PSO algorithm simulates social behaviors such as bird flocks and fish schools, and searches for the optimal solution through collaboration and information sharing among individuals. It demonstrates promising results in solving nonlinear continuous problems. Additionally, it exhibits a faster rate of convergence, quicker computation, and more accurate solutions compared to other optimization algorithms39,50. In PSO, each solution of the optimization problem corresponds to a ‘particle’, which moves in the search space and updates its position and velocity by tracking the individual extrema (the optimal solution found by each particle itself) and the group extrema (the optimal solution found by the whole swarm of particles). The velocity and position of a particle are its two main attributes: the velocity determines the direction and distance the particle moves, while the position represents the potential solution to the problem. PSO-NMPC control strategy

To address the shortcomings of the NMPC controller in the existing LHD control process, we optimized the weight matrix in the NMPC controller’s objective function to enhance its effectiveness. The weight matrix related to the system state deviation is indicative of the degree of punishment for any deviation of the system state from the desired trajectory. The elements on its diagonal are used to indicate the importance variables for different state deviations, which can be used to control different system states by adjusting the size of the parameters within the matrix. The weight matrices associated with control input deviations are also set as diagonal matrices, reflecting penalties for the size of the control inputs. This serves to limit the control forces applied, thus avoiding the potential for system instability or exceeding the capabilities of the actuators.

PSO offers a number of significant advantages. Accordingly, this paper applies it to the optimization of parameters within the weight matrix of the objective function. In the control process, each particle searches for the local optimum and passes it to the NMPC controller for trajectory tracking. Thereafter, it iteratively searches for the optimum by comparing with the effect of the set initial weight matrix. This process is repeated until the optimal parameters in the weight matrix corresponding to the particle with the best fitness value are found and then passed to the NMPC controller for optimal control.

The design of the objective function in nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) represents the fundamental aspect of the control strategy, and thus defines the optimization objective of the control process. This paper presents a novel approach to enhancing the objective function of NMPC. The proposed approach considers the optimization of both the system state deviation and the control input deviation, thereby ensuring that the operation of the system plays a more critical role in the tracking process of the reference trajectory. Analysis of simulation results

The PSO-NMPC control strategy was simulated and verified using MATLAB2020a and solved using the fmincon function in MATLAB. To accurately reflect the characteristics of the underground metal mine tunnel, it is essential to select a representative section for simulation experiments. This paper refers to several real underground tunnel paths. In the NMPC, the controller is responsible for selecting parameters for sampling time, prediction time domain, and control time domain. These parameters are chosen based on the information available. If the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T}_{s}$$\end{document} is too large, the controller may not be able to react quickly enough to a disturbance. If the sampling time is insufficient, the controller will respond more rapidly to disturbances and setpoint changes. However, this can result in an excessive computational load. It is typical for the control and prediction time domains to exhibit certain constraints. The selection of the control time domain is closely related to the prediction time domain. Adjustment of the control time domain can optimize control performance in accordance with changes to the prediction time domain. It is typical to set the control time domain at a value of less than or equal to the prediction time domain56,57. And it is recommended that the control time domain be set at one-tenth to one-fifth of the prediction time domain58. Following a series of experimental trials, it was determined that the optimal setting for the prediction time domain was 50. Therefore, the time step of the NMPC controller was set to 0.05 s, the prediction time domain to 50 and the control time domain to 5.

In accordance with the authentic lane path characteristics, a multitude of local paths have been selected, and five distinct simulation path conditions have been established in this paper. These simulation paths can be approximated by Eqs. (34)–(38), with some smoothing at the path turns. The first linear simulation path is set to evaluate the stability of the vehicle when traversing a straight lane. The second simulation path is of the linear-semicircular type. The third simulation path is of the straight-line-curve type, where in the curvature of the curves is larger. This enables an effective testing of the control effect of the improved NMPC controller. The fourth simulation path is of the continuous curve type, which is employed to assess the efficacy of the controller in this particular road condition. To enhance the diversity of experimental outcomes, this study also establishes a U-track path for evaluating the tracking efficacy of continuous minor curves. The local path of the actual tunnel is intercepted as the simulation path for simulation experiments, which is also proximate to the real tunnel environment. This enables the effective verification of the path tracking effect under a variety of working conditions, thereby establishing a foundation for subsequent field tests. Conclusion

In this paper, the PSO—NMPC control strategy is proposed to improve the NMPC controller, in order to solve the LHD in the NMPC controller for a variety of path trajectory tracking process of large curvature turn delay, correction lag and other issues, the simulation path is set based on the local characteristics of the actual underground metal mine tunnel, which also lays the foundation for the future development of real field experiments, and finally through the theoretical analysis and experimental simulation results, the following conclusions are drawn:This paper introduces a method of path tracking control of the LHD vehicle using a nonlinear model predictive control algorithm. It presents the establishment of a kinematic model of the LHD and a stability analysis.A control strategy combining PSO and NMPC is proposed, namely the PSO-NMPC control strategy. The effectiveness of the control strategy in improving the path tracking performance of LHD is demonstrated by introducing the local features of a real tunnel. The strategy is particularly effective in enhancing the reference path tracking performance in large curvature. The tracking data for paths 2, 3, and 5 demonstrate a notable improvement in the maximum absolute lateral deviation, with values of 0.2809 m, 0.2644 m, and 0.3578 m, respectively. In comparison to the tracking effect under pure NMPC control, the lateral deviation of the LHD tracking effect is improved by 89.7%, 72.2% and 68.9% respectively.A series of simulation tests on various paths are conducted using MATLAB. The results demonstrate that the improved NMPC controller is capable of precise tracking of the reference path. The RMSE error data calculated for the reference path tracking results are also presented in Table 8. Based on the RMSE error, it can be seen that the root mean square of the tracking results of the improved NMPC control under the set paths are smaller than those before the improvement, with reductions of 17.5%, 90.5%, 70.7%, 47.3%, and 72.1%, respectively. This suggests that the enhanced model exhibits enhanced predictive accuracy and stability.

The entire PSO-NMPC control strategy involves numerous optimization iterations, leading to high computational time complexity. Since the initial path-tracking results of the LHD, controlled by NMPC under the unit weight matrix, are input into the PSO for weight matrix parameter optimization, each subsequent particle and iteration should be based on new path-tracking data obtained from the NMPC control of the LHD. This iterative optimization process continues until the optimal weight matrix that satisfies the constraints is found, ultimately achieving the best path-tracking control results. On the one hand, due to the increasing number of iterations, the operation complexity rises; on the other hand, because the simulation environment uses MATLAB, the actual algorithm running time has a certain increase.

However, in the low-speed operation scenario of real mine intelligent vehicles, the strategy can still work relatively well, the reason is as follows:

Underground tunnels and environments are unique, often accompanied by challenges such as poor lighting and high dust levels, which limit vehicle speeds to below 15 km/h, sometimes even as low as a few kilometers per hour.

In these conditions, the control system’s response time does not need to be as fast as it would be in surface operations because underground vehicle speeds are inherently slower, and the environment demands less urgency for rapid responses. This allows the control system more time to process data and make decisions without sacrificing operational efficiency due to longer response times.

Thus, the PSO-NMPC control strategy proposed in the paper still works well, but it is not suitable to be generalized to other high-speed operation scenarios of the vehicle.

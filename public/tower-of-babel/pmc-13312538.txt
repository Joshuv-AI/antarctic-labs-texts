Beyond the scale: Strengthening the role of physical activity in obesity care
Authors: Mariz Rochelle Canonigo Sedon
Published: 2026

I read with great interest the article by Jakicic and colleagues1 on “Physical activity and exercise within the context of obesity treatment: Enhancing health beyond weight loss”. The authors provide a comprehensive and well-articulated synthesis that meaningfully advances current perspectives in obesity management. By integrating a broad body of evidence, the article effectively highlights the multidimensional benefits of physical activity, extending beyond traditional weight-centric outcomes. This contribution is particularly valuable in reshaping how clinicians, researchers, and policymakers conceptualize success in obesity treatment.

A notable strength of the article lies in its clear conceptual framing of physical activity as an independent and essential therapeutic strategy. The authors convincingly demonstrate that improvements in cardiometabolic health, physical function, and psychological well-being can be achieved even in the absence of significant weight loss. This is consistent with existing evidence indicating that higher levels of cardiorespiratory fitness are strongly associated with reduced morbidity and mortality regardless of body mass index.2,3 By emphasizing outcomes such as mobility, muscular strength, and cognitive function, the article broadens the scope of clinically meaningful indicators and supports a more patient-centered approach to care. This perspective is particularly important in addressing the longstanding overreliance on weight as the primary measure of progress.

In addition, the article’s emphasis on body composition and tissue quality represents a significant advancement in understanding obesity-related health outcomes. Moving beyond conventional metrics such as body weight and body mass index, the authors highlight how physical activity influences adiposity, lean mass, and bone health. This aligns with growing evidence that improvements in fat distribution, muscle quality, and functional capacity are critical determinants of metabolic and cardiovascular health.4,5 Such a nuanced approach enhances the clinical relevance of physical activity as a cornerstone of obesity management.

The inclusion of psychological and cognitive outcomes further strengthens the article. By recognizing the role of physical activity in improving mood, cognitive function, and overall quality of life, the authors underscore the importance of adopting a holistic approach to obesity care. These dimensions are particularly relevant given the complex interplay between physical and mental health among individuals living with obesity, reinforcing the need for integrated and person-centered interventions.

While the article provides a strong and compelling foundation, it also invites reflection to further advance research and practice. First, there is a need to more fully operationalize the health-centered outcomes emphasized by the authors. Although the benefits of physical activity beyond weight loss are well established, their consistent application in clinical and research settings would benefit from the wider adoption of standardized and practical measures. Indicators such as cardiorespiratory fitness, functional capacity, and patient-reported quality of life are closely associated with long-term health outcomes and may complement traditional metrics in evaluating intervention effectiveness.2,3 Strengthening the integration of these measures may support a more comprehensive and meaningful assessment of health improvements.

Second, the evolving landscape of obesity treatment, particularly with the increasing use of anti-obesity pharmacotherapy, presents important opportunities for integrative approaches. While these therapies have demonstrated significant efficacy in weight reduction, considerations regarding the preservation of lean mass and functional health remain important. Emerging evidence suggests that structured physical activity, particularly resistance training, may play a supportive role in optimizing outcomes when combined with pharmacological interventions.6 Future studies exploring these combined strategies may contribute to the development of more holistic and sustainable models of care.

In conclusion, the article by Jakicic and colleagues1 represents a significant and timely contribution that reinforces the central role of physical activity in promoting health beyond weight loss. Building on this work, future research should focus on refining health-centered outcome measures and exploring integrative, multimodal approaches to obesity management. Such efforts will be instrumental in advancing a more inclusive and holistic definition of success, one that values not only changes in body weight but also improvements in function, well-being, and overall quality of life.

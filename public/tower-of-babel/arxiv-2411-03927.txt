On the steady motion of a Navier-Stokes flow across a sieve with prescribed pressure drop in a finite pipe
Gianmarco Sperone

Abstract
The steady motion of a viscous incompressible fluid through a sieve (that is, a wall perforated with a large number of small holes), in a pipe of finite length, is modeled through the Navier-Stokes equations under mixed boundary conditions involving the Bernoulli pressure and the tangential velocity on the inlet and outlet of the tube, while the pressure drop is prescribed along the pipe. Applying the classical energy method in homogenization theory, we study the asymptotic behavior of the solutions to this system, without any restriction on the magnitude of the data, as the diameters of the perforations vanish. Regardless of the initial scaling and distribution of the holes, we show that the sieve asymptotically becomes a wall, meaning that the effective equations are two, independent, stationary Navier-Stokes systems with a no-slip boundary condition on the wall. In the absence of external forces we prove, furthermore, that the fluid motion becomes quiescent in the homogenization limit. AMS Subject Classification: 76M50, 76D05, 35B27, 35M12. Keywords: incompressible fluids, mixed boundary conditions, homogenization, sieve.

\mathcal{L}_{\pm}\doteq\mathcal{L}\cap\partial\Omega_{\pm}
, we further introduce the following two spaces of vector fields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{-})\doteq\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{-})\ |\ v\times\nu=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \Gamma_{I}\,;\qquad v=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \mathcal{L}_{-}\cup\Sigma\,\right\}\,,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{+})\doteq\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{+})\ |\ v\times\nu=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \Gamma_{O}\,;\qquad v=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \mathcal{L}_{+}\cup\Sigma\,\right\}\,.

The main result of this section provides uniform bounds (with respect to
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
) for the solutions of problem (
1.10
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
be as in (
1.4
). For any
p^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
(u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of problem (
1.10
). Then, the uniform bound

\sup_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\left(\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\right)\leq C_{*}\,,

(3.2)

holds for some constant
C_{*}>0
that depends on
\Omega
,
p^{\pm}
,
f
and
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
, but is independent of
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
.

Proof.

In what follows,
C>0
will always denote a generic constant that depends on
\Omega
and
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
(independently of
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
), but that may change from line to line.

We start by noticing that, in view of (
1.8
), we have

\int_{\Gamma_{I}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\widehat{k}=\int_{\Gamma_{O}}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\widehat{k}=\int_{\Sigma}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\widehat{k}\,.

Then, by taking
\varphi=u_{\varepsilon}
as a test function in the weak formulation of Definition
2.1
we get

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f\cdot u_{\varepsilon}-(p^{+}-p^{-})\int_{\Sigma}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\widehat{k}\,.

(3.3)

Applying the Hölder and Poincaré inequalities (see Lemma
2.1
), together with (
2.3
), on the right-hand side of (
3.3
), gives us

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\sqrt{\pi}\,R\,|p^{+}-p^{-}|\,\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sqrt{r_{\varepsilon}}\,|p^{+}-p^{-}|\right)\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\,,

so that

\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|p^{+}-p^{-}|\right)\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,.

(3.4)

Now, given any vector field
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\pm})
, by trivial extension to the complement
\Omega_{\mp}
we have that
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
. Once taken as a test function in the weak formulation of (
1.10
) (see Remark
2.3
), this gives

\int_{\Omega_{-}}\nabla u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\mathcal{E}(u^{-}_{\varepsilon})u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi-p^{-}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\Phi^{-}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=\int_{\Omega_{-}}f^{-}\cdot\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{-})\,,

(3.5)

and also

\int_{\Omega_{+}}\nabla u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\mathcal{E}(u^{+}_{\varepsilon})u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi+p^{+}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{O}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)-\int_{\Omega_{+}}\Phi^{+}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=\int_{\Omega_{+}}f^{+}\cdot\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{+})\,.

(3.6)

On the other hand, the Divergence Theorem trivially implies that

\int_{\Omega_{-}}\nabla\cdot\varphi=-\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{-})\,;\qquad\int_{\Omega_{+}}\nabla\cdot\varphi=\int_{\Gamma_{O}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{+})\,.

(3.7)

Therefore, defining the functions
P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
by

P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\doteq\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}-\phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\doteq\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}-\dfrac{1}{|\Omega_{\pm}|}\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{in}\ \ \Omega_{\pm}\,,

(3.8)

the identities (
3.5
)-(
3.6
) become, owing to (
3.7
),

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{-}}\nabla u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\mathcal{E}(u^{-}_{\varepsilon})u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi-\int_{\Omega_{-}}P^{-}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{-}}f^{-}\cdot\varphi-\left(\phi^{-}_{\varepsilon}-p^{-}\right)\left(\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{-})\,,

and also

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{+}}\nabla u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\mathcal{E}(u^{+}_{\varepsilon})u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi-\int_{\Omega_{+}}P^{+}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)

(3.10)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{+}}f^{+}\cdot\varphi-\left(p^{+}-\phi^{+}_{\varepsilon}\right)\left(\int_{\Gamma_{O}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{+})\,.

In view of (
3.4
)-(
3.8
), it now suffices to show the uniform bound

\sup_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\left(\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+|\phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}|\right)\leq C\,.

(3.11)

Since
P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\in L_{0}^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
and both domains
\Omega_{\pm}
have Lipschitz-continuous boundaries, there exist vector fields
J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})
such that

\nabla\cdot J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}=P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\pm}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\leq C\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\,,

(3.12)

see again
[
4
]
. As
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\subset\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\pm})
, we can take
\varphi=J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}
as test functions in (
3.9
)-(
3.10
), thereby obtaining

\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}+\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\cdot J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}-\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}=\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}f^{\pm}\cdot J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\,.

We then apply the inequalities of Hölder, Poincaré and Sobolev (in
\Omega_{\pm}
), together with (
3.12
), to estimate

\displaystyle\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}+\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\cdot J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}-\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}f^{\pm}\cdot J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\|\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\|u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\pm})}\|J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{4}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\|\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+C\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\|\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla J^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\,,

and thus, after further imposing (
3.4
), we get

\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\leq C\left(1+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|p^{+}-p^{-}|^{2}\right)\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,.

(3.13)

Define the vector field
\varphi_{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
by

\varphi_{*}(x,y,z)\doteq\dfrac{2}{\pi R^{4}}\left(\dfrac{z}{h}\right)^{2}(R^{2}-x^{2}-y^{2})\widehat{k}\qquad\forall(x,y,z)\in\overline{\Omega}\,,

(3.14)

so that clearly
\varphi_{*}^{\pm}\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\pm})
with

\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi_{*}^{-}\cdot\widehat{k}=\int_{\Gamma_{O}}\varphi_{*}^{+}\cdot\widehat{k}=1\,.

Taking
\varphi=\varphi_{*}^{\pm}
as test functions in (
3.9
)-(
3.10
) we obtain, respectively,

\phi^{-}_{\varepsilon}=p^{-}+\int_{\Omega_{-}}f^{-}\cdot\varphi_{*}^{-}-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\nabla u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{*}^{-}-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\mathcal{E}(u^{-}_{\varepsilon})u^{-}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{*}^{-}+\int_{\Omega_{-}}P^{-}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi_{*}^{-})\,,

(3.15)

and also

\phi^{+}_{\varepsilon}=p^{+}-\int_{\Omega_{+}}f^{+}\cdot\varphi_{*}^{+}+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\nabla u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{*}^{+}+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\mathcal{E}(u^{+}_{\varepsilon})u^{+}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi_{*}^{+}-\int_{\Omega_{+}}P^{+}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi_{*}^{+})\,.

(3.16)

Then, applying Hölder’s inequality and (
2.2
) to both identities (
3.15
)-(
3.16
) gives us the bounds

|\phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\left(|p^{\pm}|+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|P^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\right)\,,

and again from (
3.4
)-(
3.13
) we conclude

|\phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}|\leq C\left(1+|p^{\pm}|^{2}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,.

(3.17)

Putting together (
3.13
)-(
3.17
) provides the uniform bound (
3.11
), thus finishing the proof.

\Box

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
and
(u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of problem (
1.10
). From Lemma
2.4
we deduce the existence of a vector field
Y_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
such that

\nabla\cdot Y_{\varepsilon}=\Phi_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\nabla Y_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{*}(1+C_{B}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\,,

(3.18)

where
C_{B}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})>0
is the Bogovskii constant of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
(as in Lemma
2.3
), and
C_{*}>0
is a constant depending on
\Omega
and
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
(independently of
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
). Then, arguing as in the proof of Theorem (
3.1
) (see the estimates prior to (
3.13
)) we can derive the bound

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{*}(1+C_{B}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))\left(1+\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,.

Nevertheless, in view of (
2.7
)-(
3.4
), this last inequality prevents us from reaching any conclusion concerning the growth of the
L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
-norm of the Bernoulli pressure
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
with respect to
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
. That is the reason why, in the proof of Theorem (
3.1
), we separated the analysis into each sub-domain
\Omega_{\pm}
.

Combined with Lemma
2.2
, the uniform bounds obtained in Theorem (
3.1
) allow us to state the following decay rate for the tranverse flux rate and trace over
\Sigma
of any weak solution of problem (
1.10
). More precisely:

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
be as in (
1.4
). For any
p^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of problem (
1.10
), whose tranverse flux rate along
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is defined as in (
1.8
). Then, the estimate

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}+|F_{\varepsilon}|\leq C_{*}\sqrt{r_{\varepsilon}}\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,,

(3.19)

holds for some constant
C_{*}>0
that depends on
\Omega
,
p^{\pm}
,
f
and
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
, but is independent of
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
.

Proof.

In what follows,
C>0
will always denote a generic constant that depends on
\Omega
and
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
(independently of
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
), but that may change from line to line.

It suffices to observe that, since
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
vanishes on
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, as a consequence of Hölder’s inequality, the trace inequality (
2.3
) and the uniform bound (
3.2
), we deduce

|F_{\varepsilon}|=\left|\int_{\Sigma}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\widehat{k}\right|\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Sigma)}\leq\sqrt{\pi}\,R\,\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}\leq C\sqrt{r_{\varepsilon}}\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,,

which concludes the proof.

\Box

4
Asymptotic behavior as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
: homogenized equations

By employing the uniform bounds obtained in Theorem (
3.1
), through a compactness argument (which can be seen as a simplified version of the renowned
energy method
of Tartar
[
42
, Appendix]
) we derive in this section the effective (or
homogenized
) equations satisfied by the solutions of (
1.10
) as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\Omega_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}
be the family of domains (
1.4
). Given any
p^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
(u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of problem (
1.10
). Then, up to the extraction of a subsequence, the sequences of restrictions
\{(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
converge strongly to weak solutions
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
of the following boundary-value problems, in
\Omega_{\pm}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u^{-}+(u^{-}\cdot\nabla)u^{-}-(\nabla u^{-})^{\top}u^{-}+\nabla\Phi^{-}=f^{-}\,,\ \quad\nabla\cdot u^{-}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega_{-}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{-}\times\nu=0\,,\quad\Phi^{-}=p^{-}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \Gamma_{I}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{-}=0\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \mathcal{L}_{-}\cup\Sigma\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u^{+}+(u^{+}\cdot\nabla)u^{+}-(\nabla u^{+})^{\top}u^{+}+\nabla\Phi^{+}=f^{+}\,,\ \quad\nabla\cdot u^{+}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega_{+}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{+}\times\nu=0\,,\quad\Phi^{+}=p^{+}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \Gamma_{O}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{+}=0\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \mathcal{L}_{+}\cup\Sigma\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.2)

Furthermore,
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\times H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})
and they satisfy in strong form the systems (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), respectively.

Proof.

In what follows,
C>0
will always denote a generic constant that depends on
\Omega
and
\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
(independently of
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
), but that may change from line to line.

Let us introduce the following (closed) spaces of solenoidal vector fields:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{-})\doteq\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{-})\ |\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega_{-}\,;\qquad v\times\nu=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \Gamma_{I}\,;\qquad v=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \mathcal{L}_{-}\,\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{+})\doteq\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{+})\ |\ \nabla\cdot v=0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega_{+}\,;\qquad v\times\nu=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \Gamma_{O}\,;\qquad v=0\ \ \mbox{on}\ \ \mathcal{L}_{+}\,\right\}\,.

The bounds in (
3.2
) entail that the sequences of restrictions
\{(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
are uniformly bounded, so there exist
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
such that the following convergences hold as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:

u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u^{\pm}\ \ \text{weakly in}\ \mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})\,;\quad u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\to u^{\pm}\ \ \text{strongly in}\ L^{4}(\Omega_{\pm})\,;\quad\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\Phi^{\pm}\ \ \text{weakly in}\ L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\,,

(4.3)

along subsequences that are not being relabeled, see
[
35
, Theorem 6.2]
. Moreover, in (
3.19
) we deduce that, along the subsequences (
4.3
),

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\|u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=0\,,

(4.4)

so that, in fact,
u^{\pm}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})\cap\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\pm})
. Now, given any test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\pm})
, the identities (
3.5
)-(
3.6
) are valid for every
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
, along the subsequences (
4.3
). With the help of the convergences in (
4.3
) we can easily prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\nabla u^{\pm}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,,\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\varphi=\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm})u^{\pm}\cdot\varphi\,,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=\int_{\Omega_{\pm}}\Phi^{\pm}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)\,,

thereby implying

\int_{\Omega_{-}}\nabla u^{-}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\mathcal{E}(u^{-})u^{-}\cdot\varphi-p^{-}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\Phi^{-}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=\int_{\Omega_{-}}f^{-}\cdot\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{-})\,,

(4.6)

and also

\int_{\Omega_{+}}\nabla u^{+}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\mathcal{E}(u^{+})u^{+}\cdot\varphi+p^{+}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{O}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)-\int_{\Omega_{+}}\Phi^{+}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=\int_{\Omega_{+}}f^{+}\cdot\varphi\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{+})\,.

(4.7)

Notice that, by reasoning as in (
1.8
), we immediately deduce that

\int_{\Sigma(s)}u^{-}\cdot\widehat{k}=0\qquad\forall s\in[-h,0]\,;\qquad\int_{\Sigma(s)}u^{+}\cdot\widehat{k}=0\qquad\forall s\in[0,h]\,.

(4.8)

Now, consider the domains

\Omega^{*}_{-}\doteq\left\{(x,y,z)\in\Omega\ \Big|\ -h<z<-\dfrac{h}{2}\,\right\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Omega^{*}_{+}\doteq\left\{(x,y,z)\in\Omega\ \Big|\ \dfrac{h}{2}<z<h\,\right\}\,.

Fix any
\varepsilon\in I_{*}
along the subsequences (
4.3
). The same extension argument of
[
46
, Theorem 3.2]
(which employs the usual interior regularity results for the steady-state Navier-Stokes equations, see
[
19
, Theorem IX.5.1]
or
[
26
, Section 2]
) allows us to deduce that
(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\in H^{2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})\times H^{1}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})
, and so

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}+\nabla\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}=f^{\pm}-\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\quad\text{almost everywhere in}\ \Omega^{*}_{\pm}\,;\\[6.0pt]
&\Phi_{\varepsilon}=p^{-}\ \ \mbox{ almost everywhere on}\ \ \Gamma_{I}\,;\quad\Phi_{\varepsilon}=p^{+}\ \ \mbox{ almost everywhere on}\ \ \Gamma_{O}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.9)

The Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\subset L^{6}(\Omega_{\pm})
also implies that
f^{\pm}-\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\in L^{3/2}(\Omega_{\pm})
. Moreover, from the inequalities of Hölder and Sobolev (applied in
\Omega_{\pm}
) we derive the estimate

\displaystyle\|f^{\pm}-\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\leq C\left(\|f\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\right)\,.

(4.10)

The pair
(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\in W^{2,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})\times W^{1,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})
is also a strong solution to the Stokes system (
4.9
)
1
in
\Omega^{*}_{\pm}
, with a right-hand side given by
f-\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})
. If we apply the same extension argument of the proof of
[
46
, Theorem 3.2]
, we can then invoke local regularity results for the Stokes equations (see
[
19
, Theorem IV.4.1]
or the
Cattabriga estimates
of
[
9
, Teorema, page 311]
) and (
4.10
) to obtain the bound

\displaystyle\|u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{2,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})}+\|\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{W^{1,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|f^{\pm}-\mathcal{E}(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\|f\|_{L^{3/2}(\Omega)}+\|\nabla u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}+\|\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}\right)\qquad\forall\varepsilon\in I_{*}\,.

Once inserted into the right-hand side of (
4.11
), the uniform bounds in (
3.2
) entail that the sequences
(\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\subset W^{1,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})
are uniformly bounded. Therefore, there exist
\widehat{\Phi}_{\pm}\in W^{1,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})
such that the following convergences hold as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:

\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\widehat{\Phi}_{\pm}\ \ \ \text{weakly in}\ W^{1,3/2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}\to\widehat{\Phi}_{\pm}\ \ \ \text{strongly in}\ L^{1}(\partial\Omega^{*}_{\pm})\,,

(4.12)

along sub-sequences that are not being relabeled, see again
[
35
, Theorem 6.2]
. We readily notice that, since also
\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}\rightharpoonup\Phi^{\pm}
weakly in
L^{2}(\Omega^{*}_{\pm})
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, by uniqueness of the weak limit there must hold
\widehat{\Phi}_{\pm}=\Phi^{\pm}
in
\Omega^{*}_{\pm}
, respectively. Moreover, in view of (
4.9
)
2
and the strong convergence in (
4.12
), we deduce that

\Phi^{-}=p^{-}\ \ \mbox{ almost everywhere on}\ \ \Gamma_{I}\,;\quad\Phi^{+}=p^{+}\ \ \mbox{ almost everywhere on}\ \ \Gamma_{O}\,.

(4.13)

The identities in (
4.6
)-(
4.7
)-(
4.13
) allow us to conclude that
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
are weak solutions of systems (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), respectively. The improved regularity
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\times H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})
follows afterwards by repeating, again, the extension argument of
[
46
, Theorem 3.2]
.

In order to show the strong convergence in
H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
, we start by defining the vector field
u_{*}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
as

u_{*}\doteq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{-}&\text{in}\ \ \Omega_{-}\\[4.0pt]
u^{+}&\text{in}\ \ \Omega_{+}\,,\end{array}\right.

(recall that
u^{-}=u^{+}=0
on
\Sigma
) so that
u_{*}
is divergence-free separately in
\Omega_{-}
and
\Omega_{+}
. Fix any
\varepsilon\in I_{*}

In the absence of external force, the result of Theorem
4.1
yields a rather surprising statement:

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
(\Omega_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}
be the family of domains (
1.4
). Given any pressure drop
p^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
, let
(u_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
be a weak solution of problem (
1.10
) (with
f=0
). Then, up to the extraction of a subsequence, the sequences of restrictions
\{(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\subset H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
converge strongly to the constant vectors
(0,p^{\pm})
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
4.1
we know that the sequences
\{(u^{\pm}_{\varepsilon},\Phi^{\pm}_{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon\in I_{*}}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
converge strongly to weak solutions
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in\mathcal{Y}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})\times L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})
of the following boundary-value problems, in
\Omega_{\pm}
, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u^{-}+(u^{-}\cdot\nabla)u^{-}-(\nabla u^{-})^{\top}u^{-}+\nabla\Phi^{-}=0\,,\ \quad\nabla\cdot u^{-}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega_{-}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{-}\times\nu=0\,,\quad\Phi^{-}=p^{-}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \Gamma_{I}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{-}=0\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \mathcal{L}_{-}\cup\Sigma\,,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.26)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u^{+}+(u^{+}\cdot\nabla)u^{+}-(\nabla u^{+})^{\top}u^{+}+\nabla\Phi^{+}=0\,,\ \quad\nabla\cdot u^{+}=0\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \ \Omega_{+}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{+}\times\nu=0\,,\quad\Phi^{+}=p^{+}\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \Gamma_{O}\,,\\[3.0pt]
&u^{+}=0\ \ \mbox{ on }\ \ \mathcal{L}_{+}\cup\Sigma\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.27)

Furthermore,
(u^{\pm},\Phi^{\pm})\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\times H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})
. The weak formulations of problems (
4.26
)-(
4.27
) read, respectively,

\int_{\Omega_{-}}\nabla u^{-}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{-}}\mathcal{E}(u^{-})u^{-}\cdot\varphi-p^{-}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{I}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)-\int_{\Omega_{-}}\Phi^{-}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{-})\,,

(4.28)

and also

\int_{\Omega_{+}}\nabla u^{+}\cdot\nabla\varphi+\int_{\Omega_{+}}\mathcal{E}(u^{+})u^{+}\cdot\varphi+p^{+}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{O}}\varphi\cdot\widehat{k}\right)-\int_{\Omega_{+}}\Phi^{+}(\nabla\cdot\varphi)=0\qquad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{+})\,.

(4.29)

Recalling, from the proof of Theorem
4.1
, that
u^{\pm}\in\mathcal{V}(\Omega_{\pm})
have zero flux across
\Omega_{\pm}
, it suffices to select
\varphi=u^{\pm}
as test functions in (
4.28
)-(
4.29
), respectively, to conclude the proof.

Remark 4.1
.

The result of Corollary
4.1
dictates that, even under the action of an arbitrarily large prescribed pressure drop, the fluid motion becomes quiescent in the homogenization limit.

Compactness of products of block Hankel and Toeplitz operators
Caixing Gu, Meng Li, Pan Ma

Abstract
Motivated by the Sarason problem on the products of Hankel and Toeplitz operators on analytic function spaces, we characterize the compactness of products of block Hankel and Toeplitz operators on the vector-valued Hardy space of the unit disk via harmonic extension of the symbols and Douglas algebras generated by the symbols. Additionally, we provide a complete answer to the question of when the product of a block Hankel operator and a block Toeplitz operator is a block Hankel operator.

Due to the non-commutative property of the product of
matrices, the characterization of the product of block Hankel operators and

Question 1.1

Assume
E=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\Phi,\Psi\in L_{B(E)}^{\infty}.
When is the
product of block Hankel operators and block Toeplitz operators
H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}
compact on the vector-valued Hardy space
H_{E}^{2}
of the unit
disk
\mathbb{D}?

In order to state the first main result, we need to recall the harmonic
extension. Let
g(w)\in L^{1}=L^{1}(\mathbb{T)}
, and
g(z)
denote its
harmonic extension at
z\in\mathbb{D}
, via Poisson integral

g(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}g(w)\frac{1-\left|z\right|^{2}}{\left|1-\overline{z}w\right|^{2}}d\sigma(w),

where
d\sigma(w)
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
. In
fact, we often identify a function
g(w)\in L^{2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{T)}
with its
harmonic extension
g(z).
In this sense,
H^{2}
is the subspace of harmonic
functions which happen to be analytic. If
g\in L_{E}^{1}
or
g
is a
matrix-valued function, then the harmonic extension of
g
is defined
entrywise. If we write
\Phi\in L_{B(E)}^{\infty}
as
\Phi=\Phi_{+}+\Phi_{-}

with
\Phi_{+}=P(\Phi)
in
H_{E}^{2}
and
\Phi_{-}=Q(\Phi)
in
\overline{zH_{E}^{2}}
, then
the answer to the above question can be expressed by the harmonic extensions
of the symbols
\Phi
and
\Psi
.

Theorem 1.2

Assume
E=\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\Phi,\Psi\in L_{B(E)}^{\infty}.
Then
H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}
is compact on the vector-valued
Hardy space
H_{E}^{2}
if and only if

\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}trace\left[\left|\Phi_{-}-\Phi_{-}(z)\right|^{2}(z)\cdot\left|\widetilde{\Psi_{-}}^{\ast}-\widetilde{\Psi_{-}}^{\ast}(\overline{z})\right|^{2}(\overline{z})\right]=0

and

\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}trace\left[\left|\left(\Phi_{-}\Psi_{+}\right)_{-}-\left(\Phi_{-}\Psi_{+}\right)_{-}(z)+\left(\left[\Phi_{-}-\Phi_{-}(z)\right]\Psi_{-}(z)\right)\right|^{2}(z)\right]=0.

In the scalar case, we get a new result that complements Theorem

1.7
below.

Corollary 1.3

Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}.
Then
H_{\varphi}T_{\psi}
is compact on the
Hardy space
H^{2}
if and only if

\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left|\varphi_{-}-\varphi_{-}(z)\right|^{2}(z)\left(\left|\psi_{-}-\psi_{-}(z)\right|^{2}(z)\right)=0

and

\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left[\left|\left(\phi_{-}\psi_{+}\right)_{-}-\left(\phi_{-}\psi_{+}\right)_{-}(z)+\left(\left[\phi_{-}-\phi_{-}(z)\right]\psi_{-}(z)\right)\right|^{2}(z)\right]=0.

Theorem 1.4

Assume

Then

\bigcap_{A\in(M_{n})_{2^{2n}}}H^{\infty}\!\bigl[\Phi(I-A),\,A\Psi,\,\Phi A\Psi\bigr]

denotes the intersection, over all

of the Douglas algebras generated by

together with
\Phi(I-A)
,
A\Psi
and
\Phi A\Psi

2
Product of block Hankel and Toeplitz operators

The study of the compactness of the product of a Hankel operator and a
Toeplitz operator naturally leads to the question of when their product is
zero or another Hankel operator. It turns out this question is considered by
the first author in
[
24
]
where a partial answer is given, see

[
16
]
. Later, Lee
[
33
]
proved under what conditions the finite sum
of products of Hankel and Toeplitz operators equals zero. C. Chu
[
12
]
gave the necessary and sufficient conditions for the compactness of the
finite sum of products of Hankel and Toeplitz operators. The tools we will
develop to study the compactness of the product of block Hankel and Toeplitz
operators render an answer to this question. We first collect a few facts
about block Hankel operators and block Toeplitz operators with brief proofs.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\Phi,\Psi\in L_{B(E)}^{\infty}.
Then
T_{\Phi}^{\ast}=T_{\Phi^{\ast}},

H_{\Phi}^{\ast}=H_{\widetilde{\Phi}^{\ast}},
and

\displaystyle T_{\Phi\Psi}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}+H_{\widetilde{\Phi}}H_{\Psi},

(3)

\displaystyle H_{\Phi\Psi}

\displaystyle=H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}+T_{\widetilde{\Phi}}H_{\Psi}.

(4)

Proof.
With respect to the decomposition
L_{E}^{2}=H_{E}^{2}\oplus\overline{zH_{E}^{2}},
the multiplication operator
M_{\Phi}
can be written as

M_{\Phi}=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}T_{\Phi}&H_{\widetilde{\Phi}}J\\
JH_{\Phi}&JT_{\widetilde{\Phi}}J\end{array}\right].

Then writing
M_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}=M_{\Phi}{}_{\Psi}
as a
2\times 2
matrix
operator equation will prove the lemma.

Corollary 2.2

Let
\Phi\in L_{B(E)}^{\infty}
and
\Psi\in H_{B(E)}^{\infty}.
Then

\displaystyle T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=T_{\Phi\Psi},

(5)

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=H_{\Phi\Psi}.

(6)

Similarly,
T_{\Psi}^{\ast}T_{\Phi}=T_{\Psi^{\ast}\Phi}
and
T_{\widetilde{\Psi}}H_{\Phi}=H_{\Psi\Phi}.

Proof.
By (
3
),
T_{\Phi\Psi}=T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}+H_{\widetilde{\Phi}}H_{\Psi}.

Note
H_{\Psi}=0
since
\Psi\in H_{B(E)}^{\infty}.
So
T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}=T_{\Phi\Psi}.

The formula (
5
) is related to Brown-Halmos Theorem
[
7
]
,
which states that the product of two scalar Toeplitz operators
T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}=T_{\Phi\Psi}
(
\dim E=1
) if and only if either
\Phi
is
co-analytic or
\Psi
is analytic. There are many generalizations of
Brown-Halmos Theorem on different function spaces, see

[
1
,
23
,
37
]
. A somewhat complicated result is obtained in the
case
\dim E>1
in
[
5
]
. Recently, a simple characterization of the
product of two block Toeplitz operators
T_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}
being a block
Toeplitz operator is given in
[
26
]
. A natural question from (
6
)
is when the product of two block Hankel operators
H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}
is a
block Hankel operator. Indeed partial results are obtained for this question
and we will give a simple answer in Theorem
2.15
.

In this paper, we mainly study the compactness of the product
H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi}.
However, some compactness results hold for a (finite) sum of
(finite) products of several block Hankel operators and block Toeplitz
operators. Next we introduce the notion of an odd product.

Definition 2.3

We call the product of several block Toeplitz operators and an odd number of
block Hankel operators an odd product of block Hankel operators and block
Toeplitz operators.

The proof of the next lemma shows that the product of several block Toeplitz
operators and an even number of block Hankel operators is a finite sum of
finite products of block Toeplitz operators.

Lemma 2.4

If
A,B\in B(H_{E}^{2})
are sums of odd products of block
Hankel operators and block Toeplitz operators, then
AB
is a sum of products
of block Toeplitz operators. In particular,
B^{\ast}B
is a sum of products
of block Toeplitz operators.

Proof.
It is clear that we only need to prove that the product of two odd products of
block Hankel operators and block Toeplitz operators is a sum of products of
block Toeplitz operators. On a reflection, we need to prove the following
claim: the product

H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}H_{\Phi_{2}},

where
\Phi_{i},\Psi_{j}\in L_{B(E)}^{\infty},
is a sum of finite products of
Toeplitz operators. For
m=0,
by (
3
),

H_{\Phi_{1}}H_{\Phi_{2}}=T_{\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}\Phi_{2}}-T_{\widetilde{\Phi}_{1}}T_{\Phi_{2}},

so the claim is true. Now assume the claim holds for
m.
Then, by
(
4
),

\displaystyle H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}T_{\Psi_{m+1}}H_{\Phi_{2}}

\displaystyle=H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}(H_{\widetilde{\Psi}_{m+1}\Phi_{2}}-H_{\widetilde{\Psi}_{m+1}}T_{\Phi_{2}})

\displaystyle=H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}H_{\widetilde{\Psi}_{m+1}\Phi_{2}}-\left(H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}H_{\widetilde{\Psi}_{m+1}}\right)T_{\Phi_{2}}.

By the induction hypothesis,
H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}H_{\widetilde{\Psi}_{m+1}\Phi_{2}}
and
H_{\Phi_{1}}T_{\Psi_{1}}\cdots T_{\Psi_{m}}H_{\widetilde{\Psi}_{m+1}}
are sums of products of block Toeplitz
operators, so the claim holds for
m+1.

Essentially, a sum of odd products of block Hankel operators and block
Toeplitz operators, denoted by
B,
is related to a block Hankel operator in
the sense that
S_{E}^{\ast}B-BS_{E}
is a finite rank operator. A sum of
products of block Toeplitz operators, denoted by
A,
is related to a block
Toeplitz operator in the sense that
A-S_{E}^{\ast}AS_{E}
is a finite rank
operator. For the study of compactness, we actually will need more general
formulas which are extensions of these observations using an automorphism of

\mathbb{D}
.

For
z\in\mathbb{D},
let
\varphi_{z}
be the automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
,

\varphi_{z}(w)=\frac{w-z}{1-\overline{z}w},w\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that
\varphi_{0}(w)=w
and
\widetilde{\varphi}_{z}=\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}.
Let
k_{z}
be the normalized reproducing kernel of

H^{2},

k_{z}(w)=\frac{\sqrt{1-\left|z\right|^{2}}}{1-\overline{z}w}.

We now state a few facts about block Hankel operators and block Toeplitz
operators related to
\varphi_{z}(w).
By an abuse of notation, we will often
write
T_{\varphi_{z}I_{E}}
and
H_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}I_{E}}

(10)

The following lemma provides

Lemma 3.1

Assume
E=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If

is a compact operator, then

\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\Delta_{z}(X)\right\|=0\text{ and }\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\Omega_{z}(X)\right\|=0\text{.}

Proof.
The
\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\Delta_{z}(X)\right\|=0
in the scalar case is due to Lemma 2 in
[
55
]
,
and the extension to the vector-valued case is immediate, as we show how to
reduce the proof of
\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\Omega_{z}(X)\right\|=0
in the vector-valued case to the scalar-valued
case below.

Assume
E=\mathbb{C}^{n}.
If
X\in B(H_{E}^{2})
is a compact operator, then

X=\left[X_{i,j}\right]_{i,j=1}^{n}\text{,}

where each
X_{i,j}
is a compact operator on
H^{2}
. Thus
\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\Omega_{z}(X)\right\|=0
if and only
if
\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}
\left\|\Omega_{z}(X_{i,j})\right\|
=
\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|X_{i,j}T_{\varphi_{z}}-T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}X_{i,j}\right\|=0

for all
i,j
.

Since the set of finite rank operators is dense in the set of compact
operators, given
\varepsilon>0,
there exist

and

g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}
in
H^{2}
so that

\left\|X_{i,j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\otimes g_{i}\right\|<\varepsilon.

Thus, this lemma follows once we prove it for any operator of rank one.

Note that for almost all

, as
|z|\rightarrow 1,

\displaystyle z+\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}(w)

\displaystyle=z+\frac{\bar{w}-z}{1-\bar{z}\bar{w}}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\bar{w}}{1-\bar{z}\bar{w}}\rightarrow 0,

\displaystyle\overline{z}+\overline{{\varphi}_{z}}(w)

\displaystyle=\overline{z}+\frac{\bar{w}-\overline{z}}{1-z\bar{w}}=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})\bar{w}}{1-z\bar{w}}\rightarrow 0.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}.
By Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, as

|z|\rightarrow 1
,

\left\|zf+\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}f\right\|\rightarrow 0\text{ and }\left\|\overline{z}g+\overline{\varphi_{z}}g\right\|\rightarrow 0.

If
f,g\in H^{2}
, we apply the projection
P
to obtain, as
|z|\rightarrow 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|P\left(zf+\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}f\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|zf+T_{\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}}f\right\|\rightarrow 0,

(16)

\displaystyle\left\|P\left(\overline{z}g+\overline{\varphi_{z}}f\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\overline{z}g+T_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}}g\right\|\rightarrow 0.

(17)

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|zf\otimes T_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}}g-T_{\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}}f\otimes\overline{z}g\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(zf+T_{\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}}f\right)\otimes T_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}}g-T_{\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}}f\otimes\left(T_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}}g+\overline{z}g\right))\right\|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\Omega_{z}(f\otimes g)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|(f\otimes g)T_{\varphi_{z}}-T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}(f\otimes g)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|zf\otimes T_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}}g-T_{\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}}f\otimes\overline{z}g\right\|+(1-\left|z\right|)\left\|\Omega_{z}(f\otimes g)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|zf+T_{\overline{{\varphi}_{\overline{z}}}}f\right\|\left\|g\right\|+\left\|\overline{z}g+T_{\overline{\varphi_{z}}}g\right\|\left\|f\right\|+2(1-\left|z\right|)\left\|g\right\|\left\|f\right\|.

It follows from (
16
) and (
17

\lim_{\left|z\right|\rightarrow 1}\left\|\Omega_{z}(f\otimes g)\right\|=0.

Lemma 3.4

Assume

\Delta_{z}(XY)=XC_{\overline{z},E}Y-XT_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}\Omega_{z}(Y)+\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)\Omega_{z}(Y)+\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}Y.

\Delta_{z}(XX^{\ast})=XC_{\overline{z},E}X^{\ast}+\left[\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}X^{\ast}\right]^{\ast}-\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)^{\ast}+\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}X^{\ast}.

By definition,

\displaystyle YT_{\varphi_{z}I_{E}}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{z}(Y)+T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}I_{E}}^{\ast}Y,

\displaystyle T_{\varphi_{z}I_{E}}^{\ast}X

\displaystyle=XT_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}I_{E}}-\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X).

Hence

\displaystyle\Delta_{z}(XY)

\displaystyle=XY-T_{\varphi_{z}}^{\ast}XYT_{\varphi_{z}}

\displaystyle=XY-\left[XT_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}-\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)\right]\left[\Omega_{z}(Y)+T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}Y\right]

\displaystyle=XY-XT_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}Y-XT_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}\Omega_{z}(Y)+\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)\Omega_{z}(Y)+\Omega_{\overline{z}}(X)T_{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}^{\ast}Y

\Omega_{z}(H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi})=0,
heuristically, to show

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[c]{ccc}H_{\Phi}\left(k_{z}e_{1}\right)&\cdots&H_{\Phi}\left(k_{z}e_{n}\right)\end{array}\right](I-A)

\displaystyle\text{ and }\left[\begin{array}[c]{ccc}H_{\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{1}\right)&\cdots&H_{\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{n}\right)\end{array}\right]\text{ }A^{\ast}

go to zero for some

Note that

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[c]{ccc}H_{\Phi}\left(k_{z}e_{1}\right)&\cdots&H_{\Phi}\left(k_{z}e_{n}\right)\end{array}\right](I-A)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{ccc}H_{\Phi(I-A)}\left(k_{z}e_{1}\right)&\cdots&H_{\Phi(I-A)}\left(k_{z}e_{n}\right)\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[c]{ccc}H_{\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{1}\right)&\cdots&H_{\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{n}\right)\end{array}\right]\text{ }A^{\ast}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{ccc}H_{A\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{1}\right)&\cdots&H_{A\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{n}\right)\end{array}\right].

For notational simplicity, we also use the more compact form of
\Omega_{z}(H_{\Phi}T_{\Psi})=H_{\Phi}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{\Psi}.
These considerations lead to the definition of

\Gamma_{1}(z):=\inf\left\{\left\|H_{\Phi(I-A)}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}\right\|+\left\|H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{A\Psi}\right\|:A\in\left(M_{n}\right)_{2^{2n}}\right\}.

The reason we need
\left(M_{n}\right)_{2^{2n}}
instead of
\left(M_{n}\right)_{1}
as in Lemma
2.14
is due to Lemma
4.3
below.

We also need an equivalent definition of

Note that

\displaystyle H_{\Phi(I-A)}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{\Phi(I-A)}\left(k_{z}e_{i}\right)\otimes\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{i}\right),

\displaystyle H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{A\Psi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(k_{z}e_{i}\right)\otimes H_{A\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{i}\right).

Since
\left\{k_{\overline{z}}e_{i},1\leq i\leq n\right\}
is an
orthonormal set, if we define

\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}(z)=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|H_{\Phi(I-A)}\left(k_{z}e_{i}\right)\right\|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|H_{A\Psi}^{\ast}\left(k_{\overline{z}}e_{i}\right)\right\|:A\in\left(M_{n}\right)_{2^{2n}}\right\},

then there exist positive constants

and

that only depend on

\displaystyle\left\|H_{\Phi}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{\Psi}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|H_{\Phi(I-A)+\Phi A}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{\Psi}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|H_{\Phi(I-A)}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{\Psi}+H_{\Phi}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{A\Psi}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|H_{\Phi(I-A)}H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}\right\|\left\|H_{\Psi}\right\|+\left\|H_{\Phi}\right\|\left\|H_{\overline{\varphi_{\overline{z}}}I_{E}}H_{A\Psi}\right\|.

Since

is arbitrary, we have

Corollary 5.1
(
[
11
]
)

Let
\varphi,\psi\in L^{\infty}.
Then
H_{\varphi}T_{\psi}
is compact if and only if

H^{\infty}\left[\varphi\right]\cap H^{\infty}\left[\psi,\varphi\psi\right]\subset H^{\infty}+C.

Proof.
By Theorem
1.4
,
H_{\varphi}T_{\psi}
is compact if and only

\bigcap_{A\in\left(M_{1}\right)_{2^{2}}}H^{\infty}\left[\varphi(I-A),A\psi,\varphi A\psi\right]\subset H^{\infty}+C.

In this case, the matrix
A
is a scalar. In the case
A=0,
we get

H^{\infty}\left[\varphi(I-A),A\psi,\varphi A\psi\right]=H^{\infty}\left[\varphi\right]
.

In the case
A=1,
we get
H^{\infty}\left[\varphi(I-A),A\psi,\varphi A\psi\right]=H^{\infty}\left[\psi,\varphi\psi\right].
In all other
cases, we get
H^{\infty}\left[\varphi,\psi,\varphi\psi\right]
. Thus we
will have

H^{\infty}\left[\varphi,\psi,\varphi\psi\right]\cap H^{\infty}\left[\varphi\right]\cap H^{\infty}\left[\psi,\varphi\psi\right]\subset H^{\infty}+C

if and only if for each support set
s(m)
, one of the following holds: (1)

\varphi|_{s(m)}\in H^{\infty}|_{s(m)}
,
\psi|_{s(m)}\in H^{\infty}|_{s(m)}
,

(\varphi\psi)|_{s(m)}\in H^{\infty}|_{s(m)}.
(2)
\varphi|_{s(m)}\in H^{\infty}|_{s(m)}.
(3)
\psi|_{s(m)}\in H^{\infty}|_{s(m)}
,
(\varphi\psi)|_{s(m)}\in H^{\infty}|_{s(m)}.
Therefore, we have

H^{\infty}\left[\varphi,\psi,\varphi\psi\right]\cap H^{\infty}\left[\varphi\right]\cap H^{\infty}\left[\psi,\varphi\psi\right]\subset H^{\infty}+C

if and only if

H^{\infty}\left[\varphi\right]\cap H^{\infty}\left[\psi,\varphi\psi\right]\subset H^{\infty}+C,

which completes the proof.

\displaystyle\subset H^{\infty}+C.

\psi_{2}&0\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}\psi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\psi_{2}&0\\

In this situation, there are three

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}1&\lambda\\
0&0\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&0\\
\lambda&1\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}1-\lambda t&\lambda\left(1-\lambda t\right)\\
t&\lambda t\end{array}\right],\left|\lambda\right|\leq 16.

Case 1:
If
\lambda=0
in the matrix (

), we have

H^{\infty}\left[\Phi(I-A),A\Psi,\Phi A\Psi\right]=H^{\infty}\left[\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&\varphi_{2}\\

0&0\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}\psi_{1}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\right],

which is the same Douglas algebras as (

If
\lambda\neq 0
in the matrix (
5

\displaystyle\Phi(I-A)=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}&\varphi_{2}\\

\displaystyle A\Psi=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}1&\lambda\\

\psi_{2}&0\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\psi_{1}+\lambda\psi_{2}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle\Phi A\Psi=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}&\varphi_{2}\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\psi_{1}+\lambda\psi_{2}&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}\left(\psi_{1}+\lambda\psi_{2}\right)&0\\

Therefore, we have

H^{\infty}\left[\Phi(I-A),A\Psi,\Phi A\Psi\right]=H^{\infty}\left[\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&\varphi_{2}-\lambda\varphi_{1}\\
0&0\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\psi_{1}+\lambda\psi_{2}0&\\
0&0\end{array}\right],\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}\left(\psi_{1}+\lambda\psi_{2}\right)&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right]\right],

which is the same Douglas algebras as (
57
).

\displaystyle\Phi A\Psi

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}\varphi_{1}&\varphi_{2}\\

0&0\end{array}\right].

\displaystyle H^{\infty}\left[\Phi(I-A),A\Psi,\Phi A\Psi\right]

0&0\end{array}\right]\right],

\displaystyle A\Psi=\left[\begin{array}[c]{cc}1-\lambda t&\lambda\left(1-\lambda t\right)\\

Combing all the above results, we complete the proof of the Corollary.

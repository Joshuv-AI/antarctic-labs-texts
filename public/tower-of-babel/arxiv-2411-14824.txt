Spectral regularity with respect to dilations for a class of pseudodifferential operators.
Horia D. Cornean and R. Purice

Abstract
. We continue the study of the perturbation problem discussed in [ 2 ] and get rid of the ’slow variation’ assumption by considering symbols of the form a ⁡ ( x + δ ​ F ​ ( x ) , ξ ) a\big(x+\delta\,F(x),\xi\big) with a a a real Hörmander symbol of class S 0 , 0 0 ​ ( ℝ d × ℝ d ) S^{0}_{0,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}) and F F a smooth function with all its derivatives globally bounded, with | δ | ≤ 1 |\delta|\leq 1 . The main results

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists
C(a,F)>0
such that for
|\delta|\leq 1
we have the estimation:

\text{\tt{\bf d}}_{\text{\tt{\bf H}}}\Big(\sigma\big(K_{\delta}\big)\,,\,\sigma\big(K_{0}\big)\Big)\,\leq\,C(a,F)\sqrt{|\delta|}.

Remark 2.2
.

Counter-examples from the literature show that this estimation is ’sharp’, i.e. spectral gaps of order
\sqrt{|\delta|}
may be created by these type of perturbations.

From Theorem 1.5 in
[
2
]
and some other similar results from the literature, one may expect a more regular behaviour of the spectral edges seen as functions of
\delta
. By spectral edges we understand quantities like
\sup\sigma(K_{\delta})
,
\inf\sigma(K_{\delta})
, or the extremities of the possible inner gaps in the spectrum. In this situation we obtain the following result depending on the decay at infinity of the second order derivatives of the ’perturbing function’
F\in C^{\infty}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
\big|\big(\partial_{x_{j}}\partial_{x_{k}}F\big)(x)\big|\leq\,C<x>^{-(1+\mu)}
for some
C>0
,
\mu>0
and for any pair of indices

. Then there exists
C(a,F)>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
such that for
|\delta|\leq\delta_{0}
we have the estimation:

\big|\,\mathcal{E}_{\pm}(\delta)\,-\,\mathcal{E}_{\pm}(0)\,\big|\,\leq\,C(a,F)|\delta|^{(1+\mu)/(2+\mu)}.

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,\overline{\phi(z+v/2)}\phi(z-v/2)\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\big[F_{\theta}(z)\cdot\big(\partial_{z}\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(z),v\big)\big],

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathfrak{W}[\mathfrak{K}_{\delta}]^{\circ}\,,\,(\overline{\phi}\otimes\phi)\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}-\big\langle\mathfrak{K}_{0}\,,\,(\overline{\phi}\otimes\phi)\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}du\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}(z-u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,\overline{\phi(z+v/2)}\phi(z-v/2)\,\Big[\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big(z+\delta F_{\theta}(u),v\big)-\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big(z,v\big)\Big]\Big|=

\displaystyle=\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,\overline{\phi(z+v/2)}\phi(z-v/2)\,\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}du\,\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}(z-u)\,\times

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt\times\,\Big(\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\big[F_{\theta}(u)\cdot\big(\partial_{z}\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(u),v\big)\big]\Big)\Big]

We estimate the above two differences in the next two Lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

The symbol
a^{\prime}_{\delta,\theta}(z,\eta)
associated to the kernel
\mathfrak{K}_{\delta,\theta}^{\prime}(z,v):=\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\big[F_{\theta}(z)\cdot\big(\partial_{z}\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(z),v\big)\big]
belongs to
S^{0}_{0,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with seminorms bounded by
C\theta^{-1}
uniformly for
(\delta,\theta)\in(0,1]^{2}
.

Proof.

We can write that:

\displaystyle a^{\prime}_{\delta,\theta}(z,\eta)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,e^{-i<\eta,v>}\,\mathfrak{K}_{\delta,\theta}^{\prime}(z,v)=

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,e^{-i<\eta,v>}\,\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\big[F_{\theta}(z)\cdot\big(\partial_{z}({\rm{1}\hskip-3.0pt\mathbf{l}}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{-})a\big)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(z),v\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\big[F_{\theta}(z)\cdot(\partial_{z}a)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(z),\eta\big)\big].

As in Remark
1.3
, we notice that

(4.14)

\nu_{n,m}(a^{\prime}_{\delta,\theta})\,\leq\,\big(\underset{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\sup}\big|F_{\theta}(z)\big|\big)\,\underset{0\leq s\leq 1}{\sup}\nu_{n+1,m}(a[F]_{s})\leq\,M_{F}\,\theta^{-1}\,\underset{0\leq s\leq 1}{\sup}\nu_{n+1,m}(a[F]_{s})

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The symbol
a^{\prime\prime}_{\delta,\theta}(z,\eta)
associated to the kernel

\mathfrak{K}_{\delta,\theta}^{\prime\prime}(z,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}du\,\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}(z-u)\,\Big(\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\big[F_{\theta}(u)\cdot\big(\partial_{z}\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(u),v\big)\big]\Big)

belongs to
S^{0}_{0,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

with seminorms bounded by
C\theta^{-1}
uniformly for
(\delta,\theta)\in(0,1]^{2}
.

Proof.

We can write that:

\displaystyle a^{\prime\prime}_{\delta,\theta}(z,\eta)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,e^{-i<\eta,v>}\,\mathfrak{K}_{\delta,\theta}^{\prime\prime}(z,v)=

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}ds\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}du\,\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}(z-u)\,\Big(\big[F_{\theta}(u)\cdot(\partial_{z}\,a)\big(z+s\delta\,F_{\theta}(u),\eta\big)\big]\Big).

As in Remark
1.3
, we notice that

(4.15)

\nu_{n,m}(a^{\prime}_{\delta,\theta})\,\leq\,\big(\underset{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\sup}\big|F_{\theta}(u)\big|\big)\,\underset{0\leq s\leq 1}{\sup}\nu_{n+1,m}(a[F]_{s})\leq\,M_{F}\,\theta^{-1}\,\underset{0\leq s\leq 1}{\sup}\nu_{n+1,m}(a[F]_{s})

∎

Putting the above results together we conclude that:

(4.16)

\Big|\big\langle\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{\delta}\,,\,(\overline{\phi}\otimes\phi)\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,-\,\big\langle\mathfrak{W}[\mathfrak{K}^{\circ}_{\delta}]\,,\,(\overline{\phi}\otimes\phi)\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big|\,\leq\,C(F)\delta\theta^{-1}.

Let us consider now the ’outer region’ integrals:

(4.17)

\displaystyle\big\langle\mathfrak{K}^{\bot}_{\delta}\,,\,(\overline{\phi}\otimes\phi)\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,-\,\big\langle\mathfrak{W}[\mathfrak{K}^{\bot}_{\delta}]\,,\,(\overline{\phi}\otimes\phi)\circ\Upsilon^{-1}\big\rangle_{\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=

\displaystyle\hskip 256.0748pt=\ \delta\mathcal{I}_{1}[\phi](\delta,\theta,\kappa)\,+\,\delta^{2}\mathcal{I}_{2}[\phi](\delta,\theta,\kappa)

where:

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}[\phi](\delta,\theta,\kappa):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}du\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}(z-u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,\overline{\phi(z+v/2)}\phi(z-v/2)\,\big(\nabla_{z}\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big)\big(z+\delta F_{\theta}^{\bot}(z),v\big)\,\times

\displaystyle\hskip 5.69046pt\times\,\Big[\big((z-u)\cdot\nabla F_{\theta}^{\bot}\big)(z)+(1/2)\int_{0}^{1}ds\,(1-s)\big((z-u)\otimes(z-u)(\nabla\otimes\nabla)\,F_{\theta}^{\bot}\big)\big(z+s(u-z)\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}[\phi](\delta,\theta,\kappa)

\displaystyle:=\,(1/2)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}du\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,\mathfrak{W}_{\kappa}(z-u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dv\,\overline{\phi(z+v/2)}\phi(z-v/2)\,\times

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\,\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)ds\big((\nabla_{z}\otimes\nabla_{z})\mathfrak{K}_{0}\big)\big(z+\delta F_{\theta}^{\bot}(z)+s\delta(F_{\theta}^{\bot}(u)-F_{\theta}^{\bot}(z)),v\big)\,\times

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\,\int_{0}^{1}dt\,\big((z-u)\cdot\nabla F_{\theta}^{\bot}\big)(z+t(u-z))\,\int_{0}^{1}dt^{\prime}\,\big((z-u)\cdot\nabla F_{\theta}^{\bot}\big)(z+t^{\prime}(u-z))

We may conclude that:

(4.18)

\big|\mathcal{I}_{1}[\phi](\delta,\theta,\kappa)\big|\,\leq\,\kappa^{-2}\theta^{1+\mu}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad\big|\mathcal{I}_{2}[\phi](\delta,\theta,\kappa)\big|\,\leq\,\kappa^{-2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

∎

The result of this second Proposition clearly implies that:

(4.19)

\ \big|\widetilde{\mathcal{E}_{+}}(\delta)\,-\,\mathcal{E}_{+}(\delta)\big|\,=\,\mathcal{O}\big(\delta/\theta+\delta\kappa^{-2}\theta^{1+\mu}+\delta^{2}\kappa^{-2}\big).

Taking into account (
4.11
) and (
4.19
), in order to finish the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we only have to make the following choices for our scaling parameters:

•

\theta=\delta^{1-\rho}
for some
\rho\in(0,1)
, so that
\delta\theta^{-1}=\delta^{\rho}
;

•

\kappa^{2}=\delta^{\rho}
so that
\delta\kappa^{-2}\theta^{1+\mu}=\delta^{(1-\rho)+(1-\rho)(1+\mu)}=\delta^{(2+\mu)(1-\rho)}
and
\delta^{2}\kappa^{-2}=\delta^{(2-\rho)}
.

•

imposing
\rho=(2+\mu)(1-\rho)\in(0,1)
means taking
\rho=(1+\mu)/(2+\mu)
.

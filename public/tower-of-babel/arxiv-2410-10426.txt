On Sum-Free Functions
Alyssa Ebeling, Xiang-dong Hou, Ashley Rydell, Shujun Zhao

Abstract
. A function from 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} to 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} is said to be k k th order sum-free if the sum of its values over each k k -dimensional 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} -affine subspace of 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2^{n}} is nonzero. This notion was recently introduced by C. Carlet as, among other things, a generalization of APN functions. At the center of this new topic is a conjecture about the sum-freedom of the multiplicative inverse function f inv ​ ( x ) = x − 1 f_{\text{\rm inv}}(x)=x^{-1} (with 0 − 1 0^{-1} defined to be 0 0 ). It is known that f inv f_{\text{\rm inv}} is 2nd order (equivalently, ( n − 2 ) (n-2) th order) sum-free if and only if n n is odd, and it is conjectured that for 3 ≤ k ≤ n − 3 3\leq k\leq n-3 , f inv f_{\text{\rm inv}} is never k k th order sum-free. The conjecture has been confirmed for even n n but remains open for odd n n . In the present paper, we show that the conjecture holds under each of the following conditions: (1) n = 13 n=13 ; (2) 3 | n 3\mid n ; (3) 5 | n 5\mid n ; (4) the smallest prime divisor l l of n n satisfies ( l − 1 ) ​ ( l + 2 ) ≤ ( n + 1 ) / 2 (l-1)(l+2)\leq(n+1)/2 . We also determine the “right” q q -ary generalization of the binary multiplicative inverse function f inv f_{\text{\rm inv}} in the context of sum-freedom. This q q -ary generalization not only maintains most results for its binary version, but also exhibits some extraordinary phenomena that are not observed in the binary case.

1.
Introduction

In a recent paper
[
4
]
, Carlet introduced the notion of
sum-free
functions. A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is said to be
k
th order sum-free
if
\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\neq 0
for all
k
-dimensional affine subspaces
A
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. Unless specified otherwise, subspaces and affine subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
are over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. This definition does not involve the multiplicative structure of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, hence sum-freedom can be defined for functions from
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
to
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. However, the multiplicative structure of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
plays an important role in the constructions and analysis of such functions. As described in
[
4
]
, the motivation for the notion of sum-free functions was two-fold: First, sum-free functions are a natural generalization of
almost perfect nonlinear
(APN) functions; the latter have been extensively studied in cryptography for their resistance against differential attacks
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
. In particular, APN functions are precisely 2nd order sum-free functions
[
11
]
. Second, sum-freedom addresses the vulnerability of block ciphers to integral attacks which exploit the predicability of the sums of values of S-boxes on affine subspaces
[
14
,
22
]
. Moreover, from a theoretic point of view, sum-freedom is a notion with rich mathematical content; beneath its simple appearance, there are difficult and enticing questions that require sophisticated mathematical tools.

Let
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
denote the multiplicative inverse function of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, that is,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(x)=x^{-1}
for
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}^{*}
and
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(0)=0
. For
n\geq 2
, we have
f_{\text{\rm inv}}(x)=x^{2^{n}-2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. The sum-freedom of
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
has been the focus of several recent papers
[
4
,
5
,
7
]
. It is well known that
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is APN (i.e., 2nd order sum-free) if and only if
n
is odd
[
20
]
. Carlet
[
4
]
determined that
\sum_{x\in A}1/x\neq 0
for all affine subspaces
A
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
not containing
0
. Hence
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free if and only if
\sum_{0\neq u\in E}1/u\neq 0
for all
k
-dimensional subspaces
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
. It is trivial that
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
1
st order sum-free and it is known from
[
5
]
that for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free if and only if it is
(n-k)
th order sum-free. Therefore, for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free if
k\in\{1,n-1\}
for even
n
and
k\in\{1,2,n-2,n-1\}
for odd

. Theoretic results and computer searches, which we will summarize in the next section, strongly suggest that these are the only values of
k
for which
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
is
k
th order sum-free. This claim has been confirmed in
[
7
]
for even
n
, but for odd

the question remains open. (We remind the reader that the even
n
case does not represent “half” of the question as we will see later that a small prime divisor of
n
gives an advantage for the treatment of the question.) For
n\geq 2
, define

(1.1)

\mathcal{K}_{n}=\{1\leq k\leq n-1:\text{$f_{\text{\rm inv}}$ is not $k$th order sum-free on $\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}$}\}.

(Note that the meaning of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
here is different from that in
[
7
]
.)
Trivially,
\mathcal{K}_{2},\mathcal{K}_{3},\mathcal{K}_{5}
are empty, and we already know that
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\{2,3,\dots n-2\}
for even
n\geq 4
and
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset\{3,4,\dots,n-3\}
for odd
n\geq 7
. Thus the above open question can be formulated as the following

Conjecture 1.1
.

For odd
n\geq 7
,
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\{3,4,\dots,n-3\}
.

In the present paper, we obtain several new results concerning the above conjecture. We show that the conjecture holds under any of the following conditions:

•

n=13
;

•

3\mid n
;

•

5\mid n
;

•

the smallest prime divisor
l
of
n
satisfies
(l-1)(l+2)\leq(n+1)/2
.

Because of the last condition, the most difficult cases of Conjecture

are the scenarios where

is a prime or a product of two primes, not necessarily different, close to each other.
Carlet
[
5
]
proved that

for all

. We found a different proof of this fact using the Lang-Weil bound. The new proof is included in the present paper because we believe that the approach can be applied to more general situations.

The notion of sum-freedom can be generalized to functions from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, mutatis mutandis, where
q
is a prime power. A function
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is said to be
k
th order sum-free
if
\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\neq 0
for all
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine subspaces

of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}

-ary version of the binary multiplicative inverse function

that maintains the properties of

? We determine that the “right”
q
-ary version is the function
g_{q-1}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
defined by
g_{q-1}(x)=1/x^{q-1}
with
1/0
defined as
0
. The reason for this, as we shall see in Section 4, is not entirely obvious. It turns out that most existing results concerning the sum-freedom of

still hold for

. However, more interesting things seem to be happening in the
q
-ary case than the binary case. For example, through a computer search, we find that with

and

,

on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{7}}
is

th order sum-free for all

; similar phenomena have not been observed in the binary case. We are not aware of any immediate applications of the functions
g_{2}(x)=1/x^{2}
on
\mathbb{F}_{3^{7}}
and
g_{4}(x)=1/x^{4}
on
\mathbb{F}_{5^{7}}
. Nevertheless, these are extraordinary examples from a mathematical point of view.

The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides the preliminaries for the paper. We recall the existing results about binary sum-free functions, especially the multiplicative inverse function
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
. We also include a determination of all
(n-1)
th order sum-free functions on
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
; this question has not been addressed previously. In Section 3, we confirm Conjecture

under each of the conditions described above and we prove that

for all

. In Section 4, we explore

-ary sum-free functions with focus on the generalization of the binary multiplicative inverse function
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
. It follows from some nontrivial computation that the “right”
q
-ary version of

is the function
g_{q-1}
defined by
g_{q-1}(x)=1/x^{q-1}
. We show that most existing results concerning the sum-freedom of

still hold for
g_{q-1}
. We also include some limited computer search results on

with

. The appendix contains a proof of the absolute irreducibility of a symmetric homogeneous polynomial of degree 8 in 4 variables over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

Throughout the paper, a number of tools are used in the proofs and discussions (e.g., the Moore determinant, Carlitz’s formula, symmetric polynomials, the Lang-Weil bound). The relations with other areas of mathematics is part of the reason that we find the notion of sum-free functions and the questions that arise from it attractive.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
k
th order sum-free functions with
k=0,1,2,n-1,n

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be a function. We know that

•

f
is
0
th order sum-free if and only if
f(x)\neq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
;

•

f
is
1
st order sum-free if and only if
f
is a bijection;

•

f
is
2
nd order sum-free if and only if
f
is APN;

•

f
is
n
th order sum-free if and only if
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}f(x)\neq 0
, i.e.,
\deg f=2^{n}-1
when
f
is represented by a polynomial modulo
X^{2^{n}}-X
.

The last statement follows from the fact that

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}x^{i}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if}\ 0\leq i\leq 2^{n}-2,\cr 1&\text{if}\ i=2^{n}-1.\end{cases}

There does not appear to be a determination of
(n-1)
th order sum-free functions in the literature. Hence the following theorem is useful for the record.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
be represented by

f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}a_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}[X].

Then
f
is
(n-1)
th order sum-free if and only if
a_{2^{n}-1}=0
and

(2.1)

\left|\begin{matrix}a_{2^{n}-1-2^{0}}&a_{2^{n}-1-2^{1}}&\cdots&a_{2^{n}-1-2^{n-1}}\cr a_{2^{n}-1-2^{1}}^{2}&a_{2^{n}-1-2^{2}}^{2}&\cdots&a_{2^{n}-1-2^{0}}^{2}\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr a_{2^{n}-1-2^{n-1}}^{2^{n-1}}&a_{2^{n}-1-2^{0}}^{2^{n-1}}&\cdots&a_{2^{n}-1-2^{n-2}}^{2^{n-1}}\end{matrix}\right|\neq 0.

Proof.

When
n=1
, the claim is obviously true, so we assume
n\geq 2
.

(\Leftarrow
) For
0\neq b\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, let

\displaystyle A_{b}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}:\text{\rm Tr}(bx)=0\},

\displaystyle B_{b}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}:\text{\rm Tr}(bx)=1\},

where
\text{\rm Tr}=\text{\rm Tr}_{2^{n}/2}
. Each hyperplane (affine subspace of co-dimension one) of
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
is of the form
A_{b}
or
B_{b}
, depending on whether it contains 0 or not.
For
0\neq b\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in B_{b}}f(x)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\cr\text{\rm Tr}(bx)=1\end{subarray}}f(x)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}f(x)\text{\rm Tr}(bx)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}a_{i}x^{i}\Big)\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}b^{2^{j}}x^{2^{j}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{2^{n}-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{i}b^{2^{j}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}x^{i+2^{j}}.

In the above,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}x^{i+2^{j}}=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if}\ i+2^{j}=2^{n}-1,\cr 0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence

\sum_{x\in B_{b}}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{2^{n}-1-2^{j}}b^{2^{j}}.

By
[
13
, Theorem 2.29]
, the
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-linear map
\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}
,
x\mapsto\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_{2^{n}-1-2^{j}}x^{2^{j}}
is bijective if and if condition (
2.1
) is satisfied. Therefore,
\sum_{x\in B_{b}}f(x)\neq 0
. Since
a_{2^{n}-1}=0
, we have
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{n}}}f(x)=0
, hence
\sum_{x\in A_{b}}f(x)=\sum_{x\in B_{b}}f(x)\neq 0
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Simply trace back the proof of (
\Leftarrow
).
∎

2.3.
The Moore determinant

The Moore determinant (see
[
18
]
) plays an important role in the study of the sum-freedom of
f_{\text{\rm inv}}
and its
q
-ary version.
Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
be independent indeterminates. The
Moore determinant
in
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is defined to be

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\left|\begin{matrix}X_{1}&\cdots&X_{k}\cr X_{1}^{q}&\cdots&X_{k}^{q}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{q^{k-1}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{q^{k-1}}\end{matrix}\right|\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X_{1},\dots,X_{k}].

Lemma 2.2

(
[
16
, Lemma 3.51]
)
.

We have

\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{a_{1},\dots,a_{i-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Bigl(X_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}a_{j}X_{j}\Bigr).

Corollary 2.3
.

A set of
k
elements

v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\neq 0
.

For
0\leq i\leq k
, let

\Delta_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\left|\begin{matrix}X_{1}&\cdots&X_{k}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{q^{i-1}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{q^{i-1}}\cr X_{1}^{q^{i+1}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{q^{i+1}}\cr\vdots&&\vdots\cr X_{1}^{q^{k}}&\cdots&X_{k}^{q^{k}}\end{matrix}\right|.

Note that
\Delta_{k}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
and
\Delta_{0}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})^{q}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

is the
\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}
-span of

(i)

Let

(Note that condition (

s\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}}\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V\leq s+1
.

(ii)

We have

for some

).

Note that in Lemma

, which gives Corollary

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

(i) We have a short exact sequence of

-subspaces

0\longrightarrow(\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V)\cap E\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V\xrightarrow{\ L_{E}\ }L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V)\longrightarrow 0,

where

(3.3)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}}\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}}\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}U+\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}}(\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V)\cap E=\begin{cases}s&\text{if}\ E\not\subset\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V,\cr s+1&\text{if}\ E\subset\mathbb{F}_{2^{l}}V.\end{cases}

4.

Recall that a function

is said to be
k

if
\sum_{x\in A}f(x)\neq 0
for all
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-affine subspaces
A
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. In general, compared with their binary counterpart,
q
-ary sum-free functions are more difficult to characterize, construct and analyze. APN functions in odd characteristic, studied in
[
9
,
10
]
, do not represent the 2nd order
q
-ary sum-free functions. On the other hand, the
generalized almost perfect nonlinear
(GAPN) functions in odd characteristic, introduced and explored in
[
15
]
, appear to be related to the 1st order sum-free functions. However, this connection will not be pursued in the present paper.

We first take a look of the 1st order and the
(n-1)
th order
q
-ary sum-free functions.

4.1.
The 1st order

Let
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be a function represented by
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{q^{n}-1}a_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
. Then
f
is 1st order sum-free if and only if
\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f(b(x+c))\neq 0
for all

and

. We have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f(b(x+c))=\sum_{i=0}^{q^{n}-1}a_{i}b^{i}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+c)^{i}.

There is a polynomial
g_{i,q}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
(
p=\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
) such that

(4.1)

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+X)^{i}=g_{i,q}(X^{q}-X).

The polynomial
g_{i,q}
was introduced and studied in
[
12
]
for a different purpose and it satisfies the recursive relation

\begin{cases}g_{0,q}=\cdots=g_{q-2,q}=0,\cr g_{q-1,q}=-1,\cr g_{i,q}=Xg_{i-q,q}+g_{i-q+1,q},\quad i\geq q.\end{cases}

Therefore,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}f(b(x+c))=\sum_{i=0}^{q^{n}-1}a_{i}b^{i}g_{i,q}(c^{q}-c).

Consequently, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{q^{n}-1}a_{i}X^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
. Then
f:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is
1
st order sum-free if and only if

\sum_{i=0}^{q^{n}-1}a_{i}b^{i}g_{i,q}(d)\neq 0

for all

and

with
\text{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(d)=0
.

In particular, a power function
X^{i}
is 1st order sum-free on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
if and only if

g_{i,q}(d)\neq 0
for all
d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
\text{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(d)=0
. Here, when

(

),

g_{i,q}(d)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(x+c)^{i}=-\sum_{0<j\equiv 0\,\text{(mod $q-1$)}}\binom{i}{j}c^{i-j}.

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(bx)=c}f(x)

When

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(bx)=0}f(x)=

\displaystyle-a_{q^{n}-1}+\sum_{j_{0}+\cdots+j_{n-1}=q-1}\binom{q-1}{j_{0},\dots,j_{n-1}}a_{q^{n}-1-(j_{0}q^{0}+\cdots+j_{n-1}q^{n-1})}b^{j_{0}q^{0}+\cdots+j_{n-1}q^{n-1}}.

In the above notation,

4.3.

Recall that the

Lemma 4.5
.

(i)

We have

(4.7)

\prod_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}(Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})=(-1)^{k}\frac{\Delta(Y,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}.

(ii)

Let
\Delta=\Delta(Y,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
and
\Delta_{i}=\Delta_{i}(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
.
For
l\geq 0
, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{1}{(Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})^{l+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta}\sum_{l_{0}q^{0}+\cdots+l_{k}q^{k}=l}\binom{l_{0}+\cdots+l_{k}}{l_{0},\dots,l_{k}}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\Bigl(\frac{(-1)^{i}\Delta_{i})}{\Delta}\Bigr)^{l_{i}}.

Proof.

(i)
\Delta(Y,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is a polynomial of degree
q^{k}
in
Y
with roots
-(a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
;
(-1)^{k}\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})
is the leading coefficient.

(ii) Differentiating both sides of equation (
4.7
) with respect to
Y
gives

(4.9)

\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{1}{Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}}=\frac{\Delta(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})^{q}}{\Delta(Y,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}=\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta}.

Consider the generating function

G_{k}(Z)=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{1}{(Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})^{l}}\Bigr)Z^{l}.

We have

\displaystyle G_{k}(Z)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\frac{Z}{Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}}\Bigr)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{\displaystyle\frac{Z}{Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}}}{\displaystyle 1-\frac{Z}{Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}}}

\displaystyle=Z\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{1}{Y-Z+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k}}

\displaystyle=Z\,\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta(Y-Z,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}\kern 70.0001pt\text{(by eq. \eqref{delta0/delta})}

\displaystyle=Z\,\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta-\Delta(Z,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}

\displaystyle=Z\,\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta}\cdot\frac{1}{\displaystyle 1-\frac{\Delta(Z,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})}{\Delta}}

\displaystyle=Z\,\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Delta(Z,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})^{l}}{\Delta^{l}}.

In the above,

\displaystyle\Delta(Z,X_{1},\dots,X_{k})^{l}=\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{k}(-1)^{i}\Delta_{i}Z^{q^{i}}\Bigr)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{0}+\cdots+l_{k}=l}\binom{l}{l_{0},\dots,l_{k}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{k}((-1)^{i}\Delta_{i})^{l_{i}}\Bigr)Z^{l_{0}q^{0}+\cdots+l_{k}q^{k}}.

Therefore

G_{k}(Z)=Z\,\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\;\sum_{l_{0}q^{0}+\cdots+l_{k}q^{k}=l}\binom{l_{0}+\cdots+l_{k}}{l_{0},\dots,l_{k}}\Bigl(\prod_{i=0}^{k}\Bigl(\frac{(-1)^{i}\Delta_{i})}{\Delta}\Bigr)^{l_{i}}\Bigr)Z^{l}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}}\frac{1}{(Y+a_{1}X_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}X_{k})^{l+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta_{0}}{\Delta}\sum_{l_{0}q^{0}+\cdots+l_{k}q^{k}=l}\binom{l_{0}+\cdots+l_{k}}{l_{0},\dots,l_{k}}\prod_{i=0}^{k}\Bigl(\frac{(-1)^{i}\Delta_{i})}{\Delta}\Bigr)^{l_{i}}.

∎

E
be a

Proposition 4.7

(ii)

Proof.

(i) Immediate from Theorem

(ii) Let

.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
E
and
F
be subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

. Then

(4.11)

\sum_{0\neq u\in E\oplus F}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}=\sum_{0\neq u\in E}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}+\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})^{(q-1)^{2}}\sum_{0\neq v\in L_{E}(F)}\frac{1}{v^{q-1}},

where

is a basis of
E
and
L_{E}(X)=\prod_{u\in E}(X+u)
.

Proof.

We have

(4.12)

\sum_{0\neq u\in E\oplus F}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}=\sum_{0\neq u\in E}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}+\sum_{0\neq w\in F}\,\sum_{u\in E}\frac{1}{(w+u)^{q-1}}.

By equation (
4.10
),

\sum_{u\in\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle}\frac{1}{(Y+u)^{q-1}}=\Bigl(\frac{\Delta_{0}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})}{\Delta(Y,v_{1},\dots,v_{k})}\Bigr)^{q-1}.

In the above,
\Delta_{0}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})^{q}
, and by equation (
4.7
),

\Delta(Y,v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=(-1)^{k}L_{E}(Y)\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k}).

Hence

(4.13)

\sum_{u\in\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\rangle}\frac{1}{(Y+u)^{q-1}}=\Bigl(\frac{\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})^{q}}{(-1)^{k}L_{E}(Y)\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})}\Bigr)^{q-1}=\frac{\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})^{(q-1)^{2}}}{L_{E}(Y)^{q-1}}.

Using equation (
4.13
) in (
4.12
) gives

\sum_{0\neq u\in E\oplus F}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}=\sum_{0\neq u\in E}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}+\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})^{(q-1)^{2}}\sum_{0\neq w\in F}\frac{1}{L_{E}(w)^{q-1}}.

Since

\ker(L_{E})\cap F=E\cap F=\{0\}
. Hence the restriction
L_{E}|_{F}:F\to L_{E}(F)
is an isomorphism. Therefore,

\sum_{0\neq w\in F}\frac{1}{L_{E}(w)^{q-1}}=\sum_{0\neq v\in L_{E}(F)}\frac{1}{v^{q-1}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

\{xy:x\in E,\ y\in F\}
and let
F^{\bot}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}:\text{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(xy)=0\ \text{for all}\ y\in F\}
.

Theorem 4.11

(
q
-ary version of §2.2 Result (5))
.

If
k,l\in\mathcal{K}_{n,q}
and
kl<n
, then
k+l\in\mathcal{K}_{n,q}
.

Proof.

Let
E
and
F
be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}E=k
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}F=l
, and
\sum_{0\neq u\in E}1/u^{q-1}=0
,
\sum_{0\neq v\in F}1/v^{q-1}=0
. Since

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\bigl(F\cdot L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})^{\bot}\bigr)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}F\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}E=lk<n,

there exists
0\neq a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\text{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}\bigl(aF\cdot L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})^{\bot}\bigr)=0
, i.e.,
aF\subset L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
. Since
L_{E}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
induces an isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/E\to L_{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
, there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

V\cap E=\{0\}
,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}V=l
and
L_{E}(V)=aF
. Let
b=\Delta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})^{(q-1)^{2}}
, where
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}
is a basis of
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Now by Lemma
4.9
,

\displaystyle\sum_{0\neq u\in E\oplus V}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\neq u\in E}\frac{1}{u^{q-1}}+b\sum_{0\neq v\in L_{E}(V)}\frac{1}{v^{q-1}}

\displaystyle=b\sum_{0\neq v\in aF}\frac{1}{v^{q-1}}=\frac{b}{a^{q-1}}\sum_{0\neq v\in F}\frac{1}{v^{q-1}}=0.

Hence
k+l\in\mathcal{K}_{n,q}
.
∎

Theorem 4.12

(
q
-ary version of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

The following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
\Delta(x,x^{q},x^{q^{2}},\dots,x^{q^{k-1}})\neq 0
and

\Delta_{1}(x,x^{q},x^{q^{2}},\dots,x^{q^{k-1}})=0
.

(ii)

X^{n}-1
has a factor
X^{k}+a_{k-1}X^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{2}X^{2}+a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
denote the Frobenius automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Note that

\displaystyle\Delta(x,x^{q},x^{q^{2}},\dots,x^{q^{k-1}})\neq 0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle x,\sigma(x),\dots,\sigma^{k-1}(x)\ \text{are linearly independent over}\ \mathbb{F}_{2},

and

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(x,x^{q},x^{q^{2}},\dots,x^{q^{k-1}})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle x,\sigma^{2}(x),\dots,\sigma^{k}(x)\ \text{are linearly dependent over}\ \mathbb{F}_{2}.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be a normal element over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Write
X^{n}-1=fg
, where
f=X^{k}+a_{k-1}X^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{2}X^{2}+a_{0}
. Let
x=(g(\sigma))(\alpha)
. For each
0\neq h\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
with
\deg h<k
,
hg\not\equiv 0\pmod{X^{n}-1}
, whence
(h(\sigma))(x)=((hg)(\sigma))(\alpha)\neq 0
. Hence
x,\sigma(x),\dots,\sigma^{k-1}(x)
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Since
(f(\sigma))(x)=((fg)(\sigma))(\alpha)=0
, the elements
x,\sigma^{2}(x),\dots,\sigma^{k}(x)
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Since
x,\sigma^{2}(x),\dots,\sigma^{k}(x)
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exists
0\neq f=a_{k}X^{k}+a_{k-1}X^{k-1}+\cdots+a_{2}X^{2}+a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
such that
(f(\sigma))(x)=0
. We claim that
a_{k}\neq 0
and
f\mid X^{n}-1
. Otherwise,
f_{1}:=\text{gcd}(f,X^{n}-1)
has degree
<k
and
(f_{1}(\sigma))(x)=0
. Then
x,\sigma(x),\dots,\sigma^{k-1}(x)
are linearly dependent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

, then
k\in\mathcal{K}_{n,q}
.

Appendix. Absolute Irreducibility of
\Theta_{4}

Recall that

\Theta_{4}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})=m_{(2^{3})}+m_{(2^{2},2^{2})}+m_{(2^{2},2,2)}+m_{(2^{2},2,1,1)}+m_{(2,2,2,2)},

where

m_{(2^{3})}=X_{1}^{2^{3}}+X_{2}^{2^{3}}+X_{3}^{2^{3}}+X_{4}^{2^{3}},

m_{(2^{2},2^{2})}=X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2^{2}}+X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2^{2}},

\displaystyle m_{(2^{2},2,2)}=\,

\displaystyle X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}.

\displaystyle m_{(2^{2},2,1,1)}=\,

\displaystyle X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}X_{4}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}+X_{1}^{2^{2}}X_{2}X_{3}X_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2^{2}}X_{3}X_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}+X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}^{2^{2}}X_{4}^{2}

\displaystyle+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}X_{3}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}X_{4}^{2^{2}}+X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2^{2}},

m_{(2,2,2,2)}=X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}X_{4}^{2}.

Theorem A1
.

The polynomial
\Theta_{4}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})
is irreducible over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.

To prove Theorem
A1
, we need a couple of lemmas. We have

\Theta_{4}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})=s_{4}^{2}+s_{1}(s_{1}s_{2}+s_{3})s_{4}+(s_{1}^{4}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{1}s_{3})^{2}=:G(s_{1},\dots,s_{4}),

where
s_{i}
is the
i
th elementary symmetric polynomials in
X_{1},\dots,X_{4}
.

Lemma A2
.

The polynomial
G(s_{1},\dots,s_{4})
is irreducible in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[s_{1},\dots,s_{4}]
.

Proof.

We treat
G(s_{1},\dots,s_{4})
as a polynomial in
s_{4}
. Assume to the contrary that

G=(s_{4}+A)(s_{4}+B)

for some
A,B\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}]
. Then

\begin{cases}A+B=s_{1}(s_{1}s_{2}+s_{3}),\cr AB=(s_{1}^{4}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{1}s_{3})^{2}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
s_{1}^{4}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{1}s_{3}
is irreducible in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}]
. Hence
A=a(s_{1}^{4}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{1}s_{3})^{i}
and
B=b(s_{1}^{4}+s_{2}^{2}+s_{1}s_{3})^{2-i}
for some
a,b\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{*}
and
i\in\{0,1,2\}
. In any case,
A+B
does not contain any monomial of degree
3
. Thus
A+B\neq s_{1}(s_{1}s_{2}+s_{3})
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma
A2
implies that
\Theta_{4}(X_{1},\dots,X_{4})
does not have a proper factor that is symmetric in
X_{1},\dots,X_{4}
.

We have

\Theta_{4}(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},0)=H(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})^{2},

where

H(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})=X_{1}^{4}+X_{2}^{4}+X_{3}^{4}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}^{2}X_{3}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}X_{3}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}(X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3}).

Lemma A3
.

The polynomial
H(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3})
is irreducible over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
.

Proof.

We treat
H
as a polynomial in
X_{3}
:

H=X_{3}^{4}+(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2})X_{3}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2}(X_{1}+X_{2})X_{3}+(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2})^{2},

where

X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2}=(X_{1}+\alpha X_{2})(X_{1}+\alpha^{-1}X_{2}),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}.

First assume that
H
has a factor
X_{3}+A
, where
A\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{1},X_{2}]
is homogeneous of degree
1
. Then
A\mid(X_{1}+\alpha X_{2})(X_{1}+\alpha^{-1}X_{2})
, say
A=c(X_{1}+\alpha X_{2})
, where
c\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{*}
. Then we have

\displaystyle 0=\,

\displaystyle H(X_{1},X_{2},c(X_{1}+\alpha X_{2}))

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle(1+c+c^{2})^{2}X_{1}^{4}+c(1+c)X_{1}^{3}X_{2}+(1+c)(1+\alpha c)X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle+c(\alpha+c+\alpha c)X_{1}X_{2}^{3}+(1+c)^{2}(1+\alpha c^{2})X_{2}^{4}.

It follows that

\begin{cases}1+c+c^{2}=0,\cr c(1+c)=0,\end{cases}

which is impossible.

Now assume that
H
has an absolute irreducible factor
h=X_{3}^{2}+AX_{3}+B
, where
A,B\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{1},X_{2}]
. It follows that
B=c(X_{1}+\alpha X_{2})^{2}
or
c(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2})
, where
c\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}^{*}
.

Case 1.
Assume that
B=c(X_{1}+\alpha X_{2})^{2}=c(X_{1}^{2}+\alpha^{-1}X_{2}^{2})
. Then
X_{3}^{2}+A(X_{2},X_{1})X_{3}+B(X_{2},X_{1})
is a
different
irreducible factor of
H
(different since
B(X_{2},X_{1})\neq B(X_{1},X_{2})
). Thus

H=(X_{3}^{2}+A(X_{1},X_{2})X_{3}+B(X_{1},X_{2}))(X_{3}^{2}+A(X_{2},X_{1})X_{3}+B(X_{2},X_{1})).

Comparing the coefficients gives

\displaystyle A(X_{1},X_{2})+A(X_{2},X_{1})=0,

\displaystyle B(X_{1},X_{2})+B(X_{2},X_{1})+A(X_{1},X_{2})A(X_{2},X_{1})=X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2}.

It follows that

c\alpha(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2})+A(X_{1},X_{2})^{2}=X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2}.

This is impossible since
X_{1}X_{2}
does not appear at the left hand side.

Case 2.
Assume that
B=c(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2})
.

Case 2.1.
Assume that
A(X_{1},X_{2})=A(X_{2},X_{1})
. Then
A(X_{1},X_{2})=b(X_{1}+X_{2})
for some
b\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, and hence

h=X_{3}^{2}+b(X_{1}+X_{2})X_{3}+c(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+X_{1}X_{2}).

However,

\displaystyle H\equiv\,

\displaystyle X_{3}\left(b^{3}X_{1}^{3}+b^{3}X_{1}^{2}X_{2}+b^{3}X_{1}X_{2}^{2}+b^{3}X_{2}^{3}+bX_{1}^{3}+bX_{2}^{3}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}+X_{1}X_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+b^{2}cX_{1}^{4}+b^{2}cX_{1}^{3}X_{2}+b^{2}cX_{1}X_{2}^{3}+b^{2}cX_{2}^{4}+c^{2}X_{1}^{4}+c^{2}X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}+c^{2}X_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle+cX_{1}^{4}+cX_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}+cX_{2}^{4}+X_{1}^{4}+X_{1}^{2}X_{2}^{2}+X_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\not\equiv\,

\displaystyle 0\pmod{h},

which is a contradiction.

Case 2.2.
Assume that
A(X_{1},X_{2})\neq A(X_{2},X_{1})
. Then

h(X_{2},X_{1},X_{3})=X_{3}^{2}+A(X_{2},X_{1})X_{3}+B(X_{2},X_{1})

is a different irreducible factor of
H
. Thus

H=(X_{3}^{2}+A(X_{1},X_{2})X_{3}+B(X_{1},X_{2}))(X_{3}^{2}+A(X_{2},X_{1})X_{3}+B(X_{2},X_{1})).

Comparing the coefficients of
X_{3}^{3}
gives
0=A(X_{1},X_{2})+A(X_{2},X_{1})
, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A1
.

Assume to the contrary that
\Theta_{4}
has an absolutely irreducible factor
f
of degree
d
with
0<d<8
. Treating
f
as a polynomial in
X_{4}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}]
, we may write

f=X_{4}^{d}+A_{d-1}X_{4}^{d-1}+\cdots+A_{0},

where
A_{i}\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}]
is homogeneous of degree
d-i
. Then
A_{0}\mid\Theta_{4}(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},0)=H^{2}
. Since
H
is absolutely irreducible (Lemma
A3
) and
\deg H=4
, we must have
d=4
and
A_{0}=cH
for some
c\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}
, so

f=X_{4}^{4}+A_{3}X_{4}^{3}+A_{2}X_{4}^{2}+A_{1}X_{4}+cH.

The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
acts on
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{1},\cdots,X_{4}]
by permuting
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
. We claim that
f
is symmetric in
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
. Otherwise, there exists
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{3}
such that

\sigma(f)=X_{4}^{4}+\sigma(A_{3})X_{4}^{3}+\sigma(A_{2})X_{4}^{2}+\sigma(A_{1})X_{4}+cH

is a different irreducible factor of
\Theta_{4}
. It follows that

\Theta_{4}=f\sigma(f)=X_{4}^{8}+\cdots+(A_{1}+\sigma(A_{1}))cHX_{4}+c^{2}H^{2}.

However, the coefficient of
X_{4}
in
\Theta_{4}
is

X_{1}X_{2}X_{3}(X_{1}+X_{2})(X_{1}+X_{3})(X_{2}+X_{3})(X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3}),

which is not divisible by
H
. This is a contradiction, hence the claim is proved.

We may also treat
f
as a polynomial in
X_{1}
over
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{2}[X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}]
. By the same argument,
f
is also symmetric in
X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}
. It follows that
f
is symmetric in
X_{1},\dots,X_{4}
. However, this is impossible by Lemma
A2
.

The proof of Theorem
A1
is now complete.
∎

We suspect that in general,
\Theta_{k}
is absolutely irreducible for
k\geq 3
, although the proof for the case
k=4
given here is already quite technical.

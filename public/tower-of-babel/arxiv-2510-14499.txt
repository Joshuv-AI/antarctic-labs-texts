Intersection of conjugate Hadamard subfactors arising from Fourier matrices
Keshab Chandra Bakshi, Satyajit Guin, Guruprasad

Abstract
Given two distinct complex Hadamard matrices belonging to the same equivalence class generated by the tensor products of Fourier matrices, we show that if the corresponding Hadamard subfactors are conjugate, then their intersection is a factor with finite Jones index. We compute the index of the intersection explicitly and determine its relative commutant. Furthermore, we precisely characterize when these intersections give rise to vertex model subfactors, thereby extending our earlier results in low dimensions. As an application, we derive an explicit formula for the Connes-Størmer relative entropy associated with these intersections. These results reveal how the internal algebraic structure of complex Hadamard matrices governs the relative position and entropic behaviour of the subfactors.

1
Introduction

The study of
two subfactors
inside a common ambient factor reveals subtle structural and categorical phenomena that go far beyond the single-subfactor theory
[
11
]
. Given two subfactors
N_{1},N_{2}\subset M
of a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor, the intersection
N_{1}\cap N_{2}
can display a rich and sometimes unexpected behavior. In general,
N_{1}\cap N_{2}
need not be a factor, and even when it is, its Jones index in
M
may be infinite. For instance, Jones constructed (in
[
14
]
, see also
[
2
]
) an example of
N_{1},N_{2}\subset M
with
[M:N_{1}]=[M:N_{2}]=2
for which the (Pimsner–Popa) index of
N_{1}\cap N_{2}
in
M
is infinite. Latter, Jones and Xu established in
[
13
]
that the intersection of two finite-index subfactors has finite index if and only if the corresponding angle operator
[
19
]
has finite spectrum.

In the absence of a general theory describing intersections of subfactors, it becomes natural to examine specific but meaningful families where explicit computation is possible. Motivated by this, we focus on
Hadamard
(or
spin model
) subfactors, a class introduced and emphasized by Jones, which lie at the crossroads of operator algebras, combinatorics, and quantum symmetries. These subfactors, arising from complex Hadamard matrices, offer a concrete setting where one can analyse the behaviour of intersections in depth (see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
for recent developments).

The main goal of this paper is to investigate the intersection of two
conjugate Hadamard subfactors

R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
arising from distinct complex Hadamard matrices
U,V
belonging to the same equivalence class generated by tensor products of Fourier matrices. We show that the intersection
R_{U}\cap R_{V}
is always a factor of finite Jones index, and we determine this index explicitly in terms of an associated finite abelian group. Moreover, we compute the relative commutant of the intersection and identify the precise condition under which it yields a
vertex model subfactor
.

Our first main result asserts that factoriality of the intersection holds whenever the Hadamard subfactors are conjugate.

Theorem A
.

Let
U
and
V
be two distinct complex Hadamard matrices in the Hadamard equivalence class generated by tensor products of Fourier matrices
F_{n_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes F_{n_{k}}
with
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
. If the Hadamard subfactors
R_{U}\subset R
and
R_{V}\subset R
are conjugate to each other, then their intersection
R_{U}\cap R_{V}
is a factor.

We remark that in Theorem
5.2
,
[
4
]
, we have provided a complete characterization exactly when the Hadamard subfactors are conjugate.

We further compute the Jones index and the relative commutant of the intersection.

Theorem B
.

Let
U,V\in[F_{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{n_{k}}]
be distinct complex Hadamard matrices with associated Hadamard subfactors
R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
. If
R_{U}\subset R
and
R_{V}\subset R
are conjugate, then

[R:R_{U}\cap R_{V}]=\frac{N^{2}}{|H|}\,,

where
N=n_{1}\ldots n_{k}
and
H
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{n_{k}}
with

|H|=\dim\big(\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\Delta_{N})\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{V}(\Delta_{N})\big).

Here
\Delta_{N}
is the abelian subalgebra of diagonal matrices in
M_{N}(\mathbb{C})
. Moreover,

(R_{U}\cap R_{V})^{\prime}\cap R=(\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\Delta_{N})\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{V}(\Delta_{N}))^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{C}^{N}.

We also prove that every possible value of the index of the form
N^{2}/|H|
can indeed be realized by a suitable pair
(U,V)
of Hadamard matrices in the same equivalence class. This shows that although each Hadamard subfactor
R_{U}\subset R
has the same Jones index
N
, the index of their intersection varies with the specific algebraic form of
U
and
V
, revealing a delicate dependence on the underlying matrix data. We also obtain a precise characterization of exactly when the subfactors
R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R
correspond to
vertex model
subfactors, and it generalizes aspects of our earlier work in low dimensions
[
1
,
3
]
. Since vertex models are connected to fusion categories, quantum groups, and statistical mechanics, identifying when a subfactor arises from such a model provides valuable information for classification and theoretical understanding.

Finally, we analyse the
Connes–Størmer relative entropy
associated with these intersections and obtain explicit bounds and formulae that quantify their mutual position within
R
. In particular, we derive an exact expression for the modified relative entropy in terms of the entries of
U^{*}V
, demonstrating once again how the interplay between matrix data and operator-algebraic structure governs the behaviour of the subfactors.

2
Preliminaries

We begin with briefly discussing two special classes of subfactors, known as the
Hadamard/spin model
and the
vertex model
subfactors. Their importance has been emphasized by Jones
[
15
]
. We recall them briefly here, and interested readers can see
[
12
]
for details, or Section
2
in
[
4
]
for a concise overview.

Throughout the article, we shall use the shorthand notation
M_{n}
to denote the set of all
n\times n
complex matrices, and
\Delta_{n}\subset M_{n}
denotes the unital subalgebra of the diagonal matrices in
M_{n}
. We always use the standard embedding of
M_{n}
into
M_{k}\otimes M_{n}=M_{kn}
by the map

x\mapsto I_{k}\otimes x\quad\text{for all }x\in M_{n}.

Definition 2.1
.

A complex Hadamard matrix is a unitary matrix in
M_{n}
such that each entry is of modulus
1/\sqrt{n}
.

Let
F_{n}
denote the discrete Fourier matrix
({\omega^{ij}}/{\sqrt{n}})_{i,j=0,1,...,n-1}
of order
n
, where
\omega=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n}
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Two complex Hadamard matrices
H_{1},H_{2}\in M_{n}
are said to be
Hadamard equivalent
, denoted by
H_{1}\simeq H_{2}
, if there exist diagonal unitary matrices
D_{1},D_{2}\in\Delta_{n}
and permutation matrices
P_{1},P_{2}\in M_{n}
such that

H_{2}=D_{1}P_{1}H_{1}P_{2}D_{2}.

(2.1)

This implements an equivalence relation on the set of all complex Hadamard matrices of order
n
. The
Hadamard equivalence class
of a complex Hadamard matrix
u
is denoted by
[U]
. It is known that for
n=2,3,5
, all the complex Hadamard matrices are Hadamard equivalent to the Fourier matrix
F_{n}
, whereas for
n=4
, there exists a one-parameter continuous family of complex Hadamard matrices up to equivalence. Note that for
n\geq 6
, a complete classification of complex Hadamard matrices up to Hadamard equivalence remains open in the literature.

Complex Hadamard matrices are a rich source of hyperfinite subfactors, or more specifically, Hadamard/spin model subfactors. Any complex Hadamard matrix
U\in M_{n}
induces the following

\begin{matrix}\Delta_{n}&\subset&M_{n}\cr\cup&\ &\cup\cr\mathbb{C}&\subset&\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\Delta_{n})\end{matrix}

non-degenerate commuting square (
[
18
,
12
]
). Iterating Jones’ basic construction on this non-degenerate commuting square horizontally, a subfactor
R_{U}\subset R
is obtained, which is called the Hadamard subfactor. This subfactor is always irreducible, and the Jones index equals
n
. For more details, see
[
12
]
. Note that if
U
lies in the Hadamard equivalence class of the Fourier matrix
F_{n}
, the subfactor
R_{U}\subset R
is a crossed product by an outer action of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
on
R
.

Now, let
U
and
V
be two distinct complex Hadamard matrices of the same order, and consider the corresponding Hadamard subfactors
R_{U}\subset R
and
R_{V}\subset R
. Although
U\neq V
, the associated subfactors may coincide, that is,
R_{U}=R_{V}
, and we obtain a single Hadamard subfactor. To this end, we have a characterization result obtained in
[
1
]
. Given
U\neq V
, we write
U\sim V
, if there exists a permutation matrix
P\in M_{n}
and a diagonal unitary matrix
D\in\Delta_{n}
such that
V=UPD
. This implements an equivalence relation ’
\sim
’ on the set of all complex Hadamard matrices of order
n
that is finer than the Hadamard equivalence relation ’
\simeq
’ described in
Equation

2.1
. We have the following result.

Theorem 2.2

(Theorem
4.7
,
[
1
]
))
.

Let
U\neq V
be complex Hadamard matrices of order
n
. Then,
R_{U}=R_{V}
if and only if
U\sim V
. Therefore, if
U
and
V
are Hadamard inequivalent, that is,
U\not\simeq V
, then the corresponding Hadamard subfactors
R_{U}\subset R
and
R_{V}\subset R
are always distinct.

Another important class of hyperfinite subfactors is the so called
vertex model
subfactors.

Definition 2.3

(
[
12
,
5
]
)
.

A unitary matrix
U=(U^{\beta b}_{\alpha a})\in M_{n}\otimes M_{k}
is said to be a biunitary matrix if its block-transpose
W=(W^{\beta b}_{\alpha a})
, defined by
W^{\beta b}_{\alpha a}:=U^{\alpha b}_{\beta a}
, is also a unitary matrix in
M_{n}\otimes M_{k}
.

Let
W
be a unitary matrix in
M_{nk}
. Consider the following quadruple of finite-dimensional algebras:

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{C}\otimes M_{k}&\subset&M_{n}\otimes M_{k}\\
\cup&&\cup\\
\mathbb{C}&\subset&\mathrm{Ad}_{W}(M_{n}\otimes\mathbb{C})\end{matrix}

This forms a non-degenerate commuting square if and only if
W
is a biunitary matrix
[
12
]
. Iterating Jones’ basic construction horizontally, we obtain a subfactor
R_{W}\subset R
with index
[R:R_{W}]=k^{2}
. This class of subfactors is called the vertex model subfactors. In contrast to the Hadamard subfactors, vertex model subfactors need not be irreducible, but they are always of integer index.

3
Vertex model subfactors from complex Hadamard matrices

It is a natural question whether there is any crucial relation between the Hadamard and the vertex model subfactors. In
[
4
]
, the authors investigated this question and obtained infinitely many vertex model subfactors (in fact, a nested chain of such subfactors) arising from a fixed
pair
of complex Hadamard matrices of the same order. All these vertex model subfactors have the Hadamard subfactor as an intermediate. We recall a few essential ingredients and results from that paper that we shall use here.

Let
U=(U_{ij})
be a complex Hadamard matrix of order
n
and consider the diagonal unitary matrix
D_{U}:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{U_{ij}}(E_{ii}\otimes E_{jj})
in
M_{n}\otimes M_{n}=M_{n^{2}}
.
For any complex Hadamard matrix
w\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
, consider the following finite-dimensional unital subalgebra

A:=\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(W\Delta_{n}W^{*})\subset\Delta_{n}\otimes M_{n}

where
U_{1}:=UD_{U}
. Denote the unique normalised tracial state on
M_{n}\otimes M_{n}
by
tr
. Let
E_{1},E_{2}
denote the unique trace preserving conditional expectations from
\Delta_{n}\otimes M_{n}
onto
A
and
M_{n}
respectively. Consider the following quadruple

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\quad\begin{matrix}M_{n}&\subset^{\,E_{2}}&\Delta_{n}\otimes M_{n}\\
\,\cup^{\,tr}&&\,\,\cup^{\,E_{1}}\\
\mathbb{C}&\subset^{\,tr}&A\end{matrix}

of finite-dimensional unital algebras. Then,
\Gamma
is a nondegenerate commuting square (see Lemma 3.6 of
[
4
]
in this regard), that is,
E_{1}E_{2}=E_{2}E_{1}
and
A\cap M_{n}=\mathbb{C}
. Iterating Jones’ basic construction on
\Gamma
, we obtain the following grid

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}M_{n}&\subset&\Delta_{n}\otimes M_{n}&\subset^{e_{1}\otimes I_{n}}&M_{n}\otimes M_{n}&\subset^{e_{2}\otimes I_{n}}&\cdots&\subset R\\
\cup&&\cup&&\cup&\,\,\,\,\,&&\cup\\
\mathbb{C}&\subset&A&\subset&\langle A,e_{1}\otimes I_{n}\rangle&\subset&\cdots&\subset R^{u,w}\end{matrix}

(3.1)

of algebras, where
e_{1}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}\in M_{n}
,
e_{2}=\sum_{i}E_{ii}\otimes E_{ii}\in\Delta_{n}\otimes M_{n}
etc. are the Jones’ projections. The unital subalgebra

\displaystyle R^{U,W}:=\overline{\bigvee\{A,\,e_{1}\otimes I^{(k)}_{n},e_{2}\otimes I^{(k)}_{n}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{sot}\subset R

(3.2)

is a subfactor of the hyperfinite type
{II}_{1}
factor
R
.
We recall the following fundamental result from
[
4
]
.
In that work, the notation
R^{U,W}_{0}
was used instead of
R^{U,W}
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
4
]
, Theorem 4.1)
.

The inclusion
R^{U,W}\subset R
is a vertex model subfactor of index
n^{2}
. Moreover, the Hadamard subfactor
R_{U}\subset R
is an intermediate subfactor, i.e.,

R^{U,W}\subset R_{U}\subset R.

Now, let
W
be any complex Hadamard matrix, and
U
be a complex Hadamard matrix in the Hadamard equivalence class
[W]
. By
Equation

2.1
, we write
u=DPW\widetilde{P}\widetilde{D}
for some diagonal unitary matrices
D,\widetilde{D}\in\Delta_{n}
and permutation matrices
P,\widetilde{P}\in M_{n}
. Further, applying
2.2
, we can discard
\widetilde{D},\widetilde{P}
and assume that
U=DPW
. We recall the following result.

Proposition 3.2

(Theorem
5.2
,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
U,V\in[W]
be Hadamard equivalent complex Hadamard matrices and
R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
be the corresponding Hadamard subfactors. Assume that
U=D_{1}P_{1}W
and
V=D_{2}P_{2}W
. For any arbitrary complex Hadamard matrix
W
of the same order as
U
and
V
, there is an automorphism
\theta\in\mathrm{Aut}(R)
such that
\theta(R_{U})=R_{V}
and the subfactors
R^{U,W}\subset R\mbox{ and }R^{V,W}\subset R
are isomorphic. Moreover,

(i)

if
P_{1}\neq P_{2}
, then
\theta\in\mathrm{Out}(R)
;

(ii)

if
P_{1}=P_{2}
, then
\theta=\mathrm{Ad}_{D_{2}D_{1}^{*}}\in\mathrm{Inn}(R)
.

Condition
(ii)
above precisely says that the subfactors
R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
are conjugate to each other when
P_{1}=P_{2}
, and vice-versa. Note that Theorem
5.2
in
[
4
]
is a much stronger result; however, we only require the above in this paper. In fact, it is possible to construct the automorphism
\theta
explicitly. To see this, we adopt the notation
M_{n}^{(k)}
for the
k
-fold tensor product of
M_{n}
with itself, and write
A^{(k)}
for
A^{\otimes k}
when
A\in M_{n}
. Let
D_{1},D_{2}\in\Delta_{n}
be diagonal unitary matrices and
P_{1},P_{2}\in M_{n}
be permutation matrices as defined in
3.2
, and set
P:=P_{2}P_{1}^{*}
. Then, the sequence
\{\mathrm{Ad}_{P^{(k)}}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Inn}(R)
converges pointwise to an automorphism
\widetilde{\theta}\in\operatorname{Aut}(R)
in the strong operator topolgy (see Proposition
5.4
,
[
4
]
). The automorphism
\theta
is then given by the composition
\mathrm{Ad}_{D_{2}D_{1}^{*}}\circ\widetilde{\theta}
. Note that if
P_{1}=P_{2}
, then
\widetilde{\theta}=id_{R}
, and consequently,
\theta=\mathrm{Ad}_{D_{2}D_{1}^{*}}
.

for all

, and then
(\text{Ad}_{DP}(\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}))^{*}y\in R\cap R_{U}^{{}^{\prime}}
. Since
R_{U}\subset R
is irreducible, it follows that

for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that

\mathrm{Ad}_{DP}(\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}})\in R_{U}\cap\Delta_{N}

\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}\not\in\mathbb{C}
,
\vec{r}\neq 0\in G

\theta_{\vec{r}}\in\operatorname{Out}(R_{U})
for all
\vec{r}\neq 0\in G

and
\{\text{Ad}_{DP}(\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}):\vec{r}\in G\}

\Box

Suppose that
M
be an type
II_{1}
factor and
\theta:G\to\mathrm{Aut}(M)
be an outer action of a finite group

\theta_{g}\in\mathrm{Out}(M)

Recall,
M\subset M\rtimes_{\theta}G
is irreducible, and so is

. Moreover,
(M^{G})^{\prime}\cap(M\rtimes_{\theta}G)=\mathbb{C}G
, where

.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\theta:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(M)
be an outer action of a finite group
G
on the type
II_{1}
factor

. Then, for any unitary element

is always a type

[M:\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M)\cap M]
is finite and equal to the order of some subgroup

.

Proof
:
Let
u\in\mathbb{C}G\subset M\rtimes G
be a unitary element. Observe that,
u
normalizes the fixed-point algebra

, that is
\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M^{G})=M^{G}
. In particular, this implies
M^{G}=\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M^{G})\subset\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M)
. Therefore, we get the following

M^{G}\subset\,M\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M)\,\subset M.

Since the fixed-point subalgebra
M^{G}\subset M
is irreducible, it immediately follows that the intersection
M\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M)
is a subfactor of
M
, and
M\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M)=M^{H}
for some subgroup
H
of
G
. Hence, we have the index
[M:\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(M)\cap M]=|H|
.

\Box

4.2
Index and relative commutant

Now that we know that
R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R
is always a subfactor when
R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
are conjugate to each other and
U,V\in[W]
, it is natural to investigate the index
[R:R_{U}\cap R_{V}]
and the relative commutant
(R_{U}\cap R_{V})^{\prime}\cap R
. First we establish a few technical results.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\vec{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
be a fixed vector. Consider the complex Hadamard matrix
V_{k}=F_{n_{1}}\otimes F_{n_{2}}\otimes F_{n_{k}}
of order
N_{k}=n_{1}\ldots n_{k}
, where
F_{n_{j}}
’s are the Fourier matrices of order
n_{j}
. Then, we have
(V_{k})_{1}=P_{k}W
, where
P_{k}
is a block-diagonal permutation matrix of order
N_{k}^{2}
and
(V_{k})_{1}=V_{k}D_{V_{k}}
(see
4.1
).

Proof
:
For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by definition, we have
(F_{n})_{1}=F_{n}D_{F_{n}}
(see
4.1
) and by using
Equation

4.3
, we get
F_{n}D_{F_{n}}=PF_{n}
where
P:=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{1}}E_{ii}\otimes\sigma_{n,n-i+1}
is a permutation matrix (see
4.1
). Therefore, it follows that
(F_{n})_{1}=PF_{n}
; this concludes the base step.

Assume that the result holds for
(t-1)-
th step. That is,
(V_{t-1})_{1}=V_{t-1}D_{V_{t-1}}=P_{t-1}V_{t-1}
for some block-diagonal permutation matrix
P_{t-1}
of order
N_{t-1}=n_{1}\cdots n_{t-1}
. From the definition of
V_{t}
and
D_{V_{t}}
(see
4.1
) we have

V_{t}=V_{t-1}\otimes F_{n_{t}},\quad\text{and}\quad D_{V_{t}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}E_{ii}\otimes D_{V_{t-1}}\otimes\mathscr{D}_{n_{t},i}^{*}.

Therefore, we get the following

\displaystyle(V_{t})_{1}

\displaystyle=V_{t}D_{V_{t}}

\displaystyle=(V_{t-1}\otimes F_{n_{t}})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}E_{ii}\otimes D_{V_{t-1}}\otimes\mathscr{D}_{n_{t},i}^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}E_{ii}\otimes V_{t-1}D_{V_{t-1}}\otimes F_{n_{t}}\mathscr{D}_{n_{t},i}^{*}\quad\text{(by induction hypothesis and \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{second})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}E_{ii}\otimes P_{t-1}V_{t-1}\otimes\sigma_{n_{t},i}^{*}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}E_{ii}\otimes P_{t-1}\otimes\sigma_{n_{t},i}^{*}\right)(V_{t-1}\otimes F_{n_{t}})

\displaystyle=P_{t}V_{t},

where
P_{t}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{t}}E_{ii}\otimes P_{t-1}\otimes\sigma_{n_{t},i}^{*}
is a permutation matrix of order
N_{t}
. This completes the proof.

\Box

Proposition 4.6
.

Consider the Hadamard subfactor
R_{U}\subset R
corresponding to the complex Hadamard matrix
U
of order
N
and
W=F_{n_{1}}\otimes F_{n_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{n_{k}}
where
n_{1}\ldots n_{k}=N
. Obtain the vertex model subfactor
R^{U,W}\subset R
described in
Section

3
. Then,
(R^{U,W})^{\prime}\cap R_{U}=\mathbb{C}
and
(R^{U,W})^{\prime}\cap R=\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

Proof
:
Recall that the vertex model subfactor
R^{W,W}\subset R
is obtained by iterating Jones’ basic construction on the following non-degenerate commuting square

\Gamma:=\quad\begin{matrix}M_{N}&\subset&\Delta_{N}\otimes M_{N}\\
\cup&&\cup\\
\mathbb{C}&\subset&\mathrm{Ad}_{W_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*})\end{matrix}

as described in
Equation

3.1
. By the Ocneanu compactness theorem (see Theorem 5.7.1,
[
12
]
), it follows that the relative commutant is given by

(R^{W,W})^{\prime}\cap R=\left(\mathrm{Ad}_{W_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*})\right)^{\prime}\cap M_{N},

(4.7)

where
W_{1}=WD_{W}\in\Delta_{N}\otimes M_{N}
(see
4.1
).

By
4.5
, we know that
W_{1}:=WD_{W}=PW
for some block-diagonal permutation matrix
P\in\Delta_{N}\otimes M_{N}
. Since
\mathrm{Ad}_{F_{n}}(\Delta_{n})=\mathrm{Ad}_{F^{*}_{n}}(\Delta_{n})
for all

, it follows that
\mathrm{Ad}_{W}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*})=\Delta_{N}
. Therefore, we get the following

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{W_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}_{PW}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}_{P}\circ\mathrm{Ad}_{W}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ad}_{P}(\Delta_{N}).

Substituting this into
Equation

4.7
, and using the fact
P
is a block-diagonal permutation matrix of order
N^{2}
, we obtain

(R^{W,W})^{\prime}\cap R=\left(\mathrm{Ad}_{P}(\Delta_{N})\right)^{\prime}\cap M_{N}=\mathbb{C}^{N}.

(4.8)

Further, by the construction of Hadamard subfactors, we know that

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\Delta_{N}&\subset&R\\
\cup&&\cup\\
\mathbb{C}&\subset&R_{W}\end{matrix}

is a non-degenerate commuting square. Therefore, it follows that
R_{W}\cap\Delta_{N}=\mathbb{C}
. Hence, from
Equation

4.8
, we get that
(R^{W,W})^{\prime}\cap R_{W}=R_{W}\cap\mathbb{C}^{N}=\mathbb{C}
.

We have the Hadamard subfactor
R_{U}\subset R
associated to the complex Hadamard matrix
U\in[W]
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
U=DPW
due to
2.2
, for some diagonal unitary matrix
D
and permutation matrix
P
, each of order
N=n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}
. By
3.2
, we know that the following chain of subfactors

R^{W,W}\subset R_{W}\subset R\quad\mbox{and}\quad R^{U,W}\subset R_{U}\subset R

are isomorphic. Therefore, by
Equation

4.8
and the fact that
(R^{W,W})^{\prime}\cap R_{W}=\mathbb{C}
, we conclude that

(R^{U,W})^{\prime}\cap R=\mathbb{C}^{N}

(4.9)

and
(R^{U,W})^{\prime}\cap R_{U}=\mathbb{C}
.

\Box

Proposition 4.7
.

Let

be the complex Hadamard matrix

and

be the Hadamard subfactor corresponding to the complex Hadamard matrix

. Then,

R_{U}=R^{U,W}\rtimes_{\theta^{\prime}}G

Proof
:
By
Equation

3.2
, we have the following vertex model subfactor

\displaystyle R^{U,W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\bigvee\{\mathrm{Ad}_{U_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*}),\,e_{2}\otimes I^{(k)}_{N},\,e_{1}\otimes I^{(k)}_{N}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}}^{sot}\subset R

(4.10)

We claim that for

\displaystyle\theta^{{}^{\prime}}_{\vec{r}}(\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Ad}_{U_{1}\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}U_{1}^{*}}(\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*}))\hfill{\qquad(\mbox{since }U_{1}=(I_{n}\otimes U)D_{U})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Ad}_{U_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}})U^{*}_{1}}(\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(F_{n}\Delta_{n}F^{*}_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}W\Delta_{N}W^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}})\hfill{(\text{by \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{second}})}

\displaystyle\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(W\sigma_{\vec{r}}\Delta_{N}\sigma^{*}_{\vec{r}}W^{*})

\displaystyle\text{Ad}_{U_{1}}(W\Delta_{N}W^{*}).

Since for

(4.11)

Hence, by the continuity of

4.10

By the construction of the Hadamard subfactor

\begin{matrix}M_{N}&\subset&R\\
\cup&&\cup\\
U\Delta_{N}U^{*}&\subset&R_{U},\end{matrix}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\begin{matrix}M_{N}&\subset&R\\
\cup&&\cup\\

3.1

(4.12)

\theta^{\prime}
is an outer action of the group
G

\theta^{\prime}_{\vec{r}}\in\text{Out}(R^{U,W})
for all
\vec{r}\neq\vec{0}\in G
, where
\vec{0}
is the identity element of

. Consider any

Proposition 4.8

For every subgroup

Proof
:
We begin by establishing the following :

Claim:
For any divisor
k
of

, there exists a pair of complex Hadamard matrices

\mathrm{dim}(\mathrm{Ad}_{u}\Delta_{n})\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{v}(\Delta_{n}))=k

Let

is not a scalar multiple of diagonal unitary matrices

4.1

4.3

\mathrm{Ad}_{v}(\mathscr{D}_{n,i})=\beta_{i}\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(\mathscr{D}_{n,i})

u\Delta_{n}u^{*}\cap v\Delta_{n}v^{*}=\big\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_{i}\mathrm{Ad}_{u}(\mathscr{D}_{n,il})\;:\;r_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\big\}.

Hence,

Now, let
H
be a subgroup of the finite abelian group

. Then
H=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}

divides

. From the claim above, for each

of order

such that

\dim\big(\mathrm{Ad}_{F_{n_{i}}}(\Delta_{n_{i}})\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{D_{i}F_{n_{i}}}(\Delta_{n_{i}})\big)=m_{i}.

(4.18)

Let
U=W=F_{n_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes F_{n_{k}}
and define
D=D_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes D_{k}
so that
V=DU
is also a complex Hadamard matrix of order
N=n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}
. Using
Equation

4.18
, we conclude the following :

\dim(U\Delta_{N}U^{*}\cap V\Delta_{N}V^{*})=m_{1}m_{2}\cdots m_{k}=|H|.

Finally, from Theorem C, it follows that

[R:R_{U}\cap R_{V}]=N^{2}/|H|,

which completes the proof.

\Box

4.3
Intersection as a vertex model subfactor

Recall the construction of the
vertex model
subfactors from
Section

2
, and the abelian unital subalgebra
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}
of
M_{N}
from
Equation

4.13
.

Theorem 4.9
.

The intersection
R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R
is a vertex model subfactor if and only if
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proof
:
By
Equation

4.17
, we have
R_{U}\cap R_{V}=R^{U,W}\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}
. If
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}=\mathbb{C}
, then clearly
R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R
is a vertex model subfactor. For the converse direction, we show that whenever
\mathrm{dim}(\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V})>1,\,R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R
is not a vertex model subfactor. Recall from
Section

2
that a vertex model subfactor
R_{Z}\subset R
, where
Z\in M_{N}\otimes M_{N}
is a bi-unitary matrix, is constructed by iterating the basic construction on the following

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{C}\otimes M_{N}&\subset&M_{N}\otimes M_{N}\\
\cup&&\cup\\
\mathbb{C}&\subset&\mathrm{Ad}_{Z}(M_{N}\otimes\mathbb{C})\end{matrix}

non-degenerate commuting square. Therefore,

R_{Z}\cap M_{N}=\mathbb{C}.

(4.19)

However, from the definition of
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}
, we have
\mathbb{C}\subsetneq\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}\subseteq M_{N}
and
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}\subseteq R_{U}\cap R_{V}
. Therefore,
(R_{U}\cap R_{V})\cap M_{N}\neq\mathbb{C}
. Hence, the intersection fails to satisfy
Equation

4.19
. Thus,
R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R
is not a vertex model subfactor.

\Box

The above result generalizes our earlier works in
[
1
,
3
]
, where the orders of the complex Hadamard matrices considered were small.

5
Application to the Connes-Størmer relative entropy

In this final section, we analyse the relative position of two conjugate Hadamard subfactors through an invariant. The
Connes–Størmer relative entropy
provides a quantitative measure of the mutual position of two subalgebras of a finite von Neumann algebra. For inclusions of the form
R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
, this entropy captures how far the two subfactors are from being simultaneously contained in a common intermediate subfactor.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a fixed faithful normal trace. For finite-dimensional subalgebras
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{M}
, Connes & Størmer introduced the notion of relative entropy
H(\mathcal{P|Q})
between
\mathcal{P}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{Q}
. In
[
17
]
, Pimsner & Popa observed that the definition of
H(\mathcal{P|Q})
works as it is for arbitrary von Neumann subalgebras
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathcal{M}
. For more on entropy, see
[
16
]
.

In our situation, we have a pair of subfactors
R_{U},R_{V}\subset R
, and in the present subsection, we determine legitimate lower and upper bounds for the Connes–Størmer relative entropy
H(R_{U}|R_{V})
. Note that the quadruple
(R_{U}\cap R_{V}\subset R_{U},R_{V}\subset R)
often fails to become a commuting square. Therefore, our situation is a bit more complicated since results from
[
20
]
is not applicable here.

Definition 5.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{P,Q}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be von Neumann subalgebras of the finite von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Let
S
denotes the set of finite families
\gamma=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})
of positive elements in
\mathcal{M}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1
. Then,

H_{\gamma}(\mathcal{P|Q}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau\circ\eta\,E_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{M}}(x_{j})-\tau\circ\eta\,E_{\mathcal{P}}^{\mathcal{M}}(x_{j})\right)

where
\eta:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the continuous function
t\mapsto-t\log t
. The Connes-Størmer relative entropy between
\mathcal{P}\mbox{ and }\mathcal{Q}
is defined as
H(\mathcal{P|Q}):=\sup_{\gamma\in S}\,H_{\gamma}(\mathcal{P|Q})
.

It is known that finding the exact value of the relative entropy is often very hard. In
[
2
]
, the authors have calculated the exact value of the relative entropy for a very special instance. In the literature, a modified version of the Connes-Størmer relative entropy exists due to Choda
[
8
]
(see also
[
7
]
), which is less complicated to compute. This is defined as
h(\mathcal{P}|\mathcal{Q}):=\sup_{\gamma\in S}\,h_{\gamma}(\mathcal{P|Q})
, where

h_{\gamma}(\mathcal{P|Q}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\tau\circ\eta\,E_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\mathcal{M}}(x_{j})-\tau\circ\eta\,(x_{j})\right).

We recall the following result from
[
1
]
.

Proposition 5.2

(Proposition
2.6
,
[
1
]
)
.

(i)

Let
\{M_{n}\},\{A_{n}\}
and
\{B_{n}\}
be increasing sequences of von Neumann subalgebras of a finite von Neumann algebra
M
such that
\{A_{n}\},\{B_{n}\}\subset M
and
M=\big(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}M_{n}\big)^{\dprime}.
If
A=\big(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\big)^{\dprime}
and
B=\big(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\big)^{\dprime}
, then
H(B|A)\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}H(B_{n}|A_{n}).

(ii)

If in addition,
E_{A_{n+1}}E_{M_{n}}=E_{A_{n}}
and
E_{B_{n+1}}E_{M_{n}}=E_{B_{n}}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
H(B|A)=\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}H(B_{n}|A_{n})
increasingly.

Note that a similar statement also holds for ‘
h
’ in place of ‘
H
’. Our main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
U,V\in[F_{n_{1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes F_{n_{k}}]
be distinct complex Hadamard matrices and consider the corresponding Hadamard subfactors
R_{U}\subset R
and
R_{V}\subset R
. If the Hadamard subfactors are conjugate, then we have the following :

(i)

The modified Connes-Størmer entropy is given by

h(R_{U}|R_{V})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\eta|(U^{*}V)_{ij}|^{2},

where
(U^{*}V)_{ij}
denotes the
ij-
th entry of the matrix
U^{*}V
.

(ii)

One has the following :

h(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq H(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq\log\big(N/\mathrm{dim}(\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V})\big)

where
\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V}=\mathrm{Ad}_{U}(\Delta_{N})\cap\mathrm{Ad}_{V}(\Delta_{N})
as defined in
Equation

4.13
.

Proof
:
Since
R_{U}\subset R
and
R_{V}\subset R
are conjugate subfactors by applying
3.2
, we assume that
R_{U}=\mathrm{Ad}_{D}(R_{PW})
and
R_{V}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\widetilde{D}}(R_{PW})
, where
R_{PW}\subset R
denotes the Hadamard subfactor associated with the complex Hadamard matrix
PW
and
D,\widetilde{D}
are diagonal unitary matrices. Therefore, we have the following

\displaystyle h(R_{U}\,|\,R_{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\big(\mathrm{Ad}_{D}(R_{PW})\,\big|\,\mathrm{Ad}_{\widetilde{D}}(R_{PW})\big)

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\big(R_{PW}\,\big|\,\mathrm{Ad}_{D^{*}\widetilde{D}}(R_{PW})\big).

From
4.3
, it is known that
R=R_{PW}\rtimes^{\theta}G
, where the outer action
\theta
is given by
\theta_{\vec{r}}=\mathrm{Ad}_{\left(P\,\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}\,P^{*}\right)}
for all
\vec{r}\in G
. Since
\{\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}:\vec{r}\in G\}
forms a basis for the diagonal subalgebra
\Delta_{N}\subset M_{N}
, where
N=n_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{k}
, a diagonal unitary matrix
D^{*}\widetilde{D}
can be written as

D^{*}\widetilde{D}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\tau\big(D^{*}\widetilde{D}\,P^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}}P\big)\,P^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}P.

and therefore, applying Theorem
3.14
of
[
8
]
, from
Equation

5.1
we obtain the following

\displaystyle h(R_{U}\,|\,R_{V})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\big(R_{PW}\,\big|\,\mathrm{Ad}_{D^{*}\widetilde{D}}(R_{PW})\big)

(5.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{r}\in G}\eta\big(\tau\big(D^{*}\widetilde{D}\,P^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}P\big)\;\tau\big(D^{*}\widetilde{D}\,P^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}}P\big)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{r}\in G}\eta\,\big|\tau\big(D^{*}\widetilde{D}\,P^{*}\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}P\big)\big|^{2}.

By Corollary
3
of
[
7
]
, we have the following

\displaystyle h(U\Delta_{N}U^{*}\,\big|\,V\Delta_{N}V^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\big(\Delta_{N}\,\big|\,U^{*}V\Delta_{N}V^{*}U\big)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\eta\big(\big|(U^{*}V)_{ij}\big|^{2}\big).

Now to compute the
(i,j)
-th entry of
U^{*}V
, observe that

\displaystyle U^{*}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{W^{*}}\big(PD^{*}\widetilde{D}P\big)

(5.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{W^{*}}\Big(\sum_{\vec{r}\in G}\tau\big(PD^{*}\widetilde{D}P^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}}\big)\,\mathscr{D}_{\vec{r}}\Big)\hfill{\qquad(\text{apply \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lemma1}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{r}\in G}\tau\big(PD^{*}\widetilde{D}P^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}}\big)\,\sigma_{\vec{n}-\vec{r}}

Since each
\sigma_{\vec{n}-\vec{r}}
is a permutation matrix of order
N
(see
4.1
), it follows from
Equation

5.4
that for every
\vec{r}\in G
, the scalar
\tau(PD^{*}\widetilde{D}P^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}})
occurs exactly
N
-times among the entries of the unitary matrix
U^{*}V
. Moreover, from the definition of the permutation matrices
\{\sigma_{\vec{r}}:\vec{r}\in G\}
, we conclude that for each
\vec{r}\in G
that there exists unique
j\in\{1,2,..,N\}
such that
(U^{*}V)_{1j}=\tau\big(PD^{*}\widetilde{D}P^{*}\mathscr{D}^{*}_{\vec{r}}\big)
. Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\eta|(U^{*}V)_{1j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{r}\in G}\eta|\tau(D^{*}\widetilde{D})|^{2}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\eta|(U^{*}V)_{ij}|^{2}

By
5.2
, we know that
h(U\Delta_{N}U^{*}\big|V\Delta_{N}V^{*})\leq h(R_{U}|R_{V})
. Combining this with
Equations

5.2
and
5.5
, we deduce the following

h(R_{U}|R_{V})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\eta|(U^{*}V)_{ij}|^{2},

which establishes the first part of the theorem.

Next, by
4.6
we have
(R^{U,W})^{\prime}\cap R_{U}=\mathbb{C}
. Since
R^{U,W}\subset R_{U}\cap R_{V}
, it follows that
(R_{U}\cap R_{V})^{\prime}\cap R_{U}=\mathbb{C}
. Applying Corollary
4.6
of
[
17
]
and using Theorem C, we obtain the following :

H(R_{U}\,|\,R_{U}\cap R_{V})=\log([R_{U}:R_{U}\cap R_{V}])=\log\big(N/\dim(\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V})\big).

Moreover, by definition we have
h(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq H(R_{U}|R_{V})
, and from
[
17
]
it follows that
H(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq H(R_{U}|R_{U}\cap R_{V})
. Combining these inequalities, we finally get the following

h(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq H(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq\log\left(N/\dim(\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V})\right),

which finishes the proof.

\Box

Concluding remarks:
Note that in general,
H(R_{U}|R_{V})\leq H(R|R_{V})=\log[R:R_{V}]=\log N
. Therefore, the upper bound
\log\big(N/\mathrm{dim}(\mathscr{A}_{U}^{V})\big)
is indeed an improvement for the relative entropy. The interplay between index, relative commutant, and entropy gives a complete picture of the intersection of conjugate Hadamard subfactors. The index
[R:R_{U}\cap R_{V}]
measures the algebraic size of the intersection, the relative commutant records its internal symmetry, and the entropy
H(R_{U}|R_{V})
captures its analytic separation.
Together, these invariants reveal a rich geometric structure underlying the space of Hadamard subfactors associated with tensor products of Fourier matrices. Furthermore, the explicit formulae obtained here suggest further directions : extending the analysis to non-conjugate pairs, more general Hadamard matrices which are not necessarily from the equivalence classes of DFT-type matrices, and a pair of biunitary matrices.

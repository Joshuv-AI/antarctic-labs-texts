Twin-free K r K_{r} -saturated Graphs and Maximally Independent Sets in K 3 K_{3} -free Graphs
Asier Calbet

Abstract
We say that two vertices are twins if they have the same neighbourhood and that a graph is K r K_{r} -saturated if it does not contain K r K_{r} but adding any new edge to it creates a K r K_{r} . In 1964, Erdős, Hajnal and Moon showed that sat ⁡ ( n , K r ) = ( r − 2 ) ​ n + o ⁡ ( n ) \sat(n,K_{r})=(r-2)n+o(n) for r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 , where sat ⁡ ( n , K r ) \sat(n,K_{r}) is the minimum number of edges in a K r K_{r} -saturated graph on n n vertices, and determined the unique extremal graph. This graph has many twins, leading us to define tsat ⁡ ( n , K r ) \tsat(n,K_{r}) to be the minimum number of edges in a twin-free K r K_{r} -saturated graph on n n vertices. We show that ( 5 + 2 / 3 ) ​ n + o ⁡ ( n ) ≤ tsat ⁡ ( n , K 3 ) ≤ 6 ​ n + o ⁡ ( n ) (5+2/3)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{3})\leq 6n+o(n) and that ( r + 2 ) ​ n + o ⁡ ( n ) ≤ tsat ⁡ ( n , K r ) ≤ ( r + 3 ) ​ n + o ⁡ ( n ) \left(r+2\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{r})\leq(r+3)n+o(n) for r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 . We also consider a variant of this problem where we additionally require the graphs to have large minimum degree. Both of these problems turn out be intimately related to two other problems regarding maximally independent sets of a given size in K 3 K_{3} -free graphs and generalisations of these problems to K r K_{r} with r ≥ 4 r\geq 4 . The first problem is to maximise the number of maximally independent sets given the number of vertices and the second problem is to minimise the number of edges given the number of maximally independent sets. They are interesting in their own right.

Introduction

Given a graph
H
, we say that a graph

is
H
-saturated if it is maximally
H
-free, meaning

contains no copy of
H
but every graph
G+e
obtained by adding a new edge

to

contains

\sat(n,H)
, defined (for graphs

with at least one edge and integers

) to be the minimum number of edges in an
H
-saturated graph

on
n

and large

\ex(n,H)
, defined to be the maximum number of edges in an
H
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices. However, much less is known for the saturation problem than for the Turán problem. See
[
2
]
for a survey of the saturation problem.

For certain

the saturation number is known exactly. In particular, Erdős, Hajnal and Moon showed (Theorem 1 in
[
4
]
) that for
r\geq 2
and

,
\sat(n,K_{r})=(r-2)n-\binom{r-1}{2}
and that the unique extremal graph consists of a

fully connected to an independent set of size
n-(r-2)
(for

,
K_{n}
is the unique
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices). Note that for large

this graph contains many vertices of degree
r-2
. Moreover, this is the smallest possible degree of a vertex in a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n\geq r-1
vertices. One might therefore ask what happens if we forbid vertices of degree
r-2
, and more generally, vertices of small degree. In
[
1
]
(see the second paragraph of the introduction), this led us to define
\sat(n,K_{r},t)
for integers

,

and large enough integers

-saturated graph

on

vertices with

(more precisely, such graphs exist if and only if

). This quantity (or rather the one obtained by replacing the condition
\delta(G)\geq t
with
\delta(G)=t
) was first considered by Duffus and Hanson (second paragraph of the Introduction in
[
3

One could also note that the extremal graph has

conical vertices (vertices adjacent to all other vertices), leading one to define

to be the minimum number of edges in a

-saturated graph

to be the minimum number of edges in a twin-free

-saturated graph

.

Let

and

-saturated graph on

,

,

Note the trivial lower bound

Theorem 2
.

We have the following estimates for
\tsat(n,K_{r})
.

1.

For all integers

,

\left(r+2\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{r})\leq(r+3)n+o(n)\ .

2.

In the special case

\left(5+\frac{2}{3}\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{3})\leq 6n+o(n)\ .

The author conjectures that the upper bound is tight.

One can also combine the twin-free and minimum degree conditions. The naive approach would be to define

A blow-up of a graph

Theorem

s(v)=|\mathcal{F}_{v}|\leq s_{3,3}^{\prime}\left(|\Gamma^{\prime}(v)|\right)=m-d(v)+O(1)
, or equivalently,
d(v)+s(v)\leq m+O(1)
. It is easy to check that
x+9y\geq 16xy
for all real numbers
x,y\geq 0
with
x+y\leq 1
. Taking
x=s(v)/[m+O(1)]
and
y=d(v)/[m+O(1)]
and summing over all
v\in H
gives

\frac{4s+18e}{m+O(1)}\ \geq\ \frac{16\sum_{v\in H}d(v)s(v)}{[m+O(1)]^{2}}\ \geq\ \frac{16(m-4)s}{[m+O(1)]^{2}}\ ,

which implies
e\geq 2s/3+o(s)
.

We now prove the upper bound. Let
s
be a large integer. We need to construct a maximal
(3,5)
-system
(H,\mathcal{F})
with
|\mathcal{F}|=s
and
e(H)\leq 2s+o(s)
. Let
(H_{4,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime})
be as in property 5 in Construction
1
, where
l
is the smallest integer with
|\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime}|\geq s
. Then
l=\left[2^{1/4}+o(1)\right]s^{1/4}
by property 4 in Construction
1
. Since
(H_{4,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime})
is a maximal
(3,5)
-system, for every missing edge
e
in
H_{4,l}^{\prime}
such that
H_{4,l}^{\prime}+e
is
K_{3}
-free there is a set in
\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime}
containing
e
. Pick a subfamily
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime}
with
\left|\mathcal{F}\right|=s
such that for every missing edge
e
in
H_{4,l}^{\prime}
such that
H_{4,l}^{\prime}+e
is
K_{3}
-free there is a set in
\mathcal{F}
containing
e
. This is possible since
s\leq|\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime}|
and the number of missing edges
e
in
H_{4,l}^{\prime}
such that
H_{4,l}^{\prime}+e
is
K_{3}
-free is
O(s^{3/4})
by property 5 in Construction
1
, which is at most
s
, since
s
is large. Then it is easy to see that, since
(H_{4,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{4,l}^{\prime})
is a maximal
(3,5)
-system and for every missing edge
e
in
H_{4,l}^{\prime}
such that
H_{4,l}^{\prime}+e
is
K_{3}
-free there is a set in
\mathcal{F}
containing
e
,
(H_{4,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})
is also a maximal
(3,5)
-system. Finally,
e(H_{4,l}^{\prime})=l^{4}-2l^{3}+2l^{2}=2s+o(s)
by property 3 in Construction
1
.

∎

We will use the following lemma in the proof of Theorem
8
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r-2
be integers and
(H,\mathcal{F})
be a maximal
(r,t)
-system with one of the following properties.

1.

There is a vertex
v\in H
with
d(v)+s(v)\leq t
.

2.

There are vertices
v\neq w\in H
with
s(v)=0=s(w)
and
\Gamma(v)=\Gamma(w)
.

Then there exists a maximal
(r,t)
-system
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
with
|H^{\prime}|=|H|-1
,
e(H^{\prime})\leq e(H)+O(1)
and
|\mathcal{F}|-O(1)\leq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{F}|
.

Proof.

Suppose first that property 1 holds. Let
H^{\prime\prime}=H\setminus\{v\}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{S\in\mathcal{F}:v\not\in S\}
. Then it is easy to see that
(H^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is an
(r,t)
-system, so we can add edges to
H^{\prime\prime}
to obtain a graph
H^{\prime}
such that
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is a maximal
(r,t)
-system. Note that
|H^{\prime}|=|H|-1
and
|\mathcal{F}|-t\leq|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{F}|
. It remains to show that
e(H^{\prime})\leq e(H)+O(1)
. Clearly,
e(H^{\prime\prime})\leq e(H)
, so it suffices to show that only
O(1)
edges were added to
H^{\prime\prime}
when obtaining
H^{\prime}
.

Let
e
be such an added edge. Then, since
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
(r,t)
-system, either
H+e
contains
K_{r}
or
(H+e)[S]
contains
K_{r-1}
for some
S\in\mathcal{F}
. But
H
and
H^{\prime}
are
K_{r}
-free and
H[S]
and
H^{\prime}[T]
are
K_{r-1}
-free for all
S\in\mathcal{F}
and
T\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, since
(H,\mathcal{F})
and
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
are
(r,t)
-systems, so either
v
and
e
belong to a copy of
K_{r}
in
H+e
or
e
belongs to a copy of
K_{r-1}
in
(H+e)[S]
for some
S\in\mathcal{F}
with
v\in S
. In particular, either
e\subseteq\Gamma(v)
or
e\subseteq S
for some
S\in\mathcal{F}
with
v\in S
. It follows that there are at most
(t+1)\binom{t}{2}
such added edges
e
.

Now suppose property 2 holds. Let
H^{\prime}=H\setminus\{v\}
. Then it is easy to check that
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
(r,t)
-system with
|H^{\prime}|=|H|-1
and
e(H^{\prime})\leq e(H)
.

We now prove Theorem
8
, which we will use in the proofs of parts 1 and 2 of Theorem
3
.

Theorem
8
.

For all integers
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r+3
,

tn+\Omega\left(e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]\right)\leq\tsat(n,K_{r},t)\leq tn+O\left(e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]\right)\ .

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound. The proof will be similar to that of Theorem 2 in
[
1
]
. Let
G
be a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq t
in which every pair of twins is adjacent to a vertex of degree
t
. We need to show that
e(G)\geq tn+\Omega\left(e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]\right)
.

By Lemma 4 in
[
1
]
, there is a set
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|=O(1)
such that for all
v\in G
, either
|\Gamma(w)\cap S|>t
for all
w\in\Gamma(v)\setminus S
or

|\Gamma(v)\cap S|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus S|>t\ .

Let
T=S\cup\{v\in G:|\Gamma(v)\cap S|>t\}
. Then

e(T)\ \geq\ e(S,T\setminus S)\ \geq\ (t+1)|T\setminus S|\ =\ (t+1)|T|+O(1)

by the definition of
T
(where
e(T)=e(G[T])
and
e(S,T\setminus S)
is the number of edges between
S
and
T\setminus S
).

We now claim that for all
v\in G
we have

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|\geq t\ ,

with equality if and only if
\Gamma(v)\subseteq T
and
d(v)=t
. Indeed, if
|\Gamma(w)\cap S|>t
for all
w\in\Gamma(v)\setminus S
, then
\Gamma(v)\subseteq T
, so

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|=d(v)\geq t\ ,

since
\delta(G)\geq t
, and if

|\Gamma(v)\cap S|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus S|>t\ ,

then

|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}|\Gamma(v)\setminus T|>t\ ,

since
S\subseteq T
.

Let
R=\{v\in G:\Gamma(v)\subseteq T,d(v)=t\}
. Note that
R\setminus T
is an independent set of vertices of degree
t
. Let
H=G\setminus(R\setminus T)
and for each subset
Q\subseteq R\setminus T
, let
H_{Q}=G\left[V(H)\cup Q\right]
. Then we have
e(G)=tn+e(H_{Q})-t|H_{Q}|
and

e(H_{Q})\ =\ e(T)+\sum_{v\in H_{Q}\setminus T}\left(|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}\left|\Gamma(v)\cap V(H_{Q})\setminus T\right|\right)

=\ e(T)+\sum_{v\in H_{Q}\setminus T}\left(|\Gamma(v)\cap T|+\frac{1}{2}\left|\Gamma(v)\setminus T\right|\right)

\geq\ (t+1)|T|+O(1)+t|H_{Q}\setminus T|+\frac{1}{2}|H_{Q}\setminus\left(T\cup R\right)|\ \geq\ \left(t+\frac{1}{2}\right)|H_{Q}|-\frac{1}{2}|Q|+O(1)\ .

Taking
Q=\emptyset
, we obtain
e(G)=tn+e(H)-t|H|\geq tn+|H|/2+O(1)
, so if
|H|=\Omega(n)
, we have
e(G)\geq tn+\Omega(n)\geq tn+\omega\left(e^{\prime}_{r,t}(n)\right)
, since by part 3 of Theorem
6
,
e^{\prime}_{r,t}(n)\ll n^{4/5}
. So suppose
|H|=o(n)
. Take
Q
to be the set of vertices in
R\setminus T
which have a twin. Then every vertex in
Q
is adjacent to a vertex in
H
of degree
t
, so
|Q|\leq t|H|=o(n)
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\Gamma(v):v\not\in H_{Q}\}
. Then
(H_{Q},\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
(r,t)
-system by Observation
1
, so
e(H_{Q})\geq e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]
. Also,
|Q|\leq t|H|=t|H_{Q}|-t|Q|
and
e(H_{Q})\geq\left(t+1/2\right)|H_{Q}|-|Q|/2+O(1)
, so

t|H_{Q}|\ \leq\ \left(1-\frac{1}{2t^{2}+2t+1}\right)e(H_{Q})+O(1)\ .

Hence

e(G)\ =\ tn+e(H_{Q})-t|H_{Q}|\ \geq\ tn+\frac{e(H_{Q})}{2t^{2}+2t+1}+O(1)\ \geq\ tn+\Omega\left(e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]\right)\ ,

since by part 3 of Theorem
6
,
e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]=\omega(1)
.

We now prove the upper bound. Let
n
be a large integer. We need to construct a
K_{r}
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq t
and
e(G)\leq tn+O\left(e_{r,t}^{\prime}\left[n+o(n)\right]\right)
in which every pair of twins is adjacent to a vertex of degree
t
. Let
C
be a constant, large enough for the proof to go through, and
N
be the smallest integer with
N\geq n+Ce_{r,t}^{\prime}(N)
. Such integers do exist, since by part 3 of Theorem
6
,
e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N)\ll N^{4/5}
. Then
N-1<n+Ce_{r,t}^{\prime}(N-1)
, so
N=n+o(n)
.

Take a maximal
(r,t)
-system
(H,\mathcal{F})
with
|\mathcal{F}|=N
and
e(H)=e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N)
. Starting with
(H,\mathcal{F})
, repeatedly apply Lemma
12
until neither property 1 nor property 2 hold. Let
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
be the resulting maximal
(r,t)
-system. Then
d(v)+s(v)>t
for all
v\in H^{\prime}
and either
s(v)\geq 1
or
s(w)\geq 1
for all
v\neq w\in H^{\prime}
with
\Gamma(v)=\Gamma(w)
. Let
m=|H|
. Then we applied Lemma
12
at most
m
times. By Lemma
1
,
m\ll e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N)
, so
e(H^{\prime})\ll e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N)
and

N\ \geq\ |\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\ =\ N-O(e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N))\ \geq\ n+Ce^{\prime}_{r,t}(N)-O(e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N))\ \geq\ n\ ,

provided
C
is large enough. Let
m^{\prime}=\left|H^{\prime}\right|
. Then

m^{\prime}\ \leq\ m\ \ll\ e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N)\ \ll\ N^{4/5}\ \asymp\ n^{4/5}\ ,

so
(t+1)m^{\prime}\leq n-m^{\prime}
, since
n
is large.

Pick a subfamily
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
with
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|=n-m^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}(v)\geq\min\left(s(v),t+1\right)
for all
v\in H^{\prime}
, where
s^{\prime}(v)=|\{S\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}:v\in S\}|
. This is possible since
(t+1)m^{\prime}\leq n-m^{\prime}\leq n\leq\left|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\right|
. Then
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
is an
(r,t)
-system and we still have
d(v)+s^{\prime}(v)>t
for all
v\in H^{\prime}
and either
s^{\prime}(v)\geq 1
or
s^{\prime}(w)\geq 1
for all
v\neq w\in H^{\prime}
with
\Gamma(v)=\Gamma(w)
.

Add edges to
H^{\prime}
to obtain a graph
H^{\prime\prime}
such that
(H^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
is a maximal
(r,t)
-system. We claim that every added edge
e
is contained in a set in
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
. Indeed, since
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
is a maximal
(r,t)
-system, either
H^{\prime}+e
contains
K_{r}
or
(H^{\prime}+e)[S]
contains
K_{r-1}
for some
S\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. But
H^{\prime\prime}
is
K_{r}
-free and
H^{\prime}[S]
and
H^{\prime\prime}[T]
are
K_{r-1}
-free for all
S\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
and
T\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
, since
(H^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})
and
(H^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
are
(r,t)
-systems, so
e
must belong to a copy of
K_{r-1}
in
(H^{\prime}+e)[S]
for some
S\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
. In particular,
e\subseteq S
.

It follows that for all added edges
e
and vertices
v\in e
,
s(v)\geq 1
and so
s^{\prime}(v)\geq\min\left(s(v),t+1\right)\geq 1
. Equivalently,
s^{\prime}(v)\geq 1
for all
v\in H^{\prime}
with
\Gamma(v)\subset\Gamma^{\prime}(v)
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}(v)
is the neighbourhood of
v
in
H^{\prime\prime}
. Hence, we still have either
s^{\prime}(v)\geq 1
or
s^{\prime}(w)\geq 1
for all
v\neq w\in H^{\prime}
with
\Gamma^{\prime}(v)=\Gamma^{\prime}(w)
. We also still have
d^{\prime}(v)+s^{\prime}(v)>t
for all
v\in H^{\prime}
, where
d^{\prime}(v)
is the degree of
v
in
H^{\prime\prime}
, since
d^{\prime}(v)\geq d(v)
. Let
N^{\prime}\in\{N-1,N\}
be such that
\max[e_{r,t}^{\prime}(N-1),e_{r,t}^{\prime}(N)]=e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N^{\prime})
. Note that
N^{\prime}=n+o(n)
. Since

|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}|\ \leq\ N-n+m^{\prime}\ <\ Ce_{r,t}^{\prime}(N-1)+m+1\ \ll\ e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N^{\prime})\ ,

it also follows from the previous paragraph that the number of added edges is
O(e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N^{\prime}))
, so
e(H^{\prime\prime})\ll e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N^{\prime})
.

Let
G=G(H^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime})
. Then
G
is
K_{r}
-saturated by Observation
1
and
|G|=|H^{\prime\prime}|+\left|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\right|=n
. For every vertex
v\in H^{\prime\prime}
, the degree of
v
in
G
is
d^{\prime}(v)+s^{\prime}(v)>t
, and all other vertices in
G
have degree
t
. Hence
\delta(G)\geq t
. Let
v\neq w\in G
be twins. We need to show that they are adjacent to a vertex of degree
t
, or equivalently, that
v,w\in H^{\prime\prime}
and
s^{\prime}(v)=s^{\prime}(w)\geq 1
. Since
v
and
w
have the same degree, either
v,w\not\in H^{\prime\prime}
or
v,w\in H^{\prime\prime}
. The former is impossible, since
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
has no repeated elements, and if the latter holds, we must have
s^{\prime}(v)=s^{\prime}(w)\geq 1
, since
s^{\prime}(v)=s^{\prime}(w)
and
\Gamma^{\prime}(v)=\Gamma^{\prime}(w)
. Finally,
e(G)=e\left(H^{\prime\prime}\right)+t\left|\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\right|=tn+O(e^{\prime}_{r,t}(N^{\prime}))
.

∎

Next, we prove Theorem
2
.

Theorem
2
.

We have the following estimates for
\tsat(n,K_{r})
.

1.

For all integers
r\geq 3
,

\left(r+2\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{r})\leq(r+3)n+o(n)\ .

2.

In the special case
r=3
, we obtain a stronger lower bound.

\left(5+\frac{2}{3}\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{3})\leq 6n+o(n)\ .

Proof.

We first prove the lower bounds. Let
G
be a twin-free
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices. We need to show that
e(G)\geq\left(r+2\right)n+o(n)
and
e(G)\geq\left(5+2/3\right)n+o(n)
in the special case
r=3
. If
e(G)=\omega(n)
, there is nothing to prove, so we may assume
e(G)=O(n)
. Then by Theorem 4 in
[
1
]
,
G
has a vertex cover
C
of size
m=O(n/\log n)
. Let
H=G[C]
and
\mathcal{F}=\{\Gamma(v):v\not\in C\}
. Then
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
r
-system by Observation
1
. Note that
\mathcal{F}
has no repeated elements, since
G
is twin-free.

Recall (see the second paragraph of the introduction) that
r-2
is the smallest possible degree of a vertex in a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on at least
r-1
vertices. Since
n
is large, it follows that every set in
\mathcal{F}
has size at least
r-2
. For each integer
r-2\leq t\leq r+1
, let
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\{S\in\mathcal{F}:|S|=t\}
. Then, since
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
r
-system,
(H,\mathcal{F}_{t})
is an
(r,t)
-system, so
|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq s_{r,t}(m)\ll m\ll n/\log n
by parts 1 through 4 of Theorem
4
. Hence there are only
O(n/\log n)
vertices outside
C
of degree at most
r+1
, so
e(G)\geq\left(r+2\right)n+O(n/\log n)
.

In the special case
r=3
, let
\mathcal{F}_{5}=\{S\in\mathcal{F}:|S|=5\}
. Then, as before, since
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
3
-system,
(H,\mathcal{F}_{5})
is a
(3,5)
-system. Hence
e(H)\geq 2|\mathcal{F}_{5}|/3+o\left(|\mathcal{F}_{5}|\right)
by part 5 of Theorem
6
. Also, since there are only
O(n/\log n)
vertices outside
C
of degree at most
4
,
e\left(C,V(G)\setminus C\right)\geq 6n-|\mathcal{F}_{5}|+O(n/\log n)
. So

e(G)\ =\ e(H)+e\left(C,V(G)\setminus C\right)\ \geq\ 6n-|\mathcal{F}_{5}|/3+o\left(|\mathcal{F}_{5}|\right)+O(n/\log n)\ .

But
|\mathcal{F}_{5}|\leq n
, so
e(G)\geq\left(5+2/3\right)n+o(n)
.

We now prove the upper bounds. By Lemma
2
, it is sufficient to prove the upper bound in part 2. Let
n
be a large integer. We need to construct a twin-free
K_{3}
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices with
e(G)\leq 6n+o(n)
. Let
l=\left\lceil n^{1/5}\right\rceil
and
(H_{5,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{5,l}^{\prime})
be as in property 5 in Construction
1
. By properties 2 and 4 in Construction
1
,
|H_{5,l}^{\prime}|=5l^{2}+1
and
|\mathcal{F}_{5,l}^{\prime}|=l^{5}
. Pick a subfamily
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{5,l}^{\prime}
with
\left|\mathcal{F}\right|=n-5l^{2}-1
such that every missing edge in
H_{5,l}^{\prime}
that is contained in a set in
\mathcal{F}_{5,l}^{\prime}
is contained in a set in
\mathcal{F}
. This is possible since
n-5l^{2}-1\leq l^{5}=|\mathcal{F}_{5,l}^{\prime}|
and the number of missing edges in
H_{5,l}^{\prime}
is
O(n^{4/5})
, which is at most
n-5l^{2}-1=n+O\left(n^{2/5}\right)
, since
n
is large. Then it is easy to see that, since
(H_{5,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}_{5,l}^{\prime})
is a maximal
(3,6)
-system, so is
(H_{5,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})
.

Let
G=G(H_{5,l}^{\prime},\mathcal{F})
. Then
G
is
K_{3}
-saturated by Observation
1
and
|G|=|H_{5,l}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{F}|=n
. The vertices in
H_{5,l}^{\prime}
have degree
\omega(1)
in
G
by property 3 in Construction
1
and all other vertices in
G
have degree
6
. It follows that
\delta(G)=6
. We will use this to prove the upper bound in part 4 of Theorem
3
. It also follows that for all twins
v\neq w\in G
, either
v,w\not\in H_{5,l}^{\prime}
or
v,w\in H_{5,l}^{\prime}
. But
\mathcal{F}
has no repeated elements and
H_{5,l}^{\prime}
is twin-free by property 5 in Construction
1
, so
G
is twin-free. Finally,
e(G)=e(H_{5,l}^{\prime})+6|\mathcal{F}|=6n+O(n^{4/5})
.

∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
3
.

Theorem
3
.

We have the following estimates for
\tsat(n,K_{r},t)
.

1.

For all integers
r\geq 3
and
t\geq r+3
,

tn+\Omega\left(n^{1/(t-r+2)}\right)\leq\tsat(n,K_{r},t)\leq tn+O\left(n^{4/(t-r+2)}\right)\ .

2.

For all integers
t\geq 6
,

tn+\Omega\left(n^{2/(t-3)}\right)\leq\tsat(n,K_{3},t)\leq tn+O\left(n^{4/(t-1)}\right)\ .

3.

For all integers
r\geq 3
and
r-2\leq t\leq r+2
,

\left(r+2\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{r},t)\leq(r+3)n+o(n)\ .

4.

For all integers
1\leq t\leq 5
,

\left(5+\frac{2}{3}\right)n+o(n)\leq\tsat(n,K_{3},t)\leq 6n+o(n)\ .

Proof.

Parts 1 and 2 follow from Theorem
8
and parts 3 and 4 of Theorem
6
, respectively. We now prove parts 3 and 4. We first prove the lower bounds. The proof will be similar to that of the lower bounds in Theorem
2
. Let
G
be a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq t
in which every pair of twins is adjacent to a vertex of degree
t
. We need to show that
e(G)\geq\left(r+2\right)n+o(n)
and
e(G)\geq\left(5+2/3\right)n+o(n)
in the special case
r=3
.

If
e(G)=\omega(n)
, there is nothing to prove, so we may assume
e(G)=O(n)
. Then, as in the proof of the lower bounds in Theorem
2
, by Theorem 4 in
[
1
]
,
G
has a vertex cover
C
of size
O(n/\log n)
. Let
S
be the set of vertices
v\not\in C
which have a twin. Then every vertex in
S
is adjacent to a vertex in
C
of degree
t
, so
|S|\leq t|C|=O(n/\log n)
. Let
H=G\left[C\cup S\right]
,
\mathcal{F}=\{\Gamma(v):v\not\in C\cup S\}
and
m=|H|=O(n/\log n)
. Then
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
r
-system by Observation
1
. Note that
\mathcal{F}
has no repeated elements.

Recall again (see the second paragraph of the introduction) that
r-2
is the smallest possible degree of a vertex in a
K_{r}
-saturated graph on at least
r-1
vertices. Since
n
is large, it follows that every set in
\mathcal{F}
has size at least
r-2
. For each integer
r-2\leq t\leq r+1
, let
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\{S\in\mathcal{F}:|S|=t\}
. Then, since
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
r
-system,
(H,\mathcal{F}_{t})
is an
(r,t)
-system, so
|\mathcal{F}_{t}|\leq s_{r,t}(m)\ll m\ll n/\log n
by parts 1 through 4 of Theorem
4
, as before. Hence there are only
O(n/\log n)
vertices outside
C\cup S
of degree at most
r+1
, so
e(G)\geq\left(r+2\right)n+O(n/\log n)
.

In the special case
r=3
, let
\mathcal{F}_{5}=\{S\in\mathcal{F}:|S|=5\}
. Then, as before, since
(H,\mathcal{F})
is a maximal
3
-system,
(H,\mathcal{F}_{5})
is a
(3,5)
-system. Hence
e(H)\geq 2|\mathcal{F}_{5}|/3+o\left(|\mathcal{F}_{5}|\right)
by part 5 of Theorem
6
. Also, since there are only
O(n/\log n)
vertices outside
C\cup S
of degree at most
4
,
e\left(C,V(G)\setminus C\right)\geq 6n-|\mathcal{F}_{5}|+O(n/\log n)
. So

e(G)\ =\ e(H)+e\left(C,V(G)\setminus C\right)\ \geq\ 6n-|\mathcal{F}_{5}|/3+o\left(|\mathcal{F}_{5}|\right)+O(n/\log n)\ .

But
|\mathcal{F}_{5}|\leq n
, so
e(G)\geq\left(5+2/3\right)n+o(n)
, as in the proof of the lower bounds in Theorem
2
.

Finally, we prove the upper bounds. By Lemma
3
, it is sufficient to prove the upper bound in part 4. Recall that in the proof of the upper bound in part 2 of Theorem
2
, we constructed a twin-free
K_{3}
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)=6
and
e(G)=6n+O(n^{4/5})
for every large integer
n
. The upper bound in part 4 follows.

∎

5

Open problems

The obvious open problems are to improve the estimates for the extremal quantities we defined.

Problem 1
.

Improve the estimates for
\tsat(n,K_{r})
in Theorem
2
.

Problem 2
.

Improve the estimates for
\tsat(n,K_{r},t)
in Theorem
3
.

Problem 3
.

Improve the estimates for
s_{r,t}(m)
in Theorem
4
.

Problem 4
.

Improve the estimates for
e_{r,t}(s)
and
e^{\prime}_{r,t}(s)
in Theorem
6
.

In light of Theorem
1
, one might ask the following.

Question 1
.

For which
r
,
t
and
n
does there exist a
K_{r}
-saturated graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq t
that cannot be obtained by blowing up a smaller
K_{r}
-saturated graph
H
with
\delta(H)\geq t
?

Though in some sense the
K_{r}
case is the most natural one, one could also define
\tsat(n,H)
to be the minimum number of edges in a twin-free
H
-saturated graph on
n
vertices for other graphs
H
. As in the case
H=K_{r}
, it is not immediately clear whether such graphs even exist. By Theorem
1
, in the case
H=K_{r}
, such graphs do exist for fixed
r
and large
n
.

Question 2
.

For which graphs
H
do there exist twin-free
H
-saturated graphs on
n
vertices for large enough
n
?

The answer to Question
2
is not “All graphs”. Indeed, it is easy to show that when
H
is a matching, all
H
-free graphs are blow-ups of a graph with
O(1)
vertices. Indeed, let
G
be an
H
-free graph. Let
S
be the set of matched vertices in a maximal matching in
G
. Then, since
G
is
H
-free,
|S|=O(1)
, and since the matching is maximal,
V(G)\setminus S
is an independent set. Together, these imply that
G
is a blow-up of a graph with
O(1)
vertices.

Kászonyi and Tuza showed that
\sat(n,H)=O(n)
for all graphs
H
(see Theorem 1 in
[
7
]
). It is not at all clear whether the same should hold for
\tsat(n,H)
, when it is well-defined.

Question 3
.

Do we have
\tsat(n,H)=O(n)
for every graph
H
for which there exist twin-free
H
-saturated graphs on
n
vertices for large enough
n
?

Tuza conjectured that the ratio
\sat(n,H)/n
converges for all graphs
H
(see Conjecture 10 in
[
9
]
). Tuza’s Conjecture is still wide open. It is again not at all clear whether the same should hold for
\tsat(n,H)
when
\tsat(n,H)=O(n)
.

Question 4
.

Does
\tsat(n,H)/n
converge for all graphs
H
for which
\tsat(n,H)=O(n)
?

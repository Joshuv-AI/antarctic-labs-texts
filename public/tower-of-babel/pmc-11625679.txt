Small cell lung cancer presenting with pleural effusion and superior vena cava obstruction
Authors: Allahdad Khan, Arshia Batool, Qura‐tul Ain, Ali Haider Hashmi, Muhammad Islam, Aseel Kamal
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Key messageSmall cell lung cancer with concurrent pleural effusion and superior vena cava obstruction is a rare presentation with high risk of respiratory and circulatory compromise warranting prompt and multidisciplinary treatment.

CLINICAL IMAGE

A 60 year old male marble miner presented to the emergency department with complaints of progressive shortness of breath, productive cough, headache and facial swelling (Figure 1A) for 2 weeks. There was a history of 10 kg weight loss. He had a 30 pack year history of smoking. Vital signs were within normal limits, with SpO2 93% on room air. There were decreased breath sounds, dull percussion notes and fine inspiratory basal crepitations on the right side of the chest. x‐Ray chest PA view (Figure 2A) showed right sided pleural effusion with suspected mass. HRCT chest (Figure 2B–D) and CT chest with contrast (Figure 1B–D) showing right hilar and para hilar region lesion, extending into right mediastinal region, encasing and partially obliterating the superior vena cava confirmed these findings. Cytology of pleural fluid showed atypical small cells. Immunohistochemical staining was positive for Cytokeratin CAM 5.2, CD56, Synaptophysin, TTF‐1. These findings were diagnostic of neuroendocrine neoplasm consistent with metastatic small cell carcinoma of lung (SCLC). Patient was referred to the oncology department for further management of SCLC.

(A) Facial swelling suggestive of superior vena cava obstruction. (B) Post contrast CT chest, axial section, mediastinal window showing heterogeneously enhancing soft tissue density lesion, involving right hilar and para hilar region, extending into right mediastinal region(long arrow), encasing and partially obliterating the superior vena cava(short arrow). (C) Post contrast CT chest axial section, mediastinal window, showing enlarged mediastinal lymph nodes in right para tracheal and perivascular location. (D) Post contrast CT chest, axial section, mediastinal window showing collaterals formation in left chest wall.

(A) x‐Ray chest PA view shows opacification of right hemithorax (long arrow) in upper, middle and lower lung zones with blunting of right costophrenic angle (short arrow), however no significant mediastinal shift. NO evidence of bony erosion. Left hemithorax is unremarkable. HRCT chest axial sections shows abrupt cut‐off at the level of right main bronchus with adjacent heterogeneous lesion involving carina and right main bronchus (B, C), resulting in distal lung collapse and consolidation with partial sparing of lower lobe of right lung (C) and moderate right sided pleural effusion(D).

These findings are often indicators of advanced disease with a poorer prognosis, as they reflect extensive tumour burden and local invasion. Malignant pleural effusion is seen in 11% of SCLC patients. 1 SVCO is identified at diagnosis in 10% of patients with SCLC. 2

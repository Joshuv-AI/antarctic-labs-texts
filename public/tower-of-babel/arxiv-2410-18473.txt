Strictly convex norms and the local diameter two property
Trond A. Abrahamsen, Petr Hájek, Vegard Lima, Stanimir Troyanski

Abstract
. We introduce and study a strict monotonicity property of the norm in solid Banach lattices of real functions that prevents such spaces from having the local diameter two property. Then we show that any strictly convex 1-symmetric norm on c 0 ​ ( Γ ) c_{0}(\Gamma) possesses this property. In the opposite direction, we show that any Banach space which is strictly convex renormable and contains a complemented copy of c 0 ​ ( ℕ ) , c_{0}(\mathbb{N}), admits an equivalent strictly convex norm for which the space has the local diameter two property. In particular, this enables us to construct a strictly convex norm on c 0 ​ ( Γ ) , c_{0}(\Gamma), where Γ \Gamma is uncountable, for which the space has a 1-unconditional basis and the local diameter two property.



1.
Introduction

A Banach space (or a norm
\|\cdot\|
on a Banach space)
X
is said
to be
strictly convex
if for all
x,y\in X
with

\frac{1}{2}\|x+y\|=\|x\|=\|y\|,
we have
x=y.
Another way of
stating this is that the unit sphere
S_{X}
of
X
does not contain any
non-trivial line segments.

A lot of effort has gone into understanding when a Banach space admits
an equivalent strictly convex norm. It is known for example that
every separable Banach space admits an equivalent strictly convex norm

[
Cla36
, Theorem 9]
. For non-separable spaces things get much
more murky. Day showed that when
\Gamma
is an uncountable set,

\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
cannot be renormed to have a strictly convex
norm, while
c_{0}(\Gamma)
can
[
Day55
]
. Uncountability of

\Gamma
is important here as
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
is strictly
convex renormable (see
[
GMZ22
, Remark 151 and
Proposition 152]
). Haydon
[
Hay90
]
showed that
there exist uncountable scattered compacts
K
for which
C(K)
fails
to be strictly convex renormable. At the present moment there does not
exist an effective (checkable) criterion for when a (non-separable)
Banach space admits an equivalent strictly convex norm or when it does
not. For more on this topic the introduction in
[
OST12
]
is a
good place to start.

Recall that a
slice
of the unit ball
B_{X}
of a Banach space
X

is a set of the form

S(x^{*},\varepsilon):=\{x\in B_{X}:x^{*}(x)>1-\varepsilon\},

where
x^{*}\in S_{X^{*}}
and
\varepsilon>0.

A Banach space
X
is said to have the

local diameter two property (LD2P)

provided every slice of
B_{X}
has diameter two.
While the usual focus of renorming theory is to find an equivalent
norm with the nicest properties possible our focus is different.
We will study to what degree the LD2P and strict convexity can coexist.
While at first sight the LD2P seems to be incompatible with strict
convexity, there are examples showing that these properties indeed can
coexist.
In fact, even stronger properties are compatible with strict convexity.
A Banach space is
almost square

if for every finite subset
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in S_{X}

and every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
y\in S_{X}

such that
\|x_{i}+y\|\leq 1+\varepsilon
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Any almost square Banach space has the LD2P
(see e.g
[
ALL16
, Theorem 1.3 and Proposition 2.5]
).
Recall that a Banach space
X
is
M-embedded
if

X^{***}=X^{*}\oplus_{1}X^{\perp}
, that is

X
is an M-ideal in its bidual.
Note that non-reflexive M-embedded spaces are almost square

[
ALL16
, Corollary 4.3]
.
The quotient
C(\mathbb{T})/A
, where
C(\mathbb{T})
is the space of
continuous functions on the complex unit circle
\mathbb{T}
and where

A
is the disc algebra, is an example of a strictly convex
Banach space with the LD2P.
In fact,
C(\mathbb{T})/A
is M-embedded and even has a smooth dual

[
HWW93
, Remark IV.1.17]
.
Another example can be constructed as follows:
Let
\varphi
be the function on
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
defined by

\varphi(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x^{2n}_{n}
where
x=(x_{n}).

The
Nakano norm
on
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
is defined by

\|x\|=\inf\{\lambda>0:\varphi(x/\lambda)\leq 1\}

for every
x\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
. The space
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
with
the Nakano norm is strictly convex and is almost square

[
AHN+16
]
. A third and recent example is the strictly convex
renorming of
L_{1}[0,1]
constructed in
[
NPoTV24
]
. This example
is not almost square, but has the LD2P.

While the LD2P and strict convexity can coexist in a Banach space
X
,
strict convexity in
X^{**}
is asking too much, at least if we ask

X
to have the property that all finite convex combinations of slices
of its unit ball have diameter two. (Whether the same conclusion holds
only assuming the LD2P, is an open question). Indeed, in this case not
only does
X^{**}
fail to be strictly convex, it also fails to be
smooth
[
ALNT16
, Corollary 2.6]
. Another result in this
direction is that if
X
has a bimonotone basis and the property that
all non-empty relatively weakly open subsets of its unit ball have
diameter two, then
X^{**}
is not strictly convex

[
ALNT16
, Proposition 2.10]
.
These results point to the fact that
constructing strictly convex Banach spaces with the LD2P is not
completely straightforward.

Note that all the examples above with the LD2P and strictly convex
norms, are separable. If
\Gamma
is an uncountable set we know that
Day’s norm on
c_{0}(\Gamma)
is strictly convex (and even locally
uniformly rotund), but it is not immediately obvious how to give

c_{0}(\Gamma)
a strictly convex norm with the LD2P. If the norm of a
Banach space
X
is locally uniformly rotund then every
x\in S_{X}
is
strongly exposed, so the LD2P and local uniformly rotundity cannot
coexist. In the present paper, we construct the first,
to the best of our knowledge,
example of a strictly convex renorming of

c_{0}(\Gamma)
with the LD2P.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: After some preliminary
results in Section
2
, we introduce in
Section
3

a strict monotonicity property of the norm in solid Banach lattices of
real functions and show that this property cannot coexist with the
LD2P.

In Section
4
we show that
under some assumptions on the norm, solid Banach lattices of real
functions possess the strict monotonicity property just mentioned. In
particular, we show that for any set
\Gamma,
any strictly convex
norm on
c_{0}(\Gamma)
with a 1-symmetric basis, fails the LD2P.

Finally, in Section
5
we prove that every
strictly convex renormable Banach space which contains a complemented copy
of
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
has an equivalent norm which is both strictly convex and
almost square. In particular, this enables us to prove that for any infinite
set
\Gamma
,
c_{0}(\Gamma)
has a strictly convex renorming with a
1-unconditional basis that is almost square.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us start by recalling some definitions and results that we will
need later in the paper.

A
solid Banach lattice of real functions
on some set
\Gamma
is a
Banach space
(X,\|\cdot\|)
consisting of real-valued bounded functions

x:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
with the following properties

•

if
x\in X
and
y:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
with
|y(\gamma)|\leq|x(\gamma)|

for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then
y\in X
and

\|y\|\leq\|x\|
;

•

for every
\alpha\in\Gamma,
the function
e_{\alpha}:=\delta_{\alpha}
,
where
\delta_{\alpha}(\gamma)=1
if
\gamma=\alpha,
and

\delta_{\alpha}(\gamma)=0
if
\gamma\neq\alpha
, belongs to
X
.

We will assume that
\|e_{\gamma}\|=1
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma.

Hence
\ell_{1}(\Gamma)\subseteq X\subseteq\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)

and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|\leq\|\cdot\|_{1}
.
When
\Gamma
is the set
\mathbb{N}
of natural numbers, we obtain the
well known class of Köthe sequence spaces.

For
\gamma\in\Gamma,
we let
e_{\gamma}^{*}\in X^{*}
denote the biorthogonal functional to

e_{\gamma}.
Note that for
x\in X
we have
e_{\gamma}^{*}(x)=x(\gamma).

Clearly
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a 1-unconditional

basic set
in
X,
i.e. a 1-unconditional basis for the norm
closure of the linear span of
\{e_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
in

X.
Hence if
c_{00}(\Gamma)
, the space of finitely supported
real-valued functions on
\Gamma,
is dense in
X
, the unit vectors

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
form a 1-unconditional basis for

X
. Recall that if
\Gamma
is a non-empty set, then

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is called
an unconditional
basis
for a Banach space
X
if for every
x\in X
there is a
unique family of real numbers
(a_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
such
that
x=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}a_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}
in the sense
that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a finite set
F\subset\Gamma

such that
\|x-\sum_{\gamma\in G}a_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}\|<\varepsilon
for
every
G\supset F.
If moreover, for any finite set
F
in
\Gamma

and any set of real numbers
(a_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in F}
and

(b_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in F}
with
|b_{\gamma}|\leq|\alpha_{\gamma}|
for

\gamma\in F,
we have

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in F}b_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}\right\|\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in F}a_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}\right\|,

then we say that
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is

1-unconditional
.

If
X
is a solid Banach lattice of real functions on
\Gamma,
we say
that
X
is
1-symmetric
if for every
x=x(\gamma)\in X
and
every permutation
\pi:\Gamma\to\Gamma
we have

x_{\pi}=x(\pi(\gamma))\in X
and
\|x_{\pi}\|=\|x\|
. We say that
the norm of the lattice
X
is
strictly monotone
if

\|y\|<\|x\|
whenever
|y|<|x|
, that is if

|y(\gamma)|\leq|x(\gamma)|
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
and

|y(\beta)|<|x(\beta)|
for some
\beta\in\Gamma
.

Let
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
be a 1-unconditional basis for a
Banach space
X.
For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
let
e_{\gamma}^{*}\in X^{*}

denote the biorthogonal functional of
e_{\gamma}.
We say that

(e_{\gamma},e_{\gamma}^{*})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is
shrinking
if

\clinspan\{e_{\gamma}^{*}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}=X^{*}.

The following results will be used several times in the paper. The
first one is James’ classical representation of the bidual of a Banach
space with a shrinking basis in case the shrinking basis is
uncountable and unconditional. We include a proof for easy reference.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with a shrinking 1-unconditional basis

(e_{\gamma},e_{\gamma}^{*})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
, and
\mathcal{F}
the set

\{F\subset\Gamma:|F|<\infty\}
ordered by inclusion. Then for every

x^{**}\in X^{**}
we have

\|x^{**}\|=\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in F}x^{**}(e_{\gamma}^{*})e_{\gamma}\right\|=\lim_{\mathcal{F}}\left\|\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}x^{**}(e_{\gamma}^{*})e_{\gamma}\right\|.

Proof.

Let
F\subset\Gamma
be finite and
P_{F}:X\to X
the projection
onto
\linspan\{e_{\gamma}:\gamma\in F\}
defined by

P_{F}(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in F}e_{\gamma}^{*}(x)e_{\gamma}.

Since the basis is shrinking, we have

(1)

\displaystyle P_{F}^{**}(x^{**})=\sum_{\gamma\in F}x^{**}(e_{\gamma}^{*})e_{\gamma},

for every
x^{**}\in X^{**}.

Indeed if
\gamma_{0}\in\Gamma
and
x^{*}=e_{\gamma_{0}}^{*},
then

(2)

\displaystyle\langle P_{F}^{**}(x^{**}),x^{*}\rangle=\langle x^{**},P_{F}^{*}(e_{\gamma_{0}}^{*})\rangle=\begin{cases}x^{**}(e_{\gamma_{0}}^{*}),&\gamma_{0}\in F\\
0,&\gamma_{0}\notin F,\end{cases}

so

\langle P_{F}^{**}(x^{**}),x^{*}\rangle=\left\langle\sum_{\gamma\in F}x^{**}(e_{\gamma}^{*})e_{\gamma},x^{*}\right\rangle.

From this in tandem with linearity and continuity of the functionals
involved and the fact that

\clinspan\{e_{\gamma}^{*}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}=X^{*},
the equality
(
1
) follows. Moreover, since the basis is
1-unconditional,

\|x^{**}\|=\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\|P_{F}^{**}x^{**}\|.

Indeed, because of
(
2
) and since

\clinspan\{e_{\gamma}^{*}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}=X^{*},
the net

(P_{F}^{**}x^{**})_{F\in\mathcal{F}},

converges weak
∗
to
x^{**}.
Thus by weak
∗
lower
semi-continuity of the norm we have

\|x^{**}\|\leq\liminf_{\mathcal{F}}\|P_{F}^{**}x^{**}\|\leq\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\|P_{F}^{**}x^{**}\|.
Also, since

(e_{\gamma},e_{\gamma}^{*})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is 1-unconditional (in
particular monotone) we have
\|P_{F}\|=1,
so we can conclude

\|x^{**}\|=\sup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\|P_{F}^{**}x^{**}\|=\lim_{\mathcal{F}}\|P_{F}^{**}x^{**}\|.\qed

The next result is well known.
For a proof see that of Theorem 2.6 (ii) implies (iii)
in
[
MOTZ07
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a solid Banach lattice of real functions.
If the norm of
X
is strictly convex,
then
X
is strictly monotone.

The converse does not hold in general, just consider
X=\ell_{1}
.
But if
(X,\|\cdot\|)
is a solid Banach lattice of real functions on some
set
\Gamma
such that
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}\leq\|\cdot\|,
we have
from
[
MOTZ07
, Theorem 2.6]
, that the following statements are
equivalent:

(i)

X
admits a pointwise lower semi-continuous strictly convex norm;

(ii)

X
admits a lattice pointwise lower semi-continuous strictly
convex norm;

(iii)

X
admits a pointwise lower semi-continuous strictly monotone
norm (in
[
MOTZ07
]
the term strictly lattice norm is used).

3.
A uniform strict monotonicity property of the
norm

Throughout this section
X
will be a solid Banach lattice of real
functions on a set
\Gamma
with a (normalized) 1-unconditional basic
set
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}.

In this section we will introduce a property that will
prevent
X
from having the LD2P.
The Nakano norm on
c_{0}
shows that a strictly monotone norm is
not enough to prevent the LD2P.
On the other hand, the space
c_{0}\oplus_{1}\mathbb{R}

has a 1-unconditional basis, fails the LD2P, and the norm
is not strictly monotone.
The last example can serve as a motivation for our property.

Definition 3.1
.

For each
\alpha\in\Gamma
and
\varepsilon>0
define

E_{\alpha}(\varepsilon):=\sup\left\{\|x-e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}\|:x\in S_{X},e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)>1-\varepsilon\right\}.

If there exist
\alpha\in\Gamma
and

\varepsilon>0
such that
E_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)<1
, then we say
that
X
is
uniformly strictly monotone at

\alpha\in\Gamma
.

We note that
E_{\alpha}
is a non-decreasing function of
\varepsilon
since
we are taking supremum over a smaller set.

Proposition 3.2
.

If there exists
\alpha\in\Gamma
such that

X
is uniformly strictly monotone at
\alpha
,
then
X
fails to have the LD2P.

Proof.

Assume
X
is uniformly strictly monotone at
\alpha\in\Gamma.

Let
\varepsilon>0
such that
E_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)<1
. We may
and will assume that
\varepsilon<2/3
. We will show that the
diameter of the slice

S(e_{\alpha}^{*},\varepsilon)
is
bounded away from
2
.

Claim 3.3
.

There exists a
C>0
such that

\left\|x-\left(e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)e_{\alpha}\right\|\leq 1-C

for all
x\in S(e_{\alpha}^{*},\varepsilon)
.

Proof of claim.

Let
C:=(1-3\varepsilon/2)(1-E_{\alpha}(\varepsilon))>0
.

If
x\in S(e_{\alpha}^{*},\varepsilon)

we set

\lambda=\frac{e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)-\varepsilon/2}{e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)}.

Then, since
e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)\leq 1
and
\varepsilon<2/3
,

\displaystyle 1>\lambda>\frac{1-\varepsilon-\varepsilon/2}{e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)}>1-\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon>0

and

x-\left(e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)e_{\alpha}=(1-\lambda)x+\lambda(x-e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}),

so by convexity of the norm

\displaystyle\left\|x-\left(e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)e_{\alpha}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\lambda)\|x\|+\lambda\|x-e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq 1-\lambda+\lambda E_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)\leq 1-C

as claimed.
∎

Let
y,z\in S(e_{\alpha}^{*},\varepsilon)
.
We use unconditionality,
i.e.
\|u\|\leq\|v\|
whenever
|u|\leq|v|
,
and the above claim and get

\displaystyle\|y-z\|

\displaystyle=\left\|e_{\alpha}^{*}(y-z)e_{\alpha}+\bigl(y-e_{\alpha}^{*}(y)e_{\alpha}\bigr)-\bigl(z-e_{\alpha}^{*}(z)e_{\alpha}\bigr)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\varepsilon e_{\alpha}+\bigl(y-e_{\alpha}^{*}(y)e_{\alpha}\bigr)-\bigl(z-e_{\alpha}^{*}(z)e_{\alpha}\bigr)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\bigl(y-e_{\alpha}^{*}(y)e_{\alpha}\bigr)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{\alpha}\right\|+\left\|\bigl(z-e_{\alpha}^{*}(z)e_{\alpha}\bigr)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}e_{\alpha}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|y-\left(e_{\alpha}^{*}(y)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)e_{\alpha}\right\|+\left\|z-\left(e_{\alpha}^{*}(z)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)e_{\alpha}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2(1-C).

Hence the diameter of
S(e_{\alpha}^{*},\varepsilon)
is strictly
less than
2
.
∎

4.
Spaces with 1-unconditional bases that have
uniformly strictly monotone norms

Let
X
be a solid Banach lattice of real functions on a set

\Gamma
with a (normalized) 1-unconditional basic set

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}.
For
A\subset\Gamma,
let

\mathbbm{1}_{A}\in\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
denote the characteristic
function on
A.
Note that
\mathbbm{1}_{A}\in X
if
A
is finite. We
define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L_{F}}=\sup\left\{\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{A}\right\|:A\subset\Gamma,|A|<\infty\right\}\text{ and }\mathfrak{L}=\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}\right\|.

Clearly
\mathfrak{L}\geq\mathfrak{L_{F}}.
Also, since

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is normalized we have

\mathfrak{L_{F}}\geq 1
, and if
X
is strictly monotone we have

\mathfrak{L_{F}}>1
. When we write
\mathfrak{L}<\infty,
we
implicitly assume that
\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}
is an element in
X.

Clearly
\|\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}\|<\infty
is equivalent to
X
being lattice isomorphic to
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma).

For a finite subset
F
of
\Gamma
let
P_{F}:X\to X
be the
natural projection onto the set
\linspan\{e_{\gamma}:\gamma\in F\}.

If
F\subset\Gamma
is finite and
(a_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in F}
is a
sequence of scalars, then

\max_{\gamma\in F}|a_{\gamma}|\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in F}a_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}\right\|\leq\max_{\gamma\in F}|a_{\gamma}|\cdot\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in F}e_{\gamma}\right\|\leq\mathfrak{L_{F}}\cdot\max_{\gamma\in F}|a_{\gamma}|.

Thus
\clinspan\{e_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is isomorphic to
c_{0}(\Gamma)

if and only if
\mathfrak{L_{F}}<\infty
.

Let
X
be
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
with the Nakano norm. The canonical basis
for
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
,
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, is then a normalized strictly
monotone 1-unconditional basis for
X
. If we want to show that
X

has the LD2P, then we can make use of the following fact: For every

j\in\mathbb{N}
we have that for any fixed
r\in(0,1)

\inf_{n\neq j}\left\{\|e_{j}+re_{n}\|\right\}=1.

In particular, this holds for
r=1/\mathfrak{L_{F}}
. Actually, we
can replace
e_{j}
with any
x\in S_{X}
with finite support in the
infimum above. Hence inside every slice
S(x^{*},\varepsilon)
of
B_{X}
we can
for every
r\in(0,1)
find elements of the form
x\pm re_{n}.

This tells us that every such slice has diameter two. We will return
to this example in Section
5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a strictly monotone solid Banach lattice of real
functions on the set
\Gamma
with a basic set

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}.

(i)

Assume
\mathfrak{L_{F}}<\infty
and for every
x\in X
we
have
\|x\|=\sup_{F\subset\Gamma,|F|<\infty}\|P_{F}x\|.
If there
exists
\alpha\in\Gamma
such that

\inf_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left\{\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{\beta}\right\|\right\}>1,

then
X
fails the LD2P. In particular, any equivalent renorming of

c_{0}(\Gamma)
such that the canonical basis
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

is strictly monotone and normalized, and there exists

\alpha\in\Gamma
with

\inf_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left\{\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{\beta}\right\|\right\}>1,

fails the LD2P.

(ii)

Assume that
X
is lattice
isomorphic to
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N}).
If there exists

k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\inf_{n\neq k}\left\{\left\|e_{k}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L}}e_{n}\right\|\right\}>1,

then
X
fails the LD2P.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that it follows from Day’s proof in
[
Day55
]
that when

\Gamma
is uncountable,
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
does not have an
equivalent strictly monotone norm.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\Gamma
.
We aim to show that
X
is uniformly strictly monotone at
\alpha

and the proof of
(i)
and

(ii)
are the same up to a point.

Assume
K>1
is a real number such that

l:=\inf_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left\{\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{K}e_{\beta}\right\|\right\}>1.

Fix
\eta<1
such that

\eta>\frac{K+1}{lK+1}.

Note that since
l\leq 2

\eta>\frac{K+1}{lK+1}>\frac{K+1}{l(K+1)}=\frac{1}{l}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Claim 4.3
.

There exists
\mathfrak{a}<1/K
such that for all

\eta e_{\alpha}+ae_{\beta}\in B_{X}
with
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta\neq\alpha,
we have
|a|\leq\mathfrak{a}
.

Proof of claim.

For notational reasons, let us introduce the function

f(\tau):=\tau\eta l-(1-\tau)\eta-(1-\eta)\tau/K.

Rewriting the assumption

\eta>\frac{K+1}{lK+1}

gives
f(1)=\eta l-(1-\eta)/K>1
.
Since
f
is continuous we can find
\theta\in(0,1)
such that

f(\theta)=\theta\eta l-(1-\theta)\eta-(1-\eta)\theta/K>1.

Set
\mathfrak{a}:=\theta/K.

Let

\|\eta e_{\alpha}+ae_{\beta}\|\leq 1
for some

\beta\in\Gamma\setminus\{\alpha\}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}.

Define

g_{\beta}(t):=\left\|\eta e_{\alpha}+te_{\beta}\right\|.

Since

\eta e_{\alpha}+\theta e_{\beta}/K=\theta\eta(e_{\alpha}+e_{\beta}/K)+(1-\theta)\eta e_{\alpha}+(1-\eta)\theta e_{\beta}/K,

we have

\displaystyle g_{\beta}(\theta/K)

\displaystyle=\|\eta e_{\alpha}+\theta e_{\beta}/K\|

\displaystyle\geq\theta\eta\|e_{\alpha}+e_{\beta}/K\|-(1-\theta)\eta\|e_{\alpha}\|-(1-\eta)\theta/K\|e_{\beta}\|

\displaystyle\geq\theta\eta l-(1-\theta)\eta-(1-\eta)\theta/K

\displaystyle=f(\theta)

\displaystyle>1\geq g_{\beta}(a).

By 1-unconditionality of the basic set

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma},
we have that
g_{\beta}
is even
and is non-decreasing for
t\geq 0.

Hence
|a|\leq\theta/K=\mathfrak{a}.

∎

We are now ready to prove that
X

is uniformly strictly monotone at
\alpha
.
Let
0<\varepsilon<1-\eta
be such that

\mathfrak{a}K<1-2\varepsilon
.
We will show that

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}(\varepsilon)=\sup\{\|x-e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}\|:x\in S_{X},e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)>1-\varepsilon\}\leq 1-\varepsilon.

Let
x\in S_{X}
and assume
e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)>1-\varepsilon
.
Let
\beta\neq\alpha
.
Now for some
z_{\beta}\in B_{X}
we have

x=e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}+e_{\beta}^{*}(x)e_{\beta}+z_{\beta}

and by strict monotonicity and
1
-unconditionality

1=\|x\|\geq\|e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}+e_{\beta}^{*}(x)e_{\beta}+z_{\beta}\|>\|\eta e_{\alpha}+e_{\beta}^{*}(x)e_{\beta}\|.

From Claim
4.3
we get that

|e_{\beta}^{*}(x)|\leq\mathfrak{a}<1/K
for all
\beta\neq\alpha
.

(i)
.
We assume
\mathfrak{L_{F}}<\infty.
Put
K:=\mathfrak{L_{F}},

and note that
\mathfrak{L_{F}}>1
since
X
is strictly
monotone. Therefore
K>1,
and we get by the assumption that there
exists a finite subset
F\subset\Gamma
such that

\displaystyle\|x-e_{\alpha}^{*}(x)e_{\alpha}\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\sum_{\beta\in F\setminus\{\alpha\}}e_{\beta}^{*}(x)e_{\beta}\right\|+\varepsilon\leq\mathfrak{a}\left\|\sum_{\beta\in F\setminus\{\alpha\}}e_{\beta}\right\|+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{a}\left\|\sum_{\beta\in F\cup\{\alpha\}}e_{\beta}\right\|+\varepsilon<\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{L_{F}}+\varepsilon=1-\varepsilon.

This proves that
X
is uniformly strictly monotone at
\alpha,
and
we have from Proposition
3.2
that
X

fails the LD2P.

For the particular case, let
X
be an equivalent renorming of

c_{0}(\Gamma)
such that the canonical basis

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is strictly monotone and
normalized. We then have that
X
is a strictly monotone solid Banach
lattice consisting of functions
x:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}.
Now we
only need to note that under the assumptions on
X
we have

\mathfrak{L_{F}}<\infty
and from
Proposition
2.1
that

\|x\|=\sup_{F\subset\Gamma,|F|<\infty}\|P_{F}x\|
for every

x\in X.

(ii)
. We assume
\Gamma=\mathbb{N}
and

\mathfrak{L}<\infty.
Put
K:=\mathfrak{L}
and note that

\mathfrak{L}>1
since
X
is strictly monotone. Therefore

K>1,
and with
k=\alpha
we get

\displaystyle\|x-e_{k}^{*}(x)e_{k}\|\leq\left\|\mathfrak{a}\mathbbm{1}_{\supp(x)\setminus\{k\}}\right\|<\mathfrak{a}\left\|\mathbbm{1}_{\Gamma}\right\|=\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{L}<1-\varepsilon,

so
E_{k}(\varepsilon)<1-\varepsilon
and
X
is therefore uniformly
strictly monotone at
k.

As in
(i)
we get that

fails the
LD2P by Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-empty set and
let
\|\cdot\|
be an equivalent norm on

c_{0}(\Gamma)
such that the canonical basis

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is normalized
and 1-symmetric.
If
\|\cdot\|
is strictly monotone, in particular if
\|\cdot\|

strictly convex, then it fails the LD2P.

Proof.

Let
X
be a renorming of

c_{0}(\Gamma)
such that the canonical basis

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is strictly monotone, normalized,
and 1-symmetric. Choose any
\alpha\in\Gamma
.
By Theorem
4.1

(i)
,
it is enough to show that

\inf_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left\{\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{\beta}\right\|\right\}>1.

But by symmetry we have

\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{\beta}\right\|=\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{\gamma}\right\|

for all
\beta,\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\{\alpha\}
,
so it is enough to observe that for
\beta\neq\alpha

\left\|e_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{\beta}\right\|>\|e_{\alpha}\|=1,

by strict monotonicity.

The strictly convex case now follows from
Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

It was shown in
[
HZ95
, Corollary 6]
that

\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
admits no equivalent
strictly convex 1-symmetric norm.
It can also be shown that
\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
does not admit
a strictly monotone 1-symmetric norm.

Question 4.6
.

Let
X
be a strictly monotone renorming of
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})

such that the canonical basis
(e_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is normalized.
Are then the following two conditions equivalent?

(i)

X
fails the LD2P;

(ii)

There exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\inf_{n\neq j}\left\{\left\|e_{j}+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{L_{F}}}e_{n}\right\|\right\}>1.

Let us end this section with some remarks about
the assumption
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{F}}<\infty
in
Theorem
4.1
.

First let us give an example of a Banach space
X

with a 1-symmetric basis and a strictly convex norm, which has the LD2P,
but which is not uniformly strictly monotone at any coordinate
and with
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{F}}=\infty
.

Example 4.7
.

Define a function
M:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

M(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t=0;\\
\frac{1}{4}e^{2}\cdot e^{-1/t},&t\in(0,\frac{1}{2});\\
t^{2},&t\geq\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Then
M
is a convex, non-decreasing continuous function
satisfying
M(0)=0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}M(t)=\infty
.
If we consider the space
\ell_{M}
of all sequences

x=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)
of scalars such that

\Phi(x/\lambda):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(|a_{n}|/\lambda)<\infty

for some
\lambda>0
, then
\ell_{M}
is a Banach space with norm

\|x\|=\inf\{\lambda>0:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}M(|a_{n}|/\lambda)\leq 1\}.

The unit vectors
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
form
a 1-symmetric basis of
h_{M}=\clinspan\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

(see e.g.
[
LT77
, p. 115]
).

Let us show that
X=h_{M}
is also strictly
convex, has the LD2P (it is actually M-embedded), is not uniformly
strictly monotone at any coordinate, but

\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{F}}=\infty
.

A straightforward computation shows that

M
is strictly convex, so
h_{M}
is strictly convex
(see e.g.
[
Che96
, p. 56]
).

From Example III.1.4 in
[
HWW93
]
we have that
h_{M}

is M-embedded since the complementary Orlicz function
M^{*}

satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition at zero while
M

fails it.
In particular,
h_{M}
has the LD2P by
[
BGLPPRP04
, Proposition 2.1]
.
Let us prove the claimed properties of
M
and
M^{*}
.
We have that
M^{\prime}(t)=M(t)\frac{1}{t^{2}}
on
(0,\frac{1}{2})

and hence

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{tM^{\prime}(t)}{M(t)}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{tM(t)/t^{2}}{M(t)}=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}=+\infty.

Since the above limit is infinite
M
fails the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
at zero (see e.g.
[
LT77
, p. 140]
).
We have that
M^{\prime}(t)
is continuous and strictly increasing
on
[0,\infty)
and has a continuous and strictly increasing
inverse
q(t)
.
The complementary function to
M
is given by

M^{*}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}q(t)\,dt
and

M^{*}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero
(see the remarks following Proposition 4.b.2 in

[
LT77
, p. 148]
)
since the above limit is strictly greater than
1

(here we use that
q
is continuous).

Next let us show that
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{F}}=\infty
.
For
s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
in
h_{M}
we have

\Phi(s_{n}/\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M(1/\lambda)=nM(1/\lambda).

It is well known that the infimum in the norm is attained
so we need only solve
\Phi(s_{n}/\lambda)=1

to get
M(1/\lambda)=1/n
.
For
n\geq 4
we use

M(1/\lambda)=\frac{1}{4}e^{2}e^{-\lambda}
and get

(3)

\|s_{n}\|=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right\|=\lambda=2-2\ln(2)+\ln(n)

From this we see that
\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{F}}=\infty
.

Finally, we show that
h_{M}
is not uniformly strictly monotone
at any coordinate. By 1-symmetry of the basis we need
only consider the first coordinate.

Let
0<d<1/2
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
define

x_{n}=(1-d)e_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}e_{i+1}

where
k
is chosen (depending on
n
) such that
\|x_{n}\|=1
.
Using (
3
) we have

\|x_{n}-e_{1}^{*}(x_{n})e_{1}\|=\frac{1}{k}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i+1}\right\|=\frac{\|s_{n}\|}{k}=\frac{2-2\ln(2)+\ln(n)}{k}.

Let us find
k
.
Solve

\Phi(x_{n})=(1-d)^{2}+\frac{n}{4}e^{2}e^{-k}=1

for
k
to get

e^{-k}=e^{-2}\frac{4}{n}(2d-d^{2})

hence

k=2-2\ln(2)+\ln(n)-\ln(2d-d^{2}).

We therefore have

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-e_{1}^{*}(x_{n})e_{1}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{2-2\ln(2)+\ln(n)}{2-2\ln(2)+\ln(n)-\ln(2d-d^{2})}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\ln(2d-d^{2})}{2-2\ln(2)+\ln(n)-\ln(2d-d^{2})}.

Since
d
is fixed and
0<d<1/2
we have
that
\ln(2d-d^{2})
is a fixed negative number and

\ln(n)
can be made arbitrarily large,
hence
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|x_{n}-e_{1}^{*}(x_{n})e_{1}\|=1
.
It follows that
h_{M}
is not uniformly strictly monotone
at any coordinate.

As proved in
[
HWW93
, III.Examples 1.4]
, if
M
is an Orlicz
function which fails the
\Delta_{2}
-condition at zero, while its
complementary function
M^{*}
satisfies it, then the Orlicz sequence
space
h_{M}
is M-embedded and thus has the LD2P. The same conclusion
holds for the preduals
d(w,1)_{*}
of the Lorentz sequence space

d(w,1)
. Example
4.7
shows that there exist
strictly convex Orlicz sequence spaces
h_{M}
with a 1-symmetric basis
and the LD2P. As for Lorentz sequences spaces
d(w,1),
however,
their preduals
d(w,1)_{*}
are never strictly convex since their
canonical basis is monotone, but not strictly monotone.
We will come back to these preduals once more in the
next section.

Remark 4.8
.

In general,
\mathfrak{L_{F}}=\infty
does not tell us anything
about uniform strict monotonicity at a coordinate or the LD2P.

Any Orlicz space with
M
non-degenerate
(hence
M
strictly increasing)
satisfies
\mathfrak{L_{F}}=\infty
.
The Orlicz space
h_{M}
in Example
4.7

has the LD2P and is not uniform strict monotonicity at any coordinate.
On the other hand,
\ell_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
clearly does not have the LD2P.
If
x\in B_{\ell_{p}}
with
e_{1}^{*}(x)=(1-d)
we have

\|x-e_{1}^{*}(x)e_{1}\|=(1-(1-d)^{p})^{1/p}

which for small
d
is roughly
(pd)^{1/p}

so
\ell_{p}
is uniformly strictly monotone at a coordinate.

Furthermore, the diameter of the slice
S(e_{i}^{*},\delta)

does not depend on the rate at which

\mathfrak{L}_{n}=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\|
grows.
Indeed, for
h_{M}
in Example
4.7
we have

\mathfrak{L}_{n}\approx\ln(n)
,
for
\ell_{p}
we have
\mathfrak{L}_{n}=n^{1/p}
,
while for the predual
d(w,1)_{*}
where
w_{n}=1/n

we have
\mathfrak{L}_{n}\approx n/\ln(n)
.

5.
Strictly convex renormings with the LD2P

In the first part of the paper, our aim was to find properties on
strictly convex solid Banach lattices of real functions, that
prevented such spaces from having the LD2P. In particular, we found
that any strictly convex norm on
c_{0}(\Gamma)
with a 1-symmetric
basis, fails the LD2P. One of the main results in this section, tells
us that this is close to optimal, even in the non-separable case,
since
c_{0}(\Gamma),
for any set
\Gamma,
will be shown to admit a
strictly convex renorming with a 1-unconditional basis and the
LD2P.
In the separable case
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
with the Nakano norm
is such a renorming.
The Nakano renorming of
c_{0}(\mathbb{N})
will play a crucial
role in the proofs below and it will for example allow us to
construct strictly convex renormings with the LD2P of any Banach space
containing complemented copies of
c_{0}(\mathbb{N}).

We start by a general construction of a norm. Let

\varphi:\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})\to[0,\infty]
be the function
given by
\varphi(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{2n}
where
x=(x_{n}).
Let

(X,\|\cdot\|)
be any Banach space. Define the function

\Phi:X^{**}\oplus_{\infty}\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N})\to[0,\infty]
by

(4)

\displaystyle\Phi(z)=\|x\|+\varphi(y),

where
z=(x,y)
and
\|\cdot\|
denotes the canonical norm in the
bidual of
X.
To avoid heavy use of parentheses, we will sometimes
just write
\Phi(x,y)
instead of
\Phi((x,y)),

\|\cdot\|
instead
of
\|(\cdot,\cdot)\|
when
\|\cdot\|
is a norm on

X^{**}\oplus\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N}),
and
Px
instead of
P(x)

when
P
is an operator on
X^{**}.
For

\displaystyle Z_{\infty}

\displaystyle:=(X\oplus_{\infty}c_{0}(\mathbb{N}),\|(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{\infty}),

we have

\displaystyle Z_{\infty}^{**}

\displaystyle=(X^{**}\oplus_{\infty}\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N}),\|(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{\infty}).

We start by collecting some properties of
\Phi.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Phi
be the function given in (
4
).
We have:

(i)

The set

C=\{(x,y)\in X^{**}\oplus_{\infty}\ell_{\infty}(\mathbb{N}):\Phi(x,y)\leq 1\},

is convex, symmetric, and absorbing with
\frac{1}{2}B_{Z_{\infty}^{**}}\subseteq C\subseteq B_{Z_{\infty}^{**}}.

In particular, if
(x,y)\in C,
then
\|(x,y)\|_{\infty}\leq\Phi(x,y).

(ii)

The Minkowski functional on
C
is a norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\cdot,\cdot)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
given by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(x,y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\inf\{\lambda>0:\Phi((x,y)/\lambda)\leq 1\}.

(iii)

\Phi
is continuous on
Z_{\infty}.

(iv)

For each
(x,y)\in Z_{\infty}^{**},

if
\lambda>1,
then

\Phi((x,y)/\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\Phi(x,y).

(v)

For each
(x,y)\in Z_{\infty}
,
if
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(x,y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1
, then
\Phi(x,y)=1
.

Proof.

(i)
.
Since
\|\cdot\|
and
\varphi
are convex and symmetric,
C

is convex and symmetric as well.

If
\|(x,y)\|_{\infty}\leq 1/2,
then

\Phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{2n}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}<1,

so
\frac{1}{2}B_{Z_{\infty}^{**}}\subseteq C
from which we can also
infer that
C
is absorbing.

If
(x,y)\in\lambda C,
then

\frac{1}{\lambda}\|x\|\leq\Phi((x,y)/\lambda)\leq 1,

so
\|x\|\leq\lambda.
Similarly,

\frac{1}{\lambda^{2n}}|y_{n}|^{2n}\leq\varphi(y/\lambda)\leq\Phi((x,y)/\lambda)\leq 1

and
|y_{n}|\leq\lambda
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
In particular, if

\lambda=1,
we get
\|(x,y)\|_{\infty}\leq\Phi(x,y).

(ii)
.
The Minkowski functional on
C
is given by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(x,y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=\inf\{\lambda>0:(x,y)\in\lambda C\},

which means that
(x,y)/\lambda\in C.

The rest follows from
(i)
.

(iii)
.
We have that

(x,y)\to x\to\|x\|

is continuous and that for any
N

(x,y)\to y\to\sum_{n=1}^{N}y_{n}^{2n}

is continuous.
It follows that
\Phi(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous
on a dense subset of
Z_{\infty}
which is enough.

(iv)
.
We have

\Phi((x,y)/\lambda)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\|x\|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(y_{n}/\lambda)^{2n}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\Phi(x,y).

(v)
.
Let
z=(x,y)\in Z_{\infty}
with
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(x,y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1
.
By
(iii)

\Phi
is continuous on
Z_{\infty}

so that

\Phi(z)=\lim_{\lambda\to 1^{+}}\Phi(z/\lambda)\leq 1.

Assume for contradiction that
\Phi(z)<1
.

Find
z^{*}=(x^{*},y^{*})
with
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(x^{*},y^{*})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1

such that

z^{*}(z)=x^{*}(x)+y^{*}(y)=1.

Note that
x^{*}(x)\geq 0
and
y^{*}(y)\geq 0.
Indeed, if

x^{*}(x)<0,
then since
\Phi(-x,y)=\Phi(x,y)\leq 1
we have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(-x,y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 1,
while

x^{*}(-x)+y^{*}(y)>x^{*}(x)+y^{*}(y)=1.
Similarly, we get a
contradiction if
y^{*}(y)<0.

Consider first the case where
y^{*}(y)>0.

We can write

y=(y_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
y^{*}=(y_{n}^{*})_{n=1}^{\infty}
.
Note that
|y_{n}|<1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Choose
k\in\mathbb{N}

such that
y_{k}^{*}\cdot y_{k}\neq 0
and find
t>|y_{k}|
with

\Phi(z)+t^{2k}-(y_{k})^{2k}\leq 1.

Define
w=(x,(v_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty})
where

v_{n}=y_{n}
for
n\neq k
and
v_{k}=t\cdot\sgn(y_{k}^{*})
.
Since

\Phi(w)=\Phi(z)+t^{2k}-(y_{k})^{2k}\leq 1,

we get
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 1.

On the other hand

z^{*}(w)-z^{*}(z)=|y_{k}^{*}|\cdot t-y_{k}^{*}\cdot y_{k}>0.

So
1\geq z^{*}(w)>z^{*}(z)=1
, which is a contradiction.

Consider now the case
x^{*}(x)>0.
Choose
t>1
such that

\Phi(z)+(t-1)\|x\|\leq 1.

Define
w=(tx,y).
Since

\Phi(w)=\Phi(z)+(t-1)\|x\|\leq 1,

we get
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq 1.

On the other hand

z^{*}(w)-z^{*}(z)=tx^{*}(x)+y^{*}(y)-(x^{*}(x)+y^{*}(y))=(t-1)x^{*}(x)>0.

So
1\geq z^{*}(w)>z^{*}(z)=1
, which is a contradiction.

We conclude that
\Phi(x,y)=1
as desired.
∎

Assume for the moment that our
X
is a strictly convex Banach space
and has a normalized shrinking 1-unconditional basis

(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}.

Note that for
e_{\gamma}\in X
and
e_{n}\in c_{0}(\mathbb{N}),
we have

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(e_{\gamma},0)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(0,e_{n})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\Gamma\cup\mathbb{N}
and define by

f_{\alpha}=(e_{\gamma},0)
when
\alpha=\gamma\in\Gamma
and

f_{\alpha}=(0,e_{n})
when
\alpha=n\in\mathbb{N}.
Since

(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a shrinking 1-unconditional basis for

c_{0}(\mathbb{N}),
we get by a standard argument that

(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
is a shrinking 1-unconditional
basis for
Z_{\infty}.

Note that if
(x,y),(u,v)\in Z_{\infty}^{**}
and
|(x,y)|\leq|(u,v)|
,
meaning

|x_{\gamma}|\leq|u_{\gamma}|
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma

and

|y_{n}|\leq|v_{n}|
for all

,
then
\Phi(x,y)\leq\Phi(u,v)
.

In the presence of a 1-unconditional basis

\Phi
will have some additional properties.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
X
is a strictly convex Banach space with a
normalized shrinking 1-unconditional basis
(e_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma},

then the function
\Phi
given in (
4
)
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For each
(x,y)\in Z_{\infty}^{**},
we have

\Phi(x,y)=\sup_{G}\Phi(P_{G}^{**}(x,y)),

where
P_{G}:Z_{\infty}^{**}\to Z_{\infty}^{**}
denotes the natural
projection onto the finite dimensional subspace

\linspan(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in G}\subset Z_{\infty}^{**}
and where

G
is a finite subset of
\mathcal{A}.

(ii)

For each
(x,y)\in Z_{\infty}^{**},

if
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(x,y)\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=1,
then
\Phi(x,y)\leq 1.

Proof.

(i)
.
Let

\mathcal{F}=\{F\subset\Gamma:|F|<\infty\}
and

\mathcal{G}=\{G\in\mathcal{A}:|G|<\infty\}
. Order

\mathcal{F}
by set inclusion and
\mathcal{G}
by the rule

F_{1}\times\{1,\ldots N_{1}\}\leq F_{2}\times\{1,\ldots N_{2}\}
if

F_{1}\subseteq F_{2}
and
N_{1}\leq N_{2}.
Since

(e_{\gamma}^{*})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
is a shrinking 1-unconditional
basis for
Z_{\infty}
and

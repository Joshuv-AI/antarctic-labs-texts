Single-cell transcriptomic profiling of patient-derived pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma primary cell cultures
Authors: Vladimir Chocoloff, Alex Chauvin, Brice Chanez, Loïc Moubri, Julie Roques, Philippe Soubeyran, Juan Iovanna, Nelson Dusetti, Nicolas Fraunhoffer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Single-cell RNA sequencing is a powerful approach for characterising cellular heterogeneity and elucidating transcriptional programs that drive tumour plasticity, therapeutic resistance, and disease progression. In this study, we present a single-cell transcriptomic dataset comprising 41 patient-derived cell cultures (PDCs) established from pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). The dataset was generated using Evercode™ technology, which is based on Split Pool Ligation-based Transcriptome sequencing (SPLiT-seq). This scalable method enables high-throughput profiling across multiple samples without droplet-based microfluidics, allowing efficient capture of inter-sample heterogeneity. This functionally annotated collection of experimentally derived models reveals transcriptional heterogeneity both within and across PDCs, enabling in-depth exploration of PDAC cell diversity, to support the development of computational tools for single-cell analysis, and to guide functional studies on tumour cell subpopulations. This resource constitutes a valuable reference for computational and experimental studies aiming to decipher PDAC heterogeneity and identify therapeutic vulnerabilities.

Background & Summary

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a highly aggressive malignancy, currently the twelfth most common cancer and the sixth leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide1. Despite advances in oncology, the 5-year survival rate remains only 11%, and due to its increasing incidence, PDAC is expected to become the second leading cause of cancer mortality by 20302. This poor prognosis is primarily attributed to late-stage diagnosis and limited efficacy of current therapeutic options, including chemotherapy and radiotherapy3.

A major obstacle in the management of PDAC is the extensive intratumoural heterogeneity of malignant epithelial cells, which shapes tumour behaviour, treatment response, and clinical outcome4. Understanding these cellular programs are essential for improving patient stratification and designing effective therapeutic interventions5.

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) enables transcriptomic analysis at single-cell resolution, providing a powerful approach to investigate cell-intrinsic mechanisms that drive phenotypic diversity. This is particularly relevant in PDAC, where elucidating epithelial cell states and transitions may inform precision oncology strategies6. However, a key limitation in PDAC research is the scarcity of representative preclinical models that reflect patient diversity, particularly in advanced disease stages7. Most transcriptomic studies to date rely on resected tumours, overlooking the broader spectrum of tumour heterogeneity, particularly in advanced disease. Patient-derived cell cultures (PDCs) established from patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) offer an accessible and scalable platform to study PDAC biology. When carefully developed, these models can retain essential molecular and cellular heterogeneity. Their proper characterisation is critical to ensure biological validity and reproducibility in downstream applications, including the development of predictive transcriptomic signatures for patient stratification and therapeutic decision-making8–10.

Here, we present a comprehensive single-cell transcriptomic resource derived from 41 PDCs, offering a bridge between molecular profiling and biological validation. This dataset complements previous analyses of drug response11, transcriptional profiling7,12, epigenetic regulation13, and metabolism14 performed on these PDCs. The samples encompass the full spectrum of PDAC clinical stages in 40 samples, with 37.5% (n = 15) classified as localised, 15.0% (n = 6) as locally advanced, and 47.5% (n = 19) as metastatic (Table 1). Among the metastatic cases, some originated from distant sites, including liver metastases and peritoneal carcinomatosis. In total, 85.4% of samples were obtained from primary tumours and 14.6% from metastases, collected through either biopsy or surgical resection (Table 1). To capture transcriptional heterogeneity, we used Parse Biosciences’ combinatorial barcoding (EvercodeTM) based on Split Pool Ligation-based Transcriptome sequencing (SPLiT-seq)15, which enables high-throughput single-cell analysis compatible with cell fixation. SPLiT-seq was chosen over droplet-based technologies such as 10X Genomics due to its scalability and cost-effectiveness for large sample cohorts. Its compatibility with fixed material enabled parallel processing of multiple samples and minimised technical variation between simultaneously processed samples (Fig. 1b).

After quality control, we provide a before QC dataset encompassing 30,593 cells (Fig. 1c). While we acknowledge certain limitations of SPLiT-seq technology, particularly regarding mitochondrial content and sequencing saturation, this dataset represents a unique and valuable resource. It offers broad coverage of the disease spectrum, supports the evaluation of in-vitro model fidelity, and provides a benchmark for the development of bioinformatics tools tailored to SPLiT-seq data.

Altogether, this study presents one of the most comprehensive single-cell characterisations of patient-derived PDAC cultures to date, supporting future efforts to dissect tumour cell diversity and therapeutic vulnerabilities. Making this resource publicly available enables the community to benchmark computational tools, investigate model fidelity, and leverage these cultures in experimental settings and develop new hypotheses about PDAC biology from a single-cell perspective.

Methods

Culture media

PDCs were cultured using one of three different media formulations: Serum-Free Ductal Medium (SFDM), Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) with GlutaMAX (Gibco, 61965-026) supplemented with 10% Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS, Sigma F7524), or a 1:1 mixture of both (Table 1). SFDM was adapted from Schreiber et al.16 and consisted of DMEM/F-12 supplemented with HEPES (Gibco, 31330-038), 10 mM nicotinamide (Alfa Aesar, A15970), 5 mg/mL glucose (Euromedex, UG3050), 5 mL/L ITS + (Corning, 354352), 5% NuSerum IV (Corning, 355504), 25 µg/mL Bovine Pituitary Extract (BPE, Gibco, 13028-014), 20 ng/mL Epidermal Growth Factor (EGF, Corning, 354001), 50 nM triiodothyronine (T3, Sigma, T2877), 1 µM dexamethasone (Sigma, D1756), and 100 ng/mL cholera toxin (CT, Sigma, C8052).

Establishment and cryopreservation of patient-derived cell cultures

Forty-one PDCs were established from PDAC samples collected as part of the PaCaOmics clinical trial (NCT01692873). Three of these PDCs required Matrigel coating (Corning, 356237), while the others were cultured directly in standard tissue culture flasks, as detailed in Table 1. In addition, different culture media were used depending on the growth requirements of each PDC during the initial derivation (Table 1). Serum-Free Ductal Medium (SFDM) was used for 35 PDCs, 2 PDCs were grown in DMEM supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum (FBS), and 4 PDCs were cultured in a 1:1 combination of SFDM and DMEM. Cultures were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂, and the culture medium was refreshed every 3 to 4 days. Once confluent, cells were dissociated using a digestion solution consisting of PBS with 5% StemPro Accutase (Gibco, A11105-01) and 0.5 mM EDTA. Detached cells were then collected, washed with DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, centrifuged at 300 g for 3 minutes, and resuspended for counting using Invitrogen Countess 3. Cells were expanded to approximately 40 million per culture and cryopreserved in multiple vials, each containing 2 million cells.

Single-cell transcriptomic profiling

To perform scRNA-seq, PDCs were cultured in two batches, 14 samples in the first batch (R1) and 27 samples in the second batch (R2). Following thawing, each PDC was cultured for a maximum of two passages over approximately two weeks. At that point, cells at 60–70% confluency were harvested, and aliquots of 500,000 cells were pelleted, resuspended in 500 µL of freezing medium (FBS + 10% DMSO; Sigma, D8418), counted, and cryopreserved at −80 °C using a Corning CoolCell, for subsequent single-cell transcriptomic analysis. Frozen samples were processed using the Evercode™ Cell Fixation v2 kit (Parse Biosciences, ECF2101) and subsequently barcoded and prepared for sequencing according to the manufacturer’s workflow provided with the Evercode™ Whole Transcriptome v2 kit (Parse Biosciences, ECW02130).

The SPLiT-seq protocol applies three rounds of combinatorial indexing to uniquely label individual cells. Briefly, for each PDC, approximately 2,000 fixed cells were used as input. Cells were first distributed into 48 wells for reverse transcription, during which the first molecular tag was incorporated. They were then pooled and redistributed twice into 96-well plates for the sequential ligation of the second and third indexes. After this indexing process, cells were evenly divided into sublibraries and lysed. The uniquely tagged cDNA molecules were isolated using a streptavidin-coated magnetic bead pulldown, followed by PCR amplification. The cDNA was then enzymatically fragmented, ligated to sequencing adapters, and assigned a sublibrary-specific fourth index during the final PCR step.

Data processing

FASTQ file quality control (QC), barcode correction, alignment to the human genome (GRCh38.p13), multiplet detection, and raw count matrix generation were performed using the split-pipe pipeline (v1.1.1, Parse Biosciences) with the default parameters. The raw count matrices were subsequently loaded into R using the Seurat package (v5.3.0)17. To account for potential batch-specific variation, QC filtering was applied independently to each batch. Initially, duplicated barcodes were removed, retaining only one per cell. Then, for batch R1, cells with a total of expressed genes between 2,000 and 10,000 were retained. Concomitantly, for batch R2, cells with expressed genes between 1,500 and 9,000 were retained.

Cells with high percentage of mitochondrial RNA (mtRNA) reads were filtered out using a data-driven approach implemented in the miQC18 R package. For representation purposes, raw counts were normalised using log normalisation with a scale factor of 10,000. The top 2,000 features were selected based on variance-stabilising transformation, and the data was subsequently scaled. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed on the selected features. Harmony16 integration was applied using the first 37 principal components, which explained 90% of the variance in the dataset.

The PDAC Molecular Gradient (PAMG)12 is a stratification score that assigns each transcriptome to a continuous spectrum ranging from classical (high scores) to basal-like (low scores), with intermediate states represented along the gradient. PAMG scores are computed by weighing the expression of a set of master regulators (transcriptional factors) that define classical and basal-like subtypes. PAMG was calculated for each cell and standardised as z-scores to facilitate comparison between and within samples. It should be noted that PAMG is not the only stratification method available for PDAC, other approaches, such as PurIST19, have also been described.

Data Records

The final dataset has been deposited in the ArrayExpress repository and is publicly accessible (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/arrayexpress/studies/E-MTAB-16856)20. This file contains 30,593 cells after the removal of duplicated barcodes and quality control filtering based on mtRNA percentage and number of expressed genes. It includes raw counts, normalised counts. The metadata consist of 14 columns, which are described in the provided file “metadata_elements_description.txt”, also deposited in the repository.

Technical Validation

Library quality control was performed using the Qubit 4 fluorometer with the dsDNA HS Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Q32854), and the Bioanalyzer 2100 with the High Sensitivity DNA Kit (Agilent Technologies, 5067-4626), to assess cDNA concentration and fragment size, respectively. Final libraries had an average fragment size of 432 bp (±24 bp) and a concentration of 27 ng/µL (±5 ng/µL). Sequencing was performed separately for each culture batch using the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus platform at the iGenSeq facility. Paired-end reads were generated using a 10B flow cell with 200 sequencing cycles.These results support the scalability and flexibility of SPLiT-seq for performing scRNA-seq in primary cell cultures, despite differences in culture times and conditions. However, inter-batch variability related to the handling and library preparation workflow remains a challenge for this system.

Additionally, in this dataset, the PDCs were cultured and sequenced in two batches with different numbers of samples, which could represent a potential source of bias in downstream analyses and biological interpretation. Despite the mentioned limitations, this dataset offers a single-cell-level view of phenotypic heterogeneity across well-characterised, patient-derived PDAC cultures. It also highlights potential biases introduced by in-vitro culture conditions, such as the high prevalence of basal-like profiles, which may not fully reflect the composition of primary tumours. This phenotypic bias is not exclusive to PDCs and has also been described in patient-derived organoids (PDOs)7, which often exhibit a classical transcriptional profile. Overall, the complementary use of preclinical models represents the most effective approach to capture meaningful biological features of PDAC tumors.

Iitaka fibrations and integral points: a family of arbitrarily polarized spherical threefolds
Ulrich Derenthal, Florian Wilsch

Abstract
. Studying Manin’s program for a family of spherical log Fano threefolds, we determine the asymptotic number of integral points whose height associated with an arbitrary ample line bundle is bounded. This confirms a recent conjecture by Santens and sheds new light on the logarithmic analogue of Iitaka fibrations, which have not yet been adequately formulated.

11D45 (14G05, 14M27, 11G35)

1.
Introduction

How often is a
(2\times 2)
-determinant a perfect power?
A first step in treating such a Diophantine problem is to study geometric properties of the varieties it describes. In this case, solutions
correspond to points on the family

X^{\prime}_{n}=\left\{\det\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}=z^{n+1}\right\}\subset\mathds{P}_{\mathds{Q}}(1,n,1,n,1)

of subvarieties of a weighted projective space. These are Fano varieties, which tend to have many rational points.
For
n\geq 2
, they are singular, and desingularizations
\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X^{\prime}_{n}
are obtained by blowing up the singular locus
V(a,c,z)
. Moreover, both the varieties
X^{\prime}_{n}
and
X_{n}
are
spherical
, admitting an action by the algebraic group
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
(Section
2.2
).

Manin’s program
[
11
]
asks a precise quantitative question: how many rational points of bounded anticanonical height does such a variety contain? For the singular family
X^{\prime}_{n}
, this problem was solved by the first author and Gagliardi
[
8
, Thm. 1.2]
.
As
\pi_{n}
is not crepant if
n\geq 3
, their interpretation of the asymptotic formula rests on a theory by Batyrev and Tschinkel
[
1
]
, who provide a framework to analyze asymptotic formulas for the number of rational points of bounded height with respect to height functions induced by arbitrary ample (or at least big and nef) line bundles
L
.
In particular, the leading constant of their formula involves a sum over the fibers of the
Iitaka fibration
associated with the bundle
\pi_{n}^{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}_{n}}^{\vee}
on
X_{n}
.

For integral points, a program similar to that kick-started by Manin is underway: see
[
18
,
3
,
20
]
and the references therein for contributions and work of Santens
[
16
, § 6]
for a current conjecture.
Notably, there is not yet a logarithmic analogue to the Iitaka fibrations appearing in the work of Batyrev and Tschinkel
[
1
]
(originally dubbed
\mathcal{L}
-primitive fibrations by them).
The aim of this article is to start filling in this gap by studying the integral analogue of Manin’s problem with respect to arbitrary polarizations on open log Fano subvarieties of
X_{n}
. The equation defining
X_{n}
defines the model

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}_{n}=\{ad-bc=z^{n+1}\}\subset\mathds{P}_{\mathds{Z}}(1,n,1,n,1).

A model
\pi_{n}\colon\mathfrak{X}_{n}\to\mathfrak{X}_{n}^{\prime}
of the desingularization can be obtained by blowing up
V_{\mathfrak{X}}(a,c,z)
.

Of particular interest are those subvarieties that are still spherical. There are two
G
-invariant effective divisors
D
with strict normal crossings on each
X_{n}
such that
(X_{n},D)
is log Fano, that is, such that the log anticanonical bundle
\omega_{X_{n}}(D)^{\vee}
is ample. As the variety
X_{n}
itself is not Fano, the empty divisor is not among these two. Theorem
1.3
provides an asymptotic formula for the number of integral points
x
outside any such
D
such that a height function
H_{L}
associated with an arbitrary ample
\mathds{Q}
-divisor
L
is bounded by a parameter
B
tending to infinity.
This result confirms the conjecture formulated by Santens
[
16
]
; in those cases in which he conjectures one, this includes his leading constant.

Some cases (those that are not
adjoint rigid
) fall beyond the scope of his conjecture in terms of the leading constant. We observe that the fibrations appearing in these cases are completely analogous to those for rational points and believe this to be a general phenomenon as long as the analytic Clemens complex is a simplex, that is, as long as all geometric components of the boundary divisor share a real point: in this case, the variants of the Picard group pertinent to the study of integral points coincide with the usual one
[
16
]
*Def. 3.6, Lem. 3.10
[
20
]
*Def. 2.2.1, Rmk. 2.2.2.

1.1.
Iitaka fibrations in the integral Manin problem

Let
\mathcal{X}=(\mathfrak{X},D,L)
be a
polarized log Fano variety
consisting of a flat proper
\mathds{Z}
-scheme
\mathfrak{X}
whose generic fiber
X=\mathfrak{X}_{\mathds{Q}}
is smooth and projective, a divisor
D
on
X
with geometrically strict normal crossings such that the log anticanonical class
-K_{X}-D
is ample, and an ample metrized line bundle
L
on
X
. Let
H_{L}\colon X(\mathds{Q})\to\mathds{R}_{>0}
be the height function induced by
L
.

In this setting, Santens
[
16
, § 6]
defines constants
a(\mathcal{X})
and
b(\mathcal{X})
and conjectures that, under suitable hypotheses, there is a thin set
Z\subset X(\mathds{Q})
such that the number

(1.1)

N_{\mathcal{X},Z}(B):=\#\{x\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{Z})\setminus Z\mid H_{L}(x)\leq B\}

of integral points on
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{X}\setminus\overline{D}
of bounded height grows asymptotically like

(1.2)

c(\mathcal{X})B^{a(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{b(\mathcal{X})-1}

for some positive constant
c(\mathcal{X})
as
B\to\infty
.

If all geometric components of
D
are defined over
\mathds{R}
and share a real point, these constants admit the following description: let

a(\mathcal{X})=\inf\{t\in\mathds{R}\mid K_{X}+D+tL\in\Eff(X)\}

be the inverse of the Kodaira energy,

E_{\mathcal{X}}=K_{X}+D+a(\mathcal{X})L\in\Pic(X)_{\mathds{Q}}

the adjoint bundle and

b(\mathcal{X})
be the codimension of the minimal face of the effective cone containing
E_{\mathcal{X}}
. Under these assumptions, the polarized log variety
\mathcal{X}
is said to be adjoint rigid if the adjoint bundle
E_{\mathcal{X}}

is rigid, that is, if
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(rE_{\mathcal{X}}))
is one-dimensional for all
r\in\mathds{Q}_{>0}
such that
rE_{\mathcal{X}}
is integral.
In this case, Santens provides a conjecture for the leading constant
c(\mathcal{X})
in (
1.2
) that is similar to the leading constant in Manin’s conjecture by Peyre
[
14
]
and Batyrev–Tschinkel
[
1
]
.
Whenever this is not the case, we believe that the leading constant
c(\mathcal{X})
can be described in terms of the standard Iitaka fibration

\phi_{(X,L)}\colon X\dashrightarrow W=\Proj\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(kdE_{\mathcal{X}}))\right),

where
d
be a positive integer such that
dE_{\mathcal{X}}\in\Pic(X)
— the target space is a point if and only if
E_{\mathcal{X}}
is rigid.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Assume that the set
\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{Z})
of integral points is not thin.
Let
\phi=\phi_{(X,L)}
be the Iitaka fibration associated with
L
,
induced by its adjoint bundle
E_{\mathcal{X}}
.
For each
t\in W(\mathds{Q})
for which the fiber
\phi^{-1}(t)
is defined, let
X_{t}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{t}
be its closures in
X
and
\mathfrak{X}
, respectively.

Then there are thin sets
Z\subset X(\mathds{Q})
and
Y\subset W(\mathds{Q})
such that for every
t\in W(\mathds{Q})\setminus Z
, the fiber
\mathcal{X}_{t}=(\mathfrak{X}_{t},D\cap X_{t},L|_{X_{t}})
is adjoint rigid with
a(\mathcal{X}_{t})=a(\mathcal{X})
and
b(\mathcal{X}_{t})=b(\mathcal{X})
, such that the sum

c(\mathcal{X})=\sum_{t\in W(\mathds{Q})\setminus Y}c(\mathcal{X}_{t})

converges, and such that

N_{\mathcal{X},Z}(B)\sim c(\mathcal{X})B^{a(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{b(\mathcal{X})-1}.

Remark 1.2
.

Under these assumptions, the real component of the fibration Santens obtains while counting integral points on toric varieties
[
16
, Eq. (35)]
coincides with the construction by Batyrev and Tschinkel
[
1
, p. 332]
after replacing the canonical by the log canonical bundle in the latter. Therefore, it coincides with the Iitaka fibrations discussed above.

1.2.
Integral points on the spherical family

Turning back to the spherical family
X_{n}
for
n\geq 2
and the polarized log Fano varieties
\mathcal{X}=(\mathfrak{X}_{n},D,L)
with a
G
-invariant divisor
D
and an ample line bundle
L
that is metrized as in Section
2.4
, the Iitaka
fibration
\phi:X_{n}\to\mathds{P}^{k}_{\mathds{Q}}
is in fact a morphism, with
k\in\{0,2\}
, depending on whether the adjoint bundle is rigid or not (Section
2.3
). For each
t\in\mathds{P}^{k}(\mathds{Q})
, let
X_{n,t}=\phi^{-1}(t)
be the fiber.

By constructions of Chambert-Loir–Tschinkel
[
3
]
and Santens
[
16
]
, for each fiber
\mathcal{X}_{t}
, the metric on
L
induces a Tamagawa measure
\tau_{\mathcal{X}}
on
X_{n,t}(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})
— a product of measures concentrated on
\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{Z}_{p})\cap X_{n,t}(\mathds{Q}_{p})
with convergence factors for finite primes
p
and a
residue measure
concentrated on a subset of
X_{n,t}(\mathds{R})
(Section
3
). Finally, let
\alpha(\mathcal{X}_{t})
be the
\alpha
-constant associated with each fiber (Sections
3.4
–
3.5
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be one of the polarized spherical log Fano threefolds described above. The number of integral points of bounded height satisfies

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=c(\mathcal{X})B^{a(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{b(\mathcal{X})-1}(1+O_{\mathcal{X}}(1/\log B)),

for
B\geq 2
with

c(\mathcal{X})=\sum_{t\in\mathds{P}^{k}(\mathds{Q})}\frac{\alpha(\mathcal{X}_{t})}{a(\mathcal{X})}\tau_{\mathcal{X}_{t}}(X_{n,t}(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}})).

In fact, we obtain a power-saving error term (as in Proposition
4.5
) whenever
b(\mathcal{X})=1
(that is, whenever the adjoint bundle
E_{\mathcal{X}}
is nonzero). In particular, this confirms Conjecture
1.1
for this family. The exponents and leading constants are completely explicit. The former are described in (
2.3
,
2.4
,
2.9
). In the
adjoint rigid
case, the fibration
\phi
is in fact a constant morphism. The leading constant is associated with its unique fiber: the
\alpha
-constant is described in (
3.9
,
3.10
). The Tamagawa volume decomposes as

\tau_{\mathcal{X}}(X_{n,t}(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}))=\prod_{v}\omega_{\mathcal{X},v};

its archimedean factor is described in (
3.4
,
3.5
,
3.6
) and contains the height function (
2.10
) its denominator, and its
p
-adic factors are described in (
3.8
).
Whenever the adjoint bundle is
moving
, the Iitaka fibration is described in (
2.8
). The leading constant associated with each fiber is described in (
4.8
).

The ample line bundle
L
(the polarization of
X
) associated with the height function is arbitrary. Such general height functions have mostly been studied using an approach that analyzes the height zeta function, exploiting an almost transitive action of an algebraic group; for integral points, this notably includes work by Takloo-Bighash, Tschinkel
[
17
]
, and Chow
[
5
]
on wonderful compactifications and by Santens
[
16
]
on toric varieties (using harmonic analysis on their universal torsors). For varieties without such a special structure, the torsor method has previously only been used to count rational or integral points of bounded (log) anticanonical height; see
[
19
,
9
,
13
]
for applications in the integral setting.

2.
A family of spherical threefolds

We start by collecting some facts on the family
X_{n}
. Throughout, let
n\geq 2
be an integer. The Picard group of the desingularization
\pi_{n}\colon X_{n}\to X^{\prime}_{n}
is
\Pic X_{n}\cong\mathds{Z}^{2}
, generated by the strict transform of the coordinate plane
V(z)
and the exceptional divisor.

2.1.
Cox rings and universal torsors

The Cox ring of
X_{n}
and combinatorial data associated with it have been computed by the first author and Gagliardi
[
8
, §2]
using constructions by Brion
[
2
]
and Gagliardi
[
12
]
; it is

R(X_{n})=\mathds{Q}[a,b,c,d,z,w]/(ad-bc-z^{n+1}w),

and its grading is given by

\deg(a)=\deg(c)=\deg(z)=(1,0),\ \deg(b)=\deg(d)=(n,1),\ \text{and}\ \deg(w)=(0,1)

so that the relation has degree
(n+1,1)
. Hence, the anticanonical class is

-K_{X_{n}}=(n+2,2)

by the adjunction formula. As

\pi_{n}^{*}(-K_{X^{\prime}_{n}})=(n+2,1+2/n)=\frac{n+2}{n}(n,1),

the resolution
\pi_{n}
is not crepant if
n\geq 3
.

To construct a universal torsor,
X_{n}
can be represented as a hypersurface in the toric variety
Z_{n}
that is obtained by blowing up the singular locus of
Z_{n}^{\prime}=\mathds{P}_{\mathds{Q}}(1,n,1,n,1)
. The fan of
Z_{n}^{\prime}
consists of all cones spanned by at most four of the vectors

u_{a}=e_{1},\ u_{b}=e_{2},\ u_{c}=e_{3},\ u_{d}=e_{4},\ \text{and}\ u_{z}=-e_{1}-ne_{2}-e_{3}-ne_{4}

in
\mathds{Z}^{4}\cong\mathds{Z}^{5}/\mathds{Z}(1,n,1,n,1)
. The singular locus corresponds to the cone spanned by
u_{a},u_{c},u_{z}
, and subdividing it by adding the ray generated by
u_{w}=(u_{a}+u_{c}+u_{z})/n
yields the fan
\Sigma_{n}
of
Z_{n}
. One can check that the maximal cones of
Z_{n}
are generated by any two of
u_{a},u_{c},u_{z}
together with any two of
u_{b},u_{d},u_{w}
. Hence, the irrelevant ideal is generated by the products of one of
a,c,z
and one of
b,d,w
.

The spherical variety
X_{n}
is the hypersurface defined by
ad-bc-z^{n+1}w
in
Z_{n}
, and its integral model
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
is described by the same equation in the toric scheme
\mathfrak{Z}_{n}
over
\mathds{Z}
described by the same fan
[
15
]
*p. 187
[
6
]
, the blow-up of
\mathds{P}_{\mathds{Z}}(1,n,1,n,1)
in
V(a,c,z)
. Salberger
[
15
, Remark 8.6b]
constructs universal torsors
\mathds{A}^{6}\setminus(V(a,c,z)\cup V(b,d,w))
over
\mathds{Q}
and
\mathds{Z}
of this toric variety and scheme. Restricting these to the hypersurfaces
X_{n}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
results in the universal torsor

\rho\colon\Spec R(X_{n})\setminus(V(a,c,z)\cup V(b,d,w))=Y_{n}\to X_{n}

and its model
\rho\colon\mathfrak{Y}_{n}\to\mathfrak{X}_{n}
with

\mathfrak{Y}_{n}=\Spec(\mathds{Z}[a,b,c,d,z,w]/(ad-bc-z^{n+1}w))\setminus(V(a,c,z)\cup V(b,d,w)).

As in
[
8
]
, rational points
x
on
X_{n}
can thus be represented in
Cox coordinates
, that is, as an equivalence class
x=(a:b:c:d:z:w)
of rational numbers
a
,
b
,
c
,
d
,
z
, and
w
satisfying

(a,c,z),(b,d,w)\neq(0,0,0)

and
ad-bc=z^{n+1}w
that share an orbit under the action of
\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}(\mathds{Q})=(\mathds{Q}^{\times})^{2}
encoded in the grading of the Cox ring.
Alternatively, and more conveniently for counting purposes, they are represented by
integers
that, in addition to the equation, satisfy the coprimality condition

(2.1)

\gcd(a,c,z)=\gcd(b,d,w)=1,

with their equivalence being induced by the action of the subgroup
\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}(\mathds{Z})=\{\pm 1\}^{2}
. The former representation is that of a point on
X_{n}
by an orbit in
Y_{n}(\mathds{Q})
, the latter by an orbit in
\mathfrak{Y}_{n}(\mathds{Z})
.

2.2.
Admissible boundaries

Our next aim is to identify
G
-invariant divisors
D
with strict normal crossings such that the log anticanonical bundle is ample. Recall from
[
8
]
that the action of
G=\mathrm{SL}_{2}\times\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
on
X_{n}^{\prime}
is defined by

\left(\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},t\right)\cdot\left(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix},z\right)=\left(\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\
0&t^{-1}\end{pmatrix},z\right).

Observe that the only
G
-invariant prime divisors are
D_{z}=V(z)
and
D_{w}=V(w)
; neither of the classes
-K_{X_{n}}=(n+2,2)
and
-K_{X_{n}}-[D_{z}]=(n+1,2)
are ample (or even nef if
n\geq 3
), while both
-K_{X_{n}}-[D_{w}]
and
-K_{X_{n}}-[D_{w}]-[D_{z}]
are.

Hence,
D
is
G
-invariant and
-K_{X}-D
ample if and only if

(2.2)

D\in\{D_{w},D_{w}+D_{z}\}.

For a fixed such divisor, denote by
U=X_{n}\setminus D
its complement, by
\mathfrak{D}=\overline{D}
its closure in
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
, and by
\mathfrak{U}=\mathfrak{X}_{n}\setminus\mathfrak{D}
the induced model.

Remark 2.1
.

The varieties
X^{\prime}_{n}
,
X_{n}
, and
X_{n}\setminus D_{w}
are (partial) equivariant compactifications of
\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{3}
. Indeed, a
\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{3}
-action on
X_{n}^{\prime}
is given by

(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\cdot(a:b:c:d:z)=(a^{\prime}:b^{\prime}:c^{\prime}:d^{\prime}:z^{\prime})

where
a^{\prime}=a
,
b^{\prime}=b+t_{1}a^{n}
,
c^{\prime}=c+t_{2}a
,
z^{\prime}=z+t_{3}a
, and

d^{\prime}=d+t_{1}t_{2}a^{n}+t_{1}a^{n-1}c+t_{2}b+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\tbinom{n+1}{i}t_{3}^{n-i+1}a^{n-i}z^{i},

which is
(z^{\prime n+1}+b^{\prime}c^{\prime})/a^{\prime}
if
a\neq 0
. It fixes the singular locus
V(a,c,z)\cong\mathds{P}^{1}_{\mathds{Q}}
and extends to the desingularization
X_{n}
by letting
\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{3}
act trivially on the second factor of the exceptional divisor
D_{w}
, which turns out to be isomorphic to
V(a,c,z)\times\mathds{P}^{1}_{\mathds{Q}}
.
Hence, for the boundary divisor
D=D_{w}
, the asymptotic number of integral points on
U
of bounded log anticanonical height is known by work of Chambert-Loir and Tschinkel
[
4
]
;
however, the case of general ample height functions remained open so far.

The varieties
X_{n}\setminus(D_{w}\cup D_{z})
, on the other hand, have no such structure as a partial equivariant compactification of a vector group. Indeed, if one of them were a compactificaton of
\mathds{A}^{3}
, the inclusion
\mathds{A}^{3}\to X_{n}\setminus(D_{w}\cup D_{z})
of the open orbit would spread out to one over sufficiently large primes, leading to at least
p^{3}
points modulo
p
away from the divisors for such primes, more than the actual number
p^{3}-p
of such points obtained in (
3.7
).

Remark 2.2
.

The divisors
V(a)
and
V(b)
are not invariant under
G
, and hence their complement is not spherical.
As they are invariant under a Borel subgroup, they still seem to be natural candidates for components of
D
.
Nevertheless, no combinations of
V(a)
,
V(b)
, and possibly
D_{z}
and
D_{w}
that have strict normal crossings result in an ample log anticanonical class.
On the singular variety
X_{n}^{\prime}
, the empty divisor results in an ample (log) anticanonical class.
As the Picard group of
X_{n}^{\prime}
is one-dimensional, all height functions are equivalent to a power of the anticanonical one; hence, this case is completely treated by
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
.
The only
G
-invariant prime divisor on
X^{\prime}_{n}
is
V_{X^{\prime}_{n}}(z)
, whose preimage is
D_{w}+D_{z}
, a divisor that is included in our analysis.

2.3.
Polarizations

The nef cone of
X_{n}
is spanned by the degrees
(n,1)
and
(1,0)
of generators of the Cox ring that can easily be verified to correspond to base point free divisors. In particular, the ample cone is its interior

\Ample(X_{n})=\Cone((n,1),(0,1))^{\circ}.

Hence, any ample
\mathds{Q}
-divisor
L
has the shape

L=L_{(l_{1},l_{2})}=(l_{1}+nl_{2},l_{2})\quad\text{for $(l_{1},l_{2})\in\mathds{Q}_{>0}^{2}$.}

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X_{n},D,L)
be a triple consisting of one of the spherical varieties
X_{n}
with
n\geq 2
, one of the
G
-invariant divisors
D
obtained in (
2.2
), and an ample line bundle
L
as above.
The opposite

a(\mathcal{X})=\inf\{t\in\mathds{R}\mid K_{X_{n}}+D+tL\in\Eff(X_{n})\}

of the Kodaira energy is readily computed as

(2.3)

a(\mathcal{X})=\max\left\{\frac{1}{l_{2}},\frac{n+2}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}\right\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{l_{2}}&\text{if }l_{1}>2l_{2},\\
\frac{n+2}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}&\text{if }l_{1}\leq 2l_{2},\\
\end{cases}

if
D=D_{w}
, and

(2.4)

a(\mathcal{X})=\max\left\{\frac{1}{l_{2}},\frac{n+1}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}\right\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{l_{2}}&\text{if }l_{1}>l_{2},\\
\frac{n+1}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}&\text{if }l_{1}\leq l_{2},\\
\end{cases}

if
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
. The adjoint class

(2.5)

E_{\mathcal{X}}=K_{X_{n}}+D+a(\mathcal{X})L

attaining this minimum is

(2.6)

E_{\mathcal{X}}=\begin{cases}(\frac{l_{1}}{l_{2}}-2,0)&\text{if }l_{1}>2l_{2},\\
(0,\frac{2l_{2}-l_{1}}{l_{1}+nl_{2}})&\text{if }l_{1}\leq 2l_{2}\end{cases}

if
D=D_{w}
, and

(2.7)

E_{\mathcal{X}}=\begin{cases}(\frac{l_{1}}{l_{2}}-1,0)&\text{if }l_{1}>l_{2},\\
(0,\frac{l_{2}-l_{1}}{l_{1}+nl_{2}})&\text{if }l_{1}\leq l_{2},\end{cases}

if
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
.
In the latter cases, the adjoint class is trivial or a positive multiple of the class
(0,1)
of the exceptional divisor of
\pi_{n}
, hence rigid. In the former cases, it is a positive multiple of the base point free class
(1,0)
, which is the degree of the monomials
a
,
c
, and
z
in the Cox ring. This class induces the Iitaka fibration

(2.8)

\phi\colon X_{n}\to\mathds{P}^{2},\ (a:b:c:d:z:w)\mapsto(a:c:z).

Finally, the Fujita invariant
b(\mathcal{X})
is the codimension of the minimal face of the effective cone containing
E_{\mathcal{X}}
. It is thus
2
if and only if both entries in the respective maximum in (

,
2.4
) are equal and the corresponding adjoint bundle is trivial, and
1
in any other case:

(2.9)

b(\mathcal{X})=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $D=D_{w}$ and $l_{1}=2l_{2}$,}\\
2&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$ and $l_{1}=l_{2}$, and}\\
1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

2.4.
Height functions

For a polarization
L=(l_{1}+nl_{2},l_{2})
, the map

(2.10)

\displaystyle H_{L}(a,b,c,d,z,w)=\max\left\{\begin{aligned} &\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{l_{1}}\max\{|b|,|d|\}^{l_{2}},\\
&\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{l_{1}+nl_{2}}|w|^{l_{2}}\end{aligned}\right\}

induces a height function on
X_{n}
associated with
L
by setting

H_{L}(x)=H_{L}(a,b,c,d,z,w)

for a point
x=(a:b:c:d:z:w)\in X_{n}(\mathds{Q})
represented in integral Cox coordinates
(a,b,c,d,z,w)\in\mathfrak{Y}_{n}(\mathds{Z})
, in particular satisfying the coprimality condition (
2.1
). Indeed, if
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are both integers, then the set

\{\{a,c,z\}^{l_{1}}\{b,d\}^{l_{2}},\{a,c,z\}^{l_{1}+nl_{2}}w^{l_{2}}\}

of monomials of degree
L
does not admit a common zero, and so these sections of a bundle of degree
L
define a metric on
L
and a height function; if they are not, then passing to an integral multiple
kL
leads to the height function
H_{L}=H_{kL}^{1/k}
, using the linearity of the exponents in (
2.10
) in
L
.

Throughout this section, let

For now, assume that

3.1.
Archimedean Tamagawa measures

Aiming to interpret the asymptotic formulas obtained in Section
4
geometrically, we start by describing the relevant types of Tamagawa measures. To this end, we shall make use of the chart

\psi\colon X_{n}\dashrightarrow\mathds{A}^{3},\quad(a:b:c:d:z:w)\mapsto(c/a,z/a,wa^{n}/b)

and its inverse

(3.1)

\tilde{\psi}\colon\mathds{A}^{3}\to X_{n},\quad(c_{0},z_{0},w_{0})\mapsto(1:1:c_{0}:c_{0}+z_{0}^{n+1}w_{0}:z_{0}:w_{0}).

The descriptions (
2.6
,
2.7
) of the adjoint bundle (currently assumed to be rigid) motivate the definition

e_{\mathcal{X}}=\begin{cases}\frac{2l_{2}-l_{1}}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}&\text{if $D=D_{w}$ and}\\
\frac{l_{2}-l_{1}}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$,}\end{cases}

so that
E_{\mathcal{X}}=e_{\mathcal{X}}D_{w}
(which is possibly zero); let
k>0
be an integer such that
ke_{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathds{Z}
.
Appealing to (
2.5
), the meromorphic section

(3.2)

1_{D_{w}}^{ke_{\mathcal{X}}}1_{D}^{-k}(\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0})^{-k}

has degree
ka(\mathcal{X})L
and corresponds to the monomial

(3.3)

w^{ke_{\mathcal{X}}}s_{D}^{-k}(a^{n-2}b^{-2})^{-k},

where
s_{D}\in\{w,wz\}
depending on
D
,
under suitable isomorphisms between the bundles
\mathcal{O}(D_{w})
,
\mathcal{O}(D_{z})
, and
\omega_{X}
, and the bundles whose meromorphic sections are homogeneous elements of
\Frac(R(X_{n}))
of the respective degrees. (Note that up to scalars,
\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}
is the unique section of the anticanonical bundle and

the unique fraction of canonical degree
(-n,-2)
with neither zeroes nor poles on
\im\tilde{\psi}

As
\mathcal{X}
is adjoint rigid, the adjoint divisor

is well-defined as a divisor (not just as a class). The relevant (residue) measures are supported on subvarieties corresponding to maximal faces of the analytic Clemens complex
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}(D\setminus|E_{\mathcal{X}}|)
of the complement of its support
[
16
, after Def. 6.3]

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{an}}(D)
is a simplex, so is this subcomplex; denote by

its unique maximal face.

If

, we restrict ourselves to the case

, in which this analytic Clemens complex is trivial with

as its only face. Under this condition, the relevant Tamagawa measure is

\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset,\infty}=\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{\lVert 1_{D_{w}}\rVert^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}\rVert},

the denominator being the
k
-th root of the norm of (
3.2
). As
e_{\mathcal{X}}>0
by the above assumption on

, this measure is finite.
Using the correspondence with the monomial (
3.3
), this measure can be expressed as

\frac{|a^{n-2}b^{-2}|^{-1}\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{|w|^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}H_{L}(a,b,c,d,z,w)^{a(\mathcal{X})}}

in Cox coordinates and as

\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset,\infty}=\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{|w_{0}|^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}H_{L}(1,1,c_{0},c_{0}+z_{0}^{n+1}w_{0},z_{0},w_{0})^{a(\mathcal{X})}}

in local coordinates (
3.1
). Finally, let

(3.4)

\omega_{\mathcal{X},\infty}=\int_{X_{n}(\mathds{R})}\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset,\infty}=\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{|w_{0}|^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}H_{L}(1,1,c_{0},c_{0}+z_{0}^{n+1}w_{0},z_{0},w_{0})^{a(\mathcal{X})}}

be the resulting volume of
X_{n}(\mathds{R})
.

Turning to

, the relevant analytic Clemens complex is a single vertex with maximal face
A=\{D_{z}\}
if

and a
1
-simplex with maximal face
A=\{D_{z},D_{w}\}
if

.
In the former case, the Tamagawa measure

\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\{D_{z}\},\infty}=2\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{\lVert 1_{D_{w}}\rVert^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}\rVert}

is supported on
D_{z}(\mathds{R})
and finite as

. It includes a renormalization factor

. The norm of the
2

\omega_{X}(D_{z})
via the adjunction isomorphism. This isomorphism identifies

with the restriction of the
3

z_{0}^{-1}\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}

\lVert 1_{D_{w}}\rVert^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert z_{0}^{-1}\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}\rVert=\lim_{z_{0}\to 0}\frac{\lVert 1_{D_{w}}\rVert^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}\lVert 1_{D_{z}}\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}\rVert}{|z_{0}|}.

Once more using the correspondence between (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), changing into local coordinates, and passing to the limit, the measure can thus be explicitly described as

\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\{D_{z}\},\infty}=2\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{|w_{0}|^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}H_{L}(1,1,c_{0},c_{0},0,w_{0})^{a(\mathcal{X})}}

in local coordinates; denote by

(3.5)

\omega_{\mathcal{X},\infty}=\int_{D_{z}(\mathds{R})}\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\{D_{z}\},\infty}=2\int_{\mathds{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{|w_{0}|^{1-e_{\mathcal{X}}}H_{L}(1,1,c_{0},c_{0},0,w_{0})^{a(\mathcal{X})}}

If

, this measure is infinite. However, as the analytic Clemens complex is larger in this case, the relevant measure is a residue measure on the real points of the boundary stratum
Z_{A}=D_{z}\cap D_{w}
corresponding to the maximal face

of the Clemens complex. Analogously to the previous cases, it can be determined to be

\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\{D_{w},D_{z}\},\infty}=4\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}}{H_{L}(1,1,c_{0},c_{0},0,0)^{a(\mathcal{X})}},

again including the renormalization factor

(3.6)

\omega_{\mathcal{X},\infty}=\int_{D_{w}\cap D_{z}(\mathds{R})}\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},\{D_{w},D_{z}\},\infty}=4\int_{\mathds{R}}\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{0}}{H_{L}(1,1,c_{0},c_{0},0,0)^{a(\mathcal{X})}}

3.2.
Non-archimedean Tamagawa measures

For finite places, the measures of interest

\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},p}=\frac{\lVert 1_{D_{w}}\rVert^{e_{\mathcal{X}}}_{p}\mathrm{d}c_{0}\,\mathrm{d}z_{0}\,\mathrm{d}w_{0}}{\lVert 1_{D}\rVert_{p}\lVert\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}\rVert_{p}}

are supported on
\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{Z}_{p})
,
and we denote by

\omega_{\mathcal{X},p}=\lambda_{\mathcal{X},p}\int_{\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{Z}_{p})}\mathrm{d}\tau_{\mathcal{X},p}

the volume of the set of
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-integral points with a convergence factor
\lambda_{\mathcal{X},p}
as in
[
3
, Def. 2.8]
.
The sections
1_{D_{w}}
,
1_{D_{z}}
, and
\mathrm{d}c_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{0}\wedge\mathrm{d}w_{0}
are integral and primitive; as the same holds true for
w
,
z
, and

, the isomorphisms appearing in the beginning of Section
3.1
spread out to the models
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{X}_{n}\setminus V(ab)
, respectively. As a consequence of the coprimality conditions on the torsor, the
p
-adic analogue of the height (
2.10
) evaluates to
1
on all
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-points.
In particular, the corresponding adelic metrics are induced by the model
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
at all finite places. Consequently, evaluating
\lVert 1_{D}\rVert_{p}
in any
\mathds{Z}_{p}
-point on
\mathfrak{U}
results in
1
; as
E_{\mathcal{X}}\subset D
for all
n
,
D
, and
L
, the same holds true for
\lVert 1_{D_{w}}\rVert_{p}
.

The fiber over any prime
p
is cut out by the polynomial
ad-bc-z^{n+1}w
in the toric
\mathds{F}_{p}
-variety described by
\Sigma_{n}
. Its universal torsor is étale locally trivial, hence étale locally isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{Z}_{n})_{\mathds{F}_{p}}\times\mathds{G}^{2}_{\mathrm{m},\mathds{F}_{p}}
. The preimage of the subvariety
(\mathfrak{X}_{n})_{\mathds{F}_{p}}
is thus étale locally isomorphic to
(\mathfrak{X}_{n})_{\mathds{F}_{p}}\times\mathds{G}^{2}_{\mathrm{m},\mathds{F}_{p}}

using the Jacobi criterion, and the same criterion then implies the smoothness of its factor
(\mathfrak{X}_{n})_{\mathds{F}_{p}}
.
Therefore, the identity

\omega_{\mathcal{X},p}=\lambda_{\mathcal{X},p}\frac{\#\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{F}_{p})}{p^{3}}

that a priori holds for almost all primes

(see e.g.
[
3
, § 2.4.1]
) in fact holds for all of them.

Lemma 3.1
.

The number of
\mathds{F}_{p}
-points on the integral models is

\#\mathfrak{X}_{n}(\mathds{F}_{p})=p^{3}+2p^{2}+2p+1.

Proof.

This number can be obtained elementarily by counting points on the universal torsor and dividing by
\#\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}(\mathds{F}_{p})=(p-1)^{2}
to obtain the number of fibers or from an application of the Lefschetz trace formula as in
[
14
, Lem. 2.2.4]
:
Peyre’s arguments yield

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{0}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})

\displaystyle\cong H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{6}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})\cong\mathds{Q}_{l},

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{1}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})

\displaystyle\cong H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{5}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})=0,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{2}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})

\displaystyle\cong H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{4}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})=\Pic(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}})\otimes_{\mathds{Z}}\mathds{Q}_{l}\cong\mathds{Q}_{l}^{2}

with a trivial action by the Galois group using Poincaré duality, leaving only degree
3
.
The exponential sequence results in an exact sequence

\Br(X(\mathds{C}))=H^{2}(X(\mathds{C}),\mathcal{O}^{\times}_{X(\mathds{C})})\to H^{3}(X(\mathds{C}),\mathds{Z})\to H^{3}(X(\mathds{C}),\mathcal{O}_{X(\mathds{C})});

the group on the left is trivial as
X_{\mathds{C}}
is rational, and the one on the right is so by Kodaira vanishing.
Hence, the cohomology group in the middle vanishes. Then so does
l
-adic cohomology

H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{3}(X_{\overline{\mathds{Q}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})
for any prime
l
.
The smooth base change theorem now implies that these groups coincide with the
l
-adic cohomology groups
H_{\textrm{\'{e}t}}^{i}((\mathfrak{X}_{n})_{\overline{\mathds{F}_{p}}},\mathds{Q}_{l})
for
l\neq p
.
∎

For the following arguments to count points on the two relevant open subvarieties,
let
\mathfrak{D}_{w}=\overline{D_{w}}
be the closure in
\mathfrak{X}
, and analogously for the remaining generators of the Cox ring.
The map

\mathfrak{D}_{w}\to\mathds{P}^{1},\quad(a:b:c:d:z:0)\mapsto(b:d)

makes
\mathfrak{D}_{w}
an (in fact trivial)
\mathds{P}^{1}
-bundle over
\mathds{P}^{1}
. Hence,
\#\mathfrak{D}_{w}(\mathds{F}_{p})=(p+1)^{2}
and

\frac{\#\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{F}_{p})}{p^{3}}=\frac{\#\mathfrak{X}_{n}(\mathds{F}_{p})-\#\mathfrak{D}_{w}(\mathds{F}_{p})}{p^{3}}=1+\frac{1}{p}

if
D=D_{w}
.

As integral points on
D_{w}
and on
D_{z}
satisfy the same torsor equation with symmetric coprimality conditions, their numbers are equal. The intersection
D_{w}\cap D_{z}
is a line on
D_{w}
with
p+1
points, whence

(3.7)

\frac{\#\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{F}_{p})}{p^{3}}=\frac{\#\mathfrak{X}_{n}(\mathds{F}_{p})-\#\mathfrak{D}_{w}(\mathds{F}_{p})-\#\mathfrak{D}_{z}(\mathds{F}_{p})+\#(\mathfrak{D}_{w}\cap\mathfrak{D}_{z})(\mathds{F}_{p})}{p^{3}}=1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}

if
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
.

Finally, as the Galois group acts trivially, the convergence factors are simply

\lambda_{\mathcal{X},p}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\rk\Pic U},

resulting in

(3.8)

\omega_{\mathcal{X},p}=1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}

for both choices of
D
.

3.3.
Adelic Tamagawa measures

Again denoting by
A
the unique maximal face of the analytic Clemens complex, multiplying these local measures results in the adelic Tamagawa measure

\tau_{\mathcal{X}}=\tau_{\mathcal{X},A,\infty}\prod_{p}\lambda_{\mathcal{X},p}\tau_{\mathcal{X},p}

on

, where

.
The total volume of the space of adelic points is

\omega_{\mathcal{X}}=\tau_{\mathcal{X}}(X(\mathds{A}_{\mathds{Q}}))=\omega_{\mathcal{X},\infty}\prod_{p}\omega_{\mathcal{X},p}.

3.4.
Effective cones

The remaining ingredient for asymptotic formulas is a constant

related to the geometry of effective cones. As the base field

4.
Counting

Keep the notation of the previous section; in particular, let
\mathcal{X}=(\mathfrak{X}_{n},D,L)
be a polarized log variety consisting of the model
\mathfrak{X}_{n}
of the spherical variety
X_{n}
for an integer

, one of the
G
-invariant divisors

that make

log Fano, and an ample line bundle

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\#\{x\in\mathfrak{U}(\mathds{Z})\mid H_{L}(x)\leq B\}

for
B\geq 2
.
By the description of rational points in Section
2.1
, this function can be expressed more explicitly as

(4.1)

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\#\left\{(a,b,c,d,z)\in\mathds{Z}^{5}\ \middle|\ \begin{subarray}{c}ad-bc=z^{n+1},\\
\gcd(a,c)=1,\\
H_{L}(a,b,c,d,z,1)\leq B\end{subarray}\right\}&\text{if }D=D_{w},\text{ and}\\[10.00002pt]
\#\left\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathds{Z}^{4}\ \middle|\ \begin{subarray}{c}ad-bc=1,\\
\gcd(a,c)=1,\\
H_{L}(a,b,c,d,1,1)\leq B\end{subarray}\right\}&\text{if }D=D_{w}+D_{z}.\end{cases}

Indeed, integral points are precisely those that satisfy

and
w=\pm 1
,

, respectively. Setting these coordinates to

turns the

-to-

-correspondence for rational points into a

-to-

-correspondence and
1
-to-
1
-correspondence, respectively. In particular, the restraint

makes the second coprimality condition in (
2.1
) automatic, while the torsor equations
ad-bc=1
and
ad-bc=z^{n+1}
(together with
\gcd(a,c,z)=1
in the latter case) imply

, which is stronger than the coprimality condition in (

).

4.1.
The first summation

We begin by estimating the number of solutions involving a fixed vector

-height at most

) with fixed

,

, and

,

, and

, let

V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)=\int_{|b|,|a^{-1}(z^{n+1}+bc)|\leq B^{1/l_{2}}\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{-l_{1}/l_{2}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}b}{|a|}

if

, and

, the linear change of variables

Lemma 4.1

and
\gcd(a,c)=1
or
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
,
\gcd(a,c)=1
, and
z\in\mathds{Z}^{\times}
.

Moreover, the total number of integral points of bounded height is

(4.2)

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c,z\in\mathds{Z}\\
|a|,|c|,|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)+O(B^{3/(l_{1}+nl_{2})})

if
D=D_{w}
, and

(4.3)

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c\in\mathds{Z}\\
|a|,|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,1;B)+O(B^{2/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}),

if
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
.

Proof.

For fixed
(a,c,z)\in\mathds{Z}^{3}
with
\gcd(a,c)=1
and
a\neq 0
, the coordinate

d=(bc+z^{n+1})/a,

is integral if and only if
b\equiv-c^{-1}z^{n+1}\pmod{a}
. As a consequence, the number of
(b,d)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}
satisfying
H_{L}(a,b,c,d,z,1)\leq B
and the torsor equation is

V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)+O(1),

with an analogous argument treating the case
c\neq 0
, settling the first part of the lemma.
Summing this error term over
a
and
c
results in

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c\in\mathds{Z}\\
|a|,|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\gcd(a,c)=1\end{subarray}}1\ll B^{2/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}

and the second part of the lemma after specializing to
z=1
; further summing over
z
results in the first part. (The factor
1/2
stemming from above is explained by the fact that each fiber is presented by two vectors
(a,c,z)
in the first case and by a unique vector
(a,c,1)
in the second case.)
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(a,c,z)\in\mathds{R}^{3}
with
(a,c)\neq(0,0)
. Let

(4.4)

V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z)=\int_{|b|,|a^{-1}bc|\leq\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{-l_{1}/l_{2}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}b}{|a|}

if
a\neq 0
, and
V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(0,c,z)=V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(c,0,z)
.
Then

(4.5)

V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)=V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z)B^{1/l_{2}}+\begin{cases}O(|z|^{n+1}/|ac|)&\text{if $ac\neq 0$,}\\
O(|z|^{n+1}/(|a|^{2}-|ac|))&\text{if $|c|<|a|$,}\\
O(|z|^{n+1}/(|c|^{2}-|ac|))&\text{if $|a|<|c|$,}\\
\end{cases}

where each applicable error term suffices on its own whenever more than one of the conditions is met.

Proof.

By symmetry, we may assume
a\neq 0
. Setting

Z=B^{1/l_{2}}\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{-l_{1}/l_{2}},

the first step is to bound the difference

(4.6)

\left|V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)-\int_{|b|,|a^{-1}bc|\leq Z}\frac{\mathrm{d}b}{|a|}\right|.

If
c\neq 0
and
a/c>0
, this means comparing an integral over those
b\in\mathds{R}
satisfying

-Z\leq b\leq Z
and

-Zac^{-1}-z^{n+1}c^{-1}\leq b\leq Zac^{-1}-z^{n+1}c^{-1}

with one over the set of
b
satisfying
-Z\leq b\leq Z
and

-Zac^{-1}\leq b\leq Zac^{-1}
; if
a/c<0
, the comparison is similar. In each of these cases,
the difference of the two sets is contained in the set of
b
with

Z|ac^{-1}|-|z^{n+1}c^{-1}|\leq|b|\leq Z|ac^{-1}|+|z^{n+1}c^{-1}|,

which form two intervals of length
O(|z^{n+1}c^{-1}|)
; multiplying with the integrand
|a|^{-1}
results in the first error term in (
4.5
).

To obtain the second bound for the error term, assume
|c|<|a|
. If

Z|ac^{-1}|-|z^{n+1}c^{-1}|\geq Z,

both integration domains in (
4.6
) are given by
|b|\leq Z
, hence the difference is
0
in this case. Otherwise,
Z<|z^{n+1}|/(|a|-|c|)
, and we end up with an error term

\int_{|b|\leq Z}\frac{\mathrm{d}b}{|a|}\ll\frac{|z^{n+1}|}{|a^{2}|-|ac|}

as stated. The final case
|a|<|c|
is symmetric.

In each case, the main term arises from the integral on the right of (
4.6
) by the change of variables
b\mapsto B^{-1/l_{2}}b
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

If
D=D_{w}
, then

(4.7)

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,c,z)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{prim}}\times\mathds{Z}\\
|a|,|c|,|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z)B^{1/l_{2}}+O(B^{(n+2)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\log B).

If
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
, then

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,c)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{prim}}\\
|a|,|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,1)B^{1/l_{2}}+O(B^{2/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}).

Proof.

To arrive at (
4.7
), we may assume
|c|\leq|a|
by the symmetry of all expressions involved. Summing the error term (
4.6
) over
a
,
c
, and
z
results in

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|,|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
|a|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{|c|\leq|a/2|}\frac{|z|^{n+1}}{|a|^{2}-|ac|}+\sum_{|a/2|<|c|\leq|a|}\frac{|z|^{n+1}}{|ac|}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|,|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
|a|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{|z^{n+1}|}{|a|}\ll B^{(n+2)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\log B.

Setting
z=1
and only summing over
a
and
c
to treat the second case results in the error term
O(\log B)
that is dominated by the error term in Lemma
4.1
.
∎

4.2.
Moving adjoint divisors: extending the sum

For now, we investigate those polarizations
L
for which the adjoint divisor
E_{\mathcal{X}}
computed in equations (
2.6
,
2.7
) is a positive multiple of
(1,0)
, that is, moving.

For
t=(a:c:z)\in\mathds{P}^{2}(\mathds{Q})
with coprime integral coordinates, let

c^{\prime}(\mathcal{X}_{t})=\begin{cases}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}(a,c,z)&\text{if $D=D_{w}$ and $\gcd(a,c)=1$,}\\
V_{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}(a,c,1)&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$ and $\gcd(a,c)=z=1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

These constants can further be evaluated to

(4.8)

\frac{1}{\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}\max\{|a|,|c|\}}

whenever they are nonzero by integrating (
4.4
).
In light of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, the number of points on each fiber
t\in\mathds{P}^{2}(\mathds{Q})
is thus

(4.9)

N_{\mathcal{X}_{t},\emptyset}=c^{\prime}(\mathcal{X}_{t})B^{1/l_{2}}+O_{t}(1).

Proposition 4.4
.

If the adjoint divisor is a positive multiple of
(1,0)
, then

(4.10)

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{t\in\mathds{P}^{2}(\mathds{Q})}c^{\prime}(\mathcal{X}_{t})B^{1/l_{2}}+O(B^{1/l_{2}-\delta}(\log B)^{2})

with

\delta=\begin{cases}(l_{1}/l_{2}-2)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})&\text{if $D=D_{w}$ and}\\
(l_{1}/l_{2}-1)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For
D=D_{w}
, the error arising from removing the height condition

|a|,|c|,|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}

in (
4.7
) is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(a,c)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{prim}},\,z\in\mathds{Z}\\
\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}V^{\prime}_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z)B^{1/l_{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c,z\geq 0\\
a\leq c,\ c\neq 0\\
\max\{c,z\}>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{\max\{c,z\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}c}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,z\geq 0,\\
\max\{c,z\}>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{\max\{c,z\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}},

using (
4.8
) and its symmetry in
a
and
c
.
Using the symmetry of this expression in
c
and
z
, this is at most a constant multiple of

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq c\leq z,\\
z>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{z^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{z>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{z^{l_{1}/l_{2}-1}}\ll B^{1/l_{2}}B^{(2-l_{1}/l_{2})/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}

as
l_{1}/l_{2}>2
. In particular, the sum in equation (
4.10
) converges.

Turning to
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
, an analogous argument bounds the error term by a constant multiple of

\sum_{c>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{c^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\ll B^{1/l_{2}}B^{(1-l_{1}/l_{2})/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}

as
l_{1}/l_{2}>1
.

Finally, note that for
D=D_{w}
, each point
(a:c:z)\in\mathds{P}^{2}(\mathds{Q})
corresponds to two vectors
(a,c,z)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{prim}}\times\mathds{Z}
, resulting in an additional factor of
2
, while for
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
, each of the relevant points
(a:c:1)\in\mathds{P}^{2}(\mathds{Q})
corresponds to precisely one vector
(a,c)\in\mathds{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{prim}}
.
∎

4.3.
Rigid adjoint divisors: the remaining summations

Now, let
l_{1}<2l_{2}
for
D=D_{w}
and
l_{1}\leq l_{2}
for
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
; in particular,
E_{\mathcal{X}}
is rigid. In the latter case, this includes
E_{\mathcal{X}}=0
, in which
L
is a multiple of the log anticanonical bundle. In the former case, the log anticanonical bundle
L=(n+2,2)
with
l_{1}=2
and
l_{2}=1
has already been treated by Chambert-Loir and Tschinkel
[
4
]
(see Remark
2.1
); statements for its multiples with arbitrary
l_{1}=2l_{2}
can easily be deduced from their theorem.

For both boundaries, the first step in the remaining estimates is to remove the coprimality condition in (
4.2
) and (
4.3
) by means of a Möbius inversion: for fixed
z\neq 0
, the sum over coprime
a
and
c
in either equation is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|,|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
(a,c)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\sum_{k\mid(a,c)}\mu(k)V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B),

which can be rewritten as

(4.11)

\sum_{k\geq 1}\mu(k)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|,|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}/k\\
(a,c)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(ka,kc,z;B).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

\displaystyle W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{L}(a,b,c,(bc+z^{n+1})/a,z,1)\leq B\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c\,\mathrm{d}z}{|a|}}&\text{if $D=D_{w}$,}\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle{\int_{H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)\leq B}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c}{|a|}}&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$, $l_{1}<l_{2}$,}\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle{\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)\leq B\\
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c}{|a|}}&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$, $l_{1}=l_{2}$.}\end{cases}

Then

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)+\begin{cases}O\!\left(B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{3}\right),&\text{if $D=D_{w}$,}\\
O\!\left(B^{1/l_{2}}\right),&\text{if $D=D_{w}+D_{z}$,}\end{cases}

with the saving

(4.12)

\delta(\mathcal{X})=\frac{\min\{l_{1},2l_{2}-l_{1}\}}{l_{2}(l_{1}+nl_{2})}

in the former case.

Proof.

The error terms in Lemma
4.3
dominate the main term if
D=D_{w}
, and so our starting point is Lemma
4.1
combined with (
4.11
). Observe that (
4.8
) is still valid as an upper bound, though; that is,

(4.13)

V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)\ll\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{\max\{|a|,|c|,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}\max\{|a|,|c|\}}.

Summation over
a

By
[
7
, Lem. 3.6]
,
V_{\mathcal{X}}
treated as a function in
a
is piecewise monotonous, where the number of pieces is bounded independently of
c
,
z
, and
B
; by
[
10
, Lem. 3.1]
, the sum over
a
can thus be estimated by an integral, introducing an error

(4.14)

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|ka|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(ka,kc,z;B)-\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|ka|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(ka,kc,z;B)\mathrm{d}a\right\rvert

\displaystyle\ll\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|ka|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(ka,kc,z;B).

By the linear change of variables
a\mapsto a/k
, the integral in (
4.14
) can be rewritten as
k^{-1}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,kc,z;B)
with

V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,c,z;B)=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
\max\{|a/k|,|c/k|\}\geq 1\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B)\mathrm{d}a

(which vanishes for
k>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}
). Using (
4.13
), the second line of (
4.14
) is at most

\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{\max\{k,|kc|,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}\max\{k,|kc|\}}

since the supremum ranges only over
|a|\geq 1
for
c=0
, while the terms
k
do not contribute to the maximum for
c\neq 0
. If
D=D_{w}
, its sum over
c
,
z
and
k
can be bounded by a constant multiple of

(4.15)

B^{\frac{n+2}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}-\frac{\min\{l_{1},2l_{2}-l_{1}\}}{l_{2}(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}(\log B)^{3},

by cutting it into three regions to simplify the maximum in the denominator; this bound can be rewritten as
B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{3}
by (
2.3
) and (
4.12
).

If
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
(hence
z=1
), the error term is bounded by

\sum_{k,c\in\mathds{Z},\ k\geq 1}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{k^{l_{1}/l_{2}+1}\max\{1,|c|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}+1}}\ll B^{1/l_{2}},

whence

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,c\in\mathds{Z},\ k\geq 1\\
|kc|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,kc,1;B)+O(B^{1/l_{2}}).

For
D=D_{w}
, an analogous expression involving an additional sum over
|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}
, a factor
1/2
, and the error term (
4.15
) holds.

Summation over
c

Arguing analogously to before and setting

V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,z;B)=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,c,z;B)\mathrm{d}c,

the difference

\left|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}/k}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,kc,z;B)-\frac{1}{k^{2}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,z;B)\right|

is at most
\sup_{|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}/k}(V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,kc,z;B))/k
.
Treating the case
D=D_{w}
first, the bound (
4.13
) results in

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,c,z;B)

\displaystyle\ll\int_{|a|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{k\max\{1,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}\max\{|a|,1\}}\mathrm{d}a

\displaystyle\ll\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}\log B}{k\max\{1,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}.

Summing this error over
k\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}
and
z
results in a total error of at most

\max\{B^{(n+1)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})},B^{1/l_{2}}\}(\log B)^{3}\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{3},

yielding

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,z;B)+O\!\left(B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{3}\right).

For the case
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
(with
z=1
), the estimate (
4.13
) for
V_{\mathcal{X}}
together with
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq k
implies

\frac{1}{k}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(1)}(k,c,1;B)\ll\int_{\mathds{R}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{k\max\{|a|,|k|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}+1}}\mathrm{d}a\ll\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{k^{l_{1}/l_{2}+1}}.

Summing this error over
k\geq 1
now results in a total error of order of magnitude
B^{1/l_{2}}
. In other words,

(4.16)

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,1;B)+O(B^{1/l_{2}}).

Summation over
z

This step is only needed for
D=D_{w}
. Using the symmetry of the volumes in
a
and
c
, the new volume is at most

(4.17)

\displaystyle V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,z;B)

\displaystyle\ll\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\\
|a|\leq|c|\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|^{l_{1}/l_{2}+1}}\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}c\ll\int_{1\leq|c|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\mathrm{d}c

\displaystyle\ll\max\left\{B^{\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{1-l_{1}/l_{2}}{l_{1}+nl_{2}}},B^{1/l_{2}}\right\}\log B\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}\log B,

where the factor
\log B
is only necessary if
l_{1}=l_{2}
. Defining

V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(3)}(k;B)=\int_{|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,z;B)\mathrm{d}z,

it follows from (
4.17
) that

\left|\frac{1}{k^{2}}\sum_{|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,z;B)-\frac{1}{k^{2}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(3)}(k;B)\right|\ll\frac{B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}\log B}{k^{2}},

and summing
1/k^{2}
over
k\geq 1
contributes only a bounded factor.
In total,

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(3)}(k;B)+O\!\left(B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{3}\right).

Integrating near the origin

The difference between
V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(3)}(k;B)
and
W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)
for
D=D_{w}
lies in the condition
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq k
; removing it introduces the error

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|,|c|\leq k\\
|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}V_{\mathcal{X}}(a,c,z;B).

Appealing to (
4.13
) and using its symmetry in
a
and
c
, this error is bounded by a constant multiple of

2\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|\leq|c|\leq k\\
|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\!\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{\max\{|c|,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}|c|}\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}c\,\mathrm{d}z\!\ll\!\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}|c|\leq k\\
|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\!\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{\max\{|c|,|z|\}^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\mathrm{d}c\,\mathrm{d}z.

Dividing the integral into ranges with
|z|\leq|c|
and
|c|<|z|
, the one over the former is easily seen to be at most

(4.18)

\int_{|z|\leq|c|\leq k}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\mathrm{d}c\,\mathrm{d}z\ll B^{1/l_{2}}k^{2-l_{1}/l_{2}};

for the latter integral, we first estimate

\int_{|c|\leq|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|z|^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\mathrm{d}z\ll\begin{cases}B^{(n+1)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})},&\text{if $l_{1}<l_{2}$,}\\
B^{1/l_{2}}(\log B+|\log|c||),&\text{if $l_{1}=l_{2}$, and}\\
B^{1/l_{2}}|c|^{1-l_{1}/l_{2}},&\text{if $l_{2}<l_{1}<2l_{2}$,}\end{cases}

and thus

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq|c|\leq k\\
|c|\leq|z|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|z|^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}c\ll\begin{cases}kB^{(n+1)/(l_{1}+nl_{2})},&\text{if $l_{1}<l_{2}$,}\\
kB^{1/l_{2}}(\log B+\log k),&\text{if $l_{1}=l_{2}$, and}\\
B^{1/l_{2}}k^{2-l_{1}/l_{2}},&\text{if $l_{2}<l_{1}<2l_{2}$.}\end{cases}

In any case, both these errors and (
4.18
) can be summed over
k\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}
, so that

(4.19)

\left|N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)-\sum_{1\leq k\leq B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{\mu(k)}{k^{2}}W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)\right|\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{3}.

Using the symmetry of
4.13
in
a,c
, one can bound

W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})}.

Therefore, extending the sum over
k
in
4.19

to all
k\geq 1
introduces an error of at most

\displaystyle\sum_{k>B^{1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})-\delta(\mathcal{X})}.

This completes the proof in the case
D=D_{w}
.

For
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
, comparing
V_{\mathcal{X}}^{(2)}(k,1;B)
and
W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)
, the region of the former integral is described by
H_{L}(a,b,c,(1+bc)/a,1,1)\leq B
and
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq k
, while that of the latter is described by
H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)\leq B
and
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1
(resp. no lower bound on this maximum if
l_{1}<l_{2}
). Replacing
(1+bc)/a
in the former height condition by
bc/a
as in the latter introduces an error term of size
O(\log B)
by the same arguments used to obtain the estimate (
4.5
) and its sum over the remaining variables in Lemma
4.3
. Replacing
z=1
by
z=0
preserves the value of the integral as
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq k\geq 1
, so that
z
never contributes to the maxima anyway. Removing the lower bound
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq k
if
l_{1}<l_{2}
introduces an error of at most

2\int_{|a|\leq|c|<k}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|^{l_{1}/l_{2}+1}}\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}c\ll\int_{|c|\leq k}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|^{l_{1}/l_{2}}}\mathrm{d}c\ll B^{1/l_{2}}k^{1-l_{1}/l_{2}},

whose sum over
k\geq 1
converges to a function in
O(B^{1/l_{2}})
after multiplying it with the factor
k^{-2}
in (
4.16
). If
l_{1}=l_{2}
, replacing
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq k
by
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1
results in the error

2\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|c|\leq k\\
|a|\leq|c|\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|^{2}}\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}c\ll\int_{1\leq|c|\leq k}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c|}\mathrm{d}c\ll B^{1/l_{2}}\log k,

whose sum over
k\geq 1
converges after multiplication by
k^{-2}
as well.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

The volume
W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)
in Proposition
4.5
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{B^{a(\mathcal{X})}}{2}\int_{H_{L}(a,b,c,(bc+z^{n+1})/a,z,1)\leq 1}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c\,\mathrm{d}z}{|a|}

if
D=D_{w}
,

\displaystyle B^{a(\mathcal{X})}\int_{H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)\leq 1}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c}{|a|}

if
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
,
l_{1}<l_{2}
, and

\displaystyle\frac{2B^{a(\mathcal{X})}\log B}{l_{1}(n+1)}\int_{H_{L}(1,b,c,bc,0,0)\leq 1}\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c+O(B^{a(\mathcal{X})})

if
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
,
l_{1}=l_{2}
.

Proof.

For
D=D_{w}
, the change of variables
(a,c,z)\mapsto B^{-1/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}(a,c,z)
and
b\mapsto B^{-n/(l_{1}+nl_{2})}b
transforms
W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)
to the integral on the right. If
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
and
l_{1}<l_{2}
, the same change of variables without
z
achieves this end.
In the final case, with
D=D_{w}+D_{z}
and
l_{1}=l_{2}
, we first replace
\max\{|a|,|c|\}\geq 1
by
|a|\geq 1
. Using (
4.8
), the difference is

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)\leq B\\
|a|\leq 1,\ |c|\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c}{|a|}\ll\int_{|a|\leq 1,\ |c|\geq 1}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c^{2}|}\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}c\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})}.

Observe that

H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)=\max\{H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,0),|a|^{l_{1}(n+1)},|c|^{l_{1}(n+1)}\};

removing
|c|^{l_{1}(n+1)}\leq B
from this height condition once more results in an error of

\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,1)\leq B\\
|a|\geq 1,\ |c|^{l_{1}(n+1)}>B\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c}{|a|}\ll\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq|a|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}(n+1))}\\
|c|>B^{1/(l_{1}(n+1))}\end{subarray}}\frac{B^{1/l_{2}}}{|c^{2}|}\mathrm{d}a\,\mathrm{d}c\ll B^{a(\mathcal{X})}.

The resulting main term is

\int_{1\leq|a|\leq B^{1/(l_{1}(n+1))}}\frac{1}{|a|}\left(\int_{H_{L}(a,b,c,bc/a,0,0)\leq B}\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c\right)\mathrm{d}a;

via the change of variables
b\mapsto B^{-1/l_{1}}ab,c\mapsto a^{-1}c
, its inner integral is equal to

B^{a(\mathcal{X})}\int_{H_{L}(1,b,c,bc,0,0)\leq 1}\mathrm{d}b\,\mathrm{d}c

and in particular independent of
a
. Integrating
|a|^{-1}
over
a
results in the remaining factor
2(l_{1}(n+1))^{-1}\log B
.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

The volumes in Proposition
4.5
satisfy

W_{\mathcal{X}}(B)=\frac{\alpha(\mathcal{X})\omega_{\mathcal{X},\infty}}{a(\mathcal{X})}B^{a(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{b(\mathcal{X})-1}

if

, and the same expression holds true up to the addition of an error term

.

Proof.

, our starting point is (

).
We observe that

H_{L}(1,1,c,c+z^{n+1}w,z,w)=|b|^{-l_{2}}H_{L}(1,b,c,bc+z^{n+1},z,1)

using the homogeneity of

. By this change of variables and using the identity

Using the identity

\frac{1}{H}=\beta\int_{0\leq t^{\beta}H\leq 1}t^{\beta-1}\mathrm{d}t

H_{L}(t,t^{n}b,tc,t^{n}(bc+z^{n}),tz,1)=t^{l_{1}+nl_{2}}H_{L}(1,b,c,bc+z^{n+1},z,1)

following from the homogeneity of the height function, and

in the exponent of

4.4.
The asymptotic formula

Comparing this counting result with the analysis in Section
3
leads to Theorem
1.3
in the following concrete form.

Proposition 4.8
.

If
\mathcal{X}=(\mathfrak{X}_{n},D,L)
is adjoint rigid, then

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\frac{\alpha(\mathcal{X})}{a(\mathcal{X})}\prod_{v\in\Omega_{\mathds{Q}}}\omega_{\mathcal{X},v}B^{a(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{b(\mathcal{X})-1}(1+O(1/\log B));

if it is not, then

N_{\mathcal{X},\emptyset}(B)=\sum_{t\in\mathds{P}^{2}(\mathds{Q})}\frac{\alpha(\mathcal{X}_{t})}{a(\mathcal{X})}\prod_{v\in\Omega_{\mathds{Q}}}\omega_{\mathcal{X}_{t},v}B^{a(\mathcal{X})}(\log B)^{b(\mathcal{X})-1}(1+O(1/\log B)).

Proof.

In the rigid case, this follows from Proposition
4.5
, Lemma
4.7
, and (
3.8
). The only remaining case is
l_{1}=2l_{2}
. In this case,
L
is a multiple of the log anticanonical bundle and
(X,D)
is a partial equivariant compactification of
\mathds{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{3}
; thus, the statement is a special case of
[
4
]
.

In the moving case, using the notation from (
1.1
),
N_{\mathcal{X}_{t},\emptyset}(B)
denotes the number of integral points in the fiber
\phi^{-1}(t)
of
L
-height at most
B
. On the one hand, recall that

N_{\mathcal{X}_{t},\emptyset}(B)=c^{\prime}(\mathcal{X}_{t})B^{1/l_{2}}+O_{t}(1)

by (
4.9
). On the other hand,

N_{\mathcal{X}_{t},\emptyset}(B)=c(\mathcal{X}_{t})B^{a(\mathcal{X}_{t})}+O_{t}(B^{a(\mathcal{X}_{t})-\delta(t)})

with

c(\mathcal{X}_{t})=\frac{\alpha(\mathcal{X}_{t})}{a(\mathcal{X}_{t})}\prod_{v\in\Omega_{\mathds{Q}}}\omega_{\mathcal{X}_{t},v}

for some
\delta(t)>0
by
[
4
]
. Therefore, the leading constants and exponents of
B
have to agree. Now the result follows from Proposition
4.4
.
∎

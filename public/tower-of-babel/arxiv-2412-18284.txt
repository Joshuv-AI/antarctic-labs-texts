Molla Basir Ahamed, Rajesh Hossain

Abstract
. We will also derive the sharp bound of pre-Schwarzian norm for a certain class of harmonic mappings whose analytic part is fixed.

Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of analytic functions in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, and
\mathcal{LU}
denote the subclass of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of all locally univalent functions, namely,
\mathcal{LU}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0,\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}
. The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives for a function
f\in\mathcal{LU}
are defined by

\displaystyle P_{f}(z)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;S_{f}(z)=\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2},

respectively. It is well-known that
P(A\circ f)=Pf
for all linear (or affine) transformations
A(w)=aw+b
,
b\neq 0
. Moreover, the Schwarzian derivative is invariant under (non-constant) linear fractional (or Möbius) transformations. In other words
S(T\circ f)=S(f)
for every function
T
of the form

\displaystyle T(w)=\frac{aw+b}{cw+d},\;ad-bc\neq 0.

These are just particular cases of the chain rule for the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives:

\displaystyle P(g\circ f)=(Pg\circ f)f^{\prime}+Pf\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;S(g\circ f)=(Sg\circ f)\left(f^{\prime}\right)^{2}+Sf,

respectively, which hold whenever the composition
g\circ f
is defined.

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of locally univalent analytic functions

f
are essential tools for analyzing their geometric properties. They provide conditions for global univalence and impose geometric constraints on the range of
f
. Notably, any univalent analytic function
f
in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
satisfies the sharp inequality

\displaystyle|Pf(z)|\leq\frac{6}{1-|z|^{2}},\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Becker’s univalence criterion (see
[
10
]
) states that if
f
is locally univalent and analytic in
\mathbb{D}
, then

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|zPf(z)|\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\leq 1.

Then
f
is univalent in the unit disk. In
[
11
]
, Becker and Pommerenke later proved that the constant
1
in (
1.1
) is sharp.

Also, the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms (the hyperbolic sup-norms) of
f\in\mathcal{LU}
are defined by

\displaystyle||P_{f}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)|P_{f}(z)|\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;||S_{f}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)^{2}|S_{f}(z)|,

respectively.

These norms have significant meaning in the theory of Teichmüler spaces (see
[
30
]
). For a univalent function
f\in\mathcal{LU}
, it is well known that
||P_{f}||\leq 6
and
||S_{f}||\leq 6
(see
[
29
,
35
]
) and these estimates are best possible. On the other hand, for a locally univalent function
f\in\mathcal{LU}
, it is also known that if
||P_{f}||\leq 1
(see
[
13
,
10
]
) or
||S_{f}||\leq 2
(see
[
35
]
), then the function
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. In
1976
, Yamashita (see
[
42
]
) proved that
||P_{f}||
is finite if, and only if,
f
is uniformly locally univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Furthermore, if
||P_{f}||\leq 2
, then
f
is bounded in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
28
]
). The pre-Schwarzian norm has been studied by many researchers (see, for example,

\displaystyle f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let

1.1.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of analytic functions.

The pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives are important research tools in geometric function theory, especially to characterize Teichumüller space by using the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives embedding models. They are also powerful tools in the research of the inner radius of univalency for planer domains and quasiconformal extensions, (see
[
30

31
]

Later, people have done a lot of research on the relationship between Schwarzian derivative and Univalent function. Several sufficient conditions for univalent analytic functions are obtained by using the notions of Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives.

It is well known that the pre-Schwarzian norm
||Ph||\leq 6
for the Univalent analytic function
h
is defined in
\mathbb{D}
. In

, Becker
[

]
used the pre-Schwarzian derivative to obtain the sufficient condition that the function in
\mathbb{D}
is univalent, in other words, if
||Ph||\leq 1
, then the function

is univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. For different aspect of Schwarzian derivatives, we refer to the articles
[

,

,
2
]
and references therein.

Pre-Schwarzian and derivatives of Harmonic functions

A twice continuously differentiable complex-valued function
f=u+iv
in a domain
\Omega
is called harmonic if
u
and
v

\Omega
or equivalently if it satisfies the Laplace equation
\Delta f=4f{{}_{z\bar{z}}}=0
. Every harmonic mapping
f
has a canonical representation of the form
f=h+\bar{g}
, where
h
and
g
are analytic functions in
\Omega
called the analytic and co-analytic part of
f
, respectively. The jacobian of
f=h+\bar{g}
is given by
J_{f}(z)=|f{{}_{z}}|^{2}-|f{{}_{\bar{z}}}|^{2}=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}

is called orientation-preserving or sense-preserving mapping if
J_{f}(z)>0
and is called orientation-reversing or sense-reversing mapping if
J_{f}(z)<0
. For a sense-preserving harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
, its second complex dilatation is
\Omega
with
\omega=g^{\prime}/h^{\prime}
and
|\omega(z)<1|
in
\Omega_{1}
. According to Lewy’s theorem, see
[
32
]
, a harmonic mapping
f=h+\bar{g}
is locally univalent in
\Omega
if its Jacobian
J_{f}(z)\neq 0
. Stuyding the sufficient conditions for a locally univalent harmonic mapping to be univalent , as well as the necessary conditions for a univalent harmonic mapping is an interesting research area. For harmonic pre-Schwarzian and its applications, we refer to the articles
[
16
,
44
,
20
,
21
,
34
]
.

Harmonic mappings play the natural role in parameterizing minimal surfaces in the context of differential geometry. Planner harmonic mappings have application not only in the differential geometry but also in the various field of engineering, physics, operations research and other intriguing aspects of applied mathematics. The theory of harmonic functions has been used to study and solve fluid flow problems (see
[
1
]
. The theory of univalent harmonic functions having prominent geometric properties like starlikeness, convexity and close-to-convexity appear naturally while dealing with planner fluid dynamical problems. For instance, the fluid flow problem on a convex domain satisfying an interesting geometric property has been extensively studied by Aleman and Constantin
[
1
]
. With the help of geometric properties of harmonic mappings, Constantin and Martin
[

have obtained a complete solution of classifying all two dimensional fluid flows.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denotes the class of all harmonic mappings

with the normalization

\displaystyle h(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}.

As harmonic mapping is the generalization of analytic function, there are many open problem and interesting fact in planer harmonic mappings, (see, e.g.

In

\displaystyle Sf

\displaystyle=\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{zz}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{z}

\displaystyle=Sh+\frac{\bar{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\omega-\omega^{\prime\prime}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\omega^{\prime}\bar{\omega}}{1-|\omega|^{2}}\right)^{2},

The pre-Schwarzian derivative of harmonic mapping

\displaystyle Pf(z)=\left(\log J_{f}\right)_{z}=\frac{h^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{\omega^{\prime}(z)\bar{\omega}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}.

2.
Pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norm Estimates for Ozaki Close-to-Convex functions

In this section, we firstly give the equivalent characterization of the class
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
(Ozaki close-to-convex class), next we present the distortion and growth theorem, and then we derive the results of pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms for the class of
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
in terms of the value of
f^{\prime\prime}(0)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1
, the following are equivalent:

(i)
.

f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
.

(ii)
.

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+2\lambda}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}

for

\displaystyle\lambda\in[1/2,1]
,

(iii)
.

\displaystyle\bigg|(1-|z|^{2})\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-(1+2\lambda)\bar{z}\bigg|\leq 1+2\lambda
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Firstly, we will prove
(i)
is equivalent to
(ii)
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
, then there exists a Schwarz function
\omega:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1+2\lambda\omega(z)}{1-\omega(z)}

holds with
\omega(0)=0
.

Let
\omega(z)=z\phi(z)
for some analytic function
\phi
that satisfies
\phi(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
. Then it follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{(1+2\lambda)\omega(z)}{z(1-\omega(z))}

which shows that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{(1+2\lambda)\phi(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))}.

Consequently, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}}{(1+2\lambda)+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}}.

Since
|\phi(z)|^{2}\leq 1
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq(1+2\lambda)^{2}+2(1+2\lambda){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+|z|^{2}\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}.

By factorizing, an easy computation gives that

(2.4)

\displaystyle(1+2\lambda)\left[(1+2\lambda)+2{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\right]\geq(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}

which implies that

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq-(1+2\lambda)+\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+2\lambda}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}.

When
\lambda\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, it is easy to see that

(2.5)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)\geq\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{1+2\lambda}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}.

Next, we will prove that
(ii)
is equivalent to
(iii)
.

By multiplying (
2.4
) by
(1-|z|^{2})
both side, we have

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}\leq(1+2\lambda)^{2}(1-|z|^{2})+2(1+2\lambda)(1-|z|^{2}){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)

which turns out that

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|^{2}-2(1+2\lambda)(1-|z|^{2}){\rm Re}\left(\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)+(1+2\lambda)^{2}|z|^{2}\leq(1+2\lambda)^{2}.

Thus, we have the desired inequality

(2.6)

\displaystyle\bigg|(1-|z|^{2})\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)-(1+2\lambda)\bar{z}\bigg|\leq(1+2\lambda).

Hence, complete the proofs.

for all

(2.16)

\displaystyle|f(z)|\geq|f(z_{0})|\geq\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{1}{(1+\xi^{2})^{\frac{1+2\lambda}{2}}}d|\xi|.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we will find the sharp bound of the pre-Schwarzian and Schwarzian norms in terms of value
f^{\prime\prime}(0)
in the class
\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
, under the assumption that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. In order to prove our result, we need the following lemma, which we can find in the proof of

Lemma A
.

[
16
]

If
\phi(z):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be analytic function, then

(2.17)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{(\phi(0)+|z|)^{2}}{(1-|\phi(0)|)^{2}(1-|z|^{2})|)}

We derive results that provide sharp bounds for the pre-Schwarzian norm in terms of the value
f^{\prime\prime}(0)
for the function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
, particularly under the assumption
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
.

Theorem 2.3
.

If
h\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1
, then

(2.18)

\displaystyle||Pf||\leq(1+2\lambda).

The inequality is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Since
\phi_{1}(z)=z\xi_{1}(z)
, with
|\xi_{1}(z)|<1
, then in (
2.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\frac{(1+2\lambda)|z\xi_{1}(z)|}{1-|z|^{2}|\xi_{1}(z)|}

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda)\sup_{0\leq r\leq 1}\frac{r(1-r^{2})}{(1-r^{2})}

\displaystyle=(1+2\lambda).

Thus we desired bound is established.

The next part of the proof is to show that the bound is sharp. Henceforth, we consider the extremal function
f^{*}(z)
is given by

\displaystyle f^{*}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{1+2\lambda}{2}}}d\xi\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1.

It can be easily shown that
||Pf^{*}||=(1+2\lambda)
.
∎

From Theorem
2.3
, we obtain the following immediate result for the class
\mathcal{C}
of convex functions.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(1/2)=\mathcal{C}
then for all

, we have

\displaystyle||Pf||\leq 2.

The bound is sharp.

Sharpness of Corolary 4.3 :

For
\lambda=1/2

) that

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{\frac{1}{2}}}{f_{\frac{1}{2}}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{2z}{1-z^{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Pf_{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2z}{1-z^{2}}.

A simple computation thus yields that

\displaystyle||Pf_{\frac{1}{2}}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)|Pf_{\frac{1}{2}}|=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\frac{2|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}=2

and we see the constant
2
is sharp.

Remark 2.2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\frac{1}{2})=\mathcal{C}
, then for all

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{h_{1}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h_{1}^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|\leq 2|z|.

Our next result gives a sharp bound for the norm of the Schwarzian derivative when
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
by a direct application of the Schwarz lemma.

Theorem 2.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
, for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1
, then the Schwarzian norm

\displaystyle||Sf_{\lambda}||=(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sh(z)|\leq\frac{(1+2\lambda)(3-2\lambda)}{2}.

The inequality is sharp.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

From (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)=\frac{(1+2\lambda)\phi(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))}

A simple calculation shows that

(2.19)

\displaystyle Sf(z)=(1+2\lambda)\frac{\phi^{\prime}(z)+(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2})\phi^{2}(z)}{(1-z\phi(z))^{2}}.

By using triangle inequality and Schwarz pick lemma, we obtain

(2.20)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf|

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda)\bigg|\phi^{\prime}(z)+\left(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}\right)\phi^{2}(z)\bigg|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+2\lambda)(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}\left(\frac{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}+\left(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}\right)|\phi(z)|^{2}\right).

We define the function
\Phi(z):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
such that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\Phi(z)=\frac{\bar{z}-\phi(z)}{1-z\phi(z)}.

Since
\phi(\mathbb{D})\subseteq\mathbb{D}
, then we have
(1-|z|^{2})(1-|z\phi(z)|^{2})>0
, hence it follows that

(2.22)

\displaystyle|\bar{z}-\phi(z)|^{2}<|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}.

Thus, we conclude that
|\Phi(z)|^{2}<1
.

Consequently, we have the estimates

(2.23)

\displaystyle 1-|\Phi(z)|^{2}=\frac{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}

and

(2.24)

\displaystyle\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{|1-z\phi(z)|^{2}}=\frac{(1-|\Phi(z)|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})}.

If we replace the expression (
2.24
) in (
2.20
), we have

(2.25)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq(1+2\lambda)(1-|\Phi(z)|^{2})\left(1+(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2})\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})}{(1-|\phi(z)|^{2})}\right).

Since
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
implies that
\phi(0)=0
, using Lemma A, we easily obtain that

(2.26)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{|z|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}.

In view of (
2.26
) and (
2.25
), we obtain

(2.27)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq(1+2\lambda)(1-|\Phi(z)|^{2})\left(1+(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2})|z|^{2}\right).

Also, becouse
1-|\Phi(z)|^{2}
\leq
1, we see that

(2.28)

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|

\displaystyle\leq(1+2\lambda)\left(1+\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+2\lambda)(3-2\lambda)}{2}.

Thus our desired inequality is established.

Next part of the proof is to show the sharpness of the inequality. Henceforth, we consider the function
f_{\lambda}(z)
is given by

(2.29)

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{1+2\lambda}{2}}}d\xi,\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1

maximizes the Schwarzian norm defined as:

\displaystyle||Sf||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf|

and from this, the sharpness of the inequality holds for
\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda\leq 1
.

Note that

(2.30)

\displaystyle\frac{f^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}}{f_{\lambda}^{\prime}}(z)=\frac{(1+2\lambda)z}{1-z^{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;Sf_{\lambda}=\frac{(1+2\lambda)}{(1-z^{2})^{2}}\left[1+\left(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}\right)|z|^{2}\right]

which calculates

\displaystyle||Sf_{\lambda}||=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf_{\lambda}|=(1+2\lambda)\frac{(3-2\lambda)}{2}.

In general, the integral formula for
f_{\lambda}
given in above does not give primitives in terms of elementary functions, however when
\lambda=1/2
, we see that

\displaystyle f_{\frac{1}{2}}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{1}{(1-\xi^{2})}d\xi=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right),

where
||Sf_{\frac{1}{2}}||=2
.
∎

For the class
\mathcal{C}
, we have the an immediate result from Theorem
2.4
.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\frac{1}{2})=\mathcal{C}
, then for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

\displaystyle||Sf_{\frac{1}{2}}||\leq 2.

The inequality is sharp.

For the value
|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|
which is not necessarily zero, we will give a bound for the quantity
(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|
with
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
in the following.

Theorem 2.5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{0}(\lambda)
, for all

and
\gamma=|\phi(0)|=\frac{|f^{\prime\prime}(0)|}{(1+2\lambda)}

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq(1+2\lambda)\left(1+\frac{1-2\lambda}{2}\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right).

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Let
\gamma=|\phi(0)|
. In view of the Lemma A, we see that

(2.31)

\displaystyle\frac{|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}\leq\frac{(\gamma+|z|)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}.

If we substitute in (

) in (
2.25
), then we obtain

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|Sf(z)|\leq(1+2\lambda)(1-|\Phi(z)|^{2})\left(1+(\frac{1-2\lambda}{2})\frac{(\gamma+|z|)^{2}}{(1-\gamma^{2})}\right).

From the fact that

3.
Schwarzian derivatives for Harmonic mapping with fixed analytic part

In this article, we are concerned with a class of functions
\mathcal{G}(\beta)
,
\beta>0
, defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\beta)=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{A}:{\rm Re}\left(1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)<1+\frac{\beta}{2}\;\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\bigg\}.

In
1941
, Ozaki
[
36
]
introduced the class
\mathcal{G}:=\mathcal{G}(1)
and proved that functions in
\mathcal{G}
are univalent in
\mathbb{D}
. Later on, Umezawa
[
45
]
studied the class
\mathcal{G}
and showded that functions in
\mathcal{G}
are convex in one direction,
\emph{i.e.,}
every
f\in\mathcal{G}
maps
|z|=\rho<r
for every
\rho
near
r
into a contour which may be cut by every straight line parallel too this direction in not more than two points. Moreover, functions in
\mathcal{G}
are starlike in
\mathbb{D}
(see
[
22
,
37
]
) . Thus, the class
\mathcal{G}(\beta)
is included in
\mathcal{S}^{*}
whenever
\beta\in(0,1]
. One can easily show that functions in
\mathcal{G}(\beta)
are not univalent in
\mathbb{D}
for
\beta>1
.
0<\beta\leq 2/3
, the class
\mathcal{G}(\beta)
was studied by Uralegaddi
et al.
in
[
40
]
.

In this section, our main aim to derive estimates for the modulus of the pre-Schwarzian norm for the functions in
\mathcal{G}(\beta),\;\beta>0
. These result will provide a precise estimate of the pre-Schwarzian norm for functions
\mathcal{G}(\beta)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

If
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{G}(\beta)
, then
||P_{f}||\leq 2\beta+1
. The estimate is best possible.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let,
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{G}(\beta)
. Then
f
holds the subordination relation

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zf^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1-(1+\beta)z}{1-z},

which gives us

(3.1)

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|\leq\frac{\beta}{1-|z|}.

By the Schwarz-Pick lemmma and (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle||P_{f}||

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)|P_{f}|

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}-\frac{\bar{\omega}(z)\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left(\bigg|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{\bar{\omega}(z)\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\bigg|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(1-|z|^{2}\right)\left(\frac{\beta}{1-|z|}+\frac{|\bar{\omega}(z)|}{1-|z|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\left(\beta(1+|z|)+|\omega(z)|\right)

\displaystyle=2\beta+1.

We now show that the estimate is best possible. For

\begin{cases}\sqrt{1-\beta}\leq t<1\;\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;\beta\in[0,1)\vskip 5.69054pt\\
\dfrac{1-2\alpha}{3-2\alpha}\leq t<1\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;\beta\in[1,\infty),\end{cases}

we consider the function
f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{G}(\beta)
with the second complex dilatation
\omega_{t}(z)=\frac{z-t}{1-tz}
and
h(z)
be such that

\displaystyle 1+\frac{zh_{1}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{h_{1}^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1-(1+\beta)z}{1-z}.

Then clearly
f\in\mathcal{G(\beta)}
for all t in
[0,\infty)
. A simple calculation gives that

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\omega_{t}}(z)\omega_{t}(z)}{1-|\omega_{t}(z)|^{2}}=\frac{\bar{z}-t}{(1-tz)(1-|z|^{2})}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle||P_{f_{t}}||

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{h^{\prime\prime}_{1}(z)}{h^{\prime}_{1}(z)}-\frac{\bar{\omega}(z)\omega^{\prime}(z)}{1-|\omega(z)|^{2}}\bigg|

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{\beta}{1-|z|}-\frac{\bar{z}-t}{(1-tz)(1-|z|^{2})}\bigg|.

We set

\displaystyle M_{t}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})\bigg|\frac{\beta}{1-|z|}-\frac{\bar{z}-t}{(1-tz)(1-|z|^{2})}\bigg|

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in[0,1)}\bigg|\beta(1+r)-\frac{r-t}{1-tr}\bigg|=\sup_{z\in[0,1)}\;\phi(r),

where

\displaystyle\phi(r)=\beta(1+r)-\frac{r-t}{1-tr}.

A simple computation shows that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(r)=\beta-\frac{1-t^{2}}{(1-rt)^{2}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)=-\frac{2t(1-t^{2})(1-rt)}{(1-rt)^{4}}<0\;\;\mbox{for all r}\;\in[0,1).

Now,
\phi^{\prime}(r)=0
gives

\displaystyle r=r_{0}:=\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{t}\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{\beta}}.

Thus the maximum value of
\phi
is attained at
r_{0}
. Hence, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle M_{t}=\phi(r_{0})=\frac{1+2\beta-2\beta\sqrt{\frac{1-t^{2}}{\beta}}}{t}.

In view of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), it is clear that
M_{t}\leq||P_{f_{t}}||\leq 2\lambda+1
. We note that
M_{t}
is increasing function for t and
M_{t}\rightarrow 1+2\beta
as
t\rightarrow 1
. This shows that estimate is the best possible.
∎

The incompressible inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes-Vlasov-Fokker-Planck equations: global well-posedness and inviscid limit
Fucai Li, Jinkai Ni, Ling-Yun Shou, Dehua Wang

Abstract
. The global well-posedness and inviscid limit are investigated for the fluid-particle interaction system, described by the Navier-Stokes equations for the inhomogeneous incompressible viscous flows coupled with the Vlasov-Fokker-Planck equation for particles through a density-dependent nonlinear friction force in three-dimensional space. It is challenging to establish the inviscid limit over large time periods for the incompressible Euler equations under the influence of the weak dissipative mechanism generated by the friction force. We first prove the global stability of the equilibrium, in the sense that initial perturbations with appropriate Besov spatial regularity lead to global well-posedness and uniform regularity estimates with respect to the viscosity coefficient for strong solutions of the inhomogeneous Navier-Stokes-Vlasov-Fokker-Planck equations. In particular, we establish the optimal rates of convergence to equilibrium uniformly in Navier-Stokes. Then, we construct global solutions to the inhomogeneous Euler-Fokker-Planck equations via the vanishing viscosity limit. Furthermore, by capturing the dissipation arising from two-phase interactions, we rigorously justify the global-in-time strong convergence of the inviscid limit process, with a convergence rate that is in sharp contrast to that in the pure incompressible fluid case. To achieve this global convergence, novel ideas and new techniques are developed in the analysis and may be applied to other significant problems.

1.1.
Main results

Without loss of generality, we normalize
\bar{\rho},\bar{u}
and

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}=1,\quad\bar{u}=0,\quad M=M_{[1,0]}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{3}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}.

For the Euler-VFP system (
1.5
),
applying the transformations

we can reformulate it as

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\varrho+u\cdot\nabla\varrho=0,\\
&\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P+u-b=\frac{\varrho}{1+\varrho}\nabla P{-au},\\
&{\rm div}\,u=0,\\
&\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-u\cdot v\sqrt{M}-\mathcal{L}f\\
&\quad=\,{\frac{1}{2}}u\cdot vf-u\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\varrho\Big(\mathcal{L}f-u\cdot\nabla_{v}f+\frac{1}{2}u\cdot vf+u\cdot v\sqrt{M}\Big),\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle(\varrho,u,f)|_{t=0}=\,

\displaystyle(\varrho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x),f_{0}(x,v))

\displaystyle:=\,

\displaystyle\bigg(\rho_{0}(x)-1,u_{0}(x),\frac{F_{0}(x,v)-M}{\sqrt{M}}\bigg),\quad(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

\displaystyle a^{f}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sqrt{M}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v,\quad b^{f}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v\sqrt{M}f(t,x,v)\mathrm{d}v.

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\nabla(f^{\mu}-f)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}(f^{\mu}-f)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{v,\nu}(H^{1})}^{2}\big){\rm d}\tau\leq C_{2}\mu^{2}.

Remark 1.3
.

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.3
provides the first result concerning the global-in-time convergence of the inviscid limit for the incompressible Euler equations with weak dissipative effects (i.e.,
the dissipation mechanisms of
b^{f}-u
from the friction force). The coupled Euler-VFP system (
1.1
) is derived globally-in-time from the NS-VFP system (
1.5
) with the convergence rate of
\mathcal{O}(\mu)
.

For the pure incompressible Euler equations in three dimensions, the inviscid limit converge only locally in time due to possible finite-time blow-up. Regarding the inviscid limit problem of the pure incompressible Euler equations, we refer to the important works
[
53
,
16
,
17
,
34
,
45
,
44
]
and references therein.

Remark 1.4
.

In the proof of our results, one of the main difficulties arises in the nonlinear friction force
\varrho(u-v)
, which causes some additional nonlinear terms in the Fokker-Planck equations (

)
3
and (
1.9
)
3
, for example,
\varrho\mathcal{L}f
, where

is degenerate, in the sense that it does not possess any dissipation due to its transport nature. To overcome this difficulty, we establish the uniform evolution of lower-order energy on the spatial
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
(

) regularity for decay estimates, which ensures the

time integrability of
\|\nabla u\|_{H^{2}}
.

Remark 1.5
.

For
\mu\geq 1
, the well-posedness and large-time behavior stated in Theorem

still hold. Here we assume that
\mu<1
to establish uniform estimates with respect to
\mu
in this regime,
in order to take the inviscid limit
\mu\rightarrow 0
.

Remark 1.6
.

Theorems
1.1
–
1.3
hold true for more general coefficients
\kappa(\rho)
and
\lambda(\rho)
. For example, one may consider

\kappa(\rho)=\lambda(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha},\quad\alpha\geq 0.

Remark 1.7
.

The
L^{2}
and
\dot{H}^{1}
decay rates obtained in Theorems

–
1.2
are optimal since they coincide with the rates of the heat equation, while the
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
regularity assumption seems to be sharp for these optimal decay rates (see
[
8
, Page 610]
). If the
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
regularity is replaced by the
L^{q}
regularity for some
q\in[1,\frac{6}{5})
, then the following classical
L^{q}
–
L^{2}
type of
the optimal decay estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{l}u(t)\|_{H^{2-l}}+\|\nabla^{l}f(t)\|_{L_{v}^{2}(H^{2-l})}\leq\,

\displaystyle C_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{l+s_{q}}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{p}}+\|\nabla f(t)\|_{L_{v}^{2}(L^{p})}\leq\,

\displaystyle C_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{s_{q}}{2}},

for any
t\geq 0
,
2\leq p\leq 6
and
l=0,1
.
Here,
s_{q}:=3(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2})
. It should be noted that such a range

coincides with the significant work by Duan, Liu, Ukai, and Yang
[
21
]
.

1.2.
Difficulties, strategies and novelty

We now explain the challenges and main ideas used in the proofs of the above theorems. The results
in Theorem
1.1
are based on
a priori
estimates that are uniform with respect to both
\mu
and time (see Proposition
3.1
). Note that the dissipation effects of the relative quantity
b^{f^{\mu}}-u^{\mu}
and the microscopic part
\{\mathbf{I-P}\}f^{\mu}
can be derived from the effects of the friction term and the Fokker-Planck operator
-\mathcal{L}
. On the other hand, using the ideas developed in
[
28
,
20
,
12
]
, one can capture the degenerate dissipation for
a^{f^{\mu}}
and
b^{f^{\mu}}
due to the interactions of
v\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{\mu}
and
\mathcal{L}f^{\mu}
. Consequently, the dissipation for
u^{\mu}
and
f^{\mu}
can be derived as follows:

(1.21)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u^{\mu}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla f^{\mu}(t)\|_{L^{2}_{v}(H^{2})}^{2}\lesssim\mathcal{D}(u^{\mu},P^{\mu},f^{\mu})(t).

Note that the above inequality is independent of the viscosity
\mu
, which plays a key role in controlling nonlinear terms, e.g.,
u^{\mu}\cdot\nabla u^{\mu}
, uniformly with respect to both the time and
\mu
.

However, new observations and ideas are needed to handle the nonlinear terms involving the density perturbation
\varrho^{\mu}
. Indeed, different from the scenarios in compressible fluid
[
22
,
36
]
, the variable
\varrho^{\mu}
does not exhibit any dissipation due to its transport nature and the divergence-free condition
{\rm div}\,u^{\mu}=0
. Consequently, it is only possible to obtain a
L^{\infty}
time bound for
\varrho^{\mu}
, while
u^{\mu}
and
f^{\mu}
must take on all time integrability in the nonlinear analysis. In view of the transport theorem, establishing the uniform bound for
\varrho^{\mu}
relies on the
L^{1}
time integrability of
\|\nabla u^{\mu}\|_{H^{2}}
:

(1.22)

\displaystyle\|\varrho^{\mu}(t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq e^{\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u^{\mu}\|_{H^{2}}{\rm d}\tau}\|\varrho_{0}^{\mu}\|_{H^{3}}.

However, the dissipation generated from (
1.21
) only provides an
L^{2}
time integrability, which causes a time growth in the nonlinear analysis. To overcome this challenge, we aim to obtain sufficiently fast decay rates for
u^{\mu}
under suitable initial conditions, ensuring the uniform-in-time control of
\|\nabla u^{\mu}\|_{L^{1}(0,t;H^{2})}
. Due to the fact that there are higher velocity derivatives and weights in the nonlinear terms from the density-dependent friction force, e.g.,
\varrho\mathcal{L}f
, the classical method based on the use of pointwise estimates and Duhamel’s principle as in
[
12
,
22
]
may not be applicable here.

We develop a pure energy argument to address the above loss of decay. Instead of relying on the standard
H^{3}
energy, a key ingredient in our analysis is to construct a higher-order Lyapunov functional
\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)\sim\|\nabla u^{\mu}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla f^{\mu}\|_{L^{2}_{v}(H^{2})}^{2}
such that

(1.23)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}_{1}(t)+c\big(\|\nabla^{2}u^{\mu}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}f^{\mu}\|_{L^{2}_{v}(H^{2})}^{2}\big)\leq 0,

for some uniform constant
c>0
(see (
3.55
)). Such construction (
1.23
) requires the careful control of every nonlinear term to be absorbed by the dissipation in (
1.23
). Then, inspired by
[
29
]
, we apply the interpolation control in (
1.23
) to derive the time decay associated with the
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
regularity:

\displaystyle\|\nabla u^{\mu}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\nabla f^{\mu}\|_{L^{2}_{v}(H^{2})}

(1.24)

\displaystyle\quad\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\|\nabla u_{0}^{\mu}\|_{H^{2}}+\|\nabla f_{0}^{\mu}\|_{L^{2}_{v}(H^{2})}+\|u^{\mu}\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}}+\|f^{\mu}\|_{L^{2}_{v}(\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty})}\Big),

which is crucial for obtaining the bound (
1.22
) uniformly in time when
\frac{s}{2}+\frac{1}{2}>1
, i.e.,
s>1
. Note that the
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
–assumption was introduced by Sohinger and Strain
[
51
]
for the Boltzmann equation and by Xu and Kawashima
[
57
]
for partially dissipative hyperbolic systems. A key difference of our work is the
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
–norm of the solution, which ensures the decay in (
1.2
), is incorporated into enclosing
a priori
estimates to compensate for the lack of dissipation for
\varrho
. In particular, the decay relationship (
1.2
) is used in establishing the uniform evolution of the
\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}
regularity (see Proposition
3.8
). The above procedure leads to the uniform
a priori
estimates, and along with a bootstrap argument, it completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Building up the global existence and uniform regularity estimates with respect to
\mu
of solutions to the NS-VFP system (
1.9
), we can perform the compactness argument to obtain the global existence of the Euler-VFP system (
1.6
) as the viscosity coefficient
\mu\rightarrow 0
.

Finally, we establish a uniform-in-time error estimate (
1.20
) between the solutions to the inhomogeneous NS-VFP system (
1.9
) and the inhomogeneous Euler-VFP system (
1.6
), based on the existence of solutions obtained in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. This is accomplished by performing energy estimates on the difference between the two systems and analyzing each nonlinear term involving the error variables, utilizing the uniform regularity estimates (
1.12
) and (
1.17
) (see Lemmas
4.1
–
4.5
). It is important to note that the bound on
\varrho^{\mu}-\varrho
has to grow over time due to the nonlinear term in the error equation for
\varrho^{\mu}-\varrho
. To overcome this, we employ additional time-weighted regularity estimates. In particular, the uniform error estimate (
1.20
) leads to the global-in-time strong convergence of this limiting process, which contrasts sharply with the pure incompressible fluid case where only local convergence results are available.

Outline of the paper

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce some notations, state the macro-micro decomposition, the properties of the Fokker-Planck operator

Section 3 is devoted to the proof of Theorem

Using these estimates, we then establish the decay rates associated with the negative Besov evolution for

2.1.
Notations

The symbol
C

,

And

, and denote

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v,\nu}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(|\nabla_{v}g(v)|^{2}+\nu(v)|g(v)|^{2}\big){\rm d}v,\quad g=g(v).

When involving the spatial variable, we write
L^{2}_{x,v}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v})
associated with its norm
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}
. The norm in the space
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
(resp.
L^{2}_{v}(L^{p})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v};L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}))
) is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}_{v}(L^{p}_{v})}
).

For the two multi-indices
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3})
, we consistently use the notation

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{\beta}:=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}_{x_{1}}\partial^{\alpha_{2}}_{x_{2}}\partial^{\alpha_{3}}_{x_{3}}\partial^{\beta_{1}}_{v_{1}}\partial^{\beta_{2}}_{v_{2}}\partial^{\beta_{3}}_{v_{3}},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
denotes the spatial variable
and
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
denotes the microscopic velocity of the particles.
For any
s\geq 0
, the norm in the Sobolev space
H^{s}=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
is endowed by the norm:

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{s}}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\|\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}}.

We also write the norm of
L^{2}_{v}(H^{s})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}))
by

\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}(H^{s})}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\|\partial^{\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Next, let’s recall the definition of a homogeneous Besov space. Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a smooth function such that
\varphi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1

and
\varphi(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq 2
. Let
\phi(\xi)=\varphi(\xi)-\varphi(2\xi)
and
\phi_{j}(\xi)=\phi(2^{-j}\xi)
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By construction, we have
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi_{j}(\xi)=1
for all

\xi\neq 0
.
Define
\dot{\Delta}_{j}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\phi_{j})*f
, where
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denotes the inverse Fourier transform. Then, we have the Littlewood-Paley decomposition

u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u,

which holds in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
modulo polynomials.
For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, we define the
homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}:=\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{rsj}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\|_{L^{p}}^{r}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}},

and if
r=\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,\infty}}:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{sj}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\|_{L^{p}}.

For more details on Besov spaces, one can refer to
[
2
]
.

We shall use the notation
L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})
to denote
the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v};\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})
equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L_{v}^{2}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}^{2}\mathrm{d}v\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, we have
\dot{H}^{s}=\dot{B}^{s}_{2,2}
. Besides, we set the following weighted norm:

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v,\nu}(\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\|\nabla_{v}g\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\nu(v)\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}v\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

2.2.
Macro-micro decomposition and properties of
\mathcal{L}

We introduce the macro-micro decomposition for the distribution function, which was introduced in
[
28
]
for the Boltzmann equation and developed by
[
20
]
for the Fokker-Planck equation (see also
[
22
,
12
]
).

The function
f(t,x,v)
is decomposed into
its macroscopic (fluid) component
\mathbf{P}f
and its microscopic component
\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f
, such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle f=\mathbf{P}f+\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f.

Here,
\mathbf{P}
represents the velocity orthogonal projection operator defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}:L^{2}\rightarrow{\rm Span}\big\{\sqrt{M},v_{1}\sqrt{M},v_{2}\sqrt{M},v_{3}\sqrt{M}\big\},

and can be further decomposed as

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}:=\mathbf{P}_{0}\oplus\mathbf{P}_{1}.

Define

\displaystyle a_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\sqrt{M}\,dv,\quad b_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}fv\sqrt{M}\,dv.

Then, we have
\mathbf{P}f=\mathbf{P}_{0}f+\mathbf{P}_{1}f
with
\mathbf{P}_{0}f:=a^{f}\sqrt{M}
and
\mathbf{P_{1}}f:=b^{f}v\sqrt{M}
.

It is well-known that the Fokker-Planck operator
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint and possesses the following coercive property

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{L}f,f\right\rangle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|\nabla_{v}\left(\frac{f}{\sqrt{M}}\right)\right|^{2}M\mathrm{d}v.

Define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ker}\mathcal{L}:=\operatorname{Span}\big\{\sqrt{M}\big\},\quad\operatorname{Range}\mathcal{L}:=\operatorname{Span}\big\{\sqrt{M}\big\}^{\perp}.

Then there is a constant
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle-\left\langle\mathcal{L}f,f\right\rangle\geq\lambda_{0}\|\{\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{P}\}f\|_{L^{2}_{\nu,v}}^{2}+|b^{f}|^{2}.

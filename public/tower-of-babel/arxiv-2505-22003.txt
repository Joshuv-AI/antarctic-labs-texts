Abstract
Pursuit of accessible legal assistance in India faces a critical gap, as many citizens struggle to leverage their legal rights due to limited awareness and access to relevant legal information. This paper introduces Legal Assist AI, a transformer-based model designed to bridge this gap by offering effective legal assistance through large language models (LLMs). The system retrieves relevant legal information from a curated database and generates accurate responses, enabling effective assistance for diverse users, including legal professionals, scholars, and the general public. The model was fine-tuned on extensive datasets from the Indian legal domain, including Indian Constitution, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) and so forth, providing a robust understanding of the complexities of Indian law. By incorporating domain-specific legal datasets, the proposed model demonstrated remarkable efficiency and specialization in legal Question-Answering. The model was evaluated against state-of-the-art models such as GPT-3.5 Turbo and Mistral 7B, achieving a 60.08% score on the AIBE, outperforming its competitors in legal reasoning and accuracy. Unlike other models, Legal Assist AI avoided common issues such as hallucinations, making it highly reliable for practical legal applications. It showcases the model’s applicability in real-world legal scenarios, with future iterations aiming to enhance performance and expand its dataset to cover a broader range of multilingual and case-specific queries as well.

3.1
Data Preparation

Legal documents pertaining to the Indian Judicial System, including the Indian Constitution, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), Labor Laws, State Legislatures, SEBI Guidelines, Judicial Judgements and so forth were used in this study. Multiple legal contracts, their formats, etc., were also included in the dataset. All the legal documents for the study were downloaded from governmental and public websites, including court decisions, legislative text, and legal guidelines.

The dataset was then loaded using Directory Loader. This utility was used to effectively handle different tasks in natural language processing by aggregating and combining datasets within several directories. Langchain provides a strong support system for all such operations, ensuring that the data is prepared correctly and incorporated with ease into the model’s training pipeline.

Further, the documents were broken into manageable chunks. A chunk size of 1000 characters with an overlap of 20 characters across adjacent chunks was preferred to maintain continuity of context between chunks, which was important in maintaining fluency during legal document processing. This made the process of segmentation optimal and implied an improved treatment of long and complex legal texts.

Embeddings are dense vector representations of text that encode its semantic meanings, thus making it possible to treat much larger models in a better way and understand languages more. These vectors transform text into a numerical format that can be utilized for classification and similarity searches. It uses embeddings to compare and analyze content in terms of meaning. For generating the embeddings of each text chunk, HuggingFaceEmbeddings was used. The sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model was chosen for its effective and quick embeddings. This allowed a powerful base in indexing and retrieval for a legal assistant model.

After generating the embeddings, they were indexed in a process that uses FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search), a high-speed library for similar vector search and clustering. FAISS indexes made it possible to conduct fast and scalable searches by indexing the embeddings into a vector store that had been optimized for the retrieval of similar vectors at speed. This is important for comparing and finding similar text chunks, which many other processes in legal document analysis required.

After indexing, the vector store was saved locally, and that was easier for access in further queries and analysis. The embeddings were kept in a local vector store to allow effective information retrieval by boosting performance and accuracy in legal document processing.

Architecture of Base Model Used

The Proposed Legal Assist AI Architecture integrates a Retrieval QA Chain and a Large Language Model (LLM) to efficiently handle legal queries. The model processes input text tokens (queries) by retrieving relevant information from a vector store using semantic search, which fetches contextual data to enhance the query. This enriched query is then passed to the LLM, which generates a legal response based on the query and its context. The response is finally converted into output text tokens. By combining retrieval-based techniques with LLM capabilities, this architecture ensures accurate and context-aware answers to complex legal questions. Specifically, the process involves parsing the input
X
into a query using a retrieval QA chain (
RQAC
), retrieving contextual data from a vector store via semantic search (
\text{search}(f(X),\text{VectorStore})
), merging the retrieved information into a final prompt, and then utilizing the LLM to generate the response
Y

3.2.3
Training

The quantized version of the transformer model had been used, with Q4_0 as the quantization method. The methodology had brought down the model size to circa 4.7 GB, which had significantly improved the inference time and efficiency with no loss in high accuracy. The choice of the type of quantization to apply had been informed by the necessity to be able to run this model in a very resource-constrained environment without sacrificing performance
[
13
]
.

The original base model had been pretrained on more than 15 trillion tokens from publicly available sources. Later, the prepared set of embedded vector store had been fine-tuned on the model by adjusting the parameters of the model. This process had focused on maximizing the model’s performance in legal question answering while making use of the detailed nuances and complex structures in legal texts to ensure the model’s understanding and responses to a broad spectrum of legal questions had been accurate and relevant.

4.2
Evaluation using All India Bar Examination dataset

This test was conducted to assess the model’s knowledge domain and to evaluate its accuracy based on objective-based questions. By leveraging a standardized legal examination, the evaluation aimed to determine how effectively the model could understand and respond to various legal scenarios.

4.2.1
Dataset

All India Bar Examination is a test conducted by the Bar Council of India for law graduates who are interested in practicing as advocates. The candidate will be provided with a certificate of practice on passing the examination and can appear in any court throughout India. The dataset is made up of questions that have been drawn from the AIBE examinations running from AIBE 4 to AIBE 16, and it covers a time span of 12 years. There are a total of 1156 questions, all multiple-choice. The passing grade in the examination stands at 40%.

5
Discussions

Previous legal assistance models, such as GPT3.5 Turbo, Mistral 7B, and AALAP, have struggled to achieve better performance due to limitations in their training datasets. The scarcity of high-quality, domain-specific, and case specific training data hindered the development of effective legal AI models. The proposed Legal Assist AI Model outperformed the current state-of-the-art models such as GPT3.5 Turbo, Mistral 7B, and AALAP in delivering results and insights. The proposed Legal Assist AI model achieved a score of 60.08% on the AIBE examination, outperforming all other models. Notably, unlike GPT 3.5 Turbo, the proposed model did not hallucinate. The superior performance of the Legal Assist AI Model highlights the importance of high-quality training data in developing effective legal AI solutions. However, due to the lack of data specific to the case, Legal Assist AI model was unable to answer few questions. Still, this can be further generalized by enhancing the training corpus and adding more case-specific data such that a possible increment or decrement in the performance of the model could be noticed in future studies.

6
Conlusion

This paper introduced the development and evaluation of a fine-tuned large language model that is specifically designed for legal tasks and queries in the Indian judicial system. The capability of this model was quite impressive, as it generated accurate responses to legal queries in various languages and obtained high similarity scores. Unlike other models—for instance, the ChatGPT model—this model has greatly reduced instances of hallucination, making the outputs reliable and trustworthy.

Of particular mention is the performance of this Legal Assist AI model on the AIBE examination, where the model scored an impressive 60%, surpassing the performance of other models tested earlier. Notably, this was achieved despite the model using significantly fewer parameters—just 8 billion compared to the 175 billion parameters of GPT-3.5—yet still outperforming the larger model. This demonstrates the potential of the model to assist legal practitioners by carrying out tedious tasks, such as looking for relevant sections and articles pertaining to a specific case, and thus making valuable contributions to the field.

Quality performance of the model can be enhanced by supplementing quality training data with more expert reviews and additional case specific data. However, it should be noted that increasing the data amount may result in either significant improvements or potential decreases in accuracy, respectively. Continuous improvement will ensure that the Legal Assist AI model remains a useful tool for the general public, providing them accurate and efficient assistance.

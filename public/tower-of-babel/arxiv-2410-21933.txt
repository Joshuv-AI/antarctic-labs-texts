Hydrodynamic limits and non-equilibrium fluctuations for the Symmetric Inclusion Process with long jumps
Mario Ayala, Johannes Zimmer

Abstract
We consider a d d -dimensional symmetric inclusion process ( SIP ), where particles are allowed to jump arbitrarily far apart. We establish both the hydrodynamic limit and non-equilibrium fluctuations for the empirical measure of particles. With the help of self-duality and Mosco convergence of Dirichlet forms, we extend structural parallels between exclusion and inclusion dynamics from the short-range scenario to the long-range setting. The hydrodynamic equation for the symmetric inclusion process turns out to be of non-local type. At the level of fluctuations from the hydrodynamic limit, we demonstrate that the density fluctuation field converges to a time-dependent generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process whose characteristics are again non-local.



1
Introduction

Within the field of non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, Interacting Particle Systems (IPS) are simple
models to study interactions in complex systems, both in equilibrium and nonequilibrium. Suitable IPS offer
the
possibility to rigorously formalize the micro-to-macro transition
[
16
,
8
]
.

Here we focus on establishing scaling limits for an IPS that can be viewed as the bosonic counterpart of the
well-studied exclusion
process
[
21
,
16
,
10
,
13
,
9
]
:
the symmetric inclusion process, introduced in
[
12
]
. Specifically, we aim to derive the
hydrodynamic limit and investigate non-equilibrium fluctuations in the case where particles can interact with
other particles arbitrarily far apart, thus allowing for long jumps.

In the short-range context, the prototypical hydrodynamic equation for both exclusion and inclusion process is
of diffusive type, i.e., it is known that the empirical measure converges in distribution to a measure which
is absolutely continuous and whose density solves the partial differential equation

\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)=\alpha\Delta\rho(t,x),

(1)

with
\alpha>0
and suitable initial conditions. Moreover, fluctuations from the hydrodynamic limit are also
well established for the short-range setting. It is known
[
10
,
4
]
that the
density fluctuation field converges, in equilibrium, both for the exclusion and inclusion process, to a
limiting field that formally can be understood as solving the stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE)

dY_{t}=\alpha\Delta Y_{t}+\sqrt{\rho(\alpha+\sigma\rho)\nabla^{2}}dW_{t},

with
dW_{t}
being space-time white noise. Here the case
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma=-1
corresponds to the

\alpha
-exclusion
process and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\sigma=1
to inclusion dynamics.

Hydrodynamic limits in the context of long-jumps for the exclusion process were first established by
Jara
[
15
]
, who formulated a Cauchy problem associated with an infinitesimal generator
of non-local type. The first main result of this paper establishes that structural similarities between the
exclusion and inclusion processes persist even in the long-jump setting. Specifically, we show that the
hydrodynamic equation of the long-jump setting coincides with that of the exclusion process, namely

\partial_{t}\rho(t,x)=\alpha(\,K_{q}-\mathrm{Id})\,\rho(t,x),

where
\mathrm{Id}
denotes the identity operator and

K_{q}f(x):=q\star f(x)

is the convolution with
q
.

Subsequently, Gonçalves and Jara
[
13
]
investigated fluctuations around the
hydrodynamic limit for the exclusion process in equilibrium, considering initialization from Bernoulli
product measures. They demonstrate that the scaling limit of density fluctuations corresponds to a fractional
generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process. Our main contribution lies in the establishment of

non-equilibrium fluctuations
for the inclusion process, i.e., fluctuations around states which are
not necessarily in equilibrium, for the long-range situation. We find that the density fluctuation field
converges to a time-dependent generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process of characteristics

\{\alpha(\,K_{q}-\mathrm{Id}),\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}\}
, namely

dY_{t}=\alpha(\,K_{q}-\mathrm{Id})Y_{t}\,dt+\sqrt{\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}}dW_{t},

with

\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}f(x):=\int(\alpha+\rho(t,y))q(y-x)\left(f(y)-f(x)\right)^{2}\,dy,

and
\rho(t,x)
solving the non-local hydrodynamic equation. Reduced to equilibrium, this coincides with the
results of
[
13
]
for the exclusion process.

To facilitate our analysis, we rely on self-duality, a property exhibited by both the inclusion and the
exclusion process. As a consequence of this property, the
k
-point correlation functions in the model obey
closed-form equations. The availability of closed-form correlations greatly facilitates the derivation of
scaling limits, as it allows us to control the growth of moments. In addition to duality, we depart from the
traditional assumption that an invariance principle holds for the underlying dynamics of a single particle;
instead we consider the convergence of Dirichlet forms in the sense of Mosco. We assume the Mosco convergence
of Dirichlet forms for only a single particle, and from the convergence of
k
copies (
k\in\mathbb{N}
) of the
independent motions we derive (using results from
[
2
]
) the convergence of Dirichlet forms
for the inclusion process. The Mosco convergence of
k

SIP
-particles enables us, in particular, to
express the quadratic variation of the density fluctuation field in terms of the solution of the hydrodynamic
equation, and hence by standard arguments establish the non-equilibrium fluctuations. To the best of our
knowledge, this is the first time that this approach has been employed to establish non-equilibrium
fluctuations of IPS.

The rest of this work is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide the necessary
background, including the precise formulation of the model and the concept of
self-duality. Section
3
presents our main results along with the required
assumptions. Subsequently, in Section
4
, we rigorously introduce and establish the uniqueness of
what we term a time-dependent generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process of characteristics

\{(\alpha,K_{q}-Id),\rho(t,\cdot),\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}\}
. The proofs of our main results are
presented in Section
5
, which is divided into two subsections: one dedicated to the proof of the
hydrodynamic limits and the other to the fluctuations from the hydrodynamic limit. Finally, in the Appendix,
we include auxiliary notions and results, such as the concept of Mosco convergence of Dirichlet forms, and the
proof of the uniqueness of the time-dependent generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The model

The
Symmetric Inclusion Process
with parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, abbreviated

\text{SIP}(\alpha)
, is an interacting particle system where particles randomly hop on a lattice

\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Configurations of particles are denoted by Greek letters

\eta
and
\xi
, belonging to the set
\Omega_{n}:=\mathbb{N}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{n}}
, where we reserve the letter
\xi
for
configurations with a finite number of particles. Here, the expression
\eta(x)
represents the number of
particles at the site
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{n}
. We are interested in the time evolution of the processes

\{\,\eta_{r(n)t}\mid t\geq 0\,\}
, where
r(n):=n^{\beta}
. This process can be described in terms of the
infinitesimal generator

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{n}f(\eta)

\displaystyle=n^{\beta}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}}q(n(y-x))\,\eta(x)\left(\alpha+\eta(y)\right)\left(f(\eta-\delta_{x}+\delta_{y})-f(\eta)\right),

(2)

for some
\beta\in(0,2)
. Here the subscript
n
denotes scaling in space (through
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}
) and in time
(through
n^{\beta}t
), and
q\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
is a symmetric, continuous strictly positive function
with total mass equal to one,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}q(x)\,dx=1,

(3)

and such that for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

q(z)=n^{-(d+\beta)}q(z/n),

(4)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

A
SSUMPTION
2.1
.

Assume that there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{|x-y|^{d+\beta}}\leq q(x-y)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{|x-y|^{d+\beta}},

(5)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
x\neq y
.

Consider the case
n=1
, i.e., ignoring the time-space rescaling. Then the process governed by the
generator (
2
) can be interpreted as a system of particles equipped with two Poisson clocks in the
following way. Firstly, the first clock rings at random exponential times of rate
\alpha
. When this first
clock rings for a particle at a position
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the particle independently moves to a new
position
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with a probability of
q(y-x)
, unaffected by other particles. The second clock
rings at a constant rate of one. The particle transitions in this case from position
x
to
y
at a rate
proportional to
\eta(y)q(y-x)
. The process is called inclusion process as a particle at position
x

is attracted by the
\eta(y)
particles position
y
.

2.2
Self-duality

The process
\text{SIP}(\alpha)
satisfies a self-duality property that will facilitate the derivation of the main
results of this work. The notion of self-duality is analogous to that of integrable systems in the sense that
IPS satisfying a moment duality are systems for which the BBGKY hierarchy closes, and as a consequence of this,
the
k
-particle
correlation functions obey closed-form
equations. Here we introduce this notion in terms of semigroups and generators. Moreover, we explain the additional point
of view of self-duality in terms of compatible coordinate processes.

2.2.1
Self-duality in terms of semigroups

Let us denote the set of configurations with a finite number of particles by
\Omega_{n}^{f}
,

\Omega_{n}^{f}:=\mathop{\bigcup}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{n}^{(k)},

(6)

where

\Omega_{n}^{(k)}=\Big\{\xi\in\Omega_{n}:\|\xi\|:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\xi(x)=k\Big\}.

(7)

A
self-duality function
is a function
D\colon\Omega_{n}^{f}\times\Omega_{n}\to\mathbb{R}

such that

\mathbb{E}_{\eta_{0}}\big[D(\xi_{0},\eta_{t})\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\xi_{0}}\big[D(\xi_{t},\eta_{0})\big],

(8)

for all
\xi_{0}\in\Omega_{n}^{f}
and
\eta_{0}\in\Omega_{n}
. Notice that in the left hand side of (
8
)
we are computing the conditional expectation of the duality function evaluated at the time-evolved
\eta_{t}

given the knowledge of the initial configuration
\eta_{0}
, while on the right hand side we are evolving the
simpler configuration
\xi_{t}
given the information
\xi_{0}
.

For the
\text{SIP}(\alpha)
the self-duality functions are of factorized form

D(\xi,\eta)=\prod_{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}}d(\xi(x),\eta(x)),

(9)

where the single-site (triangular) duality functions are given by

d(m,n):={\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{\{m\leq n\}}\,\frac{n!}{(n-m)!}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha+m)}.

(10)

Notice that these duality functions are multinomials on the
\eta
variables with degree equal to the number
of particles in the finite configuration
\xi
. In particular, we have
d(0,n)=1
and

d(1,n)=\frac{n!}{(n-1)!}\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}=n\cdot\frac{1}{\alpha}
, hence
with
n=\eta(x)

\eta(x)=\alpha\,D(\delta_{x},\eta).

(11)

2.2.2
Self-duality in terms of generators

The self-duality relation (
8
) can also be express at the level of generators as

\mathscr{L}_{n}D(\xi,\cdot)(\eta)=\mathscr{L}_{n}^{(k)}D(\cdot,\eta)(\xi),

(12)

again for all
\xi\in\Omega_{n}^{f}
and
\eta\in\Omega_{n}
, where
\mathscr{L}_{n}
denotes the
generator (
2
) and
\mathscr{L}_{n}^{(k)}
its restriction to
\Omega_{n}^{(k)}
. Whenever the choice of

k\in\mathbb{N}
is clear, we denote by
\{\,\xi(t)\mid t\geq 0\,\}
the
\Omega_{n}^{(k)}
-valued Markov
process, namely the process with generator

\mathscr{L}_{n}^{(k)}f(\xi)=n^{\beta}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}}q(n(y-x))\xi(x)(\alpha+\xi(y))(f(\xi-\delta_{x}+\delta_{y})-f(\xi))

(13)

acting on functions
f\colon\Omega_{n}^{(k)}\to\mathbb{R}
.

2.2.3
Self-duality in coordinate notation

The finite process
\{\,\xi(t)\mid t\geq 0\,\}
can be realized in terms of a compatible
(see
[
6
]
for details on this notion) coordinate process on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{dk}
, where
particles have fixed labels, namely

X^{(k)}(t)=(X_{1}(t),\ldots,X_{k}(t)),\qquad X_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d},\quad\forall i=1,\ldots,k,

(14)

and
X_{i}(t)
is the position of the
i
th particle at time
t\geq 0
. More
precisely, for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{kd}_{n}
the compatible configuration

\xi(\mathbf{x})\in\Omega_{n}^{(k)}
is given by

\xi({\mathbf{x}})=\left(\xi({\mathbf{x}})(y),y\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}\right)\qquad\text{with}\qquad\xi(\mathbf{x})(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}_{x_{i}=y}.

(15)

The evolution of the coordinate process can be described in terms of the infinitesimal generator

L_{n}^{(k)}f(\mathbf{x})=n^{\beta}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q(r)\Bigg(\alpha+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{k}\mathbf{1}_{x_{j}=x_{i}+r/n}\Bigg)\left(f(\mathbf{x}^{i,i+r})-f(\mathbf{x})\right),

(16)

where
\mathbf{x}^{i,i+r}
denotes
\mathbf{x}
after moving the particle in position
x_{i}
to position
x_{i}+r/n\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{d}
.

By compatibility, see
[
6
]
, the self-duality relations can then also be expressed in
terms of the coordinate process generator as

\mathscr{L}_{n}D(\xi,\cdot)(\eta)=\mathscr{L}_{n}^{(k)}D(\cdot,\eta)(\xi)=L_{n}^{(k)}D(\xi(\cdot),\eta)(\mathbf{x}),

(17)

or, equivalently, in terms of semigroups as

\mathbb{E}_{\eta}\big[D(\xi,\eta_{t})\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\big[D(\xi_{t},\eta)\big]=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}}\big[D(\xi(\cdot),\eta)(X^{(k)}(t))\big],

(18)

in both cases for all
\xi\in\Omega_{n}^{f},\eta\in\Omega_{n}
, and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{kd}
.

Assumptions and main results

Let

3.3
Second main result: Non-equilibrium fluctuations

In the case of
\text{SIP}(\alpha)
with a symmetric transition probability restricted to nearest neighbors,
starting from a reversible measure with homogeneous density
\rho
, it is known (see for
example
[
4
]
) that the sequence of processes

\{\,Y_{t}^{(n)}(\cdot,\eta)\mid n\in\mathbb{N},t\in[0,T]\,\}
converges in distribution to the
distribution-valued process
\{\,Y_{t}\mid t\in[0,T]\,\}
which formally solves the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck
SPDE

dY_{t}=\alpha\Delta Y_{t}+\sqrt{\rho(\alpha+\rho)\nabla^{2}}dW_{t}:=\alpha\Delta Y_{t}+\sqrt{\rho(\alpha+\rho)}\nabla dW_{t},

(31)

where loosely speaking the term
\sqrt{\rho(\alpha+\rho)\nabla^{2}}dW_{t}
has to be interpreted by saying
that the integral

\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\rho(\alpha+\rho)\nabla^{2}}dW_{s}(\varphi)

(32)

is a continuous martingale of quadratic variation

\rho(\alpha+\rho)\left\lVert\nabla\varphi(x)\right\rVert^{2}t

(33)

for all test functions
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
denotes the usual
L^{2}
-norm. This can be
made rigorous in terms of martingale problems in the spirit of Chapter 11 in
[
16
]
.

Before stating our main result relative to fluctuations, let us introduce the non-local operator

\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}f(x):=\int(\alpha+\rho(t,y))q(y-x)\left(f(y)-f(x)\right)^{2}\,dy,

(34)

with
\rho(t,x)
denoting the unique solution to the hydrodynamic equation (
29
).

, the sequence of processes

\{\,Y_{t}^{(n)}(\cdot,\eta)\mid n\in\mathbb{N},t\in[0,T]\,\}
converges in distribution, as
n\to\infty
,
with respect to the

-Skorohod topology of

to a process

\{\,Y_{t}\mid t\in[0,T]\,\}
, which
formally
is the unique solution of the generalized
time-dependent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process of characteristics

\{L,\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}\}

(35)

with
dW_{t}
being space-time white noise, and
\rho(t,x)
solves the hydrodynamic equation
(
29
).

Analogous to the short-range situation, and at a formal level, the term
\sqrt{\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}}dW_{t}
has to be interpreted by saying that the integral

\int_{0}^{t}\sqrt{\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}}dW_{s}(\varphi)

(36)

is a continuous martingale of quadratic variation

\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}\varphi(x)}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(s,x)\,dx)}\,ds

(37)

for all test functions
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is then our task to make rigorous this definition. We refer the reader to Section
4
were this task is completed following ideas from
[
13
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
16
]
.

EMARK
3.5
.

Notice that in (

). The rationale for this choice is to emphasize the analogous nature

4
Generalized time-dependent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

We now properly define a generalized time-dependent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process of characteristics

\{L,\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}\}
. We call this process
time-dependent
since the
quadratic characteristic
\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}
is allowed to depend on time. Formally, we would like to define
this process as being the solution to the SPDE

dY_{t}=LY_{t}+\sqrt{\rho(t,\cdot)\,\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}}dW_{t},

(38)

where
\Gamma_{t}^{\rho}
is given is as in (
34
). In order to make sense of this definition, we
will make use of martingale problems along the lines of
[
13
]
and

[
9
]
. Since the space
S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is not necessarily invariant under the action of the generator
L
, some care has to
be taken to make sense of the first term in the right hand side of (
38
),
see
[
13
]
. Namely,
Y_{t}(L\varphi)
is in principle not defined for a general test function

\varphi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This can be overcome introducing the notion of stationary
S^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued processes,
see
[
13
, Definition 2.5]
.

D
EFINITION
4.1
.

We say that an
S^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued process
\{\,Y_{t}\mid t\in[0,T]\,\}
, defined on some
probability space

, is

if for any
t\in[0,T]
the process

is a
white noise of covariance given by

\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{t}(\varphi)^{2}\right]=\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},m(\rho(t,x))\,dx)}^{2},

(39)

where
\rho(t,x)
is the unique solution to (
29
), and

is the

.

R
EMARK

An admissible process

5.1.2
Dynkin Martingales: hydrodynamics

We will make extensive use of integrals of non-negative test functions
\varphi
in
C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
against the
measures
\{\,\pi_{n}(\cdot)\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\,\}
. It is then convenient to give a name to these integrals,

\int\varphi\,d\pi_{n}(t)=:\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)=\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi).

(51)

We will abuse notation and write
\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi)
whenever it is convenient and clear. We have the well-known Dynkin formula have at our disposal, which says that the processes

M_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi):=\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)-\pi_{0}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)-\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{L}_{n}\pi_{s}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)\,ds

(52)

are mean zero martingales with quadratic variation

\langle M_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi)\rangle=\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{n}\pi_{s}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)\,ds,

(53)

where the operator
\Gamma_{n}
is the so-called Carré du Champ operator associated to the generator
\mathscr{L}_{n}
.

First, we have the following application of duality with one particle.

L
EMMA
5.1
.

The Dynkin martingale (
52
) has the following properties.

1.

For every
\varphi
and

, it can be rewritten as

M_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi)=\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)-\pi_{0}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)-\int_{0}^{t}\pi_{s}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi,\eta)\,ds,

(54)

where

is the one-particle generator of (

2.

For any

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}M_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi)^{2}\right]=0,

(55)

for all

.

P
ROOF
.  To show (
54
), it is enough to compute the action of the generator
\mathscr{L}_{n}
on

written in terms of self-duality functions. By linearity and self-duality we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{n}\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(x/n)\mathscr{L}_{n}D(\delta_{x/n},\eta_{r(n)t})=\frac{\alpha}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(x/n)\mathscr{L}_{n}^{(1)}D(\delta_{x/n},\eta_{r(n)t})

\displaystyle=\pi_{t}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi,\eta),

(56)

where in the last equality we used the reversibility of the generator

with respect to the counting

\mathbb{Z}^{d}

To show (

(57)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}\pi_{\cdot}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)

\displaystyle=r(n)\sum_{x,y}q(x-y)\eta(x/n)(\alpha+\eta(y/n))\left(\pi_{\cdot}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta-\delta_{x/n}+\delta_{y/n})-\pi_{\cdot}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)\right)^{2}

(58)

(59)

5.1.3
Tightness: hydrodynamics

The processes
\{\,\pi_{n}(\cdot)\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\,\}=\{\,\pi_{t}^{(n)}\mid n\in\mathbb{N},t\geq 0\,\}
have
their paths in the càdlàg space of right-continuous functions with left-sided limits from

[0,\infty)
to the space of Radon measures
{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, denoted by
D([0,\infty),{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Let
Q_{n}

be the distribution of these processes, defined for Borel sets
B\subset D([0,\infty),{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by

Q_{n}(B):=\mathbb{P}_{n}(\pi_{n}(\cdot)\in B),

(60)

where as before
\mathbb{P}_{n}
is the measure induced by
\eta_{r(n)t}
.

L
EMMA
5.2
.

The sequence of measures
\{\,Q_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\,\}

P
ROOF
.  By Prohorov’s theorem (see for example
[
16
, Theorem 1.3]

or
[
20
, Appendix A.10]
, it is enough to show the following:

1.

Compact containment: For each

and any

there exists a compact set

(61)

2.

Modulus of continuity: For every
\epsilon>0
and any

, there exists a
\delta>0

such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{n}\left(\omega(\pi_{n}(\cdot),\delta,T)\geq\epsilon\right)\leq\epsilon,

(62)

where the (modified) modulus of continuity is given by

\omega(\pi,\delta,T)=\sup\{d_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\pi(s),\pi(t)):s,t\in[0,T],|s-t|\leq\delta\},

(63)

with

d_{{\mathscr{M}}}(\pi_{1},\pi_{2}):=\sum_{i\geq 1}2^{-i}\min\left\{\int\varphi_{i}\,d(\pi_{1}-\pi_{2}),1\right\},

(64)

where
\varphi_{i}\in C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a rich enough countable family of functions (see
[
20
, Appendix
A.10]
for more details).

To show compact containment, fix
\epsilon>0
, and let us consider the set

K=\left\{\mu\in{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\mu([-k,k]^{d})\leq\frac{\alpha\left(C_{1}+\left\lVert\rho_{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\right)}{\epsilon}(2k+1)^{d}\quad\text{for all }[-k,k]^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\},

(65)

where
\rho_{0}
and

are already given in

3.1
. By
[

, Proposition A.25]
,

{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and hence for

being the closure of

in
{\mathscr{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to

5.1.4
Uniqueness of limit points: hydrodynamics

Let
\pi
be a limit point of the sequence
\pi_{n}
, relabelled for convenience; existence of
\pi
is ensured by
Lemma
5.2
. In order
to conclude uniqueness, the idea is to use Lemma

to obtain that
\pi_{t}
satisfies

\pi_{t}(\varphi)=\pi_{0}(\varphi)+\int_{0}^{t}\pi_{s}(L\varphi)ds,

(72)

for any

. However, there is an additional need of justification to establish the

\pi_{s}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi)\to\pi_{s}(L\varphi).

(73)

It is enough to show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{t}\pi_{s}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi)-\pi_{s}(L\varphi)\,ds\right)^{2}\right]=0,

(74)

which along the same lines as in (
71
), is a consequence of (
27
) and
Proposition
5.2
.

Propagation of absolute continuity

At time zero, the limiting measure
\pi_{0}
is absolutely continuous, with density
\rho_{0}
, with respect to
the Lebesgue measure of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let

Now that we have established absolute continuity of the measure

\int\rho(t,x)\varphi(t,x)\,dx=\int\rho(0,x)\varphi(0,x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int\rho(s,x)(\partial_{s}+L)\varphi(s,x)\,dx.

5.3
Dynkin Martingales: fluctuations

Closing the drift

First we deal with the drift term.

L
EMMA
5.3
.

For all
\varphi\in S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for all

P
ROOF
.  By Dynkin’s formula we have that

By self-duality with one particle we have

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{n}Y_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)

\displaystyle=n^{d/2}\mathscr{L}_{n}\pi_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)

\displaystyle=n^{d/2}\pi_{t}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi,\eta),

(84)

where in the first equality we used that the expectation in the definition of

\eta

In order close the drift, i.e. write

\mathbb{E}_{n}\left[n^{d/2}\pi_{t}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi,\eta)\right].

However, again by duality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{n}\left[n^{d/2}\pi_{t}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi,\eta)\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n^{d/2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{E}_{x/n}\left[D(\delta_{X_{t}/n},\eta)\right]\nu_{\rho_{0}}(d\eta)L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi(x/n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{n^{d/2}}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p_{r(n)t}^{(1)}(x/n,x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)\nu_{\rho_{0}}(x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi(x/n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d/2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}L_{n}^{(1)}\left(\alpha\sum_{x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p_{r(n)t}^{(1)}(x/n,x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)\nu_{\rho_{0}}(x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)\right)\varphi(x/n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d/2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\partial_{t}\left(\alpha\sum_{x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}p_{r(n)t}^{(1)}(x/n,x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)\nu_{\rho_{0}}(x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)\right)\varphi(x/n)

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}Y_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta),

(85)

where
\nu_{\rho_{0}}(x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n):=\int\eta(x^{\prime}\mkern-1.2mu/n)\nu_{\rho_{0}}(d\eta)
, in the third line we used
reversibility of

with respect to the counting measure in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{n}

\;\qed

5.3.1
Tightness: fluctuations

We show tightness using Mitoma’s criterion. This criterion allows us to simplify the situation to the real-valued scenario. Moreover, thanks to
Proposition
5.3
, it is enough to show tightness for each of the three processes

\left\{\,Y_{0}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)\;\middle|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\,\right\},\quad\left\{\,N_{t}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)\;\middle|\;t\in[0,T],n\in\mathbb{N}\,\right\},

and

\left\{\,\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}^{(n)}(L_{n}^{(1)}\varphi,\eta)\,ds\;\middle|\;t\in[0,T],n\in\mathbb{N}\,\right\}.

We will work separately on each process.

Tightness for the fluctuation field at time zero

We consider the first process. Here it is just enough to notice that by the same computations as those
in (
48

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}^{n}}\left[Y_{0}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\rho}^{n}}\left[\eta(x/n)\eta(y/n)\right]-\rho_{0}^{n}(x/n)\rho_{0}^{n}(y/n)\right)\varphi(x/n)\,\varphi(y/n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\int_{\Omega}D(2\delta_{x/n},\eta)\,\nu_{\rho_{0}}^{n}(d\eta)-\rho_{0}^{n}(x)^{2}\right)\varphi(x/n)^{2}

\displaystyle{}\quad+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\Omega}D(2\delta_{x/n},\eta)\,\nu_{\rho_{0}}^{n}(d\eta)\varphi(x/n)^{2}

\displaystyle{}\quad+\frac{\alpha}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{\Omega}D(\delta_{x/n},\eta)\,\nu_{\rho_{0}}^{n}(d\eta)\varphi(x/n)^{2},

(96)

which by Assumption
3.1
is bounded.

For the second process, we follow the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 5.2]
, which shows the
convergence of a similar martingale to a mean zero Gaussian process of quadratic variation of time-integral
form. From the same arguments in
[

it is enough to check that the following holds.

1.

Jumps size:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{n}\left[\sup_{s\leq t}\left|N_{s}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)-N_{-s}^{(n)}(\varphi,\eta)\right|\right]=0.

(97)

A.2.1
Convergence of Hilbert spaces

The convergence of Dirichlet forms we are interested in takes place in the setting of convergence of a
sequence of Hilbert spaces. We recall this notion of convergence introduced in
[
17
]
.

D
EFINITION
A.1

(Convergence of Hilbert spaces)
.

A sequence of Hilbert spaces
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

converges
to a Hilbert space
H
if a dense
subset
{\mathfrak{C}}\subseteq H
exists and a family of linear maps

\left\{\Phi_{n}\colon{\mathfrak{C}}\to H_{n}\right\}_{n}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\Phi_{n}f\|_{H_{n}}=\|f\|_{H},\qquad\text{ for all }f\in{\mathfrak{C}}.

It is also necessary to introduce the concepts of strong and weak convergence of vectors defined on a
convergent sequence of Hilbert spaces. In Definitions
A.2
,
A.3
and
A.5
we assume that the spaces
\{H_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

converge to the space
H
, in the sense just defined, with the dense set
{\mathfrak{C}}\subset H
and the sequence
of operators
\{\Phi_{n}\colon{\mathfrak{C}}\to H_{n}\}_{n}
witnessing the convergence.

D
EFINITION
A.2

(Strong convergence on Hilbert spaces)
.

A sequence of vectors
\{f_{n}\}
with
f_{n}
in
H_{n}
, is said to
strongly converge
to a vector

f\in H
if there exists a sequence
\{\tilde{f}_{M}\}\in{\mathfrak{C}}
such that

\lim_{M\to\infty}\|\tilde{f}_{M}-f\|_{H}=0

and

\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\Phi_{n}\tilde{f}_{M}-f_{n}\|_{H_{n}}=0.

D
EFINITION
A.3

(Weak convergence on Hilbert spaces)
.

A sequence of vectors
\{f_{n}\}
with
f_{n}\in H_{n}
, is said to
weakly converge
to a vector
f
in a
Hilbert space
H
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle f_{n},g_{n}\right\rangle_{H_{n}}=\ \left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{H},

for every sequence
\{g_{n}\}
which converges strongly to
g\in H
.

R
EMARK
A.2
.

Notice that, as expected, strong convergence implies weak convergence, and, for any
f\in{\mathfrak{C}}
, the
sequence
\Phi_{n}f
strongly-converges to
f
.

Given these notions of convergence, we can also introduce related notions of convergence for operators.
We denote by
L(H)
the set of all bounded linear operators in
H
.

D
EFINITION
A.4

(Convergence of bounded operators on Hilbert spaces)
.

A sequence of bounded operators
\{T_{n}\}
with
T_{n}\in L(H_{n})

converges strongly
(resp. weakly)
to an operator
T
in
L(H)
if for every strongly (resp. weakly) convergent sequence

\{f_{n}\}
with
f_{n}\in H_{n}
and limit
f\in H
, the sequence
\{T_{n}f_{n}\}
converges
strongly (resp. weakly) to
Tf
.

We are now ready to introduce Mosco convergence.

A.2.2
Definition of Mosco convergence

In this section we assume the Hilbert convergence of a sequence of Hilbert spaces
\{H_{n}\}_{n}
to
H
.

D
EFINITION
A.5

(Mosco convergence)
.

A sequence of Dirichlet forms
\{({\mathscr{E}}_{n},D({\mathscr{E}}_{n}))\}_{n}
, defined on Hilbert spaces
H_{n}
,
Mosco
converges
to a Dirichlet form
({\mathscr{E}},D({\mathscr{E}}))
, defined on some Hilbert space
H
, if:

Mosco I.

For every sequence of
f_{n}\in H_{n}
weakly converging to
f
in
H
, one has

{\mathscr{E}}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathscr{E}}_{n}(f_{n}).

(105)

Mosco II.

For every
f\in H
, there exists a sequence
f_{n}\in H_{n}
strongly converging to
f
in

H
, such that

{\mathscr{E}}(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathscr{E}}_{n}(f_{n}).

(106)

The Markovian properties of the Dirichlet form correspond to the properties of the associated semigroups and
resolvents. The following theorem from
[
17
]
, which relates Mosco convergence with
convergence of semigroups and resolvents, is a powerful application of this correspondence and one of the main
ingredients of our work.

T
HEOREM
A.1
.

Let
\{({\mathscr{E}}_{n},D({\mathscr{E}}_{n}))\}_{n}
be a sequence of Dirichlet forms on Hilbert spaces
H_{n}
and let

({\mathscr{E}},D({\mathscr{E}}))
be a Dirichlet form in some Hilbert space
H
. The following statements are equivalent:

1.

\{({\mathscr{E}}_{n},D({\mathscr{E}}_{n}))\}_{n}
Mosco-converges to
\{({\mathscr{E}},D({\mathscr{E}}))\}
.

2.

The associated sequence of semigroups
\{S_{n}(t)\}_{n}
strongly converges to the semigroup
S(t)

for every
t>0
.

A.2.3
Some comments on Mosco convergence and Assumption
3.2

In this section we explain in more detail the content of Assumption
3.2
. From the
definitions of the previous sections we can see that Assumption
3.2

ensures the following statements:

•

Convergence of Hilbert spaces with a particular projection operator
\Phi_{n}
given by the restriction to

\tfrac{1}{n}\mathbb{Z}^{d}

•

That condition Mosco I is satisfied for any weakly convergent sequence.

The first item is easy to prove. We refer to
[
3

for a proof of this fact which does
not depend on the size of the jumps of the underlying process.
The second point is rather restrictive, but we believe that can be shown along the same lines as in
[
3
]
for independent particles in the finite range situation. Namely, it is enough to have that for all
g\in H
, there exists a sequence
g_{n}\in H_{n}
strongly-converging to
g
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathscr{E}}^{*}_{n}(g_{n})={\mathscr{E}}^{*}(g),

(107)

where
*
denotes the conjugate dual.

Besides
\Phi_{n}\varphi
converging strongly to
\varphi
we also have the following convergence result consequence
of Assumption
3.2
.

P
ROPOSITION
A.1
.

For each
\varphi\in C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the following strong converge with respect to
SIP
-Hilbert convergence:

1.

The sequence
\{L_{n}^{(1)}\Phi_{n}\varphi:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges strongly to the function

L\varphi
.

2.

The sequence
\{\Gamma_{n}^{(1)}\Phi_{n}\varphi:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges strongly to the
function
\Gamma\varphi
.

P
ROOF
.  We only show the first statement. The second one is analogous. Let
\varphi\in C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To show the
strong convergence
L_{n}^{(1)}\Phi_{n}\varphi
to
L\varphi
, it is enough to find a sequence of functions

\tilde{f}_{M}\in C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\lim_{M\to\infty}\|\tilde{f}_{M}-L\varphi\|_{H}=0

(108)

and

\lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\Phi_{n}\tilde{f}_{M}-L_{n}^{(1)}\Phi_{n}\varphi\|_{H_{n}}=0.

(109)

Since
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a sequence
q_{M}\in C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\lim_{M\to\infty}\left\lVert q_{M}-q\right\rVert_{H}=0.

(110)

We can then define the sequence
\tilde{f}_{M}\in C_{k}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\tilde{f}_{M}:=\varphi*q_{M}-\varphi.

Notice that then

\|\tilde{f}_{M}-L\varphi\|_{H}=\left\lVert\varphi*q_{M}-\varphi*q\right\rVert_{H}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{1}\left\lVert q_{M}-q\right\rVert_{H},

where in the last line we used Young’s convolution inequality. From (
110
) we obtain (
108
).

For the second condition (
109
) we estimate

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi_{n}\tilde{f}_{M}-L_{n}^{(1)}\Phi_{n}\varphi\right\rVert_{H_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\Phi_{n}\varphi*q_{M}-\Phi_{n}\varphi-L_{n}^{(1)}\Phi_{n}\varphi\right\rVert_{H_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\Phi_{n}\varphi*q_{M}-R_{n}\Phi_{n}\varphi\right\rVert_{H_{n}}^{2}+\left\lVert\Phi_{n}\varphi-S_{n}\Phi_{n}\varphi\right\rVert_{H_{n}}^{2},

(111)

where

R_{n}\Phi_{n}\varphi(x/n)=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q((y-x)/n)\varphi(y/n)

and

S_{n}\Phi_{n}\varphi(x/n)=\varphi(x/n)\left(\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}q((y-x)/n)\right).

We first deal with the second term in the right hand side of (
111
). Namely,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi_{n}\varphi-S_{n}\Phi_{n}\varphi\right\rVert_{H_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi^{2}(x/n)+\frac{1}{n^{3d}}\sum_{x,y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi^{2}(x/n)q((y_{1}-x)/n)q((y_{2}-x)/n)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{2}{n^{2d}}\sum_{x,y_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi^{2}(x/n)q((y_{1}-x)/n),

which by (
3
) vanishes as
n\to\infty
.

In the same way we also estimate the first term in the right hand side
of (
111
). Namely,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Phi_{n}\varphi*q_{M}-R_{n}\Phi_{n}\varphi\right\rVert_{H_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\varphi*q_{M})^{2}(x/n)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\frac{1}{n^{3d}}\sum_{x,y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(y_{1}/n)q((y_{1}-x)/n)\varphi(y_{2}/n)q((y_{2}-x)/n)

\displaystyle\quad{}-\frac{2}{n^{2d}}\sum_{x,y_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(\varphi*q_{M})(x/n)\varphi(y_{1}/n)q((y_{1}-x)/n).

As

\;\qed

A.3
Uniqueness of distributions of the generalized time depedent Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

For the readers convenience we include the proof of Proposition

We start with an auxiliary statement.

P
ROPOSITION
A.2
.

For all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the mapping

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}^{\rho}f}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(s,x)\,dx)}\,ds,

is continuous in
[0,T]
.

P
ROOF
.  It is enough to notice that by (
104
)

\left\lVert S_{t}^{L}f\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{1},

for some
C
that does not depend on
f
or
t
.

\;\qed

Proof of Proposition
4.1
:

By
[
18
, Proposition 3.4]
we know that for all
g\colon[0,T]\to S(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the process

Z_{t}(g)=\exp\left\{i\left(Y_{t}(g)-\int_{0}^{t}Y_{s}((\partial_{s}+L)g)\,ds\right)+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}^{\rho}g}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(s,x)\,dx)}\,ds\right\}

is a local Martingale. To make
\{\,Z_{t}(g)\mid t\in[0,T]\,\}
an actual martingale, it is enough to have

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left|Z_{t}(g)\right|\right]<\infty,

which is a consequence of the mapping

t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}^{\rho}g}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(s,x)\,dx)}\,ds

being continuous. Hence
\{\,Z_{t}(g)\mid t\in[0,T]\,\}
is a martingale. In particular, if we plug

g(t,x)=S_{T-t}^{L}\varphi(x)
(where
S^{L}
is the semi-group associated to
L
), we obtain that

\bar{Z}_{t}(\varphi)=\exp\left\{iY_{t}(S_{T-t}^{L}\varphi)+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{s}^{\rho}S_{T-s}^{L}\varphi}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(s,x)\,dx)}\,ds\right\}

is also a martingale. From here, after substitution of
\varphi=\lambda\varphi
and
T=t
, we find that for

s\leq t

\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left\{i\lambda Y_{t}(\varphi)\right\}\mid{\mathscr{F}}_{s}\right]=\exp\left\{i\lambda Y_{s}(S_{t-s}^{L}\varphi)-\lambda^{2}\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{u}^{\rho}S_{t-u}^{L}\varphi}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(u,x)\,dx)}\,ds\right\}.

(112)

This implies that conditioning on

,

is Gaussian with mean

Y_{s}(S_{t-s}^{L}\varphi)

and variance

2\int_{s}^{t}\left\lVert\sqrt{\Gamma_{u}^{\rho}S_{t-u}^{L}\varphi}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\rho(u,x)\,dx)}\,ds.

Then a standard Markov argument (see for example
[
16
, Chapter 11]
or the explicit
computations in
[

) guarantees the uniqueness of finite-dimensional
distributions.

\square

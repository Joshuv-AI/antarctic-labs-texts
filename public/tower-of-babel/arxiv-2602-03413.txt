On the Convergence of Wasserstein Gradient Descent for Sampling
Van Chien Ta

Abstract
This paper studies the optimization of the Kullback–Leibler functional on the Wasserstein space of probability measures, and develops a sampling framework based on Wasserstein gradient descent (WGD). We identify two important subclasses of the Wasserstein space for which the WGD scheme is guaranteed to converge, thereby providing new theoretical foundations for optimization-based sampling methods on measure spaces. For practical implementation, we construct a particle-based WGD algorithm in which the score function is estimated via score matching. Through a series of numerical experiments, we demonstrate that WGD can provide good approximation to a variety of complex target distributions, including those that pose substantial challenges for standard MCMC and parametric variational Bayes methods. These results suggest that WGD offers a promising and flexible alternative for scalable Bayesian inference in high-dimensional or multimodal settings.



Keywords:
Particle-based Variational Bayes; Bayesian computation; Monte Carlo methods

Introduction

The main task in Bayesian statistics is to conduct inference based on a computationally intractable posterior distribution with density
\pi(x)\propto\exp(-V(x))
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, generally known up to a normalizing constant.
Traditional sampling methods, such as Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) and Sequential Monte Carlo, have been a main workforce in Bayesian inference. However, these methods are generally not scalable and can be computationally too expensive.

Alternatively, the sampling problem can be viewed as an optimization problem of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) functional,
F(\mu)=\text{\rm KL}(\mu\|\pi)=\int\log(\mu/\pi){\rm d}\mu
,
on the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Wibisono, 2018
provides a comprehensive discussion on the inter-connection between the sampling and optimization problems.
Consider the Wasserstein space
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a finite second moment, equipped with the 2-Wasserstein metric.
An important observation made by
Wibisono, 2018
is that the KL functional is a sum of two terms: the potential energy, which is the expectation of
V
with respect to
\mu
, and the negative entropy of
\mu
.
Under certain conditions on
V
, the potential energy
is geodesically convex and smooth with respect to the Wasserstein metric on
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, while the negative entropy is geodesically convex but non-smooth.
This could allow one to borrow the idea of proximal gradient descent in optimization to develop a convergence-guaranteed forward-backward algorithm to optimize
F(\mu)
. This is formally established in
Salim et al., 2020
.
In their algorithm,
the smooth potential energy is implementable by a Wasserstein gradient descent step, and the non-smoothness issue of the negative entropy is handled by a backward step, which is a proximal point algorithm.
However, the contribution of
Salim et al., 2020
is more of theoretical interest than practice, as it is in general challenging to implement the backward step.
By limiting to the Gaussian setting, this backward step is implementable with a closed form solution, leading to the Forward-Backward Gaussian Variational Inference framework of
Diao et al., 2023
; see also
Lambert et al., 2022
.

The previous results of
Lambert et al., 2022
and
Diao et al., 2023
are confined to the Gaussian setting within the Bures–Wasserstein space, limiting their applicability to more general probability distributions. This limitation serves as the primary motivation for our work, where we aim to extend the optimization framework of
F(\mu)
beyond the Gaussian target setting to develop a more practical approach.
This paper focuses on the Wasserstein gradient descent (WGD) approach for optimizing
F(\mu)
. As aforementioned, WGD is not guaranteed to converge on the Wasserstein space
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
; this is because
F(\mu)
is non-smooth, which does not ensure the reduce of the functional value after each WGD step. Indeed, this is the primary reason for developing the forward-backward algorithm in
Salim et al., 2020
, who use the backward step to bypass the non-smoothness.
Non-smoothness is not the only reason causing the biasedness of WGD, some regularity of the space of distributions that WGD operates on is needed.
Xu and Li, 2024
construct two counter-examples showcasing the failure of WGD for optimizing
F(\mu)
, and highlighting the necessary condition that the densities visited by WGD must be infinitely differentiable.

In this paper, we identify two subspaces of the Wasserstein space under which WGD for optimizing the KL functional
F(\mu)
is guaranteed to converge.
The first subspace, referred to as
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures, includes absolutely continuous measures whose densities are indefinitely differentiable,
\alpha
-log-concave and
\beta
-log-smooth.
The second subspace, called (
c_{1},c_{2}
)-regular measures and introduced in
Polyanskiy and Wu, 2016
, includes absolutely continuous measures whose densities are indefinitely differentiable with score functions having a linear-bounded norm.
Operating in these two spaces, WGD is provably convergent, albeit in different meaning.

We provide a range of numerical examples, ranging from Bayesian logistic regression to banana-shaped non-standard distributions and mixtures.
These examples are not only to confirm the theoretical findings, but also to showcase the performance of WGD as an attractive sampling technique.

The organization of this paper is as follows. Section
2
considers conditions on
\mu
so that
F(\mu)
possess some analogue of
\alpha
-convexity and
\beta
-smoothness on a subspace of the Wasserstein space.
Section
3
considers another set of conditions on
\mu
so that
F(\mu)
is convex and Lipschitz.
These allow us to obtain convergence guarantee for WGD of
F(\cdot)
under various scenarios.
We provide numerical examples in Section
4
and Section
5
concludes the paper.
Technical details and proofs are included in the Appendix.

2
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures

Let
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the Wasserstein space.
The reader is referred to
Ambrosio et al., 2005
and
Villani et al., 2009
for a detailed introduction to the key concepts in Wasserstein geometry, such as differentiability and Wasserstein gradient of functionals, which will be used throughout this paper.

We first define the space of
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures.

Definition 1

(
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures)
.

A measure
\mu(dx)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular if it is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lesbegue measure with the density
\mu(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore, the potential function
f(x)=-\log\mu(x)
is
\alpha
-convex and
\beta
-smooth, i.e.

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq\beta\|x-y\|,

(1)

and

f(y)\geq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)+\frac{\alpha}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2)

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures.

Note that, (
1
) is equivalent to

f(y)\leq f(x)+\nabla f(x)^{\top}(y-x)+\frac{\beta}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},\;\;\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3)

Also, as
\mu(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, (
1
) and (
2
) can be equivalently written as

\alpha I\preccurlyeq\nabla^{2}f(x)\preccurlyeq\beta I,\;\;\forall x.

Here, for squared matrices
A
and
B
, by
A\preccurlyeq B
we mean that
B-A
is positive definite.

It can be seen that all Gaussian distributions
\mathcal{N}(m,\Sigma)
,
with the eigenvalues of
\Sigma
bounded below by
\alpha
and above by
\beta
, belong to the
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measure family.
The regularity conditions (
1
) and (
2
)
are used in
Dalalyan, 2017
for convergence analysis of Langevin Monte Carlo algorithms.

Starting from a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the WGD step pushes
\mu
to a new measure
\nu
as follows

\nu=(\text{Id}-\epsilon\nabla_{\mu}F)_{\#}\mu

where
\epsilon>0
is a step size,
\nabla_{\mu}F(x)=\nabla\log\frac{\mu(x)}{\pi(x)}
is the Wasserstein gradient of
F

(
Wibisono, 2018
;
Salim et al., 2020
)
.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that the target
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
let
\nu=\big(\text{Id}-\epsilon\nabla_{\mu}F\big)_{\#}\mu
, with
\nabla_{\mu}F=\nabla\log\frac{\mu}{\pi}
the Wasserstein gradient of
F
. Then, we have

F(\nu)-F(\mu)\leq-\epsilon(1-\frac{3}{2}\beta\epsilon)\|\nabla_{\mu}F\|_{\mu}^{2}+C\epsilon^{2}+o(\epsilon^{2}),

(4)

where
C
is a finite constant depending only on
\alpha,\beta
and dimension
d
.

The proof can be found in the Appendix. Proposition
1
provides an analogue of
\beta
-smoothness for
F(\mu)
on
\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which guarantees a reduce in the objective
F
after one WGD update provided that the step size
\epsilon
is small enough.
However, the extra terms independent of
\|\nabla_{\mu}F\|_{\mu}^{2}
on the right hand side of (
4
)
prevent the WGD with a fixed step size from enjoying a linear convergence rate.
Instead, by using a decreasing step size, we can obtain a sublinear rate as shown below. Algorithm
1
summarizes the WGD procedure for sampling from the target measure
\pi
.

Algorithm 1

(WGD for
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures)
.

Let
\mu_{0}
be an initial measure in
\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
k=0,1,2,...
, iterating:

\mu_{k+1}=\big(\text{Id}-\eta_{k}\nabla\log\frac{\mu_{k}}{\pi}\big)_{\#}\mu_{k}

where the step size
\{\eta_{k}\}
satisfies

\eta_{k}>0,\;\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}=\infty,\;\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty.

We now establish the convergence of this WGD algorithm.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\{\mu_{k},k=0,1,...\}
is a sequence of iterates from Algorithm
1
, and that
\{\mu_{k},k=0,1,...\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then,

F(\mu_{T+1})\leq\frac{F(\mu_{0})+C\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}{1+2\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}-3\alpha\beta\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

(5)

and

W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T+1},\pi)\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}\frac{F(\mu_{0})+C\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}{1+2\alpha\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}-3\alpha\beta\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

(6)

for some finite constant
C
.

The proof is deferred to the Appendix B.
The assumption in Theorem
1
that all the iterates
\mu_{k}
belong to
\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be challenging to check in general. However, we argue in Appendix A that this can be the case under certain conditions.

2.1
WGD with Estimated Wasserstein Gradient

In practice, we generally need to estimate the Wasserstein gradient
\nabla_{\mu}F=\nabla\log\frac{\mu}{\pi}
by
\widehat{\nabla_{\mu}F}=\widehat{\nabla\log{\mu}}-\nabla\log{\pi}
,
where
\widehat{\nabla\log{\mu}}
is an estimate of
\nabla\log{\mu}
. For example,
\widehat{\nabla\log{\mu}}
can be obtained by the score-matching estimation method as considered in this paper.
This section considers the setting where we can write the estimate
\widehat{\nabla_{\mu}F}
as
\widehat{\nabla_{\mu}F}=\nabla_{\mu}F+\xi
with
\xi
an error map from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We study conditions on the error map under which the convergence of WGD is still guaranteed.

Assumption 1
.

•

Bounded norm:
\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mu}=\int\|\xi(x)\|^{2}d\mu(x)\leq c_{\xi}
for all measure
\mu
.

•

Correct direction: There exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

\left\langle\nabla\log\frac{\mu}{\pi},\nabla\log\frac{\mu}{\pi}+\xi\right\rangle_{\mu}\geq\delta\Big\|\nabla\log\frac{\mu}{\pi}\Big\|_{\mu}.

•

L
-Lipschitz:
\xi
is
L
-Lipschitz continuous, i.e.,

-LI\preccurlyeq\nabla\xi(x)\preccurlyeq LI,\;\;\forall x.

One-step update of the WGD with estimated gradient is

\nu=\left(\text{Id}-\epsilon(\nabla_{\mu}F+\xi)\right)_{\#}\mu.

Similar to Proposition
1
, the following proposition establishes an analogue of the smoothness condition for the target functional
F
.

Proposition 2
.

Assume that the target
\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and that Assumption
1
is satisfied. For any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
\nu=\big(\text{Id}-\epsilon(\nabla_{\mu}F+\xi)\big)_{\#}\mu
, with
\nabla_{\mu}F=\nabla\log\frac{\mu}{\pi}
. Then,

F(\nu)\leq F(\mu)-\epsilon\delta\|\nabla_{\mu}F\|_{\mu}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}(\beta+2)\|\nabla_{\mu}F\|_{\mu}^{2}+C\epsilon^{2}+o(\epsilon^{2}).

(7)

where
C=(\beta+2)c_{\xi}+\frac{d}{2}(\beta-\alpha+L)^{2}
.

The proof can be found in the Appdendix. Algorithm
1
can be extended to the case with estimated Wassertein gradients.

Algorithm 2

(WGD for
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular measures with estimated Wassertein gradients)
.

Let
\mu_{0}
be an initial measure in
\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
k=0,1,2,...
, iterating:

\mu_{k+1}=\big(\text{Id}-\eta_{k}(\nabla\log\frac{\mu_{k}}{\pi}+\xi_{k})\big)_{\#}\mu_{k}

where the step size
\{\eta_{k}\}
satisfies

\eta_{k}>0,\;\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}=\infty,\;\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty.

The following theorem establishes the convergence of Algorithm
2
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\{\mu_{k},\ k=0,1,...\}
be the iterates from Algorithm
2
. Assume that the errors
\{\xi_{k}\}
satisfy the Assumption
1
and that the iterates
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k}
remain in
\mathcal{P}_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then, we have

F(\mu_{T+1})\leq\frac{F(\mu_{0})+C\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}{1+2\alpha\delta\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}-2\alpha(\beta+2)\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0

(8)

and

W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{T+1},\pi)\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}\frac{F(\mu_{0})+C\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}{1+2\alpha\delta\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}-2\alpha(\beta+2)\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

(9)

for some finite constant
C
.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1
, hence omitted.

3
(c_{1},c_{2})
-regular measures

As discussed earlier, the non-smoothness of the entropy functional
\mathcal{H}(\mu)=\int\mu\log\mu
prevents WGD from enjoying convergence guarantees on the general Wasserstein space. Restricting to the
(\alpha,\beta)
-regular space ensures that the KL objective functional decreases after each WGD step, as shown in Proposition
1
. This section considers another class of regular measures under which a form of convergence for WGD can be established. Following
Polyanskiy and Wu, 2016
, who study conditions under which the entropy possesses additional regularity, we define the class of
(c_{1},c_{2})
-regular measures as follows.

Definition 2

(
(c_{1},c_{2})
-regular measures)
.

We say that a probability measure
\mu(dx)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
(c_{1},c_{2})
-regular
if
\mu
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lesbegue measure with the density
\mu(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Furthermore, the potential function
f(x)=-\log\mu(x)
satisfies

\|\nabla f(x)\|\leq c_{1}\|x\|+c_{2},\;\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(10)

with
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}\geq 0
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of
(c_{1},c_{2})
-regular measures.

Theorem 3
.

The entropy functional is Lipschitz continuous in
\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. That is, for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

|\mathcal{H}(\nu)-\mathcal{H}(\mu)|\leq L_{\mathcal{H}}W_{2}(\nu,\mu)

with
L_{\mathcal{H}}=O(d^{1/2})
.

Proof.

The conclusion is a direct implication of Propositions 1, 2 and 3 in
Polyanskiy and Wu, 2016
.
∎

Optimization of a convex and Lipschitz continuous objective function on the Euclidean space is well established; see, e.g.
Drusvyatskiy, 2020
.
We extend this theory into optimization on the
\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
subspace.
Recall that a functional
\phi
defined on the Wasserstein space is said to be geodesically convex if

\phi(\nu)\geq\phi(\mu)+\int<\nabla_{\mu}\phi,t_{\mu}^{\nu}-id>d\mu(x),\;\;\forall\nu\in\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(11)

where
t_{\mu}^{\nu}
is the optimal map from
\mu
to
\nu
and
\nabla_{\mu}\phi
is the Wasserstein gradient of
\phi
at
\mu
.

We shall establish that, if the potential
V(x)=-\log\pi(x)
is convex and Lipschitz, then one WGD update with a small enough step size will push any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to a new measure that is closer to the target
\pi
.

Proposition 3
.

Suppose that the potential
V(x)=-\log\pi(x)
is convex and
L_{V}
-Lipschitz.
For any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
\nu=(Id-\eta\nabla_{\mu}F)_{\#}\mu
.
Denote
L=\max\{L_{\mathcal{H}},L_{V}\}
where
L_{\mathcal{H}}
is from Theorem
3
. Then,

W_{2}^{2}(\nu,\pi)\leq W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\pi)-2\eta F(\mu)+\eta^{2}L^{2}.

(12)

Hence,
W_{2}(\nu,\pi)<W_{2}(\mu,\pi)
if
\eta<2F(\mu)/L^{2}
.

The proof is deferred to Appendix B.

Algorithm 3

(WGD for
(c_{1},c_{2})
-regular measures)
.

Let
\mu_{0}
be an initial measure in
\mathcal{P}_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For
k=0,1,2,...
, iterating:

\mu_{k+1}=\big(\text{Id}-\eta_{k}\nabla_{\mu_{k}}F\big)_{\#}\mu_{k}

where the step sizes
\{\eta_{k}\}
satisfy

\eta_{k}>0,\;\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}=\infty,\;\;\;\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta_{k}^{2}<\infty.

We now establish the convergence analysis for Algorithm
3
.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose that the potential
V(x)=-\log\pi(x)
is convex and
L_{V}
-Lipschitz. Let
\{\mu_{k},\ k=0,1,...\}
be the sequence of measures generated from Algorithm
3
.
Suppose that the iterates
\mu_{k}
remain in
P_{(c_{1},c_{2})}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then

F(\overline{\mu}_{T})\leq\frac{W_{2}^{2}(\mu_{0},\pi)+L^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}^{2}}{2\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle T\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}0,

(13)

where
L=\max\{L_{\mathcal{H}},L_{V}\}
and

\overline{\mu}_{T}=\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}}\sum_{k=0}^{T}\eta_{k}\mu_{k}.

The proof can be found in Appendix B.

3.1
WGD with Estimated Wasserstein Gradient

This section considers Algorithm
3
where the Wasserstein gradient
\nabla_{\mu}F
is replaced by an estimate

\widehat{\nabla_{\mu}F}=\nabla_{\mu}F+\xi
with
\xi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
a random map. Then, one-step update of the WGD with estimated gradient is

\nu=\left(\text{Id}-\eta(\nabla_{\mu}F+\xi)\right)_{\#}\mu,\;\;\;\eta>0.

Let
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k}
be the sequence of measures generated from Algorithm
3
with estimated Wasserstein gradients.
We study the conditions on
\xi
under which the average
\bar{\mu}_{T}
of the iterates
\{\mu_{k}\}_{k=0}^{T}
converges.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}=\sigma(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},...,\mu_{k})
.

Assumption 2
.

(i)

Bounded norm: there exists a constant
c^{2}_{\xi}
such that
{\mathbb{E}}(\|\xi\|^{2}_{\mu}|\mathcal{F}_{k})\leq c^{2}_{\xi}
for any measure
\mu
.

(ii)

Unbiasedness:
{\mathbb{E}}(\xi|\mathcal{F}_{k})=0
.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that the potential

4
Numerical examples

4.1
Implemenation strategy

Standard WGD.

We use the score matching method of
Hyvärinen, 2005
to estimate the score function
\nabla\log\mu_{t}
at each iteration
t
of the WGD algorithm.
The details of this method are provided in Appendix A.
Let
s_{t}(x)
be the score matching estimate of
\nabla\log\mu_{t}(x)
, and
\{x_{t}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be the particles at iteration
t
.
Let
\{x_{0}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N}\sim\mu_{0}
with
\mu_{0}
some initial distribution.
The particle-based WGD update is

x_{t+1}^{(i)}=x_{t}^{(i)}-\eta_{t}\big(s_{t}(x_{t}^{(i)})-\nabla\log\pi(x_{t}^{(i)})\big),\;\;\;\;i=1,...,N,\;\;t=0,1,...

(15)

The step size is set as
\eta_{t}=\epsilon_{0}/(1+t)^{\alpha}
,
1/2<\alpha\leq 1
, that satisfies the condition in Algorithm
1
. We choose
\epsilon_{0}=0.01
and
\alpha=0.6
in the examples below.

The squared norm of the Wasserstein gradient
\|\nabla\log\frac{\mu_{t}}{\pi}\|_{\mu_{t}}^{2}
, which can be estimated by

\text{Err}_{t}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\|s_{t}(x_{t}^{(i)})-\nabla\log\pi(x_{t}^{(i)})\|^{2},

is expected to reduce to zero when the algorithm converges.
However,
\text{Err}_{t}
will never go down to exact zero because of the Monte Carlo error.
We choose to stop the WGD iterations if
\text{Err}_{t}
does not reduce any further after some pre-determined number of iterations,
P
.
We set
P=20
in the examples below.

Annealing WGD.

We observe that the update in (
15
) might be unstable in applications where the score of the target
\nabla\log\pi(x)
is large; that is, when some or all elements of the vector
\nabla\log\pi(x)
have large magnitudes.
To facilitate this issue and to create a smooth transition between the iterations, we employ the annealing strategy often used in the Monte Carlo literature
(
Rezende and Mohamed, 2015
)
.
Let
0=a_{0}<a_{1}<...<a_{T}=1
be a sequence of temperature levels; consider a sequence of annealed distributions

\pi_{t}(x)\propto\mu_{0}(x)^{1-a_{t}}\pi(x)^{a_{t}},\;\;t=0,1,...,T,

where
\mu_{0}
is the initial distribution.
We set
a_{t}=\min\{1,t/T\}
, hence
\pi_{0}=\mu_{0}
and
\pi_{t}=\pi
for all
t\geq T
.
The updating in the annealing WGD procedure is

x_{t+1}^{(i)}=x_{t}^{(i)}-\eta_{t}\Big(s_{t}(x_{t}^{(i)})-\nabla\log\pi_{t}(x_{t}^{(i)})\Big),\;\;\;\;i=1,...,N,\;\;t=0,1,...

(16)

This induces a smooth transition of the particles from
\mu_{0}
and
\pi
when
t\leq T
,
after which the particles settle within the support of
\pi
for
t>T
.

4.2
Bayesian logistic regression

We consider a Bayesian logistic regression model

\theta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{0}^{2}I_{d}),\quad y_{i}\sim\mbox{Binomial}(1,\sigma(x_{i}^{T}\theta)),\quad\sigma(x_{i}^{T}\theta)=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-x_{i}^{T}\theta)}

(17)

where the
y_{i}
are binary responses,
x_{i}

Conclusion

The paper investigated the sampling problem via optimizing the Kullback–Leibler functional on the Wasserstein space of probability measures over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We analyzed the WGD scheme and identified subclasses of the Wasserstein space under which WGD is guaranteed to converge. We also demonstrated the performance of the WGD algorithm through a series of numerical experiments, highlighting its ability to approximate complex target distributions.

This work takes an important step toward relaxing the parametric constraints inherent in the conventional variational Bayes framework. As a particle-based method, WGD provides a principled bridge between sampling techniques such as Markov chain Monte Carlo and Sequential Monte Carlo, and classical parametric VB. In doing so, it leverages advantages from both worlds: the scalability and optimization-driven structure of VB, and the flexibility and expressiveness of particle-based samplers.

The main limitation in our current implementation of WGD is its computational time. Depending on the number of particles used, it can be not much faster than sampling-based techniques. Our implementation relies on a basic score-matching approach to estimate the score function; however, the efficiency of this estimate is central to the overall performance of WGD. Recent developments in the machine learning literature have produced more advanced and computationally efficient score-matching techniques. Integrating these into the WGD framework is likely to yield substantial improvements. Another concern is step-size adaptation. In standard Euclidean gradient descent, adaptive step-size schemes play a crucial role in accelerating convergence and improving numerical stability. We conjecture that incorporating adaptive step-size mechanisms into WGD could significantly enhance both efficiency and robustness, and this warrants further exploration.

A1. Score matching

This section presents the score matching method used in the paper for estimating the score function.
Let
\{x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ i=1,...,n\}
be i.i.d data from an unknown distribution with density
p(x)
.
The goal is to estimate the score function defined as
\nabla\log p(x)
.
Score matching
(
Hyvärinen, 2005
)
allows us to approximate the score
\nabla\log p(x)
directly without approximating the density
p(x)
.
Let
s_{\theta}(x):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a transformation parameterized by parameter
\theta
; often
s_{\theta}(x)
is a neural network such as a planar flow map or a Sylvester flow map
(
Papamakarios et al., 2021
)
.
The score network
s_{\theta}(x)
is trained by minimizing the loss

\ell(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim p(x)}\big[\|s_{\theta}(x)-\nabla_{x}\log p(x)\|^{2}\big].

(20)

Minimizing
\ell(\theta)
can be shown to be equivalent to minimizing

{\mathbb{E}}_{x\sim p(x)}\Big[\text{\rm tr}(\nabla_{x}s_{\theta}(x))+\frac{1}{2}\|s_{\theta}(x)\|^{2}\Big].

(21)

The objective (
21
) only requires sampling from
p(x)
, it does not involve the density
p(x)
nor the score
\nabla_{x}\log p(x)
, hence it can be optimized by SGD.
There exist other variants of the score matching method which can be more scalable in high-dimensional settings. We refer the reader to
Song and Ermon, 2019
for more details.

We used the following map in the paper

y=x+V\tanh{(W^{\top}x+b)}

where
V,W
are matrices of size
d\times d
and
b
is a
d
-vector.
In some of the experiments, the final score network
s_{\theta}(x)
might be a composition of several transformations above.

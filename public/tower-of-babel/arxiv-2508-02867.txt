Total Failure of Approachability at Successors of Singulars of Countable Cofinality
Hannes Jakob

Abstract
. Relative to class many supercompact cardinals, we construct a model of 𝖹𝖥𝖢 + 𝖦𝖢𝖧 \mathsf{ZFC}+\mathsf{GCH} where for every singular cardinal δ \delta of countable cofinality and every regular uncountable μ < δ \mu<\delta there are stationarily many non-approachable points of cofinality μ \mu in δ + \delta^{+} . This answers a question of Mitchell and provides a decisive answer to a question of Foreman and Shelah.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 03E05, Secondary: 03E35, 03E55

The notion of independence is fundamental to set-theoretic research. It refers to the phenomenon that many set-theoretic statements are neither proven nor refuted by
\mathsf{ZFC}
, the most commonly accepted axioms of set theory. One particular theme in the study of independence statements is the fact that successors of singular cardinals are often much less malleable than successors of regular cardinals – recall that a cardinal
\kappa
is
singular
if we can write
\kappa=\bigcup_{i\in x}y_{i}
, where
x
and every
y_{i}
has size
{<}\,\kappa
and
regular
otherwise. Additionally, many independence results cannot be transferred from regular cardinals to singular cardinals. The most famous example of this phenomenon is the combination of two results due to William Easton and Saharon Shelah: Easton showed that the continuum function – mapping a cardinal
\kappa
to the size of its powerset – when restricted to the class of
regular
cardinals can be equal to any function, provided that function is nondecreasing and maps every cardinal to a cardinal with a larger cofinality. On the other hand, Shelah showed that, assuming
2^{\aleph_{n}}<\aleph_{\omega}
for every
n<\omega
,
2^{\aleph_{\omega}}<\min(\aleph_{(2^{\aleph_{0}})^{+}},\aleph_{\omega_{4}})
, so Easton’s result cannot be replicated for the class of
all
cardinals.

Another example highlighting the difference between successors of singular cardinals and successors of regular cardinals is Shelah’s
approachability ideal
: Shelah introduced the ideal
I[\kappa^{+}]
in
[
She79
]
in order to study the indestructibility of stationary subsets of
\kappa^{+}
both for singular and regular cardinals
\kappa
. Despite the definition of
I[\kappa^{+}]
being the same in both cases, its behavior differs greatly depending on whether
\kappa
is regular or singular: When
\kappa
is a regular cardinal, it is commonly referred to as the
critial cofinality
, since the set
E_{<\kappa}^{\kappa^{+}}
(consisting of all ordinals below
\kappa^{+}
with cofinality
{<}\,\kappa
) is always a member of
I[\kappa^{+}]
and so the behavior of
I[\kappa^{+}]
is determined by which subsets of
E_{\kappa}^{\kappa^{+}}
it contains. On the other hand, if
\kappa
is singular, it was only proven that the set
E_{\leq\cf(\kappa)}^{\kappa^{+}}
belongs to
I[\kappa^{+}]
and so many more options regarding the behavior of
I[\kappa^{+}]
seem possible.

Recently, in joint work of Maxwell Levine and the author (see
[
JL25
]
), we solved an almost four-decades-old open problem due to Saharon Shelah by constructing a model – for an arbitrary
n\in(0,\omega)
– where
E_{\aleph_{n}}^{\aleph_{\omega+1}}\notin I[\aleph_{\omega+1}]
. Previously it had only been known that there could be stationarily many non-approachable points of cofinality
\aleph_{1}
in
\aleph_{\omega+1}
. Our result begs the natural question (which was also raised by Mitchell in
[
Mit09
]
and by Dobrinen in private communication) whether it is consistent that
E_{\mu}^{\kappa^{+}}\notin I[\kappa^{+}]
for every
\mu\in(\aleph_{0},\kappa)
(where
\kappa
is singular of countable cofinality).

In this paper, we answer the preceding question and even answer it for every cardinal of countable cofinality simultaneously. Since
I[\kappa^{+}]
contains a stationary subset of
E_{\mu}^{\kappa^{+}}
for every regular
\mu<\kappa
(see
[
Cum05
, Theorem 9.2]
), the following theorem shows that the greatest possible failure of the approachability property at successors of singular cardinals of countable cofinality is actually consistent:

Theorem
.

Assume that there is a class of supercompact cardinals. There exists a class forcing extension in which, for every singular cardinal
\delta
with countable cofinality and every regular uncountable
\mu<\delta
, the set
E_{\mu}^{\delta^{+}}
is not a member of
I[\delta^{+}]
.

This result is made possible by a technical advancement of the main crux of our previous paper (see
[
JL25
, Theorem 4.11]
). The major obstacle towards obtaining models in which a stationary set of a given cofinality is not in
I[\delta^{+}]
is finding posets which can collapse successors of singular cardinals without making them
d
-approachable
. Whether this was possible without preserving the successor of the singular as a cardinal had been open for around four decades and was a major obstacle both in non-approachable sets concentrating on specific cofinalities and iterating such constructions to obtain the failure of
\mathsf{AP}
for many cardinals simultaneously. However, we solved that issue in
[
JL25
]
by constructing a poset which, under suitable hypotheses, collapses the successor of a singular cardinal of countable cofinality to any desired cofinality without making it
d
-approachable. That theorem relied on the tension between a weak form of the approximation property (see
[
JL25
, Theorem 4.3.]
) and our iteration not adding new short functions into small sets (see
[
JL25
, Lemma 4.10]
). In this work, we achieve a strengthening of
[
JL25
, Theorem 4.3]
(see Theorem
4.11
) which allows us to dispense with
[
JL25
, Lemma 4.10]
. Using this, we are able to replace the Namba forcing used there by a new forcing notion which we call
supercompact Namba forcing
. This forcing is constructed using higher variants of the
\mathsf{LIP}
-ideals which we used in
[
JL25
]
and which have previously been considered by Matsubara (see
[
Mat02
]
). In technical terms, the poset from
[
JL25
]
uses infinitely many generically measurable cardinals while our new poset only uses a single generically supercompact cardinal (despite the latter being a stronger hypothesis it is much easier to obtain in our case).

Using this poset, we first construct a forcing which, given a regular cardinal
\mu
and a supercompact cardinal
\kappa>\mu
, collapses
\kappa
to become
\mu^{+}
while forcing that for every singular cardinal
\delta>\kappa
with countable cofinality,
E_{\mu}^{\delta^{+}}\notin I[\delta^{+}]
. We then define a class-length iteration of instances of that forcing which gives us the model of the main theorem.

The paper is organized as follows: After giving preliminary definitions and results, we construct the tools we need to carry out the main construction: In Section 2, we prove iteration theorems and other statements regarding Prikry-type forcings. In Section 3, we investigate the Laver-Ideal Property which is used in the definition of the Namba forcing. In Section 4, we define a forcing which collapses the successor of a singular cardinal without making it approachable and derive its properties. In Section 5, we define an iteration of instances of the forcing from Section 4 and show that it forces, for a specific
\mu
, that for every singular
\delta>\mu
with countable cofinality, there are stationarily many non-approachable points in
\delta^{+}
with cofinality
\mu
. Finally, in Section 6, we construct an iteration of instances of the forci ng from Section 5 and prove our main theorem.

1.1.
Approachability, Colorings and Supercompact Cardinals

The approachability ideal was introduced by Shelah in
[
She79
]
in order to obtain results regarding the indestructibility of stationary sets under sufficiently closed forcings:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a cardinal. A set
S\subseteq\mu^{+}
is in the
approachability ideal

I[\mu^{+}]
if there is a club
C\subseteq\mu^{+}
and a sequence
(x_{i})_{i<\mu^{+}}
of elements of
[\mu^{+}]^{<\mu}
such that for any
\gamma\in S\cap C
,
\gamma
is
approachable with respect to
(x_{i})_{i<\mu^{+}}
, i.e. there is
A\subseteq\gamma
unbounded of minimal ordertype such that whenever
\beta\in\gamma
,
A\cap\beta\in\{x_{i}\;|\;i<\gamma\}
.

The
approachability property
\mathsf{AP}_{\mu}
states that
I[\mu^{+}]
is improper, i.e.
\mu^{+}\in I[\mu^{+}]
.

This ideal can be defined both for any cardinal
\mu
but its behavior depends heavily on whether
\mu
is regular or singular: If
\mu
is regular,
E_{<\mu}^{\mu^{+}}\in I[\mu^{+}]
and so
I[\mu^{+}]
is completely determined by the membership of stationary subsets of
E_{\mu}^{\mu^{+}}
. On the other hand, if
\mu
is singular, we can only prove that
E_{\leq\mu}^{\mu^{+}}\in I[\mu^{+}]
which leaves open many more possibilities regarding the possible cofinalities for which
I[\mu^{+}]
is nontrivial. As we will show, this result is sharp for successors of singular cardinals of countable cofinality.

Another difference is in the way the failure of
\mathsf{AP}_{\mu}
is commonly forced: For regular
\mu
there is a natural correspondence between the non-approachability of points and the “approximation property” which occurs implicitly in work of Mitchell (see
[
Mit72
]
) and was explicitly defined by Hamkins (see
[
Ham03
]
). Due to this, models where
\mathsf{AP}_{\mu}
fails are often obtained by employing variants of Mitchell forcing or side condition posets. On the other hand, the failure of
\mathsf{AP}_{\mu}
for
\mu
singular is most commonly obtained by taking the “natural failure” of
\mathsf{AP}_{\mu}
above supercompact cardinals and “moving it down”. The most direct path to this concept comes by considering a characterization of approachability using colorings (this idea is also due to Shelah, see
[
She79
]
). We use the presentation from
[
Eis10
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mu
be a singular cardinal and
\lambda=\mu^{+}
. Let
d\colon[\lambda]^{2}\to\cf(\mu)
be a coloring. We view
d
as a function on the ascending pairs of elements of
\lambda
.

(1)

d
is
subadditive
if for all
\alpha<\beta<\gamma<\lambda
,

d(\alpha,\gamma)\leq\max\{d(\alpha,\beta),d(\beta,\gamma)\}

(2)

d
is
normal
if for all
\alpha\in\lambda
and
i<\cf(\mu)
,

|\{\beta<\alpha\;|\;d(\beta,\alpha)\leq i\}|<\mu

(3)

An ordinal
\alpha\leq\lambda
is
d
-approachable
if there exists an unbounded set
A\subseteq\alpha
such for all
\beta\in A
,

\sup\{d(\beta,\alpha)\;|\;\beta\in A\cap\alpha\}<\cf(\mu)

We let
S(d)
consist of all
\alpha<\lambda
which are
d
-approachable.

As Shelah shows in
[
She79
]
, whenever
\mu
is singular, there is a normal subadditive coloring
d\colon[\mu^{+}]\to\cf(\mu)
.

It is clear that
\lambda
itself is never
d
-approachable with respect to any normal subadditive coloring
d\colon[\lambda]^{2}\to\cf(\mu)
. However, we have chosen to include it in the definition in order to be able to say “
\lambda
does not become
d
-approachable in
W
”, where
W
is some outer model.

d
-approachability can be simplified as follows (see
[
Eis10
, Corollary 3.27]
):

Fact 1.3
.

Let
\mu
be a singular cardinal and
d\colon[\mu^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\mu)
a normal, subadditive coloring. An ordinal
\alpha<\mu^{+}
is
d
-approachable if and only if
\cf(\alpha)\leq\cf(\mu)
or there is a cofinal set
A\subseteq\alpha
of minimal ordertype and
i<\cf(\mu)
such that for all
\beta<\gamma
, both in
A
,
d(\beta,\gamma)\leq i
.

We now connect the previous two notions of approachability. In most of our models we will have that the
\mathsf{GCH}
holds, so all our singular cardinals will be strong limits. This simplifies the previous concepts, because whenever
\mu
is a singular strong limit, the following holds:

(1)

The approachability ideal
I[\mu^{+}]
is equal to the
weak approachability ideal

I[\mu^{+},\mu]
(where instead of requiring
A\cap\beta\in\{x_{i}\;|\;i<\gamma\}
for every
\beta<\gamma
we merely require each
A\cap\beta
to be a subset of
x_{i}
for some
i<\gamma
).

(2)

The approachability ideal is generated by a single set modulo the nonstationary ideal, i.e. there is
A\in I[\mu^{+}]
such that for any
S\subseteq\mu^{+}
,
S\in I[\mu^{+}]
if and only if
S\smallsetminus A
is nonstationary.

Combining this with the connection between
I[\mu^{+},\mu]
and
d
-approachability, we have the following:

Fact 1.4

(
[
Eis10
, Corollary 3.35]
)
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a strong limit singular cardinal and
d\colon[\mu^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\mu)
is a normal, subadditive coloring. Then
S(d)\cup E_{\leq\cf(\mu)}^{\mu^{+}}
generates
I[\mu^{+}]
modulo the nonstationary ideal.

We finish this small exposition with the following helpful fact which states that whenever
\alpha
is
d
-approachable, any cofinal subset of
\alpha
can be refined to another cofinal subset on which
d
is bounded:

Fact 1.5

(
[
She79
, Remark 28]
)
.

Let
\mu
be a singular cardinal and
d\colon[\mu^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\mu)
a subadditive coloring. Let
\alpha\in\mu^{+}
with
\cf(\alpha)>\cf(\mu)
and suppose that
\alpha
is
d
-approachable. Then whenever
A\subseteq\alpha
is unbounded, there is
B\subseteq\alpha
unbounded and
i<\cf(\mu)
such that whenever
\alpha<\beta
, both in
B
,
d(\alpha,\beta)\leq i
.

We now turn to supercompact cardinals. We use the following idea of Magidor (see
[
Mag71
]
; the terminology is borrowed from
[
MV21
]
):

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\kappa\leq\Theta
be regular cardinals.
M\prec H(\Theta)
is a
\kappa
-Magidor model
if the following holds:

(1)

M\cap\kappa\in\kappa
,

(2)

whenever
B\subseteq M
is such that
B\subseteq C\in M
, there is
A\in M
such that
A\cap M=B
.

Magidor showed that the supercompactness of a cardinal
\kappa
is equivalent to the existence of sufficiently many
\kappa
-Magidor models. We only use one direction which is as follows:

Theorem 1.7

(Magidor)
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal and
\Theta\geq\kappa
regular. Then whenever
x\in H(\Theta)
there is a
\kappa
-Magidor model
M\prec H(\Theta)
with
x\in M
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta
and
x
be given. Let
j\colon V\to M
be a
|H^{V}(\Theta)|
-supercompact embedding with critical point
\kappa
. Then
j(x)\in j[H^{V}(\Theta)]\prec H^{M}(j(\Theta))
. We verify that
j[H^{V}(\Theta)]
is a
j(\kappa)
-Magidor model. Clearly
j[H^{V}(\Theta)]\cap j(\kappa)=\kappa\in j(\kappa)
. Let
B\subseteq j[H^{V}(\Theta)]
,
B\subseteq C\in j[H^{V}(\Theta)]
. Then
B=j[A]
where
A\subseteq H^{V}(\Theta)
. Furthermore,
A\in H^{V}(\Theta)
, since
B
is a subset of an element of
j[H^{V}(\Theta)]
. It follows that
B=j(A)\cap j[H^{V}(\Theta)]
.

It follows by elementarity that there is a
\kappa
-Magidor model
M\prec H(\Theta)
with
x\in M
.
∎

We will also make use of Laver functions in order to obtain indestructibility results:

Definition 1.8
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal.
l\colon\kappa\to V_{\kappa}
is a
Laver function
if for any
x
and any sufficiently large
\lambda
there is a
\lambda
-supercompact embedding
j\colon V\to M
with critical point
\kappa
such that
j(l)(\kappa)=x
.

Laver showed (see
[
Lav78
]
) that any supercompact cardinal carries a Laver function:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\kappa
be supercompact. Then there is a Laver function
j\colon V\to M
.

Since elementary embeddings and transitive collapses are “dual” to each other, the following lemma is not surprising. Recall that for any extensional well-founded structure
(M,E)
, the Mostowski-collapse
\pi_{M}\colon(M,E)\to(N,\in)
is defined inductively by
\pi_{M}(x):=\{\pi_{M}(y)\;|\;y\in M\wedge yEx\}
.

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal and
\Theta\geq\kappa
regular. Let
l\colon\kappa\to V_{\kappa}
be a Laver function. Then whenever
x,y\in H(\Theta)
there is a
\kappa
-Magidor model
M\prec H(\Theta)
such that
x,y\in M
and
l(M\cap\kappa)=\pi_{M}(y)
.

Proof.

Let
\Theta
and
x,y
be given. Let
j\colon V\to M
be a sufficiently supercompact embedding with critical point
\kappa
such that
j(l)(\kappa)=y
. As before, the pointwise image
j[H^{V}(\Theta)]\prec H^{M}(j(\Theta))
is a
j(\kappa)
-Magidor model. One shows by induction that
\pi_{j[H^{V}(\Theta)]}(j(x))=x
whenever
x\in H^{V}(\Theta)
, which shows the statement (for more details, see
[
Jak25
, Lemma 5.4.14]
)
∎

Lastly, there is the following connection between Magidor models and normal, subadditive colorings:

Lemma 1.11
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal, let
\delta\geq\kappa
be a singular cardinal such that
\cf(\delta)<\kappa
and let
\Theta>\delta
be regular. Let
d\colon[\delta^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\delta)
be normal and subadditive and let
M\prec H(\Theta)
be a
\kappa
-Magidor model with
d,\delta\in M
. Then the following holds:

(1)

Letting
\rho:=\sup(M\cap\delta^{+})
,
\cf(\rho)
is the successor of a singular cardinal
\mho
with cofinality
\cf(\delta)
.

(2)

There is an unbounded subset
A\subseteq\rho
(we can choose
M\cap\delta^{+}
) such that whenever
\alpha\in A
and
i<\cf(\delta)
,

|\{\beta\in A\cap\alpha\;|\;d(\beta,\alpha)\leq i\}|<\mho

Proof.

For (1), we first note that whenever
\mu\in M
is a cardinal,
\otp(M\cap\mu)
is a cardinal as well: Otherwise, there is
\alpha<\otp(M\cap\mu)
and a well-order
X\subseteq\alpha\times\alpha
with ordertype
\otp(M\cap\mu)
. It follows that
\alpha=\otp(M\cap\nu)
for some
\nu<\mu
,
\nu\in M
and there exists
B\subseteq M\cap\nu\times M\cap\nu
with ordertype
\otp(M\cap\mu)
. Let
A\in M
be such that
A\cap M=B
. Then by elementarity
A\subseteq\nu\times\nu
and
\otp(M)=\mu
. But this contradicts the fact that
\mu
is a cardinal.

Furthermore, we know that
\sup(M\cap\delta)=\delta
, since
\cf(\delta)<\kappa
. It follows that
\otp(M\cap\delta)
is a cardinal and has cofinality
\cf(\delta)
(since, whenever
f\in M
is a cofinal function from
\cf(\delta)
into
\delta
,
f\upharpoonright(M\cap\cf(\delta))=f
is a cofinal function from
\cf(\delta)
into
M\cap\delta
). It follows that
\otp(M\cap\delta^{+})=\otp(M\cap\delta)^{+}
, so
\otp(M\cap\delta^{+})
(which equals
\cf(\sup(M\cap\delta^{+}))
) is the successor of a singular cardinal with cofinality
\cf(\delta)
. We let
\mho:=\otp(M\cap\delta)
.

For (2), as claimed, we choose
A:=M\cap\delta^{+}
. Let
\alpha\in A
and
i<\cf(\delta)
. Then
A_{\alpha}:=\{\beta\in A\cap\alpha\;|\;d(\beta,\alpha)\leq i\}\subseteq M\cap\alpha
and so there is
B\in M
with
B\cap M=A_{\alpha}
. It follows by elementarity that
B=\{\beta\in\alpha\;|\;d(\beta,\alpha)\leq i\}
. Again by elementarity,
|B|<\delta
, so there is (in
M
) some
\mu<\delta
and a surjection
f\colon\mu\to B
. Then
f\upharpoonright(\mu\cap M)
is a surjection from
(\mu\cap M)
onto
B\cap M=A_{\alpha}
. Since
\mu<\delta
and
\otp(M\cap\delta)
is a cardinal, it follows that
\otp(M\cap\mu)<\otp(M\cap\delta)=\mho
and we are done.
∎

It follows from (2) that
d
induces a normal, subadditive coloring on
\mho^{+}
in the following way: Let
f\colon\mho^{+}\to M\cap\delta^{+}
be order-preserving. We let
e\colon[\mho^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\mho)=\cf(\delta)
be defined by
e(\alpha,\beta):=d(f(\alpha),f(\beta))
. Then
e
is a normal, subadditive coloring on
\mho^{+}
. Additionally, in any outer model,
\rho
is
d
-approachable if and only if
\mho^{+}
is
e
-approachable (by Fact
1.5
). This shows that the problem of obtaining non-approachable points of arbitrary cofinality reduces to the question of whether or not, given a singular cardinal
\delta
and a regular cardinal
\mu<\delta
, it is possible to collapse
|\delta^{+}|=\mu
without making
\delta^{+}

d
-approachable with respect to any normal subadditive coloring
d\colon[\delta^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\delta)
. This problem was answered by the author and Levine in
[
JL25
]
. In Section 4, we will introduce a poset with the same crucial property which can be defined using (in practice) much weaker hypotheses which allows us to iterate the construction and prove our main theorem.

So in summary, we have shown:

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal. let
\mu>\kappa
be singular with
\cf(\mu)<\kappa
. Then whenever
d\colon[\mu^{+}]^{2}\to\cf(\mu)
is a normal, subadditive coloring, there are stationarily many
\alpha\in\mu^{+}
which are not
d
-approachable. In particular,
\mu^{+}\notin I[\mu^{+}]
.

Lastly, we prove that Magidor models have transitive collapses which reflect many set-theoretical statements (this mirrors the fact that for all “bounded” statements
\phi
, one can find a sufficiently supercompact embedding
j\colon V\to M
such that
V\models\phi\leftrightarrow M\models\phi
due to the closure of
M
):

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
\kappa
be a supercompact cardinal, let
\Theta>\kappa
be regular and let
M\prec H(\Theta)
be a
\kappa
-Magidor model. Let
\pi\colon M\to N
be the transitive collapse of
M
. Whenever
\delta\in M
is regular,
H(\pi(\delta))\subseteq N
.

Proof.

Since every element of
H(\pi(\delta))
can be coded as a subset of
\pi(\delta)
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{P}(\pi(\delta))\subseteq N
. To this end, let
x\subseteq\pi(\delta)
. Then
\pi^{-1}[x]\subseteq\delta\cap M
and so there is
A\in M
with
A\cap M=\pi^{-1}[x]
. Then
\pi(A)=\pi[A\cap M]=x\in N
.
∎

So this means that whenever
M\prec H(\Theta)
is a
\kappa
-Magidor model, the transitive collapse
N
is elementary in
V
for every statement which is verifiable by some large enough
H(\pi(\Theta^{\prime}))
for
\Theta^{\prime}\in H(\Theta)
.

1.2.
Forcing

In this subsection, we introduce some lesser known definitions and facts regarding the technique of forcing. Our notation is standard. To reduce confusion, we write
{<}\,\kappa
-closed (directed closed; distributive; etc.). We follow the convention that filters are upwards closed, so that if
p\leq q
,
p
forces more than
q
. Given a poset
\mathbb{P}
, we let
\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
be the canonical
\mathbb{P}
-name for the
\mathbb{P}
-generic filter, i.e.
\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}:=\{(p,\check{p})\;|\;p\in\mathbb{P}\}
.

We start with the separative quotient:

Definition 1.14
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset. On
\mathbb{P}
, define the equivalence relation
\sim
by
p\sim q
if and only if
\forall z(z\perp p\leftrightarrow z\perp q)
. Define
\preceq
on
\mathbb{P}/\sim
by
[p]\preceq[q]
if and only if
\forall r\leq p(r||q)
. Then
(\mathbb{P}/\sim,\preceq)
is called the
separative quotient
of
\mathbb{P}
and
h\colon\mathbb{P}\to(\mathbb{P}/\sim)
,

is the quotient mapping.

It is easy to verify that

and

are compatible in
\mathbb{P}
if and only if they are compatible in
\mathbb{P}/\sim
. Additionally, one sees easily that
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}/\sim
are forcing equivalent.

We will also make use of projection analyses:

Definition 1.15
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be posets. A function
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
is a

if the following holds:

(1)

\pi(1_{\mathbb{P}})=1_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(2)

Whenever

and

, there is

such that
\pi(p^{\prime})\leq q
.

The existence of a projection from
\mathbb{P}
onto
\mathbb{Q}
implies that any forcing extension by
\mathbb{Q}
can again be extended by forcing to a forcing extension by
\mathbb{P}
. This is done using the
quotient forcing
:

Definition 1.16
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be poset,
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
a projection. Let
G
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter. In

, we let
\mathbb{P}/G
consist of all those

such that

, ordered as a suborder of
\mathbb{P}
. We call
\mathbb{P}/G
the

of
\mathbb{P}
and

. We also let
\mathbb{P}/\mathbb{Q}
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-name for
\mathbb{P}/\Gamma_{\mathbb{Q}}
and refer to

as the

of

and

.

The quotient forcing actually works as intended:

Lemma 1.17
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{Q}
be posets,
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
a projection. Let
G
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-generic filter and let
H
be
\mathbb{P}/G
-generic over

. Then

is
\mathbb{P}
-generic over
V
and

. In particular,

.

It follows that every forcing extension by
\mathbb{Q}
can be once again forcing extended to a forcing extension by
\mathbb{P}
. This allows us to obtain regularity properties for a poset

by finding a well-behaved poset
\mathbb{P}
and a projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{Q}
. It is clear that in this situation, if
\mathbb{P}
does not collapse certain cardinals or add sequences of ordinals of a certain length, neither does
\mathbb{Q}
, since any forcing extension by
\mathbb{Q}
is contained in a forcing extension by
\mathbb{P}
.

For iterations, one can easily find projections by using the

, an idea due to Laver:

Definition 1.18
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset and
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
a
\mathbb{P}
-name for a poset. Then the termspace forcing

\mathbb{P}
-names
\dot{q}
for elements of
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
(i.e.

), ordered by

Using standard arguments on names, one shows:

Lemma 1.19
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset and
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
a
\mathbb{P}
-name for a poset. The identity function is a projection from
\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{P},\dot{\mathbb{Q}})
onto
\mathbb{P}*\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
.

We will actually use an extension of the previous result (which is nonetheless shown using almost the same arguments):

Lemma 1.20

(Foreman
[
For83
]
)
.

Let
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\kappa}
be a full support (Easton support) iteration. Let
\mathbb{T}:=\prod_{\alpha<\kappa}\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha})
, where the product is taken with full support (Easton support). Then the identity is a projection from
\mathbb{T}
onto the inverse (Easton) limit of
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\kappa}
.

Proof.

We do the proof in the full support case, but the proof in the Easton support case is entirely the same.

Let
\mathbb{P}:=(\mathbb{P},\leq_{\mathbb{P}})
be the inverse limit of
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\kappa}
. Denote by
\leq_{\mathbb{T}}
the ordering on
\mathbb{T}
. It is clear that the identity is a function from
\mathbb{T}
onto
\mathbb{P}
and preserves the ordering. Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
and let

. We want to find
p^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{T}}p
with
p^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{P}}t
. By induction on
\alpha
, let
p^{\prime}(\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for an element of
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
forced by
p^{\prime}\upharpoonright\alpha
to be equal to

and by conditions incompatible with
p^{\prime}\upharpoonright\alpha
to be equal to
p(\alpha)
. Then clearly
p^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{T}}p
(since
p^{\prime}(\alpha)
is in any case forced to be below
p(\alpha)
) and
p^{\prime}\leq_{\mathbb{P}}t
(since
p^{\prime}(\alpha)
is always forced by
p^{\prime}\upharpoonright\alpha
to be equal to
t(\alpha)
).
∎

We use the following new closure property simply because it fits our proofs:

Definition 1.21
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset.
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is
weakly directed
if whenever
p,q\in X
, there is
r\in X
such that
r\Vdash\check{p}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}\wedge\check{q}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
. If
\mu
is a cardinal,
\mathbb{P}
is
strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed
if whenever
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is weakly directed and
|X|<\mu
, there exists
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
p\leq q
for every
q\in X
.

We note that
r\Vdash\check{q}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
if and only if
q
is compatible with every extension of
r
: On one hand, if there is
s\leq r
which is incompatible with
q
, any generic filter containing
s
contains
r
but not
q
. On the other hand, if
q
is compatible with every extension of
r
and
G
is
\mathbb{P}
-generic containing
r
, the upward closure of
G\cup\{q\}
is a filter and thus equal to
G
by genericity. Hence,
q\in G
and since
G
was arbitrary,
r\Vdash\check{q}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
.

Clearly, for separative forcings
\mathbb{P}
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}
is weakly directed if and only if it is directed, since
p\Vdash\check{q}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}}
if and only if
p\leq q
. One might think that the notion of strong directed closure could be made redundant by simply working with the separative closures of our partial orders but in general the separative closure of a partial order must not share the same closure properties as the original poset.

Strong directed closure however is inherited by the separative quotient:

Lemma 1.22
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset and
\mu
a cardinal. If
\mathbb{P}
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed, then the separative quotient of
\mathbb{P}
is
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed.

Proof.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}/\sim
be the quotient mapping and let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}/\sim
be directed,
|X|<\mu
. Let
\hat{X}
be such that
X=\{[p]_{\sim}\;|\;p\in\hat{X}\}
. Then
|\hat{X}|=|X|
and
\hat{X}
is weakly directed: Let
p,q\in\hat{X}
. Since
X
is directed, there is
r\in\hat{X}
such that
[r]_{\sim}\preceq[p]_{\sim},[q]_{\sim}
. It follows that any extension of
r
is compatible with
p
and
q
. Ergo,
[r]_{\sim}\Vdash[\check{p}]_{\sim}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}/\sim}\wedge[\check{q}]_{\sim}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{P}/\sim}
. Since
\mathbb{P}
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed, there exists
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
p\leq q
for every
q\in\hat{X}
. Then whenever
[q]_{\sim}\in X
,
q\in\hat{X}
, so
h(p)\leq h(q)=[q]_{\sim}
. Ergo
h(p)
is a lower bound of
X
.
∎

It is easy to see that strong directed closure is productive. Additionally, the usual proof of the iterability of directed closure goes through for strong directed closure as well:

2.
Iteration Theorems for Prikry-Type forcings

Due to technical reasons, most of our results will be obtained using Prikry-type forcings. In this section, we give definitions and prove results regarding iterations of these posets.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
be a partial order and let
\leq_{0}
be another partial order on
\mathbb{P}
such that
\leq
refines
\leq_{0}
. We say that
(\mathbb{P},\leq,\leq_{0})
is a
Prikry-type forcing
if for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
and every statement
\sigma
in the forcing language there is
q\leq_{0}p
which decides
\sigma
(this is known as the
Prikry property
).

For any Prikry-type forcing
(\mathbb{P},\leq,\leq_{0})
, the order
\leq_{0}
is only used to derive regularity properties. As such, we will abuse notation and refer to forcing “with
\mathbb{P}
” as forcing with
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
. In the same way, we will let “
\tau
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name” refer to the fact that
\tau
is a
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
-name.

The first example of a Prikry-type forcing is the original poset used by Karel Prikry to give a measurable cardinal
\kappa
cofinality
\omega
in a forcing extension without collapsing any cardinals (see
[
Pri70
]
): For
\kappa
measurable and
U
a normal measure over
\kappa
, the forcing
\mathbb{P}(U)
adds a sequence
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\omega}
which is cofinal in
\kappa
. In particular,
\mathbb{P}(U)
is not countably closed. However, there is an additional order
\leq^{*}
on
\mathbb{P}(U)
such that
(\mathbb{P}(U),\leq,\leq^{*})
is a Prikry-type forcing notion and
(\mathbb{P}(U),\leq^{*})
is
{<}\,\kappa
-closed. This implies that
\mathbb{P}(U)
does not add any new bounded subsets of
\kappa
and thus does not collapse cardinals below and including
\kappa
. Combined with the
\kappa^{+}
-cc of
\mathbb{P}(U)
we can see that no cardinals are collapsed even though the regularity of
\kappa
is destroyed.

Other examples include variants of Namba forcing. The original Namba forcing (see
[
BCH90
]
) shares some similarities with Prikry forcing. It adds a countable cofinal sequence to
\aleph_{2}
without collapsing
\aleph_{1}
and without adding reals (assuming
\mathsf{CH}
holds in the ground model). However, in the case of Namba forcing, both of these regularity properties follow from abstract considerations (such as Shelah’s
\mathbb{I}
-condition
, see
[
She17
, Chapter XI]
) which introduce complications regarding the preservation of larger cardinals and iterability. To remedy both of these problems, we will be using Namba forcings defined from particularly well-behaved ideals which function more similarly to supercompact Prikry forcing.

We start with the problem of iterating Prikry-type forcings. This was largely solved by Magidor in his famous paper proving that it is consistent that the first strongly compact cardinal can also be the first measurable cardinal (which he achieved by successively singularizing every smaller measurable cardinal; see
[
Mag76
]
). We will be using iterations both with full support and with Easton support. The most important point that makes sure the iterations have the Prikry property is the restriction on where non-direct extensions can be used: For full support, only finitely many non-direct extensions are allowed while for Easton support, arbitrarily many non-direct extensions are allowed outside the previous support and only finitely many non-direct extensions are allowed inside the previous support.

We begin with the definition and properties of full support iterations:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
be a sequence such that each
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0})
is a poset and each
(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0})
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for a Prikry-type poset. We define the statement “
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
is a full support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho
” by induction on
\rho
.

((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
is a full support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho
if
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho^{\prime}}
is a full support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho^{\prime}
for every
\rho^{\prime}<\rho
and moreover:

(1)

If
\rho=\rho^{\prime}+1
, then
(\mathbb{P}_{\rho},\leq_{\rho})=(\mathbb{P}_{\rho^{\prime}},\leq_{\rho^{\prime}})*(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rho^{\prime}},\dot{\leq}_{\rho^{\prime}})
and
(p^{\prime},\dot{q}^{\prime})\leq_{\rho,0}(p,\dot{q})
if and only if
p^{\prime}\leq_{\rho^{\prime},0}p
and
p^{\prime}\Vdash\dot{q}^{\prime}\dot{\leq}_{\rho^{\prime},0}\dot{q}
.

(2)

If
\rho
is a limit, then
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}
consists of all functions
p
on
\rho
such that for all
\alpha<\rho
,
p\upharpoonright\alpha\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
and the following holds:

(i)

p^{\prime}\leq_{\rho}p
if and only if
p^{\prime}\upharpoonright\rho^{\prime}\leq_{\rho^{\prime}}p\upharpoonright\rho^{\prime}
for every
\rho^{\prime}<\rho
and there exists a finite subset
b
of
\rho
such that whenever
\rho^{\prime}\notin b
, then
p^{\prime}\upharpoonright\rho^{\prime}\Vdash p^{\prime}(\rho^{\prime})\dot{\leq}_{\rho^{\prime},0}p(\rho^{\prime})
.

(ii)

p^{\prime}\leq_{\rho,0}p
if and only if
p^{\prime}\leq_{\rho}p
and the set
b
is empty.

So we take the usual full support iteration with the caveat that we are only allowed to pass to a non-direct extension at merely finitely many coordinates. This is necessary in order to obtain that such iterations have the Prikry property:

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Git10
, Lemma 6.2]
)
.

Let
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
be a full-support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho
. Then each
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
has the Prikry property.

It follows just as in Lemma
1.23
that the following holds (since
\leq_{\alpha,0}
refines
\leq_{\alpha}
so that any
\leq_{\alpha,0}
-lower bound is also a
\leq_{\alpha}
-lower bound):

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
be a full-support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho
and
\mu
a cardinal. Assume that for each
\alpha<\rho
,
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
forces that
(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0})
is strongly
{<}\,\check{\mu}
-directed closed. Then for each
\alpha<\rho
,
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0})
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed.

We now introduce the Easton-support Magidor iteration which has the following curious change: In the full-support iteration we are only allowed to take non-direct extensions finitely often. However, in the Easton-support iteration we are allowed to take arbitrarily many non-direct extensions provided that they occur outside of the support (inside the support, only finitely many extensions are allowed). As before, this is necessary in order to obtain the Prikry property.

.

Let
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}

is a poset and each
(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0})
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for a Prikry-type poset. We define the statement “
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
is an Easton-support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho
” by induction on
\rho
.

((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho}
is an Easton-support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho
if
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\rho^{\prime}}
is an Easton-support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings of length
\rho^{\prime}
for every
\rho^{\prime}<\rho
and moreover:

(1)

If
\rho=\rho^{\prime}+1
, then
(\mathbb{P}_{\rho},\leq_{\rho})=(\mathbb{P}_{\rho^{\prime}},\leq_{\rho^{\prime}})*(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\rho^{\prime}},\dot{\leq}_{\rho^{\prime}})
and

\leq
refines
\leq_{0}
, we also obtain a projection from

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
\kappa
be an ordinal and let
((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))_{\alpha<\kappa}

As in the proof of Lemma

\mathbb{P}

and preserves

3.
The Laver-Ideal Property

In this section, we introduce the Laver-ideal property and prove a characterization for posets which force it. This expands on
[
JL25
, Section 3]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mu<\nu
be regular cardinals. We let
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\nu,\kappa)
state that there exists
I
, a
{<}\,\nu
-complete and normal ideal over
[\kappa]^{<\nu}
such that there is a set
B\subseteq I^{+}
which is dense in
I^{+}
with respect to
\subseteq
and strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed.

Classically, by work of Laver (unpublished) and Galvin-Jech-Magidor (see
[
GJM78
, Theorem 2]
), whenever
\mu
is regular and
\nu>\mu
, the Levy collapse
\Coll(\mu,<\nu)
, which forces
\nu=\mu^{+}
, also forces
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\nu,\nu)
. In this section, we prove a characterization for posets which force
\mathsf{LIP}
(which basically follows from their proof, combined with ideas of Foreman) and show that when starting from a supercompact cardinal instead of a measurable cardinal, we even obtain
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\nu,\kappa)
for all
\kappa\geq\nu
. We do this because we later have to show that the direct extension ordering on the tail of our iteration forces enough instances of
\mathsf{LIP}
to make other parts of the iteration work (this will become apparent in the proof of the main theorem).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mu<\nu
be regular cardinals such that
\nu
is measurable. Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset. We say that
\mathbb{P}
is a
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\nu)
-forcing
if the following holds:

(1)

\mathbb{P}
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed and
\nu
-cc.

(2)

\mathbb{P}\subseteq V_{\nu}
.

(3)

There is a club
C\subseteq\nu
such that whenever
\alpha\in C
is inaccessible, there is a projection
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}\to(\mathbb{P}\cap V_{\alpha})
such that whenever

is weakly directed,

and

for every

such that

and

for every

.

Condition (3) is a strengthening of the statement that
\mathbb{P}/(\mathbb{P}\cap V_{\alpha})
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed but it is in general easier to verify.

\mu<\nu

4.
Collapsing without Approachability

As stated in the discussion below Lemma
1.11
, the main obstacle to obtaining a model where there are many non-approachable points in the successor of a singular cardinal is finding a poset which collapses the successor of a singular cardinal of a given cofinality without making it approachable with respect to any normal, subadditive coloring. To achieve this for
\kappa=\mu^{+}
,
\mu
singular, Shelah used
\Coll(\cf(\mu),\mu)
. This poset has size
\mu
(under
\mathsf{GCH}
) and thus preserves
\mu^{+}
and, in particular, does not make it approachable with respect to any normal, subadditive coloring. However, this approach has two major drawbacks: On one hand, it is specific to obtaining non-approachable points of cofinality
\cf(\mu)^{+}
(since larger collapses would also collapse
\kappa
) and it cannot be iterated (since
\kappa
always needs to be preserved). As such, it was a major open problem whether there could be a poset which can collapse
\kappa
as a cardinal without making it approachable with respect to any normal subadditive coloring.

A positive solution to this problem was found by the author and Levine for successors of singulars of countable cofinality (see
[
JL25
]
). In the cited work, we relied on having
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\aleph_{n},\aleph_{n})
for infinitely many
n\in\omega
. In this section we will show that it is possible to obtain a poset which accomplishes the same task but can be defined from weaker hypotheses (i.e. assuming
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\aleph_{k},\aleph_{n})
for a single
k
and infinitely many
n\in\omega
). This is crucial in making the later iteration work since we will just need a single generically supercompact cardinal instead of infinitely many generically measurable cardinals which is much easier to obtain in practice.

We first introduce a variant of Namba forcing which we call

. This forcing functions similarly to supercompact Prikry forcing but can be defined for successor cardinals.

). To show this, we first prove some elementary statements regarding ideal combinatorics.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
I
and
J
be ideals on
X
and
Y
respectively. We define the
Fubini product

I\otimes J
(which is an ideal on
X\times Y
) by

X\times Y\supseteq A\in I\otimes J:\Leftrightarrow\{x\in X\;|\;\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in A\}\in J^{+}\}\in I

I.e.,
A\in(I\otimes J)^{+}
if and only if
\{x\in X\;|\;\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in A\}\in J^{+}\}\in I^{+}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

The Fubini product is associative. In other words, whenever
I,J,K
are ideals (on
X,Y,Z
respectively), then
(I\otimes J)\otimes K=I\otimes(J\otimes K)
.

Proof.

Let
A\subseteq X\times Y\times Z
. For
x\in X
, define
A_{x}:=\{(y,z)\in Y\times Z\;|\;(x,y,z)\in A\}
.

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\in(I\otimes J)\otimes K

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\{(x,y)\in X\times Y\;|\;\{z\in Z\;|\;(x,y,z)\in A\}\in K^{+}\}\in(I\otimes J)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\{x\in X\;|\;\{y\in Y\;|\;\{z\in Z\;|\;(x,y,z)\in A\}\in K^{+}\}\in J^{+}\}\in I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\{x\in X\;|\;\{y\in Y\;|\;\{z\in Z\;|\;(y,z)\in A_{x}\}\in K^{+}\}\in J^{+}\}\in I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\{x\in X\;|\;A_{x}\in(J\otimes K)^{+}\}\in I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\{x\in X\;|\;\{(y,z)\in Y\times Z\;|\;(x,y,z)\in A\}\in(J\otimes K)^{+}\}\in I

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow A\in I\otimes(J\times K)

This finishes the proof.

∎

In particular, we can write expressions such as “
I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n}
” without having to worry about the order in which these products are taken.

The Fubini product is related to Namba forcing as follows: Given finitely many sets
(X_{k})_{k<n}
and ideals
(J_{k})_{k<n}
, we say that
p\subseteq\bigcup_{l<n}\prod_{k\leq l}X_{k}
is a
(J_{k})_{k<n}
-Laver-tree
if
p
is nonempty, closed under restriction and for every
s\in p
with
|s|<n
,
\osucc_{p}(s):=\{x\in X_{|s|}\;|\;s^{\frown}x\in p\}\in J_{|s|}^{+}
. We let
[p]:=\{s\in p\;|\;|s|=n\}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
J_{0},\dots,J_{n-1}
be ideals. Then a set
A\subseteq X_{0}\times\dots\times X_{n-1}
is in
(J_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes J_{n-1})^{+}
if and only if there is a
(J_{k})_{k<n}
-Laver-tree
p
such that
[p]\subseteq A
.

Proof.

By induction. The beginning is clear. Assume the statement has been shown for
J_{0},\dots,J_{n-1}
and let
J_{n}
be some ideal. Let
A\in(J_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes J_{n})^{+}
. It follows that for
(J_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes J_{n-1})^{+}
many
s\in\prod_{k<n}X_{k}
,
\{x\in X_{n}\;|\;s^{\frown}x\in A\}\in J_{n}^{+}
. By the inductive hypothesis, let
p
be a
(J_{k})_{k<n}
-Laver-tree such that whenever
s\in p
,
\{x\in X_{n}\;|\;s^{\frown}x\in A\}\in J_{n}^{+}
. Let
p^{\prime}
be the tree of all
n+1
-element sequences
s
with
s\upharpoonright n\in p
and
(s\upharpoonright n,s(n))\in A
. then
p^{\prime}
is a
(J_{k})_{k\leq n}
-Laver-tree and
[p^{\prime}]\subseteq A
.
∎

We also have to show that the completeness of ideals is preserved by Fubini products.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
I
and
J
be
{<}\,\kappa
-complete. Then
I\otimes J
is
{<}\,\kappa
-complete.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq I\otimes J
be of size
{<}\,\kappa
. We want to show that
A:=\bigcup\mathcal{A}\in I\otimes J
. For every
x\in X
,
\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in A\}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{A}}\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in B\}
. This set is in
J^{+}
if and only if
\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in B\}\in J^{+}
for some
B\in\mathcal{A}
, by the completeness of
J
. So

\{x\in X\;|\;\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in A\}\in J^{+}\}=\bigcup_{B\in\mathcal{A}}\{x\in X\;|\;\{y\in Y\;|\;(x,y)\in B\}\in J^{+}\}

which is in
I
by completeness. Ergo,
A\in I\otimes J
.
∎

Now we can show the result we are actually after:

Lemma 4.9
.

The poset
(\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega}),\leq_{0})
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
be weakly directed with respect to
\leq_{0}
. Let
p:=\bigcap X
. Note that
p
is not necessarily a condition in
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
, since
X
is only weakly directed. E.g. it is possible that
X
contains two conditions
q_{0}
and
q_{1}
such that for some
\alpha\in\osucc_{q_{0}}(\stem(q_{0}))\cap\osucc_{q_{1}}(\stem(q_{1}))
, the
\osucc
-sets of
\stem(q_{i})^{\frown}\alpha
are disjoint for
i=0,1
(as long as this does not occur for too many
\alpha
). In that case, the intersection
q_{0}\cap q_{1}
contains a terminal node, namely
\stem(q_{0})^{\frown}\alpha
, and is therefore not a condition in
\mathbb{L}
. However, we will show that
p
can be refined to a condition in
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
with the same stem. Let
s:=\stem(p)
(which is also the stem of every
q\in X
). We will assume for notational simplicity that
s=\emptyset
.

We first prove a general claim. Let
\mathbb{L}:=\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
.

Claim 1
.

If
q_{0},q_{1}\in\mathbb{L}
,
\stem(q_{0})=\stem(q_{1})=\emptyset
and
q_{0}\Vdash\check{q}_{1}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{L}}
, then for any
n\in\omega
,

\osucc_{q_{0}}^{n}(\emptyset)\subseteq^{*}\osucc_{q_{1}}^{n}(\emptyset)

where
\subseteq^{*}
is defined with regards to
I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Assume toward a contradiction that
A:=\osucc_{q_{0}}^{n}(\emptyset)\smallsetminus\osucc_{q_{1}}^{n}(\emptyset)
is
(I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n-1})
-positive. By Lemma
4.7
there is an
(I_{k})_{k<n}
-tree
T
such that
[T]\subseteq A
. Let
q^{\prime}:=\{s\in q_{0}\;|\;s\upharpoonright n\in T\}
. We claim that
q^{\prime}
is a condition in
\mathbb{L}
: Let
s\in q^{\prime}
. If
|s|\geq n
, then
\osucc_{q^{\prime}}(s)=\osucc_{q_{0}}(s)\in I_{|s|}^{+}
so we are done. If
|s|<n
, then
\osucc_{q^{\prime}}(s)=\osucc_{T}(s)
, since
[T]\subseteq A\subseteq\osucc_{q_{0}}^{n}(\emptyset)
. But that set is in
I_{|s|}^{+}
as well by assumption. It follows that
q^{\prime}\leq_{0}q_{0}
and
q^{\prime}\perp q_{1}
, since for every
s\in q^{\prime}
with
|s|=n
,
s\in A
and therefore
s\notin\osucc_{q_{1}}^{n}(\emptyset)
. This contradicts the fact that
q_{0}\Vdash\check{q}_{1}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{L}}
.
∎

This claim implies that the first
\osucc
-set is as required:

Claim 2
.

\osucc_{p}(\emptyset)\in I_{0}^{+}
.

Proof.

We claim that the collection
\{\osucc_{q}(\emptyset)\;|\;q\in X\}
is a weakly directed subset of
(B_{0},\subseteq)
. Given
q_{0},q_{1}\in X
, let
q_{2}\in X
be such that
q_{2}\Vdash q_{0},q_{1}\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{L}}
. It follows from Claim
1
with
n=0
that
\osucc_{q_{2}}(\emptyset)\subseteq^{*}\osucc_{q_{0}}(\emptyset)\cap\osucc_{q_{1}}(\emptyset)
, so
\osucc_{q_{2}}(\emptyset)
witnesses that
\osucc_{q_{0}}(\emptyset)
and
\osucc_{q_{1}}(\emptyset)
are weakly compatible.

Since
(B_{0},\subseteq)
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed and since
\osucc_{p}(\emptyset)=\bigcap_{q\in X}\osucc_{q}(\emptyset)
, the latter set is in
I_{0}^{+}
.
∎

We also have that at least for “most”

, the
\osucc
-sets are large enough:

Claim 3
.

For
n\in\omega
,
\{t\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(\emptyset)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t)\in I_{n}\}\in I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Let
q_{0},q_{1}\in X
. Then there is
q_{2}\in X
such that

. By Claim

\{t\in\osucc_{q_{2}}^{n}(\emptyset)\;|\;\osucc_{q_{2}}(t)\not\subseteq^{*}\osucc_{q_{i}}(t)\}\in I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n-1}

It follows that

A_{q_{0},q_{1},q_{2}}:=\{t\in\osucc_{q_{2}}^{n}(s)\;|\;\exists i<2(t\notin\osucc_{q_{i}}(\emptyset)\vee\osucc_{q_{2}}(t)\not\subseteq^{*}\osucc_{q_{i}}(t))\}

since it is the union of four sets in
I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n-1}
. Let

be the union of all

where
q_{0},q_{1}\in X
and
q_{2}
witnesses their weak compatibility. Then

since that ideal is
{<}\,\mu
-complete by Lemma

. It follows that whenever

, the collection

is weakly directed (first note that

): Given

, let
q_{2}
witness their weak compatibility. By assumption

and so
\osucc_{q_{2}}(t)\subseteq^{*}\osucc_{q_{0}}(t),\osucc_{q_{1}}(t)
. So whenever
t\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(\emptyset)\smallsetminus A
, we have that
\osucc_{p}(t)=\bigcap_{q\in X}\osucc_{q}(t)\in I_{|t|}^{+}
, since
(B_{n},\subseteq)
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed. It follows that

\{t\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(\emptyset)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t)\in I_{n}\}\subseteq A\in I_{0}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{n-1}

∎

Now we let
q^{\prime}
consist of all
t\in p
such that
\osucc_{p}(t)\in I_{|t|}^{+}
and for every
n
,

\{r\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(t)\;|\;\osucc_{p}^{n}(t^{\frown}r)\in I_{|t|+n}\}\in I_{|t|}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+(n-1)}

Let
q
consist of all those

such that every initial segment of

is also in
q^{\prime}
. We claim that
q\in\mathbb{L}
and
\stem(q)=\emptyset
. By Claim
2
and Claim
3
,
\emptyset\in q
and so
q
is nonempty. It is clearly closed under initial segments by construction. So we have to show that it splits wide enough. Let

. By assumption,
\osucc_{p}(t)\in I_{|t|}^{+}
. Additionally,
\osucc_{q}(t)
consists of all

such that
\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}x)\in I_{|t|+1}^{+}
and for every
n
,

\{r\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(t^{\frown}x)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}x^{\frown}r)\in I_{|t|+1+n}\}\in I_{|t|+1}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+n}

\osucc_{q}(t)\in I_{|t|}
. Then
\osucc_{p}(t)\smallsetminus\osucc_{q}(t)\in I_{|t|}^{+}
and so for every
x\in\osucc_{p}(t)\smallsetminus\osucc_{q}(t)
, either
\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}x)\in I_{|t|+1}
or there is some

such that

\{r\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(t^{\frown}x)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}x^{\frown}r)\in I_{|t|+1+n}\}\in(I_{|t|+1}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+n})^{+}

By the
{<}\,\mu^{+}
-completeness of
I_{|t|}
, one of those cases has to occur for
I_{|t|}^{+}
many

. If it is the first one, we directly have

\{x\in\osucc_{p}(t)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}x)\in I_{|t|+1}\}\in I_{|t|}^{+}

contradicting the fact that

(more precisely, the second requirement for
n=1
). In the second case, we have that the set

\{r\in\osucc_{p}^{n}(t^{\frown}x)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}x^{\frown}r)\in I_{|t|+1+n}\}

is in
(I_{|t|+1}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+n})^{+}
. Let
A:=\{r\in\osucc_{p}^{n+1}(t)\;|\;\osucc_{p}(t^{\frown}r)\in I_{|t|+1+n}\}
. Then the above equation states that

\{x\in[\kappa_{|t|}]^{<\mu^{+}}\;|\;\{r\in\prod_{i\in[|t|+1,|t|+n]}[\kappa_{i}]^{<\mu^{+}}\;|\;(x,r)\in A\}\in(I_{|t|}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+n})^{+}\}\in I_{|t|}^{+}

In other words,
A\in(I_{|t|}\otimes(I_{|t|+1}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+n}))^{+}=(I_{|t|}\otimes\dots\otimes I_{|t|+n})^{+}
(the equality follows from Lemma
4.6
). But this clearly contradicts the fact that
t\in q
(more precisely, the second requirement for
n+1
).

In summary,
q
is a condition in
\mathbb{L}
and a
\leq_{0}
-lower bound of

. This finishes the proof.
∎

It is easy to see that, after forcing with
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
, every
\kappa_{n}
has cofinality
\omega
(but we will not use this): If

is the generic branch and

, the function
n\mapsto\sup(b(n)\cap\kappa_{k})
is a cofinal function from
\omega
into
\kappa_{k}
. However,
\kappa^{*}
does not have such a small cofinality in the extension:

Lemma 4.10
.

Assume that
\tau
is an
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
-name for an ordinal and
p\in\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
forces
\tau<\check{\kappa}^{*}
. Then there is
\alpha<\kappa^{*}
and
q\leq_{0}p
which forces
\tau<\check{\alpha}
. Consequently, after forcing with
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
,
\cf(\kappa^{*})\geq\mu
.

Proof.

Let
D
be the open dense set of conditions which decide
\tau
. By Lemma
4.3
there is
q\leq_{0}p
and
n\in\omega
such that whenever
s\in q
,
|s|=n
,
q\upharpoonright s\in D
, i.e.
q\upharpoonright s\Vdash\tau=\check{\alpha}_{s}
for some
\alpha_{s}
. Since
\{s\in q\;|\;|s|=n\}
has size
\leq\kappa_{n}
, there is
\alpha>\alpha_{s}
for every
s\in q
with
|s|=n
. It follows that
q\Vdash\tau<\check{\alpha}
.

Now assume that there is
\gamma<\mu
,
\dot{F}
and
p\in\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
which forces
\dot{F}\colon\check{\gamma}\to\check{\kappa}^{*}
. Using the fact that the direct extension ordering is in particular
{<}\,\mu
-closed, we can find a condition

and ordinals
(\alpha_{\delta})_{\delta<\gamma}
such that for every
\delta<\gamma
,

. However,
\sup_{\delta<\gamma}\alpha_{\delta}<\kappa^{*}
and so
q
forces that
\dot{F}
is bounded in

, a contradiction.
∎

The following is our crucial strengthening of the main crux of our earlier work (see
[
JL25
, Theorem 4.3]
). Instead of showing that any function
\dot{F}
of the stated form has an initial segment outside of the ground model we are showing that any such function has an initial segment which is not even covered by a small set which is in the ground model. This directly implies that
\kappa^{*}
does not become approachable without relying on our collapsing forcing not adding short functions into small sets.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
be an
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})
-name for a
{<}\,\mu
-closed forcing. Whenever
\dot{F}\colon\check{\mu}\to\check{\kappa}^{*}
is forced to be unbounded by some
(p,\dot{q})\in\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})*\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
, there is
(p^{\prime},\dot{q}^{\prime})\leq_{0}(p,\dot{q})
and
i<\mu
such that there is no
(p^{\prime\prime},\dot{q}^{\prime\prime})\leq(p^{\prime},\dot{q}^{\prime})
and

with

such that
(p^{\prime\prime},\dot{q}^{\prime\prime})\Vdash\im(\dot{F}\upharpoonright\check{i})\subseteq\check{x}
.

Let us first prove that this Theorem implies that
\kappa^{*}
does not become approachable after forcing with
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})*\dot{\Coll}(\check{\mu},\check{\kappa}^{*})
:

Theorem 4.12
.

Let

By Lemma

\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})*\dot{\Coll}(\check{\mu},\check{\kappa}^{*})

. Assume toward a contradiction that there is an
\mathbb{L}(\mu,(\kappa_{n},I_{n},B_{n})_{n\in\omega})*\dot{\Coll}(\check{\mu},\check{\kappa}^{*})

and a condition

on which

, there is

and

on the image of

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.11
.

(\mathbb{T}(\mu,\kappa),\leq)
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed.

(5)

(\mathbb{T}(\mu,\kappa),\leq)
is a
\mathsf{LIP}(\mu,\kappa)
-forcing.

Proof.

(1) follows from standard methods.

(2) follows from Lemma
2.13
.

(3) follows from the fact that
\mathbb{T}(\mu,\kappa)
preserves
\kappa
by the
\kappa
-cc and collapses every
\beta\in(\mu,\kappa)
to have size
\mu
: For every
\beta<\kappa
there is some
\alpha\in(\beta,\kappa)
such that
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for
(\dot{\Coll}(\check{\mu},\check{\alpha}),\dot{\leq},\dot{\leq})
. It follows easily that, in this case,
\mathbb{T}((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))
collapses
\alpha
to have size
\mu
.

(4) follows from Lemma
1.24
: By that Lemma (and by Lemma
4.9
), for every
\alpha<\kappa
,
\mathbb{T}((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed. This is clearly preserved by the product.

For (5), we only have to verify Definition
3.2
(3). Let
C\subseteq\kappa
consist of all
\alpha\in\kappa
such that
|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}|<\alpha
whenever
\beta<\alpha
.
C
is clearly club in
\kappa
. Whenever
\nu\in C
is inaccessible,
\mathbb{T}(\mu,\kappa)\cap V_{\nu}=\prod_{\alpha<\nu}\mathbb{T}((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\alpha},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,0}))
and the projection is just given by
p\mapsto p\upharpoonright\nu
. Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}
be weakly directed and suppose
p\in\mathbb{P}\cap V_{\nu}
is such that
p\leq q\upharpoonright\nu
for every
q\in X
. Simply let
r\in\mathbb{P}
be such that
r\upharpoonright\nu=p
and
r(i)
is a lower bound of
\{q(i)\;|\;q\in X\}
whenever
i\in(\nu,\kappa)
. Then
r
is easily seen to be as required.
∎

6.
The Main Iteration

In this section we will prove the main theorem. Let us first explain the idea behind the construction: The poset
\mathbb{A}(\mu,\kappa)
forces that for every singular cardinal
\delta>\kappa
of countable cofinality, there are stationarily many points in
\delta^{+}
of cofinality
\mu
which are not approachable. Given this poset, it makes sense to iterate the poset
\mathbb{A}
for an increasing sequence of supercompact cardinals. The main problem, of course, is showing that the stationarity of the set of non-approachable points of cofinality
\mu
is preserved. The solution to this problem consists of the following two ideas:

(1)

By Theorem
5.3
, after forcing with
\mathbb{A}(\mu,\kappa)
the stationarity of the set of non-approachable points of cofinality
\mu
in
\delta^{*}:=(\sup_{n}\delta_{n})^{+}
is witnessed by models for which we have
\mathbb{P}
-master conditions whenever
\mathbb{P}
is a
{<}\,\kappa
-directed closed forcing which preserves
\delta^{*}
, every
\delta_{n}
and forces
\mathsf{LIP}(\check{\mu},\check{\mu}^{+},\check{\delta}_{n})
for every
n\in\omega
.

(2)

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the part of the tail forcing which adds subsets of
\delta^{*}
. Then
\mathbb{P}
is a Prikry-type poset which has almost direct
\delta^{*}
-decidability and a
{<}\,\kappa
-directed closed direct extension ordering. Hence, if we can find a model
M
with a strong master condition
p
for
(\mathbb{P},\leq_{0})
, by almost direct
\delta^{*}
-decidability
p
forces
\sup(M\cap\delta^{*})
to be in
\dot{C}
whenever
\dot{C}\in M
is a
(\mathbb{P},\leq)
-name for a club in
\delta^{*}
.

So the main obstacle is showing that
(\mathbb{P},\leq_{0})
forces many instances of
\mathsf{LIP}
. However, in general working with the direct extension ordering on a Magidor iteration is complicated, so we work with the product of the termspace forcings instead (which suffices by the projection). To show that this forces
\mathsf{LIP}
, we need that our cardinal
\kappa
is indestructably supercompact. By work of Hamkins and Shelah (see
[
HS98
]
), the indestructibility of a supercompact cardinal is destroyed by any nontrivial small forcing. Because of this, we follow every
\mathbb{A}
-iterand with the poset making the next supercompact cardinal indestructible. The following lemma easily follows from well-known work of Laver (see
[
Lav78
]
) together with our iteration lemma for strong directed closure (see Lemma
1.23
):

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mu<\kappa
be cardinals such that
\mu
is regular and
\kappa
is supercompact. There is a poset
\mathbb{I}(\mu,\kappa)
such that

(1)

\mathbb{I}(\mu,\kappa)
is strongly
{<}\,\mu
-directed closed.

(2)

After forcing with
\mathbb{I}(\mu,\kappa)
,
\kappa
is supercompact and the supercompactness of
\kappa
is indestructible under strongly
{<}\,\kappa
-directed closed forcing.

Let us now define the iteration. Let
(\kappa_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\On}
be an increasing sequence of cardinals such that
\kappa_{0}:=\aleph_{1}
,
\kappa_{\alpha}
is supercompact whenever
\alpha
is a successor and
\kappa_{\alpha}=\sup_{\beta<\alpha}\kappa_{\beta}
whenever
\alpha
is a limit. For any ordinal
\alpha
, let
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
be the smallest regular cardinal above
\kappa_{\alpha}
, i.e.
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}=\kappa_{\alpha}
whenever
\alpha
is a successor or
\alpha
is a regular cardinal such that
\kappa_{\alpha}=\alpha
and
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}=\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
otherwise.

Now we can construct our iteration as follows:

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be the direct limit of the full-support Magidor iteration
((\mathbb{P}_{\beta},\leq_{\beta},\leq_{\beta,0}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\beta,0})_{\beta\in\On}
, where for each
\beta
,
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
-name for the Magidor iteration
(\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\beta}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\beta+1}),\dot{\leq},\dot{\leq})*(\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\beta},\check{\kappa}_{\beta+1}),\dot{\leq},\dot{\leq}_{0})
.

We will verify in a series of lemmas that we obtain the desired model after forcing with
\mathbb{P}
. We have to show the following:

(1)

The iteration is well-behaved so that “forcing with
\mathbb{P}
” actually leads to a model of
\mathsf{ZFC}
.

(2)

After forcing with
\mathbb{P}
, for any singular
\delta<\kappa
of countable cofinality and every regular
\mu<\delta
, there are stationarily many
\gamma\in\delta^{+}\cap\cof(\mu)
which are not approachable.

Of particular importance is the behavior of the tail forcings. For
\alpha<\beta
, let
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\beta),0})
be a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-name for a poset such that
(\mathbb{P}_{\beta},\leq_{\beta},\leq_{\beta,0})\cong(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha},\leq_{\alpha,0})*(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\beta),0})
. We have the following:

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
\alpha<\beta
,
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\beta),0})
is forced to be a Prikry-type forcing such that
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\beta),0})
is strongly
{<}\,\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed.

Proof.

This is clear, as
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\beta},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\beta),0})
is forced to be a full-support Magidor iteration of Prikry-type forcings starting with
\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})
. Therefore, any iterand is forced to be strongly
{<}\,\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed, so we can apply Lemma
2.4
.
∎

In particular, for any
\alpha<\beta
, forcing with
\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{G}
(where
G
is
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-generic) does not add any new bounded subsets of
\kappa_{\alpha}
or
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
, so the construction stabilizes and the class forcing extension satisfies
\mathsf{ZFC}
. Abusing language, we will refer to this as a forcing extension “by
\mathbb{P}
”, ignoring that
\mathbb{P}
is a class and not a set.

Additionally, we show that the direct extension ordering on the tail forcing can decide “enough”:

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\alpha<\gamma
be ordinals such that
\gamma
is a limit with countable cofinality. Let
G
be
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-generic and
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma}:=\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G}
. Then
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{(\alpha,\gamma),0})
has almost direct
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
-decidability.

Proof.

This is clear by Lemma
2.12
: For any
\beta<\gamma
,
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\beta}
forces that
(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\beta},\dot{\leq}_{\beta,0})
has size
{<}\,\check{\kappa}_{\gamma}^{+}
and thus the
(\check{\kappa}_{\gamma}^{+},\check{\kappa}_{\gamma}^{+})
-covering property. By Lemma
2.12
,
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{(\alpha,\gamma),0})
has the direct
(\kappa_{\gamma}^{+},\kappa_{\gamma}^{+})
-covering property as well. In particular, since
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
is regular,
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{(\alpha,\gamma),0})
has almost direct
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
-decidability.
∎

We now analyze the cardinal structure of the forcing extension by
\mathbb{P}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

After forcing with
\mathbb{P}
, the class of infinite cardinals is given by
\{\aleph_{0}\}\cup\{\kappa_{\alpha},\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}\;|\;\alpha\in\On\}
.

Proof.

First assume that
\alpha\in\On
is a successor ordinal,
\alpha=\beta+1
. Then
\kappa_{\alpha}>\kappa_{\beta}
is supercompact. It follows that
|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}|<\kappa_{\alpha}
and so
\kappa_{\alpha}
is supercompact after forcing with
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
. After forcing with
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}=\mathbb{P}_{\beta}*\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\beta}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\beta+1})*\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\beta}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha})
,
\kappa_{\alpha}
is preserved and every cardinal below
\kappa_{\beta}^{*}
and
\kappa_{\alpha}
is collapsed by Lemma
5.2
(1) and (4), so
\kappa_{\alpha}
becomes
(\kappa_{\beta}^{*})^{+}
. Lastly, for every
\gamma>\alpha
,
\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\gamma}
is forced to be a Prikry-type forcing such that
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\gamma},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\gamma),0})
is strongly
{<}\,\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}
-directed closed. In particular,
\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\gamma}
is forced to preserve that
\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}
is a cardinal.

If
\alpha\in\On
is a limit, then
\kappa_{\alpha}
is preserved since cofinally many
\nu<\kappa_{\alpha}
(namely, all
\kappa_{\beta}
,
\beta<\alpha
) are preserved. As for
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
(in case
\alpha
is singular or
\kappa_{\alpha}\neq\alpha
): Assume first that
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
collapses
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
. Since
\kappa_{\alpha}
is singular (if
\alpha
is singular, then clearly
\kappa_{\alpha}
is singular, otherwise
\kappa_{\alpha}
is singular because
\cf(\kappa_{\alpha})=\alpha<\kappa_{\alpha}
), it follows that there is
\nu<\kappa_{\alpha}
such that
\cf(\kappa_{\alpha}^{+})=\nu
after forcing with
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
. There is
\beta<\alpha
such that
\kappa_{\beta}>\nu
and
\kappa_{\beta}>\cf(\kappa_{\alpha})
.

Let
G_{\beta}
be
\mathbb{P}_{\beta}
-generic. We want to show that
\cf(\kappa_{\alpha}^{+})=\nu
in
V[G_{\beta}]
, obtaining a contradiction from the fact that
|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}|<\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{\beta,\alpha}:=\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\beta,\alpha}^{G_{\beta}}
. By Lemma
6.4
,
\mathbb{P}_{\beta,\alpha}
has almost direct
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
-decidability. It follows that every function from
\nu
to
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
added by
\mathbb{P}_{\beta,\alpha}
is bounded pointwise by a function from
\nu
to
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
in
V[G_{\beta}]
(as in Lemma
4.10
). In particular
\cf(\kappa_{\alpha}^{+})=\nu
in
V[G_{\beta}]
. As mentioned before, this presents a contradiction, as
|\mathbb{P}_{\beta}|<\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
.

So we have shown that all
\kappa_{\alpha}
and
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
remain cardinals. Clearly, whenever
\mu
does not have this form,
\mu
is collapsed. So the above set contains precisely all cardinals in the forcing extension.
∎

It follows that the singular cardinals of the forcing extension are precisely
\kappa_{\alpha}
for
\alpha
a limit and thus that in the extension the successors of singular cardinals of countably cofinality are precisely
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
where
\cf(\kappa_{\alpha})=\omega
(this occurs if and only if
\cf(\alpha)=\omega
since there is always an unbounded and increasing function from
\alpha
into
\kappa_{\alpha}
).

We want to show that
\mathbb{P}
gives us exactly the model we need. The proof idea is as follows: The regular cardinals of the forcing extension are precisely the
\kappa_{\alpha}
for
\alpha
a successor and
\kappa_{\alpha}^{+}
for
\alpha
a limit. For any such cardinal,
\mathbb{A}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
forces the existence of stationarily many non-approachable points of cofinality
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
in any successor of a singular cardinal
>\kappa_{\alpha+1}
of countable cofinality. Then all that is left is to show that the tail of the iteration does not destroy that stationarity (since it cannot make points of cofinality
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
approachable due to its closure). For this we use that we have used a guessing function and thus added many partially generic filters to many strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}
-directed closed forcings (namely, those forcings which force enough instances of
\mathsf{LIP}
). We now show that the direct extension ordering on the tail of the iteration can be projected onto from one of those suitable forcings.

Definition 6.6
.

Let
\alpha<\beta
be ordinals. Let
G_{\alpha+1/2}=G_{\alpha}*H_{\alpha}
be
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}*\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})
-generic and work in
V[G_{\alpha+1/2}]
(replacing every
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma}
for
\gamma\geq\alpha
by the corresponding
\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha+1/2}}
-name). Define

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}:=\left(\mathbb{T}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})\times\prod_{\gamma\in(\alpha,\beta)}\mathbb{T}((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha+1/2}},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha+1/2}}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma},\dot{\leq}_{\gamma,0}))\right)

where
\mathbb{T}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
is as in Definition
5.4
.
Working in
V[G_{\alpha}]
, let
\dot{\mathbb{T}}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}
be an
\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})
-name for
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}
and let

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{T}}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}

We use this mixture of a product and an iteration because otherwise the remainder of the termspace forcing would not be directed-closed in the model
after
making the supercompactness of
\kappa_{\alpha+1}
indestructible under
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha+1}
-directed closed forcings.

We now show that
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
is indeed one of those suitable forcings which were incorporated into the definition to construct
\mathbb{A}(\mu,\kappa)
:

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\alpha<\beta
be ordinals. Let
G_{\alpha}
be
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}
-generic and work in
V[G_{\alpha}]
(replacing every
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma}
for
\gamma\geq\alpha
by the corresponding
\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha}}
-name).

(1)

There is a projection from
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
onto
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{G_{\alpha}},\dot{\leq}_{(\alpha,\beta),0}^{G_{\alpha}})
.

(2)

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
is strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed.

(3)

For every
\gamma\geq\alpha+1
,
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
preserves
\kappa_{\gamma}
and forces
\mathsf{LIP}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*})^{+},\kappa_{\gamma})
.

Proof.

For (1), by Lemma
5.5
, in
V[G_{\alpha}]
,
\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
forces that there is a projection from
\dot{\mathbb{T}}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}
onto

\mathbb{T}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})\times\prod_{\gamma\in(\alpha,\beta)}\mathbb{T}((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha+1/2}},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha+1/2}}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma},\dot{\leq}_{\gamma,0}))

By Lemma
2.13
, it is forced that this forcing projects onto
\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha+1,\beta}
.

This clearly implies the existence of a projection from
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{T}}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}
onto
\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha+1,\beta}=\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\beta}
.

(2) is also clear: For any
\gamma\in[\alpha,\beta)
,
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma},\leq_{\alpha,\gamma})
forces that
(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma},\dot{\leq}_{\gamma,0})
is strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed. Thus, the termspace forcing has the same degree of closure by Lemma
1.24
. It follows that
\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
forces that
\dot{\mathbb{T}}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}
is strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed. Since
\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
is strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed, the iteration (which is equal to
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha,\beta}
) is strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
-directed closed.

We now prove (3). We will show that
\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
forces that
\dot{\mathbb{T}}_{\alpha+1/2,\beta}
forces
\mathsf{LIP}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*})^{+},\check{\kappa}_{\gamma})
for every
\gamma\geq\alpha+1
, which clearly suffices. So let
H_{\alpha}
be
\mathbb{I}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
-generic over
V[G_{\alpha}]
. In
V[G_{\alpha}*H_{\alpha}]
, the supercompactness of
\kappa_{\alpha+1}
is preserved by any strongly
{<}\,\kappa_{\alpha+1}
-directed closed forcing. In particular, it is preserved by
\mathbb{T}:=\prod_{\gamma\in(\alpha,\beta)}\mathbb{T}((\mathbb{P}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha}*H_{\alpha}},\dot{\leq}_{\alpha,\gamma}^{G_{\alpha}*H_{\alpha}}),(\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_{\gamma},\dot{\leq}_{\gamma,0}))
. That forcing also preserves the fact that
\mathbb{T}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
is a
\mathsf{LIP}
-forcing (which it is by Lemma
5.5
(5)). Ergo,
\mathbb{T}
followed by
\mathbb{T}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},\kappa_{\alpha+1})
forces
\mathsf{LIP}(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*},(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*})^{+},\kappa_{\gamma})
for every
\gamma\geq\alpha+1
, since it collapses
\kappa_{\alpha+1}
to become
(\kappa_{\alpha}^{*})^{+}
. The preservation of each
\kappa_{\gamma}
follows just as in Lemma
6.5
.
∎

Now we can finally prove our main theorem:

Theorem 6.8
.

After forcing with
\mathbb{P}
,
\mathsf{GCH}
holds and whenever
\delta
is singular of countable cofinality and
\mu<\delta
is regular uncountable, there are stationarily many
\gamma<\delta^{+}
of cofinality
\mu
which are not approachable.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.5
, it suffices to show that whenever
\gamma
is an ordinal with countable cofinality and
\alpha<\gamma
, then after forcing with
\mathbb{P}
there are stationarily many
\beta\in\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
of cofinality
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
which are not approachable (since the class of regular cardinals consists precisely of the
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
). So fix, in the ground model, a normal coloring
d\colon[\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}]^{2}\to\omega
(it suffices to consider only one specific coloring by Fact
1.4
)

Since for any
\gamma^{\prime}>\gamma
,
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}},\dot{\leq}_{(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}),0})
is forced to be
{<}\,\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
-closed (and to have direct
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
-decidability by Lemma
2.10
),
(\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}},\dot{\leq}_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}})
is forced to preserve stationary subsets of
\check{\kappa}_{\gamma}^{+}
(by the usual arguments). Therefore it suffices to show that the statement holds after forcing with
\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}
. We write

\mathbb{P}_{\gamma}\cong\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}*\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}

Let
G
be
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha}*\dot{\mathbb{I}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})*\dot{\mathbb{A}}(\check{\kappa}_{\alpha}^{*},\check{\kappa}_{\alpha+1})
-generic and work in
V[G]
. Assume that
\dot{C}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}:=\dot{\mathbb{P}}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}^{G}
-name
\dot{C}
for a club in
\check{\kappa}_{\gamma}^{+}
, forced by some condition
p\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}
. Without loss of generality, assume
p=1_{\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}}
(otherwise, work with
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}\upharpoonright p
instead). Let
\sigma\colon\mathbb{T}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}\to(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma},\leq_{(\alpha+1,\gamma),0})
be the projection obtained from Lemma
6.7
(1).

By Theorem
5.3
and Lemma
6.7
(2) and (3), let
M\prec H^{V[G]}(\Theta)
with
|M|<\kappa_{\alpha+1}
be such that
M
contains all relevant parameters,
\dot{C}\in M
,
\sup(M\cap\kappa_{\gamma}^{+})
is not
d
-approachable and has cofinality
\kappa_{\alpha}^{*}
and there is a condition
p\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}
such that whenever
D\in M
is open dense in
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}
, there is
q\in D\cap M
with
p\leq q
. We claim that
\sigma(p)\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}
forces (over the non-direct ordering
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma},\leq_{\alpha+1,\gamma})
) that
\sup(M\cap\kappa_{\gamma}^{+})\in\dot{C}
. Since clearly
\sup(M\cap\kappa_{\gamma}^{+})
does not become
d
-approachable by the distributivity of
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}
, this suffices.

Let
\beta<\sup(M\cap\kappa_{\gamma}^{+})
, i.e.
\beta<\beta^{\prime}
for some
\beta^{\prime}\in M\cap\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
. Since
\dot{C}
is forced to be unbounded in
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
,
\Vdash\exists\xi(\xi\in\dot{C}\wedge\beta^{\prime}<\xi)
. Since
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma},\leq_{\alpha+1,\gamma},\leq_{(\alpha+1,\gamma),0})
has almost direct
\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
-decidability by Lemma
6.4
, the set
D
of all
q\in\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma}
such that for some
\zeta\in\kappa_{\gamma}^{+}
,
q\Vdash\exists\xi<\check{\zeta}(\xi\in\dot{C}\wedge\beta^{\prime}<\xi)
, is open dense in
(\mathbb{P}_{\alpha+1,\gamma},\leq_{(\alpha+1,\gamma),0})
. Hence

Robust Quantum Algorithmic Binary Decision-Making on Displacement Signals
Aishwarya Majumdar, Yuan Liu

Abstract
A relevant signal in the quantum domain may manifest as a displacement or a phase shift operator in the bosonic phase space. For a real parameter β \beta embedded in such a displacement operator, the task of determining if β ∈ [ β − t ​ h , β + t ​ h ] \beta\in[\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th}] for real asymmetric thresholds ( β − t ​ h ≠ − β + t ​ h \beta_{-th}\neq-\beta_{+th} ) is a binary decision problem. We propose a framework based on generalized quantum signal processing interferometry (GQSPI) on hybrid qubit-bosonic oscillator systems that addresses this parameter detection problem by recasting the practical task of active binary hypothesis testing on quantum systems to that of a polynomial approximation. We achieve a small decision error probability p e ​ r ​ r p_{err} on the order of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 d ​ log ⁡ ( ( d ) ) ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{d}\log{(d)}) , with d d as the circuit depth. We analyze the protocol when (i) β \beta is a deterministic parameter, and (ii) when β \beta is drawn randomly from a known prior distribution. The performance of the sensing protocol under dephasing noise is also shown to be robust. We further extend our protocol from two thresholds to more general multi-threshold cases as well. Overall, the proposed framework enables decision-making over arbitrary thresholds for any general displacement signal in a single or a few shots.

\hat{x}

II-B

Tools

Of the several advances in quantum algorithms in the past decade, Quantum Signal Processing (QSP)
[
25
,
24
,
23

III-A

Main Problem

The signal is assumed to interact with the qubit-bosonic oscillator system such that it effectively applies a position change of
\beta
to the oscillator state via the operator
S_{\beta}
. Such an operator, also known as displacement signal, in the joint qubit-oscillator space is given by,

\displaystyle S_{\beta}=I\otimes e^{i\beta\hat{p}}=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\beta\hat{p}}&0\\
0&e^{i\beta\hat{p}}\end{bmatrix},\quad\beta\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

With the goal to determine whether the value of
\beta
lies within the range
[\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th}]
,
the GQSPI protocol prepares the qubit–oscillator system so as to minimize the overall binary decision error. The main problem we solve in this work is:

Given thresholds
\beta_{-th}
,
\beta_{+th}\in\mathbb{R}
, determine whether the parameter
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, embedded in a displacement operator
S_{\beta}

in the qubit-oscillator space,
lies within the range
[\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th}]
or outside of it, with a small decision error probability
p_{err}
, using a quantum circuit constructed using qubit rotation gates and qubit-oscillator entangling gates.

We assume that the qubit is in ground state
\ket{\downarrow}
, and the oscillator is in the vacuum state
\ket{0}_{osc}
at the beginning of the protocol. Following the GQSPI protocol, the qubit is measured along the Pauli-Z basis, where the measurement outcome
\downarrow
signifies that the parameter
\beta
is in the range
[\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th}]
, while measuring
\uparrow
implies that
\beta
does not belong to this range. We denote
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
as the probability of measuring
\downarrow
on the qubit for a given value of
\beta
.

III-B

GQSP Theorem for Qubit-Oscillator Systems

Consider the following single-qubit rotation parametrized by angles
\{\theta,\phi,\lambda\}

\displaystyle R(\theta,\phi,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i(\lambda+\phi)}\cos{\theta}&e^{i\phi}\sin{\theta}\\
e^{i\lambda}\sin{\theta}&-\cos{\theta}\end{bmatrix}

(2)

and an entangling operator between the qubit and the oscillator

\displaystyle W_{z}(\hat{\omega})=\begin{bmatrix}\hat{\omega}(\hat{x},\hat{p})&0\\
0&\hat{\omega}^{-1}(\hat{x},\hat{p})\end{bmatrix},

which applies operator
\hat{\omega}(\hat{x},\hat{p})
to the oscillator state if the qubit state is
\ket{\downarrow}
and
\hat{\omega}^{-1}(\hat{x},\hat{p})
if the qubit state is
\ket{\uparrow}
. As a building block, we define
generalized quantum signal processing
(GQSP) for hybrid qubit-oscillator systems as follows:

Theorem 1
.

A quantum circuit on hybrid qubit-oscillator system parameterized by the set of angles
\vec{\theta}=\{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{d}\},~\vec{\phi}=\{\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\cdots\phi_{d}\}
, and
\lambda_{0}
realizes a block-encoding of a degree-
d
complex Laurent polynomial transformation
P(\hat{\omega})
on an oscillator operator
\hat{\omega}(\hat{x},\hat{p})
using the following sequence given by
G_{\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{\omega})
:

\displaystyle G_{\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{\omega})

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(R(\theta_{i},\phi_{i},0)\otimes I_{osc})W_{z}(\hat{\omega})\right)(R(\theta_{0},\phi_{0},\lambda_{0})\otimes I_{osc}),

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}P_{d}(\hat{\omega})&-Q^{\dagger}_{d}(\hat{\omega})\\
Q_{d}(\hat{\omega})&P^{\dagger}_{d}(\hat{\omega})\end{bmatrix},

(3)

such that,

•

P_{d}(\hat{\omega})
and
Q_{d}(\hat{\omega})
are both degree-
d
polynomials in
\hat{\omega}
,

•

|P_{d}(\hat{\omega})|^{2}+|Q_{d}(\hat{\omega})|^{2}=1~\forall~\hat{\omega}
,

•

P_{d}(\hat{\omega})
and
Q_{d}(\hat{\omega})
are of the form,

P_{d}(\hat{\omega})=\sum_{n=-d}^{d}p_{n}\hat{\omega}^{n},\quad Q_{d}(\hat{\omega})=\sum_{n=-d}^{d}q_{n}\hat{\omega}^{n}
where

n=\{-d,-d+2,\cdots,d-2,d\}
, and
p_{n},q_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Similar form of GQSP for hybrid qubit-bosonic oscillator systems was also independently obtained by
[
17
]
.
The nature of polynomials and relationships between polynomial coefficients can be found in Appendix
A
.

III-C

Generalized Quantum Signal Processing Interferometry

Fig. 1:
Quantum circuit for Generalized Quantum Signal Processing Interferometry (GQSPI). Note that for the qubit rotation gate in Eq. (
2
),
R^{\dagger}(\theta,\phi,\lambda)=R(\theta,-\lambda,-\phi)
.

Let the qubit-oscillator entangling operator
W_{z}(\hat{\omega})
be a conditional displacement gate on the oscillator, conditioned on the qubit state, given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{c}(\alpha)=e^{(\alpha a^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}a)\sigma_{z}}

(4)

where
a
and
a^{\dagger}
are the bosonic annihilation and creation operators on the oscillator as noted in Sec.
II-A
.
In particular, for
\alpha=i\kappa/\sqrt{2}
where
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, the
\mathcal{D}_{c}(\alpha)
operator imparts a momentum kick to the oscillator by
+\kappa
if the qubit state is
\ket{\downarrow}
and
-\kappa
if the qubit state is
\ket{\uparrow}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})=e^{i\kappa\hat{x}\hat{\sigma}_{z}}=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}&0\\
0&e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}\end{bmatrix}.

(5)

It can be observed that the
\mathcal{D}_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})
gate is periodic in the position eigen state
\ket{x}_{osc}
with periodicity
T_{\mathcal{D}}=\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}
in the bosonic phase-space.

For the interferometry protocol, we sandwich the displacement signal containing operator
S_{\beta}
between a degree-
d
GQSP sequence and its inverse sequence,

\displaystyle U(\beta,\kappa)

\displaystyle=G^{-1}_{d,\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{x})~S_{\beta}~G_{d,\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{x})

(6)

where the GQSP sequence consists of qubit rotation gates
R(\theta,\phi,\lambda)
and qubit-oscillator entangling gate
\mathcal{D}_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})
as follows:

\displaystyle G_{d,\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\left[(R(\theta_{d},\phi_{d},0)\otimes I_{osc})D_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})\right]

\displaystyle\cdots\times\left[(R(\theta_{1},\phi_{1},0)\otimes I_{osc})D_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})\right]

\displaystyle\times(R(\theta_{0},\phi_{0},\lambda_{0})\otimes I_{osc})

(7)

From the hybrid GQSP Theorem
1
,
G_{\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{x})
can be expressed as,

\displaystyle G_{\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}P_{d}(\hat{x})&-Q^{\dagger}_{d}(\hat{x})\\
Q_{d}(\hat{x})&P^{\dagger}_{d}(\hat{x})\end{bmatrix}

where,
P_{d}(\hat{x})=\sum_{n=-d}^{d}p_{n}e^{in\kappa\hat{x}}
and
Q_{d}(\hat{x})=\sum_{n=-d}^{d}q_{n}e^{in\kappa\hat{x}}
with
p_{n},q_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.

By choosing appropriate phase angles
\vec{\theta}=\{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{d}\},~\vec{\phi}=\{\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\cdots\phi_{d}\}
, and
\lambda_{0}
, the probability of measuring the qubit in state
\ket{\downarrow}
for given
\beta
, denoted by
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
can be made close to 1 for
\beta\in[\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th}]
and close to 0 when
\beta
is outside this range.

The probability of measuring
\ket{\downarrow}
for a given
\beta
is obtained to be,

\displaystyle P(M=\downarrow|\beta)

\displaystyle=\langle 0|_{osc}U^{\dagger}(\beta,\kappa)_{00}U(\beta,\kappa)_{00}|0\rangle_{osc}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=-d}^{d}c_{s}e^{i(2\kappa)\beta s}

(8)

\displaystyle\text{with,}\quad c_{s}=\sum_{n,m,r=-d}^{d}\left(p_{n}p^{*}_{m}+q_{n}q^{*}_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(p^{*}_{n+2s}p_{m+2r}+q^{*}_{n+2s}q_{m+2r}\right)e^{-\kappa^{2}(r-s)^{2}}

(9)

\displaystyle\text{where }s,r,m,n=\{-d,-d+2,\cdots,d-2,d\}.

U(\beta,\kappa)_{00}
in Eq. (
8
) denotes the top-left element of the operator
U(\beta,\kappa)
which can be expressed as,

\displaystyle U(\beta,\kappa)_{00}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m=-d}^{d}\left(p^{*}_{n}p_{m}+q^{*}_{n}q_{m}\right)e^{-in\kappa\hat{x}}e^{i\beta\hat{p}}e^{im\kappa\hat{x}}

(10)

It is interesting to note that
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
is a polynomial in
e^{i(2\kappa)\beta}
, and has a periodicity of
T_{P}=\frac{\pi}{\kappa}
. Therefore, it is necessary to choose
\kappa
appropriately such that
\beta\in\left(-\frac{\pi}{2\kappa},\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}\right)
so as to cover the entire range of
\beta
.

P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
can be an asymmetric (
c_{s}\neq c_{-s}
) or symmetric (
c_{s}=c_{-s}
) function of
\beta
depending on the choice of phase angles, unlike in QSPI
[
40
]
where
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
is always a symmetric function, thus limiting its usefulness for asymmetrically distributed parameter detection.

In the absence of any displacement signal,
\beta=0
, thus no displacement is applied to the oscillator, and the GQSP and its inverse sequence cancel out, implying no change in the initial qubit state. This implies that
\sum_{s=-d}^{d}c_{s}=1
for
\beta=0
. For cases where
0\notin[\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th}]
,
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)=1
at
\beta=0
can be treated as a point of exception.

III-D

Error Scaling and Circuit Depth

Fig. 2:
Error analysis of the GQSPI protocol.

Now that the qubit measurement probability response function has been expressed as polynomial in Eq. (
8
), the error density of the protocol can be viewed as the error in approximating the ideal function,

\displaystyle P_{ideal}=\begin{cases}1,\quad\beta_{-th}\leq\beta\leq\beta_{+th}\\
0,\quad\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

(11)

The probability of decision error density
p_{err}(\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th},\kappa)
of the protocol can be expressed as,

\displaystyle p_{err}(\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th},\kappa)=\frac{\kappa}{\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}}^{\frac{\pi}{2\kappa}}|P_{ideal}-P(M=\downarrow|\beta)|d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{(\beta_{+th}-\beta_{-th})\kappa}{\pi}+\sum_{s=-d}^{d}c_{s}H_{s},

(12)

\displaystyle\text{where,}~H_{s}=\text{sinc}(\pi s)+i\frac{e^{i(2\kappa\beta_{+th})s}-e^{i(2\kappa\beta_{-th})s}}{\pi s}

(13)

As shown in Fig.
2
, assume that the error tolerance of the protocol is
\epsilon
everywhere expect for
\beta\in[\beta_{-th}-\frac{\sigma}{2},\beta_{-th}+\frac{\sigma}{2}]\cup[\beta_{+th}-\frac{\sigma}{2},\beta_{+th}+\frac{\sigma}{2}]
which are considered as the transition regions of width
\sigma
around each of the thresholds.
Then the total probability of decision error denoted by
p_{err}
can be approximately expressed as,

\displaystyle p_{err}\approx\epsilon\left(\frac{\pi}{\kappa}-2\sigma\right)+\frac{\sigma}{2}

(14)

where we assume that
\epsilon
and
\sigma
are of the same order of magnitude. Using the polynomial approximation of a rectangular function result obtained in
[
22
,
40
]
, the degree of the polynomial
d
is related to
p_{err}
as,

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle\propto\frac{1}{\kappa p_{err}}\log{(\kappa p_{err})}\quad\text{or equivalently,}\quad p_{err}\propto\frac{1}{\kappa d}\log{(d)}.

(15)

This brings us to concluding the following theorem:

Theorem 2
.

An interferometry protocol obtained by applying a degree-
d
hybrid-GQSP before and after the displacement signal
S_{\beta}
acts on an oscillator, realizes the probability of measuring the qubit in ground state
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)=\sum_{s=-d}^{d}c_{s}e^{i(2\kappa)\beta s},\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, achieving arbitrary polynomials w.r.t.
e^{i2\kappa\beta}
, thus improving over QSPI
[
40
]
which can only realize symmetric (even) functions. Such polynomial transformation produces a desirable interference on the quantum state of the system suitable for binary decision-making with probability of decision error
p_{err}\propto\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\kappa d}\log{d})
.

Numerical optimization and analytical methods established in several of the QSP angle finding techniques
[
3
,
11
,
19
]
can be used to obtain
\vec{\theta}=\{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{d}\},~\vec{\phi}=\{\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\cdots\phi_{d}\}
, and
\lambda_{0}
that create the optimal detection state on the qubit-oscillator system.

The detailed treatment of the GQSPI protocol is presented in Appendix
B
with derivations for
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
and
p_{err}(\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th},\kappa)
.

III-E

Displacement Signal Detection for Stochastic Parameter

Oftentimes the parameter of interest is a random variable. Let
\beta
follow a probability density function
f_{\beta}(\mu_{\beta},\sigma^{2}_{\beta})
with mean
\mu_{\beta}
, and variance
\sigma^{2}_{\beta}
. In this case, the decision error density transforms into an expectation value

\displaystyle p_{err}(\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th},\kappa,\sigma_{\beta},\mu_{\beta})=\frac{\kappa}{\pi}\mathbb{E}\left[|P_{ideal}-P(M=\downarrow|\beta)|\right]

\displaystyle\text{where, }\mathbb{E}[P(M=\downarrow|\beta)]=\sum_{s=-d}^{d}c_{s}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i(2\kappa)\beta s}f_{\beta}(\mu_{\beta},\sigma^{2}_{\beta})d\beta\right]

(16)

Further, when actually using the GQSPI protocol, since the only change required is to adjust the decision error density
p_{err}(\beta_{-th},\beta_{+th},\kappa)
, therefore, the optimization algorithm minimizing the decision error density can accommodate the prior information of
\beta
to realize an appropriate polynomial transformation.
The particular case of
\beta
following the Gaussian probability density is presented in Appendix
C-I
. The current treatment can also be recast for deterministic
\beta
buried in classical random noise with a known distribution.

III-F

GQSPI with Dephasing Noise on the Oscillator

Similar to qubit decoherence errors, the oscillator also suffers from dephasing, such that a dephasing error occurs during the application of each conditional displacement gate, given by the dephasing operator
R_{osc}(\gamma)=e^{-i\gamma\hat{n}}
. The parameter
\gamma
could be a random variable drawn from a known distribution, but is usually a fixed value as a coherent error during the duration of the protocol (for shorter circuits).
This will change
\mathcal{D}_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})
into a noisy version:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{c}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=R_{osc}^{\dagger}(\gamma)\mathcal{D}_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})R_{osc}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\kappa(\hat{x}\cos\gamma+\hat{p}\sin\gamma)}&0\\
0&e^{-i\kappa(\hat{x}\cos\gamma+\hat{p}\sin\gamma)}\end{bmatrix}

(17)

Then the GQSPI sequence with dephasing error occurring for each iteration is of the form,

\displaystyle G_{\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(R(\theta_{i},\phi_{i},0)\otimes I_{osc})\mathcal{D}_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})R_{osc}(\gamma_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\times(R(\theta_{0},\phi_{0},\lambda_{0})\otimes I_{osc})

\displaystyle=R_{osc}(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\gamma_{i})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{d}(R(\theta_{i},\phi_{i},0)\otimes I_{osc})\mathcal{D}_{c}(\alpha^{\prime}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\times(R(\theta_{0},\phi_{0},\lambda_{0})\otimes I_{osc})

(18)

where,

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{i\kappa\cos\Gamma_{i}+\kappa\sin\Gamma_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i(\frac{\pi}{2}-\Gamma_{i})},\quad\Gamma_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\gamma_{j}

Thus, the dephasing noise rotates each of the oscillator displacements conditioned on the qubit by a varying amount, accumulating all the random rotation angles from the previous iterations.
For such a GQSP set up, the general form of the polynomial

P_{d}(\vec{\gamma})
, where
\vec{\gamma}=\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\cdots,\gamma_{d}\}
, can be expressed as,

\displaystyle P_{d}(\vec{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textbf{s}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}p_{\textbf{s}}\mathcal{D}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}s_{k}\alpha^{\prime}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\times\exp{
\left(
-i\frac{\kappa^2}{2}
\sum_{1 \leq l < k \leq d } s_k s_l
\sin(\Gamma_k - \Gamma_l)
\right)},

(19)

where
s_{k}\in\{-1,1\}~\forall~k~\in\{1,2,\cdots,d\}
, and
p_{\textbf{s}}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Q_{d}(\vec{\gamma})
is also of similar form.
Thus, the polynomial
P
can be viewed as a linear combination of rotated oscillator displacement operators with varying magnitude and direction.

The overall unitary acting on the qubit-oscillator system taking into account the dephasing noise for the entire interferometry protocol is given by,

\displaystyle U(\beta,\kappa,\vec{\gamma})=G^{-1}_{d,\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\vec{\gamma})~S_{\beta}~G_{d,\vec{\theta},\vec{\phi},\lambda_{0}}(\vec{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}P^{\dagger}_{d}(\vec{\gamma})&Q^{\dagger}_{d}(\vec{\gamma})\\
*&*\end{bmatrix}I\otimes\mathcal{D}\left(-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2}}e^{i\Gamma_{d}}\right)\begin{bmatrix}P_{d}(\vec{\gamma})&*\\
Q_{d}(\vec{\gamma})&*\end{bmatrix}

(20)

The
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
obtained for the dephasing noise scenario is of the form,

\displaystyle P(M=\downarrow|\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\textbf{s},\textbf{s'},\textbf{r},\textbf{r'}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}}(p^{*}_{\textbf{s}}p_{\textbf{s'}}+q^{*}_{\textbf{s}}q_{\textbf{s'}})(p_{\textbf{r}}p^{*}_{\textbf{r'}}+q_{\textbf{r}}q^{*}_{\textbf{r'}})

\displaystyle\times e^{i\Theta_{\textbf{s},\textbf{s'},\textbf{r},\textbf{r'}}}e^{-|\alpha_{\textbf{s},\textbf{s'},\textbf{r},\textbf{r'}}|^{2}/2}e^{i(\kappa\beta)\Lambda_{\textbf{s},\textbf{r}}}

where,
e^{i\Theta_{\textbf{s},\textbf{s'},\textbf{r},\textbf{r'}}}
and
e^{-|\alpha_{\textbf{s},\textbf{s'},\textbf{r},\textbf{r'}}|^{2}/2}
are overall phase and amplitude factors, respectively, obtained from composing the scrambled conditional displacement gates, and
e^{i\kappa\beta\Lambda_{\textbf{s},\textbf{r}}}
is comparable to the factor
e^{i2\kappa\beta s}
obtained previously in Eq. (
8
), and (
9
). Detailed expressions of the parameters can be found in Appendix
D
.

It can be shown that for small dephasing rates
\gamma
(same
\gamma
in every iteration),
P(M=\downarrow|\beta)
has an overall rescaling factor of

IV
Conclusions

The ability to actively detect a general quantum displacement signal over a single or multiple threshold bands in the presence of classical and quantum noise is a challenging practical problem. We address these aspects of quantum detection problem using the proposed GQSPI framework that is capable of systematically improving the decision accuracy while being robust against quantum dephasing noise. By viewing the displacement signal detection problem from a polynomial approximation perspective, we uncover deeper connections between quantum detection theory and quantum algorithms, thereby opening an exciting avenue for binary decision problems in the quantum domain.

A-I
Nature of Polynomials

Assume
\hat{\omega}=e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}
for simplicity of calculation.

For GQSP of degree 1, the polynomials obtained by simply multiplying one iteration of the qubit rotation gates and the conditional displacement gate results in the following polynomials,

\displaystyle P_{1}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=e^{i(\phi_{1}+\lambda_{0})}\left(e^{i\phi_{0}}\cos{\theta_{1}}\cos{\theta_0}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}+\sin{\theta_{1}}\sin{\theta_0}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle Q_{1}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=e^{i\lambda_{0}}\left(e^{i\phi_{0}}\sin{\theta_{1}}\cos{\theta_0}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}-\cos{\theta_{1}}\sin{\theta_0}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}\right)

It can be observed that the coefficients of the polynomial can be complex or real, depending on the choice of
\vec{\theta}=\{\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\cdots\theta_{d}\},~\vec{\phi}=\{\phi_{0},\phi_{1}\cdots\phi_{d}\}
, and
\lambda_{0}

Going from degree

\displaystyle(R(\theta_{i},\phi_{i},0)\otimes I_{osc})D_{c}(i\kappa/\sqrt{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\phi_{d+1}}\cos{\theta_{d+1}}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}&e^{i\phi_{d+1}}\sin{\theta_{d+1}}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}\\
\sin{\theta_{d+1}}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}&-\cos{\theta_{d+1}}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}\end{bmatrix}

Thus the recursive relationship between the polynomials are:

\displaystyle P_{d+1}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=e^{i\phi_{d+1}}\cos{\theta_{d+1}}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}P_{d}(\hat{x})+e^{i\phi_{d+1}}\sin{\theta_{d+1}}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}Q_{d}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle Q_{d+1}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=\sin{\theta_{d+1}}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}P_{d}(\hat{x})-\cos{\theta_{d+1}}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}Q_{d}(\hat{x})

(25)

Combining the Eq. (

\displaystyle P_{1}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=p_{1}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}+p_{-1}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}

\displaystyle P_{2}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=p_{-2}e^{-i2\kappa\hat{x}}+p_{2}e^{i2\kappa\hat{x}}+p_{0}

\displaystyle P_{3}(\hat{x})

\displaystyle=p_{3}e^{i3\kappa\hat{x}}+p_{-3}e^{-i3\kappa\hat{x}}+p_{-1}e^{-i\kappa\hat{x}}+p_{1}e^{i\kappa\hat{x}}

Thus, the general form of the polynomial is

\displaystyle P_{d}(\hat{x})=\sum_{n=-d}^{d}p_{n}\hat{x}^{n},\quad n=\{-d,-d+2,\cdots,d-2,d\}.

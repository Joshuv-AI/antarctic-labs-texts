Connectivity keeping trees in triangle-free graphs
Hojin Chu, Shinya Fujita, Boram Park, Homoon Ryu

Abstract
In 2012, Mader conjectured that for any tree T T of order m m , every k k -connected graph G G with minimum degree at least ⌊ 3 ​ k 2 ⌋ + m − 1 \lfloor\frac{3k}{2}\rfloor+m-1 contains a subtree T ′ ≅ T T^{\prime}\cong T such that G − V ⁡ ( T ′ ) G-V(T^{\prime}) remains k k -connected. In 2022, Luo, Tian, and Wu considered an analogous problem for bipartite graphs and conjectured that for any tree T T with bipartition ( X , Y ) (X,Y) , every k k -connected bipartite graph G G with minimum degree at least k + max ⁡ { | X | , | Y | } k+\max\{|X|,|Y|\} contains a subtree T ′ ≅ T T^{\prime}\cong T such that G − V ⁡ ( T ′ ) G-V(T^{\prime}) remains k k -connected. In this paper, we relax the bipartite assumption by considering triangle-free graphs and prove that for any tree T T of order m m , every k k -connected triangle-free graph G G with minimum degree at least 2 ​ k + 3 ​ m − 4 2k+3m-4 contains a subtree T ′ ≅ T T^{\prime}\cong T such that G − V ⁡ ( T ′ ) G-V(T^{\prime}) remains k k -connected. Furthermore, we establish refined results for specific subclasses such as bipartite graphs or graphs with girth at least five.

Introduction

Throughout this paper, all graphs are finite, simple, and undirected. The
order
of a graph is the cardinality of its vertex set. For a vertex set
W\subseteq V(G)
of a graph
G
,
G[W]
is the subgraph induced by
W
and
G-W=G[V(G)\setminus W]
.
For a graph
G
, we denote the
minimum degree
, the
maximum degree
, and the

of
G
by
\delta(G)
,
\Delta(G)
, and
\kappa(G)
, respectively.
We also denote by
g(G)
the

of
G
, that is, the length of its shortest cycle.
For a positive integer

is a graph with
\kappa(G)\geq k
, then

is called
k
-connected
. Unless otherwise specified,
k
and
m
are always positive integers.

The problem of connectivity keeping subgraphs studies how large the minimum degree of a
k
-connected graph must be to ensure that the deletion of a specified subgraph does not destroy its
k
-connectivity. (See
[
15
]
for a survey.)
A fundamental result was established by Chartrand, Kaugars, and Lick
[
3
]
in 1972.

Theorem 1.1

(​​
[
3
]
)
.

Let
k
be a positive integer.
Every
k
-connected graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{3k}{2}\rfloor
has a vertex
v
such that
G-v
remains still
k
-connected.

In 2008, Fujita and Kawarabayashi
[
7
]
proved that every
k
-connected graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{3k}{2}\rfloor+2
contains an edge
e=uv
such that
G-\{u,v\}
remains still
k
-connected.
They conjectured that given positive integers
k
and

, there is a function
f_{k}(m)
such that every
k
-connected graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{3k}{2}\rfloor+f_{k}(m)
contains a connected subgraph

of order

satisfying

.
Indeed, they gave examples to show that

is at least
m-1
.
In 2010, Mader solved the conjecture by proving that such a subgraph can be chosen to be a path as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(​​
[
13
]
)
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers.
Every
k
-connected graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{3k}{2}\rfloor+m-1
contains a path

of order
m
such that
\kappa(G-V(P))\geq k
.

Mader further conjectured that Theorem
1.2
holds not only for a path of order

, but also any tree of order
m
.

Conjecture 1.3

(​​
[
13
]
)
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers.
For any tree
T
of order

, every
k
-connected graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\lfloor\frac{3k}{2}\rfloor+m-1
contains a subtree

such that

In a follow-up study, Mader
[

proved that, for any tree

of order

-connected graph

with
\delta(G)\geq 2(m+k-1)^{2}+m-1

T^{\prime}\cong T
of

remains

-connected.
Diwan and Tholiya
[
5
]
proved that the conjecture is true for

, and Hong and Liu
[
8
]
proved that it is true for

. The conjecture remains open for

. Recently, Liu, Ying, and Hong
[

]
proved that Conjecture

holds for minimum degree up to linear in

and

Theorem 1.4

(​​
[
11
]
)
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers.
For any tree
T
of order
m
, every
k
-connected graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq 3k+4m-6
contains a subtree
T^{\prime}\cong T
such that
G-V(T^{\prime})
remains
k
-connected.

The problem of connectivity keeping subgraphs has also been
studied extensively in the context of bipartite graphs.
Luo, Tian, and Wu
[
12
]
showed that the minimum degree condition of Theorem

can be relaxed under the bipartite condition.

Theorem 1.5

(​​
[
12
]
)
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers.
Every
k
-connected bipartite graph

with
\delta(G)\geq k+m
contains a path

of order

such that
\kappa(G-V(P))\geq k
.

They also proposed the following bipartite version of Conjecture

.

Conjecture 1.6

(​​
[
12
]
)
.

Let
k
be a positive integer.
For any tree
T
with bipartition

, every

-connected bipartite graph

\delta(G)\geq k+\max\{|X|,|Y|\}

such that

.

Conjecture

was studied for special classes of trees when

improved these results by confirming Conjecture

.
Yang and Tian
[

proved that Conjecture

holds for odd paths by showing that every

-connected bipartite graph

with

of order

-connected.
Recently, Fujita
[

relaxed the bipartite condition to the triangle-free condition

In this paper, we consider the existence of a connectivity keeping tree in a triangle-free graph as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers, and
T
be any tree
of order
m
. Then every

-connected triangle-free graph

\delta(G)\geq 2k+3m-4

We also obtain a refined result for subfamilies of triangle-free graphs, such as bipartite graphs or graphs with girth at least five, by an argument analogous to that used in the proof of Theorem

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers, and
T
be any tree of order

. Then every
k
-connected graph

contains a subtree

such that
\kappa(G-V(T^{\prime}))\geq k
if

satisfies one of the following:

(i)

g(G)\geq 5
and
\delta(G)\geq 2k+2m+\max\{\frac{m-1}{2},\Delta(T)\}-3
;

(ii)

G
is bipartite and
\delta(G)\geq 2k+2m+\max\{|X|,|Y|\}-3
.

While Conjecture
1.6
remains open, the best known previous result for bipartite graphs was obtained in Theorem
1.4
. Our result, Theorem
1.8
(ii), gives a further improvement.

In the remainder of this paper, we prove the main results stated above.
We first revisit a degree condition that guarantees the existence of a suitable subtree.
We use the notion of a connected triple, which is a structure that seems to have broad applications to graph connectivity problems.
Our results also arise from an application of connected triples.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Tree embedding

The following well-known lemma provides a sufficient minimum degree condition ensuring that every tree on
m
vertices appears in a graph.

Lemma 2.1

(​​
[
4
]
)
.

If
G
is a graph of minimum degree at least
m-1
for a positive integer
m
, then
G
contains every tree on
m
vertices.

Given two graphs
H
and
G
, an
embedding
of
H
into
G
is an injective map from
V(H)
to
V(G)
preserving the adjacency.
That is, the existence of an embedding of
H
into
G
guarantees that there is a subgraph
H^{\prime}
of
G
which is isomorphic to
H
.
For a vertex set
W\subseteq V(G)
, we let
\delta_{G}(W)=\min_{v\in W}d_{G}(v)
.

In a bipartite graph, the minimum degree condition ensuring the existence of a tree as a subgraph depends on the sizes of the partite sets of the tree.
The following lemma guarantees an embedding of a tree into a bipartite graph.

Lemma 2.2

(​​
[
9
]
)
.

Let
T
be a tree with bipartition
(X,Y)
. For a bipartite graph
G
with bipartition
(U,V)
,
if
\delta_{G}(U)\geq|Y|
and
\delta_{G}(V)\geq|X|
, then there exists an embedding
\phi:V(T)\to V(G)
such that
\phi(X)\subseteq U
and
\phi(Y)\subseteq V
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph and
T
be a tree with bipartition
(X,Y)
.
If
\delta(G)\geq\max\{|X|,|Y|\}
, then there exists a subtree
T^{\prime}\cong T
of
G
.

Erdős and Sós in 1963 considered a problem of the existence of arbitrary tree as a subgraph, called Erdős-Sós conjecture, asking if every graph of order
n
with more than
\frac{n(k-1)}{2}
edges contains every tree with
k
edges as a subgraph.
The following result was one of attempts to solve Erdős-Sós conjecture.

Theorem 2.4

(​​
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with
g(G)\geq 5
and
T
be a tree of order
m
. If
\delta(G)\geq\frac{m-1}{2}
and
\Delta(G)\geq\Delta(T)
, then G contains a subgraph
T^{\prime}\cong T
.

2.2
A connected triple

Let
G
be a graph.
For a pair of vertices
u,v\in V(G)
, we denote by
p_{G}(u,v)
the maximum number of internally vertex-disjoint
(u,v)
-paths in
G
.
Let
u
and
v
be nonadjacent vertices in
G
.
A
\{u,v\}
-separating set
is a subset
S
of
V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}
that
u
and
v
belong to different components of
G-S
.

Theorem 2.5

(Menger’s Theorem
[
1
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph. For a pair of nonadjacent vertices
(u,v)
, the maximum number of pairwise internally vertex-disjoint
(u,v)
-paths is equal to the minimum cardinality of a
\{u,v\}
-separating set.

For a vertex set
U\subseteq V(G)
, we let
\kappa_{G}(U)=\min_{u,v\in U}p_{G}(u,v)
.
By Theorem
2.5
, for a positive integer
p
,
\kappa_{G}(U)\geq p+1
implies that the vertices in
U-R
lie in the same connected component of
G-R
for every vertex set
R\subseteq V(G)
of size at most
p
.
Note that
\kappa_{G}(V(G))=\kappa(G)
.
A connected component of a graph is said to be
non-trivial
if it has at least two vertices.

Liu, Ying, and Hong
[
11
]
introduced a special structure, called a
p
-connected triple
(see Figure
1
for an illustration).

Definition 2.6

(​​
[
11
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq V(G)
.
Let
F
be a nontrivial connected component of
G-(S_{1}\cup S_{2})
(
F=G-(S_{1}\cup S_{2})
is allowed).
For a positive integer
p
,
(S_{1},S_{2},F)
is called a
p
-connected triple of
G
if

(i)

S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset
and
|S_{1}\cup S_{2}|\leq 2p-1
,

(ii)

|N_{G}(v)\cap V(F)|\leq p
for any
v\in S_{1}
,

(iii)

\kappa_{G[S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup V(F)]}(S_{2}\cup V(F))\geq p+1
.

Figure 1
:
A
p
-connected triple
(S_{1},S_{2},F)
of
G
, where the region surrounded by dotted lines is related to the condition (iii) of Definition
2.6

They also showed the existence of a
p
-connected triple as follows.

Lemma 2.7

(​​
[
11
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\delta(G)\geq 2p
and let
S_{0}\subseteq V(G)
with
|S_{0}|\leq 2p-1
for a positive integer
p
.
Then for any connected component
C
of
G-S_{0}
, there exists a
p
-connected triple
(S_{1},S_{2},F)
such that
V(F)\subseteq V(C)
.

3
Proof of Theorems
1.7
and
1.8

It is well-known that for triangle-free graphs, the following hold.

Fact 3.1
.

Let
G
be a triangle-free graph.
Then the following are true.

(a)

For any two adjacent vertices
u
and
v
,
N_{G}(u)
and
N_{G}(v)
are disjoint.

(b)

There is a vertex
v\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(v)\leq\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
.

Moreover, we use the following classical result.
Let
G
be a graph.
For a graph
G
and disjoint vertex sets
U
and
V
of
G
, a
matching
between
U
and
V
is an edge set
M\subseteq E(G)
such that every edge has one endpoint in
U
and the other in
V
, and no two edges in
M
share a common vertex.
For a vertex set
W\subseteq V(G)
, a matching
M
in
G

saturates

W
if every vertex in
W
is incident with an edge of
M
.

Theorem 3.2

(Hall’s Theorem
[
1
]
)
.

For a bipartite graph
G
with bipartition
(X,Y)
,
G
has a matching which saturates
X
if and only if
|N_{G}(S)|\geq|S|
for all
S\subseteq X
.

To prove Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
, we present two useful lemmas.
For a vertex set
W\subseteq V(G)
, we let
N_{G}(W)=\bigcup_{v\in W}N_{G}(v)\setminus W
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a triangle-free graph with
\delta(G)\geq 2p
for a positive integer
p
.
Suppose that
(S_{1},S_{2},F)
is a
p
-connected triple of
G
such that
|V(F)|
is as small as possible.
Then
|V(F)|>|S_{1}|
and there exists a matching
M
between
S_{1}
and
V(F)
that saturates
S_{1}
.

Proof.

We want to claim that, for any
S\subseteq S_{1}
,
|N_{G}(S)\cap V(F)|\geq|S|+1
.
Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists
S\subseteq S_{1}
such that
|N_{G}(S)\cap V(F)|\leq|S|
.
Let
K=N_{G}(S)\cap V(F)
and
S_{0}^{\prime}=(S_{1}\setminus S)\cup S_{2}\cup K
.
Then
|K|\leq|S|
and so
|S_{0}^{\prime}|\leq|S_{1}\cup S_{2}|\leq 2p-1
by Definition
2.6
(i).
If
V(F)\setminus K\neq\emptyset
, then
V(F)\setminus K
is a connected component of
G-S_{0}^{\prime}
by Definition
2.6
(iii) and so, by Lemma
2.7
applied to
S_{0}^{\prime}
and
V(F)\setminus K
, there is a
p
-connected triple
(S_{1}^{\prime},S_{2}^{\prime},F^{\prime})
with
V(F^{\prime})\subseteq V(F)\setminus K\subsetneq V(F)
, which contradicts the choice of
F
.
Therefore
V(F)=K
.
If there is no edge in
F
, then for any
u\in V(F)
,
N_{G}(u)\subseteq S_{1}\cup S_{2}
, so
2p\leq d_{G}(u)\leq|S_{1}\cup S_{2}|\leq 2p-1
, a contradiction.
Thus there is an edge in
F
.
Take an edge
uv
in
F
.
Then
N_{G}(u)
and
N_{G}(v)
are disjoint by Fact
3.1
(a).
Thus, since
V(F)=K
and
|K|\leq|S|\leq|S_{1}|
,

4p\leq 2\delta(G)\leq d_{G}(u)+d_{G}(v)\leq|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|V(F)|\leq|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|+|S|\leq 2(|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|)\leq 4p-2,

which is a contradiction.
Therefore for any
S\subseteq S_{1}
,
|N_{G}(S)\cap V(F)|\geq|S|+1
.
Then

|V(F)|\geq|N_{G}(S_{1})\cap V(F)|\geq|S_{1}|+1>|S_{1}|.

Moreover, by Theorem
3.2
, there is a matching
M
which saturates
S_{1}
such that every edge in
M
has one endpoint in
S_{1}
and the other in
V(F)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
k
-connected triangle-free graph with
\delta(G)\geq 2p
for positive integers
p
and
k
satisfying
p\geq k
.
Suppose that
(S_{1},S_{2},F)
is a
p
-connected triple of
G
and there exists a matching
M
between
S_{1}
and
V(F)
that saturates
S_{1}
.
Then
G-R
is still
k
-connected for any vertex set
R\subseteq V(F)\setminus V(M)
of size
p-k+1
.

Figure 2
:
An illustration for the proof of Lemma
3.4

Proof.

Let
F_{M}=V(M)\cap V(F)
.
Suppose, to the contrary, that there exist a vertex set
R\subseteq V(F)\setminus V(M)
with
|R|=p-k+1
, and a cut
A
with
|A|\leq k-1
in
G-R
. (See Figure
2
for an illustration, where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
will be defined later.)
Then
A\cup R
is a cut in
G
of order at most
p
.
By Definition
2.6
(iii),
\kappa_{G}(S_{2}\cup V(F))\geq\kappa_{G[S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup V(F)]}(S_{2}\cup V(F))\geq p+1
.
Therefore the remaining vertices of
S_{2}\cup V(F)
in
G-(A\cup R)
must be contained in the same connected component.
Thus, there exists a connected component
C
of
G-(A\cup R)
such that
C\cap(S_{2}\cup V(F))=\emptyset
.
We note that
N_{G}(V(C))\subseteq A\cup R
.
Let

C_{1}=V(C)\cap S_{1},\quad C_{2}=V(C)\setminus S_{1},\quad A_{1}=A\cap V(F),\quad A_{2}=A\setminus V(F),\quad\text{and}\quad A_{3}=N_{G}(C_{1})\cap F_{M}.

Then, since the matching
M
saturates
S_{1}
,
|A_{3}|\geq|C_{1}|
. Moreover,

A_{3}=N_{G}(C_{1})\cap F_{M}\subseteq N_{G}(V(C))\cap(V(F)\setminus R)\subseteq(A\cup R)\cap(V(F)\setminus R)=A\cap V(F)=A_{1}.

To the contrary, suppose that
C_{2}\neq\emptyset
.
Then
N_{G}(C_{2})\subseteq N_{G}(V(C))\cup C_{1}\subseteq A\cup R\cup C_{1}
.
Since
N_{G}(V(F))\subseteq S_{1}\cup S_{2}
and
C_{2}\cap(S_{1}\cup S_{2})=\emptyset
,
N_{G}(C_{2})\cap V(F)=\emptyset
and so
N_{G}(C_{2})\subseteq(A\cup R\cup C_{1})\setminus V(F)=(A\setminus V(F))\cup C_{1}=A_{2}\cup C_{1}
.
Thus

|N_{G}(C_{2})|\leq|A_{2}|+|C_{1}|\leq|A_{2}|+|A_{3}|\leq|A_{2}|+|A_{1}|=|A|\leq k-1,

which contradicts the
k
-connectivity of
G
.
Therefore
C_{2}=\emptyset
and so

V(C)=C_{1}
.
Since
G[V(C)]
is triangle-free, there is a vertex
w\in V(C)
such that
d_{G[V(C)]}(w)\leq\frac{|V(C)|}{2}
by Fact
3.1
(b).
Moreover, since
C
is a connected component of
G-(A\cup R)
, we have
N_{G}(w)\subseteq N_{G[C]}(w)\cup(A\cup R)
.
Therefore

2p\leq\delta(G)\leq d_{G}(w)\leq d_{G[C]}(w)+|A\cup R|\leq\frac{|V(C)|}{2}+p

and so
|V(C)|\geq 2p
, which contradicts the fact that
V(C)=C_{1}\subseteq S_{1}
and
|S_{1}\cup S_{2}|\leq 2p-1
.
Hence we have shown that
G-R
is
k
-connected for any vertex set
R\subseteq V(F)\setminus V(M)
of size
p-k+1
.
∎

Now we are ready to finish the proof.

Proof of Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
.

Let
T
be a tree of order
m
with bipartition
(X,Y)
, and
G

be a graph with
\delta(G)\geq 2k+2m+\beta-3
where

\beta=\begin{cases}m-1&\text{if $G$ is a triangle-free graph,}\\
\max\{|X|,|Y|\}&\text{if $G$ is a bipartite graph,}\\
\max\{\frac{m-1}{2},\Delta(T)\}&\text{if $g(G)\geq 5$.}\end{cases}

If
m=1
, then Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
are true by Theorem
1.1
.
We assume
m\geq 2
.
Let
p=k+m-1
. Then
\delta(G)\geq 2p+\beta-1\geq 2p
.
By Lemma
2.7
, there exists a
p
-connected triple
(S_{1},S_{2},F)
of
G
.
We assume that
|V(F)|
is as small as possible.
Then, by Lemma
3.3
, there is a matching
M
between
S_{1}
and
V(F)
saturating
S_{1}
.
Let
F_{M}=V(M)\cap V(F)
.
Then
|F_{M}|=|S_{1}|
and
F_{M}\subseteq N_{G}(S_{1})\cap V(F)
.
Since
|V(F)|>|S_{1}|
by Lemma
3.3
,
|V(F)\setminus F_{M}|=|V(F)|-|F_{M}|=|V(F)|-|S_{1}|>0

and so

V(F)\setminus F_{M}\neq\emptyset
.
Take a vertex
v\in V(F)\setminus F_{M}
.
Then, since
G
has no triangles and
M
is a matching between
S_{1}
and
F_{M}
,
|N_{G}(v)\cap(S_{1}\cup F_{M})|\leq|S_{1}|
.
Thus

\displaystyle|N_{G[V(F)\setminus F_{M}]}(v)|

\displaystyle=|N_{G}(v)\setminus(S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup F_{M})|

\displaystyle\geq\delta(G)-|N_{G}(v)\cap(S_{1}\cup F_{M})|-|N_{G}(v)\cap S_{2}|

\displaystyle\geq\delta(G)-(|S_{1}|+|S_{2}|)

\displaystyle\geq(2p+\beta-1)-(2p-1)

\displaystyle=\beta.

In all three cases, the corresponding previous results (Lemma
2.1
, Corollary
2.3
, or Theorem
2.4
) yield a subtree
T^{\prime}\cong T
in
G[V(F)\setminus F_{M}]
.
Consequently, Lemma
3.4
implies that
G-V(T^{\prime})
is
k
-connected.
∎

4
Concluding Remarks

Yang and Tian
[
16
]
proved that Conjecture
1.6
holds for odd paths, and Fujita
[
6
]
generalized this result by relaxing the bipartite condition to the triangle-free setting. Thus, we are led to ask whether Conjecture
1.6
can be extended to triangle-free graphs.

Question 4.1
.

Let
k
be a positive integer.
For any tree
T
with bipartition
(X,Y)
, does every
k
-connected triangle-free graph
G
with minimum degree at least
k+t
, where
t=\max\{|X|,|Y|\}
, contain a subtree
T^{\prime}\cong T
such that
G-V(T^{\prime})
is still
k
-connected?

Furthermore, the Erdős-Sós conjecture discusses results concerning tree embeddings, and related results suggest that our result might be further improved. For example, Jiang
[
10
]
showed that, for a tree
T
of order
m
, a graph
G
satisfying
g(G)\geq 2t+1
and
\delta(G)\geq\max\{\frac{m-1}{t},\Delta(T)\}
has a subtree
T^{\prime}\cong T
. From this, one can observe that the following is a direct consequence of our proof.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
G
be a
k
-connected graph with
g(G)\geq 2t+1
and
T
be a tree of order
m
.
If
\delta(G)\geq 2k+2m+\max\{\frac{m-1}{t},\Delta(T)\}-3
, then
G
contains a subtree
T^{\prime}\cong T
such that
\kappa(G-V(T^{\prime}))\geq k
.

Exotic hypercomplex structures on a torus do not exist
Alberto Pipitone Federico,

Abstract




Abstract.

A hypercomplex manifold is a manifold with three complex
structures satisfying quaternionic relations.
Such a manifold admits a unique torsion-free connection
preserving the quaternionic action, called
the Obata connection.

A compact Kähler manifold admitting a hypercomplex
structure always admits a hyperkähler structure as well;
however, it is not obvious whether the original
hypercomplex structure is hyperkähler.
A non-hyperkähler hypercomplex structure on a
Kähler manifold is called
exotic
.
We show that the Obata connection for an
exotic hypercomplex structure on a torus is flat
and classify complete flat affine structures
on real tori. We use this classification
to prove that exotic hypercomplex structures
do not exist.

1
Introduction

Throughout this paper, a “complex manifold”
is an almost complex manifold
(M,I)
, with the tensor

I\in\operatorname{End}(TM)
,
I^{2}=-\operatorname{Id}
being integrable.
In this situation, the operator
I
is called
“the complex structure”.

A hypercomplex manifold is a manifold
M

equipped with three complex structures
I,J,K

satisfying the quaternionic relations

IJ=-JI=K
. This defines, in particular, a
quaternionic action on the tangent bundle,
hence the real dimension of
M
is divisible by four.

As shown by Obata (
[
Ob
]
),
every hypercomplex manifold admits a unique torsion-free
connection preserving the quaternionic action, known as

Obata connection
; its holonomy group is
contained in
GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})
. Conversely,
if a manifold admits a torsion-free connection
with holonomy in
GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})
, it
admits three complex structures
I,J,K

preserved by the connection and
satisfying the quaternionic relations.
A complex structure preserved by
a torsion-free connection is always
integrable (
[
Ko
, Example I.2.4]
,
see also
[
F
]
), hence
hypercomplex manifolds can be
defined as manifolds equipped with
a torsion-free connection with
holonomy in
GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})
.

Hyperkähler manifolds
are manifolds equipped with a hypercomplex structure
I,J,K

and a Riemannian metric which is Kähler with respect
to
I,J,K
. However, this notion also has an equivalent
definition in terms of holonomy: a Riemannian manifold
is hyperkähler if the holonomy of its Levi-Civita
connection belongs to
Sp(n)
(
[
Bes
]
).
Clearly, this is equivalent to holonomy of Obata connection
preserving a metric, or having a compact closure.

By definition, the Levi-Civita connection on a Riemannian
manifold is the unique torsion-free connection preserving
the metric, thus if the Obata connection preserves a metric,
it is automatically the Levi-Civita connection of this
metric. In this case its holonomy belongs
to
O(4n)\cap GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})=Sp(n)
.
In other words, a hypercomplex manifold is hyperkähler
if and only if its Obata connection preserves a metric.

In this paper we study the compact hypercomplex manifolds

(M,I,J,K)
such that
(M,I)
admits a Kähler structure.
As shown in
[
V4
]
, in this case

(M,I)
admits a hyperkähler structure, i.e.
there is a hypercomplex structure
(I,J^{\prime},K^{\prime})
on
M

such that its Obata holonomy belongs to
Sp(n)
.

However, this result does not imply that
(I,J,K)

is hyperkähler. We call a hypercomplex structure
(M,I,J,K)

exotic
if the holonomy of its Obata connection
does not preserve any metric, but
(M,I)
admits a Kähler structure.

In
[
V4
]
it was conjectured
that exotic hypercomplex structures does not exist.
If
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}M=4
, this statement is contained

[
Boy
]
.

A

2
Hypercomplex manifolds

2.1
Hypercomplex structures and Obata connection

Definition 2.1:
Let
M
be a smooth
manifold equipped with endomorphisms

I,J,K:\;TM{\>\longrightarrow\>}TM
, satisfying the quaternionic relation

I^{2}=J^{2}=K^{2}=IJK=-\operatorname{Id}.
Suppose that
I
,
J
,
K
are
integrable. Then
(M,I,J,K)
is called
a hypercomplex manifold
.

Theorem 2.2:
Let
(M,I,J,K)
be
a hypercomplex manifold. Then
M
admits a unique torsion-free
affine connection preserving
I,J,K
.

Proof:
[
Ob
]
.

Definition 2.3:
This connection is called
the Obata connection
.

Remark 2.4:
Holonomy of Obata connection lies in
GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})
.
Conversely, a manifold equipped with an affine,
torsion-free connection with holonomy in
GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})

is hypercomplex. Indeed, suppose that
I,J,K
are operators defining quaternionic structure
on
TM
and
\nabla
is an affine torsion-free connection
preserving
I
,
J
,
K
. Then
I
,
J
,
K
are integrable
almost complex structures and
(M,I,J,K)
is hypercomplex.

Remark 2.5:
This can be used as a definition of a hypercomplex
structure:
a hypercomplex manifold

(M,\nabla,I,J,K)
is a
manifold equipped with a torsion-free connection such that
its holonomy preserves a quaternionic structure on a tangent bundle.

Remark 2.6:
Compatibility of
\nabla
with
I,J,K
is equivalent to having
\nabla^{0,1}_{I}=\overline{\partial}_{I}
,
\nabla^{0,1}_{J}=\overline{\partial}_{J}
,
\nabla^{0,1}_{K}=\overline{\partial}_{K}
. The curvature form of Obata’s connection is of type
(1,1)
with respect to
I,J,K
. This follows directly from Bianchi identity, the fact that
\nabla
preserves
I,J,K
and that
J
maps
T^{1,0}_{I}M
to
T^{0,1}_{I}M
.

2.2
Hyperkähler structures

The following definition is equivalent to the standard one;
we leave the equivalence as an exercise to the reader.

Definition 2.7:
A hypercomplex manifold
(M,\nabla,I,J,K)
is called

hyperkähler
if the holonomy
\operatorname{Hol}(\nabla)
of

\nabla
preserves a
quaternionic invariant Riemannian metric
g
. Such a metric
is called
hyperkähler
. A
hyperkähler structure

is
(M,\nabla,I,J,K,g)
. In this situation,

\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection.

Theorem 2.8:
Let
(M,I,J,K)
be a compact hypercomplex manifold.
Assume that
(M,I)
admits a Kähler structure.
Then
(M,I)
admits a hyperkähler structure

(I,J^{\prime},K^{\prime})
.

Proof:
[
V4
]
.

Definition 2.9:
Let
(M,I,J,K)
be a compact hypercomplex manifold.
Assume that
(M,I)
admits a Kähler structure.
The hypercomplex structure
(I,J,K)
is called

exotic
if it is not compatible
with a hyperkähler metric, that is, if the
holonomy of its Obata connection is non-compact.

2.3
Exotic hypercomplex structures on K3

The following, as well as the same
result for two dimensional complex tori, can be found in

[
Boy
]

We give a short proof of this result for the benefit of the reader.

Theorem 2.10:
Exotic hypercomplex structures on a K3 surface do not exist.

Proof. Step 1:
Let
K_{M_{I}}
,
K_{M_{J}}
,
K_{M_{K}}
denote the canonical
bundle of a hypercomplex manifold
M
with the complex structures
I,J,K
.
Since the action of
SU(2)=U({\mathbb{H}},1)
on
TM
interchanges
the complex structures
I,J,K
, it also interchanges

K_{M_{I}}
,
K_{M_{J}}
,
K_{M_{K}}
considered as sub-bundles
in
\Lambda^{2n}(M)
. This implies that
K_{M_{I}}
,
K_{M_{J}}
,
K_{M_{K}}

are isomorphic as complex vector bundles with the connection
induced by the Obata connection.

Let
(M,I,J,K)
be a hypercomplex structure on
a K3 surface and
\Theta
the curvature of the Obata connection on
its canonical bundle
K_{M_{I}}=K_{M_{J}}=K_{M_{K}}
. Since
\Theta
is a
(1,1)
-form with respect to
I,J,K
, it is
SU(2)
-invariant with respect to the

SU(2)
-action on
\Lambda^{*}(M)
generated by quaternions.
In particular, for any Hermitian metric
g
, one has
*\Theta=-\Theta
, thus

\Theta\wedge\Theta=-\|\Theta\|^{2}_{g}{\operatorname{Vol}}_{g}

By Bianchi identity,
\Theta
is closed, and cohomologous
to
c_{1}(M,I)
. Since
(M,I)
admits a non-degenerate

(2,0)
-form, we have
c_{1}(M,I)=0
.
Integrating the above formula, we find
\Theta=0
.

Given that
\pi_{1}(K3)=0
, we obtain that
K_{M_{I}}
(resp.
K_{M_{J}}
,
K_{M_{K}}
)
is holomorphically trivialized by an Obata-parallel section
s_{I}
(resp.
s_{J}
,
s_{K}
).

Step 2:

The holomorphic sections
s_{I},s_{J},s_{K}

generate a rank 3 sub-bundle
B
in
\Lambda^{2}(M)
,
which has flat connection because
s_{I},s_{J},s_{K}
are parallel.
Recall that the natural map
SL(4,{\mathbb{R}})\to SO(3,3)
taking
endomorphisms of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
to endomorphisms of
\Lambda^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})

is a 2 to 1 covering. Fixing a maximal compact subgroup

SO(3)\times SO(3)
in
SO(3,3)
is the same as fixing
a metric on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
; this reduces the structure group

SL(4,{\mathbb{R}})
to
SO(4)\subset SL(4,{\mathbb{R}})
, which is also
a 2:1 covering of
SO(3)\times SO(3)
. Finally, reducing
the structure group of
\Lambda^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})
to
SO(3)\subset SO(3)\times SO(3)

means that the structure group
SL(4,{\mathbb{R}})
is reduced to
Sp(1)=SU(2)
,
which is a 2:1 cover of
SO(3)
. In our case, this reduction is
given by a trivialization of
B
. Therefore, the holonomy
of
\nabla
is contained in
Sp(1)
, hence the hypercomplex
structure on
M
is in fact hyperkähler.

3
Twistor spaces for hypercomplex manifolds

3.1
Twistor spaces: definition and basic notions

Definition 3.1: Induced complex structures

on a hypercomplex manifold are
complex structures of the form
L=aI+bJ+cK
, with
a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}=1
.

Remark 3.2:
The induced complex structures are usually non-algebraic. Indeed,
if
M
is compact and hyperkähler, for generic
a,b,c
,
(M,L)
has no divisors
(
[
Fu
]
).

A twistor space
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
of a hypercomplex manifold
is a complex manifold obtained by gluing
these complex structures into
a holomorphic family over
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. More formally:

Definition 3.3:
Let
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
be the smooth manifold

M\times S^{2}
. Consider the complex structure
I_{m}:T_{m}M\to T_{m}M

on
M
induced by
J\in S^{2}\subset{\mathbb{H}}
. Let
I_{J}

denote the complex structure on
S^{2}={\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.
The operator

I_{\operatorname{Tw}}=I_{m}\oplus I_{J}:T_{x}\operatorname{Tw}(M)\to T_{x}\operatorname{Tw}(M)

satisfies
I_{\operatorname{Tw}}^{2}=-\operatorname{Id}
, defining
an almost complex structure on
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
which is known to be integrable
(
[
Ka
]
).

Example 3.4:
If
M={\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, then

\operatorname{Tw}(M)=\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus n})\cong{\mathbb{C}}P^{2n+1}\backslash{\mathbb{C}}P^{2n-1}

(see e. g.
[
JV1
]
).

Remark 3.5:

A curve
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\times\{m\}\subset\operatorname{Tw}(M)
is by
construction holomorphic; such a curve is called

a horizontal curve
. Its normal bundle
is
\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus n}
(
[
HKLR
]
).

Remark 3.6:
For
M
compact,
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
never admits a Kähler, or Moishezon structure
(
[
Pi
,
Go
]
).

3.2
The twistor data

Hypercomplex structures can be characterized (and,
in fact, defined) in terms of twistor spaces.
The main advance in this direction was made
by Hitchin, Karlhede, Lindström, Roček in
[
HKLR
]
;
see also
[
Si
,
V1
]
.
We use the notion of “the twistor data”
of a hypercomplex manifold to signify the twistor space
equipped with additional structures needed to reconstruct
the hypercomplex manifold.

Let
\check{\tau}
denote the central symmetry
on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
; if we identify
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
with imaginary unit
quaternions, we have

\check{\tau}(L)=-L
. It is an anticomplex involution without fixed points.

Definition 3.7:

The
twistor data

is a complex manifold
\operatorname{Tw}
,
\dim\operatorname{Tw}=2n+1
, equipped with the following
structures.

(i)

A holomorphic submersion
\pi:\;\operatorname{Tw}{\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}

and an anticomplex involution
\tau:\;\operatorname{Tw}{\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Tw}

which makes this diagram commutative:

\begin{CD}\operatorname{Tw}@>{\tau}>{}>\operatorname{Tw}\\
@V{\pi}V{}V@V{}V{\pi}V\\
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}@>{\check{\tau}}>{}>{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}\end{CD}

(ii)

A connected component
\operatorname{Hor}

in the set
\operatorname{Sec}^{\tau}\subset\operatorname{Sec}
of
\tau
-invariant sections of
\pi

such that for each
S\in\operatorname{Hor}
, the normal bundle to
S

is
\mathcal{O}(1)^{2n}
and for each point
x\in\operatorname{Tw}
there exists a unique
S\in\operatorname{Hor}
passing through
x
.

Remark 3.8:
With any twistor space
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
of a hypercomplex manifold,
one associates the twistor data in a natural way:

\tau(I,m)=(-I,m)
, and
\operatorname{Hor}(M)
the space
of all sections
S_{m}
, taking
I\in{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
to

(I,m)\in\operatorname{Tw}(M)
, where
m\in M
is a fixed point.

Theorem 3.9:

Let
M
be a hypercomplex manifold. Then
the twistor data on
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
can be used to recover the
hypercomplex structure on
M
, which is identified with
\operatorname{Hor}

by
3.2
(ii).
Moreover, for any twistor data
(\operatorname{Tw},\tau,\operatorname{Hor})
,
there exists a hypercomplex structure
(I,J,K)
on
\operatorname{Hor}

such that these twistor data are associated with

(I,J,K)
.

Proof:
[
HKLR
]
.

Remark 3.10:
This theorem has a version appropriate for hyperkähler
metrics (also in
[
HKLR
]
).

4
Flat affine structures and Obata connection

4.1
Flat affine manifolds

To approach the exotic hypercomplex structures on a torus, we
would introduce flat affine manifolds. The following
notions are standard (see
e. g.
[
A
]
,
[
FGH
]
or

[
OV
, Chapter 27]
).

Definition 4.1:
A
flat affine structure
on a manifold
M

is a flat torsion-free connection on the tangent bundle.

It can be easily seen that this is equivalent to the existence of an affine atlas.

Definition 4.2:

A smooth map between smooth manifolds
f:M\to N
is

compatible with the affine structures
, or
affine
if the differential

Df
takes parallel vector fields to parallel vector fields.

Remark 4.3:
Equivalently,
f
is affine if local parallel
forms on
N
are pulled back to parallel forms on
M
. This
is happens precisely if there exist affine charts such
that
f
in local coordinates is an affine map. Any
diffeomorphism compatible with the affine structures is an
affine isomorphism.

Definition 4.4:
Let
M
be a simply connected affine manifold,
and
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n}\in\Lambda^{1}M

a basis of parallel 1-forms. Since a parallel 1-form
is closed and
H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})=0
, the forms
\theta_{i}
are
exact. Then
\theta_{i}=dx_{i}
.
The map
\delta:\;M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
taking
m
to
(x_{1}(m),...,x_{n}(m))

is called
the development map.
We consider

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as a flat affine manifold, with the standard
flat affine structure. The development map
is compatible with the affine structure:

Claim 4.5:

The development map
\delta:\;M\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

is compatible with the flat affine connections.

Proof:
It takes the coordinate 1-forms
dx_{1},...,dx_{n}\in\Lambda^{1}(M)

to
\theta_{1},...,\theta_{n}\in\Lambda^{1}M
.
However, these 1-forms are parallel.

Definition 4.6:
A flat
n
-dimensional affine manifold
(M,\nabla)
is said

complete
if its universal cover is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
as a flat affine manifold. In this case,

\pi_{1}(M)\to\text{Diff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
factors through

\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
1
1

1

The image of
\pi_{1}(M)\to\text{Diff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is identified with the affine holonomy,
see
4.2
below.

Equivalently,
M
is complete if and only if
\nabla
is geodesically complete or if the developing map is a diffeomorphism.

Remark 4.7:
Marcus conjecture (
5.2
) claims that a compact flat affine manifold
is complete if and only if the flat connection preserves a volume form.

4.2
Linear and affine holonomy

Definition 4.8:
The
linear holonomy
(or
holonomy
)
of a flat affine connection
\nabla
is its monodromy in
TM
;
by definition, the holonomy group belongs to
GL(T_{x}M)
,
where
x\in M
is a base point.

Definition 4.9:
Let
\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
denote the group of affine transforms of

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Clearly,
\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a semidirect product,

\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})=GL(n,{\mathbb{R}})\rtimes{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The natural map
\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}){\>\longrightarrow\>}GL(n,{\mathbb{R}})

is called
the linearization.

Definition 4.10:

Let
M
be a flat affine
n
-manifold,

\tilde{M}
its universal cover
2
2

2

Any finite cover of an affine manifold admits a canonical affine structure.
and

\delta:\;\tilde{M}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
the development map. For any
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(M)
, let
\tilde{\gamma}
be the induced homeomorphism
\tilde{M}\to\tilde{M}
.
Then define
{a}:\;\pi_{1}(M){\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
to be the map making the following diagram commute

\begin{CD}\tilde{M}@>{\delta}>{}>{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\\
@V{\tilde{\gamma}}V{}V@V{}V{a(\gamma)}V\\
\tilde{M}@>{\delta}>{}>{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.\end{CD}

The map
{\cal a}:\;\pi_{1}(M){\>\longrightarrow\>}\operatorname{Aff}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})

is called
affine holonomy map.

In terms of transition functions, the map
a
can be described explicitly: fix
x_{0}\in M
and let
\gamma
be a smooth loop based in
x_{0}
and contained in the affine charts
(U_{i},\varphi_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
, where
x_{0}\in U_{1}\cap U_{k}
and every
U_{i}\cap U_{i+1}
is non-empty and connected. Then
a(\gamma)=\varphi_{1}\varphi_{k}\varphi_{k}^{-1}\varphi_{k-1}\dots\varphi_{2}\varphi_{1}^{-1}

For affine manifolds, the two notions are related by the following

The holonomy of an affine manifold is
the linearization if its affine holonomy.

Proof:
It is clear from the above description of

4.3
Complex tori: definition and basic notions

Definition 4.12:
A
complex torus
is a complex manifold
M
biholomoprhic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}/\Lambda
, where
\Lambda
is a
2n
dimensional integral lattice. In particular,
M
is Kähler and the Albanese map

\operatorname{Alb}:M{\>\longrightarrow\>}\frac{H^{0}(M,\Omega^{1})^{*}}{H_{1}(M,{\mathbb{Z}})}

is an isomorphism.

Remark 4.13:
Any Kähler manifold
X

diffeomorphic to a torus has this nature: in
(
[
Cat2
]
) it is indeed
proven that, in the class of Kähler manifolds, being a
complex torus is equivalent to the purely topological
condition
\Lambda H^{1}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\backsimeq H^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})

(the isomorphism is in the category of rings).

Non-Kähler complex structures on a manifold
diffeomorphic to a torus were constructed in

[
Bl
,
Som
]
.
For the convenience of the reader, we include
one of such constructions here (see also

[
Cat2
]
).

Example 4.14:

Let
M
be a hyperkähler torus, and

\operatorname{Tw}(M)
its twistor space, equipped with
a holomorphic projection
\operatorname{Tw}(M)\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.
Let
E
be an elliptic
curve equipped with the standard 2-sheeted
ramified cover map
\sigma:E\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
.
Since
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
is diffeomorphic to a
product
M\times{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
, the fibered
product
E\times_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}\operatorname{Tw}(M)
is
diffeomorphic to a torus.
To see that
E\times_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}\operatorname{Tw}(M)

is not of Kähler type, we use the formula
[
KV
, (8.12)]
:

dd^{c}\omega=\omega\wedge\pi^{*}\omega_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}
,
where
\omega
is the standard Hermitian form on
the twistor space, and
\omega_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}
the Fubini-Study
form on
{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. This form is clearly positive
and exact, but a Kähler manifold cannot
admit positive exact forms (otherwise this
form, integrated with an appropriate power
of the Kähler form, would be positive, which
is impossible because it is exact).
Since
E\times_{{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}}\operatorname{Tw}(M)

is a ramified double cover of the twistor space,
this manifold also admits a positive exact form,
hence it cannot be Kähler.

Further on in this paper, we will use the following
important theorem.

Theorem 4.15:
Let
{\cal X}
be a connected family of complex
structures on a manifold
M
diffeomorphic to a torus.
Assume that for some
I\in{\cal X}
, the manifold
(M,I)

is a complex torus. Then
(M,I^{\prime})
is a torus for all

I^{\prime}\in{\cal X}
.

Proof:
[
Cat1
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Remark 4.16:

Let
\theta_{1},...\theta_{n}
be holomorphic
differentials on a complex torus
M
. Their antiderivatives
define a flat affine chart on
M
; the corresponding
flat affine structure on
M
is canonically defined.
This also defines a flat affine
connection on
M
compatible with the holomorphic structure.

4.4
Flatness of Obata connection for exotic hypercomplex structures on a torus

Theorem 4.17:

Let
(I,J,K)
be a hypercomplex structure on a complex
torus
(M,I)
and
\nabla
the corresponding Obata connection.
Then
(M,I,J,K)
is a quotient of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
by an affine
action of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{4n}
. In particular,
\nabla
is flat.

Proof. Step 1:
Consider the affine structure on
(M,I)
obtained from the
holomorphic structure as in
4.3
.
Since the fibers of the holomorphic projection

\pi:\;\operatorname{Tw}(M){\>\longrightarrow\>}{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
are affine manifolds,
the universal cover
\tilde{\pi}:\operatorname{Tw}(\widetilde{M})\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
is an affine
bundle. Fixing a horizontal
section of
\pi
is the same as fixing an origin for the
torus. This identifies
\operatorname{Tw}(\widetilde{M})
with

\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{O}(1)^{2n})
(the linearization of this affine
bundle is determined by
3.1
).

The anticomplex involution
\tau
of
\operatorname{Tw}(M)
clearly
respects the affine structure of the fibers and its lift

\tilde{\tau}
to
{\operatorname{Tw}}(\widetilde{M})
preserves the vector bundle structure.

Step 2:
Consider the twistor data on

\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{O}(1)^{2n})=\operatorname{Tw}(\widetilde{M})
which are compatible
with the vector bundle structure, as in Step 1. Such twistor data are
uniquely determined by the vector bundle structure;
indeed, the space of sections of the twistor projection
has only one component, and the anticomplex involution
on a vector bundle is unique up to an automorphism
of
\mathcal{O}(1)^{2n}
which has constant coefficients,
hence is standard in appropriate coordinates.
This implies that
\widetilde{M}
is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
as a hypercomplex manifold.

Step 3:

The map
\operatorname{Tw}(\widetilde{M})\to\operatorname{Tw}(M)
is a morphism
of twistor data, locally an
isomorphism. Therefore, the Obata connection of

(M,I,J,K)
is flat as well. Moreover,
\pi_{1}(\operatorname{Tw}(M))

acts on
{\operatorname{Tw}}(\tilde{M})
preserving the fibers of

\tilde{\pi}:\;\operatorname{Tw}(\widetilde{M})\to{\mathbb{C}}P^{1}
. This action is affine on each fiber,
because hypercomplex automorphisms of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}

are affine. Therefore,
(M,I,J,K)
is a quotient
of the flat affine hypercomplex manifold
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
by
an affine action of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{4n}
.

Remark 4.18:
If the holonomy of Obata connection
on
M
is trivial (or just compact), it would
immediately follow that
M
is a hyperkähler torus.

Corollary 4.19:

Let
(M,I,J,K)
be a hypercomplex manifold, and
\nabla

its Obata connection. Assume that
(M,I)
is a compact
complex torus. Then
\nabla
is flat and the hypercomplex
structure is exotic if and only if the holonomy
of
\nabla
is non-trivial.

5
Flat affine structures on a torus

5.1
Non-standard flat affine structures on a torus

Remark 5.1:
A flat affine structure on a torus
T
is called
standard
if its linear holonomy is trivial. It is
equivalent to say that
T
is
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/\Lambda
as an affine manifold, where
\Lambda
is an
integral lattice of rank
n
.

Remark 5.2:

Let
(M,\nabla)
be a standard flat affine torus. Then
\pi_{1}(M)
acts on
\tilde{M}

by translations, hence
\tilde{M}={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
M
is
isomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
with the standard flat affine
structure.

Remark 5.3:
In
[
TS
]
,
W. Thurston and D. Sullivan give examples of non-standard
flat affine structures on a torus. In
[
Bau
]
,
O. Baues discusses the deformation space of flat affine
structures on 2-torus.

Example 5.4:

Consider the quotient

M=\frac{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\backslash 0}{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}
acts by homotheties.
The linear holonomy of
M
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
acting
on
TM
by homotheties. This is also an
example of non complete affine manifold.

Example 5.5:

Consider the
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
-action
\rho
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
generated by

(x,y)\to(x+1,y)
and
(x,y)\to(x+y,y+1)
.
The projection to the second component maps
the quotient
X
to
S^{1}
, with the fiber
S^{1}
,
hence
X
is a compact manifold. It is diffeomorphic to a
torus
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
by construction. Its linear holonomy
is generated by
A(x,y):=(x+y,y)
.
Therefore this action defines a non-standard flat
affine structure on a torus.

5.2
Fried-Goldman-Hirsch theorem

The tools needed to show the non-existence of the exotic hypercomplex
structures on tori are provided by Fried, Goldman and
Hirsch, who laid out the groundwork in (
[
FGH
]
).

From
4.4
, the following is clear:

Proposition 5.6:
Any hypercomplex torus
M
is a complete affine manifold.

Proof:
Indeed,
M
is a quotient of
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
by
a discrete group preserving the affine structure.

Conjecture 5.7:

(“Marcus conjecture”,
[
A
]
)

A compact flat affine manifold is complete if and only if
it admits a parallel volume form.

Fried, Goldman and Hirsch proved
the Marcus conjecture, assuming that the affine holonomy
group is nilpotent.

Theorem 5.8:

Let
(M,\nabla)
be a compact flat affine manifold
with the affine holonomy group nilpotent. Then the following
are equivalent:

(a)

(M,\nabla)

is complete,

(b)

(M,\nabla)
admits a paralell volume
form, and

(c)

its linear holonomy action is unipotent.

Proof:
[
FGH
, Theorem A]
.

Remark 5.9:
5.2
can be applied to any
complete flat affine torus; it implies that its
linear holonomy is unipotent, unlike

5.1
.

Theorem 5.10:

Let
(M,I,J,K)
a hypercomplex torus and

\nabla
its Obata connection. If
(M,I)
admits a Kähler metric, then
(M,\nabla)
satisfies (a)-(c) of Fried-Goldman-Hirsch theorem.

Proof:
Since
(M,I)
is Kähler, it is HKT (
[
V4
]
).
Since its canonical bundle is trivial and
(M,I,J,K)
is
HKT, the Obata holonomy is contained in
SL(n,{\mathbb{H}})

(this observation was essentially proven in

[
V2
]
and spelled out explicitly in

[
V3
]
).
In other words,
\nabla
fixes a volume form.

5.3
Complete flat affine structures on real tori

We start from a classification of complete flat affine
structures on a torus.
Several arguments in the proofs rely on Maltsev’s work,
originally contained in
[
Mal
]
. We suggest to
consult
[
Ra
,
GO
]
for a
comprehensive description.

Theorem 5.11:

Let
(M,\nabla)
be a flat affine structure on a
compact torus. Assume that
\nabla
is complete.
Then

(i)

its linear holonomy is unipotent.

(ii)

For some real basis
t_{1},...,t_{n}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the
action
\Gamma:=\pi_{1}(M)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, considered
as a universal cover of
M
, is generated by

\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}
, with
\tau_{i}(x):=t_{i}+L_{i}(x)
.
Here
L_{1},...,L_{n}\in GL(n,{\mathbb{R}})
is a collection of commuting
unipotent matrices which satisfy

(L_{i}-\operatorname{Id})(t_{j})=(L_{j}-\operatorname{Id})(t_{i})

(5.1)

for any
i,j
.

(iii)

For any collection of commuting affine maps

\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}
with
\tau_{i}(x):=t_{i}+L_{i}(x)
,
with
t_{1},...,t_{n}
a basis and
all
L_{i}
unipotent and satisfying (
5.1
),
there exists a flat affine structure on a torus
M
with
\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}

generating the action of
\pi_{1}(M)
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Proof of (i):

The linear holonomy of
\nabla
is unipotent
by Fried-Goldman-Hirsch’s
5.2
,
because it is complete and
\pi_{1}(M)
is nilpotent.

Proof of (ii), Step 1:

Let us write the generators of
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(M)

as
\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}
, with
\tau_{i}(x):=L_{i}(x)+t_{i}
,
where
L_{1},...,L_{n}
. Clearly,
L_{i}
are commuting
and (by
5.2
) unipotent.
The equation (
5.1
)
follows because
\tau_{i}\tau_{j}(x)=L_{i}(t_{j})+L_{i}L_{j}(x)+t_{i}
,
which gives

0=[\tau_{i},\tau_{j}]=(L_{i}-\operatorname{Id})(t_{j})-(L_{j}-\operatorname{Id})(t_{i}).

To complete the proof of
(ii), it remains to show that
all the
t_{i}
are linearly independent.

Step 2:
Let
G
be the Maltsev completion of

{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}=\pi_{1}(M)
, identified with the
Zariski closure of
\pi_{1}(M)
in
\operatorname{Aff}(R^{n})

(
[
GO
]
).
Since
G
is a nilpotent simply connected
Lie group,
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a
diffeomorphism. Moreover, it is possible to
find a basis
(X_{i})
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\langle\exp(X_{1}),\dots,\exp(X_{n})\rangle=\rho({\mathbb{Z}}^{n})\subset G
.
The canonical left invariant connection
\nabla
on
G
is both left and right invariant, since the left action is equal to
the right action. It is torsion free (since

T(X,Y)=[X,Y]
) and flat, thus bestows
G
with a flat
affine structure. In particular the inverse map
\log:\;G\to{\mathfrak{g}}
coincides with the
development map
dev:G\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, explicitly:
g\in G
is
mapped to
g\cdot 0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Passing to the
differential

d_{e}(dev):\mathfrak{g}\to T_{0}{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, a tangent vector
X\in\mathfrak{g}
is identified with
\frac{d}{ds}\left(\exp(sX)\cdot 0\right)|_{s=0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. In particular,
setting
X_{i}=\log(\rho(z_{i}))
, we have

t_{i}=\exp(X_{i})\cdot 0=\frac{d}{ds}\left(\exp(sX_{i})\cdot 0\right){\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{s=0}}\right.}

i. e. under
d_{e}(dev)
every
X_{i}
is
identified with
t_{i}
. Then they are all linearly independent.
This conlcudes the proof of (ii).

Proof of (iii):

Let
V={\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
We need to show that the action of the group
\langle\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}\rangle\subset\operatorname{Aff}(V)
is free and cocompact.
Let
V_{0}\subset V_{1}\subset...\subset V
be a filtration
on
V
such that
L_{i}=\operatorname{Id}
on its associated graded space
V_{gr}

(such a filtration exists by Lie theorem, because all
L_{i}
are unipotent
and commute). Since the constant terms
t_{1},...,t_{n}

are a basis in
V
, the group
\langle\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}\rangle
acts on
V_{gr}

as a lattice, and the quotient
V_{gr}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
is homeomorphic
to a torus. Then the action of

\langle\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}\rangle
on
V
is free; indeed,
if it has a fixed point, it would have a fixed point
on the associated graded space. Identifying
V
with the
Maltsev completion of
\langle\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}\rangle

as in Step 2 above, we obtain that
\langle\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}\rangle

is a lattice in a commutative Lie group, hence the
quotient
V/\langle\tau_{1},...,\tau_{n}\rangle
is compact.

Remark 5.12:

In assumptions of
5.3
, let
\Psi
be the tensor
written in the basis
t_{1},...,t_{2n}
as

\Psi(u,v):=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2n}(A_{i})_{j}^{k}u^{i}v^{j}t_{k},

where
A_{i}=L_{i}-\operatorname{Id}
. Then
\Psi\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}V^{*}\otimes V
.
If, in addition, the flat connection preserves a
complex or a hypercomplex structure on a torus,
this would mean that
A_{i}\in GL(n,{\mathbb{C}})

or
A_{i}\in GL(n,{\mathbb{H}})
.

Example 5.13:
Let
W_{1},W_{2}
be complex
vector spaces. Take
V=W_{1}\oplus W_{2}

and let
A_{0}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(W_{1},W_{2})
be any
complex linear map. Define
A\in\operatorname{End}(V)
as
A{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{W_{1}}}\right.}=A_{0}
,
A{\left|{}_{{\phantom{|}\!\!}{}_{W_{2}}}\right.}=0
.
Write this matrix in a basis as
A:=\sum_{i,j}a_{j}^{i}t_{i}\otimes t^{j}
.
Take
\Psi:=\sum_{i,j}a_{j}^{i}t_{i}\otimes t_{i}\otimes t^{j}
.
This tensor is symmetric on first two variables
and complex linear on last two; also,
L:=-A+\operatorname{Id}
is
unipotent, defining a collection of
complex linear affine transforms satisfying
the assumptions of
5.3
(iii).

5.4
Exotic hypercomplex structures on tori do not exist

Theorem 5.14:

Exotic hypercomplex structures on tori do not exist.

Proof:
Let
(M,I,J,K,\nabla)
be
a hypercomplex structure and Obata
connection on a complex torus.
By
4.4
,

\nabla
is flat and complete, hence

5.3

can be applied. Let

\Psi\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(V)\otimes V^{*}
be the
(2,1)
-tensor obtained as
in
5.3
, which now by hypothesis
commutes with
I,J,K
on last two arguments.
This forces
\Psi
to be zero, since

K\Psi(x,y)=\Psi(IJx,y)=\Psi(Jx,Iy)=\Psi(x,JIy)=-K\Psi(x,y)

and
K
is a real automorphism of
V
.

Remark 5.15:
The argument which proves

5.4

also proves the uniqueness of Obata connection.
Indeed, we prove that, given a quaternionic vector space,
there are no tensors
\Psi\in V^{*}\otimes V^{*}\otimes V

which are symmetric in the first two variables and

{\mathbb{H}}
-linear on last two.
If
\nabla
is a torsion free and quaternionic connection, then
\nabla=\nabla^{\text{Ob}}+\Psi
and
\Psi\in\operatorname{Sym}^{2}{T^{*}M}\otimes TM
, commuting with
I,J,K
. Since
\Psi
vanishes everywhere,
\nabla=\nabla^{\text{Ob}}
, as shown in
[
Ob
]
.

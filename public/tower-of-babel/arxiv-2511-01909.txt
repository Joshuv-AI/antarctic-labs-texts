Forbidden Electron Transfer in the Adiabatic Limit of the Marcus-Inverted Region Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Ethan Abraham

Abstract
Here it is shown that in the adiabatic limit of condensed-phase electron transfer, the onset of barrierless transition occurs at a lower driving force than predicted by the non-adiabatic Marcus formulation. Furthermore, in the adiabatic limit of the Marcus-inverted region, isoenergetic electron transfer is strictly forbidden in the absence of nuclear tunneling. This "forbidden" behavior arises from a topological change in the mapping between the adiabatic and diabatic electronic surfaces, emerging precisely at the onset of the Marcus-inverted region.



Electron transfer (ET) processes underpin many areas of scientific and technological relevance such as batteries
Zhang et al. 2024
;
Zhao et al. 2023
, electrocatalysis
Qin et al. 2023
;
Zhang et al. 2020
, solar energy conversion
Brédas et al. 2004
, and biological redox
Marcus and Sutin 1985
. The physics underlying ET processes in the non-adiabatic limit has been illuminated by Marcus
Marcus 1956
;
Marcus 1964
;
Marcus 1965
. The theory argues that condensed-phase electronic transitions must satisfy energy conservation and also the Franck-Condon principle that ET occurs at fixed nuclear positions. This suggests that the reaction coordinate must describe the difference in solvent polarization between the initial and final electronic states. The further assumption that the free energy of a particular electronic state is harmonic in deviations from the equilibrium polarization of that state culminates in an expression for the activation barrier
Marcus 1956
;
Marcus 1964
;
Marcus 1965
.

A key assumption in many applications of Marcus theory has been that the process is non-adiabatic, which is in part because this assumption leads to elegant analytical expressions for the reaction rate based on the Fermi-Golden-Rule
Nitzan 2006
;
Chidsey 1991
. The enigmatic Marcus inverted region, in which the reaction rate is expected to decrease at extremely high driving force as a consequence of the harmonic potentials, has also been conventionally analyzed through this non-adiabatic perspective
Nitzan 2006
.

However, the observation of strongly adiabatic reactions obeying Marcus-Hush-Chidsey rate expressions
Lees et al. 2024
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Huang et al. 2021
;
Mayer 2011
;
Brown et al. 2020
;
Chidsey 1991
warrants reconsideration of the assumptions related to non-adiabadicity. In our recent work
Abraham et al. 2025a
, we have argued that the quantum level-repulsion between the diabatic Marcus states can play a significant and underappreciated role in the ET free-energy landscape, and that the experimentally relevant reorganization
\lambda_{\text{eff}}
may in fact be a distinct quantity from the theoretical diabatic curvature
\lambda
described in Marcus theory. We found that the two quantities are related in the case of constant coupling by

\lambda_{\text{eff}}=\lambda\left(1-\frac{2V}{\lambda}\right)^{2},

(1)

or in the more general case of reaction-coordinate-dependent coupling by

\lambda_{\rm eff}=\lambda-4V(q^{*})+\frac{4V(q_{\text{r}})^{2}}{\lambda}.

(2)

We denote by
V
the coupling between the electronic diabatic states when independent of the reaction coordinate
q
, and otherwise we denote by
V(q^{*})
and
V(q_{\text{r}})
the coupling value evaluated at the transition state and reaction minimum respectively. The activation barrier is given to a good approximation by

E^{*}=\frac{(\lambda_{\rm eff}+\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}}{4\lambda_{\rm eff}},

(3)

where
\Delta G^{\circ}
is the thermodynamic bias. It is obvious that this equation reduces to the standard Marcus activation barrier in the case where the coupling is negligible

\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\xrightarrow[V\to 0]{}\lambda,

(4)

but differences can be enormous when
V
is not much smaller than
\lambda.
For instance, for aqueous electroreduction of carbon dioxide on a gold electrode in the presence of potassium ions, the reorganization energy has been fitted experimentally by Zhang
et al.
as
\lambda_{\text{eff}}=0.75
eV
Zhang et al. 2020
, while Qin
et al.
have measured
\lambda=6.3
eV in
ab initio
simulations of the same system
Qin et al. 2023
. Using these values in Eq. (
1
) predicts
V=2.1
eV, which has been corroborated by our own recent
ab initio
calculation of the coupling value
Abraham et al. 2025b
. These results may be critical to quantitatively understand the kinetics of strongly adiabatic electrochemical reactions.

The previous analysis of Ref.
Abraham et al. 2025a
was confined to the adiabatic limit of the normal region, leaving open the corresponding behavior in the inverted region. This Letter addresses this case, extending our adiabatic model to address the inverted region. The theoretical finding is that there are three distinct regions of adiabatic ET characterized by categorically different behavior:

(i)
-\lambda_{\text{eff}}<\Delta G^{\circ}<\lambda_{\text{eff}}
: ET is governed by the activation barrier given by Eq. (
2
)–(
3
).

(ii)
-\lambda<\Delta G^{\circ}<-\lambda_{\text{eff}}
: ET is barrierless and will occur immediately.

(iii)
\Delta G^{\circ}<-\lambda
: ET in this inverted region is
forbidden
and can only be achieved via nuclear tunneling, which is generally negligible compared to typical finite-temperature rates.

To justify these findings, we proceed with the analysis presented in Ref.
Abraham et al. 2025a
, which represents the Marcus system in the basis of donor (D) and acceptor (A) diabatic states, parametrized by a solvent polarization coordinate
q
. The Hamiltonian is given by

H(q)=\begin{pmatrix}E_{\text{D}}(q)&V(q)\\
V(q)&E_{\text{A}}(q)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda q^{2}&V(q)\\
V(q)&\lambda(1-q)^{2}+\Delta G^{\circ}\end{pmatrix},

(5)

upon the diagonalization of which we obtain

\begin{split}E_{\pm}(q)&=\frac{\lambda(2q^{2}-2q+1)+\Delta G^{\circ}}{2}\pm\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(\lambda(2q-1)-\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}+4V(q)^{2}}\end{split}

(6)

where
q=0
and
q=1
are the minima of the donor and acceptor diabats respectively.

We now justify the propositions (i)–(iii). To show (i), we recall the proof given in Ref.
Abraham et al. 2025a
that Eq. (
3
) differs from the true activation barrier obtained from
E_{-}(q)
only on the order of correction terms
O\left(V^{n}(\Delta G^{\circ})^{3-n}/\lambda^{2}\right)
for
n=0,1,2,3
. Since in the adiabatic limit we have
\lambda_{\text{eff}}\ll\lambda,
Eq. (
3
) must hold for all
\Delta G^{\circ}<\lambda_{\text{eff}},
and so (i) is established by Ref.
Abraham et al. 2025a
.

To show (ii), we note that by the same argument as in (i), Eq. (
3
) must also hold when
\Delta G^{\circ}\approx\lambda_{\text{eff}},
and so Eq. (
3
) predicts that the transition becomes barrierless when
\Delta G^{\circ}=-\lambda_{\text{eff}},
which we confirm in Fig. 1(b) on the toy numerical model. As
\Delta G^{\circ}
is further increased, we notice that no local minimum corresponding to state
D
exists on the adiabatic ground state. The physical interpretation is that if the coupling is turned off (such as if the donor and acceptor species are sufficiently far apart), the system will reside on the donor
diabat
. But with the coupling turned on (such as if the species approach each other), the local minimum in the energy landscape ceases to exist, at which point the polarization coordinate will experience a force
-\partial E/\partial q
driving it to the minimum
A
of the adiabatic surface. In this case, there will be no ET limitation in the strict sense, and the rate will be limited only by any barriers for the species’ approach, which can in general involve reaction coordinates linearly independent from the ET coordinate
Abraham et al. 2025b
;
Bazant 2023
.

To show (iii), which may be the most remarkable and counterintuitive proposition of this Letter, we note that it is not guaranteed that when the coupling is turned, that the electron will end up on the lower energy adiabat, even in the strong coupling limit. If the reactant coordinate is not near the diabatic crossing when the coupling is turned on, it will remain on the adiabat that most closely resembles its initial diabat regardless of whether it is the higher or lower energy one. For the same reason that ET in Marcus theory is assumed to be an isoenergetic process satisfying the Franck Condon principle
Marcus 1956
, the system will remain trapped on the higher adiabat and no reaction will occur, with the exception of rare nuclear tunneling events
Hänggi et al. 1990
;
Nitzan 2006
. In this system, the criteria for the higher-energy adiabat coinciding with the
D
diabat is precisely the criteria of being in the inverted region of the diabatic Marcus picture, because this is the point at which the mapping between the diabats and adiabats is altered.

At this point, it should be noted that the separation between regions (ii) and (iii) is expected to be exact only in the
T\rightarrow 0
limit. When the temperature is finite, the probability of the system becoming trapped on the higher energy adiabat can be approximated by the Landau-Zener solution
Nitzan 2006
;
Zener 1932
. The probability of an ET at a crossing event is given by

P_{\text{ET}}=\text{exp}\left(-\frac{2\pi V^{2}}{\hbar\dot{q}\,|\Delta F|}\right),

(7)

where
\dot{q}=\partial q/\partial t
is related to the temperature and
\Delta F=(\partial E_{\text{A}}/\partial q-\partial E_{\text{D}}/\partial q)|_{q=q^{*}}.
Indeed, the latter can easily be evaluated from
E_{\text{D}}(q)
and
E_{\text{A}}(q)
given in Eq. (
5
) by substituting in the coordinate of the diabatic crossing
q^{*}=\frac{1}{2}(1+\Delta G^{\circ}/\lambda),
a standard result in Marcus theory. Interestingly, we obtain

|\Delta F|=2|\Delta G^{\circ}|\approx 2\lambda,

(8)

which shows how the probability of decays exponentially with the second order mixing parameter
V^{2}/\lambda,
the same parameter which was found in Ref.
Abraham et al. 2025a
to be an important omission in standard Marcus theory. That is,

P_{\text{ET}}\approx\text{exp}[-\alpha(V^{2}/\lambda)],~~\text{with}~\alpha=\pi/(\hbar\dot{q}).

(9)

The reaction rate in the adiabatic inverted region (iii) will therefore be

k_{\text{ET}}\approx\nu\exp\!\left\{-\frac{\alpha V^{2}}{\lambda}-\frac{(\lambda+\Delta G^{\circ})^{2}}{4\lambda k_{\mathrm{B}}T}\right\}.

(10)

where
\nu
is the classical attempt frequency. The adiabatic limit implies
\alpha V^{2}/\lambda\gg 1
, and so
k_{\text{ET}}\rightarrow 0
regardless of the value of the Marcus activation factor. This result reveals a previously unrecognized property of the Marcus inverted region: in addition to the fact that increasing the thermodynamic bias suppresses the reaction rate, so too does increasing the coupling.

Notably, as Marcus recalled in his Nobel lecture, experimental evidence for the inverted-region behavior was not obtained until
"almost 25 years after it was predicted"
but in that interim
"experimentally in some reactions 100% formation of an electronically excited state of a reaction product has been observed"

Marcus 1992
. Further, in a canonical review of ET theory by Barbara, Ratner, and Meyer, it is stated that
"there are additional problems with the simple form [of the Marcus-inverted activation barrier]…including an excessively rapid falloff of
k_{\text{ET}}
with
\Delta G^{\circ}
"

Barbara et al. 1996
. The results of this Letter suggest that such observations—or any experiments in which increasing the driving force leads not to a gradual suppression of the rate but to a sudden transition into an alternative reaction channel—should be revisited for possible reinterpretation. Likewise, cases where the rate saturates and becomes insensitive to changes in driving force may correspond to the extended region of barrierless transition (
-\lambda<\Delta G^{\circ}<-\lambda_{\text{eff}}
) discussed here. Finally, tunable Hamiltonian model systems, where both the driving force and electronic coupling can be systematically varied, may provide a route toward direct experimental testing of these predictions
Han et al. 2020
;
Shuai et al. 2020
;
Herrera and Krems 2011
;
Mei et al. 2013
;
Shuai et al. 2020

On free wreath products of classical groups Thanks: P.F. is supported by the CNRS IEA GAOA and the ANR CroCQG
Pierre Fima, Yigang Qiu

Abstract
. We study the generalized free wreath product of classical groups introduced by the first author and Arthur Troupel. We give an explicit computation of the Haar state and deduce important properties of their associated operator algebra: in many cases, the von Neumann algebra is a full type II 1 {\rm II}_{1} -factor and the reduced C*-algebra is simple with unique trace.



1.
Introduction

The free wreath product, introduced by Bichon in
[
2
]
to describe the quantum automorphism group of
N
disjoint copies of a given graph, has been a source of important examples in quantum group theory. It has later been generalized in various directions
[
11
,
4
,
12
,
6
,
7
,
5
]
. The more advanced construction from
[
5
]
produces, given any compact quantum groups
G,H
and an action
\beta\,:\,H\curvearrowright(B,\psi)
on a finite dimensional C*-algebra preserving a faithful state
\psi
, a compact quantum group
G\wr_{*,\beta}H
, called the generalized free wreath product and it generalizes the previous constructions. It also produces new interesting examples that do not fit in the previous constructions. One of them is the free wreath product of classical groups:
\Gamma
is a discrete group and
\Lambda
is a finite group with action
\Lambda\curvearrowright(C^{*}(\Lambda),\tau_{\Lambda})
by left translation, where
\tau_{\Lambda}
is the canonical trace on
C^{*}(\Lambda)
. Let us call
\mathbb{G}:=\widehat{\Gamma}\wr_{*,\beta}\Lambda
the generalized free wreath product. Most of the results from
[
5
]
, except the ones about approximation properties, are not applicable to
\mathbb{G}
, since
\Lambda
is assumed to be finite.

The goal of the present paper is to study in details the compact quantum group
\mathbb{G}
defined above and see if some of the results of
[
5
]
still hold. Let us give bellow the presentation of
\mathbb{G}
by generators and relations from
[
5
]
.
C(\mathbb{G})
is the universal unital C*-algebra generated by elements
\nu_{\gamma}(g)
for
\gamma\in\Lambda
and
g\in\Gamma
and by
C^{*}(\Lambda)
with relations,

•

(\nu_{\gamma}(g))^{*}=\nu_{\gamma^{-1}}(g^{-1})
and
\nu_{\gamma}(1)=\delta_{\gamma,1}1
for all
g\in\Gamma
and
\gamma\in\Lambda
.

•

\nu_{\gamma}(gh)=\sum_{r,s\in\Lambda,\,rs=\gamma}\nu_{r}(g)\nu_{s}(h)
, for all
g,h\in\Gamma
,
\gamma\in\Lambda
,

•

s\nu_{rs}(g)=\nu_{sr}(g)s
, for all
g\in\Gamma
,
r,s\in\Lambda
,

The comultiplication on
C(\mathbb{G})
is the unique unital
*
-homomorphism
\Delta\,:\,C(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
such that:

\Delta(\gamma)=\gamma\otimes\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(\nu_{\gamma}(g))=\sum_{r,s\in\Lambda,\,rs=\gamma}\nu_{r}(g)s\otimes\nu_{s}(g)\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\Lambda,\,g\in\Gamma.

It has been proved in
[
5
, Theorem A]
that
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
has the Haagurp property if and only if
\Gamma
has the Haagerup property,
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
is hyperlinear if and only if
\Gamma
is hyperlinear and
\mathbb{G}
is co-amenable if and only if
\Gamma=\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Our first result is an explicit combinatorial formula for the Haar state on
\mathbb{G}
, which is not known in the general setting of
[
5
]
. Given
n\geq 1
,
\vec{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
and
\vec{g}=(g_{1},\dots g_{n})\in\Gamma^{n}
define

\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}):=\nu_{\gamma_{1}}(g_{1})\dots\nu_{\gamma_{n}}(g_{n})\in C(\mathbb{G}).

Let
{\rm NC}(n)
be the set of non-crossing partitions on
\{1,\dots,n\}
. We denote by
{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma})
the set of
\pi\in{\rm NC}(n)
such that, for each block
\{r_{1}<\dots<r_{s}\}\in\pi
one has
\gamma_{r_{1}}\dots\gamma_{r_{s}}=1
. We use the same notation
{\rm NC}(\vec{g})
for
\vec{g}\in\Gamma^{n}
and we also write
\vec{g}|_{V}:=(g_{r_{1}},\dots,g_{r_{s}})
. Define, for
\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})
and
V\in\pi
,
\mu_{V}(\vec{g}|_{V}):=\sum_{\sigma\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g}_{\restriction V})}\mu(\sigma,1_{|V|})
and
\mu_{\pi}(\vec{g}):=\prod_{V\in\pi}\mu_{V}(\vec{g}|_{V})
, where
\mu
be the Möbius function on
{\rm NC}(n)
(see
[
10
]
).

Theorem A
.

The Haar state
h\in C(\mathbb{G})^{*}
is the unique state such that, for all
n\geq 1
,
\vec{\gamma}\in\Lambda^{n}
,
g\in\Gamma^{n}
with
g_{i}\neq 1
for all
i
, and all
s\in\Lambda
, one has:

h(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}({\vec{g}})s)=\delta_{s,1}\sum_{\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})\cap{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma})}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(\vec{g})}{|\Lambda|^{n-|\pi|}}.

Using the Haar state formula, we can study the structure of the reduced C*-algebra
C_{r}(\mathbb{G})
and the von Neumann algebra
{\rm L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
.

Theorem B
.

If
\Gamma
is icc and
\Lambda
has trivial center then
C_{r}(\mathbb{G})
is simple with unique trace and
{\rm L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is a full
{\rm II}_{1}
-factor.

We actually prove a more general result, without assuming that
\Lambda
has trivial center in which case
{\rm L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})
is not a factor but we can compute its center, which is
{\rm L}(\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda))
, whenever
\Lambda
is non-trivial.

2.
Preliminaries

The same symbol
\otimes
will be used to denote the tensor product of Hilbert spaces as well as the minimal tensor product of C*-algebras.

2.1.
Crossed products

Let
(M,\omega)
be a von Neumann algebra with a faithful normal state
\omega
and
\alpha\,:\,\Lambda\curvearrowright M
a state preserving action of a discrete countable group
\Lambda
. We recall that the crossed product
M\rtimes\Lambda
is the unique, up to a canonical isomorphism, von Neumann algebra
N
satisfying the following properties:

•

M\subset N
is a von Neumann subalgebra and there is a group homomorphism
\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(N)
,
\gamma\mapsto u_{\gamma}
such that
u_{\gamma}xu_{\gamma}^{*}=\alpha_{\gamma}(x)
for all
\gamma\in\Lambda
and
x\in M
.

•

N=\left(M\cup u(\Lambda)\right)^{\prime\prime}
.

•

There is a faithful normal conditional expectation
E\,:\,N\rightarrow M
such that

E(xu_{\gamma})=\delta_{\gamma,1}x\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\Lambda,\,x\in M.

We use the standard notation
N=M\rtimes\Lambda
. Then, the faithful normal state
\widetilde{\omega}:=\omega\circ E
is called
the dual state
. It is the unique f.n.s. on
M\rtimes\Lambda
satisfying
\widetilde{\omega}(xu_{\gamma})=\delta_{\gamma,1}\omega(x)
.

The following simple Lemma is well-known, we include a proof for the reader’s convenience. Let
\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda):=\{r\in\Lambda:rs=sr\text{ for all }s\in\Lambda\}
be the center of
\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma<\Lambda
be a subgroup of the kernel of the action
\Lambda\curvearrowright M
. If
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda)
then, there is a canonical isomorphism
M\rtimes\Lambda\simeq\left(M\rtimes\Lambda/\Sigma\right)\otimes{\rm L}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is a subgroup of the kernel of the action, we may consider the crossed product
M\rtimes\Lambda/\Sigma=\langle M,u_{g\Sigma}\,:\,g\in\Lambda\rangle
and we define
N:=\left(M\rtimes\Lambda/\Sigma\right)\otimes{\rm L}(\Sigma)
. We view
M\subset N
via the map
x\mapsto x\otimes 1
. Let
\{g_{n}\,:\,n\in I\}\subset\Lambda
be a complete set of representatives of
\Lambda/\Sigma
, with
I\subset\mathbb{N}
,
0\in I
,
g_{0}=1
. Write
\Lambda=\sqcup_{i\in I}g_{i}\Sigma
and write any
g\in\Lambda
as
g=g_{i}\sigma
, where
i\in I
and
\sigma\in\Sigma
are uniquely determined. Then, since
\Sigma<\mathcal{Z}(\Lambda)
, the map
\psi\,:\,\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda/\Sigma\times\Sigma
,
g=g_{i}\sigma\mapsto(g_{i},\sigma)
is a group isomorphism. Consider now the group homomorphism
v\,:\,\Lambda/\Sigma\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(N)
defined by
v_{(g\Sigma,\sigma)}=u_{g\Sigma}\otimes\lambda_{\sigma}
. Then,
v\circ\psi\,:\,\Lambda\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(N)
is a group homomorphism such that
v_{\psi(g)}(x\otimes 1)v_{\psi(g)}^{*}=u_{g\Sigma}xu_{g\Sigma}^{*}\otimes 1=\alpha_{g}(x)\otimes 1
. Consider the canonical normal faithful conditional expectation
E\,:\,M\rtimes\Lambda/\Sigma\rightarrow M
and define
E^{\prime}:=E\otimes\tau_{\Sigma}
. Note that
E^{\prime}
is a faithful normal conditional expectation onto
M
and one has
E^{\prime}(xv_{\psi(g)})=E(xu_{g_{i}})\tau_{\Sigma}(\sigma)
, for
g=g_{i}\sigma
. Hence,
E^{\prime}(xv_{\psi(g)})=\delta_{g,1}x
. We conclude the proof using the universal property of the crossed product.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The same result holds for C*-algebraic full/reduced crossed-products, by using the universal property of C*-algebraic full/reduced crossed-products.

Free probability

Definition 2.3
.

Then

We fix once and for all a unital algebra

Definition 2.4
.

The corresponding

(\kappa_{\pi})_{\pi\in{\rm NC}}

For each

3.
Universal quantum homomorphisms

In this section we provide a new approach on universal quantum homomorphisms and, in the case of universal quantum homomorphisms from
C^{*}(\Gamma)
to
C^{*}(\Lambda)

\Gamma,\Lambda
are two discret groups and
\Lambda

[

]

3.1.
Generalites on universal quantum homomorphisms

Let
A
,
B
be two unital C*-algebras. Whenever
B
is finite dimensional there exists a unique, up to a canonical isomorphism, pair
(\rho,\mathcal{U})
of a unital C*-algebra
\mathcal{U}
and a unital
*
-homomorphism
\rho\,:\,A\rightarrow B\otimes\mathcal{U}
such that, for any pair
(\rho^{\prime},\mathcal{U}^{\prime})
of a unital C*-algebra
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
and a unital

\rho^{\prime}\,:\,A\rightarrow B\otimes\mathcal{U}^{\prime}

*
-homomorphism
\pi\,:\,\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
such that
({\rm id}\otimes\pi)\rho=\rho^{\prime}
. Following
[
3
]
, the morphism
\rho
is called
the universal quantum homomorphism from
A
to
B
. Both the morphism
\rho
and the C*-algebra

are studied in details in
[
3
]

Write

, where

is the unique trace on

such that
{\rm Tr}_{\kappa}(1)=N_{\kappa}
. Let
(H,\pi,\xi)
be the GNS construction of

Lemma 3.1
.

If
A^{\prime\prime}

\rho^{\prime\prime}(A^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}\cap(B\otimes\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime})\subset\mathcal{Z}(B)\otimes 1.

Proof.

We use freely the results from

3

. Let now
x\in\rho^{\prime\prime}(A^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}\cap(B\otimes\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime})

(\chi_{\kappa}\otimes{\rm id})(x)\in\rho_{\kappa}(A^{\prime\prime})^{\prime}\cap(M_{N_{\kappa}}(\mathbb{C})\otimes\mathcal{U}^{\prime\prime})
. From case

4.
Free wreath product and crossed-product

4.1.
Generalities on compact quantum groups

For a discrete group
\Gamma
we denote by
C^{*}(\Gamma)
its full C*-algebra,
C^{*}_{r}(\Gamma)
its reduced C*-algebra and
{\rm L}(\Gamma)
its von Neumann algebra. We briefly recall below some elements of the compact quantum group (CQG) theory developed by Woronowicz
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

For a CQG
G
, we denote by
C(G)
its
maximal
C*-algebra, which is the enveloping C*-algebra of the unital
*
-algebra
{\rm Pol}(G)
given by the linear span of coefficients of irreducible unitary representations of
G
. The set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations will be denoted by
{\rm Irr}(G)
. We will denote by
\varepsilon_{G}\,:\,C(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
the counit of
G
which satisfies (
{\rm id}\otimes\varepsilon_{G})(u)={\rm{\rm id}}_{H}
for all finite dimensional unitary representations
u\in\mathcal{B}(H)\otimes C(G)
.

We denote by
h\in C(G)^{*}
the Haar state of
G
. It is the unique state on
C(G)
such that
(h\otimes{\rm id})\Delta=h(\cdot)1
. It is also the unique state such that
({\rm id}\otimes h)\Delta=h(\cdot)1
.

4.2.
A generalized free wreath product

We recall bellow the main object of study, the free wreath product of classical groups, as defined in
[
5
, Section 3.3.4]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite group and
\Gamma
a discrete group. Consider the
\widehat{\Lambda}
-action on the finite dimensional C*-algebra
B:=C^{*}(\Lambda)
by left translation i.e.
\beta\,:\,C^{*}(\Lambda)\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes C^{*}(\Lambda)
,
\beta(\gamma)=\gamma\otimes\gamma
for all
\gamma\in\Lambda
and note that
\beta
is
\tau
-preserving, where
\tau
is the canonical trace of
C^{*}(\Lambda)
(i.e. the Haar state of
\widehat{\Lambda}
). Consider
C(\mathbb{G})
the universal unital C*-algebra generated by elements
\nu_{\gamma}(g)
for
\gamma\in\Lambda
and
g\in\Gamma
and by
C^{*}(\Lambda)
with relations,

•

(\nu_{\gamma}(g))^{*}=\nu_{\gamma^{-1}}(g^{-1})
and
\nu_{\gamma}(1)=\delta_{\gamma,1}1
for all
g\in\Gamma
and
\gamma\in\Lambda
.

•

\nu_{\gamma}(gh)=\sum_{r,s\in\Lambda,\,rs=\gamma}\nu_{r}(g)\nu_{s}(h)
, for all
g,h\in\Gamma
,
\gamma\in\Lambda
,

•

s\nu_{rs}(g)=\nu_{sr}(g)s
, for all
g\in\Gamma
,
r,s\in\Lambda
,

By the universal property, there exists a unique unital
*
-morphism
\Delta\,:\,C(\mathbb{G})\rightarrow C(\mathbb{G})\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
such that:

\Delta(\gamma)=\gamma\otimes\gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta(\nu_{\gamma}(g))=\sum_{r,s\in\Lambda,\,rs=\gamma}\nu_{r}(g)s\otimes\nu_{s}(g)\quad\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Lambda,\,g\in\Gamma.

It is shown in
[
5
]
that
(C(\mathbb{G}),\Delta)
is a compact quantum group.

We now give a new description of the C*-algebra
C(\mathbb{G})
in terms of crossed-product. Following Section
3.2
, let
\mathcal{U}
be the universal unital C*-algebra generated by the coefficients of a unital
*
-homomorphism
\rho\,:\,C^{*}(\Gamma)\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{U}
. Note that, for each
r\in\Lambda
, the universal property of
\mathcal{U}
, applied the to unital
*
-homomorphism
\rho^{(r)}\,:\,C^{*}(\Gamma)\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{U}
defined by
\rho^{(r)}=({\rm Ad}(r)\otimes{\rm id})\rho
, where
{\rm Ad}(r)\,:\,C^{*}(\Lambda)\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)
is defined by
{\rm Ad}(r)(s)=s^{-1}rs
, gives a unique unital
*
-homomorphism
\alpha_{r}\,:\,\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
such that

({\rm id}\otimes\alpha_{r})\rho=\rho^{(r)}\quad\text{for all }r\in\Lambda.

In the sequel, we write, for all
a\in C^{*}(\Gamma)
,
\rho(a)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\gamma\otimes\rho_{\gamma}(a)
, where
\rho_{\gamma}(a)\in\mathcal{U}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The map
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}
is an action
\Lambda
on
\mathcal{U}
by automorphisms and there is a unique isomorphism
\psi\,:\,\mathcal{U}\rtimes_{\alpha}\Lambda\rightarrow C(\mathbb{G})
such that

\psi(r)=r\text{ for all }r\in\Lambda\text{ and }\psi(\rho_{\gamma}(g))=\nu_{\gamma}(g)\text{ for all }\gamma\in\Lambda,\,g\in\Gamma,

where
\mathcal{U}\rtimes_{\alpha}\Lambda
denotes the full crossed product, generated by
\mathcal{U}
and
C^{*}(\Lambda)
with the relations
rar^{-1}=\alpha_{r}(a)
for all
a\in\mathcal{U}
and
r\in\Lambda
.

Proof.

Since
{\rm Ad}(1)={\rm id}
, it follows that
\rho^{(1)}=\rho
hence, from the uniqueness of
\alpha_{1}
that
\alpha_{1}={\rm id}_{\mathcal{U}}
. Now, since
{\rm Ad}(rs)={\rm Ad}(s)\circ{\rm Ad}(r)
we have

({\rm id}\otimes\alpha_{rs})\rho=\rho^{(rs)}=({\rm Ad}(s)\otimes{\rm id})\rho^{(r)}=({\rm Ad}(s)\otimes\alpha_{r})\rho=({\rm id}\otimes\alpha_{r})\rho^{(s)}=({\rm id}\otimes\alpha_{r}\alpha_{s})\rho.

Again, uniqueness of
\alpha_{rs}
implies that
\alpha_{rs}=\alpha_{r}\circ\alpha_{s}
. Hence,
r\mapsto\alpha_{r}
is an action of
\Lambda
on
\mathcal{U}
by automorphisms.

Let us now show the existence of
\psi
. We note that, the first two relations defining
C(\mathbb{G})
imply that
\nu\,:\,\Gamma\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
,
g\in\Gamma\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\gamma\otimes\nu_{\gamma}(g)
is a unitary representation of
\Gamma
, hence extends to a unital
*
-homomorphism
\nu\,:\,C^{*}(\Gamma)\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes C(\mathbb{G})
. By the universal property of
\mathcal{U}
, there is a unique unital
*
-homomorphism
\psi_{0}\,:\,A\rightarrow C(\mathbb{G})
such that
({\rm id}\otimes\psi_{0})\rho=\nu
i.e.
\psi_{0}(\rho_{\gamma}(g))=\nu_{\gamma}(g)
for all
\gamma\in\Lambda
,
g\in\Gamma
. Note that, for all
g\in\Gamma
and
r\in\Lambda
,

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\psi_{0}\circ\alpha_{r})\rho(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\rm id}\otimes\psi_{0})\rho(g)=({\rm Ad}(r)\otimes\psi_{0})\rho(g)=({\rm Ad}(r)\otimes{\rm id})\nu(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}r\gamma r^{-1}\otimes\nu_{\gamma}(g)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\gamma\otimes\nu_{r\gamma r^{-1}}(g)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Lambda}\gamma\otimes r\nu_{\gamma}(g)r,

where we used the last relation defining
C(\mathbb{G})
in the last equality above. We deduce that
\psi_{0}\circ\alpha_{r}(a)=r\psi_{0}(a)r^{-1}
, for all
a\in A
and
r\in\Lambda
. The existence of
\psi
now follows from the universal property of the full crossed product.

Let us now construct the inverse of
\psi
. Since
\rho\,:\,C^{*}(\Gamma)\rightarrow C^{*}(\Lambda)\otimes\mathcal{U}
is a unital
*
-homomorphism, the elements
\rho_{\gamma}(g)\in\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{U}\rtimes_{\alpha}\Lambda
do satisfy the same relations as the elements
\nu_{\gamma}(g)
. Moreover, the last relation
s\rho_{rs}(g)=\rho_{sr}(g)s
follows from the relations in the crossed product since, by construction, we have
\alpha_{r}(\rho_{\gamma}(g))=\rho_{r\gamma r^{-1}}(g)
. By the universal property of the full crossed product, we get the inverse of
\psi
.∎

5.
The Haar state

While the Haar state on a generalized free wreath product has been computed in
[
5
]
, we give below a more explicit and combinatorial formula in the case of a free wreath product of classical groups.

Given
n\geq 1
,
\vec{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
and
\vec{g}=(g_{1},\dots g_{n})\in\Gamma^{n}
define

\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}):=\nu_{\gamma_{1}}(g_{1})\dots\nu_{\gamma_{n}}(g_{n})\in C(\mathbb{G}).

We denote by
{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma})
the set of non-crossing partitions
\pi\in{\rm NC}(n)
such that, for each block
\{r_{1}<\dots<r_{s}\}\in\pi
one has
\gamma_{r_{1}}\dots\gamma_{r_{s}}=1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The Haar state
h\in C(\mathbb{G})^{*}
is the unique state such that, for all
n\geq 1
,
\vec{\gamma}\in\Lambda^{n}
,
g\in\Gamma^{n}
with
g_{i}\neq 1
for all
i
, and all
s\in\Lambda
, one has:

h(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}({\vec{g}})s)=\delta_{s,1}\sum_{\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})\cap{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma})}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(\vec{g})}{|\Lambda|^{n-|\pi|}}.

Proof.

Identifying, by Proposition
4.2
,
C(\mathbb{G})
with the crossed product
\mathcal{U}\rtimes\Lambda
, where
\rho_{\gamma}(g)\in\mathcal{U}
is identify with
\nu_{\gamma}(g)\in C(\mathbb{G})
, it suffices to show, by Theorem
3.3
, that the dual state
h
of
\widetilde{\omega}\in\mathcal{U}^{*}
, in the crossed product sense, is
\Delta
-invariant (either left or right).

For
\vec{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}),\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
define:

\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}):=(d_{1},\dots,d_{n}):=(\gamma_{1}s_{1}^{-1},s_{1}\gamma_{2}(s_{1}s_{2})^{-1},\dots,(s_{1}\dots s_{n-1})\gamma_{n}(s_{1}\dots s_{n})^{-1}),

so that we have
d_{1}\dots d_{n}=\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n}(s_{1}\dots s_{n})^{-1}
. Note that, by the relations in
C(\mathbb{G})
and the definition of
\Delta
one has:

\Delta(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))=\sum_{\vec{s}\in\Lambda^{n}}\nu_{\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})}(\vec{g})s_{1}\dots s_{n}\otimes\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g}).

It follows that
(h\otimes{\rm id})(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))=\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n}}\widetilde{\omega}(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})}(\vec{g}))\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g})
,
where
I_{n}:=\{\vec{s}\in\Lambda^{n}:s_{1}s_{2}\dots s_{n}=e\}
.

By Theorem
3.3
one has:

(h\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))=\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n}}\sum_{\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})\cap{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(\vec{g})}{|\Lambda|^{n-|\pi|}}\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g}).

Note that if
\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n}\neq 1
then
{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma})=\emptyset
and, for all
\vec{s}\in I_{n}
, and
{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))=\emptyset
. It follows that
(h\otimes{\rm id})(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))=0=h(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))
and we may and will assume that
\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{n}=1
. Also,
(h\otimes{\rm id})(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))=0=h(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))
as soon as
g_{1}\dots g_{n}\neq 1
and we may and will assume that
g_{1}\dots g_{n}=1
as well. For
V\in\pi
, write
\pi\setminus V:=\cup_{W\in\pi,W\neq V}W\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
. We will view
\pi\setminus\{V\}
as a non-crossing partition on
\pi\setminus V
.

Claim.

Let
\vec{g}\in\Lambda^{n}
and
\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})
with a consecutive block
V\in\pi
,
|V|\geq 2
i.e.
V=\{k+1,\dots,k+m\}
for some
k\geq 0
,
m\geq 2
and
k+m\leq n
. Then,
\forall\vec{\gamma}\in I_{n}
,

\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n}}1_{{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))}(\pi)\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n-m}}1_{{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}(\vec{s}))}(\pi\setminus\{V\})\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g}|_{\pi\setminus V})&\text{if }\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V}=1,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

with the obvious conventions when
k=0
or
k+m=n
.

Proof of the Claim.
Write
\{1,\dots,n\}=V_{-}\sqcup V\sqcup V_{+}
where
V_{-}:=\{1,\dots,k\}
and
V_{+}:=\{k+m+1,\dots,n\}
so that
\pi\setminus V=V_{-}\sqcup V_{+}
. For
\vec{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})\in I_{n}
, write
\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})=(d_{1},\dots,d_{n})
, where
d_{t}=(\prod_{i=1}^{t-1}s_{i})\gamma_{t}(\prod_{i=1}^{t}s_{i})^{-1}
. Then,

\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})|_{V}=d_{k+1}\dots d_{k+m}=(\prod_{i=1}^{k}s_{i})(\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V})(\prod_{i=1}^{k+m}s_{i})^{-1}.

Hence, if
\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))
then,
\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V}=\prod_{V}\vec{s}|_{V}
. Let
W\in\pi
,
W\neq V
. Write
W=\{w_{1}<\dots<w_{r}\}
. If
W\subset V_{-}
then,
w_{r}\leq k
and we have
\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})|_{W}=\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}(\vec{s}|_{\pi\setminus V})|_{W}
. If
W\subset V_{+}
then
w_{1}\geq k+m+1
and we have, with
a:=s_{k+1}\dots s_{k+m}
,

\displaystyle\prod_{W}\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})|_{W}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{w_{1}}\dots d_{w_{r}}=(\prod_{i=1}^{w_{1}-1}s_{i})\gamma_{w_{1}}(\prod_{i=1}^{w_{1}}s_{i})^{-1}\dots(\prod_{i=1}^{w_{r}-1}s_{i})\gamma_{w_{r}}(\prod_{i=1}^{w_{r}}s_{i})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a(\prod_{i=k+m+1}^{w_{1}-1}s_{i})\gamma_{w_{1}}(\prod_{i=w_{1}+1}^{w_{2}-1}s_{i})\gamma_{w_{2}}\dots(\prod_{i=w_{r-1}+1}^{w_{r}-1}s_{i})\gamma_{w_{r}}(\prod_{i=k+m+1}^{n}s_{i})^{-1}a^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\prod_{W}\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}(\vec{s}|_{\pi\setminus V})|_{W}a^{-1}.

Finally, if both
W\cap V_{-}\neq\emptyset
and
W\cap V_{+}\neq\emptyset
then there exists
1\leq t\leq r
such that
w_{t}\leq k
and
w_{t+1}\geq k+m+1
. In that case we have, using the two previous cases and writing
W_{-}:=W\cap V_{-}=\{w_{1}<\dots<w_{t}\}
and
W_{+}:=W\cap V_{+}=\{w_{t+1}\dots,w_{n}\}
we have:

\prod_{W}\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})|_{W}=\prod_{W_{-}}\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}(\vec{s}|_{\pi\setminus V})|_{W_{-}}a\prod_{W_{+}}\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}(\vec{s}|_{\pi\setminus V})|_{W_{+}}a^{-1}.

It follows that, for any
\vec{s}\in I_{n}
, writing
\vec{s^{\prime}}:=(s_{1},\dots,s_{k+m},as_{k+m+1},s_{k+m+2},\dots,s_{n})
we have, for any block
W\in\pi
,
W\neq V

\prod_{W}\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s})|_{W}=\prod_{W}\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}(\vec{s^{\prime}}|_{\pi\setminus V})|_{W}
.

Write and element
\vec{x}\in\Lambda^{n}
as
\vec{x}=(\vec{s}_{-},\vec{s},\vec{s}_{+})
, where
\vec{s}_{-}:=\vec{x}|_{V_{-}}
,
\vec{s}:=\vec{x}|_{V}
and
\vec{s}_{+}:=\vec{x}|_{V_{+}}
. Let
E_{\pi}:=\{s\in I_{n}:\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))\}
and:

E_{\pi}^{\prime}:=\left\{(\vec{s}_{-},\vec{s},\vec{s}_{+})\in\Lambda_{n}\,:(\vec{s}_{-},\vec{s}_{+})\in I_{n-m},\,\pi\setminus\{V\}\in{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}((\vec{s}_{-},\vec{s}_{+}))\text{ and }\prod_{V}\vec{s}=\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V}\right\}

By the discussion above, the map
\psi\,:\,E_{\pi}\rightarrow E^{\prime}_{\pi}
,
\vec{s}\mapsto\vec{s^{\prime}}
is a bijection. Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n}}1_{{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))}(\pi)\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(s_{-},s_{+})\in I_{n-m}}1_{{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}|_{\pi\setminus V}((\vec{s}_{-},\vec{s}_{+}))}\nu_{\vec{s}_{-}}(\vec{g}|_{V_{-}})X\nu_{\vec{s}_{+}}(g|_{V_{+}}),

where:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{s_{k+1}\dots s_{k+m}=\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V}}\nu_{s_{k+1}}(g_{k+1})\dots\nu_{s_{k+m}}(g_{k+m})=\nu_{\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V}}(g_{k+1}\dots g_{k+m})=\nu_{\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V}}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{\prod_{V}\vec{\gamma}|_{V},1},

by the relations in
C(\mathbb{G})
and since
\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})
. The formula of the Claim follows.

∎

End of the Proof of Theorem
5.1
.
One has, for all
\vec{\gamma}\in\Lambda^{n}
and all
\vec{g}\in\Gamma^{n}
,

\displaystyle(h\otimes{\rm id})\Delta(\nu_{\vec{\gamma}}(\vec{g}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})}\frac{\mu_{\pi}(\vec{g})}{|\Lambda|^{n-|\pi|}}\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n}}1_{{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))}(\pi)\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g})

Hence, it suffices to show that, for all
\pi\in{\rm NC}(\vec{g})
,

Let us prove this formula by induction on
|\pi|
. If

so, since we assumed that

, we have

, we have

for all

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{s}\in I_{n}}1_{{\rm NC}(\vec{\gamma}(\vec{s}))}(\pi)\nu_{\vec{s}}(\vec{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

. Assume now that

or,

6.1.
The von Neumann algebra

Theorem 6.1
.

Corollary 6.4

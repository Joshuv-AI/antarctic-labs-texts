Comparison of Chemico-Physical Properties and Calcium Ion Release of Bioceramic Root Canal Sealers: A Pilot In vitro Study
Authors: Rose S. Avaran, V. P. Prabath Singh, Sapna C. Mudappa, Anju Varughese, Asha Joseph, P. Gayathri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Objective:The study aimed to determine the calcium ion release (Ca-IR) of three root canal sealers (RCSs) and to compare their chemico-physical properties, such as flow, pH, and working time, with those of the gold standard (resin sealer).Materials and Methods:Polyethylene tubes with the respective sealers were tested for Ca-IR using an atomic absorption spectrophotometer and pH. The total sample size was 18. Flow and working time were evaluated following ISO 6876 (2012) guidelines.Results:SafeEndo Bioactive RCS exhibited the highest pH, and Bio-C Angelus sealer demonstrated the highest calcium ion release. The flow and working time of all tested sealers were within the ISO 6876 standards.Conclusion:In this study, Bio-C Angelus demonstrated favorable physicochemical properties concerning prolonged Ca-IR. In terms of flow and working time, all three sealers followed ISO standards. These findings suggest that utilizing bioceramic sealers would improve the long-term outcome of the endodontic therapy.

INTRODUCTION

A root canal sealer (RCS) is employed to occlude the lateral and accessory canals, anatomical irregularities, and to seal the space between the dentinal wall and the gutta-percha core materials.[1] A newer category of RCS employing tricalcium silicate has evolved, characterized by bioactivity and the ability to release calcium ions, thereby creating hydroxyapatite-like crystals following interaction with tissue fluids.[2]

The alkalinity of sealers correlates with the antibacterial efficacy and formation of mineralized tissue. Consequently, both pH and calcium ion release (Ca-IR) are essential determinants in the effectiveness of root canal (RC) therapy.[3] The flow rate of a RCS is a critical element affecting the outcome of RC therapy. An increased flow rate is associated with the filling of RC irregularities, accessory canals, and isthmuses.[4] AH Plus is considered the standard by which newer sealers are evaluated. However, notwithstanding its benefits, the cytotoxic nature of AH Plus remains a significant issue.[5]

This study intended to assess the pH and Ca-IR of bioceramic RC sealers and to contrast their flow and working time with the established resin RC sealer. The findings from this research would assist clinicians in comprehending the characteristics of various sealers and making appropriate clinical decisions.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This study assessed two brands of bioceramic sealers (SafeEndo Bioactive RCS, Angelus Bio-C) and one brand of epoxy resin-based RCS (AH Plus).

A polyethylene tube was segmented into 18 sections; each section measured 5 millimeters in diameter and 3 millimeters in height. The sample was thereafter separated into three experimental groups, with each group containing six tubes that were filled with the corresponding sealers:

Group SEB: Safe Endo Bioactive RCS (SafeEndo Dental, India);

Group ANG: Angelus Bio-C (Angelus, Londrina, Brazil);

Group AH: Resin Sealer (AH Plus Dentsply, Germany).

The three primary groups were subdivided into two subgroups, each consisting of three participants, according to the testing intervals of the first and fourteenth days. Following the manufacturer’s recommendations, sealers were introduced into the tubes, which were thereafter placed in an incubation chamber at 37°C with 100% humidity until fully cured.

To determine the pH and Ca-IR, the sealers were injected into the tubes using 1-mL syringes until loaded. Every sample was positioned in a flask with 20 mL of deionized water and incubated at 37°C. The pH assessment was performed following one day and 14 days of immersion, utilizing a validated digital pH meter.

The assessment of Ca-IR was carried out at the same experimental interval established for pH evaluation (first and 14th days) using an atomic absorption spectrophotometer (AAS) (AA-6300 Shimadzu). The water specimens comprising leached calcium ions from the RCS were aspirated into the spectrophotometer and assessed for absorbance at the pertinent wavelength for calcium (422.7 nm). The concentration of calcium ions was usually expressed in milligrams per liter or parts per million. The flow and working time were evaluated following ISO 6876 (2012) standards.

Statistical analysis

The statistical evaluation was performed with IBM SPSS version 20 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as median (Q1, Q3) for skewed distributions and mean (SD) for data with normal distributions. The Shapiro–Wilk test was applied to analyze the normality of the distribution. The Kruskal–Wallis test, accompanied by the Games–Howell post hoc test, was applied to determine the variations in calcium, pH, and flow between the groups to be statistically significant. To evaluate the statistical significance of the variations in mean working time between three groups, a one-way ANOVA test then followed by a Bonferroni correction test was applied. The significance level was set at less than or equal to 0.05.

RESULTS

The median of calcium changes among the ANG, AH, SEB group was 13.6, −0.6, 3.5, respectively [Table 1]. The median change of calcium ion diffusion was higher among ANG group compared to other groups. The results showed statistical significance (P < 0.001). On comparing the intergroup median change of calcium ion diffusion, the results revealed statistically significant changes (P < 0.001). The average change of working time was lower among ANG (111.2 ± 5.43 min) compared to AH (237.7 ± 6.34 min) and SEB (270 ± 4.73 min) groups [Figure 1]. The results revealed statistical significance (P < 0.001).

DISCUSSION

Calcium silicate-based RCS is noted for its alkaline pH and Ca-IR, which promote the generation of apatite-like formations on the RCS surfaces when interacting with body fluids, hence increasing bioactivity.[6] Furthermore, it accelerates apical healing and tissue mineralization, resulting in bacteriostatic actions.[78] Premixed calcium silicate-associated bioactive RCS (SafeEndo Bioactive RCS, Angelus Bio-C) has been shown to have better handling properties (flow, working duration) than resin-based RCS (AH Plus).[7] According to Souza et al.,[9] the higher concentration of calcium silicate, calcium sulphate, and magnesium components in the Bio-C RCS accounted for a more substantial Ca-IR in atomic absorption spectrophotometry.

This study noticed that the flow of both calcium silicate-based RCSs surpassed that of AH Plus. This is due to the existence of calcium aluminate in bioceramic sealers, which generates ettringite as a precipitate amid the exothermic hydration reaction, raising the temperature during the testing process. This reduces the mixture’s viscosity, allowing the sealers to flow more freely under pressure.[10] All sealers assessed in this research exhibited flow values of more than 17 mm, following the ISO 6876 recommendation. Furthermore, the greater content of tricalcium aluminate in Bio-C RCS sets rapidly and requires reduced working time.[1112]

Based on the findings, the group ANG has a shorter working time, appropriate flow, and higher calcium ion diffusion when contrasted with group SEB RC sealers and AH Plus.

CONCLUSION

Given the limitations of the investigation, it can be concluded that the two evaluated bioceramic RCSs exhibited similar Ca-IR over time and sustained an alkaline pH, which is crucial for fostering an optimal condition for tissue healing, alongside clinically appropriate working times and flow compared to the resin sealer. Further longitudinal and clinical studies are required to confirm these in vitro findings and their clinical relevance.

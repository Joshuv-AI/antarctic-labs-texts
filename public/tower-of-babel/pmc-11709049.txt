Ensuring safe and effective pharmacotherapy: The role of “community pharmacology” in attaining “health for all” from the Indian perspective
Authors: Tirthankar Deb, Alapan Das, Bisweswar Ojha, Prasanjit Das
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe novel approach of “Community Pharmacology” integrates pharmacological principles with community health to achieve the “Health for all” goal through safe and efficient health care. Pharmacovigilance, medication errors (ME), irrational prescriptions, and antimicrobial resistance in the community could be the key areas. Though life expectancy and other health indicators have improved in India, the disparity between rural and urban quality healthcare access should be addressed. Despite India’s enormous progress in vaccine manufacture, supply, and mass immunization through the Universal Immunization Programme (UIP) and Mission Indradhanush, issues remain in incomplete coverage and vaccine hesitancy. Other key challenges include high health expenditures, shortage of trained healthcare professionals, and lack of patient safety and irrational pharmacotherapy in remote areas. Community Pharmacology offers a multifaceted solution in community healthcare delivery through a well-designed integrated network equipped with skills in practical implications of pharmacology aimed at better medication supply, storage and dispensing, adherence to formularies according to the community needs, preparing Essential Medicine List and implementation of rational prescribing. Antimicrobial stewardship (AMS), adverse drug reaction (ADR) monitoring, and evidence-based usage of different drug formulations or drug delivery systems must be implemented beyond the tertiary level, involving both public and private stakeholders. Integrating Community Pharmacology into public health is crucial for fulfilling the global health initiatives to reach the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) as well as attaining India’s ambitious overall development targets.

Introduction

The concept of “Community Pharmacology” is a novel approach of implementing safe and effective pharmacotherapy in community health care. Being a new concept, the definition of the term Community Pharmacology has been put forth by the authors as a field of pharmacology that focuses on applying pharmacological principles in community settings involving each level of health care, encompassing the supply, storage, and dispensing of medicines, preparation and adherence to Essential Medicine Lists, rational prescribing practices, antimicrobial stewardship (AMS), adverse drug reaction (ADR) monitoring, and evidence-based drug use. The lack of awareness about Community Pharmacology leads to challenges, such as irrational drug prescription, improper dosing, adverse drug events, antimicrobial resistance, and economic overburdening at every level of our healthcare delivery system. These are several sectors of community health care that can be improved by experts in Community Pharmacology.

Indian health care: Overcoming bottlenecks with Community Pharmacology initiatives

Patterns of buying medicines:Health consumers who belong to the lower and middle classes from developing nations favor direct purchase from the retail medical shop without doctors’ prescription.[20] Medicines bought from pharmacies without consultation and diagnosis from a physician come with several drawbacks, including wrong prescription and wrong dosage application. In addition, self-medication might mask the symptoms of primary diseases, maintaining the patient’s symptoms and health status untreated, resulting in dreadful condition.[21] Additionally, over-the-counter (OTC) drugs are medicines whose purchase does not require any prescription. They are safe and effective if the consumer follows the direction on the label or healthcare professional.[22] However, the continued and unrestricted purchasing of OTC drugs from medical pharmacies may trigger adverse consequences, drug-drug interactions, and deteriorating patient’s health.[23] A routine OTC drug phenylephrine, which is used as a remedy of common cold, can cause fixed drug eruptions as an adverse reaction.[24] Prescribing peoples’ choice drugs, such as proton pump inhibitors (PPI) and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), are also in overdrive in healthcare sectors. Though they belong to the Schedule H category (cannot be sold without prescription), many a time, they are used chronically or often purchased without consulting with a healthcare practitioner.[25] Irrational use of PPIs, originally intended for gastric diseases, results in increased rate of fractures, vitamin and mineral deficiencies, and potentially life-threatening infections.[26] Likewise, NSAIDs, which are analgesic and anti-inflammatory in nature, have the potential to increase the risk of gastrointestinal (GI) ulcers and bleeding, cardiovascular issues, renal dysfunction, etc., especially in specified high-risk groups or long-term treatment.[27] Community Pharmacology can stress upon the accessibility of the health care in the rural areas and formulate uniform guidelines for OTC. Besides that, prompt legal action should be taken against companies for manufacturing and promoting irrational and banned fixed dose combination (FDC). Possible ill effects of irrational drug combinations should be conveyed to community health workers through various awareness programs and digital media policy decisions regarding strict rules on selling medicines. Prescription with rational FDC should also be adhered to. Community Pharmacology experts can also have a great role in deprescribing and medication reconciliation at the grassroot level to ensure safe and effective pharmacotherapy especially in the geriatric population.Storage and transport maintaining cold chain and dispensing of correct drugs from medical dispensary:Medication management encompassing aspects, such as preparing Essential Medicine List, streamlining supply chain of medicines, transport and storage of medicines, and prescription, and dispensing or administration of the drugs, play pivotal roles in the delivery of standard quality of health care which are often compromised in remote and resource-restricted areas.[28] Factors that affect the safety and efficiency of stored drugs are optimized temperature, humidity, and light exposure. Although most of the tertiary care centers have proper facility of medication management, the lacunae persist in the non-availability of non-electrical cold chain equipment and distinct cold chain rooms at the PHC level. In addition, due to inadequate training of the pharmacists about Coherent Dispensing Protocols in medical pharmacies, medication errors (ME), such as wrong drug choice, wrong dosages, and inadequate directions on proper use, may occur.[29] Mandatory training of all healthcare staff, especially those working in the most remote areas, needs to be conducted regularly by experts in Community Pharmacology and maintenance and functioning of drug storage and dispensing centers with surprise visits should be ensured.[30]Availability of formularies at health center or institute level:Formulary includes the medicine list that is required for regular use in a health facility, most of which are informed by the standard protocol, fiscal impact, and security factors.[31] Currently, there is the absence of formularies in PHC and community health center (CHC) which often causes difference in prescribed medications by different clinicians, therefore creating confusion and compromising the safety of patients. When formularies are not regularly updated and well-vetted, practitioners are often influenced by personal preference, bias, or inducement as opposed to the existing evidence. This can hinder rational prescribing, increases unnecessary expenditures, and induces more adverse effects. Lastly, the absence of formulary also disrupts the balance between supply and demand for certain drugs due to complicated procurement and stock management.[32] The formulary should comply with the updated National List of Essential Medicine (NLEM). Experts in Community Pharmacology can play a vital role in making formularies at every level of healthcare delivery and maintaining as well as updating them periodically and applying stringent regulations regarding formulary-based drug dispensing. Collaboration between the teaching institutes and healthcare providers is imperative in this regard.Awareness and practice of AMS: AMS is a vital intervention to fight the ever-increasing worldwide antimicrobial resistance. Nevertheless, AMS is not practiced in most of the healthcare setups and thus origins overconsumption and inappropriate use of antimicrobials leading to the antimicrobial resistance progress.[3334] This, in turn, can increase healthcare costs and prolonged hospital stays, and contribute to higher morbidity and mortality rates. The experts in Community Pharmacology can create social awareness about the guideline-based short- and long-term use of antibiotics. For example, they can be vital members of a team providing telephonic and online consultation through e-Sanjivani portal about the irrationality of using long-term and unnecessary consumption of antibiotics.[35] They can have a pivotal role in awareness programs among the local dais, pharmacist, as well as teachers about the injudicious use and its adverse effect ensuring safe pharmacotherapy to individuals in the community irrespective of their socioeconomic status.Reporting of adverse drug reactions (ADRs), Adverse Events Following Immunization (AEFI), drug interactions, and ME:ADRs, AEFIs, drug-drug interactions, and ME pose great threats to patient safety, leading to poor health, death, and added expenses to the health system.[36] Physicians, nurses, and other healthcare professionals may not be sufficiently skilled or alert enough for the documentation and reporting of ADRs, AEFI, and ME. India shares only 2% of the total Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR). Routine active surveillance in ADR monitoring is challenging due to the constraints in healthcare resources as well as large population size of India. Furthermore, the medical literature may not adequately cover all severe ADRs or product complaints associated with these events.[37] Imprecise or incomplete reporting of ADRs, AEFI, and ME may result in poor understanding of the pathogenesis and disease complications. “TTWVAA,” [Figure 1] a multistep approach to implement innovative active surveillance and assisted reporting of ADRs, was found to be useful to augment ADR monitoring during mass drug administration events, such as National Deworming Day and COVID vaccination drive, involving diverse Govt and non-Govt workforce, such as Harford County Public School (HCPS), school teachers, and social workers. The model includes forming a dedicated team, training the trainers, distributing work among team members, performing physical visits by experts for active data collection, and training and assisting peripheral stakeholders in reporting ADRs. This proactive system addresses the limitations of passive surveillance by ensuring comprehensive reporting and management of ADRs, ultimately aiming to improve patient safety and provide timely insights for regulatory decisions.[3839]Nationwide mass vaccination and prophylactic therapies:Vaccination and organizational antimicrobial therapy have been revealed to be significant in avoiding communicable disease transmission and resulting in reduction in the mortality and morbidity rates. However, deficiencies in effective “roll out” of these programs across India ensue in less-than-optimal coverage and increase progress of the diseases.[40] This failure can be accredited due to poor logistics, lack of proper funding, vaccine hesitancy, lack of promotion about the available lifestyle changes, mass vaccination, and prophylactic rehabilitations.[41] They may prime to develop lacunae in areas of low vaccination coverage and clusters of people stand high risks to vaccine-preventable diseases. On an optimistic note, community pharmacologists can seal these lacunae by involving in the vaccination initiatives by offering awareness, and teaching to fight vaccine paranoia by encouraging improved healthcare initiatives within the public field.Prescription auditing in each healthcare delivery level:Prescription auditing is also another crucial activity which involves the evaluation of prescription in a systematic and linear fashion to determine whether the drug administered is appropriate as per defined standards and to identify potential error signals of irrational prescribing and thus support reasonable and lawful prescribing. Stringent prescription auditing can capture irrational prescribing practices, including overprescribing, underprescribing, wrong type of drug or wrong dosages, and incorrect or inadequate advice to patients. In a Rajasthan PHC, of 783 legible prescriptions analyzed in a study for its rationale, 36% were found inappropriate in terms of their documented reasoning for the treatment recommended.[42] Community and clinical pharmacologists with their expert knowledge on medication management and pharmacotherapy can play a pivotal role in the auditing process. It can be helpful in the enhancement of the right utilization of antibiotics, enhance the safety of direct consumers, users of the healthcare service, and in general encourage proper use and management of the available healthcare resources.Rules and regulations on qualified and trained pharmacist and midwifery in centers:Retail outlets, and institutional and drug distribution stations, including community dispensary and hospital pharmacies, are the key players which are particularly engaged in reasonable and safe practice of drugs.[43] Therefore, skilled and competent grassroot level pharmacists and experts in midwifery and nursing (Auxiliary Nursing Midwifery (ANM), General Nursing Midwifery (GNM), and Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA)) have profound responsibilities in drug distribution in the outskirt region and notifying the prescribing physician about possible drug-drug interactions and adverse effects as well as tasks of counseling patients on the status of medication adverse event and compliance through rigorous follow-up.[44] However, in many of the CHC and PHC, there is no legislative or standard authority that governs the administration and training of Community Pharmacology, thus possibly compromising the patient’s health status and the quality drug services. Community pharmacologists may advocate for the expansion of specific actions, such as engagement and training of pharmacist and capacity building within the drug delivery network. Simultaneously, they can collaborate with administration and regulatory authorities, non-governmental organizations, and educational institutions to establish definitive requirements and develop standards for methods and rules used in dynamic education in pharmacology. This ensures that each drug supply center is equipped with qualified specialists in Community Pharmacology.

Nevertheless, AMS is not practiced in most of the healthcare setups and thus origins overconsumption and inappropriate use of antimicrobials leading to the antimicrobial resistance progress.[3334] This, in turn, can increase healthcare costs and prolonged hospital stays, and contribute to higher morbidity and mortality rates. The experts in Community Pharmacology can create social awareness about the guideline-based short- and long-term use of antibiotics. For example, they can be vital members of a team providing telephonic and online consultation through e-Sanjivani portal about the irrationality of using long-term and unnecessary consumption of antibiotics.[35] They can have a pivotal role in awareness programs among the local dais, pharmacist, as well as teachers about the injudicious use and its adverse effect ensuring safe pharmacotherapy to individuals in the community irrespective of their socioeconomic status.

Reporting of adverse drug reactions (ADRs), Adverse Events Following Immunization (AEFI), drug interactions, and ME:ADRs, AEFIs, drug-drug interactions, and ME pose great threats to patient safety, leading to poor health, death, and added expenses to the health system.[36] Physicians, nurses, and other healthcare professionals may not be sufficiently skilled or alert enough for the documentation and reporting of ADRs, AEFI, and ME. India shares only 2% of the total Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR). Routine active surveillance in ADR monitoring is challenging due to the constraints in healthcare resources as well as large population size of India. Furthermore, the medical literature may not adequately cover all severe ADRs or product complaints associated with these events.[37] Imprecise or incomplete reporting of ADRs, AEFI, and ME may result in poor understanding of the pathogenesis and disease complications. “TTWVAA,” [Figure 1] a multistep approach to implement innovative active surveillance and assisted reporting of ADRs, was found to be useful to augment ADR monitoring during mass drug administration events, such as National Deworming Day and COVID vaccination drive, involving diverse Govt and non-Govt workforce, such as Harford County Public School (HCPS), school teachers, and social workers. The model includes forming a dedicated team, training the trainers, distributing work among team members, performing physical visits by experts for active data collection, and training and assisting peripheral stakeholders in reporting ADRs. This proactive system addresses the limitations of passive surveillance by ensuring comprehensive reporting and management of ADRs, ultimately aiming to improve patient safety and provide timely insights for regulatory decisions.[3839]

Nationwide mass vaccination and prophylactic therapies:Vaccination and organizational antimicrobial therapy have been revealed to be significant in avoiding communicable disease transmission and resulting in reduction in the mortality and morbidity rates. However, deficiencies in effective “roll out” of these programs across India ensue in less-than-optimal coverage and increase progress of the diseases.[40] This failure can be accredited due to poor logistics, lack of proper funding, vaccine hesitancy, lack of promotion about the available lifestyle changes, mass vaccination, and prophylactic rehabilitations.[41] They may prime to develop lacunae in areas of low vaccination coverage and clusters of people stand high risks to vaccine-preventable diseases. On an optimistic note, community pharmacologists can seal these lacunae by involving in the vaccination initiatives by offering awareness, and teaching to fight vaccine paranoia by encouraging improved healthcare initiatives within the public field.

Prescription auditing in each healthcare delivery level:Prescription auditing is also another crucial activity which involves the evaluation of prescription in a systematic and linear fashion to determine whether the drug administered is appropriate as per defined standards and to identify potential error signals of irrational prescribing and thus support reasonable and lawful prescribing. Stringent prescription auditing can capture irrational prescribing practices, including overprescribing, underprescribing, wrong type of drug or wrong dosages, and incorrect or inadequate advice to patients. In a Rajasthan PHC, of 783 legible prescriptions analyzed in a study for its rationale, 36% were found inappropriate in terms of their documented reasoning for the treatment recommended.[42] Community and clinical pharmacologists with their expert knowledge on medication management and pharmacotherapy can play a pivotal role in the auditing process. It can be helpful in the enhancement of the right utilization of antibiotics, enhance the safety of direct consumers, users of the healthcare service, and in general encourage proper use and management of the available healthcare resources.

Rules and regulations on qualified and trained pharmacist and midwifery in centers:Retail outlets, and institutional and drug distribution stations, including community dispensary and hospital pharmacies, are the key players which are particularly engaged in reasonable and safe practice of drugs.[43] Therefore, skilled and competent grassroot level pharmacists and experts in midwifery and nursing (Auxiliary Nursing Midwifery (ANM), General Nursing Midwifery (GNM), and Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA)) have profound responsibilities in drug distribution in the outskirt region and notifying the prescribing physician about possible drug-drug interactions and adverse effects as well as tasks of counseling patients on the status of medication adverse event and compliance through rigorous follow-up.[44] However, in many of the CHC and PHC, there is no legislative or standard authority that governs the administration and training of Community Pharmacology, thus possibly compromising the patient’s health status and the quality drug services. Community pharmacologists may advocate for the expansion of specific actions, such as engagement and training of pharmacist and capacity building within the drug delivery network. Simultaneously, they can collaborate with administration and regulatory authorities, non-governmental organizations, and educational institutions to establish definitive requirements and develop standards for methods and rules used in dynamic education in pharmacology. This ensures that each drug supply center is equipped with qualified specialists in Community Pharmacology.

Discussion

The pursuit of “Health for all” is still a daunting yet vital goal which is not limited to only availability of the medical and technocratic resources, and comprehensive efforts encompassing the complicated interactions of social, organizational, financial, and environmental determinants are imperative. The complexities and heterogeneities of the Indian health scenario come with its own opportunities and challenges. For example, though India has made significant progress in the production of drugs, vaccines, and mass vaccination programs, still there is room for improvement regarding affordability, accessibility, and rational prescription of drugs and vaccination coverage. Discrepancies in healthcare availability across the economic classes and geographical regions need to be addressed with optimized distribution of healthcare facilities and human resources. Comparative high cost of health care has impelled the population to use 15–20% of household income on medical expenditure,[45] and this fuels up the OOP and reaching 58.7% of the total health expenditure, which is one of the highest in the world.[5] For the lesser privileged section, the only way to manage hospital bills appears to be the informal loans with high interest, followed by selling of assets, which is evidenced by 23% and 6%, respectively,[7] which only depicts the dire need to improve the affordability and accessibility of safe and effective therapy. Although India has emerged as one of the major producers as well as suppliers taking care of around 60% of the global demand and mega programs of mass immunization running across the country,[12] safe and effective implementation of immunization programs to improve public participation for optimal utilization of India’s vaccine potential is vital for long-term success. Challenges in addressing logistic issues, insufficient fundings, vaccine hesitancy, and limited public awareness on role of lifestyle modifications have hampered vaccine efficacy and failed to halt the progression of vaccine-preventable diseases. The glimmer of hope could be implementing programs, such as Mission Indradhanush which has left a significant impact on the vaccine coverage increasing up to 18.5%.[8] Integration of Community Pharmacology with the National Immunization Program may immensely fulfill the pledge of Health for all with regard to the prevention of communicable diseases. Digital healthcare assistance by experts in Community Pharmacology through e-Sanjivani initiative of GoI could also be another solution to increase healthcare coverage in the community, in the form of providing teleconsultation on antibiotic usage, drug combinations, management of ADRs, as well as rational prescription writings, thus creating a mass awareness in remote regions to ensure patient safety and effective pharmacotherapy. To overcome the challenges in health sector, active participation, and cooperation among all stakeholders, including governmental organizations at different levels, teaching institutions and healthcare delivery systems, and even manufacturing units are warranted. The novel approach of Community Pharmacology can bridge the gaps between the above diverse stakeholders through a well-designed training and challenge mitigation program. Enhancing public interest about the necessity of seeking medical advice from a qualified professional and adhering to prescribed drugs for their indications and discouraging self-medication and direct pharmacy purchases can improve patient safety.[23] To avoid the trend of doctor shopping, deploying quality health care, comprehensive medical record-keeping, and transparent communication should be practiced. Community Pharmacology networks can play a vital role to ensure proper storage, transportation, and dispensing of drugs by implementing strict standard operating protocols (SOP), training the personnel, regular vigilance, and improving the facilities using technology to maintain the cold chain and prevent ME. Pharmacologists from teaching institutes can design standardized formularies customized for different levels of health care based on evidence-based guidelines,[46] cost-effectiveness, and local endemicity to ensure rational prescribing and optimization of the resource allocation. Prevention of the injudicious use of common medications, such as PPIs and NSAIDs, and providing them to the population with appropriate indications and prescriptions is another area where Community Pharmacology can be specifically helpful. Practicing rational use of antimicrobials, prevention of resistance against pathogens, creating awareness, implementing, and executing the guidelines of AMS can be a powerful weapon.[47] Strengthening vigilance systems at secondary and PHC in reporting and monitoring of ADRs, AEFIs, drug interactions, as well as ME will enrich the clinical database and ensure patient safety.

Key message

In order to fulfill the mission of “Health for All,” apart from cooperation of governments, medical personnel, international health organizations, and the public, we need experts with knowledge of Community Pharmacology to implement safe and effective pharmacotherapy and to provide the right to health to the last person in the queue [Table 1] [Figure 2]. The multidimensional objective of ensuring quality care for people at large can only be achieved when effective strategies to solve the social, economic, and environmental determinants of health will be complemented by methods of improving the standard-of-care delivery system focusing on both clinical and preventive services. In this way, we can navigate toward a healthier future and can actively participate in fulfilling the global agenda of Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

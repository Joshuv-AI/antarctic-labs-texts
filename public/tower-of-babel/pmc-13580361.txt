Complete chloroplast genome sequence and phylogenetic analysis of Chrysosplenium insularis J. E. Jang, K. H. Lee & H. Y. Gil (Saxifragaceae), an endemic Korean species
Authors: Hye Been Kim, Eun Su Kang, Dong Chan Son, Ju Eun Jang, Hee-Young Gil
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe determined the complete chloroplast genome of Chrysosplenium insularis, an endemic species of the Republic of Korea. The genome was 152,689 bp with a typical quadripartite structure: a large single-copy region (83,587 bp), a small single-copy region (17,098 bp), and two inverted repeat regions (26,002 bp each). It contained 131 genes: 86 coding sequences, 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis of 79 coding sequences showed that C. insularis is closely related to C. alternifolium and C. japonicum. This chloroplast genome provides a molecular basis for phylogenetic and evolutionary studies of Chrysosplenium and may contribute to conservation research.Keywords: Plastome, coding sequences, Chrysosplenium, phylogenetic relationship

Introduction

Chrysosplenium insularis J.E.Jang, K.H.Lee & H.Y.Gil (2024) is endemic to the Republic of Korea, where it is restricted to southern coastal regions, including Jeju-do and Gageo-do (Jang et al. 2024). This species can be distinguished from related taxa by its greenish-yellow to green bracteal leaves at flowering and a cylindrical papillose seed surface (Jang et al. 2024).

Next-generation sequencing (NGS) has facilitated chloroplast genome sequencing and analysis (Nock et al. 2011; Li et al. 2015; Daniell et al. 2016). Chloroplast genome data are widely used in plant taxonomy, phylogeny, and evolution, providing insights into complex evolutionary histories and supporting genetic diversity assessment, species identification, and conservation (Yang et al. 2013; Luo et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2023).

Although phylogenetic analyses based on partial sequences have been conducted for C. insularis (Jang et al. 2024), its chloroplast genome remains unreported. Therefore, this study aimed to present the complete chloroplast genome of C. insularis and analyze its phylogenetic relationships with related taxa to provide foundational data for future phylogenetic and evolutionary studies of the genus Chrysosplenium.

Materials and methods

C. insularis was collected in Hogeun-dong, Seogwipo-si, Jeju-do, the Republic of Korea (33.25084°N, 126.54434°E) (Figure 1) in March 2020 by Kang-Hyup Lee. A voucher specimen was deposited in the Korea National Arboretum Herbarium (KH) under the voucher number KHB1666683 (http://www.nature.go.kr; Contact: Dong Chan Son, sdclym@korea.kr). Fresh leaves were dried in silica gel for DNA extraction.

Total DNA was extracted using a DNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), following the manufacturer’s protocol. The extracted DNA was assessed on a 2% agarose gel, and high-purity genomic DNA (gDNA) was sequenced using an Illumina MiSeq platform with 2 × 301 bp paired-end reads, generating 10,168,428 reads. The plastome was assembled de novo using NOVOPlasty v.4.3.5 with the matK sequence of C. japonicum (LC850428) as the seed, a k-mer size of 33, and an expected genome size of 150,000–200,000 bp; all other parameters were set to default values. The assembly was finalized in Geneious Prime v.2025.2.2 (Kearse et al. 2012) and verified using Draw_SequencingDepth.py following Ni et al. (2023). The genome was annotated with GeSeq (Tillich et al. 2017), and gene arrangement was verified against related plastomes in NCBI GenBank (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov). Circular genome maps and schematic representations of cis- and trans-splicing events were visualized using CPGview (Liu et al. 2023). IRscope (https://irscope.shinyapps.io/irapp/) was used to compare IR sizes and gene arrangements at the four IR/SC junctions among C. insularis and four closely related Chrysosplenium species.

The phylogenetic analysis included 19 Saxifragaceae species and one Grossulariaceae outgroup. Except for C. insularis, plastome sequences were downloaded from NCBI GenBank; accession numbers and references are listed in Supplementary Table S1. Taxa were selected based on previous phylogenetic studies of Saxifragaceae and Chrysosplenium, prioritizing species from the same series as C. insularis and including one species from each of the other series represented in the Republic of Korea (Wu et al. 2020; Yan et al. 2020a, 2020b, 2021; Kim et al. 2023). A total of 79 coding sequences (CDSs) shared by all taxa were extracted, with one copy retained for genes duplicated in the IR regions. The CDSs were concatenated and aligned using MAFFT in Geneious Prime v.2025.2.2 (Katoh and Standley 2013). The best-fit model for maximum-likelihood (ML) analysis was selected using ModelFinder v.3.0.1 (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017) in PhyloSuite v.2.0.2 (Zhang et al. 2020; Xiang et al. 2023). ML analysis was performed using IQ-TREE v.3.0.1 (Wong et al. 2026) by setting the mix {TVM+FO, GTR+FO, JC}+I + R2 model for 5,000 ultrafast bootstraps (Minh et al. 2013). The resulting phylogenetic tree was visualized using FigTree v.1.4.4 (http://tree.bio.ed.ac.uk/software/figtree).

Results

The chloroplast genome of C. insularis was 152,689 bp long, with a GC content of 37.5% (Figure 2). A total of 314,660 reads mapped to the plastome, providing 100% coverage and an average depth of 772.07× (range: 231–1,951×; Figure S1). The genome had a typical quadripartite structure with a pair of inverted repeats (IR, 26,002 bp each) that separated a large single-copy region (LSC, 83,587 bp) from a small single-copy region (SSC, 17,098 bp). The complete genome sequence was deposited in GenBank under accession number PZ286360. The ten Chrysosplenium plastomes analyzed were similar in genome size, region lengths, gene content, and GC content (Table S2). Among C. insularis and four closely related species, IR lengths ranged from 25,956 to 26,002 bp, with conserved gene arrangements at the four IR/SC junctions (Figure S2).

The plastome contained 131 genes, including 86 CDSs, 37 tRNAs, and 8 rRNAs, with duplicated IR copies counted separately. Each IR contained 17 genes, including 6 CDSs, 7 tRNA genes, and 4 rRNA genes (CDSs: ndhB, rpl2, rpl23, rps7, rps12, ycf2; tRNAs: trnA-UGC, trnI-CAU, trnI-GAU, trnL-CAA, trnN-GUU, trnR-ACG, trnV-GAC; rRNAs: rrn4.5, rrn5, rrn16, rrn23). A total of 10 cis-splicing genes were observed, with eight genes containing a single intron (atpF, ndhA, ndhB, petB, petD, rpl16, rpoC1, and rps16) and two genes containing two introns (clpP1 and ycf3) (Figureure S3). The trans-spliced rps12 gene had exon 1 in the LSC region and duplicated exons 2 and 3 in the IR regions (Figure S4). ML phylogenetic analysis supported the monophyly of Chrysosplenium and its separation into C. sect. Oppositifolia and C. sect. Alternifolia (bootstrap value = 100 for both; Figure 3). Furthermore, C. insularis was placed in C. ser. Alternifolia and was recovered as sister to a strongly supported clade comprising C. alternifolium and C. japonicum (bootstrap value = 100).

Discussion and conclusion

The chloroplast genome of C. insularis was determined and analyzed. The plastome exhibited the typical quadripartite structure of most angiosperms (Kim et al. 2024). Its genome size, gene content, and GC content were similar to those of the other Chrysosplenium plastomes, indicating overall conservation within the genus. Similar IR sizes and IR/SC junction gene arrangements suggest that these regions are conserved among the five closely related Chrysosplenium species examined.

The ML results were consistent with previous phylogenetic studies of Saxifragaceae (Hara 1957; Nakazawa et al. 1997; Pan and Ohba 2001; Soltis et al. 2001; Deng et al. 2015; Wu et al. 2020; Yan et al. 2021; Shen et al. 2026). In addition, Chrysosplenium is divided into two major clades: C. sect. Oppositifolia with opposite leaves and C. sect. Alternifolia with alternate leaves, consistent with the results of previous morphological and molecular studies (Franchet 1890; Hara 1957; Nakazawa et al. 1997; Pan and Ohba 2001; Soltis et al. 2001; Deng et al. 2015; Shen et al. 2026). In particular, C. insularis was placed in C. ser. Alternifolia and was closely related to C. alternifolium and C. japonicum, consistent with previous morphological, nuclear, and chloroplast DNA evidence that these species form a clade (Jang et al. 2024).

These findings clarify the phylogenetic position of C. insularis and its relationships with closely related taxa and provide a framework for understanding relationships within Chrysosplenium. However, broader sampling across all recognized series is needed for a more comprehensive evaluation of phylogenetic relationships within the genus.

This study provides comprehensive chloroplast genome data for C. insularis. As C. insularis is endemic to the Republic of Korea, the genomic data presented here provide valuable information for phylogenetic and evolutionary studies of Chrysosplenium and may also contribute to future conservation research.

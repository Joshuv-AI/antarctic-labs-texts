Distributions of consecutive level spacings of circular unitary ensemble and their ratio: finite-size corrections and Riemann ζ \zeta zeros
\name \fname Shinsuke \surname M. Nishigaki∗

Abstract
We compute the joint distribution of two consecutive eigenphase spacings and their ratio for Haar-distributed U ⁡ ( N ) \mathrm{U}(N) matrices (the circular unitary ensemble) using our framework for Jánossy densities in random matrix theory, formulated via the Tracy-Widom system of nonlinear PDEs. Our result shows that the leading finite- N N correction in the gap-ratio distribution relative to the universal sine-kernel limit is of 𝒪 ⁡ ( N − 4 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-4}) , reflecting a nontrivial cancellation of the 𝒪 ⁡ ( N − 2 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{-2}) part present in the joint distributions of consecutive spacings. This finding suggests the potential to extract subtle finite-size corrections from the energy spectra of quantum-chaotic systems and explains why the deviation of the gap-ratio distribution of the Riemann zeta zeros { 1 / 2 + i ​ γ n } , γ n ≈ T ≫ 1 \{1/2+i\gamma_{n}\},\gamma_{n}\approx T\gg 1 from the sine-kernel prediction scales as ( log ⁡ ( T / 2 ​ π ) ) − 3 \left(\log(T/2\pi)\right)^{-3} .

4

5

As the zeta function is arguably regarded as an “ideal” quantum chaotic system,

prompting a wave of researches into the deviations of
\order{N^{-2}}

(see
[
6
]
and references therein).

On the other hand, recent studies of quantum many-body and chaotic systems
have popularized the gap-ratio distribution

two consecutive energy level spacings

as a leading measure of quantum chaoticity

,

]

The primary reason for this popularity is

which is system-specific and can grow rapidly with energy

*
*

*

Such DOS’s of physical relevance include
the mobility edge of QCD Dirac spectra at

[
10
]
.
,
unlike the conventional level spacing
s=\rho(E_{n})(E_{n+1}-E_{n})

In light of these two trends this article aims to present analytical expressions for
two spectral-statistical distributions of CUE
N

the gap-ratio distribution and a related quantity:
the distribution

of the nearest-neighbor level spacing

[

]

These results are derived using a framework developed by the author

[

13
]

This article is structured as follows.
In Section 2, we list key properties of the determinantal point process (DPP)

Finally, in Section 5, we present a numerical study of how the joint distribution of

\mathrm{U}(N)

If

\displaystyle K(x,y)=\frac{{}^{t}\Phi(x)J\Phi(y)}{\mathrm{e}^{ix}-\mathrm{e}^{iy}},~~\Phi(x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi(x)\\
\psi(x)\end{array}\right]=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{ix/2}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{e}^{iNx/2}\\
\mathrm{e}^{-iNx/2}\end{array}\right],~~J=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\

After unfolding

\displaystyle K(x,y)|_{\mathrm{unfold.}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x-y)}{\pi N\sin((x-y)/N)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(x-y)}{\pi(x-y)}+\frac{(x-y)\sin(x-y)}{6\pi\,N^{2}}+\frac{7(x-y)^{3}\sin(x-y)}{360\pi\,N^{4}}+\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{N^{6}}\Bigr).

(7)

Given that the

\displaystyle\tilde{K}(x,y)=K(x,y)-K(x,s)K(s,s)^{-1}K(s,y).

In the case of CUE

By translational invariance under the shift

The transformation between the kernels (

\displaystyle\tilde{K}(x,y)=\frac{{}^{t}\tilde{\Phi}(x)J\tilde{\Phi}(y)}{\mathrm{e}^{ix}-\mathrm{e}^{iy}},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d}{dx}-\frac{b}{2}\right)\Phi(x)=\Omega(x)\Phi(x),~\Omega(x)=\frac{1}{m(x)}\left[\begin{array}[]{rr}A(x)&B(x)\\

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~\mbox{where}~m,A,B,C~\mbox{are polynomials in}~\mathrm{e}^{bx},

(16)

\displaystyle\tilde{m}(x)=i(1-\mathrm{e}^{ix}),~~\tilde{A}(x)=-\frac{N}{2}(1-\mathrm{e}^{ix})+\frac{1}{N},~~\tilde{B}=-1-\frac{1}{N},~~\tilde{C}=1-\frac{1}{N}.

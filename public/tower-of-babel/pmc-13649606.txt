Preoperative Magnetic Resonance Imaging Findings Associated with Preexisting Basilar Artery Stenosis in Endovascular Revascularization for Basilar Artery Occlusion
Authors: Hirofumi ASANO, Tatsuya SHIMIZU, Kazuki WAKABAYASHI, Masashi YOSHIZAWA, Masanori AIHARA, Rei YAMAGUCHI, Ryosuke SHINTOKU, Naoto MUKADA, Soichi OYA
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Underlying atherosclerotic stenosis is more common in acute basilar artery occlusion than in anterior circulation, and often necessitates additional procedures such as balloon angioplasty or stent placement during endovascular revascularization. Preoperative recognition of underlying basilar artery stenosis may be clinically relevant, but reliable imaging indicators remain unclear. This study aimed to identify preoperative magnetic resonance imaging findings associated with underlying basilar artery stenosis in patients undergoing acute endovascular revascularization. We retrospectively analyzed patients at three institutions between 2018 and 2023. Patients were divided into stenosis and non-stenosis groups based on post-recanalization angiographic findings. Stenosis was identified in 13 patients and was found to be absent in 35 patients. Pontine hyperintensity on diffusion-weighted imaging was more frequent in the stenosis group and remained independently associated with preexisting basilar artery stenosis after adjustment for preoperative National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score. Non-visualization of the proximal basilar artery on magnetic resonance angiography was also more frequent in the stenosis group, but did not remain independently associated after adjustment. Additional endovascular procedures were more frequently required in the stenosis group, whereas recanalization was achieved with mechanical thrombectomy alone in the non-stenosis group. Functional outcomes at discharge were comparable between the groups. In acute basilar artery occlusion, pontine hyperintensity on diffusion-weighted imaging was independently associated with preexisting basilar artery stenosis. Its absence may help exclude underlying stenosis, whereas concordant positivity of pontine hyperintensity and proximal basilar artery non-visualization may support suspicion of stenosis before treatment.

Introduction

Basilar artery (BA) occlusion (BAO) accounts for roughly 1% of acute ischemic strokes.1,2) Patients with BAO often present with more severe neurological deficits and experience poorer outcomes than individuals with large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the anterior circulation.3,4) Two randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have reported that acute endovascular revascularization for severe BAO resulted in significantly better outcomes than medical treatment.5,6) A meta-analysis of four RCTs likewise showed a lower mortality rate and improved outcomes in the acute endovascular revascularization group.7,8) In light of recently accumulated evidence, the number of endovascular revascularization procedures for BAO has increased.9)

Though the majority of LVOs in the anterior circulation are due to cardiogenic cerebral embolism, LVOs in the posterior circulation are often associated with arteriosclerotic stenosis.10) In a series of patients undergoing mechanical thrombectomy for acute BAO, atherosclerotic stenotic lesions in the BA were identified in 24.1%-29.2% of cases.11,12) However, two recent RCTs investigating BAO found that percutaneous transluminal angioplasty (PTA) or stent placement was added in 40%-55% of cases of acute BAO,5,6) suggesting a trend toward broader indications for stent use. Although evidence from previous studies of BAO suggested that the presence of BA stenosis does not adversely affect the effectiveness of acute endovascular revascularization therapy,11,13) preprocedural identification of imaging findings associated with underlying BA stenosis may help guide the selection of anticoagulant versus antiplatelet therapy, and assist in anticipating the possible need for PTA or stent placement. However, imaging characteristics associated with BA stenosis are yet to be established.

In the majority of countries, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is seldom obtained before acute endovascular revascularization therapy, with computed tomography (CT) being the standard modality for assessing the extent of ischemia.14) MRI is widely used in Japan, where many facilities evaluate the indications for acute endovascular revascularization based on findings from diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and magnetic resonance angiography (MRA).15) At our hospital and affiliated institutions, MRI is routinely performed before acute endovascular revascularization. In this study, we conducted a retrospective analysis to assess the significance of MRI findings in the treatment of acute BAO and to examine radiological features associated with underlying BA stenosis.

Materials and Methods

This study included patients with BAO treated by acute endovascular revascularization between January 2018 and December 2023 at three hospitals in Japan. All patients first underwent CT on arrival to exclude intracranial hemorrhage, followed by MRI to assess the site and extent of infarction and MRA to evaluate the site of vessel occlusion. Patients who did not undergo MRI before treatment were excluded. Similarly, patients in whom effective recanalization (Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score ≥2b) was not achieved were also excluded, as preexisting stenosis could not be assessed in those cases. After BAO was diagnosed, patients received tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) if they met the criteria for its use in Japan.16) The presence or absence of BA stenosis was evaluated by angiography during acute endovascular revascularization. Stenosis was defined as luminal narrowing exceeding 50%.17)

We collected information regarding age, sex, past medical history (atrial fibrillation, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and diabetes), pretreatment National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, preadmission modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, mRS score at discharge, and surgical procedures. As for radiological characteristics on MRI, DWI–posterior circulation–Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (DWI-pc-ASPECTS),18,19) diameter of the posterior communicating artery (PcomA), and BA visualization were evaluated. The diameter of the PcomA was assessed by postoperative time-of-flight MRI. If postoperative MRI was unavailable, pre-stroke MRI was used. Patients without either MRI were excluded from the PcomA analysis. MRI was performed according to the acute stroke protocol at each hospital using 1.5-T or 3.0-T scanners. At minimum, DWI and fluid attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) were acquired, and vascular assessment was performed using three-dimensional time-of-flight MRA. DWI was obtained with a b value of 1,000 s/mm2 and a slice thickness of 5 mm. It was determined whether the diameter of the PcomA was larger than the diameter of the ipsilateral P1 segment of the posterior cerebral artery (PCA).

For cases with preexisting BA stenosis, the location of stenosis was classified as proximal, middle, or distal, determined by cerebral angiography findings after recanalization. The segment from union with the vertebral artery to the proximal anterior inferior cerebellar artery was defined as proximal, that from the proximal anterior inferior cerebellar artery to the superior cerebellar artery was defined as middle, and that from the superior cerebellar artery to the tip of the BA was defined as distal.

Endovascular procedures were selected at the discretion of the surgeon. Thrombectomy was performed under local anesthesia using a stent retriever, a contact aspiration catheter, or both. For stenotic lesions, PTA or stent placement was performed at the discretion of the surgeon.

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Gunma University (Approval No. HS2023-086). Written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study, and an opt-out consent process was implemented.

Statistical analysis

Chi-square test was used to compare qualitative data, t-test was used to compare means when data were normally distributed, and the Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare medians when data did not show a normal distribution. Values of p < 0.05 were considered significant. Univariable analyses were first performed to evaluate associations between clinical and imaging variables and the presence of preexisting BA stenosis. Variables showing statistical significance in univariable analyses were considered for inclusion in multivariable logistic regression analysis.

In addition, we assessed the diagnostic performance of selected MRI/MRA findings for detecting preexisting BA stenosis. For each index test, 2 × 2 contingency tables were constructed using preexisting BA stenosis as the reference standard, and sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated. Ninety-five percent confidence intervals (CIs) for each proportion were calculated using the Wilson score method. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 31.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Patient selection and group classification

Fifty-eight patients with BAO underwent acute endovascular revascularization therapy. Among them, five patients were excluded as MRI had not been obtained before treatment. Another five patients were excluded because of unsuccessful recanalization. Consequently, data from 48 patients were included for analysis in this study (Figure 1). After recanalization, 13 patients were found to have BA stenosis, with the remaining 35 patients showing no stenosis on angiography.

We compared patient characteristics between the stenosis group and the non-stenosis group (Table 1). Atrial fibrillation was less frequent in the stenosis group than in the non-stenosis group, although the difference was not significant. Hypertension tended to be more prevalent in the stenosis group than in the non-stenosis group, although the difference was not significant. Imaging findings associated with preexisting BA stenosis

To identify imaging characteristics of BAO associated with BA stenosis, DWI positivity was evaluated for each site according to DWI-pc-ASPECTS. Pontine hyperintensity on DWI was significantly more frequent in the stenosis group than in the non-stenosis group, whereas DWI positivity in other regions was not associated with stenosis. On preoperative MRA, non-visualization of the proximal BA was significantly more frequent in the stenosis group.

Because preoperative NIHSS score differed significantly between the stenosis and non-stenosis groups, multivariable logistic regression analysis adjusted for preoperative NIHSS score was performed for MRI findings associated with preexisting BA stenosis in univariable analysis (Table 2). Pontine hyperintensity on DWI remained independently associated with preexisting BA stenosis after adjustment for NIHSS score. In contrast, proximal BA non-visualization on MRA was not independently associated with preexisting BA stenosis after adjustment for NIHSS score.

Diagnostic performance of MRI findings

We evaluated the diagnostic performance of pontine hyperintensity on DWI and proximal BA non-visualization on preoperative MRA for detecting preexisting BA stenosis. Pontine hyperintensity on DWI showed high sensitivity and NPV, whereas concordant positivity of both findings improved specificity and PPV at the expense of sensitivity (Table 3).

Treatment characteristics

We compared treatments performed between the groups (Table 4). As a medical treatment, tPA was less frequent in the stenosis group than in the non-stenosis group. Antiplatelet drugs, including ozagrel, prasugrel, aspirin, and clopidogrel, were administered immediately before or during endovascular revascularization only in the stenosis group, with overlap with tPA treatment in two patients. Regarding endovascular procedures, recanalization was achieved with mechanical thrombectomy in all cases in the non-stenosis group, whereas additional treatments were frequently required in the stenosis group, including PTA and stent placement.

Discussion

This study showed that pontine hyperintensity on DWI and non-visualization of the proximal BA on MRA were associated with preexisting BA stenosis in patients with BAO undergoing acute endovascular revascularization. Among these findings, pontine hyperintensity on DWI remained independently associated with preexisting BA stenosis after adjustment for preoperative NIHSS score, suggesting that this finding may be a particularly relevant MRI marker of underlying BA stenosis. In contrast, non-visualization of the proximal BA on MRA was not independently associated with preexisting BA stenosis after adjustment for preoperative NIHSS score, and should therefore be interpreted as a supplementary imaging finding. In addition, pontine hyperintensity on DWI showed a high sensitivity and a high NPV, suggesting that its absence may help exclude preexisting BA stenosis. Concordant positivity of pontine hyperintensity on DWI and proximal BA non-visualization on MRA improved specificity and PPV, suggesting a more confirmatory role in identifying preexisting BA stenosis.

Anticipating preexisting BA stenosis may be clinically relevant because intracranial atherosclerosis-related vertebrobasilar artery occlusion may require antiplatelet therapy and adjunctive endovascular procedures such as PTA or stent placement.20) This was consistent with these findings. However, this study did not directly evaluate whether preoperative identification of BA stenosis improves procedural efficiency or clinical outcomes.

The association between pontine hyperintensity on DWI and preexisting BA stenosis may be explained by the anatomical distribution of stenotic lesions and perforating arteries. The proximal-to-middle distribution of BA stenosis observed in this cohort was consistent with a previous report2) and may predispose to pontine ischemia because pontine perforators arise from these segments. In contrast, thrombi in cases without BA stenosis are more likely to reach the top of the BA, thus reducing the risk of pontine infarction.

Some limitations to this study needed to be kept in mind while interpreting the results. First, this was a retrospective study of a relatively small cohort, which may have reduced the statistical power to detect certain associations. Second, MRI was performed at three institutions using different scanners, which may have introduced heterogeneity in image quality and diagnostic performance. In addition, although all radiological assessments were performed by board-certified neurosurgeons, formal inter-rater reliability analysis was not performed. Therefore, larger multicenter studies using standardized MRI protocols are needed to validate the reproducibility of our findings. Third, although no difference in treatment outcomes was apparent between the stenosis and the non-stenosis groups, potential biases may have existed because the actual treatment approach depended on the judgment of the attending surgeons. Notably, the rate of tPA administration differed significantly between the groups, and the observed outcomes were achieved under the condition that the decision to perform stent placement in the stenosis group was made by each surgeon based on intraoperative findings of stenosis. Fourth, exclusion of five patients who did not achieve recanalization may have introduced selection bias, possibly underestimating BA stenosis prevalence and affecting the diagnostic performance of MRI findings. However, this limitation may be unavoidable because assessment of underlying stenosis requires successful recanalization. Finally, because MRI is not routinely performed before endovascular treatment in many countries, these findings may be less generalizable to institutions in which treatment decisions for acute BAO are based primarily on CT-based evaluation.

Conclusion

In cases of acute BAO, pontine hyperintensity on DWI was independently associated with preexisting BA stenosis, whereas non-visualization of the proximal BA on preoperative MRI may provide complementary information. These findings may help estimate the likelihood of preexisting BA stenosis before treatment. Further studies are needed to clarify their clinical impact on treatment selection, procedural efficiency, and outcomes.

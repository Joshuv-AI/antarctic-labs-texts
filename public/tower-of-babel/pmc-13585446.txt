Response to the letter regarding ‘Outcomes of functional ventricular tachycardia ablation vs. medical therapy in Chagas cardiomyopathy patients with implantable cardioverter-defibrillators: a competing risks analysis’
Authors: Andre Assis Lopes do Carmo, Gustavo de Araújo Silva, Maria do Carmo Pereira Nunes
Published: 2026

Response to the Letter to the Editor by Henrique Horta Veloso, Mauro Felippe Felix Mediano: Amiodarone dose to treat ventricular tachycardia in Chagas cardiomyopathy. https://doi.org/10.1093/europace/euag142, in press.

We thank Veloso et al. for their interest in our study comparing catheter ablation strategies with antiarrhythmic drug therapy for the prevention of ventricular tachycardia recurrence in Chagas cardiomyopathy.1 We welcome the opportunity to address their comments regarding amiodarone dosing in this population, particularly because some of their interpretations do not fully reflect the data presented in our manuscript and may lead to misleading conclusions.

First, the differences in amiodarone dose between the medical therapy and catheter ablation groups reported in Table 1 reflect baseline treatment at study entry. As expected, patients referred for catheter ablation were experiencing recurrent ventricular tachycardia despite receiving significantly higher doses of amiodarone. Therefore, the higher amiodarone dose in the ablation group should be interpreted as a marker of greater arrhythmic disease severity and treatment refractoriness, rather than as a factor favouring improved outcomes observed following ablation.

In addition, to directly address the concern regarding amiodarone exposure after catheter ablation, we performed an additional analysis of amiodarone dose during follow-up, considering all available measurements before censoring. The median amiodarone dose in the ablation group was 200 mg/day (interquartile range 100–200 mg/day), which was identical to that observed in the medical therapy group. These findings clearly demonstrate that differences in amiodarone dosing during follow-up cannot account for the superior freedom from ventricular tachycardia recurrence observed in the catheter ablation group.

Finally, our findings are consistent with the growing body of evidence demonstrating the superiority of catheter ablation over antiarrhythmic drug therapy for the prevention of ventricular tachycardia recurrence across a variety of structural heart diseases.2,3 Although Chagas cardiomyopathy is unquestionably one of the most challenging arrhythmogenic substrates, characterized by high rates of VT recurrence and extensive, complex scar architecture,4,5 our study employed a comprehensive substrate modification strategy incorporating dynamic functional mapping.6 This approach may have enhanced substrate identification and elimination, thereby reducing the relative impact of antiarrhythmic drug therapy on long-term VT control. Importantly, these results suggest that, when performed with contemporary substrate-guided techniques, catheter ablation may provide substantial benefits even in this particularly high-risk population. Taken together, these observations support consideration of a more proactive and earlier ablation strategy in patients with Chagas cardiomyopathy; however, this hypothesis warrants confirmation in prospective studies.

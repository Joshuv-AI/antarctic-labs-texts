Abstract
. For every prime number p p congruent to three modulo four, we prove that there exists a smooth curve of genus five in characteristic p p that is supersingular. This extends earlier work of Ekedahl and of Harashita, Kudo, and Senda. Keywords: curve, Jacobian, abelian variety, positive characteristic, supersingular, Frobenius, Newton polygon, automorphism. 2020 MSC primary: 11G20, 11M38, 14G10, 14H10, 14H40. Secondary 11G10, 14G15, 14H37, 14H30.



1.
Introduction

Suppose
k
is an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic
p
.
Suppose
A
is a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
g
defined over
k
.
We say that
A
is
supersingular
if the only slope of its Newton polygon is
1/2
.
This is equivalent to
A
being isogenous to a product of
g
supersingular elliptic curves,
by
[
Oor74
, Theorem 4.2]
which relies on
[
Tat66
, Theorem 2d]
.

Suppose
X
is a smooth curve of genus
g
.
We say that
X
is supersingular if its Jacobian is supersingular.
For each positive integer
g
and prime
p
, one can ask whether there exists a smooth curve of
genus
g
defined over
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
that is supersingular.
The answer is known to be yes:

(1)

when
g=1
for all
p
, by work of Deuring
[
Deu41
]
;

(2)

when
g=2
for all
p
, by work of Serre
[
Ser83
, Théorème 3]
, also
[
IKO86
, Proposition 3.1]
;

(3)

when
g=3
for all
p
, by work of Oort
[
Oor91
, Theorem 5.12]
; also
[
Ibu93
, Theorem 1]
;

(4)

and when
g=4
for all
p
, by work of Harashita, Kudo, and Senda
[
KHS20
, Corollary 1.2]
;
for an alternative proof, see
[
Pri
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The answer is also yes when
p=2
for all
g
, by work of Van der Geer and van der Vlugt
[
vdGvdV95
]
; thus
it suffices to consider this question when
p
is odd.

We recently found a new construction of a smooth supersingular curve
X
of genus
g=5
over
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}

for every prime
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
.
Like the curves in
[
KHS20
]
,
X
is a Klein-four cover of the projective line
\mathbf{P}^{1}
.
This is the main result of the paper, and its proof can be found in Section
3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
is a prime number, then there exists a smooth curve
X
of genus five defined over

\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
that is supersingular.

To verify that Theorem
1.1
is new, we review some earlier work.
In
[
Eke87
, page 173]
, Ekedahl
constructs a superspecial curve (meaning its Jacobian is isomorphic to a
product of supersingular elliptic curves) of genus five when
p\equiv-1\bmod 11,12,15,20,8
or
p\equiv-4\bmod 15
.
These curves are abelian covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}

branched at exactly three points.
In Section
4
, we determine which of these covers are supersingular using the Shimura–Taniyama formula.
These results do not appear in the literature, so
we are including them in Section
4
to show that the congruence condition
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
in
Theorem
1.1
is less restrictive than the congruence conditions arising in earlier work.
There is also a construction of supersingular curves of genus
5

for
p
a quadratic non-residue modulo
11
in
[
LMPT19b
]
and
for
p\gg 0
with
p\equiv 7\bmod 8
in
[
LMPT19a
]
.

2.
A Criterion for Supersingularity

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a branched cyclic cover of smooth projective curves over
k
.
In this section, we describe a technique for obtaining information about the location of the break points of the Newton polygon of
Y
that exploits the group action.
The approach is based on work of Tamagawa about generalized Hasse-Witt invariants
[
Tam03
, Section 3]
.

2.1.
The Group Action and Frobenius

Suppose
\pi:Y\to X
is cyclic of degree
m
, with
m
relatively prime to
p
.
The
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
-action on
Y
induces a
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
-action on
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
and on
H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
. Fix a primitive character
\chi:\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}\to k^{\times}
. As in
[
Tam03
]
,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}
decomposes as a direct sum of line bundles on
X
:

(2.1)

\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}}L_{i},

where
\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}
acts on
L_{i}
via
\chi^{i}:\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}\to k^{\times}
.

The branched cover
\pi:Y\to X
can be described using geometric class field theory
as the pullback of a cyclic degree
m
cover of a generalized Jacobian of
X
.
Let
S\subset X(k)
be the set of branch points of
\pi
. Let
U\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X-S
and

V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Y-\pi^{-1}(S)
.
Then
\pi|_{U}:V\to U
is étale, and
V
is a
\mu_{m}
-torsor over
U
.
By
[
Tam03
, Proposition 3.5]
, the cover
\pi:Y\to X
corresponds to a line bundle
L
on
X
and a divisor
D
supported on
S
with coefficients in
\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
such that
L^{\otimes m}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is trivial. Furthermore, the proof shows that we may assume that
L=L_{1}
.

For a divisor
D
on
X
, we set
\left\lfloor D/m\right\rfloor\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{P\in X}\left\lfloor\ord_{P}(D)/m\right\rfloor P
where
\left\lfloor x\right\rfloor
denotes the greatest integer less than or equal to
x
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The line bundle
L_{i}
appearing in (
2.1
) equals
L^{\otimes i}(\left\lfloor iD/m\right\rfloor)
.

Proof.

This is established in the proof of
[
Tam03
, Claim (3.8)]
.
∎

We wish to study the Frobenius map on

(2.2)

H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})\simeq\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}}H^{1}(X,L_{i}).

The Frobenius sends
H^{1}(X,L_{i})
to
H^{1}(X,L_{pi})
, where the subscript is taken modulo
m
. Therefore it is natural to study its
r
th power, where
r
is the order of
p
in
(\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z})^{\times}
.
This is a semi-linear map
\varphi_{\pi,i}:H^{1}(X,L_{i})\to H^{1}(X,L_{i})
.
The next result provides an explicit description of
\varphi\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\varphi_{\pi,1}
;
a similar description is available for the other values of
i
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a cover
\pi:Y\to X
corresponding to
(L,D)
as above, fix an isomorphism
\iota:L^{\otimes m}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
. Then
\varphi:H^{1}(X,L)\to H^{1}(X,L)
is induced by the composition

L\overset{F^{r}}{\to}L^{\otimes p^{r}}=L\otimes L^{\otimes(p^{r}-1)}\overset{\iota}{\simeq}L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-(p^{r}-1)/mD)\hookrightarrow L.

Proof.

See
[
Tam03
, §3]
.
∎

Example 2.3
.

Let
Y
be an elliptic curve given in Legendre form by
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)
, and
\pi:Y\to X=\mathbf{P}^{1}
be the projection map onto the
x
-axis. Then
m=2
and
S=\{0,1,\lambda,\infty\}
.
The cover
\pi
is determined by the line bundle
L
of degree
-2
and the divisor
D=[0]+[1]+[\lambda]+[\infty]
.
Without loss of generality,
L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-2[\infty])
. Then the isomorphism
L^{\otimes 2}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-D)
is given by multiplying by the function
x(x-1)(x-\lambda)
.

So
L_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}
and
L=L_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-2[\infty])
.
Thus
\dim H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{0})=0
and
\dim H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L)=1
.
In the standard Čech description, the equivalence class of
x^{-1}
is a basis element of
H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L)
.
It follows that

\varphi(x^{-1})=x^{-p}\left(x(x-1)(x-\lambda)\right)^{(p-1)/2}\equiv H_{p}(\lambda)\mod{x^{-2}k[x^{-1}]\oplus k[x]},

where
H_{p}(\lambda)
is the coefficient of
x^{-1}
in
x^{-p}\left(x(x-1)(x-\lambda)\right)^{(p-1)/2}
.
Note
\varphi
is the zero map if and only if
H_{p}(\lambda)=0
, so this condition on
\lambda

is equivalent to
Y
being supersingular.

In fact,
H_{p}(\lambda)
is the polynomial appearing in
[
Sil09
, Theorem V.4.1(b)]
.
To see this, note that
H_{p}(\lambda)
is the coefficient of
x^{(p-1)/2}
in
\left((x-1)(x-\lambda)\right)^{(p-1)/2}
.
By the binomial theorem,

(2.3)

H_{p}(\lambda)=\sum_{j=0}^{(p-1)/2}\binom{(p-1)/2}{j}\binom{(p-1)/2}{(p-1)/2-j}(-1)^{j}(-\lambda)^{(p-1)/2-j}=\sum_{j=0}^{(p-1)/2}\binom{(p-1)/2}{j}^{2}\lambda^{j}.

2.2.
Special Families of Curves

We now consider a family of covers
\pi:C_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
such that there are restrictions on
the Newton polygons of the curves
C_{1}
in the family.

Consider the family
M[8]
of genus
3
curves that are
\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}
-covers of
\mathbf{P}^{1}
branched at 5 points
with inertia type
a=(1,1,2,2,2)
. (The notation
M[8]
is based on
[
Moo10
, Table 2]
.)
Each curve in this family can be given by an affine equation of the form:

(2.4)

C_{1}:=C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2}):y^{4}=x^{2}(x-1)^{2}(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2}),

for some
t_{1},t_{2}\in k-\{0,1\}
with
t_{1}\not=t_{2}
. The group
\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}\simeq\mu_{4}
naturally acts on
C_{1}
via acting on
y
, and projecting to the
x
-coordinate gives a
\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}
-cover
\phi:C_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. For the generic choice of
t_{1},t_{2}
,
the
p
-rank of
C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})
is
2
. If its
p
-rank is less than
2
, then
C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})
is supersingular.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
LMPT19a
, Section 6.1]
.
The main idea is that the signature (see Definition
4.3
) of the
\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}
-cover
\phi:C_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}

is
(2,0,1)
. The Kottwitz conditions place constraints on the Newton polygon of a principally polarized threefold with an action of

\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}
with this signature. In particular, there are only two choices for the Newton polygon,
which can be distinguished by the
p
-rank. For
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
, the generic one has
p
-rank
2
and the other
one is supersingular.
∎

We make this explicit by determining conditions on
t_{1},t_{2}
for
C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})
to be supersingular.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(p^{2}-1)/4
, and let
b_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})\in k[t_{1},t_{2}]
be the coefficient of
x^{2A}
in

(2.5)

\left((x-1)^{2}(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})\right)^{A}.

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. The curve
C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})
is supersingular if and only if
b_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})=0
.

Proof.

We apply the techniques from Section
2.1
to the cover
\phi:C_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
. In that notation,
X=\mathbf{P}^{1}
,
m=4
, and
S=\{0,1,t_{1},t_{2},\infty\}
. The cover corresponds to
L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-2[\infty])
and
D=2[0]+2[1]+2[\infty]+[t_{1}]+[t_{2}]
. The isomorphism
L^{\otimes 4}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-D)
is given by multiplying by the function
x^{2}(x-1)^{2}(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})
.

We decompose
\phi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}}
as a direct sum of four line bundles based on the
\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}
-action. By Proposition
2.1
, we see that

\displaystyle L_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}},\quad L_{1}

\displaystyle\simeq L=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-2[\infty]),\quad L_{2}\simeq L^{\otimes 2}([0]+[1]+[\infty])=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-[\infty])\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle L_{3}

\displaystyle\simeq L^{\otimes 3}([0]+[1]+[\infty])=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-3[\infty]).

Thus
\dim_{k}H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{i})
is
0,1,0,2
for
i=0,1,2,3
, respectively.

As
p\equiv 3\bmod{4}
, the Frobenius map permutes these subspaces of
H^{1}(C_{1},\mathcal{O}_{C_{1}})
.
In particular, it exchanges
H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{1})
and
H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{3})
.
The
p
-rank is the stable rank of the Hasse-Witt matrix for the Frobenius map.
If the composition

\varphi:H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{1})\to H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{3})\to H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},L_{1})

is the zero map, then the
p
-rank of
C_{1}
is at most one.
In this case,
C_{1}
is supersingular by Proposition
2.4
.
On the other hand, if
\varphi
is non-zero,
then the eventual image of the Frobenius is two dimensional and so the
p
-rank is two.
Thus
C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})
is supersingular if and only if
\varphi
is the zero map.

We use Proposition
2.2
to see the effect of the parameters
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
on
\varphi
.
Recall that
\varphi
comes from the map of line bundles
L\to L
which on local sections sends

(2.6)

f\mapsto f^{p^{2}}\left(x^{2}(x-1)^{2}(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})\right)^{A},

where
A=(p^{2}-1)/4
.
In the C̆ech description,
H^{1}(\mathbf{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^{1}}(-2))

consists of the ring of functions
k[x,x^{-1}]
, which are regular except at
0
and infinity,
modulo the functions in
k[x]
(which are regular except at infinity),
and modulo the functions in
x^{-2}k[x^{-1}]
(which are regular except at
0
and have at least a double zero at infinity).
In this quotient, the equivalence class of
x^{-1}
is non-zero and hence a basis element.
Thus using (
2.6
), we see that
\varphi(x^{-1})
is the equivalence class represented by
b_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})x^{-1}
,
where
b_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})
is the coefficient of
x^{-1}
in

x^{-p^{2}}\left(x^{2}(x-1)^{2}(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})\right)^{A}
.
Simplifying,
b_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})
equals the coefficient of
x^{(p^{2}-1)/2}=x^{2A}
in (
2.5
).
Thus
C_{1}(t_{1},t_{2})
is supersingular if and only if
\varphi
is the zero map if and only if
b_{p}(t_{1},t_{2})=0
.
∎

3.
Construction of the supersingular curve

We construct a supersingular curve of genus five as an unramified double cover of a supersingular curve in the
M[8]
family. This requires a special choice of the parameters
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
.

3.1.
Constructing Double Covers

In this subsection,
p
is an arbitrary odd prime. As before, let
C_{1}
be the curve in (
2.4
) and
let
\phi:C_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
be the projection onto the
x
-coordinate.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
h_{1}:D_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
be the cover given by
w^{2}=x
, with
h_{1}
again projection onto the
x
-coordinate.
Let
\pi:X\to C_{1}
be the pullback of
h_{1}
by
\phi
.

Note that
D_{1}
has genus
0
and
h_{1}
is ramified over
0
and infinity. When we want to denote dependence of
X
on the parameters
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, we write
X(t_{1},t_{2})
for
X
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The cover
\pi:X\to C_{1}
is an unramified double cover, and
X
has genus
5
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Abhyankar’s Lemma, because the covers
h_{1}
and
\phi
both have inertia
of order two above
x=0
and
x=\infty
.
The second statement follows from the Riemann–Hurwitz formula.
∎

.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be the elliptic curves given by the following Weierstrass equations:

(3.1)

E_{1}:y^{2}=x(x-t_{1})(x-t_{2})\mbox{ and }E_{2}:y^{2}=x(x-r)(x+r)\mbox{ where }r=\sqrt{(1-t_{1})/(1-t_{2})}.

Proposition 3.4
.

With notation as in (
3.1
),
there is an isogeny
\mathrm{Jac}(X)\sim\mathrm{Jac}(C_{1})\times E_{1}\times E_{2}
.
In particular, the Prym of
\pi:X\to C_{1}
is isogenous to
E_{1}\times E_{2}
.
The degree of these isogenies is a power of
2
.

Proof.

The reader may find it helpful to reference Figure
1
.
The
\mathbf{Z}/4\mathbf{Z}
-cover
\phi
factors as a composition of degree two covers
\psi_{1}:C_{1}\to D_{2}
and
h_{2}:D_{2}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
.
Above each of
x=0,1,\infty
, the curve
C_{1}
has two points, with inertia group of order
2
.
Above each of
x=t_{1},t_{2}
, the curve
C_{1}
has one point, with inertia group of order
4
.
Thus
h_{2}
is ramified only over
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, so
D_{2}
has genus
0
.

Consider the fiber product of
h_{1}:D_{1}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
and
h_{2}:D_{2}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
.
It is a Klein-
4
cover
C_{2}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
, and factors through a third double cover
D_{3}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Note that this third cover is ramified over
0,\infty,t_{1},t_{2}
, the set of points over which exactly one of
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
is ramified. Thus
D_{3}
is isomorphic to the elliptic curve
E_{1}
. The Kani-Rosen theorem
[
KR89
, Theorem B]
shows that
\Jac(C_{2})
is isogenous to
E_{1}
as
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
have genus
0
.

Let
\psi_{2}:C_{2}\to D_{2}
be the pullback of
h_{1}
by
h_{2}
. The fiber product of
\psi_{1}:C_{1}\to D_{2}
and
\psi_{2}:C_{2}\to D_{2}
is a Klein-
4
cover
X\to D_{2}
. It factors through a third double cover
\psi_{3}:C_{3}\to D_{2}
. Note that
\psi_{1}
is ramified over eight points: the unique point of
D_{2}
above each of
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbf{P}^{1}
and the two points of
D_{2}
above each of
0,1,\infty\in\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Furthermore,
\psi_{2}
is ramified at four points: the two points of
D_{2}
above
0
and
\infty
. Thus
\psi_{3}
is branched over four points of
D_{2}
: those above
t_{1},t_{2},1\in\mathbf{P}^{1}
. Thus
C_{3}
has genus one. By the Kani-Rosen theorem
[
KR89
, Theorem B]
, we conclude that

\Jac(X)\sim\Jac(C_{1})\times\Jac(C_{2})\times\Jac(C_{3}).

To find an explicit equation for
C_{3}
, we note that an affine equation for
h_{2}:D_{2}\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is
z^{2}=\frac{x-t_{1}}{x-t_{2}}
. The function
z
identifies
D_{2}
with
\mathbf{P}^{1}
, and the points of
D_{2}
above
t_{1},t_{2},1\in\mathbf{P}^{1}
correspond to
z=0,\infty,\pm r
, respectively. Thus
C_{3}
is isomorphic to
E_{2}
. Thus

\Jac(X)\sim\Jac(C_{1})\times E_{1}\times E_{2}.

The final statement follows because each decomposition in the Kani-Rosen theorem
arises from an idempotent relation and the constants in the idempotent relation are powers of
2
.
∎

3.2.
The supersingular locus

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
. Recall Notation
3.1
. Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

the genus
5
curve
X
is supersingular;

(ii)

the genus

4.
Refining Ekedahl’s Results

Suppose
\phi:Y\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
is a tamely ramified abelian cover branched at exactly the three points
0,1,\infty
.
In
[
Eke87
, page 173]
, Ekedahl enumerates ways
\phi
can ramify so that
Y
has genus
5
.
Up to rearrangement of
\{0,1,\infty\}
, there are seven cases,
as listed in Propositions
4.6
,
4.8
,
and
4.11
.

Ekedahl studies the situation when
Y
is superspecial, meaning that
\mathrm{Jac}(Y)
is
isomorphic to a product of supersingular elliptic curves. The superspecial condition is equivalent to the Frobenius operator being trivial on
H^{1}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y})
.
For each of the seven cases,
Ekedahl determines when
Y
is superspecial in terms of a congruence condition on
p
.
His result demonstrates that there exists a superspecial smooth curve of genus
5
which is an abelian cover of
\mathbf{P}^{1}

branched at
3
points if and only if

p\equiv-1\bmod 11,12,15,20,8
or
p\equiv-4\bmod 15
.

If
Y
is superspecial, then it is supersingular, but the converse
is not always true.
To determine the (slightly less restrictive) conditions under which
Y
is
supersingular, we use the Shimura–Taniyama formula.
We include this material to demonstrate that these previously-known constructions do not produce supersingular curves of genus
5
for all primes
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
.

4.1.
Ramification Data

Let
\phi:Y\to\mathbf{P}^{1}
be a tamely ramified Galois cover branched exactly at
\Br:=\{0,1,\infty\}
.
We suppose that the Galois group

of

is abelian.
For

, let

be the inertia group above

.
Note that

is cyclic.

Definition 4.1
.

The

for the cover

, where

is the index of

in

The ramification data

to simplify the equations for

After we fix an identification of

Definition 4.2
.

See e.g.,
[

, Definition 3.2, Definition 4.1]
.
The
inertia type

, where

denotes the

for

found from the action of the

Note that

It is well-known that

4.2.

We recall some material from
[

Suppose

coprime to

Definition 4.3
.

be the dimension of the

Let

Then

[

For degree

[

, Lemma 3.1]

•

\Jac(Y)
has complex multiplication by
\prod_{d}\mathbf{Q}(\zeta_{d})
,
where the product is taken over all integers

•

f_{j}\in\{0,1\}

The Newton polygon of

can be found by studying the action of Frobenius. Let

denote the set of orbits of the multiplication by

map
[\times p]
on the set
\{x\in\mathbf{Z}/m\mathbf{Z}:x\neq 0\}

The set

For each orbit

For

S_{\epsilon}=\{1\leq j<m\mid d_{j}\text{ does not divide }a_{b}\text{ for }b\in\Br,\text{ and }f_{j}=\epsilon\}.

For

.

There is a canonical decomposition of the

When

,
fix a

-divisible group

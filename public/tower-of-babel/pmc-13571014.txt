Osteoporosis Increases the Risk of Periprosthetic Fracture and Loosening after Total Elbow Arthroplasty: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Akin A. Adio, Mohammad Daher, Jehad Feras Alsamhori, James Satalich, Eric M. Black, Brian W. Hill, John G. Horneff, Joseph A. Abboud
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionOsteoporosis may adversely affect implant fixation and bone integrity following total elbow arthroplasty (TEA), yet its impact on postoperative complications remains incompletely defined. This study compared outcomes following TEA in patients with and without osteoporosis.MethodsA retrospective analysis was performed including 1,410 adult patients who underwent total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) with a minimum follow-up of two years. Patients with osteoporosis were matched to an equal number of patients without osteoporosis based on demographics, comorbidities, surgical indication, and preoperative inflammatory laboratory markers. Outcome measures included two-year rates of revision, periprosthetic fracture, implant loosening, prosthetic joint infection (PJI), ulnar nerve injury, and instability. A secondary analysis of 520 patients with osteoporosis compared those who received anti-osteoporosis medication to those who did not. Complication rates were compared between groups using chi-squared testing.ResultsPatients with osteoporosis had higher two-year rates of periprosthetic fracture (4.4% vs. 1.4%, p<0.001) and implant loosening (5.0% vs. 2.3%, p=0.007). There were no differences in revision (p=0.144), PJI (p=0.479), ulnar nerve injury (p=0.228), or instability (p=0.692). Among patients with osteoporosis, medication use was not associated with differences in revision, implant loosening, PJI, or ulnar nerve injury. Patients on medication had a lower rate of periprosthetic fracture that did not reach significance (p=0.061).ConclusionOsteoporosis is associated with increased risk of periprosthetic fracture and implant loosening following TEA. These findings suggest that impaired bone quality may contribute to structural modes of failure, highlighting the importance of preoperative bone health optimization in patients undergoing TEA.

Introduction

Total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) is an established reconstructive procedure for patients with end-stage rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, and other advanced elbow pathologies.1-3 In recent years, trauma has become the leading indication for TEA, accounting for approximately 40–50% of procedures worldwide.4-6 Among patients aged ≥65 years, the proportion of TEA performed for distal humerus fractures increased from 60% in 2010 to 71% in 2018, reflecting a shift toward fracture-related indications in osteoporotic populations. 7 Although TEA demonstrates favorable survivorship, high rates of implant loosening, periprosthetic fracture, and infection have been reported.5,8,9

Osteoporosis is highly prevalent among patients undergoing TEA.5,10 Osteoporosis is characterized by decreased bone mineral density and compromised structural integrity, leading to reduced fixation strength and increased fragility. 11 Prior arthroplasty literature has demonstrated that osteoporosis is associated with higher rates of periprosthetic fracture and hardware failure.12,13 In the setting of TEA, where distal humeral bone stock may already be limited, compromised bone quality may further amplify the risk of aseptic loosening and periprosthetic fracture. 14 Despite the projected increase in osteoporotic distal humerus fractures, large-scale data specifically evaluating osteoporosis-related postoperative outcomes in TEA remain limited. 15

Given the evolving indications for TEA and the increasing burden of osteoporosis in the surgical population, clarifying the impact of reduced bone mineral density on postoperative outcomes is clinically important. This study aims to evaluate the association between osteoporosis and postoperative outcomes following TEA. This study compares 2-year surgical outcomes between patients undergoing TEA with and without osteoporosis. We hypothesized that patients with osteoporosis would experience higher complication rates compared with patients with normal bone density.

Methods

This study used a retrospective cohort design to examine postoperative outcomes after TEA among patients with and without osteoporosis. Data were drawn from the TriNetX Research Network, which compiles deidentified electronic health records contributed by approximately 170 participating healthcare organizations across the United States. The query for deidentified patient data was executed on February 14, 2026. Adult patients undergoing primary TEA between January 1, 2005, and February 1, 2024, were included. Patients were required to have a minimum of 2 years of documented activity in the database following the index procedure to ensure adequate follow-up and reduce loss-to-follow-up bias. As the analysis relied exclusively on deidentified, aggregated records containing no individually identifiable information, it was exempt from Institutional Review Board review.

Cohort Identification

Patients undergoing primary TEA were identified using the Current Procedural Terminology code for total elbow arthroplasty (CPT 24363). A complete list of all procedure, diagnosis, medication, and outcome codes is provided in Supplemental Table 1. Osteoporosis was defined by the presence of International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) codes for osteoporosis (M80 and M81) prior to the index TEA. The primary analysis compared patients with osteoporosis to patients without osteoporosis. Patients with osteopenia or any disorder of bone density were excluded. Osteopenia and other disorders of bone density and structure are classified under distinct ICD-10 codes (e.g., M85.8-), separate from osteoporosis (M80, M81); these patients were excluded so that the comparison contrasted patients with definite osteoporosis against those without any documented low-bone-density disorder, preventing intermediate bone-density states from diluting either cohort. A separate analysis was attempted with these patients, but not reported due to insufficient numbers. A secondary analysis was performed within the osteoporosis cohort to evaluate the impact of anti-osteoporosis medication use. Medication exposure was defined by the presence of prescriptions for bisphosphonates, denosumab, estrogen modulators, parathyroid hormone (PTH) analogs, and calcitonin in the year leading up to TEA. 16 Patients with osteoporosis were stratified into those with and without anti-osteoporosis medication use.

1:1 Propensity-Matched Cohort

The TriNetX platform was used to perform 1:1 propensity score matching employing logistic regression. Nearest-neighbor matching with a caliper of 0.01 was applied, and balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (reported in Table 1); demographic and comorbidity covariates achieved a standardized mean difference < 0.1 after matching. Outcomes

Two-year postoperative complications were evaluated following the index TEA. Outcomes of interest included periprosthetic fracture, implant loosening, prosthetic joint infection, ulnar nerve injury, instability, and revision arthroplasty.

Statistical Analysis

For all outcomes of interest, relative risks (RRs), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and p-values were computed using the TriNetX analytic platform. Categorical variables were compared using chi-squared tests, and continuous variables were assessed using Student’s t-tests. Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. Because multiple outcomes were compared, a Bonferroni correction was applied; with six outcomes assessed per analysis, the adjusted significance threshold was p < 0.0083, and Bonferroni-corrected p-values are reported in Tables 2 and 3. Relative risks with 95% confidence intervals are reported as the primary measure of association, and absolute risk differences with the corresponding number needed to harm are reported for outcomes that were significantly increased. Results

Prior to propensity score matching, a total of 851 patients with osteoporosis and 4,358 patients without osteoporosis were identified, with osteoporosis comprising 16.3% of the overall cohort. Patients with osteoporosis demonstrated significant baseline differences compared with those without osteoporosis (Table 1). Inflammatory polyarthropathies, including rheumatoid arthritis, fracture of the distal humerus, and spondyloarthropathies were all more prevalent in the osteoporosis cohort (all p < 0.001). After 1:1 propensity score matching, demographic characteristics, comorbidities, surgical indications, and inflammatory laboratory markers were well balanced between cohorts, with no significant differences observed (p > 0.05).

Primary Analysis

Postoperative outcomes following TEA were compared between patients with and without osteoporosis (n = 705 per cohort). Osteoporosis was associated with higher rates of periprosthetic fracture (4.40% vs 1.42%; RR 3.10, 95% CI 1.53-6.28; absolute risk difference [ARD] 3.0%, number needed to harm [NNH] 34) and implant loosening (4.97% vs 2.27%; RR 2.19, 95% CI 1.22-3.92; ARD 2.7%, NNH 37); both remained significant after Bonferroni correction (p < 0.001 and p = 0.007, respectively). Revision was also more frequent in the osteoporosis cohort (4.82% vs 2.55%; RR 1.89, 95% CI 1.08-3.31; ARD 2.3%, NNH 44), although this did not reach the Bonferroni-corrected threshold (p = 0.024). No significant differences were observed in prosthetic joint infection (RR 1.24, 95% CI 0.68-2.26), ulnar nerve injury (RR 1.42, 95% CI 0.80-2.50), or instability (RR 0.86, 95% CI 0.40-1.84). These findings are summarized in Table 2.

Subgroup Analysis: Osteoporosis With vs Without Pharmacologic Treatment

Among patients with osteoporosis, outcomes were compared between those receiving pharmacologic treatment and those without treatment (n = 260 per cohort). There were no significant differences in implant loosening (RR 1.12, 95% CI 0.59-2.10), prosthetic joint infection (RR 1.73, 95% CI 0.84-3.55), ulnar nerve injury (RR 1.13, 95% CI 0.51-2.51), or revision (RR 1.50, 95% CI 0.74-3.05). Periprosthetic fracture demonstrated a lower rate in the treatment group, though this did not reach statistical significance (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.29-1.35; p = 0.061). Instability outcomes could not be reported due to cell suppression. These results are summarized in Table 3.

Discussion

This cohort study is, to our knowledge, the first large database analysis to evaluate the impact of osteoporosis on postoperative outcomes following TEA. We found that patients with osteoporosis experienced significantly higher rates of periprosthetic fracture and implant loosening compared with patients without osteoporosis. Collectively, these findings suggest that impaired bone quality in osteoporosis may be associated with structural modes of failure following TEA. As an observational analysis, this study identifies associations rather than causal relationships and should be regarded as hypothesis-generating.

The observed increase in implant failure may be related to the effects of osteoporosis on bone quality and implant fixation. Reduced bone mineral density is associated with cortical thinning and trabecular deterioration. 17 These are factors that severely compromise the stability of the bone–implant interface. 18 In arthroplasty, this manifests as increased micromotion and reduced resistance to physiologic loading, predisposing to aseptic loosening and early implant failure.19-21 Additionally, osteoporotic bone is inherently more susceptible to fragility-type injury. 22 This further increases the risk of periprosthetic fracture, even under low-energy conditions.

Poor bone stock is particularly pronounced in TEA. Prior studies have highlighted that limited bone stock and smaller osseous structures in TEA reduce tolerance to mechanical stress. 23 For example, Egol et al demonstrated that fixation in osteoporotic distal humerus fractures is prone to failure, underscoring the mechanical limitations of compromised bone in this region. 24 In addition, aseptic loosening and periprosthetic fracture have been identified as leading causes of failure and revision. 25 This further supports the central role of structural bone integrity in determining outcomes. Together, elbow biomechanics and poor bone quality may contribute to structural failure and revision risk.

In our study, pharmacologic management of osteoporosis was not associated with significant differences in postoperative outcomes. However, this analysis was likely underpowered to detect meaningful effects. We nonetheless retained this analysis because it provides the first TEA-specific data on pharmacologic osteoporosis treatment and structural outcomes, offering a preliminary, hypothesis-generating signal to inform future adequately powered studies. We observed a non-significant trend toward reduced periprosthetic fracture in patients with anti-osteoporosis medication use. Prior arthroplasty literature has suggested potential benefits of anti-osteoporotic therapy.26,27 While our results did not demonstrate a significant effect, the role of osteoporosis treatment in TEA remains an important area for further investigation. Clinically, these findings highlight the importance of identifying osteoporosis prior to TEA. Surgeons may consider strategies to optimize fixation in patients with osteoporosis. This includes the use of cemented components, longer stems, and careful canal preparation.28,29 Augmentation techniques may also be considered when necessary to mitigate the risk of structural complications in the setting of limited bone stock. 23

This study has several limitations. As a retrospective database analysis, it is subject to potential coding inaccuracies and misclassification of both osteoporosis and postoperative complications. Osteoporosis is substantially underdiagnosed, so some control patients likely had undiagnosed disease, while patients carrying a diagnosis may represent those with more severe disease or greater healthcare contact; excluding patients with osteopenia and other low-bone-density disorders may further accentuate this misclassification, which threatens the validity of the exposure classification. Bone mineral density and DXA values were not available to corroborate diagnostic coding. In addition, TriNetX does not provide laterality modifiers, and several codes lack anatomic specificity. Although propensity score matching was performed, residual confounding may persist. Unmeasured confounders, including glucocorticoid use, activity level, and specific surgical indication, were not captured; analyses were restricted to primary TEA, which mitigates confounding by revision status. Important factors relating to implant survival such as bone mineral density measurements, implant design, and fixation technique were not available. Additionally, pharmacologic treatment does not account for adherence or duration of therapy. Moreover, the medication cohort was also smaller in size, limiting statistical power to detect differences between treated and untreated patients.

Conclusion

Osteoporosis is associated with increased risk of periprosthetic fracture, implant loosening, and revision following TEA. These findings highlight the importance of bone quality in structural failure mechanisms and may support the incorporation of osteoporosis assessment into preoperative risk stratification and surgical planning.

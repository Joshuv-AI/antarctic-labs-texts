Contour Integrations and Parity Results of Hurwitz-type Cyclotomic Euler Sums
Hongyuan Rui

Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we investigate the parity of three class of Hurwitz-type cyclotomic Euler sums using the methods of contour integration and residue computation, and derive explicit parity formulas for linear, quadratic, and some higher-order cases. Based on their connection with cyclotomic multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm functions, we further obtain certain parity results for these functions. At the end of the paper, we propose two conjectures regarding the parity and symmetry of multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm functions of arbitrary depth.

Keywords
: Contour integration; Hurwitz-type Cyclotomic Euler sums; Residue theorem; Parity result; Cyclotomic multiple zeta values; Cyclotomic multiple
t
-values; Cyclotomic multiple
T
-values.

11M32, 11M99.

Introduction

In 1735, Euler solved the famous
Basel problem
and discovered the following well-known formula:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{1^{2}}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2}}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{3^{2}}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{2}}+\cdots=\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

(1.1)

Furthermore, he provided a more general formula:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{1^{2m}}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2m}}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{3^{2m}}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{2m}}+\cdots=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m-1}B_{2m}(2\pi)^{2m}}{2(2m)!}\quad(m\in\mathbb{N}),

(1.2)

where
B_{k}
denotes the
Bernoulli numbers
defined by

\displaystyle\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{B_{n}}{n!}x^{n}}.

The series on the left side of the above formula is now known as the value of the Riemann zeta function at positive even integers, where the
Riemann zeta function
is defined as:

\displaystyle\zeta(s):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{s}}\quad(\Re(s)>1).

(1.3)

In a paper published in 1776, Euler
[
3
,
9

\displaystyle S_{p,q}:=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{H_{n}^{(p)}}{n^{q}}\quad(p,q\in\mathbb{N}\ \text{and}\ q>1),

(1.4)

where

In

In 1998, Flajolet and Salvy developed a contour integral theory to systematically study Euler sums of the following general form:

\displaystyle{S_{{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}},q}}:=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{{H_{n}^{\left({{p_{1}}}\right)}H_{n}^{\left({{p_{2}}}\right)}\cdots H_{n}^{\left({{p_{k}}}\right)}}}{{{n^{q}}}}},

(1.5)

A nonlinear Euler sum

reduces to a combination of sums of lower orders whenever the weight
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}+q

Moreover, Flajolet and Salvy highlighted the connection between these Euler sums and multiple zeta values. By utilizing the stuffle relations (see

Due to their profound connections with various mathematical and physical disciplines such as knot theory, algebraic geometry, and theoretical physics, the study of multiple zeta values has attracted considerable interest from numerous mathematicians and physicists. (2.26)

where

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

be roots of unity,

and

\displaystyle=x\Li_{p}(y;a)\Li_{q}\Big((xy)^{-1};a\Big)+(-1)^{q}y^{-1}\Li_{p}(y;a)\Li_{q}(xy;1-a)+(-1)^{p+q-1}\Li_{p+q}(x;1-a)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\binom{p+q-m-2}{q-1}\left((-1)^{m}\Li_{m+1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{m+1}\Big(x^{-1};a\Big)\right)\Li_{p+q-m-1}(xy)

(3.37)

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is based on residue calculations of the following contour integral:

The integrand

has the following poles throughout the complex plane: 1. All integers (simple poles); 2.
-a
(pole of order
p+q)
and 3.
-(n+a)
(for positive integer
n
, poles of order
p
). Applying Lemma
2.2
-
2.5
, by direct calculations, we deduce the following residues

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p,q}(x,y;s),n\right)=\displaystyle\frac{x^{-n}y^{-n-1}}{(n+a)^{q}}\left(\Li_{p}(y;a)-\zeta_{n}(p;y;a-1)\right)\quad(n\geq 0),

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p,q}(x,y;s),-n\right)=(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\frac{(xy)^{n}}{(n-a)^{q}}\left(\Li_{p}(y;a)y^{-1}+(-1)^{p}\zeta_{n}\Big(p;y^{-1};-a\Big)\right)\quad(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p,q}(x,y;s),-n-a\right)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-n-a}\displaystyle\frac{d^{p-1}}{ds^{p-1}}\left((s+n+a)^{p}F^{(a)}_{p,q}(x,y;s)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{p-1}\binom{p+q-m-2}{q-1}\left((-1)^{m}\Li_{m+1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{m+1}\Big(x^{-1};a\Big)\right)\displaystyle\frac{(xy)^{n}}{n^{p+q-m-1}}\quad(n\geq 1)

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p,q}(x,y;s),-a\right)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p+q-1)!}\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow-a}\displaystyle\frac{d^{p+q-1}}{ds^{p+q-1}}\left((s+a)^{p+q}F^{(a)}_{p,q}(x,y;s)\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p+q-1}\Li_{p+q}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{p+q}\Big(x^{-1};a\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{m+k=q-1,\atop m,k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\binom{k+p-1}{p-1}\Li_{k+p}(y)\left((-1)^{m}\Li_{m+1}(x;1-a)-x\Li_{m+1}\Big(x^{-1};a\Big)\right).

, we know that

Finally, combining these four contributions yields the statement of Theorem

∎

3.3
Generalized Hurwitz-type Cyclotomic Euler Sums

Finally, in this subsection, we employ contour integration to present three statement theorems for the case of arbitrary order of these three types of Hurwitz-type cyclotomic Euler sums.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q\geq 1
with
(p_{j},x_{j})
and
(q,xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})\neq(1,1)
. The

\displaystyle xS_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}^{(a-1)}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q+r}S_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}^{(-a)}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};xx_{1}\cdots x_{r}\Big)

reduces to a combination of sums of lower orders.

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is based on residue calculations of the following contour integral:

\displaystyle\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};s)ds

\displaystyle:=\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p_{1}-1)}(s+a;x_{1})\phi^{(p_{2}-1)}(s+a;x_{2})\cdots\phi^{(p_{r}-1)}(s+a;x_{r})}{(p_{1}-1)!(p_{2}-1)!\cdots(p_{r}-1)!(s+a)^{q}}(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}-r}ds=0.

Obviously, the integrand
F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};s)
possesses the following poles in the complex plane: 1. All integer points are simple poles; 2.
s=-a
is a pole of order
p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q
; 3.
s=-n-a
(where
n
is a positive integer) is a pole of order
p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n-a\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-a\right)=0.

(3.43)

At integer points, which are simple zeros, the residue values can be calculated using Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)=\displaystyle\frac{x^{-n}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-n-1}}{(n+a)^{q}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(\Li_{p_{j}}(x_{j};a)-\zeta_{n}(p_{j};x_{j};a-1)\right)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)=\displaystyle\frac{(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{n}}{(-n+a)^{q}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(\Li_{p_{j}}(x_{j};a)x_{j}^{-1}+(-1)^{p_{j}}\zeta_{n}\Big(p_{j};x_{j}^{-1};-a\Big)\right)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

By expanding the two residue values above and then summing them, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)

\displaystyle=xS_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}^{(a-1)}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}\Big)(-1)^{r}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q}S_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}^{(-a)}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};xx_{1}\cdots x_{r}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\{\text{combinations of lower-order sums}\}.

Applying Lemmas
2.2
and
2.5
, we can also compute the latter two residue values in (
3.3
). However, the resulting sum obtained after summation will still be of order less than
r
, namely:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n-a\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F^{(a)}_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-a\right)

\displaystyle\in\{\text{combinations of lower-order sums}\}.

Finally, substituting these two conclusions into (
3.3
) completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

In this section, by employing the relationship between Hurwitz-type cyclotomic Euler
S

According to definition of linear Hurwitz-type cyclotomic Euler
S
-sums and double Hurwitz polylogarithm function with
2
-variables, we have

\displaystyle S_{p;q}^{(a)}(x;y)=\Li_{p,q}(x,y;a+1)+\Li_{p+q}(xy;a+1)

Therefore, we can derive the following corollary regarding the parity of multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm function.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
x,y
be
N
-th roots of unity and
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, and
p,q\geq 1
with
(p,y),(q,y)\neq(1,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\Li_{p,q}(x,y;a)-(-1)^{p+q}xy\Li_{p,q}\Big(x^{-1},y^{-1};1-a\Big)

(4.44)

reduces to a combination of single Hurwitz polylogarithm functions.

Example 4.2
.

Let
(p,q)=(2,2)
and
(p,q)=(2,3)
in Corollary
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\Li_{2,2}\Big(x,y;a\Big)-xy\Li_{2,2}\Big(x^{-1},y^{-1};1-a\Big)

\displaystyle=-\Li_{4}(xy;a)+xy\Li_{4}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)+\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{2}(y;a)+y\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{2}\Big(y^{-1};1-a\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-xy\Li_{4}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)+2xy\left(\Li_{1}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)-(xy)^{-1}\Li_{1}(xy;a)\right)\Li_{3}\Big(y^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-xy\left(\Li_{2}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)+(xy)^{-1}\Li_{2}(xy;a)\right)\Li_{2}\Big(y^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2xy\left((xy)^{-1}\Li_{1}(xy;a)-\Li_{1}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)\right)\Li_{3}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-xy\left((xy)^{-1}\Li_{2}(xy;a)+\Li_{2}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)\right)\Li_{2}(x),

\displaystyle\Li_{2,3}\Big(x,y;a\Big)+xy\Li_{2,3}\Big(x^{-1},y^{-1};1-a\Big)

\displaystyle=-\Li_{5}(xy;a)-xy\Li_{5}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)+\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{3}(y;a)-y\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{3}\Big(y^{-1};1-a\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+xy\Li_{5}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)-3xy\left(\Li_{1}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)-(xy)^{-1}\Li_{1}\Big(xy;a\Big)\right)\Li_{4}\Big(y^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+xy\left(\Li_{2}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)+(xy)^{-1}\Li_{2}(xy;a)\right)\Li_{3}\Big(y^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-3xy\left((xy)^{-1}\Li_{1}\Big(xy;a\Big)-\Li_{1}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)\right)\Li_{4}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+2xy\left((xy)^{-1}\Li_{2}\Big(xy;a\Big)+\Li_{2}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)\right)\Li_{3}(x)

\displaystyle\quad-xy\left((xy)^{-1}\Li_{3}\Big(xy;a\Big)-\Li_{3}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)\right)\Li_{2}(x).

According to definition of Hurwitz-type cyclotomic quadratic Euler
S
-sums and multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm function with
3
-variables, we have

\displaystyle S^{(a)}_{p_{1},p_{2};q}(x_{1},x_{2};x)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(p_{1};x_{1};a)\zeta_{n}(p_{2};x_{2};a)}{(n+a)^{q}}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(\zeta_{n}(p_{1};x_{1};a)-\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1};a+1))\zeta_{n}(p_{2};x_{2};a)}{(n+a)^{q}}x^{n}+\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1};a+1)\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(p_{2};x_{2};a)}{(n+a)^{q}}x^{n}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{(\zeta_{n}(p_{1};x_{1};a)-\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1};a+1))\zeta_{n-1}(p_{2};x_{2};a)}{(n+a)^{q}}x^{n}+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(p_{1};x_{1};a)-\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1};a+1)}{(n+a)^{p_{2}+q}}(xx_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1};a+1)\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n-1}(p_{2};x_{2};a)+\displaystyle\frac{x_{2}^{n}}{(n+a)^{p_{2}}}}{(n+a)^{q}}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-\Li_{p_{2},q,p_{1}}(x_{2},x,x_{1};a+1)-\Li_{p_{2}+q,p_{1}}(x_{2}x,x_{1};a+1)

\displaystyle\quad+\Li_{p_{1}}(x_{1};a+1)\left(\Li_{p_{2},q}(x_{2},x;a+1)+\Li_{p_{2}+q}(xx_{2};a+1)\right).

.

reduces to a combination of multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm function with depth
\leq 2

Example 4.4
.

Let
(p,q,r)=(2,1,2)
in Corollary
4.3

\displaystyle\Li_{2,1,2}(x,y,z;a)-xyz\Li_{2,1,2}\Big(x^{-1},y^{-1},z^{-1};1-a\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-xyz\Li_{2}\Big(z^{-1};1-a\Big)\left(\Li_{2,1}\Big(x^{-1},y^{-1};1-a\Big)+\Li_{3}\Big((xy)^{-1};1-a\Big)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-S_{2;3}^{(a-1)}(z;xy)-S_{2;3}^{(a-1)}(x;yz)-\Li_{2}(z;a)S_{2;1}^{(a-1)}(x;y)-\Li_{2}(x;a)S_{2;1}^{(a-1)}(z;y)

\displaystyle\quad+xyz\Li_{5}\Big((xyz)^{-1};1-a\Big)+\Li_{2}(z;a)\Li_{3}(xy;a)+\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{3}(yz;a)

\displaystyle\quad+\Li_{2}(z;a)\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{1}(y;a)-y\Li_{2}(z;a)\Li_{2}(x;a)\Li_{1}\Big(y^{-1};1-a\Big)

Finally, based on our observations and computations, we conclude this paper by proposing the following conjecture concerning the parity of multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm functions of arbitrary depth.

Conjecture 4.5
.

Let
r>1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity and
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 1
with
(k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r};a)+(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{r}\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\Big(x^{-1}_{1},\ldots,x^{-1}_{r};1-a\Big)

can be expressed in terms of a rational linear combination of multiple Hurwitz polylogarithm functions with depth
<r
.

Evidently, Conjecture 1.2 regarding cyclotomic multiple
t
-values in Reference
[
25
]
represents a special case of this conjecture.
Actually, the parity result conjectured in Conjecture
4.5
also has a counterpart, known as the symmetry conjecture, which can be stated as follows:

Conjecture 4.6
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity and
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
. For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
(k_{1},x_{1}),\ (k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r};a)\equiv(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}-1}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})\Li_{k_{r},\ldots,k_{1}}(x^{-1}_{r},\ldots,x_{1}^{-1};1-a)\quad(\text{mod products}),

where “mod products” means discarding all product terms of cyclotomic multiple Hurwitz zeta values with depth
<r
.

Using the antipode relations for multiple polylogarithms established in
[
31
]
, Conjecture
4.6
can be directly derived from Conjecture
4.5
.
The symmetric result presented in Conjecture
4.6
should be regularizable. In a recent paper by Charlton and Hoffman
[
8
]
, they established symmetric results for regularized multiple
t
-values. The methods in their paper may also be applied to prove the symmetric result for cyclotomic multiple Hurwitz zeta values in Conjecture
4.6
of this paper, and even extend to the regularized setting. This, in turn, could help prove the parity result in Conjecture
4.5
and its regularized version as well. Due to space limitations, we will not elaborate in detail here. The authors plan to pursue this direction in subsequent work. Interested readers are also encouraged to explore this topic further.

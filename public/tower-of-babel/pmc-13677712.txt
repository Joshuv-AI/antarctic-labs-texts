Meter-depth and multi-polarization addressable zoom 3D meta-holography
Authors: Di Wang, Xin Xie, Qian Huang, Chao Liu, Xiao-Ke Lu, Fan-Chuan Lin, Yu-Cheng Lin, Yi-Long Li, Rui-Yi Zhao, Zhao-Song Li, Long Ren, Xiao-Qi Gao, Din Ping Tsai, Qiong-Hua Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPropelled by rapid advances in nanofabrication, meta-holography has become a frontier hot spot in optics. 3D meta-holography, in particular the large depth and multi-polarization addressable ones, with extraordinary expansion of the control dimension and information capacity, offers tremendous application prospects. However, the existing meta-holography is predominantly limited to a centimeter-level depth range, and independent regulation of multi-polarization is difficult to achieve. Here, we propose a meter-depth and multi-polarization addressable zoom 3D meta-holography. By revealing a polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle, the designed metasurface not only overcomes the conventional depth range limitations, but also enables multiple polarization control. By developing the special liquid materials, a 6 mm large-aperture polarized liquid lens with high focal power is proposed and integrated with the metasurface. Furthermore, the designed polarized liquid lens achieves simultaneous regulation capability of both focal length and polarization state by using a single driving voltage based on the integration of the liquid crystal light valve. The proposed meta-holography achieves multi-polarization addressable 3D reconstruction, with the depth of the meta-holographic image extending to the meter scale, which is ~80 times larger than that of the conventional Fourier meta-holography. This meter-scale depth and multi-polarization addressable zoom 3D meta-holography is expected to meet broad application requirements in encryption, optical storage and super-resolution imaging.Subject terms: Metamaterials, Displays, Electronic devices, Imaging and sensing

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of nanofabrication, meta-holography has emerged as a vibrant frontier in photonics owing to its exceptional characteristics, including ultra-high resolution, compact integration, lightweight size, and multi-dimensional control1–8. Meta-holography enables free manipulation of the amplitude, phase, and polarization state of light wavefront, thus greatly expanding the control dimension and information capacity9–12. Therefore, it has important research value in the fields of encryption, optical storage, and super-resolution imaging13–16. Large depth 3D meta-holography is very important for accurately judging the spatial information relationship of objects. In fields such as 3D detection and industrial inspection, an increase in depth enables more depth planes, which in turn enhances the plane density and detection accuracy. Compared with traditional holography, meta-holography offers higher resolution, larger viewing angle, and a more compact system. Therefore, achieving large‑depth meta-holography is of great importance.

The traditional meta-holography based on Fresnel diffraction is near-field diffraction, and it is necessary to balance the relationship between aliasing and depth in the process of diffraction propagation17–20. As a far-field diffraction method, Fourier meta-holography usually uses lenses for reconstruction. The reconstruction distance is intrinsically limited to the focal length of the lens, constraining reconstructed images to the focal plane and consequently preventing 3D reconstruction across large depth ranges. So, the traditional Fourier meta-holographic reconstruction is a 2D image21–25. In 2015, researchers proposed a helicity-multiplexed meta-hologram, which realized 2D polarized image reconstruction at a distance of 5 cm26. In 2022, an advanced noninterleaved metasurface was designed based on the optimized Gerchberg-Saxton algorithm27. This approach achieved independent phase modulation for orthogonal circular polarization states, and the 3D depth range of 100 μm was demonstrated. In 2021, researchers proposed a polarization-switchable and versatile angular momentum structuring metasurface, which achieves 3D imaging with a depth range of 12 mm by using a phase-only Fourier meta-hologram28. In 2025, researchers proposed a 3D Fourier hologram generation method based on the fractional Fourier transform (FRFT), extending the depth of Fourier meta-holography to centimeter level, which is the maximum depth to date29. This depth limitation in 3D meta-holography fails to meet the depth requirements of practical applications, thereby hindering the realization of large‑depth meta-holography and further development of its applications.

Currently, large-depth 3D meta-holography still faces numerous technical hurdles, including degraded phase modulation accuracy with increasing depth and the crosstalk between layers at different depths. Although Fresnel diffraction is widely used in 3D meta-holographic reconstruction, it is usually near-field diffraction30. As the propagation distance increases, under-sampling operations lead to spectral aliasing, resulting in blurred reconstructed images and thus hindering large‑depth meta-holographic reconstruction. While for the far-field Fourier-based meta-holograms, it typically relies on an optical lens for reconstruction, and the imaging distance is inherently limited by the focal length of the lens. Consequently, the reconstructed images are confined to the focal plane, which restricts the realization of large‑depth 3D meta-holography. These challenges make large-depth and multi-polarization-addressable 3D meta-holography one of the difficult directions in the field of meta-holography. Moreover, large-depth and polarization-addressable 3D meta-holography can increase the spatial information capacity, which holds profound potential in advanced applications such as encryption, optical storage, and super-resolution imaging31–36. To our knowledge, 3D meta-holography with meter-level depth, multi-polarization addressability, and a zoom function has never been achieved before.

Here, we demonstrate a multi-polarization addressable zoom meta-holography at meter-scale depth, as shown in Fig. 1. First, a polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle based on layer propagation is developed. By introducing the depth information and polarization information of 3D objects into the Fourier frequency domain, it not only breaks through the limitation of traditional Fourier meta-holography’s depth by focal length, but also realizes control of multiple polarization states. Second, it solves the problem that existing meta-holography makes it difficult to achieve both large depth and multi-polarization. By introducing a phase difference to encode polarization information, we realize independent control of arbitrary polarization states, thereby further expanding the information capacity of 3D meta-holography. A metasurface based on orthogonal basis vector control is prepared. Meta-holograms at different depths can be accessed with different incident polarization states, which greatly increases the amount of spatial information. Third, a polarized liquid lens is proposed to integrate it with the metasurface. Just by adjusting the driving voltage, the polarization state, size, and position of the meta-holographic images can be changed. The holographic reconstruction with a depth of 1 m is realized, which is the maximum depth achieved by the known 3D meta-holography. Finally, experiments verify the application of the proposed structure in multi-wavelength display and encryption. The proposed meta-holography is expected to find broad applications in high-security encryption, large-capacity storage, AR/VR, and automotive displays.

Results

Polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle

The traditional Fourier meta-holographic theory is usually limited to 2D image diffraction, and polarization information is not introduced in the calculation process of the meta-hologram. Therefore, 3D reconstruction is difficult to achieve, and the polarization control function depends entirely on the metasurface structure design. To realize 3D meta-holography with controllable polarization and large depth, the polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle is proposed, as shown in Fig. 2a. This approach successfully overcomes the critical technical bottleneck of large‑depth and multi-polarization-addressable 3D meta-holography.

The right circular polarization (RCP) and left circular polarization (LCP) states are considered as two orthogonal basis vectors of linear polarization. Firstly, the 3D object is diffracted and propagated in layers according to its depth information, and the phase difference between RCP and LCP is denoted as a matrix ΔΦ, which is introduced to characterize the polarization state of each layer. Secondly, the fractional domain propagation mode is introduced into Fourier diffraction. Based on FRFT and inverse fractional Fourier transform (IFRFT), the phase of the 3D object is iteratively constrained in the fractional domain and the spatial domain, and the phase difference matrix ΔΦ is added/subtracted in the iteration corresponding to the RCP/LCP state, respectively, so as to realize independent regulation of linear polarization. Finally, the final phase distributions MRCP and MLCP are extracted, and the 3D meta-hologram with large depth and adjustable polarization states is generated by the row-alternating method.

This phase difference is in the form of a phase constraint, which can mitigate the uncertainty introduced by random phase and effectively reduce relative blurring between depth planes, thereby mitigating inter-layer crosstalk (Supplementary materials S2).

Design of the multi-polarization metasurface

The metasurface is designed based on the Pancharatnam-Berry (PB) geometric phase principle38, where each unit cell corresponds to an element in the phase modulation matrix M, and consists of a rectangular gold nanorod positioned on a quartz substrate. The geometric parameters of each nanorod—length (L), width (W), and height (H)—as well as the period (p) of the unit cell are carefully engineered to support birefringence. The rotation angle (θ) of the nanorod relative to the x-axis determines the phase response of the unit, enabling a broadband and dispersionless phase shift of φ = ±2θ, in accordance with the PB phase mechanism. Here, the positive sign applies to incident LCP light, while the negative sign applies to RCP light.

During the meta-hologram generation method, the linear polarization state is characterized by the preset phase difference between LCP and RCP states. By using the proposed polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle, sub-holograms corresponding to LCP and RCP light are obtained, respectively. Then, to simultaneously and independently modulate both polarization components, the metasurface adopts a row-alternating phase modulation strategy. Specifically, odd-numbered rows are designed to encode the PB phase for the RCP sub‑hologram, while even-numbered rows are tailored for the LCP sub‑hologram. This interleaved design allows the metasurface to address both polarization states within a single device. By treating each pair of vertically adjacent unit cells—one from an odd (RCP) row and one from an even (LCP) row—as a macro-pixel, the metasurface achieves precise control over the relative phase difference between the two polarization components at each spatial location. This row-pairing strategy allows the metasurface to achieve polarization multiplexing without increasing the spatial sampling density per channel.

Structure and principles of the polarized liquid lens

A polarized liquid lens prioritizing both high optical power and polarization control is designed and fabricated. By integrating a liquid crystal light valve, the proposed lens enables simultaneous adjustment of focal length and polarization state, thereby matching the requirements of the multi‑polarization-addressable meta-holography. The polarized liquid lens can achieve an adjustable focus function based on the electrowetting-on-dielectric (EWOD) effect. The designed polarized liquid lens consists of a plastic package, a hydrophobic layer, an insulating gasket, a dielectric layer, two liquids (an insulating liquid and a conductive liquid), three electrodes (an upper electrode, a lower electrode, and a flexible electrode), a window glass, and a liquid crystal light valve, as shown in Fig. 2b.

To make the liquid lens achieve both the zoom ability and the polarization control, a biphasic liquid formulation for an electrowetting liquid lens is proposed, ensuring that the polarized liquid lens exhibits a wide optical power range under low driving voltages. On one aspect, 1,2-propanediol (PG) is employed as the base solvent for the conductive liquid, and the addition of an appropriate concentration of tetrabutylammonium chloride (TC) enhances its conductivity, enabling the conductive droplet to be driven at lower voltages; On the other hand, a mixture of ISOPAR™ V (IV) and 1-bromo-4-ethylbenzene (EB) as the insulating liquid is formulated to achieve density matching while relatively increasing the refractive index of the surrounding medium. This configuration allows for a greater change in optical power under the same liquid-liquid interface curvature variation. Furthermore, the duplex liquid ensures a large initial contact angle, which allows the polarized liquid lens to have a large optical power range at low drive voltage.

In addition, the liquid crystal light valve modulates its molecular alignment in response to the applied voltage, thereby enabling polarization control of the transmitted light. By adjusting the driving voltage, the light emerging from the liquid crystal light valve can be switched to different linear polarization states. By changing the voltage, the focal length and polarization of the liquid lens can be changed at the same time, so as to control the size, position, and polarization state of the meta-holographic image (Supplementary materials S3).

Fabrication and optical characteristics of the metasurface

To verify the feasibility of the proposed polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier diffraction principle based on metasurface, a meta-hologram is designed and fabricated. A 3D object (with resolutions of 2000 × 4000) composed of three letters ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ is selected as the recorded object. Based on the FRFT-based 3D Fourier diffraction principle, different fractional orders m are assigned to the three letters to characterize their respective depth positions. According to the polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle, two independent meta-holograms are generated for the RCP and LCP polarization channels, each with resolutions of 2,000 × 4,000. The horizontal and vertical pixel pitches are set to 0.3 µm and 0.6 µm, respectively. A final polarization-multiplexed 3D meta-hologram is constructed by interleaving the two channels row by row. Then, the plasmonic metasurface consisting of an array of sub-wavelength gold nanorods is fabricated. The parameters of each nanorod are H = 50 nm, W = 100 nm, L = 160 nm, and p = 300 nm. The sample is fabricated on a quartz substrate using standard electron-beam lithography, followed by the deposition of a 50 nm gold layer and a lift-off process. The scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images of the fabricated sample are shown in Fig. 3a.

Parameters and tests of the polarized liquid lens

As shown in Fig. 2b, the polarized liquid lens contains two-phase liquids in its chamber. The inner surface of the aluminum electrode is coated with a dielectric layer (Parylene C, ~3.15 dielectric constant, ~4 μm thickness) and a hydrophobic layer (TeflonTM AF 2400, ~150 nm thickness). The upper and lower electrodes are separated by a plastic gasket insulation. 58 μL of conductive liquid is injected into the aluminum chamber, and the remaining space is filled with the insulating liquid, followed by a delicate seal with two-component epoxy resin. The liquid crystal light valve is attached to the glass surface under the liquid lens by means of a custom-made housing. The liquid lens and the liquid crystal light valve have the same aperture size of 6 mm. To miniaturize the system structure, the thickness of the liquid lens is designed to be 4 mm, and the thickness of the liquid crystal light valve is 1.4 mm. Flexible electrodes are used to connect to the electrodes of both components, which are integrally assembled into the plastic housing.

To evaluate the operational performance of the designed polarized liquid lens, transmittance and response time measurement experiments are carried out. As shown in Fig. 3b, with an applied voltage ranging from 0 to 100 V, the optical power of the polarized liquid lens can change from −7.08 to 20.41 m-1. The rise time of the polarized liquid lens is ~80 ms, and the decay time is ~78 ms, as shown in Fig. 3c. In the visible light band, the overall transmittance of the proposed polarized liquid lens is greater than 90%. The resolution, magnification, and polarization state of the polarized liquid lens are tested, respectively (Supplementary Materials S4).

Multi-polarization addressable zoom meta-holographic reconstruction

The reconstruction system for multi-polarization addressable zoom Fourier meta-holography consists of a laser with a wavelength of 671 nm, a quarter-wave plate, a polarizer, the fabricated metasurface, the designed polarized liquid lens, and a receiving screen, as shown in Fig. 3d. The incident light, generated by the laser, passes through the quarter-wave plate and polarizer to adjust its linear polarization state. The designed polarized liquid lens can simultaneously adjust its polarization state and focal length by applying the driving voltages. The receiving screen is moved forward and backward to capture the focused images.

In the generation process of the meta-hologram, the depths of three letters ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ are preset to 5.0 cm, 3.5 cm, and 2.0 cm, corresponding to transformation orders mv = 1.0, mc = 0.8, and mr = 0.6, respectively. Using RCP and LCP states as the orthogonal basis vectors, the linear polarization angles of three letters are preset to 90°, 135°, and 0°, respectively. We initially positioned the receiving screen at a depth of 10.0 cm. By adjusting the driving voltage of the polarized liquid lens, we controlled both its focal length and polarization state to sequentially bring the letters ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ into focus on the receiving screen. As shown in Fig. 3e, the brightness of ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ reaches its maximum under the polarized light of 90°, 135°, and 0°, respectively. However, under the irradiation of polarized light of 0°, 45°, and 90°, ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ disappear in turn. It can be seen that, when ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ are focused separately, the letters of other depth planes become blurred (Supplementary materials S5). Meta-holograms at different depths can be accessed with different incident polarization states. Besides, the size and position of the 3D object can be adjusted. Experimental verification demonstrates the dual-control capability, as shown in Fig. 3f. With the receiving screen moving from 0.2 m to 1.0 m, the letter ‘v’ can be projected on the screen by changing the driving voltages. Actually, the proposed method enables meter‑scale depth meta-holography in both simulations and experiments, opening up possibilities for cutting‑edge applications such as high‑capacity holographic storage (Supplementary materials S6).

Substantially, by changing the applied driving voltages, 3D zoom polarization-addressable meta-holography can be realized, as shown in Fig. 4. Three letters ‘v’, ‘c’, and ‘r’ are clearly reconstructed at their respective depths according to the focal length of the polarized liquid lens fpll, thereby demonstrating depth-selective 3D reconstruction (Supplementary materials S7).

Furthermore, our proposed method integrates the polarization state into the process of meta-hologram generation and the metasurface design. By using the phase difference between LCP and RCP to control the linear polarization state, independent control of any linear polarization state can be realized, which is not limited to 0°, 90°, and 135°, but can also include other parameters, such as 20°, 80°, 140°, and others. Thus, the proposed method can significantly increase the number of polarization states, offering insights for high‑capacity holographic storage.

In addition, in the experimental results, as depth varies, when one target plane is in focus, the blurring of other planes remains relatively minor across varying depths. This phenomenon may be intrinsically related to the introduction of a phase matrix designed to encode polarization information. In the future, to increase the variation of images in the large-depth regime, we can make the image of the non-focus plane more blurred or even difficult to distinguish through algorithm optimization, such as introducing additional phase factors or designing dedicated loss functions to enhance depth sensitivity and achieve improved blurring effects.

Discussion

The proposed multi-polarization addressable zoom meta-holography has broad application prospects in advanced optical technologies such as data storage and zoom display, especially for high-security encryption. We have demonstrated a high-security encryption mechanism as a future‑oriented prospect, which relies on the joint design of triple keys, and correct decryption can only be achieved when the metasurface, the polarization state of the incident light, and the reconstruction distance are simultaneously satisfied. As shown in Fig. 5a, for a recorded 3D object, we set different polarization angles for patterns in each layer and generate the meta-hologram based on the polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle. The fabricated multi-polarization addressable metasurface serves as the primary encryption key I. The polarization states of each part serve as the encryption key II. Then, the pre-determined depth of the receiving screen is denoted as dr, which is recorded as the encryption key III, and the driving voltages required for focused imaging of each object layer at dr are pre-calculated. This sophisticated encryption architecture achieves high security through the combination of three distinct encryption keys, and successful decryption necessitates the simultaneous fulfillment of all three keys.

Concept of the high-security encryption with the proposed method.

a Keys of the high-security encryption. b Encryption and c decryption processes of the high-security encryption.

Four distinct messages ‘Go ahead’, ‘Turn around’, ‘Turn left’, and ‘Turn right’ for different users are encoded into corresponding patterns ‘square’, ‘circle’, ‘triangle’, and ‘parallelogram’, as shown in Fig. 5b. These patterns constitute a 3D object with specific depth and polarization states. The ‘square’ and ‘circle’ reside in one depth plane with a fractional transformation order of m = 1.0, while the ‘triangle’ and ‘parallelogram’ occupy another depth plane with a fractional transformation order of m = 1.6. Meanwhile, the polarization states are configured with ‘square’ and ‘parallelogram’ being set at 0°, while ‘circle’ and ‘triangle’ are set at 90°. Based on the proposed polarization-multiplexed 3D Fourier meta-holographic diffraction principle, a metasurface is designed and fabricated. During the decryption process, according to the reconstruction depth given by key III, we calculate in advance the driving voltages required for the polarized liquid lens to focus each pattern onto the receiving screen. As demonstrated in Fig. 5c, only when key I (metasurface), key II (polarization state), and key III (reconstruction depth) are matched precisely, four patterns ‘square’, ‘circle’, ‘triangle’, and ‘parallelogram’ can be brightly focused on the receiving screen, thus successfully decrypting as ‘Go ahead’, ‘Turn around’, ‘Turn left’, and ‘Turn right’ corresponding to four users respectively.

It can be seen that the proposed high-security encryption mechanism relies on the joint design of triple keys, and correct decryption can only be achieved when the metasurface, the polarization state of the incident light, and the reconstruction distance are simultaneously satisfied. However, if we only rely on one of the keys, such as key III, there may be a risk of information leakage. In the future, to improve the security of key III, we can make the image of the non-focus plane more blurred or even difficult to distinguish through algorithm optimization39. Also, we will continue to study the application of meta-holography in encryption and other fields, and further prevent information leakage by optimizing the algorithm or introducing other strategies such as interference information.

Moreover, both the metasurface and polarized liquid lens exhibit full operational capability within the visible light band, and the polarization selectivity of the generated polarization-multiplexed meta-hologram is wavelength-independent. Therefore, the proposed method can realize a multi-polarization addressable zoom meta-holographic 3D display at different wavelengths (Supplementary materials S8). In the future, the wavelength is expected to become the fourth encryption key to extend our encryption framework, further enhancing the security of encryption. Of course, in 3D meta-holography, achromatic meta-holographic display is an important research direction and hotspot. Accordingly, pursuing research in this area, for instance by pre-compensating chromatic aberration algorithmically or by optimizing the formulation of liquid materials, is an important research direction in future40–42.

In conclusion, we propose and demonstrate a meter-depth and multi-polarization addressable zoom meta-holography. Through the special coding and structural design of the metasurface, two major problems are solved: the traditional Fourier meta-holographic imaging position is limited by the focal plane of the lens, and it is difficult to manipulate multiple polarization information in large-depth meta-holography. Experiments verify an unprecedented meta-holographic reconstruction with a depth of 1 m, which is the largest depth reported to date. The multi-polarization addressable meta-holographic reconstruction is also verified. Such a large-depth, multiple polarization channels, and high degree-of-freedom meta-holography is anticipated to have broad applications, including high-security encryption, large-capacity storage, AR/VR, adaptive imaging, and so on.

Methods

Metasurface fabrication

The plasmonic metasurface consists of gold nanorods fabricated on a quartz substrate with an area of 1 × 1 cm². The substrate is first cleaned with N-Methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) for 20 min and then rinsed in isopropyl alcohol (IPA) for 5 min. It is dried with a nitrogen gun and baked on a hotplate at 180 °C for 10 min. An electron-beam resist (AR-P 6200.13) is spin-coated onto the substrate and baked at 180 °C for 5 min. Subsequently, a thin conductive protective coating (Electra 92, AR-PC 5092.02) is spin-coated onto the resist layer and baked at 90 °C for 2 min. Electron-beam lithography is then used to expose the sample for nanopatterning. The exposed sample is first rinsed in deionized water for 1 min, then developed in a MIBK:IPA (1:3) mixture for 2 min, and finally rinsed in IPA for 3 min. An electron-beam evaporator is used to deposit a 5-nm-thick chromium adhesion layer followed by a 50-nm-thick gold film. Finally, the gold nanorods are obtained after performing the lift-off process in NMP for 4 hours and then rinsed in IPA for 5 minutes.

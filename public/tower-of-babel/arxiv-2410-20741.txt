Residualities and uniform ergodicities of Markov semigroups
Nazife Erkurşun-Özcan 1,∗ and Farrukh Mukhamedov 2,∗

Abstract
. The first goal of the present paper is to study residualities of the set of uniform P P -ergodic Markov semigroups defined on abstract state spaces by means of a generalized Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient. In the last part of the paper, we explore uniform mean ergodicities of Markov semigroups.

Primary 47A35; Secondary 60J10, 28D05.

1.
Introduction

It is known that Markov operators and semigroups appear in many branches of applies sciences. For instance, Boltzmann equation and its simplified
version Tjon-Wu equation generate a nonlinear Markov semigroups. In possible treatments, it is convenient to investigate Markov operators on densities, sometimes it is more convenient to consider Markov operators on measures
[
27
]
. Notice that semigroups of Markov operators can be generated by partial differential equations (transport equations)
[
37
]
.

Several papers have delved into the exploration of general characteristics associated with Markov operators, as evidenced by works such as
[
3
,
4
,
23
,
36
]
. Conventionally, the dominant generic characteristic ascribed to Markov operators/semigroups is the notion of asymptotic stability, wherein all elements exhibiting this trait are considered residual
[
26
]
. A recent development in this context is presented in
[
25
]
, which establishes a similar outcome for Markov semigroups operating on the set of density operators within a Hilbert space. In this context, ”residuality” refers to a subset of a complete metric space, with its complement capable of being expressed as a countable union of nowhere dense sets.

Recently, an extended Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient
\delta_{P}(T)
has been introduced for Markov operators, acting on an abstract state space, in relation to a projection
P

[
33
]
. Additionally, the investigation of a generalized Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient
\delta_{P}(T)
for Markov
C_{0}
-semigroups on abstract state spaces with respect to a projection
P
has been undertaken in
[
19
]
. In the realm of open quantum systems,
[
38
]
explores invariant subspaces in the presence of symmetries, which are associated with projections
P
of the corresponding semigroups (refer also to
[
8
]
). When the projection
P
is a rank-one projection, the study of the uniform convergence of irreducible Markov semigroups has been explored in
[
2
,
20
,
22
]
. Furthermore, the Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient
\delta(T)
has been employed to examine the asymptotic stability of Markov
C_{0}
-semigroups on abstract state spaces in
[
15
,
16
]
. Particularly noteworthy are the perturbation results for
C_{0}
-semigroups of Markov operators obtained in
[
17
,
19
]
. It is important to note that within this abstract framework, both classical and quantum cases can be considered as specific instances
[
1
,
12
]
.

It is noted that the averages of operators appear in several fields of
mathematics, theoretical physics, and other fields of science
[
2
,
6
,
21
,
37
]
. In
[
13
,
14
]

In the present paper, we study residualities of the set of uniform
P
-ergodic Markov semigroups defined on abstract state spaces by means of a generalized Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient. Furthermore, we are going to explore the uniformly mean ergodicity of Markov semigroups in terms of the generalized Dobrushin’s ergodicity coefficient without irreducibility condition.

To best of our knowledge the obtained results are new even in the classical setting. Namely, if
X
is taken as either
L^{1}(E,\mu)
or a predual of a von Neumann algebra, then all the established results shed new light into the classical and non-commutative settings, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, essential definitions and findings pertaining to abstract state spaces are revisited.

Let
X
denote an ordered vector space equipped with a generating positive cone
X_{+}
(i.e.,
X=X_{+}-X_{+}
). Suppose that
X_{+}
has a base denoted as
\mathcal{K}
, where

\mathcal{K}=\{x\in X_{+}:f(x)=1\},

for a strictly positive linear functional
f
on
X
. Define
U:=\mathcal{K}\cup(-\mathcal{K})
, and introduce a seminorm as follows:

\|x\|_{\mathcal{K}}=\inf\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:\ x\in\lambda U\}.

In this context, one finds that
\mathcal{K}=\{x\in X_{+}:\|x\|_{\mathcal{K}}=1\}
, and
f(x)=\|x\|_{\mathcal{K}}
for
x\in X_{+}
. Assuming the seminorm becomes a norm and
X
is a complete space with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{K}}
, and
X_{+}
is closed, the tuple
(X,X_{+},\mathcal{K},f)
is termed an
abstract state space
. In this scenario,
\mathcal{K}
is identified as a closed face of the unit ball of
X
, and
U
encompasses the open unit ball of
X
. It’s noteworthy that if
U
is
radially compact
(as defined in
[
1
]
), meaning that the intersection of
\ell
and
U
forms a closed and bounded segment for every line
\ell
passing through the origin of
X
, then
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{K}}
becomes a norm. The radial compactness condition is equivalent to
U
coinciding with the closed unit ball of
X
. In the latter case,
X
is called as a
strong abstract state space
. Throughout, we employ
\|\cdot\|
in place of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{K}}
. For a deeper comprehension of the distinction between a strong abstract state space and a broader class of base norm spaces, the reader is directed to
[
24
,
41
]
.

It is worth noting that fundamental examples of abstract state spaces encompass classical
L_{1}
-spaces and the space of density operators operating on certain Hilbert spaces (see, for details
[
1
,
42
]
).

In the sequel, for the sake of simplicity, always
X
stands for an abstract state space.

In the current paper, we are going to consider general abstract state spaces for
which the convex hull of the base
\mathcal{K}
and
-\mathcal{K}
is not supposed to be radially compact.

A linear operator
T:X\to X
is
called
positive
, if
Tx\geq 0
whenever
x\geq 0
. If a positive linear operator
T:X\to X
satisfies
T(\mathcal{K})\subset\mathcal{K}
, it is referred to as a ”Markov” operator. It is evident that
\|T\|=1
, and its adjoint mapping
T^{*}:X^{*}\to X^{*}
operates in the ordered Banach space
X^{*}
with the unit being
f
. Furthermore, it holds that
T^{*}f=f
.

The following concept represents a generalized form of Dobrushin’s ergodicity coefficient (see
[
33
]
), defined as follows: .

Definition 2.1
.

Given a linear bounded operator
T:X\to X
one defines

(2.1)

\delta_{P}(T)=\sup_{x\in N_{P},\ x\neq 0}\frac{\|Tx\|}{\|x\|}

where

(2.2)

N_{P}=\{x\in X:\ P(x)=0\}.

If
P=I
, we put
\delta_{P}(T)=1
. The quantity
\delta_{P}(T)
is called the
generalized Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient
of
T
with respect to
P
.

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that
P
is a one-dimensional projection, i.e.
Px=f(x)y_{0}
(
y_{0}\in\mathcal{K}
), then the Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient
\delta_{P}(T)
for
T
is equal to the classical Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient
\delta(T)
studied in
[
9
,
10
,
32
]
. It’s noteworthy that in the case where
X^{*}
evolves into a von Neumann algebra, a similar coefficient was introduced in
[
31
]
.

Let us use the notation
\Sigma(X)
for the set of all Markov operators on
X
and
\Sigma_{P}(X)
for the set of all Markov operators
T
on
X
with
PT=TP
.

The next result establishes several properties of the generalized Dobrushin
ergodicity coefficient.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
33
]

Let
X
be an abstract state space,
P
be a projection on
X
and let
T,S\in\Sigma(X)
. Then the following
statements are true:

(i)

0\leq\delta_{P}(T)\leq 1
;

(ii)

|\delta_{P}(T)-\delta_{P}(S)|\leq\delta_{P}(T-S)\leq\left\|T-S\right\|
;

(iii)

if
H:X\to X
is a bounded linear
operator with
HP=PH
, then

\delta_{P}(TH)\leq\delta_{P}(T)\left\|H\right\|;

(iv)

if
H:X\to X
is a bounded linear
operator with
PH=0
, then

\left\|TH\right\|\leq\delta_{P}(T)\left\|H\right\|;

(v)

if
S\in\Sigma_{P}(X)
, then

\delta_{P}(TS)\leq\delta_{P}(T)\delta_{P}(S).

Remark 2.4
.

If
X
is a strong abstract state space, then due to
[
33
, Proposition 3.9]
one has

(2.3)

\delta_{P}(T)=\frac{1}{2}\sup\{\left\|Tu-Tv\right\|:u,v\in\mathcal{K}u-v\in N_{P}\}.

The main object in this paper is a
C_{0}
-semigroup
on a Banach space
X
, i.e. a map
{\displaystyle T_{t}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to L(X)}
such that

(i)

{\displaystyle T_{0}=I}
, identity operator on
{\displaystyle X}
;

(ii)

for all

one has
T_{t+s}=T_{t}T_{s}
;

(iii)

for every

\|T_{t}x_{0}-x_{0}\|\to 0
, as
{\displaystyle t\downarrow 0}
.

A

-semigroup

is called

-Markov semigroup
if for every

, the operator
T_{t}
is a Markov operator. Denote

\Xi(X)=\{\mathcal{T}=(T(t))_{t\geq 0}:\mathcal{T}\ \text{is a}\ C_{0}-\text{Markov semigroup}\}.

An
element
x_{0}\in X
is called a

or
stationary point
of
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
if
T_{t}x_{0}=x_{0}
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. The basic structures of positive semigroups defined on
ordered Banach spaces are presented in
[
7
]
.

Let
P
be a projection of
X
and
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a Markov semigroup.
We write
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
if for each
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
one has
T_{t}\in\Sigma_{P}(X)
. This is equivalent to
T_{t}P=PT_{t}
for all

.

3.
Residuality of Uniform-
P
-ergodic Markov semigroups

In
[
19
]
, uniformly
P
-ergodicity of
C_{0}
-Markov semigroups is studied.

Definition 3.1
.

A
C_{0}
-Markov semigroup
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
defined on
X
is called

(i)

uniformly
P
-ergodic
if there exists
P
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|T_{t}-P\|=0;

(ii)

if
P
is a one-dimensional projection, then uniformly
P
-ergodic semigroup is called
uniformly ergodic
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
19
]

Let
X
be an abstract state space. Assume that
P
is a Markov projection and
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}

is a
C_{0}
-Markov semigroup on
X
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{T}
is uniformly
P
-ergodic;

(ii)

There is a strongly-continuous semigroup
(Q_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
Q_{0}=I-P
on
X
such that
T_{t}=P+Q_{t}
and
PQ_{t}=Q_{t}P=0
(for all
t\geq 0
). There is
t_{0}>0
such that
\|Q_{t_{0}}\|<1
. Moreover,

(3.1)

\delta_{P}(T_{t})\leq\|Q_{t}\|\leq 2\delta_{P}(T_{t}),\ \ \textrm{for all}\ \ t\geq 0.

(iii)

There exists
t_{0}
such that
\delta_{P}(T_{t_{0}})<1
and
PT_{t}=T_{t}P=P
for all
t\geq 0
. Moreover,
one finds positive constants
C,\alpha,t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
with

(3.2)

\left\|T_{t}-P\right\|\leq Ce^{-\alpha t},\,\,\,\,\,\forall t\geq t_{0}.

Recall that for a bounded linear operator
T
on
X
one denoted by
\sigma(T)
its spectrum. Then the spectral radius of
T
is defined to be
r(T)=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(T)}|\lambda|
. Due to
[
18
, p. 687]
, we have the next fact.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
P
be a Markov projection on
X
. Assume that
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})
is uniformly
P
-ergodic
C_{0}
-Markov semigroup on
X
.
Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\delta_{P}(T_{n}))^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(\delta_{P}((T_{1})^{n}))^{1/n}=r(T_{1}-P).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
P
be a Markov projection on
X
. Assume that
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})
is a uniformly
P
-ergodic
C_{0}
-Markov semigroup on
X
.
Then
\delta_{P}(T_{t})=e^{-\alpha t}
for all
t>0
if and only if
\delta_{P}(T_{t})=r(T_{t}-P)
for all
t>0
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{T}
is a
C_{0}
-Markov semigroup which is commutative, then by
[
35
, Theorem 1.6.17]
,
\sigma(T_{t})
is a continuous function of
t
. Now, let us assume that
\delta_{P}(T_{t})=e^{-\alpha t}
for all
t>0
. Then by Lemma
3.3

r(T_{1}-P)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\delta_{P}(T_{1}^{n})^{1/n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}(e^{-\alpha n})^{1/n}=e^{-\alpha}.

Due to
T_{n}-P=T_{1}^{n}-P=(T_{1}-P)^{n}
, it follows that

r(T_{n}-P)=r((T_{1}-P)^{n})
and

r(T_{\frac{n}{m}}-P)=r((T_{1}-P)^{\frac{n}{m}})
. The continuity of
\sigma(T_{t})
implies

r(T_{t}-P)=r((T_{1}-P)^{t})=e^{-\alpha t}=\delta_{P}(T_{t}),\ \ \ \forall t>0.

Now, suppose that

\delta_{P}(T_{t})=r(T_{t}-P)
. Then

r(T_{t}-P)=(r(T_{1}-P))^{t}=e^{\log(r(T_{1}-P))t}

where
\alpha=-\log(r(T_{1}-P))
. This completes the proof.
∎

Furthermore, we are going to study residuality results for Markov semigroups.

By
C([0,\infty),L(X))
we denote the set of all continuous maps
\mathbf{F}:[0,\infty)\to L(X)
, where
L(X)
stands for the Banach space of all bounded linear operators on

. This set is equipped with the metric

\rho(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{m}}\frac{\rho_{m}(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G})}{1+\rho_{m}(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G})},

where

(3.3)

\rho_{m}(\mathbf{F},\mathbf{G})=\sup_{t\in[0,m]}\|F_{t}-G_{t}\|.

It is well-known
[
39
]
that
(C([0,\infty),L(X)),\rho)
is a complete metric space.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be an abstract state space and
\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}\in\Xi(X)
. For each
r>0
, we define

(3.4)

\rho_{r}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S})=\sup_{t\in[0,r]}\|T_{t}-S_{t}\|.

Then
\rho_{r}
is a metric on
\Xi(X)
. Moreover, all
\rho_{r}
and
\rho
are equivalent on
\Xi(X)
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
25
]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

\Xi(X)
is a
\rho_{r}
-closed subset of
C([0,\infty),L(X))
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\xrightarrow{\rho_{r}}\mathcal{H}
, when
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\in\Xi(X)
. From (
3.4
) one gets

\|{T}_{n,t}-H_{t}\|\leq\rho_{r}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{P}),\ \ \forall t\in[0,r].

Hence, by Lemma
3.5
we infer that
T_{n,t}-H_{t}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, for all
t\geq 0
.

Let us establish that for each
t\geq 0
the operator
P_{t}
is Markov. Indeed, let
x\in\mathcal{K}
, then

\displaystyle|1-f(H_{t}x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|f(T_{n,t}x)-f(H_{t}x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(T_{n,t}-H_{t})x\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|T_{n,t}-H_{t}\|\|x\|\to 0.

It implies that
f(H_{t}x)=1
. The positivity of
T_{n,t}
implies
H_{t}
is also positive.

Now, we show that
H_{t}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Indeed, we have

(i)

\|H_{0}-I\|=\|H_{0}-T_{0}+T_{0}-I\|=\|H_{0}-T_{0}\|\to 0\ \ as\ n\to\infty

which implies
H_{0}=I

(ii)

\displaystyle\|H_{t+s}-H_{t}H_{s}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|H_{t+s}-T_{t+s}\|+\|T_{t+s}-T_{t}T_{s}\|+\|T_{t}T_{s}-H_{t}H_{s}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|H_{t+s}-T_{t+s}\|+\|T_{t}\|\|T_{s}-H_{s}\|+\|H_{s}\|\|T_{t}-H_{t}\|\to 0

Hence
H_{t+s}=H_{t}H_{s}
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\|H_{t}x-x\|\leq\lim_{t\to 0}(\|H_{t}x-T_{n,t}x\|+\|T_{n,t}x-x\|)\to 0,
because
{\mathcal{T}}_{n}\xrightarrow{\rho_{r}}\mathcal{H}
, and
\mathcal{H}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The semigroup
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly
P
-ergodic;

(ii)

There exists a Markov projection
P
with
T_{t}P=P
such that for some (all)
t_{0}>0
we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|T_{nt_{0}}-P\|=0
.

(iii)

There exists a Markov projection
P
with
T_{t}P=P
and
t_{n}\to\infty
such that
\lim\limits_{t_{n}\to\infty}\|T_{t_{n}}-P\|=0
.

Proof.

The implications
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
are obvious. Therefore, it is enough to establish
(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
.

Assume that (i) fails, then for some
\epsilon>0
and
s_{j}\to\infty
, we have

\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\|T_{s_{j}}x-Px\|\geq\epsilon.

Let us choose
n_{j}\to\infty
such that
s_{j}>t_{n_{j}}
and denote
r_{j}=s_{j}-t_{n_{j}}\geq 0
.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\|T_{s_{j}}x-Px\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\|T_{t_{n_{j}}}T_{r_{j}}x-PT_{r_{j}}x\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in T_{r_{j}}(\mathcal{K})}\|T_{t_{n_{j}}}y-Py\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\|T_{t_{n_{j}}}x-Px\|\to 0

which is a contradiction.
∎

Denote

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}^{u}(X):=\{\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X):\mathcal{T}\ \text{is uniformly $P$-ergodic}\},

\displaystyle\Xi_{P}^{inv}(X):=\{\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X):\ T_{t}P=P,\forall t\geq 0\}.

It is obvious that
\Xi_{P}^{u}(X)\subset\Xi_{P}^{inv}(X)

Lemma 3.8

Let
X
be an abstract state space and

be a Markov projection on

\Xi_{P}^{u}(X)

\rho_{1}

Proof.

Take any

Now, let us establish that

. Indeed, take any
\mathcal{R}\in U(\mathcal{T},\varepsilon)
, then

\displaystyle\delta_{P}(R_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta_{P}(T_{t_{0}})+\delta_{P}(R_{t_{0}}-T_{t_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon+N\|R_{t_{0}/N}-T_{t_{0}/N}\|

\displaystyle<

Lemma 3.9

Let

\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,Q}\in\Xi(X)

Proof.

Now, it is enough to prove that

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(T^{Q}_{1}(t)x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}T_{t-s}QT_{s}xds\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\bigg(\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{t-s_{j}}QT_{s_{j}}x\Delta s\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}f(T_{t-s_{j}}QT_{s_{j}}x)\Delta s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Delta s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}ds=t

Moreover, let us assume that by induction for each
t\geq 0
and each
x\in\mathcal{K}
, we have
\displaystyle f(T^{Q}_{k,t}x)=\frac{t^{k}}{k!}
.

Then, for
k+1
, one gets

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(T^{Q}_{k+1,t}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}T_{t-s}QT_{s}xds\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f\bigg(\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}T_{t-s_{j}}QT^{Q}_{k,s_{j}}x\Delta s\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}f(T_{t-s_{j}}QT^{Q}_{k,s_{j}}x)\Delta s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{s^{k}_{j}}{k!}\Delta s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\frac{s^{k}}{k!}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{t^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}

Finally for
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,Q}
, we find

\displaystyle\displaystyle f(T^{\lambda,Q-I}_{t}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(e^{-\lambda t}[T_{t}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}T^{Q}_{k,t}]x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}f([T_{t}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}T^{Q}_{k,t}]x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}[f(T_{t}x)+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{k}f(T^{Q}_{k,t}x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda t}\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\lambda t)^{k}}{k!}\bigg)=1.

If
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
, then from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we infer that
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda,P}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
. This completes the proof.
∎

.

4.
Uniform mean
P
-ergodicity of
C_{0}
-Markov semigroups

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})
be a
C_{0}
-semigroup. Then the Cesaro averages is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{s}ds

where the integral above is taken with respect to the strong operator topology. The Cesaro averages is one of the well-known example for LR-nets. Uniform ergodicity of LR-nets of Markov operators on abstract state spaces in in terms of the Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient is studied in
[
15
]
. This section is devoted to study uniform mean ergodicity of
C_{0}
-semigroups by means of a generalized Dobrushin ergodicity coefficient.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
P
be a projection. A
C_{0}
-Markov semigroup
\mathcal{T}=(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is defined on
X
is called

(i)

mean-
P
-ergodic
if
for every
x\in X

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})x-Px\|=0;

(ii)

uniform mean-
P
-ergodic
if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})-P\|=0;

(iii)

weakly-
P
-mean ergodic
if

\lim_{t\to\infty}\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))=0.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi(X)
and
\mathcal{T}
be mean
Q
-ergodic. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{T}
is uniformly
Q
-mean ergodic;

(ii)

There exists
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such
that
\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(T))<1
.

Moreover

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})-Q\|\leq\frac{2t_{0}}{1-\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(T))}\frac{1}{t}.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Since
\mathcal{T}
is uniformly
Q
-mean ergodic, then
{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})
is uniformly
P
-ergodic. Then, using the
argument of Theorem
3.2
there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(T))<1
.

Let us prove (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i). Assume that there exists
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that
\rho=\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(T))<1
. Then for an arbitrary
s>0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-T_{s})\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{u}du-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{u}T_{s}du\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{u}du-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{u+s}du\right\|\ \ \ \ \text{use change of variable as }\ u+s=z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}T_{u}du-\frac{1}{t}\int_{s}^{t+s}T_{z}dz\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{s}\left\|T_{u}\right\|du+\frac{1}{t}\int_{t}^{t+s}\left\|T_{u}\right\|du\leq\frac{2s}{t}.

Hence, for every
s\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
one gets

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{s}(\mathcal{T}))\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|A_{t}(\mathcal{T})\bigg(\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}(I-T_{u})du\bigg)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}A_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-T_{u})du\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\|A_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-T_{u})\|du

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\int_{0}^{s}\frac{2u}{t}du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{t}

By Theorem
2.3
, we have

(4.2)

\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}})(\mathcal{T}))\leq\frac{t_{0}}{t}

Due to the mean
Q
-ergodicity of
\mathcal{T}
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{t}Q=Q\mathcal{A}_{t}
, hence again using Theorem
2.3
(vi) one finds

(4.3)

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))-\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}){\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))-\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\rho)\delta_{Q}{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}),

which implies
\delta_{Q}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))\leq\frac{t_{0}}{t(1-\rho)}
.
The weakly
Q
-mean ergodicity of
\mathcal{T}
implies its uniform
Q
-mean ergodicity.

Now we need to show (
4.1
). Noting that

\displaystyle\delta_{Q}{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})=\sup_{x\in N_{Q}}\frac{\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})x\|}{\|x\|}.

and
x\in N_{Q}
, there exists
y\in X
such that
x=y-Qy
. Hence

\delta_{Q}{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})\geq\sup_{y\in X}\frac{\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})y-{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})Qy\|}{\|y-Qy\|}=\sup_{y\in X}\frac{\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})y-Qy\|}{\|y-Qy\|}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})-Q\|.

This implies
\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})-Q\|\leq 2\delta_{Q}{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})\leq\frac{2t_{0}}{t(1-\rho)}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

We point out that in
[
28
]
the uniform mean
P
-ergodicity and its relations with the generator of
(T_{t})
and its resolvent were investigated. Recently, in
[

]
the uniform mean

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
and let
P
be a Markov projection.
Then the followings are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
t_{0}
, and there exists
n_{0}
such that
\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))<1
.

(ii)

\mathcal{T}
is weakly-
P
-mean ergodic.

Proof.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Since
\mathcal{T}
is weakly-
P
-mean ergodic,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))=0
. Then (i) is a direct conclusion.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) Assume that there exist
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\rho:=\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))<1
. Due to

,
{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})P=P{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})
, one finds

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))(I+{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})+{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{2}(\mathcal{T})+\cdots+{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}-1}(\mathcal{T})\right\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))\right\|(\left\|I\right\|+\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})\right\|+\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{2}(\mathcal{T})\right\|

\displaystyle+\cdots+\left\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}-1}(\mathcal{T})\right\|)\to 0.

Therefore

converges to zero as

and also

|\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))-\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}){\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))|\to 0\ \ \text{as}\ \ t\to\infty

.
By

\displaystyle\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T})(I-{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))-\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}){\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}^{n_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))(1-\rho),

it follows that
\displaystyle\delta_{P}({\mathcal{A}}_{t}(\mathcal{T}))
converges to zero.
∎

Historically, one of the most significant conditions for ergodicity is Doeblin’s
Condition
[
29
,
34
]
: Let
(E,\mathcal{F})
be a measurable space and
P(x,\cdot)
be a Markov chain on
E
. There exist a probability measure
\mu
, an integer
m_{0}\geq 1
and constants

\epsilon<1
and
\kappa>0
such that for every
A\in\mathcal{F}
if
\mu(A)\geq\epsilon
then

\inf_{x\in E}P^{m_{0}}(x,A)\geq\kappa.

Now, we are going to introduce a mean analogue of Doeblin’s Condition.

Recall that for a Markov projection
Q:X\to X
we write
Q\leq P
if
Q=QP=PQ
.

Let
(X,X_{+},\mathcal{K},f)
be an abstract state space. Assume that
P
is a Markov projection on
X
and let
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
.
We say that
\mathcal{T}
satisfies
condition
\mathfrak{D}_{P}^{(m)}
: if there exist constants

\tau\in(0,1]
,
t_{0}>0
and a Markov projection
Q
with
Q\leq P
such that for every
x\in\mathcal{K}
one finds
\varphi_{x}\in X_{+}
with

\sup\limits_{x}\|\varphi_{x}\|\leq\frac{\tau}{4}

such that

(4.4)

\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\varphi_{x}\geq\tau Qx.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume that
(X,X_{+},\mathcal{K},f)
be a strong abstract state space. Let
P
be a Markov projection on
X
and let
\mathcal{T}\in\Xi_{P}(X)
. If
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the condition
\mathcal{D}_{P}^{(m)}
, then

\mathcal{T}
is weakly
P
-mean ergodic.

Proof.

By condition
\mathfrak{D}_{P}^{(m)}
, there are
\tau\in(0,1]
,
t_{0}>0
and
Q\leq P
such that for any two elements

x,y\in\mathcal{K}
with
x-y\in N_{P}
, one finds

\varphi_{x},\varphi_{y}\in X_{+}
with

(4.5)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x}\|\varphi_{x}\|\leq\frac{\tau}{4}

such that

(4.6)

\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\varphi_{x}\geq\tau Qx,\ \ \mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})y+\varphi_{y}\geq\tau Qy.

By putting
\varphi_{xy}=\varphi_{x}+\varphi_{x}
, from (
4.6
) we obtain

(4.7)

\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\varphi_{xy}\geq\tau Qx,\ \ \mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})y+\varphi_{xy}\geq\tau Qy.

By the Markovianity of
\mathcal{T}
, and (
4.7
), (
4.5
), one gets

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\phi_{xy}-\tau Qx\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f({\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\phi_{xy}-\tau Qx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\tau+f(\phi_{xy})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\tau}{2}.

By the same argument, we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{A}}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})y+\phi_{xy}-\tau Qy\|\leq 1-\frac{\tau}{2}.

Due to
P(x-y)=0
and
Q\leq P
, one gets
Qx=Qy
. Therefore, from (
4.8
),(
4.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x-\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})y\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\varphi_{xy}-\tau Qx-(\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})y+\varphi_{xy}-\tau Qy)\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})x+\varphi_{xy}-\tau Qx\|+\|\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T})y+\varphi_{xy}-\tau Qy\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\bigg(1-\frac{\tau}{2}\bigg)

this by Remark
2.4
implies

\delta_{P}(\mathcal{A}_{t_{0}}(\mathcal{T}))\leq 1-\frac{\tau}{2}<1.

Hence, by Theorem
4.2
, we arrive at the assertion.
∎

Let us consider a simple example of discrete semigroup for which
\mathfrak{D}_{P}^{(m)}
is satisfied.

Example 4.6
.

Let
M=M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the algebra of
2\times 2
matrices. By

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}
we denote the Pauli matrices, i.e.

\sigma_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\ \sigma_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-i\\
i&0\\
\end{array}\right)\ \sigma_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right).

It is known that the set
\{{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I},\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\}
forms a
basis for
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Every matrix

x\in\textit{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
can be written in this basis as

x=w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\sigma
with
w_{0}\in\mathbb{C},{\mathbf{w}}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3},
here by
{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\sigma
we mean
the following
{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\sigma=w_{1}\sigma_{1}+w_{2}\sigma_{2}+w_{3}\sigma_{3}.
We note that
x
is positive iff
\|{\mathbf{w}}\|\leq|w_{0}|
.

Let us consider a mapping

\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,\kappa}:M_{2}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
given by

(4.10)

\Phi_{\lambda,\mu,\kappa}(w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\sigma)=w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+\lambda w_{1}\sigma_{1}+\mu w_{2}\sigma_{2}+\kappa w_{3}\sigma_{3}

which is clearly a Markov operator (see
[
31
]
) if and only if

\max\{|\lambda|,|\mu|,|\kappa|\}\leq 1
.

Let us consider the following Markov operators
\Phi:=\Phi_{-1,0,1}
,
P=\Phi_{0,0,1}
. It is evident that
P
is a projection with

P\Phi=\Phi P=P
. One can see that

\Phi^{n}(w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+{\mathbf{w}}\cdot\sigma)=w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+(-1)^{n}w_{1}\sigma_{1}+w_{3}\sigma_{3}

which yields that
\Phi
is not uniformly ergodic.
However,

A_{n}(\Phi)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Phi^{k}=w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+w_{3}\sigma_{3}-\frac{\chi_{{\mathbb{N}}^{odd}}(n)}{n}w_{1}\sigma_{1}

where
{\mathbb{N}}^{odd}
is the set of odd natural numbers. This implies that

A_{n}(\Phi)\to P
, so
\Phi
is uniformly mean
P
-ergodic.

Take any
0<\tau<1
. Then from

(4.11)

A_{n_{0}}(\Phi)(x)\geq\tau Px,\ \ x\geq 0

we infer that

(1-\tau)w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+(1-\tau)w_{3}\sigma_{3}-\frac{\chi_{{\mathbb{N}}^{odd}}(n_{0})}{n_{0}}w_{1}\sigma_{1}\geq 0

which means

w_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}+w_{3}\sigma_{3}-\frac{\chi_{{\mathbb{N}}^{odd}}(n_{0})}{(1-\tau)n_{0}}w_{1}\sigma_{1}\geq 0.

If
(1-\tau)n_{0}\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle|w_{3}|^{3}+\bigg|\frac{\chi_{{\mathbb{N}}^{odd}}(n_{0})}{(1-\tau)n_{0}}\bigg|^{2}|w_{1}|^{2}\leq|w_{3}|^{3}+|w_{1}|^{2}\leq|w_{0}|^{2}

here we have used the positivity of
x
.

Hence, if
n_{0}>1/(1-\tau)
, than (
4.11
) is satisfied. This yields that the fulfilment of
\mathfrak{D}_{P}^{(m)}
.

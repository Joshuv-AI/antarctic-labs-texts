Spatial control of Nd3+ sensitizer in 808 nm excited upconversion nanocrystals towards nearly 100% energy migration efficiency
Authors: Ni Zhang, Jin Xu, Shaohua Yu, Xiaoying Shang, Shan Lu, Datao Tu, Zhiyuan Xiao, Yao Lin, Xueyuan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
808 nm excited energy migration upconversion (EMU) nanocrystals (NCs) exhibit great superiority over their 980 nm excited counterparts in alleviating the overheating effect of excitation laser in biodetection and imaging applications based on EMU NCs. However, it remains challenging to fabricate 808 nm excited EMU NCs to acquire the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ or Eu3+ while simultaneously suppressing the ultraviolet (UV) upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ that cause the issue of autofluorescence interference. Herein, we report a novel sandwich structured core–triple shell NaLuF4:Nd,Yb@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb (or Eu) NC, wherein the efficiency of five-photon upconversion energy migration (i.e., Tm3+ → Gd3+ → Tb3+ (or Eu3+)) can approach 100% upon 808 nm excitation with power density above a threshold of ∼150 W cm−2, thus resulting in the nearly complete inhibition of UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ concomitant with the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ or Eu3+. Our findings may open new opportunities for further bioapplications of EMU NCs.

Introduction

980 nm excited energy migration upconversion (EMU) in lanthanide nanocrystals (NCs) was first reported by Liu et al.,1 who designed a core–shell NaGdF4:Yb3+,Tm3+@NaGdF4:X3+ (X3+ = Tb3+, Eu3+, Dy3+, Sm3+) NC with the sensitizer/accumulator (Yb3+/Tm3+) and the activator X3+ separately incorporated into the inner core and outer shell, respectively. In the EMU NC, the 980 nm excitation energy was absorbed by Yb3+ and then accumulated in the inner core through the Yb3+–Tm3+ five-photon upconversion process, followed by energy transfer from Tm3+ 1I6 to Gd3+ 6P7/2. Then, the energy migrated between Gd3+ ions and finally was captured by the activator X3+ in the outer shell to achieve the EMU emissions. Among the EMU activators, both Tb3+ and Eu3+ ions possess higher luminescence quantum efficiencies partially due to the large energy gap (>10 000 cm−1) between the first excited state and the highest ground state that imparts weak nonradiative vibrational relaxation.2–4 That is, the EMU emissions of Tb3+ and Eu3+ are resistant to the high-frequency phonon modes of molecules or ligands in complex biological media that promote deleterious vibrational relaxation. Therefore, the luminescence intensity or lifetime of Tb3+/Eu3+ EMU emission shows great potential to work as a stable and reliable detection signal to guarantee the high accuracy of biological labeling5–11 and imaging12–20 by employing the Tb3+/Eu3+ EMU NCs as nano-bioprobes.

For the bioapplications of 980 nm excited Tb3+/Eu3+ EMU NCs,21 one notable challenge is that the 980 nm excitation wavelength overlaps with the absorption bands of water molecules. Therefore, the 980 nm excitation source will be significantly attenuated while passing through biological samples. Moreover, overexposure of biological species under 980 nm excitation will cause overheating issues,22–25 resulting in significant cell death and tissue damage. Fortunately, the overheating effect induced by 980 nm excitation can be largely alleviated by using 808 nm excitation because of the low absorption coefficients displayed by water molecules and biological tissues at 808 nm.26 The 808 nm excited Tb3+/Eu3+ EMU emissions can be achieved in the core–multishell NCs by using the Nd3+ sensitizer27,28 to harvest the excitation energy. The harvested energy was transferred from Nd3+ to Yb3+ with a 2F5/2 excited state.29 Then, the energy was accumulated by the Yb3+–Tm3+ five-photon upconversion process, followed by energy transfer from Tm3+ (1I6) to Gd3+ (6P7/2), and finally to Tb3+ (5D4) to achieve the EMU emissions.

For instance, Hao et al.30 designed a core–quadruple shell NaGdF4:Yb,Tm@NaGdF4:Yb,Tb@NaGdF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Yb,Nd@NaGdF4 NC with Nd3+ doped in the 3rd middle shell next to the outermost shell, and Wang et al.31 constructed a core–double shell NaYbF4:Nd@Na(Yb,Gd)F4:Tm@NaGdF4:Tb NC with Nd3+ doped in the inner core to achieve the EMU emissions from Tb3+ and Eu3+ under 808 nm excitation. However, regardless of the Nd3+ doping strategy adopted, the efficiency of five-photon upconversion energy migration, i.e., the efficiency of the energy transfer of Tm3+ → Gd3+ → Tb3+ (or Eu3+), was reported to be far below 100%. Thus, it was observed that the Tm3+ and Gd3+ ultraviolet (UV) upconversion emissions of considerable magnitude always coexisted with the 5D4 → 7FJ emissions of Tb3+ or 5D0 → 7FJ emissions of Eu3+.32,33 As such, in vivo biodetection/imaging based on EMU NCs will be confronted with the issue of autofluorescence interference18,34 from endogenous chromophores and proteins triggered by the UV emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+. Recently, we demonstrated that in the sandwich structured NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs (wherein Yb3+, Tm3+, and Tb3+ were separately incorporated into the inner core, middle shell, and outer shell, respectively),35 the effective inter-shell energy transfer of Gd3+ → Tb3+ (ref. 36) accelerated the Yb3+–Tm3+ five-photon upconversion and the subsequent energy transfer of Tm3+ → Gd3+, which can eventually lead to the almost complete inhibition of UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ concurrent with the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ under 980 nm excitation. Therefore, the following question arises: is it possible to acquire the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ or Eu3+ while simultaneously inhibiting the UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ in 808 nm excited EMU NCs?

Herein, in this context, we proposed an optimized lanthanide doping strategy using the sandwich structured NCs for achieving 808 nm excited EMU emissions of Tb3+ or Eu3+. As a proof-of-concept experiment, we synthesized a core–triple shell NaLuF4:Nd,Yb@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb (or Eu) NC with the sensitizer (Nd3+), energy transfer bridge (Yb3+), accumulator (Tm3+), and activator (Tb3+ or Eu3+) separately incorporated into the inner core (with a small amount of Yb3+ co-doped with Nd3+), 1st middle shell, 2nd middle shell, and outer shell of the NC, respectively. Spectral analyses combined with a microscopic qualitative model revealed that the energy transfer process of Tm3+ → Gd3+ can be enhanced by the effective inter-shell energy transfer of Gd3+ → Tb3+ or Eu3+. More importantly, the radiative transition rates of both Tm 1I6 and Gd 6P7/2 were dramatically decreased due to the efficient energy transfer processes of Tm3+ → Gd3+ and Gd3+ → Tb3+/Eu3+, which ultimately resulted in a nearly 100% efficiency of the five-photon upconversion energy migration, as reflected in the nearly completely inhibited UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ concomitant with the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ under 808 nm excitation with a power density above a threshold of ∼150 W cm−2.

Results and discussion

To achieve 808 nm excited energy migration upconversion (EMU), three types of sandwich-structured core–multishell nanocrystals (NCs) with Nd3+ sensitizers confined in the inner core, middle shell, and outer shell, respectively, were synthesized through a seed-mediated method. For instance, the synthesis of core–triple shell NaLuF4:Nd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs involved the growth of NaLuF4:Nd (20%) core NCs via the thermo-decomposition of lanthanide trifluoroacetates,37–40 followed by the successive epitaxial growth of NaLuF4:Yb(50%), NaGdF4:Tm(2%), and NaLuF4:Tb(15%) shell layers on the core NCs serving as seeds. The synthetic procedures of core–quadruple shell NaLuF4@NaLuF4:Nd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb and NaLuF4@NaLuF4:Tb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Yb@NaLuF4:Nd NCs were analogous to that for the core–triple shell NCs.

The typical low-magnification transmission electron microscopy (TEM) images of core–triple shell NaLuF4:Nd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs showed that the core, core–shell, core–double shell, and core–triple shell NCs were almost spherical with average diameters of ∼18.17 ± 0.92, 23.03 ± 1.05, 28.09 ± 0.96 and 31.66 ± 1.11 nm, respectively (Fig. 1a and S1), as corroborated by the incrementally narrowed powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) peaks,41 which were well-indexed to the hexagonal phase39,42,43 of NaLuF4 (Fig. S2). The single crystalline property with a hexagonal structure was further validated using the high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) observation44–46 of an individual core–triple shell NC and the corresponding selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern (Fig. 1b and c).47–51 The high-angle annular dark-field scanning TEM (HAADF-STEM) image in Fig. 1d visually confirmed the inner core area and middle and outer shell layers of NCs. Meanwhile, as demonstrated by the energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) mapping conducted on several randomly selected NCs (Fig. 1e and S3), the NCs presented uniform element distributions throughout the detected regions, and the successive expansion of the distribution areas (as marked by the circles drawn with dotted lines) corresponding to the Nd, Yb, Gd and Tb elements, respectively, was consistent with the profiles of the core–triple shell NaLuF4:Nd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs. Consistently, the EDS line scanning analysis of a single NC (Fig. 1f) further verified the spatial distributions of Nd and Tb in the inner core and outer shell, respectively.47,52,53

Fig. 2a shows the characteristic EMU emission spectra (each spectrum was normalized to its maximum intensity value) of Tb3+ in the three types of sandwich-structured core–multishell NCs deposited on a quartz glass substrate with film thickness in the order of 0.1 mm, upon 808 nm excitation at a power density of ∼95 W cm−2 (Fig. S4). The strong emission bands of Tb3+ recorded at 489, 542, 584, and 620 nm were assigned to its optical transitions of 5D4 → 7F6, 7F5, 7F4, 7F3, respectively.54,55 Concomitantly, the ultraviolet (UV) upconversion emissions of Gd3+ (6P7/2 → 8S7/2) and Tm3+ (1I6 → 3H6, 1I6 → 3F4, and 1D2 → 3H6) were observed at 311, 289, 344, and 361 nm, respectively. In 808 nm excited EMU NCs, the near-infrared (NIR) excitation energy was initially harvested by Nd3+ and then transferred to Yb3+ with a 2F5/2 excited state at ∼10 000 cm−1. Subsequently, the excitation energy accumulated by the Yb3+–Tm3+ five-photon upconversion process was transferred from Tm3+ (1I6) to Gd3+ (6P7/2) and finally to Tb3+ (5D4) to achieve the EMU emissions (Fig. 2c).52,55 Thus, by considering only the dominant radiative transition (Tm3+: 1I6 → 3F4; Gd3+: 6P7/2 → 8S7/2) and interionic energy transfer (Tm3+ → Gd3+ → Tb3+) processes that depopulated the Tm3+ 1I6 and Gd3+ 6P7/2 excited states, the efficiency of five-photon upconversion energy migration, i.e., the efficiency of the energy transfer of Tm3+ → Gd3+ → Tb3+, can be reflected in the value of the intensity ratio between the strongest emission of Tb3+ at 542 nm and the sum of the maximum UV emissions of Tm3+ at 344 nm and Gd3+ at 311 nm.

The intensity ratios were calculated to be ∼8.6, 4.4, and 1.7 for the three types of core–multishell NCs with Nd3+ doped in the inner core, middle shell and outer shell, respectively. To quantitatively validate the advantage of the structure design with Nd3+ doped in the inner core, we have conducted quantum yield (QY) measurements on the three kinds of sandwich structured NCs. As shown in Table S1 and Fig. S5, the NaLuF4:Nd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs with Nd3+ doped in the inner core exhibited a significantly higher QY for Tb3+ EMU emission compared to the other two kinds of NCs, directly confirming its superiority in energy transfer efficiency. Moreover, to accurately compare the EMU emission intensities between the two kinds of NCs with Nd3+ doped in the inner core and middle shell, the upconversion spectra of the colloidal solutions of NCs (dispersed in cyclohexane at the same molar concentration) were monitored under identical excitation conditions with effective excitation power density in the order of several W cm−2 (Fig. S6). As compared in Fig. 2b, the EMU emission intensity of Tb3+ at 542 nm in the former was ∼2.4 times larger than that in the latter kind of NCs. Collectively, these results clearly evidenced that the efficiency of five-photon upconversion energy migration was strongly dependent on the Nd3+ doping strategy, which however could be well explained by scrutinizing the energy level population and depopulation details of the active lanthanide ions in the three types of sandwich-structured core–multishell NCs.

Specifically, upon 808 nm excitation, the Nd3+ 4F3/2 excited states had four depopulation channels, i.e., cross relaxation (with rate KCR) between Nd3+ ions, vibrational relaxation (with rate KVR) caused by the high-frequency phonon modes of defects (or impurities) such as organic fragments accommodated in the lattice and surface ligands such as oleic acid,46,56–60 Förster energy transfer (with rate KET (Nd–Yb))61,62 of Nd3+ (4F3/2) → Yb3+ (2F5/2)63,64 (see the Mechanism of energy transfer section in the SI), and radiative transition (with rate KR). For the NCs with Nd3+ doped in the outer shell, the large amounts of surface ligands in proximity to Nd3+ facilitated detrimental vibrational relaxation, which consequently resulted in the lowest efficiency of energy migration as compared to the other two kinds of NCs. As illustrated in Fig. 2d, when Nd3+ ions were situated within the core, the approximately spherically symmetric distribution of Nd3+ dopants permitted interionic cross relaxation to occur in all three dimensions. By contrast, when Nd3+ ions were positioned in the middle shell, the cross relaxation was spatially confined to the two-dimensional spherical shell.

Generally, as a rational approximation, the random distribution of Nd3+ ions in the NC can be regarded as the filling of uniform hard spheres centered at the positions of Nd3+ ions (with a diameter equal to the shortest distance between two Nd3+ ions) in a hexagonal close-packed manner (Fig. S7). The shortest Nd3+–Nd3+ distance (d) can be calculated using the relationship , where a (5.98 Å) and c (3.53 Å) are the unit cell parameters of the hexagonal (β)-phase NaLuF4 matrix; z (1.5) represents the number of formula units in the unit cell; x is the doping concentration of lanthanide ions. Therefore, for the core–triple shell NCs with 20% Nd3+ doped in the inner core (hereafter abbreviated as NCs(Nd in core)) or the core–quadruple shell NCs with 20% Nd3+ doped in the middle shell (NCs(Nd in mid shell)), the shortest Nd3+–Nd3+ distance is calculated to be ∼8.01 Å. Note that, for NCs(Nd in core), the average diameter of the inner core is ∼18.17 nm, which means that the maximum number of stacking layers of uniform hard spheres with a diameter of 8.01 Å equals ; meanwhile, for NCs(Nd in mid shell), the mean shell thickness is ∼2.43 nm, indicating that the maximum number of stacking layers equals . As such, for NCs(Nd in core), the number of nearest neighbors for the vast majority (23/25 = 92%) of Nd3+ ions is 12; meanwhile, for NCs(Nd in mid shell), the number of nearest neighbors for two-thirds of the Nd3+ ions is 9, and that for the remaining one-third is 12. In other words, the cross relaxation65 rate KCR between two Nd3+ ions should be larger in NCs(Nd in core) than in NCs(Nd in mid shell) due to the larger average number of neighboring Nd3+ ions. The apparent cross relaxation between the 4F3/2 → 4I15/2 and 4I9/2 → 4I15/2 transitions of Nd3+ can rapidly depopulate the 4F3/2 excited state (Fig. 2c), resulting in a significant decrease in the energy transfer rate from Nd3+ (4F3/2) to Yb3+ (2F5/2) and subsequently lowering the quantum efficiency of Yb3+ → Tm3+ → Gd3+ → Tb3+ energy migration upconversion.

On the other hand, the Förster energy transfer rate KET (Nd–Yb) of Nd3+ (4F3/2) → Yb3+ (2F5/2) was larger in NCs(Nd in mid shell) than in NCs(Nd in core) due to the shorter Nd3+–Yb3+ distance in NCs(Nd in mid shell), while the shorter distance simultaneously increased the rate of back energy transfer from Yb3+ 2F5/2 to Nd3+ 4I15/2 (KBET (Yb–Nd)).66 Additionally, the vibrational relaxation rate KVR of Nd3+ was also larger in NCs(Nd in mid shell) because the Nd3+ ions doped in the middle shell were more susceptible to the high-frequency phonon modes of surface ligands.

Note that the total depopulation rate (i.e., the sum of KCR + KET (Nd–Yb) + KVR + KR) of the Nd3+ 4F3/2 excited state can be extracted from the fitting of the photoluminescence (PL) decay curve acquired by monitoring the 4F3/2 → 4I11/2 emission of Nd3+ at 1064 nm (Fig. 2e). It was observed that the PL decay features for NCs(Nd in core) and NCs(Nd in mid shell) were almost equal to each other, indicating that the complicated competition (i.e. mutual inhibition)67–71 between the four depopulation channels (Fig. 2g) ultimately resulted in approximately equivalent total depopulation rates. Additionally, note that the luminescence decay curves of Nd3+ can be fitted by using a bi-exponential function (with the fitting parameters of A1, τ1, A2, τ2), as depicted in Fig. 2e. For the decay curves of Nd3+ doped in the inner core and in the middle shell, the corresponding pre-exponential factors A1 (1.79 and 1.67) were found to be dozens of times larger than A2 (0.03 and 0.06), respectively, which indicates that the vast majority of Nd3+ ions occupy a single site, with the remaining very small portion located at another site.

The competing relationship between different depopulation channels was also embodied in the equal total depopulation rates of the Yb3+ 2F5/2 excited states (i.e., the sum of KBET (Nd–Yb) + KET (Yb–Tm) (Yb3+ → Tm3+ energy transfer rate) + KVR + KR), as reflected by the similar PL decay curves of Yb3+ 2F5/2 → 2F7/2 emissions for NCs(Nd in core) and NCs(Nd in mid shell) (Fig. 2e, right panel). It should be mentioned that KBET (Yb–Nd) was larger in NCs(Nd in mid shell) than in NCs(Nd in core). Thus, due to competition between the Yb3+ → Nd3+ back energy transfer, Yb3+ → Tm3+ energy transfer and Yb3+ radiative transition processes, it can be deduced that both KR and KET (Yb–Tm) were smaller in NCs(Nd in mid shell) than in NCs(Nd in core) (Fig. 2g, right panel), as evidenced by the ∼5.1 times larger Yb3+ emission intensity at 980 nm (Fig. 2f) and the ∼2.4 times stronger EMU emission intensity of Tb3+ at 542 nm (Fig. 2b) in NCs(Nd in core) than in NCs(Nd in mid shell). In other words, the suppressed back energy transfer of Yb3+ → Nd3+ in the sandwich structured NCs with Nd3+ doped in the inner core was responsible for their stronger EMU emissions of Tb3+ than the NCs with Nd3+ doped in the middle shell.

For the sandwich structured NaLuF4:Nd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs, the spatial isolation of Nd3+ in the inner core from Yb3+ in the 1st middle shell helped inhibit the deleterious back energy transfer of Yb3+ 2F5/2 → Nd3+ 4I15/2. However, an appropriate amount of Yb3+ ions co-doped in the inner core might still be indispensable to act as bridges to facilitate excitation energy transfer from the inner-core Nd3+ to the middle-shell Yb3+ through the Nd3+ → Yb3+ … →Yb3+ energy transfer path.72,73 Experimentally, with the optimal doping ratio of Nd3+ (20%) in the inner core (as reflected by the strongest emission intensity in the 20% Nd3+-doped NCs) (Fig. 3a), the strongest Tb3+ EMU emission and the largest five-photon upconversion energy migration efficiency (as reflected in the biggest intensity ratio between the Tb3+ 542 nm emission and the sum of the UV emissions of Tm3+ at 344 nm and Gd3+ at 311 nm) were observed in NaLuF4:Nd,Yb@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs with 10% Yb3+ co-doped in the inner core, relative to the NCs with 0% and 30% Yb3+ co-dopants (Fig. 3b). Meanwhile, the higher QY value in the NaLuF4:Nd(20%),Yb(10%)@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs than in the NaLuF4:Nd(20%)@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs (Table S1) also verified that the luminescence efficiency of Tb3+ EMU emission was further enhanced by introducing Yb3+ ions into the core to shorten the Nd3+–Yb3+ energy transfer distance.

Furthermore, from the EMU emission spectra of the obtained optimal sandwich structured NaLuF4:Nd(20%),Yb(10%)@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs monitored under varying excitation power densities (Fig. 3c), it was observed that the Tb3+ EMU emission intensity and the corresponding intensity ratio between the Tb3+ 542 nm emission and the sum of the UV emissions of Tm3+ at 344 nm and Gd3+ at 311 nm both increased monotonically with increasing power density in the measuring range of ∼57.3–152.8 W cm−2. Particularly, the intensity ratio was calculated to be ∼9.7 at an excitation power density of ∼95.5 W cm−2, and even up to ∼111.1 at ∼152.8 W cm−2 (Fig. 3c and Table 1), indicating that the UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ were inhibited to a negligible magnitude with respect to the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+. This inhibition effect demonstrated that the energy transfer rates (KET) of Tm3+ (1I6) → Gd3+ (6P7/2) and Gd3+ (6P7/2) → Tb3+ (5D0) largely exceeded the radiative transition rates (KR) of Tm3+ (1I6) and Gd3+ (6P7/2), respectively, due to competition between the two different depopulation channels. Therefore, the efficiency (defined as KET/(KET + KR)) of five-photon upconversion energy migration approached 100% when KET ≫ KR, at an excitation power density above a threshold of ∼150 W cm−2.

Basically, the monotonic increase of Tb3+ EMU emission intensity with power density originated from the unique ladder-like energy levels of Tm3+, because a high excitation power density promoted the population of higher energy levels of Tm3+ (such as 1G4, 1D2, and 1I6), subsequently facilitating the energy transfer from Tm3+ 1I6 to Gd3+ 6P7/2 and finally to Tb3+ 5D4 to achieve enhanced EMU emissions. However, this explanation is not sufficient to account for the experimental observation that the nearly complete inhibition of UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ was concomitant with the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ when the excitation power density was above a threshold. Then, to provide a brief theoretical interpretation for the microscopic mechanism, the complex Tb3+ EMU process in the sandwich structured NCs was simplified and schematically depicted in Fig. 3d.35 In this model, the populating process of the 6P7/2 excited state (denoted as D1) of Gd3+ by the initial Yb3+–Tm3+ five-photon upconversion and subsequent energy transfer of Tm3+ 1I6 → Gd3+ 6P7/2 was described as a pseudo-ground state absorption transition from 8S7/2 (D0) of Gd3+. For brevity, the 5D0 excited state and 7F6 ground state of Tb3+ were denoted as A1 and A0, respectively. According to eqn (3), the density of pseudo-absorption transition scales with the energy transfer rate KET of Gd3+ → Tb3+. This positive correlation indicates that the depopulation of Gd3+ 6P7/2 by the Gd3+ → Tb3+ energy transfer facilitates energy transfer from Tm3+ 1I6 to Gd3+ 6P7/2, speeding up the depopulation of 1I6. That is, both the steady-state population densities of Tm3+ 1I6 and Gd3+ 6P7/2 scale inversely with the energy transfer rate KET of Gd3+ → Tb3+. On the other hand, for Tm3+ 1I6 or Gd3+ 6P7/2, due to competition between the radiative transition and energy transfer (Tm3+ → Gd3+ or Gd3+ → Tb3+) depopulation channels, the rate of radiative transition from Tm3+ 1I6 or Gd3+ 6P7/2 will be remarkably decreased with the increase of the energy transfer rate. As evidenced by the PL decay curves of Gd3+ 6P7/2 → 8S7/2 emissions for the sandwich-structured NaLuF4:Nd,Yb@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb NCs and NaLuF4:Nd,Yb@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4 NCs with no Tb3+ doping in the outer shell (Fig. 3e), the approximately equal total depopulation rates indicated that the energy transfer process of Gd3+ → Tb3+ can inhibit the other depopulation channels such as radiative transition. Altogether, it is rational to deduce that, under 808 nm excitation with a power density above a threshold, both the steady-state population densities and radiative transition rates of Tm3+ 1I6 and Gd3+ 6P7/2 were decreased due to the efficient energy transfer processes of Tm3+ → Gd3+ and Gd3+ → Tb3+, which eventually resulted in the nearly complete inhibition of UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ concomitant with the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+.

Note that there exists a similarity in the energy-level configuration between Tb3+ and Eu3+ ions, i.e., the large energy gap between the excited state 5D4 of Tb3+ (or 5D0 of Eu3+) and the ground state 7F0 of Tb3+ (or 7F6 of Eu3+), results in very weak interionic cross relaxation and vibrational relaxation processes of the 5D4 (or 5D0) excited state (Fig. 2c and 4a). Thus, the highly efficient EMU emissions of Eu3+ can also be obtained by using Gd3+ as the bridge in the sandwich-structured NaLuF4:Nd,Gd@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Eu NCs.54,74 As illustrated in Fig. 4b, the characteristic upconversion emission bands of Eu3+ 5D0 → 7FJ were observed upon 808 nm excitation. Analogous to the Tb3+ EMU mechanism, at an excitation power density of ∼152.8 W cm−2, the intensity ratio between the strongest emission of Eu3+ at 614 nm and the sum of the maximum UV emissions of Tm3+ at 344 nm and Gd3+ at 311 nm was calculated to be 93.5, indicative of an ∼100% efficiency of the five-photon upconversion energy migration (Fig. 4b). In a word, due to the similarity in the energy-level configuration between Tb3+ and Eu3+ ions, a ∼100% efficiency of the five-photon upconversion energy migration was accomplished both in the Tb3+ and Eu3+ systems.

Conclusion

We have proposed a prototypical sandwich structured core–triple shell NaLuF4:Nd,Yb@NaLuF4:Yb@NaGdF4:Tm@NaLuF4:Tb (or Eu) NC for acquiring the strong EMU emissions of Tb3+ (or Eu3+), while simultaneously suppressing the UV upconversion emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ that may provoke autofluorescence interference in bioapplications based on EMU NCs. In the core–triple shell NCs with Nd3+ doped in the inner core, the profoundly suppressed back energy transfer of Yb3+ → Nd3+ was responsible for their relatively stronger EMU emissions as compared to the NCs with Nd3+ doped in the middle shell. Meanwhile, we demonstrated that the effective inter-shell energy transfer of Gd3+ → Tb3+ or Eu3+ can induce the enhanced energy transfer of Tm3+ → Gd3+, and more importantly, the dramatically decreased radiative transition rates of Tm3+ 1I6 and Gd3+ 6P7/2 due to competition between the radiative transition and energy transfer depopulation channels, thus leading to a nearly 100% efficiency of the five-photon upconversion energy migration (Tm3+ → Gd3+ → Tb3+ (or Eu3+)). As a result, under 808 nm excitation at a moderate power density of ∼150 W cm−2, the intensity ratio between the strongest emission of Tb3+ (or Eu3+) and the sum of the maximum UV emissions of Tm3+ and Gd3+ reached a large value of ∼111.1 (or 93.5) for the core–triple shell NCs, markedly exceeding the previously reported 808 nm excited EMU NCs.30–33 These discoveries have provided a deeper mechanistic understanding of the fundamental EMU process in lanthanide NCs, which might pave the way towards their frontier bioapplications.6–8,75

Abstract
We report on the scaling and units of the Rayleigh wave elastic response function 𝐴! (𝜔) that is
output from the widely used Computer Programs in Seismology (CPS) to aid in the modeling of
ground motion sourced by atmospheric explosions. The program uses mixed units (km, second,
km/s, gm/cc) to keep 𝐴! (𝜔) near 100 and prevent any numerical underflow or overflow. We
compare two models for the response of an elastic half space to the output from CPS. Our
application inputs the recommended, mixed unit geological models to determine how researchers
must scale this output to obtain physical units for 𝐴! (𝜔) that represents the amplitude scaling for
the minimum group velocity (Airy phase) contribution to Rayleigh waves. We determine that a
CPS user must scale the output for 𝐴! (𝜔) by 10-12 (m/km)2(g/cc/m3/kg) to obtain MKS (meter,
kg, second) units and then must multiply this result by the vertical component eigenfunction
squared, that is evaluated at the free surface.

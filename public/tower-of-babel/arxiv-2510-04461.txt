The high order spectral radius of graphs without long cycles or paths
Yuntian Wang, Lizhu Sun, Changjiang Bu

Abstract
In 1959, Erdős and Gallai established two classic theorems, which determine the maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex graph with no cycles of length at least k k , and in an n n -vertex graph with no paths on k k vertices, respectively. Subsequently, generalized and spectral versions of the Erdős-Gallai theorems have been investigated. A concept of a high order spectral radius for graphs was introduced in 2023, defined as the spectral radius of a tensor and termed the t t -clique spectral radius ρ t ​ ( G ) \rho_{t}(G) . In this paper, we establish a high order spectral version of Erdős-Gallai theorems by employing the t t -clique spectral radius, i.e., we determine the extremal graphs that attain the maximum t t -clique spectral radius in the n n -vertex graphs with no cycles of length at least k k and in the n n -vertex graphs with no paths on k k vertices, respectively.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
\bar{G}\in\mathrm{SPEX}_{t}^{\prime}(n,C_{\geq k})
, then every connected induced subgraph
G
of

has diameter at most 2. Moreover, there exists at least one vertex

in
G
satisfying

Proof.

Let
\bar{G}\in\mathrm{SPEX}_{t}^{\prime}(n,C_{\geq k})
, and let
G
be any connected induced subgraph of
\bar{G}
, observe that for any adjacent vertices
u
and
v
in
G
, either
P_{u}(v)
or
P_{v}(u)
is empty in
G
.

If the diameter of
G
is greater than
2
, then there exists a pair of non-adjacent vertices
u_{1}
and
v_{1}
such that the distance between them is
3
, i.e., there exist
u_{2},v_{2}
with
u_{1}\sim u_{2}\sim v_{2}\sim v_{1}
and
u_{1}\nsim v_{2}
,
v_{1}\nsim u_{2}
. Consequently,
P_{u_{2}}(v_{2})\neq\emptyset
and
P_{v_{2}}(u_{2})\neq\emptyset
in
G
, leading to a contradiction.

Next, we prove that there exists a vertex
i\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(i)=|V(G)|-1
. Fix an arbitrary vertex
u\in V(G)
, if
d_{G}(u)=|V(G)|-1
, the proof is complete. Otherwise, we discuss the case where
d_{G}(u)<|V(G)|-1
. We make the following definition in the graph
G
, let

\displaystyle Y(v)=

\displaystyle V(G)\setminus(N_{G}(v)\cup\{v\}),S(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap N(Y(v)),T(v)=

\displaystyle N_{G}(v)\setminus S(v),

where
N(Y(v))=\{u|u\in N_{G}(w),\text{ for some }\ w\in Y(v)\}
.
If
d_{G}(u)<|V(G)|-1
, then
Y(u)\neq\emptyset
, since
G
is connected,
S(u)\neq\emptyset
. For any
v_{1}\in S(u)
and any
v_{2}\in N_{G}(u)\setminus\{v_{1}\}
,
v_{1}\sim v_{2}
hold, otherwise, there exists
w\in Y(u)\cap N_{G}(v_{1})
such that
v_{2}\in P_{v_{1}}(u)\neq\emptyset
and
w\in P_{u}(v_{1})\neq\emptyset
, which is a contradiction.
Since that
G
has diameter
2
,
Y(u)\subseteq\bigcup_{v\in S(u)}N_{G}(v)
. If
|S(u)|=1
, let
S(u)=\{v\}
, then
Y(u)\subseteq N_{G}(v)
, and thus
d_{G}(v)=|G|-1
, completing the proof. If
|S(u)|\neq 1
, take any
v_{1},v_{2}\in S(u)
, since
v_{1}\sim v_{2}
, either
P_{v_{1}}(v_{2})=\emptyset
or
P_{v_{2}}(v_{1})=\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, assume
P_{v_{2}}(v_{1})=\emptyset
. Then,

\displaystyle(N_{G}(v_{1})\cap Y(u))\subseteq(N_{G}(v_{2})\cap Y(u)),

Meaning there exists a vertex
v^{\prime}
such that for all

,

\displaystyle N_{G}(v)\cap Y(u)\subseteq N_{G}(v^{\prime})\cap Y(u).

Since
Y(u)\subseteq\bigcup_{v\in S(u)}N_{G}(v)
, it follows that
Y(u)\subseteq N_{G}(v^{\prime})
, and therefore
d_{G}(v^{\prime})=|V(G)|-1
, completing the proof.

∎

In a graph
G
, a vertex of degree
|V(G)|-1
is called a universal vertex of
G
, the set of all universal vertices in
G
is called the universal vertex set of
G
, denoted by
Un(G)
, and we write
un(G)=|Un(G)|
. It is easy to see that
Un(G)
is a clique in
G
.

Now we use Lemma
3.22
to analyze the structure of graphs in
\mathrm{SPEX}_{t}^{\prime}(n,C_{\geq k})
. Let
G\in\mathrm{SPEX}_{t}^{\prime}(n,C_{\geq k})
. Select an arbitrary connected component of
G
, denoted by
G_{1}
. By Lemma
3.22
, we know that
Un(G_{1})\neq\emptyset
. If
V(G_{1})\setminus Un(G_{1})\neq\emptyset
, let
G_{1}^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G_{1}
induced by the vertex set
V(G_{1})\setminus Un(G_{1})
. Choose an arbitrary connected component of
G_{1}^{\prime}
, denoted by
G_{2}
, and repeat this process.

It is evident that for any
G_{m}
, the set
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}Un(G_{i})
forms a clique in
G
with
\sum_{i=1}^{m}un(G_{i})
vertices. Since
G
is
C_{\geq k}
-free, we must have

\displaystyle k>\sum_{i=1}^{m}un(G_{i})\geq m,

therefore, this process must terminate in at most
k-1
steps for any sequence of choices. That is, there exists some
m<k
such that
V(G_{m})\setminus Un(G_{m})=\emptyset
, holds for any choice of connected components.

For any positive integer

, we define the connected components of the subgraph induced on
V(G_{i})\setminus Un(G_{i})
as the subordinate component of
G_{i}
. A graph

satisfying

is called a level-1 component, and it is easy to see that any level-1 component is a clique. If all subordinate components of

are level-1 components, then

is called a level-2 component. If every subordinate component of
G_{i}
is either a level-1 or level-2 component, then

is called a level-3 component.

Next, we continue to study the structure of graphs in
\mathrm{SPEX}_{t}^{\prime}(n,C_{\geq k})
. Before that, we first introduce a notation. For a subgraph

\displaystyle L_{r}(G,H,x)=\sum_{\{i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\}\in K_{r}(G)\ and\ \{i_{1},\cdots,i_{r}\}\subseteq N_{c}(H)\setminus V(H)}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r}}.

where

.

, i.e.,
K_{un(G)}\vee\{K_{l}\cup I_{n^{\prime}-un(G)-l}\}
, where

represents the number of vertices in graph

,

, since

is

-clique connected,

;
when

is a level-2 component, we have

and

, furthermore, we have

, otherwise, by fully connecting all pairs of vertices in graph

is obtained, then we have

and
\rho_{t}(K_{n^{\prime}})>\rho_{t}(G)=\rho_{t}(\bar{G})
, this is contradictory.
Then
c(G)=2un(G)+l-1<k
, that is
\frac{k}{2}>{un}(G)
. And then, since

is
t
-clique connected,

.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

When

, for

,
G
is a

-clique connected compoment of

.
This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\rho_{t}(K_{k-1})=\max\{A_{t}(K_{k-1})x^{t}\mid\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{t}=1\}=\frac{t}{k-1}\begin{pmatrix}k-1\\

Claim 3.24.2

\bar{G}=G

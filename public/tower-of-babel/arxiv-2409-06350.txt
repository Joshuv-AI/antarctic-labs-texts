Generating Extended Mapping Class Groups with Two Periodic Elements
Reid Harris

Abstract
. The extended mapping class group of a surface Σ \Sigma is defined to be the group of isotopy classes of (not necessarily orientation-preserving) homeomorphisms of Σ \Sigma . We are able to show that the extended mapping class group of an n n -punctured sphere is generated by two elements of finite order exactly when n ≠ 4 n\not=4 . We use this result to prove that the extended mapping class group of a genus 2 surface is generated by two elements of finite order.

\Sigma_{g,n}

n

In regards to torsion generating sets, Maclachlan

[

. Brendle and Farb
[

show that
\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{g,n}\right)

Korkmaz shows in

[

g\geq 3
. Yildiz
[

shows that
\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{g}\right)

However, the corresponding question about

]

that

The group
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{g,n}\right)

2.1.
Spherical Braid Group

Given any surface
\Sigma
, the classical braid group can be generalized to the
braid group on
\Sigma
, denoted
B_{n}(\Sigma):=\pi_{1}(\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(\Sigma))
, where
\mathrm{Conf}_{n}(\Sigma)
is the space of unordered configurations of
n
distinct points on
\Sigma
. In particular, we will be interested in the
spherical braid groups

B_{n}(S^{2})
. We have a surjective homomorphism
B_{n}\to B_{n}(S^{2})
with kernel generated by the central element
R_{n}:=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n-1}\dots\sigma_{1}
. Then
B_{n}(S^{2})
has the presentation given by generators
\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\sigma}_{n-1}
and relations

•

\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\tilde{\sigma}_{j}=\tilde{\sigma}_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{i}
for
|i-j|>2

•

\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\tilde{\sigma}_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{i}=\tilde{\sigma}_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{i}\tilde{\sigma}_{j}
for
|i-j|=1

•

R_{n}=1
.

We turn our attention to the relationship between
B_{n}(S^{2})
and
\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,n}\right)
. We have the exact sequence

(1)

\displaystyle 0\to\langle\beta\rangle\to B_{n}(S^{2})\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,n}\right)\to 0

where
\beta=(\tilde{\sigma}_{1}\dots\tilde{\sigma}_{n-1})^{n}
and
\langle\beta\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(see
[
6
]
, Section 9.1.4 and 9.2).

Here, we let
\sigma_{i}=\psi(\tilde{\sigma}_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. Since we are interested in elements of finite order, we record the following result:

Proposition 2.1
.

The elements of
\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,n}\right)
of finite order are conjugate to a power of one of the following:

\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}

\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-2}

n-1

\alpha_{2}

\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-3}\sigma_{n-2}^{2}

n-2

Let
\tilde{\sigma}_{i}
refer to the standard generators of
B_{n}(S^{2})
. Let

such that

. There exists a lift
\tilde{f}\in B_{n}(S^{2})
. Thus,
\tilde{f}^{k}
is a power of
\beta\in B_{n}(S^{2})

2.1.1.
Birman-Hilden

We introduce the Birman-Hilden exact sequence for
\Sigma_{2}
. For details, see
[
1
]
and
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Birman-Hilden)
.

Let
\iota\in\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)
denote the mapping class of an involution on
\Sigma_{2}
with 6 fixed points. There is an exact sequence

(5)

\displaystyle 0\to\langle\iota\rangle\to\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)\to\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,6}\right)\to 0.

The following result will be useful in Section
4.3
to prove part of the main theorem. It extends the Birman-Hilden exact sequence to the extended mapping class group.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\iota\in\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)
denote the mapping class of an involution on
\Sigma_{2}
with 6 fixed points. There is an exact sequence

0\to\langle\iota\rangle\to\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)\xrightarrow{\Psi}\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,6}\right)\to 0.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)
be orientation-reversing. Since there exists an orientation-reversing homeomorphism
T:\Sigma_{2}\to\Sigma_{2}
which is fiber-preserving, we may pick a representative
f:\Sigma_{2}\to\Sigma_{2}
of
\phi
which is fiber-preserving: there is a representative
g
of
[T]\phi
which is fiber preserving by
[
1
]
and so we may take
f=T^{-1}\circ g
. Letting
\pi:\Sigma_{2}\to\Sigma_{0,6}
denote the branched covering map, we define
\bar{f}:\Sigma_{0,6}\to\Sigma_{0,6}
by
\bar{f}=\pi\circ f\circ\pi^{-1}
.

Suppose
f
and
f^{\prime}
are both representatives of
\phi
, that is,
f
and
f^{\prime}
are isotopic. Then
T\circ f
and
T\circ f^{\prime}
are orientation-preserving, isotopic and fiber-preserving. By Theorem
2.2
, these maps are isotopic through fiber-preserving homemorphisms, say
H:\Sigma_{2}\times[0,1]\to\Sigma_{2}
is such an isotopy. Hence,
H^{\prime}=T^{-1}\circ H
is a fiber-preserving isotopy between
f
and

. This isotopy then descends to an isotopy between
\bar{f}
and
\bar{f}^{\prime}
. Thus, we have a well-defined map
\Psi:\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)\to\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,6}\right)
given by
[f]\mapsto[\bar{f}]
. Since
\Psi|_{\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-2.45836pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)}
is exactly the Birman-Hilden homomorphism from (
5
) and the kernel of this map must lie in
\mathrm{Mod}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)
, we see that
\ker(\Psi)=\langle\iota\rangle
.
∎

3.
Periodic Elements in
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,n})

Let
n\geq 1
. For our standard model of
\Sigma_{0,n}
, we take the unit sphere embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
along with marked points
p_{k}
,
k=0,\dots,n-1
, given by

p_{k}=\left(\cos\frac{2\pi k}{n},\;\sin\frac{2\pi k}{n},\;0\right).

Let
T:\Sigma_{0,n}\to\Sigma_{0,n}
denote the map given by
T(x,y,z)=(x,y,-z).
We also let
T
denote the isotopy class of this homeomorphism in
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,n}\right)
. Let
\sigma_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
, denote the mapping class of the right Dehn twist about the arc connecting
p_{i}
to
p_{i+1}
along the equator. Note that
T\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i}^{-1}T
for each
1\leq i\leq n-1
.

We have the following presentation for
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,n})
: generators are
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
, and
T
with relations

•

T^{2}=(T\sigma_{i})^{2}=1
, for
1\leq i\leq n-1
,

•

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}
, for
|i-j|\geq 2
,

•

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}
, for
|i-j|=1
,

•

(\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1})^{n}=1
,

•

\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n-1}\dots\sigma_{1}=1

This is the presentation obtained from the isomorphism
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,n})\cong\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{0,n})\rtimes\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
where the non-identity element
T
of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
acts on
\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{0,n})
by
\sigma_{i}\mapsto\sigma_{i}^{-1}
.

Recall that the orientation-preserving mapping classes of finite order are given by Proposition
2.1
. Using the presentation above, we have that

\displaystyle T\alpha_{0}T

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}^{-1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n-1}\dots\sigma_{1})\cdot\sigma_{1}^{-1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}.

Thus,
T\alpha_{0}
is periodic with order
n
if
n
is even and order
2n
if
n
is odd. We also easily see that

(T\sigma_{1}\sigma_{3}\dots\sigma_{2k-1})^{2}=1,

for each
k=0,\dots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor
. Lastly,

\displaystyle(T\sigma_{n-1}^{-1})\alpha_{2}(T\sigma_{n-1}^{-1})

\displaystyle=T\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\alpha_{0}\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\sigma_{n-2}T\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{n-1}\alpha_{0}\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n-2}^{-1}\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\sigma_{n-2}\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n-2}^{-1}\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{0}\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\sigma_{n-2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{2}.

Thus,
T\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}
and
\alpha_{2}
commute and
T\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\alpha_{2}
has order
n-2
if
n
is even or
2(n-2)
if
n
is odd.

For general
n
, these do not exhaust all possibilities of orientation-reversion periodic elements, even up to conjugacy. For example, when
n=9
, there exists an orientation-reversing mapping class of order 6, acting by the permutation
(1\;2\;3\;4\;5\;6)(7\;8\;9)
on the marked points, which is not covered by any of the above examples or their powers. However, it would be interesting to find a classification of all finite-order elements of
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{0,n}\right)
in terms of the generators
\sigma_{i}
.

4.1.
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,4})

Theorem 4.1
.

The group
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,4})

Proof.

Consider the short exact sequence

(6)

\displaystyle 0\to\langle-\mathrm{Id}\rangle\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{q}\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

If

of

so
A
is periodic.
Suppose that

is generated by two elements

of finite order. Then, if
A,B
are representatives of
\bar{A},\bar{B}
, then
A
and
B
generate a subgroup
H
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. For any

, the only representatives of

and

or

[\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):H]\leq 2

\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})/H
is abelian and

Note that

4.2.
Periodic generation of

We begin with a simple observation:

Proposition 4.2
.

If

\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,n})
is generated by

Proof.

Let

Theorem 4.6
.

The group
\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}\hskip-3.00003pt\left(\Sigma_{2}\right)
is generated by two elements of finite order.

Proof.

We have the exact sequence from Theorem
2.3
:

(7)

\displaystyle 0\to\langle\iota\rangle\to\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{2})\xrightarrow{q}\mathrm{Mod}^{\pm}(\Sigma_{0,6})\to 0,

6

Decoding Mandibular Angle Fracture Management: Biomechanical Analysis of Different Plating Techniques – An In Vitro Study
Authors: Adalarasan Subramanian, Thiruneelakandan Sambanthan, Akash Punithan, S Meera Petchiammal, R. Bhuvana Laxmi, Vishal Srikanth, E Eswar
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjective:The primary rationale of the study is to assess the biomechanical aspects of Champy’s, monoplanar, biplanar, and 3D strut plating systems using an in vitro model in the management of the angle of mandible fracture.Methods:Thirty-two hemi-sectioned mandibles (N = 32) were obtained from adult goats and divided into four groups (N = 8) for different plating techniques: monoplanar, biplanar, Champy’s, and three-dimensional curved angle strut plating. A customized biomechanical test machine simulated masticatory loads by applying compressive forces. Force versus displacement data were recorded using an Instron Lloyd LRX machine.Results:Plating techniques with two plates exhibited higher resistance to displacement than single-plate techniques. The three-dimensional curved angle strut plate group had the highest mean compressive strength (184.7913 N), followed by biplanar plating (170.29 N), monoplanar plating (159.3419 N), and Champy’s plating (55.48375 N). Statistical analysis showed significant differences between the three-dimensional curved angle strut plating technique and Champy’s plating technique (P value ≤ 0.0016*), as well as between the biplanar plating technique and Champy’s plating technique (P value ≤ 0.0010*).Conclusion:The three-dimensional curved angle strut plate and biplanar plating techniques exhibited superior biomechanical performance compared to monoplanar and Champy’s plating techniques.

INTRODUCTION

Mandibular fractures can be due to various causes, namely trauma from road traffic accidents, sports injuries, or physical altercations.[1] Angle of the mandible is a common site for fractures since it is a relatively weak area compared to other parts of the jaw. The primary aim of surgical intervention is to provide stability to the fractured bone during the healing phase.

Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) procedures have become the standard line of treatment nowadays. Despite the availability of various treatment options, there is no consensus on the ideal way of surgically managing the angle of mandible fracture. Therefore, it is important to probe the multitude of factors that are responsible for instability at the mandibular angle fracture site and establish a correlation between its mechanical and biological outcomes.

This research endeavors to provide valuable insights into optimizing the stability and functional outcomes of mandibular angle fracture fixation, ultimately benefiting patients and reducing post-surgical complications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

In this study, a total of thirty-two hemimandibles (N = 32) were obtained from adult goats with a mean weight of 40 kg. Leftover goat mandibles of similar sizes were collected from abattoirs and used in the study. No goats were specifically sacrificed for the purpose of the study. The goat mandibular specimen was kept damp and refrigerated until all testing was over. To fit it into the biomechanical apparatus, the coronoid process was removed. The goat mandibles were segmented uniformly from the retromolar area along a plane to the angle of mandible.

Group allocation and plating techniques: The hemi-sectioned mandibles were divided randomly into four groups of eight specimens each. Four different plating techniques were used for fixation: group-I was plated using the monoplanar plating technique [Figure 1], group-II was plated using the biplanar plating technique [Figure 2], group-III was plated using Champy’s plating technique [Figure 3] using 2 mm, 4 holed with gap plates, and group-IV was plated with three-dimensional curved angle strut plates [Figure 4]. To reduce the variables, all the screws used in the study are of titanium, 6 mm in size and self-tapped. The fractured goat mandible parts were reduced, and rigid fixation of the segments was done with miniplates and screws.

Biomechanical testing

A hemi-sectioned mandibular specimen was positioned snugly to fit into an Instron Lloyd LRX machine (Universal testing machine designed to perform tensile, compression, flex, peel, puncture, friction, and shear tests). A compression force (N) was applied at the mandibular angle to simulate masticatory loads. The applied loads ranged from 0 to 300 N. The separation of bone-screw-plate is construed as the experimental endpoint. The Instron equipment recorded the applied force level and the associated displacement. A two mm separation is construed as an experimental endpoint. The compressive force required to achieve this separation was measured from the displacement console of the equipment.

Statistical analysis

Different plating techniques were compared in terms of nonparametric scale variables. Mann-Whitney U test with Kruskal–Wallis test extension was used to test it. The statistical analysis was used to find out any significant differences between the four plating techniques. A ‘P’ value of less than 0.05 (P < 0.05) was taken to be statistically significant.

RESULTS

The plating techniques in which two plates were used are more resistant to displacement than single-plate techniques. Among the plate types, the highest mean compressive strength was observed in the three-dimensional curved angle strut plate, with a value of 184.7913 N (SD = 83.60159). This was followed by biplanar plating, which had a mean compressive strength of 170.29 N (SD = 75.79291), while the monoplanar plating had 159.3419 N (SD = 3.76792) and the Champy’s plating exhibited the lowest mean compressive strength of 55.48375 N (SD = 21.09586) [Graph 1].

Mean compressive strength of different plating systems

A statistically significant difference was noted when comparing Champy’s plating technique with the three-dimensional curved angle strut plating technique (P value < 0.0016*). This suggests that the three-dimensional curved angle strut plate exhibits greater resistance than Champy’s plating technique when subjected to compressive forces [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

Open internal rigid fixation reduces the prolonged time period of immobilization and is associated with limited problems of patient compliance. However, in spite of the availability of various miniplate techniques in the literature, there is no consensus on the ideal fixation modality for an angle of mandible fractures. Champy’s technique, which involves a single miniplate in the noncompression zone, has been found effective in previous studies.[234] Our study found that when compared to biplanar plate fixation and three-dimensional curved angle strut plate techniques, Champy’s technique showed less desirable biomechanical characteristics.

The composition of mandibular bone and biomechano-elastic properties vary among individuals due to age factor, gender factors, degenerative bone diseases, and anatomical sites.[5] Even in the same bone, differences are there among different regions. The maximum bite force generated by an adult human is typically between 300 and 400 N,[6] but it is reduced in the presence of a fracture.[789] Therefore, a loading force of 300 N was used in this investigation to simulate the effects of masticatory forces on the fixation systems.

The evaluation of miniplate stability in our study was conducted by applying masticatory load at the angle of the mandible and evaluating the displacement of fracture ends in normal and tangential directions. Most separations were noticed at the superior aspect of the fracture site. This is in contradiction to the commonly held belief that superior border plating provides greater stability

There is a significant variation in the literature regarding the outcomes of the location of the plate and its impact on stability.[101112] Our study provides ample evidence to suggest that biplanar plate fixation provided more stability biomechanically than monoplanar plate fixation, and the three-dimensional strut plates had more favorable biomechanical properties similar to double miniplate fixation techniques.[13] This finding was resonant with the study of Korkmaz[14] who found through simulation that there is more stability with two miniplate fixation than a single miniplate at the superior border. In the three-dimensional strut plates, the stability gets increased manifold by the presence of struts between the two plates. Chhabaria et al.[15] found these plates to provide more stability to fractured fragments and prevent them from getting separated. Our study results were in concurrence with them since the maximum resistance was provided by three-dimensional curved strut plates when compared to other plating systems.

Anatomic reduction and stable fixation of mandibular angle fracture can be better achieved by plating the superior aspect and the inferior aspect of the mandible. This increases stability along the fractured bone segments and prevents the lower border of the mandible to getting displaced laterally.

In this study, biplanar plate fixation was found to provide increased stability than monoplanar plate fixation, in consonance with existing literature. However, in spite of the advantages of two miniplate fixations for an angle of mandible fractures, high complications are reported in the literature. In the study, the second group had dual miniplate fixation, and biplanar plate fixation provided more stability than monoplanar orientation. Previous studies have shown that placing the plate in a biplanar way is superior to a monoplanar plane,[16] irrespective of whether a monocortical or bicortical fixation technique is used. Furthermore, various studies were conducted earlier confirm that placing the miniplate obliquely, rather than horizontally, provides greater stability biomechanically.

The use of two miniplates for stabilizing and fixing the mandibular angle fractures has both advantages and challenges. While it can help avoid complications associated with opening the inferior fracture line, technical difficulties, increased risk of contamination, and prolonged operation time need to be considered. Though biplanar plate fixation and oblique miniplate fixation have shown greater biomechanical stability but are associated with high complication rates.

The current study revealed that the three-dimensional strut plates and double miniplates had higher resistance to compression loads in comparison with Champy’s technique, with statistical significance. Furthermore, the biomechanical behavior of biplanar plate orientation appeared to be more advantageous than monoplanar plate placement. The obliquely fixed mini plate with two bicortical screws in the proximal segment had the highest biomechanical stability. This finding aligns with the previous literature. The three-dimensional strut plate, made of two curved miniplates joined by perpendicular strut bars, offers increased stability in three dimensions due to its unique geometry and the ability to use more screws. This type of plate is already commonly used for symphyseal fractures where they resist the torsional strain effectively. Hence, these plates can be recommended for use on areas of the mandible where similar forces are at work.

In our study, we found that the three-dimensional curved angle strut plate is superior biomechanically when compared to the Champy technique of superior plating. However, there were no significant, biomechanical variations between the three-dimensional strut plate and double miniplate fixation techniques. These results are consistent with the analysis conducted by Alper Alkan et al.,[13] as described in their publication.

CONCLUSION

The AO/ASIF strongly advocates for the application of robust internal fixation hardware capable of withstanding the maximum load of forces which ensures not only complete functionality but also steadfast stability of the fractured segments. The utilization of two miniplate systems significantly enhances the stability of fracture segments. Furthermore, the incorporation of a three-dimensional strut plate emerges as a highly effective means of achieving the most secure fixation against the formidable forces generated during biting. By adopting a biplanar plate orientation, it is possible to attain an even more favorable biomechanical outcome, surpassing the advantages offered by monoplanar plate fixation. These compelling results firmly support the notion that a meticulous and comprehensive fixation strategy is paramount in mandibular angle fracture management, ensuring optimal outcomes and empowering patients to regain their full range of oral functions with unwavering confidence.
